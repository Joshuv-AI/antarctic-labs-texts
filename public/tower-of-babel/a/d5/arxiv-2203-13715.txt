1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the initial value problem for the

nonlinear Schrödinger–Airy equation
(NLSA)

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+i\,a\,\partial_{x}^{2}u+b\,\partial_{x}^{3}u+i\,c\,|u|^{2}u+d\,|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u+e\,u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
u=u(x,t)
is a complex valued function and

a,b,c,d,e
are parameters. This model was proposed in
[
21
,
22
]
to describe the propagation of signals in optic fibers.

G. Staffilani
[
27
]
showed that the equation (
1
) is locally (in time) well-possed
(i.e. there exists a unique stable solution) for
initial data in the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for any
s\geq 1/4
provided that

a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

and
c,d,e\in\mathbb{C}
.
Later, X. Carvajal
[
5
]
established the global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for
s>1/4
and real coefficients satisfying
be\neq 0
and
c=(d-e)a/(3b)
. Moreover, Carvajal obtained in
[
6
]
that the above results are sharp, in the sense that the map data-solution is not uniformly continuous for any
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
when
s<1/4
, under the constrains
e=0
,
bd>0
and
c=ad/(3b)
.

For particular choices of the parameters, the IVP (
1
) reduces to well-known equations of great relevance in quantum mechanics and water waves. For example, taking
a=-1
,
b=d=e=0
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, one gets the cubic
nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(NLS)

i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+c|u|^{2}u=0.

Its local and global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 0
, was proven by Y. Tsutsumi
[
29
]
while for
s<0
it is ill-posed (see
[
9
,
20
]
). Now, for
a=c=e=0
, and
b=d=1
we obtain the

modified Korteweg-de Vries equation
(mKdV)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u=0.

C. Kenig, G. Ponce and L. Vega
[
19
]
showed
that it is local well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 1/4
; and J. Colliander, M. Keel, G. Staffilani, H. Takaoka and T. Tao
[
11
]
demonstrated that the real mKdV is globally well-posed for
s>1/4
. Furthermore, these results are again optimal (
[
9
,
20
]
). On the other hand,
if we fix
a=-1
,
b=c=0
and
d=2e\in\mathbb{R}
we have the

derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(DNLS)

i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+ie\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}u)=0,

whose local well-posedness was provided by H. Takaoka
[
28
]
for
s\geq 1/2
and globally for
s>1/2
in

[
10
]
.

So far, all mentioned results concern with solvability in Sobolev spaces. As T. Kato remarked in
[
18
]
, functions in

H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
do not necessarily decay fast at infinity,
which is in many applications unrealistic.
In order to show well-posedness in spaces of fast–decaying functions we can replace
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
by a weighted Sobolev space of type

H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(w(x)dx)
,
for some appropriate
w
which is typically taken of power type.
The latest results in this direction for the NLS have been obtained by
J. Nahas and G. Ponce
[
26
]
,
for the mKdV by J. Nahas
[
25
]

(see also
[
13
]
) and for the NLSA by X. Carvajal and W. Neves
[
7
]
.
It is also worth mentioning that over the past few years this weighted Sobolev approach has been carried out for several nonlinear evolution equations
(see for instance

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, and references therein).

Coming back to the IVP for the NLSA, and by means of
Duhamel’s principle, it is straightforward to show that any solution of the partial differential equation (
1
) also satisfies the integral equation

(2)

u=e^{-t(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}u_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}\big(i\,c\,|u|^{2}u+d\,|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u+e\,u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}\big)(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

where
e^{-t(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}
stands for the Schrödinger–Airy semigroup (see Section
2.2
below for its precise definition). However, notice that not all solutions of (
2
) also verify (
1
), specially if
u_{0}
belongs to a Sobolev space of low regularity (see for example
[
23
, p. 96]
).

We are now in position of stating our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and

c,d,e\in\mathbb{C}
with
d=2e
.
Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2m}dx)
for
0<m\leq 1/8
.
Then, there exist
T>0
and a unique
solution
u
of the integral equation (
2
) such that

(3)

u(\cdot,t)\in H^{1/4}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2m}dx),\quad t\in(0,T].

A few remarks are in order. Firstly, Theorem
1.1
improves on the Sobolev regularity for the local part of
[
7
, Theorem 3.7]
established for
s=2
. Recall that for the unweighted case (
m=0
),
s=1/4
is optimal for the NLSA (
[
6
]
).
Moreover, our restriction of
2m\leq s
was shown to be sharp in the context of the mKdV (see
[
13
, Remark (b) on p. 5356]
), which makes it natural to conjecture that in the NLSA setting is too.
Finally, it is worth-mentioning that our approach imposes
d=2e
only in the analysis of the term
B_{2,2,2}
at the very end of Section
4
, in order to exploit the symmetry given by (
40
)
and hence take advantage of the property (
6
). It is an open question for the authors how to adapt the reasoning for arbitrary values
d
and
e
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we introduce notation as well as the main properties used later.
Deeply inspired by the work of Nahas
[
25
]
for the mKdV equation, in Section
3

we generalize his crucial estimate to the Schrödinger–Airy setting. Finally in Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
via the contraction principle.

2.
Notation, definitions and Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we use the standard notation
A\lesssim B
when there exists
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
when simultaneously
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

2.1.
Function spaces

For
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

consider its Fourier transform

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}

and recall the inverse Fourier formula

f(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

We also introduce the
L^{2}
–based Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
via the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\Big)^{1/2}

for

\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.
It is well-known that they satisfy the inclusion

H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\subset H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
,
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
, or equivalently

(4)

\|f\|_{H^{s_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s_{2}}},\quad s_{1}\leq s_{2}.

Now, for a function of two variables

f:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, let’s
define the mixed norms

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\int^{T}_{0}|f(x,t)|^{q}\,dt\Big|^{p/q}\,dx\Big)^{1/p}

and

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{p}_{x}}:=\Big(\int^{T}_{0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\Big|^{q/p}\,dt\Big)^{1/q}

with the usual modifications when
p=\infty

or
q=\infty
. Moreover, we write
\widehat{f}
meaning that the Fourier transform is taken with respect to the spatial variable
x
.

2.2.
Schrödinger–Airy semigroup

We denote by

e^{-t(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}

to the free propagator of the linear Schrödinger–Airy equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+i\,a\,\partial_{x}^{2}u+b\,\partial_{x}^{3}u=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

whose solution

u(x,t)=:e^{-t(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}u_{0}(x)

is easily represented on the Fourier side

(5)

(e^{-t(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}u_{0})^{\wedge}(\xi)=e^{it(a\xi^{2}+b\xi^{3})}\widehat{u}_{0}(\xi).

In
[
27
, Theorem 4.1(ii)]
the following estimate was established

(6)

\Big\|\partial_{x}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})(ia\partial^{2}_{x}+b\partial^{3}_{x})}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{T}},

which will be crucial in Section
4
.

2.3.
Fractional derivatives

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, it is standard to define the fractional derivative
D_{x}^{\alpha}
as the Fourier multiplier given by

(7)

\widehat{D_{x}^{\alpha}f}(\xi):=|\xi|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

In this notes we will restrict ourselves to the case of
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Our motivation for considering these derivatives is in connection with the Sobolev spaces and also with the
L^{2}
–weighted spaces for non-integer powers
m
, since Plancherel’s theorem provides

\|f\|_{L^{2}(|x|^{2m},dx)}:=\||x|^{m}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\sim\|D^{m}_{\xi}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.

For completeness, we state below the main properties involving fractional derivatives that will be needed in the sequel.

In
[
27
, Lemma 4.7]
it was shown that, for some
\theta>0

(8)

\|f\|_{L^{5}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim T^{\theta}\big(\|f\|_{L^{5}_{x}L^{10}_{T}}+\|D^{1/4}_{x}f\|_{L^{5}_{x}L^{10}_{T}}\big).

Let’s introduce the commutator

[\phi,D_{x}^{\alpha}]f:=\phi D_{x}^{\alpha}f-D_{x}^{\alpha}(\phi f).

When
\alpha=1
, it corresponds to the so called first commutator of Calderón (see
[
4
]
).
Furthermore, it is known that
(see
[
24
, Lemma 2.2]
)

(9)

\|[\phi,D_{x}^{\alpha}]f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Now, we recall a chain rule for fractional derivatives
(
[
19
, Theorem A.7]
)

(10)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}F(f)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|D_{x}^{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{p}_{x}},\quad 1<p<\infty

and its space-time analogous (
[
19
, Theorem A.6]
)

(11)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}F(f)\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}(f)\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{T}}\|D_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{T}}

for exponents
1<p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{2}<\infty

and
1<q_{1}\leq\infty

satisfying

(12)

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}.

We finish this review with some fractional versions of the Leibniz rule. For exponents as in (
12
) one has that
(see
[
19
, Theorem A.8]
)

(13)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}(fg)-fD_{x}^{\alpha}g-gD_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{t}}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{t}}\|D_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{t}}

provided that
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}

with
0\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha
.
Moreover, the more delicate estimate

(14)

\|D^{\alpha}_{x}(fg)-gD^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|Q_{N}D^{\alpha}_{x}g\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}\ell^{1}_{N}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

was obtained in
[
25
, Lemma 4.2]
,
where
Q_{N}
represents the multiplier operator

\widehat{Q_{N}f}(x):=\big(\eta(2^{-N}x)+\eta(-2^{-N}x)\big)\widehat{f}(x),\quad N\in\mathbb{Z}

and
\eta
is a smooth function supported in
[1/2,2]
that verifies

(15)

\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(\eta(2^{-N}x)+\eta(-2^{-N}x)\big)=1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Here, as usual, we understand

\|Q_{N}D^{\alpha}_{x}g\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}\ell^{1}_{N}}:=\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}|Q_{N}D^{\alpha}_{x}g(x)|.

3.
Crucial estimate

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
we will often use property (
14
) to control the
L^{2}
-norm of fractional derivatives acting on the product of functions, in particular repeatedly for

g(\xi):=\frac{e^{it(a\xi^{2}+b\xi^{3})}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{2m}}.

Therefore, it becomes necessary to understand the behaviour of

\|Q_{N}D^{m}_{\xi}g\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}\ell^{1}_{N}}
, which is the purpose of this section
(see Corollary
3.2
below).
Furthermore, given the definition of
Q_{N}
, we will show that it is enough to estimate oscillatory integrals of type

(16)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}dz,

where
\varphi
denotes a smooth function with compact Fourier support away from the origin and

(17)

\varphi_{\omega}(z):=\omega\varphi(\omega z),\quad\omega\neq 0.

Inspired by
[
25
, p. 842]

(where the particular case of
a=0
and
b=1
was considered), we say that a point
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
is
near
if

(18)

\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{100}\frac{\omega}{|b|t}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2},

intermediate
when

(19)

\frac{1}{100}\frac{\omega}{|b|t}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}<\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}\Big|^{2}\leq 100\frac{\omega}{|b|t}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2},

and

far
provide that

(20)

\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}\Big|^{2}>100\frac{\omega}{|b|t}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}.

We analyze now the behaviour of (
16
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
t>0
and
\omega>1
verifying that

(21)

\frac{w}{|b|t}\geq\max\Big\{1,10^{4}\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}\Big\}.

Moreover, assume that
0\leq m<1
and

\varphi
is a smooth function with

\supp\widehat{\varphi}\subset[1/2,2]
.
Then

(22)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}dz\Big|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1+t)\,\omega^{-m},&\text{for}\,\,\xi\,\,\text{intermediate},\\
(1+t)\,\omega^{-1},&\text{for}\,\,\xi\,\,\text{near or far}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We start recalling some useful properties of the function
\varphi_{\omega}
.
In
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
, it was shown that it is an entire function satisfying,
for
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
and
\xi\neq x
,

(23)

|\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)|\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{|e^{2\omega y}-e^{\omega y/2}|}{\omega^{2}y|\xi-z|^{2}},&y\neq 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{1}{\omega(\xi-x)^{2}},&y=0.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, as in
[
25
, Corollary 3.1]
, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
one gets that

\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}

is analytic on

\mathbb{C}\setminus(\{|\textrm{Im}\,z|\geq 1\}\cap\{\textrm{Re}\,z=0\}
). The following estimate
will also be very helpful (see
[
25
, Lemma 3.2]
)

(24)

\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{e^{\beta\sin\theta}-e^{\alpha\sin\theta}}{\sin\theta}\,d\theta\lesssim\Big(\pi\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1\Big)+1+\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\beta}{\alpha}e^{-\pi\alpha/\beta},\quad\alpha<\beta<0.

For
\varepsilon>0
, conveniently chosen later, and taking into account (
23
), we readily deduce

(25)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[\xi-\varepsilon,\xi+\varepsilon]}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}dz\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[\xi-\varepsilon,\xi+\varepsilon]}\frac{1}{\omega(\xi-x)^{2}}dx\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega\varepsilon}.

It remains to analyze the integral

I:=\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}\,dz

for which we distinguish the situations of
\xi
intermediate, near or far.

Intermediate case
.
In this situation it is, for example, enough to fix
\varepsilon

such that

100\varepsilon^{2}:=\frac{1}{100}\frac{\omega}{|b|t}.

Hence,

(26)

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\||z|^{-2m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\xi-\varepsilon,\xi+\varepsilon)}\lesssim(|\xi|-\varepsilon)^{-2m}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2m}\lesssim t^{m}\omega^{-m},

because by the reverse triangle inequality,
(
19
) and (
21
) we have that

\displaystyle|\xi|-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}-\frac{a}{2b}\Big|-\varepsilon\geq\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}\Big|-\frac{|a|}{2|b|}-\varepsilon>8\varepsilon.

Therefore, combining (
25
) and (
26
) we deduce

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}dz\Big|\lesssim\omega^{-1}+t^{m}\omega^{-m}\lesssim(1+t)\omega^{-m},

as we wanted.

Near case
.
Notice that, as long as
\varepsilon<1
, the Cauchy-Goursat theorem guarantees that

(27)

I=\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}dz,

where the latter integral is taken along the contour

\Gamma_{1}:=\{z=\xi+\varepsilon e^{i\theta},\,-\pi\leq\theta\leq 0\}.

Furthermore, (
23
) implies

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}\,dz\Big|

(28)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi}\dfrac{|e^{-2\omega\varepsilon\sin\theta}-e^{-\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{2}\sin\theta}|}{\omega^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sin\theta}\exp\Big\{\textrm{Re}\Big[it\Big(a(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{2}+b(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{3}\Big)\Big]\Big\}\,d\theta.

For convenience, let’s introduce the notation

\xi_{a,b}:=\xi+a/(2b)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\textrm{Re}\Big[it\Big(a(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{2}+b(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{3}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=tb\varepsilon\sin\theta\Big[3\xi^{2}+\Big(\frac{2a}{b}+6\varepsilon\cos\theta\Big)\xi+\frac{2a}{b}\varepsilon\cos\theta+4\varepsilon^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-\varepsilon^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=tb\varepsilon\sin\theta\Big[2\Big(\xi_{a,b}+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\cos\theta\Big)^{2}+\Big(\xi_{a,b}-\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}-\varepsilon\cos\theta\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-3\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-\varepsilon^{2}\Big].

Thus, taking for example
\varepsilon:=10^{-1}
, by using
(
18
) and (
21
) we can estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\textrm{Re}\Big[it\Big(a(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{2}+b(\xi+\varepsilon e^{-i\theta})^{3}\Big)\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq t|b|\varepsilon\sin\theta\Big[2\Big(|\xi_{a,b}|+\frac{3}{2}10^{-1}\Big)^{2}+\Big(|\xi_{a,b}|+\frac{|a|}{2|b|}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{|a|}{2|b|}+10^{-1}\Big)^{2}+3\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}+\frac{5}{2}10^{-2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq t|b|\varepsilon\sin\theta\Big[4\Big(|\xi_{a,b}|^{2}+\frac{9}{4}10^{-2}\Big)+2\Big(|\xi_{a,b}|^{2}+\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}\Big)+2\Big(\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}+10^{-2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+3\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}+\frac{5}{2}10^{-2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq t|b|\varepsilon\sin\theta\Big[6|\xi_{a,b}|^{2}+7\Big(\frac{a}{2b}\Big)^{2}+14\cdot 10^{-2}\Big]\leq\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{4}\sin\theta.

Inserting the above bound in (
3
) and applying the property (
24
) we get

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi-z)\frac{e^{it(az^{2}+bz^{3})}}{\langle z\rangle^{2m}}\,dz\Big|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\pi}\dfrac{|e^{-2\omega\varepsilon\sin\theta}-e^{-\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{2}\sin\theta}|}{\omega^{2}\sin\theta}e^{\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{4}\sin\theta}\,d\theta

(29)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\dfrac{e^{-\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{4}\sin\theta}-e^{-\frac{7\omega\varepsilon}{4}\sin\theta}}{\sin\theta}\,d\theta\lesssim\omega^{-2}.

In conclusion, since
\omega>1
, putting together
(
25
), (
27
) and
(
3
)
we obtain (
22
) for
\xi
near.

Far case
.
This analysis is slightly different depending on whether
b
is a positive or a negative number.
Take now

\varepsilon^{2}:=\omega/(|b|t).

By the assumptions
(
20
) and (
21
)
notice that

|\xi\pm\varepsilon|\geq\Big|\xi+\frac{a}{2b}\Big|-\Big|\frac{a}{2b}\Big|-\varepsilon>(10-10^{-2}-1)\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{|b|t}}>8.

Hence, as in (
27
), applying again the
Cauchy-Goursat theorem we write for
b<0

Rigid analytic Stein algebraic groups are affine
Marco Maculan

Abstract
. Let K K be a complete non-trivially valued non-Archimedean field. Given an algebraic group over K K on which every regular function is constant, any rigid analytic function is shown to be constant too. It follows that an algebraic group over K K is affine if and only if the associated K K -analytic space is Stein; that is, rigid analytic embeddings of it in an affine space may always be chosen to be given by algebraic functions. Arguably curiously, the corresponding statement over the complex numbers is false.

, a necessarily unique

such that

. Invoking faithfully flat descent
[

, Théorème 3.17]
this is equivalent to proving the equality

f\circ\pr_{1}=f\circ\pr_{2}

of

-analytic functions on

, where

are the projections. Note that

is properly surjective in Daylies’ terminology because it is smooth and surjective, thus faithfully flat and without boundary; see
op.cit.
§1.3. It suffices to check (

) set-theoretically because

is reduced, the morphism

being smooth and

being reduced. For
s\in S
and check the wanted identity on the fiber

. Each

-analytic function on the

-analytic space

is constant by hypothesis. Therefore

as desired.
∎

Lemma 1.7
.

Let

be a finite

-analytic space whose underlying topological space is a singleton
\{s\}
and

a morphism between flat

-analytic spaces. If

is Stein in the sense of

and the natural map

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The
K
-analytic space

is the Banach spectrum of finite local
K
-algebra
A
. The ideal of nilpotent elements of

coincides with its maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. For

consider the closed subspace

defined by the ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}
and

\pi_{i}\colon Y_{i}:=Y\times_{S}S_{i}\longrightarrow X_{i}:=X\times_{S}S_{i}

the morphism induced by base-change. With this notation
S_{0}=\{s\}
with its reduced structure and
\pi_{0}=\pi_{s}
. For

, consider the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{i-1}}\longrightarrow 0

of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. The flatness of

, thus

\mathfrak{m}^{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{O}_{X}=(\mathfrak{m}^{i}\otimes_{A}K^{\prime})\otimes_{K^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}

is the residue field at

. Taking global sections of the above short exact sequence yields the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E_{i}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}\Gamma(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}})\longrightarrow\Gamma(X_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}})\longrightarrow\Gamma(X_{i-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i-1}})

where

. Moreover since

. The same arguments give an analogous exact sequence for

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{i}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}\Gamma(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma(X_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}

Proof of

.

Let
G_{\textup{ant}}
be the kernel of
\pi_{G}
. Suppose first that

\pi_{G}\colon G\to G_{\textup{aff}}

, thus

1.4

Suppose that

is a smooth subgroup of

(this is always the case if

through a surjective morphism

Corollary 1.8
.

An algebraic group

over

is Stein if and only if it is affine.

Proof.

By

. If

. Thus

is an isomorphism being smooth and surjective. In particular, if

Corollary 1.9
.

2.
Finite vector bundles on an abelian scheme

Let
S
be a scheme,
\alpha\colon A\to S
an abelian scheme and
e\colon S\to A
the zero section.

2.1.
Constant and unipotent bundles

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a faithfully flat morphism of schemes such that
\mathcal{O}_{S}\to f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is an isomorphism.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector bundle
V
on
X
is

(1)

constant (over
S
)
if it is isomorphic to
f^{\ast}E
for some vector bundle
E
on
S
;

(2)

unipotent
if there is an increasing filtration
0=V_{0}\subsetneq V_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V_{d}=V
of
V
by
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules such that, for
i=1,\dots,d
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is constant over
S
.

Note that the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
V_{i}
are automatically locally free. The direct sum, tensor product, internal homs, symmetric and exterior powers of constant (resp. unipotent) vector bundles are constant (resp. unipotent).

Remark 2.2
.

A vector bundle
V
on
X
is constant if and only if
f^{\ast}f_{\ast}V\to V
is an isomorphism.
In particular, for a constant vector bundle
V
, the
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module
f_{\ast}V
is a vector bundle.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
p\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be a finite and locally free morphism of schemes and
q\colon X^{\prime}=X\times_{S}S^{\prime}\to X
the second projection. For a vector bundle
V
on
X^{\prime}
constant over
S^{\prime}
, the vector bundle
q_{\ast}V
on
X
is constant over
S
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
V
be a unipotent vector bundle on
X
and
F
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module such that
f_{\ast}F
vanishes. Then
f_{\ast}(F\otimes V)=0
.

Proof.

Consider a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
0\to V^{\prime}\to V\to V^{\prime\prime}\to 0
where
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
are vector bundles such that
f_{\ast}(F\otimes V^{\prime})
and
f_{\ast}(F\otimes V^{\prime\prime})
vanish. Then, the sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
0\to F\otimes V^{\prime}\to F\otimes V\to F\otimes V^{\prime\prime}\to 0
is exact and, by left exactness of the push-forward along
f
, the
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module
f_{\ast}V
vanish. By induction on the rank of
V
and by definition of a unipotent vector bundle, one reduces to the case
V=f^{\ast}E
for some vector bundle
E
on
S
. The projection formula then yields
f_{\ast}(F\otimes f^{\ast}E)=f_{\ast}F\otimes E=0
which concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Finite vector bundles

Definition 2.5
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be an integer. A vector bundle
V
on
A
is
m
-finite
if the vector bundle
[m]^{\ast}V
is constant,
i.e.
it is isomorphic to
\alpha^{\ast}E
for some vector bundle
E
on
S
. It is

if it is
n
-finite for some integer
n\geqslant 1
.

Remark 2.6
.

The direct sum, tensor product, internal homs, symmetric and exterior powers of (
m
-)finite vector bundles are (
m
-)finite.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be vector bundles on
S
and
V
an extension of
\alpha^{\ast}E
by
\alpha^{\ast}E^{\prime}
. Assume there is an integer
m\geqslant 1
vanishing on
S
. Then, the vector bundle
V
is
m
-finite and, if

is moreover affine, then
[m]^{\ast}V
is the trivial extension.

Proof.

Condition (2) in
Remark

2.2
is local on the base, hence the scheme

may be assumed to be affine. The pull-back by
[m]
acts on the cohomology group

\textup{H}^{1}(A,\cHom(\alpha^{\ast}E,\alpha^{\ast}E^{\prime}))=\textup{H}^{0}(S,\textup{R}^{1}\alpha_{\ast}\cHom(\alpha^{\ast}E,\alpha^{\ast}E^{\prime}))

as the multiplication by the integer

. Since

on

, the vector bundle
[m]^{\ast}V
is isomorphic to the trivial extension of
\alpha^{\ast}E
by
\alpha^{\ast}E^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume there is an integer
m\geqslant 1
vanishing
S
. Then, a unipotent vector bundle
V
on
A
of rank
r
is

-finite.

Proof.

Let
0=V_{0}\subsetneq V_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V_{d}=V
a filtration as in the definition of a unipotent vector bundle. The vector bundles
V_{i}
are themselves unipotent, thus, arguing by induction on the rank of

, one reduces to the case where

is an extension of constant vector bundles.

2.7
then permits to conclude.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

be an exact sequence of vector bundles on

with
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
finite. If some integer

vanishes on

, then

is finite.

Proof.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer such that the vector bundles
[n]^{\ast}V^{\prime}
and

are constant. Then
[n]^{\ast}V
is unipotent and
Lemma

2.8
permits to conclude.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be a finite and locally free morphism of schemes and

an
m
-finite vector bundle

for some integer

. Then the vector bundle

on

-finite, where

Let

and

temporarily denote the multiplication-by-

and

. By flat base change along the morphism

, the natural homomorphism of

2.3.
Universal cover and Tate group scheme

Let
p
be a prime number.

•

The
universal cover
of
A
is the projective limit of the projective system
([m]\colon A\to A)_{m\in\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}
, the partial order on
\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
being divisibility.

•

The
Tate group scheme

\textup{T}A
is the projective limit of the finite flat group
S
-schemes
A[m]
, for
m\in\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, the transition maps
A[n]\to A[m]
being multiplication by
\frac{n}{m}
whenever
m
divides
n
.

•

The
p
-adic universal cover of
A
is the projective limit of the projective system
([p]\colon A\to A)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with the usual order on
\mathbb{N}
.

•

The
p
-adic Tate group scheme
\textup{T}_{p}A
of
A
is the limit of the projective system of group
S
-schemes
(A[p^{i}],[p]\colon A[p^{i+1}]\to A[p^{i}])_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

The existence of such projective limits is granted by the finiteness of the transition maps (
[
Stacks
,
Lemma 01YX
]
). The canonical projections
\textup{T}A\to\textup{T}_{p}A
induce an isomorphism
\textup{T}A\cong\prod_{\ell\textup{ prime}}\textup{T}_{\ell}A
of group
S
-schemes, always treated as understood in what follows. Let
(T_{i},\tau_{ji}\colon T_{j}\to T_{i})_{i\in I}
be an inverse system of affine
S
-schemes,
T=\varprojlim_{i\in I}T_{i}
, and, for
i\in I
,
\pr_{i}\colon T\to T_{i}
the canonical projection.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
Stacks
,
Proposition 01ZC
]
)
.

Suppose
S
quasi-compact. Let
X
be a finitely presented quasi-compact
S
-scheme and
f\colon T\to X
a morphism of
S
-schemes. Then
f=f_{i}\circ\pr_{i}
for some
i\in I
and some morphism of

-schemes

.

Lemma 2.12

(
[
Stacks
,
Lemma 01YZ
]
)
.

For an

-scheme

,
T_{S^{\prime}}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}(T_{i})_{S^{\prime}}
.

In particular, the formation of the (

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

the be the projection onto the first factor. Then, the following sequence of group

-schemes is short exact:

Proof.

For an

-scheme

let

be the functor of points of

. By faithfully flat descent

is a fppf sheaf on the category of

-schemes. The statement means that the sequence

of fppf sheaves of abelian groups is short exact, where the arrow

is the multiplication by

∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that the scheme
S
is quasi-compact. Then, the functor

\left\{\textup{finite vector bundles on $A$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\textup{representations of $\textup{T}A$}\right\},\;V\longmapsto\rho_{V},

is an equivalence of categories. Furthermore,

(1)

it preserves direct sums, tensor products, internal homs, symmetric, exterior powers, and is compatible with arbitrary base change;

(2)

for an
m
-finite vector bundle
V
, the representation
\rho_{V}
factors through
A[m]
;

(3)

for a finite vector bundle
V
, the homomorphism
\alpha_{\ast}V\to e^{\ast}V
given by evaluation at
e
induces an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules

\alpha_{\ast}V\cong(e^{\ast}V)^{\textup{T}A}.

Proof.

Property (2) holds by design. By
Lemma

2.11
a representation of
\textup{T}A
factors through a representation of
A[m]
for some integer
m\geqslant 1
. Therefore it suffices to prove that, for each
m\geqslant 1
, the functor

\left\{\textup{$m$-finite vector bundles on $A$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\textup{representations of $A[m]$}\right\},\;V\longmapsto\rho_{V,m},

is an equivalence of categories satisfying properties (1) and (3). This is essentially faithfully flat descent for modules on the the fppf principal
A[m]
-bundle
[m]\colon A\to A
. Indeed it states that

is an equivalence of categories between quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{A}
-modules and quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{A}
-modules endowed with an

-linearization
[

, Theorem 4.46]
. When

is an

-finite vector bundle such a linearization can be read of from

is constant. The linearization on
e^{\ast}V
corresponds by definition to the representation
\rho_{V,m}
whence the result.
∎

3.
The representation of the canonical extension

Let
S
be a scheme,
\alpha\colon A\to S
an abelian scheme
e\colon S\to A
the zero section.

3.1.
The canonical extension

A
homogeneous line bundle
on
A
is a line bundle together with an isomorphism
\pr_{1}^{\ast}L\otimes\pr_{2}^{\ast}L\cong\mu^{\ast}L
where
\mu
is the law group on
A
. Homogenous line bundles are parameterized by the dual abelian scheme
\check{A}
and the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{L}
is the universal homogeneous line bundle on
A\times_{S}\check{A}
. Consider the Atiyah extension of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}

0\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{A\times\check{A}/A}\longrightarrow\At_{A\times\check{A}/A}(\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{A\times\check{A}}\to 0

where
A\times_{S}\check{A}
is seen as an abelian scheme over
A
via the first projection
p
. Pushing-forward the above short exact sequence along
p
yields a sequence

(
\mathcal{U}_{A}
)

0\longrightarrow\alpha^{\ast}\omega_{\check{A}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{A}:=p_{\ast}\At_{A\times\check{A}/A}(\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{A}\longrightarrow 0

which is still short exact by
[
Mac22
, Proposition 1.3 (2)]
, where
\omega_{\check{A}}
is the dual of the Lie algebra of
\check{A}
and
p_{\ast}\Omega^{1}_{A\times\check{A}/A}=\alpha^{\ast}\omega_{\check{A}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The unipotent bundle
\mathcal{U}_{A}
is called the
canonical extension
of
\mathcal{O}_{A}
.

It earns the name canonical because any other extension of
\mathcal{O}_{A}
by some constant vector bundle is obtained by push-out of the sequence
(\mathcal{U}_{A})

[
Mac22
, Theorem 1.12]
. Here it will crucial to have another description for
\mathcal{U}_{A}
. For, let
\check{A}_{1}
the first-order thickening of
\check{A}
along its zero section,
\iota\colon A\times\check{A}_{1}\to A\times_{S}\check{A}
the the closed immersion and
\pi\colon A\times_{S}\check{A}_{1}\to A
the first projection. Then
\pi_{\ast}\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{L}
sits in the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\alpha^{\ast}\omega_{\check{A}}\longrightarrow\pi_{\ast}\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{A}\longrightarrow 0

which is seen to be canonically isomorphism to
(\mathcal{U}_{A})

[
Mac22
, Remark 1.7]
. The main interest of the canonical extension is that the associated affine bundle

A^{\natural}:=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}_{A})\smallsetminus\mathbb{P}(\alpha^{\ast}\omega_{\check{A}})

parameterizes homogeneous line bundles on
\check{A}
endowed with a connection
[
Mac22
, Theorem 1.9]
. The tensor product of line bundles with connections endows
A^{\natural}
with the structure of a vector extension of
\check{A}
. As such it is canonically isomorphic to the universal vector extension of
\check{A}

[
Mac22
, Theorem 1.20]
and thus henceforth identified with it.

3.2.

Let

be a finite and locally free commutative group

-scheme. Consider the the

of

, namely the
S
-group scheme representing the functor

(\textup{Sch}/S)\longrightarrow(\textup{Groups}),\hskip 20.00003ptS^{\prime}\longmapsto\Hom_{S^{\prime}\textup{-groups}}(G_{S^{\prime}},\mathbb{G}_{m,S^{\prime}}).

Let

and and

-schemes

be its restriction to

\displaystyle\Res_{G_{1}^{\ast}/S}(G\times_{S}G_{1}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{T}_{G/S}\otimes\omega_{G^{\ast}}),

\displaystyle\Res_{G_{1}^{\ast}/S}(\mathbb{G}_{m,S}\times_{S}G_{1}^{\ast})

.

The

of

Concretely for an

and

):

Proposition 3.3

Let

be a morphism of group

-schemes for a vector bundle

on

.

is acyclic and, given two distinct
K^{\prime}
-points
x
,
x^{\prime}
of
X
, there is a
K^{\prime}
-analytic function
f
on
X^{\prime}:=X^{\prime}_{1}\times X_{2}^{\prime}
such that
|f(x)|\neq|f(x^{\prime})|.
The vanishing of higher cohomology of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is granted by
op.cit.
, Theorem A.6. For two distinct
K^{\prime}
-points
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
x^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2})
, there is
i=1,2
for which
x_{i}\neq x^{\prime}_{i}
. Up to permuting the factors suppose
i=1
. Then, the
K^{\prime}
-analytic space
X_{1}^{\prime}
is Stein, the notion being insensible to scalar extensions, and thus holomorphically separable. Therefore, there is a
K^{\prime}
-analytic function
f
such that
|f(x_{1})|\neq|f(x^{\prime}_{1})|
. In the general case, according to
Theorem

A.3
, it suffices to exhibit a Stein exhaustion of

. Now, if
\{X_{i,a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Stein exhaustion of

, the preceding case implies that the collection

Theorem A.6
.

Let
X
be a
K
-analytic space without boundary, countable at infinity and such that

. Then

.

Proof.

If

is Stein, then the wanted embedding is given by

Conversely, the analytification of affine varieties are Stein spaces.

Proposition A.8
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a Stein
K
-analytic space
X
. Then the total space
\mathbb{V}(E)
is Stein.

Proof.

Write
V:=\mathbb{V}(E)
. When
Y
is compact, by the analogue of Cartan’s Theorem A in the current framework (
[
MP21
, Proposition 2.1]
), there exists an integer
N\geqslant 0
and an epimorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules
\varphi\colon\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{N}\to E^{\vee}
. This furnishes a closed immersion
j\colon V\to\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}
, proving that
V
is Stein. Before moving to the general case, remark that a Stein exhaustion on
V
is constructed as follows. For a real number
r>0
, set
V_{Y,\varphi,r}:=j^{-1}(D_{r})
where
D_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}:|t_{i}(x)|\leqslant r,i=1,\dots,N\}
is the disk of radius
r
in
\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{N}
are the coordinate functions on
\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}
. Then,

(1)

the
K
-analytic space
V_{Y,\varphi,r}
is a closed subspace of
D_{r}
thus Stein;

(2)

the restriction map
\Sym\Gamma(Y,E^{\vee})\to\Gamma(V_{Y,\varphi,r},\mathcal{O}_{V})
has a dense image.

To see (2) notice that the polynomial
A
-algebra
A[t_{1},\dots,t_{N}]
is dense in

\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}})=A\{t_{1}/r,\dots,t_{N}/r\},

the restriction map
\rho\colon\Gamma(D_{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}})\to\Gamma(V_{Y,\varphi,r},\mathcal{O}_{V})
is surjective because
\mathbb{A}^{N}_{Y}
is Stein, and the image of
A[t_{1},\dots,t_{N}]
via
\rho
coincides with that of
\Sym\Gamma(Y,E^{\vee})
. The sought-for Stein exhaustion is for instance
\{V_{Y,\varphi,r_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
(r_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing unbounded sequence of positive real numbers.

Now turn to the general case. Let
\{Y_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Stein exhaustion of
Y
. Then, by
[
MP21
, Proposition 5.1]
(itself based on
[
Kie67
, Satz 2.4]
), for a non-negative integer
\alpha\geqslant 0
, the restriction map
\Gamma(Y,E^{\vee})\to\Gamma(Y_{\alpha},E^{\vee})
has a dense image. In particular, for an epimorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\alpha}}
-modules
\varphi\colon\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\alpha}}^{N}\to E^{\vee}_{\rvert Y_{\alpha}}
and
r>0
, the restriction map
\Sym\Gamma(Y,E^{\vee})\to\Gamma(V_{Y_{\alpha},\varphi,r},\mathcal{O}_{V})

has a dense image, where the notation above has been adopted. Construct inductively as follows a Stein exhaustion
\{V_{\alpha}\}
for
V
where each
V_{\alpha}
is of the form
V_{Y_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}}
for some
r_{\alpha}>0
and a surjective homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\alpha}}
-modules
\varphi_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{O}_{Y_{\alpha}}^{N_{\alpha}}\to E_{\rvert Y_{\alpha}}
. For
\alpha=0
, the positive real number
r_{0}
and the epimorphism
\varphi_{0}
can be picked freely. For
\alpha\geqslant 1
, fix
\varphi_{\alpha}
arbitrarily and choose a real number
\rho_{\alpha}>1
so that the compact analytic domain
V_{Y_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha}}
contains
V_{\alpha-1}
; finally, set
r_{\alpha}=2\rho_{\alpha}
. The
K
-analytic space
V_{\alpha}
is Stein and, by the arguments above, the image of the restriction map
\Gamma(V_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{V})\to\Gamma(V_{\alpha-1},\mathcal{O}_{V})

is dense, as it contains
\Sym\Gamma(Y,E^{\vee})
. This shows that the so-obtained collection of compact analytic domains
\{V_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Stein exhaustion of
V
, concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition A.9
.

Let
X
be a
K
-analytic space without boundary and countable at infinity. Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
and
P
its total space deprived of it zero section. Then
X
is Stein if and only if
P
is.

Proof.

Suppose that
P
is Stein. By definition, showing that
X
is Stein amounts to proving the vanishing of higher coherent cohomology on
X\times_{K}K^{\prime}
for any given valued field extension
K^{\prime}
of
K
. Up to extending scalars, one reduces to the case
K=K^{\prime}
. Now, for a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
F
and an integer
q\geqslant 1
, the cohomology group
\textup{H}^{q}(P,p^{\ast}F)
vanishes, thus
\textup{H}^{q}(X,F)=0
by
Proposition

7.8
(2).

Suppose that
X
is Stein. Consider the duality pairing
\beta\colon\mathbb{V}(L)\times_{X}\mathbb{V}(L^{\vee})\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
and the ‘diagonal’ morphism
\delta\colon\mathbb{V}(L)^{\times}\to\mathbb{V}(L)^{\times}\times_{X}\mathbb{V}(L^{\vee})^{\times}
sending a trivialization
s
to
(s,\check{s})
where
\check{s}
is the unique linear form on
L
whose value on
s
is
1
. The map
\delta
induces an isomorphism of
P
with the fiber at
1
of
\beta
.
Proposition

A.4
implies that the
K
-analytic space
\mathbb{V}(L)\times_{X}\mathbb{V}(L^{\vee})
is Stein, thus
P=\beta^{-1}(1)
is Stein too.
∎

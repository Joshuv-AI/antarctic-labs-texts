Abstract
. In [ 22 ] Milnor proved that a real analytic map f : ( ℝ n , 0 ) → ( ℝ p , 0 ) f\colon(\mathbbm{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0) , where n ≥ p n\geq p , with an isolated critical point at the origin has a fibration on the tube f | : 𝔹 ε n ∩ f − 1 ​ ( 𝕊 δ p − 1 ) → 𝕊 δ p − 1 f|\colon\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1})\to\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1} . Constructing a vector field such that, (1) it is transverse to the spheres, and (2) it is transverse to the tubes, he “inflates” the tube to the sphere, to get a fibration φ : 𝕊 ε n − 1 ∖ f − 1 ​ ( 0 ) → 𝕊 p − 1 \varphi\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus f^{-1}(0)\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1} , but the projection is not necessarily given by f / ‖ f ‖ f/\|f\| as in the complex case. In the case f f has isolated critical value, in [ 9 ] it was proved that if the fibres inside a small tube are transverse to the sphere 𝕊 ε \mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon} , then it has a fibration on the tube. Also in [ 9 ] , the concept of d d -regularity was defined, it turns out that f f is d d -regular if and only if the map f / ‖ f ‖ : 𝕊 ε n − 1 ∖ f − 1 ​ ( 0 ) → 𝕊 p − 1 f/\|f\|\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus f^{-1}(0)\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1} is a fibre bundle equivalent to the one on the tube. In this article, we prove the corresponding facts in a more general setting: if a locally surjective map f f has a linear discriminant Δ \Delta and a fibration on the tube f | : 𝔹 ε n ∩ f − 1 ​ ( 𝕊 δ p − 1 ∖ Δ ) → 𝕊 δ p − 1 ∖ Δ f|\colon\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\Delta)\to\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\Delta , then f f is d d -regular if and only if the map f / ‖ f ‖ : 𝕊 ε n − 1 ∖ f − 1 ​ ( Δ ) → 𝕊 p − 1 ∖ 𝒜 f/\|f\|\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus f^{-1}(\Delta)\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}\setminus\mathcal{A} (with 𝒜 \mathcal{A} the radial projection of Δ \Delta on 𝕊 p − 1 \mathbbm{S}^{p-1} ) is a fibre bundle equivalent to the one on the tube. We do this by constructing a vector field w ~ \tilde{w} which inflates the tube to the sphere in a controlled way, it satisfies properties analogous to the vector field constructed by Milnor in the complex setting: besides satisfying (1) and (2) above, it also satisfies that f / ‖ f ‖ f/\|f\| is constant on the integral curves of w ~ \tilde{w} . 1.
Introduction

Milnor Fibration Theorem is an important result in singularity theory. It is about the topology of the fibres of holomorphic functions near its critical points. To each singular point of a complex
hypersurface it associates a fibre bundle, known as the Milnor Fibration. For an overview of its origin, generalizations and connections with other branches of mathematics we recommend
the recent survey article by Seade
[
30
]
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbbm{C}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{C},0)
be a holomorphic map with a critical point at the origin
\underline{0}\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}
. The
Milnor Fibration

[
22
, Theorem 4.8]
is given by

\phi:=\frac{f}{|f|}:\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\setminus K\longrightarrow\mathbbm{S}^{1}\,,

(1.1)

where
K
is the
link
of
f
at
\underline{0}
, that is
K=f^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}
with
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}
being a sufficiently small sphere around
\underline{0}
.

There is a second fibre bundle called the
Milnor-Lê Fibration
; this is a fibre bundle on a
Milnor tube

N(\varepsilon,\delta)=\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}\cap f^{-1}(\partial\mathbbm{D}_{\delta})
where
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}
is the ball bounded by
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}
,
\partial\mathbbm{D}_{\delta}
is the circle bounding the disc
\mathbbm{D}_{\delta}

of radius
\delta
centred at the origin of
\mathbbm{C}
. When
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon
the map
f
induces a fibration

f\colon N(\varepsilon,\delta)\rightarrow\partial\mathbbm{D}_{\delta}.

(1.2)

Its existence was proved by Milnor
[
22
, Theorem 11.2]
for the case of isolated singularity and by Lê in
[
16
, Theorem (1.1)]
for the general case, both using Ehresmann Fibration Theorem
for manifolds with boundary.

Fibrations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are equivalent, this is proved using a smooth vector field on
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}\setminus f^{-1}(0)
constructed by Milnor
[
22
, Lemma 5.9]

for an arbitrary holomorphic map
f\colon(\mathbbm{C}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{C},0)
with the following properties:

(1)

It is transverse to the spheres centred at the origin contained in
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}
,

(2)

It is transverse to the Milnor tubes,

(3)

Given an integral curve
p(t)
of such vector field
\frac{f(p(t))}{\lVert f(p(t))\rVert}
is constant.

With this vector field the Milnor tube can be “inflated” to the sphere, i. e., following the flow of the vector field one gets a diffeomorphism between the Milnor tube and the complement on the sphere of a
neighbourhood of the link.

Milnor also proved a Fibration Theorem for real singularities
[
21
, Theorem 2]
or
[
22
, Theorem 11.2]
. Let
n\geq p
and consider a real analytic map

f\colon(\mathbbm{R}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
with an isolated critical point at
\underline{0}
. Let
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
be a ball of radius
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and, as before, let
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon
.
First, Milnor proved that there is a fibration on the Milnor tube
N(\varepsilon,\delta)=\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1})

f\colon N(\varepsilon,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1},

(1.3)

where
\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}
is the sphere of radius
\delta
centred at the origin of
\mathbbm{R}^{p}
.
Then he proved that on the sphere
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}=\partial\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
one has a fibre bundle

\varphi\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus K\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}

(1.4)

where
K=f^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
is the link. This was done constructing a vector field on
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that:

(1)

It is transverse to the spheres centred at the origin contained in
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
,

(2)

It is transverse to the Milnor tubes,

and “inflating” the Milnor tube to the sphere. Milnor pointed out that his real Fibration Theorem has some weaknesses: the condition that
f
has an isolated critical point is very restrictive, and
since the vector field to “inflate” the tube to the sphere does not satisfy property (
3
) as in the complex case, the projection map
\varphi
is not necessarily given by
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
.
In fact, in
[
22
, page 99]
Milnor gives an example of a map whose fibration on the sphere does not have projection
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
.

From these remarks two natural questions arose:

(i)

When does the fibration on the sphere have projection given by
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
?

(ii)

Is it possible to relax the condition that
f
has isolated critical point and still have fibrations on the tube and on the sphere?

Question (
i
) for
f
with isolated critical point was studied by several authors
[
13
,
14
,
29
,
27
,
26
,
1
]
.
In
[
2
]
dos Santos considered the following question

(iii)

If the fibration on the tube and the fibration on the sphere given by
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
both exist, are they equivalent?

where he answered it positively for
p=2
and
f
quasi-homogeneous.

For question (
ii
), the natural generalization was to consider real analytic maps
f\colon(\mathbbm{R}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
with an
isolated critical value
, as in the case of holomorphic functions.
There are several works in this direction
[
24
,
9
,
10
,
19
,
3
]
.
To have the fibration (
1.3
) on the tube in a ball
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
of radius
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, it is necessary that
f
has the
transversality property
:
there exists a
solid Milnor tube

\widehat{N}(\varepsilon,\delta)=\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p})
, with
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon
, such that all the fibres in the tube are transverse to
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}
, to be able to apply
Ehresmann Fibration Theorem for manifolds with boundary. Examples of maps with the transversality property are maps with the Thom
a_{f}
property
[
9
, Proposition 5.1, Remark 5.7]
.
Having the fibration on the tube, one can use Milnor’s vector field to inflate the tube to the sphere to get a fibration on the sphere, but again, not necessarily with projection
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
.
The reason why the fibration on the sphere of Milnor’s example
[
22
, page 99]
does not have projection
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
is because the map

\phi:=\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus K\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}

(1.5)

is not a submersion, which is a necessary condition to be a smooth fibre bundle. In
[
9
, Definition 2.4]

the concept of
d
-regularity
was introduced (see Section
2
below).
In
[
9
, Proposition 3.2-(4)]
it was proved that the map
f
is
d
-regular if and only if the map (
1.5
) is a submersion,
so
d
-regularity is a necessary condition for (
1.5
) to be a fibre bundle. On the other hand,
[
9
, Lemma 5.2]
states that
f
is
d
-regular
if and only if there exists a vector field on
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\setminus f^{-1}(0)
which satisfies properties (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) above, which implies that “inflating”
the tube to the sphere we have that (
1.5
) is a fibre bundle which is equivalent to the fibre bundle (
1.3
), so
d
-regularity is a necessary and sufficient condition for (
1.5
) to be
a fibre bundle. Thus, the existence of such vector field answers questions (
i
) and (
iii
) for
f\colon(\mathbbm{R}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
with isolated critical value,
satisfying the transversality property.

However, Brodersen
[
11
]
and Hansen
[
23
]
pointed out that the argument of the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 5.2]
is not complete,
and Hansen in
[
23
]
gives sufficient conditions for the existence of such vector field.
Since then, other authors
[
25
,
5
,
28
]
have also given sufficient conditions.

In this article we prove of the existence of a vector field satisfying properties (
1
),
(
2
) and (
3
) if and only if
f
is
d
-regular in the more general setting of real analytic maps with linear discriminant considered in
[
8
]
.
Let
f\colon(\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n},0)\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
be a real analytic map with linear discriminant
\Delta
, i. e.,
\Delta
is a union of line segments
(see Section
5
for the precise definition). In order to simplify notation, suppose that
f
is locally surjective (see Remark
1.1
below).

Suppose that
f
satisfies the transversality property, then there exists
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that the restriction of
f
to the tube

f|\colon\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\delta})\to(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\delta})

(1.6)

is a fibre bundle where
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}:=\Delta\cap\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}_{\delta}
(see
[
8
, Theorem 2.7]
).

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.9]
it is also proved that if
f
is
d
-regular then one has a fibre bundle

f/\|f\|\colon(\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus f^{-1}(\Delta))\to(\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}\setminus\mathcal{A})

(1.7)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the radial projection of
\mathcal{A}_{\delta}
on
\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
.

The aim of the present article is to give a construction of a vector field with properties (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) which works in any open set of
\mathbbm{R}^{n}

where the maps
f
and
f/\|f\|
are submersions. Applying this construction to maps
f
with isolated critical value we get a proof of
[
9
, Lemma 5.2]

which follows the idea of the original (incomplete) proof. Also this construction of the vector field allows us to prove the equivalence of fibrations (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) for
real analytic maps with arbitrary linear discriminant.

Remark 1.1
.

Throughout this paper, we will assume that
f
is
locally surjective
, that is, the image of
f
contains an open neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathbbm{R}^{p}
,
and we shall not mention it all the time. Nevertheless, it is easy to see that in the general case the same results
hold if one intersects the bases of the locally trivial fibrations with their image. This choice is to avoid a heavy notation.

2.
The canonical pencil,
d
-regularity and the spherefication

Let
U
be an open neighbourhood of
\underline{0}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
,
n>p
, and
f\colon(U,\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
a non-constant analytic map
with a critical point at
\underline{0}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and
0\in\mathbbm{R}^{p}
is an isolated critical value. Equip
V=f^{-1}(0)
with a Whitney
stratification and let
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
be an open ball in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, centred at
\underline{0}
, of sufficiently small radius
\varepsilon
, so that every sphere
in this ball, centred at
\underline{0}
, meets transversely every stratum of
V
, if
V
is not an isolated point at the origin. Such a ball
exists by
[
22
, Corollary 2.9]
if
f
has an isolated critical point at
\underline{0}
, and by the Bertini-Sard theorem in
[
31
]
in general.

We define a family of real analytic spaces as follows. For each
\ell\in\mathbbm{RP}^{p-1}
, consider the line
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\subset\mathbbm{R}^{p}

passing through the origin corresponding to
\ell
and set

X_{\ell}=\{x\in U\,|\,f(x)\in\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\}\,.

If we let
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}
be the hyperplane orthogonal to
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
, let

\varpi_{\ell}\colon\mathbbm{R}^{p}\to\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}
be the orthogonal projection, and we set
h_{\ell}=\varpi_{\ell}\circ f\,
, then
X_{\ell}
is the vanishing set of
h_{\ell}
, which is real analytic.
Hence
\{X_{\ell}\}
is a family of real analytic varieties parameterized by
\mathbbm{RP}^{p-1}
.

Notice that the singular points of
X_{\ell}
are contained in
V
. Hence each

X_{\ell}\setminus V
is either a smooth submanifold of
U\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}
of
dimension
n-p+1
or empty. The union of all the
X_{\ell}
’s is
all of
U
and one has
V=\cap X_{\ell}=X_{\ell_{1}}\cap X_{\ell_{2}}
for each pair
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We notice that each
X_{\ell}
is naturally the union of three sets: the points

x\in U
such that
f(x)=0
,
i.e.
,
x\in V
, and the points

x\in U
such that
f(x)
is in one of the two half lines of

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\setminus\{0\}
. Write this as:

X_{\ell}=E_{\ell}^{+}\cup V\cup E_{\ell}^{-}\,.

(2.1)

The family
\{X_{\ell}\,|\,\ell\in\mathbbm{RP}^{p-1}\}
is
the canonical pencil
of
f
.
Let
\rho
be a metric in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
induced by some positive definite quadratic form.
The map
f
is said to be
d
-regular at
\underline{0}
(with respect to the metric
\rho
)

if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that every sphere (for the metric
\rho
) of radius

\leq\varepsilon
centred at
\underline{0}
meets every
X_{\ell}\setminus V

transversely whenever the intersection is not empty. If the metric is fixed we just say that
f
is
d
-regular.

In this article we consider
\rho
as the Euclidean metric in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and we only consider
d
-regular maps with respect to this metric.

2.1.
The spherefication

Consider the maps
\Phi\colon U\setminus V\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
and

\;\mathfrak{F}\colon U\setminus V\to\mathbbm{R}^{p}\setminus\{0\}
defined by

\Phi(x)=\frac{f(x)}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{F}(x)=\lVert x\rVert\Phi(x).

(2.2)

For each
\ell\in\mathbbm{RP}^{p-1}
one has that the line
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}

in
\mathbbm{R}^{p}
meets the sphere
\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
in two antipodal points, say

\pm y_{\ell}
, and one has:

\Phi^{-1}(y_{\ell})=E_{\ell}^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi^{-1}(-y_{\ell})=E_{\ell}^{-},

where
E_{\ell}^{+}
(resp.
E_{\ell}^{-}
) is the inverse image under
f
of one of the two half lines in
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\setminus\{0\}
.

Notice that given
y\in\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbbm{R}^{p}\setminus\{0\}
, the fibre
\mathfrak{F}^{-1}(y)
is the
intersection of
E_{\ell}^{\pm}
with the sphere of radius
\lVert y\rVert

centred at
\underline{0}
. Hence,
\mathfrak{F}
carries spheres in

\mathbbm{R}^{n}
of radius
r>0
into spheres in
\mathbbm{R}^{p}
of the same radius,
and each
X_{\ell}
is also a union of fibres of
\mathfrak{F}
, just
as it is a union of fibres of
f
.
The analytic map
\mathfrak{F}
is called the
spherefication
of
f
.

As we mentioned in the Introduction, a map
f
is
d
-regular if and only if the map
\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}
is a submersion.
This is given by the following proposition together with other characterizations of
d
-regularity in terms of the spherefication.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
9
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n},\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
be a real analytic map with isolated
critical value at the origin. Set
V=f^{-1}(0)
and

K_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=V\cap\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{n-1}
. The following are equivalent:

(A)

The map
f
is
d
-regular at
0
.

(B)

For
each sphere
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{n-1}
in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
centred at
\underline{0}
of radius

{\varepsilon^{\prime}}<\varepsilon
, the restriction map
\mathfrak{F}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{n-1}\setminus V\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}_{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}
of
\mathfrak{F}
is
a submersion.

(C)

The spherefication map

\mathfrak{F}
is a submersion at each
x\in\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\setminus V
.

(D)

The map
\phi=\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}\colon\mathbbm{S}_{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}^{n-1}\setminus K_{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
is a submersion for every sphere

\mathbbm{S}_{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}^{n-1}
with
{\varepsilon^{\prime}}<\varepsilon
.

The following proposition relates the differential of the
spherefication map
\mathfrak{F}
and the differential of
f
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
x\in U\setminus V
and
v\in T_{x}(U\setminus V)
. Then

D\mathfrak{F}_{x}(v)=\frac{\langle x,v\rangle}{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\mathfrak{F}(x)-\frac{f(x)}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{2}}\langle\mathfrak{F}(x),Df_{x}(v)\rangle+\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}Df_{x}(v).

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\setminus V,

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in T_{x}(\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\setminus V),

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=(w_{1},\dots,w_{p})=D\mathfrak{F}_{x}(v),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=(u_{1},\dots,u_{p})=Df_{x}(v).

Using the quotient rule for the derivative we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\biggl(\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}\biggr)=\frac{x_{j}}{\lVert x\rVert\lVert f(x)\rVert}-\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{3}}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{p}f_{l}(x)\frac{\partial f_{l}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\biggr).

(2.3)

Write
f(x)=(f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{p}(x))
; by the definition of the
spherefication map (
2.2
) we have that

\mathfrak{F}(x)=\bigl(\mathfrak{F}_{1}(x),\dots,\mathfrak{F}_{p}(x)\bigr)=\biggl(\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}f_{1}(x),\dots,\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}f_{p}(x)\biggr).

Using (
2.3
) and the product rule we obtain

\frac{\partial\mathfrak{F}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{x_{j}f_{i}(x)}{\lVert x\rVert\lVert f(x)\rVert}-\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{3}}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{p}f_{l}(x)\frac{\partial f_{l}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\biggr)f_{i}(x)+\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}\frac{\partial f_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}.

Recall that

u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}v_{j}.

(2.4)

Then using (
2.4
) we have:

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\mathfrak{F}_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}v_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\frac{f_{i}(x)x_{j}v_{j}}{\lVert x\rVert\lVert f(x)\rVert}-\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{3}}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{p}f_{l}(x)\frac{\partial f_{l}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\biggr)f_{i}(x)v_{j}+\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}\frac{\partial f_{i}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}v_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{i}(x)}{\lVert x\rVert\lVert f(x)\rVert}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}v_{j}-\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{3}}f_{i}(x)\sum_{l=1}^{p}f_{l}(x)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{l}(x)}{\partial x_{j}}v_{j}+\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}u_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle x,v\rangle}{\lVert x\rVert\lVert f(x)\rVert}f_{i}(x)-\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert^{3}}f_{i}(x)\sum_{l=1}^{p}f_{l}(x)u_{l}+\frac{\lVert x\rVert}{\lVert f(x)\rVert}u_{i}

Lemma 3.1

4.
Equivalence of the fibration on tube and on the sphere

In this section, using Theorem
3.8
, we give for completeness the proof of the equivalence between the fibration on the tube and the fibration on the sphere for a real analytic map
with isolated critical value.

Let
U
be an open neighbourhood of
\underline{0}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
,
n>p
, and
f\colon(U,\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
a non-constant analytic map defined
on
U
with a critical point at
\underline{0}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and
0\in\mathbbm{R}^{p}
is an isolated critical value.
Also assume that
f
is locally surjective (see Remark
1.1
).
Let
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
be a closed ball in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, centred at
\underline{0}
, of sufficiently small radius
\varepsilon
, so that every sphere in this ball, centred at
\underline{0}
,
meets transversely every stratum of a Whitney stratification of
V
, and such that Proposition
3.5
holds.

We say that
f
satisfies the
transversality property
if there exists
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon
such that for every
y\in\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}
the fibre
f^{-1}(y)
meets
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
transversely. Since we are assuming
f
locally surjective we can take
\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\subset\Ima(f|_{\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}})
.

Remark 4.1
.

If we work with a germ
f\colon(U,\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
instead of with just a map, we need the following stronger
transversality property
.
We say that the germ
f
satisfies the
transversality property
if for any representative
f
there exists a ball
\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{n}
as above
such that for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
there exists a real number
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
such that
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon

and for every
y\in\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}
the fibre
f^{-1}(y)
meets
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
transversely.

Remark 4.2
.

In the case that
f
is not locally surjective, for the transversality property we need to ask that every
y\in\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}\cap\Ima(f|_{\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}})
the fibre
f^{-1}(y)
meets
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
transversely.
But if we consider that an empty fibre intersects transversely the sphere
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
we can state in general the transversality property as above.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
f
satisfies the transversality property, then the restriction of
f
to the Milnor tube

f\colon N(\varepsilon,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1},

(4.1)

is a smooth fibre bundle.

Proof.

Since
f
satisfies the transversality property we can apply Ehresmann Fibration Theorem for Manifolds with Boundary (see for instance
[
15
, p. 23]
).
∎

Consider the projection
\pi\colon\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
given by
\pi(y)=\frac{y}{\lVert y\rVert}
. We have that
\pi
is a trivial fibre bundle over
\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}
.
Let
\tilde{f}
be the composition of the fibre bundle (
4.1
) with
\pi
, since the restriction of
\pi
to
\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}
is a diffeomorphism, we get an equivalent fibre bundle

\tilde{f}\colon N(\varepsilon,\delta)\rightarrow\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}.

(4.2)

Notice that
\tilde{f}
is the restriction of the map
\Phi
given in (
2.2
) to the Milnor tube
N(\varepsilon,\delta)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
f\colon(U,\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
satisfies the transversality property. Then
f
is
d
-regular if and only if the map

\phi:=\frac{f}{\lVert f\rVert}\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus K\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}

(4.3)

is a smooth fibre bundle which is equivalent to the fibre bundle (
4.2
).

Proof.

Since
f
has the transversality property, by Proposition
4.3
we have the fibration (
4.2
) on the tube. If
f
is
d
-regular, by Theorem
3.8

there exists a vector field
\tilde{w}
satisfying properties (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
). The flow associated to
\tilde{w}
defines
a diffeomorphism
\tau
between the tube
N(\varepsilon,\delta)
and
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\setminus f^{-1}(\mathring{\mathbbm{B}}_{\delta})
, where
\mathring{\mathbbm{B}}_{\delta}
is the open ball in
\mathbbm{R}^{p}
centred at
0
of radius
\delta
,
“inflating” the tube to the sphere in the following way: for a point
x\in N(\varepsilon,\delta)
follow the integral curve of
\tilde{w}
which passes through
x

until it reaches
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
at some point
\hat{x}
, which exists and it is unique because
\tilde{w}
satisfies properties (
1
) and (
2
). Define
\tau(x)=\hat{x}
.
By property (
3
) the integral curves of
\tilde{w}

lie on the
E_{\ell}
, so we have that
\tilde{f}(x)=\Phi(x)=\Phi(\hat{x})=\phi(\hat{x})
. Hence the diffeomorphism
\tau
gives an equivalence of fibre bundles
\tilde{f}=\phi\circ\tau

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N(\varepsilon,\delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\setminus f^{-1}(\mathring{\mathbbm{B}}_{\delta})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

Now we extend the fibre bundle

\phi\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\setminus f^{-1}(\mathring{\mathbbm{B}}_{\delta})\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1}

(4.4)

to
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\setminus K
as follows. Since
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p})
is compact, the restriction of
f

f\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\setminus\{0\}

is a proper submersion, and by Ehresmann Fibration Theorem, it is a smooth fibre bundle. Composing it with
\pi
, we get a fibre bundle given by the restriction of
\phi

\phi|=\pi\circ f\colon\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbbm{S}^{p-1},

(4.5)

since the composition of two smooth fibre bundles is again a smooth fibre bundle (see
[
8
, Lemma A.1]
,
[

, Corollary 7]
or
[

]
).
We can glue the fibre bundles (

, to get the smooth fibre bundle (
4.3
).

On the other hand, if (

) is a smooth fibre bundle, then it is a submersion, and by Proposition
2.2

f
is
d
-regular.
∎

5.
Real analytic maps with linear discriminant

In this section, we see that Theorem

prove the equivalence of the fibration on the tube and the fibration on the sphere for real analytic maps with arbitrary linear discriminant (Theorem

This is done in Subsection

5.1.
Equivalence of fibrations for maps with arbitrary linear discriminant

We start recalling some definitions and results from
[
8
]
.
Let
f\colon(\mathbbm{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
be an analytic map with a critical point at
0

f
is locally surjective (see Remark
1.1
).
Equip
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with a Whitney stratification adapted to

\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{n}
be a closed ball in
\mathbbm{R}^{n}

\underline{0}

in this ball, centred at
\underline{0}

.
In what follows, for

Denote by

Remark 5.1
.

(i)

The discriminant
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
is a subanalitic set
and it may depend on the choice of the radius
\varepsilon
, as showed in
[
4
]

(ii)

The results of this article extend to map-germs

if we consider

in the class of
nice analytic map-germs
defined in
[
4
, Definition 2.2]

for which the discriminant is a well-defined set-germ at

\varepsilon

Let
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, we say that the ball

0<\delta\ll\varepsilon

is transverse to the sphere
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}
.
Since we are assuming

\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\subset\Ima(f_{\varepsilon})

Remark 5.2
.

If we work with a nice analytic map-germ
f\colon(U,\underline{0})\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)
(see Remark
5.1
), the discriminant does not depend on the radius
\varepsilon
and we just denote it by

.
In this case we need the following stronger
transversality property
.
We say that
f
satisfies the

if for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

there exists a real number
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
such that
0<\delta\ll\varepsilon
and for every

f^{-1}(y)
meets
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

Remark 5.3
.

In the case that
f
is not locally surjective for the transversality property we need to ask that every
y\in(\mathbbm{B}_{\delta}^{p}\setminus\Delta_{\varepsilon})\cap\Ima(f_{\varepsilon})

f^{-1}(y)
meets
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

But if we consider that an empty fibre intersects transversely the sphere
\mathbbm{S}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}
we can state in general the transversality property as above.

Suppose that
f
has the transversality property, then Proposition
4.3
generalizes so that the restriction

f_{\varepsilon}|\colon\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\cap f^{-1}(\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\Delta_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbbm{S}_{\delta}^{p-1}\setminus\Delta_{\varepsilon}

is a smooth fibre bundle

Let
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, we say that

has

if

is a union of line-segments with one endpoint at

.
We say that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathbbm{B}_{\eta}^{p}=\Cone\big(\Delta_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathbbm{S}_{\eta}^{p-1}\big)\,.

The case when

Let
f\colon(\mathbbm{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}^{n},0)\to(\mathbbm{R}^{p},0)

and consider a linearity radius

For each point

We say that

intersects the sphere

\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, for every

and for every

Remark 5.4

In the case that

Remark 5.5

Let

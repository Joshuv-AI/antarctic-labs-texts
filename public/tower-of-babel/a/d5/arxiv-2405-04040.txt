Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of all analytic functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}

for all

, we define
||f||_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|

Theorem A
.

(see
[

Let

2.
Linearly invariant family and refined Bohr inequality

In
1964
, Pommerenke (see
[
33
]
) introduced the concept of linearly invariant family. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of all analytic functions
g
defined on
\mathbb{D}
of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle g(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}(g)z^{n},\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, the set
\mathfrak{M}
of locally univalent functions
g\in\mathcal{A}
is called a linear invariant family, also known as LIF. A linear family is defined as a set where, for any functions
g\in\mathfrak{M}
and any conformal automorphism
\phi
of
\mathbb{D}
, the function
g_{\phi}
is defined by

\displaystyle g_{\phi}(z)=\frac{g(\phi(z))-g(\phi(0))}{g^{\prime}(\phi(0))\phi^{\prime}(0)}=z+\cdots

also belongs to
\mathfrak{M}
. The order of a linearly invariant family is the number
{\rm ord}\;\mathfrak{M}=\sup\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{M}}|a_{2}(g)|.
A universally linearly invariant family of order
\alpha
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
, is the union of all LIF
\mathfrak{M}
for which
{\rm ord}\;\mathfrak{M}\leq\alpha
.
It has been proven that for any LIF
\mathfrak{M}
, its order
{\rm ord}\;\mathfrak{M}\geq 1
(see
[
33
]
). Additionally,
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\mathcal{K}
represents the class of all convex analytic functions
g
in
\mathbb{D}
of the form (
2.1
), which map
\mathbb{D}
onto convex regions. The class of univalent functions
\mathcal{S}
is also an example of a linearly invariant family (LIF). According to Bieberbach’s theorem (see
[
6
]
), we know that the order of this family is
{\rm ord}\;\mathcal{S}=2
.

Several well-known classes of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
are considered to be linear invariant families. It seems that the properties of analytic and locally univalent functions are primarily influenced by the order of their families rather than by their geometric characteristics.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an LIF of finite order. We define the family
\mathfrak{LM}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{LM}:=\{f(z)=\log g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)z^{n}:g\in\mathfrak{M}\},

where the branch of logarithm of
g^{\prime}(z)
is chosen such that
\log g^{\prime}(0)=0
.

Let
B
be the class of analytic functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
, uniformly bounded on compact sets from
\mathbb{D}
,
i.e.
for every compact set
K
from
\mathbb{D}
there is a constant, namely
M(K)>0
such that for any
function
f\in B
one has
|f(z)|\leq M(K)
for
z\in K
.

In
[
38
]
, Ponnusamy
et al.
examined the Bohr radius for the class of linearly invariant family (LIF),
e.g.

\mathfrak{L}\mathcal{K}=\{\log f^{\prime}:f\in\mathcal{K}\}
(see
[
38
, Proposition 3]
) and proved the following lemma.

Lemma B
.

[
38
, Lemma 1]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)z^{n}\in B
. Denote

\displaystyle A_{n}=\sup\limits_{f\in B}|a_{n}(f)|\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;R_{*}=\sup\biggl\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}\leq 1\biggr\}.

Then
R_{*}\leq\rho(B)
. If
B
contains a function
F(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(F)z^{n}
for which all coefficients
a_{n}(F)\geq 0,\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|F(z)|>1
, then
r_{F}
is the only root of the equation
F(r)=1
on
(0,1),
then
\rho(B)\leq r_{F}.

Refining the Bohr inequality for certain class of functions and showing that the inequality sharp is interesting as well as difficult task. To continue the research on Bohr radius for LIF, a natural question thus arises as follows.

Question 1
.

Can we establish a refined version of Lemma B?

To answer the Question
1
, we need some definitions and notations. The following definition will help to prove our main results.

Definition 2.1
.

For an arbitrary class
\mathcal{M}
of analytic functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
with
|a_{0}|<1,
and
\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
be a real valued function, the refined Bohr radius of the class
\mathcal{M}
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{*}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{M}):=\sup\biggl\{\rho^{\lambda}=\rho^{\lambda}(\mathcal{M}):A^{\lambda}_{f}(|z|)\leq 1\;\mbox{for}\;f\in\mathcal{M},z\in\mathbb{D}_{\rho}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|<\rho\}\biggr\},

where

\displaystyle A^{\lambda}_{f}(|z|):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}|z|^{2n}.

Inspired by the article
[
38
]
, we establish a refined Bohr inequality for linearly invariant family (LIF), (
e.g.
for the families
\mathfrak{L}\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{S}
) by introducing a function
\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
. In view of Definition
2.1
, we first obtain the following lemma, which will help us later to establish a refined Bohr inequality for the families
\mathfrak{L}\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)z^{n}\in B
and
\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
be a real valued function with
A_{n}=\sup\limits_{f\in B}|a_{n}(f)|
and

\displaystyle R_{*}^{\lambda}=\sup\bigg\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}A_{n}^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1\bigg\}

Then
R_{*}^{\lambda}\leq\rho^{\lambda}(B)
. If
B
contains a function
F(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(F)z^{n}
for which all coefficients
a_{n}(F)\geq 0
,
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|F(z)|>1
and
r_{F}^{\lambda}
is the only root of the equation
G^{\lambda}_{F}(r)=1
on
(0,1)
, then
\rho^{\lambda}(B)\leq r^{\lambda}_{F}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

For each
f\in B
, we denote

\displaystyle r^{\lambda}_{f}=\sup\bigg\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1\bigg\}.

In view of Definition
2.1
, we see that
\rho^{\lambda}(B)=\inf\limits_{f\in B}r^{\lambda}_{f}
. Further, definition of
A_{n}
allows us to obtain the following inequalities

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}\;\mbox{and}\;\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}A_{n}^{2}r^{2n}\;\mbox{for all}\;f\in B.

Thus, combining these two inequalities, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|^{2}r^{2n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}A_{n}^{2}r^{2n}.

Consequently, for any
f\in B
, we see that

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}(f)|^{2}r^{2n}\bigg\}\geq\sup\bigg\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}A_{n}^{2}r^{2n}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\iff r^{\lambda}_{f}\geq R^{\lambda}_{*}.

Thus, it follows that
R_{*}^{\lambda}\leq\rho^{\lambda}(B)
. Since
\rho^{\lambda}(B)\leq\inf\limits_{f\in B}r^{\lambda}_{f}
, we have the following inequality for the function
F
from the formulation of the lemma

\displaystyle\rho^{\lambda}(B)\leq

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{r:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(F)r^{n}+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(a_{n}(F))^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigg\{r:F(r)+\lambda(r)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(a_{n}(F))^{2}r^{2n}=1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{r:G^{\lambda}_{F}(r)=1\}

\displaystyle=r^{\lambda}_{F}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We have the following remark in connection with the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Remark 2.1
.

There can be many functions
F(\not\equiv 0)
in the class
B
with non-negative coefficients and for each such
F
, the inequality
\rho^{\lambda}(B)\leq r^{\lambda}_{F}
holds true. However, it is worth observing that such an upper bound for the radius
\rho^{\lambda}(B)
becomes more accurate when the coefficient
a_{n}(F)
of the function
F
is larger. In particular, if
\lambda\equiv 0
, then similar observations as in
[
38
, Remark 1]
are also true.

If the function
F
realizes the maximum in this problem, then we get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

If the assumptions in Lemma
2.1
are satisfied, then the function
G^{\lambda}_{F}(z)
achieves its maximum value in the given problem, hence leading to
R^{\lambda}_{*}=r^{\lambda}_{F}
and
\rho^{\lambda}(B)
as the only solution to
G^{\lambda}_{F}(r)=1
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\lambda=0
, then we see that Corollary
2.1
reduces to
[
38
, Corollary 1]
.

2.1
to obtain a refined Bohr radius for the class

\acute{a}

and defined as

\displaystyle T[f](z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\bigg)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt,

where

is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. The Bohr radius for Ces
\acute{a}
ro operator and integral operators are obtained in recent study. For example, Kayumov
et al.
(see
[
19
]
) generalized the classical Bohr theorem introducing a sequence of non-negative function
\{\varphi_{k}(r)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and as an application, also they obtained a counterpart of Bohr theorem for the generalized
\alpha
-Ces
\acute{a}
ro operator
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\alpha}(r)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, with
{\rm Re}\;\alpha>-1
. Kumar and Sahoo (see
[
25
]
) have obtained the Bohr radii for certain complex integral operators and Bernardi operator (see also
[
31
, p. 11]
)

\displaystyle L\beta[f](z):=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n+\beta}z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}f(zt)t^{\beta-1}dt,\;\mbox{where}\;\beta>-m,

defined on a set of bounded analytic functions in the unit disk. Later, Kumar (see
[
22
]
) have generalized Bohr inequality established by Kayumov
et al.
(see
[
19
]
) for unit disk
\mathbb{D}
to the class of bounded analytic functions defined on the simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
. Allu and Ghosh (see
[
4
]
) studied the Bohr type inequality for Ces
\acute{a}
ro operator established by (see
[
20
]
) and Bernardi integral operator established by Kumar and Sahoo (see
[
22
]
) to the space of analytic functions defined on a simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The discrete Fourier transform is defined by

\displaystyle x_{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}e^{-\frac{2\pi ink}{N}},

whereby
\mathcal{F}(y_{n})=(x_{k})
(see
[
5
]
). For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, we perform the discrete Fourier transform on the coefficients
a_{k}
from
k=0
to
n
which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}e^{-\frac{2\pi ink}{n+1}}\bigg)z^{n}.

To obtain a Bohr-type inequality for simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
, we denote the majorant series of
\mathcal{F}[f](z)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|a_{k}e^{-\frac{2\pi ink}{n+1}}\big|\bigg)r^{n},

where
r=|z|<1
.

Actually, for functions defined on the unit disk
\Omega_{0}=\mathbb{D}
, Ong
et al.
(see
[
32
]
) studied the Bohr inequality for Fourier integral transform and Laplace transform and obtained the following results.

Theorem C
.

[
32
, Theorem 1]

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{1-r}

whenever
r\leq 1/3
. The constant
1/3
cannot be improved.

In
[
32
]
, the discrete Laplace transform,
\mathcal{L}
, is considered, such that for a sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
\mathcal{L}(y_{n})=(x_{k})
, where

\displaystyle(x_{k})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{y_{n}}{(k+1)^{n+1}},

when the series on the right hand side is convergent. Thus it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\right)r^{n},

where
|z|=r<1
.

Theorem D
.

[
32
, Theorem 3]

If
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\bigg(\frac{1}{1-r}\bigg)

for all
0<r<1
.

Through a detailed study, we see that Theorem C and Theorem D can be applied to functions with domains that extend beyond the unit disk. In fact, the aforementioned discussions have inspired us to pose the following questions for further study.

Question 2
.

Can we establish the Theorem C and Theorem D for functions in simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
?

Question 3
.

Can we obtain a better estimate in Theorem D? Can we show the inequality in Theorem D is sharp?

We affirmatively answer the above questions and obtain Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
2.2
. We show that, if we change the method of proof, then we can obtain a better estimate as well as the sharp inequality in Theorem D. In order to establish our theorems, we require the following Lemma, which was established by Evdoridis
et al.
(see
[
11
]
), regarding the coefficient bound of functions in a simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
. This lemma will play a crucial role in proving our theorems.

Lemma A
.

[
11
, Lemma 2]

For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let
f
be an analytic function in
\Omega_{\gamma}
bounded by
1
with the series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\;\;\mbox{for}\;n\geq 1.

We are in a state to give our first theorem as an answer to the Question
2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{1-r}

for
r\leq r_{0}=(1+\gamma)/{(3+\gamma)}
. The radius
r_{0}
is best possible.

Remark 3.1
.

By putting
\gamma=0
, we obtain exactly
[
32
, Theorem 1]
for unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The function
f
be analytic in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, with
|f(z)|<1
. First, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\bigg)r^{n}.

By Lemma A, we have
|a_{k}|\leq(1-|a_{0}|^{2})/(1+\gamma)
. A routine computation yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}(r)\leq|a_{0}|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nr^{n}=\frac{|a_{0}|}{1-r}+\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2}r)}{(1+\gamma)(1-r)^{2}}.

For
|a_{0}|=x\in(0,1)
, we define the function

\displaystyle\Psi_{\gamma,r}(x):=\frac{x}{1-r}+\frac{(1-x^{2})r}{(1+\gamma)(1-r)^{2}}\;\mbox{for}\;r,\gamma\in[0,1).

Differentiating
\Psi_{\gamma,r}(x)
w.r.t.
x
, we see that

\displaystyle(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{2xr}{(1+\gamma)(1-r)^{2}}

and

\displaystyle(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{2r}{(1+\gamma)(1-r)^{2}}\leq 0\;\;\mbox{for}\;r,\gamma\in[0,1).

Evidently,
(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(x)
is a decreasing function of
x
. Hence,

\displaystyle(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(x)\geq(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(1)=\frac{(1-r)(1+\gamma)-2r}{(1+\gamma)(1-r)^{2}}.

Then, it is clear that
(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(1)\geq 0
for
r\leq r_{0}=(1+\gamma)/(3+\gamma)
. Thus we see that
(\Psi_{\gamma,r})^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
when
r\leq r_{0}
. For
r\leq r_{0},\Psi_{\gamma,r}(x)
being an increasing function yields that

\displaystyle\Psi_{\gamma,r}(x)\leq\Psi_{\gamma,r}(1)=\frac{1}{1-r}.

The inequality we desire has now been established.

To show that the radius
r_{0}
is best possible, we consider the function

(3.1)

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)=\frac{a-\gamma-(1-\gamma)z}{1-a\gamma-a(1-\gamma)z}=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},\;z\in\mathbb{D},

where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}A_{0}=\dfrac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma},\vskip 5.69054pt\\
A_{n}=\dfrac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\bigg(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{n}\;\mbox{for}\;n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

With this function
f_{0}
, a routine computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{k}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{n}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{n}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{1-r}\Phi^{\gamma}_{a}(r),

where

\displaystyle\Phi^{\gamma}_{a}(r):=1-\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}+\frac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{(1-a\gamma)}\bigg(-r+\frac{a(1-\gamma)r}{1-a\gamma-ar+a\gamma r}\bigg).

A further computation shows that

\displaystyle(\Phi^{\gamma}_{a})^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{(1-\gamma)(1-a^{2})}{(1-a\gamma(1-r)-ar)^{2}}<0\;\;\mbox{for}\;r\in(0,1),

hence,
(\Phi^{\gamma}_{a})(r)
is an increasing function of
r
in
(0,1)
and thus for
r>r_{0}=(1+\gamma)/(3+\gamma)
, we have

\displaystyle(\Phi^{\gamma}_{a})(r)<(\Phi^{\gamma}_{a})(r_{0})=\frac{2(1-a)^{2}(1+\gamma)^{2}}{(1-a\gamma)(3-a+\gamma-3a\gamma)},

which tends to
0
as
a\to 1^{-}
. Therefore,
(\Phi^{\gamma}_{a})(r)
is negative for
r>r_{0}
, and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{1-r}\Phi^{\gamma}_{a}(r)>\frac{1}{1+r}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\bigg(\frac{1}{1-r}\bigg)

for
0<r<1
.

Remark 3.2
.

In particular, when
\gamma=0
, we get exactly the result
[
32
, Theorem 3]
for unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In fact, it is worth pointing out that the upper bound in the inequality (
3.2
) is independent of
\gamma\in[0,1)
in Theorem
3.2
. Thus, Theorem
3.2
is a more compact form of
[
32
, Theorem 3]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The function
f
be analytic in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, with
|f(z)|<1
. First, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{|a_{k}|}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\bigg)r^{n}.

By Lemma A, a tedious computation, using the estimate

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n(n+1)^{n+1}}<0,

we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq|a_{0}|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{|a_{0}|}{r}\ln(1-r)+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{n(n+1)}-\frac{1}{n(n+1)^{n+1}}\bigg)r^{n}

\displaystyle<-\frac{|a_{0}|}{r}\ln(1-r)+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{r}-1\bigg)\ln(1-r)+1\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{\{-|a_{0}|(1+\gamma)+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})(1-r)\}\ln(1-r)}{r(1+\gamma)}+\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(|a_{0}|)

Let
|a_{0}|=x\in(0,1)
. We see that

\displaystyle(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\{2xr-2x-(1+\gamma)\}\ln(1-r)-2xr}{r(1+\gamma)}

and

\displaystyle(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{2r(1-\ln(1-r))+2\ln(1-r)}{(1+\gamma)r}\leq 0\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;x,r,\gamma\in[0,1).

Evidently,
(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime}(x)
is a decreasing function of
x
, which implies that

\displaystyle(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime}(x)\geq(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime}(1)=\frac{\{2r-2-(1+\gamma)\}\ln(1-r)-2r}{r(1+\gamma)}=\frac{\Phi^{\gamma}(r)}{r(1+\gamma)},

where

\displaystyle\Phi^{\gamma}(r)=\{2r-2-(1+\gamma)\}\ln(1-r)-2r.

We observe that
\Phi^{\gamma}(r)
is an increasing function because of the fact that

\displaystyle(\Phi^{\gamma})^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1+\gamma}{1-r}+2\ln(1-r)>0\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;r\in(0,1).

Consequently, we have
\Phi^{\gamma}(r)>\Phi^{\gamma}(0)=0
. Hence,
(\Psi_{1}^{\gamma})^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in(0,1)
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(|a_{0}|)\leq\Psi_{1}^{\gamma}(1)=\frac{1}{r}\ln\bigg(\frac{1}{1-r}\bigg).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Concluding remark on Theorem
3.2

We have used the estimate

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n(n+1)^{n+1}}<0,

in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. However, the proof can be modified without using (
3.3
). For example, using the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}=\frac{n}{(n+1)^{2}}

we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\right)r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n}{(n+1)^{2}}\right)r^{n}=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}.

Thus, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{a}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right):=F_{\gamma,r}(a).

A simple computation gives us

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}(F_{\gamma,r}(a))=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{2a}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{da^{2}}(F_{\gamma,r}(a))=-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right)\leq 0

for all
r,\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Clearly,
\frac{d}{da}(F_{\gamma,r}(a))
is a decreasing function of
a\in(0,1)
, hence

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}(F_{\gamma,r}(a))\geq\frac{d}{da}(F_{\gamma,r}(1))=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right)\geq 0

for all
a\in(0,1)
. This shows that
F_{\gamma,r}(a)
is an increasing function of
a\in(0,1)
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)\leq F_{\gamma,r}(a)\leq F_{\gamma,r}(1)=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\;\mbox{for all}\;r\in(0,1).

The same estimate, as in
[
32
]
, is obtained. Our observation is that regardless of how we arrange the series, the upper bound of
\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)
will remain the same due to the presence of the factor
(1-a^{2})
in the expression of the function
F_{\gamma,r}(a)
, as we take
a\to 1^{-}
.

However,
[
32
]
does not accurately determine the Bohr radius. In contrast, our study shows that it is possible to find it by rearranging the terms using a different approach. Let us rewrite the right side of (
3.4
) in the following manner:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{(1-a)}{r}\ln(1-r)+\frac{2(1-a)}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1+\gamma)}\bigg[\frac{(1+\gamma)}{r}\ln(1-r)+2\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1+\gamma)}\Phi_{\gamma}(r),

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{\gamma}(r):=\frac{(1+\gamma)}{r}\ln(1-r)+2\left(\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-\frac{{\rm Li}_{2}(r)}{r}\right).

Thus, the desired inequality

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)

can be obtained if
\Phi_{\gamma}(r)\leq 0
for
r\leq r_{\gamma}<1
. By using basic theorems in calculus, one can obtain
r_{\gamma}
as a root in
(0,1)
of the equation
\Phi_{\gamma}(r)=0
for values of
\gamma\in[0,\gamma_{*}]
, where
\gamma_{*}\approx 0.27713
. Consequently, we see that
r_{\gamma}
is the Bohr radius for the discrete Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}
. It is important to note that, specifically, when
\Omega_{0}=\mathbb{D}
, we observe that the root of the following equation is
r_{0}\approx 0.940599
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\ln(1-r)+\frac{2}{r}\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)-{\rm Li}_{2}(r)\right)=0.

Hence, we see that a better estimate is obtained in the radius.

Moreover, it can be shown that the inequality (
3.5
) is sharp and this can be shown using the function
f_{0}
as given by (
3.1
). When
f
is equal to
f_{0}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle=|A_{0}|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|A_{n}|}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\right)\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{k}\right)r^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)+\frac{1-a}{1-a\gamma}\bigg[-\frac{(1+\gamma)}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1+a}{a}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k+1}}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{k}\right)r^{k}\bigg].

Taking
a\to 1^{-}
in the last expression, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-r}\right)

which shows that the bound is sharp. In response to
[
32
, Remark 1]
, we now say that our result improves the estimate in the radius. Additionally, we show that a sharper inequality can be established in certain situations.

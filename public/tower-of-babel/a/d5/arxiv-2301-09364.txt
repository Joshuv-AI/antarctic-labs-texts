On Uniqueness of Submaximally Symmetric Vector Ordinary Differential Equations of C-Class This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Symmetry, Invariants, and their Applications in honor of Peter J. Olver. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Olver.html
Johnson Allen KESSY and Dennis THE

Abstract
The fundamental invariants for vector ODEs of order ≥ 3 \geq 3 considered up to point transformations consist of generalized Wilczynski invariants and C-class invariants. An ODE of C-class is characterized by the vanishing of the former. For any fixed C-class invariant 𝒰 \mathcal{U} , we give a local (point) classification for all submaximally symmetric ODEs of C-class with 𝒰 ≢ 0 \mathcal{U}\not\equiv 0 and all remaining C-class invariants vanishing identically. Our results yield generalizations of a well-known classical result for scalar ODEs due to Sophus Lie. Fundamental invariants correspond to the harmonic curvature of the associated Cartan geometry. A key new ingredient underlying our classification results is an advance concerning the harmonic theory associated with the structure of vector ODEs of C-class. Namely, for each irreducible C-class module, we provide an explicit identification of a lowest weight vector as a harmonic 2-cochain.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{0}:=\mathfrak{g}_{0,0}=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{H}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{m},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{-1}:=\mathfrak{g}_{-1,0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0,-1}=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{X}\oplus(\mathbb{R}E_{0}\otimes W),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{-i-1}:=\mathfrak{g}_{-i,-1}=\mathbb{R}E_{i}\otimes W,\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

We note that
\mathfrak{g}_{-}:=\mathfrak{g}_{-n-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is generated by
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
.

We also endow
\mathfrak{g}
with the
canonical filtration

\mathfrak{g}^{i}:=\sum_{j\geq i}\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, which turns
\mathfrak{g}
into a filtered Lie algebra. Its associated graded
\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{g}):=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{gr}_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\operatorname{gr}_{k}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}^{k}/\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}
, is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}
as graded Lie algebras. Using the isomorphism, we let
\operatorname{gr}_{k}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{k}\to\mathfrak{g}_{k}
denote the
leading part
. Explicitly, if
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{k}
with
x=x_{k}+x_{k+1}+\cdots
, where
x_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, then
\operatorname{gr}_{k}(x):=x_{k}
. The following notations will be convenient:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1},\qquad\mathfrak{p}_{+}:=\mathfrak{g}^{1}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

At the group level, let

\displaystyle G:=(\operatorname{SL}_{2}\times\operatorname{GL}_{m})\ltimes V,\qquad P:=\operatorname{ST}_{2}\times\operatorname{GL}_{m},\qquad G_{0}:=\{g\in P\colon\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}\},

where
\operatorname{ST}_{2}\subset\operatorname{SL}_{2}
is the subgroup of lower triangular matrices. (Note that
G_{0}
is isomorphic to that given in Section
2.1
.) We also let
P_{+}\subset P
denote the connected Lie subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{p}
. We remark that the canonical filtration on
\mathfrak{g}
is
P
-invariant.

2.3
Cartan geometries associated to ODE

Fix
G
,
P
and
G_{0}
as above. Recall also from Section
2.1
that all vector ODEs (
1.1
) can be formulated as filtered
G_{0}
-structures. Importantly, there is an equivalence of categories between filtered
G_{0}
-structures on
\mathcal{E}
(which is a wider category than that arising from ODE – see below) and regular, normal Cartan geometries
(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)
of type
(G,P)

[
3
,
10
]
. A Cartan geometry consists of a (right) principal
P
-bundle
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E}
endowed with a
Cartan connection

\omega
, i.e.,
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued 1-form on
\mathcal{G}
such that

(a)

For any
u\in\mathcal{G}
,
\omega_{u}\colon T_{u}\mathcal{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a linear isomorphism;

(b)

R_{g}^{\ast}\omega=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\circ\,\omega
for any
g\in P
, i.e.,
\omega
is
P
-equivariant;

(c)

\omega(\zeta_{A})=A
, where
A\in\mathfrak{p}
, where
\zeta_{A}
is the fundamental vertical vector field defined by
\zeta_{A}(u):=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\big|}_{t=0}u\cdot\exp(tA)
.

The
curvature

K\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g})
of the geometry is given by
K(\xi,\eta)={\rm d}\omega(\xi,\eta)+[\omega(\xi),\omega(\eta)]
, which is
P
-equivariant and horizontal, i.e.,
K(\zeta_{A},\cdot)=0
,
\forall A\in\mathfrak{p}
. Consequently,
K
is determined by the
P
-equivariant
curvature function

\kappa\colon\mathcal{G}\to\bigwedge^{2}(\nicefrac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, defined by

\kappa(u)(A,B)=K\bigl(\omega^{-1}(A),\omega^{-1}(B)\bigr)(u)
,
\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{g}
. Letting
\omega_{G}
be the Maurer–Cartan form on
G
, the
Klein geometry

(G\to\nicefrac{{G}}{{P}},\omega_{G})
satisfies
K\equiv 0
(Maurer–Cartan equation), and is the
flat model
for all Cartan geometries of type
(G,P)
.

In terms of the canonical filtration
\bigl\{\mathfrak{g}^{i}\bigr\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
from Section
2.2
,
\omega
is said to be
regular
if
\kappa\bigl(\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j}\bigr)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{i+j+1}
,
\forall i,j
. Importantly, it is known that for all filtered
G_{0}
-structures
arising from ODE
, the corresponding Cartan geometry has
\kappa
satisfying the
strong regularity
condition
[
3
, Remark 2.3]

\displaystyle\kappa\bigl(\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j}\bigr)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{i+j+1}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\min(i,j)-1},\qquad\forall i,j.

(2.3)

To define normality, we first fix an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
in terms of the basis introduced in Section
2.2
:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be an inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\bigl\{\mathsf{X},\mathsf{H},\mathsf{Y},e_{a}{}^{b},E_{i,a}\bigr\}
is an orthogonal basis for
\mathfrak{g}
with squared lengths of basis elements given below

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{X},\mathsf{X}\rangle=\langle\mathsf{Y},\mathsf{Y}\rangle=1,\qquad\langle\mathsf{H},\mathsf{H}\rangle=2,\qquad\big\langle e_{a}{}^{b},e_{a}{}^{b}\big\rangle=1,\qquad\langle E_{i,a},E_{i,a}\rangle=\frac{i!}{(n-i)!}.

Then
\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
and
\forall u,v\in V
, we have
\langle A,B\rangle=\tr\bigl(A^{\rm T}B\bigr)
and

\langle Au,v\rangle=\big\langle u,A^{\rm T}v\big\rangle
.

Consider
C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}):=\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
equipped with the induced canonical filtration from
\mathfrak{g}
and let
\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}
be the standard differential of the complex for computing Lie algebra cohomology groups
H^{k}\!(\mathfrak{g},\!\mathfrak{g})
. Then, define the
codifferential

\partial^{\ast}\colon C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\to C^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
to be the adjoint of
\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to the induced inner product from
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e., for each
k
we have

\langle\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}\phi,\psi\rangle=\langle\phi,\partial^{\ast}\psi\rangle
for all
\phi\in C^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
\psi\in C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. By
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
, the codifferential descends to a
P
-equivariant map
\partial^{\ast}\colon\bigwedge^{k}(\nicefrac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\bigwedge^{k-1}(\nicefrac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. A Cartan connection
\omega
has curvature function
\kappa
valued in
\bigwedge^{2}(\nicefrac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, and
\omega
is said to be
normal
if
\partial^{\ast}\kappa=0
.
In this article, we will always work with Cartan geometries of type
(G,P)
that are normal and strongly regular.

Since
(\partial^{\ast})^{2}=0
, then the (normal) curvature
\kappa
quotients to a
P
-equivariant function
\kappa_{H}
:
\mathcal{G}\to\frac{\ker\partial^{\ast}}{\img\partial^{\ast}}
called the
harmonic curvature
. By regularity,
\kappa_{H}
is valued in the filtrand of positive degree of the
P
-module
\frac{\ker\partial^{\ast}}{\img\partial^{\ast}}
, which by
[
3
, Corollary 3.8]
is completely reducible, i.e.,
P_{+}
acts on it trivially, and therefore only the
G_{0}
-action is relevant. It is well known (see Theorem
A.3
and references therein) that
\kappa_{H}
completely obstructs local flatness, i.e.,
\kappa_{H}\equiv 0
is equivalent to
\kappa\equiv 0
.

Identify
\bigwedge^{k}(\nicefrac{{\mathfrak{g}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\cong\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{-}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
as
G_{0}
-modules, and recall from Section
2.2
that
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{q}\ltimes V
. Given
\phi\in C^{k}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g}):=\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{-}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, then we have
\phi=\mathsf{X}^{*}\wedge\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
, for
\phi_{1}\in C^{k-1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
and
\phi_{2}\in C^{k}(V,\mathfrak{g})
, and where
\mathsf{X}^{*}
is dual to
\mathsf{X}
. Denoting
\phi:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, then
\partial\phi
is given by
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]

\displaystyle\partial\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{V}\phi_{1}+\mathsf{X}\cdot\phi_{2}\\
\partial_{V}\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\partial_{V}\phi_{2}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}x_{i}\cdot\phi_{2}\bigl(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{k}\bigr)

for
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\in V
, and letting
\widehat{x}_{i}
denote omission of
x_{i}
. A direct consequence of (
2.4
) is:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\phi\in\bigwedge^{k}V^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\partial\phi=0
if and only if
\mathsf{X}\cdot\phi=0
and
\partial_{V}\phi=0
. Moreover, if in fact
\phi\in\bigwedge^{k}V^{*}\otimes V
, then
\partial\phi=0
if and only if
\mathsf{X}\cdot\phi=0
.

Defining
\square:=\partial\circ\partial^{\ast}+\partial^{\ast}\circ\partial\colon\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{-}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{-}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, we then have the following
G_{0}
-isomorphisms,

\displaystyle\bigwedge{}^{\!\!k}{\mathfrak{g}_{-}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{g}}\cong\hbox to0.0pt{$\overbrace{\phantom{\,\img\partial^{\ast}\oplus\ker\square}}^{\ker\partial^{\ast}}$\hss}\img\partial^{\ast}\oplus\underbrace{\ker\square\oplus\img\partial}_{\ker\partial},\qquad\ker\square\cong\frac{\ker\partial^{\ast}}{\img\partial^{\ast}}\cong\frac{\ker\partial}{\img\partial}=:H^{k}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g}).

(2.5)

Consequently, for a regular, normal Cartan geometry, the codomain of
\kappa_{H}
can be identified with the subspace
H^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g})\subset H^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g})
on which the grading element
\mathsf{Z}=\mathsf{Z}_{1}+\mathsf{Z}_{2}
acts with positive eigenvalues. However, it should be emphasized that only part of
H^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g})
is in fact realizable for geometries associated to ODE
[
11
,
22
]
. Correspondingly, we define:

Definition 2.3
.

The
effective part

\mathbb{E}\subsetneq H^{2}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{g})
is the minimal
G_{0}
-module in which
\kappa_{H}
is valued, for any (strongly) regular, normal Cartan geometry of type
(G,P)
associated to an ODE (
1.1
) (for fixed
(n,m)
).

We will focus on ODEs (

Remark 2.5

A vector ODE (

2.6
The Doubrov–Medvedev condition

We will be able to precisely identify
\mathbb{A}_{2}
with the help of an additional linear condition formulated in
[
11
, Section 3.1, Proposition 4]
, and which we now summarize. Consider the
\mathfrak{p}
-invariant subspace
F=\sn\{E_{0},\dots,E_{n-1}\}\otimes W\subset V
, and define
\delta\colon\hm(F,\mathbb{R}\mathsf{X})\to\hm\bigl(\bigwedge^{2}F,V/F\bigr)
by

\displaystyle(\delta B)(x,y)=(B(x)\cdot y-B(y)\cdot x)\mod F,\qquad\forall B\in\hm(F,\mathbb{R}\mathsf{X}).

(2.9)

We have the inclusion
\iota_{F}\colon F\to V
, which induces
V/F\cong W
(as
\mathfrak{p}
-modules) and natural quotient
\pi_{W}\colon V\to V/F
. Also induced is the inclusion
\iota_{\bigwedge^{2}F}\colon\bigwedge^{2}F\to\bigwedge^{2}V
, from which we define
\vartheta\colon\hm\bigl(\bigwedge^{2}V,V\bigr)\to\hm\bigl(\bigwedge^{2}F,V/F\bigr)
by
\vartheta=\pi_{W}\circ\iota_{\bigwedge^{2}F}^{*}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\vartheta(A)=\left.A\right|_{\bigwedge^{2}F}\mod F.

(2.10)

From
[
11
, Section 3.1, Proposition 4]
and Remark
2.5
, we deduce that for a C-class ODE of order
\geq 4
, the
\mathbb{A}_{2}
-component
\mathcal{A}_{2}
of its harmonic curvature
\kappa_{H}
satisfies
\vartheta(\mathcal{A}_{2})\in\img(\delta)
, which we refer to as the
Doubrov–Medvedev condition
. Correspondingly, for
n\geq 3
we formulate the algebraic condition

\displaystyle\vartheta(A)\in\img(\delta),\qquad\forall A\in\mathbb{A}_{2},

(2.11)

which we refer to as the
DM condition
. (This condition is not present for 3rd order ODE.)

4
Homogeneous structures and Cartan-theoretic descriptions

Our method for proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
will rely on the fact that Cartan geometries (see Section
2.3
) associated to submaximally symmetric vector ODEs are locally homogeneous (see Lemma
4.2
). In this section, we summarize all relevant symmetry-based facts about such geometries and their corresponding algebraic models of ODE type. We will use
G
,
P
,
G_{0}
and
\mathfrak{g}
from Section
2.2
, and the filtration and grading on
\mathfrak{g}
defined there.

4.1
Symmetry gaps for ODE

An
infinitesimal symmetry
of a given Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)
of type
(G,P)
is a
P
-invariant vector field
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G}){}^{P}
on
\mathcal{G}
that preserves
\omega
under Lie differentiation, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\omega=0
. The collection of all such vector fields forms a Lie algebra, which we denote by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{inf}(\mathcal{G},\omega):=\bigl\{\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G}){}^{P}\colon\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\omega=0\bigr\}\subset\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G}).

The submaximal symmetry dimension is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{S}:=\max\bigl\{\dim\mathfrak{inf}(\mathcal{G},\omega)\colon&(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)\ \text{strongly regular, normal of type}\ (G,P)\\
&\text{associated to a vector ODE $\mathcal{E}$ \eqref{ODE}, with}\ \kappa_{H}\not\equiv 0\bigr\}.\end{split}

Recall that
\mathbb{E}
decomposes into
G_{0}
-irreducible submodules
\mathbb{U}\subset\mathbb{E}
. Analogous to
\mathfrak{S}
above, we define:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbb{U}}:=\max\bigl\{\dim\mathfrak{inf}(\mathcal{G},\omega)\colon&(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)\ \text{strongly regular, normal of type}\ (G,P)\\
&\text{associated to a vector ODE $\mathcal{E}$ \eqref{ODE},}\ \text{with}\ 0\not\equiv\img(\kappa_{H})\subset\mathbb{U}\bigr\}.\end{split}

To define suitable algebraic upper bounds, we will need the following notion from
[
18
]
:

Definition 4.1
.

Given a subspace
\mathfrak{a}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, the graded subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}=\pr(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{a}_{0}):=\mathfrak{a}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{-}:=\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{-n-1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{1}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}\colon[x,\mathfrak{g}_{-1}]\subset\mathfrak{a}_{0}\}
, is called the
Tanaka prolongation algebra
. For
\phi
in some
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module, we define
\mathfrak{a}^{\phi}:=\pr(\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{ann}(\phi))
, where
\mathfrak{ann}(\phi)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is the annihilator of
\phi
.

Now, we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}:=\max\bigl\{\dim\mathfrak{a}^{\phi}\colon 0\neq\phi\in\mathbb{E}\bigr\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}:=\max\bigl\{\dim\mathfrak{a}^{\phi}\colon 0\neq\phi\in\mathbb{U}\bigr\}.

(4.1)

By
[
16
, Theorem 2.11]
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}\leq\mathfrak{U}<\dim\mathfrak{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbb{U}}\leq\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}\quad\text{for all $G_{0}$- irreducible modules}\quad\mathbb{U}\subset\mathbb{E}.

(4.2)

Note that
\mathfrak{U}=\max_{\mathbb{U}\subset\mathbb{E}}\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}
. In fact, by
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
, in all of the
vector
cases we have equality:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbb{U}}=\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}.

Examples of some vector ODEs realizing these can be found in
[
16
, Tables 8 and 10]
.

4.2
Local homogeneity and algebraic models of ODE type

Recall that a Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)
of type
(G,P)
is said to be locally
homogeneous
if there exists a (left) action by a local Lie group
F
on
\mathcal{G}
by principal bundle morphisms preserving
\omega
that projects onto a transitive action down on
\mathcal{E}
. We then have
[
16
, Lemma A.1]
:

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix a
G_{0}
-irrep
\mathbb{U}\subset\mathbb{E}
. Then any regular, normal Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)
of type
(G,P)
with
0\not\equiv\img(\kappa_{H})\subset\mathbb{U}
and
\dim(\mathfrak{inf}(\mathcal{G},\omega))=\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}
is locally homogeneous about any point
u\in\mathcal{G}
with
\kappa_{H}(u)\neq 0
.

By
[
16
, Section A.1]
, such a homogeneous Cartan geometry can be encoded
Cartan-theoretically
by:

Definition 4.3
.

An
algebraic model

(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})

of ODE type
is a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{f},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{f}})
such that:

(i)

\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a filtered subspace whose associated graded
\mathfrak{s}:=\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{f})\subset\mathfrak{g}
has
\mathfrak{s}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{-}
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{f}^{0}
inserts trivially into
\kappa(x,y):=[x,y]-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{f}}
, i.e.,
\kappa(z,\cdot)=0
for all
z\in\mathfrak{f}^{0}
;

(iii)

\kappa
is normal and strongly regular :
\partial^{\ast}\kappa=0
and
\kappa(\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{i+j+1}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\min(i,j)-1}
,
\forall i,j
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
denote the set of all algebraic models
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
of ODE type for fixed
(G,P)
. Then
\mathcal{N}
admits a
P
-action and is partially ordered:

(1)

P
-action:  for
p\in P
and
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{N}
, we have
p\cdot\mathfrak{f}:=\operatorname{Ad}_{p}(\mathfrak{f})
. We will regard all algebraic models
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
of ODE type in the same
P
-orbit to be equivalent.

(2)

Partial order relation
\leq
:  for
\mathfrak{f},\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}\in\mathcal{N}
regard
\mathfrak{f}\leq\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
if there exists a map
\mathfrak{f}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
of Lie algebras. We will focus on maximal elements in
(\mathcal{N},\leq)
.

Combining (
4.2
), Lemma
4.2
and Definition
4.3
, we obtain the following key existence result:

Theorem 4.4
.

Fix an irreducible
G_{0}
-module
\mathbb{U}
in the effective part
\mathbb{E}
for vector ODEs (
1.1
) of order
\geq 3
. Then there exists an algebraic model
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
of ODE type with
0\not\equiv\img(\kappa_{H})\subset\mathbb{U}
and
\dim\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{U}}=\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbb{U}}
.

Remark 4.5
.

Conversely, by
[
18
, Lemma 4.1.4]
, for a given algebraic model
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
of ODE type, there exists a locally homogeneous strongly regular, normal Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{E},\omega)
of type
(G,P)
with
\mathfrak{inf}(\mathcal{G},\omega)
containing a subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{f}
.

We caution that such a geometry may not arise from an ODE (
1.1
). (For instance, the Doubrov–Medvedev condition must additionally hold.) Consequently, our strategy involves:

(i)

classifying (up to the
P
-action) the corresponding algebraic models
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
of ODE type, and then

(ii)

providing vector ODEs of C-class realizing these algebraic models.

A filtered linear space
\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathfrak{g}
can be described as the graph of some linear map on
\mathfrak{s}
into
\mathfrak{g}
as follows. Let
\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a graded subalgebra such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}
. Then

\mathfrak{f}:=\bigoplus_{i}\sn\left\{x+\mathfrak{D}(x):x\in\mathfrak{s}_{i}\right\}
,
for some
unique
linear (
deformation
) map
\mathfrak{D}:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}
such that
\mathfrak{D}(x)\in\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}
for
x\in\mathfrak{s}_{i}
.

We will use the following results from
[
16
, Section A.1]
in carrying out the classifications.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
T\in\mathfrak{f}^{0}
and suppose that the complementary graded subspaces
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}\subset\mathfrak{g}
are
\operatorname{ad}_{T}
-invariant, then the map
\mathfrak{D}\colon\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\perp}
is
\operatorname{ad}_{T}
-invariant, i.e.,
T\cdot\mathfrak{D}=0\iff\,\operatorname{ad}_{T}\circ\mathfrak{D}=\mathfrak{D}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{T}
.

Recall from Section
2.3
that
\kappa_{H}:=\kappa\mod\img\partial^{*}
, where
\partial^{*}
is the codifferential. We then have:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{f};\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{p})
be an algebraic model of ODE type. Then

(i)

(\mathfrak{f},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{f}})
is a filtered Lie algebra.

(ii)

\mathfrak{f}^{0}\cdot\kappa=0
, i.e.,
[z,\kappa(x,y)]_{\mathfrak{f}}=\kappa([z,x]_{\mathfrak{f}},y)+\kappa(x,[z,y]_{\mathfrak{f}})
,
\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{f}
,
\forall z\in\mathfrak{f}^{0}
.

(iii)

\mathfrak{s}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{\kappa_{H}}
.

Following
[
28
, Section 2.2]
, we shall refer to
\mathfrak{f}
as a (constrained)
filtered sub-deformation
of
\mathfrak{s}
.

\mathcal{A}\mathcal{E}

\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{m}\bigl(c_{2,k}-c_{k,2}\delta_{a}{}^{1}\bigr)\frac{(n-k)(k+1)(n-2)!}{2}

\displaystyle=(mc_{2,k}-c_{k,2})\frac{(n-k)(k+1)(n-2)!}{2}.

Similarly, we have

1.
Introduction

As is well known
[
18
]
, for an abelian variety
A
and its dual
A^{t}

D^{b}(A)\cong D^{b}(A^{t})

given by a Fourier-Mukai transform with kernel the normalized Poincare bundle on

. In this article, we will show that an analogous statement holds for tame abelian stacks. In
[
9
]
abelian stacks were introduced to construct albanese morphisms of algebraic stacks; they may be thought of as stacky generalizations of abelian varieties. Every abelian stack
{\mathcal{A}}
has a dual
\mathfrak{D}(A)
and the product
{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{A}})
has a well-defined Poincare bundle. We first show that tame abelian stacks are perfect in the sense of
[
5
]
in
3.6
and conclude that
\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}({\mathcal{A}})
(the stable
\infty-
category of quasi-coherent sheaves on
{\mathcal{A}}
) is self-dual by
[
5
, corollary 4.8]
. We then explicitly verify that
\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{A}}))
is dual to
\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}(A)
by a direct computation with the Fourier-Mukai transform induced by the Poincare bundle on
{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{A}})
. The benefit of this approach is that it is equivalent to showing that a composition of morphisms

\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}({\mathcal{A}})\times\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{A}}))\times\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\operatorname{QC}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{A}}))

is the identity, and this calculation can be made in a way analogous to Mukai’s computation in
[
18
]
.

Every abelian stack is a strict commutative group stack in the sense of Deligne, see
[
24
, XVIII 1.4]
where they are called Picard stacks. Commutative group stacks can be thought of as an enlargement of the category of sheaves of commutative groups, while algebraic commutative group stacks can be thought of as an enlargement of the category of commutative group schemes. Deligne’s construction provides two alternative ways of thinking of commutative group stacks. Firstly, one may think of them as a stack
{\mathcal{G}}
along with an addition law
m\colon{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
that satisfies the natural 2-categorical analogues of the commutative group scheme axioms. Alternatively, one may work with two-term complexes of sheaves of abelian groups
[G^{-1}\rightarrow G^{0}]
. The correspondence

[G^{-1}\rightarrow G^{0}]\mapsto[G^{0}/G^{-1}]

provides an equivalence of categories between two-term complexes of abelian sheaves and commutative group stacks on
S
. Here
[G^{0}/G^{-1}]
is the quotient stack. See
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
or
[
24
, 1.4]
for the precise nature of these equivalences.

5.
The Poincare Bundle

In this section, we discuss the Poincare bundle on an abelian stack. Our approach will be to show that the study of the Poincare bundle on an abelian stack can be reduced to the study of the Poincare bundle on the coarse moduli space, where we may apply the results of Mukai on relative Fourier-Mukai transforms.

Definition 5.1

(The Poincare bundle)
.

Let
G
be a commutative group stack that is flat, proper, and finitely presented over
S
. Assume in addition that the inertia stack of
G
is finite, flat, and finitely presented over
G
.
Then by
[
9
, Theorem 3.14]

\mathfrak{D}(G)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\textnormal{grp}}(G,B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is an algebraic stack. By
[
9
, Definition 4.1]

there is a canonical evaluation morphism

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{eval}_{G}\colon G\times_{S}\mathfrak{D}(G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

(5.2)

\displaystyle(g,\varphi)

\displaystyle\mapsto\varphi(g)

We define the Poincare bundle on
G\times\mathfrak{D}(G)
by

(5.3)

{\mathcal{P}}_{G}=\operatorname{eval}_{G}^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}})

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}
is the universal line bundle on
B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

The objects of
\mathfrak{D}(G)(T)
can be thought of as pairs
(L,\alpha)
where
L
is a line bundle on
G\times_{S}T
and

\alpha\colon m_{G}^{*}L\rightarrow p_{1}^{*}L\otimes p_{2}^{*}L

an isomorphism satisfying some coherence conditions; see the discussion preceding
[
9
, Theorem 3.14]
.
Here
m_{\mathcal{G}}\colon{\mathcal{G}}\times_{S}{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
is the multiplication and
p_{i}\colon{\mathcal{G}}\times_{S}{\mathcal{G}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
the projections.

Let
M\rightarrow S
be an abelian scheme, and
M^{t}\rightarrow S
its dual. The normalized Poincare bundle on
M\times_{S}M^{t}
is a line bundle
P_{M}
such that if
L
is a line bundle on
M\times_{S}T
that is numerically trivial, then
L=(1\times h)^{*}P_{M}\otimes f_{T}^{*}N
where
h\colon T\rightarrow M^{t}
is the morphism giving
L
and
N

is some line bundle on
T
. Normalized refers to the fact that
P|_{0_{M}\times M^{t}}={\mathcal{O}}_{M^{t}}
and
P|_{M\times 0_{M^{t}}}={\mathcal{O}}_{A}
. For an abelian scheme, a normalized Poincare bundle always exists as the Picard scheme exists, as the morphism is cohomologically flat and there is also a section (see Chapter 8 of
[
6
]
for details.)
We can immediately prove that this notion generalizes the classical notion.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
be an abelian scheme over a base
S
and
M^{t}
the dual abelian scheme. Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
is the normalized Poincare bundle.

Proof.

Note that
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}|_{0_{M}\times_{S}\mathfrak{D}(M)}
corresponds to the morphism to the evaluation at
0_{M}
homomorphism

M^{t}\rightarrow B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
which is the trivial homomorphism. Therefore,
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}|_{0_{M}\times_{S}\mathfrak{D}(M)}
is trivial.
On the other hand
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}|_{M\times 0_{M^{t}}}
corresponds to evaluating the trivial homomorphism, which is trivial.

On the other hand,
P_{M}
satisfies the universal property of the Poincare bundle. Given a section
\sigma\colon S\rightarrow M

we have that the restriction to the fiber
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}|_{\sigma\times M^{t}}
is given by the group homomorphism
\operatorname{eval}(\sigma,\bullet)\colon M^{t}\rightarrow B_{S}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. However, as the evaluation morphism gives a natural isomorphism between
(M^{t})^{t}\cong M
we see that the family of lines bundles on
M^{t}
given by restricting
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
to the fibers of
M\times_{S}M^{t}\rightarrow M
determines
M
.
∎

We now prove a series of lemmas that will allow us to work with the Poincare bundle on tame abelian stacks. Abelian stacks are gerbes over their coarse moduli space as they have finite and flat inertia. We will require the following basic results.

A\rightarrow S

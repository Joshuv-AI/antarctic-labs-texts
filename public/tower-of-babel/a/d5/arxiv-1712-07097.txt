Categorical Fermionic actions and minimal modular extensions
César Galindo, César F. Venegas-Ramírez

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to study minimal modular extensions of braided fusion categories, with emphasis on minimal modular extensions of super-Tannakian fusion categories. We define actions of finite super-groups on fermionic fusion categories and spin-braided fusion categories. Similar to the case of groups, our motivation stems from the study of fusion categories containing the representation category of a super-group. We develop many results analogous to the Tannakian case, including cohomological obstructions, relationships with braided G G -crossed categories, and minimal modular extensions. We apply our general framework to the construction and classification of minimal modular extensions of super-groups and braided fusion categories.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to study minimal modular extensions of braided fusion categories, with an emphasis on minimal modular extensions of non-Tannakian symmetric fusion categories.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a premodular fusion category. Minimal modular extensions were defined by Michael Müger in
[
Müg03
]
as a modular category
\mathcal{M}\supset\mathcal{B}
such that the centralizer of
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})
in
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})
is the Müger’s center of
\mathcal{B}
.

Braided fusion categories play a fundamental role in diverse areas of mathematics and theoretical physics. They appear in the representation theory of quantum groups and Hopf algebras
[
CP94
]
, the construction of invariants of 3-manifolds
[
RT91
]
, and as a mathematical framework for topological phases of matter
[
MS89
,
Wit89
,
Wen90
]
. In the context of condensed matter physics, braided fusion categories organize the particle excitations in (2+1)-dimensional topological quantum systems, leading to their importance in quantum computation
[
Kit03
,
Wan10
]
.

Following
[
LKW16
]
, we denote the set of equivalence classes of modular extensions of
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{B})
. In
[
LKW16
]
, it was proved that if
\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric fusion category,
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{E})
has a natural abelian group structure. For a general premodular category,
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{B})
is a torsor (possibly empty) over
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B}))
. Hence, the results in
[
LKW16
]
reduce the problem of constructing all minimal modular extensions of a premodular category
\mathcal{B}
to the following steps:

(1)

Determine if
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{B})
is empty or not. If
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}(\mathcal{B})
is not empty, construct one minimal extension of
\mathcal{B}
.

(2)

Construct all minimal extensions of the symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})
.

The question of existence of minimal modular extensions has a rich history dating back to Müger’s formulation
[
Müg03
]
. In an unpublished note, Drinfeld constructed examples of premodular categories without minimal modular extensions, demonstrating that such extensions need not exist in general.

Our work develops a comprehensive obstruction theory for the existence of minimal modular extensions. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions in Theorem
4.8
that characterize when these extensions exist. A key contribution of our work is the development of cohomological obstructions that precisely identify when minimal modular extensions can be constructed.

We show that for a braided fusion category
\mathcal{B}
whose Müger center is Tannakian (i.e., equivalent to
{\operatorname{Rep}}(G)
for some finite group
G
), the obstruction to having a minimal modular extension is an element in
H^{4}(G;\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, which we call the
H^{4}
-anomaly of
\mathcal{B}
. This obstruction has a concrete formula that we derive in Corollary
4.10
, allowing for explicit calculations. Using this formula, we provide specific examples of braided fusion categories without minimal modular extensions in Section
4.4
, including a noteworthy example first discovered by Drinfeld.

For non-Tannakian symmetric fusion categories, that is, for super-groups
{\operatorname{Rep}}(\widetilde{G},z)
, we establish a novel framework involving fermionic actions. We introduce the concept of categorical fermionic actions of super-groups on fermionic fusion categories, which generalizes the theory of group actions on fusion categories. This generalization provides the necessary tools to analyze minimal modular extensions in the non-Tannakian case.

A significant result in this direction is Corollary
4.9
, which characterizes when the de-equivariantization map
D:\mathcal{M}_{ext}({\operatorname{Rep}}(\widetilde{G},z))\to\mathcal{M}_{ext}({\operatorname{SVec}})
is surjective. We prove that this map is surjective if and only if the super-group
(\widetilde{G},z)
is trivial. This has important implications for the classification of minimal modular extensions of super-groups.

For Tannakian fusion categories
{\operatorname{Rep}}(G)
, the group
\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{ext}}({\operatorname{Rep}}(G))
is isomorphic to
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and the associated minimal extensions are the twisted Drinfeld doubles
\mathcal{Z}(\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
(see
[
LKW16
]
). This characterization has important applications in topological quantum field theory.

Johnson-Freyd and Reutter
[
JFR24
]
recently demonstrated that every slightly degenerate braided fusion category admits a minimal nondegenerate extension, thereby resolving a longstanding open problem. Their analysis, based on fusion 2-categories, shows that the relevant obstruction—lying in

H^{5}\bigl(K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},2);\mathbb{C}^{\times}\bigr)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
vanishes universally. This result, combined with our obstruction theory for the Tannakian case, significantly advances the classification program for minimal nondegenerate extensions of braided fusion categories. Nevertheless, interesting questions remain. Our work complements these findings by clarifying the conditions for existence and elucidating the nature of the obstructions, offering a perspective that integrates with the result of
[
JFR24
]
.

The paper is organized as follows:

In Section
2
, we recall necessary definitions and results about fusion categories, braided and symmetric fusion categories, and actions of groups on fusion categories.

Section
3
develops the theory of fermionic actions on fermionic fusion categories and spin-braided fusion categories. In Theorem
3.13
and Corollary
3.14
, we prove a 2-equivalence between the 2-category of fermionic fusion categories with fermionic action of a super-group
(\widetilde{G},z)
and the 2-category of fusion categories over
{\operatorname{Rep}}(\widetilde{G},z)
, analogous to
[
DGNO10
, Theorem 4.18]
. We define a cohomological obstruction to the existence of fermionic action and provide a group-theoretical interpretation of this obstruction. Finally, we present some results about fermionic actions on non-degenerate spin-braided fusion categories of dimension four.

In Section
4
, we study minimal non-degenerate extensions of the category of representations of a super-group
(\widetilde{G},z)
. In Subsection
4.3
, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a minimal non-degenerate extension of a fusion category. Additionally, in Corollary
4.9
, we prove that a certain group homomorphism between the minimal non-degenerate extensions of
{\operatorname{Rep}}(\widetilde{G},z)
and
{\operatorname{SVec}}
is surjective if and only if the super-group
(\widetilde{G},z)
is trivial. In Subsection
4.4
, we present some examples of braided fusion categories without minimal non-degenerate extensions. Continuing with the study of fermionic actions on pointed spin-braided fusion categories of rank four, we investigate their
H^{4}
obstruction.

Finally, in Appendix
5
, we recall some results on group cohomology that are necessary to understand some of the results in the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

We will briefly review the basic definitions and results of fusion categories and braided fusion categories that are necessary for defining fermionic actions and study minimal modular extensions. For comprehensive treatments, we refer to
[
DGNO10
]
and
[
EGNO15
]
.

2.1.
Fusion categories

A
fusion category
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear semisimple rigid tensor category
\mathcal{C}
with finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, finite-dimensional spaces of morphisms, and such that the unit object
\mathbf{1}
is simple. By a
fusion subcategory
of a fusion category, we mean a full tensor abelian subcategory.

A fusion category is called
pointed
if all its simple objects are invertible. For a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{pt}
the maximal pointed fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, consisting of all invertible objects and their direct sums.

We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
and by
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
the
Grothendieck ring
of
\mathcal{C}
. There exists a unique ring homomorphism
\operatorname{FPdim}:K_{0}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\operatorname{FPdim}(X)>0
for any
X\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})
(see
[
EGNO15
, Proposition 3.3.6]
). The
Frobenius-Perron dimension
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{X\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}\operatorname{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

2.2.
Braided fusion categories

A fusion category
\mathcal{B}
is called
braided
if it is endowed with a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}

\displaystyle:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X,

\displaystyle X,Y

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{B},

satisfying the hexagon axioms, see
[
JS93
]
. These axioms ensure that the braiding is compatible with the tensor structure of the category.

If
\mathcal{D}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
, the
centralizer
of
\mathcal{D}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
is defined as the full subcategory

C_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{D}):=\{Y\in\mathcal{B}:c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}={\operatorname{id}}_{X\otimes Y}\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{D}\}.

The
Müger’s center
of
\mathcal{B}
is the fusion subcategory
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B}):=C_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B})
, that is,

\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})=\{Y\in\mathcal{B}:c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}={\operatorname{id}}_{X\otimes Y},\ \text{for all }X\in\mathcal{B}\}.

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{B}
is called
symmetric
if
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{B}
, i.e., if
c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}={\operatorname{id}}_{X\otimes Y}
for each pair of objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{B}
.

Symmetric fusion categories are equivalent to one of the following two examples:

(a)

Tannakian categories
. The category
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
of finite dimensional complex representation of a finite group
G
, with standard braiding
c_{X,Y}(x\otimes y):=y\otimes x
for
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.

(b)

Super-tannakian categories
. A
finite super-group
is a pair
(G,z)
, where
G
is a finite group and
z
is a central element of order two. An irreducible representation of
G
is called
odd
if
z
acts as the scalar
-1
, and
even
if
z
acts as the identity. If the degree of a simple object
X
is denoted by
|X|\in\{0,1\}
, then the braiding of two simple objects
X
,
Y
is

c^{\prime}_{X,Y}(x\otimes y)=(-1)^{|X||Y|}y\otimes x.

The category
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
with the braiding
c^{\prime}
is called a
super-Tannakian
category, and will be denoted by
{\operatorname{Rep}}(G,z)
.

The super-tannakian category
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},1)
is called the category of
super-vector spaces
and will be denoted by
{\operatorname{SVec}}
. This category will play a central role in our study of fermionic actions.

In
[
Del02
]
, Deligne establishes that every symmetric fusion category is braided equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
or
\operatorname{Rep}(G,z)
for a unique finite group
G
or super group
(G,z)
.

A braided fusion category
(\mathcal{B},c)
is called
non-degenerate
if
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{B})\cong\operatorname{Vec}
, that is, its Müger center is trivial. For
spherical
braided fusion categories, non-degeneracy is equivalent to modularity, meaning the invertibility of the
S
-matrix (see
[
DGNO10
]
). This concept of non-degeneracy will be essential in our discussion of minimal modular extensions.

For any braided fusion category
\mathcal{B}
, there exists a canonical group homomorphism

(1)

\displaystyle\Xi:\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{B})\to\widehat{K_{0}(\mathcal{B})},

\displaystyle\Xi(X_{i})(X_{j})=c_{X_{j},X_{i}}\circ c_{X_{i},X_{j}},

where
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{B})
is the group of isomorphism classes of invertible objects in
\mathcal{B}
and
\widehat{K_{0}(\mathcal{B})}=\{f:K_{0}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}\mid f(X\otimes Y)=f(X)f(Y)\text{ for all }X,Y\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{B})\}
is the group of characters of the Grothendieck ring. When
\mathcal{B}
is non-degenerate,
\Xi
is an isomorphism (see
[
GN08
, Section 6.1]
), providing an important relationship between invertible objects and the group of tensor automorphisms of the identity functor.

2.3.
Drinfeld center of a fusion category

An important class of non-degenerate fusion categories can be constructed using the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
of a fusion category
(\mathcal{C},a,\mathbf{1})
, see
[
DGNO10
, Corollary 3.9]
. The center construction produces a non-degenerate braided fusion category
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
from any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

Objects of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
are pairs
(Z,\sigma_{-,Z})
, where
Z\in\mathcal{C}
and
\sigma_{-,Z}:-\otimes Z\to Z\otimes-
is a natural isomorphism such that the diagram

\textstyle{Z\otimes(X\otimes Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a_{Z,X,Y}}
\textstyle{(Z\otimes X)\otimes Y}
\textstyle{(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{X\otimes Y,Z}}
\scriptstyle{a_{X,Y,Z}}
\textstyle{(X\otimes Z)\otimes Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{X,Z}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{Y}}
\textstyle{X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{id}_{X}\otimes\sigma_{Y,Z}}
\textstyle{X\otimes(Z\otimes Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a^{-1}_{X,Z,Y}}

commutes for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{C}
.

The braided tensor structure on
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is defined as follows:

•

The tensor product is
(Y,\sigma_{-,Y})\otimes(Z,\sigma_{-,Z})=(Y\otimes Z,\sigma_{-,Y\otimes Z})
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,Y\otimes Z}

\displaystyle:X\otimes Y\otimes Z\to Y\otimes Z\otimes X,\qquad X\in\mathcal{C},

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,Y\otimes Z}

\displaystyle=a_{Y,Z,X}({\operatorname{id}}_{Y}\otimes\sigma_{X,Z})a_{Y,X,Z}^{-1}(\sigma_{X,Y}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{Z})a_{X,Y,Z}.

•

The braiding is given by the isomorphism
\sigma_{X,Y}
.

By
[
EGNO15
, Proposition 9.3.4]
, for any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
we have

\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}))=\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})^{2}.

For a braided fusion category
\mathcal{B}
, there is a canonical braided embedding functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B}),

\displaystyle X\mapsto(X,c_{-,X}).

This embedding will be important in our analysis of fermionic fusion categories and their modular extensions.

2.4.
Group actions on fusion categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. We denote by
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
the monoidal category where objects are tensor autoequivalences of
\mathcal{C}
, arrows are tensor natural isomorphisms, and the tensor product is the composition of tensor functors.

An
action
of a finite group
G
on
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal functor
*:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
, where
\underline{G}
denotes the discrete monoidal category with objects as elements of
G
and tensor product given by the multiplication in
G
.

Let
G
be a group acting on
\mathcal{C}
with action
*:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
. This action provides the following data:

•

Tensor functors
g_{*}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
for each
g\in G
,

•

Natural tensor isomorphisms
\phi(g,h):(gh)_{*}\to g_{*}\circ h_{*}
for all
g,h\in G
.

These satisfy coherence conditions that ensure the functors and natural transformations properly model the group structure (see, for example,
[
Tam01
, Section 2]
). An action in this sense is also called a categorical action, to distinguish it from the fermionic actions we will introduce later.

2.4.1.
Equivariantization

Given an action
*:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
of a finite group
G
on
\mathcal{C}
, the
G
-equivariantization
of
\mathcal{C}
is the fusion category
\mathcal{C}^{G}
defined as follows. An object in
\mathcal{C}^{G}
is a pair
(V,\tau)
, where
V
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
and
\tau
is a family of isomorphisms
\tau_{g}:g_{*}(V)\to V
,
g\in G
, such that

\tau_{gh}=\tau_{g}\circ g_{*}(\tau_{h})\circ\phi(g,h),

for all
g,h\in G
. A
G
-equivariant morphism
\sigma:(V,\tau)\to(W,\tau^{\prime})
between
G
-equivariant objects is a morphism
\sigma:V\to W
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\tau^{\prime}_{g}\circ g_{*}(\sigma)=\sigma\circ\tau_{g}
, for all
g\in G
.

The tensor product in
\mathcal{C}^{G}
is defined by

\displaystyle(V,\tau)\otimes(W,\tau^{\prime}):=(V\otimes W,\tau^{\prime\prime}),

where

\tau^{\prime\prime}_{g}=\tau_{g}\otimes\tau^{\prime}_{g}\circ\psi(g)_{V,W}^{-1},

and the unit object is
(\mathbf{1},\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf{1}})
.

2.4.2.
The de-equivariantization

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category and
{\operatorname{Rep}}(G)\subset\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
a Tannakian subcategory that embeds into
\mathcal{C}
via the forgetful functor
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
. The algebra
\mathcal{O}(G)
of functions on
G
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. The category of left
\mathcal{O}(G)
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category, called the de-equivariantization of
\mathcal{C}
by
{\operatorname{Rep}}(G)
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{G}
. For more details, see
[
DGNO10
]
. It follows from
[
DGNO10
, Lemma 3.11]
that

\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{G})=\frac{\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{|G|}.

The processes of equivariantization and de-equivariantization will be fundamental in our study of fermionic actions and minimal modular extensions.

3.
Actions of super-groups on fermionic fusion categories

In this section, we develop the theory of fermionic actions on fermionic fusion categories and establish their relationship with fusion categories over super-groups. We first introduce fermionic fusion categories and their morphisms, then define and study fermionic actions of super-groups.

3.1.
Fermionic fusion categories

We begin by defining fermionic fusion categories, which can be viewed as categories over the symmetric category of super vector spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. An object
(f,\sigma_{-,f})\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is called a
fermion
if
f\otimes f\cong\mathbf{1}
and
\sigma_{f,f}=-{\operatorname{id}}_{f\otimes f}
.

(a)

A
fermionic fusion category
is a pair
(\mathcal{C},(f,\sigma_{-,f}))
consisting of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
and a fermion
(f,\sigma_{-,f})\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.

(b)

A
spin-braided fusion category
is a pair
(\mathcal{B},f)
where
\mathcal{B}
is a braided fusion category and
(f,c_{-,f})\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})
is a fermion.

Remark 3.2
.

(a)

If
(f,\sigma_{-,f})
is a fermion, then
f
is not isomorphic to the unit object
\mathbf{1}
. This follows from the definition of the half-braiding, as
\sigma_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}={\operatorname{id}}_{\mathbf{1}}
.

(b)

If
(f,\sigma_{-,f})\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is a fermion, the fusion subcategory
\langle(f,\sigma_{-,f})\rangle
of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is braided equivalent to
{\operatorname{SVec}}
. Therefore, a fermionic fusion category
(\mathcal{C},(f,\sigma_{-,f}))
is precisely a fusion category over
{\operatorname{SVec}}
in the sense of
[
DGNO10
, Definition 4.16]
.

We now present several examples of fermionic fusion categories that will be important throughout this paper.

Example 3.3

(Fermionic pointed fusion categories)
.

According to
[
BGH+17
, Proposition 2.2]
, there is a correspondence between fermions in
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
and pairs
(f,\eta)
, where
\eta:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
satisfies:

(a)

f\in Z(G)
is of order two,

(b)

\frac{\eta(x)\eta(y)}{\eta(xy)}=\frac{w(f,x,y)w(x,y,f)}{w(x,f,y)}
for all
x,y\in G
,

(c)

\frac{w(x,f,f)w(f,x,f)}{w(x,f,f)}\eta(x)^{2}=1
for all

,

(d)

\eta(f)=-1
.

Example 3.4

(Ising categories as spin-braided fusion categories)
.

An Ising fusion category is a non-pointed fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension 4 (see
[
DGNO10
, Appendix B]
). These categories have three simple objects
\mathbf{1},f,\sigma
, with fusion rules:

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}=\mathbf{1}+f,

\displaystyle f^{2}=\mathbf{1},

\displaystyle f\sigma=\sigma f=\sigma.

The associativity constraints are given by the
F
-matrices

\displaystyle F_{\sigma\sigma\sigma}^{\sigma}=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle F_{f\sigma f}^{\sigma}=F_{\sigma f\sigma}^{f}=-1,

where
\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}
.

Ising fusion categories admit braided structures, and in all cases

is a fermion. An Ising braided fusion category is always non-degenerate and
\mathcal{I}_{pt}=\langle f\rangle\cong{\operatorname{SVec}}
. As proved in
[
DGNO10
, Appendix B]
, there are 8 equivalence classes of Ising braided fusion categories.

3.2.
Fermionic actions on fermionic fusion categories

Recall that a finite
super-group
is a pair
(G,z)
, where
G
is a finite group and

is a central element of order two. If
(\widetilde{G},z)
is a super-group, the exact sequence

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\langle z\rangle\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}/\langle z\rangle\longrightarrow 1,

is defined by a unique element

with the associated pair

, where

and

To define actions of super-groups, we first need to understand monoidal functors to
\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}({\operatorname{SVec}})

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a group. There is a canonical bijection between equivalence classes of monoidal functors

Proof.

Since every tensor autoequivalence of

is monoidally equivalent to the identity, there is a unique group homomorphism

, using the fact that
\widehat{K_{0}({\operatorname{SVec}})}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

.

3.3.

In this subsection, we describe in cohomological terms the obstruction to obtaining an action of a super-group
(G,\alpha)
on a fermionic fusion category.

Let
(\mathcal{C},(f,\sigma_{-,f}))
be a fermionic fusion category and
(G,\alpha)
be a super-group. Every fermionic action of
(G,\alpha)

Let

that realizes

Consider a categorical action

Let

(b)

.

(c)

The set of equivalence classes of
\alpha
-liftings of

is a torsor over

\operatorname{Ker}\Big(r_{*}:H^{2}(G,\widehat{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})})\to H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\Big).

Proof.

By
[
CGPW16
]
, the set of equivalence classes of liftings of
\rho
is a torsor over

. Hence, any other lifting has the form
\beta\triangleright\widetilde{\rho}
for a fixed lifting
\widetilde{\rho}
, and

(12)

\displaystyle\theta_{\beta\triangleright\widetilde{\rho}}=r_{*}(\beta)\cdot\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}},

where

is the map induced by the homomorphism of

only depends on
\rho
. If

\displaystyle O_{3}(\beta\triangleright\widetilde{\rho},\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}(\theta_{\beta\triangleright\widetilde{\rho}}/\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}(r_{*}(\beta)\cdot\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}/\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}\circ r_{*}(\beta)\cdot d_{2}(\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}/\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}(\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}/\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{3}(\widetilde{\rho},\alpha).

Hence, the element
O_{3}(\widetilde{\rho},\alpha)
does not depend on the lifting. Henceforth, we will denote

simply as

.

By definition,
\widetilde{\rho}
defines a fermionic action of
(G,\alpha)
on

if and only if

. If

.

If

is non-trivial and

-lifting, then
\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}=\alpha
and

. Conversely, if

, there exists

(since the sequence (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}/\alpha)^{-1}\cdot\theta_{\widetilde{\rho}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha.

on

.

∎

A super-group of the form

is called a

with

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
(G,\alpha)
be a super-group,
\rho:G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C},f)

This follows directly from Theorem

for some
\tau\in H^{2}(G,\widehat{K_{0}(\mathcal{C})})
.
∎

The following corollary characterizes which super-groups can act trivially on a fermionic fusion category.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},f)
be a fermionic fusion category and let

(a)

These examples illustrate how the group-theoretical interpretation helps us determine which super-groups can act fermionically on specific fusion categories, and under what conditions such actions exist.

3.5.
Fermionic actions on non-degenerate spin-braided fusion categories of rank four

In this subsection, we apply the results developed earlier to study fermionic actions on non-degenerate spin-braided fusion categories of dimension four. We first characterize fermionic actions on Ising modular categories, then extend our analysis to pointed spin-braided fusion categories of rank four.

3.5.1.
Fermionic actions on Ising modular categories

We begin by investigating which super-groups can act on Ising modular categories.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},f)
be a non-degenerate spin-braided category. The restriction map

Proof.

If

is a non-degenerate braided fusion category, there is a canonical isomorphism

given by
\Xi(X_{i})(X_{j})=c_{X_{j},X_{i}}\circ c_{X_{i},X_{j}}
(see
[
GN08
, Section 6.1]
).

For a non-degenerate spin-braided category
(\mathcal{B},f)
, consider the map
s
defined by the composition:

\textstyle{\widehat{K_{0}(\mathcal{B})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{B})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Xi}

This lemma provides a criterion for determining when the obstruction theory for fermionic actions simplifies. We now apply it to Ising categories.

Proposition 3.25
.

Only trivial super-groups
(G,\alpha\equiv 0)
act fermionically on spin-braided Ising categories. Moreover, the equivalence classes of fermionic actions of a group

on an Ising category are in bijective correspondence with elements of

Proof.

Ising categories are particular cases of Tambara-Yamagami categories. By

is monoidally equivalent to the identity functor. Thus,

By Lemma

For a trivial super-group

is equivalent to the identity, we have

∎

4.
Minimal non-degenerate extensions of super-groups

In this section, we study minimal non-degenerate extensions of braided fusion categories, with special emphasis on super-groups. We begin by reviewing relevant concepts about module categories and the Brauer-Picard group, then establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of minimal non-degenerate extensions.

4.1.
Preliminaries on module categories and Brauer-Picard groups

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. A left
\mathcal{C}

4.2.

The concept of (minimal) modular extension of a braided fusion category was introduced by Müger in

Definition 4.3

is slightly degenerate,
\mathcal{S}
has a canonical spin-braided structure. We have
{\operatorname{Rep}}(\widetilde{G},z)\subset\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}^{G}
, so by Corollary
3.14
,
(\widetilde{G},z)
acts by braided autoequivalences on
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{B}_{G}
is
G
-stable, and the restriction coincides with the canonical action of
G
on
\mathcal{B}_{G}
. The vanishing of

follows from part (

) and the fact that
\mathcal{M}
is a minimal non-degenerate extension of
\mathcal{S}^{G}
.

Conversely, assume conditions (

), (
2b
), and (

) hold. By condition (

),
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}^{G}
as braided fusion categories. By condition (

),
\mathcal{S}^{G}
has a minimal non-degenerate extension
\mathcal{M}
. By condition (

),

\displaystyle\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{M})

∎

If

is a central Tannakian subcategory of a braided fusion category

, the de-equivariantization defines a map

Corollary 4.9
.

Let

be a super-group. The map

Proof.

If

, there exists

Conversely, if

∎

Let
G
be a finite group and

\widetilde{\mu}:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B})}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}(g)={\operatorname{id}}_{\mathcal{B}},

\displaystyle\phi(g,h)=\Xi(\mu(g,h)),

\displaystyle O_{4}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})

Using this corollary, we can construct examples of modularizable braided fusion categories without minimal non-degenerate extensions. For a direct product of groups

The following example of a pre-modular category without minimal modular extensions was first discovered by Drinfeld in unpublished notes:

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
(\omega,c)\in Z^{3}_{ab}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
be the non-degenerate abelian 3-cocycle given by

\displaystyle c(x,y)=\frac{xy}{4},

\displaystyle\omega(x,y,z)=\frac{xyz}{2},

and

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is defined using (
19

Proof.

We will follow and use the notation and results of Appendix
5
.
Let

After simplification, the 4-cocycle

that if the cohomology class of

∎

4.5.
H^{4}
-obstruction for fermionic pointed categories of rank four

We have studied the fermionic actions of a super-group
(G,\alpha)
and the
\alpha
-liftings of
\rho:G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)
in terms of cohomology. In this section, we present concrete formulas for the fourth obstruction to the existence of a map

\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}:\underline{\underline{G}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B})}}\cong\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{B})}}

when
\mathcal{B}
is a non-degenerate spin-braided fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension four (see Examples
3.12
and
3.4
).

This topic is important because there is a correspondence between braided crossed
G
-extensions of
\mathcal{B}
and 3-homomorphisms
\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}:\underline{\underline{G}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{B})}}
, which provides information about the minimal non-degenerate extensions of
\mathcal{B}
.

First, consider the case
\rho:\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)
. Any global action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on a modular extension of
{\operatorname{SVec}}
has a lifting
\widetilde{\rho}
and a lifting to a 3-homomorphism
\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}:\underline{\underline{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B})}}
. This is because the obstruction to lifting to a 3-homomorphism lies in
H^{4}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
.

On the other hand, given
\widetilde{\widetilde{\xi}}:\underline{\underline{G}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)}}\subseteq\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{B})}}
, it can be factorized through a unique 3-homomorphism
\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}:\underline{\underline{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)}}
. We use this information to describe in cohomological terms the 3-homomorphisms
\widetilde{\widetilde{\xi}}
whose truncation
\widetilde{\xi}:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)}
describes a fermionic action of the super-group
(G,\alpha)
.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},f)
be a spin-braided pointed fusion category of rank four and
G
a finite group with a group homomorphism
\xi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)
. Then
\xi
can be extended to a categorical action and to a 3-homomorphism
\widetilde{\widetilde{\xi}}:\underline{\underline{G}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{B})}}
.

Proof.

Consider
\xi
a non-trivial homomorphism. Since
\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
\xi
is surjective and can be factorized through a non-trivial homomorphism
\rho:\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)
:

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\xi}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}

The homomorphism
\rho
can be lifted to a categorical action
\widetilde{\rho}
and to a 3-homomorphism
\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}
. Defining a categorical action by
\widetilde{\xi}=\widetilde{\rho}\circ\pi
, we obtain a lifting of
\xi
. Similarly, taking
\widetilde{\widetilde{\xi}}=\widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}\circ\pi
, we obtain a 3-homomorphism that lifts
\xi
.

The case of a trivial homomorphism is straightforward.
∎

By Theorem
4.13
, a group homomorphism
\xi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{B},f)
has a lifting to a 3-homomorphism whose truncation is a fermionic action of the trivial super-group
(G,\alpha=0)
. The following theorem characterizes all 3-homomorphisms whose truncation induces a fermionic action:

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
(G,\alpha)
be a super-group and
(\mathcal{B},f)
a spin-braided fusion category where
\mathcal{B}
is pointed with rank four. Given
\widetilde{\widetilde{\xi}}:\underline{\underline{G}}\to\underline{\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{B})}}
with an action of
(G,\alpha)
on
(\mathcal{B},f)
given by the truncation
\widetilde{\xi}
with data
(g_{*},\psi^{g},\vartheta_{g,h})_{g,h\in G}
. If
\mu\in Z^{2}_{\xi}(G,\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{B}))
, then the action
\mu\triangleright\widetilde{\xi}
can be extended to a 3-homomorphism if and only if the 4-cocycle
O_{4}(\widetilde{\xi},\mu)
defined by (
18
) is trivial in cohomology.

Proof.

If
\mu\in H^{2}_{\xi}(G,\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{B}))
, then
\mu
defines a new monoidal functor
\mu\triangleright\widetilde{\xi}
. According to
[
CGPW16
, Proposition 9]
, this tensor functor has a lifting to a 3-homomorphism if and only if the 4-cocycle
O_{4}(\widetilde{\xi},\mu)
defined by (
18
) is cohomologically trivial.
∎

5.
Appendix A. Group Cohomology

In Section
4.4
, we used Lyndon’s normalization of standard cocycles in group cohomology of a direct product of groups
[
Lyn48
]
. In this appendix, we provide an updated account of Lyndon’s normalization for completeness.

5.1.
Group cohomology fundamentals

Let
G
be a group and
A
a
G
-module. The
n^{th}
-
cohomology group
of
G
with coefficients in
A
is defined as

H^{n}(G,A):=\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\mathbb{Z},A).

For an arbitrary group, the bar resolution is most commonly used. This resolution consists of free abelian groups

B_{n}=\bigoplus_{g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}\in G}\mathbb{Z}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

with
G
-action given by

g\cdot(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})=(gg_{0},\ldots,gg_{n}).

The differential and chain contraction
s
(which satisfies
\partial s+s\partial={\operatorname{id}}
) are defined by

\displaystyle\partial(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(g_{0},\ldots,\widehat{g_{i}},\ldots,g_{n}),

\displaystyle s(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=(1,g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}),

where
\widehat{g_{i}}
indicates that the element
g_{i}
is omitted.

A
\mathbb{Z}G
-basis of
B_{n}
is given by

|g_{1}|g_{2}|\ldots|g_{n}|=(1,g_{1},g_{1}g_{2},\ldots,g_{1}\ldots g_{n}),

and the differential in terms of this basis becomes

\displaystyle\partial(|g_{1}|g_{2}|\ldots|g_{n}|)

\displaystyle=g_{1}|g_{2}|\ldots|g_{n}|+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}|g_{1}|\ldots|g_{i}g_{i+1}|\ldots|g_{n}|

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{n}|g_{1}|\ldots|g_{n-1}|.

The groups
\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(B_{n},A)
can be canonically identified with
C^{n}(G,A)
, the group of functions
f:G^{n}\to A
. Under this identification, the coboundary operator has the familiar formula:

\displaystyle\partial(f)(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n+1})

\displaystyle=g_{1}\cdot f(g_{2},\ldots,g_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{n+1}f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}).

5.2.
Cohomology of finite cyclic groups

Let
C_{m}
be the cyclic group of order
m
generated by
\sigma
. A free resolution of
\mathbb{Z}
as a
C_{m}
-module is given by the sequence

\cdots\xrightarrow{N}\mathbb{Z}C_{m}\xrightarrow{\sigma-1}\mathbb{Z}C_{m}\xrightarrow{N}\mathbb{Z}C_{m}\xrightarrow{\sigma-1}\mathbb{Z}C_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}\to 0,

where
N=1+\sigma+\sigma^{2}+\cdots+\sigma^{m-1}
is the norm element. This leads to an explicit description of the cohomology groups:

\displaystyle H^{n}(C_{m},A)=\begin{cases}\{a\in A:Na=0\}/(\sigma-1)A,&\text{if }n=1,3,5,\ldots\\
A^{C_{m}}/NA,&\text{if }n=2,4,6,\ldots\end{cases}

A chain map from the cyclic resolution to the bar resolution can be constructed using cup products. The 2-cocycle
u\in Z^{2}(C_{m},\mathbb{Z})
defined by

\displaystyle u(x,y)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x+y<m\\
1,&\text{if }x+y\geq m\end{cases}

is a representative of the generator of
H^{2}(C_{m},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

If
A
is a
C_{m}
-module and
a\in A
such that
Na=0
, then
f_{a}(\sigma^{i})=a+\sigma a+\cdots+\sigma^{i-1}a
defines a representative 1-cocycle with respect to the bar resolution. More generally,

f_{a}\cup u^{\cup i}\in Z^{2i+1}(C_{m},A)

defines a representative
(2i+1)
-cocycle with respect to the bar resolution.

For example, the 3-cocycle

(21)

\displaystyle\alpha(\sigma^{i},\sigma^{j},\sigma^{k})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i+j<m\\
\frac{1}{m},&\text{if }i+j\geq m\end{cases}

represents a generator of
H^{3}(C_{m},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

5.3.
Cohomology of a direct product

Let
A
and
B
be groups, and let
D_{A}
and
D_{B}
be free resolutions of
\mathbb{Z}
as
A
-module and
B
-module, respectively. The tensor product bicomplex
D_{A}\otimes D_{B}
is defined as the double complex where
(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})_{p,q}:=D_{A}^{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}D_{B}^{q}
with differentials
\partial_{A}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}
and
{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\partial_{B}
.

Each
(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})_{p,q}
is a free
A\times B
-module with action
(a,b)\cdot(m\otimes n)=am\otimes bn
for all
(a,b)\in A\times B
and
m\otimes n\in(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})_{p,q}
. By the Künneth formula, the total complex

\operatorname{Tot}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})_{n}:=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})^{p,q}

with total differential
\partial^{\operatorname{Tot}}=\partial_{A}+(-1)^{p}\partial_{B}
is a free resolution of
\mathbb{Z}
as an
A\times B
-module.

The horizontal filtration of
\operatorname{Tot}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})
, defined by

\mathfrak{F}_{p}(\operatorname{Tot}^{n}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B}))=\bigoplus_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n,\,n_{1}\leq p}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})^{n_{1},n_{2}},

induces a filtration in group cohomology

H^{n}=\mathfrak{F}_{0}(H^{n})\supset\mathfrak{F}_{1}(H^{n})\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{F}_{n-1}(H^{n})\supset\mathfrak{F}_{n}(H^{n})\supset 0,

where
H^{n}=H^{n}(A\times B,M)
. The inclusion

(D_{A}\otimes D_{B})^{k,n-k}\to\mathfrak{F}_{k}(\operatorname{Tot}^{n}(D_{A}\otimes D_{B}))

induces a group homomorphism

P_{k}:\operatorname{gr}_{k}(H^{n}(A\times B,M))\to H^{k}(A,H^{n-k}(B,M)),

as shown in
[
Lyn48
, Theorem 4]
.

5.3.1.
Lyndon’s algorithm

When we use the bar resolutions for both
A
and
B
, we obtain the double complex

C^{p,q}:=\operatorname{Map}(A^{p}\times B^{q},K)

with differentials

\displaystyle\partial_{A}

\displaystyle:C^{p}(A,C^{q}(B,K))\to C^{p+1}(A,C^{q}(B,K)),

\displaystyle\partial_{B}

\displaystyle:C^{q}(B,C^{p}(A,K))\to C^{q+1}(B,C^{p}(A,K)),

where we naturally identify
C^{p,q}
with
C^{p}(A,C^{q}(B,K))
and
C^{q}(B,C^{p}(A,K))
.

The cohomology of the total complex
\operatorname{Tot}(C):=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}C^{p,q}
with total differential
\partial^{\operatorname{Tot}}=\partial_{A}+(-1)^{p}\partial_{B}
computes the group cohomology
H^{n}(A\times B,M)
.

Given an
n
-cocycle
\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\alpha_{p,q}
of the total complex, the associated
n
-cocycle of the bar resolution is
\sum_{p+q=n}\alpha_{p,q}
, meaning

\sum_{p+q=n}\alpha_{p,q}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=\sum_{p+q=n}\alpha_{p,q}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},b_{p+1},\ldots,b_{n}),

where we adopt the convention that each element
g_{i}\in A\times B
is written as
g_{i}=a_{i}b_{i}
.

Let
(h,k)
be a pair of integers such that
0\leq h<k<n
, and consider the lexicographical ordering where
(h^{\prime},k^{\prime})<(h,k)
if
h^{\prime}<h
or if
h^{\prime}=h
and
k^{\prime}<k
. By
[
Lyn48
, Lemma 5.4]
, if

(22)

f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h},b_{h+1},\ldots,b_{k},a_{k+1},g_{k+2},\ldots,g_{n})=0

for all pairs
(h,k)
, then
\bigoplus_{p+q=n}f_{p,q}\in\operatorname{Tot}^{n}(C)
, where
f_{p,q}:=f|_{A^{p}\times B^{q}}
, and

f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=\sum_{p+q=n}f_{p,q}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},b_{p+1},\ldots,b_{n}).

Lyndon’s algorithm enables us to transform a standard
n
-cocycle
f\in Z^{n}(A\times B,M)
into a standard
n
-cocycle
f^{\prime}\in Z^{n}(A\times B,M)
that satisfies condition (
22
) for all pairs
(h,k)
. The procedure is as follows:

Let
f
be a standard
n
-cocycle such that

f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h^{\prime}},b_{h^{\prime}+1},\ldots,b_{k^{\prime}},a_{k^{\prime}+1},g_{k^{\prime}+2},\ldots,g_{n})=0

for all
(h^{\prime},k^{\prime})<(h,k)
.

We replace
f
by
f^{\prime}=f+(-1)^{k}\partial(p)
, where the
(n-1)
-cochain
p
is defined by

p(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1}):=\\
f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h},b_{h+1},\ldots,b_{k},a_{h+1}a_{h+2}\cdots a_{k},g_{k+1},\ldots,g_{n-1}).

This modification ensures that

f^{\prime}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h^{\prime}},b_{h^{\prime}+1},\ldots,b_{k^{\prime}},a_{k^{\prime}+1},g_{k^{\prime}+2},\ldots,g_{n})=0

for all
(h^{\prime},k^{\prime})\leq(h,k)
, allowing us to systematically ”normalize” the cocycle through an iterative process.

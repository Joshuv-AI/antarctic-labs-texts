A new unit-bimodal distribution based on correlated Birnbaum-Saunders random variables
Roberto Vila, Helton Saulo, Felipe Quintino, Peter Zörnig

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a new distribution over the unit interval which can be characterized as a ratio of the type Z = d Y / ( X + Y ) Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Y/(X+Y) where X X and Y Y are two correlated Birnbaum-Saunders random variables. The density of Z Z may be unimodal or bimodal. Simple expressions for the cumulative distribution function, moment-generating function and moments are obtained. Moreover, the stress-strength probability between X X and Y Y is calculated explicitly in the symmetric case, that is, when the respective scale parameters are equal. Two applications of the ratio distribution are discussed.



Keywords.
Unit distribution, Type I ratio, Maximum likelihood method, Monte Carlo simulation, R+ software.

Introduction

Traditionally, the activities in statistical distribution theory have been concentrated on distributions with unbounded support. Only in recent years, attention has been devoted to the construction of distributions with bounded support. Bounded data have applications in economics, finance, biology and medicine among others, see e.g., the introductory remarks in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
. Such data arise naturally in the form of rates and proportions. Some of the most prominent bounded distributions are the beta distribution and their generalizations and the distributions of Kumaraswamy and Topp-Leone.

A natural way to create a model for bounded data is to transform one or two unbounded random variables in a bounded variable
Z
. For example,
Z=X/(X+1)
,
Z=\exp(-X)
,

Z=\tanh(X)
,
Z=\min\{X,Y\}/\max\{X,Y\}
,
Z=(X/Y)\mathds{1}_{\{X<Y\}}
and
Z=X/(X+Y)
have the support
(0,1)
, when
X
and
Y
are positive variables. In applications of the last model it is usually assumed that
X
and
Y
are independent. We consider some specific approaches using these transformations or more complicated ones.

These techniques are simple transformations of one or two variables:

[
25
]
derive a bimodal beta distribution, applying a quadratic transformation technique due to Elal-Olivero to the beta distribution.

[
2
]
apply an approach of Marshall and Olkin to the reduced Kies distribution.

[
9
]
propose a general framework in which a monotone function is applied to a single variable.

[
28
]
applies a procedure due to
[
10
]
to a uniformly distributed variable, resulting in a three-parameter family of distributions over the unit interval.
The authors in
[
27
]
(unit log) use the approach
Z=X/(X+Y)
where
X
and
Y
are the components of a two-dimensional random vector, following a bivariate log-symmetric distribution. In this model,
X
and
Y
may be correlated.

The distribution of ratios of the latter type is of great importance in practice, for example in modeling COVID-19-related death rates, see
[
6
]
.

On the other hand, the Birnbaum-Saunders (BS) fatigue-life model, introduced by
[
4
]
, is based on renewal theory principles. This model focuses on the number of cycles needed to exceed a critical threshold and cause fatigue crack propagation. In the same year,
[
5
]
provided maximum likelihood estimates for the BS model parameters. Later, Desmond (1985)
[
11
]
, in his work on stochastic models of failure in random environments, offered an alternative derivation of the distribution using a biological model and relaxed several of the assumptions made by
[
4
]
. The BS distribution has been extensively studied and applied across various fields, including business, industry, economics, finance, management, engineering, and medical sciences. For a review of recent work on the BS distribution, see
[
24
]
and the references therein.
Bivariate (and multivariate) versions of the BS distribution have also been explored.
[
16
]
introduced the bivariate BS distribution with five parameters using the bivariate normal distribution function.
[
15
]
revisited the bivariate BS distribution and discussed several new properties.
[
24
]
introduced a mean-based bivariate BS distribution, demonstrating that the mean is one of its parameters.

In the present paper we propose the distribution of a random variable of the type
Z=Y/(X+Y)
, where
X
and
Y
are two (possibly correlated) Birnbaum-Saunders random variables. The article is structured as follows.
In Section

Birnbaum-Saunders model
, which we name by type 1 and denote by UBBS1 to differentiate from the unit-bimodal Birnbaum-Saunders (UBBS) distribution studied in
[
18
]
,
and some of its forms are outlined in graphs. Note that bimodality in the model is observed as a function of its shape and correlation parameters.
For a unit Birnbaum-Saunders model based on the transformation
Z=\exp(-X)
, with
X
having a BS distribution, see
[
19
,
20
]
.
Since the variable
Z
in
[
19
,
20
]
exhibits decreasing, upside down bathtub and bathtub shaped density,
this model is not suitable to capture bimodal pattern in data.

In Section
3
,
we show one of the main properties of the UBBS1 model, that is, the corresponding random variable originally comes from
a type I ratio
[
13
,
3
]
of two variables following a bivariate BS distribution.

Also in this section, we derive the cumulative distribution function (CDF), which is used to
obtain simple expressions of the moment-generating function and moments, and to
explicitly calculate the stress-strength probability in the case of the presence of correlation and equality of scale parameters.
Finally, the maximum likelihood and maximum product of spacings methods for parameter estimation are presented. In Section
4

In Section

The first data set presents unimodal characteristics, and in this case, the new UBBS1 model proved to be more appropriate than the Beta and UBBS models (from
[
18
]
). The second data set has bimodal characteristics and was previously modeled by
[
18
]
using the UBBS model. Now, we show that the UBBS1 model also provides a good fit for this data.

Finally, in Section
6

Note that the equation

Characterization and estimation

. Since
Z
has unitary support it is clear that the moment-generating function (MGF), denoted by
M_{Z}(t)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(tZ)]
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and the (raw) moments, denoted by
\mu_{n}=\mathbb{E}(Z^{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are finite. In particular,
Z
has a finite MGF
M_{Z}(t)

in some open interval containing
0
, then it holds
M_{Z}^{(n)}(t)\big|_{t=0}=\mu_{n}
, and
M_{Z}(t)

may be expanded in a power series about
0
as follows

\displaystyle M_{Z}(t)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mu_{n}\over n!}\,t^{n}.

On the other hand, by using the well-known formula
\mathbb{E}(X^{p})=p\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(X>x){\rm d}x
,
X>0
,
p>0
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=n\int_{0}^{1}z^{n-1}[1-F_{Z}(z;\bm{\theta}_{\rho})]{\rm d}z,

where
F_{Z}(z;\bm{\theta}_{\rho})
is as given in (
3.2
). Hence, from (
3.2
),

\mu_{n}={1\over 2}+{n\over 2}\int_{0}^{1}z^{n-1}\left[{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}{\rm erf}\left\{{\big(\scriptstyle{\alpha_{2}\over\alpha_{1}}\sqrt{s\beta_{2}\over\beta_{1}}-\rho\big)\over\scriptstyle\sqrt{2(1-\rho^{2})}}\,w\right.\right.\\[2.84544pt]
+\left.\left.{\sqrt{2}(s\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})\over\alpha_{1}\sqrt{s\beta_{1}\beta_{2}(1-\rho^{2})}}\,{1\over\alpha_{2}w+\sqrt{(\alpha_{2}w)^{2}+4}}\right\}\phi(w){\rm d}w\right]{\rm d}z.

(19)

In the special case that
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
, we have
\mu_{1}=1/2
(see Remark
2.1
). In general, the integral in (
19
) cannot be solved analytically,
so the use of numerical method is essential for its respective calculation.

3.4
The stress-strength probability

In what follows we explicitly calculate the stress-strength probability
R\equiv\mathbb{P}(X<Y)
in the case where
(X,Y)^{\top}\sim{\rm BS}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})
, that is,

X
and
Y
can admit correlation.

Indeed, by (
11
), we have

\displaystyle R=\mathbb{P}\left({X\over Y}<1\right)=1-\mathbb{P}\left({X\over Y}\geqslant 1\right)=1-F_{Z}(z;\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\Big|_{z=1/2}.

Hence, as
z=1/2
if and only if
s=1
, by using (
3.2
),
we get

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}R={1\over 2}+{1\over 2}{\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}}{\rm erf}\left\{{{\left({\alpha_{2}\over\alpha_{1}}\sqrt{\beta_{2}\over\beta_{1}}-\rho\right)\over\sqrt{2(1-\rho^{2})}}\,w+{\sqrt{2}(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})\over\alpha_{1}\sqrt{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}(1-\rho^{2})}}\,{1\over\alpha_{2}w+\sqrt{(\alpha_{2}w)^{2}+4}}}\right\}\phi(w){\rm d}w.\end{array}

In the particular case
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
, from (
3.4
), the stress-strength probability
R
is simply written as

\displaystyle R={1\over 2}+{1\over 2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm erf}\left\{\dfrac{\left({\alpha_{2}\over\alpha_{1}}-{\rho}\right)}{\sqrt{2(1-\rho^{2})}}\,w\right\}\phi(w){\rm d}w={1\over 2},\quad\forall\rho\in(-1,1),

where in the last equality we have used the fact that erf
(x)
and
\phi
are odd and even functions, respectively.

To our knowledge, in this setting (
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
), identity
R=1/2
was known only for the special case in which
X
and
Y
are independent (that is,
\rho=0
); see, e.g.
[
24
]
and
[
22
]
.

3.4.1
Maximum likelihood estimation

Let
\{Z_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
be a univariate random sample of size
n
from the UBS
(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})

distribution with PDF as in (
2
), and let
z_{i}
be the sample observation of
Z_{i}
. Then,
the log-likelihood function for
\bm{\theta}_{\rho}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\rho)^{\top}
,
\alpha_{1}>0,\alpha_{2}>0,\beta_{1}>0,\beta_{2}>0
and
\rho\in(-1,1)
, is given by (without additive constant)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})&={n\over 1-\rho^{2}}\left({1\over\alpha_{1}^{2}}+{1\over\alpha_{2}^{2}}\right)-n\log\left(4\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\right)-{\rho\over\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(1-\rho^{2})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\beta_{2}s_{i}\over\beta_{1}}+\sqrt{\beta_{1}\over\beta_{2}s_{i}}\right)\\[11.38092pt]
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\Bigg\{\left(\sqrt{\beta_{2}s_{i}\over\beta_{1}}+\sqrt{\beta_{1}\over\beta_{2}s_{i}}\right)K_{0}\left({\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\right)+\left[\sqrt{(v_{\rho,i}/u_{\rho,i})s_{i}\over\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\over(v_{\rho,i}/u_{\rho,i})s_{i}}\,\right]K_{1}\left({\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}\over 1-\rho^{2}}\right)\Bigg\},\end{array}

where

\displaystyle s_{i}={1\over z_{i}}-1,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle u_{\rho,i}\equiv{s_{i}\over\alpha_{1}^{2}\beta_{1}}+{1\over\alpha_{2}^{2}\beta_{2}}-{2\rho\over\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{s_{i}\over\beta_{1}\beta_{2}},\quad v_{\rho,i}\equiv{\beta_{1}\over\alpha_{1}^{2}s_{i}}+{\beta_{2}\over\alpha_{2}^{2}}-{2\rho\over\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\over s_{i}},

and

K_{\lambda}(x)
,
x>0
, is the modified Bessel function of the third kind defined in (
4
).

The necessary conditions for the
occurrence of a maximum (or a minimum) are

\displaystyle{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\alpha_{1}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\alpha_{2}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\beta_{1}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\beta_{2}}=0,\quad{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\rho}=0,

known as the likelihood equations, with

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\alpha_{1}}=-\frac{2n}{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\alpha_{1}^{3}}-\frac{n}{\alpha_{1}}+\frac{\rho}{\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}s_{i}}{\beta_{1}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}s_{i}}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\partial G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})\over\partial\alpha_{1}}\over G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})},\\[11.38092pt]
&{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\alpha_{2}}=-\frac{2n}{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\alpha_{2}^{3}}-\frac{n}{\alpha_{2}}+\frac{\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{2}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}s_{i}}{\beta_{1}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}s_{i}}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\partial G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})\over\partial\alpha_{2}}\over G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})},\\[11.38092pt]
&{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\beta_{1}}=-\frac{\rho}{2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}s_{i}}}-\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}s_{i}}{\beta_{1}^{3}}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\partial G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})\over\partial\beta_{1}}\over G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})},\\[11.38092pt]
&{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\beta_{2}}=-\frac{\rho}{2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}s_{i}}}-\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}^{3}s_{i}^{3}}}\right)s_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\partial\log G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})\over\partial\beta_{2}}\over G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})},\\[11.38092pt]
&{\partial\ell(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\over\partial\rho}=\frac{2n\rho}{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}^{2}}\right)-\frac{n\rho}{1-\rho^{2}}-\frac{1+\rho^{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}s_{i}}{\beta_{1}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}s_{i}}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\partial G\left(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i}\right)\over\partial\rho}\over G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})},\end{array}

where we denote

\begin{array}[]{lll}G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})&=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}s_{i}}{\beta_{1}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}s_{i}}}\right)K_{0}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)+\left[\sqrt{\frac{(v_{\rho,i}/u_{\rho,i})s_{i}}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{(v_{\rho,i}/u_{\rho,i})s_{i}}}\,\right]K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right),\end{array}

and its partial derivatives are obtained using the known fact that
\partial K_{\nu}(x)/\partial x=-K_{\nu-1}(x)-(\nu/x)K_{\nu}(x)
.

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}G\left(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i}\right)&=\beta_{2}s_{i}\,a^{\prime}\left(\beta_{2}s_{i};{1\over 2},\beta_{1}\right)K_{0}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)+{v_{\rho,i}\over u_{\rho,i}}\,s_{i}\,a^{\prime}\left({v_{\rho,i}\over u_{\rho,i}}\,s_{i};{1\over 2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\right)K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right),\end{array}

where
a^{\prime}(t,\alpha,\beta)
is given in (
13
).

We require additional partial derivatives of
G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho},s_{i})
. Essentially, the product and chain rules are employed for deriving the subsequent functions:

(a)

Partial derivatives with respect to
\alpha_{1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial v_{\rho,i}}{\partial\alpha_{1}}=-\frac{2\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{3}s_{i}}+\frac{2\rho}{\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{s_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial u_{\rho,i}}{\partial\alpha_{1}}=-\frac{2s_{i}}{\alpha_{1}^{3}\beta_{1}}+\frac{2\rho}{\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{s_{i}}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}};\end{array}

(b)

Partial derivatives with respect to
\alpha_{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial v_{\rho,i}}{\partial\alpha_{2}}=-\frac{2\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}^{3}}+\frac{2\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{s_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial u_{\rho,i}}{\partial\alpha_{2}}=-\frac{2}{\alpha_{2}^{3}\beta_{2}}+\frac{2\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{s_{i}}};\end{array}

(c)

Partial derivatives with respect to
\beta_{1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial v_{\rho,i}}{\partial\beta_{1}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{2}s_{i}}-\frac{\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}s_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial u_{\rho,i}}{\partial\beta_{1}}=-{s_{i}\over\alpha_{1}^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}}+{\rho\over\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{s_{i}\over\beta_{1}^{3}\beta_{2}};\end{array}

(d)

Partial derivatives with respect to
\beta_{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial v_{\rho,i}}{\partial\beta_{2}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}s_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial u_{\rho,i}}{\partial\beta_{2}}=-\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}^{2}\beta_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\rho}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{s_{i}}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}^{3}}};\end{array}

(e)

Partial derivatives with respect to
\rho
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial v_{\rho,i}}{\partial\rho}=-\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{s_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial u_{\rho,i}}{\partial\rho}=-\frac{2}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{s_{i}}{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}}.\end{array}

Finally, for
\theta_{k}\in\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\rho\right\},
we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}K_{0}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)=-K_{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right),\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)=\left[-K_{0}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)-\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)^{-1}K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right)\right]\mskip-3.0mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{u_{\rho,i}v_{\rho,i}}}{1-\rho^{2}}\right).\end{array}

No closed-form solution to the maximization problem is available. So, the maximum likelihood estimator of
\bm{\theta}_{\rho}
can only be obtained via numerical optimization.

3.5
Maximum product of spacings method

The maximum product of spacings (MPS) method was introduced by
[
7
,
8
]
and
[
23
]
as a different approach to estimating parameters of continuous univariate distributions compared to maximum likelihood estimation. Let
\{Z_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
be a univariate random sample of size
n
from the
UBBS1
(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})

distribution with PDF as in (
2
), and let
z_{i}
be the sample observation of
Z_{i}
. Then, the uniform spacings of
\{z_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=F_{Z}(z_{i:n};\bm{\theta}_{\rho})-F_{Z}(z_{i-1:n};\bm{\theta}_{\rho}),\quad 1,\ldots,n,

with
F_{Z}(z_{0:n};\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=0
,
F_{Z}(z_{n+1:n};\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=1
,
and
\Delta_{0}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})+\Delta_{1}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})+\cdots+\Delta_{n+1}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=1
.

The maximum product of spacings estimator,
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}_{\rho}
, is obtained by maximizing the geometric mean of the spacings

\displaystyle G(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}\Delta_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}},

or, equivalently, maximizing the function

\displaystyle H(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})=\frac{1}{1+n}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\log(\Delta_{i}(\bm{\theta}_{\rho})).

4
Simulation study

In this section, we carry out a Monte Carlo simulation study for evaluating the performance of the above described
maximum likelihood and maximum product of spacings methods
for the
UBBS1
distribution (
2
). The simulation scenario considers the following setup: 300 Monte Carlo replications, sample size
n\in\{100,200,400,800\}
, and vector of true parameters
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})^{\top}=(0.5,0,5,1.0,1.0)^{\top}
,
\rho\in\{0.10,0.25,0.5,0.75\}
.
A generator of random numbers for the UBBS1 distribution is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

Generator of random numbers from a UBBS1 distribution

1:

Generate
n
random numbers from the bivariate normal distribution,

\rho&1\\

, as

•

Generate a matrix
\bm{W}
of size
n\times 2
containing
2n
random numbers from
\text{N}(0,1)
.

•

Compute the decomposition
\bm{\Sigma}=\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{Q}
.

•

Apply the transformation
\bm{X}=\bm{W}\bm{Q}+\bm{J}\bm{\mu}^{\top}
, where
\bm{J}

•

Obtain the matrix
\bm{X}=(X_{1},X_{2})^{\top}
of size

.

2:

n
random numbers from

n

:

5
Application

We now give two applications using real data previously analyzed in the literature. We study the validity of the new model for the data sets and show that
UBBS1
distribution presents good fits in both cases.

5.1
Income-consumption data

In order to evaluate the new probabilistic model, a public data set of income and consumption data was used.
The data (available at
https://www.bancaditalia.it/statistiche/tematiche/indagini-famiglie-imprese/bilanci-famiglie/documentazione/ricerca/ricerca.html?min_anno_pubblicazione=2008&max_anno_pubblicazione=2008
, accessed on July 22, 2024) comes from the Bank of Italy’s Survey on Household Income and Wealth from the year 2008.

The income comprises payroll income, pensions and net transfers, net self-employment income, and property income.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have introduced a new unit distribution. Its generation as a ratio of possibly dependent Birnbaum-Saunders random variables has been discussed in detail. As a more theoretical part, we have derived the cumulative distribution function
to then obtain simple expressions of the moment-generating function, moments
and the stress-strength probability. As practical issues, we presented maximum product of spacings estimators and applications to income-consumption and body mass data sets.

The research could be continued in several directions. One could investigate
the entropy of the distribution and use the moments formulas in (
19
)
to
try to express shape characteristics like skewness and kurtosis.
Instead of using the ratio
Y/(X+Y)
one could explore other construction principles like those mentioned in the introduction to obtain a unit distribution. Finally, one could use a bivariate distribution other than the Birnbaum-Saunders to construct a unit distribution.

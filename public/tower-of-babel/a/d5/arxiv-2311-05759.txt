Abstract
. We consider a Rankin–Selberg integral which unfolds to a Shalika period for the unitary similitude group GU 2 , 2 {{\rm GU}}_{2,2} attached to a quadratic extension E / 𝐐 E/{\bf Q} . We show that it represents the degree 5 L L -function of GSp 4 {{\rm GSp}}_{4} twisted by the quadratic character of E / 𝐐 E/{\bf Q} of cuspidal automorphic representations which contribute to the theta correspondence for the pair ( PGSp 4 , PGU 2 , 2 ) (\mathrm{P}{{\rm GSp}}_{4},\mathrm{P}{{\rm GU}}_{2,2}) . Along the way, we obtain a local criterion to determine the multiplicity of local Shalika models for unramified representations of GU 2 , 2 {{\rm GU}}_{2,2} at unramified inert primes and prove a Casselman-Shalika formula for them.



1.
Introduction

Automorphic
L
-functions are one of the cornerstones of the Langlands program. They provide a bridge between number theory and representation theory, dispensing a profound link between the arithmetic of algebraic objects such as elliptic curves and harmonic analysis on reductive groups. Automorphic
L
-functions are defined by the data of a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
on a reductive group
G
and of a finite dimensional representation
r
of the Langlands group
{}^{L}G
. Their zeros or poles are often linked to the non-vanishing of certain periods of
\pi
and to the participation of
\pi
as a Langlands functorial transfer or theta lift of an automorphic representation of another reductive group. There are mainly two approaches to study the analytic properties of automorphic
L
-functions: via Rankin–Selberg integrals or by the Langlands–Shahidi method. In this article, we present an integral representation of a twist of the degree
5

L
-function of
{{\rm GSp}}_{4}
, which gives a new instance of the curious phenomenon where a Rankin–Selberg integral of a cusp form on one group is used to represent an
L
-function of a different cusp form on another group.

Precisely, we consider the integral

I(\varphi,s):=\int_{{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf Q})Z_{{\rm GSp}_{4}}({\bf A})\backslash{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf A})}E^{*}(s,g)\varphi(g)dg,

where
E^{*}(s,g)
is the normalised degenerate Siegel Eisenstein series for
{\rm GSp}_{4}
and
\varphi
is a cusp form in the space of a cuspidal representation
\Pi
of
{\rm GU}_{2,2}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. This integral is a “quasi-split analogous” to the integral for the group
{\rm SO}_{3,3}
studied in
[
4
]
and can be regarded as the degenerate companion of the integral for
{\rm SO}_{4,2}
studied in
[
35
]
and
[
30
]
. The integral
I(\varphi,s)
unfolds to the unitary Shalika period of
\varphi
; this fact led us to perform a detailed analysis of local Shalika models for unramified representations of
{{\rm GU}}_{2,2}
.

Shalika periods first appeared in the study of the analytic properties of the partial exterior square
L
-function on
{\rm GL}_{2n}
. More precisely, a celebrated theorem of Jacquet and Shalika (
cf
.
[
24
]
) asserts that the partial exterior square
L
-function of a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{2n}
has a simple pole at
s=1
if and only if the Shalika period does not vanish on the space of
\pi
.
A similar statement for the unitary group
{{\rm GU}}_{2,2}
, which is closely connected to
{\rm GL}_{4}
, was studied by Morimoto and Furusawa in
[
13
]
. In the subsequent work
[
29
]
, using the exceptional isomorphism
\mathrm{PGU}_{2,2}\simeq\mathrm{PGSO}_{4,2}
, Morimoto characterized the existence of unitary Shalika periods in terms of the theta correspondence for the pair
({{\rm GSp}}_{4},\mathrm{GSO}_{4,2})
. The results of
[
13
]
and
[
29
]
can be summarized as follows. Let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GU}_{2,2}({\bf A})
with trivial central character, which, using the aforementioned exceptional isomorphism, can be considered as an automorphic representation of
{\rm PGSO}_{4,2}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\Pi
is globally generic and the partial twisted exterior square
L
-function
L^{\Sigma}(s,\Pi,\wedge_{t}^{2})
has a simple pole at
s=1
.

(2)

A certain unitary Shalika period does not vanish on
\Pi
.

(3)

\Pi
comes from a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation
\sigma
of
{\rm GSp}_{4}
with trivial central character, by means of a theta correspondence for
({\rm GSp}_{4},{\rm GSO}_{4,2})
.

If any of these holds, one has that

L^{\Sigma}(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2}_{t})=L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})\zeta^{\Sigma}(s),

where
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})
denotes the partial degree 5
L
-function for
{\rm GSp}_{4}
twisted by the quadratic Hecke character associated to the imaginary quadratic extension
E/{\bf Q}
which defines
{\rm GU}_{2,2}
. By the above characterization, the integral
I(\varphi,s)
is identically zero if
\Pi
does not come from
{\rm GSp}_{4}
.

The purpose of this article is two-fold. On the one hand, we obtain a criterion to determine the multiplicity of local Shalika models for irreducible generic unramified representations of
{\rm GU}_{2,2}({\bf Q}_{p})
in the image of the local theta correspondence. When these models are unique, we prove an explicit Casselman-Shalika formula for them. On the other hand, we use this formula to relate
I(\varphi,s)
to the partial
L
-function
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})
.

We start by describing our local contributions, which could be of independent interest. Let
p
be inert in
E/{\bf Q}
and let
\sigma_{p}
be an irreducible generic unramified representation of
{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})
with trivial central character. Note that
\sigma_{p}
is is necessarily a principal series
{\rm Ind}_{B_{{\rm GSp}_{4}}({\bf Q}_{p})}^{{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})}\chi
. We denote by
g_{\chi}\in{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})
its Frobenius conjugacy class. We let
\Pi_{p}
be the small theta lift of the generic irreducible factor of
{\sigma_{p}}_{|{{\rm GSp}_{4}^{+}({\bf Q}_{p})}}
by means of the theta correspondence for the dual pair
({\rm GSp}_{4}^{+}({\bf Q}_{p}),{\rm GSO}_{4,2}({\bf Q}_{p}))
studied by
[
29
]
. Using the exceptional isomorphism,
\Pi_{p}
can be seen as an irreducible unramified representation of
{\rm GU}_{2,2}({\bf Q}_{p})
with trivial central character. If we let
S
be the Shalika subgroup defined by (
4

\chi_{\eta,p}
be the
p
-component of the character
\chi_{\eta}
on
S
defined in §
2.4
, a unitary Shalika functional for
\Pi_{p}
is a linear functional
\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}:\Pi_{p}\to{\bf C}
, such that for
s\in S({\bf Q}_{p})
and
v
in the space of
\Pi_{p}
we have

\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}(\Pi_{p}(s)v)=\chi_{\eta,p}(s)\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}(v).

We fix a spherical vector
\phi_{0}
of
\Pi_{p}
, which we normalise as in §
4.2
. Then, in view of Theorem
1.2
, we are interested in a formula for the values
\mathcal{S}_{p}(g):=\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}(\Pi_{p}(g)\phi_{0})
as

varies. By using properties of the local theta correspondence and adapting the methods of

, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorems
3.8
&
4.5
)
.

Let
\sigma_{p}
and
\Pi_{p}
be as above. Suppose that
\Pi_{p}
is generic.

(1)

(2)

For all

\mathcal{S}_{p}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{n}I_{2}&\\
&I_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=\frac{p^{-2n}}{1+p^{-1}}\mathcal{A}\left((-1)^{n}e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+,s}_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(1+p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}})\right)(g_{\chi})(\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}})(g_{\chi}))^{-1}.

We refer to §
4
for further explanation on the notation used in the formula. When

is split in

[

]
and the fact that

\sigma_{p}

for Theorem

Let us now consider the Rankin-Selberg integral
I(\varphi,s)
, with
\varphi
a cusp form in a globally generic representation
\Pi
which is in the image of the global theta correspondence studied by
[
29
]
. Thanks to the unfolding and the uniqueness of the Shalika model at the unramified places of
\Pi
, we reduce the calculation of
I(\varphi,s)
to the ones of the corresponding local zeta integrals. At unramified places these are calculated by using the Casselman-Shalika formulas of
[
33
]
and of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

Let
\Pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GU}_{2,2}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. Suppose that
\Pi
appears in the theta lift of
\sigma
in the sense of
[
29
, Theorem B]
, where
\sigma
is a globally generic irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GSp}_{4}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. We have

I(\varphi,s)=I_{\Sigma}(s)L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}}),

where
\Sigma
is a finite set of primes containing the ramified primes for
\Pi
,
\sigma
, and
E/{\bf Q}
, and
I_{\Sigma}(s)
is the integral over the places in
\Sigma
and infinity. Furthermore, the partial
L
-function
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})
extends to a holomorphic function for all

.

A few remarks on Theorem
1.2
are in order. One novelty of our integral representation of the degree 5
L
-function of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
is that it might be used to address motivic questions. Indeed, since
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
and
\mathrm{GU}_{2,2}
have Shimura varieties, our integral seems to have applications to the study of the conjectural “standard” degree 5 motive associated to cohomological cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
, such as the construction of an Euler system for the corresponding Galois representation, similar in spirit to the one given in work in progress by Skinner and Sangiovanni Vincentelli for the symmetric square of a modular form. Interestingly, our result adapts to other settings, such as for the standard
L
-function of cusp forms of the split exceptional group
G_{2}
, which will be explored in forthcoming work.

Since the theta lift of
\sigma
to
{\rm GSO}_{4,2}
is non-zero and cuspidal by hypothesis, Rallis tower property implies that the theta lift of
\sigma
to
{\rm GSO}_{3,1}
and
{\rm GSO}_{2,0}
is zero. Thus, by using
[
26
, Theorem 7.2.5]
, one could deduce that the partial
L
-function
L^{\Sigma}(s,\sigma,{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})
is entire. By analysing
I(\varphi,s)
at the points where
E^{*}(s,g)
might obtain a pole, we deduce that
I(\varphi,s)
is indeed holomorphic at any
s
. Here we use the fact that unramified generic representations on
{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})
are not
{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})
-distinguished and the vanishing of an integral which involves the degenerate Klingen Eisenstein series on
{\rm GSp}_{4}
. The latter integral is intimately related to the construction of
[
9
]
.

is the set of positive coroots.

Recall that the dual group of
\mathbf{H}
is
{}^{L}\mathbf{H}={\rm GSpin}_{5}({\bf C})
, thus via an exceptional isomorphism we have

{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}={\rm Spin}_{5}({\bf C})\simeq{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C}).

We thus can identify the set of positive roots of
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}
with the set of positive coroots of
{\rm Sp}_{4}
as (see the proof of
[

, Lemma 2.3.1]
)

\{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0},2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0},2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0}\}\to\{\check{\alpha}_{2},\check{\alpha}_{1},\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2},\check{\alpha}_{1}-\check{\alpha}_{2}\}.

Note that the set
\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}
is sent to the set
\Phi^{\vee,+,s}_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}=\{\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2},\check{\alpha}_{1}-\check{\alpha}_{2}\}
of coroots corresponding to the short roots for
{\rm Sp}_{4}
.

Here and throughout the article, if

2.3.1.
The twisted exterior square representation

Recall that the dual group of
\mathbf{G}
is

{}^{L}\mathbf{G}=({\rm GL}_{4}({\bf C})\times{\rm GL}_{1}({\bf C}))\rtimes{\rm Gal}(E/{\bf Q}),

with the action of the non-trivial element
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/{\bf Q})
given by (
cf
.
[
2
, §1.8(c)]
)

(g,\lambda)\mapsto(\Phi_{4}{}^{t}g^{-1}\Phi_{4},\lambda{\rm det}(g)),\text{ where }\Phi_{4}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&&1\\
&&-1&\\
&1&&\\
-1&&&\end{smallmatrix}\right).

The standard representation of
{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf C})
on
{\bf C}^{4}
, with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, induces the representation
\wedge^{2}
on
\wedge^{2}{\bf C}^{4}\simeq{\bf C}^{6}
by the rule
(\wedge^{2}g)\cdot(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=g\cdot e_{i}\wedge g\cdot e_{j}
. Consider the six dimensional representation

{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf C})\times{\rm GL}_{1}({\bf C})\to{\rm GL}_{6}({\bf C})

given by
(g,\lambda)\mapsto(\wedge^{2}g)\lambda
. Notice that both representations
\wedge^{2}
and
\wedge^{2}\circ\tau
have same highest weight
(1,1,0,0,0)
. Hence they are isomorphic, i.e. there exists
A\in{\rm GL}_{6}({\bf C})
such that
(\wedge^{2}\circ\tau)(g,\lambda)=A^{-1}(\wedge^{2}(g,\lambda))A
. Choose
A
with
{\rm Tr}(A)>0
and extend
\wedge^{2}
to
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
by sending

(g,\lambda,1)\mapsto(\wedge^{2}g)\lambda,\,(1,1,\tau)\mapsto A.

The resulting representation, denoted by
\wedge_{t}^{2}:{}^{L}\mathbf{G}\to{\rm GL}_{6}({\bf C})
, is called the twisted exterior square representation.

Finally, recall that the dual group of
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}
is the derived subgroup of
{}^{L}\mathbf{G}
, namely

{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}={\rm SL}_{4}({\bf C})\rtimes{\rm Gal}(E/{\bf Q}).

We still denote by
\wedge_{t}^{2}:{}^{L}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}\to{\rm GL}_{6}({\bf C})
the corresponding twisted exterior square representation.

The twisted exterior square

-function

We now define the partial twisted exterior square

of

•

Suppose that

be the completion at

. Any unramified character of

are unramified characters of

&b&&\\

Suppose that

2.4.
Shalika models and a theta correspondence

Throughout this paper we will consider the
Shalika subgroup
of
\mathbf{G}
, which is defined as follows. For any
{\bf Q}
-algebra

,
S
is given by

(4)

S(R)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&&\\
c&d&&\\
&&a&-b\\
&&-c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\alpha&x\\
&1&y&\overline{\alpha}\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\;:\;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in{\rm GL}_{2}(R),x,y\in\mathbb{G}_{a}(R),\;\alpha\in{\rm Res}_{E/{\bf Q}}\mathbb{G}_{a}(R)\right\}.

Note that the Shalika group

is isomorphic to
{\rm GL}_{2}N_{\mathbf{G}}
, where
N_{\mathbf{G}}
is the unipotent radical of the Siegel of
\mathbf{G}
, while
{\rm GL}_{2}
embeds into the Levi of the Siegel parabolic

of
\mathbf{H}
via the embedding
h\mapsto h^{*}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h&\\
&{\rm det}(h)J_{2}{}^{t}h^{-1}J_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\delta_{S}
of

is trivial.

We now fix a non-degenerate character
\psi:{\bf Q}\backslash{\bf A}\to{\bf C}^{\times}

\eta\in E^{\times}
such that
\bar{\eta}=-\eta
, we define

\chi_{\eta}:N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf A})\longrightarrow{\bf C}^{\times},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\alpha&x\\
&1&y&\overline{\alpha}\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\psi(\eta\alpha-\eta\overline{\alpha}).

By a slight abuse of notation, denote also by
\chi_{\eta}
the character on
S({\bf Q})\backslash S({\bf A})
given by
h^{*}n\mapsto\chi_{\eta}(n)

Let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
with trivial central character.

Definition 2.5
.

For a cusp form
\varphi
in the space of
\Pi
, the
unitary
Shalika period is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(g):=\int_{{\bf A}^{\times}{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})\backslash{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})}\int_{N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf A})}\varphi\left(nh^{*}g\right)\chi_{\eta}^{-1}(n)dndg.

The representation
\Pi
is said to have a Shalika model if there exist
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
such that
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(g)\neq 0
.

In
[
13
]
, Furusawa and Morimoto characterized the representations
\Pi
with unitary Shalika model in terms of the existence of a pole at
s=1
of their partial twisted exterior square
L
-function. Namely, they showed the following.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
\Pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The Shalika period
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(1)
does not vanish on the space of
\Pi
.

(2)

\Pi
is globally generic and the partial twisted exterior square
L

has a simple pole at

.

Motivated by this, in

to obtain the following.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
29

)
.

Let
\Pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
with trivial central character. Then
\Pi
has a Shalika model if and only if
\Pi

\mathbf{H}({\bf A})

Thanks to a detailed study of the local theta correspondence, Morimoto obtained the following relation.

Corollary 2.8

Suppose that
\Pi

3.1.

Lemma 3.1

The restriction

\omega\alpha<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{\alpha})}{1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha})}=\frac{\omega^{-1}\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))\right)}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))}.

Thus, the formula becomes

\displaystyle Q^{-1}\delta^{1/2}_{B_{\mathbf{G}}}(g_{n})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(\xi)\sum_{\omega\in W_{\mathbf{H}}}(-1)^{\ell(\omega)+n}\chi(a_{-\rho+\omega(\rho+n(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0}))})\frac{\omega\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))\right)}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))},

where we have used that
{}^{\omega}\chi(a_{n(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0})})=\chi(\omega^{-1}a_{n(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0})}\omega)=\chi(a_{\omega^{-1}(n(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0}))})
and we reordered the sum by changing
\omega
with its inverse.

To further simplify the formula, we can express it all in terms of the Satake paramaters
g_{\chi}
of
I_{\mathbf{H}}(\chi)
. Let
i:X^{\star}(T_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf C}))\simeq X_{\star}(T_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}({\bf C}))
be the isomorphism induced from the duality between
\mathbf{PH}({\bf C})
and
\mathrm{Spin}_{5}({\bf C})
and the exceptional isomorphism
\mathrm{Spin}_{5}({\bf C})\simeq{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})
. This map identifies the long roots
\Phi^{+,l}_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}
with the short roots
\Phi^{+,s}_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}
, in the sense that it maps (see
[
32
, Lemma 2.3.1]
)

\{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0},2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0},2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0}\}\to\{\check{\alpha}_{2},\check{\alpha}_{1},\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2},\check{\alpha}_{1}-\check{\alpha}_{2}\}.

Via the isomorphism
i
and definition of the Satake parameters, we then obtain the equality

\chi(a_{\alpha})=e^{i(\alpha)}(g_{\chi}).

We obtain the desired formula by rewriting the resulting formula in terms of the roots of
{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})
, namely we have

\displaystyle\frac{p^{-2n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(\xi)}{Qe^{\check{\rho}}(g_{\chi})\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))}\mathcal{A}\left((-1)^{n}e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+,s}_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(1+p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}})\right)(g_{\chi}),

where we used the definition of the alternator
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)=\sum_{\omega}(-1)^{\ell(\omega)}\omega(\cdot)
and that
\delta^{1/2}_{B_{\mathbf{G}}}(g_{n})=p^{-2n}
.
∎

Remark 5.21
.

Notice that, using the relations

\chi^{2}(a_{-(2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0})})=\xi(a_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0}}),\,\,\chi^{2}(a_{-(2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{0})})=\xi(a_{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}),

we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi^{+,s}_{\mathbf{H}}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(\xi)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}\frac{1-p^{-2}\chi^{2}(a_{-\alpha})}{1-\chi^{2}(a_{-\alpha})},

therefore
\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+,l}}(1+p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))
divides the numerator of
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi^{+}_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(\xi)
.

We conclude this chapter by re-normalising the Shalika functional as presented in Theorem
4.5
.

Corollary 5.22

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

For a suitable normalisation, the spherical Shalika functional satisfies

\mathcal{S}_{p}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{n}I_{2}&\\
&I_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=\frac{p^{-2n}}{1+p^{-1}}\mathcal{A}\left((-1)^{n}e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+,s}_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(1+p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}})\right)(g_{\chi})(\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}})(g_{\chi}))^{-1}.

Proof.

Recall that
I_{\mathbf{H}}(\chi)
and
I_{\mathbf{G}}(\xi)
are irreducible; this implies, by
[
6
, Proposition 3.5]
and Remark
5.21
, that the following constant

C:=\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}}^{+}\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}(\xi)}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+,l}_{\mathbf{H}}}(1-p^{-1}\chi(a_{-\alpha}))},

is invertible. Moreover,
\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}})(g_{\chi})
is invertible too (
cf
.
[
12
, Theorem 24.2]
). We can therefore normalise the Shalika functional by multiplying our formula by
\frac{Qe^{\check{\rho}}(g_{\chi})}{1+p^{-1}}(C\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}})(g_{\chi}))^{-1}
.
∎

twisted by the quadratic Hecke character

6.1.
The zeta integral and Shalika models

6.1.1.
Siegel Eisenstein series on
{{\rm GSp}}_{4}

We define the Siegel Eisenstein series associated to
\mathbf{H}
and recollect some of their properties. We follow
[
11
, §5]
closely. Recall that we have introduced the Siegel parabolic
P_{\mathbf{H}}
of
\mathbf{H}
with Levi decomposition
M_{\mathbf{H}}N_{\mathbf{H}}
and modulus character given by

\displaystyle\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}

\displaystyle:\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\star\\
&\mu I_{2}^{\prime}{}^{t}g^{-1}I_{2}^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|\tfrac{{\rm det}(g)}{\mu}\right|^{3}.

We let
I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
be the degenerate principal series representation of
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
consisting of smooth functions
f_{s}
on
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
such that

f_{s}(nmg)=\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{\tfrac{1}{3}(s+1)}(m)f_{s}(g),\;\forall n\in N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}),\forall m\in M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}).

Given a standard holomorphic section
f_{s}\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
, we define the degenerate Eisenstein series

{\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s})=\sum_{\gamma\in{P_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})\backslash\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})}f_{s}(\gamma g),

which is absolutely convergent in the half-plane
{\rm Re}(s)>2
and admits analytic continuation to a meromorphic function on
{\bf C}
which satisfies a functional equation

{\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s})={\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,1-s,M_{s}f_{s}),

where
M_{s}
is a certain intertwining operator.

At a finite prime
p
, let
f_{p,s}^{0}
be the function in
I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})}^{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q}_{p})}(s)
such that
f_{p,s}^{0}(k)=1
for all
k\in\mathbf{H}({\bf Z}_{p})
. We assume that
f_{s}
is a pure tensor and denote by
f_{p,s}
its
p
-adic component, which equals
f_{p,s}^{0}
almost everywhere. Let
S
be the finite set of places containing
\infty
and all the primes
p
such that
f_{p,s}\neq f_{p,s}^{0}
.
We then define the normalised Siegel Eisenstein series to be equal to

E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s):=\zeta^{S}(s+1)\zeta^{S}(2s){\rm Eis}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s}).

It follows from
[
26
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 4.12]
that
E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s)
has at most simple poles at the points
s=1,2
.

6.1.2.
The unfolding

Let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}
with trivial central character. Let
S
be a finite set of places containing
\infty
and all the ramified primes for
\Pi
. We take a cusp form
\varphi\in\Pi
, which we assume to be a pure tensor. Recall that we have the embedding

\mathbf{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G};

hence we consider the following integral:

I(\varphi,s):=\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh,

where
E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)
is the normalised Siegel Eisenstein series for
\mathbf{H}
defined above associated to a normalised standard holomorphic section
f_{s}=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}f_{v,s}\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
, i.e. such that

f_{p,s}=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\zeta_{p}(2s)f_{p,s}^{0},\;\forall p\not\in S.

Note that this integral converges absolutely as the restriction to
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
of
\varphi
is rapidly decreasing. Furthermore, due to the property of the Eisenstein series, it defines a meromorphic function.

We have the following.

Proposition 6.1
.

The integral
I(\varphi,s)
unfolds to

\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(h)dh.

Proof.

We start by unfolding the Eisenstein series: for
{\rm Re}(s)
big enough, we get

\int_{P_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\varphi(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over the unipotent radical
N_{\mathbf{H}}
of
P_{\mathbf{H}}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\int_{N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})}f_{s}(nh)\varphi(nh)dndh=\int_{\cdots}f_{s}(h)\int_{N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})}\varphi(nh)dndh,

as
f_{s}\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
.
Now consider the Siegel parabolic for
\mathbf{G}
and its unipotent radical
N_{\mathbf{G}}
. Fourier expanding over
[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]
, the integral equals

\int_{M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\sum_{\chi}\varphi_{\chi}(h)dh,

where the sum runs over the characters
\chi:[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
and where we have denoted

\varphi_{\chi}(h)=\int_{N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf A})}\varphi(n^{\prime}h)\chi^{-1}(n^{\prime})dn^{\prime}.

Any character
\chi:[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
can be described as follows. Recall that any character
\chi:[N_{\mathbf{G}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
is of the form
n^{\prime}\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}(A\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&x\\
y&\bar{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right))),
where
A\in M_{2\times 2}(E)
is such that
\overline{A}^{t}=I^{\prime}_{2}AI^{\prime}_{2}
,
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character on
{\bf Q}\backslash{\bf A}
, and where we have written

n^{\prime}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&\alpha&x\\
&1&y&\bar{\alpha}\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;\text{ with }x,y\in{\bf A},\alpha\in{\bf A}_{E}.

If
A=(a_{i,j})
, the associated character
\chi
is trivial on
N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})
if
a_{1,1}+a_{2,2}=0
and
a_{1,2}=a_{2,1}=0
. We deduce that any character
\chi:[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
is of the form

n^{\prime}\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\eta&\\
&-\eta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&x\\
y&\bar{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right)))=\psi(\eta\alpha-\eta\bar{\alpha}),

with
\eta\in E^{\times}
such that
\bar{\eta}=-\eta
. To emphasize its dependence on
\eta
, we denote this character by
\chi_{\eta}
.

As
M_{\mathbf{H}}
acts on
N_{\mathbf{G}}
by conjugation and this action preserves
N_{\mathbf{H}}
,
M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})
acts on the space of characters of
[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]
. Indeed, if
m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\\
&\mu I_{2}^{\prime}{}^{t}g^{-1}I_{2}^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})
,

\chi_{\eta}(mn^{\prime}m^{-1})=\chi_{\eta^{\prime}}(n^{\prime}),\;\text{ with }\eta^{\prime}=\mu^{-1}{\rm det}(g)\eta.

Then,
M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})
acts on the space of characters of
[N_{\mathbf{G}}/N_{\mathbf{H}}]
with two orbits, the trivial one and an open one associated to any
\chi_{\eta}
with
\eta\neq 0
. Using this action, we can write the integral as

(32)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\int_{[N_{\mathbf{G}}]}\varphi(n^{\prime}h)dn^{\prime}dh+\int_{M_{\eta}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\varphi_{\chi_{\eta}}(h)dh,

where
M_{\eta}({\bf Q})
denotes the stabiliser in
M_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q})
of
\chi_{\eta}
. Precisely,
m\in M_{\eta}({\bf Q})
if
\mu={\rm det}(g)
, hence we can fix an isomorphism
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})\simeq M_{\eta}({\bf Q})
, sending
g\mapsto m(g,{\rm det}(g))
. As the first integral of (
32

along

I(\varphi,s)=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\varphi_{\chi_{\eta}}(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over
[{\rm GL}_{2}]
, to get

\displaystyle I(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\varphi_{\chi_{\eta}}(h)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\backslash{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})}f_{s}(m(g,{\rm det}(g))h)\varphi_{\chi_{\eta}}(m(g,{\rm det}(g))h)dgdh

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\backslash{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})}\varphi_{\chi_{\eta}}(gh)dgdh

\displaystyle=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}(h)\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}^{\eta}(h)dh,

where we have used that
\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(m(g,{\rm det}(g)))=\left|\tfrac{{\rm det}(g)}{{\rm det}(g)}\right|^{3}=1
.
∎

As an immediate corollary of Theorems
2.6

2.7

In order to relate

with an

6.2.
Calculation at unramified primes

Suppose that
\Pi_{p}
is unramified and let
\phi_{0}
be a spherical vector in
\Pi_{p}
, then, using the Iwasawa decomposition
\mathbf{H}({\bf Q}_{p})=P_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\mathbf{H}({\bf Z}_{p})
and the fact that
f_{p,s}
and
\phi_{0}
are
\mathbf{H}({\bf Z}_{p})
-invariants, we get

I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus P_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})}\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{-1}(g)f_{p,s}(g)\mathcal{S}_{p}(g)dg.

and the multiplier identifies

, we have

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf Q}_{p}^{\times}}\delta_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{-1}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))f_{p,s}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))d^{\times}\mu

\displaystyle=f_{p,s}(1)\int_{{\bf Q}_{p}^{\times}}|\mu|^{2-s}\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))d^{\times}\mu.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))\neq 0
, then
|\mu|\geq 1
.

Proof.

As
\phi_{0}
is spherical, we have for
u\in N^{\prime}({\bf Z}_{p})

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}(\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)u\right)\cdot\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{p,\mathcal{S}}(\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)u\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu^{-1}I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)\cdot\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=\chi_{\eta,p}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)u\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu^{-1}I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)).

Hence, since by hypothesis
\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right))\neq 0
, then

\chi_{\eta,p}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu I\end{smallmatrix}\right)u\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&\\
&\mu^{-1}I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=1.

This implies that
|\mu|\geq 1
.

By Lemma

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

&p^{-n}I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)

&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)),

is assumed to be trivial.

6.2.1.
The unramified calculation at split primes

Suppose
p
is split in
E
. Then, the local component
\Pi_{p}
can be identified with an unramified representation
\pi_{p}=I_{{\rm GL}_{4}}(\chi)
of
{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})
with trivial central character. As we recalled in §
4.1
,
\pi_{p}
has a Shalika model if its Frobenius conjugacy class is in
{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})
, precisely of the form
g_{\chi}=(\chi_{1}(p),\chi_{2}(p),\chi_{2}^{-1}(p),\chi_{1}^{-1}(p))
. As the theta correspondence is functorial at primes which are split in
E
, then
\pi_{p}
is the theta lift of a representation
\sigma_{p}
of
{\mathbf{P}}\mathbf{H}({\bf Q}_{p})
with same semi-simple conjugacy class
g_{\chi}
.

Recall that we have denoted by
{\rm std}:{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})\to{\rm GL}_{5}({\bf C})
the irreducible representation
\rho_{1,1}
of highest weight
(1,1)
, which is given by the composition of the projection of
{\rm Spin}_{5}({\bf C})
to
{\rm SO}_{5}({\bf C})
with the standard representation of the latter. Following
[
3
, § 4]
, the “standard” Euler factor satisfies the following equality

L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{tr}\left(g_{\chi}|\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\rho_{1,1}\right)p^{-ks},

where
\mathrm{tr}\left(g_{\chi}|\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\rho_{1,1}\right)
is the character associated to the representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\rho_{1,1}
of
{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf C})
evaluated at
g_{\chi}
. In order to explicit the formula, we have to decompose into irreducible factors the representation
\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\rho_{1,1}
for every
k\geq 0
.

Lemma 6.5
.

We have that

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\rho_{1,1})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\rho_{k-2i,k-2i},

where
\rho_{k-2i,k-2i}
is the irreducible representation of highest weight
(k-2i)(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
.

Proof.

The symplectic form defining
{\rm Sp}_{4}
defines a surjection

{\rm Sym}^{2}\rho_{1,1}\to{\bf C},

which induces a surjection, for all
k\geq 2
,

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\rho_{1,1})\to\mathrm{Sym}^{k-2}(\rho_{1,1}),

with kernel the representation
\rho_{k,k}
of highest weight
k\alpha_{1}+k\alpha_{2}
(
cf
.
[
12
, Exercise 16.11]
). Therefore

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\rho_{1,1})=\rho_{k,k}\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{k-2}(\rho_{1,1}).

Using this formula recursively, we obtain that

\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\rho_{1,1})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\rho_{k-2i,k-2i}.

∎

Lemma
6.5
implies that

L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\mathrm{tr}\left(g_{\chi}|\rho_{k-2i,k-2i}\right)p^{-ks}.

Using Weyl’s character formula (
cf
.
[
12
, Theorem 24.2]
), the latter equals to

(34)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\frac{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}+(k-2i,k-2i)}\right)}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi})p^{-ks},

where we denoted
(k-2i,k-2i)=(k-2i)(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})
.

This is crucially used to prove the following.

Theorem 6.6
.

We have

I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)=L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std}).

Proof.

Substituting the formula of Theorem
4.4
in the integral, we have

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

\displaystyle=f_{p,s}(1)\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{n(2-s)}\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{n}I&\\
&I\end{smallmatrix}\right))

\displaystyle=f_{p,s}(1)(1+p^{-1})^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\mathcal{A}\Big(e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}^{+,s}}(1-p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}})\Big)(g_{\chi})({\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi}))^{-1}.

We now examine the alternator sum appearing above. First, recall that
\Phi_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}^{+,s}
consists of the roots
\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Then, the term
\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}^{+,s}}(1-p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}}))
equals

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}+n\check{\alpha}_{1}+n\check{\alpha}_{2}}-p^{-1}e^{\check{\rho}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{1}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{2}}-p^{-1}e^{\check{\rho}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{1}+(n+1)\check{\alpha}_{2}}+p^{-2}e^{\check{\rho}+(n-2)\check{\alpha}_{1}+n\check{\alpha}_{2}}).

Now, let
s_{1}
be the Weyl element associated to
\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
; it acts on the alternator as multiplication by
-1
, i.e.

(36)

\mathcal{A}(e^{s_{1}\cdot\check{\mu}})=-\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\mu}}).

This let us rearrange (
35
) as

(37)

\displaystyle(1+p^{-1})\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}+n\check{\alpha}_{1}+n\check{\alpha}_{2}}-p^{-1}e^{\check{\rho}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{1}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{2}}).

Substituting (
37
) in the integral yields

I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)=f_{p,s}(1)\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\frac{\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}+n\check{\alpha}_{1}+n\check{\alpha}_{2}}-p^{-1}e^{\check{\rho}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{1}+(n-1)\check{\alpha}_{2}})}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi}).

For every
n\geq-1
, denote the term
a_{n}:=\frac{\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}+n\check{\alpha}_{1}+n\check{\alpha}_{2}})}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi})
; notice that for
n=-1
we have that
a_{-1}=\frac{\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}-(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})})}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi})
. Let
s_{2}
be the Weyl element associated to the root
2\alpha_{2}
, then
s:=s_{2}s_{1}s_{2}
acts on the alternator
\mathcal{A}(e^{\check{\rho}-(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})})
as in (
36
). Since
\check{\rho}-(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})
is invariant under
s
we conclude that
a_{-1}=0
.

Recall that

f_{p,s}(1)=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\zeta_{p}(2s).

Using the Cauchy product formula, we have

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-2ns}\cdot\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}(a_{n}-p^{-1}a_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(a_{n-2k}-p^{-1}a_{n-1-2k})\Big)p^{-ns}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{n-2k}-p^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}a_{n-1-2k}\Big)p^{-ns},

where for the latter equality we have used that
a_{-1}=0
.
This expression is terribly close to the one of (
34
). Indeed, a simple manipulation of the series above gives that

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{n-2k}-p^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}a_{n-1-2k}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{n-2k}-p^{-1-s}\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-(n-1)s}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}a_{n-1-2k}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{p}(s+1)(1-p^{-1-s})\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}(\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}a_{n-2k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}+(n-2k,n-2k)}\right)}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi})p^{-ns}

\displaystyle=L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std}),

where the last equality follows from (
34
).
∎

Let
p

of

\displaystyle\xi=(\chi_{0}\circ N_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}},\chi_{2}\chi_{0}\circ N_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}},\chi_{1}\chi_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}}),

\displaystyle|\cdot|^{\pm 1}\not\in\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{1}\chi_{2},\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\},\,\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{0}^{2}=1.

If we call

the unramified principal series

.

Lemma 6.7
.

We have

L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}+(n-2i)(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\right)}{\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi})p^{-ns}.

Proof.

The proof of this is similar to the one of (

L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\cdot\mathrm{tr}\left(g_{\chi}|\mathrm{Sym}^{n}\rho_{1,1}\right)p^{-ns},

as

, and the decomposition of Lemma

.
∎

This Lemma implies the following.

Theorem 6.8
.

We have

I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)=L(s,\sigma_{p},{\rm std}\otimes\chi_{E_{p}/{\bf Q}_{p}}).

Proof.

Using the formula for
\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{n}I&\\
&I\end{smallmatrix}\right))

4.5
, we get

\displaystyle I_{p}(\phi_{0},f_{p,s},s)

\displaystyle=f_{p,s}(1)\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{n(2-s)}\mathcal{S}_{p}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p^{n}I&\\
&I\end{smallmatrix}\right))

\displaystyle=f_{p,s}(1)(1+p^{-1})^{-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}p^{-ns}\mathcal{A}\Big((-1)^{n}e^{\check{\rho}+n(\check{\alpha}_{1}+\check{\alpha}_{2})}\cdot\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}^{+,s}}(1+p^{-1}e^{-\check{\alpha}})\Big)(g_{\chi})({\mathcal{A}\left(e^{\check{\rho}}\right)}(g_{\chi}))^{-1}.

The term

6.3.
The integral at ramified places

In what follows, we look at the integral

I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}_{S})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A}_{S})}f_{S,s}(h_{S})\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(h_{S})dh_{S},

where
{\bf A}_{S}=\prod_{v\in S}{\bf Q}_{v}={\bf R}\times\prod_{p\in S}{\bf Q}_{p}
and
f_{S,s}=\otimes_{v\in S}f_{v,s}
. Adapting the methods of
[
14
]
and of
[
15
]
to our setting, we prove the following.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
s_{0}\in{\bf C}
. Then, there exist data
\varphi_{S}
and
f_{S,s}
such that
I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)
extends to a meromorphic function to all
s\in{\bf C}
, which is non-vanishing at
s_{0}
.

Before we prove the statement we need to introduce some notation and an auxiliary Lemma.

Let us denote by

G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s):=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}_{S})}\delta^{\frac{1}{3}(s+1)}(m_{S})\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{S}k_{S})dm_{S}.

The subgroup
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})\subset\mathbf{H}({\bf A}_{S})
contains the centre of
\mathbf{H}({\bf A}_{S})
. Hence
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}_{S})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A}_{S})\simeq\mathrm{PGL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})N_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf A}_{S})\setminus\mathbf{PH}({\bf A}_{S})
. Let
K({\bf A}_{S})=\prod_{v\in S}K_{v}
, with
K_{v}
the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathbf{PH}({\bf Q}_{v})
. Using the Iwasawa factorization, we get

(38)

I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)=\int_{\tilde{K}({\bf A}_{S})}f_{S,s}(k_{S})G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)dk_{S},

where
\tilde{K}({\bf A}_{S}):=\left({\rm GL}_{2}({\bf A}_{S})\cap K({\bf A}_{S})\right)\left(N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}_{S})\cap K({\bf A}_{S})\right)\setminus K({\bf A}_{S})
. Since the global zeta integral
I(\varphi,s)
and
L^{S}(s,\sigma,\mathrm{std}\otimes\chi_{E/{\bf Q}})
have meromorphic continuation,
I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)
has meromorphic continuation too. For any subgroup
R({\bf A}_{S})
of
G({\bf A}_{S})
and
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf A}_{S})\right)
we denote the action of the Hecke algebra on
\varphi_{S}
by

\phi*\varphi_{S}(g):=\int_{R({\bf A}_{S})}\phi(r)\varphi_{S}(gr)dr.

Lemma 6.10
.

(1)

When
\mathrm{Re}(s)
is sufficiently large, the integral
G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
is absolutely convergent. Furthermore, the function
k_{S}\mapsto G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
and the functional
\varphi_{S}\mapsto G(\varphi_{S},1,s)
are continuous.

(2)

The function
G(\varphi_{S},1,s)
admits meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane.

(3)

There exists
\varphi_{S}
so that
G(\varphi_{S},1,s)\neq 0
.

Proof.

The proof follows the ones of
[
14
, Lemma 15.7]
and of
[
15
, Lemma 9.1]
.

(1)
. By reproducing the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 15.7]
, one can show the function
k_{S}\mapsto G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
is absolutely convergent and the functional
\varphi_{S}\mapsto G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
is continuous. Therefore, since the function
k_{S}\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(k_{S})
is continuous and the integral
G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
is absolutely convergent, the second part of
(1)
follows.

(2)
. Since
\Pi
has trivial central character, the restriction to
\mathbf{H}
of the smooth function
\varphi_{S}
can be realised as a smooth function over
\mathbf{PH}({\bf A}_{S})
. The group
\mathbf{PH}({\bf R})
is a semisimple (adjoint) Lie group. Hence, by the Iwasawa decomposition, there is a diffeomorphism
B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R})\times K_{\infty}\to\mathbf{PH}({\bf R})
. This allows us to realise
\varphi_{\infty}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R})\times K_{\infty})
. Moreover, by
[
36
, Theorem 39.2]
, the space
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R}))\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K_{\infty})
is (sequentially) dense in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R})\times K_{\infty})
. Since, by the previous point, the functional
\varphi_{S}\to G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
is continuous, it is enough to show the meromorphic continuation for some
\varphi_{S}
, whose component
\varphi_{\infty}
(as a function on
\mathbf{PH}({\bf R})
) lies in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R}))\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K_{\infty})
. From now on, we will assume so.

To prove the statement, we establish a connection between the functions
G(\varphi_{S},1,s)
and
I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)
for a certain choice of section
f_{S,s}
. The function
G(\varphi_{S},k_{S},s)
can be expressed as the iteration of the local integrals

G_{v}(\varphi_{S},k_{v},s):=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{v})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{v})}\delta^{\frac{1}{3}(s+1)}(m_{v})\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{v}k_{v})dm_{v}.

By making use of the smoothness of
\varphi_{S}
, we will show that the integral over
\prod_{v\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\tilde{K}_{v}
of (
38
) is constant and reduce the integral
G
to an archimedean one.

When
p
is a finite place, we denote by
K^{\prime}_{p}
the compact open subgroup so that
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{p}k_{p})=\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{p})
. Due to
[
7
, Lemma 3.1.1]
, there is a section
\tilde{f}_{s,p}
so that
\tilde{f}_{s}|_{K_{p}}
equals the characteristic function of
K^{\prime}_{p}
. When
v=\infty
, by the discussion above we can assume that
\varphi_{S}|_{\mathbf{H}({\bf R})}
has
\infty
-component given by a pure tensor
\varphi_{B}\otimes\varphi_{K}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\mathbf{PH}}({\bf R}))\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K_{\infty})
. Therefore, by a theorem of Dixmier-Malliavin
[
10
, Theorem 3.3]
, we are able to express
\varphi_{\infty}
as a finite linear combination of functions of the form
\phi_{i,\infty}*\varphi_{i,\infty}
, where
\phi_{i,\infty}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K_{\infty})
and smooth
\varphi_{i,\infty}\in\Pi_{\infty}
. Writing
\varphi_{i,S}=\varphi_{i,\infty}\otimes\bigotimes_{p\in S\smallsetminus\{\infty\}}\varphi_{p}
, then we have that

(39)

\sum_{i}I_{S}\left(\phi_{i,\infty}*\varphi_{i,S},\phi_{i,\infty}^{\prime}\otimes\left(\otimes_{p\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\tilde{f}_{s,p}\right),s\right)=\left(\prod_{p\in S\smallsetminus\{\infty\}}\mathrm{vol}(K^{\prime}_{p})\right)G(\varphi_{S},1,s),

where we have set
\phi^{\prime}_{i,\infty}(g):=\int_{\left({\rm GL}_{2}({\bf R})\cap K_{\infty}\right)\left(N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf R})\cap K_{\infty}\right)}\phi_{i}(gk_{p})dk_{p}
. Since the left hand side of (
39
) admits meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane, we get the desired statement.

(3)
. To prove the statement, we trivialize the local integrals

G_{p}(\varphi_{S},1,s)
at any finite prime
p\in S
as follows.
Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p}))
. Since
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})
acts by conjugation on
N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})
, we get

G_{p}(\phi*\varphi_{S},1,s)=\int_{{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})}\hat{\phi^{\prime}}(m_{p})\delta^{\frac{1}{3}(s+1)}(m_{p})\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{p})dm_{p},

with
\phi^{\prime}(g)=\int_{N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})}\phi(ng)dn
and
\hat{\phi^{\prime}}(m_{p})=\int_{N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})}\phi^{\prime}(x)\chi_{\eta}(m_{p}xm_{p}^{-1})dx
. According to the proof of Proposition
6.1
,
M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})
acts on the set of characters
\psi:N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})/N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\to{\bf C}^{\times}
with two orbits: one generated by the trivial character
\mathds{1}
and the open orbit
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\cdot\chi_{\eta}
. Hence we may regard
\hat{\phi}^{\prime}
as the Fourier transform of
\phi^{\prime}
restricted to the characters in the open orbit
{\rm GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})\setminus M_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})\cdot\chi_{\eta}
. The averaging map used to define
\phi^{\prime}
gives a surjective map
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p}))\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})/N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p}))
. Moreover, by the Fourier inversion theorem, the Fourier transform defines a bijective map
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p}))\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\widehat{N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})/N_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf Q}_{p})})
. When
p
is finite, due to the continuity of
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{p})
, we can then choose
\phi_{p}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}\left(N_{\mathbf{G}}({\bf Q}_{p})\right)
so that
\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{p}
is the characteristic function of the compact open subgroup
K^{\prime}_{p}
so that
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(m_{p})=\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(1)\neq 0
. Therefore, up to normalising the
\phi_{p}
’s, we can choose
\phi_{p}
at each finite place in
S
for which

(40)

\displaystyle G(\otimes_{p\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}\phi_{p}*\varphi_{S},1,s)=G_{\infty}(\varphi_{S},1,s).

We are left to show that for some choice of data
G_{\infty}(\varphi_{S},1,s)\neq 0
. Take
\phi_{\infty}
such that
\hat{\phi}^{\prime}_{\infty}
has support in a sufficiently small neighborhood
K^{\prime}_{\infty}
of
1
, over which
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}
is non-zero. Note that
K^{\prime}_{\infty}
exists by continuity of
g\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(g)
and that
\mathcal{S}_{\varphi_{S}}^{\eta}(1)\neq 0
. Using the same argument as above, we get
G_{\infty}(\phi_{\infty}*\varphi_{S},1,s)\neq 0
.
∎

Using Lemma
6.10
, we can now prove Proposition
6.9
.

Proof of Proposition
6.9
.

We have already discussed that
I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s)
extends to a meromorphic function to all
s\in{\bf C}
. We are thus left to show that, given
s_{0}\in{\bf C}
, there exist some data
\varphi_{S},f_{S,s}
such that
I_{S}(\varphi_{S},f_{S,s},s_{0})\neq 0
.

Given
\varphi_{S}
, by (
39
) and (
40
), there exist data
\varphi_{S}^{\prime}
and
f_{S,s}
such that
I_{S}(\varphi_{S}^{\prime},f_{S,s},s)=G_{\infty}(\varphi_{S},1,s)
. Moreover, by Lemma
6.10
(3), there exists
\varphi_{S}
such that function
G_{\infty}(\varphi_{S},1,s)\neq 0
. The result then follows from these two facts.
∎

6.4.1.
The Klingen Eisenstein series

Let
Q_{\mathbf{H}}
denote the Klingen parabolic of
\mathbf{H}
with Levi decomposition
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
where

M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&\\
&g&\\
&&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;g\in{\rm GL}_{2},a,d\in{\rm GL}_{1}\;:\;ad={\rm det}(g)\right\}\simeq{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{1},

and
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}\simeq\mathbf{G}_{a}^{3}
. Denote by
\delta_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
the modulus character of
{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
, given by

\displaystyle\delta_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}

\displaystyle:\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&\\
&g&\\
&&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|\tfrac{a}{d}\right|^{2}.

Let
I_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
be the degenerate principal series representation of
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
consisting of smooth functions
f_{s}^{\prime}
on
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
such that

f_{s}^{\prime}(nmg)=\delta_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}^{\tfrac{1}{2}(s+1/2)}(m)f_{s}^{\prime}(g),\;\forall n\in N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A}),\forall m\in M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A}).

Given a smooth section
f_{s}^{\prime}\in I_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
, we define the degenerate (Klingen) Eisenstein series

{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s}^{\prime})={\sum_{\gamma\in{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})\backslash\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})}}f_{s}^{\prime}(\gamma g).

In the study of the poles of our integral, we employ two facts regarding this Eisenstein series.
The first one is a result of Ikeda and regards a first term identity between the Siegel and Klingen Eisenstein series.

Proposition 6.11
.

There exists a section
f_{1/2}^{\prime}\in I_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(1/2)
such that

{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)=\frac{{\rm Res}_{s=1}(\zeta^{S}(s))\zeta^{S}(3)}{2\zeta^{S}(2)}{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,1/2,f_{1/2}^{\prime}).

Proof.

This is basically
[
22
, Proposition 1.10]
. Notice that the result of
loc.cit.
is for
{\rm Sp}_{4}
-Eisenstein series. In order to use it, we consider the following. By
[
19
, p. 11]
, we have a factorization

\mathbf{H}({\bf A})=\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A}){\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf A})\mathbf{H}({\bf A}_{f}).

Then, given any Eisenstein series
E(g,s,f_{s})
defined over
\mathbf{H}({\bf A})
, we may write

E(g,s,f_{s})=E(g_{0}zg_{1}g_{f},s,f_{s})=E(g_{1}g_{f},s,f_{s})=E(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s}),

by the invariant properties of the Eisenstein series, and where
r(g_{f})
denotes the right multiplication action of
g_{f}
on
f_{s}
.
In particular, the values of the Eisenstein series
E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s})
and
{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s}^{\prime})
at
g
equal to the values at
g_{1}
of the Eisenstein series
E_{P_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s})
,
E_{Q_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s}^{\prime})
for
{\rm Sp}_{4}
associated respectively to the parabolics
P_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}=P_{\mathbf{H}}\cap{\rm Sp}_{4}
and
Q_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}=Q_{\mathbf{H}}\cap{\rm Sp}_{4}
, and to the elements
r(g_{f})f_{s}\in{\rm Ind}_{P({\bf A})}^{{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf A})}\nu_{P}^{s+1}
,
r(g_{f})f_{s}^{\prime}\in{\rm Ind}_{Q({\bf A})}^{{\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf A})}\nu_{Q}^{2s+1}
. Here we denoted

\displaystyle\nu_{P}:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&\star\\
&I_{2}^{\prime}{}^{t}g^{-1}I_{2}^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|{\rm det}(g)\right|,

\displaystyle\nu_{Q}:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&\\
&g&\\
&&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mapsto\left|a\right|.

With respect to
[
22
]
, the Eisenstein series
E_{P_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s})
, resp.
E_{Q_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s}^{\prime})
have complex variable
s
shifted by
-1/2
. We now apply
[
22
, Proposition 1.10]
, which says that, given
\omega
the longest element of
W_{M_{\mathbf{H}}}\backslash W_{\mathbf{H}}
(
M_{\mathbf{H}}
being the Levi of
P_{\mathbf{H}}
) and
M_{\omega}
the corresponding intertwining operator, then

{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s})=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{Q_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},1/2,{\rm Res}_{s=1}M_{\omega}(r(g_{f})f_{s})).

As
M_{\omega}
is an intertwining map, the action of
g_{f}
commutes with it, hence we have

{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},s,r(g_{f})f_{s})=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{Q_{{\rm Sp}_{4}}}(g_{1},1/2,{\rm Res}_{s=1}r(g_{f})M_{\omega}(f_{s})).

This implies that
{\rm Res}_{s=1}E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,f_{s})
equals to
\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s,{\rm Res}_{s=1}M_{\omega}(f_{s}))
, i.e.
f_{1/2}^{\prime}
can be taken to be
{\rm Res}_{s=1}M_{\omega}(f_{s})
. After normalising
f_{s}
, we thus get the desired result for the normalised Eisenstein series
E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)
(see also
[
22
, Proposition 1.8]
).
∎

The second fact needed concerns the vanishing of the following integral:

(41)

J(\varphi,f_{s}^{\prime},s):=\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh,

where
\varphi
is a cusp form in
\pi
and
{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,s)
is the degenerate Klingen Eisenstein series for
\mathbf{H}
.

Proposition 6.12
.

The integral
J(\varphi,f_{s}^{\prime},s)
is identically zero.

Remark 6.13
.

Notice that if the induced datum of the Klingen Eisenstein series is not degenerate, the corresponding integral calculates the
\wedge^{2}_{t}\times{\rm Std}

L
-function on
{{\rm GU}}_{2,2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
. A proof of this claim will appear in forthcoming work of the authors.

Proof of Proposition
6.12
.

We start by unfolding the Eisenstein series. For big enough
{\rm Re}(s)
, we get

\int_{{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\varphi(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over the unipotent radical
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
of
{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\int_{N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})\backslash N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})}\varphi(nh)dndh.

We now Fourier expand over
[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]
, with
N^{\prime\prime}
being the unipotent radical of the Klingen parabolic of
\mathbf{G}
. The integral equals

\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\sum_{\chi}\varphi_{\chi}(h)dh,

where the sum runs over the characters
\chi:[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
and where we have denoted

\varphi_{\chi}(h)=\int_{N^{\prime\prime}({\bf Q})\backslash N^{\prime\prime}({\bf A})}\varphi(n^{\prime\prime}h)\chi^{-1}(n^{\prime\prime})dn^{\prime\prime}.

Any character
\chi:[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
can be described as follows. Recall that any character
\chi:[N^{\prime\prime}/[N^{\prime\prime},N^{\prime\prime}]]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
is of the form
n^{\prime\prime}\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}_{E/{\bf Q}}(\alpha x+\beta y)),
where
\alpha,\beta\in E
,
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character on
{\bf Q}\backslash{\bf A}
, and where we have written

n^{\prime\prime}\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x&y&0\\
&1&&\bar{y}\\
&&1&-\bar{x}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\;\text{ mod }[N^{\prime\prime},N^{\prime\prime}]\;\text{ with }x,y\in{\bf A}_{E}.

Such a character
\chi
is trivial on
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})
if
\alpha+\bar{\alpha}=\beta+\bar{\beta}=0
. We deduce that any character
\chi:[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]\to{\bf C}^{\times}
is of the form
n^{\prime\prime}\mapsto\psi({\rm Tr}_{E/{\bf Q}}(\alpha x+\beta y))
, with
\alpha,\beta\in E
such that
\bar{\alpha}=-\alpha
and
\bar{\beta}=-\beta
. To remark the dependence by these data, denote such a character by
\chi_{\alpha,\beta}
.

As
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
acts on
N^{\prime\prime}
by conjugation and this action preserves
N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}
,
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})
acts on the space of characters of
[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]
. Indeed, if
m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&\\
&g&\\
&&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})
and
g^{-1}=(\tilde{g}_{i,j})
,

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha,\beta}(mn^{\prime}m^{-1})

\displaystyle=\psi(a\cdot{\rm Tr}_{E/{\bf Q}}(\alpha(\tilde{g}_{1,1}x+\tilde{g}_{2,1}y)+\beta(\tilde{g}_{1,2}x+\tilde{g}_{2,2}y)))

\displaystyle=\psi({\rm Tr}_{E/{\bf Q}}(a(\alpha\tilde{g}_{1,1}+\beta\tilde{g}_{1,2})x+a(\alpha\tilde{g}_{2,1}+\tilde{g}_{2,2})y))

\displaystyle=\chi_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(n^{\prime}),

with
\alpha^{\prime}=a(\alpha\tilde{g}_{1,1}+\beta\tilde{g}_{1,2})
and
\beta^{\prime}=a(\alpha\tilde{g}_{2,1}+\tilde{g}_{2,2})
.
Then,
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})
acts on the space of characters of
[N^{\prime\prime}/N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}]
with two orbits, the trivial one and an open one. A representative of the open orbit is given by the character
\chi_{\alpha,0}
with
\bar{\alpha}=-\alpha\neq 0
. Using this action, we can write the integral as

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\int_{[N^{\prime\prime}]}\varphi(n^{\prime\prime}h)dn^{\prime\prime}dh+\int_{M_{\alpha}({\bf Q})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(h)dh,

where
M_{\alpha}({\bf Q})
denotes the stabiliser in
M_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf Q})
of
\chi_{\alpha,0}
. From the explicit description above, notice that

M_{\alpha}({\bf Q})=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&&\\
&a&b&\\
&&d&\\
&&&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\;\right\}=L_{\alpha}({\bf Q})N_{\alpha}({\bf Q}),

where
L_{\alpha}
(resp.
N_{\alpha}
) denotes its Levi part (resp. unipotent part).

The first integral of (
42
) vanishes because of cuspidality of
\varphi
along
[N^{\prime\prime}]
, hence

J(\varphi,f_{s}^{\prime},s)=\int_{M_{\alpha}({\bf Q})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(h)dh.

Collapse the sum over
[N_{\alpha}]
to get

\displaystyle J(\varphi,f_{s}^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle=\int_{L_{\alpha}({\bf Q})N_{\alpha}({\bf A})N_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}({\bf A})Z_{\mathbf{H}}\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}f_{s}^{\prime}(h)\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dndh.

Notice that the inner integral
\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dn
equals the Fourier coefficient of
\varphi
with respect to the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and the choice of a character on it which is degenerate (as it’s only supported on the entry (1,2)). As a consequence,
\int_{[N_{\alpha}]}\varphi_{\chi_{\alpha,0}}(nh)dn
contains, as an inner integral, the period of
\varphi
over the unipotent radical of the Siegel parabolic of
\mathbf{G}
, which vanishes by cuspidality of
\varphi
.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.4.2.
Periods and residues

For any cusp form
\varphi
on
\mathbf{G}({\bf A}_{\bf Q})
, we consider

\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(g):=\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\varphi(hg)dh.

This automorphic period vanishes if
\varphi
is any globally generic cusp form.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
\Pi
be a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{G}({\bf A}_{\bf Q})
. For any cusp form
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A}_{\bf Q})
, the period
\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(g)=0
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm Sp}_{4}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathbf{H}
, the result is implied by showing that, for any
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}({\bf A}_{\bf Q})
,

\varphi^{{\rm Sp}_{4}}(g):=\int_{[{\rm Sp}_{4}]}\varphi(hg)dh=0.

We show the latter by contradiction. Suppose that there exists
\varphi
in
\Pi
and
g\in\mathbf{G}
for which
\varphi^{{\rm Sp}_{4}}(g)\neq 0
. This defines a non-trivial
{\rm Sp}_{4}
-functional on
\Pi
. Let
p
be an unramified prime for
\Pi
which splits in
E
. We can further assume that
g_{p}=1
. Since
p
splits, we can identify
\Pi_{p}
with an unramified generic representation
\pi_{p}
of
{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})
with trivial central character. As the linear span
V_{p}
of the
\textbf{P}\mathbf{G}({\bf Q}_{p})
-right translates of
\varphi
is isomorphic to
\pi_{p}
, the map

V_{p}\ni\varphi^{\prime}\mapsto\left(g^{\prime}\mapsto\int_{[{\rm Sp}_{4}]}\varphi^{\prime}(hgg^{\prime})dh\right)

defines an injective intertwining map in
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})}(\pi_{p},{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\rm Sp}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})\backslash{\rm GL}_{4}({\bf Q}_{p})))
. This contradicts the genericity of
\pi_{p}
(
cf
.
[
20
, Theorem 3.2.2]
).
∎

6.4.3.
Location of poles

Proposition
6.12
and Lemma
6.15
imply the following.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
\Pi
be a globally generic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathbf{G}({\bf A})
, with trivial central character and let
\varphi
be a cusp form in
\Pi
. The integral
I(\varphi,s)
is holomorphic for all
s
.

Proof.

Recall that the normalised Eisenstein series
E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)
achieves poles at
s=1,2
of order at most one, thus
I(\varphi,s)
may have a pole at these two values.

At
s=1
, the residue of the integral equals to

\displaystyle{\rm Res}_{s=1}I(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{s=1}\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Res}_{s=1}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Eis}_{Q_{\mathbf{H}}}(g,1/2,f_{1/2}^{\prime})\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot J(\varphi,f_{1/2}^{\prime},1/2),

where the third equality follows from Proposition
6.11
and
C=\frac{{\rm Res}_{s=1}(\zeta^{S}(s))\zeta^{S}(3)}{2\zeta^{S}(2)}
. The latter integral vanishes by Proposition
6.12
, hence
I(\varphi,s)
is holomorphic at
s=1
.

At
s=2
, the picture is different. Indeed,
s=2
is the end of the critical strip and the residue of the Eisenstein series is the constant representation, i.e. there exists
f_{s}\in I_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(s)
such that
{\rm Res}_{s=2}E_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}^{*}(g,s)
is constant. Therefore

\displaystyle{\rm Res}_{s=2}I(\varphi,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}{\rm Res}_{s=2}E^{*}_{P_{\mathbf{H}}}(h,s)\varphi(h)dh

\displaystyle=C\cdot\int_{\mathbf{H}({\bf Q})Z_{\mathbf{H}}({\bf A})\setminus\mathbf{H}({\bf A})}\varphi(h)dh.

Hence,
I(\varphi,s)
achieves a pole at
s=2
if
\varphi^{\mathbf{H}}(1)\neq 0
. However, the latter is always zero because of Lemma
6.15
.
∎

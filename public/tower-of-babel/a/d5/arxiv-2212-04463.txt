1
Preliminaries

Fast primality testing of a rational integer
N
relies on a theme of Lucas, wherein a finite group is constructed so (provably) large that
N
must be prime. Pomerance outlines the classical methods arising from this theme
[
26
]
. The binary modular exponentiation central to this Lucasian theme runs in
\widetilde{O}(\log^{2}N)
, providing a hypothetical lower bound to all primality testing barring a new theme. Pomerance proved that this hypothetical minimum bound is met: there exist
\widetilde{O}(\log^{2}N)
certifications of primality for every rational prime
[
25
,
26
]
.
Finding
these certifications, i.e. testing for primality, is another story. The fastest classical test relies on a known factorization of
N^{k}-1
for some fixed small
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and runs in heuristic
\widetilde{O}(\log^{3}N)
time:

Theorem 1.1

(Lenstra’s Finite Fields Test,
[
26
,
20
]
)
.

Let
N,k
positive integers,
N>1
and
f\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})[x]
monic of degree
k
. Suppose that
F|N^{k}-1
,
F>\sqrt{N}
, and
F
has a known prime factorization. If
\exists g\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})[x]
such that in
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})[x]/(f)
,

\displaystyle\text{(1) }g^{F}=1,

\displaystyle\text{(2) }\gcd(g^{\frac{F}{q}}-1,f)=1,\text{ for each prime }q|F,

\displaystyle\text{(3) }\text{each elementary symmetric polynomial in }g^{N^{j}},0\leq j\leq{k-1}

\displaystyle\text{ has coefficients in }\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},

and if none of the residues
N^{j}\mod F
,
0\leq j\leq k-1
, are proper factors of
N
, then
N
is prime.

Similar to Pocklington’s criterion, multiple bases
g
may be chosen
[
20
]
. The algorithm runs in heuristic
\widetilde{O}(k^{2}\log^{3}N)
, and tests based on it are referred to as cyclotomic primality tests
[
7
]
. The runtime of this algorithm is deterministically
\widetilde{O}(\log^{2}N)
if
k
is small and fixed, the number of prime powers of

is polynomial in
\log\log N
, and a suitable

(or multiple bases) is known; it remains heuristic
\widetilde{O}(\log^{2}N)
time even if the bases

are not known beforehand. This is the hypothetical minimum runtime of Lucasian tests, and an algorithm having such (at least heuristic) runtime will henceforth be referred to as an
efficient primality test
.

Beyond classical primality testing, the theory of Ellipitic Curve Primality Proving (ECPP) has been developed by such figues as Goldwasser, Kilian, Atkin, and Morain
[
11
,
4
]
. In the seminal Elliptic Curve Primality Proving paper by Atkin and Morain
[
4
]
, the theory of complex multiplication (CM) is used to determine the orders of the groups of points of certain elliptic curves and test for primality of any positive integer

time and stands as our fastest general algorithm in practice
[
23
,
18
]
. More recently, Milhailescu proposed a variant general primality test running one round of cyclotomic primality testing, followed by a round of ECPP, running in heuristic
\widetilde{O}(\log^{3}N)
time, which would stand as the fastest general algorithm
[
21
]
. Importantly, it does not reduce to an
efficient primality test
when the number of prime power factors of

is polynomial in
\log\log N
.
General testing is not yet
\widetilde{O}(\log^{2}N)

testing utilizing elliptic curves, including the works of Gurevich and Kunyavskiĭ, Tsumura, Gross, Denomme and Savin, and Chudnovsky and Chudnovsky

,

]
. A recent preprint in the same vein proposes an extension of the results to class number

]
. These works expand the class of rational integers amenable to efficient primality testing, including those of the form

for

and the norm of

In the open problems section of his celebrated work Reciprocity Laws: From Euler to Eisenstein

Can Lenstra’s Primality Test be generalized so as to include

In this paper, we answer this question in the affirmative for certain inputs in the case of CM elliptic curves, producing an analogue heuristic

Use the above discussion and the proof of Lemma

Now let

The norm-Euclidean quadratic number fields with

Theorem 2.20

Let

for

squarefree. Again let

Note that the lifts

are given precisely by
\iota X+\beta
for

and a choice of lift,
\beta

\mathfrak{I}=(\iota)
. In general, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
\iota,\beta,X\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be any elements. Assume
|X|\geq 4(-d)+4d^{2}
and
(-d+d^{2})|\iota|\geq|\beta|
. Then
|\iota X+\beta|\geq|\iota|
.

Proof.

If for a positive constant

, then by multiplicativity of field norms,
|\iota X|\geq(k(-d)+kd^{2})|\iota|
. Then we can write

with

or
b^{2}(-d)\geq|\iota X|/2
. Writing

\displaystyle|\iota X+\beta|

\displaystyle\geq\max((|a|-|c|)^{2},(|b|-|e|)^{2}d)\text{, since $|x+y\sqrt{d}|\geq x^{2},y^{2}d$ for $d<0$,}

\displaystyle\geq(\sqrt{|\iota X|/2}-\sqrt{(-d+d^{2})|\iota|/2})^{2}\text{, since $|\beta|\leq(-d+d^{2})|\iota|$,}

\displaystyle\geq(\sqrt{|\iota|(-kd+kd^{2})/2}-\sqrt{(-d+d^{2})|\iota|/2})^{2}\text{, by assumption,}

\displaystyle=1/2(-1+d)d(-1+\sqrt{k})^{2}|\iota|.

We want

, and for all

it suffices to choose

.

∎

To prove theorem

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a&-bd\\

\mathcal{O}_{K}
modulo
(\iota)

\iota

To transform

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}c\\

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}a\\

-a\end{bmatrix}B

(since we have subtracted elements of

\displaystyle|\beta|\leq|\max(|bd|,|a|)+\sqrt{d}\max(|a|,|b|)|

\displaystyle\leq d^{2}b^{2}(1-d)-da^{2}(1-d)

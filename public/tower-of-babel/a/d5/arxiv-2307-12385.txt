Heavy QCD axion model in light of pulsar timing arrays
Moslem Ahmadvand, Ligong Bian, Soroush Shakeri

Abstract
Recently, pulsar timing array experiments reported the observation of a stochastic gravitational wave background in the nanohertz range frequency band. We show that such a signal can be originated from a cosmological first-order phase transition (PT) within a well-motivated heavy (visible) QCD axion model. Considering the Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaking at the TeV scale in the scenario, we find a supercooled PT, in the parameter space of the model, prolonging the PT with the reheating temperature at the GeV scale.

II. PQ phase transition in the model

The CP-violating
\bar{\theta}
parameter in the strong interactions is experimentally bounded
\bar{\theta}\lesssim 10^{-10}

Baker et al. 2006

U(1)

U(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}

Peccei and Quinn 1977

(a/f_{a}+\bar{\theta})G\widetilde{G}
, and as a result the vacuum expectation value of the axion can cancel the
\bar{\theta}
term
Peccei 2008

10^{8}\,\mathrm{GeV}\lesssim f_{a}\lesssim 10^{17}\,\mathrm{GeV}

Raffelt 2008
;
Arvanitaki et al. 2010
;
Shakeri and Hajkarim 2023

Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Dine et al. 1981
;
Kim 1979
;
Shifman et al. 1980
. However, as mentioned before,
f_{a}
can be lowered,
f_{a}\sim(1-100)\,\mathrm{TeV}
in the so-called visible axion models, still addressing the strong CP problem. In this sense, there are diverse scenarios, e.g. enlarging the QCD color gauge group

Fukuda et al. 2015

SM\times SM^{\prime}

IV
IV. Discussion

Another PQ PT consequence of the model is that the symmetry breaking may induce a similar or different EW symmetry breaking scale in the ordinary and hidden sectors. For instance, as for
v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{\prime}
, this can be realized by these terms of the Lagrangian
\kappa^{\prime}_{u}h_{u}^{2}f_{a}^{2}+\lambda^{\prime}_{u}h_{u}^{4}
, expressed in terms of the electrically neutral component of the fields, and hence
v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{\prime 2}\simeq\kappa^{\prime}_{u}f_{a}^{2}/\lambda^{\prime}_{u}
. Setting
\kappa^{\prime}_{u}=0.0001
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{u}=0.1
, we obtain
v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{\prime}=1\,\mathrm{GeV}
. Even for these values of
v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{\prime}
its QCD scale could be
\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\gtrsim v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{EW}}

Fukuda et al. 2015
. Consequently, in this case, we obtain the axion mass
m_{a}\gtrsim 1\,\mathrm{MeV}
, while considering
v_{\mathrm{EW}}\lesssim v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{EW}}<f_{a}
and assuming
\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\lesssim v_{\mathrm{EW}}
, one finds
m_{a}\lesssim 10\,\mathrm{GeV}
.

Heavy axions may interact with visible and hidden (dark) photons

\frac{a}{f_{a}}\left(F\widetilde{F}+F^{\prime}\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\right),

(20)

so that the axion decay rate would be

\Gamma_{a\gamma\gamma}=\frac{g_{a\gamma}^{2}m_{a}^{3}}{64\pi},\quad\Gamma_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}=\frac{g_{a\gamma}^{\prime 2}m_{a}^{3}}{64\pi}

(21)

where

g_{a\gamma}\simeq\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2\pi}\frac{E}{Nf_{a}}\frac{2z}{1+z},~~~~~~g_{a\gamma}^{\prime}\simeq\frac{\bar{\alpha^{\prime}}}{2\pi}\frac{E}{Nf_{a}}\frac{2z^{\prime}}{1+z^{\prime}},

(22)

\bar{\alpha}=1/137
is the fine structure constant;

and
N

Di Luzio et al. 2020

\frac{c_{f}}{2f_{a}}\partial_{\mu}a\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}f+\frac{c^{\prime}_{f}}{2f_{a}}\partial_{\mu}a\bar{f^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}f^{\prime}.

Decaying the heavy axions to these photons contributes to the effective number of relativistic species which is strongly constrained by CMB measurements,

We assume a baryon asymmetric dark sector. Thus, considering

As a result, the relic abundance of these particles cannot give rise to cosmological problems. Moreover, we assume that in the hidden sector there is no seesaw mechanism, which allows the Dirac neutrino mass in this sector as

m_{v^{\prime}}\sim\frac{v^{\prime}_{\mathrm{EW}}}{v_{\mathrm{EW}}}\sqrt{M_{R}m_{v}},

(24)

V
V. Conclusion

With regard to the recently detected GW signals by PTA experiments, we have proposed a heavy QCD axion scenario, with a PQ symmetry breaking scale around the TeV scale, based on a (DFSZ) axion model which is supplemented with its hidden copy. We investigated a supercooled PQ FOPT which is derived by CW quantum corrections and analytically found important PT quantities, including the reheating temperature, the inverse duration of the PT, and the strength of the PT. We have shown that within the parameter space of the model the generated GWs from the bubble wall collisions can be consistently fitted with the recently observed PTA data. Furthermore, it is shown that the high-frequency range of such GW signals can be probed by future space-based GW detectors.

In addition, Heavy axion models are well-motivated scenarios allowing the
(m_{a},f_{a})
relation to be relaxed, still addressing the strong CP problem. It is shown that considering the EW symmetry breaking scale in the ordinary and hidden sectors induced after the PQ symmetry breaking, a range of axion masses,
1\,\mathrm{MeV}\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 10\,\mathrm{GeV}
can be obtained. Nevertheless, viable regions of the mass space are limited via CMB observation due to the dark photon contribution to
N_{\mathrm{eff}}
, and also other experimental setups such as the proton beam dump.

As a result, interpreting the recent GW data, the model provides a setup which can be probed by future GW detectors, CMB telescopes and collider experiments.

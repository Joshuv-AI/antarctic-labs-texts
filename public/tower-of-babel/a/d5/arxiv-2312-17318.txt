Spectral sum rules reflect topological and quantum-geometric invariants
Alexander Kruchkov, Shinsei Ryu

Abstract
Topological invariants are fundamental characteristics reflecting global properties of quantum systems, yet their exploration has predominantly been limited to the static (DC) transport and transverse (Hall) channel. In this work, we extend the spectral sum rules for frequency-resolved electric conductivity σ ⁡ ( ω ) \sigma(\omega) in topological systems, and show that the sum rule for the longitudinal channel is expressed through topological and quantum-geometric invariants. We find that for dispersionless (flat) Chern bands, the rule is expressed as, ∫ − ∞ + ∞ d ​ ω ​ Re ​ ( σ x ​ x + σ y ​ y ) = C ​ Δ ​ e 2 \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy})=C\Delta e^{2} , where C C is the Chern number, Δ \Delta the topological gap, and e e the electric charge. In scenarios involving dispersive Chern bands, the rule is defined by the invariant of the quantum metric, and Luttinger invariant, ∫ − ∞ + ∞ d ​ ω ​ Re ​ ( σ x ​ x + σ y ​ y ) = 2 ​ π ​ e 2 ​ Δ ​ ∑ 𝐤 Tr ​ 𝒢 i ​ j ​ ( 𝐤 ) \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy})=2\pi e^{2}\Delta\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}) +(Luttinger invariant), where Tr ​ 𝒢 i ​ j \text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij} is invariant of the Fubini-Study metric (defining spread of Wannier orbitals). We further discuss the physical role of topological and quantum-geometric invariants in spectral sum rules. Our approach is adaptable across varied topologies and system dimensionalities.



In quantum matter, topological invariants are fundamental mathematical constructs reflecting the global properties of non-interacting and interacting systems. Tracing back to the TKNN invariant in quantized Hall effect
Thouless et al. 1982
, topological and quantum-geometric notions and their experimental observables play a crucial role in advancing quantum matter research, in particular in the directions of unconventional superconductivity

;
Zeng et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023

Despite these advances, the frequency-resolved quantum transport and the generalized spectral sum rules remain an under-explored frontier in quantum matter research.

In the seminal paper

Among accomplishments of the paper, Kubo formulated the

(1)

where

The significance of the sum rule (

For Bloch-periodic solids, the conventional spectral sum rule (

Typically,

Yet there are known deviations from this procedure, namely in the systems where the effective low-energy description does not presume an effective mass (e.g. for Dirac fermions in monolayer graphene

Second, Kubo’s original framework explicitly omits the interband effects, inherent to topological systems.
Third,

In this paper, we extend the spectral sum rules to the case of topological systems, with a special focus on those with flat topological bands. We discover that in the systems described by (nearly)-dispersionless electronic bands, the spectral sum rule is defined through the topological and quantum-geometric invariants of the system.
Nontrivial Wannier orbitals in topological systems contribute extra spectral weight due to the interband Berry connections, as depicted in Fig. 1.

Specifically, for the case of two flat Chern bands, we find that the spectral sum rule is given by the

\displaystyle+\text{Luttinger invariant}.

These formulations, applicable to weakly interacting topological systems,

We shall later discuss the physical significance of these invariants.
Finally, we attribute a topological interpretation to the conventional Kubo sum rule by considering windings of the (interacting) Green’s function.

I
Methodology

In this work, we employ quantum transport theory, focusing on fermionic systems with moderate interactions that preserve the order of quasiparticle poles (albeit altering the quasiparticle weight).
We consider finite temperatures (unless stated otherwise) and use the Matsubara framework for calculations. The central object of our investigation is the current-current correlator (with

It is essential to clarify the action of current

II
Sum rule for topologically-trivial bands

Firstly, we reexamine the optical sum rule for topologically-trivial bands to rederive Kubo’s 1957 result. However, our approach here differs, based chiefly on the analytical properties of the (interacting) Green’s functions without relying on their explicit mathematical form.
In this section, we focus on a single-band electronic system characterized by the dispersion relation

{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{k}}={\varepsilon}(\mathbf{k})
, and
consider additive properties of
\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}=(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy})

Given common crystallographic symmetries, such as

\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}=\sigma

For the topologically-trivial band, the only contribution to Bloch velocity (

Thus Eq. (

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}V^{2}_{\mathbf{k}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})\,A_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon}})C_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon};\omega}).

For now, we refrain from making assumptions on the explicit form of propagators

(13)

Topological interpretation of the Kubo sum rule
.
The immunity of the spectral sum rule against interactions suggests a topological framing for Eq. (

Our derivation above,

III
Dispersionless topological bands

Having revisited the conventional Kubo sum rule (
24
), it’s important to highlight the crucial step in this derivation. It is the
omitting the interband effects
, i.e., the second term in matrix elements,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{nm}=\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}\,\delta_{nm}+\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{\mathbf{k}}u_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle.

(33)

and retaining the diagonal terms proportional to the quasiparticle velocity in the
n
th band,
\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}
, which leads to Kubo’s spectral sum rule (
24
).

In contrast, we now examine a scenario prioritizing the second, interband term in Eq. (
33
) while omitting the first, dispersive term,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{V}}^{\rm flat}_{nm}=\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{\mathbf{k}}u_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle.

(34)

This scenario characterizes bands as predominantly flat across the entire Brillouin zone, i.e.,
\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}\to 0
. Moreover, the system shows topological characteristics, as the Berry connection
\mathcal{A}_{nm}(\mathbf{k})
\equiv
-i\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{\mathbf{k}}u_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle
is significant. This set of criteria defines
dispersionless topological states
, which have been recently achieved in moiré heterostructures, including twisted graphene multilayers and twisted transition metal dichalcogenides
Tarnopolsky et al. 2019
;
Khalaf et al. 2019
;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Wang and Liu 2022
;
Ledwith et al. 2022
;
Kruchkov 2022
. In such instances, the multiband Berry connection introduces a nontrivial, momentum-dependent
quantum metric
, which will dominate our discussion in this section.

III.1
Calculation of the spectral sum rule for perfectly flat bands

In order to address the effects of quantum metric, it is useful to further symmetrize expression for the real part of frequency-resolved conductivity (
7
). Indeed, using invariance of the Trace under circular shifts, one can rewrite the same expression in the fully-symmetrized form,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{8}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})\text{Tr}\,\left[A_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon}})V_{i}C_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon};\omega})V_{j}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.C_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon};\omega})\mathcal{V}_{i}A_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon}})\mathcal{V}_{j}+(i\leftrightarrow j)\right].

(35)

This symmetrization facilitates subsequent index operations.

Using the symmetrized form (
35
), and formula (
34
), one can take trace in the band basis, leading to

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=-\frac{e^{2}}{8}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{n,m}\Delta_{nm}^{2}(\mathbf{k})\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})\left\{\left[A_{n}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{m}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})+A_{m}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{n}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{i}|u_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{m\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{j}|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle+(i\leftrightarrow j)\right\}.

(36)

Here
A_{n}(\varepsilon;\mathbf{k})
,
C_{m}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k}
) denote diagonal elements of matrices

A_{{\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)
,
C_{\mathbf{k}}(\varepsilon;\omega)

in the band basis.
The entity in the second line of Eq. (
36
) is the multiband quantum geometric tensor
\mathfrak{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
, hence

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=-\frac{e^{2}}{8}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{n,m}\Delta_{nm}^{2}(\mathbf{k})\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})\left\{\left[A_{n}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{m}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})+A_{m}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{n}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})\right]\left[\mathfrak{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})+\mathfrak{G}^{nm}_{ji}(\mathbf{k})\right]\right\}.

(37)

This represents the most refined expression of AC conductivity via the quantum metric.

Employing Eq. (
37
), we can calculate the spectral sum for dispersionless topological bands. Specifically, we find

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=-\frac{e^{2}}{4}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{nm}F_{nm}\Delta_{nm}^{2}(\mathbf{k})\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

(38)

where Trace operation on both sides of equation acts only on lower indices (
i,j
),
and tensor
F_{nm}
is given by

\displaystyle F_{nm}=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\left[A_{n}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{m}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.A_{m}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})C_{n}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})\right].

(39)

Using expression

\displaystyle C_{m}({{\varepsilon};\omega};\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{G^{R}_{m}({{\varepsilon}-\omega},\mathbf{k})-G^{R}_{m}({{\varepsilon}+\omega};\mathbf{k})}{i\omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{A}_{m}({{\varepsilon}+\omega},\mathbf{k})-G^{A}_{m}({{\varepsilon}-\omega},\mathbf{k})}{i\omega}

(40)

we arrive to expression

\displaystyle F_{nm}=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})A_{n}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})A_{m}(\omega^{\prime};\mathbf{k})I(\omega^{\prime},{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n\leftrightarrow m).

(41)

where

\displaystyle I(\omega^{\prime},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\omega}{i\omega}\left[\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}-\omega-\omega^{\prime}+i\delta}-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}+\omega-\omega^{\prime}+i\delta}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}+\omega-\omega^{\prime}-i\delta}-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}-\omega-\omega^{\prime}-i\delta}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\omega^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}+i\delta}+\frac{2\pi}{\omega^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}-i\delta}.

(42)

In the final line of Eq. (
42
), we have utilized the residue theorem. Subsequently, by invoking the Sokhotsky-Plemelj theorem, one finds that tensor
F_{nm}
, defining the sum rule, is simplified to

\displaystyle F_{nm}=4\pi\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})A_{n}({\varepsilon},\mathbf{k})\fint\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{A_{m}(\omega^{\prime};\mathbf{k})d\omega^{\prime}}{{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\prime}}+(n\leftrightarrow m).

(43)

The combination of formulas (
38
) and symmetric
\mathbf{k}
-dependent tensor (
43
) represents the most general expression for the spectral sum rule in dispersionless topological states, and serves as a cornerstone result of our work.
Below we show how this formula simplifies in the ”clean limit.”

Clean limit
.—Considering a noninteracting system at an arbitrary temperature, the spectral function simplifies to a delta function,
A^{n}_{\mathbf{k}}({{\varepsilon}})=\delta({\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})
.
This, in turn, leads to explicit expression for the tensor
F_{nm}
,

\displaystyle F_{nm}

\displaystyle=4\pi f({\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})\,\fint\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\frac{\delta(\omega^{\prime}-{\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}})}{{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\omega^{\prime}}+(m\leftrightarrow n)

\displaystyle=4\pi\frac{f({\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})}{{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}-{\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}}}+(m\leftrightarrow n)=\frac{4\pi[f({\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})-f({\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}})]}{\Delta_{mn}(\mathbf{k})}.

Using this expression in Eq. (
38
), we obtain the sum for the clean limit of perfectly flat bands as

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,[\sigma_{ij}(\omega)]=\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{nm}f_{nm}\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(44)

where
f_{nm}=f({\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})-f({\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}})
is the difference in Fermi-Dirac distribution functions.
Equation (
44
) applies to a noninteracting system and incorporates temperature dependence through function
f_{nm}(T)
. Generally, this contribution (
44
) is non-vanishing. A straightforward demonstration follows with a selected example below.

Two Flat Chern Bands at Zero Temperature
.— Consider a prototypical topological system consisting of two flat electronic bands distinguished by their Chern invariants
\pm C
, and separated by a gap of value
\Delta
. These bands fulfill the ”trace condition”
Kruchkov 2022
;
Roy 2014
,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=|\mathcal{F}_{xy}(\mathbf{k})|,

(45)

relating their quantum metric trace with the magnitude of the Berry curvature
F_{xy}
. Assuming the lower band is fully occupied, the upper band is vacant, and
\mathcal{F}_{xy}>0
for the lowest band, we derive the spectral sum rule

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}[\sigma_{xx}(\omega)+\sigma_{yy}(\omega)]

\displaystyle=2\pi e^{2}\Delta\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=2\pi e^{2}\Delta\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\mathcal{F}_{xy}(\mathbf{k}),

or simply

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}[\sigma_{xx}(\omega)+\sigma_{yy}(\omega)]=Ce^{2}\Delta.

(46)

In the last line, we have used the definition of the (first) Chern number

\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d\mathbf{k}\ \mathcal{F}_{xy}.

(47)

Therefore, we find that in the perfectly flat bands, the spectral sum rule is expressed through the topological invariants of the system, in this case the first Chern number.
For a symmetric system where
\sigma_{xx}=\sigma_{yy}=\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}\sigma(\omega)=\frac{Ce^{2}\Delta}{4}.

(48)

IV
Spectral sum rules for dispersive topological systems

In the prior sections, we have explored two distinct scenarios and their underlying assumptions leading to the spectral sum rules. Starting from the equation

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{nm}=\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}}\,\delta_{nm}+\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|\partial_{\mathbf{k}}|u_{m\mathbf{k}}\rangle,

(49)

which defines the lattice currents, we have derived:
(a) For dispersive nontopological bands (Sec. II), neglecting the second term in (
49
) resulted in the conventional spectral sum rule defined by the Luttinger invariant.
(b) For dispersionless topological bands (Sec. III), omitting the first term in (
49
) has led us to the spectral sum rule characterized by the topological invariants.
We now aim to unify these two disparate cases by examining the spectral sum rule for
dispersive topological bands
.

Gauge invariance.
— At first glance, integrating cross-terms from (
49
) might yield terms linear in the interband Berry connection,
\mathcal{A}_{nm}
, a quantity which is not gauge-invariant. On the other hand, the frequency-resolved conductivity
\sigma(\omega)
, being a physical observable,
must
maintain gauge invariance.
Previous sections sidestepped this discussion by either (i) omitting
\mathcal{A}_{nm}
in Sec. II, or (ii) including it in gauge-invariant combinations in Sec. III.
To clarify, let us rewrite the expression AC conductivity (
50
) using the properties of Trace operation,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{m,n}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d{\varepsilon}f({\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\times[A_{n}\mathcal{V}^{(i)}_{nm}C_{m}\mathcal{V}^{(j)}_{mn}+A_{m}\mathcal{V}^{(i)}_{mn}C_{n}\mathcal{V}^{(j)}_{nm}].

(50)

A direct substitution of
Eq. (
49
) in Eq. (
50
) makes evident that gauge-dependent cross-terms cancel each other.
Consequently, neither the frequency-resolved conductivity nor the spectral sum rule depends on cross-terms containing linear in
\mathcal{A}_{nm}
terms. The outcome remains
gauge-invariant
, as anticipated.
Owing to gauge invariance,
formula Eq. (
50
), has two separate contribions,
\mathcal{O}[(\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{\varepsilon}_{n\mathbf{k}})^{2}]
and
\mathcal{O}[\mathcal{A}_{nm}^{2}]
. We refer to these additive terms as
intraband
and
interband
contributions.

Intraband contributions.—
We first start from the intraband contributions. In agreement with our previous derivation (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{m}Z_{\mathbf{k}}n_{\mathbf{k}}\,\text{Tr}\,[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}{\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}}],

(51)

where the trace is taken in
i,j
basis.
When the Fermi level resides within the gap, the formula at
T=0
in the clean limit simplifies to

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma^{\rm intra}_{ij}(\omega)=\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\left.\sum_{m}\right.^{{}^{\prime}}\text{Tr}\,[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}{\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}}],

(52)

with the prime over the sum indicating summation over occupied bands.
This contribution precisely aligns with the Kubo formula of
Ref.
Kubo 1957
, which omits the interband processes.
In the insulating phase, the intraband term (
52
) vanishes.

Interband contributions.—
We now incorporate the interband processes from
Eq. (
49
) to frequency-resolved conductivity (
50
). This procedure is analogous to derivations described in Sec. III, which gives

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma^{\rm inter}_{ij}(\omega)=\pi e^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{nm}[f_{nm}\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})]

\displaystyle\times\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(53)

Consequently, the
generalized spectral sum rule
for topological systems combines both the conventional (dispersive) and quantum-geometric contributions, represented by

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{m}\left\{n_{\mathbf{k}}\,\text{Tr}\,[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}{\varepsilon}_{m\mathbf{k}}]\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\neq m}\left.[f_{nm}\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})]\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\right\},

(54)

where we assume quasiparticles well-defined, i.e., with
Z_{\mathbf{k}}\approx 1
.
The generalized sum rule (
18
) for dispersive topological systems, while not explicitly referencing topological invariants, relates to quantum-geometric and Luttinger invariants.
The link of Eq. (
18
) to the topological invariants, such as Chern numbers, manifests in the spectral inequalities
(Appendix A)
.

Two flat Chern bands at zero temperature.
—
Examining a two-band Chern insulator with the Fermi level placed within the topological gap, the dispersive term in Eq. (
54
) drops out, and the important term, incorporating the interband quantum metric effects, significantly simplifies.
Due to the inherent symmetry of the two-band Chern insulator, we have
\mathcal{G}^{(1)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{G}^{(2)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
, and
f_{12}\Delta_{12}=f_{21}\Delta_{21}
. Furtheron, by employing partial summation identity (
11
), we arrive to the sum rule

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Tr}\,\sigma_{ij}(\omega)=2\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\Delta(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

(55)

where

\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
is the Fubini-Study metric of either band.
This sum rule is reminiscent of the recent finding of integrated current noise in topological systems, which is proportional to
\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Delta^{2}(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})

Kruchkov and Ryu 2023
.
This attributes to a similar internal structure of the integrands in Kubo quantum transport theory.

Consideration for the relevant crystallographic symmetries, such as
C_{3}
, leads to symmetric conductivity tensors
\sigma_{xx}(\omega)
=
\sigma_{yy}(\omega)
=
\,\sigma(\omega)
. Therefore, the sum rule for the Chern insulator (
55
) further simplifies as

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}\sigma(\omega)=\frac{\pi e^{2}}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\Delta(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(56)

This formulation echoes the original Kubo sum rule (
1
) upon assigning

m_{*}=\left[n\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Delta(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\right]^{-1}
, where
m_{*}\neq 0
is a parameter possessing the dimensionality of mass.

Quantum-geometric invariant.

Quantum-geometric entity
\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
,
entering sum rules for

\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}[\sigma(\omega)]

and explicitly in

\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\text{Re}[\sigma(\omega)]/\omega

(and, more generically, in sum rules of form
\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{n}\,\text{Re}[\sigma(\omega)]
),
has a specific meaning as the gauge-invariant part of Wannier spread functional
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
. Its physical interpretation relates to the overlap of electronic orbitals in topological systems, connecting to the expectation values of the squared position operator.
Indeed, by introducing the Wannier functions
\mathcal{W}^{(n)}_{\mathbf{R}}
by virtue of relation.
|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle\equiv\sum_{\mathbf{R}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}}|\mathcal{W}^{(n)}_{\mathbf{R}}\rangle
, one can show that the spread of electronic orbitals

\displaystyle\Omega=\sum_{n}\left[\langle r^{2}_{n}\rangle-\langle r_{n}\rangle^{2}\right],

(57)

can be decomposed into two terms

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{I}+\tilde{\Omega}.

(58)

Both terms are positive-definite; however, only term

\Omega_{I}
is gauge-invariant. Marzari and Vanderbilt demonstrated that this gauge-invariant component can be represented as
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

(59)

which corresponds to the Brillouin-zone-averaged quantum metric. Moreover,
\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{k})
is by definition one of the invariants of quantum metric. We henceforth refer to invariant (
59
) as the
Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant
.
This invariant underpins the formulation of maximally-localized Wannier orbitals
Marzari et al. 2012
.
Notably, in topological systems, invariant
\Omega_{I}
cannot take zero values due to topological constraints that hinder the construction of maximally-localized Wannier orbitals
Thouless 1984
;
Brouder et al. 2007
. Consequently, the lower bound of the invariant

\Omega_{I}
is bounded from below by topological invariants, ensuring substantial orbital overlap
Kruchkov 2022
.

The sum rules for topological systems of type (
56
) can be physically interpreted through the role of Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant.
As quantum-geometric invariant
\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{k})
is responsible for finite and significant overlap in electronic orbitals in topological bands, electrons in such system become strongly entangled. The resulting AC currents are strongly influenced by the degree of electronic overlap
\Omega_{I}
, which result into the sum rules (
56
):
The larger is the orbital overlap, the larger is the spectral sum rule invariant in topological systems. Finally, in dispersionless Chern bands,
\Omega_{I}
up to a dimensional prefactor coincides with the Chern number
C

Kruchkov 2022
, thus offering simple physical interpretation to the sum rule invariant
\equiv Ce^{2}\Delta
in Sec. III.

V
Overview

In this study, we have refined the spectral sum rules for topological systems.
We have found that unique topological features, paired with nontrivial quantum geometry, introduce previously overlooked terms in the spectral sum rules.
Traditional derivations, starting from Kubo’s pioneering work
Kubo 1957
, largely focused on dispersive, topologically-trivial systems, neglecting the interband contributions to electric conductivity.
Yet, for topological fermions, the interband contributions culminate into the sum rule altered by the term

\displaystyle\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\sum_{m,n}f_{nm}\Delta_{nm}(\mathbf{k})\,\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

with
\mathcal{G}^{nm}_{ij}
representing the underlying quantum metric. Given the nascent exploration of quantum metric in quantum materials, it is understandable that such the quantum-geometric effects in sum rules have been overlooked for years. Existing conductivity formulas had not been addressing quantum-geometric effects.

Figure 2:
Current-current correlator. Solid lines represent the interacting propagator, wiggly lines denote insertion of the current operator, vertex corrections are not shown (footnote [27]). The diagrams naturally embed invariants of the system. For the interacting Fermi liquid, this invariant is Luttinger invariant, represented by the conserved
U(1)
charge.

Moreover, quantum-geometric effects on spectral sum rules often simplify to fundamental invariants: the Luttinger invariant (Sec. II), topological invariants (Chern number in Sec. III), and the quantum-geometric invariants (Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant in Sec. IV). Notably, even the conventional Kubo sum rule (
1
) permits a topological reinterpretation, explaining its well-established robustness against interactions from a fresh perspective.

Our detailed analysis underscores that spectral sum rules inherently convey system invariants. In diagrammatic terms (see Fig.
2
), transport diagrams upon external frequency summation embed information on the invariants of the fermionic system. For interacting Fermi liquids, these diagrams reveal Luttinger invariant, i.e., the conservation of
U(1)
charge, as was established by Kubo and subsequent research. In dispersive topological systems, the diagrams with topological currents reveal additional
Marzari-Vanderbilt
invariant, responsible for the overlap of current-carrying electronic orbitals. In a dispersionless topological state, the transport diagrams contain the only possible invariant, the system-specific topological charge.

Historically, topological invariants have been predominantly probed using quantum Hall measurements, shot noise, and local probes
Hasan and Kane 2010
. Notably, our findings suggest that the spectral sums measurement can serve as a different tool for direct probing topological and quantum-geometric invariants, and provide further information on the topological band gaps
Kruchkov and Ryu 2023
, without usage of Hall-bar experimental platforms.

Appendix A: Geometric inequalities, band gaps, and topological invariants

Mathematical inequalities
.
Since quantum geometric tensor is nonnegatively-definite, there is a number of useful mathematical inequalities between its real part (Funini-Study metric
\mathcal{G}
) and imaginary part (Berry curvature
\mathcal{F}
).
In particular, the ”determinant inequality” reads
Jackson et al. 2015
;
Roy 2014

\displaystyle 4\det\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\geq|\mathcal{F}_{xy}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}.

(60)

This inequality sets the lower bound on
quantum volume
.

The second, stricter inequality addresses relation between the trace of quantum metric and Berry curvature modulus,

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\geq|\mathcal{F}_{xy}(\mathbf{k})|.

(61)

This inequality is saturated in ideal flat Chern bands, leading to the so-called ”trace condition” (
45
);
see further discussions in Refs.
Kruchkov 2022
;
Ledwith et al. 2023
. As a consequence, the gauge-invariant part of Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant (
59
) of ideal flat Chern bands is quantized
Kruchkov 2022
.
In dimensionless units, one obtains

\displaystyle\Omega^{\text{flat}}_{I}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=|C|/2\pi.

(62)

Souza, Wilkens and Martin (SWM) formula.

Previously, Souza, Wilkens and Martin
Souza et al. 2000
have established the following sum rule

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\frac{\text{Re}[\sigma_{xx}(\omega)+\sigma_{yy}(\omega)]}{\omega}=\pi e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(63)

Note that there is an extra factor of
1/\omega
in the integrand. As a consequence, expression (
63
) does not contain the band gap
\Delta
.
This expression agrees with the results of Sec. IV and the sum rule for current noise
Kruchkov and Ryu 2023
. Comparing these results, the
n

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{n}\,\sigma(\omega)\propto\Delta^{n+1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\rm BZ}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle+\text{[dispersive terms, if any]}.

(64)

Compositional Semantics of Finite Petri Nets
Roberto Gorrieri

Abstract
Structure-preserving bisimilarity [ 11 ] is a truly concurrent behavioral equivalence for finite Petri nets, which relates markings (of the same size only) generating the same causal nets, hence also the same partial orders of events. The process algebra FNM [ 14 ] truly represents all (and only) the finite Petri nets, up to isomorphism. We prove that structure-preserving bisimilarity is a congruence w.r.t. the FMN operators, In this way, we have defined a compositional semantics, fully respecting causality and the branching structure of systems, for the class of all the finite Petri nets. Moreover, we study some algebraic properties of structure-preserving bisimilarity, that are at the base of a sound (but incomplete) axiomatization over FNM process terms.

Introduction

Structure-preserving bisimilarity
[
11
]
(sp-bisimilarity, for short) is a truly concurrent, bisimulation-based, behavioral equivalence for finite P/T Petri nets
[
27
,
7
]
,
such that if two
markings are related, then they generate the same causal nets
[
10
,
2
,
24
,
11
]
, hence ensuring that they have the same size and
generate the same partial orders of events. As discussed in
[
11
]
, sp-bisimilarity
\sim_{sp}
is the coarsest behavioral equivalence that respects the following three aspects:

(i)
the branching
structure (being based on the concept of bisimulation),
(ii)
causality (being slightly finer than
fully-concurrent bisimilarity

[
3
]
)
and
(iii)

inevitability

[
22
]
, meaning that if two
systems are equivalent, and in one the occurrence of a certain action is inevitable, then so is it in the other one. Moreover,

\sim_{sp}
is decidable on bounded finite Petri nets
[
4
]
, while its decidability for unbounded nets is an open problem.

Finite P/T Petri nets can be represented, up to isomorphism, by means of the process algebra FNM
[
14
]
, which is a CCS-like
sub-calculus extended with an additional operator, called
strong prefixing
. This operator allows for
atomic execution of action sequences, so that
a multi-party interaction can be modeled as an atomic execution of a sequence of binary interactions.

The main aim of this paper is to show that sp-bisimilarity is a congruence for the operators of FNM, thus yielding
a compositional semantics, up to sp-bisimilarity, for the class of all the finite P/T Petri nets. Moreover, we study some algebraic properties of
sp-bisimilarity and, based on these, we introduce a sound (but incomplete) axiomatization over FNM process terms.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the basic definitions about Petri nets, some novel definitions (notably, the
distinction between dynamically vs statically reachable subnet) and the definition of structure-preserving bisimilarity.
Section
3
recalls from
[
14
]
the process algebra FNM, its (operational) semantics in terms of
finite P/T Petri nets, and also
the representability theorem stating that each finite (statically reachable) marked Petri net can be represented by a suitable FNM term, up to isomorphism.
Section
4
shows that sp-bisimilarity is a congruence for the FNM operators.
Section
5
presents a set of algebraic properties of sp-bisimilarity, some of which are conditional, and hints that
these do not cover all the possible equalities that can be singled out.
Based on this set of algebraic properties, Section
6
shows a sound, but incomplete,
axiomatization of sp-bisimilarity over FNM process terms.
Finally, Section
7
is devoted to some conclusions.

Basic Definitions

Definition 1

(Multiset)

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of natural numbers.
Given a countable set

The set of all finite multisets
over

(The set of all finite subsets of

We write

The

of

of

is the number

A multiset

such

is called

and is denoted by

.
We write

if
m(s)\leq m^{\prime}(s)
for all

.
We also write

if

and
m(s)<m^{\prime}(s)
for some

.

Multiset union

is defined as:
(m\oplus m^{\prime})(s)

=m(s)+m^{\prime}(s)
.
This operation is commutative, associative and has
\theta
as neutral element.

Multiset difference

\_\ominus\_
is defined as:

(m_{1}\ominus m_{2})(s)=max\{m_{1}(s)-m_{2}(s),0\}
.
The
scalar product
of a natural

with

is the multiset

defined as

(j\cdot m)(s)=j\cdot(m(s))
. By

we denote the multiset with

as its only element.
Hence, a multiset

over
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}

can be represented as
k_{1}\cdot s_{1}\oplus k_{2}\cdot s_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus k_{n}\cdot s_{n}
,
where
k_{j}=m(s_{j})\geq 0
for

.

\Box

Definition 2

(Place/Transition Petri net)

A labeled
Place/Transition
Petri net (P/T net, or Petri net, for short) is a tuple

\bullet

is the countable set of

, ranged over by

(possibly indexed),

\bullet

is the finite set of

\bullet

ranged over by

A Petri net

Given a transition

{\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell(t)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}

.

\Box

In the graphical description of P/T nets,

are connected by directed arcs.
The arcs may be labeled with the number representing how many tokens
are to be removed from (or produced into) that place; no label on
the arc is interpreted as the number one, i.e., one token flowing on the arc.
This numerical label of the arc is called its

.

Given a P/T net

A

is a tuple

is a P/T net and

\Box

Definition 4

(Enabling, firing sequence, reachable marking)

Given a P/T net

\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}\subseteq m

.
This is written

.
A

\bullet

\bullet

[m\rangle=\{m^{\prime}\;\;\big|\;\;\exists\sigma.m[\sigma\rangle m^{\prime}\}
.

\Box

The reachable markings of a finite net can be countably infinitely many when the net is not bounded,
i.e., when the number of tokens in some places

Definition 5

(Classes of finite P/T Nets)

A
finite marked
P/T net

is:

\bullet

\forall m\in[m_{0}\rangle,\forall s\in S,

.

\bullet

\forall m\in[m_{0}\rangle,\forall s\in S

m(s)\leq k
.
If this is the case, we say that the net is
k

A
finite
P/T net

is a

if
\forall t\in T
we have
|\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}|=1
.

\Box

We now recall a basic behavioral equivalence on P/T nets, derived from standard bisimilarity on labeled transition systems
[
21
,
13
]
.

Definition 6

(Interleaving Bisimulation)

Let
N=(S,A,T)
be a P/T net.
An
interleaving bisimulation
is a relation

R\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}(S)\times{\mathcal{M}}(S)
such that if
(m_{1},m_{2})\in R

then

\bullet

\forall t_{1}
such that
m_{1}[t_{1}\rangle m^{\prime}_{1}
,
\exists t_{2}
such that
m_{2}[t_{2}\rangle m^{\prime}_{2}

with
l(t_{1})=l(t_{2})
and
(m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2})\in R
,

\bullet

\forall t_{2}
such that
m_{2}[t_{2}\rangle m^{\prime}_{2}
,
\exists t_{1}
such that
m_{1}[t_{1}\rangle m^{\prime}_{1}

with
l(t_{1})=l(t_{2})
and
(m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2})\in R
.

Two markings
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are
interleaving bisimilar
,
denoted by
m_{1}\sim_{int}m_{2}
, if there exists an interleaving bisimulation
R
such that
(m_{1},m_{2})\in R
.

\Box

Interleaving bisimilarity was proved undecidable in
[
18
]
for finite P/T nets having at least two unbounded places,
with a proof based on the comparison of two
sequential
P/T nets,
where a P/T net is sequential if it does not offer any concurrent behavior. Hence, interleaving bisimulation equivalence is
undecidable even for the subclass of sequential finite P/T nets. Esparza observed in
[
8
]
that all the non-interleaving
bisimulation-based equivalences (in the spectrum ranging from interleaving bisimilarity to fully-concurrent bisimilarity
[
3
]
)
collapse to interleaving bisimilarity over sequential P/T nets. Hence, the proof in
[
18
]
applies to all these
non-interleaving bisimulation equivalences as well.

Given a P/T net
N=(S,A,T)
and a marking
m
, we say that two transitions
t_{1},t_{2}

are
concurrently enabled
at
m
if
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}_{1}\oplus\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}_{2}\subseteq m
. The
concurrent firing
of these
two transitions produces the marking
m^{\prime}=(m\ominus(\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}_{1}\oplus\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}_{2}))\oplus(\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}\oplus\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$})
.
We denote this fact by
m[\{t_{1},t_{2}\}\rangle m^{\prime}
.
It is also possible that the same transition is
self-concurrent
at some marking
m
, meaning that two or more
occurrences of it are concurrently enabled at
m
.

We can generalize the definition of concurrently enabled transitions to
a finite, nonempty multiset
G
over the set
T
, called a
step
.
A step
G:T\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
is enabled at marking
m
if
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}G$}\subseteq m
, where

\mbox{${}^{\bullet}G$}=\bigoplus_{t\in T}G(t)\cdot\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t$}
and
G(t)

denotes the number of occurrences of transition
t
in the step
G
.
The execution of a step
G
enabled at
m
produces the marking
m^{\prime}=(m\ominus\mbox{${}^{\bullet}G$})\oplus\mbox{$G^{\bullet}$}
,
where
\mbox{$G^{\bullet}$}=\bigoplus_{t\in T}G(t)\cdot\mbox{$t^{\bullet}$}
. This is written
m[G\rangle m^{\prime}
.
We sometimes refer to
this as the
concurrent token game
, in opposition to the
sequential
token game of Definition
4
.
The label
l(G)
of a step
G
is the multiset
l(G):A\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
defined as follows:

l(G)(a)=\sum_{t_{i}\in\mathit{dom}(G).l(t_{i})=a}G(t_{i})
.

Now we define a notion of bisimulation based on the firing of steps, rather than of single transitions (as for interleaving bisimulation),
originally proposed in

(Step Bisimulation)

Let

\bullet

Two markings

.

\Box

Of course,

also step bisimilarity is undecidable for P/T nets having at least two unbounded places

]

is a pair

.
A

is a finite multiset of links, i.e.,

where

is finite.
With abuse of notation, we denote by
\theta
the empty linking, i.e., the empty multiset of links.
Given a set
L
of links, we denote by

the set of all the linkings over
L
, i.e., of all the multisets over
L
.

Given a linking
l
, the two projected markings
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
can be defined as

m_{1}(s_{1})=\pi_{1}(l)(s_{1})=\sum_{s_{2}\in S}l(s_{1},s_{2})
and

m_{2}(s_{2})=\pi_{2}(l)(s_{2})=\sum_{s_{1}\in S}l(s_{1},s_{2})
.
For instance, if
l=\{(s_{1},s_{2}),(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime})\}
, then
\pi_{1}(l)=s_{1}\oplus s_{1}^{\prime}

and
\pi_{2}(l)=s_{2}\oplus s_{2}^{\prime}
; instead, if

\pi_{2}(l)=\theta

is projected on the empty marking.

Given a linking
l

is defined as:
l^{-1}(s_{2},s_{1})=l(s_{1},s_{2})
.
Given two linkings

and

defines a set of linkings, that can be empty (so the operation is not defined) in case
\pi_{2}(l_{1})\neq\pi_{1}(l_{2})

A linking

if there exists a multiset

of triples of places, such that
l_{1}(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum_{s_{3}\in S}k(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})

.
For instance, if

and

, then

, where

and

.

We will use

,

\overline{h}

Definition 11

(Structure Preserving Bisimulation)

Let
N=(S,A,T)
be a P/T net.
A
structure-preserving bisimulation
(sp-bisimulation, for short)
is a set
R
of linkings such that if
l\in R
, then
\forall c\subseteq l
,

1.

\forall t_{1}
such that
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}=\pi_{1}(c)
, there exist a transition
t_{2}

such that
\ell(t_{1})=\ell(t_{2})
,
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}=\pi_{2}(c)
, and a linking
\overline{c}
such that

\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{1}(\overline{c})
,
\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{2}(\overline{c})
and
\overline{l}=(l\ominus c)\oplus\overline{c}\in R
;

2.

\forall t_{2}
such that
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}=\pi_{2}(c)
, there exist a transition
t_{1}

such that
\ell(t_{1})=\ell(t_{2})
,
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}=\pi_{1}(c)
, and a linking
\overline{c}
such that

\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{1}(\overline{c})
,
\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{2}(\overline{c})
and
\overline{l}=(l\ominus c)\oplus\overline{c}\in R
.

Two markings
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are
structure-preserving bisimilar
, denoted by

m_{1}\sim_{sp}m_{2}
, if there exists a linking
l
in a structure preserving bisimulation
R
such that
m_{1}=\pi_{1}(l)

and
m_{2}=\pi_{2}(l)
.

\Box

Note that the two matching transitions
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
in the structure-preserving bisimulation game must have the same shape
because
(i)

\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}=\pi_{1}(c)
and
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}=\pi_{2}(c)
implies that

|\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}|=|\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}|
, then
(ii)

\ell(t_{1})=\ell(t_{2})
and, finally,
(iii)
from
\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{1}(\overline{c})
and
\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}=\pi_{2}(\overline{c})

we derive that
|\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}|=|\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}|
.

Remark 1

(Structure-preserving bisimilarity implies interleaving bisimilarity)

It is easy to observe that if

is an sp-bisimulation, then

is an interleaving bisimulation such that

\forall t_{1}

,

|\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}|=|\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}|
,
\ell(t_{1})=\ell(t_{2})
,
|\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}|=|\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}|
and

Let

\underline{a}.s
is a strongly prefixed process, where the strong prefix

can complete the transaction).
Hence, the strong prefixing operator allows for the creation of transitions labeled by an atomic sequence

which is a sequence of visible

A consequence of the fact that transitions may be labeled by atomic sequences is the need for a new, more general,

Hence, it is not possible to synchronize two atomic sequences.

(s\{A/x\},s\{B/x\})\in R
, by induction
we have that there exist a transition
t_{2}^{\prime}
such that
\ell(t_{1}^{\prime})=\ell(t_{2}^{\prime})
,
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}^{\prime}$}=s\{B/x\}
,
and a linking
\overline{c_{1}}
such that
\pi_{1}(\overline{c_{1}})=\mbox{$t_{1}^{\prime\bullet}$}
and
\pi_{2}(\overline{c_{1}})=\mbox{$t_{2}^{\prime\bullet}$}
,

\pi_{1}(\overline{c_{1}})\sim_{sp}\underline{m}\{A/x\}
,
\pi_{2}(\overline{c_{1}})\sim_{sp}\underline{m}\{B/x\}
,
so that there exists
\overline{l}\in R^{+}
such that
\pi_{1}(\overline{l})=\underline{m}\{A/x\}
and
\pi_{2}(\overline{l})=\underline{m}\{B/x\}
.

Since
c_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq l_{1}
and
l_{1}\in R^{+}
, by induction (and by Remark
2
),
there exist a step
G^{\prime}
such that
\ell(G)=\ell(G^{\prime})
,
\pi_{2}(c_{2}^{\prime})=\mbox{${}^{\bullet}G^{\prime}$}
,
and a linking
\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime}
such that
\pi_{1}(\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime})=\mbox{$G^{\bullet}$}
and
\pi_{2}(\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime})=\mbox{$G^{\prime\bullet}$}
,

\pi_{1}((l_{1}\ominus c_{2}^{\prime})\oplus\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime})\sim_{sp}\underline{m}^{\prime}\{A/x\}
,

\pi_{2}((l_{1}\ominus c_{2}^{\prime})\oplus\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime})\sim_{sp}\underline{m}^{\prime}\{B/x\}
,
so that there exists
\overline{l}^{\prime}\in R^{+}
such that
\pi_{1}(\overline{l}^{\prime})=\underline{m}^{\prime}\{A/x\}
and
\pi_{2}(\overline{l}^{\prime})=\underline{m}^{\prime}\{B/x\}
.

Hence, there exists a step
t_{2}^{\prime}\oplus G^{\prime}
, giving origin to transition
t_{2}
,
such that
\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}=\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}^{\prime}$}\oplus\mbox{${}^{\bullet}G^{\prime}$}
,
\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}=\mbox{$t_{2}^{\prime\bullet}$}\oplus\mbox{$G^{\prime\bullet}$}
,

\mathit{MSync}(\ell(t_{2}^{\prime})\oplus\ell(G^{\prime}),\ell(t_{1}))
holds (because
\ell(t_{1}^{\prime})=\ell(t_{2}^{\prime})
and
\ell(G)=\ell(G^{\prime})

\ell(t_{1})=\ell(t_{2})
,

\pi_{1}(c_{2})=\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{1}$}
,
\pi_{2}(c_{2})=\mbox{${}^{\bullet}t_{2}$}
, and, letting
\overline{c_{2}}=\overline{c_{1}}\oplus\overline{c_{2}}^{\prime}

also that
\pi_{1}(\overline{c_{2}})=\mbox{$t_{1}^{\bullet}$}
,
\pi_{2}(\overline{c_{2}})=\mbox{$t_{2}^{\bullet}$}

we have that

\pi_{1}(h)\sim_{sp}\underline{m}\{A/x\}\oplus\underline{m}^{\prime}\{A/x\}
,

\pi_{2}(h)\sim_{sp}\underline{m}\{B/x\}\oplus\underline{m}^{\prime}\{B/x\}
,
and
\overline{l}\oplus\overline{l}^{\prime}\in R^{+}
is such that

\pi_{1}(\overline{l}\oplus\overline{l}^{\prime})=\underline{m}\{A/x\}\oplus\underline{m}^{\prime}\{A/x\}

\pi_{2}(\overline{l}\oplus\overline{l}^{\prime})=\underline{m}\{B/x\}\oplus\underline{m}^{\prime}\{B/x\}

Algebraic Properties

Now we propose a set of algebraic properties that hold for structure-preserving bisimilarity. For some of these,
we take inspiration from similar laws for
standard bisimulation equivalence on the labeled transition system semantics of CCS-like process algebras (see, e.g.,
[
21
,
13
,
30
]
).

Proposition 14

(Laws of the choice operator)

For all
p,q,r\in\mathcal{P}_{FNM}^{grd}
, the following hold:

\begin{array}[]{lrcllllll}&p+(q+r)&\sim_{sp}&(p+q)+r&&\;\mbox{(associativity)}\\
&p+q&\sim_{sp}&q+p&&\;\mbox{(commutativity)}\\
&p+\mbox{\bf 0}&\sim_{sp}&p&\mbox{if $p\neq\mbox{\bf 0}$\; }&\;\mbox{(identity)}\\
&p+p&\sim_{sp}&p&\mbox{if $p\neq\mbox{\bf 0}$\; }&\;\mbox{(idempotency)}\\
\end{array}

Proof

For each law, it is enough to exhibit a suitable structure-preserving bisimulation relation.
For instance, for idempotency, for each
p
guarded (
p\neq\mbox{\bf 0}
), take
relation
R_{p}\;=\;\{(p+p,p)\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{p}^{+}

where

\mathcal{I}_{p}=\{(s,s)\;\;\big|\;\;\exists m\in[dec(p)\rangle,s\in m\}
. It is an easy exercise to
check that
R_{p}
is a structure-preserving bisimulation.
In fact, if
p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
, then (according to rule (sum
1
)) also
p+p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
and there exists

l\in\mathcal{I}_{p}^{+}
such that
\pi_{1}(l)=m=\pi_{2}(l)
, and so
l\in R_{p}
. Symmetrically, if
p+p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
,
then (according to the rule (sum
{}_{1})
)
this is possible only if
p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
is derivable and the condition
l\in R_{p}
such that
\pi_{1}(l)=m=\pi_{2}(l)

is trivially satisfied.
As a further example, for the associativity law, the candidate sp-bisimulation relation is

R_{(p,q,r)}\;=\;\{(p+(q+r),(p+q)+r)\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{(p,q,r)}^{+}
,
where

\mathcal{I}_{(p,q,r)}=\{(s,s)\;\;\big|\;\;\exists m\in[\mbox{$dec$}(p+(q+r))\rangle,s\in m\}
.

\Box

Note that the identity law and the idempotency law have the side condition
p\neq\mbox{\bf 0}
. This is strictly necessary because

\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}
and
0
have a completely different semantics: the former originates a stuck place, while the latter the empty marking. Of course,
these are not sp-bisimilar as
|\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0})|=1
while
|\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{\bf 0})|=0
.

As we have shown that the choice operator
+
is associative (and commutative)
w.r.t.
\sim_{sp}
,
we sometimes use the
n
-ary version of this operator.
Hence, we shorten the term
(\ldots(p_{1}+p_{2})+\ldots+p_{n})
as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
.

Proposition 15

(Laws of the strong prefixing operator)

For all
p,q\in\mathcal{P}_{FNM}^{grd}
and for each
a\in\mathcal{L}
, the following hold:

\begin{array}[]{lrcllllll}&\underline{a}.(p+q)&\sim_{sp}&\underline{a}.p+\underline{a}.q&&\;\mbox{(distribution)}\\
&\underline{a}.\tau.p&\sim_{sp}&a.p&&\;\mbox{(absorption)}\\
\mbox{if $p\sim_{sp}\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}$ or $p=\mbox{\bf 0}$, then}&\quad\underline{a}.p&\sim_{sp}&\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}&&\;\mbox{(annihilation)}\\
\end{array}

Proof

For each law, it is enough to exhibit a suitable sp-bisimulation relation.
For distribution, take
relation
R_{1}\;=\;\{(\underline{a}.(p+q),\underline{a}.p+\underline{a}.q)\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{(p,q)}^{+}
, which is clearly an sp-bisimulation.
For absorption, take
relation
R_{2}\;=\;\{(\underline{a}.\tau.p,a.p)\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{p}^{+}
, which is clearly an sp-bisimulation.
For annihilation, take
relation
R_{3}\;=\;\{(\underline{a}.p,\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0})\}
, which is clearly an sp-bisimulation.

\Box

Note that in the annihilation law we have equated
\underline{a}.p
to
\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}
, as both are
stuck places, and not to
0
, whose semantics is the empty marking.

Proposition 16

(Laws of the constant)

For each
p\in\mathcal{P}_{FNM}^{grd}
, and each
C\in\mathcal{C}
,
the following hold:

\begin{array}[]{lrcllllll}\mbox{if $C\doteq\mbox{\bf 0}$, then}&\quad C&\sim_{sp}&\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}&&\;\mbox{(stuck)}\\
\mbox{if $C\doteq p$ and $p\neq\mbox{\bf 0}$, then}&\quad C&\sim_{sp}&p&&\;\mbox{(unfolding)}\\
\mbox{if $C\doteq p\{C/x\}$
and $q\sim_{sp}p\{q/x\}$ then}&\quad C&\sim_{sp}&q&&\;\mbox{(folding)}\\
\end{array}

where, in the third law,
p
is actually open on
x
(while
q
is closed).

Proof

The stuck property is trivial: since the decomposition of a constant is a place, if the body is stuck,
it corresponds to a stuck place, such as
\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}
.

The required structure-preserving bisimulation proving the unfolding property is

R_{C,p}\;=\;\{(C,p)\}\cup\mathcal{I}_{C}^{+}
,
where
\mathcal{I}_{C}=\{(s,s)\;\;\big|\;\;\exists m\in[\mbox{$dec$}(C)\rangle,s\in m\}
.
In fact, if
C{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
, then (according to the operational net semantics for
C\doteq p
) this means that
also
p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
, so that there exists
l\in\mathcal{I}_{C}^{+}
such that
\pi_{1}(l)=m=\pi_{2}(l)
, and so
l\in R_{C,p}

as required. Symmetrically if
p
moves first.

For the folding property, observe that
the statement is implied by the following:
if
q_{1}\sim_{sp}p\{q_{1}/x\}
and
q_{2}\sim_{sp}p\{q_{2}/x\}
then
q_{1}\sim_{sp}q_{2}
. In fact, if we choose
q_{1}=C
,
then
C=q_{1}\sim_{sp}p\{q_{1}/x\}=p\{C/x\}
(which holds by hypothesis, due to the unfolding property)
and
C=q_{1}\sim_{sp}q_{2}
, which is the thesis. Note that
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
must be sequential processes.
This statement can be proven by showing that, given

R=\{(s\{q_{1}/x\},s\{q_{2}/x\})\;\;\big|\;\;\exists m\in[\mbox{$dec$}(p)\rangle,s\in m\},

we have that
R^{+}
is a structure-preserving bisimulation up to
\sim_{sp}
.
Clearly, when
s=x
, we have that
(q_{1},q_{2})\in R
.
So, it remains to prove the sp-bisimulation (up to) conditions.

If
s\{q_{1}/x\}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}t
, then this
can be due to one of the following:

\bullet

s{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
and so
t=m\{q_{1}/x\}
, where the substitution is applied element-wise to
each place in
m
.
In this case, also
s\{q_{2}/x\}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m\{q_{2}/x\}
is
derivable such that there exists
l\in R^{+}
such that
\pi_{1}(l)=m\{q_{1}/x\}
and
\pi_{2}(l)=m\{q_{2}/x\}
.

\bullet

s=x
and
q_{1}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m_{1}
, and so
t=m_{1}
.
Since
q_{1}\sim_{sp}p\{q_{1}/x\}
and
p
is guarded, we have that there exists

m
such that
p{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m
,

p\{q_{1}/x\}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m\{q_{1}/x\}
and (by Remark
1
)
m_{1}\sim_{sp}m\{q_{1}/x\}
.
Therefore,
p\{q_{2}/x\}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m\{q_{2}/x\}
is derivable, too, and
l\in R^{+}
exists such that

\pi_{1}(l)=m\{q_{1}/x\}
and
\pi_{2}(l)=m\{q_{2}/x\}
.
Since
q_{2}\sim_{sp}p\{q_{2}/x\}
, it follows that there
exists a marking
m_{2}
such that

q_{2}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m_{2}
with
m_{2}\sim_{sp}m\{q_{2}/x\}
(by Remark
1
).

Summing up, if
x\{q_{1}/x\}=q_{1}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m_{1}
,
then
x\{q_{2}/x\}=q_{2}{\mbox{${\>\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\>}$}}m_{2}

such that
m_{1}\sim_{sp}m\{q_{1}/x\}
,
l\in R^{+}
exists such that

\pi_{1}(l)=m\{q_{1}/x\}
and
\pi_{2}(l)=m\{q_{2}/x\}
and, moreover,

m\{q_{2}/x\}\sim_{sp}m_{2}
, as required
by the structure-bisimulation bisimulation up to condition.

Simmetrically, if
s\{q_{2}/x\}
moves first. Hence,
R^{+}
is a structure-preserving bisimulation up to
\sim_{sp}
.

\Box

Proposition 17

(Laws of the parallel operator)

For all restriction-free FNM processes
p,q,r
, the following hold:

\begin{array}[]{lrclllll}&\quad p\mbox{$\,|\,$}(q\mbox{$\,|\,$}r)&\;\sim_{sp}&(p\mbox{$\,|\,$}q)\mbox{$\,|\,$}r&&\mbox{(associativity)}\\
&\quad p\mbox{$\,|\,$}q&\;\sim_{sp}&q\mbox{$\,|\,$}p&&\mbox{(commutativity)}\\
&\quad p\mbox{$\,|\,$}\mbox{\bf 0}&\;\sim_{sp}&p&&\mbox{(identity)}\\
\end{array}

Proof

To prove that each law is sound, it is enough to observe that the net for the process
in the left-hand-side is exactly the same as the net for the process in the right-hand-side.
For instance,
Net(p\mbox{$\,|\,$}q)=Net(q\mbox{$\,|\,$}p)
, because

\mbox{$dec$}(p\mbox{$\,|\,$}q)=\mbox{$dec$}(p)\oplus\mbox{$dec$}(q)=\mbox{$dec$}(q)\oplus\mbox{$dec$}(p)=\mbox{$dec$}(q\mbox{$\,|\,$}p)
(cf. Proposition
7
).
Therefore, taken the identity relation
\mathcal{I}
on places, the relation
\mathcal{I}^{+}
is enough to prove that

p\mbox{$\,|\,$}q\sim_{sp}q\mbox{$\,|\,$}p
.

\Box

As we have shown that parallel composition is associative (and commutative)
w.r.t.
\sim_{sp}
,
we sometimes use the
n
-ary version of this operator.
Hence, we shorten the term
(\ldots(p_{1}\mbox{$\,|\,$}p_{2})\mbox{$\,|\,$}\ldots\mbox{$\,|\,$}p_{n})
as
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
.

In order to define some algebraic properties for the restriction operator, we assume the reader familiar with the notion of free names of a process
p
(denoted by
{\it fn}(p)
),
bound names (denoted by
{\it bn}(p)
) and syntactic substitution of action
b
in place of
a
within
p
, denoted by

p\mbox{$\{b/a\}$}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
,
30
]
for introductory books on these topics).

Proposition 18

(Laws of the restriction operator)

For each process
p\in\mathcal{P}_{FNM}
and for all
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
, the following hold:

\begin{array}[]{lrclllll}&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p&\sim_{sp}&p&\mbox{if $a\not\in\mbox{{\it fn}}(p)$}&\mbox{(remove)}\\
&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}p)&\sim_{sp}&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p)&\mbox{if $a\neq b$}&\mbox{(reorder)}\\
&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p&\sim_{sp}&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}(p\mbox{$\{b/a\}$})&\mbox{if $b\not\in\mbox{{\it fn}}(p)\cup bn(p)$ }&\mbox{(alpha-conversion)}\\
\end{array}

Proof

The proof of the remove law is easy because, if
a\not\in\mbox{{\it fn}}(p)
, then
\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p)=\mbox{$dec$}(p)
, so that
by Proposition
7
we have that
Net(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p)=Net(p)
.
Hence, taken the identity relation
\mathcal{I}
on places, the relation
\mathcal{I}^{+}
is enough to prove this property.

Similarly, one can argue that the reorder law holds, because
\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}p))=\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p))
.

To prove the alpha-conversion law, consider that
\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p)=\mbox{$dec$}(p)\mbox{$\{a^{\prime}/a\}$}
and that

\mbox{$dec$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}(p\mbox{$\{b/a\}$}))=\mbox{$dec$}(p\mbox{$\{b/a\}$})\mbox{$\{b^{\prime}/b\}$}
.
Hence, consider the relation

R=\{(s\mbox{$\{a^{\prime}/a\}$},s\mbox{$\{b^{\prime}/a\}$})\;\;\big|\;\;\exists m\in\mbox{$dec$}(p),s\in m\}
.

It is easy to observe that, under the assumption that
b\not\in\mbox{{\it fn}}(p)\cup bn(p)
, relation

R^{+}
is an sp-bisimulation proving the alpha-conversion law.

\Box

Note that the remove law above implies also the following law:

\quad\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p)\sim_{sp}\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p

stating that duplicated application of the restriction operator are inessential.
This law, together with the reorder law,
justifies the notational convention, that we often adopt, of using the restriction operator
over a set of names; e.g., for
\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}p))
, we can use the notation
\mbox{$({\bf\nu}\{a,b\})$}p
.

Now we introduce some laws about the interplay between some operators, related to manipulation
of the preset or of the postset of net transitions.
However, we anticipate that these laws are rather specific, less general and less elegant than those above.
The following example illustrates the issue for the presets.

Example 4

Consider the term
p=\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}((\underline{a}.\underline{b}.c.p_{1}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{a}.p_{2})\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{b}.p_{3}))
.
The only initial transition
performable by
\mbox{$dec$}(p)
is a three-way synchronization, labeled
c
, reaching the marking

((\mbox{$dec$}(p_{1})\oplus\mbox{$dec$}(p_{2})\oplus\mbox{$dec$}(p_{3}))\mbox{$\{b^{\prime}/b\}$})\mbox{$\{a^{\prime}/a\}$}
.
The same behavior is also possible by the term
q=\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}((\underline{b}.\underline{a}.c.p_{1}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{a}.p_{2})\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{b}.p_{3}))
,
where the leader process has permuted the two strong prefixes.
As a matter of fact, the restricted actions that occur in the input sequence of the leader process, that is able to perform a multi-party synchronization in full, are to be considered
as a multiset of inputs rather than a sequence of inputs. Moreover,
also the term
r=\mbox{$({\bf\nu}a)$}(\mbox{$({\bf\nu}b)$}((\underline{a}.\underline{b}.c.p_{1}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{b}.p_{2})\mbox{$\,|\,$}\overline{a}.p_{3}))
, where the output guards have been exchanged,
is sp-bisimilar to both
p
and
q
.
The following laws focus on these aspects.

\Box

\delta

\begin{array}[]{lrcrllll}(i)&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}A)$}(((\underline{a}_{n}.\ldots\underline{a}_{1}.a_{0}.p+r)\mbox{$\,|\,$}\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a}_{i}.p_{i})\mbox{$\,|\,$}q)&\sim_{sp}\\

&\mbox{$({\bf\nu}A)$}(((\underline{a}_{n}.\ldots\underline{a}_{1}.a_{0}.p+r)\mbox{$\,|\,$}\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\overline{a}_{\delta(i)}.p_{i})\mbox{$\,|\,$}q)\\

For each
n\geq 1
, for each restriction-free process
p\in\mathcal{P}_{FNM}
,
for each

, for each

Sound Axiomatization

In this section we provide a sound, but incomplete, axiomatization of structure-preserv-ing bisimulation equivalence over FNM.
For simplicity’s sake, the syntactic definition of open FNM (cf. Definition
15
) is assumed here flattened,
with only one syntactic category, but we require that
each ground instantiation of an axiom must respect the syntactic definition of (closed) FNM given in
Section
3
.
This means that we can write the axiom
x+(y+z)=(x+y)+z
(these terms cannot be written
in open FNM according to Definition
15
), but
it is invalid to instantiate it to
C+(a.\mbox{\bf 0}+b.\mbox{\bf 0}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\mbox{\bf 0})=(C+a.\mbox{\bf 0})+(b.\mbox{\bf 0}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\mbox{\bf 0})
because these are not legal FNM
processes (the constant
C
and the parallel process
b.\mbox{\bf 0}\mbox{$\,|\,$}\mbox{\bf 0}
cannot be used as summands).

The set of axioms are outlined in Tables
7
and
8
.
We call
E
the set of axioms
\{
A1, A2, A3, A4, S1, S2, S3, C1, C2, P1, P2, P3, R1, R2, R3, Pr1, Pr2, Ps1, Ps2, Ps3, Ps4
\}
.
By the notation
E\vdash p=q
we mean that there exists an equational deduction proof
of the equality
p=q
, by using the axioms in
E
. Besides the usual equational deduction rules of reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity,
substitutivity and instantiation (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
for an introductory book on the subject), in order to deal with constants we
need also the following recursion
congruence rule:

\frac{p=q\;\wedge\;A\doteq p\{A/x\}\;\wedge\;B\doteq q\{B/x\}}{A=B}

Let us first comment on the axioms in Table
7
, which are more standard and also already appeared in the literature
in some form.

The axioms
A1-A4
are the usual axioms for choice
[
29
,
20
]
where, however,
A3-A4
have the side
condition
x\neq\mbox{\bf 0}
; hence, it is not possible to prove
E\vdash\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}=\mbox{\bf 0}
, as expected, because
these two terms have a completely different semantics; in fact, no other sequential process
p
can be equated to
0
.

The axioms
S1-S3
are the axioms for strong prefixing, similar to those originally proposed in
[
12
]
.
Note that the annihilation axiom
S3
requires that
x
is
0
or can be proved equal to a stuck place, such as
\mbox{\bf 0}+\mbox{\bf 0}
.
Note also that these axioms are actually a finite collection of axioms, one for each input prefix
a
: since the set
\mathcal{L}

is finite, the instances of
S1-S3
are finitely many.

The conditional axioms (or inference rules)
C1-C2
are about process constants, originally introduced in
[
15
]

and inspired to those for the fixpoint operator of finite-state CCS
[
20
]
.
Note that axiom
C1

summarizes the algebraic laws stuck and unfolding of Proposition
16
(cf. Remark
3
).
Note also that these conditional axioms are actually a finite collection of axioms, one for each constant
definition: since the set
\mathcal{C}
of process constants is finite, the instances of
C1-C2
are finitely many.

The axioms
P1-P3
are the usual ones for parallel composition. They are typical for non-interleaving behavioral congruences
(see, e.g.,
[
15
,
17
]
), while they are not used for standard bisimilarity, in favor of the so-called
expansion theorem (or interleaving law)
[
21
,
13
]
, according to which parallel composition can be seen as
a derived operator expressible
by a suitable combination of the operators of action prefixing and choice.

Finally, we have axioms
R1-R3
for the restriction operator. These axioms occur in many contexts as ingredients of suitable
structural congruences for the operational interleaving semantics (defined on labeled transitions systems)
of some process algebras,
notably the
\pi
-calculus (see, e.g.,
[
26
,
30
]
) or Multi-CCS
[
13
]
.

\begin{array}[]{llrcll}{\bf A1}&\;\;\mbox{Associativity}&\;\;x+(y+z)&=&(x+y)+z&\\
{\bf A2}&\;\;\mbox{Commutativity}&\;\;x+y&=&y+x&\\
{\bf A3}&\;\;\mbox{Identity}&\;\;x+\mbox{\bf 0}&=&x&\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{ if $x\neq\mbox{\bf 0}$}\\

7

Conclusion

Structure-preserving bisimulation
[
11
]
is a very intuitive, rather manageable, truly concurrent
behavioral relation, whose process-oriented characterization is causal-net bisimilarity
[
11
,
17
]
,
a behavioral equivalence fully respecting causality and the branching
structure of systems.

The decidability of sp-bisimilarity over finite (unbounded) P/T nets is an open problem, because the negative observation in
[
8
]

does not apply to it.
Nonetheless, structure-preserving bisimilarity is decidable on bounded nets in exponential time
[
4
]
. In fact, the
set of reachable markings are finitely many for a bounded net, and the set of linkings definable on
a pair of markings of equal size
k
has size
k!
. Hence, there are finitely many
relations (that are all finite) composed of linkings, and it is enough to exhaustively check whether each of them is a structure-preserving bisimulation
and, in the positive case, to check whether it contains a linking projected on the two initial markings of interest.

Note that the class of bounded finite P/T nets (i.e., the class onto which sp-bisimilarity is decidable) is much larger than
the class of nets that is used to give semantics to regular CCS
[
21
,
13
]
(also called RCS in
[
14
]
),
because these process terms give origin to finite
P/T nets whose transitions have preset of size 1 or 2, and if the preset size is 1, then its postset size is 1 at most,
while if the preset size is 2, then its label is
\tau
and the postset size is 2 at most.

Structure-preserving bisimilarity is decidable on BPP nets (i.e., nets whose transitions have preset of size 1), because on this class of
nets it coincides with team bisimilarity
[
15
,
17
]
, which is decidable in polynomial time.

Van Glabbeek
[
11
]
argued that structure-preserving bisimilarity is the most appropriate behavioral
equivalence for Petri nets, as it is the only one
respecting a list of desirable requirements he proposed. Among these, there is ‘compositionality’, up to
structure-preserving bisimilarity, of the operators (recursion not considered) of the process
algebra CCSP, that Olderog proposed in his monograph
[
24
]

and equipped with a safe net (i.e., in each reachable marking each place may contain one token at most) semantics.
In this paper we have complemented his result by proving that structure-preserving bisimilarity
can be used to give a compositional semantics of the process algebra
FNM
[
14
]
, which truly represents all (and only) the finite P/T nets, up to isomorphism. In this way, we have obtained for the first time
a compositional semantics, fully respecting causality and the branching
structure of systems, for the class of all the finite P/T Petri nets.

It is interesting to observe
[
14
]
that also the coarser step bisimilarity
[
23
]
is a congruence for all the FNM operators, while
interleaving bisimilarity is not a congruence for the FNM operator of parallel composition, so that,
in order to give a satisfactory account of this process algebra, a non-interleaving behavioral semantics is strictly necessary.
Since structure-preserving bisimilarity may appear even too concrete, as it may fail to equate markings generating the same causal nets
(cf. the definition of i-causal net bisimilarity in
[
4
]
), a challenging open problem is to see whether it is possible
to define a compositional semantics for some other behavioral equivalence in between step bisimilarity (a bit too abstract)
and structure-preserving bisimilarity (a bit too concrete).

On the stabilizing effect of swimming in an active suspension
Dallas Albritton, Laurel Ohm

Abstract
We consider a kinetic model of an active suspension of rod-like microswimmers. In certain regimes, swimming has a stabilizing effect on the suspension. We quantify this effect near homogeneous isotropic equilibria ψ ¯ = const \overline{\psi}=\text{const} . Notably, in the absence of particle (translational and orientational) diffusion, swimming is the only stabilizing mechanism. On the torus, in the non-diffusive regime, we demonstrate linear Landau damping up to the stability threshold predicted in the applied literature. With small diffusion, we demonstrate nonlinear stability of arbitrary equilibrium values for pullers (front-actuated swimmers) and enhanced dissipation for both pullers and pushers (rear-actuated swimmers) at small concentrations. On the whole space, we prove nonlinear stability of the vacuum equilibrium due to generalized Taylor dispersion.

Active matter, swimming, Landau damping, Taylor dispersion, enhanced dissipation

We consider a kinetic model of a dilute suspension of rod-like microswimmers proposed by Saintillan and Shelley

The velocity field

\displaystyle-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla q

\displaystyle=\div\bm{\Sigma},\quad\div\bm{u}=0\,,

\displaystyle=\iota\int_{S^{d-1}}\psi(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\,\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}\,d\bm{p}\,,\quad\iota\in\{\pm\}\,.

\partial_{t}\psi+\div_{x}(\dot{\bm{x}}\psi)+\div_{p}(\dot{\bm{p}}\psi)=\nu\Delta_{p}\psi+\kappa\Delta_{x}\psi\,,

What role does swimming play in stabilizing the uniform isotropic equilibrium?

In this paper, we identify and quantify three near-equilibrium effects of swimming. On
\mathbb{T}^{d}

\overline{\psi}

Defining the relative conformational entropy

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}\frac{d\mathcal{S}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\iota d\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left\lvert\nabla\bm{u}\right\rvert^{2}\,d\bm{x}-4\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left(\nu|\nabla_{p}\sqrt{\psi}|^{2}+\kappa|\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\psi}|^{2}\right)\,d\bm{p}\,d\bm{x}\,,

Our main results may be summarized as follows (

•

•

Enhanced dissipation

Main results

1.1.1
Landau damping

We consider the linearized inviscid equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\bm{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-d\overline{\psi}\nabla\bm{u}:\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}

\displaystyle=0

(7)

\displaystyle-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla q=\div\bm{\Sigma}\,,\quad\div\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(8)

with active stress

\bm{\Sigma}[f](\bm{x},t)=\iota\int_{S^{d-1}}\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}\,f(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\,d\bm{p}\,,\qquad\iota\in\{\pm\}\,.

(9)

Here
\overline{\psi}\geq 0
is the constant background solution. Solutions satisfy

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\iota d\overline{\psi}\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=0\,,

(10)

which is a linearized version of the
H
-theorem (
6

To better understand the underlying decay mechanism, we consider
\overline{\psi}=0
and perform a mode-by-mode analysis for
f(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)=h(\bm{p},t)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}
and
f^{\rm in}=f(\cdot,0)
. Without loss of generality, we may take
\bm{k}=k\bm{e}_{1}
and solve for
h
as

h=e^{-ikp_{1}t}h^{\rm in}\,.

(11)

Over time, the solution develops large oscillations and is transferred to higher and higher frequencies in
\bm{p}
. This increasingly oscillatory behavior for an isolated spatial mode can be observed in numerical simulations by Hohenegger and Shelley
[
25
, Figure 3]
. Notably,
h
converges to zero
weakly
in
L^{2}
but not strongly, and the convergence can be quantified in negative Sobolev spaces via the method of stationary phase, see Section
2
. This is known as
phase mixing
in kinetic theory.

It is observed in
[
25
,
43
,
50
]
that the linearized operator about large
\overline{\psi}
admits unstable eigenvalues in the case of pushers but not pullers. Away from this pusher instability, we show that the phase mixing described above persists, causing the velocity field
\bm{u}
to quickly decay.
We call this phenomenon
Landau damping
, terminology again borrowed from kinetic theory.

Letting
f
denote the solution to the linearized system, we show the following:

Theorem 1
(Linear Landau damping).

Let
f^{\rm in}\in L^{2}_{x}H^{d+1}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1})
. Suppose that
\iota=+
or
\overline{\psi}<\overline{\psi}^{*}
. Then the velocity field
\bm{u}
generated by the solution
f\in C_{t}L^{2}_{x,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
to the linearized PDE (
7
)–(
9

satisfies

\int\|\nabla\bm{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\langle t\rangle^{d-\varepsilon}\,dt\lesssim_{\overline{\psi},\varepsilon}\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{d+1}_{p}}^{2}\,

(12)

for all

, where
\langle t\rangle=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}

The solution

decomposes as

(13)

where

satisfies

\|f_{\rm lin}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-(d-1)}_{p}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{d-1}_{p}}\,,

(14)

and

\int\|g(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-(d+1)}_{p}}^{2}\langle t\rangle^{d-\varepsilon}\,dt\lesssim_{\overline{\psi},\varepsilon}\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}_{x}H^{d+1}_{p}}^{2}\,.

(15)

In the pusher case (
\iota=-

The standard setting in which to investigate Landau damping is the

1.1.2
Taylor dispersion

For particles swimming with speed

, various sources (see, e.g.,
[

and

We begin by extracting the Taylor dispersion effect in the linearized PDE with

\partial_{t}f+\bm{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\nu\Delta_{p}f\,.

If

\frac{\nu^{1/2}k^{1/2}}{1+\left\lvert\log(\nu/k)\right\rvert}&k\geq\nu\,.\end{cases}

1.1.3
Enhanced dissipation

We now address the

\lambda_{\nu}=\lambda_{\nu,1}
in (

Theorem 4
(Nonlinear enhanced dissipation for small concentrations).

Let

\lVert f_{\neq}(\cdot,t)\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim e^{-\delta_{\neq}\lambda_{\nu}t}\varepsilon

\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim e^{-\delta_{0}\nu t}\bigg(\lVert f^{\rm in}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}_{p}}+\nu^{-1/2}\lambda_{\nu}^{-1/2}\varepsilon^{2}\bigg)

We highlight a useful heuristic regarding the enhancement timescale
\nu^{-1/2}
. Consider the PDE (
18
)
in dimension

and, writing
\bm{p}=\cos\theta\bm{e}_{1}+\sin\theta\bm{e}_{2}
, consider a plane wave solution (note the time rescaling)

(30)

Then

satisfies

\partial_{s}h+i\cos\theta h-\frac{\nu}{k}\partial_{\theta}^{2}h=0\,.

(31)

Heuristically, the evolution
\partial_{s}h+i\cos\theta h=0
, which dominates for short times, creates large gradients in
\theta
. This can be seen from applying
\partial_{\theta}
to the explicit formula

. Once these gradients are sufficiently large, the evolution

The enhancement is slowest near
\partial_{\theta}\cos\theta=0
, where the “shearing” caused by

\theta=0
more closely. The characteristic scales at
\theta=0

\widehat{\theta}=(\nu/k)^{1/4}
and
\widehat{s}=(\nu/k)^{-1/2}
, in the sense that if we “zoom in” at
\theta=0
by writing

\Theta=\theta/\widehat{\theta}
and

, we see the following leading order behavior in

:

\partial_{S}H+\frac{i}{2}\Theta^{2}H-\partial_{\Theta}^{2}H\approx 0\,.

(32)

We note a strong analogy between (
32
) and the evolution of a passive scalar in a two-dimensional shear flow
\bm{u}=(b(y),0)

\partial_{t}f+b(y)\partial_{x}f-\nu\Delta_{x,y}f=0\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\,.

(33)

Around a critical point
y_{0}
at which
b^{\prime}(y_{0})=\cdots=b^{(N)}(y_{0})=0
but
b^{(N+1)}(y_{0})\neq 0
, a

where the scalar

is dissipated more slowly becomes readily visible in simulations; its characteristic length scale in

\nu^{1/(N+3)}
, and its characteristic “enhancement” timescale is
\nu^{-(N+1)/(N+3)}
, which is known to be sharp
[

]
. Our setting corresponds to

, which is akin to Poiseuille flow, and the PDE (

.

1.2
Comparison with existing literature

1.2.1
Classical kinetic theory

While the kinetic model has similarities with the Vlasov-Poisson equation (
16
) and its collisional cousins, a key difference is that the phase mixing effect of the swimming term
\bm{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}
is not as strong as that of the free-streaming term
\bm{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}
. For example, solutions

[

1.2.2
Complex fluids

The role of swimming near the homogeneous isotropic equilibrium was considered by Škultéty et al. in

They calculate spatial and temporal correlations, indicative of collective behavior, of fluctuations around the equilibrium, sSection 2.D therein, and find that they are suppressed by swimming. This involves solving a Volterra equation similar to the one we obtain in Section

We now turn our attention to the PDE literature.
Although there is a vast PDE literature on related models without swimming (see the survey

The system (

Different microscopic descriptions lead to two main types of models. The first treats the polymers as elastic dumbbells (two beads attached by a spring). We refer to

In the second class of models (sometimes called Doi-type), to which the system (

Finally, we mention two works which do incorporate swimming.

1.3.1

Lemma 6
(Penrose condition).

The
Penrose condition

\sup_{\Re\lambda\geq 0}\|(I+\iota d\overline{\psi}\mathcal{L}K)^{-1}\|\leq C<+\infty\,

is satisfied unconditionally in the case of pullers (
\iota=+
) with constant

\overline{\psi}

\iota=-

\iota d\overline{\psi}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{p_{1}^{2}p_{j}^{2}}{\lambda+ip_{1}}\,d\bm{p}=1

(57)

having no solution for
\Re(\lambda)\geq 0
.

Let
\overline{\psi}^{*}
denote the supremum of
\psi
such that the Penrose condition is satisfied for all
\overline{\psi}<\psi

\overline{\psi}^{*}>0

The Fourier-Laplace transforms of
\mathcal{L}K
and
\mathcal{L}\widehat{\nabla\bm{u}}[e^{-ip_{1}\cdot}h^{\rm in}]

\Re\lambda>0

\mathcal{L}\widehat{\nabla\bm{u}}

[

(72)

for all

\int\|g(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{-(d+1)}_{p}}^{2}\langle t\rangle^{d-\varepsilon}\,dt\lesssim_{s,\overline{\psi}}\|h^{\rm in}\|_{H^{d+1}_{p}}^{2}\,.

(74)

Again, we may take the Fourier-Laplace transform, this time of (

, has the same time decay as the kernel

is a multiplicative algebra, the proof follows.

Remark 8
(Pointwise-in-time decay).

The representation formula (
55
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{L}\widehat{\nabla\bm{u}}=\mathcal{L}\widehat{\nabla\bm{u}}[e^{-ip_{1}\cdot}h^{\rm in}]+(I+\iota d\overline{\psi}\mathcal{L}K)^{-1}(-\iota d\overline{\psi}\mathcal{L}K)\mathcal{L}\widehat{\nabla\bm{u}}[e^{-ip_{1}\cdot}h^{\rm in}]\,.

(75)

In principle, one can obtain pointwise-in-time decay estimates on

by studying the Green’s function
\mathcal{L}^{-1}[(I+\iota d\overline{\psi}\mathcal{L}K)^{-1}(-\iota d\overline{\psi}\mathcal{L}K)]
pointwise-in-time. A natural way to proceed is to study the optimal regularity of
\mathcal{L}K(i\tau)
in
L^{1}_{\tau}
-based spaces rather than
H^{s}_{\tau}
. One can obtain an explicit formula for
\mathcal{L}K(i\tau)
by sending

. We leave its analysis to future work.

3
Taylor dispersion

In this section, we consider stability due to Taylor dispersion near vacuum

\mathbb{R}^{3}

To begin, we study the linearized equation

As mentioned in the introduction, our method is inspired by

, which itself is inspired by work of Guo. We introduce the macro-micro decomposition

(81)

Assume that

.
Notice that

when integrated against
|\nabla_{x}|^{-2}\nabla_{x}\varrho
. Below, we refine the above reasoning to demonstrate

Theorem 9
(Linear Taylor dispersion).

Let
\nu>0
,
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

k=|\bm{k}|

\iota=+
or
\overline{\psi}\ll\mu_{\nu,k}

(85)

where

\mu_{\nu,k}=\begin{cases}\nu&k\geq\nu\\
\frac{k^{2}}{\nu}&k\leq\nu\end{cases}\,.

(86)

Proof.

Upon rotating, we may assume that
\bm{k}=k\bm{e}_{1}
without loss of generality. Furthermore, upon rescaling time and replacing
\nu

\overline{\psi}
by
\nu/k

Define

(87)

where the brackets denote the

Using (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G

\displaystyle=-\langle|\nabla_{x}|^{-2}\nabla_{x}\div_{x}\bm{m},\bm{m}\rangle

(88)

\displaystyle-\langle|\nabla_{x}|^{-2}\nabla_{x}\varrho,\frac{1}{d}\nabla_{x}\varrho+\div_{x}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{S^{d-1}}\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}g\,d\bm{p}+\nu\lambda\bm{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\,|\nabla_{x}|^{-1}\div_{x}\bm{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle|\nabla_{x}|^{-1}\nabla_{x}\varrho,|\nabla_{x}|^{-1}\div_{x}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{S^{d-1}}\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}g\,d\bm{p}\rangle-\nu\lambda\langle|\nabla_{x}|^{-2}\nabla_{x}\varrho,\bm{m}\rangle

where we recall that
\bm{m}
is estimated by

\Phi=\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon G\,.

(89)

Since

C^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Phi\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

(90)

We seek a differential inequality for

\varepsilon

\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\leq-d\iota\overline{\psi}\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\nu c_{d}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2d}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon\nu\lambda G+C_{d}\varepsilon\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

(91)

where we employ the Poincaré inequality
c_{d}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla_{p}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in (

to ensure that

may be absorbed:

\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\leq-d\iota\overline{\psi}\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\nu c_{d}}{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2d}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon\nu\lambda G\,.

(92)

The above inequality has

and
O(\varepsilon)
damping in
\varrho
.

Due to the rescaling of

Case 1.
\nu\leq 1

In this setting, the requirement

In this setting, we further require

We choose

for some

Corollary 10
(Smoothing estimates).

Let

\|h(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}\lesssim\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2c_{0}\mu_{\nu,k}(T-s)}\|\widehat{\bm{g}}_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}\,ds\,.

(99)

Proof.

Again, without loss of generality,
\bm{k}=k\bm{e}_{1}
with

, and we consider the PDEs on the torus. Observe that for mode-

(102)

The desired estimate is obtained by rewriting

and reflecting

Proof of Theorem
3
(Puller stability).

We consider the PDE for the perturbation

(103)

(107)

Our estimates will be in the function space

X_{T}=\{f\in C_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}:\|f\|_{X_{T}}<+\infty\}\,,

(108)

where

To begin, we estimate
B_{1}

\displaystyle\|B_{1}(f,f)(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}

) from above. Specifically, we require that

(117)

To conclude, we choose

and

.

Proof.

To prove (i), we extract the following from the basic energy estimate:

\displaystyle\nu\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int|\nabla_{p}f|^{2}\,d\bm{x}\,d\bm{p}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\overline{\psi}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int|f|^{2}\,d\bm{x}\,d\bm{p}\,dt\,

(213)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2c\lambda_{\nu}t_{1}}\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\overline{\psi}\int_{t_{1}}^{+\infty}e^{-2c\lambda_{\nu}t}\,dt\,\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2c\lambda_{\nu}t_{1}}\|f^{\rm in}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

since
\overline{\psi}\ll\lambda_{\nu}
. We choose
t_{2}=t_{1}+\lambda_{\nu}^{-1}
and sum the estimate over
t_{1}=k\lambda_{\nu}^{-1}
,
k=0,1,\ldots
. Finally, (ii) follows from a duality argument as in Corollary
10
.

4.2
Nonlinear enhancement

Finally, we consider the full nonlinear equation (
1
) for
\psi=\overline{\psi}+f
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Recalling the definition (
95
) of the linear operator
L_{\nu,\kappa}^{\overline{\psi},\iota}
, we write (
1
) as an equation for the perturbation
f
:

\partial_{t}f-L_{\nu,\kappa}^{\overline{\psi},\iota}f+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla\bm{u}[f]\bm{p})f]=0\,.

(214)

We show that for sufficiently small
f^{\rm in}
and
\overline{\psi}
, the nonlinear evolution of
f
satisfies the same enhanced dissipation as the linear evolution (Corollary
14
).

Noting that the
k=0
spatial mode is not enhanced, we must treat the
k=0
and
k\neq 0
modes of
f
separately. We define
\mathbb{P}_{0}
to be the projection onto the
k=0
spatial mode,

\mathbb{P}_{0}h(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}h(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\,d\bm{x}\,,

(215)

and define
\mathbb{P}_{\neq}={\rm Id}-\mathbb{P}_{0}
. We consider separately the evolution of

f_{0}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}f\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\neq}:=\mathbb{P}_{\neq}f\,.

(216)

Note that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
commutes with
L_{\nu,\kappa}^{\overline{\psi},\iota}
and that
L_{\nu,\kappa}^{\overline{\psi},\iota}f_{0}=-\nu\Delta_{p}f_{0}
. Further note that
\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}f\big)=\mathbb{P}_{0}\big(\div_{x}(\bm{u}f)\big)=0
and that, since
\mathbb{P}_{0}\nabla_{x}\bm{u}=0
, the only contribution to the
k=0
mode from the
{\rm{div}}_{p}
term in (
214
) is due to interactions between
\bm{u}[f_{\neq}]
and
f_{\neq}
. The evolution of the zero mode
f_{0}(\bm{p},t)
thus satisfies a forced heat equation in
\bm{p}
whose forcing depends only on nonzero modes:

\partial_{t}f_{0}-\nu\Delta_{p}f_{0}+\mathbb{P}_{0}\,{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}[f_{\neq}]\bm{p})f_{\neq}]=0.

(217)

Using that
\bm{u}[f_{0}]=0
, the nonzero modes
f_{\neq}(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)
evolve via

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{\neq}-L_{\nu,\kappa}^{\overline{\psi},\iota}f_{\neq}+\bm{u}[f_{\neq}]\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\neq}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{\neq}{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}[f_{\neq}]\bm{p})f_{\neq}]

(218)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{\neq}{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}[f_{\neq}]\bm{p})f_{0}]=0.

We are now equipped to prove the nonlinear enhancement result of Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We begin by defining our function spaces. For the zero mode evolution, we will consider the space
X_{T}=\{g\in C_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}\,:\,\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}<\infty\}

Y_{T}=\{g\in C_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}\,:\,\lVert g\rVert_{Y_{T}}<\infty\}
,
where the norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X_{T}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Y_{T}}

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X_{T}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,e^{\delta_{0}\nu t}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}},\qquad\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Y_{T}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,e^{\delta_{\neq}\lambda_{\nu}t}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}

(219)

for constants
0<\delta_{0}<c_{0}
and
0<\delta_{\neq}<c_{\neq}
, where
c_{0}
(defined in (
222

c_{\neq}=\alpha_{0}/4
(defined in Corollary

) are decay rates from the linear theory.

We again use a bootstrap argument to show Theorem
4
. For some
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
C_{0}>2
to be determined, we assume that
\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon
, and aim to show that

\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{2}\varepsilon\,.

(220)

To show (

in (

depends on

via (

\displaystyle f_{0}(\bm{p},t)

\displaystyle=e^{t\nu\Delta_{p}}f^{\rm in}_{0}(\bm{p})+B_{0}(f_{\neq},f_{\neq})(\bm{p},t)\,,

(221)

\displaystyle B_{0}(f,g)(\bm{p},t)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{P}_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\nu\Delta_{p}}{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}[g]\bm{p})f](\bm{x},\bm{p},s)\,ds\,.

Note that by standard estimates for the heat equation on

\lVert e^{t\nu\Delta_{p}}\rVert_{L^{2}_{p}\to L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim e^{-c_{0}\nu t}\,,\qquad\lVert e^{t\nu\Delta_{p}}{\rm{div}}_{p}\rVert_{L^{2}_{p}\to L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim(\nu t)^{-1/2}e^{-c_{0}\nu t}

\displaystyle\lVert B_{0}(f_{\neq},f_{\neq})(\cdot,t)\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(\nu(t-s))^{-1/2}e^{-c_{0}\nu(t-s)}\lVert({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}_{\neq}\bm{p})f_{\neq}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(\nu(t-s))^{-1/2}e^{-c_{0}\nu(t-s)}e^{-2\delta_{\neq}\lambda_{\nu}s}\,ds\,\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-c_{0}\nu t}\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{\nu t}(\nu t-s)^{-1/2}\underbrace{e^{s}e^{-c\lambda_{\nu}\nu^{-1}s}}_{\leq e^{-c\lambda_{\nu}\nu^{-1}s/2}}\,ds\,\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}

for

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-a}e^{-bs}\,ds\lesssim_{a}b^{a-1}\,,\quad\quad\forall a\in(0,1)\,,b>0\,,t>0\,,

with
a=1/2
,

, we have

\lVert B_{0}(f_{\neq},f_{\neq})(\cdot,t)\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim e^{-c_{0}\nu t}\nu^{-1/2}\lambda_{\nu}^{-1/2}\,\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}\,.

(225)

In particular, again using (

f_{0}
satisfies

\lVert f_{0}\rVert_{X_{T}}\lesssim\lVert f^{\rm in}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}_{p}}+\nu^{-1/2}\lambda_{\nu}^{-1/2}C_{0}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\,.

(226)

We next consider the evolution of
f_{\neq}
. Recalling the definition of

from (

using Duhamel’s formula as

where

\displaystyle B_{1}(f,g)(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\,\div_{x}(\bm{u}[g]f)(\cdot,s)\,ds

(228)

\displaystyle B_{2}(f,g)(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{P}_{\neq}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\,{\rm{div}}_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})(\nabla_{x}\bm{u}[g]\bm{p})f](\cdot,s)\,ds\,.

We proceed to estimate each term in (

, we will make use of the following more standard semigroup estimates, which follow directly from Corollary

\displaystyle\lVert B_{1}(f_{\neq},f_{\neq})(\cdot,t)\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lVert f_{\neq}\rVert_{Y_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big[1+\kappa^{-1/2}\lambda_{\nu}^{-1/2}C_{0}^{2}\varepsilon+\nu^{-1/2}\lambda_{\nu}^{-1/2}C_{0}^{2}\varepsilon

To facilitate comparison with results from the applied and computational literature, we comment here on our choice of nondimensionalization for equations (

The fully dimensional version of the kinetic model is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi+V_{0}\bm{p}\cdot\nabla\psi+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi+\div_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})\nabla\bm{u}\bm{p}\,\psi]

\displaystyle=d_{\rm r}\Delta_{p}\psi+d_{\rm t}\Delta_{x}\psi

(236)

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla q=\div{\bm{\Sigma}}\,,\;\div\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0

(237)

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}=\sigma_{0}\int_{S^{d-1}}\psi(\bm{x},\bm{p},t)\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p}

\displaystyle\,d\bm{p}\,,

(238)

where
V_{0}
is the average swimming speed of the particles,
d_{\rm r}
and
d_{\rm t}
are the rotational and translational diffusion coefficients,
\mu
is the fluid viscosity, and
\sigma_{0}
is the (signed) active stress magnitude. The above system is considered on a

-dimensional periodic box with side length

\mathbb{R}^{d}/L\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

We nondimensionalize (
236
)-(

) according to

\bm{u}^{*}=\frac{1}{V_{0}}\bm{u}\,,\quad\bm{x}^{*}=\frac{2\pi}{L}\bm{x}\,,\quad t^{*}=\frac{2\pi V_{0}}{L}t\,,\quad\psi^{*}=\frac{L\left\lvert\sigma_{0}\right\rvert}{2\pi\mu V_{0}}\psi\,.

(239)

Here, the (nondimensionalized) particle number density

\overline{\psi}=\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\psi^{*}\,d\bm{p}\,d\bm{x}

(240)

is a free parameter.
Note that we may write

\overline{\psi}=\frac{L\left\lvert\sigma_{0}\right\rvert n_{\psi}}{2\pi\mu V_{0}}\,,

(241)

where

is the dimensional particle number density, given by (

) with
\psi
in place of
\psi^{*}
. The dimensionless rotational and translational diffusion coefficients

,
\kappa
in (

\nu=\frac{d_{\rm r}L}{2\pi V_{0}}\,,\quad\kappa=\frac{d_{\rm t}2\pi}{LV_{0}}\,.

(242)

Appendix B

\nu>0

Definition B.1

(ii) the PDE (

(Existence)
. For all
0\leq\psi^{\rm in}\in H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}\cap L^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{p}

\bar{T}=\bar{T}(\|\psi^{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}},\nu)>0

)-(

\Omega^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times(0,\bar{T})

.

Proof.

(Existence)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. For
0\leq\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Define the

\psi_{-1}=0

\psi_{n}

(251)

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\nabla_{x}^{2}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla^{2}\bm{u}\rVert_{L^{4}_{x}}\lVert\nabla_{x}\psi\rVert_{L^{4}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\lVert\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}^{2}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle-\nu\lVert\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}^{2}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}-\kappa\lVert\nabla_{x}^{3}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\,.

Furthermore, we note that
\nabla\bm{u}
satisfies

\lVert\nabla\bm{u}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\,.

(252)

Using Sobolev embedding and the bound (
252
) for
\bm{u}
, and using Young’s inequality to absorb the terms
\lVert\nabla_{p}\nabla_{x}^{2}\psi\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}
into the rotational diffusion term, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{p}\psi\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\nu}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{3}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{4}\,.

(253)

By Gronwall’s inequality, there exists
\bar{T}=\bar{T}(\|\psi^{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}},\nu)>0
with the property

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}(\Omega^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times(0,\bar{T}))}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{p}\psi\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}(\Omega^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times(0,\bar{T}))}^{2}\lesssim_{\nu}\|\psi^{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}\,

(254)

While the above
a priori
estimates were for smooth
\bm{u}
, analogous computations for the Picard iterates
\psi_{n}
to the mollified equations produce the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{p}\psi_{n}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\nu}(\lVert\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}+\lVert\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2})\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}

(255)

and, consequently, the
a priori
estimates

\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}(\Omega^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times(0,\bar{T}))}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla_{p}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}(\Omega^{d}\times S^{d-1}\times(0,\bar{T}))}^{2}\lesssim_{\nu}\|\psi^{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}^{2}\,.

(256)

2. Contraction
. Letting
\bm{u}_{n}=\bm{u}[\psi_{n}]
, the difference in Picard iterates
\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(

\displaystyle\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})+\bm{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})+(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}

(257)

\displaystyle+\bm{u}_{n-2}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})+\div_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})\nabla(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\bm{p}\psi_{n}]

\displaystyle+\div_{p}[({\bf I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})\nabla\bm{u}_{n-2}\bm{p}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})]

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta_{p}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})+\kappa\Delta_{x}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\,.

Multiplying by
\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}
and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\lesssim\left|\int_{x,p}(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\,d\bm{x}\,d\bm{p}\right|

(258)

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla\bm{u}_{n-2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lVert\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}\lVert\nabla_{p}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}

\displaystyle-\nu\lVert\nabla_{p}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}-\kappa\lVert\nabla_{x}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\,.

In dimension three and on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x,p}(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\,d\bm{x}\,d\bm{p}\right|

(259)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2}\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{3}}\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}}\,.

The approach on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is more subtle, and we return to it later. By the
a priori
bound (
256
), we have that
\lVert\psi_{n}\rVert_{H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}
is controlled for sufficiently small time. Furthermore, by (
252
), we may bound
\lVert\nabla(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}
by
\lVert\psi_{n-1}-\psi_{n-2}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}
. Using Young’s inequality to absorb the
\lVert\nabla_{p}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}
term into the rotational diffusion term, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\lesssim_{\nu}\lVert\psi_{n-1}-\psi_{n-2}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}+\lVert\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,p}}^{2}\,.

(260)

This is a differential inequality of the form

\frac{d}{dt}A_{n}\leq MA_{n-1}+MA_{n}\,,

(261)

where
M>0
is independent of
n
and each
A_{n}(t)\geq 0
. By Grönwall’s inequality,

A_{n}(t)\leq M\int_{0}^{t}e^{M(t-s)}A_{n-1}(s)\,ds\,.

(262)

In particular, for sufficiently small
T
, we have
\sup_{t\in(0,T)}A_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}A_{n-1}
.
Thus, we have strong convergence of the Picard iterates
\psi_{n}\to\psi_{\infty}
in
C_{t}L^{2}_{x,p}
for short times. By lower semicontinuity, the
a priori
estimates (
256

n\to+\infty
. Then we allow the mollification parameter
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. That the solutions belong to
C([0,T];H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}\cap L^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{p})
can be justified after the fact via the linear theory. Looking ahead, once uniqueness is known, we can extend the solution to its maximal time of existence, for which a lower bound is
\bar{T}
from Step 1.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\|_{L^{1}}

(263)

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}+\|\div_{p}[(\mathbf{I}-\bm{p}\otimes\bm{p})\nabla(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\bm{p}\psi_{n}]\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\psi_{n-1}-\psi_{n-2}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\psi_{n-1}-\psi_{n-2}\|_{L^{2}})\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{1}_{p}\cap H^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x,p}(\bm{u}_{n-1}-\bm{u}_{n-2})\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{n}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1})\,d\bm{x}\,d\bm{p}\right|

First, we prove the Poincaré-type inequality (

Proof of Lemma
15
.

Without loss of generality, we may choose
\bm{k}=k\bm{e}_{1}
with
k=|\bm{k}|
and define
\bm{p}=p_{1}\bm{e}_{1}+p_{2}\bm{e}_{2}+p_{3}\bm{e}_{3}
with
p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}=1
.

Away from
p_{1}^{2}=1
, we may bound the full spatial gradient
k\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}
in terms of the spatial gradient projected off the unit sphere, i.e.,
k\lVert(\bm{e}_{1}-p_{1}\bm{p})f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}=k\lVert\sqrt{1-p_{1}^{2}}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}
.
Near
p_{1}^{2}=1
, we must instead control
k\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}
using the orientational gradient
\lVert\nabla_{p}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}
.

We thus define a cutoff function on the unit sphere:

\varphi_{\delta}=\begin{cases}1,&1-p_{1}^{2}<\delta\\
0,&1-p_{1}^{2}>2\delta\end{cases}

(265)

for some
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
, with smooth decay between. Then, away from
p_{1}^{2}=1
, we have

\lVert f_{k}(1-\varphi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\lVert\sqrt{1-p_{1}^{2}}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

(266)

Near
p_{1}^{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert f_{k}\varphi_{\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\delta\lVert\nabla_{p}(f_{k}\varphi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq c\delta\bigg(\lVert(\nabla_{p}f_{k})\varphi_{\delta}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert f_{k}(\nabla_{p}\varphi_{\delta})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)

(267)

\displaystyle\leq c\delta\bigg(\lVert\nabla_{p}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\lVert\sqrt{1-p_{1}^{2}}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigg)\,,

where we have used that
|\nabla_{p}\varphi_{\delta}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta^{1/2}}
is supported within the strip
\delta\leq 1-p_{1}^{2}\leq 2\delta
.
Together, we obtain

k^{2}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{k^{2}}{\delta}\lVert\sqrt{1-p_{1}^{2}}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta k^{2}\lVert\nabla_{p}f_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

(268)

and, choosing
\delta=\frac{1}{4}\nu^{1/2}k^{-1/2}
, we obtain Lemma
15
.

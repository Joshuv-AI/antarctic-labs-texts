Quantitative equidistribution of angles of multipliers
Yan Mary He, Hongming Nie

Abstract
. We study angles of multipliers of repelling cycles for hyperbolic rational maps in ℂ ⁡ ( z ) \mathbb{C}(z) . For a fixed K ≫ 1 K\gg 1 , we show that almost all intervals of length 2 ​ π / K 2\pi/K in ( − π , π ] (-\pi,\pi] contain a multiplier angle with the property that the norm of the multiplier is bounded above by a polynomial in K K .



1.
Introduction

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
. We consider the dynamical system
h:J(h)\to J(h)
where
J(h)
is the Julia set of
h
. We denote by
\delta
the Hausdorff dimension of the Julia set
J(h)
and by
h^{k}
the
k
-th iterate of
h
for
k\geq 1
. A periodic orbit
\hat{z}=\{z,h(z),...,h^{n-1}(z)\}
of period
n\geq 1
is called
primitive
if
h^{n}(z)=z
and
h^{m}(z)\neq z
for any
1\leq m<n
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primitive periodic orbits of
h
in
J(h)
. If
\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}
has period
n
, the quantity

\lambda(\hat{z}):=(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)

(calculated in local coordinates) is called the
multiplier
of
\hat{z}
. Its
holonomy
is given by

\lambda_{\theta}(\hat{z}):=\frac{\lambda(\hat{z})}{|\lambda(\hat{z})|}.

A celebrated result of Oh and Winter
[
6
]
states that if
h
is not conjugate to a monomial
z\mapsto z^{\pm{\deg(h)}}
, then there exists
\eta>0
such that

(1.1)

\mathcal{N}_{t}:=\#\{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}:|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\}={\rm Li}(t^{\delta})+O(t^{\delta-\eta})

where
{\rm Li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{du}{\log u}\sim x/\log x
as
x\to\infty
. We write
g_{1}(x)=O(g_{2}(x))
as
x\to\infty
if there exists
C>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|g_{1}(x)|\leq C|g_{2}(x)|
for all
x\geq x_{0}
. We also write
g_{1}(x)\sim g_{2}(x)
as
x\to\infty
if
\lim_{x\to\infty}g_{1}(x)/g_{2}(x)=1
.

Moreover, if the Julia set
J(h)
is not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, Oh and Winter showed that for any
\psi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, we have

(1.2)

\sum_{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}:|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t}\psi(\lambda_{\theta}(\hat{z}))=\int_{0}^{1}\psi(e^{2\pi i\theta})d(\theta)\cdot\mathrm{Li}(t^{\delta})+O(t^{\delta-\eta}).

In particular, for a fixed interval
I\subset(-\pi,\pi]
, we have

(1.3)

\frac{\#\{\hat{z}:|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t,{\rm Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))\in I\}}{\mathcal{N}_{t}}\sim\frac{|I|}{2\pi},\text{ as }t\to\infty

where
|I|
is the length of the interval
I
. The notation
{\rm Arg}(z)
denotes the principal argument of a complex number
z
.

In this paper we study the existence of multiplier angles in small intervals and obtain a quantitative equidistribution result on angles of multipliers.
For a fixed
t\gg 1
, if we divide
(-\pi,\pi]
equally into more than
\mathcal{N}_{t}
intervals, then
it is not hard to see that there exists an interval
I
with
|I|<2\pi\mathcal{N}_{t}^{-1}
for which (
1.3
) does not hold. Therefore, for small intervals, one can expect at most a statistical statement about the existence of multiplier angles. We show in this paper that for a fixed
K\gg 1
, almost all intervals of length
2\pi/K
in
(-\pi,\pi]
contain a multiplier angle with the property that the norm of the multiplier is bounded above by a polynomial in
K
. Our result is inspired by recent results in analytic number theory, in particular by Rudnick-Waxman
[
9
]
and Parzanchevski-Sarnak
[
7
]
. Another equidistribution result on multipliers of hyperbolic rational maps was recently obtained by Sharp-Stylianou
[
10
]
.

1.1.
Statement of main result

We now describe our results in more details. Given
K\gg 1
, we divide the interval
(-\pi,\pi]
into
K
disjoint intervals of equal length and study the number of multiplier angles
{\rm Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))
falling into each such interval subject to the constraint
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t
for some fixed
t\gg 1
. If the number
K
of intervals is larger than the number
\mathcal{N}_{t}
of angles, then there exists an interval which does not contain any multiplier angles. Our main theorem shows that if
\mathcal{N}_{t}
(or equivalently
t
) is greater than a certain power of
K
, then almost all intervals of size
2\pi/K
contain at least one multiplier angle
{\rm Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))
with
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
. Suppose that
J(h)
is not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then for almost all
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, the interval
\left[\theta-\frac{\pi}{K},\theta+\frac{\pi}{K}\right]
contains at least one multiplier angle
{\rm Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))
with

|\lambda(\hat{z})|\leq K^{\frac{7}{2\alpha}}

where
\alpha=\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{2},2\eta\right\}
, and
\delta
and
\eta
are as in (
1.1
).

Here by almost all, we mean that the probability of
\theta
not satisfying the desired property has an upper bound which tends to zero as
K
tends to infinity.
Our result suggests a polynomial bound for
|\lambda(\hat{z})|
in terms of
K
. If the angles of multipliers are equidistributed for sufficiently large
t
, we expect that a sharp bound is given by

t=K^{\frac{1}{\delta}}(\log K)^{\frac{1}{\delta}+o(1)}.

1.2.
Strategy of the proofs

We prove the theorem by studying the variance of suitable smooth counting functions. More specifically, for
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, let

\mathcal{N}_{K,t}(\theta):=\#\{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}:|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t,\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))\in\left[\theta-\frac{\pi}{K},\theta+\frac{\pi}{K}\right]\}

be the number of primitive periodic orbits with multipliers having norm smaller than
t
and angles in the interval of length
2\pi/K
centered at
\theta
.

Recall that the expected value of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}(\theta)
is

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathcal{N}_{K,t}(\theta)\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

and the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}(\theta)
is given by

{\rm Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\mathcal{N}_{K,t}-\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})|^{2}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.

If
\mathcal{N}_{t}\ll K
, we prove in Section
6
that the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}
is

{\rm Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})\sim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}.

The more interesting case is when
K\ll\mathcal{N}_{t}
. In this case, it is not straightforward to obtain an effective bound for the variance
{\rm Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})
. Alternatively, adopting ideas from analytic number theory, we consider a smoothed version
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
of the counting function
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}
as follows:

(1.4)

\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h^{n}(z)=z\\
\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)F_{K}\left({\rm Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z))-\theta\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|

where
\phi
and
F_{K}
are some cut-off functions for the norm
|\lambda(\hat{z})|
and the angle
{\rm Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))
, respectively. The function
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
is a smooth count for the number of primitive periodic orbits with multipliers whose norms are less than
t
and angles lie in
[\theta-\pi/K,\theta+\pi/K]
.

The main challenge of this paper is to derive technical estimates on various quantities which lead to a bound on the variance of
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
. A key quantity we consider is the
non-primitive
version
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
of the smooth function
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
. The function
\Phi_{K,t}
is derived from
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
by taking the same formula as (
1.4
) except that we sum over periodic points
z
satisfying
h^{n}(z)=z
that are not necessarily primitive. An important step is to find an upper bound for the variance of
\Phi_{K,t}
. To achieve this, we use methods from analytic number theory; namely, we apply the Mellin inversion formula to the function
\phi
so that we can write
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
as an expression involving the logarithmic derivative of the dynamical
L
-function for the dynamical system
(J(h),h)
. The work of Oh-Winter
[
6
]
provides an upper bound for this derivative, and therefore with some additional work we are able to obtain an upper bound for the variance of
\Phi_{K,t}
. Then to obtain a bound on the variance of
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
, we consider the difference
\Phi_{K,t}-\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
between the non-primitive and the primitive functions. In particular, we bound its
L^{2}
-norm and its expectation. Finally, we prove Theorem
5.1
, which is a slightly stronger version of Theorem
1.1
, by using these estimates together with the Chebyshev inequality.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review basic definitions and results in complex dynamics and analytic number theory. We define the functions
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
and
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
as mentioned above in Section
3
. Section

\Phi_{K,t}
,
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}
and their difference. Then we prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
5
. Finally, we study the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}
when
K\gg\mathcal{N}_{t}
in Section
6
.

2.1.
Hyperbolic rational maps and multipliers

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a rational map of degree at least
2
. We say that
h
is
hyperbolic
if there exist
C>1
and a smooth conformal metric
\rho
defined on a neighborhood of its Julia set
J(h)
such that
||h^{\prime}(z)||_{\rho}>C
for all
z\in J(h)
. For equivalent definitions, we refer the reader to
[
3
]
.

Consider the dynamical system
h:J(h)\to J(h)
. Recall that
\mathcal{P}
is the set of primitive periodic orbits of
h
in
J(h)
, and
\lambda(\hat{z})
is the multiplier for
\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}
. For
n\geq 1
, we denote
\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})
the set of all points
z\in J(h)
with
h^{n}(z)=z
, and denote
\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})
the subset of
\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})
consisting of primitive periodic points with period
n
.

We show in the next lemma that multipliers and periods are comparable.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
. Then there exist
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
such that for any
n\geq 1
and any
z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})
,

C_{1}<\frac{\log|\lambda(\hat{z})|}{n}<C_{2}.

Proof.

Up to conjugation by Möbius transformations, we may assume that
\infty\not\in J(h)
. Since
h
is hyperbolic, there exist
C>1
and a smooth conformal metric
\rho(z)=\omega(z)dz
with
\omega(z)>0
on a neighborhood of
J(h)
such that for any
z\in J(h)
, we have

||h^{\prime}(z)||_{\rho}=\frac{\omega(h(z))|h^{\prime}(z)|}{\omega(z)}>C.

For
z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})
, consider the probability measure

\mu_{z}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\nu_{h^{j}(z)},

where
\nu_{h^{j}(z)}
is the Dirac measure at
h^{j}(z)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{J(h)}\log|h^{\prime}(z)|d\mu_{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{J(h)}\log||h^{\prime}(z)||_{\rho}d\mu_{z}+\int_{J(h)}\log w(z)-\log w(h(z))d\mu_{z}.

The second term
\int_{J(h)}\log w(z)-\log w(h(z))d\mu_{z}=0
since
\mu_{z}
is invariant under
h_{\ast}
. It follows that

\frac{\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{n}>\log C.

Moreover, since
J(h)
is compact, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
|h^{\prime}(z)|<C_{0}
for any
z\in J(h)
. It follows that
\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|<n\log C_{0}
.

The conclusion follows immediately by setting
C_{1}=\log C
and
C_{2}=\log C_{0}
.
∎

2.2.
Dynamical L-functions

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
and let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primitive periodic orbits of
h
in
J(h)
. Recall that the
Ruelle dynamical zeta function
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\zeta(s):=\prod_{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}}(1-|\lambda(\hat{z})|^{-s})^{-1}.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be an unitary character. The group of unitary characters of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
can be identified with
\mathbb{Z}
via the map
\chi_{k}(x)=x^{k}
. Then the
dynamical (Hecke) L-function
is obtained by twisting the zeta function by an unitary character:

\zeta(s,k):=\prod_{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}}(1-\chi_{k}(\lambda_{\theta}(\hat{z}))|\lambda(\hat{z})|^{-s})^{-1}.

The (dynamical)
L
-function is holomorphic and non-vanishing on
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\delta
. Moreover, the
L
-function
\zeta(s,k)
can also be written as

(2.2)

\zeta(s,k)=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in{\rm Fix}(h^{n})}\chi_{k}\left(\frac{(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)}{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}\right)|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|^{-s}\right).

We refer the reader to
[
6
]
and the references therein for more details on
L
-functions.

The
L
-function
\zeta(s,k)
satisfies the following analytic property.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
.

(1)

If
h
is not conjugate to a monomial, then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\zeta(s,0)
is analytic and non-vanishing on
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\delta-\epsilon_{0}
except for the simple pole at
s=\delta
.

(2)

If
J(h)
is not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, then for any
k\neq 0
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\zeta(s,k)
is analytic and non-vanishing on
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\delta-\epsilon_{0}
.

Moreover, we have the following bound for the logarithmic derivative of
\zeta(s,k)
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
6
]
Section 6)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
, and suppose
J(h)
is not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
C_{\epsilon}>1
,
0<\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon_{0}
and
0<\beta<1
such that for all
{\rm Re}(s)\geq\delta-\epsilon_{1}
and
|{\rm Im}(s)|\geq 1
, we have

(2.3)

\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}\right|\leq C_{\epsilon}(|k|+1)^{2+\epsilon}|{\rm Im}(s)|^{\beta}.

Following arguments of
[
8
]
, we can deduce equation (
1.1
) from the above lemma. In particular, we have

(2.4)

0<\eta\leq\epsilon_{1}/2.

2.3.
The Mellin transform and the Fourier transform

For
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
, the
Mellin transform
of
g
is

\mathcal{M}(g)(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)x^{s-1}dx.

Since
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
,
\mathcal{M}(g)(s)
is analytic in the
s
-plane. The Mellin inversion formula allows us to write
g(x)
in terms of
\mathcal{M}(g)(s)
. More precisely, we have

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(s)=c}\mathcal{M}(g)(s)x^{-s}ds

for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
for more details on the Mellin transform and its inversion.

Recall that the Fourier transform of
g
is defined as

\mathcal{F}(g)(y)=\hat{g}(y):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(x)e^{-2\pi iyx}dx.

Then the Mellin transform is related to the Fourier transform as follows

\mathcal{M}(g)(-2\pi iy)=\mathcal{F}(g(e^{x}))(y).

If
g
has compact support in
(0,1]
, then
\mathcal{M}(g)(s)
is analytic in the
s
-plane. Moreover,
g\circ\exp
also has compact support. Therefore
g\circ\exp
belongs to the Schwartz class (i.e. smooth functions whose derivatives of all orders decay faster than any polynomial). Since the Fourier transform is an isometry between the Schwartz class,
\mathcal{F}(g(e^{x}))(y)
is in the Schwartz class and thus decays faster than any polynomial as
y\to\pm\infty
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}(g)(-2\pi iy)
decays faster than any polynomial along the imaginary axis as
y
goes to infinity.

Now choose
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
such that
[1/3,2/3]\subset\mathrm{supp}(g)\subset(0,1]
and
g(x)>0
on the interior of its support. Let
\gamma>0
and consider

(2.5)

\phi(x):=x^{-\gamma}g(x).

Then
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
is real-valued and non-negative. Moreover, it satisfies the following conditions:

(a.1)

||\phi||_{\infty}<\infty
,

(a.2)

\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subset(0,1]
,

(a.3)

L_{\phi}:=\min\limits_{1/3\leq x\leq 2/3}\phi(x)>0
,

(a.4)

the Mellin transform

\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)=\mathcal{M}(g)(s-\gamma)

is analytic in the
s
-plane, and

(a.5)

for
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\gamma
, there exist
A_{\phi}:=A_{\phi}(\mathrm{Re}(s))>0
and
Y>1
such that for
|\mathrm{Im(s)}|>Y
, we have

(2.6)

|\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)|\leq\frac{A_{\phi}}{(1+|\mathrm{Im(s)}|)^{2}}.

3.1.
Window functions for angles

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be even and real-valued with support
[-1/2,1/2]
such that
f>0
on
(-1/2,1/2)
. Fix
K\gg 1
and define

F_{K}(\theta):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f\left(\frac{K}{2\pi}(\theta-2\pi j)\right).

Then
F_{K}
is
2\pi
-periodic, even and nonnegative real-valued. Moreover,
F_{K}(\theta)>0
if and only if
\theta\in(2\pi j-\pi/K,2\pi j+\pi/K)
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The Fourier expansion of
F_{K}(\theta)
is given by

(3.1)

F_{K}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{F}_{K}(k)e^{ik\theta}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{ik\theta}

where
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
. Note that
\hat{f}(y)
is also even and real-valued.
For
z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})
and
k\geq 0
, we denote

\Theta_{k}(z,n):=\chi_{k}\left(\frac{(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)}{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}\right)=\left(\frac{(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)}{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}\right)^{k}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})
,

(3.2)

F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z)))=F_{K}(\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z))-\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\Theta_{k}(z,n)

and therefore

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z)))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\frac{\hat{f}(0)}{K}.

Proof.

Equation (
3.2
) follows from the Fourier expansion (
3.1
) of
F_{K}
.
For
z\in{\rm Fix}(h^{n})
,
{\rm Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z))\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Then
F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z)))>0
if and only if
\theta-{\rm Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z))\in(2\pi j-\pi/K,2\pi j+\pi/K)
for some unique
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore for such
j
, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z)))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\int_{2j\pi-\pi}^{2j\pi+\pi}F_{K}(u)\frac{du}{2\pi}.

It follows that

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z)))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\int_{2j\pi-\pi}^{2j\pi+\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-iku}\frac{du}{2\pi}=\frac{\hat{f}(0)}{K}.

∎

3.2.
Smooth counting functions

In this subsection, we define smooth functions
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
and
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
as stated in Section
1
. We will estimate their variance in the next section.

Fix
\gamma>0
and pick
\phi=\phi_{\gamma}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,\infty)
which satisfies
(a.1)-(a.5)
in Section

. Let
f
and
F_{K}
be as in Section

For
K\gg 1
and

, we define

(3.3)

\Phi_{K,t}(\theta):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)F_{K}\left({\rm Arg}(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)-\theta\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|,

and

(3.4)

\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{*}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)F_{K}\left({\rm Arg}(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)-\theta\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|.

By substituting the Fourier expansion (
3.1
) of
F_{K}
, we can rewrite
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
and
\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}(\theta)
as

(3.5)

\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i\theta k}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|,

and

(3.6)

\Phi_{K,t}^{\ast}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i\theta k}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|.

To ease notations, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we set

(3.7)

\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|,

and

(3.8)

\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|.

Then we have

(3.9)

\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta),

and

(3.10)

\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta).

4.
The variance of
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)

In this section, we let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
with Julia set
J(h)
not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. We continue to use the notation from the previous sections.
For any
\epsilon>0
, let
\epsilon_{1}>0
be as in Lemma
2.3
and set
\gamma=\delta-\epsilon_{1}
. We consider the corresponding functions
\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)
and
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
in Section
3.2
. Fixing
K\gg 1
, we prove the following theorem regarding estimates on the variance of
\Phi^{*}_{K,t}(\theta)
for sufficiently large
t
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists
M_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\mathrm{Var}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})<M_{\epsilon}\max\left\{\frac{t^{\delta}(\log t)^{2}}{K},K^{5}t^{2\delta-2\epsilon_{1}}\right\}.

Before we proceed to the proof of the theorem, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}):=||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}+||\Phi_{K,t}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})||_{L^{2}}+|\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})|

where
||\cdot||_{L^{2}}
denotes the
L^{2}
-norm.
Subsections 4.1-4.3 are devoted to estimating the three terms in the last line of the inequality. We prove Theorem
4.1
in the last subsection.

We begin with the third term involving expected values.

4.1.
Expected values: the third term

We first estimate the expected value of
\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}
.
We denote
g_{1}(x)\asymp g_{2}(x)
if there exist
0<A^{\prime}<A^{\prime\prime}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
A^{\prime}g_{2}(x)<g_{1}(x)<A^{\prime\prime}g_{2}(x)
for all
x>x_{0}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The expected value of
\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}
is

\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})\asymp\frac{t^{\delta}\log t}{K}.

Proof.

By definition of expected value, we have

\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})=\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}(0)\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}.

Note that for any fixed
n\geq 1
, equation (
1.1
) implies that

\#\{z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n}):|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\leq t\}\sim n\mathrm{Li}(t^{\delta}).

Then we obtain

\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|<2||\phi||_{\infty}t^{\delta}.

Let
C_{1}>0
be as in Lemma
2.1
. Then

\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})\leq|\hat{f}(0)|\left|\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=1}^{(\log t)/C_{1}}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}\right|<\frac{2}{C_{1}}|\hat{f}(0)|||\phi||_{\infty}\frac{t^{\delta}\log t}{K}.

Moreover, by equation (
1.1
), there exists
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(h)>0
such that

(4.1)

\#\left\{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}:\frac{t}{3}\leq|\lambda(\hat{z})|<\frac{2t}{3}\right\}\geq\mathrm{Li}\left(\frac{(2t)^{\delta}}{3^{\delta}}\right)-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\delta}\mathrm{Li}\left(\frac{t^{\delta}}{3^{\delta}}\right)\geq C^{\prime}\mathrm{Li}(t^{\delta}).

It follows that

\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})\\
\frac{t}{3}\leq|\lambda(\hat{z})|<\frac{2t}{3}\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\geq L_{\phi}C^{\prime}t^{\delta}.

Let
C_{2}>0
be as in Lemma
2.1
. Inequality (
4.1
) implies that
n\geq C_{2}\log(t/3)
. Hence

\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})\geq|\hat{f}(0)|\left|\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=1}^{C_{2}\log(t/3)}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}\right|\geq\frac{1}{K}|\hat{f}(0)|\cdot C_{2}\log(t/3)\cdot L_{\phi}C^{\prime}t^{\delta}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

The following result bounds the difference of the expected values of
\Phi_{K,t}
and
\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists
B=B(h,\phi,f)>0
such that

|\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})|<B\frac{t^{\delta/2}\log t}{K}.

Proof.

By definition of expected value, we write

\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})=\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}(0)\sum_{n\geq 1}(\Lambda_{t,n,0}-\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}).

Note that for any fixed
n\geq 1
, equation (
1.1
) implies that

\#\{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})\setminus\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n}):|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\leq t\}<n\mathrm{Li}(t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}).

Indeed, for
z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})\setminus\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})
with
|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\leq t
, we have
\lambda(\hat{z})<t^{1/2}
and
\#\hat{z}\leq n/2
.
It follows that

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{t,n,0}-\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})\setminus\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle<2||\phi||_{\infty}t^{\frac{\delta}{2}}.

Let
C_{1}>0
be as in Lemma
2.1
. Then

|\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})|\leq\frac{1}{K}|\hat{f}(0)|\sum_{n=1}^{(\log t)/C_{1}}(|\Lambda_{t,n,0}-\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,0}|)<\frac{1}{C_{1}}||\phi||_{\infty}|\hat{f}(0)|\frac{t^{\delta/2}\log t}{K}.

∎

As a corollary of the previous two results, we obtain an estimate on
\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})
.

Corollary 4.4
.

The expected value of
\Phi_{K,t}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})\asymp\frac{t^{\delta}\log t}{K}.

4.2.
Variance of
\Phi_{K,t}
: the second term

Before we prove the main result of this subsection, namely Proposition
4.6
, we need a lemma which bounds the term
\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)
. Recall that
\epsilon_{0}>0
is as in Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, if
k\not=0
, there exists
C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}:=C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(h,\phi)>0
such that

\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|<C^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(1+|k|)^{2+\epsilon}t^{\delta-\epsilon_{1}}.

Proof.

By definition,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|.

By the assumption on
\phi
, the line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\delta-\epsilon_{1}
is in the domain of holomorphy of the Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)
for
\phi
. Then we apply Mellin inversion and obtain that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(s)=\delta-\epsilon_{1}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\Theta_{k}(z,n)\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|\frac{t^{s}}{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|^{s}}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)ds.

On the other hand, by formula (
2.2
) we calculate the logarithmic derivative (with respect to
s
) of the
L
-function
\zeta(s,k)
:

-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})}\Theta_{k}(z,n)\frac{\log|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|^{s}}.

Since
k\not=0
, by Theorem
2.2
(2), the function
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}
is analytic on the half plane
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq\delta-\epsilon_{0}
.
Therefore, it follows that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(s)=\delta-\epsilon_{1}}-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}t^{s}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)ds.

Thus, to bound
\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|
, it suffices to bound
\int_{\mathrm{Re}(s)=\delta-\epsilon_{1}}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}t^{s}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)\right||ds|
.

Write
s=\delta-\epsilon_{1}+yi
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that for
|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\geq 1
,

\left|-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}\right|\leq C_{\epsilon}(1+|k|)^{2+\epsilon}|y|^{\beta}.

Moreover, by (
2.6
), there exist
A_{\phi}>0
and
Y>1
such that for
|y|>Y
,

\left|\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)\right|\leq\frac{A_{\phi}}{(1+|y|)^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{Re}(s)=\delta-\epsilon_{1}}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s,k)}{\zeta(s,k)}t^{s}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(s)\right||ds|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}t^{\delta-\epsilon_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}{\zeta(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{\delta-\epsilon_{1}}}{2\pi}\int_{|y|\leq Y}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}{\zeta(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy)\right|dy

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{t^{\delta-\epsilon_{1}}}{2\pi}C_{\epsilon}(1+|k|)^{2+\epsilon}\int_{|y|>Y}\frac{A_{\phi}|y|^{\beta}}{(1+|y|)^{2}}dy.

Note that
\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}{\zeta(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy)\right|
is analytic in
y
. It follows that

\int_{|y|\leq Y}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}{\zeta(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy,k)}\mathcal{M}(\phi)(\delta-\epsilon_{1}+iy)\right|dy<\infty.

Then the conclusion follows.
∎

Now we give a bound on the variance of
\Phi_{K,t}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

For any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists
B_{\epsilon}:=B_{\epsilon}(h,\phi,f)>0
such that

\mathrm{Var}(\Phi_{K,t})<B_{\epsilon}K^{5}t^{2\delta-2\epsilon_{1}}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\not=0}i\frac{K^{2}}{k^{3}}\widehat{f^{(3)}}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta),

where
\widehat{f^{(3)}}
is the Fourier transform of the third derivative
f^{(3)}
of
f
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\Phi_{K,t,})

\displaystyle:=||\Phi_{K,t}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\not=0}\frac{K^{4}}{k^{6}}\left|\widehat{f^{(3)}}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|^{2}.

We claim that the series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\left|\widehat{f^{(3)}}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\right|^{2}
is finite. Indeed, we observe that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{L}\left|\widehat{f^{(3)}}\left(\frac{k}{L}\right)\right|^{2}\to\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\widehat{f^{(3)}}(y)|^{2}dy

as
L\to\infty
. Since
f^{(3)}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, by Parseval’s identity,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\widehat{f^{(3)}}(y)|^{2}dy=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|f^{(3)}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty.

Applying Lemma
4.5
and noting that
\sum_{k\not=0}(1+|k|^{2+\epsilon})^{2}/k^{6}<\infty
, we obtain the desired bound.
∎

4.3.
Primitive versus non-primitive: the first term

The main result of this subsection is Lemma
4.8
where we obtain a bound on the first term
\mathbb{E}(|\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}|^{2})^{1/2}
. Similar to the previous subsection, we first prove a lemma bounding
\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)
.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists
B^{\prime}:=B^{\prime}(h,\phi,f)>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}

\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|<B^{\prime}t^{\delta/2}\log t.

Proof.

Straightforward computations give an upper bound of the left hand side as follows.

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\log t\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})\setminus\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log t\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{z\in\mathrm{Fix}(h^{n})\setminus\mathrm{Fix}^{\ast}(h^{n})}\phi\left(\frac{|(h^{n})^{\prime}(z)|}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq||\phi||_{\infty}(\log t)\cdot\frac{n}{2}\cdot\#\{\hat{x}\in\mathcal{P}:|\lambda(\hat{x})|\leq t^{1/2}\}.

Let
C_{1}>0
be as in Lemma
2.1
. Then
n\leq(\log t)/C_{1}
. Then applying equation (
1.1
), we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

There exists
B^{\prime\prime}:=B^{\prime\prime}(h,\phi,f)>0
such that

\mathbb{E}(|\Phi_{K,t}-\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}|^{2})<B^{\prime\prime}\frac{t^{\delta}(\log t)^{2}}{K}.

Proof.

Note that

\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right).

Then by Lemma
4.7
, there exists
B^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K}\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)e^{-ik\theta}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right)\right|\right|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K^{2}}\left|\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda_{t,n,k}(\theta)-\sum_{n\geq 1}\Lambda^{\ast}_{t,n,k}(\theta)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{K^{2}}\left|\hat{f}\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)\right|^{2}(B^{\prime}t^{\delta/2}\log t)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

It follows that

||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}<2||\hat{f}||_{L^{2}}B^{\prime}\frac{t^{\delta/2}\log t}{K^{1/2}}.

Thus the conclusion follows.
∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

We combine the results from the previous three subsections to give a proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By triangle inequality, we have

||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})||_{L^{2}}\leq||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}+||\Phi_{K,t,\epsilon}-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})||_{L^{2}}+|\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})|.

By Lemma
4.8
, we have

||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}=\mathbb{E}(|\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}|^{2})^{1/2}<B^{\prime\prime 1/2}\frac{t^{\delta/2}\log t}{K^{1/2}}.

By Proposition
4.6
, we have

||\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}-\Phi_{K,t}||_{L^{2}}=(\mathrm{Var}(\Phi_{K,t}))^{1/2}<B_{\epsilon}^{1/2}K^{5/2}t^{\delta-\epsilon^{\prime}}.

By Lemma
4.3
, we have

|\mathbb{E}(\Phi_{K,t})-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})|<B\frac{t^{\delta/2}\log t}{K}.

Since we fix

, we have that

Now set

.

.

4.1

\mathrm{Prob}\{\theta:|\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})|>\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t})\}<\frac{1}{\log K}.

Hence for almost all

\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}(\theta)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}(\Phi^{\ast}_{K,t}),

, implies that

with

F_{K}(\theta-\mathrm{Arg}((h^{n})^{\prime}(z))\not=0.

Thus the angle of

[\theta-\pi/K,\theta+\pi/K]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

)).

6.
Variance of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}
when
\mathcal{N}_{t}\ll K
.

In this section, we supplement our result by considering the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{K,t}
in the case when
\mathcal{N}_{t}\ll K
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}_{t}:=\#\{\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}:|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a hyperbolic rational map of degree at least
2
, and suppose
J(h)
is not contained in a circle in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\mathcal{N}_{t}\ll K
, then

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})\sim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}.

Proof.

By definition,

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2})-\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})^{2}.

By we calculate the expected value:

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathcal{N}_{K,t}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}.

Now we estimate
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2})
. Since

\mathcal{N}_{K,t}(\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta),

it follows that

\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2}(\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{w})|<t\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta).

Hence

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{w})|<t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta)).

If
\hat{z}=\hat{w}
, we have that

\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta))=\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)^{2})=\frac{1}{K}.

It follows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\hat{w}=\hat{z}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{K}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}.

If
\hat{z}\not=\hat{w}
, we have that

\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta)\not=0

for some
\theta
if and only if
|\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w})))|<2\pi/K
.
In this case we have

\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta))\leq\frac{1}{K}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{w})|<t\\
\hat{w}\not=\hat{z}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{w})|<t\\
\hat{w}\not=\hat{z}\\
|\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w})))|<2\pi/K\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\theta)\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{\pi}{K},\frac{\pi}{K}]}(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w}))-\theta))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{z}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{z})|<t\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{w}\in\mathcal{P}\\
|\lambda(\hat{w})|<t\\
\hat{w}\not=\hat{z}\\
|\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{z}))-\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda(\hat{w})))|<2\pi/K\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{K}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{t}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}\frac{1}{K},

where the last asymptotic estimate is from (
1.3
). Since
\mathcal{N}_{t}\ll K
, we have that

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2})\sim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}+\left(\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t}^{2})-\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{N}_{K,t})^{2}\sim\frac{\mathcal{N}_{t}}{K}.

∎

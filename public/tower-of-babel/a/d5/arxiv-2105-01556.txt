Abstract
. Suppose D D is a finite dimensional C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -algebra carrying a continous action Π ¯ \overline{\Pi} of the circle group 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . We study the quantum symmetry group of D D , taking Π ¯ \overline{\Pi} into account. We show that they are braided compact quantum groups 𝔾 \mathbb{G} over 𝕋 \mathbb{T} . Here, the R R -matrix, ℤ × ℤ ∋ ( m , n ) → ζ − m ⋅ n ∈ 𝕋 \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\ni(m,n)\to\zeta^{-m\cdot n}\in\mathbb{T} , for a fixed ζ ∈ 𝕋 \zeta\in\mathbb{T} , governs the braided structure. In particular, if Π ¯ \overline{\Pi} is trivial, ζ = 1 \zeta=1 or D D is commutative, then 𝔾 \mathbb{G} coincides with Wang’s quantum group of automorphisms of D D . Moreover, we show that the bosonisation of 𝔾 \mathbb{G} corresponds to the quantum symmetry group of the crossed product C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -algebra D ⋊ ℤ D\rtimes\mathbb{Z} , where the ℤ \mathbb{Z} -action is generated by Π ¯ ζ − 1 \overline{\Pi}_{\zeta{{}^{-1}}} .

1.
Introduction

In the realm of compact quantum groups of Woronowicz
[
23
,
24
]
, the inception of the theory of quantum symmetry groups of classical and quantum space goes back to the seminal paper of Wang
[
22
]
. In that article, Wang studied quantum symmetry groups of finite sets and finite dimensional noncommutative
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras or finite quantum spaces equipped with a reference state. They are extended to several discrete structures by Banica, Bichon and several others (
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
11
,
21
]
and the references therein). On the other hand, Goswami
[
9
]
introduced the continuous counterpart, namely, the quantum isometry group for spectral triples. He and his collaborators studied it extensively
[
5
,
1
,
8
,
10
]
. Goswami’s work motivated Banica and Skalski to develop a general framework of quantum symmetry groups based on orthogonal filtrations of unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras
[
2
]
.

Semidirect product construction of groups is a fundamental way of extending some homogeneous symmetries to inhomogeneous symmetries of a physical system. This perspective suggests that, within the framework of the noncommutative geometry, semidirect product construction for quantum groups might be a natural way to obtain inhomogeneous symmetries of quantum spaces or
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras from their homogeneous symmetries. Several interesting examples of unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras
D
naturally carry a continuous action
\gamma
of the circle group
\mathbb{T}
, that preserves a distinguished faithful state, say
\phi
, on
D
. Fix
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. We view the circle group
\mathbb{T}
a quasitriangular quantum group with respect to the
R
-matrix
R
, which is a bicharacter on
\mathbb{Z}
, defined by

\textup{R}(l,m)=\zeta^{-lm},\qquad\text{for all~$l,m\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

(1.1)

The quantum symmetries of compact type of
D
encoding and respecting the information of the action
\gamma
are the
\mathbb{T}
-
homogeneous compact quantum symmetries
of the system
(D,\gamma,\phi)
. If such an object exists, it becomes a
braided compact quantum group

\mathbb{G}
over
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
16
]
*Definition 6.1). The
\mathbb{T}
-action
\gamma
and the above
R
-matrix govern the braided structure of
\mathbb{G}
. Moreover, we expect to capture the inhomogeneous compact quantum symmetries of
(D,\gamma,\phi)
in terms of the bosonisation of
\mathbb{G}
(see
[
16
]
*Theorem 6.4).

This article aims to support the above model for finite dimensional
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras
D
carrying an action of
\mathbb{T}
. If
D
is commutative with
\textup{dim}(D)=n
for some natural number
n
, then it is generated by
n
many commuting projections
p_{1},\cdots p_{n}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1
. If
\gamma
is a continuous action of
\mathbb{T}
on
D
then
\gamma_{z}(p_{i})
is self adjoint and
\gamma_{z}(p_{i})=\gamma_{z}(p_{i})^{2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
and
1\leq i\leq n
. This forces
\gamma
to become trivial. Therefore, the quantum symmetry group of
D
corresponds to Wang’s quantum permutation group
[
22
]
*Theorem 3.1. So, we consider
D
to be noncommutative, that is,
D
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of matrix algebras.

In particular, let
D=\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for a fixed number
n
.
The matrix algebra
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the universal
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by

\{E_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
subject to the following conditions:

E_{ij}E_{kl}=\delta_{j,k}\cdot E_{il},\qquad E_{ij}^{*}=E_{ji},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{ii}=1.

(1.2)

Let
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
be integers.
Then it is easy to verify that
\Pi
, defined by

\Pi_{z}(E_{ij})\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}z^{d_{i}-d_{j}}E_{ij}\quad\text{for all $z\in\mathbb{T}$,}

(1.3)

is a continuous action of
\mathbb{T}
on
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
\phi
be the canonical normalised trace on
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, that is,
\phi(E_{ij})=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\delta_{i,j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. The action
\Pi
of
\mathbb{T}
on
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
preserves
\phi
, that is,
\phi\circ\Pi_{z}(\cdot)=\phi(\cdot)1
.

Consider the braided tensor product
\boxtimes_{\zeta}
of unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras
with
\mathbb{T}
-action discussed in Section
2.2
. The
braided compact quantum symmetries
of the triple
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
are
\phi
preserving actions
\eta\colon\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\boxtimes_{\zeta}B
of braided compact quantum groups
\mathbb{G}=(B,\beta,\Delta_{B})
over
\mathbb{T}
on
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi)
in the sense of the Definitions
3.1
and
3.16
, respectively. In fact,
\eta
is uniquely determined by its matrix coefficients
\{b^{ij}_{kl}\}_{1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n}\subseteq B
:

\eta(E_{ij})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}E_{kl}\boxtimes_{\zeta}b^{kl}_{ij},\qquad\text{for all~$1\leq i,j\leq n$.}

(1.4)

We construct a braided compact quantum group, denoted by
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
, in Theorem
3.4
. Subsequently, we observe in Proposition
3.18
that it acts faithfully on
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi)
and preserves
\phi
.

According to
[
16
]
*Section 6, every braided compact quantum group over
\mathbb{T}
uniquely corresponds to an ordinary compact quantum group. In the purely algebraic setting, this was discovered by Radford
[
18
]
and extensively studied by Majid
[
13
]
under the name
bosonisation
. In Section
4
, we construct the bosonisation of
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
, denoted by
\textup{Bos}(\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi))
.

In Proposition
5.1
, we observe that
\textup{Bos}(\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi))
acts on
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\boxtimes_{\zeta}\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
, extending the (braided) action of
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
on
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi)
and the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
by translation. Then we use it to prove the universal property of
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
in Theorem
5.4
.

The
R
-matrix (
1.1
) induces an action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
generated by
\Pi_{\zeta^{-1}}
. Then the associated crossed product
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
becomes isomorphic to
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})\boxtimes_{\zeta}\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Consider the orthogonal filtrations
\widetilde{\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
and
\widetilde{\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})}
of
\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
given in Example
5.15
and Example
5.16
, respectively. Then we apply
[
6
]
*Proposition 4.9 to construct an orthogonal filtration of
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})\boxtimes_{\zeta}\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Consequently, in Proposition
5.19
, we prove that the corresponding quantum symmetry group of
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})\boxtimes_{\zeta}\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to
\textup{Bos}(\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi))
. This is the universal property of the bosonisation of
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
. Furthermore, combining it with
[
6
]
*Theorem 5.1 we also observe
\textup{Bos}(\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi))\cong\mathfrak{D}_{\textup{V}}
in Corollary
5.20
, where
\mathfrak{D}_{\textup{V}}
is the Drinfeld’s double of the quantum symmetry groups
\textup{QISO}(\widetilde{\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})})
and
\textup{QISO}(\widetilde{\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})})
with respect to some bicharacter
V
suitably chosen.

Within the scope of this article, the bosonisation picture has significant advantages over the Drinfeld’s double, both computationally and conceptually. Indeed, the concrete computation of the bosonisation of a braided compact quantum group over
\mathbb{T}
is more straightforward than the computation of Drinfeld’s double. Secondly, in Remark
5.21
, we observe that
\Aut(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi,\phi)
is the homogeneous quantum symmetry group of the
\textup{C}^{*}
-dynamical system
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z},\Pi_{\zeta^{-1}})
obtained from
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\Pi)
. At the same time, its bosonisation (semidirect product) corresponds to the inhomogeneous (extending
\mathbb{T}
-homogeneous) quantum symmetry group of the same system. Conceptually, this approach is more natural while computing the quantum symmetry of the given the
\textup{C}^{*}
-dynamical system
(\textup{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z},\Pi_{\zeta^{-1}})
.

Finally, in Section
6
, we employ these analyses to study similar results for the direct sum of matrix algebras. As a byproduct, we observe in Remark
6.10
that a finite classical space, that is,
D\cong\mathbb{C}^{m}
for any natural number
m
, has no braided quantum symmetries (over
\mathbb{T}
), as expected.

2.
Preliminaries

All Hilbert spaces and
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras (which are not explicitly multiplier algebras) are assumed to be separable. For any subsets
X,Y
of a given unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
D
, the norm closure of
\{xy\mid x\in X,y\in Y\}\subseteq D
is denoted by
XY
. We use
\otimes
for both the tensor product of Hilbert spaces and the minimal tensor product of
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras, which is well understood from the context.

2.1.
Compact quantum groups and their actions

A
compact quantum group
is a pair
\mathbb{G}=(A,\Delta_{A})
consisting of a unital

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
and a unital
∗
-homomorphism
\Delta_{A}\colon A\to A\otimes A
such that

(1)

\Delta_{A}
is coassociative:
(\textup{id}_{A}\otimes\Delta_{A})\circ\Delta_{A}=(\Delta_{A}\otimes\textup{id}_{A})\circ\Delta_{A}
;

(2)

\Delta_{A}
satisfies the cancellation conditions:
\Delta_{A}(A)(1_{A}\otimes A)=A\otimes A=\Delta_{A}(A)(A\otimes 1_{A})
.

Let
D
be a unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra. An action of a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}=(A,\Delta_{A})
on
D
is a unital
∗
-homomorphism
\gamma\colon D\to D\otimes A
such that

(1)

\gamma
satisfies the action equation, that is,
(\textup{id}_{D}\otimes\Delta_{A})\circ\gamma=(\gamma\otimes\textup{id}_{A})\circ\gamma
;

(2)

\gamma
satisfies the
Podleś condition
:
\gamma(D)(1_{D}\otimes A)=D\otimes A
.

Suppose
\tau\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
be a state. Then
\gamma
is said to be
\tau
-preserving
if
(\tau\otimes\textup{id}_{A})\gamma(d)=\tau(d)\cdot 1_{A}
for all
d\in D
. In fact,
\gamma
is injective whenever
\tau
is faithful.

Example 2.1
.

Represent
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
faithfully by pointwise multiplication. Let
z\in\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
be the unitary operator of pointwise multiplication with the identity function on
\mathbb{T}
. Then
\Delta_{\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})}(z)=z\otimes z
defines the comultiplication map
on
\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})
. The pair
(\textup{C}(\mathbb{T}),\Delta_{\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})})
is
\mathbb{T}
viewed as a compact
quantum group. Suppose
D
is a unital
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\gamma\colon\mathbb{T}\to\Aut(D)
is a continuous action. An element
d\in D
is a homogeneous element of degree
\textup{deg}(d)\in\mathbb{Z}
if
\gamma_{z}(d)=z^{\textup{deg}(d)}\cdot d
for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
. Equivalently,

\gamma(d)=d\otimes z^{\textup{deg}(d)}
while
\gamma
is viewed as
an action of
(\textup{C}(\mathbb{T}),\Delta_{\textup{C}(\mathbb{T})})
on
D
.

3.

Fix

Definition 3.1

(compare with
[
16

*Definition 6.1)
.

A triple

in

(3.2)

(2)

\Delta_{A}(A)(1_{A}\boxtimes_{\zeta}A)=A\boxtimes_{\zeta}A=\Delta_{A}(A)(A\boxtimes_{\zeta}1_{A}).

\mathbb{T}
. We refer to

12

17

for genuine examples of braided compact quantum groups over
\mathbb{T}
. Indeed,
\mathbb{G}
is an ordinary compact quantum group if either

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,m}\cdot(\zeta^{d_{j}(d_{k}-d_{i}+d_{j}-d_{l}}u^{ij}_{kl}),\quad\text{for all~$1\leq i,j,k,l,m,n\leq n${;}}

Sobolev spaces with mixed weights and the Poisson equation on angular domains
Petru A. Cioica-Licht, Cornelia Schneider, Markus Weimar

Abstract
We introduce and analyse a class of weighted Sobolev spaces with mixed weights on angular domains. The weights are based on both the distance to the boundary and the distance to the one vertex of the domain. Moreover, we show how the regularity of the Poisson equation can be analysed in the framework of these spaces by means of the Mellin transform, provided the integrability parameter equals two. Our main motivation comes from the study of stochastic partial differential equations and associated degenerate deterministic parabolic equations. Keywords: weighted Sobolev space, Poisson equation, angular domain, stochastic partial differential equation, degenerate equation, Mellin transform, Dirichlet Laplacian 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 46E35; Secondary: 35J15, 35J70, 46N20, 60H15.

Introduction

In this paper we present a thorough analysis of a class of weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
involving mixed weights on angular domains

\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}:=\big\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;\vrule\;x=(r\cos(\phi),r\sin(\phi)),\,0<r<\infty,\,0<\phi<\kappa\big\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1)

with arbitrary angle
0<\kappa<2\pi
. Recently, these spaces have been used for the regularity analysis of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) and related degenerate partial differential equations (PDEs) on angles and polygons
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
29
,
31
]
.
Moreover, we initiate the analysis of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathcal{D}
in this scale of Sobolev spaces by proving existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Poisson equation

\Delta u=f\,\text{ on }\,\mathcal{D},\hskip 20.00003ptu=0\,\text{ on }\,\partial\mathcal{D},

(2)

within the aforementioned spaces.
To keep the manuscript at a reasonable length, we restrict the analysis of the Poisson equation to the case where the integrability parameter
p
equals two and postpone the general case to a forthcoming paper.
Both, the study of the spaces and the analysis of the Dirichlet Laplacian in these spaces, are important steps that are needed for generalizations of
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
29
,
31
]
towards a refined
L_{p}
-theory for SPDEs on non-smooth domains.

The context that motivates our analysis can be roughly summarized as follows: Sobolev spaces provide a natural framework for the regularity analysis of PDEs.
The usual unweighted Sobolev spaces work particularly well for deterministic, non-degenerate
PDEs on smooth domains
[
1
,
13
,
40
]
.
However, they are not very well suited for the analysis of equations that do not satisfy these ‘classical’ assumptions.
In that case, weighted Sobolev spaces turn out to be a viable alternative,
in particular, in the following situations:

•

PDEs on non-smooth domains.
Singularities at the boundary of the underlying domain, i.e., corners, edges, cusps, and any other points where the boundary is not sufficiently smooth, are known to lead to singularities of solutions to PDEs.
This results in a breakdown of the (unweighted) Sobolev regularity of higher order, see e.g.
[
8
,
10
,
15
,
22
]
; see also
[
46
]
for the same effect for SPDEs.
However, the singularities of the solution can often be described accurately by means of Sobolev spaces with weights that involve the distance to the set
of boundary singularities.
This idea goes back to Kondratiev
[
32
,
33
]
, followed by an abundant number of related papers and monographs.
In this context we only mention
[
12
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
35
,
49
,
52
,
53
]
and the references therein. This list is by no means complete.

•

PDEs that degenerate at the boundary.
If the underlying domain is sufficiently smooth (usually, at least
C^{1}
is required) but the equation is degenerate at the boundary in the sense that, for instance, the coefficients are not uniformly elliptic towards the boundary or the forcing terms have blow-ups at the boundary, then weighted Sobolev spaces based on the distance to the entire boundary have proven useful. Again, there is a long list of publications on this topic, of which we mention just a few
[
19
,
28
,
39
,
43
,

,

,

,

]
.

•

Stochastic PDEs.

In
[
14
,
37
]
it has been shown that, even if the underlying domain and the coefficients are smooth, solutions to
stochastic
PDEs may fail to have higher order unweighted Sobolev regularity.
This is due to the roughness of the noise and a resulting incompatibility between noise and boundary conditions, which leads to blow-ups of the higher order derivatives of the solution along the boundary.
However, in a series of papers
[
23
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
37
,
41
,
42
]
initiated by N.V. Krylov it has been demonstrated that, as long as the underlying domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of class
C^{1}
, second order SPDEs with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions can be analysed very accurately by means of certain weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})
; see
Section 2.3
for a definition and the basic properties of these spaces.

Thus,
by means of appropriate weighted Sobolev spaces,
a fairly comprehensive
L_{p}
-theory can be established for non-degenerate PDEs on non-smooth domains on the one hand and for degenerate PDEs as well as for SPDEs on smooth domains on the other hand.
However, up to now very little is known about the regularity of
degenerate
PDEs and of
stochastic
PDEs on
non-smooth
domains.
The main challenge in closing this long persisting gap is to find suitable function spaces that capture both the singular behaviour along the boundary (caused by the noise and/or due to the degeneracy of the equation) and the singularities of the solution caused by the singularities of the boundary.

In
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
a research program that aims at narrowing this gap for SPDEs and related degenerate parabolic PDEs has been initiated.
Therein, the focus lies on the stochastic heat equation with zero Dirichlet boundary condition on the angular domains
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
introduced above
as well as on polygonal domains.
As has been shown in
[
5
,
6
]
, see also
[
29
,
31
]
, in this setting the different types of singularities described above and their interplay can be captured accurately by means of certain weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1<p<\infty
, and
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, which consist of (equivalence classes of) locally integrable scalar-valued functions

on
\mathcal{D}
such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\colon\left|\alpha\right|\leq\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left|\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\,D^{\alpha}u\right|^{p}\varrho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}}{\varrho_{\circ}}\Big)^{\Theta-2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

(3)

where
\varrho_{\circ}:=\mathrm{dist}\!\left(\cdot,\{0\}\right)
and
\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}:=\mathrm{dist}\!\left(\cdot,\partial\mathcal{D}\right)
are the distances to the corner and to the boundary of
\mathcal{D}
, respectively.
In these spaces, existence, uniqueness, and higher order regularity for the stochastic heat equation on
\mathcal{D}
can be established for sharp ranges of weight parameters
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
; cf.
[
5
,
29
,
30
]
. Due to the nature of the problem, this is neither possible in unweighted Sobolev spaces nor in the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
mentioned above (except for a very restricted range of weight parameters
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
, see
[
26
]
).
However, so far, the analysis is limited to non-negative integer smoothness parameters
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Moreover, an analysis of the space-time regularity, including uncoupling of the integrability parameters in time and space as well as sharp initial conditions, has yet to be done.
Among other things, these extensions require a detailed analysis of the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
and the behaviour of the Dirichlet Laplacian as well as related (degenerate) PDEs within these spaces. These investigations are the subject of this paper.

2.1
The angular domains

Let

The boundary of

Moreover, we write

We let

\mathcal{I}:=\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}:=(0,\kappa)

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa}:=(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{I}_{\kappa}

\mathcal{D}=\Phi(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})

The following simple relationship between
\varrho_{\circ}
and the distance
\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}
to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}

Lemma 2.1
.

For

with
(r,\phi)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
we have
\varrho_{\circ}(x)=\left|x\right|=r
and
\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=r\,\sin(\mu(\phi))

\mu(\phi):=\min\!\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},\,\phi,\,\kappa-\phi\right\},\hskip 10.00002pt\phi\in\mathcal{I}.

Moreover,

\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}\sim\mu\sim\sin(\mu)\sim\psi_{\mathcal{I}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathcal{I},

where

\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(\phi):=\sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\phi\Big),\hskip 10.00002pt\phi\in\mathcal{I}.

The equality

Moreover,
\mu\sim\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}
on
\mathcal{I}
since for all

it holds that

\displaystyle=\min\{\pi,\phi,\kappa-\phi\}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\pi,2\phi,2(\kappa-\phi)\}=2\,\mu(\phi).

The basic inequality

\frac{2}{\pi}\alpha\leq\sin(\alpha)\leq\alpha,\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha\in[0,\pi/2],

yields
\sin(\mu)\sim\mu
on
\mathcal{I}
. Using symmetry, this also gives
\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}\sim\psi_{\mathcal{I}}
on
\mathcal{I}
.

2.2
Smooth resolutions of unity

Definition 2.4

(5)

Remark 2.5

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

(i)

(iii)

(7)

isomorphically.

(v)

Proposition 2.7
.

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta+s+\left|\alpha\right|}\,D^{\alpha}u\;\vrule\;\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{O})\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{O}}\left|\psi_{\mathcal{O}}(x)^{s}\,\psi_{\mathcal{O}}(x)^{\Theta+\left|\alpha\right|}\,(D^{\alpha}u)(x)\right|+\Big[\psi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta+s+\left|\alpha\right|}\,D^{\alpha}u\Big]_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{s}\left\|\psi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta+\left|\alpha\right|}\,D^{\alpha}u\;\vrule\;C(\mathcal{O})\right\|+\Big[\psi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta+s+\left|\alpha\right|}\,D^{\alpha}u\Big]_{\mathcal{C}^{s}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\lesssim R_{1}^{s}\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta p}(\mathcal{O})\right\|

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
, since
\psi_{\mathcal{O}}\sim\varrho_{\mathcal{O}}\leq R\leq\max\{1,\,R\}=:R_{1}
on
S\supset\supp(D^{\alpha}u)
.
∎

For
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{D}
we also have the following assertions, which we will frequently use below.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
1<p<\infty
as well as
\gamma,\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

\displaystyle\left\|u(s\,\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim s^{-\Theta/p}\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|
,
s>0
,
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
.

(ii)

Assume that
\zeta:=(\zeta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathscr{A}_{c}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
for some
c>1
.
Then

\left\|\zeta_{\nu}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},

(10)

and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
it holds that

\left\|\partial^{\alpha}\big(\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)\big)u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z}.

(11)

We first prove (i). Due to
Section 2.3
(iv)
and
(v)
, it is enough to verify the assertion for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. However, in this case the claim follows from the fact that
\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(sx)=s\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)
,
x\in\mathcal{D}
, simply by using
Section 2.3
(iii)
together with the chain rule and Jacobi’s transformation theorem.

To verify (ii), we first prove (
11
).
In view of
Section 2.3
(vi)
it suffices to show that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\partial^{\alpha}\big(\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)\big)\right|_{n}^{(0)}=\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\left|\beta\right|\leq n}\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\beta\right|}(x)\big\lvert\partial^{\beta+\alpha}[\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)](x)\big\rvert<\infty.

(12)

To see this, note that
\zeta\in\mathscr{A}_{c}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
implies

\big\lvert\partial^{\beta+\alpha}[\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)](x)\big\rvert=\big\lvert c^{\nu\left|\alpha+\beta\right|}(\partial^{\beta+\alpha}\zeta_{\nu})(c^{\nu}x)\big\rvert\leq C_{\alpha+\beta},\hskip 20.00003ptx\in\mathcal{D},

and
\supp\!\big(\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)\big)\subset\mathcal{D}_{c,k_{0}}^{[0]}(\{0\})
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
while

\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}\lesssim 1
on
\mathcal{D}_{c,k_{0}}^{[0]}(\{0\})
.

Estimate (
10
) follows now from (
11
) with
\alpha=0
and part
(i)
, which together yield

\displaystyle\left\|\zeta_{\nu}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim c^{\nu\Theta/p}\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim c^{\nu\Theta/p}\left\|u(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with constants that do not depend on
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
.
∎

3
The weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})

After the preparations in the previous section, we now introduce the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
and prove several properties of the resulting family of spaces.
Throughout this section we fix some arbitrary
0<\kappa<2\pi
and write
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa}
, see (
1
).

3.1
Definition and basic properties

We start with the definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\zeta=(\zeta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathscr{A}_{c}^{\textup{[L]}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
for some
c>1
(see
Definition 2.2
).
Let
1<p<\infty
as well as
\gamma,\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\displaystyle H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}):=H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta}:=\left\{u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D}):\ \left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}<\infty\right\},

where

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}:=\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\nu\theta}\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}\,u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(13)

For any choice
\zeta
,
p
,
\gamma
,
\Theta
,
\theta
, and
c
as in
Definition 3.1
,
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta}
is a vector space and
\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}
defines a norm on it
(to check the definiteness note that
\zeta
satisfies
[L]
with
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{D}
and that
\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{\nu}
is locally finite).
However, at first sight,
\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}
and therefore
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta}
seem to depend on the concrete choice of
\zeta
and
c
.
The following lemma shows that this is not the case: Replacing
\zeta
in the definition above by any
\xi\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{L}]}_{c_{1}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
for some
c_{1}>1

will lead to the same spaces with equivalent norms.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\zeta
,
p
,
\gamma
,
\Theta
,
\theta
, and
c
be as in
Definition 3.1
.
Moreover let

\xi:=(\xi_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathscr{A}_{c_{1}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
for some
c_{1}>1
.
Then

\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}^{\nu\theta}\left\|(\xi_{\nu}\,u)(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}^{p},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta}.

(14)

In particular,
if

\xi\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{L}]}_{c_{1}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
,
then

H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta}=H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c_{1},\xi}
with equivalent norms.

We first prove (
14
) under the additional assumptions that
\zeta\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{R}]}_{c}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
.
Then, by
definition,
\zeta\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{R}]}_{c,k_{0}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
and
\xi\in\mathscr{A}_{c_{1},k_{1}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
for some
k_{0},k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let

\widetilde{\zeta}_{\nu}:=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\zeta_{\alpha(\nu)+j},\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},

with
\alpha(\nu)=\lfloor\nu/\log_{c_{1}}(c)\rfloor
and
N=N(c_{1},c,k_{1},k_{0})
as in
Section 2.2
(i)
.
Then, for all
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\widetilde{\zeta}_{\nu}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{D}^{[\nu]}_{c_{1},k_{1}}(\{0\})\supset\supp(\xi_{\nu})
.
Therefore,
by
repeatedly applying
Section 2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|(\xi_{\nu}u)(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\xi_{\nu}(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\,(\widetilde{\zeta}_{\nu}u)(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(\widetilde{\zeta}_{\nu}u)(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c}\Big)^{-\nu\Theta/p}\left\|(\widetilde{\zeta}_{\nu}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{c}\Big)^{-\nu\Theta/p}\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left\|(\zeta_{\alpha(\nu)+j}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim c_{1}^{-\nu\Theta/p}\sum_{j=-N}^{N}c^{(\alpha(\nu)+j)\Theta/p}\left\|(\zeta_{\alpha(\nu)+j}u)(c^{\alpha(\nu)+j}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with constants that do not depend on
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{1}^{\nu\Theta}\left\|(\xi_{\nu}u)(c_{1}^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{-(\alpha(\nu)+j)\Theta}\left\|(\zeta_{\alpha(\nu)+j}u)(c^{\alpha(\nu)+j}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta}^{p},

where we used that
N
does not depend on
\nu
and that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
the cardinality
\#\big\{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}:\ \alpha(\nu)=m\big\}\leq\lfloor\log_{c_{1}}(c)\rfloor+1
.

In order to obtain (
14
) for arbitrary
\zeta\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{L}]}_{c,k_{0}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
it now suffices to prove that

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta^{*}}\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{c,\zeta},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})_{c,\zeta},

(15)

with
\zeta^{*}:=(\zeta_{\nu}/\eta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
where
\eta_{\nu}:=\sum_{j=-2k_{0}+1}^{2k_{0}-1}\zeta_{\nu+j}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As mentioned in
Section 2.2
(ii)
, it holds that
\zeta^{*}\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{R}]}_{c,k_{0}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
and
\zeta^{**}:=(\zeta_{\nu}^{*}/\eta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathscr{A}^{[\textup{L}]}_{c,k_{0}}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
.
Thus, since

\displaystyle\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}^{*}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})\right\|=\left\|\zeta_{\nu}^{**}(c^{\nu}\cdot)\,(\eta_{\nu}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},

very similar arguments as above with
\eta
instead of
\widetilde{\zeta}
yield (
15
).
∎

Section 3.1
in mind, we make the following convention.

Convention 3.3
.

For the remainder of this text, we fix some arbitrary
\zeta=(\zeta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathscr{A}^{\textup{[R]}}_{e,1}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
as constructed in
Section 2.2
(iv)
(note that
\zeta
then satisfies (
4
) with
c=e
).
If not explicitly stated otherwise, we set
\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|:=\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|_{e,\zeta}
.
By
Section 3.1
all statements and proofs below hold mutatis mutandis with
e
replaced by any
c>1
and with any other
\zeta\in\mathscr{A}^{\textup{[L]}}_{c}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
.

For
1<p<\infty
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and a normed space
X
, we define the vector-valued sequence space

\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};X):=\big\{x=(x_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}:\ x_{\nu}\in X\text{ for all }\nu\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }\left\|x\;\vrule\;\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};X)\right\|<\infty\big\},

where

\left\|x\;\vrule\;\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};X)\right\|:=\bigg(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\nu\theta}\left\|x_{\nu}\;\vrule\;X\right\|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

We write
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X):=\ell_{p}^{0}(\mathbb{Z};X)
and
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}):=\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z};\mathbb{R})
.
Recall that if
X
is a (reflexive) Banach space, then also
\big(\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};X),\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};X)\right\|\big)
is a (reflexive) Banach space, see
[
2
, Theorem VI.2.1.1]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma,\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\big(H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}),\left\|\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\big)
is a reflexive Banach space.

By
Section 2.3
(i)
,
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})
is a reflexive Banach space, thus so is
\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))
.
Take
\zeta
as in
Section 3.1
and let
(\eta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
as well as
S:=S_{e}
and
R:=R_{e}
be as in
Section 2.2
(v)
.
As mentioned therein, our assertion follows once we can prove that

S\in\mathcal{L}\big(H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}),\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptR\in\mathcal{L}\big(\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})),H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\big).

The former is obvious, where
S
is even isometric.
To see the latter, note that
by
Section 2.2
(i)
and
(iv)
, for all
f=(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))
, it holds that

\zeta_{\nu}Rf=\zeta_{\nu}\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{\mu}f_{\mu}(e^{-\mu}\cdot)=\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\zeta_{\nu}\eta_{\nu+j}f_{\nu+j}(e^{-(\nu+j)}\cdot),\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, arguing along the lines of the proof of
Section 3.1
, by applying
Section 2.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\big(\zeta_{\nu}Rf\big)(e^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\left\|f_{\nu+j}\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})),

so that

\displaystyle\left\|Rf\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{(\nu+j)\theta}\left\|f_{\nu+j}\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\sim\left\|f\;\vrule\;\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))\right\|^{p},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002ptf=(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})).

Thus,
R\in\mathcal{L}\big(\ell_{p}^{\theta}(\mathbb{Z};H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})),H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\big)
.
∎

The following embedding result includes the monotonicity of the scale
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
w.r.t. the smoothness parameter
\gamma
as well as a Sobolev-type embedding as special cases.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
1<p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty
, as well as
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\gamma_{0}\geq\gamma_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}-\frac{d}{p_{0}}\geq\gamma_{1}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}.

Then
H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0},\Theta p_{0},\theta p_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\hookrightarrow H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1},\Theta p_{1},\theta p_{1}}(\mathcal{D})
.

Since
\ell_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\ell_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
, the assertion is an immediate consequence of
Definition 3.1
and
Section 2.3
(viii)
.
∎

We conclude the current subsection with elementary monotonicity assertions related to the weight parameters
\Theta
and
\theta
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
as well as
\gamma,\Theta_{0},\Theta_{1},\theta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\Theta_{0}\leq\Theta_{1}
. Then

H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta_{0},\theta}(\mathcal{D})\hookrightarrow H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta_{1},\theta}(\mathcal{D})
.

Since
(\zeta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies (
4
),
we have that

\supp(\zeta_{\nu}u)(c^{\nu}\cdot)\subset\mathcal{D}^{[0]}_{e,1}(\{0\})
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, similar to
Section 2.3
(vii)
, we obtain

\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}u)(e^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}u)(e^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta_{0},\theta}(\mathcal{D}),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},

and the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
as well as
\gamma,\Theta,\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\theta_{0}\leq\theta_{1}
. Moreover, let
0<r<R<\infty
.

(i)

If
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})
satisfies
\supp(u)\subset\overline{B_{R}(0)}
, then
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})
and

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim R^{(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})/p}\,\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with constants independent of
u
and
R
.

(ii)

If
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})
satisfies
\supp(u)\subset\overline{\mathcal{D}}\setminus B_{r}(0)
, then
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})
and

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim r^{(\theta_{0}-\theta_{1})/p}\,\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with constants independent of
u
and
r
.

In particular, if
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
with
\supp(u)\subset(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)})\setminus B_{r}(0)
, then
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
if, and only if,
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
. In this case,
\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|
.

To show (i) we choose
K\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
e^{K-1}\leq R<e^{K}
.
Then, since
\supp(u)\subset\overline{B_{R}(0)}
, we have
\zeta_{\nu}u\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{D}
for all
\nu\geq K+1
.
Thus, since
\theta_{0}\leq\theta_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{1}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\leq K}e^{\nu\theta_{1}}\,\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}u)(e^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{K(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})}\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})}\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta_{0}}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}.

Part (ii) follows with very similar arguments.
Moreover, if
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
is such that
\supp(u)\subset(\overline{\mathcal{D}}\cap\overline{B_{R}(0)})\setminus B_{r}(0)
, then there exists some
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\zeta_{\nu}u\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{D}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\left|\nu\right|>K
.
Thus, by
Definition 3.1
and
Section 2.3
(i)
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\nu\theta}\left\|(\zeta_{\nu}u)(e^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\sim\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\nu(\theta-\Theta)}\left\|\zeta_{\nu}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\sim\sum_{\left|\nu\right|\leq K}\left\|\zeta_{\nu}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|.

Now
Section 2.3
(ii)
yields
\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|
provided
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
.
And, if
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
, then the converse estimate is implied by the triangle inequality in
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
, as
\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{\nu}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{D}
.
This proves the final assertion.
∎

3.2
Density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
, duality, and interpolation

For many properties of the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
that we address below, in particular, for the duality and interpolation statements, we shall use the density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{D})
in
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
which we prove now.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma,\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
is dense in
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
h_{k}:=\sum_{\left|\nu\right|\leq k}\zeta_{\nu}
. Then
\supp(h_{k})\subset\mathcal{D}^{[0]}_{e,k+1}(\{0\})
and
h_{k}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{D}^{[0]}_{e,k}(\{0\})
for all

3.3
Relation to weighted Sobolev spaces

The next theorem shows that for

the spaces

If

Take
\zeta
from

and

\displaystyle\left|\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)^{\left|\alpha\right|}\,(D^{\beta}\zeta_{\nu})(x)\right|\lesssim\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)^{\left|\alpha\right|-\left|\beta\right|}\,\left(\frac{\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)}{e^{\nu}}\right)^{\left|\beta\right|}\lesssim\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)^{\left|\alpha-\beta\right|},\hskip 20.00003ptx\in\mathcal{D}_{\nu},\hskip 10.00002pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z}.

for all

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}

Section 2.2
(i)

∎

As a consequence we obtain the following relationship between the spaces

(iv)

(v)

3.4
Pointwise multiplication

Using
Section 3.3
we can transfer some of the properties of the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
mutatis mutandis to
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
.
Especially, we obtain the following assertions on pointwise multipliers and weight index shifts.
Recall that for domains
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and closed sets
M\subset\partial\mathcal{O}
, we write
\textup{RD}(\mathcal{O},M)
to denote the set of regularized distances to
M
on
\mathcal{O}
, see
Section 2.2
(iii)
.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma,\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

If
a\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is a pointwise multiplier for
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
, then it is a pointwise multiplier for
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
, too.
In particular, if

\left|a\right|^{(0)}_{n}:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{O}}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq n}\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}(x)\left|D^{\alpha}a(x)\right|<\infty
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then for all
\left|\gamma\right|\leq n
we have

\displaystyle\left\|a\,u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\leq C(d,p,n)\left|a\right|^{(0)}_{n}\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}).

(ii)

Let
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}\in\textup{RD}(\mathcal{D},\partial\mathcal{D})
and
\psi_{\circ}\in\textup{RD}(\mathcal{D},\{0\})
.
Further, let
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
.
Then
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{s}\,\psi_{\circ}^{t}\,u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta p,\theta p}(\mathcal{D})
if, and only if,
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,(\Theta+s)p,(\theta+s+t)p}(\mathcal{D})
.
In this case,

\left\|\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{s}\,\psi_{\circ}^{t}\,u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta p,\theta p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,(\Theta+s)p,(\theta+s+t)p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,(\Theta+s)p,(\theta+s+t)p}(\mathcal{D}).

Both assertions immediately follow from
Section 3.3
together with
Section 2.3
(vi)
(for Assertion
(i)
) and
Section 2.3
(ix)
(for Assertion
(ii)
), respectively.
∎

The following statement complements
Section 3.4
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let

and
\gamma,\Theta_{0},\Theta_{1},\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Further assume that

\displaystyle m:=\left\lceil\left|\gamma\right|\right\rceil\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{1}{q}:=\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}.

Then

\displaystyle\left\|M_{a}\;\vrule\;\mathcal{L}\big(H^{\gamma}_{p_{0},\Theta_{0}p_{0},\theta_{0}p_{0}}(\mathcal{D}),H^{\gamma}_{p_{1},\Theta_{1}p_{1},\theta_{1}p_{1}}(\mathcal{D})\big)\right\|\lesssim\left\|a\;\vrule\;H^{m}_{q,(\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{0})q,(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})q}(\mathcal{D})\right\|.

Remark 3.18

Together with

\displaystyle\left\|a\;\vrule\;H^{m}_{q,(\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{0})q,(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})q}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left|\beta\right|\leq m}\left\|\varrho_{\circ}^{\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}-(\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{0})}\,\varrho_{\mathcal{D}}^{\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{0}-\frac{2}{q}+\left|\beta\right|}\,D^{\beta}a\;\vrule\;L_{q}(\mathcal{D})\right\|.

If we formally put

corresponds to the limiting case

Step 1.

Let

and

Step 2.

Let

3.5
Lifting

The following lifting result is quite useful in many calculations, in particular, in the context of PDEs. It generalizes the well-known lifting properties of derivatives along scales of classical Sobolev and Bessel potential spaces. In particular, it generalizes
Section 2.3
(x)
for
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{D}
.

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\gamma,\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
as well as
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, let
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}\in\textup{RD}(\mathcal{D},\partial\mathcal{D})
, see
Section 2.2
(iii)
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent for
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
.

(a)

u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
.

(b)

D^{\alpha}u\in H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta+\left|\alpha\right|p,\theta+\left|\alpha\right|p}(\mathcal{D})
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
.

(c)

\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}D^{\alpha}u\in H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
.

(d)

D^{\alpha}\big(\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}u\big)\in H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
.

In this case,

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|D^{\alpha}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta+\left|\alpha\right|p,\theta+\left|\alpha\right|p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}D^{\alpha}u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|D^{\alpha}\big(\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}u\big)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with constants independent of
u
.

Remark 3.20
.

Some comments are in order.

(i)

The implication “
(a)

\Longrightarrow

(b)
” in

(ii)

For

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim\bigg(\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|D^{\alpha}\big(\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}u\big)\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{m}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}).

Note that “
(b)

\Longleftrightarrow

(c)
” and the corresponding norm equivalence hold for all

by
Section 3.4
(ii)
. To prove the other equivalences we argue as follows.

Step 1.

We first prove “
(a)

\Longleftrightarrow

(c)
” and the corresponding norm equivalence by induction on

. We start with the base case

\zeta u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})

Moreover,

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\sim\left\|\zeta u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}).

(26)

By
Section 2.3
(x)
,
we have

\zeta u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
if, and only if,
\zeta u\in H^{\gamma-1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
and
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}D(\zeta u)\in H^{\gamma-1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\zeta u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\zeta u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|+\left\|\psi_{\mathcal{D}}D(\zeta u)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D}).

Now integration
by parts shows that

\psi_{\mathcal{D}}D(\zeta u)=\zeta\psi_{\mathcal{D}}Du+\psi_{\mathcal{D}}D(\zeta)u=\zeta\psi_{\mathcal{D}}Du+\psi_{\mathcal{D}}\zeta^{-1}D(\zeta)\zeta u.

Thus, the base case follows by means of
Section 3.3
(i)
from the fact that

\left|\psi_{\mathcal{D}}\zeta^{-1}D(\zeta)\right|_{n}^{(0)}<\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}

which may be verified by using (

We move on to the induction step. Our induction hypothesis is that “
(a)

\Longleftrightarrow

(c)
” holds with
m=k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This hypothesis and the base case show that
u\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
if, and only if,
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}D^{\alpha}u\in H^{\gamma-(k+1)}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
and
\psi_{\mathcal{D}}^{\left|\alpha\right|}D^{\alpha}(\psi_{\mathcal{D}}Du)\in H^{\gamma-(k+1)}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for all

with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
(and appropriate norm equivalences hold).
Thus, since by Leibniz’ rule,

Step 2.

Finally,

D^{\alpha}_{x}u\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\gamma
and the constants that do not depend on

.
∎

3.9
Characterization via Mellin transform

Finally, we present a characterization of the spaces
P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})
by means of Mellin transforms, which, due to
Theorem 3.27
, will also provide us with a new characterization of
H^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
. It will play a central role in our existence and uniqueness statement in
Section 4
. Recall that for any domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\real^{d}
, we write
L_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})=L_{p}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{O}),\varrho_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta-d}\lambda^{d};\mathbb{C})
, see
Definition 2.4
and
Section 2.3
.

For test functions
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\real_{+})
, the Mellin transform is defined by

(\mathcal{M}u)(\lambda):=(\mathcal{M}_{r\rightarrow\lambda}u)(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\lambda-1}u(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(31)

In the following lemma we list some basic properties of this transform, cf.
[
49
, Lemma 3.3.6]
, see also
[
4
]
. For
c\in\real
we write
\Gamma_{c}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,\colon\mathrm{Re}(z)=c\big\}
and

Y^{2}_{c}:=\big\{u\colon\Gamma_{c}\to\mathbb{C}\,\;\vrule\;\ \big(t\mapsto u(c+it)\big)\in L_{2}(\real)\big\},

endowed with the norm

\left\|u\;\vrule\;Y^{2}_{c}\right\|:=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\left|u(\lambda)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\bigg)^{1/2},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in Y^{2}_{c}.

Moreover, for
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
u\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
\alpha\in\real
we write
z^{\alpha}u
for the multiplication of
u
by
z\mapsto z^{\alpha}
, i.e.,

(z^{\alpha}u)(z):=z^{\alpha}u(z),\hskip 20.00003ptz\in\mathcal{O}.

Lemma 3.28

(Properties of the Mellin transform)
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

The transformation (
31
) realizes a linear
mapping from
C^{\infty}_{0}(\real_{+})
into the space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

(\mathcal{M}_{r\to\lambda}(r\partial_{r}u))(\lambda)=\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{r\to\lambda}u)(\lambda)
for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\real_{+})
.

(iii)

The (left) inverse Mellin transform is given by

u(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\beta-i\infty}^{-\beta+i\infty}r^{\lambda}\mathcal{M}{u}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\hskip 20.00003ptr>0,

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary.

(iv)

For
\beta\in\real
the transform (
31
) extends to an invertible linear isometry

\mathcal{M}_{\beta}\colon L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})=L_{2}(\real_{+},r^{2\beta-1}\mathrm{d}r)\rightarrow Y^{2}_{-\beta}.

Moreover, Parseval’s identity holds, i.e.,

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\beta-1}u(r)\overline{v(r)}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\beta-i\infty}^{-\beta+i\infty}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}{u}(\lambda)\overline{\mathcal{M}_{\beta}{v}(\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+}).

(v)

If for
\beta<\beta^{\prime}
we have
u\in L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\cap L_{2,2\beta^{\prime}}(\real_{+})
, then
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Re}\lambda}{u}(\lambda)
is holomorphic in the strip
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ -\beta^{\prime}<\mathrm{Re}\lambda<-\beta\}
.

We will need the following generalization of
Section 3.9
(iv)
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
c\in\real
we write

Y^{2,k}_{c}:=\big\{u\in Y^{2}_{c}\,\colon\,\left\|u\;\vrule\;Y^{2,k}_{c}\right\|<\infty\big\},

where

\left\|u\;\vrule\;Y^{2,k}_{c}\right\|:=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left\|\big(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{j}u(\lambda)\big)\;\vrule\;Y^{2}_{c}\right\|\bigg)^{1/2}.

Lemma 3.29
.

Let
\beta\in\real
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{\beta,\gamma}\colon H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\to Y^{2,\gamma}_{-\beta},\hskip 10.00002ptu\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{\beta,\gamma}u:=\mathcal{M}_{\beta}u,

is an invertible linear operator with bounded inverse
\mathcal{M}_{\beta,\gamma}^{-1}:=\mathcal{M}_{\beta}^{-1}|_{Y^{2,\gamma}_{-\beta}}
. In particular,

\big(\mathcal{M}_{\beta}(r\mapsto rD_{r}u(r))\big)(\lambda)=\lambda(\mathcal{M}_{\beta}u)(\lambda),\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Gamma_{-\beta},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in H^{1}_{2,2\beta}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

(32)

and

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\beta}u\;\vrule\;Y^{2,\gamma}_{-\beta}\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+}).

Let
\beta\in\real
.
Note first that the assertion is satisfied for
\gamma=0
due to
Section 3.9
(iv)
.
Moreover, due to Lemma
2.3
(x)
, for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})
if, and only if,
(rD_{r})^{k}u\in L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})
for all
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,\gamma\}
and

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|\sim\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\gamma}\left\|(rD_{r})^{k}u\;\vrule\;L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+}).

Thus, for arbitrary
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\real_{+})
,
Section 3.9
(iv)
yields that

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\gamma}\left\|(r\partial_{r})^{k}u\;\vrule\;L_{2,2\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\gamma}\left\|\lambda^{k}\mathcal{M}u\;\vrule\;Y^{2}_{-\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}=\left\|\mathcal{M}u\;\vrule\;Y^{2,\gamma}_{-\beta}(\real_{+})\right\|.

Since
C^{\infty}_{0}(\real_{+})
is dense in

(see
Section 2.3
(ii)
), the assertion follows as soon as we prove that
\mathcal{M}_{\beta,\gamma}
is surjective.
The latter follows by induction over
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
as soon as we can prove that if

and
\lambda v\in Y^{2}_{-\beta}

and

—which also proves (

which may be checked by means of

.
∎

As we will see below, the ranges of the spaces

under the Mellin transform with respect to

are given by the following Hilbert spaces.

Definition 3.30
.

Let
\Theta,\theta\in\real
. We write

for the space of all functions

, such that

\left\|u\;\vrule\;Y^{0}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|:=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\theta/2-i\infty}^{-\theta/2+i\infty}\left\|u(\lambda,\cdot)\;\vrule\;L_{2,\Theta-1}(\mathcal{I})\right\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

Moreover, for

endowed with the norm

\left\|u\;\vrule\;Y^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|:=\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma}\int_{-\theta/2-i\infty}^{-\theta/2+i\infty}\left|\lambda\right|^{2j}\left\|u(\lambda,\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-j}_{2,\Theta-1+2j}(\mathcal{I})\right\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\bigg)^{1/2}.

Using the preparations above we can prove the following characterization of the spaces
P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})
by means of the Mellin transform

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
\Theta,\theta\in\real
. Then the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\colon P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\to Y^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{I}),\hskip 20.00003ptu\mapsto\mathcal{M}u

with

\big(\mathcal{M}u\big)(\lambda,\phi):=\mathcal{M}_{-\theta/2}\big(r\mapsto u(r,\phi)\big)(\lambda),\hskip 20.00003pt(\lambda,\phi)\in\Gamma_{-\theta/2}\times\mathcal{I},

defines an invertible, bounded, linear operator with bounded inverse
\mathcal{M}^{-1}
. In particular,

\left\|\mathcal{M}u\;\vrule\;Y^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}).

Let
u\in P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})
.
Then,
\big(r\mapsto D^{\alpha}_{\phi}(r,\phi)\big)\in H^{\gamma-\alpha}_{2,\theta}(\real_{+})
for almost all
\phi\in\mathcal{I}
, for all
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots,\gamma\}
.
Thus, applying
Section 3.9
we get that

\displaystyle\left\|u\;\vrule\;P^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\lvert(rD_{r})^{j}D_{\phi}^{\alpha}u(r,\phi)\big\rvert^{2}r^{\theta-1}\mathrm{d}r\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}(\phi)^{\Theta-2(1-j-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\left\|D^{\alpha}_{\phi}u(\cdot,\phi)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma-\alpha}_{2,\theta}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right\|^{2}\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}(\phi)^{\Theta-2(1-j-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\left\|\lambda\mapsto\big(\mathcal{M}_{\theta/2}(D^{\alpha}_{\phi}u(\cdot,\phi))\big)(\lambda)\;\vrule\;Y^{2,\gamma-\alpha}_{-\theta/2}\right\|^{2}\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}(\phi)^{\Theta-2(1-j-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\kappa}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\theta/2-i\infty}^{-\theta/2+i\infty}\big\lvert\lambda^{j}\mathcal{M}_{\theta/2}(D_{\phi}^{\alpha}u(\cdot,\phi))(\lambda)\big\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\varrho_{\mathcal{I}}(\phi)^{\Theta-2(1-j-\alpha)}\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\mathcal{M}u\;\vrule\;Y^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|^{2}.

In the last step we used the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{\theta/2}D_{\phi}^{\alpha}u=D_{\phi}^{\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{\theta/2}u
, which can be checked for smooth
u
by means of Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and then extended to arbitrary

by means of a density argument.
Thus, linearity, boundedness and injectivity are proven. Surjectivity follows from the invertibility of
\mathcal{M}_{\theta/2}
.
∎

4
The Poisson equation in
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})

In this section we begin the study of the regularity of the Poisson equation (
2
)
within the scale
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
.
On the one hand, we show that these spaces are suitable for establishing higher order regularity for the Poisson equation in the sense that the regularity of the solution within this scale of spaces can be lifted with the regularity of the forcing term. This works for arbitrary
p>1
.
On the other hand, we establish existence and uniqueness for the case
p=2
and a sharp range of weight parameters
\Theta
and
\theta
.
The latter is done by means of the Mellin transform and suitable resolvent estimates for the Dirichlet Laplacian on an interval within the scale
H^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})
from
[
45
]
.
The case
p\neq 2
is postponed to a forthcoming paper. Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\theta,\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

Lifting.
Let
1<\Theta<p+1
, let
f\in H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D})
, and let
u\in H^{\gamma+1}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})
be such that

\Delta u=f\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\mathcal{D}.

(33)

Then
u\in H^{\gamma+2}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})
and

\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma+2}_{p,\Theta-p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|+\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma+1}_{p,\Theta,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})\right\|

with a constant that does not depend on
f
and
u
.

(ii)

Existence.
Let
p=2
and assume that

1<\Theta<3\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{\theta-2}{2}\notin\Big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

(34)

Then for all
f\in H^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta+2,\theta+2}(\mathcal{D})
there exists a unique
u\in H^{\gamma+2}_{2,\Theta-2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{D})
such that (
33

\lVert u|H^{\gamma+2}_{2,\Theta-2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{D})\rVert\lesssim\lVert f|H^{\gamma}_{2,\Theta+2,\theta+2}(\mathcal{D})\rVert

(35)

with a constant that does not depend on
f
and
u
.

Remark 4.2
.

The following remarks are in order.

(i)

The solutions

to (

2

This is because for the range of
\Theta
therein, i.e., for

has trace zero, since for all

on a suitable bounded
C^{2}

\mathcal{O}\subset\mathcal{D}
; the equality above is proven in Lemma
4

(ii)

The ranges of parameters in Theorem
4.1
include the ranges obtained so far in the analysis of the (stochastic) heat equation within the spaces

on angular domains, see
[
5
,
29

31

.

[

, the restriction

It includes the set of all

[
50

35

51

, all these operators are known to be isomorphisms. Thus, if we can prove that

is also an isomorphism, so is
\Delta_{x}
and the existence part of Theorem
4.1
is proven. Clearly,
B
is linear and bounded. To obtain its invertibility we rely on results from
[
45
]
.
Therein, among others, for bounded
C^{2}
-domains
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{O}}
is analysed as an unbounded operator in the weighted
L_{p}
-spaces
L_{p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}}):=L_{p,\nu+d}(\mathcal{O})
, cf. Definition
2.4
and Lemma
2.3
(iii)
.
In
[
45
]
, for
p-1<\nu<2p-1
, the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{O}}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}})
is given by

D(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{O}}):=W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{Dir}}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}}):=\{u\in W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}}):\ \mathrm{Tr}\,u=0\},\hskip 20.00003pt\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{O}}u:=\Delta u,\,\,u\in D(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{O}}),

where for
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
1<p<\infty
,

W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}}):=\{u:\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u\in L_{p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}}),\left|\alpha\right|\leq k\},

endowed with the norm

\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|:=\bigg(\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq k}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big\lvert D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\varrho_{\mathcal{O}}^{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1/p},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in W^{k,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}});

W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{Dir}}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}})
is endowed with the norm inherited from
W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\nu}^{\mathcal{O}})
.
Note that, other than for the spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{O})
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, see Lemma
2.3
(iii)
, here the weight does not depend on the order of the derivatives. However, due to Hardy’s inequality, the following holds.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded
C^{2}
-domain, let
1<p<\infty
, and let
d-1<\Theta<d+p-1
. Then

W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})=H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{O})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{(equivalent norms)}.

Since, on the one hand, by Lemma
2.3
(ii)
,
C^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{O})\subset H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{O})
dense, and, on the other hand, by
[
45
, Proposition 3.8]
,
C^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
is dense in
W^{2,p}_{\textup{Dir}}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})
for
d-1<\Theta<d+p-1
, it is enough to prove the norm equivalence

\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{O})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{O}).

(39)

However, since
\mathcal{O}
is assumed to be bounded, it is enough to check that “
\gtrsim
” holds (the other direction is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.3
(iii)
and
(vii)
).
Since the seminorms involving the second order derivatives in the two norms coincide, we merely have to prove that

\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left|u\right|^{p}\varrho_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta-p-d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left|\partial_{x}u\right|^{p}\varrho_{\mathcal{O}}^{\Theta-d}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|^{p},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{O}).

But this is a consequence of Hardy’s inequality, which guarantees that for
\Theta<d+p-1
,

\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{0,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta-p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{O}),

and

\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in W^{2,p}(\mathcal{O},\omega_{\Theta+p-d}^{\mathcal{O}}),

see
[
45
, Corollary 3.4]
(or
[
43
, Theorems 8.2 and 8.4]
).
∎

Recall that our goal is to prove the existence part in Theorem
4.1
. To this end we aim to prove that the operator
B
from (
38
) is invertible for the range of weight parameters
\Theta
and
\theta
from (
34
) .
For such
\Theta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, if
Bv=F
for some
F\in Y^{0}_{2,\Theta+2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{I})
and some
v\in Y^{2}_{2,\Theta-2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{I})
, then for almost all
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\frac{2-\theta}{2}}=\frac{2-\theta}{2}+i\mathbb{R}
it holds that
v(\lambda,\cdot)\in H^{2}_{2,\Theta-3}(\mathcal{I})
,
F(\lambda,\cdot)\in L_{2,\Theta+1}(\mathcal{I})
and

(\lambda^{2}+D_{\phi}^{2})v(\lambda,\cdot)=F(\lambda,\cdot)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }L_{2,\Theta+1}(\mathcal{I}).

In view of Lemma
4
this is the same as saying that

u(\lambda,\cdot)=R(\lambda^{2},-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})F(\lambda,\cdot),

where
R(\mu,-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
is the resolvent of the unbounded operator
(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}},H^{2}_{2,\Theta-3}(\mathcal{I}))
in
L_{2,\Theta+1}(\mathcal{I})
at
\mu\in\varrho(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
, where
\varrho(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
is the resolvent set of
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}
(we refer to
[
21
, Chapter 10 and Appendix G]
for notions from the theory of unbounded operators and operator semigroups).
The following lemma collects some properties of
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}
and its resolvent.
It is a slight alteration of parts of
[
45
, Corollary 6.2]
applied to the one-dimensional domain
\mathcal{I}=(0,\kappa)
. For
0<\sigma<\pi
we write

\Sigma_{\sigma}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\colon\left|\arg(z)\right|<\sigma\big\}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
0<\Theta<p
, and let

(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}},D(\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})):=(\Delta,H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I}))

be the Dirichlet Laplacian in
L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
,
\mathcal{I}=(0,\kappa)
; cf. Lemma
4
. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

The spectrum of
-\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is given by
\sigma(-\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathrm{Dir}})=\Big\{\big(n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\big)^{2}:\ n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}
.

(ii)

-\Delta_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}
is sectorial with angle of sectoriality
\omega(-\Delta_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})=0
.

(iii)

(\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathrm{Dir}},D(\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathrm{Dir}}))
is a closed and densely defined operator in
L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
. Moreover,

\left\|\Delta u\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\sim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I}).

(iv)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
, let
\lambda\in\Gamma_{s}=s+i\mathbb{R}
, let
f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
, and let

u_{\lambda}:=u_{\lambda,f}:=R(\lambda^{2},-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}).

Then
u_{\lambda,f}
is the unique
u\in H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{D})
such that
(\lambda^{2}+\Delta)u=f
. Moreover,

\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left|\lambda\right|^{j}\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;H^{2-j}_{p,\Theta-p+jp}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Gamma_{s},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}).

(40)

It is well-known that
(i)
holds for
p=2
and
\Theta=1
, see, e.g.,
[
16
, pp. 49-50]
.
The general case is thus a consequence of
[
45
, Corollary 6.2(1)]
, which states the independence of the spectrum on
p
and
\Theta
.
Assertion
(ii)
is an immediate consequence of
[
45
, Corollary 6.2(2)]
, whereas
(iii)
follows from
[
45
, Corollary 6.2(3)]
together with Lemma
4
and the fact that
0\in\varrho(-\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\textup{Dir}})
, as follows from
(i)
.
Thus, we only have to prove
(iv)
, which mainly follows from the sectoriality of
-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}}
, its spectral properties and the interpolation properties of the scale
H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})
.
We argue as follows:
First note that, due to
(i)
,
\lambda^{2}\in\varrho(-\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\textup{Dir}})
for all
\lambda\notin\big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
.
Thus, by the definition of the resolvent, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
and for all
f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
,
u_{\lambda,f}
is the unique
u\in H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I})
such that
\big(\lambda^{2}+\Delta\big)u=f
.
It thus remains to prove (
40
).
To this end, fix
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
and fix
0<\sigma<\pi
.
The sectoriality of
-\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}_{\textup{Dir}}
yields that there is a finite constant
C_{\sigma}>0
such that

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\leq C_{\sigma}\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\,\,\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}).

Since
\Delta u_{\lambda,f}=\lambda^{2}u_{\lambda,f}+\Delta u_{\lambda,f}-\lambda^{2}u_{\lambda,f}=f-\lambda^{2}u_{\lambda,f}
, the last estimate also yields that

\left\|\Delta u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\leq(C_{\sigma}+1)\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\,\,\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}),

so that, by
(iii)
, we obtain

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\leq\big(2C_{\sigma}+1\big)\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}}
and all
f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
with the same
C_{\sigma}
as above.
Moreover, using the fact that, by the interpolation statement from Lemma
2.3
(iv)
,

\big[H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I}),\left|\lambda\right|^{2}L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\big]_{1/2}=\left|\lambda\right|H^{1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I}),

we obtain that

\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left|\lambda\right|^{j}\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;H^{2-j}_{p,\Theta-p+jp}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\lesssim_{\sigma}\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}),

(41)

with
S_{\sigma}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus\big(\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma/2}}\cup\Sigma^{c}_{\pi-\sigma/2}\big)
(note that
\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\sigma}}
if, and only if,
\lambda\in S_{\sigma}
).
Then
\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c}\subset\varrho(-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
, so that
\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c}\ni\lambda\mapsto R(\lambda^{2},-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})
is continuous. Thus, due to the compactness of
\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c}
, the set
\big\{R(\lambda^{2},-\Delta_{\textup{Dir}}^{\mathcal{I}})\colon\lambda\in\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c}\big\}
is bounded in the operator norm on
L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})
. Therefore,

\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}).

With similar arguments as above (and since
\mathcal{I}
is bounded and Lemma
2.3
(vii)
holds), this yields that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left|\lambda\right|^{j}\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;H^{2-j}_{p,\Theta-p+jp}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;H^{2}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Delta u_{\lambda,f}\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\left|\lambda\right|^{2})\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in\Gamma_{s}\cap S_{\sigma}^{c},\,\,f\in L_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I}).

Together with (
41
), this proves the assertion.
∎

Now we are finally ready to prove the existence part of Theorem
4.1
.

As outlined above, it is sufficient to prove that the operator
B
introduced in (
38
) is invertible.
To this end, let
F\in Y^{0}_{2,\Theta+2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{I})
with
\Theta
and
\theta
satisfying (
34
).
We need to show that there exists a unique
v\in Y^{2}_{2,\Theta-2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{I})
satisfying

Bv=F.

(42)

Since
F\in Y^{0}_{2,\Theta+2,\theta-2}(\mathcal{I})
,
F
has a version (also denoted by
F
), such that
F(\lambda,\cdot)\in L_{2,\Theta+1}(\mathcal{I})
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\frac{2-\theta}{2}}
.
Thus, by Lemma
4
and since
(\theta-2)/2\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\big\{\pm n\frac{\pi}{\kappa}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
and
0<\Theta-1<2
as
\theta
and
\Theta
satisfy (

), for all

(43)

Moreover, there exists an essentially unique

Appendix A
Differential calculus in polar coordinates

In this appendix we collect some fundamental results, which we need when switching from Cartesian to polar coordinates, in particular, when “translating” derivatives with respect to one coordinate system into derivatives with respect to the other.
Recall that for
\mathcal{I}:=(0,\kappa)
by
\Phi\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}:=(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{D}
we denote the polar coordinate transform which is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism.
Also recall the definition of the rotation matrices
A=A(\phi)
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}
, from (
29
).
Finally, let
\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}:=\varrho_{\circ}\circ\Phi
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
g\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
, then
g\circ\Phi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})
and

(D_{x}g)\circ\Phi=A\begin{pmatrix}D_{r}\\
\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{-1}D_{\phi}\end{pmatrix}(g\circ\Phi).

(44)

In particular,
D^{\alpha}_{x}g\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\gamma
if, and only if,
D^{\alpha}_{(r,\phi)}(g\circ\Phi)\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})
for all
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\gamma
.

For
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
the assertion follows simply by using the chain rule. The assertion for arbitrary
g\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D})
then follows from the usual rules of distributional calculus.
∎

Formula (
44
) generalizes to higher order derivatives the following way.

Lemma A.2

(
[
11
, p. 1556]
)
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
let

\Lambda_{\alpha}:=\left\{\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{0\}:\ \left|\beta\right|\leq\left|\alpha\right|\right\}.

Then, for all
\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}
, there exist trigonometric polynomials
T_{\alpha,\beta}
on
\mathcal{I}
such that

\displaystyle(D_{x}^{\alpha}g)\circ\Phi=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi),\hskip 20.00003ptg\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{D}).

(45)

We argue by mathematical induction on
\gamma:=\left|\alpha\right|
.
The base case
\gamma=1
immediately follows from
Appendix A
, which yields (with
e_{1}:=(1,0)^{T}
and
e_{2}:=(0,1)^{T}
)

(D_{x}g)\circ\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}(D_{x}^{e_{1}}g)\circ\Phi\\
(D_{x}^{e_{2}}g)\circ\Phi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{e_{1},e_{1}}\,D_{r}+T_{e_{1},e_{2}}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{-1}\,D_{\phi}\\
T_{e_{2},e_{1}}\,D_{r}+T_{e_{2},e_{1}}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{-1}\,D_{\phi}\end{pmatrix}(g\circ\Phi)

with
T_{e_{1},e_{1}}(\phi):=T_{e_{2},e_{2}}(\phi):=\cos(\phi)
,
T_{e_{1},e_{2}}(\phi):=-\sin(\phi)
, and
T_{e_{2},e_{1}}(\phi)=\sin(\phi)
,
\phi\in\mathcal{I}
.
For the induction step
\gamma\mapsto\gamma+1
we assume that for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
the assertion holds for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\gamma
.
Take
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|=\gamma+1
. Then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
\left|\alpha\right|=\gamma
such that
\widetilde{\alpha}=\alpha+e_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
. Thus, using the base step and the induction hypothesis, we obtain

\displaystyle(D_{x}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}g)\circ\Phi

\displaystyle=(D_{x}^{e_{i}}(D_{x}^{\alpha})g)\circ\Phi

\displaystyle=T_{e_{i},e_{1}}D_{r}((D_{x}^{\alpha}g)\circ\Phi)+T_{e_{i},e_{2}}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{-1}D_{\phi}((D_{x}^{\alpha}g)\circ\Phi)

\displaystyle=T_{e_{i},e_{1}}D_{r}\bigg(\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)\bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+T_{e_{i},e_{2}}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{-1}D_{\phi}\bigg(\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)\bigg)=:I+I\!I.

Using the product rule, the first summand becomes

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{e_{i},e_{1}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\Big((\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|)\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-1-\left|\alpha\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)+\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}+1}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{e_{i},e_{1}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\Big((\beta_{1}-\left|\alpha\right|)\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)+\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}+1-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}+1}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}}T_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}^{I}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)

with suitable trigonometric polynomials

for
\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle I\!I

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{e_{i},e_{2}}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\Big(D_{\phi}(T_{\alpha,\beta})\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)+T_{\alpha,\beta}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}+1}(g\circ\Phi)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\alpha}}T_{e_{i},e_{2}}D_{\phi}(T_{\alpha,\beta})\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)+T_{e_{i},e_{2}}T_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}+1}(g\circ\Phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}}T^{I\!I}_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)

with suitable trigonometric polynomials
T^{I\!I}_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}
for
\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
.
Thus,

(D_{x}^{\widetilde{\alpha}}g)\circ\Phi=\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}}T_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}\,\widetilde{\varrho}_{\circ}^{\beta_{1}-\left|\widetilde{\alpha}\right|}\,D_{r}^{\beta_{1}}\,D_{\phi}^{\beta_{2}}(g\circ\Phi)

with
T_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}:=T^{I}_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}+T^{I\!I}_{\widetilde{\alpha},\beta}
for
\beta\in\Lambda_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
.
∎

Since the matrix
A=A(\phi)
is orthogonal, multiplication by
A(\phi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
preserves norms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e., for
1<p<\infty
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|A(\phi)\,x\right|_{p}\sim\left|x\right|_{p},\hskip 20.00003pt\phi\in\mathcal{I},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{2};

(46)

the constants in the equivalence depend solely on
p
.
It is easily seen that the same is true for its (component-wise) classical derivatives
(\partial_{\phi}^{n}A)(\phi)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}^{n}A\in\mathcal{M}:=\big\{M\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}:\

\displaystyle M\text{ Borel-measurable, }M(\phi)\text{ orthogonal for all }\phi\in\mathcal{I}\big\}.

The following lemma extends (
46
) to
\left\|\,\cdot\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|
-norms and is used in the proof of
Theorem 3.27
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1<p<\infty
, and
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, let
M\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be such that
\partial_{\phi}^{n}M\in\mathcal{M}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|Mv\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|\sim\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptv=(v_{1},v_{2})\in L_{1,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{I};\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(47)

We proceed by mathematical induction on
\gamma
.
For
\gamma=0
we have to consider
H^{0}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})=L_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})
.
Obviously,
v
is Borel-measurable if, and only if,
Mv
is.
Further, since
M\in\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{0}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{I}}\left|v(\phi)\right|_{p}^{p}w_{\Theta}(\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\phi\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\sim\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{I}}\left|M(\phi)\,v(\phi)\right|_{p}^{p}w_{\Theta}(\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\phi\bigg)^{1/p}\sim\left\|M\,v\;\vrule\;H^{0}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|.

Now suppose that (
47
) holds for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then
Section 2.3
(x)
and
(ix)
together with the chain rule show

\displaystyle\left\|Mv\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma+1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\sim\left\|Mv\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|D_{\phi}(Mv)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|M\,v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|(\partial_{\phi}M)\,v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|MD_{\phi}v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|.

Thus, applying three times the induction hypothesis, as well as
Section 2.3
(vii)
,
(ix)
, and
(x)
,
we obtain one of the asserted estimates:

\displaystyle\left\|Mv\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma+1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|D_{\phi}v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|+\left\|\psi\,D_{\phi}v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma+1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{I})\right\|.

The reverse estimate follows from this estimate, too, since

M(\cdot)^{T}=M(\cdot)^{-1}
and obviously
\partial_{\phi}^{n}M(\cdot)^{T}\in\mathcal{M}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

Appendix B
Bessel potential spaces

In this final appendix we recall the definition of Bessel potential spaces
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and gather some of their well-known properties which are frequently used throughout the manuscript.
To do so, we use a Fourier analytical approach similar to
[
56
, Section 1.3.2]
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(1-\Delta)^{s}\colon\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 20.00003ptf\mapsto\mathscr{F}^{-1}\big(\big\{\xi\mapsto(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{s}(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)\big\}\big),

where
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of tempered distributions (defined as the topological dual of the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions),
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the Fourier transform and
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
its inverse.
Then for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
1<p<\infty
,

H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\big\{u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big\},

denotes the space of Bessel potentials
endowed with the norm

\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|:=\left\|(1-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f\;\vrule\;L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark B.1
.

In some texts, like, for instance,
[
40
]
, the space
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the space of all distributions
f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(not necessarily tempered!) for which there exists
h\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
f=(1-\Delta)^{-\gamma/2}h
.
However, note that such an
f
can always be extended to become a tempered distribution, see
[
40
, Theorem 13.1.2(i) together with Remark 13.3.3]
.

For the convenience of the reader, the next lemma collects the properties of
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as needed in our arguments.
Therein, for
1<p<\infty
, we use
W^{k}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to denote the classical
L_{p}
-Sobolev space of order
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Lemma B.2

(Properties of Bessel potential spaces)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
, as well as
\gamma,\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a reflexive Banach space.

(ii)

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(iii)

If
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=W_{p}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalent norms.

(iv)

\big(H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)^{\prime}=H^{-\gamma}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, with equivalent norms.

(v)

If for
0<\vartheta<1
there holds

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\vartheta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\vartheta}{p_{1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\gamma=(1-\vartheta)\gamma_{0}+\vartheta\gamma_{1},

then

\big[H^{\gamma_{0}}_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),H^{\gamma_{1}}_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big]_{\vartheta}=H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with equivalent norms.

(vi)

Let
(\zeta_{\nu})_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}
denote a collection of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions
such that for some
c>1

\left|\partial^{\alpha}\zeta_{\nu}(x)\right|\lesssim_{\alpha}c^{-\left|\alpha\right|\nu},\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z},\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

then

\left\|\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 10.00002pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z}.

(vii)

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c>1
we have

\left\|f(c^{k}\cdot)\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(viii)

Let
(\zeta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
denote a collection of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left|\partial^{\alpha}\zeta_{k}(x)\right|\leq C_{\alpha},\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}.

Then, with some constant depending on
d
,
\gamma
, and
C_{\alpha}
, we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\|\zeta_{k}f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|^{p}\lesssim\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|^{p},\hskip 20.00003ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

If, in addition,

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left|\zeta_{k}(x)\right|^{p}\geq\delta>0,

then, with some constant depending on
\delta
,
d
,
\gamma
, and
C_{\alpha}
, we have

\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|^{p}\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\|\zeta_{k}f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|^{p},\hskip 20.00003ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For proofs of
(i)
-
(iii)
see
[
40
, Theorems 13.3.7 and 13.3.12]
.
For the other assertions one may use the coincidence of
H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with so-called Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{\gamma}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
, see, e.g.,
[
55
, Definition 2.3.2 and Theorem 2.5.6]
.
Then, the duality statement
(iv)
follows from
[
55
, Theorem 2.11.2]
and the complex interpolation formula in
(v)
is a consequence of
[
55
, Theorem 2.4.7]
.
Assertion
(vi)
follows, for instance, from the following multiplier assertion, proven, e.g., in
[
56
, Theorem 4.2.2]
:
If
m\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large (compared to
\left|\gamma\right|
and
p
), then

\left\|af\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|\lesssim_{m}\left\|f\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}a\;\vrule\;L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|,\hskip 20.00003pta\in C^{m}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 10.00002ptf\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
C^{m}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions with bounded derivatives up to order
m
.
Thus,
(vi)
follows if we choose
a:=a_{\nu}:=\zeta_{\nu}(c^{\nu}\cdot)
, since, by assumption, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,

\left\|\partial^{\alpha}a_{\nu}\;\vrule\;L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|=\left\|c^{\left|\alpha\right|\nu}\,(\partial^{\alpha}\xi_{\nu})(c^{\nu}\cdot)\;\vrule\;L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\|\leq C_{\alpha},\hskip 20.00003pt\nu\in\mathbb{Z}.

The statement
(vii)
follows from the fact that if
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an
m
-diffeomorphism with
m\in\mathbb{N}
large enough (again depending on
\left|\gamma\right|
and
p

[
56

)

\left\|u\circ\Psi\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}\right\|\lesssim\left\|u\;\vrule\;H^{\gamma}_{p}\right\|,\hskip 20.00003ptu\in H^{\gamma}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Finally, the localization result
(viii)
can been found in
[
38
, Lemma 6.7]
and
[
36
, Theorem 2.1]
.

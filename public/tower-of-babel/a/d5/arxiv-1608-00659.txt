A n axiomatic approach to Maxwell’s equations
José A. Heras

Abstract
This paper suggests an axiomatic approach to Maxwell’s equations. The basis of this approach is a theorem formulated for two sets of functions localized in space and time. If each set satisfies a continuity equation then the theorem provides an integral representation for each function. A corollary of this theorem yields Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles. It is pointed out that the causality principle and the conservation of electric and magnetic charges are the most fundamental physical axioms underlying these equations. Another application of the corollary yields Maxwell’s equations in material media. The theorem is also formulated in the Minkowski space-time and applied to obtain the covariant form of Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles and the covariant form of Maxwell’s equations in material media. The approach makes use of the infinite-space Green function of the wave equation and is therefore suitable for an advanced course in electrodynamics.

Introduction

Special relativity has been a very successful theory. A key part of its success is that it relies on a two relatively simple axioms: The relativity principle and the constancy of the speed of light
c.

From these axioms we derive the Lorentz transformations which turn out to be vital for the construction and understanding of the theory itself. Classical electrodynamics has also been a very successful theory. We have also a set of equations, the famous Maxwell’s equations, which are at the hearth of the theory. Nevertheless, the analogous ‘‘simple’’ axioms from which we can derive Maxwell’s equations have not been clearly identified. This is particularly disturbing because it was precisely the form invariance of Maxwell’s equations that played a central role in the derivation of the Lorentz transformations.

In analogy with special relativity, it would be desirably to identify the two fundamental axioms of Maxwell’s equations. Put differently, the profound link between classical electrodynamics and special relativity invites us to identify the fundamental physical axioms underlying Maxwell’s equations. The student and the teacher would benefit from having a consistent axiomatic approach to Maxwell’s equations as an alternative to standard approaches to these equations, which usually mix historical, heuristical and experimental elements.

In this paper we extend our axiomatic approach to Maxwell’s equations. We show how Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles can be obtained by using the continuity equations for electric and magnetic charges and the infinity-space Green function of the wave equation. In section 2 we formulate a theorem for two sets of functions localized in space and time. Each set is formed by a scalar and a vector functions which satisfy a continuity equation. The theorem provides an integral representation for each function in terms of the derivatives of the involved functions and the retarded Green function. In section 3 we show how a corollary of the theorem provides an axiomatic derivation of Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles. The basic physical axioms in this derivation are the conservation of electric and magnetic charges represented by their associated continuity equations and the causality principle represented by the retarded Green function. In section 4 we apply again the corollary of the theorem to find Maxwell’s equations in material media. In section 5 we formulate this theorem in the Minkowski space-time and apply its associated corollary to obtain the covariant form of Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles and the covariant form of Maxwell’s equations in material media. In section 6 we emphasize that charge conservation is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for Maxwell’s standard equations and that causality is a sufficient but not a necessary condition for these equations. We also note the general validity of the corollary of the theorem and show how it can be applied to find the gravitational field equations proposed by Heaviside

An extensive use of the infinite-space retarded Green function of the wave equation is made in the approach to Maxwell’s equations suggested in this paper. As is well-known, this function is typically introduced in graduate textbooks of electrodynamics

However, in section 7 we outline an elementary introduction to this Green function which could be suitable for undergraduate students. If this Green function is introduced via this non-rigours and heuristic procedure then the three-dimensional version of the approach for Maxwell’s equations proposed here, which involves well-known vector identities, could be presented in an advanced undergraduate course of electrodynamics. In section 8 we present our general conclusion. Finally, in Appendices A and B we prove some useful identities.

5
The covariant treatment

The theorem proved in section 2 can be formulated in the Minkowski space-time. Greek indices
\mu,\nu,\kappa\ldots
run from 0 to 3 and Latin indices
i,j,k,\ldots
run from 1 to 3. The summation convention on repeated indices is adopted. The signature of the metric is
(+,-,-,-).
A point is denoted by
x=x^{\mu}=\big(x^{0},{\bf x}\big)=\big(ct,{\bf x}\big).
The four gradient is defined as
\partial_{\mu}=\big[(1/c)\partial/\partial t,\nabla\big].
The totally antisymmetric four-dimensional tensor reads
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
with
\varepsilon^{0123}=1
and
\varepsilon^{ijk}
is the totally antisymmetric three-dimensional tensor with
\varepsilon^{123}=1
.

Theorem.
If the four-vectors
J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\!=\!J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}(x)
and
J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\!=\!J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}(x)
are localized in space-time and satisfy the continuity equations

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}=0,\quad\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}=0,

(69)

then they admit the following integral representations:

\displaystyle J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\!\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\,\Bigg(\partial^{\prime\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}-\partial^{\prime\nu}J^{\mu}_{\{1\}}-\frac{1}{\chi c}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\partial^{\prime}_{\kappa}J_{\lambda}^{\{2\}}\Bigg),

(70)

\displaystyle J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}

\displaystyle=\chi c\,\partial_{\mu}\!\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\,\Bigg(\frac{1}{\chi c}\big(\partial^{\prime\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}-\partial^{\prime\nu}J^{\mu}_{\{2\}}\big)+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\partial^{\prime}_{\kappa}J_{\lambda}^{\{1\}}\Bigg),

(71)

where
D
is the infinity-space Green function of the four-dimensional wave equation and the integration is over all space-time.

Corollary.
If the tensor fields
F^{\mu\nu}_{\{1\}}=F^{\mu\nu}_{\{1\}}(x)
and
F^{\mu\nu}_{\{2\}}=F^{\mu\nu}_{\{2\}}(x)
are defined as

\displaystyle F^{\mu\nu}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\,\Bigg(\partial^{\prime\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}-\partial^{\prime\nu}J^{\mu}_{\{1\}}-\frac{1}{\chi c}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\partial^{\prime}_{\kappa}J_{\lambda}^{\{2\}}\Bigg),

(72)

\displaystyle F^{\mu\nu}_{\{2\}}

\displaystyle=\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\,\Bigg(\frac{1}{\chi c}\big(\partial^{\prime\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}-\partial^{\prime\nu}J^{\mu}_{\{2\}}\big)+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\partial^{\prime}_{\kappa}J_{\lambda}^{\{1\}}\Bigg),

(73)

then equations (70) and (71) take the compact form

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}_{\{1\}}=J^{\nu}_{\{1\}},\quad\chi c\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}_{\{2\}}=J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}.

(74)

Before proving the theorem, we observe that the continuity equations given in (69) are invariant under the dual transformations:

\displaystyle J^{\prime\nu}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\cos\theta+\frac{1}{\chi c}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\sin\theta,

(75)

\displaystyle J^{\prime\nu}_{\{2\}}

\displaystyle=-\chi cJ^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\sin\theta+J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\cos\theta,

(76)

where
\theta
is a real angle. We can directly verify this invariance

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}J^{\prime\nu}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\cos\theta+\frac{1}{\chi c}\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\sin\theta=0,

(77)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}J^{\prime\nu}_{\{2\}}

\displaystyle=-\chi c\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\sin\theta+\partial_{\nu}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\cos\theta=0,

(78)

where we have used equations (69), (75) and (76). In particular, the continuity equations defined in equation (69) are shown to be invariant under the specific dual transformations:

\displaystyle J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\chi c}J^{\nu}_{\{2\}},\quad J^{\nu}_{\{2\}}\rightarrow-\chi cJ^{\nu}_{\{1\}},

(79)

which follow from equations (75) and (76) when
\theta=\pi/2
. Accordingly, when the dual changes given in equations (79) are applied to some result derived from the first continuity equation appearing in equation (69), we obtain the analogous result corresponding to the second continuity equation appearing in equation (69). For example, when the dual changes defined in equation (79) are applied to equation (70), we obtain equation (71).

The continuity equations given in equation (69) imply the following identities

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\Bigg(\partial^{\mu}J^{\nu}_{\{1\}}-\partial^{\nu}J^{\mu}_{\{1\}}-\frac{1}{\chi c}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\partial_{\kappa}J_{\lambda}^{\{2\}}\Bigg),

(80)

6
Discussion

Can Maxwell’s equations be axiomatized? Let us respond this question for the particular case of Maxwell’s standard equations with only electric sources in vacuum. We first write

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{1}={\bf E}/\alpha,\;{\bf F}_{2}=\chi{\bf B}/\beta,\;\rho_{1}=\rho_{\rm e},\;\rho_{2}=0,\;{\bf J}_{1}={\bf J}_{\rm e}\;{\bf J}_{2}=0

(104)

in equations (9)-(12). We then identify
\rho_{\rm e}
and
{\bf J}_{\rm e}
with the electric charge and current densities and assume that they
are localized in space and time and satisfy the continuity equation:
\nabla\cdot{\bf J}_{\rm e}+\partial\rho_{\rm e}/\partial t=0.
If
c
is the speed of light in vacuum and the values of
\alpha,\beta
and
\chi
are specified in the table 1 then we obtain the Maxwell equations displayed in equations (46)-(49). The basic ingredients underlying equations (46)-(49) are then the continuity equation for the electric charge and the Green function of the wave equation. In order to satisfy causality we choose the retarded form of this Green function. Accordingly, the principle of electric charge conservation (represented by the continuity equation) and the causality principle (represented by the retarded Green function of the wave equation) are the most fundamental physical axioms that underlie Maxwell’s equations. On the basis on the theorem formulated here, the answer to our initial question is in the affirmative. Charge conservation and causality are the basic postulates of Maxwell’s equations.

While the first postulate (charge conservation) is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for Maxwell’s standard equations, the second postulate (causality) is a sufficient but not a necessary condition for these equations. Let us explain these statements. Using the continuity equation we can obtain the identity

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}{\bf J}=-\nabla\times\big(\nabla\times{\bf J}\big)+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big(-\nabla\rho\big).

(105)

If we consider the additional identity

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\rho=-\nabla\cdot\big(-\!\nabla\rho\big),

(106)

then the set formed by equations (105) and (106) is different from the set formed by equations (22) and (20). Using the infinity-space instantaneous Green function
[
31
]
:
G_{I}({\bf x},t;{\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\!=\!\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t)/{R}
, which satisfies the Poisson equation
\nabla^{2}G_{I}\!=\!-4\pi\delta\big({\bf x}\!-\!{\bf x}^{\prime}\big)\delta(t\!-\!t^{\prime})
[see section 7], as an integrating factor for equations (105) and (106), we can formulate another theorem for the functions
\rho
and
{\bf J}
, whose corollary yields the time-dependent electric and magnetic fields:
{\bf E}({\bf x},t)\!=\!-\alpha\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}\rho({\bf x}^{\prime},t)/(4\pi)
and
{\bf B}({\bf x},t)\!=\!\beta\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}\times{\bf J}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)/(4\pi)
and their associated field equations:
\nabla\cdot{\bf E}=\alpha\rho,\nabla\times{\bf B}-(\beta/\alpha)\partial{\bf E}/\partial t=\beta{\bf J},\nabla\cdot{\bf B}=0
and
\nabla\times{\bf E}=0
. These equations describe the fields of an instantaneous electrodynamics
[
28
]
. Therefore, the continuity equation together with another Green’s function distinct to the Green function of the wave equation (for example, the Green function
G_{I}
) can lead to another theory different from that of Maxwell. This example shows that charge conservation is a necessary but not a sufficient condition to build Maxwell’s equations. As is well-known, electric charge conservation
has a strong experimental support
[
29
]
. Conceivably, the electron would decay into a photon and a neutrino if the law of electric
charge conservation is violated. A recent test of electric charge conservation with borexino
[
30
]
suggests that the time-decay of the electron is

\tau\geq 6.6\times 10^{28}
yr. This result strongly supports the validity of charge conservation.

As already pointed out, the Green function appearing in equations (3)-(6) can be identified either with its retarded form or with its advanced form.
Consequently, the retarded Green function of the wave equation is not a necessary condition to construct Maxwell’s equations because we can equally choose the advanced Green function. Causality is shown to be a sufficient but not a necessary condition for Maxwell’s equations.

The extension of the above results to the more general case of Maxwell’s equations with magnetic monopoles is straightforward. The fundamental physical axioms behind these generalized equations are the principles of conservation of electric and magnetic charges (represented by their associated continuity equations) and the principle of causality (represented by the infinite-space retarded Green function of the wave equation).

In the derivation of Maxwell’s equations presented here the Lorentz symmetry does not play the role of a fundamental physical axiom for these equations like do charge conservation and causality. In section 3 we have derived Maxwell’s equations in the three-dimensional space without any explicit reference to the Lorentz symmetry.

To finish this section we note that the corollary expressed in equations (7)-(12) is of general validity and can also be applied to other field theories. For example, it can be used to find a set of gravitational field equations. Let us specialize in SI units and write

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\chi=1,\;{\bf F}_{1}={\bf g},\;{\bf F}_{2}={\bf k}c^{2},\;\rho_{1}=-4\pi\mathfrak{S}\rho_{\textsc{m}},\;\rho_{2}=0,\;{\bf J}_{1}=-4\pi\mathfrak{S}{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}},\;{\bf J}_{2}=0,

(107)

where
\rho_{\textsc{m}}
and
{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}}
are the mass density and the mass current density. These densities are assumed to satisfy the continuity equation
\nabla\cdot{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}}+\partial\rho_{\textsc{m}}/\partial t=0
. The time-dependent vector fields
{\bf g}
and
{\bf k}
are respectively the gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic fields with
\mathfrak{S}
being the universal gravitational constant. Using the identifications given in equation (107) we can see that equations (7) and (8) yield

\displaystyle{\bf g}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}\!\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\Bigg(\nabla^{\prime}\!\rho_{\textsc{m}}\!+\!\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}}}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg),

(108)

\displaystyle{\bf K}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathfrak{S}}{c^{2}}\!\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\nabla^{\prime}\times{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}},

(109)

and equations (9)-(12) become

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf g}=-4\pi\mathfrak{S}\rho_{\textsc{m}},

(110)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf k}=0,

(111)

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf k}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial{\bf g}}{\partial t}=-\frac{4\pi\mathfrak{S}}{c^{2}}{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}},

(112)

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf g}+\frac{\partial{\bf k}}{\partial t}=0.

(113)

If we define the gravitational permittivity constant of vacuum as
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}=1/(4\pi\mathfrak{S})
and the gravitational permeability constant of vacuum as
\tilde{\mu}_{0}=4\pi\mathfrak{S}/c^{2}
then
\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}\tilde{\mu}_{0}=1/c^{2}
. Therefore equations (110) and (112) take a form resembling
the Gauss and Amperé-Maxwell laws:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf g}=-\rho_{\textsc{m}}/\tilde{\epsilon}_{0},

(114)

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf k}-\tilde{\epsilon}_{0}\tilde{\mu}_{0}\frac{\partial{\bf g}}{\partial t}=-\tilde{\mu}_{0}{\bf J}_{\textsc{m}}.

(115)

The existence of the gravitomagnetic vector field was first suggested by Heaviside
[
20
]
who essentially introduced equations (110)-(113).
Jefimenko
[
21
]
and McDonald
[
22
]
have also discussed equations (110)-(113).

7
Pedagogical ingredient: Elementary introduction to the retarded Green function

The Green’s function of the wave equation is not typically discussed in undergraduate books of electromagnetism. The introduction of this function is usually delayed until the graduate level and therefore the axiomatic approach to Maxwell’s equations suggested here, which makes an extensive use of the infinity-space retarded Green function of the wave equation, turns out to be suitable for a graduate course of electrodynamics. However, we have suggested elsewhere
[
31
]
that this retarded Green function could be introduced early by means of a non-rigourous and heuristic procedure at undergraduate level. Accordingly, the three-dimensional axiomatic derivation proposed in this paper could be suitable for an advanced undergraduate course of electrodynamics. For completeness of this paper, we are going to briefly discuss this informal treatment of the retarded Green function of the wave equation.

As is well-known the function
1/|{\bf x}\!-\!{\bf x}^{\prime}|\!\equiv\!1/R
satisfies the identity
\nabla^{2}(1/R)\!=\!-4\pi\delta({\bf x}\!-\!{\bf x}^{\prime})
. If we multiply this last equation by
\delta(t-t^{\prime})
then we obtain

\nabla^{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta(t-t^{\prime})}{R}\bigg]=-4\pi\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(116)

We define the infinity-space Green function
G_{I}\equiv G_{I}({\bf x},t;{\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
of the Poisson equation by

G_{I}=\frac{\delta(t\!-\!t^{\prime})}{R}.

(117)

This means that the solution of the Poisson equation

\nabla^{2}F({\bf x},t)=-4\pi f({\bf x},t).

(118)

can be written as

F({\bf x},t)=\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G_{I}f({\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

(119)

According to this solution the value of the field
F
at the point
{\bf x}
at the time
t
is related to the values of the source
F
at the point
{\bf x}^{\prime}
at the instant time
t^{\prime}
in such a way that
t=t^{\prime}
, i.e., after time integration equation (119) becomes

F({\bf x},t)=\int\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}\frac{f({\bf x}^{\prime},t)}{R}.

(120)

The function
G_{I}
can be called the instantaneous form of the Green function for the Poisson equation because it exhibits an acausal behavior. The argument of the delta function within
G_{I}
shows that an effect observed at the point
{\bf x}
at the time
t
is connected by the action of a source a distance
R
away at the point
{\bf x}^{\prime}
at the same instant of time
t^{\prime}=t
. Stated in other words: in an instantaneous interaction the time of the field is the same as the time of its source:
t^{\prime}=t
.

But instantaneous interactions violate the principle of causality which states that causes (sources) precede in time to their effects (fields), i.e., the time of the source is previous to the time of the field:
t^{\prime}\!<\!t
. Therefore, causality demands to modify the Green function
G_{I}
in such a way that the modified Green function
G
exhibits a retarded behavior. Accordingly, the argument of the delta function within
G
must show that an effect observed at the point
{\bf x}
at the time
t
is caused by the action of a source a distance
R
away at the point
{\bf x}^{\prime}
at the
retarded time
t^{\prime}\!=\!t\!-t_{0}
, where
t_{0}\!>\!0
is the time required for the carrier of the interaction to travel the distance
R
between the point
{\bf x}^{\prime}
and the point
{\bf x}
. The carrier of the electromagnetic interaction is the photon which moves with the speed of light
c
in vacuum, which implies
t_{0}\!=\!R/c
and consequently the retarded time takes the form
t^{\prime}\!=t\!-\!R/c
. Put differently, causality demands the change
\delta(t\!-\!t^{\prime})\to\delta(t\!-\!t^{\prime}\!+\!R/c)
, or equivalently, the change

G_{I}=\frac{\delta(t-\!t^{\prime})}{R}\longrightarrow G=\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c)}{R},

(121)

where we have used the property
\delta(u)=\delta(-u)
. The final step is to verify that the infinite-space retarded Green function satisfies the wave equation

\nabla^{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c)}{R}\bigg]-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\bigg[\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c)}{R}\bigg]=-4\pi\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(122)

Let us write
u\!=t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c
. Thus
G\!=\!\delta(u)/R.
Now we calculate the Laplacian of
G
:

\nabla^{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta(u)}{R}\bigg]=\delta(u)\nabla^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{R}\bigg)+2\nabla\delta(u)\cdot\nabla\bigg[\frac{1}{R}\bigg]+\frac{1}{R}\nabla^{2}\delta(u).

(123)

Using the following results
[
18
]
:

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla^{2}\bigg[\!\frac{1}{R}\!\bigg]\!=\!-4\pi\delta({\bf x}\!-\!{\bf x}^{\prime}),\;\nabla\delta(u)\!=\!-\frac{\hat{{\bf R}}}{c}\frac{\partial\delta(u)}{\partial t},\;\nabla\bigg[\!\frac{1}{R}\!\bigg]\!=\!-\frac{\hat{{\bf R}}}{R^{2}},\;\nabla^{2}\delta(u)\!=\!\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\delta(u)}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{2}{Rc}\frac{\partial\delta(u)}{\partial t},

(124)

where
\hat{{\bf R}}={\bf R}/R\equiv({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})/|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|
, equation (123) becomes

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla^{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c)}{R}\bigg]=-4\pi\delta({\bf x}\!-\!{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t\!-t^{\prime}\!-R/c)+\frac{1}{c^{2}R}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\delta(t^{\prime}\!-\!t+R/c),

(125)

which is essentially equation (122). A more formal verification that
G\!=\!\delta(u)/R
satisfies equation (122) has been given by Rowe
[
32
]
.

8
Conclusion

The idea of looking for an axiomatization of physics was suggested by Hilbert
[
33
]
in 1900. The 6th problem of his celebrated list of problems consists in treating “…by means of axioms those physical sciences in which mathematics plays an important part.” A satisfactory answer to this problem was given by Einstein in his special relativity. Partial answers to the Hilbert’s 6th problem have been suggested in the cases of quantum mechanics and general relativity (see for example references
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
). But in the case of classical electrodynamics, a branch of physics where mathematics is extremely important, a convincing axiomatization of Maxwell’s equations is still pending (see reference
[
3
]
for an attempt).

Here we have suggested an axiomatization of Maxwell’s equations including magnetic charges as well as electric ones. Our starting point was
a theorem formulated for two sets of functions localized in space and time. If each set satisfies a continuity equation then the theorem provides an integro-differential representation for the involved functions in terms of their derivatives and the retarded Green function of the wave equation. A corollary of this theorem led us to Maxwell’s equations with electric and magnetic charges.
We have then concluded that the principles of conservation of electric and magnetic charges and the principle of causality are the basic postulates underlying Maxwell’s generalized equations. We have also expressed our results in a manifestly covariant form.

For the standard case of Maxwell’s equations with only electric charges, we have again emphasized
[
18
,
19
]
that causality and charge conservation are the most fundamental axioms underlying these equations.
Finally, it all boils down to saying that charges are conserved and produce causal effects. This is the essential content of Maxwell’s equations.

Appendix A
Derivation of equations (26)-(28)

Consider the localized quantity
{\cal F}\!=\!{\cal F}({\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
and the infinity-space Green function of the wave equation
G\!=\!G({\bf x},t;{\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
. Using the identities
\nabla^{\prime}\diamond(G{\cal F})\!=\!G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}+{\cal F}\diamond\nabla^{\prime}G
and
\nabla\diamond(G{\cal F})={\cal F}\diamond\nabla G
and the property

\nabla G=-\nabla^{\prime}G
, we can obtain the relation

\displaystyle G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}=\nabla\diamond(G{\cal F})+\nabla^{\prime}\diamond(G{\cal F}).

(126)

We integrate equation (A.1) over space and time,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}=\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}\nabla\diamond(G{\cal F})+\int\!dt^{\prime}\Bigg[\!\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}\diamond(G{\cal F})\Bigg].

(127)

We extract the operator
\nabla\diamond
from the first integral on the right-hand side and transform the volume integral in the second term into a surface integral

\displaystyle\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}=\nabla\diamond\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G{\cal F}+\int\!dt^{\prime}\Bigg[\oint d{\bf S}^{\prime}\diamond(G{\cal F})\Bigg].

(128)

The surface integral in equation (A.3) vanishes at spatial infinity because
{\cal F}
is assumed to be spatially localized. Therefore equation (A.3) reduces to equation (26). Similarly, the identities
\partial(G{\cal F})/\partial t^{\prime}\!=\!(\partial G/\partial t^{\prime}){\cal F}+G(\partial{\cal F}/\partial t^{\prime})
and
\partial(G{\cal F})/\partial t\!=\!(\partial G/\partial t^{\prime}){\cal F}
and the property
\partial G/\partial t=-\partial G/\partial t^{\prime}
yield the relation

\displaystyle G\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial(G{\cal F})}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial(G{\cal F})}{\partial t^{\prime}}.

(129)

We integrate this equation over space and time,

\displaystyle\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}=\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial(G{\cal F})}{\partial t}+\int\!d^{3}x^{\prime}\Bigg[\int dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial(G{\cal F})}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg].

(130)

We extract the operator
\partial/\partial t
from the first term on the right-hand side and perform the time integration in the second term, obtaining

\displaystyle\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G{\cal F}+\int\!d^{3}x\big[G{\cal F}\big]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}.

(131)

The second term on the right-hand side vanishes because
{\cal F}
is bounded in time and
G=0
at
t^{\prime}\pm\infty
. Thus equation (A.6) reduces to equation (27). By a direct calculation we can verify the following identity

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg(\frac{{\cal F}}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial G}{\partial t^{\prime}}-\frac{G}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg)+\nabla^{\prime}\diamond\big(G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}-{\cal F}\diamond\nabla^{\prime}G\big)=G\Box^{\prime 2}{\cal F}-{\cal F}\Box^{\prime 2}G.

(132)

Time and space integration of this identity leads to

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}G\Box^{\prime 2}{\cal F}

\displaystyle=\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}{\cal F}\Box^{\prime 2}G+\!\int\!d^{3}x^{\prime}\Bigg[\int\!dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg(\frac{{\cal F}}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial G}{\partial t^{\prime}}-\frac{G}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg)\Bigg]

(133)

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\int\!dt^{\prime}\Bigg[\int\!d^{3}x^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}\diamond\big(G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}-{\cal F}\diamond\nabla^{\prime}G\big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\!\iint\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}dt^{\prime}{\cal F}\Box^{\prime 2}G\!+\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}x^{\prime}\Bigg[\frac{{\cal F}}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial G}{\partial t^{\prime}}\!-\!\frac{G}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t^{\prime}}\Bigg]_{-\infty}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\!\int\!\!dt^{\prime}\Bigg[\oint d{\bf S}^{\prime}\diamond\big(G\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}\!-\!{\cal F}\diamond\nabla^{\prime}G\big)\Bigg].

The second term on the right-hand side vanishes because
{\cal F}
and
\partial{\cal F}/\partial t
are bounded
in time and both
G=0
and
\partial G/\partial t^{\prime}=0
at
t^{\prime}\pm\infty
. The third term on the right-hand side vanishes at spatial infinity because
{\cal F}
and
\nabla^{\prime}\diamond{\cal F}
are spatially localized. Therefore, equation (A.8) reduces to the first equality of equation (28). The second equality in equation (28) follows from
substituting
\Box^{\prime 2}G=-4\pi\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})
in the first equality and performing the corresponding integration over all space and all time.

Appendix B
Derivation of equations (82) and (83)

Consider the generic tensor
{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}(x^{\prime})

D\!=\!D(x,x^{\prime})

\partial^{\mu}D=-\partial^{\prime\mu}D

\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}(D{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...})=\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}D{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}+D\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}

(134)

Integration of this identity over all space-time reads

\displaystyle\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}

(135)

\partial_{\mu}
from the first integral and transform the second term into a surface integral

\displaystyle\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}=\partial_{\mu}\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}+\oint dS^{\prime}_{\mu}D{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}.

(136)

The surface integral in equation (B.3) vanishes at the space-time infinity because
{\cal F}^{\mu\alpha...}
is localized in space-time. Therefore equation (B.3) reduces to equation (82). A direct calculation allows us to show the identity

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\big(\partial^{\prime\mu}D{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}-D\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\big)={\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}D-D\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}.

(137)

Integration of equation (B.4) over all space-time yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}D\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}=\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}D-\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\big(\partial^{\prime\mu}D{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}-D\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\int\!d^{4}x^{\prime}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}D-\oint dS^{\prime}_{\mu}\big(\partial^{\prime\mu}D{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}-D\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}\big).

(138)

The surface integral vanishes at the space-time infinity because
{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}
and
\partial^{\prime\mu}{\cal F}^{\alpha\beta...}
are localized in space-time and therefore equation (B.5) reduces to the first equality of equation (83). The second equality in equation (83) follows from
the first one by inserting the equation
\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}\partial^{\prime\mu}D=\delta^{(4)}(x-x^{\prime})
and integrating over all space-time.

Gödel-type universes in energy-momentum-squared gravity
Á. J. C. Canuto, A. F. Santos

Abstract
In this paper, a modification of general relativity is considered. It consists of generalizing the Lagrangian of matter in a non-linear way, that is, replacing the curvature scalar R \displaystyle R by a function f ⁡ ( R , T μ ​ ν ​ T μ ​ ν ) \displaystyle f(R,T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}) , where T μ ​ ν \displaystyle T_{\mu\nu} is the energy-momentum tensor. The main objective is to investigate the issue of causality in this gravitational model. To study the causality and/or its violation the Gödel-type solutions are used. For such development, different matter contents are chosen. A critical radius, beyond which causality is violated, is calculated. It is shown that both causal and non-causal solutions are allowed.

III
Gödel metric in
\displaystyle f(R,T^{2})

The Gödel metric was proposed by mathematician and logician Kurt Gödel in 1949
K.Godel
. It is an exact solution of the field equations of general relativity that allows the existence of Closed Time-like Curves (CTCs). This means that an observer moving on a CTC can find a point in the future that is also his starting point in the past. It is worth noting that the existence of CTCs in the Gödel metric is considered paradoxical, as it allows past and future events to interact. However, the exact solution of the Gödel metric is interesting from a theoretical point of view and has been studied in several areas of physics and mathematics. The line element that describes this universe is given as

ds^{2}=a^{2}\left(dt^{2}-dx^{2}+\frac{e^{2x}}{2}dy^{2}-dz^{2}+2e^{x}dtdy\right),

(12)

T_{(0)(0)}^{(S)}=-T_{(1)(1)}^{(S)}=-T_{(2)(2)}^{(S)}=T_{(3)(3)}^{(S)}=\frac{e^{2}}{2}.

(63)

The trace of the total energy-momentum tensor is

T=T_{AB}^{(M)}\eta^{AB}+T_{AB}^{(S)}\eta^{AB}=\rho-3p+e^{2}.

(64)

Considering that the Lagrangian describing the scalar field is given by

\mathcal{L}_{m}^{S}=\eta^{MN}\nabla_{M}\phi\nabla_{N}\phi,

(65)

the total tensor
\displaystyle\Theta_{AB}
can be written as

\Theta_{AB}=\Theta_{AB}^{(M)}+\Theta_{AB}^{(S)},

(66)

where
\displaystyle\Theta_{AB}^{(M)}
and
\displaystyle\Theta_{AB}^{(S)}
are the parts associated with the perfect fluid and the scalar field, respectively.

Taking these ingredients, the field equations are given as

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2f_{R}\left(3\omega^{2}-m^{2}\right)+f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa\left(\rho+3p\right)+f_{T^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}-p^{2}\right),

(67)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2f_{R}\left(\omega^{2}\right)-f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa\left(\rho-p\right)+f_{T^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}-p^{2}\right),

(68)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2f_{R}\left(m^{2}-\omega^{2}\right)-f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa\left(\rho-p\right)+2\kappa e^{2}+f_{T^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}-p^{2}\right).

(69)

This set of equations can be reduced to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa e^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{R}(m^{2}-2\omega^{2}),

(70)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa p

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{R}(2\omega^{2}-m^{2})+\frac{f}{2},

(71)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\kappa\rho+f_{T^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}-p^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{R}(6\omega^{2}-m^{2})-\frac{f}{2}.

(72)

\displaystyle\displaystyle m^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 4\omega^{2},

(73)

\displaystyle\displaystyle f_{R}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\kappa e^{2}}{2\omega^{2}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\kappa\rho+f_{T^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}-p^{2}\right)=-\omega^{2}f_{R}+\frac{f}{2}.

(75)

It is important to emphasize that the condition

\displaystyle r_{c}\rightarrow\infty

V
Conclusion

The energy-momentum-squared gravity has been considered. This is a class of theories that generalize GR by adding higher order terms of the form
\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
to the matter Lagrangian. In this context, the Gödel and Gödel-type solutions have been used to discuss the issue of causality and its violation. For a fluid perfect as the content of matter, it has been shown that the Gödel metric is the solution of
\displaystyle f(R,T^{2})
gravity for specific values of the energy density
\displaystyle\rho
and the cosmological constant
\displaystyle\Lambda
. This result leads to a violation of causality. At an appropriate limit, the GR results are recovered. For more details about this violation, the Gödel-type is introduced. For simplicity, a local frame is used. In this context, different contents of matter are chosen. Using a perfect fluid as the matter content, the solution of the field equations shows that the violation of causality arises naturally. Here a finite critical radius, which explicitly depends on the function
\displaystyle f(R,T^{2})
and of matter content, is calculated. The next investigation changes the matter content, now a perfect fluid plus a scalar field is considered. Our results lead to an infinite critical radius, therefore violation of causality is avoided. A similar result is obtained when only one scalar field is taken as matter content. Therefore, the energy-momentum-squared gravity allows Gödel-types solutions, and as a consequence, both causal and non-causal regions emerge for different matter contents. Furthermore, the study developed in this paper is a generalization of the results obtained for
\displaystyle f(R)
and
\displaystyle f(R,T)
theories. In addition, it is important to note that when the matter term is switched off, our results recover the results obtained by
\displaystyle f(R)
gravity
rebouccas2009godel
. In this case, the definition of causal and non-causal regions (which are given by the critical radius) continues to depend on the matter content that is introduced through the energy-momentum tensor.

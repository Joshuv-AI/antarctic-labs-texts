Abstract
Counting ad infinitum is the holographic observable to a statistical dynamics with finite states under independent repeated sampling. Entropy provides the infinitesimal probability for an observed frequency 𝝂 ^ \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}} w.r.t. a probability prior 𝐩 {\bf p} . Following Callen’s postulate and through Legendre-Fenchel transform, without help from mechanics, we show an internal energy 𝝁 \boldsymbol{\mu} emerges; it provides a linear representation of real-valued observables with full or partial information. Gibbs’ fundamental thermodynamic relation and theory of ensembles follow mathematically. 𝝁 \boldsymbol{\mu} is to 𝝂 ^ \hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}} what ω \omega is to t t in Fourier analysis.

probability simplex

\mathscr{M}_{n}
; Eq. (
20
) then becomes

\tilde{P}^{(N+1)}\big(\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}\tilde{P}^{(N)}\Big(\big\{\tfrac{N+1}{N}\hat{\nu}_{i}-\tfrac{1}{N}\delta_{ik}\big\}\Big).

(21)

Its limit is a Dirac-
\delta
function:
\tilde{P}^{(\infty)}=0
for all
\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\neq{\bf p}
, and
\tilde{P}^{(\infty)}=\infty
at
\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}={\bf p}
. However, “a higher order” infinitesimal analysis shows that
Dembo and Zeitouni 1998
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\tilde{P}^{(N)}\big(\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big)=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\hat{\nu}_{i}\ln\left(\frac{\hat{\nu}_{i}}{p_{i}}\right)=-\Phi\big(\hat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big).

(22)

It is clear that entropy function
-\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu})
represents the infinitesimal prior probability
e^{-N\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu})}
on
\mathscr{M}_{n}
. For two
\boldsymbol{\nu}
’s with different entropy values,
\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu})
and
\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\prime})
, their probabilities
P^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\nu})/P^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\prime})=0
if
\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu})>\Phi(\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\prime})
. This is the origin of the maximum entropy principle (MEP).

To understand the limit
P^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\to 0
, one can also introduce the probability generating function
Dembo and Zeitouni 1998
:

W^{(N)}\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}\big)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}P^{(N)}\big(\boldsymbol{\nu}\big)e^{-\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}},

(23)

in which
\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}=\mu_{0}\nu_{0}+\cdots+\mu_{n}\nu_{n}
. Then Eq. (
20
) becomes

\displaystyle W^{(N+1)}\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}\big)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}P^{(N+1)}\big(\boldsymbol{\nu}\big)e^{-\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}e^{-\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}P^{(N)}\big(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k}\big)e^{-\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W^{(N)}\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}\big)e^{-\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mu})},

\displaystyle\text{where }\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mu})=-\ln\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}e^{-\mu_{k}}.

The free energy function
\Psi
is meaningful for all finite
N
. This is why the partition function is valid even for small systems in Gibbs’ theory of ensembles
Lu and Qian 2022
. The Legendre-Fenchel transform of
\Psi(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is precisely the the right-hand-side of (
22
):

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\Big\{\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}-\Psi\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}\big)\Big\}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\nu_{i}\ln e^{-\mu_{i}}+\ln\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}e^{-\mu_{k}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\left\{-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\nu_{i}\ln\left[\frac{e^{-\mu_{i}}}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}e^{-\mu_{k}}}\right]\right\}=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\nu_{i}\ln\frac{\hat{\nu}_{i}}{p_{i}},

in which the optimal
e^{-\mu_{i}}\propto\nu_{i}/p_{i}
. Legendre-Frenchel transform arises in the limit of
N\to\infty
through the Laplace’s method of evaluating asymptotic integrals, or the related Darwin-Fowler method of maximum term.

Singularities of Bridgeland moduli spaces for K3 categories: an update
Enrico Arbarello and Giulia Saccà

Abstract
. This survey is a continuation of the study undertaken in [ AS18 ] . We examine the local structure of Bridgeland moduli spaces M σ ​ ( v , 𝒟 ) M_{\sigma}(v,\mathcal{D}) , where the relevant triangulated category 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is either the bounded derived category 𝒟 = 𝒟 b ​ ( X ) \mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{b}(X) of a K3 surface X X , or the Kuznetsov component 𝒟 = Ku ⁡ ( Y ) ⊂ 𝒟 b ​ ( Y ) \mathcal{D}=\operatorname{Ku}(Y)\subset\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y) of a smooth cubic fourfold Y ⊂ ℙ 5 Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{5} . For these moduli spaces, building on [ BMM19 ] , [ BMM21 ] we give a direct proof of formality and, using their local isomorphism with quiver varieties, we establish their normality and their irreducibility, as long as σ \sigma does not lie on a totally semistable wall. We then connect the variation of GIT quotients for quiver varieties with the changing of stability conditions on moduli spaces.



Introduction.

Let

\mathcal{D}
denote either the bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
of a K3 surface
X
, or the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{D}=\operatorname{Ku}(Y)\subset\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
of a smooth cubic fourfold
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
.
The aim of this survey is to give an account of various developments in the local analysis of moduli spaces of objects in
\mathcal{D}
. This analysis started with the seminal work of Kaledin, Lehn and Sorger
[
KLS06
]
,
[
KL07
]
. In
[
AS18
]
we followed the path they traced, we introduced the so-called ext-quiver, and we showed, among other things, that the variation of GIT quotients for quiver varieties governs the (partial) resolution of singularities of the moduli spaces
M_{H}(v,X)
of pure dimension one sheaves when they undergo a change of the polarization
H\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
. There are two constituents to that local analysis: Luna’s étale slice theorem for the Quot scheme and the quadraticity (or, even strongly, the formality) of the Kuranishi family. The first provides the étale local picture of moduli spaces of sheaves on a K3 surface, obtained as global quotient of the Quot scheme; the second builds the bridge between this local picture and the ext-quiver. For the case of sheaves on a K3 surface, the formality theorem is the result of several contributions (see Section
3
for references) and was finally established by Bandiera, Manetti and Meazzini
[
BMM19
]
, and
[
BMM21
]
(see also
[
BZ19
]
).
The picture changes substantially when passing to Bridgeland moduli spaces. Let
M_{\sigma}(v,\mathcal{D})

be the moduli space of
\sigma
-semistable objects with Mukai vector
v
, with respect to a Bridgeland stability condition
\sigma
on
\mathcal{D}
.
These spaces are not constructed as a GIT quotients of a Quot scheme. Indeed, their construction, in full generality, that is for arbitrary
\sigma
and
v
, has only been accomplished rather recently through the groundbreaking work by Alper, Hall, Rhyd, Halpern-Leistner, Heinloth and Bayer, Lahoz, Macrì, Nuer, Perry, and Stellari
[
BLM+19
]
. In Section
2
we ease into an overview of these fundamental ideas. Regarding the formality of the Kuranishi family for Bridgeland moduli spaces,
we will give direct and rather simple proofs based on the work by Bandiera, Meazzini, and Manetti
[
BMM19
]
for the K3 case,
and on ideas of Kaledin
[
Ka07
]
and Lunts
[
Lunts08
]
for the Kuznetsov case. We should mention that a very general statement of formality for 2-Calabi Yau categories (and therefore including the two cases we just mentioned) has been proved by Davison in
[
Dav21
]
. In Section
5
we address the question of normality for Bridgeland moduli spaces. Normality has been previously established, under the hypothesis that the stability condition
\sigma
be
v
-generic, in
[
BMm14
, Theorem 3.10]
for the K3 case and
[
Sa23
, Corollary 2.4]
for the case of the Kuznetsov component. When
\sigma
lies on a
v
-wall, the situation is more complicated. However, we succeed in proving that

M_{\sigma}(v,\mathcal{D})
is normal, even when
\sigma
lies on a
v
-wall, as long as
\sigma
does not lie on a totally semistable wall. In the proof of this theorem we take full advantage of the local ext-quiver description of moduli spaces. A direct consequence of the normality result is the irreducibility of
M_{\sigma}(v,\mathcal{D})
, again as long as
\sigma
does not lie on a totally semistable wall.
In Section
6
, we proceed to develop the local analysis of Bridgeland moduli spaces by interpreting the passage of a stability condition from a given stratum of a wall to a shallower one, in terms of variation of GIT quotients for the ext-quiver variety (see also
[
Hal21
]
). In this regard, the context provided
by the above mentioned paper
[
AHLH22
]
makes the treatment much simpler than the one
we described in the case of sheaves in
[
AS18
]
.

We will study

In the

We recall that a

\mathcal{D}
, consists in a pair

A stability function should satisfy the following property:

0\neq E\in\mathcal{A}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad Z(E)=Z(v(E))\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}e^{i\pi\phi(E)}\,,\qquad 0<\phi(E)\leq 1

The number
\phi(E)

of

. The

\nu(E):=\Re Z(E))/\mathcal{I}mZ(E)

\phi(E)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mbox{arccot}(-\nu(E))

An object

-

with larger phase. A (numerical) stability

admits a filtration

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\cdots\subset E_{n-1}\subset E_{n}=E

such that
E_{j}/E_{j-1}
is
\sigma
- semistable, and the sequence of phases
\{\phi(E_{j}/E_{j-1})\}

is strictly decreasing. Finally a stability condition should satisfies the
support property
:
\exists
a norm
||\cdot||
on
\Lambda
, such that

|Z(v(E))|\geq c||v(E)||
,
for every
\sigma
-semistable
E

on
\Lambda
e.g.
[
MS17
, Section 4]
.

Bridgeland endows the set
\operatorname{Stab}(X)
of stability conditions on
X
with a topology making

\operatorname{Stab}(X)
into a complex manifold.
Bridgeland’s theorem states that
the natural projection

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Stab}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)\quad\mapsto\quad Z

is a local homeomorphism onto its image.
By definition, given a numerical stability condition
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
, there exists

\Omega_{\sigma}\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(1.2)

Z(E)=\langle\,\Omega_{\sigma},v(E)\,\rangle:=-\chi(\Omega_{\sigma},v(E))

It is also convenient to introduce the class
\omega_{\sigma}=-\Omega_{\sigma}^{\vee}

so that

(1.3)

Z(E)=\chi(\omega_{\sigma}\otimes E)

It is useful to pass from a given stability condition to an ”equivalent” one
where computations might be easier. To do so, one sees a stability condition
\sigma
, as a pair
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)
, where
Z:K_{0}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathbb{C}
is a homomorphism, and where
\mathcal{P}
is a
slicing
of
\mathcal{D}
, i.e. a collection of full (a posteriori abelian) subcategories
\{\mathcal{P}(\phi)\subset\mathcal{D}\}
, one
for each
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfying a certain number of properties
[
Br08
]
, Definition 2.1 p.246]. The objects of
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
are said to be
\sigma
-semistable of phase
\phi
, and for
E\in\mathcal{P}(\phi)
one has
Z(E)=Z(v(E))\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}e^{i\pi\phi(E)}
. The heart
\mathcal{A}
can be recovered as the subcategory
\mathcal{P}(0,1]
of objects whose Harder-Narashiman factors have phases
lying in the interval
(0,1]
.
The universal cover
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
of
\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
acts on
\operatorname{Stab}(X)

in the following way. Think of an element
\widetilde{g}\in\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})

, where

, and

is an increasing function s.t.

Then

Finally we will always restrict Bridgeland’s local homeomorphism (

2.
Moduli stacks and moduli spaces

2.1.
The classical picture

Let us briefly collect the ingredients that go into the construction of the
classical moduli spaces of sheaves on a K3 surface

X
.

1)
First we need a Mukai vector
v\in H^{*}_{alg}(X,\mathbb{Z})

and polarization
H\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
.
These two data determine, and are determined by, the Hilbert polynomial of
F
:

P_{H}(F)(t):=\chi(F\otimes H^{t})=\alpha_{d}(F)t^{d}+\cdots+\alpha_{0}(F)\,,\qquad 2\geq d=\dim\operatorname{Supp}(F)

which in turn defines the notion of
H
-
Gieseker semistability
:
F
is semistable
if for
E\subset F
, then
p_{H}(E)\leq p_{H}(F)
, where
p_{H}(-):=P_{H}(-)/\alpha_{d}(-)

is the
reduced Hilbert polynomial
and where the order is lexicographical.

For a fixed rational polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
, the Quot scheme
Q_{H,P}
parametrizes equivalence classes of surjections

q_{F}:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-m)\twoheadrightarrow F
, where
P_{H}(F)=P
, where
V
is a vector space isomorphic to
H^{0}(F(m))
, for some large
m
, and where
q_{F}\sim q_{F^{\prime}}
, if
q_{F}=\alpha q_{F^{\prime}}
, for some isomorphism
\alpha:F\cong F^{\prime}
.

2)
Let
Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}
be the open subset of the quot-scheme
Q_{H,P}
whose points

[q_{F}:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-m)\twoheadrightarrow F]
are such that

a)
F
is
H
-Gieseker-semistable, and

b)
V\cong H^{0}(F(m))
.

The general linear group
\operatorname{GL}(V)
acts naturally on
Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}
.
The Moduli space of
H
–Gieseker semistable sheaves with Mukai vector equal to
v

is defined as

(2.1)

M_{H}(v):=Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}/GL(V)

To make any sense out of this quotient one needs the GIT arsenal, and the first ingredient
is a
\operatorname{GL}(V)
-linearized line bundle
L
on
Q_{H,P}
. This line bundle is provided by the determinal line bundle

(2.2)

L_{H}=\det(p_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes q^{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(\ell H))

where
\mathcal{F}
is a universal family over
Q_{H,P}\times X
, while
p
and
q
are the
natural projections, and
\ell
is a fixed large integer.
It now make sense to talk about

\operatorname{GL}(V)
-semistable points with respect to
L_{H}
, or briefly
L_{H}
-semistable points.
It is only for these points that a GIT quotient makes sense.

3)
Thus the fundamental step in giving sense to the quotient (
2.1
) is to show that points in
{Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}}
are also

L_{H}
-GIT-semistable. But more is true:

(2.3)

Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}=\left(\overline{Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}}\right)^{{(L_{H}\text{-}GIT})_{ss}}\subset Q^{{(L_{H}\text{-}GIT})_{ss}}

To decide wether a point

(2.4)

x=[q:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-m)\twoheadrightarrow F]\in\overline{Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}}

is
L_{H}
-GIT-semistable, one uses the Hilbert-Mumford criterion, and looks at one-parameter subgroups
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
. For each such, one looks at
the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)x=x_{0}
, exhibiting
\mathbb{G}_{m}
as a subgroup of
G_{x_{0}}:=\operatorname{Stab}(x_{0})
. Thus,
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts, via
\lambda
, on the fiber
(L_{H})_{x_{0}}
. Via the
\operatorname{GL}(V)
-linearization of
L_{H}
, one can write the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on that fiber as

\lambda(t)v=t^{r}v
. Setting
r=\mu^{L_{H}}(x,\lambda)
, the criterion says
x
is
L_{H}
-semistable if and only if
\mu^{L_{H}}(x,\lambda)\geq 0
, for every
\lambda
.
To prove (
2.3
), one needs a bridge between
H
-Gieseker-semistability (computed in terms of Hilbert polynomials) and
L
-semistability. This bridge is provided by expressing the invariant
\mu^{L}(x,\lambda)
in terms of Hilbert-polynomials. It goes as follows. Fix a point
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{Q_{0}^{(H\text{-}Gies)_{ss}}}
as in (
2.4
).
A one-parameter group
\lambda
determines
a decomposition of
V
into
weight subspaces
, and consequently a decreasing filtration
\{V^{w}\}
for
V
, and, via
q
, a decreasing filtration for
F
:

(2.5)

F^{\bullet}:=q(V^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-m))):\qquad\qquad\cdots\subseteq F^{w+1}\subseteq F^{w}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F

The associated graded pieces of
\{F^{w}\}
are denoted by
F_{w}
. The formula, bridging Gieseker stability with GIT stability, and expressing

\mu^{L_{H}}(x,\lambda)
in terms of Hilbert polynomials is the following (recall the definition (
2.2
)):

(2.6)

\mu^{L_{H}}(x,\lambda)=\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}wP_{H,F_{w}}(\ell)\,.

From this, the equality (
2.3
) follows, after some highly non-trivial work.

A notational remark: the datum of a one-parameter subgroup through the point

2.3.
Good moduli spaces, and the slice étale

Alper
[
Alp13
]
, following the footsteps of
[
AOV08
]
, introduces an amazingly simple notion of
good moduli space
:

(2.8)

A quasi compact morphism
\phi:\mathcal{Y}\to Y
from a (locally noetherian) Artin stack to an algebraic space is a good moduli space if:
(1)
The pushforward functor on quasi-coherent sheaves is exact (in the locally noetherian case, coherent sheaves are sufficient).
(2)
The induced morphism
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\phi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is an isomorphism

The simplest example of a good moduli space is provided by a linearly reductive group
G
acting linearly on a projective scheme
X
. Then the natural morphism
from the stack quotient to the GIT quotient :
[X/G]\to X/\mkern-6.0mu/G
is a good moduli space for
[X/G]
. In the next section we will see the central role played by the stack
[\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
, in the entire moduli theory (in our case
\mathbb{G}_{m}=\mathbb{C}^{*}
).

A good moduli space
\phi:\mathcal{Y}\to Y
enjoys all the relevant properties of a GIT quotient.
For example (but look at the complete list in
[
Alp13
, p. 2351]
):

i)
\phi
is surjective and universally closed, so that
Y
has the quotient topology
(via the induced map
|\mathcal{Y}|\to Y
).

ii) Given two geometric points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}
, then
\phi(x_{1})=\phi(x_{2})
if and only if

\overline{\{x_{1}\}}\cap\overline{\{x_{2}\}}\neq\emptyset
, (clearly pointing towards
S
-equivalence).

iii)
\phi
is universal for map to algebraic spaces.

Condition
(1)
in the definition of good moduli space, namely the exactness of the functor

\phi_{*}:Q\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal{Y})\to Q\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
is also called
cohomological affiness
. In case
\mathcal{Y}
has affine diagonal it amounts to the vanishing of
R^{i}\phi_{*}\mathcal{F}
, for quasi coherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
,
and for
i>0

[
Alp13
, Remark 3.5, p.2357]
. The proof of part (ii) of Lemma 4.9
[
Alp13
, p.2365]
is enlightening: it shows how cohomological affiness
implies the crucial property ii) above.

Under mild restrictive hypotheses, the local structure of an Artin stack
is governed by the stack version of Luna’s slice étale theorem established by Alper, Hall and Rydh in
[
AHR20
]
. Here are their beautiful results
in
[

Then:

(a)

There exist an affine scheme

2.4.
The ”one-parameter group” probe

In his comprehensive work

2.5.
The notion of
\Theta
-stability for a stack

In the classical construction, the
weight
of a point, with respect to a one-parameter subgroup,
is defined in terms of a linearization of a line bundle over the Quot-scheme
(see point
3)
above). Halpern-Leistner introduces an abstract notion of weight
as a numerical invariant
\mu
assigning, to each filtration
f
, a value
\mu(f)

in a totally ordered set
\Gamma
, equipped with a marked point
0\in\Gamma

(e.g.
\mathbb{Z}
)
[
Hal22
, Section 4.1, p.91]
.

This function should satisfy a number of quite natural requirements
[
Hal22
, Definition 0.0.3 and Definition 4.16]
. A point
x\in|\mathcal{X}|
will be called
\Theta
-unstable
or simply
unstable
if there exists
a filtration
f
, with
f(1)=x
and
\mu(f)>0
. Points that are not unstable are called
\Theta
-semistable
or simply
semistable
, and they are the support of a substack
\mathcal{X}^{ss_{\mu}}\subset\mathcal{X}
. One of the main tasks is
to decide whether the substack
\mathcal{X}^{ss_{\mu}}
is algebraic and whether it admits a good moduli space.

The inspiring ideas for the definition of the numerical function
\mu
originate in the groundbreaking work by Kempf,

[
Ke78
]
, (and especially
[
Ke18
]
!), and also in the works by Hessenlink, Ness and Mumford,
[
Hes79
]
,
[
NM84
]
. As in Kempf’s theory, this numerical function brings with it the notion of
Harder-Narasimhan filtration of a point
x\in|\mathcal{X}|
, as a filtration

f
with

\mu(f)=M^{\mu}(x):=\sup\{\mu(f^{\prime})\,\,|\,\,f^{\prime}\,\,\text{a filtration s.t.}\,\,f^{\prime}(1)=x\in|\mathcal{X}|\}.

In Kempf’s theory this filtration corresponds to the ”fastest” one-parameter group through
x
. It is of course crucial to insure uniqueness of a Harder-Narasimhan filtration. Halpern-Leistner
identifies the correct property that
\mathcal{X}
should satisfy to ensure this uniquiness: he calls it
\Theta
-reductivity
. In simple terms it can be stated as follows: given a discrete valuation domain
R
, and a morphism
T=\operatorname{Spec}R\to\mathcal{X}
, then any filtration on the generic point of

T
extends to a family of filtrations on all of
T

[
Hal22
, Definition 5.11, and Theorem 5.1.9]
.
To better explain this concept one should organize all possible filtrations for a stack
\mathcal{X}
as points of a new stack

\operatorname{Filt}(\mathcal{X})

[
Hal22
, Definition 1.1.11 with
n=1
]
and look at the evaluation morphism
\operatorname{ev}_{1}:\operatorname{Filt}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathcal{X}
, defined by
\operatorname{ev}_{1}(f)=f(1)
. Then the condition of

\Theta
-reductivity
for
\mathcal{X}
is nothing but the evaluative criterion for properness of the morphism
\operatorname{ev}_{1}

[
Hal22
, Definition 5.1.1]
.

Via the totally ordered set
\Gamma
one defines a
set-theoretical
stratification:

|\mathcal{X}|_{\leq c}=\{x\in|\mathcal{X}|\,\,\,\,|\,\,\,\,M^{\mu}(x)\leq c\in\Gamma\}\subset|\mathcal{X}|

When this stratification is the support of a bona fide stratification by open substacks

, then we are in the presence of what Halpern-Leistner calls a (
weak
)

\Theta
-stratification of
\mathcal{X}
,

[
Hal22
, Definition 2.2.1, Theorem 2.2.2, Definition 4.1.1]
.
This happens under two conditions:
\Theta
-reductivity and
”boundedness”

(see
[
Hal22
, Theorem A, item (B)]
.
Of course the semistable stratum in this stratification is given by
\mathcal{X}^{ss_{\mu}}=\mathcal{X}_{\leq 0}
.

2.7.

Alper, Halpern-Leistner, and Heinloth

4.1.
The moment map and semi-stability for quiver representations

Consider a quiver
Q=(I,E)
, and its double
\overline{Q}=(I,E\sqcup E^{op})
.
Having fixed a
dimension vector

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{s}

and vector spaces
V_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,s
, with

\dim V_{i}=n_{i},

one defines the vector space of
\mathbf{n}
-dimensional representations of

Q

by setting

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathbf{n}):=\underset{e\in E}{\bigoplus}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{s(e)},V_{t(e)})

Where
s(e)
, and
t(e)
are the source and the tail of the edge
e
.
There is
an identification

\operatorname{Rep}(\overline{Q},\mathbf{n})=\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathbf{n})\oplus\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathbf{n})^{\vee}

and thus a natural symplectic form on
\operatorname{Rep}(\overline{Q},\mathbf{n})
. The group

G:=G(\mathbf{n}):=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{GL}(n_{i})

acts on
\operatorname{Rep}(\overline{Q},\mathbf{n})
in a natural way via conjugation, and
since this action respects the symplectic form there is a
moment map
given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{n}}:\operatorname{Rep}(\overline{Q},\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\gl(\mathbf{n})\cong\gl(\mathbf{n})^{\vee}

\displaystyle(x,y^{\vee})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mu(x,y^{\vee})=\sum_{e\in E}[x_{e},y_{e}^{\vee}]

Following Marsden-Weinstein symplectic reduction, one is interested in the GIT quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{\chi}(\mathbf{n}):=\mu_{\mathbf{n}}^{-1}(0)\sslash_{\chi}G

where

\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a rational character.

Brief reminder.

Here we
recall that, if
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
, then

X\sslash_{\chi}G=\operatorname{Proj}\left(\underset{n\geq 0}{\oplus}A_{n}\right)

where

A_{n}=\{f\in A\,\,|\,\,(g\cdot f)(x)=\chi(g)^{n}f(x)\}
. Points
x\in X

for which there exists
f\in A_{n}

with
f(x)\neq 0
are said to be
\chi
-semistable
, and they form a subset
X_{\chi}^{ss}\subset X
. Points in
X_{\chi}^{ss}
with closed orbit and finite stabilizers are called
\chi
-stable
, and they form a subset
X_{\chi}^{s}\subset X_{\chi}^{ss}
.
Two
\chi
-semistable points are said to be
S_{\chi}
-equivalent
if the closure of their orbits meet in
X_{\chi}^{ss}
.
The projection

(4.2)

X_{\chi}^{ss}\longrightarrow X\sslash_{\chi}G

establishes a one-to-one correspondence between
the set of closed points in
X_{\chi}^{ss}\longrightarrow X\sslash_{\chi}G
and the set of

S_{\chi}
-equivalence classes in
X_{\chi}^{ss}
. The natural map

\xi:X\sslash_{\chi}G\longrightarrow X\sslash G=\operatorname{Spec}(A^{G})

is often a desingularization.

We are interested in desingularization maps

\mathfrak{M}_{\chi}(\mathbf{n}):=\mu_{\mathbf{n}}^{-1}(0)\sslash_{\chi}G\longrightarrow\mu_{\mathbf{n}}^{-1}(0)\sslash G=\mathfrak{M}_{0}(\mathbf{n})

Moreover we have
\dim\mathfrak{M}_{\chi}(\mathbf{n})=d(\mathbf{n})+2
, where

(4.3)

d(\mathbf{n})={}^{t}\mathbf{n}D\ \mathbf{n}

and
D
is the negative of the Cartan matrix associated to
Q
,

[
AS18
, p. 674]
. In Crawley-Boevey’s notation
[
C-B01
]

(4.4)

\dim\mathfrak{M}_{\chi}(\mathbf{n})=d(\mathbf{n})+2=2p(\mathbf{n})

Rational characters

\chi:G(\mathbf{n})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

are in a one-to-one correspondence with vectors

\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}

via the formula

\chi_{\theta}(g)=\prod\det(g_{i})^{\theta_{i}}

where
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})\in G=G(\mathbf{n})
.

It is an important fact that stability and semi-stability may be expressed in terms of slope.
Consider, in
\mathbb{Z}^{s}
, the orthogonal complement of the dimension vector:

\mathbf{n}^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{s},

and set

\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{n}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{Q}

consider a point (a character)

.
Let

be an
\mathbf{n}
-dimensional representation of
\overline{Q}
. For any sub-representation

the
\theta
-slope of

{\slope}_{\theta}(W)=\frac{\theta\cdot\dim W}{\sum\dim W_{i}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\theta_{i}\dim W_{i}}{\sum\dim W_{i}}

so that, in particular,
\slope_{\theta}(V)=0
. A non-zero representation
V
is said to be
\theta
-semistable
if, for every sub-representation
W
of

, we have
\slope_{\theta}(W)\leq 0
and is said to be
\theta
-stable
, if the strict equality holds for every non-zero, proper sub-representation. A
\theta
-semistable representation admits a
Jordan-Hölder filtration, and
two representations are said to be
S_{\theta}
-equivalent, if their associated, graded, Jordan-Hölder objects are isomorphic.

It is a theorem due to King

that a representation

-semistable if and only if it is

-semistable
(and the same is true for stability), and that

-equivalency coincides with

-equivalency.

Perturbations of principal submodules in the Drury-Arveson space
Mohammad Jabbari, Xiang Tang

Abstract
. We study the geometry in the perturbations of principal submodules in the Drury-Arveson space. We show that the perturbations give rise to smooth vector bundles of Hilbert spaces which are equipped with natural Hermitian connections. We compute the associated parallel transport operators and explore properties of the monodromy.

, we have

\left\|\widetilde{P}_{t+h}-\widetilde{P}_{t}-h\dot{\widetilde{P}}_{t}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{n,r}S(n)\frac{\sum\limits_{Q,q}\omega_{r+kQ,n}^{-1}\omega_{r+kq,n}^{-2}\omega_{r+kq,n+6}\epsilon^{2Q+2q}(Q-q)^{4}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{2!}\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum\limits_{q}\omega_{r+kq,n}^{-1}\epsilon^{2q}\right)^{2}}

\ll\sum\limits_{n,r}S(n)h^{4}\frac{\sum\limits_{q}\omega_{r+kq,n}^{-1}\epsilon^{2q}q^{4}}{\sum\limits_{q}\omega_{r+kq,n}^{-1}\epsilon^{2q}}\ll\sum_{n}S(n)h^{4}n^{4}=h^{4}\sum\limits_{n}n^{-2}.

This finishes the proof that
\widetilde{P}
is first-order differentiable.

(b)
Imitating the proof in Part (a), we set

\widetilde{\omega}_{r+kq,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\|z_{1}^{r+kq}z_{2}^{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{(2l+1+\sigma)}_{2}}=\frac{(r+kq)!n!(2l+3+\sigma)!}{(r+kq+n+2l+3+\sigma)!}=S(n)\omega_{r+kq,n+2l+3+\sigma},

where

S(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{(2l+3+\sigma)!n!}{(n+2l+3+\sigma)!}\approx n^{-2l-3-\sigma}.

Given
j=1,\ldots,l
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
, we have estimates

\left\|\frac{d^{j}\widetilde{P}_{t}}{dt^{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\ll\sum\limits_{n}S(n)n^{2j}\approx\sum\limits_{n}n^{-3-\sigma-2(l-j)}<\infty,

\left\|\frac{d^{j-1}\widetilde{P}_{t+h}}{dt^{j-1}}-\frac{d^{j-1}\widetilde{P}_{t}}{dt^{j-1}}-h\frac{d^{j}\widetilde{P}_{t}}{dt^{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\ll\sum\limits_{n}S(n)h^{2j+2}n^{2j+2}\approx h^{2j+2}\sum\limits_{n}n^{-1-\sigma-2(l-j)},

which implies that
\widetilde{P}
is
l
-th order differentiable.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Recall the identification
\mathcal{H}^{(s)}_{m}=W^{-\frac{s}{2}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathbb{B}^{m})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, between Besov-Sobolev and Bergman-Sobolev spaces mentioned in Appendix

.
Theorem
2.11
.(b) says that by taking

We do not know whether this estimate of differentiability loss is optimal.

Remark 2.13
.

Suppose a section
\xi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
.
Proposition

.(b) shows that
\frac{d^{l}P_{t}}{dt^{l}}\left(\xi(t)\right)
lives in

Similar arguments show that for each

We are now ready to conclude with the smoothness of the connection

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the set of all sections
\xi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
such that for each

\xi(t)
and all its time derivatives live in

. The connection

defined by (

\mathcal{S}
to itself.

Proof.

Suppose that
\xi
is from

. Then

by Equation (

). By the definition of

,

belongs to
\mathcal{S}
. It follows from Theorem

that

We compute the derivative of

3.
Outlook

It is natural to ask whether Theorems
2.3
,
2.4
, and
2.8
have a natural extension to more general principal ideals. In the following, we propose a series of problems attempting to generalize the results in Section
2
.

Suppose a polynomial
f\in A=\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}]
which vanishes at the origin, and it has the origin as an isolated critical point.
In geometric terms, the origin is an isolated singularity of the hypersurface
V(f)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
Consider the family of principal ideals

I(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\langle f-\epsilon e^{it}\rangle\subseteq A,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where
\epsilon
is a fixed, sufficiently small, positive real number.
We think of
t
as the time variable.
Let
P_{t}
be the orthogonal projection in
H_{m}^{2}
onto
I(t)^{\perp}
.
Let

p\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\biguplus\left\{I(t)^{\perp}\subseteq H_{m}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times H_{m}^{2},\quad p\left(I(t)^{\perp}\right)=\{t\},

and

P\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow B\left(H^{2}_{m}\right),\quad P\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(P_{t})

be respectively the assembly of Hilbert spaces
I(t)^{\perp}
and orthogonal projections
P_{t}
into a rough Hilbert bundle and a rough map between Banach spaces.
Topologize
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times H_{m}^{2}
with the subspace topology.

We would like to ask the following question about
p
.

Problem 3.1
.

Is
p
a smooth Hilbert bundle

5

5

-smoothness is enough for our purposes
[

,

]

?

At the moment, we can only prove that

is a smooth Hilbert bundle for the special case in Section
2
(see Theorem
2.3
). Note that since the base space of
p
is contractible, even the weaker property that
p
is a topological vector bundle implies that it is trivial
[
23
, IV.2.5]
,
[
29
, Corollary 1]
, hence automatically smooth, and this smooth structure is unique up to smooth vector bundle isomorphisms
[
23
, IV.3.5]
.
Let us proceed with assuming that Problem
3.1
has a positive answer. Denote the set of all (smooth) sections of
p
by
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
.

Unfortunately, the family of projections
P
is not smooth in general (see Section
2.3
, Proposition
2.10
).
Thinking of
P
as a rough connection between nearby fibers
I(t)^{\perp}
, the imitation of the standard construction of the Hermitian connection for subbundles of Hilbert bundles
[
26

,
[

gives us a rough covariant derivative:

Note that

is called a covariant derivative because it satisfies the Leibniz rule

D(g\xi)(t)=g^{\prime}(t)\xi(t)+g(t)D(\xi)(t),\quad\forall g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C}),\quad\forall\xi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{I}^{\perp}).

The

are those

which satisfy the evolution equation

This leads us to the problem about solving Equation (

Problem 3.2
.

Does the parallel transport equation (

for each initial value

?

Suppose that the operator
U_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I(0)^{\perp}\to I(t)^{\perp}
is the solution operator to Equation (

in

We would like to know the answer to the following problem.

Does

\mathfrak{T}_{I(0)}
in the sense that
U\mathfrak{T}_{I(0)}U^{\ast}\subseteq\mathfrak{T}_{I(0)}

A positive answer to Problem

Besides the examples

We end this paper by pointing out that it is interesting to study the asymptotic behavior of the unitary operator

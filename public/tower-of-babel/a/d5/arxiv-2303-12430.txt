Abstract
. Differential central simple algebras are the main object of study in this survey article. We recall some crucial notions such as differential subfields, differential splitting fields, tensor products etc. Our main focus is on differential splitting fields which connects these objects to the classical differential Galois theory. We mention several known results and raise some questions along the line.

12H05, 16H05, 16W25

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a ring with identity (not necessarily commutative). A ring
R
equipped with an additive map
d_{R}:R\rightarrow R
satisfying the Leibnitz rule
d_{R}(\alpha\beta)=\alpha d_{R}(\beta)+d_{R}(\alpha)\beta
for all
\alpha,\beta\in R
, is called a
differential ring
, and the map
d_{R}
is called a
derivation
. We denote a differential ring by an ordered pair
(R,d_{R})
. Any ring can be made into a differential ring in a trivial way by taking the zero derivation on it. A differential ring which is a field (or algebra) will be called a
differential field (or algebra)
. Here are some examples.

Example 1.1
.

The zero derivation is the only derivation on
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}(t)
be the rational function field over
\mathbb{Q}
.
For
a\in k
, setting
d_{k}(t)=a
we get a derivation on
k
.
For
a=1
, we get the usual derivation
\frac{d}{dt}
.

Example 1.3

(Inner derivations)
.

Let
R
be a ring. For any element
a\in R
, the map
\partial_{a}:R\rightarrow R
given by
\partial_{a}(x)=xa-ax
for
x\in R
is a derivation on
R
. We say that
\partial_{a}
is the
inner derivation
with respect to
a
.
Note that
\partial_{a}
restricts to the zero derivation on the center of
R
. In particular, if
R
is commutative then
\partial_{a}
is essentially the zero derivation on
R
for any
a\in R
.
Inner derivations were introduced and studied in the context of Lie algebras in
[
14
]
.

Example 1.4

(Matrix algebras and coordinate-wise derivation)
.

Let
(k,d_{k})
be a differential field.
The map
\delta^{c}:M_{m}(k)\rightarrow M_{m}(k)
defined by
\delta^{c}((a_{ij}))=(d_{k}(a_{ij}))
is a derivation on
M_{m}(k)

and
\delta^{c}
is called the
coordinate-wise derivation on
M_{m}(k)
.
Then
(M_{m}(k),\delta^{c})
is a differential algebra over
(k,d_{k})
.

Example 1.5

(Quaternion algebras and standard derivation)
.

Let
(k,d_{k})
be a differential field and
\alpha,\beta\in{k}^{\times}
. Let
Q=(\alpha,\beta)_{k}
be a quaternion algebra and let
u,v\in Q
such that
u^{2}=\alpha
,
v^{2}=\beta
and
vu=-uv
. A derivation
d
on
Q
such that
d(u)\in k(u)
and

d(v)\in k(v)
is called a
standard derivation on
Q
, and is denoted by
d_{(u,v)}
.
This is defined in
[
19
]
.

Let
(R,d_{R})
be a differential ring. Elements
c\in R
such that
d_{R}(c)=0
are called
constants.
The kernel of the derivation,
\{c\in R:d_{R}(c)=0\}
forms a ring (indeed a differential ring with the zero derivation). We call the kernel the
ring of constants
of
(R,d_{R})
and denote it by
C_{(R,d_{R})}
. A ring homomorphism
\phi:(R,d_{R})\to(S,d_{S})
is called a
differential homomorphism
if
d_{S}(\phi(\alpha))=\phi(d_{R}(\alpha))
for all
\alpha\in R
.
A ring
S\supseteq R
is called a
differential ring extension
of
R
if there is a differential ring structure on
S
which is compatible with the differential ring structure on
R
, i.e.,
d_{S}|_{R}=d_{R}
. A differential ring extension of a differential field
(k,d_{k})
is a
differential
k
-algebra
.

The main objects of interest in this article are differential central simple algebras.
We have already seen some examples of differential central simple algebras (Examples
1.4
,
1.5
). Classifications of the derivations on certain central simple algebras are noted in
Section

3
.
A crucial aspect of differential central simple algebras is their differential splitting fields.
These splitting fields can be obtained as Picard-Vessiot extensions of a matrix differential equation. This connects differential splitting fields to the differential Galois theory (see
[
17
]
). In
Section

2
, we recall the definitions and results related to Picard-Vessiot extensions of a matrix differential equation and their differential Galois groups. In
Section

4
, we have gathered several open questions concerning differential splitting fields.
Section

5
explores the tensor products of differential central simple algebras and related properties.

2.
Picard-Vessiot extensions

For an introduction to differential Galois theory we refer the interested readers to
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
20
]
.
For the results in this section, our main references are
[
22
, Chapter 1]
and
[
5
]
.

Let
(k,d_{k})
be a differential field with its field of constants
C_{k}
an algebraically closed field. Let
P\in M_{m}(k)
. A
Picard-Vessiot ring

(R,d_{R})
over
(k,d_{k})
for the matrix differential equation
\delta^{c}(Y)=PY
is a ring satisfying the following:

(a)

The only ideals
I
of
R
satisfying
d_{R}(I)\subseteq I
are
(0)
and
R
.

(b)

There exists a matrix
F\in\textup{GL}_{m}(R)
satisfying
\delta^{c}(F)=PF
.

(c)

The ring
R
is generated as a ring by
k
, the components of
F
and the inverse of the determinant of
F
.

For a matrix differential equation
\delta^{c}(Y)=PY
over
(k,d_{k})
, there exists a Picard-Vessiot ring and is unique up to
k
-differential isomorphism.
A Picard-Vessiot ring is a domain and
the fraction field of a Picard-Vessiot ring is called a
Picard-Vessiot extension field
over
k
and is abbreviated as
PVE
(see
[
22
, Proposition 1.20, Definition 1.21]
).
The next proposition describes the properties of a PVE:

Proposition 2.1
.

[
22
, Proposition 1.22]

Let
(k,d_{k})
be a differential field and
P\in M_{m}(k)
. A differential field extension
(K,d_{K})\supset(k,d_{k})
is a PVE for a matrix differential equation
\delta^{c}(Y)=PY
if and only if the following hold.

(a)

There exists
F\in\textup{GL}_{m}(K)
such that
\delta^{c}(F)=PF
.

(b)

The field
K
is generated over
k
by the entries of
F
.

(c)

The field of constants
C_{K}=C_{k}
.

Let
(K,d_{K})\supset(k,d_{k})
be a PVE for matrix equation
\delta^{c}(Y)=PY
, where
P\in M_{m}(k)
. The group of differential
k
-automorphisms of
K
is called the
differential Galois group
and is denoted by
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
. The differential Galois group can be viewed as a subgroup of
\textup{GL}_{m}(C_{k})
(see
[
22
, Observations 1.26]
), and hence is a linear algebraic group. The transcendence degree of
K
over
k
is equal to the dimension of
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
.

Similar to the classical Galois correspondence, there exists a differential Galois correspondence.
For a subgroup
H\subset\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
, let
K^{H}
denote the set of elements of
K
that are fixed by every differential automorphism in
H
. For a differential field
k\subset L\subset K
, let DAut
(L/k)
denote the set of differential automorphisms in
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
that fixes every element in
L
. We state the differential Galois correspondence in the theorem below.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
5
, Theorem 1]

For
P\in M_{m}(k)
, let
(K,d_{K})
be a PVE for a matrix equation
\delta^{c}(Y)=PY
over
(k,d_{k})
. Then

(a)

For every (Zariski) closed subgroup
H\subset\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
, there exists a differential subfield
L\subset K
containing
k
such that
K^{H}=L
and vice versa.

(b)

A differential subfield
L\subset K
containing
k
is a PVE over
k
if and only if
\mathsf{DGal}(K/L)
is a normal subgroup of
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
.

In
[
5
]
, authors established the Galois
correspondence for general PVEs, i.e. without the assumption that
C_{k}
is algebraically closed.

3.
Differential central simple algebras

Let
k
be a field.
By a
central simple
k
-algebra
we always mean
a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra that has no two-sided non-trivial ideal and whose center is
k
.
We refer to
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
or
[
4
]
, for the standard theory of central simple algebras.

Let
A
be a central simple
k
-algebra.
Let
d_{k}
be a derivation on
k
and
d
be an extension of
d_{k}
to
A
. Then

(A,d)
is a
differential central simple algebra over
(k,d_{k})
. The set of all derivations on
A
that are extensions of
d_{k}
is denoted by
Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
.
Derivations on simple algebras are studied in
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
.
Initially questions were asked about the existence of extensions of derivations, which were dealt with in
[
11
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
23
]
and
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
2
, Theorem, Proposition 1]

A derivation
d_{k}
on the field
k
can always be extended to a central simple
k
-algebra
A
.
If
d,d^{\prime}\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
, then
d^{\prime}=d+\partial_{a}
for some
a\in A
.

In particular, the above result exhibits that one derivation on a central simple
k
-algebra
A
determines the whole set
Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
d_{k}
be the zero derivation on
k
. Then

Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
consists of inner derivations on
A
.

In some cases we can define a special element in
Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
, which we may call “standard”, depending on the parameter or structure of the algebra.
In the following example, we see the structure of
Der(M_{m}(k)/(k,d_{k}))
for martix algebras
M_{m}(k)
.

Example 3.3
.

For the matrix algebra
M_{m}(k)
, we have
Der(M_{m}(k)/(k,d_{k}))=\{d_{P}=\delta^{c}+\partial_{P}\mid\ P\in M_{m}(k),\mathrm{tr}(P)=0\}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 2]
).

Symbol algebras are another important example of central simple algebras where a specific derivation is known depending on the defining parameters of the algebra.
For
m\geq 2
, assume that
k
contains a primitive
m
th root of unity
\zeta
.
For
\alpha,\beta\in{k}^{\times}
, the
symbol algebra

A=(\alpha,\beta)_{k,\zeta}
is an
m^{2}
-dimensional
k
-algebra generated by
u,v\in A
satisfying the relations
u^{m}=\alpha,v^{m}=\beta
and
vu=\zeta uv
.
Any
4
-dimensional central simple
k
-algebras is isomorphic to a symbol algebra with
m=2
; these are also called
quaternion algebras
. We denote the set of trace
0
elements in
A
by
A^{0}
.

Example 3.4

(Symbol algebras and standard derivation)
.

Let
A
be a
m^{2}
- dimensional symbol algebra over
k
. A derivation
d\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
is called a
standard derivation on
A
if there exists
u,v\in A^{0}
such that
u^{m},v^{m}\in{k}^{\times},\ vu=\zeta uv,\ d(u)\in k(u)
and
d(v)\in k(v)
, and we denote this derivation by
d_{(u,v)}
. For every
d\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
, there exists a unique element
a\in A^{0}
such that
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{a}
(see
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
).

We now generalize the above definition to an arbitrary central division algebra. Let
D
be a finite-dimensional central division
k
-algebra. As an easy application of the Double Centraliser Theorem, one gets that
D
is generated by two elements
D=k[u,v]
where
u,v
satisfies certain relations (see
[
10
, Corollary 3]
). We say
d\in Der(D/(k,d_{k}))

is a
standard derivation on
D
if there exists
u,v\in D
such that
D=k[u,v],

d(u)\in k(u)
and
d(v)\in k(v)
, and we denote this derivation by
d_{(u,v)}
.

In the context of Examples
3.3
and
3.4
, it is natural to ask the following question:

Question 3.5
.

For a central simple algebra
A
over a differential field
(k,d_{k})
, can one completely determine the set of derivations
Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
using a standard derivation?

In the context of the above question, one can study the behavior of derivations on subfields of
A
.
As we have seen in
Example

3.4
a standard derivation on a symbol algebra
A
stabilises two maximal subfields of
A
.
This motivates the study of subfields of
A
that are stable under
d
.
A subfield
L\subseteq A
is called a
differential subfield
of
(A,d)
if
d(L)\subseteq L
.
Note that, in this case,
d
restricts to the unique derivation
d_{L}
on
L
.
In
[
2
, Theorem]
, it was shown that given a central simple
k
-algebra
A
and a maximal subfield
L
of
A
, there exists
d\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
such that
L
is a differential subfield of
(A,d)
.
However, there are differential central simple algebras
(A,d)
having no differential subfields (see
[
19
]
and
[
8
]
).

Question 3.6
.

For a central simple algebra
A
over a differential field
(k,d_{k})
, classify the set of derivations
d\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
such that every maximal subfield of
A
is a differential subfield.

We say that two differential central simple algebras
(A_{1},d_{1})
and
(A_{2},d_{2})
over
(k,d_{k})
are
differentially isomorphic
if there exists a
k
-algebra isomorphism
\phi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
such that
\phi\circ d_{1}=d_{2}\circ\phi
; in this case
\phi
is called a
differential isomorphism of
(A_{1},d_{1})
and
(A_{2},d_{2})
.

Question 3.7
.

What is the relation between two differential central simple algebras having the same differential subfields? Do there exist non-isomorphic differential algebras
(A_{1},d_{1})
and
(A_{2},d_{2})
over
(k,d_{k})
having same differential subfields?

4.
Differential splitting field

Let
A
be an
m^{2}
-dimensional central simple
k
-algebra and let
K/k
be a field extension.
Then
A\otimes_{k}K
is again a central simple
K
-algebra. We say that
A
is split over
K
if
A\otimes_{k}K\simeq M_{m}(K)
.
It is well-known that a central simple algebra splits over its maximal subfields.
For every subfield
E\subseteq A
, we have
[E:k]
divides
\sqrt{\dim_{k}(A)}
.
Thus, in particular, there exists a field extension
E/k
with
[E:k]\leq\sqrt{\dim_{k}(A)}
and the algebra
A
is split over
E
.
We refer the reader to
[
21
, Chapter 13]
for the statements related to maximal subfields and splitting fields.

Analogously, Juan and Magid
[
15
]
gave a notion of a differential splitting field of a differential central simple algebra.
A differential central simple algebra
(A,d)
over
(k,d_{k})

is split over a differential field
(K,d_{K})\supseteq(k,d_{k})
if
(A\otimes_{k}K,d^{\ast}=d\otimes d_{K})
and
(M_{m}(K),\delta^{c})
are differentially isomorphic algebras over
(K,d_{K})
; we say that
(K,d_{K})
is a
differential splitting field
id
(A,d)
.

Let
(A,d)
be a differential central simple algebra over
(k,d_{k})
.
Let
L/k
be a splitting field of
A
.
Then there exists a trace zero matrix
P\in M_{m}(L)
such that
(A\otimes_{k}L,d^{\ast})\simeq(M_{m}(L),d_{P})
(see
Example

3.3
). Therefore,
in order to understand the splitting of differential central simple algebras, the first step is to understand the splitting of differential matrix algebras. In
[
15
, Proposition 2]
, a necessary and sufficient condition was obtained for a differential matrix algebra to be split.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
P,Q\in M_{m}(k)
. Then differential algebras
(M_{m}(k),d_{P})
and
(M_{m}(k),d_{Q})
are differentially isomorphic if and only if there exists
F\in GL_{m}(k)
such that
F^{-1}\delta^{c}(F)+F^{-1}QF=P
.
In particular,
(M_{m}(k),d_{P})
is split if and only if there exists
F\in GL_{m}(k)
such that
\delta^{c}(F)=FP
.

Note that, for
F^{-1}
we have
\delta^{c}(F^{-1})=PF^{-1}
and hence we can as well say that
(M_{m}(k),d_{P})
is split if and only if there exists
F\in GL_{m}(k)
such that
\delta^{c}(F)=PF
.
This leads to the following result for arbitrary differential central simple algebras.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
15
, Corollary 1]

A differential central simple algebra over
(k,d_{k})
splits over a PVE of
(k,d_{k})
.

Differential splitting fields of a differential central simple algebra are not necessarily algebraic extensions (see
[
19
, Example 4.3, Theorem 4.5]
,
[
8
, Theorem 5.5]
and
[
9
, Example 5.2, Theorem 6.5]
). It is intriguing to determine the transcendence degree of a differential splitting field for a given differential central simple algebra. We set

\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d):=\min\{\mathrm{tr.deg}_{k}(E)\mid(E,d_{E})\supseteq(k,d_{k})\mbox{ splits }(A,d)\}

and call
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d)
the
differential splitting degree of
(A,d)
.

It is clear form
Proposition

4.1
that
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d)
is bounded by
\dim_{k}(A)
. Let
(K,d_{K})
be a PVE of
(k,d_{k})
splitting
(A,d)
and let
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
be its differential Galois group.
Since we can choose the matrix
P
to be a trace zero matrix,
\mathsf{DGal}(K/k)
can be viewed as a subgroup of
\textup{SL}_{m}(C_{k})
, by
[
22
, Exercise 1.35 (5a)]
.
Then

\mathrm{tr.deg}_{k}(K)=\dim_{k}(\mathsf{DGal}(K/k))\leq m^{2}-1.

Thus
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d)
is bounded by
m^{2}-1
.

Question 4.3
.

Let
A
be a central simple
k
-algebra.
Which numbers
i
between
0\leq i\leq m^{2}-1
can occur as
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d)
for derivations
d\in Der(A/(k,d_{k}))
?

Examples of differential quaternion algebras
(Q,d)
over
(k,d_{k})
such that
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=i
for each
0\leq i\leq 3
were produced in
[
9
]
.

Example 4.4
.

[
9
, Example 5.2 and Theorem 6.5]

Let
(k=\mathbb{Q}(t),d_{k})
be a differential field with
d_{k}(t)=1
. Let
Q=(1,t)_{k}
be a quaternion algebra and
u,v\in Q^{0}
such that
u^{2}=1
,
v^{2}=t
and
vu=-uv
. Consider a derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{a}
on
Q
, where
a\in Q
. Then

(a)

For
a=0
,
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=0
.

(b)

For
a=\frac{1}{t}v
,
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=1
.

(c)

For
a=\frac{1}{4t}(-u-2v+2uv)
,
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=2
.

(d)

For
a=-\frac{1}{4t}u-v
,
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=3
.

Using
[
9
, Theorem 6.5]
, one can produce several examples of differential quaternion algebras with
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=0,1
. On the other hand, for
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)=2,3
we only know examples of split quaternion algebras. Therefore, we ask the following question.

Question 4.5
.

Does there exist a differential division quaternion algebra
(Q,d)
such that
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(Q,d)
equals
2
or
3
?

Since differential Galois groups of a PVE over
k
can be viewed as a subgroup of
\textup{SL}_{m}(C_{k})
, there may exist a relation between differential splitting fields of differential central simple algebras and subgroups of
\textup{SL}_{m}(C_{k})
.

Question 4.6
.

Which linear algebraic subgroups of
\textup{SL}_{m}(C_{k})
can occur as Galois groups of PVE over
k
that splits a differential central simple algebra over
k
?

It is also interesting to relate differential subfields to differential splitting fields
For example, it was shown in
[
8
, Theorem 5.4]
, that a symbol algebra
(\alpha,\beta)_{k,\zeta}
with a standard derivation (where we have differential subfields that are also maximal) is split by a finite extension of
k
.

Question 4.7
.

Let
(L,d_{L})
be a PVE of
(k,d_{k})
splitting
(A,d)
. Explore the relation between differential subfields of
(A,d)
and
(L,d_{L})
. More precisely we can ask:
Assuming that there is a maximal subfield
M\subseteq A
which is also a differential subfield of
A
, what can we say about
\mathsf{Ddeg}_{k}(A,d)
?

Question 4.8
.

Let
A
be a central simple
k
-algebra and let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathsf{Der}(A/(k,d_{k}))
.
Asssume that
(A,d_{1})
and
(A,d_{2})
have the same differential splitting fields. Is
(A,d_{1})\simeq(A,d_{2})
?

The following characterises differential quaternion algebras over
(k,d_{k})
that are split over a finite differential field extension of
(k,d_{k})
.

Theorem 4.9
.

[
9
, Theorem 7.3]

A differential quaternion algebra splits over a finite field extension of
k
if and only if the derivation on the quaternion algebra becomes standard over a finite extension of
k
.

Question 4.10
.

Does
Theorem

4.9
hold for symbol algebras and division algebras?

5.
Tensor products of differential central simple algebras

Given two differential central simple algebras
(A_{1},d_{1})
and
(A_{2},d_{2})
over
(k,d_{k})
, we obtain the tensor differential algebra as follows.
The algebra
A_{1}\otimes_{k}A_{2}
is again a central simple
k
-algebra and the map
d_{1}\otimes d_{2}:A_{1}\otimes_{k}A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}\otimes_{k}A_{2}
defined by
(d_{1}\otimes d_{2})(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=d_{1}(a_{1})\otimes a_{2}+a_{1}\otimes d_{2}(a_{2})
is a derivation on
A_{1}\otimes_{k}A_{2}
.
We write
(A_{1},d_{1})\otimes_{(k,d_{k})}(A_{1},d_{1})
for the differential central simple algebra
(A_{1}\otimes_{k}A_{2},d_{1}\otimes d_{2})
over
(k,d_{k})
.

Question 5.1
.

Let
A
be a central simple
k
-algebra and let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathsf{Der}(A/(k,d_{k}))

The Brauer group of a field
k
, denoted by
{\rm Br}(k)
, is the quotient group of the group generated by central simple algebras modulo the subgroup generated by matrix algebras. Due to Weddurburn’s Theorem, every central simple
k
-algebra
A
is Brauer equivalent to a unique division algebra up to isomorphism.

Analogous to the usual definition of the Brauer group of a field, a definition of the differential Brauer group was given in
[
13
]
.
However, with the definition in
[
13
]
it turned out that when the base field is of characteristic zero the differential Brauer group is the same as the usual Brauer group and thus does not contain any extra information.
In
[
15
]
, the
differential Brauer group
denoted by
Br^{diff}(k,d_{k})
is defined as the quotient group of the group generated by differential matrix algebras over
(k,d_{k})
modulo the subgroup generated by matrix algebras with coordinate-wise derivation. Note that
Br^{diff}(k,d_{k})
encompasses information only about differential matrix algebras.
When
k
is algebraically closed (or a
C_{1}
-field) then all central simple algebras are matrix algebras.

It is well-known that

is a torsion group,

It is well-known for a central division

(

Since every element in

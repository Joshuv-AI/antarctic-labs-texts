Enumerating m m -clusters using exceptional sequences
Kiyoshi Igusa

Abstract
. We give a bijection between ordered m m -clusters and (complete) m m -exceptional sequences, a concept that we introduce for this purpose. This holds for all hereditary artin algebras. This extends the bijection in the m = 1 m=1 case shown in [ 13 ] .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

18E30:16G20

For any Dynkin quiver
Q
, the number of clusters in the cluster category
[
3
]
of any hereditary algebra
\Lambda
with valued quiver
Q
is given by the formula

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{h+d_{i}}{d_{i}}

where
h
is the Coxeter number of
Q
and
d_{i}=2,\cdots,h
are the degrees of the principal
W
-invariant polynomials where
W
is the Weyl group of
Q
. The formula was generalized to
m
-clusters by Fomin and Reading
[
9
]
:

p_{\Lambda}(m)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{hm+d_{i}}{d_{i}}.

A remarkable paper by Galashin, Lam, Trinh and Williams
[
10
]
gives a uniform proof of this formula.

This paper gives another perspective on this formula using exceptional sequences. Namely, we show that the set of ordered
m
-clusters is in 1-1 correspondence with the set of
m
-exceptional sequences (Definition
1.2.1
). This can be viewed as a refinement of the observation that the leading coefficient of
n!p_{\Lambda}(m)
is the number of exceptional sequences:

e_{\Lambda}=\frac{n!h^{n}}{\prod d_{i}}.

This uniform formula for
e_{\Lambda}
was first observed by Chapoton
[
6
]
and now has several uniform proofs, e.g.
[
20
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
8
]
.

This is an old unpublished paper whose main result is being upgraded using a new joint result with Shujian Chen
[
12
]
. A closely related result of Buan, Reiten and Thomas
[
4
]
is discussed in section
3.1
.

The original version of this paper had a discussion of probability distributions of terms in exceptional sequences of type

1.
Basic definitions

We recall the definitions and basic properties of exceptional sequences from
[
7
]
,
[
23
]
. Over an hereditary algebra
\Lambda
, a module
M
is called
exceptional
if
M
is rigid and Schurian where
rigid
means
\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,M)=0
and
Schurian
means
\End_{\Lambda}(M)
is a division algebra. [Schurian modules are usually called “bricks.”] An
exceptional sequence
of length
\ell
for
\Lambda
is a sequence of exceptional modules
(E_{1},E_{2},\cdots,E_{\ell})
so that

\Hom_{\Lambda}(E_{j},E_{i})=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(E_{j},E_{i})=0\text{ for }i<j\leq\ell.

The exceptional sequence is called
complete
if it is of maximal length and this is well known to occur when
\ell=n
, the number of nonisomorphic simple
\Lambda
-modules. We consider exceptional sequences up to isomorphism where two sequences
(E_{i})
,
(E_{i}^{\prime})
are isomorphic if they have the same length and
E_{i}\cong E_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
.

1.1.
Relative projective terms

In each exceptional sequence we will define certain terms to be “relatively projective.”

For any
\Lambda
-module
M
the (right)
perpendicular category

M^{\perp}
is defined to be the full subcategory of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
of all objects
X
so that
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,X)=0=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(M,X)
. The left perpendicular category
{}^{\perp}M
is defined analogously. From the six-term exact sequence for
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,-)
it follows that
M^{\perp}
and
{}^{\perp}M
are closed under extensions, kernels and cokernels of morphisms between objects. Any full subcategory of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
having these properties is called a
wide subcategory
. It is well-known that the perpendicular categories
M^{\perp}
,
{}^{\perp}M
are finitely generated, i.e., contain a generator. Conversely, if a wide subcategory
{\mathcal{W}}
of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
has a generator
X
then
{}^{\perp}X
has a generator, say
Y
, and
{\mathcal{W}}=Y^{\perp}
. Therefore, perpendicular categories are the same as finitely generated wide subcategories of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
.

A term
E_{j}
in an exceptional sequence
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{\ell})
will be called
relatively projective
if it is a projective object of the perpendicular category of the later terms,
(E_{j+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{\ell})^{\perp}
. In a complete exceptional sequence, the first term
E_{1}
is always relatively projective since the perpendicular category
(E_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{n})^{\perp}
is semi-simple. If
\Lambda
has finite representation type then we define
f_{\Lambda}(x)
to be the degree
n
integer polynomial given by

f_{\Lambda}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}x^{k}

where
e_{k}
is the number of complete exceptional sequences in
mod\text{-}\Lambda
with
k
relatively projective terms. This polynomial is suitable for computing the number of
m
-clusters in the
m
-cluster category of
\Lambda
defined below. This polynomial also gives the number of complete exceptional sequences which, following
[
22
]
, we denote by
e_{\Lambda}=f_{\Lambda}(1)=\sum e_{k}
.

1.2.
m
-exceptional sequences

Recall that, since
\Lambda
is hereditary, the indecomposable objects of the bounded derived category of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
have the form
M[k]
where
M
is an indecomposable
\Lambda
-module and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We say that
M[k]
is
exceptional
if
M
is an exceptional
\Lambda
-module. The following definition extends an idea from
[
13
]
.

Definition 1.2.1
.

For any
m\geq 0
we define an
m
-exceptional sequence
of length
\ell
for
\Lambda
to be a sequence of exceptional objects
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{\ell})
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(mod\text{-}\Lambda)
so that

(1)

for all
j
,
E_{j}=M_{j}[d_{j}]
where
0\leq d_{j}\leq m
,

(2)

(M_{1},\cdots,M_{\ell})
is an exceptional sequence in
mod\text{-}\Lambda
and

(3)

when
d_{j}=m
, the module
M_{j}
is relatively projective in the perpendicular category
(M_{j+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{\ell})^{\perp}
.

An
m
-exceptional sequence will be called
complete
if it has the maximum length
\ell=n
. For
m=1
, an
m
-exceptional sequence is called a
signed exceptional sequence

[
13
]
.

It is clear from this definition that, to every exceptional sequence
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{\ell})
with
k
relatively projective terms, there correspond
(m+1)^{k}m^{\ell-k}

m
-exceptional sequences since there are
m+1
possibilities for
d_{j}
when
M_{j}
is relatively projective and only
m
possibilities otherwise. Therefore, for
\Lambda
of finite type, the number of complete
m
-exceptional sequences is given by

g_{\Lambda}(m):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}=m^{n}f_{\Lambda}\left(\frac{m+1}{m}\right).

We also consider the
m
-cluster category of
\Lambda

[
25
]
. This is defined to be the orbit category of the bounded derived category of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
modulo the functor
\tau^{-1}[m]
:

{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)={\mathcal{D}}^{b}(mod\text{-}\Lambda)/\tau^{-1}[m]

We identify objects of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
with their representative in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(mod\text{-}\Lambda)
in the fundamental domain of
\tau^{-1}[m]
which we take to be

(1.1)

mod\text{-}\Lambda\cup mod\text{-}\Lambda[1]\cup\cdots\cup mod\text{-}\Lambda[m-1]\cup\Lambda[m]

Thus the indecomposable objects of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
are represented by
M[j]
for some indecomposable
\Lambda
-module
M
where
0\leq j\leq m
with the additional condition that
M
is projective when
j=m
. Two object
X[j],Y[k]
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
are
compatible
if
\Ext_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}}^{i}(X[j],Y[k])=\Ext_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}}^{i}(Y[k],X[j])=0
for all
i\geq 1
and they are not isomorphic. Equivalently, either

(1)

j<k
and
\Hom_{\Lambda}(Y,X)=0=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y,X)
,

(2)

j=k
and
\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y,X)
and
X\not\cong Y
or

(3)

j>k
and
\Hom_{\Lambda}(X,Y)=0=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,Y)
,

A maximal pairwise compatible set of exceptional objects of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
has
n
objects and is called an
m
-cluster
for
\Lambda
.

The first part of the main theorem of this paper, proved in Section
2
below, is the following which generalizes the
m=2
case proved in the preliminary version of
[
13
]
. This new proof supersedes the one in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.2.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional hereditary algebra over any field and
k,m\geq 0
. Then there is a 1-1 correspondence between (isomorphism classes of) ordered
k
-tuples of pairwise compatible objects in the
m
-cluster category of
\Lambda
and (isomorphism classes of)
m
-exceptional sequences of length
k
for
\Lambda
.

For example, when
m=0
, there is only one
0
-cluster of size
n
, namely the set of projective modules. So, there are
n!
complete 0-exceptional sequences. These are the same as complete exceptional sequences in which every object is relatively projective. It follows that the number of maximal compatible subsets of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
, the
m
-clusters
, is equal to

\frac{1}{n!}g_{\Lambda}(m)=\frac{m^{n}}{n!}f_{\Lambda}\left(\frac{m+1}{m}\right)

The case of greatest interest is when
m=1
. The number of cluster for the algebra
\Lambda
is

\frac{1}{n!}f_{\Lambda}\left(2\right)

where
f_{\Lambda}(2)
is the number of signed exceptional sequences.

Some of the facts that we can see immediately from this formula are:

(1)

g_{\Lambda}(-1)=f_{\Lambda}(0)=0
(since
E_{1}
is always relatively projective)

(2)

g_{\Lambda}(0)=e_{n}=n!
(the number of ordered
m
-clusters for
m=0
).

Proposition 1.2.3
.

All real roots of the polynomial
g_{\Lambda}(x)
are between
0
and
-1
including
-1
and excluding
0
.

Proof.

If
m\geq 0
then
g_{\Lambda}(m)>0
since all terms are nonnegative:
e_{k}(m+1)^{k}m^{n-k}\geq 0
and the
k=n
term is
n!(m+1)^{n}\geq n!>0
. If
m<-1
then
(-1)^{n}g_{\Lambda}(m)>0
since all terms are nonnegative and the
k=n
term is positive.
∎

The second part of the main theorem, also proved in Section
2
is the following.

Proposition 1.2.4
.

The 1-1 correspondences of Theorem
1.2.2
can be chosen to be compatible with deletion of the first term in the sense that, if
(T_{1},\cdots,T_{k})
corresponds to
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{k})
, then
(T_{2},\cdots,T_{k})
corresponds to
(E_{2},\cdots,E_{k})
.

1.3.
Recursive formula

We will show that the recursive formula for the number of exceptional sequences given in
[
22
]
can be modified to give a recursive formula for
f_{\Lambda}(x)
.

We review briefly the argument from
[
22
]
which counts the number of exceptional sequences

e_{\Lambda}=\sum e_{k}=f_{\Lambda}(1)

Let
\Lambda
be a hereditary algebra of finite type with connected valued quiver
Q
. The number of vertices of
Q
, usually denoted
n
, is the
rank
of
\Lambda
. For each vertex
i
of
Q
, let
Q(i)
be the valued quiver obtained from
Q
by deleting the vertex
i
. Then
Q(i)
has
1,2
or
3
components. To count the number of exceptional sequences for
Q(i)
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1.3.1
.

[
22
]

Let
A,B
be hereditary algebras of finite type with rank
n_{A},n_{B}
. Then there is a 1-1 correspondence between complete exceptional sequences for
A\times B
and pairs of complete exceptional sequences for
A,B
together with a shuffling of the two sequences. Thus

e_{A\times B}=\binom{n_{A}+n_{B}}{n_{A}}e_{A}e_{B}.

Since a term in an exceptional sequence for
A\times B
is relatively projective if and only if it is relatively projective in
A
or
B
whichever it comes from, we get the following.

(1.2)

f_{A\times B}(x)=\binom{n_{A}+n_{B}}{n_{A}}f_{A}(x)f_{B}(x)

The recursive formula for
e_{\Lambda}
is:

Proposition 1.3.2
.

[
22
]

For
\Lambda
of rank
n
and Coxeter number
h
we have:

e_{\Lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{h}{2}e_{\Lambda(i)}

Proof.

(Summarized from
[
22
]
) If
P_{i}
and
I_{j}
are in the same orbit of the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau
then the sum of the lengths of the
\tau
orbits of
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
is
h
. Furthermore, for any indecomposable module
M
in the union of these
\tau
orbits,
M^{\perp}
,
P_{i}^{\perp}
and
P_{j}^{\perp}
are all isomorphic to
\Lambda(i)
. Since this counts each indecomposable module twice and since every complete exceptional sequence for
\Lambda
is given by an indecomposable module
M
preceded by a complete exceptional sequence for
M^{\perp}
, we get:

2e_{\Lambda}=2\sum_{M}e_{M^{\perp}}=h\sum_{i}e_{\Lambda(i)}.

The formula in the Proposition follows.
∎

In the above proof, in the union of the
\tau
orbits of
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
, the proportion of objects which are projective is exactly
\frac{2}{h}
. This gives the following.

Corollary 1.3.3
.

Let
\Lambda,n,h
be as above. Then

f_{\Lambda}(x)=\left(x+\frac{h}{2}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\Lambda(i)}(x)

or, equivalently,

{g_{\Lambda}(m)=\frac{hm+2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{\Lambda(i)}(m)}.

2.
Proof of main theorem

This section gives the statement and proof of the main theorem.

2.1.
Statement of main theorem

Theorem 2.1.1
.

There is a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of
m
-exceptional sequences of length
k
and the set of isomorphism classes of ordered
k
-tuples of pairwise compatible objects in the
m
-cluster category for any finite dimensional hereditary algebra
\Lambda
. Furthermore, this bijection is compatible with deletion of the first term in the sense that, if
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{k})
corresponds to
(T_{1},\cdots,T_{k})
, then
(E_{2},\cdots,E_{k})
corresponds to
(T_{2},\cdots,T_{k})
.

We will set up notation to give an explicit formula for this bijection. We choose one object from every isomorphism class of exceptional
\Lambda
-modules. Let
{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)
denote the set of these objects. For set theoretic purposes we can consider this to be a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
since every exceptional module
M
is determined up to isomorphism by its dimension vector
\undim M\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

For any finitely generated wide subcategory
{\mathcal{W}}
of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
(defined in section
1.1
), let
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{W}})={\mathcal{W}}\cap{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)
. Denote by
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
the
m
-cluster category of
{\mathcal{W}}
. Objects of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
will be represented by elements of the fundamental domain of the functor
\tau^{-1}[m]
on the bounded derived category of
{\mathcal{W}}
, as in (
1.1
). Thus the exceptional objects of
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
are (up to isomorphism)
X[j]
where
X\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{W}})
and
0\leq j\leq m
with the additional condition that
X
is a relative projective object of
{\mathcal{W}}
when
j=m
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
denote this set of objects. We call
j
the
level
of the object
X[j]
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{j}({\mathcal{W}})
denote the set of objects of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
of level
j
. In particular, the
rank

r\leq n
of
{\mathcal{W}}
is the number of elements of
{\mathcal{E}}_{m}^{m}({\mathcal{W}})
. We use the abbreviation
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)={\mathcal{E}}^{m}(mod\text{-}\Lambda)
. We consider the special case
{\mathcal{W}}=T^{\perp}
where
T\in{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)
. This is a wide subcategory of rank
n-1
. We note that
T
is uniquely determined by
T^{\perp}
since
T
is the unique exceptional object of
{}^{\perp}(T^{\perp})
.

We use the expression
exceptional pair
for an exceptional sequence of length
2
in
mod\text{-}\Lambda
. We say that two are
equivalent
:
(X,Y)\sim(Z,W)
if
(X\oplus Y)^{\perp}=(Z\oplus W)^{\perp}
, i.e., if they
span
the same rank 2 wide subcategory
\,{}^{\perp}\left((X\oplus Y)^{\perp}\right)
of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
.

2.2.
The key lemma

We state the key lemma, which uses the following notation, and show how it proves the main theorem.

For any
T[k]\in{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)
, let
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)
denote the set of all
X[j]\in{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)
which are compatible with
T[k]
. Recall that
A,B
are compatible if
A\not\cong B
and
\Hom(A,B[s])=0=\Hom(B,A[s])
for all
s>0
. For
A=X[j],B=T[k]
this is equivalent to one of the following.

(1)

j<k
and
(X,T)
is an exceptional pair,

(2)

j>k
and
(T,X)
is an exceptional pair or

(3)

j=k
and
T,X
are ext-orthogonal and nonisomorphic in
mod\text{-}\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

For any
T[k]\in{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)
there is a bijection

\sigma_{T[k]}:{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})\to{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}.

Furthermore,
A,B
are compatible in
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})
if and only if
\sigma_{T[k]}A,\sigma_{T[k]}B
are compatible in
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1.1
given Lemma
2.2.1
.

We show that the bijections
\sigma_{T[k]}
give bijections

\theta_{p}:\{\text{$p$-tuples of compatible objects in ${\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)$}\}\to\{m\text{-exceptional sequences of length $p$ for $\Lambda$}\}

compatible with deletion of the first term. The bijections
\theta_{p}
are defined recursively as follows. For
p=1
let
\theta_{1}
be the identity mapping. Next, take
p\geq 2
.

Let
(T_{1},\cdots,T_{p})
be an ordered
p
-tuple of compatible objects in
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(\Lambda)
and let
T_{p}=T[k]
be the last object. Then,
T_{i}
for
i<p
are compatible elements of
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}
. So, by the lemma,
\sigma_{T[k]}^{-1}(T_{i})
are compatible objects of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})
. So,
\sigma_{T[k]}^{-1}(T_{1},\cdots,T_{p-1})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1})
is an ordered
(p-1)
-tuple of compatible objects in the wide subcategory
T^{\perp}
of
mod\text{-}\Lambda
. Since
T^{\perp}
is isomorphic to
mod\text{-}\Lambda^{\prime}
for some hereditary algebra
\Lambda^{\prime}
, by induction on
p
, we have the bijection

\theta_{p-1}:\{\text{$(p-1)$-tuples of compatible objects in ${\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})$}\}\to\{m\text{-exc. seq. of length $p-1$ for $T^{\perp}$}\}

which is given by
\theta_{p-2}
when first terms are deleted. Let

\theta_{p-1}\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1}\right)=(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p-1})

be the corresponding
m
-exceptional sequence in
T^{\perp}
. Then we define
\theta_{p}
by

\theta_{p}(T_{1},\cdots,T_{p})=(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p-1},T[k]).

Since
\theta_{p-2}(T_{2},\cdots,T_{p-1})=(X_{2},\cdots,X_{p-1})
, it follows that
\theta_{p-1}(T_{2},\cdots,T_{p})=(E_{2},\cdots,E_{p})
as required.

Conversely, let
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p})
be an
m
-exceptional sequence for
\Lambda
. Let
E_{p}=T[k]
. Then
(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p-1})
is an
m
-exceptional sequence in
T^{\perp}
and

\theta_{p-1}^{-1}(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p-1})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1})

is a
(p-1)
-tuple of compatible objects in
T^{\perp}
. Applying
\sigma_{T[k]}
gives
\sigma_{T[k]}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1})=(T_{1},\cdots,T_{p-1})
a
(p-1)
-tuple of compatible objects in
{\mathcal{C}}^{m}(\Lambda)
. So,
\theta_{p}^{-1}(E_{1},\cdots,E_{p})=(T_{1},\cdots,T_{p})
. By construction the maps
\theta_{p}
,
\theta_{p}^{-1}
are inverse to each other. Since the maps
\theta_{p}
are compatible with deletion of the first term, so are the maps
\theta_{p}^{-1}
. Thus the key lemma implies the main theorem.
∎

2.3.
Outline of proof of key lemma

We will construct the bijection

\sigma_{T[k]}:{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})\to{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}

by decomposing each set into a disjoint union of subsets and constructing bijections between corresponding subsets as shown schematically as follows.

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccccc}{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})&=&{\mathcal{E}}_{0}&\cup\cdots\cup&{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{k}&\cup&{\mathcal{A}}_{k+1}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{k+1}&\cup\cdots\cup&{\mathcal{A}}_{m}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{m}\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}&=&{\mathcal{E}}_{0}^{\prime}&\cup\cdots\cup&{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}^{\prime}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{k}^{\prime}&\cup&{\mathcal{A}}_{k}^{\prime}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{k+1}^{\prime}&\cup\cdots\cup&{\mathcal{A}}_{m-1}^{\prime}&\cup&{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{\prime}\end{array}

Here
{\mathcal{E}}_{j}={\mathcal{E}}^{m}_{j}(T^{\perp})
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{j}^{\prime}={\mathcal{E}}_{j}^{T[k]}
denote the subsets of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}
, resp, of objects of level
j
. It follows from the definitions that, for
j<k
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{j}={\mathcal{E}}_{j}^{\prime}
. So, we take the identity mapping

\sigma_{T[k]}|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{j}}=id:{\mathcal{E}}_{j}\xrightarrow{=}{\mathcal{E}}_{j}^{\prime}\,,\quad 0\leq j<k.

With the exception of the bijection
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}_{k}
, all other bijections in Chart
2.1
are given by mutation of exceptional sequences
(X,T)\leftrightarrow(T,Y)
. This gives a bijection
\gamma:{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})\cong{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)
where the formula for
Y=\gamma(X)
is given by examining four cases. We pick out one case of this bijection. Let
{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)
be given by

{\mathcal{A}}=\{X\in{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})\,:\,\exists\text{ mono }X\hookrightarrow T^{s}\}

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}=\{Y\in{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)\,:\,\exists\text{ epi }T^{s}\twoheadrightarrow Y\}.

The bijection
\gamma:{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})\cong{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)
sends
{\mathcal{A}}
onto
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and gives a bijection
\alpha:{\mathcal{A}}\cong{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Corresponding objects
X\in{\mathcal{A}}\leftrightarrow Y\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
are related by the canonical short exact sequence

0\to X\xrightarrow{f}T^{s}\xrightarrow{g}Y\to 0

where
f,g
are minimal left/right
T
-approximations of

(from

2.6.
Compatibility

We will prove that
\sigma=\sigma_{T[k]}:{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})\cong{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}
preserves compatibility:

Proposition 2.6.1
.

X[i],X^{\prime}[i^{\prime}]
are compatible objects of
{\mathcal{E}}^{m}(T^{\perp})
if and only if
Y[j]=\sigma(X[i])
and
Y^{\prime}[j^{\prime}]=\sigma(X^{\prime}[i^{\prime}])
are compatible in
{\mathcal{E}}^{T[k]}
.

We need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.6.2
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})
and consider the pairs:

(X,X^{\prime}),\quad(X,\gamma(X^{\prime})),\quad(\gamma(X),\gamma(X^{\prime}))

If any of these is an exceptional pair, the other two are exceptional pairs.

Proof.

A pair is exceptional if the corresponding triple in the following sequence is exceptional:

(X,X^{\prime},T),\quad(X,T,\gamma(X^{\prime})),\quad(T,\gamma(X),\gamma(X^{\prime})).

But any one of these gives the other two by mutation of exceptional sequences.
∎

Lemma 2.6.3
.

Let
(X,X^{\prime})
be an exceptional pair in
{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})={\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}
and let
Y=\gamma(X),Y^{\prime}=\gamma(X^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\cup{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
. Let
X^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T^{\perp})
,
Y^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(^{\perp}T)
be given by mutation of exceptional sequences:

(X,X^{\prime},T)\sim(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime},T)\sim(X^{\prime},T,Y^{\prime\prime})\sim(T,Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime})\sim(T,Y,Y^{\prime})

Then there are unique rational numbers
a,b
and signs
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}=\pm 1
so that

\undim X+a\undim X^{\prime}+b\undim X^{\prime\prime}=0

\undim Y+\varepsilon a\undim Y^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{\prime}b\undim Y^{\prime\prime}=0

Furthermore

(1)

\varepsilon=1
if and only if
X,X^{\prime}
both lie in
{\mathcal{A}}
or they both lie in
{\mathcal{B}}
.

(2)

\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,X^{\prime})\neq 0
if and only if
a>0

(3)

\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y,Y^{\prime})\neq 0
if and only if
\varepsilon a>0

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of
a,b\in{\mathbb{Q}}
follows from the fact that
\undim X^{\prime},\undim X^{\prime\prime}
are linearly independent vectors in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
and
\undim X
is in their span.

The equation for
\gamma
gives us that
\undim Y
is congruent to
\delta\undim X
modulo
\undim T
where
\delta=-1
for
X\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
\delta=1
for
X\in{\mathcal{B}}
. This gives:

\delta\undim Y+\delta^{\prime}a\undim Y^{\prime}+\delta^{\prime\prime}b\undim Y^{\prime\prime}=c\undim T

for some
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. However,
\undim Y^{\prime},\undim Y^{\prime\prime},\undim T
are linearly independent since
(Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime},T)
form an exceptional sequence. And
\undim Y,\undim Y^{\prime},\undim Y^{\prime\prime}
are linearly dependent. So,
c=0
.

Statement (1) is now clear:
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
are both negative when
X,X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and they are both positive if
X,X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. So,
\varepsilon=\delta^{\prime}/\delta=1
in these cases and
\varepsilon=-1
in the other cases.

If
\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,X^{\prime})\neq 0
then
a=1>0
. Otherwise, either
\Hom_{\Lambda}(X,X^{\prime})\neq 0
, in which case
a<0
or both are zero in which case
a=0
. This proves (2) and (3) is similar.

T^{s}\hookrightarrow Y\twoheadrightarrow X

Y^{\prime}=X^{\prime}\in T^{\perp}
has the property that

and
\Hom_{\Lambda}(X^{\prime},T)\neq 0
. The exact sequence tells us that

\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X,X^{\prime})\cong\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y,X^{\prime})=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y,Y^{\prime}).

Also,
\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y^{\prime},T)=0
implies that

\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y^{\prime},Y)\cong\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(Y^{\prime},X)=\Ext^{1}_{\Lambda}(X^{\prime},X).

Therefore,

are compatible if and only if
Y[k],Y^{\prime}[k]
are compatible.

∎

Remark 3.1.4

Note that there may be more than one way to arrange the objects

We need to go through the proof (the same as in

Proof.

We follow the method in
[

which also appears in

. Namely, we apply to the exceptional sequence

-exceptional sequences.

We recall that the correspondence

is an exceptional sequence and, by the properties of the bijection

3.2.

From the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.2.1

Proof.

∎

\left<\undim T_{k}^{\prime},c_{k}^{\prime}\right>=-\left<\undim T_{k}^{\prime},c_{k}\right>=f_{k}

If we write
\undim T_{k}^{\prime}=\sum a_{j}\undim T_{j}

a_{j}f_{j}=\begin{cases}|b_{kj}|f_{k}=\delta(\left<c_{j},c_{k}\right>-\left<c_{k},c_{j}\right>)&\text{if the sign of $b_{kj}$ is $\delta$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

So,

a_{j}=\begin{cases}|b_{jk}|&\text{if the sign of $b_{kj}$ is $\delta$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and, by Corollary
3.2.2
,
T_{k}^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by its dimension vector.

Discrete Dislocation Dynamics with annihilation as the limit of the Peierls-Nabarro model in one dimension
Patrick van Meurs, Stefania Patrizi

Abstract
Plasticity of metals is the emergent phenomenon of many crystal defects (dislocations) which interact and move on microscopic time and length scales. Two of the commonly used models to describe such dislocation dynamics are the Peierls-Nabarro model and the so-called discrete dislocation dynamics model. However, the consistency between these two models is known only for a few number of dislocations or up to the first time at which two dislocations collide. In this paper we resolve these restrictions, and establish the consistency for any number of dislocations and without any restriction on their initial position or orientation. In more detail, the evolutive Peierls-Nabarro model which we consider describes the evolution of a phase-field function v ε ​ ( t , x ) v_{\varepsilon}(t,x) which represents the atom deformation in a crystal. The model is a reaction-diffusion equation of Allen-Cahn type with the half Laplacian. The small parameter ε \varepsilon is the ratio between the atomic distance and the typical distance between phase transitions in v ε v_{\varepsilon} . The position of a phase transition determines the position of a dislocation, and the sign of the transition (up or down) determines the orientation. The goal of this paper is to derive the asymptotic behavior of the function v ε v_{\varepsilon} as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 up to arbitrary end time T T ; in particular beyond collisions. We prove that v ε v_{\varepsilon} converges to a piecewise constant function v v , whose jump points in the spatial variable satisfy the ODE system which represents discrete dislocation dynamics with annihilation. Our proof method is to explicitly construct and patch together several sub- and supersolutions of v ε v_{\varepsilon} , and to show that they converge to the same limit v v .

Introduction

In three dimensions dislocations are line defects in crystals. These
lines can move in the crystallographic planes (slip planes), which typically happens when the crystal is submitted to shear stress. This movement
is one of the main explanations for the plastic behavior of metals. We refer the reader to the books
[
HL92
,
HB11
]
for a tour in the theory of dislocations.
Dislocations can be described at several scales by different models, see e.g. the review paper
[
DPV22
]
.
Due to the complexity of the evolution of lines in three dimensions, we follow the common simplification in which it is assumed that all dislocations are straight and parallel edge dislocations which lie and move in the same slip plane. Then, the dislocations can be represented by points on a line which lies in the slip plane and is perpendicular to the dislocation lines. In addition to their position, each dislocation has either a positive or a negative orientation.

Even after this simplification to reduce the number of spatial dimensions from three to one, there are several different models to describe the dynamics of dislocations. Here, we mention three of these models. First, the classical model by Frenkel and Kontorova is the most detailed model out of the three; it describes the deformation of all the atoms. The dislocation positions appear in the form of certain local configurations of the atoms. Second, discrete dislocation dynamics is the coarsest model out of the three; it neglects any atomic effects and simply describes the dislocation positions as a system of ODEs on the continuous line
\mathbb{R}
. Third, the Peierls-Nabarro model lies in between these two; it does not describe the position of each atom, but it does describe the atom positions through a continuous displacement function
v_{\varepsilon}
. In
[
FIM12
]
the connection between the Frenkel-Kontorova model and the Peierls-Nabarro model is established, but a complete connection between the Peierls-Nabarro model and discrete dislocation dynamics appears to be missing in the literature. In this paper we fill this gap.

1.3
Discussion

We mention three merits of Theorem
1.2
. First, with
Theorem
1.2
it follows from the literature that discrete dislocation dynamics emerges as the dilute dislocation limit from the fully atomistic Frenkel-Kontorova model, i.e. the limit in which the number of atoms in between two neighboring dislocations diverges to
\infty
. Indeed, in
[
FIM12
]
the connection is made between the Frenkel-Kontorova model and the Peierls-Nabarro model, and this connection shows that the number of atoms between neighboring dislocations is of order
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
.

The second merit is that, as expected from
[
GM12
,
PV16
]
, the mobility constant
c_{0}
in the discrete dislocation dynamics model in (
PN
) does not change beyond collisions. This is an important observation, because the value of
c_{0}
represents the macroscopic influence of the atomistic interaction potential
W
; see (
1.6
) and (
1.4
).

The third merit is that Theorem
1.2
unifies the literature on the limit
N\to\infty
. Indeed, in
[
vMPP20
]
it is shown that limit of (

) in terms of the function

\partial_{t}v=c_{0}|\partial_{x}v|\,\mathcal{I}[v].

Lemma 3.2

(Perturbation of (

.

Let
\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{x}^{0}\in\Omega^{N}

and
\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1}

Then

such that
\overline{t}\leq\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1}
and
\overline{t}\to T_{1}
as
\overline{\sigma},|\overline{\eta}|\to 0
, there holds

\displaystyle\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\|_{C([0,T_{1}-\tau])}

\displaystyle\to 0,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1}

\displaystyle\to T_{1},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{x}}(\overline{t})

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{x}(T_{1})

(3.13)

For convenience we parametrize the sequences
\overline{\sigma}
and
\overline{\eta}
by

such that “

\varepsilon\to 0

The limit (

the particles

remain separated, and thus the right-hand side in (

and (

\min_{1\leq i\leq N-1}\min_{t\in[0,T_{1}-\tau]}\overline{x}_{i+1}(t)-\overline{x}_{i}(t)>c,

which implies that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1}\geq T_{1}.

Then, to prove (

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1}\leq T_{1}.

We remark that, in the special case where

) and (

). We prove both statements together from the argument that follows. As preparation, for any particle
x_{i}
, we set

as the set of indices of all particles

(including

at time

. If

does not collide, then we take

.

Let

be small and arbitrary. By the continuity of

there exists

|x_{i}(T_{1}-\tau)-x_{i}(T_{1})|<\frac{\delta}{4}\quad\text{for any }1\leq i\leq N.

Then, by (
3.11
),
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\tau,\delta)
such that
for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

(3.15)

We are going to show that, for any

, the trajectory of
\overline{x}_{i}
remains in the time-space box
(T_{1}-\tau,\overline{\mathsf{T}}_{1})\times B(x_{i}(T_{1}),\delta)

Let

be the first time at which any particle

exits

such that either

or

for some

Let

be the shortest distance between any two particles

which do not collide with each other at time

By (

\dot{\overline{D}}\leq-\frac{1}{\overline{D}}+\sum_{j\in I_{i}^{c}}\frac{b_{\ell}b_{j}}{\overline{x}_{\ell}-\overline{x}_{j}}-\sum_{j\in I_{i}^{c}}\frac{b_{k}b_{j}}{\overline{x}_{k}-\overline{x}_{j}}+2|\overline{\sigma}|\quad\text{ in }(T_{1}-\tau,T_{e}).

Lemma 3.5

(Bounding supersolutions by other supersolutions)
.

For any
M>0
there exist
K=K(M)\geq 0
such that for all
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}>0
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and all

\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},\quad\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}=o_{\varepsilon}(1),\quad|c|,|c^{\prime}|\leq\frac{M}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}},\quad z\geq\frac{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon},

then

\left(u-\varepsilon|c\psi|\right)(y+z)-\left(u+\varepsilon|c^{\prime}\psi|\right)(y)\geq-K(M)(|c|+|c^{\prime}|)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.28)

Proof.

We set
\vartheta:=\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
. We recall
\alpha=W^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
and take

\kappa:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{10},\frac{1}{8\alpha\pi M\|\psi\|_{\infty}}\right\}>0

as an
\varepsilon
-independent constant. We split 3 cases depending on
y
.

Case 1:
y\notin B(-z,\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon})\cup B(0,\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon})
.
In this case, we don’t need the precise expression for
\kappa
. Since
|y|,|y+z|\geq\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon}
, we obtain from Lemma
2.2
that

\max\left\{|\psi|(y+z),|\psi|(y)\right\}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}

(3.29)

for some constant
C>0
and
\varepsilon
small enough. Then, simply using that
u
is increasing, we obtain (
3.28
) by

\left(u-\varepsilon|c\psi|\right)(y+z)-\left(u+\varepsilon|c^{\prime}\psi|\right)(y)>-\frac{C}{\kappa}(|c|+|c^{\prime}|)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\vartheta}.

Case 2:

y\in B(-z,\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon})
.
Using that
u
is increasing and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain for all
\varepsilon
small enough that both

u(y+z)>u\left(-\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\alpha\pi\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}-K_{1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa\vartheta}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha\pi\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}

and

Then, using that

u(y+z)-u(y)\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha\pi}\frac{1-5\kappa}{\kappa(1-\kappa)}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}\geq\frac{1}{4\alpha\pi\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}.

This yields

\displaystyle\left(u-\varepsilon|c\psi|\right)(y+z)-\left(u+\varepsilon|c^{\prime}\psi|\right)(y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\alpha\pi\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}-\varepsilon(|c|+|c^{\prime}|)\|\psi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{1}{4\alpha\pi\kappa}-2M\|\psi\|_{\infty}\Big)\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta},

which is positive since

. Hence, if

.

Case 3:

y\in B(0,\kappa\frac{\vartheta}{\varepsilon})
. By the symmetry in the estimate on
u
in Lemma
2.1

Lemma 3.6

(Removing certain dipoles)

Under the same conditions as Lemma

(u-\varepsilon c\psi)(y+z;-1)+(u-\varepsilon c^{\prime}\psi)(y;+1)\leq K(M)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence, it is sufficient to show that

\left(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}M|\psi|\right)(-y-z)-1+\left(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\vartheta}M|\psi|\right)(y)\leq K_{4}(|c|+|c^{\prime}|)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\vartheta}\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}.

in (

)

and

. This yields

\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{T}_{1},x)\leq\sum_{i\in\hat{S}_{1}}(u-\varepsilon\overline{c}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\psi)\left(\displaystyle\frac{x-\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon};b_{i}\right)+C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\theta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=:\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x),

where

\hat{S}_{1}:=\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{K,\ldots,N\}.

In this way, we reduce to the case where the only two particles colliding at
T_{1}
are
x_{k}
and
x_{K}
.
From
\{\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in\hat{S}_{1}}
and
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
we can proceed analogously as in Subsection
3.4.1
with the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
on
[\overline{T}_{1},\hat{T}_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}
(see Remark
3.7
for a minor modification to the proof).

Since in all four cases the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
can be reduced to that in Section
3.4
, we can construct
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}
beyond
\hat{T}_{1}
analogously to Subsection
3.4.2
. Consequently, (
3.1
) holds on
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

3.5.2
Proof of (
3.1
) at
t=T_{1}
.

Inequality (
3.1
) at
t=T_{1}
follows from obvious, minor modifications to the arguments in Sections
3.4.3
and
3.4.4
; we omit the details.

3.6
The general case

In this section we prove (
3.1
) at
T_{1}
for general
\mathbf{x}^{0}\in\Omega^{N}
. One example of a scenario which is not covered in the preceding sections is illustrated in Figure
2
at
t=T_{1}
. Let
M
be the number of collisions at
T_{1}
(note that
M\leq N/2
); we label them
m=1,\ldots,M
. Let

y_{1}<y_{2}<\ldots<y_{M}

be the spatial points where a collision takes place. Similar to Section
3.5
, set for each
m

I_{m}:=\{k_{m},k_{m}+1,\ldots,K_{m}\}

as the set of indices of the particles that collide at time-space point
(T_{1},y_{m})
. Note that the sets
\{I_{m}\}_{m}
are disjoint, i.e.
K_{m}<k_{m+1}
for all
m
.

We start from
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}
as constructed in Section
3.3
. Then, we construct
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
in Subsection
3.6.1
by modifying the construction in Subsections
3.4.1
and
3.5.1
. We prove (
3.1
) at
T_{1}
in Subsection
3.6.2
.

3.6.1
Construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
,
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}
on
[\overline{T}_{1},\overline{T}_{2}]

As in Subsection
3.5.1
, we start from
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}
as given in (
3.78
). Here, (
3.79
)–(
3.80
) turn into

\theta_{i}(T_{1})\geq l_{0}\quad\text{for all }i\text{ such that }x_{i}(T_{1})<x_{i+1}(T_{1})

for some fixed
l_{0}>0
, and for
\varepsilon
small enough

\overline{x}_{i+1}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{l_{0}}{2}\quad\text{for all }i\text{ such that }x_{i}(T_{1})<x_{i+1}(T_{1}),

(3.81)

\theta_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\theta}(\overline{T}_{1}-\overline{\tau}_{\varepsilon})\leq\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\overline{x}_{K_{m}}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{x}_{k_{m}}^{\varepsilon}=:\Theta_{\varepsilon}=o_{\varepsilon}(1)

and

|\overline{c}_{i}^{\varepsilon}|\leq C+\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\theta_{\varepsilon}}&\text{if }k_{m}\leq i\leq K_{m}\text{ for some }m\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.82)

We recall from Subsection
3.5.1
that it depends on the type of collision (we separated 4 types; see Figure
9
) whether
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
needs to be constructed or not. Based on this, we split three cases depending on the number of collisions which require the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
, i.e. the number of collisions which are as in Case 4 in Subsection
3.5.1
:

Case A: no collision
m
is as in Case 4.

Thanks to the separation condition (
3.81
), we can apply the argument for the bound in (
3.76
) simultaneously for each collision as in either Case 1, 2 or 3 in Subsection
3.5.1
. This yields

v_{\varepsilon}(\overline{T}_{1},x)\leq\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{T}_{1},x)\leq\sum_{i\in S_{1}}u\left(\displaystyle\frac{x-\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon};b_{i}\right)+\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\tilde{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=o_{\varepsilon}(1)
is the sum of all the errors made at each collision
m
, and
S_{1}
is a possible choice of surviving particles after the first collision consistent with
\mathbf{x}
(recall the nonuniqueness for the solution
\mathbf{x}
induced by collisions between an odd number of particles).
In particular, no
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
needs to be constructed.

Case B: precisely one collision
m_{4}
is as in Case 4.

As in Case 4 from Subsection
3.5.1
, we first construct a profile
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
such that

\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{T}_{1},\cdot)\leq\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}.

(3.83)

This construction goes as in Case A for each
m\neq m_{4}
and as in Case 4 for
m=m_{4}
. Then, the resulting profile
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
has the same properties as in Case 4, and thus we construct
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
analogously as done in Case 4.

Case C: there are at least two collisions as in Case 4.

Proceeding as in Case B, we obtain (
3.83
) for some profile
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
related to the set of particles
\{\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in\hat{S}_{1}}
for some
\hat{S}_{1}
. By this construction, the particles
\{\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in\hat{S}_{1}}
are for
\varepsilon
small enough separated by
l_{0}/2
, except for those pairs that corresponds to a collision as in Case 4. Hence, by starting from
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
with the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
, we may reduce to the case in which all collisions are simple and of type ‘
+-
’ (see Section
3.4
for this terminology). In more detail, by repeating the constructions of Cases 1-4 above, we reduce to having the set of colliding particles as follows,

I_{m}=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\text{if }\#I_{m}\text{ is even and }b_{k_{m}}=-1\text{ (as in Case 1)}\\
\{k_{m}\}&\text{if }\#I_{m}\text{ is odd and }b_{k_{m}}=1\text{ (as in Case 2)}\\
\{K_{m}\}&\text{if }\#I_{m}\text{ is odd and }b_{k_{m}}=-1\text{ (as in Case 3)}\\
\{k_{m},K_{m}\}&\text{if }\#I_{m}\text{ is even and }b_{k_{m}}=1\text{ (as in Case 4)}\\
\end{cases}

Therefore, with
2\leq\tilde{M}\leq M
as the number of Case-4 collisions, we may assume that there are only
\tilde{M}
simple (only two particles collide) collisions
as in Case 4 and that the number of particles corresponding to the profile
\hat{v}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
is equal to
\tilde{N}:=\#S_{1}+2\tilde{M}
.

By relabelling the indices and resetting
n=\tilde{N}
and
M=\tilde{M}
, we may assume that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{0}
is of the form (
3.78
) with respect to some
\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
which satisfies (
3.81
)–(
3.82
) with
K_{m}=k_{m}+1
and
b_{k_{m}}=1
for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
. Then we set

\Theta_{\varepsilon}^{m}:=x_{k_{m}+1}-x_{k_{m}}\leq\Theta_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for all }m.

Instead of the ODE system (
3.51
) for
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
, we consider the same ODE with a slightly different initial condition:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\hat{x}}_{i}&=\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\displaystyle\frac{b_{i}b_{j}}{\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}}-b_{i}\hat{\sigma}&&\text{in }(0,\hat{\mathsf{T}}_{1})\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,N\\
\hat{x}_{i}(0)&=\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}-b_{i}\Theta_{\varepsilon}&&\text{if }i\neq k_{m}+1\text{ for each }m\\
\hat{x}_{i}(0)&=\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}-b_{i}(L\Theta_{\varepsilon}+(\Theta_{\varepsilon}-\Theta_{\varepsilon}^{m}))&&\text{if there exists an }m\text{ such that }i=k_{m}+1.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.84)

The addition of the small value
\Theta_{\varepsilon}-\Theta_{\varepsilon}^{m}>0
to the initial condition in (
3.84
) is not essential; we do it for convenience such that all particles which are close to collision are separated by the same distance, i.e.

\hat{x}_{k_{m}+1}(0)-\hat{x}_{k_{m}}(0)=(L+2)\Theta_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for all }m.

(3.85)

Lemma
3.8
and its proof apply with minor modifications. Indeed, the proof applies to each of the
M
collisions separately; the arguments are based on the fact that for any collision
m
, the other
n-2
particles remain an
\varepsilon
-independent, positive distance away from the colliding particles
\hat{x}_{k_{m}}
and
\hat{x}_{k_{m}+1}
during
[0,\hat{\mathsf{T}}_{1})
. Moreover, thanks to (
3.85
), the estimates on
\hat{\mathsf{T}}_{1}
do not depend on
m
, and thus the value of
\hat{\tau}_{\varepsilon}
(and then also
\hat{T}_{1}=\overline{T}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{\varepsilon}
) is independent of
m
.

With the equivalent of Lemma
3.8
established, the remainder of the argument in Subsection
3.4.1
for the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
holds with minor modifications. The main modification among them applies to (
3.69
), which does not hold for all
x\leq(\overline{x}_{k_{m}}^{\varepsilon}+\overline{x}_{k_{m}+1}^{\varepsilon})/2
and for all
m
. However, it does hold whenever
x\leq(\overline{x}_{k_{1}}^{\varepsilon}+\overline{x}_{k_{1}+1}^{\varepsilon})/2
or whenever
(\overline{x}_{k_{m}}+\overline{x}_{k_{m}+1})/2\leq x\leq(\overline{x}_{k_{m+1}}^{\varepsilon}+\overline{x}_{k_{m+1}+1}^{\varepsilon})/2
for some
m\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}
. The precise choice for the lower bound on
x
is not important; we choose the midpoint between two consecutive collision points because it is independent of
\varepsilon
and because it also appears in the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}
.
From the construction of
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
, as in Subsection
3.4.1
, we infer that

v(\hat{T}_{1},x)\leq\sum_{i\in S_{1}}u\left(\displaystyle\frac{x-\hat{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon};b_{i}\right)+\hat{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

with
\hat{\rho}_{\varepsilon}=o_{\varepsilon}(1)
and
\hat{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}=o_{\varepsilon}(1)
.

From the results in all three cases, the construction in Subsection
3.4.2
applies to build
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}
beyond
\hat{T}_{1}
. Consequently, (
3.1
) holds on
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

3.7
Induction step

The induction statement
P_{k}
is:

\overline{v}_{\varepsilon}(\overline{T}_{k},x)
is as in (
3.78
) with some parameters
\overline{c}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and
\overline{x}_{i}^{\varepsilon}
for
i\in S_{T_{k-1}}
which satisfy (
3.81
)-(
3.82
) for all
\varepsilon
small enough, and (
3.1
) holds on

.

In Section
3.6
we prove that
P_{1}

. This proof does not depend on the parameter

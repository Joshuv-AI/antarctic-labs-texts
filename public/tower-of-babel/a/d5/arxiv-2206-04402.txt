Abstract
. The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and of the first kind, which are respectively degenerate versions of the Stirling numbers of the second kind and of the first kind, appear frequently when we study various degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials. The aim of this paper is to consider the r r -truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, which reduce to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second for r = 1 r=1 , and to investigate their explicit expressions, some properties and related identities, in connection with several other degenerate special numbers and polynomials.

1.
Introduction

Carlitz [3,4] initiated to study the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and numbers, which are degenerate versions of the Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and numbers.
In recent years, studying degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials have regained interests of some mathematicians and yielded quite a few interesting results (see [8-15,17] and the references therein). It is noteworthy that studying degenerate versions is not only limited to polynomials but also extended to transcendental functions, like gamma functions. It is also remarkable that the degenerate umbral calculus is introduced as a degenerate version of the classical umbral calculus. Degenerate versions of special numbers and polynomials have been explored by various methods, including combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus techniques,
p
-adic analysis, differential equations, special functions, probability theory and analytic number theory.

The Stirling number of the second
S_{2}(n,k)
is the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets. The (signed) Stirling number of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
is defined such that the number of permutations of
n
elements having exactly
k
cycles is the nonnegative integer
(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k)=|S_{1}(n,k)|
. The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
5
), (
8
)) and of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
4
), (
7
)) were introduced as degenerate versions of the Stirling numbers of the second and of the first kind, respectively. These degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds appear frequently when we study degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials.

Here we consider the
r
-truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the second which reduce to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second for
r=1
.
The aim of this paper is by using generating functions to study their explicit expressions, some properties and related identities on the
r
-truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, in connection with the higher-order
r
-truncated degenerate Bernoulli polynomials, the degenerate Bernoulli numbers, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli numbers.

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k}=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8-12]),

(2)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1).

When
x=1
,
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}
, (see [7,13]).

In [3,4], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials of order
\alpha
given by

(3)

\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the degenerate Bernoulli numbers of order
\alpha
.

In particular, for
\alpha=1
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(1)}(x)
are called the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials.

For
n\geq 0
, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(4)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]),

where

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-19]).

As the inversion formula of (
4
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(5)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

The degenerate logarithm
\log_{\lambda}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{\lambda}(t)
satisfying
e_{\lambda}(\log_{\lambda}(t))=\log_{\lambda}(e_{\lambda}(t))
.
Then we have

(6)

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n-1}(1)_{n,\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{n!}t^{n},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

From (
4
) and (
5
), we note that

(7)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),

and

(8)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

Let
\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\displaystyle
. For
n\geq 0
, the operator
[t^{n}]
is defined by

(9)

[t^{n}]f(t)=a_{n},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]).

We agree that
S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr)=0

S_{2,\lambda}^{[1]}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0)

From (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(e_{\lambda}(t)-\sum_{l=0}^{r-1}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{n=kr}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{k}=n\\
l_{i}\geq r\end{subarray}}\frac{n!(1)_{l_{1},\lambda}(1)_{l_{2},\lambda}\cdots(1)_{l_{k},\lambda}}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{k}!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

9

Theorem 1
.

For
n,k\geq 0
with

From the binomial expansion, we note that

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(e_{\lambda}(t)-\sum_{l=0}^{r-1}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}e_{\lambda}^{k-m}(t)(-1)^{m}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{r-1}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{m}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{m}=0}^{r-1}\frac{n!(1)_{l_{1},\lambda}(1)_{l_{2},\lambda}\cdots(1)_{l_{m},\lambda}(k-m)_{n-l_{1}-l_{2}-\cdots-l_{m,\lambda}}}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{m}!(n-l_{1}-\cdots-l_{m})!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
12
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n,k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{m}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{m}=0}^{r-1}\frac{n!(1)_{l_{1},\lambda}(1)_{l_{2},\lambda}\cdots(1)_{l_{m},\lambda}(k-m)_{n-l_{1}-l_{2}-\cdots-l_{m,\lambda}}}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{m}!(n-l_{1}-\cdots-l_{m})!}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr),&\textrm{ if $n\geq kr$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $0\leq n<kr$.}\end{array}\right.

When
k=1
in (
10

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}=\bigg(e_{\lambda}(t)-\sum_{l=1}^{r-1}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n+r,r)\frac{t^{n+r}}{(n+r)!}

\displaystyle=t^{r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n+r,r)\frac{n!}{(n+r)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

For
k=2

(14)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=t^{2r}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(j+r,r)\frac{t^{j}}{(j+r)!}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(l+r,r)\frac{t^{l}}{(l+r)!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=t^{2r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(j+r,r)S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n-j+r,r)\frac{n!}{(n-j+r)!(j+r)!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Continuing this process, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=t^{kr}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{k}=n}\frac{n!S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(j_{1}+r,r)S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(j_{2}+r,r)\cdots S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(j_{k}+r,r)}{(j_{1}+r)!(j_{2}+r)!\cdots(j_{k}+r)!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (

(16)

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{n=kr}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=k!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n+kr,kr)\frac{t^{n+kr}}{(n+kr)!}

\displaystyle=t^{kr}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{k!S_{2,\lambda}^{[r]}(n+kr,kr)}{(n+kr)!}t^{n}.

It is well known that the partial Bell polynomials are defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle=(n+k)![t^{n}]\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j}t^{k-j}t}{k!t^{k}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}(n+k)!}{k!}[t^{n}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}[t^{n}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j-1}}{j!(k-j)!t^{j-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}(n+k)!}{k!}\frac{\beta_{n,\lambda}}{n!}+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j!(k-j)!}[t^{n}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j-1}}{t^{j-1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\beta_{n,\lambda}+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}(j-1)!}{j!(k-j)!}[t^{n}]\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}(l+j-1,j-1)}{(l+j-1)!}t^{l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\beta_{n,\lambda}+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j(k-j)!}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}(n+j-1,j-1)}{(n+j-1)!}.

Therefore, by (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n,k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)\binom{n+j}{k}\beta_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\binom{n+k}{k}\beta_{n,\lambda}+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j(k-j)!(n+j-1)!}S_{2,\lambda}(n+j-1,j-1).

Observe that, for any formal power series
f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}t^{i}
, we have

(30)

[t^{n}]f^{\prime}(t)=(n+1)a_{n+1}=(n+1)[t^{n+1}]f(t).

By making use of (
30
), we have

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+k-1}{j}S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)\beta_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)}{(n-j+k-1)!}\frac{\beta_{j,\lambda}}{j!}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}(n-j+k)[t^{n-j+k}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k}}{k!}[t^{j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j+k-1}]\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k}}{k!}\bigg)[t^{j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j+k-1}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k-1}(e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}(t)-1)}{(k-1)!}[t^{j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j+k-1}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k-1}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-\lambda t)}{(k-1)!(1+\lambda t)}[t^{j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k-1}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-\lambda t)}{t^{k-1}(k-1)!(1+\lambda t)}[t^{j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)![t^{n}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k-1}}{t^{k-2}(k-1)!(1+\lambda t)}

\displaystyle\qquad-(n+k-1)!\lambda[t^{n}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k-1}}{t^{k-2}(k-1)!(1+\lambda t)}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)![t^{n}]\bigg\{\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{m}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)}{(l+k-2)!}(-\lambda)^{m-l}t^{m}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{m}\sum_{l=k}^{j}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)}{(l+k-2)!}(-\lambda)^{j-l}\frac{\beta_{m-j,\lambda}}{(m-j)!}t^{m}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)}{(l+k-2)!}(-\lambda)^{n-l}

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda(n+k-1)!\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{j}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)\beta_{n-j,\lambda}(-\lambda)^{j-l}}{(l+k-2)!(n-j)!}.

Therefore, by (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n,k\geq 1
, with
n\geq k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+k-1}{j}S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)\beta_{j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(n+k-1)!\bigg\{\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)}{(l+k-2)!}(-\lambda)^{n-l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{j}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(l+k-2,2k-2)\beta_{n-j,\lambda}(-\lambda)^{j-l+1}}{(l+k-2)!(n-j)!}\bigg\}.

Now, we observe that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)\binom{n+k}{j}\beta_{j,\lambda}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)}{(n-j+k)!}\frac{\beta_{j,\lambda}^{(k)}}{j!}

\displaystyle=(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j+k}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k}}{k!}[t^{j}]\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{n-j}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t)^{k}}{t^{k}}[t^{j}]\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}[t^{n}]\bigg(1-\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}[t^{n}]\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{k-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\frac{\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k-j)}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
n,k\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{[2]}(n-j+k,2k)\binom{n+k}{j}\beta_{j,\lambda}^{(k)}=\binom{n+k}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(k-j)}.

From Theorems 4 and 5, we have

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{j+k}S_{2,\lambda}^{[3]}(j+k,3k)\beta_{n-j,\lambda}=(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}^{[3]}(j+k,3k)}{(j+k)!}\frac{\beta_{n-j,\lambda}}{(n-j)!}

\displaystyle=(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{j+k}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t-(1)_{2,\lambda}t^{2}/2)^{k}}{k!}[t^{n-j}]\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}

\displaystyle=(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}[t^{j}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t-(1)_{2,\lambda}t^{2}/2)^{k}}{t^{k}k!}[t^{n-j}]\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(n+k)![t^{n}]\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1-t-(1)_{2,\lambda}t^{2}/2)^{k}t}{t^{k}k!(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}

\displaystyle=(n+k)![t^{n}]\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j}(t+(1-\lambda)\frac{t^{2}}{2})^{k-j}t}{k!(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)t^{k}}

\displaystyle=(n+k)![t^{n}]\frac{(-1)^{k}\big(1+(1-\lambda)\frac{t}{2}\big)^{k}t}{k!(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}[t^{n}]\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j-1}t^{1-j}\bigg(1+(1-\lambda)\frac{t}{2}\bigg)^{k-j}

\displaystyle=(n+k)![t^{n}]\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg((-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{k}{j}(1-\lambda)^{j}\frac{\beta_{m-j,\lambda}}{2^{j}(m-j)!k!}\bigg)t^{m}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(n+k)!}{k!}[t^{n}]\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{j-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k-j}\binom{k-j}{l}(1-\lambda)^{l}\frac{t^{l-j+1}}{2^{l}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{k}{j}(1-\lambda)^{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}(n-j)!k!}\beta_{n-j,\lambda}

\displaystyle+(n+k)![t^{n}]\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j(k-j)!}\sum_{l_{1}=j-1}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(l_{1},j-1)\frac{t^{l_{1}}}{l_{1}!}\sum_{l=1}^{k-j}\binom{k-j}{l}\frac{(1-\lambda)^{l}}{2^{l}}t^{l-j+1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{k}{j}(1-\lambda)^{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}(n-j)!k!}\beta_{n-j,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j(k-j)!}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\binom{k-j}{l}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}(n-l+j-1,j-1)(1-\lambda)^{l}}{2^{l}(n-l+j-1)!}.

Therefore, by (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
n,k\geq 0
, we have

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n+k}{j+k}S_{2,\lambda}^{[3]}(j+k,3k)\beta_{n-j,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}(n+k)!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{k}{j}(1-\lambda)^{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}(n-j)!k!}\beta_{n-j,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+(n+k)!\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{j(k-j)!}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\binom{k-j}{l}\frac{S_{2,\lambda}(n-l+j-1,j-1)(1-\lambda)^{l}}{2^{l}(n-l+j-1)!}.

Remark 9
.

As the counterpart of (
10
), we may consider the
r
-truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind given by

(35)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)-\sum_{l=1}^{r-1}\frac{(1)_{l,\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{l-1}}{l!}t^{l}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=kr}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{[r]}(n,kr)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
r
is a positive integer. These numbers will be investigated in a forthcoming paper.

3.
Conclusion

In recent years, we have witnessed that many degenerate versions of quite a few special numbers and polynomials were investigated and some nice results were obtained by adopting various tools.

In this paper, we considered the
r
-truncated degenerate Stirling numbers of the second, which reduce to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second for
r=1
, and studied by using generating functions their explicit expressions, some properties and related identities on those numbers, in connection with several other degenerate special numbers and polynomials.

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue this line of research, namely, to explore various degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials, and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering.

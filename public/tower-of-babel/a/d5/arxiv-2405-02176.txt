On a question of Kwakkel and Markovic on existence of wandering domains with bounded geometry Thanks: Supported by NSF grant DMS-2247364.
Sergei Merenkov

Abstract
. A question of F. Kwakkel and V. Markovic on existence of 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}^{1} -diffeomorphisms of closed surfaces that permute a dense collection of domains with bounded geometry is answered in the negative. In fact, it is proved that for closed surfaces of genus at least one such diffeomorphisms do not exist regardless of whether they have positive or zero topological entropy.



1.
Introduction

In
[
KM10
]
, F. Kwakkel and V. Markovic posed the following question (see below for the definitions):

Question 1.1
.

[
KM10
, Question 1, p. 512]

Let
M
be a closed surface. Do there exist diffeomorphisms
f\in{\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
with positive entropy that permute a dense collection of domains with bounded geometry?

The authors gave a negative answer under the assumption that
f\in{\rm Diff}^{1+\alpha}(M)
with
\alpha>0
.
In this paper we answer the above question, also in the negative. In fact, we prove that, unless
M
is the sphere, there are no such diffeomorphisms regardless of whether the entropy is positive or zero.

We recall some definitions from
[
KM10
]
. A
closed surface

M
is a smooth, closed, oriented Riemannian 2-manifold, equipped with the canonical metric induced from the standard conformal metric of the universal cover
{\hat{\mathbb{C}}},{\mathbb{C}}
, or
{\mathbb{D}}
, the sphere, the plane, or the unit disk, respectively. Let
S\subset M
be compact and
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
the collection of connected components of the complement of
S
, with the property that
{\rm Int}({\rm Cl}(D_{k}))=D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
{\rm Int}
and
{\rm Cl}
stand for the interior and the closure, respectively.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a homeomorphism
f
of
M
permutes a dense collection of domains
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
if

(1)

f(S)=S
and
{\rm Cl}(D_{k})\cap{\rm Cl}(D_{k^{\prime}})=\emptyset
if
k\neq k^{\prime}
,

(2)

f^{n}(D_{k})\cap D_{k}=\emptyset
for all
k,n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ n\neq 0
, and

(3)

\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
is dense in
M
.

We refer to the invariant set
S
in Definition
1.2
as a
residual set
of
f
.

Definition 1.3
.

A collection of domains
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
on a surface
M
is said to have bounded geometry if
{\rm Cl}(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are contractible in
M

and there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for every domain
D_{k}
in the collection, there are
p_{k}\in D_{k}
and
0<r_{k}\leq R_{k}
with

B(p_{k},r_{k})\subseteq D_{k}\subseteq B(p_{k},R_{k})\quad{\rm and}\quad R_{k}/r_{k}\leq C.

Here and in what follows,
B(p,r)
denotes the open disk in
M
centered at
p
of radius
r>0
.

Note that if
M
is a closed surface, bounded geometry of domains in
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
implies that
{\rm diam}\,D_{k}\to 0
as
|k|\to\infty
, where
{\rm diam}
stands for the diameter.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
M
is a closed surface, other than the sphere, then there does not exist
f\in{\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
that permutes a dense collection of domains with bounded geometry.

Theorem
1.4
is false in the case when
M={\hat{\mathbb{C}}}
, as can be seen by taking
f(z)=\lambda z,\ \lambda>1
. Indeed, one needs to fill the fundamental annulus
\{z\colon 1\leq|z|<\lambda\}
with geometric disks and spread them out using the dynamics of
f
.
In addition, without the bounded geometry assumption the conclusion fails even in the
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
class. This can be seen by choosing two
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphisms
g,h
of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}^{1}
that are semi-conjugate but not conjugate to irrational rotations, i.e.,
g
and
h
have wandering intervals, and taking
f=g\times h
, a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. Such diffeomorphisms
g,h
exist by
[
He79
]
, and
f
permutes the collection
\{I\times J\}
, where
I
and
J
are wandering intervals of
g
and
h
, respectively.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we demonstrate existence of an invariant conformal structure on the residual set of a hypothetical
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism
f
of
M
that permutes a dense collection of domains with bounded geometry. In Section
3
we discuss transboundary modulus of curve families and prove existence and uniqueness of extremal distributions for such a modulus. After a short discussion of the Nielsen–Thurston classification in Section
4
, we proceed by first eliminating in Section
5
the possibility of a hypothetical diffeomorphism
f
above to be pseudo-Anosov, and then, with the aid of holomorphic quadratic differentials discussed in Section
6
, we exclude periodic and reducible diffeomorphisms in the final Section
7
.

2.
Invariant Conformal Structure

A non-constant orientation preserving homeomorphism
h\colon U\to U^{\prime}

between open subsets
U
and
U^{\prime}
of Riemann surfaces
M
and
M^{\prime}
, respectively, is called
quasiconformal
, or
K
-
quasiconformal
, if the map
h
is in the Sobolev space
W_{\rm loc}^{1,2}

and

K_{h}(p)=\frac{\max_{|v|=1}|D_{p}h(v)|}{\min_{|v|=1}|D_{p}h(v)|}\leq K,\quad{\rm a.e.}\ p\in U,

where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm.
In this case we say that the
dilatation
of
h
is bounded by
K
.
The assumption
h\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,2}
means that
h
and the first distributional partial derivatives of
h
are locally in the Lebesgue space
L^{2}
.
If
S
is a measurable subset of
U
and
K_{h}(p)=1
for a.e.
p\in S
, then
the map
h
is called
conformal
on
S
.

We will need the following lemma, which we also prove.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
KM10
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
M
be a closed surface and let
f\in{\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
permute a dense collection of domains
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. If the domains in
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
have bounded geometry, then
f^{n},\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, have uniformly bounded dilatations on
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
. I.e.,
K_{f^{n}}(p)
is bounded above by a constant independent of
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p\in S
.

Remark 2.2
.

Lemma
2.1
is the only result where the assumption that
f
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism is needed to prove the main Theorem
1.4
. Once Lemma
2.1
is proved below, the rest of the arguments hold true for an arbitrary quasiconformal
f
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
\widetilde{M}
denote the universal cover of
M
and
\psi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
be the projection map.
Since
\psi
is a local isometry, below we make no distinction between
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, that have sufficiently small diameters and their lifts to
\widetilde{M}
and, whenever convenient, identify the map
f
with its lift
\tilde{f}
to
\widetilde{M}
under the map
\psi
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be fixed.
Since
S
is nowhere dense,
{\rm Int}({\rm Cl}(D_{k}))=D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and the closures
{\rm Cl}(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are pairwise disjoint, and
{\rm diam}\,D_{k}\to 0
as
|k|\to\infty
, which follows from the bounded
geometry assumption, for each
p\in S
there exists a sequence of complementary components
(D_{k_{i}})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
S
that accumulate at
p
, i.e.,

d_{\rm Hausd}(\overline{D_{k_{i}}},\{p\})\to 0\ {\rm as}\ i\to\infty,

where
d_{\rm Hausd}
denotes the Hausdorff distance.
Also, since
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-differentiable on a compact surface
M
, we have

(1)

f^{n}(q+x)-f^{n}(q)-D_{q}f^{n}(x)=o(x),\quad x\to 0,

where
|o(x)|/|x|\to 0
as
|x|\to 0
, uniformly in
q
. Note that, as discussed in the first paragraph of this proof, here we abused the notation and wrote
f^{n}
instead of
\psi^{-1}\circ f^{n}\circ\psi
with consistent choice of the branch of
\psi^{-1}
, and
q
and
x
are elements of the universal cover, i.e.,
{\mathbb{D}},{\mathbb{C}}
, or
{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
.

The domains
D_{k_{i}},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, having bounded geometry means that there exist
C\geq 1,p_{i}\in D_{k_{i}}
, and
0<r_{i}\leq R_{i},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with

B(p_{i},r_{i})\subseteq D_{k_{i}}\subseteq B(p_{i},R_{i}),\quad{\rm and}\quad{R_{i}}/{r_{i}}\leq C.

Thus, by possibly passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the Hausdorff limit of the rescaled domains

\left((D_{k_{i}}-p_{i})/r_{i}\right)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

is a closed set
\Omega
such that

(2)

\overline{B(0,1)}\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\overline{B(0,C)}.

Again, here we identified
p_{i}
and
D_{k_{i}},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with the corresponding lifts under the projection map
\psi
.
Let
D_{k_{i}}^{\prime},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote the image of
D_{k_{i}},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, under the map
f^{n}
.
Because
f^{n}
is in
{\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
, and hence is locally bi-Lipschitz, after possibly passing to yet a further subsequence, we may assume that the sequence

\left((D_{k_{i}}^{\prime}-f^{n}(p_{i}))/r_{i}\right)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}

Hausdorff converges to a closed set
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Similar to (
2
), we know that there exists
p^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}>0
such that

(3)

\overline{B(p^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{B(p^{\prime},Cr^{\prime})},

where the constant
C\geq 1
is the same as above.
Since Equation (
1
) is uniform in
q
, in particular it holds for
q=p_{i},\ q+x\in D_{k_{i}},\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one concludes that

(4)

\Omega^{\prime}=D_{p}f^{n}(\Omega).

Combining (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
), and using the fact that the constant
C
does not depend on
p\in S
or
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we conclude that the maps
f^{n},\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, have uniformly bounded dilatations on
S
, as claimed.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
1.4
we use a modification, Proposition
2.3
below, of a result by D. Sullivan
[
Su78
]
or P. Tukia
[
Tu80
]
.
A
Beltrami form
on a measurable subset
S
of a Riemann surface
M
is a measurable
(-1,1)
-form on
S
given in a local chart by
\mu=\mu(z)d\bar{z}/dz
, where we assume that
\|\mu\|_{\infty}<1
; see, e.g.,
[
BF14
]
for background on Beltrami forms. The measurable function
z\mapsto\mu(z)
is called a
Beltrami coefficient
of
\mu
. It is dependent on a local chart.
If
h\colon U\to U^{\prime}
is a quasiconformal map between open subsets
U
and
U^{\prime}
of Riemann surfaces
M
and
M^{\prime}
, respectively, and
\mu
is a Beltrami form on
U^{\prime}
, the
pullback
of
\mu
under
h
is the Beltrami form given in local charts by

h^{*}(\mu)=\left(\frac{h_{\bar{z}}+\mu(h)\overline{h_{z}}}{h_{z}+\mu(h)\overline{h_{\bar{z}}}}\right)\frac{d\bar{z}}{dz}.

The pullback of the 0 Beltrami form is denoted by
\mu_{h}
.
We say that a Beltrami form
\mu
on
S
is
invariant
under
h

if
S
is
invariant
under
h
, i.e.,
S\subseteq U=U^{\prime},\ h(S)=S
up to a measure zero set, and

\mu=h^{*}(\mu).

In particular, the 0 Beltrami form is invariant under
h
if and only if
h
is conformal on
S
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface and
S\subseteq M
be a measurable subset. Let
H
be a countable group of quasiconformal maps
h
of
M
that leave
S
invariant and that have uniformly bounded dilatation on
S
. Then there exists a Beltrami form
\mu
on
S
that is invariant under each map
h\in H
.

Proof.

We may assume that
S
has positive measure, for otherwise there is nothing to prove.

For arbitrary quasiconformal maps
f,g
, expressing the Beltrami coefficient
\mu_{g\circ f}
in terms of Beltrami coefficients
\mu_{g}
and
\mu_{f}
in some local charts, we have

(5)

\mu_{g\circ f}=f^{*}(\mu_{g})=T_{f}(\mu_{g}(f)),

where

T_{f}(w)=\frac{\mu_{f}+\frac{\overline{f_{z}}}{f_{z}}w}{1+\overline{\mu_{f}}\frac{\overline{f_{z}}}{f_{z}}w}

is an isometry of the disk model hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{D}}
for a.e.
z
. Namely,
T_{f}
is a hyperbolic isometry in
w
for each fixed
z
where
T_{f}
is defined, i.e., at each point of differentiability of
f
with
f_{z}\neq 0
. Such points
z
form a set of full measure.

We consider

B(z)=\{\mu_{h}(z)\colon\ h\in H\},

a subset of
{\mathbb{D}}
defined for a.e.
z\in S
up to a rotation which depends on a local chart, since if
\zeta=\zeta(z)
is a conformal change of local charts,
\mu_{h}(z)=\mu_{h}(\zeta(z))\frac{\overline{\zeta^{\prime}(z)}}{\zeta^{\prime}(z)}
, i.e., the value of
\mu_{h}
is multiplied by a complex number of modulus 1, a rotation.
Since
H
is a group, Equation (
5
) gives

(6)

\displaystyle T_{h}(B(h(z)))

\displaystyle=\left\{T_{h}(\mu_{h^{\prime}}(h(z)))\colon h^{\prime}\in H\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mu_{h^{\prime}\circ h}(z)\colon h^{\prime}\in H\right\}=B(z),

for each
h\in H
, a.e.
z\in S
, and an appropriate choice of local charts
z
and
h(z)
.
If
B
is a non-empty bounded subset of
{\mathbb{D}}
, we define by
P(B)
to be the center of the smallest closed hyperbolic disk that contains
B
. Elementary hyperbolic geometry shows that
P(B)
is well defined.
Moreover,
P
satisfies (see
[
Tu80
]
for details):

1) if
B
is contained in a hyperbolic disk centered at 0 with radius
\delta
in the hyperbolic metric of
{\mathbb{D}}
, then
P(B)
is at most hyperbolic distance
\delta
from the origin in
{\mathbb{D}}
;

2) the map
P
commutes with each hyperbolic isometry of
{\mathbb{D}}
, i.e.,

P(T(B))=T(P(B))

for any hyperbolic isometry
T
of
{\mathbb{D}}
and arbitrary non-empty bounded subset
B
of
{\mathbb{D}}
.

We now define, for a.e.
z\in S
,

\mu(z)=P(B(z)).

This definition, even though dependent on local charts, gives rise to a global Beltrami form
\mu
on
M
because
P
commutes with hyperbolic isometries, and in particular with rotations.
The assumption that the elements of
H
are
K
-quasiconformal on
S
with the same constant
K
and property 1) above give that
\mu
is an essentially bounded Beltrami form.
Equation (
6
) combined with the commutative property 2) give, in appropriate local charts,

\mu(z)=P(B(z))=P(T_{h}(B(h(z))))=T_{h}(P(B(h(z))))=T_{h}(\mu(h(z))),

for each
h\in H
and a.e.
z
in
S
.
By (
5
), this is equivalent to

\mu=h^{*}(\mu),

on
S
for each
h\in H
, i.e.,
\mu
is invariant under each
h\in H
.
∎

An alternative proof of Proposition
2.3
uses the barycenter of the convex hull to define
\mu
rather than the center of the smallest hyperbolic disk as above. This approach was used in
[
Su78
]
.

An application of the Measurable Riemann Mapping Theorem
[
BF14
, Theorem 1.27]
gives that for the Beltrami form
\mu
from Proposition
2.3
, extended by 0 outside
S
, there exists a Riemann surface
M^{\prime}
and a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi\colon M\to M^{\prime}
such that
\mu=\mu_{\phi}
. Since conformality of a quasiconformal map
h
of
M
on an invariant measurable subset
S
is equivalent to the 0 Beltrami form being invariant under
h
, we get the following corollary to Proposition
2.3
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface and
S\subseteq M
be a measurable subset. Let
H
be a countable group of quasiconformal maps
h
of
M
that leave
S
invariant and that have uniformly bounded dilatation on
S
. Then there exists a quasiconformal map
\phi\colon M\to M^{\prime}
of
M
onto another Riemann surface
M^{\prime}
such that the conjugate family
H^{\prime}=\{\phi\circ h\circ\phi^{-1}\colon h\in H\}
consists of quasiconformal maps of
M^{\prime}
that leave
S^{\prime}=\phi(S)
invariant and are conformal on it.

In particular, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
M
is a closed Riemann surface and

f\in{\rm Diff}^{1}(M)
permutes a dense collection of domains with bounded geometry, then there exists a quasiconformal map
\phi\colon M\to M^{\prime}
of
M
onto another Riemann surface
M^{\prime}
such that for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the maps
\phi\circ f^{n}\circ\phi^{-1}
are all quasiconformal maps that are conformal on
S^{\prime}=\phi(S)
, where
S
is the residual set of
f
.

Proof.

We first apply Lemma
2.1
to conclude that
f^{n},\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, have uniformly bounded dilatations on the residual set
S
of
f
, and then apply Corollary
2.4
to the residual set
S
of
f
and the cyclic group
f^{n},\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Therefore, in what follows, we may and will assume that
f
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
M
that is conformal on its residual set
S
.

3.
Transboundary modulus

The following notion of modulus is inspired by O. Schramm’s transboundary extremal length, introduced in
[
Sch95
]
. An adaptation in the setting of Sierpiński carpets has been used extensively, notably in
[
Bo11
,
BM13
]
.

Let
M
be a closed surface and
\sigma
the area measure on
M
. If
\gamma\colon I\to M
is a rectifiable curve, where
I
is a closed interval, then
\gamma
factors as
\gamma=\gamma_{s}\circ s_{\gamma}
, where
s_{\gamma}\colon I\to[0,l(\gamma)]
is the associated
length function
, with
l(\gamma)
being the length of
\gamma
, and
\gamma_{s}\colon[0,l(\gamma)]\to M
is the unique 1-Lipschitz continuous map, the
arc length parametrization
of
\gamma
; see
[
Va71
]
for more details. For a non-negative Borel measurable function
\rho
on
M
and a rectifiable curve
\gamma
as above,
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds
is defined to be
\int_{0}^{l(\gamma)}\rho(\gamma_{s}(t))dt
. If
\gamma
is only locally rectifiable, we define
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\sup\int_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rho ds
, where the supremum is taken over all rectifiable subcurves
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\gamma
.

By a
mass density
we mean a non-negative Borel measurable function
\rho
on
M
. If
\Gamma
is a curve family in
M
, a mass density
\rho
is called
admissible
for
\Gamma
if

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds\geq 1,\quad{\rm for\ all\ locally\ rectifiable}\ \gamma\in\Gamma,

where
ds
denotes the arc-length element.
The
2
-
modulus
of
\Gamma
is defined as

{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma)=\inf\left\{\int_{M}\rho^{2}d\sigma\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all
\rho
that are admissible for
\Gamma
.

Let
S\subset M
be a compact set and
M\setminus S=\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
, where
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a non-empty collection of disjoint complementary components of
S
.
If

\Gamma
is a family of curves in

M
, then we define the
transboundary modulus
of
\Gamma
with respect to
S
, denoted by
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)
, as follows. Let
\rho
be a
mass distribution for

S
, i.e., a function
\rho\colon S\cup\{D_{k}\colon k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\to[0,\infty]
, where
the restriction
\rho|_{S}
is a non-negative Borel measurable function, and
\rho(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is a non-negative number for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We refer to
\rho|_{S}
as the
continuous part
of a mass distribution
\rho
and to
\{\rho(D_{k})\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
as the
discrete part
.
A mass distribution
\rho
is
admissible
for

\Gamma
if
there exists
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma,\ {\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{0})=0
, called an
exceptional
curve family, such that

(7)

\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(D_{k})\geq 1,

for each

\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}
. Here, if
\gamma
is rectifiable,

\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho\,ds=\int_{[0,l(\gamma)]\cap\gamma_{s}^{-1}(S)}\rho(\gamma_{s}(t))dt,

and extended as above to locally rectifiable curves. Note that non-locally rectifiable curves have 2-modulus 0, and therefore excluded.
We set

(8)

{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)=\inf\left\{{\rm mass}(\rho)\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all mass distributions
\rho
that
are admissible for
\Gamma
, and

(9)

{\rm mass}(\rho)=\int_{S}\rho^{2}d\sigma+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho(D_{k})^{2}

is the
total mass
of
\rho
.
If the set
S
has measure zero, then we ignore the continuous part of the distribution
\rho
in (
7
) and (
9
).
Excluding an exceptional curve family
\Gamma_{0}
ensures that in many interesting cases
and for some relevant curve families
\Gamma

an admissible mass distribution exists and
0<{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
.

We think of a mass distribution
\rho
as an element of the direct sum Hilbert space
L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
, where
L^{2}=L^{2}(S)
, and
l^{2}
consists of all sequences
(\rho_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with
\sum_{k}\rho_{k}^{2}<\infty
. The norm of
\rho\in L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
is given by

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}\oplus l^{2}}=\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{l^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
, where

\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}=\left(\int_{S}\rho^{2}d\sigma\right)^{1/2},\quad\|\rho\|_{l^{2}}=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Transboundary modulus has some familiar properties of the
2
-modulus, with almost identical proofs, e.g., quasi-invariance under quasiconformal maps, monotonicity, and subadditivity; see
[
Va71
]
for classical background.
Quasi-invariance
is the property that if
h\colon M\to M^{\prime}
is a quasiconformal map between two Riemann surfaces, then there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for every curve family
\Gamma
in
M
one has

\frac{1}{C}\,{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)\leq{\rm mod}_{S^{\prime}}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq C\,{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma),

where
S^{\prime}=h(S)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=h(\Gamma)=\{h(\gamma)\colon\gamma\in\Gamma\}
. In fact, if

is
K
-quasiconformal, then one can choose
C=K
above. If
h
is conformal on
S
, the modulus is invariant, i.e.,
C=1
; see Lemma
3.7
below.

Monotonicity
means that if
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
, then

{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma).

Subadditivity
says that if
\Gamma=\cup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Gamma_{i}
, then

{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm mod}(\Gamma_{i}).

The following proposition is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\Gamma
is a curve family with
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
, and if

\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
have bounded geometry, then an extremal distribution
\rho
, i.e., an admissible distribution for which the infimum in the definition (
8
) is achieved, exists and is unique as an element of
L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
.

The proof of this proposition below mimics that of
[
BM13
, Proposition 2.4]
and requires auxiliary results.

3.1.
Auxiliary lemmas

As above, let

its area measure.
To prove Proposition

Lemma 3.2
.

[
Bo88
]
,
[

Let

be a finite or countable collection of disjoint geodesic disks in a closed surface

Remark 3.3
.

In the above cited sources the lemma is proved in the planar or spherical settings, but it extends to arbitrary closed surfaces with an almost identical proof using an uncentered maximal function.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
be a closed surface and
S\subset M
be a compact subset such that
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
, where the domains
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, have bounded geometry. If
\delta>0
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any curve family
\Gamma
in
M
consisting of curves
\gamma
with
{\rm diam}(\gamma)\geq\delta
, we have

{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma)\leq C\cdot{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma).

Proof.

We assume that
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)
is finite for otherwise there is nothing to prove.
Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary.
There exists a mass distribution
\rho
with
{\rm mass}(\rho)<{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)+\epsilon
, and an exceptional curve family
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
such that

\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(D_{k})\geq 1,

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}
.

Recall, the bounded geometry assumption is, for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the existence of disks
B(p_{k},r_{k}),B(p_{k},R_{k})
such that

B(p_{k},r_{k})\subseteq D_{k}\subseteq B(p_{k},R_{k}),\quad{\rm with}\quad{R_{k}}/{r_{k}}\leq C.

Let
r=\sup\{r_{k}\}>0
, a constant that depends on
S
.
We define a mass density
\tilde{\rho}
on
M
by the formula

\tilde{\rho}=\rho+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\rho(D_{k})}{r_{k}}\chi_{B(p_{k},2R_{k})},

where the first summand is extended to the complement of
S
as 0. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}
be an arbitrary locally rectifible curve. If
\gamma
intersects
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and is completely contained in
B(p_{k},2R_{k})
, then

\int_{\gamma}\chi_{B(p_{k},2R_{k})}ds\geq\delta.

Otherwise, i.e., if
\gamma
is not completely contained in
B(p_{k},2R_{k})
, it intersects both complementary components of
B(p_{k},2R_{k})\setminus\overline{B(p_{k},R_{k})}
, and thus

\int_{\gamma}\chi_{B(p_{k},2R_{k})}ds\geq R_{k}.

Therefore,

\int_{\gamma}\tilde{\rho}\,ds\geq\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho\,ds+\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{r},1\right\}\cdot\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(D_{k})\geq\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{r},1\right\},

i.e.,
\tilde{\rho}/\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{r},1\right\}
is admissible for
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}
.
Also, using Lemma
3.2
we conclude

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\tilde{\rho}^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{S}\rho^{2}d\sigma+C^{2}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\rho(D_{k})^{2}}{r_{k}^{2}}\sigma\left(B(p_{k},r_{k})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\cdot{\rm mass}(\rho)<C_{1}\left({\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)+\epsilon\right),

where
C_{1}
is a constant that depends on the surface
M
and the set
S
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0})+{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{0})

\displaystyle={\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0})\leq C_{2}\left({\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)+\epsilon\right),

for
C_{2}=C_{1}/\left(\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{r},1\right\}\right)^{2}
.
Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma)\leq C_{2}\cdot{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma).

∎

Remark 3.5
.

The inequality in the previous lemma fails without the lower bound assumption on diameters. Indeed, it is elementary to produce a family of curves

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
M
be a closed surface and
S\subset M
be a compact subset such that

, where the domains

, have bounded geometry. If

is a curve family in

{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
does not contain constant curves, we have

\Gamma=\cup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Gamma_{i},\quad{\rm where}\quad\Gamma_{i}=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\ {\rm diam}(\gamma)\geq 1/i\}.

gives that

then gives

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

Let
(\rho_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a minimizing sequence of admissible mass distributions for
\Gamma
, i.e.,

{\rm mass}(\rho_{i})\to{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma),\quad i\to\infty.

The condition
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
allows us to assume that
{\rm mass}(\rho_{i})\leq C
for some constant
C>0
and all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By the Banach–Alaoglu Theorem, there exists a subsequence of
(\rho_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
that weakly converges to
\rho\in L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
.
In particular, we may assume that the limits

\rho(D_{k})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rho_{i}(D_{k})

exist for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. For the continuous part of the mass distribution we choose a non-negative Borel measurable representative of the
L^{2}
-summand of
\rho
. We claim that such a representative along with
\rho(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is an extremal mass distribution.

One inequality, namely
{\rm mass}(\rho)\leq{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)
follows from the weak lower semicontinuity of norms.
To show the reverse inequality, one needs to demonstrate that
\rho
is admissible for
\Gamma
.
Since the sequence
(\rho_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges weakly to
\rho\in L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
, Mazur’s Lemma
[
Yo80
, Th. 2, p. 120]
gives that there is a sequence
(\tilde{\rho}_{K})_{K\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of convex combinations,

\tilde{\rho}_{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{K,i}\rho_{i},

that strongly converges to
\rho
in
L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
.
Since each
\tilde{\rho}_{K},\ K\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is trivially admissible for
\Gamma
, the sequence
(\tilde{\rho}_{K})_{K\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is also a minimizing sequence for
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)
.
An exceptional curve family
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{K}
for
\tilde{\rho}_{K}
is the union of exceptional curve families for
\rho_{i},\ i=1,2,\dots,K
.
By possibly passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho\right\|_{L^{2}\oplus l^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{K}},\quad K\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let

\Gamma_{K}^{\prime}=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon\int_{\gamma\cap S}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho|\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}(D_{k})-\rho(D_{k})|\geq\frac{1}{K}\right\},

and

\Gamma^{\prime}=\cap_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\cup_{K\geq i}\Gamma^{\prime}_{K}.

From this definition we have that if
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
, then

\lim_{K\to\infty}\left(\int_{\gamma\cap S}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho|\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}(D_{k})-\rho(D_{k})|\right)=0.

The mass distributions

\rho_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{K=i}^{\infty}K|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho|,\quad i\in{\mathbb{N}},

are admissible for
\Gamma^{\prime}
, and since
\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho\right\|_{L^{2}\oplus l^{2}}\leq{1}/{2^{K}}
, we have that

{\rm mass}(\rho_{i}^{\prime})\to 0,\ i\to\infty.

Thus,
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma^{\prime})=0
. Corollary
3.6
then gives
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})=0
. We conclude that
\Gamma_{\infty}=\Gamma^{\prime}\cup\left(\cup_{K\in{\mathbb{N}}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{K}\right)
is an exceptional family for
\rho
. Indeed, subadditivity gives
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{\infty})=0
, and for each
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{\infty}
, since each
\tilde{\rho}_{K}
is admissible,

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(D_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\lim_{K\to\infty}\left(\int_{\gamma\cap S}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}-\rho|\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}|\tilde{\rho}_{K}(D_{k})-\rho(D_{k})|\right)=1.

This completes the proof of existence of an extremal mass distribution for
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)
.

A simple convexity argument implies that an extremal distribution
\rho
is also unique. Indeed, if there exists another, different, extremal mass distribution
\rho^{\prime}
, then
the average
(\rho+\rho^{\prime})/2
is also admissible for
\Gamma
. The strict convexity of
L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
, now gives that the average has a strictly smaller total mass, a contradiction.
∎

To finish this section, we prove the following conformal invariance lemma, Lemma
3.7
below, that makes transboundary modulus particularly useful. Let
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
be a homeomorphism between Riemann surfaces that
takes a compact set
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
onto
S^{\prime}=M^{\prime}\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}^{\prime}
.
Assume that
f
is conformal on
S
, and
\rho
is a mass distribution for
S^{\prime}
. Let

L_{f}(p)=\limsup_{q\to p}\frac{|f(q)-f(p)|}{|q-p|},

where
|\cdot|
now stands for the intrinsic distance.
This is a non-negative Borel measurable function with
L_{f}(p)=|D_{p}f|
at each point
p
of differentiability of
f

[
Va71
, §5]
.
We define the
pullback distribution

f^{*}(\rho)
to be
\rho(f)L_{f}
on
S
and
f^{*}(\rho)(D_{k})=\rho(f(D_{k})),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
be a quasiconformal homeomorphism that is conformal on
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
. Let
\Gamma
be an arbitrary curve family in
M
. If
\rho
is the extremal mass distribution for
f(\Gamma)=\{f(\gamma)\colon\gamma\in\Gamma\}
, then the pullback distribution
f^{*}(\rho)
is the extremal mass distribution for
\Gamma
.
Moreover,

{\rm mod}_{f(S)}(f(\Gamma))={\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma).

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq f(\Gamma)
be an exceptional curve family for
\rho
, and let
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}=f^{-1}(\Gamma_{0})
be the preimage subfamily of
\Gamma
. Then, since
f
is quasiconformal, the geometric definition of quasiconformality gives
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime})=0
; see
[
He01
]
or
[
Va71
]
.
In addition, let
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime\prime}
be the subfamily of
\Gamma
consisting of all locally rectifiable curves which have closed subcurves on which
f
is not absolutely continuous. Fuglede’s Theorem
[
Va71
, Theorem 28.2]
gives
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime\prime})=0
, and thus
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\cup\Gamma_{0}^{\prime\prime})=0
. If
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus(\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}\cup\Gamma_{0}^{\prime\prime})
, then, applying
[
Va71
, Theorem 5.3]
,

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma\cap S}\rho(f)L_{f}\,ds+\sum_{k\colon\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(f(D_{k}))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{f(\gamma)\cap S}\rho\,ds+\sum_{k\colon f(\gamma)\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}\rho(D_{k})\geq 1,

i.e., the pullback mass distribution
f^{*}(\rho)
is admissible for
\Gamma
. The total mass of
f^{*}(\rho)
is

\displaystyle\int_{S}\rho(f)^{2}L_{f}^{2}d\sigma+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho(f(D_{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\rho(f)^{2}|Df|^{2}d\sigma+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho(f(D_{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S}\rho(f)^{2}|J_{f}|d\sigma+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho(f(D_{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\rho^{2}d\sigma+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho(D_{k})^{2}={\rm mass}(\rho),

where

is the Jacobian determinant of

, and the above inequality uses the assumption that

is conformal on

. Therefore,

{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)\leq{\rm mod}_{S}(f(\Gamma)).

The converse inequality, and indeed the full lemma, follow from the facts that the inverse of a quasiconformal map is quasiconformal, and the inverse of a conformal map is conformal.
∎

4.
Nielsen–Thurston Classification

Recall that the
mapping class group
of a closed surface
M
, denoted
{\rm Mod}(M)
, is the group of isotopy classes of orientation preserving homeomorphisms
{\rm Homeo}^{+}(M)
. Equivalently,

{\rm Mod}(S)={\rm Homeo}^{+}(M)/{\rm Homeo}_{0}(M),

where
{\rm Homeo}_{0}(M)
denotes the connected component of the identity in
{\rm Homeo}^{+}(M)
.
There are several equivalent variations of the definition of the mapping class group, for example, where homeomorphisms are replaced by diffeomorphisms or isotopy classes replaced by homotopy classes. For this and other facts and properties of mapping class groups one can consult
[
FM12
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
FM12
, Theorems 13.1, 13.2]

If
M
is a closed surface, each class
[f]\in{\rm Mod}(M)
is either periodic, or reducible, or pseudo-Anosov.

Recall, an element
f\in{\rm Mod}(M)
is called
periodic
if there is
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f^{n}
is isotopic to the identity. An element
f\in{\rm Mod}(M)
is called
reducible
if there is a non-empty set
\{[c_{1}],\dots,[c_{k}]\}
of isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves in
M
such that
i([c_{i}],[c_{j}])=0,\ i\neq j
, and
[f(c_{i})]=[c_{i}]
for all
i=1,2\dots,k
. Here,
[c]
denotes the homotopy class of a simple closed curve
c
, and
i([a],[b])
denotes the
geometric intersection number
, i.e., the minimal number of intersection points between a representative curve in the homotopy class
[a]
and a representative curve in the class
[b]
.
Finally, an element
f\in{\rm Mod}(M)
is called
pseudo-Anosov
if there exists a pair of transverse measured foliations
(\mathcal{F}^{s},\mu_{s})
and
(\mathcal{F}^{u},\mu_{u})
on
M
, the former is called
stable
and the latter
unstable
, a
stretch factor
\lambda>1
, and a representative
pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism

\phi
, i.e.,

\phi\cdot(\mathcal{F}^{s},\mu_{s})=(\mathcal{F}^{s},\lambda^{-1}\mu_{s})\quad{\rm and}\quad\phi\cdot(\mathcal{F}^{u},\mu_{u})=(\mathcal{F}^{u},\lambda\mu_{u}).

This definition includes Anosov classes on 2-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.
The difference between Anosov and pseudo-Anosov classes is that transverse measured foliations of the latter may have singularities.

be a base point on

and
\widetilde{P_{0}}
is one of the preimages of
P_{0}
under the covering map
\psi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
. The geodesic
\gamma
has a unique lift
\widetilde{\gamma}
to
\widetilde{M}
that contains
\widetilde{P_{0}}
and extends indefinitely in both directions. If
\gamma
is parametrized by
[0,1]
with
P_{0}=\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
, and
\widetilde{\gamma}(t),\ t\in[0,1]
, is the lift of
\gamma(t),\ t\in[0,1]
, with
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=\widetilde{P_{0}}
, there exists
a covering transformation
T_{\gamma}
, i.e., an orientation preserving isometry of
\widetilde{M}
with
\psi\circ T_{\gamma}=\psi
, such that
T_{\gamma}(\widetilde{P_{0}})=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)
. The isometry
T_{\gamma}
leaves the full lift
\widetilde{\gamma}
invariant and
the action of
T_{\gamma}
on
\widetilde{\gamma}
is a translation by the length of
\gamma
. Up to isometry, the quotient
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}=\widetilde{M}/T_{\gamma}
does not depend on either the base point
P_{0}
, or a particular preimage
\widetilde{P_{0}}
. We refer to such
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
as the
annular covering surface
associated to
\gamma
. Topologically,
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
is an open annulus. The curve
\widehat{\gamma}=\widetilde{\gamma}/T_{\gamma}
is a lift of
\gamma
to
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
.
If
\gamma^{\prime}
is a closed curve in
M
that is homotopic to
\gamma
, it has a lift
\widehat{\gamma^{\prime}}
to
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
homotopic to
\widehat{\gamma}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\gamma^{\prime}
be a closed curve homotopic to a simple closed geodesic
\gamma
in
M
and
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
be a lift in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
of
\gamma^{\prime}
homotopic to
\widehat{\gamma}
as above.
If
D
is a domain in
M
with a contractible in
M
closure and such that
\gamma^{\prime}\cap D\neq\emptyset
, then the number of preimages of
D
in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
that intersects
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
is at most the number of essential intersections of
\gamma^{\prime}
with
D
.

Proof.

Indeed, any lift to
\widetilde{M}
of each essential intersection
\beta^{\prime}
of
\gamma^{\prime}
with
D
intersects only one of the lifts of
D
to
\widetilde{M}
as follows from the fact that
\beta^{\prime}\cup{\rm Cl}(D)
is contractible in
M
. Therefore, if
\gamma^{\prime}
is parametrized by
[0,1]
, any lift to
\widetilde{M}
of the curve
\gamma^{\prime}(t),\ t\in[0,1]
, that has at most
N
essential intersections with
D
intersects at most
N
preimages of
D
. Factoring these preimages by the isometry
T_{\gamma}
defined above does not increase the number of such preimages in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

The annular covering surface
\widehat{M}
also carries a metric induced from
\widetilde{M}
. The curve
\widehat{\gamma}
is a homotopically non-trivial simple closed geodesic in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
and the length of
\widehat{\gamma^{\prime}}
for any
\gamma^{\prime}
homotopic to
\gamma
is at least that of
\widehat{\gamma}
.
Moreover, since the covering map
\widehat{\psi}_{\gamma}\colon\widehat{M}_{\gamma}\to M
is a local isometry, if
\gamma^{\prime}
is rectifiable, then so is
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
and they are isometric to each other, in particular have the same lengths. This applies to
\gamma
and
\widehat{\gamma}
as well. Finally,
\widehat{M}
supports an orthogonal projection
\Pi_{\widehat{\gamma}}
onto
\widehat{\gamma}
. It is obtained from the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{\tilde{\gamma}}
of
\widetilde{M}
onto
\tilde{\gamma}
using the quotient by
T_{\gamma}
. It follows from elementary geometry that the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{\widehat{\gamma}}
is well-defined and is a 1-Lipschitz map.

Let
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a collection of domains with pairwise disjoint closures in a surface
M
.
Let
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
.
If
\gamma
is a curve in
M
, we define its
S
-
length
by

l_{S}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma\cap S}ds+\sum_{\gamma\cap D_{k}\neq\emptyset}{\rm diam}(D_{k}).

If
\gamma
is a locally rectifiable curve in
M
and
B
is a Borel measurable subset of
M
, we denote
\int_{\gamma\cap B}ds
by
l(\gamma\cap B)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M
be a closed surface of genus at least 1, and
S\subset M
be a compact subset of
M
whose complementary components
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
have bounded geometry.
For a given
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a constant
C\geq 0
with the following property.
If
\Gamma
is a collection of closed curves
\gamma^{\prime}
in the homotopy class of a simple closed geodesic
\gamma
such that each
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
has at most
N
essential intersections with each
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
then

{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)\leq C/l(\gamma)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
be the annular covering surface associated to
\gamma
.
Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
be an arbitrary rectifiable curve homotopic to
\gamma
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that

D_{k}
intersects
\gamma
, and let
\widehat{D_{k}}
be a lift of
D_{k}
to
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
intersecting
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
, a lift of
\gamma^{\prime}
to
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
that is homotopic to
\widehat{\gamma}
. Lemma
5.3
implies that there are at most
N
such lifts
\widehat{D_{k}}
. If
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, has a small diameter relative to the injectivity radius of
M
, then the diameter of each lift
\widehat{D_{k}}
is the same as that of
D_{k}
. If
D_{k}
is relatively large, its lift in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
may have a larger diameter, but still uniformly comparable to
{\rm diam}(D_{k})
. Indeed, the bounded geometry assumption implies that
\lim_{|k|\to\infty}{\rm diam}(D_{k})=0
, and hence there are only finitely many domains
D_{k}
that are relatively large. The lifts of
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, to the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
have diameters that are uniformly comparable to the diameters of the corresponding
D_{k}
, and the covering
\widetilde{M}\to\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
does not increase the diameters. Therefore, there exists a constant
c\in(0,1)
that depends only on
M
and
S
and such that
{\rm diam}(D_{k})\geq c\cdot{\rm diam}(\widehat{D_{k}})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and each lift
\widehat{D_{k}}
of
D_{k}
to
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
. Let
\widehat{S}
be the complement in
\widehat{M}_{\gamma}
of all the lifts, necessarily having disjoint closures,
\widehat{D_{k}}
of
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The covering map
\widehat{\psi}_{\gamma}\colon\widehat{M}_{\gamma}\to M
is a local isometry, and hence
l(\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}\cap\widehat{S})=l(\gamma^{\prime}\cap S)
. Combining the above facts together we obtain that

(13)

l_{\widehat{S}}(\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime})\leq\frac{N}{c}l_{S}(\gamma^{\prime}).

Since
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
is a closed curve homotopic to
\widehat{\gamma}
, the orthogonal projections of
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}\cap\widehat{S}
and all
\widehat{D_{k}}
that intersect
\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}
, i.e., the images of these sets under
\Pi_{\widehat{\gamma}}
onto
\widehat{\gamma}
, cover
\widehat{\gamma}
. The projection
\Pi_{\widehat{\gamma}}
is a 1-Lipschitz map, and therefore

(14)

l(\widehat{\gamma})\leq l_{\widehat{S}}(\widehat{\gamma}^{\prime}).

Combining inequalities (
13
) and (
14
) along with the fact
l(\widehat{\gamma})=l(\gamma)
, we obtain

(15)

l(\gamma)\leq\frac{N}{c}l_{S}(\gamma^{\prime}),

for every rectifiable curve
\gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
.

We now choose the mass distribution
\rho
equal 1 on
S
and
\rho(D_{k})={\rm diam}(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
have bounded geometry,
\rho
has finite mass that depends only on
M
and
S
or
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
We now multiply the mass distribution
\rho
(both its discrete and continuous parts) by
N/(c\cdot l(\gamma))
. Inequality (
15
) gives that such new distribution
\rho^{\prime}
is admissible for
\Gamma
.
The total mass of this distribution is

{\rm mass}(\rho^{\prime})=\frac{N^{2}{\rm mass}(\rho)}{c^{2}l(\gamma)^{2}},

and the lemma follows.
∎

To finish excluding the pseudo-Anosov class, we will need the following proposition.

Proposition 5.5
.

[
FM12
, Section 14.5, Theorem 14.23]

Let

and suppose that

is pseudo-Anosov with stretch factor

.
If

.

Now, if

is a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism satisfying Theorem
1.4
, combining Lemmas
3.4
,
3.7
, Corollary
5.2
, Lemma
5.4
, and Proposition

, we obtain a contradiction. Indeed, let

and we define a curve family

corresponding to

as in (

, and hence by Lemma

(or Corollary

Using Remark

, we assume that

6.
Holomorphic quadratic differentials

A
holomorphic quadratic differential

q
on a Riemann surface
M
is a holomorphic section of the symmetric square of the holomorphic cotangent bundle of
M
. In other words,
q
is a
(2,0)
-form given in a local chart by
q=q(z)\,dz^{2}
, where
z\mapsto q(z)
is a holomorphic function; see
[
St84
]
for background.

If
q
is a holomorphic quadratic differential, the locally defined quantity
|dw|=|q(z)|^{1/2}|dz|
is called
the length element
of the
metric determined by

q
, or
q
-
metric
. The length of a curve
\gamma
in this metric will be denoted by
|\gamma|_{q}
and called
q
-
length
. The corresponding
area element
is
|q(z)|dxdy
, where
z=x+iy
.
The
q
-metric is dominated by the intrinsic metric of
M
, i.e., there is a constant
c>0
such that the length of every curve
\gamma
in
M
is at least
c|\gamma|_{q}
. In particular, diameters of sets in the intrinsic metric are at least
c
times the diameters in the
q
-metric.

If
q
is a holomorphic quadratic differential, its
horizontal trajectory
is a maximal arc on
M
given in charts by
\arg(q(z)\,dz^{2})=0
. A
vertical arc
is an arc in
M
given by
\arg(q(z)\,dz^{2})=\pi
.
A holomorphic quadratic differential
q
has
closed trajectories
if its non-closed horizontal trajectories cover a set of measure zero.
We need the following special case of
[
Je57
, Theorem 1]
; see
[
St84
, Theorems 21.1, 21.7]
for similar results, and
[
HM79
]
for a general result on realizing measured foliations.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
Je57
, Theorem 1]

Let
\gamma
be a homotopically non-trivial simple closed curve on a closed Riemann surface
M
, and let
\Gamma
consist of all simple closed curves homotopic to
\gamma
.
Then there exists a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
M
with closed trajectories, so that the metric
|q(z)|^{1/2}|dz|
is extremal for the 2-modulus of
\Gamma
.

Given a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
M
, one can locally define an analytic function
\Phi(z)=\int\sqrt{q(z)}dz
. Using the function
\Phi
, one can show
[
St84
, Theorem 9.4]
that each closed horizontal trajectory
\gamma
of a quadratic differential
q
can be embedded in a maximal ring domain
R
swept out by closed horizontal trajectories. It is uniquely determined except for the case when
M={\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. The function
\Phi^{-1}
maps a rectangle
(0,a)\times(b_{1},b_{2})
, for suitably defined
a,b_{1},b_{2}
conformally onto the maximal ring domain with a vertical arc removed. In the chart given by
\Phi
, the metric
|q(z)|^{1/2}|dz|
is the Euclidean metric in the rectangle
(0,a)\times(b_{1},b_{2})
.
If the points
(0,y)
and
(a,y),\ b_{1}<y<b_{2}
, are identified, the maximal ring domain is conformally equivalent to the straight flat cylinder, which we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, of circumference
a
and height
b=b_{2}-b_{1}
.
The horizontal trajectories are then the horizontal circles on such a cylinder. In particular, closed horizontal trajectories are geodesics in the metric determined by
q
. Note that for the quadratic differential
q
from Theorem
6.1
, the length
a
of the circumference of
\mathcal{C}_{q}
equals 1.

7.
Periodic and reducible diffeomorphisms

In this section with exclude the periodic and reducible possibilities for
f
from Theorem
1.4
.

If
M
is a surface and
f\colon M\to M
a homeomorphism, we say that a curve family
\Gamma
on
M
is
f
-
invariant
if
\Gamma=f(\Gamma)
.
If
[f]\in{\rm Mod}(M)
is either periodic or reducible, then there exists a non-trivial homotopy class
[\gamma]
of simple closed curves in
M
that is
f^{k}
-invariant for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By passing to the
k
’th iterate of
f
, we may and will assume that
k=1
. Let
\Gamma
be the
f
-invariant family of all simple closed curves in
[\gamma]
, or
\Gamma=[\gamma]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f
be a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
M
that permutes a dense collection of domains
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with bounded geometry. Assume further that
f
is conformal on the residual set
S=M\setminus\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{k}
.
Let
\Gamma
be an
f
-invariant curve family in
M
with
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
. If
\rho
is the extremal mass distribution for
{\rm mod}(\Gamma)
, then
\rho(D_{k})=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.7
we know that the pullback distribution
f^{*}(\rho)
is also extremal for
\Gamma
because
\Gamma
is
f
-invariant. Since the extremal distribution is unique, we conclude, in particular, that
\rho(f(D_{k}))=\rho(D_{k})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, if
D_{k},D_{l},\ k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are in the same
f
-orbit, then
\rho(D_{k})=\rho(D_{l})
. Since
f
permutes
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we have for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
f^{n}(D_{k})\cap D_{k}=\emptyset
for all
n\neq 0
. In particular, each
f
-orbit of domains is infinite. Since
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
, we conclude that
\rho(D_{k})=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

We call a mass distribution
\rho
as in the previous lemma, i.e., when
\rho(D_{k})=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
purely continuous
.

The family
\Gamma=[\gamma]
is
f
-invariant, but it is unclear whether
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma)<\infty
. By Theorem
6.1
, there exists a holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
M
with closed trajectories, so that the metric
|q(z)|^{1/2}|dz|
is extremal for the 2-modulus of
\Gamma
. Since the
q
-metric is dominated by the intrinsic metric of
M
, we have
{\rm diam}_{q}(D_{k})\leq C{\rm diam}(D_{k})
for some
C>0
and all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
{\rm diam}_{q}(D_{k})
is the diameter of
D_{k}
measured in the
q
-metric. Thus,

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\rm diam}_{q}(D_{k})^{2}<\infty.

Let
\Gamma_{h}
consist of all closed horizontal trajectories of
q
in
\Gamma
. The curve family
\Gamma_{h}
has positive 2-modulus but it is not necessarily
f
-invariant. Since each
\gamma_{y}\in\Gamma_{h},\ b_{1}<y<b_{2}
, in the above notations, has
q
-length equal 1, Lemma
5.1
implies that there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that each such
\gamma_{y}
has at most
N
essential intersections with every
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
\Gamma_{N}
consist of all curves in
\Gamma
that have at most
N
essential intersections with every
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
\Gamma_{h}\subset\Gamma_{N}\subset\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{N}
is
f
-invariant because
f
is a homeomorphism of
M
. Lemma
5.4
implies that
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma_{N})<\infty
. Also, by Lemma
3.4
(or Corollary
3.6
),
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma_{N})>0
since
{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{N})\geq{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{h})>0
.
Proposition
3.1
gives a unique transboundary extremal mass distribution
\rho_{0}
for
{\rm mod}_{S}(\Gamma_{N})
. Lemma
7.1
in turn gives that
\rho_{0}
is purely continuous. We now show that this is not possible, excluding the periodic and reducible cases.

Proposition 7.2
.

For any homotopically non-trivial simple closed curve
\gamma
in
M
,
the extremal mass distribution
\rho_{0}
for the curve family
\Gamma_{N}
defined above cannot be purely continuous.

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho_{0}
is
purely continuous.
Let
\rho_{1}
be the mass distribution for
\Gamma_{N}
that is given in local charts as
|q(z)|^{1/2}
on
S
and
\rho_{1}(D_{k})=(N/c)\,{\rm diam}_{q}(D_{k}),\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
c
is a constant as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
. As in Lemma
5.4
, this distribution is admissible for
\Gamma_{N}
, it has finite total mass, but it is not necessarily extremal. To finish the proof, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.3
.

The
L^{2}
-norm of the continuous part of the mass distribution
\rho_{1}
is strictly less than
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}\oplus l^{2}}
.

Proof.

First,
\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}}={\rm area}_{q}(S)
, the area of
S
in the
q
-metric. Since
D_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, have interior, it is strictly less than the
q
-area of the whole cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{q}
, which is the 2-modulus of the family
\Gamma_{h}
of all closed horizontal trajectories of
q
by Theorem
6.1
. Since each closed horizontal trajectory
\gamma_{y}
of
q
is homotopic to
\gamma
, integrating
\rho_{0}
over each such trajectory gives

\int_{\gamma_{y}}\rho_{0}\,ds=\int_{\gamma_{y}\cap S}\rho_{0}\,ds\geq 1,

for all
y,\ b_{1}<y<b_{2}
. This means that
\rho_{0}|_{S}
is admissible for
\Gamma_{h}
. Therefore,

\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}={\rm mass}(\rho_{0})\geq{\rm mod}_{2}(\Gamma_{h})={\rm area}_{q}(\mathcal{C}_{q})>{\rm mass}(\rho_{1}|_{S})=\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Now, consider mass distributions

\rho_{t}=(1-t)\rho_{0}+t\rho_{1},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

This is a one-parameter family of mass distributions connecting
\rho_{1}
to
\rho_{0}
. Convexity gives that each of this distributions is admissible for
\Gamma_{N}
. Strict convexity of the ball of radius
{\rm mass}(\rho_{0})
in
L^{2}\oplus l^{2}
along with Lemma
7.3
imply that there exists
t
close to 0 such that
{\rm mass}(\rho_{t})<{\rm mass}(\rho_{0})
.
Indeed,

{\rm mass}(\rho_{t})-{\rm mass}(\rho_{0})\sim 2t\int_{S}\rho_{0}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{0})d\sigma,\quad t\to 0.

But

\int_{S}\rho_{0}\rho_{1}\,d\sigma\leq\left({\rm mass}(\rho_{0})\right)^{1/2}\left({\rm mass}(\rho_{1}|_{S})\right)^{1/2}<{\rm mass}(\rho_{0})=\int_{S}\rho_{0}^{2}d\sigma,

and so
{\rm mass}(\rho_{t})<{\rm mass}(\rho_{0})
for small
t>0
.
This contradicts the assumption that
\rho_{0}
is extremal for
\Gamma_{N}
and concludes the proof of Proposition
7.2
.
∎

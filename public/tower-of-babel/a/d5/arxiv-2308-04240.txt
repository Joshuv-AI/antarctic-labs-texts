Polarization Effects in Higher-order Guiding-center Lagrangian Dynamics
Alain J. Brizard

Abstract
The extended guiding-center Lagrangian equations of motion are derived by Lie-transform perturbation method under the assumption of time-dependent and inhomogeneous electric and magnetic fields that satisfy the standard guiding-center space-time orderings. Polarization effects are introduced into the Lagrangian dynamics by the inclusion of the polarization drift velocity in the guiding-center velocity and the appearance of finite-Larmor-radius corrections in the guiding-center Hamiltonian and guiding-center Poisson bracket.



1
Introduction

Polarization effects have a rich history in plasma physics
(
Pfirsch 1984
;
Pfirsch & Morrison 1985
;
Cary & Brizard 2009
)
. Their importance stems from the assumption of quasineutrality in a strongly magnetized plasma and the dielectric properties of a guiding-center plasma
(
Hinton & Robertson 1984
)
. While these effects are traditionally associated with the presence of an electric field in a magnetized plasma
(
Itoh & Itoh 1996
;
Hazeltine & Meiss 2003
;
Wang & Hahm 2009
;
Joseph 2021
;
Brizard 2023a
)
, they are also associated with magnetic drifts
(
Kaufman 1986
;
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
.

Recently, second-order terms in guiding-center Hamiltonian theory (in the absence of an electric field) were shown to be crucial
(
Brizard & Hodgeman 2023
)
in assessing the validity of the guiding-center representation in determining whether guiding-center orbits were numerically faithful to the particle orbits in axisymmetric magnetic geometries, which partially confirmed earlier numerical studies in axisymmetric tokamak plasmas
(
Belova et al. 2003
)
. In particular, it was shown that a second-order correction associated with guiding-center polarization
(
Kaufman 1986
;
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
was needed in order to obtain faithful guiding-center orbits.

Indeed, without the inclusion of second-order effects, it was shown that, within a few bounce periods after leaving the same physical point in particle phase space, a first-order guiding-center orbit deviated noticeably from its associated particle orbit, while a second-order guiding-center orbit followed the particle orbit to a high degree of precision
(
Brizard & Hodgeman 2023
)
. In addition, as initially reported by
Belova et al. 2003
, the guiding-center Hamiltonian formulation is a faithful representation of the particle toroidal angular momentum
(
Tronko & Brizard 2015
;
Brizard & Hodgeman 2023
)
, which is an exact particle constant of motion in an axisymmetric magnetic field, only if second-order effects are included.

1.1
Lagrangian dynamics in extended phase space

In the present work, we consider time-dependent and inhomogeneous electric and magnetic fields (which still satisfy the guiding-center space-time orderings
|\nabla|^{-1}\gg\rho_{\rm th}=v_{\rm th}/\Omega
and
\partial/\partial t\ll\Omega=eB/mc
) and we assume that the
E\times B
velocity
{\bf u}_{\rm E}={\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}/B
is comparable to the particle’s thermal velocity
v_{\rm th}
. Because of the explicit time dependence of the electromagnetic fields, the Lagrangian charged-particle dynamics takes place in an odd-dimensional space
({\bf q},{\bf p},t)
, where the non-autonomous Hamiltonian
H({\bf q},{\bf p},t)
is a function of the canonical coordinates
({\bf q},{\bf p})
, from which the canonical Hamilton equations
d{\bf q}/dt=\partial H/\partial{\bf p}
and
d{\bf p}/dt=-\,\partial H/\partial{\bf q}
are derived, as well as time
t
, from which we obtain the energy equation
dH/dt=\partial H/\partial t
(i.e., energy is not conserved).

The use of an extended phase space is a well-known method in classical mechanics
(
Lanczos 1970
)
used to deal with a time-dependent Hamiltonian system by transforming it into an autonomous Hamiltonian system in an even-dimensional symplectic setting. Here, the canonical time-energy coordinates
(t,w)
are included in the extended phase-space coordinates
({\bf q},t;{\bf p},w)
, where the space-time coordinates
({\bf q},t)
are canonically conjugate to the momentum-energy coordinates
({\bf p},w)
, with the extended Hamilton equations
dw/ds=\partial{\mathcal{H}}/\partial t=\partial H/\partial t
and
dt/ds=-\,\partial{\mathcal{H}}/\partial w=1
, where
{\mathcal{H}}\equiv H({\bf q},{\bf p},t)-w
is the extended Hamiltonian and a particle orbit in extended phase space (parametrized by
s
) takes place on the energy surface
{\mathcal{H}}=0
, i.e.,
w=H({\bf q},{\bf p},t)
.

Using the dimensional ordering parameter
\epsilon
associated with the renormalized particle mass
m\rightarrow\epsilon\,m

(
Brizard 1995
)
, instead of the standard ordering
e\rightarrow e/\epsilon

(
Kulsrud 1983
;
Littlejohn 1983
)
, we begin with the extended phase-space particle Lagrangian one-form

\gamma\;=\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\;{\bf A}\;+\;\epsilon\,{\bf p}_{0}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf x}\;-\;w\;{\sf d}t\;\equiv\;\gamma_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,\gamma_{1},

(1)

and the extended particle Hamiltonian

{\mathcal{H}}\;=\;e\,\Phi\;-\;w\;+\;\epsilon\,|{\bf p}_{0}|^{2}/2m\;\equiv\;{\mathcal{H}}_{0}+\epsilon\,{\mathcal{H}}_{1},

(2)

where
{\bf p}_{0}
denotes the local particle kinetic momentum at position
{\bf x}
. In the present work, we consider the standard ordering
(
Kulsrud 1983
)
for the parallel electric field:
{\bf E}={\bf E}_{\bot}+\epsilon\,E_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
. In contrast to
Madsen 2010
and
Frei et al. 2020
, who used the same mass ordering (
m\rightarrow\epsilon\,m
), we use extended (eight-dimensional) phase space in Eqs. (
1
)-(
2
), where the energy coordinate
w
is canonically conjugate to time
t

(
Littlejohn 1981
;
Cary & Brizard 2009
)
. This extended phase-space formulation yields a simple form for the extended Poisson bracket [see Eq. (
60
)], also adopted (without derivation) by
Madsen 2010
, which plays an important role in the variational formulation of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations
(
Brizard 2023b
)
.

Here, the electric-field ordering implies that the local particle momentum

{\bf p}_{0}\;\equiv\;p_{\|0}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}({\bf x},t)\;+\;{\bf P}_{\rm E}({\bf x},t)\;+\;{\bf q}_{\bot 0}(J_{0},\theta_{0};{\bf x},t)

(3)

is decomposed into the gyroangle-independent parallel component
p_{\|0}\equiv{\bf p}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
and the
E\times B
momentum

{\bf P}_{\rm E}\;\equiv\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\;=\;m\,{\bf u}_{\rm E},

(4)

and the gyroangle-dependent perpendicular momentum
{\bf q}_{\bot 0}\equiv|{\bf q}_{\bot 0}|\,\widehat{\bot}
, respectively, where
J_{0}\equiv|{\bf q}_{\bot 0}|^{2}/(2m\Omega)
represents the lowest-order gyroaction and the gyroangle derivative

\partial{\bf q}_{\bot 0}/\partial\theta_{0}\equiv{\bf q}_{\bot 0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}=-\,|{\bf q}_{\bot 0}|\,\widehat{\rho}
introduces the rotating orthogonal unit-vector fields
(\widehat{{\sf b}},\widehat{\bot},\widehat{\rho})
.

1.2
Purpose of the present work

The purpose of the present work is motivated by the need to derive higher-order guiding-center equations that can accurately describe the magnetic confinement of charged particles in regions with steep gradients (e.g., the pedestal region of advanced tokamak plasmas). For many situations of practical interest, the presence of a strong electric field is associated with strong plasma flows with steep sheared rotation profiles for which second-order effects (including finite-Larmor-radius effects) must be included in a self-consistent guiding-center theory
(
Hahm 1996
;
Chang et al. 2004
;
Lanthaler et al. 2019
;
Frei et al. 2020
)
.

Guiding-center equations of motion with second-order corrections in the presence of time-independent electric and magnetic fields were derived using Lie-transform perturbation method by
Brizard 1995
and
Hahm 1996
, following the earlier work of
Littlejohn 1981
. These perturbation methods were also used by
Miyato et al. 2009
,
Madsen 2010
, and
Frei et al. 2020
, who derived self-consistent guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations that included guiding-center polarization and magnetization effects. Not all second-order effects were included in these models, however, and it is the purpose of the present work to derive a more complete higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell theory, with a full representation of guiding-center polarization that can be directly derived by the guiding-center push-forward method
(
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
.

2
Guiding-center Lie-transform Perturbation Analysis

The derivation of the guiding-center equations of motion by Lie-transform perturbation method is based on a phase-space transformation from the (local) particle extended (eight-dimensional) phase-space coordinates
z_{0}^{\alpha}=({\bf x},p_{\|0};J_{0},\theta_{0};w_{0},t)
, where the energy-time canonical coordinates
(w_{0},t)
are included, to the guiding-center phase-space coordinates
Z^{\alpha}=({\bf X},P_{\|};J,\theta;W,t)
generated by the vector fields
({\sf G}_{1},{\sf G}_{2},\cdots)
:

Z^{\alpha}\;=\;z_{0}^{\alpha}\;+\;\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\alpha}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\,\left(G_{2}^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{\alpha}\right)\;+\;\cdots,

(5)

and its inverse

z_{0}^{\alpha}\;=\;Z^{\alpha}\;-\;\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\alpha}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\,\left(G_{2}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{\alpha}\right)\;+\;\cdots.

(6)

In order for the particle time to be invariant under the guiding-center transformation (
5
), we require that
G_{n}^{t}\equiv 0
to all orders
n\geq 1
, i.e., the guiding-center time is identical to the particle time. From these generating vectors fields, the pull-back and push-forward Lie-transform operators
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}=\exp(\epsilon\pounds_{1})\,\exp(\epsilon^{2}\pounds_{2})\cdots
and
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}=\cdots\exp(-\epsilon^{2}\pounds_{2})\,\exp(-\epsilon\pounds_{1})
are constructed in terms of Lie derivatives
(\pounds_{1},\pounds_{2},\cdots)
generated by the vector fields
({\sf G}_{1},{\sf G}_{2},\cdots)
. More details about the Lie-transform perturbation method used in guiding-center theory can be found in
Littlejohn 1982
(as well as the unpublished UCLA report
Variational Principles for Guiding Center Motion
(PPG-611) written by Littlejohn in 1982), while the notation used here is taken from
Tronko & Brizard 2015
and
Brizard & Tronko 2016
.

We now wish to derive the extended guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian one-form

\Gamma_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\gamma+{\sf d}\sigma=\Gamma_{0{\rm gc}}+\epsilon\,\Gamma_{1{\rm gc}}+\epsilon^{2}\,\Gamma_{2{\rm gc}}+\cdots,

(7)

where the gauge scalar field
\sigma=\epsilon\,\sigma_{1}+\epsilon^{2}\sigma_{2}+\cdots
is chosen at each order in order to simplify the transformation, with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\;-\;\iota_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}\;+\;{\sf d}\sigma_{1},

(8)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\iota_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\;\iota_{1}\cdot\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{1}\;+\frac{}{}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{{\rm gc}1}\right)+{\sf d}\sigma_{2},

(9)

and the extended guiding-center Hamiltonian

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\mathcal{H}}\;=\;{\mathcal{H}}_{0{\rm gc}}+\epsilon\,{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm gc}}+\epsilon^{2}\,{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}+\cdots,

(10)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\;-\;{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}{\mathcal{H}}_{0},

(11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\sf G}_{2}\cdot{\sf d}{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\;{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\left({\mathcal{H}}_{1}+{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm gc}}\right).

(12)

Here, we use the formulas
\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\equiv G_{n}^{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha\beta}{\sf d}Z^{\beta}
(for an arbitrary two-form
\omega
) and
{\sf G}_{n}\cdot{\sf d}{\mathcal{K}}\equiv G_{n}^{\alpha}\partial{\mathcal{K}}/\partial Z^{\alpha}
(for an arbitrary scalar field
{\mathcal{K}}
), where the summation rule is used. We note that the guiding-center phase-space transformation considered in the present work will contain all terms associated with first-order space-time derivatives of the electric and magnetic fields, which will require us to consider some terms at third order in
\epsilon
in Eq. (
7
).

In order to construct the extended guiding-center Lagrangian one-form (
7
), we will need to evaluate the contractions
\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}
generated by the vector fields

({\sf G}_{1},{\sf G}_{2},\cdots)
on the zeroth-order two-form:

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}\;=\;{\sf d}\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{c}\left(\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial x^{i}}\;{\sf d}x^{i}\;+\;\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial t}\;{\sf d}t\right)\;\wedge\;{\sf d}x^{j}\;-\;{\sf d}w\wedge{\sf d}t,

(13)

so that we obtain the
n^{\rm th}
-order expression

\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}=\frac{e}{c}\;{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,G_{n}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}-\left(\frac{e}{c}\,G_{n}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}+G_{n}^{w}\right)\;{\sf d}t,

(14)

where
G_{n}^{{\bf x}}
and
G_{n}^{w}
denote the spatial and energy components of the
n
th-order generating vector field
{\sf G}_{n}
. Similarly, we will need to evaluate the contractions
\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{1}
generated by the vector fields
({\sf G}_{1},{\sf G}_{2},\cdots)
on the first-order two-form
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{1}={\sf d}\gamma_{1}
, which yields the
(n+1)^{\rm th}
-order expression

\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{1}\equiv D_{n}({\bf p}_{0})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}-G_{n}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial{\bf p}_{0}}{\partial t}\;{\sf d}t+\widehat{{\sf b}}\;{\sf d}p_{\|0}+\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot 0}}{\partial J_{0}}{\sf d}J_{0}+\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot 0}}{\partial\theta_{0}}{\sf d}\theta_{0}\right),

(15)

where
{\bf p}_{0}
is defined in Eq. (
3
) and the spatial components are expressed in terms of the operator
(
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)

\displaystyle D_{n}({\bf C})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(G_{n}^{p_{\|}}\,\frac{\partial{\bf C}}{\partial p_{\|0}}+G_{n}^{J}\,\frac{\partial{\bf C}}{\partial J_{0}}+G_{n}^{\theta}\,\frac{\partial{\bf C}}{\partial\theta_{0}}\right)\;-\;G_{n}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf C},

(16)

where
{\bf C}
is an arbitrary vector function on guiding-center phase space. In what follows, unless it is necessary, we will omit writing the subscript
0
on
local particle phase-space coordinates (i.e.,
p_{\|0}
is written as
p_{\|}
).

The purpose of the Lie-transform expressions (
7
) and (
10
) is to construct a gyroangle-independent extended guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian one-form
\Gamma_{\rm gc}
and extended guiding-center Hamiltonian

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}
in terms of which guiding-center equations are derived. The generic forms considered for the extended guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian one-form (
7
) and the extended guiding-center Hamiltonian (
10
) in the present work are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;-\;W\,{\sf d}t\;+\;\epsilon\,{\bf P}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}J\,\left({\sf d}\theta-{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}-{\mathcal{S}}\,{\sf d}t\right),

(17)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle e\,\Phi\;+\;\epsilon\,K_{\rm gc}\;-\;W,

(18)

where the gyroangle-independent guiding-center kinetic momentum
{\bf P}_{\rm gc}
and guiding-center kinetic energy
K_{\rm gc}
are expressed as asymptotic series in powers of
\epsilon
, while the vector field
{\bf R}\equiv\nabla\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}
and the scalar field
{\mathcal{S}}\equiv(\partial\widehat{\bot}/\partial t)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}
are required to preserve gyrogauge invariance. See App. A of the recent paper by
Brizard 2023b
for an updated discussion on gyrogauge invariance introduced by
Littlejohn 1981
;
Littlejohn 1983
;
Littlejohn 1988
. We note that the separation of the guiding-center transformations of the extended guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian one-form (
7
) and the extended guiding-center Hamiltonian
(
10
) might enable the application of computer algorithms previously used by
Burby et al. 2013
, but this consideration falls outside the scope of the present work.

3
Symplectic Polarization Guiding-center Theory

In the present work, we will consider the
symplectic
polarization guiding-center theory, where the guiding-center kinetic momentum
{\bf P}_{\rm gc}
retains the contribution from the
E\times B
momentum (
4
), which will then introduce the polarization drift velocity explicitly in the guiding-center equations of motion.

In the alternate
Hamiltonian
polarization guiding-center theory, on the other hand, the
E\times B
momentum (
4
) is removed from the guiding-center kinetic momentum and polarization effects enter solely through the guiding-center Hamiltonian. The Hamiltonian case generally requires a different ordering for the electric field (because it produces a polarization displacement that must be compared with the characteristic lowest-order particle gyroradius) and will be considered in a future publication.This dual representation is analogous to the treatment of the perturbed magnetic field in nonlinear gyrokinetic theory
(
Brizard & Hahm 2007
)
.

The reader interested in results of the guiding-center Lie-transform perturbation analysis can skip Section
3
and go to Section
4
, where we present the extended guiding-center Hamiltonian structure as well as the (regular) guiding-center Lagrangian, from which guiding-center polarization and magnetization can be derived by functional derivatives with respect to the electric field
{\bf E}
and magnetic field
{\bf B}
, respectively.

3.1
First-order perturbation analysis

3.1.1
First-order symplectic structure

We begin our perturbation analysis by considering the first-order guiding-center symplectic one-form (
8
), which is now explicitly written in the
symplectic
polarization representation as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf P}_{\rm E}\;+\;{\bf q}_{\bot}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\;{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,G_{1}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;+\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\,G_{1}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\;+\;G_{1}^{w}\right)\;{\sf d}t

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf P}_{\rm E}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;\equiv\;{\bf P}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X},

where the first-order gauge scalar field
\sigma_{1}
is not needed and the gyroangle-dependent terms on the right of Eq. (
19
) are eliminated by selecting

G_{1}^{{\bf x}}\;=\;{\bf q}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB}\;=\;\frac{1}{m\Omega}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial\theta}\;\equiv\;-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

(20)

and

G_{1}^{w}\;=\;-\,\frac{e}{c}\,G_{1}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\;=\;\frac{e}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

(21)

where the gyroangle-dependent vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}
represents the lowest-order particle gyroradius.

With
G_{1}^{{\bf x}}
defined by Eq. (
20
), the resulting first-order guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian
\Gamma_{1{\rm gc}}={\bf P}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}
yields the
(n+1)^{\rm th}
-order contraction

\iota_{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{{\rm gc}1}\equiv D_{n}({\bf P}_{0})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}-G_{n}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\;{\sf d}P_{\|}+\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{0}}{\partial t}{\sf d}t\right),

(22)

where, using the operator (
16
), the spatial components in Eq. (
22
) are

\displaystyle D_{n}({\bf P}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{n}^{p_{\|}}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}-G_{n}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0},

(23)

which contain gyroangle dependent and independent contributions.

3.1.2
First-order Hamiltonian

The first-order guiding-center Hamiltonian is determined from Eq. (
11
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,B+\frac{|{\bf P}_{0}|^{2}}{2m}+{\bf P}_{\rm E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf q}_{\bot}}{m}-e\,G_{1}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Phi+G_{1}^{w}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,B+\frac{|{\bf P}_{0}|^{2}}{2m}+{\bf P}_{\rm E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf q}_{\bot}}{m}-e\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E},

where the components (
20
)-(
21
) were substituted, and the electric field is defined as
{\bf E}=-\,\nabla\Phi-c^{-1}\partial{\bf A}/\partial t
. By using the identity

{\bf P}_{\rm E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}/m\;=\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}\;=\;e\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E},

(25)

the first-order guiding-center Hamiltonian is automatically gyroangle-independent

{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm gc}}\;=\;\mu\,B+|{\bf P}_{0}|^{2}/(2m)\;=\;\mu B\;+\;P_{\|}^{2}/(2m)\;+\;(m/2)\;\left|{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}/B\right|^{2},

(26)

which corresponds to the kinetic energy in the frame drifting with the
E\times B
velocity.

3.2
Second-order perturbation analysis

3.2.1
Second-order symplectic structure

We now proceed with the second-order guiding-center symplectic one-form (
9
), which is explicitly expressed as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\left[\frac{e}{c}{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,G_{2}^{{\bf x}}\;+\;D_{1}({\bf P}_{2})\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,G_{1}^{{\bf x}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial J}\,{\sf d}J+\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial\theta}\;{\sf d}\theta\right)

(27)

\displaystyle+\;\left(G_{1}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{2}}{\partial t}\;+\;G_{2}^{w}\;+\;\frac{e}{c}\,G_{2}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\right)\;{\sf d}t

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;+\;J\;\left({\sf d}\theta\;-\frac{}{}{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;-\;{\mathcal{S}}\;{\sf d}t\right),

where the second-order gauge scalar field
\sigma_{2}
is not needed, and, using the definition (
16
) with
{\bf P}_{2}\equiv{\bf P}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}
, we find

\displaystyle D_{1}({\bf P}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{1}({\bf P}_{0})\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{1}^{J}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial J}+g_{1}^{\theta}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial\theta}\right)\;+\;J\left[{\bf R}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\tau+\alpha_{1}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\right],

(28)

where
g_{1}^{J}\equiv G_{1}^{J}-J\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B
and
g_{1}^{\theta}\equiv G_{1}^{\theta}+\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}
, while
\tau\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
and
\alpha_{1}\equiv{\sf a}_{1}:\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}
is defined in terms of the gyroangle-dependent dyadic tensor
{\sf a}_{1}\equiv-\,\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\bot}\widehat{\rho}+\widehat{\rho}\widehat{\bot})

(
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
. The first-order guiding-center symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
in Eq. (
27
), which is assumed to be gyroangle-independent, will be determined based on the consistency of the Lie-transform perturbation analysis at the third order (see Sec.
3.3
) as well as the guiding-center push-forward derivation of the guiding-center polarization in the absence of a background electric field
(
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
.

Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
27
), we obtain the vector equation

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{e}{c}{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,G_{2}^{\bf x}\;-\;g_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{1}^{J}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial J}+g_{1}^{\theta}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial\theta}\right),

(29)

where
g_{1}^{p_{\|}}\equiv G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;-\;J\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\tau\;+\;\alpha_{1}\right)
, while we choose the second-order energy component

G_{2}^{w}\;=\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{0}}{\partial t}\;-\;\frac{e}{c}\frac{\partial\bf A}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,G_{2}^{\bf x},

(30)

where we used

G_{1}^{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{2}}{\partial t}\;=\;-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{2}}{\partial t}\;=\;-\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{0}}{\partial t}\;-\;J\,{\mathcal{S}}.

(31)

Next, from the parallel component of Eq. (
29
), we obtain the first-order component

G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;=\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\;+\;J\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\tau\;+\;\alpha_{1}\right)\;-\;\Pi_{1\|},

(32)

where
\Pi_{1\|}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
is the parallel component of the first-order symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
. From the perpendicular components of Eq. (
29
), on the other hand, we find

\displaystyle G_{2}^{{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{2\|}^{\bf x}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}+\left(\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\right)\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{1}^{J}\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial J}+g_{1}^{\theta}\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}\right)-\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega},

(33)

where
G_{2\|}^{\bf x}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,G_{2}^{{\bf x}}
. The interpretation of the first-order guiding-center symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
will be given in Sec.
3.3.2
.

3.2.2
Second-order Hamiltonian

The second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian is determined from Eq. (
12
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(G_{2}^{\bf x}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\right)\;+\;\frac{e}{2}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;-\;\frac{P_{\|}}{m}\,G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;-\;\Omega\;G_{1}^{J}

(34)

\displaystyle+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu\nabla B\;+\;\nabla{\bf u}_{\rm E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,m\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}\;+\;\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{0}}{\partial t}\right),

where
G_{1}^{p_{\|}}
is given by Eq. (
32
).

Next, we introduce the particle gyroradius

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\equiv{\bf x}-{\sf T}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}-\epsilon^{2}\left(G_{2}^{\bf x}-\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\right)+\cdots\;=\;\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\;+\;\cdots,

(35)

which is defined as the difference between the particle position
{\bf x}
and the pull-back
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}
of the guiding-center position
{\bf X}
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\equiv-\,G_{2}^{\bf x}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}
is the first-order particle gyroradius, where

\displaystyle{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}^{J}\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial J}\;+\;g_{1}^{\theta}\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}\;+\;\frac{J}{m\Omega}\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;4\,\alpha_{2}\right)\,\widehat{{\sf b}}.

(36)

Hence, using Eqs. (
33
) and (
36
), we obtain the first-order particle gyroradius vector

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}-\left[G_{2\|}^{\bf x}-\frac{J}{m\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-2\,\alpha_{2}\right)\right]\,\widehat{{\sf b}}

(37)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B-\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\right)\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

where
4\,\alpha_{2}\equiv-\,\partial\alpha_{1}/\partial\theta
.

We now note that the gyroangle-dependent part
\widetilde{G}_{1}^{J}\equiv G_{1}^{J}-\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle
can be defined such that the right side of Eq. (
34
) only contains terms that are gyroangle-independent. Hence, we find

\displaystyle\Omega\,\widetilde{G}_{1}^{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{p_{\|}}{m}\;\widetilde{G}_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;-\;e\,\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\;-\;\frac{2J}{m\Omega}\;e\,{\sf a}_{2}:\nabla{\bf E}

(38)

\displaystyle+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu\nabla B\;+\;\nabla{\bf P}_{\rm E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}\;+\;\frac{\partial{\bf P}_{0}}{\partial t}\right),

where
{\sf a}_{2}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\;(\widehat{\bot}\,\widehat{\bot}-\widehat{\rho}\,\widehat{\rho})=-\,\frac{1}{4}\partial{\sf a}_{1}/\partial\theta
and the gyroangle-dependent part of Eq. (
32
) is

\widetilde{G}_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;\equiv\;G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;-\;\langle G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\rangle\;=\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\;+\;J\;\alpha_{1}.

(39)

The second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian is, thus, defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{m}\;\left(\Pi_{1\|}-\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\tau\right)\;-\;\Omega\,\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle\;+\;\frac{J\,c}{2B}\;\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right):\nabla{\bf E}\;-\;\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,e\,{\bf E}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{m}\;\left(\Pi_{1\|}-\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\tau\right)-\Omega\,\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{e}{2}\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\right)+\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E},

where
\langle G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\tau-\Pi_{1\|}
and the gyroangle-averaged first-order particle gyroradius is

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J}{2\,m\Omega}\left[\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\ln B+\left(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\right]+\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}

(41)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle\right)+\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}-\frac{J}{2}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}

where
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle=(J/m\Omega)\;(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}})
. Hence, we now need expressions for
\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
in order to obtain an explicit expression for the second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian (
40
), which are determined at third order in our perturbation analysis.

3.3
Third-order perturbation analysis

The third-order term in the guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian one-form (
7
) is expressed as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3{\rm gc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\iota_{3}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{0}\;-\;\iota_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{{\rm gc}1}\;+\;\frac{\iota_{1}}{3}\cdot{\sf d}\left(\iota_{1}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{1}+\frac{\iota_{1}}{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{{\rm gc}1}\right)\;+\;{\sf d}\sigma_{3}.

(42)

In what follows, the gauge function
\sigma_{3}
will play an important role in completing the guiding-center phase-space transformation, while the third-order guiding-center Hamiltonian will not be needed in the present guiding-center formulation.

3.3.1
Third-order symplectic structure

The remaining components
(G_{2\|}^{\bf x},\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle,G_{1}^{\theta})
and the first-order guiding-center momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}
will now be determined from the momentum components of the third-order guiding-center symplectic one-form (
42
)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3{\bf p}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[G_{2\|}^{\bf x}\;+\;\frac{\partial D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})}{\partial p_{\|}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;+\;\frac{\partial\sigma_{3}}{\partial p_{\|}}\right]\;{\sf d}p_{\|}\;+\;\left[\frac{2}{3}\,G_{1}^{\theta}+\frac{\partial D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})}{\partial J}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}+\frac{\partial\sigma_{3}}{\partial J}\right]\;{\sf d}J

(43)

\displaystyle+\;\left[-\,\frac{2}{3}\,G_{1}^{J}+\frac{\partial D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})}{\partial\theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}+\frac{\partial\sigma_{3}}{\partial\theta}\right]\;{\sf d}\theta,

where
{\bf P}_{3}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,{\bf P}_{0}+\frac{1}{3}\,{\bf q}_{\bot}
, so that

\displaystyle D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\;G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\frac{1}{3}\left(G_{1}^{J}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial J}+G_{1}^{\theta}\;\frac{\partial{\bf q}_{\bot}}{\partial\theta}\right)\;+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{3}.

(44)

Since
\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}/\partial p_{\|}=0
, the
p_{\|}
-component of Eq. (
43
) suggests that we define the new gauge function

\overline{\sigma}_{3}\;\equiv\;\sigma_{3}\;+\;D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;=\;\sigma_{3}\;-\;\frac{2}{3}\;J\,G_{1}^{\theta},

(45)

where the last expression follows from Eq. (
44
). Using the new gauge function (
45
), the momentum components (
43
), therefore, become

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3{\bf p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(G_{2\|}^{\bf x}\;+\;\frac{\partial\overline{\sigma}_{3}}{\partial p_{\|}}\right)\;{\sf d}p_{\|}\;+\;\left(G_{1}^{\theta}\;+\;\frac{\partial\overline{\sigma}_{3}}{\partial J}\right)\;{\sf d}J

(46)

\displaystyle+\;\left(\frac{\partial\overline{\overline{\sigma}}_{3}}{\partial\theta}\;-\;G_{1}^{J}\;+\;\frac{J\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\right)\;{\sf d}\theta,

where, using Eq. (
44
), we introduced the identities

\displaystyle D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial J}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{3}\,G_{1}^{\theta},

\displaystyle D_{1}({\bf P}_{3})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,G_{1}^{J}\;+\;\frac{2J\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{3},

so that we can also introduce yet another gauge function

\overline{\overline{\sigma}}_{3}\;\equiv\;\overline{\sigma}_{3}\;-\;\frac{1}{3}\left(2\,J\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}\;+\;J\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\right)

(47)

in the
\theta
-component of Eq. (
43
). By requiring that the momentum components (
46
) vanish, we now obtain the definitions

\displaystyle G_{1}^{J}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{J\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}\;+\;\frac{\partial\overline{\overline{\sigma}}_{3}}{\partial\theta},

(48)

\displaystyle G_{2\|}^{\bf x}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial\overline{\sigma}_{3}}{\partial p_{\|}},

(49)

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial\overline{\sigma}_{3}}{\partial J}.

(50)

Hence, the components
G_{2\|}^{\bf x}
and
G_{1}^{\theta}
are determined from the third-order gauge function
\overline{\sigma}_{3}
, which is determined from Eq. (
47
), while
\overline{\overline{\sigma}}_{3}
is determined from the gyroangle-dependent part
\widetilde{G}_{1}^{J}\equiv\partial\overline{\overline{\sigma}}_{3}/\partial\theta
obtained from Eq. (
38
). Since these gyroangle-dependent components are not needed in what follows, however, they will not be derived here.

3.3.2
Second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian

From Eq. (
48
), we immediately conclude that
\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle
must be defined as

\langle G_{1}^{J}\rangle\;\equiv\;-\;\frac{J\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0},

(51)

which was obtained in previous derivations
(
Brizard 1995
;
Madsen 2010
;
Frei et al. 2020
)
, so that the second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian (
40
) becomes

{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}\;=\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{e}{2}\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\right)\;+\;\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf P}_{0}}{m},

(52)

where we used the identity

\frac{J}{2m\Omega}\,\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,e\,{\bf E}\;\equiv\;\frac{e}{2}\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{2}\,J\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}.

(53)

In previous works
(
Littlejohn 1981
;
Hahm 1996
;
Miyato et al. 2009
;
Madsen 2010
;
Frei et al. 2020
)
, the choice for the first-order symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\,J\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
was used to eliminate the Baños drift
(
Baños 1967
;
Northrop & Rome 1978
)
from the guiding-center velocity (i.e.,
\partial{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}/\partial P_{\|}=0
), which is instead added to the definition of the guiding-center parallel momentum (
32
). This choice, therefore, yields the second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian
{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}=(J\widehat{{\sf b}}/2)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}
.

A different choice adopted by
Tronko & Brizard 2015
for the first-order symplectic momentum

\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1{\rm pol}}\;\equiv\;-\,\frac{1}{2}\,J\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}},

(54)

on the other hand, was previously shown to yield an exact Lie-transform perturbation derivation of the standard guiding-center polarization derived by
Kaufman 1986
in the absence of an electric field. While this choice still eliminates the Baños drift from the guiding-center velocity, it also yields an expression for the second-order guiding-center Hamiltonian (
52
) that exactly represents the guiding-center finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) correction to the electrostatic potential energy
e\,\Phi

(
Brizard 2023a
)
:

{\mathcal{H}}_{2{\rm gc}}\;=\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{e}{2}\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\rangle\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\right).

(55)

Finally, we note that recent numerical studies of particle and guiding-center orbits in axisymmetric magnetic fields
(
Brizard & Hodgeman 2023
)
have shown that guiding-center orbits are faithful (i.e., remain close) to particle orbits only if second-order effects, including the correction (
54
), are included, which confirms earlier results by
Belova et al. 2003
.

4
Guiding-center Hamiltonian Dynamics

In this Section, we summarize the results of the Lie-transform perturbation analysis of the guiding-center Lagrangian dynamics presented in Sec.
3
, and we remove the explicit
\epsilon
scaling by restoring the physical mass
\epsilon\,m\rightarrow m
. Hence, we find the guiding-center phase-space extended one-form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\;{\bf A}\;+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;+\;J\;\left({\sf d}\theta\;-\frac{}{}{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf X}\;-\;{\mathcal{S}}\,{\sf d}t\right)\;-\;W\,{\sf d}t,

(56)

where the guiding-center symplectic momentum

\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\;=\;P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf P}_{\rm E}\;-\;\frac{J}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}

(57)

includes the higher-order polarization correction (
54
). The extended guiding-center Hamiltonian, on the other hand, is expressed as

{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\;=\;e\,\Phi\;+\;K_{\rm gc}\;-\;W,

(58)

where the guiding-center kinetic energy in the drifting frame is

K_{\rm gc}\;=\;\mu\,B\;+\;\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\;+\;\frac{m}{2}\,|{\bf u}_{\rm E}|^{2}\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{J\widehat{{\sf b}}}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right),

(59)

which includes the FLR correction (
55
) to the electrostatic potential energy
e\,\Phi
. We note that the presence of the gyrogauge fields
({\mathcal{S}},{\bf R})
in Eq. (
56
) guarantees gyrogauge invariance of the guiding-center equations of motion derived from them.

4.1
Extended guiding-center Poisson bracket

The extended guiding-center Poisson bracket
\{\;,\;\}_{\rm gc}
is obtained by, first, constructing an 8
\times
8 matrix out of the components of the extended guiding-center Lagrange two-form
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{\rm gc}={\sf d}\Gamma_{\rm gc}
and, then, invert this matrix to obtain the extended guiding-center Poisson matrix, whose components are the fundamental brackets
\{Z^{\alpha},Z^{\beta}\}_{\rm gc}
. From these components, we obtain the extended guiding-center Poisson bracket

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial W}\,\frac{\partial^{*}{\mathcal{G}}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial^{*}{\mathcal{G}}}{\partial t}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial W}\right)\;+\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\nabla^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial P_{\|}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial P_{\|}}\,\nabla^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\right)

(60)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla^{*}{\mathcal{F}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\;+\;\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\theta}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial J}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial J}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial\theta}\right),

where

\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}^{*}\;=\;\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf B}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\;-\;J\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf R},

(61)

and the guiding-center Jacobian is
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}=(e/c)\,B_{\|}^{*}\equiv(e/c)\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}
. In addition, we have introduced the definitions

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{*}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\;+\;{\mathcal{S}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},

(62)

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\nabla\;+\;{\bf R}^{*}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\;-\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\frac{\partial{\bf A}^{*}}{\partial t}+J\,\nabla{\mathcal{S}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial W},

(63)

where
{\bf R}^{*}\equiv{\bf R}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
. We note that the Poisson bracket (
60
) can be expressed in divergence form as

\{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{\alpha}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}{\mathcal{F}}\;\left\{Z^{\alpha},\;{\mathcal{G}}\right\}_{\rm gc}\right),

(64)

and that it automatically satisfies the Jacobi identity

\left\{{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}\{{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{{\mathcal{G}},\frac{}{}\{{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{F}}\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}+\left\{{\mathcal{K}},\frac{}{}\{{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\}_{\rm gc}\right\}_{\rm gc}=0

(65)

since the extended guiding-center Lagrange two-form
\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{\rm gc}={\sf d}\Gamma_{\rm gc}
is exact (i.e.,
{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{\rm gc}={\sf d}^{2}\Gamma_{\rm gc}=0
) provided
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}=0
.

Next, we note that the operators (
62
) and (
63
) contain the gyrogauge-invariant combinations
\partial/\partial t+{\mathcal{S}}\,\partial/\partial\theta
and
\nabla+{\bf R}\;\partial/\partial\theta
, while Eqs. (
61
) and (
63
) include the gyrogauge-independent vector fields
(
Ye & Kaufman 1992
;
Brizard 2023b
)

\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf R},\nabla{\mathcal{S}}\;-\;\frac{\partial\bf R}{\partial t}\right)\;=\;\left(-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\,b^{i}\;\nabla b^{j}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla b^{k},-\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial t}\right),

(66)

where
\epsilon_{ijk}
denotes the completely-antisymmetric Levi-Civita tensor.

4.2
Guiding-center Hamilton equations

The guiding-center Hamilton equations
\dot{Z}^{\alpha}\equiv\{Z^{\alpha},{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gc}\}_{\rm gc}
include the guiding-center velocity

\dot{\bf X}\;=\;\frac{P_{\|}}{m}\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B_{\|}^{*}},

(67)

where
\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}=P_{\|}/m
defines the parallel guiding-center velocity, and the guiding-center parallel force

\dot{P}_{\|}\;=\;e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}},

(68)

where the modified electric field is represented as

\displaystyle e\,{\bf E}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,{\bf E}-\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}-\nabla K_{\rm gc}+J\left(\frac{\partial\bf R}{\partial t}-\nabla{\mathcal{S}}\right),

(69)

and the gyroangle angular velocity

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{\rm gc}}{\partial J}\;+\;{\mathcal{S}}\;+\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}^{*}=\Omega\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right)\;+\;{\mathcal{S}}\;+\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}^{*}.

(70)

We note that the reduced guiding-center equations of motion (
67
)-(
68
) satisfy the guiding-center Liouville equation

\frac{\partial B_{\|}^{*}}{\partial t}\;=\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(B_{\|}^{*}\;\dot{\bf X}\right)\;-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial P_{\|}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\;\dot{P}_{\|}\right),

(71)

where

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(B_{\|}^{*}\;\dot{\bf X}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}-e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{c}{e}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}=-\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}^{*}}{\partial t}-e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}^{*}}{\partial P_{\|}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial P_{\|}}\left(B_{\|}^{*}\;\dot{P}_{\|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}^{*}}{\partial P_{\|}}+{\bf B}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,e\frac{\partial{\bf E}^{*}}{\partial P_{\|}}=e\,{\bf E}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}^{*}}{\partial P_{\|}}-{\bf B}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial t},

where we made use of the modified Faraday’s law
\partial{\bf B}^{*}/\partial t=-c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}^{*}
.

4.3
Eulerian variations of the guiding-center Lagrangian

The results of the Lie-transform perturbation analysis carried out in Section
3.3
can also be used to construct the following (regular) guiding-center Lagrangian

\displaystyle L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}+\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}-J\,{\bf R}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;+\;J\,\dot{\theta}\;-\;\left(e\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gc}\;+\;J\,{\mathcal{S}}\right)

(72)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(e/c)\,{\bf A}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;+\;J\,\dot{\theta}-H_{\rm gc},

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}
and
K_{\rm gc}
are defined in Eqs. (
57
) and (
59
), respectively. The guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equations are derived from this Lagrangian as

\dot{P}_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;e\,{\bf E}^{*}\;+\;(e/c)\,\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}^{*},

(73)

\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;=\;P_{\|}/m,

(74)

which are associated with virtual displacements
\delta{\bf X}
and
\delta P_{\|}
, respectively. From these equations, we easily recover the guiding-center Hamilton equations (
67
) and (
68
). Likewise, the guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equation associated with the virtual displacement
\delta J
yields Eq. (
70
), while the virtual displacement
\delta\theta
yields
\dot{J}=0
as a result of Noether’s Theorem.

In addition to variations with respect to guiding-center phase-space coordinates, the guiding-center Lagrangian (
72
) can also be varied with respect to the electric and magnetic fields
(\delta{\bf E},\delta{\bf B})
, which respectively yield expressions for the guiding-center polarization and magnetization
(
Brizard 2008
)
. Here, the Eulerian variation of the guiding-center Lagrangian (
72
) is expressed as
(
Brizard 2023b
)

\displaystyle\delta L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\delta{\bf A}\;+\;\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;-\;\left(e\,\delta\Phi\;+\frac{}{}\delta K_{\rm gc}\right)\;-\;J\left(\delta{\mathcal{S}}\;+\frac{}{}\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf R}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}-e\,\delta\Phi\right)+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf E}+\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{{\bf P}_{0}}{mc}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf B}\;+\;({\rm FLR}),

where the FLR corrections, which are ignored in Eq. (
75
), are calculated to first order in a recent paper
(
Brizard 2023b
)
. Here, the guiding-center electric dipole moment
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}
is defined as
(
Pfirsch 1984
;
Pfirsch & Morrison 1985
)

\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\frac{e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\dot{\bf X}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right),

(76)

while the guiding-center magnetic dipole moment
\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}/(mc)
is defined as the sum of the intrinsic magnetic dipole moment

\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;\mu\left(-\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\frac{1}{\Omega}\,\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right),

(77)

which includes the gyrogauge correction associated with
\delta{\mathcal{S}}+\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf R}

(
Brizard 2023b
)
, and the moving electric-dipole contribution
(
Jackson 1975
)
, expressed in terms of the lowest-order guiding-center momentum
{\bf P}_{0}=P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+{\bf P}_{\rm E}
. By ignoring these FLR corrections, the guiding-center polarization and magnetization are defined as moments of the guiding-center electric and magnetic dipole moments

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc},

(78)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{{\bf P}_{0}}{mc}\right),

(79)

where the guiding-center phase-space density
F_{\rm gc}\equiv{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\,F
includes the guiding-center Jacobian
{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}
and the notation
\int_{P}
includes an integral over guiding-center momentum space as well as a sum over particle species.

Finally, we note that, in the absence of an electric field, the classical expression
(
Kaufman 1986
)
for the guiding-center electric dipole moment
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}=(e\widehat{{\sf b}}/\Omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\dot{\bf X}
is derived by Lie-transform perturbation method
(
Tronko & Brizard 2015
)
only if the first-order polarization correction (
54
) is used.

5
Summary

In the present work, a set of higher-order guiding-center Hamilton equations was derived by Lie-transform perturbation method for the case of time-dependent electric and magnetic fields that satisfy the standard guiding-center space-time orderings. The second-order corrections in the guiding-center Hamiltonian represented finite-Larmor-radius corrections of the lowest-order electrostatic potential energy
e\,\Phi
. Additional second-order corrections in the guiding-center Lagrangian (
72
) included gyrogauge-invariance contributions to the guiding-center Hamiltonian and Poisson bracket as well as corrections leading to the correct guiding-center polarization.

When we turn our attention to the self-consistent interactions of the charged-particle guiding-centers and the electromagnetic fields associated with plasma confinement, we need to derive a set of higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations. Work presented elsewhere
(
Brizard 2023b
)
considered the variational formulation of the higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
72
) and the Maxwell Lagrangian density.

According to this variational principle, using the guiding-center Liouville equation (
71
), the guiding-center Vlasov equation for the guiding-center phase-space density
F_{\rm gc}\equiv{\mathcal{J}}_{\rm gc}\,F
is written in divergence form as

\frac{\partial F_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\dot{\bf X}\frac{}{}F_{\rm gc}\right)\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial P_{\|}}\left(\dot{P}_{\|}\frac{}{}F_{\rm gc}\right)\;=\;0,

(80)

while the Maxwell equations with particle sources are expressed as

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\,\varrho\;\equiv\;4\pi\left(\varrho_{\rm gc}\;-\frac{}{}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\right),

(81)

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}-\frac{1}{c}\,\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{c}\,{\bf J}\;\equiv\;\frac{4\pi}{c}\left({\bf J}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}\;+\;c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc}\right),

(82)

where the guiding-center charge and current densities are
\varrho_{\rm gc}=\int_{P}e\,F_{\rm gc}
and
{\bf J}_{\rm gc}=\int_{P}e\,\dot{\bf X}\;F_{\rm gc}
. Here, the guiding-center polarization charge density
\varrho_{\rm pol}\equiv-\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}
and current density
{\bf J}_{\rm pol}\equiv\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}/\partial t
are derived from the guiding-center polarization (
78
), while the guiding-center magnetization current density
{\bf J}_{\rm mag}\equiv c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc}
is derived from the guiding-center magnetization (
79
). The remaining source-free Maxwell equations are Faraday’s law
\partial{\bf B}/\partial t=-c\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf E}
and Gauss’s law
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}=0
. We note that the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell variational principle also guarantees the existence of exact energy-momentum conservation laws, derived by the Noether method
(
Brizard 2008
)
. Our recent work
(
Brizard 2023b
)
has considered the set of higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations obtained by explicitly imposing the quasineutrality constraint
\varrho_{\rm gc}=\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}
.

Future work will explore the Hamiltonian structure of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations, when guiding-center polarization and magnetization are included, which will generalize our previous work
(
Brizard 2021
)
, and its development is motivated by the desire to construct structure-preserving numerical algorithms
(
Morrison 2017
)
using an important set of reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations.

Character stacks are PORC count
Nick Bridger, Masoud Kamgarpour

Abstract
. We compute the number of points over finite fields of the character stack associated to a compact surface group and a reductive group with connected centre. We find that the answer is a Polynomial On Residue Classes (PORC). The key ingredients in the proof are Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition of complex characters of finite reductive groups and Deriziotis’s results on their genus numbers . As a consequence of our main theorem, we obtain an expression for the E E -polynomial of the character stack.

1.1.
Main result

To state our main result, we need a definition regarding counting problems whose solutions are Polynomial On Residue Classes (PORC), cf.

1.1.1.

1.1.3.

Let us outline the proof of the theorem. First, using Lang’s theorem,

|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=|\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{g},G({\mathbb{F}_{q}}))/G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|.

Thus, our goal is to count the number of homomorphisms

1.1.4.

Next, a theorem going back to Frobenius (cf.

Thus, computing

1.1.5.

According to Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition

and the set of pairs

1.1.6.

The final ingredient in the proof is results of Carter and Deriziotis on centralisers of semisimple elements and genus numbers
[
7
,
11

. The notion of “genus” is a reductive generalisation of Green’s notion of “type”. The latter is ubiquitous in point counts on character varieties in type

[

The fact

1.3.
Further directions

We expect the main theorem to hold for general reductive groups. The main difficulty with reductive groups with disconnected centre is that Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition and genus numbers are more complicated because centralisers of semisimple elements in
G^{\vee}
may be disconnected.

We also expect the theorem to hold for fundamental groups of non-orientable surfaces. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, this is proved in
[
19
]
. The main issue for general types is that the relationship between Frobenius–Schur indicators and the Lusztig–Jordan decomposition is not well-understood, cf.
[
33
]
for some results in this direction.

Finally, we expect the theorem to hold for fundamental groups of punctured Riemann surfaces. In this case, a careful choice of conjugacy classes at the punctures (generalising the notion of generic from
[
15
]
) will ensure that the resulting character stack and character variety are the same. This is the subject of work in progress
[
18
]
.

Reductive groups over finite fields

Let

Definition 7

2.1.1.

2.2.
Reductive groups over finite fields

Let

2.2.1.

Let

In particular,

Genus numbers are PORC

The aim of this section is to state a theorem of Deriziotis

3.1.
Genus map

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

\mathbf{G}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})^{\mathrm{ss}}

\mathbf{G}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})

Thus, the root system of
\mathbf{G}_{x}^{\circ}

3.1.1.

Let

\Phi_{1}

W

be the complete Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}

\sigma

Genus numbers

Let

For each genus

The freeness assumption implies that every realisation

4.1.

Let

(i)

(ii)

4.2.1.

Let

In the above theorem, the relationship between degrees of

5.
Proofs of main results

5.1.
Counting points

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
2
. Recall that
G
is a connected split reductive group over

.

(1)

As mentioned in §

, Frobenius’ theorem implies

|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G({\mathbb{F}_{q}}))}\left(\frac{|G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|}{\chi(1)}\right)^{2g-2}.

(2)

As

|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=\sum_{[x]\in G^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})^{\mathrm{ss}}/G^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}\quad\sum_{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}_{u}(G_{x}^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}}))}q^{r(x)(2g-2)}\left(\frac{|G_{x}^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|}{\rho(1)}\right)^{2g-2}.

Lusztig’s classification of unipotent representations (Theorem

(4)

Using the notion of genus (§

(5)

Observe that

depend only on the genus

,

. Hence, we can re-write the above sum as

|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi({\Phi^{\vee}})}q^{r(\xi)(2g-2)}\sum_{\rho\in\mathfrak{U}(\widehat{W_{\xi}})}\left(\frac{|\!|G_{\xi}^{\vee}|\!|}{\mathrm{Deg}_{\xi}(\rho)}\right)^{2g-2}(q)\quad\sum_{[x]\in G_{\xi}^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})}1.

(6)

Recall the definition

from Theorem

|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi({\Phi^{\vee}})}q^{r(\xi)(2g-2)}\sum_{\rho\in\mathfrak{U}(\widehat{W_{\xi}})}\left(\frac{|\!|G_{\xi}^{\vee}|\!|}{\mathrm{Deg}_{\xi}(\rho)}\right)^{2g-2}(q)|\!|G_{\xi}^{\mathrm{\vee,[ss]}}|\!|_{i}(q).

Definition 14

For

5.1.1.
Aside on representation

For each

5.2.
Counting polynomials in the case
g=1

In this subsection, we show that if
g=1
, then
|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{i}
has degree
\mathrm{rank}(G)
and leading coefficient

. This establishes Corollary
5
(i)
.

5.2.1.

By the above discussion,

|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{i}=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi({\Phi^{\vee}})}|\!|G_{\xi}^{\mathrm{\vee,[ss]}}|\!|_{i}|\mathfrak{U}(\widehat{W_{\xi}})|.

In view of equation

is empty; i.e., when

is the genus of a

semisimple element. Thus, the degree of
|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|
equals

.

5.2.2.

Next, one can easily check that for genera
(\emptyset,[w])
,
the leading coefficient of
|\!|G_{\xi}^{\mathrm{\vee,[ss]}}|\!|_{i}
equals
1/{|W_{w}|}
, where
W_{w}
denotes the centraliser of

in

.
Thus, the leading coefficient of
|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{i}
is
\sum 1/|W_{w}|
, where the sum runs over conjugacy classes of

. By the orbit stabiliser theorem, this sum equals

. This establishes Corollary
5
(i).
∎

5.2.3.

Here is an alternative (perhaps more intuitive) argument for this corollary. By (
3
),
|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|
is the counting polynomial for the number of conjugacy classes of
G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
. Now the leading term of the class number equals the leading term of the polynomial counting semisimple elements. By a theorem of Steinberg, the latter equals
|Z(G({\mathbb{F}_{q}}))|q^{\mathrm{rank}([G,G])}
. Thus, the leading coefficient is
1
and the degree is
\dim(Z(G))+\mathrm{rank}([G,G])=\mathrm{rank}(G)
.

5.3.
Counting polynomials in the case

In this subsection, we show that if

, then the polynomial

has degree

and leading coefficient

5.3.1.

We claim that only

contributes to the leading term of

. Note that a semisimple element has genus
([\Phi^{\vee}],1)
if and only if it is central.
Thus, the claim implies that the leading term of

is the same as the leading term of
\displaystyle|Z(G^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}}))||G^{\vee}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|^{2g-2}

5

5

Aside: The claim amounts to the statement that only

5.3.2.

For ease of notation, set

Observe that

7.
The character stack of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
revisited

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
g
a positive integer. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be the character stack of associated to
(\Gamma_{g},G)
. In this section, we give an explicit expression for the number of points of
\mathfrak{X}
using the same method employed in
[
16
]
. The main point is that we have a good direct understanding of character degrees of
G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
without having to resort to Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition. It would be interesting to prove directly that the polynomial obtained in this section (see (
13
)) equals the one from Definition
14
.

7.1.
Conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})

Let
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}(q)
denote the set of irreducible polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, except that we exclude
f(t)=t
. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of partitions. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I})
denote the set of maps

\Lambda\colon\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}

such that

|\Lambda|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{I}}|\Lambda(f)|\deg(f)=n.

Then we have a bijection between
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I})
and conjugacy classes of
G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
.
Let

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I}).

7.1.1.
Types

Let
\mathcal{I}_{d}\subset\mathcal{I}
denote the subset of irreducible polynomials of degree
d
over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Given
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
we define

m_{d,\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\#\{f\in\mathcal{I}_{d}\,|\,\Lambda(f)=\lambda\}.

The collection of integers
(m_{d,\lambda})
is called the
type
of
\Lambda
and is denoted by
\tau=\tau(\Lambda)
.

Remark 15
.

One can show that
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
have the same type if and only if the centraliser of the corresponding conjugacy classes in
G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
have the same genus. Thus, we have a bijection between semisimple types and genera of
G
.

7.1.2.

The weight of a type
\tau
is defined by

|\tau|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum d|\lambda|m_{d,\lambda}=n.

Thus, the weight of an element
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
equals the weight of its type.

7.1.3.
Genus number

Let
A_{\tau}(q)
denote the number of
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}(q))
of type
\tau
. (Equivalently,
A_{\tau}(q)
is the genus number of
\tau
.) Our aim is to give an explicit formula for
A_{\tau}(q)
.
Let

T(d)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}m_{d,\lambda}.

Let
I_{d}=I_{d}(q)=|\mathcal{I}_{d}|
denote the number of irreducible polynomials of degree
d
over
q
. By a result attributed to Gauss, we have

(9)

I_{d}(q)=\begin{cases}q-1&\textrm{if $d=1$}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k|d}\mu(k)q^{d/k}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 16
.

\displaystyle A_{\tau}(q)={\displaystyle\prod_{d\geq 1}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{T(d)-1}(I_{d}(q)-i)}{\displaystyle\prod_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}m_{d,\lambda}!}}.

By convention, if
T(d)=0
, then the product involving
T(d)
is defined to be one. We leave the above lemma as an exercise. As a corollary, we conclude that
A_{\tau}(q)
is a polynomial in
q
with rational coefficients.

7.2.
Irreducible characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})

We have seen that irreducible characters of
G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
are in bijection with
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I})
.
Let
\chi_{\Lambda}
denote the irreducible character of
G
corresponding to
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I})
.

7.2.1.

Define the normalised hook polynomial associated to the partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
by

(10)

H_{\lambda}(q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle}\prod(1-q^{h}).

Here the product is taken over the boxes in the Young diagram of
\lambda
and
h
is the hook length of the box. Moreover,
\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{2}
where the sum is taken over the parts in the conjugate partition
\lambda^{\prime}
.

7.2.2.

Next, define the normalised hook polynomial of
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{I})
by

(11)

H_{\Lambda}(q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{1}{2}n^{2}}\prod_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\left(H_{\Lambda(\lambda)}(q^{\deg f})\right).

It is easy to see that
H_{\Lambda}
is a monic polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

7.2.3.

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be the map conjugate to
\Lambda
; i.e.,
\Lambda^{\prime}(f)
is the partition conjugate to
\Lambda(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}
. Then by a theorem of Green, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{|G({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|}{\chi_{\Lambda}(1)}=H_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(q).

7.2.4.

It is clear that the hook polynomial
H_{\Lambda}(q)
depends only on the type of
\Lambda
; in fact, we have

H_{\Lambda}(q)=(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{1}{2}n^{2}}\prod_{d,\lambda}\left(H_{\lambda}(q^{d})\right)^{m_{d,\lambda}}.

Given a type
\tau
, we write
H_{\tau}(q)
for the hook polynomial associated to
\tau
.

7.3.
Counting points on
\mathfrak{X}

Let

(13)

|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}A_{\tau}\big(H_{\tau}(t)\big)^{2g-2}\in\mathbb{Q}[t].

From the above discussions, one easily concludes:

Theorem 17
.

For every finite field
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, we have
|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|(q)
.

7.3.1.

As an example, we consider the case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. The types of weight
2
and their associated invariants are listed in the following table:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\textrm{Representations}&m_{d,\lambda}&T(d)&A_{\tau}&H_{\lambda}&H_{\Lambda}\\
\hline\cr\textrm{Cuspidal}&m_{2,(1)}=1&1&(q^{2}-q)/2&q^{-1/2}(1-q)&q^{2}q^{-1}(1-q^{2})\\
\hline\cr\textrm{Steinberg}&m_{1,(2)}=1&1&q-1&q^{-1}(1-q)(1-q^{2})&q^{2}q^{-1}(1-q^{2})(1-q)\\
\hline\cr\textrm{Determinant}&m_{1,(11)}=1&1&q-1&q^{-2}(1-q^{2})(1-q)&q^{2}q^{-2}(1-q^{2})(1-q)\\
\hline\cr\textrm{Principle series}&m_{1,(1)}=2&2&(q-1)(q-2)/2&q^{-1/2}(1-q)&q^{2}\left(q^{-1/2}(1-q)\right)^{2}\\

Thus, we find:

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{X}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{2}-q)}{2}\big(q-q^{3}\big)^{2g-2}+(q-1)\big(q(1-q)(1-q^{2})\big)^{2g-2}

\displaystyle+(q-1)\big((1-q^{2})(1-q)\big)^{2g-2}+\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{2}\big(q(1-q)^{2}\big)^{2g-2}.

For instance, for
g=2,3
we obtain, respectively, the polynomials

q^{9}-2q^{8}-2q^{7}+11q^{6}-18q^{5}+17q^{4}-8q^{3}-q^{2}+3q-1.

and

\displaystyle q^{17}-5q^{16}+6q^{15}+11q^{14}-34q^{13}+29q^{12}-34q^{11}+124q^{10}-230q^{9}

\displaystyle+204q^{8}-74q^{7}-q^{6}-14q^{5}+29q^{4}-10q^{3}-6q^{2}+5q-1.

7.4.
The
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
character stack

In this subsection, we study the character stack associated to
(\Gamma_{g},\mathrm{PGL}_{n})
.
For each
n^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity
\zeta
, consider

\mathfrak{X}_{\zeta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2g}\backslash\mathrm{Hom}_{\zeta}(\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/\mathrm{PGL}_{n},

where

\mathrm{Hom}_{\zeta}(\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x_{1},...,x_{g},y_{1},...,y_{g}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\,|\,[x_{1},y_{1}]\cdots[x_{g},y_{g}]\zeta=1\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}=\bigsqcup_{\zeta\in\mu_{n}}X_{\zeta}

is the decomposition of
\mathfrak{X}
into its connected components. For primitive roots of unity
\zeta
, the arithmetic geometry of
\mathfrak{X}_{\zeta}
was studied in
[
16
]
. We consider the opposite case, i.e., when
\zeta=1
.

7.4.1.
Counting points on
X_{1}

Let

|\!|\mathfrak{X}_{1}|\!|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2g-1}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}A_{\tau}(q)H_{\tau}(q)^{2g-2}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\frac{A_{\tau}}{(q-1)}\left(\frac{H_{\tau}(q)}{q-1}\right)^{2g-2}.

Then Theorem
17
implies:

Corollary 18
.

For every finite field
{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, we have
|\mathfrak{X}_{1}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=|\!|\mathfrak{X}_{1}|\!|(q)
.

7.4.2.
Proof of Corollary
6
.(iii)

To obtain the Euler characteristic of
\mathfrak{X}_{1}
, we compute the value of
|\!|{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}|\!|
at
1
. Observe that
H_{\tau}(q)
is divisible by exactly one factor of
(q-1)
if and only if the only non-zero
m_{d,\lambda}
in
\tau
is
m_{n,(1)}=1
. In this case,
H_{\tau}=(1-q^{n})
. Thus,

\left(\frac{H_{\tau}(q)}{q-1}\right)(1)=-n.

Moreover, we have
A_{\tau}=I_{n}(q)
; therefore,

\frac{A_{\tau}}{(q-1)}(1)=I_{n}^{\prime}(1)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k|n}\mu(k)\frac{n}{k}=\phi(n)/n.

Here the last equality follows from the well-known relation between the Möbius and Euler functions. We therefore obtain

|\!|\mathfrak{X}_{1}|\!|(1)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left(\frac{A_{\tau}}{(q-1)}\right)(1)\Bigg(\left(\frac{H_{\tau}(q)}{q-1}\right)(1)\Bigg)^{2g-2}=\phi(n).n^{2g-3}.

∎

Appendix A
Complex character stacks

In this appendix, we discuss the implications of our main theorem for the complex character stack
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{g},G_{\mathbb{C}})/G_{\mathbb{C}}]
. To this end, we first recall a theorem of Katz
[
16
, Appendix]
on polynomial count schemes over
\mathbb{C}
.

A.0.1.

Let
Y
be a separated scheme or (algebraic) stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. A spreading out of
Y
is a pair
(A,Y_{A})
consisting of a subring
A\subset\mathbb{C}
, finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, a scheme
Y_{A}
over
A
and an identification
Y_{A}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{C}\simeq Y
.

Definition 19
.

The stack
Y/\mathbb{C}
is said to be
polynomial count
if there exists a polynomial
|\!|Y|\!|\in\mathbb{C}[t]
and spreading out
(A,Y_{A})
such that for every homomorphism
A\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, we have
|Y_{A}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=|\!|Y|\!|(q)
.

A.0.2.

If
Y/\mathbb{C}
is a polynomial count scheme, then a theorem of Katz states the
E
-polynomial of
Y
(defined using its mixed Hodge structure) is given by

(14)

E(Y;x,y)=|\!|Y|\!|(xy).

A.0.3.

Applying the above considerations to the situation of interest to us, Theorem
2
implies:

Corollary 20
.

The complex representation variety
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{g},G_{\mathbb{C}})
is polynomial count with counting polynomial
|\!|\mathfrak{R}|\!|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{1}\times|\!|G|\!|
. Thus,

E(\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}};x,y)=|\!|\mathfrak{R}|\!|(xy).

Proof.

Let
d=d(G^{\vee})
and
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Z}[1/d,\zeta_{d}]
. It is easy to see that we have a unital algebra homomorphism
A\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
if only if
q\equiv 1\mod d
, cf.
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
. Now let us choose the spreading out
\mathfrak{R}_{A}
. Then Theorem
2
states that for every homomorphism
A\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, we have
|\mathfrak{R}_{A}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})|=(|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{1}\times|\!|G|\!|)(q)
.
∎

A.0.4.

The analogue of Corollary
5
gives us the dimension and number of irreducible components of highest dimension of
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}
. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, these results were obtain in
[
28
]
, who also proved that
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}}
is irreducible and rational. On the other hand, for a general semisimple
G
, it was proved in
[
21
]
that
\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{R}_{\mathbb{C}})=|\pi_{1}([G,G])|
.

A.0.5.

Next, suppose
Y
is an algebraic stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.
Thinking of
Y
as a simplicial scheme (cf. the appendix of
[
29
]
), we have a mixed Hodge structure on the cohomology of
Y

[
10
]
and therefore an
E
-series
E(Y;x,y)
. If
Y=[Z/G]
is a quotient stack of a scheme by a connected algebraic group, then one can show that
E(Y)=E(Z)/E(G)
.
Applying these considerations to the complex character stack
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}}
, our main theorem implies:

Corollary 21
.

The complex character stack is polynomial count with counting polynomial
|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{1}
. Moreover,
E(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}};x,y)=|\!|\mathfrak{X}|\!|_{1}(xy)
.

A.0.6.

This corollary implies that the virtual Hodge numbers of
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}}
(denoted by
e_{p,q}
in
[
16
, Appendix]
) are balanced; i.e. only
(p,p)
-type appear. This is in agreement with the (a priori stronger) fact
[
29
]
that the mixed Hodge structure of
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}}
is Tate; i.e. only
(p,p)
classes appear.

A.0.7.

The above discussion implies that corollaries
5
and
6
remain valid in the complex setting. On the other hand, the formulas for the dimension and number of components of
\mathfrak{X}_{\mathbb{C}}
(or, more precisely, its coarse moduli space) can also be understood via the non-abelian Hodge theory. Namely, we have a real analytic isomorphism between the character variety and the moduli space of
semistable
G
-Higgs bundles on a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
.
The formulas for dimension and number of components have been known for a long time in the Higgs bundle setting.

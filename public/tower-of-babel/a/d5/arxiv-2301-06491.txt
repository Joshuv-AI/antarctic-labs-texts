1.1.
An anisotropic expanding flow

Expanding curvature flows have been extensively studied in the past decades. Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a smooth, closed and uniformly convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
enclosing the origin. We consider the flow

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=&F^{-\alpha}(\kappa)\nu(x,t),\quad\alpha>0\\
X(x,0)=&X_{0}(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where the curvature function
F
is symmetric, homogeneous of degree 1, strictly monotone and concave. The solution of this class of flows exists on the maximal time
t\in[0,T^{*})
(when
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
T^{*}=\infty
and when
\alpha>1
,
T^{*}
is finite) and converges to a sphere after a proper rescaling (see Gerhardt
[
12
,
13
]
and Urbas
[
31
]
). When
\alpha=1
, Urbas
[
31
]
and Gerhardt
[
12
]
studied the expanding flows of convex hypersurfaces respectively. Especially, when
0<\alpha\leq 1
, the convex assumption of the initial hypersurface is further weakened in
[
13
]
. Here
F
can be chosen as symmetric curvature function
\left(\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{n-k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}(\kappa)
(see
[
4
]
), where
\sigma_{i}
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of principal curvatures. If we further denote
\lambda
as the principal radii of curvature, then flow (
1.1
) can be written as

(1.2)

\partial_{t}X=\sigma_{k}(\lambda)^{\alpha}\nu,\quad\alpha>0.

Generally, we consider the following anisotropic curvature flow in this paper. Let
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a family of smooth, closed and uniformly convex hypersurfaces, which satisfies

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=&\psi\left(\nu\left(x,t\right)\right)\sigma_{k}(\lambda(x,t))^{\alpha}\nu(x,t),\\
X(x,0)=&X_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha>0
,
1\leq k<n
,
\nu
is the unit outward normal vector at
X(\cdot,t)
and
\psi
is a positive smooth function defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Assume
\mathcal{M}
is strictly convex. The support function
u
is defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as
u(x)=\max\limits_{X\in\mathcal{M}}\langle X,x\rangle,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
f_{i}=\nabla_{i}f
,
f_{ij}=\nabla^{2}_{ij}f
and
f_{ijk}=\nabla^{3}_{ijk}f
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection corresponding to the standard metric
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. If we choose the local orthonormal frame on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then the principal radii of curvature can be represented as eigenvalues of the matrix

b_{ij}=u_{ij}+u\delta_{ij}.

Analogous to the calculations in
[
30
]
, we first derive the normalised flow of (
1.3
). Define
\tilde{X}(t)=\left(\frac{|S^{n}|}{V_{k+1}(u,u...,u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}X(t)
and

\displaystyle\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{|S^{n}|}{V_{k+1}(u,u,\cdots,u)}\right)^{\frac{1-k\alpha}{k+1}}ds.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{X}}{\partial\tau}=\psi\left(\nu\right)\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{\alpha}\nu-X(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hskip-15.50005pt-\ \psi\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{1+\alpha}.

If no confusion arises, we substitute
t,X
for
\tau,\tilde{X}
and call

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=&\psi\left(\nu\left(x,t\right)\right)\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{\alpha}\nu(x,t)-X(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hskip-15.50005pt-\ \psi\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{1+\alpha}d\mu_{S^{n}},\\
X(x,0)=&X_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

the normalised flow of (
1.3
). Clearly, along the flow (
1.4
), we have

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\sigma_{k}d\mu_{S^{n}}\equiv|S^{n}|.

Flow (
1.4
) can be re-written as a parabolic scalar equation of the support function
u
:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t)=&\psi\left(x,t\right)\sigma_{k}\left(\nabla^{2}u+uI\right)^{\alpha}-u(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hskip-15.50005pt-\ \psi\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{1+\alpha}d\mu_{S^{n}},\\
u(x,0)=&u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

Here we denote
\eta(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\hskip-16.00008pt-\ \ \psi\sigma_{k}\left(\lambda\right)^{1+\alpha}d\mu_{S^{n}}
. Later, we will prove flow (
1.4
) converges to the solution of

(1.7)

\displaystyle u^{1-p}\left(x\right)\sigma_{k}\left(\nabla^{2}u+uI\right)=c\tilde{\psi}(x)\quad{\rm on}\ \mathbb{S}^{n},

where
c
is a positive constant,
p=1+\frac{1}{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\psi}=\psi^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. Note that the elliptic equation (
1.7
) arises naturally in the Brunn-Minkowski theory. When
k=n,p=1
, it is the well-known Minkowski problem. It concerns about the existence, uniqueness, regularities and stabilities of the prescribed Gauss curvature of hypersurfaces. Minkowski
[
26
]
, Alexandov
[

2

introduced p-sums for convex body. Later, Lutwak

studied the Minkowski problem for Firey’s

When

Guan and Ma

established constant rank theorem to prove the existence of Eq. (

generalized the constant rank theorem in

(1.9)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\tilde{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{k+p-1}}+\tilde{\psi}^{-\frac{1}{k+p-1}}g\geq 0.

For

proved the existence results of (

and proved the existence results of Eq. (

by using a constrained curvature flow. It still remained a confusing problem whether this problem can be settled with a parabolic approach for

We mainly get the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex and origin-symmetric hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume

\alpha>\frac{1}{k}
,
\psi
is a positive smooth even function on

and
\nabla^{2}\psi^{\frac{1}{1+k\alpha}}+\psi^{\frac{1}{1+k\alpha}}g

1.3

. After a proper normalisation, the solution of the flow exists for all time and the rescaled hypersurfaces

converge to the unique smooth solution of (
1.7
) in the

topology.

[

.

[

Remark 1.2

Theorem

provides a new proof to Guan-Xia’s existence results of the

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{L})\Phi

\displaystyle=-\psi\eta\dot{F}^{ij}u_{ij}+\psi\dot{F}^{ij}(\psi F-\eta u)\delta_{ij}=-k\alpha\eta\psi F+\psi^{2}F\dot{F}^{ij}\delta_{ij}

where we use the
k\alpha

in the last equality. At last we calculate the evolution of

:

(2.17)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{L})b_{ij}

\displaystyle=(\psi F-\eta u)_{ij}+(\psi F-\eta u)\delta_{ij}-\psi\dot{F}^{kl}b_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=\psi_{ij}F+\psi_{i}F_{j}+F_{i}\psi_{j}+\psi\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}b_{pqi}b_{rsj}+\psi\dot{F}^{kl}b_{klij}-\eta b_{ij}+\psi F\delta_{ij}-\psi\dot{F}^{kl}b_{ijkl}.

Using the Codazzi equation and the Ricci identity, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{kl}b_{klij}=\dot{F}^{kl}b_{kilj}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}^{kl}b_{kijl}+\dot{F}^{kl}b_{pk}R_{pilj}+\dot{F}^{kl}\delta_{pi}R_{pklj}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}^{kl}b_{ijkl}+\dot{F}^{kl}b_{kl}\delta_{ij}-\dot{F}^{kl}b_{jk}\delta_{il}+\dot{F}^{kl}b_{il}\delta_{kj}-\dot{F}^{kl}b_{ij}\delta_{kl}

\displaystyle=\dot{F}^{kl}b_{ijkl}+k\alpha F\delta_{ij}-\dot{F}^{kl}\delta_{kl}b_{ij}.

Here we used the fact that

(see
[
5
]
for reference) and

is homogeneous of degree

. By (

), we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{L})b_{ij}=\psi_{ij}F+\psi_{i}F_{j}+F_{i}\psi_{j}+\psi\ddot{F}^{pq,rs}b_{pqi}b_{rsj}+(k\alpha+1)\psi F\delta_{ij}-\dot{F}^{kl}\delta_{kl}b_{ij}-\eta b_{ij}.

Thus, we obtain (

).

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\psi\frac{\Phi}{u}\frac{\dot{F}^{ij}\rho^{2}_{i}u_{j}}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\psi\frac{\Phi}{u}\frac{\dot{F}^{ij}(2u_{i}u+2u_{li}u_{l})u_{j}}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\Phi\frac{2\psi\dot{F}^{ii}b_{ii}u_{i}^{2}}{u(1-\epsilon\rho^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Inserting (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) into (
4.6
),

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi}{u(1-\epsilon\rho^{2})}\left((1-k\alpha)\eta-(1-k\alpha)\frac{\psi F}{u}+\epsilon\frac{F\psi_{k}u_{k}-\eta\rho^{2}-\psi\dot{F}^{ij}b_{ki}b_{kj}+(1+k\alpha)\psi uF}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi}{u(1-\epsilon\rho^{2})}\left((1-k\alpha)\eta-\epsilon\frac{\eta\rho^{2}}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Phi}{u(1-\epsilon\rho^{2})}F\left(\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}\left((1+k\alpha)\psi u+\psi_{k}u_{k}\right)-(1-k\alpha)\frac{\psi}{u}\right)-\epsilon\frac{\Phi}{u(1-\epsilon\rho^{2})}\frac{\psi}{1-\epsilon\rho^{2}}\dot{F}^{ij}b_{ki}b_{kj}

\displaystyle\leq-CF^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha k}}+cF+c.

Thus F has an upper bound and we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Denote
\{h^{ij}\}
as the inverse matrix of
\{b_{ij}\}
,
G=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(b_{ij})
.

Then we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\ddot{G}^{pq,lm}+2\dot{G}^{pm}h^{ql})\eta_{pq}\eta_{lm}\geq 2G^{-1}(\dot{G}^{pq}\eta_{pq})^{2}

for any tensor
\{\eta_{pq}\}
.

Proof.

For convenience, we give a sketch of the proof here. Let
S(h^{ij})=\frac{1}{G(b_{ij})}
. We have

\displaystyle\dot{S}^{ij}:=\frac{\partial S}{\partial h^{ij}}=-G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}\frac{\partial b_{pq}}{\partial h^{ij}}=G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}b_{jq}.

Since
S
is concave, given any tensor
\{\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle S^{ij,\lambda\mu}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\tilde{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{lm}}(G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}b_{jq})\frac{\partial b_{lm}}{\partial h^{\lambda\mu}}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\tilde{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b_{l\lambda}b_{\mu m}(-2G^{-3}\dot{G}^{lm}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}b_{jq}+G^{-2}\ddot{G}^{pq,lm}b_{pi}b_{jq}+G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}\delta_{p}{}^{l}\delta_{i}{}^{m}b_{jq}

\displaystyle+G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}\delta_{j}{}^{l}\delta_{q}{}^{m})\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\tilde{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G^{-3}\dot{G}^{lm}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}b_{jq}b_{l\lambda}b_{\mu m}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\tilde{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}-G^{-2}\ddot{G}^{pq,lm}b_{pi}b_{jq}b_{l\lambda}b_{\mu m}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\bar{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}

\displaystyle-2G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}b_{pi}b_{j\lambda}b_{\mu q}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}\tilde{\eta}^{\lambda\mu}

where we used
\delta_{\lambda}{}^{m}=h^{ml}b_{\lambda l}
in the last equality of the above equation. By setting
\eta_{pm}=b_{pi}\tilde{\eta}^{ij}b_{jm}

\displaystyle 0\geq 2G^{-3}\dot{G}^{pq}\dot{G}^{lm}\eta_{pq}\eta_{lm}-G^{-2}\ddot{G}^{pq,lm}\eta_{pq}\eta_{lm}-2G^{-2}\dot{G}^{pq}h^{ml}\eta_{pm}\eta_{lq},

which implies the statement.

Interference of resonances in two-proton emission of 16 Ne
Tomohiro Oishi, Masaaki Kimura

Abstract
We investigate the two-proton ( 2 ​ p 2p ) emission from 16 Ne with the time-dependent 14 O + p + p +p+p three-body calculations. Two 0 + 0^{+} resonances are suggested to participate. For the 0 1 + 0^{+}_{1} resonance, the true emission of the spatially localized two protons is dominant, if the single-particle s 1 / 2 s_{1/2} resonance locates above the 2 ​ p 2p energy. By evaluating a time-evolution probability, the 0 2 + 0^{+}_{2} resonance is suggested at ≅ 3 \cong 3 MeV from the 2 ​ p 2p threshold. The inclusion of 0 2 + 0^{+}_{2} resonance makes the true- 2 ​ p 2p emission more dominant in the time-dependent calculations.



I
Introduction

Two-proton (
2p
) radioactivity is a characteristic
decay process beyond the proton-drip line
Pfützner et al. 2012
;
Blank and Ploszajczak 2008
;
Blank and Borge 2008
;
Grigorenko 2009
;
Pfützner et al. 2023
;
Qi et al. 2019
.
This phenomenon serves as a natural laboratory to investigate
the open-quantum systems, quantum entanglement,
and pairing correlations in nuclear systems.
From the energy release, lifetime, and
2p
correlations, we earn a deeper knowledge on these issues.
Such utility is unique in the proton-rich side
Qi et al. 2019
, whereas
the neutron-rich nuclei without Coulomb barriers rarely have the meta-stable states.
Because of the remarkable developments in the experimental
techniques
Pfützner et al. 2012
;
Blank and Ploszajczak 2008
;
Blank and Borge 2008
;
Grigorenko 2009
;
Pfützner et al. 2023
,
information on
2p
emissions has been accumulated.

In the first prediction of
2p
emissions by Goldansky, two mechanisms were proposed;
simultaneous and sequential emissions
Goldansky 1960
;
Goldansky 1961
.
However in recent measurements, the actual process
manifests more complicated characters
Bochkarev et al. 1989
;
Grigorenko 2009
;
Egorova et al. 2012
.
A combination of energy release and angular correlation of relative momenta
has been utilized to identify them
Mukha et al. 2008
;
Mukha et al. 2010
;
Egorova et al. 2012
;
Grigorenko et al. 2001
;
Grigorenko et al. 2010
;
Grigorenko et al. 2009a
;
Grigorenko et al. 2009b
;
Miernik et al. 2007
.
For such a complicated process, one cannot approximate it as the direct product of partial two-body problems, and thus, needs to deal with three particles without any discrimination.

The
16
Ne nucleus is a
2p
emitter, which can be approximated as the two valence protons around the proton-shell-closed core
14
O (
Z=8
).
Its subsystem
15
F is unbound and has two low-lying resonances in the
s_{1/2}
and
d_{5/2}
channels
Ajzenberg-Selove 1991
;
Peters et al. 2003
;
Goldberg et al. 2004
;
Guo et al. 2005
.
Therefore, the proton-proton interaction
can induce the mixing of the
(s_{1/2})^{2}
and
(d_{5/2})^{2}
configurations, which will affect the
2p
-emitting process of
16
Ne.
In particular, the
s_{1/2}
resonance in
15
F can play a role of so-called intermediate resonance to invoke the sequential emission
KeKelis et al. 1978
;
Goldberg et al. 2004
;
Guo et al. 2005
;
Grigorenko et al. 2015
.
However, the recent experiments have shown that the decay of
16
Ne is dominated by
the prompt (direct or true)
2p
emission from the lowest
0^{+}
resonance
Brown et al. 2014
;
Charity 2016
.
For its decay width, the early experiment gave
\Gamma_{2p}=0.11(4)
MeV
Woodward et al. 1983
.
Recent experiments have reported the possibility of narrower width
Brown et al. 2014
;
Wamers et al. 2014
,
which is not in contradiction to theoretical predictions of keV-order width
Grigorenko et al. 2002
;
Fortune 2014
;
Grigorenko et al. 2015
.
On the other side, there can exist the second
0^{+}
resonance
Föhl et al. 1997
.
Whether this
0^{+}_{2}
plays a sizable role or not in the emission process is still an open question.
Note that, in the isobaric-analogue partner
16
C, the bound
0^{+}_{2}
state has been expected
Fortune et al. 1977
.
The co-existence of
0^{+}_{1}
and
0^{+}_{2}
states is common in other
sd
-shell even-even nuclei, e.g.
18
Ne and
18
O
Brookhaven National Laboratory 2022
.

This paper aims to clarify (i) whether the
0^{+}_{2}
resonance exists or not in
16
Ne, and (ii) how it affects the
2p
-emitting process.
We employ the time-dependent three-body (
14
O
+p+p
) calculation, where
both the
0^{+}_{1}
and
0^{+}_{2}
resonances can be involved.
The time-dependent calculation is probably the best option to visualize the behaviour of meta-stable state
Gurvitz and Kalbermann 1987
;
Gurvitz et al. 2004
;
Talou et al. 1999
;
Talou et al. 2000
;
Oishi et al. 2014
;
Oishi et al. 2017
;
Wang and Nazarewicz 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
,
and thus,
enables us to intuitively understood the
2p
dynamics by following their time evolution.
The decaying width or equivalently lifetime is directly evaluated from the time-dependent tunneling process.

In the next Sec.
II
, we give the formalism for time-dependent three-body calculations.
Sec.
III
is devoted for presenting our results and discussions.
Finally we summarize this paper in Sec.
IV
.

model and formalism

We employ the three-body model, which has been developed and utilized in Refs.
Hagino and Sagawa 2005
;
Hagino et al. 2007
;
Bertulani and S. Hussein 2007
;

The system contains an inert core (
14
O) of mass
m_{C}
and two valence protons with the following assumptions:
(i) the inert core is spherical and has
0^{+}
spin and parity;
(ii) each valence proton interacts with the core through the spherical mean field
V
;
(iii) two protons interact through the potential
v_{pp}
.
Starting from the general coordinates
\left\{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\bm{x}_{C}\right\}
of two protons and the core,
the total Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{3B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{p}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{x}^{2}_{1}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{p}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{x}^{2}_{2}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{C}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bm{x}^{2}_{C}}

(1)

\displaystyle+V(\bm{x}_{C},\bm{x}_{1})+V(\bm{x}_{C},\bm{x}_{2})+v_{pp}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}),

where
m_{p}=938.272
MeV
/c^{2}
is the proton mass.
From these general coordinates, the core-orbital transformation is defined as

\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{r}_{1}\\
\bm{r}_{2}\\
\bm{R}_{CM}\end{array}\right]=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-1\\
0&1&-1\\
\frac{m_{p}}{M}&\frac{m_{p}}{M}&\frac{m_{C}}{M}\end{array}\right)\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{x}_{1}\\
\bm{x}_{2}\\
\bm{x}_{C}\end{array}\right],

(2)

where
M=m_{p}+m_{p}+m_{C}
, and thus,
\bm{R}_{CM}
is the center-of-mass coordinate.
With these new coordinates, the three-body Hamiltonian reads

\hat{H}_{3B}=\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{1})+\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{2})+v_{pp}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})+\frac{\bm{p}_{1}\cdot\bm{p}_{2}}{m_{C}}+\frac{\bm{P}^{2}_{CM}}{2M}.

(3)

The center-of-mass term
\bm{P}^{2}_{CM}/2M
is separated and omitted in the following.

II.1
core-proton subsystem

In Eq. (
3
),
\hat{h}(\bm{r}_{i})
is the Hamiltonian for the subsystem composed of the
i
th valence proton and the core:

\displaystyle\hat{h}(r_{i})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{d^{2}}{dr_{i}^{2}}+V(r_{i}),

\displaystyle V(r_{i})=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{l(l+1)}{r_{i}^{2}}+V_{WS}(r_{i})+V_{Coul}(r_{i}),

(4)

where
\mu=m_{p}m_{C}/(m_{p}+m_{C})
.
To describe this subsystem,
15
F
=^{14}
O
+p
,
we employ the spherical Woods-Saxon and Coulomb potentials.
The Woods-Saxon potential reads

\displaystyle V_{WS}(r)=V_{0}f(r)+U_{ls}(\bm{l}\cdot\bm{s})\frac{1}{r}\frac{df(r)}{dr},

\displaystyle f(r)=\frac{1}{1+e^{(r-R_{0})/a_{0}}},

(5)

where
R_{0}=r_{0}\cdot 14^{1/3}
and
r_{0}=1.23
fm.
Other parameters given in TABLE
1
will be discussed later.
In addition, the Coulomb potential of uniformly-charged sphere with radius
R_{0}
is employed:

V_{Coul}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hbar c\frac{Z\alpha_{0}}{r}&(r\geq R_{0})\\
\displaystyle\hbar c\frac{Z\alpha_{0}}{2R_{0}}\left[3-\left(\frac{r}{R_{0}}\right)^{2}\right]&(r<R_{0})\end{array}\right.,

(6)

where
\alpha_{0}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\hbar c}\cong\frac{1}{137.036}
and
Z=8
.
The Schödinger equation for the core-proton subsystem reads

\hat{h}\ket{\phi_{a}}=e_{a}\ket{\phi_{a}},

(7)

where
e_{a}
is the eigenenergy of the single-particle level with
the quantum numbers
a=\{n_{a},l_{a},j_{a},m_{a}\}
,
which denote the nodal quantum number, orbital angular momentum, coupled angular momentum, and
magnetic quantum number, respectively.
The single-particle wave function thus reads

\phi_{a}(\bm{r}\sigma)=R_{n_{a}l_{a}j_{a}}(r)\left[Y_{l_{a}}(\theta,\phi)\otimes\chi_{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma)\right]^{(j_{a}m_{a})}.

(8)

The radial part
R_{n_{a}l_{a}j_{a}}(r)
is numerically solved with Numerov method.
The potential
V(r)
has the bound solutions of
0s_{1/2}
,
0p_{3/2}
, and
0p_{1/2}
states.
These bound states are excluded from the model space for the three-body Hamiltonian, due to the Pauli principle.
The continuum states with
e_{a}>0
are discretized within a box of
r_{\rm max}=80
fm radius.

We define the no-correlation

\tilde{\Phi}^{(JM,\pi)}_{ab}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})=\hat{A}\left[\phi_{a}(\bm{r}_{1}\sigma_{1})\otimes\phi_{b}(\bm{r}_{2}\sigma_{2})\right]^{(JM,\pi)},

\pi=+

\ket{\Psi(0)}=\sum_{N}F(0)\ket{E_{N}}

\ket{\Psi(t)}=\exp\left[-it\frac{\hat{H}_{3B}}{\hbar}\right]\ket{\Psi(0)}=\sum_{N}F_{N}(t)\ket{E_{N}},

(15)

where
F_{N}(t)=e^{-it\frac{E_{N}}{\hbar}}F_{N}(0)

Time-evolution probability is determined as
P_{T}(t)=\left|\beta(t)\right|^{2}
, where
Krylov and Fock 1947
;
Kukulin et al. 1989

\beta(t)=\Braket{\Psi(0)|\Psi(t)}=\sum_{N}e^{-it\frac{E_{N}}{\hbar}}\left|F_{N}(0)\right|^{2}.

(16)

Note that
\beta(t)=\int e^{-it\frac{E}{\hbar}}\left|F(E,t=0)\right|^{2}dE
in the continuous limit.
The mean-energy release,

\displaystyle Q_{2p}=\Braket{\Psi(t)|\hat{H}_{\rm 3B}|\Psi(t)}=\sum_{N}\left|F_{N}(0)\right|^{2}E_{N}.

IV
summary

In this work, the
2p
-radioactive emission from the meta-stable
0^{+}
state of
16
Ne is investigated with the time-dependent three-body model.
The
2p
-decaying width of the
0^{+}_{1}
resonance is of keV-order and beyond the present reach of measurement, due to the instrumental nature.
By observing the time-dependent decaying state in the prompt case, a dominance of true emission is confirmed.
This is a natural product of the energy scheme with the high
s_{1/2}
resonance
Peters et al. 2003
.
On the other hand, in the mixed case by assuming the intermediate
s_{1/2}
resonance, the sequential emission appears as expected.

The
0^{+}_{2}
resonance is solved by using the technique of modified confining potential, where the initial state can have a finite inclusion of the
0^{+}_{2}
resonance.
In the prompt case, the
0^{+}_{2}
resonance is predicted at
\cong 3
MeV from the
2p
threshold.
In addition, by including this
0^{+}_{2}
resonance, the true
2p
emission becomes more dominant.
This is one product of the interference of two resonances.

Search for certain emission process, where the predicted
0^{+}_{2}
resonance can be apparent, is in progress now.
One possible case was reported in the pionic double-charge-exchange reaction
Föhl et al. 1997
, where its energy is
\cong 1
MeV higher than our result.
On the other side,
in the one-neutron-knockout reaction from
17
Ne to experimentally produce
16
Ne, the
0^{+}_{2}
resonance is not expected to be involved
Brown et al. 2014
.

This work is limited to the
0^{+}
configuration.
In Refs.
Grigorenko et al. 2002
;
Grigorenko et al. 2015
, the Thomas-Ehrman shift (TES) between the
0^{+}
and
2^{+}
resonances of
16
Ne and its isobaric-analogue states in
16
C has been investigated.
This TES can be utilized for the model optimization involving the uncertain
s_{1/2}
resonance in
15
F.
The TES is not regarded in this work, due to that the numerical costs of time-dependent calculations for the
2^{+}
states should be expensive.
Instead, we consider the ambiguity of
s_{1/2}
resonance in
15
F by comparing the two cases.

Checking the sensitivity to the initial state, on which the experimental production history of
16
Ne should be reflected, is waiting for further investigation
Wang and Nazarewicz 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
.
For comparison with experimental data on
2p
correlations, the final-state interactions are fully taken into account.
Several computational problems remain for this purpose.

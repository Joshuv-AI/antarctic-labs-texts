A sub-functor for Ext and Cohen-Macaulay associated graded modules with bounded multiplicity-II
Ankit Mishra, Tony J. Puthenpurakal

Abstract
. Let ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring, then notion of T T -split sequence was introduced in part-1 of this paper for 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -adic filtration with the help of numerical function e A T e^{T}_{A} . We have explored the relation between AR-sequences and T T -split sequences. For a Gorenstein ring ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) we define a Hom-finite Krull-Remak-Schmidt category 𝒟 A \mathcal{D}_{A} as a quotient of the stable category CM ¯ ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A) . This category preserves isomorphism, i.e. M ≅ N M\cong N in 𝒟 A \mathcal{D}_{A} if and only if M ≅ N M\cong N in CM ¯ ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{\underline{CM}}(A) .This article has two objectives; first objective is to extend the notion of T T -split sequence, and second objective is to explore function e A T e^{T}_{A} and T T -split sequence. When ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) be an analytically unramified Cohen-Macaulay local ring and I I be an 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -primary ideal then we extend the techniques in part-1 of this paper to the integral closure filtration with respect to I I and prove a version of Brauer-Thrall-II for a class of such rings.

In this section we prove Theorem

9.1

In this section
(Q,\mathfrak{n})

Remark 9.2

It is well-known that as

. We also have that if

The following results computes

for MCM

Proposition 9.3
.

(with hypotheses as in
9.1
) Let

be MCM
A
-modules with

having no free-summands. Then

(1)

.

(2)

Let

.

(3)

(a)

(b)

\alpha

-split.

Proof.

(1) Note
\operatorname{Syz}^{A}_{1}(M)
is also Ulrich. Using

(2) Note

.

(3) We have

\displaystyle e_{0}(V)

\displaystyle=e_{0}(M)+e_{0}(N),

\displaystyle=\mu(M)+\mu(N)\quad\text{as $M,N$ are Ulrich,}

\displaystyle=\mu(V).

In particular

has no free summands. We have

-split.
∎

We now state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 9.4
.

(with hypotheses as in
9.1
) All but a finitely many AR-sequences of

are
T
-split.

Proof.

We may assume that

The AR-quiver of

[

be the AR-sequence ending at

. Then by

[

we have

. By

-split.
∎

We now give example of an AR-sequence which is not split.

Example 9.5
.

(with hypotheses as in

) Let

(2)

(2) This follows from Theorem

∎

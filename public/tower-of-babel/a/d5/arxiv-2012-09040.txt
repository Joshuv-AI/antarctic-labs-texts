Abstract
. In this work we prove that for any dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and any γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \gamma\in(0,1) super-Brownian motion corresponding to the log-Laplace equation ∂ v ⁡ ( t , x ) ∂ t = 1 2 △ v ( t , x ) + v γ ( t , x ) , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ + × ℝ d , v ⁡ ( 0 , x ) = f ⁡ ( x ) \begin{split}\frac{\partial v(t,x)}{\partial t}&=\frac{1}{2}\bigtriangleup v(t,x)+v^{\gamma}(t,x),\>(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\ v(0,x)&=f(x)\end{split} is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure at any fixed time t > 0 t>0 . Our proof is based on properties of solutions of the log-Laplace equation. We also prove that when initial datum v ⁡ ( 0 , ⋅ ) v(0,\cdot) is a finite, non-zero measure, then the log-Laplace equation has a unique, continuous solution. Moreover this solution continuously depends on initial data.

1.
Introduction and main result

This paper is devoted to studying regularity properties of the super-Brownian motion with stable branching mechanism with infinite mean.

Let us start with a notation. For a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
\langle\mu,f\rangle
or
\langle f,\mu\rangle

denote the integral of a function
f
with respect to a measure
\mu
(whenever it is well defined):

\langle f,\mu\rangle=\langle\mu,f\rangle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Let
\gamma\in(0,2]\setminus\{1\}
. The super-Brownian motion with
\gamma
-stable branching mechanism,
X=\{X_{t},t\geq 0\}
, is a Markov measure-valued process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

which is characterized
as follows: for any finite measure
\mu
and a nonnegative not identically zero function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1.1)

E_{\mu}\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},\phi\rangle}\right)=E\left(\left.e^{-\langle X_{t},\phi\rangle}\right|X_{0}=\mu\right)=e^{-\langle\mu,v(t,\cdot)\rangle},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Here
v
is a solution to the so-called log-Laplace equation:

(1.2)

v(t,x)=\left(S_{t}f\right)(x)-\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s,\cdot))(x)\mathrm{d}s,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

if
\gamma\in(1,2]
, and for
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the sign in front of the non-linear term is reversed:

(1.3)

v(t,x)=\left(S_{t}f\right)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s,\cdot))(x)\mathrm{d}s,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here and for the rest of the paper
\{S_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
denotes a transition semigroup of Brownian motion
whose generator is Laplacian
\frac{1}{2}\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Clearly

S_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)p_{t}(x-y)\,dy,\quad t\geq 0,

where
\{p_{t}(x),t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is the transition density of the Brownian motion.

\{S_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
describes the underlying Brownian motion of
X
, whereas its
continuous-state branching mechanism described by
v\mapsto\pm v^{\gamma},v\geq 0
.
These equations were considered by Watanabe in
[
18
]
for a more general “motion” operator.
As for the case of super-Brownian motion with
\gamma
-stable branching mechanism (in what follows we will call it
\gamma
-super-Brownian motion)
it is well known that for
\gamma\in(1,2]
in dimensions
d<\frac{2}{\gamma-1}
at any fixed time
T>0
, the measure

X_{t}=X_{t}(dx)
is absolutely continuous with probability one (cf.
[
5
]
). By an abuse of
notation, we sometimes denote a version of the density function of the measure
X_{t}=X_{t}(dx)
by the same symbol,
X_{t}(dx)=X_{t}(x)\,dx
.
It is even known that for
d=1
,
\gamma\in(1,2]
, at fixed times
t
, there is a continuous version of the density in
x
variable (see
[
12
]
), and for
\gamma=2
, and again
d=1
,
there exists even jointly space-time continuous version of the density (see
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
).
More detailed regularity properties of the densities of superprocesses with stable branching mechanism with possibly more general motion have been studied in
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
.

This paper is devoted to deriving absolute continuity of
X
for the case of
\gamma\in(0,1)
. It is easy to check that in this case
E(\langle X_{t},1\rangle)=\infty
, for
t>0
, which adds some technical
dificulties for the proofs.

Before we state the main result of this paper we need to introduce some notation.

Let
E
be any Polish space.
Let
C(E)
and
B(E)
be respectively the spaces of continuous and
Borel
measurable functions on space
E
.
If
F(E)
is a space of real-valued functions on
E
we define the following subspaces of
F(E)
.

F_{b}(E)
(respectively
F^{+}(E),F_{c}(E),F_{bc}(E)
) denotes the subspace of bounded (respectively positive, with compact support, bounded with compact support) functions.
For example,
B^{+}_{bc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes a set of positive, bounded, Borel
measurable functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now let us define the explosion time of the superprocess.

Definition 1.1

(Time of explosion)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a super-Brownian motion with non-random initial state
X_{0}
.
Given nonnegative continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the time of explosion

T(X_{0},f)
of
X
as follows

T(X_{0},f)\equiv\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{\langle X_{t},f\rangle\geq n\right\}\right\}.

Now we are able to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.2

(Absolute continuity)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
0<\gamma<1
. Let
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a

\gamma
-super-Brownian motion with non-random initial state
X_{0}
being a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For each
t>0
,
X_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)
is
P-a.s.
absolutely continuous on the event
\{t<T(X_{0},1)\}
.

The proof of this theorem is based on the properties of solutions to the log-Laplace equation corresponding to the process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
These properties are stated in Theorem
2.2
. This theorem extends results of Aguirre and Escobedo (see
[
1
]
) for the case of non-zero measure-valued initial conditions. The entire Section
4

is devoted to the proof of these properties.
In Section
3.1
we will prove that for any nonnegative, non-zero continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle T(X_{0},f)=T(X_{0},1),\>P-\mathrm{a.s.}

This property allows us to define the density of the superprocess
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
for a fixed time
t>0
.
In Section
3.2
we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
— the main result of the paper.

2.
Semilinear heat equation

For the rest of the paper fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
and arbitrary dimension
d\geq 1
.
One of the main tools for investigating the
\gamma
-super-Brownian motion is the log-Laplace equation

(2.1)

v(t,x)=\left(S_{t}f\right)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s,\cdot))(x)\mathrm{d}s.

Usually in the literature (
2.1
) is studied for
f
being a non-negative function. In the sequel we will consider (
2.1
) also with
f
being a measure.

Before we discuss properties of (
2.1
) we need to introduce some notation.
For a topological space
S
,
\mathcal{B}(S)
will denote the
Borel

\sigma\textrm{-algebra}
on the space
S
.

We denote by
L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(for
p=1
or
p=\infty
) a Banach space of (equivalence classes of) measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the norms:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,w}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w(x)f(x)|\mathrm{d}x,\>\>\textnormal{for}\>\>p=1

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,\omega}

\displaystyle\equiv\inf\{M:Leb(x:|w(x)f(x)|>M)=0\},\>\textnormal{for}\>p=\infty,

where

\displaystyle w(x)\equiv C_{w}e^{-|x|},\>\>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(x)\mathrm{d}x=1,

and
Leb
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

L^{p,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(respectively
L^{p}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) will denote the nonnegative elements of
L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(respectively
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
).

Given
L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(for
p=1
or
p=\infty
) we define the Banach space

L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,\infty),L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of (equivalent classes of) measurable functions on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows:

f\in L^{\infty}_{loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
if and only if

f(t,\cdot)\in L^{p,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any fixed
t
and

t\to\|f(t)\|_{p,w}\in L^{\infty}([a,b])

for any compact interval
[a,b]\in(0,\infty)
. Similarly
L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,\infty),L^{p,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is defined.

By
M_{F}(E)
(respectively
M_{F,S}(E)
) we denote the space of finite measures and finite (respectively finite signed) signed measures on a Polish space
E
equipped with the topology of the weak convergence. We write

\mu_{n}\overset{w}{\Longrightarrow}\mu,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$,}

if the sequence
\{\mu\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of finite measures or finite signed measures weakly converges to a finite measure
\mu
.

If
F
is a set of functions or measures then
\overset{\circ}{F}
denotes this set without zero element, that is
\overset{\circ}{F}=F\setminus\{0\}
. If
F
is a topological space then, topology of
\overset{\circ}{F}
is inherited from
F
.
For example
{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(E)
is a space of finite non-zero measures on Polish space
E
with the topology inherited from
M_{F}(E)
.

With all this notation at hand we can get back to (
2.1
).
Equation (
2.1
) was studied by Aguirre and Escobedo in
[
1
]
).
We state some of their results in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Aguirre and Escobedo
[
1
]
)
.

For any not identically zero
f\in L^{\infty,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists the unique solution
v(t,x,f)
of equation (
2.1
) such that

(1)

v(\cdot,\cdot,f)\in C^{+}\left((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{\infty,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
;

(2)

v(t,x,f)>((1-\gamma)t)^{1/(1-\gamma)},\>(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(3

for
i=1,2
, let
v(t,x,f_{i})
be the solution to (
2.1
) with initial condition
v(0,\cdot,f_{i})=f_{i}
.
If
f_{1}(x)\leq f_{2}(x),\>a.e.\>x
, then

\displaystyle v(t,x,f_{1})\leq v(t,x,f_{2}),\>\forall(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4)

\lim_{t\to 0}v(t,\cdot,f)=f,\>\textnormal{for}\>a.e.\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(5)

for any fixed
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
the mapping

v(t,x,\cdot):L^{\infty,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{++}

is continuous.
Here
\mathbb{R}_{++}\equiv(0,\infty).

Remark
.

In fact, Aguirre and Escobedo prove the above theorem for a more general class of initial data.

We extend the results in Theorem
2.2
for the case of not identically zero measure-valued initial conditions.
Consider the following equation:

(2.2)

v(t,x)=(S_{t}\mu)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(\cdot,s))\mathrm{d}s,\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>t>0,

where
\mu\in{{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and again
d\geq 1
is an arbitrary dimension. We set

S_{t}\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}^{d}p_{t}(x-y)\mu(dy),x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In order to stress dependence of the solutions of this equation on initial data we will sometimes write

v(t,x,\mu)
. In what follows we will also use the following notation for solutions of (
2.2
):

(2.3)

V_{t}(\mu)(x)\equiv v(t,x,\mu),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or being a non-negative, not identically zero function.

Before we state the main result of this section,
let us define the constant
\gamma^{\prime}
in terms of
\gamma
as follows:

\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

Theorem 2.2

(Existence, uniqueness and dependence on initial data)
.

For

any
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, equation (
2.2
) has the unique solution
v(t,x)
such that

v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu)\in L_{loc}^{\infty}\left((0,\infty),L^{1,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\cap C^{+}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

(2.4)

\left((1-\gamma)t\right)^{\gamma^{\prime}}<v(t,x,\mu)\leq e^{t}\left(S_{t}\mu\right)(x)+e^{t},\>0<t<\infty.

Moreover, this solution continuously depends on initial data:
if a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
converges weakly to
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then

\lim_{n\to\infty}v(t,x,\mu_{n})\to v(t,x,\mu),

for any
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Since for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle e^{t}\left(S_{t}\mu\right)(x)+e^{t}\in L^{1,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

it easily follows from inequality (
2.4
) that the sequence of solutions

\{v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which also converges to
v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu)
in
L_{loc}^{\infty}\left(((0,\infty),L^{1,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\cap C^{+}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The proof of the next lemma is trivial and hence it is omitted.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu\in M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\left(S_{t}\mu\right)(x)\leq\frac{\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}},\>\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Now we are ready to state the corollary to Theorem
2.2
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of measures that converges weakly to
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu_{n})
be the corresponding solutions of (
2.2
). Then, for any
\chi\in M_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t\in(0,\infty)
,

(2.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t,x,\mu_{n})\chi(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t,x,\mu)\chi(\mathrm{d}x).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
we have

v(t,x,\mu)\leq e^{t}\left(S_{t}\mu\right)(x)+e^{t},n=1,2,\ldots,\>0<t<\infty.

Since the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to
\mu
, then by Lemma
2.4

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(e^{t}\left(S_{t}\mu\right)(x)+e^{t}\right)\leq e^{t}\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})+1\right)<\infty.\end{split}

Thus, we conclude that the sequence

\{v(t,\cdot,\mu_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.
Also by Theorem
2.2

\{v(t,\cdot,\mu_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges pointwise to
v(t,\cdot,\mu)
.
Hence, by the bounded convergence theorem, (
2.5
) follows.
∎

Theorem
2.2
will be proved in Section
4
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we prove the main result of this paper — absolute continuity of the super-Brownian motion
X
with the branching mechanism
v\mapsto v^{\gamma}
, for
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

In Section
3.1
we investigate explosion time for the
\gamma
-super-Brownian: this is necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.2
that will be concluded in
Section
3.2
.

3.1.
Explosion times

As we will see for any
t>0
, the
\gamma
-super-Brownian motion
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
explodes by time
t
with non-zero probability.
In this section we investigate the distribution of the explosion times.
We assume that
X
is defined on probability space
(\Omega,P,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t})
and adapted to filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. We also assume that the initial state
X_{0}
of
X
is a non-random finite measure.

Remark 3.1
.

By Corollary
4.3.2
, in
[
2
]
, it is easy to show that
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Feller process and therefore it has a strong Markov property.

The next lemma states the elementary properties of the explosion times.
The proofs are simple and easily follow from the definition, so they are omitted.

Lemma 3.2
.

(1)

For any function
f\in\overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and any
a\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle T(X_{0},f)=T(X_{0},af),\>P-\textnormal{a.s.}

(2)

For any
f\in\overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

T(X_{0},1)\leq T(X_{0},f),\>P-\textnormal{a.s.}

In the next lemma we will show that for any
t\geq T(X_{0},f)
, one has
X_{t}(f)=\infty
.
Before we proceed, let us recall
that the Laplace transform of
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is given by

(3.1)

E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}\right)=e^{-\langle X_{0},V_{t}(f)\rangle},\>f\in\overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\{V_{t}(f)\}_{t\geq 0}
solves log-Laplace equation (
2.1
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
t>0
,
f\in\ \overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t\right\}=\left\{X_{t}(f)=\infty\right\},\>P-\textnormal{a.s.}.

Proof.

The
P
-a.s. inclusion
\left\{X_{t}(f)=\infty\right\}\subset\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t\}
is trivial. Now let us show
\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t\}\subset\{X_{t}(f)=\infty\}
,
P
-a.s.
It is enough to verify that

(3.2)

E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t\right\}}\right)=0.

Define the stopping time

\displaystyle T_{n}(X_{0},f)\equiv\inf\left\{t\geq 0,X_{t}(f)=n\right\}.

Clearly
T_{n}(X_{0},f)\to T(X_{0},f),\>P
-a.s., as
n\to\infty
.
Then, for any
\delta\in(0,t)
arbitrarily small,

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right)=

\displaystyle=E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}1_{\left\{T_{n}(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T_{n}(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=E\left(E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}|\mathcal{F}_{T_{n}(X_{0},f)}\right)1_{\left\{T_{n}(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=E\left(e^{-\langle X_{T_{n}(X_{0},f)},V_{t-T_{n}(X_{0},f)}(f)\rangle}1_{\left\{T_{n}(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right).

Here, in (
3.3
), we used the strong Markov Property (see Remark
3.1
).
Fix
c(\delta)>0
sufficiently small such that
c(\delta)f(x)\leq((1-\gamma)t)^{\gamma^{\prime}}
for all
t\geq\delta
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then by Theorem
2.1
(see also
Lemma
2.2
in
[
1
]
) we have

\displaystyle c(\delta)f(x)\leq((1-\gamma)t)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\leq V_{t}(f)(x),\>\forall t\geq\delta,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore the expression (
3.4
) can be bounded from the above by

\displaystyle E\left(e^{-c(\delta)n}1_{\left\{T_{n}(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right).

By the dominated convergence theorem this expression tends to zero, as
n\to\infty
and we get

\displaystyle E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T(X_{0},f)\leq t-\delta\right\}}\right)=0,\forall t>0.

Now take
\delta\searrow 0
and by the monotone convergence theorem we get (
3.2
) and this completes the proof.
∎

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
f\in\overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}\right)=E\left(e^{-\langle X_{t},f\rangle}1_{\left\{T(X_{0},f)>t\right\}}\right),\>\forall t>0.

Now, let us calculate the distribution of
T(X_{0},1)
— the distribution of the explosion time of the total mass of the super-Brownian motion
X
.
By Corollary
3.4
and (
3.1
), we get

(3.6)

\begin{split}P(t<T(1,X_{0}))&=\lim_{a\searrow 0}E\left(1_{\{t<T(1,X_{0})\}}\left(e^{-a\langle X_{t},1\rangle}\right)\right)\\
&=\lim_{a\searrow 0}e^{-\langle V_{t}(a),X_{0}\rangle}\\
&=\lim_{a\searrow 0}\exp\left(-\langle X_{0},1\rangle\big(a^{1-\gamma}+t(1-\gamma)\big)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\\
&=\exp\left(-\langle X_{0},1\rangle\big(t(1-\gamma)\big)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right),\end{split}

where the third equality follows from the fact that
V_{t}(a)
is a solution of the ordinary differential equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\mathrm{d}v(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}}&=v^{\gamma}(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\[10.0pt]
v(0)&=a,\end{array}\right.

and hence

(3.7)

\displaystyle V_{t}(a)(x)=(a^{1-\gamma}+t(1-\gamma))^{\gamma^{\prime}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\geq 0.

Then

(3.8)

F_{T(1,X_{0})}(t)=P(t\geq T)=1-\exp\left(-\langle X_{0},1\rangle\big(t(1-\gamma)\big)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right).

But what about other test functions
f
? What is the law of
\langle X_{t},f\rangle
for a general
f\in\overset{\circ}{L}\mbox{}^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
? The answer is given in the following lemma.
In what follows, in order to simplify notation, we often write
T(f)
instead of

be a sequence of Picard iterations corresponding to
equation (
2.2
). By the previous discussion for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,
\{v_{n}(t,\cdot)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
form a non-decreasing sequence and by Lemma
4.1
we have

(4.7)

v_{n}(t,x)\leq e^{t}(S_{t}\mu)(x)+e^{t}.

Lemma
2.4
tells us that for every
t>0
,
(S_{t}\mu)(\cdot)
is bounded.
Thus, for any
(t,x)
in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the sequence
\{v_{n}(t,x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is non-decreasing and bounded. Consequently there exists a bounded limit
v(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(t,x)
.
Inequalities (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) follow from existence of the limit and (
4.7
).

Now consider the sequence of equations which defines the Picard iterations:

(4.8)

\begin{split}&v_{l+1}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x-y)v^{\gamma}_{l}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,\\
&\hskip 170.71652ptl=1,2,\ldots.\end{split}

We have already proved that the left side of (
4.8
) converges boundedly pointwise to
v(t,x)
. From the monotone convergence theorem it follows that the right side converges to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\mu(\mathrm{d}y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y))\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Thus
v(t,x)
satisfies equation (
2.2
) for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>t>0
.

Now let us verify (
4.4
).
In the following discussion we can assume without loss of generality that
\phi\in C^{+}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since the family of functions
\{p_{t}(\cdot)\}_{t>0}
builds up the
Dirac family
(see
[
10
, pp. 284-287, 348]
),
we can easily conclude that

(4.9)

\begin{split}\lim_{t\searrow 0}\langle S_{t}\mu,\phi\rangle&=\langle\mu,\phi\rangle.\end{split}

Now let us prove

(4.10)

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s,\cdot))(x)\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x=0.

First, using the inequality (
4.5
) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s))(x)\mathrm{d}s&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\big(S_{t-s}\left(e^{s}S_{s}\mu+e^{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=(e^{t}-1)(S_{t}\mu)(x)+(e^{t}-1).\end{split}

Using the above bound we get:

\begin{split}&\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s))(x)\mathrm{d}s\right)\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\lim_{t\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left((e^{t}-1)(S_{t}\mu)(x)+(e^{t}-1)\right)\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\lim_{t\searrow 0}(e^{t}-1)(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty}+\|\phi\|_{1})=0,\end{split}

and (
4.10
) follows. Equations (
4.10
) and (
4.9
) imply (
4.4
) and this completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu)
be a solution
of (
2.2
) obtained as a limit of the Picard iterations in Proposition
4.2
.
Then
v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu)\in L_{loc}^{\infty}\left(((0,\infty),L^{1,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
.

Proof.

Let
v(\cdot,\cdot,\mu)
be a solution constructed in Proposition
4.2
. Using the bound (
4.5
), it is
easy to derive the result by standard Gaussian bounds.
∎

4.2.
Continuity of solutions

In this section we will prove the continuity of the solution obtained in Proposition
4.2
.

We start with the technical lemma, whose proof is
pretty standard, and therefore it is omitted.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
and
r>0
.
Let
\{p_{s}(\cdot+z),\>s\in\left[T_{1},T_{2}\right],\>|z|\leq r\}
be a family of functions, where
p_{s}(\cdot)
is a standard Gaussian kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, there exists a constant
K
, such that

p_{s}(x+z)\leq Kp_{2T_{2}}(x),\>\forall s\in[T_{1},T_{2}],\>|z|\leq r,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Now we are ready to state and prove the main proposition of Section
4.2
.

Proposition 4.5

(Continuity)
.

Let
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
let
v(\cdot,\cdot)
be a solution of (
2.2
)
obtained as a limit of Picard iterations in Proposition
4.2
. Then
v(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{+}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By construction the solution is clearly non-negative.
Now, let us fix a point
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. Let
\delta_{i}>0,i=1,2,3,\>\,\delta_{1}<\delta_{3}<t/10
.
In what follows we will show that
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
and
\delta_{3}
can be chosen sufficiently small so that if
|\Delta t|<\delta_{1}
and
|\Delta x|<\delta_{2}
then

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left|v(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-v(t,x)\right|\leq\epsilon.

We will bound absolute value of difference
v(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-v(t,x)
only for the case of
\Delta t\geq 0
, since the case of
\Delta t<0
can be treated similarly.
We split the difference
v(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-v(t,x)
as follows:

\displaystyle v(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-v(x,t)

\displaystyle=I(\Delta t,\Delta x)+J_{1}(\Delta t,\Delta x)-J_{2}(\Delta t,\Delta x)+J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle+J_{4}(\Delta t,\Delta x)-J_{5}(\Delta t,\Delta x).

Here

\displaystyle I(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=(S_{t+\Delta t}\mu)(x+\Delta x)-(S_{t}\mu)(x),

\displaystyle J_{1}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\delta_{3}}^{t+\Delta t}\big(S_{t+\Delta t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x+\Delta x)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle J_{2}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\delta_{3}}^{t}\big(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\big(S_{t+\Delta t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x+\Delta x)\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\big(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle J_{4}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}\big(S_{t+\Delta t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x+\Delta x)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle J_{5}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta_{3}}\big(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(x)\mathrm{d}s.

Note that the integrals
J_{1},J_{2},J_{4}
and
J_{5}
have the same form:

(4.12)

J_{*}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big(S_{t_{3}-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(z)\mathrm{d}s,

for appropriate
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\geq 0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
From the definitions of
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{3}
and
\Delta t
, it follows that
t_{1},t_{2}
and
t_{3}
in (
4.12
) can vary but satisfy the inequalities
t_{1}<t_{2}
,
t\leq t_{3}
and
t_{2}-t_{1}\leq 2\delta_{3}
hold.
Let us bound
J_{*}
from above. By Lemma
2.4
and Proposion
4.2
,
we easily get

(4.13)

\begin{split}J_{*}&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big(S_{t_{3}-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\big)(z)\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(S_{t_{3}-s}(e^{s}(S_{s}\mu+1))\right)(z)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{s}\left(\left(S_{t_{3}}\mu\right)(z)+1\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=(e^{t_{2}}-e^{t_{1}})\left(\left(S_{t_{3}}\mu\right)(z)+1\right)\\
&\leq\frac{(e^{t_{2}}-e^{t_{1}})\left(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})+1\right)}{(2\pi t_{3})^{d/2}}\\
&\leq\frac{(e^{t_{2}}-e^{t_{1}})\left(\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})+1\right)}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from
t\leq t_{3}
. Recall that
t_{2}-t_{1}\leq 2\delta_{3}
, and so by (
4.13
)
we can choose
\delta_{3}
sufficiently small so that, for
i=1,2,4,5

(4.14)

\displaystyle J_{i}(\Delta t,\Delta x)\leq\epsilon/10,\>\Delta t<\delta_{1}<\delta_{3}.

Let us fix such
\delta_{3}
. Let us recall that
\Delta t<\delta_{1}<\delta_{3}
.
Now we will handle
J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)
.
Write
J_{3}
as
J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)=J_{31}(\Delta t,\Delta x)-J_{32}
, where

(4.15)

\displaystyle J_{31}(\Delta t,\Delta x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t+\Delta t-s}(x+\Delta x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,

(4.16)

\displaystyle J_{32}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

By Lemma
4.4
and Proposition
4.2
, we immediately get that there exists
K=K(\delta_{1},\delta_{1},\delta_{3},t)
such that

\displaystyle p_{t+\Delta t-s}(x+\Delta x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y)

\displaystyle\leq Kp_{2t}(x-y)e^{s}((S_{s}\mu)(y)+1)

\displaystyle\forall\Delta t\in(0,\delta_{1}),s\in(\delta_{3},t-\delta_{3}),|\Delta x|<\delta_{2}.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Kp_{2t}(x-y)e^{s}\left(\left(S_{s}\mu\right)(y)+1\right)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Therefore we can use the dominated convergence theorem and obtain:

(4.17)

\begin{split}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta t\rightarrow 0\\
\Delta x\rightarrow 0\end{subarray}}J_{31}(\Delta t,\Delta x)&=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta t\longrightarrow 0\\
\Delta x\longrightarrow 0\end{subarray}}\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t+\Delta t-s}(x+\Delta x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{\delta_{3}}^{t-\delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)v^{\gamma}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\\
&=J_{32}.\end{split}

Similarly we show

(4.18)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta t\longrightarrow 0\\
\Delta x\longrightarrow 0\end{subarray}}(S_{t+\Delta t}\mu)(x+\Delta x)=(S_{t}\mu)(x),

and thus from (
4.17
) , (
4.18
) and the definition of
I(\Delta t,\Delta x),J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)
we get

(4.19)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta t\longrightarrow 0\\
\Delta x\longrightarrow 0\end{subarray}}I(\Delta t,\Delta x)+J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)=0.

This implies that there exist
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{3})
sufficiently small such that
for
|\Delta t|<\delta_{1}
and
|\Delta x|<\delta_{2}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left|J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)\right|+\left|J_{3}(\Delta t,\Delta x)\right|\leq\epsilon/2.

Thus we get from (
4.14
) and (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\left|v(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-v(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle=|I(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)+J_{1}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-J_{2}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)

\displaystyle\quad\>+J_{3}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)+J_{4}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-J_{5}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|I(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|+\left|J_{1}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|+\left|J_{2}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\>+\left|J_{3}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|+\left|J_{4}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|+\left|J_{5}(t+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon,\>\forall\Delta t,\Delta x:\>\Delta t\in(0,\delta_{1}),|\Delta x|<\delta_{2}.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary we are done.
∎

4.3.
Uniqueness of solutions

The proof of uniqueness is again based on proofs in
[
1
]
which are adjusted to our case.
Let us recall that

\gamma^{\prime}=1/(1-\gamma).

We start with the technical lemma, whose proof is
pretty standard, and therefore it is omitted.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for every
t>0
, there exist two positive constants

c(t)
,
a(t)
such that following inequality holds:

(S_{t}\mu)(x)\geq c(t)\cdot e^{-a(t)|x|^{2}},\>\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In the next lemma we proof an important lower bound.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u(t,x)
be a non-negative function on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for any
t\in(0,\infty)
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:

v(t,x)\geq S_{t}\mu+\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\right)(x)\mathrm{d}s.

Then

(4.21)

\displaystyle v(t,x)>\left(\left(1-\gamma\right)t\right)^{\gamma^{\prime}},\>\forall(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Let us fix an arbitrary
t_{0}>0
and define

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(t)\equiv v(t+t_{0}),\>\forall t\geq 0.

Using this definition one can easily check that

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(t)

\displaystyle\geq S_{t}\tilde{v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}\tilde{v}^{\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

where
\tilde{v}_{0}=\tilde{v}(0)\geq S_{t_{0}}\mu
, and the last inequality follows by definition of
\tilde{v}
and assumptions on
v
. Thus, by Lemma
4.6
there exists
a(t_{0})>0
and
c(t_{0})>0
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{0}(x)\geq\left(S_{t_{0}}\mu\right)(x)\geq c(t_{0})e^{-a(t_{0})|x|^{2}},\>\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By Lemma
2.2
in
[
1
]
we have

\tilde{v}(t,x)\geq((1-\gamma)t)^{\gamma^{\prime}},\forall(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
t_{0}\geq 0
was arbitrary, we get

\displaystyle v(t,x)\geq((1-\gamma)t)^{\gamma^{\prime}},\forall(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

and we are done.
∎

Lemma 4.8

(Comparison lemma)
.

Let

v,u\in L_{loc}^{\infty}\left((0,\infty),L^{1,w}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\cap C((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

be non-negative functions such that, for all
t>0
,

\begin{split}u(t)\geq S_{t}\nu+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}u^{\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\\
v(t)\leq S_{t}\mu+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}v^{\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Here
\mu
,
\nu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are such that

\nu(f)\geq\mu(f),\forall f\in C^{+}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then

u(t,x)\geq v(t,x),\>\>\textnormal{for all}\>(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Define

g(t)\equiv v(t)-u(t).

We will now prove that
g_{+}(t)\equiv\max(g(t),0)=0
.

Fix arbitrary
T>0
. We use the condition
\nu\geq\mu
and an elementary inequality
(a^{\gamma}-b^{\gamma})\leq((a-b)_{+})^{\gamma}
to get

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle\leq S_{t}(\mu-\nu)+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}(v^{\gamma}(s)-u^{\gamma}(s))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}(v^{\gamma}(s)-u^{\gamma}(s))_{+}\mathrm{d}s

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}\left(\left(g_{+}(s)\right)^{\gamma}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

From this point the proof follows the proof of Theorem 2.8 in
[
1
]

while using Lemma
4.7
whenever necessary. We left the details to the reader.
∎

The uniqueness for (
2.2
) follows easily from the above comparison Lemma
4.8
.

Proposition 4.9

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There is at most one solution to
(
2.2
) which belongs to
L_{loc}^{\infty}\left([0,\infty),L^{1,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\cap C^{+}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Suppose there exist two functions
v,u\in L_{loc}^{\infty}\left([0,\infty),L^{1,w}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)\cap C^{+}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
that solve equation (
2.2
) for the same initial measure
\mu
. Then by Lemma
4.8
,
v(t,x)\geq u(t,x)
and
u(t,x)\geq v(t,x)
for any
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and thus
u=v
and we are done.
∎

4.4.
Continuous dependence of solutions on initial data

In the previous sections we proved the existence and uniqueness of solutions to equation (
2.2
), or looking from different perspective we proved for every
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
the existence of the mapping

v(t,x,\cdot):{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}_{++}.

Here
v(t,x,\mu)
is a solution to equation (
2.2
) with initial datum
\mu
.

In this section we will prove the continuity of this mapping.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the mapping

v(t,x,\cdot):{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{++}

is concave, i.e.

\begin{split}v(t,x,\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\nu)\geq&\lambda v(t,x,\mu)+(1-\lambda)v(t,x,\nu),\\
&\forall\lambda\in(0,1),\>\>\forall(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}

Proof.

Since the function
x\to x^{\gamma}
is concave, for any positive
a,b
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

(4.23)

\lambda a^{\gamma}+(1-\lambda)b^{\gamma}\leq(\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b)^{\gamma}.

Let us fix an arbitrary
\lambda\in(0,1)
and define
u(t,z,\mu,\nu,\lambda)
as follows

u(t,x,\mu,\nu,\lambda)\triangleq\lambda v(t,\mu)+(1-\lambda)v(t,\nu).

Then we have

\begin{split}u(t,x,\mu,\nu,\lambda)&=\lambda v(t,x,\mu)+(1-\lambda)v(t,x,\nu)\\
&=\big(S_{t}\sigma\big)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(S_{t-s}\Big(\lambda v^{\gamma}(s,\mu)+(1-\lambda)v^{\gamma}(s,\nu)\Big)\Big)(x)\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\big(S_{t}\sigma\big)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(S_{t-s}\Big(\lambda v(s,\mu)+(1-\lambda)v(s,\nu)\Big)^{\gamma}\Big)(x)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\big(S_{t}\sigma\big)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(S_{t-s}(u^{\gamma}(s,\mu,nu,\lambda)\Big)(x)\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

where the above inequality follows from (
4.23
), and we set

\sigma=\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\nu
. Hence we obtained

(4.24)

u(t,x)\leq\left(S_{t}\sigma\right)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{t-s}u^{\gamma}(s)\right)(x)\mathrm{d}s.

We now recall that by definition

(4.25)

v(t,x,\sigma)=\left(S_{t}\sigma\right)(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{t-s}v(s,\sigma)^{\gamma}\right)(x)\mathrm{d}s.

and it is left to use comparison Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Before we prove continuity let us recall that the space of signed finite measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
M_{F,S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is a topological vector space with topology of weak convergence.

Proposition 4.11
.

For any fixed
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the mapping

v(t,x,\cdot):{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{++}

is continuous.

Remark 4.12
.

It follows from the above proposition that the weak convergence of initial measures implies pointwise convergence of solutions to equation (
2.2
).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
for any
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the mapping
\mu\mapsto v(t,x,\cdot)
is concave, and
v(t,x,\mu)\geq 0
for any
\mu\in{\overset{\circ}{M}}_{F}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
in
[
4
]
,
mapping
\mu\mapsto v(t,x,\mu)
is continuous.
∎

Now we are able to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

The statement of the theorem follows from Propositions
4.2
,
4.5
,
4.9
, Corollary
4.3
and Proposition
4.11
.
∎

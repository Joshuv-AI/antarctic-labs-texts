On the modular Plesken Lie algebra
John Cullinan

Abstract
. Let G \mathrm{G} be a finite group. The Plesken Lie algebra ℒ ⁡ [ G ] \mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}] is a subalgebra of the complex group algebra 𝐂 ⁡ [ G ] \mathbf{C}[\mathrm{G}] and admits a direct-sum decomposition into simple Lie algebras based on the ordinary character theory of G \mathrm{G} . In this paper we review the known results on ℒ ⁡ [ G ] \mathcal{L}[G] and related Lie algebras, as well as introduce a conjecture on a characteristic p p analog ℒ p ​ [ G ] \mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}] , with a focus on when p p divides the order of G \mathrm{G} .



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to review known results on a certain Lie algebra attached to a finite group using ordinary representation theory, as well as to suggest new directions of research that incorporate modular representations. Let us begin with some definitions.

Let
k
be a field,
\mathrm{G}
a finite group, and
k[\mathrm{G}]
the group algebra of
\mathrm{G}
over
k
. The alternating bilinear map
[~,~]:k[\mathrm{G}]\times k[\mathrm{G}]\to k[\mathrm{G}]
defined by

[x,y]=xy-yx

endows
k[\mathrm{G}]
with the structure of a Lie algebra. For each
g\in\mathrm{G}
, define the element
\widehat{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm\tiny{def}}}}{{=}}g-g^{-1}\in k[\mathrm{G}]
. Following
[
3
]
, the
Plesken Lie algebra

\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
is the Lie subalgebra of
k[\mathrm{G}]
linearly spanned over
k
by the
\widehat{g}
.

This raises a very natural question:
what is the isomorphism type of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
?
In particular, we can ask whether there is a natural relation between the Lie algebra structure of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
and the group structure of
\mathrm{G}
. Since
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
might not be a simple Lie algebra, we state our main question more formally as follows.

Question 1
.

What are the isomorphism types (as Lie algebras over
k
) of the simple composition factors of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
?

A natural starting point is when
k=\mathbf{C}
, since there is a well-understood classical theory of complex simple Lie algebras. Indeed, in
[
3
]
the authors show that when
k=\mathbf{C}
, the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
is semisimple (owing to the fact that
\mathbf{C}[\mathrm{G}]
is a direct sum of matrix algebras) and admits a direct sum decomposition into Lie subalgebras according to the degrees and Frobenius-Schur indicators of the complex irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}
. We review their main theorem and give an illustrative example in Section
3
below.

Since the structure of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
is known when
k=\mathbf{C}
, we switch perspective and ask what happens for other fields
k
, in particular when the characteristic of
k
divides the order of
\mathrm{G}
. Here, there appear to be interesting connections with the modular representation theory of
\mathrm{G}
which have yet to be studied. After reviewing the current literature below, we propose new questions and conjectures for further research based on extensive calculation.

2.
History of the Problem

Before working with
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
directly, let us first consider
k[\mathrm{G}]
as a Lie algebra. If
k=\mathbf{C}
, then it is well known that
\mathbf{C}[\mathrm{G}]
decomposes as a direct sum of matrix algebras, which coincides with the decomposition into Lie algebras. Suppose now that the characteristic of
k
divides the order of
\mathrm{G}
(so that we are in the
modular
setting). Then Passi, Passman, and Sehgal determined in
[
8
]
the exact conditions under which
k[G]
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, and under which it is a solvable Lie algebra; they showed that the nilpotency/solvability can be determined purely from the group-theoretic properties of
\mathrm{G}
, foreshadowing the potential for deep connections between the group theory of
\mathrm{G}
and the Lie algebra theory of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
.

Roughly a decade later, Smirnov and Zalesskii in
[
10
]
systematically studied the Lie-algebraic properties of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
; to the best of our knowledge this is the first work that concerns
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
explicitly. In that paper the authors show (among other things) that

(1)

if
k
has characteristic 0, then
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
is solvable implies
\mathrm{G}
is solvable, and

(2)

if
k
has characteristic different than 2, then
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
is nilpotent implies
\mathrm{G}^{2}
(the subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
generated by squares) is nilpotent.

We remark that item (a) does not generalize to positive characteristic (as they point out in
[
10
, §5.1]
). Additionally, item (b) appears to be the first structure theorem relating the Lie-algebraic properties of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
to the group theory of
\mathrm{G}
in the modular setting (excluding characteristic 2).

Returning to the case
k=\mathbf{C}
, in 2008 Cohen and Taylor determined the direct-sum decomposition of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
into complex simple Lie algebras of classical type
[
3
]
(we review the exact decomposition in Section
3
). Subsequently, in
[
7
]
, Marin considered a more general setup, which is also briefly addressed in
[
10
]
, as follows. Let
\alpha:\mathrm{G}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}
be a multiplicative character, and let
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}[\mathrm{G}]
denote the span of the elements
g-\alpha(g)g^{-1}
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}[\mathrm{G}]
is also a Lie subalgebra of
k[\mathrm{G}]
when endowed with the bracket operation. When
\alpha=\mathbf{1}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{1}}[\mathrm{G}]=\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
. The main result of
[
7
]
is the determination of
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}[\mathrm{G}]
as a direct sum of complex, simple Lie algebras of classical type and contextualizes the study of the Plesken algebra within the field of harmonic analysis. More recently, Chaudhuri determined in 2020 in
[
2
]
the direct-sum decomposition of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
when
k
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbf{Q}
. And in 2023 Arjun and Romeo studied the Lie algebra representation theory of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
over
\mathbf{C}

[
1
]
.

It is evident that there is an established and significant body of work surrounding the Plesken Lie algebra in characteristic 0, relating the ordinary representation theory and group structure of
\mathrm{G}
to the classical Lie algebra structure of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
. Before addressing the modular theory in Section
4
, we review the main theorem of
[
3
]
along with some new generalizations. For the remainder of the paper we use
Atlas

[
4
]
notation for finite simple groups.

4.
The Modular Plesken Lie Algebra

Let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
and let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. Then
k[\mathrm{G}]
is no longer semisimple, but decomposes as a direct sum of blocks. The goal of this section is to introduce a conjecture on the structure of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
that takes into account the modular representation theory of
\mathrm{G}
in a similar way to the ordinary case. Observe that the dimension of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
is independent of whether
p
is ordinary or modular.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{s}
denote the Brauer characters of
\mathrm{G}
, where
s
is the number of
p
-regular classes of
\mathrm{G}
. As in the ordinary case, the set of Brauer characters is partitioned by Frobenius-Schur indicator into subsets
\mathcal{X}_{1}
,
\mathcal{X}_{-1}
, and
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. The Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
will generally not decompose into a direct sum of simple Lie algebras, but it does possess a composition series nonetheless. Let us now set some notation in order to state a conjecture on the structure of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
.

Write
k[\mathrm{G}]=b_{1}\oplus b_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus b_{r}
as the block decomposition of
k[\mathrm{G}]
. Then each
b_{j}
, being a two-sided ideal of
k[\mathrm{G}]
, assumes a Lie algebra structure over
k
as well. Let us make some further remarks.

(1)

We write
\mathsf{Ab}(n)
for an abelian Lie algebra of dimension
n
.

(2)

If
p|(n+1)
, then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is not simple over
k
, since the scalar matrices have trace 0. We write
\mathfrak{PA}_{n}
for the codimension-1 simple factor of
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
in this case.

(3)

In addition to the classical algebras, there exist exceptional simple Lie algebras in positive characteristic. When
p\geq 5
, the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
have been fully classified. Any such algebra is either of classical or Cartan type (Witt; Special; Hamiltonian; Contact) for
p\geq 7
or, additionally, of Melikian type if
p=5

.

We are now ready to introduce our conjecture on the structure of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
in the modular setting. The reader will notice that the degrees and indicators of the Brauer characters determine which classical Lie algebras appear in the decomposition, just as the the degrees and indicators of the complex characters did in the complex setting. The main difference is that the degrees of the Brauer characters need not be the same as the complex characters and there are fewer Brauer characters than complex. The remaining composition factors are all abelian, which is different from the complex setting.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a finite group and
p
a modular prime for
\mathrm{G}
. With all notation as above, we have

(1)

\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
admits a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathfrak{a}_{j},

where the
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
denote the projection of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
onto the block
b_{j}
. The Lie algebras
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
are not necessarily simple.

(2)

The composition factors of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
are either abelian or of classical type.

(3)

(a)

for each
\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, there is a composition factor isomorphic to
\mathfrak{o}_{\phi(1)}
, and

(b)

for each
\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{-1}
, there is a composition factor isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sp}_{\phi(1)}
, and

(c)

for each pair
(\phi,\phi^{\prime})
of conjugate Brauer characters in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, there is one composition factor isomorphic to
\mathfrak{gl}_{\phi(1)}
. The algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{\phi(1)}
has a codimension-1 simple factor isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{\phi(1)}
, unless
p|\phi(1)
. In that case,
\mathfrak{gl}_{\phi(1)}
has a codimension-2 simple factor of type
\mathfrak{PA}_{\phi(1)-1}
.

(4)

A block of
k[\mathrm{G}]
of defect 0 gives rise to direct summand of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
of classical type.

Remark 1
.

The degrees of the Brauer characters do not necessarily agree with those of the ordinary characters, which can result in a very different overall Lie algebra structure of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
versus
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
, though the classical factors of
\mathcal{L}[\mathrm{G}]
as described in items (3a), (3b), (3c) of Conjecture
1
mimic the decomposition of (
2
).

We conclude the paper with further observations and questions for future researchers.

(1)

Observe that certain ordinary representations remain irreducible upon reduction modulo
p
. These include the Steinberg representation, as well as any representation whose degree is a multiple of the largest power of
p
dividing
|G|
. These representations occur in blocks of defect 0. In the case of the Steinberg representation, the Frobenius-Schur indicator is
+1
, hence will give rise to a Lie algebra of type
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
.

(2)

In addition to a proof of Conjecture
1
, it would be interesting to determine the precise number and dimensions of the abelian composition factors of
\mathcal{L}_{p}[\mathrm{G}]
, as well as investigating any relation between the abelian factors and the defect groups of the blocks.

FG := GroupAlgebra(F, G);

L1,h:=Algebra(FG);

L:=[];

for g in G do

if Order(g) ne 2 and Order(g) ne 1 and Inverse(g) notin L then

Append(~L,g);

end if;

end for;

LL:=[];

for g in L do

Append((~LL,FG!g-FG!Inverse(g) ));

end for;

LLL:=[];

for g in LL do

Append((~LLL, (g@h)@f ));

end for;

P:=sub<FFG|LLL>;

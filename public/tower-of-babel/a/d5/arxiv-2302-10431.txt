A note on the partition bound for one-way classical communication complexity
Srinivasan Arunachalam, João F. Doriguello, Rahul Jain

Abstract
We present a linear program for the one-way version of the partition bound (denoted 𝗉𝗋𝗍 ε 1 ​ ( f ) \mathsf{prt}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f) ). We show that it characterizes one-way randomized communication complexity 𝖱 ε 1 ​ ( f ) \mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f) with shared randomness of every partial function f : 𝒳 × 𝒴 → 𝒵 f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z} , i.e., for δ , ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) \delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1/2) , 𝖱 ε 1 ​ ( f ) ≥ log ⁡ 𝗉𝗋𝗍 ε 1 ​ ( f ) \mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)\geq\log\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f) and 𝖱 ε + δ 1 ​ ( f ) ≤ log ⁡ 𝗉𝗋𝗍 ε 1 ​ ( f ) + log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) \mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon+\delta}^{1}(f)\leq\log\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)+\log\log(1/\delta) . This improves upon the characterization of 𝖱 ε 1 ​ ( f ) \mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f) in terms of the rectangle bound (due to Jain and Klauck, 2010) by reducing the additive O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) ) O(\log(1/\delta)) -term to log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) \log\log(1/\delta) .



1
Introduction

The two-party communication model was introduced by Yao in 1979
[
Yao79
]
and offers a simple, yet rich model useful in numerous areas in theoretical computer science. One of the main tasks in communication complexity is to prove non-trivial lower bounds, either general or to specific problems. To this end, several general methods were proposed in the settings of randomized and quantum communication, the first ones being the fooling set
[
Yao79
]
, (approximate) rank
[
MS82
,
Kra96
,
BdeW01
]
, discrepancy
[
CG88
,
BNS89
]
, and rectangle/corruption
[
Yao83
,
BFS86
,
Raz92
,
KN97
,
Kla03
]
bounds. Since then several new and stronger lower bounds were discovered. Jain, Klauck, and Nayak
[
JKN08
]
proposed the subdistribution bound, a relaxation of the rectangle/corruption bounds. Klauck
[
Kla07
]
introduced the smooth-discrepancy lower bound, while Linial and Shraibman
[
LS07
]
introduced the factorization norm (
\gamma_{2}
) lower bound, which also holds for quantum protocols. Sherstov
[
She08
]
later showed that both bounds coincide. All these bounds were later subsumed by the partition bound from Jain and Klauck
[
JK10
]
, who also introduced relaxed versions of their partition bound named smooth-rectangle and smooth-discrepancy bounds. This left the field with one unified general lower bound method for randomized communication complexity. Laplante, Lerays, and Roland
[
LLR12
]
introduced the quantum partition bound (a.k.a. the efficiency bound). The efficiency bound subsumes the factorization norm when shared entanglement is allowed, and coincides with the partition bound when shared randomness is allowed.

In this note we present a linear program for the one-way version of the partition bound and show that it lower bounds the one-way randomized communication complexity and is tight up to an additive
\log\log
term. More specifically, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
and a partial function
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
, let
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
be the optimal value of the linear program defining our one-way partition bound and let
\mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
be the one-way randomized communication complexity with shared randomness for
f
with worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
. Then our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

For
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)\geq\log\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
and
\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon+\delta}(f)\leq\log\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)+\log\log(1/\delta)
.

It is known that the rectangle bound is tight for one-way randomized communication complexity up to an additive
O(\log(1/\delta))
-term
[
JKN08
]
. Our result shows that the one-way partition bound is tight up to an additive
\log\log(1/\delta)
-term. We mention that
[
LLR12
]
had also introduced a one-way version of their efficiency bound, but through a different linear program than ours.

2
The one-way partition bound

Consider a partial function
f:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
and let
f^{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
be the set where
f(\cdot)
is defined. Define
f^{-1}(z):=\{(x,y)\in f^{-1}:f(x,y)=z\}
. In a two-party one-way communication protocol
\mathcal{P}
, Alice is given
x\in\mathcal{X}
and sends a message to Bob with input
y\in\mathcal{Y}
. Upon receiving Alice’s message, Bob produces the output of the protocol
\mathcal{P}(x,y)
. We shall always assume shared randomness between the two players. Let
\operatorname{err}_{x,y}(\mathcal{P},f):=\Pr[\mathcal{P}(x,y)\neq f(x,y)]
be the error of the protocol on input
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
and
\operatorname{err}(\mathcal{P},f):=\max_{(x,y)\in f^{-1}}\{\operatorname{err}_{x,y}(\mathcal{P},f)\}
be the error with worst-case
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
. Let
\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
be the classical one-way communication complexity with worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
,
\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f):=\min_{\mathcal{P}}\{\text{bits communicated by}~\mathcal{P}:\operatorname{err}(\mathcal{P},f)\leq\varepsilon\}
.
For ease of notation we do not include the superscript “pub” to signal that shared randomness is allowed.

In a one-way zero-communication protocol with abort,
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}\cup\{\perp\}
, Alice and Bob are given
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
, respectively. Using shared randomness
R\in\{0,1\}^{\ast}
, but without communicating to each other, Alice outputs
z_{a}\in\{\vdash,\perp\}
and Bob outputs
z_{b}\in\mathcal{Z}
. If
z_{a}=\perp
, the protocol’s output, denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)
, is
\perp
, otherwise it is
z_{b}
. Let
\operatorname{err}^{\perp}_{x,y}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},f):=\Pr_{R}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq f(x,y)|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]
be the error of the protocol on input
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
given it does not abort and
\operatorname{err}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},f):=\max_{(x,y)\in f^{-1}}\{\operatorname{err}^{\perp}_{x,y}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},f)\}
be the error with worst-case
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
. Let
\mathsf{eff}^{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},(x,y)):=\Pr_{R}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]
be the non-abort probability of
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
. We require such probability
\mathsf{eff}^{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},(x,y))
to be the same for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, denoted
\mathsf{eff}^{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp})
. Define
\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f):=\max_{\mathcal{P}^{\perp}}\{\mathsf{eff}^{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp}):\operatorname{err}(\mathcal{P}^{\perp},f)\leq\varepsilon\}
, the one-way zero-communication
efficiency
with worst-case error at most
\varepsilon

[
LLR12
]
.

We now present our linear program for the one-way partition bound.

Definition 2

(One-way partition bound)
.

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, the one-way
\varepsilon
-partition bound of
f
, denoted by
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
, is the optimal value of the following linear program.

Primal

\displaystyle\min\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w_{A},

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathcal{X}:\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A}=1,

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}:\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}w_{A,y,z}=w_{A},

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in f^{-1}:\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A,y,f(x,y)}\geq 1-\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X},(y,z)\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}:w_{A}\geq 0,w_{A,y,z}\geq 0.

Dual

\displaystyle\max\sum_{(x,y)\in f^{-1}}(1-\varepsilon)\mu_{x,y}-\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\lambda_{x},

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X},(y,z)\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}:\sum_{x\in A:(x,y)\in f^{-1}(z)}\mu_{x,y}\leq\lambda_{A,y},

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\lambda_{A,y}\leq 1+\sum_{x\in A}\lambda_{x},

\displaystyle\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X},(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}:\mu_{x,y}\geq 0,\lambda_{A,y}\geq 0,\lambda_{x}\in\mathbb{R}.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we start by showing that optimal one-way zero-communication protocols are equivalent to optimal solutions to our partition-bound linear program.

Lemma 3
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)=1/\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)\geq 1/\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
. Consider an optimal solution for the primal of
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
with weights
w_{A}
and
w_{A,y,z}
for all
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
,
(y,z)\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
. We define a one-way zero-communication protocol as follows: using public coins, Alice chooses
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with probability
w_{A}/\sum_{A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w_{A^{\prime}}
and does not abort if and only if
x\in A
. Bob, on the other hand, outputs
z_{b}=z
with probability
w_{A,y,z}/w_{A}
. Therefore, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the probability that Alice does not abort is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pr}[x\in A]=\frac{\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A}}{\sum_{A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w_{A^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)},

using that
\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A}=1
. Moreover, for all
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pr}[z_{b}=f(x,y)|z_{a}\neq\perp]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}}\operatorname{Pr}[\text{Alice chooses}~A]\operatorname{Pr}[z_{b}=f(x,y),z_{a}\neq\perp|\text{Alice chooses}~A]}{\operatorname{Pr}[z_{a}\neq\perp]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}\operatorname{Pr}[\text{Alice chooses}~A]\operatorname{Pr}[z_{b}=f(x,y)|\text{Alice chooses}~A]}{\operatorname{Pr}[z_{a}\neq\perp]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}\frac{w_{A}}{\sum_{A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w_{A^{\prime}}}\frac{w_{A,y,f(x,y)}}{w_{A}}}{\frac{1}{\sum_{A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w_{A^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A,y,f(x,y)}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Hence, our one-way zero-communication protocol has non-abort probability
1/\mathsf{prt}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
and worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
. Thus
\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)\geq 1/\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
.

On the other direction, we now prove that
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)\leq 1/\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
. Consider a one-way zero-communication protocol
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
with public randomness
R
, worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
, and non-abort probability
\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
. Given a public coin
r
with probability
p(r)
, let
\mathcal{X}_{r}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}|z_{a}(x,r)\neq\perp\}
be the set of inputs for which Alice does not abort. Then, for
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
(y,z)\in\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
, define the weights

\displaystyle w^{\prime}_{A}:=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}\sum_{r:A=\mathcal{X}_{r}}p(r),\qquad w^{\prime}_{A,y,z}:=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}\sum_{r:A=\mathcal{X}_{r},z_{b}(y,r)=z}p(r).

Clearly,

\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:\quad\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w_{A}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}\sum_{r:z_{a}(x,r)\neq\perp}p(r)=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}\operatorname{Pr}_{R}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]=1.

Also, regardless of Alice’s output, Bob must output something with probability
1
, meaning that

\forall A\subseteq\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}:\quad\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}w^{\prime}_{A,y,z}=w^{\prime}_{A}.

Finally, since for all
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
Bob outputs the correct answer with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
given that Alice does not abort,

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in f^{-1}:\quad 1-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Pr}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)=f(x,y)|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Pr}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)=f(x,y),\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]}{\operatorname{Pr}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]}=\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}:x\in A}w^{\prime}_{A,y,f(x,y)}.

Thus, the weights
w^{\prime}_{A}
and
w^{\prime}_{A,y,z}
are a feasible solution for
\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)
and therefore

\mathsf{prt}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)\leq\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal{X}}w^{\prime}_{A}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}\sum_{r}p(r)=\frac{1}{\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)}.\qed

In the next two lemmas, we relate one-way randomized and one-way zero-communication protocols, i.e., we show that an optimal one-way randomized communication protocol can simulate a one-way zero-communication protocol and vice-versa. This was already observed in
[
LLR12
]
.

Lemma 4
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
\mathsf{R}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f)\geq\log(1/\mathsf{eff}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f))
.

Proof.

Consider a one-way randomized communication protocol
\mathcal{P}
with public coin
R
, worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
, and communication complexity
c:=\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
. Consider the following one-way zero-communication protocol
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
. Using their public coin, Alice and Bob sample
m
uniformly from
\{0,1\}^{c}
and
r
from the distribution
R
. Alice checks if her transcript
M(x,r)
from protocol
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to input
x\in\mathcal{X}
and public coin
r
equals
m
. If
M(x,r)\neq m
, she aborts. Bob, on the other hand, outputs
\mathcal{P}(m,r,y)
, which is his output in protocol
\mathcal{P}
with public coin
r
, Alice’s message
m
, and his input
y
. It is clear that, for all
(x,y)\in f^{-1}
,

\Pr[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)=f(x,y)|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]=\Pr[\mathcal{P}(m,r,y)=f(x,y)|M(x,r)=m]\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Finally, since
\operatorname{Pr}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]=2^{-c}
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, we conclude that

\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)\geq 2^{-c}\implies\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)\geq\log(1/\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)).\qed

Lemma 5
.

For all
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
\mathsf{R}^{1}_{\varepsilon+\delta}(f)\leq\log(1/\mathsf{eff}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f))+\log\log(1/\delta)
.

Proof.

Consider a one-way zero-communication protocol
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
with public coin
R
, worst-case error at most
\varepsilon
, and efficiency
\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
. Consider the following one-way randomized communication protocol
\mathcal{P}
: Alice and Bob use public coins to obtain a sequence
r_{1},\dots,r_{T}
of
T
random bit-strings for
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
. Alice looks at the minimum index
j\in[T]
such that, on
r_{j}
, she does not abort in
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
, and sends
j
to Bob, who then outputs
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)
using
r_{j}
. Since
\operatorname{Pr}_{R}[\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(x,y)\neq\perp]=\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, by choosing
T=\log(1/\delta)/\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f)
we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pr}[\text{Alice does not get a good}~j\in[T]]=(1-\mathsf{eff}^{1}_{\varepsilon}(f))^{T}\leq e^{-\log(1/\delta)}\leq\delta.

If Alice does not obtain a good index
j\in[T]
, Bob outputs a random
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Hence the error probability of the protocol is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pr}[\mathcal{P}(x,y)\neq f(x,y)]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Pr}[\text{good}~j]\operatorname{Pr}[z_{b}\neq f(x,y)|\text{good}~j]+\operatorname{Pr}[\text{no good}~j]\operatorname{Pr}[z_{b}\neq f(x,y)|\text{no good}~j]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\operatorname{Pr}[\text{no good}~j]\leq\varepsilon+\delta.

The total communication from Alice to Bob is at most
\log{T}=\log(1/\mathsf{eff}_{\varepsilon}^{1}(f))+\log\log(1/\delta)
.
∎

Our main result, Theorem
1
, is thus a straightforward corollary from Lemmas
3
,
4
and
5
.

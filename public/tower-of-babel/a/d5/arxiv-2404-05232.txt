Invariant stability conditions of Local ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} (after Del Monte-Longhi)
Yirui Xiong

Abstract
. Let X X be the total space of the canonical bundle of ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} , we study an invariant subspace of stability conditions on X X under an autoequivalence of D b ​ ( X ) D^{b}(X) . We describe the complete set of stable objects with respect to the invariant stability conditions and characterize the space of invariant stability conditions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Inspired by the Douglas’ work on
\Pi
-stability for D-branes, Bridgeland introduced the notion of the stability condition on a triangulated category in
[
12
]
. It was shown in
[
12
]
that to any triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
, one can associate a complex manifold
\Stab(\mathcal{D})
which parameterises stability conditions on
\mathcal{D}
. Recall a stability condition on
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
called the heart, and
Z
is a group homomorphism called the central charge from the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
to
\mathbb{C}
which satisfies the Harder-Narasimhan property
[
12
]
(see Definition
2.15
). Bridgeland showed that if
\Stab(\mathcal{D})
is nonempty, then the forgetfull map
\Stab(\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\Hom(K_{0}(\mathcal{D}),\mathbb{C})
which sends
(Z,\mathcal{P})
to
Z
is a local homeomorphism. Given the triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
, one can ask the following three questions:

(1)

Can we find a stability condition on
\mathcal{D}
?

(2)

What is
\Stab(\mathcal{D})
as a complex manifold?

(3)

Given a stability condition
\sigma
, can we count the set of (semi)stable objects in
\mathcal{D}
for
\sigma
?

So far much progress towards the first two questions has been made for the derived categories of projective and quasi-projective (local) varieties
[
13
,
11
,
14
,
33
,
25
,
1
]
.

The answer to the final question is usually very hard for both projective and quasi-projective varieties. When
\mathcal{D}
is a Calabi-Yau category of dimension
3
, it is related to the Donaldson-Thomas invariants
[
27
,
31
]
.

We study the space of stability conditions for local
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in this paper, i.e., the total space
X
of canonical bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The work is a mathematical interpretation of the work of Del Monte-Longhi
[
34
]
. In their paper, the physicists found that there were surprisingly complete answers to all questions in the above when we restrict to an invariant subspace of stability conditions under an autoequivalence of
D^{b}(X)
.

S
. Define the full subcategories

S^{\perp}:=\{E\in\mathcal{A}\ |\ \Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(S,E)=0\},\quad{}^{\perp}S:=\{E\in\mathcal{A}\ |\ \Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(E,S)=0\}.

Then we can either view
(\langle S\rangle,S^{\perp})
or
(^{\perp}S,\langle S\rangle)
as a torsion pair. Then we can define new tilted hearts

(2.2)

L_{S}\mathcal{A}:=\langle S[1],^{\perp}S\rangle,\quad R_{S}\mathcal{A}:=\langle S^{\perp},S[-1]\rangle,

which we refer to as the left and right simple tilts of the heart
\mathcal{A}
at the simple object
S
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is easy to see that
S[-1]
is a simple object of
R_{S}\mathcal{A}
and that if the category is of finite-length, then
L_{S[-1]}R_{S}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}
. Similarly, if
L_{S}\mathcal{A}
is of finite-length then
R_{S[1]}L_{S}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}
.

The following lemmas will be useful.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(D^{\leq 0},D^{\geq 0})
and
(\widetilde{D}^{\leq 0},\widetilde{D}^{\geq 0})
be two bounded t-structures of
\mathcal{D}
, and we denote by
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
their hearts respectively. If
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
then
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. Since for any object
F\in D^{\leq 0}
,
F
has a finite filtration by objects in
\mathcal{A}[k_{i}]\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}[k_{i}]
for
k_{i}\geq 0
by Lemma
2.1
, so we have

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F,E[-1])=0.

Therefore
E[-1]\in D^{>0}
.

Similarly for any object
G\in D^{>0}
,

has a finite filtration by objects in
\mathcal{A}[k_{i}]\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}[k_{i}]
for
k_{i}<0
, therefore

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(E,G)=0.

Therefore

. So we have
E\in D^{\leq 0}\cap D^{>0}[1]=\mathcal{A}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Take an autoequivalence
\Phi\in\Aut(\mathcal{D})
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
be a heart of some bounded t-structure and of finite-length,
S\in\mathcal{A}
be a simple object. Then we have

\Phi(L_{S}\mathcal{A})=L_{\Phi(S)}\Phi(\mathcal{A}),\quad\Phi(R_{S}\mathcal{A})=R_{\Phi(S)}\Phi(\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

By the definition of simple tilts (
2.2
), we have
R_{\Phi(S)}\Phi(\mathcal{A})=\left\langle\left(\Phi(S)\right)^{\perp},\Phi(S)[-1]\right\rangle
. It is easy to check
\Phi(S^{\perp})=(\Phi(S))^{\perp}
, therefore
R_{\Phi(S)}\Phi(\mathcal{A})\subset\Phi(R_{S}\mathcal{A})
by definition. By Lemma
2.5
we have
R_{\Phi(S)}\Phi(\mathcal{A})=\Phi(R_{S}\mathcal{A})
. The proof of the left tilt case is similar.
∎

A stability function on

(Stability condition)

Definition 2.17

A stability condition is a pair
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})
which consists of a slicing

The objects in
\mathcal{P}(\phi)

. Hence
W\in\mathcal{H}^{\reg}
, this shows that
\mathcal{H}^{\reg}
is open.

Now we fix a positive real number
\epsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{8}
and assume that
\epsilon<\sin(\pi\epsilon_{0})
. Given any
\sigma=(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{P})\in\bigl(\Stab(X)^{\Phi}\bigr)_{0}
with
\mathcal{Z}(\sigma)=Z
, we define the open neighborhood of
\sigma

C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)=\left\{\tau=(W,\mathcal{Q})\in\mathcal{Z}^{-1}(B_{\epsilon}(Z)):d(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})<1/2\right\},

where
d(-,-)
is defined in Definition
2.13
. By Lemma
2.18
, the map

(6.4)

\mathcal{Z}:C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)\rightarrow B_{\epsilon}(Z)

is injective. Let
W\in B_{\epsilon}(Z)
, then for any
E
stable for
\sigma
, by Corollary
6.5
, we have

\displaystyle|W(E)-Z(E)|<\sin(\pi\epsilon_{0})|Z(E)|.

Using the deformation result Theorem
2.22
, we conclude that there is a unique stabilty condition
\tau=(W,\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\in C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)
such that
\mathcal{Z}(\tau)=W
and
d(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})<\epsilon
. Thus the map (
6.4
) is a homeomorphism. For each
\sigma\in\mathcal{Z}^{-1}(Z)
, we prove
C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)
is mapped homeomorphically by
\mathcal{Z}
onto
B_{\epsilon}(Z)
exactly in the same way.

Finally we check that

(6.5)

\mathcal{Z}^{-1}\left(B_{\epsilon}(Z)\right)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{Z}^{-1}(Z)}C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)

is disjoint. Suppose there exists
\tau=(W,\mathcal{Q})\in C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)\bigcap C_{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\prime})
, where we denote by
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})
and
\sigma^{\prime}=(Z,\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
. Then

d(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\leq d(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})+d(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{P^{\prime}})<1.

Therefore by Lemma
2.18
again, we have
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, which means
C_{\epsilon}(\sigma)=C_{\epsilon}(\sigma^{\prime})
. We have finished the proof.
∎

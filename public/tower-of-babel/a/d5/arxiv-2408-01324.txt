On Watanabe’s theta graph diffeomorphism in the 4 4 –sphere
David T Gay

Abstract
Watanabe’s theta graph diffeomorphism, constructed using Watanabe’s clasper surgery construction [ Wat19 ] which turns trivalent graphs in 4 4 –manifolds into parameterized families of diffeomorphisms of 4 4 –manifolds, is a diffeomorphism of S 4 S^{4} representing a potentially nontrivial smooth mapping class of S 4 S^{4} . The “ ( 1 , 2 ) (1,2) –subgroup” of the smooth mapping class group of S 4 S^{4} is the subgroup represented by diffeomorphisms which are pseudoisotopic to the identity via a Cerf family with only index 1 1 and 2 2 critical points. This author and Hartman [ GH22 ] showed that this subgroup is either trivial or has order 2 2 and explicitly identified a diffeomorphism that would represent the nontrivial element if this subgroup is nontrivial. Here we show that the theta graph diffeomorphism is isotopic to this one possibly nontrivial element of the ( 1 , 2 ) (1,2) –subgroup. To prove this relation we develop a diagrammatic calculus for working in the smooth mapping class group of S 4 S^{4} .

This paper exhibits a relation in the smooth mapping class group of
S^{4}
, which we denote
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4})=\pi_{0}(\mathop{\rm Diff}\nolimits^{+}(S^{4}))
, where
\mathop{\rm Diff}\nolimits^{+}(S^{4})
is the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of
S^{4}
. In other words, we will exhibit a smooth isotopy from one diffeomorphism of
S^{4}
to another. Part of the point is that the two diffeomorphisms involved are each of independent interest and arise in different contexts and thus exhibiting this relation improves our understanding of both diffeomorphisms. Another point is to illustrate new techniques for understanding relations in the smooth mapping class groups of
4
–manifolds which have the potential to be useful in other contexts.

It is important to emphasize that we do not at this time know whether or not
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4})
is trivial. Of course, if it is trivial then our relation is trivially true, but it is possible that either
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4})
is nontrivial or that to prove that
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4})
is trivial requires tools similar to those developed in this paper.

The relation we prove has the form

\mathop{\rm wat}\nolimits(\Theta)=\mathcal{H}(\alpha(1))\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4}),

so we now explain what the left and right hand sides of the equation mean. Briefly, for those for whom this is meaningful:

•

\Theta
is the theta graph embedded in

,

•

denotes Watanabe’s clasper surgery construction

]

•

is (the smooth isotopy class of) Watanabe’s theta graph diffeomorphism
[

]
,

•

is the first in a countable list

]

denotes the space of embeddings of

•

to have order at most

Given an oriented trivalent graph

Of course, if one shows that

is nontrivial, but if one shows that
\mathop{\rm wat}\nolimits(\Theta)

Instead of studying diffeomorphisms up to isotopy one may first study diffeomorphisms up to
pseudoisotopy
: A diffeomorphism
\phi:X\to X
is pseudoisotopic to the identity if there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi:[0,1]\times X\to[0,1]\times X
which is the identity on
\{0\}\times X
and
[0,1]\times\partial X
and equals
\phi
on
\{1\}\times X
. If
\Phi
is level preserving then
\Phi
is actually an isotopy. In fact every diffeomorphism of
S^{4}
is pseudoisotopic to the identity; see
[
Gay21
]
for a proof that this follows easily from the fact
[
KM63
]
that there are no exotic
5
–spheres.

A pseudoisotopy
\Phi:[0,1]\times X\to[0,1]\times X
gives rise to a pair of Morse functions
g_{0},g_{1}:[0,1]\times X\to[0,1]
, where
g_{0}
is simply projection to
[0,1]
and
g_{1}=g_{0}\circ\Phi
. Both
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
are Morse functions without critical points. Cerf’s key observation is that if these can be connected by a homotopy
g_{t}
such that each

does not have critical points then
\Phi
is isotopic to an isotopy, and thus that the problem of turning pseudosotopies into isotopies can be turned into the problem of simplifying
1
–parameter families, called “Cerf families”, of Morse functions (allowing births and deaths) between Morse functions without critical points. In our case, when the dimension of
X
is
4
and
X
is simply connected, one can always arrange that the critical points that do arise are only of index
2
and
3

[
HW73
]
, and in special cases one can arrange that they are only of index
1
and
2
. When the critical points are only of index
1
and
2
, the Cerf family is significantly easier to study.

The subgroup of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits(S^{4})
represented by diffeomorphisms which are pseudoisotopic to the identity via a pseudoisotopy with a Cerf family involving only critical points of index

and

is known as “the

this author exhibited a group homomorphism

and showed that the image is exactly

is the embedding of

as

for some

From the description of

which we will give in the next section, or from a general discussion of pseudosotopies and Watanabe’s constructions by Botvinnik and Watanabe

Theorem 1
.

Watanabe’s theta graph diffeomorphism

•

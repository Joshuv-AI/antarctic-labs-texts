Abstract
We extend known constructions of almost-Poisson brackets and their gauge transformations to nonholonomic systems whose Lagrangian is not mechanical but possesses a gyroscopic term linear in the velocities. The new feature introduced by such a term is that the Legendre transformation is an affine, instead of linear, bundle isomorphism between the tangent and cotangent bundles of the configuration space and some care is needed in the development of the geometric formalism. At the end of the day, the affine nature of the Legendre transform is reflected in the affine dependence of the brackets that we construct on the momentum variables. Our study is motivated by a wide class of nonholonomic systems involving rigid bodies with internal rotors which are of interest in control. Our construction provides a natural geometric framework for the (known) Hamiltonisations of the Suslov and Chaplygin sphere problems with a gyrostat.



Keywords:
non-holonomic systems, gyroscopic Lagrangian, almost-Poisson brackets, Hamiltonisation, gauge transformations, Suslov problem, Chaplygin sphere.

1.1
Motivation: nonholonomic Routh reduction

Consider a nonholonomic system of mechanical type on a configuration manifold
M=Q\times K
, which is the product
of the
n
-dimensional manifold
Q
and an abelian Lie group
K
. To fix ideas, suppose that
K
is the
l
-torus

\mathbb{T}^{l}=(S^{1})^{l}
. Let
(q^{j},\theta^{J})\in Q\times K
be coordinates on
M
and assume that
\theta^{J}
are cyclic variables of
a mechanical Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}:TM\to\mathbb{R}
, namely,

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(q,\dot{q},\dot{\theta})=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_{ij}(q)\dot{q}^{i}\dot{q}^{j}+\mathcal{G}_{iJ}(q)\dot{q}^{i}\dot{\theta}^{J}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_{IJ}(q)\dot{\theta}^{I}\dot{\theta}^{J}-\tilde{V}(q).

Suppose, in addition,
that the nonholonomic constraints are independent of
\theta
and
\dot{\theta}
and are given in terms of

q
and
\dot{q}
by (
1.2
).

The above setup is encountered, for instance,
when rigid bodies, containing
l
internal rotors, are subjected to nonholonomic constraints. In such
case, the coordinates
q^{j}
describe the position and orientation of the rigid bodies whereas the angles

\theta^{J}
determine the
orientation of the rotors (relative to axes that are fixed in the corresponding body frames).
The independence of the nonholonomic constraints (
1.2
) on
\theta
and
\dot{\theta}

corresponds to our assumption that the rotors lie inside of the bodies and their motion is not directly affected by the constraints.
Some concrete examples of this framework are the snakeboard, the unicycle with rotor and the Chaplygin sleigh with
rotor (see e.g.
[
9
]
). Other examples involve bodies containing a gyrostat which roll without slipping
on surfaces, or the gyrostat generalisations of the classical Suslov and
Veselova problems.

In accordance with the Lagrange-D’Alembert principle, the equations of motion of the system take the form

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{q}^{j}}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q^{j}}&=\lambda_{\alpha}A^{\alpha}_{j}(q),\qquad j=1,\dots,n,\\
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\theta}^{J}}\right)&=0,\qquad J=1,\dots,l,\end{split}

(1.3)

where the multipliers
\lambda_{\alpha}
are uniquely determined by the constraints (
1.2
). It is therefore clear that
the momenta

p_{J}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\theta}^{J}}=\mathcal{G}_{iJ}(q)\dot{q}^{i}+\mathcal{G}_{IJ}(q)\dot{\theta}^{I},\qquad J=1,\dots,l,

are first integrals. Let us consider the restriction of the system (
1.3
) to a level set of these integrals by
setting
p_{J}=\mu_{J}
for some fixed
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
. Along such level set we may express
2
2

2

As usual,
we denote by
\mathcal{G}^{IJ}
the entries of the inverse matrix of the block
\mathcal{G}_{IJ}
.

\dot{\theta}^{J}=\mathcal{G}^{IJ}(q)(\mu_{I}-\mathcal{G}_{iI}(q)\dot{q}^{i}),\qquad J=1,\dots,l,

(1.4)

and we may therefore eliminate
\dot{\theta}
from the first set of equations in (
1.3
) to obtain a reduced system
involving
q
and
\dot{q}
only. As is well known (see e.g.
[
58
]
), such elimination is conveniently done in terms of the
classical Routhian function
R^{\mu}=R^{\mu}(q,\dot{q})
defined by

R^{\mu}(q,\dot{q}):=\left[\mathcal{L}(q,\dot{q},\dot{\theta})-\mu_{J}\dot{\theta}^{J}\right]_{p_{J}=\mu_{J}},

with the convention that in the right hand side
\dot{\theta}
is written in terms of
(q,\dot{q})
as in (
1.4
).
The remarkable property of the Routhian is that

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial R^{\mu}}{\partial\dot{q}^{j}}\right)-\frac{\partial R^{\mu}}{\partial q^{j}}=\left[\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{q}^{j}}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q^{j}}\right]_{p_{J}=\mu_{J}},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

where we again think of
\dot{\theta}=\dot{\theta}(q,\dot{q})
in the right hand side.
Therefore, if we write
L(q,\dot{q}):=R^{\mu}(q,\dot{q})
, the reduced system can be written as

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}^{j}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{j}}&=\lambda_{\alpha}A^{\alpha}_{j}(q),\qquad j=1,\dots,n,\end{split}

(1.5)

together with the nonholonomic constraints (
1.2
).

Now note that (
1.5
) can be interpreted as the
equations of motion of a nonholonomic system on the configuration manifold
Q
, with constraints given by (
1.2
) and
with Lagrangian
L=R^{\mu}:TQ\to\mathbb{R}
. Our point is that
L
will
in general have a gyroscopic term
. Indeed, as shown in e.g. Section 8.9 in
[
58
]
,

L=R^{\mu}
is given by (
1.1
) with

\begin{split}\tilde{g}_{ij}=\mathcal{G}_{ij}-\mathcal{G}_{Ii}\mathcal{G}^{IJ}\mathcal{G}_{Jj},\qquad\tilde{\eta}_{j}=\mathcal{G}_{Ij}\mathcal{G}^{IJ}\mu_{J},\qquad V=\tilde{V}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}^{IJ}\mu_{I}\mu_{J}.\end{split}

(1.6)

The class of examples presented above is especially relevant in the context of control
theory since it is natural to add controls to the rotors to stabilise or accomplish desired motions of the bodies, see e.g.
[
65
,
19
,
41
,
60
,
42
]
and references therein.

1.2
The almost-Poisson formulation

We now present a brief coordinate description of how the equations of motion
of the mechanical system with configuration space
Q
, Lagrangian
L
given by (
1.1
),
and nonholonomic constraints (
1.2
),
can be formulated in terms of almost-Poisson brackets.

Our approach relies on the choice of vector frames which define quasi-velocities that are conveniently adapted to
the constraints. The usefulness of this procedure has been by now appreciated by different researchers in nonholonomic mechanics

[
52
,
35
,
27
,
12
,
49
,
29
,
20
]
.

Let
\{e_{a}\}
be a basis of sections of the distribution

\mathcal{D}
defined by the constraints (
1.2
), that is,
e_{a}\in{\mathfrak{X}}(Q)
with
e_{a}(q)\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
for all
q\in Q
, and let
\{e_{\alpha}\}
be a basis of
sections of
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
where

\perp
means perpendicular with respect to the kinetic energy metric.
3
3

3

The index convention followed throughout this work is that latin indices
a,b,c
run from
1
to
r=\mbox{rank}(\mathcal{D})

whereas greek indices
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
run from
r+1
to
n
. The indices
i,j,k
run from the whole range
1
to
n=\dim(Q)
.

Such bases may, of course, only exist locally. A tangent vector
v_{q}\in T_{q}Q
is written uniquely as a linear combination

v_{q}=v^{a}e_{a}(q)+v^{\alpha}e_{\alpha}(q),

and hence
(q^{i},v^{a},v^{\alpha})
can be used as coordinates for
TQ
. These coefficients
v^{a}
and
v^{\alpha}
are termed

quasi-velocities
.
The constraint space
\mathcal{D}
may then be interpreted as the submanifold of
TQ
determined
by the conditions
v^{\alpha}=0
and has
coordinates
(q^{i},v^{a})
. As will be explained in Subsection
2.2
, the equations of motion (
1.5
) are equivalent
to the following equations for the evolution of
(q^{i}(t),v^{a}(t))
, which do not involve the multipliers
\lambda_{\alpha}
:

\begin{split}\\
&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{a}}\right)-\rho^{i}_{a}(q)\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}+C_{ab}^{d}(q)v^{b}\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{d}}=-C_{ab}^{\alpha}(q)v^{b}\eta_{\alpha}(q),\qquad a=1,\dots,r,\\
&\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}=\rho^{i}_{a}(q)v^{a},\qquad i=1,\dots,n.\end{split}

(1.7)

In the above equations
L_{c}:=\left.L\right|_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is the
constrained Lagrangian
expressed as a function of the coordinates
(q^{i},v^{a})
for
\mathcal{D}
. The remaining coefficients appearing in the equations
are determined by the conditions

\begin{split}&e_{a}(q)=\rho^{i}_{a}(q)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}},\qquad a=1,\dots,r,\\
&e_{\alpha}(q)=\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(q)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}},\qquad\alpha=r+1,\dots,n,\\
&\eta_{\alpha}(q):=\rho^{i}_{\alpha}(q)\tilde{\eta}_{i}(q),\qquad\alpha=r+1,\dots,n,\\
&[e_{a},e_{b}](q)=C_{ab}^{j}(q)e_{j}(q)=C_{ab}^{d}(q)e_{d}(q)+C_{ab}^{\alpha}(q)e_{\alpha}(q),\qquad a,b=1,\dots,r,\end{split}

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the Lie bracket of vector fields. An important observation is
that the skew-symmetry of
[\cdot,\cdot]
implies
that
C_{ab}^{j}(q)
is antisymmetric with respect to the lower indices, i.e.
C_{ab}^{j}(q)=-C_{ba}^{j}(q)
.

To obtain an almost-Poisson formulation of the equations of motion (
1.7
) we consider the

constrained Legendre
transformation
and
constrained Hamiltonian

p_{a}:=\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{a}},\qquad H_{c}:=H_{c}(q^{i},p_{a})=p_{a}v^{a}-L_{c}.

As usual, the regularity of the Lagrangian
L
guarantees that the quasi-velocities
v^{a}
may be uniquely expressed
in terms of
(q^{i},p_{b})
which explains why we can use
(q^{i},p_{a})
as coordinates of our phase space and why
the constrained Hamiltonian
H_{c}
may be considered as a function of
(q^{i},p_{a})
.
The standard chain rule calculations of analytical mechanics show that

\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}=-\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial q^{i}},\qquad\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}}=v^{a},

and, therefore, the equations of motion (
1.7
) can be written as the following system of
equations for the evolution of
(q^{i}(t),p_{a}(t))

\begin{split}&\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}=\rho^{i}_{a}(q)\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,\\
&\frac{dp_{a}}{dt}=-\rho^{i}_{a}(q)\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}-C_{ab}^{d}(q)p_{d}\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}-C_{ab}^{\alpha}(q)\eta_{\alpha}(q)\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}},\qquad a=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

(1.8)

Given that the partial derivatives of the constrained Hamiltonian
H_{c}
appear linearly in the right hand side, we may
write (
1.8
) in matrix form:

4

4

When we use the abbreviation
(q,p)
one should keep in
mind that the index
i
of
q
runs from
1
to
n
whereas the range of the index
a
of
p
is only
1\leq a\leq r=\mbox{rank}(\mathcal{D})
.

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}q\\
\vskip-8.5359pt\\
p\end{pmatrix}=\Pi(q,p)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q}\\
\vskip-8.5359pt\\
\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Pi(q,p):=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n\times n}&\rho(q)\\
-\rho(q)^{T}&\mathcal{C}(q,p)+\mathcal{A}(q)\end{pmatrix},

(1.9)

where the block matrix
\rho(q)
is
n\times r
and has entries
\rho(q)_{ia}=\rho_{a}^{i}(q)
and
\mathcal{C}(q,p)
and

\mathcal{A}(q)
are antisymmetric
r\times r
block matrices with entries

\mathcal{C}(q,p)_{ab}=-C_{ab}^{d}(q)p_{d},\qquad\mathcal{A}(q)_{ab}=-C_{ab}^{\alpha}(q)\eta_{\alpha}(q).

Now define the
nonholonomic bracket
of functions
F_{1},F_{2}
of the variables
(q,p)
by

\{F_{1},F_{2}\}(q,p):=\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial q}^{T}&\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial p}^{T}\end{pmatrix}\Pi(q,p)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial q}\\
\vskip-8.5359pt\\
\frac{\partial F_{2}}{\partial p}\end{pmatrix}.

(1.10)

In particular, the brackets of the coordinate functions are

\{q^{i},q^{j}\}=0,\qquad\{q^{i},p_{a}\}=\rho^{i}_{a},\qquad\{p_{a},p_{b}\}=-C_{ab}^{d}p_{d}-C_{ab}^{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}.

It is clear that the nonholonomic bracket is affine in the variables
p_{a}
and satisfies Leibniz rule. It is also skew-symmetric since the
matrix
\Pi(q,p)
is antisymmetric.
We may therefore write (
1.8
) in

almost-Hamiltonian
form:

\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}=\{q^{i},H_{c}\},\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad\frac{dp_{a}}{dt}=\{p_{a},H_{c}\},\quad a=1,\dots,r.

An interesting observation, which is useful in the treatment of examples and we develop in this work,
is that, for the purposes of describing the dynamics, the block matrix
\Pi(q,p)

in (
1.9
) can be instead taken as

\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}(q,p):=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n\times n}&\rho(q)\\
-\rho(q)^{T}&\mathcal{C}(q,p)+\mathcal{A}(q)+\mathcal{B}(q,p)\end{pmatrix},

(1.11)

as long as the (antisymmetric
r\times r
) block
\mathcal{B}(q,p)
satisfies

\mathcal{B}(q,p)\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p}=0.

(1.12)

In order to preserve the structure, suppose that the block
\mathcal{B}(q,p)

has an affine dependence in
p
(which is something that will always hold in our construction, see Remark
4.6
).
The point is that a modified nonholonomic bracket of functions may be defined replacing

\Pi(q,p)
by
\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}(q,p)
in (
1.10
), and such modified bracket will also
describe the dynamics and will retain the geometric properties. This kind of bracket modifications go back to
[

,
45
]
and were
coined
gauge transformations

in
[
5
]
. For mechanical Lagrangians, and under appropriate hypothesis, gauge transformations are known to be necessary to produce Casimirs
[

]
and are essential to study Hamiltonisation in the Poisson framework
[

,

,

,

,

]
.

1.3
Summary of results

This work gives intrinsic geometric constructions of the nonholonomic bracket
and its gauge transformations described above and clarifies their main geometric properties. In our approach, the
brackets are defined on the dual bundle
\mathcal{D}^{*}
which we identify with the annihilator
(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})^{\circ}\subseteq T^{*}Q
and interpret as the phase space
manifold for the almost-Hamiltonian description of the dynamics. The variables
p_{a}
, introduced in Subsection
1.2
,
are linear coordinates on
the fibres of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
, whereas, as indicated above,

q^{i}
are coordinates on the base configuration manifold
Q
. The new feature
introduced by the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
is that the Legendre transformation is an
affine
, instead of
linear, bundle map between
TQ
and
T^{*}Q
. This property ultimately results in the affine nature of the brackets which
can be appreciated in the dependence on
p
of the bottom right block of the matrix
\Pi(q,p)
in (
1.9
),
and similarly for the matrix
\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}(q,p)
.

The nonholonomic bracket that we build is in fact a particular instance of the one given by Maschke and van der Schaft
[
63
]

and whose intrinsic nature was identified by Marle
[
57
]
and further clarified by
Ibort et al
[
50
]
(see also
[
23
,
31
]
). The novelty of our work with
respect to
[
57
,
23
,
50
]
is to give a more elementary intrinsic construction
that involves only metric projections (with respect to the kinetic energy metric) on subbundles of
TQ

instead of the more sophisticated symplectic projections on subbundles of

T(T^{*}Q)
used in
[
57
,
23
,
50
]
. This approach had been followed before in the case of mechanical Lagrangians
[
49
,
29
]
(see also
[
31
]
) but,
to the best of our knowledge, is missing for Lagrangians with gyroscopic terms. On the other hand, the
development of gauge transformations in our setting seems to be completely
new since previous constructions
[
5
,
2
,
3
,
48
,
6
]

focus
exclusively
in the case of mechanical Lagrangians. Our definition of
gauge transformations is inspired by
[
48
]
where the bracket modification is constructed in terms of
a
3
-form
\Lambda
on
Q
. Among other things, this approach provides a convenient way to ensure that the condition (
1.12
)
is satisfied by exploiting the alternating skew-symmetry of
\Lambda
.

Our work also considers the reduction in the presence of symmetries in the context of the brackets that we construct.
More precisely, we assume that that a Lie group
G
acts freely and properly on
Q
, and its tangent lift preserves
L
and
\mathcal{D}
.
Under this hypothesis, both the dynamics and the brackets drop to the quotient space
\mathcal{D}^{*}/G

and the reduced dynamics can be described in terms of the reduced brackets (an additional invariance condition
on the underlying 3-form
\Lambda
is needed for the reduction of the gauged transformed brackets). The interest of this
procedure is that, as occurs in certain examples, a particular choice of
\Lambda
may lead to a reduced
bracket which actually satisfies the Jacobi identity (perhaps after multiplication by a conformal factor), thereby giving
a
true
Hamiltonian description of the reduced dynamics. As mentioned above, this
phenomenon is known as Hamiltonisation.

We exemplify our construction by carefully treating the Suslov and Chaplygin sphere problems with a gyrostat within our framework.
We show that Hamiltonisation is achieved in both examples by proceeding as above. Even though the
Hamiltonisation of these systems
was known (see
[
16
]
for the Chaplygin sphere), our approach clarifies
their origin within a general reduction theory.
Of both examples, the treatment of the Chaplygin sphere with gyrostat is more interesting for two reasons. On the one hand,
a gauge transformation
is needed to make a certain first integral, which is affine in the velocities, into a Casimir of the reduced bracket.
This suggests that some constructions in
[
48
]
admit a generalisation to case of gyroscopic Lagrangians.
We do not pursue this generalisation here since it requires a development of the theory of first integrals of nonholonomic systems
with gyroscopic Lagrangians which is beyond our scope. On the other hand,
the reduced bracket which Hamiltonises the problem has rank 4. Therefore, in contrast with the scenario encountered for rank 2 Poisson brackets
[
36
]
, it is unlikely that its existence can be proved using purely dynamical arguments,
and a geometric approach like ours seems unavoidable.

2
Lagrangian formalism

We begin our discussion from the Lagrangian viewpoint of mechanics on which the
D’Alembert’s principle for the reaction forces is more easily formulated.
Let
Q
be an
n
-dimensional configuration manifold.
We consider the Lagrangian
L:TQ\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
defined in terms of the following three ingredients:

•

a Riemannian metric
g
on
Q
, which defines the kinetic energy
{\mathcal{K}}_{g}:TQ\to{\mathbb{R}}
on
TQ

given by
{\mathcal{K}}_{g}(v_{q})=\frac{1}{2}g_{q}(v_{q},v_{q})
;

•

a
1
-form
\eta
on
Q
, that we’ll refer to as the
gyroscopic 1-form
, which defines a fiberwise linear function,
\eta^{\ell}:TQ\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

\eta^{\ell}(v_{q})=\langle\eta(q),v_{q}\rangle
where
v_{q}\in T_{q}Q
;

•

a potential energy function
V\in C^{\infty}(Q)
.

The Lagrangian
L:TQ\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is then given by

L={\mathcal{K}}_{g}+\eta^{\ell}-V\circ\tau_{Q},

(2.1)

where
\tau_{Q}:TQ\rightarrow Q
is the tangent bundle projection. We will write
L=L_{mech}+\eta^{\ell}
where
L_{mech}:={\mathcal{K}}_{g}-V\circ\tau_{Q}
is the
mechanical Lagrangian
.

2.1
The free case

We rapidly recall aspects of the Lagrangian system on
TQ
in the absence of constraints to introduce the necessary notation.

Local expressions

Let
(q^{i})
be local coordinates on an open subset
U
of
Q
. The kinetic energy
g
and 1-form
\eta
have local expressions

g=\tilde{g}_{ij}(q)\,dq^{i}\otimes dq^{j}
, and
\eta=\tilde{\eta}_{i}(q)\,dq^{i}
, and thus

L(q^{i},\dot{q}^{i})=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}_{ij}(q)\dot{q}^{i}\dot{q}^{j}+\tilde{\eta}_{i}(q)\dot{q}^{i}-V(q).

The equations of motion are the well-known Euler-Lagrange equations

\dot{q}^{i}=\frac{dq^{i}}{dt},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}^{i}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{i}}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(2.2)

Looking ahead at the nonholonomic case, we give local expressions for
L
and the equations of motion
in terms of quasivelocities
which are coordinates for the fibers of
TQ
relative to a configuration-dependent frame.
Let
\{e_{i}\}
be such
local frame; that is,
e_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, are
linearly independent vector fields in the neighbourhood
U
.
Any tangent vector
v_{q}\in T_{q}Q
, with
q\in U
may be uniquely written as
v_{q}=v^{i}e_{i}(q)
, for certain
quasivelocity

scalars
v^{i}
.
Then
(q^{i},v^{i})
are local coordinates for
TQ
which generalise the
bundle coordinates
(q^{i},\dot{q}^{i})
.
The frame vector fields
e_{i}
may be uniquely written as

e_{i}(q)=\rho^{j}_{i}(q)\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n,\quad\forall q\in U,

(2.3)

where
\rho^{j}_{i}\in C^{\infty}(U)
are the entries of an invertible matrix
(\rho^{j}_{i}(q))
.
Associated to the frame
\{e_{i}\}
there is a dual co-frame
\{e^{i}\}
determined by the conditions
e^{i}\in\Omega^{1}(U)
and
\langle e^{i},e_{j}\rangle=\delta^{i}_{j}
. Then
dq^{i}=\rho^{i}_{k}(q)e^{k}(q)
, and the kinetic energy metric
g
and the 1-form
\eta
write as

g=g_{ij}\,e^{i}\otimes e^{j}\;,\qquad\eta=\eta_{i}\,e^{i},

where we have omitted the
q
dependence (as we will often do below) and we have summarised

g_{ij}:=\rho_{i}^{k}\rho_{j}^{l}\tilde{g}_{kl},\qquad\eta_{i}:=\rho_{i}^{k}\tilde{\eta}_{k}.

It follows that

L(q,v)=\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}+\eta_{i}v^{i}-V(q).

(2.4)

It is an exercise in the chain rule to show that the Euler-Lagrange equations in bundle coordinates (
2.2
) are equivalent
to

\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}=\rho^{i}_{j}v^{j},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{i}}\right)=\rho^{j}_{i}\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{j}}-C^{k}_{ij}v^{j}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{k}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

(2.5)

where
C^{k}_{ij}\in C^{\infty}(U)
are the
local structure functions
, determined in terms of
the Lie brackets of the frame vector fields by

[e_{i},e_{j}](q)=C^{k}_{ij}(q)e_{k}(q).

The reader can also find Eqs (
2.5
) in
[
32
]
.

The Euler-Lagrange equations as the trajectories of a second order vector field

The regularity of the Lagrangian (i.e. the invertibility of the Hessian matrix
\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial v^{i}\partial v^{j}}=g_{ij}
) implies
that the Euler-Lagrange equations determine the integral curves of a vector field
\Gamma_{L}\in\mathfrak{X}(TQ)
. Using (
2.5
) it
is easily shown that locally

\Gamma_{L}=\rho^{i}_{j}v^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}+g^{ij}\left(\rho^{k}_{j}\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{k}}-\rho^{k}_{l}v^{l}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial q^{k}\partial v^{j}}-C^{k}_{jl}v^{l}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{k}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{i}},

(2.6)

where
g^{ij}
are the entries of the matrix inverse of
g_{ij}
.
The vector field
\Gamma_{L}
is
second order
. Namely, it satisfies:

(T_{v_{q}}\tau_{Q})(\Gamma_{L}(v_{q}))=v_{q},\mbox{ for }v_{q}\in T_{q}Q.

As a consequence, the integral curves of
\Gamma_{L}
are the tangent lifts of curves on
Q
.

Effect of the gyroscopic 1-form

Write
\Gamma_{L}=\Gamma_{L_{mech}}-\Gamma_{\eta},
where
\Gamma_{L_{mech}}\in{\mathfrak{X}}(TQ)
is the vector field corresponding to

L_{mech}

and
\Gamma_{\eta}
is the
vector gyroscopic force
.
Using (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) one derives the local expressions:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L_{mech}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{i}_{j}v^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}+g^{ij}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho^{k}_{j}\frac{\partial g_{lm}}{\partial q^{k}}-\rho^{k}_{l}\frac{\partial g_{jm}}{\partial q^{k}}-C^{k}_{jl}g_{km}\right)v^{l}v^{m}-\rho^{k}_{j}\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^{k}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{i}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ij}v^{k}\left(\rho^{l}_{k}\frac{\partial\eta_{i}}{\partial q^{l}}-\rho^{l}_{i}\frac{\partial\eta_{k}}{\partial q^{l}}-\eta_{l}C^{l}_{ki}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{j}}.

As is well-known,
\Gamma_{L_{mech}}
is intrinsically given by
\Gamma_{L_{mech}}=\Gamma_{g}-(\hbox{grad}_{g}V)^{\mathbf{V}}
,
where
\Gamma_{g}\in{\mathfrak{X}}(TQ)
is the geodesic flow of the metric
g
, and
(\hbox{grad}_{g}V)^{\mathbf{V}}
is the
potential vector force
given by the vertical
lift of the
g
-gradient of the 1-form
dV
, that is
5
5

5

We employ the standard
musical
notation for the bundle isomorphisms induced by the metric
g
. Namely

\flat_{g}:TQ\rightarrow T^{*}Q
is defined by

\langle\flat_{g}(v_{q}),w_{q}\rangle=g_{q}(v_{q},w_{q})
, for all
v_{q},w_{q}\in T_{q}Q
and its inverse is
\sharp_{g}=(\flat_{g})^{-1}:T^{*}Q\rightarrow TQ
.

\hbox{grad}_{g}V=\sharp_{g}(dV),\qquad(\hbox{grad}_{g}V)^{\mathbf{V}}(u_{q})=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\left(u_{q}+t(\hbox{grad}_{g}V)(q)\right),\quad\forall u_{q}\in T_{q}Q.

On the other hand, an intrinsic interpretation of the
gyroscopic vector force
\Gamma_{\eta}
may be given by considering the
(1,1)
-tensor field
\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}

on
Q
defined in terms of
d\eta
and the metric
g
by

\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}(u_{q})=\sharp_{g}(i_{u_{q}}d\eta(q))\;,\quad u_{q}\in T_{q}Q.

We have
\Gamma_{\eta}=(\mathcal{T}_{d\eta})^{\mathbf{V}}
where, as usual,

(\mathcal{T}_{d\eta})^{\mathbf{V}}(u_{q})=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\left(u_{q}+t\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}(u_{q})\right),\qquad\forall u_{q}\in T_{q}Q\;.

This description of
\Gamma_{\eta}
shows that a closed gyroscopic 1-form has no effect
on the equations of motion. In such a case
L
and
L_{mech}
determine the same
equations of motion and are called equivalent Lagrangians (see
[
54
]
).

Energy conservation

The
energy function

E_{L}\in C^{\infty}(TQ)
is intrinsically defined as

E_{L}=\Delta L-L
,
where
\Delta\in{\mathfrak{X}}(TQ)
is the Liouville vector field given by

\Delta(u)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(u+tu)\in T_{u}TQ,

or, in coordinates,

\Delta=v^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{i}}
.
Interestingly, the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
does not contribute to the energy, which is the sum of the kinetic and potential energies. Indeed, as one easily computes

E_{L}=E_{L_{mech}}={\mathcal{K}}_{g}+V\circ\tau_{Q}
, or, in
coordinates,

E_{L}(q,v)=\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}(q)v^{i}v^{j}+V(q)\;.

(2.7)

As is well known, the time independence of
L
implies that
E_{L}
is a first integral of
\Gamma_{L}
.

2.2
The nonholonomic case

As mentioned above, quasivelocities are very convenient in the presence of nonholonomic constraints.
We assume that the constraints are linear in the velocities, that is, they are given by a smooth distribution
{\mathcal{D}}\hookrightarrow TQ
which we assume to
have constant rank
r\geq 2
. As is well known the constraints are nonholonomic if and only if
\mathcal{D}
is
non-integrable. A fundamental role in our treatment will be played by the inclusion and projection maps

i_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow TQ,\qquad\mathcal{P}:TQ\to\mathcal{D}.

(2.8)

Here
\mathcal{P}
is the bundle projector induced from the orthogonal bundle decomposition
TQ=\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
, where

\mathcal{D}^{\perp}:=\{u\in TQ\;|\,g(u,v)=0,\ \hbox{for all}\ v\in{\mathcal{D}}\}
.

Local Expressions

We work with a local frame adapted to the constraints. More precisely, our frame is

\{e_{i}\}=\{e_{a},e_{\alpha}\},\quad 1\leq a\leq r,\quad r+1\leq\alpha\leq n,

(2.9)

where
\{e_{a}\}
is a local basis of sections of
\mathcal{D}
and
\{e_{\alpha}\}

is a local basis of sections of
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
.
The induced coordinates
(q^{i},v^{a},v^{\alpha})
of
TQ
are such that

{\mathcal{D}}=\{(q^{i},v^{a},v^{\alpha})\;|\,v^{\alpha}=0,\ r+1\leq\alpha\leq n\}.

(2.10)

In other words, the nonholonomic constraints in these coordinates become
v^{\alpha}=0
,
r+1\leq\alpha\leq n
, and we may thus
use
(q^{i},v^{a})
as coordinates for the velocity phase space
{\mathcal{D}}
.

D’Alembert’s principle leads to the nonholonomic equations of motion (see e.g.

[
7
,
10
,
27
]
):

\begin{split}\displaystyle\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}&=\rho^{i}_{a}v^{a},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,\\
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{a}}\right)&=\rho^{j}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial q^{j}}-C^{c}_{ab}v^{b}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{c}}-C^{\alpha}_{ab}v^{b}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L}{\partial v^{\alpha}},\qquad a=1,\dots,r,\\
v^{\gamma}&=0,\qquad\gamma=r+1,\dots,n,\end{split}

where
L
is given by (
2.4
), and, in accordance with our index convention,

[e_{a},e_{b}](q)=C_{ab}^{c}(q)e_{c}+C_{ab}^{\gamma}(q)e_{\gamma}(q).

(2.11)

It is convenient to write the above equations of motion in terms of the
constrained Lagrangian
,
L_{c}:=L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
. In view of (
2.4
) and (
2.10
), it has local expression

L_{c}(q,v^{a})=\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}v^{a}v^{b}+\eta_{a}v^{a}-V(q).

(2.12)

A simple calculation shows that

\begin{split}\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}&=\rho^{i}_{a}v^{a},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,\\
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{a}}\right)&=\rho^{j}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}-C^{c}_{ab}v^{b}\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{c}}-C_{ab}^{\alpha}v^{b}\eta_{\alpha},\qquad a=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

(2.13)

Intrinsic geometry of the equations

The solutions of (
2.13
) are the integral curves of the nonholonomic vector field
\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}\in{\mathfrak{X}}({\mathcal{D}})
, which is intrinsically characterised by the conditions

(T_{v_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}})(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(v_{q}))-\Gamma_{L}(v_{q})\in\mathcal{F}_{v_{q}},\qquad\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(v_{q})\in T_{v_{q}}\mathcal{D},\qquad\forall v_{q}\in\mathcal{D}.

(2.14)

Here
T_{v_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}}:T_{v_{q}}\mathcal{D}\to T_{v_{q}}(TQ)
is the tangent space inclusion and the subspace
\mathcal{F}_{v_{q}}\subseteq T_{v_{q}}(TQ)
, which contains the constraint reaction force, is determined according to D’Alembert’s principle by

\mathcal{F}_{v_{q}}=\left(\left[\sharp_{g}(\mathcal{D}^{\circ})\right]^{\mathbf{V}}\right)(v_{q})=\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(v_{q}+t\,\sharp_{g}(\gamma_{q}))\;|\;\gamma_{q}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{q}^{\circ}\right\},

where

\mathcal{D}_{q}^{\circ}\subseteq T_{q}^{*}Q
is the annihilator of
\mathcal{D}_{q}
.
The nonholonomic vector field
\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}
so determined is second order with
respect to the bundle structure
(\tau_{Q})_{|{\mathcal{D}}}:{\mathcal{D}}\to Q
, namely,

T_{v_{q}}\tau_{Q}(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(v_{q}))=v_{q},\qquad\forall v_{q}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{q}.

Effect of the gyroscopic 1-form

As in the unconstrained case, the effect of the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
may be identified by decomposing the nonholonomic vector field
\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}=\Gamma_{(L_{mech},{\mathcal{D}})}-\Gamma_{(\eta,\mathcal{D})}
.
In view of (
2.13
) and (
2.12
) we have the local expressions

\begin{split}\Gamma_{(L_{mech},{\mathcal{D}})}&=\rho^{i}_{a}v^{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}+g^{cd}\left[v^{a}v^{b}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho^{k}_{d}\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{ab}}{\partial q^{k}}-\displaystyle\rho^{k}_{a}\frac{\partial g_{db}}{\partial q^{k}}-C^{e}_{ad}g_{eb}\right)-\rho^{k}_{d}\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial q^{k}}\right]\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{c}},\\
\Gamma_{(\eta,\mathcal{D})}&=g^{cd}v^{a}\left(\rho^{i}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta_{d}}{\partial q^{i}}-\rho^{i}_{d}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta_{a}}{\partial q^{i}}-\eta_{i}C^{i}_{ad}\right)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{c}}\;.\end{split}

(2.15)

An intrinsic expression for the constrained gyroscopic vector force
\Gamma_{(\eta,\mathcal{D})}
can be given
in terms of the vector bundle endomorphism
\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
. We have

\Gamma_{(\eta,\mathcal{D})}(u_{q})=(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}})^{\mathbf{V}}(u_{q}):=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(u_{q}+t(\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{T}_{d\eta}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}})(u_{q})).

The above equality may be verified using (
2.15
) and noting that, due to our choice of frame, the orthogonal projector
\mathcal{P}:TQ\to\mathcal{D}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}(q^{i},v^{a},v^{\alpha})=(q^{i},v^{a})
.

Energy conservation

Since the constraints are linear and time independent, the energy is also preserved along the constrained motion. More precisely, the restricted energy function
E_{c}:=E_{L}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
is a first integral of the nonholonomic vector field
\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}
. In local coordinates we have

E_{c}(q,v^{a})=\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}(q)v^{a}v^{b}+V(q).

(2.16)

3
The Legendre transformation and the Hamiltonian formalism

3.1
The free case

The Legendre transformation

{\mathbb{F}}L:TQ\rightarrow T^{*}Q

associated with
L:TQ\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

is defined as the fiber derivative

\langle{\mathbb{F}}L(u_{q}),v_{q}\rangle:=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}L(u_{q}+tv_{q}),

(3.1)

for all
u_{q},v_{q}\in T_{q}Q
.
For a Lagrangian of the type (
2.1
) we have

{\mathbb{F}}L=\flat_{g}+\eta\circ\tau_{Q}.

(3.2)

A fundamental observation is that
the presence of the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
, makes
{\mathbb{F}}L
into an
affine
bundle isomorphism
(instead of a linear one).

As usual, the Hamiltonian function
H\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)
associated with
L
is defined by

H:=E_{L}\circ({\mathbb{F}}L)^{-1}
.
The dynamics in this formulation is given by the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}\in\mathfrak{X}(T^{*}Q)
characterised as the unique vector field satisfying

X_{H}(F)=\{F,H\},\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad F\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q),

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\},
is the canonical Poisson bracket on
T^{*}Q

[
1
,
30
]
. The well-known
equivalence of the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian
formulations of the dynamics is expressed as

({\mathbb{F}}L)_{*}\Gamma_{L}=X_{H}.

(3.3)

The Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
is second order in the sense that
{\mathbb{F}}L\left((T_{\alpha_{q}}\pi_{Q})(X_{H}(\alpha_{q}))\right)=\alpha_{q}
for
all
\alpha_{q}\in T^{*}Q
, where
\pi_{Q}:T^{*}Q\to Q
is the cotangent bundle projection.

Local expressions

Any covector
\gamma_{q}\in T_{q}^{*}Q
may be expressed uniquely
in terms of our co-frame
\{e^{i}\}
as a linear combination
\gamma_{q}=p_{i}e^{i}(q)
.
The coefficients
p_{i}
are the cotangent bundle analog of the quasi-velocities
v^{i}
, and will be called
quasi-momenta
(although
this terminology is not very standard). If
(q^{i})
are local coordinates for
Q
, then
(q^{i},p_{i})
are local coordinates
for
T^{*}Q
which in general
are
not
Darboux coordinates.

The local expressions for the Legendre transformation
{\mathbb{F}}L
and its inverse are:

p_{i}=g_{ij}v^{j}+\eta_{i},\qquad v^{i}=g^{ij}(p_{j}-\eta_{j}).

(3.4)

Therefore, using (
2.7
), we obtain the local expression for
H
:

H(q,p)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}(p_{i}-\eta_{i})(p_{j}-\eta_{j})+V(q).

(3.5)

Note that, in contrast with the energy function
E_{L}\in C^{\infty}(TQ)
which is independent of the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
, the affine
nature of the Legendre transformation leads to the appearance of
\eta
in the Hamiltonian
H
.

The local expression for the Hamiltonian vector field in these coordinates is

X_{H}=\displaystyle\rho^{j}_{i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}-\left(\rho^{i}_{j}\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^{i}}+C^{k}_{ji}p_{k}\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}\right)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}.

(3.6)

The equivalence of the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations can be directly verified using
(
3.4
). The second order nature of
X_{H}
corresponds to the identities
\frac{dq^{j}}{dt}=\rho^{j}_{i}v^{i}
which are valid for all
j
,
and which hold in virtue of (
3.6
), (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) (see also
[
32
]
).

3.2
The nonholonomic case

The passage from the Lagrangian to the Hamiltonian formulation in the constrained case
is given by the
constrained Legendre transformation

\mathbb{F}L_{c}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{*}
which we define as the fiber derivative of the constrained Lagrangian
L_{c}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
. Namely,

\langle\mathbb{F}L_{c}(u_{q}),v_{q}\rangle:=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}L_{c}(u_{q}+tv_{q}),

for all
u_{q},v_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
. The constrained Legendre transformation is a diffeomorphism. More precisely,
and as in the free case, the gyroscopic 1-form makes
\mathbb{F}L_{c}
into an invertible bundle map which is generally affine instead of linear (see Proposition
3.1
below).

The
constrained Hamiltonian
function
H_{c}\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{D}}^{*})
and
the
nonholonomic vector field

X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
describing the dynamics on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
are defined by pushing forward with
\mathbb{F}L_{c}

the corresponding objects on
\mathcal{D}
:

H_{c}:=E_{c}\circ(\mathbb{F}L_{c})^{-1},\qquad X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}:={\mathbb{F}L_{c}}_{*}(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}).

(3.7)

In analogy with the unconstrained case, the nonholonomic vector field
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
is second order meaning
that
{\mathbb{F}}L_{c}\left(T_{\alpha_{q}}\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}(X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\alpha_{q}))\right)=\alpha_{q}
for
all
\alpha_{q}\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
, where
\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to Q
is the vector bundle projection.

A distribution on
\mathcal{D}^{*}

Following previous references e.g.
[
66
,
8
,
27
,
28
]
, we consider the distribution
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
defined as

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{q}}:=\{v\in T_{\alpha_{q}}\mathcal{D}^{*}\,:\,T_{\alpha_{q}}\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}(v)\in\mathcal{D}_{q}\},

(3.8)

for
\alpha_{q}\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
and where, as usual,
\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to Q
is the vector bundle projection. It is known that, under our hypothesis,

\mathcal{E}
is a regular distribution of rank
2r=2\,\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{D})

which is integrable if and only if
\mathcal{D}
is integrable and has the property
that
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
takes values on
\mathcal{E}
, i.e.
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\alpha_{q})\in\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{q}}
for all
\alpha_{q}\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{E}
is symplectic meaning that
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha_{q}}
is a symplectic subspace of
T_{{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(\alpha_{q})}(T^{*}Q)
for all
\alpha_{q}\in\mathcal{D}^{*}
, where
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\hookrightarrow T^{*}Q
is the dual morphism of the orthogonal projector
{\mathcal{P}}:TQ\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
.

The metric decomposition of
T^{*}Q

The orthogonal decomposition
TQ=\mathcal{D}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
, associated to the kinetic energy, induces
a bundle decomposition of the cotangent bundle in terms of the annihilators:
T^{*}Q=(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})^{\circ}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\circ}
. Moreover,
the kinetic energy induces
natural identifications

\mathcal{D}^{*}\simeq(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})^{\circ}=\flat_{g}(\mathcal{D})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})^{*}\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\circ}=\flat_{g}(\mathcal{D}^{\perp}),

(3.9)

which allow us to write

T^{*}Q=\mathcal{D}^{*}\oplus(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})^{*}.

(3.10)

In our treatment below we will make extensive use of the bundle maps

i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}:T^{*}Q\to\mathcal{D}^{*},\qquad\mathcal{P}^{*}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\hookrightarrow T^{*}Q,

(3.11)

which are dual to the maps
i_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathcal{P}
defined in Eq (
2.8
) above.
In view of our identifications, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the inclusion of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
on
T^{*}Q
and instead

i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
the projection onto
\mathcal{D}^{*}
according to the decomposition (
3.10
).

According to the decomposition (
3.10
) we may uniquely write the
gyroscopic 1-form as

\eta=\eta^{\parallel}+\eta^{\perp},\quad\mbox{with}\quad\eta^{\parallel}(q)\in\mathcal{D}^{*}_{q},\;\;\;\eta^{\perp}(q)\in(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})_{q}^{*},\;\;\;\forall q\in Q.

Proposition 3.1
.

The constrained Legendre transformation
\mathbb{F}L_{c}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{*}
is explicitly given by

\mathbb{F}L_{c}=i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\circ(\flat_{g}+\eta^{\parallel}\circ\tau_{Q})\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}.

(3.12)

In particular
\mathbb{F}L_{c}
is an affine bundle isomorphism which is linear if and only if
\eta^{\parallel}=0
.

Proof.

Let
u_{q},v_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
. Given that
u_{q}+tv_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
we have
L_{c}(u_{q}+tv_{q})=L(u_{q}+tv_{q})
and therefore,
using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have

\langle\mathbb{F}L_{c}(u_{q}),v_{q}\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}L(u_{q}+tv_{q})=\langle{\mathbb{F}}L(u_{q}),v_{q}\rangle=\langle i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(\flat_{g}(u_{q})+\eta(q)),v_{q}\rangle.

But,
\langle i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(\eta(q)),v_{q}\rangle=\langle\eta^{\parallel}(q),v_{q}\rangle
since
v_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
. The conclusion about the nature of
\mathbb{F}L_{c}

is obvious from (
3.12
).

The nonholonomic dynamics as a projection of the free Hamiltonian vector field

The following theorem is fundamental for our purposes of expressing the vector field
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
in almost-Hamiltonian form.
It states that
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
equals an appropriate projection of unconstrained Hamiltonian vector fields. In its
statement,
\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})
denotes the image of
\mathcal{D}
under the (unconstrained) Legendre transformation. In view
of (
3.2
),

\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})
equals the affine subbundle
\mathcal{D}^{*}+\eta
of
T^{*}Q
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\beta_{q}\in\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D}_{q})
. The following equalities hold

\begin{split}X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(\beta_{q}))=(T_{\beta_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(X_{H}(\beta_{q}))=(T_{\beta_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\beta_{q})).\end{split}

(3.13)

Proof.

In the next section we will give the local expressions for
H
and
H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
from which it is easily verified
that
dH(\beta_{q})=d(H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(\beta_{q})
(Lemma
3.3
). As a consequence
X_{H}(\beta_{q})=X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\beta_{q})

and the second equality of (
3.13
) holds. To prove the first equality, let
u_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
such that
\beta_{q}={\mathbb{F}}L(u_{q})
. From the intrinsic version of the Lagrange-D’Alembert Principle
(
2.14
), we have

(T_{u_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}})\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q})-\Gamma_{L}(u_{q})=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(u_{q}+t\sharp_{g}(\gamma_{q})),\quad\mbox{for some}\quad\gamma_{q}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{q}^{\circ}.

Applying
T_{u_{q}}{\mathbb{F}}L
to both sides of the equation
and using expression (
3.2
) for
{\mathbb{F}}L
yields,

\displaystyle(T_{u_{q}}({\mathbb{F}}L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}))(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q}))-(T_{u_{q}}{\mathbb{F}}L)(\Gamma_{L}(u_{q}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}({\mathbb{F}}L(u_{q}+t\sharp_{g}(\gamma_{q})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(\flat_{g}(u_{q})+t\gamma_{q}+\eta(q))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(\beta_{q}+t\gamma_{q}).

Now notice that
(T_{u_{q}}{\mathbb{F}}L)(\Gamma_{L}(u_{q}))=X_{H}(\beta_{q})
by (
3.3
). Thus, applying
T_{\beta_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
on both sides of the above equation gives

T_{u_{q}}(i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ{\mathbb{F}}L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}})(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q}))-(T_{\beta_{q}}i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})(X_{H}(\beta_{q}))=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\beta_{q}+ti_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\gamma_{q}),

but the right hand side vanishes since
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\gamma_{q}=0
because
\gamma_{q}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{q}^{\circ}=(\mathcal{D}^{\perp})_{q}^{*}
.
Therefore, we conclude that

T_{u_{q}}(i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ{\mathbb{F}}L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}})(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q}))=(T_{\beta_{q}}i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})(X_{H}(\beta_{q})).

(3.14)

Now, using (
3.2
) and (
3.12
) we conclude that
i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ{\mathbb{F}}L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathbb{F}L_{c}
. This has two consequences, the first is that

\mathbb{F}L_{c}(u_{q})=i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{F}}L(u_{q}))=i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}(\beta_{q})
, and the second is that
the left hand side of (
3.14
) may
be rewritten as

\begin{split}T_{u_{q}}(i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ{\mathbb{F}}L\circ i_{\mathcal{D}})(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q}))&=(T_{u_{q}}\mathbb{F}L_{c})(\Gamma_{(L,{\mathcal{D}})}(u_{q}))\\
&=X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\mathbb{F}L_{c}(u_{q}))\\
&=X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\beta_{q}),\end{split}

where we have used the definition (
3.7
) of
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
in the second equality.
Hence, Eq. (
3.14
) implies that
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\beta_{q})=(T_{\beta_{q}}i^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})(X_{H}(\beta_{q}))
as required.

Local expressions

Our choice of frame in (
2.9
) implies that the dual co-frame
\{e^{i}\}
also splits as

\{e^{i}\}=\{e^{a},e^{\gamma}\}
and is adapted to the decomposition (
3.10
). We write the corresponding quasi-momenta as
(p_{i})=(p_{a},p_{\gamma})
.
It follows that
(q^{i},p_{a},p_{\gamma})
are coordinates for
T^{*}Q
and

(q^{i},p_{a})
are coordinates for
\mathcal{D}^{*}
which is described as a submanifold of
T^{*}Q

by the conditions
p_{\gamma}=0
,
r+1\leq\gamma\leq n
. In these coordinates, and with the conventions introduced in the previous
Section
2.2
, we have

i_{\mathcal{D}}(q^{i},v^{a})=(q^{i},v^{a},0),\qquad i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(q^{i},p_{a},p_{\gamma})=(q^{i},p_{a}).

The components
\eta^{\parallel}
and
\eta^{\perp}
of the gyroscopic form
\eta
are

\eta^{\parallel}=\eta_{a}e^{a},\qquad\eta^{\perp}=\eta_{\gamma}e^{\gamma}.

Hence, in view of (
3.12
), the constrained Legendre transformation refines identities (
3.4
)
to

p_{a}=g_{ab}v^{b}+\eta_{a},\qquad v^{b}=g^{ab}(p_{a}-\eta_{a}).

(3.15)

Combining the second equality above with the local expression (
2.16
) for
E_{c}
, yields the following local
expression for the constrained Hamiltonian
H_{c}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}H_{c}(q^{i},p_{a})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}(p_{a}-\eta_{a})(p_{b}-\eta_{b})+V(q).\end{array}

(3.16)

Now, it is easy to verify that the following standard relations between the constrained Lagrangian
L_{c}
(given by (
2.12
))
and the constrained Hamiltonian
H_{c}
given above hold

p_{a}=\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial v^{a}},\qquad v^{a}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}},\qquad\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}=-\frac{\partial L_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}.

Hence, under the constrained Legendre transformation (
3.15
), the equations of motion (
2.13
)
transform to

\begin{split}\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}&=\rho^{i}_{a}\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}},\qquad i=1,\dots,n,\\
\frac{dp_{a}}{dt}&=-\rho^{j}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}-C^{d}_{ab}p_{d}\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}-C^{\alpha}_{ab}\eta_{\alpha}\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}},\qquad a=1,\dots,r.\end{split}

(3.17)

In particular we have
\frac{dq^{i}}{dt}=\rho^{i}_{a}g^{ab}(p_{b}-\eta_{b})=\rho^{i}_{a}v^{a}
for all
i
, which
expresses the second order nature of
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
whose local expression is

X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}=\left(\rho^{j}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}}\right)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}-\left(\rho^{i}_{a}\displaystyle\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{i}}+(C^{d}_{ab}p_{d}+C^{\gamma}_{ab}\eta_{\gamma})\displaystyle\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\right)\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{a}}.

(3.18)

The distribution
\mathcal{E}
defined by (
3.8
) is specified in our coordinates as

\mathcal{E}_{(q^{i},p_{a})}=\mathrm{span}\left\{\rho^{i}_{a}g^{ab}(p_{b}-\eta_{b})\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}\,,\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{b}}\right\}.

It is clear from this description that its rank equals
2r
and
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
takes values on it.

We finish this section by using the above local expressions for
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
and
H_{c}
to prove the following Lemma that was used in the
proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

dH(\beta_{q})=d(H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(\beta_{q})
for all
\beta_{q}\in\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})_{q}
.

Proof.

In view of (
3.4
) we have the following coordinate description of
\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})
:

\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})=\left\{(q^{i},p_{a},p_{\gamma})\,:\,p_{\gamma}=\eta_{\gamma},\quad\gamma=r+1,\dots,n\right\}.

On the other hand, using the local expressions for
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
and
H_{c}
given above we have

H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(q^{i},p_{a},p_{\gamma})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}(p_{a}-\eta_{a})(p_{b}-\eta_{b})+V(q).

Comparing with (
3.5
) we conclude that
(H-H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(q^{i},p_{a},p_{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\gamma}(p_{\beta}-\eta_{\beta})(p_{\gamma}-\eta_{\gamma}
). This expression vanishes quadratically along
\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})
so its differential along
this set also vanishes.

4
Almost-Poisson formulation

We are now ready to define the almost-Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
for the nonholonomic dynamics
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
considered above. This is a bracket of functions on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
such that

X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\varphi)=\{\varphi,H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\qquad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})$}.

4.1
Definitions and main properties

As before, denote by
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
the canonical Poisson bracket on
T^{*}Q
.
For
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
we define
\{\varphi,\psi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
by

\{\varphi,\psi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:=\{\varphi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*},\psi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\}\circ(\mathcal{P}^{*}+\eta^{\perp}\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}),

(4.1)

where
\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to Q
is the vector bundle projection.
To make sense of the above formula recall that
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}+\eta^{\perp}\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
are bundle morphisms such that

i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}:T^{*}Q\to\mathcal{D}^{*},\qquad\mathcal{P}^{*}+\eta^{\perp}\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to T^{*}Q.

(4.2)

Note that the gyroscopic term manifests itself in (
4.1
) through
the term involving
\eta^{\perp}
. In particular,
if the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
vanishes along
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
, we recover previous constructions
[
49
,
29
,
31
]

of the nonholonomic bracket for
mechanical Lagrangians.

The properties of the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
are given in Theorem
4.2
below. In particular, as is clear
from its definition, it is
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear, skew-symmetric and satisfies
Leibniz rule in each argument. As a consequence, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
, the mapping
\{\cdot,\varphi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})

is a derivation which determines a vector field
X_{\varphi}^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
that we call the
almost-Hamiltonian vector field associated
to
\varphi
, and is determined by

X_{\varphi}^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}(\psi)=\{\psi,\varphi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\qquad\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*}).

The bracket of linear and basic functions

The following proposition expresses the value of the bracket (
4.1
) applied to linear and basic functions
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
. The resulting expressions determine the bracket completely
due to the vector bundle structure of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
. In the
statement of the proposition,
\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
is the space of sections of
\mathcal{D}
, and for
Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
we denote by
Y^{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
the induced linear function
\alpha_{q}\mapsto Y^{\ell}(\alpha_{q}):=\langle\alpha_{q},Y(q)\rangle
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
Y,Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
f,g\in C^{\infty}(Q)
. We have,

\begin{split}&\{Y^{\ell},Z^{\ell}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-(\mathcal{P}[Y,Z])^{\ell}+d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\\
&\{Y^{\ell},f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-Y(f)\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\\
&\{f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},g\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=0.\end{split}

Proof.

We rely on the following well-known properties of the canonical Poisson bracket:

\{W_{1}^{\ell},W_{2}^{\ell}\}=-[W_{1},W_{2}]^{\ell},\qquad\{W_{1}^{\ell},f\circ\pi_{Q}\}=-W_{1}(f)\circ\pi_{Q},\qquad\{f\circ\pi_{Q},g\circ\pi_{Q}\}=0,

(4.3)

valid for
W_{1},W_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
where
\pi_{Q}:T^{*}Q\to Q
is the cotangent bundle projection.
These, together with (
4.1
), yield

\displaystyle\{Y^{\ell},Z^{\ell}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{P}[Y,Z])^{\ell}+\eta^{\perp}[Y,Z]\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},

\displaystyle\{Y^{\ell},f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}

\displaystyle=-Y(f)\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\ \;\{f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},g\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=0.

Now, since

\eta^{\perp}(Y)=\eta^{\perp}(Z)=0
, we have
\eta^{\perp}[Y,Z]=-d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)
,
and the result follows.

Main properties

The main properties of the bracket on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
defined above are collected in the following theorem.
In its formulation, the
characteristic distribution of the bracket
is the distribution on
\mathcal{D}^{*}

generated by all almost-Hamiltonian vector fields.

Theorem 4.2
.

The bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
defined by (
4.1
) satisfies:

(i)

it is
{\mathbb{R}}
-bilinear, skew-symmetric and satisfies the Leibniz rule in each argument;

(ii)

it is a fiberwise affine bracket on
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
which is fiberwise linear if and only if

d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)=0\;,\quad\forall\,Y,Z\in{\Gamma}({\mathcal{D}});

(iii)

its characteristic distribution coincides with
\mathcal{E}
(defined by (
3.8
));

(iv)

the Jacobi identity is satisfied if and only if the constraint distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
is integrable
(i.e. if and only if the constraints are holonomic).

Proof.

(i)
is a direct consequence of the definition of the bracket as was mentioned above.

(ii)
If
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
are fiberwise affine functions then they can be written as

\varphi=Y^{\ell}+f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\psi=Z^{\ell}+g\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},

for
Y,Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
f,g\in C^{\infty}(Q)
.
Hence, by Proposition
4.1
, we deduce that

\{\varphi,\psi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-(\mathcal{P}[Y,Z])^{\ell}+(d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)+Z(f)-Y(g))\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},

showing that
\{\varphi,\psi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
is a fiberwise affine function on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

Moreover, for
Y,Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
we have

\{Y^{\ell},Z^{\ell}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-(\mathcal{P}[Y,Z])^{\ell}+d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},

which is a fiberwise linear function on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
if and only if
d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)=0
.

(iii)
The characteristic distribution is generated by the almost-Hamiltonian vector fields of linear and basic functions
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
. Consider first the linear function
Y^{\ell}\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{D}}^{*})
corresponding to
a section
Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})
. Using Proposition
4.1
, we conclude that the
its almost-Hamiltonian vector field
satisfies

X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{Y^{\ell}}(f\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})=\{f\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}},Y^{\ell}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}=Y(f)\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}},

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(Q)

showing that
X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{Y^{\ell}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
is
\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
-projectable over
Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})
. In particular this
implies
that
X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{Y^{\ell}}
takes values on
\mathcal{E}
.
Using again Proposition
4.1
, we find that the almost-Hamiltonian vector field
X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{(g\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})}

associated to the basic function determined by
g\in C^{\infty}(Q)
,
satisfies

X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{(g\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})}(f\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})=0,\;\;\forall f\in C^{\infty}(Q),

showing that
X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{(g\circ\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})}
is
\tau_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
-vertical, and therefore it also takes values on
\mathcal{E}
.
This shows that the characteristic distribution is contained in
\mathcal{E}
. Using again Proposition
4.1

it is not difficult to argue that the characteristic distribution has rank

2\,\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{D})
and therefore must coincide with
\mathcal{E}
.

(iv)
A necessary condition for the validity of the Jacobi identity is the integrability of the characteristic distribution. As was indicated right
after its definition,

\mathcal{E}
is integrable if and only if
\mathcal{D}
is integrable, which implies that the bracket cannot satisfy the Jacobi identity if
\mathcal{D}
is non-integrable.
Conversely, if we assume that
{\mathcal{D}}
is integrable then we have
\mathcal{P}[Y,Z]=[Y,Z]
for all
Y,Z\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})
. This
implies that
d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)=0
, and in particular, the first identity of
Proposition
4.1
becomes
\{Y^{\ell},Z^{\ell}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-([Y,Z])^{\ell}
. The Jacobi identity can be easily proved using this
and the other formulas in Proposition
4.1
.

4.2
Almost-Poisson description of the dynamics

We now prove that the nonholonomic dynamics may be described in almost-Hamiltonian form with respect to the
bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
and the constrained Hamiltonian
H_{c}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
.

Theorem 4.3
.

The vector field
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
describing the nonholonomic dynamics satisfies

X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\varphi)=\{\varphi,H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\;\;\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*}).

In other words,
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
coincides with the almost-Hamiltonian vector field
X^{\mathcal{D}^{*}}_{H_{c}}
associated to
H_{c}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{q}\in\mathcal{D}_{q}^{*}
and write
\beta_{q}:=(\mathcal{P}^{*}+\eta^{\perp}\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}})(\alpha_{q})\in T_{q}^{*}Q
. On the one hand, using that
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\circ\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the identity
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(\eta^{\perp})=0
, we have
i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(\beta_{q})=\alpha_{q}
. On the other hand,
\beta_{q}\in\mathbb{F}L(\mathcal{D})_{q}
.
Indeed, from (
3.2
), we deduce that
\beta_{q}=\mathbb{F}L(\sharp_{g}(\mathcal{P}^{*}(\alpha_{q})-\eta^{\parallel}(q)))
and
since
\mathcal{P}^{*}(\alpha_{q})-\eta^{\parallel}(q)\in(D^{\perp}_{q})^{\circ}
and
\flat_{g}(\mathcal{D}_{q})=(\mathcal{D}_{q}^{\perp})^{\circ}
,
one can check that

\sharp_{g}(\mathcal{P}^{*}(\alpha_{q})-\eta^{\parallel}(q))\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
.

Therefore, using Theorem
3.2
we have

\begin{split}X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\varphi)(\alpha_{q})&=\langle d\varphi(\alpha_{q})\,,\,X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\alpha_{q})\rangle\\
&=\langle d\varphi(\alpha_{q})\,,\,(T_{\beta_{q}}i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\beta_{q}))\rangle\\
&=\langle T_{\beta_{q}}^{*}i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}(d\varphi(\alpha_{q}))\,,\,X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\beta_{q})\rangle\\
&=\langle d(\varphi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(\beta_{q})\,,\,X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\beta_{q})\rangle\\
&=X_{H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}(\varphi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*})(\beta_{q}).\end{split}

Now use the fact that, by its definition, the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{F}\in\mathfrak{X}(T^{*}Q)
of a function
F\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)
satisfies
X_{F}(G)=\{G,F\}
for all
G\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)
, to get

\begin{split}X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\varphi)(\alpha_{q})&=\{\varphi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*},H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\}(\beta_{q})\\
&=\{\varphi\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*},H_{c}\circ i_{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\}\circ(\mathcal{P}^{*}+\eta^{\perp}\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}})(\alpha_{q})\\
&=\{\varphi,H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}(\alpha_{q}).\end{split}

4.3
Local expressions

As is well known, the canonical Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on
T^{*}Q
defines a (canonical) Poisson
2
-vector
\Pi
on
T^{*}Q
by the relation
\{F,G\}=\Pi(dF,dG)
, for
F,G\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}Q)
.
As may be proved, using for instance (
4.3
) (see also
[
32
]
), the local expression for
\Pi
in our coordinates is

\Pi=\rho^{i}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}C^{k}_{ij}p_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}},

or, in matrix form,

\Pi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\rho^{i}_{j}\\[6.0pt]
-\rho^{i}_{j}&-C^{k}_{ij}p_{k}\end{array}\right).

The Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
of
H
, given previously by (
3.6
), satisfies
X_{H}=\Pi(\cdot,dH)
, which corresponds to the following vector form of Hamilton’s equations:

\left(\begin{array}[]{r}\dot{q}^{i}\\
\dot{p}_{j}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\rho^{j}_{i}\\[6.0pt]
-\rho^{j}_{i}&-C^{k}_{ij}p_{k}\end{array}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q^{i}}\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_{j}}\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand, using Proposition
4.1

expressions for the nonholonomic brackets of our

\begin{split}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}&=-C^{c}_{ab}(q)p_{c}-C^{\gamma}_{ab}(q)\eta_{\gamma}(q),\\
\{p_{a},q^{i}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}&=-\rho^{i}_{a}(q),\\
\{q^{i},q^{j}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}&=0.\end{split}

(4.4)

The corresponding

Or, in matrix form

\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\{q^{i},q^{j}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}&\{q^{i},p_{b}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\\
\{p_{a},q^{j}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}&\{p_{a},p_{b}\}_{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\rho^{i}_{b}\\
-\rho^{j}_{a}&-C^{c}_{ab}p_{c}-C^{\gamma}_{ab}\eta_{\gamma}\end{array}\right).

Theorem

is equivalent to saying that the vector field

-\rho^{j}_{a}&-C^{c}_{ab}p_{c}-C^{\gamma}_{ab}\eta_{\gamma}\end{array}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}\\[10.0pt]

4.4
Gauge transformations

As first recognised in
[
44

45
]
, in order to achieve Hamiltonisation of certain nonholonomic systems
possessing symmetries and a specific kind of first integrals, it is convenient to consider modifications of the
nonholonomic bracket to guarantee that such integrals descend as Casimir functions to the reduced space.
These modifications were termed
gauge transformations
in
[
5
]
since they can be interpreted within the
framework of
[
64
]

in our treatment. Such transformations are non-trivial when the rank
r
of
\mathcal{D}
is
\geq 3

Definition and main properties

Our presentation is inspired by
[
48
]
where the deformation of the bracket
is performed using a 3-form on
Q
. In this reference, an explicit construction of the 3-form is given
to guarantee that the so-called
invariant gauge momenta
become Casimir functions
of the modified bracket after reduction.
For our purposes, we suppose that we are given a 3-form
\Lambda
on
Q
which we assume to vanish upon
contraction of vectors in
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
.
The
gauge transformation of the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
by
\Lambda
is the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
which is
characterised by
its value on linear and basic functions on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\{Y^{\ell},Z^{\ell}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-(\mathcal{P}([Y,Z]+\sharp_{g}(i_{Y\wedge Z}\Lambda))^{\ell}+(d\eta^{\perp}(Y,Z)-\Lambda(Y,Z,\sharp_{g}(\eta^{\parallel})))\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\\
&\{Y^{\ell},f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-Y(f)\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\\
&\{f\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},g\circ\tau_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=0.\end{split}

(4.5)

As usual, in the above expressions
Y,Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
and
f,g\in C^{\infty}(Q)
. Moreover, we employ the convention
that
\langle i_{Y\wedge Z}\Lambda,W\rangle=\langle i_{Z}i_{Y}\Lambda,W\rangle=\Lambda(Y,Z,W)
for
W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
.

Remark 4.4
.

The assumption that
\Lambda
vanishes when contracted with vectors in
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
is done for concreteness and ease of presentation
since it is only the contraction of
\Lambda
with sections of
\mathcal{D}
that is relevant.
One could alternatively define the gauge transformation by a general 3-form on
Q
by the same formula (
4.5
). With this latter approach,
the 3-forms
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in\Omega^{3}(Q)
define the same gauge transformed bracket, i.e.
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda_{2}}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
,
if and only if their restrictions to
\mathcal{D}
coincide.

The
\mathbb{R}
-linearity and skew-symmetry of the gauged transformed bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
readily follows
from (
4.5
). The validity of the Leibniz rule can be established using the
local expressions (
4.6
) given below to prove the existence of a bivector on
\mathcal{D}^{*}

which is uniquely characterised by (
4.5
).
6

6

Alternatively, one could define the semi-basic 2-form
\Xi
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
as the contraction of
the 3-form on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
,

, with

(ii)

If

Local expressions

Suppose that the 3-form

\Lambda=\frac{1}{6}\lambda_{abc}\,e^{a}\wedge e^{b}\wedge e^{c},

(4.6)

Description of the dynamics in terms of gauge transformed brackets

The following theorem shows that, for any 3-form
\Lambda
as above, the resulting gauged-transformed bracket by

Theorem 4.7
.

The vector field
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
describing the nonholonomic dynamics satisfies

X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}(\varphi)=\{\varphi,H_{c}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\;\;\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*}).

Proof.

In view of Theorem
4.3
, it suffices to check that
\{\varphi,H_{c}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\{\varphi,H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
for
all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{*})
. By Leibniz rule, it suffices to check that
this holds for
\varphi
equal to the coordinate functions
q^{j}
,
p_{a}
. Using the local expressions
(
4.4
) and (
4.6
) we have

\{q^{i},H_{c}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}\{q^{i},q^{j}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}}\{q^{i},p_{a}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}\{q^{i},q^{j}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{a}}\{q^{i},p_{a}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\{q^{i},H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}.

On the other hand

\{p_{a},H_{c}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}\{p_{a},q^{j}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial q^{j}}\{p_{a},q^{j}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}.

Now we claim that

\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}.

Assuming that this identity holds we obtain
\{p_{a},H_{c}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\{p_{a},H_{c}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
as required. To show that the above identity holds
we use the local expression (

to obtain

\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{\partial H_{c}}{\partial p_{b}}\{p_{a},p_{b}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\lambda_{abd}g^{cd}(p_{c}-\eta_{c})g^{be}(p_{e}-\eta_{e}).

But
\lambda_{abd}g^{cd}(p_{c}-\eta_{c})g^{be}(p_{e}-\eta_{e})=0
for all

because of the skew-symmetry
\lambda_{abd}=-\lambda_{adb}
.

Throughout this section
we assume that the Lie group
G
acts freely and properly on the configuration space
Q
and the lifted action to
TQ

\mathcal{D}

5.1
Reduction

A crucial observation is that the invariance of
L
implies that the action preserves each of the 3 terms comprising the Lagrangian.
Specifically,
G
acts by isometries on
Q
, and the gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
and the potential
V
are invariant. This can be concluded
by a comparison of their homogeneity degrees as functions on
TQ

Theorem 5.1
.

The cotangent lifted action of
G
on
T^{*}Q
leaves
\mathcal{D}^{*}
invariant and the restricted action on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
is free and proper and
leaves the nonholonomic vector field
X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}
, the constrained Hamiltonian

H_{c}
and the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
invariant.

Proof.

Since
G
acts by isometries, the metric isomorphism
\flat_{g}:TQ\to T^{*}Q
is equivariant with
respect to the tangent and cotangent lifted actions of
G
on
TQ
and
T^{*}Q
.
Thus, the invariance of
\mathcal{D}
implies the invariance of
\mathcal{D}^{*}=\flat_{g}(\mathcal{D})
. Now, the cotangent lifted action of
G
on

T^{*}Q
is free and proper, so considering that
\mathcal{D}^{*}\subseteq T^{*}Q
is a submanifold, the same is true about its
restriction to
\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

The proof of invariance of the different objects on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
proceeds by using the invariance of the metric, of the
gyroscopic 1-form
\eta
and of the potential
V
to recognise the appropriate invariance/equivariance of their defining elements. For example,
the invariance of the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
follows from the well-known invariance of the
canonical Poisson bracket on
T^{*}Q
by cotangent lifted actions, and the equivariance of the bundle morphisms (
4.2
)
by the action of
G
on
T^{*}Q
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}
.
We omit the details.

We now outline the reduction of the system based on the above theorem.
Since the action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}^{*}
is free and proper, the orbit space
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:=\mathcal{D}^{*}/G
is smooth and the
orbit projection map
\pi_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
is a surjective submersion. As a consequence, the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
descends
to a (smooth)
reduced bracket
of functions on
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
characterised by

\{\varphi,\psi\}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}}\circ\pi_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\{\varphi\circ\pi_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\psi\circ\pi_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}},\qquad\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}).

(5.1)

On the other hand, the invariance of
H_{c}\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{D}^{*}})
induces a
reduced constrained Hamiltonian

\widehat{H_{c}}\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}})

such that
\widehat{H_{c}}\circ\pi_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=H_{c}
. Finally, the invariance of the nonholonomic vector field

implies the existence of a

\widehat{X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}}(\varphi)=\{\varphi,\widehat{H_{c}}\}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}},\qquad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}})$}.

(5.2)

In other words, the reduced dynamics may be formulated in terms of the reduced constrained Hamiltonian
\widehat{H_{c}}
and the
reduced bracket on
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
.

5.5
Hamiltonisation

A remarkable property of certain examples of symmetric nonholonomic systems is that, perhaps after a time reparametrisation,
the reduced equations of motion possess a
true Hamiltonian structure. In such a case we say that the system allows a
Hamiltonisation
.
This exceptional property allows the application of powerful methods of Hamiltonian systems to investigate the reduced
dynamics. Moreover, Hamiltonisable systems are natural candidates for the exploration of the dynamics
of more general nonholonomic systems by perturbation methods.

In our context, Hamiltonisation is achieved if one can find a 3-form
\Lambda
and a positive function
f\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}})

such that
the reduced bracket
f\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}}^{\Lambda}
satisfies the Jacobi identity. In examples, the multiplicative function

f
is often basic, i.e.
f\in C^{\infty}(Q)^{G}
, it is
related to the density of a smooth invariant measure of the system and is interpreted as a time reparametrisation of the dynamics. As mentioned before, for mechanical Lagrangians, the incorporation
of the 3-form
\Lambda
is necessary to guarantee that certain invariant first integrals descend to the quotient space as Casimirs
[
48
]
and to give a geometric framework for Hamiltonisation
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
.

We do not present any general results about conditions for Hamiltonisation but only some examples below.

6.1
Kinematics of rigid bodies

We briefly recall the standard approach to describe the
motion of a rigid body in space and introduce our notation. The orientation of the
body and at any instant

Such matrix is the change of basis matrix between

rigidly attached to the body and rotates with it. Both the inertial
and the body frame are assumed to be orthonormal. The
angular velocity vector

has coordinates
\omega:=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with respect to the inertial frame

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}

\Omega:=(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with respect to the body frame
\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\}

coordinate vectors
\omega
and
\Omega
respectively correspond to the right and left
trivialisations of the velocity vector
\dot{R}\in T_{R}SO(3)

6.3
Suslov problem with gyrostat

The classical Suslov problem concerns the rotational motion of a rigid body about a point that
is fixed in space, under its own inertia, and subjected to the nonholonomic constraint
that the component of the angular velocity
along an axis that is
fixed in the body
vanishes. The
configuration space is

, and, if the body frame is chosen such
that the
E_{3}=(0,0,1)
is aligned with the axis of forbidden rotation, the nonholonomic constraint

\Omega_{3}=0.

This constraint defines a rank 2 left invariant distribution

on

is also invariant under the (lifted) left action of
SO(3)
and hence the system may be
reduced by this action as explained in Subsection

. The shape space

is trivial and hence the reduced phase space

isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}

The reduced equations

\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see e.g.
[
18

55
]

\dot{M}=(M+B)\times\Omega+\lambda E_{3},\qquad\lambda=-\frac{(\mathbb{I}^{-1}E_{3})\cdot((M+B)\times\Omega)}{(\mathbb{I}^{-1}E_{3})\cdot E_{3}}.

(6.2)

to the
invariant plane
\Omega_{3}=0
(it is readily checked that
\Omega_{3}
is a first integral). An explicit form of these equations for
(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
, which may be taken as coordinates
in
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}

Due to our supposition that

is aligned with the axis of forbidden rotation, we may not assume

is diagonal. However, by a suitable rotation of the

-

0&I_{22}&I_{23}\\

(6.3)

Elementary algebraic manipulations show that the restriction of (

\begin{split}&\dot{\Omega}_{1}=-\frac{1}{I_{11}}\left(I_{13}\Omega_{1}+I_{23}\Omega_{2}+B_{3}\right)\Omega_{2},\\

As predicted by the theory, equations (

Our treatment in Subsection

Hamiltonisation

As is easily verified, equations (

function

The above bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity (any bivector on a 2-dimensional manifold does) and thus provides a

of the reduced system (

Geometric origin of the bracket (

The following theorem, whose proof is postponed to Subsection

The bracket (

6.4
Chaplygin sphere with gyrostat

The classical Chaplygin sphere problem
[
25
]
is one of the most celebrated examples of integrable
nonholonomic systems and
concerns the motion of an inhomogeneous sphere, whose center of mass coincides with its geometric center, that rolls without
slipping on a horizontal plane due to its own inertia. The system remains integrable in the presence of a gyrostat,
which is a generalisation of the problem due to Markeev
[
56
]
.

The configuration space is the 5 dimensional manifold
Q=SO(3)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Elements in
Q
specify the
attitude matrix of the sphere and its position on the plane on which it rolls. There are two rolling constraints
which specify a rank 3 constraint distribution
\mathcal{D}
.
The system possesses an
SE(2)
symmetry, which satisfies the assumptions of Section
5
,
corresponding to translations and horizontal rotations of the inertial frame on the plane on which
the rolling takes place.

The reduced equations of motion are conveniently formulated in terms of the vector
K\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
which gives body coordinates of the
angular momentum of the sphere about the contact point, and the Poisson vector
\Gamma:=R^{-1}e_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. One may explicitly write
K
in terms of
\Omega
and
\Gamma
as:

K=\mathbb{I}\Omega+mr^{2}\Gamma\times(\Omega\times\Gamma),

(6.7)

where
m
is the total mass and
r
is the radius of the sphere. One may assume that the body frame is chosen
aligned with the principal axes of inertia and hence
\mathbb{I}
is diagonal
\mathbb{I}=\mbox{diag}(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
.
Equation (
6.7
)
expresses
K
as a
\Gamma
-dependent linear function of
\Omega
. Such linear function may be inverted to give

\Omega=AK+\left(\frac{(AK)\cdot\Gamma}{1-mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma}\right)\,A\Gamma,

(6.8)

where
A:=(\mathbb{I}+mr^{2}\mbox{I}_{3})^{-1}
, with
\mbox{I}_{3}
denoting the
3\times 3
identity matrix. We note that the denominator

1-mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma>0.

Indeed, this follows for the fact that the eigenvalues of
A
are strictly less than
\frac{1}{mr^{2}}
so we may estimate
mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma<\|\Gamma\|^{2}=1
.

The
SE(2)
-reduced equations of motion are the restriction of following system for
(K,\Gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\dot{K}=(K+B)\times\Omega,\qquad\dot{\Gamma}=\Gamma\times\Omega,

(6.9)

to the invariant set
\|\Gamma\|^{2}=1
(see e.g.
[
16
,
33
]
). In the above equations
and in what follows, it is understood that
\Omega
is expressed in terms of
(K,\Gamma)
as in (
6.8
).

As explained in
Subsection
5.2
, the reduced space
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\mathcal{D}^{*}/SE(2)
is a rank 3 vector bundle over the shape space
Q/SE(2)=S^{2}
.
The components of
K
are linear coordinates on the fibres of
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
whereas the Poisson vector
\Gamma\in S^{2}

parametrises the shape space
S^{2}
. Therefore we write

\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\left\{(K,\Gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\|\Gamma\|^{2}=1\,\right\},

(6.10)

and interpret (
6.9
) as the equations determining the reduced vector field
\widehat{X_{\mathrm{\small{nh}}}}
.

Apart from the energy
integral

H(K,\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2}K\cdot\Omega,

(6.11)

which coincides with the reduced constrained Hamiltonian
\widehat{H_{c}}
, the system possesses the momentum integrals

F_{1}(K,\Gamma)=\|K+B\|^{2},\qquad F_{2}(K,\Gamma)=(K+B)\cdot\Gamma,

(6.12)

and the invariant measure

\mu=(1-mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma)^{-1/2}\,dKd\Gamma.

Equations (
6.9
) can be formulated in almost-Poisson form
\dot{x}=\{x,H\}
, where
x=(K,\Gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
, with respect to the
bracket

\begin{split}&\{K_{i},K_{j}\}:=-\sum_{l=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{ijl}\left(K_{l}-mr^{2}(\Omega\cdot\Gamma)\Gamma_{l}+B_{l}\right),\\
&\{K_{i},\Gamma_{j}\}:=-\sum_{l=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{ijl}\Gamma_{l},\\
&\{\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{j}\}:=0,\end{split}

(6.13)

where
\varepsilon_{ijk}
denotes the alternating tensor
9
9

9

\varepsilon_{ijl}
equals zero if two indices are equal, it equals
1
if
(i,j,k)
is a cyclic permutation of

(1,2,3)
and
-1
otherwise.
. The above bracket possesses two Casimir functions

\|\Gamma\|^{2}
and
F_{2}
. Since
\|\Gamma\|^{2}
is a Casimir, the bracket may be restricted to the 5-dimensional reduced space
\widehat{\mathcal{D}^{*}}

defined by (
6.10
) and results in a rank
4
almost-Poisson structure.

Hamiltonisation

A remarkable observation, which can be verified through a direct calculation with a symbolic software, is that the rescaled bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{*}:=\left(1-mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma\right)^{1/2}\,\{\cdot,\cdot\},

satisfies the Jacobi identity and is therefore a true Poisson bracket. Therefore, one may
write the reduced system (
6.13
) in Hamiltonian form
x^{\prime}=\{x,H\}^{*}
, where
{}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{d\tau}
and the new time variable
\tau
is related to the
original time
t
by

dt=\left(1-mr^{2}(A\Gamma)\cdot\Gamma\right)^{1/2}\,d\tau.

This Hamiltonisation was apparently found for the first time in
[
16
]
. (In the absence of the
gyrostat the Hamiltonisation goes back to
[
14
]
).

Geometric origin of the bracket (
6.13
)

The geometric origin of the bracket (
6.13
) is clarified by the following theorem.

Theorem 6.3
.

The bracket (
6.13
) coincides with the reduced bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
where the 3-form
\Lambda
can be taken as the scaling by a constant factor of the bi-invariant Cartan volume 3-form on
SO(3)
.

The precise scaling of
\Lambda
mentioned in the theorem is clarified at the end of Subsection
7.3
below.

We mention that the gauge transformation by
\Lambda
is necessary to make
F_{2}
into a Casimir of (
6.13
). We found the
specific 3-form
\Lambda
following the prescription
of
[
48

where the theory is developed only for mechanical Lagrangians. Theorem
6.3

above suggests that there is a natural
extension of Theorem 5.6 of
[
48
]
to the gyroscopic context considered in this work. We do not attempt to develop such extension here since
it would require a study of linear (and affine) first integrals for nonholonomic systems with gyroscopic Lagrangians which is
beyond our scope.

\begin{split}g=&f(\theta)\,d\varphi\otimes d\varphi+I_{1}\,d\theta\otimes d\theta+I_{3}\,d\psi\otimes d\psi+2I_{3}\cos\theta\,d\varphi\otimes d\psi+m\,(dx\otimes dx+dy\otimes dy),\end{split}

(7.9)

where we denote
f(\theta):=I_{1}\sin^{2}\theta+I_{3}\cos^{2}\theta
.
The 1-form
\eta
is again given by (
7.4
). The nonholonomic constraints
of rolling without slipping are

\dot{x}=r\omega_{1},\qquad\dot{y}=-r\omega_{2},

(7.10)

which, using (
7.2
), rewrite in local coordinates as

\dot{x}-r\sin\varphi\,\dot{\theta}+r\cos\varphi\sin\theta\,\dot{\psi}=0,\qquad\dot{y}+r\cos\varphi\,\dot{\theta}+r\sin\varphi\sin\theta\,\dot{\psi}=0,

(7.11)

and determine the rank
3
distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
on
Q
.

Denote by
(R_{\alpha},(a,b))\in SE(2)
the matrix

(R_{\alpha},(a,b)):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha&a\\
\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha&b\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then, the symmetry action of
SE(2)
on
Q
is is given in our coordinates by

(R_{\alpha},(a,b)):(\varphi,\theta,\psi,x,y)\mapsto(\varphi+\alpha,\theta,\psi,x\cos\alpha-y\sin\alpha+a,x\sin\alpha+y\cos\alpha+b).

(7.12)

As our adapted basis (
2.9
) for
{\mathfrak{X}}(Q)
we take

\begin{split}e_{1}&:=\partial_{\varphi},\\
e_{2}&:=\partial_{\theta}+r\sin\varphi\,\partial_{x}-r\cos\varphi\,\partial_{y}\\
e_{3}&:=\partial_{\psi}-r\sin\theta(\cos\varphi\,\partial_{x}+\sin\varphi\,\partial_{y}),\\
e_{4}&:=\frac{mr^{2}\sin\theta}{I_{1}}\partial_{\theta}-r\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\partial_{x}+r\sin\theta\cos\varphi\,\partial_{y},\\
e_{5}&:=-\frac{mr^{2}\cos\theta}{I_{1}}\partial_{\varphi}+\frac{mr^{2}f(\theta)}{I_{1}I_{3}}\partial_{\psi}+r\sin\theta\cos\varphi\,\partial_{x}+r\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\partial_{y}.\end{split}

(7.13)

As required,
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
is a local basis of
{\mathcal{D}}
and

\{e_{4},e_{5}\}
of
{\mathcal{D}}^{\perp}
. Moreover, as may be verified, these vector fields are invariant
under the
SE(2)
action (
7.12
). (We note that the treatment in Section 6 of
[
48
]
,
which considers the Chaplygin sphere without gyrostat,
is done with the same basis of
{\mathcal{D}}
and, therefore, some of the expressions given below
can be found there.)

The dual basis of our frame (
7.13
) is computed to be

\begin{split}e^{1}=&d\varphi+\frac{mr^{2}I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)}\left(d\psi+\frac{\cos\varphi\,dx+\sin\varphi\,dy}{r\sin\theta}\right),\\
e^{2}=&\frac{1}{I_{1}+mr^{2}}\left(I_{1}\,d\theta+mr\sin\varphi\,dx-mr\cos\varphi\,dy\right),\\
e^{3}=&\frac{1}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}\left(I_{1}I_{3}\sin\theta\,d\psi-mrf(\theta)(\cos\varphi\,dx+\sin\varphi\,dy)\right),\\
e^{4}=&\frac{I_{1}}{(I_{1}+mr^{2})\sin\theta}\left(d\theta+\frac{1}{r}\left(-\sin\varphi\,dx+\cos\varphi\,dy\right)\right),\\
e^{5}=&\frac{I_{1}I_{3}}{rG(\theta)\sin\theta}\left(r\sin\theta\,d\psi+\cos\varphi\,dx+\sin\varphi\,dy\right),\end{split}

(7.14)

where

G(\theta):=I_{1}I_{3}+I_{1}mr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+I_{3}mr^{2}\cos^{2}\theta.

The 1-form
\eta
given by (
7.4
) writes as
\eta=\eta_{j}e^{j}
with coefficients

\begin{split}&\eta_{1}=B_{1}\sin\theta\sin\psi+B_{2}\sin\theta\cos\psi+B_{3}\cos\theta,\qquad\eta_{2}=B_{1}\cos\psi-B_{2}\sin\psi,\\
&\eta_{3}=B_{3},\qquad\eta_{4}=\frac{mr^{2}\sin\theta}{I_{1}}\left(B_{1}\cos\psi-B_{2}\sin\psi\right),\\
&\eta_{5}=\frac{mr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{I_{1}I_{3}}\left(I_{1}B_{3}-\frac{I_{3}\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}(B_{1}\sin\psi+B_{2}\cos\psi)\right).\end{split}

(7.15)

The coefficients
\rho_{\alpha}^{i}
are easily read from (
7.13
). Recalling our convention
(q^{1},q^{2},q^{3},q^{4},q^{5})=(\varphi,\theta,\psi,x,y)
, we have

\begin{split}&\rho_{1}^{1}=1,\qquad\rho_{1}^{2}=0,\qquad\rho_{1}^{3}=0,\qquad\rho_{1}^{4}=0,\qquad\rho_{1}^{5}=0,\\
&\rho_{2}^{1}=0,\qquad\rho_{2}^{2}=1,\qquad\rho_{2}^{3}=0,\qquad\rho_{2}^{4}=r\sin\varphi,\qquad\rho_{2}^{5}=-r\cos\varphi,\\
&\rho_{3}^{1}=0,\qquad\rho_{3}^{2}=0,\qquad\rho_{3}^{3}=1,\qquad\rho_{3}^{4}=-r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\qquad\rho_{3}^{5}=-r\sin\theta\sin\varphi.\end{split}

(7.16)

On the other hand, via the calculation of the pairwise brackets
[e_{a},e_{b}]
,
1\leq a<b\leq 3
, one determines after long
but straightforward calculations:

\begin{split}C_{12}^{1}&=-C_{21}^{1}=\frac{mr^{2}I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\hskip 18.49988ptC_{12}^{3}=-C_{21}^{3}=-\frac{mr^{2}f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\hskip 18.49988ptC_{12}^{5}=-C_{21}^{5}=\frac{I_{1}I_{3}}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\\
C_{13}^{2}&=-C_{31}^{2}=\frac{mr^{2}\sin\theta}{I_{1}+mr^{2}},\hskip 18.49988ptC_{13}^{4}=-C_{31}^{4}=-\frac{I_{1}}{I_{1}+mr^{2}},\hskip 18.49988ptC_{23}^{1}=-C_{32}^{1}=-\frac{mr^{2}I_{3}\cos^{2}\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\\
C_{23}^{3}&=-C_{32}^{3}=\frac{mr^{2}\cos\theta f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\hskip 18.49988ptC_{23}^{5}=-C_{32}^{5}=-\frac{I_{1}I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta},\end{split}

(7.17)

where we have omitted all terms
C_{ab}^{j}
which vanish. In view of (
4.4
),
we obtain the following
expressions for the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
:

\begin{split}\{p_{1},p_{2}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}&=-\frac{mr^{2}I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}p_{1}+\frac{mr^{2}f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}p_{3}-\frac{I_{1}I_{3}}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}\eta_{5},\\
\{p_{1},p_{3}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}&=-\frac{mr^{2}\sin\theta}{I_{1}+mr^{2}}p_{2}+\frac{I_{1}}{I_{1}+mr^{2}}\eta_{4},\\
\{p_{2},p_{3}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}&=\frac{mr^{2}I_{3}\cos^{2}\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}p_{1}-\frac{mr^{2}\cos\theta f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}p_{3}+\frac{I_{1}I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin\theta}\eta_{5},\end{split}

with
\eta_{4}
and
\eta_{5}
given by (
7.15
).
The other non-vanishing brackets of the form
\{p_{a},q^{i}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
, are readily determined from (
4.4
) and (
7.16
) to be

\begin{split}&\{p_{1},\varphi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{2},\theta\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{2},x\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-r\sin\varphi,\qquad\{p_{2},y\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\cos\varphi,\\
&\{p_{3},\psi\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{3},x\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\qquad\{p_{3},y\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\sin\theta\sin\varphi.\end{split}

(7.18)

Gauge transformation

The first integral
F_{2}
defined by (
6.12
)
equals the quasi-momentum
p_{1}
(this follows immediately from
the formulae (
7.22
) and (
7.26
) given ahead). Therefore,
inspired by
[
48
]
, we consider the gauge transformation by the 3-form
\Lambda=\tilde{\lambda}\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{3}
where
\tilde{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}(Q)
is
given by

\tilde{\lambda}:=g([e_{1},e_{2}],e_{3})=-mr^{2}\sin\theta.

Hence, the coefficients
\lambda_{abc}
appearing in (
4.6
) are given by

\lambda_{abc}=-\varepsilon_{abc}mr^{2}\sin\theta,

(see footnote
9
for the definition of the alternating tensor
\varepsilon_{abc}
).

According to (
4.6
), to obtain the local expressions for the gauge-transformed bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
,
we need the entries of the matrix

g^{ab}
which is the inverse of the
3\times 3
matrix with entries
g_{ab}:=g(e_{a},e_{b})
, that determines
the quadratic part of the constrained Lagrangian
L_{c}
(see Eq. (
7.24
) below). One finds the following
expressions for
g^{ab}
(which are also given in
[
48
]
):

\begin{split}g^{11}&=\frac{I_{3}+mr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{G(\theta)\sin^{2}\theta},\qquad g^{22}=\frac{1}{I_{1}+mr^{2}},\qquad g^{33}=\frac{f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin^{2}\theta},\\
g^{12}&=g^{21}=0,\qquad g^{13}=g^{31}=-\frac{I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin^{2}\theta}\qquad g^{23}=g^{32}=0.\end{split}

(7.19)

The gauged transformed bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
can be determined substituting the above formulae into (
4.6
).
For instance, one computes

\begin{split}\{p_{1},p_{2}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}&=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\lambda_{12a}g^{ab}(p_{b}-\eta_{b})\\
&=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}+\lambda_{123}g^{3b}(p_{b}-\eta_{b})\\
&=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}-mr^{2}\sin\theta\left(-\frac{I_{3}\cos\theta}{G(\theta)\sin^{2}\theta}(p_{1}-\eta_{1})+\frac{f(\theta)}{G(\theta)\sin^{2}\theta}(p_{3}-\eta_{3})\right),\end{split}

and similarly for the other brackets. Simplifying the resulting expressions one obtains

\begin{split}&\{p_{1},p_{2}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=0,\qquad\{p_{1},p_{3}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=0,\\
&\{p_{2},p_{3}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=\frac{mr^{2}\sin\theta}{G(\theta)}\left(-(I_{3}+mr^{2})p_{1}+(I_{3}-I_{1})\cos\theta p_{3}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+(I_{3}+mr^{2})\sin\theta(\sin\psi B_{1}+\cos\psi B_{2})+(I_{1}+mr^{2})\cos\theta B_{3}\right).\end{split}

(7.20)

According to (
4.6
), the brackets of the quasi-momenta
p_{a}
with the coordinates
q^{i}
are not modified by
the gauge transformation. Hence, the non-vanishing expressions of the form
\{p_{a},q^{i}\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}

exactly agree with those given above in (
7.18
). In other words,

\begin{split}&\{p_{1},\varphi\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{2},\theta\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{2},x\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-r\sin\varphi,\qquad\{p_{2},y\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\cos\varphi,\\
&\{p_{3},\psi\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=-1,\qquad\{p_{3},x\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\qquad\{p_{3},y\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}=r\sin\theta\sin\varphi.\end{split}

(7.21)

Expressions for the bracket in terms of
K
and
\Gamma

In order to prove Theorem
6.3
, we compute bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{D}^{*}}
,
determined by Eqs. (
7.20
) and (
7.21
) above,
of the entries of the Poisson vector
\Gamma
and the momentum
K
introduced
in Subsection
6.4
. For this we need to express
\Gamma
and
K
in terms of
our coordinates
q^{j}
and quasi-momenta
p_{a}
.
In view of (
7.1
), the entries of
\Gamma=R^{-1}e_{3}
are:

\Gamma_{1}=\sin\theta\sin\psi,\qquad\Gamma_{2}=\sin\theta\cos\psi\qquad\Gamma_{3}=\cos\theta.

(7.22)

On the other hand, it may be checked, using (
7.2
), that, along the distribution
\mathcal{D}\subset TQ
defined
by nonholonomic constraints (
7.10
), the
quasi-velocities

v_{1},\dots,v_{5}
determined by our frame satisfy
v_{4}=v_{5}=0
(as expected) and

\begin{split}v_{1}&=\frac{\sin\psi\Omega_{1}+\cos\psi\Omega_{2}}{\sin\theta},\qquad v_{2}=\cos\psi\Omega_{1}-\sin\psi\Omega_{2},\\
v_{3}&=\Omega_{3}-\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}(\sin\psi\Omega_{1}+\cos\psi\Omega_{2}).\end{split}

(7.23)

Furthermore,
the constrained Lagrangian
L_{c}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\begin{split}L_{c}(\varphi,\theta,\psi,x,y,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})&=\frac{1}{2}f(\theta)v_{1}^{2}+\frac{I_{1}+mr^{2}}{2}v_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(I_{3}+mr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)v_{3}^{2}\\
&\qquad+I_{3}\cos\theta v_{1}v_{3}+\eta_{1}v_{1}+\eta_{2}v_{2}+\eta_{3}v_{3},\end{split}

(7.24)

where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}
are given by (

). Hence, the

,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, are given by

\begin{split}p_{1}=&f(\theta)v_{1}+I_{3}\cos\theta v_{3}+\eta_{1},\\
p_{2}=&(I_{1}+mr^{2})v_{2}+\eta_{2},\\
p_{3}=&I_{3}\cos\theta v_{1}+(I_{3}+mr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)v_{3}+\eta_{3}.\end{split}

(7.25)

Combining the above expressions shows that the entries of
K
as defined by
(
6.7
) satisfy:

\begin{split}K_{1}=&\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}p_{1}+\cos\psi p_{2}-\frac{\cos\theta\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}p_{3}-B_{1},\\
K_{2}=&\frac{\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}p_{1}-\sin\psi p_{2}-\frac{\cos\theta\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}p_{3}-B_{2},\\

(7.26)

Relationship between
\Lambda
and the Cartan bi-invariant volume form on
SO(3)
.

The gauge transformation above was done in terms of the 3-form

\Lambda=-mr^{2}\sin\theta\,e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{3},

with
e^{1},e^{2},e^{3}
given by (
7.14
). To complete the proof
of Theorem
6.3
, it remains to show that
\Lambda
can be also taken as a suitable scaling
of the Cartan bi-invariant volume form on
SO(3)
. For this we use that
any bi-invariant 3-form on
SO(3)
is a constant multiple of

\nu:=-\sin\theta\,d\varphi\wedge d\theta\wedge d\psi,

(see e.g.
[
48
]
). Using the above expressions for
\Lambda
and
\nu
,
together with (
7.13
), it is clear that

\Lambda(e_{a},e_{b},e_{c})=mr^{2}\nu(e_{a},e_{b},e_{c}),

for any
a,b,c\in\{1,2,3\}
. In other words, given that
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
is a basis of sections of
\mathcal{D}
,
we conclude that the 3-forms
\Lambda
and
mr^{2}\nu
coincide when restricted to
\mathcal{D}
. Therefore,
by Remark
4.4
, it follows that the gauge transformed bracket by
\Lambda
coincides with
the gauge transformed bracket by
mr^{2}\nu
.

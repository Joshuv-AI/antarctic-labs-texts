Kähler structures for holomorphic submersions
Chi Li

Abstract
In this short paper, for any holomorphic submersion π : X → B \pi:X\rightarrow B , we derive a criterion for X X to have Kähler structures. This criterion generalizes Blanchard’s criterion for a special class of isotrivial holomorphic submersions. We use this criterion to answer a question of Harvey-Lawson in the case of fiber dimension one. As the main application, we prove that the existence of Hermitian-Symplectic structures on certain class of holomorphic submersions with Kähler fibers and Kähler bases implies that the total spaces are Kähler. This class includes isotrivial submersions and torus fibrations.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
X,B
be compact complex manifolds. Assume that there is a holomorphic submersion
\pi:X\rightarrow B
.
We want to derive a criterion for
X
to be a Kähler manifold.

If
X
admits a Kähler metric with the associated Kähler form denoted by
Q
. Then
Q
is a closed
(1,1)
-form which defines the Kähler class
[Q]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
In this paper, we will work with Kähler forms and do not distinguish them from the corresponding
Kähler metrics.
Let
f_{b}:X_{b}\rightarrow X
denote the embedding of the fiber
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
.
The restriction
Q|_{X_{b}}=f_{b}^{*}Q
is a Kähler metric on
X_{b}
for any
b\in B
. On the cohomology level, we have natural restriction homomorphisms for
k\in\{0,\dots,\dim X\}
:

f_{b}^{*}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R}).

(1)

So we have the first necessary condition:

\mathbf{(Condition\;I)}
There is a class
[Q]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
that restricts to be a Kähler class on each fiber
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
,
b\in B
.

Moreover one can show that if
Q
is a Kähler form on
X
, then a Kähler form on
B
can be constructed as
\int_{X/B}Q^{n+1}
where
\int_{X/B}
denotes the operation of integration along fibers. So we get the second necessary condition:

\mathbf{(Condition\;II)}

B
is a Kähler manifold.

The main result of this paper is that these two conditions are also sufficient:

Theorem 1.1
.

In the above setting of holomorphic submersions, there is a Kähler metric on
X
if and only if
\mathbf{(Condition\;I)}
and
\mathbf{(Condition\;II)}
are both satisfied.

Note that if we know a priori that
[Q]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is represented by a closed
(1,1)
-form, then the result can be proved quite easily by applying a family version of
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. See Remark
3.2
. However here we do not assume this condition. Indeed, the main goal is to construct such a closed
(1,1)
-form. In this aspect, one could think Theorem
1.1
as a Kähler correspondent to Thurston’s well-known construction of symplectic structures for symplectic fibrations (
[
19
]
,
[
10
, Theorem 4.91]
).

Let
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
(with
b\in B
) be any fibre of
\pi
. There is a natural monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
defined by translating cohomology classes. If we denote by
H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}
the set of elements in
H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
that are invariant under the monodromy action, then we know that the image of the restriction morphism
f_{b}^{*}
in (
1
) is contained in
H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}
. In the simplest situation when the fiber has complex dimension 1, we have
H^{2}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}=H^{1,1}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
and Theorem
1.1
can answer positively a question of Harvey-Lawson (see Theorem
2.3
).

On the other hand, we have the Leray spectral sequence
\{E^{p,q}_{r},d_{r}:E^{p,q}_{r}\rightarrow E^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}\}
that converges to
\{Gr_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{p}H^{p+q}(X,\mathbb{R})\}
with respect to the Leray filtration
\mathcal{F}_{L}
.
There are isomorphisms:

E^{0,k}_{2}=H^{0}(B,R^{k}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})\cong H^{k}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}.

(2)

According to Leray spectral sequence, a class
x\in E^{0,k}_{2}
extends to be a class in
H^{k}(X,\mathbb{R})
if and only if
d_{r}x=0
for
r\in\{2,\dots,k+1\}
(see Section
2.1
for more explanations).
With these notations, there is a more refined version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There is a Kähler metric on
X
if and only if the following conditions are all satisfied:

\mathbf{(Condition\;Ia)}

There is an element
[\omega]\in H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})
restricts to be a Kähler class on
X_{b}
for any
b\in B
.

\mathbf{(Condition\;Ib)}

d_{2}[\omega]=0
in
E^{2,1}_{2}=H^{2}(B,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})
.

\mathbf{(Condition\;II)}

B
is Kähler.

The conditions of Theorem
1.2
is a priori weaker than the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. In other words, Theorem
1.2
implies
1.1
(the “if” direction).

Theorem
1.2
generalizes an old result of Blanchard in
[
2
]
who considered a special class of isotrivial holomorphic submersions. Here a holomorphic submersion is called
isotrivial
if all fibers are isomorphic as complex manifolds.
Note that by a Theorem of Fischer-Grauert
[
9
]
, isotrivial holomorphic submersions are always locally trivial and are sometimes called analytic fiber bundles. In fact, in proving the criterion in
[
2
]
Blanchard not only assumed isotriviality but also that
\pi_{1}(B)
acts trivially
H^{1}(X_{b})
.

\mathbf{(Conditions\;I)}
appeared in
[
21
, Remark 4.16]
, where it is pointed out that Deligne’s theorem on the degeneration of Leray spectral sequence at the
E_{2}
-page for projective submersions can be proved under this assumption.
This condition also appeared in
[
16
]
where it is called
cohomologically Kähler
. In fact, in the special case of torus fibrations Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are contained in
[
16
, Proposition 2.17]
which was proved there by using a precise description of torus fibrations in terms of variation of Hodge structures of weight 1.

Our motivation for proving Theorem
1.1
comes from a question of Li-Zhang
[
14
]
and Streets-Tian
[
18
]
about the existence of Hermitian-Symplectic (HS) structures on non-Kähler complex manifolds. By definition, a Hermitian-Symplectic structure on a complex manifold
X
with the integrable almost complex structure
J
is a symplectic structure
\omega
on
X

that tames
J
which means that
\omega(v,Jv)>0
for any
v\neq 0\in T_{\mathbb{R}}X
. Any Kähler form
\omega
on
(X,J)
is Hermitian-Symplectic because in this case
\omega
not just tames
J
but is also compatible with
J
which means that
\omega(Jv,Jw)=\omega(v,w)
for any
v,w\in T_{\mathbb{R}}X
. Li-Zhang and Streets-Tian asked whether there are examples of HS structure on non-Kähler complex manifolds.

In real dimension 4, Donaldson had a similar question about tamed/compatible symplectic structures but without assuming the integrability of complex structures.
The question is still open in general, though there are negative results which say that there are no such examples among complex surfaces (

]
), nilmanifolds with invariant complex structures (

), twistor spaces (

We consider this question for complex manifolds that admit structures of holomorphic submersions. As in the Kähler case, it is easy to see that the existence of HS structures for holomorphic submersions forces the fibers and the base to be HS. So if we are looking for such examples of lowest dimension, it makes sense to assume that the base and fibers are all Kähler.
Even though such an example is currently missing to us, we will show that HS submersions always satisfy
\mathbf{(Condition\;Ib)}
(Proposition
4.5
). By applying criterion in Theorem
1.2
, we can then derive a Kählerian criterion for HS submersions (Proposition
4.1
) and rule out several classes of (non-trivial) examples:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow B
be a holomorphic submersion with Kähler fibers and a Kähler base.
Assume that there is a Hermitian-Symplectic structure on
X
. Let
F
denote a fiber of
\pi
.
Then
X
must be Kähler if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

1.

The holomorphic submersion is isotrivial.

2.

The fibers of
\pi
are complex tori (of possibly varying complex structures).

3.

The monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(B)
on
H^{2}(F)
is trivial.

4.

H^{2,0}(F)=0
.

We end this introduction by sketching the organization of this note. In section
2.1
, we will review the Leray spectral sequence in terms of filtered de Rham complex, describe
d_{1}:E^{p,q}_{1}\rightarrow E^{p+1,q}_{1}
in terms of Gauss-Manin connection and explain its compatibility with the exterior differential operator. In section
2.3
, we will review Deligne’s Hodge theory for the cohomology groups with coefficients of polarized variation of Hodge structures and derive a variant of the classical
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma that will be crucial for our construction. In section
3
, we will construct a Kähler form step-by-step which is guided by the Leray spectral sequence as more explained in more detail in Section
2.1
. The main line of construction is similar to the original construction of Blanchard but differs from it significantly in some steps because we are dealing with a more general situation (see Remark
3.1
). Finally in section
4
, we will apply the criterion in Theorem
1.2
to study Hermitian-Symplectic structures for holomorphic submersions.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Leray spectral sequences

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow B
be a differentiable fiber bundle. In this subsection, we do not consider any complex structures. It is well-known that there is a Leray spectral sequence:
\{E^{p,q}_{r},d_{r}\}
with

E^{p,q}_{2}=H^{p}(B,R^{q}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})\Rightarrow E^{p,q}_{\infty}\cong GrH^{p+q}(X,\mathbb{R}).

To prepare for the later construction, we explain this convergence of spectral sequence from the point view of filtered de Rham complex in terms of differential forms.
In the following discussion, for any local calculations, we choose a small coordinate neighborhood
\{U,u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\}
of
B
such that there is a diffeomorphism

\pi^{-1}(U)\cong U\times F.

(3)

Choose local real coordinates
y=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
on
F
such that
\pi:\pi^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U
is given by the projection
\pi(u,y)=u
.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}^{\ell}(X)
the space of smooth degree-
\ell
differential forms on
X
.
A form
\eta\in\mathcal{E}^{\ell}(X)
is locally represented by the following expression:

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|+|K|=\ell}\eta_{I;K}(u,y)du_{I}\wedge dy_{K}=\sum_{I}du_{I}\wedge\eta_{I}

(4)

where, for the simplicity of notation, for an index set
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{|I|}\}
, we denote
du_{I}=du_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge du_{|I|}
(similarly for
dy_{K}
), and
\eta_{I}=\sum_{|K|=\ell-|I|}\eta_{I;K}dy_{K}
.

Consider the following Leray filtration:

\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{\ell}(X)=\left\{\eta=\sum_{|I|+|K|=\ell}\eta_{I;K}du_{I}\wedge dy_{K};|I|\geq p\right\}.

It is easy to verify that under the change of coordinates
(u,y^{\prime})=(u,y^{\prime}(u,y))
, for any
I
with
|I|=p
the term
\sum_{K}\eta_{I;K}dy_{K}
changes as tensor along any fibre
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
.
In particular, the restriction
\eta_{I}|_{X_{b}}:=f_{b}^{*}\eta_{I}
is a well-defined
(\ell-p)
-form on any fiber
X_{b}
.

Let’s recall the description of the Leray spectral sequence following
[
11
, p. 440-442]
:

E^{p,q}_{r}=\frac{\{a\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X):da\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+r}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}(X)\}}{d(\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p-r+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q-1}(X))+\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X)}.

(5)

Here the denominator is not a subgroup of the numerator and the meaning is that we take
\{\text{denominator as written}\}\cap\{\text{numerator}\}
.
The differential
d_{r}:E^{p,q}_{r}\rightarrow E^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}
is then defined as:

d_{r}[a]=[da]\in E^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}=\frac{\{b\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+r}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}(X);db\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+2r}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+2}(X)\}}{d\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X)+\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+r+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}(X)}.

(6)

If a class
[\eta]\in E^{p,q}_{r}

satisfies
d_{r}[\eta]=0
, then by (
6
) there exists
\chi\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}
such that
d\eta-d\chi\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+r+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}
and hence
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}=\eta-\chi
defines the same class in
E^{p,q}_{r}
while
\eta^{\prime}=\eta-\chi
defines a class in
E^{p,q}_{r+1}
.

When
p=0
, we have the map

d_{r}:E^{0,q}_{r}\rightarrow E^{r,q-r+1}_{r}
and
E^{0,q}_{r+1}=\mathrm{Ker}(d_{r})\subseteq E^{0,q}_{r}
. So we always have the inclusion
E^{0,q}_{r}\subseteq E^{0,q}_{r-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq E^{0,q}_{1}
.

Consider the 2nd page (i.e.
r=2
) and denote by
V^{q}=R^{q}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}
the local system with fiber
\{H^{q}(X_{b},\mathbb{R});b\in B\}
. Then there is a natural identification:

E^{0,q}_{2}=H^{0}(B,V^{q})=H^{q}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}

(7)

where
H^{0}(B,V^{q})
is the space of flat sections of
V^{q}
and
H^{q}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}
is the space of cohomology that is invariant under the monodromy action. Moreover,
[\eta]\in E^{0,q}_{r}\subseteq E^{0,q}_{2}=H^{0}(B,V^{q})
if and only if we can find a representative
\eta
of
[\eta]\in E^{0,q}_{2}
such that
d\eta\in\mathcal{F}^{r}\mathcal{E}^{q+1}(X)
. In this case,
d_{r}[\eta]=0
if and only if
there exists
\chi\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{1}\mathcal{E}^{q}
such that
d(\eta-\chi)\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{r+1}\mathcal{E}^{q+1}(X)
.
As a consequence, if
d_{r}[\eta]=0
for any
r\in\{1,\dots,q+1\}
, then the representative
\eta
can be chosen to be a closed
q
-form and we say that
[\eta]\in H^{0}(B,V^{q})
extends to be a closed
q
-form on
X
.
In other words, the subspace
E^{0,q}_{\infty}=E^{0,q}_{q+1}\subseteq E^{0,q}_{2}
is equal to the image of the natural map

f_{b}^{*}:H^{q}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{q}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{inv}}=E^{0,q}_{2}.

(8)

Note that
f_{b}^{*}
coincides with the composition
H^{q}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow Gr_{\mathcal{F}_{L}}^{0}H^{q}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow E^{0,q}_{2}
.
For our later purpose, we need to better understand the 1st and 2nd pages of the Leray spectral sequence.
For any
\eta=\sum_{I}du_{I}\wedge\eta_{I}\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X)
as in (
4
), we know that
for any
I
with
|I|=p
,
\eta_{I}|_{X_{b}}
is a closed differential form on any fibre
{X_{b}}
, i.e.
(d\eta_{I})|_{X_{b}}=0
. So
[\eta_{I}|_{X_{b}}]
defines a class in
V^{q}_{b}=H^{q}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
. Then the space
E_{1}^{p,q}
has the following description:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[\eta]=[\eta]_{p}:=\sum_{|I|=p}du_{I}\otimes[\eta_{I}|_{X_{b}}]:\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X),

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\textrm{ for any $I$ with $|I|=p$},\eta_{I}|_{F}\textrm{ is closed}\}

which is the space of
V^{q}
-valued differential forms of degree
p
.
Next we want to relate the
d_{1}
-differential to the following differential operator induced by the Gauss-Manin connection.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}D:\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q})&\rightarrow&\mathcal{E}^{p+1}(B,V^{q}).\\
{D[\eta]_{p}}&=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|=p\\
i\in\{1,\dots,\dim_{\mathbb{R}}B\}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p}\left(du_{I}\wedge du_{i}\otimes[\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{u_{i}}}\eta_{I}]\right).\end{array}

(9)

Here
\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{u_{i}}}
is the Lie derivative with respect to the vector field
\partial_{u_{i}}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Here we lift
\partial_{u_{i}}
to be vector field defined on
\pi^{-1}(U)
under the diffeomorphism in (
3
).

Using the vanishing
(d\eta_{I})|_{X_{b}}=0
, it is easy to see that the graded piece in
d\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}/\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+2}
is represented by the terms:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|=p;\\
i\in\{1,\dots,\dim_{\mathbb{R}}B\}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p}du_{I}\wedge du_{i}\wedge\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{u_{i}}}\eta_{I}+\sum_{|J|=p+1}(-1)^{p+1}du_{J}\wedge d\eta_{J}.

Note that
d\eta_{J}
in the second term is exact when restricted to any fiber. In particular, for any
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X)
, we have:

d\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad D[\eta]_{p}=[d\eta]_{p+1}\in\mathcal{E}^{p+1}(B,V^{q}).

(10)

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a natural surjective morphism:

\phi:\{a\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}(X):da\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+2}\mathcal{E}^{p+q+1}(X)\}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ker}(D:\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{p+1}(B,V^{q})).

(11)

which induces an isomorphism

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong\frac{\mathrm{Ker}(D:\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{p+1}(B,V^{q}))}{\mathrm{Im}(D:\mathcal{E}^{p-1}(B,V^{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q}))}=H^{p}(B,V^{q}).

(12)

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, denote the left-hand-side and the right-hand-side of (
11
) by
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
respectively. We also denote
\ell=p+q
.

If
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
, then
d\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+2}\mathcal{E}^{\ell+1}(X)
and hence
D[\eta]_{p}=[d\eta]_{p+1}=0
. So we get the natural morphism
\phi
that maps
\eta
to
[\eta]_{p}
.
To see that this map is surjective, choose any
[\eta]\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
with
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{\ell}(X)
such that
\eta_{I}|_{X_{b}}
is closed for any fibre
X_{b}
. Then
[d\eta]_{p+1}=D[\eta]_{p}=0
implies that
(d\eta)_{J}|_{X_{b}}
is exact for any
J
with
|J|=p+1
and any fiber
X_{b}
. Choose a coordinate system
\{(U_{k},\{u^{(k)}_{i}\}\}
of
B
and a partition of unity
\{\rho_{k}\}
subordinate to the covering
\{U_{k}\}
. For each

and any
J
with
|J|=p+1
, we can find
\chi_{J,b}
on

such that
(d\eta)_{J}|_{X_{b}}=d\chi^{(k)}_{J,b}
and
\chi^{(k)}_{J,b}
depends smoothly on

for any fixed

, the family

Define

Then
\eta^{\prime}
satisfies
\eta^{\prime}-\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p+1}\mathcal{E}^{p+q}
and
(d\eta^{\prime})_{J}|_{X_{b}}=0
for any

and satisfies

.

To see that
\phi
induces the isomorphism (

. So assume that
[\eta]=D[\chi]\in\mathrm{Im}(D:\mathcal{E}^{p-1}(B,V^{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{p}(B,V^{q}))
. Then we can assume
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{p}\mathcal{E}^{\ell}(X)
with

and

[\eta]_{p}=D[\chi]_{p-1}=\sum_{|I|=p-1}(-1)^{p-1}(du_{I}\wedge du_{i}\otimes[\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{u_{i}}}\chi_{I}])=[d\chi]_{p}.

This implies

which implies

So we get the identity
\phi^{-1}(\mathrm{Im}(D))=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1}
.
By the definition in (

E^{p,q}_{2}=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}}{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathcal{V}_{1}}
. So we conclude that
\phi
induces an isomorphism.

2.2
Kähler case

From now on, we assume that the fibers
\{X_{b};b\in B\}
and the base
B
are all Kähler manifolds.
Suppose that we have
\alpha\in H^{0}(B,V^{2})\cong E^{0,2}_{2}
that restricts to be a Kähler class on each fibre.
The main problem we are considering is whether we can find a closed
(1,1)
-form
Q
on
X
such that
[Q]|_{X_{b}}=f_{b}^{*}[Q]=\alpha
, i.e.
\alpha
is contained in the image of the natural morphism in (
8
) for

.
According to Theorem

, the answer is positive if and only if
d_{2}(\alpha)=0
.
In some sense, Theorem
1.2
is a converse to the following basic result in Kähler geometry by Blanchard and Deligne:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
2
,
4
]
)
.

Let
X
and
B
be compact Kähler manifolds and
\pi:X\rightarrow B
be a holomorphic submersion, then the Leray spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{2}
-page.

In section
4

).

Another interesting consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the following positive answer to a question of Harvey-Lawson in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Corollary of Theorem
1.1
)
.

Assume that the fiber of the submersion has dimension 1. Then there exists a Kähler metric on
X
if and only if the homology class of any fiber of

In fact, when

For any

[
2
]
instead of Deligne’s Hodge theory for polarized variation of Hodge structures used in the above proof.

Remark 3.2
.

If we know that the class
[Q]
contains a closed
(1,1)
-form
Q
, then the above construction can be greatly simplified as follows. We start with the
(1,1)
-form
\omega
constructed by using partition of unity as at the beginning of this section. For any
b\in B
, we can use the standard
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma to conclude that there exists
\psi_{b}\in C^{\infty}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
such that
\omega_{b}=Q|_{X_{b}}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{b}
and
\int_{X_{b}}\psi_{b}\omega_{b}^{n}=0
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\psi_{b}
depends smoothly on
b\in B
. This allows us to define a smooth function
\Psi
on
X
such that
\Psi|_{X_{b}}=\psi_{b}
. Define
Q=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Psi
. Then
Q
is a closed
(1,1)
-form that restricts to be a Kähler metric
\omega_{b}
on
X_{b}
. Choosing a Kähler metric
\omega_{B}
on
B
,
Q+K\cdot\pi^{*}\omega_{B}
is then a Kähler metric for
K\gg 1
.

If we just know that
Q
is a closed 2-form not necessarily of
(1,1)
-type, then a similar construction would only produces a Hermitian-Symplectic form on
X
. The Hermitian-Symplectic structure will be discussed in the next section.

4
An application to Hermitian-Symplectic structures

Let
X
be a complex manifold and
Q
be a closed 2-form on
X
.

Q
is a Hermitian-Symplectic (HS) structure precisely when
Q
satisfies the following two conditions.

1.

Q
is a symplectic form. In other words,
Q^{\dim X}
is non-vanishing and
dQ=0
.

2.

If
Q=Q^{2,0}+Q^{1,1}+Q^{0,2}
is the decomposition of
Q
into differential forms of type
(2,0)
,
(1,1)
and
(0,2)
respectively, then
Q^{1,1}
is a positive definite
(1,1)
-form.

We are going to apply Theorem
1.2
to prove the following general result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow B
be a holomorphic submersion with Kähler fibers and a Kähler base. Assume that
X
admits a Hermitian-Symplectic structure.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

X
is Kähler.

2.

There exists
[\omega]\in H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})
that restricts to a Kähler class on each fiber
F
.

3.

The variation of Hodge structure
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}
is polarizable.

4.

X
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.

The rest of this section is devoted to proving Proposition
4.1
and deriving Theorem
1.3
as its corollary.
Let
F
be any fiber of
\pi
. It is easy to see that
\chi=Q|_{F}
is a Hermitian-Symplectic form on
F
.
Because
F
is assumed to be Kähler, we have a Hodge decomposition:

H^{2}(F,\mathbb{C})=H^{2,0}(F)\oplus H^{1,1}(F)\oplus H^{0,2}(F),\quad H^{0,2}(F)=\overline{H^{2,0}(F)}.

The cohomology class
[\chi]\in H^{2}(F,\mathbb{C})
decomposes as
[\chi]=[\chi]^{0,2}+[\chi]^{1,1}+[\chi]^{0,2}
with respect to the above decomposition. On the contrast, if we denote by
\chi^{1,1}
the
(1,1)
-component of the 2-form
\chi
, then
\chi^{1,1}
is in general only
\partial\bar{\partial}
-closed but not
d
-closed.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

If
\chi
is a Hermitian-Symplectic form on a Kähler manifold
F
, then

[\chi]^{1,1}
is a Kähler class.

As pointed out in
[
14
]
, this is a consequence of deep results from
[
6
]
.
For the reader’s convenience, we provide a quick proof.

Proof.

First one shows that
[\chi]^{1,1}
is in the nef cone which is the closure of the Kähler cone. By
[
6
, Theorem 4.3]
, this is equivalent to the condition that for every irreducible analytic subset
Y\subseteq F
,
\dim=p
, and every Kähler class
[\omega_{F}]\in H^{1,1}(F,\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{Y}[\chi]^{1,1}\wedge\omega_{F}^{p-1}\geq 0.

But for reason of the types, the above integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{Y}[\chi]\wedge\omega_{F}^{p-1}=\int_{Y}\chi\wedge\omega_{F}^{p-1}=\int_{Y}\chi^{1,1}\wedge\omega_{F}^{p-1}\geq 0.

For any
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(F,\mathbb{R})
and
\epsilon
sufficiently small, the class
[\chi]+\epsilon\alpha
clearly also contains a Hermitian-Symplectic form and hence
[\chi]+\epsilon\alpha
is also contained in the nef cone. So we conclude that
[\chi]^{1,1}
is in the interior of the nef cone which is nothing but the Kähler cone.

∎

To proceed, we consider the following definition.

Definition 4.3

(see
[
10
, Definition 4.96]
)
.

A symplectic manifold
(F,\chi)
is of Lefschetz type if the multiplication by
[\chi]^{n-1}

L:=L_{[\chi]}:H^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2n-1}(F,\mathbb{R}).

(20)

is an isomorphism.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
F
be a Kähler manifold.
If
\chi
is a Hermitian-Symplectic form on
F
, then
(F,\chi)
is of Lefschetz type.

Proof.

By Poincaré duality,
H^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})
and
H^{2n-1}(F,\mathbb{R})
have the same dimension. So it is enough to prove that
L_{[\chi]}:H^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2n-1}(F,\mathbb{R})
is injective.

Because
F
is Kähler, by the Hodge decomposition and Theorem
4.2
, there exists a Kähler form
\omega_{F}
and a (closed) holomorphic 2-form
\eta
such that
[\chi]=[\omega_{F}+\eta+\bar{\eta}]
.
Moreover we have the Hodge decomposition:
H^{1}(F,\mathbb{C})=H^{1,0}(F)\oplus H^{0,1}(F)
.
So any
\alpha\in H^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})
can be represented by a sum
\xi+\bar{\xi}
where
\xi\in H^{0}(F,\Omega^{1}_{F})
is a closed holomorphic 1-form.

Assume that
L^{n-1}(\alpha)=0
. Then there exists an
(2n-2)
-form
\phi
such that:

(\omega_{F}+\eta+\bar{\eta})^{n-1}\wedge(\xi+\bar{\xi})=d\phi

From this, we get
(\omega_{F}+\eta+\bar{\eta})^{n-1}\wedge\xi\wedge\bar{\xi}=d\phi\wedge\bar{\xi}=d(\phi\wedge\bar{\xi})
.
Integrating both sides, we get:

\int_{F}(\omega_{F}+\eta+\bar{\eta})^{n-1}\wedge(\sqrt{-1}\xi\wedge\bar{\xi})=\sqrt{-1}\int_{F}d(\phi\wedge\bar{\xi})=0.

By using the positivity of
\omega_{F}
, it is easy to verify that the left-hand-side is greater than
\int_{F}\omega_{F}^{n-1}\wedge\sqrt{-1}\xi\wedge\bar{\xi}
which implies that the
L^{2}
-norm of
\xi
(with respect to
\omega_{F}
) is equal 0. So we conclude that
\xi=0
and hence
\alpha=0
.

∎

Let
(F,\chi)
be any symplectic manifold. Motivated by the Kähler case, we set:

H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{prim}}=\mathrm{Ker}\left(L_{[\chi]}^{n-1}:H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2n}(F,\mathbb{R})\right).

Then it is easy to see that there is a direct sum decomposition:

H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})=H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}[\chi]\oplus H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{prim}}.

Indeed, it is easy to verify that
\mathbb{R}[\chi]\cap H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{prim}}=\{0\}
and any
\alpha\in H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})
decomposes as

\alpha=\left(\frac{[\chi]^{n-1}\cdot\alpha}{[\chi]^{n}}[\chi]\right)+\left(\alpha-\frac{[\chi]^{n-1}\cdot\alpha}{[\chi]^{n}}[\chi]\right)\in\mathbb{R}[\chi]+H^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\mathrm{prim}}.

So we have a Lefschetz-type decomposition in degree 2 for any symplectic manifold (cf.
[
1
]
).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow B
be a holomorphic submersion. Assume that there is a closed form
Q
on
X
such that
(X_{b},Q_{b}:=Q|_{X_{b}})
is a symplectic manifold of Lefschetz type. Then any
x\in H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})
extends to a global class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By the discussion in section
2.1
,
it is enough to prove that
d_{2}:E^{0,2}_{2}\rightarrow E^{2,1}_{2}
are
d_{3}:E^{0,2}_{3}\rightarrow E^{3,0}_{3}

We will adapt Deligne’s method for proving the degeneration of Leray spectral sequence at the
E_{2}

By the same argument as in
[

Q
is closed, it induces a morphism of local systems:

L:=[Q]\cup:R^{k}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow R^{k+2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}.

(21)

L_{[Q_{b}]}=[Q_{b}]\cup

b\in B

k=1,2

Because
R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}

L\cdot H^{0}(B,R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}\cdot[Q]

d_{2}:L\cdot H^{0}(B,R^{0}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2}(B,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R})
is equal to 0. So to show
d_{2}=0

d_{2}

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

By Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
4.5
, we know that condition 1 and condition 2 are equivalent (under the Hermitian-Symplectic assumption), and it is well-known that they imply condition 3 and condition 4.

We show that condition 3 implies condition 2. We know that
[Q]\in H^{0}(B,V^{2})
is a flat section of the local system
V^{2}=R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}
with respect to the Gauss-Manin connection. If the associated variation of Hodge structure on
V^{2}
is polarized (condition 3), then by Deligne’s Hodge theory as explained in
2.3
the
(1,1)
-component of
[Q]
is also flat so that
[Q]^{1,1}\in H^{0}(B,V^{2})
. By Proposition
4.2
, we know that
[Q]^{1,1}
restricts to Kähler class on each fiber. But this means exactly condition 2.

Finally we show that condition 4 implies
\mathbf{(Condition\;I)}
of Theorem
1.1
.
If
X
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma, then by the well-known result of
[
5
]

X
admits a Hodge decomposition and the restriction morphism
f_{b}^{*}:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{2}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
is a morphism of Hodge structures.
Let
[Q]^{1,1}
be the
(1,1)
-component of
[Q]
in the Hodge decomposition of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Then
[Q]^{1,1}
is represented by a closed
(1,1)
-form and it restricts to become the
(1,1)
-component of
[Q|_{X_{b}}]\in H^{2}(X_{b},\mathbb{R})
. By Proposition
4.1
,
[Q]^{1,1}
is Kähler. So by Theorem
1.1
(or more easily by Remark
3.2
), we can construct a Kähler form on the total space
X
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume that
Q
is a Hermitian-Symplectic form on
X
.

In case 1, we know that the Kodaira-Spencer class is 0. So by the formula (
16
) we know that
\theta=0=\bar{\theta}
in the decomposition of Gauss-Manin connection in (
15
). As a consequence, the Gauss-Manin connection preserves the decomposition
\mathcal{E}^{0}(B,V^{2})=\mathcal{E}^{0}(B,V^{2})^{2,0}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{0}(B,V^{2})^{1,1}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{0}(B,V^{2})^{0,2}
. So the
(1,1)
-component of the flat section
[Q]
is also flat. By Proposition
4.2
, we know that
[Q]^{1,1}
restricts to Kähler class on each fiber. So we conclude by Proposition
4.1
.

In case 2, there is a natural polarization of
V^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}=R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{C}}=H^{1,0}\oplus H^{0,1}
. Indeed, for any
b\in X_{b}
,
\xi,\eta\in H^{1,0}(X_{b})
, define:

\langle\xi,\bar{\eta}\rangle=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X_{b}}\xi\wedge\bar{\eta}\wedge Q^{n-1}.

Then because
Q
is closed and
Q^{1,1}
is positive definite, it is easy to see that defines a polarization on
V^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
, which also induces a polarization on
V^{2}=\wedge^{2}V^{1}
. So we can conclude by Proposition
4.1
.

In both case 3 and case 4, we know that the
(1,1)
-component of
[Q]|_{X_{b}}
satisfies the condition 2 of Proposition
4.1
. We also remark that the dimension of
H^{2,0}(X_{b})
does not depend on
b\in B
by the invariance of Hodge numbers of Kähler manifolds under deformations (see
[
21
, Proposition 9.20, vI.9.3.2]
).

∎

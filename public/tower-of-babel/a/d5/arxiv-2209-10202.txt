1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
and associated norm
\left\|.\right\|
.
Throughout this paper, we assume the following assumptions:

(A1)
Q
is a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

(A2)
S:Q\rightarrow Q
is a nonexpansive mapping which means that
\left\|Sx-Sy\right\|\leq\left\|x-y\right\|
for every
x,y\in Q
.

(A3)
A:Q\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a
\nu
inverse strongly
monotone operator which means that there exists a real
\nu>0
such that

\langle Ax-Ay,x-y\rangle\geq\nu\left\|Ax-Ay\right\|^{2}~\forall x,y\in Q;

(A4)
f:Q\rightarrow Q
is a contraction with coefficient
\rho\in[0,1[,
that is

\left\|f(x)-f(y)\right\|\leq\rho\left\|x-y\right\|~\forall x,y\in Q.

We denote by
F_{ix}(S)=\{x\in Q:Sx=x\}
the set of fixed points of the
operator
S
and by
S_{VI(A,Q)}
the set of solutions of the following
variational inequality

(1.1)

\text{Find }q\in Q\text{ such that }\langle Aq,x-q\rangle\geq 0\text{ for
every }x\in Q.

It is easy to prove that the sets
F_{ix}(S)
and
S_{VI(A,Q)}
are closed
and convex subsets of
\mathcal{H}
(see Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
in
the next section); hence the set

\Omega:=F_{ix}(S)\cap S_{VI(A,Q)}

is also closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}.
Hereafter, we assume
moreover that:

(A5): The set
\Omega
is nonempty.

In this paper, we are interested in the numerical approximation of some
particular elements of
\Omega
. We recall that Takahashi and Toyoda
[
7
]
introduced the following algorithm

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}x_{n}+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),~n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

where
P_{Q}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow Q
is the metric projection from
\mathcal{H}
onto
Q
(see Lemma
2.1
for the definition. They proved that if the sequence
\{(\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n})\}
remains in a fixed compact subset of
]0,1[\times]0,2\nu[,
then any sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by the process (
1.2
) converges weakly to some element
q_{\infty}
of
\Omega.
To
overcome the drawback of the weak convergence and the non specification of
the limit point
q_{\infty}
in the algorithm (
1.2
) , Iiduka and
Tokahashi
[
4
]
have introduced in 2005 the following iterative process:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}u+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),~n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

where
u
is a fixed element of
Q.
They established that if
\{\lambda_{n}\}\in[a,b]
, with
0<a<b<2\nu
,
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in[0,1],

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}=+\infty
and
\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|+\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\right|\right)<\infty
, then
any sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by the algorithm (
1.3
) converges
strongly in
\mathcal{H}
the closed
element of
\Omega
to
u.

In 2011, Yao, Liou and Chen
[
10
]
studied two averaged version the
algorithm (
). Precisely, they of introduced the following two
algorithms

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{1}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\beta_{n}x_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})P_{Q}(\alpha_{n}u+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}\left(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}\right)),~n\geq 1;\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[l]{l}x_{1}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\beta_{n}x_{n}+(1-\beta_{n})SP_{Q}(\alpha_{n}u+(1-\alpha_{n})\left(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}\right)),~n\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

They proved that if the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{n}\}

satisfy the same assumptions as in the Theorem of Iiduka and Tokahashi and
\{\beta_{n}\}
belongs to a sub-interval
[0,b]
of
[0,1[
and satisfies
\sum_{n\geq 1}\left|\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}\right|<\infty
, then any
sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by (
1.4
) or (
1.5
) converges
strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to the closed
element of
\Omega
to
u.

In this paper, inspired by the viscosity approximation method due to A.
Moudafi
[
5
]
, we introduce the following iterative process

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\in Q\\
x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),~n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

which is a generalization of the algorithm (
1.3
). Under the same
assumptions on the sequences
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\alpha_{n}\}
in the
above convergence result of Iiduka and Tokahashi, we prove that any sequence
generated by the algorithm (
1.6
) converges strongly to
q^{\ast}
the
unique solution of the variational problem

(VP)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q^{\ast}\in\Omega,\\
\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x-q^{\ast}\rangle\leq 0,~\forall x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, we establish the strong convergence of the implicit version of the
algorithm (
1.6
). Precisely, we will prove that if
\lambda:]0,1]\rightarrow[a,b]
, with
[a,b]\subset]0,2\nu[,
then
for every
t\in]0,1]
there exists a unique
x_{t}
in
Q
such that

(1.7)

x_{t}=t~f(x_{t})+(1-t)~SP_{Q}(x_{t}-\lambda(t)Ax_{t}).

Then we show that
x_{t}
converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}

as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

The sequel of the paper is organized as follows: In the next section, we
recall some well-known results from convex analysis that will be useful in
the proof of the main results of the paper. In the third section, we
establish the strong convergence of the implicit algorithm (
1.7
). In
the fourth section, we study the strong convergence of the explicit
algorithm (
1.6
). Then we prove the stability of the process (
1.7
) under the effect of small perturbations and we apply the obtained results
to the study of a constrained optimization problem. The last section is

sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}
on the convergence rate of a particular example
of the perturbed version of the algorithm (
1.7
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some results that will be helpful in the next
sections. Most of these results can be found in any good book on convex
analysis as
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
.
Let us first recall the definition of the metric projection onto a nonempty,
closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.14]
)
.

Let
K
be a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}
. For every
x\in\mathcal{H},
there exists a unique
P_{K}(x)\in Q
such that

\left\|x-P_{K}(x)\right\|\leq\left\|x-y\right\|\ ~\forall y\in K.

The operator
P_{K}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow K
is called the metric
projection onto
K
.

The following classical properties of the projection operator
P_{K}
are
very useful.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
, Corollary 4.18]
)
.

Let
K
be a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

(1)

For every
x\in\mathcal{H},

P_{K}(x)
is the unique element of

K
which satisfies

(2.1)

\langle P_{K}(x)-x,P_{K}(x)-y\rangle\leq 0,\text{ for every }y\in K.

(2)

The operator
P_{K}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow K
is firmly
nonexpansive i.e.,

(2.2)

\langle P_{K}(x)-P_{K}(y),x-y\rangle\geq\left\|P_{K}(x)-P_{K}(y)\right\|^{2},\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

In particular

(2.3)

\left\|P_{K}(x)-P_{K}(y)\right\|\leq\left\|x-y\right\|,\text{
for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.13]
)
.

Let
C
be a closed convex and nonempty
subset of
\mathcal{H}
, and
T:C\rightarrow C
a nonexpansive mapping. Then

F_{ix}(T)=\{x\in C:T(x)=x\}
is a closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in]0,2\nu].
Then the following assertions hold true

(i)

For every
x,y\in Q,

(2.4)

\left\|(x-\lambda Ax)-(y-\lambda Ay)\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}-\lambda(2\nu-\lambda)\left\|Ax-Ay\right\|^{2}.

(ii)

The operator
\Theta_{\lambda}:=P_{Q}\circ(I-\lambda A):Q\rightarrow Q
is nonexpansive and
F_{ix}(\Theta_{\lambda})=S_{VI(A,Q)}.

(iii)

S_{VI(A,Q)}
is a closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

(i) Let
x,y\in Q.
A simple computation gives

\displaystyle\left\|(x-\lambda Ax)-(y-\lambda Ay)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}-2\lambda\langle Ax-Ay,x-y\rangle+\lambda^{2}\left\|Ax-Ay\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}-\lambda(2\nu-\lambda)\left\|Ax-Ay\right\|^{2}.

(ii) Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) yields
\Theta_{\lambda}
is
nonexpansive. Now let
q\in Q.
Clearly
q\in S_{VI(A,Q)}
if and only if

\langle q-(q-\lambda Aq),q-x\rangle\leq 0~\forall x\in Q,

which, thanks to the first assertion of Lemma
2.2
, is equivalent to
q=P_{Q}(q-\lambda Aq)=\Theta_{\lambda}(q).

The last assertion (iii) follows directly from (ii) and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

The next result is a particular case of the well-known demi-closedness
principle.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
1
, Corollary 4.18]
)
.

Let
C
be a closed convex and nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}
,
and
T:C\rightarrow C
a nonexpansive mapping. If
\{x_{n}\}
is a sequence
in
C
weakly converging to some
\bar{x}
such that
x_{n}-Tx_{n}

converges strongly to
0
in
\mathcal{H}
, then
\bar{x}\in F_{ix}(T).

The last result of this section is a powerful lemma which is a
generalization due to Xu
[
8
]
of a lemma proved by Berstrekas (
[
3
, Lemma 1.5.1]
)

Lemma 2.6
.

let
\{a_{n}\}
be a sequence of non negative real numbers such
that:

(2.5)

a_{n+1}\leq(1-\gamma_{n})a_{n}+\gamma_{n}r_{n}+\delta_{n},\text{ }n\geq 0,

where
\{\gamma_{n}\}\in[0,1]
and
\{r_{n}\}
and
\{\delta_{n}\}
are
three real sequences such that:

(1)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\gamma_{n}=+\infty;

(2)

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\delta_{n}\right|<+\infty;

(3)

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}r_{n}\leq 0.

Then the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
converges to
0.

Proof.

We give here a proof different from the original one due to Xu
[
8
]
.
Set
s_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}\delta_{k},

e_{n}=a_{n}+s_{n},
and
\beta_{n}=\max\{r_{n},0\}+s_{n}.
Using the fact that
\delta_{n}=s_{n}-s_{n+1},
we easily obtain from (
2.5
)

(2.6)

e_{n+1}\leq(1-\gamma_{n})e_{n}+\gamma_{n}\beta_{n},~n\geq 0.

Let
\varepsilon>0.
Since
\beta_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,

there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\beta_{n}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
n\geq n_{0}.
Let us suppose that
e_{n}\geq\varepsilon
for every
n\geq n_{0}.
Hence, from (
2.6
), we infer that,
for every
n\geq n_{0},

\displaystyle e_{n}-e_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}(e_{n}-\beta_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\gamma_{n},

which implies that
\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\gamma_{n}<\infty.
This is a contradiction. Then there exists
n_{1}\geq n_{0}
such that
e_{n_{1}}\leq\varepsilon.
Therefore

\displaystyle e_{n_{1}+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma_{n_{1}})\varepsilon+\gamma_{n_{1}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon.

And so on we get
e_{n}\leq\varepsilon
for every
n\geq n_{1}
. Hence
e_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
which clearly implies that
a_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
since
s_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

We close this section by proving that the problem (VP) has a
unique solution.

Lemma 2.7
.

The problem (VP) has a unique solution
q^{\ast}
. Moreover,
q^{\ast}
is
the unique fixed point of the contraction
P_{\Omega}\circ f:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us first recall that the set
\Omega
is nonempty, closed and convex
subset of
\mathcal{H}
. Then from the variational characterization of the
metric projection
P_{\Omega}
(see the first assertion of Lemma
2.2
),
the problem (VP) is equivalent to the identity
q^{\ast}=P_{\Omega}(f(q^{\ast}))
. This, thanks to Banach fixed point theorem, guarantees the
existence and the uniqueness of
q^{\ast}
since the application
P_{\Omega}\circ f:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
is clearly a contraction.
∎

3.
The convergence of the implicit algorithm (
1.7
)

The following section is devoted to the proof of the strong convergence of
the implicit algorithm (
1.7
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
a
and
b
be two reals such that
0<a<b<2\nu
and let
\lambda:]0,1[\rightarrow[a,b]
be a mapping. Then, for every
t\in]0,1[,
there exists a unique
x_{t}\in Q
such that

x_{t}=tf(x_{t})+(1-t)SP_{Q}(x_{t}-\lambda(t)Ax_{t}).

Moreover
\{x_{t}\}
converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
to
q^{\ast}
the unique solution of the variational
problem (VP).

The following simple lemma, which is an immediate consequence of the second
assertion of Lemma
2.4
, will be very useful in the proof of the
previous theorem and also in the proof of the main result of the next section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t\in]0,1]
and
\mu\in[0,2\nu].
Then the application
T_{t,\mu}:Q\rightarrow Q
defined by

T_{t,\mu}(x)=tf(x)+(1-t)SP_{Q}(x-\mu Ax),

satisfies

\left\|T_{t,\mu}(x)-T_{t,\mu}(y)\right\|\leq(1-\sigma t)\left\|x-y\right\|,~\forall x,y\in Q,

where
\sigma=1-\rho.

Let us start the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

Let
t\in]0,1].
According to Lemma
3.1
and the classical Banach fixed
point theorem, there exists a unique
x_{t}\in Q
such that
x_{t}=T_{t,\lambda(t)}(x_{t}).
Let us now prove that the family
\{x_{t}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}.
Let
q\in\Omega.
In view
of the last assertion of Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle T_{t,\lambda(t)}(q)

\displaystyle=t~f(q)+(1-t)Sq

(3.1)

\displaystyle=t~f(q)+(1-t)q

for every
t\in]0,1].
Hence, Lemma
3.1
yields

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{t,\lambda(t)}(x_{t})-T_{t,\lambda(t)}(q)\right\|+t\left\|f(q)-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\sigma t)\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|+t\left\|f(q)-q\right\|,

which implies

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|f(q)-q\right\|.

Hence
\{x_{t}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is a bounded family in
\mathcal{H}.

In the sequel, in order to simplify the notations, we will use
M
to denote
a real constant independent of
t\in]0,1]
that may change from line to
another. Moreover,
\varepsilon(t)
will simply denotes a real quantity that
converges to
0
as the variable
t
tends to
0.
By the way, let us notice
here this simple result that will be often implicitly used in the sequel:
since
\{x_{t}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is a bounded in
\mathcal{H}
, then for every
Lipschitz continuous function
g:Q\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
the family
\{g(x_{t})\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is also bounded in
\mathcal{H}
.

For
t\in]0,1],
we set
z_{t}=P_{Q}(x_{t}-\lambda(t)Ax_{t}).
Let
q\in\Omega.
Clearly, by using the classical identity

(3.2)

\left\|tu+(1-t)v\right\|^{2}\leq t\left\|u\right\|^{2}+(1-t)\left\|v\right\|^{2},\forall u,v\in\mathcal{H},

the fact that
S
and
P_{Q}
are nonexpansive operators, and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq t\left\|f(x_{t})-q\right\|^{2}+(1-t)\left\|Sz_{t}-q\right\|^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq tM+\left\|z_{t}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq tM+\left\|\left(x_{t}-\lambda(t)Ax_{t}\right)-\left(q-\lambda(t)Aq\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq tM+\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|^{2}-\lambda(t)(2\nu-\lambda(t))\left\|Ax_{t}-Aq\right\|^{2}.

We then deduce that

(3.4)

a(b-2\nu)\left\|Ax_{t}-Aq\right\|^{2}\leq tM.

Therefore, thanks to (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|z_{t}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle z_{t}-q,\left(x_{t}-\lambda(t)Ax_{t}\right)-\left(q-\lambda(t)Aq\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle z_{t}-q,x_{t}-q\rangle+\lambda(t)\left\|z_{t}-q\right\|\left\|Ax_{t}-Aq\right\|

\displaystyle=\langle z_{t}-q,x_{t}-q\rangle+\varepsilon(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|z_{t}-q\right\|^{2}+\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{t}-z_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)+\varepsilon(t).

The last inequality implies

\left\|z_{t}-q\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x_{t}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{t}-z_{t}\right\|^{2}+2\varepsilon(t).

Hence, by going back to the estimate (
3.3
), we deduce that

\left\|x_{t}-z_{t}\right\|^{2}\leq tM+2\epsilon(t),

which implies

(3.5)

x_{t}-z_{t}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

The last inequality in turn implies that

(3.6)

x_{t}-Sx_{t}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow 0^{+}

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}-Sx_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{t}-Sz_{t}\right\|+\left\|Sx_{t}-Sz_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{t,\lambda(t)}(x_{t})-Sz_{t}\right\|+\left\|Sx_{t}-Sz_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq t\left\|f(x_{t})-Sz_{t}\right\|+\left\|x_{t}-z_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq tM+\left\|x_{t}-z_{t}\right\|.

Now we are in position to prove the following key result:

(3.7)

\kappa:=\lim\sup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{t}-q^{\ast}\rangle\leq 0,

where
q^{\ast}
is the unique solution of the variational problem (VP).

From the definition of
\kappa,
there exists a sequence
\{t_{n}\}
in
]0,1]
converging to
0
such that

\kappa=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{t_{n}}-q^{\ast}\rangle.

On the other hand, since the family
\{x_{t_{n}}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is a bounded
subset of the closed and convex subset
Q
of
\mathcal{H}
, we can assume,
up to a subsequence, that
\{x_{t_{n}}\}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{H}

to some
x_{\infty}\in Q.
This fact implies

\kappa=\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{\infty}-q^{\ast}\rangle.

Therefore, in order to prove that
\kappa\leq 0,
we just need to verify that

x_{\infty}\in\Omega.
Firstly, from Lemma
2.5
and (
3.6
), we have
x_{\infty}\in F_{ix}(S).
Secondly, up to a subsequence, we can assume that
the real sequence
\{\lambda_{t_{n}}\}
converges to some real
\lambda^{\ast}
which belongs to
]0,2\nu[.
Let
\Theta_{\lambda^{\ast}}=P_{Q}\circ(I-\lambda^{\ast}A)
be the nonexpansive operator
introduced in Lemma
2.5
. Since
z_{n}=\Theta_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{t_{n}}),

we have

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t_{n}}-\Theta_{\lambda^{\ast}}(x_{t_{n}})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{t_{n}}-z_{t_{n}}\right\|+\left\|\Theta_{\lambda_{n}}(x_{t_{n}})-\Theta_{\lambda^{\ast}}(x_{t_{n}})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{t_{n}}-z_{t_{n}}\right\|+\left|\lambda_{t_{n}}-\lambda^{\ast}\right|\left\|Ax_{t_{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{t_{n}}-z_{t_{n}}\right\|+\left|\lambda_{t_{n}}-\lambda^{\ast}\right|M.

Hence, by combining (
3.5
) and Lemma
2.5
, we deduce that
x_{\infty}
is a fixed point of
\Theta_{\lambda^{\ast}}.
Thus, thanks to
the second assertion of Lemma
2.4
, we deduce that
x_{\infty}\in VI(A,Q).

The claim (
3.7
) is then proved.

Let us finally prove that
x_{t}\rightarrow q^{\ast}
in
\mathcal{H}
as
t

goes to
0^{+}.
Let
t\in]0,1].
First, from the identity (
3.1
), we have

x_{t}-q^{\ast}=u+v,

with

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=T_{t,\lambda(t)}(x_{t})-T_{t,\lambda(t)}\left(q^{\ast}\right),

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=t(f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}).

Hence, by applying the inequality

\left\|u+v\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|u\right\|^{2}+2\langle v,u+v\rangle

and Lemma
3.1
, we get the inequality

\left\|x_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}\leq(1-\sigma t)^{2}\left\|x_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+2t\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{t}-q^{\ast}\rangle

which implies

\displaystyle\left\|x_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma t}{2}\left\|x_{t}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{t}-q^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq tM+\frac{1}{\sigma}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{t}-q^{\ast}\rangle.

Hence, by letting
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and using (
3.7
), we obtain the
desired result.
∎

4.
The convergence of the explicit algorithm (
1.6
).

In this section, we study the strong convergence property of the process (
1.6
). We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in]0,1]
and
\{\lambda_{n}\}\in[0,2\nu]
two real sequences such that:

(i)

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty

(ii)

0<\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}\leq\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}<2\nu.

(iii)

\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|<\infty.

(iv)

\frac{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\right|<\infty.

Then for every initial data
x_{1}\in Q
, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated
by the iterative process

x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}),~n\geq 1,

converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}
the unique solution of the
variational inequality problem (VP).

Proof.

Since we are only interested on the asymptotic behavior of the sequence
\{x_{n}\},
we can replace hypothesis (ii) by the stronger one: there exist
two real
a
and
b
in
]0,2\nu[
such that the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is in
[a,b].

For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we set
T_{n}:=T_{\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n}}
where

T_{\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n}}
is the application defined by Lemma
3.1
.
First, we will prove that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}.
Let
q\in\Omega.
Thanks to the identity (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.1
, we
have

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}(x_{n})-T_{n}(q)\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|f(q)-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\sigma\alpha_{n})\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|+\alpha_{n}\left\|f(q)-q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|,\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|f(q)-q\right\|\}.

Hence, we deduce by induction that

\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|\leq\max\{\left\|x_{0}-q\right\|,\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|f(q)-q\right\|\},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{H}.
Hence, for every
Lipschitz function
g:Q\rightarrow\mathcal{H},
the sequence
\{g(x_{n})\}

is also bounded in
\mathcal{H}.

From hereon, as we have done in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
M
will
denotes a constant independent of
n
and
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
a real
sequence that converges to
0.

M
and
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
may change
from line to an other.

Let us now show that the sequence
\{\Delta x_{n}=x_{n+1}-x_{n}\}
converges
strongly to
0.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we clearly have

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}(x_{n})-T_{n}(x_{n-1})\right\|+\left\|T_{n}(x_{n-1})-T_{n-1}(x_{n-1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\sigma\alpha_{n})\left\|\Delta x_{n-1}\right\|+M\left[\left|\Delta\alpha_{n-1}\right|+\left|\Delta\lambda_{n-1}\right|\right],

where

\Delta\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n},

and

\Delta\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}.

Hence, by applying Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

(4.1)

\left\|\Delta x_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N},
we set
z_{n}=P_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}).

Let
q\in\Omega.
As we have proceeded in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, by using the classical identity (
3.2
) with
t=\alpha_{n}
, the fact that
S
and
P_{Q}

are nonexpansive operators, and Lemma
2.4
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\left\|f(x_{n})-q\right\|^{2}+(1-\alpha_{n})\left\|Sz_{n}-q\right\|^{2}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\left\|z_{n}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\left\|\left(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}\right)-\left(q-\lambda_{n}Aq\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|^{2}-\lambda_{n}(2\nu-\lambda_{n})\left\|Ax_{n}-Aq\right\|^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle a(2\nu-b)\left\|Ax_{n}-Aq\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{n+1}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{n}-\langle\Delta x_{n},x_{n+1}+x_{n}-2q\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+\left\|\Delta x_{n}\right\|\left\|x_{n+1}+x_{n}-2q\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{n}+M\left\|\Delta x_{n}\right\|.

Hence, thanks to (
4.1
), we deduce that

(4.3)

Ax_{n}-Aq\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, by using the fact that the operator
P_{Q}
is firmly
nonexpansive (see (
2.2
)), we get

\displaystyle\left\|z_{n}-q\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle z_{n}-q,\left(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n}\right)-\left(q-\lambda_{n}Aq\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle z_{n}-q,x_{n}-q\rangle+\lambda_{n}\left\|z_{n}-q\right\|\left\|Ax_{n}-Aq\right\|

\displaystyle=\langle z_{n}-q,x_{n}-q\rangle+\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|z_{n}-q\right\|^{2}+\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|z_{n}-x_{n}\right\|^{2}\right)+\varepsilon_{n}.

Thus, we obtain

\left\|z_{n}-q\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|z_{n}-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}.

Inserting this inequality into (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|z_{n}-x_{n}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{n}-q\right\|^{2}-\left\|x_{n+1}-q\right\|^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=-\langle\Delta x_{n},x_{n+1}+x_{n}-2q\rangle+\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq M\left\|\Delta x_{n}\right\|+\varepsilon_{n}.

Hence, by using (
4.1
), we deduce that

x_{n}-z_{n}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, by proceeding exactly as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we
first infer that

x_{n}-Sx_{n}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

then we deduce the key result:

(4.4)

\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{n}-q^{\ast}\rangle\leq 0.

Let us finally prove that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N},

x_{n+1}-q^{\ast}=T_{n}(x_{n})-T_{n}(q^{\ast})+\alpha_{n}(f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}).

Hence, by using the inequality

\left\|u+v\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|u\right\|^{2}+2\langle v,u+v\rangle,

with

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=T_{n}(x_{n})-T_{n}(q^{\ast}),

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}(f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{n}(x_{n})-T_{n}(q^{\ast})\right\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{n+1}-q^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(1-\sigma\alpha_{n})^{2}\left\|x_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha_{n}\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{n+1}-q^{\ast}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\sigma\alpha_{n})\left\|x_{n}-q^{\ast}\right\|^{2}+\alpha_{n}\left[2\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x_{n+1}-q^{\ast}\rangle+M\alpha_{n}\right].

Therefore, by applying Lemma
2.6
and using the key result (
4.4
),
we deduce that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}

to
q^{\ast}.
The proof is then achieved.
∎

Now we prove that the algorithm (
1.6
) is stable under small
perturbations. Precisely, we establish the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in]0,1]
,
\{\lambda_{n}\}\in[0,2\nu]
and
\{e_{n}\}\in\mathcal{H}
three sequences such that:

(i)

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty

(ii)

0<\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}\leq\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}<2\nu.

(iii)

\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0
or

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|<\infty.

(iv)

\frac{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0

or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\right|<\infty.

(v)

\frac{\left\|e_{n}\right\|}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0

or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left\|e_{n}\right\|<\infty.

Then for every initial data
x_{1}\in Q
, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated
by the iterative process

(4.5)

x_{n+1}=P_{Q}(\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ax_{n})+e_{n}),~n\geq 1,

converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}
the unique solution of
the variational inequality problem (VP)

Proof.

Let
\{y_{n}\}
the sequence defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y_{1}=x_{1},\\
y_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(y_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(y_{n}-\lambda_{n}Ay_{n}),~n\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Since the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
is in
Q,

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-P_{Q}(y_{n+1})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n}}(x_{n})-T_{\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n}}(y_{n})\right\|+\left\|e_{n}\right\|,

where
T_{\alpha_{n},\lambda_{n}}
is the operator defined in Lemma
3.1
.
Hence, for every
n\geq 1,

\left\|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}\right\|\leq(1-\sigma\alpha_{n})\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|+\left\|e_{n}\right\|,

where
\sigma=1-\rho.
Therefore, by invoking Lemma
2.5
., we deduce that

\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

which implies that
\{x_{n}\}
converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}
since, from Theorem
4.1
, the sequence
\{y_{n}\}

converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}.

∎

As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2
, we have the following
result which improves and generalizes (
[
9
, Theorem 5.2]
).

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\varphi:Q\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
be
C^{1}
convex function such
that its gradient
\nabla\varphi:Q\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is
Lipschitz with coefficient
L>0.
We assume that the set
F_{ix}(S)\cap\arg\min_{Q}\varphi
is nonempty, where
\arg\min_{Q}\varphi=\{q\in Q:\varphi(q)\leq\varphi(x)~\forall x\in Q\}.
Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in]0,1]
,
\{\lambda_{n}\}\in[0,\frac{2}{L}]
and
\{e_{n}\}\in\mathcal{H}

three sequences such that:

(i)

\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{n}=+\infty

(ii)

0<\lim\inf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}\leq\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda_{n}<\frac{2}{L}.

(iii)

\frac{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0
or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|<\infty.

(iv)

\frac{\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0

or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\right|<\infty

(v)

\frac{\left\|e_{n}\right\|}{\alpha_{n}}\rightarrow 0

or
\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left\|e_{n}\right\|<\infty.

Then for every
z_{1}\in Q
the sequence
\{z_{n}\}
defined iteratively by

(4.6)

z_{n+1}=P_{Q}(\alpha_{n}f(z_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})SP_{Q}(z_{n}-\lambda_{n}\nabla\varphi(z_{n}))+e_{n})),~n\geq 1,

converges strongly in
\mathcal{H}
to
q^{\ast}
the unique element of
F_{ix}(S)\cap\arg\min_{Q}\varphi
satisfying the variational inequality

(4.7)

\langle f(q^{\ast})-q^{\ast},x-q^{\ast}\rangle\leq 0

for all
x\in F_{ix}(S)\cap\arg\min_{Q}\varphi.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from Theorem
4.2
. In fact, according to
the famous Baillon-Haddad Theorem (
[
6
, Theorem 3.13]
), the operator
\nabla\varphi:Q\rightarrow

\mathcal{H}
is
\frac{1}{L}
inverse
strongly monotone and , from the classical varational characterization of
constrained convex problem solutions (
[
2
, Theorem 3.13]
), we have

S_{VI(\nabla\varphi,Q)}=\{q\in Q:\langle\nabla\varphi(q),x-q\rangle\geq 0~\forall x\in Q\}=\arg\min_{Q}\varphi.

∎

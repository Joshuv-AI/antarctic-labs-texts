Federico Sestito, Sara Vitali, Paula Jofre, Kim A. Venn, David S. Aguado, Claudia Aguilera-Gómez, Anke Ardern-Arentsen, Danielle de Brito Silva, Raymond Carlberg, Camilla J. L. Abstract
The most metal-poor stars provide valuable insights into the early chemical enrichment history of a system, carrying the chemical imprints of the first generations of supernovae. The most metal-poor region of the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy remains inadequately observed and characterised. To date, only ∼ 4 \sim 4 stars with [ Fe / H ] < − 2.0 {\rm[Fe/H]}<-2.0 have been chemically analysed with high-resolution spectroscopy. In this study, we present the most extensive chemical abundance analysis of 12 low-metallicity stars with metallicities down to [ Fe / H ] = − 3.26 {\rm[Fe/H]}=-3.26 and located in the main body of Sagittarius. These targets, selected from the Pristine Inner Galaxy Survey, were observed using the MIKE high-resolution spectrograph at the Magellan-Clay telescope, which allowed us to measure up to 17 chemical species. The chemical composition of these stars reflects the imprint of a variety of type II supernovae (SNe II). A combination of low- to intermediate-mass high-energy SNe and hypernovae ( ∼ 10 − 70 ​ M ⊙ \sim 10-70{\rm\,M_{\odot}} ) is required to account for the abundance patterns of the lighter elements up to the Fe-peak. The trend of the heavy elements suggests the involvement of compact binary merger events and fast-rotating (up to ∼ 300 ​ km ​ s − 1 \sim 300{\rm\,km\ s^{-1}} ) intermediate-mass to massive metal-poor stars ( ∼ 25 − 120 ​ M ⊙ \sim 25-120{\rm\,M_{\odot}} ) that are the sources of rapid and slow processes, respectively. Additionally, asymptotic giant branch stars contribute to a wide dispersion of [Ba/Mg] and [Ba/Eu]. The absence of an α − \alpha- knee in our data indicates that type Ia supernovae did not contribute in the very metal-poor region ( [ Fe / H ] ≤ − 2.0 {\rm[Fe/H]}\leq-2.0 ). However, they might have started to pollute the interstellar medium at [ Fe / H ] > − 2.0 {\rm[Fe/H]}>-2.0 , given the relatively low [Co/Fe] in this metallicity region.

Introduction

The formation and evolution of galaxies is significantly influenced by the merging of systems and the ingestion of smaller galaxies into larger ones
(
White & Rees 1978
;
Frenk et al. 1988
;
Navarro et al. 1997
)
. The Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf galaxy
(
Ibata et al. 1994
)
serves as an ideal laboratory for testing models of chemical evolution and hierarchical accretion dynamics. The system is estimated to have experienced its first in-fall about
5
Gyr ago
(
Ruiz-Lara et al. 2020
, e.g. )
, and it is located approximately 26.5 kpc away from us towards the inner Galactic regions
(
Vasiliev & Belokurov 2020
)
. Sgr represents the third most massive satellite galaxy in the Local Group (total mass of
\sim 4.8\cdot 10^{8}{\rm\,M_{\odot}}
), following only the two Magellanic Clouds
(
Monaco et al. 2004
;
McConnachie 2012
;
Vasiliev & Belokurov 2020
, e.g. )
.

As it is being tidally stripped by the Milky Way (MW), the system is now visible with its core and two stellar streams
(
Ibata et al. 1994
;
Mateo 1998
;
Majewski et al. 2003
;
Law & Majewski 2010
;
Belokurov et al. 2014
)
, dragging with it several globular clusters close to its core (M54) and to the stellar streams
(
Sbordone et al. 2007
;
Mucciarelli et al. 2017
, Terzan 7, Terzan 8, Arp 2, and Palomar 12,)
. Several studies have investigated its prolonged and complex star formation history (SFH), characterised by multiple star formation episodes, employing various techniques such as high-resolution spectroscopy
(
Bonifacio et al. 2000
;
Monaco et al. 2005
;
Chou et al. 2007
;
McWilliam et al. 2013
;
Hansen et al. 2018
;
Hayes et al. 2020
;
Hasselquist et al. 2017
;
Hasselquist et al. 2021
, e.g. )
and photometric techniques
(
Bellazzini et al. 1999
;
Layden & Sarajedini 2000
;
Siegel et al. 2007
;
Vitali et al. 2022
, e.g. )
, revealing the presence of at least four distinct stellar populations
(
Siegel et al. 2007
)
. According to the work of
Siegel et al. 2007
these populations are divided into the oldest and most metal-poor stars (with
{\rm[Fe/H]}\lesssim-1.0
and ages exceeding 10 Gyr), the intermediate group with ages between 4 to 8 Gyr and
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim-0.6
, a young population of
\sim 2.5
Gyr old and
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim-0.1
, and the youngest stars exhibiting super-solar metallicities (
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim+0.5
,
<2
Gyr).

Sgr’s core is dominated by metal-rich stars due to the recent star formation
(
Siegel et al. 2007
)
and to the fact that the oldest and metal-poor population has been preferentially stripped by tidal perturbations
(
Monaco et al. 2005
;
Monaco et al. 2007
;
Carlin et al. 2018
;
Ramos et al. 2022
)
. Additionally, Sgr stars overlap in the colour-magnitude diagram with the Galactic bulge
(
Monaco et al. 2005
;
Mucciarelli et al. 2017
)
, hence exploring its most metal-poor tail has posed significant challenges so far. To date, only approximately four very metal-poor stars (VMPs,
{\rm[Fe/H]}\leq-2.0
) have been analysed with high-resolution spectroscopy, providing chemical abundances for various elements
(
Hansen et al. 2018
)
, while
Chiti & Frebel 2019
and
Chiti et al. 2020
measured only metallicities and carbonicities ([C/Fe]) for 11 VMPs. On the other hand, the chemo-dynamical properties of the metal-poor population of Sgr has only been studied in its stellar streams, with a large effort by APOGEE
(
Hayes et al. 2020
)
and by the H3 survey
(
Johnson et al. 2020
)
. Consequently, three decades after its discovery
(
Ibata et al. 1994
)
, the VMP tail of Sgr’s core remains largely unexplored. Yet, this metallicity region is crucial to gain insights into the early chemical enrichment of Sgr, as these stars represent relics of the system’s initial stellar population.

The synergy between the exquisite astrometry and photometry from
Gaia

(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016
;
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2021
;
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023
)
and metal-poor dedicated photometric surveys such as Pristine
(
Starkenburg et al. 2017
;
Martin et al. 2023
)
offers the most efficient way of probing the most metal-poor tail of Sgr. While Pristine primarily focusses on the Galactic halo
(
Starkenburg et al. 2018
;
Bonifacio et al. 2019
;
Aguado et al. 2019
;
Venn et al. 2020
;
Kielty et al. 2021
;
Lucchesi et al. 2022
;
Martin et al. 2022
;
Yuan et al. 2022
, see spectroscopic works from)
, its sub-survey, the Pristine Inner Galaxy Survey (PIGS), targets the VMP candidates towards the Galactic bulge
(
Arentsen et al. 2020b
;
Ardern-Arentsen et al. 2024
;
Mashonkina et al. 2023
;
Sestito et al. 2023b
;
Sestito et al. 2024b
)
and Sgr
(
Vitali et al. 2022
)
. Both surveys use a narrow-band
\rm Ca\,H\&K
filter, sensitive to metallicity, to derive photometric metallicities. The subsequent medium-to-high-resolution spectroscopic follow-up of PIGS VMP candidates proved the powerful efficiency of the Ca HK filter even in high-extinction regions
(
Arentsen et al. 2020a
)
.

Recently,
Vitali et al. 2022
, thanks to the metallicity-sensitive photometry obtained from the Pristine Ca HK filter, investigated the metallicity distribution of
\sim 50,000
Sgr candidate members as a function of their spatial position.
This study, encompassing the largest sample of VMP candidate members in Sgr (
\sim 1200
stars), revealed a negative photometric metallicity gradient extending up to
\sim 5.5{\rm\,kpc}
from Sgr’s centre. This metallicity gradient has been recently quantified to be
-2.48\lesssim\nabla\rm{[M/H]}(10^{-2})\lesssim-2.02
dex deg
-1

(
Cunningham et al. 2024
)
. The metallicity gradient suggests outside-in star formation, indicating that metal-poor stars formed throughout the system, whereas more metal-rich stars are concentrated in the inner regions. Similar observational features have been observed in other classical dwarf galaxies
(
Tolstoy et al. 2004
;
Battaglia et al. 2008
;
Zhang et al. 2012
;
Sestito et al. 2023a
;
Sestito et al. 2023c
;
Tolstoy et al. 2023
, e.g. )
and modelled with cosmological simulations
(
Revaz & Jablonka 2018
, e.g. )
.

This work constitutes the most extensive chemical analysis of VMPs in Sgr to date. Stars have been selected within PIGS from the study of
Vitali et al. 2022
and observed with the MIKE high-resolution spectrograph at the
Magellan-Clay
Telescope
(
Bernstein et al. 2003
)
. Target selection is outlined in Section
2
, while Section
3
describes the model atmosphere analysis. A comparison with the metallicities and radial velocity from the PIGS medium-resolution analysis is outlined in Section
4
. The results and discussion on the chemical properties of the VMP tail of Sgr are reported in Section
5
. Conclusions are summarised in Section
6
.

2.1
Target selection from the Pristine Inner Galaxy Survey

A low- and medium-resolution spectroscopic follow-up was conducted for 13,000 PIGS very metal-poor candidates
(
Arentsen et al. 2020a
)

Saunders et al. 2004
;
Lewis et al. 2002
;
Sharp et al. 2006

(
Vitali et al. 2022
;
Ardern-Arentsen et al. 2024
)
. From their observed spectra, metallicities, radial velocities, carbonicities, and stellar parameters were derived using spectrum fitting techniques, employing
FERRE
1

1

http://github.com/callendeprieto/ferre

(
Allende Prieto et al. 2006
;
Aguado et al. 2017

The targets for this study were chosen from the PIGS/AAT spectroscopic catalogue, ensuring their Sgr membership based on their
Gaia

Vitali et al. 2022
. Briefly, this selection consists in
Gaia
stars with reduced proper motion within a radius of
0.6

-1
as in
Vasiliev & Belokurov 2020
and radial velocities in the range
100-200{\rm\,km\ s^{-1}}

(
Ibata et al. 1994
;
Bellazzini et al. 2008
, e.g. )
. Additionally, VMP stars with
{\rm[Fe/H]}_{\rm AAT}\leq-2.0
were selected from the Sgr core, excluding members of the nearby nuclear star cluster M54. The limiting magnitude was set to
G=16
mag. Subsequently, stars for which their AAT spectrum has S/N
>50

Candidate members of Sgr in the PIGS photometric footprint (grey dots) identified as in

2.2
Observations and spectra reduction

The observations were conducted over two runs using Chilean time spanning from 2022 to 2023 (CN2022A-30, CN2023A-62 P.I.: S. Vitali), utilising the MIKE echelle spectrograph
(
Bernstein et al. 2003
)
installed on the
Magellan-Clay
telescope. The use of the 0.7” slit and 2x2 binning ensured a resolving power of
R\sim 35,000
in the blue and
R\sim 28,000
in the red. While the wavelength range of the MIKE spectra covers the range
\sim 3500-9000
Å, the signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) restricts the usable spectra to wavelengths
>3850
Å.

Data reduction was performed utilising the default
CarPy
pipeline
(
Kelson et al. 2000
;
Kelson 2003
)
, which was tailored for MIKE spectra
2
2

2

https://code.obs.carnegiescience.edu/mike
. Following reduction, individual spectral orders were extracted, merged, flux normalised, and subsequently corrected for barycentric and radial velocities. Radial velocities are measured cross-correlating the observed normalised spectra with metal-poor templates generated from
OSMARCS
stellar atmosphere models
(
Gustafsson et al. 2008
;
Plez 2012
)
.

Model atmosphere analysis

3.1
Stellar parameters

The effective temperature and surface gravity were determined using the methodology outlined in details by
Sestito et al. 2023b
. In summary, the effective temperature was derived using a colour-temperature relation
(
Mucciarelli et al. 2021
)
, akin to the Infrared Flux Method
(
González Hernández & Bonifacio 2009
, e.g. )
, adapted to the
Gaia
DR3 photometry. The surface gravity was computed using the Stefan-Boltzmann equation
(
Kraft & Ivans 2003
;
Venn et al. 2017
, e.g. )
, and an iterative process between effective temperature (
{\rm\,T_{eff}}
) and surface gravity (logg) was employed. The surface gravity depends on the heliocentric distance of these stars, which is assumed to be
26.5\pm 2.5{\rm\,kpc}
as they are located in Sgr
(
Vasiliev & Belokurov 2020
)
.
Gaia
photometric magnitudes are de-reddened using the 2D dust map from
Schlegel et al. 1998
and updated by
Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011
. Then, the
Gaia
extinction coefficients are derived using
\rm A_{V}/E(B-V)=3.1

(
Schultz & Wiemer 1975
)
and the
\rm A_{G}/A_{V}=0.85926
,
\rm A_{BP}/A_{V}=1.06794
,
\rm A_{RP}/A_{V}=0.65199
relations
(
Marigo et al. 2008
;
Evans et al. 2018
)
. Uncertainties associated with the stellar parameters were determined through Monte Carlo simulations applied to the input parameters. Although extinction towards Sgr has large values (see Table
3
), photometric effective temperatures provide a flat relation within the uncertainties between the Fe
i
lines abundances in Local Thermodynamic Equilibrium (LTE) and the excitation potential. Photometric effective temperatures are usually preferred over spectroscopic estimates as they are not affected by non-LTE effects
(
Frebel et al. 2013
;
Ezzeddine et al. 2020
)
.

The Kiel diagram is displayed in Figure
3
with a very metal-poor isochrones from Padova
(
Bressan et al. 2012
, dark olive dashed line,)
and MESA/MIST
(
Dotter 2016
;
Choi et al. 2016
, black dashed lines)
as references. All the stars in this work are giants with effective temperature
4300<T_{\rm eff}<4700
K and surface gravity
0.8<\rm{\log g}<1.4
. Table
4
reports the effective temperature and surface gravity used in this work.

3.2
Spectral lines and atomic data

\sim 4300

, Si
i
, Ca
i
, Ti
i
and Ti
ii

, Al
i
, K

, and Sc

, Mn
i
, Co

, and Zn

, Ba
ii
, and Eu

3.3

MOOG

Sgr stars (black markers) are selected from various spectroscopic studies, with observations targeting stars with
{\rm[Fe/H]}\lesssim-1.1
. As clearly shown in Figure
6
, detailed chemical abundance analyses for VMP in Sgr are scarce and often limited to few atomic species.

The only study presenting a detailed chemical abundance analysis of VMP stars in this system and only for a restricted set of species (C, Ca, Co, Sr, Ba, La, Ce, Nd, Eu, Dy, Pb, and Th) is
Hansen et al. 2018
. Their sample comprises 13 stars (black circles), with only 4 being VMP (
{\rm[Fe/H]}\lesssim-2.0
) and just 2 falling within the range of
-3.0<{\rm[Fe/H]}<-2.5
. The authors suggest that a mixture of low- and high-mass Asymptotic Giant Branch (AGB) stars and supernovae is necessary to explain the observed chemical abundances
(
Hansen et al. 2018
)
. The measured level of [Ca/Fe] has been attributed by
Hansen et al. 2018
to the presence of massive supernovae that enriched the early interstellar medium of Sgr before its gas was lost.

Another two studies at intermediate-resolution,
Chiti & Frebel 2019
and
Chiti et al. 2020
, provide only [Fe/H] and [C/Fe] for 4 and 18 stars (black squares), respectively. The former includes 2 stars in the range
-2.5<{\rm[Fe/H]}<-2.0
and none below, while the latter consists of 9 VMPs, including one extremely metal-poor star (EMP,
{\rm[Fe/H]}<-3.0
). They found that none of their observed stars is Carbon-enhanced (CEMP), which contrasts with the fraction of CEMP stars observed in the Milky Way halo
(
Arentsen et al. 2022
, and references therein)
. However, their results might be biased against CEMPs, since these stars can appear more metal-rich if selected with photometric metallicities as discussed in
Da Costa et al. 2019
for the SkyMapper filter.

Data from the PIGS medium-resolution campaign (PIGS/AAT, open coral circles) are added as selected in
Sestito et al. 2024a
. This sample consists of 356 stars with
{\rm[Fe/H]}\leq-1.5
and measured [C/Fe]. Among this sample, the PIGS/AAT [C/Fe] ratios indicate the presence of 3 CEMP stars, likely due to binarity. While PIGS photometry in Sgr is biased against CEMP stars, C-normal stars should not be affected by any selection biases.
Sestito et al. 2024a
discusses that the level of Carbon in Sgr is similar to the overall distribution of the other classical dwarf galaxies and lower than the level of the inner Galaxy and the MW halo. As also discussed by
Vanni et al. 2023
, this disparity is attributed to the higher efficiency of the interstellar medium (ISM) in DGs in retaining the ejected yields from the more energetic SNe events, which would produce more Fe than C, vs the lower efficiency in the smaller building blocks of the MW. This effect would also produce a lower CEMP-no fraction in DGs
(
Lucchesi et al. 2024
)
, that is CEMP linked to the supernovae from the First stars.

Only three stars from
Monaco et al. 2005
are included, as they have metallicities falling within the range of
-1.5<{\rm[Fe/H]}<-1.1
, while the remainder have higher metallicities.
Mucciarelli et al. 2017
analysed 235 Sgr stars, however only 124 have
{\rm[Fe/H]}\lesssim-1.1
, with one being VMP (
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim 2.18
). These works (black circles) provide measurements only for [Mg, Ca, Ti/Fe]. The stars analysed in
Mucciarelli et al. 2017
are likely members of the massive globular cluster M 54 and the central nucleus of the galaxy (Sgr, N).

The data from Sgr exhibit good agreement in the [X/Fe] ratios, with stars from the literature being more metal-rich than our sample. Notably, the [C/Fe] ratios from
Hansen et al. 2018
are markedly lower than those from other datasets. This discrepancy is unlikely due to different populations in Sgr or stochasticity in the mixture of Type II supernovae (SNe II); rather, it is more likely attributable to different assumptions on the stellar atmosphere models, atomic data, lines list, and the
\rm{{}^{12}C/^{13}C}
isotopic ratio.

The general trend of the [X/Fe] ratios is similar between Sgr and MW halo stars for most elements. However, [Al/Fe] appears to be higher in Sgr than in MW halo stars at the same metallicity (both datasets in LTE), with the latter generally exhibiting sub-solar values in the VMP region. Al is dispersed in the ISM by SNe II and its yield is metallicity-dependent
(
Woosley & Weaver 1995
;
Nomoto et al. 2013
)
. Additionally, the quantity of Al production is strongly influenced by the initial content of C and N in the gas cloud that formed the stars
(
Kobayashi et al. 2006
)
, hence, Al should correlate with C
+
N
(
Hawkins et al. 2015
;
Das et al. 2020
, see also)
. Unfortunately, N is not measurable in our spectra, whilst [C/Fe] is measured only in 3 VMPs for which [Al/Fe] is also available, making it difficult to establish any correlation in this sample. Another potential contributor to Al production is from AGBs
(
Nomoto et al. 2013
)
, a topic discussed further in Section
5.3
.

Conversely, [Mn/Fe] and [Co/Fe] in Sgr are lower than the values observed in the MW halo. The Sgr’s deficiency in Mn and Co has been previously noted in various works at higher metallicities
(
McWilliam et al. 2003
;
McWilliam et al. 2013
;
Hasselquist et al. 2017
)
. The level of Mn reflects the different star formation histories among different systems, and its deficiency has also been found in various classical DGs
(
North et al. 2012
, e.g. )
. The lower Mn and Co abundance in Sgr compared to the MW halo has been attributed to the presence of yields from mass-dependent SNe II
(
Hasselquist et al. 2017
)
. The lower mass of SNe II results in a lower [Mn, Co/Fe] in the subsequent generation of stars
(
Chieffi & Limongi 2004
)
. Additionally,
Hasselquist et al. 2017
pointed out that theoretical yields cannot fully reproduce the [Co/Fe] pattern, however, Co can also be produced with a lower ratio than Fe in SNe Ia, leading to a decrease in the [Co/Fe] ratio.

The presence of SNe Ia would produce a net decrease in the distribution of the alpha-elements’ [X/Fe] ratios, resulting in the so-called
\alpha-
knee. The absence of an
\alpha-
knee in Sgr is evident for
{\rm[Fe/H]}<-2.0
, while it is challenging to exclude it at higher metallicities due to data limitations. However, the relatively low [Co/Fe] ratio at
{\rm[Fe/H]}>-2.0
might suggest a possible contribution from SNe Ia. This would imply a contribution of SNe Ia at a lower metallicity than what previously found,
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim-1.0

(
Hasselquist et al. 2017
)
. The metallicity at which such a feature appears provides insights into the system’s efficiency in retaining metals in its interstellar medium
(
Matteucci 2003
;
Venn et al. 2004
;
Tolstoy et al. 2009
, e.g. )
. Other classical dwarfs exhibit an
\alpha-
knee in the range
-2.1<{\rm[Fe/H]}<-1.5
, depending on the
\alpha
used to trace the knee
(
Reichert et al. 2020
;
Sestito et al. 2023c
)
. Recently,
Sestito et al. 2023c
precisely measured the
\alpha-
knee for Ursa Minor (UMi) using APOGEE DR17 data, showing in that case that the knee occurs at
{\rm[Fe/H]}\sim-2.1
. The absence of the knee in Sgr at VMP metallicities suggests slightly higher enrichment efficiencies than UMi, while we cannot draw a more firm comparison from this work with the other DGs. This is because of the lack of data between
-2.0\lesssim{\rm[Fe/H]}\lesssim-1.0
. This chemical feature will be further investigated in Vitali et al. (in prep.).

The Ba panel in Figure
6
shows that there is one Ba-rich star ([Ba/Fe]
\sim 1.2
), P185855
-
301522. According to the PIGS/AAT medium-resolution observations
(

, this star qualifies as a Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor (CEMP,
\rm{[C/Fe]}=+0.97\pm 0.24

(

\sim 15{\rm\,km\ s^{-1}}

The large dispersion might suggest a partial contribution from delayed r-process sources, such as compact binary mergers. In the Section below, we discuss the possible SNe II contributions using a best fit tool for theoretical yields, including compact binary events.

5.4

Ten models were chosen, encompassing a wide variety of supernova events, including faint SNe, core-collapse, high-energy, hypernovae, rotating massive stars, compact binary mergers, pair-instability SNe, and mixing both Population III and II stars.

It is important to note that our interpretation of the supernova events from

is more qualitative, rather than quantitative, as there may be systematic differences in the models across various theoretical assumptions and complexities in the mixing of supernova ejecta into the interstellar medium of massive systems as DGs. Additionally, it is worth mentioning that
StarFit
lacks yields from Type Ia supernovae and AGBs. The presence of SNe Ia will be investigated in a separate paper focusing on observations of more metal-rich stars (Vitali et al., in prep.).

The chemical composition of the stars in Sgr can be explained by a complex mixing of various SNe II events. Both the LTE and the NLTE case provide the same qualitative picture. The best fits (solid lines) for the three stars provided by
StarFit

-

To explain the level of the heavy elements, compact binary merger events are needed
(
Just et al. 2015
)
. This kind of delayed event is needed to likely account for the spread in the [Eu/Mg] ratio as shown in Figure

. Additionally, fast-rotating intermediate-mass and massive metal-poor stars

\sim 25-120{\rm\,M_{\odot}}

,)

).

(

\sim 10-70{\rm\,M_{\odot}}

are needed.

6
Conclusions

The very metal-poor region of the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy has so far been poorly investigated. In this work, we provide the first and most detailed chemical abundance analysis for 12 stars, shedding light on the chemical evolution of this system. The targets were previously observed with AAT medium-resolution within the Pristine Inner Galaxy survey
(
Arentsen et al. 2020a
)
. Precise radial velocities and chemical abundances of up to 17 species are measured from the high-resolution spectra of the MIKE spectrograph at the
Magellan-Clay
telescope. We find that:

1.

RVs and metallicities from this work are in good agreement with the previous AAT analysis (see Figure
4
).

2.

Only the RV of one star, P185855
-
301522, largely differs by
\sim 15{\rm\,km\ s^{-1}}
from the previous measurement. Given its [C, Ba/Fe] ratios, this star is classified as CEMP-s and likely in a binary system.

3.

The number of stars in the core of Sgr with
{\rm[Fe/H]}\leq-2.5
is more than doubled. So far only 5-6 stars were analysed providing RVs, metallicities and carbonicities
(
Hansen et al. 2018
;
Chiti et al. 2018
;
Chiti et al. 2020
)
, if PIGS/AAT are excluded. Out of the 12 stars here analysed, 9 have
{\rm[Fe/H]}\leq-2.5
(see Figure
5
).

4.

The chemical abundances of Sgr do not differ from the MW halo distribution for the majority of the species analysed in this work (see Figure
6
). The exceptions are Mn and Co, which are at the low-end of the MW trend, while the level of Al is higher than the one in the MW. These differences can be explained by the mass- and metallicity-dependent SNe II yields, and they are common in classical DGs.

5.

The abundances derived in this work are compatible with the trend of Sgr stars from the literature at higher metallicities (see Figure
6
).

6.

There is no detection of an
\alpha-
knee in the VMP region, which implies a lack of SNe Ia. However, the low [Co/Fe] at
{\rm[Fe/H]}>-2.0
in comparison with the MW halo trend might also suggest a contribution from SNe Ia. A coming PIGS paper (Vitali et al., in prep.) will investigate higher metallicities to better characterise the presence of type Ia supernovae in Sgr.

7.

The distribution of [Sr, Ba/Fe] and of the [Sr/Ba] in Sgr is similar as in other massive classical DGs (see Figure
7
). The wide spread in [Sr/Ba] is indicative that both rapid- and slow-process channels are present in the chemical history of the system.

8.

The wide spread in [Ba/Mg] as a function of [Mg/H] and in [Ba/Eu] vs[Fe/H] are indicative that asymptotic giant branch stars polluted the interstellar medium of the system (see Figures
8
and
9
), as in other classical DGs.

9.

The chemical trend of these Sgr stars can be fitted by the mixture of multiple type II supernovae (see Figure
11
). In particular, to reproduce the lighter elements up to the Fe-peak, low- to intermediate-mass high-energy SNe and hypernovae (
\sim 10-70{\rm\,M_{\odot}}
) are needed. The trend of the heavy elements is explained by the presence of compact binary merger events (see also Figures
10
) and fast-rotating (up to
\sim 300{\rm\,km\ s^{-1}}
) intermediate-mass to massive metal-poor stars (
\sim 25-120{\rm\,M_{\odot}}
), sources of rapid- and slow-processes, respectively.

Altogether, this work provides an unprecedented detailed view of the very metal-poor population in Sgr, and early chemical enrichment of dwarf galaxies in general. A coming PIGS paper will investigate the more metal-rich population of Sgr (Vitali et al., in prep.), exploring the contribution from type Ia supernovae, while future spectroscopic surveys, for example 4DWARFS
(
Skúladóttir et al. 2023
)
, will also be dedicated to dissecting the chemical properties of Sgr, its core and its stellar streams. It will be interesting to investigate the various populations inside this system and try to understand its formation and chemo-dynamical evolution.

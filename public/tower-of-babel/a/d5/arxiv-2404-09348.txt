Multifractal Analysis of F-Exponents for Finitely Irreducible Conformal Graph Directed Markov Systems
Nathan Dalaklis

Abstract
Consider a conformal graph directed Markov system (CGDMS) with a finitely irreducible symbolic representation over a countable alphabet and its corresponding limit set. Under a mild condition on the system, we give a multifractal analysis of level sets of Birkhoff averages with respect to Hausdorff dimension for a large family of functions. In the process of the development of this multifractal theory for finitely irreducible CGDMS, we repair, extend, and generalize previous analysis done for the Gauss Map. We conclude with application of these results to a few examples in the case of both finite and countably infinite alphabets.

Introduction

The study of Birkhoff averages of a
\mu
-measure preserving dynamical system may be viewed as the study of the exceptional sets to G.D. Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem from 1931
[
4
]
with respect to
\mu
. A short and elegant proof of Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem is given by Katok and Hasselblatt
[
16
, Theorem 4.1.2]
. Versions of the statement can also be found in
[
24
, Theorem 8.2.11, Theorem 8.2.12, and Corollary 8.2.14]
. Given a dynamical system
T:X\to X
with limit set
J
and
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
a Hölder potential or a bounded observable, a multifractal consideration of these averages concerns itself with sets of the form

J(\xi)=\left\{x\in J\;\bigg|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g((T^{n}(x))=\xi\right\}

and

J^{\prime}=\left\{x\in J\;\bigg|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}g(T^{n}(x))\text{ does not exist}\right\}

where
\xi\in(\xi_{\min}(g),\xi_{\max}(g))
with one or both of
\xi_{\min}(g)
and
\xi_{\max}(g)
are potentially infinite. When collected together these level sets for which
J(\xi)\neq\emptyset
along with the exceptional set form a partition of the limit set
J
called the multifractal decomposition of
J
by Birkhoff averages with respect to
g
. The resulting spectrum function
t:(\xi_{\min}(g),\xi_{\max}(g))\to[0,HD(J)]
defined by

t(\xi)=HD(J(\xi))

is then studied. Such multifractal analyses have been preformed for a variety of particular maps and general scenarios. See
[
1
,
8
,
13
,
15
,
21
,
23
]
and related multifractal analyses in
[
7
,
12
,
17
]
.

These multifractal analyses describe the geometric structure of Birkhoff averages by way of Hausdorff dimension. Such problems go back to Besicovitch
[
2
]
who looked at base
2
expansions for which the corresponding dynamical system is the doubling map
T:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. In this case, one may remove the dyadic rationals from the analysis as they have Hausdorff Dimension
0
, and so, we may think of
J
as the set of dyadic irrationals. A similar conversion to the dynamical systems setting where a negligible set may be removed from the analysis can be formulated for other number theoretic maps like other whole-number-base expansions, Lüroth expansions, and complex continued fractions among others.

The analysis here collects together many of these ideas by studying these Birkhoff decompositions in the general setting of finitely irreducible conformal graph directed Markov systems (CGDMS) under certain properties. Though initially defined in
[
20
]
and further developed in
[
19
]
, an updated (and quite detailed) introduction to such systems can be found in
[

]
.

1.1
Main Results

The multifractal theory that follows generalizes, extends, and develops the method used by Fan et al.
[
8
]
which preformed the analysis for the family
-F=\{\log e\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
and for which the corresponding exponent is the Khintchine exponent. The case of the Lyapunov exponent is addressed as well, but there are a few non-fatal issues with the argument which we repair in our general theory. Kesseböhmer and Stratmann preformed such a multifractal analysis of the Gauss map prior to Fan et al. in
[
17
]
. The interested reader may also look to
[
13
]
for further motivation for our approach. Our main theorem is as follows. (c.f
[
8
, Propositions 6.3 and 4.13, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
)

Theorem 1.1.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
be a cofinitely regular finitely irreducible CGDMS satisfying the SOSC. Let
F
be a strictly positive Hölder family of potentials that are either comparable to
\log\Phi^{\prime}
(see Lemma
2.1.2
) or bounded. Let
D
be the Manhattan region of the associated pressure function
P(t,q)
, and
(t,q)\in D
. If the amalgamated functions
\tilde{f}
and
\Xi_{\Phi}
of
F
and
\log\Phi^{\prime}
respectively are such that

\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}(|\tilde{f}|+|\Xi_{\Phi}|)d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}<\infty,

(1.1)

where
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}
is the unique shift invariant Borel probability measure equivalent to the unique
t\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f}
-conformal probability measure
\tilde{m}_{t,q}
on
E_{A}^{\infty}
such that
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{f_{t,q}}\tilde{m}_{t,q}=e^{P(t,q)}\tilde{m}_{t,q}
(defined in
[
25
, Chapter 17.6]
), then:

1.

The system of equations

\begin{cases}P(t,q)=q\xi\\
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q)=\xi\end{cases}

(1.2)

has a unique solution
(t(\xi),q(\xi))\in D_{0}=\{(t,q)\in D\;|\;0\leq t\leq h\}
for
\xi\in(\xi_{min},\infty)
and
h
the Bowen parameter of
\Phi
.

2.

t(\xi)
and
q(\xi)
are real analytic.

3.

t(\xi)=\HD(J(\xi))
.

4.

t(\xi)=\HD(J_{1}(\xi))
.

Given the comparability of

1.2
Preliminaries for CGDMSs

In most cases we will proceed in line with the definitions set in Urbański, Roy, and Munday’s dynamics volumes,
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
with slight variation. We first collect together the lengthy definition of a CGDMS.

Definition 1.2.1
.

A
Conformal Graph Directed Markov System (CGDMS)
is a triple
(\Phi,\mathcal{G},A)
where
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}:X_{t(e)}\to X_{i(e)}\}_{e\in E}
is a collection of maps between a finite family of compact metric spaces
\{X_{v}\}_{v\in V}
,
V
is the vertex set of
\mathcal{G}=(V,E,i,t)
a directed multi-graph,
A:E\times E\to\{0,1\}
is a matrix with at least one
1
in each row defining an allowable composition rule respecting the graph
\mathcal{G}
called
A
-admissibility. That is,
A_{ab}=1
if and only if the composition
\phi_{a}\circ\phi_{b}:X_{t(b)}\to X_{i(a)}
is allowable and defined. Further the underlying Graph Directed System (GDS) defined by setting
A
to be the adjacency matrix of the line graph of
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the following properties:

1.

For every vertex
v\in V
,
X_{v}
there is a
d(v)\geq 1
such that
X_{v}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d(v)}
which is regular closed.

2.

\Phi
satisfies the open set condition, that is for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
,
e_{1}\neq e_{2}

\phi_{e_{1}}(\text{Int}(X_{t(e_{1})}))\cap\phi_{e_{2}}(\text{Int}(X_{t(e_{2})}))=\emptyset.

3.

For every
v\in V
there is an open, connected and bounded set
W_{v}
such that
X_{v}\subseteq W_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and such that for every
e\in E
with
t(e)=v
, the map
\phi_{e}
extends to a contracting conformal diffeomorphism of
W_{v}
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and all of these extensions have a common contraction ratio
s<1
.

4.

There exists constants
L\geq 1
and
\kappa>0
such that

\left||\phi^{\prime}_{e}(y)|-|\phi^{\prime}_{e}(x)|\right|\leq L|\phi_{e}^{\prime}(x)|\cdot\|y-x\|^{\kappa}

for every
e\in E
and every pair of points
x,y\in W_{t(e)}
.

In other words
\Phi
defines the inverted edges of
\mathcal{G}
and
A
defines the traversable inverted paths through
\mathcal{G}
. One should note that CGDMSs are often constructed by taking the inverse branches of a distance expanding system. Since
\Phi
contains the information of the underlying GDS, in an abuse of notation we often refer to
\Phi
alone as a CGDMS. In many contexts, it is helpful to also attach the symbolic representation
(E_{A}^{\infty},\sigma)
to
\Phi
where allowable concatenations of symbols are given by
A
and
\sigma:E_{A}^{\infty}\to E_{A}^{\infty}
is the one-sided shift map. For a finite word
w=w_{1}\dots w_{n}
we define the length of the word
|w|
to be the number of concatenated symbols that form
w
. The language of the symbolic representation
E_{A}^{*}
is the set of all finite
A
-admissible words,

E_{A}^{*}=\{\varepsilon\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{w\in E^{n}\;|\;A_{w_{i}w_{i+1}}=1\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n-1\}\right).

One core property of systems we are interested in is defined by this
A
-admissibility rule.

Definition 1.2.2
.

We say that a CGDMS
\Phi
or its symbolic representation
E_{A}^{\infty}
are
finitely irreducible
if the
A
-admissibility rule is such that there exists
I\subseteq E_{A}^{*}
where
|I|<\infty
and for every
e,f\in E
there exists
\tau(e,f)\in I
such that
e\tau(e,f)f\in E_{A}^{*}
.

It is useful to note that if a CGDMS is finitely irreducible, then there exists
\tau\in I
such that there are infinitely many letters
e\in E
for which one may choose
\tau(e,e)=\tau
. This follows immediately from the pigeonhole principal. To see how the symbolic representation encodes a CGDMS,
\Phi
, we note that
w\in E_{A}^{*}
represents a composition of maps in
\Phi
with the exception that
\varepsilon\in E_{A}^{0}
, the empty word, is the unique word for which
w\varepsilon=\varepsilon w=w
and for which
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\text{Id}_{X}:\bigsqcup_{v\in V}X_{v}\to\bigsqcup_{v\in V}X_{v}
is the identity map on the disjoint union of the
X_{v}
’s. For all other
w
’s we have that

w\mapsto\phi_{w}:=\phi_{w_{1}}\circ\phi_{w_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{w_{n}}:X_{t(w_{n})}=:X_{t(w)}\to X_{i(w)}:=X_{i(w_{1})}.

To limit this composition process, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the map
\cdot|_{n}:E_{A}^{\infty}\to E_{A}^{n}
by
\omega|_{n}:=\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{n}
for all
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
. We note that
\{\phi_{\omega|_{n}}(X_{t(\omega|_{n}}))\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a descending sequence of compact sets, and the coding map
\pi:E_{A}^{\infty}\to X
is therefore defined by

\{\pi(\omega)\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{\omega|_{n}}(X_{t(\omega|_{n})}).

The limit set of a CGDMS is given by

J:=\pi(E_{A}^{\infty})=\bigcup_{\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}}\{\pi(\omega)\}.

J
need not be a compact set when
E
is infinite (if not compact, it is analytic in the set theoretic sense of the term). One should also note that
E_{A}^{\infty,\omega}
is the set of all points
\rho\in E_{A}^{\infty}
such that
A_{\omega_{|\omega|},\rho_{1}}=1
. Alternatively,
E_{A}^{\infty,\omega}=\sigma^{|\omega|}([\omega]_{A})
. It is a good time to recall the definition of the strong open set condition which we will require of our systems.

Definition 1.2.3
.

A CGDMS
\Phi
satisfies the
strong open set condition (SOSC)
if
J\cap\text{Int}(X)\neq\emptyset
, that is, if

\bigcup_{v\in V}(J\cap\text{Int}(X_{v}))\neq\emptyset.

The coding map need not be injective either. For this reason, and for the sake of well-defined partitions later on when discussing the multifractal decomposition, we take

J_{>1}:=\{x\in J\;|\;|\pi^{-1}(x)|>1\}.

J_{>1}
is the set of points in the limit set with non-unique codings. The restriction
\displaystyle\pi|_{E_{A}^{\infty}\setminus\pi^{-1}(J_{>1})}
is then bijective by construction.

There are also several types of potentials on the symbolic representation that we should take a moment to recall before developing our results.

Definition 1.2.4
.

A potential
\varphi:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is
summable
if

\sum_{e\in E}\exp(\sup(\varphi|_{[e]}))<\infty.

We say that for a CGDMS,
\Phi
, a family
F=\{f_{e}:X_{t(e)}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{e\in E}
is summable if

\sum_{e\in E}\|\exp f_{e}\|_{X_{t(e)}}<\infty.

Definition 1.2.5
.

A potential
\varphi:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is
acceptable
provided that it is uniformly continuous and

\osc(\varphi):=\osc(\varphi,\mathcal{U}_{E})=\sup_{e\in E}[\sup(\varphi|_{[e]})-\inf(\varphi|_{[e]})]<\infty.

An important subclass of acceptable potentials are those which are Hölder continuous on cylinders.

Definition 1.2.6
.

A potential
\varphi:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is
Hölder continuous on cylinders
if there exists constants
\beta\geq 0
and
c\geq 0
for which

|\omega\wedge\tau|\geq 1\implies|f(\omega)-f(\tau)|\leq c[d(\omega,\tau)]^{\beta},

and we define
v_{\beta}(f)
to be the least such
c
with this property. We also say that for a CGDMS,
\Phi
, a family of potentials
F=\{f_{e}:X_{t(e)}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{e\in E}
is
Hölder with exponent
\beta>0
if

\nu_{\beta}(F):=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{w\in E_{A}^{n}}\sup_{x,y\in X_{t(w)}}|f_{w_{1}}(\phi_{\sigma(w)}(x))-f_{w_{1}}(\phi_{\sigma(w)}(y))|e^{\beta n}<\infty.

The finiteness of the inner two suprema implies that the diameters of
f_{w_{1}}\circ\phi_{\sigma(w)}(X_{t(w)})
are finite for every finite word of length
n
while the exterior supremum requires that these diameters go to
0
exponentially fast as the length of the word goes to infinity.

Definition 1.2.7
.

Let
f:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued potential. Define

\textit{var}_{n}(f):=\sup_{|\omega\wedge\tau|=n}|f(\omega)-f(\tau)|.

We say
f
is of
summable variations
if

V(f):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\textit{var}_{n}(f)<\infty.

There is a relationship between Hölder continuity on cylinders and summable variations. To see this, we recall a portion of the Bounded Variation Principle for Ergodic Sums that we will use later on.

Lemma 1.2.8

(See
[
25
]
Lemma 17.2.3)
.

If a potential
f:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is Hölder continuous on cylinders with exponent
\beta
, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, all
w\in E_{A}^{n}
and all
\rho,\gamma\in E_{A}^{\infty,w}
, we have

|S_{n}f(w\rho)-S_{n}f(w\gamma)|\leq\frac{v_{\beta}(f)}{e^{\beta}-1}e^{-\beta|\rho\wedge\gamma|}.

In particular, if a potential
f
is Hölder continuous on cylinders with exponent
\beta
taking
w=i\in\mathbb{N}=E
then each term in the sequence of differences
|f(w\rho_{n})-f(w\gamma_{n})|
where
|\rho_{n}\wedge\gamma_{n}|=n\geq 0
is bounded by a term in a geometric sequence independent of
i
. Taking supremums over
i
and
|\rho_{n}\wedge\gamma_{n}|=n
and summing then yields summable variations.

We also recall the construction of the amalgamated function of a family
F
.

Definition 1.2.9
.

For a family of potentials
F=\{f_{e}:X_{t(e)}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{e\in E}
the
amalgamated function

\tilde{f}:E_{A}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\tilde{f}(\omega):=f_{\omega_{1}}(\pi(\sigma(\omega))).

We note that the Lyapunov family
\log\Phi^{\prime}:=\{\log|\phi_{e}^{\prime}|\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
has amalgamated function
\Xi_{\Phi}(\omega):=\log|\phi_{\omega_{1}}^{\prime}(\pi(\sigma(\omega)))|
, which is Hölder continuous on cylinders, and hence acceptable. For a CGDMS, if a family is Hölder with exponent
\beta
, then the amalgamated function is Hölder continuous on cylinders with exponent
\beta
, and a family is summable if and only if the amalgamated function is summable (See
[
25
, Lemma 19.8.4 and Lemma 19.8.6]
). We note that for a CGDMS we have that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\||\phi_{i}^{\prime}|\|_{X_{t(i)}}=0.

(1.3)

For each family we also have the associated exponents defined by Birkhoff averages.

Definition 1.2.10
.

Let
F
be a family of potentials with amalgamated function
\tilde{f}
. For
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
the
F
-exponent
at
\omega
,
\xi(\omega)
if it exists, is given by the Birkhoff average

\xi(\omega):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega)).

Further, for any shift invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
we call the quantity

F(\mu):=\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}d\mu

the characteristic
F
-exponent with respect to
\mu
.

A family of functions
F=\{f_{e}\}_{e\in E}
is uniformly bounded below by
K\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
f_{e}\geq K
for all
e\in E
. We say a family of functions is strictly positive if
F
is uniformly bounded below and
K>0
. For a family
F
the translation of the family by
a\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by
F-a:=\{f_{e}-a:X\to\mathbb{R}\}_{e\in E}
. Many of our results are stated for families of functions that are strictly positive. However, the following lemma allows us to preform the computations for the cases in which
F
is uniformly bounded below by applying our theory to a translated family and then translating back to reflect the original family.

Lemma 1.2.11
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
. If
F
is a family of functions such that
F>K
, then we may replace
F
with
F^{\prime}=F-K+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
. That is, a point
\omega
has
F
-exponent
\xi_{F}
if and only if
\omega
has
F^{\prime}
exponent
\xi_{F}-K+\varepsilon

Proof.

The argument is quick. Let
\xi_{F}
and
\xi_{F^{\prime}}
denote the
F
and
F^{\prime}
exponent functions respectively. Note that if
\xi_{F}(\omega)
exists, then

\xi_{F}(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[(\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))+\varepsilon-K)-\varepsilon+K]=\xi_{F^{\prime}}(\omega)-\varepsilon+K.

∎

If
\mu
is ergodic, then
\mu
-a.e. point has the same
F
-exponent. We also remark that the definition is different for Lyapunov exponents (with family
\log\Phi^{\prime}
) but just up to a sign. In our framework however, the Lyapunov exponent may be studied by applying our results to the positive Lyapunov family
F=-\log\Phi^{\prime}

Definition 1.2.12
.

Let
\Phi
be a CGDMS, then the
characteristic Lyapunov exponent with respect to
\mu
is given by

\chi(\mu)=-\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}d\mu>0.

2
Topological Pressure

Given CGDMS
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in E}
, the corresponding pressure function of the family
t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF
is given by the asymptotic growth rate of partition functions
Z_{n}(t,q)
:

P(t,q):=P(t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{w\in E_{A}^{n}}||\exp(S_{w}(t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF))||_{X_{t(w)}}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(t,q)

(2.1)

where

S_{w}(F):=\sum_{j=1}^{|w|}f_{w_{j}}\circ\phi_{\sigma^{j}(w)}

(2.2)

and

\|\cdot\|_{X_{t}(\omega)}:=\|\cdot|_{X_{t}(\omega)}\|_{\infty}.

(2.3)

Note that the limit exists whenever
(t,q)
produces a summable family as the sequence of sums is submultiplicative and so the convergence follows from Fekete’s lemma
[
9
]
. This pressure agrees with the pressure of the amalgamated function
f_{t,q}=t\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f}
of
t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF
whenever
(t,q)
produces a summable Hölder family of functions as per
[
25
, Proposition 19.8.9]
. In this case, the amalgamated function is acceptable and, as we are concerned with finitely irreducible CGDMSs, by
[
25
, Proposition 17.2.8]
we may write

P(t,q)=P(f_{t,q})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(f_{t,q})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{w\in E_{A}^{n}}\exp(\overline{S}_{n}f_{t,q}([w])).

We concern ourselves with the following partition-like functions

\sum_{w\in E_{A}^{n}}\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{|w|}|\phi_{w_{j}}^{\prime}|^{t}\circ\phi_{\sigma^{j}(w)}\right\|_{X_{t(w)}}\cdot\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{|w|}\exp(f_{w_{j}})^{q}\circ\phi_{\sigma^{j}(w)}\right\|_{X_{t(w)}}=:\tilde{Z}_{n}(t,q).

(2.4)

Proposition 2.0.1
.

P(t,q)<\infty\iff\tilde{Z}_{1}(t,q)<\infty
.

Proof.

According to
[
25
, Theorem 17.2.8]
and
[
25
, Proposition 19.8.9]
it is enough to show that
\tilde{Z}_{n}(t,q)\asymp Z_{n}(t,q)
. Let
K
be the bounded distortion constant for
\Phi
and let
\text{Dist}(F)
be the distortion constant for
F
given by exponentiation of the inequality in Lemma
1.2.8
for
n=1
. Then, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the distortion properties for
F
and
\Phi
, we have that

\max\{K^{-t},\text{Dist}(F)^{-q}\}\tilde{Z}_{n}(t,q)\leq Z_{n}(t,q)\leq\tilde{Z}_{n}(t,q).

(2.5)

Hence,
\tilde{Z}_{1}(t,q)<\infty\iff Z_{1}(t,q)<\infty\iff P(t,q)<\infty
.
∎

The geometric family of potentials, that is, the parameterized Lyapunov family
t\log\Phi^{\prime}
, is of particular importance in the study of the geometry of
J
for a given
\Phi
. For the choice of
F=-\log\Phi^{\prime}
we will denote the Lyapunov Pressure by
P_{L}(t,q)=:P(t-q,0)
, and sometimes we just write
P(t)=P_{L}(t,0)
for this pressure function when
q=0
. The finiteness parameter
\theta

2.1
The Manhattan Region

By analogy to hyperbolic surfaces (see
[
22
]
), we define the Manhattan region of pressure and its boundary, the Manhattan curve. The primary object of interest for us will be the Manhattan region and a particular subset of it which we call the multifractal region.

Definition 2.1.1
.

For the family
t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF
the set
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

D:=\left\{(t,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;\bigg|\;\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\exp(f_{i})\|^{q}_{X_{t(i)}}\cdot\||\phi_{i}^{\prime}|\|^{t}_{X_{t(i)}}<\infty\right\}

is called
the Manhattan region of
P(t,q)
. Its boundary,
\partial D
, is called
the Manhattan Curve of
P(t,q)
Further, for a finitely irreducible CGDMS
\Phi
with Bowen parameter
h
, we define

D_{0}:=\{(t,q)\in D\;|\;0\leq t\leq h\}

as
the multifractal region of
\Phi
with respect to
F
.

When
(t,q)\in D
by Proposition
2.0.1
we have that
P(t,q)<\infty
. Note as well that on
\text{int}(D)
the pressure
P(t,q)
is real analytic (see
[
25
, Theorems 20.1.11, 20.2.3]
). Under a cofinite regularity assumption on
\Phi
and a comparability assumption between the families
F
and
\log\Phi^{\prime}
we can show that
D
is open.

Lemma 2.1.2
.

If there exists
\alpha>0
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

-\alpha\log|\phi_{i}^{\prime}|+\gamma\leq f_{i}\leq-\alpha\log|\phi_{i}^{\prime}|+\beta

and
\Phi
is cofinitely regular, then
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\text{std}}
is open with respect to the standard topology.

Proof.

Suppose not, then there exsits
(t,q)\in\partial D\cap D
. Since
(t,q)\in D
we have that

\tilde{Z}_{1}(t,q)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\exp(f_{i})\|^{q}_{X_{t(i)}}\cdot\||\phi_{i}^{\prime}|\|^{t}_{X_{t(i)}}<\infty.

Note that for a fixed
q\in\mathbb{R}
the function
\tilde{Z}_{1}(\cdot,q)
is non-increasing, thus, since
(t,q)\in\partial D
, for every
\varepsilon>0
given
(t-\varepsilon,q)\not\in D
. By the comparability assumption we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\gamma q}\||\phi_{i}^{\prime}||\|_{X_{t(i)}}^{-\alpha q+s}\leq\tilde{Z}_{1}(s,q)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta q}\||\phi_{i}^{\prime}||\|_{X_{t(i)}}^{-\alpha q+s}.

And so,
\tilde{Z}_{1}(s,q)\asymp\tilde{Z}_{1}(-\alpha q+s,0)
. Hence by definition of
\theta
and from Proposition
2.0.1
,
(t,q)\in D
if and only if
-\alpha q+t>\theta
. So there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
-\alpha q+t-\varepsilon>\theta
. Let
a=\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha v_{2}-v_{1}}
. Then
a>0
and
\varepsilon=-av_{1}+a\alpha v_{2}
and so, for this
a
,
(t-av_{1},q+av_{2})=(t,q)+a\vec{v}\in D
, a contradiction.
\lightning

Hence it must be the case that
D\cap\partial D=\emptyset
, and so
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
does not contain any of its boundary points. Thus,
D
is open.
∎

Remark 2.1.3
.

The comparability condition can be framed in terms of families of functions as well. In this case,

where

is a bounded Hölder family of functions. As evidenced by the Khintchine Decomposition given in

, these distortions of the Lyapunov family can yield different multifractal decompositions.

Let

When we drop cofinite regularity from the previous proposition,

Corollary 2.1.5

By taking the contra-positive of the previous lemmas, and noting that

2.2
Derivatives of Pressure

Under a mild integrability assumption, we can describe the first derivatives of
P(t,q)
as well.

Lemma 2.2.1

(ND)
.

Suppose
(t,q)\in D
and that
\displaystyle\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}(|\tilde{f}|+|\Xi_{\Phi}|)\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}<\infty
where
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}:=\mu_{f_{t,q}}
from Theorem
2.0.8
, then for
F
strictly positive,

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}=\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}<0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}=\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}>0.

(2.13)

Proof.

The argument presented is similar to
[
25
, Theorem 16.4.10]
. Since
(t,q)\in D
, the family
t\log\Phi^{\prime}+qF
is summable and Hölder, so, the amalgamated function
f_{t,q}
is summable and holder continuous on cylinders. Hence,
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}
exists. Then, for
t^{\prime}>t
, by the variational principle (Theorem
2.0.5
),

\displaystyle P(t^{\prime},q)

\displaystyle\geq h(\tilde{\mu}_{t,q})+\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}(t^{\prime}\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f})\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}

\displaystyle=h(\tilde{\mu}_{t,q})+\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}f_{t,q}\;d\tilde{\mu}+(t^{\prime}-t)\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}

\displaystyle=P(t,q)+(t^{\prime}-t)\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}.

And so,

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}(t,q)=\lim_{t^{\prime}\to t^{+}}\frac{P(t^{\prime},q)-P(t,q)}{t^{\prime}-t}\geq\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}.

(2.14)

Similarly, when
t^{\prime}<t
we have that

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}(t,q)=\lim_{t^{\prime}\to t^{-}}\frac{P(t^{\prime},q)-P(t,q)}{t^{\prime}-t}\leq\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\Xi_{\Phi}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}<0.

(2.15)

The argument for
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}
is identical. However, the resulting
q
-derivative is positive since
\tilde{f}\geq K>0
. Thus, the derivatives are as stated.
∎

We will also want to avoid the case in which our amalgamated functions are cohomologous to a constant. This, as we will see, will allow us to retrieve some information about the second derivatives of
P(t,q)
. Luckily, we have the following:

Lemma 2.2.2

(ND)
.

Let
\Phi
be a cofinitely regular countably infinite CGDMS that is finitely irreducible and cofinitely regular and
F
be bounded below. Then, for
(t,q)\in D
the amalgamated function

is not cohomologous to a constant.

Proof.

Suppose first that
t\neq 0
and
f_{t,q}
is cohomologous to a constant. Then there is a bounded function
u
for which there exists
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

t\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f}=u-u\circ\sigma+C.

(2.16)

However, taking a Birkhoff average one obtains

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(t\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f})(\sigma^{j}(\omega))=C\qquad\forall\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}.

(2.17)

Now suppose
t>0
. since
\Phi
is finitely irreducible,
A
is a finitely irreducible matrix, so there exists a finite set of finite words
I\subset E_{A}^{*}
which witnesses the finite irreducibility of
E_{A}^{*}
. That is, for each
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
there exists a
\tau(e_{1},e_{2})\in I
such that
e_{1}\tau(e_{1},e_{2})e_{2}
is
A
-admissible. Set

E_{w}:=\{k\in E\;|\;(kw)^{\infty}\in E_{A}^{*}\}\;\;\forall w\in I

(2.18)

By the pigeonhole principle, we may fix a
\tau\in I
such that
|E_{\tau}|=\infty
. For this

we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(t\Xi_{\Phi}+q\tilde{f})(\sigma^{j}((k\tau)^{\infty}))=C_{k}=C\qquad\forall k\in E_{\tau}.

(2.19)

Since the point is periodic, the Birkhoff average is just the arithmetic mean of the potential evaluated at elements of the finite orbit so the equation simplifies to

\displaystyle t\Xi_{\Phi}(

\displaystyle(k\tau)^{\infty})+t\Xi_{\Phi}((\tau k)^{\infty})+\dots+t\Xi_{\Phi}((\tau_{|\tau|}k\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{|\tau|-1})^{\infty})

\displaystyle+q\tilde{f}((k\tau)^{\infty})+q\tilde{f}((\tau k)^{\infty})+\dots+q\tilde{f}((\tau_{|\tau|}k\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{|\tau|-1})^{\infty})=(|\tau|+1)C_{k},\;\forall k\in E_{\tau}.

(2.20)

Note that all terms except for

and
q\tilde{f}((k\tau)^{\infty})
may be bounded since the amalgamated functions

and

are Hölder continuous on cylinders and thus acceptable. In fact, the bound on these terms may be chosen such that it is only dependent on
\tau
. Now by (

) and since

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}t\Xi_{\Phi}((k\tau)^{\infty})=-\infty
, so we must have that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}q\tilde{f}((k\tau)^{\infty})=\infty
.
And so,

\lim_{k\to\infty}-\frac{\Xi_{\Phi}((k\tau)^{\infty})}{\tilde{f}((k\tau)^{\infty})}=\frac{q}{t}.

(2.21)

By passing to subsequences if necessary, we may suppose that the limit approaches its value from above or below. In the case that the limit approaches from below, we consider
q^{\prime}>q
, then for all
k
large enough, there exists an

\displaystyle-\frac{t\log|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\pi((\tau k)^{\infty}))|}{q^{\prime}\tilde{f}((k\tau)^{\infty})}\leq s(k)<1

\displaystyle\implies-t\log|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\pi((\tau k)^{\infty}))|-s(k)q^{\prime}f((k\tau)^{\infty})\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies t\log|\phi_{k}^{\prime}(\pi((\tau k)^{\infty}))|+s(k)q^{\prime}f((k\tau)^{\infty})\geq 0

Now noting that

(2.23)

Hence it must be the case that the limit approaches its value from above. However, here we consider

There are a few issues that arise in the argument of the Lyapunov spectrum of the Gauss map in

, Proposition 6.1]
. The errors concern the compactness of

and the upper semi-continuity of metric entropy, properties that are well known in the case of finite alphabet shifts of finite type (SFTs) and other expansive continuous self-maps of non-empty compact metrizable spaces.

However, these properties require more care in the case of the continued fraction Gauss map and other systems that may be represented by countably infinite alphabet shifts. We will work under the assumptions that we have a finitely irreducible symbolic space and
F
is bounded below. Note that the comparability condition between the Lyapunov and

families from Lemma

implies that

is bounded below. In light of Lemma

, without loss of generality we will work as if

is strictly positive. What we argue here will repair the non-fatal errors made in the derivation of the Lyapunov spectrum. (One may also adapt and consult
[
14
]
and
[
10
]
to repair these issues in the context of the Gauss map directly.) These arguments are also pertinent to our derivation of the

-exponent spectrum. Since our family

is strictly positive, and since

is continuous and strictly increasing, we know that

is the continuous image of an interval. So,

is an interval with

\inf(A)=\inf_{q}\left\{\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}\;d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}\right\}=:\xi_{\min}(t).

(3.2)

The goal of this section is to show that

is independent of
t
, that there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
for which this

is achieved, and that it results from a zero-temperature style limit of Gibbs States. For background on zero-temperature limits see
[

]
.

is bounded, is much simpler. In this case,

is a bounded continuous function. Thus,
\displaystyle\lim_{q\to-\infty}\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}=\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}d\tilde{\mu}_{\infty}
as
\tilde{\mu}_{\infty}
is a weak* accumulation point. The argument for
\tilde{f}
-minimizing is identical to the comparable
F
case.

The above argument results in the following immediate corollary which will be helpful for the multifractal analysis.

Corollary 3.2.2
.

For any
0\leq t\leq h
fixed, when
F
and
\log\Phi^{\prime}
are comparable or bounded as in Lemma
2.1.2
or Lemma
2.1.4
, respectively we have that

\lim_{q\to-\infty}\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q)=\xi_{\min}.

Remark 3.2.3
.

Without the comparability or bounded assumptions, we note that the argument above shows that the measure
\mu_{0,\infty}:=\displaystyle\lim_{q\to-\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{0,q}
is
\tilde{f}
minimizing.

4
Multifractal Analysis of
F
-exponents

Our multifractal analysis of CGDMSs concerns the decomposition of
J
derived from
\Phi
satisfying the SOSC, which are cofinitely regular and finitely irreducible. The decomposition is constructed by Birkhoff averages determined by a Hölder family of functions
F
that are uniformly bounded below. We will retrieve the spectrum by applying the SOSC to show that the Hausdorff dimensions of sets determined by the projection of the decomposition at the symbolic level agree with the Hausdorff dimensions of the decomposition formed on the level set. We define
J_{>1}
as the set of points in
J
that have non-unique codings in the symbolic representation
E_{A}^{\infty}
. The multifractal decomposition of
E_{A}^{\infty}
by Birkhoff averages with respect to the amalgamated function
\tilde{f}
of
F
is given by level sets

E_{A}^{\infty}(\xi)=\left\{\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}\;\Big|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))=\xi\right\}.

(4.1)

We let
E_{A}^{\infty^{\prime}}
denote the exceptional set where the limit does not exist. The projected level sets
J(\xi):=\pi(E_{A}^{\infty}(\xi))
and the projected exceptional set
J^{\prime}:=\pi(E_{A}^{\infty^{\prime}})
may not partition
J
. We call the collection
\{J(\xi)\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\cup\{J^{\prime}\}
the projected multifractal decomposition of
E_{A}^{\infty}
by Birkhoff averages with respect to
\tilde{f}
. By definition,
J(\xi)
is the set of points in
J
for which there exists a coding
\omega
which achieves the
F
-exponent
\xi
. Alternatively, consider
x=\pi(\omega)\in J\setminus J_{>1}
. Notice that

\pi(\sigma(\omega))=\phi_{\omega_{1}}^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{\omega|_{n}}(X_{t(\omega|_{n})})\right).

(4.2)

And so,

\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))=f_{\sigma^{j}(\omega)_{1}}(\pi(\sigma^{j+1}(\omega)))=f_{\sigma^{j}(\omega)_{1}}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\omega|_{j+1}}(x).

(4.3)

Thus, to form a proper multifractal decomposition on the limit set, we take

J_{1}(\xi)=\left\{x\in J\setminus J_{>1}\;\Big|\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f_{\sigma^{j}(\omega)_{1}}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\omega|_{j}}(x)=\xi\right\}

(4.4)

and let
J_{1}^{\prime}
be the exceptional set which is the collection of points where these limit do not exist.
Since
x\in J\setminus J_{>1}
implies that

for some unique
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
, the use of
\omega
in the definition of

is not ambiguous. We call the non-empty sets in the collection
\{J_{1}(\xi)\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\cup\{J_{1}^{\prime}\cup J_{>1}\}
the multifractal decomposition of
J
by Birkhoff averages with respect to
F
. These sets are well-defined and partition
J
.
We can see that the measures
\mu_{t,q}
for
(t,q)\in D
are supported on a
J(\xi)
for some
\xi
since by Birkhoff’s Ergodic Theorem,
\int_{E_{A}^{\infty}}\tilde{f}d\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}=\xi
for some
\xi
and this integral is equal to the Birkhoff average for
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}
-a.e.
\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}
. And so,

\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}(E_{A}^{\infty}(\xi))=1\implies\mu_{t,q}(J(\xi))=\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}\circ\pi^{-1}(\pi(E_{A}^{\infty}(\xi)))\geq\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}(E_{A}^{\infty}(\xi))=1.

(4.5)

Hence,

\mu_{t,q}(J(\xi))=1.

(4.6)

Notice that
\mu_{t,q}(J_{>1})=0
since for a point

with non-unique codings
\omega
and
\tau
there exist an

and

and

(4.7)

So, by the SOSC by way of application of
[

,
J_{>1}
is contained in a countable union of measure zero sets and, hence, is measure
0
. Thus, we observe that

\mu_{t,q}(J_{1}(\xi))=1.

(4.8)

Remark 4.0.1
.

We remind the reader here that by Lemma

translating the family

by a constant results in a bijection between the sets

and

Remark 4.0.2
.

If one can express

.

We will use a version of the volume lemma in the proof of our main theorem. First, recall that for a metric space

\HD(\mu):=\inf\{HD(Y)\;|\;\mu(Y)=1\}.

(4.9)

Let

One should note that the referenced source’s argument for the volume lemma uses the cone condition by way of

Theorem

(4.10)

.

2.

t(\xi)
and

are real analytic.

3.

.

4.

t(\xi)=\HD(J_{1}(\xi))
.

t(\xi)
gives both the Hausdorff dimension of the

component of the projected multifractal decomposition of

by Birkhoff averages with respect to
\tilde{f}
and the Hausdorff dimension of the

by Birkhoff averages with respect to

.
We call the function

[

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Suppose
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
and

are as in Theorem

. If the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}P(t,q)=q\xi\\
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q)=\xi\end{cases}

(4.12)

has a unique real analytic solution
(t(\xi),q(\xi))\in D
for

gives the Hausdorff dimension of the

-multifractal decomposition of

by Birkhoff averages. Furthermore,

gives the Hausdorff dimension of the

component of the

-multifractal decomposition of

by Birkhoff averages and
(t(\xi),q(\xi))\in D_{0}
. In the case that

is bounded we replace

above with

so that

Proof.

For the lower estimate we first recall that
\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)}(J(\xi))=1
and begin by noting that for the solution to the system
(t(\xi),q(\xi))
, the associated Gibbs state
\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)}
is an equilibrium state (Theorem
2.0.8
), so the variational principle (Theorem
2.0.5
) applies. Further since
\Phi
is finitely irreducible and satisfies the SOSC the volume lemma (Theorem
4.0.3
) applies. Applying these results along with the definition of the characteristic Lyapunov exponent with respect to
\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)}
(Definition
1.2.12
) and the definition of the Hausdorff dimension of measure given in the introduction of this section gives that

\displaystyle P(t(\xi),q(\xi))

\displaystyle=q(\xi)\cdot\xi=h(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})+t(\xi)\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}(t(\xi),q(\xi))+q(\xi)\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t(\xi),q(\xi))

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle q(\xi)\cdot\xi=h(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})-t(\xi)\chi(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})+q(\xi)\cdot\xi

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle t(\xi)=\frac{h(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})}{\chi(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})}=\HD(\mu_{t(\xi),q(\xi)})\leq\HD(J(\xi)).

For the other inequality, we first consider the case of
q(\xi)\neq 0
. The case of
q(\xi)=0
will be addressed at the end of the proof. We start by letting
(t,q)\in D
and
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}:=\mu_{f_{t,q}}
be the corresponding Gibbs measure on

where
\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}=\mu_{t,q}\circ\pi^{-1}
with
\mu_{t,q}
on
J
. Then the Gibbs property grants that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}([\omega|_{n}])\asymp\exp(-nP(t,q))\cdot\|\phi^{\prime}_{\omega|_{n}}\|^{t}_{X_{t(\omega|_{n})}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(f_{\omega_{j}}(\pi(\sigma^{j}(\omega))))^{q}.

(4.13)

By applications of the bounded distortion property
[
25
, Lemmas 19.3.9 and 19.3.11]
we have that

\displaystyle\text{diam}(\phi_{\omega|_{n}}(X_{t(\omega|_{n})}))\asymp\|\phi_{\omega|_{n}}^{\prime}\|_{X_{t(\omega|_{n})}}.

(4.14)

Then combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) one obtains

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}([\omega|_{n}])\asymp\exp(-nP(t,q))\cdot\text{diam}(\phi_{\omega|_{n}}(X_{t(\omega|_{n})}))^{t}_{X_{t(\omega|_{n})}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(f_{\omega_{j}}(\pi(\sigma^{j}(\omega))))^{q}.

(4.15)

Now, for any
t>t(\xi)
, we consider an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

0<\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{P(t(\xi),q(\xi))-P(t,q(\xi))}{q(\xi)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ when }q(\xi)>0

(4.16)

and

0<\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{P(t,q(\xi))-P(t(\xi),q(\xi))}{q(\xi)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ when }q(\xi)<0.

(4.17)

Such an
\varepsilon_{0}
exists since
\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}<0
. Now for

we set

\displaystyle J_{\xi}^{n}(\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle:=\pi\left(\left\{\omega\in E_{A}^{\infty}\;\Bigg|\;\xi-\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))<\xi+\varepsilon_{0}\right\}\right).

. Now let
\mathcal{I}(n,\xi,\varepsilon_{0})
be the collection of

[w_{1}\dots w_{n}]

\xi-\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))<\xi+\varepsilon_{0}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ when }\omega\in[w_{1}\dots w_{n}].

(4.18)

\mathcal{I}(n,\xi,\varepsilon_{0})
is non-empty for

large enough. To see this, note that

is Hölder continuous on cylinders. Then by the triangle inequality for
\omega,\tau\in[w_{1}\dots w_{n}]

\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\tau))\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c\left(k^{n-j}\right)^{\beta}<\frac{c}{n}\cdot\frac{k^{\beta}}{1-k^{\beta}}.

(4.19)

Notice that
J_{\xi}^{n}(\varepsilon_{0})=\displaystyle\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{I}(n,\xi,\varepsilon_{0})}\pi(K)
and so

covers

. We note the following multiplication by
1
which we will analyze to estimate the Hausdorff Dimension of

.

\text{diam}(\pi(K))^{t}=\frac{e^{nP(t,q(\xi))}}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(f_{\omega_{j}}(\pi(\sigma^{j}(\omega))))^{q}}\cdot\frac{\text{diam}(\pi(K))^{t}\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(f_{\omega_{j}}(\pi(\sigma^{j}(\omega))))^{q}}{e^{nP(t,q(\xi))}}.

(4.20)

We also note that for

, if

n(\xi-\varepsilon_{0})<\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega))\implies\frac{1}{e^{nq(\xi)(\xi-\varepsilon_{0})}}>\frac{1}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(\tilde{f}(\sigma^{j}(\omega)))^{q(\xi)}},

(4.21)

and if

(4.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}q(\xi)<P(t(\xi),q(\xi))-P(t,q(\xi))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow P(t,q(\xi))+\varepsilon_{0}q(\xi)-q(\xi)\xi<P(t(\xi),q(\xi))-q(\xi)\xi=0.

And so, applying the Gibbs property, (

) and (

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}(n,\xi,\varepsilon_{o})}\text{diam}(\pi(K))^{t}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{n(P(t,q(\xi))-q(\xi)(\xi-\varepsilon_{0}))}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}(n,\xi,\varepsilon_{o})}\tilde{\mu}_{t,q}(K)<\infty.

(4.24)

Next, we focus on the case of

. By choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
, (
4.17
), and since
(t(\xi),q(\xi))
solves (

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}q(\xi)>P(t,q(\xi))-P(t(\xi),q(\xi))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow P(t,q(\xi))-\varepsilon_{0}q(\xi)-q(\xi)\xi<P(t(\xi),q(\xi))-q(\xi)\xi=0.

And so, applying the Gibbs property, (

for all achievable

Corollary 4.1.2
.

If
\Phi
is finite, irreducible, and satisfies the SOSC, and if the system (

) has unique solution
(t(\xi),q(\xi))\in D
for

.

We now turn our attention to the system of equations in question, (
4.11
).

Proposition 4.1.3
.

Suppose that
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\}_{e\in\mathbb{N}}
is as in Theorem
1.1.1
. If
F
is comparable to
\log\Phi^{\prime}
, the system (
4.11
) has a unique real analytic solution
(t(\xi),q(\xi))\in D
for
\xi\in(\xi_{\min},\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. By Corollary
3.2.2

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to-\infty}\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q)=\xi_{\min}
and by Lemma
2.2.5

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q)=\infty
. Note that
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,\cdot)>0
is continuous and differentiable so for
\xi\in(\xi_{\min},\infty)
there exists
q(t,\xi)\in(-\infty,q_{0})
given by the intermediate value theorem where
(t,q_{0})\in\partial D
such that

\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q(t,\xi))=\xi.

(4.30)

This
q(t,\xi)
is unique by monotonicity of
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}
. Since
P(t,q)
is analytic, the implicit
q(t,\xi)
is also analytic with respect to
t
and
\xi
. We set

W(t,\xi):=P(t,q(t,\xi))-\xi q(t,\xi).

(4.31)

Then we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial W}{\partial t}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}(t,q(t,\xi))+\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q(t,\xi))\cdot\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}(t,\xi)-\xi\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}(t,q(t,\xi))<0.

(4.32)

So
W(t,\xi)
is strictly decreasing in
t
. Consider now a fixed
t
, then by definition of
q(t,\xi)
we have that

\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t,q(t,\xi))=\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\xi=\infty.

(4.33)

Yet by the strict convexity of
P
,
\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial q^{2}}>0
. So, there exists a sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(\xi_{\min},\infty)
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\xi_{n}=\infty

5.1
Lyapunov Spectrum

In this particular setting, we recall from the introduction that we have that

Proposition 5.1.1
.

For the Lyapunov spectrum of a CGDMS satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1.1
, we have that

t(\xi)=\frac{1}{\xi}(P(-q(\xi))-q(\xi))=\frac{1}{\xi}\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\{P(-q)-q\xi\},

(5.1)

where
P(q):=P_{L}(0,-q)
.

Proof.

Note that as discussed above
P_{L}(t,q)=P(t-q)
in the Lyapunov spectrum case, and so

\begin{cases}P(t-q,0)=q\xi\\
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(t-q,0)=\xi\end{cases}\implies\begin{cases}P(-q,0)=(t+q)\xi\\
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(-q,0)=\xi\end{cases}.

Now solving the first equation for

and expressing

and

as functions of
\xi
we have that

\begin{cases}t(\xi)=\frac{P(-q(\xi),0)}{\xi}-q(\xi)\\
\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}(-q(\xi),0)=\xi\end{cases}.

(5.2)

Hence, we have the first equality in (
5.1
). To see the second equality, we first note that since
P(-q)
is strictly convex, so is the function
P(-q)-q\xi
. Thus, the infimum is attained at a unique point
q:=q(\alpha)
. Now since
P(-q)=P_{L}(0,q)

\frac{\partial P_{L}}{\partial q}(0,q)=-\frac{dP}{dq}(-q).

Differentiating

with respect to

and solving for critical points yields

-\frac{dP}{dq}(-q)=\xi\implies\frac{\partial P_{L}}{\partial q}(0,q)=\xi.

Yet, per Theorem
1.1.1
, this only occurs when

. Hence

\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\{P(-q)-q\xi\}=P(-q(\xi))-q(\xi)\xi.

(5.3)

And so, factoring out

from the first equation in (

) and applying (

) yields the desired result.

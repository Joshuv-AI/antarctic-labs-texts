Small data global regularity for 3-D Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal model without kinematic transport
Jiaxi Huang 1, Ning Jiang 2, Yi-Long Luo 3, Lifeng Zhao 4

Abstract
. In this article, we consider the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic system for incompressible liquid crystal model without kinematic transport in three spatial dimensions, which is a nonlinear coupling of incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with wave map to 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . Global regularity for small and smooth initial data near the equilibrium is proved. The proof relies on the idea of space-time resonance.



1.
Introduction

The hydrodynamic theory of incompressible liquid crystals was established by Ericksen
[
3
,
5
,
6
]
and Leslie
[
16
,
17
]
in the 1960’s (see also Section 5.1 of
[
22
]
). The general Ericksen-Leslie’s system consists of the following equations of the velocity field
u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the orientation field
d(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla d\odot\nabla d)+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,\\
\mathrm{div}u=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\ddot{d}=\Delta d+\Gamma d+\lambda_{1}(\dot{d}+Bd)+\lambda_{2}Ad\,.\end{array}\right.

For the detailed derivation of (
1.1
) from the original form of Ericksen-Leslie’s formulation, see, for example
[
15
]
.

In the above system,
\rho_{1}>0
is the inertial constant, and the superposed dot denotes the material derivative
\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
, and

\displaystyle A=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla^{\top}u)\,,\quad B=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla u-\nabla^{\top}u)\,,

represent the rate of strain tensor and skew-symmetric part of the strain rate, respectively. We also define
N=\dot{d}+Bd
as the rigid rotation part of director changing rate by fluid vorticity. Here
A_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}u_{i}+\partial_{i}u_{j})
,
B_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}u_{i}-\partial_{i}u_{j})
,
(Bd)_{i}=B_{ki}d_{k}
, and
(\nabla d\odot\nabla d)_{ij}=\partial_{i}d_{k}\partial_{j}d_{k}
. The stress tensor
\tilde{\sigma}
has the following form:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{ji}=\nu_{1}d_{k}A_{kp}d_{p}d_{i}d_{j}+\nu_{2}d_{j}N_{i}+\nu_{3}d_{i}N_{j}+\nu_{4}A_{ij}+\nu_{5}A_{ik}d_{k}d_{j}+\nu_{6}d_{i}A_{jk}d_{k}\,.

These coefficients
\nu_{i}(1\leq i\leq 6)
which may depend on material and temperature, are usually called Leslie coefficients, and are related to certain local correlations in the fluid. Usually, the following relations are frequently introduced in the literatures
[
3
,
16
,
28
]
.

(1.3)

\lambda_{1}=\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}\,,\quad\lambda_{2}=\nu_{5}-\nu_{6}\,,\quad\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}=\nu_{6}-\nu_{5}\,.

The first two relations are necessary conditions in order to satisfy the equation of motion identically, while the third relation is called
Parodi’s relation
, which is derived from Onsager reciprocal relations expressing the equality of certain relations between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium. Under Parodi’s relation, we see that the dynamics of an incompressible nematic liquid crystal flow involve five independent Leslie coefficients in (
1.2
). Furthermore, in (
1.1
), the Lagrangian multiplier
\Gamma
is (which ensures the geometric constraint
|d|=1
):

(1.4)

\Gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{d}|^{2}+|\nabla d|^{2}-\lambda_{2}d^{\top}Ad\,.

We remark that the last two terms in the third equation of (
1.1
) is the so-called kinematic transport, i.e.

(1.5)

g=\lambda_{1}(\dot{d}+Bd)+\lambda_{2}Ad\,,

which represents the effect of the macroscopic flow field on the microscopic structure. The material coefficients
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
reflect the molecular shape and the slippery part between the fluid and particles. The first term represents the rigid rotation of the molecule , while the second term stands for the streching of the molecule by the flow.

Recently, the second and third named authors of the current paper studied in
[
15
]
the well-posedness in the context of classical solutions for the hyperbolic case, i.e.
\rho_{1}>0
. More precisely, in
[
15
]
under some natural constraints on the Leslie coefficients which ensure the basic energy law is dissipative, they proved the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the classical solution to the system (
1.1
) with finite initial energy. Furthermore, with an additional assumption on the coefficients which provides a damping effect, i.e.
\lambda_{1}<0
, and the smallness of the initial energy, the unique global classical solution was established. Here we remark that the assumption
\lambda_{1}<0
plays a crucial role in the global-in-time well-posedness.

Cai-Wang
[
2
]
recently made progress for the simplied Ericksen-Leslie system, namely, the case with
\nu_{i}=0,i=1\,,\cdots\,,6
,
i\neq 4
. They proved the global regularity of (
1.1
) near the constant equilibrium by employing the vector field method.

In the current paper, we consider the more general case: still
\nu_{2}=\nu_{3}=0
and
\nu_{4}>0
, but
\nu_{5}=\nu_{6}>-\nu_{4}
, and
\nu_{1}>-2(\nu_{4}+\nu_{5})
. Of course, from (
1.3
), we still have
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
, i.e. the kinematic transport vanishes. The aim of this paper is to prove the global regularity of this more general case (but still special, comparing to the most general case, say, (
1.2
) and (
1.3
)) near the constant equilibrium
(u,d)=(\vec{0},\vec{i})
. More precisely, we study the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal model in the following form:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p&=\tfrac{\nu_{4}}{2}\Delta u-\mathrm{div}(\nabla d\odot\nabla d)+\mathrm{div}\sigma,\\
\mathrm{div}u&=0,\\
\ddot{d}-\Delta d&=(-|\dot{d}|^{2}+|\nabla d|^{2})d.\end{aligned}\right.

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
with the constraint
|d|=1
, where

\sigma_{ji}=\nu_{1}d_{k}d_{p}A_{kp}d_{i}d_{j}+\nu_{5}(d_{j}d_{k}A_{ki}+d_{i}d_{k}A_{kj})\,.

1.1.
Historical remarks

Important special case of (
1.1
)
\rho_{1}=0,\lambda_{1}=-1
is the so-called parabolic Ericksen-Leslie system, which has been extensively studied from the mid 80’s.The static analogue of the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system is the so-called Oseen-Frank model, whose mathematical study was initialed from Hardt-Kinderlehrer-Lin
[
11
]
. Since then there have been many works in this direction. In particular, the existence and regularity or partial regularity of the approximation (usually Ginzburg-Landau approximation as in
[
19
]
) dynamical Ericksen-Leslie’s system was started by the work of Lin and Liu in
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
. The simplest system preserving the basic energy law which can be obtained by neglecting the Leslie stress and by specifying some elastic constants. In 2-D case, the existence of global weak solutions with at most a finite number of singular times were proved by Lin-Lin-Wang
[
18
]
. Recently, Lin and Wang proved global existence of weak solution for 3-D case with the initial director field lying in the hemisphere in
[
23
]
.

For the more general parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, local well-posedness is proved by Wang-Zhang-Zhang in
[
26
]
, and existence of global solutions and regularity in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was established by Huang-Lin-Wang in
[
12
]
. For more complete review of the works for the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, please see the references therein above.

If
\rho_{1}>0
, (
1.1
) is an incompressible Navier-Stokes equations coupled with a wave map type system for which there is very few works, comparing the corresponding parabolic model, which is Navier-Stokes coupled with a heat flow. The only notable exception might be for the most simplified model, say, in (
1.1
), taking
\tilde{\sigma}=0
and
u=0
, and the spatial dimension is
1
. For this case, the system (
1.1
) can be reduced to a so-called nonlinear variational wave equation. Zhang and Zheng studied systematically the dissipative and energy conservative solutions
[
29
,
30
]
.

For the multidimensional case, there has been some progress on the hyperbolic system of liquid crystal. Very recently, De Anna and Zarnescu
[
4
]
considered the inertial Qian-Sheng model of liquid crystals. They derived the energy law and proved the local well-posdedness for bounded initial data and global well-posedness under the assumptions that the initial data is small in suitable norm and the coefficients satisfy some further damping property. Furthermore, for the inviscid version of the Qian-Sheng model, in
[
7
]
, Feireisl-Rocca-Schimperna-Zarnescu proved the global existence of the
dissipative solution
which is inspired from that of incompressible Euler equation defined by P-L. Lions
[
24
]
.

1.2.
The main theorem

To state our main theorem, we need some notations. Define the perturbed angular momentum operators by

\tilde{\Omega}_{i}u=\Omega_{i}u+A_{i}u,\ \tilde{\Omega}_{i}d=\Omega_{i}d,

where
\Omega=(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3})
is the rotation vector-field
\Omega=x\wedge\nabla
and
A_{i}
is defined by

A_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&-1&0\end{array}\right),\ \ A_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&-1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{array}\right),\ \ A_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
-1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right).

We define the scaling vector-field
S
by

S=t\partial_{t}+x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}.

Let

\Gamma\in\{\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},\tilde{\Omega}_{1},\tilde{\Omega}_{2},\tilde{\Omega}_{3}\}

and
Z^{a}=S^{a_{1}}\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}
, where
a=(a_{1},a^{\prime}):=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{8})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{8}
,
\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}=\Gamma^{a_{2}}\Gamma^{a_{3}}\cdots\Gamma^{a_{8}}
, we define

u^{(a)}:=Z^{a}u,\ \ d^{(a)}:=Z^{a}d.

The main result of this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
N_{0}:=60,N_{1}:=6,h:=6
, the fixed coefficients
\nu_{1},\nu_{4}
and
\nu_{5}
satisfy

(1.7)

\nu_{4}>0,\ \nu_{1}>-2(\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}),\ \nu_{5}>-\nu_{4},

and
(u_{0},d_{0},d_{1})
are initial data near equilibrium
(\vec{0},\vec{i},\vec{0})
satisfying the smallness assumptions

(1.8)

\sup_{|a|\leq N_{1}}\{\lVert u_{0}^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}+\lVert\nabla d_{0}^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}+\lVert d_{1}^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}\}\leq\epsilon_{0},

where
N(a)=N_{0}-|a|h
for
0\leq|a|\leq N_{1}
. Then there exists a unique global solution
(u,d)
of the system (
1.6
) with initial data

u(0)=u_{0},\ \ d(0)=d_{0},\ \partial_{t}d(0)=d_{1},

satisfies the energy bounds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{|a|\leq N_{1}}\{\lVert u^{(a)}(t)\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}+\lVert\nabla u^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}([0,t]:H^{N(a)})}\}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\lVert\nabla_{t,x}d(t)\rVert_{H^{N(0)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{0},

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{1\leq|a|\leq N_{1}}\lVert\nabla_{t,x}d^{(a)}(t)\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{0}(1+T)^{\bar{\delta}}.

for any
T\in[0,\infty)
, where
\bar{\delta}<10^{-7}
depends on
\epsilon_{0}
,
N_{0},N_{1},\nu_{1},\nu_{4}
, and
\nu_{5}
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i) Even though only the case
\nu_{1}
,
\nu_{5}\geq 0
was considered in

]
, their local wellposedness results for small data hold true when

or

(ii) We don’t intend to get optimal regularity, hence we choose

and

and the term

where the second order linear operator

1.3.
Main ideas

The main strategy to prove global regularity relies on an interplay between the control of high order energies and decay estimates, which is based on the method of space-time resonances developed by Germain, Masmoudi and Shatah
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. The main ingredients include decay estimates, energy and weighted energy estimates and
L^{2}
-bounds on the derivatives of profile
\widehat{\Psi}={\mathcal{F}}(e^{-it|\nabla|}\Phi)
associated with normalized solution
\Phi=\partial_{t}\phi+i|\nabla|\phi
. However, there are still some difficulties to get around.

In the proof of the Theorem
1.3
, we need various decay estimates of
u
. However, for the decay of
u
, the presence of linear operator
\tilde{L}
coming from
\mathrm{div}\sigma
brings the first difficulty. In order to get around the difficulty, we introduce the vector
v
and diagonalize the
u
-equation, i.e

(1.12)

v=\mathbb{U}u:=(u_{1},\tfrac{-i\partial_{2}}{\sqrt{-\partial_{2}^{2}-\partial_{3}^{2}}}u_{2}+\tfrac{-i\partial_{3}}{\sqrt{-\partial_{2}^{2}-\partial_{3}^{2}}}u_{3},\tfrac{-i\partial_{3}}{\sqrt{-\partial_{2}^{2}-\partial_{3}^{2}}}u_{2}+\tfrac{i\partial_{2}}{\sqrt{-\partial_{2}^{2}-\partial_{3}^{2}}}u_{3}).

Then the system (
1.10
) can be further rewritten as

(1.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v+Lv=\mathbb{U}\mathbb{P}(-{\mathbb{U}}v\cdot\nabla{\mathbb{U}}v-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}(\nabla\phi\partial_{j}\phi)+\nabla\otimes(\phi\otimes\nabla u)+Err11+Err12),\\
&\mathrm{div}v=0,\\
&\partial_{t}^{2}\phi-\Delta\phi=-\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla\phi-2u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi-u\cdot\nabla(u\cdot\nabla\phi)+Err2\end{aligned}\right.

where
{\mathbb{P}}
is the Leray projection and
L
is the operator

(1.14)

Lv:=\Big(-\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}\Delta v_{1}+\nu_{1}\frac{\partial_{1}^{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}+\partial_{3}^{2})}{|\nabla|^{2}}v_{1},-\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}\Delta v_{2}+\nu_{1}\frac{\partial_{1}^{2}(\partial_{2}^{2}+\partial_{3}^{2})}{|\nabla|^{2}}v_{2},(-\frac{\nu_{4}}{2}\Delta-\frac{\nu_{5}}{2}\partial_{1}^{2})v_{3}\Big)^{\top}.

Thus we can obtain the linear decay estimates of
e^{-tL}
, which will be used for the decay of
v
.

Remark 1.4
.

The coefficients condition (
1.7
) is used to ensure that the above
v
-equation is a parabolic equation. Note that the system (
1.10
) is a quasilinear parabolic-hyperbolic system with dissipation and dispersion effects. Moreover, it is the dissipation of
v
that ensures the boundedness of the higher order energies of
v
which will be used for the higher order energy estimates of
\phi
. In fact, the global existence of the system (
1.10
) will be tremendously difficult without dissipation effect.

In the proof of the decay of
v
, the quadratic term
\nabla\otimes(\phi\otimes\nabla u)
coming from
\mathrm{div}\sigma
brings the second difficulty. In fact, due to the presence of the quadratic term, the argument for the decay of
v
is not closed if we only use the decay of heat semi-group
e^{-tL}
and bootstrap assumptions. Here, we have to exploit the decay of
\phi
in
L^{\infty}
and
\nabla^{2}u
in
L^{2}
.

In proving the energy and weighted energy estimates and
L^{2}
-bounds on the derivatives of profile
\widehat{\Psi}
, the quadratic term
u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi
which comes from the second order material derivative term
(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla)^{2}\phi
brings the third difficulty. More precisely, since the decays of
u
and
\nabla\phi
are not good enough, we ultilize the structure of the quadratic term to gain more decay. To get around the difficulty, we define the normalized solution
\Phi
and its associated profile by

\Phi:=\partial_{t}\phi+i|\nabla|\phi,\ \ \Psi:=e^{-it|\nabla|}\Phi.

We write Duhamel’s formula in Fourier space for the profile
\Psi
as follows:

\widehat{\Psi}(t,\xi)\approx\widehat{\Phi}_{0}(\xi)+\sum_{\nu\in\{+,-\}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-is(|\xi|-\nu|\eta|)}\widehat{u}(\xi-\eta)i\eta\widehat{\Psi}_{\nu}(\eta)d\eta ds,

where
\Psi_{+}=\Psi,\Psi_{-}=\bar{\Psi}
. Then from the phase
|\xi|-\nu|\eta|
we know that the time resonant sets are

\mathscr{T}_{+}=\{(\xi,\eta):|\xi|-|\eta|=0\},\ \ \mathscr{T}_{-}=\{(\xi,\eta):|\xi|+|\eta|=0\}=\{(0,0)\},

and space resonant set is

\mathscr{S}_{\pm}=\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}(|\xi|\mp|\eta|)=0\}=\emptyset.

From this we obtain the space-time resonant set

\mathscr{R}_{\pm}=\mathscr{T}_{\pm}\cap\mathscr{S}_{\pm}=\emptyset.

Then, for weighted energy estimates, by
\phi
-equation one needs to consider the space-time integrals

(1.15)

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}e^{-is(|\xi|-\nu|\eta|)}m(\xi,\eta)\overline{\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}}}(\xi)\widehat{u^{(b)}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\Psi_{\nu}^{(c)}}(\eta)d\xi d\eta ds,

Hence, by the above resonance analysis, for the contribution of high-low interaction, i.e
|\xi|\approx|\xi-\eta|\gg|\eta|
and high-high interaction, i.e
|\xi|\ll|\xi-\eta|\approx|\eta|
, we may integrate by parts in time and then use the decay of
\partial_{s}u^{(b)}
and
\partial_{s}\Psi^{(c)}
to control (
1.15
). For the contribution of low-high interaction, i.e
|\xi|\approx|\eta|>|\xi-\eta|
, since the space resonant set
\mathscr{S}
is null, we may use integration by parts in
\eta
and the decay of
\lVert\nabla u^{(b)}\rVert_{L^{2}}
and
\Phi^{(c)}
to control the increment of (
1.15
)

For the
L^{2}
-bounds on the derivatives of
\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}}
, by bootstrap assumptions and the following observation

\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\partial_{\xi}\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}})\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\lVert S\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N}}+\lVert\Omega\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N}}+\lVert\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N}}+\lVert t\partial_{t}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N}},

it suffices to prove

\lVert\partial_{t}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon_{0}t^{-1+\kappa(a)\delta}.

Since the space-resonant set is empty, we may use integration by parts in
\eta
to gain the estimate.

1.5.
Outline

2.
Preliminaries and the main propositions

In this section, we start by summarizing our main definitions and notations.

2.1.
Some analysis tools

The Fourier transform of
f
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx.

We use
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f)
to denote the inverse Fourier transform of
f
. Fix an even smooth function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow[0,1]
supported in
[-2,2]
and equal to 1 in
[-1,1]
. For simplicity of notation, we also let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\rightarrow[0,1]
denote the corresponding radial function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, let

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(x):=\varphi(x/2^{k})-\varphi(x/2^{k-1}),\ \ \varphi_{I}(x):=\sum_{m\in I\cap{\mathbb{Z}}}\varphi_{m}(x),

\displaystyle\varphi_{\leq k}(x):=\sum_{l\leq k}\varphi_{l}(x),\ \varphi_{>k}(x):=\sum_{l>k}\varphi_{l}(x).

The frequency projection operator
P_{k}
,
P_{I}
,
P_{\leq k}
and
P_{>k}
is defined by the Fourier multiplier
\varphi_{k}(\xi)
,
\varphi_{I}(\xi)
,
\varphi_{\leq k}(\xi)
and
\varphi_{>k}(\xi)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{k}f}(\xi)=\varphi_{k}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\ \ \ \widehat{P_{I}f}(\xi)=\varphi_{I}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\leq k}f}(\xi)=\varphi_{\leq k}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\ \mathrm{and}\ \widehat{P_{>k}f}(\xi)=\varphi_{>k}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

Moreover, we have the following Bernstein inequality: For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(2.1)

\lVert P_{k}f\rVert_{L^{q}}\lesssim 2^{3k(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\lVert P_{k}f\rVert_{L^{p}},\ \ \ 1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

Let

\mathcal{J}:=\{(k,j)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}:k+j\geq 0\}.

For any
(k,j)\in\mathcal{J}
, let

\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{(k)}(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi_{\leq-k}(x),\ \mathrm{if}\ k+j=0\ \mathrm{and}\ k\leq 0,\\
&\varphi_{\leq 0}(x),\ \mathrm{if}\ j=0\ \mathrm{and}\ k\geq 0,\\
&\varphi_{j}(x),\ \mathrm{if}\ k+j\geq 1\ \mathrm{and}\ j\geq 1,\end{aligned}\right.

then for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
fixed,
\sum_{j\geq\max(-k,0)}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{(k)}=1
. For any
(k,j)\in\mathcal{J}
, we define the operator
Q_{jk}
by

(Q_{jk}f)(x):=\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{(k)}(x)\cdot P_{k}f(x),

and denote

f_{j,k}(x):=P_{[k-2,k+2]}Q_{jk}f(x).

Then
P_{k}f
for fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
can be decomposed as

(2.2)

P_{k}f(x)=P_{[k-2,k+2]}P_{k}f(x)=P_{[k-2,k+2]}\sum_{j\geq\max(-k,0)}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{(k)}(x)P_{k}f(x)=\sum_{j\geq\max(-k,0)}f_{j,k}(x).

Moreover, for any
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
(k,j)\in\mathcal{J}
, let
\beta:=1/1000
, we have

(2.3)

\lVert\widehat{f_{j,k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\min\{2^{3j/2}\lVert Q_{jk}f\rVert_{L^{2}},\ 2^{j/2-k}2^{\beta(j+k)}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\lVert Q_{jk}\Omega^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{2}}\},

see
[
13
, Lemma 3.4.]
.

We state several decay estimates and dispersive estimates.

Lemma 2.1

(Decay estimates)
.

(i) For any Schwartz function
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lVert e^{-tL}|\nabla|^{l}f\rVert_{\dot{W}^{N,q}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{l}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{W}^{N,p}},\ \ \ \ 1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

Proof.

By (
1.7
), we obtain

\frac{\nu_{4}}{2}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{\nu_{5}}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}=\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+\frac{\nu_{4}}{2}(\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}|\xi|^{2}+\nu_{1}\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}(\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}|\xi|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{2\nu_{1}}{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}\big(\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}-\frac{\xi_{1}^{4}}{|\xi|^{4}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{4}+\nu_{5}}{2}|\xi|^{2}\cdot\min\big\{1,1+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2(\nu_{4}+\nu_{5})}\big\}.

Then by the above two bounds, we apply a similar argument to decay estimates of heat operator to
e^{-tH}
and then obtain the bound (
2.4
).

∎

We also need the following Hardy-type estimate involving localization in frequency and space.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 3.5,
[
13
]
)
.

For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
let

F_{k}(f):=\lVert P_{k}f\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{3}\lVert\varphi_{k}(\xi)\partial_{\xi_{l}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\rVert_{L^{2}},\ \ B_{k}(f):=\big[\sum\limits_{j\geq\max(-k,0)}2^{2j}\lVert Q_{jk}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\big]^{1/2}.

Then, for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

B_{k}\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{|k^{\prime}-k|\leq 4}F_{k^{\prime}},\ \mathrm{if}\ k\geq 0,\\
&\sum_{k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}F_{k^{\prime}}2^{-|k-k^{\prime}|/2}\min\{1,2^{k^{\prime}-k}\},\ \mathrm{if}\ k\leq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 2.2,
[
27
]
)
.

If
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{6}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the following estimate holds:

\lVert\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}f(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})e^{i(x_{1}\cdot\xi_{1}+x_{2}\cdot\xi_{2})}\varphi_{k_{1}}(\xi_{1})\varphi_{k_{2}}(\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\rVert_{L^{1}_{x_{1},x_{2}}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{m=0}^{7}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}2^{mk_{j}}\lVert\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{m}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

To obtain the energy estimates, one often needs to analyze the symbols. Define a class of symbol as follows

\mathcal{S}^{\infty}:=\{m:{\mathbb{R}}^{6}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\ m\ \text{is continous and }\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}m\rVert_{L^{1}}<\infty\},

whose associated norms are defined as

\lVert m\rVert_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}:=\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}m\rVert_{L^{1}},

and

\lVert m\rVert_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}_{k,k_{1},k_{2}}}:=\lVert m(\xi,\eta)\varphi_{k}(\xi)\varphi_{k_{1}}(\xi-\eta)\varphi_{k_{2}}(\eta)\rVert_{\mathcal{S^{\infty}}}.

Then we have

Lemma 2.4

(Bilinear estimate,
[
14
]
)
.

Given
m\in\mathcal{S}^{\infty}
and two well-defined functions
f_{1},\ f_{2}
, then the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}m(\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{f}_{2}(\eta)d\eta\big)(x)\rVert_{L^{r}}\lesssim\lVert m\rVert_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}\lVert f_{L^{1}}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert f_{2}\rVert_{L^{q}},\ \ \frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}.

By standard Littlewood-Paley decomposition and Hölder, we can obtain the following Hardy-type inequality.

Lemma 2.5

(Hardy-type inequality)
.

For any
1<p<\infty
and two well-defined functions
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
, then the following estimates holds:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lVert g_{1}g_{2}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{N}}\lesssim\sum_{k}2^{Nk^{+}}\lVert P_{k}g_{1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert g_{2}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{N}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lVert g_{1}g_{2}\rVert_{W^{N,p}}\lesssim\lVert g_{1}\rVert_{W^{N,p}}\lVert g_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert g_{1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert g_{2}\rVert_{W^{N,p}}.

Finally, we need to record the following weighted
L^{\infty}-L^{2}
estimate

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 3.3,
[
25
]
)
.

Let
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
r=|x|
, then there holds

(2.7)

\lVert\langle r\rangle f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\lVert\partial_{r}\tilde{\Omega}^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}\lVert\tilde{\Omega}^{\alpha}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2},

provided the right hand side is finite.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
N\geq 0
and Schwartz function
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we have

(2.8)

\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{f})\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\lVert x\cdot\nabla_{x}f\rVert_{H^{N}}+\lVert\Omega f\rVert_{H^{N}}+\lVert f\rVert_{H^{N}}.

Proof.

This bound is obtained by the relation

|\xi|\nabla_{\xi}=\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}(\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi})-\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\wedge\Omega(\xi),

where
\Omega(\xi)=\xi\wedge\nabla_{\xi}
.
∎

2.2.
The main bootstrap proposition

By standard argument, after applying vector fields
Z
to the system (
1.10
) and make a change of unknown

v^{(a)}:={\mathbb{U}}u^{(a)},

we can derive that

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{(a)}+Lv^{(a)}+{\mathbb{U}}{\mathbb{P}}L_{1}(u)=&-{\mathbb{U}}{\mathbb{P}}\sum_{b+c=a}C_{a}^{b}[u^{(b)}\cdot\nabla u^{(c)}+\partial_{j}(\nabla(S-1)^{b_{1}}\Gamma^{b^{\prime}}\phi\cdot\partial_{j}(S-1)^{c_{1}}\Gamma^{c^{\prime}}\phi)]\\
&-{\mathbb{U}}{\mathbb{P}}(S+1)^{a_{1}}\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}\partial_{j}(\phi\otimes\nabla u)+(S+1)^{a_{1}}\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}(Err11+Err12)),\\
\mathrm{div}u^{(a)}=&0,\\
\partial_{t}^{2}\phi^{(a)}-\Delta\phi^{(a)}=&-\sum_{b+c=a}C_{a}^{b}(\partial_{t}u^{(b)}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(c)}+2u^{(b)}\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi^{(c)})\\
&-\sum_{b+c+e=a}C_{a}^{b,c}u^{(b)}\cdot\nabla(u^{(c)}\cdot\nabla\phi^{(e)})+(S+2)^{(a_{1})}\Gamma^{(a^{\prime})}Err2,\end{aligned}\right.

where
L_{1}(u)
is the lower order terms from the commutation between
S,\tilde{\Omega}
and
\partial
,

L_{1}(u):=-\sum_{0\leq i\leq a_{1}-1}\frac{\nu_{4}}{2}C_{a_{1}}^{i}(-1)^{a_{1}-i}\Delta S^{i}\Gamma^{a^{\prime}}u-(L_{1,c_{1}}u^{(c_{1})},L_{2,c_{2}}u^{(c_{2})},L_{3,c_{3}}u^{(c_{3})})^{\top},

and where
L_{j,c_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq 3
are second order differential operator,

C_{a}^{b}:=\frac{a!}{b!(a-b)!},\ C_{a}^{b,c}:=\frac{a!}{b!c!(a-b-c)!}.

To state the main proposition we review the normalized solution
\Phi
and it’s profile
\Psi
, i.e for
|a|\leq N_{1}

(2.10)

\Phi^{(a)}:=(\partial_{t}+i|\nabla|)\phi^{(a)},\ \ \Psi^{(a)}:=e^{-it|\nabla|}\Phi^{(a)}.

The function
\phi
can be recovered from the normalized variable
\Phi
by the formulas

\phi=\tfrac{1}{2i}|\nabla|^{-1}(\Phi-\overline{\Phi}).

Our main result is the following proposition:

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume that
(v,\phi)
is a solution to (
1.13
) on some time interval
[0,T]
,
T\geq 1
with initial data satisfying the assumptions (
1.11
). Assume also that the solution satisfies the bootstrap hypothesis

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{|a|\leq N_{1},t\in[0,T]}\{\lVert v^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}+\lVert\nabla v^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}([0,t]:H^{N(a)})}+\langle t\rangle^{-\kappa(a)\delta}\lVert\Phi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}\}\leq\epsilon_{1},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{|a|\leq N_{1}-1,t\in[0,T]}\langle t\rangle^{-\kappa(|a|+1)\delta}\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\partial_{\xi}\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}})\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)}}\leq\epsilon_{1},

where
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{0}^{2/3}
,
\delta=10^{-10}
,

\displaystyle\kappa(0)=0;\ \kappa(a)=1,\ \mathrm{for}\ 1\leq|a|\leq N_{1}-2;\ \kappa(a)=2,\ \mathrm{for}\ N_{1}-1\leq|a|\leq N_{1}.

\displaystyle N(a)=N_{0}-|a|h,\ N_{0}=60,\ h=6.

Then the following improved bounds hold

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{|a|\leq N_{1},t\in[0,T]}\{\lVert v^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}+\lVert\nabla v^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}([0,t]:H^{N(a)})}+\langle t\rangle^{-\kappa(a)\delta}\lVert\Phi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(a)}}\}\lesssim\epsilon_{0},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{|a|\leq N_{1}-1,t\in[0,T]}\langle t\rangle^{-\kappa(|a|+1)\delta}\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\partial_{\xi}\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}})\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{0}.

The bounds (
2.12
) and (
2.14
) are our main
L^{2}
bounds on the derivatives of the profile
\Psi^{(a)}
in the Fourier space. They correspond to weighted bounds in the physical space which plays an important role in the energy estimates.

From the assumption (
2.12
) and Lemma
2.2
, we give the following useful bound.

Corollary 2.9
.

With the notation and hypothesis in Proposition
2.8
, we have for any
|a|\leq N_{1}-1
,

(2.15)

\Big(\sum_{(k,j)\in\mathcal{J}}2^{2N(|a|+1)k^{+}+2k}2^{2j}\lVert Q_{jk}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+1)\delta}.

Proof.

The corollary is an easy consequence of Lemma
2.2
and (
2.12
). Indeed, if
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{(k,j)\in\mathcal{J};j\geq 0,k\geq 0}2^{2N(|a|+1)k^{+}+2k}2^{2j}\lVert Q_{jk}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert 2^{N(|a|+1)k^{+}+k}\sum_{|k^{\prime}-k|\leq 4}F_{k^{\prime}}(\Psi^{(a)})\rVert_{l^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)+1}}+\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\partial_{\xi}\widehat{\Psi^{(a)}})\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+1)\delta}.

If
k<0
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{(k,j)\in\mathcal{J};j\geq-k,k<0}2^{2N(|a|+1)k^{+}+2k}2^{2j}\lVert Q_{jk}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert 2^{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}F_{k^{\prime}}(\Psi^{(a)})2^{-|k-k^{\prime}|/2}\min(1,2^{k^{\prime}-k})\rVert_{l^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\sum_{k^{\prime}>k}2^{k^{\prime}}F_{k^{\prime}}(\Psi^{(a)})2^{3(k-k^{\prime})/2}+\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq k}2^{k^{\prime}}F_{k^{\prime}}(\Psi^{(a)})2^{(k^{\prime}-k)/2}\rVert_{l^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert 2^{k}F_{k}(\Psi^{(a)})\rVert_{l^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+1)\delta}.

This completes the proof of the Corollary.
∎

Once Proposition
2.8
is proved, Theorem
1.3
follows directly from the standard continuity argument. The rest of this paper focuses on the proof of Proposition
2.8
. The key ingredients include Proposition
\ref{Prop_Ene_Sob}-\ref{Prop_Phia}
and Proposition
5.1
.

3.1.

In order for the decay estimates of

Lemma 3.1

(Frequency localized linear decay estimate)

For any

(3.1)

\lVert e^{it|\nabla|}P_{k}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lVert\widehat{P_{k}f}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(t^{-1}2^{2k}+t^{-1+\delta}2^{2k+\delta k})+\lVert\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{P_{k}f}\rVert_{L^{2}}(t^{-1}2^{3k/2}+t^{-1+\delta}2^{3k/2+\delta k}).

27

3.1

Next, we use the dispersive estimates to give the decay of

Lemma 3.2
.

With the notations and hypothesis in Proposition
2.8
, for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

) and (

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq hk^{+}}2^{3j/2}\lVert Q_{jk}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j>hk^{+}}2^{j/2-k+\beta(j+k)}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\lVert Q_{jk}\tilde{\Omega}^{\alpha}\Psi^{(a)}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle 2^{hk^{+}/2}\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+2)\delta}2^{-N(|a|+1)k^{+}-k}+2^{-hk^{+}/2}\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+2)\delta}2^{-N(|a|+2)k^{+}-k}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{\kappa(|a|+2)\delta}2^{-N(|a|+1)k^{+}+hk^{+}/2-k}.

On the other hand, when

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{P_{k}\Psi^{(a)}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim

∎

3.2

3.2.
Decay of

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{t}v^{(a)}(t)\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{-1/2+(3+|a|)\delta}.

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(|\xi|\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{u^{(a)}})\rVert_{H^{N(|a|+1)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{1/2+(3+|a|)\delta},\ \mathrm{for}\ |a|\leq N_{1}-1.

Proof.

Step 1: Prove the estimate (
3.5
).
We prove the bound by induction. Assume that

(3.8)

\lVert\nabla^{2}v^{(c)}\rVert_{H^{N(|c|+1)}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}\langle t\rangle^{-1/2+(3+|c|\delta)},\ \ \mathrm{for}\ |c|<|a|,

By Duhamel’s formula and (
2.9

(3.9)

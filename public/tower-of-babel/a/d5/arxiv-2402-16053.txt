Reducing multivariate independence testing to two bivariate means comparisons
Kai Xu

Abstract
Testing for independence between two random vectors is a fundamental problem in statistics. It is observed from empirical studies that many existing omnibus consistent tests may not work well for some strongly nonmonotonic and nonlinear relationships. To explore the reasons behind this issue, we novelly transform the multivariate independence testing problem equivalently into checking the equality of two bivariate means. An important observation we made is that the power loss is mainly due to cancellation of positive and negative terms in dependence metrics, making them very close to zero. Motivated by this observation, we propose a class of consistent metrics with a positive integer γ \gamma that exactly characterize independence. Theoretically, we show that the metrics with even and infinity γ \gamma can effectively avoid the cancellation, and have high powers under the alternatives that two mean differences offset each other. Since we target at a wide range of dependence scenarios in practice, we further suggest to combine the p-values of test statistics with different γ \gamma ’s through the Fisher’s method. We illustrate the advantages of our proposed tests through extensive numerical studies.



Keywords:
consistent;
independence test;
two-sample mean test;
power enhancement.

Introduction

Testing for independence is a fundamental problem in statistics. It has received extensive attention over many decades.
In univariate scenarios, classic measures of association such as Pearson’s correlation
(
Pearson 1895
)
, Kendall’s
\tau

(
Kendall 1938
)
, and Spearman’s
\rho

(
Spearman 1904
)
are widely used. However, a drawback of these measures is that they may be zero when there is nonlinear association. In other words, tests for independence based on these measures may lack consistency against general types of alternatives. Alternative metrics, including Hoeffding’s
D

(
Hoeffding 1948b
)
, Blum-Kiefer-Rosenblatt’s
R

(
Blum et al. 1961
)
, and Bergsma-Dassios-Yanagimoto’s
\tau^{\ast}

(
Bergsma and Dassios 2014
)
, were developed to provide universally consistent guarantees against general types of dependence alternatives.
In many real-world applications, it is often necessary to test the independence between random vectors.

To test the multivariate independence, various methods
have been proposed in recent years.
A typical class of these methods is
to compare
the distance between joint characteristic or distribution function of random vectors and the product of their marginal characteristic or distribution functions.
Examples include
distance covariance
(
Székely et al. 2007
)
, Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion
(
Gretton et al. 2008
)
, Heller-Heller-Gorfine’s coefficient
(
Heller et al. 2013
)
, projection covariance

(
Zhu et al. 2017
)
, symmetric rank covariances
(
Weihs et al. 2018
)
,
multivariate Blum-Kiefer-Rosenblatt’s and Bergsma-Dassios-Yanagimoto’s coefficients
(
Kim et al. 2020a
)
,
interpoint-ranking sign covariance
(
Moon and Chen 2020
)
,
ball covariance
(
Pan et al. 2020
)
,
center-outward rank-based dependence measures
(
Shi et al. 2022a
;
Shi et al. 2022b
;
Deb and Sen 2023
)
and among others.
Other types of multivariate tests include, but are not limited to,
graph-based

(
Berrett and Samworth 2019
;
Shen et al. 2020
;
Azadkia and Chatterjee 2021
)
,
ensembling
(
Lee et al. 2022
)
and binning
(
Gorsky and Ma 2022
)
methods.

We also remark that a problem closely related to testing independence is testing the equality of distributions.
Testing for independence reduces to a two-sample problem
via introducing an auxiliary label vector
(
Heller and Heller 2016
;
Dhar et al. 2016
;
Heller et al. 2016
)
.
Conversely, however, it is still interesting to
design the consistent independence tests originating from the perspective of two-sample testing.

In this article, we novelly transform the independence testing problem into checking the equality of two bivariate means.
To elucidate the motivation behind this transformation, we first revisit the existing dependence metrics
under a unified framework:
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}+S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-2S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, where
S_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
for
l=1,2,3
denotes the
expectation of the product of kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, using different independent copies of random vectors
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
.
With different choices of functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
,
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

encompasses many popular dependence measures. For example, if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are Euclidean distance,
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}=E(\|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}\|\|{\bf y}_{1}-{\bf y}_{2}\|)
,
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}=E(\|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}\|\|{\bf y}_{3}-{\bf y}_{4}\|)
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}=E(\|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}\|\|{\bf y}_{1}-{\bf y}_{3}\|)
, then
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is distance covariance
(
Székely et al. 2007
)
.
We focus on the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
that make
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
equal to zero if and only if the multivariate independence holds.
Based on this, we make an improtant observation:
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}=S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}=S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
if and only if
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent.
This finding has an important implication.
That is, the independence testing problem can be equivalently transformed into testing whether
the bivariate mean vectors
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
and
(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})
are the same.

To test the null hypothesis
H_{0}^{\prime}:(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})=(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})
, we consider a class of metrics
with a positive integer
\gamma\in[1,\infty]
:
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\big\{(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}+(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}\big\}^{1/\gamma}
.
Mathematically, it aggregates the difference between
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, and the difference between
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
Based on suitable functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
,
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is zero if and only if
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent.

For
\gamma=1
, it can be seen that
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
reduces to
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})+(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
, which is accordant with the existing dependence metrics as mentioned before. However, we notice that under some nonmonotonic and nonlinear relationships,
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
are likely to have opposite signs, see simulation results in Table
1
of Section 4.3. The possible cancellation of positive and neagtive terms would yield small values of

\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
, leading to low powers of the tests based on the
\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
.
In this sense, our newly proposed
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with even and infinity
\gamma
’s can be viewed as significant complements to

\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
. Theoretically,
we establish the asymptotic null distributions of our proposed test statistics with different
\gamma
values under the null hypothesis of independence.
We show that, for odd
\gamma
values, the null limiting distribution is a quadratic function of Gaussian field, which
involves infinitely many nuisance parameters. We suggest to use a permutation method
to provide an approximation of the asymptotic null distribution. For even or infinity
\gamma
values, the null limiting distribution is a weighted half normal, indicating that our proposed test is asymptotically distribution-free.
Furthermore, we give a comprehensive analysis of how the
value of
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
varies with respect to
\gamma
, under different alternatives characterized by the signs of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, which forms the basis for power analysis. Since there exists a wide range of dependence scenarios in practice, we further suggest to combine the p-values of test statistics through
Fisher’s method
(
Fisher 1925
)
to
maintain high power performances.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present a general framework to encompass many state-of-the-art dependence metrics in the literature. In Section 3, we propose consistent dependence metrics by solving a two-sample test problem.
In Section 4, we develop the tests for independence, and
investigate their
limiting behaviors under the null hypothesis of independence and the alternative of dependence.
In Section 5, we introduce the combined probability tests and a random permutation test procedure. In Section 6, we consider extensions with unknown population distributions. We conduct numerical experiments in Section 7 to demonstrate the usefulness of our proposed methods, and provide a brief discussion in Section 8.
The details of proofs are relegated to an on-line Supplementary Material.

2
Preliminaries

To motivate our proposal, we first revisit the existing dependence metrics
under a unified framework.
For random vectors
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, we
define

f_{j}
as the kernel
function of
m
arguments in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}
, for
j\in\{1,2\}
and integer
m\geq 4
.
Using the first
m
independent copies of
({\bf x},{\bf y})
, we further define a general metric of dependence as

\displaystyle\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}+S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-2S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},

(1)

where
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
are defined as

\displaystyle S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle E[\psi_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}],

\displaystyle S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle E[\psi_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}],

\displaystyle S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle E[\psi_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}].

In the above displays,

\displaystyle\psi_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f_{1}({\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{m})f_{2}({\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3},{\bf y}_{4},{\bf y}_{5},\ldots,{\bf y}_{m}),

\displaystyle\psi_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f_{1}({\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{m})f_{2}({\bf y}_{3},{\bf y}_{4},{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{5},\ldots,{\bf y}_{m}),

\displaystyle\psi_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f_{1}({\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{m})f_{2}({\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{3},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{4},{\bf y}_{5},\ldots,{\bf y}_{m}).

They merely differ in the ordering of
{\bf y}_{i}
s in
f_{2}
.
It is worth noting that not all observations of

\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),i=1,\ldots,m\}
must be involved in
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.

The independence of
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
implies
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=0

in the family of distributions such that
E(f_{1})
and
E(f_{2})
exist and are finite.
This property is referred to as
I-consistency
of
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
by
Weihs et al. 2018
.
If the dependence of
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
implies
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\neq 0
,
then
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
has the
D-consistency
property.
It is worth noting that some choices of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
may make
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

fail to be D-consistent. For instance, in the bivariate case with
d_{1}=d_{2}=1
,
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
can be simplified to

\mbox{cov}^{2}({\bf x},{\bf y})
if we set
m=4
,
f_{1}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2},{\bf x}_{3},{\bf x}_{4})={\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}
and
f_{2}({\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3},{\bf y}_{4})={\bf y}_{1}^{\mbox{\tiny{T}}}{\bf y}_{2}
.
The test
based on such a metric estimator is inconsistent against all
dependent alternatives.
For this reason, we focus on the class of kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
so that
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
satisfies both I- and D-consistency.

In general, the metric defined in (
1
) covers a large number of nonparametric dependence measures.
In the following, we present
five important examples.
Define
{\bf z}
to be
{\bf x}
if
j=1
and to be
{\bf y}
if
j=2
, and the dimension of
{\bf z}
may differ for
j=1
and
j=2
.

Example 2.1

(
Székely et al. 2007
, distance covariance)
.
Distance covariance is
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with
m=4
and
f_{j}^{{\rm{dCov}}}({\bf z}_{1},\ldots,{\bf z}_{4})=\|{\bf z}_{1}-{\bf z}_{2}\|

.
Here,
\|\cdot\|
stands for the Euclidean norm. Thus, we have

Example 2.2

(

.
Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion based on a Gaussian kernel is

m=4

.

.
Denote the closed ball

.

, and

3
Consistent Dependence Metrics through Comparing Two Bivariate Means

We start from the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

(Equivalence)

Assume that kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
enable
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
in (
1
) to be finite and satisfy both I- and D-consistency.
Then, we have
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
are identical, if and only if
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent.

Theorem
1
establishes the equivalence between
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}=S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}=S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and the independence of
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
, based on suitable kernel functions.
Theorem
1
inspires us to transform the independence testing problem into checking the equality of two bivariate means, one is
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
and the other is
(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})
.
Therefore, instead of testing the independence of
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
directly, we can test an equivalent
null hypothesis

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636ptH_{0}^{\prime}:(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})=(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}),\;\text{versus}\;H_{1}^{\prime}:(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})\neq(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}).

We develop a new class of dependence metrics through solving a two-sample mean testing problem, which is defined as

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Big\{(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}+(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}\Big\}^{1/\gamma},

where the power index
\gamma\in[1,\infty]
is an integer. It can be seen that
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
measures
the difference between
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, as well as the difference between
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
We provide three specific scenarios for
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with different
\gamma
values.

(i) If
\gamma=1
, we obtain an
\ell_{1}
-norm-based dependence metric, which is equivalent to
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
defined in (
1
).
This implies
\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
encompasses many popular dependence measures, such as
distance covariance
(
Székely et al. 2007
)
, Heller-Heller-Gorfine coefficient
(
Heller et al. 2013
)
, projection covariance

(
Zhu et al. 2017
)
and among others.

(ii) If
\gamma=2
, we obtain an
\ell_{2}
-norm-based dependence metric,
which
can be expressed as

\mu_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\{0.5\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})^{2}+0.5(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})^{2}\}^{1/2}.

(iii) If
\gamma=\infty
, we obtain a maximum-type measure of dependence metric, which
can be expressed as
\mu_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\max(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}).

In fact, for any even
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
, we have
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\rightarrow\max(|S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}|,|S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}|)
.
Since
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})+(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
is nonnegative,
it always holds that

\max(|S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}|,|S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}|)=\max(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
.

The following theorem states the consistent properties of our proposed metric.

Theorem 2
.

(Consistency)

Assume that kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
enable
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
in (
1
) to be finite, non-negative and satisfy both I- and D-consistency.
Then, for any integer
\gamma\in[2,\infty]
, we have
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\geq 0
, and

\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=0
if and only if
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent.

Theorem
2
ensures that
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is able to capture any nonlinear dependence, which lays the foundation for independence testing.
Intuitively, as the value of test statistics increases, there is a stronger evidence to reject the null hypothesis.
For this reason, we first compare the behaviors of
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with different values of
\gamma
,
at the population level.

Theorem 3
.

(Ordering by the magnitude)

Assume that kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
enable
\mu_{f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
in (
1
) to be finite, non-negative and satisfy both I- and D-consistency.
When
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are dependent, we make the following conclusions.

(i)

If
\gamma
is even and

2\leq\gamma<\infty
,
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})\neq 0
and either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
is negative, we have

\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>0,

and
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a monotonically decreasing function of
\gamma
;

(ii)

If
\gamma
is odd and

2\leq\gamma<\infty
,
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})\neq 0
and either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
is negative, we have

\mu_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>0,

and
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a monotonically increasing function of
\gamma
;

(iii)

If both
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
are positive, we have

\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>\mu_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>0,

for

2\leq\gamma<\infty
, and
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a monotonically decreasing function of
\gamma
;

(iv)

If either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
is zero, we have

\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\mu_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>0,

for

2\leq\gamma<\infty
.

The assertions (i), (ii) and (iv) in Theorem
3
illustrate
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}
for
\gamma\geq 2

is always not smaller than

\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
in magnitude, as long as either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
takes negative values.
The intuition behind this is the effects of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}

offset each other, leading to a small magnitude of
\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
The assertion (iii) in Theorem
3
implies that
the converse is trivially true if both
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}

are positive.
In this sense, the new class of proposed metrics not only inherits the advantages of existing dependence metrics
\mu_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
, but also
serves as significant complements under the situations where
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
have opposite signs.

4

The Proposed Tests for Independence

4.1

The test statistics

Suppose that
\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),i=1,\ldots,n\}
is a random sample drawn from
the population
({\bf x},{\bf y})
. For
l\in\{1,2,3\}
and
n\geq m
, an unbiased U-statistic of
S_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}(n)_{m}^{-1}\sum\limits_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}^{n}\psi_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{i_{1}},{\bf y}_{i_{1}}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{i_{m}},{\bf y}_{i_{m}})\},

where
(n)_{m}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)
and the summation is taken over the indexes that are different from each other.
By replacing
S_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
with
\widehat{S}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
for
l\in\{1,2,3\}
,
the empirical versions of
\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}
for integer
\gamma\in[1,\infty]
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Big\{(\widehat{S}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-\widehat{S}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}+(\widehat{S}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-\widehat{S}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma}\Big\}^{1/\gamma},\quad\gamma\in[1,\infty),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\max(\widehat{S}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-\widehat{S}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},\widehat{S}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-\widehat{S}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}),\quad\gamma=\infty.

Then we utilize
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
as the
test statistic for independence, where
w_{n,\gamma}
is the rate of convergence, defined as

w_{n,\gamma}=\begin{cases}n^{(\gamma+1)/(2\gamma)},&\text{if $\gamma$ is odd},\\
n^{1/2},&\text{if $\gamma$ is even or $\gamma=\infty$}.\end{cases}

To investigate the asymptotic properties of the test statistic, we define

\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
for
l=1,2,3
to be the symmetric kernel

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}(m)_{m}^{-1}\sum\limits_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})}^{m}\psi_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{i_{1}},{\bf y}_{i_{1}}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{i_{m}},{\bf y}_{i_{m}})\}.

Define the symmetric kernel that is induced by
\widetilde{\psi}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
\widetilde{\psi}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, as

\displaystyle h_{f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\widetilde{\psi}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\psi}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}-2\widetilde{\psi}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}.

For
c\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
l\in\{1,2,3\}
, let

\displaystyle h_{c,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{c},{\bf y}_{c})\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle E[h_{f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}\mid{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{c}],\textrm{ and }

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{c,l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{c},{\bf y}_{c})\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle E[\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}\mid{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{c}],

be the respective projections of
h_{f_{1},f_{2}}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}
.

We focus on a finite candidate set
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{L}\}
, where
L<\infty
is a positive integer.
The following two subsections display the asymptotic joint distributions of
\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
under the null and alternative hypotheses.

4.2

Joint convergence of
\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
under independence

To facilitate the theoretical analysis, we make the following assumptions.

Assumption 1. Assume that the kernels
\widetilde{\psi}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
\widetilde{\psi}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, induced by the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, satisfy the following three
properties:

(i)

\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}
for
l\in\{1,2,3\}
has finite second moments.

(ii)

h_{f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}
has zero-mean and degenerates when
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent, that is,

h_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}=0
under independence.

(iii)

h_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),({\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})\}

admits the expansion

\displaystyle h_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),({\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})\}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2}),

under independence,
where
\{\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{k\geq 1}
are the eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenfunctions of the integral
equation

E[h_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),({\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{2},{\bf y}_{2})\}\mid{\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}]=\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}).

Assumption 1 is rather general. If both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are square integrable, then this assumption can be satisfied by selecting
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
from Examples 2.1-2.5.

Define the joint Gaussian random variables
\{{G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{k\geq 1}
, and
{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
where
{G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}
is the
k
-th independent and identically distributed standard normal
N(0,1)
,
and a single variable
{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\sim N(0,E[\widetilde{\psi}_{1,1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}]^{2})
.
The covariance
between
{G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
is defined as

\mbox{cov}({G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}},{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})=E\{\phi_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})[\widetilde{\psi}_{1,1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}

-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}]\}.

Theorem 4
.

(Joint limiting null distribution)

Suppose that Assumption 1 is
fulfilled.
When
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent,
the test statistics
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
converge in distribution to

\{W_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
,
where

W_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}m\{(m-1)\gamma/2\}^{1/\gamma}\Big\{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}-1)\Big\}^{1/\gamma}{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}^{1-1/\gamma},&\text{if $\gamma$ is odd},\\
m2^{1/\gamma}|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|,&\text{if $\gamma$ is even},\\
m|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|,&\text{if $\gamma=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Theorem
4
delivers several pieces of information. First, the test statistics
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),

\gamma\in\Gamma
are not asymptotically independent across different values of
\gamma
’s.
Second, for
\gamma=1
, the original test statistic
n\,\widehat{\mu}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is asymptotically distributed as

\{m(m-1)/2\}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}

({G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}-1)
, which is consistent with the conclusions drawn
from the literature for Examples 2.1-2.5.
Thrid, for an odd
\gamma
, the limiting null distirbution
W_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}
involves infinitely many unknown
parameters
\{\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{k\geq 1}
, making it intractable. In practice, a permutation procedure can be applied to obtain the p-values.
Its consistency will be established in Theorem
6
of Section
5
.
Fourth, for an even or infinity
\gamma
, the test statistic
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is asymptotically distributed as a weighted half normal, indicating that
the test based on
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is asymptotically distribution-free. To approximate the null distribution, we need to provide a consistent estimate for the variance of
{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
We employ a jackknife estimate, given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{0,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(n-1)(n-m)^{-2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg\{(n-1)_{m-1}^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m-1})\\
i\not\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m-1}\}\end{subarray}}^{n}\Bigg[\widetilde{\psi}_{1,1,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),({\bf x}_{i_{1}},{\bf y}_{i_{1}})\ldots,

\displaystyle({\bf x}_{i_{m-1}},{\bf y}_{i_{m-1}})\}-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),({\bf x}_{i_{1}},{\bf y}_{i_{1}})\ldots,({\bf x}_{i_{m-1}},{\bf y}_{i_{m-1}})\}\Bigg]\Bigg\}^{2}.

The convergence of U-statistics
(
Korolyuk and Borovskich 2013
)
guarantees that

\widehat{\sigma}_{0,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}
is a consistent estimate. Therefore, by applying Theorem
4
and the Slutsky theorem, we further obtain

n^{1/2}\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})/\{2^{1/\gamma}m\widehat{\sigma}_{0,f_{1},f_{2}}\}\sim|N(0,1)|

under the independence, for even or infinity
\gamma
.
This provides a computationally efficient way to compute p-values.

4.3

Joint convergence of
\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
under dependence

In this section, we turn to analyze the asymptotic power and assume the following condition.
Assumption 2. Assume that the kernels
\widetilde{\psi}_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
\widetilde{\psi}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
\widetilde{\psi}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, induced by the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, satisfy the following two
properties:

(i)

\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}
have finite second moments.

(ii)

h_{f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{m})\}
has positive mean and does not degenerate when
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are not independent, that is,

\mbox{var}\{h_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\}>0
under dependence.

Assumption 2 is also satisfied for square integrable functions, such as
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in Examples 2.1-2.5.
We further define the joint Gaussian random variables
{G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}}
,
where

{G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}\sim N(0,\mbox{var}\{\widetilde{\psi}_{1,1,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\})
,

{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}}\sim N(0,\mbox{var}\{\widetilde{\psi}_{1,2,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},

{\bf y}_{1})-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})\})

and their covariance is given by

{\mbox{cov}({G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}},

{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}})=\mbox{cov}\{\widetilde{\psi}_{1,1,f_{1},f_{2}}(

{\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1}),\ \widetilde{\psi}_{1,2,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})-\widetilde{\psi}_{1,3,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{1})
}
.

In Theorem
5
, we give an explicit description for the asymptotic joint distribution of
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
for
1\leq\gamma<\infty
, and
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
under general dependencies.

Theorem 5
.

(Joint limiting alternative distribution)

Suppose Assumption 2 is
fulfilled.
When
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are dependent,
the test statistics
\{n^{1/2}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})-n^{1/2}\,\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
converge in distribution to

\{W_{1,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
,
where

W_{1,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}&m\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}^{1-\gamma}\Big\{(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma-1}{G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}+(S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})^{\gamma-1}{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}}\Big\},\\
&\hskip 256.0748pt\text{if $\gamma$ is odd or even},\\
&m{G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}I(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}>S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})+m{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}}I(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}<S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})\\
&+m\max({G}_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},{G}_{4,f_{1},f_{2}})I(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}=S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}\neq S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}),\quad\text{if $\gamma=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Theorem
4
and Corollary
5.1
indicate that
w_{n,\gamma}\ \widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
can
serve as a statistic to test for the independence, which is consistent against general alternatives.

Corollary 5.1
.

(Consistency)

Suppose all assumptions in Theorem
2
and Theorem
5
hold.
When
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are dependent, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, it always holds that

(i)

\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\rightarrow\mu_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>0
in probability;

(ii)

w_{n,\gamma}\ \widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\rightarrow\infty
in probability.

Based on Theorems
3
–
4
and Corollary
5.1
,
we provide power analysis for the test statistics
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with different values of
\gamma
. Denote the observations
\mathcal{D}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),i=1,\cdots,n\}
. Let
\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
be the p-value of the test based on
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
, given by

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\mbox{pr}\Big(\{(m-1)\gamma/2\}^{1/\gamma}\Big\{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}-1)\Big\}^{1/\gamma}{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}^{1-1/\gamma}&\\
>w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})/m\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\Big),&\text{if $\gamma$ is odd},\\
\mbox{pr}\{|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|>w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})/(m2^{1/\gamma})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\},&\text{if $\gamma$ is even},\\
\mbox{pr}\{|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|>w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})/m\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\}&\text{if $\gamma=\infty$}.\end{cases}

Intuitively, the smaller the p-value, the more compelling the evidence to reject the null hypothesis.
We first consider the scenario where
\gamma
goes to infinity. For an odd
\gamma
, as
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\{(m-1)\gamma/2\}^{1/\gamma}\Big\{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,f_{1},f_{2}}({G}_{1,k,f_{1},f_{2}}^{2}-1)\Big\}^{1/\gamma}{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}^{1-1/\gamma}\rightarrow|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|,

in distribution. Accordingly, the p-value for odd
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
can be further simplified to

\mbox{pr}\{|{G}_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}|>w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})/m\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\}.

Since
w_{n,\gamma}/m
and
w_{n,\gamma}/(m2^{1/\gamma})
are almost the same as
\gamma\rightarrow\infty
,
comparing the values of p-value is equivalently transformed into comparing the magnitudes of
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
.
From Theorem
3
and Corollary
5.1
, no matter
\gamma
is even or odd,

\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>t\}
is a monotonic function of
\gamma
for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
.
Therefore, there must exist some sufficiently large
\gamma_{0}
, such that
\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>t\}
reaches its maximum at either
\gamma=\gamma_{0}
or
\gamma=\infty
.
This implies it is reasonable to focus on a finite candidate set
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{L}\}
, rather than an infinite one.

For any finite
\gamma
, the statistic
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is of order
O(1)
under the independence, as shown in Theorem
4
.
We need to consider the simultaneous variation of
w_{n,\gamma}
and
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
with respect to
\gamma
under the dependence.
For an odd
\gamma
,
w_{n,\gamma}
is a monotonically decreasing function of
\gamma
.
Under the altervatives where both
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
are positive,

\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>t\}
is also a monotonically decreasing function of
\gamma
for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
, which leads to the test with
\gamma=1
being most powerful.
However, under the altervatives where either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
is negative,
\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>t\}
becomes a monotonically increasing function of
\gamma
for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
.
The opposite trends of
w_{n,\gamma}
and
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
make the test achieve the best power with some odd
\gamma_{0}
.
For an even
\gamma
,
w_{n,\gamma}/(2^{1/\gamma})
is a monotonically increasing function of
\gamma
, while

\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})>t\}
has an opposite trend concerning
\gamma
, under the altervatives where either
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
is negative. This implies,
there also exists some
\gamma_{0}
that enables the asymptotic power based on
w_{n,\gamma_{0}}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma_{0},f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
to perform the best.

Based on
the aforementioned theoretical analysis,
we recommend to include small values such as 1 and 2, medium ones such as
3,\ldots,6
, and extremely large values such as
\infty
into
\Gamma
to balance the asymptotic and non-asymptotic performances.

Combined Probability Test and Permutation Test

In practice, since we target at a wide range of dependence alternatives, we suggest to combine the test statistics
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\gamma\in\Gamma
, through their respective p-values, to introduce an aggregated test of the independence.
There are several p-value combination methods available to use. For example, we employ the Fisher’s method
(
Fisher 1925
)
, then the test statistic is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}-2\log\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}).

Fisher’s method achieves the asymptotic optimality with respect to Bahadur
relative efficiency
(
Littell and Folks 1971
;
Littell and Folks 1973
)
. If we adopt the minimum combination
(
Xu et al. 2016
;
He et al. 2021
)
, the test statistic is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\text{min}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}-\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}).

The test statistic based on the Cauchy combination
(
Liu and Xie 2020
)
is

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\tan\left[\pi\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\right\}\right].

We report the numerical results based on these three types of combination methods in Section
7
, which do not reveal the significant differences. Thus, we can simply
use the Fisher’s method in practice.

By Theorem
4
,
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\gamma\in\Gamma
are not asymptotically
independent across different values of
\gamma
, and their asymptotic null distributions are not tractable if
\Gamma
contains odd numbers.
A permutation procedure is useful to approximate the null
distributions of test statistics and find the corresponding p-values.

Algorithm 1

The implementation of permutation-based procedure

Step 1
: Given the sample
\{({\bf x}_{i},{\bf y}_{i}),i=1,\ldots,n\}
and kernel functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, compute the test statistics
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
.

Step 2
: For
b=1,\cdots,B
, generate a random permutation of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, defined as
\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}\}
.
Then the permutation sample
{\bf y}^{b}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{{\tiny def}}}}{{=}}{\bf y}_{j_{i}}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Re-estimate the test statistics
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
.

Step 3
: We approximate the p-values
\widehat{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\gamma\in\Gamma
with permutation probabilities

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=B^{-1}\sum_{b_{0}=1}^{B}I\left\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b_{0}})>w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\right\},\gamma\in\Gamma.

The p-values
\widetilde{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b}),\gamma\in\Gamma
can be further calculated.

Step 4
: Compute the combined test statistics
\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
and

\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})
based on the p-values
\widetilde{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
and
\widetilde{p}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})
in Step 3, where
\widehat{T}\in\{\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}},\widehat{T}_{\text{min}},\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}\}
.

Step 5
:
The corresponding p-values of
\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
can be approximated with

\displaystyle B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}I\left\{\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})>\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\right\}.

Theorem
6
shows that the joint distribution of
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, given the observations
\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
converges to the asymptotic null distribution of
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
.
Therefore, the permutation procedure is consistent.
To prove this result, it is necessary to assume the finite
(2+\delta)
moment condition, which is similar to, but slightly stronger than, those specified in Assumptions 1-2.
Using a higher-order moment condition is essential to verify the uniform integrability for a class of random variables.

Assumption 3. For any
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq m
, there exists
\delta>0
such that the kernels

\widetilde{\psi}_{l,f_{1},f_{2}}\{({\bf x}_{1},{\bf y}_{i_{1}}),\ldots,({\bf x}_{m},{\bf y}_{i_{m}})\},

l\in\{1,2,3\}
, have finite
(2+\delta)
moments.

Theorem 6
.

(Limiting distributions of permutated statistics)

Suppose that Assumptions 1 and 3 are
fulfilled, we have

(i)

If
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent, conditional on the observations,
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
converges weakly to

\{W_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, where
\{W_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
are given in Theorem
4
;

(ii)

If
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are dependent, conditional on the observations,
\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
converges weakly to

\{\widetilde{W}_{0,\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, which is the asymptotic joint distribution of
\{w_{n,\gamma}

\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},\widetilde{\bf y})\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
.
Here,
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},\widetilde{\bf y})
is calculated through independent observations
\{({\bf x}_{i},\widetilde{\bf y}_{i}),i=1,\ldots,n\}
, where
\widetilde{\bf y}_{i}
is an independent copy of
{\bf y}_{i}
, and more importantly,
\widetilde{\bf y}_{i}
is independent of
{\bf x}_{i}
.

A direct application of Theorems
4
-
6
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 6.1
.

(Consistency of permutation)

Suppose that Assumptions 1-3 are
fulfilled.

(i)

If
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent, we have the permutation p-values
\mbox{pr}\{w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})>

w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\},\gamma\in\Gamma
and
\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})>\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\},\widehat{T}\in\{\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}},\widehat{T}_{\text{min}},\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}\}
, converge weakly to the uniform distribution on
(0,1)
;

(ii)

If
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are dependent, we have the permutation p-values
\mbox{pr}\{w_{n,\gamma}\ \widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})>

w_{n,\gamma}\ \widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\},\gamma\in\Gamma
and
\mbox{pr}\{\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}^{b})>\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\},\widehat{T}\in\{\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}},\widehat{T}_{\text{min}},\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}\}
, converge to zero in probability.

The assertion (i) in Corollary
6.1
implies that the proposed permutation tests have a correct asymptotic level.
The assertion (ii) in Corollary
6.1
shows that the tests based on

w_{n,\gamma}\ \widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\gamma\in\Gamma
and
\widehat{T}_{\Gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\widehat{T}\in\{\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}},\widehat{T}_{\text{min}},\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}\}
are consistent against all possible alternatives under the permutation procedures.

Extension to Allow for Population Unknown Function

We note that some choices of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
involve population distribution functions, then
the oracle
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}
cannot be computed directly from the observations.
For example,
f_{j}^{{\rm{RdCov}}}
includes the center-outward distribution function.
To obtain the empirical estimates
\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2}}
,
we need to plug in the empirical
center-outward distribution function
\widehat{\bf F}_{j,\pm}
, and the corresponding
estimate of
f_{j}^{{\rm{RdCov}}}
is then given by

\widehat{f}_{j}^{{\rm{RdCov}}}({\bf z}_{1},\ldots,{\bf z}_{4})=\|\widehat{\bf F}_{j,\pm}({\bf z}_{1})-\widehat{\bf F}_{j,\pm}({\bf z}_{2})\|.

If
{\bf z}
has a nonvanishing probability measure, it is implied by the Glivenko-Cantelli property of an empirical center-outward distribution function that

f_{j}
is uniformly bounded and
\widehat{f}_{j}
almost surely converges to
f_{j}
.

(2)

for
j\in\{1,2\}
.

The following proposition summarizes the asymptotic validity of the proposed tests.

Proposition 1
.

(Generalization)

Suppose that the conditions in (
2
) and Assumption 1 are
fulfilled.
If
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are independent, the test statistics

\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
are asymptotically equivalent
to their oracle versions
\{\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, that is,
w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,\widehat{f}_{1},\widehat{f}_{2}}-w_{n,\gamma}\,\widehat{\mu}_{\gamma,f_{1},f_{2}}=o_{p}(1),\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Theorem
4
, Proposition
1
and
Shi et al. 2022a
together indicate that if we choose the

Hence, obtaining p-values for rejection of the independence can be approximated via a
simulation-based procedure.

S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
or
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
takes negative values, and their absolute values are mostly comparable. This leads
to small values of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}+S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-2S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
As a consequence, dCov, HSIC, MGC, pCov, RdCov and
\widehat{T}_{1}
deteriorates sharply.
Their powers are considerably lower than those of our newly proposed
\widehat{T}_{\gamma},\gamma\geq 2

and combined tests
\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}}
,
\widehat{T}_{\text{min}}
, and
\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}
. HHG performs well, slightly trailing behind our proposed methods.

It is worth noting that the same phenomenon occurs more pronouncedly for other nonmonotonic and nonlinear relationships in Tables
5
-
7
.
It appears that the powers of dCov, HSIC, MGC, pCov, RdCov and
\widehat{T}_{1}

are close to the significance level
\alpha=0.05
, due to the cancellation effects of

S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and

S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
in Table
1
.
The newly developed test statistics with
\gamma\geq 2
significantly enhance the powers of independence tests compared to the original
\widehat{T}_{1}
.
The combined tests based on
\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}}
,
\widehat{T}_{\text{min}}
, and
\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}
have similar powers, and
maintain consistently admirable performances across all settings.

A real data analysis

In this section, we provide an empirical analysis of the proposed methods using a gene expression microarray dataset, which is
collected from twelve-week-old male rats
(
Scheetz et al. 2006
)
.
It contains tissue samples harvested from the eyes of 120 rats, with a total of 18,976 probe sets exhibiting sufficient expression signals.
We focus on the gene TRIM32 at probe 1389163
\_
at, which has been found to cause the Bardet-Biedl syndrome
(
Chiang et al. 2006
)
.
For the remaining genes, we select 300 probe sets that have the largest variances.
We are interested in whether these selected genes
are associated with the gene TRIM32.

We apply the proposed tests with
f_{1}=f_{2}=f^{{\rm{dCov}}}
to analyze this dataset, compared to the six competitors mentioned in the simulation section.
To evaluate their power performances, we
randomly select subsets of size
n=45
, 60, 75 and 90 from the whole data set.
We report the empirical powers of the testing methods based on 200 repetitions in Table
8
.
It can be seen that the newly proposed tests based on
\widehat{T}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma\geq 2
and combined test statistics
\widehat{T}_{\text{Fisher}}
,
\widehat{T}_{\text{min}}
, and
\widehat{T}_{\text{Cauchy}}

generally outperform the competitors dCov, HSIC, HHG, MGC, pCov, RdCov and
\widehat{T}_{1}
.
To get an insight into the power performances,
we further calculate the values of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
, which are charted in Table
9
.
We can observe that
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
offset
each other’s effects, leading to small values of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}+S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-2S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
.
This may partially explain the reasons for low powers of the existing methods.
Since genes at 1379830
\_
at, 1377651
\_
at and 1367555
\_
at rank at the top three for most of our proposed tests, we
provide a scatter plot of them
versus TRIM32 to visualize the relationships.
Figure
2
shows that there exist nonlinear relationships between the selected genes and TRIM32.

Discussion

In this article, we find that the multivariate independence testing problem can be equivalently transformed into testing whether
the bivariate mean vectors
(S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}})
and
(S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}},S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}})
are the same.
Based on this transformation, we further notice that the cancellation of
S_{1,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
and
S_{2,f_{1},f_{2}}-S_{3,f_{1},f_{2}}
may ruin the powers
of some existing tests. These observations motivate us to propose new tests to guarantee power performances.
We study the asymptotic behaviors of new tests
thoroughly
under the null hypothesis of independence and the alternatives of general dependence.
Since we face to a wide range of dependence patterns in practice, we further develop combined probability tests to
maintain high powers.
Our numerical results echo the theoretical findings under various types of strongly nonmonotonic and nonlinear relationships.
The concept to boost the testing power can also be extended to test mutual independence for a single multivariate vector, and to test conditional independence.

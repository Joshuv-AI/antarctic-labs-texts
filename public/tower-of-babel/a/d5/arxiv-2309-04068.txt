Two classes of reducible cyclic codes with large minimum symbol-pair distances Thanks:
Xiaoqiang Wang, Yue Su, Dabin Zheng, Wei Lu

Abstract
The high-density data storage technology aims to design high-capacity storage at a relatively low cost. In order to achieve this goal, symbol-pair codes were proposed by Cassuto and Blaum [ 1 , 2 ] to handle channels that output pairs of overlapping symbols. Such a channel is called symbol-pair read channel, which introduce new concept called symbol-pair weight and minimum symbol-pair distance. In this paper, we consider the parameters of two classes of reducible cyclic codes under the symbol-pair metric. Based on the theory of cyclotomic numbers and Gaussian period over finite fields, we show the possible symbol-pair weights of these codes. Their minimum symbol-pair distances are twice the minimum Hamming distances under some conditions. Moreover, we obtain some three symbol-pair weight codes and determine their symbol-pair weight distribution. A class of MDS symbol-pair codes is also established. Among other results, we determine the values of some generalized cyclotomic numbers.



I
Introduction

In 1948, in order to to solve the problem about reliable communication in an engineering problem, Shannon
[
22
]
showed that good codes exist, gave birth to information theory and coding theory. In 1950, Hamming weight and Hamming distance were introduce by Hamming, which gives the basis for classical error-correcting codes. Since than, a lot of works on Hamming metric of linear codes have been given. These works are based on that noisy channels are analyzed by dividing the messages into individual information units.

By the application of high-density data storage technologies, due to physical limitations, an individual symbol cannot be read off the channel and each channel read contains contributions from two adjacent symbols. In order to
protect against pair errors in symbol-pair read channels, symbol-pair codes were first studied by Cassuto and Blaum

[
1
,
2
]
. The minimum symbol-pair weight and minimum symbol-pair distance of a code over finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
were defined as follows:
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})
be a vector in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
and

\pi_{p}(\mathbf{x})=[(x_{0},x_{1}),(x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots,(x_{n-2},x_{n-1}),(x_{n-1},x_{0})]\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2})^{n}.

Two pairs
(c,d)
and
(e,f)
are distinct if
c\neq e
or
d\neq f
, or both. For any two codewords
\mathbf{c_{1}}
and
\mathbf{c_{2}}
, the symbol-pair distance
between
\mathbf{c_{1}}
and
\mathbf{c_{2}}
is defined as
{d_{p}}(\mathbf{c_{1}},\mathbf{c_{2}})=d_{H}(\pi_{p}(\mathbf{c_{1}}),\pi_{p}(\mathbf{c_{2}})),

where
{d_{H}}(\cdot)
denotes the usual Hamming distance.
The minimum symbol-pair distance of a code
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined to be

d_{p}({\mathcal{C}})=min\{d_{p}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c_{2}})\mid\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c_{2}}\in{\mathcal{C}},\,\,\mathbf{c}_{1}\neq\mathbf{c}_{2}\}.

Accordingly, the minimum symbol-pair weight of a code
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as

w_{p}({\mathcal{C}})=\min\{w_{p}({\bf c})\,|\,{\bf 0}\neq{\bf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}\},

where

w_{p}({\bf c})=\Big|\{i\,|\,(c_{i},c_{i+1})\,{\not=}\,(0,0),\,\,0\leq i\leq n-1,\,\,c_{n}=c_{0}\}\Big|.

It is clear that any cyclic code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
corresponds to a subset of the quotient ring

\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. Since every ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
must be principal,
{\mathcal{C}}
can be expressed as
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle g(x)\rangle
, where

g(x)
is a monic polynomial with the smallest degree and is called the generator polynomial. Let
h(x)=(x^{n}-1)/g(x)
, then
h(x)
is referred to as the check polynomial of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
The zeros of
g(x)
and
h(x)
are called zeros and non-zeros of
{\mathcal{C}}
. If
g(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is called an irreducible cyclic code. Otherwise,
\mathcal{C}
is called a reducible cyclic code.
Let
A^{p}_{i}
denote the number of nonzero codewords with symbol-pair weight
i
in
\mathcal{C}
. Similar as Hamming weight enumerator and Hamming weight distribution, the symbol-pair weight enumerator and symbol-pair weight distribution are defined by
1+A^{p}_{1}x+A^{p}_{2}x^{2}+\cdots+A^{p}_{n}x^{n}
and
(1,A^{p}_{1},\ldots,A^{p}_{n})
, respectively. If the number of nonzero
A^{p}_{i}
in the sequence
(1,A^{p}_{1},\ldots,A^{p}_{n})
is equal to
t
, then the code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a
t
symbol-pair weight code.

In recent years, many researchers focused on symbol-pair codes and made a lot of progress on this topic. In

II-A

The generic construction of cyclic codes

In the subsection, a generic construction of cyclic codes with two nonzeros is introduced, which was first given
by Ma et al. in
[
21
]
. Recall that
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
is the cyclic code of length
n=\frac{h(r-1)}{q-1}
with the check polynomial
m_{g^{-1}}(x)m_{(\beta g)^{-1}}(x)
. By the Delsarte’s theorem,
the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
can be expressed as

{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}=\left\{{\bf c}(a,b)\,:\,a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}\right\},

(1)

where

{\bf c}(a,b)=({\rm Tr}_{q}^{r}(ag^{0}+b(\beta g)^{0}),{\rm Tr}_{q}^{r}(ag+b(\beta g)),\ldots,{\rm Tr}_{q}^{r}(ag^{n-1}+b(\beta g)^{n-1})).

It is clear that the symbol-pair weight of the codeword
{\bf c}(a,b)
is equal to

{\rm wt_{p}}({\bf c}(a,b))=n-T(a,b),

(2)

where

T(a,b)=\left|\left\{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\,:\,{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(ag^{i}+b(\beta g)^{i})=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(ag^{i+1}+b(\beta g)^{i+1})=0\right\}\right|.

Since
\langle g\rangle=\langle\alpha^{(q-1)/h}\rangle
and
\{g^{i}\,:\,0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\}=C_{0}^{((q-1)/h,r)}
, we have

\displaystyle T(a,b)

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{x\in C_{0}^{((q-1)/h,r)}\,:\,{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(ax+b\beta^{log_{g}x}x)=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(agx+b\beta^{log_{g}gx}gx))=0\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{x\in C_{0}^{((q-1)/h,r)}}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi_{1}(z{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(ax+b\beta^{log_{g}x}x))\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi_{1}(y{\rm Tr}^{r}_{q}(agx+b\beta^{log_{g}gx}gx))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{x\in C_{0}^{((q-1)/h,r)}}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(z(ax+b\beta^{log_{g}x}x))\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(y(agx+b\beta^{log_{g}gx}gx)).

It is easy to check that

C_{0}^{((q-1)/h,r)}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{e-1}C_{(q-1)i/h}^{((q-1)e/h,r)},

then we obtain

\displaystyle T(a,b)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{((q-1)e/h,r)}}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(axz+\beta^{i}bxz)\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(agxy+\beta^{i+1}bgxy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2}{q^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{((q-1)e/h,r)}}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(xz(a+\beta^{i}b))

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{((q-1)e/h,r)}}\sum_{z,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(xz(a(1+gy)+\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy))).

Let
d={\rm gcd}(m,\tfrac{e(q-1)}{h})
, from Lemma
1
we can get the following multiset equality

\displaystyle\left\{xz:x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{((q-1)e/h,r)},z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}\right\}=\tfrac{hd}{e}\ast C_{(q-1)i/h}^{(d,r)}.

Then we have

\begin{split}T(a,b)=&\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2hd}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{(d,r)}}\chi(x(a+\beta^{i}b))\\
&+\tfrac{hd}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in C_{(q-1)i/h}^{(d,r)}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a(1+gy)+\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy))).\end{split}

(3)

In general, it is very difficult to determine the value distribution of
T(a,b)
for
(a,b)
running through
(\mathbb{F}_{r},\mathbb{F}_{r})
.
In the sequel we will compute the value distribution of
T(a,b)
for
m
and
\frac{e(q-1)}{h}
satisfying some special relationships, and thus the symbol-pair weight distribution of the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
is determined from (
2
).

In order to determine the exponential sum

we need to introduce cyclotomic classes, cyclotomic numbers and Gaussian periods.

II-C

Gaussian periods

Recall that
\chi_{1}
and
\chi
are the canonical additive character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(c_{0})=e^{2\pi i{\rm Tr}_{p}^{q}(c_{0})/p}\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\chi(c_{1})=e^{2\pi i{\rm Tr}_{p}^{r}(c_{1})/p},

By the definition of canonical characters, it is clear that

\displaystyle\sum_{c_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}}\chi(c_{1}x)=\begin{cases}r,&{\rm if}\ x=0,\\

\displaystyle\eta_{i}^{(N,r)}=\sum_{x\in C_{i}^{(N,r)}}\chi(x),

\displaystyle i=0,1,2,\ldots,N-1.

.

Lemma 6
.

Let

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{(2,r)}=\begin{cases}\frac{-1-q^{\frac{m}{2}}}{2},&{\rm if}\,\,p\equiv 1\,\,{\rm(mod\,\,4)}\,\,{\rm and}\,\,sm\equiv 0\,\,{\rm(mod\,\,2)},\,\,{\rm or}\,\,p\equiv 3\,\,{\rm(mod\,\,\,4)}\,\,{\rm and}\,\,sm\equiv 0\,\,{\rm(mod\,\,4)},\\

III
The symbol-pair weight distribution of the first class of cyclic codes

In this section, we main consider the symbol-pair weight distribution of

\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,h,e)}
for the case that
m>2
is a positive integer and
{\rm gcd}(m,\tfrac{e(q-1)}{h})=1
, where
q
is a prime power. In order to obtain the desired result, we first present the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Let
m>2
,
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq e-1
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
, then
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
if and only if
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
y_{1}=y_{2}
.

Proof.

If
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
y_{1}=y_{2}
, it is clear that
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
. We now prove that
i_{1}=i_{2}
and
y_{1}=y_{2}
if
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
.

If
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
, without loss of generality, we assume that
i_{2}>i_{1}
. Let
k=i_{2}-i_{1}
, from
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
, we obtain that

(1+\beta gy_{1})(1+gy_{2})=\beta^{k}(1+\beta gy_{2})(1+gy_{1}),

which is the same as

(\beta^{k}-1)+(\beta^{k}-\beta)gy_{1}+(\beta^{k+1}-1)gy_{2}+(\beta^{k+1}-\beta)g^{2}y_{1}y_{2}=0.

Then,

(\beta^{k}-1)(1+\beta g^{2}y_{1}y_{2})=-\beta(\beta^{k-1}-1)gy_{1}-(\beta^{k+1}-1)gy_{2}.

Since
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
\beta^{k}\neq 1
for
0<k\leq e-1
, we have

\tfrac{1+\beta g^{2}y_{1}y_{2}}{g}=\tfrac{-\beta(\beta^{k-1}-1)y_{1}-(\beta^{k+1}-1)y_{2}}{(\beta^{k}-1)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

So, we obtain

\left(\frac{1+\beta g^{2}y_{1}y_{2}}{g}\right)^{q}=(g^{-1}+\beta gy_{1}y_{2})^{q}=g^{-1}+\beta gy_{1}y_{2}.

(4)

It is obvious that

(g^{-1}+\beta gy_{1}y_{2})^{q}=g^{-q}+(\beta gy_{1}y_{2})^{q}=g^{-q}+\beta y_{1}y_{2}g^{q}.

(5)

Combining (
4
) and (
5
), we have

g^{-1}-g^{-q}=\beta y_{1}y_{2}(g^{q}-g).

(6)

It is clear that
g^{q+1}(g^{-1}-g^{-q})=g^{q}-g
. Since
g\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, from (
6
) we have
g^{-(q+1)}=\beta y_{1}y_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,
g^{1-q^{2}}=1
. By the definition of
g
, we know that

{\rm ord}(g)=\frac{h(r-1)}{q-1},
then
\frac{h(r-1)}{q-1}\,|\,q^{2}-1
, which is contradictory to
r=q^{m}
and
m>2
. Hence, we have
i_{1}=i_{2}
if
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
.

If
i_{1}=i_{2}
, then
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}
becomes
\frac{1+\beta gy_{1}}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{1+\beta gy_{2}}{1+gy_{2}}
,
which is the same as

(1+\beta gy_{1})(1+gy_{2})=(1+gy_{1})(1+\beta gy_{2}).

Hence, we have

(\beta-1)gy_{1}=(\beta-1)gy_{2},

i.e.,
y_{1}=y_{2}
since
\beta-1\neq 0
. The desired conclusion then follows.
∎

Remark 8
.

When
m=2
, Lemma
7
does not hold. By the similar way as in Lemma
7
, we can obtain the following result. Let
m=2
,
0\leq i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\leq e-1
and
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
, then
\frac{\beta^{i_{1}}(1+\beta gy_{1})}{1+gy_{1}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{2}}(1+\beta gy_{2})}{1+gy_{2}}=\frac{\beta^{i_{3}}(1+\beta gy_{3})}{1+gy_{3}}
if and only if
i_{1}=i_{2}=i_{3}
and

.

To determine the symbol-pair weight distribution of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
, we next deal with the value
distribution of the exponential sum
T(a,b)
, which is given in (
3
).

Proposition 9
.

Let
m>2
be a positive integer and
{\rm gcd}(m,\tfrac{e(q-1)}{h})=1
. When
(a,b)
runs through
(\mathbb{F}_{r},\mathbb{F}_{r})
, the value distribution of
T(a,b)
is given as follows:

T(a,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\frac{h(r-1)}{q-1},&1&{\rm time,}\\
\tfrac{h(q^{m-2}-1)}{q-1},&(r-1)(r+1-eq)&{\rm times,}\\
\tfrac{h(er+2rq-2r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)},&e(r-1)&{\rm times,}\\
\tfrac{h(er+rq-r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)},&e(q-1)(r-1)&{\rm times.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Since
d=\gcd(m,\tfrac{e(q-1)}{h})=1
, we have
C_{(q-1)i/h}^{(d,r)}={\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}
for
0\leq i\leq e-1
. Then
T(a,b)
in (
3
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle T(a,b)=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a+\beta^{i}b))+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a(1+gy)+\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy))).

Let

D=\left\{\frac{\beta^{i}(1+\beta gy)}{1+gy}:i=0,1,2,\ldots,e-1,\,\,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}\right\}.

(7)

It is obvious that
\langle\beta\rangle\cap D={\O}
, where
\langle\beta\rangle
denotes
the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{*}
generated by
\beta
. Otherwise, there exists
\beta^{j}
such that

\frac{1+\beta gy}{1+gy}=\beta^{j},

(8)

where
0\leq j\leq e-1
. If
j=1
, then (
8
) does not hold. If
j\neq 1
, then
\beta^{j}+\beta^{j}gy=1+\beta gy
, i.e.,
g=\frac{\beta^{j}-1}{(\beta-\beta^{j})y}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
since
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is contradictory to
g\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We now prove this theorem from the following six cases.

Case 1:

a=0
and
b=0
. In this case, by the definition of
T(a,b)
, we have

\displaystyle T(a,b)=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q-1}.

Case 2:

a\neq 0
and
b=0
. In this case,
T(a,b)
becomes

\displaystyle T(a,b)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\chi(ax)+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(xa(1+gy))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}e(-1)+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}e(q-1)(-1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(q^{m-2}-1)}{q-1}.

In the second equality, we used the fact that for any
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
, we have
1+gy\neq 0
.

Case 3:

a=0
and
b\neq 0
. In this case,
T(a,b)
becomes

\displaystyle T(a,b)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\chi(\beta^{i}bx)+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(x\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy)).

By a discussion similar to Case 2, we have
T(a,b)=\frac{h(q^{m-2}-1)}{q-1}
.

Case 4:

a\neq 0
,
b\neq 0
,
-\tfrac{a}{b}\notin\langle\beta\rangle
and
-\tfrac{a}{b}\notin D
, where
D
is given in (
7
).
By an analysis similar to Case 2, we obtain
T(a,b)=\frac{h(q^{m-2}-1)}{q-1}
.

Case 5:

a\neq 0
,
b\neq 0
and
-\frac{a}{b}\in D
. From Lemma
7
, we know that for different pairwise
(i,y)
, the values of
\frac{\beta^{i}(1+\beta gy)}{1+gy}
are different, where
0\leq i\leq e-1
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
. Then

\displaystyle T(a,b)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a+\beta^{i}b))+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a(1+gy)+\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy)))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}e(-1)+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}[r-1+(q-2)(-1)+(e-1)(q-1)(-1)]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(er+rq-r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)}.

In the second equality, we used the fact that
a+\beta^{i}b\neq 0
for
0\leq i\leq e-1
since
|\langle\beta\rangle|=e
and
\langle\beta\rangle\cap D={\O}
.

Case 6:

a\neq 0
,
b\neq 0
and
-\tfrac{a}{b}\in\langle\beta\rangle
. By a discussion similar to Case 5, we have

\displaystyle T(a,b)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a+\beta^{i}b))+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{r}^{\ast}}\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(x(a(1+gy)+\beta^{i}b(1+\beta gy)))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)}+\tfrac{2h}{eq^{2}}[r-1+(e-1)(-1)]+\tfrac{h}{eq^{2}}e(q-1)(-1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{h(er+2rq-2r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)}.

From all the cases, we obtain that

\displaystyle T(a,b)=\begin{cases}\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q-1},&{\rm if}\,\,\,a=0\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,b=0,\\
\tfrac{h(er+2rq-2r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)},&{\rm if}\,\,\,a\neq 0,\,\,\,b\neq 0\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,-\tfrac{a}{b}\in\langle\beta\rangle,\\
\tfrac{h(er+rq-r-eq^{2})}{eq^{2}(q-1)},&{\rm if}\ a\neq 0,\,\,\,b\neq 0\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,-\tfrac{a}{b}\in D,\\
\tfrac{h(q^{m-2}-1)}{q-1},&{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

From Lemma
7
, we know that
|D|=e(q-1)
. Since
|\langle\beta\rangle|=e
and
\langle\beta\rangle\cap D={\O}
,
the desired conclusion then follows.
∎

∎

a=0
and
b=0

) and
\tfrac{h(r-1)}{q-1}\equiv 0\pmod{4}
. The following statements hold.

(1)

If
m>2
and
\gcd\left(m,\tfrac{2(q-1)}{h}\right)=1
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{(q,m,h,e)}
is an
\left[\tfrac{h(r-1)}{2(q-1)},2m\right]
code with the symbol-pair weight enumerator

1+e(r-1)z^{\frac{hq^{m-2}(eq+e-2)}{2e}}+e(r-1)(q-1)z^{\frac{hq^{m-2}(eq+e-1)}{2e}}+(r-1)(r+1-eq)z^{\frac{hq^{m-2}(q+1)}{2}}.

(2)

If
m\geq 2
and
\gcd\left(m,\tfrac{2(q-1)}{h}\right)=2
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{(q,m,h,2)}
is an
\left[\tfrac{h(r-1)}{2(q-1)},2m\right]
code with the minimum symbol-pair distance
d_{p}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{(q,m,h,2)})=\tfrac{(hq^{m-1}-hq^{\frac{m}{2}-1})}{2}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{(q,2,h,2)}
is an
\left[\tfrac{h(q+1)}{2},4\right]
code with the symbol-pair weight enumerator

1+(r-1)z^{\frac{h(q-1)}{2}}+(r-1)(q-1)z^{\frac{hq}{2}}+(r-1)(r+1-q)z^{\frac{h(q+1)}{2}}.

In addition,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{(q,2,2,2)}
is an MDS symbol-pair code.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the cyclic codes defined in
[
21
]
under the symbol-pair metric. The possible symbol-pair weights of these codes are obtained. The considered codes has three symbol-pair weights in some cases. In addition, when
e=2
, the codes achieves the largest minimum symbol-pair distances compared with their minimum Hamming distances.
The main contributions of this paper are the following:

•

The symbol-pair weight distribution of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
was given for the case that
m>2
is a positive integer and
{\rm gcd}(m,\tfrac{e(q-1)}{h})=1
(see Theorem
10
).

•

The possible symbol-pair weights and minimum symbol-pair distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,2)}
were derived for the case that
{\rm gcd}(m,\tfrac{2(q-1)}{h})=2
(see Theorem
15
and Theorem
27
).

•

The symbol-pair weight distribution of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,2,h,2)}
was given (see Theorem
24
and Theorem
28
).

•

The punctured code of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,m,h,e)}
was considered and a class of MDS symbol-pair codes was obtained (see Theorem
31
).

•

The exact values of a class of generalized cyclotomic numbers were determined (see Remark
21
).

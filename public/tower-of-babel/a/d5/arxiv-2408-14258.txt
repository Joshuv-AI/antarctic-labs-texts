Minimal model program for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures
Paolo Cascini, Jingjun Han, Jihao Liu, Fanjun Meng, Calum Spicer, Roberto Svaldi, and Lingyao Xie

Abstract
. For ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial klt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures, we prove the cone theorem, the contraction theorem, and the existence of flips. Therefore, we deduce the existence of the minimal model program for such structures. We also prove the base-point-freeness theorem for such structures of general type and establish an adjunction formula and the existence of ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial quasi-dlt modifications for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures.

1.
Introduction

We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

The minimal model program for foliations has been extensively developed in recent years, particularly for foliated surfaces
[
McQ08
,
Bru15
]
, foliated threefolds
[
CS20
,
Spi20
,
CS21
,
SS22
]
, and algebraically integrable foliations
[
ACSS21
,
CS23a
,
CHLX23
,
LMX24b
]
. However, it is also well known that many classical results in birational geometry that hold for algebraic varieties fail when attempting to extend those to foliations, e.g., abundance (cf.
[
McQ08
, Theorem 3 IV.5.11]
), effective birationality (cf.
[
Lü24
, Theorem 1.3]
), and Bertini-type theorems (cf.
[
DLM23
, Example 3.4]
,
[
CS23b
, 3.5.2]
).

One natural approach to overcome the failure of these important results is to introduce a more general class of structures which we will call

adjoint foliated structures
.
In simple terms, rather than considering the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
we consider the interpolated canonical divisor

tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
instead, where
0\leq t\leq 1
.

The major goal of this paper is to establish the existence of the minimal model program for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures; that is, to establish the cone theorem, the contraction theorem, and the existence of flips for adjoint foliated structures
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
with
\mathcal{F}
algebraically integrable. Our first main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an lc algebraically integrable foliation on a smooth projective variety
X
and
t\in[0,1]
a real number. Then we can run a
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
-MMP.

Theorem
1.1
was proved when
t=0
in
[
BCHM10
]
and when
t=1
in
[
LMX24b
]
. For further details on the exact technical statements that are proven in this paper, we refer the reader to the next subsections of the introduction.

The extra condition on the algebraic integrability of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
allows us to deal with adjoint foliated structures in dimension higher than two, thus achieving in this context a greater generality than in
[
SS23
]
.
Indeed, for algebraically integrable foliations the (non-adjoint) foliated MMP has already been established in
[
ACSS21
,
CS23a
,
CHLX23
,
LMX24b
]
, whereas for foliations that are not necessarily algebraically integrable the existence of MMP remains open in dimensions
\geq 4
;
this is one of the main obstacles to fully extending the results of
[
SS23
]
to higher dimension in full generality.

Adjoint foliated structures were first considered by the sixth author and Pereira in order to prove effective birationality results for foliated surfaces
[
PS19
]
.
These structures were later studied more systematically by the fifth and sixth authors
[
SS23
]
:
for adjoint foliated structures on surfaces, they established the minimal model program when

1\geq t>\frac{5}{6}
,
[
SS23
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
,
moreover, they showed that effective birationality, boundedness in families, lower bound of the canonical volumes, and boundedness of the birational automorphism groups hold when

t
is sufficiently close to
1
,
[
SS23
, Theorems 1.3-1.7]
. These results provide strong evidence that classical results in birational geometry hold for adjoint foliated structures, although many of these results fail for foliations.

Moreover, M
c
Kernan conjectured that the “interpolated log canonical threshold” for adjoint foliated structures, that is, the supremum of the real number
t
so that the adjoint foliated structure associated with
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
has log canonical singularities, should satisfy the ascending chain condition (ACC). See Appendix
A
for details. M
c
Kernan also proposed a proof in dimension
2
(no later than his JAMI conference talk in May 2022
[
McK22
]
). In particular, it is conjectured that if
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
has a log canonical structure for some
0<t\ll 1
, then
\mathcal{F}
is also log canonical. This highlights the importance of considering adjoint foliated structures. Methodologically, results on the
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
minimal model program could imply results on the
K_{\mathcal{F}}
minimal model program by either letting
t=1
or by letting
t
approach
1
and applying ACC-type results. In fact, this methodology will be applied in this paper to the minimal model program for algebraically integrable foliations on potentially klt varieties. See Theorem
1.11
for details.

We emphasize that, in this paper, rather than considering
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
only for
t
sufficiently close to
1
, we take into consideration arbitrary value of
t\in[0,1]
.
We also expect that similar methods as those of this paper can be applied to prove the existence of MMP for adjoint foliated structures that are not necessarily algebraically integrable in dimensions
\leq 3
.
This will be addressed in future works.

Our main theorem also holds in the log setting, that is, in the setting where we have boundary divisors.
Due to the novelty of the notation, before we proceed to state our main theorems in detail, we shall first provide the definition of adjoint foliated structures.

Definition of adjoint foliated structures.

Rather than considering pairs consisting of a normal variety
X
and foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, it is more convenient to consider pairs
(X,B)
and foliated triples
(X,\mathcal{F},B)
.
Recent developments in birational geometry and foliation theory suggest that it is sometimes even more convenient to consider generalized pairs
(X,B,{\bf{M}})

[
BZ16
]
and generalized foliated quadruples
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}})

[
LLM23
]
. When we consider adjoint foliated structures, there is one more object to consider: the parameter
t
. For the reader’s convenience, we now provide the definition of adjoint foliated structures.

Definition 1.2

(
=
Definition
2.6
)
.

An
adjoint foliated structure

(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
consists of a projective morphism
X\rightarrow U
from a normal quasi-projective variety to a quasi-projective variety, a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B\geq 0
on
X
, a nef
/U

\bm{b}
-divisor
{\bf{M}}
, and a real number
t\in[0,1]
, such that
K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}:=tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+B+{\bf{M}}_{X}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

If
B=0
,
{\bf{M}}=\bm{0}
,
U
is not important, or
\mathcal{F}=T_{X}
, we may drop
B,{\bf{M}},U,\mathcal{F}
respectively.
Depending on the objects in the parentheses we may also call an adjoint foliated structure as a quintuple, quadruple, triple, pair, etc.

The definition of singularities of an adjoint foliated structure (e.g. klt, lc) is similar to that of usual pairs or foliated triples. See Definition
2.7
for details.

MMP for adjoint foliated structures.
We establish the cone theorem, the contraction theorem, and the existence of flips for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures, which implies the existence of the MMP for such structures.
We refer the reader to Definition
2.10
for the definition of the non-lc locus of an adjoint foliated structure.

Theorem 1.3

(Cone theorem)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure. Let
K:=K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
and let
\operatorname{Nlc}:=\operatorname{Nlc}(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
be the nlc (non-lc) locus of
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
.

Let
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
be the set of
K
-negative extremal rays in
\overline{NE}(X/U)
that are rational and are not contained in
\operatorname{Nlc}
. Then:

(1)

We have

\overline{NE}(X/U)=\overline{NE}(X/U)_{K\geq 0}+\overline{NE}(X/U)_{\operatorname{Nlc}}+\sum_{j\in\Lambda}R_{j}.

In particular, any
K
-negative extremal ray in
\overline{NE}(X/U)
that is not contained in
\operatorname{Nlc}
is rational.

(2)

Each
R_{j}
is spanned by a rational curve
C_{j}
such that

0<-K\cdot C_{j}\leq 2\dim X.

(3)

For any ample
/U

\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
on
X
,

\Lambda_{A}:=\{j\in\Lambda\mid R_{j}\subset\overline{NE}(X/U)_{K+A<0}\}

is a finite set. Moreover, we can write

\overline{NE}(X/U)=\overline{NE}(X/U)_{K+A\geq 0}+\overline{NE}(X/U)_{\operatorname{Nlc}}+\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{A}}R_{j}.

In particular,
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in\Lambda}
is countable and is a discrete subset of
\overline{NE}(X/U)_{K<0}
.

(4)

Let
F
be a
K
-negative extremal face that is relatively ample at infinity with respect to
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
.
Then
F
is a rational extremal face.

For the definition of a a
K
-negative extremal face that is relatively ample at infinity with respect to an adjoint foliated structure, we refer the reader to Definition
2.20
.

Theorem 1.4

(Contraction theorem)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an lc algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure associated with a morphism
\pi:X\rightarrow U
, and let
R
be a
K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
-negative extremal ray
/U
. Assume that
X
is klt. Then there exists a contraction
/U

\operatorname{cont}_{R}:X\rightarrow T
satisfying the following.

(1)

For any integral curve
C
such that
\pi(C)
is a point,
\operatorname{cont}_{R}(C)
is a point if and only if
[C]\in R
.

(2)

Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
such that
L\cdot R=0
. Then there exists a line bundle
L_{T}
on
T
such that
L\cong f^{\ast}L_{T}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Existence of flips)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an lc algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure such that
X
is potentially klt (i.e. there exists a klt pair
(X,\Delta)
). Let
f:X\rightarrow T
be a
K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
-flipping contraction
/U
.

Then the flip
f^{+}\colon X^{+}\rightarrow T
of
f
exists. Moreover, if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, then
X^{+}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and
\rho(X/U)=\rho(X^{+}/U)
.

Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
1.5
imply the existence of the minimal model program for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures:

Theorem 1.6

(Existence of MMP)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure. Then we may run a
K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
-MMP
/U
.

It is clear that Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
1.6
.

Base-point-freeness for adjoint foliated structures of general type.

Having established the minimal model program settled, a natural question to consider regards the existence of good minimal models for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures, of which
the existence of flips can be viewed as a special case.
In this paper, we prove the base-point-freeness theorem for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures of general type.

Theorem 1.7

(Base-point-freeness)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be a klt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure such that
K:=K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
is nef
/U
and big
/U
. Then:

(1)

K
is semi-ample
/U
.

(2)

Let
m
be a positive integer such that
mK
is Cartier. Then for any integer
n\gg 0
divisible by
m
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(nK)
is globally generated
/U
.

For the general case, i.e. when
K
is nef/
U
but not necessarily big, we expect that the techniques will be different from those used in this paper;
we shall address this problem in future work.

An adjunction formula.
An adjunction formula for foliations along non-invariant divisors was established by the first and fifth authors in
[
CS23b
, Theorems 1.1 and 3.16]
.
However, for arbitrary foliations, adjunction to invariant divisors may not be well-behaved, cf.
[
CS20
, Example 3.20]
.
Nevertheless, we are able to prove a precise adjunction formula for invariant divisors in the case of algebraically integrable foliations
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 3.2]
,
[
CHLX23
, Theorem 2.4.2]
.
In this paper, we establish the following adjunction formula for algebraically integrable foliated structures, which will be crucial to establish the cone theorem, see Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.8

(Adjunction)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure.
Let
S
be an irreducible component of
B
such that
S
is an lc place of
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
,
i.e.
\operatorname{mult}_{S}B=1
if
S
is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\operatorname{mult}_{S}B=1-t
if
S
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
Let
\nu\colon S^{\nu}\to S
be the normalization of
S
and let
{\bf{M}}^{S^{\nu}}:=\nu^{\ast}{\bf{M}}
be the restricted
\bm{b}
-divisor.
Then there exists a canonically defined algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure
(S^{\nu},\mathcal{F}_{S^{\nu}},B_{S^{\nu}},{\bf{M}}^{S^{\nu}},t)/U
satisfying the following:

(1)

K_{(S^{\nu},\mathcal{F}_{S^{\nu}},B_{S^{\nu}},{\bf{M}}^{S^{\nu}},t)}=K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}|_{S^{\nu}}
.

(2)

If
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is lc, then
(S^{\nu},\mathcal{F}_{S^{\nu}},B_{S^{\nu}},{\bf{M}}^{S^{\nu}},t)

is lc.

Existence of quasi-dlt modifications.
Another result that we obtain is the existence of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial quasi-dlt (qdlt) modifications for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures. These modifications can be viewed as an analogue of dlt modifications for usual pairs and as an analogue of
(\ast)
-modifications
[
ACSS21
,
CS23a
]
or
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial ACSS modifications
[
CHLX23
,
LMX24b
]
for algebraically integrable foliations.

Theorem 1.9

(Existence of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial qdlt modifications)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure such that
t<1
. Let

B_{t}:=B\wedge(\operatorname{Supp}B^{\operatorname{ninv}}+(1-t)\operatorname{Supp}B^{\operatorname{inv}}),

where
B^{\operatorname{ninv}}
and
B^{\operatorname{inv}}
are the non-
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\mathcal{F}
-invariant part of
B
respectively. Then there exists a projective birational morphism
h:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
satisfying the following properties:

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=h^{-1}\mathcal{F}
and

B^{\prime}:=h^{-1}_{\ast}B_{t}+\operatorname{Exc}(h)^{\operatorname{ninv}}+(1-t)\operatorname{Exc}(h)^{\operatorname{inv}},

where
\operatorname{Exc}(h)^{\operatorname{ninv}}
and
\operatorname{Exc}(h)^{\operatorname{inv}}
are the non-
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant part of
\operatorname{Exc}(h)
respectively. Let
B_{X^{\prime}}:=B^{\prime\operatorname{ninv}}+\frac{1}{1-t}B^{\prime\operatorname{inv}}
, where
B^{\prime\operatorname{ninv}}
and
B^{\prime\operatorname{inv}}
are the non-
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant part of
B^{\prime}
respectively. Then the following hold.

(1)

E
is an nklt place of
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
for any
h
-exceptional prime divisor
E
.

(2)

(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime},{\bf{M}},t)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial lc. In particular, if
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is lc, then

K_{(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime},{\bf{M}},t)}=h^{\ast}K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}.

(3)

(X^{\prime},B_{X^{\prime}},{\bf{M}})
is qdlt. In particular,
X^{\prime}
is klt.

(4)

Any lc place of
(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime},{\bf{M}},t)
is an lc place of
(X^{\prime},B_{X^{\prime}},{\bf{M}})
.

(5)

If
t>0
, then there exists a positive real number
\epsilon_{0}
such that

\left(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\epsilon\{B_{X^{\prime}}\},(1+\epsilon){\bf{M}},t+\epsilon\right)

is lc for any
\epsilon\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]
.

A characterization of the singularities of the ambient variety.
We prove the following theorem, which indicates that, for any algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
with parameter
t<1
and mild singularities, its ambient variety
X
also admits mild singularities.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure. Then:

(1)

if
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is klt, then
X
is potentially klt; and,

(2)

if
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is lc,
t<1
, and
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, then
X
is lc.

Note that if
t=1
, then (2) above fails. For example, let

be a locally stable family over a normal variety with a normal generic fiber. This induces an lc algebraically integrable foliation

on

. We can choose

such that it is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and not lc. In this case,

Let

;

(2)

Sketch of the proof of the main theorems.

For simplicity, throughout this subsection we will assume that
B=0
,
{\bf{M}}=\bm{0}
, and
U=\{pt\}
.
Before discussing the technical details, we will first explain the key idea of this paper, which is the observation of the following phenomenon:
given
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
, we can first pass to a birational model
W\rightarrow X
such that the pull-back
\mathcal{F}_{W}
of
\mathcal{F}
on
W
has nice singularities.
Let
E\subset W
be the reduced divisor which is exceptional over
X
, and let
E^{\operatorname{ninv}}
and
E^{\operatorname{inv}}
be the non-
\mathcal{F}_{W}
-invariant and
\mathcal{F}_{W}
-invariant parts of
E
, respectively.
Assume that
K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}}
is pseudo-effective. We first run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}})
-MMP to produce a minimal model
Y
.
By construction, denoting by
\mathcal{F}_{Y}
(resp.
E_{Y}
)
the push-forward of
\mathcal{F}_{W}

(resp.
E
)
to
Y
,

(Y,E_{Y},{\bf{N}}:=\overline{\frac{t}{1-t}(K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E_{Y}^{\operatorname{ninv}})})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial generalized pair with generalized log canonical singularities.
Thus, thanks to results of
[
BZ16
,
HL23
]
, we can run a
(K_{Y}+E_{Y}+{\bf{N}}_{Y})
-MMP terminating with a model
V
.
Let us observe that the MMP just run is also a run of the

(tK_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+(1-t)K_{Y}+E_{Y}^{\operatorname{ninv}}+(1-t)E_{Y}^{\operatorname{inv}})
-MMP.
In general,
V
may not be a minimal model of
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
, but it is under some extra assumptions on the augmented base locus of
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}
. This methodology will be applied twice later.

Another methodology is the use of generalized pairs. We have already used it once for the
(K_{Y}+E_{Y}+{\bf{N}}_{Y})
-MMP above. Note that the use of a generalized pair to run this MMP is necessary:
(Y,E_{Y})
is only guaranteed to be
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial qdlt, and since abundance fails for foliations, we may only expect
{\bf{N}}_{Y}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E_{Y}^{\operatorname{ninv}}
to be nef rather than semi-ample. Therefore, in general, we cannot obtain an lc structure
(Y,\Delta_{Y})
such that
\Delta_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}E_{Y}+{\bf{N}}_{Y}
. Moreover, since Bertini-type theorems fail for foliations (see, e.g.,
[
DLM23
, Example 3.4]
), they also do not hold in general for adjoint foliated structures (although some weaker Bertini-type theorems hold when
t<1
). Therefore, when considering
+A
for some ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
, we usually put
A
in the moduli part (the
{\bf{M}}
part) of the adjoint foliated structure to preserve the singularities.

In the remainder of this section we will discuss the technical details of the proof.

Cone theorem and qdlt modifications.

Given the (relative) cone theorem for algebraically integrable foliations was established in
[
ACSS21
,
CHLX23
]
, our strategy will mimic the ones used to prove that result.
To do this, there are three key ingredients: bend and break (cf.
[
Spi20
, Corollary 2.28]
), adjunction, and the existence of an analogue of dlt modifications (
(\ast)
-modifications in
[
ACSS21
]
and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial ACSS modifications in
[
CHLX23
]
).

The bend and break technique,
[
Spi20
, Corollary 2.28]
, still works, and in the relative setting,
[
CHLX23
, Theorem 8.1.1]
can be applied with minor changes (cf. Proposition
5.2
). The adjunction formula for algebraically integrable foliations holds by
[
ACSS21
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
CHLX23
, Theorem 2.4.1]
. Thus, we may decompose the adjoint canonical divisor of the adjoint foliated structure into the
K_{\mathcal{F}}
part and the
K_{X}
part, apply adjunction formulae separately, and then combine them to obtain the adjunction formula for adjoint foliated structures (see Theorem
4.1
). It is worth mentioning that, if
X
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, then the decomposition is not straightforward, but it can be bypassed after we prove the existence of an analogue of dlt modifications (Theorem
1.9
).

The difficult part is the existence of an analogue of dlt modifications (Theorem
1.9
), as we cannot follow the proofs in
[
ACSS21
,
CHLX23
]
due to the fact that we cannot reduce lower-dimensional adjoint foliated MMP to an MMP for usual pairs as in
[
ACSS21
, Proof of Lemma 3.11]
. To overcome this difficulty, we apply the methodology mentioned earlier. The idea is to take a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial ACSS modification
Y\rightarrow X
of
\mathcal{F}
so that
K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}}
is ample
/X
, where
E
is the reduced exceptional
/X
divisor and
E^{\operatorname{ninv}}
is its non-invariant part. Now, by treating
K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}}
as a nef
/X

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, we can run a
(K_{Y}+E+\frac{t}{1-t}(K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}}))
-MMP
/X
, and the output of this MMP will satisfy our requirements. In practice, since we do not have the termination of flips and do not know whether
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, instead of starting with
Y
, we need to start with a foliated log resolution
W\rightarrow X
of
\mathcal{F}
and run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}+E_{W}^{\operatorname{ninv}})
-MMP
/X
to obtain the model
Y
first. We need to use an auxiliary ample divisor to guarantee the termination of the MMPs with scaling so that we can obtain the model
Y
. To obtain the model
X^{\prime}
, we further apply the general negativity lemma
[
Bir12
, Lemma 3.3]
. See Theorem
3.6
for details. Theorem
1.9
is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.6
and an easy perturbation lemma (Lemma
3.2
).

Theorem
1.9
shows that
X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is a satisfactory analogue of dlt modifications. We call it a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial qdlt modification
as the ambient variety has a natural qdlt structure. See Definition
3.1
for details.

Contraction and base-point-freeness theorems.
The next goal is the contraction theorem (Theorem
1.4
). The existence of Fano contractions is straightforward (at least for the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial case) because the bend and break theorem implies that the variety is covered by rational curves spanning the extremal ray. Thus, any
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
-negative extremal ray is also
K_{X}
-negative, and the contraction theorem follows from the usual contraction theorem for klt varieties (see Theorem
6.7
). Therefore, we only need to be concerned with the existence of flipping contractions and divisorial contractions.

In either case, we have an ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
on
X
such that
H:=tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A
is nef and big and is the supporting function of a
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
-negative extremal ray. Proving the existence of a flipping contraction is equivalent to proving that
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A
has a good minimal model. As mentioned earlier, we consider the
\bm{b}
-divisor
{\bf{A}}:=\overline{A}
instead of
A
due to the failure of Bertini-type theorems. Now we apply our methodology again: take a foliated log resolution
W\rightarrow X
with reduced exceptional divisor
E
, and run a
(K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}+E^{\operatorname{ninv}}+{\bf{A}}_{W})
-MMP. After obtaining a minimal model
Y
, we treat
P_{Y}:=K_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}+E_{Y}^{\operatorname{ninv}}+{\bf{A}}_{Y}
as a nef
\mathbb{R}
-divisor and run an MMP for the generalized pair
(Y,E_{Y},\overline{\frac{t}{1-t}P_{Y}})
to obtain a good minimal model
V
.

The problem is that
tK_{\mathcal{F}_{V}}+(1-t)K_{V}+E_{V}^{\operatorname{ninv}}+(1-t)E_{V}^{\operatorname{inv}}+A_{V}
is usually not a minimal model of
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A
, even in the sense of Birkar-Shokurov. In fact, we cannot exclude the possibility that
W\dashrightarrow V
contracts some divisors that are not in the augmented base locus of
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A
. For example, if
t
is very small, the singularities of
\mathcal{F}
can be very bad. However, the entire process
X\leftarrow W\dashrightarrow Y
only concerns
\mathcal{F}
and completely ignores the structure of
X
. It is very possible that some divisors not in
{\bf{B}}_{+}(H)
are contracted by the map
W\dashrightarrow Y
. However, if we contract any such divisor, we would never obtain a minimal model by a simple positivity computation (see Lemma
2.26
(3)).

Therefore, we cannot generally obtain the existence of good minimal models by simply considering
V
. However, there is a straightforward way to resolve this issue: instead of considering
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A
, we consider
tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A+lH
with
l\gg 0
. The corresponding minimal model program is
H
-trivial by the length of extremal rays, which is part of the cone theorem (Theorem
1.3
(2)). Therefore, when
H
is associated with a flipping contraction,
{\bf{B}}_{+}(H)
is small, so the divisorial part contracted is contained in the exceptional divisors of
W\rightarrow X
. This leads to the proof of the existence of flipping contractions (see Theorem
6.3
). We also note that when we consider
\mathbb{R}
-divisors, we need to prove that
H
is NQC (i.e., it is a positive sum of nef Cartier divisors). This follows from the finiteness of
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+A)
-negative extremal rays, which is also part of the cone theorem (Theorem
1.3
(3)).

For the existence of divisorial contractions, the proof is more complicated as we need to have more explicit control of the divisors that are contracted by
W\dashrightarrow V
. However,
H
is big, and
{\bf{B}}_{+}(H)
contains finitely many divisors. We want to contract, and only contract, these divisors for
X\dashrightarrow V
. Therefore, we can take
W
to be a sufficiently high resolution and increase the coefficients of all boundary divisors that are contained in
{\bf{B}}_{+}(H_{W})
to the maximum. Now we can use the fact that the structures on
W
and
V
are both lc to deduce that
V
is a good minimal model of the new structure on
W
. Moreover,
X
is a minimal model of the new structure on
W
by some easy Nakayama-Zariski decomposition arguments. This implies that
H
is actually semi-ample, and we are done. See Theorem
6.3
for details. The proof of the general type base-point-freeness theorem (Theorem
1.7
) follows the same lines as the proof of the contraction theorem.

We also remark that there is an alternative way to consider the MMP we run in the proof of the contraction theorem: suppose that the contraction
X\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
defined by
H
already exists; then running the MMP by adding a sufficiently large multiple of
H
is essentially equivalent to running an MMP
/\mathcal{T}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{O}_{H}(-H|_{H})
is ample, then the contraction
X\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
exists in the category of algebraic spaces
[
Art70
, Theorem 6.2]
, and with the MMP for algebraic spaces, we can show that
X\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
is indeed a contraction in the category of projective varieties. This is a common strategy in previous works on foliations
[
CS20
,
CS21
,
Spi20
]
. However, since we are not in dimension
3
, it is not easy to show that
\mathcal{O}_{H}(-H|_{H})
is ample. Moreover, we want to work with normal quasi-projective varieties associated with projective morphisms rather than normal projective varieties only. Thus, although it is an inspiring strategy, we do not argue in this way.

Existence of flips.
Next, we prove the existence of flips (Theorem
1.5
). The key idea of the proof is as follows: in the proof of divisorial contraction and flipping contractions, we obtain a birational map
X\dashrightarrow V
that is
H
-trivial. Now the crepant transform
H_{V}
of
H
on
V
has a “
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial qdlt generalized pair
+
ample” structure, which induces the contractions
V\rightarrow T
, hence
H
also induces a contraction
\pi:X\rightarrow T
. Following this construction, we know that
T
is potentially klt. In particular, there exists a small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialization
T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
. Since
T^{\prime}
is of Fano type over
T
,
tK_{\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}}+(1-t)K_{T^{\prime}}
has a good minimal model
/T
, hence
tK_{\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}}+(1-t)K_{T^{\prime}}
has an ample model
/T
, denoted by
X^{+}
. It is easy to check that the induced birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{+}
is the
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
-flip we want.

We also note that in the non-
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial minimal model program, a step of an MMP may be of the form
X\rightarrow T\leftarrow X^{+}
, where
X\rightarrow T
is a divisorial contraction and
X^{+}\rightarrow T
is a small modification. The arguments above could also imply that the small modification
X^{+}\rightarrow T
exists if
X\rightarrow T
is a divisorial contraction of a
(tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X})
-negative extremal ray.

Existence of the minimal model program and Theorem
1.10
.
With the cone theorem, the contraction theorem, and the existence of flips, and after proving that some auxiliary properties (e.g.,
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, potentially klt) are preserved under the minimal model program, we can deduce that we can run an MMP for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures (Theorem
1.6
). Theorem
1.1
and the main part of Theorem
1.11
are corollaries of the aforementioned theorems, while the “moreover” part of Theorem
1.11
follows from
[
LMX24b
, Theorems 1.10 and 1.11]
.

We are only left to prove Theorem
1.10
. When
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, Theorem
1.10
(2) can be proved by taking a proper
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial ACSS modification
h:(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}},{\bf{M}};G)/Z\rightarrow(X,\mathcal{F},B^{\operatorname{ninv}},{\bf{M}})
such that
G
contains all the
h
-exceptional prime divisors and the strict transform of
B^{\operatorname{inv}}
, and using the fact that
(X^{\prime},B_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+G,{\bf{M}})
is lc. When
X
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, we need to choose the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial ACSS model for another structure
(X,\mathcal{F},B_{\mathcal{F}},{\bf{P}})
, and the proof follows from similar arguments. See Proposition
3.3
for details.

The proof of Theorem
1.10
(1) is more complicated. The idea is to first use the existence of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial qdlt modifications (Theorem
1.9
) to obtain a model
h:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
. It is easy to show that
h
is small. Now a key observation is that, following the proof of the contraction theorem, one can actually show that the contraction
X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is the contraction of a
(K^{\prime}+A^{\prime})
-trivial extremal face, where
K^{\prime}
is associated with a klt adjoint foliated structure and
A^{\prime}
is ample
/X
. Following the proof of the contraction theorem, this implies that
X
is potentially klt, which is Theorem
1.10
(1).

2.2.
Foliations

Definition 2.4

(Foliations, cf.
[
ACSS21
,
CS21
]
)
.

Let
X

\mathcal{F}\subset T_{X}

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is saturated in

\mathcal{F}

The

.
The

\mathcal{F}

. The
canonical divisor
of
\mathcal{F}
is a divisor

such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})\cong\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F})
. If
\mathcal{F}=0
, then we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a

.

Given any dominant map

and a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X

h^{-1}\mathcal{F}
the
pullback
of
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
as constructed in
[
Dru21
, 3.2]
and say that
h^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is
induced by

\mathcal{F}
. Given any birational map
g:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
, we denote by
g_{\ast}\mathcal{F}:=(g^{-1})^{-1}\mathcal{F}
the
pushforward
of
\mathcal{F}
on
X^{\prime}
and also say that

is
induced by

\mathcal{F}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is an

\mathcal{F}=f^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{Z}

\mathcal{F}_{Z}

, and we say that
\mathcal{F}
is

.

A subvariety

\mathcal{F}

, where

in
U
.
For any prime divisor
P
on
X
, we define
\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(P):=1
if

is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(P):=0
if

is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant. For any prime divisor
E
over
X
, we define
\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(E):=\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}(E)
where
h:Y\dashrightarrow X

is on
Y
and
\mathcal{F}_{Y}:=h^{-1}\mathcal{F}
.

Suppose that the foliation structure
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is clear in the context. Then, given an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}D_{i}
where each
D_{i}
is a prime Weil divisor,
we denote by

the

\mathcal{F}
-invariant part
of

\mathcal{F}
-invariant part
of

.

(Tangent, cf.

Definition 2.7

The following lemma provides an equivalent definition for an adjoint foliated structure to be lc.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be a sub-adjoint foliated structure
such that

a(E,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)\geq-1

E
over

Proof.

Suppose not. Then there exists a prime divisor

over

Since

is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
Possibly replacing

with a resolution we may assume that

is smooth and

is on

. Now we may repeatedly blow-up points on

as in
[

and obtain a divisor with discrepancy

with respect to
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)

∎

be the irreducible components of
E
and let

\epsilon:=\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{mult}_{E_{i}}E}{\operatorname{mult}_{E_{i}}F}\right\}.

Then we may write

F+E=(1+\epsilon)F+F_{1}-F_{2}

such that
F_{1}\geq 0,F_{2}\geq 0
,
F_{1}\wedge F_{2}=0
,
E\wedge F_{2}=0
, and
\operatorname{Supp}F_{1}\subsetneq\operatorname{Supp}E
.

If
F_{1}=0
, then

N_{\sigma}(X/U,(1+\epsilon)D-\epsilon A)=N_{\sigma}(X/U,D+E+F_{2})\leq F_{2}.

Thus

\operatorname{Supp}E\subset{\bf{B}}_{+}(D/U)={\bf{B}}_{+}((1+\epsilon)D/U)\subset{\bf{B}}_{-}(((1+\epsilon)D-\epsilon A)/U)\subset\operatorname{Supp}F_{2},

which is not possible as
E\wedge F_{2}=0
. Thus
F_{1}\not=0
, so

\operatorname{Supp}F_{1}\supset N_{\sigma}(X/U,(1+\epsilon)D+F_{1})=N_{\sigma}(X/U,\epsilon A+(D+E)+F_{2})\subset\operatorname{Supp}F_{2}.

Since
F_{1}\wedge F_{2}=0
,

N_{\sigma}(X/U,(1+\epsilon)D+F_{1})=N_{\sigma}(X/U,\epsilon A+(D+E)+F_{2})=0,

so

N_{\sigma}\left(X/U,D+\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}F_{1}\right)=0.

This contradicts our assumption as
\operatorname{Supp}F_{1}\subsetneq\operatorname{Supp}E
and
F_{1}\not=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.29
.

Let

be a projective morphism from a normal quasi-projective variety to a quasi-projective variety, and

and

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Let

/U

Let

be an ample

\mathbb{R}

3.
Qdlt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures

In this section we introduce the concept of qdlt singularities for algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures and study its properties.

3.1.
Definition of qdlt

Definition 3.1

(Qdlt)
.

We say that an adjoint foliated structure
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is
(sub-)qdlt
if the following conditions hold. Note that this definition does not require the foliation to be algebraically integrable.

(1)

t<1
.

(2)

(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is (sub-)lc.

(3)

(X,B_{X}:=B^{\operatorname{ninv}}+\frac{1}{1-t}B^{\operatorname{inv}},{\bf{M}})
is (sub-)qdlt.

(4)

Any lc place of
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is an lc place of
(X,B_{X},{\bf{M}})
.

In particular,
K_{X}+B_{X}+{\bf{M}}_{X}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

There are two important properties of qdlt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures. The first property is that qdlt is preserved under small perturbations:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/X
be a sub-qdlt algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure such that
0<t<1
,
and let
B_{X}:=B^{\operatorname{ninv}}+\frac{1}{1-t}B^{\operatorname{inv}}
. Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor,
{\bf{N}}
a
\bm{b}
-divisor, and
\lambda
a real number, such that

(1)

is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier,

(2)

near the generic point of any strata of
\lfloor B_{X}\rfloor
,

(3)

{\bf{N}}
descends to
X
near the generic point of any strata of
\lfloor B_{X}\rfloor
, and

(4)

{\bf{M}}+\epsilon_{1}{\bf{N}}

/X

Then there exists a positive real number
\epsilon_{0}
, such that

(X,\mathcal{F},B+\epsilon D,{\bf{M}}+\epsilon{\bf{N}},t+\lambda\epsilon)

is sub-qdlt for any
\epsilon\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]

Proof.

Let
h:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a foliated log resolution of
(X,\mathcal{F},B+D,{\bf{M}}+{\bf{N}})
associated with an equidimensional toroidal contraction
f:(X^{\prime},\Sigma_{X^{\prime}})\rightarrow(Z,\Sigma_{Z})

a_{i}(0)\geq-t\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(F_{i})-(1-t)
for each

. Therefore, there exists
\epsilon_{2}>0
, such that
\epsilon_{1}>\epsilon_{2}
, and for any
i
such that

a_{i}(x)>-(t+\lambda x)\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(F_{i})-(1-t-\lambda x)
for any

is an lc place of
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
, so

is an lc place of

Since

is a stratum of
\lfloor B_{X}\rfloor

\operatorname{center}_{X}F_{i}

and
{\bf{N}}
descends to
X
. We have

\displaystyle a_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=a(F_{i},X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t+\lambda x)

\displaystyle=(t+\lambda x)a(F_{i},\mathcal{F},B^{\operatorname{ninv}},{\bf{M}})+(1-t-\lambda x)a(F_{i},X,B_{X},{\bf{M}})

\displaystyle=-(t+\lambda x)\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(F_{i})-(1-t-\lambda x).

Therefore,
a_{i}(x)\geq-(t+\lambda x)\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(F_{i})-(1-t-\lambda x)
for any
i
and any
x\in[0,\epsilon_{2}]
. By Lemma
2.14
,

(X,\mathcal{F},B+xD,{\bf{M}}+x{\bf{N}},t+\lambda x)

is sub-lc for any
x\in[0,\epsilon_{2}]
.

Since
(X,B_{X},{\bf{M}})
is sub-qdlt, by conditions (2-4), there exists a positive real number
\epsilon_{0}<\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}
such that for any
x\in[0,2\epsilon_{0}]

\left(X,B_{x}:=B_{X}+x\left(D^{\operatorname{ninv}}+\frac{1}{1-t}D^{\operatorname{inv}}\right),{\bf{M}}+x{\bf{N}}\right)

(X,B_{X},{\bf{M}})

•

Since

and

•

(X,B_{\epsilon},{\bf{M}}+\epsilon{\bf{N}})

.

•

Since
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
and

(X,\mathcal{F},B+\epsilon D,{\bf{M}}+\epsilon{\bf{N}},t+\lambda\epsilon)

(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)

(X,B_{X},{\bf{M}})
, and hence an lc place of
(X,B_{\epsilon},{\bf{M}}+\epsilon{\bf{N}})

Thus
\epsilon_{0}
satisfies our requirements.
∎

The second property is that qdlt is preserved under the minimal model program. We first prove the following important proposition which characterizes the singularities of the ambient variety of any adjoint foliated structure with
t<1
:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/X
be an algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure such that
t<1
. Let
B_{X}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
and let
{\bf{N}}
be a nef
/X

\bm{b}
-divisor on

such that

(1)

B^{\operatorname{ninv}}\geq B_{X}^{\operatorname{ninv}}
and
B^{\operatorname{inv}}\geq(1-t)B_{X}^{\operatorname{inv}}

(2)

{\bf{M}}-{\bf{N}}
is nef
/X
, and

(3)

K_{X}+B_{X}+{\bf{N}}_{X}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

\operatorname{Nlc}(X,B_{X},{\bf{N}})\subset\operatorname{Nlc}(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)

\operatorname{Nklt}(X,B_{X},{\bf{N}})\subset\operatorname{Nklt}(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t).

(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)

(X,B_{X},{\bf{N}})
is sub-lc (resp. sub-klt).

We may assume that
t>0
, otherwise the proposition is trivial.

Let
B_{\mathcal{F}}
be the unique
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
tB_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)B_{X}=B
. Let
{\bf{P}}
be the unique
\bm{b}
-divisor such that
t{\bf{P}}+(1-t){\bf{N}}={\bf{M}}
.
Then
{\bf{P}}={\bf{N}}+\frac{1}{t}({\bf{M}}-{\bf{N}})
is nef
/X
, and
{\bf{P}}-{\bf{N}}
is nef
/X
. Since

t(K_{\mathcal{F}}+B_{\mathcal{F}}+{\bf{P}}_{X})+(1-t)(K_{X}+B_{X}+{\bf{N}}_{X})=K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)},

K_{\mathcal{F}}+B_{\mathcal{F}}+{\bf{P}}_{X}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. Since

tB_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{inv}}=B^{\operatorname{inv}}-(1-t)B_{X}^{\operatorname{inv}}\geq 0,

. Since

tB_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{ninv}}=B^{\operatorname{ninv}}-(1-t)B_{X}^{\operatorname{ninv}}\geq tB^{\operatorname{ninv}},

B_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{ninv}}\geq B^{\operatorname{ninv}}
.

Since lc and klt are local properties, we may assume that
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is lc (resp. klt). Let
h:(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},B_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}},{\bf{P}};G)/Z\rightarrow(X,\mathcal{F},B_{\mathcal{F}},{\bf{P}})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial proper ACSS modification. By definition,

B_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=h^{-1}_{\ast}(B_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{ninv}}\wedge\operatorname{Supp}B_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{ninv}})+\operatorname{Supp}\operatorname{Exc}(h)^{\operatorname{ninv}},

and for any
h
-exceptional prime divisor
E
,

a(E,\mathcal{F},B_{\mathcal{F}},{\bf{P}})\leq-\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(E).

Since
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is lc (resp. klt),

a(E,X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)\geq\text{( resp. }>\text{)}-t\epsilon_{\mathcal{F}}(E)-(1-t).

Since

a(E,X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)=ta(E,\mathcal{F},B_{\mathcal{F}},{\bf{P}})+(1-t)a(E,X,B_{X},{\bf{N}}),

we have
a(E,X,B_{X},{\bf{N}})\geq-1
(resp.
>-1
).
Let
B_{X^{\prime}}:=h^{-1}_{*}B_{X}+\operatorname{Exc}(h)
. Then

.

B_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+G\geq B_{X^{\prime}}
, and if
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)
is klt, then

.

Case 4
.

is a component of

\operatorname{Nlc}:=\operatorname{Nlc}(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)

First we show that

Next we show that any each

is exposed. For any fixed

, There exists an ample
/U

\mathbb{R}
-divisor

is a
(K+A)

/U

(1) follows from Theorem

/U

\mathbb{R}

For any

Proposition 5.6

Let

/U

\mathbb{R}

6.1.
Existence of flipping and divisorial contractions

Definition 6.1

(NQC)
.

Let
X\rightarrow U
be a projective morphism from a normal quasi-projective variety to a variety. Let
D
be a nef
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
and
{\bf{M}}
a nef
\bm{b}
-divisor on
X
.

We say that
D
is NQC
/U
if
D=\sum d_{i}D_{i}
, where each
d_{i}\geq 0
and each
D_{i}
is a nef
/U
Cartier divisor. We say that
{\bf{M}}
is NQC
/U
if
{\bf{M}}=\sum\mu_{i}{\bf{M}}_{i}
, where each
\mu_{i}\geq 0
and each
{\bf{M}}_{i}
is a nef
/U
Cartier
\bm{b}
-divisor.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)/U
be an lc algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structure and
A
an ample
/U

\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Let
K:=K_{(X,\mathcal{F},B,{\bf{M}},t)}
. Let
D
be a nef
/U

\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
D-K
is ample
/U
. Then
D
is NQC
/U
.

Proof.

We write
D=\sum_{i=1}^{c}r_{i}D_{i}
, where
r_{1},\dots,r_{c}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
and each
D_{i}
is a Cartier divisor. We define
D(\bm{v}):=\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}D_{i}
for any
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{c})\in\mathbb{R}^{c}
. By
[
HLS19
, Lemma 5.3]
,
D(\bm{v})
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{c}
.

Let
L:=D-K
. Since ample
/U
is an open condition, there exists an open set
V\ni\bm{r}
in
\mathbb{R}^{c}
, such that

\frac{1}{2}L+D(\bm{v})-D
is ample
/U
for any
\bm{v}\in V
.

By Theorem
1.3
, there exist finitely many
\left(K+\frac{1}{2}L\right)
-negative extremal rays
/U

R_{1},\dots,R_{l}
, and
R_{j}=\mathbb{R}_{+}[C_{j}]
for some curve
C_{j}
. Since
D
is nef,
D\cdot C_{j}\geq 0
for each
j
. Thus possibly shrinking
V
, we may assume that for any
\bm{v}\in V
, we have that
D(\bm{v})\cdot C_{j}>0
for any
j
such that
D\cdot C_{j}>0
. Since
r_{1},\dots,r_{c}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, for any
j
such that
D\cdot C_{j}=0
, we have
D(\bm{v})\cdot C_{j}=0
for any
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{c}
. Therefore,
D(\bm{v})\cdot C_{j}\geq 0
for any
j
and any
\bm{v}\in V
.

By Theorem
1.3
, for any curve
C
on
X
, we may write

[C]=\eta+\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}[C_{i}]

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{l}\geq 0
and
\eta\in\overline{NE}(X/U)_{K+\frac{1}{2}L\geq 0}
. For any
\bm{v}\in V
, since

D(\bm{v})\cdot\eta=\left(K+\frac{1}{2}L\right)\cdot\eta+\left(\frac{1}{2}L+D(\bm{v})-D\right)\cdot\eta\geq 0,

D(\bm{v})\cdot C\geq 0
. Therefore,
D(\bm{v})
is nef
/U
for any
\bm{v}\in V
. We let
\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{c+1}\in V\cap\mathbb{Q}^{c}
be rational points such that
\bm{r}
is in the interior of the convex hull of
\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{c+1}
. Then there exist positive real numbers
a_{1},\dots,a_{c+1}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{c+1}a_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{c+1}a_{i}\bm{v}_{i}=\bm{r}
. Since
D(\bm{v}_{i})
is a nef
/U

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor for each
i
and
D=\sum_{i=1}^{c+1}a_{i}D(\bm{v}_{i})
,

is NQC
/U
.
∎

Theorem 6.3

(Existence of flipping and divisorial contractions)

/U

\mathbb{R}
-divisor on

is potentially klt, and
K+A
is nef and big
/U
. Then
K+A
is semi-ample
/U
. Moreover, the following hold.

(1)

If

(2)

(3)

Suppose that

Remark A.1

We state the conjectures in this section for arbitrary foliations in arbitrary dimensions.

Let

We also remark that there is a more general version of the ACC conjecture for interpolated lc thresholds proposed by M
c
Kernan. Instead of considering a normal variety
X

\mathcal{F}
on
X

\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
on
X
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}
and the adjoint foliated structure
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F},t)

We may define the singularities of

Similarly to the previous definitons, we can also define a new version of the interpolated lc thresholds in this new context simply by taking

\displaystyle\sup\{t\in[0,1]\mid(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F},t)\text{ is lc}\}.

M
c
Kernan conjectured that also in this framework the set of all possible interpolated lc threshold satisfies the ACC in any fixed dimension.

d

Finally, inspired by the ACC for log canonical threshold polytopes
[
HLQ21
, Theorem 1.1]

\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}

tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{\mathcal{G}}

\sum a_{i}=1

\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\Big|\sum x_{i}=1,x_{i}\in[0,1],\sum x_{i}K_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}\text{ has an lc adjoint foliated structure}\right\}.

Due to technicality we do not provide a more detailed conjecture here.

Statistical Inference of Optimal Allocations I: Regularities and their Implications
Kai Feng, Han Hong

Abstract
\vskip-12.0pt Abstract. In this paper, we develop a functional differentiability approach for solving statistical optimal allocation problems. We first derive Hadamard differentiability of the value function through a detailed analysis of the general properties of the sorting operator. Central to our framework are the concept of Hausdorff measure and the area and coarea integration formulas from geometric measure theory. Building on our Hadamard differentiability results, we demonstrate how the functional delta method can be used to directly derive the asymptotic properties of the value function process for binary constrained optimal allocation problems, as well as the two-step ROC curve estimator. Moreover, leveraging profound insights from geometric functional analysis on convex and local Lipschitz functionals, we obtain additional generic Fréchet differentiability results for the value functions of optimal allocation problems. These compelling findings motivate us to study carefully the first order approximation of the optimal social welfare. In this paper, we then present a double / debiased estimator for the value functions. Importantly, the conditions outlined in the Hadamard differentiability section validate the margin assumption from the statistical classification literature employing plug-in methods that justifies a faster convergence rate. Keywords : Optimal treatment allocation, Functional differentiability and delta method, Plug-in classification, ROC curve, Double / debiased machine learning JEL Codes : C14, C38, C44

Introduction

Currently, as a consequence of the rapid digitization of social-economic data,
characterizing individual heterogeneity has become crucial
in the optimal allocation of scarce resources.
Consider, for instance,
the medical treatment assignment problem when doctors adopt
different therapeutic approaches to different patients;
the pretrial bail problem when judges decide where defendants will await trial;
the college admission problem when committees assign students to various educational programs.
In order to address the heterogeneity inherent in such problems,
where the potential effects of a treatment on
an outcome vary among individuals,
personalized allocation is more of a necessity than just an option.

Based on past experimental or observational data,
the core objective of a typical allocation problem is to find an allocation
strategy that can perform well in the future.
To achieve this goal, at least based on experience,
an effective approach of decision making is to rely on information provided by
prediction algorithms.

(
Kleinberg et al. 2018
;
Agrawal et al. 2019
;
Babina et al. 2024
)
.
In empirical applications, a common practice is to form
a nonrandom treatment rule using an indicator function, in which
a first step regression estimate of individual level treatment effect
is plugged-in and benchmarked against certain decision thresholds.
In this case, mathematically, optimal allocation
is treated as a weighted classification problem.

A plug-in strategy is based on a population solution to
the optimal allocation problem.
For a joint distribution of
\{X,Y\}
,
Y=\left(Y_{j},j=0,\ldots,J\right)
,
consider the
social welfare potential function

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)\coloneqq\max_{\phi\in\Phi}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)Y_{j}\right]=\max_{\phi\in\Phi}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)g_{j}\left(X\right)\right],\end{split}

(1)

where
\Phi
is the set of multi-critical functions, i.e.

\phi=\left(\phi_{j},j=0,\ldots,J\right)
,

\phi_{j}:\Omega\rightarrow R
, with
\left(\Omega,F,\mu\right)
being
a Borel probability space, such that

\phi_{j}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mu\right),0\leq\phi_{j}\leq 1,\forall j\in\{0,\ldots,J\}
, and such that
\sum_{j=0}^{J}\phi_{j}\left(x\right)\equiv 1
for all
x\in\Omega
;

g\left(\cdot\right)=\left\{g_{j}\left(\cdot\right),j=0,\ldots,J\right\}

and
g_{j}\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{j}|X=x\right)
.
Under very general conditions, the problem posed in
(
1
) admits an intuitive solution
where the
j
th-category receives all allocation
when
\mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{j}Y_{j}|X\right)
is uniquely the largest. In other words,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\text{if}\ \mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{j}Y_{j}|X\right)>\mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{l}Y_{l}|X\right),\ \forall l\neq j,\\
0,&\text{if}\ \mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{j}Y_{j}|X\right)<\mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{l}Y_{l}|X\right),\ \exists l\neq j.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(2)

If there exists some
l
such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{j}Y_{j}|X\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\lambda_{l}Y_{l}|X\right)
,
then
\phi\left(\cdot\right)
can be divided among all the maximal indexes.

In this paper, we are interested in differentiating
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
functionally
with respect to both
\lambda
and
g\left(\cdot\right)
.
The solution of such problem can help answering the core problem of the statistical treatment rule literature,
i.e. establishing probabilistic
regret
guarantees for treatment rules obtained from empirical data,
as considered by
Manski 2004
and the subsequent works.
The regret there is defined as the difference between the social
welfare potential and the expected utility achieved by an estimated decision
rule.
Our approach can also provide answers for multiple other problems of interdisciplinary interest.
For instance, we will derive asymptotic distributions for the binary optimal allocation subject to one resource constraint and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.

Our paper is an attempt to synthesize the methodology and perspective of several strands of literature.
Hereafter, the core nature of issues,
as will be readily apparent, comes from the statistical treatment rule literature.
A widely-known surprising phenomenon in the plug-in classification literature is that the second step classification is simpler than the first step regression, in the sense that the former usually converges faster than the latter

(
Devroye et al. 1996
;
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
)
.
Built on seminar works
Chen et al. 2003
and
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b

on method of moments allowing nonsmoothness,
especially the latter,
our paper can provide sets of conditions to support
\sqrt{n}
and faster convergence rates of both the regret and other objective of interest.
It will be shown that our differentiation results allow for
asymptotic analysis under settings for both classical empirical processes and recent advances in double / debiased machine learning
(
Chernozhukov et al. 2018a
;
Chernozhukov et al. 2022
)
.

To get some intuition of our approach, consider the
finite dimensional derivatives of
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
with respect to
\lambda
when
J=1
.
Assume that
\mathbb{P}\{\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)=\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\}=0
.
Then we can write,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},g\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right)\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\right)\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right].\end{split}

(3)

Formally, given the definition of
\gamma\left(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},g\right)
, we expect that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\partial\gamma\left(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},g\right)}{\partial\lambda_{0}}=\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right)g_{0}\left(X\right)\right],\\
&\frac{\partial\gamma\left(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},g\right)}{\partial\lambda_{1}}=\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\right)g_{1}\left(X\right)\right].\end{split}

A rigorous proof of the above is by direct calculation of the limit.
It is not hard to see that the terms of major difficulties are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{h\to 0}&\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\left(\lambda_{0}+h\right)g_{0}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right)-1\left(\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right)\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\right]}{h}\quad\text{and}\\
\lim_{h\to 0}&\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[1\left(\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)>\left(\lambda_{0}+h\right)g_{0}\left(X\right)\right)-1\left(\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(X\right)\right)\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(X\right)\right]}{h}.\end{split}

(4)

We will see later in section
3
that under mild conditions,
the two terms in (
4
) both converge to the same integral on the level set (i.e. fiber)

\{x:\lambda_{0}g_{0}\left(x\right)=\lambda_{1}g_{1}\left(x\right)\}
with opposing signs, so that they cancel out.
As a consequence, the desired partial derivatives exist and take the displayed simple form.

Our approach in section
3
is greatly inspired by the
integration on manifold
approach in
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b
.
In particular, we reapproach and generalize their results based on the concept of Hausdorff measure and powerful integration formulas, i.e. area and coarea formulas from geometric measure theory.
By precise calculation, we manage to show that the Hadamard derivative of
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
contains terms that are integrals under certain Hausdorff measure.
The Hadamard differentiability results are derived under mild primitive conditions,
which differentiates us from previous works relying on differentibility or pathwise differentibility assumptions. See for example
Sherman 1993
and
Luedtke and van der Laan 2016a
.

Returning to the general multivariate setting, the social welfare potential function
in (
1
) is not only subadditive but also positive homogeneous of degree one with respect to both
\lambda
and
g\left(\cdot\right)
, respectively,
which implies
that
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
is convex in
g\left(\cdot\right)
given
\lambda
and is convex in
\lambda
given
g\left(\cdot\right)
.
For example,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda,\alpha g+\left(1-\alpha\right)g^{\prime}\right)&=\max_{\phi\in\Phi}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)\left(\alpha g_{j}\left(X\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)g_{j}^{\prime}\left(X\right)\right)\right]\\
&\leq\max_{\phi\in\Phi}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\alpha\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)g_{j}\left(X\right)\right]+\max_{\phi\in\Phi}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(1-\alpha\right)\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)g_{j}^{\prime}\left(X\right)\right]\\
&=\alpha\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)\gamma\left(\lambda,g^{\prime}\right).\end{split}

However,
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
is not necessarily jointly convex in
\left(\lambda,g\right)
. We can not be assured that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\alpha\lambda+\left(1-\alpha\right)\lambda^{\prime},\alpha g+\left(1-\alpha\right)g^{\prime}\right)\leq\alpha\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right).\end{split}

Interestingly, the above simple observations seem to have gone completely unnoticed by the existing literature.
In section
4
, we apply deep results from
geometric functional analysis, in particular those by
Stegall 1978
,
Preiss 1990
and
Lindenstrauss and Preiss 2003
,
to show that under nearly no assumptions,
the social welfare potential function
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
is Fréchet differentiable on a generic set.

The surprising Fréchet differentiability property inpires us to reconsider the first order approximation of
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
.
Section
4
shows that the primitive conditions in section
3
can also be used to support the faster convergence rate in classical classification literature. See for example
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
among others.
Further, the same methodology can be combined with recent double / debiased machine learning approach
(
Chernozhukov et al. 2018a
;
Chernozhukov et al. 2022
)
to speed up the estimation of
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)
.
The roles of the regularity conditions clarified in this paper are
fully demonstrated when they collaborate in tandem with
the insights from the corresponding previous literature.

2
Literature

The construction of optimal decision rules from experimental or observational data have been considered by both the classical and recent literature in
multidisciplines.
Different terminology have been used, such as treatment allocation, policy learning in econometrics and individual treatment rule (ITR) in statistics.
A large body of the literature, including
Manski 2004
,
Qian and Murphy 2011
,
Zhao et al. 2012
,
Swaminathan and Joachims 2015
,
Zhou et al. 2017
,
Kitagawa and Tetenov 2018
,
Rai 2018
,
Luedtke and Chambaz 2020
,
Athey and Wager 2021
,
Mbakop and Tabord-Meehan 2021
,
Zhou et al. 2023
and
Ben-Michael et al. 2024
, among others, study in details this problem in an empirical risk minimization (ERM) framework.
The basic objective there is to provide probabilistic bound for

\displaystyle\begin{split}R\left(\hat{\phi},\phi^{*}\right)&=\mathbb{E}_{X,Y}\left[\sum_{j}\phi^{*}_{j}\left(X\right)Y_{j}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{X,Y}\left[\sum_{j}\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)Y_{j}\right],\\
\text{ for }\hat{\phi}&\in\mathop{\arg\max}\limits_{\phi\in\Phi_{0}}\sum_{i}\langle\phi\left(X_{i}\right),F\left(X_{i},D_{i},Y_{i}\right)\rangle,\end{split}

where
\phi^{*}
solves the population optimization program of (
1
).
The function
F\left(X,D,Y\right)
usually takes the inverse propensity weighting (IPW) or doubly robust (DR) form (see for example
Kitagawa and Tetenov 2018
and
Athey and Wager 2021
), and
\Phi_{0}\subset\Phi
is not too complex in an entropy sense.

The difficulty involved in the optimal allocation problem is due to the indicator function in the population solution which is hard to handle
(
Qian and Murphy 2011
)
.
Our solution of such a problem built on a divergent literature motivated by very different purposes.
To address the restoration of monotonicity in conditional quantile estimation,
the functional derivative of the sorting operator in the univariate case is studied by
Chernozhukov et al. 2010
.
The analysis of the sorting operator
is then extended to the multivariate cases
by
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b

using calculus on manifold techniques.
Kim and Pollard 1990
also hinted at a rudimentary form
of calculus on manifold in the derivation of the large sample properties of cube root consistent estimators.

Sasaki 2015
incorporated the tools based on the Hausdorff measure developed in fluid mechanics to characterize the information content of quantile partial derivatives for general structural functions.

The optimal allocation problem has also been discussed under a plethora of different but closely related settings.
Examples include asymptotically minimax optimal decision under the limits of experiments framework
(
Hirano and Porter 2009
)
, optimal decision under minimax regret
(
Stoye 2009
;
Tetenov 2012
;
Ben-Michael et al. 2024
)
, uniform confidence interval
(
Armstrong and Shen 2023
)
, allocation with spillover effects
(
Kitagawa and Wang 2023a
;
Kitagawa and Wang 2023b
)
and binary constrained optimal allocation
(
Bhattacharya and Dupas 2012
;
Luedtke and van der Laan 2016a
)
.
Our general functional Hadamard derivative results in subsection
3.3
are applicable for solving
the last problem above.
This problem is also closely relevant to the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve widely used in biostatistics and computer science.
A previous literature that has studied the inference of the ROC curves, including
Hsieh and Turnbull 1996
,
Lloyd 1998
,
Li et al. 1999
,
Hall et al. 2004
and
Bertail et al. 2008
,
did not account for the estimation error in the sample propensity score.
An exception is
Luckett et al. 2021
,
where the authors derive the uniform asymptotic results for estimated ROC curve when the classification is done by a support vector machine (SVM).
Unlike the studies above,
we derive the asymptotic properties of
the ROC curves obtained by plugging-in
the propensity score estimators,
which is consistent with the usage of
predictive classifiers in various disciplines.

The methodology in this paper also has intriguing connection with the faster convergence phenomenon in the classification literature
(
Devroye et al. 1996
;
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
)
. Consider,
especially, the margin assumption (MA) evoked by works including
Tsybakov 2004
,
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
and
Boucheron et al. 2005
, i.e.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left\{|p\left(X\right)-c|<t\right\}\leq Ct^{\alpha}\end{split}

for fixed
c
and some constant
C>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
.
Our conditions in section
3
directly imply the marginal
assumption with
\alpha\geq 1
.
This observation can be further combined with the recent double / debiased machine learning approach
(
Chernozhukov et al. 2018a
;
Chernozhukov et al. 2022
)
to form a relatively primitive level, plug-in and non empirical process complement of the optimal allocation literature.

3
Hadamard differentiability

Following the seminal research of

Chernozhukov et al. 2018b
, we consider (
3
)
as a functional mapping from
\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
to
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
,
and are interested in the differential change in
\gamma\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
induced by a
marginal change in
\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
. More precisely, we rigorously derive the
Hadamard derivative of

\gamma\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
with respect to
\left(\lambda,g\left(\cdot\right)\right)
, which justifies and generalizes the calculations in (
4
).
In this section, we assume that the underlying distribution of
X
,
denoted as
\mu
, is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Then we write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)g_{j}\left(X\right)\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(x\right)g_{j}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(x\right)g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x.\end{split}

where
f\left(x\right)
is the density function. Typically, the optimizing policy function takes the form of

\phi_{j}\left(x\right)=\prod_{l\neq j}1\left(\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\right).

For simplicity, if we abbreviate
\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)
as
g_{j}\left(x\right)
, then the main object that we are going turns out to be

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)\prod_{l\neq j}1\left(g_{j}\left(x\right)>g_{l}\left(x\right)\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x\end{split}

which can be further generalized to a form of the following sorting operator (as defined by
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)\prod_{l}1\left(h_{l}\left(x\right)>c\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x,\end{split}

(5)

where the number of terms in the product should be
less than
n
, the dimension of
X
.

3.1
Hausdorff measure and integration formulas

The following discussions are based on standard references by

Federer 1969
and
Evans and Garzepy 2015
.
In order to rigorously present surface integral
in
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b
, this section first aims to define
extrinsically the intuitive notion of length, area, and volume.
For this purpose, the Carathéodory criterion is used to
construct a measure space from a
\sigma
-subadditive set function (outer measure) on power set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
More specifically, we introduce Hausdorff’s construction, which
does not require any local parameterization on these sets. Therefore
no regularity assumption is needed.

Definition 3.1.1
.

(Outer measure)
For a set
O
, an outer measure is a function

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu^{*}:2^{O}\to\left[0,\infty\right]\quad\text{such that}\end{split}

(a)

\mu^{*}\left(\emptyset\right)=0
.

(b)

For arbitrary subsets
A,B
,

A\subset B\subset O
,
\mu^{*}\left(A\right)\leq\mu^{*}\left(B\right)
.

(c)

For arbitrary subsets
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots

of
O
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu^{*}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{i=1}B_{i}\right)\leq\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\mu^{*}\left(B_{i}\right).\end{split}

If an outer measure
\mu^{*}
is defined
on a metric space
\left(O,d\right)
and satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}d\left(A,B\right)>0\Rightarrow\mu^{*}\left(A\cup B\right)=\mu^{*}\left(A\right)+\mu^{*}\left(B\right),\quad\forall A,B\subset O.\end{split}

Then
\mu^{*}
is called a
metric outer measure.

In this paper, we use a series of metric outer measure with the following specific construction, called Hausdorff outer measure.

Definition 3.1.2
.

(Hausdorff outer measure)
Let
d
be the metric on
O
.
For arbitrary subset

E\subset O
, define its diameter as

\mathrm{diam}\ E=\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in E}d\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right),\mathrm{diam}\ \emptyset=0.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{k}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{k}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\},\end{split}

where
\Gamma\left(\cdot\right)
is the gamma function. For arbitrary
E\subset O
,
and for
\delta>0
,
define

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k,\delta}\left(E\right)=\inf\left\{\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{k}\left(\frac{\mathrm{diam}\ B_{j}}{2}\right)^{k}:E\subset\bigcup_{j\geq 1}B_{j},\mathrm{diam}\ B_{j}\leq\delta\right\}.\end{split}

The
k
-dimensional Hausdorff outer measure
of
E
is defined as

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k}\left(E\right)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k,\delta}\left(E\right)
, and can be verified to
be a metric outer measure on the metric space
\left(O,d\right)
.

Definition 3.1.3
.

(Hausdorff measure)
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{M}_{k}=\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(O\right)
is the
\sigma
-algebra
generated by
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k}
by the
Carathéodory criterion (See Theorem

T.1.1
).
Then the restriction of
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k}
to

\mathcal{M}_{k}
,

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k}|_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}
is
called Hausdorff measure on
\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{k}=\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k}
,
\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathcal{L}_{k}
,
where
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k}
denotes the
Lebesgue outer measure and
\mathcal{L}_{k}

denotes the Lebesgue measure on

\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{0}\left(E\right)
measures the cardinality of the set

E
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{*}_{0}\left(E\right)=\mathcal{H}_{0}\left(E\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{number of element in }E,&E\text{ is empty or finite,}\\
+\infty,&E\text{ is infinite}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

In this application,
we focus on the Hausdorff measure defined on
Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Definition 3.1.4
.

(Jacobian) For

k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, define the Jacobian at
x
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}Jf\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\det\left(\nabla f\left(x\right)^{T}\nabla f\left(x\right)\right)},&f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\sqrt{\det\left(\nabla f\left(x\right)\nabla f\left(x\right)^{T}\right)},&f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{k}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

The following Theorem
3.1.1
for
change of variable generalizes the conventional
integration change of variable formula for
invertible
mapping to allow for non-invertible mapping and an increase in dimension.

Theorem 3.1.1
.

Area formula

Let

Then for all measurable

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{S}J\psi\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{k}x=\int_{\psi\left(S\right)}\mathcal{H}_{0}\left(S\cap\psi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y.\end{split}

(6)

If a measurable function

is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{S}f\left(x\right)J\psi\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{k}x=\int_{\psi\left(S\right)}\left[\int_{S\cap\psi^{-1}\left(y\right)}f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{H}_{0}x\right]d\mathcal{H}_{k}y.\end{split}

(7)

Furthermore, the next

Theorem 3.1.2
.

Coarea formula

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set,

k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

\varphi:E\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a

S\subset E

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{S}J\varphi\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y.\end{split}

(8)

(9)

Remark 3.1.1

(

3.2
Hadamard derivatives of the sorting operator

In this section,
we introduce
our mathematics results on the
Hadamard differentiability of the sorting
operator in (
5
), where we intend to compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\left[\int\left(g_{j}\left(x\right)+tG_{j}\left(x\right)\right)f\left(x\right)1\left(h\left(x\right)+tH\left(x\right)>c\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x-\int g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)1\left(h\left(x\right)>c\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x\right].\end{split}

It should be clear that the main term that needs to be accounted for is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}\left[\int g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)1\left(h\left(x\right)+tH\left(x\right)>c\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x-\int g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)1\left(h\left(x\right)>c\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x\right].\end{split}

Therefore we focus on the following simplified form of the sorting operator
(with respect to the Lebesgue measure):

\displaystyle\begin{split}F\left(h,c\right)\coloneqq\int_{h\left(x\right)>c}f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x=\int\mathds{1}\left(h\left(x\right)>c\right)f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x,\end{split}

(10)

where, without loss of generality, the

g_{j}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)
term is replaced by
f\left(x\right)
above.

First, consider a special case of the sorting operator where

h\left(x\right)
is a scalar function, where
the directions of differentiation are limited to
1
.
Let
h
be a
C^{1}
or Lipschitz
function, with

\|\nabla h\left(x\right)\|>0,\mathcal{L}_{n}\;a.e.\;x.

Suppose also that
f
is
a integrable (nonnegative measurable) function.
Then by the coarea formula Theorem
3.1.2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{h\left(x\right)>c}f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x=\int^{\infty}_{c}\left[\int_{h^{-1}\left(c^{\prime}\right)}\frac{f\left(x\right)}{\left\|\nabla h\left(x\right)\right\|}d\mathcal{H}_{n-1}x\right]d\mathcal{L}_{1}c^{\prime}.\end{split}

Further, if

\essinf\left\|\nabla h\left(x\right)\right\|>0,

and
f
is integrable, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dc}\left(\int_{h\left(x\right)>c}f\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x\right)=-\int_{h^{-1}\left(c\right)}\frac{f\left(x\right)}{\left\|\nabla h\left(x\right)\right\|}d\mathcal{H}_{n-1}x\end{split}

for
\mathcal{L}_{1}\;a.e.\;c
,
by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,
where the derivative is conventionally defined. The right hand side above
is also a derivative in the distributional (weak) sense.

Definition 3.2.1
.

(Hadamard differentiability) Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be normed spaces equipped with norms

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}}
.
Consider the map
\phi:\mathcal{X}_{\phi}\subset\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
.
Then
\phi
is called Hadamard differentiable
at
\theta\in\mathcal{X}_{\phi}
tangentially to

\mathcal{X}_{0}\subset\mathcal{X}
,
if there is a continuous linear map

\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\frac{\phi\left(\theta+t_{n}\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{x}_{n}\right)-\phi\left(\theta\right)}{t_{n}}-\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}\left(x\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\to 0,\;\text{for all}\;t_{n}\to 0\;\text{and}\;\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{x}_{n}\to x,\end{split}

(11)

as
n\to\infty
,
where
\{\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{x}_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{X}
,
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
,
and
\theta+t_{n}\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{x}_{n}\in\mathcal{X}_{\phi}
for
n

large enough.

Remark 3.2.1
.

1.

When
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is a linear subspace,
by

2.

.

Definition 4.1.1

(Conservative set-valued fields,

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int^{1}_{0}&\left\langle\dot{\ell}\left(t\right),D\left(\ell\left(t\right)\right)\right\rangle dt\\
&\coloneqq\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)dt:\omega\left(t\right):[0,1]\to R\text{ is a measurable selection of}\left\langle\dot{\ell}\left(t\right),D\left(\ell\left(t\right)\right)\right\rangle\right\}=\{0\}.\end{split}

(17)

It is shown in

(

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\max_{v\in D\left(\ell\left(t\right)\right)}\left\langle\dot{\ell}\left(t\right),v\right\rangle dt=0,\end{split}

where the integral is understood in the Lebesgue sense, which is possible by Theorem 18.19 and Theorem 18.20 in
Aliprantis and Border 2006

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\min_{v\in D\left(\ell\left(t\right)\right)}\left\langle\dot{\ell}\left(t\right),v\right\rangle dt=0\quad\text{for all absolutely continuous loop $\ell$}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\dot{\ell}\left(t\right),v\left(t\right)\right\rangle dt=0.\end{split}

Definition 4.1.3

4.2
Envelope like theorem for social welfare potential function

Consider, for a given
g\left(\cdot\right)
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}e\left(\lambda^{\prime};\phi^{*}\left(\cdot;\lambda,g\right)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j}\right]-\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g\right),\end{split}

by construction,
e\left(\lambda^{\prime};\phi^{*}\left(\cdot;\lambda,g\right)\right)
achieves its maximum of zero
at
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
.
When there is no arbitrariness, we also denote
\phi^{*}\left(\cdot\right)\coloneqq\phi^{*}\left(\cdot;\lambda,g\right)

Note first that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j}\right]
is everywhere differentiable in
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
given

\phi^{*}\left(\cdot\right)
and
g\left(\cdot\right)
.
Because of the convexity of
\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g\right)
in
\lambda^{\prime}
given
g\left(\cdot\right)
,
\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g\right)
is differentiable in
\lambda^{\prime}
given
g\left(\cdot\right)
in a set
\Lambda
such that its complement
\Lambda^{c}
is a Lebesgue null set.
Then for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, and for each selection
\phi^{*}
given this choice of

\lambda
,
e\left(\lambda^{\prime};\phi^{*}\right)
is differentiable in
\lambda^{\prime}
at
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
.
Since zero must be in the set of supgradients
at a point of maximum of any function, there is
0\in\nabla^{+}e\left(\lambda^{\prime};\phi^{*}\right)\big|_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda}
and thus

\nabla e\left(\lambda^{\prime};\phi^{*}\right)\big|_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda}=0
,
implying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g\right)\Bigg|_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda}=\nabla\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j}\right]\Bigg|_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda}=\left(\mathbb{E}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j},j=0,\ldots,J\right)^{T}.\end{split}

An additional implication of the previous result is that for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

\phi^{*}\left(x;\lambda,g\right)
is unique in an almost surly sense since the expectation

\left(\mathbb{E}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j},j=0,\ldots,J\right)^{T}
is uniquely defined.

Intuitively, we expect such envelope theorem like statement remains true with respect to certain functional derivatives, so that we can discuss the functional derivatives of

\displaystyle\begin{split}e\left(\lambda^{\prime},g^{\prime};\phi^{*}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\phi_{j}^{*}g_{j}^{\prime}\right]-\gamma\left(\lambda^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right)\end{split}

(18)

at
\left(\lambda^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right)=\left(\lambda,g\right)
(here,
\phi^{*}\left(\cdot\right)\coloneqq\phi^{*}\left(\cdot;\lambda,g\right)
).
This conjecture will be subsequently verified.
Surprisingly, some Fréchet differentiability arguments can be established.

Definition 4.2.1
.

(Fréchet differentiability)
Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be normed spaces equipped with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

be an open set. Consider the map

is called Fréchet differentiable at

\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\xi\left(x\right)-\xi\left(\theta\right)-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\left(x-\theta\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=o\left(\|x-\theta\|_{\mathcal{X}}\right).\end{split}

Before we describe the next result we need to recall several topological and measure theoretic concepts. In a topological space
\mathcal{X}

satisfies

\overset{\circ}{\bar{S}}=\emptyset

has empty interior, then

is called
a nowhere dense subset of
\mathcal{X}
. If
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
is a countable union of

\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathcal{Z}
is called a meager subset of
X
, or
of the first category in
\mathcal{X}
. A subset
A\subset\mathcal{X}
is called residually
many (or just residual) in
\mathcal{X}
if
\mathcal{X}\setminus A
is meager in
\mathcal{X}
.
A set

is called a
G_{\delta}
set of
\mathcal{X}
if

is a
countable intersection of open sets; it is call a
F_{\sigma}
set if it is a countable union
of closed sets.

In

magnitude
of sets, call
\Gamma
-null sets. Let
T=\left[0,1\right]^{\mathbb{N}}
, where

\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers, be endowed with the product topology and product

. By the Tikhonov Theorem,

is a compact space. It can also be metrized by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\left(x,y\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{n}\left(x_{n},y_{n}\right)}{2^{n}\left(1+\rho_{n}\left(x_{n},y_{n}\right)\right)}\end{split}

where

By the product
\sigma

-algebra generated by all the coordinate projections. The product measure then should satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu\left[\prod_{i}B_{i},B_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}_{i},\ \text{and}\ B_{i}\neq E_{i},\forall i\in\mathcal{I},B_{i}=E_{i},\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\mathcal{I},\ \text{where}\ \mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{N},0<|I|<\infty\right]=\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mu_{i}\left(B_{i}\right),\end{split}

The existence and uniqueness of the countable product Lebesgue measure is guaranteed by the Kolmogorov extension theorem (See for example Chapter 1 and 3 in

Now, let

be a Banach space and

with a topology generated by

||\gamma||_{k}=\sup_{t\in T}||D_{k}\gamma\left(t\right)||

Given convexity and local Lipschitzness,
Under the conditions of Theorem

Condition 2 in Theorem

When

4.3
Fast convergence rates

It is also worth noting that our analysis is closely related to the fast
learning rates for plug-in classifiers in
Devroye et al. 1996
and
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
.
To illustrate these results consider a weighted version of the population accuracy measure
defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),R\right)&=\mathbb{E}\left[\tau Y1\left(X\in R\right)+\left(1-\tau\right)\left(1-Y\right)1\left(X\in R^{c}\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\tau\right)\left(1-p\left(X\right)\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(p\left(X\right)-\left(1-\tau\right)\right)1\left(X\in R\right)\right]\end{split}

(20)

Then an optimal choice of
R
, which is

1\left(p\left(x\right)>\left(1-\tau\right)\right)
,
for a given
\tau
leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)&=\max_{R}\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),R\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\tau\right)\left(1-p\left(X\right)\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(p\left(X\right)-\left(1-\tau\right)\right)1\left(p\left(X\right)>\left(1-\tau\right)\right)\right].\end{split}

The welfare regret is defined as the difference between
the optimized population welfare
and the feasible welfare based on an estimate of the optimal policy

\hat{R}=1\left(\hat{p}\left(x\right)>1-\tau\right)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(p\left(X\right)-\left(1-\tau\right)\right)\left(1\left(p\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left(p\left(X\right)-\left(1-\tau\right)\right)\right|\left|\left(1\left(p\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)\right)\right|\right].\end{split}

The key insight for analyzing convergence speed of above regret
is that on the set where
1\left(p\left(x\right)>\left(1-\tau\right)\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(x\right)>\left(1-\tau\right)\right)\neq 0
, or where either

\left\{p\left(x\right)>1-\tau,\hat{p}\left(x\right)\leq 1-\tau\right\}
or

\left\{p\left(x\right)\leq 1-\tau,\hat{p}\left(x\right)>1-\tau\right\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|p\left(x\right)-\left(1-\tau\right)|\leq|p\left(x\right)-\hat{p}\left(x\right)\right|.\end{split}

Therefore the regret can be bounded by,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)\right|&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|p\left(X\right)-\hat{p}\left(X\right)\right|\left|\left(1\left(p\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)\right)\right|\right]\\
&\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left|p\left(x\right)-\hat{p}\left(x\right)\right|\mathbb{E}\left|\left(1\left(p\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(X\right)>1-\tau\right)\right)\right|\\
\end{split}

As in
Chen et al. 2003
, for
\delta_{n}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left|p\left(x\right)-\hat{p}\left(x\right)\right|
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\left(1\left(p\left(x\right)>1-\tau\right)-1\left(\hat{p}\left(x\right)>1-\tau\right)\right)\right|\leq 1\left(1-\tau-\delta_{n}\leq p\left(x\right)\leq 1-\tau+\delta_{n}\right).\end{split}

As such the regret can be further bounded by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)\right|\leq\delta_{n}\mathbb{P}\left(1-\tau-\delta_{n}\leq p\left(X\right)\leq 1-\tau+\delta_{n}\right)\end{split}

Under the conditions in section
3
(see for example Proposition
3.2.3
) where
p\left(x\right)
has a bounded density at
1-\tau
, for a constant
C
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(1-\tau-\delta_{n}\leq p\left(X\right)\leq 1-\tau+\delta_{n}\right)\leq C\delta_{n}.\end{split}

In conclusion,

\left|\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)\right|\leq C\delta_{n}^{2}
.
Many nonparametric estimators
\hat{p}\left(x\right)
achieves
\delta_{n}=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(n^{-\frac{\beta}{2\beta+d}}\log n\right)
where
d
is the dimension of
x
, and
\beta
a smoothness parameter, implying that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+d}}\log^{2}n\right)\end{split}

and whenever
\beta>d/2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right)\right)-\gamma\left(\tau,p\left(\cdot\right),\hat{R}\right)\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).\end{split}

Note that in the above, we provide primitive conditions that offer
the case of
\alpha\geq 1

for the
margin
assumption (MA) in

Mammen and Tsybakov 1999
,
Tsybakov 2004
,
Boucheron et al. 2005
,

Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
,
Kitagawa and Tetenov 2018
and
Luedtke and Chambaz 2020
.

The misclassification error rate in
Audibert and Tsybakov 2007
is the most important
case of the weighted population accuracy (
20
) where
\tau=\frac{1}{2}
, since

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(Y\neq 1\left(X\in R\right)\right)&=1-\mathbb{P}\left(Y=1\left(X\in R\right)\right)\\
&=1-2\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}Y1\left(X\in R\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(1-Y\right)1\left(X\in R^{c}\right)\right]\\
&=1-2\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2},p\left(\cdot\right),R\right),\end{split}

where
p\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(Y|X=x\right)
.

Recall that in a general discrete allocation problem, we define welfare under policy as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\phi\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)Y_{j}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\left(X\right)g_{j}\left(X\right)\right],\end{split}

where

g_{j}\left(x\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{j}|X=x\right)
and
\phi_{j}\left(x\right)\geq 0,\forall j
,
\sum_{j=0}^{J}\phi_{j}\left(x\right)=1
.
Then an optimal choice of
\phi\left(\cdot\right)
, which be
given by (
2
), for a given
\lambda
leads to the social welfare potention function
(
1
).
The feasible analog based on an estimate
\hat{g}\left(x\right)
should then satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\text{if}\ \lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)>\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right),\ \forall l\neq j,\\
0,&\text{if}\ \lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)<\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right),\ \exists l\neq j.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

If there exists some
l
such that
\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)=\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{l}\left(X\right)
, then

\hat{\phi}\left(\cdot\right)
can be divided among all the maximal indexes. In the following we adopt a convention of allocating the optimal
\phi^{*}\left(x\right)
and
\hat{\phi}\left(x\right)
such that all the weight
among the maximal indexes is allocated to the smallest index member:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{j}^{*}\left(x\right)&=\prod_{l=j+1}^{J}1\left(\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)\geq\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\right)\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}1\left(\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\right),\\
\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(x\right)&=\prod_{l=j+1}^{J}1\left(\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(x\right)\geq\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{l}\left(x\right)\right)\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}1\left(\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{l}\left(x\right)\right).\end{split}

The welfare regret is defined as the difference between the
social welfare function
and
its feasible version based on the plug-in estimate of the optimal policy:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\phi^{*}\right)-\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\hat{\phi}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathbb{E}\left[\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)\left(\phi_{j}^{*}\left(X\right)-\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\right]\end{split}

Note that by definition, for each
x
,
\phi_{j}^{*}\left(x\right)
is either
0
or
1
for each

j=0,\ldots,J
. Furthermore, one and only one of
\phi_{j}^{*}\left(x\right)
out of
j=0,\ldots,J
takes
the value
1
.
The same is also true for
\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(x\right)
for each
x
.
Therefore we can write

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\phi^{*}\right)-\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\hat{\phi}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\lambda_{i}g_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\phi_{i}^{*}\left(X\right)\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\lambda_{i}g_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)-\left(\lambda_{i}\hat{g}_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\right|\phi_{i}^{*}\left(X\right)\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\lambda_{i}g_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)-\left(\lambda_{i}\hat{g}_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\right|1\left(\phi_{i}^{*}\left(X\right)=\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X\right)=1\right)\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\lambda_{i}g_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)-\left(\lambda_{i}\hat{g}_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\right|1\left(\phi^{*}\left(X\right)\neq\hat{\phi}\left(X\right)\right)\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\neq j}\left|\lambda_{i}g_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(X\right)-\left(\lambda_{i}\hat{g}_{i}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X\right)\right)\right|\sum_{k\neq l}\left|1_{kl}^{*}-\hat{1}_{kl}\right|\right]\end{split}

(21)

where we define, for
k\neq l,k,l=0,\ldots,J
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}1_{kl}^{*}\left(x\right)=1\left(k<l\right)1\left(\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(x\right)\geq\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\right)+1\left(k>l\right)1\left(\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\right).\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{1}_{kl}\left(x\right)=1\left(k<l\right)1\left(\lambda_{k}\hat{g}_{k}\left(x\right)\geq\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{l}\left(x\right)\right)+1\left(k>l\right)1\left(\lambda_{k}\hat{g}_{k}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{l}\hat{g}_{l}\left(x\right)\right).\end{split}

As previously, following
Chen et al. 2003
, for

\delta_{n}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{j=0,\ldots,J}|\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)-\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(x\right)|
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|1_{kl}^{*}-\hat{1}_{kl}|\leq 1\left(-2\delta_{n}\leq\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(x\right)-\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)\leq 2\delta_{n}\right).\end{split}

(22)

Then we can further bound the regret as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\phi^{*}\right)-\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\hat{\phi}\right)\leq 2\left(J+1\right)^{2}\delta_{n}\sum_{k\neq l}\mathbb{P}\left(-2\delta_{n}\leq\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(X\right)\leq 2\delta_{n}\right).\end{split}

Under the conditions in section
3

where for each pair
k\neq l
,

\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(x\right)-\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(x\right)

has a bounded density at zero,

\mathbb{P}\left(-2\delta_{n}\leq\lambda_{k}g_{k}\left(X\right)-\lambda_{l}g_{l}\left(X\right)\leq 2\delta_{n}\right)\leq C\delta_{n}
,
leading to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\phi^{*}\right)-\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\hat{\phi}\right)\leq C\delta_{n}^{2}.\end{split}

Given nonparametric estimates of
\hat{g}_{j}\left(x\right),j=0,\ldots,J
achieving

there is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)-\gamma\left(\lambda,g,\hat{\phi}\right)\right|=O_{\mathbb{P}}\left(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+d}}\log^{2}n\right)\end{split}

The unknown social welfare potential function
\beta_{0}=\gamma\left(\lambda,g\right)=\mathbb{E}\max_{j=0,\ldots,J}\lambda_{j}g_{j}\left(x\right)

can be estimated by its sample analog

\hat{\gamma}\left(\lambda,\hat{g}\left(\cdot\right)\right)=\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\max_{j=0,\ldots,J}\lambda_{j}\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)
. Following
Chernozhukov et al. 2018a
and
Chernozhukov et al. 2022
, we consider a Neyman-orthogonal
debiased version as, where with
Y_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}D_{ij}Y_{ij}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\beta}=\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\left[\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{\hat{p}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right]\end{split}

where
D_{ij}=1
if
D_{i}=j
,
p_{j}\left(x_{i}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(D_{ij}=1|X_{i}=x_{i}\right)
,

g_{j}\left(x_{i}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(Y_{j}|D_{ij}=1,X_{i}=x_{i}\right)
.
We should note that
Luedtke and Van Der Laan 2016b
and
Luedtke and Chambaz 2020
utilize a formula similar to the one above for the binary case under empirical process settings.
In a split sample scheme,
\hat{p}\left(\cdot\right)
and
\hat{g}\left(\cdot\right)
that are applied to each

are obtained in a different subsample that excludes

.
The goal is to show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{n^{\prime}}\left(\hat{\beta}-\beta_{0}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}^{*}\left(X_{i}\right)\left[g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{p_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right]-\beta_{0}\right)+o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(1\right).\end{split}

This will follow if we can show that
\Delta=o_{\mathbb{P}}\left(1\right)
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\Biggl(\lambda_{j}\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)&\left[\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{\hat{p}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right]\\
&-\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}^{*}\left(X_{i}\right)\left[g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{p_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right]\Biggl).\end{split}

For this purpose we decompose
\Delta=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\left[\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{\hat{p}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-\hat{g}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)-g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)-\frac{D_{ij}}{p_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right]\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^{\prime}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\lambda_{j}\left(\hat{\phi}_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)-\phi_{j}^{*}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\left[g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)+\frac{D_{ij}}{p_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)}\left(Y_{i}-g_{j}\left(X_{i}\right)\right)\right].\end{split}

Next we decompose

which is bounded using the same method as in the following.

21
) and (

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we explore a functional differentiability approach for a class of statistical optimal allocation
problem.
Inspired by
Chernozhukov et al. 2018b
, Hadamard differentiability is facilitated by a study of the general properties of the sorting operator.
Our derivation depends indispensably on the concept of Hausdorff measure and the area and coarea integration formula from geometric measure theory.
Based on our general Hadamard differentiability results, we have showed in subsection
3.3
that
both the asymptotic properties of the value function process of the binary constrained optimal allocation problem and the two-step ROC curve estimator can be directly derived from the functional delta method.
When the first step propensity score estimator is correctly specified, a computationally feasible bootstrap procedure is also validated for the two-step ROC estimator.
More surprisingly, utilizing deep geometric functional analysis results on convex and local Lipschitz functional,
we can demonstrate generic Fréchet differentiability for the social welfare potential function.
These intriguing results further motivate us to deliberate on estimation methods that take into consideration the first order term of the social welfare potential function.
Combining techniques from the literature of nonsmooth method of moment, plug-in classification and the recent development of double / debiased machine learning, we provide a debiased estimator of the social welfare potential function.
Here, the conditions required for Hadamard differentiability validate the margin assumption, which leads to a faster convergence rate.

We deliberately avoid aiming for more general but also more complex conditions in the application subsections
3.3
and
4.3
, in order to highlight our concise methodology.
Besides the details, a key message we would like to convey in this paper is that the value functions of the optimal allocation problems are usually more
regular
than expected. Much can be done by taking advantage of the
regularity property.
Extensions and generalizations of this paper, including semiparametric efficiency and multiclassification ROC surfaces,
are studied in follow-up work by
Feng et al. 2024
.

Proposition T.2.1

Let

\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(y\right)-\varphi^{\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(x-y\right)|\leq\epsilon|x-y|\end{split}

(32)

for all

Let
x_{0}\in E
,

k

\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\varphi\left(x_{0}\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\varphi\left(x_{0}\right),\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\varphi\left(x_{0}\right)\right\}

Define

By (

by definition,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(J\varphi\left(x\right)\right)^{2}=\det\left(\varphi^{\prime}\left(x\right)\varphi^{\prime}\left(x\right)^{T}\right)=\det\left(A\circ g^{\prime}\left(x\right)\circ g^{\prime}\left(x\right)^{T}\circ A^{T}\right).\end{split}

By SVD / PD,

, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\det\left(A\circ g^{\prime}\left(x\right)\circ g^{\prime}\left(x\right)^{T}\circ A^{T}\right)=\det\left(PU^{T}Q^{T}CQUP^{T}\right)=\left(\det\left(A\right)\right)^{2}\det\left(U^{T}Q^{T}CQU\right)\end{split}

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(1-\epsilon\right)^{2k}\leq\det\left(U^{T}Q^{T}CQU\right)\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)^{2k}.\end{split}

As a result,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(1-\epsilon\right)^{k}JA\leq J\varphi\left(x\right)\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)^{k}JA\end{split}

(34)

on
B\left(x_{0},\delta\right)
.
Compare (

) and

),
then see that the ideas of

When

,

\epsilon>0
, define

\varphi_{\epsilon}:E\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(x,z\right)\mapsto\varphi\left(x\right)+\epsilon z,\end{split}

.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y-\epsilon w\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y\\
&=\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}\int_{B\left(0,1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y-\epsilon w\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}yd\mathcal{H}_{k}w.\end{split}

,

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{\prime}\cap\varphi^{-1}_{\epsilon}\left(y\right)\cap\left(P^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\left(w\right)=\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y-\epsilon w\right)\right)\times\{w\}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}&\left(S\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y\\

Corollary T.3.4
.

Another Sard type lemma

Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,

be an open set,

\varphi:E\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
C^{1}

function. Then for

\mathcal{L}_{k}\;a.e.\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(Crit\left(\varphi\right)\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)=0\end{split}

Since
\varphi

Let

,

8

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{Crit\left(\varphi\right)}J\varphi\left(x\right)d\mathcal{L}_{n}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathcal{H}_{n-k}\left(Crit\left(\varphi\right)\cap\varphi^{-1}\left(y\right)\right)d\mathcal{H}_{k}y.\end{split}

The integral in the left hand side

,

and the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{k}=\mathcal{H}_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}

) holds for

\mathcal{L}_{k}\;a.e.\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

For arbitrary

(
35

Note that

.
Specifically, pick
k
linear independent
columns of

,
without loss of generality, assuming that
the first
k
columns

\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\varphi\left(x\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\varphi\left(x\right),\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\varphi\left(x\right)\right\}

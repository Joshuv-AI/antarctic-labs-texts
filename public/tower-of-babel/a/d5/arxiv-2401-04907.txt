Stability Criteria and Calculus Rules via Conic Contingent Coderivatives in Banach Spaces
Boris S. Mordukhovich, Pengcheng Wu, Xiaoqi Yang

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper addresses the study of novel constructions of variational analysis and generalized differentiation that are appropriate for characterizing robust stability properties of constrained set-valued mappings/multifunctions between Banach spaces important in optimization theory and its applications. Our tools of generalized differentiation revolves around the newly introduced concept of
\varepsilon
-regular normal cone to sets and associated coderivative notions for set-valued mappings. Based on these constructions, we establish several characterizations of the central stability notion known as the relative Lipschitz-like property of set-valued mappings in infinite dimensions. Applying a new version of the constrained extremal principle of variational analysis, we develop comprehensive sum and chain rules for our major constructions of conic contingent coderivatives for multifunctions between appropriate classes of Banach spaces.

Keywords:
variational analysis, generalized differentiation, well-posedness, relative Lipschitzian stability, constrained systems, normal cones and coderivatives, calculus rules

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010)
: 49J53, 49J52, 49K40

Introduction

This paper is devoted to developing adequate machinery of variational analysis and generalized differentiation to study
robust well-posedness
properties of set-valued mappings
subject to constraints
. The importance of such properties and their applications to optimization, control, and related areas has been highly recognized over the years, while the main attention was paid to unconstrained mappings in finite and infinite dimensions; see, e.g., the books
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
with the extensive bibliographies therein. The study and applications of such properties for set-valued mappings
relative
to given sets has begun quite recently in
[
6
,
10
,
13
]
, where the challenges in the study of the constrained properties in comparison with their unconstrained counterparts have been well realized.

In this paper, we focus on investigating the
relative Lipschitz-like property
of set-valued mappings between Banach spaces, which in fact is equivalent to the
relative metric regularity
and
relative linear openness
of the inverses; see
[
10
]
. We address here infinite-dimensional settings of Banach spaces, although the major results obtained below are new even in finite dimensions. Besides mathematical reasons, the main motivation for our research comes from the fact that infinite-dimensional problems naturally arise in many areas of applied mathematics and practical modeling such as dynamic optimization and equilibria, stochastic programming, finance, systems control, economics etc.

To accomplish our goals, we introduce a novel type of normals to sets labeled as the
regular
\varepsilon
-normal cone
. It is essentially different from the “collection of
\varepsilon
-normals” (which is not a cone) overwhelmingly used in infinite-dimensional variational analysis; see
[
7
]
. Based on this new normal cone and invoking also the
contingent cone
to constraint sets, we define various coderivative constructions for set-valued mappings and show that these newly introduced coderivatives lead us to
complete characterizations
(of the neighborhood and pointbased types) of the relative Lipschitz-like property of constrained multifunctions with the precise calculations of the
exact Lipschitzian bound
.

The efficient usage of the established stability criteria in the case of structured multifunctions, which appear in constrained problems and applications, requires well-developed
calculus rules
. We derive here comprehensive
pointbased chain
and
sum rules
for
conic contingent coderivatives
, the limiting constructions from the obtained characterizations of the relative Lipschitz-like property for general multifunctions. Our proofs are mainly based on the fundamental
extremal principle
of variational analysis and its new
relative
version needed for the study of constrained set systems.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the
\varepsilon
-regular normal cone and recall the needed preliminaries from variational analysis and generalized differentiation. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted, respectively, to establishing neighborhood and pointbased necessary conditions, sufficient conditions, and characterizations of the relative Lipschitz-like property. In Sections
5
and
6
, we derive pointbased chain and sum rules, respectively, for the newly introduced conic contingent coderivatives with establishing the novel relative extremal principle and relative fuzzy intersection rule for set systems in Banach spaces. The concluding Section
7
summarizes the main achievements of the paper and discusses some directions of the future research.

2
\varepsilon
-Regular Normal Cone and Preliminaries

First we recall some standard
notations
used in this paper. Given a Banach space
X
(which is assumed throughout below unless otherwise stated) and its topological dual
X^{*}
, the norms of
X
and
X^{*}
are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{X^{*}}
, respectively. Denote the closed unit balls of
X
and
X^{*}
by
B_{X}
and
B_{X^{*}}
, respectively, and use

\mathbb{S}:=\{x\in X~|~\|x\|_{X}=1\}
for the unit sphere. For
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, the symbol
\langle x^{*},x\rangle
indicates the canonical pairing between
X
and
X^{*}
. We use the notation
``x_{k}\to x"
for the strong convergence of sequences, while
``x_{k}\rightharpoonup x"
denotes the weak convergence, i.e.,

\langle x^{*},x_{k}\rangle\to\langle x^{*},x\rangle
for all
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. The notation
``x_{k}^{*}\rightharpoonup^{*}x^{*}"
stands for the weak
∗
convergence, i.e.,
\langle x_{k}^{*},x\rangle\to\langle x^{*},x\rangle
for any
x\in X
. The symbol
``x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}x"
tells us that
x_{k}\to x
with
x_{k}\in\Omega
as
k\to\infty
. The collection of natural numbers is denoted by
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.

Recall that a multifunction
F\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
between Banach spaces is
positively homogeneous
if
0\in F(0)
and
F(\lambda x)=\lambda F(x)
for all
x\in X
and
\lambda>0
. The
norm
of a positively homogeneous multifunction is defined by

\|F\|:=\sup\big\{\|y\|\;\big|\;y\in F(x),\;\|x\|\leq 1\big\}.

(2.1)

Given a set
\Omega\subset X
with
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, the symbol
T(\bar{x};\Omega)
stands for the
tangent/contingent cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
. That is,
w\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
if there exist sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
\{w_{k}\}\subset X
such that
w_{k}\to w
as
k\to\infty
and
\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}\in\Omega
whenever
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Fixing any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we now introduce our major new notion of the
\varepsilon
-regular normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
by

\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(\bar{x};\Omega):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}~\Big|~\limsup\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}}\leq\varepsilon\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\Big\},

(2.2)

where “
c
” stands for “cone”. Observe that
\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(\bar{x};\Omega)
is indeed a cone for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, in contrast to the
\varepsilon
-normal set
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
defined by

\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x};\Omega):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}~\Big|~\limsup\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}}\leq\varepsilon\Big\},

(2.3)

which is broadly used in variational analysis; see
[
7
]
. When
\varepsilon=0
, both sets in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) reduce to the
regular normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
denoted by
\widehat{N}(\bar{x};\Omega)
. It is well known that the latter notion is associated with the
regular subdifferential
of an extended-real-valued function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
at
\bar{x}
with
\varphi(\bar{x})<\infty
given by

\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}~\Big|~\limsup\limits_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}}\geq 0\Big\}.

(2.4)

Consider further a multifunction
S:X\rightrightarrows Y
between Banach spaces with the graph

\mbox{\rm gph}\,S:=\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;\big|\;y\in S(x)\big\}

and denote by
{\cal N}(z)
the collection of (open) neighborhoods around a point
z
.

Definition 2.1
.

A mapping
S:X\rightrightarrows Y
has the
Lipschitz-like property relative to

\Omega
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,S
with
\bar{x}\in\Omega
if
there are neighborhoods
V\in\mathcal{N}(\bar{x})
and
W\in\mathcal{N}(\bar{y})
together with a constant
\kappa\geq 0
such that

S(x^{\prime})\cap W\subset S(x)+\kappa\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{X}B_{X}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega\cap V.

The
exact Lipschitzian bound
of
S

relative to

\Omega
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{\Omega}S(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=\inf\big\{\kappa\geq 0

\displaystyle\big|~\exists V\in\mathcal{N}(\bar{x}),W\in\mathcal{N}(\bar{y})~\operatorname{such~that}

\displaystyle S(x^{\prime})\cap W\subset S(x)+\kappa\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{X}B_{X}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega\cap V\big\}.

The
duality mapping

J\colon X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
between a Banach space
X
and its dual is given by

J(x):=\big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}~\big|~\langle x^{*},x\rangle=\|x\|_{X}^{2},~\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=\|x\|_{X}\big\}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in X.

(2.5)

We clearly have
J(\lambda x)=\lambda J(x)
whenever
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and get by
[
1
, Proposition 2.12]
that
J(x)\not=\emptyset
for all
x\in X
. It is well known from geometric theory of Banach spaces (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) that any reflexive Banach space
X
admits a renorming
\|\cdot\|
such that both
X
and
X^{*}
are locally uniformly convex and
\|\cdot\|
is Fréchet differentiable at each nonzero point of
X
. Unless otherwise stated, in what follows we always consider
reflexive Banach spaces
with such a renorming. Recall from
[
3
, Proposition 8]
that the duality mapping (
2.5
) in reflexive spaces is single-valued and bicontinuous.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space, let
\Omega\subset X
be a closed and convex set, and let
x^{*}\in J(T(\bar{x};\Omega))
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that we have

x^{*}\in J\big(T(x;\Omega)\big)+\varepsilon B_{X^{*}}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in\Omega\cap(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X}).

Proof
.

Suppose on the contrary that the claimed inclusion fails and then find a number
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sequence
x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
such that

x^{*}\notin J\big(T(x_{k};\Omega)\big)+\varepsilon_{0}B_{X^{*}}\;\mbox{ for all }\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.6)

By the aforementioned properties of the duality mapping, we deduce from
J^{-1}(x^{*})\in T(\bar{x};\Omega)
and the well-known relationship

T(\bar{x};\Omega)\subset s\mbox{-}\liminf\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}T(x;\Omega),

valid for convex sets
\Omega
, that there exists a sequence of
z_{k}\in T(x_{k};\Omega)
such that
z_{k}\to J^{-1}(x^{*})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Letting
x_{k}^{*}:=J(z_{k})
, we get that

x_{k}^{*}\in J\big(T(x_{k};\Omega)\big)~~\operatorname{and}~~x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty,

which contradicts (
2.6
) and completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

3
Neighborhood Conditions for Relative Lipschitz-like Multifunctions

The goal of this section is to derive necessary conditions, sufficient conditions, and complete characterizations of the Lipschitz-like property for set-valued mappings
S\colon X\to Y
relative to sets
\Omega
in terms of the
\varepsilon
-regular normal cone
(
2.2
) to the
graph
of the constrained mapping
S|_{\Omega}
with the usage of the
contingent cone

T_{\Omega}(x)
to the constraint set
\Omega
. According to our general scheme (cf.
[
7
,
10
]
), such a construction can be viewed as the
\varepsilon
-coderivative
of constrained mapping in question; see Definition
4.2
(i). Note the conditions derived in this section in the case of set-valued between reflexive Banach spaces are
neighborhood
, i.e., they involve points nearby the reference one.

First we present a neighborhood
necessary
condition for the Lipschitz-like property of multifunctions relative to
arbitrary
constraint sets.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
S:X\rightrightarrows Y
be a set-valued mapping between reflexive Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, let
\Omega\subset X
be a nonempty set, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}
. If
S
has the Lipschitz-like property relative to
\Omega
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant
\kappa\geq 0
, then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}})
there exists
\delta>0
such that we have the condition

\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\big((x,y);\mbox{\rm gph}\,S|_{\Omega}\big)\cap[J\big(T(x;\Omega)\big)\times\{0\}]=\{(0,0)\}

(3.1)

whenever
(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
.

Proof
.

Having the imposed relative Lipschitz-like property gives us
\delta>0
with

S(x)\cap(\bar{y}+2\delta B_{Y})\subset S(x^{\prime})+\kappa\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{X}B_{X}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x,x^{\prime}\in\Omega\cap(\bar{x}+2\delta B_{X}).

(3.2)

Picking
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}})
, fix
(x,y)\in\text{gph}~S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
and
x^{*}\in T(x;\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
. By definition of the contingent cone, there exist
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
such that

x_{k}:=x+t_{k}x_{k}^{*}\in\Omega\;\mbox{ for all }\;k\in{\rm I\!N},

which implies the existence of
k_{0}\in{\rm I\!N}
ensuring the estimates

t_{k}<\frac{\delta}{2\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}}~~\text{and}~~\|x_{k}^{*}-x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}<\frac{\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}}{4}\;\mbox{for all }\;k>k_{0}.

This clearly leads us to the inequalities

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{k}-x\|_{X}+\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}<\frac{\delta}{2\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}}\|x_{k}^{*}\|_{X^{*}}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}}(\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}+\|x^{*}-x_{k}^{*}\|_{X^{*}})+\delta<2\delta.

It follows from (
3.2
) that for all natural numbers
k>k_{0}
we get

y\in S(x)\cap(\bar{y}+2\delta B_{Y})\subset S(x_{k})+\kappa\|x-x_{k}\|_{X}.

Therefore, there exist sequences
y_{k}\in S(x_{k})
and
\kappa_{k}\downarrow\kappa
such that

\|y_{k}-y\|_{Y}\leq\kappa_{k}\|x_{k}-x\|_{X},

which implies in turn that

\|(x_{k},y_{k})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}=\sqrt{\|x_{k}-x\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{k}-y\|_{Y}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\kappa_{k}^{2}+1}\|x_{k}-x\|_{X}.

Furthermore, the convergence
x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
allows us to find
k_{1}>k_{0}
such that

\langle J(x^{*}),x_{k}^{*}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}>0\;\mbox{ for all }\;k>k_{1}.

Thus for all
k>k_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle(J(x^{*}),0),(x_{k},y_{k})-(x,y)\rangle}{\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\|(x_{k},y_{k})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}}\geq\frac{t_{k}\langle J(x^{*}),x_{k}^{*}\rangle}{\sqrt{\kappa_{k}^{2}+1}\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\|x_{k}-x\|_{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa_{k}^{2}+1}}\frac{\langle J(x^{*}),x_{k}^{*}\rangle}{\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\|x_{k}^{*}\|_{X}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}>\varepsilon~~\text{as}~k\to\infty,

which yields
(J(x^{*}),0)\notin\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\big((x,u);\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\big)
. This verifies (
3.1
) due to the arbitrary choice of

(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}~S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
and
x^{*}\in T(x;\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

The next lemma provides a refinement of (
3.1
), which is valid in the same setting for
S
and
\Omega
as in Lemma
3.1
, while being useful to establish the neighborhood sufficient condition for the relative Lipschitz-like property in Lemma
3.3
below.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S
,
\Omega
, and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be the same as in Lemma
3.1
. If there exist numbers
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\delta>0
such that (
3.1
) holds, then whenever
\kappa>\sqrt{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-1}
we find
\gamma\in(0,\varepsilon)
ensuring the fulfillment of the estimate

\|x^{*}\|\leq\kappa\|y^{*}\|\;\mbox{ when }\;(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\gamma}^{c}\big((x,y);\mbox{\rm gph}\,S|_{\Omega}\big)\cap[J\big(T(x;\Omega)\big)\times Y^{*}]

(3.3)

for all
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
.

Proof
.

Set
\gamma:=\varepsilon-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}
and verify that (
3.3
) holds. Suppose on the contrary that there exist
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
and
(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\gamma}^{c}\big((x,y);\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\big)\cap[J\big(T(x;\Omega)\big)\times Y^{*}]
such that

\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}>\kappa\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}.

Considering further the pairs

(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*}):=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\kappa^{2}+1}(x^{*},y^{*})~~~\text{and}~~~(x_{T}^{*},y_{T}^{*}):=(x^{*},0)-(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*}),

we easily check the relationships

\displaystyle\|(x_{T}^{*},y_{T}^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}=

\displaystyle\left\|(x^{*},0)-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\kappa^{2}+1}(x^{*},y^{*})\right\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{(\kappa^{2}+1)^{2}}\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{4}}{(\kappa^{2}+1)^{2}}\|y^{*}\|_{Y^{*}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}},

\|(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}\leq\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}.

(3.5)

Then we deduce from (
3.4
) the inequalities

\displaystyle\langle(x_{T}^{*},y_{T}^{*}),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\|(x_{T}^{*},y_{T}^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y},

which, being combined with (
3.5
) and
(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\gamma}^{c}\big((x,u);\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\big)
, lead us to

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{gph}~S|_{\Omega}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(x,y)}\frac{\langle(x^{*},0),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\rangle}{\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(x,y)}\frac{\langle(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*})+(x_{T}^{*},y_{T}^{*}),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\rangle}{\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(x,y)}\frac{\langle(x_{N}^{*},y_{N}^{*}),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\rangle}{\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\gamma+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{2}+1}}\Big)\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}=\varepsilon\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}},

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\varepsilon
-normal cone. The latter yields
(x^{*},0)\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\big((x,y);\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}\big)
, which clearly contradicts (
3.1
) and hence verifies the claimed condition (
3.3
).
∎

Having Lemma
3.2
in hand and employing fundamental tools of variational analysis and generalized differentiation, we show next that the neighborhood condition (
3.3
) is actually
sufficient
for the relative Lipschitz-like property. Observe that, in contrast to the necessary conditions in Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we now require that the graph of
S
is
locally closed
, and the set
\Omega
is
closed
and
convex
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S\colon X\rightrightarrows Y
be a set-valued mapping between reflexive Banach spaces, and let
\Omega\subset X
be a closed and convex set. Fix
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}
, and assume that
\operatorname{gph}S
is locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. If there exist
\delta>0
,
\kappa\geq 0
, and
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that condition (
3.3
) holds for all
(x,y)\in\operatorname{gph}~S|_{\Omega}\cap[(\bar{x}+\delta B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\delta B_{Y})]
, then
S
has the Lipschitz-like property relative to
\Omega
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant
\kappa
.

Proof
.

Assume without loss of generality that
\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}
is closed in
X\times Y
and define the positive number
\sigma:=\delta/4(\kappa+1)
. Arguing by contradiction, suppose that
S
does not have the Lipschitz-like property relative to
\Omega
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant
\kappa
. Then there are
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega\cap(\bar{x}+\sigma B_{X})
with
x^{\prime}\not=x^{\prime\prime}
and
y^{\prime\prime}\in S(x^{\prime\prime})\cap(\bar{y}+\sigma B_{Y})
such that

d(y^{\prime\prime};S(x^{\prime}))>\kappa\|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}\|_{X}:=\beta

(3.6)

via the distance function in
Y
.
Clearly, we have
0<\beta\leq 2\kappa\sigma
. Let us now show that condition (
3.3
) from Lemma
3.2
fails in this case whenever
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Define the extended-real-valued function
\varphi:X\times Y\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
by

\varphi(x,y)=\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{X}+\delta_{\operatorname{gph}S|_{\Omega}}(x,y),\quad(x,y)\in X\times Y,

via the indicator function of the set
\mbox{\rm gph}\,S_{\Omega}
and observe that
\varphi
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) by the closedness of
gph

S|_{\Omega}
), that
\inf\varphi
is finite, and that

\varphi(x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime})\leq\inf\varphi+\frac{\beta}{\kappa}.

Equip the product space
X\times Y
with the norm

p(x,y):=\kappa\varrho\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{Y},\;\mbox{ where }\;\varrho:={\rm min}\Big\{\frac{\gamma}{4},\frac{1}{2k}\Big\},

(3.7)

and apply the fundamental Ekeland variational principle (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Theorem 2.26]
) to
\varphi
on
X\times Y
with the norm (
3.7
). This gives us a pair
(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\in X\times Y
such that

\displaystyle p(\widetilde{x}-x^{\prime\prime},\widetilde{y}-y^{\prime\prime})\leq\beta,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\varphi(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\leq\varphi(x^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime\prime}),\;\mbox{ and}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varphi(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})\leq\varphi(x,y)+\frac{1}{\kappa}p(x-\widetilde{x},y-\widetilde{y})\;\mbox{ for all }\;(x,y)\in X\times Y.

(3.10)

It immediately follows from (
3.8
) that

\|\widetilde{y}-y^{\prime\prime}\|_{Y}\leq\beta.

(3.11)

So we have
\widetilde{x}\not=x^{\prime}
, since otherwise

5
Chain Rules for Conic Contingent Coderivatives

In this section, we show that both conic mixed and normal contingent coderivatives from Definition
4.2
(ii,iii) for constrained multifunctions between reflexive Banach spaces
satisfy
pointbased chain rules
under a mild qualification condition. This qualification condition is expressed in terms of the relative mixed contingent coderivative (

Since chain rules have never been developed for coderivative constructions from
[

, the obtained results are also new for those constructions. The proofs below mostly revolve around the (approximate)
extremal principle
of variational analysis; see
[
7
]

We begin with evaluating the
\varepsilon
-regular normal cone to inverse images of sets under single-valued continuous mappings that holds in any Banach spaces.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a single-valued continuous mapping between Banach spaces, and let

be a nonempty subset of

Proof
.

Fix
x^{*}\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(\bar{x};f^{-1}(\Theta))
and get by
Definition (
2.2
) of the
\varepsilon
-regular normals that

\limsup\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{-1}(\Theta)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}}\leq\varepsilon\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}.

The continuity of

and the convergence

yields
f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta}}{{\longrightarrow}}f(\bar{x})=\bar{y}
. Thus

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{(x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[X\times\Theta]\cap\operatorname{gph}f}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\frac{\langle(x^{*},0),(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\rangle}{\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}}\leq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{-1}(\Theta)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle(x^{*},0),(x,f(x))-(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))\rangle}{\sqrt{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|f(x)-f(\bar{x})\|_{Y}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{-1}(\Theta)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}}\leq\varepsilon\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}},

which yields

∎

6
Relative Extremal Principle and Sum Rules for Conic Contingent Coderivatives

The primary goal of this section is to establish, for the first time in the literature,
pointbased sum rules
for both normal and mixed conic contingent coderivatives from Definition
4.2
(ii,iii), and hence for their equivalent versions from
[
10
]
. To derive such rules, we develop two major results of the undoubted own interest for variational analysis and applications, which go far beyond coderivative calculus. They are the
relative extremal principle
for systems of closed sets and the
relative fuzzy intersection rule
for
\varepsilon
-regular normals whose proof is based on the relative extremal principle.

We start with the definition of
relative extremal points
of set systems, where
\Pi_{X}(\Lambda)
signifies the projection of a set
\Lambda\subset X\times Y
onto the space
X
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset X\times Y
and
\Omega\subset X
be nonempty subsets of Banach spaces. We say that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
is a
local extremal point
of the set system
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
relative to
\Omega
if there exist sequence
\{b_{k}\}\subset X
and a neighborhood
U
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
b_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
,
\Pi_{X}(\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2})\subset\Omega
, and

\big(\Lambda_{1}+(0,b_{k})\big)\cap\Lambda_{2}\cap U=\emptyset\;\mbox{ for all~large }\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

Here is the
relative extremal principle
for closed sets in reflexive Banach spaces.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two reflexive Banach spaces, let
\Omega
be a closed and convex subset of
X
, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be a local extremal point of the system of closed sets
\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
relative to
\Omega
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist pairs
(x_{i},y_{i})\in\Lambda_{i}\cap[(\bar{x}+\varepsilon B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\varepsilon B_{Y})]
and
(x_{i}^{*},y_{i}^{*})\in X^{*}\times Y^{*}
,
i=1,2
, such that we have the relationships

\displaystyle(x_{i}^{*},y_{i}^{*})\in\big[\widehat{N}\big((x_{i},y_{i});\Lambda_{i}\big)+\varepsilon(B_{X^{*}}\times B_{Y^{*}})\big]\cap\big[J\big(T(x_{i};\Omega)\big)\times Y^{*}\big],\quad i=1,2,

(6.1)

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})+(x_{2}^{*},y_{2}^{*})=0,\;\mbox{ and }\;\|(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}+\|(x_{2}^{*},y_{2}^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}=1.

Proof
.

Take a neighborhood
U
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
from Definition
6.1
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
b\in Y
with
\|b\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon^{3}/2
and
(\Lambda_{1}+(0,b))\cap\Lambda_{2}\cap U=\emptyset
. Assume for simplicity that
U=X\times Y
and that
\varepsilon<1/2
. Then considering the function

\varphi(z):=\|(x_{1},y_{1})-(x_{2},y_{2})+(0,b)\|_{X\times Y}~~\operatorname{for}~z=(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in X\times Y\times X\times Y,

we conclude that
\varphi(z)>0
on
\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
, and hence
\varphi
is Fr
\acute{\text{e}}
chet differentiable at any point
z\in\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
due to our choice of the equivalent norm on the reflexive space
X\times Y
; see Section
2
. In what follows, we always use the product norm

\|z\|:=(\|x_{1}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{1}\|_{Y}^{2}+\|x_{2}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{2}\|_{Y}^{2})^{1/2}\;\mbox{ on }\;X\times Y\times X\times Y,

which is obviously Fr
\acute{\text{e}}
chet differentiable at nonzero points of
X\times Y\times X\times Y
.

Let
z_{0}:=(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{x},\bar{y})
and observe that the set

W(z_{0}):=\{z\in\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}~|~\varphi(z)+\varepsilon\|z-z_{0}\|^{2}/2\leq\varphi(z_{0})\}

is nonempty and closed. Furthermore, for each
z:=(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in W(z_{0})
we get

\|x_{1}-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{1}-\bar{y}\|_{Y}^{2}+\|x_{2}-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{2}-\bar{y}\|_{Y}^{2}\leq 2\varphi(z_{0})/\varepsilon=2\|b\|_{Y}/\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{2},

(6.2)

which implies that
W(z_{0})\subset(\bar{x}+\varepsilon B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\varepsilon B_{Y})\times(\bar{x}+\varepsilon B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\varepsilon B_{Y})
. Next we inductively construct sequences of vectors
z_{k}\in\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
and nonempty closed sets
W(z_{k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as follows. Given
z_{k}
and
W(z_{k})
,
k=0,1,\ldots
, select
z_{k+1}\in W(z_{k})
satisfying

\varphi(z_{k+1})+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\|z_{k+1}-z_{j}\|^{2}}{2^{j+1}}<\inf\limits_{z\in W(z_{k})}\left\{\varphi(z)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\|z-z_{j}\|^{2}}{2^{j+1}}\right\}+\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{2^{3k+2}}.

Construct further the set
W(z_{k+1})
by

W(z_{k+1}):=\left\{z\in\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}~|~\varphi(z)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k+1}\frac{\|z-z_{j}\|^{2}}{2^{j+1}}\leq\varphi(z_{k+1})+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\|z_{k+1}-z_{j}\|^{2}}{2^{j+1}}\right\}

and then define the function

\phi(z):=\varphi(z)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|z-z_{j}\|^{2}}{2^{j+1}}.

(6.3)

Following the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 2.10]
, we know that there exists
\bar{z}:=(\bar{x}_{1},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{x}_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\in X\times Y\times X\times Y
such that
z_{k}\to\bar{z}
as
k\to\infty
and that
\bar{z}
is a minimum point of
\phi
over the set
\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
. Hence the function
\psi(z):=\phi(z)+\delta_{\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}}(z)
achieves at
\bar{z}
its minimum over
X\times Y\times X\times Y
, which immediately yields
0\in\hat{\partial}\psi(\bar{z})
. Note that
\phi
is Fr
\acute{\text{e}}
chet differentiable at
\bar{z}
due to
\varphi(\bar{z})\not=0
and the smoothness of
\|\cdot\|^{2}
. Now applying the sun rule of
[
7
, Proposition 1.107(i)]
, we get

-\nabla\phi(\bar{z})\in\widehat{N}(\bar{z};\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2})=\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x}_{1},\bar{y}_{1});\Lambda_{1}\big)\times\widehat{N}\big((\bar{x}_{2},\bar{y}_{2});\Lambda_{2}\big).

It follows from (
6.3
) that
\nabla\phi(\bar{z})=(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*},v_{2}^{*})\in X^{*}\times Y^{*}\times X^{*}\times Y^{*}
, where

\displaystyle(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*}):=(x^{*},y^{*})+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(J(\bar{x}_{1}-x_{1j}),J(\bar{y}_{1}-y_{1j})\big)}{2^{j}},

\displaystyle(u_{2}^{*},v_{2}^{*}):=-(x^{*},y^{*})+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(J(\bar{x}_{2}-x_{2j}),J(\bar{y}_{2}-y_{2j})\big)}{2^{j}}

with
(x_{1j},y_{1j},x_{2j},y_{2j})=z_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots
, and where

(x^{*},y^{*}):=\left(\frac{J(\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2})}{\sqrt{\|\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{y}_{2}+b\|_{Y}^{2}}},\frac{J(\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{y}_{2}+b)}{\sqrt{\|\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{y}_{2}+b\|_{Y}^{2}}}\right).

The latter tells us that
\|(x^{*},y^{*})\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}=1
. It follows from (
6.2
) that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(J(\bar{x}_{i}-x_{ij}),J(\bar{y}_{i}-y_{ij})\big)\big\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}=\sqrt{\|\bar{x}_{i}-x_{ij}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\bar{y}_{i}-y_{ij}\|_{Y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\|\bar{x}_{i}-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\bar{y}_{i}-\bar{y}\|_{Y}^{2}}+\sqrt{\|\bar{x}-x_{ij}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\bar{y}-y_{ij}\|_{Y}^{2}}\leq 2\varepsilon<1

for

and
i=1,2
. Therefore,

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big\|\big(J(\bar{x}_{1}-x_{1j}),J(\bar{y}_{1}-y_{1,j})\big)\big\|_{X^{*}\times Y^{*}}}{2^{j}}\leq 2,\quad i=1,2.

Putting

and
(x_{i}^{*},y_{i}^{*}):=(-1)^{i}(x^{*},y^{*})/2
for
i=1,2
, we arrive at the relationships in (
6.1
) by
J(\bar{x}_{2}-\bar{x}_{1})\in J(T(\bar{x}_{1};\Omega))
and
J(\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2})\in J(T(\bar{x}_{2};\Omega))
due to the convexity of
\Omega
and the construction of
(x^{*},y^{*})
, which completes the proof.
∎

The next theorem establishes a
relative fuzzy intersection rule
for closed sets in Hilbert spaces. The proof is based on the relative extremal principle from Theorem
6.2
while using the convexity-preserving property of the duality mapping, which holds in Hilbert spaces; see Remark
6.4
for more discussions. Note that in Hilbert spaces, the duality mapping reduce to the identity operator.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
and

be Hilbert spaces, let
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}\subset X\times Y
be locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}

\Pi_{X}(\Theta_{1}\cup\Theta_{2})\subset\Omega

\Omega\subset X

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and pick
(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}\big)\cap[T(\bar{x};\Omega)\times Y]
. Then for any
\nu>0
, there exist
\lambda\geq 0
,
(x_{i},y_{i})\in\Theta_{i}\cap[(\bar{x}+\nu B_{X})\times(\bar{y}+\nu B_{Y})]
, and
(x_{i}^{*},y_{i}^{*})\in[\widehat{N}\big((x_{i},y_{i});\Theta\big)+(\|(x^{*},y^{*})\|_{X\times Y}+\nu)\varepsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y})]\cap[T(x_{i};\Omega)\times Y]
,
i=1,2
, such that

\lambda(x^{*},y^{*})-(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})-(x_{2}^{*},y_{2}^{*})\in\nu(B_{X}\times B_{Y})\;\mbox{ and }\;\max\{\lambda,\|(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\|_{X\times Y}\}=1.

(6.4)

Proof
.

Take any
\nu>0
and
(x^{*},y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y});\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}\big)\cap[T(\bar{x};\Omega)\times Y]
, and denote
L:=\|(x^{*},y^{*})\|_{X\times Y}
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that

\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle y^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle-(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)\varepsilon\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}\leq 0

(6.5)

for all
(x,y)\in\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}\cap U
. Define subsets of
X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}:=\big\{(x,y,\alpha)\in X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}~\big|

\displaystyle~(x,y)\in\Theta_{1},~\alpha\geq 0\big\};

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}:=\big\{(x,y,\alpha)\in X\times Y\times\mathbb{R}~\big|

\displaystyle~(x,y)\in\Theta_{2},~\alpha\leq\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle y^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle

\displaystyle-(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)\varepsilon\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}\big\}.

Observe that
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)\in\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
and that the sets
\Lambda_{i}
are locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
. Moreover, it is easy to check that

\Lambda_{1}\cap\big(\Lambda_{2}-(0,0,\nu)\big)\cap(U\times\mathbb{R})=\emptyset~~\operatorname{for~all}~\nu>0

due to (
6.5

\Lambda_{i}
,
i=1,2

(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)

\Omega

\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
. For any

, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that there exists a positive number
\rho

x^{*}\in T(x;\Omega)+\tau\varepsilon B_{X}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\Omega\cap(\bar{x}+2\rho B_{X}).

(6.6)

6.2
to the set system

\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\}
, we find
(x_{i},y_{i},\alpha_{i})\in\Lambda_{i}
and
(x_{i}^{*},y_{i}^{*},\lambda_{i})\in\widehat{N}\big((x_{i},y_{i},\alpha_{i});\Lambda_{i}\big)\cap[T(x_{i};\Omega)\times Y\times\mathbb{R}+\tau\varepsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y}\times\mathbb{R})]
,

, such that

\displaystyle\|x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{1}^{*}+y_{2}^{*}\|_{Y}^{2}+|\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}|^{2}<\tau^{2}\varepsilon^{2},

(6.7)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\|x_{i}^{*}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{i}^{*}\|_{Y}^{2}+|\lambda_{i}|^{2}}\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\tau\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}+\tau\varepsilon\right),

\displaystyle\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y_{i}-\bar{y}\|_{Y}^{2}+|\lambda_{i}|^{2}<{\rm min}\{\tau^{2}\varepsilon^{2},\rho^{2}\},\quad i=1,2.

It is easy to deduce from the above that
\lambda_{1}\leq 0
,
(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\in\widehat{N}\big((x_{1},y_{1});\Theta_{1}\big)\cap[T(x_{1};\Omega)\times Y+\tau\varepsilon(B_{X}\times B_{Y})]
, and

\limsup\limits_{(x,y,\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Lambda_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(x_{2},y_{2},\alpha_{2})}\frac{\langle x_{2}^{*},x-x_{2}\rangle+\langle y_{2}^{*},y-y_{2}\rangle+\lambda_{2}(\alpha-\alpha_{2})}{\sqrt{\|x-x_{2}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y-y_{2}\|_{Y}^{2}+|\alpha-\alpha_{2}|^{2}}}\leq 0.

(6.8)

We get from the structure of
\Lambda_{2}
that
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
and

\alpha_{2}\leq\langle x_{2}^{*},x_{2}-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle y_{2}^{*},y_{2}-\bar{y}\rangle-(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)\varepsilon\|(x_{2},y_{2})-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}.

(6.9)

6.9

6.8

and

It remains to consider the case of equality in (

The usage of the triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities ensures that

|\alpha-\alpha_{2}|\leq(L+L\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}\nu\varepsilon)\|(x,y)-(x_{2},y_{2})\|_{X\times Y}.

Observe that the left-hand side

\displaystyle\vartheta=

\displaystyle\langle x_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}x^{*},x-x_{2}\rangle+\langle y_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}y^{*},y-y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)\varepsilon(\|(x_{2},y_{2})-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}-\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}),

and thus (

\displaystyle\langle x_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}x^{*},x-x_{2}\rangle+\langle y_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}y^{*},y-y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq[\lambda_{2}(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)+\tau(1+L+L\varepsilon+\nu\varepsilon)]\varepsilon\|(x,y)-(x_{2},y_{2})\|_{X\times Y}

for all

\displaystyle(x_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}x^{*},y_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}^{*}y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\mu\varepsilon}\big((x_{2},y_{2});\Theta_{2}\big)

(6.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{with}~\mu:=\lambda_{2}(L+\frac{1}{2}\nu)+\tau(1+L+L\varepsilon+\nu\varepsilon).

Employing Lemma

ensures the existence of
\rho^{\prime}\in(0,\rho)

x_{2}^{*}\in T(x;\Omega)+\tau\varepsilon B_{X}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\Omega\cap(x_{2}+\rho^{\prime}B_{X}).

It follows from the representation of

that there exists a pair

Then we get
x_{2}^{*}\in T(u;\Omega)+\tau\varepsilon

x^{*}\in T(u;\Omega)+\tau\varepsilon
from (

Denoting

\lambda:=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\eta},~~(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*}):=-\frac{1}{\eta}(x_{2}^{*},y_{2}^{*}),~~(u_{2}^{*},v_{2}^{*}):=\frac{1}{\eta}(x_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}x^{*},y_{2}^{*}+\lambda_{2}y^{*}),

we have that

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
S_{i}:X\rightrightarrows Y
,
i=1,2
, be set-valued mappings between Hilbert spaces, let
\Omega\subset X
be a closed and convex set, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\operatorname{gph}(S_{1}+S_{2})|_{\Omega}
. Consider the mapping
G\colon X\times Y\rightrightarrows Y^{2}
defined by

G(x,y):=\big\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in Y\times Y~\big|~y_{1}\in S_{1}(x),~y_{2}\in S_{2}(x),~y_{1}+y_{2}=y\big\}.

Fix
(\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})\in G(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, and let
G
be inner semicontinuous relative to
\Omega\times Y
at

(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})
. Assume that the graphs of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are locally closed around

(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})
and
(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{2})
, respectively, that either
S_{1}
is PSNC relative to
\Omega
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})
or
S_{2}
is PSNC relative to
\Omega
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{2})
, and that
\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
satisfies the mixed coderivative qualification condition

D_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{1}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{1})(0)\cap(-D_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{2}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{2}))(0)=\{0\}.

(6.15)

Then for all
y^{*}\in Y
, we have the inclusions

D_{\Omega}^{c*}(S_{1}+S_{2})(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(y^{*})\subset D_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{1}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{1})(y^{*})+D_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{2}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{2})(y^{*}),

(6.16)

\widetilde{D}_{\Omega}^{c*}(S_{1}+S_{2})(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(y^{*})\subset\widetilde{D}_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{1}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{1})(y^{*})+\widetilde{D}_{\Omega}^{c*}S_{2}(\bar{x}|\bar{y}_{2})(y^{*}).

(6.17)

Proof
.

We verify only (
6.16
), since the proof of (
6.17
) is similar. Take any
(x^{*},y^{*})\in X\times Y
with
x^{*}\in D_{\Omega}^{c*}(S_{1}+S_{2})(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(y^{*})
and find sequences

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0,\;(x_{k},y_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{gph}(S_{1}+S_{2})|_{\Omega}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\;\mbox{ and}

(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{c}\big((x_{k},y_{k});\operatorname{gph}(S_{1}+S_{2})|_{\Omega}\big)\cap[T(x_{k};\Omega)\times Y]

x_{k}^{*}\rightharpoonup^{*}x^{*}~~\operatorname{and}~~y_{k}^{*}\to y^{*}\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty.

By the inner semicontinuity of
G
relative to
\Omega\times Y
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})
, there exists a sequence
(y_{1k},y_{2k})\to(\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})
with
(y_{1k},y_{2k})\in G(x_{k},y_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the sets

\Theta_{i}:=\big\{(x,y_{1},y_{2})\in X\times Y\times Y~\big|~(x,y_{i})\in\operatorname{gph}S_{i}|_{\Omega}\big\}~~\operatorname{for}~i=1,2,

which are locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1},\bar{y}_{2})
since the graphs of
S_{i}
are assumed to be locally closed around
(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{i})
,
i=1,2
. We have
(x_{k},y_{1k},y_{2k})\in\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}
and easily check that

(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{c}\big((x_{k},y_{1k},y_{2k});\Theta_{1}\cap\Theta_{2}\big)\cap[T(x_{k};\Omega)\times Y\times Y]~~\operatorname{for~all}~k\in\mathbb{N}.

(6.18)

We know that the sequence
\{(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*})\}
is bounded and hence find

such that

for all

6.3
to the intersection in (

gives us

is PSNC relative to
\Omega

(\bar{x},\bar{y}_{1})

x_{1k}^{*}\to 0
that contradicts the condition

due to
\lambda_{k}\downarrow 0

y_{1k}^{*}\to 0
as

Hence we can put

7
Concluding Remarks

This paper introduces and develops new notions of relative generalized normals and corresponding coderivatives for set-valued mappings in Banach spaces and use them for deriving necessary conditions, sufficient conditions, and characterizations of the relative Lipschitz-like property of general multifunctions. Moreover, it is shown that the limiting conic contingent coderivatives in the obtained characterizations enjoy pointbased calculus rules, which justify their broad usage in applications. To achieve such a calculus, we develop a powerful technique of variational analysis resulting in the novel relative extremal principle for systems of sets and the corresponding fuzzy intersection rule.

The major goals of our future research revolve around applications of the newly developed results to various problems of optimization and equilibria. From the technical viewpoint, we plan to relax the convexity assumption on the constraint set in the stability results and calculus rules. Furthermore, we intend to generalize the Hilbert space requirement in the fuzzy intersection and pointbased sum rules derived in Section
6
.

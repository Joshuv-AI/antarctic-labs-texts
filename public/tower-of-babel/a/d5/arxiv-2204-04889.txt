Muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) and Thermal WIMP DM in U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ {\rm U(1)}_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} Models Preprint: KIAS-P22019
Seungwon Baek, Jongkuk Kim, and P. Ko

Abstract
The U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ {\rm U(1)}_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} model is anomaly-free with the Standard Model (SM) fermion content, and can make substantial contributions to the muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) at the level of Δ ​ a μ ∼ O ⁡ ( 10 ) × 10 − 10 \Delta a_{\mu}\sim O(10)\times 10^{-10} for M Z ′ ∼ O ⁡ ( 10 − 100 ) M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim O(10-100) MeV and g X ∼ ( 4 − 8 ) × 10 − 4 g_{X}\sim(4-8)\times 10^{-4} . In this light Z ′ Z^{\prime} region, it was claimed that the model can also incorporate thermal WIMP dark matter (DM) if M DM ∼ M Z ′ / 2 M_{\rm DM}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2 . This setup relies on DM particles annihilating into SM particles through a Z ′ Z^{\prime} -mediated s s -channel. In this work, we show that this tight relationship between M Z ′ M_{Z^{\prime}} and M DM M_{\rm DM} can be evaded or nullified both for scalar and spin-1/2 DM by considering the contributions from the dark Higgs boson ( H 1 H_{1} ). The dark Higgs boson plays an important role, not only because it gives mass to the dark photon but also because it introduces additional DM annihilation channels, including new final states such as H 1 ​ H 1 H_{1}H_{1} , Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} , and Z ′ ​ H 1 Z^{\prime}H_{1} . As a result, the model does not require a close mass correlation between the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson and dark matter M DM ∼ M Z ′ / 2 M_{\rm DM}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2 any longer, allowing for a broader range of mass possibilities for both scalar and fermionic dark matter types. We explore in great details various scenarios where the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry is either fully broken or partially remains as discrete symmetries, Z 2 Z_{2} or Z 3 Z_{3} . This approach broadens the model’s capacity to accommodate various WIMP dark matter phenomena in the light Z ′ Z^{\prime} region where the muon ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu} makes a sensitive probe of the model.

\Delta\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}=-\mu(X^{2}\Phi^{\dagger}+\text{H.c.})

(24)

This term supplements the generic dark matter Lagrangian specified in Eq. (

{\rm U}(1)_{X}
symmetry is spontaneously broken down to its

subgroup (
X\rightarrow-X
),
á la

Krauss-Wilczek mechanism

, after
\Phi

This phenomenon has been previously explored in the literature for explaining anomalies such as the Galactic Center

Baek:2014kna

Okada:2019sbb

Baek:2020owl

Upon symmetry breaking induced by a nonzero

\mu

\mu(X^{2}\Phi^{\dagger}+\text{H.c.})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu v_{\Phi}(X^{2}_{R}-X^{2}_{I})\left(1+\frac{\phi}{v_{\Phi}}\right),

(25)

where
X=(X_{R}+iX_{I})/\sqrt{2}

X_{R}

X_{I}

\displaystyle M^{2}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{2}_{X}+\sqrt{2}\mu v_{\Phi},

\displaystyle M^{2}_{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{2}_{X}-\sqrt{2}\mu v_{\Phi}.

(26)

Assuming
\mu>0
, the lighter state
X_{I}
becomes the DM candidate.
The mass splitting is quantified by the dimensionless parameter
\Delta\equiv(M_{R}-M_{I})/M_{I}
. Notably, the interaction between the dark photon and
DM is off-diagonal (or inelastic) and described by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset g_{X}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}(X_{R}\partial^{\mu}X_{I}-X_{I}\partial^{\mu}X_{R}).

(27)

This introduces intriguing aspects to the DM phenomenology, particularly regarding the mass splitting’s implications on DM stability and detection prospects.

4

4.1
Generic Case:
Q_{\chi}/Q_{\Phi}\neq\pm 1/2

For the generic case of Dirac fermion dark matter (DM), the DM Lagrangian is expressed as

\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}=\overline{\chi}(i\not{D}-m_{\chi})\chi.

In this scenario, direct renormalizable interactions between
\chi
and
\Phi
are absent. This situation stands in stark contrast to the scalar DM cases previously discussed. Assuming a gauge coupling
g_{X}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})
, the DM pair annihilation cross sections for processes
\chi\bar{\chi}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime}H_{1}
are on the order of
\mathcal{O}(g_{X}^{4}/M_{\chi}^{2})
. Consequently, these cross sections are too small to yield the correct thermal relic density of DM for
M_{\chi}\gg M_{Z^{\prime}}
. The desired relic density can only be achieved in the resonance region,
M_{\chi}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
, via
\chi\bar{\chi}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow
SM particles, confirming findings from previous studies.

Specifically the thermal-averaged DM annihilation cross section into leptons is given by

\langle\sigma v_{\rm rel}(\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}\to\ell\bar{\ell})\rangle\simeq\frac{g^{4}_{X}Q^{2}_{\chi}}{2\pi}\frac{\left(2M^{2}_{\chi}+M^{2}_{\ell}\right)}{\left(4M^{2}_{\chi}-M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\Gamma^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{M^{2}_{\ell}}{M^{2}_{\chi}}},

and similarly for neutrinos,

\langle\sigma v_{\rm rel}(\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}\to\nu_{\ell}\bar{\nu_{\ell}})\rangle\simeq\frac{g^{4}_{X}Q^{2}_{\chi}}{2\pi}\frac{M^{2}_{\chi}}{\left(4M^{2}_{\chi}-M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\Gamma^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}},

where
\ell=\mu,\tau
.
For
g_{X}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim(10-100)
MeV, the total annihilation cross section falls short of achieving the correct relic density in the off-resonance region.
An appropriate DM relic abundance is attainable only in the resonance region with
M_{\chi}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
.

If kinematically allowed, additional DM annihilation channels include:

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v_{\rm rel}(\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{g^{4}_{X}Q^{4}_{\chi}}{4\pi}\frac{M^{2}_{\chi}-M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{\left(M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}-2M^{2}_{\chi}\right)^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{M^{2}_{\chi}}}

(34)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v_{\rm rel}(\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}H_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{c^{2}_{\alpha}g^{4}_{X}Q^{2}_{\Phi}Q^{2}_{\chi}}{256\pi}\frac{\left[16M_{\chi}^{4}-8M_{\chi}^{2}(M^{2}_{H_{1}}-5M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}})+\left(M^{2}_{H_{1}}-M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right]}{M_{\chi}^{4}\left[\left(4M^{2}_{\chi}-M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\right)^{2}+M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\Gamma^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right]}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{M^{4}_{H_{1}}+\left(4M^{2}_{\chi}-M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-2M^{2}_{H_{1}}\left(M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}+4M^{2}_{\chi}\right)}

(35)

Nevertheless, achieving the correct relic density of DM is constrained primarily to the vicinity of the resonance region because of the too-small gauge coupling
g_{X}
, even when these additional annihilation channels
are considered.

4.2
Local
Z_{2}
fermion DM:
Q_{\chi}=1,Q_{\Phi}=2

In the context of the Dirac fermion DM model, we explore a specific scenario characterized by
Q_{\chi}=1,Q_{\Phi}=2
.
This choice modifies the DM Lagrangian at the renormalizable level:

\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}=\overline{\chi}(i\not{D}-m{\chi})\chi-\left(y_{\Phi}\overline{\chi^{C}}\chi\Phi^{\dagger}+\text{H.c.}\right).

(36)

The symmetry breaking pattern is
{\rm U}(1)_{X}\to Z_{2}
due to the
y_{\Phi}
terms. This model, serving as a dark gauge model for inelastic fermion DM, has been previously investigated in the context of DM bound state formation
Ko:2019wxq
and the XENON1T excess
Baek:2020owl

Kamada:2018zxi

Upon
{\rm U}(1)_{X}
symmetry breaking with a nonzero
v_{\Phi}
, the original Dirac fermion
\chi
decomposes into two Majorana fermions (
\chi_{R}
and
\chi_{I}
) with a mass splitting proportional to
v_{\Phi}

\delta\equiv M_{R}-M_{I}=2y_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}.

(37)

Assuming
y_{\Phi}>0
, we ensure
\delta>0
, causing the lighter state
\chi_{I}

5
Conclusions

In this study, we investigated the implications of a light
Z^{\prime}
on thermal dark matter,
incorporating both scalar and Dirac fermion dark matter (DM) candidates within the framework of

U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
extensions to the Standard Model (SM).
The light
Z^{\prime}
is not only sensitive to the muon anomalous magnetic moment
(g-2)_{\mu}
, but it can also significantly enhance the dark matter annihilation cross section through its longitudinal polarization. This enhancement is most relevant in the
XX^{\dagger}\to H_{1}\to Z^{\prime}_{L}Z^{\prime}_{L}
channel.
Traditionally, thermal DM is introduced under the assumption that the dark matter mass meets the condition
M_{\text{DM}}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
.
The correct DM relic density is achieved by DM annihilation through the
Z^{\prime}
resonance, which dictates
M_{\text{DM}}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2\approx\mathcal{O}(10)
MeV.

In this paper, we considered the contributions of the dark Higgs boson, an often overlooked aspect in previous studies, in various DM models.
Notably, we found that the stringent mass relationship
M_{\text{DM}}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
can be circumvented.
Our analysis demonstrated that weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) with masses ranging from the GeV to TeV scales, significantly
exceeding the
M_{Z^{\prime}}\approx\mathcal{O}(10)
MeV constraint indicated by the above mass relationship
in the absence of the dark Higgs boson, can still be viable thermal WIMP DM candidates without violating other constraints.
We have established that the dark Higgs is essential for achieving the electroweak-scale dark matter. With the dark Higgs in place, DM annihilation into light
Z^{\prime}
modes is enhanced, facilitating efficient thermal DM production.
Note that these solutions are independent of the
Z^{\prime}
contribution to
\Delta a_{\mu}
, as DM annihilations are primarily controlled by the dark matter and the dark Higgs coupling
\lambda_{\Phi X}
, and the dark Higgs mass
M_{H_{1}}
, while
\Delta a_{\mu}
is governed by the dark gauge coupling
g_{X}
.

The details of DM phenomenology depend on the
U(1)
charge assignments to the DM and the dark Higgs (
\Phi
) fields. The
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
symmetry can be broken in a generic or a specific manner, leading to either
Z_{2}
(inelastic scalar or fermion DM models) or a
Z_{3}
scalar DM model. The inclusion of the dark Higgs boson introduces new DM (co-)annihilation channels, such as
{\rm DM}+{\rm\overline{DM}}\to H_{1}H_{1},H_{1}Z^{\prime}
, and
{\rm DM}+{\rm\overline{DM}}\to H_{1}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
. In the latter process, there is an enhancement in longitudinal
Z^{\prime}
pair production. These newly opened channels significantly broaden the viable DM mass range, spanning from the GeV to a few TeV

3
3

3

Recently two of the present authors also explored lighter DM
(X)
mass regions
(
O(10)
MeV
\lesssim m_{X}\lesssim 10
GeV)
in the same gauge models
Ho:2024cwk
, motivated by the Belle II announcement of the branching ratio for

B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
, which was reported to exceed the SM prediction by
2.7\sigma
.
In Ref.
Ho:2024cwk
, the excess was interpreted as the
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}+
dark sector particles,
either into DM pair or dark photon pair. In the latter case, the dark photon decays invisibly into a pair of
the SM neutrinos,
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\nu\bar{\nu}
.
,
while satisfying the constraints from the direct DM searches.
This deviation from the traditional tight correlation between
M_{Z^{\prime}}
and
M_{\rm DM}
(
M_{\rm DM}\sim M_{Z^{\prime}}/2
) underscores the importance of considering the dark Higgs boson for a comprehensive understanding of DM phenomenology in the presence of a massive dark photon. Our analysis clearly demonstrates that a thorough exploration of DM phenomenology involving a massive dark photon necessitates the inclusion of the dark Higgs boson.

The models under consideration offer opportunities for further tests through dark matter indirect detection and collider experiments.
Among the most significant DM annihilation channels are
{\rm DM}+{\rm\overline{DM}}\to H_{1}H_{1},H_{1}Z^{\prime},Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
.
The resulting
H_{1}
predominantly undergoes decay into a pair of
Z^{\prime}
bosons. In our analysis, we have adopted benchmark points with
M_{Z^{\prime}}=10
MeV,
11.5
MeV and
50
MeV.
Consequently, for
M_{Z^{\prime}}<M_{\mu}
,
Z^{\prime}
decays into either muon-type or tau-type neutrinos.
Anticipating DM annihilation in the vicinity of the Galactic center, we predict a substantial neutrino flux.
This flux stemming from DM annihilation stands as a target for detection by experiments like IceCube and Hyper-Kamiokande detectors
Baek:2008nz
;
Asai:2020qlp
.
As the dark photon exclusively couples to the second- and third-generation leptons,
the proposed muon colliders represent the most promising avenue for probing the model.
However, verifying
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
-charged DM models at proton-proton colliders, such as the LHC, poses challenges.
Nevertheless, investigations near the
Z
boson mass region can be pursued through processes like
pp\to Z\to 2\mu Z^{\prime}\to 4\mu

as outlined in
CMS:2018yxg
, even though the allowed zone conflicts with the current muon
(g-2)
data.

A.1
Muon
(g-2)

The magnetic moment of the muon is expressed as

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\mu}\left(\frac{e}{2m}\right)\vec{S},

(40)

where
g_{\mu}
denotes the gyromagnetic ratio. Considering radiative corrections, the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon is defined by

\displaystyle a_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\mu}-2\right).

(41)

{\rm U(1)}_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}

Z^{\prime}

Baek:2001kca

\Delta a_{\mu}=\frac{\alpha_{X}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{2M_{\mu}^{2}x^{2}(1-x)}{x^{2}M_{\mu}^{2}+(1-x)M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}},

where
\alpha_{X}=g^{2}_{X}/4\pi
.

If one extends the particle content by including exotic leptons and/or dark matter,

A.2
BaBar and LHC 4
\mu

Dark photon searches at electron beam dump experiments impose constraints on our model’s parameter space. For instance, BaBar conducted searches for the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{\prime}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
followed by

BaBar:2016sci

We use the
2\sigma
exclusion limit from the BaBar bounds, as depicted in Fig. 1.

The

Z

and a muon pair. If

2\sigma

A.3
Neutrino trident production

Neutrino beam experiments, such as CHARM-II
CHARM-II:1990dvf
and CCFR
CCFR:1991lpl
, have established neutrino trident production, wherein muon-neutrino scattering on a nucleus produces a muon pair,
\nu_{\mu}N\to\nu_{\mu}N\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

Altmannshofer:2014pba
. The observed scattering cross sections are

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\rm CHARM-II}}{\sigma_{\rm SM}}=1.58\pm 0.57,\qquad\frac{\sigma_{\rm CCFR}}{\sigma_{\rm SM}}=0.82\pm 0.28.

(43)

The observational data is consistent with the Standard Model (SM) prediction. Therefore, neutrino trident production imposes a stringent constraint, essentially excluding the parameter region with
M_{Z^{\prime}}>O(1)
GeV
Altmannshofer:2014pba
. In our study, we consider the
2\sigma
exclusion limit from the CCFR data.

A.4
Cosmic Microwave Background

There is an interaction between
Z^{\prime}
and neutrinos in our model. We assume that
Z^{\prime}
and DM were in thermal equilibrium with the SM bath in the early Universe. If the
Z^{\prime}
mass is much below the muon mass, the
Z^{\prime}
decay process will provide energy and entropy to neutrinos. If this occurs after the epoch of neutrino decoupling, corresponding to
T\sim O(1)
MeV, it introduces additional entropy and energy, potentially affecting the successful Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN). This effect modifies
N_{\rm eff}
, and we consider
N_{\rm eff}<3.4
as the exclusion limit.

However, there exists a discrepancy between the observed Hubble constant today, as derived from the Cosmic Microwave Background, and other astrophysical observations. This is known as the Hubble tension. To address the Hubble tension, an increase in
N_{\rm eff}
is considered beneficial
Escudero:2019gzq
;
Huang:2021dba
;
DiValentino:2021izs
.
For two of our benchmark points,
BPIII
and
BPI
,
(M_{Z^{\prime}},g_{X})=(10\text{ MeV},10^{-5})~\text{and}~(11.5\text{ MeV},4\times 10^{-4})
, we allow
N_{\rm eff}>3.2
to reconcile this Hubble tension.

The decay width of the dark Higgs to a lepton pair is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(H_{1}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}_{\ell}M_{H_{1}}}{8\pi v^{2}_{H}}\sin^{2}\alpha\left(1-\frac{4M^{2}_{\ell}}{M^{2}_{H_{1}}}\right)^{3/2},

(44)

If kinematically allowed, the dark Higgs can decay to a pair of

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(H_{1}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}_{X}c^{2}_{\alpha}}{32\pi}\frac{M^{3}_{H_{1}}}{M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{4M^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}}{M^{2}_{H_{1}}}+\frac{12M^{4}_{Z^{\prime}}}{M^{4}_{H_{1}}}\right).

(45)

For large

M_{H_{1}}\geq 2M_{X}

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(H_{1}\to XX^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\Phi X}v^{2}_{\Phi}\cos^{2}\alpha}{16\pi M_{H_{1}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{4M^{2}_{X}}{M^{2}_{H_{1}}}}.

(46)

In our parameter space of interest, corresponding to
M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim\mathcal{O}(10)
MeV and
M_{H_{1}}\geq\mathcal{O}(0.1)
GeV, the dark Higgs predominantly decays into a pair of either
Z^{\prime}
bosons or DMs.

Z^{\prime}
subsequently decays to
\nu_{\alpha}\overline{\nu}_{\alpha}

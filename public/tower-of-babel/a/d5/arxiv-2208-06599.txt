Abstract
We study tautological vector bundles over the Hilbert scheme of points on surfaces. For each K K -trivial surface, we write down a simple criterion ensuring that the tautological bundles are big and nef, and illustrate it by examples. In the K ​ 3 K3 case, we extend recent constructions and results of Bini, Boissière and Flamini from the Hilbert scheme of 2 and 3 points to an arbitrary number of points. Among the K K -trivial surfaces, the case of Enriques surfaces is the most involved. Our techniques apply to other smooth projective surfaces, including blowups of K ​ 3 K3 s and minimal surfaces of general type, as well as to the punctual Quot schemes of curves.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface, and let
X^{[k]}
denote the Hilbert scheme of
k
points on
X
. Each vector bundle
F\to X
of rank
r
yields a tautological vector bundle
F^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
of rank
rk
given by

\displaystyle F^{[k]}=p_{\star}(q^{\star}F\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}}).

Here,
p
,
q
are the natural projections from
X^{[k]}\times X
, and
\mathcal{Z}\subset X^{[k]}\times X
denotes the universal subscheme.

The literature surrounding the geometry of the tautological bundles is vast. Likewise, many notions of positivity for vector bundles have been studied in algebraic and complex differential geometry. Merging these two themes, it is natural to investigate the positivity properties of the tautological bundles.

In this note, we address the question whether the bundles
F^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
are big and nef.
To our knowledge, for
K3
s, this has been considered for the first time in the recent article
[
7
]
, alongside the stability and bigness of twists of the tangent bundle of
X^{[k]}
. Specifically, if
X
a
K3
surface of Picard rank
1
, and the number of points is
k=2,3
, it is shown in
[
7
]
that
F^{[k]}
is big and nef when
F
is either

(a)

a positive line bundle,

(b)

a twist of a Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundle (for suitable numerics),

(c)

a twist of an Ulrich bundle.

Recall that a vector bundle
V
over a scheme
Y
is said to be big and nef if the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(1)\to\mathbb{P}(V)
is big and nef, where
\mathbb{P}(V)
denotes the projective bundle of one dimensional quotients. A discussion of big and nef vector bundles can be found in
[
21
, Chapters 6 and 7]
.
A useful well-known characterization occurs when
V\to Y
is globally generated. In this case, if the top Segre class

(-1)^{\dim Y}\int_{Y}s(V)>0

(1.1)

it follows that
V\to Y
is big and nef.

1.1
Results

The original motivation for our work was provided by the recent results of
[
7
]
, which we extend in several directions.

(i)

For
K3
s, we allow for arbitrary number of points
k
, and derive a condition that ensures
F^{[k]}
is big and nef, see Theorem
2.1
. We apply this theorem to obtain analogues of examples
(a)
–
(c)
above for any
k
.

(ii)

We allow for arbitrary
K
-trivial surfaces. The case of Enriques surfaces is the most difficult, and we only have results in odd rank, see Theorem
3.6
, as well as a conjectural bound in general.

(iii)

We consider other smooth projective surfaces, including blowups of
K3
s in Theorem
4.3
, and minimal surfaces of general type, in rank
1
, in Theorem
4.4
. The latter theorem is the most involved result we prove here, requiring a more detailed analysis than for other geometries.

(iv)

We show how the same ideas yield similar results over the punctual Quot schemes of curves, see Theorem
5.2
.

Compared to
[
7
]
, the new ingredient is the closed form calculation of the Segre integrals in
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
33
]
. The formulas are explicit, and the goal here is to show how to apply them to derive geometric positivity results. This is not always immediate, and the arguments require several different ideas. We thus believe it is worthwhile to record the outcome. We also illustrate our calculations by a few geometric examples.

2
\boldsymbol{K3}
surfaces

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface. The bundle
F^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
is globally generated, and therefore nef, provided
F\to X
is
(k-1)
-very ample. By definition,
(k-1)
-very ampleness is the requirement that the natural map

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,F)\to H^{0}(X,F\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\zeta})

is surjective for all zero-dimensional subschemes
\zeta
of
X
of length
k
. Thus, via (
1.1
), if
F
is
(k-1)
-very ample and

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s\big(F^{[k]}\big)>0,

then
F^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
is big and nef. This is explained for instance in
[
7
, Propositions 2.4 and 4.5]
.

Let
(X,H)
be a polarized
K3
surface, and let
r=\operatorname{rank}F
. Central for our argument is the following structural expression for the Segre integrals established in
[
27
]
:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\int_{X^{[k]}}s\big(F^{[k]}\big)=A_{0}(z)^{c_{2}(F)}A_{1}(z)^{c_{1}^{2}(F)}A_{2}(z).

(2.1)

The series
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
, and
A_{2}
are given by explicit algebraic functions

\displaystyle A_{0}(z)=(1+(1+r)t)^{-r-1}(1+(2+r)t)^{r},

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)=(1+(1+r)t)^{\frac{r}{2}}(1+(2+r)t)^{-\frac{r-1}{2}},

\displaystyle A_{2}(z)=(1+(1+r)t)^{r^{2}+2r}(1+(2+r)t)^{-r^{2}+1}(1+(1+r)(2+r)t)^{-1},

(2.2)

for the change of variables

z=t(1+(1+r)t)^{1+r}.

We point out that in
[
27
]
,
r
stands for
\operatorname{rank}(F)+1
, while for us
r=\operatorname{rank}(F)
; the above expressions account for the different notational conventions. Related formulas over the moduli space of higher rank sheaves were proposed in
[
17
]
and were recently proven in
[
31
]
.

For each rank
r
vector bundle
F\to X
, we write
v=v(F)=\operatorname{ch}(F)\sqrt{\operatorname{Td}(X)}
for its Mukai vector, and we set

\displaystyle\chi=\chi(F),\qquad\delta=1+\frac{1}{2}\langle v,v\rangle.

Here
\langle\,,\,\rangle
is the Mukai pairing given by

\displaystyle\langle v,v\rangle=\int_{X}v_{2}^{2}-2v_{0}v_{4}\qquad\text{for vectors}\quad v=(v_{0},v_{2},v_{4})\in H^{0}(X)\oplus H^{2}(X)\oplus H^{4}(X).

Moreover,
\delta
equals the expected dimension of the moduli space of sheaves of type
v
.
We show

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
\chi\geq(r+2)k
and
\delta\geq 0
.
If
F
is
(k-1)
-very ample, then
F^{[k]}
is a big and nef vector bundle over
X^{[k]}
.

Proof.

By the first paragraph of this section, it suffices to show that the integral of the Segre class
s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)
is positive. To this end, we use an equivalent form of equation (
2.1
), which can be found in
[
27
, p. 11]
.
Specifically, via a residue calculation, it was established there that

\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)=\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{k}}\bigl[(1+(2+r)t)^{\delta}(1+(1+r)t)^{\chi-\delta-(r+1)k}\bigr].

(2.3)

Expanding via the binomial theorem, equation (
2.3
) shows that the Segre integral is positive when the expression between brackets is a polynomial of degree at least
k
, which is the case for

\displaystyle\delta\geq 0,\qquad\chi-\delta-(r+1)k\geq 0,\qquad\chi-(r+1)k\geq k.

However, since
\delta
could be large, we seek better bounds. To this end, we change variables, setting

\displaystyle t=\frac{u}{1-(1+r)u}.

We rewrite (
2.3
) as

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}(1+(2+r)t)^{\delta}(1+(1+r)t)^{\chi-\delta-(r+1)k}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{u=0}(1+u)^{\delta}(1-(1+r)u)^{-\chi+(r+2)k-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Coeff}_{u^{k}}(1+u)^{\delta}(1-(1+r)u)^{-\chi+(r+2)k-1}.

Letting
a_{i}
denote the coefficients of the term
(1+u)^{\delta}
, we have
a_{i}>0
for
0\leq i\leq\delta
. Similarly, the coefficients of the second term are

\displaystyle b_{j}=(1+r)^{j}(-1)^{j}\binom{-\chi+(r+2)k-1}{j}>0,

since
-\chi+(r+2)k-1<0
. Here, we use the standard definition of binomial numbers

\displaystyle\binom{x}{j}=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-j+1)}{j!}

for arbitrary
x
. Thus, the Segre integral equals
\sum a_{i}b_{j}
, the sum ranging over
i+j=k
,
0\leq i\leq\delta
,
j\geq 0
. The integral is positive since each term
a_{i}b_{j}>0
, and the sum is non-empty (it contains the term
a_{0}b_{k}
). The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The theorem is certainly not optimal in all cases, but it suffices for our purposes. To illustrate it, when
\delta=0
, equation (
2.3
) yields the following result originally noted in
[
27
, Proposition 2.1]
:

\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)=(r+1)^{k}\binom{\chi-(r+1)k}{k}.

(2.4)

In this case, the positivity of the Segre integral is guaranteed when
\chi\geq(r+2)k
, but also when
\chi<(r+1)k
and
k
even.

The vanishing of the Segre integrals (
2.4
) for exceptional bundles with
(r+1)k\leq\chi<(r+2)k
played an important role in the proof of (2.2) in
[
27
]
. The point of Theorem
2.1
is that we can furthermore pin down the sign of the Segre integral for
\chi
to the right of the above interval.

Remark 2.3
.

In rank
1
,
(k-1)
-very ampleness of nef line bundles over
K3
surfaces can be effectively studied using
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
. Specifically, if

L
is nef and
L^{2}>4k
, then either
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample or else there exists an effective divisor
D
such that
L-2D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective, with

L.D-k\leq D^{2}<L.D/2<k.

(2.5)

Furthermore,
D
contains a subscheme
\zeta
of length less or equal to
k
such that

\displaystyle H^{0}(L)\to H^{0}(L|_{\zeta})\quad\text{is not surjective.}

Over arbitrary smooth projective surfaces, a similar result ensures the
(k-1)
-very ampleness of the adjoint bundles
K_{X}+L
.

To our knowledge, an analogous criterion in higher rank is missing. We point out two constructions yielding
(k-1)
-very ample bundles over
K3
surfaces:

(i)

If
(X,H)
satisfies
\text{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\langle H\rangle
and
F
is a
\mu_{H}
-stable vector bundle with
\det F=H
and
\chi\geq(r+1)k+\delta
then
F
is
(k-1)
-very ample. This assertion follows by the proof of
[
27
, Proposition 2.2]
.

(ii)

By
[
7
, Proposition 4.5]
, over any surface, twisting a globally generated vector bundle by a
(k-1)
-very ample line bundle yields a
(k-1)
-very ample bundle (with large determinant).

For abelian surfaces, other constructions are possible via isogenies or extensions, see Section
3.1
.

2.1
Big and nef tautological bundles over the Hilbert scheme of
\boldsymbol{K3}
s

Theorem
2.1
applies to the three examples considered in Theorems 5.3, 5.5 and 5.7 and Corollaries 5.4, 5.6 and 5.8 of
[
7
]
, and mentioned in items
(a)
–
(c)
of the Introduction: line bundles and twists of Lazarsfeld–Mukai or Ulrich bundles. The goal here is to show how to extend the results in
[
7
]
from
k\leq 3
to any number of points.

Throughout this section, we assume
(X,H)
is a
K3
surface of Picard rank
1
, and
\text{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\langle H\rangle
. Let
H^{2}=2g-2
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
L_{n}=H^{\otimes n}
for
n\geq 1
. Assume
g\geq 3k-1
. Then
(L_{n})^{[k]}
is big and nef over
X^{[k]}
.

Proof.

Global generation, and thus nefness, is explained in
[
7
, Theorem 5.3]
. To prove bigness, as also noted in
[
7
]
, it suffices to establish the positivity of the Segre integral

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big((L_{n})^{[k]}\big)>0.

By formula (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big((L_{n})^{[k]}\big)=2^{k}\binom{\chi(L_{n})-2k}{k},

which is positive provided

\displaystyle\chi(L_{n})\geq 3k\iff 2+n^{2}(g-1)\geq 3k.

The latter inequality is clear under our hypothesis.
∎

We next consider Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundles and their induced tautological bundles over
X^{[k]}
. This geometric situation corresponds to Theorem 5.5 and Corollary 5.6 in
[
7
]
. There are several ways of formulating the result, but in keeping with
[
7
]
, we prefer bounds which do not depend on the rank.

Recall that the Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundles are obtained as duals
E=K_{C,L}^{\vee}
to kernels

\displaystyle 0\to K_{C,L}\to H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\iota_{\star}L\to 0.

Here
L\to C
is a line bundle over a nonsingular curve
C\in|H|
, of degree
d
and with
r\geq 2
sections, such that
L
and
\omega_{C}\otimes L^{\vee}
are globally generated. It follows that
E
is globally generated, with

\displaystyle\operatorname{rk}E=r\geq 2,\qquad c_{1}(E)=H,\qquad c_{2}(E)=d\implies v(E)=(r,H,g-1-d+r).

We let
\rho=g-r(r-1+g-d)
denote the Brill–Noether number.

Example 2.5
.

Let
E
be a globally generated Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundle as above. Assume that

\displaystyle\rho\geq 0,\qquad g>2k-2>0,\qquad g>\frac{2}{5}(d+1).

Then
(E\otimes H)^{[k]}
is big and nef.

Proof.

Let
F=E\otimes H
. The assumption
g>2k-2
was used in
[
7
, Theorem 5.5]
to prove that
F=E\otimes H
is
(k-1)
-very ample; this is based on the result cited in Remark
2.3
(ii)
. As noted in
[
7
]
, bigness follows once we verify that
\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)>0
. To this end, we check that the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
hold true. A simple calculation yields

\displaystyle\chi(F)=g(r+3)-d+r-3,\qquad\delta(F)=1+\frac{1}{2}\langle v(F),v(F)\rangle=\rho.

The inequality
\chi(F)\geq(r+2)k
is satisfied.
Indeed, by hypothesis
g\geq 2k-1
and
g\geq\frac{2d+3}{5}
, hence averaging we have
g\geq k+\frac{d-1}{5}
. Then

\displaystyle\chi(F)-(r+2)k

\displaystyle=g(r+3)-d+r-3-(r+2)k

\displaystyle\geq\biggl(k+\frac{d-1}{5}\biggr)(r+3)-d+r-3-(r+2)k

\displaystyle=\frac{d+4}{5}(r-2)+(k-2)\geq 0,

since
r,k\geq 2
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

The (untwisted) Lazarsfeld–Mukai bundles also yield big and nef vector bundles over
X^{[k]}
, under more restrictive assumptions. Take
r=2
for simplicity. Assume

\displaystyle 2d-2\geq g>2k-3+\frac{3}{2}d,\qquad\text{which implies}\quad\chi(E)\geq 4k+\rho,\quad\rho\geq 0.

This is a bit stronger than what is needed, but it ensures
\chi\geq 4k
and
\chi\geq 3k+\rho
simultaneously. It is well known that
E
is
\mu_{H}
-stable when
\text{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}\langle H\rangle
. (Reason: any destabilizing quotient has rank
1
, slope
\leq 0
, and is globally generated since
E
is. Hence the quotient is trivial, and thus
c_{2}(E)=d=0
, a contradiction.) Since
c_{1}(E)=H
, it follows
E
is
(k-1)
-very ample by Remark
2.3
(i)
. By Theorem
2.1
, we have that
E^{[k]}
is big and nef.

Finally, we turn to Ulrich bundles considered in Theorem 5.7 and Corollary 5.8 in
[
7
]
. We write
H^{2}=2h
, so that
g=h+1
. Recall that a bundle
E
over
(X,H)
is said to be Ulrich if

\displaystyle H^{\star}(X,E(-H))=H^{\star}(X,E(-2H))=0.

Such bundles always exist for
K3
surfaces of Picard rank
1
by
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
, and they have numerics

\displaystyle\operatorname{rk}E=2a,\qquad c_{1}(E)=3aH,\qquad c_{2}(E)=9a^{2}h-4a(h-1).

Example 2.7
.

Assume
h>2k-3>0
. Consider an Ulrich bundle
E
as above. Then
(E\otimes H)^{[k]}
is big and nef on the Hilbert scheme
X^{[k]}
.

Proof.

Letting
F=E\otimes H
, we compute

\displaystyle\chi(F)=12ah,\qquad\delta(F)=1+a^{2}h+4a^{2}.

As noted in
[
7
, Theorem 5.6]
, the bundle
F=E\otimes H
is
(k-1)
-very ample if
h>2k-3
; this uses the statement cited in Remark 3(ii). To prove bigness, it remains to verify the positivity of the top Segre integral. By Theorem
2.1
, we check that

\displaystyle\chi(F)\geq(r+2)k\iff 12ah\geq(2a+2)k.

This is clear since
h>2k-3>0
.
∎

Example 2.8
.

We can extend the result to Ulrich bundles
E
over the polarized
K3
surface
(X,mH)
for all
m\geq 1
. By
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
, the Mukai vectors of such Ulrich bundles are of the form

\displaystyle v=\biggl(r,\frac{3rm}{2}H,h\big(2m^{2}r\big)-r\biggr).

By the same arguments,
(E\otimes H)^{[k]}
is big and nef for
h>2k-3>0
.

Other
\boldsymbol{K}
-trivial surfaces

3.1
Abelian and bielliptic surfaces

The formulas in
[
27
]
can be used to treat the case of abelian or bielliptic surfaces. For vector bundles
F\to X
, we set

\displaystyle r=\operatorname{rank}F,\qquad\chi=\chi(F),\qquad v=\operatorname{ch}(F),\qquad\delta=\frac{1}{2}\langle v,v\rangle.

We show

Theorem 3.1
.

For a
(k-1)
-very ample vector bundle
F\to X
,
the tautological bundle
F^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
is big and nef provided that

\chi\geq(r+2)k
and
\delta\geq 0
.

Proof.

We follow the same steps as for
K3
surfaces, but a few numerical changes are necessary. First, it was noted on
[
27
, p. 19]
that the analogue of equation (
2.3
) for abelian or bielliptic surfaces takes the form

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)=\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{k}}\bigl[(1+(2+r)t)^{\delta}(1+(1+r)t)^{\chi-\delta-(r+1)k-1}(1+(1+r)(2+r)t)\bigr].

Next, the change of variables

\displaystyle t=\frac{u}{1-(1+r)u}

turns the above expression into

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)=\operatorname{Coeff}_{u^{k}}\bigl[(1+u)^{\delta}(1-(1+r)u)^{-\chi+(r+2)k-1}\big(1+(1+r)^{2}u\big)\bigr].

The proof is completed by the same argument as in Theorem
2.1
, this time letting
a_{i}
be the coefficients of
(1+u)^{\delta}\big(1+(1+r)^{2}u\big)
and letting
b_{j}
be the coefficients of
(1-(1+r)u)^{-\chi+(r+2)k-1}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

If
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample line bundle over an abelian or bielliptic surface and
L^{2}\geq 6k
, then
L^{[k]}
is big and nef.

In the following examples, we assume
X
is an abelian surface with Picard rank
1
, with Néron–Severi ample generator
H
.

Example 3.3
.

Assume
H^{2}\geq 6k
. For
H^{2}>4k
, the line bundle
L_{n}=H^{\otimes n}
is
(k-1)
-very ample for all
n\geq 1
. This is an immediate consequence of
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
. Indeed, using that the Picard rank is
1
, the inequality (
2.5
) is impossible. For
H^{2}\geq 6k
, we also have
\chi(L_{n})\geq 3k
. It follows from Corollary
3.2
that
(L_{n})^{[k]}
is big and nef.

In higher rank, just as for
K3
s, the reader can consider twists of Ulrich and Lazarfeld–Mukai bundles. Here, taking advantage of the abelian surface geometry, we discuss simple semihomogeneous bundles, and twists of unipotent and homogeneous bundles.

Example 3.4
.

Assume that
H
is a principal polarization. Let
(a,b)=1
be coprime positive integers with

\displaystyle b>a^{2}k.

By
[
29
, Remark 7.13]
, there exist simple semihomogeneous vector bundles
W\to X
with

\displaystyle\operatorname{rk}W=a^{2},\qquad\mu(W)=\frac{bH}{a}\in NS(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q},\qquad\chi(W)=b^{2}.

We claim
W^{[k]}
is big and nef.

We note first that
W
is
(k-1)
-very ample. Indeed, it is shown in
[
29
, Theorem 5.8]
that

\displaystyle W=f_{\star}L

for some line bundle
L\to Y
, where
f\colon Y\to X
is an isogeny of degree
a^{2}
. It was remarked in
[
32
, Section 2.4]
that
W
has no higher cohomology for
b>0
. Consequently, the same is true about
L
. Since

\displaystyle h^{0}(L)=h^{0}(W)=\chi(W)=b^{2},

we conclude that
L
is effective and
L^{2}=2b^{2}
.

We claim
L
is
\big(a^{2}k-1\big)
-very ample for
b>a^{2}k
. This follows again by
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
. Indeed, the Picard rank is invariant under isogenies
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
, hence
Y
has Picard rank
1
since
X
does. If
M
is the ample Néron–Severi generator, write
L\equiv_{\text{num}}M^{\ell}
, for
\ell>0
. We have

\displaystyle 2b^{2}=L^{2}=\ell^{2}M^{2}\geq 2\ell^{2}\implies 0<\ell\leq b.

For any effective divisor
D\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle L.D=\ell M.D\geq\ell M.M=\frac{L^{2}}{\ell}=\frac{2b^{2}}{\ell}\geq 2b\geq 2a^{2}k.

This violates (
2.5
). Since
L
is nef and
L^{2}=2b^{2}>4a^{2}k
, it follows that
L
is
\big(a^{2}k-1\big)
-very ample.

To check
(k-1)
-very ampleness for
W
, note that if
\zeta
is a subscheme of
X
of length
k
, then

\displaystyle H^{0}(W)\to H^{0}(W\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\zeta})\text{ surjective }\iff H^{0}(L)\to H^{0}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{f^{\star}\zeta})\text{ surjective}.

The latter is true since
L
is
\big(a^{2}k-1\big)
-very ample and
f^{\star}\zeta
has length
a^{2}k
.

Finally, the inequality

\displaystyle\chi(W)\geq(r+2)k\iff b^{2}\geq\big(a^{2}+2\big)k

is certainly true when
b>a^{2}k
.

Example 3.5
.

Assume
H^{2}>4k
. Let
E
be a unipotent bundle of rank
r\geq 2
, that is,
E
admits a filtration whose successive quotients are the trivial line bundle
[
29
, Definition 4.5]
. Let
F=E\otimes H
. If
H^{2}>4k
then
H
is
(k-1)
-very ample by
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, see (
2.5
). It follows that
F
is also
(k-1)
-very ample. Indeed,
F
is obtained as an iterated extension of
H
. At each step, we inductively show that the extension is
(k-1)
-very ample without higher cohomology, by examining the relevant short exact sequences. The filtration of
F
also gives

\displaystyle\chi(F)=r\chi(H)>2rk\geq(r+2)k.

Thus
(E\otimes H)^{[k]}
is big and nef.

Let
E
be homogeneous bundle of rank
r\geq 2
, that is,
E
is invariant by translations on
X
. By
[
29
, Theorem 4.17]
, we can write

\displaystyle E=\bigoplus_{i}U_{i}\otimes P_{i},

with
U_{i}
unipotent, and
P_{i}
a line bundle of degree
0
. Repeating the argument above for each summand, we show first that
E\otimes H
is
(k-1)
-very ample, and then
(E\otimes H)^{[k]}
is big and nef.

For bielliptic surfaces, we leave specific examples to the reader, mentioning only that
(k-1)
-very ampleness of line bundles is studied in
[
28
]
.

3.2
Enriques surfaces

By contrast, the case of Enriques surfaces requires more care, due to the shape of the algebraic functions giving the Segre integrals. As before, we write
v(F)=\operatorname{ch}(F)\sqrt{\operatorname{Td}(X)}
for the Mukai vector, and set

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle v,v\rangle\implies\delta=rc_{2}-\frac{r-1}{2}c_{1}^{2}-\frac{r^{2}-1}{2}.

Note that
\delta
is an integer if and only if the rank
r
is odd. In this case, we prove:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F
be a
(k-1)
-very ample bundle of odd rank
r
on an Enriques surface, with

\displaystyle\chi\geq 2k(r+1),\qquad\delta\geq 0.

Then
F^{[k]}
is big and nef.

Computer experiments show that the bound
\chi\geq(r+2)k
imposed for the other
K
-trivial surfaces is insufficient here. Nonetheless, we have the following

Conjecture 3.7
.

For all ranks, odd or even, Theorem
3.6
holds under the weaker assumption

\displaystyle\chi\geq\biggl(\frac{5r}{4}+2\biggr)k,\quad\delta\geq 0.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Since
F
is
(k-1)
-very ample,
F^{[k]}
is globally generated, hence nef. To establish bigness, it remains to show that the degree of the top Segre class
s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)>0
.

By
[
27
, Theorem 1]
, the Enriques analogue of (
2.1
) takes the form

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\int_{X^{[k]}}s\big(F^{[k]}\big)=A_{0}(z)^{c_{2}(F)}A_{1}(z)^{c_{1}(F)^{2}}A_{2}(z)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.1)

for exactly the same universal functions
A_{0}
,
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
which appear for
K3
surfaces. Using (
3.1
) and following the reasoning in
[
27
, p. 11]
, with the modified numerics, we obtain an expression for the top Segre integral

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{k}}\big[(1+(1+r)t)^{\chi-\delta-k(r+1)-\frac{1}{2}}(1+(2+r)t)^{\delta}(1+(1+r)(2+r)t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big].

(3.2)

This is the Enriques analogue of equation (
2.3
), but the half integer exponents complicate our analysis.

To determine the sign of the Segre integral, we carry out the usual change of variables

\displaystyle t=\frac{u}{1-(1+r)u}.

Then we rewrite (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s_{2k}\big(F^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}(1\!+(1\!+r)t)^{\chi-\delta-k(r+1)-\frac{1}{2}}(1\!+(2\!+r)t)^{\delta}(1\!+(1\!+r)(2\!+r)t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{u=0}(1-(1+r)u)^{-\chi+k(r+2)-1}(1+u)^{\delta}\big(1+(1+r)^{2}u\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Coeff}_{u^{k}}(1-(1+r)u)^{-\alpha}(1+u)^{\delta}\big(1+(1+r)^{2}u\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\alpha=\chi-k(r+2)+1\geq kr+1
.

We note that when
r
is odd,
\delta\geq 0
is an integer. Thus, the middle term
(1+u)^{\delta}
has nonnegative coefficients and constant term equal to
1
. We claim that

\displaystyle(1-(1+r)u)^{-\alpha}\big(1+(1+r)^{2}u\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

has positive coefficients up to order
k
. The same will therefore be true after multiplying by
(1+u)^{\delta}
, showing that the top Segre class is positive.

A different idea is needed to establish the above claim. We change variables
u\mapsto u/(1+r)
and consider instead the series

\displaystyle W=(1-u)^{-\alpha}(1+(1+r)u)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For each
0\leq m\leq k
, the coefficient of
u^{m}
in
W
equals

\sum_{i+j=m}\binom{-\alpha}{i}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j}(-1)^{i}(1+r)^{j}=\sum_{i+j=m}\binom{\alpha+i-1}{i}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j}(1+r)^{j}.

(3.3)

The term corresponding to
i=m
,
j=0
is clearly positive since
\alpha\geq 1
. We will ignore this term for the analysis. The next term
i=m-1
,
j=1
is positive as well. The remaining terms however have alternating signs because of the fractional binomials. We show nonetheless that the sum of the consecutive
(i,j)
and
(i-1,j+1)
terms is positive, for
j
odd:

\binom{\alpha+i-1}{i}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j}(1+r)^{j}+\binom{\alpha+i-2}{i-1}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j+1}(1+r)^{j+1}>0.

(3.4)

This proves that the alternating sum (
3.3
) is positive as well. To justify (
3.4
), we note that

\displaystyle\binom{\alpha+i-1}{i}=\binom{\alpha+i-2}{i-1}\frac{\alpha+i-1}{i}>0,\qquad\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j+1}=\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j}\frac{\frac{1}{2}-j}{j+1}.

For
j
odd, we have
\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{j}>0
. After cancellation, the inequality to establish becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+i-1}{i}+(1+r)\frac{\frac{1}{2}-j}{j+1}>0.

Writing
i=m-j
, and using
\alpha-1=\chi-(r+2)k\geq rk\geq rm
, it suffices to establish

\displaystyle\frac{rm+m-j}{m-j}+(1+r)\frac{\frac{1}{2}-j}{j+1}>0\iff j^{2}r+\frac{r+3}{2}(m-j)+mr>0,

which is clearly true.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

If
L
is a
(k-1)
-very ample line bundle over an Enriques surface
X
,
k\geq 2
, then
L^{[k]}
is big and nef.

In particular, if
H
is an ample line bundle over an Enriques surface, and
L_{n}=H^{\otimes n}
then
(L_{n})^{[k]}
is big and nef for all
n\geq k+1
.

Proof.

The second half of the corollary follows from the first. Indeed, it is noted in
[
37
, Proposition
2.5
]
that if
H
is ample then
L_{n}=H^{\otimes n}
is
(k-1)
-very ample for all
n\geq k+1
.

We prove the first statement. If
\chi:=\chi(L)\geq 4k
, the assertion follows from Theorem
3.6
with
r=1
. Now, over Enriques surfaces,
(k-1)
-very ampleness imposes numerical restrictions on
L
which are stronger than for the other
K
-trivial surfaces. Indeed, it was noted in
[
37
, Theorem 2.4]
that if
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample, then
L^{2}\geq(k+1)^{2}
.
When
k\geq 6
, this is sufficient to guarantee that

\displaystyle\chi(L)=1+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\geq 1+\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{2}>4k.

When
2\leq k\leq 5
, the bound
\chi\geq 4k
required by Theorem
3.6
may fail. However, the finitely many cases

\displaystyle 4k>\chi\geq 1+\frac{(1+k)^{2}}{2},\qquad 2\leq k\leq 5,

can be checked by hand using equation (
3.2
).
∎

4
Other geometries

The Segre integrals for arbitrary surfaces are established only when
F
has rank
1
or
2
, see
[
26
,
27
]
. In rank
1
, the answers were conjectured by Lehn
[
22
]
. The formulation below can be found in
[
27
]
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\int_{X^{[k]}}s\big(L^{[k]}\big)=A_{1}(z)^{L^{2}}A_{2}(z)^{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})}A_{3}(z)^{L.K_{X}}A_{4}(z)^{K_{X}^{2}}.

(4.1)

Here, for
z=t(1+2t)^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)=(1+2t)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle A_{2}(z)=(1+2t)^{\frac{3}{2}}(1+6t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle A_{3}(z)=\frac{1}{2}(1+2t)^{-1}\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right),

\displaystyle A_{4}(z)=4(1+2t)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{-2}.

4.1
A positivity result

It is more difficult to determine the sign of the top Segre integral from the above formulas. The following lemma plays an important role in the analysis. We will apply it in the next section to geometric situations.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all integers
m
,
n
,
p
with
m\geq 0
,
p\geq 0
such that
m+n+p
is even, the series

\displaystyle f(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}{(1+2t)}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

has positive coefficients up to order less or equal than
\min\big(\frac{1}{2}(m+n+p)-1,m-1\big)
.

Taking the minimum is necessary. Indeed, for
(m,n,p)=(2,19,1)
the term of order
\frac{1}{2}(m+n+p)-1
has negative coefficient, while for
(m,n,p)=(4,0,0)
, the term of order
m-1
has zero coefficient. The lemma also holds for
m+n+p
odd, but this case
never
occurs geometrically. The hypothesis
m,p\geq 0
can fail in geometric examples, but our proof requires it.

Proof.

The argument is not straightforward due to the alternating signs
in the expansions

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+2t}=1+t-\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\frac{t^{3}}{2}-\frac{5t^{4}}{8}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+6t}=1+3t-\frac{9t^{2}}{2}+\frac{27t^{3}}{2}-\frac{405t^{4}}{8}+\cdots.

However, a residue calculation and suitable changes of variables will render the answer manifestly positive.

We begin by considering the case
n\geq 0
. In this situation, assume first
m
,
n
,
p
are even, and set

\displaystyle F(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}{(1+2t)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+6t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Claim 4.2
.

The series
F
has positive coefficients up to order less or equal than
\frac{m}{2}-1
, and no terms of order between
\frac{m}{2}
and
m-1
.

We assume this for now. Note that

\displaystyle f(t)=F(t)P(t),\quad P(t)=(1+2t)^{\frac{n}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Clearly,
P
is a polynomial of degree
\frac{n+p}{2}
with positive coefficients. This observation together with Claim
4.2
gives the argument. Indeed, let
k\leq\min\big(\frac{1}{2}(m+n+p)-1,m-1\big)
. Writing
F_{i}
and
P_{j}
for the coefficients of
F
and
P
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{k}}f(t)=\sum F_{i}P_{j},\qquad\text{the sum ranging over}\quad i+j=k,\quad i\geq 0,\quad 0\leq j\leq\frac{n+p}{2}.

The sum is nonnegative since
i\leq k\leq m-1
, so
F_{i}\geq 0
by the Claim
4.2
, and
P_{j}>0
. The sum is in fact strictly positive. Indeed, for
k\geq\frac{n+p}{2}
, it contains the term

\displaystyle F_{k-\frac{n+p}{2}}P_{\frac{n+p}{2}}>0.

The last statement is also a consequence of Claim
4.2
, using that
k-\frac{n+p}{2}\leq\frac{m}{2}-1
. When
k<\frac{n+p}{2}
, the sum contains the term
F_{0}P_{k}=2^{m}P_{k}>0
.

Proof of Claim
4.2
.

We seek to show that for all
k\leq\frac{m}{2}-1
, the residue

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}\frac{F(t)}{t^{k+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}t}>0.

The peculiar change of variables

\displaystyle t=\frac{s(s+1)}{2\sqrt{3}s+(2+\sqrt{3})}

will simplify the calculation.
For convenience, set

\displaystyle a=\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{3}}>0\qquad\text{so that}\quad t=\frac{s(s+1)}{2\sqrt{3}(s+a+1)}.

We note the following identities

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+2t}=\frac{s+\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}}{3^{\frac{1}{4}}(s+a+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad\sqrt{1+6t}=\frac{3^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(s+\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2\sqrt{3}}\big)}{(s+a+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}=\frac{(\sqrt{3}+1)(s+1)}{3^{\frac{1}{4}}(s+a+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\big(s+\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}\big)\big(s+\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2\sqrt{3}}\big)}{2\sqrt{3}(s+a+1)^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}s}.

From here, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}\frac{F(t)}{t^{k+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}t}=\operatorname{Res}_{s=0}\frac{G(s)}{s^{k+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}s},

where

\displaystyle G(s)=\alpha(s+1)^{m-k-1}(s+a+1)^{k-\frac{m}{2}},\qquad\alpha=2^{k}\big(\sqrt{3}+1\big)^{m}\sqrt{3}^{k-\frac{m}{2}}>0.

A second change of variables will be needed next (this change of variables could have been carried out simultaneously with the first, but this is a price worth paying for readability). We set

\displaystyle s=\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{a}\frac{u}{1-\frac{a+1}{a}u}.

The reader can verify that

\displaystyle s+1=\frac{1+(a+1)u}{1-\frac{a+1}{a}u},\qquad s+a+1=\frac{a+1}{1-\frac{a+1}{a}u},\qquad{\mathrm{d}s}=\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{a}\frac{1}{\big(1-\frac{a+1}{a}u\big)^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}u}.

By direct calculation, we find

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{s=0}\frac{G(s)}{s^{k+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}s}=\operatorname{Res}_{u=0}\frac{H(u)}{u^{k+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}u}

for

\displaystyle H(u)=\beta(1+(a+1)u)^{m-k-1}\biggl(1-\frac{a+1}{a}u\biggr)^{k-\frac{m}{2}},\qquad\beta=\alpha a^{k}(a+1)^{-k-\frac{m}{2}}>0.

The first term is a polynomial with positive coefficients
a_{i}
given by binomial numbers, for
0\leq i\leq m-k-1
. The second term also has positive coefficients

\displaystyle b_{j}=\biggl(-\frac{a+1}{a}\biggr)^{j}\binom{k-\frac{m}{2}}{j}>0

since
k-\frac{m}{2}<0
. Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Coeff}_{u^{k}}H(u)=\!\sum a_{i}b_{j},\quad\text{the sum ranging over}\!\quad i+j=k,\quad 0\leq i\leq m-k-1,\quad j\geq 0.

This coefficient is positive since
a_{i}>0
,
b_{j}>0
and the sum is non-empty (the term
a_{0}b_{k}=b_{k}>0
appears in the sum).

When
m
is even, and
\frac{m}{2}\leq k\leq m-1
, the expression
H(u)
is a polynomial in
u
of degree
\frac{m}{2}-1<k
. Hence, the coefficient of
u^{k}
in
H(u)
vanishes.
This proves the claim (and completes the argument when
m
,
n
,
p
are even and
n\geq 0
).
∎

When
n\geq 0
, there are three other cases to consider, which require different choices for
F
and
P
:

(i)

when
m
even,
n
,
p
odd, we set

\displaystyle F(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m},\qquad P(t)=(1+2t)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(ii)

when
m
odd,
n
even,
p
odd, we set

\displaystyle F(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}{(1+2t)}^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad P(t)=(1+2t)^{\frac{n}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(iii)

when
m
odd,
n
odd,
p
even, we set

\displaystyle F(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}(1+6t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad P(t)=(1+2t)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

In case
(i)
,
F
has positive coefficients up to order
\frac{m}{2}
, and no terms of order between
\frac{m}{2}+1
and
m-1
.
In cases
(ii)
and
(iii)
,
F
has positive coefficients up to order
\frac{m-1}{2}
, and no terms of order between
\frac{m+1}{2}
and
m-1
. The arguments are similar, and we leave the details to the reader.

When
n<0
, the reasoning used to prove Claim
4.2
also works to deal directly with the function

\displaystyle f(t)=\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}{(1+2t)}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

Following exactly the same steps, first changing from
t
to
s
and then from
s
to
u
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(u)={}

\displaystyle\gamma(1+(a+1)u)^{m-k-1}\biggl(1-\frac{a+1}{a}u\biggr)^{k-\frac{m+n+p}{2}}\biggl(1-\frac{(\sqrt{3}+1)^{3}}{4\sqrt{3}}u\biggr)^{n}

\displaystyle\times\biggl(1+\frac{\big(\sqrt{3}+1\big)^{3}}{4}u\biggr)^{p},

for
\gamma>0
. By the same arguments, this has positive coefficients when the exponents

\displaystyle m-k-1\geq 0,\qquad k-\frac{m+n+p}{2}<0,\qquad n<0,\qquad p\geq 0,

which we assumed.
∎

4.2
\boldsymbol{K3}
blowups

The top Segre classes computed by formula (
4.1
) are always coefficients in series of the form
f(t)
as in Lemma
4.1
, barring the condition
m,p\geq 0
. When this condition is satisfied, we easily obtain big and nef criteria for the tautological bundles
L^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
.

There are several specific examples where our techniques apply. We illustrate them first when

\displaystyle\pi\colon\ X\to S

is the blowup of a
K3
surface
S
at a point
p\in S
. We assume

S
has Picard rank
1
, with ample Picard generator
H
. Let
H^{2}=2h
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
E
be the exceptional divisor on
X
, and set
L=H-\ell E
. Assume
\ell\geq k-1
and

\displaystyle 2h>\max\bigl((\ell+2)^{2}-6,(\ell+1)^{2}+4k,\ell(\ell+1)+6k-6\bigr).

Then
L^{[k]}
is big and nef on
X^{[k]}
.

Proof.

We first show
H-(\ell+1)E
nef. Recall the Sheshadri constant

\displaystyle\epsilon(S,p)=\max\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\colon H-tE\text{ is nef}\},

see for instance
[
20
, Definition 5.1.1]
. Note that since
H
is in the nef cone of
X
, if
H-tE
is nef, then
H-t^{\prime}E
is also nef for all
0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
. Thus it suffices to explain that

\displaystyle\epsilon(S,p)\geq\ell+1.

The Sheshadri constants of
K3
surfaces of Picard rank
1
have been studied in
[
18
]
and shown to satisfy

\displaystyle\epsilon(S,p)\geq\big\lfloor{\sqrt{H^{2}}}\big\rfloor,

with two possible exceptions

\displaystyle H^{2}=\alpha^{2}+\alpha-2,\qquad\epsilon(S,p)\geq\alpha-\frac{2}{\alpha+1}

and

\displaystyle H^{2}=\alpha^{2}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2},\qquad\epsilon(S,p)\geq\alpha-\frac{1}{2\alpha+1},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Since the inequality
2h>\ell(\ell+3)
is implied by our hypothesis, it follows that
\epsilon(S,p)\geq\ell+1
in all cases (using
\alpha\geq\ell+2
in the two exceptional cases).

We next show that
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample. Note that
K_{X}=E
, and write

\displaystyle L=K_{X}+M,\qquad M=H-(\ell+1)E.

Observe that
M
is nef by the first paragraph of the proof, and

\displaystyle M^{2}=2h-(\ell+1)^{2}>4k.

By
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
, if
L
is not
(k-1)
-very ample, there exists an effective divisor
D\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle D.M-k\leq D^{2}<\frac{D.M}{2}<k.

Furthermore,
M-2D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective and
D
contains a subscheme
\zeta
of length at most
equal to
k
such that

\displaystyle H^{0}(L)\to H^{0}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\zeta})

is not surjective. Write

\displaystyle D=aH+bE,

and note that
D
effective implies
a\geq 0
. Similarly,

\displaystyle M-2D=(1-2a)H+(-\ell-1-2b)E

is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective, so
1-2a\geq 0
. Thus
a=0
,
D=bE
with
b>0
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{D.M}{2}<k\implies b(\ell+1)<2k\implies b<\frac{2k}{\ell+1}\leq 2

since
k\leq\ell+1
. Hence
b=1
and
D=E
. For subschemes
\zeta
of
E
, the map

\displaystyle H^{0}(L)\to H^{0}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\zeta})

can be written as composition

\displaystyle H^{0}(L)\to H^{0}(L|_{E}),\qquad H^{0}(L|_{E})\to H^{0}(L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\zeta}).

Since
L|_{E}=\mathcal{O}_{E}(\ell)
and
\zeta
has length less or equal to
k\leq\ell+1
, the second map is clearly surjective. The first map is also surjective since

\displaystyle H^{1}(L(-E))=0.

This is a consequence of
[
38
, Proposition 4.1]
and requires the bound
2h>(\ell+2)^{2}-6
, which we assumed. We conclude
L^{[k]}
is globally generated and thus nef.

It remains to explain that the top Segre class of
L^{[k]}
is positive. We use (
4.1
) and we change variables

\displaystyle z=t(1+2t)^{2}\qquad\text{so that}\quad\mathrm{d}z=(1+2t)(1+6t)\,{\mathrm{d}t}.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{X^{[k]}}s\big(L^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}A_{1}^{2h-\ell^{2}}A_{2}^{2}A_{3}^{\ell}A_{4}^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}2^{-\ell-2}(1+2t)^{h-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}-2k-\ell+\frac{3}{2}}(1+6t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{\ell+2}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\ell-2}\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{k}}(1\!+2t)^{h-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}-2k-\ell+\frac{3}{2}}(1\!+6t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+2t}\!+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{\ell+2}\!.

(4.2)

We are now in the situation considered in Lemma
4.1
. Thus, the coefficient above is positive provided
k\leq\ell+1
and

\displaystyle k\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigg((\ell+2)+2\bigg(h-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}-2k-\ell+2\bigg)\bigg)-1\iff 2h>\ell(\ell+1)+6k-6.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Surfaces of general type

It is natural to wonder how far these techniques take us. We show

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
X
is a smooth projective minimal surface of general type. Let
L
be a
(k-1)
-very ample line bundle such that

\displaystyle\chi(L)\geq 3k,\qquad L.K_{X}\geq 2K_{X}^{2}+k+1.

Then
L^{[k]}
is big and nef over
X^{[k]}
.

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, in particular equation (
4.2
), we express the Segre integral as the
t^{k}
-coefficient in the series

\displaystyle f(t)=2^{-m}\left(\sqrt{1+2t}+\sqrt{1+6t}\right)^{m}{(1+2t)}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+6t)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

where

\displaystyle m=L.K-2K^{2},\qquad n=(L-K)^{2}+3\chi-4k-1,\qquad p=K^{2}-\chi+3,

with
K=K_{X}
and
\chi=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Note that

\displaystyle m+n+p=2\chi(L)-4k+2

is even. Furthermore,
p\geq 0
. Indeed, let
p_{g}
,
q
denote the genus and irregularity of
X
. By Noether’s inequality
K^{2}\geq 2p_{g}-4
, and
K^{2}>0
since
X
is minimal of general type. Averaging, we obtain
K^{2}>p_{g}-2
, and thus

\displaystyle p=K^{2}-\chi+3>(p_{g}-2)-(1-q+p_{g})+3=q\geq 0.

By Lemma
4.1
, the coefficients of

f(t)
up to order
\min\big(\frac{1}{2}(m+n+p)-1,m-1\big)
are positive. In particular, if

\displaystyle k\leq\min\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(m+n+p)-1,m-1\bigg),

then the Segre integral is positive. The latter inequality is exactly our hypothesis, and thus
L^{[k]}
is big and nef.
∎

5
Curves

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
, and let
V\to C
be a vector bundle with
\chi=\chi(V)
. It is natural to ask whether the previous results also apply to the tautological bundles
V^{[k]}\to C^{[k]}
. In fact, by similar considerations, we establish an analogue of Theorem
2.1
:

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume
V\to C
is a
(k-1)
-very ample rank
r
vector bundle with
\chi\geq(r+1)k
.
Then
V^{[k]}\to C^{[k]}
is big and nef over
C^{[k]}
.

To illustrate, if
V
is stable of degree
d>r(2g-2+k)
, then
V
is
(k-1)
-very ample. This holds because for any divisor
Z\subset C
of degree
k
, we have

\displaystyle H^{1}(V(-Z))=H^{0}\big(V^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}(Z)\big)=0

by Serre duality, stability and the assumption
\mu(V)>2g-2+k
.

Proof.

Since
V
is
(k-1)
-very ample, it follows that
V^{[k]}
is globally generated, hence nef. To prove
V^{[k]}
is big, it suffices to verify

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\int_{C^{[k]}}s\big(V^{[k]}\big)>0.

The latter integrals are computed in
[
26
, Theorem 2]
; they can be viewed as higher rank analogues of the classical
k
-secant integrals. The answer bears analogies with equation (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}\int_{C^{[k]}}s_{k}\big(V^{[k]}\big)=A_{1}^{d}A_{2}^{1-g},

where

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)=1+t,\qquad A_{2}(z)=\frac{(1+t)^{r+1}}{1+(1+r)t},\qquad z=-t(1+t)^{r}.

We can express the Segre integrals as residues

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\int_{C^{[k]}}s_{k}\big(V^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}A_{1}^{d}A_{2}^{1-g}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{t=0}(1+t)^{\chi-kr-g}(1+(1+r)t)^{g}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{k+1}}.

Just as in Theorem
2.1
, we further change variables
t=\frac{u}{1-u}
, so that

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\int_{C^{[k]}}s_{k}\big(V^{[k]}\big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Res}_{u=0}(1+ru)^{g}(1-u)^{-\chi+k(r+1)-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Coeff}_{u^{k}}(1+ru)^{g}(1-u)^{-\chi+k(r+1)-1}.

By the same reasoning as in Theorem
2.1
, we see that this coefficient is positive when
-\chi+k(r+1)-1<0\iff\chi\geq k(r+1)
. This completes the argument.
∎

To go further, we consider the punctual Quot schemes
\mathsf{Quot}_{C}\big(\mathbb{C}^{N},k\big)
parametrizing quotients

\displaystyle 0\to S\to\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\to Q\to 0,\qquad\operatorname{rk}Q=0,\qquad\text{length }Q=k.

These are smooth projective varieties of dimension
Nk
, and carry tautological vector bundles

for each vector bundle

:

\displaystyle V^{[k]}=p_{\star}(\mathcal{Q}\otimes q^{\star}V).

Here

is the universal quotient and

,

are the two projections over
\mathsf{Quot}_{C}\big(\mathbb{C}^{N},k\big)\times C

The associated Segre integrals were studied in

. We extend the above results to the punctual Quot scheme, in rank
1
. The higher rank case appears more involved.

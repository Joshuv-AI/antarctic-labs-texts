Congruences of the q q -Fibonacci sequence related with its transcendence
Takumi Anzawa and Hidetaka Funakura

Abstract
. By using Andrews’ explicit formulae of the q q -Fibonacci sequence introduced by Schur, we prove certain congruences of the q q -Fibonacci sequence which relate the sequence with the original Fibonacci sequence. As a corollary, we show that it yields a transcendental element in the ℚ \mathbb{Q} -algebra 𝒜 \mathscr{A} of integers modulo infinitely large primes under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.



1.
Introduction

In 1917, Schur (
[
11
]
) introduced the so-called the
q
-
Fibonacci sequence

\{F_{n}(q)\}
which is the sequence of
\mathbb{Q}[q]
defined by the initial value
(F_{0}(q),F_{1}(q))=(0,1)
and the recurrence relation

F_{n+2}(q)-F_{n+1}(q)-q^{n}F_{n}(q)=0

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It recovers the ordinary Fibonacci sequence
\{F_{n}\}
when
q=1
.
Andrews (
[
1
]
) gave an explicit formula (cf. Theorem
2.1
) of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence to prove some kind of the Rogers-Ramanujan identities.

Let
P
be the set of prime numbers and
let
v_{p}(\alpha)
denote the
p
-adic valuation of
\alpha
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
p\in P
and set
v_{p}(0)=0
.
For a pair
(\alpha,p)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\times P
with
v_{p}(\alpha)=0
,

\ord_{p}(\alpha)
denotes the order of
\alpha
in the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and
I_{p}(\alpha):=(p-1)/\ord_{p}(\alpha)
, i.e.
I_{p}(\alpha)
is the index of the subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
generated by
\alpha
.
So when
\alpha
is a primitive root, we have
\ord_{p}(\alpha)=p-1
and
I_{p}(\alpha)=1
.
The values
\ord_{p}(\alpha)
and
I_{p}(\alpha)
are called the
residual order
of
\alpha
and the
residual index
of
\alpha
respectively.
Our main theorem is on congruence which relates the
q
-Fibonacci sequence with the ordinary one:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
p\in P
satisfying
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
and
\ord_{p}(\alpha)\not\equiv 0\mod 5
,

F_{p}(\alpha)\equiv F_{I_{p}(\alpha)+\left(\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}\right)}\mod p

holds, where
\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)
is the Legendre symbol for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The following quotient
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

\mathscr{A}:=\left(\prod_{p\in P}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)\Bigm/\left(\bigoplus_{p\in P}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)

appeared in
[
4
]
, has been studied in several kinds of literature (
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
, etc) in relation with the study of finite multiple zeta values (FMZVs, in short) introduced by Kaneko and Zagier (
[
3
]
).
Rosen (
[
9
]
) introduced the notion of finite algebraic numbers in
\mathscr{A}
by using recurrent sequences.
It should be noted that solutions of
\mathbb{Q}
-polynomials in
\mathscr{A}
are not always finite algebraic numbers in
\mathscr{A}
(in Rosen’s sense).
In this paper, we consider
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental numbers
, elements in
\mathscr{A}
which are not roots of non-zero
\mathbb{Q}
-polynomials in
\mathscr{A}
(Definition
3.6
).
It is expected that non-zero FMZVs are
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental,
however, so far no single example has been obtained as far as authors know.
Our interests in this paper are to construct examples of
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental numbers in the algebra
\mathscr{A}

We show that the
(F_{p}(\alpha))_{p}\in\mathscr{A}
is an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number by combining Theorem
1.1
and Moree’s result (
[
6
]
) on the density of certain primes related to the residual index.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH, in short),
(F_{p}(g))_{p}\in\mathscr{A}
is an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number when
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
is square-free.

Congruences of
q
-Fibonacci sequence

In this section, we prove our main theorem (Theorem
1.1
).

2.1.
Review on
q
-analogues

We recall the following standard notation:

•

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the
q
-integer
[n]_{q}
is defined by

\displaystyle[n]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}.

•

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the
q
-factorial
[n]_{q}!
is defined by

\displaystyle[n]_{q}!:=[n]_{q}[n-1]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}.

•

For a pair of integers
n
,
m
, the
q
-binomial coefficient
\displaystyle\left[n\atop m\right]_{q}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\left[n\atop m\right]_{q}:=\begin{cases}\frac{[n]_{q}\cdots[n-m+1]_{q}}{[m]_{q}\cdots[1]_{q}}&\text{if }0\leq m\leq n\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Andrews gave a general explicit formula of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
1
, Theorem]
)
.

For any non-negative integer
n
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle F_{n+1}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{j(5j+1)/2}\left[n\atop\lfloor(n-5j)/2\rfloor\right]_{q}

holds,
where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer not exceeding
x
.

2.2.
Preliminaries

This subsection presents some lemmas of
q
-integers and
q
-binomial coefficients.
We show the prime congruence of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence by the lemmas.
Let
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
be the localization of
\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the prime ideal
(p)
generated by a prime number
p
.
Note that the isomorphism of field
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}/p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}

and
p\in P
with
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
.
Let
l
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be integers satisfying
1\leq l\leq p-1
and
k\equiv l\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. Then
\displaystyle\frac{[k]_{\alpha}}{[l]_{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
and

(2.2)

\frac{[k]_{\alpha}}{[l]_{\alpha}}\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{k}{l}&\mod p&\text{if }k\equiv 0\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)\\
1&\mod p&\text{if }k\not\equiv 0\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)\\
\end{array}\right.

holds.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
p
be a prime number satisfying
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
,
[n]_{\alpha}\equiv 0\mod p
holds if and only if
n\equiv 0\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. We omit the proof.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
i
be an integer satisfying
0\leq i\leq\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1
and
k\equiv i\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)
.
If
i\neq 0
, then
[l]_{\alpha}\not\equiv 0\mod p
. We have

\frac{[k]_{\alpha}}{[l]_{\alpha}}=\frac{\alpha^{k-i}\alpha^{i}-1}{\alpha^{l-i}\alpha^{i}-1}\equiv\frac{\alpha^{i}-1}{\alpha^{i}-1}=1\mod p.

If
i=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{[k]_{q}}{[l]_{q}}\right|_{q=\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{q^{k}-1}{q^{l}-1}\right|_{q=\alpha}=\left.\frac{(1-q^{\ord_{p}(\alpha)})\sum_{s=0}^{k/\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1}q^{s\ord_{p}(\alpha)}}{(1-q^{\ord_{p}(\alpha)})\sum_{t=0}^{l/\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1}q^{t\ord_{p}(\alpha)}}\right|_{q=\alpha}=\frac{\sum_{s=0}^{k/\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1}\alpha^{s\ord_{p}(\alpha)}}{\sum_{t=0}^{l/\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1}\alpha^{t\ord_{p}(\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{k/\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{l/\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\mod p

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{l}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
p\in P
satisfying
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
. Let
k
be a positive integer with
0\leq k\leq p-1-\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. Put
\displaystyle C_{k}:=\frac{[p-k-1]_{\alpha}\cdots[p-k-\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}\cdots[k+1]_{\alpha}}
.
Then
C_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
and

\left[p-1\atop k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)\right]_{\alpha}\equiv C_{k}\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}\mod p

holds.
Especially if there exists
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
k=l\ord_{p}(\alpha)
, then we have

\displaystyle C_{l\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\equiv\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)-l}{l+1}\mod p
.

Proof.

By the definition, we have

\displaystyle\left[p-1\atop k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)\right]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{[p-1]_{\alpha}\cdots[p-k-\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}\cdots[1]_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[p-k-1]_{\alpha}\cdots[p-k-\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}\cdots[k+1]_{\alpha}}\times\frac{[p-1]_{\alpha}\cdots[p-k]_{\alpha}}{[k]_{\alpha}\cdots[1]_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=C_{k}\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}.

Note that for any
1\leq i\leq\ord_{p}(\alpha)
, there exists unique
j_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,\ord_{p}(\alpha)\}
such that
k+i\equiv p-k-j_{i}\mod\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. This correspondence is one-to-one.
We have

\displaystyle C_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{[p-k-1]_{\alpha}\cdots[p-k-\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}\cdots[k+1]_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[p-k-j_{1}]_{\alpha}}{[k+1]_{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{[p-k-j_{2}]_{\alpha}}{[k+2]_{\alpha}}\cdots\frac{[p-k-j_{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}.

By
1\leq k+1,\ldots,k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)\leq p-1
,
Lemma
2.2
implies
\displaystyle\frac{[p-k-j_{i}]_{\alpha}}{[k+i]_{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
for every
1\leq i\leq\ord_{p}(\alpha)
and
C_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\setminus\{0\}
holds.

In tha case where
k
is given by
k=l\ord_{p}(\alpha)
,
Lemma
2.2
implies

\displaystyle C_{l\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{[p-k-1]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}\times\frac{[p-k-2]_{\alpha}}{[k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1]_{\alpha}}\times\cdots\times\frac{[p-k-\ord_{p}(\alpha)]_{\alpha}}{[k+1]_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{p-k-1}{k+\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\equiv\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)-l}{l+1}\mod p.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
p\in P
satisfying
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
. Let
k
be a positive integer satisfying
0\leq k<\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. Then

\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}\equiv\begin{cases}1\mod p&\text{if }k=0\\
0\mod p&\text{if }k\neq 0\end{cases}

holds.

Proof.

We have a conclusion immediately if
k=0
.
Assume
1\leq k<\ord_{p}(\alpha)
. Note that
[k]_{\alpha}!\not\equiv 0\mod p
holds since
k<\ord_{p}(\alpha)
holds.
By
[p-1]_{\alpha}\equiv 0\mod p
, we have
\displaystyle\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}\equiv 0\mod p
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}

and
p\in P
with
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
,

\displaystyle\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k/\ord_{p}(\alpha)}&\mod p&\text{if }k\in(\ord_{p}(\alpha))\\
0&\mod p&\mathrm{else}\\
\end{array}\right.

holds.

Proof.

The case of
k\not\in(\ord_{p}(\alpha))
is clear by Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
.
If we suppose
k=l\ord_{p}(\alpha)
, then we have

\displaystyle\left[p-1\atop k\right]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(I_{p}(\alpha)-(l-1))(I_{p}(\alpha)-(l-2))\cdots(I_{p}(\alpha)-0)}{(l-1+1)(l-2+1)\cdots(0+1)}\cdot 1

\displaystyle\equiv\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{l}\mod p.

∎

The following proposition is the prime congruence of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}

and
p\in P
with
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
,

\displaystyle F_{p}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{10}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}\mod p

holds, where

S_{p,i}(\alpha)=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\equiv p-i\mod 5\}

for
1\leq i\leq 4
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\lfloor(p-1-5j)/2\rfloor=k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\textrm{\,\, for\,\,some\,\,}j

\displaystyle\qquad\Leftrightarrow k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\leq(p-1-5j)/2<k\ord_{p}(\alpha)+1\textrm{\,\, for\,\,some\,\,}j

\displaystyle\qquad\Leftrightarrow 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)=p-1-5j\textrm{\,\,or\,\,}p-2-5j\textrm{\,\, for\,\,some\,\,}j

\displaystyle\qquad\Leftrightarrow k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)\textrm{\,\,or\,\,}k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha).

We note that

j=\frac{p-i-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}\in\mathbb{Z}

holds for
k\in S_{p,i}(\alpha)
.
Since we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{\frac{j(5j+1)}{2}}=\alpha^{\frac{(p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha))(p-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha))}{10}}=\left(\alpha^{p-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{10}}=\alpha^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{10}}

for every
k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)
and

\displaystyle\alpha^{\frac{j(5j+1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\frac{(p-2-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha))(p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha))}{10}}=\left(\alpha^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{2}}\right)^{\frac{p-2-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)

for every
k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)
, we obtain

F_{p}(\alpha)\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{10}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}\mod p

by Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.6
.
∎

2.3.
The certain congruence of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence

In this subsection, we show our main theorem (Theorem
1.1
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0,1\}

and
p\in P
with
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0
.
If
\ord_{p}(\alpha)\not\equiv 0\mod 5
, then

F_{p}(\alpha)\equiv G_{I_{p}(\alpha),\ord_{p}(\alpha)}\mod p

holds, where

G_{n,m}:=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3\left(n-\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m\right)+k}\right\}

for any
n
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.9
.

For
n
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
G_{n,m}
is a finite sum. It is also noted
G_{n,0}=0
in the case where
m\equiv 0\mod 5
.

Proof.

Since
\ord_{p}(\alpha)\not\equiv 0\mod 5
, we get

\displaystyle S_{p,1}=

\displaystyle\{k\mid 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\equiv p-1\mod 5\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\mid 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\equiv I_{p}(\alpha)\ord_{p}(\alpha)\mod 5\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\mid k\equiv 3I_{p}(\alpha)\mod 5\}

and

\displaystyle S_{p,2}=

\displaystyle\{k\mid 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\equiv p-2\mod 5\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\mid 2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)\equiv I_{p}(\alpha)\ord_{p}(\alpha)-1\mod 5\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\mid k\equiv 3I_{p}(\alpha)-3\ord_{p}(\alpha)^{-1}\mod 5\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{k\mid k\equiv 3\left(I_{p}(\alpha)-\left(\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}\right)\ord_{p}(\alpha)\right)\mod 5\}.

By using Proposition
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle F_{p}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\frac{p-1-2k\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{10}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\left(\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)(I_{p}(\alpha)-2k)}{10}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\alpha^{\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)I_{p}(\alpha)}{2}}\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}\mod p.

i)

In the case where
2|I_{p}(\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{p}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\ord_{p}(\alpha)\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)-2k}{10}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\alpha^{\ord_{p}(\alpha)\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)}{2}}\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{3I_{p}(\alpha)+k}-\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{3\left(I_{p}(\alpha)-\left(\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha}{5}\right)\ord_{p}(\alpha)\right)+k}\right\}\mod p.

ii)

In the case where
2\not|I_{p}(\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{p}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\alpha^{\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{2}\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)-2k}{5}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-\alpha^{\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{2}I_{p}(\alpha)}\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}(-1)^{\frac{I_{p}(\alpha)-2k}{5}}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}-(-1)^{I_{p}(\alpha)}\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{k\in S_{p,1}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}+\sum_{k\in S_{p,2}(\alpha)}\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{k}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{3I_{p}(\alpha)+k}-\binom{I_{p}(\alpha)}{3\left(I_{p}(\alpha)-\left(\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha}{5}\right)\ord_{p}(\alpha)\right)+k}\right\}\mod p.

∎

Lemma 2.10
.

i)

These

G_{1,1}=1,\;G_{2,1}=1,\;G_{1,2}=0\text{ and }G_{2,2}=1

hold.

ii)

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. For any
m
,
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
m\equiv\pm m^{\prime}\mod 5
,

G_{n,m}=G_{n,m^{\prime}}

holds.

iii)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

G_{n,m}+G_{n+1,m}=G_{n+2,m}

holds. Therefore, if
m\not\equiv 0\mod 5
,
G_{n,m}=F_{n+\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)}
holds.

Proof.

i)

They are verified directly.

ii)

Since the following property of the finite summation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\binom{m}{k-i}=\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\binom{m}{k+5-i}

holds for
i=0
,
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
,
it is enough to show the case when
m^{\prime}\equiv-m\mod 5
. Since

\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)=\left(\frac{-1}{5}\right),

we have

\displaystyle G_{n,m}=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3\left(n-\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m\right)+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{-2n+3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3\left(n-\left(\frac{-m}{5}\right)(-m)\right)+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3\left(n-\left(\frac{m^{\prime}}{5}\right)m^{\prime}\right)+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{n,m^{\prime}}.

iii)

By
Pascal’s rule and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle G_{n,m}+G_{n+1,m}-G_{n+2,m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+k}-\binom{n+1}{3n+3+k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\binom{n+1}{(3n+3-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m)+k}-\binom{n+2}{3n+6+k}+\binom{n+2}{(3n+6-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m)+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left\{\binom{n}{3n+k}-\binom{n}{3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+k}-\binom{n}{3n+3+k}-\binom{n}{3n+2+k}\right.

\displaystyle+\binom{n}{(3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+3+k}+\binom{n}{(3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+2)+k}

\displaystyle-\binom{n+1}{3n+6+k}-\binom{n+1}{3n+5+k}

\displaystyle\left.+\binom{n+1}{3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+1+k}+\binom{n+1}{3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)m+k}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\sum_{k\in 5\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{0\leq i\leq 5}-\binom{n}{3n+i+k}+\binom{n}{3n-3\left(\frac{m}{5}\right)+i+k}\right)=0.

∎

By Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.10
,
we conclude as follows:

Theorem 2.11

(Theorem
1.1
)
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

and
p\in P
satisfying
v_{p}(\alpha)=v_{p}(\alpha-1)=0

and
\ord_{p}(\alpha)\not\equiv 0\mod 5
,

F_{p}(\alpha)\equiv F_{I_{p}(\alpha)+\left(\frac{\ord_{p}(\alpha)}{5}\right)}\mod p

holds.

In this section, we show that, under GRH, the element of
\mathscr{A}
associated with the

3.1.
Review on finite algebraic numbers

This subsection discusses finite algebraic numbers introduced in
[
9
]
which are defined by recurrent sequences.

For
(\alpha_{p})_{p}\in\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define

P_{n}((\alpha_{p})):=\{p\mid\alpha_{p}\equiv n\mod p\}\subset P.

By abuse of notation,
the image of
\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{p})_{p}\in\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

under the natural projection to
\mathscr{A}
is denoted to be
\alpha
.
We say
n

occurs infinitely
often in
\alpha
if
|P_{n}((\alpha_{p})_{p})|
is infinite (we note that it is independent of any choice of representatives in
\displaystyle\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
).

A sequence
(a_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{Q}
is called
recurrent
if there exists a monic polynomial
f(x):=x^{d}+c_{1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+c_{d}\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

a_{n+d}+c_{1}a_{n+d-1}+\cdots+c_{d}a_{n}=0\;\;(\text{on $\mathbb{Q}$})

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Such
f
and
(a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1})
is called the characteristic polynomial and the initial value of
(a_{n})_{n}
respectively.

Definition 3.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

An element
(\alpha_{p})_{p}\in\mathscr{A}
is called a
finite algebraic number
if there exists a recurrent sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
such that

\alpha_{p}\equiv a_{p}\mod p

for sufficiently every large
p
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}\subset\mathscr{A}
be the set of finite algebraic numbers.

The finite algebraic numbers are characterized in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
\alpha:=(\alpha_{p})_{p}\in\mathscr{A}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The element
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
is a finite algebraic number.

(2)

There exists a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
and a map
\phi:\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow L
satisfying
\phi(\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1})=\sigma\phi(\tau)
for
\sigma
,
\tau\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
such that

(\alpha_{p})_{p}=(\phi(\mathfrak{F}_{p})\mod p)_{p},

where
\mathfrak{F}_{p}
is a Frobenius map of
L
at prime
p
.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)

The set
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebra of
\mathscr{A}
in
[
9
]
.

(2)

[
9
,
\lx@sectionsign 4
]
explains that the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
is regarded as a finite analogue of an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}:=\{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\mid{}^{\exists}f(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]\setminus\{0\}\text{ such that }f(\alpha)=0\}
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

(1)

For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
, there exists a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]^{\times}
such that
f(\alpha)=0
in
\mathscr{A}
.

(2)

There is a sequence of inclusions of subsets:

\mathbb{Q}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}\subset\mathscr{A}.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
is countable and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
is uncountable (
[
9
]
).

Definition 3.6
.

An element of
\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
is called an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number
.

FMZV is expected to be an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number when it is non-zero, but a single example has not been found. According to the
\mathscr{A}
-analogue of the period conjecture proposed by Rosen (
[
8
]
), at least FMZV of depth
2
is expected to be non-zero.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
. If there exists a sequence of distinct integers
(a_{n})_{n}
such that
a_{n}
occurs infinitely often in
\alpha
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\alpha
is an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
.
By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
, we can take
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]\setminus\{0\}
with
f(\alpha)=0
as an element in
\mathscr{A}
.
Since the components of
(a_{n})_{n}
are distinct,
we can take
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(a_{n})\neq 0

in
\mathbb{Q}
. This implies that

f(a_{n})\not\equiv 0\mod p

for sufficiently every large
p
. Since
a_{n}
occurs infinitely often in
\alpha
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

f(\alpha)\neq 0

holds on
\mathscr{A}
. It contradicts that
f(\alpha)=0
in
\mathscr{A}
.

∎

Example 3.8
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
be represented by

(\overbrace{1}^{1},\overbrace{1,2}^{2},\overbrace{1,2,3}^{3},\ldots)
.
Then every
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
occurs infinitely often in
\alpha
. By Proposition
3.7
, this
\alpha
is an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number.
Hence
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}\subsetneq\mathscr{A}
holds.

3.2.
The
\mathscr{A}
-transcendence of the
q
-Fibonacci sequence

This subsection gives the proof of Theorem
1.2
(Theorem
3.15
).

By Theorem
1.1
, the
q
-Fibonacci sequence is related with the residual indices.
Several results on the residual indices are obtained under the following conjecture called the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH, in short).

Conjecture 3.9
.

The real part of every non-trivial zero of the Dedekind zeta function of an algebraic field
K
is equal to
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 3.10
.

In this paper, we assume the GRH for all fields
K_{s,r}^{g}=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{s},g^{\frac{1}{r}})
, where
g
is a positive square-free integer,
s
and
r
are integers satisfying
r|s
and
\zeta_{u}
is a
u
-th root of the unity.

Under GRH, Hooley (
[
2
]
) calculated the density of primes
p
satisfying
I_{p}(\alpha)=1
for a square-free positive integer
\alpha
.
Under GRH, Lenstra (
[
5
]
) calculated the density of primes satisfying
I_{p}(\alpha)=k
for a square-free integer
\alpha
and a positive integer
k
.
He also calculated the condition that the density of primes satisfying
I_{p}(\alpha)=k
is equal to
0
.
Murata (
[
7
]
) showed the asymptotic formulae on such primes less than a given positive real number
x
for a given square-free integer
\alpha
.

We prepare some notations to prove Theorem
3.15
(Theorem
1.2
).
Let
v
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Let
\zeta_{s}
be a root of unity and
\sigma_{b}:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{s})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{s}),\;\zeta_{s}\mapsto\zeta_{s}^{b}
for
b\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
(s,b)=1
.

•

Let
[a,b]
be the least common multiple of
a
and
b
and
(a,b)
be the greatest common divisor of
a
and
b
.

•

C_{g}(b,f,v)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\sigma_{b}|_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f})\cap K_{v,v}^{g}}=\id\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

•

Let
\mu:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\rightarrow\{0,\pm 1\}
be the Möbius function, i.e.

\mu(n):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $n$ has a squared prime factor}\\
(-1)^{k}&\text{if $n$ is the product of $k$ distinct primes}.\end{cases}

Moree showed the following lemma under GRH.

Lemma 3.11

(
[
6
, Lemma 11]
)
.

Let
a
,
d
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}
be square-free and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Put

V_{g}(a,d;t)(x):=|\{p\leq x\mid p\in P,\;I_{p}(g)=t,p\equiv 1+ta\mod dt\}|.

For sufficiently large
x
,

V_{g}(a,d;t)(x)=\frac{x}{\log x}\delta(a,d;t)+O_{g,d}\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\varphi(t)\log^{2}x}+\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}\right)

holds under GRH, where

\delta(a,d;t):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,d)|a\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)C_{g}(1+ta;dt;nt)}{[K_{[d,n]t,nt}^{g}:\mathbb{Q}]},

\varphi
is Euler’s totient function and
O_{g,d}
is the Landau notation with respect to
g
and
d
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Under GRH for all such fields
K_{s,r}^{g}
, if
t\in(5\mathbb{Z}_{>0}+1)\cap P
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
g
is a square-free integer, then there are infinitely many primes
p
satisfying
I_{p}(g)=t
and
p\equiv 1+t\mod 5t
.

We set up the following auxiliary lemmas to prove the above proposition.

Lemma 3.13

(
[
2
, Equation (12)]
)
.

Let
g
be a square-free integer. Let
s
and
r
be positive integers satisfying
r|s
.
Then we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle[K_{s,r}^{g}:\mathbb{Q}]=\frac{r\varphi(s)}{\varepsilon_{g}(s)},

where

\varepsilon_{g}(s):=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $2g|s$ and $g\equiv 1\mod 4$}\\
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
a\geq 2
,
b\geq 2
and
p
be an odd prime. Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}
be a square-free integer.

(1)

If
b|a
, then we have
\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{ap},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
.

(2)

If
bp|a
, then
[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{bp}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]=p
holds.

Proof.

(
1
)
By (
3.1
), we have

[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{ap},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]=[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{ap},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}]/[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}]=\frac{\frac{b\varphi(ap)}{\varepsilon_{g}(ap)}}{\frac{b\varphi(a)}{\varepsilon_{g}(a)}}=\frac{\varphi(ap)}{\varphi(a)}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{g}(a)}{\varepsilon_{g}(ap)}\geq\frac{\varphi(ap)}{2\varphi(a)}.

•

Suppose
(a,p)=1
. Since
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(ap)}{\varphi(a)}=\varphi(p)=p-1
, we have
\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{ap},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
.

•

Suppose
(a,p)=p
and take
a=cp^{k}

(c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0})
. Since
\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(c)\varphi(p^{k+1})}{\varphi(c)\varphi(p^{k})}=p
, we have
\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{ap},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]=\frac{p}{2}\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
.

(
2
)
The equation (
3.1
) shows

[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{bp}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}})]=[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{bp}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a})]/[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a},g^{\frac{1}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{a})]=\frac{bp}{\varepsilon_{g}(a)}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{g}(a)}{b}=p.

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.12
.

By Lemma
3.11
, it is sufficient to show
\delta(1,5;t)>0
.

If we consider the prime factorization of
n
and that
(n,5)|1
and
(n,5)=1
are equivalent, then
\delta(1,5;t)
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\delta(1,5;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{t}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k+1}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}\right).

Note that if
\sigma_{b}|_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f})\cap K_{v,v}}\neq\id
, then
\sigma_{b}|_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f})\cap K_{pv,pv}}\neq\id
for
b
,
f
and
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and a prime number
p
.
This implies if
C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)=0
, then
C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)=0
holds.
Moreover,
C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)=C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)
holds.
Thus we have

\displaystyle\delta(1,5;t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k+1}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})][\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}]}\times

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<p_{k+1}\\
p_{k+1}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]}\right).

Hence it is sufficient to show

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<p_{k+1}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]}<1

for every
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
By Lemma
3.14
,

\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p_{k+1}\frac{(p_{k+1}-1)}{2}\geq\frac{(p_{k+1}-1)^{2}}{2}

holds. Therefore,

(i)

In the case where
k>0
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<p_{k+1}\\
p_{i}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k+1}t}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t},g^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k}t}})]}

\displaystyle<2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<p_{k+1}\\
p_{k}\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(p_{k+1}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}\frac{1}{4l^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}<\frac{5}{6}<1.

(ii)

In the case where
k=0
, since we have

[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5pt},g^{\frac{1}{pt}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5t},g^{\frac{1}{t}})]=\frac{\frac{pt\varphi(5pt)}{\varepsilon_{g}(5pt)}}{\frac{t\varphi(5t)}{\varepsilon_{g}(5t)}}\geq\frac{5p\varphi(pt)}{2}\geq\frac{5p(p-1)}{2}\geq\frac{5(p-1)^{2}}{2}

by (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P\\
p\neq 5\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{g}(1+t,5t,pt)}{[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5pt},g^{\frac{1}{pt}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5t},g^{\frac{1}{t}})]}\leq

\displaystyle\;\frac{2}{5}\sum_{p\in P}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{15}<\frac{2}{3}.

So (
3.2
) is obtained. Hence we show the claim.
∎

Using the above lemma, we obtain the result on
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental numbers.

Theorem 3.15

(Theorem
1.2
)
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
be a square-free integer.
Then
F=(F_{p}(g))_{p}
is an
\mathscr{A}
-transcendental number under GRH for all such fields
K_{s,r}^{g}
.

Proof.

Let
t\in(5\mathbb{Z}_{>0}+1)\cap P
. By Proposition
3.12
, there are infinitely many primes
p
which satisfy
I_{p}(g)=t
and
p\equiv 1+t\mod 5t
.
Therefore there are infinitely many primes
p
which satisfy
I_{p}(g)=t
and
p\equiv 2\mod 5
by
t\equiv 1\mod 5
.
By Theorem
1.1
, at least
one of
F_{t-1}
or
F_{t+1}
occurs infinitely often in
F
.
That means there exists a subsequence
(a_{n})
of the Fibonacci sequence such that

a_{n}
occurs infinitely on
F

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
F\notin\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{A}}^{0}
by Proposition
3.7
.
∎

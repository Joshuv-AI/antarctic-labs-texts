Recurrent Point Processes for Dynamic Review Models
Kostadin Cvejoski, Ramsés J. Sánchez, Bogdan Georgiev, Jannis Schuecker, Christian Bauckhage, César Ojeda

Abstract
Recent progress in recommender system research has shown the importance of including temporal representations to improve interpretability and performance. Here, we incorporate temporal representations in continuous time via recurrent point process for a dynamical model of reviews. Our goal is to characterize how changes in perception, user interest and seasonal effects affect review text.



Introduction

Costumer reviews provide a rich and natural source of unstructured data which can be leverage to improve interactive and conversational recommender system performance
[
\citeauthoryearLiu et al.2019
]
. Reviews are effectively a form of recommendation. Although causal and temporal relations have been know to improve the performance of recommender systems
[
\citeauthoryearWu et al.2017
]
, recent natural language process (NLP) methodologies for rating and reviews
[
\citeauthoryearZheng, Noroozi, and Yu2017
]
lack behind at incorporating temporal structure in language representations. In the present work, we exploit recurrent neural network (RNN) models for point process and include neural representations of text to characterize costumer reviews. Our goal is to capture the changes in taste and importance of items during time, and how such changes reflect on the text produced by the different users.

The reviews research have sought to characterize usefulness and generation of reviews
[
\citeauthoryearFan et al.2019
,
\citeauthoryearNovgorodov et al.2019
]
and provide better representations for rating prediction
[
\citeauthoryearEsmaeili et al.2019
]
. The need to interact with costumers have lead to question answering solutions
[
\citeauthoryearChen et al.2019
,
\citeauthoryearYu and Lam2018
]
. Deep neural networks models for rating predictions use embedding representations as well as convolutions neural networks
[
\citeauthoryearCatherine and
Cohen2017
]
.
Dynamic models of text however have shown more success from the bayesian perspective within topic models
[
\citeauthoryearRudolph and Blei2018
,
\citeauthoryearWang, Blei, and

]
. Self exciting point processes have allow for clustering of document streams
[
\citeauthoryearDu et al.2015

Recurrent Point Review Model (RPRM)

Consider an item
a
(e.g. a business, service or movie) and assume that, since its opening to the public, it has received a collection of
N_{a}
reviews
\mathbf{r}^{a}=\{(\mathbf{x}^{a}_{j},t^{a}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N_{a}}
, where
t^{a}_{j}
labels the creation time of review
\mathbf{r}^{a}_{j}
and
\mathbf{x}^{a}_{j}=(\mathbf{w}^{j}_{1},...,\mathbf{w}^{j}_{M_{j}})
corresponds to its text
1
1

1

In what follows we shall drop the index
a
over items.
. Such a collection of reviews effectively defines a point process in time.
Our main idea is to model these point processes as RNNs
in continuous time
and use their hidden representations, which encode the nonlinear relations between text and timing of past reviews, to predict how the reviews’ text of a given item changes with time.
The model thus consists of two components: a point process model which leverages the information encoded in the review text and a dynamic neural text model.

We start by transforming the text of each review into a
bag of words
(BoW) representation
\mathbf{X}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, where
V
is the vocabulary size
[
\citeauthoryearHinton and
Salakhutdinov2009
]
.

Recurrent Point Process (RPP):

Let us consider a point process with compact support
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}
. Formally, we write the likelihood of a new arrival (i.e. a new review)
\mathbf{r}_{j+1}
as an inhomogeneous Poisson process between reviews, conditioned on the history
\mathcal{H}_{j}\equiv\{\mathbf{r}_{1},...,\mathbf{r}_{j}\}
2
2

2

a.k.a. filtration.

[
\citeauthoryearDaley and Vere-Jones2007
]
.
For the one-dimensional processes we are concerned here, the conditional likelihood function reads

f^{*}(t)=\lambda^{*}(t)\exp\left\{\int^{t}_{t_{j}}\lambda^{*}\left(t^{\prime}\right)dt^{\prime}\right\}\,,

(1)

where
\lambda^{*}
is (locally) integrable and is known as the intensity function of the point process.
Following
[
\citeauthoryearMei and Eisner2017
]
,
[
\citeauthoryearDu et al.2016a
]
, we define the functional dependence of the intensity function to be given by a RNN with hidden state
\mathbf{h}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where an exponential function guarantees that the intensity is
non-negative

\lambda^{*}(t)=\exp{\left\{\mathbf{v}^{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{j}+w^{t}\left(t-t_{j}\right)+b^{t}\right\}}\,.

(2)

Here the vector
\mathbf{v}^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the scalars
w^{t}
and
b^{t}
are trainable variables. The update equation for the hidden variables of the recurrent network can be written as a general non-linear function

\mathbf{h}_{j}=f_{\theta}(t_{j},\mathbf{X}_{j},\mathbf{h}_{j-1}),

(3)

where
t_{j}
and
\mathbf{X}_{j}
label the creation time and the text’s BoW representation of review
\mathbf{r}_{j}
, respectively, and
\theta
denotes the network’s parameters. We thus use the BoW representation of the review text as marks in the recurrent marked temporal point process
[
\citeauthoryearDu et al.2016b
]
. Inserting Eq. (
2
) into Eq. (
1
) and integrating over time immediately yields the likelihood
f^{*}
as a function of
\mathbf{h}_{j}
.

Dynamic Neural Text Model:
To model the text component of reviews we assume the words in review
\mathbf{r}_{j+1}
are generated independently, conditioned on
\mathbf{h}_{j}
, the temporal representation of the RPP above. Specifically, we follow
[
\citeauthoryearMiao, Yu, and Blunsom2016
]
and write the conditional probability of generating the
i
th word
\mathbf{w}^{j+1}_{i}
of the
(j+1)
th review as

\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{w}^{j+1}_{i}|\mathbf{h}_{j})=

\displaystyle\frac{\exp{\{-z(\mathbf{w}^{j}_{i},\mathbf{h}_{j})\}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{V}\exp{\{-z(\mathbf{w}^{j}_{k},\mathbf{h}_{j})\}}},

(4)

\displaystyle z(\mathbf{w}^{j}_{i},\mathbf{h}_{j})=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{h}^{T}_{j}\mathbf{R}\,\mathbf{w}^{j}_{i}-\mathbf{b}\,\mathbf{w}^{j}_{i},

(5)

where
\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times V}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
are trainable parameters, and
\mathbf{w}^{j}_{i}
is the one-hot representation of the word at position
i
.
The complete log-likelihood of the RPRM model can then by written as

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{a}}(\log f^{*}(\delta^{a}_{j+1}|\mathbf{h}_{j})+\log P(\mathbf{X}^{a}_{j+1}|\mathbf{h}_{j})),

(6)

where
\delta^{a}_{j+1}=t^{a}_{j+1}-t^{a}_{j}
denotes the inter-review time for item
a
and
P(\mathbf{X}^{a}_{j+1}|\mathbf{h}_{j})
is a multinomial distribution over word probabilities (Eq. (
4
)) and counts.

Baseline model:
In order to test our model we define LSTM-BoW, which models the inter-review time
\delta_{j+1}
as the mean of an exponential distribution with parameter
\lambda_{\phi}(\mathbf{h}^{\prime}_{j})
, and the probability over words
p(\mathbf{w}^{j+1}_{i}|\mathbf{h}^{\prime}_{j})
as
\mbox{softmax}(g_{\phi}(\mathbf{h}^{\prime}_{j}))
. The functions
\lambda_{\phi}
and
g_{\phi}
are given by neural networks with parameter
\phi
, and
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}_{j}=f_{\phi}(t_{j},\mathbf{X}_{j},\mathbf{h}^{\prime}_{j-1})
is the hidden state of an LSTM network
[
\citeauthoryearHochreiter and Schmidhuber1997
]
.
We also consider additional LSTM and RPP models which only take
t_{j}
as input, as to check whether the BoW representation
\mathbf{X}_{i}
helps in the prediction of the inter-review times.

We test our models on the Yelp19 dataset

01 Jan 2016

\citeauthoryearHinton and
Salakhutdinov2009

On average we have 9.6 reviews per business with standard deviation of 22.8. Each review has on average 7.2 sentences and 50.2 words, with 5.9 and 46.14 standard deviation respectively. In the experiments, we randomly split each dataset into two parts: training set (80%) and test set (20%). We use grid search for hyper-parameters finding.

We trained all models on maximum likelihood and use two evaluation metrics: Root-mean-squared error (RMSE) on the inter-review times and predictive perplexity on the review text. The latter is defined as

Conclusion and Future Work

In this work we incorporate a bag of word language model as the marks of a recurrent temporal point process. This creates a model which characterize temporal and causal representation for text, allowing for a richer representation for costumers reviews. We show that this improves predictive performance for the time of the reviews, as well as opening the door for
text prediction
. We will extend this methodology for rating prediction as well as more complex models of text.

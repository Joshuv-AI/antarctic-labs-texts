On some infinitary logics
Jouko Väänänen, Boban Veličković

Abstract
. We define a new class of infinitary logics ℒ κ , α 1 \mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha} generalizing Shelah’s logic 𝕃 κ 1 \mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa} defined in [ 14 ] . If κ = ℶ κ \kappa=\beth_{\kappa} and α < κ \alpha<\kappa is infinite then our logic coincides with 𝕃 κ 1 \mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa} . We study the relation between these logics for different parameters κ \kappa and α \alpha . We give many examples of classes of structures that can or cannot be defined in these logics. Finally, we give a different version of Lindström’s Theorem for 𝕃 κ 1 \mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa} in terms of the ϕ \phi -submodel relation.

1.
Introduction

Lindström Theorem
[
8
]
characterizes first order logic as the maximal logic satisfying the Compactness Theorem and the downward Löwenheim-Skolem Theorem. Despite intensive efforts, no such model theoretic characterizations were obtained for infinitary logics, until Shelah’s paper
[
14
]
. There is a generally held intuition that if a logic has a model theoretic characterization, it should satisfy the Craig Interpolation Theorem. Thus a good direction to look for such logics is to look for infinitary logics with the Craig Interpolation Theorem. Malitz
[
11
]
proved that infinitary logics
{\mathscr{L}}_{\kappa\omega}
, for
\kappa\geq\aleph_{2}
, fail to have Interpolation but, if
\kappa
is regular, any valid implication can be interpolated in
{\mathscr{L}}_{(2^{<\kappa})^{+},\kappa}
. Shelah’s
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
, which was defined in
[
14
]
,
has Interpolation, and is actually between
{\mathscr{L}}_{\kappa\omega}
and
{\mathscr{L}}_{\kappa\kappa}
for strongly inaccessible
\kappa
, and also for many other
\kappa
.

Thus
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
satisfies the requirement of being a
nice logic
, but has nevertheless attracted very little attention (this was actually predicted by Shelah himself in
[
14
]
).
In this paper we try to remedy this situation. We first define a variation of Shelah’s game by adding an extra ordinal parameter
\alpha
. Shelah’s game corresponds to setting
\alpha=\omega
.
We also introduce the infinite version of the game. We study the relations between these games for various parameters and give examples of properties that can or cannot be detected by our games.
Finally, we consider a class of logics
\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}
, for a cardinal
\kappa
and ordinal
\alpha<\kappa
. If
\kappa=\beth_{\kappa}
then these logics coincide with
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
, for all
\alpha<\kappa
.
We also give a version of Lindström’s theorem characterizing the logic
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
in terms of a
\phi
-submodels relation, for all formulas
\phi
.

The paper is organized as follows. In §2 we review the basic notions of generalized logics and recall Lindström’s characterization of first order logic.
In §3 we define our variation
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}
of Shelah’s game and study the relation between these games for various parameters.
The main result of this section is a theorem that allows to reduce the ordinal
\alpha
in these games to
\omega
. More precisely, given a cardinal
\theta
and an ordinal
\beta
,
we find
\theta^{*}
and
\beta^{*}
such that if
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
are two structures in the same vocabulary, and if Eve has a winning strategy in
{\rm DG}^{\beta^{*}}_{\theta^{*},\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
,
then she has a winning strategy in
{\rm DG}^{\beta}_{\theta,\omega}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
. In §4 we give a number of examples of properties of structures
that can or cannot be detected by our games
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}
, for various parameters
\beta,\theta
and
\alpha
.
In §5 we define a class of logics
\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}
and show that they are regular. For the above theorem on the reduction on
\alpha
we deduce
that for
\kappa=\beth_{\kappa}
all these logics coincide with Shelah’s
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
. §6 contains our characterization of
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}

in terms of the
\phi
-submodel relation
\preceq_{\phi}
that has the Union Property and the Löwenheim-Skolem number below
\kappa
.
We deduce Shelah’s characterizations of
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
as a corollary.
Finally, in §7 we list some open questions related to our logics
\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We start by reviewing the general framework of generalized logics. We work in von Neumann–Bernays–Gödel set theory (NBG) which allows us to speak of proper classes.
For simplicity we restrict ourselves to notions of logics based on conventional algebraic structures with a single sorted vocabulary. For a more systematic presentation we refer the reader to
[
1
, Chapter 2]
.
Given a vocabulary
\tau
we denote by
{\rm Str}[\tau]
the class of
\tau
-structures. Given a structure
\mathfrak{A}
we write
\tau_{\mathfrak{A}}
for the vocabulary of
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\sigma\subseteq\tau
are vocabularies and
\mathfrak{A}

is a
\tau
-structure we write
\mathfrak{A}\restriction\sigma

for the
\sigma
-
reduct

\tau

\sigma

Given two vocabularies
\tau

\sigma

\rho:\tau\to\sigma

is called a

function symbols to functions symbols of the same arity, and similarly for relation symbols. Given a renaming

and a

-structure

we can rename
\mathfrak{A}
by
\rho

\sigma

We now recall the basic definition of a generalized logic.

Definition 2.1
.

A logic is a pair
(\mathscr{L},\models_{\mathscr{L}})
defined on vocabularies, where
\mathscr{L}[\tau]
is the set (or proper class) of all
\mathscr{L}
-sentences of vocabulary
\tau
, and
\models_{\mathscr{L}}
(the
\mathscr{L}
-satisfaction relation) is a relation between
\tau
structures and
\mathscr{L}
-sentences and the following hold:

(1)

If
\tau\subseteq\sigma
then
\mathscr{L}[\tau]\subseteq\mathscr{L}[\sigma]
;

(2)

If
\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
then
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau_{\mathfrak{A}}]
;

(3)

(Isomorphism property) If
\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi

and
\mathfrak{B}\simeq\mathfrak{A}
then
\mathfrak{B}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
;

(4)

(Reduct property) If
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
,
\mathfrak{A}
is a structure, and

then

\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ iff }\hskip 14.22636pt\mathfrak{A}\restriction\tau\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi.

(5)

(Renaming property) Let
\rho:\tau\to\sigma
be a renaming of vocabularies.
Then for each
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
there is a sentence
\phi^{\rho}
in

\mathscr{L}[\sigma]
such that

\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ iff }\hskip 14.22636pt\mathfrak{A}^{\rho}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi^{\rho}.

It is often convenient to have

Suppose

and we write

and the

are new variables.
Given a

and a sequence

of elements of

we write
\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi[\bar{a}]

if by interpreting

we obtain a
\tau^{\prime}
-structure

such that

.
In this way we can replace

by two functions

\mathscr{L}
-

of vocabulary
\tau

and
{\rm Form}_{\mathscr{L}}[\tau]
of
\mathscr{L}
-

of vocabulary

We can then define notions such as the free occurrence of variables in a canonical way.
For further details and discussion we refer the reader to
[
1
, Chapter 2]
.

Given a logic
\mathscr{L}
, a vocabulary
\tau
, and
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]

{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{L}}^{\tau}(\phi)
denote the class of all models of

, i.e.

{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{L}}^{\tau}(\phi)=\{\mathfrak{A}\in{\rm Str}[\tau]:\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi\}.

Given two sentences

we say that
\phi

\mathscr{L}
-
implies

\psi
and write

if
{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{L}}^{\tau}(\phi)\subseteq{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{L}}^{\tau}(\psi)
, i.e. every
\tau
-structure that satisfies

.

We let
{\mathscr{L}}_{\omega,\omega}
denote the usual first order logic.
We now recall some basic closure properties of a logic which ensure that it is at least as strong as
{\mathscr{L}}_{\omega,\omega}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a logic. We have:

(1)

(Atom property) For all
\tau
and all atomic
\phi\in{\mathscr{L}}_{\omega,\omega}[\tau]
, there is
\psi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
such that

{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\psi)={\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}_{\omega,\omega}}(\phi).

(2)

(Negation property) For all
\tau
and all
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
, there is a
\psi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
, denoted by
\neg\phi
, such that:

{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\psi)={\rm Str}[\tau]\setminus{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\phi).

(3)

(Conjunction property) For all
\tau
and
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]

there is
\psi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
, denoted by
\phi_{0}\wedge\phi_{1}
,
such that:

{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\psi)={\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\phi_{0})\cap{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}}(\phi_{1}).

(4)

(Particularization property) If
\tau
is a vocabulary and
c\in\tau

is a constant symbol, then for every
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]

there is
\psi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau\setminus\{c\}]
such that
for all
(\tau\setminus\{c\})
-structures
\mathfrak{A}

we have
\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\psi
iff there is
a\in A
(the universe of
\mathfrak{A}
), such that
(\mathfrak{A},a)\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
.
If such
\psi
exists we denote it by
\exists c\phi
.

Let
\tau
be a vocabulary and
\mathfrak{A}\in{\rm Str}[\tau]
.
We say that
C\subseteq A
is
\tau
-
closed
if
C
is nonempty,

c^{\mathfrak{A}}\in C
, for all constant symbols
c\in\tau
, and

C
is closed under
f^{\mathfrak{A}}
, for all function symbols
f\in\tau
.

Definition 2.3
.

(Relativization property) We say that a logic
\mathscr{L}
satisfies the relativization property if the following holds. Suppose
\sigma
and
\tau
are vocabularies,
c
a constant symbol with
c\notin\sigma\cup\tau
,
\chi\in\mathscr{L}[\sigma\cup\{c\}]

and
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
. Then there is
\psi\in\mathscr{L}[\sigma\cup\tau]

such that if
\mathfrak{B}
is a
(\sigma\cup\tau)
-structure and

\chi^{\mathfrak{B}}=\{b\in B:(\mathfrak{B},b)\models_{\mathscr{L}}\chi\}
is
\tau
-closed in
\mathfrak{B}
, then

\mathfrak{B}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\psi\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{iff}\hskip 14.22636pt(\mathfrak{B}\restriction\tau)|\chi^{\mathfrak{B}}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi.

Intuitively,
\psi
is the relativization of
\phi
to
\chi^{\mathfrak{B}}
and is often written as

\phi^{\{c|\chi(c)\}}
. If
\chi
is the formula
P(c)
, for some unary predicate symbol
P
, we then simply write it as
\phi^{P}
.

Relativization allows us to represent one
\tau
-structure in another one by relativizing to a definable subset of the bigger structure. However, if we want to be able to talk about many
\tau
-structures inside a
\sigma
-structure
\mathfrak{A}
we should be able to express the parameterized version of the symbols of
\tau
in the vocabulary of
\mathfrak{A}
and then we should be able to say that for each suitable choice of the parameters the corresponding induced structure satisfies a given
\mathscr{L}[\tau]
-sentence sentence
\phi
. We only state a weaker form of substitution called

which is sufficient for our needs.

Definition 2.4
.

(Atomic substitution) We say that a logic
\mathscr{L}
satisfies atomic substitution if the following holds. Suppose that
\tau
and
\sigma
are vocabularies. Let
U\in\sigma
be a binary relation. Suppose for every constant symbol

n

n+1
-ary relation symbol

, and for every
n
-ary function symbol

n+1
-ary function symbol

Now, let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
\sigma
-structure and let
a=d^{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Let

B_{a}=\{b\in A:U^{\mathfrak{A}}(b,a)\}.

If

is an

R^{\mathfrak{B}_{a}}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{iff}\hskip 14.22636ptK_{R}^{\mathfrak{A}}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1},a).

If

is a constant symbol, we
c^{\mathfrak{B}_{a}}=K_{c}^{\mathfrak{A}}(a)
.
If

n
-ary function symbol,

n

by letting

, for all

If

is closed under these interpretation of the symbols of

\tau

. We require that for each
\tau

\phi
, there is a

\mathfrak{A}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\psi\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{iff \hskip 14.22636pt $\mathfrak{B}_{a}$ is a $\tau$-structure and }\mathfrak{B}_{a}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi.

Let us note that the stronger form of substitution is obtained if the interpretation of the symbols of
\tau
in the structure

is given not by interpretations of corresponding symbols of

-formulas.

We say that a logic
\mathscr{L}
is

of
\tau

if it is small and satisfies all the properties of Definition

We shall also need the notion of the occurrence number. Given a logic

, the

of

,
if it exists is the smallest cardinal

\mathscr{L}[\tau]=\bigcup_{\tau_{0}\in[\tau]^{<\gamma}}\mathscr{L}[\tau_{0}].

Note that the occurrence number of the usual first order logic is

and the occurrence number of the infinitary logic,

is

There is a natural way to compare logics.

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
\mathscr{L}
and

are regular logics.

(1)

We say that

is stronger than

, and write

.

(2)

We say that
\mathscr{L}
and

are equivalent and write

\mathscr{L}\leq\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}\leq\mathscr{L}

We now recall some desirable properties of logics. We will restrict ourselves to regular logics to avoid some pathologies.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a regular logic and let

(1)

We say that
\mathscr{L}
is

-weakly compact if for every vocabulary
\tau

is

.

(2)

We say that

\tau
and

, if

.

(3)

We say that
\mathscr{L}
satisfies the Craig interpolation property if for every vocabularies

and

, and

One of the first results in abstract model theory is the following well-known result of Lindström.

]
)

Let

be a regular logic. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

It is possible to replace

We then say that

Note that if the occurrence number of

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a regular logic. We say that

satisfies the union property if, for every vocabulary

,
and an increasing sequence of

In

Lindström showed that (1) in Theorem

(

)

is

-compact and has the union property.

It was felt that Lindström’s theorem underscores the importance of first order logic as a maximal logic having some desirable properties. It then became a
holy grail
of Abstract Model Theory to find stronger logics which have similar characterizations by varying the defining properties.
The search of such logics has been largely unsuccessful until Shelah introduced his logic
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
in
[
14
]
. Unfortunately, by that time there were very few people left working in Abstract Model Theory and therefore, as predicted by Shelah himself, the ideas were not picked up by other researchers. One of the goals of the current paper is to revive interest in this subject by initiating a systematic study of Shelah’s logic and its variations.

3.
The similarity game

A logic measures the similarity of two structures of the same vocabulary. The strongest similarity is if the two structures are isomorphic. Quite often we can think of a logic as a type of a game between two players,
Adam
and
Eve
. Let us suppose
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
are two structures in the same vocabulary
\tau
. We will write
M_{i}
for the universe of
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
, and we will always assume that they are disjoint so that no ambiguity arises. If they are not, this can be arranged by a standard coding argument.

Now, we think of our game as follows. Eve claims to know that
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
are isomorphic, but she won’t tell us the isomorphism. Instead she will answer a series of Adam’s questions of a particular kind. In the process she has to match some elements of
M_{0}
with elements of
M_{1}
, i.e. she reveals a part of the unknown isomorphism. So if at some position the matching Eve played is not a partial isomorphism then Eve loses the game. The game may be finite or infinite, in fact we may allow games of uncountable length, although this will not be done in this paper.

For the logics we are interested in we will start by defining the positions in the game and then describe the moves from one position to another. Our basic definition is a slight generalization of the one from
[
14
]
.
Throughout this section we fix two structures
\mathfrak{M}_{0}

and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
in the same vocabulary
\tau
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given ordinals
\alpha
and
\beta
, and a cardinal
\theta
, a
(\beta,\theta,\alpha)
-
position
for
(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
is a tuple

p=(\beta_{p},A_{p}^{0},A_{p}^{1},g_{p},h_{p})
, where

(1)

\beta_{p}\leq\beta
.

(2)

A_{p}^{0}\in[M_{0}]^{\leq\theta}
and
A_{p}^{1}\in[M_{1}]^{\leq\theta}
.

(3)

g_{p}:A_{p}^{0}\to A_{p}^{1}
a partial isomorphism.

(4)

h_{p}:A_{p}^{0}\cup A_{p}^{1}\to\alpha
.

(5)

h_{p}^{-1}(0)\cap A_{p}^{0}\subseteq\mathop{{\rm dom}}(g_{p})
and
h_{p}^{-1}(0)\cap A_{p}^{1}\subseteq\mathop{{\rm ran}}(g_{p})
.

We refer to
\beta_{p}
as the height of
p
. For
a\in\mathop{{\rm dom}}(h_{p})
, we refer to
h_{p}(a)
as the height of
a
in the position
p
.
We say that a position
q

extends
position
p
, and write

, if

(1)

\beta_{q}<\beta_{p}
.

(2)

A_{q}^{0}\supseteq A_{p}^{0}
and
A_{q}^{1}\supseteq A_{p}^{1}
.

(3)

g_{q}\restriction A_{p}^{0}=g_{p}
.

(4)

If
a\in A_{p}^{0}\cup A_{p}^{1}
, then
h_{q}(a)\leq h_{p}(a)
, and if
h_{p}(a)>0
, then
h_{q}(x)<h_{p}(a)
.

Note that any
(\beta,\theta,\alpha)
-position is also
an
(\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
-position, for any
\beta^{\prime}\geq\beta
,
\theta^{\prime}\geq\theta
, and
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given ordinals
\alpha
and
\beta
, and a cardinal
\theta
, the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
is played between two players, Adam and Eve.
The starting position is
(\beta,\emptyset,\emptyset,\emptyset,\emptyset)
. Eve consecutively plays
(\beta,\theta,\alpha)
-positions according to the following rule. If the current position is
p
then:

•

Adam chooses
\beta^{\prime}<\beta_{p}
,
B_{0}\in[M_{0}]^{\leq\theta}
and
B_{1}\in[M_{1}]^{\leq\theta}
,

•

Eve chooses a position
q<p
such that
\beta_{q}=\beta^{\prime}
,

B_{0}\subseteq A_{q}^{0}
and
B_{1}\subseteq A_{q}^{1}
.

We declare the next position to be
q
. The player who cannot move loses and the opponent wins.

The intuition is the following. A position describes a partial information about an unknown isomorphism between
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
.
We think of
\gamma
as the height of the position. This height is chosen by Adam and it should be at most
\beta
.
Elements of
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
are assigned heights by Eve and they must be ordinals
<\alpha
. Going from one position
p
to the next one
p^{\prime}
, Adam asks questions about the unknown isomorphism by requiring that the next sets
A^{\prime}_{0}
and
A^{\prime}_{1}
contain
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
respectively. Each time he does this he must decrease the height of the position. Eve is not required to answer the questions immediately. Instead, she assigns heights to elements of
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
. These heights are ordinals
<\alpha
.
Going from one position to the next one, Eve is required to decrease the heights of the elements that were present in the old position. Once the height of an element
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
reaches
0
, Eve must match it with an element of height
0
in the other structure. This matching must be a partial isomorphism, otherwise Eve loses.
Since the heights of the positions go down the game must stop after finitely many steps. There may still be elements of
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
whose heights are not
0
and Eve is not obliged to match them with elements of the other structure.

Since the game must end in finitely many moves, by the Gale-Stewart theorem
[
4
]
it is determined.
We will write
A^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
if Adam has a winning strategy, and

E^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
if Eve has a winning strategy in the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
.

We first observe that if Eve has a winning strategy, she has a
positional
winning strategy, i.e. a strategy which at every move only depends on the position of the game, not on the history how the game evolved into that position.

Lemma 3.3
.

Eve has a winning strategy in the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})

if and only if there is a set

of
(\beta,\theta,\alpha)
-positions such that:

against all possible legal move combinations of Adam, including the starting position

This

clearly satisfies the requirements. ∎

While the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
is obviously symmetric, the existence of a winning strategy for Eve may not be transitive. In other words, if Eve wins
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
and
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{1},\mathfrak{M}_{2})
, she may not win
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
. Nevertheless, there is a weak version of transitivity that we now explain. In order to do this we will use natural sum of ordinals, see
[
15
]
.
Recall that for ordinals
\alpha
and
\beta
, the
natural sum
of
\alpha
and
\beta
,
denoted by
\alpha\oplus\beta
, is defined by simultaneous induction on
\alpha
and
\beta

as the smallest ordinal greater than
\alpha\oplus\gamma
, for all
\gamma<\beta
,
and
\gamma\oplus\beta
, for all
\gamma<\alpha
.
Another way to define the natural sum of two ordinals
\alpha
and
\beta

is to use the Cantor normal form: one can find a sequence of ordinals
\gamma_{0}>\ldots\gamma_{n-1}

and two sequences (
k_{0},\ldots,k_{n-1})
and
(j_{0},\ldots,j_{n-1})

of natural numbers (including zero, but satisfying
k_{i}+j_{i}>0
, for all
i
) such that

\alpha=\omega^{\gamma_{0}}\cdot k_{0}+\cdots+\omega^{\gamma_{n-1}}\cdot k_{n-1}

and
\beta=\omega^{\gamma_{0}}\cdot j_{0}+\cdots+\omega^{\gamma_{n-1}}\cdot j_{n-1}

and defines

\alpha\oplus\beta=\omega^{\gamma_{0}}\cdot(k_{0}+j_{0})+\cdots+\omega^{\gamma_{n-1}}\cdot(k_{n-1}+j_{n-1}).

What is important for us is that the natural sum is associative and commutative. It is always greater or equal
to the usual sum, but it may be strictly greater.

We now turn to a partition theorem due to Komjath-Shelah

\gamma

of ordinals from
\lambda

\lambda>s(0)>s(1)>\ldots s(n-1)

is the length of

c_{1}(n+1)(a)\leq c_{1}(n)(a)
, for all
a\in B^{1}
,

(2)

if
c_{1}(n)(a)\neq 0
then
c_{1}(n+1)(a)<c_{1}(n)(a)
.

It follows that for every
a\in B^{1}
, there is
k
such that
c_{1}(k)(a)=0
.
Let
h_{1}^{\prime}(a)
be the least such
k
. This defines the height function on
B^{1}
.
The key point is that, for all
n
,

c_{1}(n)\restriction B^{0}=c_{0}(n+1).

To see this fix
a\in B^{0}
and some
s\in FS(\Gamma(\beta_{1}))
of length
n
.
Then
F_{1}(s)(a)=h_{t_{0}(s)}(a)
, and since the length of
t_{0}(s)
is
n+1
,
we have

h_{t_{0}(s)}(a)=c_{0}(n+1)(a).

It follows that if
a\in B^{0}
and
h^{\prime}_{0}(a)\neq 0
, then
h^{\prime}_{1}(a)=h^{\prime}_{0}(a)-1
.

Let
\lambda_{1}=e_{0}(e_{1}(\Gamma(\beta_{1})))
. Then
p_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}}\in H_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}}
.
Let

(1)

C^{0}_{1}=B^{0}_{1}\cup g_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}}^{-1}[B^{1}_{1}]
,

(2)

C^{1}_{1}=B^{1}_{1}\cup g_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}}[B^{0}_{1}]
,

(3)

g^{*}_{1}=g_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}}\restriction C^{0}_{1}
.

Finally, define the function
h^{*}_{1}:C^{0}_{1}\cup C^{1}_{1}\to\omega
by:

h^{*}_{0}(a)=\begin{cases}h^{\prime}_{1}(a)&\text{ if $a\in B^{0}_{1}\cup B^{1}_{1}$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Let
q_{1}=(\beta_{1},C^{0}_{1},C^{1}_{1},g^{*}_{1},h^{*}_{1})
and note that
q_{1}
is a legitimate response by Eve
to the second move played by the strategy
\sigma
in the small game.
Eve can continue playing in this way indefinitely; referring to the big game in order to decide what to play in the small game. Since Adam has to play a decreasing sequence of ordinals
\beta_{0}>\beta_{1}>\ldots
,
the game has to end after finitely many moves, and so Eve wins this run of the game.
Therefore,
\sigma
is not a winning strategy for Adam in the small game, a contradiction.

∎

We give some examples on properties that can or cannot be detected by our games.

4.1.
Cardinality

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
\mbox{cof}(\theta)>\omega
,

\alpha

be two structures in the empty vocabulary such that
|M_{0}|\geq\theta

Proof.

We describe a winning strategy for Adam.
We start by picking a subset

of size
\theta

Eve then plays some

Z_{0}=h_{0}^{-1}(0)
.
Eve also has to play an injective function
g_{0}:Z_{0}\to M_{1}
.
Note that
Z_{0}
has to be of size
<\theta
.
Since
{\rm cof}(\theta)>\omega
and
\alpha

\alpha_{0}<\alpha

such that

has cardinality
\theta
.
We then play
(\alpha_{0},\emptyset,\emptyset)

Eve responds by playing some

, and if

We find an ordinal

such that

\theta

At that point

R_{1}
has

∎

4.2.

Let
\mathscr{C}

\tau

\theta

and
\beta

\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}

Suppose

If

The following is straightforward.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
\lambda=\lambda^{\omega}
, then there is a model in

.
∎

4.7.
Prikry forcing and the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{<\kappa,\alpha}

Elementary equivalence in infinitary logic is closely related to isomorphism in a forcing extension. This is particularly evident in the case of
L_{\infty\omega}
where elementary equivalence is equivalent to isomorphism in
some
forcing extension. For stronger infinitary logics we have to limit ourselves to special classes of forcings and we do not in general get an equivalence as in the case of
L_{\infty\omega}
(
[
12
]
). Given two structures
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
in the same vocabulary, we write
\rm DG^{\beta}_{<\kappa,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})

for the variation of the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{\theta,\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}_{0},\mathfrak{M}_{1})
in which the two players play subsets of
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
of size
<\kappa

instead of subsets of size
\leq\theta
.
We demonstrate below a connection between the game
\rm DG^{\beta}_{<\kappa,\alpha}
and Prikry-forcing, introduced in
[
13
]
.

Let
\kappa
be a measurable cardinal and let
\mathcal{U}
be a normal ultrafilter on
\kappa
.
We recall the definition of Prikry forcing
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
.
Conditions in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
are pairs

, where
s_{p}
is a finite increasing sequence of elements of
\kappa
and

is an element of
\mathcal{U}
with
\max(s_{p})<\min(Y_{p})
.
We will simultaneously think of
s_{p}
either as a finite increasing sequence and as a finite set which is the range of the sequence.
We say that a condition

and write

if:

(1)

is an initial segment of
s_{q}
,

(2)

Y_{q}\subseteq Y_{p}
,

(3)

s_{q}\setminus s_{p}\subseteq Y_{p}
.

We say that a condition

and

.
Given a generic filter

is a cofinal
\omega
-sequence in

.
It is well known that
\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{U}}
has the

-c.c. and does not add new bounded subsets of

Moreover if

5.

Let
\theta

\alpha

\beta

Recall that for two structures

This relation is reflexive and symmetric by definition, but we do not know if it is transitive.

Definition 5.1

\alpha
and
\beta

Remark 5.2
.

Note that if

,
\theta^{\prime}\geq\theta
and
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha

Definition 5.3
.

\kappa
is a cardinal and
\alpha
is an ordinal. For any signature
\tau

(1)

\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}
-sentence is a class of
\tau_{0}
-structures closed under

be the least

\mathfrak{M}\models\phi\hskip 8.53581pt\mbox{ if and only if }\hskip 8.53581pt\mathfrak{M}\restriction\tau_{\phi}\in\phi.

B^{n+1}_{0}\in[N]^{\leq\theta}
, and
B^{n+1}_{1}\in[M]^{\leq\theta}

Eve finds some

such that

and

.
Let
A^{n+1}_{0}={\rm dom}(g_{n+1})
. We may assume that
A^{n+1}_{0}
is a substructure of
\mathfrak{N}
.
Let
A^{n+1}_{1}=g_{n+1}[A^{n+1}_{0}]\cup B^{n+1}_{1}
.
Observe that if
x\in A^{n+1}_{1}
and

then

.
Let
\bar{a}_{n+1}
be a sequence enumerating

and let

be such that

\bar{a}_{n}\sqsubseteq_{f_{n}}\bar{a}_{n+1}
. Let
\bar{b}_{n+1}
be defined by letting

\bar{b}_{n+1}(\xi)=g_{n+1}(\bar{a}_{n+1}(\xi))
, for all

. Then we have that
\bar{b}_{n}\sqsubseteq_{f_{n}}\bar{b}_{n+1}
.
We also have that:

In her next move Eve plays
(\beta_{n+1},A^{n+1}_{0},A^{n+1}_{1},g_{n+1},h_{n+1})
.
This completes the inductive step and finishes the proof of the lemma.

In

]
Shelah gave a Lindström type characterization of his logic
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
,
for

which is a fixed point of the

This property roughly says that if a sentence

-submodel relation.

Lemma 6.4

(The SUDWO
κ
Lemma)
.

Let
\kappa
be a strong limit cardinal. Assume
\mathscr{L}
is a regular logic which has the
\phi
-submodel relation
with the Union Property and the Löwenheim-Skolem number strictly below
\kappa
, for every sentence
\phi
.
Let
\tau
be a vocabulary of cardinality
<\kappa
containing the symbol
\in
, and a unary predicate symbol
K
.
Suppose, for some
\lambda\geq\kappa
, a sentence
\phi\in\mathscr{L}[\tau]
has a model of the form

\mathfrak{A}=(H(\lambda),\in,\kappa,\ldots)

where the interpretation of
\in
is the standard
\in
-relation on
H(\lambda)
and the predicate
K
is interpreted as the set
\kappa
. Then
\phi
has a model of the form

\mathfrak{B}=(B,\in^{\mathfrak{B}},K^{\mathfrak{B}},\ldots)

with some
b\in K^{\mathfrak{B}}
and a sequence
\{B_{n}:n<\omega\}
of elements of

such that:

(1)

\in^{\mathfrak{B}}
is ill-founded below

,

(2)

for all

,

\mathfrak{B}\models|B_{n}|\leq b
, for all

Proof.

Suppose
\phi
is an
\mathscr{L}[\tau]
-sentence which has a model of the form

\mathfrak{A}=(H(\lambda),\in,\kappa,\ldots).

Since
\mathscr{L}_{\omega,\omega}\leq\mathscr{L}
we may assume that
\phi
implies a sufficient (first order) fragment
of
{\rm ZFC}^{-}
. Let
\theta<\kappa
be the Löwenheim-Skolem number of
\phi
. We must have that
|\tau|\leq\theta
.
We recursively models
(P_{n},\ldots)\preceq_{\phi}\mathfrak{A}
of size at most

together with

e_{n}:P_{n}\to P_{n+1}
such that
e_{n}[P_{n}]\preceq_{\phi}P_{n+1}
. Moreover, for each
n
there is a distinguished element
c_{n}\in P_{n}

such that
c_{n+1}\in e_{n}(c_{n})
, and
e_{n}[P_{n}]\in P_{n+1}
, for all
n
.
We will then take the direct limit of this system which will give us the desired model
\mathfrak{B}
.

Step
0
. For every
\beta\in(2^{\theta})^{+}\setminus\theta
,
we choose a model

of size
\leq\theta
such that
\beta\in B^{0}_{\beta}
.
There are at most

,
so we can fix an unbounded subset

of
(2^{\theta})^{+}
such that for all

. Thus, for every
\beta\in X_{0}

there is an isomorphism of

to
P_{0}
which maps
\beta
to
c_{0}
.

Step
1
. For each
\beta\in(2^{\theta})^{+}\setminus\theta
pick some
x_{0}(\beta)\in X_{0}
above
\beta
,
and then apply the Löwenheim-Skolem Property to choose some

of size
\leq\theta

such that

B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)}\cup\{\beta\}\cup\{B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)}\}\subseteq B^{1}_{\beta}.

By the Tarski-Vaught Property we have that
B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)}\preceq_{\phi}B^{1}_{\beta}
.
Consider the structures
(B^{1}_{\beta},\in,\beta,B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)},\ldots)

By the Pigeon Hole Principle we can find an unbounded subset
X_{1}
of
(2^{\theta})^{+}
such that, for all
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in X_{1}

there is an isomorphism

(B^{1}_{\beta},\in,\beta,B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)},\ldots)\simeq(B^{1}_{\beta^{\prime}},\in,\beta^{\prime},B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta^{\prime})},\ldots).

which maps
\beta\mapsto\beta^{\prime}
and
B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta)}\mapsto B^{0}_{x_{0}(\beta^{\prime})}
.
Let
(P_{1},\in,c_{1},R_{0},\ldots)
be a representative of this isomorphism class.
Note that
P_{1}\models|R_{0}|\leq c_{1}
.
Moreover, we have an isomorphic embedding

such that
e_{0}[P_{0}]=R_{0}
,
and

. By Isomorphism Correctness it follows that
(R_{0},\in,\ldots)\preceq_{\phi}(P_{1},\in,\ldots)
.

Step

. Suppose

and we have done the construction up to

.

(P_{n},\in,c_{n},R_{n-1},\ldots)
of size
\leq\theta
, and an unbounded subset

of

\beta\in X_{n}
there is an isomorphic copy of

and

.

and an unbounded subset

of
(2^{\theta})^{+}\setminus\theta
,
such that for every

\beta<x_{n}(\beta)

(B^{n+1}_{\beta},\in,\beta,B^{n}_{x_{n}(\beta)},\ldots)\simeq(P_{n+1},\in,c_{n+1},R_{n},\ldots).

Then

must be isomorphic to

be the unique isomorphism.
Finally, since

(R_{n},\in,\ldots)\preceq_{\phi}(P_{n+1},\in,\ldots).

Suppose we have completed the construction for all

.
We know that if

and

.
We also have elements

satisfying the predicate

, such that

, for each

.
Finally, we have that
P_{n+1}\models|R_{n}|\leq c_{n+1}
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}=(B,\in^{\mathfrak{B}},\ldots)
be the direct limit of this system. The interpretation of the symbol
\in
is now
some binary relation
\in^{\mathfrak{B}}
on
B
. Let

be the limit embedding, for all

.
Let

, and let

,

, for all

.
Hence the relation

is ill-founded below

.
Moreover,
P_{n+1}\models|R_{n}|\leq c_{n}

.
Every element

of

is of the form

, for some

and some

.
Note that then

and hence

and

.

It remains to check that
\mathfrak{B}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
.
First note that
P_{n}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
, for all

, since
P_{n}
is isomorphic to
B^{n}_{\beta}
, for any
\beta\in X_{n}
.
We already observed that
R_{n}\preceq_{\phi}P_{n+1}
, for all
n
.
By the Isomorphism Correctness we must have
B_{n}\preceq_{\phi}B_{n+1}
, for all
n
, as well.
Finally, by the Union Property, we have that
B
and
B_{n}
agree on
\phi
, for any
n
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{B}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi
, as required.

Let
\mathscr{L}

\mathcal{K}

\sigma

\mathfrak{N}

\tau

Recall that a class

Lemma 6.5

(The Separation Lemma)
.

Suppose
\kappa
is a cardinal such that
\kappa=\beth_{\kappa}
and
\alpha<\kappa
is an ordinal.
Let
\mathscr{L}

\kappa

{\rm SUDWO}_{\kappa}
Lemma and
such that
\mathscr{L}_{\delta,\omega}\leq\mathscr{L}

\delta<\kappa
.
Let
\mathcal{K}_{0}

\mathcal{K}_{1}

\mathscr{L}

\mathcal{K}_{0}

\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}

Proof.

Fix a unary predicate symbol
P\notin\tau
and a vocabulary
\sigma\supseteq\tau\cup\{P\}

of size
<\kappa

such that for some
\mathscr{L}[\sigma]

\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
, for

,

\mathcal{K}_{i}=\{\mathfrak{M}\restriction(P,\tau):\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi_{i}\}.

\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}[\tau]
-sentence
\psi

\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}}(\psi)\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{K}_{1}\cap{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}_{\kappa,\alpha}}(\psi)=\emptyset.

\sigma

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,0}

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,1}

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,0}\restriction(P,\tau)\equiv^{\beta}_{|\beta|,\alpha}\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,1}\restriction(P,\tau).

\mathfrak{N}_{\beta}^{0},\ldots,\mathfrak{N}_{\beta}^{k}

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,0}\restriction(P,\tau)=\mathfrak{N}_{\beta}^{0}

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,1}\restriction(P,\tau)=\mathfrak{N}_{\beta}^{k}

\gamma
be the natural sum of
\omega

\alpha

By Proposition

\rm DG^{\beta}_{|\beta|,\gamma}(\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,0}\restriction(P,\tau),\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,1}\restriction(P,\tau)).

Let
\lambda

We now define a structure

\mathfrak{A}=(H(\lambda),\in,\kappa,\ldots)

\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,0}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{\beta,1}

We first introduce a unary symbol

and interpret it as
\kappa
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}
be ternary relation symbols.
We define their interpretations as follows:

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathfrak{A}}(\beta,i,a)\hskip 2.84526pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 2.84526pt\beta\in\kappa,i\in\{0,1\},\mbox{ and }a\in M_{\beta,i}.

We introduce symbols

\dot{\sigma}

(1)

\forall x[x\in\dot{\sigma}\longleftrightarrow\bigvee_{s\in\sigma}x=s]

a finite fragment of

\mathscr{L}_{\delta^{+},\omega}\leq\mathscr{L}

\Phi

\mathscr{L}

\Phi

\mathfrak{A}

has a model of the form:

\mathfrak{B}=(B,\in^{\mathfrak{B}},K^{\mathfrak{B}},\ldots)

with some

B

(1)

\in^{\mathfrak{B}}

,

(2)

for all

\mathfrak{B}\models|B_{n}|\leq b
, for all

.

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathfrak{B}}_{b,i}

\mathfrak{B}
.
They can be seen as
\sigma
-structures. We then have

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathfrak{B}}_{b,i}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi_{i},\mbox{ for }i=0,1.

\mathfrak{P}_{i}=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathfrak{B}}_{b,i}\restriction(P,\tau)

Then the

\tau

\mathfrak{B}\models E^{b}_{|b|,\dot{\gamma}}(\mathfrak{P}_{0},\mathfrak{P}_{1}).

\in^{\mathfrak{B}}

\Sigma

\mathfrak{B}

and
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
.

We also have Craig’s interpolation theorem for the logic
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}
, for
\kappa
such that
\kappa=\beth_{\kappa}
.
Suppose
\mathscr{L}
is a logic,
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
are
\mathscr{L}
-sentences in vocabularies
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
.
We write

\phi_{0}\vdash\phi_{1}

if, for every
(\tau_{0}\cup\tau_{1})
-structure
\mathfrak{M}
,
if
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi_{0}
then
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathscr{L}}\phi_{1}
.

Theorem 6.7

(Craig Interpolation Theorem)
.

Suppose
\kappa=\beth_{\kappa}
. Suppose
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
are vocabularies and let
\tau=\tau_{0}\cap\tau_{1}
.
Let
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}[\tau_{0}]
and
\phi_{1}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}[\tau_{1}]
.
Suppose
\phi_{0}\vdash\phi_{1}
. Then there is
\psi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}[\tau]

such that
\phi_{0}\vdash\psi
and
\psi\vdash\phi_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{0}
the class of
\tau
-structures that have a
\tau_{0}
-expansion which satisfies
\phi_{0}

and let
\mathcal{K}_{1}
be the class of
\tau
-structures that have a
\tau_{1}
-expansion that satisfies
\neg\phi_{1}
.
By our assumption
\mathcal{K}_{0}
and
\mathcal{K}_{1}
disjoint.
By Lemma
6.5
there is a sentence
\psi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}[\tau]

such that

\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}}(\psi)\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{K}_{1}\cap{\rm Mod}^{\tau}_{\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\kappa}}(\psi)=\emptyset.

It follows that
\phi_{0}\vdash\psi
and
\psi\vdash\phi_{1}
, as required.

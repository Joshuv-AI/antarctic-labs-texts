Free fermions and Schur expansions of multi-Schur functions
Shinsuke Iwao

Abstract
. Multi-Schur functions are symmetric functions that generalize the supersymmetric Schur functions, the flagged Schur functions, and the refined dual Grothendieck functions, which have been intensively studied by Lascoux. In this paper, we give a new free-fermionic presentation of them. The multi-Schur functions are indexed by a partition and two “tuples of tuples” of indeterminates. We construct a family of linear bases of the fermionic Fock space that are indexed by such data and prove that they correspond to the multi-Schur functions through the boson-fermion correspondence. By focusing on some special bases, which we call refined bases, we give a straightforward method of expanding a multi-Schur function in the refined dual Grothendieck polynomials. We also present a sufficient condition for a multi-Schur function to have its Hall-dual function in the completed ring of symmetric functions. Keywords. multi-Schur functions, free fermions, boson-fermion correspondence, MSC Classification. 05E05, 13M10

In the sequel, we fix the following four tuples of tuples

{\bf x}=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\dots),\ {\bf y}=(y^{(1)},y^{(2)},\dots),\ {\bf p}=(p^{(1)},p^{(2)},\dots),\ {\bf q}=(q^{(1)},q^{(2)},\dots).

Suppose that all of the indeterminates here are contained in the base field
k
.

3.1.
The symmetric function
s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}

Let
\Lambda(X)
be the
k
-algebra of symmetric functions in
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
.
There exists a nondegenerate
k
-bilinear form

\Lambda(X)\otimes_{k}\Lambda(X)\to k;\quad f\otimes g\mapsto\langle f,g\rangle\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu},

that is called the Hall inner product.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{|\lambda\rangle_{1}\}_{\lambda}
and
\{|\lambda\rangle_{2}\}_{\lambda}
be two
k
-basis of
\mathcal{F}^{0}
labeled by all partitions
\lambda
(possibly
\lambda=\emptyset
).
Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

(i)

If
f_{\lambda}(X)=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle_{1}
and
g_{\mu}(X)=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\mu\rangle_{2}
, then
\langle f_{\lambda},g_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}
.

(ii)

\sum_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(X)g_{\lambda}(Y)=\prod_{i,j}(1-X_{i}Y_{j})^{-1}
.

(iii)

If
(|\mu\rangle_{2})^{\ast}={}_{2}\langle\mu|
, then
{}_{2}\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{1}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}
.

Proof.

See
[
18
, I.§4]
for proofs.
∎

For a partition
\lambda
and an integer
r\geq\ell(\lambda)
, let

(24)

|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}:=\left(e^{H(x^{(1)}/y^{(1)})}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{-H(x^{(1)}/y^{(1)})}\right)\left(e^{H(x^{(2)}/y^{(2)})}\psi_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{-H(x^{(2)}/y^{(2)})}\right)\\
\cdots\left(e^{H(x^{(r)}/y^{(r)})}\psi_{\lambda_{r}-r}e^{-H(x^{(r)}/y^{(r)})}\right)|-r\rangle.

The definition (
24
) does not depend on the choice of
r
because

e^{H(x/y)}\psi_{-s}e^{-H(x/y)}|-s\rangle=\psi_{-s}|-s\rangle=|-s-1\rangle.

Since the vector
|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}
is expanded as

|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=|\lambda\rangle+\sum_{\left|{\mu}\right|<\left|{\lambda}\right|}\alpha_{\mu}|\mu\rangle,\qquad\alpha_{\mu}\in k,

the set
\{|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}\}_{\lambda}
forms a
k
-basis of
\mathcal{F}^{0}
.

Define the new symmetric function
s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}\in\Lambda(X)
by

(25)

s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}:=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}.

When
X_{i}=0
for all
i
,
s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}
reduces to the multi-Schur function
S_{\lambda}({\bf x}/{\bf y})
.

3.2.
Refined bases and orthonormality

What we are interested in is the transition matrix between the two bases
\{|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}\}
and
\{|\mu\rangle_{{\bf p}/{\bf q}}\}
of
\mathcal{F}^{0}
.
Unfortunately, we have no explicit formula for general bases at this stage. Instead, we present a certain subclass of bases whose transition matrices are directly calculated from our fermionic presentation.

Set
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
.
We consider the refined tuple
[{\bf t}]=(t[1],t[2],\dots)
, which we have defined in (
1
).
When
{\bf x}=[{\bf t}]
and
{\bf y}=\mbox{\boldmath{$\emptyset$}}
, the vector
|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}
(
24
) reduces to

(26)

\begin{split}|\lambda\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}=\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H(t_{1})}\psi_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{H(t_{2})}\cdots e^{H(t_{r-1})}\psi_{\lambda_{r}-r}e^{H(t_{r})}|-r\rangle.\end{split}

To discuss the “orthonormality” property, we also define the transposed vector

(27)

{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\lambda|:=\langle-r|e^{-H(t_{r})}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{r}-r}e^{-H(t_{r-1})}\cdots e^{-H(t_{2})}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{1}-1}

for sufficiently large
r
.
The definition (
27
) may seem to depend on the choice of
r
.
However, we will prove later that it is in fact independent of the choice of
r
when
r
is sufficiently large.
See Remark
4.8
.

The following is the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 3.2

(Orthonormality of refined basis)
.

We have

{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
.
We here note the equations

(28)

\displaystyle e^{-H(t_{i})}\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i}e^{H(t_{i})}=\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i}-t_{i}\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i-1},

(29)

\displaystyle e^{-H(t_{i})}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{i}-i}e^{H(t_{i})}=\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{i}-i}+t_{i}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{i}-i+1}+t_{i}^{2}\psi^{\ast}_{\lambda_{i}-i+2}+\cdots,

which are obtained from (
17
–
18
).

Let
0\leq s\leq r
.
We temporally use the notation

\begin{split}|\lambda,s\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}=\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H(t_{1})}\psi_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{H(t_{2})}\cdots\psi_{\lambda_{s}-s}e^{H(t_{s})}|-r\rangle\end{split}

and prove the theorem by induction on
s
.
The theorem follows from the three facts:

(I).

If
N\geq\lambda_{1}
, then
\psi^{\ast}_{N}|\lambda,s\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}=0
.

(II).

If
N\geq\mu_{1}
, then
{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu,s|\psi_{N}=0
.

(III).

If
\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}
,
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{2}-1,\dots,\lambda_{r}-1)
,
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{2}-1,\dots,\mu_{r}-1)
, and
{\bf t}^{\prime}=(t_{2},t_{3},\dots)
.
Then

{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu,s|\lambda,s\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}={}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}.

(I) follows from the fact that
|\lambda,s\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}
is a linear combination of vectors of the form
\psi_{n_{1}}\psi_{n_{2}}\dots\psi_{n_{s}}|-r\rangle
with
N>n_{i}
for each
i
.
We prove (II) by induction on
s
.
If
s=0
, (II) is obvious.
For general
s>0
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\lambda,s|\psi_{N}

\displaystyle={}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\lambda^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1}\psi_{N}

\displaystyle=-{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\lambda^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi_{N}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=-{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\lambda^{\prime},s-1|(\psi_{N}-t_{1}\psi_{N-1})e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1}.

By induction hypothesis, the last expression should be zero, which concludes (II).
(III) is proved as

\displaystyle{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu,s|\lambda,s\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}

\displaystyle={}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H(t_{1})}|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle={}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}(1-\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1})e^{H(t_{1})}|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle={}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}-{}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1}e^{H(t_{1})}|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle={}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t_{1}^{m}\cdot{}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|e^{-H(t_{1})}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H(t_{1})}\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{1}-1+m}|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(I)}}}{{=}}{}_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}\langle\mu^{\prime},s-1|\lambda^{\prime},s-1\rangle_{[{\bf t}^{\prime}]}.

By using (I–III) repeatedly, we conclude the proof.
∎

Theorem
3.2
provides a straightforward method for giving the linear expansion
|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=\sum_{\mu}C_{\lambda}^{\mu}|\mu\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}
.
By using the orthonormality, we obtain the simple formula

(30)

C_{\lambda}^{\mu}={}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}},

which implies the generalized Schur expansion

(31)

s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=\sum_{\mu}{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}\cdot s_{\mu}(X)_{[{\bf t}]}.

Example 3.3

(Schur expansion of
s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}
)
.

If
t=(0,0,0,\dots)
, then
|\lambda\rangle_{[{\bf t}]}=|\lambda\rangle
.
Assuming
r\geq\max[\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu)]
, we obtain the determinantial formula

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\langle{\mu}|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}

\displaystyle=\det\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}h_{l}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)})\cdot\langle-r|\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{j}-j}\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i-l}|-r\rangle\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}

\displaystyle=\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}.

Since
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>\ell(\lambda)
and
h_{n}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)})=0
for
n<0
, it follows that
\mu\not\subset\lambda\Rightarrow\langle{\mu}|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=0
.
This fact gives the Schur expansion

(32)

s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}s_{\mu}(X).

Example 3.4

(Schur expansion of refined dual Grothendieck functions)
.

Let
r\geq\ell(\lambda)
.
By substituting
{\bf x}=[{\bf t}]
and
{\bf y}=\mbox{\boldmath{$\emptyset$}}
to (
32
), we have the Schur expansion

g_{\lambda}(X;t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1})=\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}s_{\mu}(X).

Remark 3.5
.

The function

S_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf x}/{\bf y}):=\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)})\right)_{i,j}

is called the skew multi-Schur function
[
14
, §1.4]
.

Example 3.6

(Expansion of
s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}
in refined dual Grothendieck functions)
.

Let
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
and
r\geq\max[\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu)]
.
Then we have

\displaystyle{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle{\mu}|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}

\displaystyle=\det\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}h_{m}(t_{j},t_{j+1},\dots,t_{r})\cdot h_{l}(x^{(i)}/y^{(i)}\cup(t_{1},\dots,t_{r}))\cdot\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 180.00027pt\left.\langle-r|\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{j}-j+m}\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i-l}|-r\rangle\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}

\displaystyle=\det\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+m=\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j\\
0\leq l\leq\lambda_{i}-i+r\end{subarray}}h_{m}(t_{j},t_{j+1},\dots,t_{r})\cdot h_{l}(x^{(i)}/(y^{(i)}\cup(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})))\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}

\displaystyle=\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\left(x^{(i)}/(y^{(i)}\cup(t_{1},\dots,t_{j-1}))\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r},

which implies
\mu\not\subset\lambda\Rightarrow{}_{[{\bf t}]}\langle{\mu}|\lambda\rangle_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=0
.
Finally, we have the generalized Schur expansion

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X)_{{\bf x}/{\bf y}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}\det\left(h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\left(x^{(i)}/(y^{(i)}\cup(t_{1},\dots,t_{j-1}))\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}\cdot g_{\mu}(X;t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}).

4.
Dual symmetric functions

This section aims to study the dual functions of multi-Schur functions.
Unfortunately, since
\Lambda(X)
is infinite-dimensional, not every linear basis has its dual in
\Lambda(X)
.
Thus we often need to use the extended ring
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
(see
[
7
]
, for example) to obtain desired dual functions.
As the ring
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
has a linear topology and is complete, an element of
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
can be specified by giving a convergent sequence of symmetric functions.

4.1.
r
-truncated dual function
s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{\bf x}

We will use the “dual operator”

H^{\ast}(x/y):=\sum_{n>0}\frac{p_{n}(x/y)}{n}a_{-n}

to define dual functions.
However, since the sum

e^{-H^{\ast}(x/y)}\psi_{n}e^{H^{\ast}(x/y)}=\psi_{n}-e_{1}(x/y)\psi_{n+1}+e_{2}(x/y)\psi_{n+2}+\cdots

is finite only if
y=\emptyset
, we should assume
y
to be empty to have a well-defined linear map from
\mathcal{F}
to itself.

For
r\geq\ell(\lambda)
, set

(33)

\begin{split}|\lambda\rangle_{r}^{{\bf x}}:=\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(1)})}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(1)})}\right)\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(2)})}\psi_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(2)})}\right)\\
\cdots\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(r)})}\psi_{\lambda_{r}-r}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(r)})}\right)|-r\rangle.\end{split}

Since all the expressions on the right hand side of (
33
) are finite sums, the
|\lambda\rangle_{r}^{{\bf x}}
is contained in the Fock space
\mathcal{F}
.
Let us define the new symmetric function

s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}}:=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle_{r}^{{\bf x}}.

We call
s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}}
the
r
-truncated dual function
(see
[
7
, Section 3]
).

Example 4.1

(Truncated Grothendieck function)
.

Let
t=(-\beta,-\beta,\dots)
.
Then
s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{[{\bf t}]}
coincides with the
r
-truncated Grothendieck function

G^{r}_{\lambda}(X)
introduced in
[
8
, §3.1]
.
Even if
r
tends to be sufficiently large, the sequence of
r
-truncated Grothendieck functions does not stable:
G^{r}_{\lambda}(X)\neq G^{r+1}_{\lambda}(X)\neq G^{r+2}_{\lambda}(X)\neq\cdots
.
Instead, the sequence converges to some element in the topological ring
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
.
The limit
G_{\lambda}(X):=\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}G_{\lambda}^{r}(X)
is called the stable Grothendieck polynomial.

Example 4.2

(Schur expansion of
s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}}
)
.

For
{\bf x}=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\dots)
, let

\overline{x}^{(i)}=\begin{cases}x^{(i)},&1\leq i\leq r,\\
\emptyset,&i>r\end{cases}

be a “
r
-truncated” tuple.
For any
R\geq\max[\ell(\mu),r]
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{r}^{{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=\det\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}e_{m}(\overline{x}^{(i)})\langle-R|\psi^{\ast}_{\mu_{j}-j}\psi_{\lambda_{i}-i+m}|-R\rangle\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq R}

\displaystyle=\det\left(e_{-\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}+i-j}(-\overline{x}^{(i)})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq R}.

Because
e_{n}(\emptyset)=\delta_{n,0}
and
\lambda_{i}=0

(i>r)
, the determinant
\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle_{r}^{{\bf x}}
must vanish if
\ell(\mu)>r
.
Hence we have the Schur expansion

s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\supset\lambda\\
\ell(\mu)\leq r\end{subarray}}\det\left(e_{-\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}+i-j}(-x^{(i)})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}s_{\mu}(X).

Example 4.3

(Schur expansion of
s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{[{\bf t}]}
)
.

Assume
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
and
r\geq\ell(\lambda)
.
We call
G^{r}_{\lambda}(X;t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}):=s^{r}_{\lambda}(X)^{[{\bf t}]}
the
r
-truncated refined Grothendieck function.
From Example
4.2
, we have the Schur expansion

G^{r}_{\lambda}(X;t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\supset\lambda\\
\ell(\mu)\leq r\end{subarray}}\det\left(e_{-\lambda_{i}+\mu_{j}+i-j}(-t_{1},\dots,-t_{i-1})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}s_{\mu}(X).

4.2.
r
-th stable dual function
s^{[r]}_{\lambda}(X)^{\bf x}

Seeing Example
4.1
, one might expect that the sequence

(34)

s^{1}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}},s^{2}_{\lambda}(X)^{{\bf x}},\dots

converges in
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
, but this is not the case for general
{\bf x}
.
Here we do not go into details of the topology of
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
but just recall the following fact:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f_{1}(X),f_{2}(X),\dots\in\Lambda(X)
be a sequence of symmetric functions.
Then the sequence converges in
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
if for any
n>0
there exists some
M>0
such that

i,j>M\quad\Rightarrow\quad f_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},0,0,\dots)=f_{j}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},0,0,\dots).

Proof.

See
[
7
, §3.1]
.
∎

For
r\geq\ell(\lambda)
, we define the vector

(35)

\begin{split}|\lambda\rangle^{{\bf x}}_{[r]}:=\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(1)})}\psi_{\lambda_{1}-1}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(1)})}\right)\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(2)})}\psi_{\lambda_{2}-2}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(2)})}\right)\\
\cdots\left(e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(r)})}\psi_{\lambda_{r}-r}e^{H^{\ast}(x^{(r)})}\right)e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(r+1)})}|-r\rangle,\end{split}

which “approximates”
|\lambda\rangle^{\bf x}_{r}
.
Note that
|\lambda\rangle^{\bf x}_{r}
and
|\lambda\rangle^{\bf x}_{[r]}
are indeed different since
e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(r+1)})}|-r\rangle\neq|-r\rangle
in general.
Furthermore,
|\lambda\rangle^{{\bf x}}_{[r]}
is
not always
an element of
\mathcal{F}
because
e^{-H^{\ast}(x^{(r+1)})}|-r\rangle
may not be a finite sum of vectors (

).

Let us introduce the “formal” symmetric function

s^{[r]}_{\lambda}(X)^{\bf x}:=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle_{[r]}^{\bf x}.

In the sequel, we prove that
s^{[r]}_{\lambda}(X)^{\bf x}
is an element of
\widehat{\Lambda}(X)
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
7
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})
be a tuple of
p
indeterminates.
Suppose
r>0
.
Then there exists a certain sequence
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{A}
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The vector
e^{-H^{\ast}(x)}|-r\rangle
is formally expanded as

e^{-H^{\ast}(x)}|-r\rangle=|-r\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}Y_{i}|-r-i\rangle.

(2)

For any

, the vector
\psi_{m_{1}}\dots\psi_{m_{r}}Y_{i}|-r-i\rangle
is contained in
\mathcal{F}^{0}_{(r+i+1)}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{0}_{(r+i)}
.

(3)

The element
\psi_{-r+p-1}\cdots\psi_{-r+1}\psi_{-r}Y_{i}

Proof.

The proof is a variant of
[

Z_{r}:=\sum_{l=1}^{p}(-1)^{l}e_{l}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\psi_{-r+l}

By using this, we can prove that

[

.

By using Lemma

(36)

\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle^{{\bf x}}_{[r]}=\langle 0|e^{H(X)}|\lambda\rangle^{{\bf x}}_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle 0|e^{H(X)}EY_{i}|-r-i\rangle,

where

Applying Lemma

(2) to (

.
Moreover, we have

We call

the

-th stable dual function
.

4.3.
Stable dual function
s_{\lambda}(X)^{\bf x}

Next, we consider letting
r
tend to infinity.

Lemma 4.6

(See
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{p})
be a tuple.
For
n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p>0
, define

I_{p}=\psi_{n}e^{-H^{\ast}(t_{1})}\psi_{n-1}e^{-H^{\ast}(t_{2})}\cdots\psi_{n-p+1}e^{-H^{\ast}(t_{p})}.

Then we have

(1)

I_{p}=\psi_{n}\psi_{n-1}\cdots\psi_{n-p+1}e^{-H^{\ast}(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})}
,

(2)

I_{p}|n-p+1\rangle=|n+1\rangle
.

Proof.

We prove (1) by induction on
p>0
.
For
p=1
, the equation is obvious.
For general
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{p+1}

\displaystyle=I_{p}\psi_{n-p}e^{-H^{\ast}(t_{p+1})}

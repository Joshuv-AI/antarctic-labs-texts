Frobenius Monoidal Functors of Dijkgraaf–Witten Categories and Rigid Frobenius Algebras
Samuel HANNAH a, Robert LAUGWITZ b and Ana ROS CAMACHO a

Abstract
We construct a separable Frobenius monoidal functor from 𝒵 ⁡ ( 𝖵𝖾𝖼𝗍 H ω | H ) \mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega|_{H}}\big) to 𝒵 ⁡ ( 𝖵𝖾𝖼𝗍 G ω ) \mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big) for any subgroup H H of G G which preserves braiding and ribbon structure. As an application, we classify rigid Frobenius algebras in 𝒵 ⁡ ( 𝖵𝖾𝖼𝗍 G ω ) \mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big) , recovering the classification of étale algebras in these categories by Davydov–Simmons [ J. Algebra 471 (2017), 149–175, arXiv:1603.04650] and generalizing their classification to algebraically closed fields of arbitrary characteristic. Categories of local modules over such algebras are modular tensor categories by results of Kirillov–Ostrik [ Adv. Math. 171 (2002), 183–227, arXiv:math.QA/0101219] in the semisimple case and Laugwitz–Walton [ Comm. Math. Phys. , to appear, arXiv:2202.08644] in the general case.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Algebraic structures play an important role in the study of conformal field theory (CFT) and topological field theory (TFT). A key structure in these applications are
modular categories
, i.e., non-degenerate ribbon categories
[
25
,
42
]
. In rational CFT, modular fusion categories appear as categories of representations over a vertex operator algebra (VOA)
[
22
]
while modular fusion categories are utilized to construct 3d TFTs of surgery type
[
39
,
44
]
, and appear in the classification of 3d TFTs
[
2
]
.

Generalizations of part of the theory and applications of modular fusion categories to low-dimensional topology have been obtained for non-semisimple (i.e., not necessarily semisimple) modular categories. These constructions include equivalent characterizations of modularity conditions
[
42
]
, mapping class group actions and modular functors
[
19
,
29
,
41
]
, and partially defined non-semisimple TFTs
[
10
,
25
]
.
In general, it is still open whether the non-semisimple braided categories of representations of a logarithmic conformal field theories are modular
[
24
,
30
]
. A first example of modular categories obtained from groups are the
Dijkgraaf–Witten
(
DW
)
categories

\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
associated to a finite group
G
and a
3
-cocycle
\omega
on
G

[
11
]
. These categories are only semisimple if the characteristic of
\Bbbk
does not divide
|G|
and are equivalent to representations of certain lattice VOAs.

In this paper, we focus on the study of (Frobenius) algebras in modular categories. On the one hand, modules over such algebras describe boundary conditions of the associated rational CFT associated to a certain VOA
[
17
,
18
]
. On the other hand, given a VOA, its possible extensions are in a one-to-one correspondence with commutative algebras in its category of representations
[
23
]
. This result extends to vertex operator superalgebras
[
6
]
. These results give us motivation for classifying algebra objects in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
. Many categories of representations of a VOA are pointed fusion categories, like the case of, e.g., lattice VOAs coming from an even, integral lattice (here,
G=\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda
is the discriminant form of the lattice, note that this
G
is abelian)
[
13
,
30
]
. In this sense, an important family of vertex operator algebras are the holomorphic ones, those whose category of representations is simply
\mathsf{Vect}
. Given a certain group
G
, one can take the so-called orbifold of a holomorphic VOA, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
33
]
. Its category of representations will be then equivalent to
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)

[
32
]
.

Given a commutative algebra
A
in a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
one defines a braided tensor category
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
of
local modules

[
26
,
28
,
37
,
40
]
. Such categories of local modules have been of particular interest in the mathematical physics literature, see, e.g.,
[
17
,
18
]
.
For instance, categories of local modules relate the representations of a VOA to those of its extensions
[
6
,
23
,
26
]
. Given a
rigid Frobenius algebra
(i.e., a connected commutative special Frobenius algebra) in
\mathcal{C}
, it was shown that the rigid monoidal category
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
of local modules is again modular (see
[
26
]
in the semisimple case, and
[
28
]
in the general case). Such rigid Frobenius algebras were classified for the semisimplification of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-modules
[
26
]
, for the Drinfeld center of modules over a finite group
[
7
,
28
]
, and for DW categories
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{\Bbbk G}^{\omega}\big)
in
\operatorname{char}\Bbbk=0

[
9
]
.

In the present paper, we construct Frobenius monoidal functors. Given two monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, a Frobenius monoidal functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
comes with a choice of natural transformations

\displaystyle\mu_{V,W}\colon\ F(V)\otimes F(W)\to F(V\otimes W),\qquad\nu_{V,W}\colon\ F(V\otimes W)\to F(V)\otimes F(W),

which make
F
a lax and oplax monoidal functor and satisfy compatibility conditions which are analogue to those of a product and coproduct of a Frobenius algebra. While any monoidal functor is, in particular, Frobenius monoidal, for general Frobenius monoidal functors, like those considered in this paper,
F(V)\otimes F(W)
and
F(V\otimes W)
are not isomorphic. However, any Frobenius monoidal functor sends Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{C}
to Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{D}
.
Frobenius monoidal functors have recently appeared in different contexts in the quantum algebra literature, see, e.g.,
[
16
,
34
,
47
]
. Here, we construct Frobenius monoidal functors to categories of the form
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
. These functors are separable, so that
F(V\otimes W)
is naturally a direct summand of
F(V)\otimes F(W)
, and compatible with braidings whence they preserve connected commutative Frobenius algebras. We apply these functors to classify rigid Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
for a field of arbitrary characteristic.

Algebra objects in
\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}
were classified up to equivalence of the associated
\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}
-module categories and representatives are given by twisted group algebras
A(N,\kappa)
associated to a normal subgroup
N
and a
2
-cocycle
\kappa
such that
{\rm d}\kappa=\omega|_{N}

[
35
,
36
]
, see also
[
34
]
for explicit Frobenius algebra structures on these algebras. In this paper we find conditions for the existence of lifts of these twisted group algebras to rigid Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
in terms of homological algebra data building on results of
[
9
]
. To these central lifts of the twisted group algebra
A(N,\kappa)
one can then associate tensor categories of representations whose centers are given by local modules.

1.2
Statements of results

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. We fix a finite group
G
, with a subgroup
H
, and a
3
-cocycle
\omega
on
G
and prove the following result.

{theorem*}

[see Propositions
3.9
and
3.10
]There is a separable Frobenius monoidal functor
I\colon\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega|_{H}}\big)\to\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
. This tensor functor
I
is compatible with braidings and preserves ribbon twists.

Using the Frobenius monoidal functors
I
, we classify rigid Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
generalizing results by Davydov–Simmons
[
9
]
to the non-semisimple case. In fact, all rigid Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
are of the form
A=I(B)
for some subgroup
H
of
G
, and
B=B(N,\kappa,\varepsilon)
a rigid Frobenius algebra in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega|_{H}}\big)
with
\mathsf{dim}_{\Bbbk}B_{1}=1
. Such algebras
B
are parametrized by certain elements
\varepsilon\oplus\kappa
of the second cohomology group
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Tot}}(H,N,\Bbbk^{\times})
of a truncated total complex
\big(\widetilde{F}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Tot}}(H,N,\Bbbk^{\times}),{\rm d}_{\mathrm{Tot}}\big)
which computes the group cohomology of the semi-direct product
H\ltimes N
, described in Appendix
A.3
and
[
9
, Appendix A]
.

The following result recovers, and extends to arbitrary characteristic, the classification of connected étale algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
in
[
9
, Theorem 3.15]
.

{theorem*}

[see Theorem
3.29
]
Every connected étale algebra in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
is isomorphic to one of the form
A(H,N,\kappa,\epsilon)
for some choice of data
H
,
N
,
\gamma
,
\epsilon
, where

•

H
is a subgroup of
G
, with
N
a normal subgroup of
H
,

•

\kappa\colon N\times N\to\Bbbk^{\times}
is a function satisfying
{\rm d}(\kappa)=\omega|_{N}
,

•

\epsilon\colon H\times N\to\Bbbk^{\times}
is a function such that

{\rm d}_{\mathrm{Tot}}(\varepsilon\oplus\kappa)=\tau\oplus\gamma\oplus\omega
,

•

the compatibility
\epsilon(n,m)=\frac{\kappa(nmn^{-1},n)}{\kappa(n,m)}
holds for all
n,m\in N
.

Every such connected étale algebra has trivial twist and is a rigid Frobenius algebra if and only if
|N|\cdot|G:H|\neq 0\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.

We provide explicit formulas for the Frobenius algebras
A(H,N,\kappa,\epsilon)
in Lemma
3.28
.

An interpretation of Section
1.2
is that the algebras
B(N,\kappa,\epsilon)
are lifts of the twisted group algebras
A(N,\kappa)
in
\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega|_{H}}
to the center
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega}\big)
. These twisted group algebras were used to classify indecomposable module categories over
\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega}

[
35
,
36
]
and are separable Frobenius algebras
[
34
]
.
Our results show that lifts of these algebras to the center along the forgetful functor are parametrized by functions
\epsilon\colon H\times N\to\Bbbk^{\times}
satisfying the conditions from Section
1.2
.

The category of local modules
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega})}(A)
over a rigid Frobenius algebra
A
as in Section
1.2
is a modular category by
[
28
, Theorem 4.12]
and
[
26
, Theorem 4.5]
in the semisimple case. In fact,
[
9
, Theorem 3.16]
shows that such modular categories are equivalent as ribbon categories to
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H/N}^{\overline{\omega}}\big)
for a
3
-cocycle
\overline{\omega}
on
H/N
such that its pullback to
H
via the quotient homomorphism is equivalent to
\omega|_{H}
.

In Section
3.6
, we classify all rigid Frobenius algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
for an odd dihedral group
G=D_{2m+1}
, up to
isomorphism
of algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}\big)
rather than up to equivalence of their categories of local modules.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the necessary background on (non-semisimple) modular categories, algebraic structures in ribbon categories, and local modules, concluding with a brief review of DW categories. Section
3
contains the results of the paper, starting with a discussion on DW categories associated to quotient groups, followed by the construction of the Frobenius monoidal functors, and the classification of rigid Frobenius algebras in DW categories. In Appendix
A
, we include basic definitions from group cohomology and several cocycle identities used throughout the text.

2
Background

2.1
Modular tensor categories

Throughout this paper, we fix
\mathbbm{k}
to be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic.

In this section, we collect some basic definitions, see, e.g.,
[
15
]
for details. A
monoidal category

\mathcal{C}
consists of a tuple
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1},\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
where
\mathcal{C}
is a category,
\otimes\colon\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is a bifunctor,
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
,
\alpha_{X,Y,Z}\colon(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\to X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)
is a natural isomorphism for each
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, and
\lambda_{X}\colon\mathbbm{1}\otimes X\to X
and
\rho_{X}\colon X\otimes\mathbbm{1}\to X
are natural isomorphisms for all
X\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
,
satisfying coherence axioms (pentagon and triangle).
A functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between two monoidal categories is a
monoidal functor
if there exist natural isomorphisms

\mu^{F}\colon\ F(X)\otimes^{\mathcal{D}}F(Y)\to F\big(X\otimes^{\mathcal{C}}Y\big),\qquad\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{D}}\to F\big(\mathds{1}^{\mathcal{C}}\big),

satisfying certain coherence conditions, see
[
15
, Definition 2.4.1]
.

A monoidal category is called
rigid
if it comes equipped with left and right dual objects. That means, for every
X\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
there exists respectively an object
X^{*}\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
with evaluation and coevaluation maps
\mathsf{ev}_{X}\colon X^{*}\otimes X\to\mathbbm{1}
and
\mathsf{coev}_{X}\colon\mathbbm{1}\to X\otimes X^{*}
, as well as an object
{{}^{*}X\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})}
with evaluation and coevaluation maps
\widetilde{\mathsf{ev}}_{X}\colon X\otimes{}^{*}X\to\mathbbm{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{coev}}_{X}\colon\mathbbm{1}\to{}^{*}X\otimes X
satisfying in both cases the usual conditions.
If a rigid monoidal category comes equipped with isomorphisms
j_{X}\colon X\to X^{**}
natural in
X\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
and satisfying that
j_{X\otimes Y}=j_{X}\otimes j_{Y}
, then it is called
pivotal
. The
quantum dimension
of an object
X
in a pivotal category is the composition
\mathrm{qdim}_{j}(X):=\mathsf{ev}_{X^{*}}(j_{X}\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{X^{*}})\mathsf{coev}_{X}\in\mathsf{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

A
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is
locally finite
if, for any two objects
V,W\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
,
\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,W)
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space and every object has a finite filtration by simple objects. Further, we say
\mathcal{C}
is
finite
if
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to a category of finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-algebra. A
tensor category
is a locally finite, rigid, monoidal category such the tensor product is
\mathbbm{k}
-linear in each slot and the monoidal unit is a simple object of the category.

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is called
braided
if it comes equipped with natural isomorphisms
c_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
for all
X,Y\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, called the
braiding
, that are compatible with the monoidal structure of the category. This means, the braiding satisfies the so-called
hexagon identities
for any three objects
X,Y,Z\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
:

\textstyle{(Y\otimes Z)X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

An example of a braided category is that of the
Drinfeld center
(or
monoidal center
, or simply
center
) of a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. Its objects are pairs
\big(X,c^{X}\big)
where
X\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
and
c^{X}_{V}\colon X\otimes V\to V\otimes X
(for any
V\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
) is a natural isomorphism called the
half-braiding
satisfying that

c^{X}_{V\otimes W}=\big(\mathsf{Id}_{V}\otimes c^{X}_{W}\big)\big(c^{X}_{V}\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{W}\big).

The braiding of this category is given by
c_{(X,c^{X}),(Y,c^{Y})}:=c^{X}_{Y}
.

A
ribbon category
is a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
together with a
ribbon twist
, i.e., a natural isomorphism
\theta_{X}\colon X\to X
which satisfies

\displaystyle\theta_{X\otimes Y}=(\theta_{X}\otimes\theta_{Y})c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y},\qquad\theta_{\mathds{1}}=\mathsf{Id}_{\mathds{1}},\qquad(\theta_{X})^{*}=\theta_{X^{*}}.

(2.1)

A tensor functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between ribbon categories
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{D}
with ribbon twists
\theta^{\mathcal{C}}
,
\theta^{\mathcal{D}}
is a
ribbon tensor functor
if it commutes with the ribbon structures in the sense that
F\big(\theta^{\mathcal{C}}_{V}\big)=\theta^{\mathcal{D}}_{F(V)}
. If
F
is part of an equivalence of categories, then
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are equivalent as ribbon categories.

In order to define modular tensor categories, we require the notion of non-degeneracy of a braided category. We say that an object
X

centralizes
another object
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
if

c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\mathsf{Id}_{X\otimes Y}.

A braided finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is
non-degenerate
if the only objects
X
that centralize
all
objects of
\mathcal{C}
are of the form
X=\mathds{1}^{\oplus n}

[
15
, Section 8.20]
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{C}
is non-degenerate if and only if it is
factorizable
, i.e., there is an equivalence of braided monoidal categories
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
is
\mathcal{C}
as a tensor category, but with reversed braiding given by the inverse braiding
[
42
]
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category (i.e., a semisimple finite tensor category) then the above notion of non-degeneracy is equivalent to the commonly used condition that the
S
-matrix is non-singular.
A key definition for this paper is the concept of modular category that allows for using general finite tensor categories which are not necessarily non-semisimple.

Definition 2.1

(
[
25
,
42
]
)
.

A braided finite tensor category is
modular
if it is a non-degenerate ribbon category.

2.2
Frobenius algebras in tensor categories

In this section, let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbbm{1},\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
be a pivotal finite tensor category.

Definition 2.2
.

(a)

An
algebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a triple
(A,m,u)
, with
A\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, and
m\colon A\otimes A\to A
(multiplication),
u\colon\mathbbm{1}\to A
(unit) being morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
,
satisfying unitality and associativity constraints:

\displaystyle m(m\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A})=m(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes m)\alpha_{A,A,A},\qquad m(u\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A})=\lambda_{A},\qquad m(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes u)=\rho_{A}.

(b)

A
coalgebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a triple
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, where
C\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, and
\Delta\colon C\to C\otimes C
(comultiplication) and
\varepsilon\colon C\to\mathbbm{1}
(counit) are morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
,
satisfying counitality and coassociativity constraints:

\displaystyle\alpha_{C,C,C}(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{C})\Delta=(\mathsf{Id}_{C}\otimes\Delta)\Delta,\qquad(\varepsilon\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{C})\Delta=\lambda_{C}^{-1},\qquad(\mathsf{Id}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta=\rho_{C}^{-1}.

(c)

A
Frobenius algebra
in
\mathcal{C}
is a tuple
(A,m,u,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, where

(A,m,u)
is an algebra and
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a coalgebra so that

(m\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A})\alpha^{-1}_{A,A,A}(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes\Delta)=\Delta m=(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes m)\alpha_{A,A,A}(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A}).

Remark 2.3
.

Alternatively, a Frobenius algebra in
\mathcal{C}
is a tuple
(A,m,u,p,q)
, where
(A,m,u)
is an algebra,
p\colon A\otimes A\to\mathbbm{1}
and
q\colon\mathbbm{1}\to A\otimes A
are morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying an invariance condition,

p(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes m)\alpha_{A,A,A}=p(m\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A}),

and the ‘snake’ equations.
To convert from
(A,m,u,p,q)
to
(A,m,u,\Delta,\varepsilon)
in the previous definition, take

\Delta:=(m\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A})\alpha_{A,A,A}^{-1}(\mathsf{Id}_{A}\otimes q)\rho_{A}^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\varepsilon:=p(u\otimes\mathsf{Id}_{A})\rho_{A}^{-1}}.

On the other hand, to convert from
(A,m,u,\Delta,\varepsilon)
to
(A,m,u,p,q)
, take

p:=\varepsilon_{A}m_{A}
and
q:=\Delta_{A}u_{A}
, cf.
[
20
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

(a)

An algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is
indecomposable
if it is not isomorphic to a direct sum of non-trivial algebras in
\mathcal{C}
.

(b)

An algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is
connected
(or
haploid
) if

\mathsf{dim}_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1},A)=1
.

(c)

An algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is
separable
if there exists a morphism
\Delta^{\prime}\colon A\to A\otimes A
in
\mathcal{C}
so that
m\Delta^{\prime}=\mathsf{Id}_{A}
as maps in

Lemma 2.16
.

If

Next, define the category of

\displaystyle\tau(h,k)(d):=\frac{\omega(h,k,d)\omega\big(hkd(hk)^{-1},h,k\big)}{\omega\big(h,kdk^{-1},k\big)}

and

that action with

Lemma 2.17
.

The tensor product of two twisted YD modules is itself a twisted YD module.

Proof.

We need to check that

This condition is equivalent to the equality

\displaystyle\gamma(k)(d,f)\gamma(h)\big(kdk^{-1},kfk^{-1}\big)\tau(h,k)(d)\tau(h,k)(f)=\tau(h,k)(df)\gamma(hk)(d,f),

which is proven in Appendix

∎

Lemma 2.18

The tensor product gives twisted YD modules the structure of a monoidal category.

Proof.

The tensor product should be compatible with the monoidal structure morphisms (associator and unitors) of the category of twisted YD modules that we are working on.
Since in this category the unitors are the identity this is clear, but for the case of the associator (following the opposite convention of

\displaystyle\omega^{-1}\!\big(hgh^{-1}\!,hg^{\prime}h^{-1}\!,hg^{\prime\prime}h^{-1}\big)\gamma(h)(gg^{\prime}\!,g^{\prime\prime})\gamma(h)(g,g^{\prime})=\gamma(h)(g,g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime})\gamma(h)(g^{\prime}\!,g^{\prime\prime})\omega^{-1}(g,g^{\prime}\!,g^{\prime\prime}),

which is proven in Appendix
A
, Lemma
A.2
. ∎

For two twisted YD modules

3.1

In this section, we derive a general result on categories of local modules for algebras given by

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

\mathcal{D}

and a braided tensor equivalence between
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{D})
and
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}(R(\mathds{1}))

Proof.

Set

. This is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.
By
[
5

, there is a tensor equivalence
\mathcal{D}\to\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
for the monoidal category from Definition

is tensor equivalent to

∎

We would like to be able to apply this result to the categories

and

. By

, Section 2.6]
, any monoidal functor

are equal in

If we combine this characterization with the adjunction condition, we get that a functor

The functor defined, for a an object

, by

and, for a morphism

satisfies the above condition of being a right adjoint to

.

Assume that

is a twisted YD module.

3.2
The induction functor

Let

In this section, we define a functor

and show that this functor is Frobenius monoidal.
In objects, this functor is defined for any

as
V\mapsto I(V):=\mathbbm{k}G\otimes V
, with relations:

\displaystyle gh\otimes v_{d}=\tau(g,h)(d)^{-1}g\otimes h\cdot v_{d}\qquad\text{for}\quad g\in G,\quad h,d\in H.

(3.1)

The
\Bbbk G
-coaction is given by

\displaystyle\delta(g\otimes v_{d}):=gdg^{-1}\otimes g\otimes v_{d}.

Lemma 3.6
.

I(V)
has the structure of a YD module with action:
{g\!\vartriangleright\!(k\otimes v_{d}):=\tau(g,k)(d)gk\!\otimes\!v_{d}}
.

Proof.

First, we check that the coaction is compatible with the comultiplication, namely that
(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{Id})\delta=(\mathsf{Id}\otimes\delta)\delta
. Here,

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{Id})\delta(g\otimes v_{d})=(\Delta\otimes\mathsf{Id})(gdg^{-1}\otimes g\otimes v_{d})=gdg^{-1}\otimes gdg^{-1}\otimes g\otimes v_{d},

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Id}\otimes\delta)\delta(g\otimes v_{d})=gdg^{-1}\otimes(g\otimes v_{d})=gdg^{-1}\otimes gdg^{-1}\otimes g\otimes v_{d}.

Both sides agree.

Next, the YD condition requires that for any
g,k\in G
,
d\in H
, the composition

\displaystyle g\otimes(k\otimes v_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto(g\otimes g)\otimes(k\otimes v_{d})\mapsto\tau(g,k)(d)g\otimes(gk\otimes v_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto\tau(g,k)(d)g\otimes gkdk^{-1}g^{-1}\otimes(gk\otimes v_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto\tau(g,k)(d)gkdk^{-1}\otimes(gk\otimes v_{d})

needs to be equal to

\displaystyle g\otimes(k\otimes v_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto(g\otimes g)\otimes kdk^{-1}\otimes(k\otimes v_{d})\mapsto\tau(g,k)(d)gkdk^{-1}\otimes(gk\otimes v_{d}),

which match.

For the defined map to be a twisted action, we require that, for any
g,h,k\in G
,
d\in H
:

g\vartriangleright(h\vartriangleright(k\otimes v_{d}))=\tau(g,h)\big(kdk^{-1}\big)gh\vartriangleright(k\otimes v_{d}).

By expanding both sides using the proposed action, this is equivalent to requiring that

\tau(h,k)(d)\tau(g,hk)(d)ghk\otimes v_{d}=\tau(g,h)(kdk^{-1})\tau(gh,k)(d)ghk\otimes v_{d}.

This equality holds by Lemma
A.1
.
∎

We claim that the functor

monoidal with natural transformation

\displaystyle\nu_{V,W}\colon\ I(V\otimes V)

\displaystyle\to I(V)\otimes I(W),

\displaystyle g\otimes(v_{d}\otimes w_{f})

\displaystyle\mapsto\gamma(g)(d,f)(g\otimes v_{d})\otimes(g\otimes w_{f}),

with
\gamma
as defined in (
2.25
), for any

, and

. The counit is given by

I(\mathds{1})\to\mathds{1},\qquad g\otimes 1\mapsto 1.

Lemma 3.7

Finally,

0&\text{else},\end{cases}

As a consequence of this quotient, the op-lax monoidal structure from Lemma

extends to

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\colon\ I(U\otimes V)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow I(U)\otimes_{A_{H}}I(V),

\displaystyle g\otimes(u_{d}\otimes u_{f})

\displaystyle\mapsto\gamma(g)(d,f)(g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(g\otimes v_{f}),

also giving an op-lax monoidal structure.

The natural transformation

is in fact an isomorphism. To observe this, we define the morphism

\displaystyle\Lambda_{U,V}\colon\ I(U)\otimes I(V)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow I(U\otimes V),

\displaystyle(g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f})

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\lambda(g,d,l,f)g\otimes\big(u_{d}\otimes g^{-1}l\cdot v_{f}\big)&\text{if }gH=lH,\\

in

.

The morphism

is inverse to

. Indeed, we compute that

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f}))=\lambda(g,d,l,f)\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\big(g\otimes(u_{d}\otimes g^{-1}l\cdot v_{f})\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f}))}{}=\gamma(g)\big(d,g^{-1}lfl^{-1}g\big)\lambda(g,d,l,f)\big((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes\big(g\otimes g^{-1}l\cdot v_{f}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f}))=}{}\cdot((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f}))

\displaystyle\phantom{\bar{\nu}_{U,V}\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f}))}{}=((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(l\otimes v_{f})),

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}\bar{\nu}_{U,V}(g\otimes(u_{d}\otimes v_{f}))=\gamma(g)(d,f)\bar{\Lambda}_{U,V}((g\otimes u_{d})\otimes(g\otimes v_{f}))

is a natural isomorphism and thus

Theorem 3.18
.

The functor
I
defines an equivalence of monoidal categories between
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega}\big)
and
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega})}(A_{H})
.

Proof.

We first show that
I
is faithful. On morphisms,
I
is given by the map

\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega})}(V,W)\to\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega})}(A_{H})}(\Bbbk G\otimes V,\Bbbk G\otimes W),\qquad p\mapsto I(p)=\mathsf{Id}_{A_{H}}\otimes p.

As this map is injective, seen, for example, by restricting to the subspace
1\otimes V
of
I(V)
, we see that
I
is faithful.

To prove that
I
is full, suppose
q\colon\Bbbk G\otimes_{A_{H}}V\to\Bbbk G\otimes_{A_{H}}W
is a morphism in
\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega}}(A_{H})
.
Then,
for all
g,k\in G
,
v\in V
, we have

q(g\otimes v)\cdot\delta_{kH}=q((g\otimes v)\cdot\delta_{kH})=\begin{cases}q(g\otimes v)&\text{if $k^{-1}g\in H$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We choose a set of coset representatives
\{g_{i}\}
for
H
in
G
such that
g_{1}=1
.
Using the above and equation (
3.1
), we see that the
q(g_{i}\otimes v)
is contained in the subspace
g_{i}\otimes W
of
I(W)
. Hence, there exists a vector
w_{i}\in W
such that

q(g_{i}\otimes v)=g_{i}\otimes w_{i}
. However,
q
commutes with the twisted left
G
-action, which implies that

g_{i}\otimes w_{i}=q(g_{i}\otimes v)=q(g_{i}\cdot(1\otimes v))=g_{i}\cdot q(1\otimes v)=g_{i}\cdot(1\otimes w_{1})=g_{i}\otimes w_{1}.

Thus,
w_{i}=w_{1}
for all
i
. Hence, we obtain a
\Bbbk
-linear map
q^{\prime}\colon V\to W
,
v\mapsto w_{1}
. This map satisfies
I(q^{\prime})=q
, i.e.,

q(g\otimes v)=g\otimes q^{\prime}(v)\in I(W)\qquad\forall g\in G,\quad v\in V.

By restricting to
g\in H
, it follows that
q^{\prime}\colon V\to W
is a morphism of twisted YD modules over
H
. This proves that
I
is full.

It remains to show that
I
is essentially surjective. For this, take a local module
L\in\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathsf{loc}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega})}(A_{H})
. The actions of the idempotent elements
\delta_{gH}
of
A_{H}
define a family of idempotent endomorphisms

e_{g}\colon\ L\to L,\qquad l\mapsto l\cdot\delta_{gH},\qquad e_{g}\in\mathsf{End}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathsf{Vect}_{G}^{\omega})}(L).

Setting
L^{i}=\operatorname{Im}(e_{g_{i}})
gives a direct sum decomposition
L=\bigoplus\limits_{i}L^{i}
. Here, we use that

1_{A_{H}}=\sum\limits_{i}\delta_{g_{i}H}.

We now observe that
l\in L^{i}
if and only if
g_{j}g_{i}^{-1}\cdot l\in L^{j}
. In particular,
l\in L^{1}
if and only if
g_{i}l\in L^{i}
. Further,
L^{1}
is a submodule of
L
under the left twisted
H
-action. The assumption that
L
is a local module implies that if
l_{d}\in L^{1}
has degree
|l|=d
, then

l=l\cdot\delta_{dH}
. We can write
d=g_{i}h
, with
h\in H
, and find that
l\in L^{i}
. However, as the subspaces
L^{i}
intersect trivially it follows that
d\in H
. Thus,
L^{1}
correspond to an object in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega}\big)
.

Using the twisted right
G
-action on
L
, we define a map

\displaystyle\pi\colon\ \Bbbk G\otimes L_{1}\longrightarrow L,\qquad g\otimes l\mapsto g\cdot l.

The map
\pi
is surjective. Indeed,
L_{1}
is given by all elements of the form
l\cdot\delta_{H}
, with
l\in L
. Now,

\displaystyle g_{i}\left(\frac{\big(g_{i}^{-1}l\big)}{\gamma(g_{i})(g_{i},|l|)}\cdot\delta_{H}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(g_{i})\big(g_{i}|l|g_{i}^{-1},1\big)}{\gamma(g_{i})(g_{i},|l|)}\big(g_{i}\big(g_{i}^{-1}l\big)\big)\cdot(g_{i}\delta_{H})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(g_{i})(g_{i},|l|)}{\gamma(g_{i})(g_{i},|l|)}l\cdot\delta_{g_{i}H}=l\cdot\delta_{g_{i}H}.

Thus,
l\cdot\delta_{g_{i}H}
is in the image of
\pi
and hence, for any
l\in L
,

l=\sum_{i}l\cdot\delta_{g_{i}H}\in\operatorname{Im}(\pi).

It follows that

\pi(gh\otimes v_{d})=(gh)l=\tau(g,h)(d)^{-1}g(hl)=\tau(g,h)(d)^{-1}g\otimes hv_{d}.

Thus, by equation (
3.1
),
\pi
descents to a quotient map

\overline{\pi}\colon I\big(L^{1}\big)\to L

which is still surjective. The right twisted action by
g\in G
gives an isomorphism of vector spaces and hence
\mathsf{dim}(L^{i})=\mathsf{dim}\big(L^{1}\big)
for all
i
. This shows that

\mathsf{dim}L=|G:H|\mathsf{dim}_{\Bbbk}L^{1}=\mathsf{dim}_{\Bbbk}I\big(L^{1}\big).

Thus,
\overline{\pi}
is injective and hence gives an isomorphism
I\big(L^{1}\big)\cong L
.
∎

Proposition 3.34

is zero in
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{Tot}}(H,N,\Bbbk^{\times})

Assume that

is an isomorphism of algebras in
\mathcal{Z}\big(\mathsf{Vect}_{H}^{\omega}\big)
. Thus,
\mathsf{dim}A=\mathsf{dim}A^{\prime}
and hence

.
Then

is a morphism of twisted YD modules over

This implies that

and

. Then

3.6

Let

Let

\cdots\mathbb{Z}G^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\partial_{n}}\mathbb{Z}G^{n-1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\partial_{n-1}}\cdots\mathbb{Z}G^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}\mathbb{Z}G,

A.3
Cohomology of crossed products of groups

Let
H
,
G
be groups together with a left action of
H
on
G
by group automorphisms, i.e.,
H\mapsto\operatorname{Aut}(G)
,
h\mapsto\big(g\mapsto{}^{h}g\big)
. Then we can form the
crossed product

G\rtimes H
, which is
G\times H
as a set with multiplication given by

(g_{1},h_{1})\cdot(g_{2},h_{2})=\big(g_{1}{}^{h_{1}}g_{2},h_{1}h_{2}\big).

Let
A
be a right
\mathbb{Z}G
-module. Then
F^{n}(G,A)=\mathsf{Fun}(G^{n},A)
becomes a right
H
-module with action

(f\cdot h)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=f\big({}^{h}g_{1},\dots,{}^{h}g_{n}\big).

Following
[
9
, Appendix A]
, define a double complex

F^{n,m}(H,G,A)=\mathsf{Fun}(H^{n},\mathsf{Fun}(G^{m},A))=F^{n}(H,F^{m}(G,A)).

The two differentials are denoted by

{\rm d}^{n,m}\colon\ F^{n,m}(H,G,A)\to F^{n+1,m}(H,G,A),\qquad\partial^{n,m}\colon\ F^{n,m}(H,G,A)\to F^{n,m+1}(H,G,A),

where

(\partial^{n,m}(f))(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})={\rm d}^{n}(f(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})).

The differentials commute, i.e.,
{\rm d}^{n,m+1}\partial^{n,m}=\partial^{n+1,m}{\rm d}^{n,m}
making
F^{\bullet,\bullet}(H,G,A)
a double complex. Hence, one can consider the associated
truncated
double complex

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\mathrm{Tot}}^{n}(H,G,A)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}F^{n-i,i}(H,G,A),

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{\mathrm{Tot}}^{n}(f):={\rm d}^{n-i,i}(f)+(-1)^{i}\partial^{n-i,i}(f)\qquad\text{for $f\in F^{n-i,i}(H,G,A)$ with $i<n$}.

We will typically denote an element
f\in\widetilde{F}^{i,n-i}(H,G,A)\subseteq F_{\mathrm{Tot}}^{n}(H,G,A)
by a function

f\colon H^{i}\times G^{n-i}\to A
.

Letting
G\rtimes H
act on
A
via the surjective homomorphism
G\rtimes H\to G
, the
untrucated
total complex

F_{\mathrm{Tot}}^{n}(H,G,A)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}F^{n-i,i}(H,G,A)

is quasi-isomorphic to the complex
F^{\bullet}(G\rtimes H,A)
computing group cohomology, see
[
21
]
.

Several cocycles considered in this paper have interpretations as elements of the truncated total complex
\widetilde{F}_{\mathrm{Tot}}^{\bullet}
with
A=\Bbbk^{\times}
.

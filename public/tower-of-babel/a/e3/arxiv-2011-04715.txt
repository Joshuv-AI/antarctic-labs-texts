Global well-posedness and critical norm concentration for inhomogeneous biharmonic NLS
Mykael Cardoso, Carlos M. Guzmán, Ademir Pastor

Abstract
We consider the inhomogeneous biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger (IBNLS) equation in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , i ​ ∂ t u + Δ 2 ​ u − | x | − b ​ | u | 2 ​ σ ​ u = 0 , i\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u=0, where σ > 0 \sigma>0 and b > 0 b>0 . We first study the local well-posedness in H ˙ s c ∩ H ˙ 2 \dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2} , for N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 and 0 < s c < 2 0<s_{c}<2 , where s c = N 2 − 4 − b 2 ​ σ s_{c}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{4-b}{2\sigma} . Next, we established a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality in order to obtain sufficient conditions for global existence of solutions in H ˙ s c ∩ H ˙ 2 \dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2} with 0 ≤ s c < 2 0\leq s_{c}<2 . Finally, we study the phenomenon of L σ c L^{\sigma_{c}} -norm concentration for finite time blow up solutions with bounded H ˙ s c \dot{H}^{s_{c}} -norm, where σ c = 2 ​ N ​ σ 4 − b \sigma_{c}=\frac{2N\sigma}{4-b} . Our main tool is the compact embedding of L ˙ p ∩ H ˙ 2 \dot{L}^{p}\cap\dot{H}^{2} into a weighted L 2 ​ σ + 2 L^{2\sigma+2} space, which may be seen of independent interest.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the initial value problem (IVP) for the focusing inhomogeneous biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger (IBNLS) equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u=0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\sigma
,
b>0
and
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function in space-time
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\Delta^{2}
stands for the biharmonic operator, that is,
\Delta^{2}u=\Delta(\Delta u)
. Equation in (
1.1
) may be seen as an inhomogeneous version of the fourth order NLS equation,

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta^{2}u-|u|^{2\sigma}u=0,

(1.2)

in much the same way, the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger (INLS) equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\sigma}u=0,

(1.3)

may be seen as an inhomogeneous version of the standard NLS equation. Equation (
1.2
) was introduced by Karpman
[
13
]
and Karpman-Shagalov
[
14
]
to take into account the role of small fourth-order dispersion terms in the propagation of intense laser beams in a bulk medium with a Kerr nonlinearity.

Let us start by observing if
u
is a solution of (
1.1
) so is
u_{\lambda}
given by

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{4-b}{2\sigma}}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{4}t),\quad\lambda>0.

(1.4)

In addition, a straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\lambda^{s-s_{c}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

(1.5)

where
s_{c}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{4-b}{2\sigma}
is the critical Sobolev index. If
s_{c}=0
(or
\sigma=\frac{4-b}{N}
) the IVP (
1.1
) is known as mass-critical or
L^{2}
-critical; if
s_{c}=2
(or
\sigma=\frac{4-b}{N-4}
) it is called energy-critical or
\dot{H}^{2}
-critical; finally, the problem is known as mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical (also called intercritical) if
0<s_{c}<2
(or
\tfrac{4-b}{N}<\sigma<4^{*}
), where

4^{*}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{4-b}{N-4}\quad\textnormal{if}\quad N\geq 5,\\
\infty\quad\textnormal{if}\quad N=1,2,3,4.\end{cases}

(1.6)

For solutions in
H^{2}
it is not difficult to see that we have the conservation of mass
M[u]
and energy
E[u]
defined by

M\left[u(t)\right]=\int|u(t)|^{2}dx,

(1.7)

and

E\left[u(t)\right]=\frac{1}{2}\int|\Delta u(t)|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2\sigma+2}\int|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{2\sigma+2}dx.

(1.8)

Recently, the second and third authors in
[
10

studied the initial value problem (

for

0<b<\min\{\frac{N}{2},4\}
and
\min\left\{\frac{1-b}{N},0\right\}<\sigma<4^{*}

\min\left\{\frac{1-b}{N},0\right\}<\sigma\leq\frac{4-b}{N}
. In the mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical cases, the authors shown the small data global existence under the same assumptions on

for dimensions

and

In the sequel we will be concerned with global well-posedness results. To do that, we first prove a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality and use it to establish sufficient conditions for global existence.

Theorem 1.2

Let

\displaystyle K_{opt}=\left(\frac{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})}{\sigma(2-s_{c})}\right)^{\frac{(4-b)(p-\sigma_{c})}{2p(2-s_{p})}}\left(\frac{(\sigma+1)(2-s_{p})}{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})}\right)\|V\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{8\sigma-(p-2)(4-b)}{4-2s_{p}}}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\geq\|V\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}.

For the intercritical NLS equation they were studied, for instance, in

We begin introducing the notation used throughout the paper and list some useful inequalities. We use

means that there exists a positive constant

. Given a real number

to denote

sufficiently small. For a subset

For

the number

is such that

\displaystyle 2^{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{2N}{N-4},\,\,N\geq 5\\
+\infty,\,\,\,\,N=1,2,3,4.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The norm in the usual Sobolev spaces
H^{s,p}=H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined by
\|f\|_{H^{s,p}}:=\|J^{s}f\|_{L^{p}}
,
where
J^{s}
stands for the Bessel potential of order
-s
, given via Fourier transform by
\widehat{J^{s}f}=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{f}
. If
p=2
we denote
H^{s,2}
simply by
H^{s}
.

Let

\dot{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N});(D^{\alpha}\widehat{f})(0)=0,\,\mbox{for all}\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\},

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the Schwarz space. Endowed with the topology of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\dot{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
becomes a locally convex space. By
\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we denote the topological dual of
\dot{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which can be identified with the factor space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})/\mathcal{P}
with
\mathcal{P}
representing the collection of all polynomials of the form
\sum a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
(see
[
23
]
, page 237). In particular, elements in
\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
may be considered as elements in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
modulo a polynomial. Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the homogeneous spaces
\dot{H}^{s}=\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
may be defined in the following way

\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{f\in\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N});\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}:=\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\},

where
D^{s}=(-\Delta)^{s/2}
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|^{s}
.
Equivalently,
\dot{H}^{s}
may also be defined in view of the Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Moreover,
\dot{H}^{s}
are reflexive Banach spaces (see
[
24
, Proposition 1.19]
for the completeness). The reflexibility follows taking into account that the map
T:\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
defined by
Tf=(-\Delta)^{s/2}f
is an isometry from
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
onto a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 1
, we also define the space

\dot{W}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N});D^{\alpha}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,\mbox{with}\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N},|\alpha|=m\}

and set

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{m}}:=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Identifying functions that differ by a polynomial the spaces
\dot{H}^{m}
and
\dot{W}^{m}
are identical (see
[
21
, Theorem 3.13]
). So, in what follows we will not distinguish these two spaces. In particular the space
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
\dot{H}^{m}\cap L^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
(see
[
11
, Theorem 7]
).

By

,
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote the space of all measurable functions

such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}_{b}}:=\left(\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

(2.2)

Thus,
L^{p}_{b}
is nothing but the weighted Lebesgue space

.

If

is a Banach space and

an interval; the mixed norms in the spaces

, of a function

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{I}X}=\left(\int_{I}\|f(\cdot,t)\|^{q}_{X}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

with the standard modification if

. Moreover, if

we shall use the notation

(4.37)

and

\displaystyle A=\frac{(\sigma+1)(2-s_{p})}{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,B=\frac{\sigma(2-s_{c})}{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})}.

(4.38)

Hence
V
is a solution of (
1.11
) and

\displaystyle\|V\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\frac{\beta^{N}}{\alpha^{p}}\|g^{*}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\frac{\beta^{N}}{\alpha^{p}}=B^{\frac{N}{4}}\left(\frac{AJ}{B^{\frac{4-b}{4}}}\right)^{\frac{N(p-2)-4p}{(p-2)(4-b)-8\sigma}}.

(4.39)

Note that this implies

\displaystyle K_{opt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J}=AB^{-\frac{(4-b)(p-\sigma_{c})}{2p(2-s_{p})}}\|V\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{8\sigma-(p-2)(4-b)}{4-2s_{p}}}

(4.40)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})}{\sigma(2-s_{c})}\right)^{\frac{(4-b)(p-\sigma_{c})}{2p(2-s_{p})}}\left(\frac{(\sigma+1)(2-s_{p})}{\sigma(s_{c}-s_{p})+(2-s_{p})}\right)\|V\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{8\sigma-(p-2)(4-b)}{4-2s_{p}}},

(4.41)

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

As a consequence of the Theorem
1.2
, we are able to establish the global well-posedness result in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

As usual, the idea is to get an a priori bound of the local solution in
H^{2}
. Since, by (
1.7
) the
L^{2}
norm is already conserved, we only need to bound
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. To do so, we will use the conservation of the energy in (
1.8
) and Corollary
1.3
(part (i)). Indeed, first note that

\displaystyle 2E(u_{0})=2E(u(t))=

\displaystyle\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma+1}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L_{b}^{2\sigma+2}}^{2\sigma+2}

(4.42)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{K_{opt}}{\sigma+1}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma s_{c}+2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma(2-s_{c})}.

(4.43)

By setting
X(t)=\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
B=\frac{K_{opt}}{\sigma+1}M[u_{0}]^{\frac{\sigma(2-s_{c})}{2}}
, the last inequality reads as

\displaystyle X(t)-BX(t)^{\frac{\sigma s_{c}+2}{2}}\leq 2E[u_{0}],\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall t\in[-T,T],

(4.44)

where
T>0
is the existence time provided by the local theory in Theorem A.

Next, for
x\geq 0
we define the function
f(x)=x-Bx^{\frac{\sigma s_{c}+2}{2}}
. Since
\sigma>\frac{4-b}{N}
it is easy to see that
f
has a maximum point at

\displaystyle x_{0}=\left(\frac{2}{B(\sigma s_{c}+2)}\right)^{\frac{2}{\sigma s_{c}}}

(4.45)

with maximum value

\displaystyle f(x_{0})=\frac{\sigma s_{c}}{\sigma s_{c}+2}\left(\frac{2}{B(\sigma s_{c}+2)}\right)^{\frac{2}{\sigma s_{c}}}.

(4.46)

By requiring that

2E[u_{0}]<f(x_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad X(0)<x_{0},

(4.47)

the continuity of
X(t)
yields that
X(t)<x_{0}
, for any
t\in[-T,T]
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle E[Q]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma+2}\|Q\|_{L_{b}^{2\sigma+2}}^{2\sigma+2}=\left(\frac{N\sigma+b}{4\sigma(2-s_{c})}-\frac{1}{\sigma(2-s_{c})}\right)\|Q\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{s_{c}}{2(2-s_{c})}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.48)

In addition, since

\displaystyle K_{opt}=\left(\frac{\sigma(2-s_{c})}{\sigma s_{c}+2}\right)^{\frac{\sigma s_{c}}{2}}\left(\frac{2\sigma+2}{\sigma s_{c}+2}\right)\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{-2\sigma},

(4.49)

a straightforward calculation gives that (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) are equivalent to those relations in (
4.47
).
Furthermore, (
1.16
) is equivalent to
X(t)<x_{0}
. The proof of the theorem is thus completed.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

An argument similar to that in the proof of Theorem
1.4
also holds in the case
\sigma=\frac{4-b}{N}
. Indeed, since
s_{c}=0
, as in (
4.42
) we obtain

2E[u_{0}]\geq\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{K_{opt}}{\sigma+1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\right).

(4.50)

Moreover, because
K_{opt}=(\sigma+1)\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{-2\sigma}
(see (
4.49
)) it follows that

2E[u_{0}]\geq\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}}\right).

(4.51)

If we assume

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}},

(4.52)

from (
4.51
) we get that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is uniformly bounded. Thus, under assumption (
4.52
) we obtain the global well-posedness in
H^{2}
in the case
\sigma=\frac{4-b}{N}
. This result complements the one in
[
10
, Proposition 1.5]
, where the authors proved the global well-posedness assuming that
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
is sufficiently small. In the limiting case
b=0
this result was also established in
[
8
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Since we are assuming (
1.19
) it is sufficient to show that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
remains bounded. But, using the conservation of the energy and Corollary
1.3
(part (ii)) we deduce

\begin{split}2E[u_{0}]&\geq\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}{\|V\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}\right)\\
&\geq\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}{\|V\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}\right).\end{split}

Thus, under assumption (
1.17
) we obtain the desired and the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
1.6
is also true in the case
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma=\frac{4-b}{N}
, in which case we have
\sigma_{c}=2
and (
1.17
) reduces to (
4.52
).

5
Concentration in the critical Lebesgue norm

In this section, we prove our main result about
L^{\sigma_{c}}
-norm concentration in the intercritical regime for finite time blow-up solution.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Clearly it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq\lambda(t_{n})}|u(x,t_{n})|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx\geq\|V\|^{\sigma_{c}}_{L^{\sigma_{c}}},

(5.1)

for any sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
t_{n}\uparrow T^{*}
, as
n\to\infty
.

To prove this, let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence such that
t_{n}\uparrow T^{*}
and define

\displaystyle\rho_{n}=\left(\frac{1}{\|u(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-s_{c}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\,\,\,v_{n}(x)=\rho_{n}^{\frac{4-b}{2\sigma}}u(\rho_{n}x,t_{n}).

(5.2)

By using (
1.19
) we may take a positive constant
C
such that

\|v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=\|u(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}\leq C.

Thus, in view of the embedding
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\subset L^{\sigma_{c}}
we deduce that
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\sigma_{c}}
.
Also, a straightforward calculation gives

\|v_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}=\rho_{n}^{\frac{2(4-b)}{2\sigma}+4-N}\|u(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}=\rho_{n}^{2(2-s_{c})}\|u(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}^{2}=1.

So
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{\sigma_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
. Consequently, there exists
v^{*}\in L^{\sigma_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
such that, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
in
L^{\sigma_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
, as
n\to\infty
. This implies

\displaystyle\|\Delta v^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\Delta v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\|v^{*}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}.

(5.3)

Furthermore, by Proposition
4.2
(passing to a subsequence if necessary)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{L_{b}^{2\sigma+2}}=\left\|v^{*}\right\|_{L_{b}^{2\sigma+2}}.

(5.4)

On the other hand, note that

E(v_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma+2}\int|x|^{-b}|v_{n}|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx=\rho_{n}^{2(2-s_{c})}E(u_{0})

and since
\rho_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E(v_{n})=0.

(5.5)

Combining Corollary
1.2
together with (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) one has

\displaystyle 0=\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(v_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|v^{*}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}{\|V\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}}\right),

(5.6)

which implies
\|v^{*}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\geq\|V\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}
.

Next, the weak convergence
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v^{*}
in
L^{\sigma_{c}}
, gives that for any
R>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{|y|\leq\rho_{n}R}|u(y,t_{n})|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dy=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho_{n}^{\frac{\sigma_{c}(4-b)}{2\sigma}}|u(\rho_{n}x,t_{n})|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx

(5.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}|v_{n}(x)|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx\geq\int_{|x|\leq R}|v^{*}|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx,

(5.8)

Since assumption (
1.20
) implies
\lambda(t_{n})/\rho_{n}\to\infty
, as
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq\lambda(t_{n})}|u(x,t_{n})|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx\geq\int_{|x|\leq R}|v^{*}|^{\sigma_{c}}\,dx,

(5.9)

for any
R>0
. Finally, from (
5.9
) and the fact that
\|v^{*}\|_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}\geq\|V\|^{2\sigma}_{L^{\sigma_{c}}}
we obtain (
5.1
),
which completes the proof.
∎

6
Appendix

In this appendix we give another concentration result for the IBNLS equation.
First of all, we note that Proposition
4.2
also allows us to obtain a Gagliardo-Niremberg type inequality for functions in
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
0<b<4
,
\frac{4-b}{N}<\sigma<4^{*}
and
s_{c}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{4-b}{2\sigma}
, then for any
f\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|f(x)|^{2\sigma+2}\,dx\leq\frac{\sigma+1}{\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{2\sigma},

(6.1)

where
W
is a solution of the elliptic equation,

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}W+|x|^{-b}|W|^{2\sigma}W-(-\Delta)^{s_{c}}W=0

(6.2)

with minimal
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-norm.

Proof.

(Sketch)
Since the proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.2
we will give only the main steps. Define the functional

\displaystyle J(f)=\frac{\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{2\sigma}}{\left\|f\right\|_{L_{b}^{2\sigma+2}}^{2\sigma+2}}

(6.3)

and set

J=\displaystyle\inf_{f\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2},f\neq 0}J(f).

(6.4)

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence for (
6.4
) and consider the sequence
w_{n}(x)=\mu_{n}f_{n}(\theta_{n}x)
with

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=\frac{\|f_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{\frac{N-4}{2(2-s_{c})}}}{\|\Delta f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-b}{2\sigma(2-s_{c})}}}\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\theta_{n}=\left(\frac{\|f_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}}{\|\Delta f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-s_{c}}}.

(6.5)

It is easy to check that
\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also a minimizing sequence satisfying
\|w_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. An application of Proposition
4.2
gives a function
w^{*}\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
such that
w_{n}\rightharpoonup w^{*}
in
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
, as
n\to\infty
, and
J(w^{*})=J.
In addition,
w^{*}
must be a solution of the equation

-\Delta^{2}w^{*}-(\sigma+1)J|x|^{-b}|w^{*}|^{2\sigma}w^{*}+\sigma(-\Delta)^{s_{c}}w^{*}=0.

The scaling
w^{*}(x)=\alpha W(\beta x)
with

\alpha=\left(\frac{\sigma^{\frac{4-b}{2(2-s_{c})}}}{(\sigma+1)J}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=\sigma^{\frac{1}{2(2-s_{c})}}

then gives that
W
is a solution of (
6.2
) with minimal
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-norm and

\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=\frac{\beta^{\frac{4-b}{2\sigma}}}{\alpha}\|w^{*}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=[(\sigma+1)J]^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}}.

Consequently, for any
f\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}

\int|x|^{-b}|f(x)|^{2\sigma+2}\leq\frac{1}{J}\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{2\sigma}=\frac{\sigma+1}{\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}}\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}^{2\sigma},

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

With this Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality in hand we are able to prove the following.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
0<b<\min\{\frac{N}{2},4\}
and
\max\{\tfrac{4-b}{N},\tfrac{1}{2}\}<\sigma<\frac{4-b}{N-4}
. For
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}
, let
u(t)
be the corresponding solution to (
1.1
) given by Theorem
1.1
and
T^{*}>0
the maximal time of existence. Suppose that
\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}
, where
W
is a solution of the elliptic equation (
6.2
) with minimal
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
-norm. Then
u(t)
exists globally in the time.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
0<b<\min\{\frac{N}{2},4\}
and
\max\{\tfrac{4-b}{N},\tfrac{1}{2}\}<\sigma<\frac{4-b}{N-4}
. For
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}\cap\dot{H}^{2}

u(t)
be the corresponding solution to (
1.1
) given by Theorem
1.1
and assume that it blows up in finite time

If

(6.6)

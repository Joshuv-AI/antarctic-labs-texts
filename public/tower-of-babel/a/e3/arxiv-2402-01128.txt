Positive Ground State Solutions to a Nonlocal Singular Elliptic Problem
Mustafa Avci

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study the existence and uniqueness of solutions to some nonlocal singular elliptic problem under Dirichlet boundary condition. Problem is settled in Musielak-Sobolev spaces.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we are concerned with a nonlocal singular elliptic problem of the form

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rlll}-\mathcal{A}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)\mathrm{div}(a(x,|\nabla u|)\nabla u)&=&g(x)u^{-\gamma(x)}\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>&0\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 3)
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\gamma:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow(0,1)
is a continuous function,
\mathcal{A}
and
g
are continuous functions.

The function
\varphi(x,t):=a(x,|t|)t
is an increasing homeomorphism from
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
onto
\mathbb{R}
. We want to remark that if we let
a(x,t)=|t|^{p(x)-2}
, where
p(x)
is a continuous function on
\overline{\Omega}
with
\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}p(x)>1
, equation
1.1
turns into the well-known singular
p(x)
-Kirchhoff equation. If we additionally consider the case
\mathcal{A}(t)=1
, equation
1.1
becomes singular the
p(x)
-Laplace equation, a generalization of
p
-Laplace equation, given by
\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u)=f(x,u)
,
1<p(x)<N
. Therefore, equation
1.1
particularly generalizes the problems involving variable exponent. This kind of equations have been intensively studied by many authors for the past two decades due to its significant role in many fields of mathematics, such as in the study of calculus of variations, partial differential equations
[
2
,
17
,
18
]
, but also for their use in a variety of physical and engineering contexts: the modeling of electrorheological fluids
[
32
]
, the analysis of Non-Newtonian fluids
[
36
]
, fluid flow in porous media
[
3
]
, magnetostatics
[
14
]
, image restoration
[

]
, and capillarity phenomena
[

]
, see also, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
23
,
35
]
and references therein. Therefore, equation (
1.1
) may represent a variety of mathematical models corresponding to certain phenomena:

For
\varphi(t):=p|t|^{p-2}t
;

•

Nonlinear elasticity:
\varphi(t)=\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{\alpha}-1,

\alpha>\frac{1}{2},

•

Plasticity:
\varphi(t)=t^{\alpha}\left(\log\left(1+t\right)\right)^{\beta},

\alpha\geq 1,\beta>0,

•

Generalized Newtonian fluids:
\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s^{1-\alpha}\left(\sinh^{-1}s\right)^{\beta}ds,

0\leq\alpha\leq 1,\beta>0.

For
\varphi(t)=\varphi(x,t):=p(x)|t|^{p(x)-2}t
;

•

There is a new model for image restoration given in
[
15
]
. In this model, main aim is to recover an image,
u
, from an observed, noisy image,
u_{0}
, where the two are related by
u_{0}=u+noise
. The proposed model incorporates the strengths of the various types of diffusion arising from
the minimization problem

E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u\right|^{p\left(x\right)}+\lambda\left(u-u_{0}\right)^{2}\right]dx

for
1\leq p\left(x\right)\leq 2
, where

is a regularizing term to remove the noise and

We use the theory of Orlicz spaces since problem

\varphi

[

The function
a(x,t):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a function such that the mapping
\varphi(x,t):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\varphi(x,t)=\begin{cases}a(x,|t|)t&\text{ for }t\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{ for }t=0,\end{cases}

(2.1)

and for all
x\in\Omega
,
\varphi(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an odd, increasing homeomorphism. For the function
\varphi
above, if we define

\Phi(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(x,s)ds,\,\,\forall x\in\Omega,\,t\geq 0

(2.2)

then the function
\Phi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a
generalized

N
-
function
if it satisfies the following conditions (see e.g.,
[
1
,
30
,
31
]
):

(\Phi_{0})

for almost all
x\in\Omega
,
\Phi(x,\cdot)
is a
N
-
function
, i.e., convex, nondecreasing and continuous function of
t
such that,
\Phi(x,0)=0
,
\Phi(x,t)>0
for all
t>0
, and

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Phi(x,t)}{t}=0,\qquad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi(x,t)}{t}=+\infty

(\Phi_{1})

\Phi(\cdot,t)
is a measurable function on
\Omega
for all
t\geq 0
.

The set of all generalized
N
-functions is denoted by
N(\Omega)
.
The function
\bar{\Phi}
defined by

\bar{\Phi}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi^{-1}(x,s)ds,\,\,\forall x\in\Omega,t\geq 0

(2.3)

is called the
complementary (or conjugate) function
to
\Phi
, where
\bar{\Phi}
satisfies the following

\bar{\Phi}(x,t)=\sup_{s>0}\{st-\Phi(x,s):~s\in\mathbb{R}\},~\forall x\in\Omega,t\geq 0

It is well known that
\bar{\Phi}\in N(\Omega)
, and then the following Young inequality holds

st\leq\Phi(x,t)+\bar{\Phi}(x,s)~\text{for}~x\in\Omega~\text{and}~t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.4)

The function
\Phi
allow us to define the
Musielak-Sobolev spaces
, also called
the generalized Orlicz spaces
, by

L^{\Phi}(\Omega)=\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}~\text{is measurable};\exists\lambda>0~\text{such that}~\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|u(x)|/\lambda)dx<+\infty\}.

Moreover, by
\Delta_{2}
-
condition
(see below),
L^{\bar{\Phi}}(\Omega)
is the dual space of
L^{\Phi}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
(L^{\Phi}(\Omega))^{*}=L^{\bar{\Phi}}(\Omega)
.

In the sequel, we also use the following assumptions for
\Phi
:

1<\varphi_{0}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi(x,t)}{\Phi(x,t)}\leq\frac{t\varphi(x,t)}{\Phi(x,t)}\leq\varphi^{0}:=\sup_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi(x,t)}{\Phi(x,t)}<\infty,~\forall x\in\Omega,t\geq 0

(2.5)

\einf_{x\in\Omega}\Phi(x,t)>0,~\forall t>0

(2.6)

\text{the function}\,\,\,t\rightarrow\Phi(x,\sqrt{t})\,\,\,\text{is convex, }~\forall x\in\Omega,t\geq 0

(2.7)

By help of assumption (2.5), the Musielak-Sobolev spaces coincides the equivalence classes of measurable functions
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|u(x)|)dx<\infty

(2.8)

and is equipped with the Luxembourg norm

|u|_{\Phi}:=\inf\left\{\mu>0:~\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|u(x)|/\lambda)dx\leq 1\right\}

(2.9)

For the Musielak-Orlicz spaces, Hölder inequality reads as follows (see
[
1
]
,
[
31
]
)

\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\bar{\Phi}}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ for all }u\in L^{\Phi}(\Omega)\text{ and }v\in L^{\bar{\Phi}}(\Omega)

The
Musielak-Sobolev spaces

W^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
is the space defined by

W^{1,\Phi}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{\Phi}(\Omega):~\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{\Phi}(\Omega),~i=1,2,...,N\right\}

under the norm

\|u\|_{1,\Phi}:=|u|_{\Phi}+|\nabla u|_{\Phi}

(2.10)

Now we introduce
Musielak-Sobolev spaces with zero boundary traces

W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
under the norm
\|u\|_{1,\Phi}
. Moreover, by help of the well-known
Poincaré inequality
, we can define an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Phi}
on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
by

\|u\|_{\Phi}:=|\nabla u|_{\Phi}

(2.11)

First, we note that for

The following Proposition generalizes the definition of convexity, and therefore, we give a proof for the convenience.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a vector space and let
I:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then
I
is convex if and only if

I((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)<(1-\lambda)\theta+\lambda\beta,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<1

(2.16)

whenever
I(u)<\theta
and
I(v)<\beta
, for all
u,v\in X
and
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Assume that functional
I:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is convex. Moreover, since
I
is a real-valued functional, there are real numbers
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
I(u)<\theta
and
I(v)<\beta
. Then

I((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)<(1-\lambda)I(u)+\lambda I(v)<(1-\lambda)\theta+\lambda\beta,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<1.

On the other hand, assume that (2.16) holds. Since
I(u)<\theta
and
I(v)<\beta
, we can write, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

I(u)<I(u)+\varepsilon:=\theta

I(v)<I(v)+\varepsilon:=\beta

Therefore,

I((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)<(1-\lambda)I(u)+\lambda I(v)+\varepsilon,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<1

(2.17)

If we consider that (2.17) holds for any
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude

I((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)\leq(1-\lambda)I(u)+\lambda I(v)

∎

3.
The main results

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that the following assumptions hold:

(G0)

g(x)\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
is a nontrivial nonnegative function.

(A0)

\mathcal{A}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function and satisfies the growth condition

m_{1}t^{\alpha-1}\leq\mathcal{A}(t)\leq m_{2}t^{\alpha-1}

where
m_{1},m_{2},\alpha
are real numbers such that
m_{2}\geq m_{1}>1
and
\alpha>1
.

Then problem
1.1
has a positive ground state solution in
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
with a negative energy level.

We define the functional
J:W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
corresponding to problem
1.1
by

J(u)=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(x)|u|^{1-\gamma(x)}}{1-\gamma(x)}dx

where
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\mathcal{A}(s)ds
.

Definition 3.2
.

A function
u
is called a weak solution to problem
1.1
if
u\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
such that
u>0
in
\Omega
and

\mathcal{A}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)\int_{\Omega}a(x,|\nabla u|)\nabla u\cdot\nabla vdx=\int_{\Omega}g(x)u^{-\gamma(x)}vdx

(3.1)

for all
v\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
.

We would like to notice that due to singular term, the derivative operator
J^{\prime}
is not continuous on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
, that is,
J
is not Fréchet differentiable on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we must show that any global minimizer is in fact a solution to problem
1.1
. To this end, to obtain the main result given in Theorem 3.1, it is necessary to show that Lemma 3.3 holds.

Lemma 3.3
.

The functional
J
attains the global minimizer in
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
, that is, there exists a function
u_{*}\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
such that

m=J(u_{*})=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)}J(u)<0

(3.2)

Proof.

By (G0), (A0), Hölder inequality, Proposition 2.3 and the continuous embeddings
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)+\gamma(x)-1}}(\Omega)
, it follows

\displaystyle|J(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)^{\alpha}+\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{1-\gamma(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}+\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}||u|^{1-\gamma(x)}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{1-\gamma(x)}}(\Omega)}|\mathbf{1}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)+\gamma(x)-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}+\frac{c|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}\|u\|^{1-\gamma^{-}}_{\Phi}<+\infty

which shows that
J
is well-defined on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
.

Denote
K:W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
K(u):=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\rho(u))
.
Considering the fact that the functional
\rho
is of class
C^{1}(W^{1,\Phi}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
(see Remark 2.6), and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
is a continuous function, it is easy to see that the composition functional
K
is continuous on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. Further, by the well-known inequality

|a^{p}-b^{p}|\leq|a-b|^{p},\,\,\,\text{for any real numbers}\,\,a,b\geq 0\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<p<1,

we obtain

\displaystyle|J(u)-J(v)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\rho(u))-\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\rho(v))\right|+\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}\int_{\Omega}||u|^{1-\gamma(x)}-|v|^{1-\gamma(x)}|dx

\displaystyle\leq|K(u)-K(v)|+\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|u-v|^{1-\gamma(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq|K(u)-K(v)|+\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}||u-v|^{1-\gamma(x)}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{1-\gamma(x)}}(\Omega)}|\mathbf{1}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)+\gamma(x)-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq|K(u)-K(v)|+\frac{c|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}\|u-v\|^{1-\gamma^{-}}_{\Phi}

for any
u,v\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. Therefore
J
is continuous on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. Then, applying the same steps as we did above, it follows

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m_{1}}{\alpha}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)^{\alpha}-\frac{|g|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma^{+}}||u|^{1-\gamma(x)}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{1-\gamma(x)}}(\Omega)}|\mathbf{1}|_{L^{\frac{p(x)}{p(x)+\gamma(x)-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m_{1}}{\alpha}\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi_{0}}-c\|u\|^{1-\gamma^{-}}_{\Phi}

(3.3)

Since
\alpha\varphi_{0}>1-\gamma^{+}
,
J
is coercive, namely,
J(u)\rightarrow+\infty
as
\|u\|_{\Phi}\rightarrow\infty
, and bounded below.

Now, we shall show that
J
is convex on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. To this end, using (A0) and considering the assumptions for
g
and
\gamma
, we have

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla u|)dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(x)|u|^{1-\gamma(x)}}{1-\gamma(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\max\{\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi_{0}},\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}\}:=\theta

and

\displaystyle J(v)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla v|)dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(x)|v|^{1-\gamma(x)}}{1-\gamma(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\max\{\|v\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi_{0}},\|v\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}\}:=\beta

for all
u,v\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
. Since
\Phi
is convex, so is
\Theta(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla\cdot|)dx
(see
[
18
]
).

Since
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies (A0), it is a continuous and monotone function on
(0,+\infty)
, and hence, its convexity follows. Therefore, for
0<\lambda<1
, we have

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v))\leq(1-\lambda)\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta)+\lambda\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\Theta)

Therefore, considering the all pieces of information obtained above along with (A0) and (F1), it follows

\displaystyle J((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v))

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,|\nabla((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)|)dx\right)-\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(x)|(1-\lambda)u+\lambda v|^{1-\gamma(x)}}{1-\gamma(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\max\{\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi_{0}},\|u\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}\}+\lambda\frac{m_{2}}{\alpha}\max\{\|v\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi_{0}},\|v\|_{\Phi}^{\alpha\varphi^{0}}\}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\theta+\lambda\beta

Hence, by Proposition 2.7,
J
is convex on
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
.

As the functional
J
is continuous, coercive and convex, it has a global minimum belonging to
W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
, which in turn becomes a solution to problem
1.1
.

Let us denote

m=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)}J(u)

which is well-defined due to (3.3).

Now, applying the same arguments used in Remark 2.5, we can obtain that for
t>0
small enough and

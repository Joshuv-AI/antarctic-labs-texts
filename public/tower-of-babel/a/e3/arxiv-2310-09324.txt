Supercurrent-induced spin switching via indirect exchange interaction
Chi Sun, Jacob Linder

Abstract
Localized spins of single atoms adsorbed on surfaces have been proposed as building blocks for spintronics and quantum computation devices. However, identifying a way to achieve current-induced switching of spins with very low dissipation is an outstanding challenge with regard to practical applications. Here, we show that the indirect exchange interaction between spin impurities can be controlled by a dissipationless supercurrent. All that is required is a conventional superconductor and two spin impurities placed on its surface. No triplet Cooper pairs or exotic material choices are needed. This finding provides a new and accessible way to achieve the long-standing goal of supercurrent-induced spin switching.



Introduction –
Electrical manipulation of spin or magnetization is crucial in the development of spintronics devices and technologies for data storage and computation
Brataas et al. 2012
;
Shi et al. 2019
;
Yamada et al. 2007
. Current-induced magnetization switching is presently used in magnetic random access memory through spin-transfer torque
Chun et al. 2013
;
Tudu and Tiwari 2017
. However, the inclusion of electric current inevitably involves Joule heating and therefore high energy dissipation. An important objective is therefore to identify a way to electrically switch the directions of spins with very low dissipation. In spintronics, major efforts have been devoted to optimizing the choice of materials and hybrid structures
Li et al. 2019
;
Fukami et al. 2016
;
Safeer et al. 2016
;
Fan et al. 2014
;
Sun et al. 2019
in order to reduce the power consumption for switching, making it comparable to that in present semiconductor field-effect transistors
Matsukura et al. 2015
.

At low temperatures, an obvious candidate for achieving low-dissipation electric control over magnetism is superconducting materials due to their ability to host dissipationless supercurrents. Combining superconductivity and spintronics
Linder and Robinson 2015
offers possibilities to achieve supercurrent-induced magnetization dynamics, by which Joule heating and dissipation can be minimized. To achieve this, several theory papers have proposed to utilize spin-polarized triplet supercurrents
Eschrig 2015
, which have been experimentenlly verified in superconductor (SC)/ferromagnet (FM) Josephson junctions
Keizer et al. 2006
;
Robinson et al. 2010
;
Khaire et al. 2010
;
Singh et al. 2016
. It has also been theoretically shown that triplet supercurrents can
induce spin-transfer torque switching
Waintal and Brouwer 2002
;
Zhao and Sauls 2008
;
Linder and Yokoyama 2011
and magnetization
dynamics
Buzdin 2008
;
Teber et al. 2010
;
Holmqvist et al. 2011
;
Kulagina and Linder 2014
;
Hals 2016
;
Rabinovich et al. 2019
;
Bobkova et al. 2020
. However, there exists no experimental observation of supercurrent-induced torque or magnetization dynamics. Part of the challenge lies within the complexity of the appropriate fabrication of the SC/FM multilayered structures, in which the SC/FM interface plays an essential role to create the triplet Cooper pairs for spin-polarized supercurrent.

In this work, we theoretically demonstrate a new and conceptually simple way in which the goal of spin switching via singlet supercurrents can be achieved. We consider a conventional SC and two spin impurities placed on its surface (see Fig.
1
). Without the requirement of triplet Cooper pair or exotic material choices, this is a drastically simpler setup than previous studies, theoretical and experimental, that have considered magnetization dynamics in the superconducting state. By investigating the indirect exchange interaction between the two spin impurities, also known as the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction
Ruderman and Kittel 1954
;
Kasuya 1956
;
Yosida 1957
, we find that the spin orientation can be controlled by applying a supercurrent flowing through the SC. In the presence of the supercurrent, the quasiparticle bands become asymmetric in momentum space due to the broken parity symmetry, which also modulates the RKKY interaction. Further, the resulting sign change in the RKKY interaction causes the preferred spin orientation to be switched between parallell and antiparallel alignments, both by varying the magnitude of the supercurrent as well as its direction, providing two experimental routes to
observe this effect.

Theory –
We consider a conventional Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS)
Bardeen et al. 1957
SC with two impurity spins on its surface. For the superconducting part of the Hamilton-operator, the presence of a supercurrent can be modelled by allowing the order parameter to have a phase gradient. Thus, we may write in real space

\displaystyle H_{\text{SC}}=\frac{\Delta_{0}}{2}\sum_{i\alpha\beta}\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}\boldsymbol{Q}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{i}}(\text{i}\sigma^{y})_{\alpha\beta}c_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}c_{i\beta}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}

(1)

Here,
\Delta_{0}
is the magnitude of the superconducting order parameter,
\boldsymbol{Q}
quantifies the magnitude and direction of the supercurrent, whereas
c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}
are electron creation operators at site
i
for spin
\sigma
. For
\boldsymbol{Q}=0
,
H_{\text{SC}}
reduces to the standard BCS Hamiltonian.

The full Hamilton-operator for the superconducting part, which includes a hopping term, takes the form

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}&\sigma\Delta_{0}\\
\sigma\Delta_{0}&-\varepsilon_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}\end{pmatrix}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma},

(2)

after performing a Fourier transformation
c_{i\sigma}^{{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{i}}
where
N
is the total number of the lattice points. Above,
\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}=-2t[\cos(k_{x}a)+\cos(k_{z}a)]-\mu
is the dispersion relation, in which
t
is the hopping parameter,
a
is the lattice constant, and
\mu
is the chemical potential. Here a 2D model in the
xz
-plane is chosen for concreteness and
\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}=(c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\quad c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\sigma})
is the fermion basis. The two pairs of energy eigenvalues and eigenstates of the matrix in Eq. (
2
) are obtained as
E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}
with
(u_{\boldsymbol{k}},\sigma v_{\boldsymbol{k}})^{T}
and
E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
with
(-\sigma v_{\boldsymbol{k}},u_{\boldsymbol{k}})^{T}
, in which
E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\varepsilon_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}\pm\sqrt{(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\varepsilon_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}})^{2}+4\Delta_{0}^{2}})
and

\displaystyle u_{\boldsymbol{k}}(v_{\boldsymbol{k}})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(1+(-)\frac{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\varepsilon_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}}{\sqrt{(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\varepsilon_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}})^{2}+4\Delta_{0}^{2}}})}.

(3)

Based on the eigenpairs, the Hamiltonian is diagonalized as

H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{-})\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma},

(4)

where the operators satisfy

\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}\\
c_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\sigma}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{\boldsymbol{k}}&-\sigma v_{\boldsymbol{k}}\\
\sigma v_{\boldsymbol{k}}&u_{\boldsymbol{k}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\sigma}\\
\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\sigma}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

To model the impurity spins interacting with the SC, we consider a Hamilton-operator which is treated as a perturbation:
\Delta H=J\sum_{j}\boldsymbol{S}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{s}_{j},

in which
J
is the strength of the interaction between the impurity classical spin
\boldsymbol{S}_{j}
and the conduction electron spin
\boldsymbol{s}_{j}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{j\alpha}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}c_{j\beta}
where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
denotes the Pauli matrix vector. We set
S\equiv|\boldsymbol{S}_{j}|=1
, meaning that the magnitude of the impurity spin is absorbed into the coupling constant
J
. After Fourier-transforming and expressing the
c
-operators in terms of
\gamma
-operators described by Eq. (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta H

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}[u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}-\beta u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle-\alpha v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}+\alpha\beta v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}],

(6)

in which
T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}=\sum_{j}\frac{J}{N}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{j}}\boldsymbol{S}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
is defined.

We now perform a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation to obtain the RKKY interaction between the impurity spins, mediated by the SC. This is in essence a second order perturbation theory for
\Delta H
achieved by applying a
canonical transformation
H_{\text{eff}}=e^{\eta S}He^{-\eta S}
for
H=H_{0}+\Delta H
. Subsequently, one identifies
\eta S
so that it satisfies
\Delta H+[\eta S,H_{0}]=0
which projects out the first order effect of the perturbation, which does not generate any interaction between the impurity spins. This
gives rise to the effective Hamiltonian

H_{\text{eff}}=H_{0}+\frac{1}{2}[\eta S,\Delta H],

(7)

in which one can express
\eta S
with the same operators as in Eq. (
6
):
\eta S=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}
[A_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}+B_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{\dagger}+C_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}\gamma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}+
D_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}\gamma_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}].

The coefficients are consequently identified as

\displaystyle A_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}^{+}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{-}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{+}+E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}^{-}},

\displaystyle B_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\beta u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{+}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}^{-}+E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{+}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}^{-}},

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}^{+}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{-}+E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}^{+}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{-}},

\displaystyle D_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha\beta v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}T_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}{{}^{\prime}}\alpha\beta}}{E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\beta}^{+}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}\beta}^{-}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q},-\alpha}^{+}+E_{\boldsymbol{k}\alpha}^{-}}.

(8)

Given
\eta S
, the expectation value of the effective Hamiltonian given by Eq. (
7
) may now be evaluated to obtain the RKKY interaction.

Results and discussion –
Defining
S_{j}^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\boldsymbol{S}_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
and using
\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}S_{j}^{\alpha\beta}S_{i}^{\beta\alpha}=2\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j}
and
\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}\alpha\beta S_{j}^{\alpha\beta}S_{i}^{-\alpha,-\beta}=-2\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j}
, we obtain the expectation value

\langle H_{\text{eff}}\rangle=E_{0}+\Sigma_{ij}E_{\text{RKKY}}\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{j},

(9)

in which
E_{0}
is a constant and
E_{\text{RKKY}}
describes the RKKY interaction. The sum
\sum_{ij}
in Eq. (
9
) is over the two impurity spins. Applying
u_{\boldsymbol{k}}=u_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}
,
v_{\boldsymbol{k}}=v_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}
and
E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\pm}=-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{\mp}
, the RKKY interaction can after lengthy calculations be expressed via the quantities

\displaystyle F_{1}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(|u_{\boldsymbol{k}}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2}+u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}})\frac{n(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})-n(E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{+})}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{+}-E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}},

\displaystyle F_{2}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(-|u_{\boldsymbol{k}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2}+u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}})\frac{n(E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})+n(E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+})-1}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}+E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+}},

\displaystyle F_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}-|u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}}|^{2})\frac{n(E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{+})+n(E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+})-1}{E_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{+}+E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+}},

\displaystyle F_{4}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(u_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}u_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{*}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}+|v_{\boldsymbol{k}}v_{\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}|^{2})\frac{n(E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+})-n(E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+})}{E_{-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+}-E_{-\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}}^{+}},

(10)

in the following form

\displaystyle E_{\text{RKKY}}

\displaystyle=-(\frac{J}{N})^{2}\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{{}^{\prime}})\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_{ij}}\Big[F_{1}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})+F_{2}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+F_{3}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})+F_{4}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\Big]

(11)

where
\boldsymbol{R}_{ij}=\boldsymbol{r}_{j}-\boldsymbol{r}_{i}
and
n(E)=(1+e^{\beta E})^{-1}
denotes the Fermi-Dirac distribution at energy
E
with
\beta=1/k_{B}T
. The above expression can be further simplified since
E_{\text{RKKY}}
is real, and thus the exponential prefactor can be replaced with its corresponding cosine component. Subsequently, one observes that the contribution from
F_{2}
is the same as
F_{3}
, which can be seen by renaming indices
\boldsymbol{k}\leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}
and using that
u,v
are real.

We also give an estimate for the effect of the supercurrent Oersted field acting on the impurity spins via a Zeeman-effect, and show that it is negligible compared to the RKKY interaction. Considering a thin superconducting film of thickness
d
with a critical current density
J_{c}=10^{7}
A/cm
2
, the field at the surface can be approximated as
B=\mu_{0}J_{c}d/2
at the critical supercurrent strength. For
d=15
nm, this gives
B\simeq 10^{-3}
T, corresponding to a very small Zeeman-coupling
E_{Z}\simeq 10^{-5}
meV at about half of the critical current density. This can be compared to
tE_{\text{RKKY}}/J^{2}
in our plots, which is typically of order
10^{-4}
at a separation distance of several lattice sites. Using a weak impurity spin coupling
J=0.05t<\Delta_{0}
, as appropriate for the perturbative approach employed here, we get for
t=500
meV that
E_{\text{RKKY}}\simeq 10^{-4}
meV which is
\gg E_{Z}
. Although this is a rough estimate, we note that larger couplings

, outside the regime of our approach, between the impurity and conduction electron spins are accessible experimentally

. This will make the RKKY interaction even larger, in particular compared to the Oersted-field effect. A thinner SC film decreases the Oersted field further. The effect of supercurrent flow in the strong-coupling regime could be an interesting topic for future studies where in-gap Yu-Shiba-Rusinov states
Yu 1965
;
Shiba 1968
;
Rusinov 1969
are expected to have a more prominent role. The main conclusion of this work, being the tunability of the RKKY interaction and thus the possibility to switch the ground state spin configuration, is expected to hold also in this case.

Concluding remarks –
Our proposed system setup should be experimentally feasible. In Ref.
Kuster et al. 2021
, the RKKY interaction between Cr impurity spins coupled to a SC was studied using scanning tunneling spectroscopy. All that is required in addition to observe the supercurrent-induced spin switching is the application of a current bias to the SC. We hope that the present work will stimulate the anticipated experimental realization of supercurrent-induced spin switching.

Combinatorial and Topological Aspects of Path Posets, and Multipath Cohomology
Luigi Caputi, Carlo Collari, Sabino Di Trani

Abstract
. Multipath cohomology is a cohomology theory for directed graphs, which is defined using the path poset. The aim of this paper is to investigate combinatorial properties of path posets, and to provide computational tools for multipath cohomology. In particular, we develop acyclicity criteria, and provide computations of multipath cohomology groups of oriented linear graphs. We further interpret the path poset as the face poset of a simplicial complex, and we investigate realisability problems.



1.
Introduction

Cohomology theories of directed graphs (shortly, digraphs) have become extremely important tools, and are, nowadays, of central interest for the mathematical and scientific community.
This is mainly due to the emergence of new techniques in Topological Data Analysis, which hinge on (co)homological and homotopical methods.

In this paper we are concerned with a cohomology theory of digraphs called multipath cohomology
[
primo
]
, and denoted by
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}
. This is defined as the poset homology
[
chandler2019posets
,
primo
]
of the path poset (cf.
[
turner
]
).
More abstractly, multipath cohomology can be seen as a functor cohomology, or as a cellular cohomology
[
TurnerEverittCell
]
– see
[
primo
, Section 6]
for a comparison. The main advantage of using poset homology over functor/cellular cohomology is its amenability to computations.
In view of the discussion in
[
primo
, Section 6]
, the computations provided here yield information about the functor and cellular cohomology groups of (a mild modification of) path posets.

The goal of this paper is to analyse how the combinatorics of the path poset may affect multipath cohomology. As a by-product, we develop a number of techniques which can be used to explicitly compute
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{\ast}
with coefficients in a field. A first application is the following theorem (see Theorem

Our interest in the combinatorial properties of the path poset is not limited to the purpose of understanding multipath cohomology groups, but it extends to its connections with the so-called monotone properties.
A property of (di)graphs is called

– and the study of the homotopy type of simplicial complexes associated to monotone properties of (di)graphs is a central topic in combinatorial topology;

The study of a simplicial complex associated to the path poset fits into this framework.

In the last part of the paper we describe the relationship between multipaths and simplicial complexes. We show that for a given digraph

whose (reduced) simplicial cohomology is the multipath cohomology of
{\tt G}

For each

In virtue of this theorem, we reinterpret some of our

Then, we turn to the question of which simplicial complexes arise as multipath complexes. Among others, we observe that all wedges of spheres of the same dimension can be obtained in this way (Example

It remains open the problem of whether all wedges of spheres, or more complicated spaces, can be realised as

On the combinatorial techniques

{\tt G}
its path poset. Then, using the fact that every poset can be seen as a category, certain abstract categorical constructions can be used to define the multipath cohomology of

2.1.
Digraphs and posets

Recall that a
directed graph

{\tt G}
, often shortened to

(V({\tt G}),E({\tt G}))
, called
vertices

v
and

(v,w)
and
(w,v)

By definition, an edge of a digraph is an ordered set of two distinct vertices, say

of

target
of

and

. Furthermore, we say that

is a

(resp. a

) if for every

incident to

we have
v=t(e)
(resp.
v=s(e)
).
Finally, a digraph
{\tt G}
with
n
edges is a
sink
(resp. a
source
)
over
n
vertices
if it has a unique sink (resp. source), and every edge in
{\tt G}
is incident to it.

We now review some basic notions about partially ordered sets.
A
partially ordered set
, or simply

, is a pair
(S,\triangleleft)
consisting of a set
S
and a partial order
\triangleleft
on
S
. A morphism of posets

Example 2.2
.

The

\mathbb{B}(n)
(of size

) is the poset
(\wp(\{0,...,n-1\}),\subset)
, where
\wp

A poset is called a

, if it is isomorphic to the standard Boolean poset
\mathbb{B}(n)
, for some

.

Example 2.3
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a digraph with
n
edges. The poset
(SSG({\tt G}),<)
of
spanning subgraphs
of
{\tt G}
is given by all the spanning subgraphs of
{\tt G}
with order relation given by the property of being a subgraph. The associated covering relation
\prec

{\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\exists\ e\in E({\tt H}^{\prime})\setminus E({\tt H}):{\tt H}^{\prime}={\tt H}\cup e\ .

Then,

is a Boolean poset isomorphic to

– see also
[

.

Given a partial order
\triangleleft

, there is an associated

x\triangleleft y

In order to visually represent posets associated to digraphs, we use covering relations and the associated Hasse graphs.
Recall that the

Recall also that, given a poset

Definition 2.5

Let

(2)

We say that

If

(1)

(x_{1},x_{2})\triangleleft_{S\times S^{\prime}}(y_{1},y_{2})\Longleftrightarrow(x_{1},x_{2})=(y_{1},y_{2})\mbox{ or }x_{1}\triangleleft_{S}y_{1}\mbox{ and }x_{2}\triangleleft_{S^{\prime}}y_{2}.

The definition of product poset is essential to introduce the cone of a poset. Let

be a poset.

Definition 2.6
.

The

, denoted by

, is the product poset

.

As

is (isomorphic to) the poset on the set

, the cone

The covering relation in

is covered by

if and only if

a\triangleleft b
or

a=b
.

2.2.
Path posets

We introduce one of the main tools in the definition of multipath cohomology of directed graphs, the
path poset
associated to a directed graph
{\tt G}
.
By a
simple path
in
G
we mean a sequence of edges
e_{1},...,e_{n}
of
G
such that
s(e_{i+1})=t(e_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
, and no vertex is encountered twice, i.e. if
s(e_{i})=s(e_{j})
or
t(e_{i})=t(e_{j})
, then
i=j
, and is not a cycle, i.e.
s(e_{1})\neq t(e_{n})
.
A connected component of
G
is a sub-graph
{\tt H}
of
G
whose geometric realisation (as CW-complex)
|{\tt H}|
is connected.
Following
[
turner
]
, a
multipath
of
{\tt G}
is a spanning sub-graph such that each connected component is either a vertex or its edges admit an ordering such that it is a simple path.
The set of multipaths of
{\tt G}
has a natural partially ordered structure:

Definition 2.7
.

The
path poset
of
{\tt G}
is the poset
(P({\tt G}),<)
associated to
{\tt G}
, that is, the set of multipaths of
G
ordered by the relation of “being a sub-graph”.

Observe that the order relation makes sense, because each multipath is a subgraph of
{\tt G}
.

Remark 2.8
.

The path poset
P({\tt G})
is a downward closed subposet of a Boolean poset – the Boolean poset of all spanning subgraphs. Therefore, it is faithful and squared.

When the partial order on
P({\tt G})
is not specified, we will always implicitly assume it to be the order relation
<
. Moreover, with abuse of notation, we will also write
P({\tt G})
instead of

.

2.3.
Multipath cohomology

Given a special type of poset coherently assigned to each digraph, and a choice of a sign assignment on it (see Definition

[

]
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Definition 2.10
.

A

on a poset
(S,\triangleleft)
is an assignment of elements
\epsilon_{x,y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to each pair of elements
x,y\in S
with
x\ \widetilde{\triangleleft}\ y

(2)

\epsilon_{x,y}+\epsilon_{y,z}\equiv\epsilon_{x,y^{\prime}}+\epsilon_{y^{\prime},z}+1\mod 2

holds for each square
x\ \widetilde{\triangleleft}\ y,y^{\prime}\ \widetilde{\triangleleft}\ z
.

A priori
, the existence of a sign assignment on a given poset is not clear. For a cohomological sufficient condition for a sign assignment on a given poset see
[

. For the poset of spanning sub-graphs – or, better, for any Boolean poset – and their sub-posets, a sign assignment can be easily described – see
[

.
More generally, one may ask when two sign assignments on a given poset are isomorphic. We refrain here from giving the definition of isomorphism of sign assignments – cf.

together with the differential

\ell({\tt H})=n\end{matrix}}\sum_{\tiny\begin{matrix}{\tt H}^{\prime}\in P({\tt G})\\
{\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime}\end{matrix}}(-1)^{\epsilon({\tt H},{\tt H}^{\prime})}\mu({\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime})\ .

It has been proved that

is a cochain complex
[
primo
, Theorem 4.10]
. Furthermore, the path poset

is squared and faithful by Remark

, hence the homology groups of

do not depend on the sign assignment
\epsilon
used in the definition of

and on the choice of the ordering on

[
primo
, Corollary 3.18 & Proposition 4.11]
. We are ready to give the definition of the multipath cohomology of a directed graph:

Definition 2.12
.

The

\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};A)
of a digraph
{\tt G}
with with coefficients in an algebra

is the homology of the cochain complex

.

Observe that, when

is the ring

, the tensor products in Equation (

) simply give
\mathcal{F}_{R}({\tt H})={R_{c_{0}}}\otimes_{R}\cdots\otimes_{R}R_{c_{k}}\cong R
, for each multipath

. The isomorphism between the tensor powers of

and

itself is given by multiplication

\ell({\tt H})=n\end{matrix}}\sum_{\tiny\begin{matrix}{\tt H}^{\prime}\in P({\tt G})\\
{\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime}\end{matrix}}(-1)^{\epsilon({\tt H},{\tt H}^{\prime})}\mathrm{Id}_{{\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime}}\ .

Identifying
\mathcal{F}_{R}({\tt H})
with a copy of

gives us a a set of linearly independent generators

(as free

indexed by multipaths.

If
\phi\colon{\tt G}_{1}\to{\tt G}_{2}
is a regular morphism of digraphs, then it induces (functorially) a morphism of posets

, as it sends multipaths of

to multipaths of

by restriction.

3.
A Combinatorial Description of Path Posets

The aim of this section is to give a combinatorial description of the path poset associated to a directed graph
{\tt G}
; we show that the path poset can be constructed by gluing together simpler path posets associated to (suitable) subgraphs of
{\tt G}
.
In the follow up, we will deal with disconnected graphs. A straightforward observation is that the multipath cohomology of disconnected graphs is the tensor product of the multipath cohomologies:

Remark 3.1
.

Let
{\tt G}
be the disjoint union of connected digraphs
{\tt G}_{1},\dots,{\tt G}_{n}
. Then, the path poset
P({\tt G})
is the product
P({{\tt G}_{1}})\times\dots\times P({{\tt G}_{n}})
– cf. Equation (
1
). Hence, the multipath cohomology of
{\tt G}
splits as the graded tensor product:

\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{1};\mathbb{K})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{n};\mathbb{K})\ .

In particular, if
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{i};\mathbb{K})=0
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})=0
.

We introduce a gluing operation for directed graphs.

Definition 3.2

(Gluing)
.

Let
{\tt G},{\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}
be digraphs, and
\imath_{1}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{1}
and
\imath_{2}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{2}
be regular morphisms.
The
gluing
of
{\tt G}_{1}
and
{\tt G}_{2}
along
{\tt G}
is the digraph
\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})
defined as follows:

(1)

V(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))\coloneqq V({\tt G}_{1})\sqcup V({\tt G}_{2})/\sim
, where
x\sim y
if, and only if, either
x=y
or
x\in\imath_{1}({\tt G})
,
y\in\imath_{2}({\tt G})
, and
\imath_{1}^{-1}(x)=\imath_{2}^{-1}(y)
;

(2)

([v],[w])\in E(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))
if, and only if, there exist
v^{\prime}\in[v]
,
w^{\prime}\in[w]
, and
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in E({\tt G}_{i})
, where
[\cdot]
denotes an equivalence class with respect to
\sim
.

Roughly speaking,
\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})
is the graph obtained from
{\tt G}_{1}
and
{\tt G}_{2}
by identifying the vertices and edges belonging to the image of
{\tt G}
.

When clear from the context and for ease of notation, we denote the edge
([v],[w])
in the set
E(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))
as
(v,w)
.
For a given graph
{\tt G}
, the operation
\nabla_{{\tt G}}(-,-)
is commutative and associative up to isomorphism of digraphs.
Let
\mathbf{Digraph}
be the category of digraphs and regular morphisms of digraphs. We can reinterpret the gluing as a categorical pushout:

Remark 3.3
.

The operation
\nabla_{{\tt G}}(-,-)
is the categorical push-out – cf.
[
maclane:71
, Section III.3]
– in the category
\mathbf{Digraph}
.
Since
\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})
is an object of
\mathbf{Digraph}
, and since the inclusions of
{\tt G}_{1}
and
{\tt G}_{2}
in
\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})
are regular morphisms of digraphs, we have a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{2}}

in
\mathbf{Digraph}
.
Note that
V(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))
is the push-out of
V({\tt G}_{1})
and
V({\tt G}_{2})
along
V({\tt G})
in the category
\mathbf{Set}
of sets.
Now, given another digraph
{\tt G}^{\prime}
such that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tt G}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{1}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{2}^{\prime}}

commutes, then we get a function
V(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))\to V({\tt G}^{\prime})
since
V(\nabla_{\tt G}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}))
is a push-out in
\mathbf{Set}
. Such a function extends to a map of digraphs by definition of morphism of digraphs, which is injective as it is composition of injective functions.

If
\tt{G}^{\prime}
is a digraph, and

\imath\colon{\tt G}^{\prime}\rightarrow\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2})
and
\jmath\colon{\tt G}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\tt G}_{3}
are regular morphisms, we define:

\nabla_{{\tt G}^{\prime},{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2},{\tt G}_{3})\coloneqq\nabla_{{\tt G}^{\prime}}(\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}),{\tt G}_{3})\ .

In general, if
{\tt G}_{1},\dots,{\tt G}_{n}
is a family of digraphs such that for every
k<n
there exist a (regular) morphisms of digraphs
\imath_{k}\colon{\tt G}\rightarrow{\tt G}_{k}
we will denote
\nabla_{{\tt G},\dots,{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},\dots,{\tt G}_{n})
by
\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},\dots{\tt G}_{n})
.
Note that
\nabla_{{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},\dots{\tt G}_{n})
does not depend, up to isomorphism of digraphs, on the order of the digraphs
{\tt G}_{1},\dots,{\tt G}_{n}
, whereas
\nabla_{{\tt G}^{\prime},{\tt G}}({\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2},{\tt G}_{3})
might.

Definition 3.4
.

The gluing of two posets
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
along a common subposet
P
is the poset, denoted by
\nabla_{P}(P_{1},P_{2})
, whose Hasse diagram is the gluing of
{\tt Hasse}(P_{1})
and
{\tt Hasse}(P_{2})
along
{\tt Hasse}(P)
.

Observe that the gluing does not commute with the operation of taking path posets. To see it, let
{\tt G}_{1}
be the graph
v_{0}
v_{1}
v_{2}

and
{\tt G}_{2}
the graph

v_{1}
v_{2}
v_{3}

and consider the gluing
{\tt G}
of
{\tt G}_{1}
and
{\tt G}_{2}
over
v_{1}
v_{2}
. Then, the poset
P({\tt G})
is isomorphic to the Boolean poset
\mathbb{B}(3)
, whose Hasse diagram is (the
1

3
-dimensional cube. On the other hand, the Hasse diagram of
\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),P({\tt G}_{2}))
is the gluing of two copies of
{\tt Hasse}(\mathbb{B}(2))
along a copy of
{\tt Hasse}(\mathbb{B}(1))
– that is two (empty) squares attached along an edge.

We now relate the path poset of a graph to the gluing of the path posets certain subgraphs. First, for a vertex
v\in V({\tt G})
, consider the set
E_{v}
of edges
e_{1},\dots e_{n}
in
{\tt G}
incident to
v
, ordered so that
v=t(e_{i})
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, and
v=s(e_{j})
, for
j=k+1,\dots,n
. Denote by
{\tt G}_{v}^{k}
the graph obtained by deleting the edges
e_{1},\dots e_{k}
from
{\tt G}
, and set
{\tt G}_{v}^{(h)}\coloneqq{\tt G}_{v}^{k}\cup e_{h}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

If the vertex
v
is a target for
k\geq 2
edges, then

P({\tt G})\cong\nabla_{P({{\tt G}_{v}^{k}})}\left(P\left({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}\right),\dots,P\left({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)}\right)\right)\ .

In other words, the path poset
P({\tt G})
is isomorphic to an iterated gluing of the path posets of the subgraphs
{\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}
, … ,
{\tt G}_{v}^{(k)}
over the path poset of
{{\tt G}_{v}^{k}}
.

Proof.

We recall that, if
{\tt G}^{\prime}
is a subgraph of a digraph
{\tt G}
, then every multipath in
P({\tt G}^{\prime})
can be seen as a multipath in
P({\tt G})
.
This means that
P({\tt G}^{\prime})
can be seen as an downward closed (and, in particular, faithful) subposet of
P({\tt G})
.

To prove the theorem, we want to produce an isomorphism of posets

P({\tt G})\cong\nabla_{P\left({{\tt G}_{v}^{k}}\right)}\left(P\left({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}\right),\dots,P\left({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)}\right)\right).

First, we start by identifying the underlying sets and, then, we proceed with proving that the respective poset structures are isomorphic.

For the rest of the proof, denote by
T
the set
\{e\in E({\tt G})\mid t(e)=v\}
.
Let
{\tt H}
be a multipath in
P({\tt G})
. We have two possible cases:

Case 1:

{\tt H}
does not contain any edge in
T
. Then, all simple paths in
{\tt H}
are simple paths in
{\tt G}_{v}^{k}
, and thus
{\tt H}\in P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
.

Case 2:

{\tt H}
contains an edge
e_{h}\in T
. In this case
{\tt H}
cannot contain any other
e_{j}\in T
. Therefore, we have
{\tt H}\in P({\tt G}_{v}^{(h)})\setminus P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
.

On the other hand, observe that any multipath
{\tt H}\in\nabla_{P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})}\left(P({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}),\dots,P({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)})\right)
can be identified with either an element of
P({\tt G}_{v}^{(j)})\setminus P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
, for some
j\in\{1,...,k\}
, or with an element of the poset
P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
. Thus, we have a way to uniquely identify
{\tt H}
with an element of
P({\tt G})
and, consequently, the underlying sets of
P({\tt G})
and
\nabla_{P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})}\left(P({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}),\dots,P({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)})\right)
. With abuse of notation, we denote this element again by
{\tt H}
.

Now, we want to prove that the general multipath
{\tt H}
covers the same elements both in
P({\tt G})
and
\nabla_{P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})}\left(P({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}),\dots,P({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)})\right)
. This is obvious if
{\tt H}
is a multipath of
P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
. Assume that
{\tt H}
is in
P({\tt G})\setminus P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})
. Then, there exists a unique
e_{j}\in T
such that
e_{j}\in{\tt H}
. A multipath covered by
{\tt H}
is then a multipath in
P({\tt G}_{v}^{(j)})
and consequently the same covering relations hold in
\nabla_{P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})}\left(P({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}),\dots,P({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)})\right)
.
Finally, if
{\tt H}\in\nabla_{P({\tt G}_{v}^{k})}\left(P({\tt G}_{v}^{(1)}),\dots,P({\tt G}_{v}^{(k)})\right)
, then all the elements covered by
{\tt H}
are contained in
P({\tt G}_{v}^{(j)})
and it is possible to conclude the proof because
P({\tt G}_{v}^{(j)})
in an downward closed subposet of
P({\tt G})
.
∎

4.
Applications to Multipath Cohomology

In this section, we prove a Mayer-Vietoris-type theorem, and some aciclicity criteria for multipath cohomology with coefficients in a field.

4.1.
The cohomology of the cone construction

Recall first that a poset
P=(S,\triangleleft)
can be seen as a category
\mathbf{P}
.
Given a functor
\mathcal{F}
from
\mathbf{P}
to the category of vector spaces, with some mild assumptions on
P
, there are well-defined cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{*}(\mathbf{P};\mathcal{F})
of
\mathbf{P}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{F}
– cf.
[
primo
, Theorem 3.7]
– which, when
P=P({\tt G})
is the path poset of a digraph
{\tt G}
, gives the multipath cohomology.

We denote by
(C^{*}(P;\mathbb{K}),\partial_{P})
the cochain complex
(C^{*}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{K}}}(P),\partial)
associated to a poset
P
(and level
\ell
) and to the functor
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{K}}
assigning a copy of
\mathbb{K}
to each object of
\mathbf{P}
and the identity
\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{K}
to each arrow. Analogously, we denote by
\mathrm{H}^{*}(P;\mathbb{K})
the cohomology groups of the cochain complex
(C^{*}(P;\mathbb{K}),\partial_{P})
.
Recall that for a map
f\colon(A,\partial_{A})\to(B,\partial_{B})
of cochain complexes, the mapping cone
{\rm Cone}f
is the cochain complex defined in degree
n
as
({\rm Cone}f)^{n}\coloneqq A^{n+1}\oplus B^{n}
and differential

\partial\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{A}[1]&0\\
f[1]&\partial_{B}\end{pmatrix}

where
\partial_{A}[1]
and
f[1]
represent the differential
\partial_{A}
and the morphism
f
shifted by one.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a digraph. Then, we get an isomorphism of cochain complexes

C^{*}({\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G});\mathbb{K})\cong{\rm Cone}\left({\rm Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}\right){[-1]}

where
{\rm Cone}\left({\rm Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}\right)
represents the mapping cone of the identity map on the cochain complex
C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})
.
Consequently, we have
\mathrm{H}^{*}({\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G});\mathbb{K})=0
.

To simplify the notation, we drop the reference to
\mathbb{K}
in the proof of the theorem.

Proof.

Recall that the cone of a poset
P
is the product poset
P\times\mathbb{B}(1)
– cf. Definition
2.6
.
Consider the partition
P({\tt G})\times\{0\}\sqcup P({\tt G})\times\{1\}={\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G})
.
Furthermore, we also have that
\ell_{{\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G})}({\tt H},i)=\ell_{P({\tt G})\times\{i\}}({\tt H},i)+i=\ell_{P({\tt G})}({\tt H})+i
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
.
As a consequence, we have the isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces

C^{*}({\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G}))\cong C^{*}(P({\tt G})\times\{0\})\oplus C^{*}(P({\tt G})\times\{1\})[-1]

where
C^{*}(P({\tt G}))[-1]
denotes the complex
C^{*}(P({\tt G}))
shifted by one.
In turn, we have an isomorphism of posets
P({\tt G})\cong P({\tt G})\times\{i\}
, for
i=0,1
, given by the identification
{\tt H}\mapsto({\tt H},i)
.
These identifications induce the isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces

C^{*}(P({\tt G})\times\{0\})\oplus C^{*-1}(P({\tt G})\times\{1\})\cong C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G})\oplus C_{\mu}^{*-1}({\tt G})={\rm Cone}\left({\rm Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}\right)[-1].

Now, we have to show that the above isomorphism commutes with the differentials.
The differential
\partial
of
{\rm Cone}\left({\rm Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}\right)[-1]
is defined as

\partial\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{\mu}&0\\
\mathrm{Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}&\partial_{\mu}[-1]\end{pmatrix}

where
(\partial_{\mu}[{-1}])^{n}\coloneqq(-1)^{n}\partial_{\mu}^{{n-1}}
.
The differential of
C^{*}({\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G}))
, can be explicitly written:

\partial_{{\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G})}(e_{({\tt H},i)})=(1-i)e_{({\tt H},1)}+\sum_{{\tt H}\prec{\tt H}^{\prime}}(-1)^{\epsilon({\tt H},{\tt H}^{\prime})+i}e_{({\tt H}^{\prime},i)}

where

is a sign assignment on
P({\tt G})
and
e_{({\tt H},i)}
is the generator of

associated to the multipath
{\tt H}
in
{\tt G}
. It is easy to check that
\epsilon^{\prime}(({\tt H},i),({\tt H}^{\prime},j))\coloneqq\epsilon({\tt H},{\tt H}^{\prime})+i
is a sign assignment on
{\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G})
.

The isomorphism
C^{*}({\mathfrak{Cone}\penalty\ }P({\tt G}))\cong{\rm Cone}\left({\rm Id}_{C^{*}_{\mu}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})}\right)[-1]
described above commutes with these differentials, concluding the proof of the first part of the statement.
The vanishing result follows from the classical properties of the mapping cone of chain complexes
[
Weibel
]
.
∎

We observe that the second part of Theorem

if we consider the edges in

4.2.
A Mayer-Vietoris theorem

The goal of this subsection is to prove a result which is the analogue, in the framework of multipath cohomology, of the classical Mayer-Vietoris theorem. In the classical statement, given a decomposition of a topological space as union of two subspaces, there is an induced long exact sequence of (co-)homology groups featuring also their intersections. In the setting of multipath cohomology, the
rôle
played by unions of topological spaces is given by the gluing of posets. Recall that, for
\phi\colon{\tt G}^{\prime}\to{\tt G}
, we have an induced morphism of posets
P\phi\colon P({\tt G}^{\prime})\to P({\tt G})
– see
[
primo
, Remark 2.33]
. Furthermore,

[
primo
, Proposition 5.11]

gives us a map between the multipath cochain complex of a graph
{\tt G}
, and the multipath cochain complex of a sub-graph
{\tt G}_{1}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\tt G},{\tt G}_{1},{\tt G}_{2}
be directed graphs, and
i_{1}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{1}
and
i_{2}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{2}
be regular morphisms of digraphs. Then, we have a short exact sequence of cochain complexes

(5)

0\rightarrow C^{*}(\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),\,P({\tt G}_{2}));\mathbb{K})\xrightarrow{I^{*}}C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{1};\mathbb{K})\oplus C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{2};\mathbb{K})\xrightarrow{J^{*}}C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})\rightarrow 0

inducing the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{i-1}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),\,P({\tt G}_{2}));\mathbb{K})\to\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{i}({\tt G}_{1};\mathbb{K})\oplus\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{i}({\tt G}_{2};\mathbb{K})\to\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{i}({\tt G};\mathbb{K})\to\cdots

of cohomology groups.

Proof.

We first observe that, as a consequence of the definition of gluing – cf. Definition
3.2
–
P({\tt G}_{1})
and
P({\tt G}_{2})
are isomorphic to subposets of
\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),\,P({\tt G}_{2}))
; call
\jmath_{1}
and
\jmath_{2}
these isomorphisms.
The inclusions of graphs
i_{1}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{1}
and
i_{2}\colon{\tt G}\to{\tt G}_{2}
induce morphisms of posets
\imath_{1}\colon P({\tt G})\rightarrow P({\tt G}_{1})
and
\imath_{2}\colon P({\tt G})\rightarrow P({\tt G}_{2})
. All the resulting morphisms fit into the following commutative square of posets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P({\tt G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P({\tt G}_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P({\tt G}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),\,P({\tt G}_{2}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\imath_{2}}

and, induce a commutative diagram of cochain complexes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{*}(\nabla_{P({\tt G})}(P({\tt G}_{1}),\,P({\tt G}_{2}));\mathbb{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{1};\mathbb{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{2};\mathbb{K})}

5.

This section is devoted to the study of the multipath cohomology of oriented linear graphs.
Firstly, we focus on the case of coefficients in a field

5.1.
Multipath cohomology of linear graphs

An
oriented linear graph

L
(on
n

\{v_{0},...,v_{n-1}\}

and

An oriented linear graph
{\tt L}
is called

if
whenever
(v_{i-1},v_{i})\in E({\tt L})
for some

(v_{i+1},v_{i})\in E({\tt L})
and, analogously, if
(v_{i},v_{i-1})\in E({\tt L})
then
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E({\tt L})
.
We denote by
{\tt A}_{n}
an alternating linear graph on
n+1
vertices.
Observe that the alternating graph
{\tt A}_{n}
is unique up to orientation reversing.

Definition 5.1
.

A vertex of an oriented linear graph
{\tt L}
is called

if it is both a source and a target, and
stable
otherwise. We denote by

the set of stable vertices of

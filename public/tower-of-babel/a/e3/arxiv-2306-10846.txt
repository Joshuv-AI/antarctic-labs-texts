Transience of continuous-time conservative random walks
Satyaki Bhattacharya, Stanislav Volkov

Abstract
We consider two continuous-time generalizations of conservative random walks introduced in [ 10 , Englander and Volkov (2022)] , an orthogonal and a spherically symmetrical one; the latter model is also known as random flights . For both models, we show the transience of the walks when d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 and the rate of changing of direction follows power law t − α t^{-\alpha} , 0 < α ≤ 1 0<\alpha\leq 1 , or the law ( ln ⁡ t ) − β (\ln t)^{-\beta} where β > 2 \beta>2 .

: 60G50, 60J05, 60J75

Keywords
: Random flight, non-time-homogeneous Markov chain, conservative random walk, transience, recurrence

Introduction

Conservative random walk (discrete time) was introduced in
[
10
]
as time-nonhomogeneous Markov chain
X_{n}
,
n=1,2,\dots
, defined as a process on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that for given (non-random) sequence
p_{1},p_{2},\dots
, where all
p_{i}\in(0,1)
, the walk at time
n
with probability
p_{n}
randomly picks one of the
2d
directions parallel to the axis, and otherwise it continues moving in the direction it was going before. This walk can be viewed as generalization of Gillies random walk
[
7
]
A special interesting case studied in
[
10
]
was when
p_{n}\to 0
, and in particular when
p_{n}\sim n^{-\alpha}
where
\alpha\in(0,1]
;
note that the case
\alpha>1
is trivial as the walk would make only finally many turns. The question of recurrence vs. transience of this walk was one of the main questions of that paper.

Similar processes were known in the literature under different names. Some of the earliest papers which mention the continuous process with memory of this type were probably
[
8
,
12
]
. The term
persistent random was
used in
[
3
,
4
]
; in these papers some very general criteria of recurrence vs. transience were investigated. A planar motion with just three directions was studied in
[
6
]
. A book on
Markov random flights
was recently published by Kolesnik
[
14
]
.
Planar random motions with drifts
with four directions/speeds, switching at Poisson times, were studied in
[
16
]
. Applications of telegraph processes to option pricing can be found in
[
18
]
. Characteristic functions of correlated random walks were studied in
[
5
]
.

The main difference between the conservative random walk and most of the models studied in the literature (except, perhaps,
[
19
]
, which has a more applied focus) is that the underlying process of switching the direction is
time-inhomogeneous
, thus creating various new phenomena. The recurrence/transience of discrete space conservative random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{1}
was thoroughly studied in
[
9
]
(please also see the references therein), and we believe that the continuous time version in one dimension will have very similar features. The establishment of recurrence in one dimension is more or less equivalent to finding that
\limsup
of the process is
+\infty
and
\liminf
of the process is
-\infty
, while in higher dimensions the situation is much more intricate. Hence, we concentrate on the case when the dimension of the space is at least 2, except Theorem
1
which deals with the embedded process.

Below we introduce formally the two versions of a
continuous-time
conservative random walk on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.

Model A (orthogonal model).
Let
\lambda(t)
be a non-negative function such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda(T)&=\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\quad\text{for all }T\geq 0;\\
\lim_{T\to\infty}\Lambda(T)&=\infty.\end{split}

(1)

Let
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\dots
be the consecutive points of a non-homogeneous Poisson point process (PPP for short) on
[0,\infty)
with rate
\lambda(t)
, and
\tau_{0}=0
. Then conditions (
1
) will guarantee that there will be finitely many
\tau_{i}
’s in every finite interval and that
\tau_{n}\to\infty
. Let
{\bf f}_{0},{\bf f}_{1},{\bf f_{2}}
be an i.i.d. sequence of vectors, each of which has a uniform distribution on the set of
2d
unit vectors
\{\pm{\bf e}_{1},\pm{\bf e}_{2},\dots,\pm{\bf e}_{d}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The (orthogonal, continuous-time) conservative walk generated by the rate function
\lambda(\cdot)
is a process
Z(t)
,
t\geq 0
, in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, such that
Z(0)=0
and at each time
\tau_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, the walk starts moving in the direction
{\bf f}_{k}
, and keeps moving in this direction until time
\tau_{k+1}
, when it updates its direction. Formally, define

N(t)=\sup\{k\geq 0:\ \tau_{k}\leq t\},

as the number of points of the PPP by time
t
, then

Z(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{N(t)-1}\left(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\right){\bf f}_{k}+\left(t-\tau_{N(t)}\right){\bf f}_{N(t)}.

We can also define the embedded process
W_{n}=Z(\tau_{n})
so that
W(0)=0
and for
n\geq 1

W_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\tau_{k+1}-\tau_{k}\right){\bf f}_{k}.

The process
Z(t)
can be viewed as a continuous equivalent of the conservative random walk introduced in
[
10
]
.

Model B (von Mises–Fisher model).
This model is defined similarly to the previous one, except that now random vectors
{\bf f}_{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots
, have a uniform distribution on a
d-
dimensional unit sphere
\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
, often called
von Mises–Fisher distribution
, instead of just on
2d
unit vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Please note that this model is similar to the “random flights” model studied e.g. in
[
17
]
, however, their results are only for a time-homogeneous Poisson process, unlike our case.

The results that we obtain in the current paper are somewhat different for the two models, however, since they share a lot of common features, certain statements will hold for both of them. The main goal is establishing transience vs. recurrence of the walks, defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Let
\rho\geq 0
. We say that the walk
Z(t)
is
\rho-
recurrent, if there is an infinite sequence of times
t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots
converging to infinity, such that
Z(t_{i})\in[-\rho,\rho]^{d}
for all
i=1,2,\dots
.

We say that the walk
Z(t)
is transient, if it is not
\rho-
recurrent for any
\rho>0
, i.e. equivalently
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|Z(t)\|=\infty
.

Recurrence and transience of the embedded process
W_{n}
are defined analogously, with the exception that instead of
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\dots
in the above definition, we have a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
n_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots
.

Remark 1
.

Note that a priori it is unclear if transience and recurrence are zero-one events, neither can we easily rule out a possibility of “intermediate” situations (e.g.
\rho-
recurrence only for some
\rho
).

Our main result, which shows transience for two type of rates, are presented in Theorems
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
.

There exists

Suppose we have an inhomogeneous PPP with rate

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}},\qquad t>0,

(3)

The following statement is probably known, but for the sake of completeness, we provide its short proof.

Claim 1
.

Let
Z
be a Poisson random variable with rate
\mu>0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\frac{3\mu}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{3\ln(3/2)-1}{2}\mu}=e^{-0.108\dots\mu},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{1-\ln 2}{2}\mu}=e^{-0.153\dots\mu}.

Proof.

By Markov inequality, since
\mathbb{E}e^{uZ}=e^{\mu(e^{u}-1)}
, we have for

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\frac{3\mu}{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(e^{uZ}\geq e^{\frac{3\mu u}{2}}\right)\leq e^{-\frac{3\mu u}{2}}\mathbb{E}e^{uZ}=e^{-\mu\left[\frac{3u}{2}-e^{u}+1\right]}.

Setting
u=\ln(3/2)
yields the first inequality in the claim.

For the second inequality, we use

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(e^{-uZ}\geq e^{-\frac{\mu u}{2}}\right)\leq e^{\frac{\mu u}{2}}\mathbb{E}e^{-uZ}=e^{-\mu\left[-\frac{u}{2}-e^{-u}+1\right]}.

Now let
u=\ln 2
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that the rate of the PPP is given by (
3
). For some
c_{1}>c_{0}>0
, depending on

only, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{k}\leq c_{0}k^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-k/15};\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{k}\geq c_{1}k^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\right)\leq e^{-k/15}.

Proof.

Recall that
N(s)
denote the number of points of the PPP by time

N(s)\sim Poi(\Lambda(s))
and

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{k}\leq s)=\mathbb{P}(N(s)\geq k).

Let
T_{n}=\Lambda^{(-1)}(n)=\sqrt[1-\alpha]{(1-\alpha)n}

Noting that
N(T_{n})\sim Poi(n)
for all

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{k}\leq T_{2k/3})=\mathbb{P}\left(N(T_{2k/3})\geq k\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(Z\geq\frac{3}{2}\mu\right)\leq e^{-0.07213\dots k}.

by Claim
1
, with
Z\sim Poi(\mu)
where
\mu=2k/3
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{k}\geq T_{2k})=\mathbb{P}(N(T_{2k})\leq k)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu\right)\leq e^{-0.30685\dots k}.

by Claim
1
, with
Z\sim Poi(\mu)
where
\mu=2k
.
Note that
0.07213>\frac{1}{15}
,
0,30685>\frac{1}{15}
.

Now the statement follows with
c_{0}=\sqrt[1-\alpha]{2(1-\alpha)/3}
and
c_{1}=\sqrt[1-\alpha]{2(1-\alpha)}
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let

Assume w.l.o.g. that

Lemma 3

For

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{i}\in[x-\rho,x+\rho]\mid\mathcal{D}\right)\leq\ \frac{c\rho}{n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Given
\tau_{i-2}=t_{i-2}

\tau_{i}=t_{i}

\tau_{i-1}
has the distribution of the only point of the PPP on

Assume w.l.o.g. that

Lemma 5

Theorem 3
.

Let

, and the rate be given by

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem

Lemma 6

Let

Then

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda(T)}{T(\ln T)^{-\beta}}=1

.

Fix an

At the same time, trivially,

Now the limit of the ratio of the upper and the lower bounds of
{\Lambda(T)}

The rest of the proof goes along the same lines as that of Theorem

(1)

First note that for some

Lemma 7
.

Suppose

Remark 3

The statement is trivially true for the case

Proof.

We will use the following well-known representation (see e.g.
[
2

{\bf f}=\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{\|\eta\|},\dots,\frac{\eta_{d}}{\|\eta\|}\right)

where

\|\eta\|=\sqrt{\eta_{1}^{2}+\dots+\eta_{d}^{2}}

For some large enough

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{i=3,\dots,d}|\eta_{i}|<A\right)=\left(\Phi(A)-\Phi(-A)\right)^{d-2}\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}},

On the intersection of these two independent events we have

Remark 4
.

In fact, one can rigorously compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(f_{1}^{2}+f_{2}^{2}\geq\gamma^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}{\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}+\dots+\eta_{d}^{2}}\geq\gamma^{2}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}}(\eta_{3}^{2}++\dots+\eta_{d}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{2}(2)\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}}\chi^{2}(d-2)\right)=\iint_{x\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}}y\geq 0}\frac{e^{-x/2}}{2}\times\frac{y^{d/2-2}e^{-y/2}}{2^{d/2-1}\Gamma(d/2-1)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y}{2}\cdot\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2(1-\gamma^{2})}}\times\frac{y^{d/2-2}e^{-y/2}}{2^{d/2-1}\Gamma(d/2-1)}\,\mathrm{d}y=(1-\gamma^{2})^{d/2-1},

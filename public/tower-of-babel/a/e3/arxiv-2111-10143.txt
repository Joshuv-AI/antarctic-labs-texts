Hilbert genus fields of some number fields with high degrees
Mohamed Mahmoud Chems-Eddin, Moulay Ahmed Hajjami, Mohammed Taous

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to give some properties of Hilbert genus fields and construct the Hilbert genus fields of the fields L m , d := ℚ ⁡ ( ζ 2 m , d ) L_{m,d}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{m}},\sqrt{d}) , where m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 is a positive integer and d d is a square-free integer whose prime divisors are congruent to ± 3 ( mod 8 ) \pm 3\pmod{8} or 9 ( mod 16 ) 9\pmod{16} .

Introduction

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

This question have been investigated by many mathematicians for fields of small degrees (eg.
2
and
4

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{d})
, for
a prime number

or

(cf.
[
1

Thereafter, Ouyang and Zhang have determined the Hilbert genus field of the imaginary biquadratic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{d})
, where
\delta=-1,-2
or
-p
with
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
a prime number and
d
any square-free integer. Thereafter, they constructed the Hilbert genus field of real biquadratic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{d})
,
for any positive square-free integer
d
, and
\delta=p,2p
or
p_{1}p_{2}
where
p
,
p_{1}
and

are prime numbers congruent to

, such that the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\delta})

[
10
,
11
]
). For more works on this subject, we refer the reader to the papers
[
11
,
12
,
5
,

]

In this paper, we shall construct the Hilbert genus field of the fields

,

2.
Some results on Hilbert genus fields

Let us start this section by recalling some definitions and results. Let
k
be a number field and denote by
\mathbf{C}l(k)
its class group. It is know by class field theory that we have
\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{Gal}(H(k)/k)\simeq\mathbf{C}l(k)
, where

H(k)
is the Hilbert class field. The
Hilbert genus field of
k
is defined as the invariant field
E(k)
of
\mathrm{G}^{2}
. Then, we have:

\mathbf{C}l(k)/\mathbf{C}l(k)^{2}\simeq\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{G}^{2}\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(E(k)/k),

and thus,
r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k))
=
r_{2}(\mathrm{Gal}(E(k)/k))
. On the other hand,
E(k)/k
is the maximal unramified Kummer extension of exponent
2
. Thus, by Kummer theory (cf.
[
9
, p. 14]
), there exists a unique multiplicative group
\Delta(k)
such that

E(k)=H(k)\cap k(\sqrt{k^{*}})=k(\sqrt{\Delta(k)})\text{ and }{k^{*}}^{2}\subset{\Delta(k)}\subset k^{*}.

Therefore, the construction of the Hilbert genus field of
k
is equivalent to the determination of a set of generators for the finite group
\Delta(k)/k{{}^{*}}^{2}
.

We need the following proposition which is a particular case of the result in
[
8
, p. 239]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 5.20)
.

Let
k/k^{\prime}
be a quadratic extension of number fields and
\alpha
a element of
k^{\prime}
, coprime with
2
, such that
k=k^{\prime}(\sqrt{\alpha})
. The extension
k/k^{\prime}
is unramified at all finite primes of
k^{\prime}
if and only if the two following items hold:

1.

the ideal generated by
\alpha
is the square of the fractional ideal of
k^{\prime}
, and

2.

there exists a nonzero number
\xi
of
k^{\prime}
verifying
\alpha\equiv\xi^{2}\pmod{4}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
k/k^{\prime}
be an extension of number fields and let
\alpha\in k^{\prime}
be a square-free in
k
. If
k^{\prime}(\sqrt{\alpha})/k^{\prime}
is unramified, then
k(\sqrt{\alpha})/k
is also unramified.

Proof.

As
k^{\prime}(\sqrt{\alpha})/k^{\prime}
is unramified, then there exist an ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}
such that
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{b}^{2}
and
\xi\in k^{\prime}
verifying
\alpha\equiv\xi^{2}\pmod{4}
.
Therefore,
\alpha\mathcal{O}_{k}=(\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{k})^{2}
and
\xi\in k^{\prime}
verifying
\alpha\equiv\xi^{2}\pmod{4}
. So the result by
Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Let us put the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

A number field
k
is said QO-field if it is a quadratic extension of certain number field
k^{\prime}
whose class number is odd. We shall call
k^{\prime}
a base field of the QO-field
k
and the
k/k^{\prime}
is a QO-extension.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k/k^{\prime}
be a QO-extension. Let
\Delta(k)
denote the multiplicative group such that
{k^{*}}^{2}\subset{\Delta(k)}\subset k^{*}
and
k(\sqrt{\Delta(k)})
the Hilbert genus field of
k

(
see the beginning of this section
)
. Then

r_{2}(\Delta(k)/{k^{*}}^{2})=t-e-1,

with
t
is the number of ramified primes (finite or infinite) in the extension
k/k^{\prime}
and
e
is defined by
2^{e}=[E_{k^{\prime}}:E_{k^{\prime}}\cap N_{k/k^{\prime}}(k^{*})]
, where
E_{k^{\prime}}
is the unit group of
k^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Put
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{Gal}(H(k)/k)
.
By the definition of
E(k)

(
see the beginning of this section
)
we have:

\mathbf{C}l(k)/\mathbf{C}l(k)^{2}\simeq\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{G}^{2}\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(E(k)/k),

Since
E(k)=k(\sqrt{\Delta(k)})
, then by class field theory:

r_{2}(\Delta(k)/{k^{*}}^{2})=\log_{2}[k(\sqrt{\Delta(k)}):k]=\log_{2}[E(k):k]=r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k)).

So the result by the well known ambiguous class number formula (cf.
[
13
, Lemma 2.4]
). ∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
k=k^{\prime}(\sqrt{\mu})
be a ramified quadratic extension of QO-fields that have the same base field. Assume furthermore, that
r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k))=r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k^{\prime}))
.
Then we have

E(k)=k(E(k^{\prime})).

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{1}
, …,
\gamma_{r}
be
r=r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k^{\prime}))
elements of
k^{\prime}
such that,

E(k^{\prime})=k^{\prime}(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}},...,\sqrt{\gamma_{r}}).

By Corollary
2.2
,
k(\sqrt{\gamma_{i}})/k
is unramified for all
i
. Therefore,
\{{\gamma_{1}},...,{\gamma_{r}}\}
is a representative set of

\Delta(k)/k^{2}
. In fact, it suffices to prove that it is linearly independent modulo
{k^{*}}^{2}
. Put
\beta={\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}^{a_{2}}...{\gamma_{r}}^{a_{r}}
, for some
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, and assume that
\beta\in k^{2}
. One can easily check that we have
\beta\in k^{\prime 2}
or
\mu\beta\in k^{\prime 2}
.

\bullet

Since
\{{\gamma_{1}},...,{\gamma_{r}}\}
is a representative set of

\Delta(k^{\prime})/k^{\prime 2}
, the case
\beta\in k^{2}
, is clearly impossible.

\bullet

Assume that
\mu\beta\in k^{\prime 2}
. Thus,
\mu{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}^{a_{2}}...{\gamma_{r}}^{a_{r}}=\alpha^{2}
, for some
\alpha\in k^{\prime}
.
Since
k/k^{\prime}
is ramified, this equality implies that there is a square-free element
\mu^{\prime}
of
k^{\prime}
such that
\mu^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime 2}
, for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in k^{\prime}
, which is impossible.

Since
r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k))=r
, we have the result. ∎

Thus, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
k
be a QO-number field,
k^{\prime}
its base field and
k_{n}
be the
n
th layer of its cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension. Let
n_{0}
be an integer such that every prime which ramify in
k_{\infty}/k_{n_{0}}
is totally ramified and
r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k_{n_{0}}))=r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k_{n_{0}+1}))=r
. Assume furthermore that
k_{n_{0}+1}
is a QO-field whose base field is the
(n_{0}+1)
th layer of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension of
k^{\prime}
.
If
E(k)=k_{n_{0}}(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}},...,\sqrt{\gamma_{r}})
and
k_{n_{0}}^{\prime}
has odd class number, then

E(k_{n})=k_{n}(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}},...,\sqrt{\gamma_{r}}),

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Furthermore,
E(k_{\infty})=k_{\infty}(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}},...,\sqrt{\gamma_{r}}).

Proof.

Under the hypothesis of the corollary, one can use the well known result of Fukuda (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 1]
) to check what follows:

\bullet

The class number of
k^{\prime}_{n}
, the
n
th layer of cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension of
k^{\prime}
, is odd for all
n
.

\bullet

r_{2}(\mathbf{C}l(k_{n}))=r
, for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Therefore, a recurrent application of Theorem
2.5
gives the corollary.
∎

The Talenti comparison result in a quantitative form
Vincenzo Amato, Rosa Barbato, Alba Lia Masiello, Gloria Paoli

Abstract
In this paper, we obtain a quantitative version of the classical comparison result of Talenti for elliptic problems with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The key role is played by quantitative versions of the Pólya-Szegő inequality and of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality. Keywords: Laplace operator; Talenti comparison; stability MSC 2020: 35J05, 35J25, 35B35

Introduction

Symmetrization techniques in the context of qualitative properties of solutions to second-order elliptic boundary value problems have been widely studied in the last decades. In the seminal paper
[
42
]
, Talenti considers an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
u=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1)

where
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
if
n>2
,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p>1
if
n=2
, and he proves that it is possible to compare the solution to (
1
) with the solution to the symmetrized problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f^{\sharp}&\text{in}\;\Omega^{\sharp}\\
v=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

(2)

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball centered in the origin with the same measure as
\Omega
and
f^{\sharp}
is the Schwarz rearrangement of
f
(see Definition
2.3
). More precisely, the following point-wise comparison is proved

u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{\sharp},

(3)

and it also holds for a generic uniformly elliptic linear operator in divergence form.

The result by Talenti is a very powerful tool, as it adapts well to solving various types of variational problems. For instance, let us mention Saint Venant’s conjecture, which asserts that the torsional rigidity, that is the
L^{1}-
norm of the solution to (
1
) in the case
f\equiv 1
, is maximum on balls among sets of given measure. This conjecture can be solved by integrating (
3
).

Over the years, the topic of comparison results has gained more and more interest. A version of the aforementioned result of Talenti for nonlinear operators in divergence form is contained in
[
43
]
, which includes the case of the
p-
Laplace operator as a special instance; see also
[
12
]
. Further extensions can be found, for instance, in
[
3
]
for anisotropic elliptic operators, in
[
5
]
for the parabolic case, in
[
11
,
44
]
for higher-order operators, and in
[
7
]
, where the Steiner symmetrization is used in place of the Schwarz one.
In the recent paper
[
6
]
, the
authors consider problem (
1
) replacing the Dirichlet boundary conditions with the Robin one
and they prove a comparison result involving Lorentz norms of
u
and
v
whenever
f
is a non-negative function in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. In particular, in the case
f\equiv 1
and
n=2
, they recover the point-wise inequality as in
[
42
]
.
Generalizations of the results contained in
[
6
]
can be found for the anisotropic case in
[
41
,
8
]
, for mixed boundary conditions in
[
1
]
, in the case of the Hermite operator in
[
15
]
and in the nonlinear case
[
9
]
.
For further generalizations and general overviews of comparison results we refer, for instance, to
[
2
,
16
,
19
,
38
,
30
]

and to the references therein, not claiming to give here a comprehensive list of all the results developed in this context.

Another interesting question about the topic is trying to understand if comparison inequalities are rigid, as it has been done in
[
4
]
.
Indeed, the authors characterize the equality in (
3
) in the case
f\geq 0
, proving that if equality occurs, then
\Omega
is a ball,
u
is radially symmetric and decreasing, and
f=f^{\sharp}
.
Rigidity results for a generic linear, elliptic second-order operator can be found in
[
21
]
and
[
31
]
and for Robin boundary conditions in
[
36
,
37
]
.

Once a comparison result and a rigidity result hold, it is natural to ask whether the comparison result can be improved in a quantitative version. More precisely,
considering the rigidity result in
[
4
]
,
if equality
almost
holds in (
3
), is it true that the set
\Omega
is
almost
a ball, and the function
u
and
f
are
almost
radially symmetric and decreasing?
The main objective of this paper is to study the stability of (
3
) and to answer the following question: if
u^{\sharp}
is
close
to
v
in some sense, can we say that the set
\Omega
and the functions
u
and
f
are
almost
symmetric? One of the difficulties we have to deal with is to give the most suitable notions of "closeness" for this type of problem.

More precisely, we state our main result in the following way.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set of
\R^{n}
and let
f
be a non-negative function in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Suppose that

and

are the solutions to (

) and (

), respectively. Then, there exist some positive constants
\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(n),\theta_{2}=\theta_{2}(n)
and
C_{1}:=C_{1}(n,{\left|\Omega\right|},f^{\sharp}),\,C_{2}:=C_{2}(n,{\left|\Omega\right|},f^{\sharp}),\,C_{3}:=C_{3}(n,{\left|\Omega\right|},f^{\sharp}),
such that

C_{1}\alpha^{3}(\Omega)+C_{2}\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|u-u^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}^{\theta_{1}}+C_{3}\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|f-f^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}^{\theta_{2}}\leq||v-u^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(4)

Moreover, the dependence of
C_{1},\,C_{2}
and

from
{\left|\Omega\right|}
and

is explicit.

In order to prove Theorem

bounds from above each term on the left-hand side: this is the aim of Theorems

1.3

The first step is contained in Theorem

and

has a small Fraenkel asymmetry index, defined as

\alpha(\Omega):=\min_{x\in\R^{n}}\bigg\{\dfrac{|\Omega\Delta B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\;,\;|B_{r}(x)|=|\Omega|\bigg\},

(5)

where the symbol

stands for the symmetric difference and

is the measure of the set.
This is a standard way to measure how much a set
\Omega
differs from a ball

with the same measure in the

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

,

(6)

where

\widetilde{C}_{1}=\max\left\{\left(8n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{3},(16n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}}\gamma_{n})\right\},

(7)

\omega_{n}
is the measure of the unit ball of
\R^{n}
and

is the constant appearing in the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (see Theorem

).

To prove this result we make use of a clever idea introduced in
[
28
]
, used for obtaining non-sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for the principal frequency and the capacity.
This idea is also explained and exploited in the survey paper
[
13
]
to prove a quantitative version of the Pólya-Szegő principle (see Theorem

1.2
implies the quantitative result contained in
[

in the case

In Theorem

of the function

solution to (
1

The second step for proving Theorem

is contained in Theorem

distance between

and
u^{\sharp}
is small, assuming that
u^{\sharp}
is close to
v
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-distance.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
if
n>2
,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p>1
if
n=2
be a non-negative function and let
u
and
v
be the solution to (
1
) and (

) respectively. Then, there exist two constants
\tilde{\theta}_{1}=\tilde{\theta}_{1}(n)>0
and
\widetilde{C}_{2}=\widetilde{C}_{2}(n)>0
such that

\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}||u-u^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})||_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}\dfrac{{\left\|f\right\|}_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}^{1-2\tilde{\theta}_{1}}}{{\left\|f\right\|}_{1}^{1-3\tilde{\theta}_{1}}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1+\left(\frac{2}{n}-1\right)\tilde{\theta}_{1}}||v-u^{\sharp}||_{{}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}^{\tilde{\theta}_{1}}.

(8)

In order to prove this result, we observe in Lemma

that a small

and

\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}|\nabla u^{\sharp}|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}\,dx,

So, at this stage, it is natural to consider the Pólya-Szegő quantitative result proved in

\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}{\left|u(x)-u^{\sharp}(x+x_{0})\right|}\,dx\leq C\left[M_{u^{\sharp}}(E(u)^{r})+E(u)\right]^{s}

Hence, to prove the closeness of

.

Eventually, to conclude the proof of Theorem

and

Let

Throughout this article, we will denote by

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with

, and by

For convenience, we will denote by

If

Theorem 2.1

(Coarea formula)
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\R
be a Lipschitz function and let

be a measurable function. Then,

{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla f(x)|dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\int_{(\Omega\cap f^{-1}(t))}u(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)}.

(9)

The rest of this Section is dedicated to stating some useful results on the rearrangement of functions, on quantitative inequalities and rescaling properties.

2.1
Rearrangement of functions

For a general overview of this topic, we refer, for instance, to

Definition 2.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
distribution function
of
u
is the function
\mu:[0,+\infty[\,\to[0,+\infty[
defined as the measure of the superlevel sets of

\mu(t)={\left|\Set{x\in\Omega\,:\,{\left|u(x)\right|}>t}\right|}.

In particular, if
u
is the solution to (
1

v
is the solution to (
2
), we fix the following notations, for

U_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\},\quad\mu(t)={\left|U_{t}\right|}

and

V_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega^{\sharp}:v(x)>t\right\},\quad\nu(t)={\left|V_{t}\right|}.

By using the Coarea formula (
9
), one can deduce the following expression for
\mu

\mu(t)={\left|\{u>t\}\cap\{|\nabla u|=0\}\right|}+\int_{t}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{u=s}\frac{1}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\,ds,

as a consequence, for almost all

\infty>-\mu^{\prime}(t)\geq\displaystyle\int_{u=t}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(10)

and if

Definition 2.2
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
decreasing rearrangement
of
u
, denoted by

,is defined as

u^{*}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu(t)<s\}.

From Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, one can prove that

u^{\ast}(\mu(t))\leq t,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

\mu(u^{\ast}(s))\leq s\quad\forall s\geq 0.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us notice that the function
\mu(\cdot)
is decreasing and right continuous and the function
u^{\ast}(\cdot)
is its generalized inverse.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Schwartz rearrangement
of

is the function
u^{\sharp}
whose superlevel sets are balls with the same measure as the superlevel sets of

We have the following relation between
u^{\sharp}
and

u^{\sharp}(x)=u^{*}(\omega_{n}{\left|x\right|}^{n}),

where

. One can easily check that the functions

\displaystyle{{\left\|u\right\|}_{p}={\left\|u^{*}\right\|}_{p}=\lVert{u^{\sharp}}\rVert_{p}},\quad\text{for all }p\geq 1.

The following Lemma characterizes the absolute continuity of
\mu
(we refer, for instance, to

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\R^{n})

{\left|\{0<u<||u^{\sharp}||_{\infty}\}\cap\{|\nabla u^{\sharp}|=0\}\right|}=0.

An important property of the decreasing rearrangement is the Hardy-Littlewood inequality, see

Theorem 2.3

(Hardy-Littlewood inequaliy)
.

Let us consider

\int_{\Omega}{\left|h(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}h^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds.

\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}{\left|h(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}h^{*}(s)\,ds.

If we require that the function

Theorem 2.4

Let

\lVert\nabla u^{\sharp}\rVert_{p}\leq{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}.

Remark 2.2
.

We explicitly observe that the function

\varphi:s\rightarrow\dfrac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}f^{*}

Indeed, let

, and let us show that

This is true if and only if

Theorem 2.5

(Talenti,
[
42
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
if

,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,

if

and let
u
and

be the solutions to problems (

) and (

u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x).

Proof.

Let us observe that
u
is a weak solution to (
1
) if and only if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx,\qquad\forall\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

(17)

Let us fix
t\in[0,{\left\|u\right\|}_{\infty}[
and
h>0
, and let us define the function
\varphi
:

\left.\varphi(x)=\right.\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }u<t\\
u-t&\text{ if }t<u<t+h\\
h&\text{ if }u>t+h.\end{cases}

(18)

Using (
18
) as a test in the weak formulation (
17
), we have

\int_{U_{t}\setminus U_{t+h}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}\,dx=\int_{U_{t}\setminus U_{t+h}}f(x)(u-t)\,dx+\int_{U_{t+h}}hf(x)\,dx.

If we divide now by
h
, and we send
h
to 0, we get

\int_{\partial U_{t}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{U_{t}}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds.

Applying the isoperimetric inequality to the superlevel set
U_{t}
, the Hölder inequality and the Hardy-Littlewood inequality, we get, for almost every

,

\displaystyle n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}}\mu(t)^{2-\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq P^{2}(U_{t})\leq\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right)\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}\frac{1}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds\right)\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)\right).

Equivalently, we can write

1\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\mu(t)^{-2+\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds\right)\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)\right),

and, integrating from
0
to

, we obtain

t\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}}}\int_{\mu(t)}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}r^{-2+\frac{2}{n}}\int_{0}^{r}f^{\ast}(s)\,ds\,dr=v^{\ast}(\mu(t)).

(19)

Since
u^{\ast}(r)=\inf\{\tau\,|\,\mu(\tau)<r\}
, (
19
) implies

\displaystyle u^{\ast}(r)=\inf\{\tau\,|\,\mu(\tau)<r\}\leq\inf\left\{v^{\ast}(\mu(\tau))\,|\,\mu(\tau)<r\right\}\leq v^{\ast}(r),

that gives (
3
).

\displaystyle\leq K_{7}(p,n)\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}(f^{*})^{q-\frac{1}{p}}s^{-\frac{2}{np}}\;ds=K_{7}(p,n)\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}\left(f^{*}s^{\frac{p-\frac{2}{n}}{qp-1}}\right)^{q-\frac{1}{p}}\;\dfrac{ds}{s},

where

K_{7}(p,n)=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\left[\left(2-\frac{2}{n}\right)\frac{1}{p-1}+1\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}(n^{2}\omega_{n}^{\frac{2}{n}})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

So, we have that

{\left\|g\right\|}^{q}_{{\Lambda}_{p}^{q}}\leq K_{7}(p,n){\left\|f\right\|}^{l}_{L^{k,l}(\R^{n})},

where

k=\dfrac{qp-1}{p-\frac{2}{n}},\qquad l=\dfrac{qp-1}{p}.

By imposing
l>0
and
0<k<2
, we obtain the following constraints on
p
and
q
:

p>1,\qquad\dfrac{1}{p}\leq q<2+\left(1-\frac{4}{n}\right)\dfrac{1}{p}.

(86)

So, by the classical embedding Theorem for Lorentz space (see
[
34
]
), we have

L^{2,2}\hookrightarrow L^{k,l}.

Now, we are in position to use Theorem
2.9
and we obtain

\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|g-g^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{m}(\R^{n})}\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{2^{p+1}\,e\,q}{\left\|g\right\|}_{\Lambda_{p}^{q}(\Omega)}^{pq}\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}(g^{\sharp}-g)v\right)^{\frac{1}{1+pq}},

where

m=\dfrac{qp+1}{p+1}=\dfrac{2n(k-l)+2kl}{2k+n(k-l)}.

Moreover, we observe that, from (
86
), it follows that
m<2
.

∎

Let us prove now Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Firstly, let us observe that

\displaystyle\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}||f-f^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq||f-f^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|f-g\right\|}_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+||g-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+||f^{\sharp}-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Let us estimate from above the quantities
||f^{\sharp}-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
{\left\|f-g\right\|}_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
:

\displaystyle||f^{\sharp}-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq{\left\|f-g\right\|}_{L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle={\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{m}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\sharp})}

(87)

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\sharp}\right|}^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{4}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2-m}{8m}}

where in the last inequality we have used Holder’s inequality and (
82
).

It only remains to estimate
||g-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\R^{n})}
, and thanks to Lemma
5.1
and (
80
), it is enough to estimate the following quantity

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}(g^{\sharp}-g)v

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}v-\int_{\Omega}fu+\int_{\R^{n}}f(u-u^{\sharp})+\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f(u^{\sharp}-v)

(88)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}+\int_{\R^{n}}f(u-u^{\sharp})

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}||u-u^{\sharp}||_{L^{2}(\R^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+K_{6}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}\left({{\left\|f\right\|}_{{\frac{2n}{n+2}}}^{2+n-4\tilde{\theta}_{1}}}\varepsilon^{\tilde{\theta}_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+K_{6}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}^{3-\frac{8}{n+2}\tilde{\theta}_{1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{n+2}\tilde{\theta}_{1}}.

Hence,

||g-g^{\sharp}||_{L^{m}(\R^{n})}\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{2^{p+1}\,e\,q}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+pq}}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}^{\frac{pq-1}{1+pq}}\left(2\varepsilon+K_{6}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}^{3-\frac{8}{n+2}\tilde{\theta}_{1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{n+2}\tilde{\theta}_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+pq}}.

(89)

Combining (
87
) and (
89
), there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{3}
depending only on
m
and the dimension
n
, such that

\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|f-f^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{m}(\R^{n})}\leq\widetilde{C}_{3}{\left\|f\right\|}_{2}^{\tilde{\theta}_{2}}\varepsilon^{\tilde{\theta}_{3}},

where

\tilde{\theta}_{2}=\max\left\{1,\left(2+pq-\frac{8\tilde{\theta}_{1}}{n+2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1+pq}\right)\right\},\qquad\tilde{\theta}_{3}=\min\left\{\frac{2-m}{8m},\frac{1}{1+pq},\left(\frac{2\tilde{\theta}_{2}}{n+2}\right)\frac{1}{1+pq}\right\}.

∎

Remark 5.1
.

We explicitly observe that in Theorem
1.4
we cannot obtain a comparison result for the quantity
||f-f^{\sharp}||_{L^{2}(\R^{n})}
. Indeed, let
B
be the unitary ball, centered at the origin, in the plane, and let us consider the following problem and its symmetrized:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\sigma}=f_{\sigma}&\text{in}\;B\\
u_{\sigma}=0&\text{on}\;\partial B.\end{cases};\qquad\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{\sigma}=f^{\sharp}_{\sigma}&\text{in}\,B\\
v_{\sigma}=0&\text{on}\,\partial B,\end{cases}

where

f_{\sigma}(x)=1+\sigma^{-1}\chi_{B_{\sigma}(1/2,0)}(x).

and, consequently,

f^{\sharp}_{\sigma}(x)=1+\sigma^{-1}\chi_{B_{\sigma}(0,0)}(x).

With these choices, we obtain

{\left\|f_{\sigma}-f^{\sharp}_{\sigma}\right\|}_{L^{2}(B)}=\sqrt{2\pi}

and

{\left\|f_{\sigma}-f^{\sharp}_{\sigma}\right\|}_{L^{r}(B)}=\left(2\pi\right)^{1/r}\sigma^{2/r-1}\to 0

as
\sigma\to 0
and
r<2
.
In order to conclude, we refer now to
[
25
, Theorem 8.16]
,
that states that the following estimate holds

{\left\|w\right\|}_{\infty}\leq C\left(\sup_{\partial\Omega}w+{\left\|g\right\|}_{\frac{q}{2}}\right)

where
w
solves in
\Omega

-\Delta w=g

with
g\in L^{\frac{q}{2}}
,
q>2
. So, in our case, we can choose
w=v_{\sigma}-u_{\sigma}
and any
r>1
, obtaining

{\left\|v_{\sigma}-u_{\sigma}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C{\left\|f^{\sharp}_{\sigma}-f_{\sigma}\right\|}_{L^{r}(B)},

so it is clear that, if
1\leq r<2
,
{\left\|v_{\sigma}-u_{\sigma}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\to 0
.

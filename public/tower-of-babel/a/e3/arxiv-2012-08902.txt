On quantum 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ ℓ ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell) -Toda chain Talk given by the second author at the ”Polivanov-90” conference, 16-17 December 2020, Steklov Mathematical Institute of Russian Academy of Science.
A.A. Gerasimov, D.R. Lebedev, S.V. Oblezin

Abstract
. The orthosymplectic superalgebra 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ ℓ ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell) is the closest analog of standard Lie algebras in the world of super Lie algebras. We demonstrate that the corresponding 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ ℓ ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell) -Toda chain turns out to be an instance of a B ​ C ℓ BC_{\ell} -Toda chain. The underlying reason for this relation is discussed.



1
Introduction

Representation theory is an essential tool in finding explicit
solutions of known quantum integrable systems as well as
construction of new ones. An important class of finite-dimensional
quantum integrable systems allowing representation theory
interpretation is provided by Toda chains. It is known that
integrable Toda chains are classified by a class of root systems
that include root systems of finite dimensional Lie algebras as well
as their affine counterparts. For the Toda chains associated with
the root systems of finite dimensional Lie algebras the
corresponding integrable systems can be solved explicitly by
representation theory methods
[
K1
]
,
[
GW
]
(see
[
STS
]

for a review). Precisely, the eigenfunctions of the quantum
Hamiltonians are given by special matrix elements of principal
series representations of the totally split real form of the
corresponding Lie group. The resulting functions should be
considered as generalized Whittaker functions associated with the
corresponding finite-dimensional Lie algebras
[
K1
]
,
[
Ha
]
.
These functions allow quite explicit integral representations (see
e.g.
[
GLO1
]
).

The class of integrable Toda chains is however a bit larger than the
class of finite/affine Lie algebras and includes in particular
non-reduce root systems
BC_{\ell}
combining
B_{\ell}
and
C_{\ell}

root systems. The corresponding
BC_{\ell}
-Toda system is an
important element of the web of Toda type theories connected by
various intertwining relations
[
GLO2
]
. Although
BC_{\ell}
root
system fits naturally in the classification of finite Lie algebra
root systems the problem of construction of the Lie algebra type
object corresponding to the non-reduced root
BC_{\ell}
systems seems
not yet obtained a satisfactory resolution. However, one should
recall that
BC_{\ell}
root systems appear in the Cartan
classification of symmetric spaces
[
H
]
,
[
L
]
. Still

BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain can be solved via representation theory methods
using a generalization of
C_{\ell}
-Whittaker functions (see e.g.

[
J
]
for
\ell=1
and a remark in
[
RS
]
, relevant to

BC_{\ell}
classical Toda system). This unfortunately does not
elucidate the question of the
interpretation of
BC_{\ell}
-Toda eigenfunctions as standard Whittaker
functions for some group-like object. One should add that the
integrability of the quantum
BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain for generic coupling
constants was proven independently in
[
S
]
using Yangian
representation theory (aka quantum inverse scattering methods).
This however also does not clarify the question of existence of
a group-like structure behind
BC_{\ell}
root systems.

In this note we consider quantum Toda chains associated with the
super Lie algebras
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. This series of super Lie
algebras occupy a special place in the world of super
Lie algebras. In particular, it is the only instance of simple
super Lie algebras for which the corresponding category of
finite-dimensional representations is semi-simple
and thus allows direct analogs of the standard constructions of
representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras
[
Kac1
]
.
In connection with this fact one should mention that

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
is the unique super Lie algebra
with finitely-generated center of its universal enveloping algebra.
The special properties of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
makes it natural to consider
the associated quantum integrable systems.

In this note we demonstrate that
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain
may be also considered as a Toda chain associated with the
BC_{\ell}

root system. This allows us to solve
BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain
by standard representation theory methods i.e. by identifying
the corresponding eigenfunctions with
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker functions.

The underlying reason for the appearance of
BC_{\ell}

root structure in
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain becomes clear by
comparing
BC_{\ell}
root data with that of the super Lie algebra

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. Actually the only difference is the opposite parity
of the maximal commutative subalgebra eigenspaces in the Cartan
decomposition corresponding to short roots
of non-reduced
BC_{\ell}
root system. This difference however does not
affect the expressions for quantum Hamiltonians of the
corresponding Toda chain.

Exposition of the paper goes as follows. In Section 2 we provide
basic facts on the structure of the orthosymplect super Lie algebra

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. In Section 3 we construct
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker
functions associated with representations of the
super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
and demonstrate that these functions are
eigenfunctions of the quadratic quantum Hamiltonian of
BC_{\ell}
-Toda
chain for special values of the coupling constants. Finally,
in Section 4 we discuss the structure of root system of

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
versus
BC_{\ell}
root system and provide an
explanation of the apparent identification of
quadratic Hamiltonian of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain with
that of
BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain.

2
Basic facts on the super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)

We start with the basic definition of a Lie superalgebra structure
and then we describe explicitly the algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
in
detail. This is a standard material that can be found in standard
sources on super algebras e.g.
[
Kac1
]
,
[
Kac2
]
.

The notion of Lie superalgebra is a direct generalization of the
notion of Lie algebra to the category of vector superspaces. Vector
superspace
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
with the parity
p
taking values
0
and
1
on
V_{\bar{0}}
and

V_{\bar{1}}
respectively. The tensor product structure is given by
twisting of the standard tensor product structure in the category of
vector spaces

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}v\otimes w=(-1)^{p(v)\cdot p(w)}\,w\otimes v,\qquad v\in V,\quad w\in W,\end{array}

for
v
and
w
are homogeneous
elements with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading.

Definition 2.1

The structure of super Lie algebra on super vector space

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
is given by
a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\,\cdot]
, called the bracket, so
that for any homogeneous elements
X,\,Y,\,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
the following hold:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}p\bigl([X,\,Y]\bigr)\,=\,p(X)\,+\,p(Y)\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\,[X,\,Y]\,=\,-(-1)^{p(X)\cdot p(Y)}[Y,\,X]\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\,[X,\,[Y,\,Z]](-1)^{p(X)\cdot p(Z)}\,+\,[Z,\,[X,\,Y]](-1)^{p(Y)\cdot p(Z)}\\
+\,[Y,\,[Z,\,X]](-1)^{p(X)\cdot p(Y)}\,=\,0\,.\end{array}

We will be interested in a special instance of super Lie algebras,
the ortho-symplectic super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. To define
this algebra let us first introduce the super Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)
.

Definition 2.2

The super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(1|\,2\ell)
is generated by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{0,\,i}\,,\quad E_{i,\,0}\,,\quad p(E_{0,\,i})\,=\,p(E_{i,\,0})\,=\,1\,,\qquad 0\leq i\leq 2\ell\,,\\
\text{and}\qquad E_{kl}\,,\qquad p(E_{kl})\,=\,0\,,\qquad 1\leq k,\,l\leq 2\ell\,,\end{array}

subjected to the following relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl[E_{ij},\,E_{kl}\bigr]\,=\,\delta_{jk}E_{il}\,-(-1)^{p(i)p(l)}\,\delta_{il}E_{kj}\,,\qquad 0\leq i,\,j\leq 2\ell\,,\quad 0\leq k,\,l\leq 2\ell\,.\end{array}

The super Lie agebra
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)
may be identified with the super
Lie algebra structure
\bigl({\rm End}(V),\,[\cdot,\,\cdot]\bigr)

on the space
{\rm End}(V)
of endomorphisms of the superspace

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}V\,=\,\mathbb{R}^{1|2\ell}\,=\,V_{\bar{0}}\,\oplus\,V_{\bar{1}}\,,\qquad V_{\bar{0}}\,=\,\mathbb{R}^{0|2\ell}\,,\qquad V_{\bar{1}}\,=\,\mathbb{R}^{1|0}\,,\end{array}

in the following way.
Any zero parity linear endomorphism
A\in{\rm End}(V)
is given by the matrix of the
following shape:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\,,\quad\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}:\,V_{\bar{1}}\,\longrightarrow\,V_{\bar{1}}\,,&A_{12}:\,V_{\bar{0}}\,\longrightarrow\,V_{\bar{1}}\,,\\
A_{21}:\,V_{\bar{1}}\,\longrightarrow\,V_{\bar{0}}\,,&A_{22}:\,V_{\bar{0}}\,\longrightarrow\,V_{\bar{0}}\,,\end{array}\end{array}

where entries of blocks
A_{11},\,A_{22}
are even while the
entries of
A_{12}\,,A_{21}
are odd so that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm End}(V)_{\bar{0}}\,=\,\Big\{\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}A_{11}&&0\\
&&\\
0&&A_{22}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\Big\}\,,\qquad{\rm End}(V)_{\bar{1}}\,=\,\Big\{\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&&A_{12}\\
&&\\
A_{21}&&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\Big\}\,.\end{array}

The super brackets on
{\rm End}(V)
are defined on homogeneous elements
X,\,Y\in{\rm End}(V)

as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X,\,Y]\,=\,X\circ Y\,-\,(-1)^{p(X)\cdot p(Y)}Y\circ X\,.\end{array}

The description of
\mathfrak{g}(1|2\ell)
given in Definition

2.2
is then obtained via fixing a bases in
V

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\{\varepsilon_{0},\,\varepsilon_{1},\,\ldots,\,\varepsilon_{2\ell}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1|2\ell}\,,\qquad p(\varepsilon_{0})=1\,,\quad p(\varepsilon_{k})=0,\quad 1\leq k\leq 2\ell\,.\end{array}

The generators
E_{ij}
are identified with the elementary matrices
in
{\rm End}(V)
with the only non-zero elements in the
i
-th row
and the
j
-th column.

Definition 2.3

The super transposition of a matrix
A\in{\rm End}(V)
is
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{\top}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)^{\top}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}^{t}&-A_{21}^{t}\\
A_{12}^{t}&A_{22}^{t}\end{array}\right)\,,\end{array}

where
X^{t}
if the standard transposition of a matrix
X
.

Lemma 2.1

Super transposition (
2.3
) possesses the
following properties:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(A\,v)^{t}\,=\,v^{t}A^{\top}\,,\qquad A\in{\rm End}(V)\,,\quad v\in V\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(A\cdot B)^{\top}\,=\,B^{\top}\cdot A^{\top}\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(A^{\top})^{\top}\,=\Pi\,A\,\Pi^{-1}\,,\end{array}

where
\Pi
is the parity operator with the matrix

\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&&0\\
&&\\
0&&{\rm Id}_{2\ell}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)
.

Proof
: Given
v\in V
let us write it down in the basis

\{\varepsilon_{0},\,\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{2\ell}\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}v\,=\,\xi\varepsilon_{0}\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{2\ell}v_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\,,\end{array}

with odd Grassmann coordinate
\xi
, and even coordinates
v_{i}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(A\,v)^{t}_{i}\,=\,a_{i0}\xi\,+\,a_{i1}v_{1}\,+\ldots+\,a_{i,\,2\ell}v_{2\ell}\,,\end{array}

and on the other hand,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(v^{t}A^{\top})_{0}\,=\,\xi a_{00}\,+\,v_{1}a_{0,1}\,+\ldots+\,v_{n}a_{0,n}\,,\\
(v^{t}A^{\top})_{k}\,=\,-\xi a_{k0}\,+\,v_{1}a_{k,1}\,+\ldots+\,v_{n}a_{k,n}\,,\quad 1\leq k\leq 2\ell\,.\end{array}

Taking into account that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi a_{00}\,=\,a_{00}\xi\,,\qquad-\xi a_{k,0}\,=\,a_{k,0}\xi\,,\quad 1\leq k\leq 2\ell\,,\end{array}

we deduce the first assertion. The second assertion can be verified
by straightforward computation. The third assertion follows from the
definition: on the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(A^{\top})^{\top}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}^{t}&-A_{21}^{t}\\
A_{12}^{t}&A_{22}^{t}\end{array}\right)^{\top}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&-A_{12}\\
-A_{12}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\,;\end{array}

on the other hand, in the standard basis (
2
) the matrix of
parity operator reads

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Pi\,=\,\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&&0\\
&&\\
0&&{\rm Id}_{2\ell}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,,\end{array}

so the assertion easily follows.
\Box

Now
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
may be defined as a subalgebra of the general linear
superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)
. Introduce the following involution:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\theta\,:\quad\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)\,,\qquad X\,\longmapsto\,X^{\theta}\,:=\,-JX^{\top}J^{-1}\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}J\,=\,\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&-{\rm Id}_{\ell}\\
0&{\rm Id}_{\ell}&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,\in\,{\rm End}(V)_{\bar{0}}\,.\end{array}

Definition 2.4

The orthosymplectic super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
is defined as
the
\theta
-invariant subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)\,=\Big\{X\,\in\,\mathfrak{gl}(1|2\ell)\,:\quad X^{\theta}\,=\,X\Big\}\\
=\,\Big\{X\,=\,\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x&y\\
y^{t}&A&B\\
-x^{t}&C&-A^{t}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,:\quad B^{t}\,=\,B\,,\quad C^{t}\,=\,C\Big\}\,\subset\,\mathfrak{gl}(1|\,2\ell)\,.\end{array}

According to the classification of simple super Lie algebras

[
Kac1
]
one associates the root system
B_{0,\ell}
to the super
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. Let

\{\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\,\epsilon_{\ell}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be an orthogonal
basis in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with respect to the scalar product
(\,,\,)
.
Then simple root system
{}^{s}\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})
of type

B_{0,\,\ell}
consists of even simple positive roots

{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}
and odd simple positive roots

{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}(B_{0,\,\ell})\,=\,\bigl\{{\alpha}_{k}\,=\,\epsilon_{\ell+1-k}-\epsilon_{\ell+2-k}\,,\quad 1<k\leq\ell\bigr\}\,\,,\\
{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}(B_{0,\,\ell})\,=\,\bigl\{{\alpha}_{1}=\epsilon_{\ell}\bigr\}\,,\end{array}

indexed by
I=\{1,\,\ldots,\,\ell\}
. The simple co-roots

{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee},\,i\in I
are defined in a standard way:

{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}\,:=\,\frac{2{\alpha}_{i}}{({\alpha}_{i},{\alpha}_{i})}\,,\quad i\in I\,.

Note that the set
\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})
of positive roots contains the
sub-system of even positive roots of
C_{\ell}
root system with the
corresponding set of simple roots:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{}^{s}\Delta^{+}(C_{\ell})\,=\,\bigl\{2{\alpha}_{1}=2\epsilon_{\ell}\,,\quad{\alpha}_{k}\,=\,\epsilon_{\ell+1-k}-\epsilon_{\ell+2-k}\,,\,\,1<k\leq\ell\bigr\}\,\subset\,\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})\,.\end{array}

The Cartan matrix
A=\|A_{ij}\|
associated with the simple
root system (
2
) is defined by the standard
formula

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{ij}\,=\,\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\,,\qquad i,j\in I\,.\end{array}

Thus the Cartan matrix of
{}^{s}\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})
coincides with
the standard
B_{\ell}
-type Cartan matrix

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}2&-2&0&\ldots&0\\
\hline\cr-1&2&-1&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&-1&2&-1\\
0&\ldots&0&-1&2\end{array}\right).\end{array}

The Cartan decomposition for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
reads

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)(\mathbb{C})\,=\,\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{C}h_{i}\,\oplus\,\bigoplus_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\bigl(\mathbb{C}X_{{\alpha}}\,\oplus\,\mathbb{C}X_{-{\alpha}}\bigr)\,\oplus\,\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}}\bigl(\mathbb{C}X_{\beta}\,\oplus\,\mathbb{C}X_{-\beta}\bigr)\,,\\
\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\,=\,\bigl\{2\epsilon_{i}\,;\qquad\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\,,\quad i<j\,,\quad i,\,j\in I\bigr\}\,,\qquad\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}\,=\,\bigl\{\epsilon_{i}\,,\quad i\in I\bigr\}\,.\end{array}

and the Cartan-Weyl relations are the following:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,(1+\delta_{ij})X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad\bigl[X_{-\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{-\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,-(1+\delta_{ij})X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\,,\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{-\epsilon_{i}}\bigr]\,=\,a_{ii}\,,\qquad i\in I\,;\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}}\,,\qquad\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{-\epsilon_{i}}\bigr]\,=\,-X_{-\epsilon_{j}}\,,\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{-\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad\bigl[X_{-\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad i<j\,;\\
\bigl[X_{{\alpha}},\,X_{-{\alpha}}\bigr]\,=\,h_{{\alpha}^{\vee}}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}\langle{\alpha}^{\vee},\,\epsilon_{i}\rangle a_{ii}\,,\\
\bigl[h_{{\alpha}^{\vee}},\,X_{{\gamma}}\bigr]\,=\,{\alpha}^{\vee}({\gamma})X_{{\gamma}}\,,\qquad{\alpha},\,{\gamma}\in\Delta^{+}\,.\end{array}

The Serre relations on
X_{{\alpha}_{i}},\,{\alpha}_{i}\in{}^{s}\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})

have the following form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm ad}_{X_{{\alpha}_{1}}}^{2}(X_{{\alpha}_{1}})\,=\,0\,,\qquad{\rm ad}_{X_{-{\alpha}_{1}}}^{2}(X_{-{\alpha}_{1}})\,=\,0\,,\\
{\rm ad}_{X_{{\alpha}_{i}}}^{1-a_{ij}}(X_{{\alpha}_{j}})\,=\,0\,,\quad{\rm ad}_{X_{-{\alpha}_{i}}}^{1-a_{ij}}(X_{-{\alpha}_{j}})\,=\,0\,,\qquad i,j\in I\,\,.\end{array}

The Cartan-Weyl generators
X_{\alpha}
may be represented via matrix
embedding (
2.4
) of

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\,=\,E_{i,\,0}\,+\,E_{0,\,\ell+i}\,,\qquad X_{-\epsilon_{i}}\,=\,E_{0,\,i}\,-\,E_{\ell+i,\,0}\,;\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\,=\,E_{ij}-E_{2\ell+1-i,\,2\ell+1-j}\,,\\
X_{-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,=\,E_{ji}-E_{2\ell+1-j,\,2\ell+1-i}\,,\\
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,=\,E_{i,\,\ell+j}+E_{j,\,\ell+i}\,,\quad X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\,=\,E_{\ell+j,\,i}+E_{\ell+i,\,j}\,,\quad i<j\,,\\
X_{2\epsilon_{i}}\,=\,E_{i,\,\ell+i}\,,\qquad X_{-2\epsilon_{i}}\,=\,E_{\ell+i,\,i}\,,\qquad i\in I\,.\end{array}

The Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)
is spanned by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{i}\,=\,E_{ii}\,-\,E_{\ell+i,\,\ell+i}\,,\qquad i\in I\,.\end{array}

For a class of super Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
allowing
non-degenerate invariant pairing
(\,|\,)
there is a canonical
construction of the quadratic Casimir element

C_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))
of the center of the universal enveloping
algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
(see e.g.

[
Kac1
]
). Let us chose a pair
\{u_{i},\,i\in I\}
,

\{u^{i},\,i\in I\}
of dual bases
in the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, and let

\{X_{{\alpha}},\,X^{{\alpha}},\,{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}\}
be the Cartan-Weyl generators
normalized by
(X^{{\alpha}}|\,X_{{\alpha}})=1
. Then the quadratic Casimir element

C_{2}
allows for the following presentation:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}u^{i}u_{i}\,+\,\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}}\bigl((-1)^{p({\alpha})}X_{{\alpha}}X^{{\alpha}}\,+\,X^{{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\bigr)\,.\end{array}

To define the quadratic Casimir element for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
we shall
first introduce an invariant non-degenerate invariant pairing.
The super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
allows the invariant scalar
product defined as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(X|Y)\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}{\rm str}\,\bigl(\rho_{t}(X)\circ\rho_{t}(Y)\bigr)\,,\qquad X,\,Y\in\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)\,,\end{array}

where
\rho_{t}:\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)\to{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2\ell})
is the
tautological representation of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
in
\mathbb{C}^{1|2\ell}
.
The supertrace of
A\in{\rm End}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2\ell})
of the shape
(
2
) is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm str}\,(A)\,=\,{\rm str}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{array}\right)\,=\,-{\rm tr}\,(A_{11})\,+\,{\rm tr}\,(A_{22})\,.\end{array}

The explicit form of the invariant scalar product (
2
)
may be directly derived using the matrix representation
(
2.4
).

Lemma 2.2

The invariant scalar product (
2
) on super Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
is as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(h_{i}|h_{i})=1\,,\quad i\in I\,;\\
(X_{\alpha}|X_{-\alpha})=\frac{2}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\,,\quad\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\,;\qquad(X_{\beta}|X_{-\beta})=1\,,\quad\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}\,,\end{array}

with the rest of the products being zero.

Proof
: Validity of (
2.2
) may be checked directly. Thus for
example we have using (
2
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(h_{i}|h_{j})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm str}\,\,\Big(\rho_{t}(E_{ii}-E_{\ell+i,\ell+i})\rho_{t}(E_{jj}-E_{\ell+j,\ell+j})\Big)\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,.\end{array}

Similarly, using (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(X_{\epsilon_{i}}|X_{-\epsilon_{j}})\,=\,\frac{1}{2}{\rm str}\,\Big(\rho_{t}(E_{i,0}+E_{0,\ell+i})\rho_{t}(E_{0,j}-E_{\ell+j,0})\Big)\,=\,\delta_{ij}\,.\end{array}

Similarly one might check the expressions for remaining products.
\Box

Proposition 2.1

The following expression provides the quadratic
Casimir element (
2
) for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}\Big(h_{i}^{2}\,-\,X_{\epsilon_{i}}X_{-\epsilon_{i}}\,+\,X_{-\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\Big)\\
+\,\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\frac{({\alpha},\,{\alpha})}{2}\bigl(X_{{\alpha}}X_{-{\alpha}}\,+\,X_{-{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\bigr)\,.\end{array}

Proof
: Using the expressions (
2.2
) for the invariant pairing
we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}h^{i}\,=\,h_{i}\,,\quad i\in I\,;\\
X^{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{(\alpha,\alpha)}{2}\,X_{-\alpha}\,,\quad\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\,;\qquad X^{\beta}\,=\,X_{-\beta}\,,\quad\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}\,.\end{array}

Substituting (
2
) into (
2
) we arrive at
(
2.1
), and complete the proof.
\Box

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[b_{ij},b_{kl}]=0,\qquad[c_{ij},c_{kl}]=0,\qquad[b_{ij},c_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}a_{il},\\
\,[a_{ii},b_{kl}]=(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{il})b_{kl},\qquad[a_{ii},c_{kl}]=-(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{il})b_{kl}\,.\end{array}

Using these notations the quadratic Casimir element (

may be written as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(a_{ii}^{2}+x_{i}y_{i}-y_{i}x_{i})\,+\,2(c_{ii}b_{ii}+b_{ii}c_{ii})\\
+\,\sum_{i<j}(a_{ij}a_{ji}+a_{ji}a_{ij})\,+\,(b_{ij}c_{ij}+c_{ij}b_{ij})\,.\end{array}

From now on we will consider the real form
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)(\mathbb{R})
of
the orthosymplectic super Lie algebra, such that the generators

,

as well as

and

3
The
\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)
-Whittaker function

In this section we construct the Whittaker function associated with
the super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. There is a classical approach
to the construction of Whittaker functions associated with
semisimple Lie algebras
[
J
]
,
[
K1
]
,
[
K2
]
,
[
H
]
.
Below we give a modified version of this construction due to
Kazhdan-Kostant (see
[
E
]
).

Given a super Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
be the corresponding
universal enveloping algebra and let
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))\subset\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})

be its center. A
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
\mathcal{V}
admits an infinitesimal
character
\zeta
if there is a homomorphism
\zeta:\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
zv=\zeta(z)v
for all

z\!\in\!\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}))
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
. Given a character
\chi
of a
nilpotent super Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi\,:\quad\mathfrak{n}\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}^{1|1}\,;\end{array}

we define a Whittaker vector
\psi\in\mathcal{V}
by the following
relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}X\cdot\psi\,=\,\chi(X)\,\psi\,,\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{n}\subset\mathfrak{g}\,.\end{array}

The Whittaker vector
\psi\in\mathcal{V}
is called cyclic, if it
generates
\mathcal{V}
:
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\,\cdot\psi=\mathcal{V}
. A
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module

\mathcal{V}
is called a Whittaker module if it contains a cyclic Whittaker
vector. A pair of
\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is called
dual if there exists a non-degenerate pairing

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\,,\,\rangle\,:\quad\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}^{1|1}\,,\end{array}

which is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear in the first
variable and
\mathbb{C}
-linear in the second one, and such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle X\cdot v^{\prime},\,v\rangle\,=\,-(-1)^{p(v^{\prime})\cdot p(X)}\langle v^{\prime}\,,X\cdot v\rangle\,,\qquad v\in\mathcal{V},\,\,v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\,,\quad X\in\mathfrak{g}\,.\end{array}

Now we restrict ourselves to the case of the orthosymplectic super
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
be a
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell))
-module
with an infinitesimal central character allowing
a vector
v_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
defined by (see the notations
of (
2
), (
2
)):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{{\alpha}^{\vee}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\,=\,{\alpha}^{\vee}(\lambda)v_{\lambda}\,,\quad X_{{\alpha}}\cdot v_{\lambda}\,=\,0\,,\qquad\forall X_{{\alpha}}\in\mathfrak{n}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)\,,\quad{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}\,,\end{array}

where
\lambda
is an element of the dual to the Cartan subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
.
Value of the quadratic Casimir element
C_{2}
on
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
is
uniquely determined by (
3
). Indeed let us re-write the
Casimir element
C_{2}
from (
2.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}\Big(a_{ii}^{2}\,-\,a_{ii}\,+\,2X_{-\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\Big)\,+\,\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\frac{({\alpha},\,{\alpha})}{2}\Big(h_{{\alpha}^{\vee}}\,+\,2X_{-{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\Big)\\
=\,\sum_{i\in I}\Big(a_{ii}^{2}\,+\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})a_{ii}\,+\,2X_{-\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\Big)\,+\,\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}({\alpha},\,{\alpha})\,X_{-{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho(q)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}{\alpha}(q)\,-\,\sum_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}}\beta(q)\Big)\,.\end{array}

Thus using (
3
)
C_{2}
takes the following value on

v_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}(v_{\lambda})\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}\bigl(a_{ii}^{2}\,+\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})a_{ii}\bigr)(v_{\lambda})\,=\,(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)\,v_{\lambda}\,.\end{array}

In the following we consider those
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
that allow a
structure of the Whittaker modules and also allow integration of
the action of the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
to the
action of the corresponding maximal torus
H
. Precisely let

\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
be a dual pair of Whittaker
modules cyclically generated by
Whittaker vectors
\psi_{R}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and
\psi_{L}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{\prime}
.
Explicitly, the Whittaker vectors
\psi_{R}\in\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and

\psi_{L}\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
are defined by the following conditions

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{{\alpha}}\cdot\psi_{R}\,=\,\chi_{R}(X_{{\alpha}})\,\psi_{R}\,,\qquad X_{-{\alpha}}\cdot\psi_{L}\,=\,\chi_{L}(X_{-{\alpha}})\,\psi_{L}\,,\qquad\forall{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}\,,\end{array}

where
\chi_{R}:\,\mathfrak{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
and
\chi_{L}:\,\mathfrak{n}_{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{1|1}

are the characters of the opposite nilpotent super Lie subalgebras

\mathfrak{n}_{\pm}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}\,:\quad\mathfrak{n}_{+}\,=\,\Big(\bigoplus_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\mathbb{C}X_{{\alpha}}\,\oplus\,\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}}\mathbb{C}X_{\beta}\Big)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}^{1|1}\,,\\
\chi_{L}\,:\quad\mathfrak{n}_{-}\,=\,\Big(\bigoplus_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\mathbb{C}X_{-{\alpha}}\,\oplus\,\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}}\mathbb{C}X_{-\beta}\Big)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}^{1|1}\,.\end{array}

Lemma 3.1

(i)

The function
\chi_{R}:\,\mathfrak{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}\,\in\,\imath^{3/2}\mathbb{R}^{1|0}\,,\quad\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{2\epsilon_{\ell}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}\,\in\,\imath\mathbb{R}^{0|1}\,,\\
\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\epsilon_{\ell+1-k}-\epsilon_{\ell+2-k}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath\xi_{{\alpha}_{k}}^{+}\,\in\,\imath\mathbb{R}^{0|1}\,,\quad 1<k\leq\ell\,,\\
\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\epsilon_{k}}\bigr)\,=\,\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{{\alpha}}\bigr)\,=\,0\,,\qquad 1\leq k<\ell\,,\quad{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\setminus{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\,,\end{array}

is a character of the super Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{n}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)
.

(ii)

Similarly, the function
\psi_{L}:\,\mathfrak{n}_{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{L}\bigl(X_{-\epsilon_{\ell}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\,\in\,\imath^{3/2}\mathbb{R}^{1|0}\,,\qquad\chi_{L}\bigl(X_{-\epsilon_{2\ell}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-})^{2}\,\in\,\imath\mathbb{R}^{0|1}\,,\\
\chi_{L}\bigl(X_{-\epsilon_{\ell+1-k}+\epsilon_{\ell+2-k}}\bigr)\,=\,\imath\xi_{{\alpha}_{k}}^{-}\,\in\,\imath\mathbb{R}^{0|1}\,,\quad 1<k\leq\ell\,,\\
\chi_{L}\bigl(X_{-\epsilon_{k}}\bigr)\,=\,\chi_{L}\bigl(X_{-{\alpha}}\bigr)\,=\,0\,,\qquad 1\leq k<\ell\,,\quad{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\setminus{}^{s}\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}\,,\end{array}

is a character of the super subalgebra Lie

\mathfrak{n}_{-}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)
.

Proof
: We provide the proof in the case of
\chi_{R}
while the case of

\chi_{L}
can be treated in a similar way. Let us verify that
(
(i)
) defines a character
\chi_{R}
of the super
subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
by checking the compatibility
of (
(i)
) with the appropriate Cartan-Weyl relations
(
2

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X_{\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{\epsilon_{i}}]\,=\,2X_{\epsilon_{i}}^{2}\,=\,2X_{2\epsilon_{i}}\,,\quad[X_{\epsilon_{i}},\,X_{\epsilon_{j}}]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad i,j\in I\,,\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}},X_{\epsilon_{i+1}}\bigr]=X_{\epsilon_{i}},\quad\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}},[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}},X_{2\epsilon_{i+1}}]\bigr]=2X_{2\epsilon_{i}},\quad 1\leq i<\ell\,,\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{2\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad i<j\,,\\
\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}}\,,\qquad i<j<k\,,\end{array}

and with the Serre relations (
2
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}}^{2}(X_{\epsilon_{\ell}})\,=\,0\,,\quad{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}}^{3}(X_{\epsilon_{\ell-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}})\,=\,{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{\ell-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}}}^{2}(X_{\epsilon_{\ell}})\,=\,0\,,\\
{\rm ad}_{X_{2\epsilon_{\ell}}}^{2}(X_{\epsilon_{\ell-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}})\,=\,{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{\ell-1}-\epsilon_{\ell}}}^{3}(X_{2\epsilon_{\ell}})\,=\,0\,,\\
{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{i-1}-\epsilon_{i}}}^{2}(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}})\,=\,{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}}}^{2}(X_{\epsilon_{i-1}-\epsilon_{i}})\,=\,0\,,\quad 1<i<\ell\,.\end{array}

From the defining relations (
(i)
) we see that
\chi_{R}

takes non-zero values only on the simple root generators

X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}
,
X_{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+1}},\,1\leq k<\ell
and on the
special non-simple root generator
X_{2\epsilon_{\ell}}
. The latter
follows from the first relation from (
3
) for
i=\ell
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[X_{\epsilon_{\ell}},\,X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}]\,=\,2X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}^{2}\,=\,2X_{2\epsilon_{\ell}}\,.\end{array}

Indeed, given
X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\cdot\psi_{R}=\imath^{3/2}\,\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}\psi_{R}
one
readily deduces

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}2X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}^{2}\cdot\psi_{R}\,=\,2X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\cdot(X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\cdot\psi_{R})\,=\,2X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\cdot(\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}\psi_{R})\,=\,-2\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}(X_{\epsilon_{\ell}}\cdot\psi_{R})\\
=\,2\imath(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}\psi_{R}\,,\end{array}

which matches with

2X_{\epsilon_{2\ell}}\cdot\psi_{R}\,=\,2\imath(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}\psi_{R}
.
Similarly, the first relation from (
3
) for
1\leq i<\ell
yields

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}(X_{2\epsilon_{i}})\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\chi_{R}(X_{\epsilon_{i}})^{2}\,=\,0\,,\qquad 1\leq i<\ell\,,\end{array}

and the other relation in the first line of (
(i)
)
entails

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}(X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}})\,=\,\chi_{R}(X_{\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{j}}+X_{\epsilon_{j}}X_{\epsilon_{i}})\,=\,0\,,\qquad i<j\,.\end{array}

The Serre relations imply that

. Thus the rest of
the defining relations (

) are provided by the fact
that given

and

and not both

are odd, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}(X_{{\alpha}})\,=\,\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\beta}X_{{\gamma}}-X_{{\gamma}}X_{\beta}\bigr)\,=\,0\,.\end{array}

Namely, the last line of (

) for each

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}})\,=\,\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}-X_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\bigr)\,=\,0\,.\end{array}

Then the second and the third lines of (
3
) for each

we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{R}(X_{\epsilon_{i}})\,=\,\chi_{R}\bigl(X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}}X_{\epsilon_{k}}-X_{\epsilon_{k}}X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}}\bigr)\,=\,0\,.\end{array}

Finally, we check that the remaining relations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{2\epsilon_{j}}\bigr]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,,\qquad[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},\,X_{2\epsilon_{j}}]\,=\,X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}\,,\\
{\rm ad}_{X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}}^{2}(X_{2\epsilon_{j}})\,=\,\bigl[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},[X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}},X_{2\epsilon_{j}}]\bigr]\,=\,X_{2\epsilon_{i}}\,,\qquad i<j\,,\end{array}

are consistent with the defining relations (

). This
completes our proof.

Remark 3.1

Our choice of the characters (
(ii)
), (
(i)
)
in Lemma

Definition 3.1

Let
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
be a dual pair of
cyclic Whittaker modules with the action of the Casimir element given
by (

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker function is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\,=\,e^{-\rho(q)}\langle\psi_{L}\,,\,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,,\qquad h_{q}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}q_{i}a_{ii}\,,\end{array}

where

is the half-sum of positive even roots minus the half-sum
of positive odd roots, given by (

).

Proposition 3.1

The
\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)
-Whittaker function (
3.1
) is
a solution to the following eigenvalue problem:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)}\cdot\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\,=\,-(\lambda+\rho)^{2}\,\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)}\,=-\,\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,2\!\!\sum_{{\alpha}_{i}\in{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\,e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\,+\,4(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}\,e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,,\end{array}\end{array}

where
\rho
is given by (
3
), and

{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}={}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}(B_{0,\ell})
is defined in
(
2
).

Proof
: On the one hand, by our construction we read from
(
3
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\,C_{2}\,\psi_{R}\rangle\,=\,(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)\,\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\,C_{2}\,\psi_{R}\rangle\,.\end{array}

On the other hand, the action of the Casimir element

C_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)))
is equivalent to action on
(
3.1
) of a certain second-order differential operator.
Namely, from (
2
) we take

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{2}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}\Big(a_{ii}^{2}\,+\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})a_{ii}\,+\,2X_{-\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\Big)\,+\,\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}({\alpha},\,{\alpha})\,X_{-{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\,,\end{array}

and substituting this into (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{i\in I}\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{h_{q}}\bigl(a_{ii}^{2}\,+\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})a_{ii}\bigr)\,\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,\sum_{i\in I}\Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,-\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\Big\}\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,.\end{array}

Taking into account the defining equations (
(ii)
),
(
(i)
) and
the hermitian property (
3
) of
\langle\,,\,\rangle
we find out

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}2\sum_{i\in I}\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}X_{-\epsilon_{i}}X_{\epsilon_{i}}\,\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-2\sum_{i\in I}e^{q_{i}}(-1)^{p(X_{-\epsilon_{i}})\,p(\psi_{L})}\bigl\langle X_{-\epsilon_{i}}\psi_{L}\,\,,e^{-h_{q}}\,X_{\epsilon_{i}}\,\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-2(-1)^{p(X_{-\epsilon_{\ell}})\,p(\psi_{L})}e^{q_{\ell}}\bigl\langle\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-2(-1)^{p(X_{-\epsilon_{\ell}})\,p(\psi_{L})}(-1)^{p(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})\cdot p(\psi_{L})}\imath^{-3/2}\imath^{3/2}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}e^{{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-2\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+}e^{\alpha_{1}(q)}\,\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\,.\end{array}

Here we use the fact that
\langle\,,\,\rangle
is
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear in the
first variable and it is
\mathbb{C}
-linear in the second variable. In a similar way we
derive

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}({\alpha},\,{\alpha})\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}X_{-{\alpha}}X_{{\alpha}}\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}({\alpha},\,{\alpha})\,e^{{\alpha}(q)}\bigl\langle X_{-{\alpha}}\,\psi_{L}\,,e^{h_{q}}X_{{\alpha}}\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\
=\,-2\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\imath^{-1}\imath\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\bigl\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\psi_{R}\bigr\rangle\\

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\,C_{2}\,\psi_{R}\rangle\,=\,\Big\{\sum_{i\in I}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,-\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\Big)\\
-\,4(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,-\,2\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\,e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\Big\}\langle\psi_{L}\,,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,.\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{-\rho(q)}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}e^{\rho(q)}\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\,+\,\rho(q)^{\prime}_{q_{i}}\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\,+\,\rho(\epsilon_{i})\,,\\

=\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\,+\,\rho(\epsilon_{i})^{2}\,,\end{array}

hence we deduce the following:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{i\in I}e^{-\rho(q)}\Big\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,-\,2\rho(\epsilon_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}\Big\}e^{\rho(q)}\\

=\,\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,-\,\rho^{2}\,.\end{array}

Finally, we collect all the contributions and substitute them into
(

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,-\,4(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,-\,2\sum_{{\alpha}_{i}\in{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\,e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}-\,\rho^{2}\Big\}\cdot\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\\

where

. This easily
entails the assertion (

).

Remark 3.2

In the special case

3.1
) reads

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)}\,\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\,=\,\mu^{2}\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q})\,,\\
\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)}\,=\,-\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,2\!\!\sum_{{\alpha}_{i}\in{}^{s}\Delta^{+}}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\,e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\,+\,4(\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{-}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{+})^{2}\,e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,.\end{array}

Let us introduce the corresponding couplings:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{i}^{2}\,=\,\xi^{-}_{{\alpha}_{i}}\,\xi^{+}_{{\alpha}_{i}}\,,\qquad i\in I\,.\end{array}

Lemma 3.2

The
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker function (
3.1
)
depends on
\xi^{\pm}_{{\alpha}_{i}}
via
g_{i}^{2}
,
i\in I
.

Proof
: The
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker function (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Psi_{\lambda}(e^{q}|\xi^{\pm}_{{\alpha}_{i}})\,=\,e^{-\rho(q)}\langle\psi_{L}\,,\,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,,\qquad h_{q}\,=\,\sum_{i\in I}q_{i}a_{ii}\,,\end{array}

satisfies the following obvious relation: given

Q=\exp\bigl\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\theta_{i}h_{i}\bigr\}\in H

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\psi_{L}\,,\,Qe^{-h_{q}}Q^{-1}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,=\,\langle\psi_{L}\,,\,e^{-h_{q}}\cdot\psi_{R}\rangle\,.\end{array}

The adjoint action of
Q
on the left and right Whittaker vectors

\psi_{R},\,\psi_{L}
(
(ii)
), (
(i)
) changes them,
so that the eigenvalues
\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{\pm}
of the corresponding

\mathfrak{n}_{\pm}
-characters are changed as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{\pm}\longrightarrow\xi_{{\alpha}_{1}}^{\pm}e^{\pm\theta_{\ell}}\,,\qquad\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{\pm}\longrightarrow\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{\pm}e^{\pm(\theta_{\ell+1-i}-\theta_{\ell+2-i})}\,,\quad 1<i\leq\ell\,.\end{array}

The invariance of the Whittaker function under this transformation
implies that the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Whittaker function depends on

\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{\pm}
only via quadratic combinations

\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}
.
\Box

Lemma 3.3

Let us consider a specialization of the

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain
by taking arbitrary special values
g_{i}^{2}=\kappa_{i}^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
of
the couplings. Then by a linear change of variables
q_{i}
one can bring the quadratic
Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)}\,=\,-\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,2\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}^{2}\,e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\,+\,2\kappa_{1}^{2}\,e^{{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,+\,4\kappa_{1}^{4}\,e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,,\end{array}\end{array}

to the following canonical form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|\,2\ell)}\,=\,-\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}e^{{\alpha}_{i}(q)}\,+\,e^{{\alpha}_{1}(q)}\,+\,e^{2{\alpha}_{1}(q)}.\end{array}

Proof
: Indeed it is easy to check that the following transformation
of variables,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{\ell}\,\longmapsto\,q_{\ell}\,-\,\ln 2\,-\,\ln\kappa_{1}^{2}\,,\\
q_{k}\,\longmapsto\,q_{k}\,-\,(\ell+1-k)\ln 2\,-\,\ln\kappa^{2}_{k}\,+\,\ln\kappa^{2}_{\ell-k}\,,\qquad 1\leq k<\ell\,,\end{array}

applied to (
3.1
) gives (
3.3
).
\Box

4
On
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
as a Lie algebra of type
BC_{\ell}

Let us recall the construction of the Toda chain associated with the
general root system. Let
\Delta
be a rank
\ell
root system
realized as a set of vectors in
V=\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
. Chose an orthogonal
basis
\{\epsilon_{i},\,i\in I\}
in
V
and the dual basis
\{\epsilon^{i},\,i\in I\}
in
V^{*}
, both indexed by
I=\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
. Then elements

q\in V^{*}
allow decomposition
q=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}\epsilon^{i}
.
Let
{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}
be as a set of simple positive roots in

\Delta
. The quadratic quantum Hamiltonian of the Toda chain
associated with the root system
\Delta
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\Delta^{+}}\,=\,-\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}+\sum_{\alpha\in{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}}\,g^{2}_{\alpha}\,e^{\alpha(q)}\end{array}

with the coupling constants
g_{\alpha}^{2}
. Note that the Hamiltonian
depends only on the structure of simple positive roots

{}^{s}\!\Delta^{+}
.

Now let us specialize this expression to the case of
BC_{\ell}
-root
system, the unique non-reduced root system satisfying basic
axioms of root systems of finite-dimensional Lie algebras (for a
description of
BC_{\ell}
root system see e.g.
[
H
]
,
[
L
]
).
The set of simple positive roots of the
BC_{\ell}
root system is
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{}^{s}\Delta^{+}(BC_{\ell})\,=\,\bigl\{2\epsilon_{\ell};\,\,\epsilon_{\ell}\,,\quad\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\,,\,\,1\leq i<\ell\bigr\}\,.\end{array}

The Cartan matrix
A=\|A_{ij}\|
associated with the set of simple
positive roots is defined via standard formula

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{ij}=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}\,,\qquad i,j\in I\,.\end{array}

Note that the Cartan matrix corresponding to
BC_{\ell}
is degenerate.
For example the Cartan matrix for
\ell=5
is given by
(
2
):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}A\,=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&4&-2&0\\
1&2&-1&0\\
-1&-2&2&-1\\
0&0&-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right).\end{array}

Using the general formula (
4
) for
BC_{\ell}
-root system we
arrive at the following

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{BC_{\ell}}\,=\,-\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}g_{i}^{2}\,e^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}\,+\,g_{\ell}^{2}\,e^{q_{\ell}}\,+\,g_{\ell+1}^{2}\,e^{2q_{\ell}}.\end{array}

It is clear that specialization of the quadratic Hamiltonian
(
4
) of the
BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain to the case of the
coupling constants
g_{i}^{2}=1,\,i\in I
coincides with the quadratic
Hamiltonian
(
3.3
)
of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain. On the
other hand for generic values of
g_{i}
in (
4
) it is not
possible by linear changes of variables
q_{i}
to transform the
Hamiltonian (
4
) into the Hamiltonian with
g_{i}^{2}=1
.
Thus
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
-Toda chain realized a special class of

BC_{\ell}
-Toda chains.

There is a question on the underlying reason for this
phenomenon. It is easy to see that the simple
positive roots (
4
) of
BC_{\ell}
and that of
B_{0,\ell}

(
2
) are closely related. There are however two
differences. First, the short simple root of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
has odd
parity while in
BC_{\ell}
root system it is an even root. Second,
while in the case of super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
the
corresponding root system includes the roots
\pm 2\epsilon_{\ell}
, these
roots are not simple and thus do not enter the expression for the
corresponding Cartan matrix. If however we formally add the root

2\epsilon_{\ell}
to the set of positive simple roots then the
corresponding Cartan matrix constructed according to (
4
)
precisely coincides with the Cartan matrix of
BC_{\ell}
root system.

The fact that in the case of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
the terms of the
Cartan decomposition (
2
) corresponding to short
roots are odd actually does not manifest itself in the expressions
for the Hamiltonians of the corresponding Toda chain.
Indeed, according to Lemma
3.2
the eigenvalues

\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{\pm}
in (
(i)
), (
(ii)
) enter the
expressions for quantum Hamiltonians only via combinations

g_{i}^{2}=\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{+}\xi_{{\alpha}_{i}}^{-}
. Therefore
B_{0,\ell}
-Toda
chains turns out to be a special case of
BC_{\ell}
-Toda chain. We
have checked this explicitly for quadratic Hamiltonian in the
previous Section 3.

It is natural to wonder whether we might treat the super Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
as a proper candidate for the Lie algebra
structure associated with
BC_{\ell}
root system. Such identification
has at least one obvious caveat. The root system
BC_{\ell}
allows
embedding of roots systems
B_{\ell}
and
C_{\ell}
having isomorphic
Weyl groups
W_{B_{\ell}}\simeq W_{C_{\ell}}
. It would be natural to
expect the same property for the corresponding Lie algebras i.e. a
candidate for the Lie algebra associated with
BC_{\ell}
should allow
an embedding of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{so}_{2\ell+1}
and

\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell}
associated with the roots systems
B_{\ell}

and
C_{\ell}
correspondingly. While there indeed exists an embedding

\mathfrak{sp}_{2\ell}\subset\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
the super Lie algebra

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2\ell)
does not allow an embedding of

\mathfrak{so}_{2\ell+1}
.

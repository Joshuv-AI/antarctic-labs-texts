1.
Introduction

The main goal of this work is to analyse the existence or non-existence of positive global solutions to the non-local parabolic equation given by

(1)

\begin{cases}(w_{t}-\Delta w)^{s}=\dfrac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}w+w^{p}+f,&\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,t)=0&\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0],\end{cases}

where
s\in(0,1)
,
N\geq 2
,
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s})
(see (
6
)),
p>1
, and
f
is a
non-negative function satisfying suitable integrability hypothesis.

The operator
H^{s}(w):=(w_{t}-\Delta w)^{s}
is the fractional heat
operator. In the literature there are many possible ways to define

H^{s}
, see for example
[
3
]
for an overview. Among the papers
concerning the operators
H^{s}
and its properties we cite

[
24
]
for regularity results, a Harnack inequality, an
extension result and a monotonicity formula for solution to the
equation
H^{s}(w)=0
. The more general case of smooth potentials
has been treated by
[
4
]
, where the authors obtain a
monotonicity formula by the means of a suitable Hölder
regularity results and an extension theorem.

In the presence of an Hardy-type potential, a monotonicity
formula and the asymptotic behaviour near the
singular point
0
was investigated in
[
12
]
. A
detailed analysis of local solutions to the extended problem has
been carried out in
[
3
]
. Furthermore, a more general
extension theorem for a vast class of parabolic fractional
operators, including
H^{s}
, as been obtained in
[
5
]
. We
will give a precise definition of the operator
H^{s}
by means of
the Fourier transform in Section
2.1
.

The study of existence of global solutions to problem
(
1
), is related to the study of the so
called Fujita-type exponent, see
[
16
]
. Furthermore the
presence of an Hardy-type potential obstructs even the existence
of local solutions for large
p
. Before stating our main results,
let us make a brief overview of the literature concerning Fujita
exponents and non-existence results when there is a Hardy-type
potential.

In the semi-linear case,
s=1
and
\lambda=0
, Fujita proved, in his
fundamental work
[
16
]
, the existence of a critical
exponent
F=1+\frac{2}{N}
such that if
1<p<F
, then any solution
to the semi-linear problem

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=\Delta u+u^{p},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

blows up in finite time. The critical case
p=F
was considered
later in
[
28
]
. It is proved that in that case, a suitable norm
of the solution goes to infinity in a finite time.

In the case of fractional diffusion, the author in
[
25
]

considered the equation

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)&\text{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}\right.

where
N>2s
and
0<s<1
. Notice that
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the
fractional Laplacian defined by

(4)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=a_{N,s}\text{ P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy},\,s\in(0,1),

where

a_{N,s}=2^{2s-1}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{|\Gamma(-s)|}

is the normalization constant. See
[
8
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
19
]
and the references therein for additional properties of
the fractional Laplacian. In this case the Fujita critical
exponent takes the form
F(s)=1+\frac{2s}{N}
.

Now, in the case
\lambda>0
, the problem is related to the following Hardy-Leray type
inequality:

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

(5)

\Lambda_{N,s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2s}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy,

with

(6)

\Lambda_{N,s}=2^{2s}\dfrac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N+2s}{4})}{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N-2s}{4})}.

The constant
\Lambda_{N,s}
is optimal and is not attained.
Moreover,
\Lambda_{N,s}\to\Lambda_{N,1}:=\left(\dfrac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
, the classical Hardy
constant, when
s
tends to
1
.

This inequality was first proved in
[
17
]
. See also

[
23
]
for a direct proof.

The case
s=1
and
\lambda>0
was treated in
[
1
]
. More
precisely, if we consider the problem

(7)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=\lambda\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2}}+u^{p},&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

setting

\mu(\lambda)=\frac{N-2}{2}-\sqrt{\Big(\frac{N-2}{2}\Big)^{2}-\lambda}
,
then if
p\geq p_{+}(\lambda):=1+\frac{2}{\mu(\lambda)}
there are no local positive supersolutions.

Due to the presence of the Hardy potential, then
it is possible to show that any positive solution of
(
7
) satisfies

u(x,t)\geq C(t,r)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\mbox{ in }B_{r}(0),t>0.

See for instance
[
1
]
.

Furthermore in the spirit of Fujita blow-up
exponent, it is proved in
[
1
]
that if

1<p<1+\frac{2}{N-\mu(\lambda)}
, there exists
T^{*}>0
, independent
of the initial datum, such that the solution
u
to equation
(
7
) satisfies

(8)

\lim\limits_{t\to T^{*}}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}u(x,t)\,dx=\infty,

for any ball
B_{r}(0)
. Finally if

1+\dfrac{2}{N-\mu(\lambda)}<p<1+\dfrac{2}{\mu(\lambda)}
, if the initial
datum is small enough, there exists a global solution to
(
7
).

Hence we can define the Fujita type exponent in this case by
setting
F(\lambda)=1+\dfrac{2}{N-\mu(\lambda)}
. Under
fractional diffusion the equation (
7
) takes the form

(9)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2}}+u^{p},&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

In this case, it was proved in
[
2
]
that
if
p\geq\tilde{p}_{+}(\lambda,s):=1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}
, there are no local
positive supersolutions, while the Fujita critical exponent is
given by
\tilde{F}(\lambda,s)=1+\dfrac{2s}{N-\mu(\lambda)}

where

(10)

\mu(\lambda)=\dfrac{N-2s}{2}-\alpha_{\lambda}

and

\alpha_{\lambda}
is defined by the implicit formula

\Upsilon(\alpha_{\lambda})=\dfrac{2^{2s}\,\Gamma(\frac{N+2s+2\alpha_{\lambda}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{N+2s-2\alpha_{\lambda}}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s+2\alpha_{\lambda}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{N-2s-2\alpha_{\lambda}}{4})},

see also Proposition
3.1
.

Going back to the operator
H^{s}
, in the case
\lambda=0
, the author in
[
26
,
27
]
considers the problem

(11)

\begin{cases}0\leq(w_{t}-\Delta w)^{s}\leq w^{p},&\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,t)=0,&\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0].\end{cases}

Using an integral representation formula for the inverse of
H^{s}
he was able to show the existence of a
critical Fujita exponent given by
F(s)=1+\frac{2s}{N+2-2s}
. The proof is deeply based on some convolution properties of Gaussian
functions.

The situation when
\lambda>0
is more delicate. The argument used in
[
27
]
seems to be directly not applicable in our
situation. Our approach is instead based on the extension result proved for fractional powers of the heat operator in
[
5
]
.

Let us define the critical non-existence exponent
p_{+}(\lambda,s)
and the Fujita exponent
F(\lambda,s)
respectively as:

(12)

p_{+}(\lambda,s):=1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}\quad\text{ and }F(\lambda,s):=1+\frac{2s}{N+2-2s-\mu(\lambda)},

with
\mu(\lambda)
as in (
10
). We distinguish different cases according to the value of the exponent
p
. Our main results are the following.

•

The case
p>p_{+}(\lambda,s)
. Here, as in the elliptic case,
using the local behaviour of the solution near the origin obtained
in Proposition
5.6
, and thanks to a
Picone-type inequality (see Proposition
6.1
below),
we are able to show that there are not any weak non-trivial
solution to problem (
1
). In particular
if
f\not\equiv 0
there are not any non-negative weak
supersolutions.

•

The case
p<p_{+}(\lambda,s)
. Here it is possible
to show the existence of local solutions, see Remark

6.3
. On the other hand, concerning global solutions
we distinguish two cases:

–

The case
F(\lambda,s)<p<p_{+}(\lambda,s)
. Here we prove the
existence of a very weak supersolution. Thanks to
Proposition
4.8
, we get the
existence of a very weak solution to equation
(
1
).

–

The case
1<p\leq F(\lambda,s)
.
Here we show that any positive weak or very weak solution of
(
1
) blows up, in a suitable sense, in
finite time. See Theorem
6.4
.

We remark that our result are coherent with
[
27
]
. Indeed
\mu(\lambda)\to 0^{+}
,
p_{+}(\lambda,s)\to\infty
and
F(\lambda,s)\to F(s)
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, where
F(s)
is the Fujita exponent of problem (
11
). Furthermore the technique that we used to
prove Theorem
6.4
can be easily adapted to the case
\lambda=0
, see Remark
6.5
and also
[
27
]
.

It is also interesting to notice that the critical non-existence
exponent
p_{+}(\lambda,s)
is the same for problems (
9
) and
(
1
) and that
p_{+}(\lambda,s)\to p_{+}(\lambda)
as

s\to 1^{+}
, where
p_{+}(\lambda)
is the critical non-existence exponent
of problem (
7
). This is due to the fact the exponent

p_{+}(\lambda,s)
is determined only by the elliptic part of the
operator and the presence of the Hardy-type
potential, which induces a singular behaviour near
0
for any
non-negative supersolutions.

On the other hand the Fujita exponents
F(\lambda,s)
and

\tilde{F}(\lambda,s)
of problems (
9
) and
(
1
) are different and in particular

F(\lambda,s)<\tilde{F}(\lambda,s)
for any
s\in(0,1)
and
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s})
. Nevertheless we have that
F(\lambda,s)\to F(\lambda)
and
\tilde{F}(\lambda,s)\to F(\lambda)
as
s\to 1^{-}
, where

F(\lambda)
is the Fujita exponent of (
7
).

This paper is organized as follows. In the Section

2
we introduce some
definitions and useful tools about the fractional power of the
heat equation, the sense for which solution are defined, some
representation formulas and preliminary results.

In Section
3
we consider the operator

H^{s}
perturbed by the Hardy potential and prove ground state
representation formula for
H^{s}(\cdot)-\lambda\frac{\cdot}{|x|^{2s}}
.
In Section
4
we obtain a comparison
principle for weak solutions using the extension procedure
developed in
[
5
]
. As a consequence, we show the existence
of weak solutions in a suitable sense of problem

1
, if there exists a supersolution of the
same problem. In Section
5
, we analyse the
local behaviour of weak supersolutions. In particular we are able
to obtain pointwise estimates from below and a strong maximum
principle as consequence.

Finally our main results are proved in Section

6
, while in Section

7
, we discuss some remaining open problems
and possible further developments.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Functional setting

For any real Hilbert space
X
we denote with
X^{*}
its
dual space and with
\sideset{{}_{X^{*}}}{{}_{X}}{\mathop{\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle}}
the duality between
X^{*}
and
X
;
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{X}
denotes the scalar product in
X
.

We define the operator
H^{s}
by means of the Fourier transform as follows:

(13)

\widehat{H^{s}(w)}(\xi,\theta):=(i\theta+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\widehat{w}(\xi,\theta),

where the Fourier transform of
w
is defined as

\mathcal{F}(w)(\xi,\theta)=\widehat{w}(\xi,\theta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}e^{-i(x\cdot\xi+t\theta)}w(x,t)\,dx\,dt.

Then the natural domain for a pointwise definition of
H^{s}
is the set

\left\{w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}):|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}\widehat{w}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})\right\}.

Furthermore, if
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then by
[
24
, Theorem
1.1]
, we have the following pointwise formula for the
operator
H^{s}

(14)

H^{s}\phi(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-s)}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,\sigma)}{(t-\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\sigma)}}dyd\sigma.

Note that we have stated the previous formula for
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for the sake of simplicity,
see
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
for less restrictive regularity assumption on
\phi
.

We can extend
H^{s}
on

\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}):=\left\{w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}|\widehat{w}(\xi,\theta)|^{2}\,d\xi\,d\theta<+\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}|\widehat{w}(\xi,\theta)|^{2}d\xi\,d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

as the map from
\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
into its dual space
(\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}))^{*}
, defined as

(15)

\sideset{{}_{(\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}))^{*}}}{{}_{\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})}}{\mathop{\left\langle H^{s}(w),v\right\rangle}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}(i\theta+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\widehat{w}(\xi,\theta)\overline{\widehat{v}(\xi,\theta)}d\xi d\eta,

for any
w,v\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
.
We remark that
\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
coincides with the set of pointwise definition for the operator
H^{\frac{s}{2}}
.

The space
\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
can be characterized more explicitly. Let

(16)

X^{s}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},W^{\frac{s}{2},2}(\mathbb{R})),

where we are identifying a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}
with variables
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
with the map
t\mapsto u(\cdot,t)
or with the map
x\mapsto u(x,\cdot)
respectively.
We can endow
X^{s}
with the natural norm

(17)

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{X^{s}}:=\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}+\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},W^{\frac{s}{2},2}(\mathbb{R}))}\\
=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|\xi|^{2s}|\mathcal{F}_{x}(w)(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|\theta|^{s}|\mathcal{F}_{t}(w)(x,\theta)|^{2}dxd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\mathcal{F}_{x}
and
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denote the Fourier transforms in the variables
x
and
t
respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
s\in(0,1)
,

(18)

X^{s}=\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})

with equivalent norms. In particular the natural embedding

(19)

\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

is linear and continuous.

Proof.

It is clear that
|\xi|^{2s}\leq|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}
and
|\theta|^{s}\leq|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}
for any
(\theta,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.
Furthermore it is easy to see that there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
s
, such that for any
a,b\in[0,+\infty)

(20)

(a+b)^{s}\leq C(a^{s}+b^{s}).

Indeed the function
f(\tau):=\frac{(\tau+1)^{s}}{1+\tau^{s}}
is bounded in
[0,+\infty)
.
Hence
|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{s}\leq C(|\theta|^{s}+|\xi|^{2s})
for any
(\theta,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Then (
18
) follows from the Plancherel identity.
∎

We also have an inversion formula for the operator
H^{s}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
q\in[1,2]
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,T))
, for any
T\in\mathbb{R}
, be fixed. We define the
operator

(21)

J_{s}g(x,t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}g(x-z,t-\tau)\,dz\,d\tau.

By
[
26
]
, the operator
J_{s}
has the following properties:

(22)

\displaystyle H^{s}(\phi)=g\text{ if and only if }J_{s}(g)=\phi,\quad\text{ for any }\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),

(23)

\displaystyle J_{s}(g)\in L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),

(24)

\displaystyle J_{s}=J_{\alpha}J_{\beta}\text{ for any }\alpha,\beta>0\text{ such that }\alpha+\beta=s.

(25)

\displaystyle\text{ If }g\text{ is non negative, then }J_{s}(g)=0\text{ in
}(-\infty,0)\text{ if and only if }g=0\text{ in }(-\infty,0).

In view of (
22
), we say that
J_{s}
is the inverse of the operator
H^{s}
.

In the spirit of
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
it makes sense to give the following definitions for weak supersolution, subsolutions and solutions of the problem

(26)

\begin{cases}H^{s}(w)=g&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty),\\
w=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0].\end{cases}

Definition 2.3
.

Let
q\in[1,2]
,
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,T))
for any

T\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose that
g(t,x)=0
a.e. in
(-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then, we say that
w
is a very weak
supersolution (subsolution) of (
26
) if
w\in L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and

(27)

w\geq(\leq)\,J_{s}(g)\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

If
w
is both a supersolution and a subsolution of
(
26
), then, we say that
w
is a
solution of (
26
).

The definition above already encodes that condition
w=0
in

\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0]
by (
25
).

We can also define energy solutions to (
26
) for a suitable class of data
g
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
be non-negative and suppose that
g(t,x)=0
a.e. in
(-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Assume that

(28)

\phi\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\phi\,dx\right)dt

belongs to
(\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}))^{*}
.
Then we say that
w
is a energy or weak supersolution (subsolution) of (
26
) if
w\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
,

(29)

\sideset{{}_{(\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}))^{*}}}{{}_{\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})}}{\mathop{\left\langle H^{s}(w),\phi\right\rangle}}\geq(\leq)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\phi\,dx\right)dt,\\

for any non-negative
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and
w=0
in

\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0]
. If
w
is a supersolution and a
subsolution of the equation in (
26
), we say that

w
is a solution of the equation (
26
).

To deal with (
1
) we recall the
following fractional Sobolev inequality (see
[
8
, Theorem
6.5]
)

(30)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\phi|^{\frac{2N}{N-2s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-2s}{N}}\leq S_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dxdy\quad\text{ for any }\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and suppose that
f(t,x)=0
a.e. in
(-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
w\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
be such that

(31)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{\frac{2Np}{N+2s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N+2s}{N}}\,dt<+\infty\quad\text{ or }\quad w\in L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),

w=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0]
, and

(32)

for any non-negative

In this section we prove some preliminary results about the operators

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Then
J_{s}(g)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.

Proof.

We proceed in the spirit of
[
26
]
. Let

(33)

Y(z,\tau):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\mbox{
where }\tau>0\mbox{ and }z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We can write
J_{s}(g)
as

J_{s}(g)=(Y\chi_{(0,1)})\ast g+(Y\chi_{(1,+\infty)})\ast g,

where for any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}

\chi_{E}(t):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }t\in E,\\
0,&\text{ if }t\not\in E.\end{cases}

By the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
, we know that
Y\chi_{(0,1)}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and so by the Young inequality
(Y\chi_{(0,1)})\ast g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,
since
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Furthermore, by the Young inequality

\left\lVert(Y\chi_{(1,+\infty)})\ast h_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}\leq\left\lVert h_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}\left\lVert Y\chi_{(1,+\infty)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}

and by a change of variables

\left\lVert Y\chi_{(1,+\infty)}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}^{2}=\frac{1}{(\Gamma(s))^{2}(4\pi)^{N}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2\tau}}}{\tau^{N+2-2s}}\,dzd\tau\\
=\frac{1}{(\Gamma(s))^{2}(4\pi)^{N}}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\tau^{-\frac{N}{2}-2+2s}\,d\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}}\,dz<+\infty,

since
N\geq 2
and
s\in(0,1)
. In conclusion
J_{s}(g)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and suppose that
J_{s}(g)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Then

(34)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|i\theta+|\xi|^{2}|^{2s}|\widehat{J_{s}(g)}(\xi,\theta)|^{2}d\xi\,d\theta<+\infty.

In particular,
J_{s}(g)\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
and

H^{s}(J_{s}(g))
is well defined pointwise.

Proof.

Since

\mathcal{F}(J_{\frac{s}{2}}g)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\mathcal{F}\left(\chi_{[0,+\infty)}(\tau)\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\right)\widehat{g},

we need to compute the Fourier transform of
\chi_{(0,+\infty)}(\tau)\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}
, where

\chi_{[0,+\infty)}(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\tau\in(0,+\infty),\\
0,&\text{ if }\tau\in(-\infty,0).\end{cases}

We make this computation in details for the sake of completeness, see
[
26
]
and the references within. The Fourier transform of Gaussian function and a change of variables yield

(35)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}e^{-i(z\cdot\xi+\tau\theta)}\chi_{[0,+\infty)}(\tau)\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\,dzd\tau=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-\frac{N}{2}-1+s}e^{-i\tau\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-iz\cdot\xi}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dzd\tau\\
=2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-1+s}e^{-\tau(i\theta+|\xi|^{2})}\,d\tau=2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(s)(i\theta+|\xi|^{2})^{-s}.

Hence, since
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, we have proved (
34
) and the last claim follows from (
34
).
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, we have that

(36)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}H^{s}(\phi)\psi\,dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\phi(-x,-t)H^{s}(\psi(-\cdot))\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\phi)(-x,-t)H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\psi(-\cdot))\,dxdt,

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}J_{s}(\phi)\psi\,dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\phi(-x,-t)J_{s}(\psi(-\cdot))\,dxdt.

Proof.

By (
13
) and the Plancherel identity

(38)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}H^{s}(\phi)\psi\,dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}(i\theta+|\xi|^{2})^{s}\widehat{\phi}(\xi,\theta)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(\xi,\theta)}d\xi d\eta

and so the first equality in (
36
) follows from
\overline{\widehat{\psi}}=\widehat{\psi(-\cdot)}
and the Plancherel identity.
The second identity is an easy consequence of the first one. On the other hand by (
21
), the Fubini-Tonelli theorem and a change of variables

(39)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}J_{s}(\phi)\psi\,dxdt=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\phi(x-z,t-\tau)\psi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\,dz\,d\tau\\
=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\psi(x+z,t+\tau)\,dz\,d\tau\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt,

which yields (
37
).
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,
g(t,x)=0
a.e. in
(-\infty,0)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and that
w\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
is a weak supersolution of the problem

(40)

\begin{cases}H^{s}(w)=g,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
w=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0),\end{cases}

in the sense given by Definition
2.4
. Then

(41)

H^{\frac{s}{2}}(w)\geq J_{\frac{s}{2}}(g)\quad\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

Proof.

By (
37
) and a change of variables for any positive
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,

(42)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\phi\,dx\right)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}J_{\frac{s}{2}}(g)(-x,-t)H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\phi(-\cdot))\,dx\,dt.

Hence by (
36
) we deduce that for any positive
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})

(43)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}[H^{\frac{s}{2}}(w)(-x,-t)-J_{\frac{s}{2}}(g)(-x,-t)]H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\phi(-\cdot))\,dxdt\geq 0.

For any positive
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
let

(44)

\phi:=J_{\frac{s}{2}}(\psi)(-\cdot).

Then
H^{\frac{s}{2}}(\phi(-\cdot))=\psi
, and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. It follows that

(45)

H^{\frac{s}{2}}(w)(-x,-t)-J_{\frac{s}{2}}(g)(-x,-t)\geq 0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N+1},

).
∎

3.
Ground state transformation for the operator
H^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
.

We start by recalling the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
,
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
\Lambda_{N,s}
be as in (
6
). Then the function
\Upsilon:\Big[0,\frac{N-2s}{2}\Big)\to(0,\Lambda_{N,s}]
defined as

(46)

\Upsilon(\alpha):=2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s+2\alpha}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s-2\alpha}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N-2s-2\alpha}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{N-2s+2\alpha}{4}\right)}

is well-defined, continuous, surjective, decreasing,
\Upsilon(0)=\Lambda_{N,s}
and
\lim_{\alpha\to(\frac{N-2s}{2})^{-}}\Upsilon(\alpha)=0
.

Let us define for any
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s}]

(47)

\mu(\lambda):=\frac{N-2s}{2}-\Upsilon^{-1}(\lambda).

Then clearly

(48)

0<{\mu(\lambda)}<\frac{N-2s}{2}\quad\text{ for any }\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s}).

The most important result of this section is a ground state
transformation for the operator
H^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
, in the
spirit of
[
15
, Proposition 4.1]
where a similar
representation was computed for the elliptic operator

(-\Delta)^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
. We need a preliminary lemma
about radial functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be radial. Then there exists a constant
K_{N,s,\lambda}>0
depending only on
N,s
and
\lambda
such that

(49)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\mu(\lambda)}}dy\leq K_{N,s,\lambda}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(y)|\,dy,\quad\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

Passing in polar coordinates and letting
y^{\prime}=\frac{y}{\rho}
and
x^{\prime}=\frac{y}{r}
we obtain

(50)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\mu(\lambda)}}dy=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{N-1}|f(\rho)|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|x^{\prime}r-\rho y^{\prime}|^{-\mu(\lambda)}dS_{y^{\prime}}\,d\rho\\
=r^{-\mu(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(r\sigma)^{N-1}|f(r\sigma)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|x^{\prime}-\sigma y^{\prime}|^{-\mu(\lambda)}dS_{y^{\prime}}\right)r\,d\sigma,

by the change of variables
\sigma=\frac{\rho}{r}
.
Let us define

(51)

K(\sigma):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|x^{\prime}-\sigma y^{\prime}|^{-\mu(\lambda)}dS_{y^{\prime}}.

Since
|x^{\prime}-\sigma y|^{2}=1-2\sigma x^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime}+\sigma^{2}
, with a
change of variables, we deduce that

(52)

K(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|1-2\sigma y_{1}+\sigma^{2}|^{-\frac{\mu(\lambda)}{2}}dS_{y^{\prime}},

and so
K
does not depends on
x
. Using spherical coordinates, see also
[
14
]
, we obtain

(53)

K(\sigma)=C\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(\theta)^{N-2}}{|1-2\sigma\cos(\theta)+\sigma^{2}|^{\frac{\mu(\lambda)}{2}}}d\theta,

where
C>0
is a positive constant depending only on
N
. Since for
\sigma=1
the singularity in
0
is integrable in view of (
48
),
we conclude that
K
is bounded.
It follows that, thanks to the change of variables
\rho=r\sigma
,

(54)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\mu(\lambda)}}dy\leq r^{-\mu(\lambda)}\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(r\sigma)^{N-1}|f(r\sigma)|r\,d\sigma\\
=r^{-\mu(\lambda)}\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho^{N-1}|f(\rho)|\,d\rho=r^{-\mu(\lambda)}\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(y)|dy,

and so we have proved (
49
).
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s})

, depending only on
N,s
and
\lambda
, such that for any

|L^{s}\phi(t,x)|\leq C|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\left(\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}+\left\lVert|\nabla_{(x,t)}\phi|\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}+\left\lVert|\nabla^{2}_{x}\phi|\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}\right),

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, where

denotes the matrix of the second derivates of
\phi
with respect to the spacial variable
x
.
Furthermore for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})

(57)

where

By a change of variables it is clear that

(58)

\leq\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}|\Gamma(-s)|}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(x,t)-\phi(x-z,t-\tau)}{|x-z|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}dzd\tau\right|\\
+C_{1}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{|x-z|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}dzd\tau

for some constant positive
C_{1}
depending on
N
, and
s
. In

3.2

(59)

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{|x-z|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}dzd\tau\leq C_{2}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}

N,s
and
\lambda

We will estimate the last two integrals. We start by noticing that

(62)

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B^{\prime}_{1}}\frac{\phi(x,t)-\phi(x-z,t-\tau)}{|x-z|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}dzd\tau\\

Then the change of variables

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B^{\prime}_{1}}\frac{\phi(x,t)-\phi(x,t-\tau)}{|x-z|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}}dzd\tau\leq C_{4}\left\lVert\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B^{\prime}_{1})}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}.

\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}\leq\frac{C_{5}}{|z|^{N+2+2s}+\tau^{\frac{N+2+2s}{2}}}\quad\text{ for any }z\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\tau\in(0,\infty).

Then arguing, as in Lemma

and

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B^{\prime}_{1}}\frac{|\phi(x,t)-\phi(x-z,t-\tau)|}{|x-z|^{\mu(\lambda)}\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}dzd\tau\leq C_{6}\left\lVert|\nabla\phi|\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B^{\prime}_{1}}\frac{(|z|+\sqrt{\tau})^{-N-2s}}{|x-z|^{\mu(\lambda)}}dzd\tau\\

As a direct application of the ground state representation, we get

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
is a non-negative function. Then, for any
m>1
, we have

(70)

L^{s}(\phi^{m})(x,t)\leq m\phi^{m-1}(x,t)L^{s}(\phi)(x,t).

Proof.

We start by showing the next algebraic inequality

(71)

a^{m}-b^{m}\leq ma^{m-1}(a-b)\quad\text{ for any }a,b\geq 0.

To this end, let
\gamma(x)=1-x^{m}-m(1-x)
for any
x\in[0,+\infty)
. Since
\gamma^{\prime}(x)=m(1-x^{m-1})
, it is clear that
\gamma(x)\leq\gamma(1)=0
for any
x\in[0,+\infty)
. Taking

x=b/a
we obtain (
71
). Hence

(72)

L^{s}(\phi^{m})(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-s)}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi^{m}(x,t)-\phi^{m}(y,\sigma)}{|y|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\sigma)}}dyd\sigma\\
\leq m\phi^{m-1}(x,t)\displaystyle\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-s)}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,\sigma)}{|y|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\sigma)}}dyd\sigma\\
=m\phi^{m-1}(x,t)L^{s}(\phi)(x,t),

and so we have proved (
70
).
∎

Hence, we will follow a different approach, based on the

4.1.
An extension result

We will use the extension procedure of

(see also

We denote as
(z,t)=(x,y,t)
the variable in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)
.
Furthermore we use the symbols
\nabla
and

to denote the gradient, respectively the divergence, with respect to the space variable
z=(x,y)
.

For any

and any open set

If

is an open Lipschitz set contained in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}

is defined as the completion of

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(E,y^{1-2s})}:=\left(\int_{E}y^{1-2s}(\phi^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2})\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In view of
[

H^{1}(E,y^{1-2s})=\left\{V\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(E):\int_{E}y^{1-2s}(V^{2}+|\nabla V|^{2})\,dz<+\infty\right\}.

By
[
20
]
there exists a linear and continuous trace operator

(73)

\mathop{\rm{Tr}}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1},y^{1-2s})\to W^{2,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For any
w\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
and any
(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, let

(74)

W(x,y,t):=\frac{y^{2s}}{4^{\frac{N}{2}+s}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}+y^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1+s}}w(x-\xi,t-\tau)\,d\xi d\tau.

The function
W
is an extension of

which possesses good
properties, as it has been proved in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
,
see also
[
21
, Theorem 1]
,
[
24
, Theorem 1.7]
and

[
4
, Section 3, Section 4]
. More precisely there holds the
following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.1, Remark 4.3]
,
[
4
, Corollary 3.2]

Let
w\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
and let
W
be as in (

). Then
W\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{1-2s}))
and weakly solves

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}W_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla W)=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\mathop{\rm{Tr}}(W(\cdot,t))=w(\cdot,t),&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N},\text{ for a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R},\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial W}{\partial y}=\kappa_{s}H^{s}(w),&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

in the sense that for a.e.

and any

(75)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}W\phi_{t}\,dz\,\right)dt-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}W(z,T)\phi(z,T)\,dz+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}W(z,0)\phi(z,0)\,dz\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}\nabla W\cdot\nabla\phi\,dz\,\right)dt-\kappa_{s}\sideset{{}_{(\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s}))^{*}}}{{}_{\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})}}{\mathop{\left\langle H^{s}(w),\phi(\cdot,0,\cdot)\right\rangle}},

with
\kappa_{s}:=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2^{2s-1}\Gamma(s)}
.

4.2.
A comparison principle

Let us begin by proving a weak maximum principle for the extended problem.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T>0
and suppose that
W\in L^{2}((0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{1-2s}))
is a weak positive subsolution of the problem

(76)

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}W_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla W)=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\times(0,T),\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial W}{\partial y}=0,&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
W(z,0)=0,&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1},\end{cases}

in the sense that for any positive
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}\times[0,T])

(77)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}\nabla W\cdot\nabla\phi\,dz\,\right)dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}W\phi_{t}\,dz\,\right)dt\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}W(z,T)\phi(z,T)\,dz\leq 0.

Then
W\equiv 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times(0,T)
.

Proof.

By an approximated procedure, for example the Faedo-Galerkin method, we may suppose that
W_{t}\in L^{2}((0,+\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{1-2s}))
.
Hence , we may test (
77
) with
W
. Since

(78)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}WW_{t}\,dz\,\right)dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}W^{2}(z,T)\,dz

we obtain

(79)

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla W|^{2}\,dz\,\right)dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}W^{2}(z,T)\,dz\leq 0,

in view of (
77
). We conclude that
W\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
T>0
and suppose that

satisfy the problem

(80)

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}W_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla W)\leq y^{1-2s}V_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla V),&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\times(0,T),\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial W}{\partial y}\leq-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial V}{\partial y},&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
W(z,0)\leq V(z,0)&\text{ a.e. }z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1},\end{cases}

in a weak sense. Then
W\leq V
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times(0,T)
.
Furthermore, letting

and

, we have that

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)

Proof.

Let

. By the Kato inequality, and
(
80
), it is easy to see that
U^{+}

satisfies (
76
). Hence
U^{+}\equiv 0

in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times(0,T)

, that is

a.e. in

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\times(0,T)
. Let us define

(81)

\widetilde{U}(z,t)=\widetilde{U}(x,y,t):=\begin{cases}U(x,y,t),&\text{ if }y>0,\\
U(x,-y,t),&\text{ if }y<0,\end{cases}

and let

be a family of standard mollifiers on

[

, Lemma 1.5]
,
\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\widetilde{U}\to\widetilde{U}
in

and

and deduce that

a.e. in

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)
.

∎

We recall the definition of the class

Definition 5.4

For any

6.

In this section we analyse the existence and behaviour of solutions to problem (

6.1.
A non-existence result

In order to prove Theorem
6.2
, we need a version of the Picone-type inequality for the extended problem.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
r>0
and assume
W\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+},y^{1-2s})
is such that
W>\delta
for some positive constant
\delta>0
and
W
weakly solves the problem

(118)

\begin{cases}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla W)=y^{1-2s}g,&\text{ in }B_{r}^{+},\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial W}{\partial y}=f(x),&\text{ on }B_{r}^{\prime},\end{cases}

in the sense that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}^{+}\cup B_{r}^{\prime})

(119)

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}\nabla W\cdot\nabla\phi\,dz=\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}g\phi\,dz+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f\phi(x,0)\,dx,

where

,

with
f\geq 0
,
f\neq 0
. Then for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}\cup B_{r}^{\prime})

(120)

\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dz\geq\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}\frac{f(x)}{\mathop{\rm{Tr}}(W)}\phi^{2}(x,0)dx+\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{g}{W}\phi^{2}\,dz.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}\cup B_{r}^{\prime})
. Then

(121)

y^{1-2s}|\nabla\phi(x,y)|^{2}\geq y^{1-2s}\nabla\left(\frac{\phi^{2}(x,y)}{W(x,y)}\right)\nabla W(x,y)\quad\text{ a.e. in }B_{r}^{+}.

Hence by integration, since
\phi^{2}W^{-1}\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+},y^{1-2s})
, we obtain

(122)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dz\\
\geq\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}\nabla\left(\frac{\phi^{2}(x,y)}{W(x,y)}\right)\nabla W(x,y)\,dz=\int_{B_{r}^{+}}y^{1-2s}g\frac{\phi^{2}(x,y)}{W(x,y)}\,dz+\int_{B_{r}^{\prime}}f\frac{\phi^{2}(x,0)}{\mathop{\rm{Tr}}(W)}\,dx

which proves (
120
).
∎

We have the following non-existence theorem for weak supersolutions.

Theorem 6.2

Assume that

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Let

and
suppose that (
32
) has a non-negative

. Then there exists a solution of
(
32
) and a sequence

as in Proposition

4.7
. For any

and any

(t_{1},t_{2})\subset(0,+\infty)
by standard abstract parabolic

[

(W_{n})_{t}\in L^{2}((t_{1},t_{2}),B_{r}^{+})
. Fix

and

. Then for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}\cup B_{r}^{\prime})
by (

6.1

,
and the regularity of

(123)

and so for any

(137)

L^{s}(\varphi_{R})(x,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-s)}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi_{R}(x,t)-\varphi_{R}(y,\sigma)}{|y|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4(t-\sigma)}}dyd\sigma\\
=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(-s)}R^{-\mu(\lambda)-2s}\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{t}{R^{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\varphi(R^{-1}x,R^{-2}t)-\varphi(\tilde{y},\tilde{\sigma})}{|\tilde{y}|^{{\mu(\lambda)}}(R^{-2}t-\tilde{\sigma})^{\frac{N}{2}+s+1}}e^{-\frac{|R^{-1}x-\tilde{y}|^{2}}{4(R^{-2}t-\tilde{\sigma})}}d\tilde{y}d\tilde{\sigma}\\
\leq C_{1}R^{-2s}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)},

for some positive constant
C_{1}>0
depending only on
N,s,\lambda
and
\varphi
, by Proposition
3.3
.
Hence

(138)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\varphi^{m}_{R}(x,t)\,dxdt\\
\leq C_{2}R^{-2sp^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi_{R}^{m-p^{\prime}}(x,t)\,dxdt,

for some positive constant
C_{2}>0
.
The change of variables
\tilde{x}=R^{-1}x
and
\tilde{t}=R^{-2}t
yields

(139)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\varphi_{R}^{m}(x,t)\,dxdt\\
\leq C_{2}R^{N+2-\mu(\lambda)-2sp^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-p^{\prime}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\,d\tilde{x}d\tilde{t}.

Thanks to Fatou’s Lemma, we can pass to the limit as
R\to\infty
and conclude that

(140)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\,dxdt<+\infty.

In particular, if
p<1+\frac{2s}{N+2-2s-\mu(\lambda)}
, we obtain

(141)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\,dxdt=0,

thus
v\equiv 0
. Since
w(x,t)=v(x,t)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}
, then
w\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
, a contradiction.

We deal now with the critical case
p=1+\frac{2s}{N+2-2s-\mu(\lambda)}
. Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1})
. Then

(142)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-1}_{R}(x,t)L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t))\,dxdt\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-1}_{R}(x,t)(1-\varphi_{R}(x,t))^{\delta}(1-\varphi_{R}(x,t))^{-\delta}L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t))\,dxdt\\
\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|x|^{\mu(\lambda)(p^{\prime}-1)}\varphi_{R}^{m-p^{\prime}}(x,t)(1-\varphi_{R}(x,t))^{\delta(1-p^{\prime})}(L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t)))^{p^{\prime}}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\varphi^{m}_{R}(x,t)(1-\varphi_{R}(x,t))^{\delta}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore, arguing as above, we can show that

(143)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|x|^{\mu(\lambda)(p^{\prime}-1)}\varphi_{R}^{m-p^{\prime}}(x,t)(1-\varphi_{R}(x,t))^{\delta(1-p^{\prime})}(L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t)))^{p^{\prime}}\,dxdt\\
\leq C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-p^{\prime}}(x,t)(1-\varphi(x,t))^{\delta(1-p^{\prime})}\,dxdt.

Since we have chosen
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1})
, we conclude that the integral in the right hand side of (
143
) is finite.
In conclusion from (
142
) and (
143
) we deduce that there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

(144)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-1}_{R}(x,t)L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t))\,dxdt\leq C_{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus B^{\prime}_{R}}v(x,t)^{p}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)(p+1)}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
R\geq 1
. Passing to the limit as
R\to\infty
we conclude that

(145)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v(x,t)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda)}\varphi^{m-1}_{R}(x,t)L^{s}(\varphi_{R}(x,t))\,dxdt=0.

Then from (
136
) and Fatou’s Lemma we deduce that
v\equiv 0
and so
w\equiv 0
.
∎

It is easy too see that, if
\lambda=0

6.4
, we can show that
there exists
T^{*}<\infty
such that

\lim\limits_{t\to T^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w^{p}(x,t)\,dx=\infty,

]
.

6.3.
Existence of supersolutions

We start by proving the following result.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
p\in\left(1+\frac{2s}{N-\mu(\lambda)+2-2s},1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}\right)
. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\delta>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and any
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\lambda+\delta]
the function

(146)

U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}(x,y,t):=\varepsilon(1+t)^{\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}-\frac{s}{p-1}}\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(t+1)}}

is a positive classical solution of the problem

(147)

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}(U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}})_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}})\geq 0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\times(0,+\infty),\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{\partial y}\geq\kappa_{s}(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}),&\text{ on }(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times(0,+\infty),\\
U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}(z,0)\geq 0,&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}

where
u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}(x,t):=U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}(x,0,t)=\varepsilon(1+t)^{\frac{s}{p-1}-\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(t+1)}}
and
k_{s}
is as in Theorem

4.1
. Furthermore for any
T>0
, we have that

U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}\in L^{2}((0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{1-2s}))
and

(U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}})_{t}\in L^{2}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+},y^{1-2s})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the family of functions

U(x,y,t):=\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\theta}\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(t+1)}},

with
\varepsilon>0
,
\theta>0
and
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\Lambda_{N,s})
.
With a direct computation we can see that

\displaystyle U_{t}(z,t)=\varepsilon\left[-\theta+\frac{1}{4}t^{-1}|z|^{2}\right](1+t)^{-\theta-1}\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(1+t)}},

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla U)(z,t)=\varepsilon y^{1-2s}\left[-\mu(\lambda_{1})-\frac{1}{2}(N+2-2s)+\frac{1}{4}t^{-1}|z|^{2}\right](1+t)^{-\theta-1}\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(1+t)}},

thanks to (
98
) and (
102
). Hence

(148)

y^{1-2s}U_{t}(z,t)-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla U)(z,t)\\
=\varepsilon y^{1-2s}\left[-\theta-\mu(\lambda_{1})+\frac{1}{2}(N+2-2s)\right](1+t)^{-\theta-1}\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}(z)e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(1+t)}}.

On the other hand by (
98
)

-\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}=\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\theta}\kappa_{s}\frac{\lambda_{1}}{|x|^{2s}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(1+t)}}.

Then
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{\partial y}\geq k_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}\right)
if and only if

(1+t)^{-\theta}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)|x|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})-2s}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(1+t)}}\geq\varepsilon^{p-1}(1+t)^{-\theta p}|x|^{-p\mu(\lambda_{1})}e^{-p\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(1+t)}}.

The change of variables
x:=(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi
yields

(1+t)^{-\theta-\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}-s}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)|\xi|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})-2s}\geq\varepsilon^{p-1}(1+t)^{-\theta p-\frac{p\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}}|\xi|^{-p\mu(\lambda_{1})}e^{-(p-1)\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}.

Choosing
\theta:=\frac{s}{p-1}-\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}
we obtain

(149)

(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)|\xi|^{p\mu(\lambda_{1})-\mu(\lambda_{1})-2s}\geq\varepsilon^{p-1}e^{-(p-1)\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}.

With this choice of
\theta
and a direct computation we can see that

(150)

-\theta-\mu(\lambda_{1})+\frac{1}{2}(N+2-2s)>0

if and only if

(151)

p>1+\frac{2s}{N+2-2s-\mu(\lambda_{1})}.

Hence, choosing
\lambda_{1}
close enough to
\lambda
,

(152)

y^{1-2s}U_{t}(z,t)-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla U)(z,t)\geq 0.

Finally, up to choosing
\lambda_{1}
closer to
\lambda
, we also have that

(153)

p<1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda_{1})}

and so (
149
) holds for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough.
In conclusion we have proved that
U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}
is a classical solution of problem (
147
). Since
\Phi_{\lambda_{1}}\in H^{1}(B^{+}_{1},y^{1-2s})
, the last claim is clear.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
p\in\left(1+\frac{2s}{N-\mu(\lambda)+2-2s},1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}\right)
and

\delta,\varepsilon_{0}
as in Proposition
6.6
.
Let
f
be a measurable non-negative, non-trivial function such that
f(x,t)=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
. Assume that for some
\delta_{1}\in(0,\delta)

(154)

f(x,t)\leq\delta_{1}(1+t)^{\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}-\frac{s}{p-1}}|x|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})-2s}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(1+t)}}.

Then for some a small enough
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\lambda+\delta)
close enough to
\lambda
, the function

(155)

w(x,t):=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2^{2s-1}\Gamma(s)^{2}(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{N}{2}+1-s}}\,\,\Big[\varepsilon\lambda(1+t-\tau)^{\frac{s}{p-1}-\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}}|x-z|^{-\mu(\lambda_{1})-2s}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-\tau+1)}}\\
+\varepsilon^{p}(1+t-\tau)^{\frac{ps}{p-1}-\frac{p\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}}|x-z|^{-p\mu(\lambda_{1})}e^{-p\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-\tau+1)}}+f(x-z,t-\tau)\Big]\,dz\,d\tau,

is a non-trivial, non-negative, very weak supersolution of problem
(
1
) and
w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.

Proof.

We start by observing that for any
p\in\left(1+\frac{2s}{N-\mu(\lambda)+2-2s},1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}\right)
and
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\lambda+\delta)
the function

(156)

\varphi(t,x)=(1+t)^{(p-1)\frac{\mu(\lambda_{1})}{2}-s}|x|^{-p\mu(\lambda_{1})+\mu(\lambda_{1})+2s}e^{-(p-1)\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(t+1)}}

is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)
.
It follows that, if
f
is as above, taking for example
\lambda_{1}:=\lambda+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that the function
U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}
is a classical supersolution of the problem

(157)

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}(U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}})_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}})\geq 0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\times(0,+\infty),\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{\partial y}\geq k_{s}(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f),&\text{ on }(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})\times(0,+\infty),\\
U_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}(z,0)\geq 0,&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}.\end{cases}

Let us extend trivially the function
u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and still denote, with a slight abuse of notation, the extended function with
u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}
. Then

\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,T))
for any
T>0
and
\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
.
It follows that, letting
k_{s}
be as in Theorem
4.1
,

(158)

w:=k_{s}J_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f\right)

is well-defined,
w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and
w\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(-\infty,0)
by (
25
).
Let
\eta_{n}
be as in Subsection
4.3
and let us define for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(159)

w_{n}:=k_{s}J_{s}\left(\eta_{n}\left[\frac{\lambda}{(|x|+\frac{1}{n})^{2s}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{f}{1+\frac{1}{n}f}\right]\right).

Then
w_{n}\in\mathop{\rm{Dom}}(H^{s})
thanks to Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
. Hence we may define its extension
W_{n}
as in Theorem
4.1
and notice
that
W_{n}
weekly solves the problem

\begin{cases}y^{1-2s}(W_{n})_{t}-\mathop{\rm{div}}(y^{1-2s}\nabla W_{n})=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N+1}\times(0,+\infty),\\
-\lim\limits_{y\to 0^{+}}y^{1-2s}\frac{\partial W_{n}}{\partial y}=\kappa_{s}\eta_{n}\left[\frac{\lambda}{(|x|+\frac{1}{n})^{2s}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{f}{1+\frac{1}{n}f}\right],&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\times(0,+\infty),\\
W_{n}(z,0)=0&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}.\end{cases}

By Corollary
4.3
, it follows that
w_{n}\leq w_{n+1}\leq u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

w=k_{s}J_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f\right)\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{s}J_{s}\left(\eta_{n}\left(\frac{\lambda}{(|x|+\frac{1}{n})^{2s}}\frac{u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}{1+\frac{1}{n}u^{p}_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{f}{1+\frac{1}{n}f}\right)\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}\leq u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}},

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. We conclude that

w=k_{s}J_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}+u_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{1}}^{p}+f\right)\geq J_{s}\left(\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}w+w^{p}+f\right).

Hence
w
is a very weak supersolution of (
1
), that is, is a supersolution in the sense of Definition
2.3
.
∎

From Theorem
6.7
and Proposition
4.8
we can immediately deduce the existence of very weak solutions of problem (
1
)
for small datum.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
p\in\left(1+\frac{2s}{N-\mu(\lambda)+2-2s},1+\frac{2s}{\mu(\lambda)}\right)
and suppose that
f
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
6.7
.
Then (
1
) has a non-negative, non-trivial very weak solution
w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.

7.
Open problems and subjects of further investigation

In this last section we make a brief overview over remaining questions and further developments concerning existence and non-existence results for the operator
H^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
and other
fractional parabolic operators.

As already stated in Remark
3.5
, it is an
interesting open problem to obtain an inversion formula for the
operator
H^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
. Furthermore in the case

p=p_{+}(\lambda,s)
it is still not known if there exists or not weak
supersolution of (
1
). It is reasonable to
think that a non-existence result may hold, coherently with the
classical case
s=1
, but to obtain such a result seems to be
technically demanding.

Further possible subject of investigation includes considering
different non-linearity in problem (
1
),
for example of the form
|\nabla u|^{p}
or

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{p}
, which, to the best of the authors
knowledge, have yet to be studied with or without the presence of
an Hardy-type potential. It may also be interesting to study
similar questions for more general parabolic fractional
operators, for example
(w_{t}-\Delta-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2}})^{s}
, under
assumption of positivity of the elliptic part
-\Delta-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2}}
. Finally the critical case

\lambda=\Lambda_{N_{s}}
is yet to be studied for the operator

H^{s}-\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{2s}}
and could be of interest.

All the subject mentioned above are object of current investigation by the authors.

Abstract
The entanglement of purification E P ​ ( A : B ) E_{P}(A\colon B) is a powerful correlation measure, but it is notoriously difficult to compute because it involves an optimization over all possible purifications. In this paper, we prove a new inequality: E P ​ ( A : B ) ≥ 1 2 ​ S R ( 2 ) ​ ( A : B ) E_{P}(A\colon B)\geq\frac{1}{2}S_{R}^{(2)}(A\colon B) , where S R ( n ) ​ ( A : B ) S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B) is the Renyi reflected entropy. Using this, we compute E P ​ ( A : B ) E_{P}(A\colon B) for a large class of random tensor networks at large bond dimension and show that it is equal to the entanglement wedge cross section E ​ W ​ ( A : B ) EW(A\colon B) , proving a previous conjecture motivated from AdS/CFT.



I
Introduction

Given a bipartite density matrix
\rho_{AB}
, the entanglement of purification
E_{P}(A\colon B)
is defined as
Terhal et al. 2002

E_{P}(A\colon B)=\min_{\ket{\psi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}}S(AA^{\prime}),

(1)

where
S(R)=-\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{R}\log\rho_{R}\right)
is the von Neumann entropy.
The minimization runs over all possible purifications of
\rho_{AB}
, i.e.,
\ket{\psi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
such that
\operatorname{tr}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\left(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}\right)=\rho_{AB}
, and the
\ket{\psi}
that achieves the minimum is called the optimal purification.

E_{P}(A\colon B)
is a useful measure of correlations in a bipartite mixed state and is proven to be monotonic under local operations
Terhal et al. 2002
.
However, it is generally intractable to compute because of the optimization over all possible purifications
1
1

1

Exceptions to this include pure states like Bell pairs and classically correlated states like GHZ states, see Ref.
Nguyen et al. 2018
for details.
.

In the context of AdS/CFT
2
2

2

See Ref.
Harlow 2018
for a review of the quantum information perspective on AdS/CFT.
, it has been conjectured that for
A,B
subregions of the CFT, there is a simple geometric, AdS dual to
E_{P}(A:B)
.
The entanglement wedge of subregion
AB
of the CFT is the bulk region between
AB
and the minimal surface
\gamma_{AB}
(also called the Ryu-Takayanagi (RT) surface
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
).
This is, in appropriate settings, the bulk region reconstructable from the corresponding boundary subregion
Dong et al. 2016
.
Based on this, Refs.
Takayanagi and Umemoto 2018
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
conjectured that
E_{P}(A\colon B)
is given by

E_{P}(A\colon B)=EW(A\colon B)=\frac{\text{Area}(\Gamma_{A:B})}{4G_{N}},

(2)

where
\Gamma_{A:B}
is the entanglement wedge cross section, the minimal surface dividing the entanglement wedge into portions containing
A
and
B
respectively, as depicted in Fig. (
1
).

G_{N}
is Newton’s constant and in this paper, we will set
\hbar=c=1
by choosing natural units.

Figure 1:
The entanglement wedge cross section
\Gamma_{A:B}
divides the entanglement wedge (blue) into two regions which contain
A
and
B
respectively. The RT surface is denoted
\gamma_{AB}
.

Proving this AdS/CFT conjecture appears quite challenging.
However, there exists a toy model of AdS, called random tensor networks (RTNs), which have proven useful in discovering new insights into AdS/CFT entanglement properties
Dong et al. 2021
;
Akers and Penington 2021
;
Akers et al. 2022a
;
Akers et al. 2023
;
Akers et al.
, especially because of their connection to fixed-area states
Akers and Rath 2019
;
Dong et al. 2019
;
Dong and Marolf 2020
;
Dong et al. 2022
.
The goal of this note is to present progress on proving the conjecture (
2
) in RTNs.

We compute
E_{P}
by using a known upper bound and deriving a new lower bound (Theorem
1
), which we are able to argue matches the upper bound in certain RTNs.
This argument relies on results obtained previously for the reflected entropy,
S_{R}(A\colon B)
, in RTNs
Akers et al. 2022a
;
Akers et al. 2023
;
Akers et al.
.
The reflected entropy is defined as
Dutta and Faulkner 2021

S_{R}(A\colon B)=S(AA^{*})_{\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}},

(3)

where the state
\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}
is the canonical purification, which lives in the Hilbert space
\mathrm{End}\left(\mathcal{H}_{AB}\right)
of operators acting on
\mathcal{H}_{AB}
.

\mathrm{End}\left(\mathcal{H}_{AB}\right)
is isomorphic to the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A^{*}B^{*}}
.

The bounds are as follows.
It is conjectured that the reflected entropy in AdS/CFT satisfies

S_{R}(A:B)=2EW(A:B),

(4)

and this has been proven rigorously for a large class of RTNs
Akers et al. 2022a
;
Akers et al. 2023
;
Akers et al.
, as we will discuss.
Moreover, as argued in
Takayanagi and Umemoto 2018
, RTNs in general satisfy

E_{P}(A:B)\leq EW(A:B).

(5)

This places the upper bound
E_{P}\leq S_{R}/2
.
The rest of this paper proves the lower bound and discusses when it matches this upper bound.

II
Reflected Entropy from Modular Operator

Definition 1
.

The Renyi reflected entropy is

\displaystyle S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B)=S_{n}(AA^{*})_{\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}},

(6)

where
S_{n}(R)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{R}^{n}\right)
is the
n
th Renyi entropy.

The lower bound in Theorem
1
will require the following lemma
that
rewrites the Renyi reflected entropy using the formalism of modular operators appearing in Tomita-Takesaki theory
3
3

3

See Ref.
Witten 2018
for a review.
.
Consider a finite dimensional system with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
, where subsystem
C
is completely general. Given a state
\ket{\psi}

4
4

4

\ket{\psi}
does not need to be cyclic and separating.
and subsystem
AB
, the modular operator is defined as

\Delta_{AB,\psi}=\rho_{AB}\otimes\rho_{C}^{-1},

(7)

where the inverse is defined to act only on the non-zero subspace of
\rho_{C}
and
\Delta_{AB,\psi}
is defined to annihilate the orthogonal subspace.

Lemma 1
.

For integer
n\geq 2
,

S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{1/2}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}~,

(8)

where
\Sigma_{A(A^{*})}
are twist operators that cyclically permute the
n
copies of
\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}
on subregion
A(A^{*})
,
\ket{\psi}
is an arbitrary purification of
\rho_{AB}
, and
\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}=\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

Start with Eq. (
6
) and rewrite it as
Dutta and Faulkner 2021

\displaystyle S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{*}}^{n}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{*}}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\bra{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Sigma_{A^{*}}\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}}.

(10)

As described in Ref.
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
, operators act on
\mathrm{End}\left(\mathcal{H}_{AB}\right)
by left and right actions, i.e.,

\displaystyle O_{AB}\ket{M_{AB}}

\displaystyle=\ket{O_{AB}\,M_{AB}}

(11)

\displaystyle O_{A^{*}B^{*}}\ket{M_{AB}}

\displaystyle=\ket{M_{AB}\,O_{AB}^{\dagger}},

(12)

and the inner product is defined by

\braket{M|N}=\operatorname{tr}\left(M^{\dagger}N\right).

(13)

Using this, one finds that Eq. (
10
) is given by

\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{*}}^{n}\right)=\operatorname{tr}_{(AB)^{\otimes n}}\left(\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{A}\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\right).

(14)

To express Eq. (
14
) in terms of modular operators, we consider an arbitrary purification of
\rho_{AB}
denoted
\ket{\psi}
, giving

\begin{split}\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{*}}^{n}\right)&=\operatorname{tr}_{(AB)^{\otimes n}}\left(\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{A}\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\right)\\
&=\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{1/2}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{-1/2}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}\\
&=\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{1/2}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}},\end{split}

where we have used the fact that the
\rho_{C}
dependence cancels out in the second line.
For the last line, we have used
\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{-1/2}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
which is easy to see by working in the Schmidt basis.
∎

III
Lower bound

Theorem 1
.

For integer
n\geq 2
,

E_{P}(A:B)\geq S^{(n)}_{R}(A:B)/2.

(15)

Remark 1
.

In Ref.
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
, it was proven that for integer
n\geq 2
, the Renyi reflected entropy is monotonic under partial trace, i.e.,
S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon BC)\geq S_{R}^{(n)},(A\colon B)
.
This immediately implies Theorem
1
by the following argument.
Let
\ket{\psi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
be the optimal purification.
Then

2S(AA^{\prime})\geq 2S_{n}(AA^{\prime})=S_{R}^{(n)}(AA^{\prime}\colon BB^{\prime})\geq S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B),

(16)

where we have used the fact that
S^{(n)}_{R}(C:D)=2S_{n}(C)
for a pure state on
CD
.
That said, we choose to present the proof below because it is self-contained and far simpler than the proof of monotonicity in Ref.
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We first define the Renyi generalization of
E_{P}(A\colon B)
as

\displaystyle E_{P}^{(n)}(A\colon B)=\min_{\ket{\psi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}}S_{n}(AA^{\prime}).

(17)

Applying the monotonicity of Renyi entropy, i.e.,
\partial_{n}S_{n}\leq 0
, for
n>1
we have

E_{P}(A\colon B)\geq E_{P}^{(n)}(A\colon B).

(18)

Now consider an arbitrary purification
\ket{\psi}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
. For integer
n\geq 2
, the Renyi entropy for subregion
AA^{\prime}
can be computed using twist operators in a fashion similar to Eqs. (
9
,
10
), i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{n}(AA^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{\prime}}^{n}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{\prime}}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Sigma_{A^{\prime}}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}.

(20)

Define the operators
\Pi_{AB,\psi}
(
\Pi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime},\psi}
) to be projectors onto the non-zero subspaces of the reduced density matrices on
AB
(
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}
). Then, using
\Pi_{AB,\psi}\ket{\psi}=\Pi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime},\psi}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
, we can insert
\Pi_{AB,\psi}
(
\Pi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime},\psi}
) from the right (left) in Eq. (
20
) for each of the
n
copies of
\ket{\psi}
. Note that
\Pi_{AB}\Pi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{1/4}\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{-1/4}
as the inverse density matrices in the modular operators annihilate the orthogonal subspaces. We can use this fact to insert a pair of modular operators into Eq. (
20
) to get

\begin{split}\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{\prime}}^{n}\right)&=\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\left(\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{1/4}\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{-1/4}\right)^{\otimes n}\Sigma_{A^{\prime}}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}\\
&\leq\left(\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{1/2}\Sigma_{A}^{\dagger}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}\right.\\
&~~~~\left.\bra{\psi^{\otimes n}}\Sigma_{A^{\prime}}\Delta_{AB^{\otimes n},\psi^{\otimes n}}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{A^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\ket{\psi^{\otimes n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(21)

where we have applied the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality between the modular operators.

Using
\Delta_{AB,\psi}^{-1}=\Delta_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime},\psi}
and Eq. (
II
), the two terms in the last line of Eq. (
21
) can be related to Renyi reflected entropies on
A:B
and
A^{\prime}:B^{\prime}
respectively. Thus, we have

2\frac{1}{1-n}\log\operatorname{tr}\left(\rho_{AA^{\prime}}^{n}\right)\geq S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B)+S_{R}^{(n)}(A^{\prime}\colon B^{\prime}).

(22)

Finally using the fact that
S_{R}^{(n)}(A^{\prime}\colon B^{\prime})\geq 0
, applying Eq. (
22
) to the optimal purification arising in the calculation of
E_{P}^{(n)}(A\colon B)
and using Eq. (
18
), we have our desired inequality.
∎

Remark 2
.

We will use the inequality at
n=2
since it is the strongest.

Remark 3
.

It is important to note that this inequality was derived using twist operators which only exist at integer
n
.
In the context of computing entanglement entropy, one usually analytically continues the answer obtained at integer
n
to non-integer values using Carlson’s theorem.
However, it is not necessarily possible to analytically continue an inequality.
For example, the monotonicity of Renyi reflected entropy under partial trace, i.e.,
S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon BC)\geq S_{R}^{(n)},(A\colon B)
was proved to be true at integer
n

Dutta and Faulkner 2021
, whereas counterexamples were found for non-integer
n
in Ref.
Hayden et al. 2023
.

IV
Random Tensor Networks

We can now use these bounds to compute
E_{P}
in many random tensor network states.
These states are defined as (up to normalization)
Hayden et al. 2016

\ket{\psi}=\left(\prod_{<xy>\in E}\bra{xy}\right)\left(\prod_{x\in V}\ket{V_{x}}\right),

(23)

where we are considering an arbitrary graph defined by vertices
V
and edges
E
.
The states
\ket{V_{x}}
are Haar random and the states
\ket{xy}
are maximally entangled.
This defines a state on the vertices living at the boundary of the graph.
We will consider RTNs in the simplifying limit where all bond dimensions
\chi_{xy}
are large such that
\log\chi_{xy}\propto\log D
and
D\to\infty

5
5

5

\log D\sim\frac{1}{4G_{N}}
in AdS/CFT in units where
l_{AdS}=1
.
.

The triway cut (yellow) minimizes the energy cost of the domain walls with tensions

and
t_{A:C}=t_{B:C}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}

\mathcal{A}(AB:C)

\{e,g_{A},g_{B},X\}

For RTN states, the Renyi reflected entropy is computed by finding the optimal configuration of permutations that minimizes a certain free energy (see Ref.
Akers et al.
for details).
It was proved in Ref.
Akers et al.
that the optimal configuration involves four permutation elements
\{e,g_{A},g_{B},X\}
and takes the general form shown in Fig. (
2
).
In detail, we have

\lim_{D\to\infty}\frac{S_{R}^{(n)}(A\colon B)}{\log D}=2\mathcal{A}_{n}(A\colon B\colon C)-\frac{n}{n-1}\mathcal{A}(AB\colon C),

(24)

where
\mathcal{A}_{n}(A\colon B\colon C)
is the triway cut with tensions
t_{A:B}=1
and
t_{A:C}=t_{B:C}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}
(see Fig. (
2

\mathcal{A}(AB\colon C)
is the minimal cut separating
AB
from
C
.

While the triway cut problem provides a natural analytic continuation in
n
and Refs.
Akers et al. 2022a
;
Akers et al. 2023
have provided evidence that this in fact is the correct prescription, it is not necessary to assume this for the purpose of this paper.
For now we note that at
n=2
, all the tensions are equal and normalized to 1.
On the other hand, in the limit
n\to 1
, the RHS of Eq. (
24
) approaches
2EW(A:B)
.

Now, the key point is that there exist networks where the triway cut configuration is identical for
n\to 1
and
n=2
.
This corresponds to networks where the
X
region in Fig. (
2
) vanishes at
n=2
.
We will demonstrate such examples in Sec. (
V
).
For now, assuming such a network and using Eq. (
15
), we have

E_{P}(A\colon B)\geq\frac{1}{2}S_{R}^{(2)}(A\colon B)=EW(A\colon B).

(25)

To prove the opposite inequality, we repeat the arguments made in Refs.
Takayanagi and Umemoto 2018
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
.
There is an approximate isometry relating the RTN state
\ket{\psi}_{ABC}
to the state
\ket{\psi}_{ABC^{\prime}}
defined on the same graph truncated to the entanglement wedge of
AB
, with
C^{\prime}=\gamma_{AB}
.
The RT formula can still be applied and optimizing over the choice of decomposition
C=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
, we have
S(AA^{\prime})=EW(A\colon B)
.
Since we have found one such purification, we have

E_{P}(A\colon B)\leq EW(A\colon B)

(26)

Note that each of the above inequalities is in the
D\to\infty
limit.
Combining these two inequalities, we have
E_{P}(A\colon B)=EW(A\colon B)
up to terms vanishing in the
D\to\infty
limit.
It is then also clear that the geometric purification in Refs.
Takayanagi and Umemoto 2018
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
is the optimal purification to leading order in
D
.

VI
Discussion

In this note we have proven
E_{P}=EW
for a large class of RTNs.
Our result relied on the inequality
E_{P}\geq\frac{1}{2}S_{R}^{(2)}
proven as Theorem
1
.

Proving the stronger inequality
E_{P}\geq\frac{1}{2}S_{R}
would prove
E_{P}=EW
more generally, but this cannot be achieved with our proof technique.
It would be interesting to check this numerically using the techniques of Ref.
Hauschild et al. 2018
.

Figure 5:
A correlation function computed by the geodesic of length

(solid blue) can be compared to the mirror correlation functions analogous to Eq. (

) computed by mirror geodesics (red and green) meeting the RT surface (dashed) orthogonally. Each of the mirror geodesics with length

An inequality of the form of Eq. (

(see Fig. (

)).
In AdS
3
/CFT
2
, twist operators are local and can be analytically continued to
n\approx 1
.
Applying the inequality, we would then find
S(AA^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}S_{R}(A:B)+\frac{1}{2}S_{R}(A^{\prime}:B^{\prime})
in any geometric purification.
It would be interesting if this argument can be generalized to non-geometric states, so that we can minimize the LHS and find the strengthened inequality.

Appendix A
Non-maximally Entangled RTNs

In a standard RTN, the edges are projected onto maximally entangled states.
These RTN states can be deformed to nearby states by simply changing the entanglement spectrum on the edges.
One may then ask whether we can prove
E_{P}=EW
for a larger class of states by considering such a deformation, and attempting to enlarge the parameter space where the inequality in Theorem
1
is saturated.
It turns out the answer is no, and we give an example in this section to highlight the basic issue.

Consider the 1TN model of Sec. (
V.1
) with a non-maximally entangled leg for subregion
C
.
This state, for a specific choice of spectrum, is identical to that of the PSSY model, an evaporating black hole in JT gravity coupled to end-of-the-world branes with flavour indices entangled with a radiation system
Penington et al. 2022
.
Here, we will not restrict to the PSSY spectrum, and find more generally how this deformation affects the phase diagram of reflected entropy.

For generality, consider the state
\ket{\rho_{AB}^{m/2}}
, a one parameter generalization of the canonical purification.
Ref.
Akers et al. 2022b
computed the entanglement spectrum of
\rho_{AA^{*}}
for this state.
It consists of two features: a single pole of weight
p_{d}(m)
and a mound of
\min(d_{A}^{2}-1,d_{B}^{2}-1)
eigenvalues with weight
p_{c}(m)
.
The weights are given by

\displaystyle p_{d}(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{AB}^{m/2})^{2}}{d_{A}d_{B}\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{AB}^{m})}

(27)

\displaystyle p_{c}(m)

\displaystyle=1-p_{d}(m).

(28)

Now, we would like to compare the phase diagram of this model with the standard 1TN with maximally entangled legs.
First note that the transition between
e
and
X
in Fig. (
3
) is dictated by the location of the entanglement wedge phase transition, which we hold fixed to compare the two models.
Then the remaining question is where the transition from
X
to
g_{A}/g_{B}
happens.

Consider the region of the phase diagram where
d_{A}>d_{B}
.
The transition happens in the connected sector.
Thus, we have
p_{c}(m)\approx 1
and the spectrum of
\rho_{AB}
is well approximated by the spectrum on the
C
leg.
Using this, we find that the location of the transition for
S_{R}^{(2)}
is given by

\displaystyle p_{d}(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{B}}.

(29)

Using Eq. (
27
), we then have

(2-m)S_{m/2}-(1-m)S_{m}=\log d_{A},

(30)

where
S_{n}
is the
n
th Renyi entropy of the non-maximal spectrum on the
C
leg.

Then it is clear that at
m=1
, the location of the phase transition is

x_{A}=\frac{S_{1/2}}{S_{1}}\geq 1
. The standard 1TN has a flat spectrum, i.e,
S_{n}=S_{1}
and the transition is at

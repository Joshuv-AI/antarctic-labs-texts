A characterization of some finite simple groups by their character codegrees
Hung P. Tong-Viet

Abstract
. For a finite group G G and an irreducible complex character χ \chi of G G , the codegree of χ \chi is defined by cod ( χ ) = | G : ker ( χ ) | / χ ( 1 ) \textrm{cod}(\chi)=|G:\textrm{ker}(\chi)|/\chi(1) , where ker ​ ( χ ) \textrm{ker}(\chi) is the kernel of χ \chi . In this paper, we show that if H H is a finite simple exceptional group of Lie type or a projective special linear group and G G is any finite group such that the character codegree sets of G G and H H coincide, then G G and H H are isomorphic.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and let
\chi
be an irreducible complex character of
G
. Let
\ker(\chi)
denote the kernel of
\chi
, that is, the set of all elements
g\in G
such that
\chi(g)=\chi(1)
. The positive integer
\chi(1)
is called the character degree of
\chi
.
This is the degree of a complex representation of
G
that affords the character
\chi
. In
[
8
]
, Chillag and Herzog call the number
|G|/\chi(1)
a ‘character degree quotient’, and obtain several interesting results concerning the structure of the finite groups
G
by putting certain arithmetic restriction on these character degree quotients. For example, they show that if
|G|/\chi(1)
is a prime power for some irreducible character
\chi
of
G
, then
G
is not a finite nonabelian simple group (see
[
8
, Theorem 1]
). In a subsequent paper
[
9
]
, Chillag, Mann and Manz called the quotient
|G|/\chi(1)
the ‘codegree of
\chi
’.
Among other results, they show that if
G
has an irreducible character
\chi
which satisfies the same condition as above for a prime
p
, then
\chi
is monomial and
F^{*}(G)=O_{p}(G)>1
, where
F^{*}(G)
is the generalized Fitting subgroup of
G
and
O_{p}(G)
is the largest normal
p
-subgroup of
G
. This result was later reproved by Riese and Schmid in
[
33
]
.

Qian, Wang and Wei
[
32
]
modified the definition of codegrees of characters by defining it to be
|G:\ker(\chi)|/\chi(1)
. Clearly, if
\chi
is faithful, then the two definitions coincide. However, the new definition of codegrees which we will use in this paper is more compatible with taking quotients in the sense that the codegree of a quotient of a finite group
G
is also the codegree of
G
. Now let
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
and
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)
be the set of all degrees and codegrees of
G
, respectively. It is well known that these two sets have strong influence on the group structure. In the late 1990s, Huppert
[
22
]
conjectured that every finite nonabelian simple group is essentially determined
by the set of its character degrees, that is, if
H
is a finite nonabelian simple group and
G
is a finite group such that
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)={\mathrm{cd}}(H)
, then
G\cong H\times A
for some abelian group
A
. This conjecture has been verified for all sporadic simple groups, the alternating groups of degree at least
5
and low degree finite nonabelian simple groups of Lie type (see
[
6
,
19
,
31
]
and the references therein). In
[
41
]
, we are able to verify Huppert’s conjecture for the remaining finite simple exceptional groups of Lie type.
Motivated by Huppert’s conjecture, Qian
[
26
, Problem 20.79]
proposed the following:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
H
be a finite nonabelian simple group and let
G
be a finite group such that
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H)
. Then
G\cong H.

This conjecture has been verified for all
26
sporadic simple groups, the alternating groups,
{}^{2}{\rm B}_{2}(q^{2}),

{}^{2}{\rm G}_{2}(q^{2}),

{}^{2}{\rm F}_{4}(q^{2}),

\PSL_{2}(q),\PSL_{3}(q)
,
\textrm{PSU}_{3}(q),\PSp_{4}(q)
(
q
even) and other small finite nonabelian simple groups in
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
28
,
42
,
44
]
.
In a recent paper, Hung and Moretó
[
20
]
show that the set of character codegrees together with their multiplicity determines the finite nonabelian simple groups up to isomorphism. This is analogous to a result stating that finite nonabelian quasisimple groups are uniquely determined by the structure of their complex group algebra or equivalently their character degrees together with their multiplicity obtained in
[
5
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
. The authors also show that if
S
and
H
are finite nonabelian simple groups such that
{\mathrm{cod}}(S)\subseteq{\mathrm{cod}}(H)
, then
S\cong H
. This important result allows us to complete the verification of Conjecture
1
for all finite simple exceptional groups of Lie type and
{\rm PSL}_{n}(q)
for
n\geq 4
and
q
a prime power.

Theorem 2
.

Let
H
be a finite nonabelian simple exceptional group of Lie type or
{\rm PSL}_{n}(q)
with
n\geq 4
and
q
a prime power. Let
G
be a finite group such that
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H).
Then
G\cong H.

We should mention that this is the first time a conjecture in this flavor has been confirmed for a family of finite simple groups of Lie type with unbounded Lie rank, defined over an arbitrary finite field.

We now describe our approach to the proof of Theorem
2
. Let
H
be one of the finite nonabelian simple groups in the statement of the theorem defined over a finite field of size
q=p^{f}
for some prime
p
. So
H
is a finite simple group of Lie type in characteristic
p
. Let
G
be a counterexample to Conjecture
1
with minimal order. By
[
20
, Theorem 8.3]
,
G
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
and
G/N\cong H
. By Lemma
2.1
,
N
is an elementary abelian
r
-group of order
r^{m}
for some prime
r
and
G
is perfect. Hence, either
N=Z(G)
or
C_{G}(N)=N
.
Since
G
is perfect, the former case implies that
G
is a finite quasisimple group with
G/Z(G)\cong H
and
|Z(G)|=r
. This case can be eliminated by finding certain small degree faithful irreducible character
\chi
of
G
such that
\chi(1)/r
is not a degree of
H
(see Lemma
2.2
).
In the latter case,
N
is a faithful irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{r}H
-module and
r^{2m}
divides
|H|
(see Lemma
2.3
). At this point, we can divide the proof into two cases according to whether
r
is the defining characteristic
p
or not. For the cross characteristic cases, we can use the well-known Landazuri-Seitz-Zalesskii’s bounds (see
[
25
, Table 5.3.A]
) to obtain a lower bound for
m
and check that
r^{2m}
is too large comparing to the order of the Sylow
r
-subgroup of
H
and we arrive at a contradiction. For the defining characteristic cases, we first get the lower bound
m\geq Df
, where
D
is the dimension of the natural module or the minimal module for
H
(see
[
16
]
). For finite simple exceptional groups of Lie type, this bound is large enough to guarantee that
r^{2m}\nmid|H|
and we are done. However for
{\rm PSL}_{n}(q)
this is not the case. To obtain a contradiction, we use some results on the representation theory of finite groups of Lie type in defining characteristic to reduce it to the cases where
N
is essentially the natural module for
\SL_{n}(q)
and then using the fact that the second cohomology group
H^{2}(\SL_{n}(q),N)
is trivial to deduce that
G\cong N:\SL_{n}(q)
is a split extension and produce a faithful irreducible character of
G
whose degree is not divisible by
p
, which leads to a contradiction.

Our approach could be used to verify Conjecture
1
for other small rank classical groups. However, we might need a different method to verify the last step for large rank cases.

.
We verify Conjecture

for the finite simple exceptional groups of Lie type in Section

and

in Section

. In the last section, we consider the minimal counterexamples to Conjecture

.

Notation.
Our notation is standard. We follow
[
25
]
for the notation of simple groups and
[
23
,
24
]
for the representation and character theories of finite groups. For a positive integer

, we write
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
for a cyclic group of order

For a prime
p
and a positive integer

, the
p
-part of

, denoted by

is the largest power of
p

and
n_{p^{\prime}}=n/n_{p}
is the
p^{\prime}
-part of

For a finite group

, let
M(G)
denote the Schur multiplier of
G
. We denote by
\Irr(G)
the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
. For
\chi\in\Irr(G)
, the codegree of

is defined by
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G:\ker(\chi)|/\chi(1)
. If

, we write

for the set of all irreducible characters of

As usual,

, where

. Finally, a finite group

2.
Preliminaries

We prove several lemmas that are needed for the proof of the main theorem. We first deduce some properties of a minimal counterexample to Conjecture
1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a finite nonabelian simple group and let
G
be a finite group with
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H)
. Suppose that
G
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
such that
G/N\cong H.
Then
G
is perfect and
N
is an elementary abelian
r
-group, for some prime
r
.

Proof.

We have
N\cong S^{k}
for some finite simple group
S
and integer
k\geq 1.
Assume that
S
is nonabelian.
It is well-known that
S
has a nontrivial irreducible character
\theta
that extends to
\Aut(S)
and thus the irreducible character
\theta^{k}\in\Irr(N)
extends to
\chi\in\Irr(G)
(see
[
38
]
). Now
\chi
is faithful by the uniqueness of
N
. Hence
|G|/\chi(1)=|H|/\varphi(1)
for some
\varphi\in\Irr(H)
.
It follows that
\varphi(1)=\theta(1)^{k}/|S|^{k}
. However, since
S
is a finite nonabelian simple group,
\theta(1)<|S|
and so
\varphi(1)=(\theta(1)/|S|)^{k}<1
, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
S
is abelian and hence
N
is an elementary abelian
r
-group for some prime
r
. The fact that
G
is perfect follows from
[
20
, Theorem 2.3]
.
∎

Assume the hypothesis and the conclusion of Lemma
2.1
. Since
N
is the unique minimal normal subgroup of
G
and is elementary abelian,
N\leq C_{G}(N)\unlhd G
. Since
G/N\cong H
is a finite nonabelian simple group, either
C_{G}(N)=G
and thus
G
is a finite quasisimple group with center
N
or
C_{G}(N)=N
. To handle the former case, we use the following observation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
be a finite nonabelian simple group and let
G
be a finite quasisimple group with
N=Z(G)
a cyclic group of prime order
r
such that
G/N\cong H
. Assume
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H)
. Then for every faithful character
\chi\in\Irr(G)
,
\chi(1)/r\in{\mathrm{cd}}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a faithful character of
G
. Since
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/\chi(1)\in{\mathrm{cod}}(H)
,
|G|/\chi(1)=|H|/\varphi(1)
for some
\varphi\in\Irr(H)
. Hence
\varphi(1)=\chi(1)|H|/|G|=\chi(1)/r\in{\mathrm{cd}}(H)
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Therefore, to show that
G
is not quasisimple, for each prime divisor
s
of
|M(H)|
, where
M(H)
denotes the Schur multiplier of
H
, and every cyclic central extension
L
of
H
with
|Z(L)|=s
, we need to find a faithful irreducible character
\chi
of
L
such that

\chi(1)/s\not\in{\mathrm{cd}}(H)
.
The next lemma is key to our approach to verify Conjecture
1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H
be a finite nonabelian simple group and let
G
be a finite group with
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H)
. Suppose that
G
has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
, where
N
is an elementary abelian
r
-group of order
r^{m}
for some prime
r
,
G/N\cong H
and
C_{G}(N)=N
. Then
N
is a nontrivial faithful irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{r}H
-module and the following hold.

(i)

r^{2m}
divides
|H|
.

(ii)

If
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is faithful, then
r^{m}\mid\chi(1)
and
\chi(1)/r^{m}\in{\mathrm{cd}}(H)
.

Proof.

Since
N
is a minimal normal elementary abelian
r
-subgroup of
G
and
C_{G}(N)=N
,
H\cong G/N=G/C_{G}(N)
is isomorphic to an irreducible subgroup of
\GL(N)\cong\GL_{m}(\mathbb{F}_{r})
. Therefore,
N
is a nontrivial faithful irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{r}H
-module.

We show that
r^{2m}\mid|H|
. We know that
G
acts on
\Irr(N)\cong N
by conjugation and let
\{\mathcal{O}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{t}
be
G
-orbits of this action with
\mathcal{O}_{0}=\{1_{N}\}.
Since
G
does not centralize any nontrivial element of
N
,
G
does not stabilize any nontrivial irreducible character of
N
and thus
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is the only
G
-orbit on
\Irr(N)
that has size
1
. For any
j\geq 1
, fix
\theta_{j}\in\mathcal{O}_{j}
and let
T_{j}=I_{G}(\theta_{j})
. Then
|\mathcal{O}_{j}|=|G:T_{j}|
. If
r\mid|\mathcal{O}_{j}|
for all
j
with
1\leq j\leq t
, then
r\mid|N|-1=r^{m}-1
, which is impossible. Therefore, there exists an index
j_{0}
with
1\leq j_{0}\leq t
such that
r\nmid|\mathcal{O}_{j_{0}}|
. Let
\theta=\theta_{j_{0}}
and
T=T_{j_{0}}
.

Write
\theta^{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{a}e_{i}\phi_{i}
, where
\Irr(T|\theta)=\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{a}\}
and
e_{i}=\phi_{i}(1)
for all
i\geq 1
. By Clifford’s correspondence theorem,
\theta^{G}=\sum_{i=1}^{a}e_{i}\chi_{i}
, where
\chi_{i}:=\phi_{i}^{G}\in\Irr(G|\theta)
. For each
1\leq k\leq a,

\chi_{k}
is faithful and thus
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi_{k})=|G|/\chi_{k}(1)=|H|/\varphi_{k}(1)
for some
\varphi_{k}\in\Irr(H).
It follows that
\chi_{k}(1)=r^{m}\varphi_{k}(1)
, whence
|G:T|\phi_{k}(1)=r^{m}\varphi_{k}(1)
. Since
r
does not divide
|\mathcal{O}_{j_{0}}|=|G:T|
,
r^{m}\mid\phi_{k}(1)
and hence
\phi_{k}(1)=r^{m}c_{k}
for some positive integer
c_{k}.
Now

|T:N|=\theta^{T}(1)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}e_{i}\phi_{i}(1)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\phi_{i}(1)^{2}=r^{2m}\sum_{i=1}^{a}c_{i}^{2}.

Therefore,
r^{2m}
divides
|H|
as wanted.

Finally, let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a faithful character of
G
. Then
{\mathrm{cod}}(\chi)=|G|/\chi(1)=|H|/\varphi(1)
for some
\varphi\in\Irr(H)
. It follows that
\chi(1)=|N|\varphi(1)=r^{m}\varphi(1)
and (ii) follows.
∎

Let
q
be a prime power and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. A prime divisor
r
of
q^{n}-1
is called a primitive prime divisor of
q^{n}-1
if
r
divides
q^{n}-1
but
r
does not divide

for any integer

with
1\leq i<n
. By Zsigmondy’s theorem
[

]
, such a prime

exists unless

or

and

is a Mersenne prime. For a prime

, we write

for the order of

modulo

. In this case,

is a primitive prime divisor of

for

and hence
j\mid(r-1)
; in particular,
r\geq j+1.

We also need the following number theoretic results that will be used to compute the orders of the Sylow subgroups of finite simple groups of Lie type.

\displaystyle(q^{d}-\epsilon)_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if $d$ is even and $\epsilon=-$}\\

\displaystyle(q^{d}+\epsilon)_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(q-\epsilon)_{r}(d)_{r}&\mbox{if $d$ is even and $\epsilon=-$}\\

Proof.

This is Lemma 2.1 in
[

The orders of the Sylow

If

Assume

r\geq n+1
. Now
|H|_{r}=(q^{n}-1)_{r}
. Clearly,
r\nmid(q-1)
so
r\mid(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. It follows that
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq|H|_{r}\leq(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. However,
2(q^{n}-2q+1)/(q-1)>(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
if and only if
q^{n}>4q-3
, which is true since
q\geq 2
and
n\geq 4.
Therefore,
2m\geq(q^{n}-2q+1)/(q-1)>(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

Assume
n/2<j\leq n-1.
Then
k=\lfloor n/j\rfloor=1
and
|H|_{r}=(q^{j}-1)_{r}.
With the same argument as above, we get
2m\geq 2(q^{n-1}-1)>q^{n-1}-1\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
, a contradiction.

Assume
1\leq j\leq n/2
. Then

\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq k\log_{r}(q^{j}-1)+\log_{r}((k!)_{r})\leq n/j(q^{j}-1)+n/j=nq^{j}/j\leq nq^{j}\leq nq^{n/2}.

Since
2m\geq 2(q^{n-1}-1)>q^{n-1}
, it suffices to show that
q^{n-1}>nq^{n/2}
or equivalently
q^{n-2}>n^{2}
for all integers
n\geq 4
and
q\geq 2
.
If
q\geq 5
, then
q^{n-2}\geq 5^{n-2}>n^{2}
by induction on
n\geq 4.
So, assume
2\leq q\leq 4.
We have
q^{n-2}>n^{2}
except for the following cases:
q=2
and
5\leq n\leq 8
;
q=3
and
n=4
or
q=4
and
n=4.
For these cases, direct calculation shows that
{2m}>\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
for every prime divisor
r\neq p
of
|H|
.

(2b) Case
r=p.
Recall that
H\cong\LL_{n}(q)
,
L\cong\SL_{n}(q)
and
L/Z(L)\cong H
, where
q=p^{f}
. Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbf{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Note that
N
is a faithful irreducible
FH
-module with
\dim_{F}(N)=m.

Let
K
be field extension of
F
and let
U
be an irreducible
KH
-module. Then
U
is also an irreducible
KL
-module, where the center of
L
acts trivially on
U.
Consequently, if
U
is an absolutely irreducible
KH
-module, then
U
is an absolutely irreducible
KL
-module.
Assume
K
is a finite field extension of
F
and let
\phi\in\Gal(K/F)
. For each
KH
-module
W
, one can define a
KH
-module
W^{\phi}
such that
W
is irreducible if and only if
W^{\phi}
is irreducible and
\dim_{K}(W)=\dim_{K}(W^{\phi})
(see
[
23
, VII.1.13]
or
[
25
, p. 48]
).

By the discussion above,
N
is an irreducible
FL
-module.
Since
L
is a finite Lie-type group of simply connected type defined over
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, by
[
25
, Table 5.4.C]
the smallest dimension of nontrivial irreducible representations of
L
over
\mathbf{k}
is
n
. Hence it follows from
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
that
|N|=p^{m}\geq q^{n}
(we can also get this bound using the existence of primitive prime divisors of
p^{nf}-1
in
H
, which always exists by Zsigmondy’s theorem except for the case
(n,q)=(6,2)
.) It follows that
m\geq nf.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have
2m\leq\log_{p}(|H|_{p})=fn(n-1)/2
. Hence
2nf\leq fn(n-1)/2
, which implies that
n\geq 5.

By
[
25
, Proposition 5.4.4]
,
E
is a splitting field for
L.

Let
\rho:L\rightarrow\GL_{m}(F)
be an
F
-representation of
L
afforded by
N
.
Let
K
be the centralizer in
\textrm{M}_{m}(F)
of
\rho(L)
. Let
\chi
be the character of an irreducible constituent of
\rho^{E}
(
\rho^{E}
is just
\rho
viewed as an
E
-representation of
L
.) By Problems
9.7
and
9.8
in
[
24
]
,
K=F(\chi)
is a finite field extension of
F
of degree
t
, where
t
divides
f
as
F\subseteq K\subseteq E
. Let
W
be a
KL
-module affording
\chi
.
By Theorems 9.21 and 9.14 in
[
24
]
(see also
[
25
, Lemma 2.10.2]
applies to
H\leq GL(N,F)
),
W
is an absolutely irreducible
KL
-module,
N\otimes_{F}K\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{t}W^{\psi^{i}}
and
\langle\psi\rangle=\Gal(K/F)
.
Observe that

m=\dim_{F}(N)=\dim_{K}(N\otimes_{F}K)=t\dim_{K}(W).

Hence

|W|=|K|^{\dim_{K}(W)}=|F|^{t\dim_{K}(W)}=|F|^{m}=|N|.

Next, let
p^{e}
be the smallest power of
p
such that
W
is absolutely irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}
.
Here
L
is simply connected of Lie type defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
and
W
is an absolutely irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}L
-module which is realized over no proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}
.
By
[
25
, Proposition 5.4.6(i)]
,
e
divides
f
and there exists an irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-module
M
such that

W\otimes\mathbf{k}\cong M\otimes M^{(e)}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{(f-e)}.

In particular,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}}(W)=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(W\otimes\mathbf{k})=\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)^{f/e}
. Hence

p^{m}=|N|=|W|=p^{e\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)^{f/e}}.

Thus
m=e\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)^{f/e}
. Let
s=f/e
.
Following
[
16
]
, we call the Frobenius twists of restricted modules of groups of Lie type ‘restricted’. Note that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)\geq n
.

Assume
s\geq 3
. Here
m\geq en^{s}.
We claim that
en^{s}\geq n^{2}f
. This is equivalent to
n^{s-2}\geq s
, where
n\geq 5
and
s\geq 3
. This can be proved easily by induction on
s\geq 3.
Hence
2fn^{2}\leq 2m\leq fn(n-1)/2
, which is equivalent to
4n\leq n-1
, which is impossible.

Assume
s=2
. Assume
M
is not restricted. Then
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)\geq n^{2}
and hence
m\geq 2n^{2}f
. In this case,
2m\geq 4n^{2}f>fn(n-1)/2
. Assume
M
is restricted. Then
W\otimes{\mathbf{k}}\cong M\otimes M^{(f/2)}
and the smallest field over which
W
can be realized is
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f/2}}.
Again, we get
m\geq en^{2}=n^{2}f/2
, whence
2m\geq n^{2}f>n(n-1)f/2=\log_{p}(|H|_{p})
, a contradiction.

Assume
s=1
. Here
e=f.
If
M
is a tensor product of at least two nontrivial restricted irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-modules, then
m\geq n^{2}f
and we get a contradiction as in the previous cases. Assume
M
is a restricted irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-module.
Then
m=f\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)
. Assume first that
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)>n
. By
[
29
]
or
[
25
, Table 5.4.A]
,
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(M)\geq n(n-1)/2
and thus
2m\geq fn(n-1)>fn(n-1)/2=\log_{p}(|H|_{p})
, a contradiction.
We are left with the case when
W\otimes\mathbf{k}\cong M
is a restricted
\mathbf{k}L
-module with
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}(W)=n
and hence
M
is quasi-equivalent to the natural module for
L
. It follows that
|N|=|W|=q^{n}
.

Recall that if
X
is a finite group and
A
is an
X
-module, let
H^{2}(X,A)
be the second cohomology group of
X
with coefficients in
A
. It is well-known that
H^{2}(X,A)
is nonzero if and only if there is a nonsplit extension of
A
by
X.

From
[
3
, Table I]
, we know that since
n\geq 5,

H^{2}(\SL_{n}(q),V)
is trivial, where
V
is the natural module for
\SL_{n}(q)
, except for the case
H\cong\SL_{5}(2)
.

Assume that
H\cong\SL_{5}(2)
and
N
is not quasi-equivalent to the natural module for
H
so that
G
is isomorphic to the nonsplit extension
2^{5}.\GL_{5}(2)
which is called the Dempwolff group and appears as a maximal subgroup of the Thompson sporadic simple group Th.
By GAP
[
12
]
, we can check that this group has a faithful irreducible character
\chi
of degree
248=2^{3}\cdot 31.
Clearly
\chi(1)/2^{5}
is not a character degree of
H
and thus this case cannot occur.
By using GAP
[
12
]
, we can also assume that
H\not\in\{\LL_{5}(2),\LL_{6}(2)\}
.

Assume
f=1
. Then
N
is quasi-equivalent to the natural module for
\SL_{n}(p)
and hence
\SL_{n}(p)
has exactly one nontrivial orbit on
N
and since
N\cong\Irr(N)
,
\SL_{n}(p)
has exactly one nontrivial orbit on
\Irr(N)
of size
p^{n}-1
. Since
H
is perfect and embeds into
\GL_{n}(p)
, we see that
H
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\SL_{n}(p)
. Since
\SL_{n}(p)
is quasisimple as
n\geq 5,
and contains a subgroup isomorphic to
\LL_{n}(p)
, it forces
H\cong\SL_{n}(p)
. In particular,
\gcd(n,p-1)=1
.

Therefore,
G=N:H_{0}
with
H_{0}\cong H\cong\SL_{n}(p)
. Furthermore, since
N\cong\Irr(N)
,
G
has exactly one nontrivial orbit
\mathcal{O}
on
\Irr(N)
of size
p^{n}-1.
Let
\theta\in\mathcal{O}
and
T=I_{G}(\theta)\cong N:T_{0},
where
T_{0}
is the stabilizer of a vector in the natural module
N
. By
[
25
, Proposition 4.1.17]
, we deduce that
T_{0}\cong C:A
, where
C
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of order
p^{n-1}
and
A\cong\SL_{n-1}(p)
. Since
\theta
is
T
-invariant and
T
splits over
N
, by
[
21
, Lemma 22.2]
,
\theta
extends to
\theta_{0}\in\Irr(T)
and so since
T_{0}/C\cong\SL_{n-1}(p)
,
T_{0}
has an irreducible character of degree
(p^{n-1}-p)/(p-1)
(the degree of a unipotent character of
\SL_{n-1}(p)
) and hence by Gallagher’s Theorem
[
24
, Corollary 6.17]
,
T
has an irreducible character
\phi
lying over
\theta
of degree
(p^{n-1}-p)/(p-1)
. It follows that
\chi=\phi^{G}\in\Irr(G|\theta)
is faithful and hence by Lemma
2.3
,
|N|=p^{n}
divides
\chi(1)=|G:T|\phi(1)=(p^{n}-1)(p^{n-1}-p)/(p-1)
, which is impossible as
n\geq 5.
(We can also take
\theta_{0}\in\Irr(I_{G}(\theta))
that is an extension of
\theta
to
I_{G}(\theta)
and thus
\theta_{0}^{G}\in\Irr(G|\theta)
with degree
p^{n}-1
and get a contradiction as well.)

Now assume that
f>1
. Note that
(nf,p)\neq(6,2)
since
n\geq 5
and
f\geq 2
. Thus a primitive prime divisor of
p^{nf}-1
exists and divides
|H|
. Since
H\cong\LL_{n}(p^{f})
is a subgroup of
\SL_{m}(p)=\SL_{nf}(p)
, by
[
4
, Section 3]
or
[
15
]
,
H
must be isomorphic to a
{\mathcal{C}}_{3}
-subgroup of
\SL_{m}(p)
and thus
H\cong\SL_{k}(p^{e})
with

. It follows that
k=n
and
e=f
is the only possibility and thus
H\cong\SL_{n}(q)
which also forces
\gcd(n,q-1)=1
. Furthermore,
H
acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of
N
as a natural module for
\SL_{m}(p)
. (See
[
27
, Appendix 1]
).
From the construction of
{\mathcal{C}}_{3}
-subgroups of
\SL_{m}(p)
,
N
can be considered an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
n
and it is in fact the natural module for
\SL_{n}(q)
. (We start with the natural module
N
for
\SL_{n}(q)
of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
and then consider
N
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module of dimension

.) We can then apply the same argument as in the case

to obtain a contradiction.
The proof is now complete.
∎

Theorem
2
now follows from Theorems
3.2
and
4.1
.

5.
Symplectic groups

Let
L=\Sp_{2n}(q)
, where
n\geq 2
and
q=p^{f}
. If
n=2
, we assume
q>2.
Then
H=L/Z(L)\cong\SSS_{2n}(q)
with
d=|Z(L)|=\gcd(2,q-1)
.
Let
D
be the dimension of the natural module for
L
, so
D=2n
, and it is also the smallest dimension of a nontrivial absolutely irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-module, where
\mathbf{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}.
We have

|\SSS_{2n}(q)|=\frac{1}{d}q^{n^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{2i}-1).

Moreover, for
(n,q)\neq(3,2)
, we have
|M(H)|=d
while
M(\SSS_{6}(2))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
(see
[
25
, Theorem 5.1.4]
).
In this section, we study the structure of minimal counterexamples to Conjecture
1
for the finite simple symplectic groups and as a consequence, confirming the conjecture for
n=2
and
n=3.

Unfortunately, we cannot complete the verification of the conjecture for these groups since the second cohomology group
H^{2}(\Sp_{2n}(q),V)
may not be trivial, where
V
is the natural module for
\Sp_{2n}(q)
. In particular, the extension of
H
by
N
is not a split extension and thus our approach does not work.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
H=\SSS_{2n}(q)
, where
n\geq 2,q=p^{f}
and
(n,q)\neq(2,2).
Let
G
be a counterexample to Conjecture
1
with minimal order. Let
L=\Sp_{2n}(q)
. Then
n\geq 4
and the following hold.

(i)

G
is perfect and has a unique minimal normal subgroup
N
which is an elementary abelian
p
-group.

(ii)

All the irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-submodules of
N\otimes\mathbf{k}
is quasi-equivalent to the natural module for
L
.

(iii)

|N|=q^{2n}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a counterexample to the theorem with minimal order. Then
{\mathrm{cod}}(G)={\mathrm{cod}}(H)
but
G\not\cong H
. By Lemma
2.1
,
G
is perfect and has a unique minimal normal elementary abelian
r
-subgroup
N
of order
r^{m}
with
G/N\cong H
. Now either
G
is quasisimple with center
N
of order
r
or
C_{G}(N)=N
.

(1) Assume that
G
is quasisimple with center
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}.

Assume first that
H=\SSS_{6}(2)
. Then
G\cong 2.\SSS_{6}(2)
and by using GAP
[
12
]
,
G
has a faithful irreducible character of degree
8
but
8/2=4
is not a character degree of
H
, so
G
cannot be
2.\SSS_{6}(2)
by Lemma
2.2
.
Now assume that
(n,q)\neq(3,2)
. Then
G\cong\Sp_{2n}(q)
,
r=2
and
q
is odd. By
[
37
, Lemma 2.6]
,
\Sp_{2n}(q)
always has a faithful irreducible character of degree
(q^{n}-\alpha)/2
, where
4\mid(q^{n}-\alpha)
and
\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}
. Now
(q^{n}-\alpha)/4
is not a degree of
H
since the minimal nontrivial degree of
H
is at least
(q^{n}-1)/2
by
[
25
, Table 5.3.A]
.

(2) Assume
C_{G}(N)=N
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
N
is an irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{r}H
-module and
r^{2m}
divides
|H|
. Hence
2m\leq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

(2a) Case
r\neq p
.

Assume that
H\cong\SSS_{6}(2)
. Then
m\geq 7
by
[
25
, Table 5.3.A]
. We have
|\SSS_{6}(2)|=2^{9}\cdot 3^{4}\cdot 5\cdot 7
. We can see that
r^{2m}
cannot divide
|H|
for any odd prime
r
. Therefore, we assume
H\not\cong\SSS_{6}(2).

By
[
25
, Table 5.3A]
),
m\geq(q^{n}-1)/2
if
q
is odd and
m\geq q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)(q-1)/2
if
n
is even. In both cases,
2m\geq q^{n}-1.

(i) Assume
r=2
. So
q
is odd and
|H|_{2}=(q^{2}-1)_{2}^{n}(n!)_{2}/2<(q^{2}-1)^{n}2^{n-1}
and so
\log_{2}(|H|_{2})<n-1+n(q^{2}-1)=nq^{2}-1
. If
n\geq 3
, then
2m\geq q^{n}-1\geq nq^{2}-1>\log_{2}(|H|_{2})
for any
q\geq 3
as
q^{n-2}\geq 3^{n-2}\geq n
. Assume
n=2.
Then
|H|_{2}\leq(q^{2}-1)_{2}^{2}
and so
\log_{2}(|H|_{2})<2\log_{2}(q^{2}-1)
. Thus we need to show that
2m\geq q^{2}-1>2\log_{2}(q^{2}-1)
which is always true since
q^{2}-1\geq 8.

(ii) Assume
r\geq 3
is odd. Then
r
divides
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{2i}-1)
.
Let
j=d_{q}(r)
be the order of
q
modulo
r
. Then
1\leq j\leq 2n
and
r\geq j+1
.

(ii)(1) Assume
j
is odd. By
[
34
, Table 1]
,
|H|_{r}=|\GL_{n}(q)|_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)_{r}^{k}r^{k/(r-1)}
and hence
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq k/(r-1)+k\log_{r}(q^{j}-1)
.

Assume
j=n
is odd. Then
r\geq n+1
and so
|H|_{r}=(q^{n}-1)_{r}
. Since
r\nmid(q-1)
, we have
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. If
q>2
, then
2m\geq q^{n}-1>(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
. If
q=2
, then
2m\geq 2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1)>2^{n}-1\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

Assume
n-1\geq j>n/2
. Then
k=\lfloor n/j\rfloor=1
and thus
|H|_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)_{r}
. Hence
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq q^{j}-1\leq q^{n-1}-1
. Now
2m\geq q^{n}-1>q^{n-1}-1\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

Assume
1\leq j\leq n/2
. Then
|H|_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)^{k}_{r}(k!)_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)_{r}^{k}r^{k/(r-1)}
and thus
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})<k(q^{j}-1)+k=kq^{j}\leq nq^{n/2}.

Assume that
q\geq 3
. Then
2m\geq q^{n}-1>q^{n}/2
. Observe that
q^{n}/2\geq nq^{n/2}
is equivalent to
q^{n}\geq 4n^{2}
which is always true for any
q\geq 3
and
n\geq 4.
Therefore,
2m>q^{n}/2\geq nq^{n/2}\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.
Assume
n=2
. Then
j=1
and
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})=2\log_{r}((q-1)_{r})\leq 2(q-1)
and so
2m\geq q^{2}-1>2(q-1)\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
. Similarly, if
n=3
, then
j=1
and
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq 1+3\log_{r}((q-1)_{r})\leq 1+3(q-1)<3q
and so
2m\geq q^{3}-1>3q\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

Assume
q=2
. Then
n\geq 4
and
2m\geq 2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1)/2=2^{n-2}(2^{n-1}-1)
. Now
2^{n-2}(2^{n-1}-1)>n2^{n/2}
if and only

2^{(n-4)/2}(2^{n-1}-1)>n
. Since
n\geq 4,

2^{n-1}>n
. Therefore,
2m\geq 2^{n-2}(2^{n-1}-1)>n2^{n/2}\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r}),
a contradiction.

(ii)(2) Assume
j
is even. So
(q^{j}-1)_{r}=(q^{j_{0}}+1)_{r}
. By
[
34
, Table 1]
,
|H|_{r}=|\GL_{2n}(q)|_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)_{r}^{k}(k!)_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)^{k}_{r}r^{k}
, where
k=\lfloor 2n/j\rfloor
and hence
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq k+k\log_{r}(q^{j}-1)_{r}
.

Assume
j=2n.
Then
r\mid(q^{n}+1)
and
r\geq 2n+1
so
|H|_{r}=(q^{2n}-1)_{r}=(q^{n}+1)_{r}
. Assume
q
is odd. Then
(q^{n}+1)_{r}=((q^{n}+1)/2)_{r}
since
r
is odd. Thus
2m\geq q^{n}-1>(q^{n}+1)/2\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
. Assume
q
is even. If
q=2
, then
2m\geq 2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1)/2=2^{n-2}(2^{n-1}-1)>(2^{n}+1)\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
since
n\geq 4.
Assume
q\geq 4
is even. Then
2m\geq q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)(q-1)/2>q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)>q^{n}+1\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

Assume
2n>j>n
. Then
k=1
. We have
|H|_{r}=(q^{j}-1)_{r}=(q^{j_{0}}+1)_{r}
and so
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})=\log_{r}(q^{j_{0}}+1)\leq q^{n-1}+1
.
Now
2m\geq q^{n}-1>q^{n-1}-1\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r}).

Assume
n\geq j\geq 2.
Then
k\leq n
and
|H|_{r}\leq(q^{j}-1)_{r}^{k}(k!)_{r}\leq(q^{j_{0}}+1)^{k}_{r}r^{k}
and hence
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq k+k(q^{j_{0}}+1)\leq k(q^{n/2}+2)\leq n(q^{n/2}+2)\leq 2nq^{n/2}
as
n\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
.

Assume first that
q
is even. So
2m\geq q^{n-1}(q^{n-1}-1)>q^{2n-2}/2
. Now observe that
q^{n}\geq 4n
for any
n\geq 4
and
q\geq 2
. Hence
q^{2n-2}/2=q^{n}q^{n-2}/2\geq q^{n}/2\geq 2nq^{n-2}\geq 2nq^{n/2}
and thus
2m>\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.
Assume that
n=2
or
3
. Then
q\geq 4
and clearly
q^{n}\geq 4^{n}\geq 4n
and again
2m>\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
as above.

Assume
q
is odd.
Assume
n=2
. Then
j=2
and
2m\geq q^{2}-1
. Now
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})=2\log_{r}((q^{2}-1)_{r})\leq 2(q+1)
. However, we see that
2m\geq q^{2}-1>2(q+1)\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
. Assume next that
n=3
. Again,
j=2
,
2m\geq q^{3}-1
and
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})=1+3\log_{r}((q^{2}-1)_{r})\leq 1+3(q+1)<q^{3}\leq 2m.
Assume that
n=4
. Then
j=2
or
4
and
2m\geq q^{4}-1
. If
j=2
, then
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq 1+4(q+1)<q^{4}-1\leq 2m.
If
j=4
, then
\log_{r}(|H|_{r})\leq 2(q^{2}+1)<q^{4}-1\leq 2m.

Assume
n\geq 5.
By induction on
n\geq 5
,
q^{n}>4n^{2}
for
q\geq 3
and thus
q^{n/2}>2n
. Finally
q^{n}-1>2nq^{n/2}
is equivalent to
q^{n/2}(q^{n/2}-2n)>1
which is true by the previous claim. Thus
2m\geq q^{n}-1>2nq^{n/2}\geq\log_{r}(|H|_{r})
.

(2b) Case
r=p
. Let
L\cong\Sp_{2n}(q)
with center
Z(L)
of order
\gcd(2,q-1)
and
L/Z(L)\cong H.
Again, let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\mathbf{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
q=p^{f}
. Recall that
N
is an irreducible
FH
-module and hence it is an irreducible
FL
-module.

By
[
25
, Table 5.4.C]
, the smallest dimension
D
of nontrivial irreducible representations of
L
over
\mathbf{k}
is
2n
. By
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
,
|N|\geq q^{D}
and hence
m\geq 2nf
. As
2m\leq\log_{p}(|H|_{p})
, we get
2m\leq n^{2}f

and thus
n^{2}f\geq 4nf
, so
n\geq 4.

Since
D=2n\geq 8
, we can apply
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
directly and hence cases (i), (ii) or
(v)
holds. If (v) holds, then
|N|\geq q^{2D^{2}}
and hence
m\geq 2fD^{2}\geq 8n^{2}f
. If
(ii)
holds, then
f
is even and
m\geq(f/2)(2n)^{2}=2n^{2}f
. Finally, if (i) holds and
N\otimes\mathbf{k}
is a tensor product of at least two restricted irreducible
\mathbf{k}L
-modules, then
m\geq 4n^{2}f
. For these cases, we see that
m\geq 2fn^{2}
and so
2m\geq 4n^{2}f>n^{2}f
and thus
p^{2m}
cannot divide
|H|
. Therefore, we are left with the case when
N\otimes\mathbf{k}
is a restricted
\mathbf{k}L
-module and
f=e
. Assume
n\geq 7
. By
[
29
]
or
[
25
, Table 5.4.A]
, the dimension of a restricted
\mathbf{k}L
-module is either
2n
or at least
2n^{2}-n-2
. If the latter case holds, then
2m\geq 2(2n^{2}-n-2)f\geq 2n^{2}f>n^{2}f
and thus
p^{2m}\nmid|H|
. Therefore,
m=2nf
and
N\otimes\mathbf{k}
is quasi-equivalent to the natural module for
L
. Hence
m=2nf
and
|N|=q^{2n}
.

Assume
4\leq n\leq 6.
If
q
is odd, then either
m=2nf
or
m\geq(2n^{2}-n-2)f
and as above, the former case holds.
Assume
q
is even. Then
L
has a spin module of dimension
2^{n}
(see
[
25
, Table 5.4.A]
). If
m\geq 2^{n}f
, then
2m\geq 2^{n+1}f
and we can check that
2^{n+1}>n^{2}
for these values of
n
and hence
2m>n^{2}f
. Thus
m=2nf
as wanted.
∎

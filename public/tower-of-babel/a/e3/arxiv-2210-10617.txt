A generalization of Ando’s dilation, and isometric dilations for a class of tuples of q q -commuting contractions
Sibaprasad Barik, Bappa Bisai

Abstract
. Given a bounded operator Q Q on a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} , a pair of bounded operators ( T 1 , T 2 ) (T_{1},T_{2}) on ℋ \mathcal{H} is said to be Q Q -commuting if one of the following holds: T 1 ​ T 2 = Q ​ T 2 ​ T 1 ​ or ​ T 1 ​ T 2 = T 2 ​ Q ​ T 1 ​ or ​ T 1 ​ T 2 = T 2 ​ T 1 ​ Q . T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}\text{ or }T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}QT_{1}\text{ or }T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}Q. We give an explicit construction of isometric dilations for pairs of Q Q -commuting contractions for unitary Q Q , which generalizes the isometric dilation of Ando [ 2 ] for pairs of commuting contractions. In particular, for Q = q ​ I ℋ Q=qI_{\mathcal{H}} , where q q is a complex number of modulus 1 1 , this gives, as a corollary, an explicit construction of isometric dilations for pairs of q q -commuting contractions which are well studied. There is an extended notion of q q -commutativity for general tuples of operators and it is known that isometric dilation does not hold, in general, for an n n -tuple of q q -commuting contractions, where n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . Generalizing the class of commuting contractions considered by Brehmer [ 8 ] , we construct a class of n n -tuples of q q -commuting contractions and find isometric dilations explicitly for the class.

1.
Introduction

One of the beautiful results in operator theory is the isometric dilation of a Hilbert space contraction (that is, an operator on a Hilbert space with norm not greater than 1) due to Sz.-Nagy
[
23
]
. Indeed, in 1953, Sz.-Nagy proved that for a contraction
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}

(\supset\mathcal{H})
and an isometry
V
on
\mathcal{K}
such that

T^{k}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V^{k}|_{\mathcal{{H}}}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
P_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the orthogonal projection in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
with range
\mathcal{H}
. A decade later, Ando
[
2
]
generalized Sz.-Nagy’s dilation result for pairs of commuting contractions, that is, he established that for a pair of commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there exists a pair of commuting isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathcal{K}
(
\mathcal{K}\supset\mathcal{H}
) such that

T_{1}^{k_{1}}T_{2}^{k_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V_{1}^{k_{1}}V_{2}^{k_{2}}|_{\mathcal{{H}}}

for all
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
. These dilation results of Sz.-Nagy and Ando are useful for analyzing the structures of bounded operators or pairs of bounded operators, respectively, on some Hilbert space. It is natural to ask whether we can extend Sz.-Nagy’s dilation to arbitrary
n
-tuple of commuting contractions for
n\geq 3
. Producing some counterexample of a triple of commuting contractions, Parrott
[
19
]
showed that, for
n\geq 3
, an
n
-tuple of commuting contractions does not have a dilation to an
n
-tuple of isometries, in general (also see
[
9
]
). There is growing literature on isometric dilation of different classes of
n
-tuples of commuting contractions considered by several authors. Some of the significant references are
[
8
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
.

Beyond the commutative setup, finding isometric dilations of non-commuting tuples of contractions is an active area of research. Isometric dilations of general non-commuting contractive tuples (that is, a non-commuting tuple
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\leq I
, also known as row contractions) have been studied in
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
. The present paper deals with the isometric dilations of a special type of non-commuting tuples, known as the tuple of
q
-commuting operators, and defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
q:\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}\to\mathbb{T}
is a function such that
q(i,i)=1
and
q(i,j)=\overline{q(j,i)}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
. An
n
-tuple of operators
(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
q
-
commuting
if
T_{i}T_{j}=q(i,j)T_{j}T_{i}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
. In particular, if
q(i,j)=-1
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
and
i\neq j
, we call the tuple as
anti-commuting
tuple.

These types of operator tuples have been seen to be useful in quantum theory and those have drawn the attention of many researchers in recent years. Trivially, every
n
-tuple of commuting operators are
q
-commuting, where
q(i,j)=1
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,n
. For non-trivial examples of
q
-commuting operators, an interested reader is referred to
[
16
]
. For dilations and models of
q
-commuting row contractions see
[
12
]
and
[
7
]
. For models of tuples of
q
-commuting isometries, one can see
[
3
]
.

In
[
22
]
, Sebestyén took a fascinating next step to extend the celebrated Ando’s result for pairs of anti-commuting contractions. Indeed, by a commutant lifting approach, he proved that for a tuple of anti-commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and a pair of anti-commuting isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathcal{K}
such that

T_{1}^{k_{1}}T_{2}^{k_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V_{1}^{k_{1}}V_{2}^{k_{2}}|_{\mathcal{{H}}}

for all
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
.
Recently, Keshari and Mallick
[
16
]
have generalized the result of Sebestyén by proving the existence of
q
-commuting isometric dilations for pairs of
q
-commuting contractions, and they have also done it through a commutant lifting approach. Mallick and Sumesh
[
17
]
improved Keshari and Mallick’s
q
-commuting dilation result for pairs of
Q
-commuting contractions, where
Q
is a unitary. Their definition of a pair of
Q
-commuting contractions is as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
Q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, a pair bounded operators
(T_{1},T_{2})
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
Q
-commuting if one of the following condition holds:

T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}\text{ or }T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}QT_{1}\text{ or }T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}Q.

Examples of non trivial
Q
-commuting operators can be found in
[
17
]
. In
[
17
]
, the authors have shown that if
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a pair contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with the
Q
-commuting relation
T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}
(resp.
T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}Q
), then there exist a pair of isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on some bigger Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
with
V_{1}V_{2}=\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}
(resp.
V_{1}V_{2}=V_{2}V_{1}\widetilde{Q}
) for some unitary
\widetilde{Q}
which extends
Q
and
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a dilation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
in the above sense. But, for the other case, that is, for
T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}QT_{1}
, the existence of
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting isometric dilation is so far unknown. Also, there is no explicit construction of isometric dilations for a pair of
Q
-commuting contractions in the literature, even if for its well studied particular case of
q
-commuting pairs. In this article, we prove by an explicit construction that a pair of
Q
-commuting contractions can always be dilated to a pair of
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting isometries and further into a pair of
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting unitaries (see Theorem
2.5
). Indeed, in Theorem
2.1
, for a pair of
Q
-commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we explicitly construct a pair of
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathcal{K}

(\supset\mathcal{H})
such that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a co-extension of
(T_{1},T_{2})
, that is,
V_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}=T_{i}^{*}
for
i=1,2
. From the
Q
-commutative case, as a corollary, we get an explicit construction of isometric dilations for
q
-commuting pairs.

It is well known that, for
n\geq 3
, an
n
-tuple of
q
-commuting contractions does not possess an isometric dilation, in general, and this is due to the counterexample of Parrott
[
19
]
for commutative case. In this paper, we consider a class of
n
-tuples of
q
-commuting contractions which generalizes the class considered by Brehmer
[
8
]
for the commutative setup and find their isometric dilations (see Theorem
3.9
). Moreover, we construct the dilation explicitly and see that the dilating tuples also lie in the same class. Here also, the dilations we find are co-extensions. In some particular case, our dilation result generalizes the isometric dilation result for a class of commuting tuples considered by Curto and Vasilescu
[
10
]
.

Rest of the paper has two sections. Next section concerns with explicit construction of isometric dilations for pairs of
Q
-commuting contractions and consequently for
q
-commuting pairs. In the last section, we deal with general
n
-tuples (
n\geq 3
) of
q
-commuting contractions and construct isometric dilations explicitly for our class.

2.
Dilation for pair of
Q
-commuting contractions

This section is devoted to the construction of an explicit isometric dilation for a pair of a
Q
-commuting contractions, where
Q
is a unitary operator. For this purpose we follow the line of construction of Ando
[
2
]
. Our main theorem of this section is as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of
Q
-commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{{H}}
, where
Q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a unitary. Then there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{{H}}
, isometries
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
and a unitary
\widetilde{Q}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting isometries and

T_{1}^{k_{1}}T_{2}^{k_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V_{1}^{k_{1}}V_{2}^{k_{2}}|_{\mathcal{H}}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}
is a reducing subspace for
\widetilde{Q}
and
\widetilde{Q}|_{\mathcal{H}}=Q
.

Proof.

We break the proof into three cases following the Definition
1.2
of
Q
-commutativity.

Case-I:
First assume that
T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}
. Let us consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}
. We embed
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{K}
via a map
h\mapsto(h,0,0,\dots)
. For a contraction
T
, let
D_{T}:=(I-T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Define the operators
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
on
\mathcal{K}
by

\displaystyle W_{1}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=(T_{1}h_{0},D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2},Q^{3}h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle\text{and}\,\,W_{2}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=(T_{2}h_{0},D_{T_{2}}h_{0},0,h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots).

The operators
W_{1},W_{2}
are isometries as
\|D_{T_{i}}h\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}-\|T_{i}h\|^{2}
, for all
i=1,2
. Set
\widetilde{Q}=\bigoplus\limits_{0}^{\infty}Q
. Clearly,
\widetilde{Q}
on
\mathcal{K}
is a unitary. In general,
W_{1}W_{2}\neq\widetilde{Q}W_{2}W_{1}
. We reshape
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
in such a way that they give rise to an isometric dilation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
and satisfy the
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting relation.

Let us consider the space
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
and identify
\mathcal{K}
with
\mathcal{H}\oplus\big(\bigoplus\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}\big)
by the natural identification

(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)=(h_{0},(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),\dots).

Define operators
R
and
P
on
\mathcal{G}
by

\displaystyle R(h_{i},h_{j},h_{k},h_{m})

\displaystyle=(Qh_{i},Qh_{j},Qh_{k},Qh_{m})

\displaystyle\text{and}\,\,P(h_{i},h_{j},h_{k},h_{m})

\displaystyle=(h_{i},Qh_{j},Q^{2}h_{k},Q^{3}h_{m}).

Needless to say,
R
and
P
are unitaries.
Let
G:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
be a unitary, resolved later. Define
\widetilde{G_{1}}
and
\widetilde{G_{2}}
on
\mathcal{K}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{1}}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(h_{0},G(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),GR^{-3}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),GR^{-6}(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots),

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{2}}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(h_{0},GP(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),GRP(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),GR^{2}P(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots).

Clearly
\widetilde{G_{1}},\widetilde{G_{2}}
are unitaries and

\displaystyle\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(h_{0},(GP)^{-1}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(GRP)^{-1}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),(GR^{2}P)^{-1}(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots\right).

Set
V_{1}=\widetilde{G_{1}}W_{1}
and
V_{2}=W_{2}\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}
. Clearly
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are isometries. Our aim is to find the unitary
G
on
\mathcal{G}
such that
V_{1}V_{2}=\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}
. To this end, first we write down
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
separately.

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G_{1}}W_{1}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G_{1}}(T_{1}h_{0},D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2},Q^{3}h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=\big(T_{1}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),GR^{-3}R^{3}P(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378ptGR^{-6}R^{7}P(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),GR^{-9}R^{11}P(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=\big(T_{1}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),GP(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378ptGRP(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),GR^{2}P(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big).

Also,

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=W_{2}\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=W_{2}\big(h_{0},(GP)^{-1}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(GRP)^{-1}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),(GR^{2}P)^{-1}(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=\big(T_{2}h_{0},D_{T_{2}}h_{0},0,(GP)^{-1}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt(GRP)^{-1}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),(GR^{2}P)^{-1}(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots\big).

Using these expressions of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, we calculate
V_{1}V_{2}
and
\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}
.

\displaystyle V_{1}V_{2}

\displaystyle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{1}\big(T_{2}h_{0},D_{T_{2}}h_{0},0,(GP)^{-1}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(GRP)^{-1}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(GR^{2}P)^{-1}(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(T_{1}T_{2}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0),(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),(h_{9},h_{10},h_{11},h_{12}),\dots\big).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}

\displaystyle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}W_{2}\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}\big(T_{1}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),GP(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),GRP(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad GR^{2}P(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}W_{2}\big(T_{1}h_{0},(GP)^{-1}G(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),(GRP)^{-1}(GP)(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad(GR^{2}P)^{-1}(GRP)(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),(GR^{3}P)^{-1}(GR^{2}P)(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}W_{2}\big(T_{1}h_{0},(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Q^{-1}h_{1},Q^{-1}h_{2}),R^{-1}(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),R^{-1}(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 312.9803pt[\text{since }RP=PR]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}\big(T_{2}T_{1}h_{0},(D_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0},0,D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0),Q^{-1}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),Q^{-1}(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(QT_{2}T_{1}h_{0},(QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0),(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),\dots\big).

Since
T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}
, therefore,
V_{1}V_{2}=\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}
if and only if

(2.1)

G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0)=(QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0)\text{ for all }h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.

Suppose

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\overline{\left\{(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0):h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}}\quad\text{and}\,\,\mathcal{L}_{2}=\overline{\left\{(QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0):h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}}.

Since

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}D^{2}_{T_{1}}T_{2}+D_{T_{2}}Q^{*}QD_{T_{2}}=

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}D^{2}_{T_{1}}T_{2}+D^{2}_{T_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})T_{2}+(I-T_{2}^{*}T_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}(I-T_{2}^{*}Q^{*}QT_{2})T_{1}+(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})\quad\quad[\text{using}\,\,T_{1}T_{2}=QT_{2}T_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}(I-T_{2}^{*}T_{2})T_{1}+(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}D^{2}_{T_{2}}T_{1}+D^{2}_{T_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}D_{T_{2}}Q^{*}QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}+D_{T_{1}}Q^{*}QD_{T_{1}},

we have

\|D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0}\|^{2}+\|QD_{T_{2}}h_{0}\|^{2}=\|QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0}\|^{2}+\|QD_{T_{1}}h_{0}\|^{2}

for all

.

G:\mathcal{L}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}

defined by

G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0)=(QD_{T_{2}}T_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0)

is an isometry. It is easy to check that
\text{dim }\mathcal{G}\ominus\mathcal{L}_{1}=\text{dim }\mathcal{G}\ominus\mathcal{L}_{2}
. Therefore,
G
extends to a unitary, denoted again by
G
, on
\mathcal{G}
such that (
2.1
) holds. Thus
V_{1}V_{2}=\widetilde{Q}V_{2}V_{1}
. It is clear from the above expression of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
that

V_{i}^{k_{i}}(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\dots)=(T_{i}^{k_{i}}h_{0},\dots)

for all
i=1,2
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Hence

T_{1}^{k_{1}}T_{2}^{k_{2}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}V_{1}^{k_{1}}V_{2}^{k_{2}}|_{\mathcal{H}}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Case-II:
Suppose
T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}QT_{1}
. For this case, consider

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\overline{\left\{(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0):h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}}\quad\text{and}\,\,\mathcal{L}_{2}=\overline{\left\{(D_{T_{2}}QT_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0):h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}\right\}}.

Since

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}D^{2}_{T_{1}}T_{2}+D_{T_{2}}Q^{*}QD_{T_{2}}=

\displaystyle T_{2}^{*}(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})T_{2}+(I-T_{2}^{*}T_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I-T_{2}^{*}T_{1}^{*}T_{1}T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}Q^{*}(I-T_{2}^{*}T_{2})QT_{1}+(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}Q^{*}D^{2}_{T_{2}}QT_{1}+D_{T_{1}}Q^{*}QD_{T_{1}},

we have

\|D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0}\|^{2}+\|QD_{T_{2}}h_{0}\|^{2}=\|D_{T_{2}}QT_{1}h_{0}\|^{2}+\|QD_{T_{1}}h_{0}\|^{2}

for all
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
. So, the operator

G:\mathcal{L}_{1}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}

defined by

G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0)=(D_{T_{2}}QT_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0)

is an isometry. Therefore,
G
extends to a unitary, denoted again by
G
, on
\mathcal{G}
such that
G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0)=(D_{T_{2}}QT_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0)
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{G}
is defined as in Case-I. Also let
W_{1},W_{2},R,P,\widetilde{G}_{1},\widetilde{G}_{2},V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be all as in Case-I. So,
V_{1}V_{2}
is as in the previous case. Set
\widetilde{Q}=Q\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{0}^{\infty}GRG^{-1}
on
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{{H}}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}
. We calculate

\displaystyle V_{2}\widetilde{Q}V_{1}

\displaystyle(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\dots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{2}\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}\widetilde{Q}\big(T_{1}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),GP(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),GRP(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad GR^{2}P(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{2}\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}\big(QT_{1}h_{0},GR(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),GRP(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),GR^{2}P(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad GR^{3}P(h_{11},h_{12},h_{13},h_{14}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{2}\big(QT_{1}h_{0},P^{-1}R(D_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,Qh_{1},Q^{2}h_{2}),(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{2}\big(QT_{1}h_{0},(QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0,h_{1},h_{2}),(h_{3},h_{4},h_{5},h_{6}),(h_{7},h_{8},h_{9},h_{10}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(T_{2}QT_{1}h_{0},(D_{T_{2}}QT_{1}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{1}}h_{0},0),(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),\dots\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(T_{1}T_{2}h_{0},G(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h_{0},0,QD_{T_{2}}h_{0},0),(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}),(h_{5},h_{6},h_{7},h_{8}),\dots\big)

This shows that
V_{1}V_{2}=V_{2}\widetilde{Q}V_{1}
. Also, from the previous case it follows that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is an isometric dilation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

Case-III:
Suppose,
T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}Q
. In this case, we take
V_{1},V_{2}
and
\widetilde{Q}
same as in Case-I and get the required result.
∎

-commuting contractions where

.

.

be a pair of contractions on a Hilbert space such that

, where

is a unimodular complex number. Then there exist a Hilbert space

\mathcal{{H}}

for all

.

Proof.

If we consider

and

.

Remark 2.3
.

In the particular case when
q=1
, we recover the Ando’s dilation theorem where the explicit construction matches with the original construction of Ando.

The following proposition shows that every pair of
Q
-commuting isometries admits a
\widetilde{Q}
-commuting unitary extension. We write the proposition in two separate parts. Part (i) has been proved in
[
17
]
(see Theorem 2.18 and Corollary 2.19 in
[
17
]
) and we include it in the statement for the sake of completeness. We only prove part (ii) of the proposition and for this we follow the same technique as in the proof of Theorem 2.18 in
[
17
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a pair of isometries on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
Q\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a unitary.

(i)

If
V_{1}V_{2}=QV_{2}V_{1}
(resp.
V_{1}V_{2}=V_{2}V_{1}Q
), then there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}

(\supset\mathcal{H})
, a unitary
\widetilde{Q}
with
\widetilde{Q}|_{\mathcal{H}}=Q
and a pair of unitaries
(U_{1},U_{2})
on
\mathcal{K}
such that
U_{1}U_{2}=\widetilde{Q}U_{2}U_{1}
(resp.
U_{1}U_{2}=U_{2}U_{1}\widetilde{Q}
) and
U_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}}=V_{i}
for all
i=1,2
.

(ii)

If
V_{1}V_{2}=V_{2}QV_{1}
, then there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}

\supset\mathcal{H}
, a unitary
\widetilde{Q}
with
\widetilde{Q}|_{\mathcal{H}}=Q
and a pair of unitaries
(U_{1},U_{2})
on
\mathcal{K}
such that
U_{1}U_{2}=U_{2}\widetilde{Q}U_{1}
and
U_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}}=V_{i}
for all
i=1,2
.

In fact, we can choose
\widetilde{Q}=Q\oplus I|_{\mathcal{K}\ominus\mathcal{H}}
in all cases.

Proof.

We prove part (ii) only. Let
V_{2}
extends to some unitary
\widetilde{V_{2}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
such that the extension is minimal, that is,
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=\overline{\text{span}}\{\widetilde{V_{2}}^{n}\mathcal{H}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Set
\widehat{Q}=(Q\oplus I_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\ominus\mathcal{H}})\in\mathcal{B}(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}})
. Define
\widetilde{V_{1}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

\widetilde{V_{1}}(\widetilde{V_{2}}^{n}h)=(\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q})^{n}V_{1}h\quad(h\in\mathcal{H},n\in\mathbb{Z}).

Now, for all
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
and
n\geq m
,

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{V_{1}}(\widetilde{V_{2}}^{n}h),\widetilde{V_{1}}(\widetilde{V_{2}}^{m}h^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q})^{n}V_{1}h,(\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q})^{m}V_{1}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q})^{n-m}V_{1}h,V_{1}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(V_{2}Q)^{n-m}V_{1}h,V_{1}h^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 56.9055pt\big[\text{since}\,(V_{2}Q)^{n}=(\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q})^{n}|_{\mathcal{H}}\big]

\displaystyle=\langle V_{1}V_{2}^{n-m}h,V_{1}h^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 102.43008pt[\text{using }V_{1}V_{2}=V_{2}QV_{1}]

\displaystyle=\langle V_{2}^{n-m}h,h^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 110.96556pt[\text{since $V_{1}$ is an isometry}]

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{V_{2}}^{n-m}h,h^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 147.95424pt[\text{since }\widetilde{V_{2}}|_{\mathcal{H}}=V_{2}]

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{V_{2}}^{n}h,\widetilde{V_{2}}^{m}h^{\prime}\rangle.

Therefore,
\widetilde{V_{1}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
is an isometric extension of
V_{1}
on
\mathcal{H}
. It is clear that
\widetilde{V_{1}}\widetilde{V_{2}}=\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q}\widetilde{V_{1}}
. Suppose,
U_{1}
on
\mathcal{K}
is the minimal unitary extension of
\widetilde{V_{1}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. Then,

\mathcal{K}=\overline{\text{span}}\big\{U_{1}^{n}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}=\overline{\text{span}}\big\{U_{1}^{*n}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\big\},

as
\widetilde{H}
is an invariant subspace for
U_{1}
. Define
U_{2}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
, through its adjoint, by

U_{2}^{*}(U_{1}^{*n}k)=(\widetilde{Q}U_{1})^{*n}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k\quad(k\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}),

where
\widetilde{Q}=(Q\oplus I_{\mathcal{K}\ominus\mathcal{H}})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
. Then, for
k,k^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
and
n\geq m
,

\displaystyle\langle U_{2}^{*}(U_{1}^{*n}k),U_{2}^{*}(U_{1}^{*m}k^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\widetilde{Q}U_{1})^{*n}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k,(\widetilde{Q}U_{1})^{*m}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\widetilde{Q}U_{1})^{*(n-m)}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k,\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\widehat{Q}\widetilde{V_{1}})^{*(n-m)}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k,\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k^{\prime}\rangle\quad\big[\text{since }(\widehat{Q}\widetilde{V_{1}})^{*l}=P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}(\widetilde{Q}U_{1})^{*l}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}\big]

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}\widetilde{V_{1}}^{*(n-m)}k,\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}k^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 73.97733pt\big[\text{using }\widetilde{V_{1}}\widetilde{V_{2}}=\widetilde{V_{2}}\widehat{Q}\widetilde{V_{1}}\big]

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{V_{1}}^{*(n-m)}k,k^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle U_{1}^{*(n-m)}k,k^{\prime}\rangle\hskip 110.96556pt\big[\text{since }\widetilde{V_{1}}^{*}=P_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}U_{1}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}\big]

\displaystyle=\langle U_{1}^{*n}k,U_{1}^{*m}k^{\prime}\rangle.

Therefore,
U_{2}^{*}
on
\mathcal{K}
is an isometric extension of
\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. Now we shall prove that
U_{2}^{*}
on
\mathcal{K}
is unitary. Since
U_{2}^{*}
is isometry, it suffices to show that
U_{2}^{*}
is onto. For
n>0
, set

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\overline{\text{span}}\left\{U_{1}^{*j}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}:0\leq j\leq n\right\}.

We prove by induction that
U_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{K}_{n})=\mathcal{K}_{n}
, for all
n>0
. Now for
h\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
,
U_{1}^{*}\widetilde{V_{2}}\widetilde{Q}h\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
and

U_{2}^{*}\left(U_{1}^{*}\widetilde{V_{2}}\widetilde{Q}h\right)=\left(\widetilde{Q}U_{1}\right)^{*}\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}\widetilde{V_{2}}\widetilde{Q}h=U_{1}^{*}\widetilde{Q}^{*}\widetilde{Q}h=U_{1}^{*}h.

This implies that
U_{2}^{*}\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}\right)=\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Now assume that
U_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{K}_{n})=\mathcal{K}_{n}
. To prove
U_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{K}_{n+1})=\mathcal{K}_{n+1}
, it is enough to show that for
h\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
,
U_{1}^{*(n+1)}h
has a pre-image under the operator
U_{2}^{*}
. As
U_{1}^{*n}h\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
such that
U_{2}^{*}(y)=U_{1}^{*n}h
. Again, since
\mathcal{K}_{n}
is a reducing subspace for
\widetilde{Q}
, there exists
z\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
such that
U_{2}^{*}(z)=\widetilde{Q}U_{2}^{*}(y)
. Now it is clear that
U_{1}^{*}(z)\in\mathcal{K}_{n+1}
and

U_{2}^{*}\left(U_{1}^{*}z\right)=U_{1}^{*}\widetilde{Q}^{*}U_{2}^{*}(z)=U_{1}^{*}U_{2}^{*}(y)=U_{1}^{*(n+1)}(h).

Therefore,
U_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{K}_{n+1})=\mathcal{K}_{n+1}
and hence
U_{2}
is unitary. Using the facts that
U_{2}^{*}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}=\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}
and
\widetilde{V_{2}}^{*}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
is a unitary one can easily prove that the block matrix form of
U_{2}^{*}
, with respect to the decomposition
\mathcal{K}=\widetilde{H}\oplus(\mathcal{K}\ominus\widetilde{\mathcal{H}})
, will be a diagonal block matrix. Thus
U_{2}
is an extension of
\widetilde{V_{2}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

be a pair of

, where

3.
Dilation for a class of
q
-commuting tuples

In this section, we shall work with the tuples of
q
-commuting contractions. For a given
q:\{1,\dots,n\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}\to\mathbb{T}
as is Definition
1.1
, first we denote
\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
as the set of all
n
-tuple of
q
-commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, that is,

\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H}):=\{T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})^{n}:\|T_{i}\|\leq 1\text{ and }T_{i}T_{j}=q(i,j)T_{j}T_{i}\,\,\,\forall\,i,j=1\ldots,n\}.

We shall find isometric dilation for a class of tuples lies in
\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
, which generalizes the dilation result for the class of Curto and Vasilescu
[
10
]
and Brehmer
[
8
]
, obtained for the commuting case. We define our class later in this section. In Theorem
3.9
below, we describe dilation for that class. Very soon we shall define some operators, namely
q
-commuting rotational shifts, on the Hardy space over the unit polydisc, which are crucial in describing dilations for our class. We start with recalling the well-known Hardy space and shift operators on it.

Hardy space and shift operators:
The Hardy space over the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with respect to the kernel function
\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})
, where
\mathbb{S}_{n}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is the Szegö kernel over the unit polydisc, defined by

\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-z_{i}\bar{w_{i}}}\quad(\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),\,\bm{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

In the vector valued case, for a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space over the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, denoted by
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with respect to the kernel function
\mathbb{S}_{n}(\bm{z},\bm{w})\cdot I_{\mathcal{E}}
. One can also concretely describe the space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as follows:

H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\Big\{\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:a_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{E}\,\,\text{and}\,\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\|a_{\bm{k}}\|^{2}<\infty\Big\}.

Note that, the space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
can be viewed as
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{E}
. Shift operators are well-known isometries on the Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. With respect to each co-ordinate function
z_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, the
i
-th shift operator
M_{z_{i}}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is defined by

M_{z_{i}}f(\bm{w})=w_{i}f(\bm{w})\quad(\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{n},f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})).

It is easy to check that,
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
is an
n
-tuple of doubly commuting isometries.

Szegö positivity:
Looking at the Szegö kernel over
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, one can check inverse of it is a polynomial, more precisely

(3.1)

\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(\bm{z},\bm{w})=(1-z_{1}\bar{w}_{1})\cdots(1-z_{n}\bar{w}_{n})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\,0\leq\bm{k}\leq\bm{e}}(-1)^{|\bm{k}|}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\bar{\bm{w}}^{\bm{k}},

where,
\bm{e}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
,
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}
and
\bar{\bm{w}}^{\bm{k}}=\bar{w}_{n}^{k_{n}}\cdots\bar{w}_{1}^{k_{1}}
.
For
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
, connecting with (
3.1
), we define

\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(T,T^{*}):=\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}

where, for
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
and for an ordered subset
F=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
T_{F}
and
T_{F}^{*}
are the notations for

T_{F}:=T_{k_{1}}\cdots T_{k_{r}}\text{ and }T_{F}^{*}:=T_{k_{r}}^{*}\cdots T_{k_{1}}^{*}.

Note that, in the notations
T_{F}
and
T_{F}^{*}
, maintaining the order of appearance of the operators, involve in the composition, is important and we fix these notations for the rest of the article. We say that
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
satisfies
Szegö positivity
if

\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(T,T^{*})\geq 0.

It follows from the doubly commutativity of the tuple
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
that it satisfies the Szegö positivity. Next proposition gives us an example of a large class of operators tuples which satisfies Szegö positivity. Before going into the proposition we make a definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
q
be as in Definition
1.1
. An
n
-tuple of operators
(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
doubly
q
-commuting
if
T_{i}T_{j}=q(i,j)T_{j}T_{i}
and
T_{i}T_{j}^{*}=\overline{q(i,j)}T_{j}^{*}T_{i}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
.

With the above definition we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
be doubly
q
-commuting. Then,
T
satisfies Szegö positivity.

Proof.

Let
\emptyset\neq F=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
be an ordered subset. Then,

\displaystyle T_{F}T_{F}^{*}=

\displaystyle T_{n_{1}}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}^{*}T_{n_{1}}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{m\in F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}\overline{q(n_{1},m)}\Big)T_{n_{1}}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}T_{n_{1}}^{*}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{m\in F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}\overline{q(n_{1},m)}\overline{q(m,n_{1})}\Big)T_{n_{1}}T_{n_{1}}^{*}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{n_{1}}T_{n_{1}}^{*}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}T_{F\setminus\{n_{1}\}}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p\in F}T_{p}T_{p}^{*}.\hskip 42.67912pt[\text{Repeating the previous steps for remaining elements in $F$}]

Now,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(T,T^{*})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}=\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\prod_{p\in F}T_{p}T_{p}^{*}=\prod_{p\in F}(I-T_{p}T_{p}^{*})\geq 0.

The last inequality follows as
T_{p}
’s are contractions. Hence proved.
∎

q
-commuting rotational shifts:
We define an
n
-tuple of
q
-commuting operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as follows: For a given function
q
as in Definition
1.1
we denote
[q]_{m}
, as a tuple in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, by

[q]_{m}=\big(q(m,1)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,q(m,n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
. With this tuple we define a rotation operator
R_{[q]_{m}}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by

R_{[q]_{m}}f(\bm{z})=f([q]_{m}\bm{z}),\quad\quad\big(f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\big)

where for
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
,
[q]_{m}\bm{z}=\big(q(m,1)^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{1},\ldots,q(m,n)^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{n}\big)
. More precisely,

(3.2)

R_{[q]_{m}}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{m}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

where for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
,
[q]_{m}^{\bm{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}q(m,i)^{\frac{k_{i}}{2}}
.
Now, consider the
n
-tuple of operators

(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})\text{ on }H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, where
M_{z_{i}}
’s, for
i=1,\ldots,n
are the shift operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as defined earlier. We call this kind of tuple as an
n
-tuple of rotational shifts
. Some useful properties of this operator tuple are listed in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

The above defined rotational shifts have the following properties:

(i)

The tuple
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}\big(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\big)

(ii)

For each
m=1\ldots,n
, the adjoint of
M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}}
is given by

(3.3)

(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})^{*}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}+e_{m}}\overline{[q]_{m}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

where,
\overline{[q]_{m}}=\Big(\overline{q(m,1)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,\overline{q(m,n)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)
and
e_{m}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
where
1
appears in the
m
-th position only.

(iii)

For each
m=1\ldots,n
,
M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}}
is an isometry and for all
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

\big\|\big(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}}\big)^{*p}(f)\big\|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad p\to\infty.

(iv)

The tuple
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})
is doubly
q
-commuting and hence satisfies Szegö positivity.

Proof.

(i) To prove
q
-commutativity we calculate

(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})
and
(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})
separately, for arbitrary
i,j=1,\ldots,n
.

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})M_{z_{j}}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}{[q]_{j}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}{[q]_{j}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{j}^{\bm{k}}[q]_{i}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}+e_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{j}^{\bm{k}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}q(i,j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}+e_{i}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}{[q]_{i}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}[q]_{j}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}+e_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}[q]_{j}^{\bm{k}}q(j,i)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}+e_{i}}.

Since,
q(i,j)=\overline{q(j,i)}
and
|q(i,j)|=1
, we have from the above

q(j,i)^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})=q(i,j)^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}}).

Multiplying both sides by
\overline{q(j,i)}^{\frac{1}{2}}
we get

(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})=q(i,j)(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}}).

Therefore,
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}\big(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\big)
.

(ii) For each
m=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\Big<(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big),\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}b_{\bm{l}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}}\Big>=

\displaystyle\Big<\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{m}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{m}},\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}b_{\bm{l}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}}\Big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}[q]_{m}^{\bm{k}}\Big<a_{\bm{k}},b_{{\bm{k}}+e_{m}}\Big>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big<\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}},\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}b_{{\bm{l}}+e_{m}}\overline{[q]_{m}}^{\bm{l}}\bm{z}^{\bm{l}}\Big>.

(iii) For each
m=1,\ldots,n
, rotation operators
R_{[q]_{m}}
, are isometries, which is easy to see from (
3.2
), so are the operators
M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}}
. Also, for each
m=1,\dots,n
,

\Big\|(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})^{*p}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)\Big\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\|a_{\bm{k}+pe_{m}}\|^{2}\to\infty\quad\text{as}\,p\to\infty.

(iv) For arbitrary
i,j=1,\ldots,n
,

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})^{*}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}+e_{j}}\overline{[q]_{j}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}+e_{j}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}\overline{[q]_{j}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})^{*}(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big)=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})^{*}\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{{\bm{k}}+e_{j}}[q]_{i}^{\bm{k}}q(i,j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\overline{[q]_{j}}^{\bm{k}}q(i,j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{z}^{{\bm{k}}+e_{i}}.

So,

(3.4)

(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})^{*}=\overline{q(i,j)}(M_{z_{j}}R_{[q]_{j}})^{*}(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}}).

The above relation and part (i) of this proposition together imply that
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})
is doubly
q
-commuting. Finally, we conclude from Proposition
3.2
that the tuple satisfies Szegö positivity.
∎

Isometric dilations.
For given
q
as in Definition
1.1
and for a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we choose a tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
. Under certain assumptions we shall find isometric dilation of
T
. Our aim is to dilate
T
in the following way: We shall find a tuple of isometries
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{K})
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
such that

(3.5)

\Pi T_{m}^{*}=V_{m}^{*}\Pi

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
. Taking
\mathcal{R}=\ran\Pi
, the above equation tells us that
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is unitarily equivalent to the tuple
(P_{\mathcal{R}}V_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{R}}V_{n}|_{\mathcal{R}})
. Also,

(3.6)

T_{m}^{*}\cong V_{m}^{*}|_{\mathcal{R}}

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
and hence

T^{*\bm{k}}\cong V^{*\bm{k}}|_{\mathcal{R}},\text{ consequently }T^{\bm{k}}\cong P_{\mathcal{R}}V^{\bm{k}}|_{\mathcal{R}}

for all
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, where for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,
T^{\bm{k}}=T_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots T_{n}^{k_{n}}
and
T^{*\bm{k}}=T_{n}^{*k_{n}}\cdots T_{1}^{*k_{1}}
(see
[
5
]
for more detailed explanation). This shows that
V
is a dilation of
T
and the relation
(\ref{co})
tells us that
V
is in particular a co-extension of
T
. Note that, in the above notations of
T^{\bm{k}}
and
T^{*\bm{k}}
, maintaining the order of appearance of the operators which involve in the composition, is important and we shall use these notations very often in the rest of the article.

The following theorem is very crucial for our purpose, as well as, after adding some extra assumption in the hypothesis, it provides isometric dilations for certain class of operator tuples which shall be discussed right after the theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
be a tuple which satisfies Szegö positivity. Denote,

D_{T^{*}}:=\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(T,T^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ and }\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}:=\overline{ran}\,\mathbb{S}_{n}^{-1}(T,T^{*}).

Then, there is a map
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
defined by

\Pi h(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*\bm{k}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

such that

(3.7)

\Pi T_{m}^{*}=(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})^{*}\Pi

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
and

\|\Pi h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\|T_{F}^{l}h\|^{2}

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

First we shall prove the identity (
3.7
). Now for
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle(M_{z_{m}}

\displaystyle R_{[q]_{m}})^{*}\Pi h(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})^{*}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*\bm{k}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<m}q(i,m)^{\frac{k_{i}(k_{m}+1)}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{m<j\leq n}q(m,j)^{\frac{(k_{m}+1)k_{j}}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Big(\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}\overline{q(m,j)}^{\frac{k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}+e_{m}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\hskip 193.47882pt[\text{by}\,(\ref{adjoint})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<m}q(i,m)^{\frac{k_{i}(k_{m}+1)}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{m<j\leq n}q(m,j)^{\frac{k_{m}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Big(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\overline{q(m,j)}^{\frac{k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}+e_{m}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\hskip 122.34692pt[\text{as }\overline{q(m,j)}q(m,j)=1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<m}q(i,m)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{m}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{m<j\leq n}q(m,j)^{\frac{k_{m}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Big(\prod_{1\leq j<m}{q(j,m)}^{k_{j}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}+e_{m}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq j<m}q(j,m)^{k_{j}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}+e_{m}}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq j<m}q(j,m)^{k_{j}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{1\leq j<m}\overline{q(j,m)}^{k_{j}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}}T_{m}^{*}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\hskip 110.96556pt[\text{since},\,\,T_{j}T_{m}=q(j,m)T_{m}T_{j}\,\,\,\text{implies}\,\,\,T_{m}^{*}T_{j}^{*}=\overline{q(j,m)}T_{j}^{*}T_{m}^{*}\,]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*{\bm{k}}}T_{m}^{*}h\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi T_{m}^{*}h(\bm{z}).

This proves the identity (
3.7
). For the remaining part we follow the technique of
[
1
]
.

\displaystyle\|\Pi h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\Big\|\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q(i,j)^{\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{2}}\Big)D_{T^{*}}T^{*\bm{k}}h\Big\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\|D_{T^{*}}T^{*\bm{k}}h\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\\
\max k_{j}\leq l-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\|T_{F}^{*}T^{*\bm{k}}h\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\\
\max\beta_{j}\leq l\end{subarray}}\|T^{*\bm{\beta}}h\|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\leq\bm{\beta}\\
\max(\beta_{j}-k_{j})\leq 1\\
\max k_{j}\leq l-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\bm{\beta}|-|\bm{k}|}.

Observe that, for all
\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\leq\bm{\beta}\\
\max(\beta_{j}-k_{j})\leq 1\\
\max k_{j}\leq l-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|\bm{\beta}|-|\bm{k}|}=0

except the case that
\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}\subset\{0,l\}
and if
\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}\subset\{0,l\}
the sum is equal to
(-1)^{|\text{supp}\,\beta|}
. So,

\|\Pi h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\|T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2},

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

A tuple of bounded operators
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
pure
if for all
i=1,\ldots,n
and for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
,
\|T_{i}^{*m}(h)\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. We have seen in Proposition
3.3
that
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}\big(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\big)
is a pure tuple of isometries. In the following theorem, we describe explicit isometric dilations for a class of pure tuples in
\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
which generalizes the dilation result of
[
10
]
for commutative case and it is a particular case of above theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
be a pure tuple satisfying Szegö positivity. Let
D_{T^{*}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
and
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be as in Theorem
3.4
. Then,
\Pi
is an isometry and

(3.8)

\Pi T_{m}^{*}=(M_{z_{m}}R_{[q]_{m}})^{*}\Pi.

for all
m=1,\ldots,n
. In other words,
(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q]_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q]_{n}})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is an isometric dilation of the
q
-commuting tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
.

Proof.

Only to prove that
\Pi
is an isometry as everything else follows from Theorem
3.4
. Observe that,

\|\Pi h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\|T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}+\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{\emptyset\neq F\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1)^{|F|}\|T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2}.

Now if
T
is pure, the last term of the above equality is
0
. Therefore,
\Pi
is an isometry.
∎

Generalizing Szegö positivity, we say that
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
satisfies
Brehmer positivity
if for all
G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}

\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}\geq 0.

With this we define another class of operator tuple, namely
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
, as the set of all
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
such that
T
satisfies Brehmer positivity, that is,

\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H}):\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}\geq 0,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\Big\}.

This class naturally extends the Brehmer class of commuting operator tuples having isometric dilations. We find explicit isometric dilations for the class
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
and in order to do that we mostly follow the explicit construction from
[
1
]
. To prove the dilation theorem for this class, we establish several technical lemmas. Before proceeding further we fix some more notations which will be used afterwords. For
T\in\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
and for a subset
G=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we denote

T(G):=(T_{j_{1}},\ldots,T_{j_{r}}),\quad\mathbb{S}_{r}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})=\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}

and

D_{T(G)^{*}}:=\mathbb{S}_{r}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ and }\mathcal{D}_{T(G)^{*}}:=\overline{\ran}\,D_{T(G)^{*}}.

Our first lemma is the
q
-commuting version of Fuglede-Putnam’s theorem
[
15
]
for commuting operators and proof of it is a simple application of the same theorem. The lemma is as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
N
is normal operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
X:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is an operator such that
XN=qNX
, where
q
is a complex number, then
XN^{*}=\overline{q}N^{*}X
.

Proof.

Consider

A=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
X&0\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }B=\begin{bmatrix}N&0\\
0&qN\end{bmatrix}.

Clearly,
B
is normal operator on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
and
AB=BA
. Then by Fuglede-Putnam’s theorem,
AB^{*}=B^{*}A
and hence
XN^{*}=\overline{q}N^{*}X
.
∎

Our next lemma in this sequel is following.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\overline{G}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\smallsetminus G
. Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
be such that
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})\geq 0
.
Suppose
T_{j}
is a co-isometry for all
j\in\overline{G}
. Then there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{G}
and an
n
-tuple of isometries
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{r})
such that
V
is doubly
q
-commuting and

\Pi_{G}T_{i}^{*}=V_{i}^{*}\Pi_{G}\ (1\leq i\leq n)

where
\Pi_{G}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{r})
is a map such that

\|\Pi_{G}h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2},

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Moreover,

V_{m_{i}}=M_{z_{m_{i}}}R_{[q]_{m_{i}}}(1\leq i\leq r)\text{ and }V_{j}=(I\otimes U_{j})R_{[q]_{j}}^{2}\ (j\in\overline{G})

for some
q
-commuting unitaries
U_{j}
on
\mathcal{H}_{G}
. (For this lemma we fix that
\bm{z}=(z_{m_{1}},\ldots,z_{m_{r}})\in\mathbb{D}^{r}
).

Proof.

First we observe that, for each
j\in\overline{G}
,

\displaystyle T_{j}D^{2}_{T(G)^{*}}T_{j}^{*}=\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{j}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}T_{j}^{*}=\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\Big(\prod_{i\in F}q(j,i)\overline{q(j,i)}\Big)T_{F}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{F}^{*}=D^{2}_{T(G)^{*}}.

So, by Douglas’ lemma (see
[
13
]
), there exist co-isometries
S_{j}:\mathcal{D}_{T(G)^{*}}\to\mathcal{D}_{T(G)^{*}}

(j\in\overline{G})
such that

(3.9)

S_{j}^{*}D_{T(G)^{*}}h=D_{T(G)^{*}}T_{j}^{*}h,

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. It follows from the
q
-commutativity of the sub-tuple
T(G)
and the above equation that
\{S_{j}:j\in\overline{G}\}
is a tuple of
q
-commuting co-isometries on
\mathcal{D}_{T(G)^{*}}
. Since, due to Theorem 6.1 of
[
3
]
, every tuple of
q
-commuting isometries extends to a tuple of
q
-commuting unitaries, the tuple
\{S_{j}:j\in\overline{G}\}
co-extends to a tuple of
q
-commuting unitaries, say, to
\{U_{j}:j\in\overline{G}\}
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{G}
.

On the other hand, since the
r
-tuple of
q
-commuting contractions

\displaystyle\Pi_{G}T_{j}^{*}h(\bm{z})

\displaystyle(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})

\displaystyle q(i,j)\big((I\otimes U_{j})R^{2}_{[q]_{j}}\big)(M_{z_{i}}R_{[q]_{i}})\Big(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{r}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\Big).

We need one more lemma which describes a canonical way to construct co-isometries out of
q
-commuting contractions.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\overline{G}=\{1,\dots,n\}\smallsetminus G
. Then there exist a positive operator
X:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
and contractions
\tilde{T}_{j}:\overline{\ran}\,X\to\overline{\ran}\,X

(1\leq j\leq n)
defined by

\tilde{T}_{j}^{*}Xh=X{T}_{j}^{*}h,\quad(h\in\mathcal{H})

such that
\tilde{T}_{j}
is a co-isometry for all
j\in\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a tuple of contractions, strong operator limit (SOT) of
T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}
exists as
m\to\infty
.
Set

X^{2}:=\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}.

Since
T_{j}
is a contraction, it is easy to see that

T_{j}X^{2}T_{j}^{*}=\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}\Big(\prod_{i\in\overline{G}}q(j,i)^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{q(j,i)}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Big)T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}\leq X^{2},

for all
j=1\ldots,n
. Consequently by Douglas’ lemma, for each
1\leq j\leq n
, there exists a contraction
\tilde{T}_{j}:\overline{\mbox{ran}}\,X\to\overline{\ran}\,X
such that

\tilde{T}_{j}^{*}Xh=X{T}_{j}^{*}h\quad(h\in\mathcal{H}).

Moreover, for all
j\in\overline{G}
,

\displaystyle X^{2}\geq T_{j}X^{2}T_{j}^{*}=

\displaystyle\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}\Big(\prod_{i\in\overline{G}}q(j,i)^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{q(j,i)}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Big)T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{\overline{G}}^{(m+1)}T_{\overline{G}}^{*(m+1)}=X^{2},

that is,

T_{j}X^{2}T_{j}^{*}=X^{2}.

Hence
\tilde{T}_{j}
is a co-isometry for all
j\in\overline{G}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Combining the above lemmas we now find co-extensions for

n
-tuples in
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})\in\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
. Then, for each
G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{G}
, doubly
q
-commuting isometries
V_{G,1},\ldots,V_{G,n}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})

and an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\to\bigoplus_{G}H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})
such that

\Pi T_{j}^{*}=\big(\bigoplus_{G}V_{G,j}^{*}\big)\Pi,\ (1\leq j\leq n)

where, by convention
H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\emptyset|}):=\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}
. Moreover, for
i\in G
,
V_{G,i}
’s are the rotational shifts, and for
j\in\overline{G}
,
V_{G,j}
’s are some unitaries on the Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})
.

Proof.

Let us fix
G=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{|G|}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
and set
\overline{G}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\smallsetminus G
. Let
X_{G}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be the positive operator defined by

X_{G}^{2}:=\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}.

Then, by Lemma
3.8
, we have a contraction

S_{j}:\overline{\ran}\,X_{G}\to\overline{\ran}\,X_{G}
defined by

S_{j}^{*}X_{G}h=X_{G}{T}_{j}^{*}h,\quad(h\in\mathcal{H})

for all
j=1,\dots,n
so that
S_{j}
is a co-isometry
for all
j\in\overline{G}
. Since,
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is
q
-commuting, it is clear from the definition that
S:=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is also a
q
-commuting tuple. Also, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\Big\langle\mathbb{S}_{|G|}^{-1}(S(G),S(G)^{*})

\displaystyle X_{G}h,X_{G}h\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}X_{G}X_{G}^{*}T_{F}^{*}h,h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}\Big(\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}\Big)T_{F}^{*}h,h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\Big(\prod_{j\in\overline{G},i\in G}q(i,j)^{\frac{m}{2}}\overline{q(i,j)}^{\frac{m}{2}}\Big)\text{SOT-}\lim_{m\to\infty}T_{\overline{G}}^{m}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}h,h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\langle\Big(\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}T_{F}T_{F}^{*}\Big)T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}h,T_{\overline{G}}^{*m}h\right\rangle\geq 0.

This implies,
\mathbb{S}_{|G|}^{-1}(S(G),S(G)^{*})\geq 0
. Apply Lemma
3.7
to get a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{G}
, a map
\tilde{\Pi}_{G}:\overline{\mbox{ran}}\,X_{G}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})
and
n
-tuple of doubly
q
-commuting isometries

V=(V_{G,1},\ldots,V_{G,n})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})
such that

\|\tilde{\Pi}_{G}h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|S^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2},

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and

\tilde{\Pi}_{G}S_{i}^{*}=V_{G,i}^{*}\tilde{\Pi}_{G},\ (1\leq i\leq n)

where

V_{G,m_{i}}=M_{z_{m_{i}}}R_{[q]_{m_{i}}}(1\leq i\leq|G|)\ \text{and }V_{j}=(I\otimes U_{j})R_{[q^{2}]_{j}}\ (j\in\overline{G}).

Now, using the identity
S_{j}^{*}X_{G}=X_{G}T_{j}^{*}
and setting

\Pi_{G}:=\tilde{\Pi}_{G}X_{G},

we have that

(3.10)

\Pi_{G}T_{i}^{*}=V_{G,i}^{*}\Pi_{G},\ (1\leq i\leq n)

where
\Pi_{G}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(\mathbb{D}^{|G|})

is a contraction with

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{G}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|S^{*l}_{F}X_{G}h\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|X_{G}T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2},

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Take
\Pi=\bigoplus_{G}\Pi_{G}
.
Then it follows from (
3.10
) that

\Pi T_{j}^{*}=(\bigoplus_{G}V_{G,j}^{*})\Pi

for all
j=1,\dots,n
. To show
\Pi
is an isometry, for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we compute

\displaystyle\|\Pi h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\|\Pi_{G}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|X_{G}T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{G\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{F\subset G}(-1)^{|F|}\|T_{\overline{G}}^{*l}T^{*l}_{F}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\sum_{E\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\|T^{*l}_{E}h\|^{2}\sum_{F\subset E}(-1)^{|F|}.

If
E\neq\emptyset
one can see that
\sum_{F\subset E}(-1)^{|F|}=0
. So
\|\Pi h\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and hence
\Pi
is an isometry. The moreover part is now clear from the construction
of
V_{G,j}
’s. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

(1)

In the above theorem, the dilating tuple
V=\Big(\bigoplus_{G}V_{G,1},\ldots,\bigoplus_{G}V_{G,n}\Big)
is doubly
q
-commuting so are all the sub-tuples of
V
. Proposition
3.2
tells us that
V
satisfy Brehmer positivity and hence
V
belongs to the class
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}\Big(\bigoplus_{G}H^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}\big(\mathbb{D}^{|G|}\big)\Big)
.

(2)

The class
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
enlarges the class discussed in Theorem
3.5
and this is for the following reason: Given a pure tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
, a subset
G\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
m\in\overline{G}
, observe that

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,\mathbb{S}_{|G|}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}T_{m}^{p}\Big(\mathbb{S}_{|G|}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})-T_{m}\big(\mathbb{S}_{|G|}^{-1}(T(G),T(G)^{*})\big)T_{m}^{*}\Big)T_{m}^{*p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}T_{m}^{p}\Big(\mathbb{S}_{|G|+1}^{-1}\big(T(G\cup\{m\}),T(G\cup\{m\})^{*}\big)\Big)T_{m}^{*p}.

This shows, by induction, that if
T\in\mathcal{T}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
is a pure tuple satisfying Szegö positivity, every sub-tuple of
T
satisfies Szegö positivity and hence
T\in\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
. On the other hand, if
T
is also a pure tuple in
\mathfrak{B}^{q,n}(\mathcal{H})
then the positive operator
X_{G}
, defined in the proof of Theorem
3.9
, is
0
for all
G\subsetneq\{1,\dots,n\}
and

X_{G}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
for
G=\{1,\dots,n\}
. This implies
\Pi_{G}=0

for all
G\subsetneq\{1,\dots,n\}
, and for
G=\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\Pi_{G}
is an isometry and
\mathcal{H}_{G}=\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
. Then it follows from Theorem
3.9
that the
q
-commuting tuple of isometries

(M_{z_{1}}R_{[q_{1}]},\ldots,M_{z_{n}}R_{[q_{n}]})
on

H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a dilation of
T
. Thus isometric dilation for the larger class in Theorem
3.9
is a generalization the dilation result of Theorem
3.5
.

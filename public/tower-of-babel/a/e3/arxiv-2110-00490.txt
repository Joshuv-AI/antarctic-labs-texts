Fully nonlinear elliptic equations on Hermitian manifolds for symmetric functions of partial Laplacians Thanks: The third author was partially supported by NSFC (Grant No. 11971401).
Mathew George, Bo Guan, Chunhui Qiu

Abstract
. We consider a class of fully nonlinear second order elliptic equations on Hermitian manifolds closely related to the general notion of 𝔾 -plurisubharmonicity of Harvey-Lawson and an equation treated by Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove in the proof of Gauduchon conjecture. Under fairly general assumptions we derive interior estimates and establish the existence of smooth solutions for the Dirichlet problem as well as for equations on closed manifolds. Mathematical Subject Classification (MSC2020): 35J15, 35J60, 58J05.



1.
Introduction

In a remarkable paper where the Gauduchon conjecture which extends Calabi-Yau Theorem to non-Kähler metrics was proved,
Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove
[
23
]
solved the Monge-Ampère type equation

(1.1)

(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+\chi(\partial u,\bar{\partial}u))^{n}=\psi\omega^{n}

on a compact Hermitian manifold
(M^{n},\omega)
, where for a function
u\in C^{2}(M)
,
\chi(\partial u,\bar{\partial}u)
is a real
(1,1)
form which depends on

\partial u
,
\bar{\partial}u
linearly and satisfies some additional assumptions
(which for convenience we shall refer as
STW conditions
).
This equation also has close connections to the form-type Monge-Ampère equations studied by
Fu-Wang-Wu
[
4
,
5
]
, and the notion of
n-1
plurisubharmonic functions
in the sense of Harvey-Lawson
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

In the same paper
[
23
]
, the authors also treated more general equations of the form

(1.2)

f(\lambda(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+\chi(\partial u,\bar{\partial}u))=\psi

under the same (STW) conditions on
\chi
, where
\lambda(U)
denotes the eigenvalues of a real
(1,1)
form
U
with respect to
\omega
, and

f
is a symmetric function of
n
variables satisfying the structure conditions of
Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
1
]
, i.e.

f
is assumed to be defined in a symmetric
open and convex cone
\Gamma\subset\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}
with vertex
at the origin,

(1.3)

\Gamma_{n}=\{\lambda\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0\}\subset\Gamma,

and satisfies the conditions

(1.4)

f_{i}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}>0\;\;\mbox{in $\Gamma$},\;\;1\leq i\leq n,

(1.5)

\mbox{$f$ is a concave function in $\Gamma$},

(1.6)

\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f<\inf_{M}\psi

and, in addition

(1.7)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}f(t\lambda)=\sup_{\Gamma}f,\;\;\forall\;\lambda\in\Gamma.

There are other important fully nonlinear equations of second order in complex geometry with explicit dependence on the solution and its gradient,
such as the Fu-Yau equation
[
6
,
7
]
arising from the Strominger system in superstring theory and its higher dimension extensions studied by Phong-Picard-Zhang
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
and Chu-Huang-Zhu
[
2
]
.
In general, the presence of these terms and the gradients especially, cause substantial difficulties in solving the equations, even in the case of linear dependence for the Monge-Ampère type equation (
1.1
).

It is clear that Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove
[
23
]
primarily focused on
equation (
1.1
) in order to prove the Gauduchon conjecture. It seems worthwhile to take a closer look at equation (
1.2
) from different angles, and from a more PDE point of view. This is one of our main purposes
in the current paper.
We shall assume
in place of (
1.4
) that
f
satisfies the weaker condition

(1.8)

f_{i}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\geq 0\;\;\mbox{in $\Gamma$},\;\;1\leq i\leq n.

In order to describe our results, we recall some notions from
[
9
]
and

[
11
]
.
For a fixed real number
\sigma\in(\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f,\sup_{\Gamma}f)

define

\Gamma^{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)>\sigma\}.

Lemma 1.1
.

Under conditions (
1.8
) and (
1.5
), the level hypersurface of
f

\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)=\sigma\},

which is the boundary of
\Gamma^{\sigma}
, is smooth and convex.

Proof.

If
f
satisfies both (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), then

\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
is clearly smooth and convex.
This is still true under the weaker condition (
1.8
) in place of (
1.4
).
For if
Df(\lambda_{0})=0
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\Gamma
then
by (
1.8
) and (
1.5
) we see that

in the cone
\lambda_{0}+\Gamma_{n}
and therefore
f(\lambda_{0})=\sup_{\Gamma}f>\sigma
,
showing that
Df\neq 0
on
\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
. So
\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
is smooth.
∎

For
\lambda\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
let

\nu_{\lambda}=\frac{Df(\lambda)}{|Df(\lambda)|}

denote the unit normal vector to
\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
at
\lambda
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

For
\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}

let

S^{\sigma}_{\mu}=\{\lambda\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}:\nu_{\lambda}\cdot(\mu-\lambda)\leq 0\}.

The
tangent cone at infinity
to
\Gamma^{\sigma}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\{\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:S^{\sigma}_{\mu}\;\mbox{is compact}\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\sigma}
is a symmetric convex cone. As in
[
9
]
one can show that
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{\sigma}
is open.

Definition 1.3

(
[
11
]
)
.

The
rank of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{+}
is defined to be

\min\{r(\nu):\mbox{$\nu$ is the unit normal vector of a supporting plane
to ${\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{+}$}\}

where

denotes the number of non-zero
components of
\nu
.

Our first result concerns
a priori
interior estimates for second order derivatives.
Let

be an admissible solution of the equation

(1.9)

f(\lambda(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+\chi[u]))=\psi[u]

where
\chi[u]=\chi(z,u,\partial u,\bar{\partial}u)

and

in

We assume

(1.10)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t{\bf{1}})-\sup_{M}\psi[u]\geq c_{0}>0

where
{\bf{1}}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\Gamma
. So

may depend on

. This is clearly a necessary and fairly mild condition for the following result to hold.

Theorem 1.4
.

In addition to (
1.5
), (
1.6
), (
1.8
) and (
1.10
)
assume that

(1.11)

\sum f_{i}\lambda_{i}\geq-C_{0}\sum f_{i}\;\;\mbox{in $\Gamma$}

for some constant

, and

(1.12)

\mbox{rank of}\;\;\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}\geq 2,\;\;\forall\;\inf_{M}\psi[u]\leq\sigma\leq\sup_{M}\psi[u].

Let

be a geodesic ball of radius

in

(1.13)

\sup_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}}|\partial\bar{\partial}u|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}

c_{0}
,

,

and other known data.

Geometrically, condition (

\lambda_{0}\in\Gamma
, the distance from the origin to the tangent plane at
\lambda_{0}

of the level hypersuface

has a uniform bound

. It is satisfied in most applications and is weaker than assumption (

) which implies

(1.14)

\sum f_{i}\lambda_{i}\geq 0\;\;\mbox{in $\Gamma$}.

By an inequality of Lin-Trudinger

]
,
the rank of
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
is

for
f=\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}

and
\sigma>0

is the
k
-th elementary symmetric function defined on the Garding cone

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\;\mbox{for $1\leq j\leq k$}\}.

So Theorem
1.4
applies to

but excludes equation (
1.1
) which corresponds to

f=\sigma_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}
.

Theorem
1.4
heavily relies on a crucial lemma in Section
2

From the proof we shall see
that there exists
\beta_{0}>1
such that Theorem
1.4
extends to the equation

(1.15)

f(\lambda(\Delta u\omega-\beta\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+\chi[u]))=\psi[u],

for all
\beta<\beta_{0}
.
It would be interesting to decide the supremum value,

, of such

and study the equation in the limiting case.
For equation (

\sup{\beta}_{0}=1

The second purpose of the current paper is to extend
Theorems

Let

be a fixed positive integer. Set

\mathfrak{I}_{K}=\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{K}):1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{K}\leq n,\;i_{j}\in\hbox{\bbbld N}\}

\Lambda_{I}(\lambda)=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{K}},\;\;\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}.

For convenience we fix an order for the elements in

I_{1},\ldots,I_{N},\;\mbox{where}\;N=\frac{n!}{K!(n-K)!}

and write

\Lambda(\lambda)=(\Lambda_{I_{1}}(\lambda),\ldots,\Lambda_{I_{N}}(\lambda))

\Lambda(\lambda)=(\Lambda_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,\Lambda_{N}(\lambda)).

We consider the equation

(1.17)

f(\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]))=\psi[u],

where

\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u])=\Lambda(\lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]))

\lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u])=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]
with respect to

\omega
,

is a real
(1,1)
form depending on
u
,
\partial u
and
\bar{\partial}u
, and

is a symmetric concave function defined in an open symmetric convex cone
\Gamma\subset\hbox{\bbbld R}^{N}

,
and satisfies

(1.18)

f_{i}\equiv f_{\Lambda_{i}}\equiv\frac{\partial f}{\partial\Lambda_{i}}\geq 0\;\;\mbox{in $\Gamma$},\;\;1\leq i\leq N.

We may similarly define admissible functions by requiring
\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u])\in\Gamma
.
Clearly, equations (
1.2
) and (
1.17

Equation (

As in

For

Clearly,

2.
Rank of tangent cone at infinity

In this section
f
is assumed to satisfy (
1.5
), (
1.8
)
and (
1.11
).
We shall prove the following key lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P=\{\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\nu\cdot\mu=c\}
be a hyperplane, where
\nu
is a unit vector. Suppose that
there exists a sequence

\{\lambda_{k}\}
in
\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
with

(2.1)

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}=\nu,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot\lambda_{k}=c,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}|\lambda_{k}|=+\infty.

Then
P
is a supporting hyperplane to
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
at a non-vertex point.

Proof.

Assume
\mu_{0}\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}
with
\nu\cdot\mu_{0}\leq c-\epsilon

for some
\epsilon>0
. Then

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot(\mu_{0}-\lambda_{k})=\nu\cdot\mu_{0}-c\leq-\epsilon.

This means that
S_{\mu_{0}}^{\sigma}
is noncompact and therefore

\mu_{0}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
. As
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary,
we see that
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
is contained in the half space

P^{+}=\{\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\nu\cdot\mu>c\}
.

By symmetry the vertex of
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
is at a point

{\bf a}=(a,\ldots,a)
for some real number
a
.
It is easy to see that
{\bf a}\in P
, i.e.

a=\frac{c}{\sum\nu^{i}}

where
\nu=(\nu^{1},\ldots,\nu^{n})
. Indeed, clearly
a\geq c(\sum\nu^{i})^{-1}

for otherwise
{\bf a}\notin\overline{P^{+}}
. Let
R_{k}
be the ray from
{\bf a}
passing

\lambda_{k}
so
R_{k}
lies in
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}\subset P^{+}
. On the other hand,
R_{k}
would not completely lie in
\overline{P^{+}}
for
k
large if
a>c(\sum\nu^{i})^{-1}
, a contradiction.

Let
\mu_{k}
be the point of intersection of
R_{k}
and the unit sphere centered at

{\bf a}
.
Passing to a subsequence we may assume

\mu_{k}\rightarrow\mu\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}}
as

k\rightarrow+\infty
. Clearly,

0\geq\nu_{k}\cdot(\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k})\geq\nu_{k}\cdot({\bf a}-\lambda_{k}).

Taking limit as
k\rightarrow+\infty
we obtain
\nu\cdot\mu=c
.
This show that
\mu\in P\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
be the rank of
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
. There exists
c_{1}>0

such that at any point
\lambda\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
where without loss of generality we assume
f_{1}\leq\cdots\leq f_{n}
,

\sum_{i\leq n-r+1}f_{i}\geq c_{1}\sum f_{i}.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction. Suppose that for each
k=1,2,\ldots
there exists

\lambda_{k}\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
with

f_{1}(\lambda_{k})\leq\cdots\leq f_{n}(\lambda_{k})
and

(2.2)

\sum_{i\leq n-r+1}f_{i}(\lambda_{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum f_{i}(\lambda_{k}).

Note that there is
t>0
such that
t{\bf{1}}\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
, that is,

f(t{\bf{1}})=\sigma
. By the concavity of
f
,

\sum f_{i}(t-\lambda_{i})\geq f(t{\bf{1}})-f(\lambda)=0.

Therefore

\sum f_{i}\lambda_{i}\leq t\sum f_{i}.

By (
1.11
),
we can pass to a subsequence and assume that

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}=\nu,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot\lambda_{k}=c,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}|\lambda_{k}|=+\infty.

According to Lemma
2.1
,
the hyperplane

P=\{\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\nu\cdot\mu=c\}
is a supporting hyperplane to

\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
. However,
\nu
has at most
r-1
nonzero components,
which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

P=\{\mu\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\nu\cdot\mu=c\}
, where
\nu
is a unit vector, be a
tangent hyperplane to

\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
. Then
there exists a sequence

\{\lambda_{k}\}
in
\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
with

(2.3)

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}=\nu,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot\lambda_{k}=c,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}|\lambda_{k}|=+\infty.

Proof.

We may assume

\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}\cap P=\emptyset

for otherwise it contains a ray (see
[
9
]
) and
(
2.3
) holds for some

\lambda_{k}\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}\cap P
.

Suppose that
P
is the tangent hyperplane to
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}

at
\mu\in P\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
.
We have
\mu\notin\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}
and

\mu_{k}=\mu+\frac{\nu}{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
for all
k\geq 1

as
\nu\in\overline{\Gamma_{n}}
and
\mu+\Gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
.
Let

S_{\mu_{k}}^{\sigma,0}=\{\lambda\in\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}:\nu_{\lambda}\cdot(\mu_{k}-\lambda)=0\}

and choose
\lambda_{k}\in S_{\mu_{k}}^{\sigma,0}
satisfying

\nu\cdot(\lambda_{k}-\mu)=\min_{\lambda\in S_{\mu_{k}}^{\sigma,0}}\nu\cdot(\lambda-\mu)\geq 0;

such
\lambda_{k}
exists as
S_{\mu_{k}}^{\sigma,0}

is compact.
As

\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}\cap\partial\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}\cap P=\emptyset
,
we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}|\lambda_{k}|=+\infty.

So the last limit in (
2.3
) holds.
Next,

\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot(\lambda_{k}-\mu)=\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k})+\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot(\mu_{k}-\mu)=\frac{\nu_{k}\cdot\nu}{k}.

Therefore

(2.4)

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot(\lambda_{k}-\mu)=0.

Passing to a subsequence we may assume

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}=\nu^{\prime}.

By (
2.4
) we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{\lambda_{k}}\cdot\lambda_{k}=\nu^{\prime}\cdot\mu\equiv c^{\prime}.

By Lemma
2.1
we see that

P^{\prime}=\{\eta\in\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}:\nu^{\prime}\cdot\eta=c^{\prime}\}
is a supporting plane
to
\mathcal{C}_{\sigma}^{+}
at
\mu
. By uniqueness of the tangent plane,
P^{\prime}=P
and therefore

\nu^{\prime}=\nu
.
∎

From Lemma
2.2
we have the following key inequality
for each
i\geq 1
,

(3.29)

G^{i\bar{i}}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}f_{\Lambda_{l}}\frac{\partial\Lambda_{l}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=\sum_{i\in I}f_{\Lambda_{I}}\geq c_{1}\sum_{l=1}^{N}f_{\Lambda_{l}},

where the sum
\sum_{i\in I}f_{\Lambda_{I}}
is taken over all
I\in\mathfrak{I}_{K}
with
i\in I
, since

\lambda(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{n\bar{n}})
.
Note that when
\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{j}}
is diagonal, so is
G^{i\bar{j}}
.

Consequently, by (
3.28
) we obtain

(3.30)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}|A|^{2}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}+\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}G^{i\bar{i}}\Big(\frac{\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\eta}{\eta}-\frac{C\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta}{\eta^{2}}\Big)\leq C|A|^{2}

provided that
\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}
is large enough.

By the concavity of
f
and Schwarz inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}-\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}\Lambda_{l}(\mathfrak{g})+\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}\Lambda_{l}(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle f(\sqrt{\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}}{\bf{1}})-f(\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}))-\epsilon\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}\Lambda_{l}^{2}-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2}-\epsilon\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}\Lambda_{l}^{2}-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}

by assumption (
1.10
), provided that
\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}
is sufficiently large.
So from (
3.30
) we obtain

(3.31)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}|A|^{2}\sum f_{\Lambda_{l}}+\sqrt{\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}}|A|^{2}+C\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}G^{i\bar{i}}\Big(\frac{\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\eta}{\eta}-\frac{C\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta}{\eta^{2}}\Big)\leq C|A|^{2}.

To derive the interior estimate, following
[
13
]
we take
\eta
to be a smooth
function with compact support in
B_{r}\subset M

satisfying

(3.32)

0\leq\eta\leq 1,~~\eta|_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}}\equiv 1,~~|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{C\sqrt{\eta}}{r},~~|\nabla^{2}\eta|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}},

so that

(3.33)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\eta-\frac{C}{\eta}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\sum f_{\Lambda_{i}}.

We derive a bound
\eta\mathfrak{g}_{1\bar{1}}\leq C/r^{2}
at
z_{0}
which gives (
1.13
).

Suppose that
(M^{n},\omega)

is a Hermitian manifold with smooth boundary
\partial M
.
Taking
\eta=1
we obtain the global estimate (
1.21
).
The boundary estimate (
1.22
) may be derived as in
[
12
]

with some minor modifications; we shall omit the details here.

Remark 3.1
.

When
X
and
\psi
are independent of

and

, we obtain

\sup_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}}|\bar{\partial}\partial u|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\Big\{1+\sup_{B_{r}}|\partial u|^{2}\Big\}.

4.
Gradient estimates

In this section we derive gradient estimates. As usual it requires suitable growth conditions of
X
and
\psi
on
u
and its gradient. Concerning
X
, we assume the following sub-linear growth assumption
for
D_{\zeta}X
when
|\zeta|
is sufficiently large,

(4.1)

|D_{\zeta}X(z,u,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})|\leq\varrho_{0}|\zeta|,\;\;D_{u}X\leq(\varrho_{0}|\zeta|^{2}+\varrho_{1})\omega

where
\varrho_{1}=\varrho_{1}(z,u)
and

\varrho_{0}=\varrho_{0}(z,u,|\zeta|)\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
|\zeta|\rightarrow\infty
;
clearly we may assume
t\varrho_{0}(z,u,t)
to be increasing in
t>0
.
It follows that

|X|\leq C\varrho_{0}|\zeta|^{2}+\varrho_{1}(z,u)

for some function
\varrho_{1}
; we shall only need (for convenience we drop the
constant
C
)

(4.2)

X\leq(\varrho_{0}|\zeta|^{2}+\varrho_{1})\omega

which will also be crucial to the existence, more precisely to the upper bound, of admissible solutions of the Dirichlet problem in Theorem
5.2
in Section
5
.
We impose a similar condition on
\psi
but, as in
[
12
]
, associate it with the growth of
f
:

(4.3)

|D_{\zeta}\psi(z,u,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})|\leq\varrho_{0}f(|\zeta|^{2}{\bf{1}})/|\zeta|,\;\;-D_{u}\psi\leq\varrho_{0}f(|\zeta|^{2}{\bf{1}})+\varrho_{1}(z,u)

which in particular implies

(4.4)

\psi\leq\varrho_{0}f(|\zeta|^{2}{\bf{1}})+\varrho_{1}(z,u).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u\in C^{3}(\bar{B}_{r})
be an admissible solution of
equation (
1.17
).
Under conditions
(
1.5
), (
1.6
), (
1.18
), (
1.11
),
(
1.19
), (
4.1
) and (
4.3
),

u
satisfies the following interior gradient estimate

(4.5)

|\partial u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\Big\{1+\sup_{\bar{B}_{r}}u-u\Big\}\;\;\mbox{in ${B}_{\frac{r}{2}}$}

for some uniform constant
C>0
.

Remark 4.2
.

We may replace (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) by the weaker
assumptions (
4.2
), (
4.4
) and the linear-like growth conditions

(4.6)

|D_{\zeta}X(z,u,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})|\leq\varrho(z,u)|\zeta|,

(4.7)

|D_{\zeta}\psi(z,u,\zeta,\bar{\zeta})|\leq\varrho(z,u)f(|\zeta|^{2}{\bf{1}})/|\zeta|,

where
\varrho>0
. In this case we can derive the bound

(4.8)

|\partial u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\Big\{1+u-\inf_{\bar{B}_{r}}u\Big\}^{N}\;\;\mbox{in ${B}_{\frac{r}{2}}$}

for sufficiently large
N>0
; see e.g.
[
12
]
.

Remark 4.3
.

When
\omega
is Kähler, (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) can be replaced
by the assumptions

(4.9)

D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}X(z,u,p)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq\varrho(z,u)|\xi|^{2},\;\;\mbox{for $\xi\in T_{z}M$},

that is,
X
is semi-concave in
\nabla u
, the real gradient of
u
, and

(4.10)

D^{2}_{p_{i}p_{j}}\psi(z,u,p)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq-\varrho(z,u)f(|p|^{2}{\bf{1}})|\xi|^{2}/|p|^{2},\;\;\xi\in T_{z}M.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Consider

\max_{\bar{M}}e^{\phi}|\nabla u|^{2}

where
\phi
is a function to be chosen.
Suppose it is attained at an interior point
z_{0}\in M
.
We choose local coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
such that

g_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{ij}
,

T_{ij}^{k}=2\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{j}}
are diagonal at
z_{0}
.
The function

\log|\nabla u|^{2}+\phi

achieves a maximum at
z_{0}
where we assume
|\nabla u|\geq 1
.
Therefore at
z_{0}
,

(4.11)

\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\partial_{i}\phi=0,\;\;\bar{\partial}_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\bar{\partial}_{i}\phi=0

and

(4.12)

G^{i\bar{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}-G^{i\bar{i}}\frac{\bar{\partial}_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{4}}+G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\phi\leq 0.

By (
3.24
), (
3.25
) and Schwarz inequality,

(4.13)

\bar{\partial}_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla u|^{2}|Q_{i}|^{2}

and

(4.14)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)|Q_{i}|^{2}+\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\bar{i}}\nabla_{i}u+\nabla_{k}u\nabla_{\bar{k}}\nabla_{\bar{i}}\nabla_{i}u-C|\nabla u|^{2}.

where
0<\gamma<1/6
and

|Q_{i}|^{2}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\bar{i}}u+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}u\nabla_{\bar{i}}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u=\sum_{k}(|\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u|^{2}+|\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}u|^{2}).

It follows from (
3.3
) that

(4.15)

G^{i\bar{i}}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\bar{i}}\nabla_{i}u=G^{i\bar{i}}(\nabla_{k}\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{i}}-\nabla_{k}{X}_{i\bar{i}})=\nabla_{k}\psi-G^{i\bar{i}}\nabla_{k}{X}_{i\bar{i}}.

So

(4.16)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}(1-\gamma)|Q_{i}|^{2}-C|\nabla u|^{2}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}+R

where

R=2\mathfrak{Re}\{(\nabla_{k}\psi-G^{i\bar{i}}\nabla_{k}X_{i\bar{i}})\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\}.

We derive

(4.17)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\phi-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\phi\partial_{i}\phi\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{R}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+C\sum G^{i\bar{i}}.

Let

\phi=\log(\eta/h)
where
h=1+\sup_{M}u-u
. So

G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\phi=\frac{1}{h}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}u+\frac{1}{h^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}u\partial_{i}u+\frac{1}{\eta}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\eta-\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta.

As before,
|\nabla\phi|\leq|\nabla u|/h+|\nabla\eta|/\eta
and

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\phi\partial_{i}\phi\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+\gamma}{h^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}u\partial_{i}u+\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma\eta^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta.

From (
3.29
) it follows that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}u\partial_{i}u-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\phi\partial_{i}\phi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-3\gamma}{2h^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}u\partial_{i}u-\frac{C}{\eta^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{1}|\nabla u|^{2}}{4h^{2}}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}-\frac{C}{\eta^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta.

By the concavity of
f
and assumption (
1.11
),

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}\geq

\displaystyle f(|\nabla u|^{2}{\bf{1}})-f(\Lambda)+G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{i}}\geq f(|\nabla u|^{2}{\bf{1}})-\psi-C\sum G^{i\bar{i}},

Similarly,

\begin{aligned} G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}u=\,&G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{g}_{i\bar{i}}-G^{i\bar{i}}X_{i\bar{i}}\geq-G^{i\bar{i}}X_{i\bar{i}}-C\sum G^{i\bar{i}}\end{aligned}.

Combining the above inequalities, and taking
\eta
as in the previous section, we derive

(4.19)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}|\nabla u|^{2}}{8h^{2}}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}+\frac{c_{1}}{8h^{2}}f(|\nabla u|^{2}{\bf{1}})\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{h}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}u-\frac{1}{\eta}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\eta+\frac{C}{\eta^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\bar{\partial}_{i}\eta\partial_{i}\eta

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{1}\psi}{8h^{2}}+C\sum G^{i\bar{i}}-\frac{R}{|\nabla u|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}G^{i\bar{i}}X_{i\bar{i}}+\frac{c_{1}\psi}{8h^{2}}-\frac{R}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{C}{\eta}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}.

Consequently, we obtain a bound
\eta|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C/r^{2}
if
\psi
and
X
are independent of
u
and
\partial u
.
In the general case, by (
3.21
) and (
4.11
),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}\{\nabla_{k}\psi\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\}=

\displaystyle\psi_{u}|\nabla u|^{2}+\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{k}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u+\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}+\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha k}^{l}\nabla_{l}u\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}(\psi_{u}-\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\})+\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{k}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u+\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha k}^{l}\nabla_{l}u\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}A-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\eta}\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}\eta\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}A-\frac{C|\nabla u|^{2}|D_{\zeta}\psi|}{\sqrt{\eta}}

and similarly,

(4.21)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{Re}\{\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\nabla_{k}X_{i\bar{i}}\}=

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}B-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\eta}G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{Re}\{X_{i\bar{i},\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}\eta\}

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla u|^{2}B+\frac{C|\nabla u|^{2}|D_{\zeta}X|}{\sqrt{\eta}}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}

where

A=\psi_{u}-\frac{1}{h}\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}u\}+\frac{1}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\mathfrak{Re}\{\psi_{k}\nabla_{\bar{k}}u+\psi_{\zeta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha k}^{l}\nabla_{l}u\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\},

B=G^{i\bar{i}}X_{i\bar{i},u}-\frac{1}{h}G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{Re}\{X_{i\bar{i},\zeta_{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha}u\}+\frac{1}{|\nabla u|^{2}}G^{i\bar{i}}\mathfrak{Re}\{(X_{i\bar{i}k}+X_{i\bar{i},\zeta_{\alpha}}\Gamma_{\alpha k}^{l}\nabla_{l}u)\nabla_{\bar{k}}u\}.

By assumptions (
4.1
) and (
4.3
),

(4.22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}G^{i\bar{i}}X_{i\bar{i}}+\frac{c_{1}\psi}{8h^{2}}-\frac{R}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle CH\sum G^{i\bar{i}}+CE

\displaystyle+\frac{C\varrho_{0}|\nabla u|}{\sqrt{\eta}}\sum G^{i\bar{i}}+\frac{C\varrho_{0}f(|\nabla u|^{2}{\bf{1}})}{|\nabla u|\sqrt{\eta}}

where

E=|\nabla_{z}\psi||\nabla u|^{-1}+(\psi_{u})^{-}+\psi^{+}+|D_{\zeta}\psi||\nabla u|\leq\varrho_{0}f(|\nabla u|^{2}{\bf{1}})

by (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), and

H=|\nabla_{z}X||\nabla u|^{-1}+\mbox{tr}X^{+}+\mbox{tr}(D_{u}X)^{+}+|D_{\zeta}X||\nabla u|\leq\varrho_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}

by (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
Plugging these back to (
4.22
), we obtain a bound
\eta|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C/r^{2}
at
z_{0}
from which (
4.5
) follows.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Assume both
X
and
\psi
are independent of

. Taking

we obtain

for any admissible solution

It follows that

\Big|\Big(1+\sup_{M}u-u(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\Big(1+\sup_{M}u-u(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\leq C\mbox{dist}(x,y),\;\;\forall x,y\in M

where

denotes the distance
between

and

in

. In particular,

(4.23)

\sup_{M}u-\inf_{M}u\leq Cd^{2}

where

is the diameter of

.

.

Assume

on

and take

in the previous proof. We can derive

at

5.
Existence

In this section we outline proofs of Theorems
1.6
,
1.7
.
With the aid of the
a priori
estimates in the specific forms derived in the previous sections we can extend these theorems to cover more general cases where
X
and
\psi
are allowed to depend on

u
and
\nabla u
.

The following result extends Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold. Assume that
(
1.5
), (
1.18
), (
1.11
), (
1.19
), (
1.29
), (
1.30
), (
4.1
)
and (
4.3
) hold. In addition, assume that

X=X(z,\nabla u)
and
\psi=\psi(z,\nabla u)
are both independent of
u
,
and
X(z,0)>0
on
M
.
Then there exists a unique constant
b
such that the equation

(5.1)

f(\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]))=e^{b}\psi[u]

has a unique admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
up to a constant.

Proof.

To begin with we assume
X[u]=X(z,u,\nabla u)

and
\psi[u]=\psi(z,u,\nabla u)
satisfying

(5.2)

-D_{u}X,\;D_{u}\psi\geq 0.

First we choose
A>0
sufficiently large
such that
H\equiv f(\Lambda(A\omega))>0
.
For
t\in[0,1]
consider the equation

(5.3)

f(\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]+tA\omega))=e^{tu}\psi^{t}[u]

where

\psi^{t}[u]=tH+(1-t)\psi[u]
.
Note that for
t>0
, by (
5.2
)

D_{u}(e^{tu}\psi^{t}[u])\geq Ht^{2}e^{tu}>0.

So the solution of equation (
5.3
) is unique and its
linearized operator is nonsingular for
t>0
. When
t=1
the solution is
u^{1}=0
.

Suppose
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
is the admissible solution of
equation (
5.3
)
for fixed
t>0
, and assume

\sup_{M}u\geq 0
. At a point where
u
attains its maximum value,
by (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle e^{tu}\psi^{t}(z,0,0)\leq

\displaystyle e^{tu}\psi^{t}(z,u,0)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(\Lambda(X(z,u,0)+tA\omega)\leq f(\Lambda(X(z,0,0)+tA\omega).

Therefore,

(5.4)

t\sup_{M}u\leq\sup_{z\in M}\log\frac{f(\Lambda(X(z,0,0)+A\omega)}{\psi^{t}(z,0,0)}\leq C.

Similarly, suppose
\inf_{M}u\leq 0
. Then

(5.5)

t\inf_{M}u\geq\inf_{z\in M}\log\frac{f(\Lambda(X(z,0,0))}{\psi^{t}(z,0,0)}\geq-C.

Consequently, we may apply the continuity method to obtain a unique admissible solution
u^{t}\in C^{\infty}(M)
of equation (
5.3
) for all
t\in(0,1]
with the bound

(5.6)

-C\leq t\inf_{M}u^{t}\leq t\sup_{M}u^{t}\leq C,\;\;0<t\leq 1

where
C
is independent of
t
.

By (
5.6
) we can find a sequence
t_{k}\rightarrow 0
such that
both of the following limits exist

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}t_{k}\inf_{M}u^{t_{k}}=a,\;\;\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}t_{k}\sup_{M}u^{t_{k}}=b.

Suppose now that
X
and
\psi
are both independent of
u
. By (
4.23
) we see that
a=b
and therefore

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}t_{k}u^{t_{k}}=b\;\;\mbox{on $M$}.

Moreover, by (
4.23
) and the estimates we have established there exists a subsequence of

\Big\{u^{t_{k}}-\sup_{M}u^{t_{k}}\Big\}

converging in
C^{2,\alpha}(M)

to a smooth admissible solution
u
of equation (
5.1
). Clearly,

\inf_{M}u\geq-Cd^{2},\;\;\sup_{M}u=0

where
d
is the diameter of
M
.
∎

We next consider the Dirichlet problem

(5.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} f(\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u+X[u]))=\,&\psi[u]\;\mbox{in $\bar{M}$},\\
u=\varphi\;\mbox{on $\partial M$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
(M^{n},\omega)
is assumed as in Theorem
1.6

to be a Hermitian manifold with smooth boundary

\partial M
and compact closure
\bar{M}=M\cup\partial M
, and

\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial M)
.

Theorem 5.2
.

In addition to (
1.5
), (
1.6
), (
1.18
), (
1.11
),
(
1.19
), (
4.1
), (
4.3
) and (
5.2
), assume that
there exists an admissible subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{0}(\bar{M})
satisfying

(5.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} f(\Lambda(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\underline{u}+X[\underline{u}]))\geq\,&\psi[\underline{u}]\;\mbox{in $\bar{M}$},\\
\underline{u}=\varphi\;\mbox{on $\partial M$}\end{aligned}\right.

in the viscosity sense.
The Dirichlet problem (
5.7
) admits a unique admissible solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M)\cap C^{0}(\bar{M})
.
Moreover,
u\in C^{\infty}(\bar{M})
if
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
.

Proof.

We assume
\underline{u}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{M})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
; the general case follows from approximation.
Let
u\in C^{4}(M)\cap C^{0}(\bar{M})
be an admissible solution of
problem (
5.7
).
From the comparison principle and (
5.2
) we see that
u\geq\underline{u}
on
M

and therefore

(5.9)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\leq\frac{\partial\underline{u}}{\partial\nu}\;\;\mbox{on $\partial M$}

where
\nu
denotes the exterior unit normal to
\partial M
.
By assumptions (
4.1
) and (
5.2
),

\mbox{tr}X[u]\leq\mbox{tr}X(z,\inf_{M}\underline{u},\partial u,\bar{\partial}u)\leq\varrho_{1}(z,\inf_{M}\underline{u})+\varrho_{0}(z,\inf_{M}\underline{u},|\partial u|)|\partial u|^{2}.

Since
\Delta u+\mbox{tr}X[u]\geq 0
, we have

(5.10)

\Delta u+\mbox{tr}X(z,\inf_{M}\underline{u},\partial u,\bar{\partial}u)\geq 0.

It follows from the maximum principle that
u\leq h
on
\bar{M}
where
h\in C^{\infty}(\bar{M})
is the unique solution of

(5.11)

\Delta h+\mbox{tr}X(z,\inf_{M}\underline{u},\partial h,\bar{\partial}h)=0\;\;\mbox{in $M$},\;\;h=\varphi\;\;\mbox{on $\partial M$}.

By the assumption
\varrho_{0}=\varrho_{0}(z,u,t)\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.1
) we see that problem (
5.11
) is solvable.
Combining with the gradient estimate in Section
4

(see Remark
4.6
), we have established the global
C^{1}
bound

(5.12)

\sup_{\bar{M}}|u|+\sup_{\bar{M}}|\nabla u|\leq C.

By Theorem
1.5
and Evans-Krylov Theorem we obtain the
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate

(5.13)

|u|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar{M})}\leq C.

The existence and smoothness of a unique admissible solution now follows from the standard continuity method and the classical Schauder theory for linear uniformly elliptic equations.

Remark 5.3
.

The assumption
\varrho_{0}=\varrho_{0}(z,u,t)\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.1
) is critical to the existence of solution of
problem (
5.11
).

Consider the problem in a domain
\Omega\subset\hbox{\bbbld R}^{n}

(5.14)

\Delta h+|\nabla h|^{2}+b=0\;\;\mbox{in $\Omega$},\;\;h=0\;\;\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}.

Clearly
h
satisfies
\Delta e^{h}+be^{h}=0
. Therefore, problem (
5.14
) has no solution if
b
is the first eigenvalue of
\Omega
. Indeed, let
\phi_{1}
be
the first eigenfunction

(5.15)

\Delta\phi_{1}+b\phi_{1}=0\;\;\mbox{in $\Omega$},\;\;\phi_{1}=0\;\;\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$},

normalized so that

\sup_{\Omega}\phi_{1}=1.

Note that
e^{h}>1\geq\phi_{1}
in
\Omega
since
\Delta e^{h}<0
in
\Omega
. We can find
t>1
such that

0<\inf_{\Omega}(e^{h}-t\phi_{1})<1.

Consequently, in the interior point
x_{0}
where
e^{h}-t\phi_{1}
attains the minimal value,

\Delta(e^{h}-t\phi_{1})+b(e^{h}-t\phi_{1})\geq b(e^{h}-t\phi_{1})>0

which is a contradiction.

When
u
is a radial function, in spherical coordinates equation (
5.14
)
reduces to the simple Riccati equation for
y=u^{\prime}

y^{\prime}+\frac{n-1}{r}y+y^{2}+b=0.

In the special case
n=1
and
b=1
, the solution is

u(x)=-\log\cos x

which is only defined for
-\frac{\pi}{2}<x<\frac{\pi}{2}
.

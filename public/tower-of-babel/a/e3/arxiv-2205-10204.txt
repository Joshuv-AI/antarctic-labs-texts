Planar graphs with separation are dp-minimal
J. de la Nuez González

Abstract
. We prove that given a planar embedding of a graph in the sphere the expansion of the graph structure by predicates encoding separation of vertices by simple cycles of the graph is dp-minimal.



1.
Introduction

The notion of an NIP theory is a central to classification theory.
While it is usually defined in terms of the absence of uniformly definable families of sets shattering infinite families of tuples, an alternative characterization can be given in terms of the boundedness of so called dp-rank (see
[
3
]
Section 4.2), which describes the behavior of collections of mutually indiscernible sequences under the addition of parameters.

The aim of this note is to provide a family of simple geometrical examples of non-stable theories that are dp-minimal, i.e. for which the dp-rank equals one.
We will assume the reader is familiar with indiscernible sequences and other basics of model theory. We refer to
[
4
]
for a general introduction to model theory and to
[
3
]
for an introduction to NIP theories. We work with indiscernible sequences indexed by arbitrary infinite linear orders.

Definition 1.1
.

Two indiscernible sequences
I=(a_{j})_{j\in J}
and
I^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in J^{\prime}}
are mutually indiscernible if
I
is indiscernible over
I^{\prime}
and viceversa.

Definition 1.2
.

A theory
T
is dp-minimal if for any model of
M
of
T
, any two mutually indiscernible sequences of tuples
(a_{i}^{1})_{i\in J_{1}}
and
(a_{i}^{2})_{j\in J_{2}}
and any element
b
in
M
there is
l\in\{1,2\}
such that

(a_{i}^{l})_{i\in J_{l}}
is indiscernible over
b
.

Fix a countable planar graph
G=(V,E)
and consider a planar embedding
\iota:|G|\to S^{2}
of its geometric realization into the sphere. With
\iota
we associate an expansion
G^{\iota}
of the graph structure
G
as follows. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we introduce a new
(n+2)
-ary predicate symbol
R_{n}
and we let
G^{\iota}\models R_{n}(a,b,c_{1,},c_{2},\cdots c_{n})
if and only
c_{1},c_{2},\cdots c_{n}
is the sequence of vertices read along a simple cycle
\gamma
of the graph, possibly with cyclically contiguous repetitions,
\{a,b\}\cap\{c_{1},c_{2},\cdots c_{n}\}=\emptyset
and
\iota(a),\iota(b)
lie on the same connected component of

S^{2}\setminus\iota(|\gamma|)
.

We prove the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

The theory
T=Th(G^{\iota})
is dp-minimal

Note that the theory of a pure planar graph
G
is known to be stable. In fact
[
1
]
shows that much more in general the theory of any superflat graph is dp-minimal. Superflat graphs are also long known to be stable (see
[
2
]
). The structure
G^{\iota}
in generally is not.

Basic observations and definitions

From now on we will work in the monster model
\mathbb{G}^{\iota}
of
G^{\iota}
, the elements of whose universe we will keep referring to as vertices. We will refer to a tuple
(c_{1},c_{2}\dots c_{n})
, possibly with repetitions, read along a simple path in the graph as an
n
cycle or simply a cycle. If we drop the condition that

and

Given an

outside of

we say that

does not separate

and

. We also write
\mathbf{D}^{+}_{c}(a):=\{b\,|\,a\sim_{c}b\}
and

\mathbf{D}^{-}_{c}(a):=\{b\,|\,a\nsim_{c}b\}
.

Let us single out some basic properties encoded in
T
resulting from the definition of

:

(A)

If
a_{1},a_{2}\cdots a_{m}
is a path and

a_{1}\nsim_{c}a_{m}
for some cycle

, then there are
j<j^{\prime}\in\{1,2,\dots n\}
such that

,

,
a_{j}\nsim_{c}a_{j^{\prime}}
, and
a_{j+1},a_{j+2},\dots a_{j^{\prime}-1}
is a subpath of

.

(B)

Given two cycles

such that

lies on a single side of
c_{2}
, then the same is true if we exchange the roles of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
. If this is not the case, then we say that
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

cross
.

(C)

If
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
do not cross, then
\sim_{c_{1},c_{2}}
divides

in at most three classes, i.e. there is one side of
\gamma_{1}
and another of
\gamma_{2}
which are nested.

Remark 2.1
.

By

if the intersection of

and
c_{2}
is a single path (in particular, if it contains a single vertex), then
c_{2}
and
c_{2}
cannot cross.

Cages and nests

By a

•

Notice that

for ordered pairs of indices

if and only if

and

.

By a

(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}
of simple cycles of bounded length that are either disjoint
or intersect pair-wise in a unique vertex

in

separates

from

.

Let

in the second. Write

.
Now, for some suitable choice of a side

. In analogy with the previous definition, for

,

.

3.
Properties of large cages

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a large cage or nest. Let
a,b
be two elements such that either:

•

a\in int(\mathcal{D})
and
b\in ext(\mathcal{D})

•

a\in\delta_{i}
and
b\in\delta_{j}
for distinct
i,j

Then every path from
a
to
b
must pass through
pol(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a path from
a
to
b
and assume without loss of generality that
p
does not cross
pol(\mathcal{D})
. Now, there exist an infinity of cycles with pair-wise intersection equal to
pol(\mathcal{D})
of the form
\delta_{k,l}
separating

a
from
b
. Since
p
must intersect either
\mathring{\delta}_{k}
or
\mathring{\delta}_{l}
in each of those cases implies
p
has to contain infinitely many vertices. This settles the first claim.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a large cage and
c
a cycle. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

c\subseteq ext(\mathcal{C})

(2)

c\subseteq\widebar{int}(\mathcal{C})

(3)

c
is a concatenation
d*e
, where
e
and
d
are paths between the two poles of
\mathcal{C}
, with
d\subseteq ext(\mathcal{C})
and
e\subseteq\widebar{int}(\mathcal{C})
.
If
pol(\mathcal{C})\nsubseteq c
, then there are
c
can intersect
\mathring{\gamma}_{j}
for at most one value of
j
.

Proof.

Indeed, if
c
contains points both in
int(\mathcal{C})
and
\widebar{int}(\mathcal{C})^{c}
, then by the previous Lemma it must intersect
pol(\mathcal{C})
two times and can only exit
int(\mathcal{C})
once.
∎

A similar argument shows:

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{N}=(\nu_{j})_{j\in J}
is a large nest, then for any cycle
c
either
c\subseteq\widebar{int}(\mathcal{N})
or
c\subseteq ext(\mathcal{N})
. In the former case
c
can intersect
\mathring{\nu}_{j}
for at most one value of
j
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
c
is a cycle and
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
a large sequence disjoint from

and fitting into a large cage or nest
\mathcal{D}=(\delta_{l})_{l\in L}
. If we partition

into two sets according to the side of

lies the resulting partition contains a subinterval

of

. Moreover:

•

If
\mathcal{D}
is a nest and

, then

is empty or a singleton.

•

If
\mathcal{D}
is a cage and

is as in case
3
of Corollary

, then both

and

are unbounded and non-empty intervals.

Proof.

By Observation
2.5
we may assume

is disjoint from
c
.

Consider first the case in which
\mathcal{D}
is a cage and
pol(\mathcal{D})\subset c
and. Adding the one or two subpaths of

that lie in
\widebar{int}(\mathcal{D})
to
\mathcal{D}
we obtain a new cage by Observation

and the result easily follows.

Take

in
I
. If

separates
\{a_{i_{0}},a_{i_{2}}\}
from
\{a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{3}}\}
.
Then some cycle of the form

(if

is a nest) or

(if

is a cage) separates

from
\{a_{i_{2}},a_{i_{3}}\}
and thus must cross

. This impossible unless

is a cage and

in which case it is ruled out by the argument above.

Suppose now that

separates
\{a_{i_{0}},a_{i_{1}}\}
from
\{a_{i_{2}},a_{i_{3}}\}
.

If
\mathcal{D}
a cage then there are

in

.

If

and both

Lemma 3.5
.

Let each of
\mathcal{D}=(\delta_{j})_{j\in J}
and
\mathcal{E}=(\epsilon_{k})_{k\in K}
be either a large cage or a large nest with a pole. Then either
pol(\mathcal{E})\subseteq\widebar{int}(\mathcal{D})
or
pol(\mathcal{E})\subseteq ext(\mathcal{D})
. Moreover if

pol(\mathcal{D})\neq pol(\mathcal{E})
then one of the following mutually exclusive possibilities hold:

•

•

up to exchanging
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}

there exist

such that
\widebar{int}(\mathcal{D})\setminus pol(\mathcal{E})\subset E_{j,k}
.

Proof.

If
pol(\mathcal{E})\cap int(\mathcal{D})
and

are both non-empty, then Lemma

implies the existence of

contained in
\mathring{\epsilon}_{i},\mathring{\epsilon}_{j}
for different

: a contradiction, hence the first claim.

For the second claim suppose that
|pol(\mathcal{D})|=max\{|pol(\mathcal{D})|,|pol(\mathcal{E})|\}
.

Consider first the case in which
pol(\mathcal{E})\subseteq ext(\mathcal{D})
.
Take
j^{\prime}<k^{\prime}<l^{\prime}
in
K
. In view of Corollary
3.2
we must have
\epsilon_{j^{\prime}}\cup\epsilon_{k^{\prime}}\cup\epsilon_{l^{\prime}}\subseteq ext(\mathcal{D})

and then either

or

. If the former does not hold for any

, then

.

Assume now that

. Choose

in
J
such that

. We claim that

. Indeed, otherwise there are infinite sequences

and

in

such that for every

\epsilon_{j_{n}}
or
\epsilon_{k_{n}}
is not contained in

. Since

intersects

, it follows that
\delta_{j_{0}}\cup\delta_{k_{0}}
and

must intersect in at least two vertices, one of which must lie outside of

.
This implies that one of

or

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let

4.
Indiscernibles and cages

We start with a couple of observations regarding cages and indiscernible sequences of elements.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(a_{j})_{j\in J}
be a large sequence of elements indiscernible over some set
B
of parameters and assume that

(a_{j})_{j\in J}
fits into a large nest or cage
\mathcal{D}
. Then
B\subseteq ext(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

We will focus on the case in which
\mathcal{D}=(\delta_{l})_{l\in L}
is a cage, since the case in which it is a nest is formally identical.
Assume the result is false and pick
l_{2}<l_{3}\in L
such that
b\in C_{l_{2},l_{3}}
.
Now, choose
l_{0},l_{1},l_{4},l_{5}
such that
l_{0}<l_{1}<l_{2}
and
l_{3}<l_{4}<l_{5}
. By definition, there exists
j_{0}<j_{1}<j_{3}<j_{4}
in
J
such that

,

.
Let

be the maximum length of the cycles

and consider the following formula over

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\cong\exists z_{1}\dots z_{N}\,R_{N}(x_{1},b,z)\wedge\neg R_{N}(x_{2},b,z)\wedge\neg R_{N}(x_{3},b,z)

∎

The cycle
\gamma_{l_{1},l_{3}}
witnesses
\models\phi(a_{j_{1}},a_{j_{3}},a_{j_{4}})
.
On the other, after removing finitely many components from
(a_{j})_{j\in J}
and
\mathcal{D}
we may assume one can add

to the sequence
(a_{j})_{j\in J}
between the position of

and

then implies the formula

cannot hold, contradicting indiscernibility of

Observation 4.2
.

Assume that
(a_{j})_{j\in J}
is an indiscernible sequence and there is some infinite

By compactness, if any finite subsequence of a sequence

.

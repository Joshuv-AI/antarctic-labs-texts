Induced charge generated by a Coulomb impurity in transition metal dichalcogenides
V. K. Ivanov, I. S. Terekhov

Abstract
We calculate analytically the charge density ρ ind ​ ( 𝒓 ) \rho_{\text{ind}}(\bm{r}) at small distances induced by a Coulomb impurity in two-dimensional transition metal dichalcogenides. The calculations were performed exactly in the coupling constant. We found the first three leading terms of the charge density asymptotics at small distances. Using this result, we demonstrate that the impurity with charge number Z = 1 Z=1 remains subcritical at any value of the coupling constant, whereas impurities with higher charge number could become supercritical at certain coupling constant values. Such a behavior is similar to that in graphene.



I
Introduction

In the pioneering work of Geim and Novoselov
Novoselov et al. 2004
the properties of the first fabricated two-dimensional Dirac material (graphene) were investigated. This work became an incentive for a theoretical and experimental study of both graphene and other two-dimensional Dirac materials. In particular, such materials include transition metal dichalcogenides (TMD) (see, for example
Xiao et al. 2012
), which, in contrast to graphene, are two-dimensional semiconductors with characteristic band gap of order of
1
eV. The application of such materials is promising in many areas of technology, from optics and nanoelectronics, to spintronics
Chernozatonskii and Artyukh 2018
. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the electronic properties of these materials. In particular, the response of an electron gas to the external impurities is of interest, since they affect the conductivity of the material.

In the present paper, we study the response of an electron gas in TMD to an external Coulomb impurity, namely, we calculate the induced charge density. The induced charge can be described as a polarization of the electron gas ground state by an external field. This process is analogous to the vacuum polarization, studied in quantum electrodynamics in the field of an atomic nucleus (see
Wichmann and Kroll 1956
;
Brown et al. 1975
;
Mil’shtein and Strakhovenko 1983
;
Zeldovich and Popov 1972
), where the occurrence of an induced charge leads to modification of the potential on the distances of the Compton wavelength of an electron. In the case of Dirac materials, the induced charge in graphene was discussed in many papers (see
DiVincenzo and Mele 1984
;
Nomura and MacDonald 2007
;
Ando 2006
;
Hwang et al. 2007
;
Katsnelson 2006
;
Shytov et al. 2007
;
Pereira et al. 2007
;
Biswas et al. 2007
;
Fogler et al. 2007
;
Terekhov et al. 2008
;
Uchoa et al. 2008
, see also a review
Pereira et al. 2009
).
For TMD, in the works
Voronina et al. 2019
;
Sveshnikov et al. 2019
, numerical results were obtained for the induced charge density with exact account of an impurity field. Moreover, in these papers the authors investigated the behavior of the induced charge in supercritical fields, in which the field creates an electron-hole pair.

We calculate analytically the induced charge density induced by a Coulomb impurity on distances much smaller than the Compton wavelength
\lambda
of an electron in TMD. The calculations are performed exactly in the impurity field, using the convenient integral representation of an electron Green’s function in a Coulomb field
Terekhov et al. 2008
and calculation techniques developed in
Mil’shtein and Strakhovenko 1983
. We find the leading, next-to-leading, and next-to-next-to-leading terms of asymptotics in the parameter
r/\lambda
of the induced charge density. We find the value of a charge, localized on the impurity, exactly in the impurity field. This allows us to demonstrate, that when the value impurity field is in the vicinity of the critical one, the induced and impurity charges become comparable. Moreover, we show that, similar to the graphene case, for an impurity with charge number
Z=1
, its field remains subcritical at any strengths of the field.

II
Induced charge density

The induced charge density can be expressed using an electron Green’s function in an external field:

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{ind}}({\bm{r}})=-ieN\int\limits_{C}\frac{d\epsilon}{2\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\{G({\bm{r}},{\bm{r}}|\epsilon)\}\,,

(1)

where
e
is an electron charge, while a coefficient
N=4
reflects spin and valley degeneracy. The integration contour depends on the chemical potential value (see below). The electron Green’s function
G({\bm{r}},{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|\epsilon)
in a Coulomb field for TDM obeys the next equation
Xiao et al. 2012
:

\left[\epsilon-v_{F}\sigma\cdot\bm{p}-\Delta\sigma_{z}+\frac{Ze^{2}}{r}\right]G({\bm{r}},{\bm{r}}^{\prime}|\epsilon)=\delta(\bm{r}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime}),

(2)

where
{\bm{p}}=-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)
is a momentum operator,
{\bm{\sigma}}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y})
,
\sigma_{i}
are Pauli
\sigma
-matrices,
v_{F}
is a constant with the dimension of velocity, which depends on the material,
2\Delta
is the band gap,
Z|e|
is the impurity charge. In (
2
) we neglect the spin-orbit interaction, since the energy of this interaction is much smaller than
\Delta
, see
Xiao et al. 2012
.

In
Terekhov et al. 2008
a convenient integral representation of Green’s function was found, see
(
Terekhov et al. 2008
, Eq.(5))
. Using this representation and equation (
1

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{ind}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{eN\Delta}{\hbar v_{F}\pi^{2}r}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\epsilon\,ds\,f(s,y)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle f(s,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-y\cosh s}\bigg(2Z\alpha\cos(\mu s)\coth sI_{2\gamma}(y)

(4)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sin(\mu s)\frac{\epsilon y}{k}I^{\prime}_{2\gamma}(y)\bigg)\,,

k=\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+1}\,
,
y=2rk\Delta/(\hbar v_{F}\sinh s)
,

\mu=2Z\alpha\epsilon/k\,
,
I_{\gamma}(y)
is the modified Bessel function of the 1-st kind,
I^{\prime}_{2\gamma}(y)=dI_{2\gamma}(y)/dy
,
\gamma=\sqrt{(l+1/2)^{2}-(Z\alpha)^{2}}
,
\alpha=e^{2}/(\hbar v_{F}\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is a permittivity of the substrate. When obtaining this formula, the integration contour

\epsilon

\alpha

v_{F}
and
\varepsilon

\alpha
is not small since
v_{F}\sim c/200
, where

is the speed of light
Xiao et al. 2012
. Below we set
\hbar=v_{F}=1
.

The expression (
3

Mil’shtein and Strakhovenko 1983
;
Terekhov et al. 2008
, since it contains divergencies when energy is large and parameter
s
is small. To obtain a regular expression, it is necessary to renormalize the charge density (
3
)
Terekhov et al. 2008
. We imply, that the valence band is fully occupied, while the conductance band is empty (chemical potential is equal to zero). Therefore, for subcritical external field, the total induced charge should be equal to zero. Since there is no empty states in the valence band, the renormalization condition takes the following form:

\displaystyle Q_{\text{ind}}=\int d^{2}r\,\rho_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})=0,

(5)

where
\rho_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})
is the renormalized induced charge density. A detailed description of the renormalization procedure can be found in
Terekhov et al. 2008
;
Mil’shtein and Strakhovenko 1983
, where the calculations were performed for the case of atomic nucleus in three-dimensional quantum electrodynamics and for graphene. Let us find the renormalized expression for the induced charge density. Following
Mil’shtein and Strakhovenko 1983
, we go to the momentum representation:

\displaystyle\rho(\bm{q})=\int d^{2}r\,e^{i\bm{q}\bm{r}}\rho(\bm{r}).

(6)

Integrating over the directions of
\bm{r}
and introducing a new variable

\displaystyle\rho(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{eN}{\pi}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}ds\,dy\,d\epsilon\frac{\sinh s}{k}\times

(7)

\displaystyle f(s,y)J_{0}\left(\frac{qy\sinh s}{2k}\right),

where

is the Bessel function of the 1-st kind. In momentum representation, the renormalization condition (
5

\displaystyle\lim_{\bm{q}\to 0}\rho_{\text{ren}}(\bm{q})=0,

(8)

III
Asymptotic behavior at
r\ll 1/\Delta

Let us find the behavior of the induced charge density at
R\ll 1
. We begin with
\rho^{(1)}_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})
. It is convenient to represent it in a following way:

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})=\left(\rho^{(1)}_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})-\tilde{\rho}(\bm{r})\right)+\tilde{\rho}(\bm{r}).

(20)

Here

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\bm{r})=\frac{eNZ\alpha\Delta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}R^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x\,J_{0}(x)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\epsilon\bigg\{-\frac{2R}{x}+

\displaystyle\frac{1+4\epsilon^{2}R^{2}/x^{2}}{\epsilon\sqrt{1+x^{2}/(4\epsilon^{2}R^{2})}}\ln\left(\frac{x}{2\epsilon R}+\sqrt{1+\frac{x^{2}}{4\epsilon^{2}R^{2}}}\right)\bigg\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\bm{r})=eNZ\alpha\frac{\pi}{8}\,\delta(\bm{r}).

(22)

To obtain this result, we perform the Fourier transformation, integrate over

To calculate the difference

\displaystyle 1\ll\lambda\ll R^{-1},\quad\lambda R\ll\mu\ll 1\,.

(23)

\rho^{(1)}_{\text{ren}}(\bm{r})-\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\bm{r})\approx\frac{eNZ\alpha\Delta^{2}}{8}\left(2\ln R+2\gamma_{E}+1\right),

(24)

where
\gamma_{E}\approx 0.577
is the Euler constant. By summing the contributions (

IV
Conclusion

We have considered the behavior of the charge density induced by a Coulomb impurity in TMDs. We have calculated leading, next-to-leading and next-to-next-to-leading asymptotics in the parameter
r\Delta\ll 1
. The calculations have been performed exactly in
Z\alpha
. We have demonstrated, that, similar to the case of graphene, an impurity with charge number
Z=1
does not create a supercritical field, for arbitrary value of coupling constant
\alpha
. The reason is the strong screening of the impurity. The value of the induced charge, localized on the impurity, becomes comparable to the charge of the impurity itself for
Z\alpha\approx 0.49
. For such fields, a self-consistent equation for the
potential of the impurity and the induced charge should be considered. For graphene, such equation was obtained and solved. However, in TMDs a distributed charge should be taken into account in the equation for self-consistent potential. This problem will be investigated in the future.

Similar to the graphene, impurities with
Z>1
can turn into supercritical regime (
Z\alpha>1/2
). Note, that the calculations were performed for a point-like charge, however, at the scale of the lattice spacing it is necessary to take into account the difference between real potential and the Coulomb model, which can significantly modify the induced charge behavior on these scales. This problem will also be considered in the future work.

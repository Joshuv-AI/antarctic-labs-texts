A tutorial introduction to quantum stochastic master equations based on the qubit/photon system
Pierre Rouchon

Abstract
From the key composite quantum system made of a two-level system (qubit) and a harmonic oscillator (photon) with resonant or dispersive interactions, one derives the corresponding quantum Stochastic Master Equations (SME) when either the qubits or the photons are measured. Starting with an elementary discrete-time formulation based on explicit formulae for the interaction propagators, one shows how to include measurement imperfections and decoherence. This qubit/photon quantum system illustrates the Kraus-map structure of general discrete-time SME governing the dynamics of an open quantum system subject to measurement back-action and decoherence induced by the environment. Then, on the qubit/photon system, one explains the passage to a continuous-time mathematical model where the measurement signal is either a continuous real-value signal (typically homodyne or heterodyne signal) or a discontinuous and integer-value signal obtained from a counter. During this derivation, the Kraus map formulation is preserved in an infinitesimal way. Such a derivation provides also an equivalent Kraus-map formulation to the continuous-time SME usually expressed as stochastic differential equations driven either by Wiener or Poisson processes. From such Kraus-map formulation, simple linear numerical integration schemes are derived that preserve the positivity and the trace of the density operator, i.e. of the quantum state.



Keywords:

Open quantum systems, decoherence, quantum stochastic master equation, Lindblad master equation, Kraus-map, quantum channel, quantum filtering, Wiener process, Poisson process, qubit/photon composite system. Positivity and trace preserving numerical scheme.

2.4
Stochastic Master Equation (SME) in discrete-time

In fact, the general structure of discrete-time SME can always be constructed from the knowledge of a quantum channel (trace preserving completely positive map) having the following Kraus decomposition (which is not unique)

\mathbb{K}(\rho)=\sum_{\mu}{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}^{\dagger}\quad\text{where $\sum_{\mu}{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}={\mathbf{I}}$}

and a left stochastic matrix
(\eta_{y,\mu})
where
y
corresponds to the different imperfect measurement outcomes.
Set
\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)=\sum_{\mu}\eta_{y,\mu}{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
.
The SME associated to
\mathbb{K}
and
\eta
reads

\rho_{k+1}=\frac{\mathbb{K}_{y_{k}}(\rho_{k})}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y_{k}}(\rho_{k})\right)}\quad\text{where $y_{k}=y$ with probability ${\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho_{k})\right)$}

Notice that
\mathbb{K}=\sum_{y}\mathbb{K}_{y}
since
\eta
is left stochastic.
Here the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is arbitrary and can be of infinite dimension, the Kraus operator
{\mathbf{M}}_{\mu}
are bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
and
\rho
is a density operator on
\mathcal{H}
(Hermitian, trace-class with trace one, non-negative).

To recover the previous discrete-time SME (
6
), use the above general formulae with the quantum channel
\mathbb{K}(\rho)={\mathbf{M}}_{g}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{g}^{\dagger}+{\mathbf{M}}_{e}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{e}^{\dagger}
and the left stochastic matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{g,g}=1-\eta_{e}&\eta_{g,e}=\eta_{g}\\
\eta_{e,g}=\eta_{e}&\eta_{e,e}=1-\eta_{g}\\
\end{array}\right)

where
\eta_{g}
(resp.
\eta_{e}
) is the error probability associated to outcome
g
(resp.
e
). Notice that in (
6
) the error model is symmetric with
\eta_{g}=\eta_{e}
corresponding to
\eta\in[0,1]
.

Contrarily to section
2
, photons measure here a qubit.

3.1
Qubits measured by photons: dispersive interaction and discrete-time

The qubit wave function is denoted by
\left|\psi\right\rangle
. The photons, before interacting with the qubit, are in the coherent state
\left|i\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle
with
\alpha
real and strictly positive. The interaction is dispersive according to (
4
).
After the interaction and just before the measurement performed on the photons, the composite qubit/photon wave function
\left|\Psi\right\rangle
reads:

\Big(\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|e^{-i\theta{\mathbf{n}}}+\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|e^{i\theta{\mathbf{n}}}\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle\left|i\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle=\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle~\left|ie^{-i\theta}\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle+\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle~\left|ie^{i\theta}\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle

since for any coherent state
\left|\beta\right\rangle
of complex amplitude
\beta
,
e^{i\theta{\mathbf{n}}}\left|\beta\right\rangle
is also a coherent state of complex amplitude

e^{i\theta}\beta
(
e^{i\theta{\mathbf{n}}}\left|\beta\right\rangle=\left|e^{i\theta}\beta\right\rangle
).

Assume that the perfect measurement outcome
y
belongs to
\mathbb{R}
and corresponds to the phase-plane observable
{\mathbf{Q}}=\frac{{\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}}
having the entire real line as spectrum. Its spectral decomposition reads formally
{\mathbf{Q}}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q\left|q\right\rangle\!\left\langle q\right|dq
where
\left|q\right\rangle
is the wave function associated to the eigen-value
q
.
\left|q\right\rangle
is not a usual wave function, i.e., in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
, but one has formally
\left\langle q\right|\!\left.q^{\prime}\right\rangle=\delta(q-q^{\prime})
(see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).
Since

\left|ie^{\pm i\theta}\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{iq\alpha\cos\theta}e^{-\frac{(q\pm\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left|q\right\rangle dq,

we have

\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle~\left|ie^{-i\theta}\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle+\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle~\left|ie^{i\theta}\tfrac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2}}\right\rangle\\
=\tfrac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{iq\alpha\cos\theta}\left(e^{-\frac{(q-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle+e^{-\frac{(q+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\right)\left|q\right\rangle dq.

Thus

\left|\psi_{k+1}\right\rangle=e^{iy_{k}\alpha\cos\theta}\frac{e^{-\frac{(y_{k}-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle+e^{-\frac{(y_{k}+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle}{\sqrt{e^{-(y_{k}-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}|\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle|^{2}+e^{-(y_{k}+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}|\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle|^{2}}}

where
y_{k}\in[y,y+dy]
with probability
\frac{e^{-(y-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}|\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle|^{2}+e^{-(y+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}|\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi_{k}\right\rangle|^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}dy
.

The density operator formulation reads then

\rho_{k+1}=\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{y_{k}}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{y_{k}}^{\dagger}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{y_{k}}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{y_{k}}^{\dagger}\right)}\quad\text{where $y_{k}\in[y,y+dy]$ with probability ${\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}\right)dy$}

and measurement Kraus operators

{\mathbf{M}}_{y}=\tfrac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}e^{-\frac{(y-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+\tfrac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}e^{-\frac{(y+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{2}}\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|.

Notice that

{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-(y-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}\left\langle g\right|\rho\left|g\right\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-(y+\alpha\sin\theta)}\left\langle e\right|\rho\left|e\right\rangle

(7)

and
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{M}}_{y}~dy=\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|={\mathbf{I}}
.

For
\alpha\neq 0
, one has almost sure convergence towards
\left|g\right\rangle
or
\left|e\right\rangle
deduced from the following Lyapunov function

V(\rho)=\sqrt{\left\langle g\right|\rho\left|g\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|\rho\left|e\right\rangle}

and

\mathbb{E}\left(V(\rho_{k+1})~\Big|~\rho_{k}\right)=e^{-\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}~V(\rho_{k}).

Assume that the detection of
y
is not perfect. The probability density of
y
knowing that the perfect detection is
q
is also a Gaussian given by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\sigma}}e^{-\frac{(y-q)^{2}}{\sigma}}
for some error parameter
\sigma>0
.
Then the above Markov process becomes

\rho_{k+1}=\frac{\mathbb{K}_{y_{k}}(\rho_{k})}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y_{k}}(\rho_{k})\right)}

where

\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\sigma}}e^{-\frac{(y-q)^{2}}{\sigma}}{\mathbf{M}}_{q}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{q}^{{\dagger}}~dq

Standard computations show that

\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(1+\sigma)}}\left(e^{-\frac{(y-\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{1+\sigma}}\left\langle g|\rho|g\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+e^{-\frac{(y+\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}{1+\sigma}}\left\langle e|\rho|e\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\right.\\
\left.+e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{1+\sigma}-(\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}}\big(\left\langle e|\rho|g\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+\left\langle g|\rho|e\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\big)\right).

3.2
Dispersive interaction and continuous-time diffusive limit

Consider the above Markov process with perfect detection
y\in\mathbb{R}
. From (
7
), one gets

\mathbb{E}\left(y_{k}~\Big|~\rho_{k}=\rho\right)\triangleq\overline{y}=-\alpha\sin\theta~{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right),~\mathbb{E}\left(y_{k}^{2}~\Big|~\rho_{k}=\rho\right)\triangleq\overline{y^{2}}=1/2+(\alpha\sin\theta)^{2}.

When
0<\alpha\sin\theta=\epsilon\ll 1
, we have up-to third order terms versus
\epsilon y
,

\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}\right)}=\frac{(\cosh(\epsilon y)-\sinh(\epsilon y)\mathbf{\sigma}_{z})\rho(\cosh(\epsilon y)-\sinh(\epsilon y)\mathbf{\sigma}_{z})}{\cosh(2\epsilon y)-\sinh(2\epsilon y){\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)}\\
\approx\frac{\rho-\epsilon y(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z})+(\epsilon y)^{2}(\rho+\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z})}{1-2\epsilon y{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)+2(\epsilon y)^{2}}\\
=\rho+(\epsilon y)^{2}\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho\Big)+\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\rho\Big)\Big(-\epsilon y-2(\epsilon y)^{2}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\Big).

Replacing
\epsilon^{2}y^{2}
by its expectation value independent of
\rho
one gets, up to third order terms versus
\epsilon y
and
\epsilon
:

\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{y}\rho{\mathbf{M}}_{y}^{\dagger}\right)}\approx\rho+\tfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho\Big)+\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\rho\Big)\Big(-\epsilon y-\epsilon^{2}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\Big).

Set
\epsilon^{2}=2dt
and
\epsilon y=-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)dt-dW
.
Since by construction

\mathbb{E}\left(\epsilon y_{k}~\Big|~\rho_{k}=\rho\right)=-\epsilon^{2}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\left((\epsilon y_{k})^{2}~\Big|~\rho_{k}=\rho\right)=\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon^{4}

one has
\mathbb{E}\left(dW~\Big|~\rho\right)=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left(dW^{2}~\Big|~\rho\right)=dt
up to order
4
versus
\epsilon
.
Thus for
dt
very small, we recover the following diffusive SME

\rho_{t+dt}=\rho_{t}+dt\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho\Big)+\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\right)\rho\Big)\Big(dy_{t}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\right)dt\Big)

with
dy_{t}=2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\right)dt+dW_{t}
replacing
-\epsilon y
and
dy_{t}^{2}=dW_{t}^{2}=dt
according to Ito rules.

With measurement errors parameterized by
\sigma>0
, the partial Kraus map

\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(1+\sigma)}}\left(e^{-\frac{(y-\epsilon)^{2}}{1+\sigma}}\left\langle g|\rho|g\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+e^{-\frac{(y+\epsilon)^{2}}{1+\sigma}}\left\langle e|\rho|e\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\right.\\
\left.+e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{1+\sigma}-\epsilon^{2}}\big(\left\langle e|\rho|g\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+\left\langle g|\rho|e\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\big)\right)

yields

\mathbb{E}\left(y_{k}~\Big|~\rho_{k}\right)\triangleq\overline{y}=-\epsilon{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right),~\mathbb{E}\left(y_{k}^{2}~\Big|~\rho_{k}\right)\triangleq\overline{y^{2}}=(1+\sigma)/2+\epsilon^{2}.

From

\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)\\
=\frac{e^{-\frac{y^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}}}{2\sqrt{\pi(1+\sigma)}}\Big(\cosh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)\big(\rho+\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\big)-\sinh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)\big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\big)+e^{-\frac{\sigma\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}}\big(\rho-\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\big)\Big).

and

{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)\right)=\frac{e^{-\frac{y^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}}}{2\sqrt{\pi(1+\sigma)}}\left(\cosh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)-\sinh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big){\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\right)

one gets

\frac{\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)\right)}=\frac{\left(1+\frac{e^{-\tfrac{\sigma\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}}}{\cosh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)}\right)\rho+\left(1+\frac{e^{-\tfrac{\sigma\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}}}{\cosh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)}\right)\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\tanh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big)(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z})}{2\left(1-\tanh\big(\tfrac{2y\epsilon}{1+\sigma}\big){\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\right)}.

Up to third order terms versus
\epsilon y
, one has then

\frac{\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)\right)}=\rho+\left(\tfrac{(\epsilon y)^{2}}{(1+\sigma)^{2}}+\tfrac{\sigma\epsilon^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho)-\left(\tfrac{\epsilon y}{1+\sigma}+\tfrac{2(\epsilon y)^{2}}{(1+\sigma)^{2}}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\right)\big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\rho\big).

Replacing
\epsilon^{2}y^{2}
by its average independent of
\rho
one gets

\frac{\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{K}_{y}(\rho)\right)}=\rho+\tfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho)-\left(\tfrac{\epsilon y}{1+\sigma}+\tfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{1+\sigma}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\right)\big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho+\rho\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\rho\big).

Set
\epsilon^{2}=2dt
and
\epsilon y=-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)dt-\sqrt{1+\sigma}~dW
.
Since by construction

\mathbb{E}\left(\epsilon y~\Big|~\rho\right)=-\epsilon^{2}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\left((\epsilon y)^{2}~\Big|~\rho\right)=\epsilon^{2}/(1+\sigma)+\epsilon^{4}

one has

\mathbb{E}\left(dW~\Big|~\rho\right)=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left(dW^{2}~\Big|~\rho\right)=dt
. Thus for
dt
very small, we recover the following SME with detection efficiency
\eta=\frac{1}{1+\sigma}

\rho_{t+dt}=\rho_{t}+dt\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-\rho\Big)+\sqrt{\eta}\Big(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}-2{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}\right)\rho\Big)dW_{t}

with
dy_{t}=\sqrt{\eta}{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\right)+dW_{t}
corresponding to
-\epsilon y/\sqrt{1+\sigma}
and
dW_{t}
a Wiener process satisfying Ito rules
dW_{t}^{2}=dt
.

Convergence towards either
\left|g\right\rangle
or
\left|e\right\rangle
is based on the following Lyapunov fonction
V(\rho)=\sqrt{1-{\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)^{2}}
. According to Ito rules, one has

dV=-\frac{zdz}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}-\frac{dz^{2}}{2(1-z^{2})^{3/2}}=-\frac{zdz}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}-2\eta^{2}Vdt

where
z={\rm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{\sigma}_{z}\rho\right)
,
dz=2\eta(1-z^{2})dW
and
dz^{2}=4\eta^{2}(1-z^{2})^{2}dt
.
Since
\mathbb{E}\left(dz~\Big|~z\right)=0
,
\bar{V}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left(V(z_{t})~\Big|~z_{0}\right)
converges exponentially to
0
since governed by the linear differential equation

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{V}_{t}=-2\eta^{2}\bar{V}_{t},\quad\bar{V}_{0}=V(z_{0}).

For more general and precise results on diffusive SME corresponding to QND measurements and measurement-based feedback issues see
[
10
,
32
,
15
]
.

3.3
Diffusive SME

As studied in
[
8
]
, the general form of diffusive SME admits the following Ito formulation:

\displaystyle d\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(-i[{\mathbf{H}},\rho_{t}]+\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu})\right)dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu}\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}\left({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}-{\rm{Tr}}\left(({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}+{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger})\rho_{t}\right)\rho_{t}\right)dW_{\nu,t},

\displaystyle dy_{\nu,t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)dt+dW_{\nu,t}

with efficiencies
\eta_{\nu}\in[0,1]
and
dW_{\nu,t}
being independent Wiener processes.
Here the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is arbitrary,
{\mathbf{H}}
is Hermitian and
{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}
are arbitrary operators of
\mathcal{H}
not necessarily Hermitian. Each label
\mu
such that
\eta_{\mu}=0
corresponds here to a decoherence channel that can be seen as an unread measurement performed by a sub-system belonging to the environment, see
[
25
, chapter 4]
.

With Ito rules, this SME admits also the following equivalent formulation:

\rho_{t+dt}=\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu}){\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu}){\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt\right)}

with

{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}={\mathbf{I}}+\left(-i{\mathbf{H}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\right)dt+\sum_{\nu}\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}dy_{\nu,t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}.

Moreover
dy_{\nu,t}=s_{\nu,t}\sqrt{dt}
follows the following probability density knowing
\rho_{t}
:

\hskip-10.00002pt\mathbb{P}\Big(\left(s_{\nu,t}\in[s_{\nu},s_{\nu}+ds_{\nu}]\right)_{\nu}~|~\rho_{t}\Big)={\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{s\sqrt{dt}}~\rho_{t}{\mathbf{M}}_{s\sqrt{dt}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu}){\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt\right)\prod_{\nu}\tfrac{e^{-\frac{s_{\nu}^{2}}{2}}ds_{\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

that remains a linear function of
\rho
, as imposed by the quantum measurement law.

In finite dimension
N
, this formulation implies directly that any diffusive SME admits a unique solution remaining for all
t\geq 0
in
\{\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}~:~\rho=\rho^{\dagger},~\rho\geq 0,~{\rm{Tr}}\left(\rho\right)=1\}
.

3.4
Kraus maps and numerical schemes for diffusive SME

From the above formulation, one can construct a linear, positivity and trace preserving numerical integration scheme for such diffusive SME (see
[
28
, appendix B]
):

\displaystyle d\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(-i[{\mathbf{H}},\rho_{t}]+\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu})\right)dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu}\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}\left({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}-{\rm{Tr}}\left(({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}+{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger})\rho_{t}\right)\rho_{t}\right)dW_{\nu,t},

\displaystyle dy_{\nu,t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}\right)dt+dW_{\nu,t}

With

{\mathbf{M}}_{0}={\mathbf{I}}+\big(-i{\mathbf{H}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\big)dt,\quad{\mathbf{S}}={\mathbf{M}}_{0}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{M}}_{0}+\left(\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}^{\dagger}_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\right)dt

set

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{0}={\mathbf{M}}_{0}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1/2},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}={\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}{\mathbf{S}}^{-1/2}

Sampling of
dy_{\nu,t}=s_{\nu,t}\sqrt{dt}
according to the following probability law:

\hskip-10.00002pt\mathbb{P}\Big(\left(s_{\nu,t}\in[s_{\nu},s_{\nu}+ds_{\nu}]\right)_{\nu}~|~\rho_{t}\Big)={\rm{Tr}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{s\sqrt{dt}}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{s\sqrt{dt}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu})\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt\right)\prod_{\nu}\tfrac{e^{-\frac{s_{\nu}^{2}}{2}}ds_{\nu}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.

where

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{0}+\sum_{\nu}\sqrt{\eta_{\nu}}dy_{\nu,t}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}.

The update
\rho_{t+dt}
is then given by the following exact Kraus-map formulation:

\rho_{t+dt}=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu})\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu})\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}\rho_{t}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt\right)}.

Notice that the operators
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}
are bounded operators even if
{\mathbf{H}}
and
{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}
are unbounded.

One can also use the following splitting scheme when the unitary operator
e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}
is numerically available and where in the above calculations
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
is reduced to

{\mathbf{I}}-\tfrac{dt}{2}\sum_{\nu}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}
:

\rho_{t+dt}=e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\rho_{t}e^{\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu})\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\rho_{t}e^{\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\rho_{t}e^{\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{dy_{t}}^{\dagger}+\sum_{\nu}(1-\eta_{\nu})\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}e^{-\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\rho_{t}e^{\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\nu}^{\dagger}dt\right)}e^{\frac{idt}{2}{\mathbf{H}}}.

3.5
Qubits measured by photons (resonant interaction)

The qubit wave function is denoted by
\left|\psi\right\rangle
. The photons, before interacting with the qubit, are in the vacuum state
\left|0\right\rangle
. The interaction is resonant according to (
5
).
After the interaction and just before the measurement performed on the photons, the composite qubit/photon wave function
\left|\Psi\right\rangle
reads:

\Bigg(\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|\cos(\theta\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}})+\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\cos(\theta\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{I}}})\\
+\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|\frac{\sin(\theta\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}})}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}}}{\mathbf{a}}^{\dagger}-\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|{\mathbf{a}}\frac{\sin(\theta\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}})}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{n}}}}\Bigg)\left|\psi\right\rangle\left|0\right\rangle\\
=\big(\left\langle g\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle+\cos\theta\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|e\right\rangle\big)\left|0\right\rangle+\sin\theta\left\langle e\right|\!\left.\psi\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\left|1\right\rangle.

The Markov process with measurement observable
{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}n\left|n\right\rangle\!\left\langle n\right|
and outcome
y\in\{0,1\}
reads (density operator formulation)

\rho_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{0}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{0}^{\dagger}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{0}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{0}^{\dagger}\right)}&\hbox{if $y_{k}=0$ with probability ${\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{0}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{0}^{\dagger}\right)$ ;}\\
\frac{{\mathbf{M}}_{1}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{\dagger}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{1}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{\dagger}\right)}&\hbox{if $y_{k}=1$ with probability ${\rm{Tr}}\left({\mathbf{M}}_{1}\rho_{k}{\mathbf{M}}_{1}^{\dagger}\right)$ ;}\end{array}\right.

with measurement Kraus operators
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}=\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle g\right|+\cos\theta\left|e\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|
and
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}=\sin\theta\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|
.
Notice that

Since in the above Markov process

4
Conclusion

These SME driven by diffusive measurements or counting measurements are now the object of numerous control-theoretical and mathematical investigations that can be divided into two main issues.
The first issues are related to feedback stabilization of a target quantum state (quantum state preparation) or of quantum subspace as in quantum error correction. One can distinguish several kinds of quantum feedback:

•

Markovian feedback
[
49
]
which is in fact a static output feedback usually used in discrete-time quantum error correction. Its main interest relies on the closed-loop ensemble average dynamics which is a linear quantum channel for which several stability properties are available (see, e.g.,
[
34
,
36
,
43
]
).

•

measurement-based feedback where the control-loop is still achieved by a classical controller taking into account the past measurement outcomes. Quantum-state feedback is such typical feedback where the quantum state is estimated via a quantum filter (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
42
,
4
,
32
,
15
]
).

•

Coherent feedback where the controller is a quantum dissipative system
[
24
]
. It has its origin in optical pumping and coherent population trapping
[
30
,
6
]
. Such feedback structures are now the object of active researches in the context of autonomous quantum error correction (see, e.g.,
[
48
,
41
,
31
,
33
,
37
]
).

The second issues are related to filtering and estimation. They are closely related to quantum-state or quantum-process tomography:

•

Quantum filtering that can be seen as the quantum analogue of state asymptotic observers. It has its origin in the seminal work of
[
12
]
. It can be shown that quantum filtering is always a stable process in average (see
[
38
,
3
]
). Characterization of asymptotic almost-sure convergence is an open-problem with recent progresses (see, e.g.,
[
47
,
5
]
).

•

Estimation of a quantum state or classical parameters estimation based on repeated measurements including imperfection and decoherence during the measurement process relies on quantum SME (see, e.g.,
[
44
,
46
]
)

Appendix B
Three quantum rules

This appendix is borrowed from
[
39
]

1.

The state of a quantum system is described either by the wave function
\left|\psi\right\rangle
a vector of length one belonging to some separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of finite or infinite dimension, or, more generally, by the density operator
\rho
that is a non-negative Hermitian operator on
\mathcal{H}
with trace one. When the system can be described by a wave function
\left|\psi\right\rangle
(pure state), the density operator
\rho
coincides with the orthogonal projector on the line spanned by
\left|\psi\right\rangle
and
\rho=\left|\psi\right\rangle\left\langle\psi\right|
with usual Dirac notations. In general the rank of
\rho
exceeds one, the state is then mixed and cannot be described by a wave function. When the system is closed, the time evolution of
\left|\psi\right\rangle
is governed by the Schrödinger equation (here
\hbar\equiv 1
)

\frac{d}{dt}\left|\psi\right\rangle=-i{\mathbf{H}}\left|\psi\right\rangle

(8)

where
{\mathbf{H}}
is the system Hamiltonian, an Hermitian operator on
\mathcal{H}
that could possibly depend on time
t
via some time-varying parameters (classical control inputs).
When the system is closed, the evolution of
\rho
is governed by the Liouville/von-Neumann equation

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=-i\big[{\mathbf{H}},\rho\big]=-i\big({\mathbf{H}}\rho-\rho{\mathbf{H}}\big).

(9)

2.

Dissipation and irreversibility has its origin in the ”collapse of the wave packet” induced by the measurement. A measurement on the quantum system of state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
or
\rho
is associated to an observable
{\mathbf{O}}
, an Hermitian operator on
\mathcal{H}
, with spectral decomposition
\sum_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}
:
{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}
is the orthogonal projector on the eigen-space associated to the eigen-value
\lambda_{\mu}
(
\lambda_{\mu}\neq\lambda_{\mu^{\prime}}
for
\mu\neq\mu^{\prime}
). The measurement process attached to
{\mathbf{O}}
is assumed to be instantaneous and obeys to the following rules:

•

the measurement outcome
\mu
is obtained with probability
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}=\left\langle\psi\right|{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\left|\psi\right\rangle
or
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}={\rm{Tr}}\left(\rho{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\right)
, depending on the state
\left|\psi\right\rangle
or
\rho
just before the measurement;

•

just after the measurement process, the quantum state is changed to
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{+}
or
\rho_{+}
according to the mappings

\left|\psi\right\rangle\mapsto\left|\psi\right\rangle_{+}=\frac{{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\left|\psi\right\rangle}{\sqrt{\left\langle\psi\right|{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\left|\psi\right\rangle}}\quad\text{ or }\quad\rho\mapsto\rho_{+}=\frac{{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\rho{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}}{{\rm{Tr}}\left(\rho{\mathbf{P}}_{\mu}\right)}

where
\mu
is the observed measurement outcome. These mappings describe the measurement back-action and have no classical counterpart.

3.

Most systems are composite systems built with several sub-systems. The quantum states of such composite systems live in the tensor product of the Hilbert spaces of each sub-system. This is a crucial difference with classical composite systems where the state space is built with Cartesian products. Such tensor products have important implications such as entanglement with existence of non separable states. Consider a bi-partite system made of two sub-systems: the sub-system of interest
S
with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{S}
and the measured sub-system
M
with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{M}
. The quantum state of this bi-partite system
(S,M)
lives in
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{M}
. Its Hamiltonian
{\mathbf{H}}
is constructed with the Hamiltonians of the sub-systems,
{\mathbf{H}}_{S}
and
{\mathbf{H}}_{M}
, and an interaction Hamiltonian
{\mathbf{H}}_{int}
made of a sum of tensor products of operators (not necessarily Hermitian) on
S
and
M
:

{\mathbf{H}}={\mathbf{H}}_{S}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{M}+{\mathbf{H}}_{int}+{\mathbf{I}}_{S}\otimes{\mathbf{H}}_{M}

with
{\mathbf{I}}_{S}
and
{\mathbf{I}}_{M}
identity operators on
\mathcal{H}_{S}
and
\mathcal{H}_{M}
, respectively.
The measurement operator
{\mathbf{O}}={\mathbf{I}}_{S}\otimes\boldsymbol{O}_{M}
is here a simple tensor product of identity on
\mathcal{H}_{S}
and the Hermitian operator
{\mathbf{O}}_{M}
on
\mathcal{H}_{M}
, since only system
M
is directly measured. Its spectrum is degenerate: the multiplicities of the eigenvalues are necessarily greater or equal to the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{S}
.

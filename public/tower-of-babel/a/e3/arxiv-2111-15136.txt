Orbital stability of two-component peakons
Cheng He, Xiaochuan Liu, Changzheng Qu

Abstract
. We prove that the two-component peakon solutions are orbitally stable in the energy space. The system concerned here is a two-component Novikov system, which is an integrable multi-component extension of the integrable Novikov equation. We improve the method for the scalar peakons to the two-component case with genuine nonlinear interactions by establishing optimal inequalities for the conserved quantities involving the coupled structures. Moreover, we also establish the orbital stability for the train-profiles of these two-component peakons by using the refined analysis based on monotonicity of the local energy and an induction method.



Key words:
Novikov equation, two-component Novikov system, peakons, orbital stability, conservation law, Camassa-Holm equation.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are devoted to the orbital stability of the two-component peakon solutions and the corresponding configuration of train-profiles. The system we are concerned with is the following integrable two-component Novikov system
[
33
]

\left\{\begin{aligned} &m_{t}+uvm_{x}+(2vu_{x}+uv_{x})m=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},\\
&n_{t}+uvn_{x}+(2uv_{x}+vu_{x})n=0,\hskip 9.24994ptn=v-v_{xx}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Note that this system reduces to the well-studied integrable Novikov equation
[
29
,
46
]

m_{t}+u^{2}m_{x}+3uu_{x}m=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},

(1.2)

when
v=u
.

Since the celebrated work
[
5
]
by Camassa and Holm, in which they first discovered the non-smooth peaked soliton solutions (called peakons) to the Camassa-Holm (CH) equation, the existence of peakons and multi-peakons is one of the significant properties of the integrable CH-type equations. The basic wave profile of a peakon takes a quite compact form

\varphi_{c}(x-ct)=a(c)\,e^{-|x-ct|},

(1.3)

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is the wave speed and the amplitude
a(c)
is a function related to
c
. Remarkably, the seemingly simple form of the peakon displays the deep relationship with some important phenomena of wave propagation in shallow water waves. Indeed, due to the discussion in
[
13
,
14
,
52
]
, the feature of the peakons that their profile (
1.3
) is smooth, except at the crest where it is continuous but the lateral tangents differ, is similar to that of the so-called Stokes waves of greatest height, i.e. traveling waves of largest possible amplitude which are solutions to the governing equations for irrotational water waves. There is no closed forms available for these waves, and the peakons can capture these described essential features. It is well-understood that the CH equation

m_{t}+um_{x}+2u_{x}m=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},

and the Degasperis-Procesi (DP) equation

m_{t}+um_{x}+3u_{x}m=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},

both arise as the appropriate asymptotic approximations of the Euler equations for the free-surface shallow water waves in the moderately nonlinear regime
[
16
]
, and admit the following peakon solutions (
c>0
, and anti-peakon for
c<0
) in the line
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
20
,
32
,
39
]

u(t,x)=\varphi_{c}(x-ct)=ce^{-\left|x-ct\right|},\hskip 18.49988ptc\neq 0.

(1.4)

On the other hand, the peakons and the corresponding multi-peakons admit a rich mathematical structure related to the underlying integrable features. Indeed, both the CH and DP equations are integrable equations with Lax-pair formulations, and the inverse scattering approach can be used to derive explicitly these peakons (
1.4
) and the related multi-peakon solutions
[
2
,
19
,
40
]
. In the past ten years, two typical CH-type integrable equations with cubic nonlinearity that support peakon dynamics attracted much attention. One is the Novikov equation (
1.2
), whose peakon solutions take the form of
[
29
]

u(t,x)=\varphi_{c}(x-ct)=\sqrt{c}e^{-\left|x-ct\right|},\hskip 18.49988ptc>0.

Another one is the modified Camassa-Holm (mCH) equation
[
47
]

m_{t}+\left((u^{2}-u_{x}^{2})m\right)_{x}=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},

which has the following peakon structure
[
26
]

u(t,x)=\varphi_{c}(x-ct)=\sqrt{\frac{3c}{2}}\,e^{-\left|x-ct\right|},\hskip 18.49988ptc>0.

Although the physical background of the Novikov and mCH equations is not so clear, their peakon and multi-peakon dynamics are demonstrated to have several non-trivial properties in the framework of Lax integrability (see the discussion in
[
9
,
28
]
, etc).

In this paper, special concern for these peakon solutions is the issue of their stability, which lies in the fact that they are the explicit weak solutions in the sense of distribution to the corresponding equations. The peakon equations exhibit different features in contrast with the classical integrable equations such as the KdV equation, the modified KdV equation and the Schrödinger equation that admit the smooth solitions, especially in the study of qualitative properties related to stability and instability. There are huge number of papers to study the stability and instability of solitons for classical integrable systems. We don’t attempt to exhaust all the literatures, one can refer to
[
3
,
4
,

,

,
25
,
48
]
for stability issue and
[
43
,
44

49
]
for the issue of asymptotic stability, as well as the references therein. Note that the peakons don’t admit the classical second-order derivatives and the linearized operators at peakons appear to be degenerate. So the classical methods based on the spectral analysis are not available in the case of peakons. In an intriguing paper due to Constantin and Strauss
[
18
]
, they proved the orbital stability of peakons for the CH equation by discovering several precise optimal inequalities relating to the maximum value of the approximate solutions and the conserved quantity of quadratic form and higher-order conserved quantity (see also
[
17
]
for a variational argument and
[
31
]
for the periodic case). This approach in
[

]
was further developed by Dika and Molinet
[

]
to study orbital stability of the train of peakons to the CH equation. Orbital stability of peakons and the train of peakons of other CH-type integrable equations were investigated in
[

,

,

. More recently, the issue of instability of peakons for the CH equation and the Novikov equation are addressed in

Compared with the rich results of the stability or instability properties of peakons for the CH-type integrable equations of scalar form, the corresponding work for the multi-component peakon profiles is quite a few. It is worth noticing that multi-component CH-type integrable systems, that are verified to admit the peakon structures as the distributional solutions, are very rare up to now. A celebrated integrable multi-component extension to the CH equation is the so-called two-component CH system
[

,

,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\;m_{t}+um_{x}+2u_{x}m+\rho\rho_{x}=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},\\
&\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

However, system (
1.5
) does not admit the peaked solitons
[
27
]
. The orbital stability of a kind of reduced peakon profile was studied via variational method
[
11
]
. The Novikov equation (
1.2
) admits the following two-component integrable extension, called the Geng-Xue system
[

\left\{\begin{aligned} &m_{t}+uvm_{x}+3vu_{x}m=0,\hskip 9.24994ptm=u-u_{xx},\\
&n_{t}+uvn_{x}+3uv_{x}n=0,\hskip 9.24994ptn=v-v_{xx},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

which has been paid much attention recently
[
34
,
41

42
]
. Although system (
1.6
) supports the multi-peakon structures, they are derived in the framework of Lax-pair formulation of (

) (see

), which are not weak solutions in the sense of distribution. To the best of our knowledge, there is no work to study orbital stability of multi-component peakons for integrable multi-component CH-type equations.

Recently, another kind of two-component integrable generalization (
1.1
) of the Novikov equation (
1.2
) was introduced in
[
33
]
and we find that this system admits the two-component peakon structure, which are given by

\big(u(t,x),v(t,x)\big)=\big(\varphi_{c}(x-ct),\psi_{c}(x-ct)\big)=\big(a\varphi(x-ct),b\psi(x-ct)\big)=\big(ae^{-\left|x-ct\right|},be^{-\left|x-ct\right|}\big),

(1.7)

traveling at constant speed
c=ab\neq 0
. It is demonstrated that these peakons (
1.7
) are indeed the weak solutions of (
1.1
) in the distribution form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}+uvu_{x}+P_{x}*\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}v+uu_{x}v_{x}+u^{2}v\right)+\frac{1}{2}P*(u_{x}^{2}v_{x})=0,\\
&v_{t}+uvv_{x}+P_{x}*\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}u+vv_{x}u_{x}+v^{2}u\right)+\frac{1}{2}P*(v_{x}^{2}u_{x})=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

where
P(x)=e^{-\left|x\right|}/2
and
*
stands for convolution with respect to the spatial variable
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Here a question arises: are these multi-component peakons and the corresponding train-profiles for system (
1.1
) stable in the energy space?

System (
1.1
) adapts the following conserved densities

E_{0}[u,v]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(mn)^{\frac{1}{3}}\,dx,

E_{u}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx,\hskip 9.24994ptE_{v}[v]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(v^{2}+v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx,\hskip 9.24994ptH[u,v]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(uv+u_{x}v_{x}\big)\,dx

and

F[u,v]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}v^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}uvu_{x}v_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2}v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx,

which will play prominent role in proving stability of peakons, while the corresponding three conserved quantities of Novikov equation (
1.2
) are

H_{0}[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m^{\frac{2}{3}}\,dx,\;\;E[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})}\,dx,\;\;F[u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left(u^{4}+2u^{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{4}\right)}\,dx.

If we choose
u=v
, then we have
H_{0}=E_{0}[u,u]
,
E[u]=E_{u}[u]=E_{v}[v]=H[u,v]
and
F[u,v]=F[u]
. Due to the existence of the
H^{1}
conservation law respectively for the
u
and
v
-components as well as the mutual interaction conservation laws
H[u,v]
, it is expected to prove stability for the two-component Novikov system in the sense of the energy space of
H^{1}\times H^{1}
-norm. In general, a small perturbation of a solitary wave can yield another one with a different speed and phase shift. It is appropriate to define the orbit of traveling-wave solutions
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
to be the set
U(\varphi,\psi)=\{(a\varphi(\cdot+x_{1}),b\psi(\cdot+x_{2})),x_{1}\in\mathbb{R},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}
. However, if
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, the functionals
F[\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c}]
and
H[\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c}]
are not conserved in the time evolution for
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
in this set
U(\varphi,\psi)
. Thus, we consider here a suitable orbit of the traveling-wave solutions
\varphi_{c}
and
\psi_{c}
to be the set
U_{0}(\varphi,\psi)=\{(a\varphi(\cdot+x_{0}),b\psi(\cdot+x_{0})),x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\}
and the peakon solutions of the two-component Novikov equation are called orbitally stable if a wave standing close to the peakon remains close to the orbit
U_{0}(\varphi,\psi)
at all the later existence time.

The first main result is stated as follows. Here, we only consider the case of peakons traveling to the right, i.e. the case of
a>0
,
b>0
and then
c=ab>0
in (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
be the peaked solitons in (
1.7
), traveling with speed
c=ab>0
. Then
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
are orbitally stable in the following sense. Assume that
u_{0},v_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for some
s\geq 3
,
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u_{0}(x)
and
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})v_{0}(x)
are nonnegative, and there is a
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|(u_{0},v_{0})-(\varphi_{c},\psi_{c})\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left\|u_{0}-\varphi_{c}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|v_{0}-\psi_{c}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<\delta.

Then the corresponding solution
(u(t,x),v(t,x))
of the Cauchy problem for the two-component Novikov system (
1.1
) with the initial data
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
and
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\left\|\big(u(t,\cdot),v(t,\cdot)\big)-\big(\varphi_{c}(\cdot-\xi(t)),\psi_{c}(\cdot-\xi(t))\big)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T)}{\left\|u(t,\cdot)-\varphi_{c}(\cdot-\xi(t))\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|v(t,\cdot)-\psi_{c}(\cdot-\xi(t))\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}<A\delta^{\frac{1}{4}},\end{aligned}

where
T>0
is the maximal existence time,
\xi(t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the maximum point of the function
u(t,x)v(t,x)
, the constant
A
depends only on
a
,
b
as well as the norms
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}
.

To prove orbital stability of the two-component peakons
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
, some new insights are developed. We aim to obtain for each component
u
and
v
the dynamical estimates
|u(t,\xi(t))-a|
and
|v(t,\xi(t))-b|
along some trajectory

, where

and

are the maximal value of the component
\varphi_{c}
and
\psi_{c}
, respectively. Here, due to the nonlinear interaction between

and

involved in system (
1.1
), the key obstacle is how to find the suitable location of
\xi(t)
in order to derive the precise estimates for
|u(t,\xi(t))-a|
and
|v(t,\xi(t))-b|
(note that in the case of scalar peakons such
\xi(t)
is always chosen to locate at the maximal point of perturbed solution, which is no longer valid in the multi-component case considered here and
\xi(t)
must change according to the appearance of characteristic speed of nonlinear interaction

F[u,v]
is much more complicated than

of the Novikov equation. Therefore, the stability issue of the two-component peakon solutions is more subtle. To overcome the difficulties, two observations will be crucial. System (
1.1
) not only has the separated

\int(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})dx
and
\int(v^{2}+v_{x}^{2})dx
with which one can derive the pointwise estimates separately for each component

and

, but also the second-order interacting conserved quantity

with which we are motivated to find the exact location of

as the maximal point of the multiplication function

of two components. This argument is quite different from the case for the scalar CH-type equations. On the other hand, new optimal energy identities for
H[u,v]
and

are established. The new insight is that the precise control of two components is involved in one optimal energy identity. This point is also different from the scalar case. Based on these observations together with corresponding refined analysis, we are able to prove orbital stability of the two-component peakons
(\varphi_{c},\,\psi_{c})
on the line
{\mathbb{R}}
.

being nonnegative, satisfy

\displaystyle{\left\|u_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\varphi_{c}(\cdot-z_{i}^{0})}\right\|_{H^{1}}}+{\left\|v_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\psi_{c}(\cdot-z_{i}^{0})}\right\|_{H^{1}}}\leq{\epsilon}

(1.9)

In general, two main ingredients in the proof of orbital stability for the train-proflies of peakons are involved

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t,x)=exp\left(\int^{t}_{0}(2vu_{x}+uv_{x})(s,q(s,x))ds\right),\;\gamma_{2}(t,x)=exp\left(\int^{t}_{0}(2uv_{x}+vu_{x})(s,q(s,x))ds\right).

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(t,x)=(2vu_{x}+uv_{x})(t,q(t,x))\gamma_{1},\hskip 9.24994pt\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(t,x)=(2uv_{x}+vu_{x})(t,q(t,x))\gamma_{2}.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{m}(t,x)=\gamma_{1}(t,x)m(t,q(t,x)),\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{n}(t,x)=\gamma_{2}(t,x)n(t,q(t,x)).\end{aligned}

(
2.4
) become

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{m}^{\prime}(t,x)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{n}^{\prime}(t,x)=0.\end{aligned}

(2.5)

The equations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{m}(t,x)=m_{0}\hskip 9.24994ptand\hskip 9.24994pt\tilde{n}(t,x)=n_{0}\end{aligned}

lead to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &m(t,q(t,x))=exp\left(-\int^{t}_{0}(2vu_{x}+uv_{x})(s,q(s,x))ds\right)m_{0},\\
&n(t,q(t,x))=exp\left(-\int^{t}_{0}(2uv_{x}+vu_{x})(s,q(s,x))ds\right)n_{0}.\end{aligned}

Thus, for all
t\in[0,T)
, we have
m(t,\cdot)\geq 0
,
n(t,\cdot)\geq 0
. And then
u(t,\cdot)\geq 0
,
v(t,\cdot)\geq 0
.

Formally regarding
m(x)=u(x)-u_{xx}(x)
, it holds that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(x)=\frac{e^{-x}}{2}\int^{x}_{-\infty}e^{y}m(y)dy+\frac{e^{x}}{2}\int^{\infty}_{x}e^{-y}m(y)dy\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{x}(x)=-\frac{e^{-x}}{2}\int^{x}_{-\infty}e^{y}m(y)dy+\frac{e^{x}}{2}\int^{\infty}_{x}e^{-y}m(y)dy.\end{aligned}

Then we infer that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(x)\geq|u_{x}(x)|,\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}

Similarly, we find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v(x)\geq|v_{x}(x)|,\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Stability of two-component peakons

In this section, we prove Theorem 1.1, which will be based on a series of lemmas. Note that the assumptions on the initial profile guarantee the existence of a unique positive solution for the Cauchy problem (
2.1
) of the two-component Novikov system. In general, for
a>0
and
b>0
, the profile functions of peakon solutions
\varphi_{c}(x)=ae^{-\left|x\right|}
and
\psi_{c}(x)=be^{-\left|x\right|}
are in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which have peaks at
x=0
, and thus

\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}{\varphi_{c}(x)}=\varphi_{c}(0)=a\hskip 9.24994pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}{\psi_{c}(x)}=\psi_{c}(0)=b.

A direct calculation gives

E_{u}[\varphi_{c}(x)]=\left\|\varphi_{c}\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=2a^{2},\hskip 18.49988ptE_{v}[\psi_{c}(x)]=\left\|\psi_{c}\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=2b^{2}

and

H[\varphi_{c}(x),\psi_{c}(x)]=2ab,\hskip 18.49988ptF[\varphi_{c}(x),\psi_{c}(x)]=\frac{4}{3}\,a^{2}b^{2}.

Due to the conservation of the
H^{1}
-norm of each component
u
and
v
, the following pointwise identities still hold for the two-component Novikov system as in the scalar CH and Novikov cases.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
u,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &E_{u}[u]-E_{u}[\varphi_{c}]=\left\|u-\varphi_{c}(\cdot-\xi)\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+4a(u(\xi)-a),\\
&E_{v}[v]-E_{v}[\psi_{c}]=\left\|v-\psi_{c}(\cdot-\xi)\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+4b(v(\xi)-b).\end{aligned}

(3.1)

In the following two lemmas, two energy identities relating some kind of critical values of
u
and
v
to the invariants
H[u,v]
and
F[u,v]
are established. Consider
0\not\equiv u,\,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 3
, and
u,v\geq 0
. Then
u,v\in C^{2}
due to the Sobolev imbedding theory. Since
u
and
v
decay at infinity, it must have a point with global maximal value of the multiplication function
u(x)v(x)
. Thus, in the following, set for some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle M=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{u(x)v(x)\}=u(\xi)v(\xi).

(3.2)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\not\equiv u,\,v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 3
and
u,v\geq 0
. Define the functions
g_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)
by

g_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x)-u_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx<\xi,\\
&u(x)+u_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx>\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

and

g_{2}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &v(x)-v_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx<\xi,\\
&v(x)+v_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx>\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathbb{R}}{g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)}dx=H[u,v]-2M.\end{aligned}

(3.5)

Proof.

To show (
3.5
), we evaluate the integral of
g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}}{g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)}dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}{\left(u(x)-u_{x}(x)\right)\left(v(x)-v_{x}(x)\right)}dx+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}{\left(u(x)+u_{x}(x)\right)\left(v(x)+v_{x}(x)\right)}\,dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}{\left(uv-(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\right)}dx+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}{\left(uv+(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\right)}\,dx\\
&=H[u,v]-2M.\end{aligned}

∎

The construction of the auxiliary function
h(x)
in the following lemma is crucial in the proof of stability of peakons, which is different from the scalar cases of CH and DP equations. This new defined function is a nontrivial refinement of the case in the Novikov equation.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the same assumptions and notation as in Lemma
3.2
, define the function
h
by

h(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &uv-\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x},\hskip 9.24994ptx<\xi,\\
&uv+\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x},\hskip 9.24994ptx>\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathbb{R}}{h(x)g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)}dx=F[u,v]-\frac{4}{3}\,M^{2}.\end{aligned}

(3.7)

Proof.

To show (
3.7
), we evaluate the integral of
h(x)g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}}{h(x)g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)}dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\left[uv-\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\right]\left[uv-(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\right]\,dx\\
&\hskip 18.49988pt+\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\left[uv+\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\right]\left[uv+(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\right]\,dx\mathchar 13356\relax I+\Pi.\end{aligned}

We do computation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\left[uv-\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\right]\left[uv-(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\right]\,dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\left(u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}v^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}uvu_{x}v_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2}v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx-\frac{4}{3}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}(uv)(uv)_{x}\,dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\left(u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}v^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}uvu_{x}v_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2}v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx-\frac{2}{3}M^{2}.\end{aligned}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Pi=\int_{\xi}^{\infty}\left(u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}v^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}uvu_{x}v_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2}v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx-\frac{2}{3}M^{2}.\end{aligned}

Combining above, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}}{h(x)g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)}dx\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{1}{3}u^{2}v_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}v^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}uvu_{x}v_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}^{2}v_{x}^{2}\right)\,dx-\frac{4}{3}\,M^{2}=F[u,v]-\frac{4}{3}\,M^{2}.\end{aligned}

∎

With the two energy identities (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) in hand, one can derive the following delicate relation between the second order conserved quantity
H[u,v]
and the higher-order conserved quantity
F[u,v]
for the strong solution
(u,v)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
0\not\equiv u_{0},\,v_{0}\in H^{s}
,
s\geq 3
and
m_{0}=u_{0}-u_{0xx}\geq 0
,
n_{0}=v_{0}-v_{0xx}\geq 0
. For the corresponding strong solution
(u(t,x),\,v(t,x))
with initial data
(u_{0},\,v_{0})
in the lifespan
[0,\,T)
, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F[u,v]-\frac{4}{3}M(t)H[u,v]+\frac{4}{3}M(t)^{2}\leq 0,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall t\in[0,\,T),\end{aligned}

(3.8)

where
M(t)=\max_{x\in R}\{u(t,x)v(t,x)\}=u(t,\xi(t))v(t,\xi(t))
for some trajectory
\xi(t)\in\mathbb{R}
in
[0,\,T)
.

Proof.

First, by the sign-invariant property, the solution
(u(t,x),\,v(t,x))
satisfies
m(t,x)=u(t,x)-\partial^{2}_{x}u(t,x)\geq 0
and
n(t,x)=v(t,x)-\partial^{2}_{x}v(t,x)\geq 0
for all
(t,x)\in[0,\,T)\times\mathbb{R}
. It follows that
(u(t,x),\,v(t,x))
is positive solution and fulfills all the conditions assumed in Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3. Hence, we have the following energy identities of the dynamical forms in
[0,\,T)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{g_{1}(t,x)g_{2}(t,x)}dx=H[u,v]-2M(t)

(3.9)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{h(t,x)g_{1}(t,x)g_{2}(t,x)}dx=F[u,v]-\frac{4}{3}\,M(t)^{2}.

(3.10)

We now claim that for any
(t,x)\in[0,\,T)\times\mathbb{R}

h(t,x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(uv-\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\right)(t,x),\hskip 9.24994ptx<\xi(t),\\
&\left(uv+\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\right)(t,x),\hskip 9.24994ptx>\xi(t)\end{aligned}\right.\leq\frac{4}{3}u(t,x)v(t,x).

In fact, due to the definition of

, it follows from the fact
u(t,x)\geq|u_{x}(t,x)|
and
v(t,x)\geq|v_{x}(t,x)|
that

h(t,x)=\frac{4}{3}u(t,x)v(t,x)-\frac{1}{3}(u\pm u_{x}(t,x))(v\pm v_{x}(t,x))\leq\frac{4}{3}u(t,x)v(t,x).

Thus, the combination of the above inequalities yields

\displaystyle h(t,x)\leq\frac{4}{3}u(t,x)v(t,x)\leq\frac{4}{3}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\{u(t,x)v(t,x)\}=\frac{4}{3}M(t),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall(t,x)\in[0,\,T)\times\mathbb{R}.

Now, using estimates
|u_{x}|\leq u
and
|v_{x}|\leq v
again in the expressions (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain (
3.8
) from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
).
∎

For

Similarly, we have

Lemma 3.6

Assume that

,

Proof.

In view of the conservation of the functionals

\displaystyle\int_{D^{c}_{i}}\left[uP_{x}\ast(u^{2}_{x}v_{x})\right]\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}(t)\,dx\leq\frac{C}{K}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}e^{-\frac{1}{K}(\sigma_{0}t+\frac{L}{8})}.

The estimate of
\mathaccent 869{I}_{1,3}
on

leads to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{i})}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{i})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[P\ast\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}(t)\right]u^{2}_{x}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{20}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}+u^{2}_{x}\right)\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}\,dx.

Therefore, for

and
L>L_{0}>0
, with
\alpha_{0}\ll 1
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}^{u}_{j,K}(t)\leq\frac{C}{K}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}e^{-\frac{1}{K}(\sigma_{0}t+\frac{L}{8})}.

Integrating between

and

Similarly, we also obtain

\mathchar 13356\relax&\mathaccent 869{I}_{2,1}+\mathaccent 869{I}_{2,2}+\mathaccent 869{I}_{2,3}-\dot{y}_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(uv+u_{x}v_{x}\right)\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}\,dx.\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{I}_{2,2}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{20}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(uv+u_{x}v_{x}\right)\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}\,dx+\frac{C}{K}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}e^{-\frac{1}{K}(\sigma_{0}t+\frac{L}{8})}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathaccent 869{I}_{2,3}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{20}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(uv+u_{x}v_{x}\right)\Psi^{\prime}_{i,K}\,dx+\frac{C}{K}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}e^{-\frac{1}{K}(\sigma_{0}t+\frac{L}{8})}.

Lemma 4.5

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{4}{3}M^{2}_{i}-\frac{4}{3}M_{i}H_{i}(u,v)+F_{i}(u,v)\leq O(L^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\hskip 9.24994pti\in\{1,...,N\}.\end{aligned}

&\hskip 18.49988pt+\int_{x_{i}}^{\infty}{\Big(uv+\frac{1}{3}(uv)_{x}-\frac{1}{3}u_{x}v_{x}\Big)\Big(uv+(uv)_{x}+u_{x}v_{x}\Big)}\Phi_{i}\,dx\\

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume

&\left|E_{vi}(v_{0})-E_{vi}(\psi_{c_{i}})\right|<O(\epsilon)+O\left(e^{-\sqrt{L}}\right).\end{aligned}

Define the interval

. Using (
4.24
), (
4.25
) and the exponential decay of

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|H_{i}(\varphi_{c_{i}},\psi_{c_{i}})-H_{i}(u_{0},v_{0})\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{c_{i}}\psi_{c_{i}}+\varphi^{\prime}_{c_{i}}\psi^{\prime}_{c_{i}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u_{0}v_{0}+u_{0x}v_{0x}\right)\Phi_{i}\,dx\right|\\

Using the same way in proving the third inequality of (

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|E_{ui}(\varphi_{c_{i}})-E_{ui}(u_{0})\right|<O(\epsilon)+O\left(e^{-\sqrt{L}}\right)\;{\rm and}\;\left|E_{vi}(\psi_{c_{i}})-E_{vi}(v_{0})\right|<O(\epsilon)+O\left(e^{-\sqrt{L}}\right).

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume

,

,

\displaystyle|M_{i}-c_{i}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big),\hskip 9.24994pti=1,...,N.

\bar{P}^{i}

(4.31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum^{N}_{i=1}\bar{P}^{i}&(M_{i})=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\hat{P}^{i}_{0}(M_{i})+\sum^{N}_{i=1}M_{i}\left(H(\varphi_{c_{i}},\psi_{c_{i}})-H_{i}(u_{0},v_{0})\right)\\

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}(M_{i}-c_{i})^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)+O(\epsilon),

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume
|M_{i}-c_{i}|\leq O\left(L^{-1/4}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{1/2}\right),\hskip 9.24994pti=1,...,N
. Then for any
i=\{1,...,N\}
, we have

\displaystyle|u(x_{i})-a_{i}|\leq O\left(L^{-1/4}\right)+O\Big(\epsilon^{1/2}\Big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{i})-b_{i}|\leq O\left(L^{-1/4}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{1/2}\right).

Note the monotonicity property that
\mathcal{J}^{u}_{j,K}(t)
is close to
\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(x>y_{j}(t))}
and thus measures the energy at the right of the
(j-1)th
bump of
u
and
\mathcal{J}^{v}_{j,K}(t)
is close to
\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(x>y_{j}(t))}
and thus measures the energy at the right of the
(j-1)th
bump of
v
. We thus use the induction argument to prove this lemma.

Proof.

Step 1. Define

g_{uN}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x)-u_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx<x_{N},\\
&u(x)+u_{x}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx>x_{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.32)

Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{2}_{uN}(x)\Phi_{N}(x)\,dx=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}+u^{2}_{x}\right)\Phi_{N}(x)\,dx-2u^{2}(x_{N})\Phi_{N}(x_{N})\\
&+\int^{x_{N}}_{-\infty}u^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{N}\,dx-\int^{+\infty}_{x_{N}}u^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{N}\,dx.\end{aligned}

Using
|x_{i}-\tilde{x}_{i}|\leq L/12
, it follows from (
4.24
) that
\Phi_{i}(x_{i})=1+O(e^{-\sqrt{L}})
and thus

\displaystyle E_{uN}(u(t))-2u^{2}(x_{N})+O\left(L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\geq 0.

Note that

\displaystyle E_{uN}(u(t))=\mathcal{J}^{u}_{N,K}(t)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994ptE_{uN}(u(t))-E_{uN}(u(0))\leq O\big(e^{-\sigma_{0}\sqrt{L}}\big).

We thus get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u^{2}(x_{N})\leq O(L^{-\frac{1}{2}})+O(\epsilon)+a^{2}_{N}.\end{aligned}

Similarly,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v^{2}(x_{N})\leq O(L^{-\frac{1}{2}})+O(\epsilon)+b^{2}_{N}.\end{aligned}

Combining the identity
|M_{N}-c_{N}|\leq O\left(L^{-1/4}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{1/2}\right)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |u(x_{N})-a_{N}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{N})-b_{N}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).\end{aligned}

Step 2. Assume for any
k\leq i<N
, we have

\displaystyle|u(x_{i})-a_{i}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{i})-b_{i}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

We need to prove

\displaystyle|u(x_{i-1})-a_{i-1}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{i-1})-b_{i-1}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Firstly, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}u^{2}(x_{i})\leq

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}E_{ui}(u(t))+O(L^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{J}^{u}_{k-1,K}(t)-\mathcal{J}^{u}_{k-1,K}(0)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathcal{J}^{u}_{k-1,K}(0)-\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}E_{u}(\varphi_{c_{i}})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}E_{u}(\varphi_{c_{i}})+O\Big(L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}u^{2}(x_{i})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum^{N}_{i=k-1}E_{u}(\varphi_{c_{i}})+O\Big(L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)+O(\epsilon).

Using the assumption, we find

\displaystyle u^{2}(x_{k-1})\leq a^{2}_{k-1}+O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\right).

Similarly

\displaystyle v^{2}(x_{k-1})\leq b^{2}_{k-1}+O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\left(\sqrt{\epsilon}\right).

Those together with the identity (
4.8
) imply that for any
i=1,\cdots,N

\displaystyle|u(x_{i})-a_{i}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{i})-b_{i}|\leq O\big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}\big)+O\big(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

∎

End of the Proof of Theorem 1.2.

To conclude the proof, from the (
4.6
), it thus suffices to prove that there exists
C>0
which does not depend on
A
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |u(x_{N})-a_{N}|\leq C\Big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|v(x_{N})-b_{N}|\leq C\Big(L^{-\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big),\end{aligned}

which has been verified by (
4.9
).
From (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we know that for
i=2,...,N
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x_{i}-x_{i-1}\geq\frac{2}{3}L.\end{aligned}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Stretching Newton polygons using pure polynomials
Rylan Gajek-Leonard and Uri Tomer

Abstract
. The p p -adic Newton polygon is a visual tool that encodes information about the roots and factorization of a polynomial relative to a prime p p . In this article, we investigate how the Newton polygon changes under polynomial composition. If f f and g g are polynomials with rational (or p p -adic) coefficients and the Newton polygon of g g is pure (has only one segment), we show under some mild conditions that the Newton polygon of f ∘ g f\circ g is the same as that of f f , but stretched horizontally by deg ⁡ g \deg g . When f = g f=g , this implies that all iterates of certain pure polynomials are irreducible, recovering a classical result of Robert Odoni on the irreducibility of iterated Eisenstein polynomials.

1.
Introduction.

Fix a prime
p
and a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
with
f(0)\neq 0

f

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p

\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

f
over
\mathbb{Q}
(e.g., see
[
Col87
]
), making the Newton polygon a useful tool for studying arithmetic properties of
f
.

In this article, we consider the behavior of the Newton polygon under composition. Specifically, what can be said about the Newton polygon of
f\circ g
as
g
varies? Can one describe families of polynomials
S
that preserve, in some sense, the key features (segments and slopes) of the Newton polygon of
f
under composition? The case
S=\{f\}
lies at the heart of arithmetic dynamics, whose central focus is understanding the behavior of
f
upon iteration. When
f
is
p^{r}
-pure
(i.e., its Newton polygon is a single line segment connecting the points
(0,r)
and
(\deg f,0)

consists of
p^{r}
-pure polynomials, it is known (see
[
DS23
, Theorem 3.10]
and
[

]
) that the Newton polygon of
f\circ g
is also
p^{r}
-pure, assuming
\deg f>1

Our main result shows that a similar phenomenon holds when the Newton polygon of

has multiple segments. Roughly speaking, as long as the Newton polygon of
f
does not have any segments with steep negative slopes, composition with a
p^{r}
-pure polynomial

Note that for any fixed

Recall the Eisenstein-Dumas criterion

[

-Dumas polynomials are

Corollary 1.3
.

If

is

-Dumas then

is dynamically irreducible.

Proof.

The case

is clear, so assume
\deg f>1
. By Theorem
1.1
, the Newton polygon of
f\circ f
has a single segment of slope
-r/(\deg f)^{2}
, and is therefore irreducible by the Eisenstein-Dumas criterion. The result now follows from induction.

2.
Newton polygons of products and sums.

Fix a prime
p
and a polynomial
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
. The
p
-adic Newton polygon of
f
, denoted
\operatorname{NP}_{p}(f)
, is the lower convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the points
(i,\operatorname{ord}_{p}a_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq n
, where
\operatorname{ord}_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic valuation. Thus, the Newton polygon is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
bounded below by a sequence of line segments of increasing slopes and on either side by vertical lines. (The Newton polygon of a monomial
ax^{i}
is therefore a vertical ray extending from the point
(i,\operatorname{ord}_{p}a)
.)
By the
length
of a segment of the Newton polygon, we mean the length of the segment upon projection to the
x
-axis.
We say that
f
is
pure at
p
if its Newton polygon has exactly one segment. Following
[
Jak20
,
DS23
]
, we say that
f
is
p^{r}
-pure
for an integer
r\geq 1
if
f
is pure,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a_{0})=r
, and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a_{n})=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note the distinction between a polynomial that is pure at
p
(one segment, arbitrary slope) and
p
-pure (one segment, slope
\frac{-1}{\text{degree}}
).

.

,
(2,0)

(6,3)
. It therefore has two segments of lengths 2 and 4 with slopes
-\frac{1}{2}
and

, respectively.

An immediate consequence of this theorem is that the Newton polygon of the product of two polynomials can be obtained by concatenating the segments of their respective Newton polygons in order of increasing slope. In the case of pure polynomials, this yields the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
are pure of slopes
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}
. Then the Newton polygon of
f=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
consists of
n
segments of slopes
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}
.

.

-1/4

Corollary 3.6
.

There are infinitely many irreducible polynomials

(1)

has the same number of segments as the Newton polygon of

(2)

have valuation

4.
Sample application.

Recall that a polynomial
g\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is called
dynamically irreducible
if
g^{\circ n}
is irreducible for all
n\geq 0
. We give a mild generalization of this notion in the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
. We say that
g
is
dynamically irreducible at
f
if
f\circ g^{\circ n}
is irreducible for all
n\geq 0
.

Let

f_{n}(x)=1+x+\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\in\mathbb{Q}[x]

denote the
n
th Taylor polynomial of the exponential function. It is well known that
f_{n}
is irreducible for all
n
(see
[
Sch30
,
Col87
]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and suppose
g\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is
p^{r}
-Dumas at all primes
p\mid n
. If
d=\deg(g)
is prime and
d
is strictly larger than all prime divisors of
n
, then
g
is dynamically irreducible at
f_{n}
.

Proof.

Choose a prime
p\mid n
and fix
m\geq 0
. We must show that
f_{n}\circ g^{\circ m}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Writing
n=\sum_{i=0}^{N}b_{i}p^{n_{i}}
with
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{N}
and
0<b_{i}<p
, we know from
[
Col87
, Lemma §II]
that
\operatorname{NP}_{p}(f_{n})
has
N
segments of slopes

-\frac{(p^{n_{i}}-1)}{p^{n_{i}}(p-1)}.

From Proposition
3.1
(see also Remark
3.2
), we know that
g^{\circ m}
is
p^{r}
-pure, thus Theorem
1.1
implies that
\operatorname{NP}_{p}(f_{n}\circ g^{\circ m})
has
N
segments of slopes

-\frac{(p^{n_{i}}-1)}{d^{m}p^{n_{i}}(p-1)}.

In particular,
d^{m}p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}n}=d^{m}p^{n_{1}}
divides the denominator of each slope of the Newton polygon
\operatorname{NP}_{p}(f_{n}\circ g^{\circ m})
, thus by
[
Col87
, Corollary §I]
,
d^{m}p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}n}
divides the degree of any irreducible factor of
f_{n}\circ g^{\circ m}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
It follows that any irreducible factor of
f_{n}\circ g^{\circ m}
has degree
d^{m}n
, hence the result.
∎

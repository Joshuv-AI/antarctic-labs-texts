Generalised gradients for virtual elements and applications to a posteriori error analysis
Théophile Chaumont-Frelet, Joscha Gedicke, Lorenzo Mascotto

Abstract
We rewrite the standard nodal virtual element method in two dimensions as a generalised gradient method. This re-formulation allows for computing a reliable and efficient error estimator by locally reconstructing broken fluxes and potentials on elemental subtriangulations. We prove upper and lower bounds with constants independent of the stabilisation of the method and, under technical assumptions on the mesh, the degree of accuracy. AMS subject classification : 65N12; 65N15. Keywords : adaptivity; polygonal mesh; flux reconstruction; potential reconstruction; virtual element method; generalised gradient.



1
Introduction

The virtual element method (VEM)
[
1
]

is a generalisation of the finite element method (FEM)
to polytopic meshes whose elements may have
curved and/or hanging facets, internal cuts, and nonconvex shapes.
Virtual element spaces typically consist of functions that
solve local variational problems with polynomial data
and are not required to be known in closed form,
but rather via their evaluation through suitable degrees of freedom.
Such degrees of freedom are selected so as to permit
the computation of projection operators onto polynomial spaces,
which are inserted in the bilinear form
in order to preserve the polynomial consistency of the scheme.
The well posedness of the method is guaranteed by
supplementing the projected bilinear form with a stabilisation,
which has to be computable via the degrees of freedom.

One among the appealing features of the scheme
is that adaptive mesh refinements admit the creation of hanging facets
without the need of generating extra (useless) elements.
The literature on the adaptive VEM is rather wide.
We mention here only the early contributions
[
3
,
8
,
5
]
.
Almost all the related references are concerned with residual-type error estimators,
which present two major downsides:

•

the constants in the reliability and efficiency bounds depend on the choice of the stabilisation;

•

as in the finite element case
[
21
]
,
the efficiency constant depends on the degree of accuracy of the method
[
4
]
.

The first issue becomes relevant when performing aggressive mesh refinement and/or coarsening:
ad-hoc stabilisations should be designed so as to derive a posteriori
error bounds, which are robust with respect to the presence of
small facets, highly distorted elements, and so on.
Partial improvements are discussed in
[
2
]

where the upper bound is independent of the stabilisation
for lowest order elements on triangular grids.

Using local flux equilibration strategies,

p
-robust error estimators were introduced
for the FEM and discontinuous Galerkin methods;
see, e.g.,
[
6
,
19
]
.
Inspired by the above works,
a first attempt in deriving a
p
-robust error estimator for the VEM
was performed in
[
12
]
.
There, a
p
-robust error estimator was derived
based on the computation of a virtual
global
flux.
However, straightforwardly adapting the
localisation

procedure in
[
14
,
18
]
to the VEM
led to the loss of the efficiency,
one of the main reasons being the lack of Galerkin orthogonality
for the gradient of the discrete solution.

This motivated us to rewrite in this contribution the VEM as
a generalised gradient method.
This amounts to introducing a post-processing of
the discrete solution into a generalised gradient
that exhibits favourable orthogonality properties.
More precisely, on each element
K
,
we rewrite the usual virtual element discrete bilinear form,
which typically looks like

a^{K}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)=a^{K}(\Pi\cdot,\Pi\cdot)+S^{K}((I-\Pi)\cdot,(I-\Pi)\cdot),

\Pi
being a computable polynomial projector
and
S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
a computable stabilisation,
as a generalised gradient bilinear form.
In particular, we shall write

a^{K}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)=(\mathfrak{G}_{h}^{K}(\cdot),\nabla\cdot)_{K},

where
\mathfrak{G}_{h}^{K}(\cdot)
is a generalised gradient
given by a piecewise Raviart-Thomas function over a subtriangulation of
K
,
which contains information on the stabilisation.
This generalised gradient is available for
any
computable
stabilisation and is obtained by solving local finite element problems.
These problems can be independently solved in parallel
and their solution is cheap.
The subtriangulations mentioned above
are always used in practice on the elemental level;
as such, the approach presented in this paper
can be interesting per se while compared to a finite element one,
where typically elements are refined using newest-vertex bisection
and possibly neighbouring elements are to be refined to maintain
the conformity of the method.

Even if the focus of the paper is on conforming virtual elements
for the Poisson problem in two dimensions,
extensions to other settings are possible.
For instance, nonconforming virtual elements
or virtual elements in three dimensions (with simplicial facets)
in principle fit to the setting described above.
In fact, the construction of a generalised gradient
in the context of the virtual element
is based on only two ingredients:
a projection onto piecewise polynomials
and a lifting of the stabilisation term.

The use of a generalised gradient is also instrumental in the design
of a posteriori error estimators
for interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin schemes on simplicial and tensor product meshes,
which also suffer from a lack of Galerkin orthogonality caused by the stabilisation
[
18
]
.
A similar construction was also used in
[
15
]

in the framework of approximation of solutions to
nonlinear problems for skeletal methods.

This generalised gradient allows for the design of an error estimator
that is reliable and efficient with bounds independent of the chosen stabilisation
as well as of the degree of accuracy of the method;
see Theorem
5.3
.
Specifically, once the solution
u_{h}
and the generalised gradient

\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})
are known,
the computation of the error estimator involves the solution
to local primal and mixed finite element problems
on vertex patches as defined
in (
5.5
) below.

As such, the implementation of the error estimator can be made fully parallel
and no more expensive than computing a residual error estimator.
As we detail in Remark
5.5
below,
the constants involved in the upper and lower bounds
generalise those obtained in the corresponding bounds
for discontinuous Galerkin schemes in
[
18
]

to polytopic meshes:
the resulting estimates are as good as the state-of-the-art
for another nonconforming numerical method on standard meshes,
yet keeping in mind that a modified error measure is considered.

Numerical examples show that the generalised gradient
provides us with an approximation of the exact solution
comparable to that obtained with a polynomial
energy projection of the discrete solution,
i.e., the standard error measure in virtual elements.
These examples also illustrate that the estimator provides a fairly
sharp upper bound on the error that does not deteriorates
as
p
increases.

3.2
Stabilisations and polynomial projectors

Consider the splitting

(\nabla u,\nabla v)=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{K}.

On each element

The degrees of freedom of

We define

(\nabla(v_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}v_{h}),\nabla q_{p})=0,\quad S^{K}(v_{h}-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}v_{h},1)=0\qquad\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}(K),\;q_{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(K).

(3.1)

The second condition above fixes constants

Differently from the standard virtual element setting,

We define

(3.2)

In what follows, with an abuse of notation,

3.4
The VEM as a generalised gradient method

We rewrite method (
3.4
) as a generalised gradient method.
In other words, we describe how to design a suitable, fully-computable
operator
\mathfrak{G}_{h}:V_{h}\to\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

a_{h}(\phi_{h},v_{h})=(\mathfrak{G}_{h}(\phi_{h}),\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(3.5)

We propose a construction that is fully-localised to each element, so that

\mathfrak{G}_{h}(\phi_{h})_{|K}
belongs to
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{K})

and (
3.5
)
holds true element-wise.

A key ingredient of the generalised gradient reconstruction
is a “stabilisation lifting”: for the explicit stabilisation

S^{K}(\cdot,\cdot):=h_{K}^{-2}(\Pi^{0}_{p-2}\cdot,\Pi^{0}_{p-2}\cdot)_{K}+h_{K}^{-1}(\cdot,\cdot)_{\partial K},

we can write

S^{K}((I-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p})\phi_{h},v_{h})=(\mu_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}),v_{h})_{\partial K}-(r_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}),v_{h})_{K},

(3.6)

where we have set

\mu_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}):=h_{K}^{-1}(I-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p})\phi_{h}{}_{|\partial K}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{K}),\quad\quad r_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}):=-h_{K}^{-2}\Pi^{0}_{p-2}(I-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p})\phi_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(K).

We show that it is
always

possible to rewrite the stabilisation as in (
3.6
),
and that the data
\mu_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h})
and
r_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h})

can be found by solving a local linear system.

Lemma 3.2

(Stabilisation lifting)
.

For all
\phi_{h}
in
V_{h}(K)

\mu_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h})

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{K})

(\mu_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}),v_{h})_{\partial K}-(r_{h}^{K}(\phi_{h}),v_{h})_{K}=S^{K}((I-\Pi^{\nabla}_{p})\phi_{h},v_{h})\qquad\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}(K).

(3.7)

Proof.

A practical construction of

Consider the space

of piecewise continuous polynomials

Define

Consider the canonical basis

V_{h}(K)
,
which is dual to the degrees of freedom in Section
3.1
.
This basis, and a (polynomial) basis
\{q_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\dim(V_{h}(K))}
,

q_{\ell}=(q_{\ell}^{\partial K},q_{\ell}^{K})
,
of

have the same cardinality.
In fact, the numbers of boundary and bulk degrees of freedom

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{K})
and
\mathbb{P}_{p-2}(K)
,

In particular, there exists a natural bijection between

We have to prove that there exists a unique element

It suffices to solve the square system

5.2
A generalised Prager-Synge identity

The construction of the error estimator
hinges upon a generalised Prager–Synge identity,
as introduced in
[
18
]

for discontinuous Galerkin methods.
For completeness, we adapt the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]

to the current setting.

Theorem 5.2

(Prager–Synge)
.

For all
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)

and given
u
the solution to (
2.1
),
we have

\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\min_{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\right\}^{2}.

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
s^{\star}
be the orthogonal projection of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}

onto
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

(\nabla s^{\star},\nabla v)=(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(5.2)

Pythagoras’ theorem gives

\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s^{\star}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-s^{\star})\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\min_{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-s^{\star})\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Using that
u
is the solution to (
2.1
)
and definition (
5.2
),
the conclusion is a consequence of the following identities:

\begin{split}\|\nabla(u-s^{\star})\|_{\Omega}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}(\nabla(u-s^{\star}),\nabla v)_{\Omega}\\
&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\nabla s^{\star},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\right\}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\right\}.\end{split}

∎

5.3
Motivation and definition of the error estimator

We can employ duality in optimisation
to control the last term
on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) as

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\{(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\Omega)\\
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{\Omega}.

Therefore, identity (
5.1
) states that the error
is bounded by the sum of two contributions
measuring the lack of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\Omega)
conformity of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}
, i.e.,

\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\min_{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\Omega)\\
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

(5.3)

In the construction of the error estimator,
we measure these lacks of conformity
using local computations on vertex patches
as in (
4.1
).
In other words, we expect that

\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\simeq\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\Big\{\min_{s^{\nu}\in\widetilde{H}^{1}(\omega^{\nu})}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s^{\nu}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}+\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}\Big\}

(5.4)

in some suitable sense, where
\widetilde{H}^{1}(\omega^{\nu})
is defined in (
4.2a
).
This idea of “broken equilibration” was already partially explored in
[
9
]

but only for the second term.

The minimisation problems on the right-hand side are local
but still not computable.
Due to the discrete stable minimisations in Section
4.2
,
the proposed error estimator
is based on replacing the minimisation problems
with their natural finite element counterparts, namely,

\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}:=\min_{v_{h}\in\widetilde{H}^{1}(\omega^{\nu})\cap\mathbb{P}_{p+2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla v_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}

(5.5a)

and

\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\cap\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\\
\div\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

(5.5b)

The minimisers, which we shall henceforth denote by
s_{h}^{\nu}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}
,
are computable as solution to local finite element problems,
so that the proposed estimator is computable.

5.4
Practical computation of the estimator on vertex patches

In order to compute
\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}
in (
5.5a
),
we solve

\begin{cases}\text{find }s_{h}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{P}_{p+2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\widetilde{H}^{1}(\omega^{\nu})\text{ such that}\\
(\nabla s_{h}^{\nu},\nabla z_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}=(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla z_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}&\forall z_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{p+2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\widetilde{H}^{1}(\omega^{\nu}),\end{cases}

(5.6)

where the mean-value may be fixed arbitrarily for internal vertices,
since only
\nabla s_{h}^{\nu}
appears in the definition of the estimator.

In order to compute
\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}
in (
5.5b
),
we have to compute the discrete minimiser in (
5.5b
),
i.e., the discrete flux solving

\begin{cases}\text{find }\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\text{ and }r^{\nu}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\text{ such that}\\
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}+(\div\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h},r^{\nu}_{h})=(-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}&\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
(\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h},q^{\nu}_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}=(f,q^{\nu}_{h})_{\omega^{\nu}}&\forall q^{\nu}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu}).\end{cases}

(5.7)

The above finite element problems are local on each vertex patch
and can be solved in parallel.

5.5
Reliability and efficiency

For all
\nu
in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
, we define the vertex estimator as

\eta_{\nu}^{2}:=\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}^{2}+\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h,\nu}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\Pi^{0}\llbracket\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{e}^{2}

\eta^{2}:=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\eta_{\nu}^{2}.

(5.8)

For a generic union
D

we shall be interested in the error measure

\mathcal{E}^{2}(D):=\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{D}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h})\|_{D}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},\;e\subset D\end{subarray}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\Pi^{0}\llbracket\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{e}^{2}.

(5.9)

In Section
8.1

we shall assess the performance of (
5.9
)
and compare it with a more standard choice:
the two measures behave essentially the same on different
meshes and for different degrees of accuracy.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\gamma
be the shape-regularity constant introduced in Section
2.3
;

N_{{\rm vert}}
the maximal number of vertices per element in the mesh;

C_{{\rm cont},\nu}
as in (
4.6
);

C_{{\rm stab},\nu}
as in (
4.7
).
The following reliability
and local efficiency estimates hold true:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{2}(\Omega)\leq 4N_{{\rm vert}}\sum_{\nu}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\eta_{\nu}^{2}\leq 4N_{{\rm vert}}\max_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\sum_{\nu}\eta_{\nu}^{2}=:c_{\rm rel}\eta^{2},\\
&\eta_{\nu}^{2}\leq 2C_{{\rm stab},\nu}^{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}(\omega^{\nu})=:c_{\rm eff}\mathcal{E}^{2}(\omega^{\nu}).\end{split}

(5.10)

The constants
c_{\rm rel}
and
c_{\rm eff}
are positive,
independent of the choice of the stabilisation
and the mesh size,
but dependent on the regularity parameter
\gamma

in Section
2.3
.
The constant
c_{\rm eff}
possibly depends also on the degree of accuracy
p

through
C_{{\rm stab},\nu}
.
If two distinct mesh elements only share one facet
as stated in Assumption
2.1
,
then
c_{\rm eff}
is independent of
p
.

We conjecture that
c_{\rm eff}
in (
5.10
)
is independent of
p
also for meshes,
which do not satisfy Assumption
2.1
.

The computation of the error estimator involves
the computation of the following quantities:

•

the generalised gradient
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}
in (
3.8
),
which can be seen as an elementwise post-processing of the VE solution;

•

the solution to primal finite element problems on vertex patches
as in (
5.6
) for the computation of
\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}
;

•

the solution to mixed finite element problems on vertex patches
as in (
5.7
)
for the computation of
\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}
.

The computation of the error estimator is local and parallelizable,
whence its cost is comparable to that of a residual error estimator.
Furthermore, on meshes satisfying Assumption
2.1
,
it is
p
-robust and the constants in the upper and lower bound are independent of the choice of the stabilisation.

Remark 5.4

(Element-based estimator)
.

The estimator
\eta_{\nu}
is vertex-based. To obtain an element-based estimator, we can set

\eta_{K}^{2}:=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}^{K}}\eta_{\nu}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

The estimates in (
5.10
) also hold true for
\eta_{K}

if the summation happens on the elements in the upper bound,
and the vertex patch
\omega^{\nu}

is replaced by the following element patch in the lower bound:

\omega^{K}=\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}^{K}}\omega^{\nu}.

Remark 5.5

(Comparison with discontinuous Galerkin methods)
.

As detailed in Lemmas
6.1
and
7.1

below,
the estimator relies on the “broken” Prager–Synge estimation (
5.4
),
instead of the usual Prager–Synge identity in (
5.3
).

For discontinuous Galerkin schemes,
the authors of
[
18
]
constructed two fields

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
in
\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\Omega)
with
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=f
,
and
s_{h}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to insert in (
5.3
).
Similar to ours, the construction in
[
18
]

for
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
and
s_{h}
relies on vertex-patch finite element problems,
with the crucial difference that these problems
involve hat functions of the mesh as a partition of unity.
Specifically, for each vertex
\nu
in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
,
the local finite element problems

s_{h}^{\nu}:=\arg\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{p+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\nu})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\omega^{\nu})\end{subarray}}\|\nabla_{h}(\varphi^{\nu}u_{h})-\nabla v_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}

(5.11a)

and

\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}:=\arg\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\boldsymbol{H}_{0}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
\div\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\varphi^{\nu}f-\nabla\varphi^{\nu}\cdot\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})\end{subarray}}\|\varphi^{\nu}\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})+\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}

(5.11b)

are solved with suitable modifications at boundary vertices.

These local contributions are summed to obtain
the globally conforming fields
s_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
.
For all
K
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
the lower bound takes the form

\begin{split}&\|\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\|_{K}^{2}+\|\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})-\nabla s_{h}\|_{K}^{2}\\
&\leq N_{{\rm vert}}C_{{\rm stab},K}^{2}C_{{\rm cont},K}^{2}\Big(\|\nabla u-\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})\|_{\omega^{K}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-\mathfrak{G}_{h}(u_{h})\|_{\omega^{K}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\\
e\subset\overline{\omega^{K}}\end{subarray}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\Pi^{0}\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{e}^{2}\Big),\end{split}

where

C_{{\rm stab},K}:=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}^{K}}C_{{\rm stab},\nu},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad C_{{\rm cont},K}:=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}^{K}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}.

A key asset of this construction is that the upper-bound in (
5.1
) is constant-free,
leading to guaranteed error bounds.
However, the presence (and the computability) of the partition of unity in (
5.11
)
is essential to obtain globally equilibrated fields
s_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
.
As a result, this approach is fundamentally limited to simplicial
and tensor-product meshes,
where such closed-form partition of unity functions are available.

In contrast, the broken approach we propose here enables to compute the estimator
even if the partition of unity is not explicitly available,
at the price of displacing the constants
N_{{\rm vert}}

and
C_{{\rm cont},\nu}
from the lower bound to the upper bound;
see Theorem
5.3
,
and Lemmas
6.1
and
7.1
below.
For this reason,
the constants and terms in the upper and lower bounds here
are a generalisation of those appearing in the
Galerkin schemes in
[
18
]
,
and therefore they are as good as the state-of-the-art.

6
The flux-type term

We prove upper and (local) lower bounds for the second term on the right-hand side
of (
5.1
).

Lemma 6.1

(Upper bound for the flux-type term)
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}
satisfies Assumption
5.1
. Then,
the following upper bound holds true:

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\right\}^{2}\leq N_{{\rm vert}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}^{2},

(6.1)

where the constant
C_{{\rm cont},\nu}
is that appearing
in (
4.6
) and
N_{{\rm vert}}
denotes the maximal number
of vertices of the elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

We introduce the linear functional
\mathcal{R}\in(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}

given by

\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle:=(f,v)_{\Omega}-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\nabla v)_{\Omega}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

and its dual norm

\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{*}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle.

By Assumption
5.1
, we have

\langle\mathcal{R},\varphi^{\nu}\rangle=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{I}.

(6.2)

Fix
v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
and
\zeta_{\nu}=0
if
\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{B}
and

\zeta_{\nu}=(v,1)_{\omega^{\nu}}/|\omega^{\nu}|
if
\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{I}
.
Since the set
\{\varphi^{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}

is a partition of unity, using (
6.2
),
we have

\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\langle\mathcal{R},\varphi^{\nu}v\rangle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\langle\mathcal{R},\varphi^{\nu}(v-\zeta_{\nu})\rangle.

Introducing the local dual norms

\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega^{\nu})\\
\|\nabla v\|_{\omega^{\nu}}=1\end{subarray}}\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle

(6.3)

and using (
4.6
) and the properties of
\zeta_{\nu}
,
we arrive at

\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle\leq\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}\|\nabla(\varphi^{\nu}(v-\zeta^{\nu})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\leq\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}\|\nabla v\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

Further using Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality leads to

|\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\|\nabla v\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}\Big)\leq N_{{\rm vert}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}^{2}\Big)\|\nabla v\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

Since
v
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{*}^{2}\leq N_{{\rm vert}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}^{2}.

(6.4)

So far, we proved an upper bound for the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
),
i.e., we split it into the sum of local contributions.
Next, we bound such local
contributions
on the right-hand side of (
6.4
)
by means of a quantity that is
computable via the degrees of freedom and can be obtained by means of local computations on
vertex patches.

By Riesz’ representation theorem,
there exists
r^{\nu}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\omega^{\nu})
such that

(\nabla r^{\nu},\nabla v)_{\omega^{\nu}}=\langle\mathcal{R},v\rangle\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega^{\nu}),\qquad\qquad\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}=\left\|\nabla r^{\nu}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

(6.5)

The definition of
\mathcal{R}
allows us to write

(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\nabla r^{\nu},\nabla v)_{\omega^{\nu}}=(f,v)_{\omega^{\nu}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega^{\nu}).

It follows that the function
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}:=-(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\nabla r^{\nu})
belongs to
\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})
with
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}=f
.
As a result,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}
and
r^{\nu}

are the solutions to the following mixed problem:

\begin{cases}\text{find }(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu},r^{\nu})\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\times L^{2}(\omega^{\nu})\text{ such that}\\
(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu},\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu})_{\omega^{\nu}}+(\div\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu},r^{\nu})=(-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu})_{\omega^{\nu}}&\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
(\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu},q^{\nu})_{\omega^{\nu}}=(f,q^{\nu})_{\omega^{\nu}}&\forall q^{\nu}\in L^{2}(\omega^{\nu}).\end{cases}

(6.6)

Due to (
6.3
), (
6.5
), and (
6.6
),
since
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}
solves the above mixed problem, we write

\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}=\left\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}=\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu}),\ \div\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}=f}\left\|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

Recall that
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu}
is the subtriangulation
of
\omega^{\nu}

obtained by merging the subtriangulations
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{K}

of all elements
K
contained in
\omega^{\nu}
.
For future convenience, we also define
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\nu}

as the set of elements
K
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
that are contained in
\omega^{\nu}
.
Recalling the definition in (
5.5
),
we arrive at

\left\|\mathcal{R}\right\|_{\nu,*}\leq\min_{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu}),\ \div\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h}=f}\left\|\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\nu}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}=:\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}.

The bound in (
6.1
) follows from (
6.4
).
∎

Next, we show the (local) lower bound for the flux-type term.

Lemma 6.2

(Lower bound for the flux-type term)
.

Let
C_{{\rm stab},\nu}
be the constant in (
4.7b
).
For all
\nu
in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
, we have

\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

Proof.

The assertion is a consequence of the results stated in (
4.7b
)
and established in Appendix
A
.
Indeed, since
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}
and
-\nabla u

belong to
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
and
\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})

with
\div(-\nabla u)=f
, respectively, we have

\begin{split}\eta_{\nu,{\rm FL}}&:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}\in\boldsymbol{H}(\div,\omega^{\nu})\\
\div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}=f\end{subarray}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\nu}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla u\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.\end{split}

∎

7
The potential-type term

We prove upper and (local) lower bounds
for the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
).

Lemma 7.1

(Upper bound for the potential-type term)
.

For all
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the following upper bound holds true

\begin{split}&\min_{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s\|_{\Omega}^{2}\\
&\leq 4N_{{\rm vert}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\Big(\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h,\nu}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\Pi^{0}\llbracket\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{e}^{2}\Big),\end{split}

where the constant
C_{{\rm cont},\nu}
appears in (
4.6
)
and
N_{{\rm vert}}
denotes the maximal number of vertices of the elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

Recall that
s_{h}^{\nu}
are the minimisers
in the definition of
\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}
in (
5.5
).
For interior vertices, the mean value of the
s_{h}^{\nu}

can be freely chosen;
henceforth, we assume that
(s_{h}^{\nu},1)_{\omega^{\nu}}=(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h},1)_{\omega^{\nu}}

whenever
\nu
belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{I}
.

We introduce

s_{h}:=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\varphi^{\nu}s_{h}^{\nu}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

The triangle inequality entails

\min_{s\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq 2\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+2\|\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h})\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

As for the first term, we have

\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}.

As for the second term,
we plug in the partition of unity
\{\varphi^{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}
:

\|\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h})\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\nabla_{h}(\varphi^{\nu}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h}^{\nu}))\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq N_{{\rm vert}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\left\|\nabla_{h}(\varphi^{\nu}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h}^{\nu}))\right\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}.

Next, we employ (
4.6
)
and the fact that
s_{h}^{\nu}
belongs to
H^{1}(\omega^{\nu})

with
(s_{h}^{\nu},1)_{\omega^{\nu}}=(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h},1)_{\omega^{\nu}}
:

\|\nabla_{h}(\varphi^{\nu}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h}^{\nu}))\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}\leq C_{{\rm cont},\nu}^{2}\Big(\|\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h}^{\nu}))\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h,\nu}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\Pi^{0}\llbracket\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{e}^{2}\Big).

The assertion follows from the triangle inequality:

\|\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h}-s_{h}^{\nu}))\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}\leq 2\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla_{h}(\Pi^{\nabla}_{p}u_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}+2\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s_{h}^{\nu}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}^{2}.

∎

Next, we show the (local) lower bound for the potential-type term.

Lemma 7.2

(Lower bound for the potential-type term)
.

Let
C_{{\rm stab},\nu}
be the constant in (
4.7a
).
For all
\nu
in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
, we have

\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\|\nabla u-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

Proof.

The assertion is a consequence of the results stated in (
4.7a
)
and established in Appendix
A
.
Indeed, since
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}
and
u
belong to
\mathbb{P}_{p+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})

and
H^{1}(\omega^{\nu})
, respectively,
we have

\begin{split}\eta_{\nu,{\rm PT}}&:=\min_{s_{h}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{P}_{p+2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap H^{1}(\omega^{\nu})}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s_{h}^{\nu}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\\
&\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\min_{s^{\nu}\in H^{1}(\omega^{\nu})}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla s^{\nu}\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\leq C_{{\rm stab},\nu}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}_{h}-\nabla u\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.\end{split}

∎

8.2
The adaptive algorithm

We consider the usual adaptive algorithm based on the four steps procedure

\textbf{SOLVE}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\textbf{ESTIMATE}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\textbf{MARK}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\textbf{REFINE}.

Some details follow:

•

while solving the virtual element method
for a given mesh

and degree of accuracy

and is computed in a subsequent step as a postprocessing of

;

•

while computing the local error estimator

on each vertex patch

,
it is necessary to compute the generalised gradient

,
the vertex contributions

,
and the jumps of

•

we use Dörfler’s marking strategy

with bulk parameter

;

•

one of the advantages of employing polytopic meshes in adaptive methods resides in easily handling hanging nodes;
since we are not interested in coarsening the mesh,
we only employ initial uniform Cartesian and triangular meshes;

h

by a standard splitting of each quadrilateral and triangular shape into four siblings with halved diameter, respectively.

9
Conclusions

We proposed a generalised gradient formulation for the standard nodal virtual element method in two dimensions.
On the one hand, the employed generalised gradient can be seen as a post-processing of the discrete solution,
which provided us with an alternative computable
approximation of the exact solution.

On the other hand, the generalised gradient formulation allowed us to improve the state-of-the-art of a posteriori error estimates in virtual elements
and other polytopic methods in general,
yet keeping in mind that a modified error measure is considered.
In fact, standard a posteriori error bounds on general meshes for arbitrary order degree of accuracy
p

typically involve constants depending on
p
and the choice of the stabilisations;
the reason for this is to be sought both in the choice of the computable error measure and the error estimator.
By changing these two quantities,
for the first time in the literature on polytopic elements
we ended up with robust a posteriori error estimates.
The proposed error estimator contains terms and constants
that extend those appearing while using other nonconforming methods,
such as the discontinuous Galerkin method,
on standard meshes.

(A.2)

A.1
Bounds for fully general meshes

Theorem A.1

(Non-
p
-robust stable minimisation)
.

We have

m^{\rm g}_{h}\leq C(p,\gamma)m^{\rm g},\qquad\qquad\qquad m^{\rm d}_{h}\leq C(p,\gamma)m^{\rm d},

where the constants are independent of the data
r_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}
.

Proof.

First, we establish the existence of generic constants
C^{\rm g}
and
C^{\rm d}

such that
m^{\rm g}_{h}\leq C^{\rm g}m^{\rm g}

and
m^{\rm d}_{h}\leq C^{\rm d}m^{\rm d}
.
We shall investigate the scaling properties in a second step.
We only focus on
C^{\rm g}
;
the estimate involving
C^{\rm d}
is established similarly.

Given
s_{h}
and
s
the discrete and continuous minimisers,
the operators

A^{\rm g}_{h}:\boldsymbol{G}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\to\mathbb{P}_{p+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\ni\boldsymbol{G}_{h}-\nabla s_{h}

and

A^{\rm g}:\boldsymbol{G}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\to\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\ni\boldsymbol{G}_{h}-\nabla s

are linear.

Hence, the following applications
are seminorms on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
:

\boldsymbol{G}_{h}\to\|A^{\rm g}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}},\qquad\qquad\boldsymbol{G}_{h}\to\|A^{\rm g}_{h}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

Since the space
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
is finite dimensional,
if we can show that
A^{\rm g}_{h}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})=0
whenever
A^{\rm g}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})=0
,
then these seminorms have the same kernel.
Thus, since all seminorms with the same kernel on finite dimensional spaces are equivalent,
we establish that there exists a constant
C^{\rm g}
such that

\|A^{\rm g}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}\leq C^{\rm g}\|A^{\rm g}_{h}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})\|_{\omega^{\nu}}.

(A.3)

Estimate (
A.3
) would then imply
that
m^{\rm g}\leq C^{\rm g}m^{\rm g}_{h}

for all
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}
in
\mathbb{P}_{p+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
, which concludes the argument.

Therefore, we consider
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}
in
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{RT}}_{p}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
such that
A^{\rm g}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})=0
.
We have that
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}=\nabla s
in
H^{1}(\omega^{\nu})
for the continuous minimiser
s
,
so that
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}
belongs to
\nabla(\mathbb{P}_{p+2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})\cap H^{1}(\omega^{\nu}))
.
It follows that
\boldsymbol{G}_{h}=\nabla s_{h}
for the discrete minimiser too,
leading to
A^{\rm g}_{h}(\boldsymbol{G}_{h})=0
.

The constants
C^{\rm g}
and
C^{\rm d}
obtained above depend on the space

\mathbb{P}_{p+1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu})
, meaning that
they depend on
p
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu}
.
We can show that the dependence on
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\nu}

occurs only through
\gamma
by using
standard scaling arguments involving the Piola map and reference patches.
∎

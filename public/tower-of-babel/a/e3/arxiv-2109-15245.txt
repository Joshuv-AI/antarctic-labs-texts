A conjectural formula for DR g ​ ( a , − a ) ​ λ g \mathrm{DR}_{g}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}
Alexandr Buryak, Francisco Hernández Iglesias, Sergey Shadrin

Abstract




Abstract.
We propose a conjectural formula for
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}
and
check all its expected properties. Our formula refines the one point
case of a similar conjecture made by the first named author in
collaboration with Guéré and Rossi, and we prove that the two
conjectures are in fact equivalent, though in a quite non-trivial
way.

Keywords.
moduli of curves; tautological ring; double ramification
cycle

1. Introduction

In
[
Bur15
]
the first named author defined new Hamiltonian
integrable hierarchies, the so-called double ramification hierarchies,
associated to cohomological field theories. They are conjectured
in
[
Bur15
]
to be Miura equivalent to the Dubrovin–Zhang
hierarchies constructed in
[
DZ01
,
BPS12
]
. This conjecture is further
refined and made more explicit in
[
BDGR18
]
, and in
[
BGR19
]
it
is reduced to a system of conjectural relations between some
explicitly defined classes in the tautological ring of the moduli
space of curves
R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
.

The one point case of the conjecture in
[
BGR19
]
gives a
surprisingly simple expression for the product of the top Chern class
of the Hodge bundle
\lambda_{g}\in R^{g}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1})
and the push-forward
of the double ramification cycle
\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\in R^{g}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})
under the map that forgets the second marked point. For
the definition of the double ramification cycle
\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)
we
refer, for instance, to
[
BSSZ15
]
, and for general information on
the tautological rings of the moduli spaces of curves to a recent
survey of Schmitt
[
Sch20
]
.

In this paper we propose a refinement of the one point case of the
conjecture in
[
BGR19
]
. We conjecture a formula for

\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}\in R^{2g}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})
in terms of a very
simple linear combination of natural strata equipped with psi classes
of the same type as in
[
BGR19
]
. We analyze our formula in detail
and we prove that it satisfies virtually all properties one might
expect from the class
\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}
, including the
intersections with all natural boundary divisors in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}
and
with the psi classes, and finally using these properties we also show
that our conjecture is in fact equivalent to the one point case of the
conjecture in
[
BGR19
]
.

Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we formulate our conjecture,
explain its relation to the one point case of the conjecture
in
[
BGR19
]
, and state the expected properties of our formula. In
Section
3
we introduce our main tools, a variety of
corollaries of the Liu–Pandharipande relations among the tautological
classes
[
LP11
]
, and prove all properties stated before.

2. Conjectural formula and its properties

2.1. Notation

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the Deligne–Mumford compactification of the moduli
space of curves with
n
marked points. There is a natural action of
the symmetric group
S_{n}
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
by relabeling the points. In
particular, for
n=2
we will use the morphism that permutes the first
and second marked points that we denote by
\mathop{\mathsf{}}{\scriptstyle{(12)_{*}}}\colon R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})\to R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})
.

Let
\sigma\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},2}

glue the second marked point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}
and the first marked
point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}
into a node and identify the first marked point
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}
(respectively, the second marked point in

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}
) with the first (respectively, the second) marked point
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},2}
. Let
c_{1}\in R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2})
,
c_{2}\in R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2})
. It is convenient for us to denote throughout the
text
c_{1}\diamond c_{2}\coloneqq\sigma_{*}(c_{1}\otimes c_{2})
and we use

\diamond
as an associative operation on classes in moduli spaces
with two marked points.

With the first two points distinguished, we can extend the notation

\diamond
to the push-forwards of the morphisms
\sigma\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2+n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},2+n}
that glue the
second marked point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}
with the first marked point of

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2+n}
into a node and identify the first marked point in

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}
(respectively, the second marked point in

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2+n}
) with the first (respectively, the second) marked
point in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},2+n}
. We can do the same for the similar
morphisms
\sigma\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2+n}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},2+n}
.

.

We have

prove the conjecture for the case

Proposition 2.2
.

We have

It follows from the fact that

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that an expected property of
a^{-2g}\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}
is that it is invariant under
\mathop{\mathsf{}}{\scriptstyle{(12)_{*}}}
, and indeed we prove below that
\mathop{\mathsf{}}{\scriptstyle{(12)_{*}}}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}={\mathsf{B}}^{g}
,
g\geq 1
, so in fact we can reformulate the statement of Proposition
2.2
as
\pi_{*}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}=B^{g}_{2g-1}
.

In fact, it is also possible to prove a much stronger statement than Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 2.4
.

The two conjectural formulas,
a^{-2g}\pi_{*}(\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\lambda_{g})=B^{g}_{2g-1}
and
a^{-2g}\mathrm{DR}_{{g}}(a,-a)\lambda_{g}={\mathsf{B}}^{g}
, are equivalent.

The first formula follows from the second one by Proposition
2.2
. The implication in the other direction is quite non-trivial, and we postpone its proof until Section
3.7
.

3.2. The symmetry property

Denote
\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g}_{d\mathop{|}k}:=\mathop{\mathsf{}}{\scriptstyle{(12)_{*}}}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g}_{d\mathop{|}k}
. We will
use the following conventions to simplify notation:

\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{0}_{-1\mathop{|}0}\diamond\psi^{d}_{2}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}:=\psi^{d}_{2}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{1}^{d}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{0}_{-1\mathop{|}0}:=\psi_{1}^{d}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}.

Note that there is the
following recursion relation for the classes

\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g}_{d\mathop{|}k}
:

\displaystyle\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g}_{d\mathop{|}k+1}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\

where
d_{1}=-1
is allowed in the sum to include the case
k=0
, as explained before. Let us now prove Equation (
2.3
). Let

\displaystyle E:={\mathsf{B}}^{g}-\mathop{\mathsf{}}{\scriptstyle{(12)_{*}}}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=2g-1\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{g_{1}}(-1)^{k}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{1}}_{d_{1}\mathop{|}k}\diamond\psi_{2}^{d_{2}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}}-\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=2g-1\end{subarray}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{g_{2}}(-1)^{k}\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{2}}_{d_{2}\mathop{|}k}.

Let
E_{\ell}
denote the terms of
E
consisting of exactly
\ell
components,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle E_{\ell}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=2g-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell-1}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{1}}_{d_{1}\mathop{|}\ell-1}\diamond\psi_{2}^{d_{2}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}}-\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
d_{1}+d_{2}=2g-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\ell-1}\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{2}}_{d_{2}\mathop{|}\ell-1}.

Lemma 3.4
.

We can write
E_{1}+\dots+E_{\ell}
as an expression involving only graphs with
\ell+1
vertices. In particular:

\displaystyle E_{1}+\dots+E_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+s=\ell-1\\

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction. The base of induction is the
\ell=1
case, which follows immediately from (
3.4
):

\displaystyle E_{1}=\psi_{2}^{2g}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}-\psi_{1}^{2g}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}}=-\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\

In order to prove the step of induction, assume the lemma is true for
\ell\geq 1
. Then

(3.5)

\begin{split}E_{1}+\dots+E_{\ell+1}&=(-1)^{\ell+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+s=\ell-1\\

&\quad+\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\

We can split the first summand into two in the following way:

•

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+s=\ell-1\\

\displaystyle(-1)^{d_{1}+d_{2}}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{1}}_{d_{1}\mathop{|}r}\diamond\psi_{2}^{d_{2}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}}\diamond\psi_{1}^{d_{3}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{3},2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{4}}_{d_{4}\mathop{|}s}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+s=\ell,\,r\geq 1\\
g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}=g\\
d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}=2g-2\end{subarray}}(-1)^{d_{1}}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{1}}_{d_{1}\mathop{|}r}\diamond\psi_{1}^{d_{2}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{3}}_{d_{3}\mathop{|}s}.

)
corresponds to the missing terms with

in the last

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+s=\ell-1\\

\displaystyle(-1)^{d_{1}+d_{2}}\overset{\rightharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{1}}_{d_{1}\mathop{|}r}\diamond\psi_{2}^{d_{2}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},2}}\diamond\psi_{1}^{d_{3}}|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{3},2}}\diamond\overset{\leftharpoondown}{\mathsf{c}}^{g_{4}}_{d_{4}\mathop{|}s}

∎

\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}\cdot\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{2}(\mathrm{DR}_{g}(1,-1)\lambda_{g})=\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}\cdot\mathrm{DR}_{g}(1,0,-1)\lambda_{g}=1|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}}\diamond\mathrm{DR}_{g}(0,0)\lambda_{g}=(-1)^{g}1|_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}}\diamond\lambda_{g}^{2}=0.

In the case
C^{g}={\mathsf{B}}^{g}
, from (
3.21
) it is easy to
see that the class
\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{2}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}-\psi_{1}^{2g}
is supported on the
stratum in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,3}
that doesn’t intersect the divisor
corresponding to
\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}
. Therefore,

\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}\cdot(\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{2}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}-\psi_{1}^{2g})=0
, but we also
obviously have
\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}\cdot\psi_{1}^{2g}=0
, which gives

\delta_{0}^{\{1,3\}}\cdot\widetilde{\pi}^{*}_{2}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Assuming
\pi_{2*}\left(\mathrm{DR}_{g}(1,-1)\lambda_{g}\right)=\pi_{2*}{\mathsf{B}}^{g}
,
the equality
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(1,-1)\lambda_{g}={\mathsf{B}}^{g}
immediately follows from
Formula (
3.26
) by the induction on
g
. This
completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

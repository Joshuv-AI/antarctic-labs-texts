Stereographic compactification and affine bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms. Thanks: We are very grateful to André Costa and Maria Michalska for conversations, comments and insight.
Vincent Grandjean, Roger Oliveira

Abstract
. Let σ q : ℝ q → 𝐒 q ∖ N q \sigma_{q}:{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\to{\bf S}^{q}\setminus N_{q} be the inverse of the stereographic projection with centre the north pole N q N_{q} . Let W i W_{i} be a closed subset of ℝ q i \mathbb{R}^{q_{i}} , for i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 . Let Φ : W 1 → W 2 \Phi:W_{1}\to W_{2} be a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism. The main result states that the homeomorphism σ q 2 ∘ Φ ∘ σ q 1 − 1 \sigma_{q_{2}}\circ\Phi\circ\sigma_{q_{1}}^{-1} is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism, extending bi-Lipschitz-ly at N q 1 N_{q_{1}} with value N q 2 N_{q_{2}} whenever W 1 W_{1} is unbounded. As two straightforward applications in the polynomially bounded o-minimal context over the real numbers, we obtain for free a version at infinity of: 1) Sampaio’s tangent cone result; 2) Links preserving re-parametrization of definable bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms of Valette.



1.
Introduction

Any subset
S
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is equipped with the
outer metric structure, where the distance between points of
S
is their (Euclidean) distance in
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.
Thus (outer) Lipschitz mappings
S_{1}\to S_{2}
, for

S_{i}
a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{q_{i}}
, are well defined.

Given a positive integer
q
, let
N_{q}=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}
,
the north pole of the unit sphere
{\bf S}^{q}
of
\mathbb{R}^{q+1}
. The inverse of the
stereographic projection of
{\bf S}^{q}
onto
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
with centre
N_{q}
is denoted
\sigma_{q}:\mathbb{R}^{q}\to{\bf S}^{q}\setminus N_{q}
.
Let
\widetilde{S}
be the closure in
{\bf S}^{q}
of
\sigma_{q}(S)
where
S
is a closed
subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.

For
i=1,2
, let
W_{i}
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{q_{i}}
.
If
\Phi:W_{1}\to W_{2}
is a homeomorphism, the stereographic pre-compactification of
\Phi
is the following homeomorphism

\sigma_{q_{2}}\circ\Phi\circ\sigma_{q_{1}}^{-1}:\sigma_{q_{1}}(W_{1})\to\sigma_{q_{2}}(W_{2}).

Since
W_{1},W_{2}
are closed, we observe that the stereographic pre-compactification
of
\Phi
extends as a homeomorphism
\widetilde{\Phi}:\widetilde{W}_{1}\to\widetilde{W}_{2}
mapping

N_{q_{1}}
onto
N_{q_{2}}
whenever
W_{1}
is unbounded.
We call this extension the stereographic compactification of
\Phi
. If
W_{1}
is compact, so is
W_{2}
and the stereographic pre-compactification of
\Phi
is
its stereographic compactification.

The main
result of this note is the
following

Theorem
10
.
The mapping
\Phi
is bi-Lipschitz if
and only if its stereographic compactification
\widetilde{\Phi}
is bi-Lipschitz.

The main result is a consequence of Lemma
9
presented below.
We recall that the Euclidean inversion of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
is the
following mapping

\iota_{q}:\mathbb{R}^{q}\setminus{\bf 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}\setminus{\bf 0},\;\;{\bf x}\;\mapsto\;\frac{{\bf x}}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}.

Let
\Phi:W_{1}\to W_{2}
be a homeomorphism between the closed subsets
W_{i}

of
\mathbb{R}^{q_{i}}
,
i=1,2
.
The inversion of the mapping
\Phi:W_{1}\to W_{2}
is defined as follows

{\iota(\Phi)}:\iota_{q_{1}}(W_{1}\setminus{\bf 0})\to\iota_{q_{2}}(W_{2}\setminus{\bf 0}),\;\;{\bf x}\mapsto{\iota(\Phi)}({\bf x}):=\iota_{q_{2}}\circ\Phi\circ\iota_{q_{1}}^{-1}({\bf x}).

The next result (so called the inversion lemma) is the main tool we use to obtain the main result. It is
most certainly of interest on its own, and can be applied in many different
contexts.

Lemma
9
.
Assume furthermore that,
either
W_{i}
contains the origin
{\bf 0}\in\mathbb{R}^{q_{i}}
for
i=1,2
and

\Phi({\bf 0})={\bf 0}
, or
{\bf 0}\notin W_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

The homeomorphism
{\iota(\Phi)}
is bi-Lipschitz
if and only
\Phi
is.
Moreover, if
W_{1}
is unbounded, then
{\iota(\Phi)}
extends bi-Lipschitz-ly at

{\bf 0}
taking the value
{\bf 0}
.

Our interest in this problem arose from results of the recent PhD
Thesis of the second named author
[
6
]
. Indeed, the bi-Lipschitz
classification of local plane objects germs, at the origin, respectively at
infinity and in correspondence by the Euclidean inversion, presented
strikingly similar properties, now explained by Lemma
9
.

The main result is a convenient reformulation
of the inversion lemma, and is also very much in tune with the joint works
of the first named author
[
2
,
3
]
which expand
the results of the recent PhD Thesis
[
1
]
.

The paper is organised as follows: Section 2 introduces various
preliminary materials and notations. Section
3

presents the special case of a global bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.
Section

4
and Section
5
, respectively,
show germ-ified versions of the inversion lemma, namely Lemma

7
at
\infty
and, respectively Lemma

8
at
{\bf 0}
. Section

6
is the short proof of our main tool, the inversion lemma

9
. The main result is dealt with in Section
7
.
The last section presents two immediate applications,
versions at infinity of two results about germs of definable subsets at
the origin: Proposition
15
(Sampaio’s tangent
cone result
[
8
]
) and Proposition
17

(Valette’s Links preserving re parametrization of definable bi-Lipschitz

[
10
]
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

The Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is equipped with the Euclidean distance, denoted

|-|
. We denote by
B_{r}^{q}
the open ball of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
of radius
r
and centred
at the origin
{\bf 0}
, by
{\bf B}_{r}^{q}
its closure and by
{\bf S}_{r}^{q-1}
its
boundary. The open ball of radius
r
and with centre
{\bf x}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is

B^{q}({\bf x}_{0},r)
, its closure is
{\bf B}^{q}({\bf x}_{0},r)
and
{\bf S}^{q-1}({\bf x}_{0},r)
is
its boundary. The unit sphere
{\bf S}_{1}^{q-1}
is simply denoted by
{\bf S}^{q-1}
.

If
S
is any subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
, its closure in
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is
{\rm clos}(S)
, and
S^{*}

is
S\setminus{\bf 0}
.

Let
{\bf U}_{q}
be the punctured affine space
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}^{*}
.

Compactifying the space
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
with the point
\infty
at infinity as

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}:={\mathbb{R}^{q}}\sqcup\infty={\bf 0}\sqcup{\bf U}_{q}\sqcup\infty

yields a space that is smoothly diffeomorphic to the unit
sphere
{\bf S}^{q}
of
\mathbb{R}^{q+1}
, using the stereographic projections centred at
the ”north” and ”south” poles of
{\bf S}^{q}
. Under this correspondence, the
points
{\bf 0}
and
\infty
are antipodal.

If
S
is any subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
its closure in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is
\overline{S}
.
Thus
S
is unbounded if and only if
\overline{S}={\rm clos}(S)\cup\infty
.

The germ
({\mathbb{R}^{q}},\infty)
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
at infinity is well defined, and can be
considered as a germ in
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
as well as in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.

Let
{\gamma}
be a point of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. Let
({\bf x}_{n})_{n},({\bf y}_{n})_{n}
be two sequences
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
converging to
{\gamma}
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. Let
{\bf z}_{n}
be
{\bf x}_{n}
or
{\bf y}_{n}

and let

z_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|{\bf z}_{n}-{\gamma}|&{\rm if}&{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\\
|{\bf z}_{n}|&{\rm if}&{\gamma}=\infty\end{array}\right.

We will use the following notation

{\bf x}_{n}\sim{\bf y}_{n}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}}\in[a,b]\;\;{\rm for}\;\;a,b>0\;\;{\rm whenever}\;\;n\gg 1,

as well as the next one

{\bf x}_{n}\;{\lesssim}\;{\bf y}_{n}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}}\in[0,a]\;\;{\rm for}\;\;a>0\;\;{\rm whenever}\;\;n\gg 1,

and the last one

{\bf x}_{n}=o({\bf y}_{n})\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\lim_{n}\frac{x_{n}}{y_{n}}=0.

2.2.
On affine subsets

Any non-empty subset
S
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
inherits from the ambient Euclidean
structure of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
the outer metric space structure
(S,d_{S})
, where

d_{S}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}):=|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|

for any pair of points
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}
of
S
.
We recall that if a mapping

\varphi:(S,d_{S})\to{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
C
it extends
as a Lipschitz mapping
({\rm clos}(S),d_{{\rm clos}(S)})\to{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
with the same
Lipschitz constant
C
. In practise we can assume that
S
is closed in
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.

In order to ease accumulation of hypotheses and notations, we introduce the
following

Definition 1
.

A
q
-affine subset is a non-empty closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
with
q\geq 1
.

An affine subset is a
q
-affine subset for some positive integer
q
.

Since any affine subset
S
is equipped with the outer metric space
structure
(S,d_{S})
described above, we introduce the following

Definition 2
.

A Lipschitz mapping
S\to T
between the affine subsets
S,T
is a Lipschitz
mapping
(S,d_{S})\to(T,d_{T})
.

2.3.
On the inversion

The inversion of the (punctured) affine space
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
, defined as

\iota_{q}:{\bf U}_{q}\to{\bf U}_{q},\;\;{\bf x}\mapsto\frac{{\bf x}}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}

is a
C^{\infty}
(semi-algebraic) diffeomorphism, and extends as a (semi-algebraic) homeomorphism (
C^{\infty}
actually) over
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
exchanging the origin

{\bf 0}
and the point at infinity
\infty
.

Let
{\bf x}
be any point of
{\bf U}_{q}
. Let

R({\bf x}):=\mathbb{R}{\bf x}

be the real vector line through

{\bf x}
.
The tangent space of
{\bf U}_{q}
at
{\bf x}
decomposes as the Euclidean orthogonal sum

T_{\bf x}{\bf U}_{q}=R({\bf x})\oplus{\bf S}({\bf x})\;\;{\rm where}\;\;{\bf S}({\bf x}):=T_{\bf x}{\bf S}_{R}^{q-1}.

Observe that
{\bf S}(s{\bf x})={\bf S}({\bf x})
and
R(s{\bf x})=R({\bf x})
,
as vector subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
, whenever
s\neq 0
.
An elementary computation shows that in the previous orthogonal basis of

T_{\bf x}{\bf U}_{q}
we obtain

D_{\bf x}\iota_{q}:=-\frac{1}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}Id_{R({\bf x})}\oplus\frac{1}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}Id_{{\bf S}({\bf x})}=\frac{1}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}\left[-Id_{R({\bf x})}\oplus Id_{{\bf S}({\bf x})}\right]

In particular
D_{\bf x}\iota_{q}
is an orthogonal mapping, since
|{\bf x}|^{2}D_{\bf x}\iota_{q}
is simply the orthogonal symmetry w.r.t. the hyperplane

{\bf S}({\bf x})
. We thus deduce the (Euclidean) norm of
D_{\bf x}\iota_{q}

(2.1)

\|D_{\bf x}\iota_{q}\|=\frac{1}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}.

2.4.
Elementary, yet very useful, identities

We recall the following known estimates

Claim 3
.

Let
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
and
C>0
such that
|{\bf x}^{\prime}|\geq(1+C)|{\bf x}|
. Then

\frac{C}{1+C}|{\bf x}^{\prime}|\,\leq\,|{\bf x}^{\prime}-{\bf x}|\,\leq\,\frac{2+C}{1+C}|{\bf x}^{\prime}|.

Given
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}\in{\bf U}_{q}
, we define

e:=|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}|,\;\;r_{i}:=|{\bf x}_{i}|,\;\;{\bf y}_{i}:=\iota_{q}({\bf x}_{i}),\;\;E:=|{\bf y}_{1}-{\bf y}_{2}|\;\;{\rm and}\;\;R_{i}:=|{\bf y}_{i}|,\;\;i=1,2.

We assume that
r_{1}=(1+C)r_{2}
for some
C\geq 0
. Let
2{\theta}\in[0,\pi]
be
the angle between
{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}
(thus between
{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2}
as well).
Then
r_{1}-r_{2}=Cr_{2}
and
R_{2}-R_{1}=CR_{1}
. We recall that the law of cosines is the following identity

(2.2)

e^{2}=(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}+(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}=r_{2}^{2}[C^{2}+4(1+C)\sin^{2}{\theta}].

The inversion and the law of cosines give the following identity

(2.3)

E=R_{1}R_{2}\cdot e\;\;{\rm and}\;\;e=r_{1}r_{2}\cdot E.

3.
Inversion mapping and global bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms

We present a special occurrence of the inversion lemma.
Although it is likely that it has already been written in a few books,
we give a proof, following from elementary Lipschitz analysis.

Let
\mathcal{L}(a,b)
be the space of
\mathbb{R}
-linear mappings
\mathbb{R}^{a}\to\mathbb{R}^{b}
.

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
be a Lipschitz mapping with Lipschitz constant

A_{\varphi}
:

{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\;\Longrightarrow\;|\varphi({\bf x})-\varphi({\bf x}^{\prime})|\;\leq\;A_{\varphi}\cdot|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\varphi)
be the set of points where
\varphi
is differentiable. Rademacher
Theorem states that the complement
{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\setminus\mathcal{D}(\varphi)
is of null
measure
[
7
,
5
]
. We consider the following closed subset

{\Delta}(\varphi):={\rm clos}\left(\left\{({\bf x},D_{\bf x}\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi)\times\mathcal{L}(q,p)\right\}\right)\subset{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\times\mathcal{L}(p,q).

Let
\pi_{\mathcal{L}}:{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\times\mathcal{L}(p,q)\to\mathcal{L}(p,q)
be the projection onto the second
factor. Let

\mathcal{L}(\varphi):=\pi_{\mathcal{L}}({\Delta}).

For any
{\bf x}\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi)
the Lipschitz
condition on
\varphi
yields the following estimate about the norm
of
D_{\bf x}\varphi

\|D_{\bf x}\varphi\|\,\leq\,A_{\varphi}.

Since the norm is continuous over
\mathcal{L}(q,p)
,
we deduce that that

(3.1)

L\in\mathcal{L}(\varphi)\;\Longrightarrow\;\|L\|\leq A_{\varphi}.

Let
H:{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
be a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism mapping
the origin onto itself, with Lipschitz constant
A_{H}>0
:

{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{A_{H}}\cdot|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|\;\leq\;|H({\bf x})-H({\bf x}^{\prime})|\;\leq\;A_{H}\cdot|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|.

Therefore from Estimate (
3.1
) we get

L\in\mathcal{L}(H)\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{A_{H}}\;\leq\;\|L\|\;\leq\;A_{H}.

The mapping
H
extends as a homeomorphism of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
mapping
\infty
onto

\infty
.

The inversion of
H
is the mapping
\iota_{q}\circ H\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}
. It is a homeomorphism of
{\bf U}_{q}
which extends continuously
to
{\bf 0}
, taking the value
{\bf 0}
, as the homeomorphism
\iota(H):{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\to{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. More precisely the following holds true

Proposition 4
.

The inversion
\iota(H)
of
H
is bi-Lipschitz.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\iota(H)
is Lipschitz, since
\iota(H^{-1})=\iota(H)^{-1}
. Let
{\bf y}\in{\bf U}_{q}
and
let
{\bf x}:=\iota_{q}^{-1}({\bf y})
and
{\bf z}:=H({\bf x})
. Since
H({\bf 0})={\bf 0}
,
we find

\frac{1}{A_{H}\cdot|{\bf y}|}\;\leq\;|{\bf z}|\;\leq\;\frac{A_{H}}{|{\bf y}|}

If
{\bf x}
is a point of
{\bf U}_{q}
at which
H
is differentiable, we find
the following estimate

\|D_{\bf y}\iota(H)\|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{|{\bf z}|^{2}}\cdot A_{H}\cdot\frac{1}{|{\bf y}|^{2}}\;\leq\;A_{H}^{3}.

Since
\iota_{q}
is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism, the subset

\iota_{q}({\mathbb{R}^{q}}\setminus\mathcal{D}(H))
has null measure. Thus
\iota(H)
is
differentiable outside a subset of zero measure with uniformly bounded
first derivatives. Thus it is Lipschitz.
∎

Let
\omega
be either
{\bf 0}
or
\infty
. Let
h:(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}},\omega)\to(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}},\omega)
be a germ of homeomorphism which is bi-Lipschitz over

({\bf U}_{q},\omega)
. Let
\omega^{*}
be the point of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
antipodal to

\omega
, that is

\{\omega,\omega^{*}\}=\{{\bf 0},\infty\}.

The map germ
\iota_{q}\circ h\circ\iota_{q}^{-1}:({\bf U}_{q},\omega^{*})\to({\bf U}_{q},\omega^{*})
extends as a homeomorphism germ
\iota(h):(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}},\omega^{*})\to(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}},\omega^{*})
. A consequence of Proposition

4
is the (now expected) following result,
initial motivation of the paper:

Corollary 5
.

The germ of homeomorphism
\iota(h)
is bi-Lipschitz over

({\bf U}_{q},\omega^{*})
.

Remark 6
.

The proof of Proposition
4
we gave uses Rademacher
Theorem, and is a direct proof. But this result is a special case of Lemma

9
, whose demonstration, although longer and mostly by absurd,
uses even more elementary arguments.

4.
Inversion and germs of bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms at infinity

Let
\sigma
be a point of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. Following Definition
1

the notion of germ of
q
-affine subset at
\sigma
is well defined. If
\tau
is
a point of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, the notion of Lipschitz mapping of affine germs
(S,\sigma)\to(T,\tau)
is also well defined by Definition
2
.

Let
\phi:(Y_{1},\infty)\to(Y_{2},\infty)
be a germ of bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
between
q_{i}
-affine subsets germs
(Y_{i},\infty)
with
i=1,2
.
There exists a positive constant
A_{\phi}
such that

{\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}\in Y_{1}\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{A_{\phi}}\cdot|{\bf y}^{\prime}-{\bf y}|\,\leq\,|\phi({\bf y}^{\prime})-\phi({\bf y})|\,\leq\,A_{\phi}\cdot|{\bf y}^{\prime}-{\bf y}|,

Thus we can assume that the Lipschitz constant
A_{\phi}
is such that the following estimates are
also satisfied

{\bf y}\in Y_{1}\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{A_{\phi}}\cdot|{\bf y}|\,\leq\,|\phi({\bf y})|\,\leq\,A_{\phi}\cdot|{\bf y}|.

With the notations of Section
2.1
, we find
\phi({\bf y})\sim{\bf y}
.

For
i=1,2
, let
X_{i}
be the closure
{\rm clos}(\iota_{q_{i}}(Y_{i}))
of
\iota_{q_{i}}(Y_{i})

in
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. The inversion of
\phi
is the mapping defined as follows

{\iota(\phi)}:(X_{1},{\bf 0})\to(X_{2},{\bf 0}),\;\;{\bf x}\to{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\iota_{q_{2}}\circ\phi\circ\iota_{q_{1}}^{-1}({\bf x})&{\rm if}&{\bf x}\in X_{1}^{*}\\
{\bf 0}&{\rm if}&{\bf x}={\bf 0}\end{array}\right.

It is
clearly a germ of homeomorphism which extends continuously at
{\bf 0}
taking
the value
{\bf 0}
at
{\bf 0}
.
The homogeneity of the Euclidean metric as well
as the existence of the inversion mapping yield the following result.

Lemma 7
.

If
\phi:(Y_{1},\infty)\to(Y_{2},\infty)
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism germ between

q_{i}
-affine subsets germs
(Y_{i},\infty)
, for
i=1,2
, then its inversion

{\iota(\phi)}:(X_{1},{\bf 0})\to(X_{2},{\bf 0})
is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism germ.

Proof.

First let us denote
h:=\phi\circ\iota_{q_{1}}^{-1}
, that is

h({\bf x})=\phi\left(\frac{{\bf x}}{|{\bf x}|^{2}}\right).

Therefore we get that

{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x})=\frac{h({\bf x})}{|h({\bf x})|^{2}}.

Since
|h({\bf x})|\sim|{\bf x}^{-1}|
, we observe that
{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x})\sim{\bf x}
,
more precisely:

\frac{1}{A_{\phi}^{3}}\cdot|{\bf x}|\,\leq\,|{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x})|\,\leq\,A_{\phi}^{3}\cdot|{\bf x}|.

It is sufficient to show that
{\iota(\phi)}
is Lipschitz.

Assume that
{\iota(\phi)}
is not
Lipschitz. Therefore there exist two sequences

({\bf x}_{n})_{n}
and
({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
of
{\bf U}_{q_{1}}
such that

(4.1)

\lim_{n}\frac{|{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n})-{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})|}{|{\bf x}_{n}-{\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}|}=\infty.

We work with a representative of
\phi
outside a compact subset
C_{1}
of

\mathbb{R}^{q_{1}}
containing
{\bf 0}
and with the representative of
{\iota(\phi)}
over

X_{1}
,
the closure
{\rm clos}(\iota_{q_{1}}(Y_{1}\setminus C_{1}))
. Thus
X_{1}
is
compact.
For convenience sake let

{\bf y}_{n}:=\iota_{q_{1}}({\bf x}_{n})\;\;{\rm and}\;\;{\bf y}_{n}^{\prime}:=\iota_{q_{1}}({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}).

We further define the following numbers:

e_{n}:=|{\bf x}_{n}-{\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}|,\;\;{\iota(\phi)}_{n}:=|{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n})-{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})|,\;\;t_{n}:=|{\bf x}_{n}|,\;\;t_{n}^{\prime}:=|{\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}|,\;\;s_{n}:=|{\bf y}_{n}|\;\;{\rm and}\;\;s_{n}^{\prime}:=|{\bf y}_{n}^{\prime}|.

Of course we have
s_{n}t_{n}=s_{n}^{\prime}t_{n}^{\prime}=1
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the sequence

({\bf x}_{n})_{n}
converges to
\chi\in X_{1}
and
({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
converges to

\chi^{\prime}\in X_{1}
.

\bullet

Case 1.

\chi\neq{\bf 0}
and
\chi^{\prime}\neq{\bf 0}
.

In other words there exists a compact subset
K_{1}
of
{\bf U}_{q_{1}}
which
contains
{\bf x}_{n},{\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}
for all
n
. Since the inversion
\iota_{q_{1}}
is
bi-Lipschitz over
K_{1}
, so is the mapping
{\iota(\phi)}
, contradicting the
estimate (
4.1
).
Therefore this case cannot happen and we can assume that
\chi={\bf 0}
.

\bullet

Case 2.

\chi={\bf 0}
and
\chi^{\prime}\neq{\bf 0}
.

Observe that the following estimates hold true

e_{n}=t_{n}^{\prime}+o(t_{n}^{\prime}),\;\;|{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n})|\sim t_{n}\to 0,\;\;{\rm and}\;\;|{\iota(\phi)}({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})|\sim t_{n}^{\prime}\in[a,b]

for positive real numbers
b>a
. Therefore we deduce that

{\iota(\phi)}_{n}\sim t_{n}^{\prime}

contradicting the estimate (
4.1
). Therefore this case cannot
happen and thus
\chi^{\prime}={\bf 0}
as well.

\bullet

Case 3.

\chi=\chi^{\prime}={\bf 0}
and there exists

such that

Lemma 9

\displaystyle:=

The same argument involving the point
{\bf y}_{1}^{\prime}={\bf y}_{1}
implies that

\liminf_{n}|{\bf x}_{n}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{n}|=0
, so we can assume that
({\bf x}_{n}^{\prime})_{n}

converge to
{\bf y}_{1}
as well. Since
\sigma_{q_{1}}^{-1}

is bi-Lipschitz nearby
{\bf y}_{1}
and
\sigma_{q_{2}}
is bi-Lipschitz nearby

\Phi({\bf y}_{1})
, the mapping
\Phi
is Lipschitz nearby
{\bf y}_{1}
, yielding a
contradiction.
∎

8.
Geometry at infinity of tame sets

There are many possible applications of the inversion Lemma
9
.
In particular, any
bi-Lipschitz classification problem of subsets at infinity is equivalent to
a bi-Lipschitz classification problem at the origin.

There are quite a few questions of bi-Lipschitz definable geometry
at infinity which now reduce to a problem at the origin by our main result.
Many of them
would require some specific preparations, that is why we present here only two
such applications, which are immediate consequences of
Lemma
9
.

8.1.
Bi-Lipschitz definable sets at infinity and their tangent cones

A non-negative cone
C
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
is any subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
stable by
non-negative rescaling:

{\bf x}\in C\;\Longrightarrow\;t\cdot{\bf x}\in C,\;\forall t\geq 0.

For a given non-negative cone
C
of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
, the link of
C
is defined as

{\bf S}(C):=C\cap{\bf S}^{q-1}.

Let
S
be a non-empty subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
. The non-negative cone over
S
with vertex the
origin
{\bf 0}
is the subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
defined as

\widehat{S}^{+}:=\{t{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}^{q}}:{\bf u}\in S,\;t\geq 0\}.

In particular, a subset
C
is a non-negative cone of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
if and only if it is
the non-negative cone over its link:

C=\widehat{{\bf S}(C)}^{+}.

Definition 12
.

Let
S
be a subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{q}}
.

(i) The asymptotic
set of

at
{\bf 0}
is the closed subset of the unit sphere
{\bf S}^{q-1}

(ii) The asymptotic
set of
S
at
\infty
is the closed subset of the unit sphere
{\bf S}^{q-1}

S^{\infty}:=\left\{{\bf u}\in{\bf S}^{q-1}\;:\;\exists\;({\bf x}_{k})_{k}\in S\;\;{\rm such\>that}\;\;|{\bf x}_{k}|\to\infty\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\frac{{\bf x}_{k}}{|{\bf x}_{k}|}\to{\bf u}\right\}.

The subset
S^{\omega}
is not empty if and only if
\overline{S}
contains

{\rm clos}(S)^{\omega}=S^{\omega}

or

The non-negative cone

Given

\frac{{\bf x}}{|{\bf x}|}=\frac{\iota_{q}({\bf x})}{|\iota_{q}({\bf x})|}.

\widehat{X^{\infty}}^{+}=\widehat{\iota(X)^{\bf 0}}^{+}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\widehat{X^{\bf 0}}^{+}=\widehat{\iota(X)^{\infty}}^{+}.

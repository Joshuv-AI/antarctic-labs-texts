Coordinate descent methods beyond smoothness and separability
Flavia Chorobura, Ion Necoara

Abstract
This paper deals with convex nonsmooth optimization problems. We introduce a general smooth approximation framework for the original function and apply random (accelerated) coordinate descent methods for minimizing the corresponding smooth approximations. Our framework covers the most important classes of smoothing techniques from the literature. Based on this general framework for the smooth approximation and using coordinate descent type methods we derive convergence rates in function values for the original objective. Moreover, if the original function satisfies a growth condition, then we prove that the smooth approximations also inherits this condition and consequently the convergence rates are improved in this case. We also present a relative randomized coordinate descent algorithm for solving nonseparable minimization problems with the objective function relative smooth along coordinates w.r.t. a (possibly nonseparable) differentiable function. For this algorithm we also derive convergence rates in the convex case and under the growth condition for the objective.



keywords
Convex optimization, growth condition, nonsmooth and nonseparable objective, coordinate descent, convergence analysis.

Introduction

In this paper we study (accelerated) random (block) coordinate descent methods for solving nonsmooth convex optimization problems of the form:

\displaystyle F^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x),\vskip-11.38092pt

(1)

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is a general proper closed convex function (possibly nonseparable). First order methods for nonsmooth optimization, e.g. subgradient type methods, have convergence rates of order
\mathcal{O}(1/\epsilon^{2})
, where
\epsilon
is the desired accuracy for the approximate solution. In order to improve convergence, smooth approximations of the original function can be considered and gradient type methods
Nesterov 2004
can be used for solving these smooth approximations.
Some examples of smoothing techniques are Moreau envelope
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
, Forward-Backward envelope
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Stella et al. 2017
, Douglas-Rachford envelope
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Patrinos et al. 2014
, Nesterov’s smoothing
Nesterov 2005
and Gaussian smoothing
Nesterov and Spokoiny 2017
. General properties for these smoothing techniques can be found in
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Nesterov 2005
;
Nesterov and Spokoiny 2017
;
Patrinos et al. 2014
;
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
;
Stella et al. 2017
;
Necoara and Fercoq 2022
;
Necoara and Suykens 2008
. Moreover, in e.g.,
Nesterov 2005
;
Nesterov and Spokoiny 2017
;
Necoara and Fercoq 2022
;
Necoara and Suykens 2008
gradient type methods are considered for solving the smooth approximation. However, in large-scale problems the computation of the full gradient of the smooth approximation can be prohibitive. In this case one can use coordinate descent algorithms
Fercoq and Qu 2020
;
Fercoq and Richtarik 2015
;
Nesterov and Stich 2017
;
Nesterov 2012
for solving the smooth approximation, as it was done e.g., in
Aberdam and Beck 2021
;
Fercoq and Richtárik 2019
;
Nesterov and Stich 2017
. On other hand, if the original function is still nonsmooth, but relative smooth w.r.t. a differentiable function, coordinate descent type methods still converge (sub)linearly
Lu et al. 2018
. Some examples of convex relative smooth functions were given in
Lu et al. 2018
. Relative coordinate descent algorithms were also considered in
Gao et al. 2021
;
Hanzely and Richtarik 2021
;
Hien et al. 2022
. However, in all these papers the original function is relative smooth along coordinates with respect to a separable function.

Previous work:
In
Nesterov 2005
the following problem is considered

F(x)=f(x)+\max_{u\in Q}\left\{\langle Ax,u\rangle-{\phi}(u)\right\},\vskip-2.84544pt

(2)

with the gradient of
f
assumed Lipschitz and a smooth approximation was proposed for this problem, which we call in this paper Nesterov’s smoothing. Moreover,
Nesterov 2005
also presents an accelerated gradient descent method for solving the smooth approximation for which sublinear rate is obtained in the convex case. In the context of coordinate descent algorithms,
Nesterov and Stich 2017
presents an accelerated coordinate descent algorithm for solving the Nesterov’s smooth approximation. Paper
Fercoq and Richtárik 2019
considers the problem (
2
) with
f
being separable, i.e.,
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(x_{i})
, and considers the Nesterov’s smoothing for the second term of the objective. Finally, a coordinate descent algorithm is used for solving the smooth approximation
and rates of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)
are derived in the convex case and improved to
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)
, when the smooth approximation is strongly convex. Moreover, in
Aberdam and Beck 2021
an objective function of the form

\displaystyle F(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Ax+b^{T}x+\psi(x)\vskip-2.84544pt

(3)

is considered, with
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix and
\psi
is a proper closed convex function (possibly nonsmooth and nonseparable) which is proximal easy. Then, the Forward-Backward envelope is employed as a smooth approximation for the objective and a modified accelerated coordinate descent algorithm is used for solving this smooth approximation (in each iteration it is necessary to compute two proximal operators). Rate of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3/2}}\right)
is obtained in function values at some point
\Pi_{\text{dom}\psi}(x)
, where
\Pi_{\text{dom}\psi}(\cdot)
is the projection onto the domain of
\psi
, provided that
\psi
is Lipschitz in any bounded subset. Finally, when the function is relative smooth along coordinates in
Hanzely and Richtarik 2021
a relative randomized coordinate descent is proposed and sublinear rates are obtained in the convex case and linear rates for an objective that is relative strong with respect to a separable function.

Contributions.
In this paper we first introduce a general smoothing framework for the convex objective function
F
in problem (
1
) and we use random (accelerated) coordinate descent methods from the literature to solve the smooth approximation. We also present a relative randomized coordinate descent algorithm for minimizing convex objective functions that are relative smooth along coordinates w.r.t. a (possibly nonseparable) differentiable function. More precisely, our main contributions are:

(i) We introduce a novel smoothing framework for general nonsmooth and nonseparable convex objective functions (Assumption
1
), which covers in particular the most important smooth approximations from the literature (Moreau envelope, Forward-Backward envelope, Douglas-Rachford envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing).

In the Moreau envelope, we assume that
F
is a proper closed convex function and proximally easy. In the Forward-Backward and Douglas-Rachford envelopes our results are valid when
F
has the form (
3
). In the Douglas-Rachford envelope, matrix

must be computed easily, where
\gamma>0
is the parameter of the smoothing and

is the identity matrix. Finally, for Nesterov’s smoothing our framework covers the case when
f
has coordinate-wise Lipschitz gradient in the problem (
2
).

Then, we consider random (accelerated) coordinate descent algorithms for solving this smooth approximation. Under this general framework we derive sublinear convergence rates in function values for the original problem (
1
).

(ii) Moreover, the rates are improved (to even linear) if the original objective function satisfies a
q
-growth condition (Assumption
3
). We prove that Moreau envelope, Forward-Backward envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing inherits such a
q
-growth. Table
1
summarizes the main convergence results from this paper for each smoothing technique, where
\bar{\kappa}
is the constant in Assumption
3
.

(iii) We also consider nonsmooth objective functions (possibly nonseparable), but relative smooth along coordinates with respect to a given function (possibly also nonseparable). For this problem a relative randomized coordinate descent algorithm is introduced, for which we derive sublinear rate when the objective
F
is convex. Moreover, if the function
F
satisfies a
q
-growth condition, we prove that the algorithm converges linearly with high probability.

The major difference between our result corresponding to the Forward-Backward envelope and the result in

. Another major difference is that the modified accelerated coordinate descent algorithm considered in

1.1
Preliminaries

In this section we present some notations, definitions and some preliminary results.
Let
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a column permutation of the
n\times n
identity matrix and further let
U=[U_{1},...,U_{N}]
be a decomposition of
U
into
N
submatrices, with
U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n_{i}}
, where
\sum_{i=0}^{N}n_{i}=n
. Any vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be written uniquely as
x=\sum_{i=0}^{N}U_{i}x^{(i)}
, where
x^{(i)}=U_{i}^{T}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
. For spaces
R^{n_{i}}
, let us fix some norms
\|\cdot\|_{(i)}
,
i=1,\cdots,N
. Moreover,
\|x\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|x^{(i)}\|^{2}
is defined as the Euclidean norm and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{p}}
is the
\ell_{p}
norm,
for
p\neq 2
.
We consider
\delta_{B}
the indicator function of the set
B
and
e_{i}
is the
i
-th component vector of the canonical basis.
We denote with
X^{*}
the set of minimizers of problem (
1
) and with
\bar{x}=[x]_{X^{*}}
, the projection of
x
onto
X^{*}
(all the letters with bar above denote the projection of a point onto
X^{*}
).

For a given function
F
and
y\in\text{dom}F
its level set is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{F}(y)=\{x\in\text{dom}F:\;F(x)\leq F(y)\}.

Next, we present the definition of convexity along coordinates.

Definition 1
.

For any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
i=1,\cdots,N
denote
\phi^{x}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
as:

\phi^{x}_{i}(d)=\psi(x+U_{i}d).

(4)

We say that
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is (strictly) convex along coordinates if the partial functions
\phi^{x}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
are (strictly) convex for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
i=1,\cdots,N
.

Let us define the notion of block coordinate-wise Lipschitz gradient
Nesterov 2012
.

Definition 2
.

Given a closed convex set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. The gradient of
f
is block coordinate-wise Lipschitz continuous on
X
, with constants
L_{i}>0
, if for all
x,x+U_{i}h\in X
and
i=1,\cdots,N
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\|U_{i}^{T}(\nabla f(x+U_{i}h)-\nabla f(x))\|_{(i)}\leq L_{i}\|h\|_{(i)}.

(5)

If (
5
) holds, then we have the following relation
Nesterov 2012
:

\displaystyle|f(x+U_{i}h)-f(x)-\langle U_{i}^{T}\nabla f(x),h\rangle|\leq\frac{L_{i}}{2}\|h\|^{2}_{(i)}.

(6)

When
N=1
in the inequality above, we say the function
f
has Lipschitz gradient. Next result was proved for the case
N=1
, see for example

(
Nesterov 2004
, Thm 2.1.5)
. We adapt this result for the (block) coordinate case.

Lemma 1
.

Consider
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
a function and
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a closed convex set. Assume that
f
is convex along coordinates and the following inequality holds for all
x,x+U_{i}h\in X
and
i=1,\cdots,N

f(x+U_{i}h)-f(x)-\langle U_{i}^{T}\nabla f(x),h\rangle\leq\dfrac{L_{i}}{2}\|h\|_{(i)}^{2}.

(7)

Then, we have:

f(x+U_{i}h)-\langle U_{i}^{T}\nabla f(x+U_{i}h),h\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2L_{i}}\|U_{i}^{T}(\nabla f(x+U_{i}h)-\nabla f(x))\|^{2}_{(i)}\leq f(x),

and

\|U_{i}^{T}(\nabla f(x+U_{i}h)-\nabla f(x))\|_{(i)}\leq L_{i}\|h\|_{(i)}.

Proof.

See appendix for a proof.

2
Smooth approximations for convex problems

In this section we introduce a general smoothing framework for convex functions. Our framework covers the most important smoothing techniques from literature: Moreau, Forward-Backward, Douglas-Rachford envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing.

Assumption 1
.

Consider
F_{\gamma}(x)
, a smooth approximation for the function
F
(with parameter
\gamma>0
). Assume that:

A.1 Consider
F_{\gamma}^{*}=\min F_{\gamma}(x)
. We have that
F_{\gamma}^{*}\leq F^{*}
.

A.2 There exist operators
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
and some constant
D\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{B}(x))-\gamma D\leq F_{\gamma}(x)\leq F(\mathcal{C}(x)),\quad\forall x\in\text{dom}F.\vskip-5.69046pt

A.3 The gradient of
F_{\gamma}
is block coordinate-wise Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with constants
L_{i}(F_{\gamma})>0
, see Definition
2
.

A.4
F_{\gamma}(x)
is convex.

Next, we give some examples of smooth approximations that satisfy Assumption
1
. The reader may find others examples of smoothing techniques that fit our framework.

2.1
Moreau envelope

As a first example of a smoothing technique we have the Moreau Envelope
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
. The Moreau envelope of a function
F
with
\gamma>0
is defined as

F_{\gamma}(x):=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{F(u)+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}\right\},

(8)

while the minimizer of the problem above, called proximal mapping, is

\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x):=\arg\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{F(u)+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}\right\}.

(9)

Below, we show that the Moreau Envelope satisfies Assumption
1
:

A.1 It is well-known that
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
:

F^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)=F^{*}_{\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x).

(10)

A.2 Consider
\hat{u}=\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x)
. A direct implication from the definition of
F_{\gamma}
is that
F(\hat{u})\leq F_{\gamma}(x)\leq F(x)
, for all
\gamma>0
. Hence
\mathcal{B}(x)=\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x)
,
\mathcal{C}
is the identity operator and

D=0
.

A.3 If
F
is a proper closed convex function, then
F_{\gamma}
is differentiable and its gradient
Rockafellar and Wets 1998

\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma^{-1}\left(x-\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x)\right)

(11)

is
\gamma^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous. Moreover, note that the coordinate-wise Lipschitz constant of
\nabla F_{\gamma}
is also
L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=\gamma^{-1}
. In fact,

\displaystyle\|U_{i}^{T}(\nabla F_{\gamma}(x+U_{i}h)-\nabla F_{\gamma}(x))\|_{(i)}\leq\|\nabla F_{\gamma}(x+U_{i}h)-\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)\|\leq\gamma^{-1}\|h\|_{(i)}.

A.4 If
F
is convex, then
F_{\gamma}
is also convex (see for example
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
).

2.2
Forward-Backward envelope

Consider the problem (
1
) defined as the following composite problem

F^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x):=f(x)+\psi(x),

(12)

where
\psi
is a proper closed convex function. Moreover,
\psi
is proximal easy and
f
has Lipschitz gradient with constant
L>0
. The Forward-Backward envelope of the pair
(f,\psi)
with a smooth parameter
\gamma>0
is the function
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Stella et al. 2017
:

F_{\gamma}(x)=f(x)-\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+\psi^{\gamma}(x-\gamma\nabla f(x)),

(13)

where
\psi^{\gamma}
is the Moreau envelope of the function
\psi
defined in (
8
). Note that the Forward-Backward envelope can also be defined as

F_{\gamma}(x)=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),u-x\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}+\psi(u).\vskip-5.69046pt

(14)

Now, we summarize the results obtained in
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Stella et al. 2017
that fits in our framework.
If
f
is twice differentiable and has Lipschitz gradient, and
\psi
is a proper closed convex function, then
F_{\gamma}
is continuously differentiable with

\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma^{-1}\left(I-\gamma\nabla^{2}f(x)\right)\left[x-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x-\gamma\nabla f(x))\right].

(15)

Moreover, Forward-Backward envelope satisfies Assumption
1
when
f
is quadratic
:

A.1 For all
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right]
, we have

\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)\subset\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad F^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)=F^{*}_{\gamma}.

(16)

Moreover, if
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
, we have

\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x).

(17)

A.2 Consider
\hat{u}=\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(x-\gamma\nabla f(x)\right)
, recall that
F(\hat{u})\leq F_{\gamma}(x)
for
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right]
. Moreover, from the definition of
F_{\gamma}
, we have
F_{\gamma}(x)\leq F(x)
, for all
\gamma>0
. Hence,
\mathcal{B}(x)=\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(x-\gamma\nabla f(x)\right)
,
\mathcal{C}
is the identity operator and
D=0
.

A.3 Consider

F(x)=f(x)+\psi(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Ax+b^{T}x+\psi(x),

(18)

for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\psi
is a proper closed convex function. Then, (
15
) becomes

\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma^{-1}\left(I-\gamma A\right)\left[x-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x-\gamma\nabla f(x))\right].

(19)

If
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
, then the gradient (
19
) is Lipschitz continuous with constant
\gamma^{-1}(1-\gamma\lambda_{\min}(A))
, where
\lambda_{\min}(A)
is the smallest eigenvalue of A. Moreover, for
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
, using Proposition 4.3 in
Giselsson and Fält 2018
and Lemma
1
, we get that the

th coordinate-wise Lipschitz constant of
\nabla F_{\gamma}
in (
19
) is

L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=\dfrac{\|U_{i}^{T}\left(I_{n}-\gamma A\right)U_{i}\|}{\gamma}=\dfrac{\|I_{n_{i}}-\gamma A_{ii}\|}{\gamma}.

Note that the gradient,
\nabla F_{\gamma}
, from (

) is in fact Lipschitz and block coordinate-wise Lipschitz for all
\gamma>0
. However, the Lipschitz and coordinate-wise Lipschitz constants take larger values when
\gamma L\geq 1
than when
\gamma L<1
. In fact, a more general result holds, i.e., the gradient,
\nabla F_{\gamma}
, given in (

) is Lipschitz (coordinate-wise Lipschitz) on a set

when

such that
\|x-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x-\gamma\nabla f(x))\|\leq R\quad\forall x\in X
. However, in our analysis we consider
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
and

quadratic, since under these conditions convexity is also preserved, see the next statement.

A.4 When
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
and

F
is defined as in (
18
), we have
F_{\gamma}
also convex,
see
(
Giselsson and Fält 2018
, Proposition 4.4)
.

2.3
Douglas-Rachford envelope

Let us present the results obtained in
Giselsson and Fält 2018
;
Patrinos et al. 2014
that are relevant in this paper. Consider the problem (
12
) where
\psi
is a proper closed convex function and
f
has Lipschtz gradient with constant

. The Douglas-Rachford envelope for the problem (
12
) is defined as:

F_{\gamma}(x)=f^{\gamma}(x)-\gamma\|\nabla f^{\gamma}(x)\|^{2}+\psi^{\gamma}(x-2\gamma\nabla f^{\gamma}(x)),

(20)

where
f^{\gamma},\psi^{\gamma}
are the Moreau Envelopes of the functions

and
\psi
, defined in (

). The Douglas-Rachford envelope can also be expressed as

F_{\gamma}(x)=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(P_{\gamma}(x)\right)+\langle\nabla f\left(P_{\gamma}(x)\right),u-P_{\gamma}(x)\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-P_{\gamma}(x)\|^{2}+\psi(u),\vskip-5.69046pt

with
P_{\gamma}(x)\!=\!\text{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)
. Moreover, Douglas-Rachford envelope satisfies Assumption
1
when f is quadratic:

A.1 If
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
we have:

F^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)=F_{\gamma}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(x)=\text{prox}_{\gamma f}\left(\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x)\right).\vskip-5.69046pt

A.2 Consider
\hat{u}=\text{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)
and
\hat{v}=\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(2\hat{u}-x\right)
, if
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
we have
F(\hat{v})\leq F_{\gamma}(x)
.
Moreover,
F_{\gamma}(x)\leq F(\hat{u})
, for all
\gamma>0
.
Hence
\mathcal{B}(x)=\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(2\text{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)-x\right)
,
\mathcal{C}(x)=\text{prox}_{\gamma f}(x)
and
D=0
.

A.3 Consider

F
defined as in (
18
). If
\gamma L\in\left(0,1\right)
we have
\nabla F_{\gamma}
is Lipschitz continuous:

\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(\dfrac{2}{\gamma}\left(\gamma^{-1}I_{n}+A\right)^{-1}-I_{n}\right)G(x),\vskip-5.69046pt

with

\displaystyle G(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma^{-1}I_{n}+A\right)^{-1}(\gamma^{-1}x-b)-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(2\left(\gamma^{-1}I_{n}+A\right)^{-1}(\gamma^{-1}x-b)-x\right).

Matrix
\left(I_{n}+\gamma A\right)^{-1}
can be computed easily e.g., when

is block diagonal. For (
18
), using Proposition 4.5 in
Giselsson and Fält 2018
and Lemma

A.4 When

2.4
Nesterov’s smoothing

Consider the function

F(x)=f(x)+\max_{u\in Q}\left\{\langle Ax,u\rangle-{\phi}(u)\right\},\vskip-5.69046pt

(21)

where
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed convex bounded subset,
{\phi}
a continuous convex function on Q, and
f
a convex function having a coordinate-wise Lipschitz gradient, with constants
L_{i}(f)>0
, for
i=1,\cdots,N
. Examples of function that satisfies (

) can be found in
Nesterov 2005
. Let us recall the main properties of Nesterov’s smoothing
Nesterov 2005
;
Nesterov and Stich 2017
:

F_{\gamma}(x)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f(x)}+\max_{u\in Q}\left\{\langle Ax,u\rangle-{\phi}(u)-\gamma d(u)\right\},\vskip-5.69046pt

(22)

where
d
is strongly convex function on
Q
w.r.t. some norm
\|\cdot\|_{W}

with parameter
\sigma>0
and
d(u)\geq 0
for all
u\in Q
. Then, Nesterov’s smoothing satisfies Assumption
1
:

A.1 Defining
\bar{D}=\max_{u}d(u)
, we have that:

F_{\gamma}(x)\leq F(x)\leq F_{\gamma}(x)+\gamma\bar{D},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\vskip-5.69046pt

(23)

Hence
F_{\gamma}^{*}\leq F^{*}
.

A.2 From (
23
), we have
D=\bar{D}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are the identity operators.

A.3 The gradient of
F_{\gamma}
is

\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\nabla f(x)+A^{T}u_{\gamma}(x),\vskip-5.69046pt

(24)

where
u_{\gamma}(x)
is the unique solution of the optimization problem in (
22
). Moreover, the gradient of
F_{\gamma}
is coordinate-wise Lipschitz with constant

\displaystyle L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=L_{i}(f)+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(\|Ae_{i}\|^{*}_{U}\right)^{2},\;\text{where}\;\|Ae_{i}\|_{U}^{*}=\max_{u}\{\langle Ae_{i},u\rangle:\|u\|_{U}\leq 1\}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(25)

A.4
Note that
F_{\gamma}
is a maximum of linear functions in

, hence it is convex.

Based on the previous examples, we can see that our framework is general and covers a wide range of smoothing techniques.

3
Functional
q
-growth

Strong convexity type conditions, such as
q
-growth property, allow to derive linear rates for first order methods, see
Necoara et al. 2019
. In this section we prove that smooth approximations of objective functions satisfying some
q
-growth condition inherits such a property.
Recall that
X^{*}
is the set of minimizers of problem (
1
) and
\bar{x}=[x]_{X^{*}}
is the projection of
x
onto
X^{*}
.

In this section we consider the following additional assumption on
F
.

Assumption 2
.

Consider
X\subseteq\text{dom}F
. We assume that the function
F
satisfies a functional
q
-growth on
X
, i.e., there exists a constant
\kappa>0
and
q\in[1,2]
such that:

F(x)-F^{*}\geq\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{q}\quad\forall x\in X.\vskip-5.69046pt

Below, we prove that Moreau envelope, Forward-Backward envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing satisfy also a
q
-growth like condition.
Let us introduce some notations that will be used in the sequel. We define
\displaystyle L_{\max}=\max_{i=1:N}L_{i}(F_{\gamma})
. We consider the following norms, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
:

\|x\|^{2}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(L_{i}(F_{\gamma}))^{\alpha}\|x^{(i)}\|^{2}_{(i)}.

3.1
Moreau and Forward-Backward envelopes

Theorem 1
.

Let Assumption
2
hold for a given function
F
and
X=\mathcal{L}_{F}(x_{0})
. For
q\in[1,2)
assume additionally that the level set of the smooth approximation
F_{\gamma}
satisfies:

\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq R\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{L}_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0})\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{and some}\quad R>0.}\vskip-5.69046pt

If
F
is a proper closed convex function and
F_{\gamma}
is the Moreau envelope, then it satisfies the following inequality:

F_{\gamma}(x)-F^{*}_{\gamma}\geq\dfrac{\hat{\kappa}}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}_{1}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{L}_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0}),\vskip-5.69046pt

(26)

with

\hat{\kappa}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\kappa\gamma}{(\gamma\kappa+1)}\;\text{ if }q=2\\
\dfrac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{2}}{(\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q})^{2}}\;\text{ if }q\in[1,2).\end{array}\right.

(27)

If
F=f+\psi
, such that
f
has Lipschitz gradient with constant
L>0
,
\psi
is a proper closed convex function and
F_{\gamma}
is the Forward-Backward envelope with
\gamma L\in(0,1)
, then it satisfies (
26
) with

\hat{\kappa}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\kappa(1-\gamma L)}{(\gamma\kappa+1-\gamma L)L_{\max}(F_{\gamma})}\;\text{ if }q=2\\
\dfrac{\kappa^{2}\gamma(1-\gamma L)}{(\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q}(1-\gamma L))^{2}L_{\max}(F_{\gamma})}\;\text{ if }q\in[1,2).\end{array}\right.

(28)

Proof.

Let us first prove the case of Moreau envelope. Consider
x\in\mathcal{L}_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0})
and
z=\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x)
. Note that
z\in\mathcal{L}_{F}(x_{0})
. Since
F
is a proper closed convex function and satisfies the
q
-growth condition, then we have:

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(x)-F^{*}_{\gamma}=F(z)+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|z-x\|^{2}-F^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|z-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}.\vskip-5.69046pt

For
q=2
, from the optimality condition (considering
u
the optimal point of the previous problem), we get:

\displaystyle\kappa(u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}})+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}(u-x)=0\iff u=\dfrac{1}{\kappa\gamma+1}x+\dfrac{\gamma\kappa}{\gamma\kappa+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}},\vskip-5.69046pt

and we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\kappa}{2}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}=\dfrac{\kappa}{2(\gamma\kappa+1)}\|x-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2}\geq\dfrac{\kappa}{2(\gamma\kappa+1)L_{\max}(F_{\gamma})}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}_{1}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Since
L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=\gamma^{-1}
, for
i=1,\cdots,N
, in the Moreau envelope, then
F_{\gamma}
is 2-growth w.r.t. the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, with constant
\hat{\kappa}=\dfrac{\kappa\gamma}{(\gamma\kappa+1)}
.

For
q\in[1,2)
, considering
u={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}
, we get

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}-x\|^{2}\geq\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}_{1}.\vskip-5.69046pt

On other hand, considering
u\neq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}
, from the optimality conditions there exists
u
:

\kappa\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q-2}\left(u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\right)+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(u-x\right)=0.\vskip-5.69046pt

Define
\alpha=\kappa\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q-2}
and
\beta=\dfrac{1}{\gamma}
, then from equality above we get

\displaystyle\alpha(u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}})+\beta(u-x)=0\Leftrightarrow(\alpha+\beta)u=\alpha{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}+\beta x\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;u=\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}+\dfrac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}x.\vskip-5.69046pt

This implies that

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\left\|\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}+\dfrac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}x-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\right\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\left\|\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}+\dfrac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}x-x\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\kappa\beta^{q}}{q(\alpha+\beta)^{q}}\left\|x-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\right\|^{q}+\dfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2\gamma(\alpha+\beta)^{2}}\left\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}-x\right\|^{2}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(29)

Since
u
is the minimizer of the problem above, we get

\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}\leq\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}\leq\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|x-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}\Rightarrow\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|\leq\|x-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|.\vskip-5.69046pt

From the boundedness assumption of the level set we have that there exists
R>0
such that
\|y-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|\leq R
, for all
y\in L_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0})
. Using the fact that
x\in L_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0})
and the inequality above, we obtain
\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|\leq R
. Moreover,

\dfrac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}=\dfrac{\kappa}{\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2-q}}\cdot\dfrac{\gamma\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2-q}}{\gamma\kappa+\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2-q}}=\dfrac{\kappa\gamma}{\gamma\kappa+\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{2-q}}\geq\dfrac{\kappa\gamma}{\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q}}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{2}}{2\gamma(\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q})^{2}}\|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}-x\|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\kappa^{2}\gamma}{2(\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q})^{2}L_{\max}(F_{\gamma})}\|\bar{x}-x\|^{2}_{1}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Since
L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=\gamma^{-1}
in the Moreau Envelope, then
F_{\gamma}
satisfies 2-growth condition
w.r.t. norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
with the constant
\hat{\kappa}=\dfrac{\kappa^{2}\gamma^{2}}{(\kappa\gamma+R^{2-q})^{2}}
.

Now, let us prove for Forward-Backward envelope. Consider
x\in\mathcal{L}_{F_{\gamma}}(x_{0})
and
z=\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(x-\gamma\nabla f(x)\right)
. Note that, if
\gamma L\in(0,1)
, then

satisfies functional
q
-growth,

is a proper closed function and

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(x)-

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(\bar{x})=f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),z-x\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|z-x\|^{2}+\psi(z)-F^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}-\dfrac{L}{2}\right)\|z-x\|^{2}+F(z)-F^{*}\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{\gamma}-L\right)\|z-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|z-\bar{z}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{\gamma}-L\right)\|u-x\|^{2}+\dfrac{\kappa}{q}\|u-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bar{z}}\|^{q}.\vskip-5.69046pt

where in the first equality we use (

In this section we analyze the convergence of (accelerated) coordinate descent methods for minimizing the smooth approximation and consequently the original objective function in the convex and

4.1
Coordinate descent: convex case

In the framework of smoothing as given in Assumption
1
, the smooth approximation
F_{\gamma}
has coordinate-wise gradient. Therefore, we can use (accelerated) coordinate descent algorithms
Fercoq and Qu 2020
;
Nesterov 2012
;
Nesterov and Stich 2017
for solving the smooth approximation of problem (
1
):

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}^{*}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}F_{\gamma}(x).\vskip-5.69046pt

(41)

In the next algorithm, we
consider a
random counter
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, which generates an integer number
i\in\{1,...,N\}
with probability
Nesterov 2012
:

p^{(i)}_{\alpha}=L_{i}^{\alpha}(F_{\gamma})\cdot\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{j}^{\alpha}(F_{\gamma})\right]^{-1}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(42)

Thus,

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
is a discrete
random variable over
\{1,...,N\}
and its distribution is specified by probabilities
as in (
42
). Note that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
generates a uniform distribution
. Let us recall the algorithm proposed in
Nesterov 2012
.

Algorithm 1
:

Given a starting point
x_{0}\in\text{dom}F_{\gamma}
.
For
k\geq 0
do:

1.

Sample
i_{k}
from
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
.

2.

Compute
h^{(i_{k})}(x_{k})=-U_{i_{k}}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k})
.

3.

Update:

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\dfrac{1}{L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma})}U_{i_{k}}h^{(i_{k})}(x_{k})
.

From inequality (2.4) in
Nesterov 2012
, we have for all
k\geq 0
the descent:

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{\gamma}(x_{k}).\vskip-5.69046pt

(43)

Denote the set of optimal solutions of (
41
) by
\bar{X}^{*}_{\gamma}
and let
x_{*}
be an element of this set. Define also:

R_{\alpha}=\max_{k\geq 0}\min_{x_{*}\in\bar{X}^{*}_{\gamma}}\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{\alpha}<\infty\text{ and }S_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{\alpha}(F_{\gamma}).\vskip-5.69046pt

Theorem 3
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then, the iterates of Algorithm 1 satisfy

\mathbb{E}[F_{\gamma}(x_{k})]-F_{\gamma}^{*}\leq\dfrac{2S_{\alpha}(\gamma)R_{1-\alpha}^{2}}{k+4}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Proof.

The result follows using Theorem 1 in
Nesterov 2012
and Assumption
1
[A3-A4].
∎

Next, we can also provide the convergence rate for the original function in problem (
1
).

Theorem 4
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Then, the iterates of Algorithm 1 satisfy

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k}))]-F^{*}\leq\dfrac{2S_{\alpha}(\gamma)R_{1-\alpha}^{2}}{k+4}+\gamma D.\vskip-5.69046pt

(44)

Proof.

By Assumption
1
[A.1-A.2] we have
F^{*}\geq F_{\gamma}^{*}
and
\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k})]\leq\mathbb{E}[F_{\gamma}(x_{k})]+\gamma D
. Using Theorem
3
, the result follows.
∎

If we want to get
\epsilon
accuracy, since in the Nesterov’s smoothing
D>0
, we need to take
\gamma=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{\epsilon}{2D}\right)
. Additionally, we have (see (
25
))

\displaystyle L_{i}(F_{\gamma})=\mathcal{O}\left(\gamma^{-1}\right),\quad\text{then}\quad S_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\mathcal{O}\left(\gamma^{-\alpha}\right),\vskip-5.69046pt

and

\displaystyle R_{1-\alpha}^{2}=\|x_{\bar{k}}-x_{*}\|_{1-\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{1-\alpha}(F_{\gamma})\|x_{\bar{k}}^{(i)}-x_{*}^{(i)}\|_{(i)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{O}\left(\gamma^{\alpha-1}\right)\|x_{\bar{k}}^{(i)}-x_{*}^{(i)}\|_{(i)}^{2},\vskip-5.69046pt

for some
\bar{k}>0
and for some
x_{*}\in\bar{X}^{*}_{\gamma}
. Therefore,

S_{\alpha}(\gamma)R_{1-\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{O}\left(\gamma^{-1}\right)\|x_{\bar{k}}^{(i)}-x_{*}^{(i)}\|_{(i)}^{2}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, inequality (
44
) becomes (recall that in this case
\mathcal{B}
is the identity operator):

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{k})]-F^{*}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{\gamma(k+4)}\right)+\gamma D.\vskip-5.69046pt

(45)

Since
\gamma=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{\epsilon}{2D}\right)
, it implies that the complexity is of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-2}\right)
. On other hand for the Moreau, Forward-Backward and Douglas-Rachford envelopes we have
F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k}))\leq F_{\gamma}(x_{k})
, i.e.,
D=0
. Hence in these cases, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k}))]-F^{*}\leq\dfrac{2S_{\alpha}(\gamma)R_{1-\alpha}^{2}}{k+4}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Then, in the Moreau envelope, we have
S_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\dfrac{N}{\gamma^{\alpha}}
for some
\gamma>0
(see Section
2.1
); in the Forward-Backward,
S_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{\|I_{n_{i}\times n_{i}}-\gamma A_{ii}\|^{\alpha}}{\gamma^{\alpha}}
for some
\gamma\in\left(0,1/L\right)
(see Section
2.2
); and in the Douglas-Rachford we have
S_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\dfrac{\|U_{i}^{T}\left(P+P^{2}\right)U_{i}\|^{\alpha}}{(2\gamma)^{\alpha}}
for some
\gamma\in\left(0,1/L\right)
and
P=2\left(I_{n}+\gamma A\right)^{-1}-I_{n}
(see Section
2.3
).
Since the constant
\gamma
does not depend on
\epsilon
, the complexity in these cases is of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
.

Below we discuss the computation cost of
U_{i_{k}}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k})
at each iteration for the previous four examples.

Example 1:
Consider the Moreau envelope. In this case we have

U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma^{-1}\left(x^{(i)}-U_{i}^{T}\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x)\right).\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, this smoothing is efficient in the context of coordinate descent when
a block of components
of the
prox
of
F
can be evaluated easily based on the previously computed
prox
, given that only a block of coordinates is modified in the prior iteration. For example:

1.

F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}F_{i}(U_{i}^{T}x)
, with
prox
of
F_{i}
computed in
\mathcal{O}(n_{i})
operations.

2.

F(x)=g(Ax+b)
, with
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, such that

AA^{T}=1/\alpha I
. Then:

\text{prox}_{F}(x)=x-\alpha A^{T}\left(Ax+b-\text{prox}_{\alpha^{-1}g}(Ax+b)\right).\vskip-2.84544pt

As explained in
Nesterov and Stich 2017

the computational cost of updating
Ax
in each iteration is given by
\mathcal{O}(mn_{i})
operations
. If prox of
g
is computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations, then
the complexity of updating
U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)
is given by
\mathcal{O}(mn_{i}+n)
operations. One example of such
g
whose prox can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations is
g(x)=\|x\|_{\ell_{1}}
.

Example 2
: Consider the Forward-Backward envelope, for problem (
18
), we have

U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma^{-1}U_{i}^{T}\left(I-\gamma A\right)\left[x-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x-\gamma(Ax+b)))\right].\vskip-5.69046pt

The computational cost of
Ax
in each iteration is of order
\mathcal{O}(nn_{i})
=
\mathcal{O}(n)

. In this case, it is interesting to apply Algorithm 1 for solving the Forward-Backward envelope when the prox of
\psi
can also be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations, such as:

1.

\psi(x)=\lambda\|x\|
. Then, the prox of
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle\text{prox}_{\lambda\|x\|}(x)=\left(1-\dfrac{\lambda}{\|x\|}\right)_{+}x.\vskip-5.69046pt

2.

\psi(x)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\|x_{s}\|
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a partion of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
. This function serves as regularizer to induce group sparsity. For
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the components of the proximal mapping indexed by
s
are

\displaystyle(\text{prox}_{\lambda\psi}(x))_{s}=\left(1-\dfrac{\lambda}{\|x_{s}\|}\right)_{+}x_{s}.\vskip-5.69046pt

3.

\psi=\delta_{B_{2}[0,r]}=\delta_{\{x:\|x\|\leq r\}}
for some
r>0
. The projection onto
B_{2}
is given by:

\text{prox}_{\delta_{B_{2}[0,r]}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x\;\text{ if }\;\|x\|\leq r\\
\dfrac{rx}{\|x\|}\;\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\vskip-5.69046pt

4.

\psi=\delta_{B_{1}[c,r]}=\delta_{\{x:\|x-c\|_{\ell_{1}}\leq r\}}
for some
c>0
. Then, the prox can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations, see
Duchi et al. 2008
.

5.

\psi=\delta_{C}
, where
C=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:a^{T}x=b,l\leq x\leq u\}
. Then, the prox can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations.

6.

\psi=\delta_{C}
where
C=\{x:Ax=b\}
with a full row rank
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Assuming that
m\ll n
, then for any
\gamma>0
,
\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x)=P_{C}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\left(x-A^{T}(AA^{T})^{-1}(Ax-b)\right)
.
After a preprocessing step in which
AA^{T}
is computed, we can compute
P_{C}(x)
in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations.

7.

\psi(x)=\lambda TV(x)
, where
\lambda>0
, the total variation regularization. In this case one can also compute the prox in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations, see
Barbero and Sra 2014
;
Johnson 2013
;
Kolmogorov et al. 2016
.

8.

\psi(x)=\|x\|^{r+2}
, where
r\geq 0
. Then, computing the prox is equivalent to solving a polynomial of degree
r+1
, i.e.,
\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}(x)=-\theta c
, where
\theta
is a root of the polynomial
1-\theta-\|c\|^{r}\theta^{r+1}=0
(when
c\neq 0
) . See also
Lu et al. 2018
for other examples.

Example 3
: Consider the Douglas-Rachford envelope, for problem (
18
), we have

\displaystyle U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x)=U_{i}^{T}\left(2H-I_{n}\right)\gamma H\left((x-\gamma b)-\text{prox}_{\gamma\psi}\left(2H(x-\gamma b)-x\right)\right),\vskip-8.5359pt

(46)

with
H=\left(I_{n}+\gamma A\right)^{-1}
. In this case, we assume that
H
can be computed easily, e.g., the matrix
A
is diagonal. Note also that in this case the computation of the vector
Hx
can be done in
\mathcal{O}(nn_{i})=\mathcal{O}(n)
operations and consequently Algorithm 1 can be efficiently implemented for minimizing the Douglas-Rachford envelope when the prox of
\psi
can be also computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
operations, see examples for the Forward-Backward envelope.

Example 4
: Consider the Nesterov’s smoothing. As explained in
Nesterov and Stich 2017
, if the set
Q
and function
\phi
in (
22
) are simple and the product
Ax
is known (in the coordinate descent methods this vector can be updated at each iteration in
\mathcal{O}(mn_{i})
operations), then the vector
u_{\gamma}(x)
is computed in
\mathcal{O}(m)
operations. Moreover, one component of the second term of the gradient in (
24
),
U_{i}^{T}A^{T}u_{\gamma}(x)
, is computed in
\mathcal{O}(mn_{i})
operations
.
The following examples satisfy this structure:

1.

Consider
F(x)=\|\tilde{A}x-\tilde{b}\|_{\infty}=\max_{u\in Q}\{\langle Ax,u\rangle-\langle b,u\rangle\}
, where

\tilde{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\,\ \tilde{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\,\ A=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{A}\\
-\tilde{A}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2m\times n},\,\ b=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{b}\\
-\tilde{b}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}\vskip-5.69046pt

and
Q:=\{u_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}:\sum_{j}u_{j}=1,u_{j}\geq 0\}
is the unit simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
.
The smooth approximation of
F
is given by

F_{\gamma}(x)=\gamma\log\left(\dfrac{1}{2m}\sum_{j=1}^{2m}\exp\left(\dfrac{e_{j}^{T}Ax-b_{j}}{\gamma}\right)\right).\vskip-5.69046pt

2.

Consider
F(x)=\|Ax-b\|_{\ell_{1}}=\max_{u\in Q}\{\langle Ax,u\rangle-\langle b,u\rangle\}
, where
Q=[-1,1]^{n}
. The smooth approximation of
F
is given by

F_{\gamma}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|e_{j}^{T}A\|\cdot\phi_{\gamma}\left(\dfrac{|e_{j}^{T}Ax-b_{j}|}{\|e_{j}^{T}A\|}\right),\quad\phi_{\gamma}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{t^{2}}{2\gamma},\;0\leq t\leq\gamma\\
t-\dfrac{\gamma}{2},\;\gamma\leq t.\end{array}\right.\vskip-5.69046pt

3.

Consider the total variation problem
F(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\|Bx-c\|^{2}+\lambda\|Dx\|_{\ell_{1}}
, with
B\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
,
D\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. We can use the smoothing function described above for the nonsmooth term,
\|Dx\|_{\ell_{1}}
.

4.

Consider the function
F(x)=\|x\|=\max_{\|u\|\leq 1}\langle x,u\rangle
. Choosing
d(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}
, the smooth approximation is given by:

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|x\|^{2},\quad\|x\|\leq\gamma\\
\|x\|-\dfrac{\gamma}{2},\quad\gamma\leq\|x\|.\end{array}\right.\vskip-5.69046pt

Next, let us discuss the computational cost of
\mathcal{B}(x_{k})
. Note that, in the Forward-Backward and Douglas-Rachford envelopes,

\mathcal{B}(x_{k})
is computed at each iteration since we need this vector for updating

U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k})
. Moreover, in the Nesterov’s smoothing
\mathcal{B}
is the identity operator, which obviously has no additional computational cost. On other hand, in the Moreau envelope we have an additional cost since
\mathcal{B}
is the full prox, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(x_{k})=\text{prox}_{\gamma F}(x_{k})
and in
U_{i}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k})
we only need to compute one block of components of the full prox
. However, it is not necessary to compute
\mathcal{B}(x_{k})
at each iteration, we only need this computation when the algorithm stops.

4.2
Accelerated coordinate descent: convex case

In this section we consider the algorithm proposed in
Nesterov and Stich 2017
. In the next algorithm,
\sigma_{1-\alpha}\geq 0
is the
strong convexity parameter
of
F_{\gamma}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1-\alpha}
.

Algorithm 2

Define
\nu_{0}=x_{0}\in\text{dom}F_{\gamma}
,
z_{0}=x_{0}
,
A_{0}=0
,
B_{0}=1
and
\beta=\dfrac{\alpha}{2}
.

For
k\geq 0
do:

1.

Sample
i_{k}
from
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
.

2.

Find parameter
a_{k+1}>0
from equation
a^{2}_{k+1}S_{\beta}^{2}=A_{k+1}B_{k+1}
,

where
A_{k+1}=A_{k}+a_{k+1}
and
B_{k+1}=B_{k}+\sigma_{1-\alpha}a_{k+1}
.

3.

Define
\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{A_{k+1}}
,
\beta_{k}=\dfrac{\sigma_{1-\alpha}a_{k+1}}{B_{k+1}}
and
y_{k}=\dfrac{(1-\alpha_{k})x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\nu_{k}}{1-\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}}
.

4.

Compute
h^{(i_{k})}(y_{k})=-U_{i_{k}}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(y_{k})
.

5.

Update
x_{k+1}=y_{k}+\dfrac{1}{L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma})}U_{i_{k}}h^{(i_{k})}(y_{k})
and

\nu_{k+1}=(1-\beta_{k})\nu_{k}+\beta_{k}y_{k}+\dfrac{a_{k+1}}{(L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma}))^{1-\alpha}B_{k+1}\pi_{\beta}[i_{k}]}U_{i_{k}}h^{(i_{k})}(y_{k}).

Theorem 5
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and
x_{*}
be a minimizer of problem (
41
). Then, the iterates of Algorithm 2 satisfy

\mathbb{E}[F_{\gamma}(x_{k})]-F_{\gamma}^{*}\leq\dfrac{2S_{\beta}^{2}(\gamma)\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{1-\alpha}}{k^{2}}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Proof.

The result follows from
(
Nesterov and Stich 2017
, Theorem 2)
and Assumption
1
[A.3-A.4].
∎

Next theorem presents the convergence rate for the original function.

Theorem 6
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and
x_{*}
be a minimizer of problem (
41
). Then, the iterates of Algorithm 2 satisfy

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k}))]-F^{*}\leq\dfrac{2S_{\beta}^{2}(\gamma)\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{1-\alpha}}{k^{2}}+\gamma D.\vskip-5.69046pt

Proof.

By Assumption
1
[A.1-A.2] we have
F^{*}\geq F^{*}_{\gamma}
and
\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{B}(x_{k})]\leq\mathbb{E}[F_{\gamma}(x_{k})]+\gamma D
. Using the Theorem
5
, it follows the result.
∎

Note that we need to take
\gamma=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{\epsilon}{2D}\right)
, when
D>0
. Making a similar analysis as in the non-accelerated case, we have that the complexity for Nesterov’s smoothing is of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
.
On other hand for the Moreau, Forward-Backward and Douglas-Rachford envelopes we get that the complexity is of the order
\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
,
since
D=0
and
\gamma
does not depend on
\epsilon
.

4.3
Coordinate descent:
q
-growth case

Under the additional
q
-growth condition from Assumption
3
we derive below improved convergence rates for the previous algorithms. Recall that
X^{*}
is the set of minimizers of the original problem (
1
),
\bar{x}=[x]_{X^{*}}
is the projection of

onto
X^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}
is the identity operator for Moreau envelope, Forward-Backward envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing. For simplicity, let us define

\displaystyle\eta=\min\left\{\bar{\kappa},\bar{\kappa}^{\frac{2}{\bar{q}}}\right\},\,\ R=\max_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{1}{2}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}_{1},\,\ \Delta_{0}=F_{\gamma}(x_{0})-F^{*}+R,

(47)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\max\left\{1-\dfrac{\bar{\kappa}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{q-2}{2}}}{N(1+\bar{\kappa})},1-\dfrac{\eta}{N(1+\bar{\kappa})}\right\}.

Theorem 7
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
3

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}_{1}+L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma})\langle x_{k+1}^{(i)}-x_{k}^{(i)},x_{k}^{(i)}-x^{(i)}\rangle+\dfrac{L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma})}{2}\|x_{k+1}^{(i)}-x_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}_{(i)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\langle U_{i_{k}}^{T}\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k+1}^{(i)}-x^{(i)}_{k}\rangle-\dfrac{L_{i_{k}}(F_{\gamma})}{2}\|x_{k+1}^{(i)}-x_{k}^{(i)}\|^{2}_{(i)}

i_{k}

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{i_{k}}\left[\|x_{k+1}-x\|^{2}_{1}\right]\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\|x_{k}-x\|^{2}_{1}+\dfrac{1}{N}\langle\nabla F_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\rangle+F_{\gamma}(x_{k})-\mathbb{E}_{i_{k}}\left[F_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\right]

Moreover, taking expectation with respect to

(48)

in the previous relation for

\displaystyle\geq\left(F_{\gamma}(x_{k})-F^{*}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}_{1}\right)\left(F_{\gamma}(x_{0})-F^{*}+R\right)^{\frac{\bar{q}}{2}-1}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, from (

Hence, from (

5
Relative smoothness along coordinates

In the previous sections we have considered nonsmooth (possibly nonseparable) objective functions
F
. In this section we still assume nonsmooth functions (possibly nonseparable), but relative smooth along coordinates with respect to a given function (possibly also nonseparable). Consider
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
a strictly convex differentiable function.
Then, the corresponding Bregman distance is defined as
Bauschke et al. 2016
:

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(y,x):=\phi(y)-\phi(x)-\langle\nabla\phi(x),y-x\rangle.\vskip-5.69046pt

Note that
D_{\phi}(y,x)\geq 0
and
D_{\phi}(y,x)=0
if and only if
y=x
. However,
D_{\phi}(y,x)
is not necessarily symmetric. Recall that a function
F
is
L
-smooth (or
F
has Lipschitz gradient) when it satisfies Definition
2
with
N=1
and
L_{1}=L
. As a generalization of a smooth function, papers
Bauschke et al. 2016
and
Lu et al. 2018
introduced the definition of relative smooth functions. Function
F
is smooth relative to a function
\phi
if there exists
L>0
such that

\displaystyle F(y)\leq F(x)+\langle\nabla F(x),y-x\rangle+LD_{\phi}(y,x)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(63)

Note that in relative smooth coordinate descent methods
Gao et al. 2021
;
Hanzely and Richtarik 2021
;
Hien et al. 2022
, the function
\phi
is considered separable, i.e,
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}(x^{(i)})
and inequality (
63
) is defined accordingly, as a generalization of the inequality (
7
). In this section, we define the notion of Bregman distance along coordinates for
\phi
possibly nonseparable.

Definition 3
.

Consider
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function that is strictly convex along coordinates as given in Definition
1
. We define
D_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as the Bregman distance along coordinates associated to the kernel function
\phi
as follows

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x+U_{i}d,x):=\phi(x+U_{i}d)-\phi(x)-\langle U_{i}^{T}\nabla\phi(x),d\rangle\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,N.\vskip-5.69046pt

Since
\phi
is strictly convex along coordinates, then
D_{\phi}(x+U_{i}d,x)>0
for all
d\neq 0
. Next, we introduce the notion of relative smoothness along coordinates.

Definition 4
.

We say that the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is relative smooth along coordinates with respect to the function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, when it satisfies the following inequality, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
and
i=1,\cdots,N
,

\displaystyle F(x+U_{i}d)\leq F(x)+\langle U_{i}^{T}\nabla F(x),d\rangle+L_{i}D_{\phi}(x+U_{i}d,x).\vskip-5.69046pt

In this section, we again consider the following probabilities for
i=1,\cdots,N
:

p^{(i)}_{\alpha}=\dfrac{L_{i}^{\alpha}}{S_{\alpha}},\quad\text{with}\quad S_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{j}^{\alpha},

(64)

and the following norms, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
:

\|x\|^{2}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{\alpha}\|x^{(i)}\|^{2}_{(i)}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\|x\|^{*}_{\alpha}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}^{-\alpha}\|x^{(i)}\|^{2}_{(i)}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Next, we introduce a relative randomized coordinate descent algorithm.

Algorithm 4 (RRCD):

Given a starting point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
k\geq 0
do:

1.   Choose
i_{k}\in\{1,...,N\}

with probability
p_{\alpha}^{(i_{k})}
.

2.   Solve the following subproblem:

\;\;d_{k}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}F(x_{k})+\langle U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla F(x_{k}),d\rangle+L_{i_{k}}D_{\phi}\left(x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d,x_{k}\right).\vskip-5.69046pt

(65)

3.   Update
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d_{k}
.

Throughout this section the following assumptions are valid:

Assumption 5
.

Assume that:

A.1
F
is convex and relative smooth along coordinates with respect to the function
\phi
, with constants

for all
i=1,\cdots,N
.

\phi
is strictly convex along coordinates, see Definition
1

is block coordinate-wise Lipschitz continuous on the level set of
F
,
\mathcal{L}_{F}(x_{0})
, with constants
H_{i}>0
, for all

, where
x_{0}
is the starting point of the algorithm RRCD, see Defintion
2

1

H_{i}>0
. For the proof of next lemma let us define

H_{\phi,\max}=\max_{i=1,\cdots,N}H_{i}.\,\

(66)

Lemma 3
.

Let the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be generated by RRCD and Assumption
5
hold. Then,

a.s. for all
k\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\leq F(x_{k})-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\dfrac{1}{2S_{\alpha}H_{\phi,\max}}\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Proof.

From the optimality condition of the subproblem (

U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla F(x_{k})+L_{i_{k}}U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d_{k})-L_{i_{k}}U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k})=0.\vskip-5.69046pt

) and the inequality above, we get

\displaystyle F(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq F(x_{k})-\dfrac{L_{i_{k}}}{2H_{\phi,\max}}\|U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d_{k})-U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k})\|^{2}_{(i_{k})}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(70)

Hence,
F(x_{k})\leq F(x_{0})
for all
k\geq 0
and the first statement follows. On the other hand, from (
64
) we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{1}{L_{i_{k}}}\|U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{2}_{(i_{k})}\ |\ x_{k}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dfrac{p_{\alpha}^{(i)}}{L_{i}}\|U^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{2}_{(i)}=\dfrac{1}{S_{\alpha}}\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}.

Hence, from (
68
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{S_{\alpha}}\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}[L_{i_{k}}\|U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d_{k})-U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla\phi(x_{k})\|^{2}_{(i_{k})}\ |\ x_{k}].\vskip-5.69046pt

(71)

Taking expectation with respect to
x_{k}
in (
70
) and using (
71
), we get (
67
).

∎

. In particular, Lemma

implies the inequality (6.1) in

, with

\displaystyle C=\dfrac{1}{2S_{\alpha}H_{\phi,\max}}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(72)

Following the same argument as in
Chorobura and Necoara 2023
, we can get convergence rates when
F
is convex. Define:

R_{1-\alpha}=\max_{k\geq 0}\min_{x^{*}\in X^{*}}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|_{1-\alpha}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{0}=F(x_{0})-F^{*}.

(73)

Theorem 10
.

Let the sequence

be generated by RRCD. If Assumption
5
hold, then the following sublinear rate in function values holds:

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{k})-F^{*}]\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\dfrac{2S_{\alpha}H_{\phi,\max}R^{2}_{1-\alpha}}{k+2S_{\alpha}H_{\phi,\max}R^{2}_{1-\alpha}/\Delta_{0}}.}\vskip-5.69046pt

Proof.

5.2
Relative coordinate descent:
q
-growth case

In this section we consider

\displaystyle\bar{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:0<F(x)-F^{*}<r\}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{dist}(x,X^{*})\leq\gamma\},\vskip-5.69046pt

for some
r,\gamma>0
. We assume that the function
F
satisfies Assumption
2
with
X=\bar{X}
and
q\in[1,2]
. Since
F
is convex, we have that Assumption
2
is equivalent to (
31
), see
J. Bolte and Suter 2016
. In algorithm RRCD,
i_{k}
is chosen with the probability
p_{\alpha}^{(i_{k})}
and the inequality (
31
) is valid only in a neighborhood. Using similar arguments as in
Maulen et al. 2022
;
Chorobura and Necoara 2023
one can show that there exists an iteration
\bar{k}>0
such that inequality (
31
) holds for all
k\geq\bar{k}
, with some probability.
Next lemma presents some basic properties for
X(x_{0})
, the limit points of the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by RRCD be bounded. If Assumption
5
holds, then
X(x_{0})
is a compact set,
F(X(x_{0}))=F^{*}
,
F(x_{k})\to F^{*}
a.s., and
\nabla F(X(x_{0}))=0
,

\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|_{1-\alpha}^{*}\to 0
a.s.

Proof.

See Lemma 6.4 in
Chorobura and Necoara 2023
for a proof.
∎

Next theorem presents the convergence rate for the relative smooth algorithm RRCD when the function
F
satisfies the
q
-growth assumption with
q\in[1,2]
(i.e., Assumption
2
). Consider
C_{2}=C\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\Delta_{0}^{2p-1}
and
C_{3}=\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\Delta_{0}^{2p}C
, with
p=\frac{q-1}{q}
,
C
defined in (
72
),
\Delta_{0}
defined in (
73
) and
\mu_{\alpha}>0
being a constant such that

\mu_{\alpha}(F(x)-F^{*})^{p}\leq\|\nabla F(x)\|_{1-\alpha}^{*}\quad\forall x\in\text{dist}(x,X^{*})\leq\gamma,F^{*}<F(x)<F^{*}+r.\vskip-8.5359pt

(75)

Theorem 11
.

Let the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by RRCD be bounded. If Assumptions
2
and
5
hold, with
X=\bar{X}
, then for any
\delta>0
there exist a measurable set
\Omega_{\delta}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{\delta}]\geq 1-\delta
and
k_{\delta,\gamma,r}>0
such that with probability at least
1-\delta
the following statements hold, for all
k\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}
:

\mathbb{E}[F(x_{k})-F^{*}]\leq\left(1-C_{2}\right)^{k-k_{\delta,\gamma,r}}\left(F(x_{k_{\delta,\gamma,r}})-F^{*}+C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}\right)+C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(76)

Proof.

Since the function
F
is convex, Assumption
2
with
X=\bar{X}

is equivalent to (
31
) with
p=\frac{q-1}{q}
(see
J. Bolte and Suter 2016
). Moreover, (
31
) is equivalent to (
75
).

From Lemma
4
, we have that
F(x_{k})\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}F^{*}
and
\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|_{1-\alpha}^{*}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\to}0
, i.e., there exists a set
\Omega
such that
\mathbb{P}[\Omega]=1
and for all
\omega\in\Omega:F(x_{k}(\omega))\to F^{*}(\omega)
and
\|\nabla F(x_{k}(\omega))\|_{1-\alpha}^{*}\to 0
. Moreover, from the Egorov’s theorem (see
(
Stein and Shakarchi 2005
, Theorem 4.4)
), we have that for any
\delta>0
there exists a measurable set
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
satisfying
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{\delta}]\geq 1-\delta
such that
F(x_{k})
converges uniformly to
F^{*}
and
\nabla F(x_{k})
converges uniformly to
0
on the set
\Omega_{\delta}
.
Since
F
satisfies inequality (
75
), there exists a
k_{\delta,\gamma,r}>0
and
\Omega_{\delta}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{\delta}]\geq 1-\delta
such that
\text{dist}(x_{k}(\omega),X(x_{0}))\leq\gamma,\;F^{*}<F(x_{k}(\omega))<F^{*}+r
for all
k\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{\delta}
, and additionally:

\mu_{\alpha}(F(x_{k}(\omega))-F^{*})^{p}\leq\|\nabla F(x_{k}(\omega))\|_{1-\alpha}^{*}\quad\forall k\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}\text{ and }\omega\in\Omega_{\delta}.\vskip-5.69046pt

(77)

Equivalently, we have:

\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\mu^{2}_{\alpha}(F(x_{k})-F^{*})^{2p}\right)\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}\quad\forall k\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Taking expectation on both sides of the previous inequality and since
F(x_{k})\leq F(x_{0})
, from
(
Chorobura and Necoara 2023
, Lemma 9.1)
we have for all
k\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}
:

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})^{2p}]-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\Delta_{0}^{2p}\sqrt{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}^{2}(F(x_{k})-F^{*})^{2p}\right)]

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\delta}}\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\|\nabla F(x_{k})\|^{*}_{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}\right].}\vskip-5.69046pt

Taking expectation in the inequality (
67
), w.r.t.
\{x_{0},\cdots,x_{k-1}\}
, and combining with the inequality above, we get:

\displaystyle C\left(\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})^{2p}]-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\Delta_{0}^{2p}\sqrt{\delta}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]-\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k+1})-F^{*})].\vskip-5.69046pt

Since
(F(x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing and
p\in[0,1/2]
, we have that

\displaystyle C\left(\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]\Delta_{0}^{2p-1}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\alpha}^{2}\Delta_{0}^{2p}\sqrt{\delta}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]-\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k+1})-F^{*})].\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence,

\displaystyle C_{2}\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]-C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}\leq\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]-\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k+1})-F^{*})].\vskip-5.69046pt

(78)

Note that, if
\mathbb{E}[F(x_{\bar{k}})-F^{*}]\leq C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}
for some
\bar{k}\geq k_{\delta,\gamma,r}
, then the statement holds for
k\geq\bar{k}
, since
(F(x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing. Otherwise, if
\mathbb{E}[F(x_{k+1})-F^{*}]>C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}
, from (
78
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k+1})-F^{*})]-C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}\leq(1-C_{2})\left(\mathbb{E}[(F(x_{k})-F^{*})]-C_{3}\sqrt{\delta}\right).\vskip-5.69046pt

Unrolling the sequence, we get the linear rate.
∎

5.3
Implementation details in relative smooth case

Some examples of relative smooth functions and their constant are given in
Lu et al. 2018
. In this section we derive the relative smooth constant along coordinates of a function presented in
Lu et al. 2018
. Moreover, we present an explicit solution for the subproblem in RRCD, for two examples of functions
\phi
.

Example of a function which is relative smooth along coordinates:

Consider the function

G(x)=\dfrac{1}{4}\|Ex\|^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{\ell_{4}}^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|Cx-d\|^{2}
. In
Lu et al. 2018
it was proved that this function
G
is relative smooth w.r.t. the function
\phi(x)=\dfrac{1}{4}\|x\|^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
. We consider a more general function defined as
F(x)=f(x)+\dfrac{1}{4}\|Ex\|^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{\ell_{4}}^{4}
, where
f
is coordinate-wise Lipschitz with constants
L_{i}(f)>0
for
i=1,\cdots,N
, see Defintion
2
.
Let us derive the relative smooth constants along coordinates of
F
. Consider
D(x)=\text{diag}(Ax-b)
, then we have

\displaystyle U_{i}^{T}\nabla^{2}F(x)U_{i}

\displaystyle=U_{i}^{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)U_{i}+\|Ex\|^{2}(EU_{i})^{T}(EU_{i})

\displaystyle+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2(EU_{i})^{T}Exx^{T}E^{T}EU_{i}}+3(AU_{i})^{T}D^{2}(x)AU_{i}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|U_{i}^{T}\nabla^{2}F(x)U_{i}\|\leq\|U_{i}^{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)U_{i}\|+\|EU_{i}\|^{2}\|E\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\|EU_{i}\|^{2}\|E\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}+3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}(\|b\|+\|A\|\|x\|)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(L_{i}(f)+3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}\right)\!+\!6\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|b\|\|A\|\|x\|\!+\!\left(3\|EU_{i}\|^{2}\|E\|^{2}\!+\!3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|A\|^{2}\right)\|x\|^{2}.

Since

\displaystyle U_{i}^{T}\nabla^{2}\phi(x)U_{i}=2U_{i}^{T}xx^{T}U_{i}+(\|x\|^{2}+1)I_{n_{i}}\succeq(\|x\|^{2}+1)I_{n_{i}},

we have

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=L_{i}(f)+3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}+6\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|b\|\|A\|+3\|EU_{i}\|^{2}\|E\|^{2}+3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\|A\|^{2}

\displaystyle=L_{i}(f)+3\|AU_{i}\|^{2}\left(\|b\|+\|A\|\right)^{2}+3\|EU_{i}\|^{2}\|E\|.

Hence, we can use algorithm RRCD for minimizing this function
F
and Step 2 of the algorithm RRCD can be efficiently implemented, i.e., in closed form as we will show below. On the other hand, if we consider
F=f+\psi
, where
f
has coordinate-wise Lipschitz gradient and
\psi(x)=\dfrac{1}{4}\|Ex\|^{4}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|_{\ell_{4}}^{4}
, then one can use the algorithms from
Chorobura and Necoara 2023
;
Necoara and Chorobura 2021
for minimizing
F
. However, in these algorithms one needs to solve a subproblem of the form:

\;\;d_{k}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}f(x_{k})+\langle U^{T}_{i_{k}}\nabla f(x_{k}),d\rangle+\dfrac{L_{i_{k}}}{2}\|d\|^{2}+\psi(x_{k}+U_{i_{k}}d),\vskip-2.84544pt

(79)

which does not have a closed form solution and it is more difficult to solve it.

Efficient solution for subproblem:

Note that solving (
65
) is equivalent to minimizing

\displaystyle d^{*}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}\langle U^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)-L_{i}U^{T}_{i}\nabla\phi(x),d\rangle+L_{i}\phi(x+U_{i}d).\vskip-8.5359pt

(80)

Next, we show that if a function
F
is relative smooth along coordinates w.r.t.
\phi_{1}(x)=\dfrac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
, with
p>2
, or
\phi_{2}(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle
, then the subproblem of RRCD can be solved in closed form. Moreover, if the level set of the function
F
,
\mathcal{L}_{F}(x_{0})
, is bounded, then Assumption
5
[A3] holds for
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, since both are twice differentiable.

1. Consider
\phi(x)=\dfrac{1}{p}\|x\|^{p}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
with
p>2
. In this case we have,

\nabla\phi(x)=\|x\|^{p-2}x+x
. Moreover, the subproblem (
80
) becomes

\displaystyle d^{*}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}\langle U^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)-L_{i}\|x\|^{p-2}U_{i}^{T}x,d\rangle+\dfrac{L_{i}}{2}\|d\|^{2}+\dfrac{L_{i}}{p}\|x+U_{i}d\|^{p}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Denoting
g_{i}(x):=U^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)-L_{i}\|x\|^{p-2}U_{i}^{T}x
, we have

\displaystyle d^{*}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}\langle g_{i}(x),d\rangle+\dfrac{L_{i}}{2}\|d\|^{2}+\dfrac{L_{i}}{p}\|x+U_{i}d\|^{p}.\vskip-5.69046pt

From the optimality condition, we have

\displaystyle g_{i}(x)+L_{i}d^{*}+L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}U_{i}^{T}(x+U_{i}d^{*})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(L_{i}+L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2})d^{*}=-g_{i}(x)-L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}U_{i}^{T}x.

(81)

Note that if the value of
\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|
is known, then
d^{*}
can be computed as:

\displaystyle d^{*}=\dfrac{-g_{i}(x)-L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}U_{i}^{T}x}{L_{i}(1+\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2})}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Next, in order to get the value of
\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|
we need to find a positive root of a polynomial equation. Indeed, multiplying the equality (
81
) by
U_{i}
and adding
\left(L_{i}+\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}\right)x
on both sides of the inequality, we have

\displaystyle(L_{i}+L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2})(x+U_{i}d^{*})

\displaystyle=-U_{i}g_{i}(x)-L_{i}\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}U_{i}U_{i}^{T}x+\left(L_{i}+\|x+U_{i}d^{*}\|^{p-2}\right)x.\vskip-5.69046pt

Taking the norm on both sides of the equality and denoting
\alpha=\|x+U_{i}d\|
we have

\displaystyle L_{i}\alpha+L_{i}\alpha^{p-1}=\|U_{i}g_{i}(x)+L_{i}\alpha^{p-2}(U_{i}U_{i}^{T}x-x)-L_{i}x\|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(L_{i}\alpha+L_{i}\alpha^{p-1})^{2}=\|U_{i}g_{i}(x)+L_{i}\alpha^{p-2}(U_{i}U_{i}^{T}x-x)-L_{i}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2(p-1)}+2L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{p}+L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2}=\|g_{i}(x)-L_{i}U^{T}_{i}x\|^{2}+(L_{i}\alpha^{(p-2)}+L_{i})^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2(p-1)}+2L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{p}+L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\|g_{i}(x)-L_{i}U^{T}_{i}x\|^{2}+(L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2(p-2)}+2L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{(p-2)}+L_{i}^{2})\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2(p-1)}-L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2(p-2)}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}+2L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{p}-2L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{(p-2)}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}+L_{i}^{2}\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\|g_{i}(x)-L_{i}U^{T}_{i}x\|^{2}+L_{i}^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, the value of
\|x+U_{i}d\|
is a nonnegative root of the polynomial

\displaystyle a\alpha^{2(p-1)}-b\alpha^{2(p-2)}+2a\alpha^{p}-2b\alpha^{(p-2)}+a\alpha^{2}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c}=0,\vskip-5.69046pt

with

\displaystyle a=L_{i}^{2},\,\ b=L_{i}^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}\text{ and }{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c}=\|U_{i}g_{i}(x)-L_{i}U^{T}_{i}x\|^{2}+L_{i}^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\|x^{(j)}\|^{2}.\vskip-5.69046pt

In particular, for
p=4
(as in the previous example) we have

\displaystyle a\alpha^{6}+(2a-b)\alpha^{4}+(a-2b)\alpha^{2}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c}=0.\vskip-5.69046pt

(82)

Note that the polynomial above has only one change of sign when
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c}\neq 0
.
Then, using Descarte’s rule of signs we have that (
82
) has only one positive root. Moreover, if
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}c}=0
, then
\alpha=0
is a root of (
82
).

2. Now consider
\phi(x)=\langle Ax,x\rangle
. Let us consider the scalar case, i.e.,
U_{i}=e_{i}
. Then,
\nabla\phi(x)=Ax
and the subproblem (
80
) becomes

\displaystyle d^{*}=\arg\min_{d\in\mathbb{R}}\langle e^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)-L_{i}e^{T}_{i}Ax,d\rangle+L_{i}\langle A(x+e_{i}d),x+e_{i}d\rangle.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence, from the optimality conditions we have

\displaystyle e^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)-L_{i}e^{T}_{i}Ax+L_{i}e^{T}_{i}A(x+e_{i}d^{*})=0.\vskip-5.69046pt

Then,

\displaystyle d^{*}=\dfrac{-e^{T}_{i}\nabla F(x)}{L_{i}A_{ii}},\vskip-5.69046pt

where
A_{ii}
is the
i
th component of the diagonal of A.

Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

7
Conclusions

In this paper we have considered a general framework for smooth approximations of nonsmooth and nonseparable objective functions, which covers in particular Moreau envelope, Forward-Backward envelope, Douglas-Rachford envelope and Nesterov’s smoothing. We have derived convergence rates for random (accelerated) coordinate descent methods for minimizing the smooth approximation. Moreover, under an additional
q
-growth assumption on the original function, we have derived improved rates (even linear) for (restart accelerated) coordinate descent variants. We have also introduced a relative randomized coordinate descent algorithm when the original function is relative smooth w.r.t. a (possibly nonseparable) differentiable function. Convergence rates have been also derived for this algorithm in the convex and
q
-growth cases. We have tested our numerical methods on two well-known applications and the numerical results show their efficiency.

On the Chow theory of projectivizations
Qingyuan Jiang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a decomposition result for the Chow groups of projectivizations of coherent sheaves of homological dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 . In this process, we establish the decomposition of Chow groups for the cases of Cayley’s trick and standard flips. Moreover, we apply these results to study the Chow groups of symmetric powers of curves, nested Hilbert schemes of surfaces, and the varieties resolving Voisin maps for cubic fourfolds.

Primary: 14C15, 14C25, 14E05, 14H51, 14D06

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a Cohen–Macaulay scheme of pure dimension, and
\mathscr{G}
a coherent sheaf on
X
of rank
r
and homological dimension
\leq 1
, that is, locally over
X
, there is a two-step resolution
0\to\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{G}\to 0
, where
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{E}
are finite locally free sheaves. (If
X
is regular, this condition on
\mathscr{G}
is equivalent to
\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{i}_{X}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})=0
for all
i\geq 2
.) The projectivization
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}):=\operatorname{Proj}_{X}\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}^{\bullet}\mathscr{G}\to X
of
\mathscr{G}
is generically a projective bundle with fiber
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
; However, the dimension of the fiber of
\pi
jumps along the degeneracy loci (see §
2.1
) of
\mathscr{G}
.

The derived category of
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
was studied in
[
JL18
]
, where we prove (under certain regularity and dimension conditions) that there is a semiorthogonal decomposition:

{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}))=\big\langle{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))),~{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(X)\otimes\mathscr{O}(1),\ldots,{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(X)\otimes\mathscr{O}(r)\big\rangle.

(For a space
Y
,
{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(Y)
stands for its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves). The theorem states that the (right) orthogonal of the “projective bundle part” of
{\rm D^{b}_{coh}}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}))
is given by the derived category of another projectivization
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))
, which is a Springer type partial desingularization of the singular locus of
\mathscr{G}
. See
[
JL18
]
for more details

In this paper, we establish the Chow-theoretic version of the above formula:

Theorem
.

(See Theorem
4.1
) Let
X
and
\mathscr{G}
be as above. Assume either

(A)

\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))
are non-singular and quasi-projective, and the degeneracy loci of
\mathscr{G}
satisfy a weak dimension condition
(\ref{eqn:weak.dim})
; Or

(B)

All degeneracy loci of
\mathscr{G}
(are either empty or) have expected dimensions.

Then for each
k\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of integral Chow groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}))\simeq\operatorname{CH}_{k-r}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})))\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-1)+i}(X).

Since the isomorphism of the theorem commutes with product with another space, by Manin’s identity principle, if
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))
and
X
are smooth and projective over the ground field
\Bbbk
, then there is an isomorphism of integral (pure effective) Chow motives:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}))\simeq\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})))(r)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak{h}(X)(i).

See Corollary
4.3
. Note that this result compares nicely with Vial’s work
[
Vi13
]
on
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
-fibrations; In our case,
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
is a
generic

\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
-fibration. By taking a cohomological realization, for example by taking the Betti cohomology if
\Bbbk\subset\mathbb{C}
, the isomorphism of Motives induces an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures:

\displaystyle H^{n}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}),\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{n-2r}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})),\mathbb{Q})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}H^{n-2i}(X,\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\forall n\geq 0.

This paper provides two approaches to proving the above theorem, each under one of the conditions (A) and (B). The idea behind both approaches is that one could view the projectivization phenomenon as a combination of Cayley’s trick and flips.

We study the Chow theory for Cayley’s trick in §
3.1
(see Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
), and the Chow theory of standard flips in §
3.2
(see Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.10
). These results are of independent interest on their own. For example, it follows from Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
that the Chow group (resp. motive) of every complete intersection variety can be split embedded into that of a Fano variety,
see Example
3.5
(cf.
[
KKLL17
]
).

First examples of the theorem are: (i) universal
\operatorname{Hom}
spaces, see §
4.3.1
, (ii) flops from Springer type resolutions of determinantal hypersurfaces, see §
4.3.2
, and (iii) a blowup formula for blowing up along Cohen–Macaulay codimension
2
subschemes, see §
4.3.3
.

Applications

The following applications parallel the applications of the projectivization formula in the study of derived categories
[

(1)

Symmetric powers of curves §
5.1
.
Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 1
and denote by
C^{(k)}
the
k
-th symmetric power. For any
0\leq n\leq g-1

C^{(g-1+n)}
and
C^{(g-1-n)}
(and also the Jacobian variety
\operatorname{Jac}(C)

Tod18b

[
JL18

BK19
]
using the projectivization formula. The main theorem of this paper implies the corresponding Chow-theoretic version of the formula: for any

, there is an isomorphism of integral Chow groups

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(C^{(g-1+n)})\simeq\operatorname{CH}_{k-n}(C^{(g-1-n)})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(n-1)+i}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)),

and a similar decomposition for integral Chow motives, see Corollary
5.1
.

(2)

Nested Hilbert schemes of surfaces §
5.2
. Let
S
be a smooth quasi-projective surface, and denote by
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(S)
the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
S
, by convention
\mathrm{Hilb}_{1}(S)=S
,
\mathrm{Hilb}_{0}={\rm point}
. Denote
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n,n+1}(S)
the nested Hilbert scheme. Then projectivization formula of derived categories
[
JL18
]
can be applied to obtain a semi-orthogonal decomposition of

, see Belmans–Krug
[
BK19
]
. In this paper, we show that for any

, there is an isomorphism of integral Chow groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n,n+1}(S))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{CH}_{k-1}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n-1,n}(S))\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(S)\times S)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\operatorname{CH}_{k-i}(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n-i}(S)\times S),

and a similar decomposition for Chow motives, see Corollary
5.4
.

(3)

.

Y
be a cubic fourfold not containing any plane, let

be the Fano variety of lines on

be the corresponding LLSvS eightfold
[

[

constructed a rational map

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\operatorname{Bl}_{Z}(F(Y)\times F(Y)))\simeq\operatorname{CH}_{k-1}(\widetilde{Z})\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{k}(F(Y)\times F(Y)),

where
\widetilde{Z}=\mathbb{P}(\omega_{Z})
is a Springer type (partial) resolution of the indeterminacy locus
Z
, which is an isomorphism over
Z\backslash\Delta_{2}
, and a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over the type II locus

which is an algebraic surface. See Corollary
5.6
.

Convention

Throughout this paper,
X
is a Noetherian scheme of pure dimension, and
\mathscr{G}
is a coherent sheaf over
X
. We say that
\mathscr{G}
has rank
r
if the rank of
\mathscr{G}(\eta):=\mathscr{G}\otimes\kappa(\eta)
is
r
at the generic point
\eta
of each irreducible component of
X
. Assume all schemes in consideration are defined over some fixed ground field
\Bbbk
. The terms “locally free sheaves” and “vector bundles” will be used interchangeably. We use
Grothendieck’s notations
: for a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on a scheme
X
, denote by
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathscr{F})=\operatorname{Proj}_{X}\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}^{\bullet}\mathscr{F}
its projectivization; we will write
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{F})
if the base scheme is clear from context. For a vector bundle
V
, we also use
\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(V):=\mathbb{P}(V^{\vee})
to denote the moduli space of
1
-dimensional linear subbundles of
V
.

For motives, we use the
covariant
convention of
[
KMP
,
Mu1
,
Mu2
,
Vi13
,
Vi15
]
. In particular,
[
KMP
]
contains a dictionary for translating between covariant and contravariant conventions. For a smooth projective variety
X
over a field
\Bbbk
, denote by
\mathfrak{h}(X)
its class
(X,\operatorname{Id}_{X},0)
in Grothendieck’s category of integral Chow motives of smooth projective varieties over
\Bbbk
. Notice that under the
covariant
convention, for a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of smooth projective varieties,
\Gamma_{f}
induces the pushforward map
f_{*}\colon\mathfrak{h}(X)\to\mathfrak{h}(Y)
and
[\Gamma_{f}^{t}]
induces the pullback map
f^{*}\colon\mathfrak{h}(Y)\to\mathfrak{h}(X)(\dim Y-\dim X)
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=1\oplus\mathbb{L}=1\oplus 1(1)
, where
1=\mathfrak{h}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
, and
\mathbb{L}=1(1)
is the Lefschetz motive. In particular, the covariant Tate twist coincide with tensoring with
\mathbb{L}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{h}(X)(i)=\mathfrak{h}(X)\otimes\mathbb{L}^{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{CH}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{h}(X)(n))=\operatorname{CH}^{\ell-n}(X)
and
\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathfrak{h}(X)(n))=\operatorname{CH}_{k-n}(X)
. We will use
h
to denote the action
c_{1}(\mathscr{O}(1))\cap(\underline{\hphantom{A}})
on motives when the line bundle
\mathscr{O}(1)
is clear from the context.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Degeneracy loci

Standard references are
[
FP
,
Ful
,
GKZ
,
GG
,
Laz
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a coherent sheaf of (generic) rank
r
over a scheme
X
. For an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
degeneracy locus of
\mathscr{G}
of rank
\geq k
is defined to be

X^{\geq k}(\mathscr{G}):=\{x\in X\mid\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{G}(x)\geq k\},

where
\mathscr{G}(x):=\mathscr{G}_{x}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X,x}}\kappa(x)
is the fiber of
\mathscr{G}
at
x\in X
. Notice that
X^{\geq k}(\mathscr{G})=X
if
k\leq r
;
X_{\mathrm{sg}}(\mathscr{G}):=X^{\geq r+1}(\mathscr{G})
is called
first degeneracy locus
(or the
singular locus
) of
\mathscr{G}
.

(2)

Let
\sigma:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}
a morphism of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules between locally free sheaves
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{E}
on
X
. For an integer
\ell
, the
degeneracy locus of
\sigma
of rank
\ell
is defined to be

D_{\ell}(\sigma):=\{x\in X\mid\operatorname{rank}\sigma(x)\leq\ell\},

where
\sigma(x):=\sigma_{x}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X,x}}\kappa(x)\colon\mathscr{F}(x)\to\mathscr{E}(x)
is the map induced by
\sigma
on the fibers.

The degeneracy loci
X^{\geq k}(\mathscr{G})
and
D_{\ell}(\sigma)
have natural closed subscheme structures given by Fitting ideals, see
[
Laz
, §7,2]
. The two notions are related as follows: let
\sigma\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}
be an
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module map between finite locally free sheaves, and let
\mathscr{G}:=\operatorname{Coker}(\sigma)
be the cokernel, then
X^{\geq k}(\mathscr{G})=D_{\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E}-k}(\sigma)
as closed subschemes of
X
.

The expected codimension of
D_{\ell}(\sigma)\subset X
is
(\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E}-\ell)(\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{F}-\ell)
(if
\ell\leq\min\{\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E},\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{F}\}
). If
\mathscr{G}
has homological dimension
\leq 1
and rank
r
, for example if
\mathscr{G}=\operatorname{Coker}(\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\mathscr{E})
is the cokernel of an injective map of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-modules between finite locally free sheaves, then for any
i\geq 0
, the expected codimension of
X^{\geq r+i}(\mathscr{G})\subset X
is
i(r+i)
.

In the universal local situation where
X=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(W,V)
is the total space of maps between two vector spaces
W
and
V
over a field
\Bbbk
, there is a tautological map
\tau\colon W\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\to V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}
over
X
, such that
\tau(A)=A
for
A\in\operatorname{Hom}(W,V)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
FP
,
GKZ
,
GG
]
)
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(W,V)
, denote
D_{\ell}=D_{\ell}(\tau)\subseteq X
the degeneracy locus of the tautological map
\tau
of
\operatorname{rank}\ell
. Then for any
0\leq\ell\leq\min\{\operatorname{rank}W,\operatorname{rank}V\}
, the singular locus of
D_{\ell}
is
D_{\ell-1}
. Furthermore, for any regular point
A\in D_{\ell}\backslash D_{\ell-1}
:

(1)

The tangent space of
D_{\ell}
at
A
is
T_{A}D_{\ell}=\{T\in\operatorname{Hom}(W,V)\mid T(\operatorname{Ker}A)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}A\}
.

(2)

The normal space of
D_{\ell}
to
X
at
A
is
N_{D_{\ell}}X|_{A}=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}A,\operatorname{Coker}A)
.

Proof.

See
[
FP
, §5.1, p. 54-55]
, or
[
GKZ
, Lemma 4.12]
, or
[
GG
, Ex. V (4), p. 145]
.
∎

In general, let
\sigma:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}
be a map between vector bundles over a scheme
X
. For a fixed integer
\ell
, regarding the open degeneracy locus
D:=D_{\ell}(\sigma)\backslash D_{\ell-1}(\sigma)
, we have the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
X
is a Cohen-Macaulay
\Bbbk
-scheme, and
D:=D_{\ell}(\sigma)\backslash D_{\ell-1}(\sigma)\subset X
has the expected codimension
(\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E}-\ell)(\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{F}-\ell)
. Then
\sigma|_{D}\colon\mathscr{E}|_{D}\to\mathscr{F}|_{D}
has constant rank
\ell
over
D
;
K:=\operatorname{Ker}\sigma|_{D}
and
C:=\operatorname{Coker}\sigma|_{D}
are locally free sheaves over
D
of ranks
\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E}-\ell
and
\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{F}-\ell
respectively. Moreover,
D\subset X
is a locally complete intersection subscheme with normal bundle
N_{D/X}\simeq K^{\vee}\otimes C
.

Proof.

First, we prove the lemma for the total Hom space
H=|\operatorname{Hom}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E})|
. Denote
\pi\colon H=|\operatorname{Hom}_{X}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E})|\to X
the projection, and let
\mathbb{D}_{\ell}:=D_{\ell}(\tau_{H})\subset H
be the degeneracy locus for the tautological map
\tau_{H}\colon\pi^{*}\mathscr{F}\to\pi^{*}\mathscr{E}
. As the statement is local, we may assume
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
,
\mathscr{F}=W\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
,
\mathscr{E}=V\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
, where
A
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra,
W,V
are
\Bbbk
-vector spaces. Then
H=\operatorname{Hom}(W,V)\times_{\Bbbk}X
is the flat base-change of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(W,V)
along
X\to\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk
, and
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{\ell}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{\ell-1}=D\times_{\Bbbk}X
. The desired result holds for
H
and
\mathbb{D}
by Lemma
2.2
.

In general, the map
\sigma\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}
induces a section map
s_{\sigma}\colon X\to H
, such that
\sigma=s_{\sigma}^{*}\tau_{H}
and
D=\mathbb{D}\times_{X}H
. Since
s
is the section of a smooth separated morphism, it is a regular closed immersion. Since
H
and
X
are Cohen-Macaulay,
\mathbb{D}\hookrightarrow H
is a regular immersion, and the intersection
D=\mathbb{D}\times_{X}H\hookrightarrow X
has the expected codimension, therefore the inclusion
D\hookrightarrow X
is also a regular immersion, with normal bundle
N_{D/X}=s_{\sigma}^{*}N_{\mathbb{D}/H}
. Finally,
s_{\sigma}^{*}N_{\mathbb{D}/H}=K^{\vee}\otimes C
holds since
K^{\vee}=s_{\sigma}^{*}\operatorname{Coker}(\tau_{H}^{\vee})
and
C=s_{\sigma}^{*}\operatorname{Coker}(\tau_{H})
.
∎

2.2.
Chow groups of projective bundles

Let
X
be a scheme, and
\mathscr{E}
a locally free sheaf of rank
r
on
X
. Denote
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}):=\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}\mathscr{E})\to X
the projection. Notice that our convention
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})=\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(\mathscr{E}^{\vee})
is dual to Fulton’s
[
Ful
]
. For simplicity, from now on we will denote
\zeta=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})}(1))
, and use the notation
\zeta^{i}\cdot\beta:=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})}(1))^{i}\cap\beta
, where
\beta\in\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}))
, to denote the cap product. For each
i\in[0,r-1]
, we introduce the following notations:

\pi_{i}^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})=\zeta^{i}\cdot\pi^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})\colon\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-1)+i}(X)\to\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})),\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The following results are summarised and deduced from
[
Ful
, Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.3]
but presented in a way that fits better into our current work.

Theorem 2.4

(Projective bundle formula)
.

(1)

(Duality) For any
\alpha\in\operatorname{CH}(X)
,

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\,\pi_{i}^{*}(\alpha)=\pi_{*}(c_{1}(\mathscr{O}(1))^{i}\cap\pi^{*}(\alpha))=\begin{cases}0,&i<r-1,\\
\alpha,&i=r-1.\end{cases}

(2)

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is an isomorphism of Chow groups:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\pi_{i}^{*}\colon\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-1)+i}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})).

(3)

The projection to the
i
-th summand of the above isomorphism is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{i\,*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1-i}(-1)^{j}c_{j}(\mathscr{E})\cap\pi_{*}(\zeta^{r-1-i-j}\cdot(\underline{\hphantom{A}})),\quad\text{for}\quad i=0,1,\ldots,r-1.

Therefore for any
i,j\in[0,r-1]
, the following holds:

\pi_{i\,*}\,\pi_{i}^{*}=\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(X)},\qquad\pi_{i\,*}\pi_{j}^{*}=0,i\neq j,\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}))}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\pi_{i}^{*}\,\pi_{i\,*}.

Proof.

(
1
) follows from
[
Ful
, Proposition 3.1(a)]
, and (
2
) follows from
[
Ful
, Theorem 3.3]
, which could also be viewed as a special case of
[
Ful
, Proposition 14.6.5]
. For (
3
), to agree with Fulton’s notation, let
E=\mathscr{E}^{\vee}
be the dual vector bundle, so
\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(E)=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})
. From (
2
), for any
\beta\in\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}))
, there exists unique
\alpha_{i}\in\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-1)+i}(X)
,
i\in[0,r-1]
such that

\beta=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\zeta^{i}\cdot\pi^{*}\alpha_{i}.

It follows from the definition of Segre classes
s_{i}(E)\cap\alpha:=\pi_{*}(\zeta^{i+r-1}\cdot\pi^{*}\alpha)
that:

\pi_{*}(\zeta^{j}\cdot\beta)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}s_{i}(E)\cap\alpha_{r-1-j+i},\quad\text{for}\quad j=0,1,\ldots,r-1.

Then the desired results follow from solving
\alpha_{i}
’s from the above equations by using
1=c(E)s(E)=(1+c_{1}(E)+c_{2}(E)+\ldots)(1+s_{1}(E)+s_{2}(E)+\ldots)
.
∎

Notice that our maps
\pi_{i\,*}
(resp. projectors
\pi_{i}^{*}\,\pi_{i\,*}
) are nothing but the explicit expressions of the correspondences
g_{i}
(resp. orthogonal projectors
p_{r-i}
) that are inductively defined in
[
Man
, §7, p 457, Definition]
(resp.
[
Man
, §7, p 456, Proposition]
). By using these maps, Manin
[
Man
, §7, p 457]

The projector

Remark 2.6
.

(Change of basis) For any identification
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{L})
, where
\mathscr{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}X
, if we denote
\zeta^{\prime}=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\otimes\mathscr{L})}(1))=\zeta+\pi^{*}c_{1}(\mathscr{L})
, and
\pi^{\prime}_{i\,*}
the projectors with respect to

\operatorname{Span}\{\zeta^{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq k\}=\operatorname{Span}\{\zeta^{\prime i}\mid 0\leq i\leq k\},

where for any subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\operatorname{CH}^{*}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}))
, its span is defined by

\operatorname{Span}\mathcal{S}:=\big\{\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\cap\pi^{*}\beta_{i}\mid\alpha_{i}\in S,\beta_{i}\in\operatorname{CH}(X)\big\}.

Similarly, for any
0\leq k\leq r-1
,
\pi_{k\,*}^{\prime}
can be expressed as
\operatorname{CH}(X)
-linear combination of
\pi_{k\,*},\pi_{k+1\,*},\ldots,\pi_{r-1\,*}
, and vice versa.

Theorem 2.8

(Blowups)
.

(1)

The following holds:

3.
Cayley’s trick and standard flips

The projectivization can be viewed as a combination of the situation of Cayley’s trick and flips. In this chapter we study the Chow theory of the latter two cases.

3.1.
Cayley’s trick and Chow group

Cayley’s trick is a method to relate the geometry of the zero scheme of a regular section of a vector bundle to the geometry of a hypersurface; see the discussions of
[
JL18
, §2.3]
. The relationships for their derived categories were established by Orlov
[
O06
, Proposition 2.10]
, we now focus on their Chow groups.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a locally free sheaf of rank
r\geq 2
on a scheme

, and

a regular section, and denote

the zero locus of the section

of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})}(1)
on

Then

, where

. Thus

is a
\mathbb{P}^{r-2}
-bundle over
X\backslash Z
, and a
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
-bundle over

coincides with

p_{1\,*}\varepsilon=\ldots=p_{r-1\,*}\varepsilon=0
. Hence by the projective bundle formula,
\varepsilon=p^{*}\gamma
for a unique
\gamma\in\operatorname{CH}(Z)
. Now by the flat base-change formula,

\pi_{i\,*}(j_{*}\,p^{*}\,\gamma)=q_{i+1\,*}(\iota\circ j)_{*}\,p^{*}\,\gamma=q_{i+1\,*}q^{*}(i_{*}\,\gamma)=0,\qquad i\in[0,r-2].

Therefore
\pi_{i\,*}\beta=\pi_{i\,*}(\sum_{i=0}^{r-2}\pi_{i}^{*}\,\alpha_{i}+j_{*}\,\,p^{*}\,\gamma)=\alpha_{i}
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,r-2
. Hence we have established the identification:

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathcal{H}_{s})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-2}\pi_{i}^{*}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-2)+i}(X)\oplus j_{*}(\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N}_{i}))_{p_{1\,*}=\ldots=p_{r-1\,*}=0})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-2}\pi_{i}^{*}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-2)+i}(X)\oplus j_{*}\,p^{*}\,\operatorname{CH}_{k-(r-1)}(Z)

(where
\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N}_{i}))_{p_{1*}=\ldots=p_{r-1\,*}=0}
denotes the subgroup
\{\gamma\in\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N}_{i}))\mid p_{1\,*}\gamma=\ldots=p_{r-1\,*}\gamma=0\}
of
\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N}_{i}))
.) Moreover, the projection maps to first
(r-1)
-summands are respectively given by
\beta\mapsto\alpha_{i}=\pi_{i\,*}\beta
, for
i=0,1\ldots,r-2
. For the formula of the projection to the last summand, it suffices to notice that
p_{*}\,j^{*}\,\pi_{i}^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})=p_{*}(p_{i}^{*}(i^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})))=0
for
i\in[0,r-2]
and that
p_{*}\,j^{*}\,j_{*}\,p^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}})=p_{*}(c_{r-1}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})}(1))\cap p^{*}(\underline{\hphantom{A}}))=(-1)^{r-1}\operatorname{Id}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If we denote
\Gamma=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N}_{i})=\mathcal{H}_{s}\times_{X}Z
, and
\Gamma_{*}\colon\operatorname{CH}(\mathcal{H}_{s})\to\operatorname{CH}(Z)
(resp.

(resp. by the transpose

), then

\Gamma_{*}=p_{*}\circ j^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma^{*}=j_{*}\circ p^{*}.

In the above proof, we have actually shown that the relations

\Gamma_{*}\,\Gamma^{*}=(-1)^{r}\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(Z)},\quad\pi_{i\,*}\pi_{j}^{*}=\delta_{i,j}\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(X)},\quad\Gamma_{*}\pi_{i}^{*}=\pi_{i\,*}\Gamma^{*}=0,

hold for any

, and that the isomorphism (
3.3
) is given by:

\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-2}\pi_{i}^{*}\,\pi_{i\,*}+\Gamma^{*}\,\Gamma_{*}.

If

Theorem 3.6

(Standard flips)
.

Let
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a standard flip as above, and assume
X^{\prime}
is non-singular and quasi-projective. Then

(1)

The following holds:

\Gamma_{*}\,\Gamma^{*}=\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(X^{\prime})}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{*}\,\Phi^{*}=\operatorname{Id}_{\operatorname{CH}(P^{\prime})}.

(2)

There exists a split short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\to\operatorname{CH}_{k}(P^{\prime})\xrightarrow{(\Phi^{*},-i^{\prime}_{*})}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(P)\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X^{\prime})\xrightarrow{(\gamma,\alpha^{\prime})\mapsto i_{*}\gamma+\Gamma^{*}\alpha^{\prime}}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X)\to 0,

where a left inverse of the first map is given by
(\gamma,\alpha^{\prime})\mapsto\Phi_{*}\,\gamma
.

(3)

The above exact sequence induces an isomorphism of Chow groups

(3.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X^{\prime})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-m-1}\operatorname{CH}_{k-n+i}(S)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X),

given by
(\alpha^{\prime},\oplus_{i=0}^{n-m-1}\beta_{i})\mapsto\Gamma^{*}\,\alpha^{\prime}+i_{*}(\sum_{i=0}^{n-m-1}\zeta^{i}\cdot\psi^{*}\beta_{i})
. Furthermore, in the above decomposition, the projection to the first summand is given by
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\prime}=\Gamma_{*}\alpha
.

Notice that in the flop case
m=n
, this result recovers the invariance of Chow groups under flops in
[
LLW10
]
; and in the flip case
m<n
, this theorem completes the discussion of
[
LLW10
, §2.3]
by providing the complementary summands of the image of
\Gamma^{*}
in the Chow group
\operatorname{CH}(X)
. Finally, as a blowup can be viewed as a standard flip of type
(n,0)
, above theorem recovers the blowup formula Theorem
2.8
.

Proof of first part of (
1
).

The equality
\Gamma_{*}\,\Gamma^{*}=\operatorname{Id}
follows exactly the same line of proof of
[
LLW10
, Theorem 2.1]
, as already mentioned in
[
LLW10
, §2.3]
. We sketch the proof here for completeness. For any class
[W^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X^{\prime})
, by Chow’s moving lemma, (if allowing negative coefficients) we may assume it is represented by a cycle
W^{\prime}
which intersects
P^{\prime}
transversely. Therefore
\pi^{\prime*}[W^{\prime}]=[\widetilde{W}]
by
[
Ful
, Corollary 6.7.2]
, where
\widetilde{W}
is the blowup of
W^{\prime}
along
W^{\prime}\cap P^{\prime}
. Hence
\Gamma^{*}[W^{\prime}]=\pi_{*}[\widetilde{W}]=[W]
, where
W
is the image of
\widetilde{W}
, and is also the proper transform of
W^{\prime}
along the birational rational map
f^{-1}
. Now we have:

\pi^{*}[W]=[\widetilde{W}]+j_{*}\sum_{B}[E_{B}],

where let
B^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}\cap P^{\prime}
be a component, then
E_{B}\subset E
are
k
-cycles supported over components
\overline{B}=\psi^{\prime}(B^{\prime})\subset\psi^{\prime}(W^{\prime}\cap P^{\prime})\subset S
. A direct computation of dimensions shows that, for a general point
s
, the fiber
E_{B,s}
over
s
has dimension:

\dim E_{B,s}\geq\dim E_{B}-\dim(\overline{B})\geq\dim E_{B}-\dim({B^{\prime}})=k-(k-(n+1))=n+1.

Now
E_{B,s}
must contain positive fibers of of
p^{\prime}_{s}\colon\mathbb{P}_{s}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}_{s}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}_{s}^{m}
, as
n+1>n\geq m
. Hence
\pi_{*}j_{*}[E_{B}]=p_{*}[E_{B}]=0
, and
\Gamma_{*}\,\Gamma^{*}[W^{\prime}]=\pi^{\prime}_{*}\,\pi^{*}\,[W]=\pi^{\prime}_{*}[\widetilde{W}]=[W^{\prime}]
.
∎

(i)

4.1.
First approach

In this approach, we use Chow’s moving lemma, hence need
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
to be nonsingular and quasi-projective. The idea is: over the open part of the first degeneracy locus, the theorem is almost the case of Cayley’s trick. The following identities hold: for any

Proof.

For the first two statements, notice that if we write

,

, with rank

and

, and

, and we are in a very similar situation as the standard flip case §

. In fact, for (

), using the notation of the proof of Theorem

, then
\Psi_{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
correspond to the correspondence given by

and

respectively (instead of

for

and
c_{n}(\mathscr{V}^{\prime})
for

). However the composition

For (

4.3.
First examples

4.3.1.
Universal
\operatorname{Hom}
spaces

Let
S
be a Cohen–Macaulay scheme, and let
V
and
W
be two vector bundles over
S
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\operatorname{rank}W\leq\operatorname{rank}V
. Consider the total space of maps between
V
and
W
:

X=|\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{S}(W,V)|=|\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{S}(V^{\vee},W^{\vee})|.

Then there are tautological maps over
X
:

\phi\colon W\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\to V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{\vee}\colon V^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\to W^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}.

Let
\mathscr{G}={\rm Coker}(\phi)
and
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})={\rm Coker}(\phi^{\vee})
. Then it is easy to see that the condition (B) of Theorem
4.1
is satisfied, and Theorem
4.1
holds for

\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})=\operatorname{Tot}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(W^{\vee}\otimes_{S}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}(V)/S}(1))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})=\operatorname{Tot}_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\vee})}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\vee})/S}(1)\otimes_{S}V).

Notice that any map
\sigma\colon W\to V
over
S
determines a section
s_{\sigma}\colon S\to X
, such that
s_{\sigma}^{*}\phi=\sigma
,
s_{\sigma}^{*}\phi^{\vee}=\sigma^{\vee}
. Then
\operatorname{Coker}(\sigma)
and
\operatorname{Coker}(\sigma^{\vee})
(and their projectivizations) are just the pull-backs of
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{K}
(and the projectivizations
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
) along the section map
s_{\sigma}
.

Similarly, we can consider the projectivization version:

Y=\mathbb{P}_{S,\,\rm sub}(\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{S}(W,V))=\mathbb{P}_{S,\,\rm sub}(\mathscr{H}\kern-5.0pt\mathcalligra{om}_{S}(V^{\vee},W^{\vee})).

Over
Y
there are tautological maps:

\psi\colon W\otimes\mathscr{O}_{Y}(-1)\to V\otimes\mathscr{O}_{Y},\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{\vee}\colon V^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{Y}\to W^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{Y}(1).

Then the condition (B) of Theorem
4.1
is satisfied for
\mathscr{M}={\rm Coker}(\psi)
and
\mathscr{N}=\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{O}_{X})={\rm Coker}(\psi^{\vee})
, and Theorem
4.1
holds for

\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{M})=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}(V),\,\rm sub}(W^{\vee}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}(V)/S}(1))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{N})=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\vee}),\,\rm sub}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\vee})/S}(1)\otimes V).

One may also consider the linear sections of the space
Y
as in HPD theory
[
Kuz07
,
BBF16
]
.

4.3.2.
Flops and Springer resolutions

In the situation of Theorem
4.1
, if we take
r=0
, then
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))
are both Springer type partial desingularizations of the first degeneracy locus
X_{\rm sg}(\mathscr{G})=X^{\geq 1}(\mathscr{G})\subset X
. They are related by a flop, and
\Gamma=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})\times_{X}\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
is the graph closure for the rational map
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})\dasharrow\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
. For simplicity, we assume
X
is irreducible. Then Theorem
4.1
states that if either

(A)

\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
are smooth and quasi-projective (hence they are both resolutions of
X_{\rm sg}(\mathscr{G})
),
\Gamma=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})\times_{X}\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
is irreducible and
\dim\Gamma=\dim X-1
; or

(B)

X
is Cohen–Macaulay and
\operatorname{codim}X^{\geq i}(\mathscr{G})=i^{2}
for
i\geq 1
.

Then the graph closure
\Gamma
of the flop
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})\dasharrow\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})
induces isomorphisms:

\Gamma^{*}\colon\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K}))\simeq\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})),\qquad\Gamma_{*}\colon\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}))\simeq\operatorname{CH}(\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K})).

4.3.3.
Cohen–Macaulay subschemes of codimension
2

Let
X
be an irreducible scheme, and
Z\subset X
a codimension
2
subscheme whose ideal
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
has homological dimension
\leq 1
. This holds in particular for any codimension two Cohen-Macaulay subscheme
Z\subset X
inside a regular scheme
X
, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum theorem. (In fact, in this case
X
clearly has the resolution property, there always exist locally free sheaves
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{E}
, and a short exact sequence
0\to\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{I}_{Z}\to 0
, with
\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{F}=\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{E}-1
; and by the Hilbert–Burch theorem, any Cohen–Macaulay codimension
2
subscheme of
X
arises in this way.)

Consider the degeneracy
X^{\geq 1+i}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})
for
i\geq 0
as before (note
\operatorname{rank}\mathscr{I}_{Z}=1
), then
X^{\geq 1+i}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})
is the loci where the ideal
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
needs no less than
i+1
generators. It is known (e.g. see
[
ES
]
) that if
\operatorname{codim}X^{\geq 1+i}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\geq i+1
for
i\geq 1
, then

\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})=\operatorname{Bl}_{Z}X\to X

is the blowup of
X
along
Z
and is irreducible, and

\widetilde{Z}:=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{I}_{Z},\mathscr{O}_{X}))

is the Springer type desingularization of
Z
. Notice that if
X
is Goreinstein, then
\widetilde{Z}\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{I}_{Z},\omega_{X}))=\mathbb{P}(\omega_{Z})
, where
\omega_{X}
and
\omega_{Z}
are the dualizing sheaves. Theorem
4.1
states that if whether

(A)

\operatorname{Bl}_{Z}X
and
\widetilde{Z}
are smooth and quasi-projective,
\widetilde{Z}
maps birational to
Z
(hence
\widetilde{Z}
is a resolution of
Z
), and
\operatorname{codim}X^{\geq 1+i}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})\geq 1+2i
for
i\geq 1
(or equivalently
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Bl}_{Z}X\times_{X}\widetilde{Z}
is irreducible and
\dim\Gamma=\dim X-1
); or

(B)

X
is Cohen–Macaulay and
\operatorname{codim}X^{\geq 1+i}(\mathscr{I}_{Z})=i(1+i)
for
i\geq 1
.

Then for any
k\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of Chow groups:

\Gamma^{*}\oplus\pi^{*}\colon\operatorname{CH}_{k-1}(\widetilde{Z})\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{k}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(\operatorname{Bl}_{Z}X).

5.
Applications

5.1.
Symmetric powers of curves

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 1
over
\mathbb{C}
, for
d\in\mathbb{Z}
denote by
C^{(d)}
the
d
-th symmetric power of
C
. Then
C^{(d)}
is smooth projective of dimension
d
, parametrises effective zero cycles of degree
d
on
C
. By convention
C^{(0)}=\{0\}
is the trivial zero cycle;
C^{(d)}=\emptyset
for
d<0
. There is an
Abel–Jacobi map
:

AJ\colon C^{(d)}\to\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C),\qquad AJ\colon D\mapsto\mathscr{O}(D),

where
\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
is the Picard variety of line bundles of degree
d
on
C
. The fibre of
AJ
over a point
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{O}(D)\in X=\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
is the linear system
|\mathscr{L}|=\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}))=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})^{\vee})
. If
d\geq 2g-1
, by Riemann-Roch
AJ
is a projective
\mathbb{P}^{d-g}
-bundle over
\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
, which makes the case
0\leq d\leq 2g-2
most interesting. If
g\leq d\leq 2g-1
, then
AJ
is surjective, with generic fiber
\mathbb{P}^{d-g}
, and the fiber dimension jumps over
W_{d}^{d-g+i}
for
i\geq 1
, where
W_{d}^{k}
is the
Brill–Noether locus
, defined as:

W_{d}^{k}:=W_{d}^{k}(C):=\{\mathscr{L}\mid\dim H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})\geq k+1\}\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C).

If
0\leq d\leq g-1
, then
AJ
maps birationally onto the Brill–Noether loci
W_{d}^{0}\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
, which has codimension
g-d
, and the dimension jumps over
W_{d}^{i}
for
i\geq 1
.

The cases
g-1\leq d\leq 2g-2
and
0\leq d\leq g-1
are naturally related by the involution
\mathscr{O}(D)\mapsto\mathscr{O}(K-D)
, which induces canonical isomorphism

W^{k}_{d}\simeq W^{g-d+k-1}_{2g-2-d}
.
Following Toda
[
Tod18b
]
, from now on we use the following notation: set an integer
n\geq 0
, and set

d=g-1+n,\quad\text{and}\quad d^{\prime}=2g-2-d=g-1-n.

(We do not restrict ourselves to
n\leq g-1
, though this is the most interesting case.) Therefore apart from the usual Abel-Jacobi map, we also have its involution version:

AJ^{\vee}\colon C^{(d^{\prime})}=C^{(g-1-n)}\to\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C),\qquad AJ^{\vee}\colon D\mapsto\mathscr{O}(K_{C}-D).

The fiber of
AJ^{\vee}
over a point
\mathscr{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(C)
is the linear system
|\mathscr{L}^{\vee}(K_{C})|=\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(H^{1}(C,\mathscr{L})^{*})=\mathbb{P}(H^{1}(C,\mathscr{L}))
. Therefore we have the following fibered diagram:

Corollary 5.1
.

For a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g\geq 1
, and integers
n\geq 0
,
k\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of integral Chow groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{CH}_{k-n}(C^{(g-1-n)})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{CH}_{k-(n-1)+i}(\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1+n}C)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}_{k}(C^{(g-1+n)})

given by
(\gamma,\oplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{i})\mapsto\beta=\Gamma^{*}\gamma+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{1}(\mathscr{O}(1))^{i}\cap(AJ)^{*}\alpha_{i}
, where
\Gamma^{*}=r_{+\,*}\,r_{-}^{*}
as usual, and
\mathscr{O}(1)
is the line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})}(1)
under the identification
C^{(g-1+n)}=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G})
below. The same map also induces an isomorphism of Chow motives:

\displaystyle[\Gamma]^{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}h^{i}\circ(AJ)^{*}\colon\mathfrak{h}(C^{(g-1-n)})(n)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathfrak{h}(\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1+n}(C))(i)\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{h}(C^{(g-1+n)}).

Notice that
C^{(g-1-n)}=\emptyset
if
n>g-1
, hence the result is most interesting if
0\leq n\leq g-1
. To prove the corollary, we show that the above situation fits into the picture of Theorem
4.2
and satisfies condition (A) (if
C
is not hyperelliptic).

Set
X:=\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1+n}(C)
, and let
D
be an effective divisor of large degree on
C
.
\forall\mathscr{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, the exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{L}(D)\to\mathscr{L}(D)|_{D}\to 0
induces an exact sequence:

0\to H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})\to H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}(D))\xrightarrow{\mu_{D}}H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L}(D)|_{D})\to H^{1}(C,\mathscr{L})\to 0.

Globalizing (the dual of) above sequence yields the desired picture: let
\mathscr{L}_{\rm univ}
be the universal line bundle of degree
g-1+n
on
C\times X
, and
\operatorname{pr}_{C},\operatorname{pr}_{X}
be obvious projections, then

\mathscr{E}:=(\operatorname{pr}_{X*}(\operatorname{pr}_{C}^{*}\mathscr{O}(D)\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\rm univ}))^{\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{F}:=(\operatorname{pr}_{X*}(\operatorname{pr}_{C}^{*}\mathscr{O}_{D}(D)\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\rm univ}))^{\vee}

are vector bundles on
X
of ranks
\deg(D)+n
and
\deg(D)
, with
a short exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{\sigma=\mu_{D}^{\vee}}\mathscr{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}\to 0,

where
\mathscr{G}:={\rm Coker}(\sigma)
is the sheafification of
H^{0}(C,\mathscr{L})^{\vee}
, has homological dimension
\leq 1
and rank
n
, and
\mathscr{K}:=\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X})={\rm Coker}(\sigma^{\vee})
is the sheafification of
H^{1}(C,\mathscr{L})
. Therefore

C^{(d)}\equiv C^{(g-1+n)}\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{G}),\quad\text{and}\quad C^{(d^{\prime})}\equiv C^{(g-1-n)}\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{K}).

Then the stratification
X_{i}:=X^{\geq n+i+1}(\mathscr{G})
for
i\geq-1
of the Theorem
4.2
corresponds to Brill–Noether loci as follows (recall
d=g-1+n
,
d^{\prime}=g-1-n
):

X_{i}=W_{d}^{n+i}\simeq W_{d^{\prime}}^{i}

Recall the following facts from
[
ACGH
]
:

(1)

(Brill–Noether inequality)
The expected dimension of
W_{d}^{k}
is the
Brill–Noether number

\rho(g,k,d):=g-(k+1)(g-d+k)
. The following holds:

\dim W_{d}^{k}\geq\rho(g,d,k),

and
W_{d}^{k}\neq\emptyset
if
\rho(g,k,d)\geq 0
,
W_{d}^{k}
is connected if
\rho(g,k,d)\geq 1
.

(2)

(Clifford’s inequality)
For an effective divisor
D
of degree
d
,
1\leq d\leq 2g-1
, then
r(D):=\dim H^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}(D))-1\leq\frac{1}{2}d
.

(3)

(Martens theorem)
Assume
g\geq 3
, and
(d,k)\in\{2\leq d\leq g-1,1\leq k\leq\frac{d}{2}\}\cup\{g-1\leq d\leq 2g-4,d-g+2\leq k\leq\frac{d}{2}\}
. If
C
is not hyperelliptic, then
\dim W_{d}^{k}\leq d-2k-1
.
If
C
is hyperelliptic, then
\dim W_{d}^{k}=d-2k
.

Proof of Corollary
5.1
.

Uninteresting cases.

Note that
\Gamma\neq\emptyset
if and only if
0\leq n\leq g-1
; The corollary for the cases
n\geq g
follow from the projective bundle formula Theorem
2.4
. If
n=g-1
, then
AJ^{\vee}\colon C^{(g-1-n)}\simeq\{[\omega_{C}]\}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{2g-2}(C)
, and
\Gamma=\mathbb{P}_{\rm sub}(H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}))\simeq\mathbb{P}^{g-1}\subset C^{(2g-2)}
, and the fibered diagram is a Cayley’s trick diagram with
Z=\{[\omega_{C}]\}
a point, then the results follows from Theorem
3.1
. Hence we need only consider the case
0\leq n\leq g-2
and
g\geq 2
. If
g=2
, then
n=0
,
d=d^{\prime}=1
,
\Gamma\simeq C
,
\Gamma^{*}\colon\operatorname{CH}(C)\simeq\operatorname{CH}(C)
is the isomorphism induced the hyperelliptic involution on
C
. Hence we may assume from now on
g\geq 3
,
0\leq n\leq g-2
and
d=g-1+n\in[g-1,2g-3]
.

The case when
g\geq 3
and

is not hyperelliptic.

We show the condition (A) is satisfied, i.e.

{\rm codim}(W_{d}^{n}\subset X)=n+1,\quad{\rm codim}(W_{d}^{n+i}\subset X)\geq n+2i+2\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$}.

The first equality always holds, since

maps birationally onto

. For the second inequality, notice that if

,

, then

if

by Clifford’s inequality, since

if

. Hence we may assume

, and Marten’s theorem can be applied. Therefore if

is not hyperelliptic, then for any

:

{\rm codim}(W_{d}^{n+i}\subset X)\geq g-(d-2(n+i)-1)=g-(g-1+n)+2(n+i)+1=n+2i+2.

The case

, and
C
is hyperelliptic.
Take a disc

in the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
intersecting transversely to the hyperelliptic locus, with zero point
[C]
, and consider the universal curve
\mathscr{C}
over

. Then the general fiber of
\mathscr{C}
is non-hyperelliptic, and by above estimates condition (A) is satisfied by the family
\mathscr{C}
(with relative Hilbert schemes

of zero dimensional subscheme on the fibres of length

) as well as the generic fibre

(or

) specialize to the same identities for the central fiber

(see

, Ch. 10]
), hence induces the isomorphism of Corollary

for the hyperelliptic curve

.
∎

Further speculations

(1)

Let
\sigma\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{E}
be a map between vector bundles over a Cohen–Macaulay scheme
S
, then there is a section map
s_{\sigma}\colon S\to|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})|
. The condition (B) of Theorem
4.2
always holds over
|\operatorname{Hom}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{F})|
. Assume that a suitable relative Chow theory
\operatorname{CH}(X\to S)
has a well-behaved Tor-independent base-change theory, similar to the base-change theory for derived categories
[
Kuz11
]
. Then one can pull back along the section map
\sigma
and obtain a projectivization formula for
S
under a much weaker condition. The candidate theories that the author has in mind are Fulton’s bivariant intersection theory
[
Ful
, Chapter 17]
, the theory of pure Chow motives over a base
S

[
CH
]
, and the theory of Higher Chow groups over a base
S

[
Lev
, Chapter II]
.

(2)

This work is inspired by its counterpart in derived categories
[
JL18
]
, where the projectivization formula was proved using the techniques developed in
[
JLX17
,
T15
,
Kuz07
]
. It is interesting whether or not one can “decategorify” other interesting semiorthogonal decompositions obtained by these techniques. Examples include various cases of homological projective duality and flops, see
[
JLX17
,
JL18
,
T15
,
Kuz07
]
. Note that usually, results of derived categories only imply
ungraded
results for
rational
Chow groups and motives; but see
[
BT16
]
where essential
graded
information of Chow groups is recovered from derived categories.

(3)

The projectivization formula for derived categories is closely related to the wall–crossing and d-critical flips studied by Toda
[
Tod18a
,
Tod18b
]
. It would be interesting to extend the results of this paper to the cases of Donaldson-Thomas type moduli spaces considered there.

(4)

The projectivization formula considered in this paper fits into a broad framework of the study of Quot schemes of locally free quotients
[
J20
,
J21
]
.

(5)

Since the resolution
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptxt^{1}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{O}_{X}))\to X_{\rm sg}(\mathscr{G})
is usually
\operatorname{IH}
-small, it is reasonable to expect one may replace

by the
intersection
Chow group
[
CH
]
of

.

(6)

The projectivization formula of Chow groups should hold for Deligne–Mumford stacks, with
\operatorname{CH}
replaced by
\operatorname{CH}_{\mathbb{Q}}
; It would also be interesting to study the ring structure of

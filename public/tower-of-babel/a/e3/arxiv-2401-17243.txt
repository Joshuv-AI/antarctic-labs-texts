Stochastic motions of the two-dimensional many-body delta-Bose gas, IV: Transformations of relative motions Support from an NSERC Discovery grant is gratefully acknowledged.
Yu-Ting Chen

Abstract
This paper is the last in a series devoted to constructing stochastic motions representing the two-dimensional N N -body delta-Bose gas for all integers N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 via Feynman–Kac-type formulas. The main result here supplements [ 1 , 2 ] of the series by proving a bijective transformation between two general classes of Langevin-type SDEs such that the SDEs of one class describe precisely the stochastic relative motions of the SDEs of the other class. Keywords: Delta-Bose gas; Schrödinger operators; interacting diffusions. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 60H10; 60G07; 35Q40.

Introduction

This paper is the last in a series devoted to constructing stochastic motions representing the two-dimensional many-body delta-Bose gas via Feynman–Kac-type formulas. Our goal here is to study two closely related problems on stochastic descriptions in the sense of E. Nelson
[
5
]
for general many-body quantum Hamiltonians of the following form:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{z^{i}}+\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}V_{\mathbf{i}}(z^{i\prime}-z^{i}),\quad z^{i}\in E,

(1.1)

where
E\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\,{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 2
,
\mathcal{E}_{N}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\,\{\mathbf{i}=(i\prime,i)\in\mathbb{N}^{2};1\leq i<i\prime\leq N\}
,
\Delta_{z}
denotes the
d
-dimensional Laplacian in
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
V_{\mathbf{i}}(\cdot)
are meant as potential energy functions.

The two problems mentioned above concern the transformations of SDEs. Specifically, the first problem considers the following Langevin-type SDEs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}=\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{0}-\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}a_{ij}(s)(\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{j}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}s+W^{i}_{t},\quad\forall\;1\leq i\leq N,

(1.2)

obeyed by a family of
E

\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
for
N
particles.
Here,
a_{ij}(s)
are progressively measurable real-valued processes such that integral terms in (
1.2
) are well-defined, and
\{W^{i}_{t}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
consists of independent
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motions with zero initial conditions. Under this setting, we are interested in the transformations of the SDEs in (
1.2
) to the SDEs obeyed by the
stochastic relative motions
defined by the following
E
-valued processes:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Z}^{i\prime}_{t}-\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t})/{\sqrt{2}},\quad\mathbf{i}=(i\prime,i)\in\mathcal{E}_{N}.

(1.3)

Moreover, we seek to determine the SDEs of the corresponding transformation in (
1.3
)
to the degree of expressing the drift coefficients of the SDEs
in terms of
the stochastic relative motions.
By contrast, the second problem is a converse on reconstructing the SDEs of
E
-valued processes
\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
for
N
particles from given SDEs obeyed by a family of
E
-valued processes
\{\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{i}}_{t}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
such that
\{\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{i}}_{t}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
recovers the stochastic relative motions (

\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}

[
1

2
]
for the quantum Hamiltonian of the two-dimensional many-delta Bose gas, whose formal description uses (
1.1

V_{\mathbf{i}}(z)\equiv-\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}}\delta(z)
and
d=2
. In particular, the SDEs in (
1.2

\delta

\delta
motions studied in
[
1

2
]

Transformations of Langevin-type SDEs

Our goal in this section is to solve Problem
1.1

Definition 2.1.

Fix an integer
N\geq 2
. A family
\{z^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
of
E
-valued points is
difference-consistent
if for any
\mathbf{i}=(i\prime,i),\mathbf{j}=(j\prime,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
with
i=j\prime
,

\displaystyle z^{\mathbf{i}}+z^{\mathbf{j}}=z^{\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j}},\quad\mbox{ where }\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}\oplus\mathbf{i}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\,(i\prime,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{N}.

(2.1)

The set of
(z^{(2,1)},\cdots,z^{(N,N-1)})
from
difference-consistent families is denoted by
\delta E^{\it N}
.

Definition
2.1
specifies the first minimal condition for solving Problem
1.1
(1
∘
).
Note that for
N=2
,
\{z^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}}
is a singleton, so the difference-consistency holds trivially by definition. For any
N\geq 3
,
any difference-consistent family
\{z^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
is uniquely determined by
(z^{(2,1)},\cdots,z^{(N,N-1)})
via the following
telescoping representation
:

\displaystyle\forall\;\mathbf{i}=(i\prime,i)\in\mathcal{E}_{N},\;z^{(i\prime,i)}=z^{(i\prime,i\prime-1)}+\cdots+z^{(i+1,i)}.

(2.2)

In particular, since, given any
(z^{(2,1)},\cdots,z^{(N,N-1)})\in E^{N-1}
, (
2.2
) defines a difference-consistent family, the linear space
\delta E^{\it N}
is isomorphic to
E^{N-1}
as vector spaces.

Proposition 2.2 (Main result).

(1
∘
)
As vector spaces,

E^{\it N}

via the following
linear transformation

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}\;{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\,\begin{bmatrix}-1/{\sqrt{2}}&1/{\sqrt{2}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&-1/{\sqrt{2}}&1/{\sqrt{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&-1/{\sqrt{2}}&1/{\sqrt{2}}\\
1/N&1/N&\cdots&1/N&1/N\end{bmatrix}_{N\times N}:\begin{bmatrix}z^{1}\\
z^{2}\\
\vdots\\
z^{N}\end{bmatrix}\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}z^{(2,1)}\\
\vdots\\
z^{(N,N-1)}\\
z^{\Sigma}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.3)

Specifically,
\mathsf{R}
is invertible, and the matrix inverse admits the following formula:

\displaystyle(\mathsf{R}^{-1})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-{\sqrt{2}}(N-j)/N,&\;i\leq j,\;j\leq N-1,\\
\displaystyle{\sqrt{2}}j/N,&\;i>j,\;j\leq N-1,\\
\displaystyle 1,&\;1\leq i\leq N,\;j=N.\end{cases}

(2.4)

(2
∘
)
There is a pathwise one-to-one correspondence between the two classes [
N

] of families of processes
\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
and
\{\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{i}}_{t}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\cup\{\mathcal{Z}^{\Sigma}_{0}+W^{\Sigma}_{t}\}
defined as follows.

[
\boldsymbol{N}
]

The processes
\{\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}\}
satisfy the following SDEs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{t}=\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{0}-\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}a_{ij}(s)(\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{j}_{s}){\mathrm{d}}s+W^{i}_{t},\quad\forall\;1\leq i\leq N,

(2.5)

such that the following conditions hold:

\bullet

For all

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{i}}_{t}=\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{i}}_{0}-\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\sigma(\mathbf{i})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{j})\int_{0}^{t}a_{\mathbf{j}}(s)\mathcal{Z}^{\mathbf{j}}_{s}{\mathrm{d}}s+W^{\mathbf{i}}_{t},\quad\forall\;\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N},

Proof of Proposition
2.2
(1
∘
).

To prove (
2.4
), we make two observations to simplify the algebra a bit.
First, observe that by (

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}=\begin{bmatrix}1/{\sqrt{2}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1/{\sqrt{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&1/{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&\cdots&0&1/N\end{bmatrix}\mathsf{Q},\quad\mbox{for }\mathsf{Q}\;{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\,\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&-1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-1&1\\
1&1&1&\cdots&1&1\end{bmatrix}.

(2.9)

Second, for any
N\times N
-matrix
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}=[\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{N}]
and any scalars
D_{1},\cdots,D_{N}
,

\displaystyle[\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{N}]\mathsf{\rm diag}(\mathsf{D}_{1},\cdots,\mathsf{D}_{N})=[\mathsf{D}_{1}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{1},\cdots,\mathsf{D}_{N}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{N}].

(2.10)

Thus, the proposed inverse in (
2.4
) takes the form of the left-hand side of (
2.10
) with
\mathsf{D}_{1}=\cdots=\mathsf{D}_{N-1}=\sqrt{2}
,
\mathsf{D}_{N}=N
, and
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}
chosen to be
\mathsf{M}
given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{ij}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle-(N-j)/N,&\;i\leq j,\;j\leq N-1,\\
\displaystyle j/N,&\;i>j,\;j\leq N-1,\\
\displaystyle 1/N,&\;1\leq i\leq N,\;j=N.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Combining these two observations shows that it is enough to prove the invertibility of
\mathsf{Q}
with
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{Q}^{-1}
, or just the identity

\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M}={\rm Id}
.

Now, to verify
\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M}={\rm Id}
, we use the definitions of
\mathsf{Q}
and
\mathsf{M}

in (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) and consider
(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathsf{Q}_{ik}\mathsf{M}_{kj}
,
1\leq i,j\leq N
, for five cases which are mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive:

(1)
For all
1\leq i<j\leq N
,

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Q}_{ii}\mathsf{M}_{ij}+\mathsf{Q}_{i(i+1)}\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)j}=-\mathsf{M}_{ij}+\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)j}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{N-j}{N}-\frac{N-j}{N}=0,&j\leq N-1,\\
\vskip-8.5359pt\\
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{N}=0,&j=N.\end{cases}

(2)
For all
1\leq j<i\leq N-1
,

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Q}_{ii}\mathsf{M}_{ij}+\mathsf{Q}_{i(i+1)}\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)j}=-\mathsf{M}_{ij}+\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)j}=-\frac{j}{N}+\frac{j}{N}=0.

(3)
For
1\leq j<i=N
,

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{Nj}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathsf{M}_{kj}=j\left(-\frac{N-j}{N}\right)+(N-j)\frac{j}{N}=0.

(4)
For all

,

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{ii}=\mathsf{Q}_{ii}\mathsf{M}_{ii}+\mathsf{Q}_{i(i+1)}\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)i}=-\mathsf{M}_{ii}+\mathsf{M}_{(i+1)i}=\frac{N-i}{N}+\frac{i}{N}=1.

(5) For
i=j=N
,

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M})_{NN}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathsf{M}_{kN}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N}=1.

By the last equalities in these five cases, we have proved that the matrix
\mathsf{M}
defined by (
2.11
) satisfies
\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{M}={\rm Id}
. The proof is complete.

\min(i\prime,j_{1})=i\prime=\max\mathbf{i}
so that
\sigma(i\prime\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{1})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})=-1

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\prime\vee\!\wedge j_{1}}(s)\frac{(\mathcal{Z}^{i\prime}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{j_{1}}_{s})}{{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{d}}s&=-(-1)\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\prime\vee\!\wedge j_{1}}(s)\frac{(\mathcal{Z}^{j_{1}}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{i\prime}_{s})}{{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&=-\sigma(i\prime\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{1})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\prime\vee\!\wedge j_{1}}(s)\mathcal{Z}^{i\prime\vee\!\wedge j_{1}}_{s}{\mathrm{d}}s.\end{aligned}

(2.16)

(3)
For

(2.17)

(4)
For

, we have
\min(i,j_{2})=i=\min\mathbf{i}
so that
\sigma(i\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{2})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})=1
. This gives

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{t}a_{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}(s)\frac{(\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{j_{2}}_{s})}{{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{d}}s&=-\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}(s)\frac{(\mathcal{Z}^{j_{2}}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{s})}{{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&=-\sigma(i\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{2})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}(s)\mathcal{Z}^{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}_{s}{\mathrm{d}}s.\end{aligned}

(2.18)

(5)
For
i>j_{2}

\max(i,j_{2})=i=\min\mathbf{i}
so that
\sigma(i\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{2})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})=-1

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{t}a_{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}(s)\frac{(\mathcal{Z}^{i}_{s}-\mathcal{Z}^{j_{2}}_{s})}{{\sqrt{2}}}{\mathrm{d}}s=-\sigma(i\!\vee\!\!\wedge j_{2})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{i})\int_{0}^{t}a_{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}(s)\mathcal{Z}^{i\vee\!\wedge j_{2}}_{s}{\mathrm{d}}s.\end{aligned}

from class [

(2.20)

\displaystyle[W^{1}_{t},\cdots,W^{N}_{t}]^{\top}

Lemma 2.5.

Given an integer
N\geq 3
and an arbitrary family
\{p^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
of
E
-valued points, the family

\{q^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
defined by
q^{\mathbf{i}}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}}-\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\sigma(\mathbf{i})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{j})p^{\mathbf{j}}
is difference-consistent.

Proof.
For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
with
i=j\prime
and for
\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j}
defined in (
2.1
),
\sigma(\mathbf{i})+\sigma(\mathbf{j})=\sigma(\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j})
. Thus,

q^{\mathbf{i}}+q^{\mathbf{j}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\sigma(\mathbf{i})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{k})p^{\mathbf{k}}-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\sigma(\mathbf{j})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{k})p^{\mathbf{k}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}\sigma(\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j})\cdot\sigma(\mathbf{k})p^{\mathbf{k}}=q^{\mathbf{i}\oplus\mathbf{j}},

which gives the required difference-consistency of
\{q^{\mathbf{i}}\}_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}}
by Definition
2.1
.

\blacksquare

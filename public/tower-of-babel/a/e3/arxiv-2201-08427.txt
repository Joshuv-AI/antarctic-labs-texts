1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the global existence of weak solution to the modified incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

(
NSD
)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+u.\nabla u+\alpha|u|^{\beta-1}u=-\nabla p&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
{\rm div}\,u=0\hfill&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
u(0,x)=u^{0}(x)\hfill&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\alpha>0,\;\beta>1\hfill&.\end{matrix}\right.

where
u=u(t,x)=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
,
p=p(t,x)
denote respectively the unknown velocity and the pressure of the fluid at the point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\nu
is the viscosity of fluid and
u^{0}=(u_{1}^{0}(x),u_{2}^{0}(x),u_{3}^{0}(x))
the initial given velocity.
The damping is from the resistance to the motion of the flow. It describes various physical situations such as porous media flow, drag or friction effects, and some dissipative mechanisms (see
[
7
,
8
,
14
,
15
]
and references therein).
The fact that
{\rm div}\,u=0
, allows to write the term
(u.\nabla u):=u_{1}\partial_{1}u+u_{2}\partial_{2}u+u_{3}\partial_{3}u
in the following form

{\rm div}\,(u\otimes u):=({\rm div}\,(u_{1}u),{\rm div}\,(u_{2}u),{\rm div}\,(u_{3}u)).

If the initial velocity
u^{0}
is quite regular, the divergence free condition determines the pressure
p
.

In order to simplify the calculations and the proofs of our results, we consider the viscosity unitary (i.e.
\nu=1
).

The global existence of weak solution of initial value problem of the classical incompressible Navier-Stokes were proved by Leray and Hopf (see
[
13
]
-
[
16
]
) long before. The uniqueness remains an open problem for the dimensions
d\geq 3
.

The polynomial damping
\alpha|u|^{\beta-1}u
is studied in
[
10
]
by Cai and Jiu, where they proved the global existence of weak solution in

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

The purpose of this paper is to study the uniqueness, continuity and large time decay of the global solution of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with damping
(NSD)
. We recall that in
[
10
]
employ the Galerkin approximations to construct the global solution of
(NSD)
with
\beta\geq 1
. But, in our case we use Friedrich method to prove the continuity and the uniqueness of such solution for
\beta>3
. The study of large time decay is studied for
\beta\geq\frac{10}{3}
. Precisely, our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\beta>3
and
u^{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a divergence free vector fields, then there is a unique global solution of
(NSD)
:

u\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Moreover, for all
t\geq 0

(1.1)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)|_{L^{\beta+1}}^{\beta+1}ds\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, if
\beta\geq\frac{10}{3}
we have

(1.2)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Remark 1.2
.

In theorem
1.1
, the inequality (
1.1
) is proved in

[
10
]
. The new parts of this theorem is the uniqueness, the continuity of the
global solution in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the asymptotic result (
1.2
).

For a function

\displaystyle J_{R}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{B_{R}}\widehat{f}),

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(u)_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\delta_{kj}-\frac{\xi_{k}\xi_{j}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{u}_{j}(\xi)\,e^{i\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi,

is the Schwartz space.
Define also the operator
A_{R}(D)
by:

\displaystyle A_{R}(D)u=\mathbb{P}J_{R}(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(M(\xi)\chi_{B_{R}}(\xi)\widehat{u}).

3.1.
Existence of Solution

Consider the approximate system:

(NSD_{n})\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta J_{n}u+J_{n}(J_{n}u.\nabla J_{n}u)+\alpha J_{n}[|J_{n}u|^{\beta-1}J_{n}u]=\;\;-\nabla p_{n}\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\

where

\displaystyle J_{n}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi_{B_{n}}\widehat{f})
and

the ball of center

\bullet

By Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem, there exists a unique solution

such that

\bullet

The sequence

is bounded in

and on

.

Using proposition
2.4
and the interpolation method, we deduce that the sequence

\bullet

For

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to+\infty}T_{q}=\infty

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\theta_{q}(x)=1,\ {\rm for}\ |x|\leq q+\frac{5}{4}\\

Using the energy estimate (

for all

.

\bullet

Using the same method in

(3.3)

for all

, and

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle v_{n,\varepsilon}(t)=u_{\varphi(n)}(t+\varepsilon),\;p_{n,\varepsilon}(t)=p_{\varphi(n)}(t+\varepsilon),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla p_{\varphi(n)}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{n,\varepsilon}-\Delta v_{n,\varepsilon}+J_{\varphi(n)}(v_{n,\varepsilon}.\nabla v_{n,\varepsilon})+\alpha J_{\varphi(n)}(|v_{n,\varepsilon}|^{\beta-1}v_{n,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla p_{n,\varepsilon}.

The function

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{n,\varepsilon}-\Delta w_{n,\varepsilon}+\alpha J_{\varphi(n)}\Big(|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{\beta-1}u_{\varphi(n)}-|v_{\varphi(n)}|^{\beta-1}v_{n,\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{n,\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla w_{n,\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\langle J_{\varphi(n)}\Big(|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{\beta-1}u_{\varphi(n)}-(|v_{n,\varepsilon}|^{\beta-1}v_{n,\varepsilon}\Big);w_{n,\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=\langle(|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{\beta-1}u_{\varphi(n)}-|v_{n,\varepsilon}|^{\beta-1}v_{n,\varepsilon};J_{\varphi(n)}w_{n,\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}}

w
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\langle\Big(|u|^{\beta-1}u-|v|^{\beta-1}v\Big);w\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle w.\nabla u;w\rangle_{L^{2}}.

By adapting the same method for the proof of the continuity of such solution in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with

u,v,w
instead of
u_{\varphi(n)},v_{n,\varepsilon},w_{n,\varepsilon}
in oreder,

we find

\alpha\langle\Big(|u|^{\beta-1}u-|v|^{\beta-1}v\Big);w\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{\beta-1}|w|^{2}.

and

|\langle w.\nabla u;w\rangle_{L^{2}}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w|^{2}|u|^{2}+C_{\alpha,\beta}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining the above inequalities, we find the following energy estimate:

\frac{d}{dt}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2C_{\alpha,\beta}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By Gronwall Lemma, we obtain

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|w^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{2C_{\alpha,\beta}t}.

As
w^{0}=0
, then
w=0
and
u=v
, which implies the uniqueness.

3.4.
Asymptotic Study of the Global Solution

To prove the asymptotic behavior (
1.2
), we need some preliminaries lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

If
u
is a global solution of
(NSD)
with
\beta\!\geq\!\frac{10}{3}
, then
u\in L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1}=\{(t,x):\ |u(t,x)|\leq 1\}
and
E_{2}=\{(t,x):\ |u(t,x)|>1\},

\displaystyle L_{1}=\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta}dxds
and
\displaystyle L_{2}=\displaystyle\int_{E_{2}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta}dxds.

We have

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta}dxds=\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta-\frac{10}{3}}|u(s,x)|^{\frac{10}{3}}dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u(s)\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}}^{\frac{10}{3}}ds.

By using the Sobolev injection

\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we get

(3.6)

L_{1}\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{5}}}^{\frac{10}{3}}ds.

By interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{5}}}\leq\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}^{0}}^{\frac{2}{5}}\|u(s)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{3}{5}}
,
we obtain

(3.7)

L_{1}\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For the therm
L_{2}
, we have

L_{2}=\displaystyle\int_{X_{2}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta}dxds\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta+1}dxds.

Hence

\|u\|_{L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(s,x)|^{\beta+1}dxds.

Therefore
u\in L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
u
is a global solution of
(NSD)
, with
\beta\!\geq\!\frac{10}{3}
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{t\!\to\!\infty}\|u(t)\|_{H^{\!-\!2}}\!=\!0
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, using the energy inequality
(
1.1
) and Lemma
3.1
, there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

(3.8)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}([t_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4},

and

(3.9)

\|u\|_{L^{\beta}([t_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Now, consider the following system

(
NSD^{\prime}
)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\partial_{t}v-\nu\Delta v+v.\nabla v+\alpha|v|^{\beta-1}v=\;\;-\nabla q\hfill&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
{\rm div}\,v=0\hfill&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
v(0,x)=u(t_{0},x)\;\;\hfill&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\hfill&.\end{matrix}\right.

By the existence and uniqueness part, the system (
NSD^{\prime}
) has a
unique global solution
v\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

such that
v(t_{0})=u(t_{0},x)
and
q(t)=p(t_{0}+t).

The energy estimate for this system is as follows:

\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2a\int_{0}^{t}\|v(s)\|_{L^{\beta+1}}^{\beta+1}\leq\|u(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the Duhamel formula, we obtain

\displaystyle v(t,x)=e^{t\Delta}v^{0}(x)+f(t,x)+g(t,x),

where

f(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}{\rm div\,}(v\otimes v)(s,x)ds

and

g(t,x)=-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}{\rm div\,}|v(s,x)|^{\beta-1}v(s,x)ds.

By Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|e^{t\Delta}v^{0}\|_{L^{2}}=0
and hence
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|e^{t\Delta}v^{0}\|_{H^{-2}}=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}{\rm div}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|ds\right)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|ds\right)^{2}d\xi.

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}ds\right)\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{F}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi|^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{F}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|^{2}ds,

then

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{F}(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|^{2}dsd\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{-1}|(v\otimes v)(s,\xi)|^{2}d\xi)ds=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|v\otimes v)(s)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}ds.

Using the product law in homogeneous Sobolev spaces, with

s_{1}=0
,
s_{2}=1
, we get

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\|v(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds.

Using inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(t_{0}+s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla u(t_{0}+s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9(C\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)},

which implies that

\|f(t)\|_{H^{-2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\;\forall t\geq 0.

For an estimation of
\|g(t)\|_{H^{-2}}
and using

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\forall s>3/2,

with
s=2
, we get

\displaystyle\|g(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}(|v|^{\beta-1}v)(s,\xi)|ds\right)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|(|v|^{\beta-1}v)(s,.)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\||v(s,.)|^{\beta}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

where
\displaystyle C=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-2}d\xi.

Also using inequality (
3.9
), we get

\displaystyle\|g(t)\|_{H^{-2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\||u(t_{0}+.)\|_{L^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{L^{\beta}([t_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{9C}.

which implies that

\displaystyle\|g(t)\|_{H^{-2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\;\forall t\geq 0.

Combining the above inequalities, we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t)\|_{H^{-2}}=0.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
u
is a global solution of
(NSD)
and
\beta\geq\frac{10}{3}
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Proof.

Let

w_{1}={\bf 1}_{|D|<1}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big({\bf 1}_{|\xi|<1}\widehat{u}\big)\quad{\rm and}\quad w_{2}={\bf 1}_{|D|\geq 1}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big({\bf 1}_{|\xi|\geq 1}\widehat{u}\big).

Using the second step, we get

\|w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=c_{0}\|w_{1}(t)\|_{H^{0}}\leq 2c_{0}\||w_{1}(t)\|_{H^{-2}}\leq 2\||u(t)\|_{H^{-2}},

which implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

For
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
t_{1}>0
such that

\|w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\;\forall t\geq t_{1}.

We have

\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\|w_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\|\nabla w_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt<\infty.

Since the map
t\longmapsto\|w_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is continuous, there exists
t_{2}\geq t_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\|w_{2}(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
Hence

\|u(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|w_{1}(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w_{2}(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Using the following energy estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{t_{2}}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+2\alpha\int_{t_{2}}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{\beta+1}}ds\leq\|u(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\,\forall t\geq t_{2},

we get

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon,\;\forall t\geq t_{2},

and the proof is completed.

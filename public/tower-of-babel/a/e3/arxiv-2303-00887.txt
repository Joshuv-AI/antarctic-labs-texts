Ill-posedness for a generalized Camassa-Holm equation with higher-order nonlinearity in the critical Besov space
Wei Deng, Min Li, Xing Wu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we prove that the Cauchy problem for a generalized Camassa-Holm equation with higher-order nonlinearity is ill-posed in the critical Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. It is shown in (J. Differ. Equ., 327:127-144,2022) that the Camassa-Holm equation is ill-posed in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
, here we turn our attention to a higher-order nonlinear generalization of Camassa-Holm equation proposed by Hakkaev and Kirchev (Commun Partial Differ Equ 30:761-781,2005). With newly constructed initial data, we get the norm inflation in the critical space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
which leads to ill-posedness.

Keywords:
Generalized Camassa-Holm equation; Ill-posedness; Critical Besov space.

MSC (2010):
35Q53, 37K10.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for the higher-order nonlinear generalized Camassa-Holm equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-u_{xxt}+\frac{(Q+2)(Q+1)}{2}u^{Q}u_{x}=\big(\frac{Q}{2}u^{Q-1}u_{x}^{2}+u^{Q}u_{xx}\big)_{x},~~t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},~~x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
Q\geqslant 1
is a positive integer and
u(t,x)
stands for the fluid velocity at time
t\geqslant 0
in the spatial direction. The equation (
1
) was introduced by Hakkaev and Kirchev
[
15
]
in a more general case

u_{t}-u_{xxt}+(a(u))_{x}=\big(\frac{b^{\prime}(u)}{2}u_{x}^{2}+b(u)u_{xx}\big)_{x},

take
a(u)=\frac{Q+2}{2}u^{Q+1},~b(u)=u^{Q}
we get equation (
1
). When
Q=1
, (
1
) is reduced to the well-known Camassa-Holm equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}.

(CH)

The Camassa-Holm equation (
CH
) was first founded by Fuchssteiner and Fokas
[
12
]
as a completely integrable generalization of the Korteweg-de-Vries (KdV) equation with bi-Hamiltonian structure, and was later recovered as a water wave model by Camassa and Holm
[
4
]
to describe the unidirectional propagation of shallow water waves over a flat bottom. Most importantly, CH equation has peakon solutions of the form
Ce^{-|x-Ct|}
which aroused a lot of interest in physics, see
[
7
,
25
]
. There is an extensive literature about the strong well-posedness, weak solutions and analytic or geometric properties of the CH equation, here we name some. Local well-posedness and ill-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the CH equation were investigated in
[
8
,
11
,
13
]
. Blow-up phenomena and global existence of strong solutions were discussed in
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. The existence of global weak solutions and dissipative solutions were investigated in
[
2
,
3
,
27
]
, more results can be found in the references therein.

As mentioned by Hakkaev and Kirchev in
[
16
]
, (
1
) is a generalization of Camassa-Holm equation in the way that it preserves two important conservation laws of (
CH
), namely

\displaystyle E(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})dx,~~~~F(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{Q+2}+u^{Q}u_{x}^{2})dx.

(1.4)

These are the key conserved quantities to explore the orbital stability and instability of solitary wave solutions of (
CH
). We are mainly concerned with the Cauchy problem of (
1
), here is a brief review of the previous literature. Hakkafv and Kirchev
[
15
]
proved that (
1
) is locally well-posed with the initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s>\frac{3}{2}
, and also obtained the stability of peakons and orbital stability of solitary wave solution of (
1
). Yan et al.
[
28
]
proved that the solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1
) do not depend uniformly continuously on the initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s<\frac{3}{2}
and showed the local well-posedness in
B^{3/2}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Recently, a lot of literature was devoted to studying the well-posedness (especially the non-uniform dependence and ill-posedness) problem of the Camassa-Holm type equations in the critical Besov spaces
[
17
,
18
,
26
]
. For example, Guo et al.
[
13
]
proved norm inflation and hence ill-posedness for the Camassa-Holm equation in the critical Sobolev space
H^{3/2}(\mathbb{R})
which solves the open problem left by Danchin
[
11
]
. In fact, in
[
13
]
they proved the ill-posedness for the Camassa-Holm type equations in Besov space
B^{1+1/p}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
with
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]
which means
B^{1+1/p}_{p,1}
is the critical Besov space. Then, Li et al.
[
19
,
20
]
demonstrated the non-continuity and sharp ill-posedness of the Camassa-Holm type equations in
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\max\{\frac{3}{2},1+\frac{1}{p}\}
. Later, the local well-posedness for the Camassa-Holm type equations in
B^{1+1/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})
with
p\in[1,\infty)
has been proved by Ye et al.
[
29
]
through the compactness argument and Lagrangian coordinate transformation. In the remaining case
p=\infty
, Guo et al.
[
14
]
proved the ill-posedness for the CH equation in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
by exhibiting the norm inflation, then Li et al.
[
21
]
has got the ill-posedness for the Novikov equation in the same space. Quite unexpectedly, Li et al.
[
22
]
have fund that the Degasperis-Procesi equatoin is well-posed in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. In this paper, we will deduce the ill-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1
) in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. Due to the strong nonlinearity of the equation (
1
), our construction of the initial data is quite different from the previous proof.

We first rewrite (
1
) in the following equivalent nonlocal form

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u^{Q}\partial_{x}u=-\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}[\frac{Q^{2}+3Q}{2(Q+1)}u^{Q+1}+\frac{Q}{2}u^{Q-1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}],\;&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\;&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

Setting
\Lambda^{-2}=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}
, then
\Lambda^{-2}f=G*f
where
G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}
is the kernel of the operator
\Lambda^{-2}
. We can transform the generalized Camassa-Holm equation into the following transport type equation

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u^{Q}u_{x}=\mathbf{P}_{1}(u)+\mathbf{P}_{2}(u),\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.6)

where

\mathbf{P}_{1}(u)=-\Lambda^{-2}(c_{1}u^{Q-2}u_{x}^{3})-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(c_{2}u^{Q+1}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{P}_{2}(u)=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(c_{3}u^{Q-1}u^{2}_{x}\right),

(1.7)

with
c_{1}=0,c_{2}=\frac{Q^{2}+3Q}{2(Q+1)}
and
c_{3}=\frac{Q}{2}
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q\geq 2
. The Cauchy problem for the generalized Camassa-Holm equation (
1
) is ill-posed in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
in the sense of Hadamard. More precisely, for large enough
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there exists an initial data
u_{0,n}
such that the generalized Camassa-Holm equation (
1
) has a solution
u_{n}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H^{3})
satisfying

\displaystyle\|u_{0,n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq\frac{1}{\log\log n}\quad\text{but}\quad\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq{\log\log n},

with
t_{n}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\log n}\right]
.

Remark 1.1
.

In fact Theorem
1.1
shows the norm inflation in the space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
, which implies the data-to-solution map of the equation (
1
) is discontinuous at the origin
u_{0}=0
. In the sense of Hadamard, that means the Cauchy problem for the generalized Camassa-Holm equation (
1
) is ill-posed in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some basic definitions and lemmas related to Fourier transform, such as Littlewood-Paley decomposition, Besov spaces and Moser type estimation. Some common notations of this paper are given at the end.

Let us recall that for all
f\in\mathcal{S}
, the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
, is defined by

(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any}\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

The inverse Fourier transform of any
g
is given by

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}g)(x)=\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition and the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces (see
[
1
]
for more details).
Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq 4/3\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}.

Choose a radial, non-negative, smooth function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[0,1]
such that it is supported in
\mathcal{B}
and
\chi\equiv 1
for
|\xi|\leq 3/4
. Setting
\varphi(\xi):=\chi(\xi/2)-\chi(\xi)
, then we deduce that
\varphi
is supported in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\mbox{ for any }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We should emphasize that the fact
\varphi(\xi)\equiv 1
for
4/3\leq|\xi|\leq 3/2
which will be used in the sequel.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
, the inhomogeneous dyadic blocks
{\Delta}_{j}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle 0,

if
j\leq-2
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

if
j=-1
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\right),

if
j\geq 0
.

In the inhomogeneous case, the following Littlewood-Paley decomposition makes sense

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{for any}\;u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{sjr}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

if
r=\infty
.

We need the following inequality, which is a generalization of a result due to Bernstein.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 2.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Ball and
\mathcal{C}
be an annulus. There exist constants
C>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and any function
f\in L^{p}
with
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\widehat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{B}\;\Rightarrow\;\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k+(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle{\rm{supp}}\widehat{f}\subset\lambda\mathcal{C}\;\Rightarrow\;C^{-k-1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.100 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
1\leq p\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}
. There exists a constant
C
depending continuously on
p,p_{1}
, such that

\left\|\left(2^{j}\left\|[\Delta_{j},v\partial_{x}]f\right\|_{L^{p}}\right)_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{r}}\leq C\left(\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{B_{p_{1},r}^{0}}\right).

Notations :

C
stands for some positive constant independent of
n
, which may vary from line to line.
The symbol
A\approx B
means that
C^{-1}B\leq A\leq CB
.
We shall call a ball
B(x_{0},r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x-x_{0}|\leq R\}
with
R>0
and an annulus
\mathcal{C}(0,r_{1},r_{2})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:0<r_{1}\leq|x|\leq r_{2}\}
with
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
.
Given a Banach space
X
, we denote its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. We shall use the simplified notation
\|f,\cdots,g\|_{X}=\|f\|_{X}+\cdots+\|g\|_{X}
if there is no ambiguity.
We will also define the Lipschitz space
C^{0,1}
using the norm
\|f\|_{C^{0,1}}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I;X)
the set of continuous functions on
I
with values in
X
. Sometimes we will denote
L^{p}(0,T;X)
by
L_{T}^{p}X
.

3
Construction of Initial Data

Define a smooth cut-off function
\chi
with values in
[0,1]
which satisfies

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\leq\frac{1}{4^{Q}},\\
0,\quad\mathrm{if}\ |\xi|\geq\frac{1}{2^{Q}}.\end{cases}

For simplicity, we set
\gamma:=\frac{17}{24}
and dnote

\displaystyle n\in 16\mathbb{N}=\left\{16,32,48,\cdots\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{N}(n)=\left\{k\in 8\mathbb{N}:\frac{n}{4}\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}\right\}.

We introduce the following new notation which will be used often throughout this paper

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{k}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{k}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad k\in\{0,1\}.

Inspired by
[
14
,
21
]
, we define the initial data
u_{0}
which contains high frequency and low frequency by

\displaystyle u_{0,n}

\displaystyle=n^{-\frac{1}{Q+1}}\left(u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}+u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\right),

where

\displaystyle u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}

\displaystyle:=2^{-n}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma).

For any
1\leq M\leq Q
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \mathcal{F}\left(\cos\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}^{M}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ 2^{n}\gamma-2^{\ell}\gamma-\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{n}\gamma+2^{\ell}\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\right\},

(3.8)

which implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ |\xi|\leq\frac{1}{2^{Q}}\right\},\qquad\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\ \frac{4}{3}2^{n-1}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}2^{n-1}\right\}.

(3.9)

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle 2^{n}\|u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\log n,\qquad\|u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq C.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
c
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\left\|u^{Q-1}_{0,n}(\partial_{x}u_{0,n})^{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\geq c(\log n)^{2},\qquad n\gg 1.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\quad\ u^{Q-1}_{0,n}(\partial_{x}u_{0,n})^{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}(\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n})^{2}}_{=:\;\mathbf{I}_{1}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}[u^{Q-1}_{0,n}-(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}](\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}+\partial_{x}u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{2}}_{=:\;\mathbf{I}_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\left((\partial_{x}u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{2}+2\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}\partial_{x}u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\right)}_{=:\;\mathbf{I}_{3}}.

For the term
\mathbf{I}_{2}
, we can deduce from Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\leq Cn\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}||u_{0,n}||^{Q-2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n},\partial_{x}u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n}(\log n)^{Q+1}.

(3.10)

For the term
\mathbf{I}_{3}
, using (
3.9
), one has

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\mathbf{I}_{3}=0\quad\text{for}\;j\in\mathbb{N}(n)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\|\mathbf{I}_{3}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}=0.

(3.11)

For the term
\mathbf{I}_{1}
, noticing that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u^{\rm{H}}_{0,n}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\sin\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad+2^{-n}\log n\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\partial_{x}\Big(\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\Big),

then we decompose
\mathbf{I}_{1}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}=\frac{1}{n}(\log n)^{2}\left(\mathbf{I}_{11}+\mathbf{I}_{12}-\mathbf{I}_{13}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{11}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\sin\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{12}

\displaystyle=2^{-2n}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\partial_{x}\Big(\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\Big)\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{13}

\displaystyle=2\gamma 2^{-n}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\sin\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\partial_{x}\Big(\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\Big).

Easy computations give that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{12}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-2n}\|u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\|^{Q-1}_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\partial_{x}\Big(\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\Big)\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-2n}\left\|\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\frac{2^{\ell}}{(1+|x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma|)^{M}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n},

which implies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{I}_{12}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\leq Cn2^{-n}.

(3.12)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{I}_{13}\right\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}\leq Cn2^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

(3.13)

Using
\sin^{2}a\cos^{2}b=\frac{1}{4}(1-\cos(2a))(1+\cos(2b))
, we can decompose
\mathbf{I}_{11}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{11}=\gamma^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathbf{I}_{11i},\quad\text{where}

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{111}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\big(2^{\ell+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{112}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{113}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\big(2^{n+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\cdot\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{114}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\big(2^{n+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\cdot\cos\big(2^{\ell+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\cdot\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma),

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{115}

\displaystyle=(u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n})^{Q-1}\sum_{\ell,j\in\mathbb{N}(n)\atop\ell\neq j}\Big(\sin\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{\ell}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sin\left(2^{n}\gamma(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\cos\left(2^{j}\gamma(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\right)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\Big).

By (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\mathbf{I}_{112}=\Delta_{j}\mathbf{I}_{113}=\Delta_{j}\mathbf{I}_{114}=0\quad\text{for}\;j\in\mathbb{N}(n)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\mathbf{I}_{112},\mathbf{I}_{113},\mathbf{I}_{114}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}\left(\mathbb{N}(n)\right)}=0.

(3.14)

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{115}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\|\mathbf{I}_{115}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn\|u^{\rm{L}}_{0,n}\|^{Q-1}_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\ell,j\in\mathbb{N}(n)\atop\ell\neq j}\left\|\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot\check{\chi}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|(1+|x+2^{j+1}\gamma|^{2})^{-M}(1+|x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma|^{2})^{-M}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-M}(1+|x-(2^{j+1}-2^{\ell+1})\gamma|^{2}))^{-M}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\sum_{j>\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left(\gamma(2^{j}-2^{\ell})\right)^{-2M}\leq Cn^{3}2^{-\frac{nM}{2}}.

(3.15)

Finally, we can break
\mathbf{I}_{111}
down into three parts

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{111}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\big(2^{\ell+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{Q+1}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\ell,j\in\mathbb{N}(n)\atop\ell\neq j}\cos\big(2^{\ell+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\check{\chi}^{Q-1}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\cos\big(2^{\ell+1}\gamma(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{2}(x+2^{\ell+1}\gamma)\cdot\sum_{\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{Q-1}\in\mathbb{N}(n)\atop\exists\ell_{i}\neq\ell_{j},\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq Q-1}\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell_{1}+1}\gamma)\cdots\check{\chi}(x+2^{\ell_{Q-1}+1}\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{I}_{1111}+\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{I}_{1112}+\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{I}_{1113}.

Due to (
3
), we have

{\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{I}_{1111}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}\mathbf{I}_{1111}\right)=}\begin{cases}\cos\big(2^{j+1}\gamma(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{Q+1}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma),&\text{if}\;\ell=j,\\
0,&\text{if}\;\ell\neq j,\end{cases}

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{1111}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|\cos\big(2^{j+1}\gamma(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\big)\check{\chi}^{Q+1}(x+2^{j+1}\gamma)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\check{\chi}^{Q+1}(0)\geq cn.

(3.16)

Following the same procedure as
\mathbf{I}_{115}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{1112},\mathbf{I}_{1113}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\leq Cn^{Q}2^{-\frac{nM}{2}},

(3.17)

Combining the above estimates (
3.10
)-(
3.17
), we obtain that for large enough
n

\displaystyle\quad\|u^{Q-1}_{0,n}(\partial_{x}u_{0,n})^{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathbf{I}_{1}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}-\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq c\frac{1}{n}(\log n)^{2}\left(\|\mathbf{I}_{1111}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}-\|\mathbf{I}_{12},\mathbf{I}_{13},\mathbf{I}_{115},\mathbf{I}_{1112},\mathbf{I}_{1113}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\right)-C2^{-n}(\log n)^{Q+1}

\displaystyle\geq c(\log n)^{2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

4
Proof of the main theorem

By classical result, we can obtian that the generalized Camassa-Holm equation has a solution
u_{n}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1];H^{3})
with the initial data
u_{0,n}
. Let
\phi_{n}
satisfy the following ODE:

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi_{n}(t,x)=u^{Q}_{n}(t,\phi_{n}(t,x)),\\
\phi_{n}(0,x)=x,\end{cases}

(4.18)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(t,x)=x+\int^{t}_{0}u^{Q}_{n}(\tau,\phi_{n}(\tau,x))\mathrm{d}\tau.

(4.19)

Considering the transport equation

\displaystyle\quad\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}v=P,\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(4.20)

we get from (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\Delta_{j}v)+u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}\Delta_{j}v

\displaystyle=R_{j}+\Delta_{j}P,

with
R_{j}=[u^{Q}_{n},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}v=u^{Q}_{n}\Delta_{j}\partial_{x}v-\Delta_{j}(u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}v)
. Due to (
4.18
), then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left((\Delta_{j}v)\circ\phi_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=R_{j}\circ\phi_{n}+\Delta_{j}P\circ\phi_{n},

which means that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}v\circ\phi_{n}=\Delta_{j}v_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}R_{j}\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}P\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(4.21)

For
n\gg 1
, we have for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq C\|u_{0,n}\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{Q+1}}\log n.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that there exists
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq\log\log n.

(4.22)

If (
4.22
) were not true, then

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in(0,\frac{1}{\log n}]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}<\log\log n.

(4.23)

Utilizing (
4.21
) to (
1.6
) yields

\displaystyle(\Delta_{j}u_{n})\circ\phi_{n}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}u_{0,n}+\int^{t}_{0}R^{1}_{j,n}\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}F_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{3}\int^{t}_{0}\big(\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau+c_{3}t\Delta_{j}E_{0,n},

where

\displaystyle R^{1}_{j,n}=[u^{Q}_{n},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}u_{n},\qquad F_{n}=-\Lambda^{-2}\left(c_{1}u^{Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3}+c_{2}\partial_{x}(u^{Q+1}_{n})\right),

\displaystyle E_{n}=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}\right),\qquad E_{0,n}=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u^{Q-1}_{0,n}(\partial_{x}u_{0,n})^{2}\right).

Due to Lemma
3.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\approx\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\|\Delta_{j}\partial_{x}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq c\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}\left\|\Delta_{j}[u^{Q-1}_{0,n}(\partial_{x}u_{0,n})^{2}]\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq c(\log n)^{2}.

(4.24)

Then, using the fact
\|f(t,\phi_{n}(t,x))\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}
and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j,n}\circ\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{j}\|R^{1}_{j,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}(u^{Q}_{n})\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{Q-1}_{C^{0,1}}\|u_{n}\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{Q-1}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{Q+1}.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}F_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u^{Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3}+\partial_{x}(u^{Q+1}_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq C\|u_{0,n}\|^{Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-1}(\log n)^{Q+1}.

(4.26)

Combining (
4.24
)-(
4
) and using Lemmas
3.1
-
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u_{n})\circ\phi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq t\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad-Cn^{-\frac{Q-1}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{Q+1}-C\|u_{0,n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\geq ct\log^{2}n-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}-Cn^{-\frac{1}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{Q+1}.

(4.27)

Next, we need to estimate the term
\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}
. Using

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}[(Q-1)u^{2Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3}+2u^{Q-1}_{n}\partial_{x}(u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}u_{n})\partial_{x}u_{n}]

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}[Qu^{2Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3}+\partial_{x}(u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})]

\displaystyle=Q\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{2Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3})+\Lambda^{-2}(u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})-u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2},

then we find that

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{t}E_{n}+u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}E_{n}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\partial_{t}(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})-u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}((Q-1)u^{Q-2}_{n}\partial_{t}u_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}+2u^{Q-1}_{n}\partial_{x}u_{n}\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u_{n})+u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}-u^{Q}_{n}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{J}_{n}+\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}[(Q-1)u^{2Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3}+2u^{Q-1}_{n}\partial_{x}(u^{Q}_{n}\partial_{x}u_{n})\partial_{x}u_{n}]+u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}-u^{Q}_{n}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{J}_{n}+\mathrm{K}_{n},

(4.28)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}_{n}=-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left((Q-1)u^{Q-2}_{n}[\mathbf{P}_{1}(u_{n})+\mathbf{P}_{2}(u_{n})](\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}+2\partial_{x}[\mathbf{P}_{1}(u_{n})+\mathbf{P}_{2}(u_{n})]u^{Q-1}_{n}\partial_{x}u_{n}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{n}=Q\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{2Q-2}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{3})+\Lambda^{-2}(u^{2Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2})-u^{Q}_{n}\Lambda^{-2}(u^{Q-1}_{n}(\partial_{x}u_{n})^{2}).

Utilizing (
4.21
) to (
4
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}=\int^{t}_{0}[u^{Q}_{n},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\Delta_{j}(\mathrm{J}_{n}+\mathrm{K}_{n})\circ\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using the commutator estimate from Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|[u^{Q}_{n},\Delta_{j}]\partial_{x}E_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\partial_{x}(u^{Q}_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|E_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}E_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}^{Q}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{2Q+1}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1}.

Due to the facts

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\partial^{2}_{x}\Lambda^{-2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},

then we have

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}\mathrm{J}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\approx\|\partial_{x}\mathrm{J}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{2Q+1}}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}\mathrm{K}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{2Q+1}}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1}.

Then, we deduce that

\displaystyle 2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{2Q+1}_{C^{0,1}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{2Q+1}}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1},

which leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|\Delta_{j}E_{n}\circ\phi_{n}-\Delta_{j}E_{0,n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{Q}}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1}.

(4.29)

Combining (
4
) and (
4.29
), then for
t=\frac{1}{\log n}
, we obtain for
n\gg 1

\displaystyle\|u_{n}(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\geq\|u_{n}(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{j}\|(\Delta_{j}u)\circ\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq ct(\log n)^{2}-Cn^{-\frac{{Q}}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{2Q+1}-Cn^{-\frac{1}{Q+1}}(\log n)^{Q+1}

\displaystyle\geq\log\log n,

which contradicts the hypothesis (
4.23
). Thus, Theorem
1.1
is proved.
\square

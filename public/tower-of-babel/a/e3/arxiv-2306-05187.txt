Safe Adaptive Multi-Agent Coverage Control
Yang Bai, Yujie Wang, Xiaogang Xiong, and Mikhail Svinin

Abstract
This paper presents a safe adaptive coverage controller for multi-agent systems with actuator faults and time-varying uncertainties. The centroidal Voronoi tessellation (CVT) is applied to generate an optimal configuration of multi-agent systems for covering an area of interest. As conventional CVT based controller cannot prevent collisions between agents with non-zero size, a control barrier function (CBF) based controller is developed to ensure collision avoidance with a function approximation technique (FAT) based design to deal with system uncertainties. The proposed controller is verified under simulations.

The research on multi-agent coverage problems has received a considerable attention in the recent decades

,

,

]
.
The controllers proposed in

can react to dynamic environments with time-varying density functions,
and the research conducted in
[

,

,

dealt with systems with uncertainties.

These algorithms are based on CVT, representing agents by points, that stay in their corresponding cells to avoid collisions with each other. When the algorithms are implemented in real robots however, collisions may still occur unless the radius of the robots is zero, which is impractical. To tackle this problem, several works based on the buffered Voronoi cell (BVC) have been proposed in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. The BVC retracts the edge of each Voronoi cell by a safety radius, such that collisions are avoided if agents stay in the cells
[
7
]
. Nevertheless, this redesign of the Voronoi tessellation included path planning and tracking in each step. The collision avoidance capability of this process has not been tested in the presence of system uncertainties, which may drive agents out of the BVC and cause collisions.

Different from our previous work
[
10
]
, in this paper, we present a solution to the collision avoidance problem in the coverage control based on the CBF
[
11
]
, which does not require a redesign of the Voronoi tessellation. Instead, safety range for each agent is constructed such that the collision avoidance between agents can be guaranteed. Moreover, we consider actuator faults and time-varying disturbances for the agents in the controller design, which could seriously affect the system performance in a safety critical problem.

Conventional CBF approaches require accurate model information and are commonly inapplicable to control systems with uncertainties. Robust CBF methods
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
were developed to attenuate the influence of uncertainties whereas they rely on assumptions for boundness or Lipschitzness of the uncertainty.
Apart from robust approaches, several adaptive CBF methods have been proposed for systems with parametric uncertainties
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. The control scheme in
[
16
]
guaranteed safety using an online parameter adaption, and the strategy in
[
17
]
allowed systems to operate in larger safe sets, reducing conservatism. The concurrent learning technique based controller in
[
18
]
ensured the exponential convergence of the parameter estimation, and a high-order robust adaptive CBF has been developed in
[
19
]
for systems with high-relative-degree safety constraints.
To the best of our knowledge however, none of the CBF based techniques above is applicable to our system, which includes uncertainties in both the input matrix (actuator faults) and the drift term (time-varying disturbances).

Note that although FAT can transform time-varying uncertainties to parametric ones, the control of our system is still challenging because of the inclusion of actuator faults. It may cause a controllability loss issue
[
20
]
due to that the transient response of update laws, for uncertain parameters in the input matrix, is commonly uncontrollable. Techniques that addressed this issue, such as the Nussbaum gain approach
[
21
]
, have been proposed for stabilization problems, whereas systematic methods for CBF related problems have not been developed yet. In this paper, a novel adaptive CBF based approach is proposed that addresses the controllability loss issue with a special design of the update laws, and deals with the time-varying disturbances based on the FAT.

II
Preliminaries

In this section, the concepts about FAT, CBF, and CVT are reviewed, which are the main tools for our controller design.

II-A

CBF

Consider the following control affine system

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{f}(\bm{x})+\bm{g}(\bm{x})\bm{u},

(1)

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state,
\bm{u}\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the control input,
\bm{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bm{g}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are locally Lipchitz continuous functions.
A set
\mathcal{S}
is called forward controlled invariant with respect to system (
1
) if for every
\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a control signal
\bm{u}(t)
such that
\bm{x}(t;t_{0},\bm{x}_{0})\in\mathcal{S}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, where
\bm{x}(t;t_{0},\bm{x}_{0})
denotes the solution of (
1
) at time
t
with initial condition
\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
at time
t_{0}
.

Consider control system (
1
) and a set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mathcal{C}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(\bm{x})\geq 0\},

(2)

for a continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
that has a relative degree one. The function
h
is called a (zeroing) CBF
if there exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{u}\in U}\left[\mathcal{L}_{f}h(\bm{x})+\mathcal{L}_{g}h(\bm{x})\bm{u}+\gamma h(\bm{x})\right]\geq 0,

(3)

where
\mathcal{L}_{f}h(\bm{x})=\frac{\partial h}{\partial\bm{x}}\bm{f}(\bm{x})
,
\mathcal{L}_{g}h(\bm{x})=\frac{\partial h}{\partial\bm{x}}\bm{g}(\bm{x})
are the Lie derivatives
[
22
]
.
Given a CBF
h
, the set of all control values that satisfy (
3
) for all
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

K_{bf}(\bm{x})=\{\bm{u}\in U:\mathcal{L}_{f}h(\bm{x})+\mathcal{L}_{g}h(\bm{x})\bm{u}+\gamma h(\bm{x})\geq 0\}.

It was proven in
[
22
]
that any Lipschitz continuous controller
\bm{u}(\bm{x})\in K_{bf}(\bm{x})
for every
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
will guarantee the forward invariance of
\mathcal{C}
. The provably safe control law is obtained by solving an online quadratic program (QP) problem that includes the control barrier condition as its constraint.

II-B

FAT

The FAT is an effective tool to deal with control systems with time-varying nonlinear uncertainties
[
23
]
.
For instance, if
\bm{d}(t)
is an unknown time-varying function in a control system, one can utilize weighted basis functions to represent
\bm{d}(t)
, at each time instant, as
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
10
]

\displaystyle\bm{d}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\bm{d}_{j}\psi_{j}(t),

(4)

where
\bm{d}_{j}
denotes an unknown constant vector (weight) and
\psi_{j}(t)
is the basis function to be selected. An update law
\hat{\bm{d}}_{j}(t)
is designed to approximate
\bm{d}_{j}
so as to reject the effect of
\bm{d}(t)
to the control system.

Several candidates for the basis function
\psi_{j}(t)
in (
4
) can be chosen to approximate the nonlinear functions, and in this paper, we select the basis function
\psi_{j}(t)
as the Fourier series
[
23
]

\psi_{j}(t)=\begin{cases}1,&j=1,\\
\cos\omega_{l}t,&j=2l,\\
\sin\omega_{l}t,&j=2l+1,\end{cases}

(5)

where
\omega_{l}=2\pi l/T
are the frequencies,
l\in\{1,2,...,(N-1)/2\}
(
N
is an odd number), and
T
is the total time interval.

II-C

CVT

Let
\mathcal{\bm{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a 2-D domain to be covered by
n
agents.
Let
\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{\bm{D}},i=1,...,n
be the position of the
i
th agent and
\bm{p}=\{\bm{p}_{i}\}
where
\{.\}
denotes a collection of functions. The coverage problem is concerned with placing the agents in
\mathcal{\bm{D}}
, dividing
\mathcal{\bm{D}}
into regions of dominance of each agent
i
. A Voronoi tessellation is thus formed as

V_{i}\left(\bm{p}\right)=\left\{\bm{q}\in\mathcal{\bm{D}}\mid\left\|\bm{q}-\bm{p}_{i}\right\|\leq\left\|\bm{q}-\bm{p}_{j}\right\|,i\neq j\right\},

(6)

where
\|.\|
represents the
l_{2}
-norm for a vector,
such that agent
i
is in charge of each subregion
V_{i}
.

To measure the performance of the coverage for the multi-agent system (how well a given point
\bm{q}\in\mathcal{\bm{D}}
is covered by agent
i
at position
\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{\bm{D}}
),
one can define the locational cost

\mathcal{H}\left(\bm{p},t\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{V_{i}}\left\|\bm{q}-\bm{p}_{i}\right\|^{2}\phi\left(\bm{q}\right)\textrm{d}\bm{q},

(7)

where
\phi(\bm{q})
denotes the associated density function, which is assumed to be positive and bounded.
It captures the relative importance of a point
\bm{q}\in\mathcal{\bm{D}}
[
2
]
.

From (
7
), the time derivative of
\mathcal{H}
can be expressed by

\dot{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}.

(8)

Note that as the density
\phi
in time-invariant,
\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial t}=0
. In
[
2
]
, it was shown that

\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}=2m_{i}(\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{c}_{i})^{\top},

(9)

where the mass
m_{i}
and the center of mass
\bm{c}_{i}
of the
i
-th Voronoi cell
V_{i}
are defined as

m_{i}=\int_{V_{i}}\phi\left(\bm{q}\right)\textrm{d}\bm{q},\ \bm{c}_{i}=\frac{\int_{V_{i}}\phi\left(\bm{q}\right)\bm{q}\textrm{d}\bm{q}}{m_{i}}
.
Note that
m_{i}>0
because the density function
\phi(\bm{q})
is strictly positive
[
25
]
.

At a given time
t
, an optimal coverage for the domain
\mathcal{\bm{D}}
requires a configuration of agents
\bm{p}
to minimize
\mathcal{H}
.
From (
9
), one can see that a critical point is

\bm{p}_{i}\left(t\right)=\bm{c}_{i}\left(\bm{p},t\right),\>\>i=1,...,n.

(10)

When (
10
) is satisfied, an agents’ network is said to be in a locally optimal coverage configuration
[
25
]
. The corresponding
\bm{p}
defines the CVT.

III
Controller design

In this section, a coverage problem is first defined. Then, a safe adaptive controller based on the FAT and CBF techniques is proposed for a multi-agent system which guarantees the collision avoidance in the presence of system uncertainties.

III-A

Control problem formalization

The following single integrator model is adopted for the agents in the control strategy design

\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}=\overline{\bm{u}}_{i}+\bm{d}_{i}(t),\ i\in\{1,2,...,n\},

(11)

where
n
is the number of agents,
\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the state of an agent,
\overline{\bm{u}}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the input signal, and
\bm{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a time-varying uncertainty which is assumed to be continuous and bounded.

Note that the expected system performance and stability can be severely compromised when the actuators do not operate as desired (actuator fault)
[
29
,
30
]
. In order to improve the performance and reliability of systems, control methods need to be designed to compensate for the effects of actuator fault, which leads to a fault-tolerant control problem. To study this problem, the following actuator fault model is introduced (when the partial loss of actuator effectiveness is considered)
[
29
,
30
]

\overline{\bm{u}}_{i}=\theta_{i}\bm{u}_{i},

(12)

and (
11
) can be rearranged as

\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}=\theta_{i}\bm{u}_{i}+\bm{d}_{i}.

(13)

Assumption 1
.

The actuator fault coefficient
\theta_{i}
satisfies
\theta_{i}\in[\alpha_{i},1]
, where
\alpha_{i}
is a small positive constant.
The motivation of imposing this assumption is to guarantee the controllability of system (
13
). If
\theta_{i}=0
, the system is uncontrollable, and the controller design procedure becomes invalid.

In the conventional design of CVT based coverage control algorithms, agents (robots) are represented by points that stay in their corresponding cells to avoid collisions with each other. However, when the algorithms are implemented in real robots, collisions may still occur unless the radius of the robots is zero. To deal with this safety issue, in this paper, we design a safe adaptive controller for system (
13
) based on the CBF technique. According to Section
II-A
, one defines a safe set
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for agent
i
as

\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},h_{i}(\bm{p}_{i})\geq 0\},

(14)

where
h_{i}
is a Lipschitz continuous function with a relative degree 1. To avoid collisions, one can design
h_{i}
as

h_{i}=\|\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{z}_{i}\|^{2}-(2r_{\textrm{safe}})^{2},

(15)

where
\bm{z}_{i}
denotes the position of the nearest detected neighbor in the sensing range of agent
i
, and the velocity of the neighbor
\dot{\bm{z}}_{i}
is measurable by agent
i
, and
r_{\textrm{safe}}
is the safety radius which the size of agent
i
can fit into.

Assuming that there exists a nominal control input
\hat{\bm{u}}_{i}
for
n
agents to achieve a CVT within
\mathcal{\bm{D}}
(
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{c}_{i}=\bm{0}
) regardless to the safety concerns, the control problem can be stated as follows. Construct the actual input
\bm{u}_{i}
such that the corresponding
\bm{p}_{i}(t)
resulted from system (
13
) will stay inside the safety set
\mathcal{C}_{i}
defined as (
14
), i.e.,
h(\bm{p}_{i}(t))\geq 0
for
\forall t>0
, in the presence of a parametric uncertainty
\theta_{i}
and a time-varying uncertainty
\bm{d}_{i}
.

To the best of our knowledge, the CBF based safe adaptive controller design for systems with both the time-varying uncertainties and the actuator faults, has not been proposed yet in the literature, which constitutes the novelty of this work.

III-B

A safe adaptive coverage controller design

To compensate for the effects of time-varying uncertainty
\bm{d}_{i}
in system (
13
), following the FAT approach (see Section
II-B
), the approximation of (
13
) can be represented as

\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}=\theta_{i}\bm{u}_{i}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\bm{d}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)+\bm{\epsilon}_{i},

(16)

where
\bm{d}_{ij}
denotes an unknown constant vector (weight),
\psi_{j}(t)
is the basis function to be selected, and
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
describes the deviation between the uncertainty
\bm{d}_{i}
and the weighted basis functions.

Assumption 2
.

The unknown parameter
\bm{d}_{ij}
, error
\bm{\epsilon}_{i}
, and velocity
\dot{\bm{z}}_{i}
are bounded such that
\|\bm{d}_{ij}\|\leq\bar{d}_{ij}
,
\|\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\|\leq E
,
\|\dot{\bm{z}}_{i}\|\leq V_{z}
, where
\bar{d}_{ij}
,
E
, and
V_{z}
are positive constants.

Regarding the actuator faults, setting
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}^{*}+\frac{1+\alpha_{i}}{2}
, we can see that
\theta_{i}^{*}\in[\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{2},\frac{1-\alpha_{i}}{2}]
.
By selecting
\bar{\theta}_{i}=\frac{1-\alpha_{i}}{2}
, we have
\theta_{i}^{*}\in[-\bar{\theta}_{i},\bar{\theta}_{i}]
. Then, system (
16
) becomes

\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}=\theta_{i}^{*}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha_{i}}{2}\bm{u}_{i}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\bm{d}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)+\bm{\epsilon}_{i}.

(17)

To compensate for the effects of the unknown parameters
\theta_{i}^{*}
and
\bm{d}_{ij}
to the control system (
17
), corresponding update laws
\hat{\theta}_{i}
and
\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}
need to be designed for approximating these parameters. A projection operator is adopted in the consequent design of update laws, which is defined as
[
31
]

\displaystyle{\rm Proj}(\bm{x},\bm{y},l)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\bm{y}-l(\bm{x})\frac{\nabla l(\bm{x})\nabla l(\bm{x})^{\top}}{\|\nabla l(\bm{x})\|^{2}}\bm{y},&\text{if}\ l(\bm{x})>0\ \!\wedge\ \!\bm{y}^{\top}\nabla l(\bm{x})>0,\\
\bm{y},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here
\bm{x}
,
\bm{y}
are arbitrary vectors and
l(\bm{x})
is convex function defined as

l(\bm{x})=\frac{\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{x}-\bar{x}^{2}}{2\eta\bar{x}+\eta^{2}},

(18)

where
\bar{x}
and
\eta
are constants.

Lemma 1
.

Given
\bm{x}^{*}
as the nominal value for
\bm{x}
which may vary from
\bm{x}
,
if
l(\bm{x}^{*})\leq 0
, one can see that
[
31
]

(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{*})^{\top}({\rm Proj}(\bm{x},\bm{y},l)-\bm{y})\leq 0.

(19)

Lemma 2
.

If
\bm{x}(t)
is governed by

\dot{\bm{x}}={\rm Proj}(\bm{x},\bm{y},l),

(20)

and
l(\bm{x}(0))\leq 1
, one can get

l(\bm{x}(t))\leq 1
for
\forall t\geq 0
, which implies
\|\bm{x}(t)\|\leq\bar{x}+\eta
[
31
]
.

Theorem 1
.

By constructing the update laws
\hat{\theta}_{i}
and
\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}
for the parameter estimation as

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\theta}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Proj}\Bigg(\hat{\theta}_{i},-\frac{1}{2K_{i}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\bm{u}_{i}-\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\theta}_{i},l_{\theta i}\Bigg),

(21)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{d}}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Proj}\Bigg(\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij},-\frac{1}{2Q_{ij}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)\psi_{j}-\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij},l_{di}\Bigg),

(22)

where

\displaystyle l_{\theta i}(\hat{\theta}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\theta}_{i}^{2}-\bar{\theta}_{i}^{2}}{\bar{\theta}_{i}\alpha_{i}+(\alpha_{i}/2)^{2}},\ l_{di}(\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij})=\frac{\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}^{\top}\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}-\bar{d}_{ij}^{2}}{2\nu_{i}\bar{d}_{ij}+\nu_{i}^{2}},

(23)

\alpha_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
are small constants,
\|\hat{\theta}_{i}(0)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and

K_{i}\leq\frac{h_{i}(\bm{p}_{i}(0))}{2(\|\hat{\theta}_{i}(0)\|+\bar{\theta}_{i})^{2}},\ Q_{ij}\leq\frac{h_{i}(\bm{p}_{i}(0))}{2N(\|\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}(0)\|+\bar{d}_{ij})^{2}},

(24)

any Lipschitz continuous controller
\bm{u}_{i}(\bm{p}_{i})\in K_{bf}(\bm{p}_{i},\hat{\theta}_{i},\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij})
where

\displaystyle K_{bf}(\bm{p}_{i},\hat{\theta}_{i},\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij})

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigg\{\bm{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\!\!\top}\!\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}\psi_{j}+(\hat{\theta}_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha_{i}}{2})\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\!\!\top}\!\bm{u}_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\zeta_{i}+\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg(h_{i}-K_{i}\bar{\theta}_{i}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}\bar{d}_{ij}^{2}\bigg)\geq 0\bigg\},

(25)

with
\zeta_{i}=\Big\|\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\Big\|E+\Big\|\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{z}_{i}}\Big\|V_{z}
,
will guarantee the safety of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
in regard to system (
16
).

Proof.

Define
\bar{h}_{i}
as

\bar{h}_{i}=h_{i}-K_{i}\tilde{\theta}_{i}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}\tilde{\bm{d}}_{ij}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{d}}_{ij},

(26)

where
\tilde{\theta}_{i}=\theta_{i}^{*}-\hat{\theta}_{i}
and
\tilde{\bm{d}}_{ij}=\bm{d}_{ij}-\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}
. To prove Theorem
1
, one needs to show that
\bar{h}_{i}(t)\geq 0
for
\forall t
, such that
h_{i}(t)\geq 0
for
\forall t
as required by (

). This property holds if

can be expressed in form of (or larger than)

where

A reconstruction of

It can be seen that

is calculated as

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\dot{\bm{p}}_{i}+\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{z}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\dot{\bm{z}}_{i}-2K\tilde{\theta}_{i}\dot{\tilde{\theta}}_{i}-2\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{ij}\tilde{\bm{d}}_{ij}^{\top}\dot{\tilde{\bm{d}}}_{ij},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\bigg(\theta_{i}^{*}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{1+\alpha_{i}}{2}\bm{u}_{i}+\sum^{N}_{j=1}\bm{d}_{ij}\psi_{j}(t)\bigg)-\zeta_{i}

As update laws
\dot{\hat{\theta}}_{i}

) and (

from Lemma

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{i}\dot{\hat{\theta}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta_{i}^{*}-\hat{\theta}_{i}){\rm Proj}\Bigg(\hat{\theta}_{i},-\frac{1}{2K_{i}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\bm{u}_{i}-\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\theta}_{i},l_{\theta i}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-(\theta_{i}^{*}-\hat{\theta}_{i})\Bigg(\frac{1}{2K_{i}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)^{\top}\bm{u}_{i}+\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\theta}_{i}\Bigg),

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{d}}_{ij}^{\top}\dot{\hat{\bm{d}}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bm{d}_{ij}-\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij})^{\top}

(29)

\displaystyle{\rm Proj}\Bigg(\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij},-\frac{1}{2Q_{ij}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)\psi_{j}-\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij},l_{di}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-(\bm{d}_{ij}-\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij})^{\top}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2Q_{ij}}\bigg(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\bm{p}_{i}}\bigg)\psi_{j}+\frac{\mu}{2}\hat{\bm{d}}_{ij}\Bigg).

Substituting (

) and (

) into (

) yields

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{h}}_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

V
Conclusions

A safe adaptive coverage problem has been studied in the presence of actuator faults and time-varying uncertainties. An optimal coverage configuration for a multi-agent system has been generated though CVT. The agents have been adaptively driven to the generated configuration by the proposed FAT based controller, and collision avoidance between agents has been guaranteed by a CBF based design. The proposed controller has been verified under simulations and we also plan to test its validity by experiments in the future work.

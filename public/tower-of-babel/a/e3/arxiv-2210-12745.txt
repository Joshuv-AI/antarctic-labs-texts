Generalized Balancing and Balancing-Lucas numbers
Hasan Al-Zoubi, Ala’a Al-Kateeb

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a generalization of Balancing and Balancing-Lucas numbers. We describe some of their properties also we give the related matrix representation and divisibility properties.



Keywords:
Balancing numbers, balancing-Lucas numbers, generalized Balancing numbers, generalized balancing-Lucas numbers, generating functions, Binet formula.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
11B39, 11C20, 15B36.

1
Introduction

In mathematics, the Lucas sequences
U_{n}(P,Q)
and
V_{n}(P,Q)
are certain constant-recursive integer sequences that satisfy the recurrence relation

x_{n}=P\cdot x_{n-1}-Q\cdot x_{n-2}

where
P
and
Q
are fixed integers. Any sequence satisfying this recurrence relation can be represented as a linear combination of the Lucas sequences
U_{n}(P,Q)
and
V_{n}(P,Q)
.
Famous examples of Lucas sequences include the Fibonacci numbers, Mersenne, Pell, Lucas, Jacobsthal and, balancing numbers. Lucas sequences are named after the French mathematician E. Lucas. The sequences mentioned above satisfy many common properties and identities for
example Binet formulas, Catalan identities and matrix representation. Recently,
these sequences were investigated, generalized or extended for example see
[
1
,
4
,
3
,
13
]
.

When
P=6
and
Q=1
, the sequence
x_{n}
is called the sequence of Balancing numbers and denoted by
B_{n}
, that is

B_{n}=6B_{n-1}-B_{n-2},B_{0}=0,B_{1}=1

Balancing numbers have several nice properties for example
8B_{n}^{2}+1
is a perfect square for any integer
n
. Also, it can be easily proved that
B_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}
, where
\alpha=3+2\sqrt{2}
and
\beta=3-2\sqrt{2}
, this formula is called the Binet formula of the sequence. One may refer to
[
5
]
, where the author proved interesting properties of this sequence of numbers. Many of their arithmetic properties were studied in
[
7
,
2
,
11
]
.

There are some generalizations of the Balancing and Balancing-Lucas numbers.
In 2014, Tecan and others defined the
k
-balancing numbers such that, for any positive number
k
, the
k
- balancing number defined by
B_{k,n+1}=6kB_{k,n}-B_{k,n-1}
,
where
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
, with the initials
B_{k,0}=0
and
B_{k,1}=1,
see
[
12
,
9
]
.
The Gaussian balancing sequence was defined in 2018 by Tasci in
[
6
]
to be the sequence of complex numbers
GB_{n}

defined by the initial values
GB_{0}=i
and
GB_{1}=1
, and the recurrence relation

GB_{n+1}=6GB_{n}-GB_{n-1}
, for all
n\geq 1
.
The first few values of are
i
,
1
,
6-i
,
35-i
,…

In this study we introduce generalizations of Balancing and Balancing-Lucas numbers and examine the properties of the new sequences. The paper is structured as follows: in section 1 we introduce the new sequences and study their generating functions and Binet formulas. In section 2 we provide some basic identities of the generalized Balancing and balancing-Lucas numbers. Also, we find a matrix representation for both sequences and investigate some divisibility properties.

Definition, Generating Functions and Binet Formula

In this section we define the generalized Balancing and balancing- Lucas numbers, and we derive their generating functions and Binet formulas.

Definition 1

(Generalized Balancing Numbers)
.

For
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
we define

B_{k,n}=3kB_{k,n-1}+(1-k)B_{k,n-2}

where
B_{k,0}=0
and
B_{k,1}=1.

Clearly,

\displaystyle B_{1,n}

\displaystyle=3B_{1,n-1}

\displaystyle=3\cdot 3\cdot B_{1,n-2}

\displaystyle=3^{n}

which leads to the following remark.

Remark 1
.

B_{1,n}=3^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.

Remark 2
.

B_{2,n}
is the original sequence of Balancing numbers. That is,
B_{2,n}=6B_{2,n-1}-B_{2,n-2}
.

Definition 2

(Generalized Balancing-Lucas Numbers)
.

For
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
we define

C_{k,n}=3kC_{k,n-1}+(1-k)C_{k,n-2}

where
C_{k,0}=1
and
C_{k,1}=3.

Remark 3
.

C_{1,n}=3^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.

Remark 4
.

C_{2,n}
is the original sequence of Balancing-Lucas numbers. That is,
C_{2,n}=6C_{2,n-1}-C_{2,n-2}
.

Theorem 1

(Binet Formulas)
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
. Then we have

1.

B_{k,n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}
.

2.

C_{k,n}=\frac{\beta-3}{\beta-\alpha}\alpha^{n}+\frac{3-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}\beta^{n}
.

Where
\alpha=\frac{3k+\sqrt{9k^{2}-4k+4}}{2},\beta=\frac{3k-\sqrt{9k^{2}-4k+4}}{2}
.

Proof.

We prove both identities by mathematical induction. For
B_{k,n}
:

•

if
m=2
;
\frac{\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}}{\alpha-\beta}=\alpha+\beta=\frac{3k+\sqrt{9{k}^{2}-4k+4}}{2}+\frac{3k-\sqrt{9{k}^{2}-4k+4}}{2}=\frac{6k}{2}=3k=B_{k,2}
,

•

Assume that
B_{k,n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}
,

•

Consider

\displaystyle B_{k,n+1}

\displaystyle=3kB_{k,n}+(1-n)B_{k,n-1}

\displaystyle=3k\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}+(1-k)\frac{\alpha^{n-1}-\beta^{n-1}}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{3k\alpha^{n}-3k\beta^{n}+\alpha^{n-1}\beta^{n-1}-k\alpha^{n-1}+k\beta^{n-1}}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n-1}(3k\alpha+1-k)-\beta^{n-1}(3k\beta+1-k)}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n-1}\alpha^{2}-\beta^{n-1}\beta^{2}}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}-\beta^{n+1}}{\alpha-\beta}.

Proving the formula for
C_{k,n}
also can be done similarly by induction.
∎

Remark 5
.

For
n\geq 2
, we have
{\alpha\beta}=k-1
.

Next we extend Definition
1
to negative indices as follows:

B_{k,-n}=\frac{\alpha^{-n}-\beta^{-n}}{\alpha-\beta}=(\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}\beta^{n}})(-B_{k,n})

but,

\frac{1}{\alpha^{n}\beta^{n}}=(\frac{4}{9k^{2}-9k^{2}+4k-4})^{n}=(\frac{1}{k-1})^{n}

thus,
B_{k,-n}=-(k-1)^{-n}B_{k,n}

Definition 3
.

B_{k,-n}=-(1-k)^{-n}B_{k,n}
.

Theorem 2

(Generating Functions)
.

The generating functions of the sequences
B_{k,n}

and
C_{k,n}
respectively are

1.

B(x)=\frac{x}{1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2}}

2.

C(x)=\frac{1+3x(1+k)}{1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2}}

Proof.

Let
B(x)
and
C(x)
represents the generating functions of
B_{k,n}
and
C_{k,n}
respectively.
Note,

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=B_{k,0}+B_{k,1}x+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(3kB_{k,n-1}+(1-k)B_{k,n-2})x^{n}

\displaystyle=x+3kx\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}B_{k,n-1}x^{n-1}+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}B_{k,n-2}x^{n-2}

\displaystyle=x+3kx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{k,n}x^{n}+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=x+3kxB(x)+(1-k)x^{2}B(x)

thus
B(x)(1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2})=x\Rightarrow B(x)=\frac{x}{(1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2})}
.
Next for
C_{k,n},
Note

\displaystyle C(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=C_{k,0}+C_{k,1}x+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(3kC_{k,n-1}+(1-k)C_{k,n-2})x^{n}

\displaystyle=1+3x+3kx\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}C_{k,n-1}x^{n-1}+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}C_{k,n-2}x^{n-2}

\displaystyle=1+3x+3kx\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=1+3x+3kx(-1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n})+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=1+3x-3kx+3kx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}+(1-k)x^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{k,n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=1+3x-3kx+3kxC(x)+(1-k)x^{2}C(x)=C(x)

then,

C(x)=\frac{1+3x-3kx}{1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2}}

=\frac{1+3x(1-k)}{1-3kx+(k-1)x^{2}}

∎

3.1
Basic Identities

In this subsection we provide several basic identities like Catalan identity, d’ Ocagnes identity, and Vajda’s identity. Also we give the sum of terms of the first
n
terms of the sequences
B_{k,m}
and
C_{k,m}
.

Theorem 3

(Catalan’s Identity 1)
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Then

B_{k,n+r}B_{k,n-r}-B_{k,n}^{2}=-(k-1)^{n-r}(B_{k,r})^{2}

Proof.

By the Binet formula we have:

\displaystyle B_{k,n+r}B_{k,n-r}-B_{k,n}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{n+r}-\beta^{n+r}}{\alpha-\beta}\frac{\alpha^{n-r}-\beta^{n-r}}{\alpha-\beta}-\left(\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2n}-\alpha^{n+r}\beta^{n-r}-\alpha^{n-r}\beta^{n+r}+\beta^{2n}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2n}-2\alpha^{n}\beta^{n}+\beta^{2n}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha^{n}\beta^{n}-\alpha^{n+r}\beta^{n-r}-\alpha^{n-r}\beta^{n+r}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\beta)^{n-r}\frac{2\alpha^{r}\beta^{r}-\alpha^{2r}-\beta^{2r}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-(\alpha\beta)^{n-r}\frac{\alpha^{2r}-2\alpha^{r}\beta^{r}+\beta^{2r}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-(\alpha\beta)^{n-r}(B_{k,r})^{2}

∎

Theorem 4

(Catalan’s Identity 2)
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Then

C_{k,n+r}C_{k,n-r}-C_{k,n}^{2}=8(k-1)^{n-r+1}B^{2}_{k,r}

Proof.

Let
A=\frac{\beta-3}{\beta-\alpha}
and
B=\frac{3-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle C_{k,n+r}C_{k,n-r}-C_{k,n}^{2}

\displaystyle=(A\alpha^{n+r}+B\beta^{n+r})(A\alpha^{n-r}+B\beta^{n-r})

\displaystyle\cdot(A\alpha^{n}+B\beta^{n})^{2}

\displaystyle=AB(\alpha^{n+r}\beta^{n-r}+\alpha^{n-r}\beta^{n+2}-2\alpha^{n}\beta^{n})

\displaystyle=AB(\alpha\beta)^{n-r}(\alpha^{2r}+\beta^{2r}-2\alpha^{r}\beta^{r})

\displaystyle=(\beta-3)(3-\alpha)(\alpha\beta)^{n-r}\frac{(\alpha^{r}-\beta^{r})^{2}}{\alpha-\beta^{2}}

\displaystyle=8(k-1)^{n-r+1}B_{k,n}^{2}

\displaystyle(\beta-3)(3-\alpha)=8(k-1)

∎

Theorem 5

( d’ Ocagne’s identity 1)
.

For any two integers
m
and
n
with
m\geq n

B_{k,m}B_{k,n+1}-B_{k,n}B_{k,m+1}=(k-1)^{n}B_{k,m-n}

Proof.

Note

\displaystyle B_{k,m}B_{k,n+1}-B_{k,n}B_{k,m+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha^{m}-\beta^{m})(\alpha^{n+1}-\beta^{n+1})-(\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})(\alpha^{m+1}-\beta^{m+1})}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\alpha^{m}\beta^{n+1}-\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{m}+\alpha^{n}\beta^{m+1}+\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{n}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha\beta)^{n}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}(-\beta\alpha^{m-n}-\alpha\beta^{m-n}+\beta^{m-n+1}+\alpha^{m-n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha\beta)^{n}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}((\alpha-\beta)\alpha^{m-n}-(\alpha-\beta)\beta^{m-n})

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{n}B_{k,m-n}

∎

Theorem 6

(d’ Ocagne’s identity
2
)
.

For any two integers
m
and
n
with
m\geq n
we have

C_{k,m}C_{k,n+1}-C_{k,n}C_{k,m+1}=-8(k-1)^{n+1}B_{k,m-n}

Proof.

Let
A=\frac{\beta-3}{\beta-\alpha}
and
B=\frac{3-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle C_{k,m}C_{k,n+1}-C_{k,n}C_{k,m+1}

\displaystyle=(A\alpha^{m}+B\beta^{m})(A\alpha^{n+1}+B\beta^{n+1})

\displaystyle-(A\alpha^{n}+B\beta^{n})(A\alpha^{m+1}+B\beta^{m+1})

\displaystyle=AB(\alpha^{m}\beta^{n+1}+\alpha^{n+1}\beta^{m}-\alpha^{n}\beta^{m+1}-\alpha^{m+1}\beta^{n})

\displaystyle=AB(\alpha\beta)^{n}(\alpha^{m-n}\beta+\alpha\beta^{m-n}-\beta^{m-n+1}-\alpha^{m-n+1})

\displaystyle=AB(\alpha\beta)^{n}(\alpha^{m-n}(\beta-\alpha)+\beta^{m-n}(\alpha-\beta))

\displaystyle=AB(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha\beta)^{n}(\alpha^{m-n}-\beta^{m-n})

\displaystyle=-(\beta-3)(3-\alpha)(\alpha\beta)^{n}\frac{\alpha^{m-n}-\beta^{m-n}}{\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle=-8(k-1)^{n+1}B_{k,m-n}

∎

Theorem 7

(Vajda’s identity)
.

Let
n,m,i,j,k,\ell
be positive integers with
m>n+\ell
. Then

1.

(Formulation 1)
B_{k,n+i}B_{k,n+j}-B_{k,n}B_{k,n+i+j}=(k-1)^{n}B_{k,i}B_{k,j}

2.

(Formulation 2)
B_{k,n+\ell}B_{k,m-\ell}-B_{k,n}B_{k,m}=(k-1)^{n}B_{k,m-n-\ell}B_{k,\ell}

Proof.

We prove both identities using the Binet formula:

1.

\displaystyle B_{k,n+i}B_{k,n+j}-B_{k,n}B_{k,n+i+j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha^{n+i}-\beta^{n+i})(\alpha^{n+j}-\beta^{n+j})}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})(\alpha^{n+i+j}-\beta^{n+i+j})}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n}\beta^{n+i+j}-\alpha^{n+i}\beta^{n+j}-\alpha^{n+j}\beta^{n+i}+\beta^{n}\alpha^{n+i+j}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\beta)^{n}\frac{\beta^{i+j}-\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}-\alpha^{j}\beta^{i}+\alpha^{i+j}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{n}B_{k,i}B_{k,j}

2.

\displaystyle B_{k,n+\ell}B_{k,m-\ell}-B_{k,n}B_{k,m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}+\alpha^{m}\beta^{n}-\alpha^{n+\ell}\beta^{m-\ell}-\alpha^{m-\ell}\beta^{n+\ell}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{n}\beta^{m}+\alpha^{m}\beta^{n}-\alpha^{n+\ell}\beta^{m-\ell}-\alpha^{m-\ell}\beta^{n+\ell}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\beta)^{n}\frac{\beta^{m-n}+\alpha^{m-n}-\alpha^{\ell}\beta^{m-n-\ell}-\alpha^{m-n-\ell}\beta^{\ell}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-k)^{n}B_{k,m-n-\ell}B_{k,\ell}.

∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. The sums of first n-terms of generalized balancing and balancing-Lucas numbers are given by

1.

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{k,n}=\frac{-(2k+1)B_{k,n}+(k-1)B_{k,n-1}+1}{-2k}

2.

\sum_{i=2}^{n}C_{k,n}=\frac{-(2k+1)C_{k,n}+(k-1)C_{k,n-1}+4(1-k)}{-2k}

Proof.

For the first sum, note

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}B_{k,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}(3kB_{k,i-1}+(1-k)B_{k,n-2})

\displaystyle=3k\sum_{i=2}^{n}B_{k,i-1}+(1-k)\sum_{i=2}^{n}B_{k,i-2}

\displaystyle=3k\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}B_{k,i}+(1-k)\sum_{i=2}^{n-2}B_{k,i}

\displaystyle=3k(B_{k,1}-B_{k,n}+S)+(1-k)(B_{k,0}+B_{k,1}-B_{k,n}-B_{k,n-1}+S)

\displaystyle-2kS

\displaystyle=3k(1-B_{k,n})+(1-k)(1-B_{k,n}-B_{k,n-1})

Thus,
\sum_{i=2}^{n}B_{k,n}=\frac{(2k+1)-(2k+1)B_{k,n}+(k-1)B_{k,n-1}}{-2k}
. Also,

\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{k,n}=1+\frac{(2k+1)-(2k+1)B_{k,n}+(k-1)B_{k,n-1}}{-2k}=\frac{-(2k+1)B_{k,n}+(k-1)B_{k,n-1}+1}{-2k}

Next, for the first sum, note

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}C_{k,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}(3kC_{k,i-1}+(1-k)C_{k,n-2})

\displaystyle=3k\sum_{i=2}^{n}C_{k,i-1}+(1-k)\sum_{i=2}^{n}C_{k,i-2}

\displaystyle=3k\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}C_{k,i}+(1-k)\sum_{i=2}^{n-2}C_{k,i}

\displaystyle=3k(C_{k,1}-C_{k,n}+S)+(1-k)(C_{k,0}+C_{k,1}-C_{k,n}-C_{k,n-1}+S)

\displaystyle-2kS

\displaystyle=3k(3-C_{k,n})+(1-k)(1+3-C_{k,n}-C_{k,n-1})

Thus,
\sum_{i=2}^{n}C_{k,n}=\frac{(4+5k)-(2k+1)C_{k,n}+(k-1)C_{k,n-1}}{-2k}
. Also,

\sum_{i=0}^{n}C_{k,n}=1+3+\frac{(5k+4)-(2k+1)C_{k,n}+(k-1)C_{k,n-1}}{-2k}=\frac{-(2k+1)C_{k,n}+(k-1)C_{k,n-1}+4(1-k)}{-2k}

∎

3.2
Matrix Representation

As we know most integer sequences have matrix representation, for example the balancing numbers have the matrix
Q=\begin{pmatrix}6&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
Q^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{n+1}&-B_{n}\\
B_{n}&-B_{n-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}
. Also, the matrix
S=\begin{pmatrix}3&8\\
1&3\\
\end{pmatrix}
for which
S^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}C_{n}&8B_{n}\\
B_{n}&C_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}
. the last matrices were used to prove many properties for the balancing and balancing-Lucas numbers, see
[
8
]
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
A=\begin{bmatrix}3k&1-k\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}
. Then for
n\geq 2
we have

A^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{k,n+1}&(1-k)B_{k,n}\\
B_{k,n}&(1-k)B_{k,n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}

Proof.

We will prove this property by mathematical induction.

For
m=2
,

\displaystyle A^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}3k&1-k\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3k&1-k\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}9{k}^{2}&3k(1-k)\\
3k&1-k\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{k,3}&(1-k)B_{k,2}\\
B_{k,2}&(1-k)B_{k,1}\\
\end{bmatrix}

Now, assume that the property is true
m=n
,
now consider
m=n+1
,

\displaystyle A^{n+1}

\displaystyle=A^{n}A

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{k,n+1}&(1-k)B_{k,n}\\
B_{k,n}&(1-k)B_{k,n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3k&1-k\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}3kB_{k,n+1}+(1-k)B_{k,n}&(1-k)B_{k,n+1}\\
3kB_{k,n}+(1-k)B_{k,n-1}&(1-k)B_{k,n}\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{k,n+2}&(1-k)B_{k,n+1}\\
B_{k,n+1}&(1-k)B_{k,n}\end{bmatrix}

as desired.
∎

Lemma 1
.

For all integers
n\geq 2
, we have

\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}=B_{k,n+1}-(k-1)B_{k,n-1}

Proof.

By Bient formula :

B_{k,n+1}=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}-\beta^{n+1}}{\alpha-\beta}=\alpha^{n}+\alpha^{n-1}\beta+\alpha^{n-2}\beta^{2}+.......+\alpha\beta^{n-1}+\beta^{n}

Also,

\displaystyle(k-1)B_{k,n-1}

\displaystyle=(k-1)(\frac{\alpha^{n-1}-\beta^{n-1}}{\alpha-\beta})

\displaystyle=(k-1)(\alpha^{n-2}+\alpha^{n-3}\beta+......+\alpha\beta^{n-3}+\beta^{n-2})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n-1}\beta+\alpha^{n-2}\beta^{2}+......+\alpha^{2}\beta^{n-2}+\alpha\beta^{n-1}

Then we have

\displaystyle B_{k,n+1}-(k-1)B_{k,n-1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{n}+\alpha^{n-1}\beta+\cdots+\beta^{n})-(\alpha^{n-1}\beta+\alpha^{n-2}\beta^{2}+\cdots+\alpha\beta^{n-1})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}.

Theorem 14
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
be two integers and
k\geq 1
be an integer such that
k\not\equiv 1\mod 3
. Then
\gcd(B_{k,m},B_{k,n})=B_{k,\gcd(m,n)}
.

Proof.

We can prove this theorem by mathematical induction. Without loss of generality we may assume that
m\leq n.
The result is trivial when
n=1,
so assume that
\gcd(B_{k,m},B_{k,j})=B_{k,\gcd(m,j)}
for all
j<n.
Next use the division algorithm to write
n=mq+r,
where
0\leq r<m.
Thus,
B_{k,n}=B_{k,mq+r}=B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r}+(1-k)B_{k,mq}B_{k,r-1}
, by Lemma
2
. Also, by Lemma
3
we have
B_{k,m}|B_{k,mq}
, so we have

B_{k,n}=B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r}+(1-k)xB_{k,m}B_{k,r-1}

for some integer
x
. Note,

\displaystyle\gcd(B_{k,n},B_{k,m})

\displaystyle=\gcd(B_{k,mq+r},B_{k,m})

\displaystyle=\gcd(B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r}+(1-k)xB_{k,m}B_{k,r-1},B_{k,m})

\displaystyle=\gcd(B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r},B_{k,m})

Suppose that
d=\gcd(B_{k,n},B_{k,m})=\gcd(B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r},B_{k,m}).
Then
d|B_{k,m}
and
d|B_{k,mq+1}B_{k,r}
, by Theorem
11
we have

which gives us that

Assume that

it is clear that
d^{\prime}

Now,

\displaystyle\gcd(B_{k,n},B_{k,m})

\displaystyle=\gcd(B_{k,m},B_{k,r})

\displaystyle=B_{k,\gcd(m,r)}

\displaystyle=B_{k,\gcd(m,mq+r)}

\displaystyle=B_{k,\gcd(m,n)},

as desired.

On toric foliations
Chih-Wei Chang, Yen-An Chen

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide toric descriptions for various foliation singularities on toric varieties, especially for non-dicritical singularities and F-dlt singularities. We then show that the toric foliated minimal model program works by demonstrating that non-dicritical singularities and F-dlt singularities are preserved.

It is natural to ask for the applicability of the foliated minimal model program (FMMP) to toric foliations.
As
{\mathbb{Q}}

Therefore, the main goal for the FMMP for toric foliations is to show that the non-dicritical singularities (see Definition
4.6

In
[
Spi20
]
, C. Spicer showed that the FMMP works for toric foliations of corank one with only canonical and non-dicritical singularities.

In this paper, we provide a comprehensive affirmative answer. We first characterize non-dicritical toric foliations in terms of combinatorial data. If the toric variety

and is denoted by

(

]
and Proposition

For any sheaves

2.1.
Basics on foliations

In this subsection, most of the definitions follow from
[
CS21
]
and
[
Dru21
]
.
Let
X
be a normal variety. A
foliation
is a coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
of the tangent sheaf
{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
such that

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated, that is
{\mathcal{T}}_{X}/{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion-free, and

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket.

Let
r=\operatorname{rank}({\mathcal{F}})
be the
rank
of the foliation and
c=\dim X-r
be the
corank
of the foliation.
The
canonical divisor

K_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Weil divisor on
X
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})\cong\det{\mathcal{F}}
.

We define the
normal sheaf
of
{\mathcal{F}}
as
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}:=({\mathcal{T}}_{X}/{\mathcal{F}})^{[1]}
.
By taking the
r
-th wedge product of
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}\to\Omega_{X}^{[1]}
, we obtain a twisted form
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{r}\boxtimes\operatorname{det}{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}})
.
Here
\omega
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The zero locus of
\omega
has codimension at least two.

(2)

\omega
is locally decomposable, meaning that locally
\omega=\bigwedge_{i}\omega_{i}
where
\omega_{i}
are
1
-forms.

(3)

\omega
is integrable, that is,
\textnormal{d}\omega_{i}\wedge\omega=0
for all
i
.

Conversely, let
D
be a Weil divisor and
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{r}\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))
be a twisted form
whose zero locus has codimension at least two in
X
. If
\omega
is locally decomposable and integrable, then the kernel of

{\mathcal{T}}_{X}\to\Omega_{X}^{r-1}\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
given by the contraction via
\omega
is a foliation.

Let
\pi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map between normal varieties and
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on
X
.
We denote by
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
the
pullback foliation
on
Y
(see, for example,
[
Dru21
, Section 3.2]
).
If
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is birational, then
f_{*}\mathcal{F}
represents the transformed foliation on
X^{\prime}
induced by
f^{-1}
.

Let
X^{\circ}
be the open subset of
X
such that
{\mathcal{F}}|_{X^{\circ}}
is a subbundle of
T_{X^{\circ}}
.
A
leaf

L
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
L\subseteq X^{\circ}
such that
T_{L}={\mathcal{F}}|_{L}
.

A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
algebraically integrable
if its leaves are algebraic.
Equivalently, an algebraically integrable foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
is induced from a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
for some normal variety
Y
(see, for example,
[
Dru21
, Sections 3.2 and 3.6]
).

Definition 2.1

(Singular locus)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
.
We obtain a morphism
\phi\colon\Omega_{X}^{[r]}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{\mathcal{F}})
by taking the double dual of the
r
-th wedge product of
\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
, which is induced by the inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.
We define the
singular locus
of
{\mathcal{F}}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
, as the co-support of the image of
\phi^{\prime}\colon\Omega_{X}^{[r]}\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

Definition 2.2

(Invariance)
.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
.
We say that a subvariety
S\subseteq X
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-
invariant
if for any subset
U\subseteq X
and any section
\partial\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{F}})
, we have

\partial({\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U})\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U}

where
{\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U}
is the ideal sheaf of
S\cap U
.

(2)

For any prime divisor
D\subseteq Y\xrightarrow{\pi}X
over
X
where
\pi
is a birational morphism, we define
\iota(D)=0
if
D
is
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant and
\iota(D)=1
if
D
is non-
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}
-invariant. One can show that
\iota(D)
is independent of the choice of the birational morphism
\pi
that extracts
D
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a normal variety
X
.
Then
\operatorname{Sing}(X)
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant.

Proof.

By
[
Sei67
, Theorem 5]
,
\operatorname{Sing}(X)
is invariant under any derivation.
In particular, it is
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Dru21
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth variety
X
.
Then
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant.

Definition 2.5

(Tangency)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of any rank.
Given a (possibly analytic) subvariety
Z\subseteq X
not contained in
\operatorname{Sing}(X)\cup\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
, we say
Z
is
tangent
to
{\mathcal{F}}
if, over
X\setminus\big(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\cup\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})\cup\operatorname{Sing}(Z)\big)
, the inclusion
{\mathcal{T}}_{Z}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
factors through
{\mathcal{F}}
.

2.2.
Basics on toric varieties

In this paper, every toric variety is assumed to be normal. Our notations closely follow
[
CLS11
]
.

Let
N\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a lattice of rank
n
and
M:=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
be its dual lattice. We write
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
N_{\mathbb{C}}:=N\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
A
fan

\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection of rational, strongly convex, polyhedral cones
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, such that each face
\tau
of a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
belongs to
\Sigma
and the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of each.
For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, denote the set of all
k
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
by
\Sigma(k)
, and denote the set of all
k
-dimensional faces of
\sigma\in\Sigma
by
\sigma(k)
.
We write
\tau\preceq\sigma
when
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
.

For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the affine toric variety associated with
\sigma
is
U_{\sigma,\,N}=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[\chi^{m}\mid m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
where
\sigma^{\vee}
is the dual cone of
\sigma
.
A cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is said to be
smooth with respect to
N
if the primitive generators of the rays in
\sigma(1)
form part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis for
N
(or equivalently,
U_{\sigma,\,N}
is smooth).
If
\tau\preceq\sigma
are two cones in
\Sigma
, there is an open immersion
U_{\tau,N}\hookrightarrow U_{\sigma,N}
.
The toric variety
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
of the fan
\Sigma
is constructed by gluing all
U_{\sigma,\,N}
together via
\Sigma
.
The dense torus
U_{\{0\},\,N}=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[M]\subseteq X_{\Sigma,N}
is denoted by
T_{N}
.
The action of
T_{N}
on itself can be extended to an action on
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
.
We will omit
N
in the subscript when
N
is clear.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)
denotes the relative interior of
\sigma
,
O_{\sigma}
denotes the
T
-orbit of the distinguished point
x_{\sigma}
, and
V_{\sigma}
denotes the closure of
O_{\sigma}
in
X_{\Sigma}
(see
[
CLS11
, Chapter 3]
for further details).
If
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
is a ray, then
V_{\rho}
is a divisor and will also be denoted by
D_{\rho}
.

3.
Toric foliations

Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be the toric variety defined by a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
A subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is called
T
-
invariant
or
torus invariant
if for any
t\in T
we have
t^{*}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
as subsheaves under the natural isomorphism
t^{*}{\mathcal{T}}_{X}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.
A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is called a
toric foliation
if
{\mathcal{F}}
is
T
-invariant.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
be the toric variety defined by
\Sigma
.
Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of toric foliations on
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
and the set of complex vector subspaces
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a toric foliation, then
{\mathcal{F}}|_{T}
is a
T
-invariant vector sub-bundle of the tangent bundle
{\mathcal{T}}_{T}
, which gives rise to a complex vector subspace
W:=({\mathcal{F}}|_{T})_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{T,\,1}=N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
By
[
HL21
, Lemma 1.8]
, any two foliations that agree on a Zariski open dense subset must be the same.
Therefore,
{\mathcal{F}}
is uniquely determined by
W
.

Conversely, given any complex vector subspace
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
, we can extend it via the
T
-action to a
T
-invariant subbundle
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{T}
.
Since the Lie bracket on
{\mathcal{T}}_{T}
is trivial,
{\mathcal{E}}
becomes a foliation.
We can then uniquely extend
{\mathcal{E}}
to a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
, and it is easy to see that
\mathcal{F}
is
T
-invariant.
∎

We will use
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,\Sigma,\,N}
to denote the toric foliation on
X_{\Sigma,\,N}
corresponding to the complex vector subspace
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
If we have another fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
in the same
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, the transformed foliation on
Y=X_{\Sigma^{\prime},\,N}
is nothing but
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,\Sigma^{\prime},\,N}
. Hence we can unambiguously write
\mathcal{F}_{W}
to denote the transformed foliation on any birational model obtained by modifying the defining fan.

3.2.
Singular locus of a toric foliation

In this subsection, we present a combinatorial criterion to determine whether the orbit closure is contained in the singular locus of a toric foliation.
To establish this criterion, we rely on the following lemma, which allows us to reduce the problem to the smooth case.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
N
be a lattice of rank
n
,
\sigma
be a simplicial strongly convex rational polyhedral cone of dimension
n
, and
W
be a complex vector subspace of
N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
There is a sublattice
N^{\prime}
of
N
such that
\sigma
is smooth with respect to
N^{\prime}
, which induces a finite covering
\pi\colon U_{\sigma,\,N^{\prime}}\to U_{\sigma,\,N}
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N^{\prime}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N}
) be the toric foliation on
U_{\sigma,\,N^{\prime}}
(resp.
U_{\sigma,\,N^{\prime}}
) given by
W
.
Then we have
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N^{\prime}})=\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N}))
.

Proof.

Let
N^{\prime}
be the sublattice of
N
generated by all
v_{\rho}
for
\rho\in\sigma(1)
.
So
\sigma
is a smooth cone with respect to
N^{\prime}
.
Moreover, it introduces a finite covering
\pi\colon U_{\sigma,\,N^{\prime}}\to U_{\sigma,\,N}
.

As
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N})
is torus invariant, there are some cones
\tau_{i}\preceq\sigma
such that
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{\tau_{i},\,N}
, where each
V_{\tau_{i},\,N}
is an irreducible component of
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N})
.
Now we consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{\tau\mid\tau_{i}\preceq\tau\preceq\sigma\textnormal{ for some }i\},

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=\{\tau\mid\tau\preceq\sigma\}\setminus\mathcal{C},\textnormal{ and}

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}\cup\{\tau_{i}\}.

Note that
\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}
are indeed fans.
Actually, we have
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{0},\,N}=U_{\sigma}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{F}_{W,\,N})
and
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N}=(U_{\sigma}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{F}_{W,\,N}))\cup O_{\tau_{i}}
.
One can check that
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N}
is an open subscheme of
U_{\sigma,\,N}
for
0\leq i\leq\ell
, and thus the base change
\pi^{\prime}\colon X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N^{\prime}}\to X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N}
is finite and surjective.

Since
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{0},\,N}
has no foliation singularities, by
[
Dru21
, Proposition 5.13]
,
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{0},\,N^{\prime}}
has no foliation singularities, from which we have
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N^{\prime}})\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{\tau_{i},\,N^{\prime}}
.
If the containment is strict, then there is an
i\neq 0
such that
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N^{\prime}}
has no foliation singularities.
Thus
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}_{i},\,N}
has no foliation singularities again by
[
Dru21
, Proposition 5.13]
, which contradicts
V_{\tau_{i},\,N}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N})
.
We conclude that
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N^{\prime}})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{\tau_{i},\,N^{\prime}}
and therefore
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N^{\prime}})=\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,N}))
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation on a

-factorial toric variety

defined by a fan

in

.

By Lemma

, we can assume that

is smooth.
As this is a local problem,

,

,

, and
\tau\preceq\sigma

V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{F}_{W})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow{}

Applying Theorem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
be two foliations on a normal variety

It is clear that

It remains to show that

.

It suffices to show that

is torsion-free if each

is an

-submodule of

such that

is torsion-free.
Suppose

\overline{rm}=0\in M/(N_{1}\cap N_{2})
for some

. Then
\overline{rm}=0\in M/N_{1}
. Hence

as

is torsion-free. Similarly,

, and thus

.

W\subseteq\cap_{i=1}^{\ell}H_{i}
, and

for all

If

Otherwise,

.
Let

Therefore, there is a hyperplane

in

such that

,

.

Proposition 3.13

(1)

(2)

Proof.

Proposition 3.14

Let

be a toric variety of a fan

(1)

W=N^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{C}}

4.
Singularities of toric/toroidal foliated pairs

Let us start by recalling some definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let X be a normal variety.

(1)

A
foliated pair

on
X

{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.
Note that

.

(2)

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)

K_{\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)+\sum_{E}a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)E

where the sum is over all
\pi
-exceptional divisors and

discrepancy
of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
with respect to

.

(3)

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated pair on a normal variety
X
.
We say that

({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)\textnormal{ is }\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{terminal}\\
\textnormal{canonical}\\
\textnormal{log terminal}\\
\textnormal{log canonical}\\
\ve\textnormal{-log canonical}\end{array}\right.\textnormal{if }a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}>0\\
\geq 0\\
>-\iota(E)\\
\geq-\iota(E)\\
\geq-\iota(E)+\ve\end{array}\right.

for any birational morphism
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
and for any prime
\pi
-exceptional divisor
E
on
\widetilde{X}
.
Here

is a nonnegative real number and recall that
\iota(E)=0
if

is foliation invariant and
\iota(E)=1
otherwise.

Let
P\in X
be a point of
X
which is not necessarily closed.
We say the foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is terminal (resp. canonical, log terminal, log canonical,
\ve
-log canonical)
at

if the requirement on discrepancy is satisfied for any exceptional divisor

whose center in
X
is the Zariski closure of

.

Let
Z
be an irreducible subvariety of
X
.
We say that the foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is terminal (resp. canonical, log terminal, log canonical,
\ve
-log canonical) at the

\eta_{Z}
, the generic point of

.
And we say that the foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is terminal (resp. canonical, log terminal, log canonical,
\ve
-log canonical) at the

point of

.

We say
{\mathcal{F}}
is terminal (resp. canonical, log terminal, log canonical,
\ve

(4)

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated pair on a normal variety

.
We say

is a
log canonical center
(in short,

4.1.
Toric and toroidal foliated pairs

In this subsection, we introduce toroidal foliated pairs.

Notation 4.2
.

For any rational, strongly convex, polyhedral cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
\Sigma_{\sigma}:=\{\tau\mid\tau\preceq\sigma\}
.

Definition 4.3

(Toric foliated pairs)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
A
toric foliated pair

({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
on the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
consists of a
toric foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
on
X_{\Sigma}
where
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector subspace and a torus invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X_{\Sigma}
such that
K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier.

Definition 4.4

(Toroidal foliated pairs)
.

(1)

A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on a normal variety
X
is
toroidal
if it is formally locally toric adapted to
\Xi
for some toroidal embedding
(X\setminus\Xi)\hookrightarrow X
.
That is, there exists a reduced divisor
\Xi
on
X
such that for any closed point
x\in X
, there exist

•

a lattice
N
,

•

a rational, strongly convex, polyhedral cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
,

•

a closed point
p\in U_{\sigma}
,

•

a complex vector subspace
W_{p}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
, and

•

an isomorphism of complete local algebras
\psi_{x}\colon\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,\,x}\cong\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{U_{\sigma},\,p}
, whose induced morphism
\textnormal{d}\psi_{x}
on the tangent sheaves maps
{\mathcal{F}}
to
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
.

The divisor
\Xi
is called the
associated reduced divisor
for the toroidal foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
.

We call
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
is a
semi-local model
if
\sigma
is a top-dimensional cone,
p
is the torus-invariant point, and
\psi_{x}
maps the ideal of
\Xi
to the ideal of some torus-invariant divisor in
U_{\sigma}
.

We call
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
is a
local model
if
p
is in the orbit
{\mathcal{O}}_{\sigma}
and
\psi_{x}
maps the ideal of
\Xi
to the ideal of
U_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma}
where
T_{\sigma}
is the maximal torus in
U_{\sigma}
.

A
stratum
of
\Xi
is a closed subvariety
Z
of
\Xi
such that,
near any
z\in Z
,

is formally locally a stratum of
U_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma}
for some local model
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
at
z
. A
stratum
of
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
is defined in the same way.

(2)

We say a foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
on a normal variety
X
is
toroidal
if
\mathcal{F}
is toroidal with associated reduced divisor
\Xi
and
\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta)\subseteq\Xi
.
Let
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
be a local model of
\mathcal{F}
at
x\in X
. Then there exists a unique torus invariant divisor
\Delta_{p}=\sum_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
such that in the formal neighborhood of

,
\Delta
is given by
\Delta_{p}
via the isomorphism
\psi_{x}
.
The tuple
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p},\Delta_{p})
is called a
local model
of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
at
x\in X
.
A semi-local model of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
at
x\in X
is defined in the similar way as in (1).

Remark 4.5
.

(1)

If
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is a toroidal foliated pair on a normal variety
X
, then
(X,\Delta)
is a toroidal pair (see
[
AK00
]
and
[
ACSS21
]
).

(2)

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
be a toric foliated pair on a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
where
\Sigma
is a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector subspace.
Then by definition,
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is toroidal with associated reduced divisor

(3)

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
be a toroidal foliated pair on a normal variety

.
There exist a local model for any point

.
Indeed, from the structure of toroidal foliated pair, we have a triple
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
.
Let
\sigma_{0}\preceq\sigma
be the face such that

.
Then

at

.

4.2.
Non-dicritical singularities and condition
(\dagger)

In this subsection, we first introduce the definition of non-dicritical singularities, which agrees with the one in the literature.

Definition 4.6
.

A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
of corank
c
on a normal variety
X
is called
dicritical
if there exists a prime divisor
E
over
X
which is not foliation invariant and the center
c_{X}(E)
in
X
has dimension at most
c-1
.

{\mathcal{F}}
is
non-dicritical
if it is not dicritical. Equivalently,
{\mathcal{F}}
is non-dicritical if for any prime divisor
E
over
X
with
\dim c_{X}(E)\leq c-1
,
E
is foliation invariant.

Remark 4.7
.

If
c=1
and
\dim X=3
, this is in agreement with
[
CS21
, Definition 2.10]
.
If
c=2
and
\dim X=3
, this corresponds to the scenario described in the paragraph before
[
CS20
, Lemma 2.8]
.
If
c=0
, then
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is non-dicritical since the assumption is vacuous.
If
c=\dim X
, then
{\mathcal{F}}=0
represents the foliation by points.
In this case, all subvarieties are invariant, and hence
{\mathcal{F}}=0
is also non-dicritical.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
N
be a lattice,
\sigma
be a rational, strongly convex polyhedral cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex vector subspace.
We say
(\sigma,W)
is
non-dicritical
if

(
\dagger
)

\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset\textrm{ if and only if }\tau\subseteq W.

For any fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
we say
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
if
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

The definition is motivated by the non-resonant condition.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a toroidal foliation of co-rank one on a smooth variety
X
. If
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p})
is a local model of
\mathcal{F}
at
x\in X
, then
\mathcal{F}
has a simple singularity of type
1
at
x
in the sense of
[
CS21
, Definition 2.8]

if and only if
(\Sigma_{\sigma},W_{p})
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
(Propositions
A.4
and Proposition
A.5
).

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(\Sigma,W)
be a pair consisting of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and a complex vector subspace
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Suppose
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

(1)

If
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a refinement of
\Sigma
, that is, for any cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, there is a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma
and
|\Sigma|=|\Sigma^{\prime}|
, then
(\Sigma^{\prime},W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

(2)

If
W^{\prime}
is a complex vector subspace of
N_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
W\cap N=W^{\prime}\cap N
, then
(\Sigma,W^{\prime})
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.
Assume furthermore that
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
, then we have
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma^{\prime})\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
be the minimal cone such that
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma
. Then we have

\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)
.
Then by the condition
(\dagger)
for
(\Sigma,W)
, we have
\sigma\subseteq W
.
Thus,
\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma\subseteq W
.

(2)

Suppose
\sigma\in\Sigma
with
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W^{\prime}\cap N\neq\emptyset
.
Then
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N=\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W^{\prime}\cap N\neq\emptyset
.
Because
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
, we have
\sigma\subseteq W
.
Let
v_{1}
,
\ldots
,
v_{\ell}\in N
be primitive generators of
\sigma(1)
where
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
v_{1}
,
\ldots
,
v_{\ell}\in W\cap N=W^{\prime}\cap N\subseteq W^{\prime}
and hence,
\sigma\subseteq W^{\prime}
.
This shows
(\Sigma,W^{\prime})
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

For any
\tau\in\Sigma
such that
V_{\tau}\nsubseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W^{\prime}})
, then we have
W^{\prime}\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)
for some subset

and hence
\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)\subseteq W^{\prime}
.
Since
W\cap N=W^{\prime}\cap N
, by the similar argument as above, we have
\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)\subseteq W
.
Thus,
W^{\prime}\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)\subseteq W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau\subseteq W^{\prime}\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau
and hence,
W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)
.
Therefore,
V_{\tau}\nsubseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.

∎

4.3.
Support functions

We first recall the following definition:

Definition 4.17
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
A function
\phi\colon|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called a

if
\phi
is linear on each
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

Let

be a torus invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier

-divisor on a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then we write
\phi_{D}
as the associated support function (c.f.
[
CLS11
, Theorem 4.2.12]
) with
\phi_{D}(u_{\rho})=-a_{\rho}
where
u_{\rho}\in N
is the primitive element of
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}d_{\rho}D_{\rho})
be a toric foliated pair on a toric varieity
X_{\Sigma}
of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
where

is a complex vector subspace.
Suppose

for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, then the support function
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}
is non-negative.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.3
and Proposition
3.7
,
K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1),\,\rho\subseteq W}D_{\rho}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\iota(D_{\rho})D_{\rho}
.
Thus,
K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}(d_{\rho}-\iota(D_{\rho}))D_{\rho}
and therefore,
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}(u_{\rho})=\iota(D_{\rho})-d_{\rho}\geq 0
where
u_{\rho}
is the primitive generator of
\rho
.
So we have
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}
is non-negative as it is linear on each cone
\tau\in\Sigma
and non-negative on each ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.
∎

Proposition 4.19
.

Suppose
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)

.

(1)

[

(2)

Let

\operatorname{mult}_{D}\Delta_{Y}\leq\iota(D)

D\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta_{Y})
.
Therefore, for any exceptional divisor
E
over
X
, we have the following two cases:

(1)

If
E
is a divisor on
Y
, then there is a primitive element
u
in the lattice
N
such that
\iota(E)+a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)=\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W_{p}}}+\Delta_{p})}(u)\geq 0
.

(2)

If
E
is exceptional over
Y
, then as
K_{\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}}+\Delta_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
and
Y
is smooth, we have
\iota(E)+a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)=\iota(E)+a(E,\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}},\Delta_{Y})\geq 0
.

∎

4.5.
Foliated log resolution

Definition 4.25
.

A birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
is a
foliated log resolution
of a foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
on a normal variety
X
if
E:=\operatorname{Exc}(\pi)
is a divisor and the foliated pair
(\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}},\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
is foliated log smooth.

Remark 4.26
.

By
[
AK00
, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 4.4]
, the foliated log resolution exists for a foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
such that
{\mathcal{F}}
is algebraically integrable.
In Theorem
4.28
, we show that every toroidal foliated pair admits a foliated log resolution.

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex vector subspace.
Then there is a simplicial fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
refining
\Sigma
such that
(\Sigma^{\prime},W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

Proof.

By
[
Fuj03
, Lemma 5.9]
, we have a simplicial fan refining
\Sigma
.
Thus we can assume that
\Sigma
is simplicial.
We will then only use star subdivisions for some rational rays, and this will preserve that our fans are simplicial.

We will proceed by induction on the dimension
k
of the cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
If
k=1
, it is clear that we have
\sigma\subseteq W
provided that
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
.

Claim
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
Suppose for any
\sigma\in\Sigma(\ell)
with
\ell<k
, we have
\sigma\subseteq W
if
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
.
Then for any
\sigma\in\Sigma(k)
with
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
, we have either
\sigma\subseteq W
or
W\cap\sigma
is a rational ray which intersects
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)
.

Proof of Claim.
Suppose there are distinct rational rays
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
in
\sigma
and each of them is not contained in any proper face of
\sigma
.
Then
\operatorname{Cone}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\cap(\sigma\setminus\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma))
consists of exactly two distinct rational rays, say
\rho^{\prime}_{1}
,
\rho^{\prime}_{2}
.
There exist proper faces
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
of
\sigma
such that
\rho^{\prime}_{i}\cap\operatorname{Relint}(\tau_{i})\neq\emptyset
for
i=1
,
2
.
If there exists a proper face
\tau
of
\sigma
containing
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, then we have

\rho_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}(\rho^{\prime}_{1},\rho^{\prime}_{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Cone}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\subseteq\tau
, which is absurd.
Hence
\langle\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\rangle=\sigma
.
By assumption, we have
\tau_{i}\subseteq W
for
i=1
,
2
and thus
\sigma\subseteq W
.
This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

Thus, we inductively define
\Sigma_{k}
and

S_{k}:=\{\rho\textnormal{ is a rational ray in }N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\rho\notin\Sigma_{k-1}(1),\rho=W\cap\sigma\textnormal{ for some }\sigma\in\Sigma_{k-1}(k)\},

for
k\geq 2
with
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma
where
\Sigma_{k}
is the fan obtaining from
\Sigma_{k-1}
by performing a sequence of star subdivisions for the rays (in any order) in
S_{k}
.
Therefore, for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
, we have either
\sigma\subseteq W
or
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N=\emptyset
.
Hence,
\Sigma_{n}
is the fan required.
∎

Theorem 4.28
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a toroidal foliated pair on a normal variety
X
.
Then there is a birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that
(\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F},\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\operatorname{Exc}(\pi))
is foliated log smooth.
Moreover, we can make
Y
to be smooth.

If
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is a toric foliated pair on a toric variety
X
, then we can choose
\pi
to be a toric morphism between toric varieties.

Proof.

We first let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be a smooth resolution of the toroidal pair
(X,\Delta)
such that
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\Delta}:=\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\operatorname{Exc}(\pi))
is toroidal.
Note that
(\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\Delta})
is a toroidal foliated pair on
\widetilde{X}
.

Let
Z
be the dicritical locus of
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
on
\widetilde{X}
.
If
Z=\emptyset
, then
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is a desired morphism.
Otherwise, we may write the decomposition of
Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}Z_{i}
as a union of finitely many irreducible components where
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
By Lemma
4.15
, around a general point
z
of
Z_{i}
with

, there is a semi-local model
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p},\Delta_{p})
such that
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{U_{\sigma},\,p}\cong\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widetilde{X},\,z}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z_{i}}\otimes\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\widetilde{X},\,z}\cong{\mathcal{I}}_{V_{\tau}}\otimes\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{U_{\sigma},\,p}
for some cone
\tau\preceq\sigma
.
Applying Proposition
4.27
, we have a birational morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon Y\to\widetilde{X}
which is a sequence of weighted blow-ups corresponding to the star subdivisions such that
(\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
is non-dicritical.
Note also that

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and
\operatorname{Exc}(\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi)
is of pure codimension one.

Moreover, if
Y
is not smooth, by
[
CLS11
, Theorem 11.1.9]
we can further take
\pi^{\prime\prime}\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
as a sequence of weighted blow-ups along singular locus which corresponds to a sequence of star subdivisions on any semi-local model such that
\widetilde{Y}
is smooth.
Note that the proper transform of
{\mathcal{F}}
on
\widetilde{Y}
is still non-dicritical by Lemma
4.9
(1).
∎

Definition 4.29
.

A foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
on a normal variety
X
is
foliated divisorial log terminal (F-dlt)
if

(1)

each irreducible component of
\Delta
is non-
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant and has a coefficient between
0
and
1
, and

(2)

there exists a foliated log resolution
\pi\colon Y\to X
of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
which only extracts divisors
E
of discrepancy
>-\iota(E)
.

Similar to
[
CS21
, Remark 3.7 and Lemma 3.8]
, we have the following properties for F-dlt foliated pairs:

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
be a F-dlt foliated pair on a normal variety
X
.
Then
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical.
Moreover, it is foliated log smooth at the generic point of any lc center of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same arguments as in the one of
[
CS21
, Remark 3.7 and Lemma 3.8]
.

As
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is F-dlt, there is a foliated log resolution
\pi\colon Y\to X
which only extracts divisors
E
of discrepancy
>-\iota(E)
.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}:=\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
and
\Gamma:=\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
.
Then we may write
K_{\mathcal{G}}+\Gamma+F=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)+G
where
F
,
G
are
\pi
-exceptional effective divisors without common components.
Note that no component of
\Gamma+F
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant.
By Theorem
4.24
,
({\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F)
is log canonical.
Thus, for any divisor
E
over
X
, we have

a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)\geq a(E,{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F)\geq-\iota(E).

Therefore,
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical.

Now let
W
be an lc center of
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
.
Then there is a divisor
T
whose center on
X
is
W
and with discrepancy
a(T,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)=-\iota(T)
.
For the sake of contradiction, we assume that
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is not foliated log smooth at the generic point of
W
.
So
\pi
is not an isomorphism at the generic point of
\pi^{-1}(W)
.
Since
\operatorname{Exc}(\pi)
is a divisor, there exists a
\pi
-exceptional prime divisor
E
which contains the center of
T
on
Y
.

If
E
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant, then
E
is contained in the support of
G
and thus,
a(T,{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F)<a(T,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)=-\iota(T)
, which contradicts the log canonicity of
({\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F)
.

If
E
is non-
{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant, then
E
is contained in the support of
F
.
Note that
\lfloor F\rfloor=0
.
Then there is a
\delta>0
such that if
F^{\prime}:=F+\delta E
, then we have
\lfloor F+\delta E\rfloor=0
,
({\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F^{\prime})
is foliated log smooth, and
a(T,{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F^{\prime})<a(T,{\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F)\leq a(T,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)=-\iota(T)
, which is impossible as
({\mathcal{G}},\Gamma+F^{\prime})
is log canonical by Theorem
4.24
.
∎

4.6.
Toric description for various singularities

In this subsection, we provide toric descriptions for many singularities and show the relations among them.

The following Proposition generalizes

Proposition 4.31

If

for all
i
.

Conversely, suppose

for all

By Theorem
4.28
, there is a birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
with

smooth such that
(\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}},\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\operatorname{Exc}(\pi))

Let
\Delta^{\prime}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)-K_{\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}}
.
For any prime divisor

in
\Delta^{\prime}
, we have a local model
(U_{\sigma},p,W_{p},\Delta_{p})
around a general point

of
\pi(D)
such that
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{U_{\sigma},\,p}\cong\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,\,x}

Note that, by the construction of

Then

Since

for all

in

.

∎

For any complex vector subspace
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
and a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
where
\Sigma
is a fan in

\displaystyle I_{\sigma,\,W}

\displaystyle=\{\rho\mid\rho\in\sigma(1)\textnormal{ and }\rho\subseteq W\}\textnormal{ and}

\displaystyle\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Conv}(0,v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in I_{\sigma,\,W})+\operatorname{Cone}(v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma(1)\setminus I_{\sigma,\,W})

Proposition 4.32
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},0)
be a toric foliated pair on a toric variety

of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
where

Then we have the following:

(1)

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is canonical if and only if for any
\sigma\in\Sigma

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}
not containing the origin.

(3)

For any
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is terminal at the generic point of

if and only if
\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}\neq\sigma
and
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}\cap W\cap N=\emptyset

Proof.

(1)

As
({\mathcal{F}},0)
is a foliated pair,
K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and thus, we have the support function

which is linear on each

Hence, there are
m_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
for

.

Now we fix a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
By
[
CLS11
, Lemma 7.1.1]

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}
are contained in

.
Note that we have

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}

Hence, other facets of
\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}
must contain the origin and therefore,

is a unique facet of
\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}
not containing the origin.

(2)

Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is canonical.
Let
u
be an element in

\Sigma^{\prime}
be the fan obtained from the star subdivision of
\Sigma

.

.

Therefore,

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}
not containing the origin, shown in the proof of (1).

Conversely, we assume that for any
\sigma\in\Sigma

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}\cap W\cap N

\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}

4.27

\Sigma^{\prime}

\Sigma

(\dagger)

(3)

Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}

(a)

either

Conversely, suppose

\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap\Pi_{\sigma,\,W}\cap W\cap N=\emptyset

Proposition 4.34

N_{\mathbb{R}}
where
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector subspace.
Suppose
\Delta
is effective.
Then
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
is F-dlt if and only if the following statements hold true:

(1)

\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta)\subseteq\bigcup_{\rho\subseteq W,\,\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}

(2)

For any
\sigma\in\Sigma

, we have
\sigma
is simplicial and
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical. The latter means that either
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N=\emptyset
or
\sigma\subseteq W
.

Proof.

Note that the condition (1) is equivalent to Definition
4.29
(1).

Suppose
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
is F-dlt.
Then there is a foliated log resolution
\pi\colon Y\to X_{\Sigma}
such that
a(E,{\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)>-\iota(E)
for any
\pi
-exceptional divisor
E
.
For any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
satisfying
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\sigma}=0

V_{\sigma}
is an lc center of
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
.
By Proposition
4.30

({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
is foliated log smooth at the generic point of
V_{\sigma}
.
Then
\sigma
is simplical and
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical.

Conversely, suppose that
\sigma\in\Sigma
is simplicial and
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical if
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\sigma}=0
.
By
[
CLS11
, Exercise 11.1.10]
, there is a simplicial fan
\beta(\Sigma)
refining the fan
\Sigma
.
We recall the construction of

as follows:

We list the cone in

\sigma_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\sigma_{\ell}

.

.
Note that the condition (1) implies that
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}
is non-negative.
Moreover, for any

from assumption as
\sigma
is not simplicial.
Thus,

since
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}

\sigma
.
Hence,

.
Therefore, to show
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
is F-dlt, it suffices to show
(\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{W},\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+E)
is foliated log smooth.
As
\beta(\Sigma)
is simplicial, it is enough to show the following claims:

Claim
.

For any
\sigma\in\beta(\Sigma)

is non-dicritical.

Proof of Claim.
Let
\tau\in\Sigma

\sigma

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\tau}=0
.
By assumption,
\tau
is simplicial and

is non-dicritical.
Thus,
\sigma=\tau
by the construction of

and therefore,
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical.
This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

Claim
.

(\beta(\Sigma),W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

Proof of Claim.
By the claim above, it remains to show that
(\sigma,W)
is non-dicritical for any
\sigma\in\beta(\Sigma)

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\sigma}\neq 0

Let
\sigma\in\beta(\Sigma)

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\sigma}\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset

Let
v\in\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)\cap W\cap N
be an element and
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})
with
s=\dim\sigma
where

.
Then we write
v=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}v_{i}
where

are positive rational numbers.
We can assume that, after re-indexing, that

if and only if
1\leq i\leq\ell
for some positive integer

.
Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\ell<s
.
Let
v^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{s}a_{i}v_{i}=v-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}v_{i}\in W
.
Thus,
v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\cap W

is a fan, we have

.
By the claim above, we have

Therefore,

This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 4.36
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)

\Sigma

Moreover, if a toroidal foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)

X
is F-dlt, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is non-dicritical.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.34
, we have
\Delta
is effective,
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}
is non-negative, and for any
\sigma\in\Sigma

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}+\Delta)}|_{\sigma}=0

(\dagger)

Note that if

As

\Sigma

\sigma_{0}\in\Sigma

\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{s})

We may write

The toroidal case follows from the toric case, Corollary
4.35

4.16

4.7.
F-dlt modification

Following
[
CS21
, Definition 3.28]
, we introduce the following definition of F-dlt modification for the foliated pair of any rank.
Moreover, we show that any toric foliated pair admits an F-dlt modification.

Definition 4.39
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta=\sum_{i}a_{i}\Delta_{i})
be a foliated pair on a normal variety where
\Delta
is effective.
We denote

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\textnormal{n-inv}}=\sum_{i:\,\Delta_{i}\textnormal{ is non-${\mathcal{F}}$-invariant}}\min\{a_{i},1\}\Delta_{i}.

An
F-dlt modification
for
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
is a birational projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
such that if
{\mathcal{G}}
is the pullback foliation on
Y
then the foliated pair
({\mathcal{G}},\pi_{*}^{-1}\widetilde{\Delta}_{\operatorname{n-inv}}+\sum_{i}\iota(E_{i})E_{i})
is F-dlt where the sum is over all
\pi
-exceptional divisors and

K_{\mathcal{G}}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta+\sum_{i}\iota(E_{i})E_{i}+F=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)

for some effective
\pi
-exceptional divisor
F
on
Y
.

The existence of F-dlt modifications is shown for corank 1 foliated pairs on normal projective varieties of dimensions at most three in
[
CS21
, Theorem 8.1]
.
We demonstrate the existence of F-dlt modifications for toric foliated pairs of any rank on toric varieties.

Theorem 4.40
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)
be a toric foliated pair on a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
where

\Delta
is effective.

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial.

By
[
Fuj03
, Lemma 5.9]

[
CLS11

, there is a simplicial fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
refining
\Sigma

.

Now by Proposition
4.27

\Sigma^{\prime\prime}

\Sigma^{\prime}

\Delta^{\prime\prime}=\psi^{*}(K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})-K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}}

Note that

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial,

It remains to show that, for any prime
(\psi\circ p)
-exceptional divisor

, the log discrepancy

.

v_{\rho}

\rho

\Sigma^{\prime}(2)

and
\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})}|_{\sigma}\leq 0
as

is effective and both elements of

.

\phi_{(K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})}(v_{\rho})\leq 0
where
v_{\rho}
is the primitive element of
\rho
.

Now for

and

where
v_{\rho}

\rho

5.1.
Preliminaries

Let

be an extremal ray.
By
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.3.20]
,

for some
\omega\in\Sigma(n-1)
.
By
[

, Theorem 14-1-9]
or
[

For any

\rho_{i}\in\Sigma(1)
.
Since
\Sigma
is complete, there are two primitive vectors
v_{n}
and
v_{n+1}
in
N
such that

\displaystyle\sigma^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\quad\textnormal{and}

\displaystyle\sigma^{n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1},v_{n+1})

are
n
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma(n)
.
There is a unique non-trivial linear relation
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}v_{i}=0

with
a_{n+1}=1
and
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
for each
i
.
After re-indexing, we may assume that

a_{i}\begin{cases}<0&\textnormal{if $1\leq i\leq\alpha$}\\
=0&\textnormal{if $\alpha+1\leq i\leq\beta$}\\
>0&\textnormal{if $\beta+1\leq i\leq n+1$}\end{cases}

for some
\alpha
,
\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. The following notations will be used in the subsequent discussions:

•

\sigma(\omega)=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})
.

•

\sigma^{j}=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{1},\ldots,\widehat{v_{j}},\ldots,v_{n+1})
for

.

•

\sigma_{J}=\operatorname{Cone}(v_{j}\mid j\in J)
for any subset
J\subseteq[1,n+1]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
.

•

J_{-}=[1,\alpha]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
,
J_{0}=[\alpha+1,\beta]\cap\mathbb{N}
, and
J_{+}=[\beta+1,n+1]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
.

We recall some facts for later use. There is a decomposition
\sigma(\omega)=\bigcup_{j\in J_{+}}\sigma^{j}
with
\sigma^{j}\in\Sigma
for any

[
Mat02
, Proposition 14-2-1]
. The exceptional locus

is

[
Mat02
, Corollary 14-2-2]
. In particular,

is independent of the choice of
\omega
.

For any

not necessarily generating an extremal ray in
\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X_{\Sigma})
, we can still use the notation above.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a log canonical toric foliated pair on

with
\Delta\geq 0
, and let

be a wall such that

. Notation as above. Then there exists

such that

.

By Proposition

and the assumption that

, we have

.
Then

of

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta)

Notation as in subsection

.

(1)

5.5.
Cone Theorem

The goal of this subsection is to prove the cone theorem for log canonical toric foliated pairs (Theorem
5.13
).
In Definition
2.5
, the notion of tangency is discussed when the subvariety is not completely contained in the singular locus.
The following definition removes this restriction and allows us to talk about tangency for an arbitrary subvariety. For any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{H}
on a normal variety
X
, we write
\mathcal{H}(p):=\mathcal{H}_{p}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}\mathbb{C}(p)
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of any rank on a normal variety
X
.
A subvariety
Z\subseteq X
is
tangent to
{\mathcal{F}}
if there exist a birational morphism
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
and a prime divisor
E\subseteq X^{\prime}
with
c_{X}(E)=Z
which satisfy the following.
For any general point
q\in E
, the composition map
{\mathcal{T}}_{E}(q)\cap{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(q)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{E}(q)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{Z}(p)
is surjective where
p=\pi(q)
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
.

Remark 5.6
.

(1)

In Definition
5.5
, it suffices to find one point
q\in E\setminus\left(\operatorname{Sing}(X^{\prime})\cup\operatorname{Sing}(E)\cup\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})\right)
such that
\pi(q):=p\notin\operatorname{Sing}(Z)
and that the composition map
{\mathcal{T}}_{E}(q)\cap{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}(q)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}_{E}(q)\to{\mathcal{T}}_{Z}(p)
is surjective.

(2)

Tangency of a subvariety possibly contained in the singular locus has been discussed in the literature under extra assumptions.

•

[
Wan23
, Definition 3.2]
:
\dim Z=1
.
The definition in loc. cit. is slightly different from ours.

•

[
ACSS21
, Subsection 3.4]
:
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable.

•

[
CS21
, Definition 2.12]
:
\mathcal{F}
is non-dicritical of corank one.
In Proposition
5.8
, we show that, under the same assumption, this coincides with Definition
5.5
.

The following Lemma is a generalization of
[
CS21
, Remark 2.16]
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a non-dicritical foliation of corank one on a normal variety
X
, and let

be a subvariety.
Assume that there is a birational morphism
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X

\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{X}\to X

5.7
.

If
Z
is a divisor, then there is nothing to prove. Hence we assume that

has codimension greater than or equal to two.
Since

\widetilde{X}
by a higher model, we may assume

is smooth and
there is a sequence of smooth exceptional divisors

,
\ldots

is a prime divisor in

and

that dominates

.
It suffices to prove that

is foliation invariant assuming that

.

Suppose on the contrary that

is not foliation invariant.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}=\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}|_{G}
is a foliation of corank one on
G
.
Moreover,
\dim Z\geq 1
as
{\mathcal{F}}
is non-dicritical.
Let

Then on

{\mathcal{G}}
is the intersection of

and a leaf of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
Write
\psi=\widetilde{\pi}|_{G}
.
We have the following two cases:

Case 1
:

.

is an irreducible component of
\operatorname{Sing}{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}

Let
V
be the Zariski open subset of

\operatorname{Sing}{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}

dominates

, we may choose a dense Zariski open set

Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}

2.5

Note that

By our choice,

Appendix A
Simple singularities

In this section, we first recall some facts from
[
Can04
, Proposition 46 and Definition 13]
on simple singularities for corank one foliations on smooth varieties.
Then we introduce the simple singularities for foliations of any rank on smooth varieties.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
If for all non-zero maps
\phi\colon\{1,\ldots,m\}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, we have
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\phi(k)\lambda_{k}\neq 0
, then we say that the tuple
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
satisfies the
non-resonant condition
.

Definition A.2

(Simple singularities for corank one foliations)
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension
n
and
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of corank one on
X
.
A point
p\in X
is a
simple
singularity for
{\mathcal{F}}
if, in a formal coordinate
x_{1}
,
\ldots
,
x_{n}
around
p
,
{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is generated by a
1
-form in one of the following two types:

(1)

There are
\lambda_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\lambda_{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
satisfying the non-resonant condition with
1\leq t\leq n
such that

\omega=\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\Bigg)\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}.

(2)

There are
\lambda_{2}
,
\ldots
,
\lambda_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\lambda_{k+1}
,
\ldots
,
\lambda_{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
p_{1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq k\leq t\leq n
, and a one-variable non-unit formal series
\Psi
such that

\omega=\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}+\Psi(x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{p_{k}})\sum_{i=2}^{t}\lambda_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}\Bigg)

where the tuple
(\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
satisfies the non-resonant condition.

For
\ell=1
or
2
, we say
p
is a simple singularity of type
\ell
if
{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\mathscr{F}}
is locally generated by the
1
-forms of the
\ell
-th type.

We say
{\mathcal{F}}
has simple singularities adapted to a reduced divisor

if
{\mathcal{F}}
has simple singularities and, for every

, we can choose formal coordinates as above so that

is normal crossing at

.

Definition A.3

(Simple singularities in general)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of corank

on a smooth variety of dimension
n
.
A point

is a

singularity for
{\mathcal{F}}
if, around

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
are corank one foliation with simple singularity at

in the same formal coordinates around
p
.
For
\ell=1
or
2
, we say
{\mathcal{F}}
has simple singularity of type
\ell
at

p

{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
with simple singularity of type
\ell
at
p
such that
{\mathcal{F}}=\cap_{i=1}^{c}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
.

Before we investigate the simple singularities, we show that the toric foliations on smooth toric varieties are close to simple singularities.

Example A.7

In general, the simple singularities of type two on smooth varieties are not (log) canonical.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{xyzt}\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\textnormal{d}x}{x}+\frac{\textnormal{d}y}{y}+x^{2}y\left(\frac{\textnormal{d}z}{z}+\frac{\textnormal{d}t}{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\textnormal{d}x}{x}+2\frac{\textnormal{d}y}{y}+xy^{2}\left(\frac{\textnormal{d}z}{z}+\frac{\textnormal{d}t}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{xyzt}\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\textnormal{d}x}{x}+2\frac{\textnormal{d}y}{y}+x^{2}y^{2}\left(\frac{\textnormal{d}z}{z}+2\frac{\textnormal{d}t}{t}\right)

Appendix B
Minimal log discrepancies

Definition B.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a foliated pair on a normal variety
X
.
For any subvariety

, we define the

of the foliated pair
({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
over

\inf\{a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta)+\iota(E)\mid\textnormal{$E$ is a divisor exceptional over $X$ with center contained in $Z$}\}.

We will denote

as
\operatorname{mld}({\mathcal{F}},\Delta)
.

Proposition B.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a smooth foliation on a smooth variety
X
and

be a subvariety of
X
.
If
{\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+{\mathcal{F}}_{p}\subsetneq{\mathcal{T}}_{X,\,p}
for any general point

, then any divisor

with center

is foliation invariant.
In particular,
{\mathcal{F}}
is non-dicritical.

Proof.

Let
c
be the corank of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a birational morphism and
E\subseteq Y
be a prime exceptional divisor over
X
with
Z=c_{X}(E)
.

For a general point

, there is a submersion

which induces the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}|_{U}
where

is an analytic open neighborhood of

and

is an open submanifold of
{\mathbb{A}}^{c}

After shrinking
U
around the generic point of

, we can assume that

and

are smooth.

Claim
.

\dim f(Z)<\dim B=c
.

Proof of Claim.
Since
{\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+{\mathcal{F}}_{p}\neq{\mathcal{T}}_{X,\,p}
, we have
\dim({\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+{\mathcal{F}}_{p})<\dim{\mathcal{T}}_{X,\,p}=n
.
Then

\displaystyle\dim({\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{p})

\displaystyle=\dim{\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{p}-\dim({\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+{\mathcal{F}}_{p})

\displaystyle>\dim Z+r-n

\displaystyle=\dim Z-c.

As
p
is general and
f
is a submersion,
\dim({\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{p})=\dim(f^{-1}(f(p))\cap Z)=\dim Z-\dim f(Z)
.
Thus, we have

.
This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

By Zariski’s lemma (cf.
[
KM98
, Lemma 2.45]
), we can assume that
E
is obtained by a sequence of, say
\alpha
, blow-ups along subvarieties centered on

is foliation invariant.

As

is a submersion, by implicit function theorem, we can choose analytic coordinates
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
\ldots
,
x_{n}
such that

is a natural projection onto its
c

.
Since

is smooth near

, we can do a change of variables on first
c

f(Z)=(x_{1}=\ldots=x_{k}=0)
where
k=c-\dim f(Z)
.
Note that we still have

.

Now we may write

as the zero locus of the ideal generated by

,
\ldots
,
g_{k}=x_{k}
,
g_{k+1}
,
\ldots
,

where

and
g_{j}
are holomorphic functions in variables

,
\ldots
,
x_{n}
.
Note that

and thus

.
Note also that the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}|_{U}
is induced by

.

We have the description for blow-up of

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-m})\mid y_{i}g_{j}-g_{i}y_{j}=0\}\in{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n-m-1}_{y_{1},\,\ldots,\,y_{n-m}}.

On the affine chart

for

Note that

In particular, if

\displaystyle\leq\dim{\mathcal{T}}_{Z,\,p}+\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{p}

\displaystyle=\dim Z+\operatorname{rank}({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\leq c-1+r

\displaystyle=n-1<n

Therefore,

is non-dicritical.

Appendix C

Definition C.1
.

A foliation

on a

is said to be

if any divisor

over

such that

is contained in

is foliation invariant.

In

is a smooth threefold and

has corank one.
Here, we establish that this equivalence also holds true for any toric foliations on smooth toric varieties.

Theorem C.2
.

Suppose

is a toric foliation on a smooth toric variety

of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
where
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector subspace.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is non-dicritical.

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is strongly non-dicritical.

(3)

(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.16
, we have that (1) implies (3).

Now we suppose
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
.
By Remark

(4), we can choose

in

containing

such that

and

for

.
Then we have
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{c}{\mathcal{F}}_{H_{i}}
.
By Lemma

(2),

satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
for all

and thus, by Proposition

,

are toric foliations of corank one and with only simple singularities.
Consequently,

is strongly non-dicritical by

, Remark 2.13]
.
We consider a birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
and an exceptional divisor

over
X
whose center on
X
is
Z
.
Furthermore, we suppose

is contained in
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.
By Lemma

(2), we have

for all

This implies that

Consequently,

is invariant under

.
This shows (3) implies (2).

Suppose

is strongly non-dicritical.
To demonstrate that

is non-dicritical, we consider any birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
and an exceptional divisor

whose center on

is

with

where

is the corank of
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
.
If

, then the strong non-dicriticality of

implies that

is invariant under

.
Hence we can assume that

.
Therefore, by Proposition

,
E
is foliation invariant.
This shows (2) implies (1).
∎

Appendix D
Another description of
(\dagger)

In
[
Wan23
]
, an alternative definition for non-dicriticality is presented.
It is not immediately clear whether our definition is equivalent to the one provided in that work.
However, we can establish the following equivalence for toric foliations on a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variety:

Proposition D.1
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variety of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation on
X_{\Sigma}
where
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex vector subspace.
Then
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is non-dicritical in the sense of
[
Wan23
, Definition 3.6]
, which requires all exceptional divisors over the singular locus of the foliation is foliation invariant.

Proof.

(If part) Suppose
(\Sigma,W)
does not satisfy the condition
(\dagger)
.
Then there is a cone
\tau\in\Sigma
such that
\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
and
\tau\nsubseteq W
.
Let
u\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\cap W\cap N
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the star subdivision of
\Sigma
for the ray
\rho:={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}u
.
Then we have a birational morphism
\pi\colon X^{\prime}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\to X_{\Sigma}
with an exceptional divisor
D_{\rho}
whose center on
X_{\Sigma}
is
V_{\tau}
.
Since
\rho\subseteq W
,
D_{\rho}
is non-
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\,\Sigma^{\prime}}
-invariant by Corollary
3.3
.
We will show that
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is dicritical in the sense of
[
Wan23
, Definition 3.6]
by showing
V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.

Let
t=\#\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\subseteq W\cap\tau\}
and
\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\subseteq W\cap\tau\}=\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t}\}
.
In particular,
\rho_{i}\in\tau(1)
for all
i\in[1,t]\cap{\mathbb{N}}
.
So
t\leq\dim\tau
.
As
\tau\nsubseteq W
, we have
t<\dim\tau
as
\tau
is simplicial.
Since
u\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)
, the vectors
u_{i}
together with
u
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{R}}
and over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Thus,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau)\geq t+1
as
u_{i}
,
u\in W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau
.
Hence,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\tau-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W+{\mathbb{C}}\tau)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau)>t
and therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\tau-t>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W+{\mathbb{C}}\tau)
.
By Proposition
3.9
, we have
V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.

(Only if part) Now suppose that
E
is an exceptional divisor whose center on
X_{\Sigma}
is
Z
, which is contained in
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
. Since
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
is a torus invariant closed subset of codimension at least
2
, there exists a cone
\tau\in\Sigma(\ell)
with
\ell\geq 2
such that
Z\subseteq V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.
We will show that
E
is foliation invariant.

If
\tau\subseteq W
, then
\#\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\subseteq\tau\cap W\}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\tau
as
\tau
is simplicial.
Thus
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\tau+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W-\#\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\subseteq\tau\cap W\}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(W+{\mathbb{C}}\tau)
and hence, by Proposition
3.9
,
V_{\tau}\nsubseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
\tau\nsubseteq W
.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a smooth fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
refining
\Sigma
and
f\colon X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\to X_{\Sigma}
be the associated toric morphism, which is a toric resolution of
X_{\Sigma}
.
As
\dim\tau\geq 2
, we can consider a finer smooth fan of
\Sigma
and assume that

S:=\{\rho\in\Sigma^{\prime}(1)\setminus\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\cap\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\neq\emptyset\}\neq\emptyset.

Then since
c_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(E)
is irreducible, we have
c_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(E)\subseteq D_{\rho}
for some
\rho\in S
, and by construction
f(D_{\rho})=V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.
Note that
c_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(E)\subseteq D_{\rho}
.
By construction
f(D_{\rho})=V_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.

If
D_{\rho}
is not foliation invariant, then
\rho\subseteq W
by Corollary
3.3
.
Since
\rho\cap\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\neq\emptyset
and
(\Sigma,W)
satisfies the condition
(\dagger)
, we have
\tau\subseteq W
, which is impossible.
Thus,
D_{\rho}
is foliation invariant.
Therefore, by Corollary
B.3
,
E
is foliation invariant as
c_{X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}}(E)\subseteq D_{\rho}
.
∎

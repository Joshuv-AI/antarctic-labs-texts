Capacity Results for Multiple-Input Multiple-Output Optical Wireless Communication With Per-Antenna Intensity Constraints
Ru-Han Chen, Longguang Li, Jia-Ning Guo, Xu Yang, Jian Zhang, Lin Li

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the capacity of a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) optical intensity channel (OIC) under per-antenna peak- and average-intensity constraints. We first consider the case where the average intensities of input are required to be equal to preassigned constants due to the requirement of illumination quality and color temperature. When the channel graph of the MIMO OIC is strongly connected, we prove that the strongest eigen-subchannel must have positive channel gains, which simplifies the capacity analysis. Then we derive various capacity bounds by utilizing linear precoding, generalized entropy power inequality, and QR decomposition, etc. These bounds are numerically verified to approach the capacity in the low or high signal-to-noise ratio regime. Specifically, when the channel rank is one less than the number of transmit antennas, we derive an equivalent capacity expression from the perspective of convex geometry, and new lower bounds are derived based on this equivalent expression. Finally, the developed results are extended to the more general case where the average intensities of input are required to be no larger than preassigned constants.

Achievable Rate Based on Generalized EPI

In this subsection, another achievable rate is derived by the generalized entropy-power inequality in

]

Assume that all

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\textnormal{h}(\mathbb{H}\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{}\!\textnormal{h}(\mathbb{H}\mathbf{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\left|\det(\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{H}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}})\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left|\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}d_{i}\cdot\left(\mathbf{h}_{i}\mathbf{h}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{i}\right)\right)\right|

where (

has independent components

Define
\mathbf{d}_{\max}\triangleq\left(\exp(2\mathfrak{h}_{\text{TE}}(\alpha_{1})),\cdots,\exp(2\mathfrak{h}_{\text{TE}}(\alpha_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}))\right)

\bm{0}\prec\mathbf{d}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{d}_{\max}

Lemma 1

For
\mathbf{d}

\bm{0}\prec\mathbf{d}\preccurlyeq\mathbf{d}_{\max}

Note that
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{D}\mathbb{H}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}d_{i}\cdot\left(\mathbf{h}_{i}\mathbf{h}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{i}\right)

is positive semi-definite for any

. Thus, the inequality
\det\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}d_{i}\cdot\left(\mathbf{h}_{i}\mathbf{h}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{i}\right)\right)\geq 0
always holds.
Let
\mathbb{X}=\sum_{i=2}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}d_{i}\cdot\left(\mathbf{h}_{i}\mathbf{h}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{i}\right)
and
\mathbb{Y}=\mathbf{h}_{1}\mathbf{h}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{1}

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\log\det\left(\mathbb{X}+t\mathbb{Y}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\left(\mathbb{X}+t\mathbb{Y}\right)^{-1}\mathbb{Y}\right)
. Due to
\mathbf{d}\succ\bm{0}

\mathbb{X}+t\mathbb{Y}
,

and
\mathbb{Y}
are all positive semi-definite for any

. By using the trace inequality in
[
21
, Theorem 4.3.53]
for two positive semi-definite matrices, we have
\operatorname{tr}\left(\left(\mathbb{X}+t\mathbb{Y}\right)^{-1}\mathbb{Y}\right)\geq 0
, which indicates

for any feasible choice of
\mathbb{X}
. The lemma is concluded by similarly applying the above technique to

\cdots

IV
Equivalent Capacity Expressions for Rank-(
n_{\textnormal{T}}-1
)

Although capacity expressions of the general MIMO OIC under a total average-intensity constraint and peak-intensity constraints and the MISO EC-OIC have been given in
[
11
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively, a general solution to that of the MIMO OIC under per-antenna intensity constraints still seems to be intractable. Even for the MISO case
[
14
]
, the analysis relies on several infrequently-used concepts, such as the stop-loss function, convex ordering and comonotonicity. Unfortunately, the technique applied in the MISO case can not be generalized to our considered MIMO case since the optimality of comonotonic inputs no longer holds, e.g., independent input is asymptotically capacity-achieving when
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}
is invertible.

Despite of the above challenges, in this subsection we characterize the capacity expression for the MIMO EC-OIC in the special case
r=n_{\textnormal{T}}-1
from the perspective of convex geometry that distinguished from the idea of distribution decomposition in
[
14
]
.

As shown in
[
11
]
, the admissible region
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
of the equivalent input
\mathbf{S}=\widetilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{H}}}\mathbf{X}
under amplitude constraints (
2a
) on
\mathbf{X}
is the zonotope

\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)\triangleq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}x_{i}\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}:x_{i}\in[0,1]\textnormal{ for all }i\in[n_{\textnormal{T}}]\right\}.

Note that the volume of
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)

is equal
to the determinant of the full-rank square matrix
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}
, which implies
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
is non-degenerate in
r
-space. Moreover, it can be shown that the zonotope
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
is compact in Euclidean
r
-space.
The following theorem reveals that any MIMO EC-OIC of rank
n_{\textnormal{T}}-1
is equivalent to a vector Gaussian channel under with a zonotope support constraint, an expectation constraint, and two piecewise linear cost constraints.

Theorem 3
(Equivalent Capacity Expression)

Let

denote the entry that lies in the
j
-column and the
i
-row of the orthogonal matrix
\mathbb{V}
.
In the case of

(33)

where the supremum is over all probability laws
\mathscr{P}_{\mathbf{S}}
on
\mathbf{S}
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\right)
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{S}\right]=\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{\alpha},

(34a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{S})\right]\leq\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\bm{\alpha},

(34b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{S}\right)\right]\leq\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\left(\bm{1}-\bm{\alpha}\right),

(35)

Proof:

See Appendix

∎

As presented in Theorem

It is well-known that the differential entropy of a random vector under moment constraints is maximized by the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
[
24

(n_{\textnormal{T}}-1)
MIMO EC-OIC can be obtained by convex programming for the maximum differential entropy and the EPI.

Theorem 4

The capacity of the MIMO EC-OIC of rank

is lower-bounded by

\mathsf{C}_{\textnormal{E}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}},\bm{\alpha},\sigma\right)\geq\frac{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\exp\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{E}}/\left(n_{\textnormal{T}}-1\right)\right)}{2\pi e\sigma^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{R},\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\atop\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0}\mathbf{u}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{\alpha}+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\bm{\alpha}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\left(\bm{1}-\bm{\alpha}\right)-1-\nu

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\right)}\exp\left(\nu-\mathbf{u}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{s}-\lambda_{1}f_{\textnormal{min}}\left(\mathbf{s}\right)-\lambda_{2}f_{\textnormal{min}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}.

(37)

is the maximum differential entropy of the equivalent input
\mathbf{S}
for the MIMO EC-OIC.

Proof:

Along the same line of the proof
[
24

We conclude the proof by applying the EPI (

\mathbf{Y}
.

It is noticed that a tractable form of the optimal solution

Proof:

The bound is simply achieved by ignoring other subchannels corresponding smaller eigenvalues.

The above theorem characterizes the achievable rate of only using the strongest eigen-subchannel. VII
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we investigate the capacities of MIMO OICs under two different types of per-antenna intensity constraints. Based on a channel reduction method, we shows the positivity of channel gains of the strongest eigen-subchannel for the general MIMO OIC, which motivates the optimal rank-one transmission scheme. Then we derive various capacity bounds by analyzing achievable rates of different transceiver frameworks and some standard information-theoretic tools. Besides, an equivalent expression is given in a special case that the rank of the channel is one less than the number of transmit antennas. The developed results are generalized to the MIMO OIC with bounded-cost constraints.

So far, for the MIMO OIC under per-antenna intensity constraints, equivalent capacity expressions converting the original linear channel into the vector Gaussian channel with moment constraints are characterized only for two special cases, i.e., the rank-one case in
[
14
]
and the rank-
(n_{\textnormal{T}}-1)
case in this paper. Characterization of its capacity in general case is still an open problem. The main difficulty lies in the description of the moment constraints on the transformed vector Gaussian channel. To develop a unified theoretical framework for general MIMO EC- and BC-OIC of arbitrary rank may be an interesting research direction in the future.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\left|\check{b}_{k}\right|+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{c}}}\left|\check{b}_{k}\right|1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(-\check{b}_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\left|\beta_{k}\check{b}_{k}\right|+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{c}}}\left|\beta_{k}\check{b}_{k}\right|1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(-\check{b}_{k}),

(53)

where (
53
) follows from the fact that
\left|\beta_{k}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence, the inequality (
21c
) also holds for
\check{\mathbf{b}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}
.Thus we know the optimal solution
\mathbf{b}^{\star}
that maximizes
\mathbf{b}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{j}\mathbf{w}
must be nonnegative, and moreover
{b}^{\star}_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\text{c}}
. By using this property, the inequalities (
21b
) and (
21c
) can be converted into (
22c
) and (
22d
).
The same technique applies to the another problem of maximizing
-\mathbf{b}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{j}\mathbf{w}
as well. The proposition is concluded by combining optimization results for maximizing
\mathbf{b}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{j}\mathbf{w}
and
-\mathbf{b}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{j}\mathbf{w}
.

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
3

The following lemma reveals that any feasible input
\mathbf{X}
can be seen as some function of its image
\mathbf{S}=\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{X}
when the cost is only determined by the average intensity of the channel input.

Lemma 2

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{conv}}
be a convex and closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
. For any input
\mathbf{X}
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\mathbf{X}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{conv}}
, there exists an input
\breve{\mathbf{X}}
, which is a function of
\mathbf{S}
, satisfying

1.

\operatorname{supp}\,\breve{\mathbf{X}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{conv}}
;

2.

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\breve{\mathbf{X}}\right]
;

3.

\mathbf{S}=\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{X}\overset{\textnormal{d}}{=}\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
.

Proof:

Construct a vector-valued function
\bm{\psi}:\operatorname{supp}\,\mathbf{S}\to\mathcal{X}_{\text{conv}}
as
{\bm{\psi}}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}|\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{s}\right]
.
It is straightforward that
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\cdot{\bm{\psi}}(\mathbf{s})=\mathbf{s}
. Since the intersection of an affine set and
\mathcal{X}_{\text{conv}}
is convex and closed, we have
{\bm{\psi}}(\mathbf{s})\in\mathcal{X}_{\text{conv}}
.
The proof is concluded by letting
\breve{\mathbf{X}}={\bm{\psi}}(\mathbf{S})
, whose expectation satisfies
\mathbb{E}\left[\breve{\mathbf{X}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{X}}\right]
due to the law of total expectation.
∎

Since the zonotope
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
is convex and closed, it is sufficient to let the channel input
\mathbf{X}
be a function of a random vector
\mathbf{S}
with
\operatorname{supp}\,\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
and under constraints specified in the following.

For any
\mathbf{s}=\left(s_{1},\,s_{2},\,\cdots,s_{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\right)^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\in\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
, we define

\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}\triangleq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}:\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{s}\right\}.

It is straightforward that
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
must be nonempty and convex. Since the matrix
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}
has full row rank, its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse is given by

\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{+}=\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\right)^{-1}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}
. Note that the set
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
is the intersection of the affine set
\text{null}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}})+\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{+}\mathbf{s}
and the unit
n_{\textnormal{T}}
-cube, where the null space
\text{null}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}})=\left\{\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
since
\textnormal{rank}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\right)=n_{\textnormal{T}}-1
and
\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
is orthogonal to
\mathbb{V}_{1}
. Hence,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
is a one-dimensional convex hull, i.e., a line segment, and can be expressed in the form of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}=\left\{\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}:\lambda\in\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s}),f_{\textnormal{max}}(\mathbf{s})\right]\right\}
, where two real-valued functions
f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s})
and
f_{\textnormal{max}}(\mathbf{s})
will be characterized later.

Note that, for any
i\in[n_{\textnormal{T}}]
, the
i
-th element of
\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}
is

\kappa_{i}(\lambda)=\lambda v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}v_{j,i}s_{j}/\sigma_{j}
,
where
v_{j,i}
stands for the
i
-th element of
\mathbf{v}_{j}
.

We first study the case of
v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}=0
. Note that, for any
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
, there exists an
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}^{+}\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{1}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{I}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\right)\mathbf{x}

(54)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{x},

(55)

where
\mathbb{I}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
denotes the identity matrix of size
n_{\textnormal{T}}\times n_{\textnormal{T}}
and (
54
) follows from the equality

\mathbb{I}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}=\mathbb{V}\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{1}+\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}.

(56)

It follows immediately that the
i
-th row of the matrix
\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
is all-zero if
v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}=0
. Due to
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
, we have
\kappa_{i}(\lambda)\in[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
in the case of
v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}=0
.

We only need to consider the indices
i
such that
v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\neq 0
. Notice that
\kappa_{i}(\lambda)
is an affine function, which
is equal
to
0
at

\lambda=\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}v_{j,i}s_{j}/\sigma_{j}\right)/v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}

and equal to
1
at

\lambda=\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}v_{j,i}s_{j}/\sigma_{j}\right)/v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}.
It is clear that the inequality
0\leq\kappa_{i}(\lambda)\leq 1
holds for all
\lambda\in\left[\frac{1-\textnormal{sign}(v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i})}{2v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\frac{v_{j,i}s_{j}}{v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\sigma_{j}},\frac{1+\textnormal{sign}(v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i})}{2v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\frac{v_{j,i}s_{j}}{v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\sigma_{j}}\right]

in the case of
v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\neq 0
. Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s})=\max_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i:\,}v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\neq 0}\left\{\frac{1-\textnormal{sign}(v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i})}{2v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\frac{v_{j,i}s_{j}}{v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\sigma_{j}}\right\}

(57)

and

\displaystyle f_{\textnormal{max}}(\mathbf{s})=\min_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i:\,}v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\neq 0}\left\{\frac{1+\textnormal{sign}(v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i})}{2v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\frac{v_{j,i}s_{j}}{v_{n_{\textnormal{T}},i}\sigma_{j}}\right\}.

(58)

It is noteworthy that
f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s})
is a convex function, while
f_{\textnormal{max}}(\mathbf{s})
is concave. Especially, by the central symmetry of a cube, we can prove the following equality

f_{\textnormal{max}}(\mathbf{s})+f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})=\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}.

(59)

Recall that
\mathcal{X}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}}
consists of all points
\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}\right)
over
\lambda\in\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}),f_{\textnormal{max}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})\right]

and notice that

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}_{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\bm{1}-\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\left(\bm{1}-\left(\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\right)\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\right)-\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{1}-\left(\left(\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\lambda\right)\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\right),

(61)

where (
60
) follows from (
56
). It is clear that
\mathcal{X}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}}
is the reflection of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
across the central point
\frac{1}{2}\bm{1}
. Hence, we know that the set
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
consists of points
\left(\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\lambda\right)\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}
for all

\lambda\in\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s}),f_{\textnormal{max}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})\right]
, which immediately leads to (
59
).

Based on Lemma
2
, we can alternatively let the channel input be the image of a random vector
\mathbf{S}
under a deterministic mapping
\bm{\phi}:\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)\to[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
. Let
\mathscr{P}
be a given probability measure of
\mathbf{S}
on
\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
, and assume
\bm{\phi}
to be Lebesgue measurable and satisfy
\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{s})\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{s}}
for each
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
. We can write
\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{s})=\lambda(\mathbf{s})\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}
readily, where
\lambda(\mathbf{s})\in\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s}),\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})\right]
. Under the probability measure
\mathscr{P}
, the vector-valued expectation of
\phi(\mathbf{S})
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{P}}^{\bm{\phi}}

\displaystyle=\int\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}

(62)

\displaystyle=\left(\int\lambda(\mathbf{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}\right)\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\int\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}.

(63)

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}=\left\{\bm{\zeta}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{P}}^{\bm{\phi}}\right\}\subseteq[0,1]^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
, which contains real vectors
\bm{\zeta}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{P}}^{\phi}
over all admissible mappings
\phi
. The convexity of
\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}
can be easily proved since
\bm{\zeta}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{P}}^{\phi}
is linear with respect to the mapping
\phi
, and thus the set
\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}
must be a line segment parallel to
\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
. Furthermore, note that

\int f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}\leq\int\lambda(\mathbf{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P}\leq\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\int f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{P},

where the lower bound is reached by letting
\lambda(\mathbf{s})=f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{s})
, while the upper bound is reached by
\lambda(\mathbf{s})=\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{s})
.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}=\Big\{

\displaystyle\beta\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}+\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{B}_{11}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{S}\right]:

\displaystyle\beta\in\big[\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\mathbf{S})\right],\bm{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\mathbb{E}\left[f_{\textnormal{min}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}\bm{1}-\mathbf{S})\right]\big]\Big\}.

(64)

Note that the vector
\bm{\alpha}=\mathbb{V}\mathbb{V}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\bm{\alpha}
, i.e., a linear combination of the basis
\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}

with the coordinate representation
(\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{1}\bm{\alpha},\cdots,\mathbf{v}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\bm{\alpha})^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}
. It is straightforward that a random vector
\mathbf{S}
with the probability law
\mathscr{P}
and
\operatorname{supp}\,\mathbf{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\left({\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}}\right)
is a feasible equivalent input if and only if
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}
. Then, by comparing
\bm{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\!\mathscr{P}}
, we conclude that the probability law
\mathscr{P}
of the equivalent input
\mathbf{S}
is feasible if and only if (
34
) holds.
This is complete proof of Theorem
3
.
\blacksquare

Appendix C
Proof of Proposition

A case-by-case proof is given as what follows.

1.

\alpha_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. We conclude
\mathbf{x}^{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{1}
by showing the bivariate function
\psi(x,y)=\min\left\{x,y\right\}-xy
is maximized when
x=y=1/2
.

2.

\alpha_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}<\frac{1}{2}
. For any feasible point
\mathbf{x}
, there exists a permutation matrix
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
such that elements of
\mathbf{z}=\mathbb{P}_{\pi}\cdot\mathbf{x}
are in descending order. Note that the objective function
f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\tilde{f}_{0}(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbb{G}_{\pi}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{d}_{\pi}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbf{z}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{\pi}={\mathbb{P}_{\pi}}\mathbb{H}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}
,
\mathbf{d}_{\pi}\triangleq\left(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}-\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}-\omega_{n_{\textnormal{T}}-1}\right)^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}
, and
\omega_{i}\triangleq{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\right)}\mathbb{G}_{\pi}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}\right)
.
Let
\left(\pi(1),\pi(2),\cdots,\pi(n_{\textnormal{T}})\right)
be the permutation of
(1,2,\cdots,n_{\textnormal{T}})
associated by
\mathbb{P}_{\pi}
. Consider the directional derivative that decreases the maximum of
\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle-\mathbf{e}_{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\nabla\tilde{f}_{0}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{e}_{1}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\left(2\mathbb{G}_{\pi}{\mathbf{z}}+\mathbf{d}_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{h}_{\pi(1)}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}{\mathbf{h}_{\pi(i)}}z_{i}-\mathbf{h}_{\pi(1)}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}{\mathbf{h}_{\pi(1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{h}_{\pi(1)}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}{\mathbf{h}_{\pi(1)}}\left(1-2x_{\pi(1)}\right),

(65)

which is nonpositive when the maximum coordinate

.
Thus, we immediately know
\mathbf{x}^{\star}\preccurlyeq\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}\bm{1}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}
.

Next, we consider another directional derivative that increases the minimum of
\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\nabla\tilde{f}_{0}(\mathbf{z})=\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}\left(2\mathbb{G}_{\pi}{\mathbf{z}}-\mathbf{d}_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\mathbf{h}_{\pi(n_{\textnormal{T}})}^{\textnormal{{\tiny T}}}{\mathbf{h}_{\pi(i)}}\left(2z_{i}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 0,

(66)

which implies that, in order to minimize the objective function

\bm{x}^{\star}\succcurlyeq\alpha_{n_{\textnormal{T}}}\bm{1}
.

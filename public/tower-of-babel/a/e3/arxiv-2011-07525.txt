Abstract
. We show that a class of Dirichlet series 𝔄 # {{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#} that is much larger than the extended Selberg class S # {\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#} , and also contains the standard as well as the tensor product, exterior square and symmetric square L L -functions of automorphic L L -functions of G ​ L n GL_{n} over number fields, does not have any elements of degrees between 1 1 and 2 2 . 1.
Introduction

Let
s=\sigma+it\in{\mathbb{C}}
and let
F(s)
be a non-zero meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
We consider the following conditions on
F(s)
.

(P1)

The function
F(s)
is given by a
Dirichlet series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}

with abscissa of absolute convergence
\sigma_{a}\geq 1/2
.

(P2)

There is a polynomial
P(s)
such that
P(s)F(s)

extends to an entire function, and such that in any
vertical strip
\sigma_{1}\leq\text{Re}(s)\leq\sigma_{2}
,
P(s)F(s)\ll(1+t)^{M}
for some
M\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(P3)

There exist a real number
Q>0
, a complex number
\omega
such that

|\omega|=1
, and a function
G(s)
of the form

G(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r^{\prime}}\Gamma(\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}s+\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1},

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}>0
,
\mu_{j},\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
and
\Gamma(s)
denotes the usual gamma function, such that

\Phi(s):=Q^{s}G(s)F(s)=\omega\overline{\Phi(1-\bar{s})}.

(1.2)

We will denote by
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
the set of meromorphic functions satsifying (P1)-(P3). We set

d_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}-2\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r^{\prime}}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}

In
[
Rag20
]
(see Theorem
2.1
in Section
2.1
),
where the class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
was introduced, we showed that
d_{F}
is an invariant
of
F(s)
, that is, it does not depend on the choice of gamma factors
G(s)

in (
1.2
). The subset of
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
consisting of elements of degree

d
will be denoted
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}
and the subset of elements of degree
d
with
abscissa of absolute convergence
\sigma_{a}\leq\nu
will be denoted
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}(\nu)
.
The main theorem of this paper is

Theorem 1.1
.

For
1<d<2
, we have
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}_{d}=\emptyset
.

In
[
KP11
]
, Kaczorowski and Perelli proved the result above for the
extended Selberg class
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
, that is, when
\nu\leq 1
,
r^{\prime}=0
,
\text{Re}(\mu_{j})\geq 0
for
all
1\leq j\leq r
, and
P(s)=(s-1)^{m}
for some integer
m\geq 0
.
This is perhaps the most difficult and interesting of their many
foundational theorems about
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
. We emphasise that our proof not only
works for the (considerably) larger class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
and uses very different techniques, but also gives a much
simpler and shorter argument even for the class
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
.

As the second author has explained in
[
Rag20
]
, the primary motivation
for working in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
rather than in
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
is that the former contains a much larger number
of series that arise naturally from automorphic forms.
Dispensing with the requirement of absolute convergence for
\text{Re}(s)>1
allows
us to incorporate many more series within our theory.
The class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}(1)
is already known
to contain the standard
L
-functions as well as the exterior square,
symmetric square and tensor product
L
-functions associated
to
globally generic
unitary automorphic representations
of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
, where
k
is a number field and
{\mathbb{A}}_{k}
its
ring of adèles. The class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
further contains the

L
-functions associated to even non-generic automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
.
The simplest such example is
\zeta(s-1/2)\zeta(s+1/2)
, which is the
standard
L
-function associated to the trivial representation
of
GL_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. The
L
-series associated to Siegel modular forms
give another class of examples which often do not belong
to
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
but are part of the class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
.

For the standard
L
-functions of unitary cuspidal automorphic

L
-functions, establishing the condition
\text{Re}(\mu_{j})\geq 0
,
1\leq j\leq r
,
involves the Generalised Ramanujan Conjecture at infinity. This has not yet been
proven even in the case of the
L
-functions of cuspidal Maass eigenforms,
where the condition is equivalent to the famous Eigenvalue Conjecture
of Selberg. Thus, many of the most important
L
-functions in number theory
are easily seen to lie in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
or even in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}(1)
,
but are not known to lie in
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
.

We remark that A. Booker has also defined a class of Dirichlet series
\BOONDOX{L}

in
[
Boo15
]
similar to
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
which contains
the standard
L
-functions of unitary cuspidal automorphic representations
of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. His requirement of average Ramanujan bounds for the
mean-square of the coefficients and an Euler product like axiom
makes his class more restrictive than ours. For instance,
the tensor product, exterior square and symmetric square

L
-functions of even unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of

{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
are not known to lie in
\BOONDOX{L}
. Moreover,
L
-functions
like
\zeta(s-1/2)\zeta(s+1/2)
which are associated to non-generic representations
do not belong to
\BOONDOX{L}
.
All linear combinations of series in
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
with the
same gamma factors and the same factors
\omega Q^{s}
in the
functional equation belong
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
but not, in general, to
\BOONDOX{L}
, since elements of
the former class need not have an Euler product. Our class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
thus contains many
examples of series which do not lie in
\BOONDOX{L}
even conjecturally, contains all the series that
are known to belong to
\BOONDOX{L}
, and almost
certainly contains all of
\BOONDOX{L}
(which a priori allows somewhat more general
gamma factors). Theorem
1.1
may thus be seen as a generalisation and extension of
Theorem 1.7 of
[
Boo15
]
, which asserts that
\BOONDOX{L}_{d}=\emptyset
for
1<d<5/3
.

Allowing Dirichlet series to have a finite number of poles at
arbitrary locations also results in a larger class, since even the standard

L
-functions of
GL_{n}
may have poles at points other than
1
.
The removal of the condition
r^{\prime}=0
almost certainly does
not enlarge our class further but does enable us to treat
quotients of (automorphic)
L
-functions (these last two
weaker hypotheses are also features of
\BOONDOX{L}
).
For further motivation for introducing the class
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
we refer
to
[
Rag20
]
, where the contrasts with
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}
and
\BOONDOX{L}

and some applications are discussed in greater detail.

Remark 1.2
.

As we have also remarked in
[
Rag20
]
, we could allow an even
more general functional equation of the form
\Phi(s)=Q^{s}\Psi(1-s)
where
\Psi(s)=\tilde{G}(s)B(s)

for some Dirichlet series

1.1.
A brief sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1

We give an outline of
the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Since
\sigma_{a}
is the abscissa of absolute convergence of
F(s)
, we have the
estimate
(\sum_{T<n<4T}|a_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}=\Omega(T^{\sigma_{a}-1/2-\varepsilon})
for every

\varepsilon>0
, where
a_{n}
are the coefficients of the Dirichlet series
F(s)
.
The aim is to get an
O
-estimate for this
\ell^{2}
-norm incompatible with the

\Omega
-estimate above. This is done by using the integral transform (for
T>0
large enough that

F(1/2+it)
is holomorphic when
t>T
)

H(\alpha,T):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\int_{4\pi\alpha T}^{6\pi\alpha T}F(1/2+it)e^{it\log\left(\frac{t}{2\pi e\alpha}\right)-i\frac{\pi}{4}}dt,

first introduced in
[
Sou05
]
to treat the case
d=1
. A standard argument
allows one to replace
F(1/2+it)
by an everywhere convergent Dirichlet series and
error terms
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, the former arising from the poles of
F(1/2+it+w)
, and the latter
from the inverse Mellin transform of the function
F(1/2+it+w)\Gamma(w/p)
on a line in
the half-plane
\sigma<0
. Applying the integral transform now yields

H(\alpha,T)=\sqrt{2\pi}\sum_{T<n<4T}a_{n}e^{-2\pi i\alpha n}e^{(-n/T^{1+\rho_{1}})^{p}}+E+R_{1}+R_{2},

where
E
is an error term arising from an application of the stationary phase method,
and
R_{i}
arises from
r_{i}
,
i=1,2
by applying the transform above.
An important new input is an improvement in the method of stationary phase
for oscillatory integrals due to Bombieri and Bourgain (Lemma 3.3 of
[
BB04
]
),
which is elementary but powerful, and allows the much better estimate

E=O(T^{\sigma_{a}-1/2-\varepsilon_{1}})
,
for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. This gives a saving of
T
over the trivial estimate and
a saving of
T^{2/5}
over previous methods.
The real difficulty, however, is to obtain a similar saving for
R_{2}
.
Our key idea is to use Lemma 3.3 of
[
BB04
]
for estimating this error term.
This involves using the functional equation and then interchanging the sum and the
integral by passing to the domain of absolute convergence of
F(s)
.
This yields the same bound for
R_{2}
as the one obtained for
E
. Thus, we get

H(\alpha,T)=\sqrt{2\pi}\sum_{T<n<4T}a_{n}e^{-2\pi i\alpha n}e^{(-n/T^{1+\rho_{1}})^{p}}+O(T^{\sigma_{a}-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon_{1}}),

for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, when
\alpha
and
T
are chosen sufficiently large (see
equation
3.1
). On the other hand, applying the functional equation to
F(s)
first
and then following the procedure above, gives
H(\alpha,T)=O(T^{\sigma_{a}-1/2-\varepsilon_{1}})

(as one sees in equation (
4.7
)).
These two estimates for
H(\alpha,T)
together yield
O(T^{\sigma_{a}-1/2-\varepsilon_{1}})
for our

\ell^{2}
-norm, for some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, contradicting the
\Omega
-estimate above.

With the ideas above, we are able to avoid the delicate and technical analysis of the Fox
hypergeometric functions and other complications that arise in

[
KP02
]
and
[
KP11
]
altogether, and obtain a more direct proof
of our more general theorem. Soundararajan has remarked in
[
Sou05
]
that his methods
would work for
{\BOONDOX{S}}^{\#}_{d}
when
d<5/3
, but we have actually been able to extend them to
1<d<2
,
and for the much larger class of functions
{{\mathfrak{A}}}^{\#}
, because of our
more refined handling of the error terms.

2.3.
Replacing

by a convergent Dirichlet series

In this subsection we prove a lemma allowing one to represent the
function
F(z+it)
by the sum of a Dirichlet series convergent at all points

where

is holomorphic, and error
terms over which one has considerable control. A proof of a slightly different

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
w=u+iv
,

,
p>0
and
d>0
.
If
F(s)
is holomorphic at
s=z+it
and
0<\eta<1-x+p-\sigma_{a}
, we have

F(z+it)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}e^{-(n/X)^{p}}}{n^{z+it}}+r_{1}(t)+r_{2}(t),

(2.6)

where
r_{1}(t):=r_{1}(z,t,X)=O(X^{\sigma_{a}-x}e^{-|t|/p})

is identically zero if
F(z)
is entire, and

r_{2}(t):=\frac{1}{2\pi ip}\int_{u=-p+\eta}F(z+it+w)X^{w}\Gamma(w/p)dw\ll O(t^{d(\frac{1}{2}+p-x-\eta)}X^{-p+\eta}),

(2.7)

where

Proof.

When
c>\sigma_{a}
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi ip}\int_{u=c}F(z+it+w)X^{w}\Gamma(w/p)dw=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}e^{-(n/X)^{p}}}{n^{z+it}}.

If

integration from

to

we will cross the poles of the
integrand of the form

(this shifting of the line is made possible by the Phragmén-Lindelöf
principle, since (P2) shows that

has at most polynomial growth in lacunary vertical strips).
The residue at

is

. For any pole

of

, since

is the abscissa of

now follows easily from (

).
This completes the proof.

(3.7)

As before, we take

Combining this with (

(3.8)

Estimating the error terms

\displaystyle R_{2}(\alpha,T)=\frac{\omega}{2\pi ip\sqrt{\alpha}}\int_{u=u_{1}}\int_{K_{T}}Q^{-2w-2it}

(3.12)

To estimate

For estimating

If

\displaystyle J_{n}^{(1)}=(DQ^{2})^{d(p-\eta_{1})}(x_{n}+v)^{d(p-\eta_{1})}(f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x_{n}))^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Using (

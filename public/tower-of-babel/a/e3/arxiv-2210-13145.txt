Relative energy inequality and weak-strong uniqueness for an isothermal non-Newtonian compressible fluid
Richard Andrášik, Václav Mácha, Rostislav Vodák

Abstract
. Our paper deals with three-dimensional nonsteady Navier-Stokes equations for non-Newtonian compressible fluids. It contains a derivation of the relative energy inequality for the weak solutions to these equations. We show that the standard energy inequality implies the relative energy inequality. Consequently, the relative energy inequality allows us to achieve a weak-strong uniqueness result. In other words, we present that the weak solution of the Navier-Stokes system coincides with the strong solution emanated from the same initial conditions as long as the weak solution exists.

Introduction

This paper deals with the analysis of solution to the system describing a compressible non-Newtonian fluid – a fluid whose viscosity is non-constant, namely it depends on the shear rate in our case.

The study of such system dates back to Mamontov

10
]

2.
Relative energy inequality

Let us define

H(\rho)=\rho\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{1}{z}\ \mathrm{d}z=\rho\ln{\rho}.

Let us consider smooth functions
r
and
\mathbf{U}
such that
r
is strictly positive and
\mathbf{U}
satisfies the no-slip boundary conditions. Following
[
5
]
,
relative entropy

\mathcal{E}([\rho,\mathbf{u}]|[r,\mathbf{U}])
is defined as follows

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}([\rho,\mathbf{u}]|[r,\mathbf{U}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{U}|^{2}+H(\rho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{U}|^{2}+\rho\ln{\frac{\rho}{r}-(\rho-r)}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

We would like to point out that the function

\rho\mapsto H(\rho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r),\ \rho,\ r\geq 0.

is strictly convex with minimum
0
attained at
\rho=r
. Therefore, for every
0<\underline{r}<r<\overline{r}<\infty
there is a positive constant
c
such that

(2.2)

H(\rho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r)>c(\rho-r)^{2}

whenever
\rho\in\left(\frac{\underline{r}}{2},2\overline{r}\right)
, and

(2.3)

H(\rho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\rho-r)>c|\rho-r|

whenever
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\left(\frac{\underline{r}}{2},2\overline{r}\right)
.

Similarly as in
[
7
]
, a
suitable weak solution
to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is a couple
[\rho,\mathbf{u}]
satisfying (
1.3
)-(
1.4
), boundary and initial conditions, and the following relative energy inequality for all

and
{\bf U}
belonging to the class specified in Theorem
2.1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}([\rho,\mathbf{u}]|[r,\mathbf{U}])(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left(P(|D\mathbf{u}|)D\mathbf{u}-P(|D\mathbf{U}|)D\mathbf{U}\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{U}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}([\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0}]|[r(\cdot,0),\mathbf{U}(\cdot,0)])+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},r,\mathbf{U})\ \mathrm{d}t,

where the remainder
\mathcal{R}
is defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},r,\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{u}\nabla\mathbf{U})\cdot(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{u})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}P(|D\mathbf{U}|)D\mathbf{U}:(D\mathbf{U}-D\mathbf{u})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{f}\cdot(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{U})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left((r-\rho)\frac{\partial_{t}r}{r}+\frac{\nabla r}{r}\cdot(r\mathbf{U}-\rho\mathbf{u})\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{U}(r-\rho)\mathrm{d}x.

If we set
r=r_{0}=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\ \mathrm{d}x
and
\mathbf{U}=0
, then following the definition of the relative entropy (
2.1
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}([\rho,\mathbf{u}]|[r_{0},0])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\rho\ln{\rho}-\rho\ln{r_{0}}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}([\rho_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0}]|[r_{0},0])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}+\rho_{0}\ln{\rho_{0}}-\rho_{0}\ln{r_{0}}\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},r_{0},0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}\ \mathrm{d}x.

Since
\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\rho_{0})\ln{r_{0}}\ \mathrm{d}x=\ln{r_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(\rho-\rho_{0})\ \mathrm{d}x=0
, (

) reduces to the standard energy inequality (

). Thus, the relative energy inequality (

) implies the standard energy inequality (

). In section

, we show that this implication holds also the other way round.

.

Let
\rho
and

3.
Weak-Strong uniqueness

The relative entropy inequality can be used to prove the weak-strong uniqueness principle. In other words, it enables us to show that weak and strong solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with the same boundary and initial conditions coincide as long as the strong solution exists.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\rho}
,
\tilde{\bf u}
be a strong solution to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition and
{\tilde{\rho}}(x,t)\geq C,\ C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. If
P
satisfies (
1.6
)–(
1.11
), then every weak solution
\rho
,
{\bf u}
emanating from the same initial data
\tilde{\rho}(\cdot,0)
and
\tilde{\bf u}(\cdot,0)
is equal to
\tilde{\rho}
and
\tilde{\bf u}
.

Remark 3.2
.

We assume the strong solution satisfies (
1.1
), (
1.2
) pointwisely and all terms in this formulation are well defined. In particular,
\tilde{\varrho}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\langle 0,T\rangle)
and
\tilde{\bf u}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\langle 0,T\rangle)^{3}
with
\nabla_{x}{\bf u}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times\langle 0,T\rangle)^{3\times 3}
. It is worth to mention that the proof presented below works also for weak solutions with sufficient regularity.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Let us consider
r=\tilde{\rho}
and
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{\tilde{u}}
, where
[\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}}]
is the strong solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and
\tilde{\rho}\geq C
,

. The idea of the proof is to show that all terms in (
2.5
) can be bounded by the means of the left-hand side of (
2.4
) in order to use a Gronwall type argument. Recall that the assumptions on the strong solution imply that there is not any vacuum region, i.e., it holds that
0<\underline{r}<\tilde{\rho}<\overline{r}<\infty
for appropriately chosen constants
\underline{r}
and
\overline{r}
.

First, we introduce a decomposition of a general function
G=G(\rho)
into the essential and residual part, namely,

G=G_{\mathrm{ess}}+G_{\mathrm{res}}

where

G_{\mathrm{ess}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}G\quad\mbox{on }\rho\in\left(\frac{1}{2}\underline{r},2\overline{r}\right)\\
0\quad\mbox{otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Due to the convexity of

one can deduce the following coercivity properties (see also (

) and (

(3.1)

\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}]|[\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}}])\geq C\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho|{\bf u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|^{2}+|\rho-\tilde{\rho}|^{2}_{\mathrm{ess}}+1_{\mathrm{res}}+\rho_{\mathrm{res}}\right)\ \mathrm{d}x.

According to (
2.5
), it holds that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\partial_{t}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}+\mathbf{u}\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{f})\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}:(D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-D\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left((\tilde{\rho}-\rho)\frac{\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}+(\tilde{\rho}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\rho\mathbf{u})\cdot\frac{\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}(\tilde{\rho}-\rho)\ \mathrm{d}x.

Since
[\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}}]
is a strong solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), we can rearrange the momentum equation (

) as follows:

\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}\left(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{\rho}\partial_{t}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}+\mathrm{div}(\tilde{\rho}\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\mathbf{\tilde{u}}+\tilde{\rho}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)-\mathbf{f}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}\mathrm{div}\left(P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)-\frac{\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}

which by the means of the continuity equation (
1.1
) reduces into

\partial_{t}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}+\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{f}=\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}\mathrm{div}\left(P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)-\frac{\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}.

Hence, the first term in (

) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\partial_{t}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}+\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{f})\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\rho(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho}{\tilde{\rho}}\mathrm{div}\left(P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\rho(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x,

which leads to (using the identity
\int_{\Omega}D\mathbf{v}:D\mathbf{w}\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}(D\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{w}\ \mathrm{d}x
):

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})\mathrm{div}\left(P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\tilde{\rho}-\rho)\left(\frac{\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}+\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot\frac{\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}(\tilde{\rho}-\rho)\ \mathrm{d}x.

With respect to the continuity equation (
1.1
), the last two terms in (
3.4
) cancel each other out, because

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}+\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot\frac{\nabla\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}+\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}+\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot\nabla\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{\rho}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\rho}+\mathrm{div}(\tilde{\rho}\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\right)=0.

Thus

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,\mathbf{u},\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})\mathrm{div}\left(P(|D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}|)D\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\right)\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x.

Concerning the first term of the remainder (
3.5
), we have (recall that
\nabla\tilde{u}
is a bounded function)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{\tilde{u}})\nabla\mathbf{\tilde{u}}\cdot(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u})\ \mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathbf{\tilde{u}}-\mathbf{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathcal{E}([\rho,\mathbf{u}]|[\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{\tilde{u}}]).

Regarding the second term in (
3.5
) we decompose it into two parts and we use the regularity of the strong solution to get

\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})\mathrm{div}(P(|D\tilde{\bf u}|)D\tilde{\bf u})\cdot(\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u})\ \mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho|_{{\mathrm{ess}}}|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho|_{{\mathrm{res}}}|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Next,

I_{1}\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho|_{\mathrm{ess}}\sqrt{\rho}|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x\\
\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho|_{\mathrm{ess}}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\rho|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\right)\leq c\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}],[\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\bf u}])

according to (
3.1
). We split
I_{2}
once again as

I_{2}=C\int_{\Omega\cap\{\rho>2\overline{r}\}}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho||\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Omega\cap\{\rho<\frac{1}{2}\underline{r}\}}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho||\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x

where the first integral may be treated in the same way as
I_{1}
and second integral is estimated with the help of the Korn inequality as follows

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega\cap\{\rho<\frac{1}{2}\underline{\rho}\}}|\tilde{\rho}-\rho||\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|\ \mathrm{d}x\\
\leq C\int_{\Omega}1^{q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{res}}\ \mathrm{d}x+\delta\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\bf u}-{\bf u}|^{q}\ \mathrm{d}x\leq C\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}],[\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\bf u}])+\delta\int_{\Omega}|D\tilde{\bf u}-D{\bf u}|^{q}

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
and
\delta>0
might be as small as needed – in particular, we choose
\delta
such that (using (
1.11
))

\delta\int_{\Omega}|D\tilde{\bf u}-D{\bf u}|^{q}\ \mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(P(|D\tilde{\bf u}|)D\tilde{\bf u}-P(|D{\bf u}|)D{\bf u})(D\tilde{\bf u}-D{\bf u})\ \mathrm{d}x

so the last term of (
3.7
) can be absorbed in the left hand side of (
2.4
). Summing up, (
2.4
) yields

\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}]|[\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\bf u}])(\tau)\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}]|[\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\bf u}])(s)\ \mathrm{d}s

and, consequently,

\mathcal{E}([\rho,{\bf u}]|[\tilde{\rho},\tilde{\bf u}])\equiv 0.

4.
Assumptions on
P

The most restrictive assumption on
P
is (
1.11
) which is not needed in the proof of the existence given by Mamontov. This assumption is discussed throughout this section. First, we show that a natural choice of
P
satisfies the condition (
1.11
). Next, we formulate a replacement assumption which is more convenient than (
1.11
).

4.1.
P(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}M(z)

Let us consider a function

P(z)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\frac{M(z)}{z^{2}}$,&for $z\neq 0$,\\
$0$,&for $z=0$.\end{tabular}\right.

It satisfies all conditions (
1.6
)-(
1.10
) and it satisfies also (
1.11
) with
q=3
. Indeed, recall that

P(z)z=\frac{z}{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{6}+\frac{z^{3}}{24}+\ldots=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{i}}{(i+1)!}

and therefore we may write
P(z)z=F^{\prime}(z)+G^{\prime}(z)
where
F(z)=\frac{z^{2}}{4}
and

G(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{s^{2}}{6}+\frac{s^{3}}{24}+\frac{s^{4}}{120}+\ldots\ \mathrm{d}s.

Further, we have

P(|D{\bf u}|)D{\bf u}=P(|D{\bf u}|)|D{\bf u}|\frac{D{\bf u}}{|D{\bf u}|}=\frac{\partial}{\partial D}\left(F(D{\bf u})+G(D{\bf u})\right).

Hence,

(4.1)

\left(P(|D\mathbf{u}|)D\mathbf{u}-P(|D\mathbf{v}|)D\mathbf{v}\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}\right)\\
=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial D}F(|D\mathbf{u}|)-\frac{\partial}{\partial D}F(|D\mathbf{v}|)\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}\right)\\
+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial D}G(|D\mathbf{u}|)-\frac{\partial}{\partial D}G(|D\mathbf{v}|)\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}\right).

Since
F
complies with the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, we can apply Lemma 21 from
[
4
]
and deduce a lower bound for the first term in (
4.1
) as follows:

(4.2)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial D}F(|D\mathbf{u}|)-\frac{\partial}{\partial D}F(|D\mathbf{v}|)\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}\right)\\
\geq CF^{\prime\prime}(|D\mathbf{u}|+|D\mathbf{v}|)|D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}|^{2}\geq C|D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}|^{2}

The second term in (
4.1
) is non-negative. This follows since
D{\bf u}:D{\bf v}\leq\frac{1}{2}|D{\bf u}|+\frac{1}{2}|D{\bf v}|
and thus

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial D}G(|D\mathbf{u}|)-\frac{\partial}{\partial D}G(|D\mathbf{v}|)\right):\left(D\mathbf{u}-D\mathbf{v}\right)\\
=\left(\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}D{\bf u}-\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}D{\bf v}\right):(D{\bf u}-D{\bf v})\\
=\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}D{\bf u}:D{\bf u}-\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}D{\bf u}:D{\bf v}\\
-\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}D{\bf v}:D{\bf u}+\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}D{\bf v}:D{\bf v}\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}|D{\bf u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}|D{\bf v}|^{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}|D{\bf u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}|D{\bf v}|^{2}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf u}|)}{|D{\bf u}|}-\frac{G^{\prime}(|D{\bf v}|)}{|D{\bf v}|}\right)\left(|D{\bf u}|^{2}-|D{\bf v}|^{2}\right)\geq 0

where we used the fact that
G^{\prime}(z)/z
is non-negative and increasing for
z\geq 0
.

4.2.
Further note on (
1.11
)

In general, we claim that (
1.11
) is fulfilled whenever
P
is a non-decreasing function satisfying

(4.3)

P^{\prime}(z)\geq 0\ \mbox{and there exists }c>0\mbox{ such that }P(z)\geq cz^{\alpha}\\
\mbox{for all }z\geq 0\ \mbox{and some }\alpha>0\,.

In order to prove that (
4.3
) implies (
1.11
) we start with a generalization of
[
3
, Sect I.4., Lemma 4.4, p. 14]
. The following holds for every
U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
:

(P(|U|)U-P(|V|)V):(U-V)\\
=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left[P(|sU+(1-s)V|)(sU+(1-s)V)\right]\ ds\right):(U-V)\\
=\int_{0}^{1}P(|sU+(1-s)V|)|U-V|^{2}\ ds\\
+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{P^{\prime}(|sU+(1-s)V)|}{|sU+(1-s)V|}|(sU+(1-s)V):(U-V)|^{2}\ ds\\
\geq\int_{0}^{1}P(|sU+(1-s)V|)|U-V|^{2}\ ds=(*).

Now, let us assume for a while that
|U|\geq|U-V|
. Then

(*)=\int_{0}^{1}P(|U+(1-s)(V-U)|)|U-V|^{2}\ ds\\
\geq\int_{0}^{1}P\left(|\ |U|-(1-s)|V-U|\ |\right)|U-V|^{2}\ ds\\
\geq\int_{0}^{1}P(s|U-V|)|U-V|^{2}\ ds\\
=|U-V|\int_{0}^{|U-V|}P(z)\ dz\geq c|U-V|^{2+\alpha}.

On the other hand, if
|U|<|U-V|
, we deduce that

(*)=|U-V|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{P(|sU+(1-s)V|)|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}}{|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}}\ ds=(1)\,.

Since

|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}=|U+(1-s)(V-U)|^{2}\leq(2-s)^{2}|V-U|^{2},

we derive

(1)\geq|U-V|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{P(|sU+(1-s)V|)|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}}{(2-s)^{2}|U-V|^{2}}\ ds\\
\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}P(|sU+(1-s)V|)|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}\ ds\\
\geq c\int_{0}^{1}Q(|sU+(1-s)V|^{2})|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}\ ds=(2),

where
Q(z)=z^{\alpha/2}
. Since
G:=z^{1+\alpha/2}
is a convex and non-decreasing function, we can use Jensen’s inequality to deduce

(2)\geq C\,G\left(\int_{0}^{1}|sU+(1-s)V|^{2}\ ds\right)\\
=C\,G\left(\frac{1}{3}(|U|^{2}+|V|^{2}+U:V)\right)=(3).

Further,

|U|^{2}<|U-V|^{2}\Rightarrow 2U:V<|V|^{2},

leads to

|U|^{2}+|V|^{2}+U:V\\
=\frac{9}{10}|U|^{2}+\frac{9}{10}|V|^{2}+\frac{6}{5}U:V+\frac{1}{10}|U|^{2}+\frac{1}{10}|V|^{2}-\frac{2}{10}U:V\\
\geq\frac{9}{10}|V|^{2}-\frac{6}{10}|V|^{2}+\frac{1}{10}(|U|^{2}+|V|^{2}-2U:V)\geq\frac{1}{10}|U-V|^{2}.

Consequently,

(3)\geq C\,G\left(c|U-V|^{2}\right)=C|U-V|^{2+\alpha}.

We combine the two previous estimate to deduce that (
4.3
) implies (
1.11
) with
q=2+\alpha
.

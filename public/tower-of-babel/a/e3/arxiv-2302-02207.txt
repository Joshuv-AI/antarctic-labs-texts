The Weyl law for congruence subgroups and arbitrary K_{\infty} -types
Werner Müller

Abstract
. Let G be a reductive algebraic group over {\mathbb{Q}} and \Gamma\subset G({\mathbb{Q}}) an arithmetic subgroup. Let K_{\infty}\subset G({\mathbb{R}}) be a maximal compact subgroup. We study the asymptotic behavior of the counting functions of the cuspidal and residual spectrum, respectively, of the regular representation of G({\mathbb{R}}) in L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})) of a fixed K_{\infty} -type \sigma . A conjecture, which is due to Sarnak, states that the counting function of the cuspidal spectrum of type \sigma satisfies Weyl’s law and the residual spectrum is of lower order growth. Using the Arthur trace formula we reduce the conjecture to a problem about L -functions occurring in the constant terms of Eisenstein series. If G satisfies property (L), introduced by Finis and Lapid, we establish the conjecture. This includes classical groups over a number field.

Introduction

Let
G
be a connected semisimple algebraic group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and

\Gamma\subset G({\mathbb{Q}})
an arithmetic subgroup, which we assume to be torsion
free. A basic problem in the theory of
automorphic forms is the study of the spectral resolution of the regular
representation
R_{\Gamma}
of
G({\mathbb{R}})
in
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
. Of particular
importance is the determination of the structure of the discrete spectrum.
Let
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))

be the discrete part of
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
, i.e., the closure of the span
of all irreducible subrepresentations of
R_{\Gamma}
. Denote by
R_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k dis}}

the corresponding restriction of
R_{\Gamma}
. Denote by
\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{R}}))
the
set of isomorphism classes of irreducible unitary representations of
G({\mathbb{R}})
,
which occur in
R_{\Gamma}
. By definition we have

(
1.1
)

R_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k dis}}=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{R}}))}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\pi,

where

m_{\Gamma}(\pi)=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{G({\mathbb{R}})}(\pi,R_{\Gamma})=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{G({\mathbb{R}})}(\pi,R_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k dis}})

is the multiplicity with which
\pi
occurs in
R_{\Gamma}
.
Apart from special cases, as for example discrete series representations, one
cannot hope to describe the multiplicity function
m_{\Gamma}
on
\Pi(G({\mathbb{R}}))

explicitly. Therefor it is feasible to study asymptotic questions such as
the limit multiplicity problem
[
FLM2
]
and the Weyl law, which is the
subject of this article.

To begin with we recall that the discrete spectrum decomposes into the cuspidal
and the residual spectrum. Let
{\bf K}_{\infty}
be a maximal compact subgroup of

G({\mathbb{R}})
. Let
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the center of the
universal enveloping algebra of the complexification of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}

of
G({\mathbb{R}})
. Recall that a cusp form for

is a smooth and right

{\bf K}_{\infty}
-finite function
\phi\colon\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
which is a
simultaneous eigenfunction of
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
and which satisfies

(
1.2
)

\int_{\Gamma\cap N_{P}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash N_{P}({\mathbb{R}})}\phi(nx)dn=0

for all unipotent radicals
N_{P}
of proper rational parabolic subgroups
P
of

G
. By Langlands’ theory of Eisenstein series
[
La1
]
, cusp forms are the
building blocks of the spectral resolution.
We note that each cusp form
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
is
rapidly decreasing on
\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}})
and hence square integrable. Let

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
be the closure of the linear span of all cusp

R_{\Gamma}
to

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
decomposes discretely and

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
is a subspace of
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
. Denote
by
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k res}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
the orthogonal complement of

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
in
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))
. This is the
residual subspace. Let
(\sigma,V_{\sigma})
be an irreducible unitary
representation of
{\bf K}_{\infty}
. Set

(1.3)

L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma):=(L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}))\otimes V_{\sigma})^{{\bf K}_{\infty}}.

Define the subspaces
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
,

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
and
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k res}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
in a
similar way. Then
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k cus}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
is the space of cusp
forms with fixed
{\bf K}_{\infty}
-type
\sigma
.
Let
\widetilde{X}:=G({\mathbb{R}})/{\bf K}_{\infty}
be the Riemannian symmetric space associated
to
G({\mathbb{R}})
and
X=\Gamma\backslash\widetilde{X}
the corresponding locally symmetric
space. Since we assume that
\Gamma
is torsion free,
X
is a manifold. Let

E_{\sigma}\to\Gamma\backslash X
be the locally homogeneous vector bundle
associated to
\sigma
and let
L^{2}(X,E_{\sigma})
be the space
of square integrable sections of
E_{\sigma}
. There is a canonical isomorphism

(1.4)

L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)\cong L^{2}(X,E_{\sigma}).

Let
\Omega_{G({\mathbb{R}})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the Casimir element of
G({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

-\Omega_{G({\mathbb{R}})}\otimes\operatorname{Id}
induces a self-adjoint operator
\Delta_{\sigma}
in the
Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
which is bounded from below. With
respect to the isomorphism (

(1.5)

\Delta_{\sigma}=(\nabla^{\sigma})^{\ast}\nabla^{\sigma}-\lambda_{\sigma}\operatorname{Id},

where
\nabla^{\sigma}
is the canonical invariant connection in
E_{\sigma}
and

\lambda_{\sigma}
denotes the Casimir eigenvalue of
\sigma
. In particular,
if
\sigma_{0}
is the trivial representation, then
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma_{0})\cong L^{2}(X)
and
\Delta_{\sigma_{0}}
equals the Laplacian
\Delta
on
X
.

The restriction of
\Delta_{\sigma}
to the subspace

L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\Gamma\backslash G({\mathbb{R}}),\sigma)
has pure point spectrum consisting of
eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}(\sigma)<\lambda_{1}(\sigma)<\cdots

multiplicities. Let
{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda_{i}(\sigma))
be the eigenspace corresponding to

\lambda_{i}(\sigma)
. Then we define the eigenvalue counting function

N_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k dis}}(\lambda,\sigma)
,
\lambda\geq 0
, by

(1.6)

N_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k dis}}(\lambda,\sigma)=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(\sigma)\leq\lambda}\dim{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda_{i}(\sigma)).

The counting functions
N_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k cus}}(\lambda,\sigma)
and

N_{\Gamma,\mbox{\k res}}(\lambda,\sigma)

the cuspidal and residual spectrum are defined by considering
the restriction of
\Delta_{\sigma}
to the cuspidal and residual subspace,
respectively.
The main goal is to determine the asymptotic behavior of the counting functions
as
\lambda\to\infty
. If
X
is compact, the Weyl law holds.
Recall that for a compact Riemannian manifold
X
of dimension
n
, the Weyl law
states that the number
N_{X}(\lambda)
of eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda
,
counted with multiplicity, of the Laplace operator
\Delta
of
X
satisfies

(1.7)

N_{X}(\lambda)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(X)}{(4\pi)^{n}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}\lambda^{n/2}+o(\lambda^{(n/2})

as
\lambda\to\infty
. A standard method to prove (
1.7
) is the heat
equation method. Using the wave equation, one gets a more precise version with
an estimation of the remainder term:

(1.8)

N_{X}(\lambda)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(X)}{(4\pi)^{n}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}\lambda^{n/2}+O(\lambda^{(n-1)/2})

as
\lambda\to\infty
. This is due to Avakumovic
[
Av
]
and Hörmander.
Without
further assumptions on the Riemannian manifold, the remainder term is optimal

[
Av
]
. More generally, one can consider the Bochner-Laplace operator

\Delta_{E}
for a Hermitian vector bundle
E\to X
with Hermitian connection.
There is a similar formula (
1.7
)
for the eigenvalue counting function
N_{X}(\lambda,E)
of
\Delta_{E}
. The only
difference is the rank of
E
which appears on the right hand side in the
leading coefficient.

For non-uniform lattices
\Gamma
the self-adjoint
operator
\Delta_{\sigma}
has a large continuous spectrum so that alomost all
eigenvalues of
\Delta_{\sigma}
will be embedded in the continuous spectrum which
makes it very difficult to study them. A number of
results are known for the spherical cuspidal spectrum.
The first results concerning the growth of the cuspidal spectrum are due to
Selberg
[
Se1
]
. He proved that for every congruence subgroup

\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
the counting function of the cuspidal spectrum satisfies Weyl’s law, i.e.,
one has

(1.9)

N_{\Gamma}(\lambda)=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{2})}{4\pi}\lambda^{2}-\frac{2m}{\pi}\lambda\log\lambda+O(\lambda),

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
This shows that for congruence subgroups eigenvalues
exist in abundance. On the other hand, based on their work on the dissolution
of cusp forms under deformation of lattices, Phillips and Sarnak
[
Sa2
]

conjectured that except for the Teichmüller space of the once punctured torus,
the point spectrum of the Laplacian on
\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
for a generic
non-uniform lattice
\Gamma
in
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is finite and is contained in

[0,1/4)
. In the more general context of manifolds with cusps
Colin de Verdère
[
CV
]
has shown that under a generic compactly supported
conformal deformation of the metric of a non-compact hyperbolic surface of
finite area all eigenvalues
\lambda\geq 1/4
are dissolved.

If
\operatorname{rank}(G)>1
, the situation is very different.
By the results of Margulis, we have rigidity of irreducible lattices and
irreducible lattices are arithmetic. One expects that arithmetic groups

Conjecture 1.1

Let

There are some general results concerning the conjecture. Let
G
be a
connected real semisimple Lie group,

a maximal compact subgroup of

,
and

which holds for every
\nu\in\Pi(K)
. Concerning the residual spectrum,
it was proved in
[

that for a general lattice one has

(1.11)

However, this is not the optimal bound that one expects. In general,
one would expect
that the residual spectrum is of order
O(\lambda^{n/2})
and for arithmetic
groups of order
O(\lambda^{(n-1)/2})
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Conjecture (
1.1
) has been verified in a number of cases. Most
of the results are obtained for the spherical spectrum. The first result in
higher rank is due to S.D. Miller
[
Mil
]

who established the Weyl law for spherical cusp forms for

, where

is a split adjoint semisimple group
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
The method is different. It uses Hecke operators to eliminate the

,

[
Mu3
]
. However, the argument is simplified and strengthened, which

Recently T. Finis and E. Lapid

]

is a simply connected,

. The

LV
]
, but in a slightly different way. The

For the non-spherical case,

satisfies property (L) which was introduced by Finis
and Lapid
[
FL1
, Definition 3.4]
.
In this way we get (
1.18
).

To prove the Weyl law, we use the Arthur trace formula. We will work with
groups over a number field
F
. However, in order to explain the method
we will simply assume that
F={\mathbb{Q}}
. We proceed as in
[
Mu3
]
. We choose test functions

\phi^{\nu}_{t}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G({\mathbb{A}})^{1})
,
t>0
, which at the infinite place are
obtained from the heat kernel
H_{t}^{\nu}
of the Bochner-Laplace operator

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\nu}
on the symmetric space
\widetilde{X}=G({\mathbb{R}})^{1}/{\bf K}_{\infty}

and which at the finite places is given by the normalized characteristic
function of
K_{f}
(see (
8.11
) for the precise definition).
Then we insert
\phi_{t}^{\nu}
into the spectral side
J_{\text{spec}}
of the trace
formula and study the asymptotic behavior of
J_{\text{spec}}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})
as

t\to 0
. The spectral side is a sum of distributions
J_{\text{spec},M}
associated
to conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups
M
of
G
. For
M=G
we have

(1.21)

J_{\text{spec},G}(\phi^{\nu}_{t})=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}))}m(\pi)e^{t\lambda_{\pi_{\infty}}}\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi_{f}}^{K_{f}})\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi_{\infty}}\otimes V_{\nu})^{K_{\infty}},

which is the contribution of the discrete spectrum to the spectral side.
For
M\neq G
, the main ingredients of the distributions

J_{\text{spec},M}
are logarithmic derivatives of intertwining operators. The
intertwining operators can be normalized by certain meromorphic functions.
Then the logarithmic derivatives of the intertwining operators are expressed
in terms of logarithmic derivatives of the normalizing
factors and logarithmic derivatives of the local normalized intertwining
operators. In fact, we only need to control integrals of logarithmic
derivatives which simplifies the problem. To deal with the integrals of
logarithmic derivatives of the normalizing factors, we use
property (TWN+)
[
FL1
, Definition 3.3]
. By
[
FL1
, Proposition 3.8]
,
property (TWN+) is a consequence of property (L)
[
FL1
, Definition 3.4]
,
which we assume to be satisfied by
G
. To deal with the local intertwining
operators, we follow essentially the approach used in
[
FLM2
]
. The final
result is Theorem
8.5
, which states that if
G
satisfies
property (L), then

(1.22)

J_{\text{spec}}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})=J_{\text{spec},G}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})+O(t^{-(d-1)/2})

as
t\to 0
.

Next we come to the geometric side
J_{\mbox{\k geom}}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})
. Its asymptotic behavior
as
t\to 0
has been determined in
[
MM2
, Theorem 1.1]
. We will briefly
recall the main steps of the proof and determine the leading coefficient.
By the trace formula we have
J_{\text{spec}}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})=J_{\mbox{\k geom}}(\phi_{t}^{\nu})
, which
together with (
1.21
) and (
1.22
) leads to

(1.23)

\begin{split}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}))}m(\pi)\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi_{f}}^{K_{f}})\dim&\left({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi_{\infty}}\otimes V_{\nu}\right)^{K_{\infty}}\,e^{t\lambda_{\pi_{\infty}}}\\
&=\frac{\dim(\nu)\,\operatorname{vol}(X(K_{f}))}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}t^{-d/2}+O(t^{-(d-1)/2})\end{split}

as
t\to 0
.
Applying Karamata’s theorem, we obtain the adelic Weyl law
(
1.17
).

4.
Eisenstein series and intertwining operators

In this section we recall some basic facts about Eisenstein series and
intertwining operators, which are the main ingredients of the spectral
side of the Arthur trace formula.

Let
M\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
P\in{\mathcal{P}}(M)
with
P=M\ltimes N_{P}
.
Recall that we denote by
\Sigma_{P}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}_{P}^{*}
the set of reduced roots of

T_{M}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{P}
of
N_{P}
.
Let
\Delta_{P}
be the subset of simple roots of
P
, which is a basis for

({\mathfrak{a}}_{P}^{G})^{*}

Write
{\mathfrak{a}}_{P,+}^{*}

\mathfrak{a}_{P,+}^{*}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{*}:\left\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\right\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Sigma_{P}\}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{*}:\left\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\right\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Delta_{P}\}.

Denote by
\delta_{P}
the modulus function of
P({\mathbb{A}})
.
Let
\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
be the Hilbert space completion of

\{\phi\in C^{\infty}(M(F)U_{P}({\mathbb{A}})\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})):\delta_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\cdot x)\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{disc}}(A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}})),\ \forall x\in G({\mathbb{A}})\}

with respect to the inner product

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\int_{A_{M}M(F)N_{P}({\mathbb{A}})\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})}\phi_{1}(g)\overline{\phi_{2}(g)}\ dg.

Let
\alpha\in\Sigma_{M}
.
We say that two parabolic subgroups
P,Q\in{\mathcal{P}}(M)
are
adjacent
along

\alpha

P|^{\alpha}Q

\Sigma_{P}\cap-\Sigma_{Q}=\{\alpha\}

Alternatively,
P
and
Q
are adjacent if the group
\langle P,Q\rangle

generated by
P
and
Q
belongs to
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(M)
(see (
2.4

definition).
Any
R\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is of the form
\langle P,Q\rangle

P,Q
are
the elements of
{\mathcal{P}}(M)
contained in
R
. We have
P|^{\alpha}Q
with

\alpha^{\vee}\in\Sigma_{P}^{\vee}\cap{\mathfrak{a}}^{R}_{M}
.
Interchanging
P
and

\alpha

For any
P\in{\mathcal{P}}(M)
let
H_{P}\colon G({\mathbb{A}})\rightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}_{P}
be the
extension of
H_{M}
to a left

-and right
{\bf K}
-invariant map.
Denote by
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
the dense subspace of
\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
consisting of its

- and
\mathfrak{Z}
-finite vectors,
where
\mathfrak{Z}
is the center of the universal enveloping algebra of

\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
.
That is,
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
is the space of automorphic forms
\phi
on

N_{P}({\mathbb{A}})M(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})
such that

\delta_{P}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\cdot k)
is a square-integrable automorphic form on

A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}})
for all
k\in{\bf K}
.
Let
\rho(P,\lambda)
,
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{M,{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
, be the induced
representation of
G({\mathbb{A}})
on
\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
given by

(\rho(P,\lambda,y)\phi)(x)=\phi(xy)e^{\left\langle\lambda,H_{P}(xy)-H_{P}(x)\right\rangle}.

It is isomorphic to the induced representation

\operatorname{Ind}_{P({\mathbb{A}})}^{G({\mathbb{A}})}\left(L^{2}_{\operatorname{disc}}(A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}}))\otimes e^{\left\langle\lambda,H_{M}(\cdot)\right\rangle}\right).

For
\phi\in{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)
and
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{\ast}_{P,{\mathbb{C}}}
, the associated
Eisenstein series is defined by

(4.1)

E(g,\phi,\lambda):=\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash G(F)}\phi(\gamma g)e^{(\lambda+\rho_{p})(H_{P}(\gamma g))}.

The series converges absolutely and locally uniformly in

and
\lambda

for
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)
sufficiently regular in the positive Weyl chamber of

{\mathfrak{a}}_{P}^{\ast}
(
[
MW
, II.1.5]
.
By Langlands
[
La1
]
the Eisenstein series can be continued analytically
to a meromorphic
function of

. Its singularities lie along hypersurfaces
defined by root equations.

Let
M,M_{1}\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Let
W({\mathfrak{a}}_{M},{\mathfrak{a}}_{M_{1}})
be the set of

onto
{\mathfrak{a}}_{M_{1}}

W_{0}

(G,T_{0})

{\mathfrak{a}}_{M}
. Each

has a representative

in

. Given

,

and

, let

M_{P_{1}|P}(s,\lambda):{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)\to{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P_{1}),\quad\lambda\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{M,{\mathbb{C}}}^{*},

be the standard

for

,

. Let
M=M_{1}
. Then for

and

the identity element, we put

(4.4)

M_{Q|P}(\lambda):=M_{Q|P}(1,\lambda).

Recall that
L^{2}_{\mbox{\k dis}}(A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}}))
decomposes as the completed direct
sum of its
\pi
-isotopic components for
\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(M({\mathbb{A}}))
. We have a
corresponding decomposition of
\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)

\hat{\oplus}_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(M({\mathbb{A}}))}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)

(4.5)

{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(P)=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(M({\mathbb{A}}))}{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P).

We further decompose
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)
according to the action of
{\bf K}_{\infty}
into
isotypic subspaces

(4.6)

{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\Pi({\bf K}_{\infty})}{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)^{\nu}.

Furthermore, for an open compact subgroup
K_{f}\subset G({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
let

{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)^{K_{f}}
be the subspace of
K_{f}
-invariant functions in
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)

and for
\nu\in\Pi({\bf K}_{\infty})

{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)^{K_{f},\nu}
be the
\nu
-isotypic
subspace of
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)^{K_{f}}
.

Given
\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(M({\mathbb{A}}))
, let
(\operatorname{Ind}_{P({\mathbb{A}})}^{G({\mathbb{A}})}(\pi),{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi))
be the
induced representation. Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{P}(\pi)
be the subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi)
,
consisting of all
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi)
which are right
{\bf K}
-finite and right

{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
-finite.
There is a canonical isomorphism of

G({\mathbb{A}}_{f})\times(\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathbb{C}}},{\bf K}_{\infty})
-modules

(4.7)

j_{P}:\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,L^{2}(A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}})))\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{P}^{0}(\pi)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P).

If we fix a unitary structure on
\pi
and endow

\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,L^{2}(A_{M}M(F)\backslash M({\mathbb{A}})))
with the inner product

(A,B)=B^{\ast}A

(which is a scalar operator on the space of
\pi
), the isomorphism
j_{P}

becomes an isometry. Let

(4.8)

M_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda):=M_{Q|P}(\lambda)|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)}

be the restriction of
M_{Q|P}(\lambda)
to the subspace
{\mathcal{A}}^{2}_{\pi}(P)
.
Suppose that
P|^{\alpha}Q
.
The operator
M_{Q|P}(\pi,z):=M_{Q|P}(\pi,z\varpi)
, where
\varpi\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{M}^{\star}

is such that
\langle\varpi,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=1
, admits a
normalization by a global factor

n_{\alpha}(\pi,z)
which is a meromorphic function in
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We may write

(4.9)

M_{Q|P}(\pi,z)\circ j_{P}=n_{\alpha}(\pi,z)\cdot j_{Q}\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes R_{Q|P}(\pi,z))

where
R_{Q|P}(\pi,z)=\otimes_{v}R_{Q|P}(\pi_{v},z)

of the locally defined normalized intertwining operators and

\pi=\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}

[
Ar9
,
\lx@sectionsign
6]
, (cf.
[
Mu2
, (2.17)]

[

]
,
[
Sh2
]
.

For any
P,Q\in{\mathcal{P}}(M)
there exists a sequence of parabolic subgroups

P_{0},...,P_{k}
and roots
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}\in\Sigma_{M}
such that
P=P_{0}
,

Q=P_{k}
, and
P_{i-1}|^{\alpha_{i}}P_{i}
for

. By the product rule for

(4.10)

M_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)=M_{P_{k}|P_{k-1}}(\pi,\lambda)\circ M_{P_{k-1}|P_{k-2}}(\pi,\lambda)\circ\cdots\circ M_{P_{1}|P_{0}}(\pi,\lambda).

Thus the study of the operators
M_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)
is reduced to the case where

Q,P\in{\mathcal{P}}(M)
are adjacent along some root
\alpha\in\Sigma_{M}
. Let

(4.11)

n_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda):=\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{P}\cap\Sigma_{\bar{Q}}}n_{\alpha}(\pi,\lambda(\alpha^{\vee}))

The product is a meromorphic function of
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{M,{\mathbb{C}}}^{\ast}
.
Then
M_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)
is normalized by
n_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)
, i.e.,

(4.12)

M_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)\circ j_{P}=n_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)\cdot j_{Q}\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes R_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)).

Recall that
\pi=\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}
, where
\pi_{v}\in\Pi(M(F_{v}))
. If
{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi_{v})

is the Hilbert space of the induced representation

,
then one has

{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi)\cong\bigotimes_{v}{\mathcal{H}}_{P}(\pi_{v})

is the product of the corresponding local normalized intertwining operators

(4.13)

R_{Q|P}(\pi,\lambda)=\otimes_{v}R_{Q|P}(\pi_{v},\lambda)

5.
Normalizing factors

In this section we consider the global normalizing factors of intertwining
operators. The goal is to estimate the number of singular hyperplanes of
normalizing factors which intersect a given compact set.
The normalizing factors can be expressed in terms of
L
-functions.
To begin with we recall some basic facts about
L
-functions. As above, we
assume that
G
is a reductive group over a number field
F
. Recall that

A_{G}=T_{G_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})^{0}
, where
T_{G_{1}}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-split part of the connected
component of the center of
G_{1}=\operatorname{Res}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)
, viewed as a subgroup of

T_{G_{1}}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and hence of
G({\mathbb{A}}_{F})
.

Recall that we denote by
\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}))
the set of equivalence classes of
automorphic representations of
G({\mathbb{A}})
which occur in the discrete spectrum of

L^{2}(A_{G}G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}}))
.
For any
\pi=\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}))
let
S(\pi)
be the finite set of
places of
F
containing all archimedean places and such that for each finite
place
v\in S(\pi)
at least one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

v
is archimedean.

(2)

F/{\mathbb{Q}}
is ramified at
v
.

(3)

G
is ramified at
v
, i.e., either
G
is not quasi-split over
F_{v}
or

G
does not split over an unramified extension of
F_{v}
.

(4)

For every hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup
K_{v}
of
G(F_{v})
,
\pi_{v}

does not have a nonzero vector which is invariant under
K_{v}
.

Let
S_{\infty}
denote the set of archimedean places of
F
and let
S_{f}(\pi)

denote the set of non-archimedean places in
S(\pi)
. Thus
S(\pi)=S_{\infty}\cup S_{f}(\pi)
. For any
v\in S_{f}(\pi)
let
q_{v}
denote the order of the residue
field of
F_{v}
. Let
S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi)
be the set of rational primes which lie
below the primes in
S_{f}(\pi)
. Also set

S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi):=\{\infty\}\cup S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi\}
.

Let
W_{F}
be the Weil group of
F
and let
{}^{L}G
be the Langlands
L
-group
of
G

[
Bo2
]
. Let
r\colon^{L}G\to\operatorname{GL}(N,{\mathbb{C}})
be a continuous and

W_{F}
-semisimple
N
-dimensional complex representation of
{}^{L}G
. For any

\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}))
and any place
v
of
F
with
v\not\in S(\pi)

let
t_{\pi_{v}}\in^{L}G
be the Hecke-Frobenius parameter of
\pi_{v}
. Then the
local
L
-function
L_{v}(s,\pi,r)
is defined by

(5.1)

L_{v}(s,\pi,r):=\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{Id}-r(t_{\pi_{v}})q_{v}^{-s}\right)^{-1}.

Since
\pi
is unitary, the
|r(t_{\pi_{v}})|
are bounded by
q_{v}^{c}
, where
c

depends only on
G
and
r
,
[
Bo2
]
,
[
La2
]
. Therefore, for
S\supset S(\pi)
the partial
L
-function

(5.2)

L^{S}(s,\pi,r):=\prod_{v\not\in S}L_{v}(s,\pi,r)

converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\operatorname{Re}(s)>c+1
. One of
the goals of the Langlands program is to show that each of these
L
-functions
admits a meromorphic extension to the entire complex plane and satisfies a
functional equation. This is far from being proved. In

[
FL1
, Definition 2.1]
, Finis and Lapid formulated a precise version of the
expected functional equation. According to this definition,
(G,r)
has
property
(FE)
, if for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(G({\mathbb{A}}_{F})
the partial

L
-function
L^{S(\pi)}(s,\pi,r)
admits a meromorphic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}

with a functional equation of the form

(5.3)

L^{S(\pi)}(s,\pi,r)=\left(\prod_{p\in S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi)}\gamma_{p}(s,\pi,r))\right)L^{S(\pi)}(1-s,\pi,r^{\vee}),

where for each
p\in S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi)
,
\gamma_{p}(s,\pi,r)=R_{p}(p^{-s})
for some
rational function
R_{p}
and

(5.4)

\gamma_{\infty}(s,\pi,r)=C_{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s+\alpha_{i}^{\vee})}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+\alpha_{i})}

for certain parameters
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m},\alpha_{1}^{\vee},...,\alpha_{m}^{\vee}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and a constant
C_{\infty}
. By
[
FL1
, Lemma 2.2]
the parameters

\alpha_{1}^{\vee},...,\alpha_{m}^{\vee}
are determined by
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
.
Moreover, the integer
m
is uniquely determined. The parameters
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
are said to be
reduced
, if
\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}
is not a negative
odd integer for any
1\leq i,j\leq m
. By
[
FL1
, Lemma 2.2]
one may choose the
parameters
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
to be reduced. Assuming that this is
satisfied, Finis and Lapid introduce the reduced
L
-factor at the Archimedean
place by

(5.5)

L_{\infty}^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+\alpha_{i}).

Now let
p\in S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi)
. Then by
[
FL1
, (2.7)]
,

\gamma_{p}(s,\pi,r)
can be written in a unique way as

(5.6)

\gamma_{p}(s,\pi,r)=c_{p}p^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right){\mathfrak{e}}_{p}(\pi,r)}P_{p}(p^{-s})/\bar{P}_{p}(p^{s-1}),

where
c_{p}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
,
{\mathfrak{e}}_{p}(\pi,r)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
P_{p}
is a polynomial with

P_{p}(0)=1
such that no zeros
\alpha
and
\beta
of
P_{p}
satisfy
\alpha\bar{\beta}=p^{-1}
. Then Finis and Lapid define the reduced
L
-factor at
p
by

(5.7)

L_{p}^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r):=P_{p}(p^{-s})^{-1},\quad p\in S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi),

and introduce the
reduced completed
L
-function
by

(5.8)

L^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r):=\bigl(\prod_{p\in S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi)}L_{p}^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r)\bigr)L^{S(\pi)}(s,\pi,r).

There is also a corresponding reduced epsilon factor
\epsilon^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r)
,
which is defined by

(5.9)

\epsilon^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r)=c_{\infty}\prod_{p\in S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi)}c_{p}p^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right){\mathfrak{e}}_{p}(\pi,r)}={\mathfrak{n}}(\pi,r)^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\prod_{p\in S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi)}c_{p},

where

(5.10)

{\mathfrak{n}}(\pi,r)=\prod_{p\in S_{{\mathbb{Q}},f}(\pi)}p^{{\mathfrak{e}}_{p}(\pi,r)}.

Then the functional equation (
5.3
) becomes

(5.11)

L^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r)=\epsilon^{\mbox{\k red}}(s,\pi,r)L^{\mbox{\k red}}(1-s,\pi,r^{\vee}).

In
[
FL1
, Definition 2.4]
a stronger version of property
(FE)

is introduced. The pair
(G,r)
is said to satisfy property

(FE+)
, if it satisfies
(FE)
and in addition some uniformity
conditions for
\gamma_{\infty}
and
P_{p}
are fulfilled. For the precise
statement see
[
FL1
, Definition 2.4]
.

The normalizing factors are described in
[
FL1
, Sect. 3]
. To recall the
description, we need to introduce some notation. Let
M\in{\mathcal{L}}

and
\alpha\in\Sigma_{M}
. Let
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
be the Levi subgroup of
M
of
co-rank one, defined in
[
FL1
, p. 254]
, together with the map

p^{\mbox{\k sc}}\colon\tilde{M}^{\mbox{\k sc}}_{\alpha}\to\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
, which is also defined
in
[
FL1
, p. 254]
. Furthermore, let
U_{\alpha}
be the unipotent subgroup of

G
corresponding to
\alpha
. Thus the eigenvalues
of
T_{M}
acting on the Lie algebra of
U_{\alpha}
are positive integer multiples
of
\alpha
. The adjoint action of
{}^{L}M
on
\operatorname{Lie}(^{L}U_{\alpha})
factors through
the composed homomorphism
{}^{L}M\to^{L}\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
. The contragredient
of the adjoint representation of
{}^{L}\tilde{M}_{\alpha}
on
\operatorname{Lie}(^{L}U_{\alpha})
is
decomposed as
\oplus_{j=1}^{l}r_{j}
into irreducible representations
r_{j}
.

By T. Finis and E. Lapid
[
FL1
, Definition 3.4]
,
G
satisfies
property (L), if for any standard Levi subgroup
M
, any
\alpha\in\Sigma_{M}
,
and any irreducible constituent
r=r_{j}
as above, the pair
(\tilde{M}_{\alpha},r)

satisfies properties
(FE+)

[
FL1
, Definition 2.4]
and the
conductor condition
(CC)

[
FL1
, Definition 2.9]
.

Assume that
G
satisfies property
(L)
. Then one can describe the
normalizing
factors in terms of
L
-functions. Let
\pi\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(M({\mathbb{A}}))
and let

n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)
be the normalizing factor as in (
4.9
). First
note that
n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)
satisfies the functional equation

(5.12)

n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)\overline{n_{\alpha}(\pi,-\bar{s})}=1.

Next recall that for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0
,
n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)
factorizes as

(5.13)

n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)=\prod_{v}n_{\alpha,v}(\pi_{v},s).

By
[
FL1
, Lemma 2.13]
there exist
\sigma\in\Pi_{\mbox{\k dis}}(\tilde{M}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{A}}))

and a character
\chi
of
\tilde{M}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{A}})
, which is trivial on

\tilde{M}_{\alpha}(F)p^{\mbox{\k sc}}(\tilde{M}_{\alpha}^{\mbox{\k sc}})
, such
that
\sigma\chi
is a subrepresentation of
\pi|_{\tilde{M}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{A}})}
. Let

(5.14)

m(\sigma,s):=\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{\epsilon^{\mbox{\k red}}(1,\sigma,r_{j})L^{\mbox{\k red}}(js,\sigma,r_{j})}{L^{\mbox{\k red}}(js+1,\sigma,r_{j})}.

Then at the end of the proof of Proposition 3.8 in
[
FL1
, p. 259]
it has
been shown that there exists
C>0
such that

(5.15)

n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)=C\cdot m(\sigma,s)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{l}\prod_{p\in S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\sigma)}\frac{L_{p}^{\mbox{\k red}}(js+1,\sigma,r_{j})}{L_{p}^{\mbox{\k red}}(js,\sigma,r_{j})}\prod_{v\in S(\sigma)}n_{\alpha,v}(\pi,s).

We use this formula to estimate the number of poles of
n_{\alpha}(\pi,s)
.
First we consider the two finite products. For this purpose we need to estimate
the cardinality of
S(\sigma)
and
S_{\mathbb{Q}}(\sigma)
. Recall that the level of

\sigma
is defined as
\operatorname{level}(\sigma)=N({\mathfrak{n}})
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}
is the largest
ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
such that
\sigma^{{\bf K}({\mathfrak{n}})\cap\tilde{M}_{\alpha}}\neq 0
. We obviously
have

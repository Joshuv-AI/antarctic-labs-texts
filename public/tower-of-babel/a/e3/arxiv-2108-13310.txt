Hyperspaces and Function Graphs in Digital Topology
Laurence Boxer

Abstract
We adapt the study of hyperspaces and function spaces from classical topology to digital topology. We define digital hyperspaces and digital function graphs, and study some of their relationships and graphical properties. Key words and phrases: digital topology, digital image, Hausdorff metric, hyperspace, function space MSC: 54B20, 54C35

2.1

•

Often, a

E.g.,

•

-adjacency is 18-adjacency, and

x\leftrightarroweq x^{\prime}

2.2
Digitally continuous functions

In a metric space, the continuity of

is defined to preserve the intuition that if

and

are sufficiently close, then

function. Digital continuity is defined
to preserve connectedness, as at
Definition

using adjacency as our standard of “closeness,” we

Definition 2.1
.

[

]
(generalizing a definition of
[

Let

A function

A function

3
Hyperspaces

The book
[
27
]
is a good source for much of the material
discussed in this section that is taken from classical topology.

In classical topology, given a topological space
X
, we denote by

2^{X}
the set or
hyperspace
of nonempty compact subsets of
X
.
If
X
is a metric space with metric
d
,
2^{X}
becomes a
metric space with the Hausdorff metric, or some other
metric, based on
d
.

Given a digital image
(X,\kappa)
, we seek a somewhat parallel
construction of a graph based on finite subsets of
X
. We let

2^{X}=\{Y~|~\emptyset\neq Y\subset X,~\#Y<\infty\}.

We define the
\kappa^{\prime}
adjacency for
2^{X}
as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\{A,B\}\subset 2^{X}
,
A\neq B
. Then
A\leftrightarrow_{\kappa^{\prime}}B

if and only if given
a\in A
and
b\in B
, there exist

a_{0}\in A
and
b_{0}\in B

such that
a\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}b_{0}
and
b\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}a_{0}
.

The pair
(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})
is a graph or

tolerance space

[
34
]
, the

hyperspace of
(X,\kappa)
.
Note we do not call this hyperspace a digital image, since

2^{X}
is not a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. However, since digital
topology’s notions of continuous functions are defined in terms
of graph adjacency, or, alternately, graph connectedness, they
are naturally applied to this construction.

In classical topology, it is common to denote by
C(X)
the subset of
2^{X}

consisting of connected members of
2^{X}
. Since the notation

C(X,\kappa)
is established in the literature of digital topology as
the set of
\kappa
-continuous self maps on
X
, we use the notation

K(X,\kappa^{\prime})=(\{A\in 2^{X}~|~A\mbox{ is $\kappa$-connected}\},\kappa^{\prime}).

We will use the abbreviation
K(X)
when
\kappa
is understood.

Example 3.2
.

K([a,b]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
and

(\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}~|~a\leq x\leq y\leq b\},c_{2})

are isomorphic graphs.

Proof.

Let
X=K([a,b]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
,

Y=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}~|~a\leq x\leq y\leq b\}
. Consider the
function
F:X\to Y
given by
F([m,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}})=(m,n)
. It is
elementary to show that
F
is a
(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2})
-isomorphism.
∎

4
Cardinality

Remark 4.1
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a digital image such that
\#X=n
.
Then
\#2^{X}=2^{n}-1
. This is because for each

x\in X
and
A\in 2^{X}
, either
x\in A
or
x\not\in A
.
This yields
2^{n}
possible combinations of pixels, but we exclude the
empty set.

However, the following example shows that
K(X)
may be considerably
smaller than
2^{X}
.

Example 4.2
.

\#K([1,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})=n(n+1)/2
.

Proof.

For
i\in[1,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, the members of

K([1,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
that
have
i
as their largest member are those of

\{[j,i]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\}_{j=1}^{i}\}
. Since there are
i
digital
intervals with largest member
i
in
K([1,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
,

\#K([1,n]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1}^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i=n(n+1)/2.

∎

5
Maps on digital hyperspaces

In this section, we study maps induced on hyperspaces by
continuous maps between digital images.

5.1
Induced maps

Given a continuous map
f:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)
,
we show below that
f
induces a

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-continuous map

f_{*}:2^{X}\to 2^{Y}
such that

f_{*}|_{K(X)}:K(X)\to K(Y)
is also

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-continuous. In the following,
we will use the notation
f_{*}
to abbreviate
f_{*}|_{K(X)}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
and
(Y,\lambda)
be digital images and let

f:X\to Y
. Then
f
is
(\kappa,\lambda)
-continuous if and only if
the induced functions
f_{*}:2^{X}\to 2^{Y}
and

f_{*}:K(X)\to K(Y)

defined by
f_{*}(A)=f(A)
are

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-continuous.

Proof.

Since a digitally continuous function preserves
adjacency and connectivity,
the same argument works for both of the induced functions.

Suppose
f
is continuous. Let
A,B\in 2^{X}
such that

A\leftrightarrow_{\kappa^{\prime}}B
. Let
x\in A
.
There exists
y\in B
such that
x\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}y
. By
the continuity of
f
we have

f(x)\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}f(y)
. Similarly, for

b\in B
, there exists
a\in A
such that

a\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}b

and
f(a)\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}f(b)
. Therefore,

f_{*}(A)=f(A)\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda^{\prime}}f(B)=f_{*}(B)
. Thus
f_{*}
is

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-continuous.

Suppose
f_{*}
is
(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-continuous. Let
x,y\in X
such that

x\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}y
. Then
\{x\}\leftrightarrow_{\kappa^{\prime}}\{y\}
, so

\{f(x)\}=f_{*}(\{x\})\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda^{\prime}}f_{*}(\{y\})=\{f(y)\}.

Therefore,
f(x)\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}f(y)
. Thus,
f
is
(\kappa,\lambda)
-continuous.
∎

We have the following as an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
and
(Y,\lambda)
be digital images and let

•

is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-isomorphism;

•

f_{*}:2^{X}\to 2^{Y}
is a

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-isomorphism; and

•

f_{*}:K(X)\to K(Y)
is a

(\kappa^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
-isomorphism

Proposition 5.3
.

Given continuous functions
f:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)

and
g:(Y,\lambda)\to(W,\mu)
, we have

(g\circ f)_{*}=g_{*}\circ f_{*}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from the observation that
A\in 2^{X}
implies

(g\circ f)_{*}(A)=(g\circ f)(A)=g(f(A))=g_{*}(f_{*}(A))=(g_{*}\circ f_{*})(A).

∎

The following is elementary.

Lemma 5.4

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a digital image. Then

(\id_{X})_{*}=\id_{(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})}
and

(\id_{X})_{*}=\id_{(K(X),\kappa^{\prime})}

Theorem 5.5

The hyperspace construction yields covariant functors

from
the category of digital images and continuous functions to
the category of graphs and continuous functions (respectively, to

,

Proof.

This follows from Proposition

Let

Multivalued functions and hyperspaces

In this section, we examine relations between
various notions of continuous multivalued functions
between digital images, and hyperspaces of digital images.

Definition 7.1
.

A multivalued function

F:(X,\kappa)\multimap(Y,\lambda)

•

has
strong continuity

[
31
]
if for each pair of
adjacent
x,y\in X
, every point of
F(x)

is adjacent or equal to some point
of
F(y)
and every point of
F(y)
is adjacent or equal to
some point of
F(x)
;

•

has
weak continuity

[
31
]
if for each pair of
adjacent
x,y\in X
,
F(x)
and
F(y)

are adjacent sets in
Y
, i.e., there
exist
a\in F(x)
,
b\in F(y)
such that

a\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}b
;

•

is
connectivity preserving

[
25
]
if

F(A)\subset Y
is connected whenever

A\subset X
is connected;

•

is
continuous

[
21
,
22
]

if
X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,

\kappa=c_{u}
for
1\leq u\leq n
, and

F
is
generated

by a continuous function
f:S(X,r)\to Y

for some positive integer
r
; where

S(X,r)=\bigcup_{x\in X}S(\{x\},r)
, where
for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
S(\{x\},r)

is the set of all points

(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
such that for each index
i

we have
y_{i}=x_{i}+k_{i}/r
for some
integer
k_{i}
such that
0\leq k_{i}<r
;

S(X,r)
inherits
c_{u}
in the sense that

(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\leftrightarroweq_{c_{u}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

in
S(X,r)
if for at most
u
indices
i
,

|y_{i}-a_{i}|=1/r
and for all other indices
j
,

y_{j}=a_{j}
; and “
F
is
generated

by
f
” means for all
x\in X
,

F(x)=\bigcup_{y\in S(\{x\},r)}\{f(y)\}
.

We have the following.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
F:(X,\kappa)\multimap(Y,\lambda)
be a
strongly continuous multifunction between digital
images. Then the function

F_{*}:(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})\to(2^{Y},\lambda^{\prime})

defined by
F_{*}(A)=F(A)
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
A_{0}\leftrightarrow_{\kappa^{\prime}}A_{1}
in
2^{X}
. We must show
that
F(A_{0})\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda^{\prime}}F(A_{1})
in
2^{Y}
.

Let
x\in A_{0}
,
y\in A_{1}
such that

x\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}y
. By Definition
7.1
,
for every
p\in F(x)
there exists
q\in F(y)

such that
p\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}q
. Similarly, given

u\in A_{1}
,
v\in A_{0}
such that
u\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}v
,
for every
r\in F(u)
there exists
s\in F(v)
such
that
r\leftrightarroweq_{\lambda}s
. The assertion follows.
∎

The following shows that in substituting
weak continuity, continuity, or connectivity-preserving
for strong continuity, we fail to
obtain a result analogous to
Theorem

.

Example 7.3
.

Let
F:([0,1]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1})\multimap([0,2]_{{\mathbb{Z}}},c_{1})

be defined by

,

. Then

-neighbor in

Cycles and Girth

The reader is reminded that:

•

a point in
2^{X}
is a nonempty subset of
X
;

•

a cycle in
X
is a closed path of at least 3 distinct
points in which no node repeats, but in which a point
x

can be adjacent to points distinct from the
predecessor and successor of
x
in the path (the
cycle does not need to be chordless).

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a digital image. Then
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})

has a 3-cycle if and only if
(X,\kappa)
has a non-isolated point.

Proof.

It is elementary that if the points of
(X,\kappa)
are all isolated,
then
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
has no cycle.

Suppose
x\in X
is not isolated in
(X,\kappa)
. Then there exists

y\in X
such that
x\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}y
. Then

\left\{\{x\},\{x,y\},\{y\}\right\}
is a 3-cycle in

K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
.
∎

The
girth
of a graph
(X,\kappa)
is variously described in
the literature as the length of a shortest or of
a longest
[
1
]
cycle in
(X,\kappa)
. We may
distinguish these concepts as
girth
and
Girth
, respectively. In light of Proposition
8.1
, the
Girth is more interesting, so in the following we focus on Girth.

Example 8.2
.

If
(X,\kappa)
is a digital image and
x\in X
such that

N(X,x,\kappa)
has distinct points
u
and
v
that are
not
\kappa
-adjacent, then

K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
has Girth of at least 6.

Proof.

By hypothesis, there exist distinct
u,v\in N(X,x,\kappa)
.
Then by Definition
3.1
,
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
has a 6-cycle

\{u\},\{u,x\},\{u,x,v\},\{x,v\},\{v\},\{x\}.

∎

Example 8.3
.

The Girth of
(2^{[1,4]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
is 15, which is equal
to
\#(2^{[1,4]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
.
I.e.,
(2^{[1,4]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
has
a cycle containing all members of
(2^{[1,4]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the following sequence of the 15
distinct members of
(2^{[1,4]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}},c_{1}^{\prime})
is a

c_{1}^{\prime}
-cycle.

\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{1,3,4\},\{1,2,4\},\{1,2,3,4\},\{2,3,4\},\{2,3\},

\{2,4\},\{3,4\},\{4\},\{3\},\{2\},\{1\}

∎

Dominating set

A subset
D
of a graph
(X,\kappa)
is a

for, or
dominates
,

(X,\kappa)
, if given
x\in X
there exists

d\in D
such that
d\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}x
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a digital image and let
D\subset X
. Let

{\cal D}=\{A\in 2^{X}~|~A\cap D\neq\emptyset\}

Then

dominates
(X,\kappa)
if and only if

{\cal D}
dominates
(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose
D
dominates
(X,\kappa)
. Let
x\in A\in 2^{X}
.
There exists
y\in D
such that
x\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}y
.
It follows from Definition
3.1
that

A^{\prime}=A\cup\{y\}\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa^{\prime}}A.

Since
A
is arbitrary and
A^{\prime}\in{\cal D}
,
it follows that
{\cal D}
dominates

(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})
.

Suppose
{\cal D}
dominates
(2^{X},\kappa^{\prime})
.
Let
x\in X
. Then there exists

A\in{\cal D}
such
that
A\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa^{\prime}}\{x\}
. Therefore, for all
a\in A

we have
a\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}x
. Since there exists

,

. Thus,

dominates

.
∎

10
Diameter

Definition 10.1
.

[
23
]

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a connected graph. The

shortest path metric
for
(X,\kappa)
is

d_{\ell}(x,y)=\min\{length(P)~|~P\mbox{ is a $\kappa$-path in $X$ from $x$ to $y$}\},\mbox{ for }x,y\in X.

Definition 10.2
.

The

of a finite connected graph
(X,\kappa)

is

diam(X,\kappa)=\max\{d_{\ell}(x,y)~|~x,y\in X\}.

Definition 10.3
.

[
1
]

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a connected digital image.
For
x\in X

associated number

of

A
center
of
(X,\kappa)

is a point
x_{0}\in X
such that

e(x_{0})=\min\{e(x)~|~x\in X\}.

The associated number of the center is the

radius
of
(X,\kappa)
.

We have the following.

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
be a finite connected digital image with

r
. Let
\#X=n
. Then

diam(K(X,\kappa^{\prime}))<2(n+r-1)
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}
be a center of
(X,\kappa)
. Let

A_{0},A_{1}\in K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
. Let
y_{0}\in A_{0}
,

y_{1}\in A_{1}
. By assumption, there are
paths
P_{i}
of length at most
r
from
x_{0}
to

y_{i}
. Thus,
P_{0}\cup P_{1}
is a
\kappa
-path
in
X
of length at most

2r
from
y_{0}
to
y_{1}
. It follows from
Definition
3.1
that

{\cal P}=\{\{p\}~|~p\in P_{0}\cup P_{1}\}
is a

\kappa^{\prime}
-path in
K(X)
of length at most

from
\{y_{0}\}
to
\{y_{1}\}
.

Let
Q_{0}=\{y_{0}\}
. We argue inductively as follows.
Suppose we have

such that

Q_{k}\subset A_{0}
. If
Q_{k}\neq A_{0}
,
then since
A_{0}
is connected, there exists

such that for some

, we have

Since
Q_{\#A_{0}-1}=A_{0}

is a path in

of length

to

Similarly, we can construct a path

in
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
of length

\{y_{1}\}

. Therefore,

{\cal P}_{0}\cup{\cal P}\cup{\cal P}_{1}

is a path in

of length at most

from

to

.
Further, we may assume

;
since otherwise

, so there is a path
of length 0 from
A_{0}
to
A_{1}
in
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
. It
follows that for any

there is
a path in
K(X,\kappa^{\prime})
from
A_{0}
to

of length

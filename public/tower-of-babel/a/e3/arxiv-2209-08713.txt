Non-uniqueness of Leray solutions to the hypodissipative Navier-Stokes equations in two dimensions
Dallas Albritton, Maria Colombo

Abstract
. We exhibit non-unique Leray solutions of the forced Navier-Stokes equations with hypodissipation in two dimensions. Unlike the solutions constructed in [ ABC22b ] , the solutions we construct live at a supercritical scaling, in which the hypodissipation formally becomes negligible as t → 0 + t\to 0^{+} . In this scaling, it is possible to perturb the Euler non-uniqueness scenario of Vishik [ Vis18a , Vis18b ] to the hypodissipative setting at the nonlinear level. Our perturbation argument is quasilinear in spirit and circumvents the spectral theoretic approach to incorporating the dissipation in [ ABC22b ] .

1.
Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional fractional Navier-Stokes equations

(NS
β
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p+\Lambda^{\beta}u&=f\\
\div u&=0\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

where
\beta\in(0,2]
and
\Lambda^{\beta}=(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}
is the fractional Laplacian. We consider the Cauchy problem for (
NSβ
) with divergence-free initial velocity

(1.1)

u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

It is not difficult to adapt the seminal work
[
Ler34
]
of Leray to demonstrate the existence of a global-in-time
Leray-Hopf solution
for each divergence-free initial velocity
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and body force
f\in L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
. These are distributional solutions
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{\frac{\beta}{2}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
to (
NSβ
) satisfying
\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
and the
energy inequality
for all
t>0
:

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u|^{2}\,dx\,ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\cdot u\,dx\,ds\,.

It is well known that Leray’s solutions are unique in the classical setting
\beta=2
. In this paper, we demonstrate non-uniqueness when
\beta<2
:

Theorem 1.1

(Non-uniqueness of Leray-Hopf solutions)
.

For all
\beta\in(0,2)
, there exist a force
f\in L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})

and two distinct Leray-Hopf solutions,
u
and
\bar{u}
, with zero initial velocity
u_{0}\equiv 0
and force
f
:

(1.3)

u\neq\bar{u}\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T)\text{ for all }T>0\,.

The solutions are constructed explicitly and satisfy many desirable properties, among which is smoothness on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T)
for
T\ll 1
and energy equality in (
1.2
).

In a recent work
[
ABC22b
]
with Brué, we constructed non-unique Leray solutions of the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations. While the method therein is also applicable to (
NSβ
), at least when
\beta>1

The main idea of the proof is to perturb the examples of non-uniqueness discovered by Vishik in
[
Vis18a
,
Vis18b
]
to the viscous setting.
To begin, we recall the basic dimensional analysis for the Euler equations

(1.4)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=f\,,\quad\div u=0\,,

which can be equivalently rewritten in vorticity formulation as

(1.5)

\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=g\,,\quad u=\Delta^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}\omega\,,

where
\omega=\curl u
is the vorticity and
g=\curl f
.
They have a two-parameter scaling symmetry

(1.6)

u_{\mu,\eta}=\frac{\eta}{\mu}u(\mu x,\eta t)\,,\qquad\omega_{\mu,\eta}=\eta u(\mu x,\eta t)

corresponding to the physical dimensions
[x]=L
,
[t]=T
,
[u]=L/T
,
[\omega]=1/T
,
[f]=L/T^{2}
, and
[g]=1/T^{2}
.
We suppose a relationship
T=L^{\alpha}
between the dimensions. This yields a one-parameter scaling symmetry:

(1.7)

u_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\alpha-1}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{\alpha}t)\,,\qquad\omega_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\alpha}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{\alpha}t)\,.

One may seek special solutions
\bar{\omega}
which are invariant under the above symmetry and necessarily have the form

(1.8)

\bar{\omega}(x)=\frac{1}{t}\bar{\Omega}(\xi)\,,

where
\xi=x/t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. In fact, we may analyze an arbitrary Euler solution
\omega
in
similarity variables

(\xi,\tau)
, where
\tau=\log t
and the
similarity profile

\Omega
is defined by

(1.9)

\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{t}\Omega(\xi,\tau)\,,\qquad g(x,t)=\frac{1}{t^{2}}G(\xi,\tau)\,.

The Euler equations (
1.5
) in similarity variables are

(1.10)

\partial_{\tau}\Omega+(-1-\frac{\xi}{\alpha}\cdot\nabla)\Omega+U\cdot\nabla\Omega=G\,,\quad U=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\Omega\,.

Self-similar solutions
\bar{\omega}
correspond precisely to steady states
\bar{\Omega}
in the new variables.

If
\bar{\Omega}
is an unstable steady state of (

), then it is natural to seek its

. Suppose that
\Omega^{\rm lin}
is an exponentially growing solution of the linearization of (

) around

. One seeks a trajectory

where

ensures that
\Omega
is not identically equal to
\bar{\Omega}
. On the other hand,
\Omega\overset{\tau\to-\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bar{\Omega}
. This is the non-uniqueness scenario in
[
Vis18a
]
, which demonstrated the sharpness of the Yudovich class
[
Yud63
]
in the ‘forced category’.

How does the above non-uniqueness scenario look with viscosity? The fractional Navier-Stokes equations (
NSβ
) in self-similarity variables are

(1.12)

\partial_{\tau}\Omega+(-1-\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}/\alpha)\Omega+U\cdot\nabla\Omega+e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}\Omega=G\,,\quad U=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\Omega\,,

where

(1.13)

\gamma=1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\,.

The traditional choice of exponent is
\alpha=\beta
, so that the PDE (
1.12
) is
\tau
-autonomous. (The choice

in
[

,

]
is the classical Navier-Stokes scaling.) This choice is determined by the dimensions of the viscosity:

to prove Theorem

, that is, up to the Lions exponent. The most delicate part of the analysis in

is an eigenvalue perturbation argument which shows that not only the linearized
2

In this paper, we instead consider

becomes perturbative (formally) as

as an unstable mode for

Proposition 2.1

(Unstable vortex)
.

Let

\bar{U}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})

\bar{U}(x)=V(r)e_{\theta}

\bar{\Omega}=\curl\bar{U}
and satisfying the following properties.

1.
Linear instability
. There exists an integer

such that

has an unstable eigenvalue. More specifically, there exist

with
\Re\lambda=:a\geq a_{0}
and

\bm{L}_{\rm ss}\eta=\lambda\eta

.
The operator

generates a continuous semigroup in

, and for all

(2.2)

\lVert e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\rVert_{L^{2}_{m}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{\delta}e^{(a+\delta)\tau}\text{ for all }\tau\geq 0\,.

Proof.

1. and 2. above are contained in

, Theorem 2.4.2]
except for the compact support property of

, which was pointed out in

3. Eigenfunction estimates
. We present an alternative to the approach in
[

, where it was shown that
\eta\in W^{2,\infty}
. Suppose that
\eta\in D(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
is an eigenfunction with unstable eigenvalue
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}-1
:

(2.3)

\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\frac{\xi}{\alpha}\cdot\nabla+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\right)\eta+{\rm BS}[\eta]\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega}=0\,.

First, we demonstrate that
\eta
is compactly supported. When
|\xi|\geq 2
is beyond the support of
\bar{U}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2})
, we have

(2.4)

\frac{r}{\alpha}\partial_{r}\eta=\lambda^{\prime}\eta\,,\quad r\geq 2\,,

whose only decaying solutions are identically zero in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2}
.

Second, we prove that
\eta\in H^{k}
provided that
\Re\lambda^{\prime}>k/\alpha
. We consider
{\rm BS}[\eta]\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega}
to be a ‘forcing term’ in the resolvent problem, and we rewrite the above equation via the Laplace transform characterization of the resolvent:
R(\lambda^{\prime},\bm{L}_{\rm ss})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda^{\prime}t}e^{t\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\,dt
. Let
X_{t}
be the flow map associated to the autonomous vector field
V(\xi):=\xi/\alpha-\bar{U}(\xi)
. Then

(2.5)

\eta=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda^{\prime}t}({\rm BS}[\eta]\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega})\circ X_{t}\,dt\,.

To estimate
\eta
, we recall basic properties of
X_{t}
, which is explicitly

(2.6)

X_{t}(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{0}})=r(t,r_{0})e^{i\theta(t,\theta_{0},r_{0})}\,,

(2.7)

r(t,r_{0})=e^{t/\alpha}r_{0}\,,\quad\theta(t,\theta_{0},r_{0})=\theta_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\bar{U}_{\theta}(r(s,r_{0}))\,ds\,.

By the general formulae

(2.8)

(\nabla X_{t}e_{r})(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{0}})=\partial_{r_{0}}r\;(e_{r}\circ X_{t})+r\partial_{r_{0}}\theta\;(e_{\theta}\circ X_{t})\,,

(2.9)

(\nabla X_{t}e_{\theta})(r_{0}e^{i\theta_{0}})=\frac{1}{r_{0}}\partial_{\theta_{0}}r\;(e_{r}\circ X_{t})+\frac{r}{r_{0}}\partial_{\theta_{0}}\theta\;(e_{\theta}\circ X_{t})\,,

it is not difficult to demonstrate that
|\nabla X_{t}|\lesssim e^{t/\alpha}
. A different way of saying this is that solutions of the vector-valued ODE
\dot{\vec{y}}=(\nabla V)(X_{t})\vec{y}
satisfy
|\vec{y}(t)|\lesssim e^{t/\alpha}|\vec{y}(0)|
. By taking spatial derivatives of the ODE
\dot{X}_{t}=V(X_{t})
, this is enough to bootstrap the estimate on
\nabla X_{t}
to
|\nabla^{k}X_{t}|\lesssim_{k}e^{kt/\alpha}
for all
k\geq 0
. In particular, we have

(2.10)

\|\eta\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{(k/\alpha-\lambda^{\prime})t}\,dt\;\|{\rm BS}[\eta]\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega}\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{k-1}}\,,

where the implied constant depends on various quantities. Hence, one can bootstrap
\eta\in L^{2}
to
\eta\in H^{k}
provided that
\Re\lambda>k/\alpha-1
. Finally, since
\eta
is compactly supported and mean zero, we have
|{\rm BS}[\eta]|\lesssim|\xi|^{-2}
for
|\xi|\geq 2
. In particular,
{\rm BS}[\eta]\in L^{2}
. ∎

2.1.
Strategy

We revisit the ansatz (
1.11
):

(2.11)

\Omega=\bar{\Omega}+\Omega^{\rm lin}+\Omega^{\rm per}

where

(2.12)

\Omega^{\rm lin}(\xi,\tau):=\Re(e^{\lambda\tau}\eta)\,,

\lambda
is a maximally unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
, and
\eta
is an associated non-trivial eigenfunction. Then
\Omega^{\rm lin}
is a solution to the linearized evolution equation

(2.13)

\partial_{\tau}\Omega^{\rm lin}-\bm{L}_{\rm ss}\Omega^{\rm lin}=0\,,

and our goal is to solve for the higher-order correction
\Omega^{\rm per}
. The PDE satisfied by
\Omega^{\rm per}
is

(2.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Omega^{\rm per}-\bm{L}_{\rm ss}\Omega^{\rm per}+(U^{\rm lin}+U^{\rm per})\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm per}+U^{\rm per}\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm lin}+e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}\Omega^{\rm per}

\displaystyle=-U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm lin}-e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}\Omega^{\rm lin}\,,

where the right-hand side is considered a source term.
The fractional Laplacian
e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}
will be treated perturbatively. Hence, our approach is essentially quasilinear in nature, and it will be necessary to incorporate the transport
(U^{\rm lin}+U^{\rm per})\cdot\nabla
into the operator. Define

(2.15)

-\bm{L}[W]=-\bm{L}_{\rm ss}+(U^{\rm lin}+W)\cdot\nabla+e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}\,,

the ‘main operator’ in the PDE, and the associated (formal) solution operator
S[W]=S[W](\tau,s)
. We seek solutions satisfying Duhamel’s formula:

(2.16)

\Omega^{\rm per}(\cdot,\tau)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}S[U^{\rm per}](\tau,s)[(U^{\rm lin}+U^{\rm per})\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm lin}+e^{s\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}\Omega^{\rm lin}](\cdot,s)\,ds\,.

It will be necessary to develop the (strong) solution theory for
\Omega^{\rm per}
and the solution operator
S
in high regularity spaces, whose corresponding velocity fields are Lipschitz continuous, e.g.,
\Omega\in H^{s}
,
s>d/2
. It will be more convenient to estimate
S
in a function space
X\subset W^{1,4}
which will be defined in Section
3
.

There are two main difficulties, which are already present in the Euler equations and have been encountered in previous work, e.g.,
[
BGS02
]
; the difficulties will only be exacerbated by the dissipation
e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}
.

First, there is the apparent derivative loss in adding the transport operator
(U^{\rm lin}+U^{\rm per})\cdot\nabla
onto
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
. On the one hand, it is necessary to use the spectral information on
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
through the semigroup estimate in
L^{2}
. On the other hand, it is too naïve to write Duhamel’s formula, containing expressions like
\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm per})\,ds
, and hope to close an estimate at the
L^{2}
level. Moreover, in our setting, adding the dissipation operator
e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}
further exhibits a loss of
\beta
derivatives. The key observation for handling the loss is that the compact term
U^{\rm per}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Omega}
, responsible for the creation of the unstable eigenvalues, gains regularity. Hence, the strategy will be to lose derivatives while applying the semigroup estimate in Duhamel’s formula and regain them in a bootstrapping procedure based on energy estimates.
2
2

2

A different approach is to work with the Lagrangian formulation, in which there is no derivative loss, see the construction of unstable manifolds in
[
LZ13
,
LZ14
]
.

The second difficulty concerns the operator
\bar{U}\cdot\nabla
in high regularity spaces. One way to see the difficulty is to pass a derivative into the equation: The PDE for
\partial_{i}\Omega^{\rm per}
contains the term
\partial_{i}\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\rm per}
, which in principle can change the growth rate. In
[
BGS02
]
, it was observed that it is enough assume that
\Re\lambda
is greater than the maximal fluid Lyapunov exponent. Our approach will be Eulerian rather than Lagrangian. It is an energy method specific to shear flows and vortices (in short, the idea is to apply
\partial_{\theta}
first), and crucially, it is well behaved even with the term
e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}
. In fact, when
\beta>1
, the term
e^{\tau\gamma}\Lambda^{\beta}
will help us in the bootstrapping procedure: Once we have propagated one derivative, the other
\beta-1
derivatives needed to close the argument are regained by smoothing. Heuristically, a gain of
\beta
derivatives costs a factor
e^{\tau\gamma}
, which is why the dissipation term cannot be used to control everything. This is seen in the tracking of the dissipation norm
D
below.

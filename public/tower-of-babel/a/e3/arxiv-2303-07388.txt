AdS Witten Diagrams to Carrollian Correlators
Arjun Bagchi, Prateksh Dhivakar, and Sudipta Dutta

Abstract
Carrollian Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) have been proposed as co-dimension one holographic duals to asymptotically flat spacetimes as opposed to Celestial CFTs which are co-dimension two. In this paper, drawing inspiration from Celestial holography, we show by a suitable generalisation of the flat space limit of AdS that keeps track of the previously disregarded null direction, one can reproduce Carrollian CFT correlation functions from AdS Witten diagrams. In particular, considering Witten diagrams in AdS 4 , we reproduce two and three-point correlation functions for three dimensional Carrollian CFTs in the so-called delta-function branch. Along the way, we construct non-trivial Carrollian three-point functions in the delta-branch by considering a collinear limit. We also obtain a generalised anti-podal matching condition that now depends on the retarded time direction.

Introduction

The Holographic Principle

The progress in understanding holography beyond its natural setting in AdS has been somewhat less impressive. Of late, there is a renewed effort in the construction of holography for asymptotically flat spacetimes.

Two approaches to Flat Holography

The efforts in understanding a holographic prescription to flatspace have taken two major avenues. The first one, called Celestial holography, proposes that the dual theory to four dimensional (4d) asymptotically flat spacetimes is a 2d relativistic CFT living on the celestial sphere at null infinity, called Celestial CFTs. Following initial observations of Strominger and collaborators
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He:2014laa
;
Strominger:2014pwa
, this has led to an impressive body of work relating scattering amplitudes with asymptotic symmetries and memory effects in a web of new relations. The reader is pointed to the excellent reviews
Strominger:2017zoo
;
Pasterski:2021rjz
;
Raclariu:2021zjz
for an idea of this rapidly developing field.

The other direction is called Carrollian holography. Here the central idea, perhaps more in keeping with the original holographic principle, is that the field theory dual to gravity in asymptotically flat spacetimes lives one lower dimension and on the entire null boundary
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
of flat space. The point to emphasise is that in the Carrollian proposal, the field theory does not reside just on the celestial sphere, but also on the null direction given by the advanced (or retarded) time coordinate
Bagchi:2016bcd
. The asymptotic symmetries of flat spacetime, called Bondi-van der Burg-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) symmetries
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
are encoded on the lower dimensional theory as conformal symmetries, but now on the null surfaces
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
. This degenerate metric structure on the boundary necessitates the introduction of new non-metric structures called Carrollian structures, which are mathematically fibre-bundles and the conformal symmetries on null surfaces are Conformal Carrollian symmetries. The putative field theory duals to gravitational theories in 4d asymptotically flat spacetimes are thus 3d Carrollian CFTs.

After the initial suggestion that flat space holography should come from non-Lorentzian symmetries

;
Bagchi:2012cy

, asymptotic three point functions
Bagchi:2020rwb
. Other important developments included
Bagchi:2012yk
;
Barnich:2012aw
;
Hartong:2015usd
.

The important question of how to connect conformal Carroll correlation functions with scattering matrix elements in one higher dimension was addressed recently in

From AdS to Flatspace

One of the ways to arrive at flat spacetimes is to take the infinite radius limit of AdS spacetimes. In the same vein, if one attempts to take a large radius limit of AdS/CFT, one should arrive at a holographic correspondence for flat spacetimes. At the level of symmetries, it was shown that this limit of sending the AdS radius to infinity sends the speed of light on the boundary CFT to zero
Bagchi:2012cy
. Conformal Carrollian structures thus arise naturally. This was one of the principal reasons for the initial formulation of what we now call Carrollian holography.

There has been a lot of work in attempting to understand scattering in asymptotically flat spacetime from taking suitable limits of Witten diagrams in AdS. (We review this in Sec. 3.) It is thus very natural to wonder how one can understand arriving at conformal Carrollian correlation functions as a limit of AdS Witten diagrams and this is the question we answer in this paper.

A particularly intriguing recent piece of work was the elucidation of the connection between AdS
4
Witten diagrams in and 2d Celestial correlation functions
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
. We draw inspiration from this work in our paper and show that with a suitable generalisation of this construction, where we explicitly keep track of the null retarded time coordinate while taking the singular limit, we recover 3d Carrollian CFT correlation functions. This provides some tantalising hints at a direct connection between 2d Celestial CFTs and 3d Carrollian CFTs. It is possible that a 2d Celestial CFT is a 3d Carroll CFT restricted on a particular point on the null retarded time direction or is a suitable dimensional reduction of the higher dimensional Carrollian theory.

It is natural that the limit of AdS
4
Witten diagrams yields 3d Conformal Carrollian correlators. When one takes a limit of AdS
4
/CFT
3
, one does not expect that the boundary theory would suddenly go down in dimension. Since the radius of AdS is taken out to infinity, it is expected that something singular would happen to the boundary theory. The singular limit manifests itself as the vanishing of the speed of light. It has been shown that BMS symmetries of asymptotically flat spacetimes are isomorphic to conformal Carrollian symmetries
Duval:2014uva
. So the matching of (infinite dimensional) symmetries of the boundary theory and the theory in the bulk is manifest in this formulation of Carrollian holography.

One can think of reaching the null boundary of asymptotically flat spacetimes by performing an infinite boost on the timelike boundary of AdS. The procedure of boosting a CFT on a timelike slice to get a Carrollian CFT on a null manifold in the limit of infinite boosts has been explicitly demonstrated recently for the case of
d=2

Bagchi:2022nvj
. Generalisations to higher dimensions should work in analogous ways.

It has been recently shown that Carrollian hydrodynamics captures the physics of fluids flowing at the speed of light
Bagchi:2023ysc
. This is of course a very high energy limit of usual hydrodynamics. Flat spacetimes have also been thought of as a very high energy limit of AdS, where the Minkowski diamond sits at the centre of a very large radius AdS spacetime and the observers in this region do not feel the curvature of AdS. The fact that Carrollian symmetries can arise as the very high energy subsector of relativistic symmetries, as is manifest from the fluid example, ties in well with this intuition.

A brief outline of this paper

As advertised above, the main goal of this paper is to systematically derive Carrollian CFT correlation functions from AdS Witten diagrams. We will do this by generalising the construction of
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
by carefully keeping track of the null direction that was previously not taken into account. For the purposes of this paper, we will focus on Witten diagrams in AdS
4
and Carrollian CFT in
d=3
.

To begin, in Sec. 2, we briefly review Carrollian and conformal Carrollian symmetries in arbitrary dimensions before focussing on
d=3
. We revisit Carrollian Ward identities and remind the reader that there are two distinct branches for the correlation functions which are the delta-function branch and the CFT branch. The delta-function branch is the one which is of interest to us in this work as it is related to flatspace scattering
Bagchi:2022emh
via the modified Mellin transformations that were first introduced in
Banerjee:2018gce
. We review this briefly. We then explicitly construct non-trivial three point functions in the collinear limit. This is new material. Appendix
A
, which contains a review of modified Mellin transformations and explains how this is related to Carrollian correlators, supplements this section.

The main message of the paper is contained in Sec. 3, where we show how to go from AdS Witten diagrams to
n
-point functions of Carroll CFTs by keeping track of the null direction in the limit. Thereafter, in Sec. 4, we consider the two and three point Witten diagrams and show explicitly how these reproduce the Carrollian answers derived earlier from Ward identities. We find that the generic three-point function vanishes as expected, but we recover the non-trivial answer arrived at from symmetries in the collinear limit. We end with discussions in Sec. 5. Three appendices give details of computations that are omitted or shortened in the main text.

Carrollian Symmetries, Ward Identities and Correlators

In this section we start off with a quick review of Carrollian and conformal Carrollian symmetries and then focus on the case of

2.1
Carrollian and Conformal Carrollian Symmetries

Algebraic point of view

The Carroll algebra appears in the vanishing speed of light (
c\to 0
) limit of the Poincare algebra
LevyLeblond
;
NDS
. In a similar vein, the Inönü-Wigner contraction of the relativistic conformal algebra gives the Conformal Carroll algebra. When viewed on coordinates, this is a contraction of the time direction
Bagchi:2012cy

x^{i}\to x^{i},\quad t\to\epsilon t,\quad\epsilon\to 0

(1)

where
\epsilon
is a dimensionless version of the speed of light. This leads to changes in the algebra, e.g. Lorentz boosts become commuting Carrollian boosts:

\displaystyle J_{0i}=x_{i}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{i}\Rightarrow C_{i}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon J_{0i}=x_{i}\partial_{t}

(2a)

\displaystyle[J_{0i},J_{0j}]=-J_{ij}\Rightarrow[C_{i},C_{j}]=0.

(2b)

The relativistic conformal algebra in
d
spacetime dimensions is

. The conformal Carrollian algebra in
d
spacetime dimensions is a contraction of the time direction of

and is thus given by
iso(d,1)
. If we remember that the relativistic conformal algebra is isomorphic to the isometry algebra of AdS

d
spacetime dimensions is isomorphic to the isometry algebra of
(d+1)
dimensional Minkowski spacetime, i.e.
iso(d,1)
. This is the first rudimentary check for a holographic duality. The isometry algebra of the higher dimensional bulk theory should match with the lower dimensional boundary theory. The Carrollian version of flat holography is based on this simple but fundamental fact.

Geometric point of view

Carrollian symmetries in an arbitrary dimension

d

of rank
(d-1)
and signature
(0,+,+,\ldots+)
and a no-where vanishing vector

that generates the kernel of
h

;
Duval:2014uoa
;
Duval:2014uva

h_{\mu\nu}\tau^{\nu}=0.

(3)

The Lie algebra of vector fields
\chi=\chi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
that generates the isometry algebra of this structure:

\pounds_{\chi}h_{\mu\nu}=0,\quad\pounds_{\chi}\tau^{\nu}=0\,,

(4)

generates an infinite dimensional analogue of the Carroll algebra. This can be made finite by demanding a connection on the Carroll manifold that is compatible with the pair
\left(h_{\mu\nu},\tau^{\mu}\right)
. This algebra matches the one which we discussed earlier as arising out of the contraction of the Poincare algebra.

If we now consider conformal isometries of the Carroll manifolds:

\pounds_{\chi}h_{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{1}h_{\mu\nu},\quad\pounds_{\chi}\tau^{\nu}=\lambda_{2}\tau^{\nu}\,,

(5)

this algebra again closes to what is the infinite version of the conformal Carroll algebra. For the case where

N=-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}=2\,,

(6)

the

dimensional flat spacetime at its null boundary

It is good to pause here to understand the significance on the last statement. The isometry of Minkowski spacetime is of course the Poincare algebra, and we stressed that the finite version of the Conformal Carroll group is isomorphic to the Poincare group, and thus realises the symmetries of Minkowski spacetime holographically in one lower dimension. One of the surprises of the analysis of Bondi, van der Burg, Metzner

and Sachs

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m},\quad[{\bar{L}}_{n},{\bar{L}}_{m}]=(n-m){\bar{L}}_{n+m}

(7a)

\displaystyle[L_{n},M_{r,s}]=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-r\right)M_{n+r,s},\quad[{\bar{L}}_{n},M_{r,s}]=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-s\right)M_{r,n+s}

(7b)

\displaystyle[M_{p,q},M_{r,s}]=0

(7c)

Here

are angle dependent translations of the retarded time direction called supertranslations and

are the infinite dimensional conformal generators on the sphere

at infinity.

Barnich:2010eb
more recently. There are other versions of infinite extensions to include the whole Diff(
\mathbb{S}^{2}

A particularly important message here is that the proposed dual 3d Carrollian CFT to asymptotically 4d flat spacetime naturally encodes this infinite dimensional BMS algebra found independently by the canonical analysis in the bulk, as its symmetries, because the manifold on which it is defined realises the algebra as its conformal isometries. Carrollian CFTs are generically CFTs defined on null surfaces and hence natural co-dimension one holographic dual of asymptotically flat spacetimes.

We should mention here that recently the study of tree-level massless scattering in the bulk flat spacetime has led to the discovery of a much larger symmetry than the BMS group

2.2
3d Carroll CFTs

Our focus, as stated above, is on Carroll CFTs in 3d. The algebra of interest for these theories is the conformal Carrollian algebra or equivalently the BMS
4
algebra (
7
). The geometry of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
is that of a fibre bundle with a structure
\mathbb{R}_{u}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}

is a null line and the

is the celestial sphere at each point

ds^{2}_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}=0.du^{2}+d\Omega_{2}^{2}.

(8)

The BMS
4

(9)

The Carrollian CFT defined on this structure has fields
\Phi(u,z,{\bar{z}})
that are characterised by their weights under

[L_{0},\Phi(0)]=h\Phi(0),\quad[\bar{L}_{0},\Phi(0)]={\bar{h}}\Phi(0).

(10)

We define Carrollian primaries as ones that are annihilated by positive moded generators:

[L_{n},\Phi(0)]=0,\quad[\bar{L}_{n},\Phi(0)]=0,\quad\forall n>0,\qquad[M_{r,s},\Phi(0)]=0\quad\forall\,r\,\text{or}\,s>0.

(11)

The primary operators transform under the conformal Carroll symmetries as

\displaystyle\delta_{L_{n}}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=z^{n+1}\partial_{z}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})+(n+1)z^{n}(h+\frac{u}{2}\partial_{u})\Phi(u,z,\bar{z}),

(12a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{L}_{n}}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle={\bar{z}}^{n+1}\partial_{\bar{z}}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})+(n+1){\bar{z}}^{n}({\bar{h}}+\frac{u}{2}\partial_{u})\Phi(u,z,\bar{z}),

(12b)

\displaystyle\delta_{M_{r,s}}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=z^{r}\bar{z}^{s}\partial_{u}\Phi(u,z,\bar{z})\,.

(12c)

In what follows, we will be interested in computing vacuum correlation functions based on conformal Carroll symmetries. We will consider two and three point functions and for this the Poincare subgroup (or finite conformal Carroll symmetries which come about as the limit of relativistic conformal symmetry) comprising of the ten generators

2.4
Three Point functions

We now consider the three-point function of three Carroll primaries
\Phi_{i}
, with weights

(

G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\langle 0|\Phi_{1}(u_{1},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(u_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\Phi_{3}(u_{3},z_{3},\bar{z}_{3}|0\rangle.

The Ward identities associated with the three point functions are obviously more involved and allows different sub-classes of solutions. Especially different solutions in the delta function branch are very subtle and relevant for different physical scenarios. However for
u

\displaystyle G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\frac{c_{123}}{z_{12}^{h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3}}z_{23}^{h_{2}+h_{3}-h_{1}}z_{31}^{h_{3}+h_{1}-h_{2}}}\times\text{complex conjugate}

The solutions corresponding to the delta function branch are generically trivial and reflect the fact that in Minkowski space the 3-point scattering amplitudes for massless particles are zero due to momentum conservation
Banerjee:2018gce

Here we study the correlation functions of 3d Carroll CFT primaries, keeping in mind the above mentioned scenarios from symmetry considerations. In this work, we will only consider collinear scatterings and will keep the soft three point functions for future work.

To focus on the collinear case, we begin with the ansatz

\displaystyle G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=F(u_{i})\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13}).

The delta function in

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}z^{r}_{i}{z}^{s}_{i}\partial_{u_{i}}G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=0\qquad\forall r,s=0,1.

(27)

Because of the presence of the delta function in the ansatz all these supertarnslation equations effectively become

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{3}\partial_{u_{i}}F(u_{i})=0.

u

\displaystyle F(u_{i})\equiv F(u_{12},u_{23},u_{31})=\sum_{a,b,c}f_{abc}u_{12}^{a}u_{23}^{b}u_{31}^{c}.

(29)

There are still six more equations that would impose global superrotation invariance, which is isomorphic to bulk Lorentz group. Variations with respect to the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic generators are given by

\displaystyle\delta_{L_{n}}G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big[z_{i}^{n+1}\partial_{z_{i}}+(n+1)z_{i}^{n}(h_{i}+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}\partial_{u_{i}})\Big]G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\quad\text{and}

(30a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\bar{L}_{n}}G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big[\bar{z_{i}}^{n+1}\partial_{\bar{z}_{i}}+(n+1)\bar{z_{i}}^{n}(\bar{h}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}\partial_{u_{i}})\Big]G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}).

(30b)

We need to solve these equations for
n=0,\pm 1
. We shall do that for an arbitrary term in the series expansion given by (
29
), i.e.

G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=u_{12}^{a}u_{23}^{b}u_{31}^{c}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13}).

(31)

The
n=-1
equations only imposes translational invariance along
z
and
\bar{z}
. The ansatz we work with already satisfy these conditions. The equation for
n=0
reads

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big[z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}+z_{i}(h_{i}+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}\partial_{u_{i}})\Big]F(u_{i})\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})=0,

(32)

\displaystyle[(-2+\sum_{i=0}^{3}h_{i})+\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c)]G^{3}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=0.

(33)

This implies

\displaystyle a+b+c=-2(-2+\sum_{i}h_{i}).

(34)

Similarly the anti holomorphic equation fixes the anti-holomorphic weights as

\displaystyle a+b+c=-2(-2+\sum_{i}\bar{h}_{i}).

(35)

It can be shown that the equations imposed by special conformal generators
L_{1}
and
\bar{L}_{1}
don’t further add any constraints on the solutions. Together the above two equations become

\displaystyle a+b+c=4-\sum_{i}\Delta_{i},\qquad\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}=0.

(36)

Thus finally we have

\displaystyle G^{(3)}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\sum_{b,c}f_{bc}u_{12}^{4-b-c-\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}}u_{23}^{b}u_{31}^{c}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13}).

(37)

Later in the paper we shall show this correlation function captures the collinear scatterings in Minkowski space.

Finally, let us also mention that if we assume
z
and
\bar{z}
to be independent, then the three point function can have a different non-trivial subclass of solutions. This amounts to working on a celestial torus instead of a celestial sphere. This situation is relevant for (2,2) signature bulk spacetimes
Atanasov:2021oyu
. For these classes of spacetimes, the momentum of the particles are allowed to be complex. Thus there are non-trivial 3-point scattering amplitudes. We will not discuss these cases in this work.

3
Flat Limit of AdS Witten Diagrams

In this section, we elucidate the procedure of taking the limit on AdS Witten diagrams
Witten:1998qj
so as to be able to reach the field theory living on the null boundary of the resulting Minkowski spacetime. The main emphasis again is that the null direction of the boundary Carrollian field theory should play a pivotal role in the whole programme and taking a large radius limit of AdS
4
should not lead to a boundary theory which is a codimension two theory, but rather codimension one.

3.1
A brief history

Before we go on to describing our methods, it is good to take a look back at some important previous work in this direction. The idea of obtaining S-Matrices from AdS/CFT dates back to the original work of Polchinski
Polchinski:1999ry
and Susskind
Susskind:1998vk
. They first sketched a prescription to obtain flat space S Matrices in the large AdS radius limit. The work
Giddings:1999jq
elaborated on this prescription to relate flat space S-Matrices to analogous construction of scattering amplitudes in AdS
Balasubramanian:1999ri
;
Giddings:1999qu

3
3

3

The notion of scattering amplitudes in AdS is subtle because it is tricky to define wavepackets on the boundary of AdS
PhysRevD.94.065017
. Roughly this is because AdS has a timilike boundary (unlike the Minkowski case) and one cannot define
in
and
out
states.
. Most importantly this prescription gave a way to obtain Minkowski space in the center of AdS by a careful rescaling of the bulk coordinates of AdS. The subtleties in the prescription were refined in
Gary:2009ae
where they show that one can obtain the bulk S-Matrices only if the boundary correlators have a particular singularity
structure.

These observations were generalized in
Penedones:2010ue
where it was noticed that the Mellin space representation
Mack:2009mi
;
Mack:2009gy
of the correlation functions of the boundary CFT can be re-interpreted as AdS scattering amplitudes. For massless particles, it was conjectured
Penedones:2010ue
that the bulk S-Matrix is encoded in the large AdS radius limit of the AdS scattering amplitude. This was explicitly checked by computing the Witten diagrams of general scalar theories at tree level and
\phi^{4}
theory at one loop level. This conjecture was explicitly proved in
Fitzpatrick:2011jn
;
Fitzpatrick:2011hu
;
Fitzpatrick:2011dm
by carefully constructing scattering states in AdS that reduced to the usual plane wave basis in the center of AdS. These states correspond to special primary states in the dual CFT description, thereby relating the CFT correlation functions to flat space scattering in the center of AdS. A generalisation for massive scattering particles was addressed in
Paulos:2016fap
. A flat space limit of Witten diagrams in the momentum space was presented in
Raju:2012zr
.

Another parallel approach to obtain bulk S Matrices through the flat space limit of AdS-CFT is by using HKLL Bulk reconstruction techniques
Hamilton:2005ju
;
Hamilton:2006az
;
Hamilton:2006fh
. This approach has been pursued in
Hijano:2019qmi
;
Hijano:2020szl
;
Li:2021snj
. In this approach, one first identifies the Minkowski space in the center of AdS in large AdS radius limit following
Giddings:1999jq
. Within this “scattering region”, one reconstructs the bulk fields in the form of smearing of the CFT operators following HKLL prescription. Since the bulk fields have a mode expansion in terms of the creation and annihilation operators, one obtains scattering states by taking a Fourier transform of the HKLL smearing kernel in the large AdS radius limit. These states are a generalization of the scattering states constructed in
Fitzpatrick:2011jn
. Thus, the analysis of
Hijano:2019qmi
naturally reduces to that of
Penedones:2010ue
when the scattering particles were massless, and it also encompasses
Paulos:2016fap
when the scattering particles were massive.
Li:2021snj
also noted how the flat space limit of momentum space correlators in
Raju:2012zr
can be understood from the point of HKLL Bulk reconstruction techniques.

In this formulation, one uses the LSZ formula for S-Matrix within the “scattering region” and then reconstruct the off-shell bulk correlation functions in terms of the CFT correlators semared over the boundary
Li:2021snj
. The crucial input here is that the smearing regions of the CFT operators were localized around certain time slices of the boundary in the large AdS radius limit. Depending on the choice of the regions, we either get massless scattering or massive scattering. In the global AdS, the operators were smeared around particular future and past time slices given by

\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-1})

(38)

to get massless scattering states. An interpretation of this would be that the CFT data that is required to compute S-Matrices for massless particles is encoded in the region localized around
\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
. This point would be crucial for us when we reproduce Carrollian CFT correlation functions from the large AdS limit of AdS Witten diagrams.

Recent attempts to link flat holography to AdS/CFT using Witten diagrams are
Casali:2022fro
;
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
;
Iacobacci:2022yjo
;
Sleight:2023ojm
. The connection between Celestial amplitudes and Witten diagrams was pointed out in
Lam:2017ofc
where the feautures of the bulk point singularity in AdS Witten diagrams
Gary:2009ae
;
Penedones:2010ue
were seen in Celestial amplitudes of two lower dimensions. Then in particular examples,
Casali:2022fro
were able to obtain Celestial amplitudes in 4D flat space from
AdS_{3}
Witten diagrams.

The relation between Celestial amplitudes and AdS Witten diagrams in two higher dimensions was elaborated in
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
and this serves as a major inspiration for our work. In
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
, they gave a prescription to obtain Celestial amplitudes from AdS Witten diagrams with boundary correlators inserted at specific past and future slices
4
4

4

The intuition comes from the bulk reconstruction perspective of
Hijano:2019qmi
.
.

3.2
Our prescription: keeping track of null retarded time

Our main motivation behind reinvestigating the rather well developed literature, as stated in the introduction and above, is that we are not satisfied with the boundary CFT interpretation of the S-matrices in flat spacetimes. On the one hand, we believe that the dual of Minkowski spacetimes should inherit the symmetries of Minkowski spacetime and one should not have to think about this in terms of a CFT dual to some parent AdS which is the point of view of a majority of the literature. On the other hand, we are uncomfortable with the discarding of the null direction in the Celestial CFT picture, since we think that going from AdS to flat space should not result in the dimensional reduction of the dual theory.

It has already been established in
Bagchi:2022emh
that 3d Carrollian CFTs naturally encode S-matrix elements in 4d asymptotically flat spacetimes through the modified Mellin transformation
Banerjee:2018gce
. We will show below that the modified Mellin wave functions
Banerjee:2018gce
which we will call Carrollian wave functions below, naturally arise from the flat limit of AdS Witten diagrams, when the null direction is properly taken into account.

In what follows, we will be inspired by the analysis of
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
which established a general relation between the large AdS radius limit of AdS Witten diagrams and Celestial amplitudes in two lower dimensions.
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
showed that AdS Witten diagrams with boundary CFT operators inserted at specific past and future slices appropriately reduce to the Celestial amplitudes of two lower dimensions in the limit where the AdS radius is large.

We however consider a generalisation of the insertion points to obtain higher dimensional structures. Let us point out the major assumptions that we work with when evaluating the Witten diagrams:

•

The boundary field theory operators are inserted at global time slices
\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
.

•

The two spheres at
\tau=+\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
and
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
on the boundary are antipodally identified.

This should be contrasted with
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
where they work with:

•

The boundary CFT operators are inserted at global time slices
\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
.

•

The two spheres at
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}
on the boundary are antipodally identified.

As we will see, this will significantly change the analysis of
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
.

The computation of Witten diagrams is greatly simplified in the embedding space formalism
Dirac:1936fq
;
Penedones:2007ns
. To begin with, we will start by analysing the individual building blocks of the AdS Witten diagram namely the external lines, vertices and the internal lines in the spirit of
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
. We will carefully implement the large R limit in the individual blocks of the Witten diagrams such that resulting diagram evaluates to the Carrollian CFT correlation functions. Before we begin this analysis we collect the important elements of the embedding space representation which will be used in the next subsequent sections. Further details on the embedding space representation can be found in Appendix
B
.

The
d
-dimensional AdS solution is described in terms of embedding space coordinates as:

-(X^{0})^{2}-(X^{1})^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{d}(X^{i})^{2}=-R^{2}\,,

(39)

where the embedding space is a
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\times\mathbb{R}^{1,d-2}

ds^{2}=-dX^{+}dX^{-}-(dX^{1})^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}(dX^{i})^{2}\,.

(40)

X^{\pm}=X^{0}\pm X^{d}\,,

(41)

\mathbb{R}^{1,1}

\begin{split}X^{+}&=-\frac{R(\cos\tau-\sin\rho\,\Omega_{d})}{\cos\rho}\,,\quad X^{-}=-\frac{R(\cos\tau+\sin\rho\,\Omega_{d})}{\cos\rho}\,,\\
X^{1}&=-\frac{R\sin\tau}{\cos\rho}\,,\quad X^{i}=R\tan\rho\,\Omega_{i}~~~~~i=2,\dots,d-1\,,\end{split}

represents the coordinates on

ds^{2}=\frac{R^{2}}{\cos^{2}\rho}\left(-d\tau^{2}+d\rho^{2}+\sin^{2}\rho\,d\Omega_{S^{d-2}}^{2}\right)\,,

(43)

where
\tau\in(-\infty,\infty)

Before we go into the details of the formulation, we would like to motivate our choice for the operator insertions on the boundary theory, i.e.

\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}

It is clear that one reaches the boundary when

As a sanity check, we insert this into the boundary metric:

ds^{2}_{\text{bdy}}=-\frac{1}{R^{2}}du^{2}+d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}

We see that in the limit of

(54)

For reaching

3.3
Bulk to boundary propagator

With the setup of the embedding space formulation, we now begin the analysis of the building blocks of Witten diagrams under a large AdS radius
R
expansion. We first address the bulk to boundary propagator.

The bulk to boundary propagator
\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})
in the embedding space is given by
Penedones:2010ue
;
Penedones:2007ns
:

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=\frac{C_{\Delta}^{d}}{(-2\textbf{p}\cdot\textbf{x}+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}\,,

(55)

where

C_{\Delta}^{d}=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1)R^{\frac{(d-1)}{2}-\Delta}}\,.

(56)

A derivation of this is given in Appendix
B
. Here we have parametrized the bulk and boundary points by
\textbf{x}\in(\tau,\rho,\Omega)
and
\mathbf{p}\in(\tau_{p},\Omega_{\textbf{p}})
respectively.

We now substitute eq.(
46
) in eq.(
55
) and do a series expansion of the bulk to boundary propagator under a large
R
limit to arrive at

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=C_{\Delta}^{d}\frac{1}{(R\cos\tau_{p}+t\sin\tau_{p}-r\Omega_{\textbf{p}}\cdot\Omega+O(R^{-1})+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}\,.

(57)

In arriving at this result, we used eq.(
47
) and eq.(
45
). A derivation of eq.(
57
) is given in Appendix
C.1
. Naively, this expression vanishes as
R\to\infty
. To obtain non-zero leading order contributions to the bulk to boundary propagator we crucially impose the condition

\tau_{p}=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}\,,

(58)

where
u
is a new parameter. This should be contrasted with
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
where they strictly set
\tau=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
. This constitutes the main deviation of our approach when compared to
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
. Under this assumption, choosing
\tau_{p}=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
, the bulk to boundary propagator of eq.(
55
) is given by:

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=C^{d}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{(-u+t-r\Omega_{p}\cdot\Omega+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}+O(R^{-1})\right)\,.

(59)

This can be rewritten as

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=C^{d}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{(-u-\tilde{q}\cdot x+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}+O(R^{-1})\right)\,,

(60)

where
x=(t,r\Omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,d}
is a point in the bulk region and the vector
\tilde{q}=(1,\Omega_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{1,d}
is a null vector in the direction of the boundary point
\textbf{p}\in(\tau_{p},\Omega_{p})
.

Similarly for
\tau_{p}=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}

55
) under the large R series expansion is given by:

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=C^{d}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{(u+\tilde{q}\cdot x+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}+O(R^{-1})\right)\,,

(61)

where
\mathbf{x}
is the same as the one defined above but the
\tilde{q}=(1,\Omega_{\textbf{p}}^{A})
, where crucially, we have

\Omega_{\textbf{p}}^{A}=-\Omega_{\textbf{p}}\,,

(62)

which is the antipodal point of
\Omega_{\textbf{p}}
. The spheres at
\tau_{p}=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
and
\tau_{p}=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
are antipodally matched. This is a generalization of the antipodal matching condition proposed in
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
. Of course, when we set
u=0

58
) and the matching condition eq.(
62

PipolodeGioia:2022exe
.

The leading order bulk-to-boundary propagators are in a Carrollian primary basis associated with a modified Mellin transform

\int^{\infty}_{0}d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta-1}\,e^{\mp i\omega u}e^{\mp i\omega\,\tilde{q}\cdot x}e^{-\epsilon\omega}=\dfrac{(\pm i)^{\Delta}\Gamma(\Delta)}{(\mp u\mp\tilde{q}\cdot x+i\epsilon)^{\Delta}}\,.

\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=N_{\Delta}^{d}\psi_{\Delta,\tilde{q},u}^{\pm}(x)+O(R^{-1})\,,

\psi_{\Delta,\tilde{q},u}^{\pm}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}-1}e^{\mp i\omega(\tilde{q}\cdot x+u)}e^{-\epsilon\omega}\,.

(65)

N_{\Delta}^{d}=\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta}}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1)R^{\frac{(d-1)}{2}-\Delta}}\,.

(66)

Vertices

Internal lines

\delta(a\,x)=\delta(x)/a
. Hence, under the large R expansion of the bulk to bulk propagator is:

\left(\Box_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}+O(R^{-2})-\frac{\Delta(\Delta-d)}{R^{2}}\right)\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x}_{1},\textbf{x}_{2})=i\delta_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}(x_{1},x_{2})+O(R^{-2})\,.

(74)

This implies a series expansion of the propagator

\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x}_{1},\textbf{x}_{2})=G(x_{1},x_{2})+O(R^{-2})\,,

(75)

where
G(x_{1},x_{2})
obeys the equation

\left(\Box_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}-m^{2}\right)G(x_{1},x_{2})=i\delta_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,\quad m\equiv\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta}{R}\,.

(76)

Since
\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x}_{1},\textbf{x}_{2})
computes the time-ordered two-point function in AdS, it necessarily means that
G(x_{1},x_{2})
has to be the Feynman propagator. Eq.(
76
) implies that if we are considering massless exchanges in the
R\to\infty
limit, we should have
\Delta\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. Presumably, for massive exchanges we should have,
\Delta\sim\mathcal{O}(R)
. We will not have anything to say about massive exchanges in the explicit computations of our paper.

3.6
Construction of the diagrams

Now that we have all the required structures in eq.(
61
), eq.(
60
), eq.(
68
), eq.(
75
), we can combine them to get the complete diagrams; in doing so, we can always have a series expansion of these diagrams under the large R expansion. The term we are interested in is the leading order term.

The explicit form of the propagators in the large
R
limit are derived to be:

\begin{split}&\text{incoming:}\quad\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})=N_{\Delta_{1}}^{d}\psi_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}^{-}(x)+O(R^{-1})\,,\\
&\text{outgoing:}\quad\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{y})=N_{\Delta_{2}}^{d}\psi_{\Delta_{2},\tilde{q}_{2},u_{2}}^{+}(y)+{O{(R^{-1})}}\,,\\
&\text{bulk-bulk:}\quad\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})=G(x,y)+O(R^{-2})\,,\end{split}

(77)

where,

\begin{split}\psi_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}^{-}(x)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\,\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}e^{i\omega_{1}(\tilde{q}_{1}\cdot x+u_{1})}e^{-\epsilon\omega_{1}}\,,\\
\psi_{\Delta_{2},\tilde{q}_{2},u_{2}}^{+}(y)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}e^{-i\omega_{2}(\tilde{q}_{2}\cdot y+u_{2})}e^{-\epsilon\omega_{2}}\,,\\
G(x,y)&=\int\frac{d^{d+1}k}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}\frac{e^{ik\cdot(x-y)}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,,\end{split}

(78)

and the normalisation constant
N_{\Delta_{i}}^{d}
is given by

N_{\Delta_{i}}^{d}=\frac{(\mp i)^{\Delta_{i}}}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\Delta_{i}-\frac{d}{2}+1)R^{\frac{(d-1)}{2}-\Delta_{i}}}\,.

(79)

As a quick sanity check, one can see that the incoming and outgoing wave functions of eq.(
77
) satisfy the flat space
\mathbb{R}^{1,d}
wave equation
Banerjee:2018gce
by using the representation of eq.(
63
):

\Box_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}\textbf{K}_{\Delta}(\textbf{p},\textbf{x})=N^{d}_{\Delta}\Box_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}\int^{\infty}_{0}d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta-1}\,e^{-i\omega u}e^{-i\omega\,\tilde{q}\cdot x}\propto-\omega^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}=0\,,

(80)

as
\tilde{q}^{2}=0
.

A general Witten diagram with appropriate vertices, internal lines and external lines will be of the form

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\dots O_{\Delta_{i}}(\textbf{p}_{i})O_{\Delta_{i+1}}(\textbf{p}_{i+1})O_{\Delta_{i+2}}(\textbf{p}_{i+2})\dots O_{\Delta_{j}}(\textbf{p}_{j})\rangle\\
=(i\mu)^{V}\int_{\text{AdS}_{d+1}}d^{d+1}\textbf{x}_{1}\dots d^{d+1}\textbf{x}_{V}\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x}_{k},\textbf{x}_{l})\dots\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x}_{k}^{\prime},\textbf{x}_{l}^{\prime})\\
\times\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x}_{k})\dots\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{i}}(\textbf{p}_{i},\textbf{x}_{k}^{\prime})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{i+1}}(\textbf{p}_{i+1},\textbf{x}_{l})\dots\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{j}}(\textbf{p}_{j},\textbf{x}_{l}^{\prime})\,,\end{split}

(81)

where
V
denotes the number of vertices and there are appropriate number of external lines connected to the bulk-to-bulk propagator. For example, a four point function with the
\phi^{3}
interaction will be of the form

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})O_{\Delta_{4}}(\textbf{p}_{4})\rangle=&(i\mu)^{2}\int_{AdS_{d+1}}d^{d+1}\textbf{x}\,d^{d+1}\textbf{y}\,\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})\\
&~~\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3},\textbf{y})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{4}}(\textbf{p}_{4},\textbf{y})\,.\end{split}

(82)

Now suppose,
\textbf{p}_{m}
are inserted at
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{m}}{R}
for
m=1,\dots,i
and at
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{n}}{R}
for
n=i+1,\dots,j
, then using the form of the incoming, outgoing wavefunctions, and the bulk to bulk propagators derived in eq.(
77
), we can simplify eq.(
81
) in the large
R
limit as

\begin{split}&\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})\dots O_{\Delta_{i}}(\textbf{p}_{i})O_{\Delta_{i+1}}(\textbf{p}_{i+1})\dots O_{\Delta_{j}}(\textbf{p}_{j})\rangle\\
&=(\prod^{j}_{a=1}N^{d}_{\Delta_{a}})(i\mu)^{V}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}d^{d+1}x_{1}\dots d^{d+1}x_{V}\,G(x_{k},,x_{l})\dots G(x_{k}^{\prime},x_{l}^{\prime})\,\psi^{-}_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}(x_{k})\dots\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\psi^{-}_{\Delta_{i},\tilde{q}_{i},u_{i}}(x_{k}^{\prime})\,\psi^{+}_{\Delta_{i+1},\tilde{q}_{i+1},u_{i+1}}(x_{l})\dots\psi^{+}_{\Delta_{j},\tilde{q}_{j},u_{j}}(x_{l}^{\prime})+O(R^{-1})\,.\end{split}

(83)

For example in eq.(
82
), if
\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{p}_{2}
are inserted in
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{1}}{R}
and
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{2}}{R}
respectively, and
\textbf{p}_{3},\textbf{p}_{4}
are inserted in
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{3}}{R}
and
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{4}}{R}
respectively, we get

\begin{split}\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})O_{\Delta_{4}}(\textbf{p}_{4})\rangle=&(i\mu)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,d}}d^{d+1}x\,d^{d+1}y\,G(x,y)\\
&~~\psi_{\Delta_{1},q_{1},u_{1}}^{-}(x)\psi_{\Delta_{2},q_{2},u_{2}}^{-}(x)\psi_{\Delta_{3},q_{3},u_{3}}^{+}(y)\psi_{\Delta_{4},q_{4},u_{4}}^{+}(y)\,.\end{split}\end{split}

(84)

We will explicitly evaluate eq.(
83
) for specific cases of the two point and the three point correlation function in the next section.

4
Carroll Correlators from Witten Diagrams

In this section, we will combine all the ingredients of Section
3
to construct AdS Witten diagrams in a large
R
series expansion. We show that the Carrollian CFT correlation functions naturally arise as the leading order term in eq.(
83
).

To compare with the explicit calculations, we will work in
3+1
dimensions because
\text{BMS}_{4}
was used to constrain the correlation functions of the field theory. But it is important to stress that the procedure outlined in the previous section is valid for any dimensions. Returning to the case that we are interested in at the moment, i.e.
3+1
dimensions, we will use the following parametrization for the null vector in the direction of
\Omega_{p}
:

\tilde{q}^{\mu}=\left[1,\dfrac{z+\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}},\dfrac{-i(z-\bar{z})}{1+z\bar{z}},\dfrac{1-z\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}\right]\,.

(85)

The topology of the null boundary is
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
(z,\bar{z})
characterize the coordinates on the celestial sphere. The role of dimensions come in when we try to split the momentum conserving delta functions into a delta function in energies and the delta function in the directions.
We will now consider various
n
-point Witten diagrams in the large AdS radius limit.

4.1
Two point function

Figure 2
:
Two point Witten diagram. Red lines denote bulk to boundary propagators; Blue line denotes bulk to bulk propagator.

We begin with the two-point Witten diagram given by

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle=\int_{\text{AdS}_{4}}d^{4}\textbf{x}d^{4}\textbf{y}\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{y})\,,

(86)

where
\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})
is inserted at
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{1}}{R}
(ingoing) and
\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{y})
is inserted at
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{2}}{R}
(outgoing). This is diagramatically denoted by figure
2
where the red lines denote the bulk to boundary propagators and the blue line denotes the internal bulk to bulk propagator. This is a particular case of eq.(
81
).

Thus, the leading order term of eq.(
83
) for eq.(
86
) for the particular insertions is given by

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle\simeq N^{3}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,G(x,y)\psi^{-}_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}(x)\psi^{+}_{\Delta_{2},\tilde{q}_{2},u_{2}}(y)\,.

(87)

This evaluates to

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle\simeq&N_{\Delta_{1}}^{3}N_{\Delta_{2}}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,d\omega_{1}\,d\omega_{2}\,d^{4}k\,\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}e^{-\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}\\
&\times e^{i\omega_{1}.u_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{2}.u_{2}}\frac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,.\end{split}

(88)

Using the properties of Dirac delta function, and a series of integrations the above expression can be simplified resulting in the final expression given by central result of this paper:

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle=\mathcal{A}\frac{\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})}{(i(u_{2}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}}\,,

(89)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a normalization constant given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\frac{(2\pi)^{4}N_{\Delta_{1}}^{3}N_{\Delta_{2}}^{3}\Gamma(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2)}{m^{2}}=\frac{4\pi(-i)^{\Delta_{1}}i^{\Delta_{2}}}{m^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2)}{\Gamma(\Delta_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\Delta_{2}-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{1}{R^{2-(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})}}\,.

(90)

The detailed calculation is given in Appendix
C.2
. The result (
89
) matches with the Carrollian CFT two-point correlation function discussed in Sec. 2 (eq.(
23
)) up to normalisation.

Before we go on to the three point function, let us briefly comment on the normalisation. It seems that in order for the normalisation to be non-zero in the
R\to\infty
limit, we require
\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}=2
. This is reminiscent of the restriction of
\Delta=1
encountered in
Donnay:2022aba
;
Donnay:2022wvx
. But we should point out that there is also the mass term
m
in the bulk-to-bulk propagator which shows up in the normalisation constant and needs to go to zero at the end of the calculation. Saying something concrete about this constant is thus subtle and requires a more thorough investigation along the lines of adapting holographic renormalisation techniques to flat spacetimes which we will not address in this work. It is very likely that the restriction on the weights would disappear once this is done carefully. Similar consideration would hold for the normalisation of the three-point function which we go on to describe next.

4.2
Three point function

Figure 3
:
Three point Witten diagrams. Red lines denote bulk to boundary propagators; Blue line denotes bulk to bulk propagator.

To obtain a non-zero three point function, we need a
\phi^{3}
interaction in the bulk. There are three possible diagrams that can arise: two incoming particles can exchange an operator with one outgoing particle; one incoming particle can exchange an operator with two outgoing particles; one contact diagram. Interestingly, we find that for generic momentum, the three point function vanishes, which is of course in keeping with the flat space result from momentum conservation. But the nice thing is that we can take a limit of non-zero AdS Witten diagram answers and reach this outcome. In the previous section, we had seen that from symmetry analysis, one can get non-trivial answers for the three point functions when the momenta are collinear. Here we reproduce this answer from a bulk computation starting with an AdS Witten diagram and taking the suitable limit.

4.2.1
Generic momenta

Let us consider the diagram with two incoming particles exchanging an operator with one outgoing particle. This is diagramatically denoted in figure
3
. The AdS Witten diagram is given by

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle=&(i\mu)\int_{\text{AdS}_{4}}d^{4}\textbf{x}\,d^{4}\textbf{y}\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})\\
&~~\times\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3},\textbf{y})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{x})\,,\end{split}

(91)

where we will insert
\textbf{p}_{1}
at
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{1}}{R}
,
\textbf{p}_{2}
at
\tau=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{2}}{R}
and
\textbf{p}_{3}
at
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{3}}{R}
. According to the general analysis of section
3
((i.e.) eq.(
83
)), as
R\to\infty
, we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\simeq~&(i\mu)N^{d}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{d}_{\Delta_{2}}N^{d}_{\Delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}\,d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,G(x,y)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\psi^{-}_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}(x)\,\psi^{-}_{\Delta_{2},\tilde{q}_{2},u_{2}}(x)\,\psi^{+}_{\Delta_{3},\tilde{q}_{3},u_{3}}(y)\,.\end{split}

(92)

Using the incoming and outgoing wavefunctions derived in eq.(
77
), we can simplify the integrand by performing integrations. One can show (see Appendix
C.3.1
for details) that the integrand is proportional to

\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})\,.

(93)

Thus, we have

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\simeq 0\,,

(94)

for the two incoming-one outgoing diagram. This is because
\tilde{q}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
. One cannot have momentum conservation in
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
with just three momenta. One can see that from

(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})^{2}=2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{1}\cdot\tilde{q}_{2}~\bm{\neq}~\omega^{2}_{3}\tilde{q}^{2}_{3}\,.

(95)

This means that the argument of the delta function (
93
) is not satisfied and always gives zero. From symmetry, we can see that the diagram with one incoming particle exchanging an operator with two outgoing particles also evaluates to zero.

For the contact diagram in figure
3
also, we have a similar analysis. We have
6
6

6

We can consider all the possible cases of incoming and outgoing wavefunctions.

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle=(i\mu)\int_{\text{AdS}_{4}}d^{4}\textbf{x}\,\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3},\textbf{x})\textbf{K}_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{x})\,.

(96)

Thus, in the
R\to\infty
limit of eq.(
83
), we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle&\simeq N^{3}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{2}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}d^{4}x\,\psi^{\pm}_{\Delta_{1},\tilde{q}_{1},u_{1}}(x)\,\psi^{\pm}_{\Delta_{2},\tilde{q}_{2},u_{2}}(x)\,\psi^{\pm}_{\Delta_{3},\tilde{q}_{3},u_{3}}(x)\\
&\propto\delta^{(4)}(\mp\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}\mp\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}\mp\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3})=0\,.\end{split}

(97)

We see that for generic momentum, the three point function evaluates to zero in the large
R
limit. This matches with the expected result
Banerjee:2018gce
, where we also have a vanishing three point function.

4.2.2
Collinear limit

From eq.(
95
), we see that if
\tilde{q}_{1}=\tilde{q}_{2}
, we can still have a non-zero result because now the three particles become collinear. Thus, the split of the momentum conserving delta function becomes

\delta^{4}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\omega^{3}_{3}}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta(z_{12})\delta(z_{13})\delta(\bar{z}_{12})\delta(\bar{z}_{13})\,.

(98)

This particular momentum conserving delta function will ensure that eq.(
92
) is non-zero. Since,
\tilde{q}_{1}=(1,\Omega_{1})
,
z_{1}=z_{2}=z_{3}
ensured by the delta functions in eq.(
98
) implies
\tilde{q}_{1}=\tilde{q}_{2}=\tilde{q}_{3}
which corresponds to the collinear case. In fact, substituting eq.(
98
) in eq.(
92
), we get the following result

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})&O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}\dfrac{{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}\,\Gamma(k+\Delta_{1})\,\Gamma(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-k-4)}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+k}\,(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-4-k}}\,.\end{split}

(99)

Here
\mathcal{A}_{(3)}
is a normalization constant analogous to eq.(
90
). The result is valid for
\Delta_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta_{3}\geq 4
. A derivation of eq.(
99
) is given in Appendix
C.3.2
. This result matches with the intrinsic Carrollian CFT result derived from the Ward identities of the Carrollian CFT in Sec. 3 (in particular eq.(
36
) and eq.(
37
)).

We emphasize that we don’t really need the internal propagator to arrive at the results of eq.(
89
) and eq.(
99
). If we had started from the contact diagram of eq.(
97
), the final result would be the same apart from a factor of
\frac{1}{m^{2}}
. The imprint of the mass of the exchanged operator will show up in the normalization constants like
\mathcal{A}
of eq.(
90
). This will not show up if you consider contact diagrams.

Some features

We now illustrate certain interesting features of the result of eq.(
99
). For
\Delta_{3}=4
in eq.(
99
), we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{4}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta_{1})\Gamma(\Delta_{2})}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}}\,(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}}}\,\\
&\propto\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(100)

Upto an overall normalization, this sort of special factorization in terms of the two point function of eq.(
89
) seems to only work for
\Delta_{3}=4
. To be precise, for
\Delta_{3}=4
, we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})&O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\\
&=\dfrac{m^{2}\,N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}}}{(2\pi)^{4}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}-2}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}-2}}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{3}-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(101)

For higher
\Delta_{3}
, we have a slightly more complicated factorization in terms of two point functions.

For
\Delta_{3}=5
in eq.(
99
) we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{5}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\left[\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta_{1}+1)\Gamma(\Delta_{2})}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+1}\,(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}}}\right.\\
&\left.~~~+\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta_{1})\Gamma(\Delta_{2}+1)}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}}\,(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}+1}}\right]\,\\
&\propto\left[\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{3}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\right.\\
&~~~~+\left.\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{3}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\right]\,.\end{split}

(102)

This is more complicated than the factorization in eq.(
101
) in that it involves two point correlators of lower
\Delta_{3}
as well. One can see that the generic three point function given by eq.(
99
) also has this factorization. To see it notice that

\begin{split}\dfrac{\Gamma(k+\Delta_{1})\delta^{2}(z_{31})}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+k}}&=\dfrac{m^{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}N^{3}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{3}_{k+2}}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{k+2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,,\\
\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-k-4)\delta^{2}(z_{32})}{(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-k-4}}&=\dfrac{m^{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}N^{3}_{\Delta_{2}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}-k-2}}\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}-k-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(103)

Since
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=\Omega_{3}
, one can substitute eq.(
103
) in eq.(
99
) to obtain

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle=\dfrac{m^{2}(i\mu)}{(2\pi)^{4}}\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}\dfrac{N^{d}_{\Delta_{3}}\,{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}}{N^{3}_{k+2}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}-k-2}}&\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{k+2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\\
&~~\times\langle O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}-k-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(104)

Interestingly, for
u_{2}=u_{1}
(
\textbf{p}_{1}=\textbf{p}_{2}
) in eq.(
100
) for
\Delta_{3}=4
(the strict “fully collinear limit”), we get

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta_{1})\Gamma(\Delta_{2})}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}\,}\,\\
&\propto\langle O_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{3}-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(105)

The operator dimensions of
O_{\Delta_{1}},O_{\Delta_{2}}
seem to add up resulting in an effective operator of dimension
\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}
. This structure is exactly the two point function derived in eq.(
89
) upto a diverging
\delta^{2}(0)
. This structure is present in the generic three point function in the collinear limit as well. For instance, setting
u_{1}=u_{2}
in eq.(
99
), we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})&O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\dfrac{\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}\Gamma(k+\Delta_{1})\Gamma(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-k-4)}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-4}}\\
&\propto\langle O_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{3}-2}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(106)

This completes our present analysis of the three point function from AdS Witten diagrams.

As a final point, we note that the bulk to boundary propagator is valid for particular Poincaré patches
Penedones:2007ns
. We consider insertion points at
\mathcal{O}(R^{-1})
regions about
\tau_{p}=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
and the scattering process occurs at the center of AdS. The regime of validity of the propagator inserted at
\tau_{p}=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-1})
and the regime of validity of the propagator inserted at
\tau_{p}=+\frac{\pi}{2}+\mathcal{O}(R^{-1})
overlap in a region that encompasses the center of the AdS. The subtlety of the validity of the bulk to boundary propagator of eq.(
55
) will not matter to us. With this we conclude our analysis of the large radius limit of AdS Witten diagrams.

5
Discussions

Our work in a nutshell

In our work, we have built a prescription of obtaining correlation functions of a
co-dimensional one
holographic dual of asymptotically flat spacetimes by taking a large radius limit of AdS Witten diagrams and carefully keeping track of the null retarded (or advanced) time direction of the null boundary of the resulting flat spacetime. This is encapsulated in eq (
83
). In particular we have shown that by taking this infinite radius limit on Witten diagrams in AdS
4
, one can reproduce correlation functions of
three dimensional
field theory that is invariant under the (asymptotic) symmetries of 4d flat spacetime. This 3d dual field theory is a Carrollian CFT that lives on the entire null boundary and not just on the celestial sphere. By this method, we have reproduced (upto normalisations) the two and three point functions of the Carrollian CFT in the so-called delta function branch which we obtained first by using conformal Carroll Ward identities. Our construction generalises that of
PipolodeGioia:2022exe
, the results of which we can simply obtain by putting the null coordinate to zero. As a by-product of our analysis, we have obtained an antipodal matching condition (
62
) which is now valid for all retarded and advanced times and hence holds on the whole of the null boundary and not only on the celestial sphere.

Importance

When it was established that Carrollian CFT correlation functions connect to the one higher dimensional S-matrix elements in flat spacetimes via the modified Mellin transformations, it was unclear how or if at all this holographic correspondence was connected to AdS/CFT. The Carrollian CFT correlators in the delta function branch don’t seem to have any direct connection to relativistic CFT correlation functions since e.g. the Carrollian 2-point function (
23
) can be non-zero even when the primary fields have unequal scaling dimensions and it is unclear how to relate this to the zero answer from the relativistic CFT. This hence was a potential stumbling block in understanding holography in asymptotically flat spacetimes from a large radius limit of AdS/CFT through the Carrollian perspective. Our work in this paper shows how to resolve this issue by working in embedding space and considering the null direction.

Future directions

There are numerous directions of interesting work that would emerge from our explorations in this paper. Below we mention a few important ones.

Our prescription now provides a very general picture which now can be used to import well-known results from AdS/CFT to understand features of co-dimension one duals of asymptotically flat spacetimes. One of the immediate goals would be to understand the nature of four-point functions and what this means for Carrollian blocks and the conformal bootstrap programme in these field theories. While there has been work in understanding BMS blocks in 2d field theories
Bagchi:2016geg
;
Bagchi:2017cpu
, it would be particularly intriguing to work out the subtleties associated with the delta-branch of the correlators.

Another direction of immediate interest is how to generalise this set up to include scattering of massive particles. While it seems that one can include scaling dimensions that are proportional to the AdS radius while taking the limit and possibly land up on massive scattering, the fact that all massive particles end up not on
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, but at
i^{+}
may complicate matters.

While our procedure outlined in Sec. 3 is dimension independent, we have focussed on bulk dimensions
d=4
. In some sense,
d=3
is more tractable and it would be good to analyse this in detail and compare and contrast to existing literature on the subject, including in particular
Bagchi:2015wna
.

The methods outlined in our work can be looked upto a first step in an attempt to reformulate the bulk reconstruction programme of HKLL
Hamilton:2005ju
;
Hamilton:2006az
;
Hamilton:2006fh
and adapt it to asymptotically flat spacetimes. This is of course a long-term goal. Again, there has been some work in the direction of bulk reconstruction for 3d Minkowski spacetimes
Hartong:2015usd
. It would be good to revisit this in terms of a limit from AdS
3
/CFT
2
.

Finally, our paper gives some further insight as to how 3d Carrollian CFT and 2d Celestial CFTs may be related and it indicates that the lower dimensional Celestial CFT may be obtained as a restriction of a higher dimensional Carroll CFT to a constant
u
slice. It would be good to make this connection concrete.

A.1
Mellin transformations and Celestial Primaries

The basic observables in asymptotically flat spacetimes are the scattering amplitudes. Celestial CFT emerges as dual description of gravity theories in asymptotically flat spacetimes as it can successfully encapsulate the scattering amplitudes in the bulk. According to this proposal the dual field theory is a 2d CFT that lives on the celestial sphere of null infinity. The holographic correspondence between the S-matrix in flat spacetime and the dual celestial CFT is given by
Pasterski:2017kqt
:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{2}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})...\mathcal{O}_{n}(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}^{\Delta_{i}-1}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i},\sigma_{i})

(107)

On the left hand side we have the correlation functions of the primary operators
\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})
on celestial sphere and on the right we have Mellin transformations of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
, which denotes the
n
-point scattering amplitudes of massless particles in the bulk.

Let’s discuss about this formula in a little more detail. We are interested in scattering of massless particles. A suitable parametrisation of the four momentum of a massless particle is

p^{\mu}=\omega\Big(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z}\Big)

(108)

Here
\omega
carries the information about the energy of the particle and
z
and
\bar{z}
represents the stereographic coordinates on the celestial sphere. The action of Lorentz group in this parametrisation is manifest as the group of global conformal transformations on celestial sphere. The double cover of the Lorentz group, SL(2,C) acts on the particle momenta
p^{\mu}
as

\displaystyle z\to z^{\prime}=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\quad\bar{z}\to\bar{z}^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}},\quad\omega\to\omega^{\prime}=\omega(cz+d)(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})\omega

(109)

The single particle states in the bulk can be obtained by the Fock space creation operators
a(p^{\mu},\sigma)
/
a^{\dagger}(p^{\mu},\sigma)
. For example a state with momentum
p^{\mu}
and helicity
\sigma
is created from the vacuum by

\displaystyle|p^{\mu},\sigma\rangle=a^{\dagger}(p^{\mu},\sigma)|0\rangle

(110)

In compact notation we shall write these as
a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z},\sigma)
, where
\epsilon=\pm 1
decides if this a creation or an annihilation operator. Using these operators in the bulk we can define a local field
\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})
in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{\Delta-1}a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z},\sigma).

(111)

The weights
h
and
\bar{h}
of these operators can be determined in terms of
\Delta
and spin
\sigma
as

\displaystyle h+\bar{h}=\Delta\quad\text{and}\quad h-\bar{h}=\sigma.

(112)

Now as the scattering amplitudes in the bulk can be viewed as the correlation function of these creation and annihilation operators, they can be recast into the correlators of these local fields
\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})
on celestial sphere giving rise to (
107
).

Transformation rules of these operators under the bulk Poincare group can also be figured out directly using (
111
). The Lorentz group acts on celestial sphere as a group of global conformal transformations.

\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})
can transforms like a SL(2,
\mathbb{C}
) primary field with conformal weights
h
and spin
\bar{h}
. i.e,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\to\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(113)

However the action of the bulk translations
p^{\mu}\to p^{\mu}+l^{\mu}
on these primaries is non-trivial. Consider for example the time translation in the bulk generated by the bulk Hamiltonian
H
. The celestial primary field transforms by
H
in the following way

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{\Delta-1}[H,a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z},\sigma)]

(114)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\omega^{\Delta-1}[-\epsilon\omega(1+z\bar{z})a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z},\sigma)]

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(1+z\bar{z})\mathcal{O}_{h+\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

The action of the other translation generators are similar. One obvious peculiarity in this relation is the shift of the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic weights of these celestial primaries. The other relations are given by

\displaystyle[P^{1},\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})]

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(z+\bar{z})\mathcal{O}_{h+\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z}),

(115)

\displaystyle[P^{2},\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})]

\displaystyle=i\epsilon(z-\bar{z})\mathcal{O}_{h+\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle[P^{3},\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})]

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(1-z\bar{z})\mathcal{O}_{h+\frac{1}{2},\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}}(z,\bar{z})

We believe this changing of operator weights under spacetime translations is not a desirable feature and will now discuss a natural prescription to fix this.

A.2
Carroll and Modified Mellin

Although celestial CFTs have been very successful in capturing the scattering amplitudes and their infrared behaviour, our motivation is to describe the flat spacetimes holographically in terms of a co-dimension one field theories that live on the whole of the null boundary of flat spacetimes. To us, this is more natural because it is possible to understand the connection of flat holography with AdS/CFT by taking a suitable zero cosmological limit and this does not reduce the dimension of the dual field theory. These so called Carrollian CFTs on the null boundary have also shown to be able to describe the scattering processes in asymptotically flat spacetimes
Bagchi:2022emh
. To understand the possible relation of these Carroll CFTs and celestial CFT, we discuss the ‘Heisenberg picture’ of the above construction.

Let’s consider the evolution of
\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})
by the Hamiltonian
H

\displaystyle\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=e^{-iHU}\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})e^{iHU}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\,\omega^{\Delta-1}e^{-iHU}a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z})e^{iHU}

(116)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\,\omega^{\Delta-1}e^{-i\epsilon\omega u}a(\epsilon\omega,z,\bar{z}).

Here we have rescaled
U
and have defined
u=U(1+z\bar{z})
. In the last line we have obtained the
modified Mellin transformation

Banerjee:2018gce
. This newly defined field
\Phi^{\epsilon}(u,z,\bar{z})
lives on a 3d space spanned by
u,z
and
\bar{z}
. We can confirm this is the null boundary by deriving the transformation rules of this field
\Phi^{\epsilon}(u,z,\bar{z})
. In similar lines to celestial CFT, we can show under Lorentz transformations given by (
109
),
\Phi^{\epsilon}(u,z,\bar{z})
transforms as
Banerjee:2018gce

\displaystyle\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\to\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{(cz+d)^{2h}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}}}\Phi^{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{u}{|cz+d|^{2}},\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}+\bar{d}}\Big)

(117)

Infinitesimally this transformation is same as (
12
) for
n=0,\pm 1
. This is the action of Lorentz group on the null boundary (
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
).

Now, the action of the bulk translations are given by
Banerjee:2018gce

\displaystyle\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\to\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u^{\prime},z^{\prime},\bar{z}^{\prime})=\Phi^{\epsilon}_{h,\bar{h}}(u+p+qz+r\bar{z}+sz\bar{z},z,\bar{z})

(118)

These transformations are again equivalent to (
12
) for
r,s=0,1
. The noteworthy difference here with the previous transformations of the Celestial primary operators (
114

115

u
co-ordinate and this makes the operators transform more naturally
Banerjee:2018gce
. These transformation properties certify that the above construction gives rise to a field on
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
.

Having this construction at hand one would be able to relate the scattering amplitudes in asymptotically flat spacetimes and the correlation function of a co-dimension one field theory that lives on the whole of the null boundary
\mathscr{I}^{\pm}
. This formula is given by

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}(u_{1},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}^{\epsilon_{2}}(u_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})....\Phi_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n},z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\omega_{i}e^{-i\epsilon_{i}\omega_{i}u_{i}}\omega_{i}^{\Delta_{i}-1}\mathcal{S}_{n}

(119)

On the one hand, from a purely flat space point of view, this makes the Mellin amplitudes finite
Banerjee:2019prz
. On the other, we now have a way to relate correlation functions on the co-dimension one dual Carrollian CFT to scattering amplitudes in the bulk.

A.3
Two point correlator

Now let us consider the simplest case of free propagating amplitudes. If a massless particle starts off at
\mathscr{I}^{-}
and arrives at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
without any interaction in the deep interior. Then this amplitude is given by

\displaystyle\langle p_{1},\sigma_{1}|p_{2},\sigma_{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p_{1}}\delta^{3}(p_{1}-p_{2})\delta_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}}=4\pi^{3}\frac{\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})}{\omega_{1}}\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z_{2})\delta_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2},0}

(120)

If we just take this expression and plug it in the modified Mellin transformation of S-matrix elements (
119
), we should expect to obtain the two point correlation function of 3d Carrollian CFT. So we do this and get:

\begin{split}\langle&\Phi_{1}(u_{1},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(u_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\rangle\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt=4\pi^{3}\delta_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2},0}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}e^{-i\omega_{1}u_{1}}e^{i\omega_{2}u_{2}}\frac{\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})}{\omega_{1}}\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z_{2})\end{split}

(121)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{1}(u_{1},z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(u_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\rangle=4\pi^{3}\Gamma(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2)\frac{\delta^{2}(z_{1}-z_{2})}{i(u_{1}-u_{2})^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}}\delta_{\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2},0}

(122)

This matches up with the two point function associated with the delta function branch that we derived from the symmetry arguments in (

\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2,d},\hskip 56.9055pt\textbf{x}^{2}=-R^{2}

The Laplacian on

\Box_{\text{AdS}_{d+1}}=\bm{\partial}^{2}+\textbf{x}\cdot\bm{\partial}(d+\textbf{x}\cdot\bm{\partial})\,.

This implies that if a scalar field satisfies the massless Klein Gordon equation in the embedding space,

\bm{\partial}^{2}\psi(\textbf{x})=0\,,

This can be solved by Wick rotating to the Hyperbolic space

[\Box_{\text{H}_{d+1}}-\Delta(\Delta-d)]\Pi_{\Delta}(\textbf{x},\bar{\textbf{x}})=-\,\delta(\textbf{x},\bar{\textbf{x}})\,.

The propagator is thus given by

\begin{split}C^{d}_{\Delta}&=\dfrac{1}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\Gamma(\Delta-\dfrac{d}{2}+1)}\\

(131)

(132)

Since

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle=&N_{\Delta_{1}}^{3}N_{\Delta_{2}}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,d\omega_{1}\,d\omega_{2}\,d^{4}k\,\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}e^{-\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}\\
&\times e^{i\omega_{1}.u_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{2}.u_{2}}\frac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,.\end{split}

(145)

Now

\begin{split}&\int d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,d^{4}k\,\dfrac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=(2\pi)^{4}\int d^{4}y\,d^{4}k\,\dfrac{\delta^{4}(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+k)e^{-i(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=(2\pi)^{4}\int d^{4}y\dfrac{e^{-i(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1})\cdot y}}{\omega^{2}_{1}\tilde{q}^{2}_{1}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=(2\pi)^{8}\dfrac{\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1})}{m^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(146)

where we have been able to write the final step because
\tilde{q}^{2}_{1}=0
. We note that since
\tilde{q}_{1}
corresponds to ingoing momenta,
\tilde{q}_{1}=(1,-\Omega_{1})
because of the antipodal matching condition of eq.(
62
) with the parametrization given by eq.(
85
). Now comes the crucial point. In the delta function above, we must compare the directions in the sphere at one time slice. The direction of
\tilde{q}_{2}
is with respect to the sphere at
\tau_{p}=+\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{2}}{R}
. The direction of
\tilde{q}_{1}
is however, with respect to the sphere at
\tau_{p}=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{1}}{R}
. We use the antipodal matching condition of eq.(
62
) to express
\tilde{q}_{1}
as
\tilde{q}_{1}=(1,\Omega_{1})
where now the direction is with respect to the sphere at
\tau_{p}=+\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u_{1}}{R}
. If we set
u=0
, we are essentially comparing directions in one sphere at
\tau_{p}=+\frac{\pi}{2}
. In the parametrization of eq.(
85
), the antipodal point is reached by
z\to-\frac{1}{\bar{z}}
. Hence we have

\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{1}=(1,-\Omega_{1})\xrightarrow{z\to-\frac{1}{\bar{z}}}(1,\Omega_{1})=\left[1,\dfrac{z_{1}+\bar{z}_{1}}{1+z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}},\dfrac{-i(z_{1}-\bar{z}_{1})}{1+z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}},\dfrac{1-z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}}{1+z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}}\right]\,.

(147)

If we didn’t have the antipodal matching condition, we would have gotten the unphysical

\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1})\propto\delta^{(3)}(\Omega_{2}+\Omega_{1})\propto\delta(z_{2}+z_{1})\delta(\bar{z}_{2}+\bar{z}_{1})\,.

(148)

This will not satisfy the translation invariance. The antipodal matching condition eq.(
62
) ensures that crucial minus signs are present. Comparing the directions at the sphere at
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{u}{R}
essentially means we are looking at the scattering amplitude from the perspective of future null infinity
\mathscr{I}^{+}
only.

Thus, we can split the
3+1
dimensional delta function to simplify the final step of eq.(
146
) as

\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{2}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{1})=\dfrac{1}{\omega_{1}}\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\delta(z_{2}-z_{1})\delta(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z}_{1})\,.

(149)

The RHS is effectively a three dimensional delta function because of the null condition
\tilde{q}^{2}=0
. This combination is the lorentz invariant combination as can be seen from the analysis of Appendix
C.4
. Thus, eq.(
146
) evaluates to

(2\pi)^{8}\dfrac{\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})}{\omega_{1}m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,,

(150)

where
\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})=\delta(z_{21})\delta(\bar{z}_{21})
. Substituting this result in eq.(
88
), we have

\displaystyle\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{4}N_{\Delta_{1}}^{3}N_{\Delta_{2}}^{3}\int d\omega_{1}\,d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\,\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}\,e^{-\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}

(151)

\displaystyle\times e^{i\omega_{1}.u_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{2}.u_{2}}\frac{\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})}{\omega_{1}m^{2}+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{4}N_{\Delta_{1}}^{3}N_{\Delta_{2}}^{3}\int d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-3}e^{-2\epsilon\omega}e^{i\omega(u_{1}-u_{2})}\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})\,.

One can evaluate the final
\omega
integral using the following expression

\int d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta-k}e^{i\omega u}=(-iu)^{-1+k-\Delta}\Gamma(1-k+\Delta)\hskip 19.91684pt\text{if}~~~\text{Re}(k-\Delta)<1\,,~\text{Im}(u)>0\,.

(152)

Crucially, we have been able to use this formula because of the
e^{-2\epsilon\omega}
term that was present in eq.(
151
) due to the
i\epsilon
prescription. This makes the integrals like these convergent. Thus, using eq.(
152
) in eq.(
151
), we obtain the main result of our paper given by eq.(
89
):

\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})\rangle=\mathcal{A}\frac{\delta^{2}(z_{2}-z_{1})}{(i(u_{2}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}}\,.

(153)

C.3
Three point function calculation

We will outline the calculations concerning the three point function analyzed in section
4.2
.

C.3.1
Generic momenta

For the case of generic ingoing and outgoing momenta, we first show eq.(
93
). If we substitute eq.(
77
) into eq.(
92
), we have

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\simeq(i\mu)N^{3}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{2}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,3}}d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,d\omega_{1}\,d\omega_{2}\,d\omega_{3}\,d^{4}k\\
\times\omega^{\Delta_{1}-1}_{1}\omega^{\Delta_{2}-1}_{2}\omega^{\Delta_{3}-1}_{3}e^{i\omega_{1}u_{1}}e^{i\omega_{2}u_{2}}e^{-i\omega_{3}u_{3}}e^{-\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}\\
\times\dfrac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,.\end{split}

(154)

Consider a part of the integrand

\begin{split}&\int d^{4}y\,d^{4}k\,d^{4}x\,\dfrac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=\int d^{4}y\,d^{4}k\,\dfrac{(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)e^{-i(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=\int d^{4}y\,\dfrac{e^{-i(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})\cdot y}}{(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=(2\pi)^{8}\dfrac{\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{1}\cdot\tilde{q}_{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,\end{split}

(155)

which proves eq.(
93
).

C.3.2
Collinear limit

In this section, we will prove the result of three point function in the collinear limit given by eq.(
99
). We start with the momentum conserving delta function of eq.(
98
)

\delta^{4}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\omega^{3}_{3}}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta(z_{12})\delta(z_{13})\delta(\bar{z}_{12})\delta(\bar{z}_{13})\,.

(156)

This particular split of the delta function is lorentz invariant via the analysis of Appendix
C.4
. Since
\tilde{q}_{1}=(1,\Omega_{1})
is given by eq.(
85
), we see that
\tilde{q}_{1}=\tilde{q}_{2}=\tilde{q}_{3}
corresponds to the collinear case when
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=\Omega_{3}
. For this case, we have from eq.(
155
) in the analysis of Appendix
C.3.1
,

\begin{split}&\int d^{4}y\,d^{4}k\,d^{4}x\,\dfrac{e^{i(\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}+\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2}+k)\cdot x}e^{-i(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}+k)\cdot y}}{k^{2}+m^{2}+i\epsilon}\\
&~=(2\pi)^{8}\dfrac{\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})}{m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,\\
&~=(2\pi)^{8}\dfrac{\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{3}(z_{13})}{\omega^{3}_{3}\,m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,.\end{split}

(157)

In the last step, one should keep in mind the subtle role played by the antipodal matching condition of eq.(
62
) elaborated in Appendix
C.2
. Substituting the above equation in eq.(
154
), we find

\begin{split}\langle O_{\Delta_{1}}(\textbf{p}_{1})O_{\Delta_{2}}(\textbf{p}_{2})O_{\Delta_{3}}(\textbf{p}_{3})\rangle\simeq\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}-1}_{1}\omega^{\Delta_{2}-1}_{2}\omega^{\Delta_{3}-4}_{3}\\
\times e^{i\omega_{1}u_{1}}e^{i\omega_{2}u_{2}}e^{-i\omega_{3}u_{3}}e^{-\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\,,\end{split}

(158)

where

\mathcal{A}_{(3)}=\dfrac{(2\pi)^{4}(i\mu)N^{3}_{\Delta_{1}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{2}}N^{3}_{\Delta_{3}}}{m^{2}+i\epsilon}\,.

(159)

We can perform the integral over
\omega_{3}
using the delta function in eq.(
158
) to obtain,

\begin{split}&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}-1}_{1}\omega^{\Delta_{2}-1}_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})^{\Delta_{3}-4}e^{i\omega_{1}(u_{1}-u_{3})}e^{i\omega_{2}(u_{2}-u_{3})}e^{-2\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}-1}_{1}\omega^{\Delta_{2}-1}_{2}\\
&\hskip 128.0374pt\times\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}\,\omega^{k}_{1}\omega^{\Delta_{3}-4-k}_{2}e^{i\omega_{1}(u_{1}-u_{3})}e^{i\omega_{2}(u_{2}-u_{3})}e^{-2\epsilon(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}\int d\omega_{1}\,\omega^{\Delta_{1}+k-1}_{1}e^{i\omega_{1}(u_{1}-u_{3})}e^{-2\epsilon\omega_{1}}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\times\int d\omega_{2}\,\omega^{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-5-k}_{2}e^{i\omega_{2}(u_{2}-u_{3})}e^{-2\epsilon\omega_{2}}\\
&=\mathcal{A}_{(3)}\delta^{2}(z_{12})\delta^{2}(z_{13})\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta_{3}-4}\dfrac{{}^{\Delta_{3}-4}C_{k}\,\Gamma(k+\Delta_{1})\,\Gamma(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-k-4)}{(i(u_{3}-u_{1}))^{\Delta_{1}+k}\,(i(u_{3}-u_{2}))^{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-4-k}}\,.\end{split}

(160)

In the second step, we have used the binomial expansion and in the final step, we have used the formula eq.(
152
) (the
i\epsilon
prescription should appropriately justify the formula). The result is valid for
\Delta_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta_{3}\geq 4
. This proves eq.(

C.4
Lorentz invariance of delta functions

In this section, we will check that the split of the momentum conserving delta functions given in eq.(
149
) and eq.(
98
) will preserve lorentz invariance. In this process, one must ensure that the lorentz symmetry is preserved. In
3+1
dimensions,
SO(3,1)\equiv SL(2,\mathbb{C})
. Thus, one can use the action of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
on the celestial sphere to check the lorentz symmetry of the delta function. Under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
,

z\to\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\hskip 14.22636pt,~~ad-bc=1

(161)

Thus,

z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}\to\dfrac{z_{ij}}{(cz_{i}+d)(cz_{j}+d)}\,.

(162)

To find the transformation of the energies
\omega_{i}
under
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, we must look at the Mandelstam invariants
s_{ij}=(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}
, where
p^{\mu}_{i}=\omega_{i}\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{i}
with
\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{i}
given by the parametrization of eq.(
85
). Lorentz invariance of the Mandelstam invariants would require

\omega_{i}\to(cz_{i}+d)(c\bar{z}_{i}+d)\,\omega_{i}~~~\text{under}~~~SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,.

(163)

Let us check this for eq.(
149
):

\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{2}\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{2}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}^{\mu}_{1})=\dfrac{1}{\omega_{1}}\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\delta(z_{2}-z_{1})\delta(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z}_{1})\,.

(164)

\delta^{2}(z_{12})
will set
z_{1}=z_{2}
,
\bar{z}_{1}=\bar{z}_{2}
. Thus, under an
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation given by eq.(
161
), we have the following

\begin{split}\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})&\to\delta\left((c^{2}z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}+dc\bar{z}_{1}+cdz_{1}+d^{2})(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\right)=\dfrac{\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})}{(cz_{1}+d)(c\bar{z}_{1}+d)}\\
\delta(z_{12})&\to\delta\left(\dfrac{z_{12}}{(cz_{1}+d)(cz_{2}+d)}\right)=(cz_{1}+d)^{2}\delta(z_{12})\\
\delta(\bar{z}_{12})&\to\delta\left(\dfrac{\bar{z}_{12}}{(c\bar{z}_{1}+d)(c\bar{z}_{2}+d)}\right)=(c\bar{z}_{1}+d)^{2}\delta(\bar{z}_{12})\\
\dfrac{1}{\omega_{1}}&\to\dfrac{1}{\omega_{1}}\dfrac{1}{(cz_{1}+d)(c\bar{z}_{1}+d)}\end{split}

(165)

Multiplying the terms in the RHS, we find that eq.(
149
) is lorentz invariant.

Now let us check the lorentz invariance of eq.(
98
):

\delta^{4}(\omega_{3}\tilde{q}_{3}-\omega_{1}\tilde{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\tilde{q}_{2})=\dfrac{1}{\omega^{3}_{3}}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta(z_{12})\delta(z_{13})\delta(\bar{z}_{12})\delta(\bar{z}_{13})\,.

(166)

To make the analysis simple, let us use a particular non-trivial case of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
transformation of eq.(
161
) given by
a=0,b=1,c=-1,d=0
:

z\to-\dfrac{1}{z}\,.

(167)

Thus, we have

z_{ij}\to\dfrac{z_{ij}}{z_{i}z_{j}}~~~,~~~\bar{z}_{ij}\to\dfrac{\bar{z}_{ij}}{\bar{z}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}}~~~,~~~\omega_{i}\to z_{i}\bar{z}_{i}\omega_{i}

(168)

This implies

\begin{split}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})&\to\delta(z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}))=\dfrac{1}{z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}}\delta(\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\\
\delta(z_{ij})&\to\delta\left(\dfrac{z_{ij}}{z_{i}z_{j}}\right)=z^{2}_{1}\delta(z_{ij})\\
\delta(\bar{z}_{ij})&\to\delta\left(\dfrac{\bar{z}_{ij}}{\bar{z}_{i}\bar{z}_{j}}\right)=\bar{z}^{2}_{1}\delta(\bar{z}_{ij})\\
\dfrac{1}{\omega^{3}_{3}}&\to\dfrac{1}{\omega^{3}_{3}}\dfrac{1}{(z_{1}\bar{z}_{1})^{3}}\end{split}

(169)

The extra factors are

\dfrac{1}{(z_{1}\bar{z}_{1})^{3}}\dfrac{1}{z_{1}\bar{z}_{1}}\,z^{2}_{1}\,z^{2}_{1}\,\bar{z}^{2}_{1}\,\bar{z}^{2}_{1}=1\,.

(170)

This proves that the delta function split in eq.(
98
) is Lorentz invariant.

Jacobi–Lie Models and Supergravity Equations
Ladislav Hlavatý, Ivo Petr

Abstract
Poisson–Lie T-duality/plurality was recently generalized to Jacobi–Lie T-plurality formulated in terms of Double Field Theory and based on Leibniz algebras given by structure coefficients f a ​ b c , f c a ​ b , f_{ab}{}^{c},f_{c}{}^{ab}, and Z a , Z a Z_{a},Z^{a} . We investigate three- and four-dimensional sigma models corresponding to six-dimensional Leibniz algebras with f b b ​ a ≠ 0 f_{b}{}^{ba}\neq 0 , Z a = 0 Z^{a}=0 . We show that these algebras are plural one to another and, moreover, to an algebra with f b b ​ a = 0 f_{b}{}^{ba}=0 , Z a = 0 Z^{a}=0 . These pluralities are used for construction of Jacobi–Lie models. It was conjectured that plural models should satisfy Generalized Supergravity Equations. We have found examples of models satisfying “true” Generalized Supergravity Equations where no trivialization to usual Supergravity Equations is possible. On the other hand, we show that there are also models corresponding to algebras with f b b ​ a ≠ 0 f_{b}{}^{ba}\neq 0 , Z a = 0 Z^{a}=0 where the Killing vector appearing in Generalized Supergravity Equations either vanishes or can be removed by suitable gauge transformation. Such models then satisfy usual Supergravity Equations, i.e. vanishing beta function equations.

) on semi-Abelian Lie bialgebra
(3|1)
where the Poisson bivector also vanishes. However, then we have
\Delta=0
,
\operatorname{e}^{-2\Delta}=1
, and we get backgrounds with constant scalar curvature (the sign of
R
depends of the choice of
\lambda_{i}
in
E_{0}
) that do not satisfy Generalized Supergravity Equations for any
\Phi
or
\mathcal{J}
.

4
Jacobi–Lie T-plurality

Jacobi–Lie T-plurality was described in
[
17
]
in the DFT language. Transformation of metric and
\mathcal{B}
-field is very similar to the Poisson–Lie T-plurality of models constructed from Drinfel’d doubles. Namely, we first transform the constant matrix
E_{0}
by formula

\hat{E}_{0}=\left(\left(P+E_{0}\cdot R\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(Q+E_{0}\cdot S\right)\right)^{T}

(31)

where the matrices
P,Q,R,S
are
D\times D
blocks of the transformation matrix
C^{-1}

C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}P&Q\\
R&S\end{pmatrix},\qquad T_{A}=(C^{-1})_{A}{}^{B}\,\hat{T}_{B}

and then apply formulas for construction of Jacobi–Lie models given in Section
3
.

More difficult is the transformation of dilaton. Before we can apply the formula (
23
) we have to transform function
\varphi
. For that we have to compute generalized fluxes
\mathcal{F}_{A}
associated with generalized vielbein
\mathcal{E}_{A}{}^{M}\equiv\operatorname{e}^{\omega(x)}E_{A}{}^{M}(x)
and DFT dilaton
d
through

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{A}\equiv\mathcal{W}^{B}{}_{AB}+2\,\mathcal{D}_{A}d=\mathcal{E}_{A}{}^{M}\,F_{M}=\operatorname{e}^{\omega(x)}F_{A}

(32)

where

\mathcal{W}_{ABC}\equiv-\mathcal{D}_{A}\mathcal{E}_{B}{}^{M}\,\mathcal{E}_{MC}\,,\quad\mathcal{D}_{A}\equiv\mathcal{E}_{A}{}^{M}\,\partial_{M},\quad\operatorname{e}^{-2d}=\operatorname{e}^{-2\Phi}\sqrt{|\det\mathcal{G}|}\,.

(33)

Finally we find for
f_{b}{}^{ba}=0,\ Z^{a}=0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{M}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}F_{M}.

(34)

Transformed function
\hat{\varphi}
is then obtained from the transformation of the flux
F_{A}:=\operatorname{e}^{-\omega(x)}\mathcal{F}_{A}

\hat{F}_{A}=C_{A}{}^{B}F_{B}.

(35)

However, for the algebras of type 2, where
\hat{f}_{b}{}^{ba}\neq 0,\ \hat{Z}^{a}=0
, it may happen that the r.h.s. of (
34
) is not gradient of a function of
x^{m}
. Instead, we may define form
\hat{Y}=\hat{Y}_{M}\,d\hat{x}^{M}
,

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{M}:=\frac{1}{2}\hat{F}_{M}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{D}\\
\frac{\hat{f}_{b}{}^{ba}\,\hat{v}_{a}^{m}}{2\tilde{\sigma}}\end{pmatrix}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{m}\hat{\varphi}(x)\\
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{m}(x)\end{pmatrix}^{T}

(36)

where
\hat{v}_{a}^{m}
are components of left-invariant vector fields of the group associated with
\hat{\bf{g}}
. It turns out that
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{m}(x)
then are components of Killing vector field that occurs in the Generalized Supergravity Equations for the Jacobi–Lie plural model. Values of functions
\hat{\varphi}
and
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{m}
for various type 2 algebras are given in the Table
2
. It is evident that if
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{m}
vanishes then corresponding plurality produces Jacobi–Lie model that satisfies usual Supergravity Equations. On the other hand, there are cases with non-vanishing
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{m}
where
\mathrm{d}\hat{X}=0
and we can get rid of the Killing vector field by the gauge transformation (
6
). The model then satisfies usual Supergravity Equations - see e.g.
5.2.1
.

Let us note in the end that Jacobi–Lie T-duality
T_{a}\leftrightarrow T^{a}
for
Z_{a}\neq 0
is not useful for construction of new models as it transforms type 1 and type 2 algebras to the type 3 where
\hat{X}_{0}\neq 0
, and these produce Jacobi–Lie models dependent on dual DFT coordinates.

7
Conclusions

We have investigated examples of three- and four-dimensional Jacobi–Lie models corresponding to Leibniz algebras, introduced in
[
17
]
as type 2, i.e. those with
f_{b}{}^{ba}\neq 0,Z^{a}=0
. They are displayed in the Table
1
. It turned out that these algebras are mutually related by Jacobi–Lie T-plurality and, moreover, related to algebra

\{3|1\}:=\left(\left(III,-T^{2}+T^{3}\right),\left(I,0\right)\right)

with
f_{b}{}^{ba}=0,Z^{a}=0
.

We have found Jacobi-Lie models that satisfy Generalized Supergravity Equations that need introduction of nontrivial Killing vector. As a side product we have shown that even for type 2 algebras there are also Jacobi–Lie models where standard dilaton given by (
23
) is sufficient and no Killing vector is necessary for satisfaction of Supergravity Equations. Results are summarized in Tables
3
and
4
.

For construction of these models we have used Jacobi–Lie T-plurality. We have started with flat backgrounds and vanishing dilatons obtained as Jacobi–Lie models corresponding to the type 1 algebra
\{3|1\}
, and transformed them to Jacobi–Lie models corresponding to the type 2 algebras. Let us note that results of Jacobi–Lie T-plurality depend both on matrices
C
that define the plurality by (
12
) and form of the flat background given by different
E_{0}
.

Important criterion for decision whether a transformed background, dilaton and Killing satisfy usual or Generalized Supergravity Equations is (non)vanishing of two-form
\mathrm{d}X
where

X_{\mu}:=\partial_{\mu}\Phi+\mathcal{J}^{\kappa}{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa\mu},

and dilaton
\Phi
given by (
23
) and Killing
\mathcal{J}
by (
36
).

If
\mathrm{d}X=0
, the corresponding model satisfies vanishing beta function equations, while for
\mathrm{d}X\neq 0
we get models satisfying Generalized Supergravity Equations with nontrivial Killing field given by (
36
). It can happen that even if
\mathcal{J}\neq 0
, the form
\hat{X}
appearing in the Generalized Supergravity Equations (
1
)-(
3
) can be (locally) closed and the gauge transformation (
6
) can change dilaton and bring components of
\mathcal{J}
to zero, so that in fact the model satisfies usual Supergravity Equations (see e.g.
5.2.1
).

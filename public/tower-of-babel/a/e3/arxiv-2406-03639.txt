Abstract
. We undertake a novel approach to the existence problem for gravitating vortices on a Riemann surface based on symplectic reduction by stages, which seems to be new in the PDE as well as the gauge theory literature. The main technical tool for our study is the reduced α \alpha -K-energy, for which we establish convexity properties by means of finite-energy pluripotential theory, as recently applied to the study of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics. Using these methods, we prove that the existence of solutions to the gravitating vortex equations on the sphere implies the polystability of the effective divisor defined by the zeroes of the Higgs field. This approach also enables us to establish the uniqueness of gravitating vortices in any admissible Kähler class, in the absence of automorphisms. Lastly, we also prove the existence of solutions for the gravitating vortex equations for genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 for certain ranges of the coupling constant α \alpha and the volume.

1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the existence problem for the gravitating vortex equations.
Let
\Sigma
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
. Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
\Sigma
and
\bm{\phi}\in H^{0}(\Sigma,L)
a holomorphic section of
L
. We fix a parameter
0<\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a
coupling constant
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The gravitating vortex equations for a Kähler metric on
\Sigma
with Kähler form
\omega
and a Hermitian metric
h
on
L
are

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}iF_{h}+\frac{1}{2}(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\tau)\omega=0,\\
S_{\omega}+\alpha(\Delta_{\omega}+\tau)(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\tau)=c.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here
F_{h}
is the curvature of the Chern connection of
h
,
\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}
is the pointwise norm of
\bm{\phi}
with respect to
h
,
S_{\omega}
is the scalar curvature of
\omega
and
\Delta_{\omega}
is the Laplace operator for the metric
\omega
, whose action on a smooth function
f
is defined by
\Delta_{\omega}f\omega=2i\bar{\partial}\partial f
. The constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is topological and is obtained by integrating (
1.1
) over
\Sigma
to be the following.

c=\frac{2\pi(\chi(\Sigma)-2\alpha\tau N)}{V},

(1.2)

where
V=\int_{\Sigma}\omega
and

N=\int_{\Sigma}c_{1}(L).

The first equation in (
1.1
) is known as the abelian vortex equation, and for fixed
\omega
it is well known (see
[
12
,
27
,
28
,
42
]
and Appendix
A
) that the existence of solutions with
\bm{\phi}\neq 0
(called vortices) is equivalent to

V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}.

(1.3)

The second equation in (
1.1
) reflects a back-reaction of the vortex on the Kähler metric
\omega
, whose intensity is measured by the coupling constant
\alpha
, similarly to matter couples to the gravitational field in Einstein’s field equations in general relativity, with a push proportional to the gravitational constant. This analogy motivates referring to the solutions of (
1.1
) as
gravitating vortices
, as first introduced in
[
3
]
.

Beyond this appealing analogy, a striking observation made in
[
3
]
, based on earlier work by Linet
[
36
]
and Comtet-Gibbons
[
17
]
(see also
[
49
]
), is that, in the special case
c=0
and genus
g=0
, the gravitating vortices can be regarded as special solutions of the Einstein-Maxwell-Higgs equations in general relativity
[
13
,
16
,
23
,
45
]
. The solutions obtained from (
1.1
) impose a natural product ansatz
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\times\Sigma
for the space-time metric and minimize a suitable energy functional. Having a special physical significance, they are referred to as
cosmic strings
, while the corresponding equations are known as the Einstein–Bogomol’nyi equations or the self-dual Einstein–Maxwell–Higgs equations. Partly motivated by this interesting relation to physics, there has been some recent activity in the study of the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
), both in the complex geometry
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
25
,
43
,
52
,
53
]
and PDE communities
[
31
,
32
]
.

The basic formal structure underlying the equations (
1.1
) is provided by the theory of Hamiltonian reduction in symplectic geometry. The gravitating vortex equations describe the zeros of a moment map for the Hamiltonian
action of an infinite-dimensional gauge group
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}

[
2
,
5
]
, on a phase space given by unitary connections, Higgs fields, and complex structures on the underlying smooth surface
S
. Compared to the moment map intepretation of other equations in complex geometry, as for the renown constant scalar curvature (cscK) problem
[
22
,
24
]
, the main novelty here is the structure of the group of symmetries, given by a non-trivial extension of the form

1\to\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{H}\to 1,

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the group of Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms of a fixed symplectic structure on the surface
S
and
\mathcal{G}
is the gauge group of a unitary line bundle with degree
N
.

As discovered in
[
2
]
, the special structure of the
extended gauge group

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
reflects on a higher level of difficulty on the existence problem for the corresponding moment map equations. Not only does the group of symmetries lack a formal complexification, as is the case for the cscK problem in Kähler geometry
[
22
]
, but also the corresponding infinite-dimensional symmetric space (whose points are given by pairs
(\omega,h)
as in (
1.1
)) does not admit a natural Riemannian metric compatible with the characteristic affine connection (see
[
2
, Remark 4.4]
). This symmetric space, denoted by abuse of notation
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, has furthermore an intricate submersion structure with totally geodesic fibres
[
2
, Proposition 3.17]
over the space of Kähler potentials
[
37
]
. This weak structure of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
prevents us from directly applying to (
1.1
) the strong methods from pluripotential theory recently developed for the study of the space of Kähler potentials
[
14
]
, and so succesfully applied to the cscK problem
[
21
]
.

Motivated by this problem, and building upon the symplectic interpretation of the gravitating vortex equations
[
5
]
, in the present work we propose and carry out a novel approach to the existence problem for gravitating vortices on a Riemann surface based on symplectic reduction by stages, which seems to be new both in the PDE and in the field theory literature (cf. Example No. 3 in
[
40
, pp. 108–109]
). This approach is a two-step process in which we first consider the symplectic reduction by the normal subgroup
\mathcal{G}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, and then consider the residual Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
-action. In the Kähler context, this procedure is known as Kähler reduction by stages, and we shall explain the main formal ingredients in Section
2.1
, in a situation where one has a finite dimensional Kähler manifold. Effectively, in our setup, the reduction by stages approach to gravitating vortices stands for considering the following PDE

S_{\omega}+\alpha(\Delta_{\omega}+\tau)(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h_{\omega}}^{2}-\tau)=c

(1.5)

for a Kähler metric
\omega
on a fixed Riemann surface
\Sigma
. Here,
h_{\omega}
is the unique solution to the abelian vortex equation with respect to
\omega
established in
[
12
,
27
,
28
,
42
]
.
From the analytical viewpoint, a remarkable fact about the previous equation is that it is a non-local PDE, depending on global information on
\Sigma
via a (simple) stability condition.

Using our new approach, in the present work we prove three main results which constitute an important step forward in the theory for the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
). In our first theorem we establish the first uniqueness result for the equations (
1.1
) for arbitrary genus
g
, giving a complete solution to
[
5
, Conjecture 5.5]
in the stable case. We should stress the fact that our next theorem provides, in particular, the first known uniqueness result for the self-dual Einstein–Maxwell–Higgs equations, corresponding to the case
c=0
(cf.
[
13
]
). Namely, we have the following result (Theorem
5.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose either
(i)

g(\Sigma)\geq 1
, or
(ii)

g(\Sigma)=0
and
\bm{\phi}
vanishes at least at
3
points. Then smooth solutions to the gravitating vortex equations with fixed volume
V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}
are unique.

Before going into the specifics of our other two main theorems, let us briefly summarise the state of the art of the existence problem for the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
). The study of the existence problem for (
1.1
) in the special case
c=0
and genus
g=0
goes back to Spruck and Yang
[
47
,
48
]
, where the equations
reduce to a single PDE for a function on the sphere and have the aforementioned interesting relation to physics.
An existence theorem
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
was proven for surfaces of genus
g\geq 2
.
The case of genus
g=1
was studied in
[
3
]
, wherein it was shown that the gravitating vortex
equations (with
\bm{\phi}\neq 0
) always have a solution in the weak coupling limit

0<\alpha\ll 1
(see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
for a
precise formulation).

Unlike the cases of genus
g\geq 1
, it was shown in
[
5
]
that in
genus
g=0
there are obstructions to the existence of solutions of (
1.1
), making the
problem comparatively closer to the Calabi problem on the existence of Kähler–Einstein
metrics, where algebro-geometric stability obstructions appear on
compact Kähler manifolds with
c_{1}>0
. Based on the moment map interpretation of the gravitating vortex
equations, it was suspected that in genus
g=0
the existence of solutions had to be equivalent to some stability condition.

The first evidence pointing out to such a correspondence lay in Yang’s existence result
[
50
]
for (
1.1
) when
c=0
and
V\gg\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}
(see
[
31
,
32
]
for an alternative proof). In this case,
\Sigma
is forced topologically to be
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and (
1.1
) are known as the Einstein–Bogomol’nyi equations or the self-dual Einstein–Maxwell–Higgs equations. Yang’s result was extended in
[
25
]
to the case
c\geq 0
and any admissible volume
V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}
. These results can be reformulated in the language of Mumford’s Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT) as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
25
,
50
,
51
]
)
.

Suppose that (
1.3
) is satisfied and that
\alpha\in(0,1/\tau N]
. Let
D=\sum_{j}n_{j}p_{j}

be an effective divisor on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
corresponding to a pair
(L,\bm{\phi})
.
Then, the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
) on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},L,\bm{\phi})
have
solutions with volume
V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}
, provided that the divisor
D
is GIT polystable for the
canonical linearized
\textup{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-action on the space of effective
divisors.

In
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
the authors stated the following converse to Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},L,\bm{\phi})
admits a solution of the gravitating vortex
equations with
\alpha>0
, then (
1.3
) holds and the
divisor
D
is polystable for the
\textup{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-action.

While the result in Theorem
1.3
is right, it turns out that the proof given in
[
5
]
is not. More specifically, the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
contains an essential flaw (which nevertheless does not affect other parts of the paper). The initial goal of the present work was to give a correct proof, which we achieve in Theorem
5.7
.

In this paper we also improve the existence results given in
[
5
]
for the genus
g\geq 1
. Namely, we prove the following result (see Theorem
5.8
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
g\geq 1
. Assume the following three numerical conditions

(1)

V-\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}>0
,

(2)

\alpha\tau\left(\frac{8\pi N}{\tau}-V\right)<\frac{2g-2}{N}\left(V-\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}\right)
,

(3)

2\alpha\tau m<1
,

where
m
is the maximum multiplicity of the zeroes of
\bm{\phi}
. Then there exists a unique smooth solution to the gravitating vortex equations.

The significance of our main results is better understood from the point of view of moduli theory and the classical Teichmüller spaces. Ever since the pioneering work of Fricke and Teichmüller, it has been convenient to enhance the geometric data of the Riemann surface
\Sigma
with marked points or other types of decoration. The simplest decoration for which this moduli space has not been so well studied yet is the case of an effective divisor
D
on the surface, and the results of this paper pave the way to a complex analytic approach (cf., e.g.,
[
33
, Section 2.1.3]
, for the corresponding algebro-geometric moduli problem). As a matter of fact, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
directly imply that in genus
g\geq 1
, and for an effective range of the parameter
\alpha
, a suitable moduli space of solutions to (
1.1
) can be interpreted as a Teichmüller space for pairs
(\Sigma,D)
(cf.
[
5
, Section 3.3]
).

In the special case of genus
g=0
, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, combined with the main results in
[
25
,
50
]
, establish a bijection between an open set in the moduli space of gravitating vortices with fixed volume
V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}
and coupling constant
\alpha\in(0,1/\tau N]
and the stable locus of the moduli space of binary quantics, given by the GIT quotient

S^{N}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/\!\!/\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}),

bringing us closer to the solution of
[
5
, Conjecture 5.6]
. Important steps towards the compactification of the moduli spaces of gravitating vortices with
c\geq 0
and genus
g=0
have been taken in
[
25
,
53
]
. In the special case
c=0
, these spaces can be interpreted as moduli spaces of cosmic string solutions to the Einstein-Maxwell-Higgs equations in general relativity, and we speculate that our current developments may have an impact in future advances in theoretical physics, such as the study of cosmic string dynamics
[
46
]
via the method of
moduli space approximation

[
34
,
38
,
39
]
.

An important ingredient emerging from the reduction by stages approach to the study of the gravitating vortex equations is the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy. This is a functional

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}

defined on the infinite-dimensional space
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
of Kähler potentials determined after fixing a reference background Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
with total volume
V>0
. Here,
\mathcal{K}
is the usual K-energy in the space of Kähler potentials
[
37
]
while
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is a new functional which arises via the reduction by stages mechanism. Key to the proof of our main theorems is the fact that this last functional is continuous on the completion of the space of smooth Kähler potentials introduced by Darvas
[
18
]
(see Lemma
3.7
), whereby the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy inherits good semicontinuity properties (see Theorem
3.8
).

Equations (
1.1
) were introduced in
[
3
]
in an attempt to find symmetric solutions to the Kähler–Yang–Mills
equations
[
2
]
. These are equations for a Kähler metric on a compact complex manifold and a Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundles over it. Indeed, solutions to
(
1.1
) are equivalent to
\operatorname{SU}(2)
-invariant solutions the Kähler–Yang–Mills equations on the rank 2 holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, where
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is the Riemann sphere,
similarly to the approach to the abelian vortex equation in
[
27
]
.
The results in this paper can therefore be naturally interpreted whithin the theory for the Kähler–Yang–Mills equations, leading to new existence and uniqueness results.

To our knowledge, this is the first paper where symplectic reduction by stages is used in the context of the study of canonical metrics in Kähler geometry and gauge theory. As illustrated in this work, this is a very powerful tool that may very likely be applied to other situations, including the Kähler–Yang–Mills equations from which gravitating vortices initially emerged.

The present paper is organized as follows: the main formal ingredients of the Kähler reduction by stages are explained in Section
2.1
. In Section
2.3
we give an explicit formula for the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy. Section
3
is devoted to the extension of the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy to the completion of the space of smooth Kähler potentials introduced by Darvas. In Section
4
we study the convexity properties of the (extended) reduced
\alpha
-K-energy. In Section
5
we apply the results obtained in the previous sections on the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy and convexity to prove the main results of our paper, including Theorem

1.3
(Theorem
5.7
), Theorem
1.1
(Theorem
5.3
), and Theorem
1.4
(Theorem
5.8
). An important ingredient for Theorem
1.3
is the proof of properness of the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy given in Theorem
5.6
.

2.
Reduction by stages and gravitating vortices

2.1.
Kähler reduction by stages

In this section we describe some basic aspects of the theory of Kähler reduction by stages, which we will formally apply as a guideline for our main results on the gravitating vortex equations. For a more complete
account of this theory, we refer to
[
29
]
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})
be a symplectic manifold endowed with a symplectic left action of a Lie group
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
. We assume that the group is given by a Lie group extension of the form

1\to\mathcal{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H}\to 1.

(2.1)

The extension (
2.1
) determines an extension of Lie
algebras

0\to\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}\to 0,

(2.2)

where the use of the same symbols
\iota
and
p
should lead to no confusion. as a short exact sequence of vector spaces.
We are interested in the case where (
2.2
) does not split as a sequence of Lie algebras. Assume that the
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-action is Hamiltonian and there exists a
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-equivariant moment map

\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}\colon\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow(\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})^{*}.

Provided that
0\in(\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})^{*}
is a regular value of
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
and that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
acts freely and properly on
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)
, the symplectic reduction of
\mathcal{T}
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, defined by

\mathcal{M}=\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},

inherits a natural structure of symplectic manifold. Given the extension structure of the group
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, the symplectic quotient can be obtained in a two-step process instead: with the same hypothesis, we first take
the symplectic reduction by the normal subgroup
\mathcal{G}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,

\mathcal{N}=\mu_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}(0)/\mathcal{G},

where
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{T}\to(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G})^{*}
is a moment map for the
\mathcal{G}
-action obtained by composition of
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
with the natural map
\iota^{*}\colon(\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})^{*}\to(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G})^{*}
. The symplectic manifold

\mathcal{N}
inherits a residual Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
-action, with moment map

\mu_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H})^{*}.

More explicitly,
\mathcal{H}
is identified with the right quotient
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}/\mathcal{G}
with induced left action

(\tilde{g}\cdot\mathcal{G})\cdot[x]=[\tilde{g}\cdot x]\in\mathcal{N}

and the moment map
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}
is given by

\langle\mu_{\mathcal{H}}([x]),y\rangle=\langle\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(\tilde{x}),\tilde{y}\rangle,

(2.3)

for any
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{T}
such that
[\tilde{x}]=[x]\in\mathcal{N}
and any choice of
\tilde{y}\in\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
such that
p(\tilde{y})=y
. The fact that
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}
is well defined follows easily from the

\mathcal{G}
-equivariance of
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
, using that
x\in\mu_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}(0)
and that the adjoint
\mathcal{G}
-action on
\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
preserves the fibres of
p\colon\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\to\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}
. Secondly, we do Hamiltonian reduction of

\mathcal{N}
by
\mathcal{H}
, obtaining a natural symplectomorphism

\mu_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(0)/\mathcal{H}\cong\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},

induced by the inclusion
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)\subset\mu_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}(0)
.

Assume now that
(\mathcal{T},\mathbf{J},\omega_{\mathcal{T}})
is a Kähler manifold and that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
acts further by holomorphic isometries. Assume that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
is compact and that its complexification
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
acts by biholomorphisms
on
\mathcal{T}
. Then we can define the set of
stable points
for the
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
-action by (see
[
41
]
)

\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}}=\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}\cdot x)\cap\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset\}.

Since we are assuming that the
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-action on
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)
is free, one can prove that
\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
is an open and
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
-invariant subset of
\mathcal{T}
. By the general theory of Kähler reduction, there is a
natural diffeomorphism

\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}

(2.4)

which endowes
\mathcal{M}
with a structure of Kähler manifold. More generally, without the assumption on the isotropy group,
\mathcal{M}
inherits a structure of stratified Kähler space (see
[
29
, Theorem
1.8]
). In the special case that
\mathcal{T}
is a projective manifold, the stable locus can be defined independently of the moment map by means of Geometric Invariant Theory.

As above, the extension structure of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
enables us to construct this Kähler structure in a two-step process. For this, we first consider the set of stable points for the
\mathcal{G}^{c}
-action by

\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\mathcal{G}^{c}}=\{x\in\mathcal{T}\mid(\mathcal{G}^{c}\cdot x)\cap\mu_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset\},

where
\mathcal{G}^{c}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
is the complexification of
\mathcal{G}
. Then, there is a natural diffeomorphism

\mathcal{N}\cong\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\mathcal{G}^{c}}/\mathcal{G}^{c}

(2.5)

which endowes
\mathcal{N}
with a natural structure of Kähler manifold such that
\mathcal{H}
acts by holomorphic isometries. We then consider the complexification
\mathcal{H}^{c}
of
\mathcal{H}
and the corresponding set of stable
points for the induced
\mathcal{H}^{c}
-action, provided that it exists,

\mathcal{N}^{s}_{\mathcal{H}^{c}}=\{x\in\mathcal{N}\mid(\mathcal{H}^{c}\cdot x)\cap\mu_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset\}.

Similarly as before, we obtain now a Kähler isometry (see
[
29
, Theorem 2.1]
)

\mathcal{M}\cong\mu_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(0)/\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{N}^{s}_{\mathcal{H}^{c}}/\mathcal{H}^{c}.

A key point in the previous construction is the existence of the holomorphic
\mathcal{H}^{c}
-action on
\mathcal{N}
. This is ensured, for instance, if
\mathcal{N}
is compact or if
\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\mathcal{G}^{c}}
is a complex analytic
subset of
\mathcal{T}
.

The upshot of the previous construction is a criterion to decide whether a point
x\in\mathcal{T}
is represented in the complex quotient
\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}}/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}
, that is, when

(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}\cdot x)\cap\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset.

(2.6)

For simplicity, and since this is the case in the situation of our interest, we shall assume

\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\mathcal{G}^{c}}=\mathcal{T},

that is, for any given
x\in\mathcal{T}
there exists
g_{x}\in\mathcal{G}^{c}
(unique up to the
\mathcal{G}
-action) such that

\mu_{\mathcal{G}}(g_{x}\cdot x)=0.

Then, in order to check whether (
2.6
) holds, as in the previous construction one considers

[g_{x}\cdot x]\in\mathcal{N}

and then
x\in\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{c}}
is equivalent to

(\mathcal{H}^{c}\cdot[g_{x}\cdot x])\cap\mu_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset.

(2.7)

Condition (
2.7
) can be now addressed using standard machinery in Kähler geometry. For instance, consider the negatively curved symmetric space

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{H}^{c}/\mathcal{H}

(of all the cosets
g\mathcal{H}
where
g\in\mathcal{H}^{c}
) with metric given by a choice of bi-invariant metric on
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}
. Define a 1-form
\sigma_{x}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{R})
by

\sigma_{x}(v)=\langle\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(h^{-1}\cdot[g_{x}\cdot x]),\operatorname{Ad}(h^{-1})(iv)\rangle

for
[h]\in\mathcal{R}
and

v\in T_{[h]}\mathcal{R}\cong\operatorname{Ad}(h)i\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}.

By the defining properties of the moment map
\mu_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\sigma_{x}
is well-defined and closed and, since
\mathcal{R}
is contractible, it integrates to a functional (see e.g.
[
41
]
)

\mathcal{K}\colon\mathcal{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

normalized so that
\mathcal{K}([1])=0
. The functional
\mathcal{K}
is geodesically convex, where geodesics on

\mathcal{R}
are given by 1-parameter subgroups

t\longmapsto[he^{ity}],\qquad y\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}.

Hence, by geodesic completeness of
\mathcal{R}
, condition (
2.7
) is equivalent to the existence of a critical point of
\mathcal{K}
. The latter reduces to the
linear properness
of the
functional (see e.g.
[
41
]
), namely, the existence of a pair of positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

d([1],[h])\leq C_{1}\mathcal{K}([h])+C_{2}

for all
[h]\in\mathcal{R}
, where
d(,)
stands for the natural distance on the symmetric space. Note here that, by the special form of the geodesics and the polar decomposition
\mathcal{H}^{c}=\exp(i\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H})\cdot\mathcal{H}
,
the previous condition is equivalent to

\lvert y\rvert\leq C_{1}\mathcal{K}([e^{iy}])+C_{2}

for all
y\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}
(and possibly different constants). Finally, we note that the linear properness of
\mathcal{K}
can be recasted, similarly as in the Hilbert–Mumford criterion in algebraic geometry, in terms
of a
stability condition
for
[g_{x}\cdot x]\in\mathcal{N}
, given by numerical criterion

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sigma_{x}([e^{tiy}])>0,

for all
y\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}
.

As stated in the introduction and we will see throughout the whole paper, the application of the previous abstract picture to the situation of gravitating vortices is far from straighforward.

2.2.
Reduction by stages approach to gravitating vortices

In this section we explain how the existence and uniqueness problem for the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
) fits into the general abstract framework of Section
2.1
, and present
our overall strategy to the problem via reduction by stages. We follow closely
[
4
,
5
]
.

Fix a smooth compact oriented surface
S
of genus
g(S)
endowed with a smooth volume form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(S)
. We will regard
\omega
as a symplectic form on
S
. Let
(L,h)
be a smooth Hermitian line
bundle over
S
of degree

N=\int_{S}c_{1}(L)\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the space of almost complex structures on
S
compatible with
\omega
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the space of unitary connections on
(L,h)
and
\mathcal{S}=\Omega^{0}(L)
the space of smooth sections of
L
. Consider the
space of triples

\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S},

(2.8)

endowed with the symplectic structure

\omega_{\alpha}=\omega_{\mathcal{J}}+4\alpha\omega_{\mathcal{A}}+4\alpha\omega_{\mathcal{S}}

(2.9)

(for a choice of positive real coupling constant
\alpha>0
). Here, for
(J,A,\bm{\phi})\in\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
, we denote

\omega_{\mathcal{J}}(\dot{J}_{1},\dot{J}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\operatorname{tr}(J\dot{J}_{1}\dot{J}_{2})\omega,\;\,\omega_{\mathcal{A}}(a_{1},a_{2})=-\int_{S}a_{1}\wedge a_{2},\;\,\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{1},\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{2})=-\operatorname{Im}\int_{S}h(\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{1},\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{2})\omega

where
\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{1},\dot{\bm{\phi}}_{2}\in T_{\phi}\mathcal{S}=\Omega^{0}(L)
,
a_{1},a_{2}\in T_{A}\mathcal{A}=i\Omega^{1}(S)
, and
\dot{J}_{1},\dot{J}_{2}\in T_{J}\mathcal{J}
, identified with the space of
g_{J}
-symmetric tensors which anticommute
with
J
, where
g_{J}=\omega(,J)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the group of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of
(S,\omega)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(L,h)
the extended group of unitary automorphisms of the
(L,h)
. Any such automorphism
\psi
covers a unique
diffeomorphism
\check{\psi}\colon S\to S
and we set
p(\psi)=\check{\psi}
. We define the

extended gauge group

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(L,h),

as the group of automorphisms which cover elements of
\mathcal{H}
. Then the unitary gauge group of
(L,h)
is the
normal subgroup
\mathcal{G}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
of automorphisms covering the identity, and we have an exact sequence of Lie groups

1\to\mathcal{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H}\to 1,

(2.10)

where
\iota
is the inclusion map. The natural diagonal left action of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
on
\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
by push-forward is Hamiltonian (see
[
5
, Section 3.2]
), and there is an equivariant moment map

\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}\colon\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}\to(\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})^{*}
given
by

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(J,A,\bm{\phi}),\zeta\rangle=&-4\alpha\int_{S}A\zeta\!\cdot\!\left(F_{A}-\frac{i}{2}(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\tau)\omega\right)\\
&-\int_{S}f\left(S_{J}\omega+2i\alpha\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}_{A}\bm{\phi},\bm{\phi})_{h}-2i\alpha\tau F_{A}\right),\end{split}

(2.11)

where
S_{J}
denotes the scalar curvature of
g_{J}=\omega(,J)
and
F_{A}
is the curvature of the connection
A
. Here,
A\zeta\in iC^{\infty}(S,\mathbb{R})
is the vertical part of
\zeta\in\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
with respect to

A
, identified with a vector field on the total space of
L
, and
f
is the Hamiltonian function of the vector field
p(\zeta)\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H}
on
S
, normalized so that
\int_{S}f\omega=0
. The parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, often called
symmetry breaking parameter
, can be chosen at will in the previous formula.

Following the discussion in the previous section, one can formally consider the symplectic reduction of
\mathcal{T}
by
\mathcal{G}
, that is,
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}(0)/\mathcal{G}
, which gives a relative moduli space of solutions of the
vortex equation

iF_{A}+\frac{1}{2}(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\tau)\omega=0

(2.12)

over the space of complex structures
\mathcal{J}
, with possibly non-holomorphic section
\phi
. This space inherits then a Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
-action with moment map given by the second line in (
2.11
). Notice
that in order for the symplectic
\mathcal{G}
-reduction to be non-empty, one already needs to impose (provided that
\bm{\phi}\neq 0
)

V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau},

(2.13)

where
V=\int_{S}\omega
is the total volume of
S
with respect to
\omega
.

The moduli space of solutions of the gravitating vortex equations arises when we restrict to a natural symplectic submanifold of the space of triples
\mathcal{J}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
, dictated by its Kähler geometry.
Similarly as in
[
2
, Proposition 2.2]
, the space (
2.8
) has a formally integrable almost complex structure, given by

\mathbf{J}(\dot{J},a,\dot{\bm{\phi}})=(J\dot{J},-a(J\cdot),i\dot{\bm{\phi}})

where
(\dot{J},a,\dot{\bm{\phi}})
is a tangent vector at
(J,A,\bm{\phi})
, which is compatible with (
2.9
) when
\alpha>0
, thus inducing a Kähler structure in this case. Consider the subspace of

integrable triples

\mathcal{T}=\{(J,A,\bm{\phi})\mid\bar{\partial}_{J,A}\bm{\phi}=0\}

where
\bar{\partial}_{J,A}\bm{\phi}
denotes the
(0,1)
-part of
d_{A}\bm{\phi}
with respect to
J
. This space is in bijection with the space of Riemann surfaces
\Sigma_{J}=(S,J)
of genus
g(S)
equipped with a holomorphic
line bundle
(L,\bar{\partial}_{J,A})
with holomorphic section
\phi
. This implies, in particular, that the degree of
L
must be a positive integer

N>0,

provided that
\mathcal{T}
is non-empty, and we shall assume this in the sequel. By definition,
\mathcal{T}
is a complex subspace of (
2.8
) (away from its singularities) preserved by the
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-action, and hence
it inherits a Hamiltonian
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-action.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

The
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-action on
\mathcal{T}
is Hamiltonian with
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
-equivariant moment map
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}\colon\mathcal{T}\to(\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}})^{*}
given by

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}(J,A,\bm{\phi}),\zeta\rangle&=-4\alpha\int_{S}A\zeta\!\cdot\!\left(F_{A}-\frac{i}{2}(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\tau)\omega\right)-\int_{S}f\left(\omega\left(S_{J}+\alpha\Delta_{g}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert^{2}_{h}\right)-2\alpha\tau iF_{A}\right)\end{split}

(2.14)

for all
(J,A,\bm{\phi})\in\mathcal{T}
and
\zeta\in\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, where
\Delta_{g}=2i\Lambda_{\omega}\bar{\partial}_{J}\partial_{J}
is the Laplacian operator of the Riemannian metric
g_{J}=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
.

Formula (
2.14
) follows from (
2.11
) using the Kähler identities and the fact that
\bm{\phi}
is a holomorphic section of
(L,\bar{\partial}_{J,A})
, combined with the Bochner formula

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}=-i\Lambda\partial\bar{\partial}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}=i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{A}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h}^{2}-\lvert\partial_{A}\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h,\omega}^{2},

(2.15)

where
\lvert\partial_{A}\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h,\omega}^{2}=i\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial_{A}\bm{\phi}\wedge(\partial_{A}\bm{\phi})^{*})
. It is not difficult to see that the zeroes of the moment map
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
correspond precisely to
integrable triples
(J,A,\bm{\phi})\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying the gravitating vortex equations (
1.1
) with constant

c=\frac{2\pi(\chi(S)-2\alpha\tau N)}{V}.

(2.16)

The moduli space of solutions of the gravitating vortex equations, given by the symplectic quotient

\mathcal{M}=\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}(0)/\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}

inherits, by means of standard gauge-theoretical considerations
[
26
]
, a Kähler structure on its smooth locus.

\mathcal{T}
.
We should notice that the role played by the orbits of the complexification in the definition of the stable locus of
\mathcal{T}
is now played by the integral leaves of the distribution

D_{p}=\{Y_{\zeta_{1}}(p)+\mathbf{J}Y_{\zeta_{2}}(p)\mid\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\operatorname{Lie}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\}\subset T_{p}\mathcal{T},

for
p\in\mathcal{T}
, where
Y_{\zeta_{j}}(p)
is the infinitesimal action of
\zeta_{j}
at
p
.

In order to yield a deeper understanding of the global structure of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
, including its singularities, we adopt a
reduction by stages approach
to the gravitating vortex equations.
For this, we build on the following existence theorem for the vortex equation (

), established independently by Noguchi, Bradlow and García-Prada. For completeness, in Appendix

we provide a direct proof of this theorem with the methods of the present work.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
12
,
28
,
42
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact connected Riemann surface endowed with a holomorphic line bundle
L
with non-zero holomorphic section

Let
\omega
be a Kähler form on
\Sigma
and assume that the symmetry breaking parameter

satisfies the inequality (
2.13
). Then there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h_{\omega}
on

the vortex equation (
2.12
), where
A
is the Chern connection of
h_{\omega}
.

In the language of Section
2.1
, this result can be interpreted as follows. Assume that
\omega
and
\tau
are chosen,
a priori
, so that the inequality (
2.13
) is
satisfied. Then for any

\mu_{\mathcal{G}}(g_{p}\cdot p)=0,

where
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}=\iota^{*}\circ\mu_{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
for
\iota
the inclusion in (
2.10
). The metric

in Theorem

corresponds to the pull-back of the fixed

by

\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\mathcal{G}^{c}}=\mathcal{T}
and the symplectic

-reduction of

by

admits a more amenable description as the relative symmetric product

of the universal family of Riemann surfaces

over the space of complex structure
\mathcal{J}
on
S

[
28
]
. The
induced Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
-action on

\begin{split}\langle\mu_{\mathcal{H}}(J,[(A,\bm{\phi})]),y\rangle&=-\int_{S}f\left(\omega\left(S_{J}+\alpha\Delta_{g}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert^{2}_{h}\right)-2i\alpha\tau F_{A}\right),\end{split}

where

with normalized Hamiltonian

in the previous formula solves the vortex equation on the Riemann

by
\mathcal{H}

The main goal of the present paper is to give a characterization of the complexified

-orbits on
\mathcal{T}
which cut the zero locus of the moment map

, that is, which contain a solution of the gravitating

(with genus

) endowed with an effective divisor

corresponding to a holomorphic line bundle

with holomorphic section

. Moving along the complexified orbit is equivalent to, up the action of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, varying the Kähler metric
on
\Sigma
with fixed total volume
V
and the Hermitian metric
h
on
L
. This is the
complex viewpoint
on the gravitating vortex equations presented in Section
1
. Following the reduction by
stages approach, we now consider the following equation for a Kähler metric on
\omega

, which we shall call the

(2.18)

is given by (

is the unique solution of the vortex equation associated to

Similarly as in Section
2.1
, consider the infinite-dimensional space

\mathcal{P}=\left\{\omega\textrm{ K\"{a}hler }\Bigm|\;\int_{\Sigma}\omega=V\right\}

of Kähler metrics with total volume

. By contractibility of

, this 1-form integrates

(see
[

and

). Instead of working with

directly, it will be useful to fix a reference background Kähler metric

and consider the space of Kähler

\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}=\left\{\varphi\mid\omega_{\varphi}:=\omega_{0}+2i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0\right\}\subset C^{\infty}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{R}}).

(2.19)

Define a 1-form on
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}

\sigma_{\alpha}|_{\varphi}(\dot{\varphi})=-\int_{S}\dot{\varphi}\left(\omega_{\varphi}\left(S_{\omega_{\varphi}}+\alpha\Delta_{\omega_{\varphi}}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert^{2}_{h_{\varphi}}\right)-2\alpha\tau iF_{h_{\varphi}}-c\omega_{\varphi}\right)

. By a smooth path on

.

The reduced
\alpha
-K-energy

, given by smooth solutions to the partial differential equation

\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{t}-\lvert\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}_{t}\rvert^{2}_{\omega_{\varphi_{t}}}=0.

Furthermore, its critical points are given by solutions of the reduced gravitating vortex equation (

) on
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Consider
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
, regarded as the subspace of normalized Kähler potentials
\varphi

2.3.
Reduced
\alpha
-K-energy

The goal of this section is to provide a direct and self-contained proof of Proposition

\end{split}

&\qquad+2\alpha\int_{\Sigma}f_{\varphi}\left(iF_{h_{0}}+iF_{h_{\varphi}}\right)+\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h_{\varphi}}^{2}\omega_{\varphi}-\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h_{0}}^{2}\omega_{0}\right)-2\alpha\tau\int_{\Sigma}f_{\varphi}\omega_{\varphi}\\
&\qquad+\left(c-\langle S\rangle\right)\int_{\Sigma}\varphi i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi+\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\left(-\Ric\omega_{0}+2\alpha\tau iF_{h_{0}}+c\omega_{0}\right),\end{split}

where
h_{\varphi}=e^{2f_{\varphi}}h_{0}
is the unique solution of the vortex equation (
2.18
) associated to
\omega_{\varphi}
via Theorem
2.2
. By the explicit formula for
\sigma_{\alpha}
in
(
2.20
), critical points of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
are given by solutions of the reduced gravitating vortex equation (
2.18
) on
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
. Furthermore, by Proposition

2.8
, the second derivative of the reduced K-energy along a smooth path
\varphi_{t}
in
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
is given by

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(\varphi_{t})&=4\alpha\lVert\mathrm{d}f^{\prime}_{t}+\eta_{\varphi^{\prime}_{t}}\lrcorner iF_{h_{t}}\rVert^{2}+4\alpha\lVert J\eta_{\varphi^{\prime}_{t}}\lrcorner\mathrm{d}_{A_{t}}\bm{\phi}-f^{\prime}_{t}\bm{\phi}\rVert^{2}+2\lVert\bar{\partial}\nabla^{1,0}\varphi^{\prime}_{t}\rVert^{2}\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{t}-\lvert\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}_{t}\rvert_{\omega_{t}}^{2}\Big)\wp_{2}(f_{t},\varphi_{t})\omega_{t},\end{split}

for
\omega_{t}=\omega_{0}+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi_{t}
and
h_{t}=e^{2f_{t}}h_{0}=h_{\varphi_{t}}
, which proves the statement.
∎

To finish this section, we review in our set-up the well-known construction of smooth geodesics in the space of Kähler potentials from 1-parameter subgroups of holomorphic automorphisms. This example will be
applied in Section
5.3
.

Example 2.10
.

Given a Kähler metric
\omega
on the complex projective line
\Sigma={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, a holomorphic vector field on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
whose imaginary part is
Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega
determines a (smooth) geodesic ray emanating from
\omega
in the space of Kähler potentials.
More precisely, let
\bm{v}
be a holomorphic
(1,0)
-vector field on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that
\operatorname{Im}\bm{v}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega=\mathrm{d}\Phi
, for a real-valued smooth function
\Phi
.
Let
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the family of holomorphic automorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
generated by the real vector field
\operatorname{Re}\bm{v}=J\operatorname{Im}\bm{v}
, where
J
is the almost complex structure of

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
Then the Kähler form

\omega_{t}=\omega+2i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}:=\sigma_{t}^{*}\omega

satisfies
2i\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}_{t}=2i\partial\bar{\partial}\sigma_{t}^{*}\Phi
.
Normalizing
\varphi_{t}
and
\Phi
by the condition

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\omega_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\Phi\omega=0,

we have
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=\Phi_{t}:=\sigma_{t}^{*}\Phi
.
Using now the equation

\iota_{\text{Re}\bm{v}}\sigma_{t}^{*}\omega=\sigma_{t}^{*}\left(\iota_{\text{Re}\bm{v}}\omega\right)=\sigma_{t}^{*}\left(-J\mathrm{d}\Phi\right)=-J\mathrm{d}\Phi_{t}

and
\iota_{\text{Im}\bm{v}}\sigma_{t}^{*}\omega=\mathrm{d}\Phi_{t}
, we get that
\iota_{\bm{v}}\omega_{t}=2i\bar{\partial}\Phi_{t}
. This implies
\bm{v}=2\uparrow_{\omega_{t}}\bar{\partial}\Phi_{t}
, i.e.,
\bm{v}^{i}=2g_{t}^{i\bar{j}}\frac{\partial\Phi_{t}}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}=2\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{i}
. As a consequence,

\ddot{\varphi}_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{Re}\bm{v}}\Phi_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{v}+\overline{\bm{v}}\right)\Phi_{t}=\bm{v}\left(\Phi_{t}\right)

and, therefore,

\ddot{\varphi}_{t}-2\left|\nabla^{1,0}_{\omega_{t}}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\right|_{\omega_{t}}^{2}=0,

(2.32)

i.e.,
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth geodesic line in
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
.

The above construction of smooth geodesics will be applied in Theorem
5.7
.

Remark 2.11
.

If instead of making the normalization
\int_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\omega_{t}^{n}=0
in Example
2.10
we take
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=\Phi_{t}+b
for a fixed constant
b\in\mathbb{R}
, then the identity
\ddot{\varphi}_{t}=\bm{v}(\Phi_{t})
still holds. Therefore we also get a smooth geodesic line in
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
. This gives a family of geodesic lines
\{\varphi_{t}+bt\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, labelled by a real
number
b
, all lifting the same geodesic line in the space of Kähler metrics to the space of Kähler potentials (see Remark
2.4
).

3.1.
The metric completion of the space of Kähler potentials

Over the next three sections, we will construct an extension of the reduced

We start by reviewing briefly the Finsler-metric completion
(\mathcal{E}_{1},d_{1})
of the space of smooth Kähler potentials
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
considered in
[
18
]
, in the case of our interest. For
this, we fix a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
and a smooth background Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
with total volume

\int_{\Sigma}\omega_{0}=V.

We consider the functional
defined as (see e.g.
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
)

I(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{2V}\int_{\Sigma}u(\omega_{0}+\omega_{u}).

(3.1)

Following
[
18
]
(see also
[
21
, Definition 4.2]
), we define the
path-length distance

d_{1}
on
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
by

d_{1}(\psi_{0},\psi_{1})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{\psi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\Sigma}\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{t}\rvert\omega_{\psi_{t}},

for all
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
, where
\psi
ranges over all smooth paths
\psi\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}},\,t\mapsto\psi_{t}
, from
\psi_{0}
to
\psi_{1}
, that is, smooth maps

\psi=\psi(t,z)\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times\Sigma)
such that
\psi(0,\cdot)=\psi_{0}
and
\psi(1,\cdot)=\psi_{1}
. We will also consider the subspace
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
given by the smooth Kähler
potentials
\psi
that are normalized so that

I(\psi)=0.

By application of
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
,
d_{1}
is a metric on
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
and we also consider the induced metric in
\mathcal{R}
. In the next result, which follows by direct application of the
results in
[
18
,
30
]
, we describe the metric completion of
(\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}},d_{1})
and
(\mathcal{R},d_{1})
in the case of our interest. Recall that the space of
\omega_{0}
-plurisubharmonic functions
on
\Sigma
is defined by

\textnormal{PSH}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})=\{\psi\equiv-\infty\textrm{ or }\psi\in C^{\uparrow}\textrm{ and }\omega_{\psi}=\omega_{0}+2i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi\geq 0\textrm{ in the sense of currents}\},

where

is the set of upper semi-continuous integrable functions on
(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
. Note the standard fact that if
\psi\in\textnormal{PSH}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
, then
\omega_{\psi}
is a measure.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

The metric completion of
(\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}},d_{1})
is given by the set

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\Bigg\{\psi\in\textnormal{PSH}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})\;\Biggm|\;\int_{\Sigma}\lvert\psi\rvert\omega_{\psi}<\infty\Bigg\}.

(3.2)

The metric on
\mathcal{E}_{1}
is defined by

d_{1}(\psi_{0},\psi_{1})=\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}(\psi_{0}^{k},\psi_{1}^{k}),

for any
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, where
\{\psi_{j}^{k}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
is any sequence decreasing pointwise to
\psi_{j}
. Moreover, the completion
\overline{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}

of
\mathcal{R}
is

\overline{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap I^{-1}(0),

(3.3)

where
I
is given by the same formula as in (
3.1
).

Proof.

By
[
18
, Theorem 4.17]
, the metric completion of
(\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}},d_{1})
is the set of
\omega_{0}
-plurisubharmonic functions on
\Sigma
that satisfy (see
[
30
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\omega_{\psi}=V,

(3.4)

jointly with the inequality in (
3.2
). Using the fact that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Sigma=1
, by
[
30
, Lemma 1.2]
we have that (

) is superfluous, and hence the first part of the statement
follows. The
well-definition of the distance in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
follows from
[
18
, Section 4]
. The last part of the statement, about the completion
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
, is a consequence of
[
21
, Lemma 5.2]
.
∎

In our next result we observe that in complex dimension
1
,
d_{1}
is closely related to the familiar convergence in
W^{1,2}
for PSH functions, where
W^{1,2}=W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
is the Sobolev space of

L^{2}
functions on
(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
whose first distributional derivatives are in
L^{2}
. The following lemma is probably well known to experts, but we have not been able to find a precise reference in the
literature and so we include a proof for completeness. We will use the following notation for the average with respect to
\omega_{0}
of an integrable function
u
on
\Sigma
:

\fint u=\frac{1}{V}\int_{\Sigma}u\omega_{0}.

Lemma 3.2
.

On a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
, we have:

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{1}=W^{1,2}\cap\textnormal{PSH}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
as sets.

(2)

For any sequence
\{u_{k}\}\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}}
and any
u\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}
, we have
\displaystyle{\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}(u_{k},u)=0}
if and only if

\displaystyle{\lim_{k\to\infty}\lVert u_{k}-u\rVert_{W^{1,2}}}=0
.

Proof.

To prove (1), let
u\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
. By the Poincaré inequality, it suffices to show that
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}u<\infty
for concluding that
u\in W^{1,2}
. To
this end we note that

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{\Sigma}u\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u\Big\rvert<\infty.

Now there exists a sequence of smooth
\omega_{0}
-PSH functions
u_{i}
decreasing to
u
(see
[
11
, Theorem 1]
). From
[
20
, Lemma 3.47]
, we see that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-1}\partial u_{i}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{i}=\int_{\Sigma}u_{i}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{i}\rightarrow\int_{\Sigma}u\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u.

Thus a subsequence of
u_{i}-\fint u_{i}
converges weakly in
W^{1,2}
, strongly in
L^{2}
, and almost everywhere to a
W^{1,2}
function
v
. Thus
u
is in
W^{1,2}
.

Now, let
u\in W^{1,2}\cap\textnormal{PSH}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
. Without loss of generality (by subtracting the supremum), assume that
u\leq 0
. Let
\{\rho_{j}\}
be a partition of unity subordinate to coordinate
balls. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}-u\omega_{u}=-\sum_{j}\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{j}u\omega_{u}.

Let
v_{j,i}
be a sequence obtained by convolving
u_{0,j}+u
where
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{0,j}=\omega_{0}
. Note that
v_{j,i}
decreases to
u_{0,j}+u
and converges to it in
W_{loc}^{1,2}
, and so
does
\rho_{j}v_{j,i}
. Now

\begin{split}\displaystyle\sum_{j}\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j})\omega_{u}&=\sum_{j}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j})\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j}))u\right)\\
&=\sum_{j}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j})\omega_{0}-\sqrt{-1}\partial(\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j}))\bar{\partial}u\right),\end{split}

(3.5)

which converges to
\int_{\Sigma}\left(u\omega_{0}-\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}u\right)>-\infty
. Hence using the monotone convergence theorem, we see that
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|u|\omega_{u}<\infty
and
u\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

Finally, to prove (2), by
[
21
, Lemma 5.1]
we see that
d_{1}(u_{k},u)\rightarrow 0
iff
I(u_{k})=0\rightarrow I(u)=0
and
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}
. Thus if
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,2}
,

d(u_{k},u)\rightarrow 0
. If
d_{1}(u_{k},u)\rightarrow 0
, then
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}
. By the Poincaré inequality, the proof will follow if we prove that
\|\partial u_{k}-\partial u\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0
. To this end, we claim that the following integration-by-parts formula holds for all
u,v\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}u\partial\bar{\partial}v=-\int_{\Sigma}\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}v.

Indeed, proceeding as in the proof of
3.5
, we see (using the Monotone Convergence Theorem) that

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}u\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}v&=-\sum_{j}\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-1}\partial(\rho_{j}(v_{j,i}-u_{0,j}))\bar{\partial}v\\
&=-\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}v.\end{split}

Since
I(u_{k})=I(u)=0
, we see that
\fint u_{k}=\frac{1}{2V}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-1}\partial u_{k}\wedge\bar{\partial}u_{k}
and likewise for
u
, and hence

\|\partial u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\|\partial u\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, every
L^{2}
convergent subsequence of
u_{k}
converges to
u
, because
u_{k}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}
. Lastly, since
\|\partial u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}<C
, every subsequence has a further subsequence that
weakly converges in
W^{1,2}
and strongly converges in
L^{2}
(and hence) to
u
. Thus
\partial u_{k}
weakly converges to
\partial u
in
L^{2}
. Thus
\|\partial u_{k}-\partial u\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0
.
∎

3.2.
Weak solutions to the vortex equation

The next step to extend the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy (see Proposition
2.3
) to the completion
\mathcal{E}_{1}
of the space of smooth Kähler potentials
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
(see Lemma

3.2
), is to study the vortex equation for background metrics that satisfy low regularity conditions. More precisely, we are interested in (weak) Kähler metrics of the
form
\omega_{\psi}
for
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
.

We start this section by giving a precise definition of the weak solutions of the vortex equation that we will use. As in the previous section, we fix a smooth Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
on
\Sigma
with total
volume
V
. Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over
\Sigma
with degree
N=\int_{\Sigma}c_{1}(L)>0
and non-vanishing holomorphic section

0\neq\bm{\phi}\in H^{0}(\Sigma,L).

We also fix a symmetry breaking parameter
\tau>0
, satisfying (see (
2.13
))

V>\frac{4\pi N}{\tau}.

Furthermore, we fix a background smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
.

Definition 3.3
.

Consider the semi-positive current
\omega_{\psi}=\omega_{0}+2i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi
, where
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
.
A
weak solution
to the vortex equation with background current
\omega_{\psi}
is a function
f\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\omega_{0})
such that

\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f}\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\omega_{0}),\qquad\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f}\geq 0,

(3.6)

where the inequality is satisfied almost everywhere in
\Sigma
, and the following identity is satisfied for all
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
:

\int_{\Sigma}v\cdot iF_{h_{0}}+2i\partial v\wedge\bar{\partial}f=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}v\left(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f}\right)\omega_{0}-i\partial\left[v\left(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f}\right)\right]\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi.

(3.7)

Remark 3.4
.

By
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
, the inequality (
3.6
) follows for any smooth
f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
such that
h_{0}e^{2f}
is a solution of the vortex equation with
background metric
\omega_{\psi}
, for
\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
. Nonetheless, this inequality may fail if
\psi
or
f
have low regularity properties, and hence it is necessary to include it in
Definition
3.3
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the condition that (
3.7
) is satisfied for all test functions
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
is not
a priori
equivalent to the interpretation of the vortex equation

iF_{h_{0}}-2i\partial\bar{\partial}f=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f}\right)\omega_{\psi}

(3.8)

as an equation for currents. This is due to the fact that (
3.7
) is obtained multiplying (
3.8
) by
v
and formally integrating by parts
once
,
-2i\int_{\Sigma}v\partial\bar{\partial}f
to
2i\int_{\Sigma}\partial v\wedge\bar{\partial}f
,
while an interpretation of (
3.8
) as an equation for currents would lead to formally integrating by parts twice.
-2i\int_{\Sigma}(\bar{\partial}\partial v)f
(something similar happens with the last term of this equation).

In the next result we establish the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of the vortex equation, in the sense of Definition
3.3
. For the proof, we recall that the the Green function

G_{\omega_{0}}(P,Q)
on
\Sigma
with respect to the metric
\omega_{0}
satisfies, by definition,

2i\partial\bar{\partial}G_{\omega_{0}}(P,Q)=\frac{1}{V}\omega_{0}(Q)-[P]

(3.9)

in the sense of currents (our notation for
G_{\omega_{0}}
agrees with
[
7
, Page 108]
and
[
5
]
, but it differs by a negative sign from, e.g.,
[
25
]
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Given
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, there exists a unique
f\in W^{1,2}
satisfying the following property: for any sequence
\left\{\psi_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
, with
\psi_{n}\to\psi
in
W^{1,2}
as

n\to\infty
, consider the sequence of Kähler metrics
\omega_{\psi_{n}}
on
\Sigma
. Then the sequence of solutions
h_{n}=h_{0}e^{2f_{n}}
of the vortex equation (
2.12
) in Theorem
2.2
,
corresponding to the background metrics
\omega_{\psi_{n}}
, satisfy
f_{n}\to f
in
W^{1,2}
as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, the unique
f\in W^{1,2}
satisfying this condition is a weak solution to the vortex equation
with background metric
\omega_{\psi}
, in the sense of Definition
3.3
.

Proof.

We start obtaining uniform
W^{1,2}
estimates for the sequences
f_{n}
and
\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f_{n}}
.
By definition,
f_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
satisfies the vortex equation with
C^{\infty}
background metric
\omega_{\psi_{n}}
, that is,

iF_{h_{0}}-2i\partial\bar{\partial}f_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f_{n}}\right)\omega_{\psi_{n}},

(3.10)

so
[
12
, Proposition 5.2]
gives the pointwise inequality

\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f_{n}}\leq\tau.

(3.11)

Green’s formula (
3.9
), together with (
3.10
), (
3.11
)
implies

\widetilde{f}_{n}(P)=\int_{\Sigma}G_{\omega_{0}}(P,Q)\Delta_{\omega_{0}}f_{n}(Q)\omega_{0}(Q)\geq-\int_{\Sigma}G_{\omega_{0}}(P,Q)iF_{h_{0}}(Q)\geq-C,

(3.12)

for all
P\in\Sigma
, where
\widetilde{f}_{n}=f_{n}-\fint_{\Sigma}f_{n}\omega_{0}
and
C
does not depend on
n
. Multiplying (
3.10
) by
\widetilde{f}_{n}+C\geq 0
and integrating yields (using the fact that

iF_{h_{0}}\geq-C\omega_{0}
)

-CV+2\int_{\Sigma}i\partial\widetilde{f}_{n}\wedge\bar{\partial}\widetilde{f}_{n}\leq\frac{\tau C}{2}V-\tau\int_{\Sigma}i\partial\widetilde{f}_{n}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi_{n}.

It follows that

\int_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla\widetilde{f}_{n}\right|_{\omega_{0}}^{2}\omega_{0}\leq C+C\int_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla\psi_{n}\right|_{\omega_{0}}^{2}\omega_{0},

where
C
only depends on
N,V,\tau
. Together with the Poincaré inequality for
\widetilde{f}_{n}
, this gives a uniform
W^{1,2}
bound on the sequence
\widetilde{f}_{n}
.
We will obtain now a uniform bound for
\fint_{\Sigma}f_{n}\omega_{0}
.
Firstly, (
3.11
) gives the
a priori
estimate

2f_{n}\leq-\log\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}+\log\tau,

and therefore provides a uniform upper bound for
\fint_{\Sigma}f_{n}\omega_{0}
(recall that
\log\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}
is integrable, as shown by integration in a local polar coordinates, since
\bm{\phi}
is
holomorphic).
Suppose now that there exists a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
such that

\fint_{\Sigma}f_{n_{k}}\omega_{0}\to-\infty.

Since
\widetilde{f}_{n_{k}}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,2}
, there exists a further subsequence, still denoted
\{\widetilde{f}_{n_{k}}\}
, such that
\widetilde{f}_{n_{k}}\to\widetilde{f}
in
L^{2}
and
\widetilde{f}_{n_{k}}\to\widetilde{f}
almost everywhere, for some
\widetilde{f}\in W^{1,2}
. However,

f_{n_{k}}=\widetilde{f}_{n_{k}}+\fint_{\Sigma}f_{n_{k}}\omega_{0}\rightarrow-\infty

almost everywhere. It immediately gives that
\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f_{n_{k}}}\to 0
almost everywhere. But the Bochner formula (

\displaystyle\int\left|\nabla_{0}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle\omega_{0}+\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\int f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\Delta_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int\nabla_{0}\Big(f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\Big)\cdot(\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon})\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int|\nabla_{0}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\omega_{0}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\int\left|\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\int\left|f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|\cdot\left|\nabla_{0}|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}\right|_{0}\cdot|\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|_{0}\omega_{0}.

(4.8)

In particular, for the last term we can use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\begin{split}\int\left|f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|\cdot\left|\nabla_{0}|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}\right|_{0}\cdot|\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|_{0}\omega_{0}\leq\sqrt{\int\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\cdot\left|\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}}\sqrt{\int\frac{\left|\nabla_{0}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\right|_{0}^{2}}{\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}}\omega_{0}.}\end{split}

(4.9)

At this point, the
\epsilon
-geodesic equation
(
4.2
)
and the uniform
C^{1,\bar{1}}
estimates for
\varphi_{\epsilon}
imply

|\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}=|\nabla_{\omega_{\epsilon}}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|_{\omega_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}=\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}-\epsilon\Lambda_{\omega_{\epsilon}}\omega_{0}\right)\omega_{\epsilon}\leq C\omega_{\epsilon}.

Using this inequality, it follows that the right-hand side of (
4.9
) is controlled by

\frac{1}{2}\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}+C\int\frac{\left|\nabla_{0}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\right|_{0}^{2}}{\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}}\omega_{0},

for some
C>0
. We thus arrive at the inequalities

\begin{split}\frac{3}{4}\int|\nabla_{0}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\omega_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}&\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{4}\int\left|\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}+C\int\frac{\left|\nabla_{0}|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\right|_{0}^{2}}{|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}}\omega_{0}\leq C,\end{split}

(4.10)

by the
W^{2,p}
estimate of
f_{\epsilon}
and the
W^{1,2}
estimate of
\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
. Now, the Poincaré inequality implies

\left\lVert f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C.

(4.11)

Using (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we see that

\begin{split}\displaystyle C&\geq\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}=\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}+\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\\
&\geq\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\left[\left(\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}+2\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right]\omega_{\epsilon}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}-2\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}-2\tau C\int\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\omega_{0}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right)^{2}\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}-C,\end{split}

(4.12)

where for the inequality in the second line we simply use
2ab\geq-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}-2b^{2}
. This, together with the fact that
\displaystyle\int|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}=\tau V-4\pi N>0
, gives

\begin{split}\left\lvert\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\right\rvert\leq C.\end{split}

(4.13)

Going back to (
4.7
), using Green’s representation formula we arrive at the following representation of
f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
:

\begin{split}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(P)&=\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}+\int G_{0}(P,Q)\Delta_{0}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(Q)\omega_{0}(Q)\\
&=\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}-\int G_{0}(P,Q)|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int G_{0}(P,Q)(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})(Q)\Delta_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(Q)\omega_{0}(Q).\end{split}

(4.14)

This further transforms to

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(P)

\displaystyle-\fint f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int G_{0}(P,Q)|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)-\frac{1}{2}\int G_{0}(P,Q)\Bigg(\Delta_{0}\Big((\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})+2\nabla_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\cdot\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Bigg)(Q)\omega_{0}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int G_{0}(P,Q)|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\fint(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}-(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})(P)\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(P)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int G_{0}(P,Q)\Bigg(\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})-2\nabla_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\cdot\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Bigg)(Q)\omega_{0}(Q).

(4.15)

Write
\Phi_{0}=\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}
and
\Phi_{\epsilon}=\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}=\Phi_{0}e^{2f_{\epsilon}}
. For each term we can estimate as

\begin{split}&\left|\int G_{0}(P,Q)\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)\right|^{2}\leq\int G_{0}(P,Q)^{2}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)\cdot\!\int\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\!\\
&\qquad\leq\tau C\int G_{0}(P,Q)^{2}\omega_{0}(Q)\cdot C\\
&\qquad\leq C,\\
&\left|\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right|\leq C,\;\;\left|\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right|_{0}\leq C,\\
&\left|\nabla_{0}\Phi_{\epsilon}\right|_{0}=\left|\nabla_{0}\Phi_{0}e^{2f_{\epsilon}}+2\Phi_{\epsilon}\nabla_{0}f_{\epsilon}\right|\leq C,\\
&\left|\Delta_{0}\Phi_{\epsilon}\right|=\left|\Delta_{0}\Phi_{0}e^{2f_{\epsilon}}-4e^{2f_{\epsilon}}\nabla_{0}\Phi_{0}\cdot\nabla_{0}f_{\epsilon}+2\Phi_{\epsilon}\Delta_{0}f_{\epsilon}-4\Phi_{\epsilon}\left|\nabla_{0}f_{\epsilon}\right|_{0}^{2}\right|\leq C.\end{split}

(4.16)

Using (
4.15
) and (
4.13
), we see that

\left|f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|\leq C.

(4.17)

Differentiating (
4.15
) with respect to the
P

\begin{split}\nabla_{0}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(P)&=-\int\nabla_{0}G_{0}(P,Q)|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(Q)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(Q)\omega_{\epsilon}(Q)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{0}\Big((\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})(P)\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(P)\Big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int\nabla_{0}G_{0}(P,Q)\Big(\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})-2\nabla_{0}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\cdot\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Big)(Q)\omega_{0}(Q).\end{split}

(4.18)

and using the fact that

\left|\nabla_{0}G_{0}(P,Q)\right|_{0}\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{d_{0}(P,Q)}\right)

(in particular this implies
\nabla_{0}G_{0}\in L^{q}
for any
q\in[1,2)
), we obtain

\left|\nabla_{0}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right|_{0}\leq C.

(4.19)

4.4.
L^{\infty}
estimate of
f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}

As in the case of the
C^{1}
-estimate, differentiating (

) with respect to the real time
t
, we get the following equation for
f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}
:

\begin{split}\Delta_{0}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}=-2|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}-|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}+2|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}.\end{split}

(4.20)

It is convenient to rewrite the equation as

\left(\Delta_{0}+\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}\right)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}=-2|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}+2|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}.

(4.21)

Next, we prove a lemma about the
Green function
for the second-order linear elliptic differential operator

L_{\epsilon}=\Delta_{0}+\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\{v_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}
be a set of smooth functions on the compact Riemann surface
\left(\Sigma,\omega_{0}\right)
such that
0\leq v_{\epsilon}\leq C
and
\int_{\Sigma}v_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}\geq\frac{1}{C}
, where
C
is a constant independent of
\epsilon
(varying on a compact set). Then there exists a family of functions
\{G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)\}_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}
, each of which
is smooth everywhere except on the diagonal, such that for any
1<s<2
one has

(1)

0\leq G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)\leq C_{s}(1+\log|d_{\omega_{0}}(P,Q)|)
.

(2)

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{0}G_{\epsilon}|_{0}^{s}\omega_{0}\leq C_{s}
.

(3)

(\Delta_{0})_{Q}G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)+v_{\epsilon}(Q)G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)=\delta_{P}(Q)
in the sense of distributions (where the Dirac delta function is defined with respect to
\omega_{0}
).

Proof.

Fix an
1<s<2
. We shall drop the subscript
s
on constants
C_{s}
that appear in this proof. Recall that the standard Green’s function
G_{0}=G_{\omega_{0}}
satisfies (see (
3.9
))

(\Delta_{0})_{Q}G_{0}(P,Q)=\delta_{P}(Q)-\frac{1}{V}.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1],P\in\Sigma
, let
u_{\epsilon,P}
be the unique distributional solution of the PDE

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}u_{\epsilon,P}+v_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon,P})(Q)=\frac{1}{V}-v_{\epsilon}(Q)G_{0}(P,Q).

(4.22)

This equation can be solved uniquely for
u_{\epsilon,P}\in W^{2,2}
, because the right-hand side is in
L^{2}
, and the kernel of
\Delta_{0}+v_{\epsilon}
is trivial because of the assumptions on
v_{\epsilon}
. We
claim that

\|u_{\epsilon,P}\|_{W^{2,2}}\leq C

(where
C
is independent of
\epsilon
and
P
). To see this, we study the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{\epsilon}\geq 0
of the operator
\Delta_{0}+v_{\epsilon}
. Suppose there exists a sequence of values of

\epsilon
such that
\lambda_{\epsilon}\rightarrow 0
. Then normalising an eigenvector
e_{\epsilon}
to have
L^{2}
norm
1
, we see that

\|\nabla_{0}e_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int v_{\epsilon}e_{\epsilon}^{2}\omega_{0}\rightarrow 0.

Therefore (up to a subsequence)
e_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\tilde{e}
strongly in
L^{2}
and weakly in
W^{1,2}
. Moreover,
\tilde{e}
must be a constant (that is non-zero since it has
L^{2}
norm
1
). However,

\frac{\tilde{e}^{2}}{C}\leq\int v_{\epsilon}\tilde{e}^{2}\omega_{0}\leq 2\int v_{\epsilon}\left((\tilde{e}-e_{\epsilon})^{2}+e_{\epsilon}^{2}\right)\omega_{0}\rightarrow 0,

which is a contradiction. Thus
\lambda_{\epsilon}\geq\frac{1}{C}>0
. This implies

\begin{split}\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon}}\int u_{\epsilon,P}\left(\Delta_{0}+v_{\epsilon}\right)u_{\epsilon,P}\omega_{0}\\
&\leq C\int h_{\epsilon,P}u_{\epsilon,P}\omega_{0}\\
&\leq C\lVert h_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}\end{split}

(4.23)

where
h_{\epsilon,P}(\cdot)=1-v_{\epsilon}(\cdot)G_{0}(P,\cdot)
.
This immediately implies the desired uniform
W^{2,2}
bound:

\begin{split}\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{W^{2,2}}&\leq C\left(\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\Delta_{0}u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert h_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert v_{\epsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C\lVert h_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C.\end{split}

(4.24)

As a consequence (using the Sobolev embedding theorem), there is a uniform
L^{\infty}
bound on
u_{\epsilon,P}
(which is independent of
\epsilon,P
). Since
G_{0}(P,\cdot)
is smooth on
\Sigma\backslash\{P\}
, we know by elliptic regularity theory that
u_{\epsilon,P}
is smooth on
\Sigma\backslash\{P\}
for each fixed
\epsilon
. Let
d_{P}u_{\epsilon,P}
be the exterior differential of
u_{\epsilon,P}
with
respect to
P
. This 1-form satisfies an equation of the form

\begin{split}\left(\Delta_{0}+v_{\epsilon}\right)d_{P}u_{\epsilon,P}&=-v_{\epsilon}d_{P}G_{0}.\end{split}

(4.25)

Since
d_{P}G_{0}\in L^{p}
for any
p<2
, the 1-form
d_{P}G_{0}
can be approximated by smooth 1-forms in the sense of
L^{s}
, for any
1<s<2
, and the approximating sequence has a uniform
L^{s}
bound. For this
approximating family the above equation can be solved uniquely and
d_{P}u_{\epsilon,P}
can be understood as the limit of such solutions.

Regarding the equation

\left(\Delta_{0}+v_{\epsilon}\right)w=v_{\epsilon}h,

with
h
smooth, we claim that the following inequality holds for a uniform constant
C
:

\lVert w\rVert_{L^{s}}\leq C\lVert h\rVert_{L^{s}}.

(4.26)

Suppose this is not true. Then there exists a sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and
\left(w_{n},h_{n},v_{\epsilon_{n}}\right)
such that
\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}=1
for all
n
but
\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
, while
satisfying

\Delta_{0}w_{n}=v_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(h_{n}-w_{n}\right).

The standard
W^{2,s}
-estimate shows that

\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{W^{2,s}}\leq C\left(\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}+\lVert v_{\epsilon_{n}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left(\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}+\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}\right)\right)\leq C.

We now have

\int\left|\nabla_{0}w_{n}\right|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}=\int v_{\epsilon_{n}}\left(h_{n}w_{n}-w_{n}^{2}\right)\omega_{0}\leq C\lVert h_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s^{*}}},

where we use the Hölder inequality and
s^{*}
represents the conjugate index of
s
. The Sobolev inequality implies
\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s^{*}}}\leq\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{W^{2,s}}\leq C
, and
\lVert w_{n}-\fint w_{n}\omega_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\int\left|\nabla_{0}w_{n}\right|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}\to 0
by the Poincaré inequality. This in particular implies that (up to a subsequence)
w_{n}\to\tilde{w}
in
L^{2}
as
n\to\infty
for some nonzero constant
\tilde{w}
(this
is because
\lVert\tilde{w}\rVert_{L^{s}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert w_{n}\rVert_{L^{s}}=1
). Then we obtain a contradiction, since

\int v_{\epsilon_{n}}w_{n}^{2}\omega_{0}=\int v_{\epsilon_{n}}h_{n}w_{n}\omega_{0}-\int|\nabla_{0}w_{n}|_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}\to 0,

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int\!v_{\epsilon_{n}}w_{n}^{2}\omega_{0}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\{\int\!v_{\epsilon_{n}}\tilde{w}^{2}\omega_{0}+2\tilde{w}\!\int\!v_{\epsilon_{n}}(w_{n}-\tilde{w})\omega_{0}+\!\int\!v_{\epsilon_{n}}(w_{n}-\tilde{w})^{2}\omega_{0}\right\}\geq\frac{\tilde{w}^{2}}{C}.

This contradiction establishes the estimate (
4.26
) and thus further implies that
\lVert d_{P}u_{\epsilon,P}\rVert_{L^{s}}
is uniformly bounded, and hence so is
u_{\epsilon,P}
in
W^{1,s}
.

Define next

G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)=G_{0}(P,Q)+u_{\epsilon}(P,Q).

Then one can easily verify that the statement holds. In particular, the non-negativity of
G_{\epsilon}
in item (1) could be obtained by approximating
v_{\epsilon}
using
v_{\epsilon}+\delta_{j}
with a sequence

\delta_{j}
decreasing to
0
, and using the maximum principle for the equation

\left(\Delta_{0}\right)_{Q}G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)+\left(v_{\epsilon}+\delta_{j}\right)G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)=\delta_{P}(Q).\qed

Applying Lemma (
4.3
) to Equation (
4.20
), we see that

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(P)=\int G_{\epsilon}(P,Q)\left(-2|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}})\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}+2|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right)(Q)\omega_{0}(Q)

\displaystyle=-2\int G_{\epsilon}|\bm{\phi}|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2}(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\int\Delta_{0}\left(G_{\epsilon}(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}}^{2})\right)\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}+2\int\nabla_{0}\left(G_{\epsilon}|\bm{\phi}|^{2}_{h_{\epsilon}}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right)\cdot\nabla_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=-2\int G_{\epsilon}\Phi_{\epsilon}\left(f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\omega_{\epsilon}\\
&\;\,-\frac{1}{2}\int\left[\left(\delta_{P}-\Phi_{\epsilon}\frac{\omega_{\epsilon}}{\omega_{0}}G_{\epsilon}\right)\left(\tau-\Phi_{\epsilon}\right)+G_{\epsilon}\Delta_{0}\left(\tau-\Phi_{\epsilon}\right)-2\nabla_{0}G_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{0}\left(\tau-\Phi_{\epsilon}\right)\right]\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\omega_{0}\\
&\;\,+2\int\left(\nabla_{0}G_{\epsilon}\Phi_{\epsilon}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}+G_{\epsilon}\nabla_{0}\Phi_{\epsilon}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}+G_{\epsilon}\Phi_{\epsilon}\nabla_{0}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\right)\cdot\nabla_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\omega_{0}.\end{split}

(4.27)

Finally, by the a priori estimates for

Proposition 4.4
.

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)
is uniformly bounded on
[0,1]
for
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

According to the definition (
2.25
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\left(\left(\langle S\rangle-c\right)\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}-2\alpha i\partial\bar{\partial}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}+\alpha\tau\left(\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}-\tau\right)\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(c-\langle S\rangle\right)\int_{\Sigma}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}-2\alpha\int_{\Sigma}i\partial\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\wedge\bar{\partial}\lvert\bm{\phi}\rvert_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}-\alpha\tau\int_{\Sigma}\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)\left(\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}-\tau\right)\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}.

By the
C^{1,\bar{1}}

\varphi_{\epsilon}
and the uniform
C^{1}
estimate of
f_{\epsilon}
(see Section

), we conclude that

\left|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq C.\qed

We obtain next a uniform estimate for the second time-derivative along the curve.

Proposition 4.5
.

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)
is uniformly bounded on
[0,1]
for
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. Moreover, it is almost nonnegative at the endpoints
t=0,1
.

Proof.

According to the formula for second-order derivatives in Proposition
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=4\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\left|\mathrm{d}f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau-\Phi_{\epsilon}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\right|_{\omega_{0}}^{2}\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad+4\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\left|J\eta_{\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}\lrcorner\mathrm{d}_{A_{\epsilon}(t)}\bm{\phi}-f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{0}}^{2}e^{2f_{\epsilon}(t)}\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)-\left|\mathrm{d}\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)\right|_{\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}}^{2}\right)\left(\alpha\Delta_{\omega_{0}}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}\right)\omega_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\alpha\tau\left(\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}-\tau\right)+(\langle S\rangle-c)\right)\cdot\epsilon\omega_{0},

where we use the fact that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Sigma=1
for the integrands in the first and the third terms, and (
4.2
) for the last one. The integrand in the first term is uniformly
bounded by (
4.16
) and (
4.19
). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\lvert\langle a,c\rangle+b|^{2}\leq 2|a\rvert^{2}\lvert c\rvert^{2}+2\lvert b\rvert^{2}

combined with (
4.2

Theorem 4.7
.

The distributional second derivative of

satisfies

Proof.

Recall the formula

i\partial\bar{\partial}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h}^{2}=i\mathrm{d}_{A_{h}}\bm{\phi}\wedge_{h}\overline{\mathrm{d}_{A_{h}}\bm{\phi}}-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h}^{2}\cdot iF_{h}.

Using the fact that
h_{\epsilon}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=h_{0}e^{2f_{\epsilon}(t)}
satisfies the vortex equation for
\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=4\alpha\lVert\mathrm{d}f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)+\eta_{\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)}\lrcorner iF_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}\rVert^{2}+4\alpha\lVert J\eta_{\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)}\lrcorner\mathrm{d}_{A_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}}\bm{\phi}-f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)\bm{\phi}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)-\left|\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)\right|_{\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}}^{2}\right)\cdot i\partial\bar{\partial}\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)-\left|\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)\right|_{\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}}^{2}\right)\left(\alpha\tau\left(\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}-\tau\right)+(\langle S\rangle-c)\right)\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\int_{\Sigma}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)-\left|\mathrm{d}\varphi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(t)\right|_{\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}}^{2}\right)\left[\left(\tau-\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h_{\epsilon}(t)}^{2}\right)^{2}-(\langle S\rangle-c)\right]\omega_{\varphi_{\epsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left(c-\langle S\rangle\right)V\epsilon

\displaystyle=-4\pi\alpha\tau N\epsilon.

Using the decomposition,
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime},T\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime},T\rangle+\langle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime},T\rangle=\langle\mathcal{K}^{\prime\prime},T\rangle+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\langle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\epsilon}^{\prime\prime},T\rangle

of the K-energy along

geodesics established in
[

15
]

Since the term

is also continuous using the continuity of the usual K-energy
[

15

Indeed, if

is such a one-variable function, then its convolution with an approximate of identity is convex and smooth. Hence
f(ta+(1-t)b)\leq tf(a)+(1-t)f(b)
by making the convolution parameter go

5.
Existence and uniqueness of gravitating vortices

5.1.
Uniqueness of gravitating vortices

Here we prove uniqueness of solutions to the gravitating vortex equations in any admissible Kähler class, in the absence of automorphisms.
As a preliminary result, we also prove that the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy is bounded from below on the space of Kähler potentials, provided that these equations have solutions.

We fix a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus

By Theorem

\omega

(5.2)

We start by proving lower boundedness of the reduced
\alpha

Proposition

is used to prove uniqueness of solutions of (

Our strategy follows closely the approach of Berman–Berndtsson to the uniqueness of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics in a fixed Kähler class

As a consequence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1}^{-}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}^{+}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\lambda}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1}^{-}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}^{+}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)+\lambda\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)\right].

Finally, we apply the
strict convexity
of the functional
\mathcal{J}_{\xi}
.
More precisely, the Mabuchi distance
d\left(\omega_{\varphi_{0}^{\lambda}},\omega_{\varphi_{1}^{\lambda}}\right)
has an upper bound
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathcal{J}_{\xi}\left(\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}\right)\geq C\cdot d\left(\omega_{\varphi_{0}^{\lambda}},\omega_{\varphi_{1}^{\lambda}}\right)^{2},

(5.6)

where
C>0
depends on the upper bound of
\omega_{\varphi_{t}^{\lambda}}
(which can be taken uniformly independent of
t
and
\lambda
) and lower bound of
\xi
in terms of the fixed background Kähler metric

\omega_{0}
.
Therefore
d\left(\omega_{\varphi_{0}^{\lambda}},\omega_{\varphi_{1}^{\lambda}}\right)=0
and so
\omega_{\varphi_{0}^{\lambda}}=\omega_{\varphi_{1}^{\lambda}}
for all
\lambda\in(0,\delta)
. We conclude now that
\omega_{1}=\omega_{\varphi_{1}^{0}}=\omega_{\varphi_{0}^{0}}=\omega_{0}
by taking the limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
.
∎

We finish this section with an explicit example of the strict convexity of the functional
\mathcal{J}_{\xi}
on the Riemann sphere.

Example 5.4
.

Suppose
\Sigma={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is the complex projective line and
\omega_{0}=\frac{V}{2\pi}\omega_{FS}
, where
\omega_{FS}
is the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(with total volume
2\pi
).
Let
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the 1-parameter subgroup of automorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
given by
\sigma_{t}([z_{0}:z_{1}])=[e^{-t}z_{0}:e^{t}z_{1}]
in homogeneous coordinates
[z_{0}:z_{1}]
, and
\left\{\varphi_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
the corresponding geodesic line in the space of Kähler potentials
\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
(with

), as in Example
2.10
.
Choosing coordinates

5.2.
Properness of the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy

Our first result in this section is uniqueness and regularity of minimizers of the extended reduced
\alpha
-K-energy
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, provided that the gravitating vortex equations have smooth solutions. More
precisely, we show that the following
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-minimizer set of the extended reduced
\alpha
-K-energy
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
has a unique element:

\mathfrak{M}^{1}:=\{\psi\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}\mid\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(\psi)=\inf_{\varphi\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\}.

(5.8)

Note here that
\inf_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=\inf_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, because
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is invariant under addition of constants.
We follow the approach of Berman–Darvas–Lu
[
10
]
, with the K-energy replaced by
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose either
(i)

g(\Sigma)\geq 1
, or
(ii)

g(\Sigma)=0
and
\bm{\phi}
vanishes at least at
3
points.
If there exists a smooth solution
\omega_{\psi}=\omega_{0}+2i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi
to the gravitating vortex equation (
5.1
) on
(\Sigma,L,\bm{\phi})
, where
\psi\in\mathcal{R}
, then

\mathfrak{M}^{1}=\{\psi\}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}
. We need to show that
v=\psi
. Let
\{v_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{R}
be a sequence such that
d_{1}(v,v_{j})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.
As in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we consider the twisted reduced
\alpha
-K-energy
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
, where the twisting form is
\xi_{j}=\omega_{v_{j}}:=\omega_{0}+2i\partial\bar{\partial}v_{j}
.

Following the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
, one can easily show that for any
\lambda>0
there exists
v_{j}^{\lambda}\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}
that belongs to the
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-minimizer set of the
functional
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
(this set is defined as in (
5.8
), replacing
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
by
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
; note again that
\inf_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}})=\inf_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}})
, because
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
are invariant under addition of constants).
Additionally, for all
w\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}
and
\lambda\geq 0
,

I(v_{j}^{\lambda},v_{j})\leq 2I(w,v_{j}),

where the Aubin–Mabuchi–Yau functional (extended to
\mathcal{E}_{1}
) is given for all
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
by

I(u_{0},u_{1})=V^{-1}\int_{\Sigma}(u_{0}-u_{1})(\omega_{u_{1}}-\omega_{u_{0}})

(cf.
[
10
, Section 2.3]
).
Furthermore, by Lemma
5.2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(-\delta,\delta)
, the smooth solution
\psi\in\mathcal{R}
of the gravitating vortex equation can be perturbed to a
smooth solution
\psi_{j}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{R}
of the twisted gravitating vortex equation

\wp_{j,2}(f_{\psi_{j}^{\lambda}},\psi_{j}^{\lambda},\lambda)=0,

(5.9)

where
\wp_{j,2}
is given by (
5.4
) with
\xi
replaced by
\xi_{j}
.

Now, when
0<\lambda<\delta
, the functional-theoretic minimizer
v_{j}^{\lambda}\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}
and the (smooth) twisted gravitating vortex solution
\psi_{j}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{R}
can be joined by a
finite-energy geodesic
\{u_{j,t}^{\lambda}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, that is contained in
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
, by Theorem
4.8
.
By Theorem
4.8
and the positivity of
\xi_{j}
, the functional
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
is convex along this finite-energy geodesic, so

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0^{+}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(u_{j,t}^{\lambda})\leq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1^{-}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(u_{j,t}^{\lambda}).

(5.10)

The left-hand side of (
5.10
) is non-negative, as
u_{j,0}^{\lambda}=v_{j}^{\lambda}
is a minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
on

\mathcal{E}_{1}
.
For the right-hand side, we first observe that for any two (smooth) Kähler potentials
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{0}}
, the “sub-slope” inequality for the convex function

\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(\varphi_{t})
along the
C^{1,\bar{1}}
geodesic
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
connecting
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
gives

\frac{\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(\varphi_{1})-\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(\varphi_{t})}{1-t}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\bigg\lvert_{t=1^{-}}\cdot\wp_{j,2}(f_{\varphi_{1}},\varphi_{1},\lambda)\omega_{\varphi_{1}}.

Actually, in our case the endpoint
\varphi_{0}=v_{j}^{\lambda}
has only
\mathcal{E}_{1}
regularity, while the endpoint
\varphi_{1}=\psi_{j}^{\lambda}
is smooth, so we first take a sequence

\{v_{j,k}^{\lambda}\}\subset\mathcal{R}
such that
v_{j,k}^{\lambda}\to v_{j}^{\lambda}
as
k\to\infty
and then apply the above inequality to the
C^{1,\bar{1}}
geodesic
\{\varphi^{\lambda}_{j,k,t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}

joining
v_{j,k}^{\lambda}
and
\psi_{j}^{\lambda}
.
By
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
, there exists a subsequence of
C^{1,\bar{1}}
geodesics, still denoted
\{\varphi^{\lambda}_{j,k,t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, such that the velocities at
t=1
satisfy

\dot{\varphi}^{\lambda}_{j,k,1}\to\dot{u}_{j,1}^{\lambda}
as
k\to\infty
almost everywhere with respect to the smooth measure
\omega_{\varphi_{1}}
, where
\varphi_{1}=\psi_{j}^{\lambda}
.
Then the above “sub-slope” inequality can be passed to the limit finite-energy geodesic
\{u_{j,t}^{\lambda}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, yielding the following inequality for the right-hand
side of (
5.10
):

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=1^{-}}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}\right)(u_{j,t}^{\lambda})\leq\int_{\Sigma}\dot{u}_{j,t}^{\lambda}\bigg\lvert_{t=1^{-}}\cdot\wp_{j,2}(f_{\psi_{j}^{\lambda}},\psi_{j}^{\lambda},\lambda)\omega_{\psi_{j}^{\lambda}}=0.

For
\lambda>0
, the functional
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}+\lambda\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
is strictly convex, since
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is convex and
\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
is strictly convex. The fact that
\mathcal{J}_{\xi_{j}}
is strictly convex along geodesics with
smooth
endpoints is a consequence of
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
. However, in the present situation, the geodesic is a finite-energy geodesic connecting a smooth endpoint with a non-smooth endpoint. To justify strict convexity, we appeal to Theorem 4.12 of
[
9
]
. This theorem requires a hypothesis of
\omega_{\varphi_{t}}
being subordinate to
\omega_{0}
(in the sense of measures). This hypothesis holds in our case. Indeed, if
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is linear, then since we are considering a finite-energy geodesic (with the
I
-functional being zero),
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is bounded and hence
\mathcal{K}
is bounded. Hence, the entropy term is bounded. This can only happen if

\omega_{\varphi_{t}}
is subordinate to
\omega
, i.e., it has a Radon-Nikodym density. Otherwise, by definition, the entropy is
+\infty
.
The strictly convexity implies that
d_{1}
-distance between
\omega_{v_{j}^{\lambda}}
and
\omega_{\psi_{j}^{\lambda}}
vanishes, i.e.
v_{j}^{\lambda}=\psi_{j}^{\lambda}+C
for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
, that must be
0
because

I(v_{j}^{\lambda})=I(\psi_{j}^{\lambda})=0
.
Now, by
[
10
, Equation (3.1)]
,

I(\psi_{j}^{\lambda},v)=I(v_{j}^{\lambda},v)\leq\frac{3}{c_{1}}I(v_{j},v),

where
c_{1}>0
is a constant such that
c_{1}I(u_{0},u_{1})\leq I(u_{0},v)+I(u_{1},v)
for all
u_{0},u_{1},v\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
(see
[
10
, (2.16)]
).
Taking the limit
\lambda\to 0
for any fixed
j
, we have

I(\psi,v)\leq\frac{3}{c_{1}}I(v_{j},v).

This implies
\psi=v+C
by taking
j\to+\infty
and using the
d_{1}
-continuity of the functional
I
. Finally, we conclude that
C=0
, by the normalization condition
I(v)=I(\psi)
, so
v=\psi
.
∎

Theorem
5.5
is now used to prove properness of the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose either
(i)

g(\Sigma)\geq 1
, or
(ii)

g(\Sigma)=0

\bm{\phi}

5.3.
Stability in genus
g=0

Now that we have established properness of the reduced
\alpha
-K-energy (Theorem

Theorem 5.7
.

If there exists a smooth solution to the gravitating vortex equation (
5.1

(\mathbb{P}^{1},L,\bm{\phi})

D=\sum_{j}n_{j}p_{j}

A basic link between the gravitating vortex equation and Geometric Invariant Theory, used in the proof of Theorem

[

To review its construction, we fix a Kähler metric

Then the value of this invariant on any

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\tau},\bm{v}^{1,0}\rangle&\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=8\alpha\int_{\Sigma}i\psi_{h}\left(iF_{h}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|\bm{\phi}\right|_{h}^{2}-\tau\right)\omega\right)\\

Proof of Theorem
5.7

Let
\omega_{0}=\frac{V}{2\pi}\omega_{FS}

2.10

Locally compact sofic entropy theory
Lewis Bowen

Abstract
This paper generalizes sofic entropy theory, in both the topological and measure-theory settings, to actions of locally compact groups. We prove invariance under topological and measure conjugacy of these entropies and establish the variational principle.



Keywords
: entropy, sofic groups, locally compact groups

Introduction

The entropy of a measure-preserving transformation
T:X\to X
on a probability space
(X,{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
with respect to a measurable map
\phi:X\to A
(where
A
is finite or countable) is defined as follows. First we consider the map
\phi^{n}:X\to A^{n}

\phi^{n}(x)=(\phi(x),\phi(Tx),\ldots,\phi(T^{n-1}x)).

Then pushforward the measure
\mu
to obtain a measure
\phi^{n}_{*}\mu
on
A^{n}
. The Shannon entropy of this measure is defined by

H(\phi^{n}_{*}\mu)=\sum_{{\bf{a}}\in A^{n}}-\phi^{n}_{*}\mu({\bf{a}})\log(\phi^{n}_{*}\mu({\bf{a}})).

Finally, the entropy rate of
T
with respect to
\mu
and
\phi
is

h_{\mu}(T,\phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}H(\phi^{n}_{*}\mu).

For example, if
T
represents time and
\phi
represents a measurement on the system then
H(\phi^{n}_{*}\mu)
is the expected amount of information gained from the measurements up to time
n
. So the entropy rate is the expected information gain per unit time.

The entropy of
T
, denoted
h_{\mu}(T)
, is the supremum of
h_{\mu}(T,\phi)
over all observables
\phi
with finite range. It is a measure-conjugacy invariant and is crucial for classification purposes. In fact, Kolmogorov introduced entropy in order to prove the existence of Bernoulli shifts which are not measurably conjugate (because they have different entropies)
[
Kol58
,
Kol59
]
. Sinai proved that, if
T
is ergodic, then
T
factors onto a Bernoulli shift with the same entropy
[
Sin64
]
and Ornstein proved that two Bernoulli shifts are measurably conjugate if and only if they have the same entropy
[
Orn70
]
.

Since these classical results were obtained, entropy theory has been generalized to actions of countable amenable groups
[
Kie75
,
OW80
]
,
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

[
Fel80
]
, locally compact amenable groups
[
OW87
,
Avn10
]
and countable sofic groups
[
Bow10
,
KL11b
,
KL16
,
Bow20
]
. The goal of this paper is to generalize entropy theory to actions of locally compact sofic groups.

1.1
Sofic groups and the residually finite case

A countable group
\Gamma
is called
sofic
if it admits a sofic approximation, which is a sequence of partial actions on finite sets which locally approximates the action of
\Gamma
on itself by left-translations
[
KL16
,
Pes08
,
CL15
]
. To illustrate, suppose
\Gamma
is residually finite. This means there exist normal finite-index subgroups
N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\cdots
in
\Gamma
such that
\cap_{i}N_{i}=\{1_{\Gamma}\}
. The sequence
\Sigma
of actions
\Gamma{\curvearrowright}\Gamma/N_{i}
is a special kind of
sofic approximation
to
\Gamma
called a
transitive sofic approximation
or a
sofic approximation by homomorphisms
. To motivate our approach, we first review sofic entropy of
\Gamma
-actions with respect to such a sequence.

Let
(X,\rho_{X})
be a compact metric space on which
\Gamma
acts by homeomorphisms. An orbit
\Gamma x\subset X
is
periodic
if the stabilizer of
x
,
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(x)=\{g\in\Gamma:~gx=x\}
has finite index in
\Gamma
. We can also think of a periodic orbit as the image of a
\Gamma
-equivariant map
\phi:\Gamma/H\to X
for some finite index subgroup
H
.

While the periodic orbits are certainly of interest, they are too restrictive for entropy purposes. Instead, we consider
almost periodic orbits
. These are quantified by a finite subset
U\subset\Gamma
and an error tolerance
\delta>0
. A map
\phi:\Gamma/N_{i}\to X
is
(\rho_{X},U,\delta)
-equivariant
if

|\Gamma/N_{i}|^{-1}\sum_{hN_{i}\in\Gamma/N_{i}}\rho_{X}(\phi(ghN_{i}),g\phi(hN_{i}))<\delta

for all
g\in U
. Let
{\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
be the set of all
(\rho_{X},U,\delta)
-equivariant maps of
\Gamma/N_{i}
into
X
.

In order to quantify the ‘size’ of
{\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
, we introduce a metric on it. In general, if
\phi,\psi
are maps from
\Gamma/N_{i}
to
X
then we set

\rho_{X}^{\Gamma/N_{i}}(\phi,\psi)=|\Gamma/N_{i}|^{-1}\sum_{hN_{i}\in\Gamma/N_{i}}\rho_{X}(\phi(hN_{i}),\psi(hN_{i})).

Let
\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta))
denote the maximum cardinality of a
(\rho_{X}^{\Gamma/N_{i}},\epsilon)
-separated subset of
{\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
.

Finally, we can define topological
\Sigma
-entropy by

h_{\Sigma}(\Gamma,X)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{U\subset\Gamma}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}|\Gamma/N_{i}|^{-1}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta))

where the first infimum is over all finite subsets
U\subset\Gamma
.

To define measure entropy, let
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
denote the space of Borel probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak* topology. This is the smallest topology such that for every continuous
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the map
\mu\mapsto\int f~\mathrm{d}\mu
is continuous on
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
. Given an open set
{\mathcal{O}}\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)
, let
{\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}})
be the set of
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
such that if
u_{i}
denotes the uniform probability measure on
\Gamma/N_{i}
then
\phi_{*}u_{i}\in{\mathcal{O}}
where
\phi_{*}u_{i}
is called the
empirical measure
of
\phi
.

Let
\mu
be a
\Gamma
-invariant probability measure on
X
. The measure
\Sigma
-entropy of the action
(\Gamma,X,\mu)
is defined by

h_{\Sigma}(\Gamma,X,\mu)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{{\mathcal{O}}\ni\mu}\inf_{U\subset\Gamma}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}|\Gamma/N_{i}|^{-1}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(\Gamma/N_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}}))

where
{\mathcal{O}}
varies over all open neighborhoods of
\mu
in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
and, as above,
U
varies over finite subsets of
\Gamma
.

The approach to entropy theory through
\epsilon
-separating numbers of partial orbits was first introduced by Rufus Bowen
[
Bow71
]
. It was introduced into the sofic context by Kerr and Li
[
KL11b
]
.

Some of the main results about these entropies are: topological
\Sigma
-entropy is a topological conjugacy invariant while measure
\Sigma
-entropy is a measure conjugacy invariant. The variational principle holds: topological
\Sigma
-entropy is the supremum of
h_{\Sigma}(\Gamma,X,\mu)
over all
\Gamma
-invariant measures
\mu
. Moreover the entropies of Bernoulli shifts, Gaussian actions and many algebraic actions have been computed
[
Bow10
,
KL11a
,
Hay17
,
Hay16
]
. These entropies agree with their classical counterparts when
\Gamma
is amenable
[
KL13
]
. See
[
KL16
,
Bow20
]
for more results.

A locally compact group

is
sofic
if it admits a
sofic approximation

[

]
. Roughly speaking, a sofic approximation is a sequence of partial actions of
G
on topological spaces which locally approximates the action of
G
on itself by left-translations. This is reviewed in §
2
below. For example, suppose
G
admits a lattice

which is residually finite. Let
\{\Gamma{\curvearrowright}\Gamma/N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sofic approximation to

{\widetilde{\Sigma}}=\{G{\curvearrowright}G/N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

For the sake of motivation, we describe sofic entropy with respect to
{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
; the general case is explained in the paper. Actually, the definitions are straightforward generalizations of the countable case. Suppose
G{\curvearrowright}(X,\rho_{X})
is an action on a compact metric space. Given a pre-compact open set

and an error tolerance
\delta>0
, a measurable map
\phi:G/N_{i}\to X

(\rho_{X},U,\delta)

\int_{G/N_{i}}\rho_{X}(\phi(gp),g\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)<\delta

where
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}

\rho^{G/N_{i}}_{X}(\phi,\psi)=\int_{G/N_{i}}\rho_{X}(\phi(p),\psi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p).

h_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(G,X)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}(G/N_{i})^{-1}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta))

where

1.

{\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
is the set of
(\rho_{X},U,\delta)

(\rho_{X}^{G/N_{i}},\epsilon)

{\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
;

3.

the first infimum is over all pre-compact open subsets

;

4.

\mathrm{vol}(G/N_{i})
is the volume of

with respect to a fixed left-invariant Haar measure on
G

Similarly, if

{\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}})
be the set of all
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
such that
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\in{\mathcal{O}}
where
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(E)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(\phi^{-1}(E))
is the pushforward measure.

Let
\mu
be a
G
-invariant probability measure on
X

-entropy of the action
(G,X,\mu)
is defined by

h_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(G,X,\mu)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{\mathcal{O}}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}(G/N_{i})^{-1}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}}))

where
{\mathcal{O}}
varies over all open neighborhoods of
\mu
in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
.

Our main results are: topological
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy is invariant under topological conjugacy, measure
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy is invariant under measure conjugacy and the variational principle holds.

In the paper, we work in a more general setting. First, we do not require that
X
is compact. The reason for this is that there are many actions of locally compact groups, such as the action on the space of point measures, where the action space
X
is not compact but the action map
G\times X\to X
given by
(g,x)\mapsto gx
is uniformly continuous. Moreover, the arguments do not require compactness as long as
X
admits a Polish metric with respect to which the action is uniformly continuous.

Second, we do not require that
\rho_{X}
is a metric on
X
. Instead, we assume
\rho_{X}
is a bounded, uniformly continuous, pseudo-metric which is
dynamically generating
. The latter means that if
x,y\in X
and
x\neq y
then there exists
g\in G
with
\rho_{X}(gx,gy)>0
.

1.3
About the proofs

The rough outlines of the proofs are similar to the countable case (as in
[
KL16
]
or as outlined in
[
Bow20
]
), however there are some significant differences highlighted here in the special case of homogeneous sofic approximations.

In the case of topological entropy, we say that a measurable map
\phi:G/N_{i}\to X
is
(\rho_{X},U,\delta)
-equivariant on average
if

\int_{U}\int_{G/N_{i}}\rho_{X}(\phi(gp),g\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)<\delta\mathrm{Haar}(U)

where
\mathrm{Haar}
is a fixed left-Haar measure on
G
. Let
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
be the set of such maps. We prove that topological
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy does not change if we replace
{\operatorname{Map}}(\cdot)
with
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}(\cdot)
. The benefit is that it is easier to construct maps which satisfy this weaker equivariance and this helps establish lower bounds on topological
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy.

In the case of measure entropy, we say a map
\phi:G/N_{i}\to X
is
measure-preserving
if
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}=\mu
. Let
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\textrm{mp}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)\subset{\operatorname{Map}}(G/N_{i},X,\rho_{X}:U,\delta)
be the subset of such maps. We prove that measure
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy does not change if we replace
{\operatorname{Map}}(\cdot)
with
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\textrm{mp}}(\cdot)
, when the sofic approximation has no atoms. This helps establish upper bounds on measure
\widetilde{\Sigma}
-entropy.

Our proof of the Variational Principle is streamlined. It appears to be shorter than previous proofs even in the case when the acting group is countable.

Preliminaries on uniform actions, pseudo-metrics, separated and spanning sets, and model spaces

3.1
Uniform actions and conjugacies

If
(Z,d_{Z})
is a metric space then an action
G{\curvearrowright}Z
by homeomorphisms is
uniform
if the map
G\times Z\to Z
is uniformly continuous. This means: for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

d_{G}(g_{1},g_{2})+d_{Z}(z_{1},z_{2})<\delta\Rightarrow d_{Z}(g_{1}z_{1},g_{2}z_{2})<\epsilon

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
where
d_{G}
is a fixed left-invariant proper continuous metric on
G
.

Suppose we are given two uniform actions
G{\curvearrowright}Z
and
G{\curvearrowright}Y
. A map
\Phi:Y\to Z
is a
uniform conjugacy
if it is uniformly continuous, invertible,
\Phi^{-1}:Y\to Z
is uniformly continuous and
\Phi(gz)=g\Phi(z)
for all
g\in G
and
z\in Z
.

3.2
Pseudo-metrics and separating/spanning/covering sets

A
pseudo-metric

\rho:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
on a set
X
is a metric on
X
except that we allow
\rho(x,y)=0
even if
x\neq y
. A pseudo-metric
\rho
is

•

1-bounded
if
\rho(x,y)\leq 1
for all
x,y\in X
;

•

generating
for an action
G{\curvearrowright}X
if for every
x\neq y
there exists
g\in G
such that
\rho(gx,gy)>0
.

If
(X,d_{X})
is a metric space then a pseudo-metric
\rho
on
X
is
uniformly continuous
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\rho(x,y)<\delta
implies
d_{X}(x,y)<\epsilon
.

If
(X,\rho)
is a pseudo-metric space then a subset
Y\subset X
is

•

(\rho,\epsilon)
-separated
if
\rho(x,y)>\epsilon
for all
x,y\in Y
with
x\neq y
;

•

(\rho,\epsilon)
-spanning
if for all
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
with
\rho(x,y)<\epsilon
.

We say that a collection
{\mathcal{C}}
of subsets of
X
is a
(\rho,\epsilon)
-cover
if the union of sets in
{\mathcal{C}}
is
X
and each

has diameter

.

Let

•

\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}(X,\rho)
be the maximum cardinality of a

-separated subset;

•

\operatorname{Span}_{\epsilon}(X,\rho)

-spanning subset;

•

-cover.

It is well-known (and an easy exercise) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(\rho,2\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{Span}(\rho,\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{Sep}(\rho,\epsilon)\leq\operatorname{Cov}(\rho,\epsilon).

Notation 2

Given a finite measure space
(M,\mathrm{vol})
we let
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
denote the probability measure
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(E)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(E)}{\mathrm{vol}(M)}
.

Suppose
(M,\operatorname{vol})
is a finite measure space and
X
is a Hausdorff space. Let
{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X)
denote the space of all measurable maps from
M
to
X
. If
\rho
is a pseudo-metric on
X
then let
\rho^{M}
be the pseudo-metric on
{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X)
defined by

\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)=\operatorname{vol}(M)^{-1}\int\rho(\phi(p),\psi(p))~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}(p)=\int\rho(\phi(p),\psi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p).

More generally, suppose
\phi,\psi
are measurable maps to
X
, but the domains of
\phi
and
\psi
are not necessarily all of
M
. Let
D\subset M
be the intersection of the domains of
\phi
and
\psi
. Then define

\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M\setminus D)+\int_{p\in D}\rho(\phi(p),\psi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p).

Then
\rho^{M}
is symmetric and satisfies the triangle inequality but
\rho^{M}(\phi,\phi)
can be positive and
\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)
can be zero even if
\phi\neq\psi
. So
\rho^{M}
is a quasi-metric.

4
Topological sofic entropy

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a Polish metric space and
G{\curvearrowright}X
a uniform action. Let
\rho:X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous pseudo-metric. In this section, we define the topological
\Sigma
-entropy of the action and prove it is invariant under uniform conjugacy.

Definition 6
.

Let
M
be a finite volume local
G
-space and
\phi:M\to X
. For
g\in G
define
\phi\circ g
by
(\phi\circ g)(p)=\phi(g.p)
for every
p\in M
such that
g.p
is well-defined.
We also define
g\circ\phi:M\to X
in the associative way
(g\circ\phi)(p)=g(\phi(p))
.

Definition 7
.

Let
U\subset G
be a pre-compact neighborhood of identity,
\epsilon>0
. Suppose that
M
is a finite volume local
G
-space. A map
\phi:M\to X
is
(U,\delta,\rho^{M})
-equivariant
if

\rho^{M}\left(\phi\circ g,g\circ\phi\right)<\delta

for all
g\in U
. Denote by
{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\subset{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X)
the set of all
(U,\delta,\rho^{M})
-equivariant maps.

Definition 8
.

To simplify notation, we write
\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right)
for the maximum cardinality of a
(\rho^{M},\epsilon)
-separated subset of
{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
. We define
\operatorname{Span}_{\epsilon}(\cdot)
and
\operatorname{Cov}_{\epsilon}(\cdot)
similarly.

Definition 9
.

Let
G{\curvearrowright}X
and
\rho
be as above. Also let
\Sigma=\{M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sofic approximation to
G
. Define

\displaystyle h^{\epsilon}_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{i}\right)}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right)

\displaystyle h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}h^{\epsilon}_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)

where the first infimum is over all pre-compact neighborhoods
U
of the identity in
G
. By (
2
), we can replace
\operatorname{Sep}
with
\operatorname{Span}
or
\operatorname{Cov}
without changing the values of
h^{\epsilon}_{\Sigma}
or
h_{\Sigma}
.

Remark 3
.

The quantity
\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right)
decreases monotonically as
\delta
decreases or
U
increases. It increases as
\epsilon
decreases. So an equivalent definition of entropy is:

h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\lim_{U\nearrow G}\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{i}\right)}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right).

Our main theorem is that topological sofic entropy is invariant under uniform conjugacy.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G{\curvearrowright}X
,
G{\curvearrowright}Y
be uniform actions on Polish metric spaces
(X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})
. Also let
\rho_{X},\rho_{Y}
be uniformly continuous, generating, 1-bounded pseudo-metrics on
X
and
Y
respectively. Suppose the actions are uniformly conjugate. Then

h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho_{X}\right)=h_{\Sigma}\left(G,Y,\rho_{Y}\right)

for any sofic approximation
\Sigma
.

Definition 10
.

We will often write
h_{\Sigma}(G,X)
for
h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho_{X}\right)
where
\rho_{X}
is implicit. This invariant is called the
\Sigma
-entropy
or
topological sofic entropy
of the action
G{\curvearrowright}X
. It may depend on the choice of sofic approximation
\Sigma
, however this choice is often left implicit in informal discussion.

Remark 4
.

If
G
is a countable group then the definition of
h_{\Sigma}(G,X)
reduces in a straightforward way to the standard definition of topological sofic entropy, as presented in
[
KL16
]
for example.

Notation 3
.

To avoid being overly verbose, we use the following conventions. The letter
U,{\widetilde{U}}
and variants always denotes a pre-compact open neighborhood of the identity while
\delta,{\widetilde{\delta}},\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}
etc. are always positive.

4.1
Equivariance on average

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we first weaken the notion of a good map from “for all
g\in U
” to an average. For this section, we write
\rho=\rho_{X}
. We say a map
\phi:M\to X
is
(U,\delta,\rho^{M})
-equivariant-on-average
if

\int_{U}\rho^{M}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)<\delta\mathrm{Haar}(U).

Let
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
be the space of all such maps. Also, let

h_{\Sigma,\operatorname{avg}}\left(G,X,\rho\right)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{i}\right)}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\rho
be a uniformly continuous, 1-bounded pseudo-metric on
X
. Then

h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)=h_{\Sigma,\operatorname{avg}}\left(G,X,\rho\right).

To prove this we will need the following lemma. Let
B(r)\subset G
denote the open ball of radius
r
centered at the identity in
G
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<r_{0}<r_{1}<r_{2}
and
\delta>0
. Suppose
r_{0}+r_{1}<r_{2}
and

\delta\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{2}))<(1-\delta)\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0})).

If
W_{i}\subset B(r_{i})
are any measurable subsets with

\mathrm{Haar}(W_{i})>(1-\delta)\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{i}))

for
i=0,2
then
W_{0}W_{2}\supset B(r_{1})
.

Proof.

Because
G
is sofic, it is unimodular. So it suffices to show
W_{0}^{-1}W_{2}\supset B(r_{1})
. Equivalently, we must show that for every
g\in B(r_{1})
,
W_{0}g\cap W_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Thus it suffices to show

\mathrm{Haar}(W_{0}g\cup W_{2})<\mathrm{Haar}(W_{0})+\mathrm{Haar}(W_{2}).

This follows from:

\mathrm{Haar}(W_{0}g\cup W_{2})\leq\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{2}))\leq(1-\delta)[\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0}))+\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{2}))]<\mathrm{Haar}(W_{0})+\mathrm{Haar}(W_{2}).

The first equality holds because
B(r_{2})\supset W_{0}g\cap W_{2}
, while the other inequalities hold by hypothesis.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Because
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\supset{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
, it is immediate that

h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)\leq h_{\Sigma,\operatorname{avg}}\left(G,X,\rho\right).

To obtain the opposite inequality, it suffices to show that for every
U,\delta
there exists
{\widetilde{U}}
,
{\widetilde{\delta}}
,
I
such that if
i>I
then
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:{\widetilde{U}},{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\subset{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
.

Let
U,\delta
be given. Without loss of generality,
\delta<1
. We choose parameters
\kappa,r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},{\widetilde{\delta}},{\widetilde{U}},i
as follows. Because
\rho
and the action are uniformly continuous there exist
\kappa>0
and
r_{0}>0
such that if
x,y\in X
satisfy
\rho(x,y)<\kappa
and
g\in B(r_{0})
then
\rho(gx,gy)<\delta/10
.

Let
r_{1}>0
be such that
U\subset B(r_{1})
. Choose
r_{2}>r_{0}+r_{1}
. Let
{\widetilde{\delta}}>0
be such that

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\delta}}\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{2}))<(1-{\widetilde{\delta}})\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0}))

(3)

and
{\widetilde{\delta}}<\min(\kappa,\delta/10)
. Let
{\widetilde{U}}=B(r_{2})
.

Suppose
i
is large enough so that
\mathrm{vol}(M_{i}[{\widetilde{U}}^{2}])>(1-{\widetilde{\delta}}^{10})\mathrm{vol}(M_{i})
. Let
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:{\widetilde{U}},{\widetilde{\delta}}^{10}\right)
. It suffices to show
\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)<\delta
for all
g\in U
.

For
g\in{\widetilde{U}}
, let
M_{i}[g]
be the set of points
p\in M_{i}
such that
g.p
is well-defined. Also define

\displaystyle{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{p\in M_{i}[g]:~\rho(g\phi(p),\phi(g.p))<{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in{\widetilde{U}}:~\overline{\operatorname{vol}}({\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g))>1-{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}\right\}.

Because
\phi
is
({\widetilde{U}},{\widetilde{\delta}}^{10},\rho^{M_{i}})
-equivariant-on-average,

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\delta}}^{10}\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\int_{{\widetilde{U}}}\int_{M_{i}[g]}\rho(g\phi(p),\phi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{{\widetilde{U}}\setminus W}\int_{M_{i}[g]\setminus{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g)}\rho(g\phi(p),\phi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}\int_{{\widetilde{U}}\setminus W}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)(M_{i}[g]\setminus{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g))~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}\int_{{\widetilde{U}}\setminus W}{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}-\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[g])~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}}\setminus W){\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}({\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}-{\widetilde{\delta}}^{10})>\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}}\setminus W){\widetilde{\delta}}^{9}

Therefore,

{\widetilde{\delta}}\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}})>(1-{\widetilde{\delta}})\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}}\setminus W).

We use
{\widetilde{U}}\supset B(r_{0})
and (
3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Haar}(W\cap B(r_{0}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0}))-\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}}\setminus W)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0}))-{\widetilde{\delta}}\mathrm{Haar}({\widetilde{U}})\geq(1-{\widetilde{\delta}})\mathrm{Haar}(B(r_{0})).

Now suppose
g\in U
. By Lemma
4.3
, there exist
h\in W\cap B(r_{0})
and
k\in W
such that
g=hk
. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)=\rho^{M_{i}}(hk\circ\phi,\phi\circ hk)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho^{M_{i}}(hk\circ\phi,h\circ(\phi\circ k))+\rho^{M_{i}}(h\circ(\phi\circ k),(\phi\circ h)\circ k)+\rho^{M_{i}}((\phi\circ h)\circ k,\phi\circ hk).

To estimate the first summand, note that, because
k\in W
,
\mathrm{vol}({\widetilde{M}}_{i}(k))>(1-{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4})\mathrm{vol}(M_{i})
. Moreover, if
p\in{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(k)
then
\rho(k\phi(p),\phi(k.p))<{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}<\kappa
which implies (by choice of
r_{0}
) that
\rho(hk\phi(p),h\phi(k.p))<\delta/10
. Thus

\rho^{M_{i}}(hk\circ\phi,h\circ(\phi\circ k))\leq\delta/10+\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(k))\leq\delta/10+{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}<2\delta/10.

To estimate the second summand, suppose
k.p\in{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(h)
(i.e.
p\in k^{-1}.{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(h)
). Then
\rho(h\phi(k.p),\phi(h.k.p))\leq{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}
. Recall that volume is locally preserved by Lemma
2.2
. Since
k\in W
, it follows that

\mathrm{vol}(k^{-1}.{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(h))\geq\mathrm{vol}({\widetilde{M}}_{i}(h)\cap M[{\widetilde{U}}])\geq(1-{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}-\delta/10)\mathrm{vol}(M_{i}).

Therefore,

\rho^{M_{i}}(h\circ(\phi\circ k),(\phi\circ h)\circ k)\leq 2{\widetilde{\delta}}^{4}+\delta/10\leq 3\delta/10.

For the last summand, suppose
p\in M_{i}[{\widetilde{U}}^{2}]
. Then
h.k.p=hk.p
. Therefore,

\rho^{M_{i}}((\phi\circ h)\circ k,\phi\circ hk)\leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[{\widetilde{U}}^{2}])\leq\delta/10.

Combining these estimates yields

\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)\leq 6\delta/10<\delta.

Since
g\in U
is arbitrary, this proves
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
.
∎

4.2
Replacing pseudo-metrics with metrics

Next we prove that pseudo-metrics can be replaced with metrics.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\rho
be a uniformly continuous, generating, 1-bounded pseudo-metric on
X
, and
f:G\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous function with
1=\int f~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}<\infty
. Define
\rho_{f}
by

\rho_{f}(x,y):=\int\rho(gx,gy)f(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g).

Then
h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)=h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho_{f}\right)
. Moreover,
\rho_{f}
is a uniformly continuous
1
-bounded metric on
X
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
\rho_{f}
is a
1
-bounded uniformly continuous pseudo-metric. To check that it is a metric, suppose
x,y\in X
and
\rho_{f}(x,y)=0
. Since the integrand defining
\rho_{f}(x,y)
is non-negative, this implies
\rho(gx,gy)f(g)=0
for a.e.
g
. However
f>0
. Therefore
\rho(gx,gy)=0
for a.e.
g
. In particular,
\rho(gx,gy)=0
for all
g
in a dense subset of
G
. Since
\rho
and the action are continuous, the map
g\mapsto\rho(gx,gy)
is continuous. Therefore,
\rho(gx,gy)=0
for all
g\in G
. Since
\rho
is generating, this implies
x=y
. Therefore
\rho_{f}
is a metric.

Next, we check the inequality
h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho\right)\leq h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\rho_{f}\right)
. To see this, let
U_{f}\subset G
be a pre-compact neighborhood of the identity and
\delta_{f}>0
.

Claim 1
. There exist
\delta
and a pre-compact open
W\subset G
satisfying

•

U_{f}\subset W
;

•

0<\delta<\delta_{f}/10
;

•

if
x,y\in X
and
\mathrm{Haar}(\{g\in W:~\rho(gx,gy)>\delta\})<\delta\mathrm{Haar}(W)
then
\rho_{f}(x,y)<\delta_{f}/2
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Choose
W\supset U_{f}
large enough so that

\int_{G\setminus W}f(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)<\delta_{f}/10.

Choose
\delta>0
small enough so that
\delta<\delta_{f}/10
and if
V\subset W
is any set with Haar measure
\leq\delta\mathrm{Haar}(W)
then

\int_{V}f(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)<\delta_{f}/10.

We claim the last condition is now satisfied. To check this, let
x,y\in X
and
V=\{g\in W:~\rho(gx,gy)>\delta\}
. Suppose
\mathrm{Haar}(V)<\delta\mathrm{Haar}(W)
. Let
I(g)=\rho(gx,gy)f(g)
. Because
\rho
is
1
-bounded,

\displaystyle\rho_{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G\setminus W}I(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)+\int_{W\setminus V}I(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)+\int_{V}I(g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta_{f}/10+\delta+\delta_{f}/10\leq 3\delta_{f}/10<\delta_{f}/2.

∎

Let
U=WU_{f}
.

Claim 2
.

{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta^{10}\right)\subset{\operatorname{Map}}_{\operatorname{avg}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho_{f}:U_{f},\delta_{f}\right).

Proof of Claim 2.

Fix
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta^{10}\right)
. It suffices to show

\delta_{f}\mathrm{Haar}(U_{f})>\int_{U_{f}}\rho^{M_{i}}_{f}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g).

For
g\in U_{f}
and
p\in M_{i}
, let

\displaystyle W(g,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{h\in W:~\rho(hg\phi(p),h\phi(g.p))\leq\delta^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{p\in M_{i}:~\mathrm{Haar}(W(g,p))\geq(1-\delta^{4})\mathrm{Haar}(W)\right\}.

Because
\phi
is
(U,\delta^{10},\rho^{M_{i}})
-equivariant,
g\in U_{f}
and
WU_{f}=U
,

\displaystyle\delta^{10}\mathrm{Haar}(W)\geq\int_{W}\rho^{M_{i}}(hg\circ\phi,h\circ(\phi\circ g))~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(h)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[g])\mathrm{Haar}(W)+\int_{M_{i}[g]\setminus{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g)}\int_{W\setminus W(g,p)}\rho(hg\phi(p),h\phi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(h)~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[g])\mathrm{Haar}(W)+\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}[g]\setminus{\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g))\delta^{8}\mathrm{Haar}(W).

Solve for

to obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}({\widetilde{M}}_{i}(g))

\displaystyle 1+(1-\delta^{-8})\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}[g])-\delta^{2}\geq 1-\delta^{2}.

By Claim 1, if

then
\rho_{f}(g\phi(p),\phi(g.p))<\delta_{f}/2
. Because

is

∎

By uniform continuity of the

, for any

next.

Claim 3

It follows from Claims 2 and 3 that

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\supset{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho_{f}:U_{f},\delta_{f}\right)

(4)

whenever

and
\delta_{f}\leq\kappa
.

Claim 4
. Let
U,\delta,\epsilon_{f}
be given. Then there exist
U_{f},\delta_{f},\epsilon,I
such that if

then
\forall\phi,\psi\in{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U_{f},\delta_{f}\right)
, if

\rho^{M_{i}}_{f}(\phi,\psi)\leq\epsilon_{f}
.

Proof of Claim 4.

First we choose the parameters
U_{f},\delta_{f},\epsilon,I
.

By Claim 1, there exist
\eta>0
and

\mathrm{Haar}(\{g\in W:\rho(gx,gy)>\eta\})<\eta\mathrm{Haar}(W)

Let
U_{f}\supset W
be a pre-compact open set. Choose

2\delta_{f}+11\epsilon/10<\eta^{2}\epsilon_{f}/10.

Let

\mathrm{vol}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[W])<\epsilon/10

Let

\rho^{M_{i}}_{f}(\phi,\psi)\leq\epsilon_{f}

The constant

We will obtain this estimate using what we know about

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{W}\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,g\circ\psi)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{W}\left[\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)+\rho^{M_{i}}(\phi\circ g,\psi\circ g)+\rho^{M_{i}}(\psi\circ g,g\circ\psi)\right]~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g).

Because

\int_{W}\left[\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)+\rho^{M_{i}}(\psi\circ g,g\circ\psi)\right]~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)\leq 2\delta_{f}\mathrm{Haar}(W).

Because
\rho^{M_{i}}(\phi,\psi)\leq\epsilon

\mathrm{vol}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[W])<\epsilon/10

\displaystyle\int_{W}\rho^{M_{i}}(\phi\circ g,\psi\circ g)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus M_{i}[W])\mathrm{Haar}(W)+\int_{W}\int_{M_{i}[W]}\rho(\phi(g.p),\psi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle(\epsilon+\epsilon/10)\mathrm{Haar}(W)

where the last inequality uses Lemma

Combine this with the previous inequalities to obtain

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{W}\rho^{M_{i}}(g\circ\phi,g\circ\psi)~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M_{i}\setminus M(\eta)}\int_{W}\rho(g\phi(p),g\psi(p))~\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Haar}(g)~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M_{i}\setminus M(\eta))\mathrm{Haar}(W)\eta^{2}.

Thus

\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M(\eta))\geq\left(1-\frac{2\delta_{f}+11\epsilon/10}{\eta^{2}}\right)>1-\epsilon_{f}/10.

∎

It follows from (

5
Preliminaries to measure entropy theory

5.1
Space of probability measures

Let
X
be a Polish space and
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
denote the space of all Borel probability measures on
X
with the weak* topology. This is the smallest topology such that for every bounded continuous function
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
the map
\mu\mapsto\int f~\mathrm{d}\mu
is continuous on
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
. If
X
is compact then
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
is also compact by the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem.

If
G{\curvearrowright}X
is a jointly continuous action then let
\operatorname{Prob}_{G}(X)\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)
be the subspace of
G
-invariant Borel probability measures. This space is closed in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
.

5.2
Measure conjugacy

In this paper, all probability spaces are standard. We denote such a space by
(X,{\mathcal{B}}_{X},\mu)
or just
(X,\mu)
, leaving the sigma-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}_{X}
implicit.

An action of
G
on a space
X
equipped with a sigma-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}_{X}
is
measurable
if the action map
G\times X\to X
defined by
(g,x)\mapsto gx
is measurable.

Suppose
G{\curvearrowright}(X,{\mathcal{B}}_{X},\mu)
and
G{\curvearrowright}(Y,{\mathcal{B}}_{Y},\nu)
are measurable actions on standard probability spaces. A
measure-conjugacy
is a measurable map
\Phi:X\to Y
with measurable inverse
\Phi^{-1}:Y\to X
such that
\Phi_{*}\mu=\nu
(where
\Phi_{*}\mu
is the measure on
Y
defined by
\Phi_{*}\mu(E)=\mu(\Phi^{-1}(E))
) and
\Phi(gx)=g\Phi(x)
for
g\in G
and
x\in X
.

6
Measure sofic entropy

The goal of this section is to define measure sofic entropy and prove that it is a measure-conjugacy invariant. The definition is similar to that of topological sofic entropy with the exception that the notion of ‘good map’ must be restricted to maps which nearly take the normalized volume on a local
G
-space
M_{i}
to the target measure
\mu
on
X
.

To make this precise, let
(X,d_{X})
be a Polish metric space,
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
denote the space of Borel probability measures on
X
with the weak* topology,
G{\curvearrowright}X
a uniform action and
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(X)
a
G
-invariant Borel probability measure on
X
. We will denote the action by the triple
(G,X,\mu)
. Also let
\rho:X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a uniformly continuous, 1-bounded, pseudo-metric.

Definition 11
.

Let
U\subset G
be a pre-compact neighborhood of identity,
{\mathcal{O}}\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)
an open neighborhood of
\mu
,
\epsilon>0
. Suppose that
M
is a local
G
-space with finite measure
\mathrm{vol}
. As before we let
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
denote the normalized volume on
M
, so that
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M^{\prime})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(M^{\prime})}{\mathrm{vol}(M)}
for any

. Denote by
{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}}\right)
the set of all maps
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M,X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
such that
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\in{\mathcal{O}}
.

Definition 12
.

Let
\Sigma=\{M_{i}\}
be a sofic approximation to
G
. Define

\displaystyle h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\mu,\rho\right)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{\mathcal{O}}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{i}\right)}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}}\right)\right)

where the second infimum is over all pre-compact neighborhoods
U
of the identity in
G
and the first infimum is over all open neighborhoods
{\mathcal{O}}
of
\mu
in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
.

The goal of this section is to prove:

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
(G,X,\mu)
and
(G,Y,\nu)
are uniformly continuous, pmp actions and
\rho_{X},\rho_{Y}
are 1-bounded, uniformly continuous, generating, pseudo-metrics on
X
,
Y
respectively. If
(G,X,\mu)
and
(G,Y,\nu)
are measurably conjugate then
h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu,\rho_{X})=h_{\Sigma}(G,Y,\nu,\rho_{Y})
.

Definition 13
.

After this theorem has been proven we will write
h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu)=h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu,\rho_{X})
where
\rho_{X}
is any choice of 1-bounded, uniformly continuous, generating, pseudo-metric. This is the
measure
\Sigma
-entropy
of the action
G{\curvearrowright}(X,\mu)
. It may depend on the choice of sofic approximation

and it may take on the value
-\infty
(this occurs whenever
{\operatorname{Map}}(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}})
is empty for some choice of

and all sufficiently large

).

Remark 5
.

If
G
is a countable group then the definition of

reduces in a straightforward way to the standard definition of measure sofic entropy, as presented in
[

for example.

The first step of the proof is to show we can assume

is a metric without loss of generality.

For the rest of this section, we fix a uniformly continuous, 1-bounded metric

6.1
Entropy through measure-preserving microstates

The next step is to show we can restrict to maps
\phi:M_{i}\to X
which take the normalized volume
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
to
\mu

exactly
. This will be used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Definition 14
.

Let
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\textrm{mp}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
be the set of all
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
such that
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}=\mu
. Informally, elements of
{\operatorname{Map}}_{\textrm{mp}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)
are called
measure-preserving microstates
. We let

\displaystyle h^{\textrm{mp}}_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\mu,\rho\right)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{i}\right)}\log\operatorname{Sep}_{\epsilon}\left({\operatorname{Map}}_{\textrm{mp}}\left(M_{i},X,\rho:U,\delta\right)\right)

denote the corresponding entropy.

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose that for every
i
,
\mathrm{vol}_{M_{i}}
has no atoms. Then measure entropy can be computed using measure-preserving microstates. More precisely,

\displaystyle h_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\mu,\rho\right)=h^{\textnormal{mp}}_{\Sigma}\left(G,X,\mu,\rho\right).

To prove the nontrivial inequality, we show that if
\phi:M\to X
maps
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
into a given neighborhood of
\mu
, then there is another map
\psi:M\to X
, close to
\phi
, which maps
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
to
\mu
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
(M,\mathrm{vol})
is a nonatomic finite measure space and
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{O}}
of
\mu
in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)
such that for any map
\phi:M\to X
with
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\in{\mathcal{O}}
there exists
\psi:M\to X
such that
\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)<\epsilon
and
\psi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}=\mu
.

Proof.

Recall that if
Y\subset X
then
\partial Y=\overline{Y}\cap\overline{X\setminus Y}
is the
boundary
of
Y
. Moreover,
Y
is a
continuity set
if
\mu(\partial Y)=0
. It is a standard fact that continuity sets form a dense algebra of the measure algebra. In other words, the collection of continuity sets is closed under finite intersections and unions, and for every
\delta>0
and Borel set
Y\subset X
there exists a continuity set
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
such that
\mu(Y\vartriangle Y^{\prime})<\delta
where
\vartriangle
denotes symmetric difference.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots\}
be a partition of
X
into continuity sets such that each
P_{i}
has diameter
<\epsilon/10
and positive measure. Let
n
be large enough so that
\mu(\cup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i})>1-\epsilon/10
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be the set of all
\mu^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Prob}(X)
such that

|\mu^{\prime}(P_{i})-\mu(P_{i})|<\frac{\epsilon}{10}\mu(P_{i})

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. By the portmanteau Theorem,
{\mathcal{O}}
is an open neighborhood of
\mu
.

Let
\phi:M\to X
be a map such that
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\in{\mathcal{O}}
. It now suffices to construct a map
\psi
with
\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)<\epsilon
and
\psi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}=\mu
.

Let
Q_{i}=\phi^{-1}(P_{i})
. Let
Q^{\prime}_{i}\subset Q_{i}
be a subset with

\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q^{\prime}_{i})=\min(\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q_{i}),\mu(P_{i})).

Let
Q^{\prime}_{0}=M\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{n}Q^{\prime}_{i}
.

Next, choose a map
\psi:M\to X
satisfying

1.

\psi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}=\mu
,

2.

for all
p\in Q^{\prime}_{i}
,
\psi(p)\in P_{i}
.

To see that such a map exists, define a measure
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}
on
Q^{\prime}_{i}
by
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}(E)=\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(E\cap Q^{\prime}_{i})
. Because
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q^{\prime}_{i})\leq\mu(P_{i})
and
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}
is non-atomic, there exists a map
\psi_{i}:Q^{\prime}_{i}\to P_{i}
such that
\psi_{i*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}
is absolutely continuous to
\mu
and

\frac{d\psi_{i*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}}{d\mu}(x)\leq 1

for all
x\in P_{i}
.

Because
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
is non-atomic, there is also a map
\psi_{0}:Q^{\prime}_{0}\to X
such that the pushforward measure
\psi_{0*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
is absolutely continuous to
\mu
and the Radon-Nikodym derivative satisfies

\frac{d\psi_{0*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}}{d\mu}(x)=1-\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{d\psi_{i*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}_{i}}{d\mu}(x)

for all
x\in X
. We can define
\psi:M\to X
by setting
\psi(p)=\psi_{i}(p)
for all
p\in Q^{\prime}_{i}
and all
i=0,1,\ldots,n
.

If
p\in Q^{\prime}_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
then both
\psi(p)
and
\phi(p)
are in
P_{i}
which has diameter
<\epsilon/10
and therefore
\rho(\psi(p),\phi(p))<\epsilon/10
. On the other hand,
\rho
is 1-bounded. Combining these facts we estimate:

\displaystyle\rho^{M}(\psi,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{0}^{\prime}}\rho(\psi(p),\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{Q_{i}^{\prime}}\rho(\psi(p),\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q_{0}^{\prime})+\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M\setminus Q_{0}^{\prime})\epsilon/10\leq\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q_{0}^{\prime})+\epsilon/10.

On the other hand, since
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\in{\mathcal{O}}
, we have
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q_{i})=\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(P_{i})\geq(1-\epsilon/10)\mu(P_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. So
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q^{\prime}_{i})\geq(1-\epsilon/10)\mu(P_{i})
. Adding this up results in

\overline{\operatorname{vol}}\left(\cup_{i=1}^{n}Q^{\prime}_{i}\right)\geq(1-\epsilon/10)\mu\left(\cup_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\right)\geq(1-\epsilon/10)^{2}>1-2\epsilon/10.

So
\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(Q^{\prime}_{0})\leq 2\epsilon/10
. Combined with the previous estimate, this gives

\rho^{M}(\psi,\phi)\leq 3\epsilon/10<\epsilon
which completes the proof.

∎

The next lemma shows that a map
\psi
obtained by perturbing a good map
\phi
stays good, although with a slightly larger error depending only on the size of the perturbation and the quality of the sofic approximation.

Lemma 6.5
.

For every pre-compact open neighborhood
U
of the identity, there exists a function
f_{U}:(0,\infty)^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying:

1.

if
M
is a
(U,\kappa)
-sofic approximation to
G
,
\phi\in{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X,\rho:U,\delta)
,
\psi\in{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X)
and
\rho^{M}(\phi,\psi)<\epsilon
then
\psi\in{\operatorname{Map}}(M,X,\rho:U,\delta+f_{U}(\kappa,\epsilon))
;

2.

\lim_{\kappa,\epsilon\searrow 0}f_{U}(\kappa,\epsilon)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
M,\phi,\psi,U,\kappa,\epsilon
are as in the statement. Let
N=\{p\in M:~\rho(\psi(p),\phi(p))<\sqrt{\epsilon}\}
. Then
\mathrm{vol}(N)\geq(1-\sqrt{\epsilon})\mathrm{vol}(M)
by Markov’s inequality. So for any
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\rho^{M}(g\circ\psi,g\circ\phi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M\setminus N)+\int_{N}\rho(g\psi(p),g\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\epsilon}+\textrm{m.o.c.}[g:\sqrt{\epsilon}]

where the modulus of continuity is defined by

\textrm{m.o.c.}[g:\sqrt{\epsilon}]=\sup_{x,y\in X}\big\{\rho(gx,gy):~\rho(x,y)\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}\big\}.

Fix
g\in U
. Then
g.p
is well-defined for
p\in M[U]
. Thus.

\displaystyle\rho^{M}(\psi\circ g,\phi\circ g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(M\setminus M[U])+\int_{M[U]}\rho(\psi(g.p),\phi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa+\rho^{M}(\psi,\phi)\leq\kappa+\epsilon.

So

\displaystyle\rho^{M}(g\circ\psi,\psi\circ g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho^{M}(g\circ\psi,g\circ\phi)+\rho^{M}(g\circ\phi,\phi\circ g)+\rho^{M}(\phi\circ g,\psi\circ g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\epsilon}+\textrm{m.o.c.}[g:\sqrt{\epsilon}]+\delta+\kappa+\epsilon\leq f_{U}(\kappa,\epsilon)+\delta

where
f_{U}(\kappa,\epsilon)=\sqrt{\epsilon}+\epsilon+\kappa+\sup_{g\in U}\textrm{m.o.c.}[g:\sqrt{\epsilon}]
. This satisfies
\lim_{\kappa,\epsilon\searrow 0}f_{U}(\kappa,\epsilon)=0
because
U
is pre-compact and the action is uniformly continuous.
∎

7
Variational Principle

The purpose of this section is to prove that topological sofic entropy is the sup over measure sofic entropies. To be precise, given an action
G{\curvearrowright}X
, let
\operatorname{Prob}_{G}(X)\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)
denote the space of
G
-invariant Borel probability measures.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a compact metric space and
G{\curvearrowright}X
a jointly continuous action. Then for any sofic approximation
\Sigma
to
G
,

h_{\Sigma}(G,X)=\sup_{\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}_{G}(X)}h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu).

In particular, if there are no
G
-invariant probability measures on
X
then
h_{\Sigma}(G,X)=-\infty
.

For future applications, it is nice to have a slightly more general version. If
A\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)
is any set, then define the entropy

h_{\Sigma}(G,X,A)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\inf_{{\mathcal{O}}\supset A}\inf_{U\subset G}\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}(M_{n})^{-1}\log\operatorname{Cov}_{\epsilon}({\operatorname{Map}}(M,X,\rho:U,\delta,{\mathcal{O}}))

where
{\mathcal{O}}
varies over all open sets containing
A
and
U
varies over all pre-compact open subsets
U
of
G
.

This definition is the same as the definition of measure entropy, with the exception that the open set
{\mathcal{O}}
is required to contain
A
instead of
\mu
. In particular, if
A=\{\mu\}
then
h_{\Sigma}(G,X,A)=h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu)
. Therefore, the next theorem immediately implies Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a compact metric space and
G{\curvearrowright}X
a jointly continuous action. Then for any sofic approximation
\Sigma
to
G
and closed subset
A\subset\operatorname{Prob}(X)

h_{\Sigma}(G,X,A)=\sup_{\mu\in A\cap\operatorname{Prob}_{G}(X)}h_{\Sigma}(G,X,\mu).

The first step in the proof is showing that if
\phi:M\to X
is a good enough topological model for the action
G{\curvearrowright}X
then
\phi_{*}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}
is almost
G
-invariant.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
G{\curvearrowright}X
be as above. Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an open neighborhood of
\operatorname{Prob}_{G}(X)
in
\operatorname{Prob}(X)

and

Without loss of generality, we assume

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M[g]}f(\phi(g.p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)-\int_{M}f(g\phi(p))~\mathrm{d}\overline{\operatorname{vol}}(p)\right|

(b)

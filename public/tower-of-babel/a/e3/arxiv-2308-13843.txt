Shape dimension of maps
Pavel S. Gevorgyan, I. Pop

Abstract
. In this paper we define a numerical shape invariant of a continuous map called shape dimension of a map, which generalizes the shape dimension of a topological space. Some basic properties and applications of this invariant are given. The question of raising the shape dimension by shape finite-dimensional maps is solved. An example of a shape finite-dimensional surjective map between shape infinite-dimensional spaces is given.

Introduction

This paper continues the study of shape invariant properties of maps initiated by the authors in
[

called shape dimension of a map and denoted by

We denote the shape category of topological spaces by
\text{Sh}_{\text{(HTop,\,HPol)}}
, where HTop is the homotopy category of spaces and HPol is the homotopy category of polyhedra. Recall that the objects of HTop are all topological spaces and the morphisms are the homotopy classes

Recall that for a simplicial complex

\dim K\leq n
means that every simplex of

has dimension
\leq n

of a space

\mathcal{U}

. If

is a polyhedron

if and only if
\dim K\leq n
. The covering dimension

has a number of interesting properties but obviously it is not a homotopy invariant. This inconvenience has been solved for compact metric spaces by K. Borsuk
[
1
,
2
]
under the name of

. This notion was then studied in depth by S. Nowak
[
7
]
. Finally for arbitrary topological spaces a convenient notion of dimension was given by J. Dydak
[
3
]
as follows.

In the shape theory based on inverse systems (see
[
6
]
, Chapter II, §1) a system
\mathbf{X}=(X_{\lambda},p_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}},\Lambda)\in
pro-HPol is said to be of dimension

\mathbf{X}\in

is homotopy dominated by a polyhedron

\dim P\leq n
. Then a space

has

\sd X\leq n
provided

admits an HPol

-expansion
\mathbf{p}:X\rightarrow\mathbf{X}
, i.e., an H-Pol-expansion with

In the second section of this paper we define the dimension of a morphism of inverse systems and of a pro-morphism in the category HPol, which generalizes the dimension of an inverse system in HPol (Definition
1
), and then the shape dimension of a continuous map is defined (Definition

It is proved that a topological space

has the shape dimension
\leq n
(Theorem

If the topological space

and

are shape

When

is infinite.
Example

shows that there are shape finite-dimensional surjective maps between shape infinite-dimensional spaces. This means that the shape dimension of a map

in general does not depend on the shape dimensions of spaces

and
Y

In the third section it is proved that the notion of the shape dimension of map can defined by means of level morphisms of inverse systems in the category HPol (Theorem

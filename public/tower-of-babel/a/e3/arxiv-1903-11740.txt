Maxima and minima of homogeneous Gaussian random fields over continuous time and uniform grids
Yingyin Lu Zuoxiang Peng

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, for centered homogeneous Gaussian random fields the joint limiting distributions of normalized maxima and minima over continuous time and uniform grids are investigated. It is shown that maxima and minima are asymptotic dependent for strongly dependent homogeneous Gaussian random field with the choice of sparse grid, Pickands’ grid or dense grid, while for the weakly dependent Gaussian random field maxima and minima are asymptotically independent.

Keywords.
Maximum and minimum; joint limit distribution; continuous time; uniform grid; homogeneous Gaussian random field.

AMS 2000 Subject Classification.
Primary 60G70; Secondary 60G15.

Introduction

Let
\{X(t),t\geq 0\}
be a stationary Gaussian process with mean zero, variance one and the correlation function
r(t)
which satisfies for some
\alpha\in(0,2]
,

\displaystyle r(t)=1-|t|^{\alpha}+o\left(|t|^{\alpha}\right),\;\ t\to 0.

(1.1)

Assume that

\displaystyle r(t)\log t\to 0\;\ \mbox{as}\;\ t\to\infty.

(1.2)

Under conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), the limit distribution theory on the maximum of
\{X(t),t\geq 0\}
up to time
T
,

M_{T}=\max\{X(t),t\in[0,T]\}

is well developed that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbf{P}}\{a_{T}\left(M_{T}-b_{T}\right)\leq x\}\to\Lambda(x)

as
T\to\infty
, where
\Lambda(x)=\exp\left(-e^{-x}\right)
is the standard Gumbel distribution and

\displaystyle a_{T}=\sqrt{2\log T},\;\ b_{T}=\sqrt{2\log T}+\frac{\log\Big[(2\pi)^{-1/2}H_{\alpha}(2\log T)^{-1/2+1/\alpha}\Big]}{\sqrt{2\log T}}.

Here
H_{\alpha}
is the well-known Pickands’ constant, which is defined by
H_{\alpha}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}H_{\alpha}(\lambda)/\lambda
with

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\operatorname*{\mathbf{E}}\exp\left(\max_{t\in[0,\lambda]}\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha/2}(t)-t^{\alpha}\right)

(1.3)

and
B_{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion with
\operatorname*{\mathbf{E}}B_{H}^{2}(t)=|t|^{2H}
. It is well known that
0<H_{\alpha}<\infty
, see e.g. Leadbetter et al. (1983), Pickands (1969) and Piterbarg (1996). The limit distribution theorem about
M_{T}
extended by Mittal and Ylvisaker (1975) and McCormick and Qi (2000), and Dȩbicki et al. (2013) extended the results to homogeneous Gaussian random fields.

It is known that extreme value theory of Gaussian random fields may be applied to image analysis, quantum chaos, queuing theory, insurance mathematics, number theory and so on; see e.g. Adler (2000), Adler et al. (2014), and Hashorva and Ji (2016). Further, numerical simulation of trajectories of high extremes of continuous random processes may be performed through the discrete time random processes depending heavily on the sampling frequency, see, for instance Leadbetter et al. (1983), Piterbarg (2004) and recent work of Song et al. (2018, 2019) on flaw detection by using ultrasonic response signals.

The joint limiting distributions of
M_{T}
and its discrete time maximum
M_{T}^{\delta}=\max\{X(t),t\in[0,T]\cap\Re(\delta)\}
was first studied by Piterbarg (2004) under the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), where the uniform grid is given by
\Re=\Re(\delta)=\{k\delta,\;\ k=0,1,2\ldots\}
with
\delta\left(2\log T\right)^{1/\alpha}\to D\in[0,\infty]
as
T\to\infty.
For more details, see Piterbarg (2004). The results on multivariate stationary Gaussian processes can be found in Tan and Hashorva (2014, 2015), Tan and Wang (2013) and Tan and Tang (2014) for strongly dependent Gaussian processes. Further, Turkman (2012) considered the non-Gaussian processes and Chen and Tan (2016) studied the asymptotic behavior of
M_{T}
,
M_{T}^{\delta}
and the partial sum of dependent Gaussian processes.

For the joint asymptotic behaviors of
M_{T}
and
M_{T}^{\delta}
of homogeneous Gaussian random field, Tan and Wang (2015) considered the following model. Let
\{X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}),\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\geq\mathbf{0}\}
be a homogeneous Gaussian field with mean zero, variance one and covariance function
r(\mathbf{t})=\operatorname*{\mathbf{Cov}}(X(\mathbf{t}),X(\mathbf{0}))
satisfies the following conditions, for
d\geq 2
:

\mathbf{A1}:r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})=1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}|t_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}(1+o(1))
, as
t_{i}\to 0
, with
\alpha_{i}\in(0,2]
;

\mathbf{A2}:r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})<1
, for
\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\neq\mathbf{0}
;

\mathbf{A3}:\lim_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}\to\infty}r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}})\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i})=r\in[0,\infty)
, where
\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}\to\infty
means
T_{i}\to\infty,i=1,2,\cdots,d
. If
T_{i}=0
,
r(T_{1},\cdots,T_{i-1},0,T_{i+1},\cdots,T_{d})\log(\prod_{j\neq i}^{d}T_{j})
is bounded.

Define

\mathbf{M}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\left(M_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}},M_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\right)=\left(\max_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\in\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}}X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}),\max_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\in\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\bigcap\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Re(\delta_{i})}X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})\right),

where
\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[0,T_{i}]
, and
\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\bigcap\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Re(\delta_{i})
means
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\{[0,T_{i}]\bigcap\Re(\delta_{i})\}
, and the uniform grid

\Re(\delta_{i})=\{k\delta_{i},k\in N\}
is given by

\delta_{i}\left(2\log\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i}\right)^{1/\alpha_{i}}\to D_{i},\;\ i=1,2,\cdots,d.

We say that the grid is dense if all
D_{i}=0
and if all
D_{i}=\infty
, the grid is sparse. The grid is called a Pickands grid if all
D_{i}\in(0,\infty)
. Under conditions
\mathbf{A1}-\mathbf{A3}
, Tan and Wang (2015) derived the limiting distribution of
\operatorname*{\mathbf{M}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
when the uniform grid is sparse grid, Pickands’ grid and dense grid, respectively.

In this paper, our focus is on the joint limit distributions of maxima and minima of aforementioned homogeneous Gaussian random fields. Davis (1979) established the joint limiting distribution of maxima and minima of weakly dependent stationary sequences, and weakly dependent stationary Gaussian processes was studied by Berman (1971). For the asymptotic distributions of maxima and minima on bivariate Hüsler-Reiss models, see Liao and Peng (2015) and Lu and Peng (2017).

Similarly to the definition of maxima
\operatorname*{\mathbf{M}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
, define the minima
\operatorname*{\mathbf{m}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
as follows:

\mathbf{m}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\left(m_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}},m_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\right)=\left(\min_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\in\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}}X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}),\min_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\in\operatorname*{\mathbf{I}}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\bigcap\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Re(\delta_{i})}X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})\right)

and let

\displaystyle\begin{cases}b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}=a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}+a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{-1}\log\left((2\pi)^{-1/2}(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{i}^{-1})(a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}})^{-1}\right)\\
b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}+a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{-1}\log\left((2\pi)^{-1/2}(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}})(a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}})^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{2}{\alpha_{i}}-1}\right)\\
b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}+a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{-1}\log\left((2\pi)^{-1/2}(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{\alpha_{i}})(a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}})^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{2}{\alpha_{i}}-1}\right),\end{cases}

where
a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\sqrt{2\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i})}
and
H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}}=\lim_{\lambda_{i}\to\infty}\frac{H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}}(\lambda_{i})}{\lambda_{i}}\in(0,\infty)

with

H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}}(\lambda_{i})=\operatorname*{\mathbf{E}}\exp\left(\max_{k_{i}a_{i}\in[0,\lambda_{i}]}\sqrt{2}B_{\alpha_{i}/2}^{(i)}(k_{i}a_{i})-(k_{i}a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\right),\;i=1,2,\cdots,d,

where
B_{\alpha_{1}/2}^{(1)}(\cdot),\cdots,B_{\alpha_{d}/2}^{(d)}(\cdot)
are independent fractional Brownian motions, cf. Piterbarg (2004) and Tan and Wang (2015). Further, let the bivariate normalizing constants
\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
be given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\left(u_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}(x_{2}),u_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(y_{2})\right)=\left(b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}+\frac{x_{2}}{a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}},b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{*}+\frac{y_{2}}{a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}}\right)\\
\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}=\left(v_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}(x_{1}),v_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(y_{1})\right)=\left(-b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}+\frac{x_{1}}{a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}},-b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{*}+\frac{y_{1}}{a_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}}\right),\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{*}=b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
for sparse grids,
b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{*}=b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
for Pickands grids and
b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}^{*}=b_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
for dense grids.

Throughout this paper, let
\phi(x)
and
\Phi(x)
denote respectively the density function and distribution function of a standard normal random variable, and
\Psi(x)=1-\Phi(x)
, and operations of vectors mean componentwise operating. For example, for vectors
\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}=(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{d})
and
\operatorname*{\mathbf{s}}=(s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{d})
, operations of
\operatorname*{\mathbf{s}}\leq\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}
,
\operatorname*{\mathbf{s}}-\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}
,
\operatorname*{\mathbf{s}}\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}
, and
\operatorname*{\mathbf{s}}^{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}}
mean
s_{i}\leq t_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,d
,
(s_{1}-t_{1},s_{2}-t_{2},\cdots,s_{d}-t_{d})
,
(s_{1}t_{1},s_{2}t_{2},\cdots,s_{d}t_{d})
, and
(s_{1}^{t_{1}},s_{2}^{t_{2}},\cdots,s_{d}^{t_{d}})
, respectively. Let
C
be positive constant with values varying from place to place.

2
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
be a centered homogenous Gaussian field with unit variance and covariance function
r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
satisfying
\mathbf{A1}-\mathbf{A3}
. Then for any sparse grids
\Re(\delta_{i})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbf{P}}\left(\mathbf{M}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}},\mathbf{m}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big[1-\exp\left(-e^{x_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)-\exp\left(-e^{y_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)+\exp\left(-e^{x_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}-e^{y_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big\{-\left(e^{-x_{2}-r+\sqrt{2r}z}+e^{-y_{2}-r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)\Big\}d\Phi(z),

where
\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
,
\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
are given by (
1.4
).

Define,

\displaystyle H_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{x,y}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{s}\operatorname*{\mathbf{P}}\Big(\max_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}}\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}[0,\lambda_{i}]}\sqrt{2}\chi(\operatorname*{\mathbf{k}}\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}})>s+x,\max_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}}\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}[0,\lambda_{i}]}\sqrt{2}\chi(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})>s+y\Big)ds,

where
\chi(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{\alpha_{i}/2}^{(i)}(t_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}|t_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
be a centered homogenous Gaussian field with unit variance and covariance function
r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
satisfying
\mathbf{A1}-\mathbf{A3}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
,
\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
be given by (
1.4
). Then for any Pickands grids
\Re(a_{i}(2\log\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i})^{-1/\alpha_{i}})
with
a_{i}>0
,
i=1,2,\cdots,d
, the following limit exists,

H_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{x,y}=\lim_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\to\infty}H_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{x,y}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})/(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i})\in(0,\infty)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbf{P}}\left(\mathbf{M}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}},\mathbf{m}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big[1-\exp\Big(-e^{x_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\Big)-\exp\Big(-e^{y_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\Big)+\exp\Big(-e^{x_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}-e^{y_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}

\displaystyle-H_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{-x_{1}+\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{\alpha_{i}}),-y_{1}+\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}})}e^{r+\sqrt{2r}z}\Big)\Big]\times\exp\Big\{-\Big(e^{-x_{2}-r+\sqrt{2r}z}+e^{-y_{2}-r+\sqrt{2r}z}

\displaystyle-H_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{a}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{x_{2}+\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{\alpha_{i}}),y_{2}+\log(\prod_{i=1}^{d}H_{a_{i},\alpha_{i}})}e^{-r+\sqrt{2r}z}\Big)\Big\}d\Phi(z).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
be a centered homogenous Gaussian field with unit variance and covariance function
r(\operatorname*{\mathbf{t}})
satisfying
\mathbf{A1}-\mathbf{A3}
. Then for any dense grids
\Re(\delta_{i})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,d
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbf{P}}\left(\mathbf{M}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}},\mathbf{m}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\leq\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big[1-\exp\left(-e^{x_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)-\exp\left(-e^{y_{1}+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)+\exp\left(-e^{\max(x_{1},y_{1})+r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)\Big]

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big\{-\left(e^{-\min(x_{2},y_{2})-r+\sqrt{2r}z}\right)\Big\}d\Phi(z),

where
\mathbf{u}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
,
\mathbf{v}_{\operatorname*{\mathbf{T}}}
are given by (
1.4

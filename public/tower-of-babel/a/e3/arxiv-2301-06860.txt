Error estimates for completely discrete FEM in energy-type and weaker norms
Lutz Angermann, Peter Knabner, Andreas Rupp

Abstract
The paper presents error estimates within a unified abstract framework for the analysis of FEM for boundary value problems with linear diffusion-convection-reaction equations and boundary conditions of mixed type. Since neither conformity nor consistency properties are assumed, the method is called completely discrete. We investigate two different stabilized discretizations and obtain stability and optimal error estimates in energy-type norms and, by generalizing the Aubin-Nitsche technique, optimal error estimates in weaker norms.



Keywords:
Strang lemma, consistency, error estimate, Aubin-Nitsche technique,
discontinuous Galerkin method

AMS Subject Classification (2022):

65 N 12, 65 N 30, 46 B 10

Introduction

In this paper we present a unified approach to the analysis of FEM
for boundary value problems with linear elliptic differential equations
of the second order, where, in addition to the diffusion-convection-reaction structure
of the partial differential equation, mixed type boundary conditions
(first, second, third kind) are taken into account.
We allow completely discrete formulations in the sense that the discrete FE spaces
are not necessarily embedded into the spaces of the weak formulation of the boundary
value problem and – based on a suitable notion of consistency – that the
discrete problems do not have to be consistent.
In addition to stability and error estimates in energy-type norms,
a generalization of the Aubin-Nitsche technique
for obtaining error estimates in weaker norms is discussed.

Selected aspects of our exposition are not entirely new or even not really profound;
this applies, for example, to our version of Strang’s second lemma
[
Str72
]
,
which is circulating in the literature in several, slightly different versions.
There are also other variants of the generalization of the duality argument;
we refer here – also for an overview – to
[
DPD18
]
.
The abstract results obtained are applied to two concrete discretizations
– a Crouzeix-Raviart discretization of order one and more general
discontinuous Galerkin methods of the IPG type.
For both cases we discuss the stability, consistency and convergence properties
that result from the general theory.

As for the theoretical aspects, the work already mentioned
[
DPD18
]

and its extension
[
DPD21
]
are perhaps the most closely related publications
to our work.
Compared to these, we prefer not to include an interpolation (or quasi-interpolation) operator
in the consistency definition,
but rather require that the discrete bilinear form can be extended in such a manner
that the solution of the continuous problem
(which often has more regularity than the elements of spaces in which
the weak formulation of the boundary value problem is given), belongs to the extended domain of
definition (as in
[
DPE12
, Def. 1.31]
).
Furthermore, the extended paper
[
DPD21
]
applies its theory
only to a pure diffusion problem under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The paper is structured as follows.
In the subsequent section we present, in an abstract framework, error estimates
for the solution of discretized variational equations in both energy-type norms and weaker norms,
whereby neither conformity nor consistency are assumed.
Then, in Section
3
, we describe the model problem, the solution of which is to be approximated,
and the most important prerequisites. The model problem is a boundary value problem for
a scalar diffusion-convection-reaction equation with boundary conditions of mixed type.
Sections
4
and
5
describe the specific discretizations,
including their stabilization mechanisms,
and the theoretical results from Section
2
are applied.
In both situations and under reasonable assumptions, optimal error estimates are obtained.

2
General variational equations

Given two real Banach spaces
(U,\|\cdot\|_{U})
,
(V,\|\cdot\|_{V})
,
this paper is concerned with the application of finite element methods
to approximate the solution to the following problem:

\text{Find }u\in U\text{ such that}\quad a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in V,

(1)

where throughout the paper
\ell:\;V\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous linear form, and

a:\;U\times V\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous bilinear form.

In the above setting, the following result about the existence of a unique solution is known.

Theorem 1
.

Let
V
be reflexive.
Then the problem
(
1
) is uniquely solvable
for every right-hand side
\ell\in V^{\prime}

if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\alpha:=\inf_{{u\in U\setminus\{0\}}}\sup_{{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}}\frac{a(u,v)}{\|u\|_{U}\|v\|_{V}}>0,

(2)

\displaystyle a(u,v)=0\quad\text{for all }u\in U\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=0\quad\text{for }v\in V.

(3)

If both conditions are met,
the solution
u\in U
of (
1
)
satisfies the stability estimate

\|u\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\ell\|_{V^{\prime}}.

Proof.

First we note that the variational equation (
1
)
can be reformulated as an operator equation:

Au=\ell,

where
A:\;U\to V^{\prime}
is defined by means of the relationship

(Au)(v):=a(u,v)
for all
u\in U
,
v\in V
.
The continuity of
a
implies the continuity of
A
.

The assertion follows from the following chain of arguments,
but we omit their detailed proofs.

1)

Let
U,V
be normed spaces only. Then:

\eqref{eq:vfg1}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A^{-1}:\;{\operatorname{im}}{(A)}\to U\text{ exists and is continuous}.

2)

If, in addition to the assumption in 1),
U
is complete, i. e. a Banach space,
then
\overline{{\operatorname{im}}{(A)}}={\operatorname{im}}{(A)}
.

3)

Let, in addition to the assumptions in 2),
V
be a reflexive Banach space.
Then:

\eqref{eq:vfg2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{\operatorname{im}}{(A)}=V^{\prime}.

∎

To discretize the problem (
1
) formally we consider
real Banach spaces
(U_{h},\|\cdot\|_{U_{h}})
,

(V_{h},\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}})

of the same finite dimension,
a bilinear form
a_{h}:\;U_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and
a linear form
\ell_{h}:\;V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Here the index
h
is a positive parameter
(typically an element of a sequence of positive real numbers with accumulation point 0)
such that the dimension of
U_{h}
and
V_{h}
increases unbounded as
h
approaches zero.
The corresponding discrete problem reads as follows:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in U_{h}\text{ such that }\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(4)

We call the discretization (
4
)
conforming
,
if
U_{h}\subset U
as well as
V_{h}\subset V
, otherwise
nonconforming
.
In the conforming case, we set

\|\cdot\|_{U_{h}}:=\|\cdot\|_{U}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}:=\|\cdot\|_{V}

unless differently specified.

In the analysis of the nonconforming case, the
augmented spaces

U(h):=U+U_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad V(h):=V+V_{h}

will be useful.
This implicitly assumes the existence of linear superspaces
for
U,U_{h}
and
V,V_{h}
, respectively.
Furthermore we assume that the spaces
U(h)
,
V(h)
are equipped
with norms
\|\cdot\|_{U(h)}
,
\|\cdot\|_{V(h)}
, respectively.
It is often desirable to take advantage of additional knowledge about the solution
of the problem (
1
), e. g. certain regularity properties.
In such a case it is natural to introduce a proper subspace, say
W\subset U
,
as the solution space, which may have a stronger topology.

Definition 2
.

Let
u\in W\subset U
be the solution of the problem (
1
).
The discrete formulation (
4
) is called
consistent

on the subspace
W\subset U
,
if the discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
can be extended onto the product space
(W+U_{h})\times V_{h}

(keeping the notation
a_{h}
) such that

a_{h}(u,v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}

holds.
Otherwise the discrete formulation (
4
) is called
nonconsistent
.

If the discrete formulation (
4
) is consistent,
a solution
u_{h}\in U_{h}
has the following property of
Galerkin orthogonality
:

a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(5)

This property is lost in the nonconsistent situation,
since additional terms appear on the right-hand side, which are generally nontrivial.

An extension of the standard convergence analysis for the consistent conforming case
is given by the following generalization of Strang’s second lemma.
Extensions of this kind can be found in the literature
(e. g.
[
DPE12
]
,
[
CDGH17
]
)
but in fact all of these results (including our subsequent Theorem
3
)
are not very deep and rather technical in nature, but allow the analysis
of more general finite element (and related) methods.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u\in W\subset U
be the solution of the problem (
1
).
Assume that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{U_{h}}
can be extended to a norm on
W+U_{h}

(keeping the notation
\|\cdot\|_{U_{h}}
),
and the condition

\alpha_{h}:=\inf_{u_{h}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})}{\|u_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}}>0

(6)

is satisfied.

Further assume that the discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
can be continuously extended onto
the product space
\left(W+U_{h},\|\cdot\|_{U(h)}\right)\times\left(V_{h},\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}\right)

(keeping the notation
a_{h}
),
i. e., there exists a constant
\widetilde{M}_{h}\geq 0
such that

|a_{h}(w,v_{h})|\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}\|w\|_{U(h)}\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}\quad\text{for all }w\in W+U_{h},\ v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(7)

Then the estimate

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{U_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{w_{h}\in U_{h}}\left(\frac{\widetilde{M}_{h}}{\alpha_{h}}\|u-w_{h}\|_{U(h)}+\|u-w_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha_{h}}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|}{\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}}

holds.

Proof.

From (
6
), (
7
) and

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h}-w_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})-a_{h}(u,v_{h})+a_{h}(u-w_{h},v_{h})\quad\text{for any }w_{h}\in U_{h}

it follows immediately that

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\leq\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|}{\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}}+\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-w_{h}\|_{U(h)}.

The triangle inequality

\|u-u_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\leq\|u-w_{h}\|_{U_{h}}+\|w_{h}-u_{h}\|_{U_{h}}

yields the result.
∎

Remark 4
.

1)

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
be satisfied.
If there is a constant
\widetilde{C}_{h}>0
such that

\|w_{h}\|_{U(h)}\leq\widetilde{C}_{h}\|w_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\quad\text{for all }w_{h}\in U_{h},

we can also apply the triangle inequality w.r.t. the
U(h)
-norm and conclude

\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\leq\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{h}\widetilde{M}_{h}}{\alpha_{h}}\right)\inf_{w_{h}\in U_{h}}\|u-w_{h}\|_{U(h)}+\frac{\widetilde{C}_{h}}{\alpha_{h}}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|}{\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}}\,.

2)

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
be satisfied.
If there is a constant
\widetilde{C}_{h}>0
such that

\|w_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{h}\|w_{h}\|_{U(h)}\quad\text{for all }w\in W+U_{h},

then the estimate

\|u-u_{h}\|_{U_{h}}\leq\left(\widetilde{C}_{h}+\frac{\widetilde{M}_{h}}{\alpha_{h}}\right)\inf_{w_{h}\in U_{h}}\|u-w_{h}\|_{U(h)}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{h}}\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|}{\|v_{h}\|_{V_{h}}}

holds.

Error estimates in weaker norms

In the standard finite element literature for second-order linear elliptic
boundary value problems, the so-called
Aubin-Nitsche duality argument
[
Aub67
]
,
[
Nit68
]

is used to establish error estimates in norms which are weaker than
the natural energy norm (or equivalent norms), typically in the
L^{2}
-norm.
The main ingredient is an auxiliary variational problem of the form

Find
v\in V
such that

a(w,v)=\tilde{\ell}(w)\quad\text{for all }w\in U,

(8)

where
\tilde{\ell}\in U^{\prime}
is a suitably chosen continuous linear form.

Here we extend this setting to a more general framework,
in which the occurring discrete spaces no longer have to be subspaces
of the “continuous” spaces
U,V
of the weak formulation (
1
),
and the discretization does not necessarily have to be consistent.
This abstract framework is applied to two examples of nonconforming FEM for
diffusion-convection-reaction equations in Sections
4
and
5
.
However, the range of models and numerical techniques covered by the analytical framework
goes well beyond these examples.

We basically assume here that there exists a reflexive Banach space
Z
such that

U+U_{h}\subset Z^{\prime}
and denote by
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\cdot,\cdot\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}:\;Z^{\prime}\times Z^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}

the duality pairing on
Z^{\prime}\times Z^{\prime\prime}
.
Thanks to the reflexivity of
Z
we may identify the bidual space
Z^{\prime\prime}
with
Z
:
Z^{\prime\prime}\cong Z
.

Next we specify the right-hand side of the adjoint variational problem (
8
) as

\tilde{\ell}(w):=\tilde{\ell}_{g}(w):=\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}w,\,g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\quad\text{for all }w\in U,

where
g\in Z
is arbitrary.
That is, the particular problem

Find
v\in V
such that

a(w,v)=\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}w,\,g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\quad\text{for all }w\in U

(9)

is considered below.
Regarding the solvability of the problem (
9
)
we assume that there exists not only a unique solution
v_{g}\in V
, but that
it belongs to some proper subspace
Y\subset V
(similar to the original (“primal”) problem).
We further assume that the discrete variational formulation

Find
v_{gh}\in V_{h}
such that

a_{h}(w_{h},v_{gh})=\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}w_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\quad\text{for all }w_{h}\in U_{h}

(10)

possesses a unique solution.

The following result, on which perhaps the most interesting
part of the analysis of the concrete examples in
Sections 4 and 5 is based, has already been published in the book
[
KA21
, Lemma 6.11]
.
Nevertheless, we present the proof here in a revised, shortened version,
since it is referred to in the discussion that follows the proof
and especially in the analysis of the two examples.

Theorem 5
.

Let
u\in W\subset U
be the solution of the problem (
1
),

u_{h}\in U_{h}
the solution of the discrete problem (
4
),

v_{g}\in Y\subset V
the solution of the adjoint problem (
9
),
and
v_{gh}\in V_{h}
the solution of the discrete adjoint problem (
10
).
Assume that the bilinear form
a_{h}
can be continuously extended onto the product space
(W+U_{h})\times(Y+V_{h})

(keeping the notation
a_{h}
), i. e., there exists a constant
\widetilde{M}_{h}\geq 0
such that the estimate

|a_{h}(w,z)|\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}\|w\|_{U(h)}\|z\|_{V(h)}\quad\text{for all }w\in W+U_{h},\ z\in Y+V_{h}

(11)

holds.
Finally assume that the linear form
\ell_{h}
can be extended onto
Y+V_{h}

(keeping the notation
\ell_{h}
, too).
Then:

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g\in Z\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|g\|_{Z}}\Big\{\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}-\left[a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g})-\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\right]

(12)

\displaystyle-\left[a_{h}(u,v_{g}-v_{gh})-\ell_{h}(v_{g}-v_{gh})\right]+\left[(a_{h}-a)(u,v_{g})-(\ell_{h}-\ell)(v_{g})\right]\Big\}.

Proof.

Thanks to the relationship

\|u-u_{h}\|_{Z^{\prime}}=\sup_{g\in Z\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}}{\|g\|_{Z}}

it is sufficient to estimate the numerator term. It can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}

\displaystyle=a(u,v_{g})-a_{h}(u_{h},v_{gh})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u,v_{g})-a_{h}(u_{h},v_{gh})+(a-a_{h})(u,v_{g})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g}-v_{gh})+a_{h}(u_{h},v_{g}-v_{gh})+a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{gh})+(a-a_{h})(u,v_{g})

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

For the first term we have the estimate (
11
) by assumption:

|I_{1}|\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}.

The term
I_{3}
can be split as follows:

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-a_{h}(u,v_{g}-v_{gh})+a_{h}(u,v_{g})-a_{h}(u_{h},v_{gh})

\displaystyle=-[a_{h}(u,v_{g}-v_{gh})-a(u,v_{g})+\ell_{h}(v_{gh})]+(a_{h}-a)(u,v_{g})

\displaystyle=-[a_{h}(u,v_{g}-v_{gh})-\ell_{h}(v_{g}-v_{gh})]+(a_{h}-a)(u,v_{g})-(\ell_{h}-\ell)(v_{g}).

Finally it is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle I_{2}+I_{4}

\displaystyle=-a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g})-a_{h}(u_{h},v_{gh})+a(u,v_{g})

\displaystyle=-a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g})-\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}+\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u,g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}

\displaystyle=-[a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g})-\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}].

Putting all the above relationships together, we obtain the statement.
∎

Discussion

The success of the presented approach clearly depends on the possibility of obtaining
suitable estimates of the four individual terms in the bound in (
12
).
In particular, all addends standing in the braces have to be estimated in such a way
that they contain the term
\|g\|_{Z}
as a factor.

Concerning the first term, we can write, for
g\in Z\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\frac{\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}}{\|g\|_{Z}}\|g\|_{Z}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}\tilde{\eta}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|g\|_{Z}

with

\tilde{\eta}:=\tilde{\eta}(A^{\prime},A_{h}^{\prime},V_{h},Z):=\sup_{g\in Z\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(A^{\prime})^{-1}g-(A_{h}^{\prime})^{-1}g\|_{V(h)}}{\|g\|_{Z}},

where

(A^{\prime})^{-1}:\;Z\to Y
and
(A_{h}^{\prime})^{-1}:\;Z\to V_{h}
are the solution operators
of the problems (
9
) and (
10
), respectively.

The quantity
\tilde{\eta}
can be usefully further estimated if, for example,

(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a Banach space (in fact it was sufficient if
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
were a seminorm)
and the following two conditions are met:

•

Stable regularity of the solution of the adjoint problem:
The solution
v_{g}
of (
9
)
even belongs to the space
Y
and satisfies the stability estimate

\|v_{g}\|_{Y}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{Z}\quad\text{for all }g\in Z,

(15)

where
C_{s}\geq 0
is a constant independent of
g
.

•

Convergence of the solution of the discrete adjoint problem:
There exist constants
C_{a}\geq 0
,
q>0
such that the error of
the discrete solution
v_{gh}\in V_{h}
of (
10
)
satisfies the estimate

\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}\leq C_{a}h^{q}\|v_{g}\|_{Y}.

(16)

Indeed, if both conditions are satisfied, we have that

\|(A^{\prime})^{-1}g-(A_{h}^{\prime})^{-1}g\|_{V(h)}=\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}\leq C_{a}h^{q}\|v_{g}\|_{Y}\leq C_{s}C_{a}h^{q}\|g\|_{Z},

hence

\tilde{\eta}\leq C_{s}C_{a}h^{q}.

So if the order of convergence of the discrete solution
u_{h}\in U_{h}

of the original discrete problem (
4
) is
p>0
, that is

\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\leq C_{p}h^{p}\|u\|_{W}

(17)

with some constant
C_{p}\geq 0
, then we get the estimate

\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{gh}\|_{V(h)}\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}C_{s}C_{a}C_{p}h^{p+q}\|u\|_{W}\|g\|_{Z}.

(18)

The second addend in the bound in (
12
) can be interpreted as a consistency
error of the discrete adjoint problem at the test function
u-u_{h}
.
If it were possible to obtain a consistency error estimate of the form

|a_{h}(w,v_{g})-\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}w,g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}|\leq C_{ca}h^{\alpha}\|w\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}\|_{Y},

(19)

where
C_{ca}\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
are certain constants, this,
together with the regularity condition (
15
)
and the estimate (
17
), would lead to the relationship

|a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{g})-\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}|\leq C_{ca}C_{s}h^{\alpha}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|g\|_{Z}\leq C_{ca}C_{p}C_{s}h^{p+\alpha}\|u\|_{W}\|g\|_{Z}.

(20)

The third addend in the bound in (
12
) is a consistency
error of the discrete original problem at the test function
v_{g}-v_{gh}
.
If an estimate of the type

|a_{h}(u,z)-\ell_{h}(z)|\leq C_{cp}h^{\beta}\|z\|_{V(h)}\|u\|_{W}

with certain constants
C_{cp}\geq 0
,
\beta>0
is assumed, then,
similar to the above discussion of the first addend, it follows that

|a_{h}(u,v_{g}-v_{gh})-\ell_{h}(v_{g}-v_{gh})|\leq C_{cp}h^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}\|u\|_{W}\|g\|_{Z}\leq C_{s}C_{a}C_{cp}h^{q+\beta}\|u\|_{W}\|g\|_{Z}.

(21)

The fourth addend represents approximation errors.
If it were possible to obtain an estimate of the form

|(a_{h}-a)(u,v_{g})-(\ell_{h}-\ell)(v_{g})|\leq C_{q}h^{\gamma}\|u\|_{W}\|v_{g}\|_{Y}

with constants
C_{q}\geq 0
,
\gamma>0
,
then it would follow, together with the regularity condition (
15
),
that

|(a_{h}-a)(u,v_{g})-(\ell_{h}-\ell)(v_{g})|\leq C_{q}C_{s}h^{\gamma}\|u\|_{W}\|g\|_{Z}.

(22)

Putting the estimates (
18
)–(
22
) together,
the estimate (
12
) reads as

\|u-u_{h}\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq Ch^{r}\|u\|_{W}

with

C:=\widetilde{M}_{h}C_{s}C_{a}C_{p}+C_{ca}C_{p}C_{s}+C_{s}C_{a}C_{cp}+C_{q}C_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad r:=\min\{p+q,\,p+\alpha,\,q+\beta,\,\gamma\}.

In the frequently encountered case
U=V\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and
U_{h}=V_{h}
consisting
of conforming
\mathcal{P}_{k}
-elements, a natural choice for the space
Z
is

Z:=L^{2}(\Omega).

Provided the data of the boundary value problem are sufficiently smooth,
the relationships (
15
), (
16
) can be satisfied
by choosing
Y:=H^{2}(\Omega)
and
q=1
.

In other, less standard situations,
Z:=H^{k-1}(\Omega)
can also be taken.
This may allow to choose
Y:=H^{k+1}(\Omega)
and also
q=k
.
Then, provided that the consistency errors behave appropriately,
the the optimal case of order doubling in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1-k}
can be reached.

The importance of such negative norm estimates consists in the possibility
of deriving error estimates for some functionals
\varphi\in U(h)^{\prime}
.
Indeed, assume that such a functional
\varphi
is represented via a smooth function,
i. e., it even holds that

\varphi\in Z\quad(\cong Z^{\prime\prime}).

Then

\varphi(u-u_{h})\leq\|\varphi\|_{Z}\|u-u_{h}\|_{Z^{\prime}}\,.

Remark 6
.

1)

If the discrete formulation (
4
) is consistent, the first addend
in the bound in (
12
) can be replaced by

\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{V(h)}

with arbitrary
v_{h}\in V_{h}

thanks to the Galerkin orthogonality (
5
).
Then we have the estimate

\widetilde{M}_{h}\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|v_{g}-v_{h}\|_{V(h)}\leq\widetilde{M}_{h}\eta\|u-u_{h}\|_{U(h)}\|g\|_{Z}

with

\eta:=\eta(A^{\prime},V_{h},Z):=\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\sup_{g\in Z\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(A^{\prime})^{-1}g-v_{h}\|_{V(h)}}{\|g\|_{Z}}\quad(\leq\tilde{\eta}).

The quantity
\eta
was introduced in
[
Sau06
]
in connection with
stability and convergence investigations of conforming and consistent Galerkin discretizations
of the Helmholtz equation for large wavenumbers.

2)

In case of conforming discretizations and if
U=V
are Hilbert spaces,
an alternative estimate can be derived.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
5
we have
(using the notation of the proof):

\displaystyle\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}u-u_{h},g\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}

\displaystyle=a(u-u_{h},v_{g})=a(u-u_{h},v_{g}-\Pi_{h}v_{g})+\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g})-a(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g}),

where
\Pi_{h}:\;V_{h}\to V
is the orthogonal projector.
Then

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq\sup_{g\in Z\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|g\|_{Z}}\Big\{\widetilde{M}\|u-u_{h}\|_{V}\|v_{g}-\Pi_{h}v_{g}\|_{V}-[a(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g})-\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g})]\Big\}.

Since
V
is reflexive as a Hilbert space, we can set
Z:=V
.
Introducing
\ell_{h}:=a_{h}(u_{h},\cdot)
,
the second term can be treated as follows:

\displaystyle a(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g})-\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g})

\displaystyle=(a-a_{h})(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g})+\ell_{h}(\Pi_{h}v_{g})-\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g}).

Thanks to the the symmetry of
\Pi_{h}
it holds that

\ell_{h}(\Pi_{h}v_{g})-\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g})=\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\ell_{h}-\ell,\Pi_{h}v_{g}\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}\ell_{h}-\Pi_{h}\ell,v_{g}\mathopen{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}.

Hence we arrive at the estimate

|a(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g})-\ell(\Pi_{h}v_{g})|\leq(a-a_{h})(u_{h},\Pi_{h}v_{g})+\|v_{g}\|_{V}\|\ell_{h}-\Pi_{h}\ell\|_{V}.

3
The model problem

In this and the subsequent section we will apply the theoretical results to
finite element discretizations of the following diffusion-convection-reaction problem.
Given a bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
,
we consider the differential equation in divergence form

-\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}\,u)+r\,u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega

(23)

with the data

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{K}^{\top}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d,d},\quad\boldsymbol{c}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{d},\quad\partial_{j}c_{j}\in L^{3/2}(\Omega),\ j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},

\displaystyle r\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\quad f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where, for some constant
k_{0}>0
,

\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}(x)\boldsymbol{\xi})\geq k_{0}|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and almost all }x\in\Omega.

(24)

To formulate the boundary conditions we assume that the boundary
{\partial\Omega}

is decomposed into disjoint subsets

\Gamma_{j},

j\in\{1,2,3\}
:

{\partial\Omega}=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}\cup\Gamma_{3}\,,

where
\Gamma_{3}
is supposed to be a relatively closed subset of
{\partial\Omega}
.
Given the functions

g_{j}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{j})
,
j\in\{1,2\}
, and

\tilde{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{2})
,
the boundary conditions are
(the symbol
{\mathfrak{n}}
denotes the outer unit normal on
{\partial\Omega}
):

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}\,u)\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}

\displaystyle=g_{1}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{1},

(25)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}\,u)\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}+\tilde{\alpha}u

\displaystyle=g_{2}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{2},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{3},

i. e., we restrict our investigations to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The variational formulation (
1
) is specified
as follows:

\displaystyle U:=V

\displaystyle:=\big\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\;|\;v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{3}\text{ in the sense of traces}\big\},

\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle:=(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u,\nabla v)+(ru,v)+(\tilde{\alpha}u,v)_{\Gamma_{2}}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in V,

\displaystyle\ell(v)

\displaystyle:=(f,v)+(g_{1},v)_{\Gamma_{1}}+(g_{2},v)_{\Gamma_{2}}\quad\text{for all }v\in V.

Since we will also have to deal with the adjoint boundary value problem,
we formulate the additional requirements to the data in a form that slightly differs
from the usual one:

\displaystyle\text{1) }

\displaystyle r+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\Omega\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\text{2) }

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\geq 0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{2,1}:=\{x\in\Gamma_{2}\,|\,\tilde{\alpha}(x)=\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\}\,,\hfill\rule{99.02747pt}{0.0pt}

\displaystyle\text{3) }

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\geq 0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{2}\setminus\Gamma_{2,1}\,,\quad\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\leq 0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{1}\,.

These assumptions together with (
24
) ensure that the bilinear form
a
is
at least positively semidefinite on
V
, as the following identity shows:

\displaystyle a(v,v)

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v,\nabla v)+(r+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c},v^{2})

\displaystyle-(\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c},v^{2})_{\Gamma_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c},v^{2})_{\Gamma_{2,1}}+(\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c},v^{2})_{\Gamma_{2}\setminus\Gamma_{2,1}}\quad\text{for all }v\in V.

Remark 7
.

The above choice of boundary conditions (
25
)
together with the requirements (
26
),
especially the sign condition to
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}
on
\Gamma_{1}
in (
26
),3),
does not include the possibility to prescribe boundary data for

(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}\,u)\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}
at an outflow boundary.

At first glance this seems physically questionable, but that there are arguments
underlying this fact in two extreme situations, namely the diffusion-dominated
and the convection-dominated regimes.
First we note that the boundary term on
\Gamma_{1}
in the above identity can be controlled
thanks to the boundedness of the trace operator
V\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{1})

[
BS08
, Thm. 1.6.6]
.
That is, if
k_{0}
in (
24
) is sufficiently large
in comparison with the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}
on
\Gamma_{1}

(“diffusion-dominated regime near
\Gamma_{1}
”),
the positive definiteness of the bilinear form
a
on
V
can be preserved
even if
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}>0
on
\Gamma_{1}
.
In the contrary case, if the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{K}
on
\Omega
is
very small in comparison with the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}
on
\Gamma_{1}

(“convection-dominated regime near
\Gamma_{1}
”),
the problem is almost elliptically degenerate, and in such a case it
is not appropriate to prescribe boundary data at an outflow (“noncharacteristic”) boundary.
In practice, a so-called do-nothing boundary condition, which is more or less artificial,
is often prescribed at an outflow boundary in order to avoid boundary layer effects.
Therefore, in the convection-dominated case, outflow boundary conditions
can and have to be be modeled via
\Gamma_{2}
.

In the next step, to formulate the needed regularity conditions to
the problem (
23
)–(
25
),
and to describe the discretization, we introduce a family
(\mathcal{T}_{h})_{h}
of consistent partitions
of the domain
\Omega
.
Given an
admissible partition

\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\Omega

(in the sense of
[
Cia02
, (FEM 1)]
, where we omit the subscript
h

for simplicity of notation),
it is called
consistent
, if the following additional properties are met:

•

Every face
F
of an element
K\in\mathcal{T}
is either a subset of the boundary
{\partial\Omega}
of
\Omega

or identical to a face of another element
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
.

•

Each of the boundary subsets
\Gamma_{j}

is interrelated with a set of faces
\mathcal{F}_{j}
in the following way:

\text{cl}_{\text{\,rel}}\big(\Gamma_{j}\big)=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}F,\quad j\in\{1,2,3\},

where
\text{cl}_{\text{\,rel}}
denotes the closure of a boundary subset
in the relative topology of
{\partial\Omega}
.

To simplify the notation in the further analysis, we denote the set of all faces
of all elements of
\mathcal{T}
by
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
,
the set of those faces that are lying on the boundary
{\partial\Omega}

by
\partial\mathcal{F}
, and the set of all faces of an element
K\in\mathcal{T}
by
\mathcal{F}_{K}
.
Hence
\mathcal{F}:=\overline{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\partial\mathcal{F}
is the set
of all interior faces.

Furthermore we introduce
jumps
and
averages
of piecewise defined functions
as follows.
Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{K^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset

be an interior face in the partition
\mathcal{T}
,

K
,
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
,
K\neq K^{\prime}
.
By
\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}
we denote the outer unit normal on the boundary
\partial K

of an element
K\in\mathcal{T}
.
In case of scalar functions
v:\;\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}

such that
v|_{K}\in H^{1}(K)
,
v|_{K^{\prime}}\in H^{1}(K^{\prime})
,
we define

\displaystyle{\llbracket v\rrbracket}

\displaystyle:={\llbracket v\rrbracket}_{F}

\displaystyle\,:=

\displaystyle v|_{K}\,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}+v|_{K^{\prime}}\,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K^{\prime}}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}

\displaystyle:=\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}_{F}

\displaystyle\,:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(v|_{K}+v|_{K^{\prime}})\,,

where here and in the subsequent relationships (
28
)–(
30
)
the terms on right-hand sides are to be understood in the sense of traces on the face
\mathcal{F}
.
In case of vector fields
\boldsymbol{p}:\;\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}\in H(\operatorname{div};K)
and
\boldsymbol{p}|_{K^{\prime}}\in H(\operatorname{div};K^{\prime})
,
we set

\displaystyle{\llbracket\boldsymbol{p}\rrbracket}

\displaystyle:={\llbracket\boldsymbol{p}\rrbracket}_{F}

\displaystyle\,:=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}+\boldsymbol{p}|_{K^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K^{\prime}}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{p}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}

\displaystyle:=\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{p}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}_{F}

\displaystyle\,:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}+\boldsymbol{p}|_{K^{\prime}})\,.

If
F
is a boundary face, i. e.,
F\in\partial\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{F}_{K}
for some
K\in\mathcal{T}
,
we define

{\llbracket v\rrbracket}_{F}:=v|_{K}\,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K},\quad\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}_{F}:=v|_{K},\quad{\llbracket\boldsymbol{p}\rrbracket}_{F}:=\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K},\quad\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{p}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}_{F}:=\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}.

(29)

The definitions (
27
)–(
29
) are designed in such a way that
the averages retain the function type, while jumps of scalar functions are vector fields and vice versa.

A very useful relation between jumps and averages on interior faces is the so-called

magic formula
:

\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}{\llbracket\boldsymbol{p}\rrbracket}+{\llbracket v\rrbracket}\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{p}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}=v|_{K}\,\boldsymbol{p}|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}+v|_{K^{\prime}}\,\boldsymbol{p}|_{K^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K^{\prime}}.

(30)

Finally, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\geq 1
, we define the
broken Sobolev space

W^{k,q}(\mathcal{T})

on a partition
\mathcal{T}
of the domain
\Omega
by

W^{k,q}(\mathcal{T}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;|\;v|_{K}\in W^{k,q}(K)\quad\text{for all }K\in\mathcal{T}\},

equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{k,q,\mathcal{T}}:=\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\|v\|_{k,q,K}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\quad\text{for }q\in[1,\infty)

or

\|v\|_{k,\infty,\mathcal{T}}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\|v\|_{k,\infty,K}

respectively.
As usual, by

|v|_{k,q,\mathcal{T}}:=\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}|v|_{k,q,K}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\quad\text{for }q\in[1,\infty)

or

|v|_{k,\infty,\mathcal{T}}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}|v|_{k,\infty,K},

resp., the corresponding seminorms are denoted.
In the case
q=2
we use the standard notations
H^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=W^{k,2}(\mathcal{T})
,

\|v\|_{k,\mathcal{T}}:=\|v\|_{k,2,\mathcal{T}}
,
|v|_{k,\mathcal{T}}:=|v|_{k,2,\mathcal{T}}
.

Regularity assumptions

Basically, we assume that the problem has a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
This can be guaranteed by making additional assumptions to (
26
),
see, e. g.,
[
KA21
, Thm. 3.16]
.
To analyze the consistency error, however, we need additional regularity assumptions
(which are also additional requirements to the data of
(
23
)–(

)):

\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u\in H(\operatorname{div};\Omega),\ cu\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(31)

Note that, as consequence of (

),

\displaystyle{\llbracket\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u\rrbracket}_{F}=0\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{F},

(32)

i. e., the normal components of the flux densities are continuous across the inner element boundaries.

4
Example I: The Crouzeix-Raviart discretization

In this section we consider
shape-regular
(i. e.,
regular

in the sense of
[
Cia02
, Sect. 3.1.1]
) families of consistent simplicial partitions of
\Omega
.
To specify of the approximation spaces we introduce the space

CR_{1}(\Omega):=\{v\in L^{1}(\Omega)\;|\;v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)\text{ and }\int_{F}{\llbracket v\rrbracket}\,d\sigma=\boldsymbol{0}\text{ for all }F\in\mathcal{F}\},

(33)

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)
denotes the set of polynomials of degree one on
K
.
This space is is also known as the global
Crouzeix-Raviart space
of degree one.

It should be noted that the an element
v\in V_{h}
is bi-valued on
\mathcal{F}
in general.
With that we define

U_{h}:=V_{h}:=\Big\{v\in CR_{1}(\Omega)\;|\;\int_{F}v\,d\sigma=0\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}\Big\}.

(34)

Now, to formulate a (nonconsistent) discretization of
(
23
)–(
25
),
we introduce the forms

\begin{split}a_{h}(w,v)&:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla w-\boldsymbol{c}w,\nabla v)_{K}+(rw,v)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}+(\tilde{\alpha}w,v)_{\Gamma_{2}},\\
\ell_{h}(v)&:=\ell(v)\qquad\text{for all }w,v\in U(h):=V(h):=H^{1}(\mathcal{T}),\end{split}

(35)

where the
upwind evaluation

\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w)
of the term
\boldsymbol{c}w

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w):=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{c}w|_{K}&\text{for }\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}>0,\\
\boldsymbol{c}w|_{K^{\prime}}&\text{for }\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}\leq 0.\end{cases}

(36)

At the boundary faces
F\in\partial\mathcal{F}
of a simplex

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w):=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{c}w|_{K}&\text{for }\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}_{K}>0,\\

(37)

Then the discrete method reads as follows:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}\,.

(38)

At first we study the coercivity of the bilinear form
a_{h}
.
So let
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T})
. Starting from

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v,\nabla v)_{K}+(rv,v)+(\tilde{\alpha}v,v)_{\Gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{c}v,\nabla v)_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(v),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F},

we treat the fourth term as follows :

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{c}v,\nabla v)_{K}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{c},\nabla v^{2})_{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}((\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c})v,v)_{K}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\partial\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{c}v,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v)_{F}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{c},{\llbracket v^{2}\rrbracket})_{F}.

This gives

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v,\nabla v)_{K}+\Big(\Big(r+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\Big)v,v\Big)+(\tilde{\alpha}v,v)_{\Gamma_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\partial\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{c}v,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v)_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\Big[(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(v),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{c},{\llbracket v^{2}\rrbracket})_{F}\Big]+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(v),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}k_{0}\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\boldsymbol{c}v,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v)_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}\Big(\Big(\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\Big)v,v\Big)_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}\Big[(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(v),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{c},{\llbracket v^{2}\rrbracket})_{F}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}k_{0}\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(|\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}|,{\llbracket v\rrbracket}^{2})_{F}\geq k_{0}|v|_{1,\mathcal{T}}^{2},

where we have used the properties (
26
),
(
36
), and (
37
).

If we now include additional conditions
(which are similar to the conditions mentioned at the beginning of the subsection about
the regularity assumptions, but a little more stringent),
we obtain the
(V+V_{h})
-coercivity of

uniform in

.
Namely, we assume that, in addition to the conditions (
26
),
one of the following conditions is satisfied:

a)

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{3}|_{d-1}>0.

(39)

b)

\displaystyle\text{There exists some constant }r_{0}>0\text{ such that }r+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c}\geq r_{0}\text{ on }\Omega.

(40)

Indeed, the case b) immediately implies the estimate

a_{h}(v,v)\geq k_{0}|v|_{1,\mathcal{T}}^{2}+r_{0}\|v\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\geq\min\{k_{0};r_{0}\}\|v\|_{1,\mathcal{T}}^{2}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T})
.

To verify the case a) for
v\in V
we make use of
the Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality
[
BS08
, Exercise 5.x.13]
,
i. e., there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{PF}}>0
such that

\|v\|_{0}\leq C_{\mathrm{PF}}|v|_{1}=C_{\mathrm{PF}}|v|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{for all }v\in V.

If
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, we make use of a discrete version of this inequality
which can be proven analogously to the proof of
[
BS08
, Thm. (10.6.12)]
, i. e.,

\|v_{h}\|_{0}\leq\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{PF}}|v_{h}|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}

with some constant
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{PF}}>0
independent of
h
.
Since
\|v+v_{h}\|_{V+V(h)}:=\inf\{\|v\|_{0,\Omega}+\|v_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\}
is a norm
of
v+v_{h}
considered as an element of the subspace
V+V_{h}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
,
the combination of the above estimates shows that
|v|_{1,\mathcal{T}}
itself is already
a norm on
V+V_{h}
.
Hence
a_{h}(v,v)\geq k_{0}|v|_{1,\mathcal{T}}^{2}
is the desired coercivity estimate,
which in turn implies that the inf-sup condition (
6
) holds with
\alpha_{h}=k_{0}
.

Next we investigate the consistency error. For
u\in V\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T})
,
satisfying the regularity condition (
31
)
(this defines the regularity space
W
), and
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, we have:

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})

(41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u,\nabla v_{h})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+(ru-f,v_{h})

\displaystyle-(g_{1},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{1}}+(\tilde{\alpha}u-g_{2},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{2}}.

By integration by parts, the diffusion term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u,\nabla v_{h})_{K}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u),v_{h})_{K}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\big[(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+({\llbracket\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}\big]+\sum_{F\in\partial\mathcal{F}}(\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u,v_{h})_{F},

where we have used (
30
).
A rearrangement of the last three terms, taking into consideration
the definition (
29
) of the boundary jumps and averages, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u,\nabla v_{h})_{K}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u),v_{h})_{K}

(42)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}}({\llbracket\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}.

The next terms to consider are the convection terms. Using integration by parts
in the terms over
K\in\mathcal{T}
together with (
30
), we get

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{c}u,\nabla v_{h})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

(43)

\displaystyle=(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{c}u),v_{h})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\big[(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{c}u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+({\llbracket\boldsymbol{c}u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}\big]-\sum_{F\in\partial{F}}(\boldsymbol{c}u,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v_{h})_{F}

\displaystyle=(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{c}u),v_{h})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u)-\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{c}u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}({\llbracket\boldsymbol{c}u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{c}u,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v_{h})_{F}.

Since
{\llbracket u\rrbracket}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
\mathcal{F}
thanks to
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
see
[
DPE12
, Lemma 1.23]
,
we have

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u)-\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{c}u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u)-\boldsymbol{c}u,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v_{h})_{F}\,.

The latter term vanishes because of
u=0
on
\Gamma_{3}
.
Inserting (
42
), (
43
) into (
41
),
we arrive at

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u),v_{h})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}({\llbracket\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}}({\llbracket\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u\rrbracket},\{\hskip-2.35pt|{v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\})_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{c}u),v_{h})-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{c}u,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v_{h})_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+(ru-f,v_{h})-(g_{1},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{1}}+(\tilde{\alpha}u-g_{2},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle=(-\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u)+ru-f,v_{h})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u,\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}v_{h})_{F}-(g_{1},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{1}}+(\tilde{\alpha}u-g_{2},v_{h})_{\Gamma_{2}},

where we have used (
32
).
The first term and the sum of the last three terms vanish since the differential equation
(
23
) and the boundary conditions (
25
)
on
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
are satisfied as equations in
L^{2}(\Omega)

and
L^{2}(\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2})
, respectively.
Thus we get

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\Big(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}-\frac{1}{2}\big[\Pi_{K}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u)+\Pi_{K^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u)\big],{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\Big)_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}\Big(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\Pi_{K}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u),v_{h}\Big)_{F},

where

\Pi_{K}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u):=\frac{1}{|K|}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u,1)_{K}\quad\text{for all }K\supset F.

Thanks to the weak continuity condition in the definition (
33
) of
CR_{1}(\Omega)

and the weak homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition (cf. (
34
)),
the newly added terms do not change anything.
Therefore, by the Cauchy–Schwarz–Bunyakovsky inequality,

|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|\leq\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}h_{F}\big\|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\Pi_{K}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u)\big\|_{0,F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}h_{F}^{-1}\big\|{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\big\|_{0,F}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(44)

The multiplicative trace inequality
[
DF15
, Lemma 2.19]

\|v\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\leq C\big[\|v\|_{0,K}|v|_{1,K}+h_{K}^{-1}\|v\|_{0,K}^{2}\big]\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(K),\ K\in\mathcal{T},

a standard error estimate for
L^{2}
-projections (see, e. g.,
[
DF15
, Lemma 2.24]
),
and the relationship

C_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}h_{K}\leq h_{F}\leq C_{\mathcal{F}}h_{K}\quad\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{F}_{K},\ K\in\mathcal{T}

(45)

with a constant
C_{\mathcal{F}}>0
independent of
h_{K}

allow to obtain the upper bound

Ch|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}

for the first factor in (
44
).

The second factor in (
44
) can be treated as follows.
Denoting by
a_{S,F}
the barycentre of the face
F
, we observe that

\big\|{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\big\|_{0,F}=\big\|{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}-v_{h}(a_{S,F})\big\|_{0,F}.

Since both
\big(v_{h}-v_{h}(a_{S,F})\big)\big|_{K}
and
\big(v_{h}-v_{h}(a_{S,F})\big)\big|_{K^{\prime}}

vanish at the same point in
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{K^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
,
the scaled trace inequality (see, e. g.,
[
KA21
, Lemma 7.5]
)

\|v\|_{0,F}\leq Ch_{K}^{1/2}|v|_{1,K}\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}(K)

is applicable, leading together with (
45
)
to the following upper bound (up to a multiplicative constant) of the second factor:

\Big(\sum_{F=K\cap K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\big[h_{K}^{-1}h_{K}|v_{h}|_{1,K}^{2}+h_{K^{\prime}}^{-1}h_{K^{\prime}}|v_{h}|_{1,K}^{2}\big]+\sum_{F=K\cap\Gamma_{3}\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}h_{K}^{-1}h_{K}|v_{h}|_{1,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C|v_{h}|_{1,\mathcal{T}}.

Putting the obtained estimates together, we arrive at the following estimate
of the consistency error:

|a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})|\leq Ch|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}|v_{h}|_{1,\mathcal{T}}.

(46)

In order to be able to apply Theorem
3
,
the approximation order of
V_{h}
remains to be determined.
Since the space
\widetilde{V}_{h}
of conforming
\mathcal{P}_{1}
-elements is a subspace of
V_{h}
,
it follows, for a sufficiently smooth weak solution
u\in W\ (\subset H^{2}(\mathcal{T}))

of (
23
)–(
25
) that

\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\leq\inf_{v_{h}\in\widetilde{V}_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\leq Ch|u|_{2,\mathcal{T}}\,.

In summary, we have proved the following result.

Theorem 8
.

Let the family of triangulations be shape-regular,
the weak solution
u\in V\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T})
be such that (
31
) is satisfied,
and the diffusion coefficient
\boldsymbol{K}
be so smooth that
\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T})^{d}
.
Then, under the conditions (
26
), (
39
)
or (
26
), (
40
),
the following error estimate holds for the first-order Crouzeix-Raviart solution

u_{h}\in V_{h}\subset CR_{1}(\Omega)
of the discrete problem (
33
)–(
38
):

\|u-u_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\leq Ch\big[|u|_{2,\mathcal{T}}+|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\big],

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
h
.

The error bound can be simplified if additional smoothness of the diffusion coefficient
\boldsymbol{K}
is assumed.

Corollary 9
.

In addition to the assumptions of Theorem
8
,
let
\boldsymbol{K}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{T})
.
Then, for the solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}\subset CR_{1}(\Omega)

of the discrete problem (
33
)–(
38
), the error estimate

\|u-u_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\leq Ch|u|_{2,\mathcal{T}}

with a constant
C>0
independent of
h
holds.

So we have seen that the effect of including inter-element boundary terms in
the discrete formulation is to guarantee the coercivity.
They have no influence on the consistency error.

Convergence order in a weaker norm

In order to be able to apply Theorem
5
, the adjoint problem (
9
)
and its discretization (
10
) have to be investigated.
The adjoint problem (
9
) with
g\in Z:=L^{2}(\Omega)
is given by the forms

\displaystyle a^{\prime}(v,w)

\displaystyle:=a(w,v)=(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla w-\boldsymbol{c}w,\nabla v)+(rw,v)+(\tilde{\alpha}w,v)_{\Gamma_{2}}

(47)

\displaystyle=(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v,\nabla w)-(\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\nabla v,w)+(rv,w)+(\tilde{\alpha}v,w)_{\Gamma_{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{\ell}(w)

\displaystyle:=(w,g)\quad\text{for all }v,w\in V,

hence the adjoint problem corresponds to the following formal boundary value problem
in nondivergence form:

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v\right)-\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\nabla v+rv

\displaystyle=g\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(48)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{1}\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v\cdot\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}+\tilde{\alpha}v

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{2}\,,

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{3}\,.

Analogous to the continuous case, the discrete adjoint problem is defined
as the adjoint of the discrete problem (
35
):

a_{h}^{\prime}(v,w):=a_{h}(w,v):=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla w-\boldsymbol{c}w,\nabla v)_{K}+(rw,v)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}+(\tilde{\alpha}w,v)_{\Gamma_{2}}.

Obviously, the
V_{h}
-coercivity constant of
a_{h}^{\prime}
is the same
as that of
a_{h}
.

The consistency error

\displaystyle\quad a_{h}^{\prime}(v,w_{h})-\tilde{\ell}(w_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla w_{h}-\boldsymbol{c}w_{h},\nabla v)_{K}+(rw_{h},v)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w_{h}),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}+(\tilde{\alpha}w_{h},v)_{\Gamma_{2}}-(g,w_{h})

can be split into the consistency error of the symmetric part
(cf. (
42
) for the relevant diffusion term)
and the nonsymmetric part

-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\boldsymbol{c}w_{h},\nabla v)_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w_{h}),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}=-(\boldsymbol{c}w_{h},\nabla v)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w_{h}),{\llbracket v\rrbracket})_{F}.

The first term is part of the strong form (in the sense of
L^{2}(\Omega)
) of the differential equation,
whereas the second term vanishes. Therefore the consistency error estimation
is reduced to the estimation of the consistency error
of the symmetric part.
Thanks to symmetry, the estimate can be taken directly from (
46
)
in the proof of Theorem
8
.
Consequently, the error estimate of Theorem
8

also applies to the adjoint problem.

In order to apply Theorem
5
we have to assume that the adjoint problem
is regular in the sense that for any right-hand side
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)

a unique solution
v_{g}\in V\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T})
of the adjoint boundary value problem
(
9
) exists and
a stability estimate of the form

|v_{g}|_{2,\mathcal{T}}\leq\tilde{C}\|g\|_{0}\quad\text{for given }g\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(49)

is satisfied with some constant
\tilde{C}>0
(i. e., (
15
) holds with
Y:=V\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T})
).
Then the first term in the estimate of Theorem
5

can be bounded from above by
Ch^{2}|u|_{2,\mathcal{T}}
, and the fourth term vanishes.
The third term, a consistency error for the original problem,
can be estimated analogously to (
44
)
and the subsequent considerations, but with
v_{h}
substituted by
v_{g}-v_{gh}
,
i. e., the approximation error of the
adjoint problems (
9
), (
10
).

Thus the final estimate reads (compare (

) in the proof
of Theorem
8
):

Ch|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\,|v_{g}-v_{gh}|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\leq Ch^{2}|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\,|v_{g}|_{2,\mathcal{T}}\leq Ch^{2}|\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u|_{1,\mathcal{T}}\|g\|_{0,\Omega},

which is the required relationship.

It remains to discuss the second term, a consistency error of the adjoint problem.
It can be treated similarly to the third term (with interchanged roles

and
v_{g}-v_{gh}\leftrightarrow u-u_{h}
), resulting in the bound

But since we only have the estimate (

of the

-seminorm of

Under this assumption we can apply Corollary

to estimate

Theorem 10
.

Let the family of triangulations be shape-regular,

\boldsymbol{K}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{T})
,

), (

)
or (

), (

),

u_{h}\in V_{h}\subset CR_{1}(\Omega)

of the discrete problem (

)–(

)
satisfies the error estimate

where

5
Example II: Discontinuous Galerkin methods

Based on the setting of Section
3
,
here we consider more general consistent partitions of
\Omega

(not necessarily consisting of
d
-simplices only), namely

shape- and contact-regular
families of partitions, see
[
DPE12
, Def. 1.38]
.
We also assume that all partitions are
compatible
with
the structure of the boundary piece
\Gamma_{2}
in the sense that
there are subsets
\mathcal{F}_{2,1},\mathcal{F}_{2,2}\subset\mathcal{F}_{2}

such that the following representation holds:

\Gamma_{2,1}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2,1}}F,\quad\Gamma_{2}\setminus\Gamma_{2,1}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2,2}}F\setminus\Gamma_{2,1}.

This requirement is for clarity of presentation only. In principle, it can be omitted
if the correct integration regions, which then do not have to be complete faces,
are specified for the corresponding integrations.

We use the finite element spaces

U_{h}:=V_{h}:=\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}):=\{w_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;|\;w_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\quad\text{for all }K\in\mathcal{T}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K):=\mathcal{P}_{k}(K)
or
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K):=\mathcal{Q}_{k}(K)
.
Here
\mathcal{Q}_{k}(K)
denotes the set of tensor-product polynomials on
K
, which
is composed of
d
-variate polynomials of maximum partial degree
k
with respect to each variable.
We also allow inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
on
\Gamma_{3}
, i. e.,
g_{3}
may be a nontrivial function.
For a fixed
symmetrization parameter

\theta\in\{0,\pm 1\}

and a
penalty parameter

\mu>0
,
the interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method, in short
IPG
method, reads as follows:

Find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h},

(50)

where

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\big[(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u_{h}-\boldsymbol{c}u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{K}+(ru_{h},v_{h})_{K}\big]

(51)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}\Big[\theta(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}-\Big(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}-\frac{\mu}{h_{F}}{\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\Big)_{F}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2,1}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(u_{h}),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2,2}}(\tilde{\alpha}u_{h},v_{h})_{F},

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle:=(f,v_{h})+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}(g_{1},v_{h})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}(g_{2},v_{h})_{F}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}\Big[\frac{\mu}{h_{F}}(g_{3},v_{h})_{F}+(\theta\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v_{h}-\boldsymbol{c}v_{h},\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}g_{3})_{F}\Big].

The parameter value
\theta=0
gives the
incomplete IPG
(
IIPG
) method, while
the choice
\theta=-1
results in the
symmetric IPG
(
SIPG
).
The value
\theta=1
yields the
nonsymmetric IPG
(
NIPG
) method.
The artificial symmetrization term has no influence on the consistency properties
of the method (cf. the subsequent Lemma
11
),
nor does it generate an additional numerical flux on the interior element faces.
Based on an idea by Nitsche, the Dirichlet boundary conditions are weakly imposed.

Next we will show that the IPG methods can be characterized as consistent but nonconforming methods
(cf. Section
2
).
Analogously to Section
4
, we assume a sufficient regularity of the solution
of the continuous problem as in (
31
).

Lemma 11
.

If
u\in V\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T})
is the weak solution of the problem (
23
)–(
25
)
satisfying the regularity condition (
31
)
(this defines the regularity space
W
), then, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
the above IPG methods are consistent, i. e.,

a_{h}(u,v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Proof.

Using the property
{\llbracket u\rrbracket}_{F}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
F\in\mathcal{F}
,
it is not difficult to see that it holds, for an arbitrary test function
v_{h}\in V_{h}
:

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{h})-\ell_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\big[(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u-\boldsymbol{c}u,\nabla v_{h})_{K}+(ru,v_{h})_{K}\big]-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla u}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{3}}\Big[\theta(\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v_{h},\boldsymbol{{\mathfrak{n}}}u)_{F}+\Big(\frac{\mu}{h_{F}}u,v_{h}\Big)_{F}\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2,1}}(\boldsymbol{c}u,{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{2,2}}(\tilde{\alpha}u,v_{h})_{F}

The following lemma summarizes the hitherto obtained properties of the bilinear and linear forms.

Lemma 14
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{K}
is piecewise continuous, i. e., continuous on each
K\in\mathcal{T}
.
The bilinear form
a_{h}
and the linear form

are bounded on

with

independent of

\alpha=1

Proof.

The last statement follows immediately from the representation

, we proceed as follows.
The properties of
\boldsymbol{K}
, a discrete trace inequality
[
DPE12
, Lemma 1.46]

(here we need the shape- and contact-regularity of the family of partitions)
and Young’s inequality with

allow the estimation

\displaystyle|(\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\},{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}\nabla v_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}\|_{0,F}\|{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\|_{0,F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{K}\|_{\infty}^{1/2}C_{\mathrm{tr}}h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{K}^{1/2}\nabla v_{h}\|_{0,\Delta(F)}\|{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket}\|_{0,F}

Thus, the Lax-Milgram lemma ensures the existence of a unique solution to (

Convergence analysis for the complete problem

Assume that the weak solution

of the problem (

belongs to

In order to fulfill the assumptions of Remark

we first construct a space

such that

A suitable choice clearly is

However, on

we cannot apply a discrete trace inequality
to control

on the faces

(cf. the proof of Lemma

Therefore the norm

should be extended in such a way
that this term can also be controlled while retaining the uniform boundedness
of the (extended) bilinear form

on

A possible choice motivated by this is

\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{V(h)}:=\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{V_{h}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}}\frac{h_{F}}{\mu}\|\{\hskip-2.35pt|{\boldsymbol{K}^{1/2}\nabla w_{h}}|\hskip-2.29996pt\}\|^{2}_{0,F}+\sum_{F\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{h_{F}}{\mu}\|\boldsymbol{c}w_{h}\|^{2}_{0,F}\quad\text{for all }w_{h}\in V(h),

(55)

where the last term is to be understood in such a way that both one-sided traces
are taken into account (i. e., it is evaluated twice).

Lemma 16
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
13
be satisfied.
Then there exists a constant
\widetilde{M}_{h}>0
bounded in

such that

Proof.

The representation of the discrete bilinear form
a_{h}
in (
51
)
can obviously be split into eight sums.
Based on the assumptions, the first three sums,
the sixth sum, and the eighth sum can be estimated by means of the the Cauchy–Schwarz–Bunyakovsky
inequality (using only
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}
).
In order to estimate the fourth and the fifth sums, we first introduce the factors

(\mu/h_{F})^{1/2}(h_{F}/\mu)^{1/2}

and only then apply the Cauchy–Schwarz–Bunyakovsky inequality to control the terms
to the expense of the new terms in
\|\cdot\|_{V(h)}
.
For
w_{h}\in V_{h}
, the seventh sum can be estimated by means
of a discrete trace inequality as in the proof of Lemma
14
.
If
w_{h}\in V(h)
, the terms in the second sum are integrated by parts and combined with the seventh sum:

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}(\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{c}w_{h}),v_{h})_{K}-\sum_{F\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(\boldsymbol{c},{\llbracket w_{h}v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2,1}}(\boldsymbol{c}_{\mathrm{upw}}(w_{h}),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}.

Because of the product rule
\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{c}w_{h})=\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{c}w_{h}+\boldsymbol{c}\cdot\nabla w_{h}
,
the first sum can directly be controlled.
The other two terms either vanish at the boundary faces or can be controlled directly there.
At interior interfaces, we make use of the fact that both terms sum up
to the downwind flux
(\boldsymbol{c}_{\textup{down}}(w_{h}),{\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket})_{F}

(which is defined analogously to (
36
), (
37
)),
and conclude that these terms can be controlled by the third term in (
55
).
∎

To finish the discussion of convergence in the
\|\cdot\|_{V_{h}}
-norm,
we still need to demonstrate an estimate of the type

\|u-\Pi u\|_{V(h)}\leq Ch^{m}|u|_{m+1,\mathcal{T}}

A natural choice for

Under the assumption that the family of partitions is shape- and contact-regular,

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}\|{\llbracket v\rrbracket}\|^{2}_{0,F}

6
Conclusion

We presented a unified approach to the analysis of FEM for boundary value problems
with linear diffusion-convection-reaction equations and boundary conditions of mixed type,
where neither conformity nor consistency properties are assumed.
Being elementary in nature, it clarifies and quantifies the interplay between
stability, approximation errors, and consistency errors
– the theme guiding PDE numerical analysis from its very beginning.
As an example, we formulated and investigated two different stabilized discretizations
and obtained stability and optimal error estimates in energy-type norms and,
as a consequence of our generalization of the Aubin-Nitsche technique,
optimal error estimates in weaker norms.
We expect the described framework to provide guidelines to set up and analyze
further new stable and convergent schemes.

Phonon helicity induced by electronic Berry curvature in Dirac materials
Lun-Hui Hu, Jiabin Yu, Ion Garate, Chao-Xing Liu

Abstract
In two-dimensional insulators with time-reversal (TR) symmetry, a nonzero local Berry curvature of low-energy massive Dirac fermions can give rise to nontrivial spin and charge responses, even though the integral of the Berry curvature over all occupied states is zero. In this work, we present a new effect induced by the electronic Berry curvature. By studying electron-phonon interactions in BaMnSb 2 , a prototype two-dimensional Dirac material possessing two TR-related massive Dirac cones, we find that the nonzero local Berry curvature of electrons can induce a phonon angular momentum. The direction of this phonon angular momentum is locked to the phonon propagation direction, and thus we refer to it as “phonon helicity”, in a way that is reminiscent of electron helicity in spin-orbit-coupled electronic systems. We discuss possible experimental probes of such phonon helicity.



Introduction -

Berry phase and Berry curvature play a vital role in various branches of physics
Berry 1984
, owing to their deep connection to gauge field theories and differential geometry
Simon 1983
;
Xiao et al. 2010
.
In condensed matter systems with broken TR symmetry, the integration of the electronic Berry curvature over all the occupied states in the first Brillouin zone underpins a number of Hall-related phenomena, including the quantum Hall and quantum anomalous Hall effect
Klitzing et al. 1980
;
Thouless et al. 1982
;
Haldane 1988
;
Chang et al. 2013
, (intrinsic) anomalous Hall effect
Nagaosa et al. 2010
and orbital magnetic moments
Xiao et al. 2005
;
Xiao et al. 2006
.
Similarly, the Berry phase and curvature of nonelectronic quasiparticles such as phonons and magnons lead to a thermal Hall effect in insulating crystals with broken TR
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Takahashi and Nagaosa 2016
;
Park and Yang 2019
;
Lu et al. 2010
;
Sun et al. 2020
;
Mead and Truhlar 1979
.
In a TR invariant system, the integral of Berry curvature over all the occupied states vanishes.
Nevertheless, nonvanishing Berry curvature can still appear locally in the Brillouin zone, inducing a variety of electronic
phenomena, such as the spin
Sinova et al. 2015
and valley Hall effects
Xiao et al. 2007
;
Xiao et al. 2012
;
Mak et al. 2014
, nonlinear Hall effect
Sodemann and Fu 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2018a
;
Xu et al. 2018
;
Ma et al. 2019
;
Kang et al. 2019
, piezo-electromagnetic response
Martin 1972
;
Vanderbilt 2000
;
Vaezi et al. 2013
;
Droth et al. 2016
;
Rostami et al. 2018
;
Yu and Liu 2020
;
Yu et al. 2020

and quantized circular photo-galvanic effect in Weyl semimetals

de Juan et al. 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2018b
;
Moore 2019
;
Rees et al. 2020
.

In certain two-dimensional (2D) insulators with TR symmetry, the low-energy electronic excitations are 2D massive Dirac fermions.
In these systems, the gapped Dirac cones appear in pairs at two TR-related momenta (called two valleys below) with opposite local Berry curvatures due to TR symmetry.
Although the total Hall conductivity vanishes, as proposed in Refs. [
Yu and Liu 2020
] and [
Yu et al. 2020
], a nonzero Hall current can be induced by dynamical strain.
Specifically, the dynamical strain plays the role of an artificial gauge field, dubbed “pseudo-gauge field” in literature
Ilan et al. 2020
.
One may wonder whether there are more ways (other than dynamical strain) to induce nonvanishing charge and spin response
Sinova et al. 2015
for the local Berry curvature in TR invariant gapped systems.
Furthermore, one may ask whether the electronic local Berry curvature can induce nontrivial behaviors in quasiparticles other than electrons.

In this work, we study the phonon dynamics in a prototype of 2D Dirac material, BaMnSb
2
. By combining symmetry analysis and deformation potential theory, we find that the electron-phonon (e-ph) coupling in this material has the same form as the coupling between Dirac fermions and a
\mathrm{U}(1)
gauge field, meaning that phonons can also act as pseudo-gauge fields and thereby induce nonvanishing Hall currents
Yu and Liu 2020
;
Yu et al. 2020
.
The electronic local Berry curvature can induce an out-of-plane phonon angular momentum (PAM), whose sign reverses for opposite in-plane phonon momenta. We refer to it as ”phonon helicity”, because it resembles the helical spin texture of spin-orbit-coupled bands in the momentum space for electronic systems.
Specifically, the nonzero electronic local Berry curvature first introduces a self-energy correction to the phonon Green’s function, which is an odd function of the phonon momentum
q
, and then this self-energy correction makes the lattice vibration elliptical, eventually giving rise to nonzero PAM.
This Berry-curvature contribution to phonon dynamics can be probed through measuring the total phonon angular momentum with a temperature gradient or through optical measurements of spatial dispersion of dielectric function.

Model Hamiltonian–

We take BaMnSb
2
as a prototype model system.
BaMnSb
2
is a layered material
Liu et al. 2021
with alternating Ba-Sb layers and Mn-Sb layers stacked along the
(001)
direction. Density-functional theory (DFT) calculations in Refs. [
Farhan et al. 2014
] and [
Liu et al. 2021
] suggest that the electronic bands near the Fermi energy mainly come from the
p
-orbitals of the Sb atoms in the Ba-Sb layers, while the Mn-Sb layers serve as insulating barriers that prevent the tunneling of electrons along the
(001)
direction.
Consequently, the bands near the Fermi energy are almost non-dispersive along the
(001)
direction, and thus BaMnSb
2
is a quasi-2D material. The unit cell in each Ba-Sb layer contains two Sb atoms, labeled as Sb
1
and Sb
2
in Fig.
1
(a), both forming a square lattice. Distortion shifts Sb
2
atoms away from the center of the squares formed by Sb
1
atoms, resulting in a series of zig-zag chains.
Due to the zig-zag distortion, the point group of BaMnSb
2
is
C_{2v}
, spanned by a two-fold rotation along the
x
-axis (
C_{2x}
) and
z
-directional (
\sigma_{v}(xy)
) mirror planes. Magnetic moments in the Mn-Sb layers have little influence on the low-energy bands near the Fermi energy in the Ba-Sb layer, and thus the TR symmetry is taken into account.

We first describe the low-energy electronic properties. DFT calculations
Liu et al. 2021
indicate that the low-energy bands can be captured by two 2D massive Dirac cones at two momenta
K_{\pm}=(\pi/a,\pm k_{y_{0}})
, noted in Fig.
1

K_{\pm}
is schematically shown in Fig.
1

p_{x,y}
orbitals) and two spin states of Sb
1

The low-energy Hamiltonian is
\mathcal{H}_{s}=\int d^{2}k\Psi_{s,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}h_{s}(\mathbf{k})\Psi_{s,\mathbf{k}}
with

\displaystyle h_{s}(\mathbf{k})=sv_{0}(k_{y}\tau_{3}+k_{x}\tau_{1})+sm_{0}\tau_{2},

(1)

where

is the velocity of Dirac electrons and
m_{0}
is the Dirac mass tuned by the zig-zag distortion. Here

labels two valleys,
and at each valley, the basis is labeled by
\Psi^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{k})=(c_{s,p_{x},\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},c_{s,p_{y},\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})
, and
\tau_{i=0,1,2,3}

The spin-valley locking allows us to only consider electrons with one specific spin direction around each valley at low energies

Within this low-energy subspace,

Thus, we only consider

The Hamiltonian (

and

For the phonons, we focus on the in-plane vibrational modes, which are decoupled from the out-of-plane modes due to the opposite parities under
\sigma_{v}(xy)

C_{2v}
point group, as shown in table I of Sec. I.A in Supplemental Material (SM)

Specifically, the lattice vibrations along the

and

{\bf q}=0

C_{2x}
;
the vibration patterns of these four modes are schematically shown in Fig.
1

\vec{u}_{A_{1}}^{a},\vec{u}_{B_{1}}^{a},\vec{u}_{A_{1}}^{o},\vec{u}_{B_{1}}^{o}
, where
a/o
are for acoustic and optical phonon modes and
A_{1},B_{1}
are the corresponding irreducible representations (

The e-ph coupling takes the general form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}^{\eta}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\sum_{\lambda,s,\alpha,\beta}\mathbf{g}^{\eta,\lambda,s}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{q})Q_{\eta,\lambda,\mathbf{q}}c_{s,\alpha,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}c_{s,\beta,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}},

(2)

where
Q_{\eta,\lambda,\mathbf{q}}=(b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}+b^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda})/\sqrt{2M\omega_{\lambda}}
labels the phonon displacement operator for the phonon mode
|\eta,\lambda\rangle

(
\eta
for acoustic/optical and
\lambda
for
A_{1}
/
B_{1}
);

M
is the mass of Sb atoms;

b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}
is a bosonic operator that annihilates a phonon mode
\lambda
with momentum
\mathbf{q}
;

\alpha,\beta
represent
p
-orbitals and
\mathbf{g}^{\eta,\lambda,s}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{q})
is the element of the e-ph coupling vertex, which satisfies the Hermitian condition
\mathbf{g}^{\eta,\lambda,s}(\mathbf{q})=[\mathbf{g}^{\eta,\lambda,s}(-\mathbf{q})]^{\dagger}
.
The detailed form of
\mathbf{g}^{\eta,\lambda,s}
can be derived from the deformation theory combined with the
{\bf k\cdot p}
theory, as shown in Secs. II and III of SM
sm2
.
Here we only focus on long-wavelength optical phonons (dropping the index
\eta
below) and keep the spin-conserving intra-valley scattering process. Thus, we obtain

\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},s}=g_{0}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}\tau_{2}+g_{3}\tau_{3};\,\,\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},s}=g_{1}\tau_{1},

(3)

to the lowest order in
{\bf q}
, where
g_{i}
’s are real and material-dependent parameters

;

.
The e-ph coupling vertex matrices satisfy the relations
C_{2x}\mathcal{T}\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},s}\mathcal{T}^{-1}C_{2x}^{-1}=\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},s}
and
C_{2x}\mathcal{T}\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},s}\mathcal{T}^{-1}C_{2x}^{-1}=-\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},s}
.
Comparing Eq. (
3
) with Eq. (
1
) (see Sec. II.C of SM
sm2
),
one can see that

,
g_{1}
and
g_{3}
terms lead to a pseudo-gauge field

\displaystyle{A}_{pse,\mu}^{s}=(g_{0}Q_{A_{1}},s\tfrac{g_{1}}{v_{0}}Q_{B_{1}},s\tfrac{g_{3}}{v_{0}}Q_{A_{1}})_{\mu},

(4)

where

is assumed and
\mu=0,1,2
.
The

Taking into account both valleys, we note that
\tfrac{g_{1}}{v_{0}}Q_{B_{1}}
and
\tfrac{g_{3}}{v_{0}}Q_{A_{1}}
couple to electrons oppositely at two valleys, while the coupling between

and electrons is the same for the two valleys.
Similar considerations apply to acoustic phonons as well (cf. Sec. III of SM
sm2
).

Phonon self-energy from electronic Berry curvature –

Next, we study the optical phonon Green’s function and the optical phonon spectrum renormalized by both electron-electron (e-e) and e-ph interactions.
The bare phonon Green’s function is
D_{0}(q,i\omega_{n})=\text{Diag}\{D_{A_{1}},D_{B_{1}}\}
, where
D_{\lambda}=2\omega_{\lambda}/((i\omega_{n})^{2}-\omega_{\lambda}^{2})
and
\omega_{\lambda}
is the bare frequency for the
\lambda
mode.
Here,
\omega_{n}=2\pi n/\beta
is the Matsubara frequency and
\beta=1/k_{B}T
.
Following standard formalism
Mahan 2013
, the full phonon Green’s function
D({\bf q},i\omega_{n})
can be obtained from

D^{-1}({\bf q},i\omega_{n})=D_{0}^{-1}({\bf q},i\omega_{n})-\Sigma({\bf q},i\omega_{n})
,
where the phonon self-energy
\Sigma=\Sigma^{p}+\Sigma^{e}
contains both the correction from the bare e-ph interactions (
\Sigma^{p}
) and the additional correction due to the screened e-e Coulomb interaction (
\Sigma^{e}
).
When the Fermi level
\mu
is inside the bulk electronic gap (
|\mu|<2|m_{0}|
) and when
|\omega|<2|m_{0}|
, the
\Sigma^{e}
term only renormalizes the bare dielectric constant
\epsilon_{0}
, the bare phonon energy
\omega_{\lambda}
and the e-ph coupling strength
g_{i}
(see Sec. IV of the SM
sm2
).
Hereafter, we only consider the contribution from the bare e-ph interaction (
\Sigma^{p}
) in Eq. (
2
), assuming that
\omega_{\lambda}
and
g_{i}^{o}
have been renormalized by the e-e interaction.
Thus, the superscript
p
is hereafter omitted.

The diagonal components of the phonon self-energies are then found to be
\Sigma_{AA}=(\Pi_{00}+\Pi_{33}+\Pi_{03}+\Pi_{30})/(2M\omega_{A})
and
\Sigma_{BB}=\Pi_{11}/(2M\omega_{B})
, where

\displaystyle\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{q},i\omega_{n})=\frac{g_{i}g_{j}}{\beta}\sum_{s,\mathbf{k},iq_{m}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},iq_{m})\tau_{j}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},i\omega_{n}^{\prime})],

(5)

\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
,
i\omega_{n}^{\prime}=i\omega_{n}+iq_{m}
,
q_{m}=(2m+1)\pi/\beta
, and
G_{s}(\mathbf{k},iq_{m})=[iq_{m}-h_{s}(\mathbf{k})+\mu]^{-1}
is the Matsubara Green’s function for free electrons at valley
K_{s}
.
The diagonal self-energies only provide corrections to the dispersion of the
\lambda
-phonon, and thus can be included by redefining
\omega_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})
.

A more interesting physical effect emerges from the off-diagonal self-energy
\Sigma_{AB}=(\Pi_{01}+\Pi_{31})/(2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}})
, where the contribution from the
g_{2}
term has been neglected (see Sec. V of SM
sm2
for a detailed justification).

\Sigma_{AB}
hybridizes A and B phonons and has a deep connection to the Berry curvature of Dirac cones.
To see that, we first consider
\omega=0
and treat
{\bf q}
as a perturbation, so that
\Sigma_{AB}({\bf q},\omega=0)=\Sigma_{AB}(0)+{\bf q}\cdot(\partial_{\bf q}\Sigma_{AB})_{{\bf q}=0}+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
.
As shown in Sec. IV.C of SM
sm2
, TR symmetry requires
\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\Sigma_{AB}^{\ast}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)
, while the
C_{2x}
symmetry leads to
\Sigma_{AB}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=-\Sigma_{AB}(q_{x},-q_{y},\omega)
.
Combining these two symmetry constraints together with the perturbation expansion, we find
\Sigma_{AB}({\bf q},\omega=0)\approx iq_{y}(\partial_{q_{y}}\text{Im}[\Sigma_{AB}])_{{\bf q}=0}
.
We notice
\Sigma_{AB}(0)=0
since the
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
modes belong to different irreducible representations, but the
q_{y}
-dependent term is still allowed.
Remarkably, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{q_{y}}\text{Im}[\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},0)]\right)_{q_{y}=0}=\frac{g_{0}g_{1}\mathcal{A}(N_{3}^{+}-N_{3}^{-})}{2v_{0}M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}},

(6)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the sample area and the coefficient

N_{3}^{s}=\frac{1}{6}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\text{tr}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}G_{s}^{-1}\partial_{k_{\alpha}}G_{s}G_{s}^{-1}\partial_{k_{\beta}}G_{s}G_{s}^{-1}\partial_{k_{\gamma}}G_{s}

is the Thouless-Kohmoto-Nightingale-den Nijs formula for the integral of the Berry curvature around the
s
-valley (i.e., the valley Chern number; see Sec. IV.D of SM
sm2
).
TR requires
N_{3}^{+}+N_{3}^{-}=0
. Thus, to linear order in
{\bf q}
,

\Sigma_{AB}({\bf q})=ig_{0}g_{1}\mathcal{A}N_{3}^{+}q_{y}/(v_{0}M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}})\triangleq iq_{y}\Sigma_{0}
, and the local Berry curvature around one valley enters into the off-diagonal phonon self-energy.
This is a key result of the present work.
Alternatively (see Sec. VI of SM
sm2

6
) can be obtained from a Chern-Simons term that appears in the effective action for phonons after integrating out the electrons. Such derivation confirms that
\Sigma_{AB}
results from the valley Chern numbers.

The above analytical expression is derived in the limit
\omega\to 0
and
{\bf q}\to 0
, while the
\lambda
-optical phonon has a finite frequency

To see the finite frequency behavior of
\Sigma_{AB}
, we numerically evaluate it according to Eq. (
5

When the frequency is larger than electron energy gap (

This is reflected by the red color lines for both
\text{Re}[\Sigma_{AB}]

Below electron energy gap (

is still non-zero.
In Fig.

Phonon helicity –

Next, we explore the influence of
\Sigma_{AB}
in the phonon dynamics by studying the full phonon Green’s function
D({\bf q},\omega)
. The phonon dispersion can be directly extracted from the poles of
D({\bf q},\omega)
, and is depicted by the black dashed lines in Fig.
3
(a-b) for optical phonons.
Since the
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
phonon modes describe lattice vibration along the
x
and
y
directions, respectively, and the off-diagonal term
\Sigma_{AB}
that couples these two modes is purely imaginary, we expect that
\Sigma_{AB}
can make phonon modes elliptically polarized at finite
q_{y}
, as schematically depicted in the inset of Fig.
3
(c). To quantify this, we define the momentum-resolved PAM as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\text{Tr}\left[\left(\hat{P}_{+}-\hat{P}_{-}\right)\hat{A}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\right],

(7)

where
\hat{A}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=i[D(\mathbf{q},\omega)-D^{\dagger}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]/(2\pi)
is the phonon spectral function and the projection operators
\hat{P}_{\pm}=|\pm\rangle\langle\pm|
project the phonon modes to the left and right circular polarized basis
|\pm\rangle=(\vec{u}_{o,A_{1}}\pm i\,\vec{u}_{o,B_{1}})/\sqrt{2}
.
The total PAM discussed in literature
Zhang and Niu 2014
;
Zhang and Niu 2015
;
Hamada et al. 2018
;
Juraschek and Spaldin 2019
is the thermal average of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
over all the phonon modes.
We highlight two features of
{\cal P}
:
(1)
\mathcal{P}({\bf q},\omega)=0
when
{\bf q}||\hat{\bf x}
but
\mathcal{P}({\bf q},\omega)\neq 0
when
{\bf q}||\hat{\bf y}
(see Fig.
3
(a));
(2)
\mathcal{P}(q_{y},\omega)=-\mathcal{P}(-q_{y},\omega)
, as required by TR symmetry (see Fig.
3
(b)). The latter feature suggests the helical nature of each phonon band and gives rise to phonon helicity.
Both features can be understood from the symmetry property of
\Sigma_{AB}
:
(1)
\Sigma_{AB}
vanishes along the
q_{x}
direction;
(2)
\Sigma_{AB}\propto q_{y}
, so that its sign reverses under
q_{y}\to-q_{y}
.

More interestingly, since the local Berry curvature around one valley
changes sign under a band inversion,
we anticipate that the phonon helicity will also reverse its sign.
To test this, we tune the Dirac mass
m_{0}
in Eq. (
1
), and evaluate
\Sigma_{AB}
and
\mathcal{P}
for the lower branch in Fig.
3
(b) as a function of
m_{0}
at the momentum
q_{y}=m_{0}/2v_{0}
in Fig.
3
(c).
One can clearly see that both
\Sigma_{AB}
and
\mathcal{P}
reverse signs across the phase transition point at
m_{0}=0
. Thus, we conclude that an electronic topological phase transition will leave its fingerprint on PAM
\mathcal{P}
.

We also notice that along the
q_{y}
direction, the
B_{1}
mode is longitudinal while the
A_{1}
mode is transverse. Therefore, a nonzero
\Sigma_{AB}
mixes the longitudinal and transverse modes of the in-plane phonons to form elliptical vibrational modes. The longitudinal-transverse mixing has been discussed previously in the context of the phonon Hall viscosity
Barkeshli et al. 2012
;
Liu and Shi 2017
;
Heidari et al. 2019
.
However, there is an essential difference: our system does not break TR while the phonon Hall viscosity breaks TR. Consequently, in Ref. [
Barkeshli et al. 2012
], the PAM is an even function of
{\bf q}
, as reflected by the
q^{2}
-dependent coupling term between the longitudinal and transverse modes, while in our system, the PAM is an odd function of
{\bf q}
, thereby generating phonon helicity.

Discussion and Conclusion–.

We have demonstrated that electronic Berry curvature in a 2D TR-invariant Dirac material can induce an off-diagonal self-energy correction to the optical phonon Green’s function, giving rise to elliptical polarization of phonon modes.
Our theory can also be directly applied to acoustic phonons.
However, the e-ph coupling linearly depends on
{\bf q}
for acoustic phonons, and thus the off-diagonal phonon self-energy has a
q_{y}^{3}
dependence, as discussed in Sec. VI of SM
sm2
.

Our study is qualitatively different from recent works investigating the influence of the chiral anomaly on sound velocity and attenuation in Weyl/Dirac semimetals
Chernodub and Vozmediano 2019
;
Laliberté et al. 2020
;
Sengupta et al. 2020
;
Antebi et al. 2021
;
Sukhachov and Glazman 2021
. For one thing, we focus on electrical insulators. Despite of several early works studying the Berry phase effect on phonon dynamics
Lu et al. 2010
;
Sun et al. 2020
;
Mead and Truhlar 1979
, the influence of electronic band topology on the PAM and helicity have not been mentioned before.
Furthermore, although we take BaMnSb
2
as an example, the proposed mechanism for generating phonon helicity might be adapted to other
Dirac materials.
While a careful treatment is required to verify this for each material, possible candidates include HgTe/CdTe and InAs/GaSb quantum wells
Bernevig et al. 2006
;
König et al. 2007
;
Liu et al. 2008
;
Knez et al. 2011
, (LaO)
2
(SbSe
2
)
2
Dong et al. 2015
, boron nitride
Xue et al. 2011
;
Yankowitz et al. 2012
, 1T’-WTe
2

Wu et al. 2018
;
Fei et al. 2017
;
Tang et al. 2017
, and others
Wehling et al. 2014
;
Wang et al. 2015
;
Ren et al. 2016
;
Armitage et al. 2018
.

The Berry curvature contribution to phonon helicity can be detected in the following ways.
Due to TR symmetry, the equilibrium phonon occupation number is symmetric under
q_{y}\to-q_{y}
; the total PAM is accordingly zero. However, away from equilibrium, a temperature gradient applied along the
y
axis breaks the
q_{y}\to-q_{y}
symmetry and, combined with phonon helicity, leads to a nonzero total PAM. This mechanism is analogous to the Edelstein effect in electronic systems and has been discussed in Ref. [
Hamada et al. 2018
].
For the optical phonon that can directly couple to light, the elliptical polarization will be reflected in the dielectric function and thus might be probed through optical measurements, as discussed in Sec. VIII of SM
sm2
.

relative to the origin of the unit cell, and
\delta(m,F_{o}(n;R))
indicates which atom
m
is brought into the position of
n
under the symmetry operation.

Hereafter, we focus on
R\in\{C_{2x},\sigma_{v}(xz)\}
and
{\bf q}=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}(m,n|{\bf 0};\{R|{\bf v}(R)+{\bf x}(l)\})=R_{\alpha\beta}\delta(m,n),

(42)

where
R=\text{Diag}[1,-1]
.
Therefore,
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\text{Diag}[1,-1,1,-1]
and, according to Eq. (
40
), we arrive at

\displaystyle{\cal D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\cal D}_{xx}(1,1)&0&{\cal D}_{xx}(1,2)&0\\
0&{\cal D}_{yy}(1,1)&0&{\cal D}_{yy}(1,2)\\
{\cal D}_{xx}(2,1)&0&{\cal D}_{xx}(2,2)&0\\
0&{\cal D}_{yy}(2,1)&0&{\cal D}_{yy}(2,2)\end{array}\right).

The dynamical matrix must be Hermitian,
{\cal D}^{\dagger}({\bf q})={\cal D}({\bf q})
. In addition, time-reversal symmetry requires
{\cal D}({\bf q})={\cal D}^{\ast}(-{\bf q})
.
As a result, the
D
-matrix becomes

\displaystyle{\cal D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\cal D}_{xx}(1,1)&0&{\cal D}_{xx}(1,2)&0\\
0&{\cal D}_{yy}(1,1)&0&{\cal D}_{yy}(1,2)\\
{\cal D}_{xx}(1,2)&0&{\cal D}_{xx}(2,2)&0\\
0&{\cal D}_{yy}(1,2)&0&{\cal D}_{yy}(2,2)\end{array}\right),

where all the matrix elements are real.
One more constraint on
{\cal D}
comes from the fact that the lattice energy does not change for an overall translation of the whole lattice, i.e. the acoustic phonon should have zero energy for
{\bf q}=0
.
The overall translation of the whole lattice along the
x
-direction is described by the vector
(u_{1,x},u_{1,y},u_{2,x},u_{2,y})=(1,0,1,0)
. Thus, we expect

{\cal D}(1,0,1,0)^{T}=0.

(51)

Similarly, the overall translation of the whole lattice along the
y
direction gives rise to

{\cal D}(0,1,0,1)^{T}=0.

(52)

These two equations result in

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{xx}(1,1)={\cal D}_{xx}(2,2)=-{\cal D}_{xx}(1,2),\text{ and }{\cal D}_{yy}(1,1)={\cal D}_{yy}(2,2)=-{\cal D}_{yy}(1,2),

(53)

which makes the form of the
D
-matrix identical to the one obtained above from the force constant model (see Eq. (
A.2.3
)).

Appendix B
Electron-phonon interaction Hamiltonian for optical phonons

In this section, we derive the low-energy effective Hamiltonian for the interaction between electrons and optical phonons in BaMnSb
2
. The main objective is to obtain Eq. (5) of the main text.
We begin by briefly reviewing key aspects of low-energy electrons [
Liu et al. 2021
], which are massive Dirac fermions located at
{\bf K}_{\pm}=(\pi/a,\pm k_{y_{0}})
:

1.

Only the
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
orbitals of one atom in a unit cell contribute to the states near the Fermi energy. Thus, the basis of electronic states is given by

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}=\left(c_{p_{x},\uparrow}^{\dagger},c_{p_{x},\downarrow}^{\dagger},c_{p_{y},\uparrow}^{\dagger},c_{p_{y},\downarrow}^{\dagger}\right).

(54)

2.

The transformation of the electronic states under symmetry operations is described by

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{2x}C_{\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}C_{2x}^{-1}&=C_{C_{2x}\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}(-i\tau_{3}\sigma_{1}),\\
\mathcal{T}C_{\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}\mathcal{T}^{-1}&=C_{-\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}(i\tau_{0}\sigma_{2}),\\
\mathcal{P}C_{\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}\mathcal{P}^{-1}&=C_{\mathbf{K}_{+}}^{\dagger}(i\tau_{3}\sigma_{3}),\end{split}

(55)

where
\tau_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
are Pauli matrices acting in orbital and spin subspaces, respectively.
Also,
\mathcal{T}
is the time-reversal operator. Both
C_{2x}
and
\mathcal{T}
interchange the valleys
{\bf K_{+}}
and
{\bf K_{-}}
. Thus, on each valley, only the combined symmetry
\mathcal{P}=C_{2x}\mathcal{T}
remains.

We now derive the electron-phonon (e-ph) Hamiltonian following Ref. [
Saha et al. 2015
,
Rinkel et al. 2017
].
The displacement of atoms away from their equilibrium positions gives rise to a deformation potential
\delta U
, which couples to the electron density as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}r\,\Psi^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\Psi(\mathbf{r})\delta U(\mathbf{r}),

(56)

\displaystyle\delta U(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf l},s}\left[U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s}-\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{l},s})-U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s})\right]\simeq\sum_{{\bf l},s}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{l},s}\cdot\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s})}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s}},

(57)

where
U(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\mathbf{l},s}U_{s}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s})
is the periodic lattice potential,

{\bf l}
is the position of the
l-
th unit cell,
s
labels atoms inside the unit cell,

\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{l},s}=\mathbf{l}+\mathbf{r}_{s}
is the position of atom
s
in unit cell
l
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{l},s}=\sum_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M_{s}N\omega_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{l}+\mathbf{r}_{s})}\mathbf{P}_{{\bf q},s}^{\lambda}\left(b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}+b^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda}\right)

(58)

is the displacement operator for atom
s
in unit cell
l
,
b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}
is an operator that annihilates a phonon mode
\lambda
with momentum
\mathbf{q}
,
\omega_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})
is the phonon frequency,
N
is the number of unit cells in the crystal,
M_{s}
is
the mass of atom
s
and
{\bf P}_{{\bf q},s}^{\lambda}
is the polarization vector corresponding to atom
s
in phonon mode
\lambda
with momentum
\mathbf{q}
.
In addition, the fermion field operator
\Psi(\mathbf{r})
can be expanded into a sum over Bloch wave functions,
\Psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\tau\sigma,{\bf k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}u_{\tau\sigma}(\mathbf{r})c_{\sigma\tau}(\mathbf{k})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the area of the 2D sample.
Here,
u_{\tau\sigma,{\bf k}}(\mathbf{r})
is the periodic part of the Bloch wave function, which satisfies
u_{\tau\sigma,{\bf k}}(\mathbf{r})=u_{\tau\sigma,{\bf k}}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{l})
and is calculated by assuming that the atoms are in their equilibrium positions.

The low-energy electrons are contained in the vicinity of two valleys
K_{\pm}
, which are well-separated in momentum space. Then, we truncate the momentum sum in the fermion field operator
\Psi(\mathbf{r})
around the vicinity of the two valleys,

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}}}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|<\Lambda}\sum_{\kappa}e^{i\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\sum_{\tau\sigma}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}u_{\tau\sigma,\kappa}(\mathbf{r})c_{\sigma\tau,\kappa}(\mathbf{k})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k}{\Lambda}\right),

(59)

where
\kappa=\pm
label the two valleys, now
{\bf k}
is the momentum measured from a valley and
\Lambda
is a cutoff under which the linear dispersion of Dirac electrons is valid.
Based on this approximation, we can derive the e-ph coupling Hamiltonian at the two valleys separately.

Neglecting intervalley electron-phonon scattering and performing some straightforward manipulations (details can be found in Ref. [
Saha et al. 2015
,
Rinkel et al. 2017
]), the e-ph Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q}}\sum_{\lambda,\kappa}\sum_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}g^{\lambda,\kappa}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})c_{\sigma\tau,\kappa}^{\dagger}({\bf k})c_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime},\kappa}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}),

(60)

\displaystyle g^{\lambda,\kappa}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s}\int d^{2}r\,u_{\sigma\tau,\kappa}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})u_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime},\kappa}(\mathbf{r})e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\times\left[\mathbf{P}^{\lambda}_{{\bf q},s}\cdot\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s})}{\partial\mathbf{r}_{s}}\right],

(61)

where
\lambda=\{A_{1},B_{1}\}
labels the two optical in-plane phonon modes and
Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})}}\left(b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}+b^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda}\right)
is the displacement operator for mode
\lambda
at momentum
{\bf q}
.
Note that
\hbar=1
is assumed in the main text.
Below,
we only consider the e-ph coupling for long-wavelength phonons.
Moreover, the Hermiticity of the e-ph coupling Hamiltonian requires that
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}(-\mathbf{q})=\left[\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}(\mathbf{q})\right]^{\dagger}
, where
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}({\bf q})
is a
4\times 4
matrix whose matrix elements are
g^{\lambda,\kappa}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})
.

B.1
Symmetry analysis for the electron-phonon coupling vertex

In this subsection, we focus on the symmetry analysis for the e-ph coupling Hamiltonian in Eq. (
60
).
For notational simplicity, we relabel the two atoms in a unit cell as Sb
1
and Sb
2
(this is not to be confused with the labels in Fig.
4
(a)).
We restrict ourselves to long-wavelength optical phonons, for which the e-ph coupling matrix
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}(\mathbf{q})
is independent of
\mathbf{q}
to leading order in
{\bf q}
.
This approximation relies on
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}({\bf q})
being an analytic function of
{\bf q}
at
q\to 0
. Analyticity is expected in our problem, due to the non-degeneracy of low-energy electronic and phononic states.
Moreover, the matrix elements of
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}({\bf 0})
are real due to the Hermiticity condition.

In the low-energy effective theory for electrons, the spin degree of freedom is locked to the valley degree of freedom.
As a result, the e-ph coupling for long-wavelength phonons is spin-conserving.
We will furthermore assume that the e-ph coupling is spin-independent.
Then,
we drop the spin index and work with the spinless basis
C_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}=(c_{p_{x}}^{\dagger},c_{p_{y}}^{\dagger})
.
At the same time, the four-by-four matrix
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}({\bf 0})
is reduced to a two-by-two matrix, with elements given by

\displaystyle g^{\lambda,\kappa}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s}\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}\;u_{\tau,\kappa}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})u_{\tau^{\prime},\kappa}(\mathbf{r})\times\left[\mathbf{P}^{\lambda}_{{\bf q},s}\cdot\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s})}{\partial\mathbf{r}_{s}}\right].

(62)

Next, we determine the matrix structure of
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}({\bf 0})
for
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
in-plane optical phonons, using symmetry arguments.
For brevity, we hereafter drop the
{\bf q}=0
indicator from
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}
.
We start with the e-ph Hamiltonian for electrons at the
K_{+}
valley,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}^{+}=C_{\mathbf{K_{+}}}^{\dagger}\left[\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,+}Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q}\to 0)\right]C_{\mathbf{K_{+}}}.

(63)

The invariance of
\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}^{+}
under the combined symmetry operator
\mathcal{P}=C_{2x}\mathcal{T}
implies
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}^{+}\mathcal{P}^{-1}={\cal H}_{e-ph}^{+}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}^{+}\mathcal{P}^{-1}=C_{\mathbf{K_{+}}}^{\dagger}\left[(i\tau_{3})(x_{\lambda}\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,+}Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q}\to 0))^{\ast}(-i\tau_{3})\right]C_{\mathbf{K_{+}}}

(64)

and the coefficient
x_{\lambda}
(which will be either
+1
or
-1
, depending on
\lambda
) is defined via
\mathcal{P}Q_{\lambda}\mathcal{P}^{-1}=x_{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}
.
Therefore, we have
\tau_{3}(\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,+})^{\ast}\tau_{3}=x_{\lambda}\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,+}
.

For the
\lambda=A_{1}
phonon mode, the Sb
1
atom moves along the
x
direction. Hence,
x_{A_{1}}=+1
.
We do not need to consider the Sb
2
atom in the unit cell, since there are no electron states of Sb
2
near the Fermi surface.
Therefore, we have
\tau_{3}(\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},+})^{\ast}\tau_{3}=\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},+}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},+}=g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}+g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}.

(65)

In the
K_{-}
valley, time-reversal symmetry imposes

\mathbf{g}^{{A_{1}},-}=g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}-g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}.

(66)

For
\lambda=B_{1}
, the Sb
1
atom moves along the
y
direction.
Then,
x_{B_{1}}=-1
and
\tau_{3}(\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},+})^{\ast}\tau_{3}=-\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},+}
, which leads to

\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},+}=g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}.

(67)

For the
K_{-}
valley, time-reversal symmetry imposes

\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},-}=g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}.

(68)

In summary,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{g}^{o,A_{1},s}&=g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3},\\
\mathbf{g}^{o,B_{1},s}&=g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1},\end{split}

(69)

where
g_{0,1,2,3}^{o}
are material-dependent real parameters.
This coincides with Eq. (5) of the main text.

B.2
Analysis by deformation potential theory

In this subsection, we present an alternative derivation of the e-ph
\mathbf{g}^{\lambda,\kappa}
matrix.
The starting point is Eq. (
62
).
Again, all the low-energy electronic states are coming from the Sb
1
atom, and the e-ph coupling is assumed to be independent of spin.
Therefore, the expression for the e-ph coupling at the
K_{+}
valley is given by

\displaystyle g^{\lambda,+}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}r\,u_{\tau,+}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})u_{\tau^{\prime},+}(\mathbf{r})e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\times\left[\mathbf{P}^{\lambda}_{\bf q}\cdot\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{1})}{\partial\mathbf{r}_{1}}\right],

(70)

where
\mathbf{r}=(x,y)
,
\mathbf{r}_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1})
,
\mathbf{P}^{A}_{\bf q}=(1,0)
and
\mathbf{P}^{B}_{\bf q}=(0,1)
(when
q\to 0
).
Then, we study the constraints due to the combined symmetry operator
\mathcal{P}
.
Firstly, it gives rise to
\mathcal{P}:\,(x,y)\to(x,-y)
.
And the Bloch wave basis are transfered as
\mathcal{P}:\,u_{p_{x}}(x,y)\to u_{p_{x}}^{\ast}(x,-y)
and
u_{p_{y}}(x,y)\to-u_{p_{y}}^{\ast}(x,-y)
.
As for the lattice potential,
\mathcal{P}:\,U(x-x_{1},y-y_{1})\to U(x-x_{1},-y+y_{1})
.
Therefore,

(1)

For the
\lambda=A_{1}
phonon, all the element of
g^{A_{1},+}
matrix in the orbital subspace,

\displaystyle g^{A_{1},+}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}r\,\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{1})}{\partial x_{1}}u_{\tau,+}^{\ast}(x,y)u_{\tau^{\prime},+}(x,y),

(71)

where
\mathbf{r}_{1}=(x_{1},0)
because all the Sb
1
atoms move along the
x
-direction.
Under the combined
\mathcal{P}
symmetry operator, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}r\,\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{1})}{\partial x_{1}}u_{p_{x},+}^{\ast}(x,y)u_{p_{x},+}(x,y),\\
&\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}r\,\frac{\partial U(x-x_{1},-y)}{\partial x_{1}}u_{p_{x},+}(x,-y)u_{p_{x},+}^{\ast}(x,-y)=g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}},\end{split}

(72)

where we apply
P
to everything inside the integral of the first line, and we replace
y\to-y
for the second line.
Similarly, one can easily check that

\displaystyle g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}}\to g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}},\quad g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{y}}\to-g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{x}}.

(73)

From the above results, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}}&g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{y}}\\
g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{x}}&g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}}&-g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{x}}\\
-g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{y}}&g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}}\end{array}\right)\Rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}}&g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{y}}\\
g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{x}}&g^{A_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}}\end{array}\right)=g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}+g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}.

For the
K_{-}
valley, the time-reversal symmetry leads to
\mathbf{g}^{{A_{1}},-}=g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}-g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}
.

(2)

For the
\lambda=B_{1}
phonon, the matrix elements of the
\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},+}
matrix in the orbital subspace read

\displaystyle g^{B_{1},+}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{1})}{\partial y_{1}}u_{\tau,+}^{\ast}(\mathbf{r})u_{\tau^{\prime},+}(\mathbf{r}).

(80)

Please note that
\frac{\partial U(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{1})}{\partial y_{1}}
also changes sign under the
\mathcal{P}
symmetry operator.
In the same way, we have

\displaystyle g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}}\to-g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{x}},\quad g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}}\to-g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{y}},\quad g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{x},p_{y}}\to g^{B_{1},+}_{p_{y},p_{x}}.

(81)

Similar relations apply for the
K_{-}
valley. Then,
\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},+}=\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},-}=g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}
.

In sum, the two methods of this and the preceding subsection give consistent results to construct the e-ph Hamiltonian.
As a result, the full Hamiltonian can be written as follows,

\displaystyle\text{Electron Hamiltonian: }h_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\pm v_{0}(k_{y}\tau_{3}+k_{x}\tau_{1})\pm m_{0}\tau_{2},

(82)

\displaystyle\text{e-ph Hamiltonian at $K_{+}$: }{\cal H}^{+}_{e-ph}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}C^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\left(g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}+g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}\right)Q^{A}_{\mathbf{q}}+g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}Q^{B}_{\mathbf{q}}\right]C_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}},

(83)

\displaystyle\text{e-ph Hamiltonian at $K_{-}$: }{\cal H}^{-}_{e-ph}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}C^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\left(g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}-g_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}\right)Q^{A}_{\mathbf{q}}+g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}Q^{B}_{\mathbf{q}}\right]C_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}},

(84)

where
{\bf k}
is the momentum measured from a valley and the
g
-parameters depend on the material details.

B.3
Phonon-induced pseudo-gauge fields for Dirac electrons

In this section, we show that the e-ph coupling leads to electronic pseudo-gauge fields.
This can be seen by rewriting the relevant Hamiltonians in real space.

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}\,c^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{r})\left\{sv_{0}\left[(-i\partial_{y}+A_{y})\tau_{3}+(-i\partial_{x}+A_{x})\tau_{1}\right]+sm_{0}\tau_{2}+A_{0}\tau_{0}\right\}c_{s}(\mathbf{r}),

(85)

\displaystyle H_{e-ph}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}\,c^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{r})\left\{\left(g_{0}^{o}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}^{o}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{o}\tau_{3}\right)Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r})+g_{1}^{o}\tau_{1}Q_{B_{1}}(\mathbf{r})\right\}c_{s}(\mathbf{r})\ ,

(86)

where we have included the
U(1)
gauge field
A_{\mu}
, the matrix is
(-,+,+)
, the unit system is chosen such that
\hbar=c=1
, we have absorbed the elementary charge
e
into
A_{\mu}
, and we have used

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c^{\dagger}_{s,\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}}}\int d^{2}re^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}c^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{r})\\
&Q_{\lambda,\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}}\int d^{2}re^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r}).\end{split}

(87)

Here the unit for the above operators are:
[c^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{r})]=[nm^{-1}]=[meV]
,
[c^{\dagger}_{s,\boldsymbol{k}}]=[1]
,
[Q_{\lambda,\boldsymbol{k}}]=[nm]=[meV^{-1}]
, and
[Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})]=[nm]=[meV^{-1}]
.
Notice that
[nm]=[meV^{-1}]
in the case of
\hbar=c=1
.
Adding
H_{e-ph}
to
H
gives

\displaystyle H_{tot}=\sum_{s}\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}\,c^{\dagger}_{s}(\mathbf{r})\left\{\widetilde{A}_{s,0}\tau_{0}+sv_{0}\left[(-i\partial_{y}+\widetilde{A}_{s,y})\tau_{3}+(-i\partial_{x}+\widetilde{A}_{s,x})\tau_{1}\right]+s(m_{0}+g_{2}^{o}Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r}))\tau_{2}\right\}c_{s}(\mathbf{r})\ ,

(88)

where
\widetilde{A}_{s,0}=A_{0}+g_{0}^{o}Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r})
,
\widetilde{A}_{s,x}=A_{x}+s\frac{1}{v_{0}}g_{1}^{o}Q_{B_{1}}(\mathbf{r})
, and
\widetilde{A}_{s,y}=A_{y}+s\frac{1}{v_{0}}g_{3}^{o}Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r})
.
As a result, we can define the pseudo-gauge field at
K_{s}
as

\displaystyle{A}_{pse,\mu}^{s}(\mathbf{r})=\left(g_{0}^{o}Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r}),s\frac{1}{v_{0}}g_{1}^{o}Q_{B_{1}}(\mathbf{r}),s\frac{1}{v_{0}}g_{3}^{o}Q_{A_{1}}(\mathbf{r})\right)_{\mu},

(89)

where
\mu=0,1,2
and the pseudo-gauge field couples to the electrons in the same way as the
U(1)
gauge field at each valley.
The unit of
{A}_{pse,\mu}^{s}(\mathbf{r})
is [meV], since
[v_{0}]=[meV\cdot nm]=[1]
and
[g_{0,1,3}^{o}]=[meV\cdot nm^{-1}]=[meV^{2}]
.
In Eq. (4) of the main text, we show the above pseudo-gauge field in the momentum space.

Appendix C
Electron-phonon interaction Hamiltonian for acoustic phonons

In this section, we derive the electron-acoustic phonon coupling Hamiltonian from the deformation potential theory.
In principle, very similar expressions as the ones obtained for the optical phonons could be used.
In practice, those expressions do not clearly show how the e-ph coupling for acoustic phonons vanishes in the
q\to 0
limit.
Consequently, we follow another approach, discussed in Ref. [
mahan_book
,
abrikosov_book
] to derive the leading-order electron-phonon coupling.

Since the acoustic phonon frequency vanishes at
q\to 0
, Ref. [
abrikosov_book
] suggests to use the following operator instead of the operator
Q_{\lambda}(q)

abrikosov_book

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})=\omega_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})}{2M}}\left(b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}+b^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda}\right).

(90)

In terms of
\tilde{Q}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})
,
Ref. [
abrikosov_book
] further suggests that the coupling between long-wavelength acoustic phonons and electrons at valley is

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{e-ph}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}\sum_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda,\kappa}\tilde{g}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}^{\lambda,\kappa}\tilde{Q}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})c_{\sigma\tau,\kappa}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k})c_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime},\kappa}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}),

(91)

where
\tilde{g}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}^{\lambda,\kappa}
are all independent of the phonon momentum
\mathbf{q}
, to leading order.
Now we transform Eq. (
91
) back to the e-ph coupling in terms of
Q_{\lambda}(q)

\mathcal{H}_{e-ph}=\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q}}\sum_{\lambda,\kappa}\sum_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}g^{\lambda,\kappa}_{\sigma\tau,\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q})Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})c_{\sigma\tau,\kappa}^{\dagger}({\bf k})c_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime},\kappa}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\ ,

(92)

where Eq. (
91
) suggests that the matrix
g^{\lambda,\kappa}
must be of linear order of the
|\mathbf{q}|
.
Clearly, although the e-ph coupling matrix is
q-
independent for long-wavelength optical phonons, it is linear in
{\bf q}
for acoustic phonons.
Nevertheless, Eq. (
91
) does not take the direction of
\mathbf{q}
into consideration, though the order of
\mathbf{q}
is correct.
The direction of
\mathbf{q}
does matter here, since the distortion effect breaks the four-fold rotational symmetry.
To include the direction of
\mathbf{q}
, we perform the symmetry analysis for
g^{\lambda,\kappa}
, while only keeping the linear order of
\mathbf{q}
.
After taking the combined symmetry
\mathcal{P}=C_{2x}\mathcal{T}
and the Hermitian condition into account, all the matrix elements are purely imaginary.
Then, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{g}^{A_{1},s}&=iq_{x}(g_{0}^{a}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}^{a}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{a}\tau_{3})+iq_{y}(g_{1}^{a}\tau_{1}),\\
\mathbf{g}^{B_{1},s}&=iq_{y}(g_{0}^{a}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}^{a}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{a}\tau_{3})+iq_{x}(g_{1}^{a}\tau_{1}),\end{split}

(93)

where
s
is the valley index.
We emphasize that the pseudo-gauge field given by the acoustic phonons has the same form as the strain tensor, as reflected by linear
\bf q
dependence of the e-ph couplings
Vozmediano et al. 2010
.

Appendix D
Green’s function for two coupled in-plane phonon modes

In this section, we discuss the phonon Green’s function in the presence of electron-phonon (e-ph) and electron-electron (e-e) interactions.
The diagrammatic form of the Green’s function is shown in Fig.
5
, which follows Ref. [
Tse et al. 2008
] (we have reproduced the same results using a functional integral approach, in which electrons are integrated out with the aid of a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation in order to obtain an effective action for phonons).
We will focus on the two optical in-plane phonons labeled by
\lambda=\{A_{1},B_{1}\}
.
Then, the Matsubara Green’s function for the phonons is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\tau)=-\langle T_{\tau}A_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}(\tau)A_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda^{\prime}}(0)\rangle,

(94)

where
A_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}=b_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}+b^{\dagger}_{-\mathbf{q},\lambda}
,
\tau
is the imaginary time and
T_{\tau}
denotes the imaginary-time ordered product.
Note that the displacement operator is
Q_{\lambda}(\mathbf{q})=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{\lambda}}}A_{\mathbf{q},\lambda}
.
In
(\mathbf{q},\omega)
space, we use

\displaystyle D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\int_{0}^{\beta}e^{iq_{m}\tau}D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\tau)\,d\tau,

(95)

where
q_{m}=2\pi m/\beta
with
\beta=1/k_{B}T
the inverse of temperature and integer
m
.
Then, the bare (non-interacting) phonon Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle D_{0}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\text{Diag}\{D_{A_{1}A_{1}},D_{B_{1}B_{1}}\},

(96)

where
D_{\lambda\lambda}=2\omega_{\lambda}/((iq_{m})^{2}-\omega_{\lambda}^{2})
and
\omega_{\lambda}
is the bare frequency for the
\lambda
-phonon mode.
In the presence of electron-phonon interactions, the full Green’s function is no longer diagonal in the
\{A_{1},B_{1}\}
basis.
In our calculation, we first ignore the
g_{2}
-term in the e-ph coupling Hamiltonian because it only renormalizes the Dirac electron’s mass; we discuss its effect to the off-diagonal phonon self-energy later (see Sec.
).
The full phonon Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\left[1-D_{0}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\Sigma(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\right]^{-1}D_{0}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m}),

(97)

where
\Sigma(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\Sigma^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})+\Sigma^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

is the phonon self-energy due to bare e-ph interactions (
\Sigma^{p}
) and the screened e-e interaction (
\Sigma^{e}
).
The phonon self-energy can be represented as a
2\times 2
matrix in
\{A_{1},B_{1}\}
space. Its diagonal entries are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{AA}^{p}&=\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{A}}(\Pi_{00}^{p}+\Pi_{33}^{p}+\Pi_{03}^{p}+\Pi_{30}^{p})\text{ and }\Sigma_{BB}^{p}=\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{B}}\Pi_{11}^{p},\\
\Sigma_{AA}^{e}&=\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{A}}(\Pi_{00}^{e}+\Pi_{33}^{e}+\Pi_{03}^{e}+\Pi_{30}^{e})\text{ and }\Sigma_{BB}^{e}=\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{B}}\Pi_{11}^{e}.\end{split}

(98)

Likewise, the off-diagonal self-energies are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{AB}^{p}&=\frac{\hbar}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}(\Pi_{01}^{p}+\Pi_{31}^{p}),\\
\Sigma_{AB}^{e}&=\frac{\hbar}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}(\Pi_{01}^{e}+\Pi_{31}^{e}),\end{split}

(99)

with
\Sigma_{BA}({\bf q},\omega)=\Sigma_{AB}(-{\bf q},-\omega)
.
Again, we will take
\hbar=1
convention in the following calculations for simplicity.
Here, the components
\Pi^{p}_{ij}({\bf q},iq_{m})
with
i,j=0,1,3
are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{ij}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{g_{i}^{o}g_{j}^{o}}{\beta}\sum_{s,\mathbf{k},\omega_{n}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{n})\tau_{j}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},i\omega_{n}+iq_{m})],

(100)

where
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
,
s
labels the two valleys,
G_{s}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{n})=[-i\omega_{n}-\mu+h_{s}(\mathbf{k})]^{-1}
is the bare Matsubara Green’s function of Dirac electrons at valley
K_{s}
,
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)/\beta
is a fermionic Matsubara frequency and
q_{m}=2m/\beta
is a bosonic Matsubara frequency.
The retarded phonon self-energy
\Sigma({\bf q},\omega)
is obtained by analytical continuation
iq_{m}\to\omega+i0^{+}
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Pi_{ij}^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{g_{i}^{o}g_{j}^{o}}{\beta^{2}\mathcal{V}}\left(\sum_{s,\mathbf{k},\omega_{n}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{n})\tau_{0}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},i\omega_{n}+iq_{m})]V_{q}^{RPA}\sum_{s,\mathbf{k},\omega_{l}^{\prime}}\text{tr}[\tau_{0}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{l}^{\prime})\tau_{j}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},i\omega_{l}^{\prime}+iq_{m})]\right),

(101)

where
V_{q}^{RPA}=V_{q}/(1-V_{q}\Pi_{0}^{e})
is the RPA-screened e-e interaction, with
V_{q}=e^{2}/(\epsilon_{\infty}q^{2})
the “bare” Coulomb potential (
\epsilon_{\infty}
denotes the screening from high-energy electronic bands that are not included in Eq. (
82
)).

\mathcal{V}
is the 3D volume.
Also, the electronic polarization function
\Pi_{0}^{e}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Pi^{e}_{0}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\int_{0}^{2\pi/a_{z}}\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\left\{\frac{1}{\beta\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s,\mathbf{k},\omega_{n}}\text{Tr}[\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k},\omega_{n})\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\omega_{n}+q_{m})]\right\}.

(102)

which will be calculated in the next subsection.
Here
\mathcal{A}
is the area of the 2D Sb layer, the electric polarization is defined in 3D with a sum over
\mathbf{k}
in 2D and one integral along
k_{z}
, and
a_{z}
is the z-direction lattice constant.
Since the BaMnSb is just a layered material, and we assume the inter-layer coupling is zero.

The units for the quantities in the above expressions are summarized as
[Q]=[nm],[c_{\mathbf{k}}]=[1],[V]=[nm^{3}],[a_{z}]=[nm],[\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega_{A}}]=[nm^{2}],[g]=[meV\cdot nm^{-1}],[G]=[meV^{-1}],[\beta]=[meV^{-1}],[V_{q}]=[meV\cdot nm^{3}],[\sum_{k}]=[1],[\sum_{\omega}]=[1]
, then
[\Pi^{p}]=[\Pi^{e}]=[meV\cdot nm^{-2}]
,
[\Sigma]=[meV]
, and
[\Pi^{e}_{0}]=[meV^{-1}\cdot nm^{-3}]
.
Moreover,
[2M\omega_{A}]=[nm^{-2}]
for
\hbar=e=1
(this convention is used in the main text).
In the following sections, we take
[nm]=[meV^{-1}]
to double-check the units for simplicity by taking
[v_{0}]=[1]
(
v_{0}
is Fermi velocity of Dirac fermions).

D.1
The electronic polarization function for the insulating phase

In this subsection, we calculate the contribution of low-energy electrons in BaMnSb
2
to the electronic polarization function
\Pi_{0}^{e}({\bf q},iq_{m})
.
We assume throughout that the chemical potential is inside the electron bandgap.
We calculate the contributions from the
K_{+}
and
K_{-}
valleys separately, and add them in the end.
The corresponding electronic Green’s function at valley
\mathbf{K}_{s}
(a
2\times 2
matrix) is

\displaystyle G_{s}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{n})=\left(-i\omega_{n}-\mu+h_{s}(\mathbf{k})\right)^{-1}=\frac{P_{+}(\mathbf{k})}{-i\omega_{n}-\mu+E_{\mathbf{k}}}+\frac{P_{-}(\mathbf{k})}{-i\omega_{n}-\mu-E_{\mathbf{k}}},

(103)

where
P_{\pm}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2}[1\pm h_{s}({\bf k})/E_{\mathbf{k}}]
are projector operators and
E_{\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{v_{0}^{2}(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})+m^{2}}
.
We begin with contributions from electrons at
K_{+}
valley to the electric polarization function,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\text{Tr}[\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k},\omega_{n})\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\omega_{n}+q_{m})].

(104)

First, we recognize that

\displaystyle\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k},\omega_{n})\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\omega_{n}+q_{m})=\sum_{\alpha\beta}f_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\omega_{n},q_{m})P_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})P_{\beta}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}),

(105)

where
\alpha,\beta=\{+,-\}
and
f_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\omega_{n},q_{m})=\frac{1}{-i\omega_{n}-\mu+\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{1}{-i(\omega_{n}+q_{m})-\mu+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}
.
Then, the summation over Matsubara frequencies gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{B}T\sum_{\omega_{n}}f_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\omega_{n},q_{m})&=\frac{n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{-iq_{m}+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}},\end{split}

(106)

where
n_{F}(x)=1/(1+e^{x/k_{B}T})
is the Fermi distribution function so that
n_{F}(x>0)=0
and
n_{F}(x<0)=1
at zero temperature. For the trace part, we have

\displaystyle\text{Tr}[P_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})P_{\beta}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\beta\left[\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}+\frac{v_{0}^{2}(k_{x}q_{x}+k_{y}q_{y})}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right].

(107)

The contribution from electrons at the
K_{-}
valley can be computed in the same way, simply by replacing the projection
P_{\alpha}
with
P_{-\alpha}
.
Therefore, summing over the contribution from both valleys, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi^{e}_{0}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{1}{a_{z}\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{-iq_{m}+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}\left\{1+\alpha\beta\left[\frac{E_{\mathbf{k}}}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}+\frac{v_{0}^{2}(k_{x}q_{x}+k_{y}q_{y})}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right]\right\}.

(108)

In the
m=0
limit, the term inside the curly brackets tends to
1+\alpha\beta\cos(\theta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})
,
where
\theta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}
is the angle between
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
.
In that limit, our expression for
\Pi^{e}_{0}
reproduces that of graphene
Hwang and Das Sarma 2007
.

In the zero-temperature limit and when the chemical potential is inside the bandgap, only interband transitions
\alpha\neq\beta
contribute to
\Pi^{e}_{0}
.
In the static limit, these contributions amount to a renormalization of
\epsilon_{\infty}
in the screened Coulomb interaction.
More precisely, we obtain
Pyatkovskiy 2008

\displaystyle\Pi^{e}_{0}(\mathbf{q},\omega=0)\approx-q^{2}/(6a_{z}\pi|m_{0}|),

(109)

here
a_{z}
is the z direction lattice constant.
which is valid when we take
v_{0}q\ll m_{0}
limit.
Thus, the contribution from Dirac fermions to the static dielectric function is

\displaystyle\epsilon(\mathbf{q})=1-V_{q}\Pi_{0}^{e}(\mathbf{q},\omega=0)\approx 1+e^{2}/(6a_{z}\pi\epsilon_{\infty}|m_{0}|)\triangleq\epsilon_{RPA},

(110)

where
V_{q}=e^{2}/(\epsilon_{\infty}q^{2})
is the Coulomb potential.
We check the unit of
[V_{q}]=[meV^{-2}]
and
[\Pi_{0}^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})]=[meV^{2}]
, so that
\epsilon_{RPA}
is a dimensionless parameter.
Thus, Eq. (
110
) gives rise to the the RPA-screened Coulomb potential for the insulating electron system.

In this subsection, we discuss the diagonal phonon self-energies,

We first calculate all the necessary elements of

(114)

\displaystyle\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

(115)

\mu

(116)

D.3
The off-diagonal phonon self-energy
\Sigma_{AB}

Next, let us consider the off-diagonal self-energy defined in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{AB}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y}=0,iq_{m})&=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\left[\Pi_{31}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y}=0,iq_{m})+\Pi_{01}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y}=0,iq_{m})\right]\\

&=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{01}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},iq_{m}),\end{split}

(122)

D.3.2
Contribution of electron-electron interactions to the off-diagonal self-energy

Next, we consider the additional correction to the off-diagonal phonon coming from e-e interactions, which is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\left[\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{00}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})V_{q}^{RPA}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})+\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{30}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})V_{q}^{RPA}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\right].

(123)

Let us consider these two terms one-by-one.
From Eqs. (

\displaystyle\Pi_{30}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\sim q_{x}\text{ and }\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\sim q_{y},

(124)

to linear order in
{\bf q}
. As a result, one of these two terms must vanish when
{\bf q}
is along the
x
or

axis.
Then, it simplifies the off-diagonal self energy as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\left[\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{00}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})V_{q}^{RPA}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\right],

(125)

with
{\bf q}=(q_{x},0)
or
{\bf q}=(0,q_{y})
.

Adding the results from Eqs. (
122
) and (
125
), the total off-diagonal self-energy becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})&=\left[1+\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{00}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})V_{q}^{RPA}\right]\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m}),\\
&=\left[1+\Pi_{0}^{e}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})V_{q}^{RPA}\right]\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})\\
&=\frac{1}{\epsilon(\mathbf{q})}\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m}),\end{split}

(126)

where the RPA dielectric function
\epsilon(\mathbf{q})
is given by Eq. (
110
).
In other words, the influence of e-e interactions in the off-diagonal phonon energy amounts to renormalizing the e-ph coupling parameters as

\displaystyle g_{0}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\to\frac{1}{\epsilon(\mathbf{q})}g_{0}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\approx\frac{g_{0}^{o}g_{1}^{o}}{\epsilon_{RPA}}.

(127)

In sum, after renormalizing the e-ph couplings, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})],

(128)

with
{\bf q}=(q_{x},0)
or
{\bf q}=(0,q_{y})
.
This is the result used in the main text to determine the phonon helicity.

D.3.3
Symmetry constraints on the off-diagonal self-energy

Now, let us focus on the symmetry constraints on the off-diagonal self-energy. The symmetry operators include the time-reversal symmetry and the two-fold rotation symmetry.
In this subsection, we work with real frequencies.

(1.)

Time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}
imposes the following constraints on the electron Green’s function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}G_{\pm}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega)\mathcal{T}^{-1}=G_{\mp}^{\ast}(-k_{x},-k_{y},\omega).

(129)

It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pi_{i1}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)&=\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime})\tau_{1}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})],\\
&=\left(\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}^{\ast}G_{s}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime})\tau_{1}^{\ast}G_{s}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})]\right)^{\ast},\\
&=\left(\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{-\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}^{\ast}G_{-s}(-\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime})\tau_{1}G_{-s}(-\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})]\right)^{\ast}=\left(\Pi_{i1}^{p}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)\right)^{\ast},\end{split}

(130)

where
x(s)=s\delta_{i,2}+\delta_{i,0}+\delta_{i,3}
with
i=0,2,3
, we replace
s\to-s
in the last line, and we use

x(-s)\tau_{i}^{\ast}=x(s)\tau_{i}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\Sigma_{AB}^{\ast}(-\mathbf{q},\omega).

(131)

(2.)

The two-fold rotation symmetry
C_{2x}
imposes the following constraint on the Green’s function:

\displaystyle C_{2x}G_{\pm}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega)C_{2x}^{-1}=G_{\mp}(k_{x},-k_{y},\omega).

(132)

Along the lines of the analysis above,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pi_{i1}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)&=\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[\tau_{i}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime})\tau_{1}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})],\\
&=\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[C_{2x}\tau_{i}C_{2x}^{-1}C_{2x}G_{s}(\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime})C_{2x}^{-1}C_{2x}\tau_{1}C_{2x}^{-1}C_{2x}G_{s}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})C_{2x}^{-1}],\\
&=-\sum_{s}x_{i}(s)g_{i}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\omega^{\prime}}\text{tr}[C_{2x}\tau_{i}C_{2x}^{-1}G_{-s}(k_{x},-k_{y},\omega^{\prime})\tau_{1}G_{-s}(k_{x}^{\prime},-k_{y}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})],\\
&=-\Pi_{i1}^{p}(q_{x},-q_{y},\omega),\end{split}

(133)

where we used
x_{i}(s)C_{2x}\tau_{i}C_{2x}^{-1}=x_{i}(-s)\tau_{i}
with
i=0,2,3
.
Then we arrive at

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=-\Sigma_{AB}(q_{x},-q_{y},\omega).

(134)

Based on the above two symmetries, we find
\Sigma_{AB}({\bf q},\omega=0)\approx iq_{y}(\partial_{q_{y}}\text{Im}[\Sigma_{AB}])_{{\bf q}=0}
as shown in the main text.

D.4
Berry curvature contribution to the off-diagonal self-energy in the
\omega=0
limit

In this section, we prove that the off-diagonal self-energy
\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega)
is proportional to TKNN invariant in the
q_{y}\to 0
and
\omega=0
limit.
As discussed in the last subsection,
\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega=0)
is linearly proportional to
q_{y}
due to the TR symmetry and
C_{2x}
rotational symmetry.
Next, we demonstrate that the coefficient of the linear term is given by

\displaystyle a_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{q_{y}}}\text{Im}[\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega=0)]|_{q_{y}=0}=N_{3},

(135)

where
a_{0}=\frac{2Mv_{0}\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}{g_{0}g_{1}\mathcal{A}}
, and the right hand side is given by

\displaystyle N_{3}=\frac{\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}}{6}\text{tr}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}G^{-1}\partial_{k_{\alpha}}GG^{-1}\partial_{k_{\beta}}GG^{-1}\partial_{k_{\gamma}}G

(136)

is the TKNN invariant and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\{i\omega^{\prime},k_{x},k_{y}\}
.
Without loss of generality, we set
\hbar=1
.
To prove the above equation, let us begin with
a_{0}\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y})
with a symmetric form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\text{Tr}[\tau_{0}G(\mathbf{k}-\frac{\bf q}{2},\omega_{n})\tau_{x}G(\mathbf{k}+\frac{\bf q}{2},\omega_{n}+q_{m})].

(137)

After taking the partial differential with the
q_{y}\to 0
limit, we find that

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{y}}G(\mathbf{k}\pm\mathbf{\frac{q}{2}},\omega_{n})|_{q_{y}=0}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k_{y}}G(\mathbf{k},\omega_{n}).

(138)

Therefore, the left hand side of Eq. (
135
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\text{tr}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(-\partial_{k_{y}}G\tau_{x}G+G\tau_{x}\partial_{k_{y}}G\right).

(139)

For a single Dirac Hamiltonian of the type
h({\bf k})=v_{0}(k_{x}\tau_{x}+k_{y}\tau_{y})+m\tau_{z}
, with velocity
v_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v_{0}}\partial_{k_{x}}G^{-1}=-\tau_{x},\;\partial_{i\omega}G^{-1}=\tau_{0}.

(140)

Substituting Eq. (
140
) into Eq. (
139
), we find

\displaystyle a_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{q_{y}}}\text{Im}[\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega=0)]|_{q_{y}=0}=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Big(-(\partial_{k_{y}}G)(\partial_{k_{x}}G^{-1})G(\partial_{i\omega^{\prime}}G^{-1})+G(\partial_{k_{x}}G^{-1})(\partial_{k_{y}}G)(\partial_{i\omega^{\prime}}G^{-1})\Big).

(141)

By using the identity

\displaystyle G\partial_{\alpha}G^{-1}=-(\partial_{\alpha}G)G^{-1}

(142)

with
\alpha=\{i\omega^{\prime},k_{x},k_{y}\}
,
we can simplify the Eq. (
141
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Big((G^{-1}\partial_{k_{x}}G)(G^{-1}\partial_{k_{y}}G)(G^{-1}\partial_{i\omega^{\prime}}G)-(G^{-1}\partial_{k_{y}}G)(G^{-1}\partial_{k_{x}}G)(G^{-1}\partial_{i\omega^{\prime}}G)\Big)=N_{3}.

(143)

where the cyclic permutation for the trace is used.
This concludes the proof of Eq. (
135
).

Appendix E
The additional self-energy correction due to the
g_{2}^{o}
e-ph coupling vertex

Up until now, we have studied the contribution to the phonon self-energy originating from
g_{0}^{o}
,
g_{1}^{o}
and
g_{3}^{o}
; the corresponding electron-phonon interactions behave as pseudo-gauge fields for electrons.
In doing so, we have ignored the effect of the
g_{2}^{o}
term, which renormalizes the Dirac mass of the electrons. One may ask how this term will influence our main results concerning the electronic Berry curvature contribution to the phonon helicity. To address this question, we write the correction from the
g_{2}^{o}
e-ph coupling to the off-diagonal phonon self-energy as

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma_{AB}=\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}+\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB},

(144)

where
\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{21}^{p}
,
\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{20}^{p}\times V_{q}^{RPA}\times\Pi_{01}^{p}
and

\displaystyle\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

\displaystyle=g_{0}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{-iq_{m}+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}\left[-i\alpha\beta\frac{v_{0}q_{y}m_{0}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right],

(145)

\displaystyle\Pi_{21}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

\displaystyle=g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{-iq_{m}+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}\left[i\alpha\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}-i\beta\frac{v_{0}(k_{y}+q_{y})}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right],

(146)

\displaystyle\Pi_{20}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})

\displaystyle=g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{-iq_{m}+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}\left[\alpha\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}+\beta\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right].

(147)

E.1
The self-energy correction
\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}
in the absence of electron-electron interactions

In this subsection, let us first study the self-energy correction
\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{21}^{p}
. Because we focus on the insulating phase at zero temperature,
we can set
\mu=0
. Accordingly,

\displaystyle n_{F}(-E_{\mathbf{k}})=n_{F}(-E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})=1,\text{ and }n_{F}(E_{\mathbf{k}})=n_{F}(E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})=0,

(148)

which leads to

\displaystyle\Pi_{21}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{2}{(E_{\mathbf{k}}+E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}\left[\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}+\frac{v_{0}(k_{y}+q_{y})}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right].

(149)

Here, we have used
iq_{m}\to\omega+i0^{+}
and we have kept only the leading order term in
\omega
.
We check that
\Pi_{21}^{p}
satisfies the symmetry requirements with respect to time-reversal and the two-fold rotation
C_{2x}
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y}=0,\omega)=0,\quad\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)=-\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x}=0,-q_{y},\omega),\quad\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=\left(\Pi_{21}^{p}(-q_{x},-q_{y},\omega)\right)^{\ast}.

(150)

To compare with the
\Pi_{01}^{p}\propto iq_{y}
term,
we evaluate
\Pi_{21}^{p}
to leading order in
q_{y}
and we focus on the results along the
q_{y}
-axis, i.e.,
q_{x}=0
.
Performing a Taylor expansion on
q_{y}
and keeping terms up to linear order, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)&\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right)\left[\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}+\frac{v_{0}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}+\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\left(1-\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right)\right]\\
&\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\frac{v_{0}q_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}+\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}\times\left(2-3\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right)\right]=g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\frac{v_{0}q_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}-\frac{v_{0}k_{y}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}\frac{3v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right]\\
&=g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega v_{0}q_{y}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\left[\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}-\frac{3v_{0}^{2}k_{y}^{2}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{5}}\right]=g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times i\omega v_{0}q_{y}\left(\frac{1}{2|m|v_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{3v_{0}^{2}}{2}\frac{2}{3|m|v_{0}^{4}}\right)=0.\end{split}

(151)

This indicates that the leading order term must be
q_{y}^{3}
, namely,

\displaystyle\Pi_{21}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)\propto g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times(i\omega q_{y}^{3}).

(152)

In sum, in the long-wavelength regime, the contribution of the
g_{2}
-term to the off-diagonal phonon self-energy is small compared to that from
\Pi_{01}^{p}
.

E.2
Contribution of electron-electron interactions to the self-energy correction
\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB}

In this subsection, we study the self-energy correction
\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\frac{1}{(g_{0}^{o})^{2}\mathcal{V}}\Pi_{20}^{p}\times V_{q}^{RPA}\times\Pi_{01}^{p}
, where
\Pi_{01}^{p}\sim iq_{y}
.
To do so, we only need to evaluate
\Pi_{20}^{p}
.
Setting
q_{x}=0
, a straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\Pi_{20}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)

\displaystyle\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\times m\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{2}{(E_{\mathbf{k}}+E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}}}-\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right],

(153)

By inspection,
we notice that
\Pi_{20}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)
is at least of order
q_{y}^{2}
.
Performing a Taylor expansion to order
q_{y}^{2}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Pi_{20}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)&\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\times m\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}\left[1-\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right]\left[\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}+\frac{v_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}}{2{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}}\left(1-\frac{3v_{0}^{2}k_{y}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right)\right],\\
&\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\times m\omega\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{3}}\left[\frac{v_{0}^{2}k_{y}q_{y}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}+\frac{v_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{5v_{0}^{2}k_{y}^{2}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}}\right)\right],\\
&\approx g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\times m\omega\left(\frac{1}{4}v_{0}^{2}q_{y}^{2}\right)\times\left(0+\frac{1}{3|m|^{3}v_{0}^{2}}-5v_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{15|m|^{3}v_{0}^{4}}\right)=0.\end{split}

(154)

This indicates that the leading order must be
q_{y}^{4}
, namely,

\displaystyle\Pi_{20}^{p}(q_{x}=0,q_{y},\omega)\sim g_{2}^{o}g_{0}^{o}\times m\omega q_{y}^{4}.

(155)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB}=\left(\frac{1}{g_{0}^{o}}\right)^{2}\Pi_{20}^{p}\times V_{q}^{RPA}\times\Pi_{01}^{p}\sim g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times(i\omega q_{y}^{3}).

(156)

In sum, the full contribution of the
g_{2}^{o}
e-ph coupling to the off-diagonal phonon self-energy scales as

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma_{AB}=\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}+\Delta\Sigma^{e}_{AB}\sim\Delta\Sigma^{p}_{AB}\sim g_{2}^{o}g_{1}^{o}\times(i\omega q_{y}^{3}).

(157)

For long-wavelength phonons, this correction can be neglected compared to the
\Sigma_{AB}\propto iq_{y}
contribution obtained from
g_{0}^{o}
and
g_{1}^{o}
.

Appendix F
Field-theoretic derivation for the off-diagonal coupling between phonons

In this section, we show an alternative derivation for phonon hybridization from the field theory.
Here we use the real time, which is related to the imaginary-time formalism in other sections by a Wick rotation.

We begin by integrating out the 2D Dirac fermions
comtet2000aspects
in Eq. (
88

This gives the following Chern-Simons term in the effective action:

where the Einstein summation convention is adopted for the Greek letters (

due to

Next, we apply the above equation to BaMnSb

Check the unit:

Following the standard techniques in Ref. [

Then, the off-diagonal term plays the role of self-energy corrections, which is given by

.

In the Sec. V above, we have shown the effect of electron-phonon interaction by analyzing all the self-energy corrections due to e-e and e-ph interactions.

On the other hand,
S_{eff}^{A-pse}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ph}

(164)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega^{2}-\omega_{A}^{2}&\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}\Sigma_{AB}\\

\omega_{A}^{2}

Appendix G
Phonon helicity for acoustic phonons

The general electron-acoustic phonon coupling vertex is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{g}^{a,A_{1},s}&=iq_{x}(g_{0}^{a}\tau_{0}+sg_{2}^{a}\tau_{2}+g_{3}^{a}\tau_{3})+iq_{y}(g_{1}^{a}\tau_{1}),\\

The off-diagonal self-energy is similar to that discussed for optical phonons and is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}^{a}\omega_{B}^{a}}}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},iq_{m})],

(168)

Appendix H
The dynamical Kohn anomaly

In this section, we analytically analyze the singularity and kinks for the off-diagonal phonon self-energy
\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\sim\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
at finite
\omega
and
{\bf q}
. Using the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x\pm i\eta}=\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\mp i\pi\delta(x),

(169)

where
\mathcal{P}
means the principal value, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-\omega-i\eta+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}=\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{-\omega+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}}+i\pi\delta(-\omega+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}).

(170)

Thus, for the off-diagonal self-energy
\Sigma_{AB}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\frac{1}{2M\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}}\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
,
and the real component of
\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\text{Re}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]=g_{0}g_{1}\sum_{k}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\left(n_{F}(\mu-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}})-n_{F}(\mu-\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})\right)\left[\alpha\beta\frac{\pi v_{0}q_{y}m_{0}}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\right]\delta(-\omega+\beta E_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\alpha E_{\mathbf{k}}).

(171)

For simplicity, we concentrate on the insulating regime (
0<=\mu<|m_{0}|<\omega/2
) at zero temperature.
With
n_{F}(\mu+E_{\mathbf{k}})=n_{F}(\mu+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})=0
and
n_{F}(\mu-E_{\mathbf{k}})=n_{F}(\mu-E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})=1
(
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Re}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]}{g_{0}g_{1}\pi v_{0}m_{0}q_{y}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}\left(\delta(-\omega-E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}-E_{\mathbf{k}})-\delta(-\omega+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+E_{\mathbf{k}})\right)=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{\delta(-\omega+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+E_{\mathbf{k}})}{E_{\mathbf{k}}E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}}.

(172)

In polar coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k}=\mathcal{A}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}=\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}kdk\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta,

(173)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the area of the crystal in the
xy
plane.
Next, we choose
{\bf q}=(0,q)
with
q\geq 0
, so that
\theta
-dependence only appears for the terms involving
k^{\prime}=\sqrt{k^{2}+q^{2}+2kq\cos(\theta-\pi/2)}=\sqrt{k^{2}+q^{2}+2kq\sin\theta}
.
Then, making a change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\to 2\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}d\theta\to\int_{|k-q|}^{k+q}dk^{\prime}\frac{4k^{\prime}}{\sqrt{(2kq)^{2}-((k^{\prime})^{2}-k^{2}-q^{2})^{2}}}.\end{split}

(174)

After this replacement, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Re}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]}{g_{0}g_{1}\pi v_{0}m_{0}q_{y}}=-\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\int_{|k-q|}^{k+q}dk^{\prime}g(k,k^{\prime})\delta(-\omega+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}+E_{\mathbf{k}}),

(175)

where
g(k,k^{\prime})=\frac{4kk^{\prime}}{\sqrt{(2kq)^{2}-((k^{\prime})^{2}-k^{2}-q^{2})^{2}}}
is a continuous function and
E_{k}=\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{0}^{2}}
with
v_{0}\equiv 1
.
With some algebra, this expression can be further simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Re}[\Pi_{01}^{p}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]}{g_{0}g_{1}\pi v_{0}m_{0}q_{y}}=-\mathcal{A}\int_{0}^{+\infty}dk\,g(k,k_{0})\theta(k_{0}-|k-q|)\theta(k+q-k_{0}),

(176)

where
\theta(...)
is the step function,
k_{0}=\sqrt{(\omega-\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{0}^{2}})^{2}-m_{0}^{2}}
.
The integrand contains two step functions, which confine the range of the integration. Fix all these parameters here except
\omega
, we try to find if the range of integration changes by tunning
\omega
, which indicates a singularity/kink for this integration.

(1.)

For the step function
\theta(k+q-k_{0})
, the
k+q-k_{0}
increase monotonically by
k
. And

\left(k+q-k_{0}\right)_{k=0}=q-k_{0}
can changes sign, once it happens, the range of the integration will change.

(2.)

For the step function
\theta(k_{0}-|k-q|)
,

k_{0}-|k-q|
is a monotonically increasing function if
k<q
and a monotonically decreasing function for
k>q
. Also,
\left(k_{0}-|k-q|\right)_{k=0}=k_{0}-q
could change sign.

Therefore, we find that
(k+q-k_{0})_{k=0}=0
gives rise to the singularity/kink, therefore, the kink locates at
q_{0}=\sqrt{(\omega-m_{0})^{2}-m_{0}^{2}}
, shown in the main text.

Appendix I
Spatial dispersion of the dielectric function

In this section, we study the influence of the phonon helicity of optical phonons on the dielectric response of the material.
From the action for purely phonon modes (see Sec. (VI)),
the equation of motion for the phonon dynamics can be obtained my minimizing the effective action (
164
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{q}}\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+Q\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=0.

(177)

The optical phonon can produce a macroscopic polarization
\mathbf{P}
, given by

\mathbf{P}(\omega,q_{y})=Q\mathbf{\Psi}_{q_{y}}(\omega)=\chi\mathbf{E}
with the electric susceptibility
\chi
to be a two-by-two matrix.
The complex dielectric function matrix is then given by

\displaystyle\epsilon=\epsilon_{0}I_{2\times 2}+\chi=\epsilon_{0}I_{2\times 2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{N(q_{y},\omega)}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega^{2}-\omega_{B}^{2}&C\\
-C&\omega^{2}-\omega_{A}^{2}\end{array}\right],

where
N(q_{y},\omega)=(\omega^{2}-\omega_{A}^{2})(\omega^{2}-\omega_{B}^{2})-\omega_{A}\omega_{B}\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega)
and
C=\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}\Sigma_{AB}
.
Note that
C
is purely imaginary.
Since the off-diagonal term is an odd function in
q_{y}
, one can check the dielectric function matrix satisfies the Onsager relation due to the time-reversal symmetry.
The off-diagonal component of the dielectric constant is of particular interest and is given by

\epsilon_{xy}=i\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}\Sigma_{AB}(q_{y},\omega)/N(q_{y},\omega)\sim i\sqrt{\omega_{A}\omega_{B}}g_{0}g_{1}m_{0}q_{y}/(2\pi v_{0}|m_{0}|(\omega^{2}-\omega_{A}^{2})(\omega^{2}-\omega_{B}^{2}))
up to the lowest order in
{\bf q}
.
Along
q_{x}
axis, the off-diagonal self-energy vanishes, indicating that the off-diagonal term of the dielectric function also becomes zero.
Therefore, one can see that the Berry curvature contribution to the optical phonon self-energy will also enter into the spatial dispersion (
{\bf q}
dependence) of the off-diagonal dielectric function through the optical phonon-light coupling. Since this term will have a resonance at the optical phonon frequency, it can be easily distinguished from other contribution through tuning the light frequency.
However, it may not be easy to measure this effect in optical absorption/reflectivity, because it vanishes when
q\to 0
and the photon wave vectors corresponding to optical phonon frequencies are small.

1.
Introduction

In this article we consider a nonlinear free boundary problem and we establish the Lipschitz continuity
of its almost minimizers. The classical motivations for free boundary problems of these
types stem from flows with jets and cavities (see e.g. Section 1.1 in
[
9
]
).
In this context, the conditions arising from a free boundary problem can be seen
as the variational counterpart
of Bernoulli’s law according to which pressure is prescribed on the free streamline
as a balance with the velocity (or the kinetic energy) of the fluid.

The nonlinear feature of the corresponding differential operator
aims at modeling possibly non-Newtonian fluids, in which linear relations between physical quantities
are replaced by power-laws.

Similar models also appear in the study of electrical impedance tomography (see
[
3
]
),
optimal heat flows (see
[
1
]
), electrochemical machining (see
[
32
]
) and
high activation energy in combustion theory (see
[
4
,
10
,
29
]
).
Also, free boundary problems can be used as a sharp-interface approximation
of phase coexistence models (see e.g.
[
42
,
41
,
45
]
, roughly speaking
as a replacing for phases modeled by a smooth state parameter with a merely continuous one).

The setting of almost minimizers is also classical in the calculus of variations
(dating back, at least up to a certain extent, to a famous sentence in Leibniz’s
Specimen Geometriae Luciferae
,
probably written in the mid-1690s, “pro minimis adhiberi possunt quasi minima”, that is
“the almost minimizers can be exploited in place of minimizers”).

More specifically, the mathematical setting that we consider here goes as follows.
Let

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a given domain and
p>\max\left\{\frac{2n}{n+2},1\right\}
.
We consider the energy functional

(1.1)

J_{p}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u(x)|^{p}+\chi_{\{u>0\}}(x)\Big)\,dx

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
u\geqslant 0
.

The condition that
u
is nonnegative corresponds, in the framework of free boundary problems,
to considering “one-phase” solutions (solutions which may change sign being related to “two-phase” problems).

The precise notion of almost minimizers that we use in this paper is the following one:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\kappa\geqslant 0
and
\beta>0
. We say that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
\Omega
,
with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
, if
u\geqslant 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and

(1.2)

J_{p}(u,B_{\varrho}(x))\leqslant(1+\kappa\varrho^{\beta})J_{p}(v,B_{\varrho}(x)),

for every ball
B_{\varrho}(x)
such that
\overline{B_{\varrho}(x)}\subset\Omega
and for every
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{\varrho}(x))
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{\varrho}(x)
in the sense of the trace.

In some sense, Definition
1.1
is one of the possible modern formalizations
of Leibniz’s initial intuition reported at the beginning of this paper: namely,
almost minimizers are natural objects to look at, for instance, to deal with minimizers
of “perturbed” functionals. As a concrete example, if we consider

\widetilde{J}_{p}(u,\Omega):=J_{p}(u,\Omega)+\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\Phi(u(y))\,\Phi(u(z))\,\Phi(u(y)-u(z))\,dy\,dz

for a function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
with
\Phi=0
in
(-\infty,0]
, we readily see that
J_{p}(u,B_{r}(x))\leqslant\widetilde{J}_{p}(u,B_{r}(x))

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}_{p}(u,B_{r}(x))\leqslant J_{p}(u,B_{r}(x))+\iint_{B_{r}(x)\times B_{r}(x)}\chi_{\{u>0\}}(y)\,\chi_{\{u>0\}}(z)\,dy\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant J_{p}(u,B_{r}(x))+|B_{r}|\int_{B_{r}(x)}\chi_{\{u>0\}}(y)\,dy\leqslant(1+|B_{1}|\,r^{n})J_{p}(u,B_{r}(x)).

Accordingly, a minimizer for the “complicated” functional
\widetilde{J}_{p}
turns out
to be an almost minimizer for the “simpler” functional
J_{p}
.

As usual,
the constants depending only on
n
and
p
are called universal. If
u
is an almost minimizer, the structural constants may depend on
\kappa
and
\beta
as well.

Our main result establishes the Lipschitz regularity of the one-phase almost minimizers as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>\max\left\{\frac{2n}{n+2},1\right\}
and
u
be an almost minimizer
for
J_{p}
in
B_{1}
with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
.

Then,

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\Big(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+1\Big),

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
n
,
p
,

\kappa
and
\beta
.

In addition,
u
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in a neighborhood of
\left\{u=0\right\}
,
namely if
u(0)=0
then

\left|\nabla u\right|\leqslant C\quad\mbox{in }B_{r_{0}},

for some
C>0
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
\kappa
and
\beta
, and
r_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending
on
n
,
p
,
\kappa
,
\beta
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
.

We stress that Theorem
1.2
is new for
p\neq 2
, the case
p=2
being treated in
[
15
]
.

We recall that, when
p=2
, minimizers of (
1.1
) were studied in
[
2
]
, where the
Lipschitz regularity
of minimizers and the regularity of flat free boundaries were established.
In
[
6
,
8
,
7
]

Caffarelli developed a viscosity approach to the free boundary problem for
p=2
in the two-phase
setting (see also
[
18
]
and the references therein for related
free boundary regularity properties). For one-phase problems the viscosity approach for operators governed by the
p
-Laplace or
p(x)-
Laplace operators
has been developed in
[
34
,
23
]

and in
[
24
]
as well, where Lipschitz regularity of viscosity solutions of inhomogeneous one-phase problems, governed by the
p(x)-
Laplace operator and some regularity properties of their free boundaries were established.

The Lipschitz regularity of the minimizers of the functional in (
1.1
) has been obtained
in
[
21
]
, which has also provided a proof of the Lipschitz
regularity when
p=2
without using
monotonicity formulae.
We refer to
[
20
]
as well, where a discrete version of the Weiss monotonicity formula
for the functional in (
1.1
)
has been established for
p
close to
2
.

Minimizers of the functional in (
1.1
) have been also considered in
[
11
]
,
in which the regularity of the free boundary near flat points was established.
See also
[
12
,
30
,
39
,
35
,
36
,
40
,
5
,
33
,
31
]
for regularity results on
p
-Laplacian free boundary problems.

Regarding the setting of almost minimizers, the case
p=2
has been investigated
in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
The case of almost minimizers for
p\neq 2
was, to the best of our knowledge, not fully investigated, hence Theorem
1.2
aims at starting a research line in this direction,
and the results presented in this article are part of the PhD thesis of the third author
[
25
]
.
In this paper we take inspiration from the approach used in
[
15
]
,
see also
[
16
,
17
]
.

We point out that the case
p\neq 2
provides significant technical complications,
especially due to the fact that the sum of two solutions is not a solution any longer,
thus making it difficult to develop an exhaustive theory of harmonic replacements in the nonlinear scenario.

We highlight the fact that the regularity results put forth in this paper do not follow from the classical ones in the calculus of variations, since the integrand of the functional that we consider here is a discontinuous function, due to the presence of the term
\chi_{\{u>0\}}
(instead, the classical cases dealt with require the integrand to be continuous, or even Lipschitz continuous, see e.g. assumption (8.48) in
[
27
]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the notion of

p
-harmonic replacement and put forth some basic energy estimates needed in our main arguments.

Then, in Section
3
, we develop a dichotomy theory according to which, roughly speaking,
the average of the energy of an almost minimizer decreases in a smaller ball, unless we are arbitrarily close
to the case of linear functions.

In Section
4
we show that
almost minimizers of
J_{p}
are Lipschitz continuous, namely we prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
The
p
-harmonic replacement

One of the classical ingredients in nonlinear partial differential
equations is the notion of
p
-harmonic replacement, which we now recall:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
r>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
. We say that
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
is the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
if

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\,dx=\min_{u-w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla w\right|^{p}\,dx.

We remark that if
v
is the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
, then in particular it satisfies

(2.1)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla v(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,dx=0

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, namely
v
is a weak solution of
\Delta_{p}v=0

in
B_{r}(x_{0})
.

We now provide some energy estimates of classical flavor for the
p
-harmonic replacement
that we will use in the sequel.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r>0
and
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
.
Let
v
be the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
.
Then,

(i)

if
1<p<2,
then

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\right)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}},\end{split}

for some positive universal constant
C
;

(ii)

if
p\geqslant 2,
then

(2.3)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx,

for some positive universal constant
C
.

Proof.

For all
s\in[0,1]
, we consider the family of functions
u_{s}(x):=su(x)+(1-s)v(x)
.
Notice that
u_{0}=v
and
u_{1}=u
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|^{p}\,ds\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\int_{0}^{1}p\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{s}(x)\cdot\nabla(u-v)(x)\,ds\right)\,dx.

This and (
2.1
) (used here with
\varphi:=u-v
) lead to

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle p\,\left[\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{s}(x)\cdot\nabla(u-v)(x)\,ds\right)\,dx\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla v(x)\cdot\nabla(u-v)(x)\,dx\right)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{s}(x)-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla v(x)\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)(x)\,dx\bigg)\,ds.

We also point out that

(2.4)

u_{s}(x)-v(x)=s(u(x)-v(x)),

and therefore we arrive at

(2.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
=\;&p\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left(\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{s}(x)-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p-2}\nabla v(x)\right)\cdot\nabla(u_{s}-v)(x)\,dx\bigg)\,ds.\end{split}

Now we recall the inequality

\big(\left|\xi\right|^{p-2}\xi-\left|\zeta\right|^{p-2}\zeta\big)\cdot(\xi-\zeta)\geqslant\gamma\begin{cases}\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{2}\left(\left|\xi\right|+\left|\zeta\right|\right)^{p-2}&\mbox{if }1<p<2,\\
\left|\xi-\zeta\right|^{p}&\mbox{if }p\geqslant 2,\end{cases}

for any
\xi
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, for some
positive universal constant
\gamma
, see e.g. page 100 in
[
11
]
.
Thus, using this inequality with
\xi:=\nabla u_{s}
and
\zeta:=\nabla v

into (
2.5
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
\geqslant\;&\begin{cases}\displaystyle p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{2}(\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds,&\;\mbox{if }1<p<2,\\
\displaystyle p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds,&\;\mbox{if }p\geqslant 2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Furthermore, recalling (
2.4
), we conclude that

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
\geqslant\;&\begin{cases}\displaystyle p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{2}(\left|\nabla u_{s}(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds,&\;\mbox{if }1<p<2,\\
\displaystyle p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}^{p-1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds,&\;\mbox{if }p\geqslant 2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Now, if
1<p<2
, since
s\in[0,1]
we have that

\left|\nabla u_{s}\right|+\left|\nabla v\right|\leqslant s\left|\nabla u\right|+(1-s)\left|\nabla v\right|+\left|\nabla v\right|=s\left|\nabla u\right|+(2-s)\left|\nabla v\right|{\leqslant}2\,\big(\left|\nabla u\right|+\left|\nabla v\right|\big).

Hence, plugging this information into (
2.6
), we find that

(2.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\Big)\,dx\\
\geqslant\;&p\,\gamma\,2^{p-2}\int_{0}^{1}{s}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx\right)\,ds\\
=\;&p\,\gamma\,2^{p-3}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx.\end{split}

We now apply the Hölder’s inequality with Hölder exponent
2/p
and conjugate exponent

\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{2/p}{2/p-1}=\frac{2}{2-p},

to see that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{\frac{p(p-2)}{2}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{-\frac{p(p-2)}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\;

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{2}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p-2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

This and (
2.7
) yield that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}},

for some universal constant
C>0
, which proves the desired result in (
2.2
) when
1<p<2
.

If instead
p\geqslant 2
,
we deduce from (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant p\,\gamma\int_{0}^{1}{s}^{p-1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx,

which establishes (
2.3
) when
p\geqslant 2
and completes
the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

3.
Some dichotomy results

With the preliminary work carried out so far,
we can now provide a dichotomy statement. Roughly speaking,
either the average of the energy of an almost minimizer decreases in a smaller ball,
or the distance of its gradient and a suitable constant vector becomes as small as we wish
(that is, linear functions are the “only ones for which the average does not improve in small balls”).
The precise result goes as follows:

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

there exist
\eta\in(0,1)
,
M\geqslant 1
and
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending on
\varepsilon
,
n
and
p
, such that
if
\sigma\in[0,\sigma_{0}]
and
a\geqslant M
then the following statement holds true.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})

be such that

(3.1)

J_{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1})

for all
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{1}
, with

(3.2)

a:=\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Then, either

(3.3)

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\frac{a}{2},

or

(3.4)

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

with
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(3.5)

\frac{a}{4}<\left|q\right|\leqslant C_{0}\,a,

and
C_{0}>0
universal.

Proof.

Let
v
be the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1}
.
By Theorem 3.19 in
[
37
]
we have that, for every
x\in B_{1/2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{B_{1/4}(x)}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\leqslant C\fint_{B_{1/2}(x)}\left|\nabla v(y)\right|^{p}\,dy\leqslant C\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(y)\right|^{p}\,dy.

As a consequence, for all
x\in B_{1/2}
,

(3.6)

\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\leqslant C_{0}\,a,

for some positive universal constant
C_{0}
.

Accordingly, we denote by
q:=\nabla v(0)
and we deduce from (
3.6
)
that
|q|\leqslant C_{0}\,a
. This, together with Theorem 2 in
[
38
]
, gives that, for all
\eta\in(0,1/2]
,

(3.7)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla v(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\;&\fint_{B_{\eta}}\bigg(C\bigg(\frac{\eta}{1/2}\bigg)^{\alpha}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\bigg)^{p}\,dx\\
\leqslant\;&C\,\eta^{\alpha p}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}^{p}\\
\leqslant\;&C_{1}\,\eta^{\alpha p}\,a^{p},\end{split}

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C_{1}>0
universal.

Furthermore, using (
3.1
)
we have that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx&\leqslant J_{p}(u,B_{1})-\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1})-\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
&\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)\\
&\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)\end{split}

We distinguish two cases,
p\geqslant 2
and
1<p<2
.

If
p\geqslant 2
, then from (
2.3
) and (
3.8
)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right).

Taking the average over
B_{1}
, we thereby obtain that

\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C(\sigma a^{p}+1).

Using this and (
3.7
), we get that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla v(x)-q\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\\
&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\left|B_{1}\right|}{\left|B_{\eta}\right|}\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+C_{1}\,\eta^{\alpha p}\,a^{p}\right)\\
&\leqslant 2^{p-1}\Big(C\eta^{-n}(\sigma a^{p}+1)+C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big),\\
&=2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}\sigma a^{p}+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}+2^{p-1}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}.\end{split}

This yields that

(3.10)

\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}\left|q\right|^{p}.

If instead
1<p<2
, by virtue of (
2.2
)
and (
3.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\left(\sigma\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+1\right)\left(\int_{B_{1}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

Thus, since
v
is the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\;

\displaystyle C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+1\right)\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\right).

Consequently, taking the average integral, we have that

\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+|B_{1}|^{-\frac{p}{2}}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\right)\leqslant C\left(\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}\right).

From this and (
3.7
), we obtain that

(3.11)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{\eta}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla v(x)-q\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\\
\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}\left(\frac{\left|B_{1}\right|}{\left|B_{\eta}\right|}\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+C_{1}\,\eta^{\alpha p}\,a^{p}\right)\\
\leqslant\;&2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{p-1}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}.\end{split}

This gives that

(3.12)

\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}\left|q\right|^{p}.

Now,
given
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1/4]
, we claim that
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
there exists
\eta
small enough (depending on
\varepsilon
)
such that if
\sigma
is chosen sufficiently small and
a
sufficiently large (depending on
\eta
,
and thus on
\varepsilon
) then

(3.13)

\begin{cases}2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}\\
\mbox{if }p\geqslant 2,\\
\\
2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}\\
\mbox{if }1<p<2.\end{cases}

To prove this we distinguish two cases. If
p\geqslant 2,
we pick
\eta>0
sufficiently small
such that
\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta>2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}
. This allows us to define

M:=\left(\frac{2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}}{\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}}\right)^{1/p}.

Note also that we can suppose
M\geqslant 1
by taking
\eta
small enough.
Let also

\sigma_{0}:=\eta^{n+1}.

With this setting, we obtain that, for every
a\geqslant M
and for every
0<\sigma\leqslant\sigma_{0},

\displaystyle 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{p-1}\,M^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)+2^{p-1}\,a^{p}\Big(\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}+a^{p}\Big(2^{2(p-1)}C\eta+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}-2^{2(p-1)}C\eta-2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which proves (
3.13
) when
p\geqslant 2
.

If instead
1<p<2,
we pick
\eta>0
small enough such that
\varepsilon^{p}>2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}.
In this way, we can define

M:=\left(\frac{C2^{p-1}\eta^{-n}}{\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}}\right)^{2/p^{2}}.

Let also
\sigma_{0}:=\eta^{(n+1)\frac{2}{p}}.

Then, whenever
a\geqslant M
and
0<\sigma\leqslant\sigma_{0}
it follows that

\displaystyle 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}\sigma^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2^{2(p-1)}C\eta a^{p}+2^{2(p-1)}C\eta^{-n}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}+2^{2(p-1)}C_{1}a^{p}\eta^{\alpha p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}a^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+2^{p-1}C\eta^{-n}M^{-\frac{p^{2}}{2}}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}a^{p}\Big(2^{p-1}C\eta+\varepsilon^{p}-2^{p-1}C\eta-2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}+2^{p-1}C_{1}\eta^{\alpha p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},

which establishes (
3.13
) in the case
1<p<2
as well.

In order to complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
,
we now distinguish two cases according to the size of
\left|q\right|.
More precisely, we first suppose that

\left|q\right|\leqslant\frac{a}{4}.

Then, we use either (
3.10
) (if
p\geqslant 2
) or (
3.12
) (if
1<p<2
), and (
3.13
) to conclude that

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}+2^{p-1}\frac{a^{p}}{2^{2p}}=\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}+\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p+1}}=\frac{a^{p}}{2^{p}},

and thus

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\frac{a}{2},

which is the first alternative in (
3.3
).

Otherwise, it holds that

\frac{a}{4}<\left|q\right|\leqslant C_{0}\,a,

and therefore, by either (
3.9
) (if
p\geqslant 2
) or (
3.11
) (if
1<p<2
),
and (
3.13
), we have that

\left(\fint_{B_{\eta}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

which is the second alternative in (
3.4
).
The proof of Proposition
3.1
is thereby complete.
∎

We will now show that the alternative in (
3.4
)
can be “improved” when
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
are sufficiently small.
This result is the counterpart of Lemma 2.3 in
[
15
]
in the more general setting dealt with in this paper.
The main difficulty here with respect to Lemma 2.3 in
[
15
]
relies on the fact that
the problem is not linear when
p\neq 2
, and therefore,
if
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are the
p
-harmonic replacements of
u_{1}

and
u_{2}
in
B_{1}
, then it is not true that
v_{1}+v_{2}

is the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u_{1}+u_{2}
, unless
p=2
.

We will overcome this difficulty by showing that a “uniformly elliptic” equation
is still satisfied from the sum of
p
-harmonic replacements.
More precisely, we will show the following result:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
|\eta(x)|<\frac{|q|}{2}
.
Let
F(z):=|z|^{p-2}z
and

A(x):=\int_{0}^{1}DF\big(q+t\eta(x)\big)\,dt.

Then,

\lambda\,|q|^{p-2}\leqslant A\xi\cdot\xi\leqslant\Lambda\,|q|^{p-2},

for all
\xi\in\partial B_{1}
, for some
\Lambda\geqslant\lambda>0
, depending on
p
.

Proof.

We notice that, for all
t\in(0,1)
,

(3.14)

\begin{split}&|q+t\eta|\leqslant|q|+|\eta|<|q|+\frac{|q|}{2}=\frac{3|q|}{2}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad&|q+t\eta|\geqslant|q|-|\eta|>|q|-\frac{|q|}{2}=\frac{|q|}{2}.\end{split}

Furthermore, we observe that

DF(z)=(p-2)|z|^{p-4}z\otimes z+|z|^{p-2}{\text{Id}},

where
Id
denotes the identity matrix in dimension
n
, and therefore, for all
\xi\in\partial B_{1}
,

\displaystyle DF(z)\xi\cdot\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)|z|^{p-4}(z\cdot\xi)^{2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-(2-p)^{+}|z|^{p-2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[1-(2-p)^{+}\big]\,|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[1-(2-p)^{+}\big]\,|z|^{p-2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle A\xi\cdot\xi\geqslant\big[1-(2-p)^{+}\big]\int_{0}^{1}|q+t\eta|^{p-2}\,dt.

Accordingly, we can use (
3.14
) to find that

A\xi\cdot\xi\geqslant\frac{1-(2-p)^{+}}{2^{p-2}}\,|q|^{p-2}

if
p\geqslant 2
, and

A\xi\cdot\xi\geqslant\big[1-(2-p)^{+}\big]\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{p-2}|q|^{p-2}

if
p\in(1,2)
.

Similarly, since

\displaystyle DF(z)\xi\cdot\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(p-2)^{+}|z|^{p-2}+|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[1+(p-2)^{+}\big]\,|z|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[1+(p-2)^{+}\big]\,|z|^{p-2},

we have that

\displaystyle A\xi\cdot\xi\leqslant\big[1+(p-2)^{+}\big]\int_{0}^{1}|q+t\eta|^{p-2}\,dt.

Hence, making again use of (
3.14
),

A\xi\cdot\xi\leqslant\big[1+(p-2)^{+}\big]\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{p-2}|q|^{p-2}

if
p\geqslant 2
, and

A\xi\cdot\xi\leqslant\frac{1+(p-2)^{+}}{2^{p-2}}\,|q|^{p-2}

if
p\in(1,2)
.

These considerations complete the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

With this, we can now state the following result:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a_{1}>a_{0}>0
and

(3.15)

p>\max\left\{\frac{2n}{n+2},1\right\}.

There exist
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\widetilde{C}>0
, depending on

and

, such that
for every

there exist

•

\rho\in(0,1)
, depending on

,

and
\alpha
,

•

\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending on

,

,
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
and
\alpha
,

•

c_{0}>0
, depending on
n
,

,
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
and
\alpha
,

such that, if
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
\sigma\in(0,c_{0}\varepsilon^{P}]
, with

then the following statement holds true.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
be such that
u\geqslant 0
in

(3.16)

J_{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1})

for all

on

.

Let

(3.17)

a:=\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

and suppose that

(3.18)

a\in[a_{0},a_{1}].

(3.19)

\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

for some
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(3.20)

\frac{a}{8}<|q|\leqslant 2C_{0}a,

where

is the universal constant given by Proposition

.

Then

(3.21)

\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\widetilde{q}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{\alpha}\varepsilon a,

with
\widetilde{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(3.22)

\left|q-\widetilde{q}\right|\leqslant\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a.

The quantity
\alpha_{0}
in the statement of Lemma
3.3

measures the oscillation of

see
[
44
]
,
[
19
]
,
[

]
or, specifically for our case, Theorem 2 in
[

]
.
When

, we have that

, according to the full regularity of harmonic functions,
but when

in general we only know that

(but when

a sharp regularity exponent has been determined in

]
).

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\bar{v}
denote the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{9/10}
and
let
v
be defined as

(3.23)

v:=\begin{cases}\bar{v}&\mbox{in }B_{9/10},\\
u&\mbox{in }B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10}.\end{cases}

Then, since
v=u
on
\partial B_{1}
and
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
, we
can use (
3.16
) to see that

J_{p}(u,B_{1})\leqslant(1+\sigma)J_{p}(v,B_{1}).

Consequently,

\begin{split}J_{p}(u,B_{9/10})=\;&J_{p}(u,B_{1})-J_{p}(u,B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10})\\
\leqslant\;&J_{p}(v,B_{9/10})+J_{p}(v,B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10})+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1})-J_{p}(u,B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10})\\
=\;&J_{p}(v,B_{9/10})+J_{p}(u,B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10})+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1})-J_{p}(u,B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10})\\
=\;&J_{p}(v,B_{9/10})+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1}).\end{split}

Hence, recalling the definition of
J_{p}
in (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{9/10}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+\left|\left\{u>0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|\leqslant\int_{B_{9/10}}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx+\left|B_{9/10}\right|+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1}),

which yields that

(3.24)

\int_{B_{9/10}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\big)\,dx\leqslant\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1}).

Moreover, recalling (
3.23
), we point out that
v
is the
p
-harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{9/10}
, and therefore

(3.25)

\begin{split}J_{p}(v,B_{1})=\;&\int_{B_{1}}\Big(|\nabla v(x)|^{p}+\chi_{\{v>0\}}(x)\Big)\,dx\\
\leqslant\;&\int_{B_{9/10}}|\nabla v(x)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{9/10}}|\nabla v(x)|^{p}\,dx+|B_{1}|\\
\leqslant\;&\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\,dx+\left|B_{1}\right|\leqslant a^{p}+\left|B_{1}\right|.\end{split}

Furthermore, if
p\geqslant 2
, by (
2.3
)
and (
3.24
) we deduce that

\int_{B_{9/10}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|+C\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1}),

for some positive universal constant
C
.
Consequently, exploiting (
3.25
), we obtain that

(3.26)

\int_{B_{9/10}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|+C\sigma(a^{p}+1),

up to renaming
C
.

If instead
p<2
, we use (
2.2
), (
3.24
)
and (
3.25
)
to write that

(3.27)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{9/10}}\left|\nabla u(x)-\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\\
\leqslant\;&C\left(\int_{B_{9/10}}\left(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}-\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\right)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{9/10}}\big(\left|\nabla u(x)\right|+\left|\nabla v(x)\right|\big)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\\
\leqslant\;&C\Big(\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|+\sigma J_{p}(v,B_{1})\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{9/10}}2^{p}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\\
\leqslant\;&C\Big(\left|\left\{u=0\right\}\cap B_{9/10}\right|+\sigma(a^{p}+1)\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}a^{p\left(1-\frac{p}{2}\right)}.\end{split}

Now we claim that

(3.28)

\left|B_{9/10}\cap\left\{u=0\right\}\right|\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon^{p+\delta},

for some
C_{1}>0
and
\delta>0
.
To prove this, we consider the linear function

(3.29)

\ell(x):=b+q\cdot x,\quad{\mbox{ with }}\quad b:=\fint_{B_{1}}u(x)\,dx.

We remark that

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{1}}\big(u(x)-\ell(x)\big)\,dx=b-\left(b+\fint_{B_{1}}q\cdot x\,dx\right)=-\fint_{B_{1}}q\cdot x\,dx=0.

As a consequence, denoting by

(u-\ell)_{B_{1}}:=\fint_{B_{1}}\big(u(x)-\ell(x)\big)\,dx

and using the Poincaré inequality, we see that

\left\|u-\ell-(u-\ell)_{B_{1}}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=\left\|u-\ell\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leqslant C\left\|\nabla(u-\ell)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})},

for some
C>0
universal.

This and (
3.19
) entail that

(3.30)

\fint_{B_{1}}\left|u(x)-\ell(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla(u-\ell)(x)|^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}.

We also observe that, since
u\geqslant 0,
it holds that
\ell^{-}\leqslant\left|u-\ell\right|
.
Using this information into (
3.30
), we obtain that

(3.31)

\fint_{B_{1}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\leqslant C\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}.

Now we claim that, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small,

(3.32)

\ell\geqslant c_{1}a\quad\mbox{in }B_{9/10},

for some
c_{1}>0
. To check this, we argue by contradiction assuming that

\min_{x\in\overline{B_{9/10}}}\ell(x)<ca

for all
c>0
.
We notice that, for every
x\in B_{9/10}
,

-\frac{9|q|}{10}\leqslant\ell(x)-b\leqslant\frac{9|q|}{10}.

and therefore

ca>\min_{x\in\overline{B_{1/2}}}\ell(x)\geqslant b-\frac{9|q|}{10}.

This leads to

(3.33)

b\leqslant ca+\frac{9|q|}{10}.

Now we define

{\mathcal{B}}:=\left\{x=-\frac{tq}{|q|}+\eta,\;{\mbox{ for some }}t\in\left[\frac{38}{40},\frac{39}{40}\right]{\mbox{ and }}\eta\in B_{1/40}\right\}.

Notice that if
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
then

|x|\leqslant t+|\eta|<\frac{39}{40}+\frac{1}{40}=1,

and this shows that

(3.34)

{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq B_{1}.

Furthermore, from (
3.20
) and (
3.33
) we see that, if
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
,

\displaystyle\ell(x)=b-t|q|+\eta\cdot q\leqslant ca+\frac{9|q|}{10}-\frac{38|q|}{40}+\frac{|q|}{40}=ca-\left(\frac{38}{40}-\frac{9}{10}-\frac{1}{40}\right)|q|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=ca-\frac{|q|}{40}<ca-\frac{a}{320}.

Hence, taking
c\in\left(0,\frac{1}{640}\right)
, we infer that

\ell(x)\leqslant-\frac{a}{640}.

Accordingly, using this and (
3.34
) into (
3.31
), we obtain that

\displaystyle C\,|B_{1}|\,\varepsilon^{p}a^{p}\geqslant\int_{B_{1}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\geqslant\int_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\ell^{-}(x))^{p}\,dx\geqslant\int_{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(\frac{a}{80\cdot 2^{(3p+1)/p}}\right)^{p}\,dx\geqslant\overline{c}a^{p},

for some positive universal constant
\overline{c}
.
This establishes the desired contradiction if

Now, we distinguish the three following cases:

Then, by virtue of (

), (

) and (

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left|u(x)-\ell(x)\right|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{*}}\leqslant C\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla\big(u(x)-\ell(x)\big)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=C\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C\varepsilon a.

\displaystyle C\varepsilon a\geqslant\left(\int_{B_{9/10}\cap\left\{u=0\right\}}\left|\ell(x)\right|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{*}}\geqslant c_{1}a\left|B_{9/10}\cap\left\{u=0\right\}\right|^{1/p^{*}},

Now we notice that

Therefore, setting

(3.37)

\delta:=\frac{p^{2}}{n-p}>0,

we obtain (
3.28
) from (

)

.

If instead

we recall Theorem 7.17 as well as (7.12) and (7.16) in

, to see that

\sup\limits_{B_{1}}\left|u-\ell\right|\leqslant C\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla\big(u(x)-\ell(x)\big)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

for some

From this, and making again use of (

) and (

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{1}}\left|u-\ell\right|\leqslant C\left(\int_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla(u(x)-\ell(x))\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C\varepsilon a.

\displaystyle C\varepsilon a\geqslant\sup_{B_{1}}\left|u-\ell\right|\geqslant\sup_{B_{9/10}}(\ell-u)\geqslant\ell(x)-u(x)\geqslant c_{1}a-u(x),

) in the case

as well.

It remains to analyze the case

. For this purpose,
we point out that

Also, for all

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}a^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}\geqslant\int_{B_{1}}\left|u(x)-\ell(x)\right|^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}\,dx\geqslant\int_{B_{9/10}\cap\{u=0\}}\left|\ell(x)\right|^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geqslant c_{1}^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}a^{\frac{n^{2}}{n-1}}|B_{9/10}\cap\{u=0\}|.

We point out that

we establish (
3.28
) when

Gathering together (

) and (
3.28
)

(3.40)

Instead, when

and we obtain that

If instead

Recalling (

.

Similarly, if

, we take

, with

to be made precise later. In this case, we deduce from (

&\qquad\qquad\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon^{(p+\delta)\frac{p}{2}}a^{p}+C_{2}\varepsilon^{p}a^{p},\end{split}

up to renaming the constants.

We stress that we have not used the assumption on

given in (

) so far. We will use it
next to reabsorb the term

{\mbox{if\penalty\ $p\in\left(\max\left\{\frac{2n}{n+2},1\right\},\,2\right)$,\quad then }}(p+\delta)\frac{p}{2}>p.

Indeed, when

Also, if

Furthermore, we claim that, if

,

\left|\nabla v(x)-q\right|\leqslant C(\varepsilon a)^{\nu},

\max_{x\in\overline{B_{1/2}}}|\nabla v(x)-q|>C(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}

Then, for all

\big|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(\overline{x})\big|\leqslant\overline{C}|x-\overline{x}|^{\alpha},

for some

Hence, taking the divergence of both sides, we obtain that

\displaystyle\diverg\big(A(\nabla v-q)\big)=0\quad\mbox{ in }B_{9/10},

Notice that we are in the setting of Lemma

with

)

.

\nabla u\neq 0\quad{\mbox{in }}B_{1/2}.

Indeed, by (
3.59
), we have that, for all
x\in B_{1/2}
,

\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

which gives that

\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\geqslant|q|-\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|\geqslant\left|q\right|-C\varepsilon a.

As a consequence, from (
3.5
), we get

\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\geqslant\frac{a}{4}-C\varepsilon a>0,

as soon as
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, which yields (
3.61
).

Furthermore,

(3.62)

u>0\quad{\mbox{in }}B_{1/2}.

To check this, we recall that
u\geqslant 0
and we suppose by contradiction that
there exists a point
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
such that
u(x_{0})=0
. As a consequence, since
u\in C^{1,\gamma}(B_{1/2})
, we see that
\nabla u(x_{0})=0
, and this contradicts (
3.61
), thus proving (
3.62
).

In order to prove Corollary
3.4

we recall the definition of Campanato spaces and a result which we will use in the proof of the corollary,
see
[
27
]
.

Definition 3.6

(Campanato spaces)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

1\leqslant p<+\infty
and
\lambda\geqslant 0
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
the space of functions
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that

(3.63)

[u]^{p}_{\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{0}\in\Omega}}{{\rho>0}}}\rho^{-\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\rho}\right|^{p}\,dx<+\infty,

where

\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}:=\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad u_{x_{0},\rho}:=\fint_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}u(x)\,dx.

We point out that
the quantity
[u]_{\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)}
is a seminorm in
\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
and

(3.64)

{\mbox{it is equivalent to the quantity }}\quad\left(\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{0}\in\Omega}}{{\rho>0}}}\rho^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}\left|u(x)-\xi\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

We also define the norm in
\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)
as

(3.65)

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)}:=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(\Omega)}.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. Let
1\leqslant p<+\infty
and
\lambda\geqslant 0
.
Suppose that
n<\lambda\leqslant n+p
.

Then, the space
\mathcal{L}^{p,\lambda}(B_{r}(x))
is isomorphic to
C^{0,\alpha}(B_{r}(x)),
with
\alpha:=\frac{\lambda-n}{p}.

We also point out the following scaling property
of almost minimizers:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
u
be an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
B_{1}
with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
.
For any
r\in(0,1)
, let

(3.66)

u_{r}(x):=\frac{u(rx)}{r}.

Then,
u_{r}
is an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
B_{1/r}
with constant
\kappa r^{\beta}
and exponent
\beta
, namely

(3.67)

J_{p}(u_{r},B_{\varrho}(x_{0}))\leqslant(1+\kappa r^{\beta}\varrho^{\beta})J_{p}(v,B_{\varrho}(x_{0})),

for every ball
B_{\varrho}(x_{0})
such that
\overline{B_{\varrho}(x_{0})}\subset B_{1/r}
and for every
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{\varrho}(x_{0}))
such that
v=u_{r}
on
\partial B_{\varrho}(x_{0})
in the sense of the trace.

Proof.

By definition of almost minimizers, we know that

(3.68)

J_{p}(u,B_{\vartheta}(y_{0}))\leqslant(1+\kappa\vartheta^{\beta})J_{p}(w,B_{\vartheta}(y_{0}))

for every ball
B_{\vartheta}(y_{0})
such that
\overline{B_{\vartheta}(y_{0})}\subset B_{1/r}
and for every
w\in W^{1,p}(B_{\vartheta}(y_{0}))
such that
w=u
on
\partial B_{\vartheta}(y_{0})
in the sense of the trace.

Now, given
x_{0}\in B_{1/r}
, we take
\varrho
and
v
as in the statement of Lemma
3.8
and we define

(3.69)

w(x):=rv\left(\frac{x}{r}\right).

Then, using the notation
y_{0}:=rx_{0}
and
\vartheta:=r\varrho
,
for all
x\in\partial B_{\vartheta}(y_{0})
, we have that
\frac{x}{r}\in\partial B_{\varrho}(x_{0})
and therefore, in the sense of the trace,

w(x)=rv\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)=ru_{r}\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)=u(x).

Accordingly, we can use
w
as a competitor for
u
in (
3.68
),
thus obtaining that

(3.70)

\int_{B_{r\varrho}(y_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla u(y)\right|^{p}+\chi_{\left\{u>0\right\}}(y)\Big)\,dy\leqslant(1+\kappa r^{\beta}\varrho^{\beta})\int_{B_{r\varrho}(y_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla w(y)\right|^{p}+\chi_{\left\{w>0\right\}}(y)\Big)\,dy.

Furthermore, using, consistently with (
3.66
), the notation
w_{r}(x):=\frac{w(rx)}{r}
, with the change of variable
x:=\frac{y}{r}
we see that

(3.71)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r\varrho}(y_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla w(y)\right|^{p}+\chi_{\left\{w>0\right\}}(y)\Big)\,dy={r^{n}}\int_{B_{\varrho}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla w(rx)\right|^{p}+\chi_{\left\{w>0\right\}}(rx)\Big)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad=r^{n}\int_{B_{\varrho}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla w_{r}(x)\right|^{p}+\chi_{\left\{w_{r}>0\right\}}(x)\Big)\,dx\end{split}

and a similar identity holds true with
u
and
u_{r}
replacing
w
and
w_{r}
.

Also, recalling (
3.69
),
we observe that
v=w_{r}
. Plugging this information and (
3.71
)
into (
3.70
), we obtain the desired result in (
3.67
).∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4
.

Up to scaling, we suppose that

(3.72)

u
is an almost minimizer for
J_{p}
in
B_{2}
(with constant
\kappa
and exponent
\beta
).

We prove that we can iterate Lemma
3.3
indefinitely
with
\alpha:=\min\left\{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2},\frac{\beta}{P}\right\}
,
where

and
\alpha_{0}
is given in Lemma
3.3
.

More precisely, we claim that, for all
k\geqslant 0
, there exists
q_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(3.73)

\begin{split}&|q_{k}|\in\left[\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}},\,C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right],\\
&\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{{k\alpha}}\varepsilon a\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad&\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\in\left[\frac{|q|}{2},2|q|\right],\end{split}

where
\rho\in(0,1)
,
C_{0}>0
and
\widetilde{C}>0
are universal quantities.

To prove this, we argue by induction. When
k=0
, we pick
q_{0}:=q
. Then, in this case, the desired claims in (
3.73
) follow from (
3.5
), (
3.17
) and (
3.19
).

Now we perform the inductive step by assuming that (
3.73
) is satisfied
for
k
and we establish the claim for
k+1
. To this end, we let
r:=\rho^{k}
and utilize the rescaling
u_{r}
defined in (
3.66
). In this setting, the inductive assumption gives that

\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u_{r}(x)-q_{k}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant r^{\alpha}\varepsilon a=\varepsilon_{k}a,

with
\varepsilon_{k}:=r^{\alpha}\varepsilon=\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon
.

Notice that the inductive assumption also yields (
3.20
), as soon as
\varepsilon
is chosen conveniently
small. Therefore, thanks to Lemma
3.8
as well,
we are in position of using Lemma
3.3

on the function
u_{r}
with
\sigma:=\kappa r^{\beta}
.
We stress that the structural condition
\sigma\leqslant c_{o}\varepsilon^{P}
in Lemma

translates here into

, which is precisely the requirement in the statement of
Corollary
3.4
(by taking
\kappa_{0}
there less than or equal to

).
In this way, we deduce from (
3.21
) and (

)
that there exists
q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\begin{split}\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}\left|\nabla u_{r}(x)-q_{k+1}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{k}a\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\left|q_{k}-{q}_{k+1}\right|\leqslant\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a.\end{split}

Scaling back, we find that

\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k+1}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\rho^{\alpha}\rho^{{k\alpha}}\varepsilon a=\rho^{{(k+1)\alpha}}\varepsilon a.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle|q_{k+1}|\leqslant|q_{k}-q_{k+1}|+|q_{k}|\leqslant\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a+C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}+\widetilde{C}\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon a=C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}

and

\displaystyle|q_{k+1}|\geqslant|q_{k}|-|q_{k}-q_{k+1}|\geqslant\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}-\widetilde{C}\varepsilon_{k}a

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}-\widetilde{C}\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon a=\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-q_{k+1}\right|=\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left|\left(\int_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-q_{k+1}|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left|\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}-\left\|q_{k+1}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{|B_{\rho^{k+1}}|^{1/p}}\left\|\nabla u-q_{k+1}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{\rho^{k+1}})}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k+1}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\varepsilon a,

which yields that

\displaystyle\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-q\right|=\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-q_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left|\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k+1}}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}-q_{k+1}\right|+\sum_{j=0}^{k}|q_{j+1}-q_{j}|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\varepsilon a+\widetilde{C}a\sum_{j=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}\leqslant\varepsilon a+\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\rho^{j\alpha}=\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{C}}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right)\varepsilon a

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left(1+\frac{\widetilde{C}}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right)\varepsilon|q|\leqslant\frac{|q|}{2}.

These observations conclude the proof of the inductive step and establish (
3.73
).

By scaling the relations in (
3.73
)
(and recalling (
3.72
)), we have that, for all
x_{0}\in B_{3/4}
,

(3.74)

\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k,x_{0}}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C\rho^{k\alpha}\varepsilon a,

for some positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
and
p
, and suitable points

such that

|q_{k,x_{0}}|\in\left[\frac{a}{4}-\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}},\,C_{0}a+\frac{\widetilde{C}\varepsilon a\left(1-\rho^{k\alpha}\right)}{1-\rho^{\alpha}}\right].

We now want to exploit the Campanato estimate in Theorem
3.7
,
here applied to the gradient of
u
minus
q
,
and used with
\Omega:=B_{1/2}
and
\lambda:=n+\alpha p

(note that, with this choice,
\lambda\in(n,n+p]
and we are therefore under
the structural assumptions of Theorem
3.7
). For this purpose, we claim that, for all
x_{0}\in B_{3/4}
and
\tau>0
,

(3.75)

\left(\tau^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{B_{\tau}(x_{0})\cap B_{1/2}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q-\xi\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C\varepsilon a,

up to renaming

.

To prove this, we distinguish two cases, either
\tau\geqslant 1
or
\tau\in(0,1)
.

If
\tau\geqslant 1
, we use (
3.19
)
and we get that

\displaystyle\left(\tau^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{B_{\tau}(x_{0})\cap B_{1/2}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q-\xi\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\left(\int_{B_{1/2}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left(|B_{1}|\fint_{B_{1}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant|B_{1}|^{1/p}\,\varepsilon a,

which gives (
3.75

\displaystyle\left(\tau^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{B_{\tau}(x_{0})\cap B_{1/2}}\left|\nabla u(x)-q-\xi\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leqslant\left(\rho^{-\lambda(k+1)}\int_{B_{\rho^{k}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k,x_{0}}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\rho^{-\lambda(k+1)+kn}\,|B_{1}|\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)-q_{k,x_{0}}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

which gives (
3.75
) up to renaming

. The proof of (
3.75
)
is thereby complete.

By (

) and (

Now we define

Proof of Theorem

.

In the light of Lemma

, up to a rescaling,

(4.2)

)
sufficiently small, namely taking
s:=\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}\varepsilon_{0}^{P}}{2\kappa}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
.
Notice in particular that

(4.14)

s
depends on
n
,
p
,
\kappa
,

\beta
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
,

due to (
4.11
).

As a result of (
4.13
), for all
r\in\left(0,\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2}\right)
,

(4.15)

\begin{split}&a(r)=\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\bar{C}a(\eta^{k_{0}})\leqslant\bar{C}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\bar{C}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),\end{split}

as long as
C(M,\eta)\geqslant\bar{C}(C(\eta)M+1)
.

Moreover, if
r\in\left[\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2},\eta\right)
then we take
k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\eta^{k_{r}+1}<r\leqslant\eta^{k_{r}}
.
Note that

\frac{1}{\eta^{k_{r}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{r}\leqslant\frac{2}{\eta^{k_{0}+1}},

whence

(4.16)

k_{r}\leqslant k_{0}+C_{\star},

where
C_{\star}:=1+\frac{\log 2}{\log(1/\eta)}
.

We now distinguish two cases: if
k_{r}\in\{0,\dots,k_{0}\}
then
k_{r}\in{\mathcal{K}}
and therefore

a(\eta^{k_{r}})\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{r}}a(1).

From this we obtain that

(4.17)

\begin{split}&a(r)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\eta^{k_{r}+1}}|}\int_{B_{\eta^{k_{r}}}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\eta^{-n/p}a(\eta^{k_{r}})\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\eta^{-n/p}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{r}}a(1)\big)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)).\end{split}

If instead
k_{r}>k_{0}
, we employ (
4.16
) to see that

\displaystyle a(r)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\eta^{k_{0}+C_{\star}+1}}|}\int_{B_{\eta^{k_{0}}}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\eta^{-n(C_{\star}+1)/p}a(\eta^{k_{0}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\leqslant\eta^{-n(C_{\star}+1)/p}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k_{0}}a(1)\big)\leqslant\eta^{-n(C_{\star}+1)/p}\big(C(\eta)M+a(1)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),

as long as
C(M,\eta)
is chosen large enough.

This and (
4.17
) give that for all
r\in\left[\frac{\eta^{k_{0}+1}}{2},\eta\right)
,
we have that
a(r)\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1))
. Combining this with (
4.15
),
we deduce that (
4.3
) holds true, as desired.

Now, up to scaling and translations, we can extend (
4.3
) to all balls with center
x_{0}
in
B_{1/2}

and sufficiently small radius. Namely, we have that, for all
r\in(0,\eta]
,

a(r,x_{0})\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1)),

where

a(r,x_{0}):=\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Therefore, according to the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem, recalling that
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1}),
we find that, for all
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla u(x_{0})\right|=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}a(r,x_{0})\leqslant C(M,\eta)(1+a(1))=C\Big(1+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\Big),

for some
C>0
,
which yields

(4.18)

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C\Big(1+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\Big).

So, the first claim in Theorem
1.2
is proved.

We now show that the second claim in Theorem
1.2

holds true. For this, we can assume that

(4.19)

\{u=0\}\cap B_{s/100}\neq\varnothing

and we take a Lebesgue point
\bar{x}\in B_{s/100}
for
\nabla u
.
Up to a translation, we can suppose that
\bar{x}=0
and change our assumption (
4.19
) into

(4.20)

\{u=0\}\cap B_{s/50}\neq\varnothing.

We claim that, in this situation,

(4.21)

{\mathcal{K}}=\mathbb{N}.

Indeed, suppose not and let
k_{0}
as above (recall (
4.7
)).
Then, in light of (
4.10
),
we can apply Corollary
3.4
(rescaled as before)
and conclude, by (
3.62
), that
u>0

in
B_{s/2}
, in contradiction with our hypothesis in (
4.20
).
This establishes (
4.21
).

Therefore, in view of (
4.4
) and (
4.21
), for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

a(\eta^{k})\leqslant C(\eta)M+2^{-k}a(1),

and as a result

|\nabla u(\bar{x})|=|\nabla u(0)|=\lim_{k\to+\infty}a(\eta^{k})\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\big(C(\eta)M+2^{-k}a(1)\big)=C(\eta)M.

Recalling also (
4.14
),
the proof of the second claim in Theorem
1.2
is thereby complete.
∎

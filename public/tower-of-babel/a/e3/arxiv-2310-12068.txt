Abstract
. We define Quillen model structures on a family of presheaf toposes arising from tree unravellings of Kripke models, leading to a homotopy theory for modal logic. Modal preservation theorems and the Hennessy–Milner property are revisited from a homotopical perspective.



1.
Introduction

Many basic questions in (finite) model theory, and more generally in logic, can be phrased as
model existence
problems: is there a model satisfying a collection of (logically) specified properties? For instance, consider the problem of constructing an
\omega
-saturated elementary extension of a given structure
M
. A possible solution goes as follows: start from
M
and, using the Compactness Theorem for first-order logic, deduce the existence of an elementary extension
M_{1}
of
M
that is “saturated relative to
M
”; repeat this process, now starting from
M_{1}
, to obtain an elementary chain

M\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}\subseteq\cdots

whose union is an
\omega
-saturated elementary extension of
M
.

The approach based on the Compactness Theorem has some shortcomings: it is not applicable to finite model theory (since the Compactness Theorem fails over finite structures) and, more generally, it does not allow to properly handle logical resources, because we lose control over the combinatorial complexity of the objects involved. An alternative solution to the model existence problem is via the
model construction
method, which consists in explicitly constructing a model satisfying the desired properties. In the example above, we could construct an
\omega
-saturated elementary extension as an appropriate ultrapower of
M
.

In general, an object obtained by means of a model construction argument is overdetermined, in the sense that it is specified up to isomorphism and not up to equivalence with respect to the prescribed logical properties.
An analogous situation occurs in topology, when we are interested in studying a space up to homotopy equivalence, rather than up to homeomorphism.

The aim of this note is to make this analogy more precise in the case of modal logic, using the language of abstract homotopy theory (also referred to as
categorical homotopy
or
homotopical algebra
). To this end, after recalling the necessary preliminaries in §
2
, we define in §§
3
–
4
Quillen model structures on categories of presheaves closely related to (partial) tree unravellings of Kripke models. Arguably, this provides a flexible setting in which to manipulate objects up to logical equivalence and perform constructions that are invariant under such equivalences. To illustrate this idea, in §
5
we give a short proof of the Łoś-Tarski preservation theorem for modal logic and its relativisation to finite models
[
4
,
16
]
, and in §
6
we offer a homotopical view on the Hennessy–Milner property.

To the (abstract) homotopy theorist

The model categories we shall consider arise from Cisinski model structures on presheaf toposes. Thus, they are combinatorial model categories (i.e., locally presentable and cofibrantly generated), the cofibrations are the monomorphisms, and all objects are cofibrant.

From a logical perspective, a central question is whether, in these model categories, two objects can be connected by a span of trivial fibrations. If the answer is positive, we say that they are
Morita equivalent
. In view of Kenneth Brown’s Factorization lemma, combined with Whitehead theorem, Morita equivalence between fibrant objects reduces to homotopy equivalence.

To the (modal) logician

All results below can be extended to modal languages with any finite number of modal operators, but for simplicity we shall restrict ourselves to unimodal languages.
The presheaves we are interested in are defined over categories of traces, i.e. Kripke models in which the reflexive transitive closure of the accessibility relation is a finite linear order.

Whereas p-morphisms (i.e. functional bisimulations) are the natural notion of morphism in modal logic, in the categorical setting it is convenient to consider arbitrary homomorphisms (i.e. functional simulations).
A particularly useful class of homomorphisms is that of
pathwise embeddings
, the homomorphisms that also reflect the validity of atomic formulas.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Modal logic

We recall the basic concepts of (propositional) modal logic; further notions will be introduced when necessary. For a comprehensive treatment of the subject, see e.g.
[
5
]
.

The language of modal logic extends the language of classical propositional logic, based on propositional variables and Boolean connectives
\top
,
\bot
,
\wedge
,
\vee
and
\neg
, by adding modalities
\Box
and
\Diamond
. For each propositional variable
p
in the language, we consider a corresponding unary relation symbol
{P}
. Intuitively, the interpretation of
{P}
in a structure describes the subset of elements in which
p
is true (this viewpoint is based on the
standard translation
of modal logic into first-order logic, see e.g.
[
5
, §2.4]
). The structures in which modal formulas are interpreted are the Kripke models.

Definition 2.1

(Kripke model)
.

A
Kripke model
consists of

(i)

a set
A
equipped with a binary relation
\frown_{A}
, the
accessibility relation
,

(ii)

a subset
{P}^{A}\subseteq A
for each unary relation
{P}
in the language, and

(iii)

a distinguished element
a\in A
.

A
homomorphism of Kripke models

f\colon(A,\frown_{A},a)\to(B,\frown_{B},b)

is a function
f\colon A\to B
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

For all
x,y\in A
,
x\frown_{A}y
implies
f(x)\frown_{B}f(y)
.

(ii)

For all unary relations
{P}
and all
x\in A
,
x\in{P}^{A}
implies
f(x)\in{P}^{B}
.

(iii)

f(a)=b
.

The category of Kripke models and their homomorphisms is denoted by
\mathbb{K}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We can identify Kripke models as structures (in the usual model-theoretic sense) for a vocabulary containing a constant symbol, a binary relation, and a set of unary relations. A homomorphism of Kripke models then coincides with the usual notion of homomorphism between structures.

We systematically drop the subscript from the accessibility relation
\frown_{A}
and simply denote it by
\frown
. We also write
(A,a)
instead of
(A,\frown,a)
, or even just
A
if no confusion arises.

The interpretation of a modal formula
\varphi
in a Kripke model
(A,a)
is defined by structural induction on
\varphi
:

•

If
\varphi
is a propositional variable
p
, then
(A,a)\models p
just when
a\in{P}^{A}
.

•

The semantics of Boolean connectives is the usual one, for example
(A,a)\models\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}
just when
(A,a)\models\varphi_{1}
or
(A,a)\models\varphi_{2}
.

•

If
\varphi
is of the form
\Box\psi
, then
(A,a)\models\varphi
just when
(A,x)\models\psi
for
all

x\in A
such that
a\frown x
.

•

If
\varphi
is of the form
\Diamond\psi
, then
(A,a)\models\varphi
just when
(A,x)\models\psi
for
some

x\in A
such that
a\frown x
.

Note that the formula
\Box\varphi
is logically equivalent to
\neg\Diamond\neg\varphi
.

Next, we introduce modal depth, a complexity measure of modal formulas. Intuitively, it measures the maximum nesting of modalities in a formula.

Definition 2.3

(Modal depth)
.

The
(modal) depth
of a modal formula
\varphi
, written
\depth(\varphi)
, is defined inductively as follows:

•

If
\varphi
is either
\top,\bot
or a propositional variable
p
,
\depth(\varphi)=0
.

•

\depth(\neg\psi)=\depth(\psi)
.

•

\depth(\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2})=\max(\depth(\varphi_{1}),\depth(\varphi_{2}))=\depth(\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2})
.

•

\depth(\Box\psi)=\depth(\psi)+1=\depth(\Diamond\psi)
.

Definition 2.4

(Weak factorisation system)
.

A pair
({L},{R})
of classes of morphisms in a category

if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

Every arrow of
\C
can be factored as an arrow of

followed by an arrow of

(ii)

{L}={}^{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.7}{$\displaystyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\textstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxslash$}}}{R}

.

Definition 2.5

(Model category)
.

A
(Quillen) model structure
on a locally small and finitely bicomplete category
\C
is a triple
({W},{C},{F})
of classes of morphisms in
\C
satisfying the following two properties:

(i)

The class
{W}
has the two-out-of-three property. That is, given composable morphisms
u\colon X\to Y
and
v\colon Y\to Z
, if any two of
u,v
, and
vu
are in
{W}
, so is the third.

(ii)

({C}\cap{W},{F})
and
({C},{F}\cap{W})
are weak factorisation systems.

A
(Quillen) model category
is a category
\C
equipped with a model structure
({W},{C},{F})
.

If
({W},{C},{F})
is a model structure, we refer to the arrows in each of the three classes as the
weak equivalences
,
cofibrations
and
fibrations
, respectively. The arrows in
{C}\cap{W}
are called
trivial cofibrations
, and those in
{F}\cap{W}

. Cofibrations and fibrations are denoted, respectively, by
\rightarrowtail
and
\twoheadrightarrow
, and weak equivalences by

. E.g.,
\mathrel{\vphantom{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\displaystyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\textstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}}
denotes a trivial fibration.

Definition 2.6

(Fibrant object)
.

An object
X
of a model category
\C
is
fibrant
if the unique morphism
!\colon X\to\mathbf{1}
to the terminal object is a fibration.

Remark 2.7
.

The data defining a model structure is overdetermined: any two of the classes
{W}
,
{C}
and
{F}
determine the third. For example, an arrow is a weak equivalence just when it can be factored as a trivial cofibration followed by a trivial fibration.

In fact, by a result of Joyal, a model structure on a category is completely determined by its cofibrations together with its class of fibrant objects; see
[
11
, Proposition E.1.10]
or
[
15
, Theorem 15.3.1]
.

Definition 2.8

(Quillen adjunction)
.

Let
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
be Quillen model categories. A
Quillen adjunction
is an adjoint pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\C}
\top
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\C^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

such that the left adjoint
F
preserves cofibrations and the right adjoint
G
preserves fibrations. A
right Quillen functor
is a functor
G
admitting a left adjoint
F
such that the pair
(F,G)
is a Quillen adjunction.

Given an adjoint pair of functors
(F,G)
between model categories,
F
preserves cofibrations just when
G
preserves trivial fibrations, and
F
preserves trivial cofibrations just when
G
preserves fibrations. In particular,
(F,G)
is a Quillen adjunction if, and only if,
F
preserves trivial cofibrations and
G
preserves trivial fibrations; cf. e.g.
[
7
, Remark 2.3.8]
.

3.
Tree unravellings and the presheaf categories
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}

3.1.
Unravellings

We start by recalling the technique of
unravelling
, of central importance in modal logic, which originates from
[
8
,
17
]
.
Henceforth,
k
denotes an ordinal that is either finite or equal to
\omega
.

For any Kripke model
(A,a)
, we consider its
tree unravelling to depth
k
starting from
a
, denoted by
R_{k}(A,a)
. When
k=\omega
, this is usually referred to as the
full unravelling
of
A
.
R_{k}(A,a)
is the Kripke model whose elements are non-empty sequences
[a_{0},\ldots,a_{j}]
of length at most
k
of elements of
A
such that:

(i)

a_{0}=a
, and

(ii)

a_{\ell}\frown a_{\ell+1}
for all
0\leq\ell<j
.

The accessibility relation on
R_{k}(A,a)
is defined as follows: for any two sequences
s,t\in R_{k}(A,a)
,
s\frown t
holds just when
t
is obtained by making one further step along
\frown
. That is,
s\frown t
if, and only if, there exist
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
such that
s=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}]
,
t=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},a_{n}]
and
a_{n-1}\frown a_{n}
.
For each unary relation
{P}
, its interpretation in
R_{k}(A,a)
consists of the sequences whose last element is in
{P}^{A}
. Finally, the distinguished element of
R_{k}(A,a)
is the one-element sequence
[a]
.

Note that (the reflexive transitive closure of) the accessibility relation on
R_{k}(A,a)
is a tree order of height at most
k
whose least element, also called the
root
, is the distinguished element
[a]
. That is,
R_{k}(A,a)
is a so-called synchronization tree:

Definition 3.1

(Synchronization tree)
.

A Kripke model
(C,c)
is a
synchronization tree
if, for all
x\in C
, there is a unique finite sequence
c_{0}\frown\cdots\frown c_{n}
of elements of
C
with
c_{0}=c
and
c_{n}=x
. The full subcategory of
\mathbb{K}
consisting of the synchronization trees is denoted by
\mathbb{T}
.

Any synchronization tree
(C,c)
carries a “definable” tree order, in the sense that the reflexive transitive closure of its accessibility relation is a tree order. For every
x\in C
, we write
{\uparrow}x
and
{\downarrow}x
, respectively, for the up-set and down-set of
x
in this order.

Let
\mathbb{T}_{k}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathbb{K}
defined by the synchronization trees of height at most
k
(in particular,
\mathbb{T}_{\omega}=\mathbb{T}
). The assignment
(A,a)\mapsto R_{k}(A,a)
extends to a functor
R_{k}\colon\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{T}_{k}
sending a homomorphism of Kripke models
f\colon(A,a)\to(B,b)
to

R_{k}f\colon R_{k}(A,a)\to R_{k}(B,b),\ \ [a_{0},\ldots,a_{j}]\mapsto[f(a_{0}),\ldots,f(a_{j})].

Lemma 3.2
.

The functor
R_{k}
is right adjoint to the inclusion
\mathbb{T}_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\top}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{k}}

In other words,
\mathbb{T}_{k}
is a coreflective subcategory of
\mathbb{K}
.

Definition 3.3

(Pathwise embedding)
.

A homomorphism of Kripke models
f\colon A\to B
is a
pathwise embedding
if it reflects the unary relations, i.e. for all
x\in A
and all unary relation symbols
{P}
in the language,
f(x)\in{P}^{B}
implies
x\in{P}^{A}
. The wide subcategory of
\mathbb{T}_{k}
defined by the pathwise embeddings is denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{k}
.

The notion of pathwise embedding admits a logical reading. Recall that a modal formula is
existential
if it does not contain the modality
\Box
and all subformulas in the scope of a negation symbol are propositional variables. With this terminology, the next lemma follows e.g. from the axiomatic result in
[
2
, Proposition 6.18]
(a concrete proof will appear in the forthcoming
[
1
]
):

Lemma 3.4
.

The following statements are equivalent for all Kripke models
A,B\in\mathbb{K}
and all finite ordinals
k
:

(1)

There exists a pathwise embedding
R_{k}A\to R_{k}B
.

(2)

For all existential modal formulas
\varphi
of depth at most
k
,
A\models\varphi
entails
B\models\varphi
.

Recall that an injective homomorphism between Kripke models is an
embedding
(also called a
strong
injective homomorphism) if it reflects both the unary relations and the accessibility relation.
Every embedding is a pathwise embedding, but the converse need not hold.
An example is provided by the retraction
A+A\to A
, for any
A\in\mathbb{K}
, that is the identity on each summand. However, a pathwise embedding between synchronization trees is an embedding provided it is injective:

Lemma 3.5
.

The following statements are equivalent for any arrow
f
in
\mathbb{S}_{k}
:

(1)

f
is a monomorphism.

(2)

f
is injective.

(3)

f
is an embedding.

Proof.

(1)

\Rightarrow

(2)
Suppose
f\colon A\to B
is a monomorphism in
\mathbb{S}_{k}
and
f(x)=f(y)
for some
x,y\in A
. Since
f
is a pathwise embedding, the substructures of
A
with universes
{\downarrow}x
and
{\downarrow}y
, respectively, are isomorphic; just observe that they are both isomorphic to the substructure of
B
with universe
{\downarrow}f(x)={\downarrow}f(y)
. Thus there are
C\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
and embeddings
m,n\colon C\rightarrowtail A
such that the image of
m
is
{\downarrow}x
, and the image of
n
is
{\downarrow}y
. Then
f(x)=f(y)
implies
f\circ m=f\circ n
, and since
f
is a monomorphism in
\mathbb{S}_{k}
we get
m=n
. This shows that
x=y
, and so
f
is injective.

(2)

\Rightarrow

(3)
It is enough to prove that every injective morphism
{f\colon A\to B}
in
\mathbb{T}_{k}
reflects the relation
\frown
. Suppose that
x,y\in A
satisfy
f(x)\frown f(y)
, and let
n
be the height of
f(x)
in the tree order of
B
. If
x^{\prime}
is the unique element of height
n
below
y
, then
x^{\prime}\frown y
entails
f(x^{\prime})\frown f(y)
. But
f(x)
is the unique element covered by
f(y)
, and so
f(x)=f(x^{\prime})
. As
f
is injective, we get
x=x^{\prime}
and so
x\frown y
.

(3)

\Rightarrow

(1)
Clear, since every embedding is injective.
∎

3.2.
Presheaves over paths

In general, the categories
\mathbb{S}_{k}
are not complete nor cocomplete. In fact, if the modal vocabulary contains at least one unary relation symbol, then
\mathbb{S}_{k}
does not admit an initial object nor a terminal one. In this subsection, we will consider an embedding into a presheaf category over a forest order as a means to (co)complete
\mathbb{S}_{k}
.

The presheaves we are interested in are defined on categories of synchronization trees whose tree order is a finite chain. We shall refer to the latter as
paths
. For simplicity, we shall work with a small skeleton of the class of paths (e.g., by considering only those paths whose universes are initial segments of the natural numbers). We write
\mathbb{P}_{k}
for the (small) full subcategory of
\mathbb{S}_{k}
whose objects are paths in this skeleton. Note that
\mathbb{P}_{\omega}=\bigcup_{k<\omega}{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
.

Remark 3.6
.

The category
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is a poset, since there is at most one pathwise embedding between any two paths. In fact, every homomorphism of Kripke models whose domain is a path is injective, so the morphisms in
\mathbb{P}_{k}
are precisely the embeddings by Lemma
3.5
. Therefore,
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is a forest order and it has height
k
.

The reason for considering the category
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is that the inclusion
\mathbb{P}_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{k}
is
dense
. This is the content of the next proposition, which can be understood as exhibiting the presheaf category

\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}\coloneqq[\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}},\Set]

as a (co)completion of the category
\mathbb{S}_{k}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

The restricted Yoneda embedding

\mathbb{S}_{k}\to\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}},\ \ A\mapsto\left(\mathbb{S}_{k}(-,A)\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Set\right)

is full and faithful.

Proof.

This follows from a straightforward adaptation of the observation that the full subcategory of
\mathbb{T}_{k}
defined by the paths is dense in
\mathbb{T}_{k}
. Just observe that pathwise embeddings correspond precisely to (mediating morphisms induced by) cocones of embeddings on diagrams of paths.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

For any
A\in\mathbb{S}_{k}
, the presheaf
\mathbb{S}_{k}(-,A)\colon\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Set
sends a path
P
to the set of embeddings
P\rightarrowtail A
.

By virtue of Proposition
3.7
, we will identify
\mathbb{S}_{k}
, and thus also
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, with a full subcategory of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
. The idea, developed in the following sections, is that we can give a homotopy theoretic account of some fundamental constructions in modal logic by considering model structures on the presheaf categories
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
.

4.
A model structure on
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}

As in the previous section, we fix an arbitrary ordinal
k\leq\omega
. Further, we let
\Pi
be the set of all embeddings between paths in
\mathbb{S}_{k}
.
We shall now define a model structure on the presheaf category
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
; in view of Remark
2.7
, it suffices to specify what the cofibrations and the fibrant objects in this model structure are.

Proposition 4.1
.

The category
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
admits a model structure such that:

(i)

The cofibrations are precisely the monomorphisms and they coincide with the class
{}^{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.7}{$\displaystyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\textstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxslash$}}}(\Pi^{\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.7}{$\displaystyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\textstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptstyle\boxslash$}}{\scalebox{0.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\boxslash$}}})
.

(ii)

A presheaf
X\in\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
is fibrant just when it satisfies the following property: For all arrows
i\colon P\to Q
in
\Pi
, if there exists an arrow
Q\to X
in
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{k}}
then every arrow

5.
A preservation theorem for modal logic

Recall that a first-order sentence
\varphi
is
preserved under extensions
if, for any structure
A
, and any induced substructure
B
of
A
,

B\models\varphi\ \Longrightarrow\ A\models\varphi.

Note that
\varphi
is preserved under extensions just when, for all embeddings between structures
A^{\prime}\rightarrowtail A
, if
A^{\prime}\models\varphi
then
A\models\varphi
; in the following, we shall tacitly make use of (the modal version of) this equivalent characterisation.

The Łoś-Tarski preservation theorem for first-order logic states that a first-order sentence is preserved under extensions if, and only if, it is equivalent to an existential sentence
[
13
,
18
]
. Recall that a first-order sentence is said to be
existential
if it does not contain universal quantifiers and all subformulas in the scope of a negation symbol are atomic formulas.

A modal version of the Łoś-Tarski preservation theorem, to the effect that a modal formula is preserved under extensions of Kripke models just when it is equivalent to an existential modal formula, was proved by van Benthem; cf.
[
4
]
. In analogy with the case of first-order logic, a modal formula is
existential
if it does not contain the modality
\Box
and all subformulas in the scope of a negation symbol are propositional variables.

An alternative proof of the modal preservation theorem was offered by Rosen
[
16
]
. The advantage of Rosen’s approach is twofold: it applies uniformly both to the class of finite Kripke models and to the class of all Kripke models, and in the process it shows that the depth of formulas (i.e., the maximum number of nested modalities) is preserved.

We give a proof of Rosen’s result based on the homotopical approach introduced in §
4
.

Theorem 5.1
.

A modal formula of depth
\leq k
is preserved under extensions of Kripke models if, and only if, it is equivalent to an existential modal formula of depth
\leq k
. Further, the result relativises to finite Kripke models.

Proof.

It is easy to see that an existential modal formula is preserved under extensions of (finite) Kripke models.

To prove the converse, we rephrase the first part of the statement. Assume that
\varphi
is a modal formula of depth
k
preserved under extensions, and let
\Mod(\varphi)
be the full subcategory of
\mathbb{K}
consisting of the Kripke models that satisfy
\varphi
. Let
\Rrightarrow^{\exists}_{k}
be the preorder on Kripke models defined as follows:
A\Rrightarrow^{\exists}_{k}B
if, and only if,
B
satisfies all modal formulas of depth
\leq k
satisfied by
A
. A standard argument shows that
\varphi
is equivalent to an existential modal formula of depth
\leq k
just when
\Mod(\varphi)
is upwards closed with respect to
\Rrightarrow^{\exists}_{k}
. Suppose that
A\in\Mod(\varphi)
and
A\Rrightarrow^{\exists}_{k}B
. Since
A
and
R_{k}A
satisfy the same model formulas of depth
\leq k
, and similarly for
B
and
R_{k}B
, it is enough to prove that
R_{k}A\in\Mod(\varphi)
entails
R_{k}B\in\Mod(\varphi)
.

By Lemma
3.4
, the condition
A\Rrightarrow^{\exists}_{k}B
is equivalent to the existence of a pathwise embedding
f\colon R_{k}A\to R_{k}B
.
In view of Proposition
4.9
,
f
can be decomposed into a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration

R_{k}A\rightarrowtail X\mathrel{\vphantom{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\displaystyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\textstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ\mkern 1.0mu$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow$\cr}}}}R_{k}B

. By Lemma

is an embedding and

To see that the result holds also relative to finite Kripke models, note that

∎

Let us say that a class of synchronization trees

6.
The Hennessy–Milner property

We shall write
A\equiv^{\mathrm{ML}}B
to indicate that Kripke models
A
and
B
have the same modal theory, i.e.
A\models\varphi
if and only if
B\models\varphi
for all modal formulas
\varphi
.
Any two bisimilar Kripke models have the same modal theory, but the converse is not true. The Hennessy–Milner property holds for a class of Kripke models if the restriction of the equivalence relation
\equiv^{\mathrm{ML}}
to that class coincides with the bisimilarity relation:

Definition 6.1

(Hennessy–Milner property)
.

A class
{H}
of Kripke models has the
Hennessy–Milner property
if any two
A,B\in{H}
satisfying
A\equiv^{\mathrm{ML}}B
are bisimilar.

In this section we will see that the Hennessy–Milner property can be understood, from a homotopical standpoint, as stating the existence of global sections for certain presheaves of Morita equivalences (see Theorem
6.6
).

For any

, consider the chain of inclusions of partial unravellings

\R A\ \ \coloneqq\ \ R_{1}A\to R_{2}A\to R_{3}A\to\cdots

We denote by

the “ordinal category” generated by the graph

and regard

as an object of the functor category
[\bbomega,\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{\omega}}]
. In order to give a homotopical account of the Hennessy–Milner property, we will consider a model structure on
[\bbomega,\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{\omega}}]
induced by that of
\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{\omega}}
; this is known as the
projective model structure
.
3
3

3

In this case, the projective model structure coincides with the

obtained by viewing

as a Reedy category, cf. e.g.
[
15
, Exercise 14.2.9]

For the next result, let us say that an arrow

Proposition 6.2
.

The category
[\bbomega,\widehat{\mathbb{P}_{\omega}}]
admits a model structure in which the fibrations and weak equivalences are, respectively, the levelwise fibrations and levelwise weak equivalences.

Proof.

This follows e.g. from

, using the fact that

is a cofibrantly generated model category (cf. Appendix

∎

Remark 6.3
.

In general, the cofibrations in the model structure on

described in Proposition

are not the levelwise cofibrations. However, they can be characterised, as in any model category, as those arrows with the left lifting property with respect to all trivial fibrations.

The following statements are equivalent for any two Kripke models

By Theorem

, the diagrams

are levelwise Morita equivalent just when

; in turn, this is equivalent to

. Further, any Kripke model

4

4

.

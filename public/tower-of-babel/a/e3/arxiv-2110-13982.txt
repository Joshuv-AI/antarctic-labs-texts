Global well-posedness for a system of quasilinear wave equations on a product space
Cécile Huneau, Annalaura Stingo

Abstract
. We consider a system of quasilinear wave equations on the product space ℝ 1 + 3 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} , which we want to see as a toy model for Einstein equations with additional compact dimensions. We show global existence for small and regular initial data with polynomial decay at infinity. The method combines energy estimates on hyperboloids inside the light cone and weighted energy estimates outside the light cone.



1.
Introduction

In the present article we address the problem of global existence of small solutions to a certain class of quasilinear systems of wave equations on the product space
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The system under consideration has the following form

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&\Box_{x,y}u+u\partial_{y}^{2}u=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\textbf{N}_{1}(w_{i},w_{j})\\
&\Box_{x,y}v+u\partial_{y}^{2}v=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\textbf{N}_{2}(w_{i},w_{j})\end{cases}\qquad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

with initial conditions set at time
t_{0}=2

(1.2)

(u,v)(2,x,y)=(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})(x,y),\qquad(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v)(2,x,y)=(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(x,y).

In the above system
\Box_{x,y}=-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x}+\partial_{y}^{2}
denotes the D’Alembertian operator in the
(t,x,y)
variables where
t\in\mathbb{R}
is the time coordinate,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are the Cartesian coordinates and
y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the periodic coordinate.
The nonlinearities
\textbf{N}_{1}(\cdot,\cdot),\textbf{N}_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
are linear combination of the following quadratic null forms:

(1.3)

\displaystyle Q_{0}(\phi,\psi)=\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\psi-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi

\displaystyle Q_{ij}(\phi,\psi)=\partial_{i}\phi\,\partial_{j}\psi-\partial_{i}\phi\,\partial_{i}\psi\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq 3

\displaystyle Q_{0i}(\phi,\psi)=\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{i}\psi-\partial_{i}\phi\,\partial_{t}\psi\qquad 1\leq i\leq 3

where
\partial_{j}
denotes the derivative
\partial_{x_{j}}
in the
j
th direction for
j=\overline{1,3}
, and
w_{1},w_{2}=\{u,v\}
.

The main result we present in this paper asserts the global existence of solutions to (
1.1
) when the initial data are small and localized real functions. Our result also extends to semilinear interactions of the form
\partial_{\alpha}w_{i}\cdot\partial_{y}w_{j}
with
\partial_{\alpha}
being any of the derivatives in the
(t,x,y)
-variables.

1.1.
Motivation and a brief history

Our interest in studying nonlinear wave equations on product spaces comes from the theory of supergravity (SUGRA) in physics and more precisely from the Kaluza-Klein theory, which represents
the classical approach to the unification of general relativity with electromagnetism and more generally with gauge fields
(see the orignal works of Kaluza
[
13
]
and Klein
[
18
]
).
The Kaluza-Klein approach considers general relativity in
3+1+d
dimensions with space-time factorizing as

\mathscr{M}^{(3+1+d)}=\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times K

where
K
is a compact
d
-manifold referred to as
internal space
.
In the simplest case
d=1
dimensional gravity is compactified on a circle (
K=\mathbb{S}^{1})
to obtain at low energies a coupled Einstein-Maxwell-Scalar system in 3+1 space-time dimensions.
Kaluza-Klein space-times
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
have been studied by Witten in his influential work
[
39
]
, where he proves instability at the semiclassical level but provides heuristic arguments for classical stability.
The first result proving the classical stability of the Kaluza-Klein theory is obtained by Wyatt in
[
40
]
, where only perturbations depending on the non-compact coordinates are considered, using tools developed by Lindblad and Rodninaski
[
23
]
. More general spacetimes with supersymmetric compactifications
\mathscr{M}=\mathbb{R}^{1+n}\times K
have recently been studied by Andersson, Blue, Wyatt, and Yau in
[
2
]
. The space-times
\mathscr{M}
are equipped with the metric

\hat{g}=\eta_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}+k

where
\eta_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}
is the Minkowski metric in
\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
and
k
is such that
(K,k)
is a compact Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold having a cover that admits a spin structure and a nonzero parallel spinor. A global stability result is proved in
[
2
]
under the assumption
n\geq 9
and for Cauchy data that are Schwarzschild near infinity, but it is conjectured that these conditions can be relaxed and that space-times with a supersymmetric compactification and
n=3
are nonlinearly stable.

In both the aforementioned works
[
40
]
and
[
2
]
, as well as in many other works concerning the global stability problem for Einstein equations, the use of the so-called
wave-coordinates
allows one to write the Einstein equations as a system of quasilinear wave equations on the metric
g=(g^{\alpha\beta})_{\alpha\beta}

(1.4)

g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}=P_{\mu\nu}(\partial g,\partial g)+G_{\mu\nu}(g)(\partial g,\partial g)

where the sum is taken over repeated indices,
P_{\mu\nu}
are quadratic forms and
G_{\mu\nu}(g)(\partial g,\partial g)
contain cubic terms.
In the present paper we focus on a toy model for the Einstein equations on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
that only keeps a selection of terms from equation (
1.4
), precisely the semilinear terms with null structure and the quasilinear term
g^{yy}\partial^{2}_{y}
where
y
is the periodic coordinate on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Our goal here is in fact to study the global well-posedness for quasilinear wave equations on the product space
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
without having to make use of the full structure of the Einstein equations. The unknown
u
in system (
1.1
) plays the role of the coefficient
g_{yy}
and the unknown
v
encodes any other metric coefficient
g_{\mu\nu}
.

We mention here that wave equations on product spaces also appear in other contexts, for instance when studying the propagation of waves along infinite homogeneous waveguides (see
[
22
,
29
,
30
,
8
]
).

One key observation when studying the small data global well-posedness problem for wave equations on product spaces
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
is that they are closely related to infinite systems coupling wave and Klein-Gordon equations on the flat space
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
. In fact, if
W=W(t,x,y)
is solution to

\Box_{x,y}W=F\,\qquad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}

for some source term
F
, its Fourier modes in the periodic direction
\{W_{k}(t,x)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
solve the following equations

(-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x})W_{k}-|k|^{2}W_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{-iy\cdot k}Fdy,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}.

In particular, the zero-mode
W_{0}
is solution to a wave equation and any other non-zero mode
W_{k}
solves a Klein-Gordon equation of mass
|k|
, all on the flat space
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
.

The global well-posedness for systems coupling a finite number of wave and Klein-Gordon equations on the flat 3+1 space-time with small data have largely been studied. We cite the initial results by Georgiev
[
9
]
and Katayama
[
14
]
, followed by LeFloch and Ma
[
19
,
20
]
, Wang
[
37
,
38
]
and Ionescu and Pausader
[
12
,
11
]
who study such systems as a model for the full Einstein-Klein-Gordon equations. In
[
19
,
37
]
global well-posedness is proved for compactly supported initial data and quadratic quasilinear nonlinearities that satisfy some suitable conditions, including the
null condition
of Klainerman
[
16
]
for self-interactions between the wave components of the solution. An idea used in these works is that of employing hyperbolic coordinates
in the forward light cone; this was first introduced by Klainerman
[
15
]
for Klein-Gordon equations and Tataru in the wave context
[
33
]
, and later reintroduced by LeFloch and Ma in
[
19
]
under the name of
hyperboloidal foliation method
.
In
[
12
]
global regularity and scattering is proved in the case of small smooth initial data that decay at a suitable rate at infinity and nonlinearities that do not verify the null condition but present a particular resonant structure. We also cite the work by Dong and Wyatt
[
6
]
, who prove global well-posedness for a quadratic semilinear interaction in which there are no derivatives on the massless wave component. Other related results are
[
3
,
31
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
17
,
5
]
and see
[
24
,
28
,
26
,
25
,
32
,
10
,
7
,
27
]
for results about wave-Klein-Gordon systems in lower dimensions.

Our goal in this paper is to prove the global stability for (
1.1
) in the case where the initial data are not compactly supported but only have a mild polynomial decay at infinity. Our approach makes use of the vector field method by Klainerman
[
4
]
and follows LeFloch and Ma
[
20
]
in that a big portion of the estimates recovered for the solution in the interior of the cone
\{t=r+1\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
are estimates on hyperboloids. The main difference with
[
19
]
is that the interior estimates need to be coupled with exterior estimates in the region outside the cone. Those are weighted energy estimates that we have to propagate in time.

1.2.
The main result

In order to describe the initial data we consider for our problem we introduce the energy space
\mathscr{H}^{0}
endowed with the norm

\left\|(u[t],v[t])\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}^{0}}:=\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{xy}}+\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{xy}}+\|v_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

and the higher order energy spaces
\mathscr{H}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
endowed with the norm

\left\|(u[t],v[t])\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}^{n}}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\left\|(\partial^{\alpha}_{xy}u[t],\partial^{\alpha}_{xy}v[t])\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}^{0}}

where we use the following notation for the Cauchy data in (
1.2
) at time
t
:

(u[t],v[t]):=(u(t),u_{t}(t),v(t),v_{t}(t)).

The global well-posedness result that is the object of this paper is proved under some decay assumptions on the initial data.
A premilinary version of our main theorem states the following

Theorem 1
.

Assume the initial data
(u[2],v[2])
for (
1.1
) satisfy

\sum_{k=0}^{5}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{\leq k+{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\partial^{k}_{xy}(u[2],v[2])\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{0}}\leq\epsilon\ll 1,

for some positive fixed
\alpha
and
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
.
Then the system (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in the space
\mathscr{H}^{5}
.

We remark here that our choice to set the initial data at time
t_{0}=2
over the conventional
t_{0}=0
is more convenient for our computations and comes at no expense as the system (
1.1
) is invariant under time translations.

1.3.
The wave-Klein-Gordon structure

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be written in a more compact form as a vector equation for the unknown
W=(u,v)^{T}

(1.5)

\Box_{x,y}W+u\partial^{2}_{y}W=\mathbf{N}(W,W)

with data

(1.6)

W|_{t=2}=\Phi_{0},\qquad\partial_{t}W|_{t=2}=\Phi_{1}

where
\Phi_{0}=(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})^{T}
and
\Phi_{1}=(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})^{T}
and

\mathbf{N}(W,W)=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{N}_{1}(w_{i},w_{j})\\
\textbf{N}_{2}(w_{i},w_{j})\end{pmatrix}.

The projection of
W
onto the periodic direction
y
reveals the nature of the equation (
1.5
) as a system coupling one (vector) wave equation with an infinite sequence of (vector) Klein-Gordon equations of mass
|k|
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
. If we denote by
W_{k}=(u_{k},v_{k})^{T}
the projection of
W
onto the
k
-th frequency

W_{k}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{-iky}W(t,x,y)\,dy,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}

we see that the functions
\{W_{k}\}_{k}
satisfy the following coupled system

\begin{cases}(-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x})W_{k}-|k|^{2}(1+u_{0})W_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{-iky}\mathbf{N}(W,W)\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{-iky}(u-u_{0})\partial^{2}_{y}W\,dy\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

The zero mode
W_{0}
is solution to a wave equation while any other non-zero mode
W_{k}
is solution to a nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation of mass
|k|
. For our analysis this distinction will be fundamental and we will often work throughout the paper with the following decomposition of
W

(1.7)

W=W_{0}+\mathtt{W},\qquad\mathtt{W}(t,x,y)=\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{iky}W_{k}(t,x),

so that the equation (
1.5
) is equivalent to the following system

(1.8)

\begin{cases}(-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x})W_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W,W)\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{y}\mathtt{u}\,\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}\,dy\\[5.0pt]
(-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x})\mathtt{W}+(1+u)\,\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}=\mathbf{N}(W,W)-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W,W)\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{y}\mathtt{u}\,\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}\,dy.\end{cases}

We are now able to state a more precise version of the main theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that for some positive fixed
\alpha
the initial data for (
1.5
) satisfy

\sum_{k=0}^{5}\left\|x^{\leq k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial^{k}_{xy}W[2]\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{0}}\leq\epsilon\ll 1.

Then the solution
W
to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) exists globally in time in
\mathscr{H}^{5}
and the two components of the solution
W_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}W\,dy
and
\mathtt{W}=W-W_{0}
satisfy the following pointwise bounds

|\partial^{j}_{tx}W_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad j=\overline{1,3}

\left\|\partial^{j}_{y}\partial^{k}_{tx}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}},\quad j=\overline{0,1},\,k=\overline{0,1},

\left\|\partial^{j}_{y}\partial^{2}_{tx}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon\langle t+|x|\rangle^{-1}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad j=\overline{0,1}.

1.4.
Vector Fields

In order to describe the global bounds and decay properties of the solution
W=(u,v)^{T}
to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) we need to introduce the family of Killing vector fields associated to our problem. Those are the vector fields that exactly commute with
\Box_{x,y}
:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},\partial_{y}

(1.10)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}=x_{j}\partial_{i}-x_{i}\partial_{j}\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq 3

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0i}=t\partial_{i}+x_{i}\partial_{t}\qquad i=\overline{1,3.}

The expressions in (
1.9
) correspond to the translations in the coordinate directions; (
1.10
) correspond to the Euclidean rotations in the
x
coordinates; (
1.11
) are the hyperbolic rotations, also called boosts.
We also introduce the conformal scaling vector field

(1.12)

\mathscr{S}=t\partial_{t}+x\cdot\nabla_{x}

which is not Killing for (
1.5
) but will appear later in the analysis of the problem.
We refer to (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) as
Klainerman vector fields
and generally denote them by
Z
:

Z:=\{\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{0i}\}.

We denote the full set of admissible vector fields for
\Box_{x,y}
as

(1.13)

\mathscr{Z}:=\{\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},\partial_{y},\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{0i}\}

and for any multi-index
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
we denote

\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}=\partial^{\alpha}Z^{\beta}.

For any two non-negative integers
k,n
with
k\leq n
we say that the multi-index
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
is of type
(n,k)
if

|\gamma|=|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq n
and
|\beta|\leq k
, in other words if there are at most
k
Klainerman vector fields among the
|\gamma|
admissible vector fields in the product
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}
.

1.5.
The null structure

The nonlinearities we consider in this work are linear combination of the classical quadratic null forms (
1.3
). An important feature of the null forms is that they are combination of three types of products and can be expressed schematically as follows

(1.14)

\mathbf{N}(\phi,\psi)=\overline{\partial}\phi\cdot\partial\psi+\partial\phi\cdot\overline{\partial}\psi+\frac{t-r}{t}\partial\phi\cdot\partial\psi

where
\overline{\partial}_{j}=t^{-1}\Omega_{0j}
are the rescaled hyperbolic rotations, or also as

(1.15)

\mathbf{N}(\phi,\psi)=\mathscr{T}\phi\cdot\partial\psi+\partial\phi\cdot\mathscr{T}\psi

where
\mathscr{T}_{j}=\partial_{j}+\frac{x_{j}}{r}\partial_{t}
for
j=\overline{1,3}
are the vector fields tangent to the cones
\{t-r=const\}
. The
\mathscr{T}
vector fields are related to the boosts in general via the following relation

\mathscr{T}_{j}=\frac{1}{t}\Omega_{0j}+\frac{(t-r)}{t}\frac{x_{j}}{r}\partial_{t}.

We will often make use of the two representations (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) to recover suitable pointwise and energy estimates for the solution as they allow to recover additional decay for the interactions
W_{0}\times W_{0}
.

1.6.
The interior and exterior region

The proof of our main theorem is based on the combination of a classical local existence result with a bootstrap argument.
We will perform such argument separately in the two regions in which we decompose our space-time:

\text{interior region}\qquad\mathscr{D}^{\text{in}}:=\left\{(t,x):t\geq 2\text{ and }|x|<t-1\right\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

\text{exterior region}\qquad\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}:=\left\{(t,x):t\geq 2\text{ and }|x|\geq t-1\right\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}.

1.7.
The energy functionals

In the interior region we aim to propagate a-priori energy bounds on the solution on the truncated hyperboloids
\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}
.
The interior energy functional on
\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}
associated to the linear counterpart of equation (
1.5
) is

(1.16)

\displaystyle E^{\text{in}}(s,W)

\displaystyle:=\iint_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{2}|\partial_{t}W|^{2}+|\overline{\partial}W|^{2}+|\partial_{y}W|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{2}|\partial_{x}W|^{2}+|\mathscr{S}W|^{2}+t^{-2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}|\Omega_{ij}W|^{2}+|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\,dxdy

where
|\overline{\partial}W|^{2}
stands for
\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\overline{\partial}_{i}W|^{2}
.
The above functional can be decomposed as follows

E^{\text{in}}(t,W)=E^{\text{in}}(t,W_{0})+E^{\text{in}}(t,\mathtt{W})

where
E^{\text{in}}(t,\mathtt{W})
is defined by replacing
W
with
\mathtt{W}
in (
1.16

\displaystyle E^{\text{in}}(s,W_{0})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{2}|\partial_{t}W_{0}|^{2}+|\overline{\partial}W_{0}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{2}|\partial_{x}W_{0}|^{2}+|\mathscr{S}W_{0}|^{2}+t^{-2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}|\Omega_{ij}W_{0}|^{2}\,dx.

We also introduce and work with the conformal energy functional on truncated hyperboloids associated to the linear flat wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
. This is the functional defined as follows

(1.17)

E^{\text{c,in}}(s,W_{0}):=\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left|KW_{0}+2tW_{0}\right|^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{t^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{3}|\Omega_{0i}W_{0}|^{2}\,dx,

where
K=(t^{2}+r^{2})\partial_{t}+2rt\partial_{r}
is the Morawetz multiplier.

1.5

(1.18)

E^{\text{ex},\alpha}(t,W)=\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha+1}\left[|\partial_{t}W|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}W|^{2}+|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\right]\,dxdy

X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}

(1.19)

\left\|W\right\|^{2}_{X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}}:=\sup_{t\in[2,T]}E^{\text{ex},\alpha}(t,W)+(1+\alpha)\int_{2}^{T}\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\left(|\mathscr{T}W|^{2}+|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\right)\,dxdydt

\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\mathscr{T}_{i}W|^{2}

In the above energy functionals we have

1.8.
The quasilinear energies

Equation (
1.5
) is quasilinear and in order to propagate both the interior and exterior a-priori energy bounds for the solution we need to consider a cubic modification of the energies introduced in the previous subsection. Such
quasilinear energies
are defined as follows

E^{\text{in}}_{\text{quasi}}(s,W):=E^{\text{in}}(s,W)+\iint_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}u|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\,dxdy

E^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{\text{quasi}}(t,W):=E^{\text{ex},\alpha}(t,W)+\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}u|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\,dxdy

E^{\text{ex},h}_{\text{quasi}}(s,W):=E^{\text{ex},h}(s,W)+\iint_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{ex}}_{s}}u|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\,dxdy.

We also introduce the quasilinear modification of the stronger norm
X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}

\|W\|^{2}_{X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{\text{quasi},T}}:=\sup_{t\in[2,T]}E^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{\text{quasi}}(t,W)+(1+\alpha)\int_{2}^{T}\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\left(|\mathscr{T}W|^{2}+(1+u)|\partial_{y}W|^{2}\right)\,dxdydt.

We immediately observe that under smallness assumptions on
u
, e.g.
|u|\leq 1/10
,
our starting energies are equivalent to their corresponding quasilinear counterparts, i.e. for any
E=\{E^{\text{in}},E^{\text{ex},\alpha},E^{\text{ex},h}\}

\frac{9}{10}E(t,W)\leq E_{\text{quasi}}(t,W)\leq\frac{11}{10}E(t,W).

The same holds true for the stronger norms
X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}
and
X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{\text{quasi},T}
.

1.9.
Higher order norms

We use the vector fields introduced before to define the higher order counterparts of the energy functionals and of the stronger exterior norm
X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}

E_{n}(s,W):=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq n}E(s,\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W),\qquad E=\{E^{\text{in}},E^{\text{ex},\alpha},E^{\text{ex},h}\}

\|W\|_{X^{n,\alpha}_{T}}:=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq n}\|\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W\|_{X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T}}.

The higher order energies of
W_{0}
and
\mathtt{W}
are defined analogously. We observe that the above higher order energies control the high Sobolev regularity of the solution in the interior and exterior region respectively and also keep track of the
Z

\mathtt{W}

E^{\text{in}}_{n,k}(s,\mathtt{W}):=\sum_{\mathscr{I}_{n,k}}E(s,\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}),

where

denotes the set of indices of type

We finally introduce the higher order counterparts of the conformal energy functionals (

E^{\text{c,in}}_{n}(s,W_{0}):=\sum_{|\beta|\leq n}E^{\text{c,in}}(s,Z^{\beta}W_{0}),\qquad E^{\text{c,ex}}_{n}(t,W_{0}):=\sum_{|\beta|\leq n}E^{\text{c,ex}}(s,Z^{\beta}W_{0}).

2.
Overview of the proof

The proof of our main theorem is based on the combination of a classical local well-posedness result for equation (
1.5
) with a bootstrap argument. We will perform this argument separately in the interior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{in}}
and the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
in which we divide the space-time
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

The bootstrap assumptions in the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
are uniform-in-time energy bounds on the higher order stronger norm
X^{5,\alpha}_{T_{0}}
of the solution
W
, for any fixed
\alpha>0
:

(2.1)

\|W\|^{2}_{X^{5,\alpha}_{T_{0}}}\leq 2C^{2}_{0}\epsilon^{2}.

Such bound in particular implies the existence of some function
l\in L^{1}([2,T_{0}])
such that

(2.2)

\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\left(|\mathscr{T}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W|^{2}+|\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W|^{2}\right)\,dxdy\leq 2C^{2}_{0}\epsilon^{2}l(t).

The result we want to prove in the exterior region is the following

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
sufficiently large and a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that, for every
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
if
W=(u,v)^{T}
is a solution to (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) in an interval
[2,T_{0}]
and satisfies the energy bounds (
2.1
) then actually

\left\|W\right\|^{2}_{X^{5,\alpha}_{T_{0}}}\leq C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}.

In the above proposition the time
T_{0}
is arbitrary, therefore the solution
W
exists globally in
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
and satisfies the energy bound (
2.1
) for all times
T_{0}>2
. In particular, one has that

(2.3)

\|\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W\|_{X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{\infty}}:=\lim_{T_{0}\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W\|_{X^{\text{ex},\alpha}_{T_{0}}}\leq 2C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}.

The bootstrap assumptions in the interior region are higher order energy bounds on hyperboloids for
W_{0}
and
\mathtt{W}
and pointwise bounds on the
Z
derivative of

(2.4)

E^{\text{in}}_{5}(s,W_{0})\leq 2A^{2}\epsilon^{2}

(2.5)

E^{\text{in}}_{5,k}(s,\mathtt{W})\leq 2A^{2}\epsilon^{2}s^{2\delta_{k}},\qquad k=\overline{0,5}

(2.6)

|ZW_{0}(t,x)|\leq 2B\epsilon t^{-1}s^{\sigma}

for all

,

, and

fixed. Here

, the parameters

0<\sigma\ll\delta_{k}\ll\delta_{k+1}
for

, and

and

are large universal constants which we will improve as a part of the conclusion of the proof.
The result we want to prove in this region requires the global exterior energy bounds (
2.3
) and can be stated as follows

Proposition 2.2
.

There exist two constants
A,B>0
sufficiently large,
0<\epsilon_{0},\sigma,\delta_{k}\ll 1
sufficiently small with
\delta_{0}=0
and
\sigma\ll\delta_{k}\ll\delta_{k+1}
for

such that, for every
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
if
W=(u,v)^{T}
is a solution to (
1.5
)-(

) in the region

and satisfies the global exterior energy bounds (

) as well as the interior bounds (
2.4
)- (

) for all
s\in[2,s_{0}]
, then it actually satisfies the enhanced interior bounds

E^{\text{in}}_{5}(s,W_{0})\leq A^{2}\epsilon^{2}

E^{\text{in}}_{5,k}(s,\mathtt{W})\leq A^{2}\epsilon^{2}s^{2\delta_{k}},\qquad k=\overline{0,5}

|ZW_{0}(t,x)|\leq B\epsilon t^{-1}s^{\sigma}

for all
s\in[2,s_{0}]
and all
(t,x)\in\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{[2,s_{0}]}
.

In the above proposition the hyperbolic time
s_{0}
is arbitrary which implies the global existence of the solution in the interior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{in}}
.
The reason why we distinguish between the energies of
W_{0}
and of
\mathtt{W}
and do not propagate uniform-in-time energy bounds for the latter is related to some slow decaying semilinear terms that only appear in the equations satisfied by the differentiated function
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}
. One of such terms is for instance
Z^{\beta}u_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}
, which appears when
\gamma
is a multi-index of type
(k,k)
, i.e. when
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}=Z^{\beta}
is a pure product of Klainerman vector fields. The
L^{2}_{xy}
norm of such product can only be controlled in terms of the (square root of the) conformal energy
E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(s,W_{0})
, but even assuming the following sharp bounds

E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(s,W_{0})\lesssim\epsilon^{2}s\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s}}|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}

we are not able to recover a better
L^{2}
bound than the following one, which is at the limit of integrability and prevents us from obtaining uniform in time bounds for the higher order energies of
\mathtt{W}

\left\|Z^{\beta}u_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s})}\leq E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(s,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{s})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}s^{-1}.

These problematic terms do not appear in the equation for
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W_{0}
, for which we can instead easily prove uniform in time energy bounds using the null structure.

In both the interior and exterior region the booststrap argument is performed in two classical steps. The first step consists in recovering pointwise bounds for the solution. A first set of estimates is obtained from the a-priori energy bounds using Klainerman-Sobolev inequalities on hyperboloids in the interior region and weighted Sobolev embeddings on constant time slices in the exterior region. The pointwise bounds obtained for
\mathtt{W}
in the interior region with this approach are not optimal due to the slow growth in time assumed in (
2.5
), therefore more suitable bounds need to be recovered by directly analyzing the equation satisfied by
\mathtt{W}
.
Since Klainerman-Sobolev inequalities only yield pointwise bounds for derivatives of
W_{0}
, we will also need to recover
L^{\infty}-L^{\infty}
estimates for
W_{0}
using the equation it satisfies.

The second step of the bootstrap argument consists in writing a higher order (interior and exterior respectively) energy inequality for the solution and in using the pointwise bounds previously obtained to perturbatively estimate the cubic terms appearing in the right hand side of such inequality. As appears from the example briefly mentioned above, we also need to recover higher order conformal energy bounds for
W_{0}
from the a-priori energy bounds. This is especially important in order to propagate the interior energy bounds (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
3
we derive the energy inequalities for the linearized equation, both in the exterior and the interior region. We also derive the conformal energy inequalities. In Section
4
we prove the global existence of the solution in the exterior region. In Section
5
we recover the pointwise estimates for
\mathtt{W}
and
W_{0}
. Finally in Section
6
we improve the energy estimates in the interior region, concluding the proof of Theorem
1
.

\displaystyle\|\partial\mathtt{u}(t,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

for some small

(3.11)

E^{\text{in}}(s,\mathbf{W})\lesssim E^{\text{in}}(2,\mathbf{W})+\iint_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}}|\mathscr{T}\mathbf{W}|^{2}+|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}|^{2}\,d\sigma dydt+\int_{2}^{s}\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}(\tau,\mathbf{W})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tau

for all

,
where
d\sigma
is the surface element on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Proof.

We integrate the equality (

) in the case where

over the region

(see picture

) for any

\displaystyle E^{\text{in}}_{\text{quasi}}(s,\mathbf{W})\leq E^{\text{in}}_{\text{quasi}}(2,\mathbf{W})+\iint_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}}|\mathscr{T}\mathbf{W}|^{2}+(1+u)|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}|^{2}\,d\sigma dydt

\displaystyle+\int_{2}^{s}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)\partial_{y}u\,\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau/t)\partial_{t}u\,(\partial_{y}\mathbf{W})^{2}\,dxdyd\tau+\int_{2}^{s}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)\mathbf{F}\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\,dxdyd\tau.

The integral on the null boundary

From the assumption (

\left|\int_{2}^{s}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)\partial_{y}u\,\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau/t)\partial_{t}\mathtt{u}\,(\partial_{y}\mathbf{W})^{2}\,dxdyd\tau\right|\\

while from (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{2}^{s}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau}}\frac{1}{2}(\tau/t)\partial_{t}u_{0}\,(\partial_{y}\mathbf{W})^{2}\,dxdyd\tau\right|

Finally, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\left|\int_{2}^{s}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau}}(\tau/t)\mathbf{F}\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\,dxdyd\tau\right|\leq\int_{2}^{s_{0}}\|\mathbf{F}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}(\tau,\mathbf{W})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tau.

4.
Global existence in the exterior region

The main goal of this section is to prove the global existence of solutions
(u,v)

1.1

This result will follow immediately from the proof of proposition

This section is organized as follows: in subsections

4.1.
Weighted Sobolev inequalities

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For any sufficiently smooth function
w
we have

(4.1)

\sup_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}\,(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}|w(t,x,y)|^{2}\\
\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}(\partial_{r}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}w)^{2}+(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}(\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}w)^{2}\,dxdy.

Proof.

Let
(r,\sigma)
be the spherical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
r=|x|
and
\sigma=x/|x|\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
We begin by observing that the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
implies

\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}|w(t,r,\sigma,y)|^{2}\leq\sum_{0\leq l+k\leq 2}\int|\nabla_{\sigma}^{l}\partial_{y}^{k}w(t,r,\sigma,y)|^{2}d\sigma dy.

We then remark that for any function
v
and
(t,x,y)\in\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\Big[(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}\,v(t,x,y)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=2(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}\,v\partial_{r}v+\beta(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}r^{2}v^{2}+2(2+r-t)^{\beta}rv^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}v\partial_{r}v+\beta(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}r^{2}v^{2},

so if
v
is compactly supported in
x
we can write

(4.2)

\displaystyle(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}\,v(t,x,y)^{2}=-\int_{r}^{\infty}\partial_{\rho}\big[(2+\rho-t)^{\beta}\rho^{2}\,v(t,x,y)^{2}\big]d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\int_{r}^{\infty}(2+\rho-t)^{\beta}|v\partial_{\rho}v|\rho^{2}d\rho+\int_{r}^{\infty}(2+\rho-t)^{\beta-1}v^{2}\rho^{2}d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\int_{r}^{\infty}(2+\rho-t)^{\beta+1}(\partial_{\rho}v)^{2}\,\rho^{2}d\rho+\int_{r}^{\infty}(2+\rho-t)^{\beta-1}v^{2}\rho^{2}d\rho.

By replacing
v
with
\nabla_{\sigma}^{l}\partial_{y}^{k}w(t,r,\sigma,y)
for
k+l\leq 2
we obtain (
4.1
) in the case where
w
is compactly supported.
In the general case where
w
is not compactly supported we consider a cut-off function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and apply the inequality (
4.1
) to
\chi(\epsilon r)w
for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}\,(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}|\chi(\epsilon r)w|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k+l\leq 2}\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}(\partial_{r}\nabla_{\sigma}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}w)^{2}+(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}(\nabla_{\sigma}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}w)^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\sum_{k+l\leq 2}\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}\epsilon^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon r)|^{2}(\nabla_{\sigma}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}w)^{2}\,dxdy.

On the intersection of
\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}
with the support of
\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon r)
we have that
(2+r-t)^{2}\epsilon^{2}\lesssim 1
so

\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}\epsilon^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon r)|^{2}(\nabla_{\sigma}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}w)^{2}\,dxdy\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}(\nabla_{\sigma}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}w)^{2}\,dxdy.

By letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
we derive (
4.1
) also in the case of non compactly supported
w
. ∎

Slight modifications of the above proof yield the following three results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For a sufficiently regular function
w
we have

(4.3)

\sup_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}\,(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}|w(t,x,y)|^{2}\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}\Big((\partial_{r}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}w)^{2}+(\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}w)^{2}\Big)dxdy.

Proof.

It follows by estimating
v
and
\partial_{\rho}v
in (
4.2
) with the same weight and using the fact that
2+r-t\geq 1
on
\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For any sufficiently regular function
w
we have

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\sup_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}\,(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}\|w(t,r,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}(\partial_{r}w)^{2}+(2+r-t)^{\beta-1}w^{2}\,dxdy.\end{split}

Proof.

The inequality (
4.4
) follows by replacing
v
with the
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
norm of
w
in both left and right hand sides of inequality (

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. For any sufficiently regular function
w
we have

(4.5)

\sup_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}\,(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}\left\|w(t,r,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}(\partial^{\leq 1}_{r}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 1}w)^{2}\,dxdy.

The inequality (
4.5

and

in (

∎

4.2.
Weighted Hardy inequalities

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\beta>-1
. For any compactly supported function
w
we have

\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}w^{2}dxdy\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}(\partial_{r}w)^{2}dxdy.

Proof.

A simple computation shows that for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
(t,x,y)\in\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}

\partial_{r}\left[r^{2}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}\right]=2r(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}+(\beta+1)r^{2}(2+r-t)^{\beta}\geq(\beta+1)r^{2}(2+r-t)^{\beta}.

Consequently, if
\beta>-1
and
w
is a compactly supported function

\displaystyle\int_{r\geq t-1}(2+r-t)^{\beta}w^{2}dx=\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}r^{2}w^{2}drd\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\leq\frac{1}{\beta+1}\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1})w^{2}drd\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt=-\frac{2}{\beta+1}\int_{r\geq t-1}(2+r-t)^{\beta+1}w\partial_{r}w\ r^{2}drd\sigma-\frac{1}{\beta+1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}w^{2}r^{2}d\sigma\Big|_{r=t-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\lesssim\left(\int_{r\geq t-1}(2+r-t)^{\beta}w^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{r\geq t-1}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}(\partial_{r}w)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The result of the lemma follows then after further integration on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\beta>-1
and
Z
be any Klainerman vector field. For a sufficiently regular function

we have

\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}(Zw)^{2}dxdy\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}(\partial Z^{\leq 1}w)^{2}dxdy.

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
and any fixed cut-off function
\chi
we apply lemma
4.5
to
\chi(\epsilon r)Zw
and use the fact that
|Zw|\lesssim r|\partial u|
on
\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}
. Since

is bounded on the support of
\chi(\epsilon r)
we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta}(\chi(\epsilon r)Zw)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}|\chi(\epsilon r)|^{2}(\partial_{r}Zw)^{2}\,dxdy+\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}\epsilon^{2}|\chi^{\prime}(\epsilon r)|^{2}(Zw)^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}(\partial_{r}Zw)^{2}dxdy+\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\beta+2}(\partial w)^{2}dxdy

and obtain the result after letting
\epsilon\to 0
.
∎

4.4.

In order to prove proposition

The equation satisfied by

is obtained by commuting

(4.11)

(-\partial^{2}_{t}+\Delta_{x})W^{\gamma}+(1+u)\partial^{2}_{y}W^{\gamma}=\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}

where the inhomogeneous term

(4.12)

\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}=-\delta_{\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|=|\gamma|\\
|\gamma_{2}|<|\gamma|\end{subarray}}u^{\gamma_{1}}\partial^{2}_{y}W^{\gamma_{2}}+\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|\leq|\gamma|}\textbf{N}(W^{\gamma_{1}},W^{\gamma_{2}})

where

Lemma 4.8
.

The nonlinear term
\mathbf{N}(\cdot,\cdot)

Therefore, assuming
\epsilon\ll 1
sufficiently small we get that

\displaystyle\sup_{[2,T_{0}]}E^{\text{c,ex}}_{4}(t,W_{0})+\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 4}\int_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,T_{0}]}}\hskip-10.0pt\left|(t+r)(\partial_{t}W^{\gamma}_{0}+\partial_{r}W^{\gamma}_{0})+2W^{\gamma}_{0}\right|^{2}+(t-r)^{2}(|\nabla W^{\gamma}_{0}|^{2}-(\partial_{r}W^{\gamma}_{0})^{2})\,d\sigma dt

\displaystyle\lesssim E^{\text{c,ex}}_{4}(2,W_{0})+C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln T_{0}

so the result of the proposition follows from the smallness of the conformal energy at the initial time, which in turn follows from the assumptions on the initial data.

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
s\geq 2
and
2<T_{1}<T_{2}
be such that the portion of hyperboloid
\mathscr{H}_{s}

)-(

) in

). Then there exists constants

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 4}\int_{\mathscr{H}_{s}\cap[T_{1},T_{2}]}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left|KW^{\gamma}_{0}+2tW^{\gamma}_{0}\right|^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{t^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{3}\left|\Omega_{0j}W^{\gamma}_{0}\right|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{1}C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln T_{1}+C_{2}C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln\left(\frac{T_{2}}{T_{1}}\right)

4.5.
A sharper pointwise bound for the non-zero modes

The scope of this section is to show that the pointwise bound (
4.8
) for
\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}W
and
\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}\mathtt{W}
obtained by Sobolev’s injections can be improved and that one can replace
\sqrt{l(t)}
by the explicit decay in time
t^{-1/2}
.

Proposition 4.12
.

We have the following pointwise estimate in the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}

\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}W|+|\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}\mathtt{W}|\lesssim C_{0}\epsilon r^{-1}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(2+r-t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Proof.

Thanks to lemma
4.2
and the Poincaré inequality we have that

(2+r-t)^{\alpha}r^{2}\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left(|\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}W|^{2}+|\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 2}\mathtt{W}|\right)\leq\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}|\partial^{\leq 1}\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 4}W|^{2}\,dxdy,

so it will be enough to prove that

I(t):=\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}|\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W|^{2}\,dxdy\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}t^{-1},\quad t\in[2,\infty).

We fix
t\in[2,\infty)
and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
t\in[2^{k},2^{k+1})
.
Since
I\in L^{1}([2,\infty))
there exists
\tau_{k}\in[2^{k},2^{k+1}]
such that

I(\tau_{k})\lesssim 2^{-k}\lesssim t^{-1}.

Observe that in the above inequalities the implicit constants are independent of
k
. We can assume
\tau_{k}<t
(the case
\tau_{k}>t
being similar) and use the fundamental theorem of calculus to write that

|I(t)-I(\tau_{k})|\leq\int_{\tau_{k}}^{k}|\partial_{t}I(s)|\,ds.

By integration over
\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}
of the equality (
3.6
) with
\mathbf{W}=\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 4}W
and
\omega(z)=(1+z)^{\alpha}
and the smallness of
u
given by (
4.6
) we derive that

|\partial_{t}I(t)|\lesssim\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\left|\partial_{y}u\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}u(\partial_{y}\mathbf{W})^{2}\right|+(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\mathbf{F}^{\leq 5}\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\,dxdy

where
\mathbf{F}^{\leq 5}=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 5}\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}
is the nonlinear term appearing in the equation satisfied by
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W
given by (
4.12
).
On the one hand, the pointwise bounds (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) and the energy bound (
2.1
) imply that

\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\left|\partial_{y}u\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}u(\partial_{y}\mathbf{W})^{2}\right|\,dxdy\lesssim C^{3}_{0}\epsilon^{3}(t-1)^{-1}l(t).

On the other hand, from the proof of proposition
2.1
it follows that
\mathbf{F}^{\leq 5}=\mathbf{F}^{a}+\mathbf{F}^{b}
, where
\mathbf{F}^{a}
contains the terms analyzed in case 1 and
2.a
such that

\|(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\mathbf{F}^{a}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}(t-1)^{-1}\sqrt{l(t)}

and
\mathbf{F}^{b}
contains the products treated in case
2.b
that only satisfy

\left\|(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\mathbf{F}^{b}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}(t-1)^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\sqrt{l(t)}.

The estimate for
\mathbf{F}^{b}
with the weaker weight
(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
can actually be improved to the following one, obtained using Sobolev injections and the pointwise bound (
4.8
)

\displaystyle\left\|(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbf{F}^{b}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{xy}(\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t})}\int_{t-1}^{\infty}r^{-2}\left\|\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}\,r^{2}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim C^{4}_{0}\epsilon^{4}(t-1)^{-2}l(t).

When
\mathbf{W}=\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}
this yields

\displaystyle\iint_{\Sigma^{\text{ex}}_{t}}(2+r-t)^{\alpha}\mathbf{F}^{\leq 5}\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}\,dxdy\lesssim

\displaystyle C^{2}_{0}\epsilon^{2}(t-1)^{-1}\sqrt{l(t)}\left\|(2+r-t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\partial_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 5}W\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C^{3}_{0}\epsilon^{3}(t-1)^{-1}l(t),

which finally allows us to conclude that

|I(t)-I(\tau_{k})|\lesssim\int_{\tau_{k}}^{t}C_{0}^{3}\epsilon^{3}(s-1)^{-1}l(s)\,ds\lesssim C_{0}^{3}\epsilon^{3}t^{-1}.

∎

)-(

5.1.
Pointwise estimates from Klainerman-Sobolev inequalities

A first subset of pointwise estimates for
W_{0}
and
\mathtt{W}
is immediately obtained from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) via the following Sobolev inequality on hyperboloids, whose proof can be found in
[
21
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}(t,x)
be a sufficiently regular function in the cone
\mathscr{C}=\{t>r\}
. For all
(t,x)\in\mathscr{C}
, let
s=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
and
B(x,t/3)
be the ball centered at
x
with radius
t/3
. Then

|\mathbf{W}(t,x)|^{2}\leq Ct^{-3}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\int_{B(x,t/3)}\left|Z^{\gamma}\mathbf{W}(\sqrt{s^{2}+|y|^{2}},y)\right|^{2}\,dy

where
C
is a positive universal constant and
Z_{j}=x_{j}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{j}
,
j=\overline{1,3}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{I}_{n,k}
denote the set of multi-indices of type
(n,k)
.
Under the a-priori energy bounds (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we have the following pointwise estimates in
\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}

(5.1)

\displaystyle|\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 3}W_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-1},

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\overline{\partial}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 3}W_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathscr{I}_{3,k}}\left\|\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\partial^{\leq 1}_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{3}{2}}s^{\delta_{k+2}},\qquad k=\overline{0,3}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\sum_{|\gamma|=3}\left\|\partial_{tx}\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-1+\delta_{5}},

and

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathscr{I}_{2,k}}\left\|\bar{\partial}\partial^{\leq 1}_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{5}{2}}s^{\delta_{k+3}},\qquad k=\overline{0,2}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathscr{I}_{1,k}}\left\|\bar{\partial}^{2}\partial^{\leq 1}_{y}\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{7}{2}}s^{\delta_{k+4}},\qquad k=\overline{0,1}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{\partial}^{2}\partial_{tx}\mathscr{Z}\mathtt{W}(t,x,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{5}{2}}s^{-1+\delta_{5}}.

Moreover

(5.8)

\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}|W|\lesssim\epsilon.

Proof.

The estimates (
5.1
) to (
5.4
) are immediate consequence of lemma
5.1
and (
2.4
)-(
2.5
). The estimates on the
\overline{\partial}
derivatives of
\mathtt{W}
are deduced from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) using the fact that
\overline{\partial}=t^{-1}Z
. Finally, if we set
\tilde{W}_{0}(s,x)=W_{0}(\sqrt{s^{2}+r^{2}},x)
we see that

\displaystyle|\tilde{W}_{0}(s,x)-\tilde{W}_{0}(2,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2}^{s}\left|\partial_{\tau}\tilde{W}_{0}(\tau,x)\right|d\tau=\int_{2}^{s}\frac{\tau}{t}\left|\partial_{t}W_{0}(\sqrt{\tau^{2}+x^{2}},x)\right|d\tau\lesssim\epsilon

and hence derive from the smallness of the initial data that

|W_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim|\tilde{W}_{0}(s,x)-\tilde{W}_{0}(2,x)|+|\tilde{W}_{0}(2,x)|\lesssim\epsilon.

The combination of the above estimates with (
5.3
) yields (
5.8
).
∎

5.2.
Improved pointwise estimates on the non-zero modes

The bounds for the
\mathtt{W}
obtained in lemma
5.2
via Sobolev embeddings are affected by the small growth in
s
of the energies and are not sharp but they can be improved if one studies more closely the equation satisfied by
\mathtt{W}
. Enhancing such bounds, and in particular (
5.3
), will be fundamental to propagate the a-priori pointwise bound (
2.6
) later in proposition
5.6
.
We make use of the following result, which is motivated by the work of Klainerman
[
15
]
and whose proof is an adaptation of a similar estimate for Klein-Gordon equations initially proved in
[
20
]
, later revisited in
[
6
]
in the case of Klein-Gordon equations with variable mass.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume
\mathbf{W}
is a solution of the following equation

(5.9)

\Box_{x,y}\mathbf{W}+u\Delta_{y}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{F},\qquad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

such that
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{W}dy=0
. For every fixed
(t,x)
in the cone
\mathscr{C}=\{t>r\}
, let
s=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
and
Y_{tx},A_{tx},B_{tx}
be the functions defined as follows

(5.10)

Y^{2}_{tx}(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\lambda\left|\frac{3}{2}\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}+(\mathscr{S}\mathbf{W})_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+\lambda^{3}(1+u_{\lambda})|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}|^{2}\,dy

(5.11)

A_{tx}(\lambda):=\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\frac{1}{2\lambda}(\mathscr{S}u)_{\lambda}\right|+\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\partial_{y}u_{\lambda}\right|

(5.12)

B^{2}_{tx}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\lambda^{-1}\left|(R\mathbf{W})_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\,dy

where
f_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=f\left(\frac{\lambda t}{s},\frac{\lambda r}{s},y\right)
and

R\mathbf{W}(t,x,y)=s^{2}\bar{\partial}^{i}\bar{\partial}_{i}\mathbf{W}+x^{i}x^{j}\bar{\partial}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\mathbf{W}+\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{W}+3x^{i}\bar{\partial}_{i}\mathbf{W}-s^{2}\mathbf{F}.

Then
\mathbf{W}
satisfies the following inequality in the hyperbolic region
\mathscr{H}_{[2,\infty)}

s^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\|\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathscr{S}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\left(Y_{tx}(2)+\int_{2}^{s}B_{tx}(\lambda)d\lambda\right)e^{\int_{2}^{s}A_{tx}(\lambda)\,d\lambda}.

Proof.

For every fixed
(t,x,y)\in\mathscr{H}_{[2,\infty)}
we define
\omega_{txy}(\lambda):=\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{W}(\frac{\lambda t}{s},\frac{\lambda x}{s},y)
to be the evaluation of
\mathbf{W}
on the hyperboloid
\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}
, then dilated by factor
\lambda^{3/2}
. We have that

\dot{\omega}_{txy}(\lambda)=\lambda^{1/2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}+(\mathscr{S}\mathbf{W})_{\lambda}\right)

and

\ddot{\omega}_{txy}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}(P\mathbf{W})_{\lambda}

where

\displaystyle P\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{W}+3(t\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}+x^{i}\partial_{i}\mathbf{W})+(t^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}\mathbf{W}+2tx^{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}+x^{i}x^{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathbf{W}).

Using the equation (
5.9
) we derive that
\omega_{txy}
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\ddot{\omega}_{txy}-\left(1+u_{\lambda}\right)\Delta_{y}\omega_{txy}=-\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{F}_{\lambda}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(s^{2}\bar{\partial}^{i}\bar{\partial}_{i}\mathbf{W}+x^{i}x^{j}\bar{\partial}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\mathbf{W}+\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{W}+3x^{i}\bar{\partial}_{i}\mathbf{W}\right)_{\lambda}.

We drop the lower indices in
\omega_{txy}(\lambda)
in order to have a lighter notation and simply denote it by
\omega(\lambda)
in what follows.
We multiply the above equation by
\partial_{\lambda}\omega
and integrate over
\mathbb{S}^{1}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{\lambda}\omega\left(\partial^{2}_{\lambda}\omega-\left(1+u_{\lambda}\right)\Delta_{y}\omega\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{\lambda}\omega|^{2}\,dy\right)+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(1+u_{\lambda})\partial_{y}\omega\,\partial_{y}\partial_{\lambda}\omega\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{\lambda}\omega\,\partial_{y}u_{\lambda}\,\partial_{y}\omega\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{\lambda}\omega|^{2}+(1+u_{\lambda})|\partial_{y}\omega|^{2}\,dy\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}\,|\partial_{y}\omega|^{2}dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{\lambda}\omega\,\partial_{y}u_{\lambda}\,\partial_{y}\omega\,dy.

We obtain that

\frac{d}{d\lambda}Y^{2}_{tx}(\lambda)\lesssim A_{tx}(\lambda)Y_{tx}^{2}(\lambda)+B_{tx}(\lambda)Y_{tx}(\lambda)

with
A_{tx},B_{tx},Y_{tx}
as in the statement and from the Gronwall lemma

Y_{tx}(s)\lesssim\left(Y_{tx}(2)+\int_{2}^{s}B_{tx}(\lambda)\,d\lambda\right)e^{\int_{2}^{s}A_{tx}(\lambda)\,d\lambda}.

Finally, from the definition of
Y_{tx}
, the Poincaré inequality and the fact that
s\geq 1
we get

s^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\|\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathscr{S}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim Y_{tx}(s).

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Under the a-priori assumptions (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) we have

(5.13)

\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\left\|\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\left\|\partial_{y}\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{S}\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad j=\overline{0,1}

(5.14)

\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\left\|Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\left\|\partial_{y}Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{s}\left\|\mathscr{S}Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon s^{\sigma},

and

(5.15)

\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}st^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\partial^{2}\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|\partial_{y}\partial^{2}\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathscr{S}\partial^{2}\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon,

(5.16)

\sup_{\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}}st^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|\partial Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\left\|\partial_{y}\partial Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{S}\partial Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\epsilon s^{\sigma}.

Proof.

For any fixed
j=\overline{0,2}
and
k=\overline{0,1}
we compare the equation satisfied by the differentiated functions
\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}
and
\partial^{k}Z\mathtt{W}
respectively with the equation (
5.9
) and apply the result of proposition
5.3
. A simple computation shows that

\Box_{x,y}\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}+u\Delta_{y}\partial^{j}\mathtt{W}=F^{j}_{1},\quad j=\overline{0,2}

\Box_{x,y}\partial^{k}Z\mathtt{W}+u\Delta_{y}\partial^{k}Z\mathtt{W}=F^{k}_{2},\quad k=\overline{0,1}

with source terms given by

\begin{gathered}F^{0}_{1}=\mathbf{N}(W,W)-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W,W)dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{y}u\cdot\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}\,dy\\
F^{j}_{1}=\partial^{j}F^{0}_{1}-\sum_{1\leq h\leq j}\partial^{h}u\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\partial^{j-h}\mathtt{W},\quad j=\overline{1,2}\end{gathered}

and

\begin{gathered}F^{0}_{2}=ZF^{0}_{1}-Zu\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}\\
F^{1}_{2}=\partial ZF^{0}_{1}-\partial Zu\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}-\partial u\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}Z\mathtt{W}-Zu\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\partial\mathtt{W}.\end{gathered}

From proposition
5.3
we have that

s^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\|\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\|\partial_{y}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\right)+s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathscr{S}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\lesssim\left(Y_{tx}(2)+\int_{2}^{s}B_{tx}(\lambda)d\lambda\right)e^{\int_{2}^{s}A_{tx}(\lambda)\,d\lambda}

with
\mathbf{W}=\{\partial^{j}\mathtt{W},\partial^{k}Z\mathtt{W}:j=\overline{0,2},k=\overline{0,1}\}
and corresponding source term
\mathbf{F}=\{F^{j}_{1},F^{k}_{2}:j=\overline{0,2},k=\overline{0,1}\}
.
In order to obtain the bounds in the statement we need to estimate the quantities
Y_{tx}(2)
,
A_{tx}(\lambda)
and
B_{tx}(\lambda)
defined in (
5.10
), (
5.11
), (
5.12
) for all the different values of
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{F}
.

1. The
A_{tx}(\lambda)
term
: this is the same for all values of
\mathbf{W}
. Here we decompose

and rewrite the scaling vector field as follows:

(5.17)

\mathscr{S}=(t-r)\partial_{t}+(r-t)\partial_{r}+(t\partial_{r}+r\partial_{t}).

Using the pointwise bounds (
5.1
)-(
5.3
) and the fact that
s/t\leq 1
in the interior of the light cone we derive that

A_{tx}(\lambda)\lesssim\sup_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{s}{t+r}\,|(\partial u)_{\lambda}|+\lambda^{-1}|(Zu)_{\lambda}|+|(\partial_{y}u)_{\lambda}|\lesssim\epsilon\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta_{5}}

and consequently

(5.18)

\int_{1}^{s}A_{tx}(\lambda)d\lambda\lesssim\epsilon.

2. The
Y_{tx}(2)
term
: the functions appearing here are evaluated on the hyperboloid
\mathscr{H}_{2}
. From the bound (
5.3
) on
\mathtt{W}
, the smallness of
u
given by (
5.8
) and the decomposition (
5.17
) it follows that for all values of
\mathbf{W}

5.3.
The propagation of the a-priori pointwise bound

In order to propagate the a-priori bound (
2.6
) on the
Z

in the interior region

we use

type estimates. For this we give a closer look to the wave equation satisfied by

and use the enhanced pointwise bounds on the solution recovered in the previous subsection to estimate the nonlinear terms. The argument that follows will in addition provide us with a decay estimate for

without derivatives and hence improve on (

We will make use of the following lemma, due to Alinhac
[
1
]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathbf{W}_{0}
be the solution to
\Box_{tx}\mathbf{W}_{0}=\mathbf{F}_{0}
with zero initial data and suppose that
\mathbf{F}_{0}
is spatially compactly supported satisfying the following pointwise bound

|\mathbf{F}_{0}(t,x)|\leq Ct^{-2-\nu}(t-|x|)^{-1+\mu}

for some positive constant
C
. Then

|\mathbf{W}_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{C}{\mu\nu}(t-|x|)^{\mu-\nu}t^{-1}.

Proposition 5.6
.

There exists a constant
B>0
sufficienly large and
\epsilon_{0}
sufficiently small such that, for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
if
W=(u,v)^{T}
is solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and satisfies the a-priori bounds (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) in the interior region
\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}
and the global energy bounds (
2.1
) in the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
, then in
\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}
it actually satisfies the enhanced pointwise bound

|ZW_{0}(t,x)|\leq B\epsilon t^{-1}s^{\sigma}.

Proof.

We are interested in propagating the a-priori pointwise bound (
2.6
) and to recover improved estimates on
ZW_{0}
in the interior region
\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}
, which is contained in the cone
\{t>r+1\}
.
We consider a cut-off function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi(z)=1
for
|z|\leq 1/2
and
\chi(z)=0
for
|z|>1
, and decompose
W_{0}
into the sum
W_{0}^{1}+W_{0}^{2}
, where
W_{0}^{1}
and
W_{0}^{2}
solve to the following Cauchy problems

\Box W_{0}^{1}=(1-\chi(t-r))F_{0},\qquad(W_{0}^{1},\partial_{t}W_{0}^{1})|_{t=2}=(0,0)

\Box_{x}W_{0}^{2}=\chi(t-r)F_{0},\qquad(W_{0}^{2},\partial_{t}W_{0}^{2})|_{t=2}=(W_{0},\partial_{t}W_{0})|_{t=2}.

with
F_{0}
given by

F_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W,W)dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\partial_{y}u\cdot\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}\,dy.

The scope of such decomposition is to estimate
ZW_{0}^{1}
and
ZW_{0}^{2}
separately and in particular to apply lemma
5.5
to
ZW_{0}^{1}
, which is solution to a wave equation with zero data and source term supported in the interior of the cone
\{t=r+1/2\}

\Box ZW_{0}^{1}=(1-\chi)(t-r)ZF_{0}-[Z,\chi(t-r)]F_{0},\qquad(ZW_{0}^{1},\partial_{t}ZW_{0}^{1})|_{t=2}=(0,0).

In order to do so, we need to estimate
F_{0}
and
ZF_{0}
in the region
t>r+1/2
. We remark that the commutator term is uniformly bounded and also supported in
\{t>r+1/2\}
.

We decompose each occurrence of
W
in
\mathbf{N}(W,W)
by means of (
1.7
)
and use the null structure representation (
1.14
) of
\mathbf{N}
for the quadratic interactions
W_{0}\times W_{0}
. From (
5.1
),(
5.2
) and (
5.5
) we deduce the following

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Z^{\leq 1}\mathbf{N}(W_{0},W_{0})|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim|\overline{\partial}Z^{\leq 1}W_{0}||\partial W_{0}|+|\partial Z^{\leq 1}W_{0}||\overline{\partial}W_{0}|+\frac{s^{2}}{t^{2}}|\partial Z^{\leq 1}W_{0}||\partial W_{0}|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}t^{-2}s^{-1}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Z^{\leq 1}\mathbf{N}(W_{0},\mathtt{W})|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Z^{\leq 1}(\partial W_{0}\cdot\partial\mathtt{W})|\,dy\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\partial Z^{\leq 1}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Z^{\leq 1}\mathbf{N}(\mathtt{W},\mathtt{W})|\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|Z^{\leq 1}\left(\partial_{y}u\cdot\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}\right)\right|\,dy\lesssim\|\partial Z^{\leq 1}\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\|\partial\mathtt{W}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}.

The improved bounds (
5.13
) and (
5.16
) on
\mathtt{W}
coupled with the fact that
s=\sqrt{(t+r)(t-r)}
imply that in
\{t>r+1/2\}

|\mathbf{F}_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}t^{-\frac{5}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and

|Z\mathbf{F}_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon^{2}t^{-\frac{5}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}},

so from lemma
5.5
we obtain that

|ZW_{0}^{1}(t,x)|\leq C\epsilon^{2}t^{-1}(t-r)^{\sigma},

for some constant
C=C(A,B)
that depends quadratically on
A
and
B
.

The remaining term
ZW_{0}^{2}
is estimated via the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality of lemma
5.1
in terms of the higher order conformal energy of
W_{0}^{2}
on hyperboloids. More precisely, we have that for any
(t,x)\in\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}

t^{\frac{1}{2}}s|ZW_{0}^{2}(t,x)|\lesssim E^{\text{c,in}}_{2}(s,W_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\left\|(s/t)Z\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}_{s}\cap[T_{1}^{s},T_{2}^{s}])}

where
s^{2}=t^{2}-r^{2}
,
T_{1}^{s}=(s^{2}+1)/2
is the time the hyperboloid
\mathscr{H}_{s}
intersects the cone
t=r+1
, and
T^{s}_{2}=\sqrt{s^{2}+|y|^{2}}
with
|y-x|=t/3
(the second term in the above right hand side should be omitted when
T^{s}_{2}\leq T_{1}^{s}
).
Since the source term in the equation satisfied by
Z^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2}
is supported in the exterior region
t<r+1
, we obtain from proposition
3.5
with
\mathbf{W}_{0}=Z^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2}
, the bound (
4.16
) (which is valid also for
W_{0}^{2}
) with
T_{0}=(s^{2}+1)/2
, and the smallness assumption on the initial data, that

E^{\text{c,in}}(s,Z^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2})\leq E^{\text{c,in}}(2,Z^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2})+C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln s\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln s.

Moreover, from lemma
4.11
with
T_{j}=T_{j}^{s}
for
j=1,2
and the observation that
\ln(T_{2}^{s}/T_{1}^{s})\lesssim\ln s
we also derive that

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\left\|(s/t)Z\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma}W_{0}^{2}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}_{s}\cap[T_{1}^{s},T_{2}^{s}])}\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}\ln s.

This concludes that

|ZW_{0}^{2}(t,x)|\leq\tilde{C}C_{0}\epsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-1+\sigma}

for a universal constant

and therefore

|ZW_{0}(t,x)|\leq|ZW_{0}^{1}(t,x)|+|ZW_{0}^{2}(t,x)|\leq\tilde{C}C_{0}\epsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-1+\sigma}+C\epsilon^{2}t^{-1}(t-r)^{\sigma}\leq B\epsilon t^{-1}s^{\sigma}

if we choose

sufficiently large so that
B\geq 2\tilde{C}C_{0}
and
\epsilon_{0}
sufficiently small so that
2C\epsilon\leq B
.

\displaystyle\sup_{[2,s]}E^{\text{c,in}}(\tau,W_{0}^{\gamma})

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}}\left|(t+r)(\partial_{t}W^{\gamma}_{0}+\partial_{r}W^{\gamma}_{0})+2W^{\gamma}_{0}\right|^{2}+(t-r)^{2}(|\nabla W^{\gamma}_{0}|^{2}-(\partial_{r}W^{\gamma}_{0})^{2})\,d\sigma dt.

corresponds to the integral in the left hand side of (

) for

5.3
) in the case where
|\gamma_{1}|=|\gamma_{2}|=2
(which appears only if
k=4
) we see that

\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|=k\leq 4}\left\|\partial_{y}u^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\partial_{y}W^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left[E^{\text{in}}_{k}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau^{\sigma}E^{\text{in}}_{k-1}(W)+\nu_{4}\tau^{\delta_{4}}E^{\text{in}}_{2}(W)\right]

where
\nu_{4}=1
if
k=4
and 0 otherwise.

2. The null terms:

we apply the decomposition (
1.7
)
to both factors
W^{\gamma_{1}}
and
W^{\gamma_{2}}
so that

\mathbf{N}(W^{\gamma_{1}},W^{\gamma_{2}})=\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})+\mathbf{N}(\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})+\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}})+\mathbf{N}(\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}).

We express
\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})
using the representation formula (
1.14
)

\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})=\overline{\partial}W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\partial W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}}+\partial W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\overline{\partial}W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}}+\frac{t-r}{t}\partial W^{\gamma_{1}}_{0}\cdot\partial W^{\gamma_{2}}_{0}

and estimate all terms in the above right hand side using (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) as follows

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})\,dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(t/\tau)\overline{\partial}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 3}W_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\left\|(\tau/t)\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq k}W_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\overline{\partial}\mathscr{Z}^{\leq k}W_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\left\|\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 3}W_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}E^{\text{in}}_{k}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We neglect the null structure for all the remaining quadratic forms.
The products involving
W_{0}\times\mathtt{W}
are equivalent given the range of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
and are estimated using (
5.1
) whenever
|\gamma_{1}|\leq 3
and (
5.13
) otherwise

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}})\,dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(t/\tau)\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 3}W_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\left\|(\tau/t)\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq k}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle+\left\|(\tau/t)\partial\mathscr{Z}^{\leq 4}W_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\left\|(t/\tau)\partial\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}E^{\text{in}}_{k}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The quadratic term involving products
\mathtt{W}\times\mathtt{W}
is estimated as in step 1.

Summing up the estimates in step 1 and 2, using the a-priori energy bounds (
2.4
)-(
2.5
), and choosing
\sigma,\delta_{k-1}
small so that
\sigma+\delta_{k-1}\leq\delta_{k}
we obtain that

(6.4)

\left\|\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\mu_{k}}\qquad\text{with }\mu_{k}=\begin{cases}\delta_{k}\quad&\text{if }k\leq 3\\
\delta_{4}+\delta_{2}\quad&\text{if }k=4.\end{cases}

Plugging the above estimate together with (
6.3
) into (
6.2
) and using the smallness assumptions on the initial data we finally obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{[2,s]}E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(\tau,W_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(2,W_{0})+\epsilon^{2}s^{2C\epsilon}\ln s+\int_{2}^{s}\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{k}}E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(\tau,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{2}s^{1+2\mu_{k}}+\epsilon^{2}\sup_{[2,s]}E^{\text{c,in}}_{k}(\tau,W_{0})

and the result of the proposition follows choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small.
∎

We observe that from lemma
5.1
, the conformal energy bound (
6.1
) and lemma
4.11
with
T_{1}=(s^{2}+1)/2
and
T_{2}=\sqrt{s^{2}+|y|^{2}}
for
|y-x|=t/3
, we have the following additional pointwise bound for the
Z
derivatives of
W_{0}
in
\mathscr{H}_{[2,s_{0}]}

(6.5)

|ZZ^{j}W_{0}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon t^{-\frac{1}{2}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}+\mu_{j+2}}\,\quad\text{for }j=\overline{0,2},\qquad\text{where }\mu_{k}=\begin{cases}\delta_{k}\quad&\text{if }k\leq 3\\
\delta_{4}+\delta_{2}\quad&\text{if }k=4.\end{cases}

We now have all the ingredients to propagate the a-priori energy bounds (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).

Proposition 6.2
.

There exists a constant
A>0
sufficiently large, some small parameters
\sigma\ll\delta_{k}\ll\delta_{k+1}
for
k=\overline{1,4}
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that, if for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
the solution
W=(u,v)^{T}
to the Cauchy problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) satisfies the a-priori bounds (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) in the hyperbolic region
\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{[2,s_{0}]}
and the global exterior energy bounds (
2.1
) in the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
, then it also satisfies the enhanced energy bound

(6.6)

E^{\text{in}}_{5}(s,W_{0})\leq A^{2}\epsilon^{2},\qquad s\in[2,s_{0}].

Proof.

For any
|\gamma|\leq 5
, the equation (
4.17
) satisfied by
W_{0}^{\gamma}
has the same structure as the inhomogeneous wave equation (
3.13
), therefore proposition
3.4
with
\mathbf{W}_{0}=W_{0}^{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{F}_{0}=\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}
implies that

E^{\text{in}}(s,W_{0}^{\gamma})\lesssim E^{\text{in}}(2,W^{\gamma}_{0})+\int_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}}|\mathscr{T}W^{\gamma}_{0}|^{2}d\sigma dt+\int_{2}^{s}\|\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}(\tau,W_{0}^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tau

for all
s\in[2,s_{0}]
. The implicit constant in the above right hand side is independent of
s_{0}
.

The integral over the boundary
\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}
has already been proved to be finite and small in proposition
4.9
. The source term
\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}
has also already been estimated in the case
|\gamma|\leq 4
(see (
6.4
) in the previous proposition). When
|\gamma|=5
we simply use the estimate (
5.3
) to deduce

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|=5\\
|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\|\partial_{y}u^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\partial_{y}W^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}+\left\|\mathbf{N}(\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}})\right\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta_{4}}E^{\text{in}}_{5}(t,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.13
)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|\leq 5\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},W_{0}^{\gamma_{2}})\right\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}+\left\|\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}})\right\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}E^{\text{in}}_{5}(s,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore

(6.7)

\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 5}\|\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta_{4}}E^{\text{in}}_{5}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and from the a-priori energy bound (
2.5
) and the exterior energy bound (
4.15
) we obtain that, for some fixed
K>1
and some universal constant
C>0

\displaystyle E^{\text{in}}_{5}(s,W_{0})

\displaystyle\leq E^{\text{in}}_{5}(2,W_{0})+\frac{C^{2}_{0}\epsilon^{2}}{K}+\int_{2}^{s}A^{2}C\epsilon^{3}\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta_{4}+\delta_{5}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq E^{\text{in}}_{5}(2,W_{0})+\frac{C^{2}_{0}\epsilon^{2}}{K}+A^{2}C\epsilon^{3}.

The wished improved energy bound then follows choosing
K=3
,
\epsilon_{0}
small such that
3C\epsilon^{2}_{0}<1
and
A\geq C_{0}
sufficiently large so that

E^{\text{in}}_{5}(2,W_{0})\leq\frac{A^{2}\epsilon_{0}^{2}}{3}.

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

There exists a constant
A>0
sufficiently large, some small parameters
\sigma\ll\delta_{k}\ll\delta_{k+1}
for
k=\overline{1,4}
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that, if for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
the solution
W=(u,v)^{T}
to the Cauchy problem (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) satisfies the a-priori bounds (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) in the hyperbolic region
\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{[2,s_{0}]}
and the global exterior energy bounds (
2.1
) in the exterior region
\mathscr{D}^{\text{ex}}
, then it also satisfies the enhanced energy bound

(6.8)

E^{\text{in}}_{5,k}(s,\mathtt{W})\leq A^{2}\epsilon^{2}\,s^{2\delta_{k}},\qquad s\in[2,s_{0}].

Proof.

We start by considering a multi-index
\gamma
of type
(n,k)
with
k\leq n\leq 5
and compare the equation satisfied by
\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}=(\mathtt{u}^{\gamma},\mathtt{v}^{\gamma})^{T}
with the linear inhomogeneous equation (
3.1
). We have that

\Box_{x,y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}+u\,\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}=\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}_{0},\qquad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

so applying proposition
3.3
with
\mathbf{W}=\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{F}
replaced by
\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}_{0}
we derive the following inequality

(6.9)

\displaystyle E^{\text{in}}(s,\mathtt{W}^{\gamma})

\displaystyle\lesssim E^{\text{in}}(2,\mathtt{W}^{\gamma})+\iint_{\mathscr{C}_{[2,s]}}|\mathscr{T}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}|^{2}+|\partial_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma}|^{2}d\sigma dydt

\displaystyle+\int_{2}^{s}\|\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}(\tau,\mathtt{W}^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tau.

All quadratic terms in
\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}
can be estimated similarly to those in
\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}
– which satisfy the bound (
6.7
) – except for the products
u^{\gamma_{1}}_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}W^{\gamma_{2}}
when
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma_{1}}=Z^{\beta_{1}}
is a pure product of Klainerman vector fields, obtained after using the decomposition (
1.7
) on
u^{\gamma_{1}}
. The reason is that the
L^{2}_{x}
norm of
Z^{\beta_{1}}u_{0}
is not controlled by the higher order energies of
W_{0}
but rather by the higher order conformal energy of
W_{0}
(up to multiplication with a factor
(s/t)
).
Those are the only terms that need to be analyzed here since the argument already used in proposition
6.2
coupled with the Poincaré’s inequality shows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|=|\gamma|\\
|\gamma_{2}|<|\gamma|\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathtt{u}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}+\sum_{|\gamma_{1}|+|\gamma_{2}|\leq|\gamma|}\left\|\mathbf{N}(W^{\gamma_{1}},W^{\gamma_{2}})\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta_{4}}E^{\text{in}}_{5}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Depending on the starting multi-index
\gamma_{1}=(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
we distinguish between two cases:

1. The case
|\alpha_{1}|>0
: here we can
write
u_{0}^{\gamma_{1}}=\partial u_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}
for some other index
\tilde{\gamma}_{1}
with
|\tilde{\gamma}_{1}|=|\gamma_{1}|-1
and estimate the product
\partial u_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}
as done for
\mathbf{N}(W_{0}^{\gamma_{1}},\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}})
in the proof of proposition
6.2
.

2. The case
|\alpha_{1}|=0
: in this case
\gamma_{1}
is of type
(k_{1},k_{1})
with
k_{1}\leq k\leq n
and
\gamma_{2}
is of type
(n-k_{1},k_{2})
with
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
. We
write
u_{0}^{\gamma_{1}}=Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}
for some other index
\tilde{\gamma}_{1}
of type
(k_{1}-1,k_{1}-1)
and distinguish between the different values
k_{1}
can take.

If
k_{1}=n
then
|\gamma_{2}|=0
and we get from the pointwise bound (
5.13
) and the conformal energy bound (
6.1
) that

\left\|Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\left\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{c,in}}_{n-1}(\tau,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\mu_{n-1}}.

If
k_{1}=n-1
we have
|\gamma_{2}|=1
, so from the pointwise bounds (
5.15
), (
5.16
) and the conformal energy bound (
6.1
)

\left\|Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\left\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{c,in}}_{n-2}(\tau,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\mu_{n-2}+\sigma}.

If
k_{1}=n-2
then
|\gamma_{2}|=2
and
n=\overline{3,5}
. We distinguish the following cases depending on if
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma_{2}}
is of the form
\partial^{2},\partial Z
or
Z^{2}
.

a. The case
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma_{2}}=\partial^{2}
: here we use (
5.3
) and (
6.1
) to derive that

\left\|Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\left\|\partial^{2}_{y}\partial^{2}\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{c,in}}_{n-3}(\tau,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\mu_{n-3}+\delta_{2}}.

b. The case
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma_{2}}=\partial Z
: when
n=3
we have
k_{1}=1
and hence
|\tilde{\gamma}_{1}|=0
. From the pointwise bound (
2.6
) and the a-priori energy bound (
2.5
) we find

\left\|Zu_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|Zu_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}_{3,1}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\sigma+\delta_{1}}.

When
n=\overline{4,5}
we use (
5.3
) and (
6.1
) to derive that

\left\|Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\left\|\partial^{2}_{y}\partial Z\mathtt{W}\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{c,in}}_{n-3}(\tau,W_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\mu_{n-3}+\delta_{3}}.

c. The case
\mathscr{Z}^{\gamma_{2}}=Z^{2}
: when
n=3
we estimate
Zu_{0}^{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}
using (
2.6
) while for
n=\overline{4,5}
we use (
6.5
). Together with the a-priori bound (
2.5
) these give that

\left\|Zu^{\tilde{\gamma_{1}}}_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|Zu^{\tilde{\gamma_{1}}}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}_{3,2}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\nu_{n}+\delta_{2}}.

where
\nu_{3}=\sigma
,
\nu_{4}=\mu_{3}
and
\nu_{5}=\mu_{4}
.

The final cases to treat correspond to
k_{1}=n-3
and
|\gamma_{2}|=3
, which appears for
n=\overline{4,5}
, and
k=n-4
and
|\gamma_{2}|=4
which only appears when
n=5
. If
k_{1}=1
then
|\gamma_{2}|=n-1
and
n=\overline{4,5}
, so using (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) we obtain

\left\|Zu_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|Zu_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}_{n,n-1}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\sigma+\delta_{n-1}},\quad n=\overline{4,5}.

If
k_{1}=2
then
|\gamma_{2}|=3
and
n=4
, so the bounds (
2.5
) and (
6.5
) yield

\left\|Z^{2}u_{0}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\|Z^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}E^{\text{in}}_{4,3}(\tau,W)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+2\delta_{3}}.

Choosing appropriately
\sigma\ll\delta_{k}\ll\delta_{k+1}
for
k=\overline{1,4}
so that
\delta_{k+1}
is bigger than some linear combination of
\sigma
and
\delta_{j}
with
j\leq k
, we finally obtain that

\left\|u_{0}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}\mathtt{W}^{\gamma_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathscr{H}^{\text{in}}_{\tau})}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}\tau^{-1+\delta_{k}}

with an implicit constant that depends on
A
and
B
. The same bound holds then true for
\mathbf{F}^{\gamma}
so plugging it into (
6.9
) together with the estimate for
\mathbf{F}_{0}^{\gamma}
, the exterior energy estimate (
4.15
), and the a-priori energy bounds (
2.5
), gives us that

E^{\text{in}}_{5,k}(s,\mathtt{W})\leq CE^{\text{in}}_{5,k}(2,\mathtt{W})+\frac{CC_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{2}+\int_{2}^{s}A^{2}C\epsilon^{3}\tau^{-1+2\delta_{k}}\,d\tau.

The end of the proof follows finally by choosing appropriately the constants
A
and
\epsilon_{0}
.
∎

Local Differential Privacy for Bayesian Optimization
Xingyu Zhou, Jian Tan

Abstract
Motivated by the increasing concern about privacy in nowadays data-intensive online learning systems, we consider a black-box optimization in the nonparametric Gaussian process setting with local differential privacy (LDP) guarantee. Specifically, the rewards from each user are further corrupted to protect privacy and the learner only has access to the corrupted rewards to minimize the regret. We first derive the regret lower bounds for any LDP mechanism and any learning algorithm. Then, we present three almost optimal algorithms based on the GP-UCB framework and Laplace DP mechanism. In this process, we also propose a new Bayesian optimization (BO) method (called MoMA-GP-UCB) based on m edian- o f- m eans techniques and kernel a pproximations, which complements previous BO algorithms for heavy-tailed payoffs with a reduced complexity. Further, empirical comparisons of different algorithms on both synthetic and real-world datasets highlight the superior performance of MoMA-GP-UCB in both private and non-private scenarios.

Introduction

We consider the problem of maximizing an unknown function
f
over a set
\mathcal{D}
via sequentially querying it and received only bandit feedback, i.e., when we query at
x
, we observe a possibly noisy evaluation of
f(x)
. This model has been a main focus in machine learning research, e.g., the classic multi-armed bandit (MAB) setting
[
15
]
, linear bandit setting
[
1
]
and the general Bayesian optimization
[
21
]
, with each one generalizing the previous one. It also finds broad applications in many real-world systems, including medical experiments, online shopping websites and recommender systems
[
16
]
. These systems adaptively make a decision and receive rewards (feedback) from the user to simultaneously learn insightful facts and maximize the profit.

Recently, privacy has become a key issue in the above mentioned online learning systems. Users have become increasingly concerned about directly sharing their online information or activities to these systems, since these activities may reveal their private information. For example, a customer of an online shopping website is not willing to tell the website that he or she has purchased medicines for mental issues. Another example is the medical experiments in which the patient could reject to share the actual effects of the treatment due to privacy concerns. This stimulates the need to have a mechanism that further corrupts the feedbacks from each user to protect privacy, which exactly fits the
locally differential private
(LDP) model
[
13
,
9
]
.

In contrast to the standard differential privacy model
[
11
]
, in which the learner collects the true data while releasing a private output to protect privacy, in the LDP model, the learner only has access to corrupted input data from the users. Hence, LDP often provides a much stronger privacy protection for the user and is more appealing in real applications, especially for the systems mentioned above
[
8
]
. To the best of our knowledge, in the setting of online learning with bandit feedback, LDP model has only been studied theoretically very recently. For example, in
[
12
,
19
]
, the authors investigate MAB with LDP guarantee.
[
25
]
studies LDP in the linear (contextual) bandit setting. However, LDP in the most general scenario, i.e., Bayesian optimization (BO), remains an important open problem.

Motivated by this, in this paper, we investigate the locally differentially private BO, in which the rewards are further corrupted to protect privacy. Specifically, we consider a Gaussian process (GP) model for BO (also called Gaussian process bandit setting), which directly generalizes both MAB and linear bandit setting. The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows.

Contributions.
(i) We first derive the regret lower bounds for any LDP mechanism and any learning algorithm. (ii) Then, we present three almost optimal algorithms based on the GP-UCB framework and Laplace DP mechanism. (iii) Our two new methods developed for handling LDP also contribute to BO under heavy-tailed payoffs in general. In particular, one is a new truncation method that can be applied to any sub-Weibull rewards. The other one, called MoMA-GP-UCB, is based on median-of-means techniques and kernel approximations, which complements previous BO algorithms for general heavy-tailed payoffs
[
7
]
with a reduced complexity. (iv) We further conduct empirical comparisons of different algorithms over both synthetic and real-world datasets, which demonstrate the superior performance of our new MoMA-GP-UCB algorithm in both private and non-private settings.

is chosen based on the arms played and the private rewards obtained until

\mathcal{H}_{t-1}=\{(x_{s},\bar{y}_{s}):s\in[t-1]\}

Regularity Assumptions.
Attaining a sub-linear regret is in general infeasible for an arbitrary reward function

\displaystyle k_{\text{SE}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\exp{(-s^{2}/2l^{2})}

\displaystyle k_{\text{Mat\'{e}rn}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\left(\frac{s\sqrt{2\nu}}{l}\right)^{\nu}B_{\nu}\left(\frac{s\sqrt{2\nu}}{l}\right),

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=k_{t}(x,x).

4
Algorithms and Upper Bounds

In this section, we will present three algorithms that are able to achieve nearly optimal regret while guaranteeing
\epsilon
-LDP. All the three algorithms rely on adding additional Laplace noise on the reward (i.e., Laplace mechanism in DP) to provide privacy guarantee. Note that, due to the additional Laplace noise, the rewards received by the learner are now no longer sub-Gaussian, and hence standard algorithms will not work. As a result, the three algorithms mainly differ in the way of handling the issue of non-sub-Gaussian rewards.

4.1

\displaystyle\text{Laplace}(\mathcal{L}):l(x\mid\mathcal{L})=(2\mathcal{L})^{-1}\exp(-|x|/\mathcal{L}).

4.3
Raw Reward Truncation Algorithm and Regret

In this section, instead of using the sophisticated truncation in the feature space as in LDP-ATA-GP-UCB, we turn to adopt the simple truncation on the raw rewards. In the general heavy-tail reward setting (with at most a finite variance),

7
]
proposed TGP-UCB algorithm which truncates the reward to zero if it is larger than a truncated point

for round

\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})

However, as pointed before, the corrupted reward
\bar{y}
has a nice exponential tail property. This suggests that a truncated point of order
O(\ln t)
is enough, which will only in turn incurs an additional

,
7
]
, the key step boils down to establishing a (high-probability) confidence interval bound, i.e., in our setting,

\displaystyle|f(x)-\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)|\leq\beta_{t+1}\sigma_{t}(x).

To this end, with some linear algebra calculations, we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)|\leq\left(B+\lambda^{-1/2}\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{\eta}_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\right)\sigma_{t}(x),

where
\hat{\eta}_{t}=\hat{y}_{t}-f(x_{t})
,
\varphi(x):=k(x,\cdot)
, which maps
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to RKHS
H
associated with kernel function
k
and
V_{t}=\Phi_{t}^{T}\Phi_{t}+\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\Phi_{t}:=[\varphi(x_{1})^{T},\ldots,\varphi(x_{t})^{T}]^{T}
.

The key term is
\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{\eta}_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}
, which can be handled by the self-normalized inequality if
\hat{\eta}_{\tau}
is sub-Gaussian. However, in our setting, it is not. To overcome this issue, we will divide it into two parts. In particular, similar to
[
7
]
, we define
\xi_{t}=\hat{\eta}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
. Now, the key term can be written as

\displaystyle\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{\eta}_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}=\underbrace{\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}{\xi}_{\tau}\phi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}+\underbrace{\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]\phi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}.}_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}

(4)

For
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, note that
\xi_{t}=\hat{y}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{y}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
, which is bounded by
2b_{t}
, and hence sub-Gaussian. Thus, by the self-normalized inequality for the RKHS-valued process in
[
10
,
7
]
, we can bound
\mathcal{T}_{1}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}\leq 2b_{t}\sqrt{2(\gamma_{t}+\ln(1/\delta))}.

For
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, with some linear algebra, we can first bound it as
\sqrt{\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]^{2}}
. Further, note that
\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{y}_{\tau}\mathbbm{1}_{|\tilde{y}_{\tau}|>b_{\tau}}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]
. Hence, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality with
b_{\tau}=B+R+\mathcal{L}\ln\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{y}_{\tau}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]\mathbbm{P}(|L|>\mathcal{L}\ln\tau)\leq K\frac{1}{\tau},

where
K:=B^{2}+R^{2}+2\mathcal{L}^{2}
. The last inequality holds since
|L|\sim\text{Exp}(1/\mathcal{L})
. Therefore, by the property of Harmonic sum, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}\leq\sqrt{K(\ln t+1)}.

Hence, the (high-probability) confidence interval bound is obtained by setting

\displaystyle\beta_{t+1}=B+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}b_{t}\sqrt{\gamma_{t}+\ln(1/\delta)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sqrt{K(\ln t+1)}.

The full proof is relegated to Appendix.
∎

It is worth pointing out the truncation trick used in LDP-TGP-UCB also sheds light on the regret bounds for non-private BO under payoffs that are beyond sub-Gaussian, e.g., sub-Weibull which includes sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential as special cases
[
24
]
. More specifically, according to
[
24
]
, a random variable
X
is said to be a sub-Weibull with tail parameter
\theta
, i.e.,
X\sim\text{subW}(\theta)
, if for some constants
a
and
b
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq x)\leq a\exp(-bx^{1/\theta}),\quad\text{for all }x>0.

(5)

It can be seen that sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential distributions are special cases of sub-Weibull with
\theta=1/2
and
\theta=1
, respectively.
Thus, instead of choosing the truncation point
b_{t}=O(\ln t)
as in LDP-TGP-UCB, one turn to choose
b_{t}=O((\ln t)^{\theta})
, which in turn only incurs a log factor in the regret bound. As a result, with this simple truncation, the non-private BO under sub-Weibull noise is still
\tilde{O}(\gamma_{T}\sqrt{T})
.

4.4
Median of Means Approximate (MoMA) Algorithm and Regret

In this section, we will introduce a new BO method that is able to achieve almost optimal regret bounds under general heavy-tailed payoffs. Hence, the LDP setting is just a special case. This new method is mainly inspired by the MENU algorithm in
[
22
]
, which is introduced to handle the heavy-tailed payoffs in the linear bandit setting. Moreover, it has been shown to have a lower complexity than TOFU (which is the core of ATA-GP-UCB). The key idea in MENU is based on median of means techniques
[
3
]
. The main challenge in generalizing MENU to the BO setting is to handle the possibly infinite feature dimension associated with the kernel function. To this end, we will again use kernel approximation techniques (i.e., Nyström approximation) developed in
[
5
,
7
]

This new algorithm is called MoMA-GP-UCB, which is presented in Algorithm

4.5
Unbounded Noise Case

In previous sections, we assume that
\eta_{t}
is bounded by

. Hence, by Laplace mechanism, we can achieve
\epsilon
-LDP. In the case of unbounded noise, we show that Laplace mechanism can ensure

-LDP) while still achieving almost optimal regret bounds for a large class of noise. More specifically, consider the case
\eta_{t}

A.4
Analysis of expected cumulative regret

Note that
D_{m}^{j}
is the key term in our analysis. In particular, in our LDP setting, it is the KL divergence between two distributions on the
private
rewards, which is the key difference compared to previous works. Nevertheless, as stated before, we can relate it to the non-private case. Specifically, we have the following bound on it.

Lemma 4
.

Under the definitions above, if
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}
, we have, for any
\epsilon
-LDP mechanism

\displaystyle D_{m}^{j}\leq 8(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}2^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\Delta}{v}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}v_{m}^{j}.

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle D_{kl}(P_{0}(\cdot|x)||P_{m}(\cdot|x))+D_{kl}(P_{m}(\cdot|x)||P_{0}(\cdot|x))

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(a)}4(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}||\tilde{P}_{0}(\cdot|x)-\tilde{P}_{m}(\cdot|x)||^{2}_{TV}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(b)}8(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}D_{kl}(\tilde{P}_{0}(\cdot|x)||\tilde{P}_{m}(\cdot|x)),

where (a) follows from Theorem 1 of
[
9
]
and (b) is due to Pinsker’s inequality. Then, according to the (non-private) reward distribution in Eq. (
11
) and the analysis in
[
7
]
(cf., Eq. (17)), we have

\displaystyle D_{kl}(\tilde{P}_{0}(\cdot|x)||\tilde{P}_{m}(\cdot|x))\leq 2\left(\frac{2\Delta}{v}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}|f(x)|

for
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}
. Thus, combing the results above with the definitions of
D_{m}^{j}
and
v_{m}^{j}
in Eqs. (
13
) and (
12
), yields the result.
∎

Now, we are well-prepared to analyze the lower bounds of the expected cumulative regret in the LDP setting.

First, note that by the decomposition of expectation, we have
\mathbb{E}_{m}[f(x_{t})]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}_{m}[x_{t}\in\mathcal{R}_{j}]v_{m}^{j}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{m}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(x_{t})\right]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}\mathbb{E}_{m}\left[N_{j}\right]\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(a)}\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}\left(\mathbb{E}_{0}(N_{j})+T\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]D_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}\right),

where (a) directly follows from Lemma
3
. Now, taking the average over
m\in[M]
, yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{T}f(x_{t})\right]\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}\left(\mathbb{E}_{0}(N_{j})+T\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]D_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}\right)

(14)

For the first term, we can bound it as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j}]=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j}]\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(a)}O\left(\frac{\Delta}{M}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[N_{j}\right]\mathop{=}\limits^{(b)}O\left(\frac{T\Delta}{M}\right),

(15)

where (a) follows from the second part of Lemma
2
, and (b) holds since
\sum_{j=1}^{M}N_{j}=T
.

For the second term, we have when
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}v_{m}^{j}T\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]D_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\mathop{=}\limits^{(a)}O(T\Delta)\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sqrt{\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]D_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(b)}O(T\Delta)\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]D_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(c)}O(T\Delta)(e^{\epsilon}-1)2^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha}}\left(\frac{\Delta}{v}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{j^{\prime}}]v_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(d)}O\left(T\Delta(e^{\epsilon}-1)\frac{(2\Delta)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{T}{M}}\right),

(16)

where (a) follows from the first part of Lemma
2
; (b) follows from Jensen’s inequality; (c) follows from Lemma
4
; (d) holds by Eq. (
15
). Now, substituting Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
) into Eq. (
14
), yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{T}f(x_{t})\right]\leq CT\Delta\left(\frac{1}{M}+(e^{\epsilon}-1)\frac{(2\Delta)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{T}{M}}\right)

for some constant
C
, when
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}
.

Observe that
f(x^{*})=2\Delta
by the construction process in
A.1
, then the cumulative expected regret is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{T}\right]=Tf(x^{*})-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{T}f(x_{t})\right]\geq T\Delta\left(2-\frac{C}{M}-C(e^{\epsilon}-1)\frac{(2\Delta)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{T}{M}}\right),

when
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}
. Recall that we always choose a sufficient
\frac{\Delta}{B}
, and hence by Eq. (
10
),
M
is sufficient large. Thus,
\frac{C}{M}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{T}\right]

\displaystyle\geq T\Delta\left(\frac{3}{2}-C(e^{\epsilon}-1)\frac{(2\Delta)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\sqrt{\frac{T}{M}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq T\Delta,\text{ for }\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{M}{4C^{2}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}T}\right\}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}.

Therefore, if
M\leq 2C^{2}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}T
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{T}\right]=\Omega\left(v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}T^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}M^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}\right)\text{ for }\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{M}{4C^{2}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}T}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\leq\Delta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{M}{4C^{2}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}T}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}

(17)

Now, it only remains to combine

(in Eq. (
10
)) and Eq. (

) to arrive at the specific lower bound for

and
k_{\text{Mat\'{e}rn}}
, respectively.

•

For
k_{\text{SE}}
, note that we choose
M=\Theta\left(\left(\ln\frac{B}{\Delta}\right)^{d/2}\right)
in Eq. (

). Combing it with the upper and lower bounds on

in Eq. (

), yields that

. This, in turn, implies that

\displaystyle\ln\frac{B}{\Delta}=\ln\frac{B\left(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}-\ln\left(\Theta(1)\left(\ln\frac{B}{\Delta}\right)^{\frac{d\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)}}\right).

Since

, and

. Thus, we have
\ln\frac{B}{\Delta}=\Theta\left(\ln\frac{B\left(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\right)

\displaystyle M=\Theta\left(\left(\ln\frac{B\left(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\Delta=\Theta\left(\left(\ln\frac{B\left(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{d\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)}}T^{-\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\right).

are all constants that do not increase with

. Finally, substituting

into Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[R_{T}\right]

\displaystyle=\Omega\left(\left(\ln\frac{B\left(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}}{v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{d\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)}}T^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathop{=}\limits^{(a)}\Omega\left(\left(\ln\frac{B^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}}{v^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{d\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)}}T^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{-\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\right)

where (a) holds due to

and

•

For

M=\Theta\left(\left(\frac{B}{\Delta}\right)^{d/\nu}\right)
. Plug it into the upper and lower bounds on

in Eq. (

\Delta=\Theta\left(\left(\frac{1}{T}\frac{1}{(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2}}\left(\frac{B}{\Delta}\right)^{\frac{d}{\nu}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}v^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\Delta=\Theta\left(v^{\frac{\nu/(1+\alpha)}{\nu+d\alpha/(1+\alpha)}}B^{\frac{d\alpha/(1+\alpha)}{\nu+d\alpha/(1+\alpha)}}(T(e^{\epsilon}-1)^{2})^{-\frac{\nu\alpha/(1+\alpha)}{\nu+d\alpha/(1+\alpha)}}\right)

\displaystyle|f(x)-\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda\langle\varphi(x),f\rangle_{V_{t}^{-1}}|+|\langle\varphi(x),\Phi_{t}^{T}\hat{N}_{t}\rangle_{V_{t}^{-1}}|

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(a)}\lambda\left\lVert\varphi(x)\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\left\lVert V_{t}^{-1/2}f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\lVert\varphi(x)\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\left\lVert\Phi_{t}^{T}\hat{N}_{t}\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(b)}\lambda^{1/2}\left\lVert\varphi(x)\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\lVert\varphi(x)\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\left\lVert\Phi_{t}^{T}\hat{N}_{t}\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(c)}\sigma_{t}(x)\left(B+\lambda^{-1/2}\left\lVert\Phi_{t}^{T}\hat{N}_{t}\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}\right)

where (a) follows from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality; (b) is true since
V_{t}^{-1}\preceq\lambda^{-1}I_{\mathcal{H}}
; (c) follows from the fact that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq B
and Lemma
5
.

The key term is
\left\lVert\Phi_{t}^{T}\hat{N}_{t}\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}=\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{\eta}_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}
, which can be handled by the self-normalized inequality if
\hat{\eta}_{\tau}
is sub-Gaussian. However, in our LDP setting, it is not due to Laplace noise. To overcome this issue, we will divide it into two parts. In particular, similar to
[
7
]
, we define
\xi_{t}=\hat{\eta}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
. Now, the key term can be written as

\displaystyle\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\hat{\eta}_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}=\underbrace{\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}{\xi}_{\tau}\phi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}+\underbrace{\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]\phi(x_{\tau})\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}.}_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}

For
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, note that
\xi_{t}=\hat{y}_{t}-\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{y}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
and
\hat{y}_{t}=\bar{y}_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{|\bar{y}_{t}|\leq b_{t}}
. This implies that
\xi_{t}
is bounded by
2b_{t}
, and hence sub-Gaussian. Thus, by the self-normalized inequality for the RKHS-valued process in
[
10
,
7
]
, we have for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have for all
t\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}\leq 2b_{t}\sqrt{2(\gamma_{t}+\ln(1/\delta))}.

For
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we can first bound it as
\mathcal{T}_{2}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]^{2}}
. This is due to the following property. For any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}a_{\tau}\varphi(x_{\tau})\right\rVert_{V_{t}^{-1}}^{2}=a^{T}\Phi_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{T}\Phi_{t}+\lambda I_{\mathcal{H}})^{-1}\Phi_{t}^{T}a\mathop{=}\limits^{(a)}a^{T}\Phi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{T}(\Phi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{T}+\lambda I_{t})^{-1}a\mathop{\leq}\limits^{(b)}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

where (a) holds by Eq. (
18
); (b) is true since
\Phi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{T}(\Phi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{T}+\lambda I_{t})^{-1}\preceq I_{t}
.

Further, note that
\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{y}_{\tau}\mathbbm{1}_{|\tilde{y}_{\tau}|>b_{\tau}}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]
. Hence, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality with
b_{\tau}=B+R+\mathcal{L}\ln\tau
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\eta}_{\tau}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{y}_{\tau}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}\right]\mathbbm{P}(|L|>\mathcal{L}\ln\tau)\leq K\frac{1}{\tau},

where
K:=B^{2}+R^{2}+2\mathcal{L}^{2}
. The last inequality holds since
|L|\sim\text{Exp}(1/\mathcal{L})
. Therefore, by the property of Harmonic sum, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}\leq\sqrt{K(\ln t+1)}.

Hence, by setting

\displaystyle\beta_{t+1}=B+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda}}b_{t}\sqrt{\gamma_{t}+\ln(1/\delta)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sqrt{K(\ln t+1)},

we have for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, uniformly over all
t\geq 1
and
x\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle|f(x)-\hat{\mu}_{t}(x)|\leq\beta_{t+1}\sigma_{t}(x).

(20)

Now, the following analysis is standard. First,

\displaystyle r_{t}=f(x^{*})-f(x_{t})\leq 2\beta_{t}\sigma_{t-1}(x),

which follows from Eq. (
20
) and the GP-UCB like algorithm. Hence, for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, the cumulative regret is given by

\displaystyle R_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}r_{t}\leq 2\beta_{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sigma_{t-1}(x)\mathop{=}\limits^{(a)}O(\beta_{T}\sqrt{\gamma_{T}T})=O\left(\vartheta\sqrt{\ln T\gamma_{T}T}+\vartheta\sqrt{T}\ln T\sqrt{\gamma_{T}(\gamma_{T}+\ln(1/\delta))}\right),

where
\vartheta=(B+R)/\epsilon
and (a) holds by Lemma
7
.
∎

Lemma 10

Recall that in MoMA-GP-UCB,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{V}_{n}^{-1}\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{T}f_{n}-\tilde{\theta}_{n,j}\right\rVert_{\tilde{V}_{n}}=\left\lVert\tilde{V}_{n}^{-1}\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{T}f_{n}-\tilde{V}_{n}^{-1}\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{T}Y_{n}\right\rVert_{\tilde{V}_{n}}=\left\lVert\tilde{V}_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\Phi}_{n}^{T}(Y_{n}-f_{n})\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}(u_{i}^{T}(Y_{n}-f_{n}))^{2}},

Analysis on the black hole formations inside old neutron stars by isospin-violating dark matter with self-interaction
Guey-Lin Lin 1, Yen-Hsun Lin 2

Abstract
Fermionic dark matter (DM) with attractive self-interaction is possible to form black holes (BH) inside the Gyr-old neutron stars (NS). Therefore by observing such NS corresponding to their adjacent DM environments can place bounds on DM properties, eg. DM-baryon cross section σ χ ​ b \sigma_{\chi b} , DM mass m χ m_{\chi} , dark coupling α χ \alpha_{\chi} and mediator mass m ϕ m_{\phi} . In case of isospin violation, DM couples to neutron and proton in different strengths. Even NS is composed of protons roughly one to two percent of the total baryons, the contribution from protons to the DM capture rate could be drastically changed in the presence of isospin violation. We demonstrate that this effect can be important in certain cases. On the other hand, DM-forming BH inside the star is subject to many criteria and the underlying dynamics is rich with interesting features. We also systematically review the relevant physics based on the virial equation. Moreover, an accompanied python package dm2nsbh to realize the mechanism is also released on the github for other relevant research.

Accretion of dark matter onto a neutron star

III.1
General formalism for DM evolution

When the halo DM particles scatter with NS and lose significant
amount of energies, they are gravitationally trapped in the star.
The evolution of DM number

by the differential equation

\frac{dN_{\chi}(t)}{dt}=C_{c}-C_{e}N_{\chi}(t),

(15)

where

is negligible

In our interested parameter space,

can be ignored so that

N_{\chi}(t)=C_{c}t.

(16)

Thus, DM would accumulate without limit in the star. As a remark, it
was also pointed out in Refs.

;
Chen:2018ohx

generally negligible unless

Thus it is reasonable to neglect the DM self-capture in this study.

IV
Black hole formation in neutron star

In this section, we summarize the criteria and results for DM
collapsing into a BH in the NS from Refs.
Kouvaris:2011gb
;
Bramante:2013nma
.
We shall not re-derive the formulae since they are not the focus of this work.
Instead we refer the readers to original papers for technical details.
In addition, a python package

When DM particles are captured, they will thermalize with the surrounding
nucleons within a short time interval and form a dark spherical cloud.
The thermal radius of the cloud
r
can be determined by the virial
equation
Bramante:2013nma

2\langle E_{k}\rangle=\frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho_{b}m_{\chi}r^{2}+\frac{GN_{\chi}m_{\chi}^{2}}{r}+\sum_{j}^{N_{\chi}-1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\chi}}{r_{j}}e^{-m_{\phi}r_{j}}+\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}e^{-m_{\phi}r_{j}}\right).

(23)

The first two terms on the RHS arise from gravitational potentials of NS
and DM, respectively, and the last term is from the Yukawa interactions between DM particles.
The parameters
E_{k}
is the kinetic energy per DM particle,
\rho_{b}

the core baryon density,
\alpha_{\chi}
the dark fine structure constant defined in Eq. (
7
),

m_{\phi}
the mass of the scalar mediator, and
r_{j}
the interparticle distance.

As the number of DM
N_{\chi}
increases with time, the last two terms on the RHS of Eq. (
23
) increases with time as well. Once
N_{\chi}
crosses the critical value

N_{{\rm crit}}
that those two terms becomes more dominant than the first term, the dark spherical cloud
supported by the kinetic energy
E_{k}
can no longer sustain the
potentials on the RHS of Eq. (
23
). Therefore,
DM particles will initiate self-gravitating and collapse. At the
moment of collapse with
N_{\chi}\gtrsim 10^{36}
,
the collapse starts
from a degenerate state with the properties
Bramante:2013nma

E_{k,{\rm deg}}=\frac{(9\pi N_{\chi}/4)^{2/3}}{2m_{\chi}r_{{\rm th,deg}}^{2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad r_{{\rm th,deg}}\approx 2.4\times 10^{-4}\,{\rm cm}\,N_{\chi}^{1/6}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(24)

On the contrary, if
N_{\chi}\lesssim 10^{36}
, the collapse happens
in a non-degenerate state with

E_{k,{\rm nondeg}}=\frac{3}{2}T_{\chi}\quad{\rm and}\quad r_{{\rm th,nondeg}}\approx 250\,{\rm cm}\,\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-1/2}

(25)

where
T_{\chi}
is the DM temperature. It is reasonable to take
T_{\chi}=T_{{\rm NS}}=10^{5}\,{\rm K}

IV.1
State after collapse

When DM initiates self-gravitating, there are several phases depending
on
\alpha_{\chi}
and
m_{\phi}/m_{\chi}
. We briefly summarize
as follows:

1.

Degenerate and partly screened
. For
\alpha_{\chi}\lesssim m_{\phi}/m_{\chi}
,
DM will collapse from a degenerate state and the partly screened condition
r_{j}<1/m_{\phi}
is satisfied during the collapse.
The critical number to induce such a collapse is given by

N_{{\rm crit,dps}}\approx 1.1\times 10^{25}\,\alpha_{\chi}^{-6}\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)^{12}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-9}.

(26)

2.

Degenerate and strongly screened.
For
\alpha_{\chi}\gtrsim m_{\phi}/m_{\chi}
,
DM will also collapse from a degenerate state
but the strongly screened condition,

r_{j}>1/m_{\phi}
, is satisfied. In this scenario, the
analytical expression for the critical DM number cannot be obtained.
One must solve Eq. (
23
) numerically under strongly
screened limit. Hence, the virial equation is approximated as

\frac{(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}m_{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\chi}y^{2}}=\frac{(4\pi/3)^{1/3}GN_{\chi}\rho_{b}m_{\chi}y^{2}}{m_{\phi}^{2}}+8\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}e^{-y}\left(\frac{1}{y}+1\right),

(27)

where
y=(4\pi/3)^{1/3}r_{{\rm th,deg}}/N_{\chi}^{1/3}
. The
N_{\chi}

that satisfies the above identity with
y\gtrsim 1
is the critical
number
N_{{\rm crit,dss}}
for DM to undergo self-collapse in this phase.

3.

Non-degenerate and strongly screened.
If
N_{{\rm crit}}

obtained in cases
1
and
2
are smaller than

10^{36}
, then the collapse starts from the non-degenerate state
instead of the degenerate one. Again, one can only obtain the critical DM number
through solving Eq. (
23
) numerically in the strongly
screened limit,

\displaystyle 3T_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\frac{(4\pi/3)^{1/3}GN_{\chi}\rho_{b}m_{\chi}y^{\prime 2}}{m_{\phi}^{2}}+\frac{(4\pi/3)^{1/3}GN_{\chi}m_{\chi}^{2}m_{\phi}}{y^{\prime}}+8\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}e^{-y^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{y^{\prime}}+1\right),

where

y^{\prime}=\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)^{1/3}m_{\phi}\frac{r_{{\rm th,nondeg}}}{N_{\chi}^{1/3}}\approx 21\,\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(28)

Once
N_{{\rm crit,nss}}
is numerically obtained, there are two
separate scenarios as follows:

(a)

If the corresponding
N_{{\rm crit,nss}}
leads to
y^{\prime}<1
,
the collapse continues without being halted by the Fermi pressure
after DM becoming degenerate when

\alpha_{\chi}>0.016\,\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-3/2}.

(29)

(b)

On the other hand, if
y^{\prime}>1
for the given
N_{{\rm crit,nss}}
,
then the collapse continues for

\alpha_{\chi}>2.7\times 10^{-6}\frac{e^{21\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{{\rm GeV}}{m_{\chi}}}}}{\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)\left[1+0.047\left(\frac{{\rm MeV}}{m_{\phi}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}}\right]}.

(30)

4.

Non-degenerate and partly screened.

When DM is partly screened, we have

y^{\prime}=1.6m_{\phi}r/N_{\chi}^{1/3}\lesssim 1
and for the non-degenerate case
r
is
determined by
r_{\rm th,nondeg}
. This gives the first criterion

N_{\chi,{\rm ndps}}\gtrsim 10^{40}\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{\rm MeV}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{\rm GeV}\right)^{-3/2}

(31)

However, to trigger gravitational instability, by solving Eq. (
23
) with the
Yukawa potential term replaced by
4\pi\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}/y^{\prime 3}
in the partly screened
limit
Bramante:2013nma
, the critical number is estimated as

N_{{\rm crit,ndps}}\approx 2\times 10^{34}\,\alpha_{\chi}^{-1}\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{{\rm MeV}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-3/2}.

(32)

Therefore, for DM to collapse into non-degenerate and partly screened phase,

N_{\chi,{\rm ndps}}>N_{{\rm crit,ndps}}
or

\alpha_{\chi}\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{\rm MeV}\right)\lesssim 2\times 10^{-6}

(33)

as well as
N_{\chi}>N_{{\rm crit,ndps}}
should both be satisfied simultaneously.
However, in our interested parameter space, both conditions are usually not met simultaneously.
Even both are satisfied, the condition for collapse from non-degenerate and strongly screened phase
is achieved first.
So we will not discuss further this scenario.
A similar statement is also made in Ref.
Bramante:2013nma
and we refer the interested readers to
this article for details.

From the above discussions, we summarize that the proportional relation that describes
N_{\rm crit}
in the partly screened limit, Eqs. (
26
) and (
32
), can be approximated as

N_{\rm crit}\propto\alpha_{\chi}^{-a}m_{\phi}^{b}m_{\chi}^{-c},

(34)

where
a,b
and
c
are positive real numbers.
For strongly screened limit, the Yukawa term has a non-linear dependency on
e^{-y}\sim e^{-1/N_{\chi}^{1/3}}
. An explicit relation like Eq. (
34
) cannot be obtained. However, we found that Eq. (
34
) is still a good approximation in this limit although it is less sensitive to
\alpha_{\chi}
, or
a\to 0
. A quantitative justification of this is given in Appendix
B
.

IV.2
Criteria for continuing collapse

For each of the four cases discussed above, DMs after the collapse are always
degenerate and relativistic prior to forming a BH. To overcome
the relativistic Fermi pressure that tends to halt the collapse, the condition

\alpha_{\chi}>4.7\left(\frac{m_{\phi}}{m_{\chi}}\right)^{2}

(35)

must hold. In addition, the BH must
be heavier than
3.4\times 10^{36}\,{\rm GeV}
to prevent itself from evaporation due to Hawking radiation
Bramante:2013nma
;
Garani:2018kkd
.
Hence the condition

N_{{\rm crit}}>3.4\times 10^{36}\,\left(\frac{m_{\chi}}{{\rm GeV}}\right)^{-1}

(36)

should be satisfied in order to destroy the host star within
10^{-3}\,{\rm Gyr}
.
The content in this section describes a systematic way to determine
the collapse of DM in NS. A more compact and clear steps are summarized
in Table I of Ref.
Bramante:2013nma
.

VI
Summary

In this work, we have pointed out that isospin violation could
significantly change the NS sensitivities to
\sigma_{\chi n}
.
Up to recent NS studies, proton contribution to the capture rate is ignored due to the assumption of isospin symmetry
in the fermionic DM-baryon interaction.
7
7

7

For bosonic DM with isospin violation
in the NS is studied in Ref.
Zheng:2014fya
.

On the other hand, we have demonstrated that the
proton contribution to the capture rate becomes comparable and even
exceeds the neutron contribution for
\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{p}
much smaller than unity. Thus the sensitivity regions are enlarged.
This has not been taken into
account for the case of attractive fermionic DM. In our new study,
the DM capture rate is based on a more realistic NS profile. Moreover, the
suppression due to Pauli blocking effect has been considered in calculating
C_{c}
.

For the DM-forming BH inside the NS, the fermionic case contains very rich
dynamics as we have summarized in Sec.
IV
.
A systematic algorithm to compute the fate of NS based on the DM particle
parameters
\alpha_{\chi}
,
m_{\chi}
,
m_{\phi}
and the captured

N_{\chi}
in the NS is given in Ref.
Bramante:2013nma
. Here we not only
implement it into our study, we also provide a python package
dm2nsbh

accompanied with this work. This package is a realization of the above algorithm and released
on the github. In
dm2nsbh
, functions for calculating capture
rate
C_{c}
and DM number
N_{\chi}
with different
\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{p}

are also provided. We hope this code release could stimulate
a more thorough and improved studies on this issue.
In addition, the artifact in the algorithm mentioned in Sec.
IV

and Ref.
Bramante:2013nma
that causes the gaps between low and high
m_{\chi}
regimes
in the plots can be resolved eventually.

In closing, we stress that the microscopic feature of DM self-interaction
is not yet well understood at the present time. Thus, if DM self-interaction
is repulsive instead of attractive, the last term in the virial equation, Eq. (
23
), changes sign and consequently counteracts the gravitational attraction. This effect would disfavor the collapse of DMs into a BH. Hence NS is no longer able to constrain DM parameter space. On the other hand, if DMs attract each other as studied in this work, it is possible for them to form a
large nugget that contains enormous number of DM particles as investigated in Refs.
Wise:2014jva
;
Gresham:2017zqi
;
Gresham:2017cvl
;
Gresham:2018anj
.
The nugget may have different scattering cross section with the baryons
in NS.
Hence the method employed here for constraining DM properties should be modified. We relegate this to future studies.

Appendix A
Model details

In this appendix we present some details of the phenomenological framework given in Sec.
II
. The purpose is to understand how DM and its self-interaction are introduced in this work and the requirement for an attractive DM self-interaction.

To begin with, we introduce two Dirac fermions
\xi
,
\eta
and the complex scalar field
\Phi
in the dark sector. Both
\xi
and
\Phi
carry
U(1)_{d}
charge
g_{d}
while

\eta
is a
U(1)_{d}
singlet. Thus, we can write down the following mass related Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}_{M}=-m_{\xi}\bar{\xi}\xi-m_{\eta}\bar{\eta}\eta-y\Phi^{*}\bar{\xi}\eta-y\Phi\bar{\eta}\xi,

(40)

where
y
is the Yukawa coupling. Spontaneous symmetry breaking gives
\Phi=(\phi+v_{d}+i\sigma)/\sqrt{2}
.
Substituting this expression into Eq. (
40
), we obtain fermionic mass terms as

\mathcal{L}_{M}\supset-m_{\xi}\bar{\xi}\xi-m_{\eta}\bar{\eta}\eta-yv_{d}(\bar{\xi}\eta+\bar{\eta}\xi)/\sqrt{2}.

(41)

To diagonalize the above mass terms, we rotate
(\xi,\eta)
by an angle
\theta
and obtain
the physical fields
(\psi,\chi)
such that
\psi=\xi\cos\theta-\eta\sin\theta
and
\chi=\xi\sin\theta+\eta\cos\theta
, with

\tan 2\theta=\frac{\sqrt{2}yv_{d}}{m_{\eta}-m_{\xi}}.

(42)

The diagonalized mass terms are given by

\mathcal{L}_{m}=-m_{\psi}\bar{\psi}\psi-m_{\chi}\bar{\chi}\chi,

(43)

where
m_{\psi}=M_{+}
and
m_{\chi}=M_{-}
with

M_{\pm}=\frac{(m_{\xi}+m_{\eta})\pm\sqrt{(m_{\xi}-m_{\eta})^{2}+2v_{d}^{2}y^{2}}}{2}.

(44)

We note that
m_{\psi}>m_{\chi}
in general. In the
(\psi,\chi)
basis, the Yukawa interactions can be written as

\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=y(\sin 2\theta\bar{\psi}\psi-\sin 2\theta\bar{\chi}\chi-\cos 2\theta\bar{\psi}\chi-\cos 2\theta\bar{\chi}\psi)\phi/\sqrt{2}.

(45)

If

Appendix B
Proportional relation of
N_{\rm crit}

In this appendix, we justify the proportional relation, Eq. (
34
), given in Sec.
IV.1
.
The purpose of this appendix is to show the dependencies of
N_{\rm crit}
on

\alpha_{\chi}
,
m_{\chi}
and
m_{\phi}
in an illustrative way instead of deriving
the analytical expressions for
N_{\rm crit}
.
Various approximations are employed here.
Those who are interested in obtaining more accurate results should refer to Sec.
IV.1
or directly use the package
dm2nsbh
.

B.1
Partly screened limit

To trigger the gravitational instability, the last two terms on the RHS
of Eq. (
23
) should become more important than the NS background potential (the first term).
8
8

8

If the NS background potential always dominates, then it will be canceled by the thermal energy
2E_{k}
. The entire system will never collapse.

Moreover, when the collapse begins in the partly screened limit, the Yukawa potential (the third term) can be approximated by
the Coulomb potential due to the interparticle distance
r_{j}<1/m_{\phi}
. It will also overcome the DM self-gravity (the second term).
Thus, the virial equation becomes

\frac{(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}m_{\phi}^{2}}{m_{\chi}y^{2}}=\frac{4\pi\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}}{y^{3}}.

With
y=(4\pi/3)^{1/3}m_{\phi}r_{\rm th,deg}/N_{\chi}^{1/3}
, we obtain a simple analytical approximation for
N_{\rm crit}
in the degenerate case,

N_{\rm crit,dps}\approx\frac{243}{8192}\sqrt{\frac{3\pi^{7}}{(G\rho_{b})^{3}}}\alpha_{\chi}^{-6}m_{\phi}^{12}m_{\chi}^{-6},

(46)

which is the same as Eq. (
26
).
On the other hand, for the non-degenerate and partly screened case, the virial equation becomes

-2T_{\chi}=\frac{4\pi\alpha_{\chi}m_{\phi}}{y^{\prime 3}}

where
y^{\prime}
is given in Eq. (
28
). Solving this equation yields

N_{\rm crit,ndps}=\left(\frac{9T_{\chi}^{5/3}}{4\pi G\rho_{b}}\right)^{3/2}\alpha_{\chi}^{-1}m_{\phi}^{2}m_{\chi}^{-3/2}

(47)

which is the same as Eq. (
32
).

B.2
Strongly screened limit

Here we only present the degenerate case for simplicity. In this limit with
DM self-gravity term ignored, Eq. (
27
) can be recast as

\frac{(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}m_{\phi}}{8\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi}}=y(y+1)e^{-y}.

NS collapse begins when
y\gtrsim 1
.
Taking logarithm on both sides of the above equation, the RHS may be approximated as
-y
for
y\gg 1
. Hence we arrive at

y=-\ln\left[\frac{(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}m_{\phi}}{8\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi}}\right]\equiv-\ln f(\alpha_{\chi},m_{\phi},m_{\chi}).

Thus, even
\alpha_{\chi}
,
m_{\phi}
and
m_{\chi}
can vary over many orders of magnitude,
y

only varies slowly.
Its value is greater than unity so long as
\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi}/m_{\phi}>(3\pi^{2})^{2/3}/8\approx 0.179
. This criterion is always satisfied since the collapse from the strongly screened limit requires
\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi}/m_{\phi}\gtrsim 1
. Using the relation between
y
and
N_{\chi}
, we obtain

N_{\rm crit,dss}=\frac{16}{3}\frac{\pi^{3}}{(\ln f)^{6}}\frac{m_{\phi}^{6}m_{\chi}^{-3}}{(4\pi G\rho_{b}/3)^{3/2}},

(48)

which depends on
\alpha_{\chi}
only logarithmically.
Based on the analysis given in this appendix, we argue quantitatively that the proportional relation, Eq. (
34
),

N_{\rm crit}\propto\alpha_{\chi}^{-a}m_{\phi}^{b}m_{\chi}^{-c}

is reasonable and justified.

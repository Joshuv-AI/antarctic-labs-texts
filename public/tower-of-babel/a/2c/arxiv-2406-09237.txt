Absorptive corrections to the electromagnetic form factor in high-energy elastic proton-proton scattering
A. A. Poblaguev

Abstract
Recently, it was noted that absorptive corrections to the electromagnetic form factor in high-energy proton-proton scattering are important for the theoretical interpretation of the p ↑ ​ p p^{\uparrow}{p} and p ↑ ​ A p^{\uparrow}{A} analyzing power A N ​ ( t ) A_{\text{N}}(t) measurements with the Hydrogen Jet Target polarimeter (HJET) at RHIC. Here, a concise expression for the absorptive correction was derived within the eikonal approach. The resulting analysis reveals a systematic bias, nearly independent of the beam energy, in the experimental determination of the real-to-imaginary ratio ρ \rho when absorption effects are overlooked in the data analysis. Quantification of this bias, as ρ meas = ρ + ( 0.036 ± 0.016 ) bias \rho^{\text{meas}}=\rho+(0.036\pm 0.016)_{\text{bias}} , was achieved using a Regge fit applied to available proton-proton measurements of ρ meas ​ ( s ) \rho^{\text{meas}}(s) and σ tot meas ​ ( s ) \sigma^{\text{meas}}_{\text{tot}}(s) . Considering the potential impact of such an effect on the experimentally determined A N ​ ( t ) A_{\text{N}}(t) , one may enhance consistency between the HJET and STAR measurements of the hadronic spin-flip amplitude. While the sign of the bias in the value of ρ \rho aligns with the anticipated effective increase in the proton charge radius in p ​ p pp scattering due to absorption, it amplifies the observed discrepancy between σ tot meas \sigma^{\text{meas}}_{\text{tot}} and ρ meas \rho^{\text{meas}} values at s = 13 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=13\,\text{TeV} as measured in the TOTEM experiment. Evaluation (using published TOTEM data) of the measured proton-proton d ​ σ / d ​ t d\sigma/dt dependence on the absorptive corrections indicated that possible soft photon corrections to the hadronic amplitude slope may be essential for such data analysis.



I
Introduction

Forward elastic proton-proton scattering is notable for having an electromagnetic amplitude that can exceed the hadronic one. Because the electromagnetic amplitude is well understood, experimental measurements in the Coulomb-nuclear interference (CNI) region are valuable for probing the structure of the hadronic amplitude. However, interpreting CNI effects can be challenging due to the presence of radiative corrections
Afanasev et al. 2024
. Depending on the physical process under study, various theoretical techniques have been developed to accurately describe the experimental observables.

In
\mathit{pp}
scattering, the exchange of multiple soft photons, as depicted schematically in Fig.
1
, plays a significant role. A key consequence of this multiple photon exchange is the induction of Coulomb phases,
\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}
and
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}
, which affect the electromagnetic and hadronic
pp
amplitudes, respectively. While theoretical estimates of these Coulomb phases exhibit a nonvanishing dependence on the photon mass
\lambda\to 0
, introduced to avoid infrared divergence, the phase difference
\delta_{C}=\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}-\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}
remains free of such uncertainties. The theoretical understanding of Coulomb phases in high-energy
pp
scattering has a long history, as partially discussed in Refs.
Akhiezer and Pomeranchuk 1946
;
Bethe 1958
;
Solov’ev 1965
;
West and Yennie 1968
;
Cahn 1982
, with ongoing investigations into the theoretical aspects of Coulomb-nuclear interference.

Here, we adopt the eikonal approach, which was used in Refs.
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
;
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
to emphasize the significance of absorptive corrections in interpreting experimental measurements of the transverse analyzing power
A_{\text{N}}(t)
in elastic
p^{\uparrow}p
and, particularly,
p^{\uparrow}A
scattering. In elastic
\mathit{pp}
scattering, the proton’s electromagnetic form factor is effectively altered due to the dependence of the inelastic scattering probability on the impact parameter. As a result, both the differential cross section
d\sigma/dt
and the analyzing power
A_{\text{N}}(t)
are modified in the CNI region.

The primary goal of this paper is to obtain a compact expression for the absorptive correction which can be used in the
A_{\text{N}}(t)
measurements with the Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collider hydrogen-gas jet target (HJET) polarimeter
Poblaguev et al. 2019
;
Poblaguev et al. 2020
;
Poblaguev 2024
. For that, the absorptive correction was related to the previously calculated
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}

Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
using the eikonal approach. A simple method to remove the photon mass dependent part of
\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}^{\lambda}
was considered. The remaining ambiguity in the value of
\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}
was constrained by examining the
\rho(s)
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)
Regge fit dependence on the absorptive correction. Possible effects of the correction for the experimental measurements of forward elastic proton-proton scattering will be discussed.

By default, numerical estimates below will be given for 100 GeV proton beam scattering off a fixed target proton.

II
The Eikonal Model

Following Ref.
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
, for elastic high-energy forward
pp
scattering, the Coulomb amplitude in the Born approximation can be written as

f_{C}(t)=\frac{2\alpha}{t}e^{B_{E}t/2},

(1)

where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant,
t
stands for the momentum transfer squared, and

B_{E}=\frac{2\langle{r_{E}^{2}}\rangle}{3}=12.1\,\text{GeV}^{2}

(2)

is expressed via the proton charge radius
Workman et al. 2022
. Applying the Fourier transformation, one obtains

\displaystyle\chi_{C}(\boldsymbol{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{q_{T}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{bq_{T}}}\,f_{C}(q_{T}^{2})

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{q_{T}dq_{T}}{q_{T}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}e^{-B_{E}q_{T}^{2}/2}J_{0}(bq_{T}),

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{q_{T}}
is the transverse momentum of the scattered proton (
q_{T}^{2}\!\approx\!-t
),
J_{0}(z)
is the Bessel function of zero order, and a small photon mass
\lambda
is included in Eq. (
3
) to keep the integral finite.

Considering multiple photon exchanges, the net long-range Coulomb amplitude can be written
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
as

\displaystyle f_{C}^{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}bdbJ_{0}(bq_{T})i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]

\displaystyle=f_{C}(t)e^{i\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}}.

(4)

For the amplitude normalization used, the optical theorem relates the hadronic elastic
pp
amplitude to the total cross section
\sigma_{\text{tot}}\!=\!38.39\,\text{mb}

Fagundes et al. 2017
, as

\displaystyle f_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=(i+\rho)\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}e^{Bt/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\alpha(i+\rho)}{t_{c}}e^{Bt/2},

(5)

where
\rho\!=\!-0.079

Fagundes et al. 2017
,
B\!=\!11.2\,\text{GeV}^{-2}

Bartenev et al. 1973
, and
-t_{c}\!=\!8\pi\alpha/\sigma_{\text{tot}}\!=\!1.86\!\times\!10^{-3}\,\text{GeV}^{2}
. In the eikonal analysis, the multiple photon exchange leads to

\displaystyle f_{N}^{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=(i+\rho)\int_{0}^{\infty}bdbJ_{0}(bq_{T})\gamma_{N}(b)e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}

\displaystyle=f_{N}(t)e^{i\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}},

(6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\alpha}{Bt_{c}}e^{-b^{2}/2B}.

(7)

For the Coulomb amplitude (
1
), the phases
\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}
and
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}
were calculated analytically
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
(in lowest order of
\alpha
and
\lambda\to 0
):

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}/\alpha

\displaystyle=\ln{\frac{B_{E}\lambda^{2}}{2}}+2\gamma+\ln w-\text{Ei}\left(\frac{w}{2}\right)

\displaystyle+e^{w}\left[2E_{1}(w)-E_{1}\left(\frac{w}{2}\right)\right],

(8)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}/\alpha

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{B_{E}\lambda^{2}}{2}+2\gamma+\ln{w}+\ln{\frac{B^{2}}{B_{E}^{2}}}-\text{Ei}(z)

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{B_{E}\lambda^{2}}{2}+\gamma+\ln\frac{B+B_{E}}{B_{E}}-z+{\cal O}(z^{2})

(9)

where
\mathrm{Ei}
and
E_{1}
are the exponential integral functions,
w\!=\!B_{E}q_{T}^{2}/2
,
z\!=\!B^{2}q_{T}^{2}/2(B+B_{E})
, and
\gamma\!=\!0.5772\ldots
is Euler’s constant.

The phase difference, which for
t\to 0
can be approximated
Cahn 1982
as

\displaystyle\delta_{C}(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}\!-\!\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\approx-\alpha\left[\gamma+\ln{\frac{(B\!+\!B_{E})|t_{c}|}{2}}\right]+\alpha\ln{\frac{t_{c}}{t}},

(10)

is independent of the photon mass
\lambda
.

III
Absorptive Corrections

The electromagnetic form factor, as given in Eq. (
1
), was determined in lepton-proton interactions. However, in proton-proton scattering, absorptive corrections may substantially reduce the partial elastic amplitude at small impact parameters
\boldsymbol{b}

Krelina and Kopeliovich 2019
. To evaluate this correction, the absorptive factor,
1-\gamma_{N}(b)
, may be considered
Krelina and Kopeliovich 2019
in the impact parameter space:

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{C}^{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}b\,db\,J_{0}(bq_{T})i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]\left[1-\gamma_{N}(b)\right].

(11)

Using Eqs. (
4
), (
7
), and (
6
), one readily arrives at

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{C}^{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{C}^{\gamma}(t)-if_{N}(t)\left(1-e^{i\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx f_{C}(t)\left[e^{i\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}}+\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}\,t/t_{c}\right],

(12)

which leads to the following effective correction of the electromagnetic form factor:

B_{E}\to B_{E}^{\text{eff}}\approx B_{E}+2\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}/t_{c}.

(13)

Thus, the absorption correction to the electromagnetic form factor is proportional to the Coulomb phase
\Phi_{\text{NC}}
, which, however, explicitly depends on the photon mass used in the calculations. On the other hand, this suggests that if Eq. (
11
) adequately describes the absorption effect, there must be a way to eliminate the photon mass-dependent component from the value of
\Phi_{\text{NC}}
.

IV
The Coulomb Phases Dependence on the Photon Mass

For estimates, it is helpful to consider altering
f_{C}^{\gamma}(q_{T})
and
f_{N}^{\gamma}(q_{T})
by adding a constant term to the electromagnetic Born amplitude in the impact space,
\chi_{C}(b)\to\chi_{0}+\chi_{C}(b)
:

\displaystyle i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]

\displaystyle\to i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\right]e^{i\chi_{0}}+i\left[1-e^{i\chi_{0}}\right],

(14)

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}

\displaystyle\to\gamma_{N}e^{i\chi_{C}(b)}\times e^{i\chi_{0}}.

(15)

Notably, both
\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}
and
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}
are altered by the same value
\chi_{0}
. It should also be noted that in addition to phase
i\chi_{0}
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
14
) contains term
i[1-\exp{(i\chi_{0})}]\approx\chi_{0}
, which leads to a
\delta
-function contribution,
\chi_{0}\delta(\boldsymbol{q_{T}})
, to
f_{C}^{\gamma}(t)
. Possible appearance of such terms was discussed in Ref.
Nekrasov 2024
.

Since introducing the photon mass
\lambda
to Eq. (
1
) switches off the Coulomb field for large values of the impact parameter (
\chi_{C}(b)\to 0
if
b\gg 1/\lambda
), the photon mass-dependent constant term
\chi_{0}
may be generated by a product in this procedure.

For simplicity, let
q=q_{T}\sqrt{B_{E}/2}
and
a=\lambda\sqrt{B_{E}/2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\chi(b,a)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq\,qe^{-q^{2}}}{q^{2}+a^{2}}J_{0}(bq)=\chi_{0}(a)+\chi^{\prime}(b,a),

(16)

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(a)

\displaystyle=\chi(0,a)=\frac{e^{-a^{2}}E_{1}(a^{2})}{2}\approx-\ln{a}-\gamma/2,

(17)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}(b,a)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq\,qe^{-q^{2}}}{q^{2}+a^{2}}\left[J_{0}(bq)-1\right]

(18)

\chi^{\prime}(b,a)

18
)].

Numerical calculations of
\chi^{\prime}(b,a)
are depicted in Fig.
2
. For small values of
b

J_{0}(bq)\to 1
and
\chi_{0}^{\prime}(b,a)\to 0
. If
b
is large, it was numerically found

\chi^{\prime}(b,a=0)\approx-\ln{b}+\ln{2}-\gamma/2.

(19)

for
\lambda\!\to\!0
.
However, for a finite (but small)
\lambda
and
b\!>\!1/\lambda
,

\chi^{\prime}(b,a)=\ln{a}+\gamma/2\approx-\chi_{0}(a),

(20)

which, after adding
\chi_{0}(a)
, leads to
\chi_{C}(b)=0
if
b\gtrsim 1/\lambda
. According to Eqs. (
15
), such an independent of impact parameter
b
term
\chi_{0}(a)
may be used to eliminate the Coulomb phase dependence on
\lambda
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}^{\lambda}+2\alpha\chi_{0}(\lambda\sqrt{B_{E}/2})

\displaystyle\approx\alpha\left[C+\ln{\frac{B+B_{E}}{2B_{E}}}+\frac{B^{2}t}{2(B+B_{E})}\right].

(21)

However, since
\chi_{0}(a)
is the value of
\chi(b,a)
at
b=0
rather than a constant term
\chi_{0}
,

\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}
is still determined up to some unknown constant
C
.

Substituting
\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}
into Eq. (
13
), one finds

\displaystyle B_{E}^{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\langle r_{E}^{2}\rangle}{3}-\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}\left[C+\ln{\frac{B+B_{E}}{2B_{E}}}\right].

(22)

The
t
dependent terms were omitted in Eq. (
22
), first, because the
t
dependence was not considered for
B_{E}
in Eq. (
1
), and second, such terms are small in CNI scattering and inessential beyond the CNI region (large
|t|
) where the hadronic amplitude strongly dominates.

Using the slope
B(s)
dependence
Bartenev et al. 1973
on the center of mass squared energy
s
,
the logarithmic term in Eq. (
22
) can be approximated by

\ln{\frac{B+B_{E}}{2B_{E}}}=\ln{\left(1+\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}}{B_{E}}\ln{\sqrt{\frac{s}{s_{E}}}}\right)},

(23)

where
\alpha^{\prime}=0.278\pm 0.024\,\text{GeV}^{-2}

Bartenev et al. 1973
and
s_{E}^{1/2}\approx 32\,\text{GeV}
. Even for
s^{1/2}=13\,\text{TeV}
, such a correction to
\Phi_{\mathrm{NC}}
is small,
\sim 0.002
, and this term may be considered as negligible.

V
Systematic Bias in Measurements of the Real-to-Imaginary Ratio

For elastic proton-proton scattering, the differential cross-section dependence on
t
can be written as

\frac{d\sigma}{dt}=\frac{\sigma_{\text{tot}}^{2}}{16\pi}\left[\left(\frac{t_{c}}{t}\right)^{2}-2(\rho+\delta_{C})\frac{t_{c}}{t}+1+\rho^{2}\right]e^{Bt}.

(24)

Here, for simplicity,
t_{c}/t
denotes the ratio of the electromagnetic and imaginary parts of the hadronic amplitudes
(t_{c}/t)\exp{\left[(B_{E}-B)t/2\right]}
.

The absorptive correction effectively modifies the electromagnetic amplitude as

\frac{t_{c}}{t}\to\frac{t_{c}}{t}+\alpha C+\frac{\alpha B^{2}t_{c}}{2(B+B_{E})}\frac{t}{t_{c}}.

(25)

The
t
-dependent term in
\Phi_{\text{NC}}
results in the following alteration in
d\sigma/dt

\displaystyle 1+\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\to 1+\rho^{2}+\frac{\alpha B^{2}t_{c}}{B+B_{E}}\approx 1+\rho^{2}-7\times 10^{-5},

(26)

which is much smaller than the typical uncertainties
2\Delta\sigma_{\text{tot}}/\sigma_{\text{tot}}
due to experimental accuracy
\Delta\sigma_{\text{tot}}
for the total cross-section.

The
\alpha C
term in (
25
) leads to a systematic bias
Poblaguev 2019

\rho^{\text{meas}}=\rho-\alpha C,

(27)

in the experimental determination of the real-to-imaginary ratio
\rho
. Such a bias, which is expected to have only a very weak dependence on
s
, may potentially be recognized in a Regge fit of the experimental values of
\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)
and
\rho(s)
.

For unpolarized proton-proton scattering, the elastic amplitude’s dependence on
s
can be approximated (model AU–L
\gamma
=2 of Ref.
Fagundes et al. 2017
) by

\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)\left[i+\rho(s)\right]=f_{P}P(s)+f_{+}R^{+}(s)+f_{-}R^{-}(s),

(28)

where Regge pole terms

R^{\pm}(s,\alpha_{\pm})=\left[1\pm e^{-i\pi\alpha_{\pm}}\right]\left(\frac{s}{4m_{p}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha_{\pm}-1}

(29)

are encoded as
R^{+}
for (
f_{2},a_{2}
) and
R^{-}
for (
\omega,\rho
), and a Froissaron parametrization is used for the Pomeron contribution

P(s,\alpha_{F})=\pi\alpha_{F}\ln{\frac{s}{4m_{p}^{2}}}+i\left[1+\alpha_{F}\ln^{2}{\frac{s}{4m_{p}^{2}}}\right].

(30)

VI
Absorptive Corrections to the Measured Analyzing Power
\boldsymbol{A_{\text{N}}}

Omitting some small terms, the high-energy forward elastic
p^{\uparrow}p
analyzing power can be expressed
Kopeliovich and Lapidus 1974
;
Buttimore et al. 1999
via single spin-flip
\phi_{5}(t)
and nonflip
\phi_{+}(t)
helicity amplitudes as

A_{\text{N}}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{-t}}{m_{p}}\times\frac{(\kappa_{p}-2\mathrm{Im}\,r_{5})\,t_{c}/t-2\mathrm{Re}\,r_{5}}{(t_{c}/t)^{2}-2(\rho+\delta_{C})\,t_{c}/t+1+\rho^{2}},

(32)

where
\kappa_{p}\!=\!1.793
is the anomalous magnetic moment of the proton, and
|r_{5}|\!\sim\!0.02

Poblaguev et al. 2019
is the hadronic spin-flip amplitude parameter.

If the determination of
r_{5}
is the main goal of the experimental data analysis in the CNI region, typically, the real-to-imaginary ratio
\rho
is taken from the Regge fit of
\rho(s)
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)
, and
\delta_{C}
is calculated using Eq. (
32
). Since such a defined value of
\rho
already includes the absorptive correction, one can expect
Poblaguev 2019
that the absorption is already accounted for in the experimentally determined value of
r_{5}
. Nevertheless, since the absorption-related systematic bias modifies the Regge fit, the corresponding correction
\rho\to\rho\!+\!\Delta^{\text{abs}}\rho
should be applied in Eq. (
32
).

For the HJET energies,
\Delta^{\text{abs}}\rho\!=\!-0.0041
(
\sqrt{s}\!=\!13.76\,\text{GeV}
) and
\Delta^{\text{abs}}\rho\!=\!-0.0010
(
\sqrt{s}\!=\!21.92\,\text{GeV}
), which leads
Poblaguev et al. 2019
to a small (compared to the HJET experimental accuracy) correction

\Delta r_{5}=(-0.11+i\,0.86)\times\Delta^{\text{abs}}\rho

(33)

in the value of
r_{5}
. For the STAR measurement at
\sqrt{s}\!=\!200\,\text{GeV}

Adamczyk et al. 2013
,
\Delta^{\text{abs}}\rho\!=\!0.0256
, and the corresponding corrections
\Delta^{\text{abs}}\mathrm{Re}\,r_{5}\!\approx\!-0.003
and
\Delta^{\text{abs}}\mathrm{Im}\,r_{5}\!\approx\!0.022
may noticeably improve consistency between the extrapolation of the HJET results to
\sqrt{s}\!=\!200\,\text{GeV}
and the STAR value (see Fig. 3 in Ref.
Poblaguev 2024
).

A larger effect of the absorptive corrections is expected in the measurement of the
p^{\uparrow}\mathrm{Au}
analyzing power. It was demonstrated in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
that absorptive correction drastically changes the
A_{\text{N}}^{pAu}(t)
dependence on
t
, allowing qualitative agreement of the theoretical calculations with the results of the measurements done at HJET
Poblaguev 2024
;
Poblaguev et al.
. However, there is still room for improving the quantitative consistency. Since the absorptive correction calculations in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
had been done assuming cancellation of the photon mass term, variation of the absorption contribution, e.g., by scanning the parameter
\alpha C
, may be suggested.

VII
Discussion

Following the approach in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
for the absorption effect in forward elastic proton-proton scattering, it was shown that the absorptive correction to the electromagnetic form factor effectively biases the experimentally determined values of the real-to-imaginary ratio
\rho(s)
. This bias is almost independent of the center-of-mass energy
s
. An indication of such a bias (
31
) was found in the Regge fit (
28
) of the available proton-proton
\rho(s)
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)
measurements.

Nevertheless, it has not been definitively proven that the bias can be attributed to the absorptive correction. Therefore, alternative evaluations of the absorption are needed.

According to Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
, the proton-nucleus analyzing powers
A_{N}^{p\text{Au}}(t)
, measured at HJET
Poblaguev 2024
, clearly indicate the presence of absorption. However, in
Kopeliovich et al. 2024
, the difference between the calculated (including absorption) and measured
A_{N}^{p\text{Au}}(t)
was attributed to the hadronic spin-flip amplitude. Since this amplitude was not accurately predetermined, the normalization of the absorption effect remained essentially ambiguous. Nevertheless, relating the
pA
hadronic spin-flip amplitude to the
\mathit{pp}
amplitude
Kopeliovich and Trueman 2001
;
Poblaguev 2023
may allow one to reliably isolate the effect of the absorptive correction in the
p^{\uparrow}{A}
data analysis.

Notably, the absorptive correction to the electromagnetic form factor dependence on the photon mass
\lambda
can be canceled if the phase
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)
in Eq. (
13
) is replaced by

\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)\to\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)-\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)=-\delta_{C}(t).

(34)

Consequently, the Coulomb phase in Eq. (
24
) will be effectively doubled,

\rho+\delta_{C}(t)\to\rho+p\delta_{C}(t),\qquad p=2,

(35)

or, equivalently,

\alpha\left[C+\ln\frac{B+B_{E}}{2B_{E}}\right]\to-\delta_{C}(t_{c})-\alpha\ln\frac{t_{c}}{t}\approx-0.021.

(36)

Such an estimated value of
\alpha C
is in agreement with that given in Eq. (
31
).

To evaluate the effect of the
\ln{t}
term in Eq. (
36
) on the determination of
\rho
from the experimental
d\sigma/dt
distribution, the TOTEM data (Table 3 in
Antchev et al. 2019
) was used. For the
d\sigma/dt
parametrization (
24
),
t\!<\!|t|_{\mathit{max}}\!=\!0.07\,\text{GeV}
, and no absorptive corrections, the fit results are well consistent with those given in Table 4
Antchev et al. 2019
for
N_{b}\!=\!1
. The
\chi^{2}
difference for fits with (
p\!=\!2
) and without (
p\!=\!1
) the absorptive correction (
36
) as a function of
|t|_{\mathit{max}}
is shown in Fig.
4
. For
|t|_{\mathit{max}}\!<\!0.07\,\text{GeV}
, i.e., for the CNI scattering, the logarithmic term does not degrade the quality of the fit.

Thus, the assumption (
34
) is not in disagreement with available experimental data. However, to utilize such an approach, it is necessary either to correspondingly revise Eq. (
11
) or, for example, to postulate (and justify) that within the applicability of Eq. (
11
), the following should be assumed:

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\Phi_{\text{C}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)=0,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\lambda}(t,\lambda)=-\delta_{C}(t).

(37)

(

In the no absorptive correction fit, introducing
\beta
reduced
\chi^{2}/\text{ndf}
from 236.9/115 to a statistically excellent value of 105.9/114.

Comparing values of
\rho
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}
determined in
|t|<0.07\,\text{GeV}^{2}
fit with fixed
\beta=0.021
and
\beta=0
, one finds the following corrections due to the nonzero
\beta

\Delta^{\beta}\rho=+0.034,\qquad\Delta^{\beta}\sigma_{\text{tot}}=-3.4\,\text{mb},

(39)

which technically eliminate the observed discrepancy
Antchev et al. 2019
between measured values of
\rho
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}
at TOTEM. However, after applying the absorptive correction (
36
), the conclusion is less definitive,
\Delta^{\beta}\rho=+0.013
and
\Delta^{\beta}\sigma_{\text{tot}}=-4.2\,\text{mb}
. It should also be noted that the value of
\beta
found in the fit strongly (up to a factor of 2) depends on the
t
-range used, which suggests that the effective
B(t)
may contain other contributions, for example, due to systematic errors in the measurement. Therefore, the result (
39
) should be interpreted with caution.

In the analysis
Antchev et al. 2019
, a polynomial parametrization of the slope

B(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{b}}b_{n}t^{n-1},\quad N_{b}=1,2,3,

(40)

was utilized, allowing (if
N_{b}>1
) good approximation of the measured
d\sigma/dt
beyond the CNI region,
|t|>0.07\,\text{GeV}^{2}
.

Although consideration of the
B(t)
dependence on
t
is beyond the scope of this paper, it may be noted that Eq. (
38
) provides an example of a parametrization that is well consistent with the experimental
d\sigma/dt
for large
|t|
but leads to significant, compared to the polynomial parametrization (
40
), changes in
\rho
and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}
in the fit.

The numerical value
\beta\approx 3\alpha
of the correction (
38
), as well as its logarithmic dependence on
t
, suggest it may be caused by a possible radiative correction to the slope
B
. If so, calculating the
\ln{t_{c}/t}
corrections to the hadronic slope
B(t)
may be critically important for the experimental study of forward elastic proton-proton scattering. Depending on the value of
\beta
evaluated, a revision of the previously measured real-to-imaginary ratios
\rho(s)
may be required.

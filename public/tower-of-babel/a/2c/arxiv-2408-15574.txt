On p p -Laplace equations with a critical Hardy-Sobolev exponent and a Hardy potential
Phuong Le

Abstract
. For N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and 1 < p < N 1<p<N , we classify all positive 𝒟 1 , p ​ ( ℝ N ) \mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) -solutions to p p -Laplace equations with a critical Hardy-Sobolev exponent and a Hardy potential.

1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the doubly critical problem

(1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\dfrac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}u^{p-1}=\dfrac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-1}}{|x|^{s}}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u>0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

where
N\geq 2
,
1<p<N
,
0<\mu<\left(\frac{N-p}{p}\right)^{p}
,
0<s<p
,
p^{*}_{s}:=\frac{(N-s)p}{N-p}
is the critical Hardy-Sobolev exponent,
\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the
p
-Laplacian of
u
and

\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx<\infty\right\}

with
p^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
. Solutions
u
to (
1
) is understood in the following weak sense

(2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}u^{p-1}\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-1}}{|x|^{s}}\varphi\quad\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}).

By standard elliptic estimates, we know that any solution
u
of (
1
) belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
for some
0<\alpha<1
(see
[
9
,
18
,
28
]
). Due to the assumption
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, solutions to (
1
) are usually called
finite energy
ones. It is worth pointing out that problem (
1
) naturally arises as the Euler-Lagrange equation of the following Hardy–Sobolev–Maz’ya inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}|u|^{p}\right)dx\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p^{*}_{s}}}{|x|^{s}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{s}}},

which holds for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and some optimal constant
C>0
. This inequality is a consequence of the well known Hardy inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq\left(\frac{N-p}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}|u|^{p}\ dx

and the Sobolev–Hardy inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq C^{\prime}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{p^{*}_{s}}}{|x|^{s}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{s}}},

which both hold for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and some constant
C^{\prime}>0
(see
[
3
,
11
]
).

Problem (
1
) with
p=2
was studied extensively in the literature due to the availability of several analytic tools for the Laplace operator. In particular, Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
4
]
proved that every classical positive solution to the problem
-\Delta u=u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 3
) is radial and hence classified by the formula

u(x)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{N(N-2)}\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}

for some
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Later, Chen and Li
[
5
]
simplified the proof by combining the moving plane method
[
2
,
24
]
with the Kelvin transform. The same method can be applied to to the problem
-\Delta u=\frac{u^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{|x|^{s}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
N\geq 3
,
0<s<2
, to yield the classification of all positive solutions in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see
[
12
]
for instance). We stress that the semilinear critical problem is invariant under the Kelvin transform and the transform provides the right decaying properties of solutions so that the moving plane method on the whole space can be carried out. Hence the above results hold for all positive solutions without the finite energy assumption
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We also mention that all the solutions of the problem (
1
) for
p=2
and
s=0
have been classified by Terracini
[
27
]
.

Next, we consider the quasilinear case
p\neq 2
. This case is much more difficult due to the nonlinear nature of the
p
-Laplacian, the lack of regularity of the solutions and the fact that comparison principles are not equivalent to maximum principles in this case. Furthermore, the Kelvin type transform is not available for
p
-Laplace equations with
p\neq 2
. To overcome this difficulty, the main approach is to establish optimal estimates on asymptotic behaviors of solutions, then exploit the moving plane method or an integral identity to get the symmetry or the explicit form of the solutions. We summarize some main achievements regarding problem (
1
) in the quasilinear case below.

•

Solutions to problem (
1
) with
\mu=s=0
was classified by Sciunzi
[
23
]
and Vétois
[
31
]
via the moving plane method (the radial solutions were studied before in
[
14
]
and the symmetry of minimizers of the corresponding Sobolev inequality were derived in
[
26
]
). Later, Ciraolo, Figalli and Roncoroni
[
7
]
introduced a new approach exploiting an integral formula to classify
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\Sigma)
positive solutions to critical anisotropic
p
-Laplace equations in any convex cones
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

•

The case
\mu>s=0
was studied by Oliva, Sciunzi and Vaira
[
20
]
via the moving plane method exploiting some estimates in
[
33
,
32
]
(see also
[
1
]
for the uniqueness of radial solutions and the symmetry of minimizers).

•

Solutions to (
1
) in the case
s>\mu=0
have been classified recently in
[
17
,
6
]
exploiting estimates in
[
25
]
(see also
[
13
]
for the radial case).

•

In the general case that
\mu>0
,
s>0
, the uniqueness and asymptotic estimates for radial solutions are available, see
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
15
, Theorem 1.4]
(see also
[
13
]
). Some estimates for nonradial solutions have been obtained recently in
[
21
]
.

In this paper, we follow the approach in
[
20
]
to prove the symmetry and hence the uniqueness of solutions to (
1
). Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
1<p<N
,
0<\mu<\left(\frac{N-p}{p}\right)^{p}
,
0<s<p
. Let
u
be a solution to (
1
). Then
u
is radial and radially decreasing with respect to the origin.

Remark 1
.

Combining Theorem
1
with Theorem 1.4 in
[
15
]
, we deduce that problem (
1
) has a unique solution up to a dilation. This means that if
u
is a solution to (
1
), then all of its solutions are of the form
u_{\tau}(x):=\tau^{\frac{N-p}{p}}u(\tau x)
for some
\tau>0
.

The remainder of this paper is divided into two sections. In Section
2
, we recall some known results and prove some sharp pointwise gradient estimates which will be used later. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of our main result, namely, Theorem
1
, via the method of moving planes.

2.
Preliminaries and asymptotic estimates

It is important to study the summability of the second derivatives of the solutions to
p
-Laplace equations since such solutions are generally not in the
C^{2}
class. Such results, which are due to Damascelli and Sciunzi
[
8
]
, will be recalled here.

Theorem 2

(Hessian and reversed gradient estimates
[
8
]
)
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
1
). Then for
i=1,\dots,N
we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{p-2-\beta}|\nabla\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}dx\leq C

for any
\Omega\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and uniformly for any
y\in\Omega
, where
0\leq\beta<1
and
\gamma<N-2
if
N\geq 3
,
\gamma=0
if
N=2
and

C=C(p,\beta,\gamma,\mu,s,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},{\rm dist}(\Omega,\{0\})).

Moreover,

(3)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|^{t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}dx\leq C^{*}\text{ uniformly for any }y\in\Omega,

where
(p-2)^{+}\leq t<p-1
and
\gamma<N-2
if
N\geq 3
,
\gamma=0
if
N=2
and
C^{*}
depends on
C
.

The proof of Theorem
2
is almost the same as that of
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
with some minor changes and hence will be omitted.

Let
\rho\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be a positive function, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define
H^{1}(\Omega,\rho)
as the completion of
C^{1}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,\rho)}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\rho)},

where
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\rho)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\rho|\nabla v|^{2}dx
. We also define
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\rho)
as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega,\rho)
.
The following weighted Poincaré type inequality will be utilized later.

Theorem 3

(Weighted Poincaré type inequality
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\rho
be a positive function such that

(4)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\rho|x-y|^{\gamma}}dy\leq C_{1}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\gamma<N-2
if
N\geq 3
,
\gamma=0
if
N=2
.
Let
w\in H^{1}(\Omega,\rho)
be such that

(5)

|w(x)|\leq C_{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla w(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-1}}dy\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega.

Then we have

\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\leq C_{P}\int_{\Omega}\rho|\nabla w|^{2},

where
C_{P}
depends on
\Omega
and
C_{1}
. Furthermore,
C_{P}\to 0
as
|\Omega|\to 0
. The same inequality holds for
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\rho)
.

Here and throughout the paper, we use
|\Omega|
to denote the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We will also use
C
and
c
to denote generic positive constants which may change from line to line or even in the same line. Theorem
3
follows from the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, where condition (
5
) is used to obtain the key estimate (3.9) in
[
8
]
.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
two roots of the equation

(p-1)\gamma^{p}-(N-p)\gamma^{p-1}+\mu=0.

Then

0<\gamma_{1}<\frac{N-p}{p}<\gamma_{2}<\frac{N-p}{p-1}.

We recall the following asymptotic estimates for (
1
), which were obtained recently in
[
21
]
(see also
[
33
]
).

Theorem 4

(Asymptotic estimates
[
33
,
21
]
)
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
1
). Then

\displaystyle c|x|^{-\gamma_{1}}\leq u(x)\leq C|x|^{-\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }|x|<R_{0},

\displaystyle c|x|^{-\gamma_{2}}\leq u(x)\leq C|x|^{-\gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }|x|>R_{1},

where
C,c>0
and
0<R_{0}<1<R_{1}
are constants depending on
N,p,\mu,s
and the solution
u
.

By continuity, it is clear that for any given
R_{0},R_{1}>0
, the estimates in Theorem
4
still hold for suitable choice of
C,c>0
. By exploiting Theorem
4
, we will prove the following asymptotic estimates of the gradient of solutions.

Theorem 5

(Asymptotic gradient estimates)
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
1
). Then

\displaystyle c|x|^{-(\gamma_{1}+1)}\leq|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-(\gamma_{1}+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }|x|<R_{0}^{\prime},

\displaystyle c|x|^{-(\gamma_{2}+1)}\leq|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-(\gamma_{2}+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }|x|>R_{1}^{\prime},

where
C,c>0
and
0<R_{0}^{\prime}<1<R_{1}^{\prime}
are constants depending on
N,p,\mu,s
and the solution
u
.

Theorems
4
and
5
show that solutions to (
1
) with
s>0
exhibit the same asymptotic behaviors as in the case
s=0
. The proof of Theorem
5
relies on the following classification result.

Theorem 6

(Theorem 3.2 in
[
20
]
)
.

Let
1<p<N
,
0<\mu<\left(\frac{N-p}{p}\right)^{p}
and let
v\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
with
0<\alpha<1
be a positive solution to

-\Delta_{p}v-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}v^{p-1}=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

such that

\lim_{|x|\to 0}v(x)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}v(x)=0.

Then
v
is a radially decreasing function (i.e.,
v(x)=v(|x|)
with
v^{\prime}<0
).

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We will follow the scaling technique in
[
23
]
to show that

(6)

c|x|^{-(\gamma_{2}+1)}\leq|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-(\gamma_{2}+1)}\quad\text{ for }|x|>R_{1}^{\prime},

where
C,c>0
and
R_{1}^{\prime}>0
are constants depending on
N,p,\mu,s
and the solution
u
. The estimates for
|x|<R_{0}^{\prime}
are similar and can be proved similarly.

For
a,A
such that
0<a<R_{0}<R_{1}<A
and
R_{n}\to+\infty
, let us consider

w_{n}(x):=R_{n}^{\gamma_{2}}u(R_{n}x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

For
n
sufficiently large, from Theorem
4
, we have

\frac{c}{A^{\gamma_{2}}}\leq w_{n}(x)\leq\frac{C}{a^{\gamma_{2}}}\quad\text{ in }B_{A}(0)\setminus B_{a}(0)

and

(7)

w_{n}(x)\leq\frac{C}{A^{\gamma_{2}}}\text{ for }x\in\partial B_{A}(0),\quad w_{n}(x)\geq\frac{c}{a^{\gamma_{2}}}\text{ for }x\in\partial B_{a}(0).

In particular,
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(B_{A}(0)\setminus B_{a}(0))
and it weakly solves

(8)

-\Delta_{p}w_{n}-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}w_{n}^{p-1}=\frac{1}{R_{n}^{(p_{s}^{*}-p)\gamma_{2}+s-p}}\frac{w_{n}^{p^{*}_{s}-1}}{|x|^{s}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Notice that
(p_{s}^{*}-p)\gamma_{2}+s-p>0
thanks to
\gamma_{2}>\frac{N-p}{p}
. By standard regularity results in
[
9
,
18
,
28
]
,
(w_{n})
is also uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(K)
, for
0<\alpha<1
and for any compact set
K\subset B_{A}(0)\setminus B_{a}(0)
. Since
\nabla w_{n}(x)=R_{n}^{\gamma_{2}+1}\nabla u(R_{n}x)
, for
R_{n}
sufficiently large we get the
estimate from above in (
6
).

Now we prove the estimate from below. Suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence of points
x_{n}
such that

(9)

|x_{n}|^{\gamma_{2}+1}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to 0\quad\text{ for }|x_{n}|\to+\infty.

Since
(w_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha}(K)
, up to a subsequence, we have

w_{n}\to w_{a,A}\quad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(B_{A}(0)\setminus B_{a}(0))

for
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Moreover, passing (
8
) to the limit, we get

-\Delta_{p}w_{a,A}-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}w_{a,A}^{p-1}=0\quad\text{ in }C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(B_{A}(0)\setminus B_{a}(0)).

Now we take
a=\frac{1}{j}
and
A=j
, for large
j\in\mathbb{N}
and we construct
w_{\frac{1}{j},j}
as above. For
j\to\infty
, using a standard diagonal process, we can construct a limiting profile
w_{\infty}
so that

-\Delta_{p}w_{\infty}-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}w_{\infty}^{p-1}=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

and
w_{\frac{1}{j},j}=w_{\infty}
in
B_{j}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{j}}(0)
. Moreover, from (
7
) we know that

\lim_{|x|\to 0}w_{\infty}(x)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}w_{\infty}(x)=0.

By Theorem
6
,
w_{\infty}
is a radially decreasing function with
w_{\infty}^{\prime}<0
.

Now we set
R_{n}=|x_{n}|
and
y_{n}=\frac{x_{n}}{R_{n}}
, where
x_{n}
is determined in (
9
). Then we deduce that
|\nabla w_{n}(y_{n})|=|x_{n}|^{\gamma_{2}+1}|\nabla u(x_{n})|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This fact and the uniform convergence of the gradients imply that there exists
\overline{y}\in\partial B_{1}(0)
such that

|\nabla w_{\infty}(\overline{y})|=0.

This is a contradiction since the solution
w_{\infty}
has no critical points.
∎

3.
Radial symmetry of solutions

Our proof is based on the moving plane method. For each
\lambda\leq 0
, we denote

\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid x_{1}<\lambda\}.

We also denote by
x_{\lambda}
the reflection of
x
with respect to
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, i.e.,

x_{\lambda}:=(2\lambda-x_{1},x^{\prime})\quad\text{ for }x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

Furthermore, we set

u_{\lambda}(x):=u(x_{\lambda}).

Then
u_{\lambda}
weakly solves

(10)

-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda}-\frac{\mu}{|x_{\lambda}|^{p}}u_{\lambda}^{p-1}=\frac{u_{\lambda}^{p^{*}_{s}-1}}{|x_{\lambda}|^{s}}.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We mainly follow the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
. Let

\Lambda=\{\lambda<0\mid u\leq u_{\gamma}\text{ in }\Sigma_{\gamma}\text{ for all }\gamma\leq\lambda\}.

Step 1.
We claim that
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.

By Theorem
4
, we have that
\lim_{|x|\to 0}u(x)=+\infty
and
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}u(x)=0
. Hence there exists
\overline{R}_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{\overline{R}_{0}}(0_{\lambda})}u(x)<\inf_{x\in B_{\overline{R}_{0}}(0)}u(x)

for all
\lambda<-R_{1}
. This implies

(11)

u<u_{\lambda}\text{ in }B_{\overline{R}_{0}}(0_{\lambda})\quad\text{ for all }\lambda<-R_{1}.

Now we take
R>0
and denote by
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2R}(0))
a cut-off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(0)
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq\frac{2}{R}
. In what follows we employ the following notation
\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\prime}=\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus B_{\overline{R}_{0}}(0_{\lambda})
and
\hat{B}_{R}=B_{R}(0)\cap\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\prime}
. For
\alpha>\max\{2,p\}
and
\lambda<-R_{1}
, we consider

\varphi_{1,\lambda}:=\eta^{\alpha}u^{1-p}(u^{p}-u_{\lambda}^{p})^{+}\chi_{\Sigma_{\lambda}},\quad\varphi_{2,\lambda}:=\eta^{\alpha}u_{\lambda}^{1-p}(u^{p}-u_{\lambda}^{p})^{+}\chi_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}.

We also denote

\psi_{\lambda}:=(u^{p}-u_{\lambda}^{p})^{+},\quad\varphi_{\lambda}:=(u-u_{\lambda})^{+}.

Using
\varphi_{1,\lambda}
as a test function in (
2
) and
\varphi_{2,\lambda}
as a test function in the distributional formulation of (
10
) and subtracting, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1,\lambda}-|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2,\lambda})+\mu\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\left(-\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}+\frac{1}{|x_{\lambda}|^{p}}\right)\eta^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\left(\frac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x|^{s}}-\frac{u_{\lambda}^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x_{\lambda}|^{s}}\right)\eta^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}.

Since
|x|>|x_{\lambda}|
in
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, one has that the second term on the left hand side is nonnegative. Hence

(12)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\eta^{\alpha}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u^{1-p}\psi_{\lambda})-|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla(u_{\lambda}^{1-p}\psi_{\lambda}))}_{I_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{-\alpha\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\eta^{\alpha-1}u^{1-p}\psi_{\lambda}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta}_{I_{2}}+\underbrace{\alpha\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\eta^{\alpha-1}u_{\lambda}^{1-p}\psi_{\lambda}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\eta}_{I_{3}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\left(\frac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x|^{s}}-\frac{u_{\lambda}^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x_{\lambda}|^{s}}\right)\eta^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}}_{I_{4}}.

Working as in the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
, we have the following estimate for
I_{1}
:

(13)

I_{1}\geq c_{1}\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}\cap\{u\geq u_{\lambda}\}}\eta^{\alpha}u^{2}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla\ln u-\nabla\ln u_{\lambda}|^{2}

for
p>2
, and

(14)

I_{1}\geq c_{1}\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}\cap\{u\geq u_{\lambda}\}}\eta^{\alpha}u_{\lambda}^{2}(|\nabla u|+|\nabla u_{\lambda}|)^{p-2}|\nabla\ln u-\nabla\ln u_{\lambda}|^{2}

for
1<p<2
. We also have the following estimates for
I_{2}
and
I_{3}

(15)

I_{2}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\beta}},\quad I_{3}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\frac{\beta}{p}}},

where
\beta:=p\gamma_{2}+p-N
, which is positive since
\gamma_{2}>\frac{N-p}{p}
.
Now we estimate
I_{4}
as follows

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\left(\frac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x|^{s}}-\frac{u_{\lambda}^{p^{*}_{s}-p}}{|x|^{s}}\right)\eta^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}\leq\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s}u^{p-1}}\left(u^{p^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\lambda}^{p^{*}_{s}-1}\right)\eta^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}.

Applying twice the inequality

b^{q}-a^{q}\leq\max\{q,1\}b^{q-1}(b-a)\quad\text{ for }0<a<b\text{ and }q\geq 0

and using Theorem
4
one has that

(16)

I_{4}\leq\max\{p^{*}_{s}-1,1\}\max\{p,1\}\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\frac{u^{p^{*}_{s}-2}}{|x|^{s}}\eta^{\alpha}\varphi_{\lambda}^{2}\leq C\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s+\gamma_{2}(p^{*}_{s}-2)}}\eta^{\alpha}\varphi_{\lambda}^{2}.

For any
0<a<b
, by the Lagrange theorem for the function
f(t)=\ln t
we have

(17)

b-a\leq b(\ln b-\ln a).

We use (
17
) with
b=u
and
a=u_{\lambda}
and estimate the right hand side of (
16
) as

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\hat{B}_{2R}\cap\{u\geq u_{\lambda}\}}\frac{1}{|x|^{s+\gamma_{2}(p^{*}_{s}-2)}}\eta^{\alpha}u^{2}(\ln u-\ln u_{\lambda})^{2}

We can choose

Step 2.
We claim that
\lambda_{0}=0

Assume by contradiction that
\lambda_{0}<0

u\leq u_{\lambda_{0}}
in
\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}
. Since

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}u^{p-1}-\frac{u^{{p^{*}_{s}}-1}}{|x|^{s}}

\displaystyle=0=-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda_{0}}-\frac{\mu}{|x_{\lambda_{0}}|^{p}}u_{\lambda_{0}}^{p-1}-\frac{u_{\lambda_{0}}^{{p^{*}_{s}}-1}}{|x_{\lambda_{0}}|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda_{0}}-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}u_{\lambda_{0}}^{p-1}-\frac{u_{\lambda_{0}}^{{p^{*}_{s}}-1}}{|x|^{s}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}

we can exploit the strong comparison principle to deduce that

(20)

u<u_{\lambda_{0}}\quad\text{ in }\Sigma_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus Z_{u},

), we have that either

in
\mathcal{U}

in

\displaystyle 0=-\Delta_{p}u-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{p}}u^{p-1}-\frac{u^{{p^{*}_{s}}-1}}{|x|^{s}}

As in Step 1, there exists

By (

in

From now on, for

Step 3.
Conclusion.

By Step 2, we have

u=u(|x|)

It remains to show that

By contradiction, assume there exists

This completes the proof of the theorem.

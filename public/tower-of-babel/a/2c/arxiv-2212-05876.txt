Fast nuclear-spin gates and electrons-nuclei entanglement of neutral atoms in weak magnetic fields
Xiao-Feng Shi

Abstract
We present fast Rydberg-mediated entanglement involving nuclear spins of divalent atoms with 171 Yb as an example. First, we show a nuclear-spin controlled phase gate of an arbitrary phase realizable either with two laser pulses when assisted by Stark shifts, or with three pulses. Second, we propose to create a state ( | cc ⟩ e ⊗ | Φ ⟩ n + | Φ ⟩ e ⊗ | Ψ ⟩ n ) / 2 (\lvert\text{cc}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Psi\rangle_{\text{n}})/\sqrt{2} entangled between the electrons (e) and nuclear spins (n) of two atoms, where | Φ ⟩ \lvert\Phi\rangle and | Ψ ⟩ \lvert\Psi\rangle are two orthogonal Bell states and | c ⟩ e \lvert\text{c}\rangle_{\text{e}} denotes the clock state. For want of a better term, it is called a Super Bell State for it mimics a “large” Bell state incorporating three “smaller” Bell states. Third, we show a protocol to create a three-atom state ( 3 | ccc ⟩ e ⊗ | Λ ⟩ n + | W ⟩ e ⊗ | GHZ ⟩ n ) / 2 (\sqrt{3}\lvert\text{ccc}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Lambda\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\text{W}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\text{GHZ}\rangle_{\text{n}})/2 , where | Λ ⟩ n \lvert\Lambda\rangle_{\text{n}} is a nuclear-spin state, | W ⟩ e \lvert\text{W}\rangle_{\text{e}} is a W state in the ground-clock state space, and | GHZ ⟩ n \lvert\text{GHZ}\rangle_{\text{n}} is the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state in the nuclear-spin state space. The four protocols have a duration x ​ π / Ω m x\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}} with x x about 2.6 2.6 (two-pulse gate), 5.1 5.1 (three-pulse gate), 7.7 7.7 , and 11 11 , respectively, where Ω m \Omega_{\text{m}} is the largest Rabi frequency during the pulses. They possess high intrinsic fidelities, do not require single-site Rydberg addressing, and can be executed with large Ω m \Omega_{\text{m}} in a weak, Gauss-scale magnetic field for they involve Rydberg excitation of both nuclear-spin qubit states in each atom. The latter two protocols can enable measurement-based preparation of Bell, hyperentangled, and GHZ states.

;

;

;
Graham et al. 2022
. Meanwhile, Rydberg interactions in divalent alkaline-earth-metal or lanthanide atoms, which we call alkaline-earth-like (AEL) atoms, such as ytterbium and strontium, can also create entanglement
Madjarov et al. 2020
;
Ma et al. 2022
;
Schine et al. 2022

;

Another advantage in AEL is that for isotopes with nuclear spins, quantum information stored in nuclear spin states

;
Gorshkov et al. 2009
is insensitive to magnetic noise, can be preserved during laser cooling
Reichenbach and Deutsch 2007

Gorshkov et al. 2009
;
Omanakuttan et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2022

Boyd et al. 2007
which can lead to simultaneous Rydberg excitation of both nuclear-spin qubit states in a Rydberg-mediated gate
Shi 2021a
. A useful protocol of entanglement generation is simultaneously exciting one out of the two qubit states in each atom to Rydberg states
Shi 2019
;
Levine et al. 2019
, which means that the other qubit state in each atom shouldn’t be Rydberg excited.
To employ this method with nuclear-spin qubits, one can choose a Rydberg Rabi frequency
\Omega
small compared to the Zeeman splitting
\Delta_{\text{Z}}
in the Rydberg states as done in
Ma et al. 2022
, or use polarization rules
Shi 2021a
. Nevertheless, for nuclear spin qubits defined in the clock state, very large UV-laser Rydberg Rabi frequencies
\Omega
can be realized
Madjarov et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2022
which seems an advantage, but the polarization method of
Shi 2021a
can’t work, and using
\Omega\ll\Delta_{\text{Z}}
(e.g., the experiment in
Ma et al. 2022
had
\Omega/\Delta_{\text{Z}}<0.1
) would either lengthen the gate duration with Gauss-scale B-fields, or bring extra magnetic noise when strong B-fields are used.

Here, we present nuclear-spin entangling gates realizable with a large

5.05\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}

2.59\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}

is the maximal Rabi frequency in the pulses. With

over

I.2
Supper Bell states

We extend the idea in Sec.
I.1
and present a three-pulse protocol to realize the following state entangled between the electrons (e) and nuclear spins (n) in two atoms via Rydberg blockade,

\displaystyle|\text{SBS}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg(\lvert\text{cc}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Psi\rangle_{\text{n}}\bigg),

(1)

where

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle\lvert\Psi\rangle_{\text{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\theta}\lvert\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+e^{i\theta^{\prime}}\lvert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}}{\sqrt{2}}

(2)

are two orthogonal Bell states entangled in the two nuclear spin states
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
,
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
are two angles, and

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert\text{cg}\rangle_{\text{e}}+\lvert\text{gc}\rangle_{\text{e}}}{\sqrt{2}}

is a Bell state entangled in the electronic ground (g) and clock (c) states. Because of the similar structure,
\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}}
and
\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{e}}
are labeled by the same Greek letter. Looking at it as a whole, Eq. (
1
) is entangled between the two-atom electronic states (
\lvert\text{cc}\rangle_{\text{e}},\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{e}}
) and two-atom nuclear spin states (
\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}},\lvert\Psi\rangle_{\text{n}}
). For want of better term, Eq. (
1

SBS is prepared by three laser pulses, two UV laser pulses for the clock-Rydberg transition and one laser pulse for the ground-Rydberg transition. The three pulses have a total duration
3.4\pi/\Omega_{\text{eff}}
(or
7.7\pi/\Omega
), where
\Omega_{\text{eff}}
is the Rabi frequency for the ground-Rydberg transition and
\Omega
is the largest UV laser Rabi frequency among the two UV laser pulses for the clock-Rydberg transition.

\displaystyle\lvert\blacktriangle\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[(\sqrt{3}\lvert\text{ccc}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\Lambda\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\text{W}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\text{GHZ}\rangle_{\text{n}})\Big],

\displaystyle\lvert\Lambda\rangle_{\text{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\Big[\lvert\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+e^{i\Theta}(\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}

\displaystyle+\lvert\downarrow\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}})\Big]

\displaystyle\lvert\text{W}\rangle_{\text{e}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\lvert\text{gcc}\rangle_{\text{e}}+\lvert\text{cgc}\rangle_{\text{e}}+\lvert\text{ccg}\rangle_{\text{e}}\right)

Fang et al. 2019

II
Fast Nuclear-spin Quantum gates

II.1
A controlled-phase gate of any desired phase

We first show a sequence to realize a nuclear-spin quantum gate of the form,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail{}e^{-i\alpha}\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail e^{-i\beta/2}\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail e^{-i\beta/2}\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle,

(4)

\beta

\beta
is determined by a global laser phase, and

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle|\text{c}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle|\text{c}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle\lvert\text{g}_{\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle|\text{g}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle\lvert\text{g}_{\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle|\text{g}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes\lvert\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}},

where e (n) denotes the electronic (nuclear spin) state of the atom,

;
Shi 2021b
;

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrowtail

\displaystyle e^{i\alpha/2}\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrowtail

\displaystyle e^{i(\beta-\alpha)/2}\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail\lvert\text{c}_{\uparrow}\text{c}_{\downarrow}\rangle,

\displaystyle\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle\rightarrowtail\lvert\text{c}_{\downarrow}\text{c}_{\uparrow}\rangle,

Shi 2021a

Ma et al. 2022

III
Faster Gates assisted by Stark Shifts

Here, we show that it is possible to realize the nuclear-spin gate in Sec.
II

Barnes et al. 2022
, two nuclear spin qubit states with
m_{I}=-I,1-I
in
87

Jenkins et al. 2022

171

2\pi\times 10
MHz were demonstrated for both the
(6s6n)^{3}S_{1}

Burgers et al. 2022
and the
(6s6n)^{1}S_{0}

Pham et al. 2022
Rydberg states via the excitation of the Yb

transition of the inner electron in
174
Yb. Extending the methods of Refs.
Burgers et al. 2022
;
Pham et al. 2022
to
171
Yb would require calibrating polarization dependence of light shifts as in
Barnes et al. 2022
;
Jenkins et al. 2022

IV
Super Bell States

The SBS in Eq. (
1

\displaystyle\lvert\psi(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\text{cc}\rangle_{\text{e}}\otimes(\lvert\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{\text{n}}+\lvert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{\text{n}})/2,

;
Shi 2021b

(\theta,\theta^{\prime})
are dependent on the Rydberg interactions and they approach

in the ideal blockade condition. Because

\displaystyle\lvert+\rangle_{\text{e}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\lvert\text{cc}\rangle_{\text{e}},

\displaystyle\lvert-\rangle_{\text{e}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle\lvert+\rangle_{\text{n}}

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle\lvert\Psi\rangle_{\text{n}},

\displaystyle|\text{SBS}\rangle

IV.2
Numerical analyses

Here, we numerically study the fidelity for creating SBS with the pulses of Secs.

,
IV.1.2
, and
IV.1.3

V

V

The Rydberg state decay is inversely proportional to the Rabi frequencies in the protocol. The ground-Rydberg transition requires two-photon excitation, for which the Rabi frequency can be small. As analyzed in Ref.
Shi 2021a
,
\Omega_{\text{eff}}/2\pi=1.4
MHz can be realized for exciting a Rydberg state with principal quantum number
n\sim 70

\lvert r_{+}\rangle

\lvert r_{-}\rangle

\lvert r_{+}\rangle
and
\lvert r_{-}\rangle
can be realized by dividing the laser beam to two halves for which the frequency of one half shall be shifted by
2\Delta
via pulse pickers. This is experimentally feasible as shown in Ref.
Barnes et al. 2022

Shi 2021a

19

18

Shi 2021a
were quite conservative, and in principle higher ground-Rydberg Rabi frequencies should be achievable. The error due to Rydberg-state decay is given by Eq. (

VIII
conclusions

We present fast and high-fidelity nuclear-spin controlled-phase gates of an arbitrary phase in AEL atoms. By exciting two AEL atoms with global laser fields where both nuclear spin qubit states of each atom are Rydberg excited, a controlled-phase gate of an arbitrary phase can be realized via three laser pulses of total duration
5.05\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}
, where
\Omega_{\text{m}}
is the largest Rydberg Rabi frequency during the pulses. We also show that if spin-dependent Stark shift (
\sim\Omega_{\text{m}}
) is realizable, the same gate can be realized with two laser pulses of total duration
2.59\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}
. With large
\Omega_{\text{m}}
for the transition between the clock and Rydberg states of AEL realizable as recently demonstrated in experiments, and concerning that fast gates are easier to atain a higher fidelity when executed, the protocols can enable high-fidelity entanglement in nuclear spins of AEL atoms.

We also present two protocols to entangle electrons and nuclear spins in AEL. First, we show a super Bell state (SBS) entangled between the electrons and nuclei of two atoms. SBS is like a large “Bell” state entangled between three “small” Bell states and one product state. For three atoms, we show a protocol to realize a highly entangled state that includes W and GHZ states simultaneously. These two multi-atom entangling protocols have durations
7.69\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}
and
11.4\pi/\Omega_{\text{m}}
, respectively, and can be used for preparing Bell, hyperentangled, and nuclear-spin GHZ states based on measurement.

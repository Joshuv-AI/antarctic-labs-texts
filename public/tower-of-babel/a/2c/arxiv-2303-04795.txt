Abstract
. We define a new bicategorical model of linear logic that refines the bicategory of groupoids, profunctors, and natural transformations. An object of this new model is a groupoid with additional structure, called a Boolean kit , used to constrain profunctors by stabilizing the groupoid action on their elements. The theory of generalized species of structures, based on profunctors, is then refined to a new theory of stable species of structures between groupoids with Boolean kits. While generalized species are in correspondence with analytic functors between presheaf categories, stable species are shown to correspond to stable functors between full subcategories of presheaves determined by the kits. One motivation for stable functors is that, in the special case where kits enforce free actions, they correspond to finitary polynomial functors between categories of indexed sets, also known as normal functors. We show that the bicategory of groupoids with Boolean kits, stable species, and natural transformations is cartesian closed. This makes essential use of the logical structure of Boolean kits and explains the well-known failure of cartesian closure for the bicategory of finitary polynomial functors (between categories of indexed sets) and cartesian natural transformations. The paper additionally develops the model of classical linear logic underlying the cartesian closed structure and clarifies the connection to stable domain theory.

Introduction

Profunctors, sometimes called distributors or bimodules, are categorical
structures appearing in various areas of logic and computer science. This
paper is concerned with profunctors between
groupoids
and their
application to linear logic and structural combinatorics. A main goal is to develop a formal connection with the theory of

polynomial functors

[

,
AAG05
]

Let us summarize the context and the main results of the paper.

Profunctors in logic and combinatorics

A profunctor from
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{B}

\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{B}

\mathbb{B}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{A}\to\mathbf{Set}

[

,
Bén67

Bén00

It is common to define a bicategory whose objects are small categories,
morphisms are profunctors, and 2-cells are natural transformations; in this
paper we will call

the full sub-bicategory whose objects are

The bicategory

First,

The bicategory

Polynomial functors and analytic functors

The starting point for this paper is a connection between Joyal’s
combinatorial species and polynomial functors on sets, that we review briefly
(Section
2

On one side, we consider
finitary
polynomial functors
\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
,
which correspond to operators on sets of the form

X\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}\times X^{n}

(1)

where the coefficients
A_{n}
are sets. On the other side, a combinatorial
species consists of a family of sets

left action of the symmetric group

on
n

X\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}\underset{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}{\times}X^{n}

(2)

]

In special cases, when the actions on

are

5

polynomial. Conversely, every finitary polynomial functor is analytic when
its coefficients are regarded as freely generated actions.

Thus, finitary polynomial functors determine a subcategory of

\mathbf{Prof}(\Sigma\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
, and the language of species gives a basic
combinatorial perspective on polynomial functors. Our purpose here is to
generalize this connection, and to provide a new
logical
perspective,
following the methodology offered by the logical structure of profunctors and
generalized species.

Structure of the paper

Beyond the two background sections that follow, the paper is organized into
two main parts. In the first part (Sections
4

to
6
), we regard profunctors as groupoids actions, and
introduce kits to control and stabilize these actions. We study kits from a
logical perspective, and introduce an orthogonality relation for families of
subgroups of endomorphisms in a groupoid. The induced Boolean algebra
structure gives rise to the notion of Boolean kit. We show that the bicategory
of Boolean kits and stabilized profunctors is a model of differential linear
logic.

In the second part (Sections
7

to
9
), we study the extensional aspects of stabilized
profunctors and stable species. We show that orthogonality for kits can be
translated to an orthogonality relation on presheaves. We then prove that
stabilized profunctors and stable species can be characterized respectively as
linear and stable functors between subcategories of presheaves closed under
double-orthogonality. This provides a 2-dimensional stable domain theory.

2.
Motivation: polynomial and analytic functors over sets

At the heart of this paper is the relationship between two families of
functors from
\mathbf{Set}
to
\mathbf{Set}
: finitary polynomial functors (also known as

normal
functors) and analytic functors. We start by giving the
relevant definitions.

Polynomial functors.

A function

p:E\to B

between sets
E
and
B
determines an endofunctor on
\mathbf{Set}
defined
as

X\longmapsto\sum_{b\in B}X^{E_{b}}

where
E_{b}=p^{-1}\{b\}
is the fibre of
p
over
b\in B
, and
X^{E_{b}}
is
the set of functions
E_{b}\to X
. A functor of this form is called a polynomial
functor. If we restrict to those
p
such that

E_{b}
are finite sets for all
b\in B
, then the functor is naturally isomorphic to one of the
form

X\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}\times X^{n}

(3)

where each set
F_{n}
corresponds to the set of all
b\in B
such that

|E_{b}|=n
. The
analogy with traditional polynomials is manifest in this representation.
These polynomial functors are called
finitary
and are determined by their action on finite input
sets.

Analytic functors

We now turn to a strictly more general family of functors. An endofunctor on
\mathbf{Set}
is an
analytic functor

[
Joy86
]
if
it is naturally isomorphic to one of the form

X\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F(n)\underset{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}{\times}X^{n},

(4)

where:

(1)

Each
F(n)
is a set with a left action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

on
n
elements. Concretely, this means that we have an assignment that to
every permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of the set

[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
associates a permutation of the set
F(n)

preserving identity and composition. We write
\sigma\cdot p
for the action
of
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on an element
p\in F(n)
.

(2)

The set
F(n)\underset{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}{\times}X^{n}
is obtained by quotienting the
product
F(n)\times X^{n}
under the equivalence relation
\sim
containing the
pairs

(p,(x_{\sigma 1},\dots,x_{\sigma n}))\kern 5.0pt\sim\kern 5.0pt(\sigma\cdot p,(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))

for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
p\in F(n)
and

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}
.

The notation
F(-)
is justified, because the coefficients
F(n)
,

together with
the group actions, can be bundled into a functor

F:\mathbf{Bij}\to\mathbf{Set}
where
\mathbf{Bij}
is the category whose objects are
the natural numbers, and whose morphisms
m\to n
are the bijections

[m]\to[n]
.
This category is equivalent to the category of finite sets and bijections. The action of a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on the
set
F(n)
is then simply given by the functorial action

F(\sigma):F(n)\to F(n)
.

The functor
F:\mathbf{Bij}\to\mathbf{Set}
is a
species of structures
(or just a

species
, with its elements referred to as
structures
)
corresponding to the analytic functor (
4
). Every analytic
functor has, up to isomorphism, a unique generating species, which may be
recovered using so-called
weak generic elements

(Section
8.1
).

For some more combinatorial intuition,
each
F(n)
is intended to model a type of structures (trees, partitions,
\dots
) with

n
labels (indexed
1
to
n
) on which the symmetric group
acts by permutation. The induced analytic functor then constructs for each set
X

the set of
X
-labelled
F
-structures. These are elements of
F(n)

at any
n
in which labels are elements of
X
.
This theory was developed by Joyal

[
Joy81
,
Joy86
]
for application in
combinatorics. Joyal also noticed the connection to polynomial
functors that we explain next.

Finitary polynomial functors are free analytic functors

Finitary polynomial functors can be identified with the sub-family of

free
analytic
functors. The basic idea is as follows. Every set
A
generates a

free action
of a group
G
, given by the product set
A\times G
with
the action

\tau\cdot(a,\sigma)\ \coloneq\ (a,\tau\,\sigma)

(5)

for every
\tau\in G
and
(a,\sigma)\in A\times G
.
We extend this to polynomial functors. Consider the finitart polynomial functor
X\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}\times X^{n}
. Taking the free action generated
by
A_{n}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain a species

\mathbf{Bij}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}
given by

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\longmapsto A_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}

\displaystyle(\tau:n\to n)

\displaystyle\longmapsto((a,\sigma)\mapsto(a,\tau\,\sigma))

which, via the construction (
4
), generates an analytic
functor. For this species, when taking the quotient in
(
4
), we have

(A_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n})\underset{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}{\times}X^{n}\ \cong\ A_{n}\times X^{n}

and therefore recover the polynomial functor. Thus, every finitary
polynomial functor is, in particular, analytic.

Stabilizer subgroups and free species

In this paper, “finitary polynomial” and
“analytic” are viewed as the two extremes in a spectrum of classes of functors

\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
. The intermediate classes are defined in terms of
the underlying species of structures, using a new notion we call a
kit.

Recall that, if a group
G
acts on a set
P
, the
stabilizer
of an element
p\in P
, defined as
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(p)\coloneq\{\sigma\in G\mid\sigma\cdot p=p\}
, is a
subgroup of
G
. The key observation is that free actions, as in
(
5
), are exactly those for which every element has trivial
stabilizer.
Thus, if
F:\mathbf{Bij}\to\mathbf{Set}
is a species such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
F(n)
is
free
in the sense that every structure
in
F(n)
has trivial stabilizer, then the associated analytic endofunctor on

\mathbf{Set}
is polynomial.
This characterizes the analytic functors that are polynomial in terms
of their generating species.

Kits on
\mathbf{Bij}

One key idea of this paper is to use families of subgroups to specify the extent to which
a species may be free. Indeed, by specifying, for each
n\in\mathbf{Bij}
, a set

\mathcal{K}(n)
of subgroups of
\mathbf{Bij}(n,n)
that are to be regarded as

permitted stabilizers
, we may restrict to species with structures
having only permitted stabilizers and thereby
identify a class of functors
\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
.

As extreme special cases, one can take
\mathcal{K}(n)
to contain all the
subgroups of
\mathbf{Bij}(n,n)
and recover the analytic functors; or, instead, take

\mathcal{K}(n)
to consist only of the trivial subgroup, forcing the species
to be free, and recover polynomial functors. Such
families of subgroups
\mathcal{K}=\{\,\mathcal{K}(n)\,\}_{n\in\mathbf{Bij}}
, subject to a compatibility condition, will be called kits
(Definition
4.1
).

There is no need that the choice of permitted stabilizers be uniform across
all
n\in\mathbf{Bij}
as in the two examples above. But it is natural to require
that permitted stabilizers are closed under conjugation, since for every
structure
p\in F(n)
and permutation
\sigma\in\mathbf{Bij}(n,n)
, the stabilizer
subgroups of
p
and of
\sigma\cdot p
are conjugate of each other:

\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma\cdot p)=\sigma\,\,\mathrm{Stab}(p)\,\,\sigma^{-1}
.

First we appropriately restrict species:

{defi}

For each
n\in\mathbf{Bij}
, let
\mathcal{K}(n)
be a family of subgroups of

\mathbf{Bij}(n,n)

. A
\mathcal{K}

is a

such that every element of

has stabilizer in

This way, every family

determines a subclass of
analytic endofunctors on
\mathbf{Set}
, namely, those whose generating species are

\mathcal{K}

groupoid
\mathbf{Bij}
.

By varying the kit structure, one obtains different subclasses of
analytic functors on
\mathbf{Set}

about the connection to polynomial functors, so our construction of

3.2.
Generalized species

We now describe a bicategory of generalized species of structures indexed by arbitrary
groupoids.

From species to generalized species

For a species of structures
\mathbf{Bij}\to\mathbf{Set}
, consider the following two basic observations. First,
the groupoid
\mathbf{Bij}
is the
free symmetric strict monoidal completion
of
the terminal category
\mathbf{1}
. This completion is denoted
\Sigma\mathbf{1}

and described formally below. Second,
\mathbf{Set}

is isomorphic to the category of presheaves
\mathbfcal{P}\mathbf{1}
over
\mathbf{1}
.
The version, due to Fiore, Gambino, Hyland and
Winskel
[
FGHW08
]
extends the basic notion of a species

\mathbf{Bij}\to\mathbf{Set}
, corresponding to

\Sigma\mathbf{1}\to\mathbfcal{P}\mathbf{1}
, to

\Sigma\mathbb{A}\longrightarrow\mathbfcal{P}\mathbb{B}

(6)

for
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
groupoids (in fact, they can be arbitrary small
categories, but we do not use this generality here). Their main result is that
these assemble into a bicategory
\mathbf{Esp}
of groupoids, generalized species, and
natural transformations, and that moreover this bicategory is cartesian closed.

Since
\mathbfcal{P}(\mathbb{B})=[\mathbb{B}^{\circ},\mathbf{Set}]
, a generalized species is
equivalently a profunctor
\mathbb{B}^{\circ}\times\Sigma\mathbb{A}\to\mathbf{Set}
, and
the bicategory
\mathbf{Esp}
can be obtained as a coKleisli bicategory for the
pseudo-comonad
\Sigma
over the bicategory of profunctors. This is the
approach we follow in this paper.

The symmetric strict monoidal completion

For a category
\mathbb{A}
, define
\Sigma\mathbb{A}
as the category whose objects are finite sequences
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
of objects of
\mathbb{A}
and a morphism
\alpha
between two sequences
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
and
\langle b_{1},\dots b_{n}\rangle
consists of a pair
(\sigma,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in[n]})
where
\sigma
is a permutation of the set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
(\alpha_{i}:a_{i}\rightarrow b_{\sigma(i)})_{i\in[n]}
is a sequence of morphisms in
\mathbb{A}
.
There are no morphisms in
\Sigma\mathbb{A}
between sequences of different
lengths.

If
\mathbb{A}
is a groupoid, then
\Sigma\mathbb{A}
is also a groupoid,
equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure given by the
concatenation of sequences
(u,v)\mapsto u\otimes v
. This
construction has a canonical
2
-monad structure on
\mathbf{Cat}
, that can
be lifted to a pseudo-comonad on
\mathbf{Prof}
, see

e.g.

[
FGHW18
]
. It maps a profunctor
P:\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{B}
to the profunctor
\Sigma P:\Sigma\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\Sigma\mathbb{B}
given by:

\Sigma P(\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i\in[n]},\langle b_{j}\rangle_{j\in[m]})=\begin{cases}\coprod_{\varphi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\prod_{j\in[m]}P(b_{j},a_{\varphi(j)}),&\text{if }n=m\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The counit and
comultiplication, denoted
\mathrm{der}
and
\mathrm{dig}
respectively (for

and

, following the usual terminology of linear logic) are pseudo-natural transformations whose components are the profunctors

\displaystyle\mathrm{der}_{\mathbb{A}}:\Sigma\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle(a,u)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dig}_{\mathbb{A}}:\Sigma\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\Sigma\Sigma\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle(\langle u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\rangle,u)

\displaystyle\mapsto\Sigma\mathbb{A}(u_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes u_{n},u).

Generalized species of structures are the

Intensional Theory:

Stabilized Profunctors and Stable Species

We proceed to develop our new model based on groupoids
with kits. We first study profunctors and species over groupoids
with kits; this is what we call the “intensional theory”. This
provides a refinement of the bicategories
\mathbf{Prof}
and
\mathbf{Esp}
.

4.
Kits and stabilized profunctors

4.1.
Groupoids and Kits

We first make some basic remarks about groupoids, and fix some notation. For every object
a
in a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
, the set of endomorphisms
\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)=\mathbb{A}(a,a)
forms a group under composition. The groups
\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)
will play a central role in our development and we often simply write
\mathrm{Endo}(a)
.

For a group
G
, we write
H\leq G
to mean that
H
is a subgroup of
G
; a canonical example is the
trivial subgroup

\{\mathrm{id}_{a}\}\leq\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)
for an object
a
in a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
.
Importantly, subgroups of endomorphisms in
\mathbb{A}
can be transferred across morphisms by conjugation: for a morphism
\alpha:a\to a^{\prime}
and
G\leq\mathrm{Endo}(a)
, the conjugate subgroup
\alpha\,G\,\alpha^{-1}=\{\alpha\,g\,\alpha^{-1}\mid g\in G\}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Endo}(a^{\prime})
.

Our approach involves attaching to a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
some additional structure which we will call a
kit
, comprising a family of subgroups of endomorphisms for each object.

{defi}

A

Definition 3
.

kit on a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
is a family

\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}(a)\}_{a\in\mathbb{A}}
consisting of sets
\mathcal{A}(a)
of
subgroups of
\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)
that is closed under conjugation; that is, such
that, for all
\alpha:a^{\prime}\to a
in
\mathbb{A}
, if
G\leq\mathrm{Endo}(a)
is in

\mathcal{A}(a)
then the conjugate subgroup

\alpha^{-1}\,G\,\alpha\leq\mathrm{Endo}(a^{\prime})
is in
\mathcal{A}(a^{\prime})
.

When
\mathcal{A}
is a kit on
\mathbb{A}
, we often refer to the pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
as a kit, which we also denote by
\underline{\mathbb{A}}
for convenience.

{exa}

Every groupoid
\mathbb{A}
has the following canonical choices of kits: the trivial one
\mathrm{Triv}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)=\left\{\{\mathrm{id}_{a}\}\right\}
, and the maximal one
\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)=\{G\mid G\leq\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)\}
.

As usual with linear logic, our model exhibits several aspects of
duality. Every groupoid
\mathbb{A}
has an

Definition 4
.

opposite or

Definition 5
.

dual
groupoid
\mathbb{A}^{\circ}
, to which it is isomorphic via the mapping

\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}
, and moreover
(\mathbb{A}^{\circ})^{\circ}=\mathbb{A}
;
this holds in particular for any group
G
seen as a one-object groupoid. Observe that for any object
a
of a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
one has
\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}(a)=(\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}(a))^{\circ}
.

To construct kits on dual groupoids we consider a notion of subgroup orthogonality.

{defi}

For a group
G
and subgroups
H\leq G
and
K\leq G^{\circ}
, we say that
H
and
K
are

Definition 6
.

orthogonal if
H\cap K=\{\mathrm{id}\}
, where
\mathrm{id}
is the identity element in
G
(and
G^{\circ}
).

From this we get the following construction:

Lemma 7
.

For a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
and a kit
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathbb{A}^{\circ}
may be equipped with its

Definition 8
.

orthogonal kit
\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
defined for
a\in\mathbb{A}^{\circ}
by:

\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a)=\{G\leq\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}(a)\mid\forall H\in\mathcal{A}(a),G\perp H\}.

Proof 4.1
.

For the closure under conjugation observe that for a morphism
\alpha:a^{\prime}\to a
and subgroups
G\leq{\mathrm{Endo}}_{\mathbb{A}}(a)
and
H\leq{\mathrm{Endo}}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}(a^{\prime})
,

\alpha^{-1}\,G\,\alpha\perp H
if and only if

G\perp\alpha\,H\,\alpha^{-1}
.

As a basic example observe that, for a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
, the canonical kits from Example
3
are orthogonal:

\mathrm{Triv}_{\mathbb{A}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}\qquad\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}}^{\perp}=\mathrm{Triv}_{\mathbb{A}^{\circ}}.

We define
Boolean kits
to be those kits which are closed under
double orthogonality
.

{defi}

A

Definition 9
.

Boolean kit
\mathcal{A}
on a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
is a kit
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
such
that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
.

Writing
\mathbf{Kit}(\mathbb{A})
for the poset of kits on
\mathbb{A}
under (component-wise) inclusion, the orthogonality relation on kits induces a Galois connection
\mathbf{Kit}(\mathbb{A})^{\circ}
\mathbf{Kit}(\mathbb{A}^{\circ})
(-)^{\perp}
(-)^{\perp}
\bot

whose fixed points
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
are Boolean kits. The requirement that structures be closed under double orthogonality is common in the model theory of linear logic
[
HS03
]
and is necessary when one seeks a model for the
classical
system, in which negation is involutive. More concretely, this condition ensures that certain basic properties always hold for a kit, as we will see shortly.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be kits on a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
. Then:

(1)

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
, where
\subseteq
is component-wise inclusion.

(2)

If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
.

It follows that
\mathcal{A}^{\perp}=\mathcal{A}^{{\perp\perp}\perp}
; so, in particular,
\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
is a Boolean kit.

Lemma 11
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
be a Boolean kit, and let
a\in\mathbb{A}
. Then, for each
a\in\mathbb{A}
, the set
\mathcal{A}(a)
is closed under subgroups (in particular,
\{\mathrm{id}_{a}\}\in\mathcal{A}(a)
) and under unions of directed subsets.

Proof 4.2
.

Let
H^{\prime}\leq H\in\mathcal{G}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}^{\perp}
, then
H^{\prime}\cap G\subseteq H\cap G=\{\mathrm{id}\}
wich implies that
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}^{\perp\perp}=\mathcal{G}
.
Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a directed family in
\mathcal{G}
, then
\bigcup_{i\in I}G_{i}
is a subgroup of
G
and for
H\in\mathcal{G}^{\perp}
,
\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}G_{i}\right)\cap H=\bigcup_{i\in I}(G_{i}\cap H)=\{\mathrm{id}\}
so that
\bigcup_{i\in I}G_{i}
is in
\mathcal{G}^{\perp\perp}=\mathcal{G}
as desired.

We consider as an example the possible Boolean kits on the group
C_{6}
, the cyclic group of order 6, seen as a groupoid. For an element
g
of a group
G
, we denote by
\langle g\rangle
the

Definition 12
.

cyclic subgroup generated by
g
; that is,
\langle g\rangle=\{g^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.
Now, writing
\gamma
for any generator of
C_{6}
, it can be verified that the only possible Boolean kits are the maximal and trivial ones, as well as the subsets
\{\langle\gamma^{2}\rangle,\{\mathrm{id}\}\}
and
\{\langle\gamma^{3}\rangle,\{\mathrm{id}\}\}.

These form a complete Boolean algebra, and in fact this is the case for the set of Boolean kits on any groupoid.

It is sometimes useful to unfold the condition
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
by looking at the family of subsets
\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\subseteq\mathrm{Endo}(a)
, for
a\in\mathbb{A}
.
This brings us close to Taylor’s
creeds

[
Tay89
]
, which are subsets of endomorphisms
meeting certain explicit closure conditions. In practice, to show that a kit
\mathcal{A}
is a Boolean kit, we show that for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
, for all
\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)^{\perp\perp}
,
\alpha
is in
\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)
which corresponds to the
saturation
property defined below:

{defi}

A kit
\mathcal{A}
on a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
is
saturated
if it satisfies the following condition: for
a\in\mathbb{A}
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Endo}(a)
, if the formula
\Phi_{\bigcup{\mathcal{A}}}(\alpha)
given by

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha^{n}=\mathrm{id}\vee(\exists m\in\mathbb{N},\alpha^{nm}\neq\mathrm{id}\wedge\alpha^{nm}\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a))

holds, then
\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)
.

Saturation is however not enough to prove
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
, it only shows that
\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)=\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}(a)
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
. We need further that
\mathcal{A}(a)=\{H\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid H\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\}
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
.

Lemma 13
.

For a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
and a kit
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{A}
,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
if and only if

(1)

for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
,
\mathcal{A}(a)=\{H\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid H\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\}

(2)

\mathcal{A}
is saturated.

Proof 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
and let
H\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)
be such that
H\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)
i.e. for all
\alpha\in H
,
\langle\alpha\rangle
is in
\mathcal{A}(a)
since
\mathcal{A}(a)
is downclosed by Lemma
11
. Let
K
be in
\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a)
, for any
\alpha\in H\cap K
, we have
\langle\alpha\rangle\cap K=\{\mathrm{id}\}
which implies that
H
is in
\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}(a)=\mathcal{A}(a)
so that
\mathcal{A}(a)=\{H\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid H\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\}
as desired. The saturation property is an immediate unfolding of the double orthogonality.
For the other direction, if
\mathcal{A}
is saturated, we have
\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)=\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}(a)
for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
which implies that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp\perp}
by
(1).

For a kit
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
we will use the notation
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})^{\perp}=(\mathbb{A}^{\circ},\mathcal{A}^{\perp})
, and continue to use underline symbols
\underline{\mathbb{A}},\underline{\mathbb{B}},
for pairs
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A}),(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
.

4.2.
Stabilized Profunctors

For groupoids equipped with kits, we will consider certain constrained profunctors which we call
stabilized
.

{defi}

For kits
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
, a

Definition 14
.

stabilized profunctor
P:(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
is a profunctor

P:\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{B}
such that, for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
,
b\in\mathbb{B}
,

p\in P(b,a)
,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Endo}(a)
,
\beta\in\mathrm{Endo}(b)
, if

\alpha\cdot p\cdot\beta=p
then

\textstyle\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\implies\beta\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}(b)\;\text{and}\;\beta\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)\implies\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a).

It is instructive to consider some examples.

{exa}

(1)

Any profunctor
P:\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{B}
is a stabilized profunctor
P:(\mathbb{A},\mathrm{Triv}_{\mathbb{A}})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{B},\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{B}})
.

(2)

A stabilized profunctor
(\mathbb{A},\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{A}})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{B},\mathrm{Endo}_{\mathbb{B}})
is a profunctor
P:\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{B}
that acts freely on
\mathbb{A}
. In other words, for all
a\in\mathbb{A}
,
b\in\mathbb{B}
,
p\in P(b,a)
,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Endo}(a)
, if
\alpha\cdot p=p
then
\alpha=\mathrm{id}_{a}
.

(3)

For any kit
\underline{\mathbb{A}}=(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
, the identity
\mathrm{y}:\mathbb{A}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{A}\to\mathbf{Set}:(a,a^{\prime})\mapsto\mathbb{A}(a,a^{\prime})
is a stabilized profunctor
\underline{\mathbb{A}}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\underline{\mathbb{A}}
. This is because,
for
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}
,
\alpha\in\mathrm{Endo}(a)
,
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Endo}(a^{\prime})
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{A}(a,a^{\prime})
,
\alpha^{\prime}\cdot\gamma\cdot\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\gamma\circ\alpha=\gamma
implies that
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are conjugate; and both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
are closed under conjugation.

Stabilized profunctors are closed under composition:

Lemma 15
.

For kits
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
,
(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
and
(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{C})
, if
P:(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
and
Q:(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{C})
are stabilized profunctors, then their composite
Q\circ P
is an stabilized profunctor
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{C},\mathcal{C})
.

Proof 4.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{A}
,
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(a,a)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}(c,c)
and
t\in(Q\circ P)(c,a)
be such that
\alpha\cdot t\cdot\gamma=t
. The element
t
is of the form
p\mathrel{\mkern-2.0mu\bowtie\mkern-17.0mu{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\scriptsize$b$}}\mkern 2.5mu}q\in\int^{b}P(b,a)\times Q(c,b)
for some
b\in\mathbb{B}
,
p\in P(b,a)
and
q\in Q(c,b)
so
\alpha\cdot t\cdot\gamma=t
is equivalent to
(\alpha\cdot p)\mathrel{\mkern-2.0mu\bowtie\mkern-17.0mu{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\scriptsize$b$}}\mkern 2.5mu}(q\cdot\gamma)=p\mathrel{\mkern-2.0mu\bowtie\mkern-17.0mu{\raisebox{-2.84526pt}{\scriptsize$b$}}\mkern 2.5mu}q
which implies that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b,b)
such that
\alpha\cdot p\cdot\beta=p
and
\beta^{-1}\cdot q\cdot\gamma=q
. If

then since

which implies that
\beta^{-1}\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}(b)
by closure under powers (

is an element of a group

\beta^{-1}\cdot q\cdot\gamma=q

Boolean kits, stabilized profunctors, and natural transformations assemble into a bicategory which we denote

5.
Logical structure of stabilized profunctors

We show that
\mathbf{SProf}
is a model of classical linear logic, and that
this structure extends and refines that of
\mathbf{Prof}
. In this
section we focus on multiplicative and additive structure. The
exponential modality will be discussed in the next section.

5.1.
Biproduct structure.

In the standard
1
-dimensional models of stability (coherence spaces, probabilistic coherence spaces, etc.), the linear categories do not have biproducts which implies in particular that they are not models of differential linear logic. In our setting, the biproduct structure in
\mathbf{Prof}
can be transported to
\mathbf{SProf}
.
For a family of groupoids
(\mathbb{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
, we denote by
\with_{i\in I}\mathbb{A}_{i}
their coproduct in
\mathbf{Cat}
, whose objects are given by the disjoint union
\bigcup_{i\in I}\{i\}\times\mathbb{A}_{i}
. In
\mathbf{Prof}
, this is a
biproduct
, meaning that
\with_{i\in I}\mathbb{A}_{i}
is a product
and
a coproduct for the family
(\mathbb{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
.

For a finite family of kits
\{\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}=(\mathbb{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{i})\}_{i\in I}
, the catgorical coproduct
\with_{i\in I}\mathbb{A}_{i}
has a canonical kit structure
(\with_{i\in I}\mathbb{A}_{i},\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i})
defined as
(\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i})(j,a)=\mathcal{A}_{j}(a)
for all
j\in I,a\in\mathbb{A}
. Since
(\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i}
, it is in fact a Boolean kit which implies that the product kit
\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}:=(\with_{i\in I}\mathbb{A}_{i},\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i})
is equal to the coproduct kit
\oplus_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}:=(\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}^{\perp})^{\perp}
.

The projections
\pi_{j}:\with_{i\in I}{\mathbb{A}}_{i}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{A}}_{j}
in
\mathbf{Prof}
determine stabilized profunctors
\with_{i\in I}\underline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{i}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\underline{{\mathbb{A}}}_{j}
, and one can show the existence of an adjoint equivalence

\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{SProf}(\underline{\mathbb{B}},\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i\in I})\simeq\mathbf{SProf}(\underline{\mathbb{B}},\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i})

making
\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}
a bicategorical product. From the self-dual structure of
\mathbf{SProf}
, we obtain that the inclusions
\mathrm{inj}_{j}:\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{j}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{i}
, obtained as the dual of the projection
\pi_{j}:\with_{i\in I}\underline{\mathbb{A}}^{\perp}_{i}\mathrel{\mkern 11.0mu\raisebox{0.7113pt}{$\shortmid$}\mkern-16.0mu{\longrightarrow}}\underline{\mathbb{A}}^{\perp}_{j}
, are also stabilized profunctors and yield the desired adjoint equivalence in
\mathbf{SProf}
for coproducts.
We thus have finite biproducts, with the zero object defined by the kit
(\mathbf{0},\varnothing)
on the empty groupoid
\mathbf{0}
.

5.2.
*
-Autonomous structure.

The bicategory
\mathbf{Prof}
can be equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure defined by the cartesian product of categories
\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}
, with monoidal unit given by the terminal category
\mathbf{1}
. Additionally, the duality pseudo-functor
\mathbb{A}\mapsto\mathbb{A}^{\circ}
makes
\mathbf{Prof}
a compact closed bicategory
[
Sta16
]
. From the point of view of linear logic,
\mathbf{Prof}
is a degenerate model:
(\mathbb{A}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{B}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}
, hence the multiplicative connectives
\invamp
and
\otimes
are identified. The addition of kits to groupoids eliminates this degeneracy:
\mathbf{SProf}
is not compact closed but merely
*
-autonomous, as we will see.

For kits
(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
, we construct a tensor product as

(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})\otimes(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B}):=(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B},(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B})^{\perp\perp})

where for
a\in\mathbb{A}
and
b\in\mathbb{B}
,
(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B})(a,b):=\mathcal{A}(a)\times\mathcal{B}(b)
.
The closure under double orthogonality is necessary because
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
is not a Boolean kit in general. The monoidal unit is the kit
(\mathbf{1},\mathrm{Triv}_{\mathbf{1}})
. To show that this induces a symmetric monoidal structure, it suffices to show that the symmetric monoidal structure of
\mathbf{Prof}
lifts to
\mathbf{SProf}
, i.e. that associator, braiding, and left and right unitors are pseudo-natural transformations with components in
\mathbf{SProf}
, and that the tensor product is a pseudo-functor
\mathbf{SProf}\times\mathbf{SProf}\to\mathbf{SProf}
. All coherence axioms will then follow from the (faithful) forgetful pseudo-functor
\mathbf{SProf}\to\mathbf{Prof}
.

This way, we avoid much of the work in defining a bicategorical monoidal structure. The proof is not automatic, however: our use of the closure operator
(-)^{\perp\perp}
in the definition of
\otimes
makes certain properties, e.g. associativity, difficult to verify. The following lemma is a key tool in this process.

Lemma 17
.

For Boolean kits
\underline{\mathbb{A}}=(\mathbb{A},\mathcal{A})
and
\underline{\mathbb{B}}=(\mathbb{B},\mathcal{B})
, define the kit
\underline{\mathbb{A}}\multimap\underline{\mathbb{B}}
as
(\mathbb{A}^{\circ}\times\mathbb{B},\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})
where
(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})(a,b)
is the set of all subgroups
H\leq\mathrm{Endo}(a,b)
such that:

\forall(\alpha,\beta)\in H,\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)\implies\beta\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}(b)\text{ and }\beta\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)\implies\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a).

The kit
\underline{\mathbb{A}}\multimap\underline{\mathbb{B}}
is Boolean.

Proof 5.1
.

To prove that
\underline{\mathbb{A}}\multimap\underline{\mathbb{B}}
forms a Boolean kit, we first show that for objects
a\in\mathbb{A},b\in\mathbb{B}
and subgroups
K\in\mathcal{A}(a)
and
G\in\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)
, we have
K\times G\in(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})^{\perp}(a,b)
. Let
H
be in
(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})(a,b)
and
(\alpha,\beta)\in(K\times G)\cap H
. Since
\alpha\in K\in\mathcal{A}(a)
, we have
\beta\in\mathcal{B}(b)
by definition of
\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B}
which implies that
\beta=\mathrm{id}
since
\beta\in G\in\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)
. Likewise, since
\beta\in G\in\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)
, we have
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a)
which implies that
\alpha=\mathrm{id}
as
\alpha\in K\in\mathcal{A}(a)
.

Now, let
H
be in
(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})^{\perp\perp}(a,b)
, we show that
H
is in
(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})(a,b)
. Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be in
H
and assume that
\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}(a)
and that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\beta^{n}\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)
. Hence, we have
\langle\alpha^{n}\rangle\times\langle\beta^{n}\rangle\in(\mathcal{A}(a)\times\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b))
which implies that
\langle\alpha^{n}\rangle\times\langle\beta^{n}\rangle\in(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})^{\perp}(a,b)
so that
\alpha^{n}=\mathrm{id}
and
\beta^{n}=\mathrm{id}
as desired. We can show similarly that if
\beta\in\bigcup\mathcal{B}^{\perp}(b)
then
\alpha\in\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\perp}(a)
.

For a subgroup
H\leq\mathrm{Endo}(a,b)
such that
H\subseteq\bigcup(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})(a,b)
, it is immediate that
H
is in
(\mathcal{A}\multimap\mathcal{B})(a,b)
. Hence, by Lemma

6.3.
Differential structure.

To a combinatorial species

, one can associate an exponential generating series of the form

.

This combinatorial notion extends to the wider universe of generalized

[
Fio05
]

which coincides with the above construction when

The combinatorial notion of differentiation has a logical counterpart in the framework of

put forward by Ehrhard et al.

]

provides a model for differential linear logic, in which logical derivatives are formally assigned a combinatorial interpretation
[
Fio05
]
.

The differential operators of
\mathbf{Prof}
extend to
\mathbf{SProf}
, which we
show is also a model for differential linear logic. Indeed, given a
model of linear logic with biproducts, the structure required to model

[
Fio07
]
. The fact that

is a model for
differential linear logic follows from the forgetful functor to

8.2.
Cartesian natural transformations.

Our purpose now is to extend this result to a bicategorical equivalence, which we achieve by investigating the correspondence at the level of natural transformations.

The situation at this level has subtle ramifications already in the setting of Berry’s domain theory: for cpos
A
and
B
the pointwise order on the set of stable functions does not lead to a cartesian closed category. Indeed, to obtain cartesian closure one is forced to restrict the objects to cpos with bounded meets, and the stable function space
A\Rightarrow B
must be ordered according to

f\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{st}}g\iff\forall\,x\leq_{A}y\in A.\,\ g(x)\wedge f(y)=f(x).

By considering
x\leq_{A}x
in
A
, one necessarily has that
f(x)\leq_{B}g(x)
, so this is a strengthening of the pointwise order. In fact the order
\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{st}}
is precisely what is needed to characterize inclusion at the level of traces, and it is via a categorification of this order that we will establish our correspondence result.

The appropriate notion is that of

Definition 68
.

cartesian natural transformations, whose naturality squares are pullbacks. These generalize the order
\sqsubseteq_{\mathrm{st}}
on stable functions (consider the naturality square for
x\leq_{A}y
), and additionally preserve and reflect generic morphisms
[
Web04
]
. Cartesian natural transformations are considered also in the settings of normal functors and polynomial functors
[
Gir88
,
GK13
]
. With the interpretation of polynomial functors as arising from sets of operations with arities, a cartesian natural transformation corresponds to a mapping between operations that preserves arities.

We show in this section that our construction yields a biequivalence between the bicategory of stable species and the
2
-category
\mathbf{Stable}
defined below:

{defi}

\mathbf{Stable}
has Boolean kits as objects, stable functors
\mathbfcal{S}(\underline{\mathbb{A}})\to\mathbfcal{S}(\underline{\mathbb{B}})
as morphisms
\underline{\mathbb{A}}\to\underline{\mathbb{B}}
, and cartesian natural transformations as 2-cells.

Natural transformations between stable species in

yield cartesian transformations between the corresponding stable functors.

10.
Conclusion

We have introduced a new bicategorical model of classical linear logic
based on groupoids with kits and stabilized profunctors. We have
also presented this model in extensional form, in terms of stable
functors between subcategories of presheaves. Restricted to discrete
groupoids (
i.e.
sets), stable functors coincide with finitary polynomial
functors
\mathbf{Set}^{I}\to\mathbf{Set}^{J}
between categories of indexed sets,
called
normal functors
in the influential work of
Girard
[
Gir88
]
. The construction of a cartesian closed
bicategory that embeds finitary polynomial functors is a key
contribution of this work.

Some aspects of our construction should be understood more
abstractly. The bicategory of stable species of structures refines the
bicategory of generalized species
[
FGHW08
]
by means
of an orthogonality construction. Refinements of this kind are
well-studied in the
1
-categorical setting and provide a rich family
of models for linear logic
[
HS03
]
. Our
construction provides a new example of orthogonality and

The notion of kit also deserves further exploration. In particular,
various families of non-Boolean kits with additional closure
conditions provide models of intuitionistic linear logic. It seems
less clear how to understand these models in extensional form.

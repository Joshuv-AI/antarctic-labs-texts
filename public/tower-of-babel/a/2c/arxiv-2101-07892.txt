Direct evidence of ferromagnetism in MnSb 2 ​ Te 4 \text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
Wenbo Ge, Paul M. Sass, Jiaqiang Yan, Seng Huat Lee, Zhiqiang Mao, Weida Wu

Abstract
We report the magnetic imaging of ferromagnetic domains in the van der Waals single crystal MnSb 2 ​ Te 4 \text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} from two different sources using cryogenic magnetic force microscopy. The magnetic field dependence of the domains reveals very weak pinning of domain walls in MnSb 2 ​ Te 4 \text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} , resulting in a negligibly small magnetic hysteresis loop. The temperature dependence of the domain contrast reveals a mean field like behavior, in good agreement with that of bulk magnetization measurements.

\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

Here, we report cryogenic magnetic force microscopy (MFM) of ferromagnetic
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}

The magnetic field dependence of domain population shows little hysteresis, in good agreement with that of bulk magnetization. The temperature dependence of domain contrast follows mean field behavior, in good agreement with that of the saturation magnetization.

II
materials and methods

Ferromagnetic
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
single crystals from Penn State were grown using a self-flux method
Yan et al. 2019a
;
Lee et al. 2020
. The mixture of manganese powder (99.95%), antimony shot (99.9999%), and tellurium ingot (99.9999+%) with the molar ratio of Mn:Sb:Te = 1:10:16 were loaded into alumina crucible and sealed in double evacuated quartz tubes. The mixture was heated up to 900
∘
C for 12 h and then slowly cooled down to 630
∘
C at a rate of 2
∘
C/h and dwelt at 630
∘
C for 24 h. Finally, the extra flux was removed by centrifuging at 630
∘
C. The phase and crystallinity of single crystals are checked by x-ray diffraction. Single crystals from Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) were grown with similar methods
Yan et al. 2019a
. The ferromagnetic order with Curie temperature of 33 K was confirmed through magnetization measurements using a commercial superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) magnetometer.

Here, we denote S1 for single crystals from Penn State, and S2 for single crystals from ORNL. Samples were cleaved in air before MFM experiments. The MFM experiments were carried out in a homemade cryogenic magnetic force microscope using commercial piezoresistive cantilevers (spring constant
\approx 3
N/m, resonant frequency
\approx 42
kHz)
Sass et al. 2020
. The homemade MFM is interfaced with a Nanonis SPM controller and a commercial phase-lock loop (SPECS). Out-of-plane magnetic field was applied via a superconducting magnet. MFM tips were prepared by depositing a nominally 100 nm Co film onto the bare tips using sputtering. The MFM signal, the change of cantilever resonant frequency, is proportional to the out-of-plane stray field gradient. Electrostatic interaction was minimized by balancing the tip-surface potential difference. Dark (bright) regions in MFM images represent attractive (repulsive) magnetization, where magnetization are parallel (antiparallel) with the tip polarization.

III
results and discussion

We performed magnetic field dependent MFM measurements on S1 (6 K) and S2 (10 K), respectively. The MFM images are shown in Fig.

. In Fig.

1

\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
where domain walls were visualized
Sass et al. 2020

\mu

\mu
m in S2. After ZFC, we observed equal populations of up and down domains. After increasing the out-of-plane positive applied magnetic field, the domain walls propagate in such a way that the up domains (dark region) expand until they fully occupy the scanning area at around 0.1 T [Figs.
1
(a)-(e) and (j)-(n)]. Consistently, S2 is magnetically saturated above 0.1 T
Liu et al. 2020b
. Down domains nucleate as the external field is reduced from the saturation field [Figs.
1
(f) and (o)] and their area increases as the field decreases [Figs.
1
(f)-(i) and (o)-(r)]. The populations of up and down domains become nearly the same again at zero applied field. These results show that both
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
samples are soft ferromagnets with extremely weak domain wall pinning. Moreover, the domain contrast is relatively stronger at high fields possibly due to the enhancement of the MFM tip moment. There exists subtle difference in the domain shape between S1 and S2. Domains in S1 are more stripy than domains in S2, suggesting that the magnetism in S1 is more 2D-like. This subtle difference might be related to the different correlation length along the
c

-1

2
(j) is the binarized version of Fig.
2
(i). The normalized magnetization

of the sample can then be estimated from the binarized MFM images from
(N_{\uparrow}-N_{\downarrow})/(N_{\uparrow}+N_{\downarrow})
by counting
N_{\uparrow}
and
N_{\downarrow}

M_{\textrm{S}}
is the saturation magnetization and
N_{\uparrow}
(
N_{\downarrow}
) represents the population of up (down) domains. Using the method described above, we obtain the normalized
M(H)
data as plotted in Fig.
2
(k). The external field was swept from 0 T to
-0.1
T so that the sample was saturated, and then reduced back to 0 T. No visible hysteresis was found in the normalized magnetization

IV
conclusion

The magnetic imaging of domains in
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
single crystals from two different sources provides direct evidence of long-range ferromagnetic ordering in
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
.
The extremely weak pinning of domain walls observed in the field dependence explains why little hysteresis was observed in bulk magnetization measurements.
The agreement between domain population and the bulk magnetization demonstrates that the MFM measurements capture the representative domain behavior.
Also, the temperature dependence of domain contrast agrees with the that of the saturation magnetization.
The direct evidence of ferromagnetism in
\text{MnSb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
will encourage further exploration of potential ferromagnetic Weyl physics in the
\text{MnBi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
-family.

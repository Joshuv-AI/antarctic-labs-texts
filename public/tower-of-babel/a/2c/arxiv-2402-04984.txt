The cardinal characteristics of the ideal generated by the F σ F_{\sigma} measure zero subsets of the reals
Miguel A. Cardona

Abstract
Let ℰ \mathcal{E} be the ideal generated by the F σ F_{\sigma} measure zero subsets of the reals. The purpose of this survey paper is to study the cardinal characteristics (the additivity, covering number, uniformity, and cofinality) of ℰ \mathcal{E} .

Notation.
A
forcing notion
is a pair
\langle\mathbb{P},\leq\rangle
where
\mathbb{P}\neq\emptyset
and
\leq
is a relation on
\mathbb{P}
satisfying reflexivity and
transitivity. We also use the expression pre-ordered set (abbreviated p.o. set or just poset) to refer to a forcing notion. The elements of
\mathbb{P}
are called conditions and we say that a condition
q
is

than a condition

q\leq p

One of the fundamental properties of forcing is the Laver property:

A forcing notion
\mathbb{P}\in V
has the
Laver property
if for any
\mathbb{P}
-generic
G
over
V
, any function
f\in\omega^{\omega}\cap V
and any
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{g}
for a member in
\omega^{\omega}
such that
\Vdash\dot{g}\leq^{*}f
, there exists a function
\varphi\in([\omega]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\cap V
such that
\Vdash\dot{g}(n)\in\varphi(n)
and
|\varphi(n)|\leq n+1
for every
n\in\omega
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathbb{P}
has the Laver property. Let
\dot{x}
be a name for a real in
2^{\omega}
, and
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Then there are
q\leq p
and a closed null set
C\subseteq 2^{\omega}
such that
q\Vdash\dot{x}\in C
.

Proof.

Let
\langle I_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
be
a partition of
\omega
into finite intervals such that
\min(I_{0})=0
,
\max(I_{n})+1=\min(I_{n+1})
, and
|I_{n}|=2n+1
. Define a name
\dot{h}
for a function with domain
\omega
such that the trivial condition forces
\dot{h}(n)=\dot{x}{\upharpoonright}I_{n}
. In particular,
\dot{h}(n)
is forced to be an element of
2^{I_{n}}
. By the Laver property, there are
q\leq p
in
\mathbb{P}
and
\varphi\in\prod_{n<\omega}\mathcal{P}(2^{I_{n}})
such that
|\varphi(n)|\leq 2^{n}
and
q\Vdash\dot{h}(n)\in\varphi(n)
for all
n<\omega
. Hence
q\Vdash\dot{x}{\upharpoonright}I_{n}\in\varphi(n)
for all
n
, so let
C:=\{y\in 2^{\omega}:\forall n<\omega\colon y{\upharpoonright}I_{n}\in\varphi(n)\}
(coded in the ground model). It is not hard to prove that
C
is a closed set, so it remains to see that
C
has measure zero set. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Lb}(C)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\in\omega}\mathrm{Lb}\big(\{y\in 2^{\omega}:y{\upharpoonright}I_{n}\in\varphi(n)\}\big)

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\in\omega}\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{|I_{n}|}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\in\omega}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{|I_{n}|}}\leq\prod_{n\in\omega}\frac{2^{n}}{2^{2n+1}}=\prod_{n\in\omega}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}=0.

By
\mathrm{Lb}
we denote Lebesgue measure zero on
2^{\omega}
. Since
q\Vdash\dot{x}\in C
, we are done.
∎

Definition 3.1
.

For an increasing function
b\in\omega^{\omega}
, define the following sets of slaloms:

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{b}:=\{\varphi\in\prod_{n<\omega}\mathcal{P}(b(n)):\,\forall\,n\in\omega\colon\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{w}}_{b}:=\{\varphi\in\prod_{n<\omega}\mathcal{P}(b(n)):\,\exists^{\infty}\,n\in\omega\colon\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\}
.

Note that
\mathcal{S}_{b}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{w}}_{b}
and that

\begin{split}\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{w}}_{b}&\text{ implies }\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{|b(n)|}<1,\\
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{b}&\text{ implies }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{|b(n)|}<1.\end{split}

Also observe that, if
b
is a function with the domain
\omega
such that
b(i)\neq\varnothing
for all
i<\omega
, and

, then

(
\varotimes
)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}<1

\displaystyle\text{\ iff\ }\forall\,m<\omega\colon\prod_{n\geq m}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}=0,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}<1

\displaystyle\text{\ iff\ }\forall\,A\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}\colon\prod_{n\in A}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}=0.

We can construct sets in
\mathcal{E}
using slaloms in the following way.

Let

Notice that

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Lb}(B_{\tilde{b},\varphi}^{m})=

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq m}\mathrm{Lb}\left(\{x\in 2^{\omega}:\,x{\upharpoonright}[\tilde{b}(n),\tilde{b}(n+1))\in\varphi(n)\}\right)

Lemma 3.4

(
[
BS92
, Thm. 4.3]
)
.

Suppose that

. Then there is some increasing
\tilde{b}\in\omega^{\omega}
and some

such that

Proof.

Let us assume wlog that
C\subseteq 2^{\omega}
is a null set of type

. Then
C
can be written as
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
where
\langle C_{n}:\,n\in\omega\rangle
is an increasing family of closed sets of measure zero.

Note that each

is a compact set. It is easy to see that, if
K\subseteq 2^{\omega}
is a compact null set, then

\forall\,\varepsilon>0\ \forall^{\infty}\,n\ \exists\,T\subseteq 2^{n}\colon K\subseteq[T]\text{ and }\frac{|T|}{2^{n}}<\varepsilon

where
[T]:=\bigcup_{t\in T}[t]
.
Hence, we can define an increasing
\tilde{b}\in\omega^{\omega}
by
\tilde{b}(0):=0
and

\tilde{b}(n+1):=\min\{m>\tilde{b}(n):\,\exists\,T_{n}\subseteq 2^{m}\colon C_{n}\subseteq[T_{n}]\text{ and }\frac{|T_{n}|}{2^{m}}<\frac{1}{4^{\tilde{b}(n)}}\}\textrm{\ for $n>1$}.

Next, choose
T_{n}\subseteq 2^{\tilde{b}(n+1)}
such that

and
\frac{|T_{n}|}{2^{\tilde{b}(n+1)}}<\frac{1}{4^{\tilde{b}(n)}}

\varphi(n):=\{s{\upharpoonright}[\tilde{b}(n),\tilde{b}(n+1)):\,s\in T_{n}\}.

It is clear that

for every

\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{\tilde{b}(n+1)-\tilde{b}(n)}}<\frac{1}{2^{\tilde{b}(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Thus,

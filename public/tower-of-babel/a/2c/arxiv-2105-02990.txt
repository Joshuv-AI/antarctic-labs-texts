Abstract
. For any affine semigroup S S the set S ∪ { ∞ } S\cup\{\infty\} has a natural structure of semigroup, additionally if S S is endowed with the discrete topology, the semigroup S ∪ { ∞ } S\cup\{\infty\} can be studied as the one-point compactification of S S . In this article we study the derivations on the semigroup algebra ℂ ⁡ [ S ∪ { ∞ } ] \mathbb{C}[S\cup\{\infty\}] in relation to the derivations on the semigroup algebra ℂ ⁡ [ S ] \mathbb{C}[S] considering the metrizable topology on ℂ ⁡ [ S ∪ { ∞ } ] \mathbb{C}[S\cup\{\infty\}] induced by the one-point compactification topology of S ∪ { ∞ } S\cup\{\infty\} .

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Semigroup and topological semigroup

By a
semigroup
we mean to a nonempty set
S
with an associative binary operation
+\colon S\times S\rightarrow S
where
+(a,b)
will be denoted by
a+b
. An element
0
in a semigroup is an
identity
if for every element
a
in
S
, the equations
0+a=a
and
a+0=a
hold. A semigroup is said to be
commutative
if
a+b=b+a
for all
a,b\in S
. Typical example of semigroup is the natural number
\mathbb{N}
, for us, the element
0
will be in
\mathbb{N}
. A
face

F
of a semigroup
S
is a subsemigroup such that if
a+b
is in
F
then
a
and
b
are elements of
F
. Morphisms in the category of semigroup are functions preserving the semigroup structure, this morphism will be called semigroup homomorphisms.

cancellative
semigroup are semigroup such that if
a+b=a+c
then
b=c
. In what follows all semigroups are cancellative and commutative since commutative semigroups
S
are embeddable in a group
M
if and only if
S
is cancellative (see
[
1
, theorem 3.10]
).

A
Semitopological semigroup
is a semigroup
S
endowed with a topology such that for all
a\in S
the function
\lambda_{a}\colon S\rightarrow S
defined by
b\mapsto a+b
is continuous. More strong, a
topological semigroup
is a semigroup
S
endowed with a topology such that associative binary operation
+\colon S\times S\rightarrow S
is continuous, where
S\times S
is endowed with the product topology. All topological semigroup are semitopological semigroup but the converse in general is false has we can see in the example proposed in
[
7
, Exercise 2.1.2]
or Example
2.2
in this article. Morphisms in the category of semitopological semigroup and topological semigroup are continuous semigroup homomorphisms.

1.1.1.
Affine semigroup and convex geometry

Affine semigroups
are commutative semigroups
S
with an identity and a finite set
A\subset S
such that
S=\{\sum_{a\in A}\alpha_{a}a\mid\alpha_{a}\in\mathbb{N}\}
verifying that
S
is embeddable in a lattice
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
integer, particularly we will assume
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}S=\{a-b\mid a,b\in S\}
where
a-b
represent an equivalence class where the equivalence relation
\sim
is defined on
S\times S
as
(a,b)\sim(c,d)
if and only if
a+d=c+b
. We identify
S
in
M
via
a\mapsto a-0
. Together to
M
we associate to
S
the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\simeq N=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and a bilinear function
\langle\ ,\ \rangle\colon M\times N\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
naturally expandable to bilinear function
\langle\ ,\ \rangle\colon M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
where
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}
are the vectorial spaces
M\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
N\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
respectively. For any affine semigroup
S
with finite generator set
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
we define the convex polyhedral cone
\operatorname{cone}(A)=\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}a_{n}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\lambda_{i}\geq 0\}
and
\operatorname{cone}(A)^{\vee}=\{u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ m\in\operatorname{cone}(A)\}
this also is a convex polyhedral cone verifying
(\operatorname{cone}(A)^{\vee})^{\vee}=\operatorname{cone}(A)
(
[
3
, Proposition 1.2.4]
). A face of a convex polyhedral cone
\sigma
is a subset
\tau_{m}=\{u\in\sigma\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\}
where
m\in\sigma^{\vee}
, convex polyhedral cones where
\{0\}
is a face are called
strongly convex
or pointed.
The dimension of a face
\tau
is the dimension of the vectorial space generated by
\tau
. The set of faces of dimension
1
of a convex polyhedral cone
\sigma
will be denote as
\sigma(1)
and the shortest vector in
N
(or
M
) generator of a face
\rho\in\sigma(1)
will be denoted indistinctly by
\rho
. Faces of polyhedral cones
\sigma
are in correspondence with faces of the dual polyhedral cone
\sigma^{\vee}
via
\tau\mapsto\tau^{*}:=\tau^{\perp}\cap\sigma^{\vee}
where
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
and
\tau^{\perp}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\text{ for all }u\in\tau.\}

An affine semigroup is said to be
\emph{saturated}
if
ka\in S
when
k
is a positive integer and
a\in M
, then
a\in S
. When
S
is not saturated, we can associate to
S
the affine semigroup
S^{sat}=\{a\in M\mid ka\in S,\text{ where $k$ is a positive integer}\}
called the saturation of
S
. Additionally
S^{sat}=M\cap\operatorname{cone}(A)
where
A
is a finite generator set of
S
.

1.2.
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of semigroup

Let
S
be a semigroup, a
S
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra is an associative, commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with unity and a direct sum decomposition
B=\bigoplus_{a\in S}B_{a}
where
B_{a}\subset B
are
\mathbb{C}
-subvectorial spaces and
B_{a}B_{b}\subset B_{a+b}
. The
B_{a}
are
S
-homogeneous component and we will say that the elements
f
in
B_{a}
for some
a\in S
are
S
-homogeneous element of degree
a
. So for all semigroup
S
we can associate it a
S
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra defined by
\mathbb{C}[S]=\bigoplus_{a\in S}\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{a}
where
\chi^{a}
are formal elements, verifying
\chi^{a}\chi^{b}=\chi^{a+b}
and the identity is the element
1=\chi^{0}
. Morphisms between
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of semigroup are homomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras induced by semigroup homomorphisms i.e.
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}[S]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[S^{\prime}]
is a morphism if there exists a semigroup homomorphism
\psi\colon S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
such that
\phi(\chi^{a})=\chi^{\psi(a)}
. If
M
is a group such that
S
is embeddable in
M
the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathbb{C}[S]
also is
M
-graded via the identification
\mathbb{C}[S]=\bigoplus_{a\in M}B_{a}
where
B_{a}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{a}
if
a\in S
and
B_{a}=\{0\}
for the other case.

1.3.
Derivations

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, a
\mathbb{C}
-derivation on
B
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear function
\partial\colon B\rightarrow B
such that
\partial(f)=0
for all
f\in\mathbb{C}
and
\partial(fg)=f\partial(g)+g\partial(f)
for all
f,g\in B
(
Leibniz’s rule
). A derivation on
B
is said to be locally nilpotent derivation (lnd for short) if for all
f\in B
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
depending of
f
such that
\partial^{(n)}(f)=0
where
\partial^{(n)}
is the
n
-th composition of
\partial
. The set of derivations on
B
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Der}(B)
and by
\operatorname{LND}(B)
we mean to the set of lnd in
\operatorname{Der}(B)
additionally the set of
\mathbb{C}
-derivations on
B
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)
and by
\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)
we mean to the set of lnd in
\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)
.

If
B=\bigoplus_{a\in S}B_{a}
is a
S
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra for some semigroup
S
, a derivation
\partial\in\operatorname{Der}(B)
is said to be
homogeneous
if it sends
S
-homogeneous elements into
S
-homogeneous elements, particularly when
S
is affine semigroup,
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
the lattice associated to
S
and
\mathbb{C}[S]=\bigoplus_{a\in M}B_{a}
is the
M
-graded previously described, for every nontrivial homogeneous derivation
\partial
there exists an element
e\in M
such that
\partial(B_{a})\subset B_{a+e}
for all
a\in M
as is proved in the follow lemma:

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
M
be the lattice associated to an affine semigroup
S
. If
\partial\colon\bigoplus_{a\in M}B_{a}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{a\in M}B_{a}
is a homogeneous derivation, then there exists
e\in M
such that
\partial(B_{a})\subset B_{a+e}
for all
a\in M
.

Proof.

Let
a\neq b
be an element in
M
and let
f
,
g
be elements in
B_{a}
and
B_{b}
respectively such that
\partial(f)\neq 0\in B_{a+e(a)}
and
\partial(g)\neq 0\in B_{b+e(b)}
, where
e(a)
and
e(b)
are elements in
M
depending of
a
and
b
respectively. By Leibniz’s rule
\partial(fg)=f\partial(g)+g\partial(f)\in B_{b+a+e(a)}\bigoplus B_{a+b+e(b)}
but also
\partial(fg)\in B_{a+b+e(a+b)}
where
e(a+b)
is an element in
M
depending of
a+b
, we conclude that

a+b+e(b)=b+a+e(a)
thus

e(a)=e(b)
.
∎

The element
e\in M
associated to
\partial
will be called the
degree of

\partial
and will be denoted by
e=\deg\partial
. We will say homogeneous derivation instead of
S
-homogeneous derivation when the context is clear.

1.4.
Topological structures

A
topological ring

R
is a ring with topological structure such that the functions
R\times R\rightarrow R
defined by
(f,g)\mapsto f-g
and
(f,g)\mapsto fg
are continuous, morphisms between topological rings are continuous homomorphisms between the rings. A
Topological
R
-algebra
on a topological ring
R
is a topological ring
B
such that the function induced since the structure of
R
-algebra
R\times B\rightarrow B
is continuous, also the morphism between topological
R
-algebras are continuous homomorphism between
R
-algebras.
A topology on a topological ring
R
(or
R
-algebra
B
) will be called linear if there exists a fundamental system of open neighborhood of its additive identity element
0
. In this article we will assume
R=\mathbb{C}
with discrete topology and
B
a separated topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with fundamental system of open neighborhood of its neutral element
0
consisting in a countable family of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{0}\supset\mathfrak{a}_{1}\supset\dots
.

1.4.1.
Topologically integrable derivation.

Topologically integrable derivation was introduced by
[
4
]

as a generalization of locally nilpotent derivation in the context of topological
R
-algebras and affine ind-schemes, theory develop for example in
[
8
,
9
]
. This generalization is motivated since geometric aspect because, in the same form that locally nilpotent derivation, the topologically integrable derivations are in correspondence with
\mathbb{G}_{R,a}
-actions on the formal spectrum of a topological
R
-algebra
B
(see
[
4
, Section 3]
). In this article our interest is on topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, there exists a more general definition, but for this article we work with the simplified definition:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
B
be a topological
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We call a continuous
\mathbb{C}
-derivation
\partial
of
B

topologically integrable
if the sequence of
\mathbf{k}
-linear endomorphisms

(\partial^{(n)})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
B
converges continuously to the zero homomorphism, that is, if for every
f\in B
and every

i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an indices
n_{0},j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\partial^{(n)}(f+\mathfrak{a}_{j})\subset\mathfrak{a}_{i}
for every integer
n\geq n_{0}
.

When
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\{0\}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
the topology on
B
is the discrete topology and the definition of topologically integrable derivation coincide with locally nilpotent derivation. Allow us a natural example to clarify the topologically integrable derivation.

Example 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{C}[[u]]
the ring of formal power series endowed with the
u
-adic topology, the
u
-adic topology is induced by the fundamental system of open ideal
\mathbb{C}[[u]]=\langle 1\rangle\supset\langle u\rangle\supset\langle u^{2}\rangle\supset\dots
where
\langle u^{j}\rangle
are the ideals generated by
u^{j}
for all integer
j\geq 0
. We define the derivation
\partial=u^{2}\dfrac{d}{du}\colon\mathbb{C}[[u]]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[u]]
. The derivation
\partial
is continuous since
\partial(\langle u^{j}\rangle)\subset\langle u^{j+1}\rangle
, also is topologically integrable since
\partial^{(n)}(f+\langle u^{j}\rangle)\subset\langle u^{n+1}\rangle
thus for every
f\in\mathbb{C}[[u]]
and
i\in n
, with arbitrary
j
and
n_{0}=i
it is verified that
\partial^{(n)}(f+\langle u^{j}\rangle)\subset\langle u^{i}\rangle
for all
n\geq n_{0}=i
. A similar argument verifies that
u^{l}\dfrac{d}{du}\colon\mathbb{C}[[u]]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[u]]
is topologically integrable for all
l\geq 2
.

As a rewriting of
[
4
, Lemma 1.9]
we can conclude that:

Lemma 1.4
.

A derivation
\partial\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)
is topologically integrable if and only if the follows are verified:

(P.1)

The sequence
(\partial^{(n)}{(f)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to
0
for all
f\in B
and

(P.2)

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\partial^{(n)}(\mathfrak{a}_{j})\subset\mathfrak{a}_{i}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.
one-point compactification of affine semigroup

Let
S
be a semigroup we can define the semigroup
S_{\infty}
associated to
S
as the set
S\cup\{\infty\}
with the associative binary operation induced by the binary operation of
S
and
a+\infty=\infty+a=\infty
for all
a\in S_{\infty}
. The construction give us a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{\infty}\times S_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{\infty}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta_{+}}

where
\zeta_{+}|_{S\times S}=\eta_{+}
. The notation
\zeta_{+}
and
\eta_{+}
will be used to describe the binary operations instead of

+
when we need to differentiate between the binary operations on
S
and
S_{\infty}
.

If
S
is a Hausdorff and locally compact topological semigroup we will endow
S_{\infty}
with the one-point compactification topology (Alexandroff extension), i.e. the subsets
U
in
S_{\infty}
are open if:

(1)

U\subset S
and is an open in
S
,

(2)

U=S_{\infty}\backslash C
where
C
is compact in
S
.

Equivalently the subsets
F
in
S_{\infty}
are closed if:

(1)

F=F^{\prime}\cup\{\infty\}
where
F^{\prime}
is a closed set in
S
,

(2)

F
is a compact set in
S
.

additionally
S_{\infty}
verify to be Hausdorff, compact. In this section always we will assume
S
is an affine semigroup endow with the discrete topology and
S_{\infty}
will be endowed with the one-point compactification topology, easily we can see that
S_{\infty}
is a semitopological semigroup as is proved in Lemma

but in general it is not a topological semigroup as we can see in Example
2.2
.

Although some proofs prove more general cases, but for our purpose, the above conditions are sufficient.

Lemma 2.1
.

The semigroup
S_{\infty}
is a semitopological semigroup.

Proof.

We need to study the options
a=0
,
a=\infty
and other cases.

(1)

a=0
. The function
\lambda_{0}\colon S_{\infty}\rightarrow S_{\infty}
is the identity function so preimage of closed sets are closed sets.

(2)

a=\infty
. for all subset
A\subset S_{\infty}
the preimage by
\lambda_{\infty}
is
S_{\infty}
if
\infty\in A
or the empty set if
\infty\notin A
thus also is continuous.

(3)

Other cases. If
F
is a compact in
S\subset S_{\infty}
then is finite, additionally
S
is cancellative so preimage of
F
by
\lambda_{a}
is finite thus compact in
S
and closed in
S_{\infty}
. The other possibility is
F=F^{\prime}\cup\{\infty\}
for some closed
F^{\prime}\subset S
, the preimage of
F
contain
\infty
and since
S
is endowed with the discrete topology any sub set is closed thus the preimage of
F
is closed in
S_{\infty}
.

∎

Generally not true that for some affine semigroup
S
endowed with the discrete topology the semigroup
S_{\infty}
with the one-point compactification topology is a topological semigroup.

Example 2.2
.

Let
S=\mathbb{Z}
be the semigroup (group) of integer number. The binary function

is not continuous in

has an element

for some

then
+(U)\subsetneq S_{\infty}\backslash\{0\}
.

Conversely, if
S=\mathbb{N}
the semigroup
S_{\infty}
as we will prove more generally in Theorem

Recall a
Face
of a semigroup
S
is a subsemigroup
F
such that if
a+b\in F
then
a\in F
and
b\in F
.

Definition 2.3
.

By a pointed affine semigroup
S
we mean to an affine semigroup
S
where
\{0\}
is a face of
S
, equivalently if
a+b=0
then
a=b=0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
S\simeq\mathbb{N}^{l}
for some

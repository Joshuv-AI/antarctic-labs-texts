A property equivalent to being semi-Kelley Journal: Topology and its Applications
Mauricio Chacón-Tirado, María de J. López, Ivon Vidal-Escobar

Abstract
We present a property equivalent to the property of being semi-Kelley. Using this equivalence we prove that being semi-Kelley is a hereditary property for atriodic continua. We prove that semi-Kelley remainders are atriodic, moreover, we prove that semi-Kelley continua are semi-Kelley remainders for chainable continua, circularly chainable continua, and arc continua, and we give an example of an atriodic Kelley continuum which is a semi-Kelley remainder and not a Kelley remainder. We also prove that hereditarily semi-Kelley dendroids are smooth.

2
An equivalence to the property of being semi-Kelley

In this section we introduce an equivalence to the property of being semi-Kelley. Recall that a continuum
X
is
irreducible between
p
and
q
provided that
p,q\in X
and no proper subcontinuum of
X
contains
p
and
q
. We say that

is

if there exist

and

such that

is irreducible between

and

.

The proof of the following theorem is easy and we leave it to the reader.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a continuum. Then,

is Kelley if and only if for every
a\in X
, for every
I\in C(X)
irreducible between
a
and some other point of
X
, and for every sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
X
converging to
a
, there exists a sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
C(X)
converging to
I
such that
a_{n}\in A_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We prove the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a continuum. Then,
X
is semi-Kelley if and only if for every
a,b\in X
, for every
I\in C(X)
irreducible between
a
and
b
, and for every sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

there exists a sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

converging to

a_{n}\in A_{n}
for each

, or for every sequence

converging to

\{B_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

Necessity
. Suppose that

and

Claim 1
. For any sequences

for each

, or there exist a subsequence

of the sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and a sequence

in

such that

Proof of Claim 1.

By Proposition 3.9 and Statement 3.4 of
[
7
]
, there exists

. Since

is semi-Kelley, without loss of generality we may assume

Claim 2

, or for every sequence

in

\{B_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

converging to

. Assume that

A continuum is

[

Theorem 4.5

5
Hereditarily semi-Kelley dendroids

We prove that hereditarily semi-Kelley dendroids are smooth. We say that a continuum
X
is
semi-locally connected at a point

x\in X
provided that for every open set
U
of
X
such that
x\in U
, there exists an open set
V
of
X
such that
x\in V\subset U
and
X\setminus V
consists of a finite number of components. We say that
X
is
colocally connected at a point

x\in X
provided that for every open set
U
of
X
such that
x\in U
, there exists an open set
V
such that
x\in V\subset U
and
X\setminus V
is connected.

Given a dendroid
X
, a point
p\in X
is
end point
of
X
, if
x
is an end point of every arc in
X
to which
x
belongs. Denote by
E_{S}(X)
the set of end points of
X
at which
X
is semi-locally connected. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
,
E_{S}(X)
is equal to the subset of
X
consisting of all points at which
X
is colocally connected. Let
AE_{S}(X)=\cup\{ab\subset X:a,b\in E_{S}(X)\}
. By
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
,
AE_{S}(X)
is a dense subset of
X
.

We thank Prof. A. Illanes for a proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a dendroid different from an arc and let
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of arcs in
X
. Then,
X\neq\lim A_{n}
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
,
AE_{S}(X)
is a dense subset of
X
, thus there exist
a,b\in E_{S}(X)
, with
a\neq b
; since
X
is not an arc,
X\neq ab
, hence there exists
c\in E_{S}(X)\setminus\{a,b\}
. Let
U_{a}
,
U_{b}
,
U_{c}
be open sets of
X
such that
a\in U_{a},b\in U_{b},c\in U_{c}
, the sets
X\setminus U_{a}
,
X\setminus U_{b}
, and
X\setminus U_{c}
are connected, and
cl(U_{a})
,
cl(U_{b})
,
cl(U_{c})
are pairwise disjoint.

Assume that
X=\lim A_{n}
, thus there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A_{N}\cap U_{a}\neq\emptyset
,
A_{N}\cap U_{b}\neq\emptyset
, and
A_{N}\cap U_{c}\neq\emptyset
. Let
a^{\prime}\in A_{N}\cap U_{a}
,
b^{\prime}\in A_{N}\cap U_{b}
, and
c^{\prime}\in A_{N}\cap U_{c}
. Assume without loss of generality that

. Notice that

. Since

is a subdendroid of

,
a^{\prime}c^{\prime}\subset X\setminus U_{b}
. Hence,

, contradicting

. This finishes the proof.
∎

In
[
9
]
E. E. Grace and E. J. Vought introduced the definitions of a Type 1 dendroid and Type 2 dendroid, where they used these conceps to prove that a dendroid is non-smooth if and only if it contains a Type 1 or Type 2 subdendroid, see
[
9
, Theorem 1]
. We prove that a hereditarily semi-Kelley dendroid does not contain a Type 1 subdendroid nor a Type 2 subdendroid, thus hereditarily semi-Kelley dendroids are smooth.

A dendroid

is a
Type 1 dendroid
provided that there exist points
p,a\in X
, a sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in

converging to

, such that
X=cl(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}pa_{i})
, and the following two properties are satisfied:

T1.1.

the sequence
\{pa_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges to some
L\in C(X)
,

T1.2.

there exists an end point
s
of
L
, with
s\neq a
, and there exists an open set
U
of
X
such that
s\in U
and
C_{s}\cap(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}pa_{i})=\emptyset
, where
C_{s}
is the component of
L\cap U
containing

.

Moreover, if the following property is satisfied:

T1.3.

if
i\neq j
, then
pa_{i}\cap pa_{j}\cap cl(U)=\emptyset
,

then

is called
strong Type 1 dendroid
.
The point

is called an

of

, the point

is called an

of

, and the point

is called a

of

. Notice that

, moreover,

. In the rest of the paper, if we say that

is a Type 1 dendroid, we mean that

is an emanation point, the sequence

converges to an absorption point

, and the sequence

converges to

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be a strong Type 1 dendroid with

. Then, the quotient

is a strong Type 1 dendroid with an emanation point equal to an absorption point.

Let

and let

be an open set of

, we can assume that

.
Let

be the quotient function. Since

[

. Let

,
\overline{a}=\pi(a)
,
\overline{a}_{i}=\pi(a_{i})
for each

,
\overline{L}=\pi(L)
,
\overline{s}=\pi(s)
, and

. Notice that
\overline{p}=\overline{a},\{\overline{a}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},\overline{s},\overline{L},

∎

Lemma 5.3

Let

\lim(pa_{k_{i}}\cap L)=M

Lemma 5.4

By Lemma

Discrete Orthogonal Ensemble on the Exponential Lattices
Shi-Hao Li, Bo-Jian Shen, Guo-Fu Yu, Peter J. Forrester

Abstract
. Inspired by Aomoto’s q q -Selberg integral, the orthogonal ensemble in the exponential lattice is considered in this paper. By introducing a skew symmetric kernel, the configuration space of this ensemble is constructed to be symmetric and thus, corresponding skew inner product, skew orthogonal polynomials as well as correlation functions are explicitly formulated. Examples including Al-Salam & Carlitz, q q -Laguerre, little q q -Jacobi and big q q -Jacobi cases are considered.

15B52, 15A15, 33E20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Random matrix theory has many connections within mathematics and its applications. The analogy with certain Boltzmann factors from equilibrium statistical mechanics, already known to Dyson
[
21
]
, is of particular relevance to the present work. The
\beta
-ensemble, as one of the most important consequences of this perspective, provides a one-parameter families of particle systems that interpolate between eigenvalue distributions of several classical random matrix models. The joint probability density function (j.p.d.f) of the
\beta
-ensemble, which is also viewed as the Boltzmann factor of certain interacting particles with log-potentials, is specified by

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\omega_{\beta}\left(x_{l}\right)\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left|x_{k}-x_{j}\right|^{\beta},\hskip 10.00002pt\beta>0,

(1.1)

where
\omega_{\beta}(x)
is a proper weight function and
Z_{N}
is the corresponding partition function.
Particular attention is paid to the specific
\beta=1,\,2,\,4
cases for the reason that the corresponding random matrix ensembles admit orthogonal, unitary and symplectic symmetry respectively, as relevant to particular applications
[
22
]
.

One of the most significant quantities in random matrix theory are the
k
-point correlation functions

\displaystyle\rho_{N,k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\frac{N!}{(N-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-k}}\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})dx_{k+1}\cdots dx_{N}

since they quantify many primary statistical quantities of an ensemble. For example, one-point correlation function represents the (particle or eigenvalue) density function of the random matrix. It is well known that for different
\beta
, these correlation functions admit different algebraic structures. In the case
\beta
equals to
2
, it is a standard result that
[
10
,
41
]

\displaystyle\rho_{N,k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=\det\left[K_{N}(x_{j_{1}},x_{j_{2}})\right]_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{k},

for a certain kernel
K_{N}(x,y)
independent of
k
. However, for
\beta
equals to
1
or
4
, Pfaffian structures are involved and as such bring difficulties to the analysis of the correlation functions.

In
[
1
]
, it was demonstrated that the correlation functions for
\beta=2
and
\beta=1,\,4
are inter-related. This has been exhibited by introducing the particular inner product and skew inner products respectively, namely,

\displaystyle\beta=1:\hskip 20.00003pt\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{sgn}(y-x)\phi(x)\psi(y)e^{-V(x)-V(y)}dxdy,

\displaystyle\beta=2:\hskip 20.00003pt\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{2}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)\psi(x)e^{-2V(x)}dx,

\displaystyle\beta=4:\hskip 20.00003pt\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{4}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\phi(x)\psi^{\prime}(x)-\phi^{\prime}(x)\psi(x))e^{-2V(x)}dx.

For classical weights
\omega_{2}(x)=e^{-2V(x)}
satisfying

\displaystyle 2V^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{g(x)}{f(x)},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{deg $f$ $\leq 2$, deg $g$ $\leq 1$},

one can construct the operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}:=f\frac{d}{dx}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}-g}{2}\right)

(1.2)

and its inverse
\mathbf{n}^{-1}
to connect the skew inner products with the inner product via

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\mathbf{n}^{-1}\psi\rangle_{2}=\left.-\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{1}\right|_{2V(x)\mapsto 2V(x)+\log f(x)},

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\mathbf{n}\psi\rangle_{2}=\left.\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{4}\right|_{2V(x)\mapsto 2V(x)-\log f(x)}.

(1.3b)

From this, a relation between the classical orthogonal polynomials and the corresponding skew orthogonal polynomials can be constructed. As a result, correlation functions of orthogonal and symplectic ensembles are obtained as a rank-one perturbation of the unitary one
[
1
,
52
]
, thus facilitating calculations for the edge limits of orthogonal and symplectic ensembles
[
18
,
23
,
26
]
. Moreover, it sheds light on the connections between Pfaff lattice and Toda lattice, which is an important result in classical integrable systems
[
3
]
.

A new strand of random matrix theory was created by the advent of various physically motivated, combinatorially based models which give rise to discrete analogues of (
1.1
); see e.g.
[
23
, Ch. 10]
. Specific examples include the random increasing subsequence problem for a random permutation
[
6
]
, and the polynuclear growth model
[
46
]
. The discrete generalization of (
1.1
) in the case of unitary symmetry (
\beta=2
) is the simplest with a determinant structure for the correlations, and has been developed to include all weight functions of the Wilson class
[
34
,
35
,
7
]
due to its relevance to applications. Applications also give rise to discrete models whose correlation functions exhibit Pfaffian structures, such as combinatorial models relating to random involutions
[
28
]
and certain tiling models in the non-intersecting paths picture
[
43
]
. These correspond to discrete generalisations of random matrix ensembles with orthogonal and symplectic symmetry.
Subsequently, discrete orthogonal and symplectic ensembles on the linear lattice were considered by Borodin and Strahov
[
12
]
, in which the authors showed that the correlation kernels for discrete orthogonal and symplectic ensembles are rank-one perturbations of the discrete unitary ensemble in operator forms. Recently, a
q
-symplectic ensemble on exponential lattice was proposed by the inspiration of the
q
-Selberg integral in
[
25
]
.

The functional form on a linear lattice that generalizes (
1.1
), and maintains integrability properties, is hinted at in Jack polynomial theory
[
39
, Ch. VI, §10]
. It is given explicitly in
[
13
]
by a j.p.d.f in terms of gamma functions as

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{\Gamma(\ell_{j}-\ell_{i}+1)\Gamma(\ell_{j}-\ell_{i}+\theta)}{\Gamma(\ell_{j}-\ell_{i})\Gamma(\ell_{j}-\ell_{i}+1-\theta)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega(\ell_{i};N)

(1.4)

on ordered
N
-tuples
\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{N}
, such that
\ell_{i}=\lambda_{i}+\theta i
and
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
are integers. Here
\ell_{i}
is the positions of
N
particles, and moreover, the ratios of gamma functions behave as
(\ell_{j}-\ell_{i})^{2\theta}
if we set
\ell_{i}=Lx_{i}
and take
L\to\infty
. Therefore, it is a natural discretization of the original
\beta
-ensemble. It should be remarked here that those particles does not necessarily lie on the integer lattices since
\theta
is not always an integer.
In
[
45
]
, Olshanski considered a Macdonald level extension, that is,
\beta
-ensemble on the exponential lattices (or so called
q
-lattices) with j.p.d.f. proportional to

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\prod_{r=0}^{\tau-1}(x_{i}-q^{r}x_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega(x_{i}).

(1.5)

The factor over pairs is familiar in the theory of
q
-generalizations of the Selberg integral, which itself contains the product over pairs as appears in (
1.1
); see e.g. the review
[
30
]
, and point (2) in Section
1.2
below.
One notices that when
\tau=1
, the product over pairs is equal to the Vandermonde squared and thus it is a
q
-version of the unitary symmetry ensemble; and when
\tau=2
, the j.p.d.f could be formulated as

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}(x_{i}-qx_{j})(x_{j}-qx_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega(x_{i}),

(1.6)

which is the j.p.d.f of the
q
-symplectic symmetry ensemble considered in
[
25
]
. However, this extension doesn’t contain the
q
-orthogonal symmetry ensemble case, which is the main topic of this article.

Motivations and main results of the article

(1)

To find a well motivated model on a

(2)

q

[

.
Likewise in relation to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\int_{[0,1]^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}

\displaystyle\left((z_{j}-z_{k})\prod_{1-\gamma\leq l\leq\gamma-1}(z_{j}-q^{l}z_{k})\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{\alpha-1}(qz_{i};q)_{\beta-1}d_{q}z_{i}

(1.7)

\displaystyle=q^{\alpha\gamma{n\choose 2}+2\gamma^{2}{n\choose 3}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(j\gamma)}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+\beta+(n+j-2)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(\gamma)}

has relevance as the partition function of the j.p.d.f (

) with weight functions chosen as the little

For this purpose we need knowledge of a generalisation of this

In

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\int_{z_{n}=0}^{q^{\gamma}z_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}z_{j}^{2\gamma-1}\frac{(q^{1-\gamma}z_{k}/z_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\gamma}z_{k}/z_{j};q)_{\infty}}(z_{j}-z_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{\alpha-1}(qz_{i};q)_{\beta-1}d_{q}z_{i}

(1.8)

\displaystyle=q^{\alpha\gamma{n\choose 2}+2\gamma^{2}{n\choose 3}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(j\gamma)}{\Gamma_{q}(\alpha+\beta+(n+j-2)\gamma)\Gamma_{q}(\gamma)},

which holds true for arbitrary
\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}

when
i=1,\,\cdots,\,n
. It was shown in
[

]
that upon an

Moroever, when

) with a Jacobi weight. This motivates us to take the product over differences

This definition shares the property of the orthogonal symmetry linear lattice in (

) that not

\displaystyle\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\,|\,0<q^{-(n-1)/2}z_{n}<\cdots<z_{1}<1\}.

(1.9)

Here

Organization of the article

This article is organized as follows. In Section

In this part, we start with the
q
-Selberg integral (

) given by Askey-Habsieger-Kadell.
When
\gamma=1

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(z_{j}-z_{k})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{q}z_{i},

(2.1)

with
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in the configuration space
X
of exponential lattices. The latter is a subspace of the quantization of the real line

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}:=\mathbb{L}_{-}\cup\mathbb{L}_{+}\subset\mathbb{R},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{L}_{\pm}:=\xi_{\pm}q^{\mathbb{Z}}

with
\xi_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
and

The particles
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

\omega(z_{i};q)
is a weight function supported on this configuration space. The Askey-Hasbieger-Kadell’s formula (
1.7
) suggests the special weight
\omega(z;q)=z^{\alpha-1}(qz;q)
, which is the little
q
-Jacobi weight and can be taken as representative of more general
classical weights underlying the Askey scheme. For more example, one can refer to
[
45
]
for big
q
-Jacobi case. The form of (
2.1
) can be regarded as a generalised j.p.d.f of an Hermitian matrix model with unitary invariance, whose eigenvalues are supported on the exponential lattices. Therefore, it can be analysed in the framework of determinantal point process
[
10
]
.

There is another structured random matrix ensemble arising from (
1.7
).
Thus when
\gamma=2
, the j.p.d.f (
1.6
)
is extracted, and this is a
q
-generalization of the symplectic invariant ensemble as specified by (
1.1
) with
\beta=4
.
In the recent work
[
25
]
, the integrability of such
q
-symplectic ensembles with weight functions from the Askey scheme is given in terms of the correlation function, which is a rank one perturbation of the
q
-unitary ensemble, and certain examples were explicitly given.

The requirement
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
prohibits (

) relating to (
1.1
) with
\beta=1
, which is the orthogonal symmetry case.
For this we turn to Aomoto’s integral (

).
The special choice

allows us to extract the j.p.d.f (

). The particle coordinates must satisfy the inequalities

More explicitly, according to the definition of

-integral
[

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{z_{n}=0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}z_{n-1}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d_{q}z_{j}}{z_{j}}:=(1-q)^{n}\sum_{\langle\xi_{F}\rangle}f(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})

with the summation region
\langle\xi_{F}\rangle

\displaystyle t_{1}=q^{\mu_{1}},\,t_{2}/t_{1}=q^{\mu_{2}+1/2},\,\cdots,\,t_{n}/t_{n-1}=q^{\mu_{n}+1/2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mu_{1},\,\cdots,\,\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(2.2)

Therefore, for general weights, one can define the following
q
-orthogonal ensemble.

Definition 2.1
.

The discrete
q
-orthogonal ensemble with general weight can be defined by

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\omega(z_{i};q),

(2.3)

with configuration space

\displaystyle\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\,|\,a<q^{-(n-1)/2}z_{n}<\cdots<z_{1}<b\},\hskip 10.00002pta,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, for
(a,b)=(0,1]
, the

\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
obey the same relations as the
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in (
2.2
).

To display the algebraic structure for this model, we make use of a
q
-version of the de Bruijn formula
[
17
]
to write
the corresponding partition function in terms of a Pfaffian. For simplicity, we assume that the weight is supported on the interval
(a,b)=(0,1]
.
Firstly we suppose
n=2m
is even. According to the Vandermonde determinant formula

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{i}-t_{j})=(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\det(t_{i}^{j-1})_{i,j=1}^{n}.

Summing over
t_{1}
for the first row gives (where
\tilde{\omega}(x)=x\omega(x;q)
)

\displaystyle(1-q)\left[\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{\bar{\mu}_{1}}\tilde{\omega}(q^{\mu_{1}}),\,\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{\bar{\mu}_{1}}q^{\mu_{1}}\tilde{\omega}(q^{\mu_{1}}),\,\cdots,\,\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{\bar{\mu}_{1}}q^{(n-1)\mu_{1}}\tilde{\omega}(q^{\mu_{1}})\right]

with
\bar{\mu}_{1}
defined by
q^{\bar{\mu}_{1}}:=t_{2}q^{-1/2}
. If we sum over all odd numbered rows we can see the summation is over
\sum_{\hat{\mu}_{n-1}}^{\bar{\mu}_{n-1}}
with
\hat{\mu}_{n-1}
and
\bar{\mu}_{n-1}
defined by
q^{\hat{\mu}_{n-1}}=t_{n-2}{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}
and
q^{\bar{\mu}_{n-1}}=t_{n}q^{-1/2}
respectively. We observe that adding row
1
to row
3
allows the row
3
to be written as
\sum_{\mu_{3}=0}^{\bar{\mu}_{3}}
. Proceeding in this way, all odd numbered rows can be chosen to begin from
\mu=0
, and the final such row, numbered
n-1
, is then over
\sum_{\mu_{n-1}=0}^{\bar{\mu}_{n-1}}
.
The sum over the even labelled variables is parametrised by
t_{2j}={q^{\frac{1}{2}}}q^{\mu_{2j}}
with
0\leq\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{2j}
.
Moreover, the determinant is symmetric in the
\mu_{2j}
so we can sum over
0\leq\mu_{2j}<\infty
for
j=1,\cdots,n/2
provided we divide by
(n/2)!
. Therefore, as a result of de Bruijn formula
[
17
]
, these facts lead us to the Pfaffian expression

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{z_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\int_{z_{2m}=0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}z_{2m-1}}&\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2m}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{2m}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{q}z_{i}\\
&=\text{Pf}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}y}(x^{i}y^{j}-x^{j}y^{i})\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{q}xd_{q}y\right]_{i,j=0}^{2m-1}.\end{aligned}

(2.4)

In the case that
n=2m+1
is odd, similar calculations following the strategy of
[
17
]
show

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots

\displaystyle\int_{z_{2m+1}=0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}z_{2m}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2m+1}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{2m+1}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{q}z_{i}=\text{Pf}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[m_{j,k}]&[\xi_{j}]\\
-[\xi_{k}]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{j,k=0}^{2n},

where bi-moments
\{m_{j,k}\}
and single moments
\{\xi_{j}\}
are defined by

\displaystyle m_{j,k}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}y}(x^{j}y^{k}-x^{k}y^{j})\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{q}xd_{q}y,\hskip 10.00002pt\xi_{j}=\int_{0}^{1}x^{j}\omega(x;q)d_{q}x.

(2.5)

We see from the elements of the Pfaffian in both the even and odd cases that underpinning these algebraic expressions is
the particular skew-symmetric inner product

\displaystyle\langle\phi(x),\psi(y)\rangle_{1,\omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}y}\left(\phi(x)\psi(y)-\phi(y)\psi(x)\right)\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{q}xd_{q}y

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{2}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\gamma}q^{\gamma+\kappa+1/2}\left(\phi(q^{\gamma+1/2})\psi(q^{\kappa})-\phi(q^{\kappa})\psi(q^{\gamma+1/2})\right)\omega(q^{\gamma+1/2};q)\omega(q^{\kappa};q),

which can be taken as a characterization of the
q
-orthogonal ensemble.

2.1.
An alternative definition of
q
-orthogonal ensemble in symmetric configuration space

As remarked, the
q
-orthogonal ensemble induced by Aomoto’s
q
-Selberg integral formula is defined on a special configuration space, which is asymmetric and distinguished by both half integer and integer lattices.
The aim of this subsection is to construct a skew symmetric kernel
s(x,y)
, such that the configuration space is symmetric. We start with an identity relating to the iterated
q
-integral
in (
2.6
).

Proposition 2.2
.

If we take

\displaystyle s(q^{\frac{i}{2}},q^{\frac{j}{2}})=(1+{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})^{2}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}1,&\text{if $i=2m+1,j=2n$ and $m\geq n$},\\
-1,&\text{if $i=2n,j=2m+1$ and $m\geq n$},\\
0,&\text{other cases},\end{array}\right.

then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(x)\psi(y)s(x,y)\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}xd_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}y=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}y}(\phi(x)\psi(y)-\phi(y)\psi(x))\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{q}xd_{q}y.

Proof.

To prove this, we first write out the skew symmetric inner product

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(x)\psi(y)s(x,y)\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{q}xd_{q}y,\hskip 10.00002pts(x,y)=-s(y,x),

which is equivalent to the double sum expression

\displaystyle(1-q)^{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\phi(q^{i})\psi(q^{j})s(q^{i},q^{j})\omega(q^{i};q)\omega(q^{j};q)q^{i+j}.

To coincide with the skew inner product (
2.6
), we should scale
q
to
{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}
and obtain

\displaystyle(1-{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})^{2}\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\phi(q^{\frac{i}{2}})\psi(q^{\frac{j}{2}})s(q^{\frac{i}{2}},q^{\frac{j}{2}})\omega(q^{\frac{i}{2}};q)\omega(q^{\frac{j}{2}};q)q^{\frac{i+j}{2}}.

Comparing with the series form in (
2.2
), one can verify the stated identity.
∎

Use of the de Bruijn formula is now made backwards, and we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{i}y^{j}s(x,y)\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}xd_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}y\right]_{i,j=0}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\int_{[0,1]^{2n}}\text{Pf}\left[s(z_{i},z_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{2n}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z_{i}.

Therefore, an alternative j.p.d.f specifying the
q
-orthogonal ensemble is

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[s(z_{i},z_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{2n}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z_{i},

where
s(x,y)
is a skew symmetric kernel defined on
q
-lattices as (
2.2
) and the configuration space is

\displaystyle\left\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{2n})|z_{i}\in q^{\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}/2}\right\}.

We remark that the skew symmetric kernel
s(x,y)
has a similar representation in the linear discrete case as given by Borodin and Strahov in their proof of
[
10
, Lemma 6.1]
.

In a similar manner, if we define

\displaystyle F(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&x\in q^{\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}},\\
1+q^{\frac{1}{2}},&x\in q^{\mathbb{Z}},\end{array}\right.

then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}g(x)\omega(x;q)d_{q}x=\int_{0}^{1}g(x)F(x)\omega(x;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x.

Thus, by the de Bruijn formula, we have

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[m_{j,k}]&[\xi_{j}]\\
-[\xi_{k}]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{j,k=0}^{2n}=\int_{[0,1]^{2n+1}}\text{Pf}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[s(z_{i},z_{j})]&[F(z_{i})]\\
-[F(z_{j})]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}\Delta_{2n+1}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{2n+1}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z_{i},

where the moments
\{m_{j,k}\}
and
\{\xi_{j}\}
are given in (
2.5
) and
\Delta_{2n+1}(z)
is the Vandermonde product
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n+1}(z_{i}-z_{j})
.
Therefore we get the j.p.d.f in the odd case

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[s(z_{i},z_{j})]&[F(z_{i})]\\
-[F(z_{j})]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}\Delta_{2n+1}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{2n+1}\omega(z_{i};q).

These finding are summarised in the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

At the level of the partition function, we have an alternative j.p.d.f for the
q
-orthogonal ensemble, defined by

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[s(z_{i},z_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n}\Delta_{2n}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{2n}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z_{i},

for even number of particles and

\displaystyle\text{Pf}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[s(z_{i},z_{j})]&[F(z_{i})]\\
-[F(z_{j})]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}\Delta_{2n+1}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{2n+1}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}z_{i}

for odd number of particles, where
s(x,y)
and
F(x)
are defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.1
) respectively. Moreover, the configuration space is given by

\displaystyle\left\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})|z_{i}\in\{aq^{\mathbb{N}/2},\,bq^{\mathbb{N}/2}\}\right\},\hskip 10.00002pta,\,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 2.4

2.2.
q
-skew orthogonal polynomials induced from (
2.6
)

It is well known how to construct a family of skew orthogonal polynomials from a given skew inner product via the so-called skew Borel decomposition
[
2
,
16
,
25
]
. In this part, we give a brief review of this theory to illustrate how to find skew orthogonal polynomials from (
2.6
).

Let’s denote

\displaystyle m_{i,j}:=\langle x^{i},y^{j}\rangle_{1,w}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{i}y^{j}s(x,y)\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}xd_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}y.

and define the skew-symmetric moment matrix
M=[m_{i,j}]_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
.
From the skew Borel decomposition
[
2
]
, one knows that

\displaystyle M=S^{-1}JS^{-\top}.

(2.12)

Here
S
is a lower triangular matrix with diagonals
1
and
J
is a block diagonal matrix of the form

\displaystyle J=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&u_{0}&&&\\
-u_{0}&0&&&\\
&&0&u_{1}&\\
&&-u_{1}&0&\\
&&&&\ddots\end{array}\right)

with
u_{i}=\tau_{2(i+1)}/\tau_{2i}
, where
\tau_{2i}
is the Pfaffian of the
2i\times 2i
principle minor of
M
and
\tau_{0}=1
.

Therefore, by setting

\displaystyle Q(x)=(Q_{0}(x),Q_{1}(x),\cdots)^{\top}=S\chi(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\chi(x)=(1,x,x^{2},\cdots)^{\top},

one could easily demonstrate that the family of polynomials
\{Q_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the skew orthogonality relation

\displaystyle\langle Q_{2m}(x),Q_{2n}(y)\rangle_{1,\omega}=\langle Q_{2m+1}(x),Q_{2n+1}(y)\rangle_{1,\omega}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\langle Q_{2m}(x),Q_{2n+1}(y)\rangle_{1,\omega}=u_{m}\delta_{n,m}

(2.13)

with nonzero normalization factors
\{u_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
. From
[
16
, Sec.2]
, one knows that the linear system (
2.13
) leads to the Pfaffian expressions

\displaystyle Q_{2m}(z)=\frac{1}{\tau_{2m}}\text{Pf}(0,\cdots,2m,z),\hskip 10.00002pt\tau_{2m}=\text{Pf}(0,\cdots,2m-1),

\displaystyle Q_{2m+1}(z)=\frac{1}{\tau_{2m}}\text{Pf}(0,\cdots,2m-1,2m+1,z),

where the Pfaffian elements are defined by
\text{Pf}(i,j)=m_{i,j}
and
\text{Pf}(i,z)=z^{i}
. Moreover, by substituting the Pfaffian expressions into the skew orthogonality relation, one finally finds
u_{m}=\tau_{2m+2}/\tau_{2m}
.
From (
2.13
)
Q_{2m+1}(z)\mapsto Q_{2m+1}(z)+\alpha Q_{2m}(z)
is still skew orthogonal, allowing for the coefficient of
z^{2m}
to be chosen to equal 0.

According to the formula (
2.4
), we know that
\tau_{2n}
in fact is the partition function of the
q
-orthogonal ensemble considered. Therefore, if we know exactly the classical skew orthogonal polynomials from the
q
-orthogonal polynomials, then the partition function of the
q
-orthogonal ensemble with classical Askey scheme weights can be explicitly computed, which is similar to the computation of Catalan-Hankel Pfaffian in
q
-symplectic case
[
49
]
.

3.1.
Scale of

-OPs

The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{2,\rho}
in this paper is a variation of the standard one in the
q

.
For this purpose we define the
{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}
-integral (c.f.
[
32

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}\phi(x)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[bq^{\frac{n}{2}}-bq^{\frac{n+1}{2}}]\phi(bq^{\frac{n}{2}})=(1-{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}bq^{\frac{n}{2}}\phi(bq^{\frac{n}{2}}).

When

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x:=(1-{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{\frac{n}{2}}\phi(q^{\frac{n}{2}}).

Similarly, let us define the

\displaystyle(D_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\phi)(x)=\frac{\phi(x)-\phi({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x)}{(1-{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})x}.

Consider the original monic

-OPs which satisfy the orthogonality relation

\displaystyle h_{n}(q)\delta_{n,m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x;q)p_{m}(x;q)\rho(x;q)d_{q}x=(1-q)\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{n}(q^{s};q)p_{m}(q^{s};q)\rho(q^{s};q)q^{s},

(3.1)

where

If we scale

\displaystyle\langle p_{n}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),p_{m}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\rangle_{2,\rho}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})p_{m}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\rho(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x:=h_{n}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\delta_{n,m}.

(3.2)

Thus in this case, the
q
-OPs are defined in the lattices

and their expressions are scalings of
q
to
{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. For example, the monic little

-Jacobi polynomials in this case are given by

\displaystyle p_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})=(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{4}}\frac{(q^{\frac{{\alpha}+1}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n}}{(q^{\frac{{\alpha}+{\beta}+n+1}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n}}\tilde{p}_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),

where
\tilde{p}_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})
is the original little
q
-Jacobi polynomial
[
40
]

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})=\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-\frac{n}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{r}(q^{\frac{{\alpha}+{\beta}+n+1}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{r}}{({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{r}(q^{\frac{{\alpha}+1}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{r}}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x)^{r}.

The orthogonality relation then reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}p_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})p_{m}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})x^{\alpha}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{\beta}d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\delta_{n,m}q^{\frac{n(n+{\alpha})}{2}}[{\alpha}+{\beta}+2n+1]_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}^{-1}\frac{({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n+{\alpha}}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n+{\beta}}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{n+{\alpha}+{\beta}}}{({q^{\frac{1}{2}}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{2n+{\alpha}+{\beta}}^{2}}.

Moreover, the classical
q
-weight
\rho(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})
satisfies the Pearson equation
[
44
]

\displaystyle\frac{\rho({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}{\rho(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}=\frac{f(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})-(1-{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})xg(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}{f({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in q^{\mathbb{Z}/2}

(3.4)

where the degrees of

and
g
are less or equal to
2
and
1
respectively. The polynomials

(f,g)
are referred to as a Pearson pair.

3.2.

In this part, we give a brief review of

-SOPs arising from the

-symplectic invariant ensemble and scale

to

Here the weight

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})=f({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\rho({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),

according to the orthogonality.

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}=(1+{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})^{2}\mathcal{A}_{q}^{-1},

(3.12)

To this end,

Proposition 3.1
.

The inverse operator of

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\epsilon_{q}\cdot\psi)(q^{m})=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\omega}(q^{m+1/2})\cdots\tilde{\omega}(q^{k-1/2})}{\tilde{\omega}(q^{m})\cdots\tilde{\omega}(q^{k})}\psi(q^{k+1/2}),\\
&(\epsilon_{q}\cdot\psi)(q^{m+1/2})=-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{m}\frac{\tilde{\omega}(q^{k+1/2})\cdots\tilde{\omega}(q^{m-1/2})}{\tilde{\omega}(q^{k})\cdots\tilde{\omega}(q^{m})}\psi(q^{k}),\end{aligned}

Proof.

Here we provide the proof which contains the

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{q}[(\epsilon_{q}\cdot\psi)(q^{m})]=\left.[\tilde{\omega}(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)\epsilon_{q}\cdot\psi(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x)-\tilde{\omega}(x)\epsilon_{q}\cdot\psi({q^{\frac{1}{2}}}x)]\right|_{x=q^{m}}

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}=\frac{(-1)^{j+1}q^{-\frac{1}{4}j^{2}-\frac{5}{4}j-\frac{3}{2}}(aq^{\frac{1}{2}},bq^{\frac{1}{2}},cq^{\frac{1}{2}},abc^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}};q^{\frac{1}{2}})_{\infty}(abq^{\frac{1}{2}};q^{\frac{1}{2}})_{2j+2}(abq^{\frac{j+1}{2}};q^{\frac{1}{2}})_{j}}{a^{j+2}c^{j+1}(1-q)^{2}(q^{\frac{1}{2}},a^{-1}c,ac^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}},abq^{\frac{1}{2}};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})_{\infty}(q^{\frac{1}{2}},aq^{\frac{1}{2}},bq^{\frac{1}{2}},cq^{\frac{1}{2}},abc^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}};q^{\frac{1}{2}})_{j}}.

From this data the skew orthogonal system can be specified.

Proposition 4.5
.

The skew big

-Jacobi orthogonal polynomials

, which are skew orthogonal under skew inner product defined by (

are connected with the big q-Jacobi polynomials

\displaystyle=J_{2n}^{(a,b,c)}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle Q_{2n+1}^{(a,b,c)}(x;q)

\displaystyle=J_{2n+1}^{(a,b,c)}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})

Furthermore

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}=c{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}^{a{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\cdots\int_{z_{2n}=c{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}^{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}z_{2n-1}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq 2n}(z_{k}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{2n}\frac{\left(a^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{i},c^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}}z_{i};q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(z_{i},bc^{-1}z_{i};q\right)_{\infty}}d_{q}z_{i}

5.
Correlation kernels for
q
-orthogonal ensembles

In this section, attention is paid to the
k
-point correlation function for the
q
-orthogonal ensemble in Prop.
2.3

\displaystyle\rho_{N,k}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}):=\frac{1}{(N-k)!\tau_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{C}^{N-k}}\text{Pf}[X]\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=k+1}^{N}\omega(z_{i};q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z_{i},

(5.1)

where

\displaystyle X=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[s(z_{i},z_{j})]_{i,j=1}^{2n},&N=2n,\\
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}[s(z_{i},z_{j})]&[F(z_{i})]\\
-[F(z_{j})]^{\top}&0\end{array}\right]_{i,j=1}^{2n+1},&N=2n+1,\end{array}\right.

s(x,y)
and
F(x)
are functions defined by (
2.2
) and (
2.1
),
\tau_{N}
is the partition function of the corresponding
q
-orthogonal ensemble and
\mathcal{C}
is the corresponding support contour.
Due to the fact that the correlation function have different formula depending on the parity of
N
, we treat them separately. For more details about Pfaffian point process, one can refer to
[
11
,
15
,
47
]
.

5.1.
N=2n
even

In the earlier work
[
29
]
, it was shown that the correlation function admitting the form (
5.1
) for
N=2n
can be written as a Pfaffian point process by assuming that
the single moments

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}x^{i}\omega(x;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x

and bi-moments

\displaystyle m_{i,j}=\int_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}s(x,y)x^{i}y^{j}\omega(x;q)\omega(y;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}xd_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}y

are well-defined and the corresponding skew-symmetric moment matrix is invertible, i.e. the partition function is nonzero.
With these conditions assumed, we can make the Pfaffian point process explicit by specifying the kernel for the
correlation function.

Proposition 5.1
.

The correlation function
\rho_{2n,k}
in (
5.1
) can be expressed as a Pfaffian according to

\displaystyle\rho_{2n,k}=\text{Pf}\left[\tilde{K}_{2n}(z_{i},z_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{k}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\omega(z_{l};q),

(5.2)

where
\tilde{K}_{2n}(x,y)
is the skew symmetric
2\times 2
kernel independent of
n
,

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{2n}(x,y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{2n}(x,y)&J_{2n}(y,x)\\
-J_{2n}(x,y)&-I_{2n}(x,y)\end{array}\right).

With
\{Q_{l}(x;q)\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
denoted the monic skew orthogonal polynomials with respect to the skew inner product (
2.6
) under the weight
\omega(z;q)
,
K_{2n}(x,y)
,
J_{2n}(x,y)
and
I_{2n}(x,y)
are skew symmetric kernels defined by

\displaystyle K_{2n}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{u_{i}}(Q_{2i}(x;q)Q_{2i+1}(y;q)-Q_{2i}(y;q)Q_{2i+1}(x;q)),

\displaystyle J_{2n}(x,y)=s_{x}K_{2n}(x,y),\hskip 10.00002ptI_{2n}(x,y)=s(x,y)-s_{x}s_{y}K_{2n}(x,y).

The operator
s_{x}
represents the action

\displaystyle s_{x}f(x)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}s(z,x)f(z)\omega(z;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}z.

(5.3)

Proof.

Let
M
be the moment matrix
(m_{i,j})_{i,j=0}^{2n-1}
. It is known from
[
47
, Thm. 1.1]

that the correlation function admits the form
\rho_{2n,k}=\text{Pf}(\tilde{K}_{2n}(z_{i},z_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{k}
, where with
s^{i}(x)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}y^{i}s(x,y)\omega(y;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}y

the kernel
\tilde{K}
is expressed in terms of the skew symmetric moments according to

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{2n}(x,y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq 2n-1}x^{i}M_{i,j}^{-\top}y^{j}&\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq 2n-1}x^{i}M_{i,j}^{-\top}s^{j}(y)\\
\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq 2n-1}s^{i}(x)M_{i,j}^{-\top}y^{j}&-s(x,y)+\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq 2n-1}s^{i}(x)M_{i,j}^{-\top}s^{j}(y)\end{array}\right).

By making use of the skew Borel decomposition (
2.12
), one knows that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq 2n-1}

\displaystyle x^{i}M_{i,j}^{-\top}y^{j}=\chi^{\top}(x)(S^{-1}JS^{-\top})^{-\top}\chi(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{u_{i}}(Q_{2i}(x;q)Q_{2i+1}(y;q)-Q_{2i}(y;q)Q_{2i+1}(x;q))=K_{2n}(x,y).

Moreover, by realizing that the operators
s_{x}
and
s_{y}
only act on the monomials, which doesn’t make an influence on the structure of skew orthogonal polynomials, the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We use the terminology
skew
K
-Christoffel-Darboux kernel for the element
K_{2n}(x,y)
of
\tilde{K}_{2n}(x,y)
.
It is a reproducing kernel as it satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}^{2}}K_{2n}(x,y)K_{2n}(w,z)s(y,w)\omega(y;q)\omega(w;q)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}yd_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}w=K_{2n}(x,z),

as can be checked by making use of the skew orthogonality. Similarly the element
J_{2n}(x,y)
of
\tilde{K}_{2n}(x,y)
is to be
referred to as the skew
J
-Christoffel-Darboux kernel. According to (
5.2
), the latter specified the density according to

\rho_{2n,k}(x)=\omega(x;q)J_{2n}(x,x)
.

In the subsequent working, we plan to show that for the classical weights
\rho(x;q)
, the aforementioned skew
J
-Christoffel-Darboux kernel
J_{2n}(x,y)
is in fact a rank-one perturbation of the classical Christoffel-Darboux kernel

\displaystyle S_{2n}(x,y):

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2n-1}\frac{p_{l}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})p_{l}(y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}{\langle p_{l},p_{l}\rangle_{2,\rho}},

where
\{p_{l}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
are orthogonal polynomials. Note also that polynomials
\{p_{k}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\}
and
\{Q_{k}(x;q)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are two basis in the Hilbert space and so we have the transition relation

\displaystyle p_{n}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\alpha_{n,k}Q_{k}(x;q),

(5.4)

where the coefficients are given in (
3.24
). The latter can be used to specify the action of the operator
\mathcal{B}_{q}
on the
q
-skew orthogonal polynomials.

Proposition 5.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}Q_{2i}(x;q)=u_{i}\sum_{k=2i+1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2i+1}}{h_{k}},

(5.5a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}Q_{2i+1}(x;q)=-u_{i}\sum_{k=2i}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2i}}{h_{k}},

(5.5b)

where
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are the normalization factors for the
q
-skew orthogonal polynomials
\{Q_{k}(x;q)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
q
-orthogonal polynomials
\{p_{k}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
respectively, and
\{\alpha_{n,k}\}_{k\leq n}
are the transition coefficients.

Proof.

To prove this proposition, we first need to introduce a delta function
\delta(x,y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}):\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\delta(x,y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{p_{n}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})p_{n}(y;q^{\frac{1}{2}})}{h_{n}({q^{\frac{1}{2}}})},

and it admits the following reproducing property

\displaystyle\langle\delta(x,y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),\xi(y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\rangle_{2,\rho}=\xi(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\xi(x,{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\in\mathcal{H}.

(5.6)

By the relation (
3.15
) and (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}Q_{2i}(x;q)=\langle\delta(x,y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),\mathcal{B}_{q}Q_{2i}(y;q)\rangle_{2,\rho}=-\langle\delta(x,y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),Q_{2i}(y;q)\rangle_{1,w}.

Substituting the expansion (
5.4
) in the definition of
\delta(x,y,{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})
and using the skew orthogonality (
2.13
), we get the desired results for the first equation. The second equation is proved similarly.
∎

Now we are well prepared to relate
J_{2n}
to the classical Christoffel-Darboux kernel.

Proposition 5.4
.

For the classical weights, the skew
J
-Christoffel-Darboux kernel can be expressed as a rank-one perturbation of the classical Christoffel-Darboux kernel by

\displaystyle J_{2n}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}{\omega(x;q)}S_{2n-1}(x,y)+\gamma_{2n-2}\left(s_{x}p_{2n-2}(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})\right)p_{2n-1}(y;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}),

(5.7)

where
\gamma_{2n-2}
is defined by (
3.4
) and
s_{x}
is the operator defined by (
5.3
).

Proof.

We first show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}\phi(x)=\frac{\omega(x;q)}{\rho(x;{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}s_{x}\phi(x)

(5.8)

holds true for an arbitrary
q
-summable function
\phi(x)
. Since the configuration space is on the half-integer and integer
q
-lattices, we discuss the equation separately.
By the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{q}
, we can rewrite
\mathcal{B}_{q}\phi(q^{m+1/2})
in an integration form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}\phi(q^{m+1/2})

\displaystyle=-(1+{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})(1-q)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{m}\frac{\omega(q^{k};q)\omega(q^{k+1/2};q)\dots\omega(q^{m+1/2};q)}{\omega(q^{k+1/2};q)\omega(q^{k+1};q)\dots\omega(q^{m};q)\rho(q^{m+1/2};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}q^{k}\phi(q^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(q^{m+1/2};q)}{\rho(q^{m+1/2};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}\int_{q^{m}}^{\infty}s(x,q^{m+1/2})\omega(x;q)\phi(x)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(q^{m+1/2};q)}{\rho(q^{m+1/2};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}s(x,q^{m+1/2})\omega(x;q)\phi(x)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}\phi(q^{m})=\frac{\omega(q^{m};q)}{\rho(q^{m};{q^{\frac{1}{2}}})}\int_{0}^{\infty}s(x,q^{m})\omega(x;q)\phi(x)d_{{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}}x,

and thus the equation (
5.8
) holds.
Therefore, by acting
\mathcal{B}_{q,x}
on
K_{2n}(x,y)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q,x}K_{2n}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{u_{i}}(\mathcal{B}_{q,x}Q_{2i}(x;q)Q_{2i+1}(y;q)-Q_{2i}(y;q)\mathcal{B}_{q,x}Q_{2i+1}(x;q)),

from which we can insert formulae (
5.5a
) and (
5.5b
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q,x}K_{2n}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(Q_{2i}(y;q)\sum_{k=2i}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2i}}{h_{k}}+Q_{2i+1}(y;q)\sum_{k=2i+1}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2i+1}}{h_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,i}Q_{i}(y;q)}{h_{k}}+\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,i}Q_{i}(y;q)}{h_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{2n}(x,y)+\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,i}Q_{i}(y;q)}{h_{k}}.

To evaluate the sum, by making use of the property of the transition coefficients

\displaystyle\alpha_{k,i}=\alpha_{k,2n-1}\alpha_{2n-1,i},\hskip 10.00002ptk\geq 2n,

we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,i}Q_{i}(y;q)}{h_{k}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2n-1}\alpha_{2n-1,i}Q_{i}(y;q)}{h_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\frac{p_{k}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})\alpha_{k,2n-1}}{h_{k}}p_{2n-1}(y;q^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}_{q}Q_{2n-2}(x;q)p_{2n-1}(y;q^{\frac{1}{2}})}{u_{n-1}}-\frac{p_{2n-1}(x;q^{\frac{1}{2}})p_{2n-1}(y;q^{\frac{1}{2}})}{h_{2n-1}}.

In view of the definition of
S_{2n}(x,y)
, substracting the last term from

gives

. The proposition is then proved by noting that

and making use of (
5.8
).

∎

Proposition 5.6

By performing elementary row operations in the Vandermonde determinant, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{1}^{odd}=[J^{odd}_{2n+1}(z_{j},z_{k})\omega(z_{k};q)]_{j,k=1}^{2n+1},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{I}_{1}^{odd}=[I_{2n+1}^{odd}(z_{j},z_{k})]_{j,k=1}^{2n+1}

6.
Further remarks

In this paper, we mainly consider the skew orthogonal polynomials and correlation kernels related to the discrete
q
-orthogonal ensemble, compared with the previous work for discrete
q
-symplectic ensemble
[
25
]
. The orthogonal ensemble is more difficult than the symplectic ensemble to analyze, especially for the configuration space supported on the exponential lattices. We make use of the duality relation between orthogonal ensemble and symplectic ensemble, by which a rank one perturbation of the correlation kernel for
q
-orthogonal ensemble is obtained.

There are some interesting problems to be continued. One is to find a proper combinatorial model such as enumeration of boxed plane partitions for the corresponding
q
-orthogonal ensemble with certain classical weights. Classical orthogonal polynomials and skew orthogonal polynomials theories will be applied to analyze the corresponding correlation kernels and limiting behaviors will be obtained. Another is about the model of discrete orthogonal ensemble on the linear lattice, motivated by the discrete Selberg integral. For example, in the work
[
14
]
, the authors considered a discrete analog of Macdonald-Mehta integral

\displaystyle S_{r,n}({\alpha},\gamma,\delta)=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}=-n}^{n}|\Delta(k^{\alpha})|^{2\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{r}|k_{i}|^{\delta}{2n\choose{n+k_{i}}}

and in Section 5, they gave many evaluations when
\gamma=1/2
. It seems that the discrete orthogonal ensemble can be embedded into the symmetric configuration space and it needs further investigation.

Appendix A

In order to prove Proposition
5.5
, we need to perform a calculation involving quaternion determinants. We start with the following definition of quaternions.

Definition A.1
.

Quaternions are an algebra with elements of the form

\displaystyle a_{0}\mathbf{1}+a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{3}\mathbf{e}_{3},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{2}^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{3}^{2}=-1,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{2}\mathbf{e}_{3}=-1,

where
a_{0},\dots,a_{3}
are scalars taking real or complex values.

The quaternions can be represented by
2\times 2
complex matrices where the basis elements
\mathbf{1},\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3}
are realized by Borel matrices

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{1}:=i\sigma_{z}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}i&0\\
0&-i\end{array}\right]\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{2}:=i\sigma_{y}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right]\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{e}_{3}:=i\sigma_{x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}0&i\\
i&0\end{array}\right].

The dual of a quaternion
q=a_{0}\mathbf{1}+a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{3}\mathbf{e}_{3}
is defined by

\displaystyle\bar{q}=a_{0}\mathbf{1}-a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}-a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}-a_{3}\mathbf{e}_{3},

and
|q|^{2}=q\bar{q}=a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}
gives the module of
q
. For example, for a quaternion
q
with representation

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}u&v\\
w&z\end{bmatrix},

the dual element
\bar{q}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}z&-v\\
-w&u\end{bmatrix}.

For a matrix
Q=[q_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{N}
with quaternion elements, we define its dual as the matrix
Q^{D}:=[\bar{q}_{j,i}]_{i,j=1}^{N}
and we use
\hat{Q}
to denote the
2N\times 2N
matrix with complex elements obtained by replacing each element
q_{i,j}
of
Q
with its
2\times 2
matrix representation.

Moreover, the quaternion determinant for a self-dual quaternion matrix
Q
is defined as follows

Definition A.2
.

The determinant qdet of an
N\times N
self-dual quaternion matrix
Q
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{qdet}Q=\sum_{P\in S_{N}}(-1)^{N-l}\prod_{1}^{l}\left(q_{ab}q_{bc}\cdots q_{da}\right)^{(0)}.

The superscript
(0)
means that we take scalar part of the product which is equavalent to
\frac{1}{2}Tr
when we compute with matrix representations of quaternions. P is any permutation of the indices

(1,\dots,N)
consisting of
l
disjoint cycles of the form
a\rightarrow b\rightarrow c\dots\rightarrow d\rightarrow a
and
(-1)^{N-1}
is equal to the
parity of
P
.

Determinants of self-dual quaternion matrices are in close relation with Pfaffians. If we define
\mathbb{Z}_{2N}
as the
2N\times 2N
antisymmetric matrix with
2\times 2
blocks

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right]

along the main diagonal and zeros elsewhere, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2N}=\oplus_{i=1}^{N}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right]

then it can be checked that a
N\times N
quaternion matrix
Q
is self-dual if and only if
\hat{Q}\mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{-1}
is antisymmetric. Moreover, we have the following.

Proposition A.3
.

For an
N\times N
self-dual quaternion matrix
Q

\displaystyle\operatorname{qdet}Q=\text{Pf}(\hat{Q}\mathbb{Z}_{2N}^{-1}).

(A.1)

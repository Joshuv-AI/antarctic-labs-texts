Finite-time and fixed-time consensus control of multi-agent systems driven by parabolic partial differential equations Thanks: This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Nos.11671282 and 12171339).
Xu-hui Wang, Xue-song Li and Nan-jing Huang

Abstract




Abstract
.
This paper focuses on the study of the finite-time consensus (FTC) and fixed-time consensus (FXC) issues of multi-agent systems (MASs) driven by parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs). Compared with the study in the existing literature, the topic of FTC and FXC control is first embodied in MASs driven by parabolic PDEs. Based on the Lyapunov theorems, the FTC and FXC controllers are devised to ensure that the MASs converge to a stable state with external disturbance. Furthermore, we simplify the controllers to guarantee the FTC and FXC of MASs without external disturbance. Finally, two illustrative examples are given to verify the feasibility of controllers.

Keywords:
Multi-agent systems; finite-time consensus; fixed-time consensus; parabolic partial differential equations; external disturbance.

AMS Subject Classification:
34A08; 34D05.

Introduction

In recent decades, multi-agent systems (MASs) have aroused the extensive interest of researchers in many areas, especially in computer science, neural network, and cooperative control. Compared with the traditional asymptotical consensus control theory, the FTC and FXC control methods can achieve higher control accuracy, faster convergence speed, and better control effect. It is worth to be noted that most of the current research results on the FTC and FXC are obtained under the assumption that dynamical systems of agents are driven by ordinary differential equations (ODEs). However, in many cases, the dynamics system of agents is not only related to time but also affected by spatial location, such as the spread of infectious diseases, the vehicle formation, the traffic management of large-scale UAV systems, the urban traffic networks, the noise control, the temperature control in industrial production, and so on, see

[

[
50

investigated the output synchronization problem for parabolic partial differential systems and designed the feedback controller according to output information to ensure the synchronization. Very recently, Wang et al.
[
51
]
considered a special kind of first-order hyperbolic systems and designed the boundary protocol to reach the FTC. Nevertheless, in some practical cases, it is necessary to consider MASs based on parabolic PDEs with external disturbance having the following form:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}y_{i,t}(x,t)=ky_{i,xx}(x,t)+u_{i}(x,t)+d_{i}(x,t),\\
\end{array}

where
y_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the state variable of the
i
th agent,
u_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the protocol of the
i
th agent,

A food web can be described by a complex network model in which nodes represent each species.

In limited habitats, the distribution of species is usually uneven, with different spatial densities of species leading to corresponding different migration. The spatial distribution of species can be described as state variables of nodes in parabolic partial differential systems, which depends on both spatial and temporal variables. In particular, a simple food web network model can be obtained from
[
52
]

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}w_{i,t}(x,t)=kw_{i,xx}(x,t)+u_{i}(x,t),\\
\end{array}

where
w_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the spatial density of the
i
th species,
u_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the control input of the
i
th species, and
k>0
is the diffusion coefficient. Clearly, MASs (
1
) is a special case of MASs (
1
) with
d_{i}(x,t)\equiv 0
. It should be noted that the state of nodes in MASs may not be accurately measured in general since they are often destroyed by various disturbances and noise. Inspired by this fact and taking into account the external disturbance, we consider MASs (
1

The abovementioned observations show that it is very important for agents to complete all planned tasks in a limited time in engineering applications. On the other hand, there is a lack of unified results addressing the FTC and FXC issues for MASs described by PDEs. Thus, how to design the controllers to solve the FTC and FXC issues for PDE systems is still an open problem, which motivates this study.

The overarching goal of this paper is to investigate the FTC and FXC issues for MASs described by parabolic PDEs with external disturbance. Compared with the study in the existing literature, the main contributions of this paper can be listed as follows.

(1) A new model of MASs driven by parabolic PDEs with external disturbance is proposed, which can be used to describe the heat process utilized in science, engineering and practical experience in chemical and biochemical engineering. It is the first time that the FTC and FXC distributed controllers are designed to satisfy different control effects in MASs with external disturbance.

(2) Compared with other works on FTC and FXC control for MASs with ODEs, the main difficulties in studying MASs with PDEs are to construct the Lyapunov function, to estimate the state variables, and to design the controllers. In order to overcome such difficulties, we first construct the Lyapunov function in the integral form to avoid the estimation of multivariate Lyapunov functions. Because the state variables are correlated with
x
and
t
, it is inevitable to estimate the state variables’ first and second partial derivatives with respect to
x
. We then use the integration by parts method and Wirtinger’s inequality to transform the estimations of the first and second partial derivatives of the state variables into the ones of the state variables, which can be estimated via some known inequalities in
[
6
,
21
,
53
,
54
]
. Finally, we design the controllers using the Lyapunov method for PDEs
[
19
,
26
]
.

(3) Different from the research on consensus control for MASs driven by PDEs, the control objective considered in this paper is to achieve consensus in finite time or fixed time, which is more difficult to use the information of neighboring agents to improve the convergence speed. A systematic approach is first proposed to accelerate the convergence speed and improve the control effect by adding a new control coefficient to each controller.

The remainder of this work is structured as follows. The next section recalls some critical lemmas, and formulates the FTC and FXC problem. After that in Section III, we analyze the FTC and FXC of MASs with external disturbance via the controllers. Before summarizing the results in Section V, we provide two simulation examples to demonstrate the feasibility of controllers in Section IV.

2.1
Graph theory

In MASs,
G=\{\mathfrak{V},\mathfrak{E},A\}
represent a fixed communication topology graph, where
\mathfrak{V}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
means the vertex set of the graph
G
with
n
nodes,
\mathfrak{E}\in\mathfrak{V}\times\mathfrak{V}
is an edge set, and
A=(a_{ij})
is the adjacency matrix of the graph
G
. If the node
v_{i}
can receive the data from
v_{j}
, then
a_{ij}>0
, else
a_{ij}=0
. Thus, we can define
N_{i}=\{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{V}:(v_{j},v_{i})\in\mathfrak{E}\}
. The Laplace’s matrix
\mathcal{L}
of the graph
G
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(A)=\left(l_{ij}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-a_{ij},&v_{j}\in N_{i};\\
\sum_{v_{j}\in N_{i}}a_{ij},&j=i;\\
0,&other.\end{array}\right.

Definition 2.1
.

An undirected graph is said to be connected if there exists a sequence
S=\{a_{ii_{1}},a_{i_{1}i_{2}},\cdots,a_{i_{m}j}\}
such that each element of
S
is positive for any pair of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A directed graph is said to be

(i)

strongly connected (s-con) if there exists a sequence
S=\{a_{ii_{1}},a_{i_{1}i_{2}},\cdots,a_{i_{m}j}\}
such that each element of
S
is positive for any pair of
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
;

(ii)

detail-balanced (d-bal) if there exists a vector
\omega=[\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots,\omega_{N}]^{T}
such that
\omega_{i}>0
and
\omega_{i}a_{ij}=\omega_{j}a_{ji}
for
i,j=1,2,\cdots,N
.

Definition 2.3
.

If
A^{T}=A
, then
R(x;A)=\frac{\langle x,Ax\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}
is said to be the Rayleigh quotient of

, where
x\neq 0
.

2.3
Problem Formulation

In this work, we will consider the FTC and FXC problems of parabolic partial differential MASs with external disturbance. Assume that MASs including
N
agents have the following form:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}y_{i,t}(x,t)=ky_{i,xx}(x,t)+u_{i}(x,t)+d_{i}(x,t),\\
y_{i}(0,t)=y_{i}(L,t)=0,\\
y_{i}(x,0)=y_{i}^{0}(x),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N,\end{array}\right.

where
x\in[0,L]
is the location,
t\in\{0\}\bigcup\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the elapsed time,
y_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the state variable of the
i
th agent,
y_{i}^{0}(\cdot)
is the initial condition of the
i
th agent such that
y_{i}^{0}(\cdot)
is bounded and continuous with respect to
x
,
u_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the protocol of the
i
th agent,
d_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the unknown external disturbance, and
k>0
is a constant.

Assume that
d_{i}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

d_{i}(x,t)
is continuous with respect to
x
and
t
;

(ii)

|d_{i}(x,t)|\leq d_{\max}
for any given
x\in[0,L]
,
t\in\left[0,+\infty\right)
and
i=1,2,\cdots,N
, where the upper bound
d_{\max}
is a known constant.

Definition 2.4
.

MASs (
2.3
) achieves the FTC if there exists a finite time
t^{*}
for any given
x\in[0,L]
and
i,j=1,2,\cdots,N
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t^{*}}|y_{i}(x,t)-y_{j}(x,t)|=0.

Moreover, MASs (
2.3
) achieves the FXC if the finite time
t^{*}<T_{\max}
, where
T_{\max}
is a positive constant and independent of the initial condition.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth mentioning that the control objectives of MASs (
2.3
) are to design the corresponding controllers to achieve the FTC or the FXC.

For the sake of simplicity, let’s note
\xi_{ij}(x,t)=y_{i}(x,t)-y_{j}(x,t)
.
To achieve the FTC, we design the controller
u_{i}(x,t)
via state values by setting

\displaystyle u_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t)}_{(I)}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\bar{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}sign(\xi_{ij}(x,t))}_{(II)},

where
0<\alpha<1
,
a_{ij}\geq 0
and
\bar{d}>0
are given constants.

Remark 2.2
.

In the controller (
2.15
), Part (I) ensure the FTC and the role of Part (II) is to counteract the influence of external disturbance.

To reach the FXC, we design the controller
u_{i}(x,t)
via state values by setting

\displaystyle u_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t)}_{(I)}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t)}_{(II)}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\bar{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}sign(\xi_{ij}(x,t))}_{(III)},

where
0<\alpha<1
,
\beta>1
,
a_{ij}\geq 0
and
\bar{d}>0
are given constants.

Remark 2.3
.

Compared with the controller (
2.15
), Part (II) of controller (
2.16
) is to make the finite terminal time independent of the initial condition.

Remark 2.4
.

Noting that the FXC problem can be considered as an evolution of the FTC problem. In fact, the FTC and the FXC controllers ensure the systems to achieve consensus in finite time, but they have the different convergence rates and the different estimations for the finite terminal time.

Obviously, if
d_{i}(x,t)\equiv 0
, then MASs (
2.3
) is reduced to the following form:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}y_{i,t}(x,t)=ky_{i,xx}(x,t)+u_{i}(x,t),\\
y_{i}(0,t)=y_{i}(L,t)=0,\\
y_{i}(x,0)=y_{i}^{0}(x),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N.\end{array}\right.

In other words, MASs (
2.3
) is a special case of MASs (
2.3
).

To achieve the FTC, we design the controller
u_{i}(x,t)
via state values by setting

\displaystyle u_{i}(x,t)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t),

(2.21)

where
0<\alpha<1
and
a_{ij}\geq 0
are constants.

Similarly, to achieve the FXC, we design the controller
u_{i}(x,t)
via state values by setting

\displaystyle u_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t)

(2.22)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}(x,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta-1}\xi_{ij}(x,t),

where
0<\alpha<1
,
\beta>1
and
a_{ij}\geq 0
are constants.

Remark 2.5
.

Since MASs (
2.3
) is a special case of MASs (
2.3
) with
d_{i}(x,t)\equiv 0
, we can simplify the controller (
2.15
) as the controller (
2.21
) to reach FTC via omitting the Part (II). Similarly, we can obtain the fixed-time controller (
2.22
) of MASs (
2.3
).

2.4

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that graph

Proof
For convenience, the variables

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}y_{t}=y_{xx}+d,\\
y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0,\\
y(x,0)=y^{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
y=[y_{1},\cdots,y_{N}]^{T}
,
d=[d_{1},\cdots,d_{N}]^{T}
, and
y^{0}(x)=[y_{1}^{0}(x),\cdots,y_{N}^{0}(x)]^{T}
. In fact, since
y^{0}(x)
and
d
are continuous, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that the system (
2.4
) has a unique solution.

Next we show that the following system (
2.4
) has a unique solution

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}y_{t}-ky_{xx}=u(y),\\
y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0,\\
y(x,0)=y^{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
y=[y_{1},\cdots,y_{N}]^{T}
and
y^{0}(x)=[y_{1}^{0}(x),\cdots,y_{N}^{0}(x)]^{T}
. Indeed, since
y^{0}(x)
is bounded and continuous with respect to
x
, we know that
y^{0}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}([0,L],\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Noting that the controllers (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) include sign functions, we can set the signal switching times to be
t_{m}
with
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}<\cdots
. Thus, it follows that
u(\cdot)
is continuously differentiable with respect to
y
on
[t_{m},t_{m+1})
. From Lemma
2.7
, there exists a constant
T_{M,m}>0
such that the system (
2.4
) has a unique solution on
[0,L]\times[t_{m},\min\{t_{m+1},T_{M,m}\})
. Construct a Lyapunov generalized energy function

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i})^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Then it is easy to see that the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
with
t
can be given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{V}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}y_{i,t}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}y_{i,xx}\mathrm{d}x+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}u_{i}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}\bar{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}sign(\xi_{ij})\mathrm{d}x-2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}(y_{i,x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bar{d}a_{ij}\left(\xi_{ij}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}kL^{-2}\pi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}(y_{i})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

Thus, one has
\mathbb{V}(t)\leq\mathbb{V}(0)<\infty
. According to Lemma
2.7
, we have
T_{M,m}=\infty
for
m=0,1,2,\cdots
and so the system (
2.4
) has a unique solution on
[0,L]\times[t_{m},t_{m+1})
for
m=0,1,2,\cdots
, which means that the system (
2.4
) has a unique solution in the whole domain.

Thus, by the unique solvability of systems (
2.4
) and (
2.4
), we know that MASs (
2.3
) with the controller (
2.15
) (or (
2.16
)) has a unique solution. This completes the proof.

) (or (

3
Main results

Based on the controllers (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), the sufficient conditions for the FTC and FXC of MASs (
2.3
) are obtained. For convenience, the variables
(x,t)
are omitted in the following proof.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that graph
G
is undirected and connected. Then the controller (
2.15
) can ensure MASs (
2.3
) to achieve the FTC providing
\bar{d}\geq\frac{2d_{\max}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))}
with
E=(a_{ij}^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
and the finite terminal time
t^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq\frac{\left[\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(y_{i}^{0}(x)-y_{j}^{0}(x))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{1-\alpha}}{C_{1}(1-\alpha)},

where
C_{1}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))\right)^{\alpha}
with
B=(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
.

Proof
Design an auxiliary variable
y^{*}
as follows

\displaystyle y^{*}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}.

Obviously, the dynamic equation of
y^{*}(x,t)
has the following form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}y^{*}_{t}=ky^{*}_{xx}+u^{*}+d^{*},\\
y^{*}(0,t)=y^{*}(L,t)=0,\\
y^{*}(x,0)=y^{*,0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
u^{*}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}
is the average protocol,
d^{*}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}
is the average disturbance and
y^{*,0}(x)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}y^{0}_{i}(x)
is the average initial condition. Denote
\delta=[\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\cdots,\delta_{N}]^{T}
with
\delta_{i}=y_{i}-y^{*}
. Then
\langle\delta,\textbf{1}\rangle=0
.

Now we need to prove that
y_{i}
achieve the consensus in finite time, which is equivalent to that
y_{i}
converges to
y^{*}
in finite time for
i=1,2,\cdots,N
. To this end, we construct a Lyapunov generalized energy function

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-y^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

which is equivalent to the following form

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Clearly, the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
with
t
can be given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{V}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}(y_{i,t}-y^{*}_{t})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(y_{i,xx}-y^{*}_{xx})\mathrm{d}x}_{(I)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(d_{i}-d^{*})\mathrm{d}x}_{(II)}+\underbrace{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(u_{i}-u^{*})\mathrm{d}x}_{(III)}.

To estimate the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
, we need to estimate Part (I), Part (II) and Part (III), respectively. Using Lemma
2.5
to estimate Part (I), one has

\displaystyle 2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(y_{i,xx}-y^{*}_{xx})\mathrm{d}x

(3.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}(y_{i,x}-y^{*}_{x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}kL^{-2}\pi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

To estimate Part (II), it is easy to have

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(d_{i}-d^{*})\mathrm{d}x

(3.33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}d_{i}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2d_{\max}\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\delta_{i}|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2d_{\max}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

Since
G
is undirected, we obtain that
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
. Employing (
2.15
) and Lemma
2.3
to estimate Part (III), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(u_{i}-u^{*})\mathrm{d}x

(3.34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}\mathrm{d}x-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}y_{i}\bar{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}sign(\xi_{ij})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\xi_{ij}^{2}dx\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bar{d}a_{ij}\xi_{ij}sign(\xi_{ij})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bar{d}a_{ij}|\xi_{ij}|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\bar{d}a_{ij}\left(\xi_{ij}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\bar{d}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a^{2}_{ij}\xi_{ij}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\bar{d}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a^{2}_{ij}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x.

Define
B=(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
and
E=(a_{ij}^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. According to Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\delta_{j}-\delta_{i})^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}

(3.35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\langle\delta,\mathcal{L}(B)\delta\rangle}{\langle\delta,\delta\rangle}\geq 2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))

and

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{2}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{2}(\delta_{j}-\delta_{i})^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}}

(3.36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\langle\delta,\mathcal{L}(E)\delta\rangle}{\langle\delta,\delta\rangle}\geq 2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E)).

Substituting (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) into (
3.34
), one has

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}(u_{i}-u^{*})\mathrm{d}x

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\bar{d}\int_{0}^{L}\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B)))^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}-\sqrt{2}\bar{d}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))\int_{0}^{L}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(V(t))^{\alpha}-\sqrt{2}\bar{d}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))\int_{0}^{L}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

where
C_{1}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))\right)^{\alpha}
. Since
\bar{d}\geq\frac{2d_{\max}N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))}
, it follows from (
3.32
), (
3.33
) and (
3.37
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{V}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}kL^{-2}\pi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\left(2d_{\max}N^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sqrt{2}\bar{d}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}(\mathcal{L}(E))\right)\int_{0}^{L}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}.

Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
and the undirected graph
G
is connected, we know that
C_{1}>0
. Thus, Lemma
2.1
shows that MASs (
2.3
) achieves the FTC in
t^{*}
with

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq\frac{\mathbb{V}(0)^{1-\alpha}}{C_{1}(1-\alpha)}=\frac{\left[\int_{0}^{L}\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(y_{i}^{0}(x)-y_{j}^{0}(x))^{2}dx\right]^{1-\alpha}}{C_{1}(1-\alpha)}.

The proof is completed.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that graph

is undirected and connected. Then the controller (
2.16
) can ensure MASs (

) to achieve the FXC providing

with

and the finite terminal time

satisfies

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq T_{\max}:=\frac{1}{C_{1}(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{(N^{1-\beta})^{2}C_{2}(\beta-1)},

where

\displaystyle C_{1}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))\right)^{\alpha},\quad C_{2}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(P))\right)^{\beta}

with
B=(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
and

.

Proof
Define a Lyapunov generalized energy function for MASs (
2.3
) by setting

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Similar to the discussion in Theorem
3.1
, the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
with
t
can be given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{V}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}(y_{i,t}-y^{*}_{t})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-(N^{1-\beta})^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{1}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-(N^{1-\beta})^{2}C_{2}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\beta},

where

\displaystyle C_{1}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(B))\right)^{\alpha},\quad C_{2}=\left(2\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{L}(P))\right)^{\beta}

with
B=(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
and
P=(a_{ij}^{\frac{1}{\beta}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. Thus, Lemma
2.2
shows that MASs (
2.3
) achieves the FXC in
t^{*}
with

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq T_{\max}:=\frac{1}{C_{1}(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{(N^{1-\beta})^{2}C_{2}(\beta-1)}.

This completes the proof.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that graph
G
is directed, s-con and d-bal. Then the controller (
2.21
) can ensure MASs (
2.3
) to achieve the FTC and the finite terminal time
t^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq\frac{\left[\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}(y_{i}^{0}(x)-\hat{y}^{0}(x))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{1-\alpha}}{C_{3}(1-\alpha)},

where

\displaystyle\hat{y}^{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}y_{i}^{0}(x)

and

\displaystyle C_{3}=\left(\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D))\right)^{\alpha}

with
D=((\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
.

Proof
Since graph
G
is d-bal, there exists a vector
\omega=[\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots,\omega_{N}]^{T}
with
\omega_{i}>0
satisfying
\omega_{i}a_{ij}=\omega_{j}a_{ji}(i,j=1,\cdots,N)
. Design an auxiliary variable
\hat{y}
as follows

\displaystyle\hat{y}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}y_{i}.

Obviously, the dynamic equation of
\hat{y}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\hat{y}_{t}=k\hat{y}_{xx}+\hat{u},\\
\hat{y}(0,t)=\hat{y}(L,t)=0,\\
\hat{y}(x,0)=\hat{y}^{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
\hat{u}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}u_{i}
is the weighting average protocol, and
\hat{y}^{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}y_{i}^{0}(x)
is the weighting average initial condition. Let
\hat{\delta}=[\hat{\delta}_{1},\hat{\delta}_{2},\cdots,\hat{\delta}_{N}]^{T}
with
\hat{\delta}_{i}=y_{i}-\hat{y}
. Then
\langle\hat{\delta},\omega\rangle=0
.

Now we need to prove that
y_{i}
achieves the consensus in finite time, which is equivalent to that
y_{i}
converges to
\hat{y}
in finite time for
i=1,2,\cdots,N
. To this end, we construct a Lyapunov generalized energy function

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(t)=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}(y_{i}-\hat{y})^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{V}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}(y_{i,t}-\hat{y}_{t})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}(y_{i,xx}-\hat{y}_{xx})\mathrm{d}x}_{(I)}+\underbrace{2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}(u_{i}-\hat{u})\mathrm{d}x}_{(II)}.

To estimate the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
, we need to estimate Part (I) and Part (II), respectively. Using Lemma
2.5
to estimate Part (I), one has

\displaystyle 2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}(y_{i,xx}-\hat{y}_{xx})\mathrm{d}x

(3.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2k\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}(y_{i,x}-\hat{y}_{x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}kL^{-2}\pi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\omega_{i}a_{ij}=\omega_{j}a_{ji}

(i,j=1,\cdots,N)
, using (
2.21
) and Lemma
2.3
to estimate Part (II), we have

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}(u_{i}-\hat{u})\mathrm{d}x

(3.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{L}\omega_{i}y_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}y_{i}\omega_{i}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}\xi_{ij}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\omega_{i}a_{ij}\xi_{ij}^{2}dx\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\omega_{i}a_{ij}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left((\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}.

Define
D=((\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. According to Lemma
2.4
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\xi_{ij}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\hat{\delta}_{j}-\hat{\delta}_{i})^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}}

(3.41)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2\langle\hat{\delta},\mathcal{L}(D)\hat{\delta}\rangle}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}\langle\hat{\delta},\hat{\delta}\rangle}\geq\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D)).

It is easy to see that
\langle\omega,\textbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
and so
\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D))>0
from Lemma
2.4
. Substituting (
3.41
) into (
3.40
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}(u_{i}-\hat{u})\mathrm{d}x

(3.42)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{L}\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D))\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D)))^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha},

where

\displaystyle C_{3}=\left(\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D))\right)^{\alpha}>0.

Now, it follows from (
3.39
) and (
3.42
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{V}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}kL^{-2}\pi^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\int_{0}^{L}\hat{\delta}_{i}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}.

Thus, Lemma
2.1
shows that MASs (
2.3
) achieves the FTC in
t^{*}
with

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq\frac{\mathbb{V}(0)^{1-\alpha}}{C_{3}(1-\alpha)}=\frac{\left[\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}(y_{i}^{0}(x)-\hat{y}^{0}(x))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right]^{1-\alpha}}{C_{3}(1-\alpha)}.

The proof is completed.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that graph
G
is directed, s-con and d-bal. Then the controller (
2.22
) can ensure MASs (
2.3
) to achieve the FXC and the finite terminal time

satisfies

\displaystyle t^{*}\leq T_{\max}:=\frac{1}{C_{3}(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{(N^{1-\beta})^{2}C_{4}(\beta-1)},

where

\displaystyle C_{3}=\left(\frac{2}{\underset{i=1,\cdots,N}{\max}\omega_{i}}\lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(D))\right)^{\alpha}

and

and

Proof
Construct a Lyapunov generalized energy function by setting

Similar to the discussion in Theorem
3.3
, the derivative of
\mathbb{V}(t)
with

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left((\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\int_{0}^{L}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta}

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-(N^{1-\beta})^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(\omega_{i}a_{ij})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C_{3}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\alpha}-(N^{1-\beta})^{2}C_{4}(\mathbb{V}(t))^{\beta},

5
Conclusions

This paper studies the FTC and FXC issues of MASs driven by parabolic PDEs. We design the FTC and FXC distributed controllers to satisfy different control effects in MASs with external disturbance. Theoretical analysis shows that agents can reach the FTC and FXC under an undirected and connected graph or a directed, s-con, and d-bal graph. We also simplify controllers to solve the FTC and FXC issues of MASs without external disturbances. This work provides theoretical support for solving the FTC and FXC control problems of MASs described by parabolic PDEs. Furthermore, it gives some technical guidance towards the design of the controller to satisfy different control effects in MASs with/without external disturbance. However, for MASs driven by parabolic PDEs under event-triggering scheme or cyber attacks, how to ensure the reliability of the controller is still an open problem.

Noting that, in some real situations, it is necessary and important to co-consider other control problems in MASs such as the event-triggered control problem, the fuzzy fast-tracking control problem, the cyber attack problem and so on. Our future work will consider designing finite-time event-triggered and observer-based control strategies to solve the FTC and FXC problems of MASs driven by more general PDEs.

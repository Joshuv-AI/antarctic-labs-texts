Abstract
User scheduling and beamforming design are two crucial yet coupled topics for wireless communication systems. They are usually optimized separately with conventional optimization methods. In this paper, a novel cross-layer optimization problem is considered, namely, the user scheduling and beamforming are jointly discussed subjecting to the requirement of per-user quality of service (QoS) and the maximum allowable transmit power for multicell multiuser joint transmission networks. To achieve the goal, a mixed discrete-continue variables combinational optimization problem is investigated with aiming at maximizing the sum rate of the communication system. To circumvent the original non-convex problem with dynamic solution space, we first transform it into a 0-1 integer and continue variables optimization problem, and then obtain a tractable form with continuous variables by exploiting the characteristics of 0-1 constraint. Finally, the scheduled users and the optimized beamforming vectors are simultaneously calculated by an alternating optimization algorithm. We also theoretically prove that the base stations allocate zero power to the unscheduled users. Furthermore, two heuristic optimization algorithms are proposed respectively based on brute-force search and greedy search. Numerical results validate the effectiveness of our proposed methods, and the optimization approach gets relatively balanced results compared with the other two approaches.

IV-B

Brute-force Search Algorithm

As discussed in the last paragraph of Section
II
, we can obtain the optimal the solution to problem (
5
) or problem (
11
) via fixing set
\mathcal{S}
determined by the brute-force search method. The first step of the brute-force search algorithm is to check the feasibility of the set
\mathcal{S}
, i.e., whether the minimum user rate of each user in the set
\mathcal{S}
is met or not. The feasibility can be judged via addressing a power minimization problem, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right\}}~\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|^{2}

(41a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{k^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\left|\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right|^{2}}{\sum\limits_{l^{\prime}\neq k^{\prime}}\left|\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{l^{\prime}}\right|^{2}+1},\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}.

(41b)

Let
\mathbf{o}=\left[\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1^{\prime}},\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{2^{\prime}},\cdots,\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{K^{\prime}},1\right]^{T}
. Thus, problem (
41
) can be rewritten as follows:

\min\limits_{\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right\}}~\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|^{2},~\mathrm{s.t.}~\left\|\mathbf{o}\right\|\leq\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{k^{\prime}}}}\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}.

(42)

Problem (
42
) can be easily solved by the classical convex optimization tools, such as CVX
[
31
]
. Let
\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}^{\left(*\right)}\right\}
be the optimal solution to problem (
42
). If
\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\|\mathbf{Q}_{b}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{b}
,
\forall b\in\mathcal{B}
, and
\left|\mathcal{S}\right|\leq BN_{\mathrm{t}}
, then the minimum user rate of each user in the set
\mathcal{S}
is met, i.e., the combination of users in the set
\mathcal{S}
is valid. Further, for a given set
\mathcal{S}
, the
\left\{\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}^{\left(*\right)}\right\}
and
\left\{q_{k^{\prime}}^{\left(*\right)}\right\}
can be obtained via solving the following problem:

\displaystyle\max_{\left\{q_{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right\}}\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\overleftarrow{R}_{k^{\prime}},

(43a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~

\displaystyle q_{k^{\prime}}\geq 0,\left\|\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}=1,\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},

(43b)

\displaystyle r_{k^{\prime}}\leq\overleftarrow{R}_{k^{\prime}},\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},

(43c)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{k^{\prime}}\leq\sum\limits_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\lambda_{b}P_{b}.

(43d)

As discussed in the last paragraph of Section
II
,
\sum\limits_{\left|\mathcal{S}\right|=1}^{BN_{\mathrm{t}}}\frac{K!}{\left|\mathcal{S}\right|!\left(K-\left|\mathcal{S}\right|\right)!}
combinational problems are needed to be addressed and the optimal combination can be selected out to achieve the maximal sum rate. Note that problem (
43
) can be solved with a similar method as Algorithm
1
.In particular, with fixed virtual uplink transmit powers
\left\{q_{k}\right\}
, the optimal beamforming vectors
\left\{\mathbf{w}_{k}\right\}
is calculated with (
31

\left\{\mathbf{w}_{k}\right\}
and auxiliary variable
\left\{\lambda_{b}\right\}
,
\forall k\in\mathcal{K}
and
\forall b\in\mathcal{B}
, problem (
43
) is reformulated as follows:

\displaystyle\max_{\left\{q_{k^{\prime}},\theta_{k^{\prime}}\right\}}\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\log_{2}\left(1+\theta_{k^{\prime}}\right),

(44a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~

\displaystyle\psi_{k^{\prime}}\left(\theta_{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)-\phi_{k^{\prime}}\left(\theta_{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\leq 0,\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},

(44b)

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{k^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)-\tilde{\phi}_{k^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)\leq 0,\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},

(44c)

\displaystyle q_{k^{\prime}}\geq 0,\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{k^{\prime}}\leq\sum\limits_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\lambda_{b}P_{b},

(44d)

where

,

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{k^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\gamma}_{k^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-q_{k^{\prime}}\left|\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}}^{H}\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right|^{2}+\tilde{\varphi}_{k^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{q}^{{}^{\prime}}\right),

IV-C

ZFBF with SUS Algorithm

The process of searching all possible combinations of scheduling users in the brute-force search algorithm is with high complexity. Hence, in this subsection, we use the semiorthogonal user scheduling (SUS) method to generate the scheduling user set
\mathcal{S}
with zero-forcing beamforming (ZFBF)
[
7
]
. For a given set
\mathcal{S}
, beamforming vectors
\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=\left[\mathbf{w}_{1^{\prime}},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{K^{\prime}}\right]
is given by

\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}=\frac{\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]}{\left\|\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]\right\|_{2}}

(47)

where
\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=\mathbf{H}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left(\mathbf{H}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)^{H}\mathbf{H}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\right)^{-1}
with
\mathbf{H}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)=\left[\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{1^{\prime}},\cdots,\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{k^{\prime}},\cdots,\overline{\mathbf{h}}_{K^{\prime}}\right]
. Thus, the SINR of the
k^{\prime}
user is calculated as

\gamma_{k^{\prime}}=\frac{p_{k^{\prime}}}{\left\|\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2}}
. To satisfy the minimum rate requirement
r_{k^{\prime}}
of the
k^{\prime}
user, the required minimum power
is
p_{k^{\prime}}^{\flat}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{k^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2}
. If
\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p_{k^{\prime}}^{\flat}\left\|\mathbf{Q}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{b}
,
\forall b\in\mathcal{B}
, and
\left|\mathcal{S}\right|\leq BN_{\mathrm{t}}
, the set
\mathcal{S}
of scheduling users is effectiveness. Furthermore, for a fixed set
\mathcal{S}
of scheduling users obtained using the ZFBF and the SUS Algorithm, problem (
3
) is rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\max_{\left\{p_{k^{\prime}}\right\}}\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{p_{k^{\prime}}}{\left\|\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2}}\right),

(48a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~

\displaystyle p_{k^{\prime}}>0,\widetilde{\gamma}_{k^{\prime}}\leq\frac{p_{k^{\prime}}}{\left\|\mathbf{W}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\left[:,k^{\prime}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2}},\forall k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},

(48b)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}p_{k^{\prime}}\left\|\mathbf{Q}_{b}\mathbf{w}_{k^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{b},\forall b\in\mathcal{B},

(48c)

Note that problem (
48

[
31
]

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we focus on investigating the joint user scheduling and beamforming design for the downlink of multicell multiuser joint transmission networks, while subjecting to the requirement of QoS and the maximum allowable transmit power. For this cross-layer optimization topic, a mixed discrete-continue variables combinational optimization problem was first formulated. To overcome the difficulties encountered in solving the problem of interest, some basic transformation methods were derived to reformulated the original problem into a tractable form. Then, an effective and efficient optimization method was developed to address it. We also demonstrated that the proposed optimization approach could avoid to allocating power for the unscheduled users. The other two methods, namely, the brute-force search based approach and greedy search based approach, are also proposed. Finally, a large number of experimental results were provided to validate the effectiveness of the developed algorithm, and the proposed optimization approach obtained balanced result compared with the other two approaches.

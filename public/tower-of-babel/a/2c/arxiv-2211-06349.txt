Abstract
. The spectral variant of the quantum marginal problem asks: Given prescribed spectra for a set of overlapping quantum marginals, does there exist a compatible joint state? The main idea of this work is a symmetry-reduced semidefinite programming hierarchy that detects when when no such joint state exists. The hierarchy is complete, in the sense that it detects every incompatible set of spectra. The refutations it provides are dimension-free, certifying incompatibility in all local dimensions. The hierarchy also applies to the sums of Hermitian matrices problem, the compatibility of local unitary invariants, for certifying vanishing Kronecker coefficients, and to optimize over equivariant state polynomials.

Introduction

The compatibility of quantum marginals (also known as reduced density matrices)
is central to quantum phenomena such as entanglement and non-locality.
It also plays a key role in quantum algorithms like quantum error correction and adiabatic quantum computation.
At the heart of this

it is QMA-complete, with QMA being the quantum analogue of NP
[

]
.
This renders
molecular-structure and ground state calculations in chemistry

A more fundamental problem is to decide compatibility of the spectra instead of the reduced density matrices.
The simplest formulation of this
spectral
variant of the quantum marginal problem
is perhaps the following:
Given a set of prescribed eigenvalues
\vec{\lambda}_{AB}
and
\vec{\lambda}_{BC}
associated to subsystems

and

does there exist a joint state
\varrho_{ABC}
such that its reduced density matrices

\vec{\lambda}_{AB}
and
\vec{\lambda}_{BC}

If such a joint state exists, the spectra are said to be compatible;
they are incompatible otherwise.
This spectral formulation also maintains an intimate connection to
fundamental questions in representation and matrix theory

The works by Klyachko

allow one

Complete lists of inequalities for non-overlapping spectra of bi- and tripartite systems are
given in Refs.

More generally, the non-negativity of these coefficients can be established with algorithms from

giving rise to a hierarchy of one-sided criteria for compatibility for the non-overlapping case.
It is harder, however, for these methods to determine the
incompatibility

of marginal spectra, in particular when they have overlap.

The aim of this manuscript is to provide such complementary method:
a semidefinite programming hierarchy for certifying spectral incompatibility,
where the marginals are allowed to overlap (Section
5
).
It is complete, in the sense that it detects every set of incompatible spectra at some level of the hierarchy.
Our formulation in terms of a symmetric extension hierarchy is furthermore symmetry-reduced,
drastically reducing the size of the optimization problem (Section
6
).
This approach can produce spectral incompatibility certificates for both finite fixed local dimensions
and for arbitrary local dimensions (Section
7
).
A modern desktop computer can access up to the fourth level of the hierarchy in the case of four-partite states,
and the fifth level in the case of tripartite states (Section
9

(3)

Local unitary invariants and quantum codes.

Local unitary invariants of

Modifying our SDP to include these more general objective functions and constraints,

4.
Spectra are polynomial in
\varrho

4.1.
Spectrum from
\varrho^{\otimes k}

We first show how the spectral quantum marginal problem can be formulated as a
constraint that is polynomial in
\varrho
.
This is done with a generalization of the swap trick.

Consider a single quantum system
\varrho\in L(\mathds{C}^{d})
.
It is clear that
\tr(\varrho^{\ell})=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{\ell}
,
where the
\mu_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
\varrho

A complex

matrix has
d

the set
\{\tr(\varrho^{\ell})\,:\,\ell=1,\dots,d\}
determines

\varrho

Recall that
\sigma\in S_{k}
acts on
(\mathds{C}^{d})^{\otimes k}
via its representation
\eta_{d}(\sigma)
that permutes the tensor factors,

(7)

\eta_{d}(\sigma)\mathinner{|v_{1}\rangle}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathinner{|v_{k}\rangle}=\mathinner{|v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\rangle}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathinner{|v_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}\rangle}\,.

For a cycle
(\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{\ell})\in S_{k}
of length
\ell\leq k
and a Hermitian matrix

it is known that

\tr(\eta_{d}((\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{\ell}))B^{\otimes k})=\tr(B^{\ell})\tr(B)^{k-\ell}

]

For a density matrix this simplifies further to

(8)

\tr(\eta_{d}((\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{\ell}))\varrho^{\otimes k})=\tr(\varrho^{\ell})\,.

Consequently, under a global trace, the permutation operators acting
on copies of a state
\varrho
can recover its spectrum.

5.
SDP refutation

5.1.
Primal and dual programs

We follow Watrous
[
Wat12
]
and Doherty, Parrilo, and Spedalieri
[
DPS04
]
to recall:
a semidefinite program (SDP) is specified by a
hermiticity preserving linear map
\Xi:L(\mathcal{X})\to L(\mathcal{Y})
and Hermitian operators
C
and
D
.
Define the inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\tr(A^{\dagger}B)
,
and denote the set of positive and hermitian operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
by
\Herm(\mathcal{H})
and
\Pos(\mathcal{H})
respectively.
Then the primal and dual problem of the semidefinite program read

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{Primal:}}

\displaystyle\underset{X}{\mathrm{maximize}}

\displaystyle\langle C,X\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{such~that}

\displaystyle\Xi(X)=D

\displaystyle X\in\Pos(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{Dual:}}

\displaystyle\underset{Y}{\mathrm{minimize}}

\displaystyle\langle D,Y\rangle

(20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{such~that}

\displaystyle\Xi^{*}(Y)\geq C

\displaystyle Y\in\Herm(\mathcal{Y})

Operators
X
and
Y
satisfying the constraints of (
5.1
) and (
5.1
)
are said to be primal and dual feasible, respectively. Denote the set of primal and dual feasible operators by

\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{D}
.
Every semidefinite program satisfies
weak duality
,
that is, for all
X\in\mathcal{P}
and
Y\in\mathcal{D}
,

(21)

\displaystyle\langle D,Y\rangle-\langle C,X\rangle=\langle\Xi(X),Y\rangle-\langle C,X\rangle=\langle\Xi^{*}(Y)-C,X\rangle\geq 0\,.

Interestingly, weak duality (
21
) can be used to give an
SDP refutation
for the feasibility (
C=0
)
of a primal problem:
if there exists a feasible
Y\in\mathcal{D}

with
\langle D,Y\rangle<0
,
weak duality (
21
) is violated.
This implies that the primal problem is infeasible.
The operator
Y
provides then a certificate of infeasibility.

7.

We now show that when

\|A\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}s_{i}(A)
.
With this, (
8.2
) is bounded by

(68)

\displaystyle\tr\big((\eta(\sigma^{A})-q_{A,\ell}\mathds{1})\otimes(\eta(\sigma^{A})-q_{A,\ell}\mathds{1})^{\dagger}Y\big)

\displaystyle\leq(2+4q_{A,\ell})||Y-X||_{1}.

At this point, we use the finite quantum de Finetti theorem in Eq. (
64
). Together with the fact that
q_{A,\ell}\leq 1
, we get from Eqs. (
65
) and (
68
) that

(69)

\displaystyle\int\big|\tr\big((\eta(\sigma^{A})-q_{A,\sigma}\mathds{1})\varrho^{\otimes\ell}\big)\big|^{2}dm(\varrho)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{24d^{2n}\ell}{k}

Note that the left-hand side of Eq. (
69
) can be interpreted as the average of
\big|\tr\big((\eta(\sigma^{A})-q_{A,\ell}\mathds{1})\varrho^{\otimes\ell}\big)\big|^{2}
over all
\varrho
in the decomposition of
Y
. Thus, there must exist a
\varrho
of non-zero measure such that

(70)

\displaystyle\big|\tr(\varrho_{A}^{\ell})-q_{A,\ell}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big|\tr\big((\eta(\sigma^{A})-q_{A,\sigma}\mathds{1})\varrho^{\otimes\ell}\big)\big|^{2}\leq\frac{24d^{2n}\ell}{k}.

A similar argument can be made for all spectra.
Consider the sum of the left-hand sides of Eqs. (
8.2
) over all
A
and
j\leq\ell
. Thus,

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\int\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\big|\tr\big((\eta((1\ldots j)^{A})-q_{A,j}\mathds{1})\varrho^{\otimes\ell}\big)\big|^{2}dm(\varrho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\tr\big((\eta((1\ldots j)^{A})-q_{A,j}\mathds{1})\otimes(\eta((j+1,\ldots,2j)^{A})-q_{A,j}\mathds{1})^{\dagger}Y\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{24|\mathcal{A}|d^{2n}}{k}\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}j

(71)

\displaystyle=\frac{12|\mathcal{A}|d^{2n}}{k}(\ell-1)(\ell+2)\,.

Thus, there is again a state
\varrho
in the decomposition of
Y
with

(72)

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\big|\tr\big((\eta((1\ldots j)^{A})-q_{A,j}\mathds{1})\varrho^{\otimes\ell}\big)\big|^{2}\leq\frac{12|\mathcal{A}|d^{2n}}{k}(\ell-1)(\ell+2)\,.

As the left-hand side of this inequality is a sum of positive terms, each of them must be bounded individually, yielding the claim.

10.
Extensions

We shortly sketch further extensions of our method.

(1)

Local unitary invariants and quantum codes.

We sketch the construction of local unitary invariants as described by Rains
[
Rai00
]
.
Any polynomial in the coefficients of a matrix
M
can,
with suitable operators
F^{(k)}
, be written as

\sum_{k}\tr(F^{(k)}M^{\otimes k})
.
A unitary-invariant polynomial satisfies

(78)

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\tr\big(F^{(k)}M^{\otimes k}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\tr\big(F^{(k)}U^{\otimes k}M^{\otimes k}(U^{\otimes k})^{-1}\big)\,\quad\forall U\in\mathcal{U}(d)\,.

This implies that

F^{(k)}=(U^{\otimes k})^{-1}F^{(k)}U^{\otimes k}
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}(d)
.
By the Schur-Weyl duality [Eq. (
31
)],

F^{(k)}
is spanned by elements that are
in a one-to-one correspondence with elements of
S_{k}
.
In other words,
F^{(k)}=\eta(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathds{C}S_{k}
.
Considering a local unitary invariant polynomial, the invariance is

(79)

\tr(FM^{\otimes k})=\tr(F\mathfrak{U}^{-1}M^{\otimes k}\mathfrak{U})

for all
\mathfrak{U}=(U_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes U_{n})^{\otimes k}\,:U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}(d)
.
With Eq. (
32
), it follows that
F=\eta(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathds{C}S_{k}^{n}
.

Consider now the problem of the compatibility of local unitary invariants with a quantum state.
Then the arguments of Section
5
and
8
,
but replacing
\sigma^{A}
and
q_{A,\sigma}
by a more general
\alpha\in\mathds{C}S_{k}^{n}
and
q_{\sigma}\in\mathds{C}
,
establishes a complete semidefinite programming hierarchy
for the compatibility of a set of local unitary invariants.

This can be used to refute the existence of quantum codes with given parameters
(\!(n,K,\delta)\!)_{d}
:
a variant of the Knill-Laflamme condition states that a projector
\Pi
on
(\mathds{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}

corresponds to a quantum code of distance
\delta
,
if and only if

(80)

KB_{j}=A_{j}\quad j=0,\dots,\delta-1\,.

Here the weight enumerators
A_{j}
and
B_{j}
are local unitary invariants given by

(81)

\displaystyle A_{j}=\sum_{|E|=j}\tr(E\Pi)\tr(E^{\dagger}\Pi)\,,\quad\quad B_{j}=\sum_{|E|=j}\tr(E\Pi E^{\dagger}\Pi)\,.

Above the sum is over all elements
E
of an orthonormal tensor-product basis of weight
|E|=j

(e.g., the Pauli basis).
The
A_{j}
and
B_{j}
are both local unitary invariants
[
Rai98
]
.
Our hierarchy can then be used to rule out the existence of a compatible
\varrho=\Pi/K
,
certifying that a code with given parameters does not exist.

(2)

Equivariant state polynomials.

Equivariant state polynomials are a type of polynomials whose variables are states,
and whose positivity is invariant under local unitary transformations.
To see how they are constructed from our formalism, note that
local unitary invariants of degree
k
of
n
-partite quantum states
correspond to the expectation values of elements of
\mathds{C}S_{k}^{n}
on
\varrho^{\otimes k}
.
For example, for a bipartite state
\varrho

the element
(123)\times(132)\in S_{3}^{2}
gives the invariant

(82)

\tr\big((123)_{1}\otimes(132)_{2}(\varrho\otimes\varrho\otimes\varrho)\big)=\tr\big((\varrho^{T_{2}}\varrho^{T_{2}})^{T_{2}}\varrho\big)\,.

More generally, the expectation values can also be taken with respect to

\varrho_{1}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\varrho_{m}^{k_{m}}
. For our example above one can form
expressions in two and three variables,

\tr\big(\varrho^{T_{2}}\varrho^{T_{2}})^{T_{2}}\nu\big)
and

\tr\big(\varrho^{T_{2}}\sigma^{T_{2}})^{T_{2}}\nu\big)
.

Equivariant state polynomials are obtained by varying over a state that is linear in such expression.
More precisely,
every non-negative unitary invariant that is linear in at least one state
is in a one-to-one correspondence with a positive semidefinite equivariant state polynomial.
To see this, note that

(83)

\displaystyle\tr\big(\eta_{d}(\alpha)\varrho_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\varrho_{m}^{\otimes k_{m}}\otimes\nu\big)\geq 0

for all states
\varrho_{1},\dots,\varrho_{m},\nu
if and only if the following is a positive semidefinite matrix,

(84)

\displaystyle\tr_{1\dots m-1}\big(\eta_{d}(\alpha)\varrho_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\varrho_{m}^{\otimes k_{m}}\otimes\mathds{1}\big)\succeq 0\,.

This follows from the self-duality of the positive cone,
A\succeq 0
if and only if
\tr(AB)\geq 0
for all
B\succeq 0
,
and the defining property of the partial trace,
\tr(M(N\otimes\mathds{1}))=\tr(\tr_{2}(M)N)
.
Thus, to determine whether Eq. (
84
) is positive semidefinite for all
\varrho_{1},\dots,\varrho_{m}
,
it is enough to minimize Eq. (
83
)
over all states
\varrho_{1},\dots,\varrho_{m},\nu
.
This can be done by taking
\tr(\eta(\sigma)X)
as objective function in Eq. (
22
)
with a relaxed symmetry constraint on
X
: one does not aim to approximate
\varrho^{\otimes k}
,
but
\varrho_{1}^{k_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\varrho_{m}^{k_{m}}\otimes\nu
, and thus
X
is invariant under the permutation
of the subsystems corresponding to
\varrho_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}},\dots,\varrho_{m}^{\otimes k_{m}}
individually.
This yields a complete hierarchy to optimize over equivariant state polynomials under equivariant state polynomial or local unitary constraints.
With this one can systematically search for new purity and moment inequalities,
relevant for example in entanglement detection
[
Hub21
,
RH24
]
.

12.
Conclusions

Our main result, Theorem
7
,
combines the techniques of
symmetric extension and symmetry reduction
to certify the incompatibility of marginal spectra.
This simple idea turns out to be quite powerful,
allowing for a complete hierarchy for spectral compatibility in arbitrary local dimensions
(Theorem
9
).
At the same time, it can be used to differentiate different dimensions with respect to spectral compatibility:
There exist spectra which are non-trivially incompatible in dimension
d
, but compatible in
d^{\prime}>d
.

We think it is particularly interesting that not only is our hierarchy applicable to reformulations of the spectral marginal problem
such as non-vanishing Kronecker coefficients and sums of hermitian matrices,
but also to the compatibility of local unitary invariants and the existence of quantum codes.
Finally, we believe that the equivariant state polynomial optimization
framework sketched in Section
10
could find further applications.

A natural question is how to include in the hierarchy (

)
constraints arising from a positive partial transpose, which could strengthen the symmetric extension hierarchy.
A symmetry-reduction similar to the one employed here
would require a decomposition of the Brauer algebra.

can be expressed as a linear combination of elements from

[

]

and it should be possible to formulate a semidefinite program analogous
to level

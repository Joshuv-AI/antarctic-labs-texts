Multichannel decay: alternative derivation of the i i -th channel decay probability
Francesco Giacosa

Abstract
In the study of decays, it is quite common that an unstable quantum state/particle has multiple distinct decay channels. In this case, besides the survival probability p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) , also the probability w i ​ ( t ) w_{i}(t) that the decay occurs between ( 0 , t ) (0,t) in the i i -th channel is a relevant object. The general form of the function w i ​ ( t ) w_{i}(t) was recently presented in PLB 831 (2022), 137200. Here, we provide a novel and detailed ‘joint’ derivation of both p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) and w i ​ ( t ) w_{i}(t) . As it is well known, p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) is not an exponential function; similarly, w i ​ ( t ) w_{i}(t) is also not such. In particular, the ratio w i / w j w_{i}/w_{j} (for OPEN i ≠ j ) i\neq j) is not a simple constant, as it would be in the exponential limit. The functions w i ​ ( t ) w_{i}(t) and their mutual ratios may therefore represent a novel tool to study the non-exponential nature of the decay law.

Let

H=H_{0}+H_{int}

\left\langle S|S\right\rangle=1\text{ , }\left\langle S|E,i\right\rangle=0\text{ , }\left\langle E,i|E^{\prime},j\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}\delta(E-E^{\prime})\text{ ;}

(2)

\left|S\right\rangle\left\langle S\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{E_{th,i}}^{\infty}dE\left|E,i\right\rangle\left\langle E,i\right|=1\text{ .}

(3)

The decays

are encoded in the matrix elements

\left\langle S|H_{j}|E,j\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E)}{2\pi}}

\Gamma_{i}(E)

(6)

Note,

The quantity
U(t)=e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Ht}
is the well-known time evolution
operator. In our case, we are interested in the evaluation of the survival
probability amplitude and the
i
-th channel decay amplitude:

\left\langle S|U(t)|S\right\rangle\text{ , }\left\langle E,i|U(t)|S\right\rangle.

(7)

In order to accomplish it, let us introduce the operator
F(t)
(
F
for
‘future’) as

F(t)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-H+i\varepsilon}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{c}U(t)\text{ for }t>0\\
0\text{ for }t<0\end{array}\right.\text{ .}

(8)

The previous equation should be understood as an an operatorial equation: for
an arbitrary eigenstate
\left|E_{0}\right\rangle
with
H\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=E_{0}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle
, one has

F(t)\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-E_{0}+i\varepsilon}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{c}e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}E_{0}t}\left|\Psi_{0}\right\rangle\text{ for }t>0\\
0\text{ for }t<0\end{array}\right.,

(9)

where the last equation is obtained integrating on the lower half-plane of the
complex variable
E
for
t>0
and on the upper half-plane for
t<0.

Formally,
F(t)
is not defined for
t=0
since the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{1}{E-E_{0}+i\varepsilon}
does not converge. Yet,
we summarize the previous equation by writing

F(t)=\theta(t)U(t)

(10)

together with the choice
\theta(0)=1/2
, thus
F(0)=1/2
. Similarly, let us
introduce the operator
P(t)
(
P
for ‘past’):

P(t)=F^{\ast}(-t)=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-H-i\varepsilon}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{c}0\text{ for }t>0\\
U(t)\text{ for }t<0\end{array}\right.\text{ ,}

(11)

hence
P(t)=\theta(-t)U(t)
and
P(0)=1/2
. For each time
t
(including

t=0
) we get the consistent result:

\displaystyle U(t)\text{ }

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Ht}=F(t)+P(t)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-H+i\varepsilon}-\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}}{E-H-i\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\text{ }\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\frac{\varepsilon}{\left(E-H\right)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}\delta(E-H)e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}\text{
.}

(12)

Next, we turn to the time evolution of the expectation values of Eq.
(
7
). In order to evaluate them, we need to determine the propagators
defined as:

G_{S}(E)=\left\langle S\right|\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle\text{ , }T_{i}(E^{\prime},E)=\left\langle E^{\prime},i\right|\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle\text{ .}

(13)

Namely, once these quantities are known, the time evolution is obtained by
using the ‘future’ representation
F(t)
of Eq. (
8
). To this end,
we write down an operatorial Lippmann-Schwinger equation:

\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon}H_{int}\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\text{ ,}

(14)

which can be proven by considering the operator
O
defined as (note, dealing
with the operators the ordering is important):

\displaystyle O

\displaystyle=(E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon)\left(\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon}\right)=(E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon)\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}-1

\displaystyle=(E-H_{0}+i\varepsilon)\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}-(E-H+i\varepsilon)\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(H-H_{0})\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}=H_{int}\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\text{ .}

(15)

Then, the propagator of the unstable state
S
reads:

\displaystyle G_{S}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\langle S\right|\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle=\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}\left\langle S\right|H_{int}\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{E_{th,i}}^{\infty}\mathrm{dE}^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{2\pi}}T_{i}(E^{\prime},E)\text{ ,}

(16)

while the propagators for the transitions
\left|S\right\rangle\rightarrow\left|E,i\right\rangle
are given by:

T_{i}(E^{\prime},E)=\left\langle E^{\prime},i\right|\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle=\frac{1}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}\left\langle E^{\prime},i\right|H_{int}\frac{1}{E-H+i\varepsilon}\left|S\right\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{2\pi}}\frac{G_{S}(E)}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}\text{ .}

(17)

Plugging
T_{i}(E^{\prime},E)
into Eq. (
16
), we obtain the
Dyson-Schwinger equation of the
S
propagator:

G_{S}(E)=\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{E-M+i\varepsilon}\Pi(E)G_{S}(E)\text{ ,}

(18)

where the total self-energy
\Pi(E)
and the partial self-energies
\Pi_{i}(E)
read:

\Pi(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Pi_{i}(E)\text{ , }\Pi_{i}(E)=-\int_{E_{th,i}}^{\infty}\frac{dE^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}\text{ ,}

(19)

for which
\operatorname{Im}\Pi_{i}(E)=\Gamma_{i}(E)/2
(optical
theorem)

As a consequence of the adopted formalism, once
G_{S}(E)
is fixed, also

in Eq. (

\displaystyle w_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}dE^{\prime}\left|\left\langle E^{\prime},i\right|U(t)\left|S\right\rangle\right|^{2}\overset{t>0}{=}\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}dE^{\prime}\left|\left\langle E^{\prime},i\right|F(t)\left|S\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}dE^{\prime}\left|\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dET_{i}(E^{\prime},E)e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}\right|^{2}=\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}dE^{\prime}\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{2\pi}\left|\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dE\frac{G_{S}(E)}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}\right|^{2}\text{ .}

(24)

This is indeed the expression for the quantity
w_{i}(t)
that we were looking
for. Yet, it still involves the complex propagator
G_{S}(E)
, so it is better
to recast it in a form that is simpler in practical applications. By
introducing the spectral representation of Eq. (
21
)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{G_{S}(E)}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dE\int_{E_{1,th}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}\frac{1}{E-E^{\prime}+i\varepsilon}\frac{d_{S}(y)}{E-y+i\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}Et}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{1h,1}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}d_{S}(y)\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t}-e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}yt}}{E^{\prime}-y}\right)

(25)

(note, the integrand contains no singularity) we obtain the expression of Ref.

[
22
]
:

w_{i}(t)=\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}dE^{\prime}\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{2\pi}\left|\int_{E_{1,th}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}d_{S}(y)\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t}-e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}y^{\prime}t}}{E^{\prime}-y}\right)\right|^{2}\text{ .}

(26)

This quantity can be calculated numerically when the functions
\Gamma_{i}(E)

(and thus
d_{S}(E)
too) are known. Roughly speaking, it is ready to be used
by just “plug in and calculate”.

There is another useful way to express
w_{i}(t)
mentioned in Ref.

[
22
]
. By introducing

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{dt}^{\prime}\frac{a(t^{\prime})}{\hslash}e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\left(\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}d_{S}(y)e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}yt^{\prime}}\right)e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{dy}}{\hslash}d_{S}(y)\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}(E^{\prime}-y)t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}d_{S}(y)\frac{e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}(E^{\prime}-y)t}-1}{i(E^{\prime}-y)}=ie^{\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t}\int_{E_{th,1}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dy}d_{S}(y)\frac{e^{-\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t}-e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}y}}{E^{\prime}-y}

we find

w_{i}(t)=\int_{E_{th,i}}^{+\infty}\mathrm{dE}^{\prime}\frac{\Gamma_{i}(E^{\prime})}{2\pi}\left|\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\frac{a(t^{\prime})}{\hslash}e^{\frac{i}{\hslash}E^{\prime}t^{\prime}}\right|^{2}\text{ }.

In QFT, there is no BW limit and no exponential decay (a threshold is
always present, since
s\geq 0

to some inconsistencies. An interesting model, discussed in Ref.

turns out to be, as expected,
non-exponential, in agreement wit the QM case.

expressions of the survival probability

and the decay probability in

by using a Lippmann-Schwinger equation at the
level of operators. The propagator for the state
S
and the transition
propagator for
S
into any decay product are intertwined. In this way,
p(t)

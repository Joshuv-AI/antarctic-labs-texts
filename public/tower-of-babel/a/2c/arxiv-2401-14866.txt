Solutions for a free-boundary problem modeling multilayer films with coherent and incoherent interfaces MUR Excellence Department 2023-2027
Randy Llerena, Paolo Piovano

Abstract
. In this paper, we move forward from the results of [ 42 ] by introducing a variational model for the study of multilayer films that allows for the treatment of both coherent and incoherent interfaces between layers. The model is designed in the framework of the theory of Stress Driven Rearrangement Instabilities, which are characterized by the competition between elastic and surface energy effects. The surface of each film layer is assumed to satisfy the “exterior graph condition” already introduced in [ 42 ] , for which bulk cracks are allowed to be of non-graph type. By applying the direct method of calculus of variations under a constraint on the number of connected components of the cracks not connected to the surface of the film layers the existence of energy minimizers is established in dimension 2. As a byproduct of the analysis the state of art on the variational modeling of single-layered films deposited on a fixed substrate is advanced by letting the substrate surface free, by addressing the presence of multiple layers of various materials, and by including the possibility of delamination between the various film layers.

Introduction

In this manuscript, we address the problem of modeling the morphology of multilayered film composites consisting of different crystalline materials deposited on a substrate.
The goal is to advance the literature on the variational modeling of single-layered films deposited on a fixed substrate
[
14
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
in a twofold direction: on the one hand, by letting the substrate surface free and by addressing the presence of multiple layers of various materials, and, on the other hand, by including into the analysis the possibility of a failure of the film coatings, since, as described in
[
52
]
for the case of some oxide films, the compressive stresses generated during film growth can lead to the delamination (and the buckling) between different layers.

Nowadays film-based nanostructures find several applications, in particular for the manufacturing of electronic and photonic devices, such as for the creation of their semiconductor components, and of solar and photovoltaic cells. The great interest that films and, in particular, multilayer films
[
50
,
53
,
54
]
, created by vapor deposition of different material constituents, continue attracting is due to the fact that, as they are self-assembled heterostructures, their employment represents one of the nanostructure design methods with most feasibility potential;
therefore, any advancement in the mathematical modeling of film and multilayer film materials can have an important practical impact for their design control. Examples of multilayer films that are used for optoelectronic applications are multiple quantum well structures with alternating compressive and tensile strained layers, and short-period
quantum-dot superlattices
. Also for the latter, as described in
[
53
,
54
]
, it is really the superposition of various layers of materials that allows to reach the highest degree order needed for the applications with respect to the size, the density and the distribution of the quantum dots.

The adopted strategy consists in combining the implementation to the multiphase setting of the film models considered in
[
14
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
, in which delamination is not taken into account, with the recent results for a two-phase setting of
[
42
]
, in which the interfaces between phases are instead allowed to present both coherent and incoherent portions. With coherency here we intend a microscopic organization of atoms that can be regarded as a (possibly deformed) uniform lattice that is homogeneous through the interface, while with incoherency we refer to the presence of debonding and delamination at the interface.
In this way the extension of the single-layer literature to the multilayer setting (with possible delamination at each layer interface) is performed within the theory of stress driven rearrangement instabilities (SDRI)
[
6
,
19
,
31
,
51
]
, which was also at the basis of the variational single-layer models introduced in
[
48
,
49
]
and analytically validated in
[
14
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
. In fact, as in
[
48
,
49
]
for thin films, and more generally for free crystals in
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
, we model the mismatch between the free-standing equilibrium lattices of the materials of each pair of film layers and of the first layer with the substrate by means of the so-called
mismatch strain
in the elastic energy. As described by the SDRI theory the lattice mismatch is responsible for the migration of the atoms of each phase from their crystalline order, since the lattice mismatch induces large stresses in the bulk material and, in order to release the related elastic energy, the atoms move forming corrugations, cracks, and other interface instabilities
[
6
,
31
,
19
,
51
]
.

In regard to the literature results for settings with phase interfaces exclusively assumed to be coherent, we refer to the literature on the optimal shape of partitions in the absence of elastic effects, which was initiated by Almgren in

-ellipticity though remains the only known condition characterizing the the

-lower semicontinuity apart from specific contexts (we refer to

,

,
3
]

Finally, in

the analogous version of the

-ellipticity condition in the framework of

Instead, in regard to the settings with only incoherent interfaces, we refer to the results obtained with respect to the related

In this context, interfaces represent the contours of the image color areas that can be characterized as the discontinuity set of an auxiliary state function. We refer to

In

E^{c}
the complement of
E
and
by
{\rm{Int}}(E)
,
\overline{E}
and
\partial E
interior, the closure and the topological boundary of
E
, respectively.

Finally, let
{\rm dist}(\cdot,E)
and
{\rm{sdist}}(\cdot,\partial E)
be the
distance
function from
E
and the
signed distance
from
\partial E
respectively, where we recall that
{\rm{sdist}}(\cdot,\partial E)
is defined by

{\rm{sdist}}(x,\partial E):=\begin{cases}{\rm dist}(x,E)&\textrm{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus E,\\
-{\rm dist}(x,E)&\textrm{if }x\in E\end{cases}

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Geometric Measure theory

We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{2}{(B)}
the
2
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of any Lebesgue measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and by
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
the characteristic function of
B
. For
\alpha\in\left[0,1\right]
we denote by
B^{(\alpha)}
the set of points of density
\alpha
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e.,

B^{(\alpha)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\lim_{r\to 0}{\frac{\mathcal{L}^{2}({B\cap B_{r}\left(x\right)})}{\mathcal{L}^{2}({B_{r}\left(x\right)})}}=\alpha\right\}.

We denote the distributional derivative of a function
f\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
Df
and define it as the operator
D:C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{Df\cdot\varphi}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{f\cdot\gradient\varphi\,dx},

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

We denote with
\mathcal{H}^{1}
the 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure. We say that
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable if
0<\mathcal{H}^{1}(K)<+\infty
and
\theta_{*}(K,x)=\theta^{*}(K,x)=1
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in K
, where

\theta_{*}(K,x):=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}{\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(K\cap B_{r}\left(x\right))}{2r}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\theta^{*}(K,x):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}{\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(K\cap B_{r}\left(x\right))}{2r}}.

We define sets of finite perimeter as in
[
4
, Definition 3.35]
and the reduced boundary
\partial^{*}E
of a set
E
of finite perimeter by

(2.1)

\partial^{*}E:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\exists\nu_{E}\left(x\right):=-\lim_{r\to 0}{\frac{D\mathbbm{1}_{E}(B_{r}\left(x\right))}{\absolutevalue{D \mathbbm{1}_ {E} }(B_{r}\left(x\right))}},\absolutevalue{\nu_E \xp}=1\right\},

where we refer to
\nu_{E}\left(x\right)
as the measure-theoretical unit normal at
x\in\partial E
.

For any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of finite perimeter, by
[
43
, Corollary 15.8 and Theorem 16.2]
it yields that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1/2)}\setminus\partial^{*}E)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{*}E\subset E^{(1/2)}.

Moreover, for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of finite perimeter, we have

(2.3)

\partial E=N\cup\partial^{*}E\cup(E^{(1)}\cup E^{(0)})\cap\partial E,

where
N
is a
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-negligible (see
[
43
, Section 16.1]
).

Functions of bounded pointwise variation

Given a function
h:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
we denote the pointwise variation of
h
by

\textrm{Var}\,h:=\sup{\left\{\sum_{i}^{n}\absolutevalue{ h(x_i) - h(x_{i-1})}:P:=\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\}\ \textrm{is a partition of $[a,b]$}\right\}}.

We say that
h:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}
has finite pointwise variation if
\textrm{Var}\,h<\infty.
We recall that for any function
h
such that
\textrm{Var}\,h<\infty
,
h
has at most countable discontinuities and there exists
h(x^{\pm}):=\lim_{z\to x^{\pm}}h(z)
. In the following given a function
h:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
with finite pointwise variation, we define

h^{-}(x):=\min\{h(x^{+}),h(x^{-})\}=\liminf_{z\to x}h(z)

and

h^{+}(x):=\max\{h(x^{+}),h(x^{-})\}=\limsup_{z\to x}h(z).

In view of
[
41
, Corollary 2.23]
and with slightly abuse of notation, the limits

(2.4)

h^{+}(a):=\lim_{x\to a^{-}}h(x)\quad\text{and}\quad h^{-}(b):=\lim_{x\to b^{-}}h(x)

are finite.

3.
Mathematical setting and main results

3.1.
Multilayer model

In this section, we introduce the family of admissible regions with finite number of composite layers and the respective family of admissible configurations.
Let
\Omega:=(-l,l)\times(-L,{\infty})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
for positive parameters
l,L\in\mathbb{R}
.

Analogously to
[
42
]
, we assume a
graph-crack
constraint of the composite of layers, in other words, we consider a
graph constraint
on the strict epigraph of the composite of layers, while inside of it, we consider
cracks
as closed and
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable sets of
\Omega
, roughly speaking, the profile of the composite is given by a function representing its thickness, plus a countable number of external vertical filaments and internal cracks.
More precisely, we consider the
family of admissible heights

\text{ AH}(\Omega)
defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{ AH}(\Omega):=\left\{h:[-l,l]\to[0,L]:h\text{ is upper semicontinuous and }\text{ Var}\,h<\infty\right\}

and let
S_{h}
denote the closed subgraph with height
h\in\text{ AH}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

(3.2)

S_{h}:=\{(x,y):-l<x<l,y\leq h(x)\}.

Furthermore, we define the
family of admissible cracks

\text{AK}(\Omega)
by

(3.3)

\text{ AK}(\Omega):=\{K\subset\Omega:\text{ $K$ is a closed set in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$,
$\mathcal{H}^{1}$-rectifiable and }\mathcal{H}^{1}(K)<\infty\}

and the
family of pairs of admissible heights and cracks

\text{AHK}(\Omega)
by

(3.4)

\text{ AHK}(\Omega):=\{(h,K)\in\text{ AH}(\Omega)\times\text{ AK}(\Omega):\,K\subset\overline{{\rm{Int}}(S_{h})}\}.

Finally, given
(h,K)\in\text{AHK}(\Omega)
we refer to the region characterized as the subgraph of the height function
h
without the internal cracks of
K
, namely,

(3.5)

S_{h,K}:=({S_{h}}\setminus K)\cap\Omega,

as the
(generalized) subgraph with height
h
and cracks
K
,
and we define the
family of admissible subgraphs
as

(3.6)

\text{ AS}(\Omega):=\{S\subset\Omega\,:\,\text{$S=S_{h,K}$ for a pair $(h,K)\in\text{ AHK}(\Omega)$}\}.

We observe that for every
(h,K)\in\text{AHK}(\Omega)

(3.7)

\overline{S_{h,K}}=S_{h},\quad{\rm{Int}}(S_{h,K})={\rm{Int}}(S_{h})\setminus K\quad\text{and}\quad\partial{S_{h,K}}=\partial{S_{h}}\cup K.

We have that
\partial{S_{h}}
is connected and,
\partial S_{h}
and
\partial S_{h,K}
have finite
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure. By
[
24
, Lemma 3.12 and Lemma 3.13]
, for any
h\in\text{AH}(\Omega)
,
\partial S_{h}
is rectifiable and applying the Besicovitch-Marstrand-Mattila Theorem (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.63]
),
\partial{S_{h}}
is
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable, and hence,
\partial S_{h,K}
is
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable. Furthermore, by applying
[
36
, Proposition A.1]

S_{h}
and
S_{h,K}
are sets of finite perimeter.

Definition 3.1

(Admissible multilayers and admissible configurations)
.

We define the family of two layers
\mathcal{B}^{1}
by

\mathcal{B}^{1}:=\{(S_{h^{1},K^{1}},S_{h^{0},K^{0}}):\text{ for $i=0,1$ there exists }(h^{i},K^{i})\in\mathrm{AHK}(\Omega),\,S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\in\mathrm{AS}(\Omega),\\
h^{0}\leq h^{1}\,\text{and }\partial S_{h^{1},K^{1}}\cap{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{0},K^{0}})=\emptyset\}\subset\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{AS}(\Omega).

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the family of admissible
(\alpha+1)
-layers
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
by

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}:=\{(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in[\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)]^{\alpha+1}:\,(S_{h^{i},K^{i}},S_{h^{i-1},K^{i-1}})\in\mathcal{B}^{1}\\
\text{ for every $1\leq i\leq\alpha$}\},

where
[\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)]^{\alpha+1}:=\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)\times\ldots\times\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)
represents the
(\alpha+1)
cartesian product of
\mathrm{AS}(\Omega)
.

We define the family of admissible configurations by

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}:=\{(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u):(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha},\,u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}}))\}.

Remark 3.2
.

In view of Definition
3.1
, it follows that every
(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
satisfy the following properties:

(i)

h^{i}\leq h^{j}
and
\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}\cap{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i},K^{i}})=\emptyset
for every
0\leq i<j\leq\alpha
and hence,
(S_{h^{j},K^{j}},S_{h^{i},K^{i}})\in\mathcal{B}^{1}
for every
0\leq i<j\leq\alpha
;

(ii)

K^{i}\subset\overline{{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i}})}\setminus{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i-1},K^{i-1}})

for every
i=1,\ldots,\alpha
;

(iii)

K^{i}\cap K^{i-2}\cap{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i-2}})\subset K^{i}\cap{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i-1}})\subset\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cap{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{i-1}})\subset K^{i-1}
for every
i=2,\ldots,\alpha
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, motivated in
[
35
,
36
,
42
]
we introduce a notion of convergence for the families
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
.

Definition 3.3

(
\tau_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}
-Convergence)
.

A sequence
\{(S_{h^{\alpha}_{n},K^{\alpha}_{n}},\ldots,S_{h^{0}_{n},K^{0}_{n}})\}\subset\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}

{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha}}
-converges to
(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
if

-

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial S_{h^{i}_{k},K^{i}_{k}})<\infty
, for every
i=0,\ldots,\lambda

-

\text{sdist}\left(\cdot,\partial S_{h^{i}_{k},K^{i}_{k}}\right)\rightarrow\text{sdist}\left(\cdot,\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\right)
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for every
i=0,\ldots,\lambda
,

where

S_{h^{i}_{k},K^{i}_{k}}:=S_{h^{i}_{k}}\setminus K^{i}_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{h^{i},K^{i}}:=S_{h^{i}}\setminus K^{i},

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=0,\ldots,\alpha
.

Definition 3.4

(
\tau_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}
-Convergence)
.

A sequence
\{(S_{h^{\alpha}_{n},K^{\alpha}_{n}},\ldots,S_{h^{0}_{n},K^{0}_{n}},u_{n})\}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}

{\tau_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}}
-converges to
(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u)\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
if

-

(S_{h^{\alpha}_{n},K^{\alpha}_{n}},\ldots,S_{h^{0}_{n},K^{0}_{n}})\xrightarrow{\tau_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}}}(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})
,

-

u_{n}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
{{\rm{Int}}(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}})}
.

Analogously to
[
35
,
36
,
42
]
, we introduce a subfamily of
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
subject to a restriction on the number of connected components of the boundary of each composite layer and the subfamily of
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
with the corresponding configurations.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\bm{m}:=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{N}^{\alpha+1}
. We refer to

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}_{\bm{m}}:=\{(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}:\,\text{$\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}$ has at most $m_{i}$-connected components}\\
\text{for $i=0,\ldots,\alpha$}\}

as the families of
admissible multilayers
and to

\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{\bm{m}}:=\{(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u)\in\mathcal{C}:\,(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}_{\bm{m}}\}

as the family of
admissible configurations
.

In the sequel we fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. Motivated in the model introduced in
[
42
]
, we consider the surface tension between two layers
\mathcal{S}^{(i,j)}:\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}\to[0,+\infty]
in the family of admissible layers
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
by

\mathcal{S}^{(i,j)}(S_{h^{j},K^{j}},S_{h^{i},K^{i}}):=\int_{\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cup\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}{\psi_{i,j}(z,\nu)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}},

where
(S_{h^{\alpha},K^{\alpha}},\ldots,S_{h^{0},K^{0}})\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
for
0\leq i<j\leq\alpha
, and the surface tension
\psi_{i,j}
is defined in different portions of
\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cup\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}
, more precisely,

(3.8)

\psi_{i,j}(x,\nu(x)):=\begin{cases}\varphi_{j}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}(x))&x\in\Omega\cap(\partial^{*}S_{h^{j},K^{j}}\setminus\partial^{*}S_{h^{i},K^{i}})\\
\varphi^{1}_{ij}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}(x))&x\in\Omega\cap\partial^{*}S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cap\partial^{*}S_{h^{j},K^{j}},\\
\varphi_{ij}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{i},K^{i}}}\left(x\right))&x\in\Omega\cap(\partial^{*}S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\setminus\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}})\\
(\varphi_{j}+\varphi^{1}_{ij})(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}(x))&x\in\Omega\cap\partial^{*}S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cap\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}\cap S_{h^{j},K^{j}}^{(1)},\\
2\varphi_{j}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}\left(x\right))&x\in\Omega\cap\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}\cap S_{h^{j},K^{j}}^{(1)}\cap S_{h^{i},K^{i}}^{(0)},\\
2\varphi^{2}_{ij}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}\left(x\right))&x\in\Omega\cap\partial{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}\cap{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}^{(0)},\\
2\varphi_{ij}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{i},K^{i}}}\left(x\right))&\begin{aligned} x\in\Omega&\cap(\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\setminus\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}})\\
&\cap\left(S_{h^{i},K^{i}}^{(1)}\cup S_{h^{i},K^{i}}^{(0)}\right)\cap S_{h^{j},K^{j}}^{(1)},\end{aligned}\\
\varphi^{1}_{ij}(x,\nu_{S_{h^{j},K^{j}}}(x))&x\in\Omega\cap\partial S_{h^{i},K^{i}}\cap\partial S_{h^{j},K^{j}}\cap S_{h^{i},K^{i}}^{(1)},\end{cases}

where
\varphi_{j},\,\varphi_{ij}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty]
and, given also the function
\varphi_{i}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty]

\varphi^{1}_{ij}
and

[
42
]

3.2.
Main results

We state here the main results of the paper.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. We fix
l,L>0
and we consider
\Omega:=(-l,l)\times(-L,{\infty})
. For every pair of integers
0\leq i\leq j\leq\alpha
we consider
\varphi^{1}_{ij}:=\min\{\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}+\varphi_{ij}\}
and
\varphi^{2}_{ij}:=\min\{\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i}\}
and we assume throughout this manuscript that:

(H1)

\varphi_{j},\varphi_{ij},\varphi^{1}_{ij},\varphi^{2}_{ij}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
are Finsler norms such that there exists
c_{2}\geq c_{1}>0
such that

(3.12)

c_{1}\absolutevalue{\xi}\leq\varphi_{j}(x,\xi),\varphi^{1}_{ij}(x,\xi),\varphi_{ij}(x,\xi)\leq c_{2}\absolutevalue{\xi}\quad\textrm{for every $x\in\overline{\Omega}$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$},

(H2)

We have

(3.13)

\varphi^{1}_{ij}(x,\xi)\geq\absolutevalue{ \varphi_{ij}(x, \xi) -\varphi_{j} (x, \xi)}\quad\textrm{for every $x\in\overline{\Omega}$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$.}

(H3)

\mathbb{C}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{2}_{\rm sym})
and there exists
c_{3}>0

Now, we are going to prove that the elastic energy is lower semicontinuous. Indeed, let

{\mathcal{W}(S_{h^{1},K^{1}},S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u)}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\mathcal{W}(S_{h^{1}_{k},K^{1}_{k}},S_{h^{0}_{k},K^{0}_{k}},u_{k})}.

) and (
4.15

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{1}(S_{h^{1},K^{1}},S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u)&:={\mathcal{W}(S_{h^{1},K^{1}},S_{h^{0},K^{0}},u)}+\mathcal{S}^{1}\left(S_{h^{1},K^{1}},S_{h^{0},K^{0}}\right)\\

\mathcal{L}^{2}(S_{h^{\alpha}_{k},K^{\alpha}_{k}})\leq\absolutevalue{\mathcal{L}^2(S_{h^\alpha_k,K^\alpha_k}) - \mathbbm{v}_\alpha}+\mathbbm{v}_{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}}\mathcal{F}^{\alpha,\bm{\lambda}}(S_{h^{\alpha}_{k},K^{\alpha}_{k}},\ldots,S_{h^{0}_{k},K^{0}_{k}},u_{k})+\mathbbm{v}_{\alpha}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

This concludes the proof.

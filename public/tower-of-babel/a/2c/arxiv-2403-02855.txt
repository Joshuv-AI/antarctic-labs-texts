Refining the grading of irreducible Lie colour algebra representations
Mitchell Ryan

Abstract
. We apply the loop module construction of Mazorchuk–Zhao in the context of Lie colour algebras. We construct a bijection between the equivalence classes of all finite-dimensional graded irreducible Lie colour algebra representations from the irreducible representations for Lie superalgebras. This bijection is obtained by applying the loop module construction iteratively to cyclic groups of prime order in the Jordan–Hölder decomposition of the grading group. Restricting to cyclic groups of prime order in this way greatly simplifies the construction. Despite the bijection between Lie colour algebra representations and Lie superalgebra representations, Lie colour algebras maintain a non-trivial representation theory distinct from that of Lie superalgebras. We demonstrate the applicability of the loop module construction to Lie colour algebras in three examples: a Hilbert space for a quantum mechanical model, representations of a colour version of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} and representations of a class of ℤ 2 × ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} -graded colour algebras of type D.

2.
Lie colour algebras and their representations

A Lie colour algebra generalises a Lie superalgebra to grading by some abelian group
\Gamma
(instead of just
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
).
Note that we will use additive notation for abelian groups throughout this paper.
For the formal definition of a Lie colour algebra, we refer the reader to
[
44
]
.
As an illustrative example:
if
\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
is an associative algebra such that
\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}\mathscr{R}_{\beta}\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{\alpha+\beta}
then
\mathscr{R}
can be given the structure of a Lie colour algebra with bracket

\cbrak{x}{y}=xy-\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)yx,\qquad x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha},\,y\in\mathscr{R}_{\beta}.

Here,
\varepsilon\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{F}
(where
\mathbb{F}
is the field of scalars) is called a commutation factor
[
44
]
(or antisymmetric bicharacter
[
64
]
, or phase function
[
48
]
).
That this generalizes a Lie superalgebra is apparent:
a Lie superalgebra is simply a Lie colour algebra graded by the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with commutation factor
\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha\cdot\beta}
.
Note that, in general, a commutation factor must satisfy certain conditions (see
[
44
]
).
Every Lie colour algebra is contained in a
\Gamma
-graded associative algebra:
we can construct the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
in the usual way
and prove that
\mathfrak{g}
embeds inside
U(\mathfrak{g})
using a generalised version of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem; see
[
44
, Section 4]
for more details.

If
\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)=1
or
\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)=-1

then we will often write
\cbrak{x}{y}=\commutator{x}{y}
or
\cbrak{x}{y}=\anticommutator{x}{y}
(respectively).
This is analogous to the notation used for Lie superalgebras.

We can define notions such as homomorphisms, subalgebras and representations similarly to superalgebras.
However, to retain information about the
\Gamma
-grading, we require some maps
(such as homomorphisms, intertwiners, and inclusion maps of subalgebras and
\Gamma
-graded submodules)
to be homogeneous of degree
0
, as per the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}
and
W=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
be vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}
.
A linear map
f\colon V\to W
is
homogeneous of degree
\gamma\in\Gamma
if

f(V_{\alpha})\subseteq W_{\alpha+\gamma}
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
.
(In particular,
f
is a homogeneous element of the Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V,\varepsilon)
, see
[
44
]
.)

2.1.
Representations

Although representations can be defined similarly to those for superalgebras, we make an important distinction between graded and ungraded representations.

Definition 2.2
.

An
ungraded representation
of a Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
is a linear map
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\End(V)

satisfying
\rho(\cbrak{x}{y})=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)\rho(y)\rho(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
,
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
.
A
\Gamma
-graded representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
on a
\Gamma
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}
is an ungraded representation that also satisfies

\rho(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})V_{\gamma}\subseteq V_{\alpha+\gamma}
.
We call
V_{\gamma}
the
\gamma
-sector

and the elements of
V_{\gamma}
are said to have
degree
\gamma
.
In both the ungraded and graded cases, we say that
V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module given by
\rho
(or simply a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
).
We will often omit the representation map
\rho
.

In the majority of this paper,
we will instead work with representations of

\Gamma
-graded associative algebras,
i.e. representations
\rho\colon\mathscr{R}\to\End(V)
where

\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
is an associative algebra
that satisfies
\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}\mathscr{R}_{\beta}\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{\alpha+\beta}
.
Such a representation is
graded

if
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}

and
\rho(\mathscr{R}_{\alpha})V_{\gamma}\subseteq V_{\alpha+\gamma}
.
In fact, Lie colour algebra representations
are a special case of representations for a graded associative algebra:
the category of ungraded (resp. graded) representations of a Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the category of ungraded (resp. graded) representations of
its universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
.
This follows immediately from the universal property of
U(\mathfrak{g})

[
44
, Proposition 1]
and the fact that
\mathfrak{g}\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
generates
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

We do not require maps related to ungraded representations (such as intertwiners and submodules) to be homogeneous of degree
0
,
but we do require this condition of the corresponding maps for graded representations.

For
\mathscr{R}
a
\Gamma
-graded associative ring (such as
U(\mathfrak{g})
),
we call a (
\Gamma
-graded)
\mathscr{R}
-module
(
\Gamma
-graded) irreducible
if it has no proper non-trivial (
\Gamma
-graded) submodules.
Note that there is an important distinction between the definitions of (ungraded) irreducible and
\Gamma
-graded irreducible: a module may be
\Gamma
-graded irreducible but not ungraded irreducible!

Recall that an
\mathscr{R}
-module is called completely reducible if it can be written as a direct sum of irreducible submodules.
Weyl’s Theorem on complete reducibility does not hold for semisimple Lie colour algebras (indeed, Weyl’s Theorem does not hold for semisimple Lie superalgebras
[
66
]
).
As such, we need to manually determine when an
\mathscr{R}
-module is completely reducible.
The following propositions will be of great help.

Proposition 2.3
.

Consider a (
\Gamma
-graded or ungraded)
\mathscr{R}
-module
V
that can be written as
V=\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}
for irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-modules
V_{i}
(
i\in I
).
Then, there exists some subset
J\subseteq I
such that
V=\bigoplus_{i\in J}V_{i}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Submodules and quotient modules of a (
\Gamma
-graded or ungraded) completely reducible
\mathscr{R}
-module are completely reducible.

These propositions are well-known facts from the module theory of rings (see e.g.
[
67
, Chapter XVII, Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.2]
).

2.2.
Graded quotient modules

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}
be a
\Gamma
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module given by the representation
\rho
and
U=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}U_{\gamma}
a
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
V
(so that
U_{\gamma}\subseteq V_{\gamma}
).

Definition 2.5
.

The
graded quotient module of
V
by
U
,
denoted
V/U
, is a direct sum of quotient vector spaces
\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}/U_{\gamma}
with
\mathscr{R}
-module structure given by
\pi\colon\mathscr{R}\to\End(V/U)

defined by

\pi(x)(v+U_{\gamma})=\rho(x)v+U_{\alpha+\gamma}\qquad\text{for}~x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha},\,v\in V_{\gamma}.

The proof that the above representation is well defined is similar to that for superalgebras.
The only additional property to check is whether
\pi
is homogeneous of degree
0
, which is easily verified.

The three isomorphism theorems hold for
\mathscr{R}
-modules.
Again, the proofs are similar to superalgebras, needing only to verify that all maps are homogeneous of degree
0
.

2.3.
Discolouration

Scheunert
[
44
]
gave a bijection between Lie colour algebras and graded Lie superalgebras and between their representations.
This bijection has many nice properties; for example, it preserves subalgebras, ideals and subrepresentations.
To compute the image of a colour algebra under this bijection, we deform the bracket using a multiplier.

Given a Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{g}

and a multiplier
\sigma\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}

(for the definition, see
[
44
]
; also called a multiplicative
2
-cocycle
[
36
]
),
we can define a new bracket
\cbrak{\cdot}{\cdot}^{\sigma}
by

\cbrak{x}{y}^{\sigma}=\sigma(\alpha,\beta)\cbrak{x}{y},\qquad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}.

We can choose this multiplier
\sigma
so that
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with
\cbrak{\cdot}{\cdot}^{\sigma}
forms a Lie superalgebra
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
.
We denote this Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
.
The map
\mathfrak{g}\mapsto\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}

is a bijection between the Lie colour algebras with commutation factor
\varepsilon

and the
\Gamma
-graded Lie superalgebras
[
44
]
.
As such, we call
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
a
discolouration
of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Discolouration is an invertible procedure, and we will call
(\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma})^{1/\sigma}=\mathfrak{g}
the
recolouration
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
.

Note that there is an alternative method of discolouration which works by adding new elements, called Klein operators, to the universal enveloping algebra (see
[
47
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
).
However, for the remainder of this paper we will use the original discolouration process of
[
44
]
described in this section.

It is also possible to discolour representations.
Given a graded representation
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V,\varepsilon)

and a discolouring multiplier
\sigma\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
,
we can define the discolouration
of
\rho
to be the graded representation
\rho^{\sigma}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
given by

\rho^{\sigma}(x)v=\sigma(\alpha,\gamma)\rho(x)v,\qquad\text{for}~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},v\in V_{\gamma}.

Similarly, the map
\rho\mapsto\rho^{\sigma}
is a bijection between
the graded representations of
\mathfrak{g}

and the graded representations of the corresponding discoloured
\Gamma
-graded Lie superalgebra
[
44
]
.
Thus, the representation theory of Lie colour algebras
can be completely derived from that of
\Gamma
-graded Lie superalgebras.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded irreducible representations of Lie superalgebras have been well studied.
However, these results cannot be immediately applied to find
\Gamma
-graded irreducible representations.
For instance, it might not be possible to find a
\Gamma
-grading for a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded representation.
On the other hand,
there might be a
\Gamma
-graded irreducible representation
that becomes reducible when considered as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded representation.

3.
loop module

Throughout the remainder of this paper,
we will assume that
\Gamma
is a finite abelian group
and that the ambient field
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
.

Let
\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathscr{R}_{\gamma}
be a
\Gamma
-graded associative algebra and let
H
be a subgroup of the abelian group
\Gamma
.
Notice that
\mathscr{R}
has a natural
\Gamma/H
grading, given by

\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}\mathscr{R}^{\Gamma/H}_{\Lambda}
where
\mathscr{R}^{\Gamma/H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
.

Definition 3.1

(Mazorchuk–Zhao
[
60
]
; see also
[
62
]
)
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}V_{\Lambda}
be a
\Gamma/H
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module
given by
\rho
.
Recall that the group algebra
\mathbb{F}\Gamma
is the algebra with basis
\{e_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}

and algebra product defined by
e_{\alpha}\cdot e_{\beta}=e_{\alpha+\beta}
.
The vector space
V\otimes\mathbb{F}\Gamma
can be given an
\mathscr{R}
-module structure by

x(v\otimes e_{\beta})=xv\otimes e_{\alpha+\beta}

for
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
and
v\otimes e_{\beta}\in V\otimes\mathbb{F}\Gamma
.
The
Loop module of
V
by
H
is the
\Gamma
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma+H}\otimes e_{\gamma}\subset V\otimes\mathbb{F}\Gamma.

The
\gamma
-sector of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}_{\gamma}=V_{\gamma+H}\otimes e_{\gamma}
.

Not every
\Gamma/H
-graded module
V
can be given a
\Gamma
-grading.
Intuitively,
the loop module adds in multiple copies of
V
to ensure that there is enough ‘space’ for a refined
\Gamma
-grading.
If
V
can be given a
\Gamma
-grading, then the loop module contains
V
:

Proposition 3.2
.

If
V
can be given a
\Gamma
-grading that refines the
\Gamma/H
-grading,
then
V
is a
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

Proof.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
be the
\Gamma
-grading,
with
V_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}W_{\gamma}
for all
\Lambda\in\Gamma/H

(i.e. the
\Gamma
-grading refines the
\Gamma/H
grading).
Define a map
\Phi\colon V\to\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
by
\Phi(w)=w\otimes e_{\gamma}
for
w\in W_{\gamma}

and extending to inhomogeneous elements by linearity.
Linearity and injectivity of
\Phi
follow immediately.
The map
\Phi
is homogeneous of degree
0
by definition.
And
\Phi
is an intertwiner:
for
w\in W_{\gamma}
and
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
,

\Phi(xw)=xw\otimes e_{\alpha+\gamma}=x(w\otimes e_{\gamma})=x(\Phi(w))\qed

As well as a
\Gamma
-grading,

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
has a natural
\Gamma/H
-grading
given by

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}V_{\Lambda}\otimes e_{\gamma}\right).

Remark 3.3
.

If
\Gamma=K\times H
for some group
K
,
then we could equivalently define the loop module
to be
V\otimes\mathbb{F}[H]

with representation given by
x(v\otimes e_{h})=xv\otimes(e_{h+\eta})

for
x\in\mathscr{R}_{(\kappa,\eta)}
and
v\otimes e_{h}\in V\otimes\mathbb{F}[H]
.
Compare with
[
61
]
, where this construction, together with a twist by a multiplier
\sigma
on
\mathbb{F}[H]
, is called an
enhancement
.
The enhancement approach is used in
[
36
]
and
[
61
]
to study the structure of simple Lie colour algebras themselves (rather than their modules).
An enhancement of a Lie colour algebra is similar to the loop algebra construction studied elsewhere
[
62
,
60
]
.

3.1.
loop module and quantum mechanical systems

A common way to obtain a
\Gamma
-graded colour supersymmetric quantum mechanical system is by modifying an existing supersymmetric system.
This is the approach taken in (for example)
[
23
]
, where
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded supersymmetric system
presented in
[
23
, Section III]

can be obtained by introducing an additional central charge to Witten’s model
[
68
, Section 6]
.
In
[
23
]
, the Hilbert space realisation for this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded system was constructed independently from that of Witten’s model.
In this section, we will demonstrate that
this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hilbert space realisation can be constructed from Witten’s realisation using a loop module.
In this way, the loop module has already appeared in the Lie colour algebra literature and is a natural structure to consider in the study of Lie colour algebras.

Witten considered
[
68
, Section 6]
a superalgebra with even sector spanned by a Hamiltonian
H
and odd sector spanned by two supercharges
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
satisfying

\commutator{Q_i}{H}=0\qquad\anticommutator{Q_i}{Q_j}=\delta_{ij}H

for
i,j=1,2
.
This algebra was realised as operators acting on two component Pauli spinors in the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
via

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(p^{2}+W^{2}(x)+\hbar\sigma_{3}\derivative{W}{x}\right),

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{1}p+\sigma_{2}W(x)),

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{2}p-\sigma_{1}W(x))

where
\sigma_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) are the Pauli matrices,
W\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
p=-i\hbar\derivative*{x}
is the momentum operator.
This Hilbert space representation is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded representation of the superalgebra, with grading given by
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{C}_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{1}

(that is, the first component of the spinor has an even grade and the second component has an odd grade).

By adding a central charge,

Z=-\frac{i}{2}\left(\sigma_{3}(p^{2}+W^{2}(x))+\hbar\derivative{W}{x}\right)

the original superalgebra becomes a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie colour algebra (without requiring recolouration) with commutation factor
\varepsilon(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2})=(-1)^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}
for
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\,\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{00,01,10,11\}
and with sectors spanned by

00\text{-sector: }H\qquad 01\text{-sector: }Q_{1}\qquad 11\text{-sector: }Z\qquad 10\text{-sector: }Q_{2}

and satisfying the following additional relations

\commutator{Q_2}{Q_1}=Z\qquad\anticommutator{Q_i}{Q_i}=H

while all other Lie colour brackets vanish.
We can easily verify that
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}
becomes an ungraded module of the Lie colour algebra.

Taking the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt{(\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}})}
turns
\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}
into a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded module.
Here, we use
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\{00,11\}\leq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Set

\mathscr{H}_{00}=\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}_{0}\otimes e_{00}\qquad\mathscr{H}_{11}=\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}_{0}\otimes e_{11}\qquad\mathscr{H}_{01}=\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}_{1}\otimes e_{01}\qquad\mathscr{H}_{10}=\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}}_{1}\otimes e_{10}.

Then, the loop module is
\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt{(\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}})}=\mathscr{H}_{00}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{01}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{11}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{10}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{4}
(note the order of the sectors).
We can then explicitly compute the representation on four-component spinors:

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(H)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}H&0\\
0&H\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Q_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{1}&0\\
0&Q_{1}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Q_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{2}\\
Q_{2}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Z)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&Z\\
Z&0\end{pmatrix}

where
\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
is the representation for
\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt{(\mathscr{H}^{\mathrm{W}})}
.
Setting
H_{00}=2\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(H),\,Q_{01}=\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Q_{1}),\,Q_{10}=\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Q_{2})
and
Z_{11}=2\rho^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(Z)
, we obtain the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-supersymmetry model in
[
23
, Section III]
(with
m=1/2
).

The above example demonstrates that the loop module provides a natural method for constructing the Hilbert space of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded supersymmetry model from a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded supersymmetry model.
The loop module can be applied to other colour supersymmetric models constructed in a similar way.

3.2.
Loop module and irreducibility

Under the assumptions outlined at the beginning of this section,
and for
\mathscr{R}
a
\Gamma
-graded associative algebra,

[
60
]
showed that every
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module can be obtained from the non-graded ones via the loop module:

Theorem 3.4

(Mazorchuk–Zhao
[
60
, Theorem 24]
)
.

Let
W
be a
\Gamma
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module.
There exists a subgroup
H\leq\Gamma
and an ungraded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module
V
such that
W
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
as a
\Gamma
-graded module.

Note that
[
60
]
only proved the above result for graded Lie algebras,
but the same proof works for modules over any associative algebra.
A similar result
[
60
, Theorem 31]
is available if
\Gamma
is an infinite group,
but we will restrict our attention to the finite case in this paper.

The above theorem reduces the study of
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules to the study of ungraded modules.
In combination with discolouration,
this allows us to derive all such
\Gamma
-graded irreducible colour algebra representations from the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded irreducible superalgebra representations.

However, one difficulty with applying
Theorem

3.4
in practice is that the choice of
H
in the theorem is not necessarily unique
i.e. potentially
W\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H_{1}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
for different subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}\leq\Gamma
.
Determining when two loop modules are isomorphic is an open problem
[
60
, Problem 36]
.

Building on the work of
[
60
]
, it was shown that the loop module construction defines a functor and can be extended to an equivalence between two categories (see
[
63
]
for details).
Moreover, using Brauer invariants, it was shown that the loop module defines a bijection between equivalence classes of
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible and
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules
[
63
, Remark 7.2]
.

4.
Iterated loop module bijection

In this section, we will
present a bijection between equivalence classes of
\Gamma/H
- and
\Gamma
-graded irreducible representations, similar to that of
[
63
]
.
However, instead of using Brauer invariants, we will provide an alternate iterative approach.
Given a finite abelian grading group
\Gamma
, we suggest using the Jordan–Hölder decomposition

(1)

\Gamma\rhd N_{\ell}\rhd N_{\ell-1}\rhd\cdots\rhd N_{1}\rhd\{0\}

and looking at submodules of the following iterated loop module

(2)

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{N_{1}/\{0\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(\cdots\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma/N_{\ell-1}}_{N_{\ell}/N_{\ell-1}}\hskip-1.0pt{(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma/N_{\ell}}_{\Gamma/N_{\ell}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})}\cdots)}.

(Note that we are making use of the isomorphism
\Gamma/N_{\ell}\cong(\Gamma/N_{\ell-1})/(N_{\ell}/N_{\ell-1})
(for instance)).
Restricting the loop module construction to cyclic groups of prime order greatly simplifies the possible options.

Our strategy is as follows:
we first prove that every finite-dimensional
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module appears as a quotient of a loop module (
Lemma

4.1
).
The loop module is completely reducible both as a
\Gamma/H
- and
\Gamma
-graded module (
Lemmas

4.5
and
4.10
respectively) and we explicitly provide the direct sum decompositions.
Comparing these direct sum decompositions limits the possible
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules to either
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible modules or their loop modules.
We use these results to construct the bijection between
\Gamma/H
- and
\Gamma
-graded modules (
Theorem

4.15
) up to equivalence defined by the irreducible direct summands.

4.1.
Quotient modules of the loop module

As in
Section

3
,
let
\mathscr{R}
be a
\Gamma
-graded associative algebra
and
V
be a finite-dimensional
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module.

In general, the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}

will not be
\Gamma
-graded irreducible.
However, to construct such a
\Gamma
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module, we can simply quotient out by a maximal
\Gamma
-graded submodule.
Since
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
may have many maximal
\Gamma
-graded submodules, this procedure may yield many nonisomorphic
\Gamma
-graded modules for each maximal
\Gamma
-graded submodule.
Regardless, we will show that every
\Gamma
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module can be constructed in this way.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathscr{R}_{\gamma}
be an associative algebra.
Let
W=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
be a finite-dimensional
\Gamma
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module.
Then, for any
H\leq\Gamma
, there exists a
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module
V

such that
W
is a graded quotient of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

Proof.

Note that
W
has a natural
\Gamma/H
-grading given by

\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}\left(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}W_{\gamma}\right).

Let
V
be a
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible submodule of
W
.
Such a
V
exists by strong induction on the dimension of
W
.
(See also
[
60
, Lemma 23]
.)

Take
v\in V_{\Lambda}
and write

(3)

v=\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}w_{\gamma}

for
w_{\gamma}\in W_{\gamma}
.
For
\gamma\in\Lambda
, let
\pi_{\gamma}\colon V_{\Lambda}\to W_{\gamma}

be the projection of
V_{\Lambda}
onto
W_{\gamma}
,

\pi_{\gamma}(v)=w_{\gamma}.

Note that
\pi_{\gamma}
is well-defined because the linear combination in (
3
) is unique due to the direct sum
V_{\Lambda}\subseteq\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}W_{\gamma}
.
Additionally, it is easy to see that
\pi_{\gamma}
is linear.

Note that,
for
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
,
we have
xv=\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}x\pi_{\gamma}(v)
.
Since
x\pi_{\gamma}(v)\in W_{\alpha+\gamma}
and
such a linear combination of homogeneous elements is unique, we have that
\pi_{\alpha+\gamma}(xv)=x\pi_{\gamma}(v)
by the definition of
\pi_{\alpha+\gamma}
.

Define a map
\Phi\colon\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\to W
by

\Phi(v\otimes e_{\gamma})=\pi_{\gamma}(v).

Then

x\Phi(v\otimes e_{\gamma})=x\pi_{\gamma}(v)=\pi_{\alpha+\gamma}(xv)=\Phi(x(v\otimes e_{\gamma}))

so
\Phi
is an intertwiner.

Therefore,
by the First Isomorphism Theorem,

\im\Phi\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}/\ker\Phi
.
But
\im\Phi
is a
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
W
,
so
\im\Phi=0
or
\im\Phi=W
by irreducibility of
W
.
However,
since
V\neq 0
,
and
\Phi
is defined using projections,
we clearly have
\im\Phi\neq 0
.
We conclude that
\im\Phi=W
;
hence
W\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}/\ker\Phi
as claimed.
∎

Lemma

4.1
provides us with a general method of obtaining the
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules from the
\Gamma/H
-graded ones; namely find all maximal
\Gamma
-graded submodules of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
and take quotients.
Note that we can first fix
H
and then find all
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules.
Compare this with
Theorem

3.4
, where we must choose a different
H
for each
\Gamma
-graded module.

4.2.
\Gamma/H
-grading: loop module is completely reducible

The loop module is a completely reducible
\Gamma/H
-graded module.
It is shown in
[
58
,
60
]
that
the irreducible direct summands are isomorphic to
V
as vector spaces, but the
\mathscr{R}
-action is twisted by an element of the character group
\widehat{\Gamma}
.
These direct summands will restrict which quotients are possible,
and hence which
\Gamma
-graded irreducible representations are possible in
Lemma

4.1
.
We will now briefly recount these results.

Let
\widehat{\Gamma}
be the character group:
the group of all homomorphisms
\widehat{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
with pointwise multiplication the group operation.
Our earlier assumption that
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed ensures that
\widehat{\Gamma}\cong\Gamma
.
Let
H^{\perp}=\{f\in\widehat{\Gamma}\mid f(\eta)=1~\text{for all}~\eta\in H\}
.
Note that since
\Gamma
is finite,
\absolutevalue*{\pdualtwist}=\absolutevalue{H}
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}V_{\Lambda}
be a

\Gamma/H
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module with representation
\rho\colon\mathscr{R}\to\End(V)
.
For each
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}
,
we define the twisted module
V^{f}
to be equal to
V
as a vector space,
but with the following twisted representation
\rho^{f}\colon\mathscr{R}\to\End(V^{f})

\rho^{f}(x)v=f(\alpha)\rho(x)v\qquad\text{for}~x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha},\,v\in V.

Note that
\rho^{f}
depends on the choice of
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}
,
but if
f
and
f^{\prime}
belong to the same coset in
\widehat{\Gamma}/H^{\perp}

then
\rho^{f}
and
\rho^{f^{\prime}}
are isomorphic
(that is,
fH^{\perp}
defines
\rho^{f}
up to isomorphism).
Note also that the definition of
\rho^{f}
depends only on the grading of
\mathscr{R}

and
not
on the grading of
V

(indeed, the definition makes sense even if
V
is ungraded).

Remark 4.2
.

\Gamma
-graded modules over
\mathscr{R}
are equivalent to modules over the smash product
\mathscr{R}\#\mathbb{F}\widehat{\Gamma}
(see
[
58
]
); that is,
\Gamma
-graded is equivalent to
\widehat{\Gamma}
-invariant.
Furthermore,
\Gamma/H
-graded modules are equivalent to modules over the smash product

\mathscr{R}\#\mathbb{F}H^{\perp}\subset\mathscr{R}\#\mathbb{F}\widehat{\Gamma}.

With this set-up, it is natural to consider the induced module
\Ind_{H^{\perp}}^{\widehat{\Gamma}}V=V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}H^{\perp}}\mathbb{F}\widehat{\Gamma}
which can be given a
\Gamma
-grading.
(Under our assumptions, this induced module is isomorphic to the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}

[
63
, Proposition 3.8]
.)
With this interpretation, we could reframe the results of this section (
Section

4
) in terms of Clifford theory.
For the relevant Clifford theory in terms of gradings, we refer the reader to
[
58
]
.

For later use, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
H\leq\Gamma
be cyclic of order
n
.
The irreducible
\Gamma/H
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module
V
can be given a
\Gamma
-grading that refines the
\Gamma/H
-grading
if and only if
V^{f}\cong V
as
\Gamma/H
-graded modules for every
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}
.

Note that the above proposition does not hold in general:
if
H
were an arbitrary group
then we would also need graded Schur indices
[
58
, Definition 4]

to characterise when
V
is
\Gamma
-gradable.

Proof.

For the forward direction,
assume that
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
is a
\Gamma
-grading.
Then it is easily verified that the linear map defined by

v\mapsto f(\xi)v

(for
\xi
-graded
v
),
is an isomorphism
V\to V^{f}
.

For the reverse direction,
choose
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}

such that
f|_{H}
is a generator for
\widehat{H}
.
Let
\Psi\colon V\to V^{f}
be an isomorphism.
Define
\Phi\colon V\to V^{f^{n}}
by

\Phi(v)=(f(\gamma))^{n}v

where
v
has degree
(\gamma+H)\in\Gamma/H
.
This map
\Phi

is well-defined
since
f^{n}\in H^{\perp}

(recall
\absolutevalue*{\pdualtwist}=n
).
As in the forward direction of the proof,

\Phi
is an isomorphism.

By the graded Schur’s lemma
[
60
, Theorem 14]
,
we have that
\Psi^{n}=\lambda\Phi
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
.
Without loss of generality, we can rescale
\Psi
so that
\Psi^{n}=\Phi
.

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define

P_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\gamma))^{-j}\Psi^{j}.

Let
h\in H
.
Restrict the domain
to
V_{\gamma+H}
:
using the fact that
\Psi^{n+1}=\Phi\Psi=(f(\gamma))^{n}\Psi
on
V_{\gamma+H}

we find that
\Psi P_{\gamma}=f(\gamma)P_{\gamma}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle P_{\gamma+h}P_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\gamma+h))^{-j}(f(\gamma))^{j}P_{\gamma}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(h))^{-j}\right)P_{\gamma}.

We know that
f(h)
is an
n
-th root of unity.
Moreover, since

f|_{H}
generates
\widehat{H}
,
we find that
f(\eta)
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity
for any generator
\eta
of
H
,
and hence
f(h)=1
if and only if
h=0
.
Consequently,

(4)

P_{\gamma+h}P_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}P_{\gamma}&\text{if}~h=0\\
0&\text{if}~h\neq 0.\end{cases}

In particular, notice that
P_{\gamma}
is a projection operator.

Additionally,

\sum_{h\in H}P_{\gamma+h}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{h\in H}(f(\gamma+h))^{-j}\Psi^{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{h\in H}(f(h))^{-j}\right)(f(\gamma))^{-j}\Psi^{j}.

Since
f|_{H}
generates
\widehat{H}
,
we know that
f^{j}|_{H}\neq 1
for
0<j<n
.
In particular,

f^{j}(\eta)\neq 1
for any generator
\eta
of
H
.
Since, moreover,
f^{j}(\eta)
is an
n
-th root of unity,
we find that
\sum_{h\in H}(f(h))^{-j}=0
for
0<j<n
.
Therefore,

(5)

\sum_{h\in H}P_{\gamma+h}=(f(\gamma))^{-n}\Psi^{n}=\Id.

Setting,
W_{\gamma}=P_{\gamma}(V_{\gamma+H})
,
the equations (
4
) and (
5
) imply that

V_{\gamma+H}=\bigoplus_{h\in H}W_{\gamma+h}

and hence
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
.
We claim that this is a
\Gamma
-grading for
V
.
Indeed, let
\rho\colon\mathscr{R}\to\End(V)
be the representation
and take
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}

and
v\in W_{\gamma}
; then

\displaystyle P_{\alpha+\gamma}(\rho(x)v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\alpha+\gamma))^{-j}\Psi^{j}(\rho(x)v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\alpha+\gamma))^{-j}\rho^{f^{j}}(x)\Psi^{j}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\alpha+\gamma))^{-j}(f(\alpha))^{j}\rho(x)\Psi^{j}(v)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)P_{\gamma}v

\displaystyle=\rho(x)v.

That is
\rho(x)v\in W_{\alpha+\gamma}

as required.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Given an isomorphism
\Psi\colon V\to V^{f}
,
we can use the projection operators

P_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f(\gamma))^{-j}\Psi^{j}

used in the above proof
to explicitly calculate
the
\Gamma
-grading
of an irreducible
\Gamma/H
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module
V
.

Using the twisted modules
V^{f}
,
we can give a direct sum decomposition of the loop module
as a
\Gamma/H
-graded module:

Lemma 4.5

(Elduque–Kochetov
[
58
, section 3.3]
; see also, Mazorchuk–Zhao
[
60
, Lemma 20]
)
.

Let
H
be a finite
group and
V=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}V_{\Lambda}
be a
\Gamma/H
-graded module of an associative algebra
\mathscr{R}
over
\mathbb{F}
.
Assume that
\mathbb{F}
has a primitive root of unity of order
\absolutevalue{\Gamma}
.
As a
\Gamma/H
-graded module, the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{fH^{\perp}\in\widehat{\Gamma}/H^{\perp}}V^{f}.

(The direct sum ranges over
f
in some transversal of
\widehat{\Gamma}/H^{\perp}

so that there are
\absolutevalue*{\pdualtwist}=\absolutevalue*{H}
direct summands.)

If we assume that
V
is irreducible,
then
Lemma

4.5
(together with
Proposition

4.6

(i)
below) shows that
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is completely reducible as a
\Gamma/H
-graded module and gives a direct sum decomposition.
In combination with
Lemma

4.1
,
this restricts the possible structures of the
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules.

We now prove two more important properties of
V^{f}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The following two properties hold:

(i)

If
V
is
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible then
V^{f}
is
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible.

(ii)

There is an equality of
\mathscr{R}
-modules:
(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{f}=\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V^{f})}
.

Remark 4.7
.

Property
(i)
has previously been proven in
[
58
, Section 3.1]
.
A result analogous to property
(ii)
for induced modules (see
Remark

4.2
) follows immediately from the definition.

Proof.

Observe that
\rho(\mathscr{R})U\subseteq U
if and only if
\rho^{f}(\mathscr{R})U\subseteq U
for any submodule
U
of
V
or
V^{f}
.
Property
(i)
follows.

For
(ii)
,

(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{f}
equals
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V^{f})}
as a vector space
and both of these modules have the same representation:
for
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
and
v\in V_{\gamma+H}
,

x\cdot(v\otimes e_{\gamma})=f(\alpha)\rho(x)v\otimes e_{\gamma}.\qed

It is clear from the definition and the above propositions that
V^{f}
has a very similar module structure to
V
.
This will be useful when examining the structure of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

4.3.
\Gamma
-grading: loop module is completely reducible

We will now show that the loop module is completely reducible as a
\Gamma
-graded module.
Similar to the
\Gamma/H
-graded case, the
\Gamma
-graded irreducible direct summands all have a similar structure: they are all (what we will call)
H
-parity shifts of the same
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module.
A
H
-parity shift is obtained by simply applying a circular shift to each of the sectors.

Comparing the
\Gamma
- and
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible direct summands will give us more information about the structure of the loop module.
This will allow us to conclude that the only possible
\Gamma
-graded direct summands of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
are
V
with a
\Gamma
-grading or
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
itself.

4.3.1.
Direct sum decomposition

Take some
h\in H
and a
\Gamma
-graded module
W
.
We can define the
H
-parity shift of
W
by
h

as a new
\Gamma
-graded module
W^{+h}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W^{+h}_{\gamma}
,
where
W^{+h}_{\gamma}=W_{\gamma+h}
.
Obviously, a
H
-parity shift of
W
is still a
\Gamma
-graded
\mathscr{R}
-module.

Following this definition, we make four immediate observations:

Proposition 4.8
.

The following properties hold:

(i)

For any
\xi\in\Gamma
,
we have
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V^{+(\xi+H)})}\cong(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+\xi}

as
\Gamma
-graded modules.

(ii)

For any
h\in H
,
the
H
-parity shift

(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+h}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

(iii)

If
W
is
\Gamma
-graded irreducible then
W^{+h}
is
\Gamma
-graded irreducible.

(iv)

The
\mathscr{R}
-modules
W
and
W^{+h}
are isomorphic as
\Gamma/H
-graded modules.

Proof.

For
(i)
,
the
\gamma
-sector of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V^{+(\xi+H)})}

is
V_{\gamma+\xi+H}\otimes\mathbb{F}e_{\gamma}

and the
\gamma
-sector of
(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+\xi}

is
V_{\gamma+\xi+H}\otimes\mathbb{F}e_{\gamma+\xi}
.
Thus, we can easily verify
the linear map
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V^{+(\xi+H)})}\to(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+\xi}
defined by
v\otimes e_{\gamma}\mapsto v\otimes e_{\gamma+\xi}

is a
\Gamma
-graded isomorphism.

Result
(ii)

is a special case of
(i)
.
The proofs of
(iii)
and
(iv)
are immediate.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
V
be a
\Gamma/H
-graded module and
W
be a
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
Then
W^{+h}
can be embedded in
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

Proof.

Define a map
\Phi_{h}\colon(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+h}\to\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
by

\Phi_{h}(v\otimes e_{\gamma+h})=v\otimes e_{\gamma}

for
v\otimes e_{\gamma+h}\in(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+h}_{\gamma}
and extending to inhomogeneous elements by linearity.
The restriction
\Phi_{h}|_{W}

is then an embedding of
W^{+h}
into
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
H\leq\Gamma
and
V=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\in\Gamma/H}V_{\Lambda}
be a finite-dimensional
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible module of an associative algebra
\mathscr{R}
over
\mathbb{F}
.
Then, the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is completely reducible as a
\Gamma
-graded module with

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\cong\bigoplus_{h\in K}W^{+h}

for any
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module
W\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}

and some subset
K\subseteq H
.

Proof.

Take any
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module
W\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
Such a module exists because
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is finite-dimensional.

Define a map
\Pi\colon\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\to V
by

\Pi(v\otimes e_{\gamma})=v

for
v\otimes e_{\gamma}\in\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}_{\gamma}

and extending to inhomogeneous elements by linearity.
We claim that
\Pi
is a
\Gamma/H
-graded intertwiner.
That
\Pi
is linear and homogeneous of degree
0
is obvious.
For
x\in\mathscr{R}_{\alpha}
, we have

\Pi(x(v\otimes e_{\gamma}))=\Pi(xv\otimes e_{\alpha+\gamma})=xv=x\Pi(v\otimes e_{\gamma})

which proves the claim.

The fact that
\Pi
is an intertwiner implies that
\Pi(W)
is a
\Gamma/H
-graded submodule of
V
.
But since
W\neq 0
,
we must have that
\Pi(W)\neq 0
.
Since
V
is
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible, we must have that
\Pi(W)=V
.

Now, consider the module
\Sigma_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W^{+h})\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}

(where
\Phi_{h}
embeds
W^{+h}
inside
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
as in the proof of
Proposition

4.9
).
In particular, consider the
\gamma
-sector

\sum_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W^{+h}_{\gamma})
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Applying the map
\Pi
,

\Pi\left(\sum_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W^{+h}_{\gamma})\right)=\Pi\left(\bigoplus_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W_{\gamma+h})\right)=V_{\gamma+H}

using the fact
\Pi(W)=V
.
By examining the definition of
\Pi
, we find that
\sum_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W^{+h}_{\gamma})=V_{\gamma+H}\otimes e_{\gamma}=\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}_{\gamma}
.
Therefore,
\sum_{h\in H}\Phi_{h}(W^{+h})=\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.

All that remains to show is that we can take a subset of
H
to obtain a direct sum.
But this follows from
Proposition

2.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
H\leq\Gamma
be a cyclic group of prime order
p

and let
V
be
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible.
Every
\Gamma
-graded irreducible submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is isomorphic as a
\Gamma/H
-graded submodule to either
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
or
V
.

Proof.

Take
W
a
\Gamma
-graded irreducible submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
By
Lemma

4.10
,

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\cong\bigoplus_{h\in K}W^{+h}.

Since
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is a completely reducible
\Gamma/H
-graded module, we know that
W
is a completely reducible
\Gamma/H
-graded module with direct summands contained in those of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
By
Lemma

4.5
,
W
is a direct sum of character twists
V^{f}
(
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}
).
Let
n
be the number of
\Gamma/H
-graded direct summands in
W
.

Then, the number of direct summands in
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
as a
\Gamma/H
-graded module is equal to
n\absolutevalue{K}
.
But, from
Lemma

4.5
, the number of direct summands is equal to
\absolutevalue*{\pdualtwist}=\absolutevalue{H}=p
.
Therefore,
n=1
or
n=p
.
If
n=p
then
W\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
If
n=1
then
W
is isomorphic to
V^{f}
for some

.
But then
W
gives
V^{f}
a
\Gamma
-grading,
so
W\cong V^{f}\cong V
by
Proposition

4.3
.
∎

In combination with
Lemma

4.1
,

Theorem

4.11
tells us that every
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module appears as either a
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible module or the loop module of one.
This result will greatly aid in the search for graded irreducible representations.
Noting that
V
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma/H}_{\{0\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
,

Theorem

4.11
is a similar result as
[
60
, Theorem 24]
(restated in
Theorem

3.4
):
every
\Gamma
-graded module is a loop module.
However, there are two important distinctions between
Theorem

4.11
and
[
60
, Theorem 24]
:
restricting

to a cyclic group of prime order greatly restricts the possible loop modules,
and
H
does not rely on the choice of
V
.

One difficulty remains: finding a
\Gamma
-grade for a
\Gamma/H
-graded module can be difficult in general.
For some results about the gradability of modules see
[
58
]
.
To aid with this, we prove a uniqueness result for the
\Gamma
-grade.

4.3.2.
Uniqueness of the
\Gamma
-grade

It is possible for two modules to be non-isomorphic as
\Gamma
-graded modules but isomorphic as
\Gamma/H
-graded modules.
This is important to keep in mind when trying to find a
\Gamma
-grading for a
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible module; there might be multiple
different

\Gamma
-gradings, all of which must be considered.
However, the following proposition gives a uniqueness result for the
\Gamma
-grade.

Proposition 4.12
.

If
V
is a finite-dimensional
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-module, then any two
\Gamma
-gradings for
V
are equivalent up to
H
-parity shift.

More precisely, this proposition tells us that if
we have two
\mathscr{R}
-modules

V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{V}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{V}_{\gamma}

with
V=\widetilde{V}
as
\Gamma/H
-graded modules
(i.e. for each
\Lambda\in\Gamma/H
the
\Lambda
-sector of
V=\widetilde{V}
is
\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}V_{\gamma}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\widetilde{V}_{\gamma}
)
then there exists
\eta\in H
such that
V^{+\eta}\cong\widetilde{V}

as
\Gamma
-graded modules.

Proof.

Both
V=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}V_{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{V}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\widetilde{V}_{\gamma}
appear as
\Gamma
-graded irreducible submodules of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
by
Proposition

3.2
.
Since all
\Gamma
-graded irreducible direct summands of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
are
H
-parity shifts of
V
by
Lemma

4.10
,
the result follows.
∎

4.3.3.
Going backwards: from
\Gamma
-graded to
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible

So far, we have been focussing on constructing
\Gamma
-graded irreducible modules from
\Gamma/H
-graded modules.
But what if we want to go in the reverse direction?

The loop module of every
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible module
V
exists
and, by
Theorem

4.11
, every
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
contains
V
as a
\Gamma/H
-graded submodule.
That is, every
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible module appears as the submodule of a
\Gamma
-graded one.

To aid in finding
\Gamma/H
-graded submodules,
we can prove the analogue of
Proposition

4.3
for
H
-parity shifts:

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
W=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}W_{\gamma}
be a
\Gamma
-graded irreducible module
and
H
be cyclic of prime order.
The module
W
is
\Gamma/H
-graded reducible if and only if all
H
-parity shifts of
W
are
\Gamma
-graded isomorphic.

Proof.

For the forward direction, assume
W
is
\Gamma/H
-graded reducible.
By
Lemma

4.1
and
Theorem

4.11
, we must have that
W
is isomorphic to a loop module.
But,
by
Proposition

4.8

(ii)
,
all
H
-parity shifts of a loop module are
\Gamma
-graded isomorphic,
as required.

For the reverse direction,
suppose
W
is isomorphic to
W^{+h}
.
There exists some
\Gamma
-graded isomorphism
\Psi\colon W\to W^{+h}
.
This
\Psi
is a
\Gamma/H
-graded automorphism
(but
not
a
\Gamma
-graded automorphism).
This automorphism
\Psi
cannot be a scalar multiple of the identity,
since
W_{\gamma}\to W^{+h}_{\gamma}=W_{\gamma+h}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Therefore, by the graded Schur’s lemma
[
60
, Theorem 14]
,

W
must be
\Gamma/H
-graded reducible.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

We can give a formula for
\Gamma/H
-graded submodules in the above proposition.
If
\Psi\colon W\to W^{+h}
is an isomorphism such that
\Psi^{p}=\Id
as a linear map
(provided
h\neq 0
),
then a projection onto an irreducible
\Gamma/H
-graded submodule is given by

P=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi^{j}.

Indeed, let
\Phi_{-h}\colon\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}\to\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}^{+h}
be the isomorphism defined by

v\otimes e_{\xi}\mapsto v\otimes e_{\xi+h}

(c.f. the proof of
Proposition

4.9
).
By the above proposition,
W
is reducible and hence
W\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
for some
\Gamma/H
-graded
V
;
identify
W
with
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
.
Then
\Psi\circ\Phi^{-1}_{-h}\colon W\to W
is equal to
\lambda\Id
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
by Schur’s Lemma.
That is,
\Psi=\lambda\Phi_{-h}
.
The assumption
\Psi^{p}=\Id
is equivalent to
\lambda^{p}=1
.
Then
\lambda
is a
p
-th root of unity;
choose
f\in\widehat{\Gamma}
such that
f(h)=\lambda^{-1}

(such an
f
exists because
\absolutevalue*{\pdualtwist}=p
)
and define a linear map
\varphi\colon V^{f}\to P(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})

by
\varphi(v)=f(\gamma)^{-1}P(v\otimes e_{\gamma})

for
v\in V^{f}_{\gamma+H}
.
The fact that
\Psi=\lambda\Phi_{-h}
and the form of
P

both ensure that
\varphi
is well-defined.
It is easily verified that
\varphi
is an isomorphism
with inverse defined by restricting the map
v\otimes e_{\xi}\mapsto f(\xi)v
.

4.4.
The bijection

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mathscr{R}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathscr{R}_{g}
be an associative algebra.
For any subgroup
M\leq G
,
let
\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,M}
be the collection of equivalence classes of finite-dimensional
G/M
-graded irreducible
\mathscr{R}
-modules,
where two
G/M
-graded modules
\widetilde{V}
and
V
are equivalent
if
\widetilde{V}\cong(V^{+\Lambda})^{f}

for some
\Lambda\in G/M
and some
f\in\widehat{G}
.
That this forms an equivalence relation is easily verified.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group
and
\mathscr{R}
a
G
-graded associative algebra.
Let
N\leq M\leq G

and
set
\Gamma=G/N

and
H=M/N
.
Assume
H
is a cyclic group of prime order.
Define a function
B\colon\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,M}\to\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,N}
by

(6)

B([V])=\begin{cases}[V_{\Gamma}]&\text{if $V$ is $\Gamma$-gradable}\\
[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}]&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
V
,
V_{\Gamma}
and
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
are representatives of the equivalence classes,
and
V_{\Gamma}
is
\Gamma
graded but isomorphic to
V
as a
\Gamma/H
-graded module.
(To construct the loop module
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
, we use the group isomorphism

.)
Then
B
is well-defined (does not depend on the choice of representative
V
) and bijective.

Proof.

First, we show that
B
is well-defined.
Choose two representatives
\widetilde{V}
and
V

of an equivalence class in
\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,M}
,
so that
[\widetilde{V}]=[V]
.
By definition,
\widetilde{V}
is isomorphic to
(V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f}

for some
\gamma+H\in\Gamma/H\cong G/M

and some
f\in\widehat{G}
.
Note that the
\Gamma
-gradability is unchanged after twisting by
f
and after shifting by
\gamma+H
.
Therefore,
B(\widetilde{V})=\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}
if and only if
B(V)=V_{\Gamma}
.
There are two cases.

•

Assume
B([\widetilde{V}])=[\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}]
and
B([V])=[V_{\Gamma}]
.
As
\Gamma/H
-graded modules,

\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}\cong(V_{\Gamma}^{+\gamma})^{f}
.
By
Proposition

4.12
,
the
\Gamma
-grading of
\widetilde{V}

is unique up to
H
-parity shift.
Therefore, as
\Gamma
-graded modules,
\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}\cong((V_{\Gamma}^{+\gamma})^{f})^{+h}\cong(V_{\Gamma}^{+(\gamma+h)})^{f}

for some
h\in H
.
That is,
[\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}]=[V_{\Gamma}]

and
B
is well-defined in this case.

•

Assume
B([\widetilde{V}])=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\widetilde{V})}]
and
B([V])=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}]
.
Then

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\widetilde{V})}\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{((V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f})}\cong((\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+\gamma})^{f}

using
Proposition

4.6

(ii)

and
Proposition

4.8

(i)
.
That is,
[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\widetilde{V})}]=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}]

and
B
is well-defined.

For injectivity, assume now that
B([\widetilde{V}])=B([V])
.
We know that
\widetilde{V}
is
\Gamma/H
-graded irreducible
but that
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
is not (by
Lemma

4.5
),
so
\widetilde{V}
cannot be equivalent to
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
in
\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{\Gamma,N}

(twists and parity shifts both preserve irreducibility—
Proposition

4.6

(i)
and
Proposition

4.8

(iii)
).
Thus, there are two cases:

•

Assume
B(\widetilde{V})=[\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}]=B([V])=[V_{\Gamma}]
.
We have that
\widetilde{V}_{\Gamma}\cong(V_{\Gamma}^{+\gamma})^{f}

for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
and some

.
Coarsening to a
\Gamma/H
-grading,
we find that
\widetilde{V}\cong(V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f}
.
That is,
[\widetilde{V}]=[V]

and
B
is injective in this case.

•

Assume
B(\widetilde{V})=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\widetilde{V})}]=B([V])=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}]
.
Then

\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\widetilde{V})}\cong((\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)})^{+\gamma})^{f}\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{((V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f})}

for some

and some

(and using
Proposition

4.6

(ii)

and
Proposition

4.8

(i)
).
By
Lemma

4.5
,
we know that
(V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f}

is a
\Gamma/H
-graded submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{((V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f})}

and also that

Therefore,
(V^{+(\gamma+H)})^{f}\cong\widetilde{V}^{f_{0}}

for some

.

and

is injective.

For surjectivity, take

.
Using

4.1
and

4.10
,
we have that

appears as a
\Gamma
-graded submodule of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
for some irreducible
\Gamma/H
-graded module

.
By
Theorem

4.11
,
the only
\Gamma
-graded submodules of
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
are either
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}
itself
or

.
If

then the loop module is irreducible and so

-gradable
(by

).
Thus,

in this case.
Similarly, if

then

.

Taking

, the above theorem (in combination with discolouration/recolouration) gives a way to construct the graded irreducible modules of the

-graded colour Lie superalgebras from the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie superalgebras.
More generally, we can iterate the theorem to produce a bijection
B_{1}\colon\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,G}\to\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,\{0\}}
.
The image of this bijection contains
all of the
G
-graded irreducible modules (up to equivalence).

Remark 4.16
.

For any
G
-graded representation, all twists by a character in
\widehat{G}
are
G
-graded isomorphic; therefore, we only need to consider the
G
-parity shifts (and not twists by a character) to determine the equivalence classes of
\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,\{0\}}

Similarly, there are no non-trivial

-parity shifts; hence, we only need to consider twists by elements of
\widehat{G}
to determine the equivalence classes of
\mathsf{Irr}^{\mathsf{tw},\mathsf{sh}}_{G,G}
in this case.

Remark 4.17
.

Using the notation from
Theorem

4.15
,
suppose that
V\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma/H}_{\Gamma/H}\hskip-1.0pt{(U)}

for some ungraded
\mathscr{R}
-module

Notice that
\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma/H}_{\Gamma/H}\hskip-1.0pt{(U)})}\cong\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{\Gamma}\hskip-1.0pt{(U)}
. Indeed,

is an isomorphism
for

and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
If

is not
\Gamma
-gradable

B(V)=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{H}\hskip-1.0pt{(V)}]=[\mathrm{L}^{\Gamma}_{\Gamma}\hskip-1.0pt{(U)}]=[\mathrm{L}^{G/N}_{G/N}\hskip-1.0pt{(U)}].

Thus, if we iterate

In general, it may be difficult to determine which case of
B
in (
6

There are multiple options we can test:

•

-grading for

•

This third option (

5.
Example: colour
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

As an example of applying

, we will determine the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}
over
\mathbb{C}

The algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie colour algebra with commutation factor
\varepsilon(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2})=(-1)^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}

\displaystyle 00\text{-sector:}

\displaystyle~0

\displaystyle 10\text{-sector:}

\displaystyle~a_{1}

\displaystyle 01\text{-sector:}

\displaystyle~a_{2}

\displaystyle 11\text{-sector:}

\displaystyle~a_{3}

\displaystyle\cbrak{a_{1}}{a_{2}}

\displaystyle=a_{3}=\cbrak{a_{2}}{a_{1}}

\displaystyle=a_{1}=\cbrak{a_{3}}{a_{2}}

\displaystyle\cbrak{a_{3}}{a_{1}}

\displaystyle=a_{2}=\cbrak{a_{1}}{a_{3}}.

\sigma(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2})=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}

\displaystyle=a_{3}

\displaystyle\commutator{a_2}{a_3}

\displaystyle=-a_{1}

\displaystyle\commutator{a_3}{a_1}

\displaystyle=-a_{2}.

These commutation relations are satisfied by the following realisation

(7)

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}(e-f)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(e+f)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}h.

in terms of a standard basis

Both the ungraded

]

[

]

[

Proof.

Lemma 5.6

If
\lambda

Proof.

Choose a new basis

\displaystyle a_{1}u_{j}

6.
Example: A class of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie colour algebras of type D

6.1.
Definition of the algebra

Let
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)

be the subspace of
\mathrm{M}_{(2m+2n)\times(2m+2n)}
spanned by
matrices of the form

(12)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}A&-B&X&Y\\
B^{\mathsf{T}}&C&Y^{\mathsf{T}}&Z\\
\hline\cr X&Y&A&-B\\
Y^{\mathsf{T}}&Z&B^{\mathsf{T}}&C\end{array}\right]

where
A,X\in\mathrm{M}_{m\times m},\,C,Z\in\mathrm{M}_{n\times n}
are anti-symmetric and
B,Y\in\mathrm{M}_{m\times n}
are arbitrary.

The vector space
\mathbb{F}^{m}\times\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}^{m}\times\mathbb{F}^{n}

can be given a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading by assigning gradings
00,10,01,11

to the direct factors
(respectively).
We can then interpret (
12
)
as elements of
\End(\mathbb{F}^{2m+2n})
;
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
\End(\mathbb{F}^{2m+2n})

induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
.
Explicitly: the
00
-sector is spanned by matrices where the only nonzero sub-matrices are
A
and
C
;
the
10
-sector has only
B
nonzero; the
01
-sector has only
X
and
Z
nonzero;
and the
11
-sector has only
Y
nonzero.

Via straightforward yet somewhat tedious computation, we can verify that
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
closes to form a Lie colour algebra with commutation factor
\varepsilon(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2})=(-1)^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}

(alternatively, see
Remark

6.1
).

For the example of this section, we will focus on the case that
m+n
is even.
Set
r=(m+n)/2
.
Note that the rank of
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
is
2r
(this will become apparent shortly).

6.2.
Discolouring the algebra

We will choose the multiplier
\sigma(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2})=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}
to discolour
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
.
We interpret the matrices (
12
) as a representation
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)\to\End(\mathbb{F}^{2m+2n})

and discolour this matrix representation.
The resulting discoloured matrix representation is spanned by

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}A&-B&X&-Y\\
B^{\mathsf{T}}&C&Y^{\mathsf{T}}&-Z\\
\hline\cr X&-Y&A&-B\\
Y^{\mathsf{T}}&-Z&B^{\mathsf{T}}&C\end{array}\right]

and closes to form a Lie algebra with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading.
Denote this discoloured algebra by
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)
.

Set

Q=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{m+n}&I_{m+n}\\
\hline\cr I_{m+n}&-I_{m+n}\end{array}\right]

where
I_{m+n}
is the
(m+n)\times(m+n)
identity matrix.
Using
Q
to change the basis, we find that
Q\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)Q^{-1}
is spanned by the matrices

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}A+X&-B-Y&0&0\\
-(-B-Y)^{\mathsf{T}}&C-Z&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&A-X&-B+Y\\
0&0&-(-B+Y)^{\mathsf{T}}&C+Z\end{array}\right]

and therefore, if we ignore the grading,
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2r)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2r)
.
(In fact,
Q
preserves the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}
-grading—but not the full
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading—so
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)\cong\mathfrak{so}(2r)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2r)
as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebras.)

Remark 6.1
.

Even though
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)
is not ungraded simple,
it is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded simple.
Indeed, one can easily verify that
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)
is the loop algebra of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
.
The loop algebra of a graded-simple algebra is also graded simple
[
62
, Lemma 5.1.1]
.

As an ungraded algebra,
\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)
is semisimple.
Appealing to the classification of irreducible representations in this case,
we find that the finite-dimensional ungraded irreducible representations are

V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}

where
\lambda
and
\mu
range over the dominant integral weights of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)

and for
V_{\lambda}
and
V_{\mu}
the irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
with highest weights
\lambda
and
\mu
respectively (see e.g.
[
69

).
Note that the action of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2r)
on

is given by

(x\oplus y)(v\otimes w)=xv\otimes w+v\otimes yw

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{so}(2r),v\in V_{\lambda},w\in V_{\mu}
.

To apply a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading to these representations, it is first beneficial to analyse the structure of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
.

6.3.
Structure of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)

Let
e_{ij}
be the elementary matrices satisfying
(e_{ij})_{k\ell}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}

where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta.
A standard basis for
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
is

E_{ij}=e_{ij}-e_{ji},\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,2r

noting that
E_{ij}=-E_{ji}
.
(Note that we could interpret
E_{ij}\in Q\mathfrak{so}_{\oplus}(2r)Q^{-1}
as a basis of the first copy
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
. A basis for the second copy can be defined similarly.)
The Lie bracket is given by

\commutator{E_{ij}}{E_{k\ell}}=\delta_{jk}E_{i\ell}-\delta_{j\ell}E_{ik}-\delta_{ik}E_{j\ell}+\delta_{i\ell}E_{jk}.

We will choose the Cartan subalgebra spanned by the elements
H_{j}=-2iE_{2j-1,2j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
.
The raising/lowering operators for this Cartan basis are

F^{\zeta\xi}_{jk}=\zeta\xi E_{2j,2k}-E_{2j-1,2k-1}-i\xi E_{2j-1,2k}-i\zeta E_{2j,2k-1}

where
j<k
and
\zeta,\xi\in\{+,-\}
are interpreted as
+1
or
-1
in the above expression.
These raising/lowering operators satisfy

\displaystyle\commutator{H_j}{F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}}

\displaystyle=\zeta 2F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}

\displaystyle\commutator{H_k}{F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}}

\displaystyle=\xi 2F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}

\displaystyle\commutator{H_\ell}{F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}}

\displaystyle=0~\text{for}~\ell\neq j,k.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r}
be the dual basis of
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
.
The root corresponding to
F_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}
is thus
\alpha_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}\coloneq\zeta 2\varepsilon_{j}+\xi 2\varepsilon_{k}
.
Let
\Phi=\{\alpha_{jk}^{\zeta\xi}\mid j<k~\&~\zeta,\xi\in\{+,-\}\}
be the root system.

For any choice of
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,

\displaystyle\spn\{H_{j},H_{k},F_{jk}^{++},F_{jk}^{+-},F_{jk}^{-+},F_{jk}^{--}\}

\displaystyle\cong\spn\{H_{j}+H_{k},F_{jk}^{++},F_{jk}^{--}\}\oplus\spn\{H_{j}-H_{k},F_{jk}^{+-},F_{jk}^{-+}\}

forms a
D_{2}\cong A_{1}\oplus A_{1}
subalgebra, see
Figure

1
.

\varepsilon_{j}
\varepsilon_{k}

\alpha_{jk}^{+-}
\alpha_{jk}^{-+}

\varepsilon_{j}=(1/4)(\alpha^{++}_{jk}+\alpha^{+-}_{jk})

and

The lattice of integral weights is

P=\{\lambda_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}\varepsilon_{r}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{r-1}+\lambda_{r}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\}.

We will impose the lexicographical order on

The positive integral weights are then

P^{+}=\{\lambda_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}\varepsilon_{r}\in P\mid\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{j-1}=0~\text{and}~\lambda_{j}>0~\text{for some}~j=1,\ldots,r\}

.
The negative integral weights

The simple roots are

\Phi_{s}=\{2\varepsilon_{j}-2\varepsilon_{j+1}\mid j=1,\ldots,r-1\}\cup\{2\varepsilon_{r-1}+2\varepsilon_{r}\}.

The dominant integral weights are

which is easily verified by taking the dot product with positive roots.

Recall that the dominant integral weights classify the finite-dimensional (ungraded) irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
.

6.4.
Twisting

Let

In this subsection,

-module.
We will use this result in the next subsection, together with

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded

Note that the only non-identity element in

f\colon\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
defined by

.

Now, consider the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{so}(2r))_{0}

\displaystyle=\spn\{E_{ij}\mid\text{($i\leq m$ and $j\leq m$) or ($i>m$ and $j>m$)}\}

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{so}(2r))_{1}

\displaystyle=\spn\{E_{ij}\mid\text{($i\leq m$ and $j>m$) or ($i>m$ and $j\leq m$)}\}.

6.4.1.
First case:

First, recall that the Cartan decomposition yields a
\mathbb{Z}^{r}

In the case that

Therefore, the Cartan grading refines the

Hence every irreducible
\mathfrak{so}(2r)

\Gamma

More specifically, every weight in the weight lattice

For each
\kappa=\kappa_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{r}\varepsilon_{r}\in P

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\kappa_{k}\right)(2\varepsilon_{j}-2\varepsilon_{j+1})\right)

Lemma 6.2
.

(V_{\lambda})^{f}\cong V_{\lambda^{f}}
where
\lambda^{f}=\lambda-2\lambda_{r}\varepsilon_{r}
.

Proof.

In the notation of
[

we have that
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{F}

and the grading of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
is inherited from
\End_{\mathcal{D}}(W)

where
W
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
(m+n)
-dimensional vector space.
(This is how we originally defined the grading for
\mathfrak{so}(2r)

The distinguished element
[
58
, Definition 34]
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is

r0+m0+n1

(choosing the standard basis of
W
,
we find that
g_{1}=\cdots=g_{m}=0
and
g_{m+1}=\cdots=g_{m+n}=1
,
using the notation of
[
58
, Section 7.1]
).

Since we are assuming that
n
is odd, the distinguished element is not the identity and thus the grading is outer;
that is, there exists an element of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
whose twist is an outer automorphism of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
.
In particular, a twist by

must be an outer automorphism.

In determining of the isomorphism class of
(V_{\lambda})^{f}
,
an outer automorphism is equivalent to a Dynkin diagram automorphism
[
58
, Section 3]
.
Since this grading is a matrix grading,
this diagram automorphism must be the one
which swaps the two terminal simple roots
2\varepsilon_{r-1}-2\varepsilon_{r}

and
2\varepsilon_{r-1}+2\varepsilon_{r}

(For

The dual Cartan basis
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r-1},\varepsilon_{r}
becomes
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r-1},-\varepsilon_{r}

after applying this diagram automorphism.
Thus,
the highest weight

becomes
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r-1},-\lambda_{r})

∎

\tau\colon(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
by

\tau(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta}.

It is easy to verify that after applying
Proposition

4.13
the representation becomes

\rho^{\tau}
defined by

\rho^{\tau}(x)v=\tau(\alpha,\Lambda)\rho(x)v

for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
v\in V_{\Lambda}
(
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\,\Lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
).

Thus, we have the following classification of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded irreducible
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
modules.

Lemma 6.6
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded irreducible representations of the colour algebra
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
are the same as those in
Lemma

6.4
,
recoloured with
the function
\tau\colon(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}

defined by
\tau(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta}

(
\tau
is induced from
\sigma
by passing to the quotient
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}
in the second variable).

6.8.
Fourth step: ungraded irreducible representations for the colour algebra

We are now ready to classify the ungraded irreducible representations for the colour algebra
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
.
In this subsection, we coarsen the grading from
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}
to the trivial group.

Like in the previous section,
V^{\tau}\cong(V^{\tau})^{+h}
if and only if
V\cong((V^{\tau})^{+h})^{\tau}
.
Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
v\in V_{\Lambda}
(for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\Lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
).
Choosing
h=1
and
\tau\colon(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}
with
\tau(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta}
, we find

\displaystyle((\rho^{\tau})^{+1})^{\tau}(x)v

\displaystyle=\tau(\alpha,\Lambda)((\rho)^{+1})^{\tau}(x)v

\displaystyle=\tau(\alpha,\Lambda)((\rho)^{+1})^{\tau}(x)v

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\Lambda}(-1)^{\alpha_{2}(\Lambda+1)}\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}}\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=f_{2}(\alpha)\rho(x)v.

Therefore, in this case,
((V^{\tau})^{+1})^{\tau}\cong(V^{f_{2}})^{+1}
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})

and set
\lambda^{f}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r-1},-\lambda_{r})
and
\mu^{f}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r-1},-\mu_{r})
.
Let
\Psi\colon(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})\to(V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{f_{2}}
be an isomorphism
such that
\Psi^{2}=\Id
.
Let
\tau\colon(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}

be the function
\tau(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta)=(-1)^{\alpha_{2}\beta}
for recolouring.

Letting
\lambda
and
\mu
range over the dominant integral weights of
\mathfrak{so}(2r)
,
the finite-dimensional ungraded irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
are as follows.

For
m
even:

(1)

the irreducible representations are
(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{\tau}
.
The recolouration by
\tau
is defined with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading in
Lemma

6.4
case
(1)
.

For
m
odd:

(2)

If
\lambda_{r}\neq 0
or
\mu_{r}\neq 0
and
\mu\notin\{\lambda,\lambda^{f}\}
: the irreducible representations are

(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})})^{\tau}\cong(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda^{f}}\otimes V_{\mu^{f}})})^{\tau}.

The recolouration by
\tau
is defined with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading of the loop module.

(3)

If
\lambda_{r}\neq 0
or
\mu_{r}\neq 0
and
\mu\in\{\lambda,\lambda^{f}\}
: the irreducible representations are

\displaystyle\{v\otimes w\otimes e_{0}+\Psi(v\otimes w)\otimes e_{1}\mid v\otimes w\in V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})})^{\tau}

\displaystyle\{v\otimes w\otimes e_{0}-\Psi(v\otimes w)\otimes e_{1}\mid v\otimes w\in V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})})^{\tau}.

(4)

If
\lambda_{r}=\mu_{r}=0
: the irreducible representations are
(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{\tau}
.
The recolouration by
\tau
is defined with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading in
Lemma

6.4
case
(3)
.

Proof.

Consider the case where
m
is even (case
(1)
in
Lemma

6.4
).
In this case, the grading is induced from the Cartan grading.
For the Cartan grading of a Lie algebra, irreducible is equivalent to graded irreducible.
The result then follows because
the action of the Cartan subalgebra (which defines the grading) does not change under recolouration.

Now assume that
m
is odd
and consider the case where
\lambda_{r}\neq 0
or
\mu_{r}\neq 0
(case
(2)
in
Lemma

6.4
).
If
\mu\notin\{\lambda,\lambda^{f}\}
then

\displaystyle(((\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})})^{\tau})^{+1})^{\tau}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{((V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{f_{2}})})^{+1}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\lambda})})^{+1}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\lambda})}

cannot be isomorphic to
\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})}
.
That is, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-parity shifts are not isomorphic in this case,
so by
Proposition

4.13
the recoloured loop module is irreducible.

Still considering the case
\lambda_{r}\neq 0
or
\mu_{r}\neq 0
,
suppose now that
\mu\in\{\lambda,\lambda^{f}\}
.
Then the map
v\otimes w\otimes e_{\gamma}\mapsto\Psi(v\otimes w)\otimes e_{\gamma}

(for

and

is an isomorphism

\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})}\to(\mathrm{L}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}/\{00,01\}}\hskip-1.0pt{((V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{f_{2}})})^{+1}.

Applying
Proposition

4.13
,
Remark

4.14
and recolouring,
we find the irreducible representations of the form in case
(3)
of this theorem.

Finally, consider the case where
\lambda_{r}=\mu_{r}=0

(case
(3)
in
Lemma

6.4
).
If
\mu\neq\lambda
then
(((V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{\tau})^{+1})^{\tau}\cong((V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu})^{f_{2}})^{+1}\cong(V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{+1}
cannot be (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded) isomorphic to
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}
.
So assume
\mu=\lambda
.
Let
v\otimes v
be the highest weight vector of
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}
.
Notice that the two possible gradings (the two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-parity shifts) in

Lemma

6.4

(3)

But the highest weight
(\lambda,\lambda)

Therefore,

That is,

(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{f_{2}}\cong V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}

as

(noting that the two

).
We deduce that
((V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{\tau})^{+1}\ncong(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{\tau}

and hence
(V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda})^{\tau}
is irreducible as an ungraded module by
Proposition

4.13
.

7.
Conclusions

The loop module is a natural structure to consider in the study of Lie colour algebras,
including their applications to quantum mechanical systems.
In this direction, we showed that the Hilbert space of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded quantum mechanical system of
[
23
]
can be obtained via a loop module.
The loop module could potentially be a useful tool for constructing quantum mechanical systems in future work.

The main result of this paper (
Theorem

4.15
) is the existence of a bijection between finite-dimensional
G/M
- and
G/N
-graded irreducible representations for
N\leq M\leq G
with
M/N
cyclic of prime order (up to equivalence relations weaker than isomorphism).
This bijection can be iterated following the cyclic groups of prime order in the Jordan–Hölder decomposition
to yield a bijection between ungraded and
G
-graded irreducible representations
for any finite abelian group
G
.
A similar bijection had previously been noted in
[
63
, Remark 7.2]
,
but the iterated loop module by cyclic groups of prime order simplifies the construction.
In combination with discolouration/recolouration, this bijection provides a general procedure for generating all of the
G
-graded Lie colour algebra representations from the well-studied
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie superalgebra representations.

As an example of applying the bijection, we constructed all of the irreducible representations for the Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
These irreducible representations agree with those previously derived in
[
64
,
65
]
using a different construction.
Despite the bijection of
Theorem

4.15
,
these
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie colour algebra representations have a remarkably different structure from those of the ungraded discoloured Lie algebra.
As a more involved example, we also classified the irreducible representations for the Lie colour algebra
\mathfrak{so}^{c}_{\oplus}(m,n)
.

The bijection (
Theorem

4.15
) also has applications to the study of Lie colour algebra quantum mechanical systems.
For example, we could potentially apply this procedure to construct the irreducible representations of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-supersymmetry algebras with
\mathcal{N}\geq 2
, whose classification is desired for the quantum mechanical systems studied in
[
33
]
.
Future research should work to obtain a general classification of irreducible Lie colour algebra representations, possibly by applying the bijection of this paper to existing classifications.

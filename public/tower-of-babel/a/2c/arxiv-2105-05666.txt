Elements of the Metric-Affine Gravity I: Aspects of F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) theories reductions and the Topologically Massive Gravity
Yessica Dominguez and Rolando Gaitan D. †

Abstract
Some classical aspects of Metric-Affine Gravity are reviewed in the context of the F ( n ) ​ ( R ) F^{(n)}(R) type models (polynomials of degree n n in the Riemann tensor) and the topologically massive gravity. At the non-perturbative level, we explore the consistency of the field equations when the F ( n ) ​ ( R ) F^{(n)}(R) models are reduced to a Riemann-Christoffel (RCh) space-time, either via a Riemann-Cartan (RC) space or via an Einstein-Weyl (EW) space. It is well known for the case F ( 1 ) ​ ( R ) = R F^{(1)}(R)=R that any path or reduction ”classes” via RC or EW, leads to the same field equations with the exception of the F ( n ) ​ ( R ) F^{(n)}(R) theories for n > 1 n>1 . We verify that this discrepancy can be solved by imposing non-metricity and torsion constraints. In particular, we explore the case F ( 2 ) ​ ( R ) F^{(2)}(R) for the interest in expected physical solutions as those of conformally flat class. On the other hand, the symmetries of the topologically massive gravity are reviewed, as the physical content in RC and EW scenarios. The appearance of a non-linearly modified selfdual model in RC and existence of many non-unitary degrees of freedom in EW with the suggestion of a modified model for a massive gravity which cure the unphysical propagations, shall be discussed.

The exercise of obtaining the field equations provides
{\tau^{(1)}}_{\mu\nu}={T^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}=Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=a^{\alpha\mu\nu}=0
.

Finishing this section we want to point out some remarks. On the one hand it can also be shown that the direct reduction path MAG
\rightarrow
RCh would be performed by means of the action
S^{\prime\prime\prime(1)}=k^{2-N}\,\langle g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}+a^{\alpha\mu\nu}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}+{b_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}{T^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}\,\rangle
which (nothing surprising) gives the same results again: Einstein’s equations are obtained for a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and all multipliers are zero. It seems too trivial and repetitive what has been stated here, fundamentally due to the fact that it does not seem to matter what one does, the Lagrange multipliers are always zero, which indicates that deep down they were never needed.

It can be shown immediately that the set of multipliers used here, that is
a^{\alpha\mu\nu}
and
{b_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}
is equivalent to the set of multipliers,
{\lambda_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}\neq{\lambda_{\alpha}}^{\nu\mu}
when the constraint
{A^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}-{\Gamma^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}
is used
[
23
]
.
In fact, the multipliers of non-metricity and torsion constraints are related to those of J. R. Ray
[
23
]
through

\displaystyle a^{\alpha\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\,\big(\lambda^{\alpha\{\mu\nu\}}-\lambda^{\{\mu\alpha\nu\}}-\lambda^{\{\mu\nu\}\alpha}\big)\,\,,\,\,

(43)

\displaystyle b^{\alpha\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\,\big(\lambda^{\alpha[\mu\nu]}-\lambda^{[\mu\alpha\nu]}-\lambda^{[\mu\nu]\alpha}\big)\,\,.\,\,

(44)

Finally, because the Lagrangian density of the action
S^{(1)}
is proportional to the Hilbert-Einstein MAG one, it does not matter what kind of reduction paths from MAG we use. However, there are physical systems where the Lagrange multipliers are not trivial
[
20
]
due to the presence of quadratic terms in the curvature and of massive type in the action (which is not the case we have looked at), which goes hand in hand with the fact that the field equations depend on which reduction path we choose. In any case, the important thing here is that we have established the different restrictions on the variational principle that we will implement in the next section where we shall observe that in effect the lagrange multipliers are no longer trivial.

3.2
Consistency of field equations in the
F^{(n)}(R)
model.

Here we will study the reduction of the metric-affine manifold in the context of a
F^{(n)}(R)
Lagrangian density with
n>1
. Let
S^{(n)}
be the action

\displaystyle S^{(n)}=k^{2n-N}\,\langle F^{(n)}(g,R)\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(45)

and its functional variation is

\delta S^{(n)}=k^{2n-N}\,\langle\tau_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}+{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\,\delta{R^{\alpha}}_{\beta\mu\nu}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(46)

where

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{\partial F^{(n)}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}-\frac{F^{(n)}}{2}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\,,\,\,

(47)

\displaystyle{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{\partial F^{(n)}(R)}{\partial{R^{\alpha}}_{\beta\mu\nu}}\,\,.\,\,

(48)

However, remembering the definition of curvature one arrive to
\delta{R^{\alpha}}_{\beta\mu\nu}=D_{\nu}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\mu\beta}-D_{\mu}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\nu\beta}+{T^{\sigma}}_{\nu\mu}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\sigma\beta}
and then (
46
) can be written in explicit terms of connection variations as follows

\displaystyle\delta S^{(n)}=k^{2n-N}\,\langle\tau_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}-2{E_{\alpha}}^{\sigma\beta}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\sigma\beta}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(49)

where the Palatini’s tensor is

\displaystyle{E_{\alpha}}^{\sigma\beta}\equiv D_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}+\big(\frac{{\delta^{\sigma}}_{\mu}g^{\lambda\rho}}{2}\,Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}-{\delta^{\sigma}}_{\mu}{T^{\lambda}}_{\lambda\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,{T^{\sigma}}_{\mu\nu}\big){F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\,\,.\,\,

(50)

From this notation we will be able to look at various scenarios and thus contrast the three classes of MAG reduction paths shown in fig.1.

3.2.1
Einstein-Weyl Class (EW)

This is the Palatini’s way where we assume the torsionless condition before making functional variations. So, from (
49
) we get

\displaystyle\delta\big(S^{(n)}\mid_{T=0}\big)=k^{2n-N}\,\langle\tau_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}-2{E_{\alpha}}^{\{\sigma\beta\}}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\{\sigma\beta\}}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(51)

from where the field equations are written (using (
50
))

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(52)

\displaystyle{E_{\alpha}}^{\{\sigma\beta\}}\equiv D^{(T=0)}_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\sigma\}\nu}+\frac{g^{\lambda\rho}}{2}\,Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}\,{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\sigma\}\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(53)

and the ad hoc condition
{T^{\sigma}}_{\mu\nu}=0
. On the other hand, if we evaluate the metric-affine equation coming from (
49
) over the torsionless condition, we obtain

\displaystyle{E_{\alpha}}^{\sigma\beta}\equiv D^{(T=0)}_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}+\frac{g^{\lambda\rho}}{2}\,Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}\,{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(54)

which is algebraically different with respect to (
53
). This is a characteristic reported in the literature
[
21
]
. Here we underline for an arbitrary Lagrangian density
F^{(n)}(R)
, none of the equations (
53
) or (
54
) guarantees that the non-metricity is necessarily zero. Only the case
n=1
guarantees that the torsionless condition implies the non-metricity is also null.

Nevertheless there exists a third reduction path in the EW class which consists of incorporating the torsionless constraint through the action

\displaystyle S^{\prime}=S^{(n)}+k^{2n-N}\,\langle{b_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}{T^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}\,\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(55)

where
{b_{\lambda}}^{\mu\nu}=-{b_{\lambda}}^{\nu\mu}
. The field equations are

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(56)

\displaystyle{E_{\alpha}}^{\{\sigma\beta\}}\equiv D^{(T=0)}_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\sigma\}\nu}+\frac{g^{\lambda\rho}}{2}\,Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}\,{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\sigma\}\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(57)

\displaystyle{T^{\lambda}}_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(58)

\displaystyle{b_{\lambda}}^{\mu\nu}={E_{\lambda}}^{[\mu\nu]}\,\,.\,\,

(59)

It can be noted that (
56
) and (
57
) match with the Palatini’s method and Lagrange multipliers are no longer necessarily trivial for
n>1
. However, no path of the EW class leads to the equations expected in the pseudo-Riemannian manifold, because even if the limit
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\rightarrow 0
were taken on the equations (
53
) and (
54
) we would get
\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\sigma\}\nu}=0
and
\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}=0
, respectively.

3.2.2
Riemann-Cartan Class (RC)

Now we explore the reduction paths which supose the
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=0
constraint. The last relation was stablished at (
35
) and induces a constraint on symmetric part of connection (see (
36
)), this means
{A^{\nu}}_{\{\alpha\mu\}}={\Gamma^{\nu}}_{\alpha\mu}+\frac{g^{\nu\lambda}g_{\mu\sigma}}{2}\,{T^{\sigma}}_{\lambda\alpha}+\frac{g^{\nu\lambda}g_{\alpha\sigma}}{2}\,{T^{\sigma}}_{\lambda\mu}
. Copying us from (
38
), the next functional variation can be written

\displaystyle\delta\big(S^{(n)}\mid_{Q=0}\big)

\displaystyle=k^{2n-N}\,\langle\big(\tau_{\mu\nu}-D_{\alpha}{P^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}+{T^{\alpha}}_{\alpha\sigma}{P^{\sigma}}_{\mu\nu}\big)\delta g^{\mu\nu}+

(60)

\displaystyle-2\big({E^{[\alpha\beta]}}_{\sigma}+{{E^{[\alpha}}_{\sigma}}\,^{\beta]}+{E_{\sigma}}^{[\alpha\beta]}\big)\delta{A^{\sigma}}_{[\alpha\beta]}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

where

\displaystyle P^{\alpha\rho\beta}\equiv 2E^{\rho\{\alpha\beta\}}-E^{\alpha\{\rho\beta\}}\,\,,\,\,

(61)

with
E^{\rho\alpha\beta}
given at (
50
).

From (
60
), the torsion field equation is

\displaystyle{E^{[\alpha\beta]}}_{\sigma}+{{E^{[\alpha}}_{\sigma}}\,^{\beta]}+{E_{\sigma}}^{[\alpha\beta]}=0\,\,,\,\,

(62)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle E_{[\alpha\sigma\beta]}=0\,\,,\,\,

(63)

or equal to
E_{\alpha\{\sigma\beta\}}-E_{\{\sigma\beta\}\alpha}=0
and this allow us to rewrite (
61
) as follows

\displaystyle P^{\alpha\rho\beta}=E^{\rho\alpha\beta}-E^{\alpha[\rho\beta]}\,\,,\,\,

(64)

hence metric and connection field equations are

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}-D_{\alpha}{{E_{\mu}}^{\alpha}}\,_{\nu}+{T^{\alpha}}_{\alpha\sigma}{{E_{\mu}}^{\sigma}}\,_{\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(65)

\displaystyle E_{[\alpha\sigma\beta]}\equiv D_{\nu}{F_{[\alpha\beta]\sigma}}^{\nu}+\big(-g_{\sigma\mu}\,{T^{\lambda}}_{\lambda\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,T_{\sigma\mu\nu}\big){F_{[\alpha\beta]}}^{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(66)

and the ad hoc constraint

\displaystyle Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}=0\,\,.\,\,

(67)

It can be noted that the torsionless limit of (
65
) does not have to match with the metric field equation in a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. On the other hand, considering
{T^{\lambda}}_{\mu\nu}=0
in (
66
) leads to
\nabla_{\nu}{F_{[\alpha\beta]\sigma}}^{\nu}=0
. Even more, taking into acount another reduction path inside the RC class, evaluation of MAG field equations coming from (
49
) on the condition
Q_{\nu\lambda\rho}=0
produces a different result

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(68)

\displaystyle D_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}+\big(-{\delta^{\sigma}}_{\mu}{T^{\lambda}}_{\lambda\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,{T^{\sigma}}_{\mu\nu}\big){F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(69)

whose torsionless limit leads to a connection field equation in the form
\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}=0
.

Another posible reduction path in the RC class consist in to incorporate the non-metricity through the action

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}=S^{(n)}+k^{2n-N}\,\langle a^{\alpha\mu\nu}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\,\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(70)

where
a^{\lambda\alpha\beta}=a^{\lambda\beta\alpha}
. The field equations are

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}-D_{\alpha}{{E_{\mu}}^{\alpha}}\,_{\nu}+{T^{\alpha}}_{\alpha\sigma}{{E_{\mu}}^{\sigma}}\,_{\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(71)

\displaystyle E_{[\mu\sigma\nu]}=0\,\,,\,\,

(72)

\displaystyle a^{\nu\lambda\sigma}=-E^{\{\lambda\nu\sigma\}}\,\,,\,\,

(73)

coinciding con (
65
) and (
66
) in addition to obtaining non-trivial multipliers.
Thus, we see that the procedure with the Lagrange multipliers, either in the EW or RC scenario, reproduces what we could call the expected field equations (but not those of a pseudo-Riemannian manifold), in contrast to what happens when the MAG expressions are specialized for each space-time. This will be evidenced once again in the next section.

3.2.3
Riemann-Christoffel class (RCh)

First we will review Hilbert’s variational principle for (
45
) and for this we consider the functional variation of the connection in a pseudo-Riemannian space, that is

\displaystyle\delta{A^{\rho}}_{\alpha\beta}\mid_{T=Q=0}\,\,=\delta{\Gamma^{\rho}}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{g^{\rho\sigma}}{2}\,(\nabla_{\alpha}\delta g_{\sigma\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}\delta g_{\sigma\alpha}-\nabla_{\sigma}\delta g_{\alpha\beta})\,\,,\,\,

(74)

and from (
49
) we write

\displaystyle\delta\big(S^{(n)}\mid_{Q=T=0}\big)=k^{2n-N}\,\langle\big(\tau_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{\sigma}{{E_{\{\mu}}^{\sigma}}\,_{\nu\}}-\nabla_{\sigma}{E_{\{\mu\nu\}}}^{\sigma}+\nabla_{\sigma}{E^{\sigma}}_{\{\mu\nu\}}\big)\delta g^{\mu\nu}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(75)

from where the following field equation emerges

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\sigma}\big({E^{\sigma}}_{\{\mu\nu\}}-{{E_{\{\mu}}^{\sigma}}\,_{\nu\}}-{E_{\{\mu\nu\}}}^{\sigma}\big)=0\,\,,\,\,

(76)

where, obviously all objects are evaluated on null torsion and non-metricity. This field equation will be our reference to compare with the reduction paths from the MAG. We have seen that none of the reduction classes (EW or RC) reproduces (
76
), only in the case of the curvature function of order
n=1
this is guaranteed. Recall that in the case in which we evaluate the MAG field equations (obtained from (
49
)) on the null torsion and non-metricity condition, that is
\tau_{\mu\nu}=0
and
\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\sigma\nu}=0
, they do not reproduce (
76
). However, by introducing the torsion and non-metricity constraints, we write

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime\prime}=S^{(n)}+k^{2n-N}\,\langle a^{\alpha\mu\nu}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}+{b_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}{T^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}\,\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(77)

from which the following equations of motion are obtained

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\sigma}\big({E^{\sigma}}_{\{\mu\nu\}}-{{E_{\{\mu}}^{\sigma}}\,_{\nu\}}-{E_{\{\mu\nu\}}}^{\sigma}\big)=0\,\,,\,\,

(78)

\displaystyle a_{\beta\mu\alpha}=-E_{\{\alpha\mu\}\beta}-E_{\{\alpha\beta\mu\}}+E_{\beta\{\mu\alpha\}}\,\,,\,\,

(79)

\displaystyle b_{\alpha\beta\mu}=E_{\alpha[\beta\mu]}+E_{[\beta\mu]\alpha}+E_{[\beta\alpha\mu]}\,\,,\,\,

(80)

\displaystyle{T^{\sigma}}_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(81)

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(82)

clearly reproducing the field equation in a pseudo-Riemannian manifold.

Note that for the purposes of the presented discussion we do not require the introduction of external sources. However, it could be considered a term with external fields represented by some ”
\psi
” in the style of the reference
[
15
]
and whose action could also depends on the metric and the affine connection (without considering the possible breaking of the projective symmetry), this means

\displaystyle S^{(n)}=k^{2n-N}\,\langle F^{(n)}(R)+\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}L(g,A,\psi)\,\rangle\,\,.\,\,

(83)

and its variation is

\displaystyle\delta S^{(n)}=k^{2n-N}\,\langle{\tau}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}-2{E^{\prime}_{\alpha}}^{\mu\beta}\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\mu\beta}\rangle\,\,,\,\,

(84)

with

\displaystyle{\tau}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\tau_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2\kappa_{0}}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,\,,\,\,

(85)

\displaystyle{E^{\prime}_{\alpha}}^{\mu\beta}={E_{\alpha}}^{\mu\beta}-\frac{1}{2\kappa_{0}}\,{J_{\alpha}}^{\mu\beta}\,\,,\,\,

(86)

where
T_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{-2\kappa_{0}k}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta L}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
and
{J_{\alpha}}^{\mu\beta}\equiv\frac{\kappa_{0}k}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta L}{\delta{A^{\alpha}}_{\mu\beta}}
are the energy-momentum tensor of material fields and the hypermomentum current
[
13
]
, respectively. Particularly in the case
n=1
the Lagrange multipliers are no longer trivially zero and would depend exclusively on the hypermomentum current.

4
Metric-affine
F^{(2)}(R)
model

The main purpose here is to explore the existence of vacuum solutions in the context of the study of reduction from a MAG manifold and how this affects the appropriate shape of the Lagrangian density. At the pseudo-Riemannian limit, where torsion and non-metricity are null, we will consider the Schouten-Weyl theorem as a key element to identify possible conformally flat solutions. From this, our interest will be focused on looking at models of the class
n=2
. In a non-pseudo-Riemannian
N
-dimensional manifold, the most general possible model
F^{(2)}(R)
without sources is

\displaystyle F^{(2)}(R)=a_{1}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+a_{2}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}+a_{3}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\nu\mu\beta}+a_{4}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\mu\nu\beta}+

\displaystyle+\,a_{5}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\nu\mu\alpha\beta}+a_{6}\,R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}+a_{7}\,R^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}+a_{8}\,R^{\mu\nu}R_{\nu\mu}+a_{9}\,R^{2}\,\,,\,\,

(87)

and its explicit derivative with respect to the Riemann tensor is

\displaystyle{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{\partial F^{(2)}(R)}{\partial{R^{\alpha}}_{\beta\mu\nu}}=2a_{1}\,{R_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}+2a_{2}\,{R_{\alpha}}^{[\mu\beta\nu]}+2a_{3}\,{{R^{[\mu\beta}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\nu]}+a_{4}\,({{R^{\beta[\mu}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\nu]}+

\displaystyle+{{R^{[\mu}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\beta\nu]})+2a_{5}\,{{R^{\beta}}_{\alpha}}^{\mu\nu}+2a_{6}\,{{R^{[\mu\nu]}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\beta}+2a_{7}\,R^{\beta[\nu}{\delta^{\mu]}}_{\alpha}+2a_{8}\,R^{[\nu\beta}{\delta^{\mu]}}_{\alpha}+

\displaystyle+\,2a_{9}\,R\,g^{\beta[\nu}{\delta^{\mu]}}_{\alpha}\,\,,\,\,

(88)

its evaluation in a pseudo-Riemannian space is

\displaystyle{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\big|_{T=Q=0}=a\,({{R^{\nu\beta}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\mu}-{{R^{\nu}}_{\alpha}}\,^{\beta\mu})+b\,(R^{\nu\beta}{\delta^{\mu}}_{\alpha}-R^{\mu\beta}{\delta^{\nu}}_{\alpha})+

\displaystyle+c\,R\,(g^{\beta\nu}{\delta^{\mu}}_{\alpha}-g^{\beta\mu}{\delta^{\nu}}_{\alpha})\,\,,\,\,

(89)

with parameters

\displaystyle a\equiv-2a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3}+a_{4}+2a_{5}-2a_{6}\,\,,\,\,

(90)

\displaystyle b\equiv a_{7}+a_{8}\,\,,\,\,

(91)

\displaystyle c\equiv a_{9}\,\,.\,\,

(92)

Let us also remember that with null torsion and non-metricity conditions, the Bianchi identities are of the form

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}R_{\mu\nu\beta\lambda}+\nabla_{\lambda}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}R_{\mu\nu\lambda\alpha}=0\,\,,\,\,

(93)

and all the well-known symmetry properties of the Riemann-Christoffel tensor, which will be useful when we examine the various reduction classes MAG
\rightarrow
RCh of
F^{(2)}(R)
theories. With the help of the Bianchi identities and their contractions, we can write an identity in a pseudo-Riemannian space that we will eventually use

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\beta\mu\nu}\equiv(a-b)\nabla_{\alpha}R^{\beta\mu}-a\nabla^{\beta}{R_{\alpha}}^{\mu}+(c+\frac{b}{2}){\delta^{\mu}}_{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}R-cg^{\beta\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}R\,\,.\,\,

(94)

4.0.1
F^{(2)}(R)
and the Einstein-Weyl Class.

Here we attempt the reduction to a pseudo-Riemannian space or RCh via Einstein-Weyl (i.e.,
{T^{\alpha}}_{\mu\nu}=0
, see fig.1) using the system (
52
) and (
53
) for the particular case
n=2
. From (
89
) we can write the pseudo-Riemannian limit of the connection equation as
{{E^{(T=0)}}_{\alpha}}^{\{\mu\beta\}}\big|_{Q=0}=\nabla_{\nu}{F_{\alpha}}^{\{\beta\mu\}\nu}=0
and with the help of the identity (
94
), we can establish it in terms of first derivatives of the Ricci tensor as follows

\displaystyle 0={{E^{(T=0)}}_{\alpha}}^{\{\mu\beta\}}\big|_{Q=0}\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{2}\nabla^{\beta}{R_{\alpha}}^{\mu}-\frac{a}{2}\nabla^{\mu}{R_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+(a-b)\nabla_{\alpha}R^{\beta\mu}+

(95)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(c+\frac{b}{2})({\delta^{\mu}}_{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}R+{\delta^{\beta}}_{\alpha}\nabla^{\mu}R)-cg^{\beta\mu}\nabla_{\alpha}R\,\,.\,\,

With the objective to explore existence of conformally flat solutions coming from
{{E^{(T=0)}}_{\alpha}}^{\{\mu\beta\}}\big|_{Q=0}=0
, we use the next algebraic combination

\displaystyle 0=\dfrac{1}{N-2}\,{E^{(T=0)}}_{\alpha\{\mu\beta\}}\big|_{Q=0}-\dfrac{1}{(N-2)(N-1)}\,\Big(g_{\mu\alpha}\,{{E^{(T=0)}}_{\lambda\{\beta}}^{\lambda\}}\big|_{Q=0}

\displaystyle+\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,{{E^{(T=0)}}_{\lambda\{\mu}}^{\lambda\}}\big|_{Q=0}-2g_{\mu\beta}\,{{E^{(T=0)}}_{\lambda\{\alpha}}^{\lambda\}}\big|_{Q=0}\Big)=

\displaystyle=a\big(\nabla_{[\alpha}\mathcal{R}_{\beta]\mu}+\nabla_{[\alpha}\mathcal{R}_{\mu]\beta}\big)-\dfrac{b}{N-2}\,\nabla_{\alpha}G_{\mu\beta}\,\,,\,\,

(96)

where
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}
and
G_{\alpha\beta}
are the Schouten’s and Einstein’s tensors defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\frac{1}{N-2}\big(R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{g_{\alpha\beta}}{2(N-1)}\,R\big)\,\,,\,\,

(97)

and

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}\equiv R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{g_{\alpha\beta}}{2}\,R\,\,,\,\,

(98)

respectively.
Equation (
96
) reveals a possible deviation from Shouten-Weyl’s condition which stablishes conformally flatness because the Schouten tensor is not guaranteed to be of the Codazzi type, in other words, in general
\nabla_{[\alpha}\mathcal{R}_{\beta]\mu}\neq 0
. In order to look at this more precisely, we seek to remove the explicit presence of the Einstein’s tensor in (
96
), assuming
a\neq 0
and
b\neq 0
. Working with symmetric-antisymmetric parts of (
96
) and with the help of definitions for Schouten and Einstein tensors, we get

\displaystyle(3a-2b)\nabla_{[\alpha}\mathcal{R}_{\beta]\mu}=\dfrac{b}{N-1}\,g_{\mu[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}R\,\,,\,\,

(99)

and

\displaystyle 0=b(\nabla_{\alpha}G_{\beta\mu}+\nabla_{\beta}G_{\mu\alpha}+\nabla_{\mu}G_{\alpha\beta})\,\,.\,\,

(100)

Furthermore, the trace of (
100
) is
0=-\dfrac{b(N-2)}{2}\,\nabla_{\mu}R
, and with this the system of equations is

\displaystyle(3a-2b)\nabla_{[\alpha}R_{\beta]\mu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(101)

\displaystyle b(\nabla_{\alpha}R_{\beta\mu}+\nabla_{\beta}R_{\mu\alpha}+\nabla_{\mu}R_{\alpha\beta})=0\,\,,\,\,

(102)

\displaystyle b\nabla_{\mu}R=0\,\,,\,\,

(103)

which admits one solution of a cosmological type at least. However, in the particular case where the parameters satisfy

\displaystyle 3a-2b=0\,\,,\,\,

(104)

the conformal flatness condition from (
101
) is no longer guaranteed and the constraints on the Ricci tensor are reduced to (
102
) and (
103
). If one wishes to contrast (
102
) with the Schouten-Weyl condition for the case (
103
), that is
\nabla_{[\alpha}R_{\beta]\mu}=0
it can be start by looking at the left member of (
102
) as a totally symmetric rank
3
object and, considering that the indices take
N
possible values, the number of independent components corresponds to that of combinations with repetition of
N
elements in groups of
3
, it means

\displaystyle{N+p-1\choose p}\Big|_{p=3}=\frac{(N+2)(N+1)N}{6}\,\,,\,\,

(105)

which is the number of the mentioned combinations.
This indicates that the difference between the number of Schouten-Weyl conditions and (
105
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(N)\equiv\frac{N^{2}(N-1)}{2}-\frac{(N+2)(N+1)N}{6}\equiv\dfrac{N}{3}\,(N^{2}-3N-1)\,\,.\,\,

(106)

In
N=3
dimension,
\mathcal{D}(3)=-1
, whereby there would be given redundant information, in addition to that the Weyl conformal tensor is identically null. However, in higher dimensions
N>3
we have
\mathcal{D}(N)>0
in a monotonic way, which does not guarantee only conformally planar solutions under the condition (
104
). Thus, we see that the existence of physically acceptable solutions is sensitive to the nullity or not of the factor
3a-2b
.

We have examined the connection equation and saw that there are configurations of the parameters that do not guarantee the existence of unique conformally flat solutions for the vacuum. This class of non-Einsteinian solutions can also be characterized through the equation of the metric, (
52
) for
n=2
, that is

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}\equiv\dfrac{\partial F^{(2)}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}-\frac{F^{(2)}}{2}\,g_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(107)

which can be particularized for the case of the pseudo-Riemannian limit. With this, from (
87
) we write

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}\Big|_{Q=T=0}=2(a_{1}-a_{5}+a_{6})\,R_{\mu\alpha\beta\lambda}{R_{\nu}}^{\alpha\beta\lambda}+2(a_{2}+a_{3}-a_{4})\,R_{\mu\alpha\beta\lambda}{R_{\nu}}^{\beta\alpha\lambda}+

\displaystyle+2(a_{7}+a_{8})\,R_{\mu\lambda}{R_{\nu}}^{\lambda}+2a_{9}\,RR_{\nu\mu}-\dfrac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\,\Big[(a_{1}-a_{5}+a_{6})\,R^{\alpha\beta\lambda\rho}R_{\alpha\beta\lambda\rho}+

\displaystyle+(a_{2}-a_{3}-a_{4})\,R^{\alpha\beta\lambda\rho}R_{\alpha\lambda\beta\rho}+(a_{7}+a_{8})\,R^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}+a_{9}\,R^{2}\Big]=0\,\,.\,\,

(108)

As an illustration we can try a very simple non-Einsteinian kind of solution thinking of a deviation with respect to the cosmological solution, i.e.

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\phi(x)\,(g_{\mu\lambda}g_{\nu\rho}-g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\lambda})\,\,,\,\,

(109)

where
\phi(x)
is an arbitray scalar field. By replacing (
109
) in (
108
) we get

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu\nu}\Big|_{Q=T=0,\phi}=\dfrac{(N-1)}{2}\,q(a,b,c)\,\phi^{2}g_{\mu\nu}=0\,\,,\,\,

(110)

where we have defined

\displaystyle q(a,b,c)\equiv(N-4)[a-(N-1)b-N(N-1)c]\,\,,\,\,

(111)

and we see that (
110
) at
N=4
is identically satisfied, leaving undetermined parameter
c
and scalar field,
\phi

8
Conclusion.

Certain classical properties of the
F(R)
theories have been briefly reviewed and explored, within which it is well known that those of the quadratic type have been presented as good candidates to correct certain disadvantages regarding the presence of non-physical propagations in gravity theories. The relevance of elaborating the discussion on the reduction of the general geometry of the MAG is important at the same time that Einstein’s gravitation could be considered as test evidence for any modified gravity model at some limit. Hence, the existence or not of conformally flat solutions must be an elementary requirement of any generalized model that is proposed.

In the case of topologically massive gravity, we have tried to stay as faithful as possible to DJT’s original idea, keeping the two original pieces, such as the Hilbert-Einstein Lagrangian density and the Chern-Simons density, and extending their theory to the level of non-pseudo-Riemannian geometry without introducing new Lagrangian terms. Through the Riemann-Cartan reduction path (
T\neq 0
,
Q=0
) we verify the same degrees of freedom as the Mielke-Baekler model but from a point of view of a modified selfdual model, while in the Einstein-Weyl scenario (
T=0
,
Q\neq 0
) the abundance of particles brought multiple non-unitary propagations which warrants an evaluation of the model itself. Obviously this has to bring stability problems in any classical non-trivial vacuum solution that is tested. The advantage is that there is apparently no unique way to correct the above. In fact, actions of the quadratic type in curvature are candidates to control non-unitary propagations as in the case of TMGEW. A classical criterion was envisioned to choose the possible families of theories
F^{(2)}(R)
that could help to control non-physical propagations based on requirement that conformally planar solutions must be included. A criterion that could be extended beyond
2+1

Several problems arise as the question about whether the modified selfdual model found is unique or not, the construction of a topologically massive gravity model that overcomes the problems shown in Einstein-Weyl, the study of classical and quantum stability, among others will be addressed elsewhere.

Appendix A
Diffeomorphisms.

A transformation under difeomorphisms is a smooth deformation (continuous, differentiable and invertible) of a manifold that is manifested by the continuous and differentiable change of the coordinates that label the points of space-time. Thus, we will think about transformations of the form
{x^{\prime}}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-\xi^{\mu}(x)
, where
\xi^{\mu}(x)
are infinitesimal functions (
0<|\xi^{\mu}(x)|<<1
) continuous and derivable. Let’s see how
infinitesimal diffeomorphism on coordinates induces functional transformation on fields. Under the group of general transformations a scalar field transforms like
{\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=\phi(x)
, then a diffeomorphism variation means

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}\phi(x)

\displaystyle\equiv{\phi^{\prime}}(x)-\phi(x)

\displaystyle={\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)-\phi(x)

\displaystyle={\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{1!}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu}{\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\xi^{\mu}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})-\phi(x)

\displaystyle={\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})-\phi(x)+\frac{1}{1!}\big(\partial_{\mu}{\phi^{\prime}}(x)-\frac{1}{1!}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}{\phi^{\prime}}(x)\xi^{\alpha}+...\big)\xi^{\mu}(x)+...

\displaystyle={\phi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})-\phi(x)+\frac{1}{1!}\partial_{\mu}\big(\phi(x)+\delta_{\xi}\phi(x)\big)\xi^{\mu}(x)+...

\displaystyle=\xi^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}\phi(x)\,\,.\,\,

(271)

A rank
1
tensor transforms as
{t^{\prime}}^{\mu}(x^{\prime})={U^{\mu}}_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}
where
U\in GL(N,R)
. For an infinitesimal transformation under diffeomorphisms the elements of
GL(N,R)
are
{U^{\mu}}_{\alpha}\mid_{\xi}={\delta^{\mu}}_{\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\mu}
and induce the variation of diffeomorphisms on field
t^{\mu}
as follows

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}t^{\mu}(x)=\xi^{\alpha}(x)\partial_{\alpha}t^{\mu}(x)-\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\mu}(x)t^{\alpha}(x)\,\,.\,\,

(272)

In the same way, the following transformation rules can be shown

(i) a rank
1
(contravariant) tensor

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}t_{\mu}(x)=\xi^{\alpha}(x)\partial_{\alpha}t_{\mu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\alpha}(x)t_{\alpha}(x)\,\,.\,\,

(273)

(ii) a rank
2

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}t_{\mu\nu}(x)=\xi^{\alpha}(x)\partial_{\alpha}t_{\mu\nu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\alpha}(x)t_{\alpha\nu}(x)+\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\alpha}(x)t_{\mu\alpha}(x)\,\,.\,\,

(274)

(iii) a scalar density of ”weight”

(275)

Towards the precise description of Composite Higgs models at colliders
Diogo Buarque Franzosi

Abstract
We present a framework to study the interactions among Nambu-Goldstone bosons (NGB), pseudo-NGB (pNGBs) and gauge bosons in Composite Higgs (CH) models at high energies, including operators of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 4 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{4}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 2 ​ g 2 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{2}g^{2}) in the chiral expansion and topological terms. The set of (p)NGBs comprises the longitudinal modes of electroweak bosons, the Higgs boson, and possibly other scalar states from the dynamical spontaneous electroweak symmetry breaking. The framework is implemented in a collider simulation tool especially suited for the study of Goldstone Boson Scattering (GBS), which includes vector boson scattering (VBS), di-Higgs production via vector boson fusion (VBF) and the pair production of other pNGBs via VBF.



I
Introduction

Composite Higgs (CH) models are promising alternatives to the Standard Model (SM), describing the electroweak (EW) symmetry breaking (SB) dynamically via a fermionic condensate, solving the hierarchy problem
Weinberg 1976
;
Susskind 1979
;
Dimopoulos and Susskind 1979
, as well as the little hierarchy between the compositeness scale and the Higgs mass via the vacuum misalignment mechanism
Dugan et al. 1985
with a SM-like light Higgs boson appearing as a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson (pNGB)
Kaplan and Georgi 1984
;
Georgi and Kaplan 1984
. It also opens several paths towards explaining dark matter with composite states,
unification and SM fermion masses hierarchy via partial compositeness (PC)
Kaplan 1991
with large anomalous dimension of fermionic operators in a near-conformal phase
Holdom 1981
.

The description of CH interactions below the condensation scale is provided by the chiral expansion of the Coleman-Callan-Wess-Zumino (CCWZ) formalism
Coleman et al. 1969
;
Callan et al. 1969
,
usual in the CH literature (see
Contino 2011
;
Bellazzini et al. 2014
;
Panico and Wulzer 2016
for reviews).
In CH models an approximate global symmetry
G
is spontaneously broken to the stability group
H
giving origin to a set of (p)NGBs.
Besides the 3 exact NGB
eaten
by the weak bosons, CH models contain the Higgs boson as well as other pNGBs in non-minimal scenarios.
Models with an explicit matter content of hyperfermions with PC mechanism via a four-dimensional gauge theory have typically non-minimal cosets
Barnard et al. 2014
;
Ferretti and Karateev 2014
;
Vecchi 2017
;
Ferretti 2016
;
Csáki et al. 2018
;
Guan et al. 2020
.

The interactions among the (p)NGBs, including the Higgs and longitudinal modes of weak bosons, are fixed by the non-linear symmetry of the CCWZ construction.
These interactions can be observed via precise measurement of SM processes or the production of other pNGBs.
In this paper we present the CCWZ Lagrangian for a generic CH coset, with a minimal set of operators that allow the description of the gauge interactions and the misalignment of the vacuum.
We include operators at orders
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
,
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
and
\mathcal{O}(p^{2}g^{2})
in the chiral expansion, as well as the topological Wess-Zumino-Witten terms (WZW)
Wess and Zumino 1971
;
Witten 1979
.
The presented
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
and
\mathcal{O}(p^{2}g^{2})
terms are intended as a minimum self-consistent set of operators that can be used to renormalize one-loop amplitudes in the chiral perturbation framework.

A framework for the automatic simulation of CH processes in collider experiments based on the
FeynRules
package
Christensen and Duhr 2009
;
Alloul et al. 2014
, Universal Feynrules Output (UFO)
Degrande et al. 2012
and
MG5_aMC@NLO

Alwall et al. 2011
is presented.
Predictions for (pseudo-)Goldstone boson scattering (GBS) are used as examples. GBS processes give the ultimate test of the dynamical origin of EWSB.
It includes Vector Boson Scattering (VBS), di-Higgs production via Vector Boson Fusion (VBF) and the pair production of extra pNGBs via VBF.
For the SM processes VBS and di-Higgs via VBF distributions are computed up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
at tree level.
Predictions for pNGB pair production via VBF for
SU(4)/Sp(4)
and
SU(5)/SO(5)
are also produced at
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
at tree level.

IV.1
Di-Higgs via VBF and VBS at
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
and tree-level

To study the Higgs and weak boson physics at high energy we neglect possible extra pNGBs, having in mind that the Higgs and EW bosons obey universal relations.
We also neglect the the spurion contributions,
\chi\to 0
in
\mathcal{L}_{4}
and the
\mathcal{L}_{g^{2}p^{2}}
(eq. (
40
)) as a first approximation.
As pointed out in
Liu et al. 2019
, the modification in these interactions can be well approximated by the MCH SO(5)/SO(4) description.
In SO(5)/SO(4) the
L_{0}
and
L_{3}
terms are redundant and can be rewritten in terms of
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
.
An interesting particularity in the
SO(5)/SO(4)
coset is that the stability group
H
is not simple,
SO(4)\sim SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
.
This fact allow us to further break the projections into the unbroken generators into two independent terms. For example
\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}
can be broken in

\displaystyle O_{L}

\displaystyle\equiv O_{L}^{a}S^{a}=2\langle S^{a}\tilde{O}\rangle S^{a},\,a=1,2,3\quad

(41)

\displaystyle O_{R}

\displaystyle\equiv O_{R}^{a}S^{a}=2\langle S^{a}\tilde{O}\rangle S^{a},\,a=4,5,6\,.

(42)

Therefore, we use a different basis for the operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{MCH}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\big\{L_{1}\langle x^{\mu}x_{\mu}\rangle\langle x^{\nu}x_{\nu}\rangle+L_{2}\langle x^{\mu}x^{\nu}\rangle\langle x_{\mu}x_{\nu}\rangle+L_{3}^{-}\langle s^{\mu\nu}_{L}s_{\mu\nu,L}-s^{\mu\nu}_{R}s_{\mu\nu,R}\rangle

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}L_{9}^{+}\langle(f^{\mu\nu}_{L}+f^{\mu\nu}_{R})x_{\mu}x_{\nu}\rangle-\mathrm{i}L_{9}^{-}\langle(f^{\mu\nu}_{L}-f^{\mu\nu}_{R})x_{\mu}x_{\nu}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}L_{10}^{+}\langle\hat{f}_{\mu\nu}\hat{f}^{\mu\nu}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}L_{10}^{-}\langle f_{L}^{\mu\nu}f_{\mu\nu,L}-f_{R}^{\mu\nu}f_{\mu\nu,R}\rangle\big\}

(43)

The coefficients carrying a minus sign break the
Z_{2}
symmetry
R\leftrightarrow L
.
Notice that in several models the
Z_{2}
symmetry is present in the strong theory and is only softly broken. For instance
O(4)
is a subgroup of
Sp(4)
.
However, the breaking of
Z_{2}
can be achieved in other cosets by gauge spurions
\Gamma^{g,g^{\prime}}
and the appropriate choice of the hyperfermion spurion
\chi
.
The Lagrangian is equivalent to
Contino et al. 2011
;
Liu et al. 2019
, using the NDA power counting
m_{\rho}=4\pi f
and the identification,

c_{1}=L_{1},\,c_{2}=L_{2},\,c_{3}=L_{3}^{-},\,c_{4}^{\pm}=L_{9}^{\pm},\,c_{5}^{\pm}=L_{10}^{\pm}\,.

(44)

The implementation in
FeynRules
requires the fields to have canonically normalized kinetic terms and diagonal masses.
The higher dimension operators induce corrections to the kinetic terms, as

\mathcal{L}_{4}\supset-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}[1+X_{B}]-\frac{1}{4}W^{i}_{\mu\nu}W^{i\mu\nu}[1+X_{W}]-\frac{1}{4}W^{3}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}X_{BW}

(45)

and one must thus redefine the fields to fulfill these requirements.
This is done by the following replacements:

\displaystyle W^{i}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to W^{i}_{\mu}(1+X_{W})

(46)

\displaystyle A_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to A_{\mu}(1+X_{W}s_{W,0}^{2}+X_{B}c_{W,0}^{2}+2s_{W,0}c_{W,0}X_{BW})+

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}(s_{W,0}c_{W,0}(X_{W}-X_{B})+(c_{W,0}^{2}-s_{W,0}^{2})X_{BW}+\delta s_{W}/c_{W,0}),

(47)

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to Z_{\mu}(1+X_{W}c_{W,0}^{2}+X_{B}s_{W,0}^{2}-2s_{W,0}c_{W,0}X_{BW})+

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(s_{W,0}c_{W,0}(X_{W}-X_{B})+(c_{W,0}^{2}-s_{W,0}^{2})X_{BW}-\delta s_{W}/cw).

(48)

Also the gauge couplings have to be modified,

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle\to g_{2}/(1+X_{W})\sim g_{2}(1-X_{W})

(49)

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle\to g_{1}/(1+X_{B})\sim g_{1}(1-X_{B})

(50)

\displaystyle s_{W,0}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-M_{W}^{2}/M_{Z}^{2})

(51)

\displaystyle s_{W}

\displaystyle=s_{W,0}(1+\delta s_{W}^{2})

(52)

\displaystyle\delta s_{W}

\displaystyle=X_{BW}c_{W,0}(1-2s_{W,0}^{2})

(53)

The exact form of the mixing coefficients in terms of the Lagrangian coefficients are

\displaystyle X_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{8s_{W,0}^{2}}[6L_{9}^{+}+L_{10}^{+}+4(4L_{3}^{+}+2L_{9}^{-}+L_{10}^{-})c_{\theta}+(2L_{9}^{+}-L_{10}^{+})c_{2\theta}],

(54)

\displaystyle X_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{8c_{W,0}^{2}}[6L_{9}^{+}+L_{10}^{+}-4(4L_{3}^{-}+2L_{9}^{-}+L_{10}^{-})c_{\theta}+(2L_{9}^{+}-L_{10}^{+})c_{2\theta}],

(55)

\displaystyle X_{WB}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{4c_{W,0}s_{W,0}}(2L_{9}^{+}-L_{10}^{+})s_{\theta}^{2}

(56)

L_{3}^{-}
,

V
Conclusion and prospects

We have presented a framework for the study of (p)NGBs in CH models, including VBS, di-Higgs via VBF, GBS in non-minimal models.
We have implemented the model in FeynRules and output it in the UFO format.
We then showed examples of simulations that can be further explored in more detailed phenomenological analysis.
We looked at the effect of
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
operators in VBS, in quartic couplings modifications and in di-Higgs production via VBF.
We also computed the branching ratios and pair-production distributions for extra pNGBs in non-minimal models of CH for some particular benchmarks.

Besides the suitability for phenomenological studies, the tool here presented is intended as a first step towards the incorporation of loop corrections
of EW and chiral perturbation theory origin.

Appendix B
Explicit breaking terms and spurions

The gauging of the EW subgroup will explicitly break the global symmetry
G
and the
j_{\mu}
can be regarded as a spurionic field.
Other interactions besides the gauge of
H^{\prime}
may also break
G
and can be included in the low energy chiral lagrangian via spurions.
The only requirement is that the local SM gauge group be unbroken, and preferably that the whole custodial symmetry be respected.
Following
Alanne et al. 2018
we concentrate here on spurions transforming in the fundamental (F), two-index symmetric (S) or anti-symmetric (A) and the adjoint (Adj) representations.
The best example of such terms is given by hyperfermion masses
\bar{\psi}\mathcal{M}\psi
, which transforms in general as
\Sigma
(),

\mathcal{M}\to g\mathcal{M}g^{T},\quad\chi=\xi^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}\xi^{*}\Sigma_{0}\to h\chi h^{\dagger}

(83)

Notice that with our way to write the
SU(N)^{2}/SU(N)
case, we can keep the same structure of a simple group, without having to specify
g_{L}
or
g_{R}
.
The other spurions representations transform as

\displaystyle\Xi_{F}\to g\Xi_{F},\quad\Xi_{S/A}\to g\Xi_{S/A}g^{T},\quad\Xi_{Adj}\to g\Xi_{Adj}g^{\dagger}.

(84)

The A/S and Adj representation can be put together in the object

\chi=\begin{cases}\xi^{\dagger}\Xi_{A/S}\xi^{*}\Sigma_{0}\\
\xi^{\dagger}\Xi_{Adj}\xi\end{cases}\to h\chi h^{\dagger}

(85)

For the symmetric and anti-symmetric cases, since we always want to add the hermitian conjugate of the terms we can define a hermitian and an anti-hermitian combination

\chi_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\chi\pm\chi^{\dagger})\,.

(86)

For the adjoint
\chi_{+}=\chi
and
\chi_{-}=0
.

H

Weighted posets and the enriched monomial basis of QSym
Darij Grinberg \addressmark 1, Ekaterina A. Vassilieva \addressmark 2

Abstract
Gessel’s fundamental and Stembridge’s peak functions are the generating functions for (enriched) P P -partitions on labelled chains. They are also the bases of two significant subalgebras of formal power series, respectively the ring of quasisymmetric functions (QSym) and the algebra of peaks. Hsiao introduced the monomial peak functions, a basis of the algebra of peaks indexed by odd integer compositions whose relation to peak functions mimics the one between the monomial and fundamental bases of QSym. We show that the extension of monomial peaks to any composition is a new basis of QSym and generalise Hsiao’s results including the product rule. To this end we introduce a weighted variant of posets and study their generating functions.

1.1
Compositions and permutation statistics

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{1,2,3,\dots\right\}
,
\mathbb{N}=\left\{0,1,2,\ldots\right\}
, and
[n]=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. A
composition

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{p})
of an integer
n
is a finite sequence of
\ell(\alpha):=p
positive integers such that

|\alpha|:=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}=n
. An
odd composition
is a composition containing only odd integers. We let
\operatorname{Comp}(n)
and
\operatorname{Odd}(n)
denote the sets of compositions and odd compositions (respectively) of
n
.

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on
[n]
. Given a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
, we look at two significant statistics. The
descent set

\operatorname{Des}(\pi)
and the
peak set

\operatorname{Peak}(\pi)
of
\pi
are the subsets of
[n-1]
defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Des}(\pi)=\{1\leq i\leq n-1|\pi(i)>\pi(i+1)\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Peak}(\pi)=\{2\leq i\leq n-1|\pi(i-1)<\pi(i)>\pi(i+1)\}.

A subset of
[n-1]
that neither contains
1
nor contains two consecutive integers is called
peak-lacunar
.
The peak set of a permutation is peak-lacunar.

There is a natural bijection between compositions of
n
and subsets of
[n-1]
. Namely, for a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{p})\in\operatorname{Comp}(n)
, let
\operatorname{Des}(\alpha)
be the subset of
[n-1]
defined as

\operatorname{Des}(\alpha)=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{p-1}\}.

As a result,
|\operatorname{Comp}(n)|=2^{n-1}
. Odd compositions of
n
, on the other hand, are in one-to-one correspondence with peak-lacunar subsets of
[n-1]
. This bijection may be stated as follows (see
[
7
]
). For
\alpha=(2i_{1}+1,2i_{2}+1,\dots,2i_{p}+1)\in\operatorname{Odd}(n)
, let

\widehat{\alpha}=(\overbrace{2,\dots,2}^{i_{1}},1,\overbrace{2,\dots,2}^{i_{2}},1,\dots,\overbrace{2,\dots,2}^{i_{p}},1).

If

\widehat{\alpha}=(\overbrace{1,\dots,1}^{j_{1}},2,\overbrace{1,\dots,1}^{j_{2}},2,\dots,\overbrace{1,\dots,1}^{j_{l}},2,1,\dots,1),

then we set

\operatorname{Peak}(\alpha)=\left\{\sum_{m=1}^{s}(j_{m}+2)|1\leq s\leq l\right\}.

Example 1
.

Let
\alpha=(1,1,3,3,1)\in\operatorname{Odd}(9)
. Then,
\operatorname{Des}(\alpha)=\{1,2,5,8\}
. Furthermore, one has
\widehat{\alpha}=(1,1,2,1,2,1,1)
and
\operatorname{Peak}(\alpha)=\{4,7\}
.

Finally, a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
may be written as a word
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\dots\pi_{n}
with
\pi_{i}=\pi(i)
. This is called the
one-line notation
of
\pi
.
Given two permutations
\pi\in S_{n}
and
\sigma\in S_{m}
, let
\pi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hss}\raise 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt}{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hss}\raise 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt}{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hss}\raise 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt}{\kern 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hss}\raise 0.0pt\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt\hfill\vrule height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt}}\sigma
be the subset of
S_{n+m}
consisting of all shuffles of the words
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\dots\pi_{n}
and
n+\sigma=(n+\sigma_{1})(n+\sigma_{2})\dots(n+\sigma_{m})
(that is, of all permutations in
S_{n+m}
that contain
\pi
and

as subwords in their one-line notation).

1.2
Quasisymmetric functions

Fix a commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
and consider the ring
\mathbf{k}\left[\left[X\right]\right]
of formal power series in countably many commuting
variables
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots\}
. In
[
2
]
, Gessel introduces the
quasisymmetric functions
, i.e., the bounded-degree formal power series in
\mathbf{k}\left[\left[X\right]\right]
such that for any non-negative integers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{p}
and any strictly increasing sequence of distinct indices
i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{p}
the coefficient of
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{p}^{\alpha_{p}}
is equal to the coefficient of
x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{\alpha_{p}}
. The set of all quasisymmetric functions is a
\mathbf{k}
-subalgebra of
\mathbf{k}\left[\left[X\right]\right]
, denoted by

\operatorname{QSym}
and called the
ring of quasisymmetric functions
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{p})\in\operatorname{Comp}(n)
, the
monomial quasisymmetric function

M_{\alpha}
and the
fundamental quasisymmetric function

L_{\alpha}
indexed by
\alpha
are defined as

M_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{\alpha_{p}},\;\;\;\;\;\;L_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n};\\
j\in\operatorname{Des}(\alpha)\Rightarrow i_{j}<i_{j+1}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}.

Example 2
.

As an example, for
n=3
, we have

\displaystyle M_{(2,1)}=\sum_{i<j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+x_{1}^{2}x_{4}+x_{2}^{2}x_{4}+x_{3}^{2}x_{4}+\dots,

\displaystyle L_{(2,1)}=\sum_{i\leq j<k}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+x_{1}^{2}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{4}+x_{2}^{2}x_{4}+\dots.

The sets
\left\{M_{\alpha}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha\in\operatorname{Comp}(n)}

and
\left\{L_{\alpha}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha\in\operatorname{Comp}(n)}

are two bases of the
\mathbf{k}
-module
\operatorname{QSym}
.
They are related through

L_{\alpha}=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\operatorname{Comp}(n);\\
\operatorname{Des}(\alpha)\subseteq\operatorname{Des}(\beta)\end{subarray}}}M_{\beta}.

1.3
Peak and monomial peak functions

In
[
10
]
, Stembridge studies another significant family of quasisymmetric functions. Given an
n\in\mathbb{N}
and an
\alpha\in\operatorname{Odd}(n)
, we define

K_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{n};\\
j\in\operatorname{Peak}(\alpha)\Rightarrow i_{j-1}<i_{j+1}\end{subarray}}2^{|\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n}\}|}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{n}}.

The
K_{\alpha}
are quasisymmetric functions named
peak functions
because of their relation to the peak statistic on permutations (see Section
1.4
). The set
\{K_{\alpha}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha\in\operatorname{Odd}(n)}
is a basis of a subalgebra of
\operatorname{QSym}
named the
algebra of peaks
(
\mathbf{\Pi}
in
[
10
]
). Hsiao defines in
[
7
]
another basis of the algebra of peaks called the
monomial peak functions
. For any odd composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{p})\in\operatorname{Odd}(n)
, let

\eta_{\alpha}=(-1)^{(n-\ell(\alpha))/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{p}\end{subarray}}2^{|\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}\}|}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{p}}^{\alpha_{p}}.

(2)

An identity similar to Equation (
1
) relates peak and monomial peak functions:

K_{\alpha}=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\operatorname{Odd}(n);\\
\operatorname{Peak}(\beta)\subseteq\operatorname{Peak}(\alpha)\end{subarray}}}\eta_{\beta}.

(3)

Hsiao shows that the monomial peak functions
\eta_{\alpha}
form a basis of the algebra of peaks and gives closed-form formulas for their expression in terms of the classical bases of
\operatorname{QSym}
, their antipode, product and coproduct. His proof relies on Equation (
3
) and uses the fact that the compositions are actually odd ones.

Nevertheless, one may extend Equation (
2
) to
any
integer composition (to the exception of the factor
(-1)^{(n-\ell(\alpha))/2}
that is not well-defined if
n
and
\ell(\alpha)
are not of the same parity). Whether this extension preserves the nice properties of the monomial peak functions appears as a natural question. We give it a
positive
answer by showing that the extended monomial peak functions form a basis of
\operatorname{QSym}
and generalising the results of Hsiao. We call this new basis of
\operatorname{QSym}
the
enriched monomial basis
. Our methods involve various algebraic and combinatorial arguments including the introduction of a new variant of posets and
P
-partitions whose generating functions generalise the monomial, fundamental, peak and enriched monomial functions as well as other classical variants of quasisymmetric functions. Before stating these results, we recall classical results for (enriched)
P
-partitions.

Part of this material will appear in
[
4
]
(work in progress).

We note that the
K_{\alpha}
basis of the algebra of peaks can also be extended to a larger family in
\operatorname{QSym}
although this extension is not a basis of
\operatorname{QSym}
; this is done in a recent work of Khesin and Zhang
[
8
]
(which also extends
\operatorname{QSym}
further by introducing two extra variables
x_{0}
and
x_{\infty}
).

1.4
Posets and
P

One of the main motivations for studying the power series
L_{\alpha}
and
K_{\alpha}
is their relation to descent and peak statistics on permutations. These relations are proved through the study of (enriched)
P
-partitions. In this section, we recall the definition and main results without proofs and refer the reader to
[

2

10

Definition 1

(Labelled posets)
.

A
labelled poset

P=([n],<_{P})
is an arbitrary partial order
<_{P}
on the set
[n]
.

Definition 2

(
P
-partition)
.

Let
P=([n],<_{P})
be a labelled poset.
A
P
-partition
is a map
f:[n]\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}
that satisfies the two following conditions:

(i)

If
i<_{P}j
, then
f(i)\leq f(j)
.

(ii)

If
i<_{P}j
and
i>j
, then
f(i)<f(j)
.

(The relations
<
and
>
with no subscript stand for the classical total order on
\mathbb{P}
.)

Definition 3
.

We let
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
be the set
\left\{-,+\right\}\times\mathbb{P}
consisting of all pairs of a sign and a positive integer. The pair
\left(\pm,n\right)\in\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
will be denoted by
\pm n
; its

\left|\pm n\right|
is defined to be
n
. We equip the set
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
with a total order given by
-1<1<-2<2<-3<\dots
. We embed
\mathbb{P}
into
\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
by identifying each
n
with
+n
; we also let
-\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
be the set of all
-n
for
n\in\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 4

(Enriched
P
-partition)
.

Let
P=([n],<_{P})
be a labelled poset.
An
enriched
P
-partition
is a map
f:[n]\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{\pm}
that satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

If
i<_{P}j
and
i<j
, then
f(i)<f(j)
or
f(i)=f(j)\in\mathbb{P}
.

(ii)

If
i<_{P}j
and
i>j
, then
f(i)<f(j)
or
f(i)=f(j)\in-\mathbb{P}
.

Note that
P
-partitions are the same as enriched

-partitions with no negative values (i.e., no values of the form

). A more general concept was defined in

2.1

We proceed with the definition of our extension of Hsiao’s monomial peak functions

. When

Definition 6

(Enriched monomials)
.

For any

and any composition
\alpha\in\operatorname{Comp}(n)
, we define a quasisymmetric function

\eta_{\alpha}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\operatorname{Comp}(n);\\

.

Tug-of-war games related to oblique derivative boundary value problems with the normalized p p -Laplacian
Jeongmin Han

Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with game-theoretic interpretations to the following oblique derivative boundary value problem { Δ p N ​ u = 0 in Ω , ⟨ β , D ​ u ⟩ + γ ​ u = γ ​ G on ∂ Ω , \displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\ \langle\beta,Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$,}\\ \end{array}\right. where Δ p N \Delta_{p}^{N} is the normalized p p -Laplacian. This problem can be regarded as a generalized version of the Robin boundary value problem for the Laplace equations. We construct several types of stochastic games associated with this problem by using ‘shrinking tug-of-war’. For the value functions of such games, we investigate the properties such as existence, uniqueness, regularity and convergence.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the existence, uniqueness, local and boundary regularity of tug-of-war type games related to oblique boundary value problems involving the normalized
p
-Laplacian

\Delta_{p}^{N}u=\Delta u+(p-2)\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u=\Delta u+(p-2)\frac{\langle D^{2}uDu,Du\rangle}{|Du|^{2}}

for
2<p<\infty
. The value function of the game we first study satisfies the following dynamic programming principle (DPP for short) in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the
C^{1,1}
-boundary regularity condition,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)\\
&\quad\quad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\bigg\}+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x)\end{split}

with
\gamma>0
and
G\in C^{1}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
where

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is the inner
\varepsilon
-boundary strip of
\Omega
(see (
2.2
) for the definition).
For
x\in\Omega
with

s_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
,
the DPP reads

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,

which is related to the game tug-of-war with noise.
We postpone a more detailed presentation of the various terms of the DPP (
1.1
) to Section
2.1
.

The games have a connection to the following boundary value problem when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\
\langle\mathbf{n},Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\mathbf{n}
is the outward normal vector on
\partial\Omega
.
Theorems
5.2
implies that the value function
u_{\varepsilon}
of DPP (
1.1
) converges uniformly to a solution of this PDE when
p\geq 2
.
We give a heuristic game interpretation for the boundary condition of (
1
).
Our game is basically played only within
\Omega
, and hence we do not consider such a situation that the token goes outside the domain.
The boundary condition of the model problem is associated with the game rule when the token is near the boundary.
More precisely, it is related to the area where a random walk occurs and the probability that the game ends at this point.

Lewicka and Peres
[
LP23a
,
LP23b
]
invented a probabilistic interpretation for the problem (
1
) in the case
p=2
.
The related DPP to the stochastic process coincides with (
1.1
) when
\alpha=0
. They showed, among other things, that the DPP has a unique, asymptotically Hölder continuous solution up to the boundary, converging uniformly to the viscosity solution of the PDE.
We aim to extend some of their results to the range
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
To this end, one of the main issues is how to set the game near the boundary.
We construct our game so that the token must not go outside
\Omega
,
and we still need to consider tug-of-war games in a small ball, since tug-of-war in an asymmetric domain may affect the boundary condition to the associated PDE.
For that reason, we considered tug-of-war games with shrinking step sizes as the token approaches the boundary.
Our main result is the boundary Hölder regularity for the solution of the DPP (
1.1
), which will be restated as Theorem
4.4
.
We also dealt with the convergence of the solution to (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
in Theorem
5.2
.

Main Theorem
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1,1}
-domain,
G\in C^{1}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
to be determined later.
Then, there exists a unique function
u_{\varepsilon}
solving (
1.1
).
Moreover, there exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}

with
|x_{0}-y_{0}|\leq\delta
and

\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega),\operatorname{dist}(y_{0},\partial\Omega)\leq\delta^{1/2},

we have

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-u_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})|\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}

for some
C
depending on
n,\alpha,\gamma,\sigma
and
\Omega
, and
\varepsilon<<\delta
.

Moreover, there exists a function
u:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
such that
u_{\varepsilon_{i}}
converges uniformly to
u
on
\overline{\Omega}
and

u
is a viscosity solution to the problem (
1
).

Section
4
is generally devoted to proving the above main theorem.
The proof is technical, based on constructing suitable submartingales and carefully estimating stopping times for the game near the boundary.
To this end, we present a geometric observation (Lemma
4.5
) and an estimate of stopping times for an alternative game (Lemma
4.6
) associated with the tug-of-war setting.
Compared to the random walk case, we need to take into account the strategies of each player in the game.
Under our setting, we could develop a suitable cancellation argument (cf.
[
MPR12
]
) to deal with those controlled processes.
And, in Section
6
, we extend our discussion to the general oblique derivative boundary value condition

(1.4)

\displaystyle\langle\beta,Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G

(Theorem
6.3
).
For those problems, we introduce stochastic processes containing random walks in ellipsoids relevant to the vector
\beta
.
Several geometric observations are required to derive the desired regularity under our oblique settings
(Proposition
6.1
-
6.2
).
We mention that the rotational invariance of balls plays an important role in deriving an estimate of terms associated with random walks in the normal case.
Of course, we cannot use this property for general
\beta
since ellipsoids are not rotationally invariant.
We will give a proper estimate of the additional terms which arose from random walks in the ellipsoids
so that our submartingale argument still works in the proof of Theorem
6.3
.

The boundary value problem (
1
) is motivated by two points of view.
When
\beta=\mathbf{n}
, the boundary condition of (
1
)
coincides with the Neumann (
\gamma=0
) or Robin boundary condition (
\gamma\neq 0
) for the Laplace equation.
In
[
Dan13
]
, game-theoretic interpretation was considered for the Neumann boundary value condition.
Such boundary conditions have been considered for the
p
-Laplace equations in the weak theory.
One can find works related to the Neumann problems for the
\infty
-Laplacian in
[
GMPR07
,
GMPR08
]
.
We also refer to
[
DS11
,
KP17
,
AGM22
]
for the
p
-Laplacian with the Robin boundary conditions, see also
[
AMNT22
,
DNOT23
,
DOS24
]
.
We remark that the boundary conditions are given in different forms from that of (
1
) since the boundary value problems are derived from the Euler-Lagrange equation: For the following energy functional

I(u)=\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p}\ dx+\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}\ dS-p\gamma\int_{\partial\Omega}gu\ dS,

its corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}u=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\
|Du|^{p-2}\langle\mathbf{n},Du\rangle+\gamma|u|^{p-2}u=\gamma G&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Meanwhile, the boundary condition can also be regarded as a special case of oblique derivative boundary conditions, that is, (
1.4
) with
|\langle\beta,\mathbf{n}\rangle|\geq\delta_{0}
for some
\delta_{0}>0
.
We present some preceding works
[
LZ18
,
CM21
]
regarding the regularity theory for fully nonlinear oblique derivative boundary value problems.
Calderon-Zygmund type regularity estimates for these problems can be found in
[
BH20
,
BH22
]
.
For further studies on oblique derivative boundary value problems, see for example,
[
GS84
,
DP96
,
Sof00
]
.
We also refer to
[
Lie13
]
for the general theory of the boundary value problems.

2.1.
Mean value characterization for (
1
)

We will provide a mean value characterization of solutions for the PDE (
1
).
This characterization gives a motivation of the DPP (
1.1
).
We begin this subsection by introducing some notations.
For two
n
-dimensional vectors
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
,
we define

\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}.

Similarly, for two
n\times n
matrices
A=(a_{ij})
and
B=(b_{ij})
, we define

\langle A:B\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}b_{ij}.

We write
B_{r}^{k}
to be the
k
-dimensional ball with the radius

, and

B_{r,d}^{k}=B_{r}^{k}\cap\{y_{k}<d\}

for
0\leq d\leq r
.
For each
x\in\Omega
, the projection of
x
to
\partial\Omega
will be denoted by
\pi_{\partial\Omega}x
.
We also define

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}(x)=\min\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\bigg\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad d^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\bigg\}

for each

.
If there is no room for confusion, we abbreviate

to

.

Mean value characterizations for the normalized
p
-Laplacian have been studied in several previous works.
We will set a DPP given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,

when

, where

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\varepsilon\}

is the inner
\varepsilon
-boundary strip of
\Omega
.
This DPP has a similar form to that covered in
[
MPR12
,
LP18
]
.
We remark that we take the supremum and infimum of the function within

We also have to consider the boundary condition of (

In

, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
LP23a
, Lemma 2.4]

one can find the following geometric observations,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)dy=-s_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathbf{e}_{n},

\displaystyle s_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{|B_{1}^{n-1}|}{(n+1)|B_{1,d_{\varepsilon}(x)}^{n}|}\varepsilon(1-d_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)\otimes(y-x)dy=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{n+2}I_{n}+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)),

.
Intuitively, it can be understood that if we cut an
\varepsilon
-ball along a plane, the geometric center of the remaining part moves in the normal direction of the plane.
In this case, the factor
s_{\varepsilon}(x)
represents the distance from the original center of the ball.
One can also check that the integration over the cut ball does not have much effect on the equation for the Laplacian.
These geometric properties enable to derive a mean value characterization for (
1
) with
p=2

just by considering the integration of the function over

(see
[
LP23a
, Theorem 3.1]

Now we state the mean value characterization for the case
2<p<\infty
.
To this end, we need to introduce new terminology and notation.
We say that
\Omega
satisfies the interior ball condition with the radius
\rho
if for each
x\in\partial\Omega
,
there exists a ball
B_{\rho}(z)\subset\Omega
for some
z\in\Omega
and

with

.
We can observe that if we assume

is a

-domain,

Next we fix
0<r<\rho
. Then for every

\pi_{\partial\Omega}(y)
to

Then there is a point

and
\pi_{\partial\Omega}(y)\in\partial B_{\rho}(z_{0})

In that case, we can define a function

One can think that it would be natural to consider the supremum or infimum over the cut ball in (

Unlike the case

To avoid this problem, we take the supremum and infimum over a smaller (but uncut) ball near the boundary.
We will investigate the relation between (

Lemma 2.1

Let

Assume that

Then

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}

.
If

\displaystyle=u(x)+\bigg\langle Du(x),\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)dy\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\bigg\langle D^{2}u(x):\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)\otimes(y-x)dy\bigg\rangle=\frac{\Delta u(x)}{n+2}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)).

Now we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\\
&=u(x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u(x)(d^{\prime}_{x}\varepsilon)^{2}+(1-\alpha)\big(\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x)-G(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x))\big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1-\alpha}{2(n+2)}\Delta u(x)\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))+o(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

Assume that
\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\geq\varepsilon
, that is,
d_{x}=1
. Then we have
s_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
and this yields

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\\
&=u(x)+\frac{\alpha}{8}\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u(x)\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2(n+2)}\Delta u(x)\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\\
&=u(x)+o(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

We used
\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0
in the last equality provided

\alpha=\frac{4(p-2)}{4p+n-6}.

Next, we consider the case
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\varepsilon
.
We see that
d^{\prime}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}
, and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy

\displaystyle=u(x)+\frac{\alpha}{8}\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u(x)\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2(n+2)}\Delta u(x)\varepsilon^{2}+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x)-G(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x))

\displaystyle\quad+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))+o(\varepsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=u(x)+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(x)-G(x))+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

We used the assumptions that
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
and
G\in C^{1}(\Gamma_{r_{0}})
to derive
the last estimate.
This gives

\displaystyle u(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)}\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)}{1+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)}G(x)+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

Since

\frac{1}{1+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)}=1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)),

we get

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(x)=&\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}\\
&+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x)+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))+o(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

Finally, we take into account near the boundary case, that is,
\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
Similarly, as above, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy

\displaystyle=u(x)+\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u(x)(d^{\prime}_{x})^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2(n+2)}\Delta u(x)\bigg)\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x)-G(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x))+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle=u(x)+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(x)-G(x))+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)).

We used

\bigg(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Delta_{\infty}^{N}u(x)(d^{\prime}_{x})^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{2(n+2)}\Delta u(x)\bigg)\varepsilon^{2}=O(\varepsilon^{2})=O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x))

since
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Now we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(x)&=\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}\\
&\quad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x)+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)).\end{split}

Combining (
2.8
), (
2.9
) with (
2.10
), we complete the proof.
∎

2.2.
Setting of the stochastic game

Here we construct a tug-of-war game associated with the DPP (
1.1
), that is,

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\bigg\}+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x).

We give a brief description of our game.
Our game is basically a tug-of-war with noise, where the step size is
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

and the random noise occurs in the
\varepsilon
-ball centered at the point.
But if the token is near the boundary,
the size of the tug-of-war is shrinking and the probability that the game ends at the point gets larger.
This game can be seen as a shrinking tug-of-war game, that is, the size of the step in the game is shrinking as the token is close to the boundary (for shrinking tug-of-war games, see
[
AL16
,
AL23
,
AL18
]
).

Now we construct the game rigorously as follows. We begin the game at
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
With a probability
\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
, the game is over and Player II pays Player I the payoff
G(x_{0})
.
Otherwise, the players play a tug-of-war game with noise.
They have a fair coin toss with a probability
\alpha
.
The winner of the coin toss can move the token within the ball
B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}(x_{0})
.
Meanwhile, with a probability
1-\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
, the token is randomly moved according to the uniform distribution in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\overline{\Omega}
.
We denote by
x_{1}
the new position of the token.
We can define
x_{2},x_{3},\dots
by repeating the same process and write
x_{\tau}

as the point where the game is over.

Set
\tilde{C}=\{0,1\}
.
For each
k=1,2,\dots
,
\xi_{k}\in[0,1]
is randomly selected with the uniform distribution,
and
c\in\tilde{C}
is defined as

\displaystyle c_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\textrm{if $\xi_{k-1}\leq 1-\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)$,}\\
1&\textrm{if $\xi_{k-1}>1-\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

A strategy is a Borel-measurable function giving the next position of the token.
For
j\in\{I,II\}
, we define

S_{j}^{k}:\to B_{d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}}(0)

for each
k=1,2,\dots
.
We also define
(\Upsilon,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}^{S_{I},S_{II}})
,
the countable product of
(\Upsilon_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{1}^{S_{I},S_{II}})
, by

\displaystyle\Upsilon

\displaystyle=(\Upsilon_{1})^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=\{\omega=\{(\omega_{i},b_{i})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}:w_{i}=\{w_{i}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},w_{i}^{j}\in B_{1}^{n},b_{1}\in(0,1)\textrm{ for all }i,j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Furthermore, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we set the probability space
(\Upsilon_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathbb{P}_{k}^{S_{I},S_{II}})
as the product on
k
-copies of
(\Upsilon_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathbb{P}_{1}^{S_{I},S_{II}})
.
Note that
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is identified with the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}

consisting of sets
A\times\prod_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{1}
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
.
We notice that
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
with
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{\varnothing,\Upsilon\}
is a filtration of
\mathcal{F}
.

We define the sequence of measurable functions
\{l_{i}^{\varepsilon}:\Upsilon\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
by

l_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)=\min\{l\geq 1:x+\varepsilon w_{i}^{l}\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega\}\qquad\textrm{for every }\omega\in\Upsilon,\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Since
l_{i}^{\varepsilon}
is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. finite, we can also set the sequence of vector-valued random variables
\{w_{i}^{\varepsilon,x}\}_{i=1}
by

w_{i}^{\varepsilon,x}(\omega)=w_{i}^{l_{i}^{\varepsilon}(\omega,x)}\qquad\textrm{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.e. }\omega\in\Upsilon.

Now we define the sequence of vector-valued random variables
\{X_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\}_{k=0}
by

X_{0}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\equiv x_{0}
and

(2.11)

X_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k-1}}S_{I}^{k-1}&\textrm{if $0<\xi_{k-1}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1}))$,}\\
X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k-1}}S_{II}^{k-1}&\\
&\hskip-80.00012pt\textrm{if $(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1}))\leq\xi_{k-1}<1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1})$,}\\
X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+\varepsilon w_{k}^{X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}}&\\
&\hskip-90.00014pt\textrm{if $\frac{\alpha}{2}(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1}))<\xi_{k-1}<(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1}))$,}\\
X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}&\textrm{if $1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1})<\xi_{k-1}<1$},\\
\end{array}\right.

for each
k=1,2,\dots
.
We note that
X_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable for each
k\geq 1
.
Given
\gamma>0
, we define
\tau^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}:\Upsilon\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}

by

\tau^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}(\omega)=\min\{k\geq 1:\xi_{k}<\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}})\}.

We also define the value functions for Players I and II as

(2.12)

\displaystyle u_{I}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[G(x_{\tau})]

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle u_{II}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=\inf_{S_{II}}\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[G(x_{\tau})],

respectively.
If the above two functions coincide, we can consider the value function of the game
as

u_{\varepsilon}=u_{I}^{\varepsilon}=u_{II}^{\varepsilon}.

In that case, the value function satisfies the DPP (
1.1
).

3.
The existence and uniqueness of value functions

In this section, we deal with the existence and uniqueness of value functions.
We first prove that the operator
T_{\varepsilon}^{G}
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(x)=&(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)\\
&\qquad\qquad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\bigg\}+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x).\end{split}

preserves continuity
for each
G\in C(\Gamma)
.
We note that this guarantees the existence of measurable strategies for players in each round of our game setting.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T_{\varepsilon}^{G}
be the operator in (
3.1
) with
G\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then
T_{\varepsilon}^{G}
preserves the continuity, that is,

T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
for any
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

First, we consider the case
x,z\in\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. In this case,

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x)\cap\Omega}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.

Then we observe that

\displaystyle\big|T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(x)-T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(z)\big|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)}u-\sup_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(z)}u-\inf_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(z)}u\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\bigg(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}u(y)dy-\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(z)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)}}u(y)dy\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\sup_{(y,w)\in B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(x)\times B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(z)}|u(y)-u(w)|+(1-\alpha)\bigg|\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}}u(y)dy-\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\omega_{u}(|x-z|)+2(1-\alpha)||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rho(|x-z|),

where
\omega_{u}
is the modulus of continuity of
u
and

\rho(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-\big(1-\frac{t}{2\varepsilon}\big)^{n}&\textrm{for $t<2\varepsilon$,}\\
1&\textrm{for $t\geq 2\varepsilon$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

We also consider the case
x\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
or
z\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
.
Recall that

s_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{|B_{1}^{n-1}|}{(n+1)|B_{1,d_{x}}^{n}|}\varepsilon(1-d_{x}^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}

and
0\leq d_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle|T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(x)-T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u(z)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon d_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{x}}(x)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}

\displaystyle\quad-(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(z))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{z}}(z)}u+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{z}}(z)}u\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon d_{z}}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{z}}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{z}}(z)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon d_{z}}(z)\cap\Omega}}u(y)dy\bigg\}\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\alpha)\big|\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x)-\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(z)G(z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{s_{\varepsilon}}(|x-z|)||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+2\omega_{u}(|x-z|)

\displaystyle\quad+4||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rho(|x-z|)+\gamma\omega_{s_{\varepsilon}}(|x-z|)||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}+\gamma\omega_{G}(|x-z|).

Since we assumed that
u
,
F
and
s_{\varepsilon}
are continuous, we see that
T_{\varepsilon}^{G}u
is continuous when
x\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
or
z\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We also have an equiboundedness for the family of functions satisfying (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
0<\varepsilon<<1
, let
u_{I}^{\varepsilon}
(
u_{II}^{\varepsilon}
, respectively) be the value function for Player I (Player II, respectively) satisfying (
1.1
) for a given boundary data
G\in C(\Gamma)
.
Then the family
u_{\varepsilon}^{G}
is equibounded.

Proof.

Recall (
2.12
) and (
2.13
).
Since we assumed that
G\in C(\Gamma)
, we directly have

||u_{\varepsilon}^{I}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},||u_{\varepsilon}^{II}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}.

Thus,
\{u_{I}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
\{u_{II}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
are equibounded.
∎

Let
u_{0}\equiv-||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}
and define
u_{k}=(T_{\varepsilon}^{G})^{k}u_{0}
for
n=1,2,\dots
.
We can see that
\{u_{k}(x)\}_{k=0}
is an increasing sequence for each
x\in\Omega
.
Then by the equiboundedness, we get that the sequence converges pointwise.
Let

\underline{u}(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(T_{\varepsilon}^{G})^{k}u_{0}(x).

Similarly, we can define

\overline{u}(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(T_{\varepsilon}^{G})^{k}u_{0}(x)

for
u_{0}\equiv||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}
.
We also observe that
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
\overline{\Omega}
, and

\underline{u}
and
\overline{u}
are lower and upper semicontinuous, respectively.

The following lemma gives the existence of functions satisfying (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\underline{u}
and
\overline{u}
be functions defined as above with
G\in C(\Gamma)
. Then
these two functions satisfy (
1.1
), respectively.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\underline{u}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}\underline{u}(y)dy\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x).

Since

\displaystyle\underline{u}(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(T_{\varepsilon}^{G})u_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x))\lim_{k\to\infty}\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}\hskip-20.00003ptu_{k}(y)dy\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x)G(x)

with
u_{0}\equiv-||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}
,
we need to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}\underline{u}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}u_{k}(y)dy\bigg\}.

We first see that

\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k},

since
\{u_{k}(x)\}
is an increasing sequence for each
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
On the other hand, for the infimum case,
we set

\lambda_{0}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k},

and
observe that there exists a point
y^{\ast}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that
u_{k}(y^{\ast})\leq\lambda_{0}
,
since each
u_{k}
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
and
u_{k}\leq u_{k+1}
in
\overline{\Omega}
for all
k\geq 0
. Hence,

\lambda_{0}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}\leq\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}\leq\underline{u}(y^{\ast})=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(y^{\ast})\leq\lambda_{0},

and it gives

\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}\underline{u}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x}}(x)}u_{k}.

Finally, since
|\underline{u}|\leq||g||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}
, we obtain the equalities for the integral terms
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Next, we show the uniqueness of the function satisfying (
1.1
).
To show that, we employ the arguments in preceding papers, for example,
[
MPR12
, Theorem 2.3]
or
[
AHP17
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

It holds
\overline{u}\leq u_{I}^{\varepsilon}\leq u_{II}^{\varepsilon}\leq\underline{u}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
u_{I}^{\varepsilon}\leq u_{II}^{\varepsilon}
, we need to show that
u_{II}^{\varepsilon}\leq\underline{u}
and
\overline{u}\leq u_{I}^{\varepsilon}
.
Without loss of generality, we only give proof of the first inequality.

Let
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. For
\zeta>0
, we set a strategy
S_{II}^{*}
for Player II such that

\underline{u}(S_{II}^{*}(x_{j}))=\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{j}}}(x_{j})}\underline{u}+\zeta 2^{-j}.

Fix a strategy
S_{I}
for Player I, and define a function
\Phi:\tilde{C}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(c,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{u}(x)&\textrm{if $c=0$,}\\
G(x)&\textrm{if $c=1$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{*}}^{x_{0}}\big[\Phi(c_{j+1},x_{j+1})+\zeta 2^{-(j+1)}|\big((c_{0},x_{0}),\dots,(c_{j},x_{j})\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{c_{j}}(\{0\})(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\underline{u}(S_{I}(x_{j}))+\underline{u}(S_{II}^{\ast}(x_{j}))\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}\underline{u}(y)dy\bigg\}+\mathbb{I}_{c_{j}}(\{1\})(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})G(x_{j})+\zeta 2^{-(j+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{I}_{c_{j}}(\{0\})(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}))\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{j}}}(x_{j})}\underline{u}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{j}}}(x_{j})}\underline{u}+\zeta 2^{-j}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})\cap\Omega}}\underline{u}(y)dy\bigg\}+\mathbb{I}_{c_{j}}(\{1\})(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})G(x_{j})+\zeta 2^{-(j+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(c_{j},x_{j})+\zeta 2^{-j}.

Thus, we see that
(\Phi(c_{k},x_{k})+\zeta 2^{-k})_{k=0}
is a supermartingale.
By using the optimal stopping theorem, we have

\displaystyle u_{II}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\inf_{S_{II}}\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[G(x_{\tau})]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[G(x_{\tau})]

\displaystyle=\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[\Phi(c_{\tau+1},x_{\tau+1})]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[\Phi(c_{\tau+1},x_{\tau+1})+\zeta 2^{-(\tau+1)}]

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(c_{0},x_{0})+\zeta

\displaystyle=\underline{u}(x_{0})+\zeta.

Since
\zeta
can be arbitrarily chosen, we have
u_{II}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\leq\underline{u}(x_{0})
.

Similarly, we can also derive
u_{I}^{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\geq\overline{u}(x_{0})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.4
with
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
\overline{\Omega}
,
we have
\overline{u}=u_{I}^{\varepsilon}=u_{II}^{\varepsilon}=\underline{u}=:u_{\varepsilon}
.
Finally, we get the uniqueness and the continuity of the game value
u_{\varepsilon}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume that
G\in C(\Gamma)
.
There exists a unique function
u_{\varepsilon}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying (
1.1
).

4.
Regularity of the value function

In this section, we are concerned with the interior and boundary regularity for DPPs relevant to (
1.1
).
The main result is the boundary Hölder estimate for the function satisfying (
1.1
), Theorem
4.4
.
For the random walk process, this type of regularity was already studied in Section 8 of
[
LP23b
]
.
Compared to that paper, the key to the proof of boundary regularity is how to control the terms arising from tug-of-war games.
Such regularity results are essential to obtain the convergence result in the next section.

We first investigate interior regularity for functions satisfying (
1.1
).
There have been several interior regularity estimates for value functions of tug-of-war games by employing coupling arguments, for example,
[
MPR12
,
AHP17
,
ALPR20
]
.
Here we give a proof of interior estimates for the solution to (

) based on the ABP and Krylov-Safanov type theory in
[
AP24

BPR23
]
.

To this end, we denote by
\mathcal{M}(B_{\Lambda})
the set of symmetric unit Radon measures with
support in
B_{\Lambda}
.
We also set
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{M}(B_{\Lambda})
such that

which defines a Borel measurable function for every Borel measurable

The following notion of extremal operators can be found in
[
AP24
]
(cf.
[
BPR23
]

Definition 4.1
.

Let

We define the extremal Pucci type operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{+}u(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\alpha\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B_{\Lambda})}\int_{B_{\Lambda}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)d\mu(h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)dh\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\alpha\sup_{h\in B_{\Lambda}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)dh\bigg)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-}u(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\alpha\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B_{\Lambda})}\int_{B_{\Lambda}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)d\mu(h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)dh\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\alpha\inf_{h\in B_{\Lambda}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\delta u(x,\varepsilon h)dh\bigg),

where

\varepsilon_{0}>0

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{B_{1}}u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon h)d\mu_{\nu}(h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}u_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon h)dh=\frac{1}{2}\big(I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{-\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)

and this implies

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{B_{1}}\delta u_{\varepsilon}(x,\varepsilon h)d\mu_{\nu}(h)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\delta u_{\varepsilon}(x,\varepsilon h)dh=I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{-\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-2u_{\varepsilon}(x).

Now we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\frac{I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{-\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-2u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{+}u_{\varepsilon}(x),

and hence, we observe that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg(\sup_{|\nu|=1}I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+\inf_{|\nu|=1}I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-2u_{\varepsilon}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\sup_{|\nu|=1}\big(I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{-\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-2u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{+}u_{\varepsilon}(x).

Similarly, we also get

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{-}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\inf_{|\nu|=1}\big(I_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+I_{\varepsilon}^{-\nu}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-2u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\leq 0.

Therefore, we can derive the following interior Hölder estimate (cf.
[
LP18
,
ALPR20
]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain and
B_{2r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
for some
R>0
.
Then, for the function satisfying (
1.1
), there exist
C>0
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
both independent of
\varepsilon

so that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(z)|\leq C||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R})}\bigg(\frac{|x-z|^{\sigma}}{R^{\sigma}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\sigma}}{R^{\sigma}}\bigg)

for each
x,z\in B_{R}(x_{0})
.

We state the main theorem in this section, which gives a boundary regularity estimate for (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the function satisfying (
1.1
) with
G\in C(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
for each
\varepsilon>0
, and
\sigma\in(0,1)
as in Lemma

.
There exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every

and

with

\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega),\operatorname{dist}(y_{0},\partial\Omega)\leq\delta^{1/2},

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-u_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})|\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}

There have been several preceding boundary regularity results for value functions such as
[
MPR12
,
PR16
,
Han22
]
.
In these papers, constructing suitable submartingales is a key step to deriving the desired boundary estimate.
For instance, the estimate of stopping time for the game played an important role in the proof of the boundary regularity in
[
MPR12
]
.
To this end, they constructed an alternative stochastic game and estimated its stopping time.
We want to apply a similar argument to our problem here.
Recall the definition of the function
Z^{\rho}
in Section
2.1
.
The following geometrical observation is preparatory work for that.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain satisfying the interior ball condition with the radius
\rho>0
.
Fix
r\in(0,\frac{\rho}{2})
and
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{r}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
depending on
n,q,\rho
and
\alpha
such that for any sufficiently small

\varepsilon>0
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|x_{0}-y_{0}|-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\big)^{-q}&\hskip-5.0pt+\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|x_{0}-y_{0}|+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\big)^{-q}\hskip-5.0pt+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}|z-y_{0}|^{-q}dz\\
&\geq|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}+C_{0}(s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})+\varepsilon^{2}),\end{split}

where

and
y_{0}=Z^{\rho}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

The basic idea of this proof comes from the convexity of the function
x\mapsto|x|^{-q}
.
By Taylor expansion, we observe that

|z-y_{0}|^{-q}=|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}+\langle D\phi(x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}\phi(x_{0})(z-x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

where

\phi(z)=|z-y_{0}|^{-q}
.
We also see that

D\phi(x_{0})=-q|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-2}(x_{0}-y_{0})

and

D^{2}\phi(x_{0})=q|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-4}\big((q+2)(x_{0}-y_{0})\otimes(x_{0}-y_{0})-|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}I_{n}\big).

Then, we get

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\big(|x_{0}-y_{0}|-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\big)^{-q}+\frac{1}{2}\big(|x_{0}-y_{0}|+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}}\big)^{-q}\\
&=|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}+\frac{q(q+1)}{2}(\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{x_{0}})^{2}|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})\geq|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}\end{split}

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}|z-y_{0}|^{-q}dz\\
&=|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}+\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\langle D\phi(x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle dz\\
&\qquad\quad+\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}\phi(x_{0})(z-x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle dz+o(\varepsilon^{2})\\
&\geq|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q}+C_{1}(s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})+\varepsilon^{2}),\end{split}

for some
C_{1}>0
depending on
q,n
and
\rho
.
We remark that the last inequality is obtained by

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\langle D\phi(x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle dz

\displaystyle=-q|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-2}\bigg\langle x_{0}-y_{0},\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}(z-x_{0})dz\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=q|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-1}\bigg\langle\frac{x_{0}-y_{0}}{|x_{0}-y_{0}|},\mathbf{n}(\pi_{\partial\Omega}(x_{0}))\bigg\rangle s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))\geq C_{1}s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})

and

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}\langle D^{2}\phi(x_{0})(z-x_{0}),z-x_{0}\rangle dz

\displaystyle=q|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{-q-4}\times

\displaystyle\quad\bigg\langle(q+2)(x_{0}-y_{0})\otimes(x_{0}-y_{0})-|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}I_{n}:\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}}(z-x_{0})\otimes(z-x_{0})dz\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{q-n+2}{n+2}|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))\geq C_{1}\varepsilon^{2},

provided
q>n-2
(we can always choose such constant

).

We finally derive (

) by choosing

.
∎

Now we consider an alternative game with a slightly different setting from the original game.
The basic setting is the same as before, but we do not finish the game even if the token is located in
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
.
Instead, the game must end when the token moves far enough away from the boundary.
We define the stopping time
\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}
and a sequence of random
variables
\{S_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
by

\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}=\min\{k\geq 0:|Z^{\rho}(X_{k})-X_{k}|<\rho-h\}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad S_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k}).

In
[
LP23b
, Lemma 8.3]
, we can find an estimate for
\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}
and
S_{\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}
when
p=2.

The following lemma is a sort of corresponding result for those stopping times under our setting.
We derive an estimate for
\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}
and
S_{\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}
under a fixed strategy for Player II, which intuitively appears to minimize them.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\overline{r}<r
with
r
as above.
Assume that
|x_{0}-Z^{\rho}(x_{0})|>\rho-h
for
h\in(0,\frac{\overline{r}}{2}-\varepsilon)
and we fix the strategy
S_{II}^{\ast}
to pull towards
Z_{\rho}(X_{k})
for Player II.
Then for every small
\varepsilon>0
,
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[\varepsilon^{2}\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}]\leq C\overline{r}^{-n-1}(h+\varepsilon)

for some constant
C
depending on
n,\alpha
and
\rho
.

Proof.

We abbreviate
\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}
by
\overline{\tau}
.
We consider a sequence of random variable
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=0}
with

Q_{k}=|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|^{-n}-C_{0}(k\varepsilon^{2}+S_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}),

where
C_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
4.5
.

To show that
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\overline{\tau}}
is a submartingale with the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k=0}
, we observe that for each
k<\overline{\tau}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[Q_{k+1}-Q_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[|X_{k+1}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k+1})|^{-n}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|^{-n}-C_{0}(\varepsilon^{2}+s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})).

Since
\Omega
satisfies the interior ball condition with the radius
\rho
and
h<\frac{\overline{r}}{2}-\varepsilon<\rho

we have

\bigg|X_{k}-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\frac{X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})}{|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|}\bigg|=|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}},

and

\sup_{\nu\in B_{1}}\big|X_{k}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\nu-Z^{\rho}\big(X_{k}+d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\nu\big)\big|=|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}.

Furthermore, we also see that

|y-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|\geq|y-Z^{\rho}(y)|=\rho-\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)

for each
y\in B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega
.
Hence, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[|X_{k+1}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k+1})|^{-n}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\big)^{-n}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\big)^{-n}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}|z-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|^{-n}dz

\displaystyle\geq|X_{k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{k})|^{-n}+C_{0}(\varepsilon^{2}+s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k}))

for any strategy
S_{I}
.
From the above estimates, we see that
Q_{k}
is a supermartingale.

Now we fix
k\geq 1
and apply the optional stopping theorem to
\overline{\tau}\wedge k
, obtaining

\displaystyle\overline{r}^{-n}\leq\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[Q_{0}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[Q_{\overline{\tau}\wedge k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[|X_{\overline{\tau}\wedge k}-Z^{\rho}(X_{\overline{\tau}\wedge k})|^{-n}]-C_{0}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[(\overline{\tau}\wedge k)\varepsilon^{2}+S_{\overline{\tau}\wedge k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}]

\displaystyle\leq(\overline{r}-h-\varepsilon)^{-n}-C_{0}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[(\overline{\tau}\wedge k)\varepsilon^{2}+S_{\overline{\tau}\wedge k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}].

We note that
|Q_{k}|<(\overline{r}-h-\varepsilon)^{-n}
and hence the optional stopping theorem is applicable.
This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}[\overline{\tau}\varepsilon^{2}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}]\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}}\big((\overline{r}-h-\varepsilon)^{-n}-\overline{r}^{-n}\big)\leq C\overline{r}^{-n-1}(h+\varepsilon)

for any strategy
S_{I}
and some
C
depending on
n,\rho
and
\alpha
.
Now we can complete the proof by taking the supremum over
S_{I}
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem in this section.
The main difficulty compared to the case
p=2
is to control additional terms arising from the game process.
We will employ a cancellation method similar to
[
MPR12
]
. But the original argument cannot be applied directly,
since the radius of the tug-of-war shrinks near the boundary and thus the set of
S_{I}
and
S_{II}
are different in this case.
To overcome this issue, we consider a modified version of that argument.
This enables us to control additional terms due to the game setting being small enough.

From now on, we as
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small and
\overline{r}<r<\frac{\rho}{2}
.
We define

\{Z^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},x_{0}}(X_{k}),Z^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},y_{0}}(Y_{k}),S_{k}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}},S_{k}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\}_{n=0}

with the stopping times
\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}},\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,y_{0}},
respectively.
For simplicity, we use the notations
Z_{k}^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},x_{0}},Z_{k}^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},y_{0}}

instead of
Z^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},x_{0}}(X_{k}),Z^{\varepsilon,\overline{r},y_{0}}(Y_{k})
.
We also define

\overline{\tau}=\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,x_{0}}\wedge\overline{\tau}^{\varepsilon,\rho,h,y_{0}}.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

The proof can be divided into three parts, similar to the proof of
[
LP23b
, Theorem 8.1]
. We observe the following decomposition

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-u_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})|\\
&\leq\big|\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]-u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\big|+\big|\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{y_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]-u_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})\big|\\
&\quad+\big|\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{y_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]\big|.\end{split}

We estimate the first two terms on the right hand side of (
4.6
) in the first step.
To deal with the last term, we will construct a proper supermartingale in the second step.
In the last step, we derive our desired estimate by using the supermartingale.

Let
h=\delta^{1/2}
and set a sequence of random variables
\{M_{k}\}_{k=0}
defined as

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{k}=u_{\varepsilon}&(X_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{i-1})\big)\\
&+(1-\alpha)\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\bigg(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j})G(X_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1}))\bigg).\end{split}

To guarantee the well-definedness of the last term on the right hand side, we considered an arbitrarily continuous extension of
G
to
\overline{\Omega}
in
\Omega\backslash I_{\varepsilon}

and still use the notation
G
for convenience.
We also remark that
M_{k}
does not depend on the extension of
G
since
s_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
in
\Omega\backslash I_{\varepsilon}
.
Then, we observe that for each
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[M_{k+1}-M_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}}(X_{k})}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}}(X_{k})}u_{\varepsilon}\bigg)+(1-\alpha)\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\cap\Omega}}u_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{i-1})\big)-u_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{i-1})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})G(X_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1})\big)

\displaystyle=-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})G(X_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})G(X_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(1-\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1})=0.

Hence,
M_{k}
is a martingale with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k=0}
.
Then we can apply the optional stopping theorem since

\displaystyle|M_{k\wedge\overline{\tau}}|\leq||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}+\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[S_{k\wedge\overline{\tau}-1}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}]

is equibounded by Lemma
4.6
.
We have

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=M_{0}

\displaystyle=\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[M_{\overline{\tau}}]

\displaystyle=\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}\bigg[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})\prod_{i=1}^{\overline{\tau}}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{i-1})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{\tau}-1}\bigg(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j})G(X_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1})\bigg)\bigg].

By using the following property

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-a_{i})\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}

for every
k
and
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]

\displaystyle=\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}\bigg[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{\overline{\tau}}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{i-1})\big)-1\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(1-\alpha)\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{\overline{\tau}-1}\bigg(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j})G(X_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{j}\big(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(X_{j-1})\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}\big[2\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\sup_{S_{I}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}^{\ast}}^{x_{0}}\big[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\overline{r}^{-n-1}C\delta^{1/2}

for some
C=C(n,\alpha,\rho)
.
The reversed inequality can be derived by using a similar argument.
Therefore, we have

|u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]|\leq 2\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\overline{r}^{-n-1}C\delta^{1/2},

|u_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{y_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]|\leq 2\gamma||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\overline{r}^{-n-1}C\delta^{1/2}.

Now we need to estimate

\big|\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{y_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]\big|.

We write strategies for the game process with the starting point at
x_{0}
as
S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x}
.
Similarly, we also use the notation
S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}
for the process starting at
y_{0}
.
Observe that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big|\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{x_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})]-\sup_{S_{I}}\inf_{S_{II}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I},S_{II}}^{y_{0}}[u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]\big|\\
&=\big|\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})]\big|\\
&\leq\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|].\end{split}

We remark that we can consider the following decomposition:

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}>1\}}]\\
&\ +\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}}]\\
&\ +\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}}].\end{split}

To derive our desired result, we have to estimate

\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|].

Observe that for each
k
, if
\operatorname{dist}(X_{k},\partial\Omega),\operatorname{dist}(Y_{k},\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2},

because we can copy the strategies
S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{y}
for
S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{x}
in this case.

Suppose that
\operatorname{dist}(X_{k},\partial\Omega)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
or
\operatorname{dist}(Y_{k},\partial\Omega)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
For the random walk, we get the following estimate

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{\alpha}{2}<\xi_{k}<1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\}]\\
&\leq(1-\alpha)|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)\end{split}

for some universal
C>0

(see the proof of
[
LP23b
, Theorem 8.1]
).

Next, we have to handle the terms corresponding to tug-of-war games.
First, we assume that Player I won the coin toss.
In that case, we have to estimate

\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{I}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big].

Fix the strategy
\nu_{x}\in B_{1}
for
S_{I}^{x}
.
If
|X_{k}-Y_{k}|\geq\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}+d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})
, we select a unit vector
\nu_{y}
with

\nu_{y}=\frac{X_{k}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\nu_{x}-Y_{k}}{|X_{k}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\nu_{x}-Y_{k}|},

which is minimizing
|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|
.
Then by the observation

|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|=\big|X_{k}+\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}\nu_{x}-Y_{k}\big|-\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}),

we have

\inf_{Y_{k+1}\in B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}}(Y_{k})}|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})

for every possible
X_{k+1}
. Similarly, we also obtain

\inf_{X_{k+1}\in B_{\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}}(X_{k})}|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{X_{k}})

for every possible
Y_{k+1}
if Player II won the coin toss.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})\big)^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{X_{k}})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))\big)

\displaystyle\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(1-\alpha)(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))+\varepsilon^{2}(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})^{2}.

We also check that
|d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and this implies for any
X_{k},Y_{k}\in\Omega
that

\varepsilon^{2}(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(Z)\leq C_{0}\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})),

where
C_{0}=\frac{(n+1)|B_{1,1/2}^{n}|}{4|B_{1}^{n-1}|}(4/3)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}>0
and
Z\in\Omega
with
d_{Z}=\frac{1}{2}
.
From the above observation, we finally get

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]\\
&\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))\end{split}

for some
C>0
.
In the case
|X_{k}-Y_{k}|<\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}+d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})
, we see that

|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|<2\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}+d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})\leq 2\varepsilon,

whatever strategies are chosen.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq 4\alpha\varepsilon^{2}+(1-\alpha)\big(\varepsilon^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))

for some universal
C>0
.
Then we can construct the following sequence of random variables
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=0}
such that

Q_{k}=|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}e^{-C(S_{k}^{x_{0}}+S_{k}^{y_{0}})}-C\varepsilon(S_{k}^{x_{0}}+S_{k}^{y_{0}}),

and check that it is a supermartingale.

Now we can apply the optional stopping theorem to
Q_{k}
by virtue of Lemma
4.6
, since

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x,\ast}}^{x_{0}}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}]+\sup_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y,\ast}}^{y_{0}}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}]

(we have used notations
S_{II}^{x,\ast},S_{II}^{y,\ast}
are corresponding to
S_{II}^{\ast}
in Lemma
4.6
).
It gives

\displaystyle\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|x_{0}-y_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{2}e^{-C(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}})}-C\varepsilon(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}})].

From Lemma
4.6
, we already know that

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\sup_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x,\ast},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y,\ast}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}]\leq C\delta^{1/2},

and this yields

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}>1)\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

We also deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-C}\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{2}e^{-C(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}})}]

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\delta^{2}+\varepsilon\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\sup_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x,\ast},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y,\ast}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}]\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2}+C\varepsilon\delta^{1/2}\leq C\delta^{2},

if
\varepsilon<<\delta
.
Therefore, we get

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{\sigma}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}]\leq C\delta^{\sigma}.

Similarly, combining (
4.12
) with (
4.13
), we also have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}\cap\bigg\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\bigg\}\bigg)\leq\frac{C\delta}{\delta_{0}}.

It still remains to estimate

\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}}].

By the definition of
\overline{\tau}
, we see that

\max\{\operatorname{dist}(X_{\overline{\tau}},\partial\Omega),\operatorname{dist}(Y_{\overline{\tau}},\partial\Omega)\}>\delta^{1/2}.

Assume that
\delta<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}
. Then, by Theorem
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|

\displaystyle\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\frac{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{\sigma}+\varepsilon^{\sigma}}{\delta^{\sigma/2}}

a.s. in
\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}\cap\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}.

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\frac{C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}}{\delta^{\sigma/2}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{\sigma}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}]+\varepsilon^{\sigma}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}(\delta^{\sigma}+\varepsilon^{\sigma})}{\delta^{\sigma/2}}\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}.

We have used (
4.13
) in the second inequality.
Combining the above estimates, we finally get

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|]\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}.

This implies the desired result.
∎

5.
Convergence of the value function as
\varepsilon\to 0

Relations between a DPP and its associated equation have been investigated in many preceding studies, for example,
[
KMP12
,
MPR12
,
AHP17
]
.
In this section, we take into account the convergence of the function satisfying (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
We will also verify that the limit of value functions solves (
1
) in some sense.

To this end, we first introduce a notion of viscosity solutions.

Definition 5.1

(viscosity solution)
.

A function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity solution to (
1
) if
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}
such that
u(x)=\phi(x)
and
u(y)>\phi(y)
for
y\neq x
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x)\leq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\Omega$,}\\
\min\big\{\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x),\gamma G(x)-\big(\langle\mathbf{n},D\phi\rangle(x)+\gamma\phi(x)\big)\big\}\leq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\partial\Omega$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

and
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}
such that
u(x)=\phi(x)
and
u(y)<\phi(y)
for
y\neq x
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x)\geq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\Omega$,}\\
\max\big\{\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x),\gamma G(x)-\big(\langle\mathbf{n},D\phi\rangle(x)+\gamma\phi(x)\big)\big\}\geq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\partial\Omega$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Combining Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
4.3
with an Arzelà-Ascoli criterion,
we get the following convergence result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the function satisfying (
1.1
) and
G\in C^{1}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
for each
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, there exists a function
u:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
such that
u_{\varepsilon_{i}}
converges uniformly to
u
on
\overline{\Omega}
and

u
is a viscosity solution to the problem (
1
).

Proof.

For the interior case, we can prove the convergence by employing a similar argument in the proof of
[
MPR12
, Theorem 4.9]
.

We will prove that

\langle\mathbf{n},Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G\qquad\textrm{on }\partial\Omega

in the viscosity sense.
Let
x\in\partial\Omega
and
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})

such that
u-\phi
has a strict local minimum at
x
.
Then we observe that

\inf_{B_{r}(x)}(u-\phi)=u(x)-\phi(x)\leq u(z)-\phi(z)

for some
r>0
any
z\in B_{r}(x)
, and the equality holds when
z=x
.

For simplicity, we just write
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
instead of
\{u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
uniformly, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\inf_{B_{r}(x)}(u_{\varepsilon}-\phi)<u_{\varepsilon}(z)-\phi(z)

for all
z\in B_{r}(x)\backslash\{x\}
when
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small.
Thus, for any

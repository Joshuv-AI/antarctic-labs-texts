Trimming and Building Freezing Sets
Laurence Boxer

Abstract
We develop new tools for the construction of fixed point sets in digital topology. We define excludable points and show that these may be excluded from all freezing sets. We show that articulation points are excludable. We also present results concerning points that must belong to a freezing set and often are easily recognized. These include points of degree 1 and some local extrema. Key words and phrases: digital topology, digital image, degree, articulation point, freezing set, 1-coordinate local extreme point MSC: 54H25

4
Excludable
sets

We develop the notion of an excludable set and
show how this notion helps us determine
minimal or small freezing sets.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\kappa)
be
a connected digital
image. Let
W\subset X
. We say

is
excludable from freezing sets for
(X,\kappa)

(

for short) if for
every freezing set

(X,\kappa)
,
if
A\setminus W\neq\emptyset
then

is
also a freezing set. If
p\in W

then

is an

excludable point
.

Remark 4.2
.

By Definition
4.1
, if
A
is
a minimal freezing
set and

is
excludable for
(X,\kappa)
, then

A\cap W=\emptyset
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,\kappa)

be a finite connected
digital image.
Let
W
be an
excludable set
for
X
. Then
every subset
of
W
is excludable.

Proof.

This follows from
Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
F:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)

be an isomorphism
of digital images.
If
W
is
an excludable
set for
X
then

is
an excludable
set for

.

Proof.

Let
A
be a freezing
set for
X
. Let

such that

f|_{F(A)\setminus F(W)}=\id_{F(A)\setminus F(W)}

(2)

Then

.

Let
b\in F(A)\setminus F(W)
. Then

a=F^{-1}(b)\in A\setminus W
. We have

g(a)=F^{-1}(f(b))=F^{-1}(b)=a.

Since every

satisfies

for some

, we have

. Since

is excludable,

.
Therefore,

Thus,

is
excludable.
∎

the finite connected
digital image

(X,\kappa)
, with

W\neq\emptyset
. If
W
is
a proper subset of
X
,
then
W
is excludable.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
freezing set for

X
. Let
f\in C(X,\kappa)
such
that
f|_{A\setminus W}=\id_{A\setminus W}
.
Let
x_{0}\in W
.
Then there exist distinct
components
K_{1},K_{2}
of

X\setminus\{x_{0}\}
.
By Lemma
5.1
,
there exists a retraction

r
of
X
to
X\setminus K_{1}
. It follows that

A\cap K_{1}\neq\emptyset
,
for otherwise

r|_{A}=\id_{A}

yet
r\neq\id_{X}
,
contrary to
A
being
a freezing set.
Similarly,
A\cap K_{2}\neq\emptyset
.

By Lemma
5.2
,

f(x_{0})=x_{0}
. Since
x_{0}

was taken as an arbitrary
member of
W
, we have

f|_{A\setminus W}=\id_{A\setminus W}~~~\Rightarrow~~~f|_{A\cup W}=\id|_{A\cup W}~~~\Rightarrow~~~f|_{A}=\id_{A}~~~\Rightarrow~~~f=\id_{X}.

Thus
W
is excludable.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Theorem
5.3

implies that if

(X,\kappa)
is a
wedge of two finite
digital images,

(X,\kappa)=(X_{1},\kappa)\vee(X_{2},\kappa)
, then
the “wedge point”
of
X
is
excludable, hence
does not belong
to any minimal
freezing set for

(X,\kappa)
.

6
1-D local extrema

We introduce a kind of local extreme point
and study the relationship of such a point
to a freezing set.

6.1
Definition and relation to freezing sets

Let
(X,\kappa)
be
a digital image, where

X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Let

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X
, where each

x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We say

x
is a

1-coordinate
local maximum at
index
i
for
(X,\kappa)

if for some index
i

and all
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in N(X,x,\kappa)
,

x_{i}>y_{i}
; and

x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i}-1,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a

justifying neighbor
at index
i
of
x
.
We say

x
is a

1-coordinate
local minimum at
index
i
for
(X,\kappa)

if for some index
i

and all
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in N(X,x,\kappa)
,

x_{i}<y_{i}
; and

x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i}+1,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a

justifying neighbor

of
x
.
We say

x
is a

1-coordinate
local extremum for
(X,\kappa)

if
x
is either a
1-coordinate
local maximum or a
1-coordinate
local minimum for
(X,\kappa)
. Such points
are often easily recognized, and, we will
show, often are members
of minimal freezing sets.

Note if
x
and
x^{\prime}

are, respectively, a
1-coordinate local
extremum and its
justifying neighbor, then

x
and
x^{\prime}
differ in
exactly one index. Thus,

x\leftrightarrow_{c_{1}}x^{\prime}
.

Neither being nor not
being a 1-coordinate
local extremum is necessarily
preserved by isomorphism, as the
following shows.

Example 6.1
.

Let

X=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\mid|x|+|y|=2\}~~~~\mbox{(see Figure~\ref{fig:diamondRadius2})}.

Figure 2:
The digital image
(X,c_{2})

of Example
6.1
.
The “corner” points

(2,0),~(0,2),~(-2,0),~(0,-2)

are 1-coordinate local
extrema. None of them has
a justifying neighbor.

Assume the points of
X
are indexed circularly with

X=\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{7}
, with
x_{0}=(2,0)
,
x_{1}=(1,1)
,
etc. Then
(X,c_{2})
is a digital simple closed curve,
for which the “corner points”

x_{0},x_{2},x_{4},x_{6}
are the 1-coordinate local
extrema. Let
f:X\to X
be the function

f(x_{i})=x_{i+1\mod 8}.

Then
f
is a
(c_{2},c_{2})
-isomorphism.
For each index
i
,

•

if
x_{i}
is a 1-coordinate local
extremum, then
f(x_{i})
is not
a 1-coordinate local extremum; and

•

if
x_{i}
is not a 1-coordinate local
extremum, then
f(x_{i})
is
a 1-coordinate local extremum.

We have the following.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(X,c_{u})
be
a connected digital image, where

X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and

1\leq u\leq n
. Let

x_{0}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X
, where each

x_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Suppose,
for some index
i
,

x_{0}
is a 1-coordinate
local extremum for

(X,c_{u})
with a
justifying neighbor

x^{\prime}\in X

at index
i
. Let

A
be a freezing set
for
(X,c_{u})
. Then

x_{0}\in A
.

Proof.

There are at most
u

indices
j
, one of
which is
j=i
,
at which

|x_{j}-p_{j}(x^{\prime})|=1
and
for all other indices
k
,

x_{k}=p_{k}(x^{\prime})
.

Therefore, if

A\subset X
and

x_{0}\not\in A
,
consider the function

f:X\to X
given by

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x&\mbox{if }x\neq x_{0};\\
x^{\prime}&\mbox{if }x=x_{0},\end{array}\right.

Then for
x\leftrightarrow_{c_{u}}y
,

f(x)
and
f(y)

differ in at most

u-1
indices. It
follows easily that

f\in C(X,c_{u})
.
Further,
f|_{A}=\id_{A}

and
f\neq\id_{X}
. Hence

x_{0}\not\in A
implies

A
is not a freezing set.
The assertion
follows.
∎

Example

We demonstrate how articulation points and

can help us determine

Example 6.3
.

Let

.

7
Bounds on size of freezing set

How small, and how big,
can a freezing set be? We provide
bounds on the size of a freezing set in
the following.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(X,c_{u})
be
a connected finite digital image, where

X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
n>1
, and

1\leq u\leq n
. Let
D_{1}
be the set of
points that have degree 1 in
(X,c_{u})
.
Let
T
be the set of 1-coordinate
local extrema of
(X,c_{u})

that have justifying
points in
X
.
Let
W
be the set
of articulation points of
(X,c_{u})
.
Then there is a minimal
freezing set
A\subset Bd(X)

for
(X,c_{u})
such that

\#(D_{1}\cup T)\leq\#A.

If
\emptyset\neq W

and
W
is a proper subset of
X
, then

\#(D_{1}\cup T)\leq\#A\leq\#Bd(X)-\#W.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.15
, there is
a minimal freezing set

A\subset Bd(X)
for
(X,c_{u})
.
We have
T\subset Bd(X)
,

D_{1}\subset Bd(X)
, and, since
n>1
,
W\subset Bd(X)
.
Thus the conclusion follows from
Theorems
5.3
,
3.1
,
and
6.2
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

We need
n>1
in
Theorem
7.1
,
since if
X=[0,2]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
,
we have that
1
is an
articulation point for
(X,c_{1})

but is not a member of
Bd(X)
.

8
Further remarks

We have presented the notion
of excludable points in
digital topology, and
have shown that these may be
excluded from all freezing
sets. We have shown that
articulation points are
excludable. We have shown
that points of degree 1
and certain local extrema
are points that must be
included in freezing sets.
We have obtained bounds on
the cardinality of a minimal
freezing set for a connected
digital image.

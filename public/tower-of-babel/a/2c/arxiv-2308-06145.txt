Finding Long Directed Cycles Is Hard Even When DFVS Is Small Or Girth Is Large
Ashwin Jacob, Michał Włodarczyk, Meirav Zehavi

Abstract
We study the parameterized complexity of two classic problems on directed graphs: Hamiltonian Cycle and its generalization Longest Cycle . Since 2008, it is known that Hamiltonian Cycle is W[1]-hard when parameterized by directed treewidth [Lampis et al., ISSAC’08]. By now, the question of whether it is FPT parameterized by the directed feedback vertex set (DFVS) number has become a longstanding open problem. In particular, the DFVS number is the largest natural directed width measure studied in the literature. In this paper, we provide a negative answer to the question, showing that even for the DFVS number, the problem remains W[1]-hard. As a consequence, we also obtain that Longest Cycle is W[1]-hard on directed graphs when parameterized multiplicatively above girth, in contrast to the undirected case. This resolves an open question posed by Fomin et al. [ACM ToCT’21] and Gutin and Mnich [arXiv:2207.12278]. Our hardness results apply to the path versions of the problems as well. On the positive side, we show that Longest Path parameterized multiplicatively above girth belongs to the class XP.

Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS.

Before describing the ideas behind our reduction, we provide some background about the related
Disjoint Paths

Here, we are given a graph with

(s_{i},t_{i})

this is not the case in our context.

While

What is interesting for us is that when the input is restricted to be a DAG, then

The latter result suggests a starting point for extending the hardness to our problem.
A simple idea to design a reduction from
Disjoint Paths
on DAGs is to just insert edges
t_{1}\to s_{2},t_{2}\to s_{3},\dots,t_{k}\to s_{1}
.
Then the set
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}
becomes a DFVS and the existence of
k
disjoint
(s_{i},t_{i})
-paths implies the existence of a long cycle.
There is a catch, though.
This construction might turn a No-instance into an instance with a long cycle because the cycle might traverse the vertices
s_{i},t_{i}
in a different order, corresponding to a family of disjoint paths which does not form a solution to the original instance.
To circumvent this, we open the black box and
give a reduction directly from the basic W[1]-complete problem—
Multicolored Clique
—while adapting some ideas from the hardness proof for

Disjoint Paths
on DAGs by Slivkins
[
73
]
.

The construction by Slivkins uses two kinds of gadgets.
First, for each
i=1,\dots,k
one needs a
choice gadget
comprising two long directed paths with some arcs from the first path to the second one.
The solution path corresponding to the
i
-th choice gadget must choose one of these arcs to “change the lane”; the location of this change encodes the choice of the
i
-th vertex in the clique.
Next, for each pair
(i,j)
, where
1\leq i<j\leq k
, one needs a
verification gadget
to check whether the
i
-th and
j
-th chosen vertices are adjacent.
The corresponding solution path must traverse the
i
-th and
j
-th choice gadgets around the locations of their transitions.
The arcs between the choice gadgets are placed in such a way that both these locations can be visited only when the corresponding edge exists in the original graph.

Our reduction relies on a similar mechanism of choice gadgets, formed by
k
directed paths,
corresponding to
k
colors in the
Multicolored Clique
instance.

The
i
-th path is divided into blocks corresponding to the vertices of color
i
.

We enforce that a Hamiltonian cycle must omit exactly one block when following such a path, thus encoding a choice of
k
vertices.
The arcs between blocks from different paths encode the adjacency matrix of the original graph. We would like to guarantee that any Hamiltonian cycle
C
visits each omitted block
k-1
times, utilizing the arcs mentioned above.
To ensure this, we need to allow
C
to “make a step backward” on the directed path during such a visit.
On the other hand, we cannot afford to create too many disjoint cycles because DFVS should have size bounded in terms of
k
.
As a remedy, we attach
k-1
long cycles to each choice gadget, which are connected to every block in a certain way (see
\Cref
fig:ham-dfvs-1 on page
3
).
Using the fact that every vertex in the gadget must be visited by
C
at some point,
we prove that each long cycle is entered exactly once and each choice gadget is entered exactly
k
times in total, imposing a rigid structure on a solution.
As a result, we get rid of the flaw occurring in the naive construction
and obtain an equivalent instance with DFVS number
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
.

Longest Cycle (Path) Above Girth.

Having established our main hardness result, it is easy to extend it to
Longest Cycle (Path) Above Girth
.
We replace each vertex
v
in the DFVS by two vertices
v_{in}
and
v_{out}
, splitting the in- and out-going arcs of
v
between them, and insert a long path from
v_{in}
to
v_{out}
.
This path is set sufficiently long to make the girth
g
of the graph comparable to its size.
Consequently, the original graph
G
is Hamiltonian if and only if the second one has a cycle of length
g\cdot(\mathsf{dfvs}(G)+1)
.
So, this problem is also W[1]-hard.

To design an XP algorithm for
Longest Path Above Girth
, we follow the win-win approach employed by the FPT algorithm for the undirected case
[
36
]
, which relies on a certain version of the Grid Minor Theorem.
In general, the theorem states that either a graph contains a
k\times k
-grid as a minor, or its treewidth is bounded by
f(k)
, for some (in fact, polynomial) function
f
.
In the first scenario, a sufficiently long path always exists, whereas in the second scenario, the problem is solved via dynamic programming on the tree decomposition.

We take advantage of the directed counterpart of the Grid Minor Theorem: either a digraph contains a subgraph isomorphic to a subdivision of the order-
{\mathcal{O}}(k)
cylindrical wall or its directed treewidth is bounded by
f(k)
, for some function
f

[
61
]
.
We prove that in the first scenario again a sufficiently long path always exists (so we obtain a Yes-instance), while in the second scenario we can utilize

the known algorithm to compute the longest path in XP time with the help of the directed tree decomposition.
Finally, let us remark that this argument does not extend to
Longest Cycle Above Girth
because we cannot guarantee the existence of a sufficiently long cycle in a subdivision of a cylindrical wall.
We leave it open whether this problem belongs to XP as well.

a directed tree decomposition of

.

3
Hardness of
Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS

This section is devoted to the proof of
\Cref
thm:HamCycleDFVS. It is based on a parameterized reduction from

Multicolored Clique
, defined as follows.

Multicolored Clique

Input:
A graph
G=(V,E)
, an integer
k
, and a partition
(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k})
of
V
.

Parameter:

k

Question:
Is there a clique of size
k
with a vertex from each
V^{i}
,
i\in[k]
?

This problem is well-known to be W[1]-hard
[
27
, Theorem 13.7]
. For an instance
(G,(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k}))
of
Multicolored Clique
, we construct an instance
(G^{\prime},X)
of
Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS
as follows.

Construction of
G^{\prime}
.

For
i\in[k]
we construct a directed path
P^{i}
corresponding to
V^{i}
as follows. Let us fix an arbitrary ordering
<_{i}
of the vertices in
V^{i}
, and accordingly, denote
V^{i}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dotsc v_{|V_{i}|}\}
. To each vertex
v\in V^{i}

we associate a directed path
P_{v}
on
2k
vertices. We let
v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}}
and
v^{{\mathsf{r}ight}}
denote the first and last vertices of
P_{v}
, respectively.
We refer to the
2(k-1)
internal vertices of
P_{v}
as
v^{1,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v^{1,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{2,{\mathsf{o}ut}},\dotsc,v^{i-1,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
,
v^{i-1,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{i+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v^{i+1,{\mathsf{i}n}},\dotsc,v^{k,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v^{k,{\mathsf{i}n}}

(note that the index
i
is avoided).
The directed path
P^{i}
is the concatenation of these paths:
P_{v_{1}}\rightarrow P_{v_{2}}\rightarrow,\dotsc,\rightarrow P_{v_{|V^{i}|}}
.

For every
i\in[k]

we create a “universal” vertex
u^{i}
and insert edges
(u_{i},v^{\mathsf{l}eft})
,
(u_{i},v^{\mathsf{r}ight})
,
(v^{\mathsf{l}eft},u_{i})
,
(v^{\mathsf{r}ight},u^{i})
for all

.
Next, we create

for

, each of length

.
The vertices of

are

.
We insert edges from
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
to
v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
and from
v^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}}
to
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}
for all

. See Figure

We add two sets of

.
For

We also create two additional sets of

, and

to

(which belongs to

.
Furthermore, we add edges from every

to every

.

Finally we encode the adjacency matrix of

: for each edge

in

with
u\in V^{i}
and

with

, we insert an edge from

to

.

.
Clearly, the graph
G^{\prime}
can be computed in polynomial time when given
(G,(V^{1},\dots,V^{k}))
.
We begin the analysis of
G^{\prime}

by showing that it has a DFVS of size
{\mathcal{O}}(k^{2})
.

Lemma 8
.

There exists a subset
X\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
such that
G^{\prime}-X
is a DAG and
|X|=k(k-1)+2k+\binom{k}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be the set of vertices

\{c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v^{i}}\colon i,j\in[k],\,i\neq j\}

where
v^{i}
stands for the first vertex in
V^{i}
.
We set
X=Y\cup T\cup\widehat{T}\cup\{u^{1},\dotsc u^{k}\}
and claim that

is a directed acyclic graph.
First observe that for each
i\in[k]

the graph
L^{i}:=P^{i}\cup\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j}-X
is acyclic because it can be drawn with each edge being either vertical or horizontal facing right (see
\Cref
fig:ham-dfvs-1).
The remaining edges in
G^{\prime}-X
either start at
S\cup\widehat{S}
(these vertices have no incoming edges) or go from
L^{i}
to

for
i<j
.
Thus

is a DAG.
Finally we check that
|X|=|Y|+|T|+|\widehat{T}|+k=k(k-1)+2k+\binom{k}{2}
.

Correctness.

We first show that if
(G,(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k}))
is a Yes-instance then there exists a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
.
In the following lemmas, we refer to the directed feedback vertex set
X
from Lemma
8
.

Lemma 9
.

If
(G,(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k}))
is a Yes-instance of
Multicolored Clique
then
(G^{\prime},X)
is a Yes-instance of
Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{1},\dotsc,v_{k}\}
be a clique in
G
with
v_{i}\in V^{i}
for
i\in k
.
For
i\in[k]
we define the path
Z^{i}
that starts at
s^{i}
, follows
P^{i}
until
v_{i}^{\mathsf{l}eft}
, visits
u^{i}
, and then follows
P^{i}
from
v_{i}^{\mathsf{r}ight}
to
t^{i}
.
Next, for
i<j
we construct the path
Q^{i\rightarrow j}
as
\widehat{s}^{i\rightarrow j}\rightarrow v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}}\rightarrow C^{i\rightarrow j}\rightarrow v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}\rightarrow v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}}\rightarrow C^{j\rightarrow i}\rightarrow v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}}\rightarrow\widehat{t}^{i\rightarrow j}
.
Note that one can traverse the entire cycle
C^{i\rightarrow j}
after entering it from
v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}}
and leave towards
v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
.
The edge
(v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})
is present in
G^{\prime}
due to the encoding of the adjacency matrix of
G
.
The union of all these paths covers the entire vertex set of
G^{\prime}
.
It suffices to observe that
these paths can be combined into a single cycle using the edges from
T\cup\widehat{T}
to
S\cup\widehat{S}
.
∎

Proving the converse of Lemma
9
is more challenging.
We will reveal the structure of a potential Hamiltonian cycle
H
in
G^{\prime}
gradually.
First, we show that if
H
enters
the cycle
C^{i\rightarrow j}
through an incoming edge corresponding to a certain vertex
v\in V^{i}
,
then it must also leave it through an outgoing edge related to
v
, and vice versa.
A priori, there might be multiple vertices
v\in V^{i}
for which this happens.

For
v\in V^{i}
we denote the edge from
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
to
v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
by
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
. Similarly, let the edge from
v^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}}
to
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}
be
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
and
1\leq i<j\leq k
. Then
E(H)\cap\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})
is of the form
\bigcup_{v\in U}\{e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\}
where
U
is some subset of
V^{i}
.

Proof.

Note that
\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})=\{e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v_{1}},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v_{1}},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v_{2}},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v_{2}},\dotsc e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v_{|V_{i}|}}

,e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v_{|V_{i}|}}\}
.
First suppose that for some
v\in V^{i}
, we have
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\in E(H)
. Since
H
is a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
, it has to visit the vertex
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
. Thus
H
contains an outgoing edge of
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
. The only two outgoing edges of
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
are
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
and
(c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v})
. Since
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\in E(H)
is an incoming edge of
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}
, the edge
(c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v})

cannot belong to
E(H)
.
The only other candidate for the outgoing edge of
c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
is
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
, so
e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}\in E(H)
.

The proof of implication in the other direction is analogous.
Hence for each
v\in V^{i}
we have

e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}\in E(H)\Leftrightarrow e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\in E(H)
.
∎

As the next step, we show that when
v\in V^{i}
and a Hamiltonian cycle enters some cycle
C^{i\rightarrow j}
adjacent to
P^{i}
through a vertex from
P_{v}
, then it must enter all the
k-1
cycles adjacent to
P^{i}
through
P_{v}
.
Again, we cannot yet exclude a scenario that this would happen for multiple vertices
v\in V^{i}
.

Lemma 11
.

For any Hamiltonian cycle
H
in
G^{\prime}
and
i\in[k]
,
there exists a subset
U\subseteq V^{i}
for which
we have
E(H)\cap E(P^{i},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})=\bigcup_{v\in U}E(P_{v},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
.

Proof.

Targeting a contradiction, suppose there exists a vertex
v\in V^{i}
such that at least one edge of
E(P_{v},

\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
is in
E(H)
and at least one edge of
E(P_{v},

\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
is not in
E(H)
.
From Lemma
10
we know that
E(H)\cap E(P_{v},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
is a union of pairs of the form
\{e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\}
for some
j\in[k]\setminus\{i\}
.
Hence there indices
p,j\in[k]\setminus\{i\}
so that
(v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}})\in E(P^{i})
, one of the sets
E(B_{v},C^{i\rightarrow p})
,
E(B_{v},C^{i\rightarrow j})
has empty intersection with
E(H)
and the other one is contained in
E(H)
.
We can assume w.l.o.g. that
j=p+1
and the first of these sets is empty.

We arrive at the following scenario:
p\in[k]\setminus\{i\}
,
e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\notin E(H)
and
e^{i\rightarrow p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}

,e^{i\rightarrow p+1,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\in E(H)
. Since
H
is a Hamiltonian cycle, it has to visit the vertex
v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}}
. Thus, it has to traverse an outgoing edge of
v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}}
. The only outgoing edges of
v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}}
are the edges
e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}
and
(v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}})
. By the assumption,
e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\notin E(H)
so
(v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}})\in E(H)
.
On the other hand, since
e^{i\rightarrow p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}\in E(H)
is an incoming edge of
v^{p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
, we have
(v^{p,{\mathsf{i}n}},v^{p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}})\notin E(H)
as it is also an incoming edge to
v^{p+1,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
.
This yields a contradiction.
∎

We want to argue now that in fact the set
U
in
\Cref

i\in[k]
each cycle
C^{i\rightarrow j}

is being entered exactly once and from the same subpath
P_{v}
of
P^{i}
.
To this end, we take advantage of the universal vertices
u^{i}
.

Lemma 12
.

For any Hamiltonian cycle
H
in
G^{\prime}
and
i\in[k]
there exists
v\in V^{i}
such that
E(H)\cap E(P^{i},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
is
E(P_{v},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
.

Proof.

Let
U
be the set from
\Cref
lemma-H-cycle-edges-union.
Targeting a contradiction, suppose that there exist two distinct
v,w\in U
. That is,
E(H)\cap E(P^{i},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})\supseteq E(P_{v},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})\cup E(P_{w},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
.

The Hamiltonian cycle
H
must contain an outgoing edge of
v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}}
, which is the first vertex on the path
P_{v}
.
The only out-neighbors of
v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}}
are
u^{i}
and
v^{p,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
where
p=1
when
i\neq 1
or
p=2
when
i=1
.
Recall that the
e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
is present in
E(B^{i}_{v},\bigcup_{j\neq i}C^{i\rightarrow j})
and thereby in
E(H)
by the assumption. Also, since the edge
e^{i\rightarrow p,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}
is an incoming edge of
v^{p,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
, the edge
(v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}},v^{p,{\mathsf{o}ut}})
cannot be in
E(H)
. Thus the outgoing edge of
v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}}
in
H
is
(v^{{\mathsf{l}eft}},u^{i})
.

Now consider the vertex
w^{{\mathsf{l}eft}}
, which is the first one of the path
P_{w}
. By the same argument as above, we have
(w^{{\mathsf{l}eft}},u^{i})\in E(H)
.
This implies that
u^{i}
has two in-neighbors in
H
, a contradiction.

It is also impossible that
U=\emptyset
because then
E(H)\cap\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})=\emptyset
for each
j\neq i
implying that
H
is disconnected.
Consequently,
U
contains exactly one element.
∎

We can summarize the arguments given so far as follows:
for a Hamiltonian cycle
H
in
G^{\prime}
and
i\in[k]
, there exists
v_{i}\in V^{i}
, such that, for all
j\neq i
, it holds that
E(H)\cap\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})=E(P_{v_{i}},C^{i\rightarrow j})=\{e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v_{i}},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v_{i}}\}
.
We make note of a simple implication of this fact.

Lemma 13
.

Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
and
i,j\in[k]
.
Let
v\in V^{i}
satisfy
E(H)\cap\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})=\{e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v},e^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\}
.
Then
e=(v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}})
does not belong to
E(H)
.

Proof.

Since
E(H)\cap\partial(C^{i\rightarrow j})
comprises exactly two edges,
we infer that
H
contains the path
Q=v^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}}\rightarrow c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{i}n}}_{v}\rightarrow\dotsc\rightarrow c^{i\rightarrow j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{v}\rightarrow v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
with all internal vertices from
V(C^{i\rightarrow j})
.
If
H
traversed the edge
e=(v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}})
, then it would contain the cycle
C
formed by

and

. This would imply
H=C
, which contradicts that
H
is Hamiltonian.
∎

We are going to show that

H
can include only one edge that goes from
V(P^{i})
to
V(P^{j})
;
it will follow that this edge must be
(v^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{i},v^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}}_{j})
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
. For each pair
i,j\in[k]
with
i<j
we have
|E(H)\cap E(P^{i},P^{j})|\leq 1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
|E(H)\cap E(P^{i},P^{j})|\geq 2
.
Then there exist
u,w\in V^{j}
and
e_{u}\in\partial^{\mathsf{i}n}(u^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})\cap\partial^{{\mathsf{o}ut}}(P^{i})
,
e_{w}\in\partial^{\mathsf{i}n}(w^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})\cap\partial^{{\mathsf{o}ut}}(P^{i})
such that
e_{u},e_{w}\in E(H)
.
This implies that the edges
(u^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}},u^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})
,
(w^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}},w^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})\in E(P^{j})
cannot be used by
H
.
Since
i<j
, the vertex
u^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
has only one out-neighbor different than
u^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}}
: the terminal
\widehat{t}^{i\rightarrow j}
.
Hence
(u^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}},\widehat{t}^{i\rightarrow j})\in E(H)
.
But the same argument applies to
w^{i,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
.
As a consequence, two incoming edges of
\widehat{t}^{i\rightarrow j}
are being used by
H
and so we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Finally, we prove the second implication in the correctness proof of the reduction.

Lemma 15
.

If
(G^{\prime},X)
is a Yes-instance of
Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS
then
(G,(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k}))
is a Yes-instance of
Multicolored Clique
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{\prime}
and

v_{i}\in V^{i}
be the vertex given by
\Cref
lemma-H-cycle-edges-one-vertex-only for
i\in[k]
.
Fix a pair of indices
i<j
.
By
\Cref
lemma:cycle-out-in we know that the edge
(v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{i}n}})
does not belong to
E(H)
.
The remaining outgoing edges of
v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}}
belong to
E(P^{i},P^{j})
and
H
must utilize one of them.
By the same argument,

H
must traverse one of the incoming edges of
v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}}
that belongs to
E(P^{i},P^{j})
.
Due to
\Cref
lemma-Pij, the Hamiltonian cycle
H
can use at most one edge from
E(P^{i},P^{j})
.
Consequently, we obtain that
(v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})\in E(H)
.
In particular, this means that
(v_{i}^{j,{\mathsf{o}ut}},v_{j}^{i,{\mathsf{i}n}})
is present in
E(G^{\prime})
and, by the construction of
G^{\prime}
, implies that
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
.
Therefore,
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}\}
forms a clique in
G
.
∎

Lemmas

and

constitute that the instances
(G,(V^{1},V^{2},\dotsc,V^{k}))
and
(G^{\prime},X)
are equivalent while

\Cref

ensures that
|X|={\mathcal{O}}(k^{2})
.
We have thus obtained a parameterized reduction from
Multicolored Clique
to
Hamiltonian Cycle By DFVS
, proving
\Cref
thm:HamCycleDFVS.

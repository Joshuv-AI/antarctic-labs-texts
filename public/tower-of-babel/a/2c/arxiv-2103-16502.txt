Intermittent non-pharmaceutical strategies to mitigate the COVID-19 epidemic in a network model of Italy via constrained optimization
Marco Coraggio, Shihao Xie, Francesco De Lellis, Giovanni Russo, Mario di Bernardo

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the design of intermittent non-pharmaceutical strategies to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 epidemic exploiting network epidemiological models. Specifically, by studying a variational equation for the dynamics of the infected, we derive, using contractivity arguments, a condition that can be used to guarantee that the effective reproduction number is less than unity. This condition (i) is easily computable, (ii) is interpretable, being directly related to the model parameters, and (iii) can be used to enforce a scalability condition that prohibits the amplification of disturbances within the network system. We then include satisfaction of such a condition as a constraint in a Model Predictive Control problem so as to mitigate (or suppress) the spread of the epidemic while minimizing the economic impact of the interventions. A data-driven model of Italy as a network of three macro-regions (North, Center, and South), whose parameters are identified from real data, is used to illustrate and evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed control strategy.

Contribution

We consider network epidemic models, and give a sufficient condition, directly dependent on the model parameters, ensuring that the epidemiological
effective reproduction number

\mathcal{R}_{t}
is less than one and that the infected population decreases to zero.
Then, we consider a network model of Italy parametrized on real data, and obtain a set of intermittent non-pharmaceutical intervention strategies at the regional level by formulating a constrained optimization problem that is effectively solved via MPC.
The formulation (i) embeds our novel stability condition as a constraint, (ii) is based on the minimization of an economic cost induced by both social distancing and testing and (iii) considers a lower bound on the control dwell time.
To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to implement the MPC on a regional model including all these features.

Reproduction number

A crucial quantity to assess the rate of propagation of an epidemic disease is the

, defined in epidemiology as “the number of secondary infections generated by one infected person”

Typically, the

[

\mathcal{R}_{t,i}=\frac{\sum_{k=t-3}^{t}[(N_{i}-S_{i}(k))-(N_{i}-S_{i}(k-1))]}{\sum_{k=t-7}^{t-4}[(N_{i}-S_{i}(k))-(N_{i}-S_{i}(k-1))]}.

(4)

Note that while an analytical expression of

Later in Section

(for the network) is below the critical unitary threshold.

Cost function

As a cost function for the optimization problems above, we consider the total economic cost of the measures undertaken by each region, computed accounting for the cost of people not being able to work and the cost of testing.

Let
P_{\mathrm{active},i}(t)\coloneqq S_{i}(t)+I_{i}(t)+R_{i}(t)
;
c_{\mathrm{m}}=\text{€}83.992
be the mean daily GDP pro-capita in Italy
[
26
]
,

c_{\mathrm{w}}=0.617
be the fraction of people that are
not
able to work when social distancing is in place
[
27
]
, and

c_{\alpha}
be the daily cost of carrying out maximal testing, normalized over the population.
Then, the economic cost at time
t
in region

is given by

J_{i}(t)=J_{i,1}(t)+J_{i,2}(t)+J_{i,3}(t)+J_{i,4}(t)+J_{i,5}(t),

where the addends are defined as follows:

1.

J_{i,1}(t)
is the loss due to people that live and work in region
i
, but cannot work because of social distancing:

J_{i,1}(t)\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{m}}c_{\mathrm{w}}\left(1-\sqrt{\rho_{i}(t)}\right)\phi_{ii}^{0}P_{\mathrm{active},i}(t)
;

2.

J_{i,2}(t)
is the loss due to people that live in other regions and work in
i

J_{i,2}(t)\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{m}}c_{\mathrm{w}}\left(1-\sqrt{\rho_{i}(t)}\right)\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{N}\phi_{ji}(t)P_{\mathrm{active},j}(t)
;

3.

J_{i,3}(t)
is the cost of travel restrictions computed in terms of people that live in other regions and work in
i
, but cannot travel:

J_{i,3}(t)\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{m}}c_{\mathrm{w}}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{M}\left(\phi_{ij}^{0}-\phi_{ij}(t)\right)P_{\mathrm{active},j}(t)
;

4.

J_{i,4}(t)
is the loss due to people that live in
i
but cannot work because becoming incapacitated:

J_{i,4}(t)\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{m}}[c_{\mathrm{w}}Q_{i}(t)+H_{i}(t)+D_{i}(t)]
;

5.

J_{i,5}(t)
is related to the cost of carrying out increased testing in region
i
and is computed as

J_{i,5}(t)\coloneqq c_{\alpha}N_{i}\sigma_{i}(t)

The expressions in items
1

1

Therefore, the portion of people that is

restrained is proportional to
\sqrt{\rho}

restrained is proportional to
(1-\sqrt{\rho})

In item

, where
c_{\mathrm{test}}=\text{€}59
is the economic cost of a single test
[
28
]
,
k_{p}=1.74

[
14

, and
\tilde{p}=3.49\times 10^{-3}
is the fraction of people tested daily when testing is maximal.

Letting the regional terminal cost be
\bar{J}_{i}(T)=0

VI
Conclusions

We considered the problem of designing localized intermittent non-pharmaceutical intervention strategies to mitigate the COVID-19 epidemic in a regional model of the Italian epidemic.
We first gave a sufficient condition to determine the stability of the infected population, in a network epidemic model.
We then exploited this condition, or a relaxed version of it, as a constraint to formulate the control problem as an optimization problem in which the levels of social distancing, travel restrictions and additional testing must be selected to avoid saturation of the regional health systems and containment (or suppression) of the epidemic spread, while minimizing the overall cost to the economy.
Our results show the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
In the future, we will study robustness of our control strategy to model uncertainties, and measurement error.

Namely, the dynamics of the infected we consider is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}(t+1)=\big[(1-\gamma){\mathcal{I}}+\text{diag}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{I}(t))\beta\mathbf{A}\big]\mathbf{I}(t),

where

Next, we derive and compare two different upper bounds on the maximum eigenvalue of

The first bound, say (B1), is derived following the arguments in Proposition
1

Bound derived from Proposition
1

By using similar arguments as those used in Proposition
1
, it can be shown that, if there exists some matrix norm and some
c<1
such that

\left\lVert(1-\gamma){\mathcal{I}}+\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{I}(t))\beta\mathbf{A}\right\rVert\leq c,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0},

(17)

then
\left\lVert{\mathbf{I}}(t)\right\rVert\leq c^{t-t_{0}}\left\lVert{\mathbf{I}}(t_{0})\right\rVert
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.
Letting
I^{\mathrm{m}}(t)\coloneqq\min_{i}I_{i}(t)
, and applying the triangle inequality and Lemma
1
, we find that (
17
) is satisfied if

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{1-I^{\mathrm{m}}(t)}\frac{\gamma+c-1}{\beta},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0},

(18)

where we used the fact that the variables
I_{i}(t)
are bounded between
0
and
1

As
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert\geq\lambda_{\max}({\mathbf{A}})
, then there exists
p\in(0,1]
such that
p\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert=\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A})

Thus, we rewrite (
18

\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A})\leq b_{1}\coloneqq\frac{p}{1-I^{\mathrm{m}}(t)}\frac{\gamma+c-1}{\beta}.

(B1)

Bound derived from
[
25

Following the arguments in
[
25
]

\mathbf{P}\coloneqq(1-\gamma){\mathcal{I}}+\beta\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{P}\succeq 0

Applying the Perron-Frobenius theorem
[
13

\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{P})\geq 0

\mathbf{v}_{\max}\succeq 0

Moreover,

\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{P})

\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{P})\geq|\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{P})|

\lambda_{i}

\mathbf{P}

Now, if

\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\mathbf{A})\leq b_{2}\coloneqq\frac{\gamma+c-1}{\beta},

(B2)

(which constitutes the second bound), we consequently get
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\mathbf{P})\leq c<1

\mathbf{0}

\begin{split}\mathbf{v}_{\max}^{{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbf{I}(t+1)&=\mathbf{v}_{\max}^{{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\big((1-\gamma){\mathcal{I}}+\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{I}(t))\beta\mathbf{A}\big)\mathbf{I}(t)\\

which means stability of
\mathbf{I}(t)
in
\mathbf{0}
recalling that
\mathbf{I}(t)\succeq 0

Note that (

On Rademacher Complexity-based Generalization Bounds for Deep Learning
Lan V. Truong lan.truong@essex.ac.uk

Abstract
We show that the Rademacher complexity-based framework can establish non-vacuous generalization bounds for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in the context of classifying a small set of image classes. A key technical advancement is the formulation of novel contraction lemmas for high-dimensional mappings between vector spaces, specifically designed for general Lipschitz activation functions. These lemmas extend and refine the Talagrand contraction lemma across a broader range of scenarios. Our Rademacher complexity bound provides an enhancement over the results presented by Golowich et al. for ReLU-based Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). Moreover, while previous works utilizing Rademacher complexity have primarily focused on ReLU DNNs, our results generalize to a wider class of activation functions.

1
Introduction

Deep models are typically heavily over-parametrized, while they still achieve good generalization performance. Despite the widespread use of neural networks in biotechnology, finance, health science, and business, just to name a selected few, the problem of understanding deep learning theoretically remains relatively under-explored. In 2002, Koltchinskii and Panchenko
(
Koltchinskii & Panchenko 2002
)
proposed new probabilistic upper bounds on generalization error of the combination of many complex classifiers such as deep neural networks. These bounds were developed based on the general results of the theory of Gaussian, Rademacher, and empirical processes in terms of general functions of the margins, satisfying a Lipschitz condition. However, bounding Rademacher complexity for deep learning remains a challenging task. In this work, we present new upper bounds on the Rademacher complexity in deep learning, which differ from previous studies in how they depend on the norms of the weight matrices. Furthermore, we demonstrate that our bounds are non-vacuous for CNNs with a wide range of activation functions.

The standard approach to develop generalization bounds on deep learning (and machine learning) was developed in seminar papers by

These bounds were developed based on the general results of the theory of Gaussian, Rademacher, and empirical processes in terms of general functions of the margins, satisfying a Lipschitz condition. They improved previously known bounds on generalization error of convex combination of classifiers. Generalization bounds for deep learning and kernel learning with Markov dataset based on Rademacher and Gaussian complexity functions have recently analysed in

Truong 2022a

1.2
Contributions

In this paper, our contributions are as follows:

•

We introduce novel contraction lemmas for high-dimensional mappings between vector spaces, which extend and enhance the Talagrand contraction lemma in various scenarios.

•

We apply these new contraction lemmas to the layers of ReLU-based Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), demonstrating that our derived Rademacher complexity bound provides improvements over the results of Golowich et al.
(
Golowich et al. 2018
)
.

•

We empirically validate our theoretical findings on CNNs for MNIST image classification, showing that our generalization bounds are non-vacuous when the number of classes is small.

As far as we know, this is the first result which shows that the Rademacher complexity-based approach can lead to non-vacuous generalisation bounds on CNNs.

(9)

where

In Theorem
2

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of functions mapping

3
Rademacher Complexity Bounds for Deep Neural Networks (DNNs)

3.1
General Deep Neural Network Models

Let

be a sequence of positive integer numbers such that
d_{0}=d
for some fixed
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

We define a class of function

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=\big\{f=f_{L}\circ f_{L-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}\circ f_{0}:f_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}\subset\{g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{i+1}}\},\quad\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,L\}\big\},

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{W}_{i}\mathbf{x}),\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}.

For a training set
\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

-norm

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F}):=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\bigg\|_{\infty}\bigg],

The
loss generalization gap

\psi

\displaystyle L(\psi\circ\mathcal{F})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\psi(f(\mathbf{x}),y)]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(f(\mathbf{x}_{i}),y_{i})\bigg|.

(39)

Here,

is a sequence of labels, and

is the distribution of

for all

For example, for the square loss, i.e.,

\displaystyle L(\psi\circ\mathcal{F})=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[(f(\mathbf{x})-y)^{2}]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(\mathbf{x}_{i}-y_{i})^{2}\bigg|.

(40)

3.2

In this section, we aim to derive Rademacher complexity bounds for ReLU-based Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). We begin with the following result.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of functions mapping

for any fixed

if

if

Proof.

See Appendix
L

∎

One such tool that can be used to upper bound the loss generalization gap is the Rademacher complexity.
It is well known that (cf.
Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David 2014
) if the magnitude of our loss function is bounded above by
c
, with probability greater than
1-\delta
for all
g\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\psi(g(\mathbf{x}),y)]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(g(\mathbf{x}_{i}),y_{i})\bigg|\leq 2\mathcal{R}_{n}(\psi\circ\hat{\mathcal{F}})+4c\sqrt{\frac{2\log\frac{4}{\delta}}{n}},

(42)

where
\psi\circ\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\{\psi(g(\mathbf{x}),y):\mathbf{x},y\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y},f\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}\}
. Therefore, if we have an upper bound on the Rademacher complexity, we can have an upper bound on the loss generalization gap.

Lemma 10
.

If
\psi
is the square loss, i.e.,
\psi(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}

\displaystyle L(\psi\circ\hat{\mathcal{F}})\leq 4M\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{g\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{+}}\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}g(\mathbf{x}_{i})\bigg|\bigg]+\frac{2M^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+4M^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log\frac{4}{\delta}}{n}},

(43)

where
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{+}=\hat{\mathcal{F}}\cup\{0\}
, and
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
is the class of output functions of the DNN, i.e.,

for some

(see the modelling of ReLU-DNNs in Section

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\psi\circ\hat{\mathcal{F}})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{g\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{1}{n}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\psi\big(g(\mathbf{x}_{i}),y_{i}\big)\bigg|\bigg].

(44)

Now, we have

\displaystyle\big|\psi(x,y)-\psi(x^{\prime},y)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|(x-y)^{2}-(x^{\prime}-y)^{2}\big|

(45)

\displaystyle=\big|(x-x^{\prime})(x+x^{\prime}-2y)\big|

(46)

\displaystyle=\big|x-x^{\prime}\big|\big|x+x^{\prime}-2y\big|

(47)

\displaystyle\leq 2M\big|x-x^{\prime}\big|.

(49)

∎

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2M^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+4M^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log\frac{4}{\delta}}{n}}.

(93)

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\big[\psi(g(\mathbf{x}),y)]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(g(\mathbf{x}_{i}),y_{i})\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 4M\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{g\in\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{+}}\bigg\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}g(\mathbf{x}_{i})\bigg\|_{\infty}\bigg]+\frac{2M^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+4M^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log\frac{4}{\delta}}{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 8M^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{+}}\bigg\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\bigg\|_{\infty}\bigg]+\frac{2M^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+4M^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\log\frac{4}{\delta}}{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 16M^{2}\inf_{p\geq 1}\inf_{L^{\prime}\in[1,L]}\bigg(\frac{d+1}{n}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\prod_{l=L^{\prime}+1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}_{l}\|_{\infty}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{l=1}^{L^{\prime}}\|\mathbf{W}_{l}\|_{p}\bigg)

4.2
Generalization Error Bounds for CNNs

Definition 23
.

Recall that the CNN model is a special case of DNNs as discussed in Section
3.1
. The margin of a labelled example
(\mathbf{x},y)
is defined as

\displaystyle m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y):=g(f(\mathbf{x}),y)-\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}g(f(\mathbf{x}),y^{\prime}),

(97)

so
f
mis-classifies the labelled example
(\mathbf{x},y)
if and only if
m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)<0
.
The generalisation error is defined as
\mathbb{P}(m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)<0)
. It is easy to see that

\mathbb{P}(m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)<0)=\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbf{w}_{y}^{T}f(\mathbf{x})<\max_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathbf{w}_{y^{\prime}}^{T}f(\mathbf{x})\big)
.

Remark 24
.

In case

\{g(f(\mathbf{x}),y)<\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}g(f(\mathbf{x}),y^{\prime})\}\subset\{\tilde{g}(f_{k}(\mathbf{x}),y))<\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}\tilde{g}(f_{k}(\mathbf{x}),y^{\prime}))\}

for some
k\in[L]
where
\tilde{g}
is a specific function, we have

\mathbb{P}(m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)<0)\leq\mathbb{P}(\tilde{g}(f_{k}(\mathbf{x}),y))<\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}\tilde{g}(f_{k}(\mathbf{x}),y^{\prime}))).

Hence, we can bound the generalisation error by using only a part of CNN networks (from layer
0
to layer
k
). More specifically, if the last layers of CNN is the softmax, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)<0)\leq\mathbb{P}(g(f_{L-1}(\mathbf{x}),y))<\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}g(f_{L-1}(\mathbf{x}),y^{\prime}))),

(98)

where
f_{L-1}
represents the output of the last hidden layer, just before the final activation function (i.e., softmax).

Now, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 25
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of function from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For CNNs for classification, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}m_{f}(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})\bigg|\bigg]\leq\beta(M)\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}m_{f}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\bigg\|_{\infty}\bigg],

(99)

where

\displaystyle\beta(M)=\begin{cases}M(2M-1),&\qquad M>2\\
2M,&\qquad M=2\end{cases}.

(100)

For
M>2
, (
99
) is a result of
(
Koltchinskii & Panchenko 2002
, Proof of Theorem 11)
. We improve this constant for
M=2
.
Based on the above Rademacher complexity bounds and a justified application of McDiarmid’s inequality, we obtains the following generalization for deep learning with i.i.d. datasets.

Theorem 26
.

Let
\gamma>0
and define the following function (the
\gamma
-margin cost):

\displaystyle\zeta(x):=\begin{cases}0,&\gamma\leq x\\
1-x/\gamma,&0\leq x\leq\gamma\\
1,&x\leq 0\end{cases}.

(101)

Recall the definition of the average Rademacher complexity
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})
in (
91
) and the definition of
\beta(M)
in (
100
). Let
\{(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}\sim P_{\mathbf{x}y}
for some joint distribution
P_{\mathbf{x}y}
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
. Then, for any

, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg\{\exists f\in\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{P}\big(m_{f}(\mathbf{x},y)\leq 0\big)>\inf_{\gamma\in(0,1]}\bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta(m_{f}(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\beta(M)}{\gamma}\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})+\frac{2t+\sqrt{\log\log_{2}(2\gamma^{-1})}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigg]\bigg\}\leq 2\exp(-2t^{2}).

.

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})
in (

\beta(M)
in (

6
Comparision with Golowich et al.’s bound
(
Golowich et al. 2018
)

In
(
Golowich et al. 2018
, Section 4)
, the authors present an upper bound on Rademacher complexity for DNNs with ReLU activation functions as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})=O\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{F}\max\bigg\{1,\log\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{L}\frac{\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{F}}{\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{2}}\ \bigg)\bigg\}\min\bigg\{\frac{\max\{1,\log n\}^{3/4}}{n^{1/4}},\sqrt{\frac{L}{n}}\bigg\}\bigg)

(125)

where
\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{W}_{L}
are the parameter matrices of the
L
layers.

Now, let
\Gamma
be the term inside the bracket in (
125
), and define

\displaystyle\beta=\min_{j}\frac{\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{F}}{\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{2}}\geq 1.

(126)

Then, from (
125
) we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\geq\prod_{j=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{F}\min\bigg\{\frac{\max\{1,\log n\}^{3/4}\sqrt{\max\{1,L\log\beta\}}}{n^{1/4}},\sqrt{\frac{L}{n}}\bigg\}.

(127)

For the general case, it holds that
\beta>1
. Hence, from (
127
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})=O\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{L}{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{F}\bigg).

(128)

As analysed in
(
Golowich et al. 2018
)
, this bound improves many previous bounds, including Neyshabur et al.’s bound
Neyshabur et al. 2015
,
Neyshabur et al. 2018
which are known to be vacuous for certain ReLU DNNs
(
Nagarajan & Kolter 2019
)
.

By using Theorem
14
with
p=2
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})=O\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{2}\bigg)

(129)

for ReLU-DNNs, i.e., all the activation functions of the DNN belong to ReLU family. Note that

for all

. Hence, our bound improves upon
(
Golowich et al. 2018
)
in both the product of the norm and the constant factor preceding the norm, particularly when
L>>
.

Additionally, by Theorem
21
and Lemma
19
, we can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathcal{F})=O\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\mu_{j}\|\mathbf{W}_{j}\|_{\infty}\bigg)

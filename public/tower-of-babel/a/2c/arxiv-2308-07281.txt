Ergodic Estimates for Toeplitz Sequences Generated by a Symbol
Giovanni Barbarino

Abstract
We analyse the convergence of the ergodic formula for sequences of Toeplitz matrices generated by a symbol. We produce explicit bounds for the convergence rate depending on the size of the matrix, the regularity of the symbol and the regularity of the test function.



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, a matrix-sequence is a sequence of the form
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
, where
A_{n}
is a square matrix and
{\rm size}(A_{n})=n
. Let
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
(resp.,
C_{c}(\mathbb{C})
) be the space of continuous complex-valued functions with bounded support defined on
\mathbb{R}
(resp.,
\mathbb{C}
).
If
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, the singular values and eigenvalues of
A
are denoted by
\sigma_{1}(A),\ldots,\sigma_{n}(A)
and
\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)
, respectively.
We denote by
\mu_{d}
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Throughout this paper, “measurable” means “Lebesgue measurable”. Moreover, we denote by
\|g\|_{p}
the
L^{p}
-norm of the function
g
over its domain (which will be clear from the context).

We say that a matrix-sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
admits a singular value symbol (or simply a symbol)
f(x)
when the sampling of
|f(x)|
over a uniform grid in its domain yields an approximation of the singular values of
A_{n}
that improves as
n\to\infty
. The rigorous definition is given below; it is based on an ergodic formula that must hold for every test function
F\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 1

(
asymptotic singular value distribution of a matrix-sequence
)
.

Let
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
be a matrix-sequence with
A_{n}
of size
n
, and let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable with
0<\mu_{d}(\Omega)<\infty
.
We say that
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
has an asymptotic singular value distribution described by
f
if the following
ergodic formula
holds:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{n}}F(\sigma_{i}(A_{n}))=\frac{1}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{k}F(\sigma_{i}(f(x)))\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\forall\,F\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

(1)

In this case,
f
is called the singular value symbol (or simply the symbol) of
\{A_{n}\}_{n}
and we write
\{A_{n}\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}f
.

Among the various matrix-sequences that possess a symbol, Toeplitz sequences generated by
L^{1}
functions stand out for their importance.
A matrix of the form

\left[f_{i-j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{0}&f_{-1}&\ f_{-2}&\ \cdots&\ \ \cdots&f_{-(n-1)}\\
f_{1}&\ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ &\vdots\\
f_{2}&\ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ \ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ \ddots&f_{-2}\\
\vdots&&\ \ddots&\ \ddots&\ \ \ddots&f_{-1}\\
f_{n-1}&\cdots&\ \cdots&\ f_{2}&\ \ f_{1}&f_{0}\end{bmatrix},

(2)

whose
(i,j)
-th entry depends only on the difference
i-j
is called a Toeplitz matrix. In other words, a Toeplitz matrix is a matrix whose entries are constant along each diagonal.
A case of special interest arises when the entries
f_{k}
are the Fourier coefficients of a function
f:[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{C}
in
L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
, i.e.,

f_{k}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx}{\rm d}x,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

In this case, the matrix (
2
) is denoted by
T_{n}(f)
and is referred to as the
n
-th Toeplitz matrix generated by
f
. One can also define the associated infinite-dimensional operator
T(f)
and represent it as the infinite Toeplitz matrix
\left[f_{i-j}\right]_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
.
It is not difficult to see that the conjugate transpose of
T_{n}(f)
is given by

T_{n}(f)^{H}=T_{n}(\overline{f})

for every
f\in L^{1}([-\pi,\pi])
and every
n
; see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 6.2]
. In particular,
T_{n}(f)
is Hermitian whenever
f
is almost everywhere a real-valued function.

The asymptotic singular value distribution of the Toeplitz sequence
\{T_{n}(f)\}_{n}
has been deeply investigated over time, starting from Szegö’s first limit theorem
[
9
]
and the Avram–Parter theorem
[
1
,
10
]
, up to the works by Tyrtyshnikov–Zamarashkin
[
16

17

and Tilli
[

Using our notations, the Avram-Parter theorem states that
\{T_{n}(f)\}_{n}\sim_{\sigma}f
for any

for any
L^{1}
function

.

The analysis of the ergodic formula for Toeplitz sequences generated by real-valued symbols

is also linked to the asymptotic study of the eigenvalues and spectral distribution of the matrices

. Several works enquire about the existence of an asymptotic expansion of the eigenvalues when we impose certain conditions on

See

4

For the case of (some) smooth even function

Several have been the studies on the ergodic formula (

In this context, Widom proved a more precise result

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}:=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\lvert k\rvert\lvert f_{k}\rvert^{2}<\infty,

we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}G(\sigma_{j}(T_{n}(f))^{2})-\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(\lvert f(\theta)\rvert^{2})d\theta\right\}=Tr[G(T(\overline{f})T(f))+G(T(f)T(\overline{f}))-2T(G(\lvert f\rvert^{2}))]

(3)

2.1
Function norms

Let

2.2
Estimates for the Fourier transform and series

For the main results, we need some estimates on the moments of the Fourier transform of
C^{3}
functions, and on the truncated Fourier series of
C^{k}_{per}[-\pi,\pi]

Lemma 2
.

For any
G\in C_{c}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\hat{G}
be its Fourier transform. Then
\hat{G}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\hat{G}(x)x^{2}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})

\|\hat{G}(x)x\|_{1}\leq 2\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{2},\qquad\|\hat{G}(x)x^{2}\|_{1}\leq 2\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|G^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{2}.

Proof.

Given any

one has that
|\hat{H^{\prime}}(x)|=|x\hat{H}(x)|
, so by Hölder Theorem
2
and the Plancherel identity,

\displaystyle\|\hat{H}(x)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq 1}|\hat{H}(x)|dx+\int_{|x|>1}|\hat{H}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\|H\|_{1}+\|x\hat{H}(x)\|_{2}\sqrt{\int_{|x|>1}\frac{1}{x^{2}}dx}

\displaystyle=2\|H\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|\hat{H^{\prime}}(x)\|_{2}=2\|H\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|H^{\prime}(x)\|_{2}

Since
G\in C_{c}^{3}(\mathbb{R})
, then surely

,
|\hat{G}(x)x^{2}|=|\hat{G^{\prime\prime}}(x)|\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\|\hat{G}(x)x\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{G^{\prime}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|G^{\prime}\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\|\hat{G}(x)x^{2}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{G^{\prime\prime}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|G^{\prime\prime}\|_{1}+\sqrt{2}\|G^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{2}.

∎

4
Conclusions and Future Works

Up to our knowledge, this article presents the first ever bounds of the rate of convergence for the ergodic formula (
1
) for sequences of Toeplitz matrices generated by a symbol that works for any size
n
. In fact, previous works always focus on the asymptotic expansion of the ergodic formula or on the spectral distribution of the matrices
T_{n}(f)
, and in the latest case, always with regular functions
f
or with additional hypotheses.

Much is left to be explored. Almost all the computed bounds are not strict and can be improved or adapted to other characteristics of the symbol
f
and the test function
F
.
For example, a missing analysis is the case in which the test function
F(x)
is an indicator of a real interval (it does not follow from Theorem
1
since it is not a continuous function) that would also be more tightly linked to the analysis of the spectral distribution of
T_{n}(f)
.

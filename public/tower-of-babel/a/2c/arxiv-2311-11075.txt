A note on the uniqueness of minimal maps into ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} via singular values
Minghao Li, Ling Yang, Taiyang Zhu

Abstract
In this note, we derive a uniqueness theorem for minimal graphs of general codimension under certain restrictions closed related to the convexity (not strict convexity) of the area functional with respect to singular values, improving the result in [ 3 ] . The crucial step of the proof is to show the local linearity of the singular value vectors along the geodesic homotopy of two given minimal maps.

Introduction

A smooth map
f
from an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
into an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
N
is called a
minimal map
, whenever the graph of
f
is a minimal submanifold in
M\times N
, i.e. the product of
M
and
N
(see
[
8
]
). For these maps, the
Dirichlet problem
is a central topic:

Given a bounded domain
D\subset M
and a map
\phi:\partial D\rightarrow N
, it asks how many minimal maps
f:D\rightarrow N
exist, so that

f|_{\partial D}=\phi
.

When
N=\mathbb{R}
, it is well-known that the solution to the Dirichlet problem is unique, and the graph of this solution is area-minimizing.
This conclusion can be easily derived from the convexity of the area functional (see
[
1
]
Chap 12). However, this beautiful result cannot be generalized
to the cases of
\dim N\geq 2
. Lawson-Osserman
[
2
]
constructed 3 distinct minimal maps
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
from the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
into

\mathbb{R}^{2}
sharing the same boundary data, and the graph
of
f_{3}
is an unstable minimal surface. Afterwards, several
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued functions on
S^{m-1}
(the boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
) were constructed in
[
9
]
, taking each of which as a boundary condition, there exist infinitely solutions to the Dirichlet problem. It is nature to ask which kind of additional conditions ensure the minimal map to be unique and stable (i.e. the corresponding graph is a stable minimal submanifold).

This problem was studied in
[
5
,
6
,
4
,
3
]
from the viewpoint of singular values. A nonnegative number
\mu
is called a
singular value
of
df

at
x\in M
whenever
\mu^{2}
is a critical value of
\langle df(v),df(v)\rangle
with
v
an arbitrary unit vector in
T_{x}M
. Thereby, the area of
the graph of
f
can be expressed as

A=\int_{D}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}dv_{M},

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m}
are singular values of
df
and
dv_{M}
is the volume form of
M
. As shown in
[
4
]
,
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a strictly convex (or convex) function on
\mathcal{M}
(or
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
), where
\mathcal{M}
consists of all vectors
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})

in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying
x_{i}x_{j}<1
for each
1\leq i<j\leq m
and

\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-x_{i}^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-x_{1}^{2})\cdots(1-x_{i-1}^{2})x_{i}^{2}(1-x_{i+1}^{2})\cdots(1-x_{m}^{2})>0

(1.2)

and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is the closure of
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, the second variation formula in terms of the singular values of
df

derived in
[
6
]
implies the following criteria for stability and uniqueness of minimal graphs:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
4
]
)
Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a minimal map with
N
having non-positive sectional curvature, then the graph of
f
is stable (or weakly stable) whenever

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathcal{M}
(or
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
) everywhere on
M
, where
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})
is the singular value vector
of
df
at
x\in M
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
3
]
)
Let
f_{0},f_{1}:D\subset M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be both minimal maps, such that
f_{0}|_{\partial D}=f_{1}|_{\partial D}
. If the singular value vectors of both
f_{0}
and

f_{1}
lies in a symmetric convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
, then
f_{0}=f_{1}
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, it is natural to consider the geodesic homotopy
\{f_{t}:t\in[0,1]\}
of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
. The additional
conditions ’symmetric’ and ’convex’ ensure the singular value vectors of
f_{t}
still lie in
\mathcal{M}
, then the area functional is
strictly
convex
along the homotopy and
f_{0}=f_{1}
follows from the second variation formula (see (
2.14
)-(
2.19
)). However, this technique cannot work when
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
,
which only ensures the
convexity
of the area functional. In the present paper, we overcome the above difficulty by studying the local
properties of the singular value vector function
t\mapsto\lambda(t):=(\lambda_{1}(t),\cdots,\lambda_{m}(t))
when it takes value in
\partial\mathcal{M}
(i.e. the boundary
of
\mathcal{M}
). By applying majorization technology in convex optimisation, we establish the following confined property of
\lambda(t)
:

For any interval
I=[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,1]
,
\lambda(t_{1}),\lambda(t_{2})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
implies
\lambda(t)\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
for all
t\in I
.
Moreover,

•

The existence of
t_{0}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
such that
\lambda(t_{0})\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and
\lambda_{1}(t_{0})>1
forces
\lambda(t)
to be a linear function
on
I
.

•

If
\lambda_{i}(t)\equiv 1
on
I
for each
i=1,\cdots,l
and
\lambda_{l+1}(t)<1
, then
\nabla_{df_{t}(v)}V=0
, where
v
is an arbitrary unit vector
in
T_{x}M
satisfying
\langle df_{t}(v),df_{t}(v)\rangle=1
and
V:=df_{1}(x)-df_{0}(x)
denotes the variation field.

Afterwards, in conjunction of the second variation formula, we use induction method to prove the main theorem as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric convex subset of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
. If
f_{0},f_{1}:D\subset M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are both minimal maps, such that
f_{0}|_{\partial D}=f_{1}|_{\partial D}
, and the singular value vectors of both
f_{0}
and

f_{1}
lie in
\mathcal{C}
, then
f_{0}=f_{1}
.

There are some intuitive examples of symmetric convex subsets of
\mathcal{M}
given in
[
3
]
, the closure of each of which corresponds
to an improvement of the existing uniqueness result:

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume
f_{0},f_{1}:D\subset M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are both minimal maps with the same boundary data, then
f_{0}=f_{1}
if either of the following occurs:

•

f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are both length non-increasing, i.e. all singular values of them are no more than
1
;

•

the singular values of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\leq 2
;

•

the singular values of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq 2
;

•

the singular values of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2
.

2
The second variation formula for the volumes of higher-codimensional graphs

Let
(M,g_{M})
and
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds of dimension
m
and
n
, respectively. An arbitrary smooth map
f
from
M
into
N

induces an embedding
X:M\rightarrow M\times N

X(x)=(x,f(x)),

(2.1)

whose image is called the graph of
f
, denoted by
\Gamma(f)
.
Let
g:=g_{M}+f^{*}g_{N}
be the pull-back metric on
M
, which is indeed the metric of
\Gamma(f)
.
For an orthonormal
frame field
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
on
M
with respect to
g_{M}
, we have

g=g_{ij}w^{i}\otimes w^{j}\qquad\text{with }g_{ij}=g(a_{i},a_{j})=\delta_{ij}+\langle df(a_{i}),df(a_{j})\rangle,

(2.2)

where
w^{1},\cdots,w^{m}
is the dual cotangent frame field of
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the metric on
N
.
Let
dv
and
dv_{M}
be the volume forms with respect to
g
and
g_{M}
, then

dv=\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}dv_{M}.

(2.3)

Assume
\{f_{t}:t\in(T_{1},T_{2})\}
is a family of maps
from
M
to
N
, such that
f_{0}=f
and for each
t\in(T_{1},T_{2})
,
f_{t}=f
outside a fixed bounded domain
D\subset M
.
Let
g_{t}=g_{ij}(t)w^{i}\otimes w^{j}
be the metric on
\Gamma(f_{t})
and
dv_{t}
be the corresponding volume form.
Now we calculate the first and second variations of the areas of these graphs, as in
[
6
]
. Let
V:=\frac{df_{t}}{dt}
be the variation field, then

\frac{dg_{ij}}{dt}=\langle\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{i})}V,df_{t}(a_{j})\rangle+\langle\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{j})}V,df_{t}(a_{i})\rangle

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}g_{ij}}{dt^{2}}=

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{i})}V,\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{j})}V\rangle+\langle\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{i})}\nabla_{V}V,df_{t}(a_{j})\rangle+\langle\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{j})}\nabla_{V}V,df_{t}(a_{i})\rangle

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\langle R_{df_{t}(a_{i}),V}V,df_{t}(a_{j})\rangle+\langle R_{df_{t}(a_{j}),V}V,df_{t}(a_{i})\rangle.

Here and in the sequel
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection with respect to
g_{N}
, and

R_{XY}:=-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}+\nabla_{[X,Y]}

(2.6)

is the corresponding curvature operator. Denote by
A(t)
the area of
(D,g_{t})
, then

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{D}dv_{t}=\int_{D}\frac{d\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}}{dt}dv_{M}

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}\sum_{i,j}\left(g^{ij}\frac{dg_{ji}}{dt}\right)dv_{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\sum_{i,j}\left(g^{ij}\frac{dg_{ji}}{dt}\right)dv_{t}

and

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\sum_{i,k,l,j}g^{ik}\frac{dg_{kl}}{dt}g^{lj}\frac{dg_{ji}}{dt}dv_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\frac{d^{2}g_{ji}}{dt^{2}}dv_{t}

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{D}\left(\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\frac{dg_{ji}}{dt}\right)^{2}dv_{t}

with
(g^{ij})
being the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
.

Given
x\in M
,
\mu\geq 0
is called a
singular value
of
df:(T_{x}M,g_{M})\rightarrow(T_{f(x)}N,g_{N})
whenever there exists a nonzero
vector
a\in T_{x}M
and a vector
b\in T_{f(x)}N
, such that

df(a)=\mu b,\quad df^{T}(b)=\mu a,

(2.9)

where
df^{T}:(T_{f(x)}N,g_{N})\rightarrow(T_{x}M,g_{M})
is the transpose of
df
, and
a
is called a
singular vector
of
df
associated with
\lambda
.
By the theory of singular value decomposition,
df
has
m
singular values, denoted by

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}>0=\lambda_{r+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{m}\quad(r=\text{rank }df).

(2.10)

Let
a_{i}
be the unit singular vector associated with
\lambda_{i}
, then
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
forms an orthonormal basis of
(T_{x}M,g_{M})
,
and there exists an orthonormal basis
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}
of
(T_{f(x)}N,g_{N})
, such that

df(a_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{i}b_{i}&1\leq i\leq r;\\
0&r+1\leq i\leq m.\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

As in
[
3
]
, we call

\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})

(2.12)

the
singular value vector
of
df
at
x
.

Let

p_{i\alpha}:=\langle\nabla_{df(a_{i})}V,b_{\alpha}\rangle\qquad(1\leq i\leq m,1\leq\alpha\leq n),

(2.13)

then substituting (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) into (
2.2
) and (
2.8
) implies

A^{\prime\prime}(0)=(I)+(II)+(III)+(IV)+(V),

(2.14)

where

(I)=\int_{D}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{p_{ii}^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{i})^{2}}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r,i\neq j}\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}p_{ii}p_{jj}}{(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})}\right)dv,

(2.15)

(II)=\int_{D}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\frac{p_{ij}^{2}+p_{ji}^{2}-2\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}p_{ij}p_{ji}}{(1+\lambda_{i}^{2})(1+\lambda_{j}^{2})}dv,

(2.16)

(III)=\int_{D}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r,r+1\leq\alpha\leq n}\frac{p_{i\alpha}^{2}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}dv,

(2.17)

(IV)=\int_{D}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}\langle\nabla_{b_{i}}\nabla_{V}V,b_{i}\rangle dv

(2.18)

and

(V)=-\int_{D}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}\langle R_{b_{i},V}b_{i},V\rangle dv.

(2.19)

As shown in
[
4
]
,
(I)
is the integration of a quadratic form of
p_{11},\cdots,p_{rr}
, which is non-negative definite if and only if

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\leq 1\qquad\forall 1\leq i<j\leq m

(2.20)

and

\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-\lambda_{i}^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-\lambda_{1}^{2})\cdots\lambda_{i}^{2}\cdots(1-\lambda_{m}^{2})\geq 0.

(2.21)

(II)\geq 0
whenever (
2.20
) holds. On the other hand, obviously
(III)\geq 0
,
(IV)
vanishes whenever
\{f_{t}\}

is a geodesic homotopy, and the non-negativity of
(V)
is a direct corollary of
K_{N}\leq 0
.

3
Confined properties of singular value vectors

Let
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}),y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
1\leq l\leq m
, and we call that
x
is
l
-majorized
by
y
, denoted by
x\preceq_{l}y
, whenever

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{\downarrow}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{\downarrow}\qquad k=1,\cdots,l,

(3.1)

where
\{x_{i}^{\downarrow}\}
and
\{y_{i}^{\downarrow}\}
are the rearrangements of
\{x_{i}\}
and
\{y_{i}\}
in descending order, respectively. Moreover,
we denote
x\asymp_{l}y
if the equalities of (
3.1
) hold simultaneously for
k=1,\cdots,l
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}
be both smooth maps from
D\subset M
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\{f_{t}:t\in[0,1]\}

be the geodesic homotopy of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
. Namely, for each
x\in D
,

t\mapsto f_{t}(x)=(1-t)f_{0}(x)+tf_{1}(x)

(3.2)

is the straight line segment from
f_{0}(x)

to
f_{1}(x)
. For any fixed
x
, denote by

\lambda(t):=(\lambda_{1}(t),\cdots,\lambda_{m}(t))

(3.3)

the singular value vector of
df_{t}
at
x
, which can be seen as a continuous vector-valued function,
then for any closed interval
I\subset[0,1]
, each value
of
\lambda(t)
with
t
lying in the interior of
I
can be
l
-majorized by the convex combinations of its values taking at the two end points. More precisely:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

\mu(t):=\frac{t_{2}-t}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda(t_{1})+\frac{t-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda(t_{2}),

(3.4)

be a linear function defined on
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,1]
,
then

\lambda(t)\preceq_{m}\mu(t)

(3.5)

for each
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
.
In particular,
\lambda(t_{0})\asymp_{l}\mu(t_{0})
for a given
t_{0}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
and
1\leq l\leq m
forces
\lambda(t)\asymp_{l}\mu(t)
for all
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, and moreover

•

\lambda_{i}(t)=\mu_{i}(t)=\frac{t_{2}-t}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda_{i}(t_{1})+\frac{t-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda_{i}(t_{2})
for all
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
and
i=1,\cdots,l
;

•

There exist
a_{1},\cdots,a_{l}\in T_{x}M
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that
g_{M}(a_{i},a_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,

\langle b_{i},b_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
and
df_{t}(a_{i})=\lambda_{i}(t)b_{i}
;

•

\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{i})}V=\frac{\lambda_{i}(t_{2})-\lambda_{i}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}b_{i}
for each
i=1,\cdots,l
.

Proof..

For any given
t_{0}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
(or
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}
) be orthonormal basis of
(T_{x}M,g_{M})
(or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
), such that

df_{t_{0}}(a_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{i}(t_{0})b_{i}&1\leq i\leq r;\\
0&r+1\leq i\leq m.\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

Define

F_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle df_{t}(a_{i}),b_{i}\rangle

(3.7)

and

S_{k}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(t),

(3.8)

for
k=1,\cdots,m
, then it is easy to check that

•

F_{k}
is a linear function;

•

F_{k}(t_{0})=S_{k}(t_{0})
;

•

F_{k}(t)\leq S_{k}(t)
;

•

F_{1}(t)=S_{1}(t),\cdots,F_{l}(t)=S_{l}(t)
if and only if

df_{t}(a_{i})=\lambda_{i}(t)b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l
.

Thus

\displaystyle S_{k}(t_{0})=F_{k}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{2}-t_{0}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}F_{k}(t_{1})+\frac{t_{0}-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}F_{k}(t_{2})

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{2}-t_{0}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}S_{k}(t_{1})+\frac{t_{0}-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}S_{k}(t_{2}),

i.e.
\lambda(t_{0})
is
m
-majorized by
\mu(t_{0})
.

The equality of (
3.9
) holds if and only if
F_{k}(t_{1})=S_{k}(t_{1})
and
F_{k}(t_{2})=S_{k}(t_{2})
, which implies
S_{k}(t)=F_{k}(t)
for all
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
,
due to the convexity of
S_{k}
. If
S_{1}(t)=F_{1}(t),\cdots,S_{l}(t)=F_{l}(t)
, then
\lambda_{i}(t)=S_{i}(t)-S_{i-1}(t)
has to be linear,
and hence

\nabla_{df_{t}(a_{i})}V=\nabla_{V}df_{t}(a_{i})=\frac{d}{dt}(\lambda_{i}(t)b_{i})=\frac{\lambda_{i}(t_{2})-\lambda_{i}(t_{1})}{t_{2}-t_{1}}b_{i}.

(3.10)

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Given
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,1]
and a symmetric convex set
\mathcal{C}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}
, if

\lambda(t_{1})
and
\lambda(t_{2})
both lie in
\mathcal{C}
, then
\lambda(t)
lies in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
for each
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. In particular,
if there exists
t_{0}\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, such that
\lambda(t_{0})\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and
\lambda_{1}(t_{0})>1
, then
\lambda(t)=\mu(t)
for each
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
.

Proof..

Let

H(x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})=\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}^{2}}

(3.11)

be a function defined on
\Omega:=\{(x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m-1}:0\leq x_{i}<1\text{ for each }2\leq i\leq m\}
. A straightforward calculation shows:

•

x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for each
2\leq i\leq m
implies
H(x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})\leq H(y_{2},\cdots,y_{m})
, and the equality holds if and only if

(x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})=(y_{2},\cdots,y_{m})
;

•

H
is a strictly convex function on
\Omega
.

For an arbitrary singular value vector
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m})
,
\lambda\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
if and only one of the following 2 cases occurs:

•

\lambda_{1}\leq 1
;

•

\lambda_{1}>1
,
\lambda_{2}<1
,
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\leq 1
and
\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}+H(\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\leq m-1
.

Furthermore,
\lambda\in\partial\mathcal{M}
is equivalent to the happening of one and only one case of the following:

•

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1
;

•

\lambda_{1}>1
,
\lambda_{2}<1
,
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=1
or
\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{1}^{2}}+H(\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m})=m-1
.

To show
\lambda(t)\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}
for each
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, it suffices to consider each given
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
satisfying
\lambda_{1}(t)>1
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is convex,
\mu(t)=\frac{t_{2}-t}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda(t_{1})+\frac{t-t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\lambda(t_{2})
still lies in
\mathcal{C}
, and Proposition

3.1
gives
\lambda(t)\preceq_{m}\mu(t)
. Then
\mu_{1}(t)\geq\lambda_{1}(t)>1
and
\mu_{2}(t)<1
. The symmetry and convexity of
\mathcal{C}
implies

are the truncations of

and

, respectively.
Let

then

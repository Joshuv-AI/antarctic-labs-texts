Tensor Completion via Tensor Train Based Low-Rank Quotient Geometry under a Preconditioned Metric 0 0 footnotetext: Authors are listed alphabetically.
Jian-Feng Cai, Wen Huang, Haifeng Wang, Ke Wei

Abstract
Low-rank tensor completion problem is about recovering a tensor from partially observed entries. We consider this problem in the tensor train format and extend the preconditioned metric from the matrix case to the tensor case. The first-order and second-order quotient geometry of the manifold of fixed tensor train rank tensors under this metric is studied in detail. Algorithms, including Riemannian gradient descent, Riemannian conjugate gradient, and Riemannian Gauss-Newton, have been proposed for the tensor completion problem based on the quotient geometry. It has also been shown that the Riemannian Gauss-Newton method on the quotient geometry is equivalent to the Riemannian Gauss-Newton method on the embedded geometry with a specific retraction. Empirical evaluations on random instances as well as on function-related tensors show that the proposed algorithms are competitive with other existing algorithms in terms of recovery ability, convergence performance, and reconstruction quality. Keywords. Low-rank tensor completion, tensor train decomposition, Riemannian optimization, quotient geometry, preconditioned metric

Then the low-rank tensor completion problem can be formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{{\cal X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}}~h\left({\cal X}\right):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\quad\text{s.t. }~\rank_{\TT}\left({\cal X}\right)=\bm{r},

(1.1)

where
{\cal T}

{\cal X}

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}\right)\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)

0,~&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

1.1
Preliminaries on Tensor Train Decomposition

Tensor train decomposition.

In the tensor train (TT) decomposition, a
d
-dimensional tensor

{\cal X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
can be expressed as a product of
d
third order tensors. More precisely, the
\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)
-th element of
{\cal X}
is

\displaystyle{\cal X}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)=\sum_{\ell_{1}=1}^{r_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\ell_{d-1}=1}^{r_{d-1}}{\cal X}^{1}\left(1,i_{1},\ell_{1}\right){\cal X}^{2}\left(\ell_{1},i_{2},\ell_{2}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{d}\left(\ell_{d-1},i_{d},1\right),

where
{\cal X}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times n_{k}\times r_{k}}
are the core tensors,
k=1,\cdots,d
, and
r_{0}=r_{d}=1
. For conciseness, we denote by
{\cal X}^{k}\left(i_{k}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times r_{k}}
the
i_{k}
-th slice of
{\cal X}^{k}
which yields the following equivalent expression

\displaystyle{\cal X}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)={\cal X}^{1}\left(i_{1}\right){\cal X}^{2}\left(i_{2}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{d}\left(i_{d}\right).

(1.2)

In addition, for any
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, the left and right unfolding operators:
L:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{2}\times n_{3}}
,
R:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}n_{3}}
are defined as

\displaystyle L\left({\cal Z}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{2}\times n_{3}}:L\left({\cal Z}\right)\left(i_{1}+n_{1}\left(i_{2}-1\right),i_{3}\right)

\displaystyle={\cal Z}\left(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\right),

\displaystyle R\left({\cal Z}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}n_{3}}:R\left({\cal Z}\right)\left(i_{1},i_{2}+n_{2}\left(i_{3}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\cal Z}\left(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\right).

k
-th unfolding and interface matrices.

The
k
-th unfolding of a tensor
{\cal X}
is a matrix of size
n_{1}\cdots n_{k}\times n_{k+1}\cdots n_{d}
, defined by

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{<k>}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k};i_{k+1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)={\cal X}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right),

where the semicolon represents the separation of the row and column indices: the first
k
indices of
{\cal X}
enumerate the rows of
{\cal X}^{<k>}
, and the last
d-k
the columns of
{\cal X}^{<k>}
. Additionally, a tensor
{\cal X}
with core tensors
\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}
can be split into left and right parts

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{\leq k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}\times r_{k}}:{\cal X}^{\leq k}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k};:\right)

\displaystyle={\cal X}^{1}\left(i_{1}\right){\cal X}^{2}\left(i_{2}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{k}\left(i_{k}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{\geq k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}n_{k+1}\cdots n_{d}\times r_{k-1}}:{\cal X}^{\geq k}\left(i_{k},i_{k+1},\cdots,i_{d};:\right)

\displaystyle=\left[{\cal X}^{k}\left(i_{k}\right){\cal X}^{k+1}\left(i_{k+1}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{d}\left(i_{d}\right)\right]^{\mathsf{T}},

the so-called interface matrices
[
27
]
. The following recursive relations between the interface matrices, which follow immediately from the definition, will be very useful: for
k=1,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{\leq k}=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{\geq k}=\left({\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},

(1.3)

where
\bm{I}_{n_{k}}
is the identity matrix of size
n_{k}\times n_{k}
,
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product and
{\cal X}^{\leq 0}={\cal X}^{\geq d+1}:=1
. With these notations, the
k
-th unfolding of
{\cal X}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal X}^{<k>}={\cal X}^{\leq k}{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}={\cal X}^{\leq k}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\left({{\cal X}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right).

(1.4)

We also define the interface matrices product as follows

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{k}

\displaystyle={{\cal X}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\leq k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bm{R}^{k}

\displaystyle={{\cal X}^{\geq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\geq k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times r_{k-1}}.

(1.6)

TT rank.

The TT rank of a tensor
{\cal X}
is defined as the smallest
\left(1,r_{1},\cdots,r_{d-1},1\right)
such that
{\cal X}
admits a TT decomposition (
1.2
) with core tensors of size
r_{k-1}\times n_{k}\times r_{k}
, for
k=1,\cdots,d
.
The TT rank
r_{k}
is closely related to the rank of the
k
-th unfolding of
{\cal X}
.
More precisely, if a tensor can be decomposed as (
1.2
), it necessarily holds that
r_{k}\geq\rank\left({\cal X}^{<k>}\right)

[
30
]
. Furthermore, there exists a TT decomposition with
r_{k}=\rank\left({\cal X}^{<k>}\right)
. Consequently,

r_{k}
is equal to the rank of
{\cal X}^{<k>}
and

\displaystyle\rank_{\TT}\left({\cal X}\right)=\bm{r}=\left(1,r_{1},\cdots,r_{d-1},1\right):=\left(1,\rank\left({\cal X}^{<1>}\right),\cdots,\rank\left({\cal X}^{<d-1>}\right),1\right).

In addition to the basics of TT decomposition, we also need the following two notions in this paper:

•

The mode-
k
product of a tensor
{\cal X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
with a matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{k}\times n_{k}}
, denoted
{\cal X}\times_{k}\bm{A}
, yields a tensor of size
n_{1}\times\cdots\times m_{k}\times\cdots\times n_{d}
. The
\left(i_{1},\cdots,j_{k},\cdots,i_{d}\right)
-th entry of
{\cal X}\times_{k}\bm{A}
is

\displaystyle\left({\cal X}\times_{k}\bm{A}\right)\left(i_{1},\cdots,j_{k},\cdots,i_{d}\right)=\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}{\cal X}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k},\cdots,i_{d}\right)\bm{A}\left(j_{k},i_{k}\right).

•

The matricization operator for a tensor
{\cal X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{k}\left({\cal X}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}\times\prod_{j\neq k}n_{j}}:~{\cal M}_{k}\left({\cal X}\right)\left(i_{k},1+\sum_{\ell=1,\ell\neq k}^{n}\left(i_{\ell}-1\right)J_{\ell}\right)={\cal X}\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right),

where
J_{\ell}=\prod_{j=1,j\neq k}^{\ell-1}n_{j}
. It can be verified that we have the following two equations related to (
1.4
): for
k=1,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{3}\left({\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\times_{3}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},

(1.7)

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{2}\left({\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\times_{3}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\right)

\displaystyle={\cal M}_{2}\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)\left({\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}.

1.2
Geometric Structure

Let
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
be a set of fixed tensor train rank tensors, that is

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}=\left\{{\cal X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}:\rank_{\TT}\left({\cal X}\right)=\bm{r}\right\}.

(1.8)

For
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
to be non-empty, the necessary and sufficient conditions are

\displaystyle r_{k-1}\leq n_{k}r_{k},~r_{k}\leq n_{k}r_{k-1},~k=1,\cdots,d,

see for example
[
30
, Section 9.3.3]
.
Moreover, it has been shown that the set
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}

forms a smooth embedded submanifold of dimension
\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k-1}n_{k}r_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}

[
14
]
.

To introduce the quotient geometry,
assume
{\cal X}
is represented in the TT format (
1.2
)
with core tensors
\bar{{\cal X}}:=\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}
. It is known that the condition
\rank_{\TT}\left({\cal X}\right)=\bm{r}
is equivalent to

\rank\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)\right)=r_{k}
and
\rank\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)\right)=r_{k-1}
,
k=1,\cdots,d

[
14
,
30
]
. We denote by
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}:=\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{r_{0}\times n_{1}\times r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{r_{d-1}\times n_{d}\times r_{d}}
the set of tensors with the following rank constraint: for any
{\cal X}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{r_{k-1}\times n_{k}\times r_{k}}
,
\rank\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)\right)=r_{k}
, and
\rank\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)\right)=r_{k-1}
,
k=1,\cdots,d
. Let the mapping
\phi
be

\displaystyle\phi:\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\rightarrow{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}:\bar{{\cal X}}\rightarrow\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right),\hbox{ such that }\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}\right)={\cal X}^{1}\left(i_{1}\right){\cal X}^{2}\left(i_{2}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{d}\left(i_{d}\right).

It is not hard to see that the image of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
under
\phi
is
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
.
Let
\bar{{\cal X}}=\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\in\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
and
\bar{{\cal Y}}=\left\{{\cal Y}^{1},\cdots,{\cal Y}^{d}\right\}\in\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
. Then by Proposition 7 in
[
29
]
,

\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\phi\left(\bar{{\cal Y}}\right)
if and only if there exist invertible matrices
\bm{A}_{1},\cdots,\bm{A}_{d-1}
of appropriate sizes such that

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal Y}}=\left\{{\cal X}^{1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},{\cal X}^{2}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{d-1}^{-1}\right\}.

(1.9)

We denote by
\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]
the set containing all points
\bar{{\cal X}}\in\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
that obeys
\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)={\cal X}
. This set is known as the equivalent class. Moreover, let
{\cal G}
be the Lie group

\displaystyle{\cal G}=\left\{\mathrm{A}=\left(\bm{A}_{1},\cdots,\bm{A}_{d-1}\right):\bm{A}_{k}\in\gl(r_{k}),~\text{for}~k=1,\cdots,d-1\right\},

where
\gl(r_{k})
is the set of non-singular matrices of size
r_{k}\times r_{k}
, and define the quotient set

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]:\bar{{\cal X}}\in\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\right\}.

(1.10)

As shown in
[
29
]
,
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
is a quotient manifold of dimension
\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k-1}n_{k}r_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}
.

The natural projection
\pi
which maps the element in the total space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
to the quotient space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
is defined as

\displaystyle\pi:\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\rightarrow\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}:\bar{{\cal X}}\rightarrow\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right].

Then, it is evident that there is a bijective mapping
\Phi
from
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
to
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
such that
\phi=\Phi\circ\pi
.

1.3
Main Contributions and Outline

For the low rank tensor completion problem in the tensor train format, several computational methods have been developed, including block coordinate descent
[
3
]
, iterative hard thresholding
[
25
]
, gradient-based optimization
[
33
]
, Riemannian optimization
[
6
,
27
,
32
]
. In this paper, we study this problem based on the quotient geometry under a specific metric. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We extend the preconditioned metric from matrix to tensor and
exploit the first order and second order geometry of the quotient manifold
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
under this metric. Even though the results are extensions from the matrix case, the mathematical derivations are by no means trivial due to the complications of the tensor algebra. In particular, to compute the projection onto the horizontal space, we have to solve a system of linear equations where the coefficient matrix is symmetric and block tridiagonal. A fundamental contribution of this paper is that the positive definiteness of the coefficient matrix has been established.

•

Riemannian optimization algorithms based on the quotient geometry are proposed, including Riemannian gradient descent, Riemannian conjugate gradient, and Riemannian Gauss-Newton.
In particular, it has been shown that the Riemannian Gauss-Newton method on the quotient geometry is equivalent to the Riemannian Gauss-Newton method on the embedded geometry with a specific retraction.
The per iteration computational complexity of the first order algorithms presented in this paper scales linearly in the dimension
d
, in the tensor size
n
and in the sampling set size
|\Omega|
, scales polynomially in the TT rank
\bm{r}
. Overall, it is comparable with that of the Riemannian conjugate gradient method proposed in
[
27
]
based on the submanifold. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed algorithms for the tensor completion problem are competitive with other state-of-the-art algorithms in terms of recovery ability, convergence performance, and reconstruction quality.

The rest of this manuscript is outlined as follows. Section
2
investigates the first order and second order geometry of
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
under the preconditioned metric. Riemannian gradient descent, Riemannian conjugate gradient, and Riemannian Gauss-Newton methods are presented in Section
3
.
Empirical performance evaluations of the algorithms are given in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we conclude this paper with some future research directions.

2
Quotient Geometry under Preconditioned Metric

2.1
Preconditioned Metric and Horizontal Lift

Recall that the total space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
is defined as
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}=\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{r_{0}\times n_{1}\times r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{r_{d-1}\times n_{d}\times r_{d}}
.
The vertical space, denoted by
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
, is the tangent space to the equivalent class
\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
. The expression for
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

The vertical space at
\bar{{\cal X}}
is

\displaystyle{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\left\{{\cal X}^{1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},-{\cal X}^{2}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{1}+{\cal X}^{2}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,-{\cal X}^{d}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{d-1}\right\}:\bm{D}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}},k=1,\cdots,d-1\right\}.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
29
, Section 4.3]
.
∎

Notice that the vertical space is a subspace of
T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
. The horizontal space, denoted by
{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
, is any subspace of
T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
that is complementary to
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
. For the quotient manifold
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
(
1.10
), any
element
\bar{\xi}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
satisfying
\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
can be seen as a representation of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
where
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
. Since the kernel of
\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right):T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\rightarrow T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
is the vertical space
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
, there are infinitely many representations of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
in
T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
. Nevertheless,
one can find a unique representation of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
in horizontal space
{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
. The tangent vector
\bar{\xi}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
satisfying
\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
is called the horizontal lift of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
. Throughout this paper, the horizontal lift of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
is denoted by
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
.

Regarding the horizontal space, a particular one by imposing orthogonal conditions is proposed in
[
29
]
. This horizontal space has also been exploited in
[
8
]
for the development of Riemannian quotient algorithms. Moreover, given a Riemannian metric
\bar{g}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
,
one can construct the horizontal space
{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
which is orthogonal complementary to the vertical space
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
:

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\bar{\xi}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}:\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=0,~\text{for all}~\bar{\eta}\in{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right\}.

(2.1)

In this manuscript, we consider a preconditioned metric which is extended from the matrix case
[
9
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
34
]
. Let
\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{1}\times r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{2}\times r}
be the space of matrices with full column rank
r
. Given
\bar{x}:=\left\{\bm{G},\bm{H}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{1}\times r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{2}\times r}
, the preconditioned metric on the tangent space of
\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{1}\times r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{2}\times r}
at
\bar{x}
is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{x}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=\trace\left({\xi^{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\eta^{1}\left(\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{H}\right)\right)+\trace\left({\xi^{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\eta^{2}\left(\bm{G}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\right)\right),

(2.2)

where
\bar{\xi}=\left\{\xi^{1},\xi^{2}\right\}
and
\bar{\eta}=\left\{\eta^{1},\eta^{2}\right\}\in T_{\bar{x}}\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{1}\times r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{2}\times r}
. Under this metric, the Riemannian gradient of a function
\bar{f}
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\displaystyle\grad\bar{f}(\bar{x})=\left(\nabla_{\bm{G}}\bar{f}(\bar{x})\left(\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{H}\right)^{-1},\nabla_{\bm{H}}\bar{f}(\bar{x})\left(\bm{G}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\right)^{-1}\right),

(2.3)

where
\nabla_{\bm{G}}\bar{f}(\bar{x})
and
\nabla_{\bm{H}}\bar{f}(\bar{x})
are the Euclidean partial derivatives of
\bar{f}
. Equivalently, we rewrite (
2.3
) in a vectorization form as

\displaystyle\vect\left(\grad\bar{f}(\bar{x})\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\left(\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{H}\right)^{-1}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{1}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\left(\bm{G}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{G}\right)^{-1}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{2}}\end{bmatrix}}_{=:\bm{J}^{-1}}\vect\left(\left(\nabla_{\bm{G}}\bar{f}(\bar{x}),\nabla_{\bm{H}}\bar{f}(\bar{x})\right)\right).

Thus, the Riemannian gradient descent direction (
2.3
) can be viewed as an approximation of the Newton direction. The metric in (
2.2
) is known as the preconditioned metric on
\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{1}\times r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{n_{2}\times r}
.

Note that it is not evident to extend the preconditioned metric from the form presented in (
2.2
) because there are tensor factors in the TT format. However, the following equivalent expression of (
2.2
) provides a more convenient form for the extension:

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{x}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=\left\langle\xi^{1}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}},\eta^{1}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\bm{G}{\xi^{2}}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{G}{\eta^{2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle.

Basically, the precondition metric is given by replacing each factors in
\left\langle\bm{G}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{G}\bm{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle
by the corresponding tangent vectors in the same mode. This observation leads to the following generalization in the tensor case.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\bar{{\cal X}}=\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\in\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
, the preconditioned metric
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}:T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\times T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{k-1},\xi^{k},{\cal X}^{k+1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{k-1},\eta^{k},{\cal X}^{k+1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle,

(2.4)

where
\bar{\xi}:=\left\{\xi^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d},\bar{\eta}:=\left\{\eta^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The preconditioned metric defined in (
2.4
) has the following equivalent expression:

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\trace\left({\cal M}_{2}\left(\xi^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal M}_{2}\left(\eta^{k}\right)\left({\bm{H}^{k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{H}^{k}\right)\right),

which coincides with that in (
2.2
). Here
\bm{H}^{k}={\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}
, for
k=1,\cdots,d
.

Proof.

The application of (
1.4
) yields that

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{k-1},\xi^{k},{\cal X}^{k+1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{k-1},\eta^{k},{\cal X}^{k+1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\eta^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\xi^{k}\times_{1}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\times_{3}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1},\eta^{k}\times_{1}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\times_{3}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle{\cal M}_{2}\left(\xi^{k}\right){\bm{H}^{k}}^{\mathsf{T}},{\cal M}_{2}\left(\eta^{k}\right){\bm{H}^{k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\trace\left({\cal M}_{2}\left(\xi^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal M}_{2}\left(\eta^{k}\right)\left({\bm{H}^{k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{H}^{k}\right)\right),

where the second and third equations follow from (
1.7
).
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Under the preconditioned metric
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
defined in (
2.4
), the horizontal space at
\bar{{\cal X}}
is

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\bar{\xi}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}:L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right)\bm{R}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\bm{L}^{k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},k=1,\cdots,d-1\bigg\}.

Proof.

By the definition of the horizontal space in (
2.1
), for a tangent vector
\bar{\xi}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
, we must have
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=0
for all
\bar{\eta}\in{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
.
With the application of Proposition
2.1
,
the equation
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}\right)=0
reduces to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,-{\cal X}^{d}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{d-1}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=2}^{d-1}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,-{\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{k-1}+{\cal X}^{k}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)-\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k+1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{k}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}-{\cal X}^{\leq k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}\right)\left({{\cal X}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right),{\cal X}^{\leq k}\bm{D}_{k}{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle{{\cal X}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}-{\cal X}^{\leq k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}\right)\left({{\cal X}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\right){\cal X}^{\geq k+1},\bm{D}_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right)\bm{R}^{k+1}-\bm{L}^{k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{D}_{k}\right\rangle

where the fourth equation is due to (
1.4
) and the last line follows from (
1.1
) and (
1.5
). Since
\bm{D}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}
is an arbitrary matrix, one can conclude that for
k=1,\cdots,d-1
,

\displaystyle L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right)\bm{R}^{k+1}=\bm{L}^{k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},

(2.5)

which completes the proof.
∎

The projections of any
\bar{\xi}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
onto the vertical and horizontal spaces, denoted
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal V}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)
and
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)
, are given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the preconditioned metric
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
defined in (
2.4
), the projections onto the vertical and horizontal spaces are given by

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal V}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\cal X}^{1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},-{\cal X}^{2}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{1}+{\cal X}^{2}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,-{\cal X}^{d}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{d-1}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi^{1}-{\cal X}^{1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},\xi^{2}+{\cal X}^{2}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{1}-{\cal X}^{2}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,\xi^{d}+{\cal X}^{d}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{d-1}\right).

(2.6)

where
\bm{D}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}
are uniquely determined by the following system of linear equations

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}&\bm{B}_{1}\\
\bm{B}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{A}_{2}&\bm{B}_{2}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&\bm{B}_{d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{A}_{d-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\vect(\bm{D}_{1})\\
\vect(\bm{D}_{2})\\
\vdots\\
\vect(\bm{D}_{d-1})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{b}_{1}\\
\bm{b}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\bm{b}_{d-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.7)

Here, the matrices
\bm{A}_{k}
,
\bm{B}_{k}
and
\bm{b}_{k}
are

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes\bm{L}^{k},

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\bm{b}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k}\times_{1}\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)-\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k+1}\times_{3}\bm{R}^{k+2}\right)\right).

Proof.

Since
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
, the elements of
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)
must satisfy the equation (
2.5
). Substituting the
k
-th core tensor of

{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\bar{\xi}\right)
into (
2.5
) yields that

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{k}\bm{D}_{k}\bm{R}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}+{\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{k-1}\right)\bm{R}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\bm{L}^{k}R\left(\xi^{k+1}-{\cal X}^{k+1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}.

Vectorizing both sides of this equation gives that

\displaystyle\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\vect\left(\bm{D}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\vect\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}+{\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}\bm{D}_{k-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\vect\left(R\left(\xi^{k+1}-{\cal X}^{k+1}\times_{3}\bm{D}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k}\times_{1}\bm{L}^{k-1}+{\cal X}^{k}\times_{1}\left(\bm{L}^{k-1}\bm{D}_{k-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k+1}\times_{3}\bm{R}^{k+2}-{\cal X}^{k+1}\times_{3}\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\bm{D}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)\vect\left(\bm{D}_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\right)\vect\left(\bm{D}_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k}\times_{1}\bm{L}^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\vect\left(\xi^{k+1}\times_{3}\bm{R}^{k+2}\right),

where the second equation is due to (
1.7
). Thus one can obtain
d-1
equations for
k=1,\cdots,d-1
. Stacking them together yields the system of linear equations (
2.7
). The existence and uniqueness of projection implies the invertibility of the coefficient matrix in (
2.7
). As a result,
\bm{D}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}
can be uniquely obtained by solving (
2.7
).
∎

Moreover, it can be shown that the coefficient matrix in (
2.7
) is positive definite.

Lemma 2.3
.

The symmetric block tridiagonal matrix in (
2.7
) is positive definite.

Proof.

Since the symmetric block tridiagonal matrix in (
2.7
) is invertible, we only need to verify the positive semidefiniteness of this matrix.
For any
\bm{x}=\left[\bm{x}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,\bm{x}_{d-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}}
with
\bm{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}^{2}}
,
k=1,\cdots,d-1
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}&\bm{B}_{1}\\
\bm{B}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{A}_{2}&\bm{B}_{2}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&\bm{B}_{d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{A}_{d-1}\end{bmatrix}}_{:=\bm{M}}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{x}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}&\bm{B}_{1}\\
\bm{B}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{1}\\
\bm{x}_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{x}_{3}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{2}&\bm{B}_{2}\\
\bm{B}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{2}\\
\bm{x}_{3}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots+\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{x}_{d-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{d-2}&\bm{B}_{d-2}\\
\bm{B}_{d-2}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{A}_{d-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{d-2}\\
\bm{x}_{d-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{d-2}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{k}&\bm{B}_{k}\\
\bm{B}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}&\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}}_{:=\bm{M}_{k}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{k}\\
\bm{x}_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}.

We will next show that the matrices
\bm{M}_{k}
are positive semidefinite which naturally yields the positive semidefiniteness of the matrix
\bm{M}
.

First, it is not hard to see that
\bm{A}_{k}
is invertible and positive definite. Thus, by Proposition 2.2 in
[
10
]
, a sufficient and necessary condition for
\bm{M}_{k}\succeq 0
is
\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{k+1}-\bm{B}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}_{k}\right)^{-1}\bm{B}_{k}\succeq 0
.
The application of (
1.1
) yields that

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right),

\displaystyle\bm{R}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}.

It can be seen that

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{k+1}-4\cdot\bm{B}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{A}_{k}^{-1}\bm{B}_{k}

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes\bm{L}^{k+1}-\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\left({\bm{R}^{k+2}}^{-1}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}-R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\right)\otimes\bm{L}^{k}\right)\left(\bm{R}^{k+2}\otimes L\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\right).

Let
\bm{R}^{k+2}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{R}^{k+2}
and
R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)=\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}
be the singular value decomposition of
R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)
. One has
\bm{R}^{k+1}=\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}^{2}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}
which is the eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{R}^{k+1}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle~=R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}^{-2}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle~=\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\right)R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}^{-2}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle~=\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\left(\bm{Y}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}^{-2}\bm{X}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{X}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle~=\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right).

Then, one can obtain

\displaystyle{\bm{R}^{k+2}}^{-1}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}-R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}R\left({\cal X}^{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}-\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}r_{k+1}}-\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\bm{U}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right)\succeq 0.

Consequently,
\bm{M}_{k}\succeq 0
for
k=1,\cdots,d-2
which indicates the positive definiteness of the matrix
\bm{M}
.
∎

2.2
Riemannian Metric

In this section we verify that the preconditioned metric defined on
T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
, see (
2.4
), indeed induces a Riemannian metric on
T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
.
To this end, we first establish the relation
between the horizontal lifts of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
at different elements in
\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
, suppose that the horizontal lift of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
is
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
. Then for any
\bar{{\cal Y}}\in\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]
, the horizontal lift of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
at
\bar{{\cal Y}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}=\theta_{A}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right):=\left\{\xi^{1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},\xi^{2}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,\xi^{d}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{d-1}^{-1}\right\},

where
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}
and
\bm{A}_{1},\cdots,\bm{A}_{d-1}
are invertible matrices such that (
1.9
) holds.

Proof.

By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\theta_{A}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal Y}}\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\theta_{A}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal Y}}\right)\left[\theta_{A}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right].

Thus, under the preconditioned metric,
it remains to show that
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}
. This fact can be verified as follows. For
k=1,\cdots,d-1
, the left hand side of the equation (
2.5
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle{{\cal Y}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal Y}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){{\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{A}_{k-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{L}^{k-1}\bm{A}_{k-1}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-1}\right)L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)\bm{A}_{k}\left(\bm{A}_{k}\bm{R}^{k+1}\bm{A}_{k}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\bm{A}_{k}^{-\mathsf{T}},

while the right-hand side of the equation (
2.5
) can be written as

\displaystyle{{\cal Y}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal Y}^{\leq k}R\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k+1}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left({{\cal Y}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right){\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}

\displaystyle\quad=\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\geq k}R\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k+1}\right)\left({{\cal X}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right){\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\bm{A}_{k}^{-\mathsf{T}}.

Since
\bm{A}_{k}
is a non-singular matrix, we conclude that

\displaystyle{{\cal Y}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal Y}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){{\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}

\displaystyle\quad={{\cal Y}^{\leq k}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal Y}^{\leq k}R\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k+1}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left({{\cal Y}^{\geq k+2}}^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\bm{I}_{n_{k+1}}\right){\cal Y}^{\geq k+1}.

As a result,
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]},\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
, define

\displaystyle g_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\left(\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]},\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right):=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right),

(2.8)

where
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
are the horizontal lifts of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]},\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
. Then
g_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is a Riemannian metric on
T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
.

Proof.

With the application of
[
4
, Theorem 9.34]
,
we only need to verify the following condition

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal X}},\bar{{\cal Y}}\in\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]\Rightarrow\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\right),

where
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\eta^{1},\cdots,\eta^{d}\right\}
(resp.
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}
) are the horizontal lifts of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]},\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
(resp.
\bar{{\cal Y}}
). Given
\bar{{\cal X}},\bar{{\cal Y}}\in\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]
, there exist invertible matrices
\mathrm{A}=\left(\bm{A}_{1},\cdots,\bm{A}_{d-1}\right)
such that (
1.9
) holds. Then one has

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal Y}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal Y}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal Y}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal Y}^{1},\cdots,\eta^{k}\times_{1}\bm{A}_{k-1}^{-1}\times_{3}\bm{A}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,{\cal Y}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)

where the first equation follows from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.3
Riemannian Gradient

Consider a real-valued function
f:\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and its lift
\bar{f}=f\circ\pi:\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
By
[
1
, Section 3.6.2]
,
the horizontal lift of the Riemannian gradient of
f
can be obtained from the Riemannian gradient of
\bar{f}
:

\overline{\grad f\left(\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)}=\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right).

Moreover, the Riemannian gradient of
\bar{f}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
is the unique element, denoted
\grad\bar{f}(\bar{{\cal X}})
, such that

\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)=\mathrm{D}\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]~\mbox{ for all }~\bar{\xi}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}.

For the low rank tensor completion problem in the tensor train format, the function
\bar{f}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}.

(2.9)

The next lemma gives the expression of
\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)
under the preconditioned metric (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.6
.

The Riemannian gradient of
\bar{f}
in (
2.9
) is given by

\displaystyle\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\left\{\left\{L^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left({\cal P}_{\Omega}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right)^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\right\},

(2.10)

where
L^{-1}
is defined as the inverse operator of the left unfolding operator
L
such that for any
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times n_{k}\times r_{k}}
and
\bm{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}n_{k}\times r_{k}}
,

\displaystyle L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal Z}\right)\right)={\cal Z}~\text{and}~L\left(L^{-1}\left(\bm{Z}\right)\right)=\bm{Z}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\xi}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
.
We have

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi},\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}+t\bar{\xi}\right)-\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\left({\cal P}_{\Omega}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right)^{<k>},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle.

(2.11)

Moreover, each term in (
2.3
) can be expanded as

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left({\cal P}_{\Omega}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right)^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle,

which yields the expression of the Riemannian gradient of
\bar{f}
.
∎

2.4
Riemannian Connection and Riemannian Hessian

For any two vector fields
\xi,\lambda\in T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
, the horizontal lift of the Riemannian connection is given by
[
1
, Proposition 5.3.3]

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla_{\xi_{[\bar{{\cal X}}]}}\lambda}={\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\nabla_{\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\lambda}\right),

where
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}
denotes the projection onto the horizontal space, see (
2.2
). Next, we derive the Riemannian connection
\nabla_{\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\lambda}
on the total space

\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
by invoking the Koszul formula. For the preconditioned metric
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
(
2.4
), the Koszul formula is

\displaystyle 2\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\nabla_{\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]+\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\eta},\bar{\xi}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\lambda}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\left[\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right]_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\left[\bar{\eta},\bar{\xi}\right]_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\left[\bar{\xi},\bar{\lambda}\right]_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right),

where the definition of the Lie bracket
\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]
can be found for example in
[
1
, Section 5.3.1]
and
\bar{g}\left(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)
.

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right],\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\mathrm{D}\bar{\eta}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{j},\cdots,\lambda^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{j},\cdots,\eta^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle.

By definition of the Lie bracket, one can obtain
[
1
, Section 5.3.4]

\displaystyle\left[\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right]_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\mathrm{D}\bar{\eta}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right].

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle 2\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\nabla_{\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]+\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\eta},\bar{\xi}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{g}\left(\bar{\xi},\bar{\lambda}\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\mathrm{D}\bar{\eta}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\mathrm{D}\bar{\xi}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{\eta}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]-\mathrm{D}\bar{\xi}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\lambda}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=2\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right],\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{4}}

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{5}}

\displaystyle\quad-\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j\neq k}\left\langle\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right\rangle}_{:=\alpha_{6}}.

To obtain a closed-form expression of the Riemannian connection on
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
under the preconditioned metric (
2.4
), we need to rewrite the sum of
\alpha_{i}
in the above equation as the form of
\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)
for a specific
\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
. For
\alpha_{1}
, following the same argument as deriving the Riemannian gradient in Lemma
2.6
, one has

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\gamma}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right),

where the left unfolding of the
k
-th element in
\bar{\gamma}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is

\displaystyle L\left(\gamma_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}.

For
\alpha_{2}
, it can be expressed as

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j<k}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}\right)L\left(\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j>k}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k+1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j<k}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j>k}\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k+1,j}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}},\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\eta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rangle,

where the modified interface matrices are defined as

\displaystyle{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}\times r_{k}}:{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k,j}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k};:\right)

\displaystyle={\cal X}^{1}\left(i_{1}\right)\cdots\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j}\left(i_{j}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{k}\left(i_{k}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}n_{k+1}\cdots n_{d}\times r_{k-1}}:{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k,j}\left(i_{k},i_{k+1},\cdots,i_{d};:\right)

\displaystyle=\left[{\cal X}^{k}\left(i_{k}\right)\cdots\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j}\left(i_{j}\right)\cdots{\cal X}^{d}\left(i_{d}\right)\right]^{\mathsf{T}}.

Similarly, one can
obtain
\alpha_{2}=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\upsilon}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)
,
where the left unfolding of the
k
-th tensor in
\bar{\upsilon}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is

\displaystyle L\left(\upsilon_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)=\sum_{j<k}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)+\sum_{j>k}L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k+1,j}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}.

The remaining four terms
\alpha_{3},\cdots,\alpha_{6}
can be rewritten in the same way and the details are omitted. As a result, we get

\displaystyle 2\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\nabla_{\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\lambda},\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\mathrm{D}\bar{\lambda}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right),

where the left unfolding of the
k
-th element of
\bar{\zeta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is

\displaystyle L\left(\zeta_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{j},\cdots,\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j<k}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)+\sum_{j>k}L\left(\lambda_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\geq k+1,j}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j<k}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\lambda}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right)+\sum_{j>k}L\left(\xi_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{{\cal X}_{\lambda}^{\geq k+1,j}}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j<k}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\lambda}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)-\sum_{j>k}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1,j}_{\lambda}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1,j}_{\xi}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j<k}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\bm{L}^{k-1}}^{-1}{{\cal X}_{\xi}^{\leq k-1,j}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}_{\lambda}^{\leq k-1,j}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)-\sum_{j>k}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1,j}_{\xi}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1,j}_{\lambda}{\bm{R}^{k+1}}^{-1}.

The above argument leads to the following lemma about the Riemannian connection on
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The Riemannian connection on

(2.12)

2.5
Retraction and Vector Transport

Let
{\cal M}
be a general manifold. The tangent space of the manifold
{\cal M}
at
x
is denoted by
T_{x}{\cal M}
and the tangent bundle is denoted by
T{\cal M}
.
A retraction
[
1
]
is a smooth mapping from the tangent bundle to the manifold such that, for all
x\in{\cal M}
,
\eta_{x}\in T_{x}{\cal M}
, (i)
R_{x}\left(0_{x}\right)=x
where
0_{x}
denotes the the zero element of
T_{x}{\cal M}
, and (ii)
\frac{d}{dt}R_{x}\left(t\eta_{x}\right)|_{t=0}=\eta_{x}
.
By
[
1
, Section 4.1.2]
, a retraction on the quotient manifold
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
is given by

\displaystyle R^{Q}_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\left(\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right)=\pi\left(R^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right)=\left[\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right],

(2.13)

where
\bar{R}^{Q}
is a retraction defined on the total space
\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
,

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)=\left\{\left\{{\cal X}^{k}+\xi^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\right\}.

(2.14)

A vector transport
[
1
]
:
T{\cal M}\oplus T{\cal M}\rightarrow T{\cal M}:\left(\eta_{x},\xi_{x}\right)\rightarrow{\cal T}_{\eta_{x}}\xi_{x}
associated with a retraction
R
is a smooth mapping
such that, for all
x\in{\cal M}
,
\eta_{x},\xi_{x}\in T_{x}{\cal M}
, (i)
{\cal T}_{\eta_{x}}\xi_{x}\in T_{R_{x}\left(\eta_{x}\right)}{\cal M}
, (ii)
{\cal T}_{0_{x}}\xi_{x}=\xi_{x}
, and (iii)
{\cal T}_{\eta_{x}}
is a linear map. Here,
\oplus
denotes the Whitney sum
[
1
, P.169]
.
As shown in
[
1
, Section 8.1.4]
,

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal T}^{Q}_{\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}}\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}}_{\bar{{\cal X}}+\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}:={\cal P}^{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}+\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}

defines a vector transport on
T_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}/{\cal G}
. In fact, it can be shown that the above vector transport coincides with the differential of the retraction (
2.13
):

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}R^{Q}_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\left(\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right)\left[\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}R^{Q}_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\left(\eta_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}+t\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right)\bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\pi\circ\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}+t\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\circ\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}+\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}+\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\left[{\cal P}^{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}+\bar{\eta}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right].

recursively via the equation (

and the matrix product requires

flops to form the coefficient matrix in (
2.7
) and
O\left(dr^{6}\right)
flops to solve the linear system

24

O(dnr^{2})
(the dimension of the manifold (

O(d^{2}nr^{4}+dnr^{6})

[
27
]

The Riemannian conjugate gradient method under the quotient geometry (RCG (Q)) for low-rank tensor completion in the tensor train format is presented in Algorithm

[

\displaystyle\beta_{t}=\max\left\{0,\frac{\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}\right)-{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}}^{\cal H}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}_{t-1}\right)\right),\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}\right)\right)}{\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}\right)-{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}}^{\cal H}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}_{t-1})\right)\right),{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}_{t}}^{\cal H}\left(\bar{\eta}_{t-1}\right)\right)}\right\}.

would be the minimizer of the objective function:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Hess f\left(\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]\right)\left[\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}\right]=-\grad f\left(\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]\right).

Lifting both sides of this equation to the horizontal space at
\bar{{\cal X}}

4

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\Hess\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right)=-\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right),

(3.3)

where
\bar{f}=f\circ\pi:\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is the horizontal lift of
\xi_{\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]}
at
\bar{{\cal X}}
. As discussed in Section
2.4
, calculating the Riemannian Hessian is quite involved. Instead, we consider the Riemannian Gauss-Newton method which is an approximation of the geometric Newton method for the case when
\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

Notice that the equation (
3.3

\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
such that for all
\bar{\eta}\in T_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\overline{{\cal M}}_{\bm{r}}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left({\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\Hess\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right),\bar{\eta}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right),\bar{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left({\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\Hess\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right),{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right),{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\Hess\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right],{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right)+\bar{g}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\grad\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right),{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right),

or equivalently,

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{D}\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right]+\nabla^{2}\bar{f}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}},{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\eta}\right],

Algorithm 5

Riemannian Gauss-Newton under the embedded geometry (RGN (E))

Next, we will present a new form of retraction which enables us to establish the equivalence of the Riemannian Gauss-Newton methods under the quotient and embedded geometries. To this end, we first show that
the map
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)|_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}:{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\rightarrow T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
is bijective.

Lemma 3.1
.

The mapping
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)|_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}:{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\rightarrow T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
is bijective.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a tangent vector
\bar{\xi}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
such that
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=0.
By the chain rule and the relation
\phi=\Phi\circ\pi
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\pi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\left(\Phi^{-1}\circ\phi\right)\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\Phi^{-1}\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=0,

which implies that
\bar{\xi}\in{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
. Since
{\cal V}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
is the orthogonal complement of
{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
,
\bar{\xi}
must be the zero element. Thus,
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)|_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}
is injective.

To show that
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)|_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}
is surjective, first note that any
\xi\in T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
can be expressed as
[
30
, Section 9.3.4]

\displaystyle\xi=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi\left({\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\xi^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right)=\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right],

where
\bar{\xi}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}
.
In addition, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\xi}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\xi}+{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal V}}\bar{\xi}\right]=\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi.

Therefore, for any
\xi\in T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
, there is at least one point
{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\xi}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
such that
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[{\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi
.
∎

One can easily verify that
\bar{{\cal X}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
and
{\cal X}\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
, where the tangent space
T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
is specified in Section
3.2.2
. In addition, it is not hard to see that

\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{{\cal X}}\right]=d\cdot\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right).

The bijective property of
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\mid_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}
allows us to define the following specific retraction on the embedded submanifold
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
:

\displaystyle R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(\xi\right)=\phi\left(\left(\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\mid_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\right)^{-1}\left(d\cdot{\cal X}+\xi\right)\right),

(3.7)

where
\xi\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
and
{\cal X}=\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)=\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},{\cal X}^{2},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)
.
The following lemma shows that
R^{E}
defined in (
3.7
) is indeed a retraction.

Lemma 3.2
.

R^{E}
defined in (
3.7
) is a retraction.

Proof.

First that
R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(0\right)={\cal X}
is evident. The bijective property of
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\mid_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}
implies that for any
\xi\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
, there is a unique
\bar{\xi}=\left\{\xi^{1},\xi^{2},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
satisfying
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(0\right)\left[\xi\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(t\xi\right)-R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(0\right)}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\phi\left(\left(\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\mid_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\right)^{-1}\left(d\cdot{\cal X}+t\xi\right)\right)-\phi\left(\left(\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\mid_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}\right)^{-1}\left(d\cdot{\cal X}\right)\right)}{t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}+t\bar{\xi}\right)-\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}\right]=\xi.

Hence
\mathrm{D}R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(0\right)\left[\xi\right]
is an identity map.
∎

Now we are in position to establish the equivalence of the Riemannian Gauss-Newton methods under different geometries.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{\cal X}}
,
\xi\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
be the solutions of the Gauss-Newton equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) respectively, where
{\cal X}=\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]=\xi.

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(\xi\right)=\phi\left(\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right),

where the retractions
\bar{R}^{Q}\left(\cdot\right)
and
R^{E}\left(\cdot\right)
are defined in (
2.14
) and (
3.7
), respectively.

Proof.

By the chain rule and the relation
\bar{F}=F\circ\phi
, the Gauss-Newton equation (
3.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathrm{D}\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}\right],\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathrm{D}\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}\right],\mathrm{D}\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}\right]\right],\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}\right]\right],\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]\right]\right\rangle,

(3.8)

for all
\bar{\eta}\in{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}
.
By the bijective property of
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)|_{{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}}:{\cal H}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\rightarrow T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
, (
3.2.1
) is further equivalent to

\displaystyle 0=\left\langle\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\eta\right],\bar{F}\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\eta\right],\mathrm{D}F\left(\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\right)\left[\xi\right]\right\rangle,~\text{for all}~\eta\in T_{\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}},

(3.9)

where
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\eta}\right]=\eta
and
\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right]=\xi
. This is indeed the same as the Gauss-Newton equation in (
3.5
). Moreover, with the retractions defined in (
2.14
) and (
3.7
), one can easily verify that

\displaystyle R^{E}_{{\cal X}}\left(\xi\right)=\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1}+\xi^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}+\xi^{d}\right\}\right)=\phi\left(\bar{R}^{Q}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\left(\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}\right)\right),

where
\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}=\left\{\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d}\right\}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Note that the Riemannian Gauss-Newton search direction only depends on the differential of
F
and is independent of the Riemannian metric. This is a key property that underlies the equivalence of the Riemannian Gauss-Newton methods under the two geometries.

3.2.2
Computational Details

For the low rank tensor completion problem in the tensor train format, the function
F\left({\cal X}\right)
is given by
F\left({\cal X}\right)={\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)
.
Notice that the update direction
\xi
is also the solution of the following least squares problem
[
1
, Section 8.4]
:

\displaystyle\xi=\argmin_{\xi\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}~\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}{\cal P}_{T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}\xi+{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}.

(3.10)

Assume
{\cal X}
is represented in the TT format (
1.2
) with left-orthogonal core tensors
\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}
,
i.e.,
L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)=\bm{I}_{r_{k}}
, for
k=1,\cdots,d-1
. The tangent space of
{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
at
{\cal X}
is given by
[
14
]

\displaystyle T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\delta{\cal X}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\bigg|~\delta{\cal X}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times n_{k}\times r_{k}},L\left(\delta{\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)=\bm{0},k=1,\cdots,d-1\right\}.

(3.11)

Given a tensor
{\cal Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}
, the orthogonal projection of
{\cal Z}
onto
T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
is
[
19
]

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}\left({\cal Z}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\delta{\cal Z}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right),

(3.12)

where the left unfolding of
\delta{\cal Z}^{k}
is given by

\displaystyle L\left(\delta{\cal Z}^{k}\right)=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}r_{k-1}}-L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\right)^{-1}\quad\mbox{for $k=1,\cdots,d-1$,}

and
L\left(\delta{\cal Z}^{d}\right)=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{d}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq d-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<d>}
.

Solving the least squares problem (
3.10
) directly is computationally prohibitive since the size of
\xi
is
\prod_{k=1}^{d}n_{k}
which grows exponentially in
d
. Fortunately,
\xi\in T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}
implies that the degree of freedom in it is
\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k-1}n_{k}r_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}
. Therefore, the problem (
3.10
) can be rewritten as a least squares problem with the number of parameters equal to
\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k-1}n_{k}r_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}
.
To do so, we need another representation of the tangent space.

Lemma 3.4
.

The tangent space
{T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}
in (
3.11
) has the following alternative form:

\displaystyle T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)+\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\bm{D}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times(n_{k}r_{k-1}-r_{k})},k=1,\cdots,d-1,\bm{D}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{d-1}\times n_{d}}\bigg\},

(3.13)

where
\ten_{<k>}\left(\cdot\right)
is the
k
-th tensorization operator:
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\cdots n_{k}\times n_{k+1}\cdots n_{d}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\cdots\times n_{d}}
,

L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}r_{k-1}\times n_{k}r_{k-1}-r_{k}}
is the orthogonal complement matrix of
L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)
, and
\QR\left({{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}\right)=\bm{Q}^{k+1}\bm{S}^{k+1}
with
{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{Q}^{k+1}=\bm{I}_{r_{k}}
.

Proof.

To establish the equivalence of the tangent spaces in (
3.11
) and (
3.4
), we need to show that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the elements in two spaces. Given
\delta{\cal X}^{k}
, the
k
-th unfolding of the
k
-th element in (
3.11
) is

\displaystyle\left(\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\delta{\cal X}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left(\delta{\cal X}^{k}\right){{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}.

Since
L\left(\delta{\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)=0
, we have
L\left(\delta{\cal X}^{k}\right)=L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{A}^{k}
for some
\bm{A}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}r_{k-1}-r_{k}\times r_{k}}
, where
L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{k}r_{k-1}\times n_{k}r_{k-1}-r_{k}}
is the orthogonal complement matrix of
L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)
. Let
{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}=\bm{Q}^{k+1}\bm{S}^{k+1}
be the QR decomposition of
{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}
with
{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{Q}^{k+1}=\bm{I}_{r_{k}}
and
\bm{S}^{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k}\times r_{k}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\delta{\cal X}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{A}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}.

Thus, the
k
-th element in (
3.4
) with
\bm{D}^{k}=\bm{A}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}
corresponds to the
k
-th element in (
3.11
).

Given
\bm{D}^{k}
, the
k
-th element in (
3.4
) is

\displaystyle\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right),\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right).

Hence, the
k
-th element in (
3.11
) with
\delta{\cal X}^{k}=L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right)
corresponds to the
k
-th element in (
3.4
).
∎

Notice that for
k=1,\cdots,d-1
, the
k
-th unfolding of the
k
-th element in (
3.12
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left(\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\delta{\cal Z}^{k},\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)\right)^{<k>}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}r_{k-1}}-L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<k>}{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}\left(\bm{R}^{k+1}\right)^{-1}{{\cal X}^{\geq k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}{L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<k>}\bm{Q}^{k+1}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}.

It is not hard to see that
{\cal P}_{T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}\left({\cal Z}\right)={\cal A}{\cal A}^{\ast}\left({\cal Z}\right)
where
the operators

and
{\cal A}^{\ast}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times(n_{1}-r_{1})}\times\mathbb{R}^{r_{2}\times(n_{2}r_{1}-r_{2})}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{r_{d-1}\times n_{d}}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\cal A}\left(\left\{\bm{D}^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)+\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right),

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\ast}\left({\cal Z}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\{{L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<k>}\bm{Q}^{k+1}\right\}_{k=1}^{d-1},L^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{d}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq d-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal Z}^{<d>}\right)\right\}.

It can be easily verify that
{\cal A}^{\ast}
is the adjoint of
{\cal A}
. For
\left(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{d}\right)\in\Omega

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{i}=\bm{e}_{i_{1}}\circ\bm{e}_{i_{2}}\circ\cdots\circ\bm{e}_{i_{d}},

where
\bm{e}_{i_{k}}
is the
i_{k}

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{i}\left(j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{d}\right)=\bm{e}_{i_{1}}\left(j_{1}\right)\cdot\bm{e}_{i_{2}}\left(j_{2}\right)\cdots\bm{e}_{i_{d}}\left(j_{d}\right).

Then the objective function in (
3.10

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}{\cal P}_{T_{{\cal X}}{\cal M}_{\bm{r}}}\xi+{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Omega|}\left(\left\langle{\cal B}_{i},{\cal A}{\cal A}^{\ast}\xi\right\rangle+\left\langle{\cal B}_{i},{\cal X}-{\cal T}\right\rangle\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Omega|}\left(\left\langle{\cal A}^{\ast}{\cal B}_{i},{\cal A}^{\ast}\xi\right\rangle+\left\langle{\cal B}_{i},{\cal X}-{\cal T}\right\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|\Omega|}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left\langle{L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal B}_{i}^{<k>}\bm{Q}^{k+1},\bm{D}^{k}\right\rangle+\left\langle\left(\bm{I}_{n_{d}}\otimes{{\cal X}^{\leq d-1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right){\cal B}_{i}^{<d>},L\left(\bm{D}^{d}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle{\cal B}_{i},{\cal X}-{\cal T}\right\rangle\right)^{2},

where

\xi={\cal A}\left(\left\{\bm{D}^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)+\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\ten_{<k>}\left(\left(\bm{I}_{n_{k}}\otimes{\cal X}^{\leq k-1}\right)L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{Q}^{k+1}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)+\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right),\cdots,{\cal X}^{d}\right\}\right)+\phi\left(\left\{{\cal X}^{1},\cdots,\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\phi\left(\bar{{\cal X}}\right)\left[\left\{\left\{L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{d-1},\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right].

\xi={\cal A}\left(\left\{\bm{D}^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}={\cal P}_{\bar{{\cal X}}}^{{\cal H}}\left(\left\{\left\{L^{-1}\left(L\left({\cal X}^{k}\right)^{\perp}\bm{D}^{k}{\bm{S}^{k+1}}^{-\mathsf{T}}\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{d-1},\bm{D}^{d}\right\}\right).

(3.15)

If we use the retraction defined in (

4.1
Recovery Ability vs. Oversampling Ratio and Condition Number

We investigate the recovery ability of the tested algorithms under different oversampling ratios and condition numbers. The oversampling (OS) ratio is defined as the ratio of the number of samples to the dimension,

\displaystyle\OS=\frac{|\Omega|}{\dim\left({\cal M}_{\bm{r}}\right)},

where
|\Omega|
is the number of sampled entries, and
\dim\left({\cal M}_{\bm{r}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}r_{k-1}n_{k}r_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}r_{k}^{2}
is the degrees of freedom of an
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{d}
tensor with TT rank
(1,r_{1},\cdots,r_{d-1},1)
. The condition number for a tensor
{\cal X}
is a natural generalization of the condition number of a matrix which is defined as
[
6
]

\displaystyle\kappa\left({\cal X}\right)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}\right)}{\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}\right)},

where
\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}\right)
and
\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}\right)
are defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}\right)

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}^{<1>}\right),\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}^{<2>}\right),\cdots,\sigma_{\max}\left({\cal X}^{<d-1>}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}\right)

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}^{<1>}\right),\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}^{<2>}\right),\cdots,\sigma_{\min}\left({\cal X}^{<d-1>}\right)\right\}.

We fix
d=3
,
n_{1}=n_{2}=n_{3}=n=100
,
r_{1}=r_{2}=r=5
. Tests are conducted for two different oversampling ratios:
\OS=\left\{4,8\right\}
, and for four different condition numbers: randomly generated tensors with condition number about
1
and
\kappa=\left\{25,50,100\right\}
. Only
\OS\times\dim\left({\cal M}_{\bm{r}}\right)
entries of the ground truth tensor
{\cal T}
are observed. The test tensor
{\cal T}
with condition number about
1
is constructed by the TT format with each core tensor being a random Gaussian tensor of appropriate size. The test tensor
{\cal T}
with a fixed condition number is generated in the following way. The core tensor
{\cal T}^{1}
is a random orthonormal matrix of size
n\times r
. The core tensor
{\cal T}^{2}
is constructed by the Tucker decomposition:
{\cal T}^{2}={\cal S}\times_{1}\bm{U}\times_{2}\bm{V}\times_{3}\bm{W}
, where
{\cal S}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r\times r}
is a diagonal tensor with ones along the superdiagonal and
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
,
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
,
\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
are random orthonormal matrices. The core tensor
{\cal T}^{3}
is given by
{\cal T}^{3}=\bm{X}\bm{\Sigma}{\bm{Y}}^{\mathsf{T}}
, where
\bm{X},\bm{Y}
are two orthonormal matrices of size
r\times r
and
n\times r
respectively, and the diagonal entries of the singular matrix
\bm{\Sigma}
are linearly distributed from
1
to
1/\kappa
. It can be easily verify that
\kappa\left({\cal T}\right)=\kappa\left(\phi\left(\left\{{\cal T}^{1},{\cal T}^{2},{\cal T}^{3}\right\}\right)\right)=\kappa
.

We run each algorithm 100 times for every combination of oversampling ratio and condition number. Tested algorithms are terminated if the relative error
\frac{\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal X}_{t}\right)-{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}}{\left\|{\cal P}_{\Omega}\left({\cal T}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}}
falls below
10^{-4}
or
250
number of iterations are reached. An algorithm is considered to have successfully reconstructed a test tensor if the output tensor
{\cal X}_{t}
satisfies
\frac{\left\|{\cal X}_{t}-{\cal T}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}}{\left\|{\cal T}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}}\leq 10^{-3}
. The rate of successful recovery for different algorithms against different oversampling ratios and condition numbers are listed in Table

Conclusion and Future Directions

In this paper, we study the quotient geometry of the manifold of fixed tensor train rank tensors under a preconditioned metric. Algorithms, including Riemannian gradient descent, Riemannian conjugate descent, and Riemannian Gauss-Newton, have been proposed for the tensor completion problem based on the quotient geometry. It has been empirically demonstrated that the proposed algorithms are competitive with other existing algorithms on random tensors as well as function-related tensors in terms of recovery ability, convergence performance, and reconstruction quality.

There are a few lines of research for future directions. First, we would like to establish theoretical recovery guarantees of the proposed algorithms for the low tensor train rank tensor completion problem. Apart from that, it is also interesting to design efficient algorithms for the tensor robust principal component analysis (RPCA) problem under the quotient geometry based on the tensor train format. Furthermore, it is likely to study the quotient geometry of the low-rank hierarchical Tucker tensors under the preconditioned metric studied in this paper.

Abstract
. In this Short Note we complement the intriguing harmonic analytic perspective due to P. Auscher and A. Axelsson for the abstract evolution equations. This concerns a unified approach to temporally weighted estimates for the forward and backward maximal regularity operators in presence of quadratic estimates and functional calculi. In particular we provide several invariance properties for the maximal regularity operators either in evolution form or in balayage form.

Primary 35K90; Secondary 47A60.

1.
Introduction

Assume that
-A
is a densely defined, closed linear operator on a Hilbert space
{\mathbf{H}}
, with domain
D(A)
and generating a bounded analytic semigroup
\{e^{-zA}:|\arg z|<\delta\}
,
0<\delta<\pi/2
.
In this Note we study the autonomous evolutions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=(0,\infty)
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+Au=f,\\
u(0)=0\end{cases}

(1.1)

and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-Av=f,\\
v(\infty)=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The so-called maximal regularity for the evolution equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the Hilbert space
{\mathbf{H}}

(also referred to as de Simon’s theorem
[
dS64
]
) states as follows:

there is some constant
C=C(A,{\mathbf{H}})>0
such that for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
,

|||Au|||\leq C|||f|||\quad\text{ and }\quad|||Av|||\leq C|||f|||,

(1.3)

where
|||\cdot|||=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})}
.

Thus via (
1.1
) (respectively, (
1.2
)) and (
1.3
), the solution
u
(respectively,
v
) lies in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};D(A))\cap H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
.
The maximal regularity conveyed in (
1.3
) means that
\partial_{t}u
and
Au
(respectively,
\partial_{t}v
and
Av
) enjoy the same regularity as the source
f
.

There are many machineries proving de Simon’s maximal regularity theorem, typically via Fourier transform,
and various extensions to Banach spaces, typically in
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{p}(\Omega))
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For more comprehensive materials about maximal regularity estimates and applications to evolution equations,
see
[
DHP03
,
Are04
,
KW04
,
Mon09
]
by Denk-Hieber-Prüss, Arendt, Kunstmann-Weis, and Monniaux.

2.
Invariance properties for maximal regularity operators

Our aim is to complement the harmonic analytic perspective due to Auscher and Axelsson
[
AA11a
]

in their unified approach to the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\pm 1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
estimates for the maximal regularity operators in presence of quadratic estimates.
Weighted maximal regularity estimates are motivated by elliptic boundary value problems
[
AA11b
]
,
and are useful also in the study of abstract evolution equations, see
[
AA11a
, Section 2]
.

Recall that the forward maximal regularity operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
is defined by

{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}Ae^{-(t-s)A}f(s)ds,

(2.1)

while the backward maximal regularity operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}
is given by

{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}Ae^{-(s-t)A}f(s)ds.

(2.2)

In the notations
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}
, we omit the dependence in
A
.
The operators
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}
are associated to the evolution equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as for appropriate
f
, we have

Au={\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)\quad\text{ and }\quad Av=-{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f).

Therefore, maximal regularity problems translate into boundedness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}
,
which are typical examples of singular integral operators with operator-valued kernels.

Recall that
A
is said to satisfy the quadratic estimate
(Q)_{A}
if for all
h\in{\mathbf{H}}
,

\big|\big|\big|sAe^{-sA}h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathbf{H}}},

(2.3)

where
|||\cdot|||_{-1}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},s^{-1}ds;{\mathbf{H}})}
.
One also refers to
\big|\big|\big|sAe^{-sA}h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}
as square function norm of
h
,
a notion widely used for the abstract Hardy spaces in harmonic analysis and elliptic boundary value problems,
see Auscher
[
Aus07
]
, Auscher-McIntosh-Russ
[
AMR08
]
, Hofmann-Mayboroda-McIntosh
[
HMM11
]
, and Auscher-Stahlhut
[
AS16
]
.

The main results of this Short Note can be summarized as below.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}
be given as in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
). We have

i) (Evolution Formulae) Suppose that
(Q)_{A}
holds. For
f_{0}\in{\mathbf{H}}
, then

{\mathcal{M}}_{+}\left((sA)^{N}e^{-sA}f_{0}\right)(t)=\frac{1}{N+1}(tA)^{N+1}e^{-tA}f_{0},

(2.4)

{\mathcal{M}}_{-}\left(Ae^{-NsA}f_{0}\right)(t)=\frac{1}{N+1}Ae^{-NtA}f_{0},

(2.5)

and the two formulae hold respectively in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
.

ii) (Balayage Formulae) Suppose that
(Q)_{A^{*}}
holds.
For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{-NtA}{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)(t)dt=\frac{1}{N+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{-NsA}f(s)ds;

(2.6)

and for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}(tA)^{N}e^{-tA}{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)(t)dt=\frac{1}{N+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(sA)^{N+1}e^{-sA}f(s)ds;

(2.7)

and both formulae hold weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
.

iii) (Endpoint Balayage Formulae) Suppose that
(Q)_{A^{*}}
holds.
For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
, if in addition
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sA}f(s)ds
converges weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-NtA}{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)(t)dt=\frac{1}{N+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-NsA}f(s)ds;

(2.8)

and for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
, if in addition
\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{-sA}f(s)ds=0
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{N-1}A^{N}e^{-tA}{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)(t)dt=\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{N}A^{N+1}e^{-sA}f(s)ds;

(2.9)

and both formulae hold weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
.

Proof.

By formal computations plus Fubini theorem.
∎

The above formulae are best understood as invariant subspace properties of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}
either in evolution form or in balayage form.
By
evolution
, we mean an extension
E
mapping boundary elements in
{\mathbf{H}}
to
L^{2}_{\text{{\rm loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
,
while by
balayage
(or,
sweeping
), we mean the dual mapping of
E
that sends elements from
L^{2}_{\text{{\rm loc}}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathbf{H}})
to boundary elements in
{\mathbf{H}}
.
Both mappings are extremely useful in the abstract Hardy space theory, see
[
AMR08
,
HMM11
]
.
For concrete balayage operators in connection with
\overline{\partial}
-equation and probability, see Amar-Bonami
[
AB79
]
and Labeye-Voisin
[
LV03
]
.

3.
Illustrations and further remarks

Note that (
2.3
) implies (see Albrecht-Duong-McIntosh
[
ADM96
]
) that
\forall\,h\in{\mathbf{H}}
,

\big|\big|\big|\psi_{1}(sA)h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}:=\big|\big|\big|(sA)^{N}e^{-sA}h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathbf{H}}}

(3.1)

and

\big|\big|\big|\psi_{2}(sA)h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}:=\big|\big|\big|e^{-sA}\left(I-e^{-NsA}\right)h\big|\big|\big|_{-1}\leq C\|h\|_{{\mathbf{H}}},

(3.2)

where
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
.
The functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
in (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) decay at 0 and
\infty
.

For convenience, we also introduce weighted spaces
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\alpha}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Recall the following weighted de Simon’s theorem
[
AA11a
]
for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
):
\forall\,\alpha<1
,

\|Au\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|Av\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-\alpha}},

which, as we mentioned before, are equivalent to

\|{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-\alpha}}.

(3.3)

For the endpoint weights, we have the following beautiful reduction
[
AA11a
]
:

\left\|{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)-Ae^{-tA}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sA}f(s)\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{1}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{1}},

(3.4)

\left\|{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)-e^{-tA}\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{-sA}f(s)\right\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-1}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{-1}}.

(3.5)

Proofs of (
3.3
)-(
3.5
) in
[
AA11a
, Section 1]
are independent of quadratic estimates.

Now we illustrate the convergence issues in Theorem
2.1
:
applying (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) we see that the formulae (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) hold respectively in

\mathfrak{H}_{-1}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
and
\mathfrak{H}_{1}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
;
using a duality argument, (
3.1
) for
A^{*}
, and (
3.3
) we see that the formulae (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) hold weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
.
For (
2.8
), note that (
3.2
) for
A^{*}
implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sA}f(s)ds-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-NsA}f(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sA}\left(I-e^{-(N-1)sA}\right)f(s)ds

converges weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
.
Hence for the family of integrals
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-NsA}f(s)ds
, the weak convergence in
{\mathbf{H}}
for
N=1
encompasses the convergence for other
N\geq 2
.
Note that by
[
AA11a
, Remark 1.7]
or (
3.4
), the assumptions for (
2.8
) guarantee
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(f)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
.
For (
2.9
), by
[
AA11a
, Proposition 1.6]
or (
3.5
), the trace condition

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{2\tau}{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}Ae^{-sA}f(s)ds=0\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathbf{H}}

plus
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
guarantee
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(f)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{-1}dt;{\mathbf{H}})
.
The weak convergence in
{\mathbf{H}}
for (
2.9
) follows upon using a duality argument together with (
3.1
) for
A^{*}
.

.

Independent of
(Q)_{A}
, (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) also hold strongly in
{\mathbf{H}}
for
\forall\,t>0
.

Remark 3.3
.

In view of the neat decomposition (
3.4
), it was observed in
[
AA11a
, Remark 1.7]
that under
(Q)_{A^{*}}
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
extends to a bounded operator on the subspace of those
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
so that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sA}f(s)ds
converges weakly in
{\mathbf{H}}
.
On one hand,
there is no simple description of this subspace
(see
[
AA11a
, Remark 1.7]
),
if no further information on
{\mathbf{H}}
and
e^{-tA}
is provided.
On the other hand, this puzzle of subspace description can be connected to the Carleson duality in harmonic analysis,
see Hytönen and Rosén
[
HR13
,
HR18
]
.
Our formula (
2.8
) gives an indirect description of this subspace: it is an invariant subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},tdt;{\mathbf{H}})
under
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}
.

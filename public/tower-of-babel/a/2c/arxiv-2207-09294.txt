The cotangent bundle of K3 surfaces of degree two
Fabrizio Anella, Andreas Höring

Abstract




Abstract.
K3 surfaces have been studied from many points of
view, but the positivity of the cotangent bundle is not well
understood. In this paper we explore the surprisingly rich geometry
of the projectivised cotangent bundle of a very general polarised K3
surface
S
of degree two. In particular, we describe the geometry of
a surface
D_{S}\subset\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
that plays a similar role to
the surface of bitangents for a quartic in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Keywords.
K3 surface, cotangent bundle, pseudoeffective cone

1. Introduction

1.1. Motivation

The cotangent bundle of a K3 surface
S
is well understood from the point of view of stability theory: we know that
S
is stable for every polarisation
L
. Moreover there are effective bounds guaranteeing that the restriction
{}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
is stable for
every
irreducible curve
C\in|dL|
; see
[
Hei06
,
Fey16
]
. Nevertheless these stability results do not provide a complete description of the positivity properties of
S
. In fact, the cotangent bundle of a K3 surface is never pseudoeffective (
cf.

[
Nak04
,
BDP+13
]
, and
[
HP19
]
for a more general result).

A way of measuring the negativity of the cotangent bundle is to describe the pseudoeffective cone of the projectivised cotangent bundle
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}({}_{S})\rightarrow S
. Denote by
\zeta_{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
the tautological class, and let
\alpha_{S}
be a Kähler class on
S
. In our recent paper
[
AH21
]
we showed that if
\alpha_{S}^{2}\geq 8
, then
\zeta_{S}+\pi^{*}\alpha_{S}
is pseudoeffective, the bound being optimal for a very general non-projective K3 surface; see
[
AH21
, Theorem 1.5]
. The bound is also optimal for infinitely many,
but not all
, families of projective K3 surfaces with Picard number one, see
[
GO20
, Theorem B]
, which leads us to a more geometric question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
S
be a projective K3 surface, and let
L
be an ample Cartier divisor on
S
. Assume that
\zeta_{S}+\lambda\pi^{*}L
is pseudoeffective for some
\lambda<\sqrt{\frac{8}{L^{2}}}
. Can we relate the pseudoeffectivity of
\zeta_{S}+\lambda\pi^{*}L
with the projective geometry of
(S,L)
?

In view of the arguments used by Lazić and Peternell
[
LP20
]
, we might ask even more boldly for a relation between
\Pseff(\mathbb{P}({}_{S}))

and families of elliptic curves on
S
. Gounelas and Ottem
[
GO20
]
give a natural framework for these questions: the embedding

\mathbb{P}\left({}_{S}\right)\subset S^{[2]}

in the Hilbert square allows one to use results on the pseudoeffective cone of
S^{[2]}
by Bayer and Macrì
[
BM14
]
. For example this approach allows one to recover the following classical result.

Theorem 1.2

(
cf.

[
Tih80
,
Wel81
]
,
[
GO20
, Section 4]
)
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be a smooth quartic surface with Picard number one,
and denote by
L
the restriction of the hyperplane class. Then the surface of bitangents
U\subset\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
has class
6\zeta_{S}+8\pi^{*}L
and generates an extremal ray of
\Pseff(\mathbb{P}({}_{S}))
.

The goal of this paper is to give an analogue of Theorem
1.2
in the case of a very general polarised K3 surface
(S,L)
of degree two;
i.e.

S
is a K3 surface with Picard number one obtained as a two-to-one cover

f\colon S\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}

with ramification divisor a smooth curve of genus ten. Oguiso and Peternell
[
OP96
]
observed that in this case, the projectivised cotangent bundle should have some exceptional properties that are not representative for general K3 surfaces. The approach of Gounelas and Ottem allows one to determine the nef cone of
\mathbb{P}({}_{S})

[
GO20
, Section 4.1]
but yields only that
\zeta_{S}+\pi^{*}2L
is pseudoeffective without determining the extremality in
\Pseff(\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
. Note that
\alpha_{S}:=2L
has
\alpha_{S}^{2}=8
, so this situation corresponds exactly to the set-up of Question
1.1
.

1.2. Main results

Let
S
be a very general K3 surface of degree two, and let
f\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the double cover. Let
d\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a line that is a simple tangent of the branch divisor
B
; then its preimage
C:=f^{-1}(d)
has a unique node, so the normalisation

is a smooth elliptic curve. The natural surjection

n^{*}{}_{S}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{}_{\tilde{C}}

determines a morphism
\tilde{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
which we call the canonical lifting of
C\in|L|
(
cf.
Section
3.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(S,L)
be a very general polarised K3 surface of degree two, and denote by
D_{S}\subset\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
the surface dominated by canonical liftings of singular elliptic curves in
|L|
. Then the normalisation of

is a smooth
(
non-minimal
)
elliptic surface. Moreover we have

D_{S}\equiv 30\zeta_{S}+54\pi^{*}L\equiv 30\left(\zeta_{S}+1.8\pi^{*}L\right).

The surface
D_{S}
itself is very singular; the description of the normalisation occupies the larger part of this paper. We will see that
D_{S}
contains a lot of geometric information about the K3 surface; for example it contains a curve isomorphic to the branch divisor
B
in its non-normal locus. While the surface
D_{S}
plays a similar role to the surface of bitangents for the quartic surfaces, it does not generate an extremal ray in the pseudoeffective cone.

2. Notation and general set-up

We work over
\mathbb{C}
. For general definitions, we refer to
[
Har77
]
and
[
Voi02
]
.

All the schemes appearing in this paper are projective; manifolds and normal varieties will always be supposed to be irreducible. For notions of positivity of divisors and vector bundles, we refer to Lazarsfeld’s books
[
Laz04a
,
Laz04b
]
. Given two Cartier divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
on a projective variety
X
, we denote by
D_{1}\simeq D_{2}
(resp.
D_{1}\equiv D_{2}
) the linear equivalence (resp. numerical equivalence) of the Cartier divisor classes, while
D_{1}=D_{2}
is used for an equality of the cycles. We will frequently identify an effective divisor with its cohomology class in
N^{1}(X)
, where
N^{1}(X):=\mbox{NS}(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
is the Néron–Severi space of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors.

In the whole paper we will work in the following setting.

Set-up 2.1
.

Let
S
be a very general polarised K3 surface of degree two, and let
L
be the primitive polarisation on
S
. We denote by

f\colon S\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}

the double cover defined by the linear system
|L|
, so we have
L\simeq f^{*}H_{1}
, where
H_{1}
is the hyperplane class on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Since
|L|=f^{*}|H_{1}|
, every curve
C\in|L|
is a double cover of a line
d\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

We denote by
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
the branch locus of
f
and by
R\subset S
the ramification divisor. By the ramification formula we know that
B
is a sextic curve, so

g(B)=10,\qquad\deg\omega_{B}=18.

Since
f^{*}B=2R
, we see that the ramification divisor
R
is an element of
|3L|
.

Denote by

\pi\colon\mathbb{P}({}_{S})\longrightarrow S

the projectivisation of the cotangent bundle and by
\zeta_{S}
the tautological class. We denote by
l\subset\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
a fibre of
\pi
.

This set-up will become increasingly rich through a series of geometric constructions which we will summarise in Diagrams (
3.4
) and (
3.8
).

Remark
.

Many of our arguments are valid for an arbitrary smooth polarised K3 surface of degree two. However the assumption that
S
is very general is necessary to ensure that the Picard number of
S
is one. Moreover this implies that the branch curve
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
satisfies the assumptions of Plücker’s theorem, which is crucial in Section
3.2
and in the proof of Theorem
3.18
.

3. Birational geometry of the projectivised cotangent bundle

3.1. Elementary transform of
S

Consider the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and its hyperplane class
H_{1}
. We denote by
p_{1}\colon\mathbb{P}({}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
the projectivisation and by
\zeta_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
its tautological class. Let us recall some elementary facts: twisting the Euler sequence by
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)
and recalling that
T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\simeq{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\otimes K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{*}\simeq{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(3)
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus 3}\longrightarrow{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)\longrightarrow 0.

Thus the vector bundle
{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)
is globally generated, and
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2))=3
.

The sequence above shows that there is an inclusion

\mathbb{P}({}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee},

and if we denote by
p_{2}\colon\mathbb{P}({}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
the fibration defined by the projection on the second factor, it allows us to identify
\mathbb{P}({}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
with the universal family of lines on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
;
i.e.
for a point

, the curve

is the line corresponding to the point
y
. The divisor class
\zeta_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}+2p_{1}^{*}H_{1}
defines a base-point-free linear system; in fact we have

\zeta_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}+2p_{1}^{*}H_{1}\simeq p_{2}^{*}H_{2},

where
H_{2}
is the hyperplane class on
(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
.

Remark

We denote by

Proof.

Definition 3.9

Let

Remarks 3.10
.

(a)

If the curve

(b)

We have

(c)

\tilde{n}(t_{1})\neq\tilde{n}(t_{2})
. This also shows that
C_{S}
meets the fibre

(d)

If

The first goal of this subsection is to describe the normalisation of the surface

would lead to a better estimate. However, it is not clear that even such an optimised necessary condition determines the pseudoeffective threshold.

4. Positivity results

In this whole section we work in the same set-up as in Section
3.1
(
cf.
the summary in Diagram (

)). We denote by

the composition
\pi\circ\mu_{S}=p\circ\mu_{P}
. The pseudoeffective

\mathbb{P}(f^{*}{}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
is generated by the

\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
or

Z\equiv aM+b\varphi^{*}L-mE_{P}.

If

-divisor such that the support does not contain

.

4.1. Necessary conditions

In this subsection we collect some basic necessary conditions. As an application we prove the second part of Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\mathbb{R}
-divisor

and

.
Let

Z_{S}\equiv a\zeta_{S}+(2a+b-3m)\pi^{*}L.

Recall that

Remark

The divisor

Let

-divisor

Let

and

(

As preparation let us note that the class of the curve

Corollary 4.4
.

Let

be a pseudoeffective

-divisor class on

that is modified nef, cf.
[

, Definition 2.2]
; write its divisor class as

Remark
.

The property that a divisor class is modified nef is equivalent to its being in the closure of the cone of mobile divisor classes;

[

.

4.2. Sufficient conditions

In this subsection we will use Boucksom’s divisorial Zariski decomposition
[
Bou04
, Theorem 4.8]
: given a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor class
Z
on
Y
, there exists a decomposition
Z_{+}+Z_{-}
such that
Z_{+}
is modified nef,
cf.

[
Bou04
, Definition 2.2]
, and
Z_{-}
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
Z
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor class on
Y
, and let

Z_{+}+Z_{-}
be its divisorial Zariski decomposition.
Assume that the support of
Z_{-}
does not contain
the exceptional divisors
E_{P}
and
E_{S}
.
Then one has

\varphi^{*}L\cdot Z^{2}>0

unless
Z\equiv b\varphi^{*}L
.

Remark 4.7
.

We will need the following elementary remark: let
Z^{\prime}\subset Y
be a prime divisor that surjects onto the surface
S
. For
d\gg 0
let
A\in|dL|
be a general element. Then the intersection
Z^{\prime}\cap\varphi^{-1}(A)
is an irreducible curve. Namely the linear system
\varphi|_{Z^{\prime}}^{*}|dL|
is globally generated, so a general element does not have an irreducible component that is in the non-normal locus of
Z^{\prime}
. Thus we can assume without loss of generality that
Z^{\prime}
is normal. Yet then a general element is normal by
[
BS95
, Theorem 1.7.1]
, hence smooth. Since it is connected by the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem, a general element is irreducible.

Proof.

We first prove the statement in the case where
Z
is a prime divisor. By our assumption on
Z_{-}
, the divisor
Z
is then distinct from
E_{S}
and
E_{P}
. We use the notation (
4.1
);
i.e.
we write

Z\equiv aM+b\varphi^{*}L-mE_{P}.

Note that if
a=0
, then
Z\cdot l_{s}=-m
. Since
Z
is distinct from
E_{S}
, we obtain that
m=0
, so
Z\equiv b\varphi^{*}L
. Thus we can assume that
a>0
.

Using the formulas in Lemma
3.7
, we obtain

\varphi^{*}L\cdot N^{2}=2(a^{2}+2ab-3m^{2}).

Since
a>0
we have
a^{2}+ab>a\cdot(\frac{1}{2}a+b)
.
Applying (
4.3
) twice, we obtain

a\cdot(\tfrac{1}{2}a+b)\geq\tfrac{9}{2}m^{2}.

Thus we have

a^{2}+ab-3m^{2}>1.5m^{2}\geq 0.

The same argument holds if
Z
is a pseudoeffective divisor class that is modified nef. Indeed the argument above only uses (
4.3
).
By Corollary
4.4
, this inequality also holds for pseudoeffective divisors classes that are modified nef.

Now we consider the general case: we write
Z=Z_{+}+\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}Z_{i}
,
where
\lambda_{i}>0
and the
Z_{i}
are the irreducible components of the negative part.
Fix
d\gg 0
such that for a very general element
A\in|dL|
, the intersection

C_{i}:=Z_{i}\cap\varphi^{-1}(A)
is irreducible (this is possible by Remark
4.7
) and
Z_{+}|_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}
is nef (this is possible since the non-nef locus of
Z_{+}
consists of at most countably many curves;
cf.

[
Bou04
, Definition 3.3]
).

Since the divisors
Z_{i}
are distinct, the irreducible
curves
C_{i}=Z_{i}\cap\varphi^{-1}(A)
are distinct. Thus we have

\left(Z|_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}\right)^{2}=\left(Z_{+}|_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}+\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}C_{i}\right)^{2}\geq\left(Z_{+}|_{\varphi^{-1}(A)}\right)^{2}+\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}^{2}C_{i}^{2}.

By what precedes all the terms are non-negative, and the sum is positive unless all the classes are pull-backs from
S
.
∎

We can now give a bigness criterion on
Y
. For the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we will need to work with
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes. This leads to some additional technical effort.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
Z\subset Y
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor class, and let

Z_{+}+Z_{-}
be its divisorial Zariski decomposition. Assume that
the support of
Z_{-}
does not contain
the exceptional divisors
E_{P}
and
E_{S}
.
Assume that there exists a rational number
\varepsilon>0
such that

(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{S}>0
and
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})^{3}>0
.
Then
Z+\varepsilon E_{S}
is big.

Proof.

Step 1. Positivity properties of
Z+\varepsilon E_{S}
.
The relative Picard number of the conic bundle
\varphi\colon Y\rightarrow S
is two, and the relative Mori cone is generated by the curve classes
l_{P}
and
l_{S}
. By assumption,we have
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{S}>0
. Since
E_{P}
is not contained in the support of
Z_{-}
and the deformations of
l_{P}
cover a divisor, we have

Z\cdot l_{P}\geq Z_{-}\cdot l_{P}\geq 0.

Thus we also have
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{P}>0
, and the divisor class

Z+\varepsilon E_{S}
is
\varphi
-ample.

Let
A\in|dL|
be a very general element for some
d\gg 0
.
Set
Y_{A}:=\varphi^{-1}(A)
, and denote by
\varphi_{A}\colon Y_{A}\rightarrow A

the restriction of
\varphi
. We denote the restriction of a divisor class
T

to
Y_{A}
by
T_{A}
. We claim that
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}
is an ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor class.

Proof of the claim.
Note that we do not have
Z\equiv b\varphi^{*}L
since otherwise
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{S}=-\varepsilon<0
. Thus we can apply Lemma
4.6
and obtain
\varphi^{*}L\cdot Z^{2}>0
. Since the class of
A
is a positive multiple of
L
, this implies
Z_{A}^{2}>0
.

By construction
Y_{A}
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}({}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A})
in the finite set
R_{S}\cap\pi^{-1}(A)
. Since
E_{S}
is the exceptional divisor of
\mu_{S}
, the exceptional locus of

\mu_{S,A}\colon Y_{A}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}({}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A})

is equal to the support of
E_{S}\cap Y_{A}
, which is a disjoint union of curves numerically equivalent to
l_{S}
. Since by assumption
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{S}>0
, we obtain that

(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot(E_{S})_{A}>0.

Thus we have

(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}^{2}>Z_{A}\cdot(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\geq Z_{A}^{2},

where in the last inequality, we used again that
E_{S}
is not in the negative part of
Z
. Since
Z_{A}^{2}>0
this finally shows that

(4.5)

(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}^{2}>0.

Now let
Z=Z_{+}+\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}Z_{i}
be the divisorial Zariski decomposition. By assumption none the prime divisors
Z_{i}
coincides
with
E_{S}
or
E_{P}
. Thus the intersection
Z_{i}\cap\varphi^{-1}(A)

is either a union of general
\varphi_{A}
-fibres or an irreducible curve
C_{i}
(
cf.
Remark
4.7
). Moreover by Lemma
4.6
we have

C_{i}^{2}=d\varphi^{*}LZ_{i}^{2}\geq 0,

and equality holds if and only if
Z_{i}\equiv b_{i}\varphi^{*}L
. In both cases, the curve
C_{i}
is a nef divisor on
Y_{A}
. Since
Z_{+}
is modified nef and
A
is very general, the restriction
(Z_{+})_{A}
is nef . In conclusion we obtain that

Z_{A}\equiv(Z_{+})_{A}+\sumop\displaylimits\lambda_{i}C_{i}

is a nef divisor.

In view of (
4.5
) and the Nakai–Moishezon criterion,
cf.

[
Laz04a
, Theorem 2.3.18]
, the claim follows if we show that
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot G>0
for every irreducible curve
G\subset Y_{A}
. Arguing by contradiction, we assume that there exists an irreducible curve
G
such that
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot G\leq 0
.

Since
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})\cdot l_{S}>0
we have
G\nsubset E_{S}
. Since
Z_{A}
is nef and
(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot G\leq 0
, we obtain that
E_{S}\cdot G=0
. Hence
G
is disjoint from the exceptional locus of
\mu_{S,A}
. Thus we have

0\geq(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot G=\left(\mu_{S,A}\right)_{*}(Z+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}\cdot(\mu_{S,A})_{*}G.=\left(\mu_{S,A}\right)_{*}Z_{A}\cdot(\mu_{S,A})_{*}G.

We claim that
(\mu_{S,A})_{*}Z_{A}
is an ample class; this yields the desired contradiction.

For the proof of the claim, note that the push-forward
(\mu_{S})_{*}Z
is pseudoeffective and not a pull-back from
S
, so it is collinear to
\zeta_{S}+\lambda\pi^{*}L
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
. Since
\zeta_{S}
is not pseudoeffective, we have
\lambda>0
.
Yet the cotangent bundle
S
is stable, so if
A
is a sufficiently positive hyperplane section, the restricted vector bundle
{}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
is stable with trivial determinant. Thus the restriction of the tautological class
\zeta_{S}
to
\pi^{-1}(A)
is nef. Since
\lambda>0
this shows that the restriction of
\zeta_{S}+\lambda\pi^{*}L
to
\pi^{-1}(A)
is ample. This proves the claim.

Step 2. Approximation.

Since ampleness is an open property, by Step 1, we can find real numbers
0\leq\delta_{i}\ll 1
such that

Z^{\delta}:=Z-\delta_{1}\varphi^{*}L-\delta_{2}E_{P}-\delta_{3}E_{S}

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor class with the following properties:

•

Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}
is
\varphi
-ample;

•

(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}
is ample;

•

(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})^{3}>0
.

We claim that such a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor class is big. Since
\delta_{1}\varphi^{*}L+\delta_{2}E_{P}+\delta_{3}E_{S}
is effective, this will finish the proof of the lemma.

Proof of the claim.

Our goal is to show that for
m
sufficiently high and divisible, we have

H^{j}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))=0

for
j=2,3
. Then we have

h^{0}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))\geq\chi(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))),

so the bigness of
Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}
follows from the asymptotic Riemann–Roch theorem and the property
(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})^{3}>0
.

Since
Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}
is
\varphi
-ample, we know by relative Serre vanishing that
R^{1}\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))=0
for sufficiently high and divisible
m
. By cohomology and base change,
cf.

[
Har77
, Theorem II.12.11]
, the direct image sheaf
\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))
is locally free and commutes with base change.

Since the higher direct images vanish, we have

H^{j}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))\simeq H^{j}(S,\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})));

in particular
H^{3}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))=0
.
Moreover, since
\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))
is locally free, Serre duality applies:

H^{2}(S,\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))=H^{0}(S,(\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))^{*}).

Hence the vanishing follows if we show that

H^{0}(A,(\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A})=0.

Since the direct image sheaf has the base-change property, we have

\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A}\simeq(\varphi_{A})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{A}}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})).

Yet
(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})_{A}
is ample, so the direct image sheaf
(\varphi_{A})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{A}}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S}))
is ample for
m\gg 0
by
[
Anc82
, Corollary 2.11, Theorem 3.1.]
. By
[
Laz04b
, Example 6.1.4]
,
this implies that its dual
(\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(Z^{\delta}+\varepsilon E_{S})))^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
does not have global sections.
∎

The next statement is an immediate application of Lemma

and a first step towards Theorem

.

Corollary 4.9

The divisor class

is big. In particular, its push-forward

(\mu_{S})_{*}(D+4E_{S})\equiv 30(\zeta_{S}+1.8\pi^{*}L)
is a big divisor class.

Proof.

We are done if we verify the conditions of Lemma

: using the formulas in Lemma

, we obtain that

. The divisor

is effective and prime, so the exceptional divisors

and

are not in the support of the negative part of its divisorial Zariski decomposition. Finally by Lemma

one has

.∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For simplicity of notation, we will work with the rounded value
1.7952024
.

We argue by contradiction and assume that
\lambda\geq 1.7952024
for all prime divisors in
\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
. Let
Z
be a pseudoeffective divisor class on
Y
such that
(\mu_{S})_{*}Z:=Z_{S}
generates the second extremal ray of the pseudoeffective cone of
\mathbb{P}({}_{S})
. Since
(\mu_{S})_{*}Z
is not big, the divisor class
Z+\varepsilon E_{S}
is not big for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
.

First assume that the negative part the divisorial Zariski decomposition of
Z
contains the exceptional divisor
E_{P}
or
E_{S}
; then we can write
Z=Z^{\prime}+\alpha E_{P}+\beta E_{S}
with
Z^{\prime}
pseudoeffective and
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
. Since

(\mu_{S})_{*}Z\equiv(\mu_{S})_{*}Z^{\prime}+\alpha(\mu_{S})_{*}E_{P}\equiv(\mu_{S})_{*}Z^{\prime}+3\alpha\pi^{*}L

generates an extremal ray, we see that
\alpha=0
. Since
(\mu_{S})_{*}Z=(\mu_{S})_{*}Z^{\prime}
, we can replace
Z
with
Z^{\prime}
and assume without loss of generality that
the negative part of
Z
does not contain
E_{P}
or
E_{S}
.

Since
\mu_{S}
contracts the extremal ray generated by
l_{S}
, we have
Z\cdot l_{S}=0
if and only if
Z=\mu_{S}^{*}Z_{S}
. Since
Z_{S}=(\mu_{S})_{*}Z
generates the second extremal ray of the pseudoeffective cone, we have
Z_{S}\equiv a(\zeta_{S}+\lambda\pi^{*}L)
with
\lambda<3
. In particular,
Z_{S}\cdot R_{S}<0
, so

1.
introduction

Studying the second-order stochastic partial differential equations of the form of (
0.1
) have been regarded as important problems in both mathematics and engineering communities for a long time since these equations naturally appear in filtering problems (cf.
[
28
,
2
]
).

Since Krylov (
[
20
]
) initiated an
L_{p}
-theory of stochastic partial differential equations (
0.1
), there have been lots of articles developing this theory in various directions. For instance, here are some examples of generalizations with respect to coefficients (
[
1
,
16
,
23
,
27
]
), domains (
[
24
,
15
,
17
]
), operators (
[
25
,
30
,
12
]
), nonlinear equations (
[
31
,
32
]
), fractional derivatives (
[
10
,
14
]
), general noises (
[
3
,
18
]
), degenerate equations (
[
5
,
11
]
), and systems (
[
19
,
26
]
).

Surprisingly, to the best of our knowledge, there is no result considering generalization of optimal exponents of moments for solutions and free data even though finite moment conditions play very important roles even in classical probability theories such as central limit theorems, law of large numbers, and etc (cf.
[
4
]
).

If
p=r
, then (
0.2
) is obtained by Krylov (
[
21
]
). For
r<p
, we believe that this paper is the first attempt to obtain the maximal regularity estimate of the type (
0.2
).

It seems to be natural that the moments of solutions are more regular even though solutions are comparatively irregular due to the effect of random noises.
Indeed, as an easy application of our theory, we show that the
r
-th moment of the solution
m^{r}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{r}
is in the parabolic Sobolev space
W_{p/r}^{1,2}\left((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
for
2\leq r\leq p<\infty
(see Definition
2.5
).

We need to mention that our estimates are non-trivial even without stochastic noises. Usually, if there is no stochastic term
g
in (
0.1
), estimates easily come from deterministic theories by solving equations for each random parameter
\omega
.
However, our estimate (
0.2
) cannot be obtained directly from deterministic estimates even though
g=0
(see Theorem
4.4
below).

To obtain our estimate, we use classical harmonic analysis tools such as heat kernel estimates, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal theorem, the Fefferman-Stein theorem, and the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.
These classical tools fit to quasilinear operators. However, since there appear some non-quasilinear operators in order to obtain our estimates, we develop a variant of Marcinkiewicz’s interpolation theorem which could be applied in our setting for non-quasilinear operators (see Lemma
5.18
below).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce stochastic Banach spaces and our main theorem, Theorem
2.3
. Heat kernel estimates are given in Section 3.
A maximal regularity moment estimate for solutions without random noises is given in Section 4. Boundedness of integral operators related to solution representations is given in Section 5. Finally, we prove all our main theorems in Section 6.

We finish the introduction with notation used in the article.

(iii)

We can construct similar initial value problems with stopping times
\tau\leq T
instead of the deterministic time
T
by considering the following extension of the free data

1_{t\leq\tau}f\quad\text{and}\quad 1_{t\leq\tau}g.

(iv)

If we only want to control the
\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
-norm of the solution
u
by
f
and
g
,
then the range of

and
p
can be relaxed to
p,r\in[2,\infty)
, that is, the condition
r\leq p
is not necessary (see Lemma
5.2
and Lemma

below).
In other words, for
p,r\in[2,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N(d,p,r,\kappa,K,T)\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\right).

(v)

Assume that
g=0
in (
2.1
). Then the range of
r
and
p
can be extended to
1<r\leq p<\infty

and the predictable assumption on
\bar{a}^{ij}(t)
can be weaken to the
\mathscr{F}\times\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
-measurable condition (see Theorem
4.4
below).
Similarly to (iv), the restriction that
r\leq p
is not required as well if we merely control the
\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
-norm of the solution
u
.
More precisely, for

(Lemma

below),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N(d,p,r,\kappa,K,T)\left(\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\right).

Moreover, if we choose a data

in a slightly different space, i.e.

f\in L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right),

then the range of

and

can be relaxed to

and

(see Theorem

below).

To state our second main theorem, we need a parabolic version of Sobolev space.

Definition 2.5

(parabolic Sobolev space)
.

By
W_{p}^{1,2}\left((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
with
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote the space of
\mathcal{B}\left((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
-measurable functions

such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|m(t,x)|^{p}dxdt<\infty,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}m(t,x)|^{p}dxdt<\infty,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{xx}m(t,x)|^{p}dxdt:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\sum_{i,j}|m_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)|^{p}dxdt<\infty.

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that the coefficients
\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)
is nonnegative symmetric matrix-valued predictable and satisfies the following ellipticity condition

\displaystyle\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq K|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall(\omega,t,\xi)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Let
2\leq r\leq p<\infty
,
T\in(0,\infty)
,
f\in\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
and

, and

be the solution to (
2.1
) in
\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{2}(T)
.
Then the

-th moment of the solution

is in

.
More precisely, defining

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|m^{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}m^{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{xx}m^{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,r}(T)}\right),

(2.6)

where

3.
Kernel Estimates

In this section, we prove kernel estimates related to deterministic coefficients
a^{ij}(t)
. These estimates might be well-known.
However, we could not find an exact reference which fit to our setting.
Moreover, if we consider random coefficients, then two different types of Hörmander’s condition appear (see Remark
3.4
below).
Thus we give the details of kernel estimates for the completeness of the paper.

Assume that there exists a
d\times d
nonnegative symmetric matrix-valued (non-random) function
a(t)=\left(a^{ij}(t)\right)
on
[0,\infty)
satisfies the following ellipticity condition

\displaystyle\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(t)\leq K|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall(t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}.

For
t>\rho
, we define the following
d\times d
matrices

A_{t\rho}=\int_{\rho}^{t}a(\eta)d\eta,\quad B_{t\rho}=A^{-1}_{t\rho},\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{t\rho}=A^{1/2}_{t\rho}.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\kappa(t-\rho)|\xi|^{2}\leq A_{t\rho}^{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq K(t-\rho)|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall t>\rho,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

\displaystyle K^{-1}(t-\rho)^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\leq B_{t\rho}^{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq\kappa^{-1}(t-\rho)^{-1}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall t>\rho,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

and

\displaystyle\kappa(t-\rho)^{1/2}|\xi|^{2}\leq\sigma_{t\rho}^{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq K(t-\rho)^{1/2}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall t>\rho,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(3.1)

Define

\displaystyle p(t,\rho,x):=1_{\rho<t}(4\pi)^{-d/2}(\det B_{t\rho})^{1/2}\exp(-(B_{t\rho}x,x)/4).

(3.2)

Then

p(t,\rho,x)=1_{\rho<t}(4\pi)^{-d/2}(\det\sigma_{t\rho})^{-1}\exp(-(|\sigma_{t\rho}^{-1}x|^{2}/4)

and by (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\kappa^{d}(t-\rho)^{d/2}\leq|\det\sigma_{t\rho}|\leq K^{d}(t-\rho)^{d/2}

Thus for any
d
-dimensional multi-index
\gamma
, there exist positive constants
N
and
c_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\left|D_{x}^{\gamma}p(t,\rho,x)\right|\leq N\left((t-\rho)^{-d/2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right)\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|x|^{2}\right)\qquad\forall t>\rho,~x\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(3.3)

where
N=N(d,\gamma,\kappa,K)
and
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,\gamma,\kappa)
.

Moreover, by the Fourier transform,

\mathcal{F}\left[D^{\gamma}p(t,\rho,\cdot)\right](\xi)=i^{|\gamma|}\xi^{\gamma}\exp\left(-A^{ij}_{t\rho}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[D_{t}D^{\gamma}p(t,\rho,\cdot)\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=i^{|\gamma|}\xi^{\gamma}a(t)^{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\exp\left(-A^{ij}_{t\rho}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=a^{ij}(t)\mathcal{F}\left[D^{\gamma}p_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,\rho,\cdot)\right](\xi).

Therefore

\displaystyle\left|D_{t}D_{x}^{\gamma}p(t,\rho,x)\right|\leq N\left((t-\rho)^{-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}\right)\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|x|^{2}\right)\qquad\forall t>\rho,~x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(3.4)

Next we introduce cylinders in
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
and obtain Hörmander’s type kernel estimates for
p(t,\rho,x)
based on these cylinders.
For
c>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
, we define the cylinder whose center is
(t_{0},x_{0})
and radius is
c
in
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
as

\displaystyle Q_{c}(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle:=\left(t_{0}-c^{2},t_{0}+c^{2}\right)\times B_{c}(x_{0})

\displaystyle:=\left(t_{0}-c^{2},t_{0}+c^{2}\right)\times\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|x-x_{0}|<c\}.

Note that the kernel
p(t,\rho,x)
is defined on
\mathbf{R}^{d+2}
due to the indicator
1_{\rho<t}
, i.e.

p(t,\rho,x):=(4\pi)^{-d/2}(\det B_{t\rho})^{1/2}\exp(-(B_{t\rho}x,x)/4)\quad\text{if}\quad\rho<t

and

p(t,\rho,x):=0\quad\text{if}\quad t\leq\rho.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a
d
-dimensional multi-index,
r\in[1,\infty)
,
c\in(0,\infty)
,
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
,
(t,x),(s,y)\in Q_{c}(t_{0},x_{0})
, and

\mathcal{A}(\rho,t_{0},x_{0}):=\left\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|\rho-t_{0}|^{1/2}+|z-x_{0}|\geq 8c\right\}.

Assume

\displaystyle r|\gamma|=2.

(3.5)

Then there exists a positive constant
N=N(d,\kappa,K,r,|\gamma|)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\int_{A(\rho,t_{0},x_{0})}|(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(s,\rho,y-z)|dz\right]^{r}d\rho\leq N.

Proof.

We may assume that
t\geq s
without loss of generality.
Moreover we only prove the case
t>s
since the proof of the case
t=s
is simpler.

Set

\displaystyle I_{1}(t_{0},c)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\rho\in\mathbf{R}:|\rho-t_{0}|>(4c)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{\rho\in\mathbf{R}:|\rho-t|>4c^{2}\right\}\bigcap\left\{\rho\in\mathbf{R}:|\rho-s|>4c^{2}\right\}

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle I_{2}(t_{0},c)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\rho\in\mathbf{R}:|\rho-t_{0}|\leq(4c)^{2}\right\}.

Then obviously,

\mathbf{R}=I_{1}(t_{0},c)\cup I_{2}(t_{0},c)

and for all
\rho\in I_{2}(t_{0},c)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\rho,t_{0},x_{0})\subset\left\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|z-x_{0}|\geq 4c\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcap_{\theta\in[0,1]}\left\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|z-(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)|\geq 2c\right\}.

(3.7)

By the fundamental theorem of calculus and the equalities

1_{\rho<t}=1_{\rho<t}1_{\rho<s}+1_{\rho<t}1_{\rho\geq s}=1_{\rho<s}+1_{s\leq\rho<t},

we have

\displaystyle\left|(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(s,\rho,y-z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|1_{\rho<t}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-1_{\rho<s}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(s,\rho,y-z)\right|

\displaystyle=|1_{\rho<s}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(s,\rho,y-z)-1_{\rho<s}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,y-z)+1_{s\leq\rho<t}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,y-z)

\displaystyle\qquad+1_{\rho<t}(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,y-z)-1_{\rho<t}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,x-z)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|1_{\rho<s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left((D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(\theta t+(1-\theta)s,\rho,y-z)\right)d\theta+1_{s\leq\rho<t}(D^{\gamma}_{x}p)(t,\rho,y-z)

\displaystyle\qquad+1_{\rho<t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}\left((D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,\theta x+(1-\theta)y-z)\right)d\theta\bigg|

Moreover, by (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and Fubini’s theorem, the above term is less than or equal to

\displaystyle N1_{\rho<s}\left||t-s||s-\rho|^{-d/2-|\gamma|/2-1}\exp\left(-c_{0}|s-\rho|^{-1}|y-z|^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+N1_{s\leq\rho<t}\left||t-\rho|^{-d/2-|\gamma|/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}|t-\rho|^{-1}|y-z|^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+N1_{\rho<t}\int_{0}^{1}|x-y||t-\rho|^{-d/2-(|\gamma|+1)/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}|t-\rho|^{-1}\left(|\theta x+(1-\theta)y-z|\right)^{2}\right)d\theta.

(3.8)

It is obvious that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t_{0},c)\bigcap\left\{\rho\in\mathbf{R}:t>\rho\geq s\right\}=\emptyset.

Then by (
3.6
), simple change of variables, the Fubini theorem, (
3.8
), and (
3.5
),

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1}}\left[\int_{A(\rho,t_{0},x_{0})}|(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(s,\rho,y-z)|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{1}}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(s,\rho,y-z)|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\int_{4c^{2}}^{\infty}|t-s|^{r}\rho^{-r(|\gamma|/2+1)}+|x-y|^{r}\rho^{-r(|\gamma|+1)/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(|t-s|^{r}c^{-r(|\gamma|+2)+2}+|x-y|^{r}c^{-r(|\gamma|+1)+2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(c^{-r|\gamma|+2}\right)\leq N(d,\kappa,K,r,|\gamma|).

On the other hand, by (
3.7
), (
3.8
), simple change of variables, and th Fubini theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{2}}\left[\int_{A(\rho,t_{0},x_{0})}|(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(t,\rho,x-z)-(D_{x}^{\gamma}p)(s,\rho,y-z)|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N|t-s|^{r}\int_{|\rho-t_{0}|\leq(4c)^{2}}|s-\rho|^{-r(|\gamma|+2)/2}\left[\int_{|z|\geq 4|s-\rho|^{-1/2}c}\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\quad+N\int_{|\rho-t_{0}|\leq(4c)^{2}}|t-\rho|^{-r|\gamma|/2}\left[\int_{|z|\geq 4|t-\rho|^{-1/2}c}\left|\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)\right|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\quad+N|x-y|^{r}\int_{|\rho-t_{0}|\leq(4c)^{2}}|t-\rho|^{-r(|\gamma|+1)/2}\left[\int_{|z|\geq 4|t-\rho|^{-1/2}c}\left|\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)\right|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N|t-s|^{r}c^{-r(|\gamma|+2)+2}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|z|^{(|\gamma|+2)}\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)dz\right]^{r}

\displaystyle\quad+Nc^{-r|\gamma|+2}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|z|^{|\gamma|}\left|\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)\right|dz\right]^{r}

\displaystyle\quad+N|x-y|^{r}c^{-r(|\gamma|+1)+2}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|z|^{|\gamma|+1}\left|\exp\left(-c_{0}|z|^{2}\right)\right|dz\right]^{r}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N(d,\kappa,K,r,|\gamma|).

The lemma is proved.

\Box

4.
A maximal regularity moment estimate for second-order PDEs without stochastic noises

In this section, we handle a random equation without stochastic noises.
We study the following equation

\displaystyle du=\left(\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+f(\omega)\right)dt,\quad t\in(0,T);\quad u(0,\cdot)=0.

(4.1)

Here we present the random parameter
\omega
to emphasize that our coefficients
\bar{a}^{ij}
and free data
f
are random in this section.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
. We introduce nice subspaces of
\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(T)
and
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(T,l_{2})
which will be used in approximations.
We denote by
\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(T)
the space of all stochastic processes
f
such that

f(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}1_{A_{i}}(\omega)1_{B_{i}}(t)f^{i}(x),

where
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
f^{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
A_{i}\in\mathscr{F}
, and
B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}((0,T))
with
|B_{i}|<\infty
.
Moreover, we denote by
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
the space of all stochastic processes
g=(g^{1},g^{2},\ldots)
such that
g^{k}=0
for all large
k
and each
g^{k}
is of the type

g^{k}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{j(k)}1_{(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i}]}(t)g^{ik}(x),

where
j(k)\in\mathbb{N}
,
g^{ik}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and
\tau_{i}
are bounded stopping times with
\tau_{i}\leq T
on the probability space.

Obviously, for all
0<T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq\infty
,
p,r\in(0,\infty]
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(T_{1})\subset\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(T_{2})\subset\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty)\subset\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(\infty)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T_{1},l_{2})\subset\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T_{2},l_{2})\subset\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty,l_{2})\subset\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(\infty,l_{2}).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
,
p,r\in(1,\infty)
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

(i)

\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(T)
is dense in
\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(T)
.

(ii)

\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
is dense in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{p,r}^{\gamma}(T,l_{2})
.

Proof.

It is well-known that
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\infty}_{c}(T,l_{2})
is dense in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}_{p}(T,l_{2})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(see
[
21
, Theorem 3.10]
).
Recall that
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}_{p}(T)
,
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}_{p}(T,l_{2})
,
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}_{p,r}(T)
,
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})
are reflexive Banach spaces
for all
T\in(0,\infty]
,
p,r\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(Remark
2.1
).
Therefore following the idea of the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 3.10]
(an application of Riesz’s theorem),
one can also easily check that
\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(T)
and
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
are dense in
\mathbb{H}_{r,p}^{\gamma}(T)
and
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{r,p}^{\gamma}(T,l_{2})

for all
p,r\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, respectively. The lemma is proved.

\Box

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
r\in(1,\infty)
, and
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Assume that the coefficients
\bar{a}^{ij}(t)
are nonnegative symmetric matrix-valued
\mathscr{F}\times\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
-measurable and satisfy the following ellipticity condition

\displaystyle\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\leq K|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall(\omega,t,\xi)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Then for any
f\in L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)
, there exists a unique solution
u
to equation (
4.1
) such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)}\leq N_{1}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)},

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{xx}\|_{L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)}\leq N_{2}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)},

(4.3)

where
N_{1}=N_{1}(d,p,r,\kappa,K,T)
and
N_{2}=N_{2}(d,p,r,\kappa,K)
.

Proof.

Note that

\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P;L_{r}\left((0,T),\mathcal{B}((0,T));L_{r}\right)\right)}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|f(\omega,t,x)|^{r}dxdt\right)^{p/r}\right]<\infty

and thus

f\in L_{r}([0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{d})

(a.s.)
.
Therefore for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, by the deterministic classical result (cf
[
22
,
13
]
), there exists a unique solution
u(\omega,t,x)
to (
4.1
) such that

\displaystyle u(\omega,t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(\omega,s,x-y)f(\omega,s,y)dyds,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u(\omega,t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{H_{r}^{2}}dt\leq N_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(\omega,t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L_{r}}dt,

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{xx}(\omega,t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L_{r}}dt\leq N_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|f(\omega,t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L_{r}}dt,

(4.6)

where
N_{1}=N_{1}(d,p,r,\kappa,K,T)
and
N_{2}=N_{2}(d,p,r,\kappa,K)
.
Thus taking the

-seminorm to the both sides of (

) and (

\Box

Remark 4.3
.

Let

\displaystyle du=\left(\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+f(\omega)\right)dt,\quad t\in(0,\infty);\quad u(0,\cdot)=0.

(4.7)

Assume that

for all

.
Then by (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u_{xx}(t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L_{r}}dt\right)^{p/r}\right]\leq N_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f(t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L_{r}}dt\right)^{p/r}\right]

(4.8)

since the constant

.

Let

.

(4.9)

and

.

5.
A boundedness of integral operators

There are close relations between boundedness of integral operators and estimates for solutions to (
2.1
) since solutions can be represented by integral operators.
We show the boundedness of many integral operators which play important roles to obtain the maximal regularity moment estimate.

In this section, we handle both the deterministic coefficients
a^{ij}(t)
on
(0,\infty)
and random coefficients
\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)
on
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(t)\leq K|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall(t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}

and

\displaystyle\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)\leq K|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall(\omega,t,\xi)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(5.1)

We assume that
a^{ij}(t)
is nonnegative symmetric matrix-valued
\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
-measurable and
\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)
is
\mathscr{F}\times\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
-measurable.
Recall

p(t,\rho,x)=1_{\rho<t}(4\pi)^{-d/2}(\det B_{t\rho})^{1/2}\exp(-(B_{t\rho}x,x)/4),

where

a(t)=(a^{ij}(t)),\quad A_{t\rho}=\int_{\rho}^{t}a(\eta)d\eta,\quad B_{t\rho}=A^{-1}_{t\rho},\quad\sigma_{t\rho}=A^{1/2}_{t\rho}.

For random coefficients
a^{ij}(\omega,t)
, we similarly define

\displaystyle\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x)=1_{\rho<t}(4\pi)^{-d/2}(\det B_{\omega,t\rho})^{1/2}\exp(-(B_{\omega,t\rho}x,x)/4),

(5.2)

where

\bar{a}(\omega,t)=(\bar{a}^{ij}(\omega,t)),\quad A_{\omega,t\rho}=\int_{\rho}^{t}\bar{a}(\omega,\eta)d\eta,\quad B_{\omega,t\rho}=A^{-1}_{\omega,t\rho},\quad\sigma_{\omega,t\rho}=A^{1/2}_{\omega,t\rho}.

Note that fixing
\omega\in\Omega
, we have kernel estimates for
\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x)
from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
Moreover these kernel estimates hold uniformly for all
\omega\in\Omega
since (
5.1
) holds uniformly for
\omega
.

Next we introduce an integral operator with a random kernel and a stochastic integral operator with a non-random kernel.

For
f\in\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
and
g\in\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})
, we denote

\mathcal{T}f(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x-y)f(\rho,y)dyd\rho

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}f(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,x-y)g^{k}(\rho,y)dydw^{k}_{\rho}.

(5.3)

Remark 5.1
.

Note that for each
\omega,t,x
, the function
\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x)
is not
\mathscr{F}_{\rho}
-adapted (actually it is
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-adapted) even if we give the predictable assumption on
a^{ij}(\omega,t)
.
Thus if we consider the random kernel
\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x-y)
in (
5.3
), then the operator
\mathbb{T}
is not well-defined since
the predictability of the stochastic process
p(\omega,t,\rho,x-y)g^{k}(\rho,y)
is not guaranteed even though
g
is a very nice stochastic process.
In other words, for each
t,\rho,x
, there is no guarantee that
\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x-y)g^{k}(\rho,y)dy
is
\mathscr{F}_{\rho}
-adapted.
Therefore
\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,x-y)g^{k}(\rho,y)dy
is not stochastic integrable.

We show that
\mathcal{T}
is a bounded operator on
\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
for all
r,p\in[1,\infty]
first.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
and
p,r\in[1,\infty]
.
Then there exists a positive constant
N(d,p,\kappa,K,T)
such that

\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\qquad\forall f\in\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T).

Proof.

Since the proof of the case
p=\infty
or
r=\infty
is easier, we only prove the lemma with the additional assumption
p,r<\infty
.
By the generalized Minkowski inequality and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,y)f(\rho,x-y)dyd\rho\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{p/r}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,y)f(\rho,x-y)|^{r}\right]^{1/r}dyd\rho\right|\right]^{p}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigg[\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbb{E}\left[|f(\rho,x-y)|^{r}\right]^{1/r}dyd\rho\bigg|\bigg]^{p}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[|f(t-\rho,x-y)|^{r}\right]^{1/r}dyd\rho\right|\right]^{p}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)dyd\rho\right]^{p}\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}^{p}.

The lemma is proved.

\Box

Similarly
\mathbb{T}
is a bounded operator from
\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p,r}(T,l)
to
\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)
with some restrictions on the range of
p
and
r
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
,
p\in[2,\infty]
, and
r\in[2,\infty)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
N(d,p,\kappa,K,T)
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\quad\forall g\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p,r}(T,l_{2}).

(5.4)

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous lemma, we only prove the more difficult case
p<\infty
.
Let
g\in\tilde{\mathbb{L}}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})
.
By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,y)g^{k}(\rho,x-y)dydw^{k}_{\rho}\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{p/r}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,y)g(\rho,x-y)dy\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}d\rho\right)^{r/2}\right]\right)^{p/r}dxdt

Moreover, by the generalized Minkowski inequality, the Hölder inequality, and the Fubini theorem, the above term is less than or equal to

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,y)g(\rho,x-y)dy\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{2/r}d\rho\right)^{p/2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|p(t,\rho,y)g(\rho,x-y)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}dy\right)^{2}d\rho\right)^{p/2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|p(t,\rho,y)g(\rho,x-y)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}dy\right)^{p}d\rho dxdt.

Applying (
3.3
) and the Minkowski inequality, we show that the above term is less than or equal to

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}1_{0<\rho<t}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g(\rho,x-y)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{p/r}dx\right)^{1/p}dy\Bigg)^{p}d\rho dt

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1_{0<t-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g(t-\rho,x-y)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{p/r}dx\right)^{1/p}dy\Bigg)^{p}dtd\rho.

Finally, Minkowski’s inequality and the following
\rho
-uniform inequality

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)dy\leq N(d,\kappa,K)

imply that the last term in the above inequalities is controlled by

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1_{0<t-\rho}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g(t-\rho,x-y)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{p/r}dxdt\Bigg)^{1/p}dy\Bigg)^{p}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\rho^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}\rho^{-1}|y|^{2}\right)dy\right)^{p}d\rho\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq N\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}.

The lemma is proved.

\Box

Remark 5.4
.

The Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality gives the equivalence of two norms

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,y)g^{k}(\rho,x-y)dydw^{k}\right\|_{L_{r}(\Omega)}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,y)g^{k}(\rho,x-y)dy\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{r}(\Omega)}

for all
r\in(0,\infty)
.
Since the proof of Lemma
5.3
heavily depends on this equivalence, (
5.4
) is not expected if
r=\infty
.
Moreover if
r\in(0,2)
, then we cannot apply the generalized Minkowski inequality for the exponent
r/2
.

Before introducing some singular integral operators related to our maximal moment estimates, we present analytic tools to control singularities first.
BMO (bounded mean oscillation) estimates have been known very important tools to treat singularities of integral operators (cf.
[
6
,
7
,
29
]
).
Thus we briefly review the definition of a BMO space.
For a locally integrable function
h
on
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
, we define the BMO semi-norm of
h
on
\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
as follows:

\|h\|_{BMO}:=\sup_{Q}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q}|h(\rho,z)-h_{Q}|d\rho dz:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|h(\rho,z)-h_{Q}|d\rho dz,

where

h_{Q}:=-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q}f(\rho,z)d\rho dz:=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(r,z)d\rho dz

and the sup is taken over all cylinders
Q
of the type

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle:=Q_{c}(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle:=(t_{0}-c^{2},t_{0}+c^{2})\times B_{c}(x_{0})

\displaystyle:=(t_{0}-c^{2},t_{0}+c^{2})\times\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:|x-x_{0}|<c\},\quad c>0,~(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}.

Moreover, we define the Feffereman-Stein sharp function and Hardy-Littlewood maximal function related to these cylinders.
For
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
, define

h^{\sharp}(t,x)=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|h(\rho,z)-h_{Q}|d\rho dz

and

\mathcal{M}h(t,x)=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|h(\rho,z)|d\rho dz,

the sup is taken over all cylinders
Q
containing
(t,x)
.
It is well-known (
[
29
]
) that for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
L_{p}
-norms of
h
,
h^{\sharp}
, and
\mathcal{M}h
are equivalent. In other words, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exist positive constants
N_{1}(d,p)
,
N_{2}(d,p)
,
N_{3}(d,p)
such that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N_{1}\|h^{\sharp}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N_{2}\|\mathcal{M}h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N_{3}\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}.

(5.5)

Finally we introduce our singular integral operators which are not linear (but sublinear).
For
f\in\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty)
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{r}[f](t,x):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}_{xx}(\omega,t,\rho,z)f(\rho,x-z)dzd\rho\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}

(5.6)

and

\displaystyle G_{r}[f](t,x,s,y):=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigg(

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{xx}(\omega,t,\rho,z)f(\rho,x-z)

\displaystyle-\bar{p}_{xx}(\omega,s,\rho,z)f(\rho,y-z)\bigg)dzd\rho\bigg|^{r}\bigg]\bigg)^{1/r}.

(5.7)

Similarly, for
g\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty,l_{2})
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{r}[g](t,x):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p_{x}(t,\rho,z)g(\rho,x-z)dz\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}d\rho\right)^{r/2}\right]\right)^{1/r}

(5.8)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{r}[g](t,x,s,y):=\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigg(

\displaystyle p_{x}(t,\rho,z)g(\rho,x-z)

\displaystyle-p_{x}(s,\rho,z)g(\rho,y-z)\bigg)dz\bigg|_{l_{2}}^{2}d\rho\bigg|^{r/2}\Bigg]\Bigg)^{1/r}.

(5.9)

We emphasize repeatedly that
\mathcal{G}_{r}
and
G_{r}
are defined with random kernels but
\mathscr{G}_{r}
and
\mathbb{G}_{r}
are defined with deterministic kernels.

Remark 5.5
.

(i)

Let
r\in[1,\infty)
. Then for each
(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, (
5.6
) is well-defined as an iterated integral since

\bar{p}(\omega,t,\cdot,\cdot)
is integrable on
(0,t)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
for each
\omega
and
t
even though the kernel
\bar{p}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(\omega,t,\cdot,\cdot)
is not integrable on
(0,t)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
for all
t
and
\omega
.
Indeed, by the integration by parts, the generalized Minkowski inequality, and (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{r}[f](t,x)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}(\omega,t,\rho,z)f_{xx}(\rho,x-z)dzd\rho\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|z|^{2}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f_{x^{i}x^{j}}(\rho,x-z)\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}dzd\rho

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|z|^{2}\right)dzd\rho\sup_{(\rho,z)\in(0,t)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f_{xx}(\rho,z)\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Similarly, for
r\in[2,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{r}[g](t,x)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,z)g_{x}(\rho,x-z)dz\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}d\rho\right)^{r/2}\right]\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,\rho,z)g_{x}(\rho,x-z)dz\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{2/r}d\rho\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left((t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|z|^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g_{x}(\rho,x-z)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}dz\right)^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(t-\rho)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-c_{0}(t-\rho)^{-1}|z|^{2}\right)dz\right)^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\sup_{(\rho,z)\in(0,t)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|g_{x}(\rho,x-z)\right|_{l_{2}}^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Thus (
5.8
) is well-defined. As shown in the above inequalities (
5.8
) is well-defined even if we assume the kernel is random.
However, if the kernel is random, then the following Itô’s isometry

\displaystyle|\mathscr{G}_{2}[g](t,x)|^{2}\approx\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}_{x}(\omega,t,\rho,z)g(\rho,x-z)dz\right]dw_{\rho}^{k}\right|^{2}\right]

does not hold since
\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}_{x}(\omega,t,\rho,z)g(\rho,x-z)dz
is not itô integrable (cf. Remark
5.1
).

(ii)

For all
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty)
and
(t,x),(s,y)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, applying Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle G_{r}[f_{1}+f_{2}](t,x,s,y)

\displaystyle\leq G_{r}[f_{1}](t,x,s,y)+G_{r}[f_{2}](t,x,s,y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](t,x)+\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](s,y)+G_{r}[f_{2}](t,x,s,y)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](t,x)+\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](s,y)+\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{2}](t,x)+\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{2}](s,y).

Thus (
5.7
) is well-defined.
Similarly, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty,l_{2})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{r}[g_{1}+g_{2}](t,x,s,y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{G}_{r}[g_{1}](t,x,s,y)+\mathbb{G}_{r}[g_{2}](t,x,s,y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{G}_{r}[g_{1}](t,x)+\mathscr{G}_{r}[g_{1}](s,y)+\mathbb{G}_{r}[g_{2}](t,x,s,y)

(5.11)

Hence (

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
r\in(1,\infty)
. Then there exists a continuous extension of
\mathcal{G}_{r}
on
\mathbb{L}_{r}(\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{r}[f]\|_{L_{r}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{r}(\infty)}\qquad\forall f\in\mathbb{L}_{r}(\infty),

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty)
is dense in
\mathbb{L}_{r}(\infty)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}]-\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{2}]\|_{L_{r}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}-f_{2}]\|_{L_{r}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d})}\qquad\forall f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{c}^{\infty}(\infty).

Thus it is sufficient to show (

(

Here is an another method to prove this estimate on the basis of solvability of equation (

Let

is nice enough, it is easy to check that the solution

Lemma 5.10

Let

Let

Step 1
We additionally assume that

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}_{xx}(\omega,t,\rho,z)f_{i}(\rho,x-z)dz\right]d\rho\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{r}\left[f_{1}+f_{2}\right](t,x)-\mathcal{G}_{r}\left[f_{1}+f_{2}\right](s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](t,x)+\mathcal{G}_{r}[f_{1}](s,y)+G_{r}[f_{2}](t,x,s,y).

\displaystyle-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{c}(t_{0},x_{0})}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{c}(t_{0},x_{0})}|\mathcal{G}_{r}\left[f_{1}+f_{2}\right](t,x)-\mathcal{G}_{r}\left[f_{1}+f_{2}\right](s,y)|dtdxdsdy

Step 2
(General case)

\mathbb{L}_{r}(\infty)

5.20

f_{1}^{\varepsilon}

f_{2}^{\varepsilon}

\varepsilon>0

5.20

To handle the boundedness of
\mathcal{G}_{r}
and
\mathscr{G}_{r}
simultaneously, we introduce a Banach space valued operator.
Let
\mathscr{B}
be a Banach space and
\mathbb{L}
be a subspace of
L_{r}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1};\mathscr{B})
. Recall that any operator
\mathcal{L}
from
\mathbb{L}
to
L_{r}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})
is called quasilinear operator if there exists a positive constant
N_{0}
such that for all
c\in\mathbf{R}
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{L}
, we have

\mathcal{L}(cf_{1})=c\mathcal{L}f_{1},

\mathcal{L}(f_{1}+f_{2})\leq N_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}f_{1}+\mathcal{L}f_{2}\right).

Note that both operators
f\mapsto(\mathcal{G}_{r}[f])^{\sharp}
and
g\mapsto(\mathscr{G}_{r}[g])^{\sharp}
are not quasilinear even though

f\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{r}[f]
and
g\mapsto\mathscr{G}_{r}[g]
are.
Thus the classical Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem cannot be applied directly and we need the following variant of the interpolation theorem.
We emphasize that the operator
\mathcal{L}
appearing in the following lemma does not have to be quasilinear.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
1<r\leq p<\infty
,
\mathscr{B}
be a Banach space,
\mathbb{L}
be a subspace of
L_{r}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1};\mathscr{B})
, and

\mathcal{L}
be a bounded operator from
\mathbb{L}
to
L_{r}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})
, i.e.
there exists a positive constant
N
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}f\|_{L_{r}((\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N_{1}\|f\|_{L_{r}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1};\mathscr{B})}

(5.24)

for all
f\in\mathbb{L}
.
Assume that there exists a positive constant
N_{1}
such that for all
(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}
,
\lambda>0
, and
f\in\mathbb{L}
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}(t,x)\leq N_{2}\left(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\lambda}])(t,x)+\|f_{2,\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d};\mathscr{B})}\right),

(5.25)

where

f_{1,\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{\{\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}>\lambda\}}(t,x)

and

f_{2,\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{\{\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\leq\lambda\}}(t,x).

Then there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,N_{1},N_{2})
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}f\|_{L_{p}((\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d};\mathscr{B})}

(5.26)

for all
f\in\mathbb{L}
.

Proof.

If
p=r
, then the result is trivial. So we assume
r<p
.
Let
f\in\mathbb{L}
.
For each
\lambda>0
and
\delta>0
, we decompose
f
into

f_{1,\delta\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{\{\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}>\delta\lambda\}}(t,x)

and

f_{2,\delta\lambda}(t,x)=f(t,x)1_{\{\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\leq\delta\lambda\}}(t,x),

where
\delta>0
will be fixed later.
Let
\lambda\leq(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}(t,x)
.
Then by (
5.25
),

\displaystyle\lambda\leq(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq N_{2}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x)+N_{1}\|f_{2,\delta\lambda}\|_{L_{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d};\mathscr{B})}

\displaystyle\leq N_{2}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x)+N_{2}\delta\lambda.

Fixing
\delta>0
so that
N_{2}\delta<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\lambda\leq 2N_{2}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x).

Thus for each
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle|\{(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}:\lambda\leq(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}(t,x)\}|\leq|\{(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}:\lambda\leq 2N_{2}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x)\}|.

Moreover by (
5.5
) and (
5.24
),

\|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\|\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}]\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\|f_{1,\delta\lambda}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}

for all
\lambda>0
.
Therefore by Fubini’s Theorem and Chebyshev’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}:\lambda\leq(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}(t,x)\}|d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{(t,x)\in\mathbf{R}^{d+1}:\lambda\leq 2N_{2}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x)\}|d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-r-1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d+1}}|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}[f_{1,\delta\lambda}])(t,x)|^{r}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-r-1}\int_{(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\|f_{1,\delta\lambda}(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r}dtdxd\lambda

\displaystyle=N\int_{(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}/\delta}\lambda^{p-r-1}d\lambda\|f(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r}dtdx

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\|f(t,x)\|^{p}_{\mathscr{B}}dtdx.

By (
5.5
) again,

\|\mathcal{L}[f]\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\|(\mathcal{L}[f])^{\sharp}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d+1})},

which completes the proof of the lemma.

\Box

Proof of Theorem

The uniqueness comes from the classical result (see

).
Thus we focus on proving the existence of a solution
u

4.9
) and (
4.10
).
We use the solution representation and boundedness of the operators
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{G}_{r}
.
As mentioned in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, for each
\omega\in\Omega

u(t,x)
to (
4.1
) such that

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{p}(\omega,t,s,x-y)f(s,y)dyds=\mathcal{T}f(t,x),

(6.1)

where
\bar{p}(\omega,t,s,x-y)

4.9

4.10

f

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{xx}(t,x)|^{r}\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bar{p}_{xx}(\omega,t,s,x-y)f(s,y)dy\right]ds\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{1/r}

4.1
,

\displaystyle d(u_{n}-u_{m})=\left(a^{ij}(\omega,t)(u_{n}-u_{m})_{x^{i}x^{j}}+f_{n}-f_{m}\right)dt,\quad t\in(0,T);\quad u(0,\cdot)=0.

4.9
) and (

Proof of Theorem
2.3

Since the uniqueness of a solution
u
easily comes from Theorem
4.4
, we only focus on showing the existence of a solution and estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
The proof of the theorem is similar to that of Theorem
4.4
. However, it should be noticed that the solution representation is impossible if the coefficients
a^{ij}(\omega,t)
are random
since for each
s<t
,
p(\omega,t,s,x-y)g(s,y)
is not
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-adapted (cf. Remark
5.1
and
[
11
, Remark 4.2]
). Thus we need to consider the simplest non-random coefficients
a^{ij}(\omega,t)=\delta^{ij}
first, where
\delta^{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

Step 1
. We assume that
f=0
,
g\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
, and
a^{ij}(\omega,t)=\delta^{ij}
. Then it is well-known (cf. the proof of Theorem 4.2 of
[
21
]
) that the solution
u
to (
2.1
) is given by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)g^{k}(s,y)dydw^{k}_{s}=\mathbb{T}g(t,x).

Moreover, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|u_{xx}(t,x)|^{r}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p_{x}(t,s,x-y)g_{x}^{k}(s,y)dy\right]dw^{k}_{s}\right|^{r}\right]\approx|\mathscr{G}_{r}[g_{x}](t,x)|^{r},

that is, there exists a positive constant
N
such that

\displaystyle N^{-1}|\mathscr{G}_{r}[g_{x}](t,x)|^{r}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}p_{x}(t,s,x-y)g_{x}^{k}(s,y)dy\right]dw^{k}_{s}\right|^{r}\right]\leq N|\mathscr{G}_{r}[g_{x}](t,x)|^{r},

where
N=N(d,r)
.
Thus by Lemma
5.3
and Theorem
5.9
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\leq N_{1}(d,p,T)\left(\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\right)

and

\displaystyle\|u_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\leq N_{2}(d,p,r)\left(\|g_{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\right).

Step 2
. We assume that
f=0
,
g\in\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{p,r}^{1}(T,l_{2})
, and
a^{ij}(\omega,t)=\delta^{ij}
.
Since
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{c}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
is dense in
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}_{p,r}^{1}(T,l_{2})
, the approximation argument in the proof of Theorem
4.4
gives the result.

Step 3
(General case). By Step 2, there exists a unique solution
u^{1}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{2}(T)
to

\displaystyle du^{1}=\left(\Delta u^{1}\right)dt+g^{k}dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T);\quad u^{1}(0,\cdot)=0.

such that

\displaystyle\|u^{1}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N_{1}(d,p,T)\left(\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\right)

(6.2)

and

\displaystyle\|u^{1}_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\leq N_{2}(d,p,r)\left(\|g_{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T,l_{2})}\right).

(6.3)

Moreover by Theorem
4.4
, there exists a unique solution
u^{2}\in\mathbb{H}_{p,r}^{2}(T)
to

\displaystyle du^{2}=\left(a^{ij}(\omega,t)u^{2}_{x^{i}x^{j}}+a^{ij}(\omega,t)u^{1}_{x^{i}x^{j}}-\Delta u^{1}+f\right)dt,\quad t\in(0,T);\quad u^{2}(0,\cdot)=0

such that

\displaystyle\|u^{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}_{p,r}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}(d,p,r,\kappa,K,T)\left(\|a^{ij}(\omega,t)u^{1}_{x^{i}x^{j}}-\Delta u^{1}+f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\left(\|u^{1}_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\right)

(6.4)

and

\displaystyle\|u^{2}_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq N_{2}(d,p,r,\kappa,K)\left(\|a^{ij}(\omega,t)u^{1}_{x^{i}x^{j}}-\Delta u^{1}+f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N_{2}\left(\|u^{1}_{xx}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|f\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}\right).

(6.5)

to (

) satisfying
(
2.4
) and (
2.5
).
The theorem is proved.

Proof of Theorem
2.6

Note that if
p\geq 2
, then the function
|x|^{p}
is twice continuous differentiable.
Thus by Theorem
2.3
and Hölder’s inequality, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|m_{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{xx}m_{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dt

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\left(\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,r}(T)}\right).

Therefore it is sufficient to control

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}m_{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dt.

Let
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Recall that
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is nonnegative function such that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\phi(y)dy=1
and
\phi^{\varepsilon}(y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\phi(y/\varepsilon)
.
Putting
\phi^{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)
in (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=(u_{0},\phi)+\int^{t}_{0}\left[a^{ij}(t)u^{\varepsilon}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)+(f^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\right]ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k}\int^{t}_{0}g^{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot)dw^{k}_{t}\qquad\forall t\in(0,T)~(a.s.),

(6.6)

where
u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\left(u(t,\cdot),\phi^{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\right)
.
Then by Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle|u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}r|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\left(\bar{a}^{ij}(s)u^{\varepsilon}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)+f^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}r|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\left(g^{\varepsilon,k}(s,x)\right)dw^{k}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}|g^{\varepsilon}|_{l_{2}}^{2}(s,x)ds

for all
t\in(0,T)
almost surely.
Moreover, due to (
6.6
) and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{2(r-1)}|g^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|_{l_{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq N\sup_{s\leq t}|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{2(r-2)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U(x)}|g(s,y)|_{l_{2}}^{2}dyds

\displaystyle\leq N(\omega)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U(x)}|g(s,y)|_{l_{2}}^{2}dyds

for all
t\in(0,T)
almost surely, where
U(x)
is a bounded subset of
\mathbf{R}^{d}
depending on
x
.
Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U(x)}|g(s,y)|_{l_{2}}^{2}dyds\right|^{r/2}\right]\leq N\|g\|^{r/2}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r(T,l_{2})}}<\infty,

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}r|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\left(g^{\varepsilon,k}(s,x)\right)dw^{k}_{t}\right]=0.

Thus we have

\displaystyle m^{\varepsilon}_{r}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}|u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}r\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\left(\bar{a}^{ij}(s)u^{\varepsilon}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(s,x)+f^{\varepsilon}(s,x)\right)\right]ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{\varepsilon}(s,x)|^{r-2}|g^{\varepsilon}|_{l_{2}}^{2}(s,x)\right]ds.

Applying the chain rule, Hölder inequality, and Young inequality with any
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m^{\varepsilon}_{r}(t,x)

\displaystyle=r\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{r-2}u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\left(\bar{a}^{ij}(t)u^{\varepsilon}_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)+f^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}r(r-1)\mathbb{E}\left[|u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{p-2}|g^{\varepsilon}|_{l_{2}}^{2}(t,x)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathbb{E}\left[\left(u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+N\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(u_{xx}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)^{r}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)^{r}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(g^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)^{r}\right]\right).

Taking
\delta>0
small enough we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}m_{r}(t,x)|^{p/r}dt\leq N_{1}\left(\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,r}(T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,r}(T)}\right).

Finally taking
\varepsilon\to 0
and applying Fatou’s lemma, we obtain (
2.6
).

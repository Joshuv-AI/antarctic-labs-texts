Kai Zhou

Abstract

Introduction and main results

In the 1920s, George Pólya
[
12
]
and Rolf Nevanlinna
[
10
]
proved some uniqueness theorems for meromorphic functions. For instance, Nevanlinna
[
10
]
obtained the famous five-value theorem which says that two nonconstant meromorphic functions on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
are necessarily equal if they have the same inverse image sets for five distinct points in the Riemann sphere.

In the 1970s, Hirotaka Fujimoto
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
gave some generalizations of the uniqueness results of Pólya
[
12
]
and Nevanlinna
[
10
]
to the case of meromorphic maps into the
n
-dimensional complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
For instance, Fujimoto proved in
[
6
]
the following famous uniqueness theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be meromorphic maps of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
,
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H_{j})

(1\leq j\leq q)
for
q
hyperplanes
H_{1},\dots,H_{q}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position. If
g
is linearly non-degenerate and
q\geq 3n+2,
then
f=g.

Since Fujimoto’s research, there have been lots of papers which deal with the uniqueness problem in higher dimensions, see for example
[
5
,
15
,
9
,
1
,
3
,
13
]
and the references therein. In the uniqueness results of these papers, the target spaces of different holomorphic maps are same. However the uniqueness problem where the identity of target spaces is not a prior assumption is seldom studied. Katsutoshi Yamanoi considered in
[
16
, subsection 3.2]
the uniqueness problem for holomorphic curves into possibly different abelian varieties. In
[
4
]
, Pietro Corvaja and Junjiro Noguchi extended Yamanoi’s result to the case of semi-abelian varieties. A corollary of the main theorem of
[
4
]
is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(see Corollary 1.14 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{C}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
be algebraically non-degenerate holomorphic maps such that
f^{-1}(D_{n})=g^{-1}(D_{N}).
Then
N=n
and there is an isomorphism
\varphi:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
such that
g=\varphi\circ f
and
\varphi(D_{n})=D_{N}.

Here, for an integer
n\geq 1
,
D_{n}
denotes the following hyperplane in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
:

D_{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\,|\,x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=1\}.

The aim of this paper is to extend the uniqueness theorems of Fujimoto to the case where the target spaces are possibly different dimensional complex projective spaces.

One of our main results is the following which extends Fujimoto’s Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem I
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position and
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in general position. Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be meromorphic maps such that
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
,
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H^{\prime}_{j}
and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H^{\prime}_{j})
for any
1\leq j\leq q.
If
g
is linearly non-degenerate and
q\geq N+2n+2,
then
N=n
and there are a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that
f=L(g)
and

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+2.

As a matter of fact, we shall prove in Section
3
an improved version of Theorem
I
, namely Theorem
3.1
.

In
[
6
]
, Fujimoto proved also the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f
,
g
and
H_{1},\dots,H_{q}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. If
n\geq 2,
and
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{n},
and
q\geq 3n+1,
then
f=g.

We shall extend the above theorem of Fujimoto to the following.

Theorem II
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
f
and
g
be as in Theorem
I
. If
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq\min\{n+1,N-n+2\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and

q\geq\max\big\{3n+1,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\},

then
N=n
and there is a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f=L(g)
and
L
satisfies the following condition: either

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+2,

for
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\},
or

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\,\,1\leq i\leq n+1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad L(H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}})=H_{k_{n+3}},\,\,L(H^{\prime}_{k_{n+3}})=H_{k_{n+2}},

for
(n+3)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+3}\in\{1,\dots,q\}.

The above Theorem
II
will be proved in Section
4
. Indeed, we shall prove Theorem
4.1
which slightly improves Theorem
II
.

Remark 1.4
.

For any
(n+3)
hyperplanes
H_{1},\dots,H_{n+3}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

in general position
where
n\geq 2,
one can easily prove that there
does not exist
a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that

L(H_{i})=H_{i},\,\,1\leq i\leq n+1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad L(H_{n+2})=H_{n+3},\,\,L(H_{n+3})=H_{n+2}.

So Theorem
II
recovers Fujimoto’s Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
g:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
be any linearly non-degenerate meromorphic map. Let
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be any hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position. Let
L
be a projective linear transformation of
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
Then
f:=L(g)
is a linearly non-degenerate meromorphic map and
H_{j}:=L(H^{\prime}_{j})
,
1\leq j\leq q,
are hyperplanes in general position. The meromorphic maps
f
and
g
satisfy the condition that

f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H^{\prime}_{j}),\quad 1\leq j\leq q.

The following is a direct corollary of Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
N
and
n
be integers such that
1\leq n\leq N\leq\frac{3}{2}n.
Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
f
and
g
be as in Theorem
I
.

(i)

If
g
is linearly non-degenerate and
q\geq 3n+2,
then the conclusion of Theorem
I
holds.

(ii)

If
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq(N-n+2)
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and
q\geq 3n+1,
then the conclusion of Theorem
II
holds.

Moreover, Fujimoto proved in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
the following deep theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
f
and
g
be meromorphic maps of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
,
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H_{j})

(1\leq j\leq 2n+3)

(2n+3)

We refer the reader to
[

]
for a note on Fujimoto’s proof of the above theorem.

Fujimoto obtained the above Theorem
1.7
after a very deep analysis of the case when the number of hyperplanes is

and

is algebraically non-degenerate.

When trying to extend Theorem
1.7
, we get a partial result, namely, Theorem
6.1
which is also one of the main results of this paper. Let

,

,

,

and

says that the dimensions

and

are necessarily equal if

is algebraically non-degenerate and

So the extension of Theorem

becomes studying the case when there are two families

and

of hyperplanes in a complex projective space

We cannot yet get a satisfactory result and a discussion about this will be given in Section

.

Preliminaries

2.1  Meromorphic maps and hyperplanes in complex projective spaces.

Let
f
be a meromorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
Denote by
I(f)
the indeterminacy locus of
f,
which is an analytic subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
of codimension
\geq 2.
A
reduced representation
of
f
is a
(n+1)
-tuple
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
such that

\{f_{0}=f_{1}=\dots=f_{n}=0\}=I(f)

and

f(z)=[f_{0}(z):\dots:f_{n}(z)]\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus I(f).

Any meromorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has a reduced representation.

We also recall the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A meromorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is said to be
linearly
(resp.
algebraically
)
non-degenerate
, if its image is not contained in any hyperplane (resp. hypersurface) in
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Let

H=\big\{[x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\,|\,a_{0}x_{0}+\dots+a_{n}x_{n}=0\big\}

be a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
where
(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}.
We shall say that the linear form
a_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a_{n}X_{n}
defines the hyperplane
H.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be a family of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
Take a linear form
L_{j}=a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}
that defines
H_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq q.
The family
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
of hyperplanes is said to be
in general position
, if, for any distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
1\leq k\leq n+1,
the linear forms
L_{i_{1}},\dots,L_{i_{k}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}.

Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
and
\{L_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be as above. If
q\geq n+1,
then
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
is in general position if and only if

\det\big(a^{i_{k}}_{0},\dots,a^{i_{k}}_{n};\,1\leq k\leq n+1\big)\neq 0

for any
(n+1)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{n+1}\in\{1,\dots,q\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a meromorphic map and let
H
be a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
By taking a reduced representation
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
of
f
and taking a linear form
a_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a_{n}X_{n}
that defines
H,
we define

(f,H):=a_{0}f_{0}+\dots+a_{n}f_{n},

which is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.
When
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H,
the pullback divisor
f^{*}H
is defined to be the zero divisor of the holomorphic function
(f,H),
which is independent of the different choices of reduced representations of
f
and linear forms that define
H.

2.2  The Borel Lemma.

Following Fujimoto’s method, we shall essentially use the following result which is referred to as the Borel Lemma.

Theorem 2.4

(Borel Lemma, see
[
2
]
and Corollary 4.2 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
h_{1},\dots,h_{q}
be nowhere zero holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
which satisfy the following equation:

c_{1}h_{1}+\dots+c_{q}h_{q}\equiv 0,

where
c_{1},\dots,c_{q}
are nonzero complex numbers. Then, for each
h_{i},
there is some
h_{j}
with
j\neq i
such that
h_{j}/h_{i}
is constant.

We now give some notations.

Notation
.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{*}_{m}
the multiplicative group of all nowhere zero holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.
We regard the set
\mathbb{C}^{*}
of all nonzero complex numbers as a subgroup of
\mathcal{H}^{*}.
For any
h\in\mathcal{H}^{*},
we denote by
[h]
the equivalence class in the quotient group
\mathcal{H}^{*}/\mathbb{C}^{*}
that contains
h.

We emphasize that the quotient group
\mathcal{H}^{*}/\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a
torsion-free
abelian group.

A corollary of the Borel Lemma is the following.

Proposition 2.5

(see Proposition 4.5 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}
be nowhere zero holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and assume they are multiplicatively independent, namely,

\eta_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot\eta_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots\eta_{t}^{n_{t}}\not\in\mathbb{C}^{*}

for any
(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}^{t}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}.
If a polynomial
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})\in\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\dots,X_{t}]
satisfies

P(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})\equiv 0,

then
P(X_{1},\dots,X_{t})
is the zero polynomial.

2.3  Combinatorial lemmas.

Let
G
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G.
We denote by
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
the subgroup of
G
generated by
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}.
Because
G
is torsion-free,
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
is free and is of finite rank. We denote by rank
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\}
the rank of the free abelian group
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle.

The following definition is due to Fujimoto
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let
q\geq r>s\geq 1
be integers. A
q
-tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
of elements in
G
is said to
have the property

(P_{r,s})
if arbitrarily chosen
r
elements
\alpha_{l(1)},\dots,\alpha_{l(r)}
in
A
(
1\leq l(1)<\dots<l(r)\leq q
) satisfy the condition that, for any
s
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in\{1,\dots,r\},
there exist distinct indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
with
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}\neq\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}
such that

\alpha_{l(i_{1})}\cdot\alpha_{l(i_{2})}\cdots\alpha_{l(i_{s})}=\alpha_{l(j_{1})}\cdot\alpha_{l(j_{2})}\cdots\alpha_{l(j_{s})}.

The following lemma is a refinement of Fujimoto’s Lemma 2.6 in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G
that has the property
(P_{r,s}),
where
q\geq r>s\geq 1.
Then there exist
(q-r+2)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{q-r+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{q-r+2}}.

Furthermore, when
r\leq 2s,
the conclusion can be refined as follows:

(i)

if
q\geq 2s-1,
then there exist
q-2(r-s)+2=:l
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{l}};

(ii)

if
q<2s-1,
then there exist
2(q-r+2)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{q-r+2},

j_{1},\dots,j_{q-r+2}
in
\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{q-r+2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{j_{1}}=\alpha_{j_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{j_{q-r+2}}.

Remark 2.8
.

A
q
-tuple
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
has the property
(P_{r,s})
if and only if it has the property
(P_{r,r-s}).
So the condition
r\leq 2s
is not a real restriction.

The proof of Lemma
2.7
is given in Appendix A. Here we give a remark. Following Fujimoto’s proof, we arrange the
\alpha_{i}
’s in a suitable order:

\alpha_{i_{1}}\prec\alpha_{i_{2}}\prec\dots\prec\alpha_{i_{q}}.

We prove that

\alpha_{i_{s}}=\alpha_{i_{s+1}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{q-(r-s)+1}}.

Also we have

\alpha_{i_{r-s}}=\alpha_{i_{r-s+1}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{q-s+1}}.

Then, when
r-s\leq s,
by comparing the two numbers
(q-s+1)
and
s,
the conclusion is naturally divided into two cases. Thus we get the conclusions of Lemma
2.7
.

The next lemma is a refinement of Fujimoto’s Lemma 2.7 in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let
q\geq r>s\geq 1
be integers such that
2\leq r-s\leq s
and
q\geq 2s-1.
Let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G
that has the property
(P_{r,s}).
Set
l:=q-2(r-s)+2.
Then, when
r-s\geq 3,
at least one of the following three cases occurs:

(\alpha)

there exist
(l+1)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{l+1}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{l+1}};

(\beta)

there exist
(l+2)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{l+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{l}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{i_{l+1}}=\alpha_{i_{l+2}};

(\gamma)

there exist an integer
k
with
2\leq k\leq\min\{r-s,2s-r+2\}
and
(l+k)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{l+k}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}=\alpha_{i_{2}}=\dots=\alpha_{i_{l}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{i_{l+1}}\cdot\alpha_{i_{l+2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{l+k}}=\alpha_{i_{1}}^{k}.

When
r-s=2,
one of the cases
(\alpha)
and
(\gamma)
occurs.

The basic idea of the proof of Lemmas
2.7
and
2.9
is essentially same as the idea of Fujimoto’s proof in

, though some new discussions are also needed. We shall give a proof of Lemmas

where

,
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k}\leq s-1,
and the unit element
1
appears
(s+1-k)
times in the right hand side.

We have the following easy observation.

Observation 2.11
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
and
(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\alpha}_{q})
be two

-tuples of elements in a torsion-free abelian group
(G,\cdot).
Assume that there exist indices

and

such that
\alpha_{i_{0}}=\tilde{\alpha}_{j_{0}}=1,
and there is an element
\beta\in G
such that
\alpha_{i}=\beta\tilde{\alpha}_{i}
for any

Then

\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\alpha}_{q}\rangle.

Now let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple that has the property
(P_{r,s})
where

and assume that at least one

equals the unit element

Set

By Lemma

, we may assume that

Put

for each

Consider the new

By Observation

and

Whatever the case, we have

and
t=s-1
implies

The above discussion shows the following result.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group. Let

Let

be a

-tuple of elements in

and assume at least one

of

Then

and

Extension of Theorem
1.1

We give first the following extension of Fujimoto’s Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position and
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in general position. Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be meromorphic maps such that
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
,
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H^{\prime}_{j}
and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H^{\prime}_{j})
for any
1\leq j\leq q.
If
g
is linearly non-degenerate and

q\geq\min\big\{N+2n+2,\max\big\{2N+1,3n+2\big\}\big\},

then
N=n
and there are a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that
f=L(g)
and

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+2.

Proof.

Take reduced representations
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
and
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
of
f
and
g,
respectively. For each
1\leq j\leq q,
take linear forms
a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}
and
b^{j}_{0}Y_{0}+\dots+b^{j}_{N}Y_{N}
that define
H_{j}
and
H^{\prime}_{j},
respectively.

Define, for each
1\leq i\leq q,

h_{i}:=\frac{(f,H_{i})}{(g,H^{\prime}_{i})},

(3.2)

where

(f,H_{i})=a^{i}_{0}f_{0}+\dots+a^{i}_{n}f_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(g,H^{\prime}_{i})=b^{i}_{0}g_{0}+\dots+b^{i}_{N}g_{N}.

By the assumption, we know that each
h_{i}
is a nowhere zero holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.

We note that the condition on
q
implies
q\geq N+n+2.
Choose
(N+n+2)
functions among the
h_{i}
’s arbitrarily, say
h_{1},\dots,h_{N+n+2}.

From definition, we get

a^{i}_{0}f_{0}+\dots+a^{i}_{n}f_{n}-b^{i}_{0}h_{i}g_{0}-\dots-b^{i}_{N}h_{i}g_{N}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq N+n+2.

Eliminating
f_{0},\dots,f_{n},g_{0}\dots,g_{N}
from these equations, we conclude that

\det\big(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{n},b^{i}_{0}h_{i},\dots,b^{i}_{N}h_{i};\,1\leq i\leq N+n+2\big)\equiv 0.

By the Laplace expansion formula, we deduce from the above equation the following identity:

\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{N+1}\leq N+n+2}A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}}\cdot h_{i_{1}}\cdot h_{i_{2}}\cdots h_{i_{N+1}}\equiv 0,

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}}=\pm

\displaystyle\det\big(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{n};\,i\in\{1,\dots,N+n+2\}\setminus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}\}\big)

\displaystyle\cdot\det\big(b^{i}_{0},\dots,b^{i}_{N};\,i\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}\}\big).

Because
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
and
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
are both in general position, the constants
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}}
are all nonzero. Thus by making use of the Borel Lemma (Theorem
2.4
), we deduce from the above identity (
3.3
) that, for any
(N+1)
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}\in\{1,\dots,N+n+2\},
there exist distinct
j_{1},\dots,j_{N+1}\in\{1,\dots,N+n+2\}
with
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{N+1}\}\neq\{i_{1},\dots,i_{N+1}\}
such that

(h_{j_{1}}\cdot h_{j_{2}}\cdots h_{j_{N+1}})/(h_{i_{1}}\cdot h_{i_{2}}\cdots h_{i_{N+1}})\in\mathbb{C}^{*},

which gives (see Section
2
for notations)

[h_{j_{1}}]\cdot[h_{j_{2}}]\cdots[h_{j_{N+1}}]=[h_{i_{1}}]\cdot[h_{i_{2}}]\cdots[h_{i_{N+1}}](\in\mathcal{H}^{*}/\mathbb{C}^{*}).

Using the Definition
2.6
, the above discussion in fact shows that the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
whose elements are in the quotient group
\mathcal{H}^{*}/\mathbb{C}^{*}
has the property
(P_{N+n+2,N+1}).

Then by Lemma
2.7
and using the condition on
q,
we get that there are
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

[h_{k_{1}}]=[h_{k_{2}}]=\dots=[h_{k_{n+2}}].

In fact, if
q\geq N+2n+2
then

q-(N+n+2)+2\geq n+2

and the above conclusion follows from Lemma
2.7
. If
q\geq\max\{2N+1,3n+2\},
then

q-2(n+1)+2\geq n+2

and the above conclusion follows from the refined conclusion (i) of Lemma
2.7
.

By choosing a new reduced representation of
f
if necessary, we may assume that
h_{k_{1}}=:c_{1}
,
h_{k_{2}}=:c_{2}
,
\dots
,
h_{k_{n+2}}=:c_{n+2}
are all constants.

In the following, if there is no confusion, we shall denote by
f
,
g
,
H_{i}
and
H^{\prime}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq q
) the row vectors
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
,
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
,
(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{n})
and
(b^{i}_{0},\dots,b^{i}_{N})
(
1\leq i\leq q
), respectively. Then, for any
1\leq i\leq q,

(f,H_{i})
equals the inner product
\langle f,H_{i}\rangle,
and
(g,H^{\prime}_{i})
equals
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle.

Since
H_{k_{1}},H_{k_{2}},\dots,H_{k_{n+2}}
are hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position, we can write

H_{k_{n+2}}=x_{1}H_{k_{1}}+x_{2}H_{k_{2}}+\dots+x_{n+1}H_{k_{n+1}},

where
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n+1}
are nonzero constants.

We note that
\langle f,H_{k_{i}}\rangle=c_{i}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}\rangle
for any
1\leq i\leq n+2.
So

\langle f,H_{k_{n+2}}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}\langle f,H_{k_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}c_{i}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}\rangle=\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}\rangle.

Thus

c_{n+2}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}}\rangle=\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}\rangle.

It then follows that

\langle g,c_{n+2}H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}\rangle=0.

By the linear non-degeneracy of
g,
we conclude that

c_{n+2}H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}=0.

(3.4)

The equation (
3.4
) shows that the
(n+2)
vectors
H^{\prime}_{k_{1}}
,
H^{\prime}_{k_{2}}
,
\dots
,
H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{C}.
If
N>n,
then
n+2\leq N+1
and (
3.4
) gives a contradiction because
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}
are hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in general position. So it necessarily holds that
N=n.

Now the matrices

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H_{k_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
H_{k_{n+1}}\end{array}\right)=:A\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H^{\prime}_{k_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
H^{\prime}_{k_{n+1}}\end{array}\right)=:B

are both invertible square matrices of order
(n+1).

Denote by
C
the diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(c_{1},\dots,c_{n+1}).
Then we have

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{0}\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}\end{array}\right)=A^{-1}CB\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{0}\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}\end{array}\right).

Put
\tilde{L}:=A^{-1}CB.
We write the homogeneous coordinate of a point in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
as a column vector. Then the correspondence

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w_{0}\\
\vdots\\
w_{n}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\tilde{L}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w_{0}\\
\vdots\\
w_{n}\end{array}\right)

defines a projective linear transformation of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which is denoted by
L.
We see that
f=L(g).

For any
1\leq i\leq n+1,
by

H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}B^{-1}=(0,\dots,0,\underset{\underset{i-{\rm th}}{\uparrow}}{1},0,\dots,0),

we deduce easily that
L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}}.

Because the
H^{\prime}_{j}
’s are hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position, we can write

H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}}=y_{1}H^{\prime}_{k_{1}}+y_{2}H^{\prime}_{k_{2}}+\dots+y_{n+1}H^{\prime}_{k_{n+1}},

where
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n+1}
are nonzero constants. Substituting this into (
3.4
), we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(c_{n+2}y_{i}-x_{i}c_{i})H^{\prime}_{k_{i}}=0,

which implies that

c_{i}=\frac{y_{i}}{x_{i}}\cdot c_{n+2},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+1.

Then easy computation tells us that
L(H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}})=H_{k_{n+2}}.
This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Next we shall reduce the number of hyperplanes in the condition of Theorem

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,

and
g
be as in Theorem

. If

is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and

q\geq\max\big\{3n+2,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\},

then the conclusion of Theorem

holds.

Proof.

Let
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
,
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
,
a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}
and
b^{j}_{0}Y_{0}+\dots+b^{j}_{N}Y_{N}
(
1\leq j\leq q
) be as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For each
1\leq i\leq q,
let
h_{i}
be defined as (
3.2
). Then each
h_{i}
is a nowhere zero holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.

Since
N+3n/2+3/2\geq N+n+2,
the assumption on
q
implies that
q\geq N+n+2.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we know that the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
has the property
(P_{N+n+2,N+1}).

Consider first the case when
q\geq 2N+1.
Since
q-2(n+1)+2\geq n+2,
the refined conclusion (i) of Lemma
2.7
tells us that there are
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

[h_{k_{1}}]=[h_{k_{2}}]=\dots=[h_{k_{n+2}}].

Then the same discussion as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
holds.

So, it suffices to prove next that the case of
q<2N+1
cannot happen.
Assume
q<2N+1.
We shall show a contradiction.

By the refined conclusion (ii) of Lemma
2.7
, we get there are
2(q-N-n)
distinct indices
i_{0},\dots,i_{q-N-n-1},j_{0},\dots,j_{q-N-n-1}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
such that

[h_{i_{0}}]=[h_{i_{1}}]=\dots=[h_{i_{q-N-n-1}}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad[h_{j_{0}}]=[h_{j_{1}}]=\dots=[h_{j_{q-N-n-1}}].

(3.6)

By
q\geq N+3n/2+3/2,
we have

2(q-N-n-1)\geq n+1.

Let
l
be the largest integer that does not exceed
(n+1)/2,
and set
\tilde{l}:=n+1-l.
One checks easily that

1\leq l\leq\tilde{l}\leq q-N-n-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad l+\tilde{l}=n+1.

From (
3.6
), we know that
h_{i_{k}}/h_{i_{0}}=:c_{k}\,(1\leq k\leq l)
and
h_{j_{k}}/h_{j_{0}}=:\tilde{c}_{k}\,(1\leq k\leq\tilde{l})
are all nonzero constants.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, sometimes we shall use the symbols
f,g
,
H_{j},H^{\prime}_{j}\,(1\leq j\leq q)
to denote the corresponding row vectors.

Since
\{H_{j}\}
are hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position, we can write

H_{i_{0}}=x_{1}H_{i_{1}}+\dots+x_{l}H_{i_{l}}+x_{l+1}H_{j_{1}}+\dots+x_{n+1}H_{j_{\tilde{l}}}

and

H_{j_{0}}=\tilde{x}_{1}H_{i_{1}}+\dots+\tilde{x}_{l}H_{i_{l}}+\tilde{x}_{l+1}H_{j_{1}}+\dots+\tilde{x}_{n+1}H_{j_{\tilde{l}}},

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1},\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n+1}
are nonzero constants.

We then have

\langle f,H_{i_{0}}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}\langle f,H_{i_{k}}\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\langle f,H_{j_{k}}\rangle.

So

\displaystyle\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}\rangle h_{i_{0}}=\langle f,H_{i_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}h_{i_{0}}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}h_{j_{0}}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle h_{i_{0}}+\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle h_{j_{0}},

which implies

\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle h_{i_{0}}=\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle h_{j_{0}}.

Similarly, we have

\langle g,H^{\prime}_{j_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}\tilde{x}_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle h_{j_{0}}=\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{l}\tilde{x}_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle h_{i_{0}}.

From the above two equations, we conclude

\displaystyle\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle\cdot\langle g,H^{\prime}_{j_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}\tilde{x}_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle\cdot\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{l}\tilde{x}_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle.

(3.7)

We note that the vectors

H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}},\quad H^{\prime}_{j_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}\tilde{x}_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{l}\tilde{x}_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}

are all nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}.

Because both the left and right hand sides of (
3
) can be written as a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2 in
g_{0},\dots,g_{N},
we conclude by the non-degeneracy condition of
g
that (
3
) holds as an identity of polynomials in
g_{0},\dots,g_{N}.
This tells us that the linear polynomial
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle
is a constant multiple of
\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}\rangle,
or is a constant multiple of
\langle g,\sum_{k=1}^{l}\tilde{x}_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}\rangle.
The latter implies that
H^{\prime}_{i_{0}},H^{\prime}_{i_{1}},\dots,H^{\prime}_{i_{l}}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{C},
which contradicts the “in general position” assumption of
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}.
So it is necessary that
H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}
is a constant multiple of
\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}},
namely, there is a nonzero constant

such that

H^{\prime}_{i_{0}}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}x_{k}c_{k}H^{\prime}_{i_{k}}-c\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{l}}x_{l+k}\tilde{c}_{k}H^{\prime}_{j_{k}}=0.

If
N>n,
then
n+2\leq N+1
and the
(n+2)
vectors
H^{\prime}_{i_{0}},H^{\prime}_{i_{1}},\dots,H^{\prime}_{i_{l}}
,
H^{\prime}_{j_{1}},\dots,H^{\prime}_{j_{\tilde{l}}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C},
which contradicts the above equation. Hence

Thus we get

q\geq N+n+2=2N+2,

which contradicts our assumption that
q<2N+1.
This shows the case of
q<2N+1
cannot happen and the proof is thus finished.

We shall prove in this section the following theorem which extends Fujimoto’s Theorem

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
f:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be as in Theorem
3.1
. If
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq\min\{n+1,N-n+2\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
and

q\geq\max\big\{3n+1,\min\big\{2N+1,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\}\big\},

then
N=n
and there is a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f=L(g)
and
L
satisfies the following condition: either

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+2,

for
(n+2)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+2}\in\{1,\dots,q\},
or

L(H^{\prime}_{k_{i}})=H_{k_{i}},\,\,1\leq i\leq n+1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad L(H^{\prime}_{k_{n+2}})=H_{k_{n+3}},\,\,L(H^{\prime}_{k_{n+3}})=H_{k_{n+2}},

for
(n+3)
distinct indices
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+3}\in\{1,\dots,q\}.

Remark 4.2
.

For positive integers
N
and
n
with
N\geq n,
we set

\alpha(n,N):=\max\big\{3n+1,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\}

and

\beta(n,N):=\max\big\{3n+1,\min\big\{2N+1,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\}\big\}.

To compare
\alpha(n,N)
and
\beta(n,N),
we consider the following three cases.

•

N\geq\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{1}{2}.
In this case,
\beta(n,N)=\alpha(n,N).

•

N\leq\frac{3}{2}n-\frac{1}{2}.
In this case,
\beta(n,N)=\alpha(n,N)=3n+1.

•

\frac{3}{2}n-\frac{1}{2}<N<\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{1}{2}.
In this case, it is necessary that
n
is even and
N=\frac{3}{2}n.
Thus
\beta(n,N)=3n+1
and
\alpha(n,N)=3n+\frac{3}{2}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
,
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
,
a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}
and
b^{j}_{0}Y_{0}+\dots+b^{j}_{N}Y_{N}
(
1\leq j\leq q
) be as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For each
1\leq i\leq q,
define
h_{i}
as (
3.2
). Then each
h_{i}
is a nowhere zero holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.

One checks easily that the assumption on
q
implies
q\geq N+n+2.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we know that the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
has the property
(P_{N+n+2,N+1}).

By assumption, we have

q\geq\min\big\{2N+1,N+\frac{3}{2}n+\frac{3}{2}\big\}.

If
q\geq N+3n/2+3/2,
then the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
tells us that
q\geq 2N+1,
because
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}.
So we always have

q\geq 2N+1.

Then by making use of Lemma
2.9
and using the condition that
q\geq 3n+1,
we conclude that at least one of the following three cases occurs: (we renumber the indices for simplicity)

(\alpha)

[h_{1}]=[h_{2}]=\dots=[h_{n+2}];

(\beta)

[h_{1}]=[h_{2}]=\dots=[h_{n+1}]
and
[h_{n+2}]=[h_{n+3}];

(\gamma)

[h_{1}]=[h_{2}]=\dots=[h_{n+1}]
and
[h_{n+2}]\cdot[h_{n+3}]\cdots[h_{n+1+k}]=[h_{1}]^{k},
where
k
is a positive integer such that
2\leq k\leq\min\{n+1,N-n+2\}.

If case (
\alpha
) occurs, then the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
tells us that
N=n
and there is a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f=L(g)
and
L(H^{\prime}_{i})=H_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n+2.

Now assume case (
\beta
) occurs. By choosing a new reduced representation of
f,
we assume that
h_{1}=:c_{1}
,
h_{2}=:c_{2}
,
\dots
,
h_{n+1}=:c_{n+1}
are all constants. We also know that
h_{n+2}=c_{n+2}h_{n+3}
for a nonzero constant
c_{n+2}.

In the following, we sometimes use the symbols
f,g
,
H_{j},H^{\prime}_{j}\,(1\leq j\leq q)
to denote the corresponding row vectors as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Since the
\{H_{j}\}
are hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position, we can write

H_{n+2}=x_{n+2,1}H_{1}+x_{n+2,2}H_{2}+\dots+x_{n+2,n+1}H_{n+1}

and

H_{n+3}=x_{n+3,1}H_{1}+x_{n+3,2}H_{2}+\dots+x_{n+3,n+1}H_{n+1},

where
x_{n+2,i}
and
x_{n+3,i}
(
1\leq i\leq n+1
) are nonzero constants.

Since
\langle f,H_{i}\rangle=c_{i}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle
(
1\leq i\leq n+1
), we have

\langle f,H_{n+2}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}\langle f,H_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle.

Similarly, we have

\langle f,H_{n+3}\rangle=\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+3,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle.

We note that both
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+3,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}
are nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}.

Substituting the above two equalities into the equation
h_{n+2}=c_{n+2}h_{n+3},
we deduce that

\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\cdot\langle g,H^{\prime}_{n+3}\rangle=c_{n+2}\cdot\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+3,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle\cdot\langle g,H^{\prime}_{n+2}\rangle.

Because
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq 2,
the above equation holds as an identity of polynomials in
g_{0},\dots,g_{N}.
Since the linear polynomial
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{n+2}\rangle
cannot be a constant multiple of
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{n+3}\rangle,
we conclude that
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{n+2}\rangle
is a constant multiple of
\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle.
This means that there is a nonzero constant
c
such that

H^{\prime}_{n+2}-c\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{n+2,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}=0,

which implies that the
(n+2)
vectors
H^{\prime}_{1},\dots,H^{\prime}_{n+2}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{C}.
The same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows
N=n.

Now using the same method as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we conclude that there is a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
f=L(g)
and
L(H^{\prime}_{i})=H_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n+2.

Finally, we assume case (
\gamma
) occurs. By choosing a new reduced representation of
f,
we assume that
h_{1}=:c_{1}
,
h_{2}=:c_{2}
,
\dots
,
h_{n+1}=:c_{n+1}
are all constants and

h_{n+2}\cdot h_{n+3}\cdots h_{n+1+k}=:c_{n+2}

is also a constant.

As before, we can write

H_{j}=x_{j,1}H_{1}+x_{j,2}H_{2}+\dots+x_{j,n+1}H_{n+1},\quad n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k,

where
x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,n+1}
are nonzero constants.

Since
\langle f,H_{i}\rangle=c_{i}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle
(
1\leq i\leq n+1
), we have

\langle f,H_{j}\rangle=\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{j,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle,\quad n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k.

We note that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{j,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}
(
n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k
) are all nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}.

Substituting these equalities into the equation
h_{n+2}\cdot h_{n+3}\cdots h_{n+1+k}=c_{n+2},
we conclude that

\prod_{j=n+2}^{n+1+k}\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{j,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle=c_{n+2}\cdot\prod_{j=n+2}^{n+1+k}\langle g,H^{\prime}_{j}\rangle.

Because
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})
is not contained in any hypersurface of degree
\leq\min\{n+1,N-n+2\},
the above equation holds as an identity of polynomials in
g_{0},\dots,g_{N}.
Then we see that there is a bijection
\sigma:\{n+2,\dots,n+1+k\}\to\{n+2,\dots,n+1+k\}
such that the linear polynomial
\langle g,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{j,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}\rangle
is a constant multiple of
\langle g,H^{\prime}_{\sigma(j)}\rangle
for any
n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k.
This means that there are nonzero constants
\tilde{c}_{j}\,(n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k)
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{j,i}c_{i}H^{\prime}_{i}-\tilde{c}_{j}H^{\prime}_{\sigma(j)}=0,\quad n+2\leq j\leq n+1+k.

(4.3)

The case of
j=n+2
implies that the
(n+2)
vectors
H^{\prime}_{1},\dots,H^{\prime}_{n+1},H^{\prime}_{\sigma(n+2)}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{C}.
The same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows
N=n.

Then we get
k=2
and the above
\sigma
is a bijection from
\{n+2,n+3\}
to
\{n+2,n+3\}.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we write the homogeneous coordinate of a point in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
as a column vector and let
\tilde{L}=A^{-1}CB,
where

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H_{1}\\
\vdots\\
H_{n+1}\end{array}\right),\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}H^{\prime}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
H^{\prime}_{n+1}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad C={\rm diag}(c_{1},\dots,c_{n+1}).

The matrix
\tilde{L}
defines a projective linear transformation
L
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and this
L
satisfies the condition that
f=L(g)
and
L(H^{\prime}_{i})=H_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n+1.

We write

H^{\prime}_{j}=y_{j,1}H^{\prime}_{1}+y_{j,2}H^{\prime}_{2}+\dots+y_{j,n+1}H^{\prime}_{n+1},\quad n+2\leq j\leq n+3,

where
y_{j,1},\dots,y_{j,n+1}
are nonzero constants.

We conclude from (
4.3
) that, for any
j\in\{n+2,n+3\},

c_{i}=\tilde{c}_{j}\cdot\frac{y_{\sigma(j),i}}{x_{j,i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n+1.

Then direct computation shows
L(H^{\prime}_{\sigma(j)})=H_{j}
for any
j\in\{n+2,n+3\}.

If
\sigma
is the identity map, then we see that
L(H^{\prime}_{i})=H_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n+3.
If
\sigma
is not the identity map, then we see that

L(H^{\prime}_{i})=H_{i},\,\,1\leq i\leq n+1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad L(H^{\prime}_{n+2})=H_{n+3},\,\,L(H^{\prime}_{n+3})=H_{n+2}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

By putting

and

we get from Theorem

the following result concerning meromorphic functions.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{4}
be two families of distinct points in the Riemann sphere. If two nonconstant meromorphic functions

and

on

satisfy the condition that

then there is a Möbius transformation

such that

and

satisfies the following condition: either

L(b_{k_{1}})=a_{k_{1}},L(b_{k_{2}})=a_{k_{2}},L(b_{k_{3}})=a_{k_{3}}

for distinct

or

L(b_{k_{1}})=a_{k_{1}},L(b_{k_{2}})=a_{k_{2}},L(b_{k_{3}})=a_{k_{4}},L(b_{k_{4}})=a_{k_{3}}

for a permutation
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})
of

5
The Zariski closure of the image of
f\times g
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}

This section is an extension of
[
7
, Section 5]
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position and
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in general position with

q\geq N+n+2.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be meromorphic maps such that
f(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H_{j}
,
g(\mathbb{C}^{m})\not\subseteq H^{\prime}_{j}
and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H^{\prime}_{j})
for any
1\leq j\leq q.

Let
f\times g
be the holomorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big)
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
that is given by
(f\times g)(z)=(f(z),g(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big),
where
I(f)
and
I(g)
are the indeterminacy loci of
f
and
g,
respectively.

Definition 5.1
.

We define
V_{f\times g}
to be the Zariski closure of the image of
f\times g
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N},
namely, the intersection of all algebraic sets in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
that contain the image of
f\times g.

One sees easily the following.

Proposition 5.2
.

The algebraic set
V_{f\times g}
is irreducible.

Proof.

Assume
V_{f\times g}
is reducible. This means that there are algebraic sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
V_{1}\subsetneqq V_{f\times g}
and
V_{2}\subsetneqq V_{f\times g}
such that
V_{f\times g}=V_{1}\cup V_{2}.
Then
(f\times g)^{-1}(V_{1})=:A_{1}
and
(f\times g)^{-1}(V_{2})=:A_{2}
are both analytic subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big)
and
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big)=A_{1}\cup A_{2}.
The definition of
V_{f\times g}
implies that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are both proper analytic subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big).
This gives a contradiction because
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big)
is irreducible. Thus
V_{f\times g}
is necessarily irreducible.
∎

Let
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
,
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
, and
a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}
,
b^{j}_{0}Y_{0}+\dots+b^{j}_{N}Y_{N}
(
1\leq j\leq q
) be as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For each
1\leq i\leq q,
let
h_{i}
be defined as (
3.2
). Then each
h_{i}
is a nowhere zero holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}.
By choosing a new reduced representation of
f
if necessary, we may assume at least one
h_{i}
is constant.

Consider the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
of elements in
\mathcal{H}^{*}/\mathbb{C}^{*}.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
has the property
(P_{N+n+2,N+1}).
We define

t:={\rm rank}\big\{[h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}]\big\}.

Remark 5.3
.

The number
t
is independent of the different choices of reduced representations of
f
and
g
and linear forms that define hyperplanes
H_{j}
and
H^{\prime}_{j},
as long as at least one
h_{i}
is constant.

By Lemma
2.10
(i) (or Lemma
2.12
), we have the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

0\leq t\leq N.

Now we give the following main theorem of this section. Let notations be as above.

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose the
q
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}])
does not have the property
(P_{2s-(N-n),s})
for some positive integer
s
with
N-n+1\leq s\leq N+1.
Then

\max\{t,\dim V_{f\times g}\}\leq N-s+t.

Proof.

Clearly we have
N-n+1\leq s\leq N
and
t\geq 1.

Take
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}
in
\mathcal{H}^{*}
such that
\{[\eta_{1}],\dots,[\eta_{t}]\}
is a basis of the free abelian group
\langle[h_{1}],\dots,[h_{q}]\rangle.
Then the functions
h_{i}
are represented as follows:

h_{i}=c_{i}\cdot\eta_{1}^{l_{i,1}}\cdots\eta_{t}^{l_{i,t}},\quad 1\leq i\leq q,

(5.6)

where
l_{i,1},\dots,l_{i,t}
are integers and
c_{i}
are nonzero constants.

By assumption, there are
h_{i_{1}},\dots,h_{i_{2s-(N-n)}}
such that the
(2s-(N-n))
-tuple
([h_{i_{1}}],\dots,[h_{i_{2s-(N-n)}}])
does not have the property
(P_{2s-(N-n),s}),
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{2s-(N-n)}
are distinct indices in
\{1,\dots,q\}.
Without loss of generality, we assume the
(2s-(N-n))
-tuple
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{2s-(N-n)}])
does not have the property
(P_{2s-(N-n),s}).

Put
s_{1}:=s-(N-n).
We list the following equalities and inequalities for readers’ convenience:

s_{1}+s=2s-(N-n),\quad s_{1}+(N+1-s)=n+1,

1\leq s_{1}\leq n,\quad 1\leq N+1-s\leq n,\quad(2s-(N-n))+(N+1-s)=s+n+1.

By making a transformation of homogenous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{n},
we may assume that

(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{n})=(0,\dots,0,\underset{i-{\rm th}}{\underset{\uparrow}{1}},0,\dots,0),\quad 1\leq i\leq s_{1},

and

(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{n})=(0,\dots,0,\underset{(i-s)-{\rm th}}{\underset{\uparrow}{1}},0,\dots,0),\quad 2s-(N-n)+1\leq i\leq s+n+1.

By making a transformation of homogenous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{N},
we may assume that

(b^{i}_{0},\dots,b^{i}_{N})=(0,\dots,0,\underset{i-{\rm th}}{\underset{\uparrow}{1}},0,\dots,0),\quad 1\leq i\leq s,

and

(b^{i}_{0},\dots,b^{i}_{N})=(0,\dots,0,\underset{(i-s_{1})-{\rm th}}{\underset{\uparrow}{1}},0,\dots,0),\quad 2s-(N-n)+1\leq i\leq s+n+1.

After these two transformations, we know that

\det\big(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{s_{1}-1};\,i=i_{1},\dots,i_{s_{1}}\big)\neq 0

for any
s_{1}
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{s_{1}}\in\{1,\dots,2s-(N-n)\},
and

\det\big(b^{i}_{0},\dots,b^{i}_{s-1};\,i=j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\big)\neq 0

for any
s
distinct indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,2s-(N-n)\}.
We also know that the functions
f_{0},\dots,f_{n}
,
g_{0},\dots,g_{N}
are all not identically zero.

Now we claim that

\det\big(a^{i}_{0},\dots,a^{i}_{s_{1}-1},b^{i}_{0}h_{i},\dots,b^{i}_{s-1}h_{i};\,i=1,\dots,2s-(N-n)\big)\not\equiv 0.

(5.7)

In fact, if the left hand side of (
5.7
) is identically zero, then using the Laplace expansion formula and the Borel Lemma as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we conclude
([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{2s-(N-n)}])
has the property
(P_{2s-(N-n),s}),
which contradicts the assumption. Thus we have (
5.7
).

From the definition of
h_{i},
we get the following equations:

a^{i}_{0}f_{0}+\dots+a^{i}_{n}f_{n}-b^{i}_{0}h_{i}g_{0}-\dots-b^{i}_{N}h_{i}g_{N}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq 2s-(N-n),

and

f_{i-s-1}=h_{i}g_{i-s_{1}-1},\quad 2s-(N-n)+1\leq i\leq s+n+1.

(5.8)

Substituting (
5.8
) into the first
(2s-(N-n))
equations, we get

a^{i}_{0}f_{0}+\dots+a^{i}_{s_{1}-1}f_{s_{1}-1}-b^{i}_{0}h_{i}g_{0}-\dots-b^{i}_{s-1}h_{i}g_{s-1}=B_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq 2s-(N-n),

(5.9)

where

B_{i}=(b^{i}_{s}h_{i}-a^{i}_{s_{1}}h_{2s-(N-n)+1})g_{s}+\dots+(b^{i}_{N}h_{i}-a^{i}_{n}h_{s+n+1})g_{N}.

By (
5.7
), we can apply the Cramer’s rule to obtain the following expressions of
f_{j}\,(0\leq j\leq s_{1}-1)
and
g_{j}\,(0\leq j\leq s-1)
:

f_{j}=\frac{\tilde{P}_{j,s}(h_{1},\dots,h_{s+n+1})g_{s}+\dots+\tilde{P}_{j,N}(h_{1},\dots,h_{s+n+1})g_{N}}{\tilde{P}(h_{1},\dots,h_{2s-(N-n)})},\quad 0\leq j\leq s_{1}-1,

g_{j}=\frac{\tilde{Q}_{j,s}(h_{1},\dots,h_{s+n+1})g_{s}+\dots+\tilde{Q}_{j,N}(h_{1},\dots,h_{s+n+1})g_{N}}{\tilde{P}(h_{1},\dots,h_{2s-(N-n)})},\quad 0\leq j\leq s-1,

where
\tilde{P}
,
\tilde{P}_{j,s},\dots,\tilde{P}_{j,N}
,
\tilde{Q}_{j,s},\dots,\tilde{Q}_{j,N}
are polynomials with constant coefficients and
\tilde{P}
is not zero.

Combining the above expressions with (
5.8
), we get expressions of
f_{j}/f_{n}.
Then by substituting (
5.6
) into these expressions and also the above expressions of
g_{j},
we get the following:

\frac{f_{j}}{f_{n}}=\frac{P_{j,s}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{s}+\dots+P_{j,N}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{N}}{P_{0}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{N}},\quad 0\leq j\leq s_{1}-1,

(5.10)

g_{j}=\frac{Q_{j,s}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{s}+\dots+Q_{j,N}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{N}}{P(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})},\quad 0\leq j\leq s-1,

(5.11)

\frac{f_{j}}{f_{n}}=\frac{R_{j}^{+}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{j+N-n}}{R_{j}^{-}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})g_{N}},\quad s_{1}\leq j\leq n-1,

(5.12)

where
P_{0},P\in\mathbb{C}[z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{t}]
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
d\geq 1,
and each of the polynomials
P_{j,s},\dots,P_{j,N}
,
Q_{j,s},\dots,Q_{j,N}
is either zero or a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d,
and
R_{j}^{+}
and
R_{j}^{-}
are monomials with
\deg R_{j}^{+}=\deg R_{j}^{-}.

Now let
\eta
be the holomorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
into
\mathbb{P}^{t}
that is given by
\eta(z)=[1:\eta_{1}(z):\dots:\eta_{t}(z)]
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{m},
and let
f\times g\times\eta
be the holomorphic map of
\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big)
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}
that is given by
(f\times g\times\eta)(z)=(f(z),g(z),\eta(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\big(I(f)\cup I(g)\big).
Let
V_{f,g,\eta}
be the Zariski closure of the image of

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}.
The same argument as in the proof of Proposition
5.2
shows that the algebraic set
V_{f,g,\eta}
is irreducible.

Let
V
be the algebraic set in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}
which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle V:=\big\{([x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}],[y_{0}:\dots:y_{N}],[z_{0}:\dots:z_{t}])\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}\,|\hskip 40.00006pt

\displaystyle x_{j}P_{0}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{N}-x_{n}P_{j,s}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{s}-\dots-x_{n}P_{j,N}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{N}=0,

\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq s_{1}-1,

\displaystyle y_{j}P(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})-Q_{j,s}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{s}-\dots-Q_{j,N}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{N}=0,

\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq s-1,

\displaystyle x_{j}R_{j}^{-}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{N}-x_{n}R_{j}^{+}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})y_{j+N-n}=0,\quad s_{1}\leq j\leq n-1\big\}.

Then by (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and (
5.12
), we see that

V\supseteq V_{f,g,\eta}.

Let
U
be the subset of
V
that contains all points
([x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}],[y_{0}:\dots:y_{N}],[z_{0}:\dots:z_{t}])
in
V
such that

x_{n}\neq 0,\quad y_{N}\neq 0,\quad z_{0}\neq 0,\quad P_{0}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})\neq 0,\quad P(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})\neq 0,

R_{j}^{-}(z_{0},\dots,z_{t})\neq 0,\quad s_{1}\leq j\leq n-1.

Then
U
is a Zariski-open subset of
V.
Because
f_{n}
,
g_{N}
,
P_{0}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})
,
P(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})
and
R_{j}^{-}(1,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t})\,(s_{1}\leq j\leq n-1)
are nonzero holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{m},
we see that

V_{f,g,\eta}\cap U\neq\emptyset.

Let
\phi:U\to\mathbb{C}^{N-s+t}
be the map

([x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}],[y_{0}:\dots:y_{N}],[z_{0}:\dots:z_{t}])\mapsto\Big(\frac{y_{s}}{y_{N}},\dots,\frac{y_{N-1}}{y_{N}},\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}},\dots,\frac{z_{t}}{z_{0}}\Big).

From the definition of
V
and
U,
we easily check that
\phi
is injective. Now since
V_{f,g,\eta}\cap U
is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of
V_{f,g,\eta}
and
V_{f,g,\eta}
is irreducible, we conclude from the injectivity of
\phi|_{V_{f,g,\eta}\cap U}:V_{f,g,\eta}\cap U\to\mathbb{C}^{N-s+t}
that

\dim V_{f,g,\eta}\leq N-s+t.

Let
\pi_{12}:\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the projection onto the first two components and let
\pi_{3}:\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{t}\to\mathbb{P}^{t}
be the projection onto the third component. Since
\pi_{12}(V_{f,g,\eta})
contains the image of

one sees that
\pi_{12}(V_{f,g,\eta})\supseteq V_{f\times g}
and thus

\dim V_{f\times g}\leq\dim\pi_{12}(V_{f,g,\eta})\leq\dim V_{f,g,\eta}.

Since
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}
are multiplicatively independent, we conclude from Proposition
2.5
that
\eta:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{t}
is algebraically non-degenerate. It then follows from
\pi_{3}(V_{f,g,\eta})\supseteq\eta(\mathbb{C}^{m})
that
\pi_{3}(V_{f,g,\eta})=\mathbb{P}^{t}.
Thus

t=\dim\pi_{3}(V_{f,g,\eta})\leq\dim V_{f,g,\eta}.

Now it follows from the above inequalities that

\max\{t,\dim V_{f\times g}\}\leq N-s+t.

This proves Theorem
5.5
.
∎

6
The equality of dimensions of target spaces when
g
is algebraically non-degenerate

We shall prove in this section the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N\geq n\geq 1
be integers. Let

be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in general position and
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in general position with

Let

and

be meromorphic maps such that

,

and
f^{*}(H_{j})=g^{*}(H^{\prime}_{j})
for any
1\leq j\leq q.
If
g
is algebraically non-degenerate, then
N=n.

As a matter of fact, we shall prove the following stronger result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
N
,
n
,
\{H_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,
\{H^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{q}
,

and

. Let

be as in Definition

. If

is linearly non-degenerate and

The following proposition shows Proposition

Proposition 6.3
.

Let

and

be meromorphic maps and let
V_{f\times g}
be as in Definition

. If

is algebraically non-degenerate, then

Let

contains the image of

we conclude from the algebraic non-degeneracy of

Thus

\dim V_{f\times g}\geq\dim\pi_{2}(V_{f\times g})=N,

which proves Proposition

.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Let
(f_{0},\dots,f_{n})
,
(g_{0},\dots,g_{N})
and
a^{j}_{0}X_{0}+\dots+a^{j}_{n}X_{n}

Appendix A   Proof of Lemmas
2.7
and
2.9

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a torsion-free abelian group and let
A=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q})
be a
q
-tuple of elements in
G
that has the property
(P_{r,s}),
where
q\geq r>s\geq 1.
Since
G
is torsion-free, the subgroup
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle
is free. We take a basis
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{t}\}
of
\langle\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}\rangle.
Then every
\alpha_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq q
) is uniquely represented as follows:

\alpha_{i}=\eta_{1}^{l(i,1)}\cdot\eta_{2}^{l(i,2)}\cdots\eta_{t}^{l(i,t)},

(A.1)

where
l(i,1),\dots,l(i,t)
are integers.

Take integers
p_{1},\dots,p_{t}
such that the integers
l_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq q
), which are defined as follows:

l_{i}:=l(i,1)\cdot p_{1}+\dots+l(i,t)\cdot p_{t},

satisfy the condition that, for any indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
1\leq k\leq s,

l_{i_{1}}+\dots+l_{i_{k}}=l_{j_{1}}+\dots+l_{j_{k}}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{i_{k}}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{j_{k}}.

(A.2)

In fact, we may put
p_{1}=1,
and once
p_{1},\dots,p_{u-1}
are chosen, we then put

p_{u}=2s\sum_{\tau=1}^{u-1}\Big(\max_{1\leq i\leq q}|l(i,\tau)|\cdot p_{\tau}\Big)+1.

For such chosen
p_{1},\dots,p_{t},
one checks easily that the integers
l_{i}
defined as above satisfy the condition that, for any
i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\in\{1,\dots,q\}
with
1\leq k\leq s,

l_{i_{1}}+\dots+l_{i_{k}}=l_{j_{1}}+\dots+l_{j_{k}}

if and only if

l(i_{1},\tau)+\dots+l(i_{k},\tau)=l(j_{1},\tau)+\dots+l(j_{k},\tau)\quad\forall\,1\leq\tau\leq t.

By (
A.1
), the latter statement is equivalent to

\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{i_{k}}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{j_{k}}.

So the integers
l_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq q
) satisfy the condition (
A.2
).

In particular, we know that
l_{i}=l_{j}
if and only if
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,q\}.
We also know that, for any
i_{1},\dots,i_{s},j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,q\},

l_{i_{1}}+\dots+l_{i_{s}}=l_{j_{1}}+\dots+l_{j_{s}}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{i_{s}}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\cdots\cdot\alpha_{j_{s}}.

Changing the indices if necessary, we may assume that

l_{1}\leq l_{2}\leq\cdots\leq l_{q}.

Choose the following
r
elements among the
\alpha_{i}
’s:

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{s},\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1},\alpha_{q-(r-s)+2},\dots,\alpha_{q}.

Assume that
l_{s}<l_{q-(r-s)+1}.
Consider the
s
elements
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{s}.
By assumption, there are indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,s,q-(r-s)+1,\dots,q\}
with
j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{s}
and
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}\neq\{1,\dots,s\},
such that

\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\alpha_{j_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{j_{s}},

which implies

l_{1}+l_{2}+\dots+l_{s}=l_{j_{1}}+l_{j_{2}}+\dots+l_{j_{s}}.

(A.3)

Since
j_{s}\geq q-(r-s)+1,
we know that

l_{j_{s}}\geq l_{q-(r-s)+1}>l_{s}.

We also see that
l_{j_{1}}\geq l_{1}
,
l_{j_{2}}\geq l_{2}
,
\dots
,
l_{j_{s-1}}\geq l_{s-1}.
Thus we have

l_{j_{1}}+\dots+l_{j_{s-1}}+l_{j_{s}}>l_{1}+\dots+l_{s-1}+l_{s},

which contradicts the equality (
A.3
).
Hence
l_{s}=l_{q-(r-s)+1}.
Then we get

l_{s}=l_{s+1}=\dots=l_{q-(r-s)+1},

which implies

\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s+1}=\dots=\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1}.

Similarly, by considering the following
r
elements:

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{r-s},\alpha_{q-s+1},\alpha_{q-s+2},\dots,\alpha_{q},

we conclude that

l_{r-s}=l_{r-s+1}=\dots=l_{q-s+1}.

Now we assume that
r\leq 2s.

If
q\geq 2s-1,
which means
q-s+1\geq s,
then

l_{r-s}=l_{r-s+1}=\dots=l_{q-(r-s)+1}

which implies

\alpha_{r-s}=\alpha_{r-s+1}=\dots=\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1}.

If
q<2s-1,
which means
q-s+1<s,
then

\alpha_{r-s}=\alpha_{r-s+1}=\dots=\alpha_{q-s+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s+1}=\dots=\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.7
.

Assume further that
q\geq 2s-1
and
r-s\geq 2.
We now know that

l_{1}\leq\dots\leq l_{r-s-1}\leq l_{r-s}=l_{r-s+1}=\dots=l_{q-(r-s)+1}\leq l_{q-(r-s)+2}\leq\dots\leq l_{q}.

If
l_{r-s-1}=l_{r-s}
or
l_{q-(r-s)+1}=l_{q-(r-s)+2},
which implies

\alpha_{r-s-1}=\alpha_{r-s}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1}=\alpha_{q-(r-s)+2},

then the case (

So we assume
l_{r-s-1}<l_{r-s}
and
l_{q-(r-s)+1}<l_{q-(r-s)+2}
in the following. We now have

l_{1}\leq\dots\leq l_{r-s-1}<l_{r-s}=l_{r-s+1}=\dots=l_{q-(r-s)+1}<l_{q-(r-s)+2}\leq\dots\leq l_{q}.

Choose the following
r
elements among
\alpha_{i}
’s:

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r-s},\alpha_{r-s+1},\dots,\alpha_{s},\alpha_{q-(r-s)+1},\dots,\alpha_{q}.

We note that
s-(r-s)=2s-r.

Consider the
s
elements
\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{s}},
where

i_{1}=1,\dots,i_{r-s-2}=r-s-2,i_{r-s-1}=r-s,\dots,i_{s-1}=s,i_{s}=q-(r-s)+1.

By assumption, there are indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\{1,\dots,s,q-(r-s)+1,\dots,q\}
with
j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{s}
and
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}=:J\neq\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\},
such that

\alpha_{i_{1}}\cdot\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{s}}=\alpha_{j_{1}}\cdot\alpha_{j_{2}}\cdots\alpha_{j_{s}},

which implies

l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}}+\dots+l_{i_{s}}=l_{j_{1}}+l_{j_{2}}+\dots+l_{j_{s}}.

(A.4)

First of all, we see that
r-s-1\in J.
For, if
r-s-1\not\in J,
then by
l_{q-(r-s)+1}<l_{q-(r-s)+2}
we see the right hand side of (

) is strictly greater than the left hand side of (

), which gives a contradiction.

We assume
r-s-1=j_{\kappa},
where
1\leq\kappa\leq s.
It is obvious that
1\leq\kappa\leq r-s-1.
Next, we consider the following two cases.

\bullet

\kappa<r-s-1.
(This is possible only when
r-s\geq 3,
so we exclude this case when
r-s=2.
)

Since
l_{j_{u}}\geq l_{r-s}\geq l_{i_{u}}
for any

and
l_{j_{u}}\geq l_{i_{u}}
for any

In particular, putting

It follows that

which implies

This shows the case (

\bullet

\kappa=r-s-1.

In this case, we necessarily have

By

We assume that

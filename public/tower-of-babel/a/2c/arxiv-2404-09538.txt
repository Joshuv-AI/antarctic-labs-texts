Light single-gluon hybrid states with various (exotic) quantum numbers
Wei-Han Tan 1, Niu Su 1,2, Hua-Xing Chen 1

Abstract
We apply the QCD sum rule method to study the light single-gluon hybrid states with various (exotic) quantum numbers. We construct twenty-four single-gluon hybrid currents, and use eighteen of them to calculate the masses of forty-four single-gluon hybrid states with the quark-gluon contents q ¯ ​ q ​ g \bar{q}qg ( q = u / d q=u/d ) and s ¯ ​ s ​ g \bar{s}sg . We concentrate on the hybrid states with the exotic quantum number J P ​ C = 1 − ⁣ + J^{PC}=1^{-+} , whose masses and widths are calculated to be M | q ¯ q g ; 1 − 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 1.67 + 0.15 − 0.17 M_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}=1.67^{+0.15}_{-0.17} GeV, Γ | q ¯ q g ; 1 − 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 530 + 540 − 330 \Gamma_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}=530^{+540}_{-330} MeV, M | q ¯ q g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 1.67 + 0.15 − 0.17 M_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}=1.67^{+0.15}_{-0.17} GeV, Γ | q ¯ q g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 120 + 160 − 110 \Gamma_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}=120^{+160}_{-110} MeV, M | s ¯ s g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 1.84 + 0.14 − 0.15 M_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}=1.84^{+0.14}_{-0.15} GeV, and Γ | s ¯ s g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ = 100 + 110 − 80 \Gamma_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}=100^{+110}_{-~80} MeV. Our results support the interpretations of the π 1 ​ ( 1600 ) \pi_{1}(1600) and η 1 ​ ( 1855 ) \eta_{1}(1855) as the hybrid states | q ¯ q g ; 1 − 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ |\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle and | s ¯ s g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ |\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle , respectively. Considering the uncertainties, our results suggest that the π 1 ​ ( 1600 ) \pi_{1}(1600) and η 1 ​ ( 1855 ) \eta_{1}(1855) may also be interpreted as the hybrid states | q ¯ q g ; 1 − 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ |\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle and | q ¯ q g ; 0 + 1 − ⁣ + ⟩ |\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle , respectively. To differentiate these two assignments and to verify whether they are hybrid states or not, we propose to examine the a 1 ​ ( 1260 ) ​ π a_{1}(1260)\pi decay channel in future experiments.

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[g^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}g^{\beta_{1}\beta_{1}^{\prime}}g^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}g^{\beta_{2}\beta_{2}^{\prime}}]~\Pi_{2^{++}}(q^{2})+\cdots\,,

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\cdots]
represents symmetrization and subtracting trace terms in the four sets
\{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\}
,
\{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\}
,
\{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\}
, and
\{\beta_{1}^{\prime}\beta_{2}^{\prime}\}

\Pi_{2^{++}}(q^{2})
is contributed only by the

component, while
\cdots
contains the contributions from all the
J^{PC}
components coupling to

. However, we do not know the explicit expression of the rearrangement
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[\cdots]
, so we are not able to calculate the decay constants of these four currents.

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu}f_{1^{-+}}\,,

\displaystyle q^{\mu}f_{0^{++}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iqx}\langle 0|{\bf T}[J^{\mu}_{1^{-+}}(x)J^{\nu\dagger}_{1^{-+}}(0)]|0\rangle

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\nu}-q^{\mu}q^{\nu}/q^{2})~\Pi_{1^{-+}}(q^{2})+(q^{\mu}q^{\nu}/q^{2})~\Pi_{0^{++}}(q^{2})\,.

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}xe^{iqx}\langle 0|{\bf T}[J^{\mu}_{1^{-+}}(x)J^{\nu\dagger}_{1^{-+}}(0)]|0\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{\mu\nu}-q^{\mu}q^{\nu}/q^{2})~\Pi_{1^{-+}}(q^{2})+\cdots,

\Pi_{1^{-+}}(q^{2})

\cdots

We use the dispersion relation to express
\Pi_{1^{-+}}(q^{2})
as

\Pi_{1^{-+}}(q^{2})=\int^{\infty}_{s_{<}}\frac{\rho_{1^{-+}}(s)}{s-q^{2}-i\varepsilon}ds\,,

(41)

where
\rho_{1^{-+}}(s)\equiv{\rm Im}\Pi_{1^{-+}}(s)/\pi
is the spectral density, and
s_{<}=4m_{q}^{2}
is the physical threshold.

At the hadron level, we parameterize
\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm phen}(s)
as one pole dominance for the state
X_{1^{-+}}
together with a continuum contribution:

\displaystyle\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm phen}(s)\times(g^{\mu\nu}-q^{\mu}q^{\nu}/q^{2})

(42)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\delta(s-M^{2}_{n})\langle 0|J^{\mu}_{1^{-+}}|n\rangle\langle n|J^{\nu\dagger}_{1^{-+}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1^{-+}}^{2}\delta(s-M^{2}_{1^{-+}})\times(g^{\mu\nu}-q^{\mu}q^{\nu}/q^{2})+\rm{continuum}\,.

At the quark-gluon level, we calculate
\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm OPE}(s)
through the method of operator product expansion (OPE). After performing the Borel transformation at both the hadron and quark-gluon levels, we use
\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm OPE}(s)
above the threshold value
s_{0}
to approximate the continuum, and derive the sum rule equation

\displaystyle\Pi_{1^{-+}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle f^{2}_{1^{-+}}e^{-M_{1^{-+}}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm OPE}(s)ds,

which can be used to further derive

\displaystyle M^{2}_{1^{-+}}(s_{0},M_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}s\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm OPE}(s)ds}{\int^{s_{0}}_{s_{<}}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho_{1^{-+}}^{\rm OPE}(s)ds},

(44)

\displaystyle f^{2}_{1^{-+}}(s_{0},M_{B})

\displaystyle\Pi_{1^{-+}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})\times e^{M_{1^{-+}}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}.

\displaystyle M_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}=M_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.67^{+0.15}_{-0.17}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle 1.84^{+0.14}_{-0.15}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle f_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.300^{+0.063}_{-0.058}{\rm~GeV}^{4}\,.

Summary and Discussions

In this paper we study the single-gluon hybrid states with various (exotic) quantum numbers. We systematically construct twenty-four single-gluon hybrid currents, and use eighteen of them to perform QCD sum rule analyses. We calculate the masses of forty-four single-gluon hybrid states with the quark-gluon contents
\bar{q}qg
(
q=u/d
) and
\bar{s}sg
. The obtained results are summarized in Table
1
and Table
2
. Especially, the masses and decay constants of the
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
hybrid states are extracted from the current
J^{\mu}_{1^{-+}}
given in Eq. (
11
) to be:

\displaystyle M_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.67^{+0.15}_{-0.17}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle f_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.243^{+0.057}_{-0.052}{\rm~GeV}^{4}\,,

\displaystyle M_{|\bar{s}sg;1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.84^{+0.14}_{-0.15}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle f_{|\bar{s}sg;1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.300^{+0.063}_{-0.058}{\rm~GeV}^{4}\,.

Since we do not differentiate the up and down quarks within the QCD sum rule method, the masses and decay constants of the states in the same isospin multiplet are calculated to be the same:

\displaystyle M_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}=M_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.67^{+0.15}_{-0.17}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle f_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}=f_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.243^{+0.057}_{-0.052}{\rm~GeV}^{4}\,,

\displaystyle M_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.84^{+0.14}_{-0.15}{\rm~GeV}\,,

\displaystyle f_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.300^{+0.063}_{-0.058}{\rm~GeV}^{4}\,.

Based on the mass calculations, we systematically study the decay properties of the
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
hybrid states. We have considered the normal decay process depicted in Fig.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 530^{+540}_{-330}{\rm~MeV}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 120^{+160}_{-110}{\rm~MeV}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 100^{+110}_{-~80}{\rm~MeV}\,.

The above QCD sum rule results suggest that the
\pi_{1}(1600)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
can be respectively interpreted as the single-gluon hybrid states
|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle
and
|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle
, so there exists another isoscalar state
|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle
, whose mass and width are smaller than those of
\eta_{1}(1855)
. Considering the uncertainties, our results suggest that the
\pi_{1}(1600)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
may also be respectively interpreted as the single-gluon hybrid states
|\bar{q}qg;1^{-}1^{-+}\rangle
and
|\bar{q}qg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle
, so there exists another isoscalar state
|\bar{s}sg;0^{+}1^{-+}\rangle
, whose mass and width are larger than those of
\eta_{1}(1855)
. To differentiate these two assignments, it is useful to examine the
a_{1}(1260)\pi
decay channel. We find in Table
3
that the
\eta^{(\prime)}
-relevant decay modes, as the characteristic decay modes of hybrid states, are enhanced to some extent. To verify whether the exotic
\pi_{1}
and
\eta_{1}
resonances are hybrid states or not, we propose to examine these decay modes in future BESIII, Belle-II, GlueX, LHCb, and PANDA experiments.

Abstract
. Assuming a weighted Nash type inequality for the generator − A -A of a Markov semigroup, we prove a weighted Nash type inequality for its fractional power and deduce non-uniform bounds on the transition kernel corresponding to the Markov semigroup generated by − A α -A^{\alpha} .

1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
T(\cdot)
be a symmetric Markov semigroup on
L^{2}(\mu)
with generator
-A
, namely a symmetric positivity preserving and
L^{\infty}
-contractive strongly continuous semigroup in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

It is well known that for every
t>0
the operators
T(t)
are contractions in
L^{p}(\mu)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
One can prove that the Dirichlet form associated with
T(\cdot)
is non-negative, so

(Af,f)=\int_{X}fAf\,d\mu\geq 0

for any
f\in D(A)
. This, together with the symmetry of the semigroup
T(\cdot)
, implies that
A
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
, see
[
13
, Chapter I]
. We note here that it suffices to consider real-valued functions since positive semigroups are real.

Very often the semigroup
T(\cdot)
is represented by a kernel density with respect to the measure
\mu
, namely for any
t>0
there exists a non-negative symmetric function
p(t,x,y)
on
X\times X
such that

T(t)f(x)=\int_{X}f(y)p(t,x,y)\,d\mu(y)

for
\mu
almost every
x\in X
.
For further properties about symmetric Markov semigroups and their generators we address the reader to
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
.

Nash type inequalities are a powerful tool to show the existence of the kernel density
p(t,x,y)
and to obtain control on it.
The model case is the classical Nash inequality, which in our context may be written as follows

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{1+\frac{n}{2}}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{1}(Af,f)^{\frac{n}{4}},

where
n
is a positive parameter. As shown in
[
13
, Chapter 2]
, the previous inequality is equivalent to the classical uniform bound
\left\|T(t)f\right\|_{\infty}\leq C^{\prime 2}t^{-n/2}\left\|f\right\|_{1}
for
t>0
, which implies the following uniform bound on the kernel density

p(t,x,y)\leq C^{\prime 2}t^{-n/2}

for any
x,y\in X
and
t>0
.
In the last few years, more general Nash inequalities together with Sobolev inequalities and super-Poincaré inequalities have been studied in order to obtain estimates for the kernel density (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
).
For instance in
[
3
]
the authors introduce weight functions
V
that are Lyapunov functions for the generator
-A
(i.e.
0\leq V\in D(A)
and
-AV\leq cV
for some non-negative constant
c
called Lyapunov constant). They deal with Nash type inequalities of the form

(1.1)

\phi\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{(Af,f)}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},

where
\phi
is a positive function defined on
(M,\infty)
with
x\mapsto\phi(x)/x
non decreasing and
M
be a non-negative real number. We point out that if we set
\phi(x)=Cx^{1+2/n}
,
M=0
and
V=1
, then (
1.1
) is equalivalent to the classical Nash inequality mentioned above.
Under suitable assumptions on the Lyapunov function
V
and the rate function
\phi
, it is proved in
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
that (
1.1
) implies that
\left\|T(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq K(2t)e^{ct}\left\|fV\right\|_{1}
for
t>0
,
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
and for a positive function
K
. From that,
[
3
, Corollary 3.7]
gives the following estimate for the kernel density:

p(t,x,y)\leq K(t)^{2}e^{ct}V(x)V(y)

for all
t>0
and
\mu\otimes\mu
almost every
x,y\in X
.
In this paper we will consider a slightly different version of inequality (
1.1
), that is

(1.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)+c\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2},

where
B:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a non-decreasing function and
c
is the Lyapunov constant associated to the weight function
V
.
We will refer to (
1.2
) as
weighted Nash inequality
.

In this work we are interested in establishing estimates for the kernel associated with the fractional operator
-A^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
. In the last few years this question has received a lot of attention. We mention here
[
17
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
, where specific operators were considered. The idea is to follow the approach described above making use of weighted Nash inequalities. However, in concrete examples it is not easy to directly prove such estimates for the fractional operator
A^{\alpha}
. Therefore, in Section
2
, Theorem
2.3
shows how to obtain a weighted Nash inequality for
A^{\alpha}
assuming that (
1.2
) holds for the operator
A
. We highlight that the non-weighted case with
c=0
was treated in
[
7
]
.

With this weighted Nash inequality for the fractional operator at hand, in Section
3
we start from the weighted Nash inequality (
1.2
) to get estimates for the kernel
p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)
associated with the fractional operator
-A^{\alpha}
. We prove kernel estimates of the following form

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq\tilde{K}(t,\phi,c)V(x)V(y)

for all
t>0
and
\mu\otimes\mu
almost every
x,y\in X
.
These results are contained in Theorem
3.3
for the case
0<\alpha<1
and in Theorem
3.5
for
\alpha\geq 1
.
Moreover, as soon as
V
belongs to
L^{2}(\mu)
, then the kernel density
p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)
is in
L^{2}(\mu\otimes\mu)
. As a consequence, for any
t>0
the operator
T_{\alpha}(t)
is Hilbert-Schmidt on
L^{2}(\mu)
and so, it has a discrete spectrum.

Finally, in Section
4
we illustate our results by applying them to the operator

A=-\Delta-\nabla\log\rho\cdot\nabla

on the space
L^{2}(\mu)
with the measure
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx
and the weight function
V=\rho^{-1/2}
, where
\rho
is a positive smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Under suitable assumptions, a weighted Nash inequality for
A
was already proven in
[
3
, Section 4]
in order to obtain estimates for the kernel associated with the operator
-A
(see
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
). Applying Theorem
3.3
,
we are now able to find a similar result for the kernel
p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)
associated with the fractional operator
-A^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
, i.e.

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{c^{\alpha}t}\rho^{-1/2}(x)\rho^{-1/2}(y)

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In particular, we consider examples of Cauchy measures
\rho(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\beta}
with
\beta>d
and exponential measures
\rho(x)=e^{-(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}
with
0<a<2,\,\rho(x)=e^{-|x|^{a}}
with
a\geq 2
.

Notation

If
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we use the following notation:

\displaystyle D_{i}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},\ D_{ij}u=D_{i}D_{j}u,\ \nabla u=

\displaystyle(D_{1}u,\dots,D_{d}u)

and

|\nabla u|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|D_{j}u|^{2},\qquad|D^{2}u|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|D_{ij}u|^{2}.

Moreover, we denote by
L^{2}(\mu)
the classical
L^{2}
-space on
(X,\mu)
endowed with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
and the scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

2.
Weighted Nash type inequalities for fractional powers of non-negative self-adjoint operators

In this section we prove weighted Nash inequalities for a class of Kolmogorov fractional operators.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
A
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
We may write
A
by means of its spectral decomposition as follows

(2.1)

A=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda\,dE_{\lambda}.

For
\alpha>0
we define the fractional power
A^{\alpha}
of
A
by

(A^{\alpha}f,f)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{\alpha}\,d(E_{\lambda}f,f)

on the domain
D(A^{\alpha})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\mid\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{\alpha}d(E_{\lambda}f,f)<\infty\}
. We have that
A^{\alpha}
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator. Moreover,
-A^{\alpha}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions
T_{\alpha}(\cdot)
given by

(2.2)

T_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}T(s)d\mu_{t}^{\alpha}(s)

for all
t>0
, where
(\mu_{t}^{\alpha})
is such that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s\lambda}d\mu_{t}^{\alpha}(s)=e^{-t\lambda^{\alpha}}

for any
\lambda>0
.
In particular, for
\alpha=1/2
, we denote by
P(\cdot)
the semigroup generated by
-A^{1/2}
.

If
0<\alpha<1
, by the Balakrishnan representation formula (see for example
[
14
, Chapter 1, Section 9]
and
[
15
, Proposition 3.1.12]
), we have

(2.3)

A^{\alpha}f=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\alpha-1}(t+A)^{-1}(Af)\,dt

for all
f\in D(A)
.

We now consider a symmetric Markov semigroup
T(\cdot)
acting on
L^{2}(\mu)
with generator
-A
, namely a family of linear operators mapping satisfying
the following properties:

(1)

Preservation of positivity: if
f\geq 0
then
T(t)f\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
.

(2)

L^{\infty}
-contractivity:
\left\|T(t)f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}
for all
t\geq 0
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

(3)

Semigroup property:
T(0)=Id
and
T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

(4)

Symmetry:
T(t)
maps
L^{2}(\mu)
into itself and
\int_{X}T(t)fg\,d\mu=\int_{X}fT(t)g\,d\mu
for all
f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)
and
t\geq 0
.

(5)

Continuity at
t=0
:
T(t)f\to f
as
t\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

We first introduce, as in
[
3
]
, Lyapunov functions for the operator
-A
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lyapunov function for the operator
-A
is a positive function
V\in D(A)
such that

-AV\leq cV

for a real constant
c
, called the Lyapunov constant.

Remark 2.2
.

Fix
0<\alpha<1
. Assume that
V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator
-A
with zero Lyapunov constant.
Then,
V\in D(A^{\alpha})
(see
[
15
, Proposition 3.1.1]
).
Moreover, since
T(\cdot)
is a positive semigroup, we have that the resolvent operator
(t+A)^{-1}
is positive for all
t>0
, cf.
[
6
, Corollary 12.10]
. Hence, combining the inequality
-AV\leq 0
with the Balakrishnan representation formula (
2.3
), it follows that
V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator
-A^{\alpha}
with zero Lyapunov constant.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of the following two results that show how to obtain weighted Nash inequalities for the fractional operator
A^{\alpha}
starting from similar inequalities for the operator
A
. For that we need to distinguish between the cases of
0<\alpha<1
and
\alpha\geq 1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
T(\cdot)
be a symmetric Markov semigroup acting on
L^{2}(\mu)
with generator
-A
. Assume that there exist a non-decreasing function
B:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and a Lyapunov function
V
for the operator
-A
in
L^{2}(\mu)
with Lyapunov constant
c\geq 0
such that the following weighted Nash inequality holds

(2.4)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)+c\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}

for all
f\in D(A)
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
Then for every
0<\alpha<1
, there exists
\gamma>0
such that

(2.5)

\gamma\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(\gamma\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\alpha}\leq(A^{\alpha}f,f)+c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}

for all
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
A
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Assume that there exist a positive function
V
and a non-decreasing function
B:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that the following weighted Nash inequality holds

(2.6)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)

for all
f\in D(A)
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
Then for every
\alpha\geq 1
we have

(2.7)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\alpha}\leq(A^{\alpha}f,f)

for all
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.

To prove Theorem
2.3
we start by considering the case where
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
T(\cdot)
be a symmetric Markov semigroup acting on
L^{2}(\mu)
with generator
-A
. Assume that there exist a non-decreasing function
B:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)

and a Lyapunov function
V
for the operator
-A
in
L^{2}(\mu)
with zero Lyapunov constant such that the following weighted Nash inequality holds

(2.8)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)

for all
f\in D(A)
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we have

(2.9)

(1-\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(\varepsilon\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(A^{1/2}f,f)

for all
f\in D(A^{1/2})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
f\in D(A^{1/2})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
f\geq 0
. Indeed otherwise we write the argument for
|f|
obtaining that

(1-\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(\varepsilon\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(A^{1/2}|f|,|f|).

Then, since the semigroup
P(\cdot)
generated by
-A^{1/2}
is positive, we have, for every
f\in D(A^{1/2}),\,|f|\in D(A^{1/2})
, and
(A^{1/2}|f|,|f|)\leq(A^{1/2}f,f)
by
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.2]
or
[
20
, Theorem 2.7]
. Thus, (
2.9
) holds true.

We now prove the theorem assuming that
f\geq 0
. We observe that, since
P(\cdot)
is a symmetric Markov semigroup, then
P(t)f\geq 0
for any
t\geq 0
.
First we show that

(2.10)

\left\|VP(t)f\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|fV\right\|_{1}

for all
t>0
. Since, by Remark
2.2
,
V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator
-A^{1/2}
with zero Lyapunov constant, i.e.
-A^{1/2}V\leq 0
. Hence, we get

(2.11)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|VP(t)f\right\|_{1}=\int(-A^{1/2}P(t)f)V\,d\mu=\int(P(t)f)(-A^{1/2}V)\,d\mu\leq 0.

This proves the inequality (
2.10
).
Moreover, since
P(t)f\in D(A)
for any
t>0
and
\left\|VP(t)f\right\|_{1}<\infty
, applying (
2.8
) to the function
P(t)f
we find that

(2.12)

\left\|P(t)f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|P(t)f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|VP(t)f\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(AP(t)f,P(t)f).

We set

\phi(t)=\frac{\left\|P(t)f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},\quad t\geq 0.

Given that
AP(t)f=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}P(t)f,\,t>0
, we have

\dot{\phi}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t)=-2\frac{(A^{1/2}P(t)f,P(t)f)}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},\quad t\geq 0,

and

\ddot{\phi}(t)=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\phi(t)=4\frac{(AP(t)f,P(t)f)}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},\quad t>0.

Using first the inequality (
2.12
) and then combining (
2.10
) with the fact that
B
is a non-decreasing function, we deduce that

(2.13)

\ddot{\phi}(t)\geq 4\phi(t)B\left(\frac{\left\|P(t)f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|VP(t)f\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\geq 4\phi(t)B(\phi(t))

for all
t>0
. We observe that
-\dot{\phi}\geq 0
because
A^{1/2}
is a non-negative operator. Multiplying both sides in (
2.13
) by
-\dot{\phi}(t)
yields

(2.14)

-[\dot{\phi}^{2}]^{\prime}(t)\geq-4[\phi^{2}(t)]^{\prime}B(\phi(t))

for any
t>0
.
We now take care of both sides individually. Fix
T>0
. Integrating the left hand side over
[0,T]
we have

-\int_{0}^{T}[\dot{\phi}^{2}]^{\prime}(s)\,ds=\dot{\phi}^{2}(0)-\dot{\phi}^{2}(T)\leq\dot{\phi}^{2}(0)=4\frac{(A^{1/2}f,f)^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{4}}.

Integrating the right hand side over
[0,T]
leads to

-4\int_{0}^{T}[\phi^{2}(s)]^{\prime}B(\phi(s))\,ds=4\int_{\phi^{2}(T)}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\sqrt{x})\,dx.

Then, by (
2.14
) we obtain

(2.15)

\frac{(A^{1/2}f,f)^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{4}}\geq\int_{\phi^{2}(T)}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\sqrt{x})\,dx.

Let us assume that

(2.16)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\left\|P(T)f\right\|_{2}=0.

It implies that
\phi^{2}(T)\to 0
as
T\to\infty
. Then, taking the limit as
T\to\infty
in (
2.15
) we get

\frac{(A^{1/2}f,f)^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{4}}\geq\int_{0}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\sqrt{x})\,dx.

From that, since
B
is non-decreasing, it follows that

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon^{2})\phi^{2}(0)B(\varepsilon\phi(0))

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{2}(0)}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\varepsilon\phi^{2}(0))\,dx\leq\int_{\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{2}(0)}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\sqrt{x})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\phi^{2}(0)}B(\sqrt{x})\,dx\leq\frac{(A^{1/2}f,f)^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{4}}.

Thus, we derive the inequality (
2.9
) under the assumption (
2.16
). In order to consider the general case, we apply the argument to the operator
A_{\rho}=A+\rho I
with
\rho>0
and we let
\rho\to 0
.
∎

Let us now iterate the result obtained in Proposition
2.5
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Proposition
2.5
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
there exist
a_{n},b_{n}>0
such that

(2.17)

a_{n}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(b_{n}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{1/2^{n}}\leq(A^{1/2^{n}}f,f)

for all
f\in D(A^{1/2^{n}})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction on
n
. For
n=1
the statement follows from Proposition
2.5
. Assume now that the inequality (
2.17
) holds true. We observe that
V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator
-A^{1/2^{n}}
with zero Lyapunov constant by Remark
2.2
. Then we can apply Proposition
2.5
with
\tilde{B}=a_{n}[B(b_{n}\cdot)]^{1/2^{n}}
and
\tilde{A}=A^{1/2^{n}}
. As a consequence, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we have

(1-\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2}a_{n}^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\left[B\left(\varepsilon b_{n}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}}\leq(A^{1/2^{n+1}}f,f)

for all
f\in D(A^{1/2^{n+1}})
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
∎

The following convexity argument is also needed.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\Lambda\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a non-decreasing function and
A
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator such that

(2.18)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\Lambda\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)

for all
f\in D(A)
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
Then for any convex non-decreasing and non-negative function
\Phi
we have

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\,\Phi\circ\Lambda\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(\Phi(A)f,f)

for all
f\in D(\Phi(A))
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume
\left\|f\right\|_{2}=1
. The general case follows by writing the argument below for the function
f/\left\|f\right\|_{2}
.
If
\left\|f\right\|_{2}=1
, then (
2.18
) becomes

(2.19)

\Lambda\left(1/\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\leq(Af,f).

Since the function
\Phi
is non-decreasing, applying it to both sides of (
2.19
) and taking into account the spectral decomposition of the operator
A
given by (
2.1
), we obtain that

\Phi\circ\Lambda\left(1/\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\leq\Phi\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,d(E_{\lambda}f,f)\right).

We now observe that
(E_{\lambda}f,f)
is a probability measure because
\left\|f\right\|_{2}=1
. By the convexity of the function
\Phi
, we apply Jensen’s inequality to get the following inequality

\Phi\circ\Lambda\left(1/\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\lambda)\,d(E_{\lambda}f,f)=(\Phi(A)f,f)

for all
f\in D(\Phi(A))
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
This proves the statement.
∎

The following comparison between
(A+c)^{\alpha}
and
A^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha}
is useful to prove Theorem
2.3
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A
be a non-negative self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\mu)
and let
c\geq 0
. Then, for all
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
, we have

((A+c)^{\alpha}f,f)\leq 2^{\alpha-1}[(A^{\alpha}f,f)+c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}]

if
\alpha\geq 1
, whereas we have

((A+c)^{\alpha}f,f)\leq(A^{\alpha}f,f)+c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}

if
0<\alpha<1
.

Proof.

By the spectral theorem the operator
A
is unitarily equivalent to a multiplication operator, namely there exists a measure space
(Y,\nu)
which is
\sigma
-finite, a measurable function
q\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
and a unitary map
U\colon L^{2}(X,\mu)\to L^{2}(Y,\nu)
with

A=U^{-1}M_{q}U.

The multiplication operator
M_{q}
on
L^{2}(Y,\nu)
is defined by
M_{q}f(x)=q(x)f(x)
for
x\in Y
with domain
D(M_{q})=\{f\in L^{2}(Y,\nu)\mid qf\in L^{2}(Y,\nu)\}
.
Hence, by the functional calculus (see
[
14
, Chapter 2, Section 6]
) the operator
(A+c)^{\alpha}
is given by

(A+c)^{\alpha}=U^{-1}M_{(q+c)^{\alpha}}U.

Let
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
and assume
\alpha\geq 1
. Since
U
is a unitary operator, we have

\displaystyle((A+c)^{\alpha}f,f)

\displaystyle=(U^{-1}M_{(q+c)^{\alpha}}Uf,f)=((q+c)^{\alpha}Uf,Uf)\leq 2^{\alpha-1}[(q^{\alpha}Uf,Uf)+c^{\alpha}(Uf,Uf)]

\displaystyle=2^{\alpha-1}[(U^{-1}q^{\alpha}Uf,f)+c^{\alpha}(U^{-1}Uf,f)]=2^{\alpha-1}[(A^{\alpha}f,f)+c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}].

Similarly, for
0<\alpha<1
we obtain

((A+c)^{\alpha}f,f)=((q+c)^{\alpha}Uf,Uf)\leq(q^{\alpha}Uf,Uf)+c^{\alpha}(Uf,Uf)=(A^{\alpha}f,f)+c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

Proof of Theorem

3.
Kernel estimates

In this section we use Theorem
2.3
to obtain estimates of the kernel of the semigroup generated by
-A^{\alpha}
. We start with the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
T(\cdot)
be a symmetric Markov semigroup acting on
L^{2}(\mu)
with generator
-A
. Fix
0<\alpha<1
. Assume that there exist a Lyapunov function
V
for the operator
-A
in
L^{2}(\mu)
with Lyapunov constant
c\geq 0
and a non-decreasing function
B:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that
A
satisfies the following weighted Nash inequality

(3.1)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)+c\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}

for all
f\in D(A)
with
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
Moreover, given
\gamma
as in (
2.5
),
assume that

(3.2)

U(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma u[B(\gamma u)]^{\alpha}}\,du<\infty

for all
x>0
.
Then the semigroup
T_{\alpha}(\cdot)
generated by
-A^{\alpha}
satisfies the following inequality

(3.3)

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq K(2t)e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|fV\right\|_{1}

for all
t>0
and all functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
Here, the function
K
is defined by

(3.4)

K(x):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{U^{-1}(x)}&\text{if }0<x<U(0),\\
0&\text{if }x\geq U(0).\end{cases}

Proof.

We first prove the theorem assuming that
c=0
. Then
V
is a Lyapunov function for
-A^{\alpha}
by Remark
2.2
and
\phi(x)=\gamma x[B(\gamma x)]^{\alpha}
is a positive function defined on
(0,+\infty)
with
\phi(x)/x
non decreasing and
\int^{\infty}1/\phi(x)\,dx<\infty
, by (
3.2
).
Moreover, by Theorem
2.3
, the operator
A^{\alpha}
satisfies a weighted Nash inequality of this form

\phi\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{(A^{\alpha}f,f)}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},

for all
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
such that
\left\|fV\right\|_{1}<\infty
.
Hence, by
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
we infer that

(3.5)

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq K(2t)\left\|fV\right\|_{1},

for all
t>0
and all functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

We now turn to the general case assuming that
c\geq 0
. Since
V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator
-(A+c)
with zero Lyapunov constant, from what we proved above we have

(3.6)

\left\|e^{-t(A+c)^{\alpha}}f\right\|_{2}\leq K(2t)\left\|fV\right\|_{1},

for all
t>0
and all functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, where
e^{-t(A+c)^{\alpha}}
is the semigroup generated by
-(A+c)^{\alpha}
.
We now prove that

(3.7)

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|e^{-t(A+c)^{\alpha}}f\right\|_{2},

for all
t>0
and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
Since
0<\alpha<1
, we apply
[
19
, Proposition 5.1.14]
to infer that there is a bounded self-adjoint operator
T_{c}
such that

(A+c)^{\alpha}=A^{\alpha}+T_{c}.

Moreover, Lemma
2.8
implies that

(T_{c}f,f)=((A+c)^{\alpha}f,f)-(A^{\alpha}f,f)\leq c^{\alpha}\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2},

for all
f\in D(A^{\alpha})
. Hence, we have that
\left\|T_{c}\right\|\leq c^{\alpha}
, from which we get that

\left\|e^{tT_{c}}f\right\|_{2}\leq e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|f\right\|_{2},

for all
t>0
and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, where
e^{tT_{c}}
is the semigroup generated by
T_{c}
.
We finally get (
3.7
) as follows

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}=\left\|e^{t(T_{c}-(A+c)^{\alpha})}f\right\|_{2}\leq e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|e^{-t(A+c)^{\alpha}}f\right\|_{2},

for all
t>0
and for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
Combining inequalities (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) we obtain the statement.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose that condition (
3.2
) holds for all
x>M
, for some
M\geq 0
. Then in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we can still apply
[

\begin{cases}\sqrt{U^{-1}(x)}&\text{if }0<x<U(M),\\
\sqrt{M}&\text{if }x\geq U(M),\end{cases}

and

the function on

given by

U(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma u[B(\gamma u)]^{\alpha}}\,du.

The rest of the proof carries over verbatim.

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, we have that the semigroup
T_{\alpha}(\cdot)
generated by
-A^{\alpha}
has a kernel
p_{\alpha}
with respect to
\mu
which satisfies

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq K(t)^{2}e^{c^{\alpha}t}V(x)V(y),

for all
t>0
and
\mu\otimes\mu
almost every
x,y\in X
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
we infer that

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq K(2t)e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|fV\right\|_{1},

for all
t>0
and for all

. Hence, applying
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have that the operator

, that is

t>0
and

almost every

.
∎

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
and let
T(\cdot)

L^{2}(\mu)

-A

V

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}B\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq(Af,f)+c\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}

c\geq 0

\left\|T_{\alpha}(t)f\right\|_{2}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K(2t)e^{c_{\alpha}t}\left\|fV\right\|_{1},\,\hbox{\ if }c_{\alpha}\geq c^{\alpha},\\
K(2t)e^{c^{\alpha}t}\left\|fV\right\|_{1},\,\hbox{\ if }c_{\alpha}<c^{\alpha}\end{array}\right.

for all
t>0
and all functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
Here, the function
K
is defined by

(3.12)

K(x):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{U^{-1}(x)}&\text{if }0<x<U(0),\\
0&\text{if }x\geq U(0).\end{cases}

Proof.

We apply Theorem
2.4
and Remark
2.10
to infer that the operator

\phi\left(\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{((A^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha})f,f)}{\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{2}},

for all

.
We observe that

.

V
is a Lyapunov function for the operator

-(A^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha})

and one takes

.

\left\|e^{-t(A^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha})}f\right\|_{2}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K(2t)e^{(c_{\alpha}-c^{\alpha})t}\left\|fV\right\|_{1},\,\,\hbox{\ if }c_{\alpha}\geq c^{\alpha},\\

4.
Some applications

In this section we aim to apply Theorem
3.3
to the operator

(4.1)

A=-\Delta-\nabla\log\rho\cdot\nabla

on the space
L^{2}(\mu)
with the measure
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx
, where
\rho
is a positive
C^{2}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By
[
1
, Corollary 3.7]
we have that
-\overline{A}|_{C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
on
L^{2}(\mu)
generates a symmetric Markov semigroup
T(\cdot)
.
We still denote by
-A
its generator.

We first consider the weight function

V=\rho^{-1/2}.

Even if
V
is never in
L^{2}(\mu)
, it will play the role of Lyapunov function.
As proved in
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
, the classical Nash inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{2+d}{4}}\leq C_{d}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{d}{4}}

is equivalent to

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}\leq C_{d}^{\frac{4}{d}}\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{\frac{4}{d}}\left((Af,f)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-AV}{V}f^{2}\,d\mu\right)

for all smooth functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support.
Moreover, if
-AV\leq cV
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}\leq C_{d}^{\frac{4}{d}}\left\|fV\right\|_{1}^{\frac{4}{d}}\left((Af,f)+c\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\right).

In other words,
A
satisfies a weighted Nash inequality such as (
1.2
) with
B(x)=x^{\frac{2}{d}}
.
Consequently, under suitable assumptions on the function
\rho
, in the next result we apply Theorem
3.3
to estimate the kernel associated with the fractional operator
A^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

With the above notation, let
0<\alpha<1
and assume that
\mu
is a probability measure.
Moreover, assume that

(1)

V\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
AV\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
-AV\leq cV
with
c\geq 0
;

(2)

the Hessian of
\log\rho
is uniformly bounded from above on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(3)

\sup_{|x|=r}\rho(x)^{1/2}r^{d-1}\to 0
and
\sup_{|x|=r}|\nabla\rho(x)|\rho(x)^{-1/2}r^{d-1}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.

Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the semigroup
T_{\alpha}(\cdot)
generated by
-A^{\alpha}
has a kernel
p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)
which satisfies

(4.2)

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{c^{\alpha}t}V(x)V(y)

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proof.

The idea is to apply Theorem
3.3
with
B(x)=x^{\frac{2}{d}}
.
We observe that
V=\rho^{-1/2}\notin L^{2}(\mu)
, so
V\notin D(A)
and it is not possible to directly apply Theorem
3.3
.
However, as pointed out in Remark
2.9
, we only need to justify inequality (
2.10
).

Let
t>0
and
f
be a smooth function with compact support.
As in the proof of
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
, we first prove that the following integration by parts holds for the operator
A

(4.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(-AT(t)f)\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-AV)T(t)f\,d\mu

for any
t>0
and any smooth function
f
with compact support.
Let
r>0
,
B_{r}
be the open ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
r
and center
0
and
\vec{\eta}
be its outward unit normal vector.
Integrating by parts on
B_{r}
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}V(-AT(t)f)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}V\rho\Delta T(t)f\,dx+\int_{B_{r}}V\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla T(t)f\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r}}\nabla(V\rho)\cdot\nabla T(t)f\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{r}}V\nabla T(t)f\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho\,d\sigma

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{div}(V\nabla\rho)T(t)f\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{r}}VT(t)f\nabla\rho\cdot\vec{\eta}\,d\sigma.

Integrating by parts again the first integral in the right hand side of the previous expression we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}V(-AT(t)f)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}[\Delta(V\rho)-\mathrm{div}(V\nabla\rho)]T(t)f\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{r}}T(t)f[V\nabla\rho-\nabla(V\rho)]\cdot\vec{\eta}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B_{r}}V\nabla T(t)f\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}(-AV)T(t)f\,d\mu-\int_{\partial B_{r}}T(t)f\nabla V\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial B_{r}}V\nabla T(t)f\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho\,d\sigma.

We denote by
S^{d-1}
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and performing a change of variables we find that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}V(-AT(t)f)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}(-AV)T(t)f\,d\mu-\int_{S^{d-1}}T(t)f(r\omega)\nabla V(r\omega)\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho(r\omega)r^{d-1}\,d\omega

\displaystyle+\int_{S^{d-1}}V(r\omega)\nabla T(t)f(r\omega)\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho(r\omega)r^{d-1}\,d\omega

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}(-AV)T(t)f\,d\mu-I_{r}+J_{r},

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle I_{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{d-1}}T(t)f(r\omega)\nabla V(r\omega)\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho(r\omega)r^{d-1}\,d\omega,

(4.6)

\displaystyle J_{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{d-1}}V(r\omega)\nabla T(t)f(r\omega)\cdot\vec{\eta}\rho(r\omega)r^{d-1}\,d\omega.

We have

|I_{r}|\leq\int_{S^{d-1}}|T(t)f(r\omega)||\nabla V(r\omega)|\rho(r\omega)r^{d-1}\,d\omega\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\int_{S^{d-1}}|\nabla\rho(r\omega)|\rho(r\omega)^{-1/2}r^{d-1}\,d\omega.

Then by assumption (c) we derive that
I_{r}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
Moreover, since the Hessian of
\log\rho
is uniformly bounded from above on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let say by a constant
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows that
-A
satisfies the Bakry-Emery Curvature-Dimension condition
CD(-\nu,\infty)
and hence, cf.
[
2
, Proposition 5.4.1]
, one obtains

|\nabla T(t)f|\leq e^{\nu t}T(t)|\nabla f|\leq e^{\nu t}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\infty}.

Hence, arguing similarly, we deduce that
J_{r}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
Consequently, the last two terms in (
4
) tend to
0
as
r
tends to infinity. Thus, the integration by parts formula (
4.3
) is valid.

We now prove that

(4.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(-A^{\beta}f)\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-A^{\beta}V)f\,d\mu,

for any
\beta\in(0,1)
. Let
\varphi
be a cut-off function such that the support of
f
is contained in the support of
\varphi
and
\varphi=1
on the support of
f
.
Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a core for
-A
, we can apply the Balakrishnan representation formula (
2.3
) obtaining that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(-A^{\beta}f)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi V(-A^{\beta}f)\,d\mu=\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}\varphi V(s+A)^{-1}(-Af)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}\varphi VT(t)(-Af)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}\varphi V(-AT(t)f)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi V(-AT(t)f)d\mu.

Using the integration by parts formula (
4.3
), which remains true if we replace
V
by
\varphi V
, and since the semigroup
T(\cdot)
is symmetric, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(-A^{\beta}f)\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-A(\varphi V))T(t)fd\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fT(t)(-A(\varphi V))d\mu

(4.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}T(t)(-A(\varphi V))dt.

Here we observe that
\varphi V\in D(A)
, since
\varphi V\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and one can see, from the proof of
[
1
, Corollary 3.7]
, that
C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a core for
-A
.
Consequently, we can apply the Balakrishnan representation formula (
2.3
) and we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}ds\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-st}T(t)(-A(\varphi V))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\beta\pi}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\mu\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{\beta-1}(s+A)^{-1}(-A(\varphi V))\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-A^{\beta}(\varphi V))f\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-A^{\beta}V)f\,d\mu.

Combining this with (
4
) leads to (
4.7
).

Moreover, since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a core for
A
and
D(A)
is a core for
A^{\beta}
by
[
15
, Proposition 3.1.1]
, then (
4.7
) holds for any
f\in D(A^{\beta})
.
Finally, applying (
4.7
) with
T_{\beta}(t)f
instead of
f
we find that

(4.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(-A^{\beta}T_{\beta}(t)f)\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-A^{\beta}V)T_{\beta}(t)f\,d\mu,

for any
\beta\in(0,1)
,
t>0
and any smooth function
f
with compact support.

It is now possible to apply Theorem
3.3
to infer that

(4.10)

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq K(t)^{2}e^{c^{\alpha}t}V(x)V(y),

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
K
is defined as in (
3.4
).
Computing the function
U(x)
defined by (
3.2
) we have

U(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma u[B(\gamma u)]^{\alpha}}\,du=\int_{x}^{\infty}(\gamma u)^{-1-\frac{2\alpha}{d}}\,du=\frac{d}{2\alpha\gamma^{1+\frac{2\alpha}{d}}}x^{-\frac{2\alpha}{d}},

for all
x>0
. Moreover, since
U(0)=+\infty
, the function
K(x)
in (
3.4
) is given by

K(x)=\sqrt{U^{-1}(x)}=Cx^{-\frac{d}{4\alpha}},

for some
C>0
.
One derives (
4.2
) from (
4.10
) by plugging in the definition of
K
.
∎

We now illustrate the previous result on the examples of measures of Cauchy and exponential types.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
p_{\alpha}
be the integral kernel associated with
A^{\alpha}
with
\rho(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\beta}
and
\beta>d
. Then

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{(\beta d)^{\alpha}t}(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}},

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

We check the assumptions of Corollary
4.1
. Since
\beta>d
, then
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx
is a probability measure up to a positive constant. Moreover, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)^{-1/2}d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\,dx<\infty,

then the function

V(x)=\rho(x)^{-1/2}=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}

belongs to
L^{1}(\mu)
.
We now verify that
-AV\leq cV
for some
c\geq 0
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By straightforward computations we have

\displaystyle\nabla V(x)

\displaystyle=\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}xV(x),

\displaystyle\Delta V(x)

\displaystyle=\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}[(d+\beta-2)|x|^{2}+d]V(x),

\displaystyle\nabla\log\rho(x)

\displaystyle=-2\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}x.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle-AV(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta V(x)+\nabla\log\rho(x)\cdot\nabla V(x)

\displaystyle=\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}[(d+\beta-2)|x|^{2}+d]V(x)-2\beta^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}|x|^{2}V(x)

\displaystyle=\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}[(d-\beta-2)|x|^{2}+d]V(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\beta d(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}-\beta(\beta+2)(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}|x|^{2}\right]V(x).

Since
(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}\leq 1
and the second term in the square brackets in the right hand side is negative, it leads to

-AV(x)\leq\beta dV(x).

It implies that we can choose
c=\beta d\geq 0
as Lyapunov constant. From the above computations it is also clear that
AV\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
This proves assumption (a) in Corollary
4.1
.

The next step is to show (b). Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. First we observe that, for
i,j=1,\dots,d
, we have

D_{ij}\log\rho(x)=4\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}x_{i}x_{j}-2\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}\delta_{ij},

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. Then, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}D_{ij}\log\rho(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=4\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\xi_{i}\right)^{2}-2\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}|x|^{2}|\xi|^{2}-2\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}|\xi|^{2}.

Given that
(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}|x|^{2}\leq 1
and that the last term in the right hand side of the previous inequality is negative, we deduce that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}D_{ij}\log\rho(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq 4\beta|\xi|^{2}.

Hence, the Hessian of
\log\rho
is uniformly bounded from above on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the constant
4\beta
.

Finally, we check (c). For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x)^{1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{d-1},

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho(x)|\rho(x)^{-1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=2\beta(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{\beta+2}{2}}|x|r^{d-1}.

Therefore, since
\beta>d
, condition (c) is satisfied.

Then the statement follows by inequality (
4.2
) considering that
c=\beta d
and
V(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
p_{\alpha}
be the integral kernel associated with
A^{\alpha}
.
If
\rho(x)=e^{-(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}
and
0<a<2
, then

(4.11)

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{c^{\alpha}t}e^{\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}{2}}e^{\frac{(1+|y|^{2})^{a/2}}{2}},

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\rho(x)=e^{-|x|^{a}}
and
a\geq 2
, then

(4.12)

p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{c^{\alpha}t}e^{\frac{|x|^{a}}{2}}e^{\frac{|y|^{a}}{2}},

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here
C
is a positive constant. Moreover,
c=\frac{1}{2}ad
if
a\leq 2
and
c=\frac{1}{2}a(a+d-2)K^{a-2}
with
K\geq\left(\frac{2(a+d-2)}{a}\right)^{1/a}
if
a\geq 2
.

Proof.

In view of applying Corollary
4.1
, we verify conditions (a), (b) and (c).
In both cases, up to a positive constant,
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx
is a probability measure and
V(x)=\rho(x)^{-1/2}
belongs to
L^{1}(\mu)
.
We now distinguish between the two cases.

Case 1
:
\rho(x)=e^{-(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}
and
0<a<2
.

We check that
-AV\leq cV
for some
c\geq 0
to show that (a) holds. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Easy computations lead to

\displaystyle\nabla V(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}a(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}xV(x),

\displaystyle\Delta V(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}a(a-2)(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-4}{2}}|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}ad(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}+\frac{1}{4}a^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{a-2}|x|^{2}\right]V(x),

\displaystyle\nabla\log\rho(x)

\displaystyle=-a(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}x.

So we get

\displaystyle-AV(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta V(x)+\nabla\log\rho(x)\cdot\nabla V(x)

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}a(a-2)(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-4}{2}}|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}ad(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{a-2}|x|^{2}\right]V(x).

Since
0<a<2
, the first term in the right hand side of (
4
) is negative together with the third one and
(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}\leq 1
. Hence, we deduce that

-AV(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}adV(x).

We then choose
c=\frac{1}{2}ad
as Lyapunov constant.

We now prove (b), namely that the Hessian of
\log\rho
is uniformly bounded from above on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
i,j=1,\dots,d
we have

D_{ij}\log\rho(x)=a(2-a)(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-4}{2}}x_{i}x_{j}-a(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}\delta_{ij}.

Thus, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}D_{ij}\log\rho(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=a(2-a)(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-4}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\xi_{i}\right)^{2}-a(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a(2-a)(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-4}{2}}|x|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\leq a(2-a)|\xi|^{2},

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It leads to condition (b).

Moreover, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we find that

\displaystyle\rho(x)^{1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}{2}}r^{d-1},

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho(x)|\rho(x)^{-1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=a(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{a-2}{2}}|x|e^{-\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{a/2}}{2}}r^{d-1}.

From that it is easy to see that condition (c) is also verified.
Finally, applying Corollary
4.1
, inequality (
4.11
) follows.

Case 2
:
\rho(x)=e^{-|x|^{a}}
and
a\geq 2
.

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

(4.14)

-AV(x)=\Delta V(x)+\nabla\log\rho(x)\cdot\nabla V(x)=\frac{1}{2}a|x|^{2a-2}V(x)\left[(a+d-2)|x|^{-a}-\frac{1}{2}a\right].

Assume that
|x|\geq K
for some
K>0
. Since
a+d-2>0
, we get

(a+d-2)|x|^{-a}-\frac{1}{2}a\leq(a+d-2)K^{-a}-\frac{1}{2}a.

Hence, the quantity in the square brackets in the right hand side of (
4.14
) is negative if we take

K\geq\left(\frac{2(a+d-2)}{a}\right)^{1/a}.

We deduce that
-AV(x)\leq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|x|\geq K
.
For the remaining values of
x
,
|x|<K
, we drop the second term in the square brackets in the right hand side of (
4.14
) because it’s negative, obtaining that

-AV(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}a(a+d-2)K^{a-2}V(x).

We conclude that
-AV(x)\leq cV(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
c=\frac{1}{2}a(a+d-2)K^{a-2}
.
This proves condition (a) in Corollary
4.1
.

We now compute the second partial derivatives of
\log\rho
for any
i,j=1,\dots,d
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

D_{ij}\log\rho(x)=a(2-a)|x|^{a-4}x_{i}x_{j}-a|x|^{a-2}\delta_{ij}.

Then, since
a\geq 2
, we have that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}D_{ij}\log\rho(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=a(2-a)|x|^{a-4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\xi_{i}\right)^{2}-a|x|^{a-2}|\xi|^{2}\leq 0,

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This yields (b).

Lastly, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

\displaystyle\rho(x)^{1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{|x|^{a}}{2}}r^{d-1},

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho(x)|\rho(x)^{-1/2}r^{d-1}

\displaystyle=a|x|^{a-1}e^{-\frac{|x|^{a}}{2}}r^{d-1}.

From that we deduce that (c) holds.
Then inequality (
4.12
) follows by Corollary
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The operator
A
in
L^{2}(\mu)
is equivalent to the Schrödinger operator
B=-\Delta-U
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

U:=\frac{1}{4}\left|\frac{\nabla\rho}{\rho}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta\rho}{\rho}.

Indeed, if we consider the transformation
T:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mu)
defined by
Tf=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}f
for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
B=T^{-1}AT
.
Moreover, computing
B^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<1
via the Balakrishnan representation formula (
2.3
), it turns out that

B^{\alpha}=T^{-1}A^{\alpha}T.

So, the fractional operator
A^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
is equivalent to
(-\Delta-U)^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We also observe that the integral kernel
k_{\alpha}(t,x,y)
associated with the operator
-B^{\alpha}
, is given by

(4.15)

k_{\alpha}(t,x,y)=\sqrt{\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,p_{\alpha}(t,x,y)

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Consequently, applying the results above, we find estimates for the kernel associated with the fractional power
B^{\alpha}
of the Schrödinger operator
B
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
0<\alpha<1
.
For instance, if
\rho(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\beta}
with
\beta>d
, then
A
is equivalent to the Schrödinger operator

B=-\Delta+\beta(\beta+2)\frac{|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}-\frac{\beta d}{1+|x|^{2}}.

Then, by (
4.15
) and Corollary
4.2
, the integral kernel
k_{\alpha}
associated with
B^{\alpha}
satisfies

k_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{(\beta d)^{\alpha}t},

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C
is a positive constant.
Similarly, in the case
\rho(x)=e^{-|x|^{a}}
with
a\geq 2
,
A
is equivalent to the Schrödinger operator

B=-\Delta-\frac{1}{2}a(a+d-2)|x|^{a-2}+\frac{1}{4}a^{2}|x|^{2a-2}.

If we now apply Corollary
4.3
, then we find the following estimate for the integral kernel
k_{\alpha}
associated with
B^{\alpha}

k_{\alpha}(t,x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}e^{c^{\alpha}t}

for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C
is a positive constant and
c
is defined as in the statement of Corollary
4.3
.

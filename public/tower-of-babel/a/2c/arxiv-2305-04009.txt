One-loop formulas for off-shell decay H ∗ → W + ​ W − H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-} in ’t Hooft-Veltman gauge and its applications
KHIEM HONG PHAN, DZUNG TRI TRAN Institute of Fundamental and Applied Sciences, Duy Tan University, Ho Chi Minh City 700000 700000 , Vietnam Faculty of Natural Sciences, Duy Tan University, Da Nang City 550000 550000 , Vietnam ANH THU NGUYEN University of Science Ho Chi Minh City, 227 227 Nguyen Van Cu, District 5 5 , Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam E-mail: phanhongkhiem@duytan.edu.vn Received August 8, 2026 Accepted for publication DD MM YYYY

Abstract
We present analytic results for one-loop radiative corrections to off-shell decay H ∗ → W + ​ W − H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-} in ’t Hooft-Veltman gauge within Standard Model framework. In numerical results, we show off-shell decay rate and its corrections with varying off-shell Higgs mass. The results show that the corrections are of 10 % 10\% contributions to total decay rates. Furthermore, we study the impacts of one-loop radiative corrections to off-shell decay H ∗ → W + ​ W − H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-} in Higgs processes at future colliders. The signal processes such as e − ​ e + → Z ​ H ∗ → Z ⁡ ( W ​ W ) e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow ZH^{*}\rightarrow Z(WW) with including the initial beam polarizations and e − ​ e + → ν e ​ ν ¯ e ​ H ∗ → ν e ​ ν ¯ e ​ ( W ​ W ) e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{e}H^{*}\rightarrow\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{e}(WW) and e − ​ γ → e − ​ H ∗ → e − ​ W ​ W e^{-}\gamma\rightarrow e^{-}H^{*}\rightarrow e^{-}WW are examined. We find that the effects are visible impacts and these should be taken into account at future colliders.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{e^{3}N^{C}_{t}m_{t}^{2}}{(32\pi^{2})s_{W}^{3}M_{W}\Big[p^{4}-2p^{2}(q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2})+(q_{1}^{2}-q_{2}^{2})^{2}\Big]}\times

(13)

2.1
One-loop virtual

H^{*}\rightarrow WW

We turn out attention to the one-loop
amplitude for off-shell

H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}
. All one-loop
virtual, soft and hard bremsstrahlung
contributions are taken into account in
the current calculation. For one-loop
virtual contributions, the amplitude
is written in the form of

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{H^{*}\rightarrow WW}^{1-\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{HWW}\Big\{F_{00,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}\;g^{\mu\nu}+F_{21,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}\;q_{2}^{\mu}q_{1}^{\nu}\Big\}\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(q_{1})\epsilon^{*}_{\nu}(q_{2}).

(14)

Where
\epsilon_{\mu},\;\epsilon_{\nu}
are
polarization vectors
for final
W
bosons.
Since one considers two real

W
bosons in final state,
we have only
F_{00},F_{21}

contributing to the amplitude.
Analytic expressions
for these form factors
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle F_{00,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{00}(p^{2};M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2})=\sum\limits_{G=\{G_{0},G_{1},G_{2}\}}F_{00}^{(G)}(p^{2};M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2}),

(15)

\displaystyle F_{21,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{21}(p^{2};M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2})=\sum\limits_{G=\{G_{1},G_{2}\}}F_{21}^{(G)}(p^{2};M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2}).

(16)

One-loop virtual off-shell decay rates
for

H^{*}\rightarrow WW
are calculated
in terms of the above form factors
as follows. Following kinematic
variables
p^{2}=M_{WW}^{2},q_{1}^{2}=M_{W}^{2}
and
q_{2}^{2}=M_{W}^{2}

are used. For tree-level decay
rates, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\textrm{tree}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{HWW}^{2}\dfrac{\sqrt{M_{WW}^{2}-4M_{W}^{2}}}{64\pi M_{W}^{4}M_{WW}^{2}}(12M_{W}^{4}-4M_{W}^{2}M_{WW}^{2}+M_{WW}^{4}).

(17)

One-loop virtual decay rates
are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\textrm{1-loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{HWW}^{2}\dfrac{\sqrt{M_{WW}^{2}-4M_{W}^{2}}}{64\pi M_{W}^{4}M_{WW}^{2}}\Bigg\{(24M_{W}^{4}-8M_{W}^{2}M_{WW}^{2}+2M_{WW}^{4})\mathcal{R}e[F_{00,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}]+

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+M_{WW}^{2}(8M_{W}^{4}-6M_{W}^{2}M_{WW}^{2}+M_{WW}^{4})\mathcal{R}e[F_{21,H^{*}\rightarrow WW}]\Bigg\}.

2.2
Soft photon
contribution for

H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}\gamma_{S}

In order to regular IR-divergent,
we have to include the soft contribution
which is corresponding to the decay process

H^{*}(p)\rightarrow W^{+}_{\mu}(q_{1})W^{-}_{\nu}(q_{2})\gamma_{\rho}(q_{3})
.
The decay rates for the soft-photon
contributions can be factorized
as follows:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{soft}}=\delta_{\text{soft}}\Gamma_{\text{tree}}

(19)

where
\Gamma_{\text{tree}}
is decay rates of

H^{*}(p)\rightarrow W^{+}_{\mu}(q_{1})W^{-}_{\nu}(q_{2})
at tree-level.
The soft factor
\delta_{s}
depends
on the infrared regulator of photon mass

\lambda
and photon energy cut-off

k_{c}
is given
[
24
]
:

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{soft}}=-\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{\lambda\leq E_{\gamma}\leq k_{c}}\dfrac{d^{3}q_{3}}{E_{\gamma}}\Big(\dfrac{q_{1}}{q_{1}\cdot q_{3}}-\dfrac{q_{2}}{q_{2}\cdot q_{3}}\Big)^{2}=-\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi^{2}}\Big[I_{11}+I_{22}-2I_{12}\Big],

(20)

where photon energy

E_{\gamma}=\sqrt{|q_{3}|^{2}+\lambda^{2}}
. The basic
integrals
I_{ij}
are given

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{22}=(2\pi)\Bigg\{\ln\dfrac{4k_{c}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{\beta}\ln\Big[\dfrac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\Big]\Bigg\},

(21)

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(2\pi)}{\beta}\Bigg[\beta^{2}+\dfrac{2M_{W}^{2}}{M_{WW}^{2}}\Bigg]\Bigg\{\ln\Big[\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\Big]\ln\dfrac{4k_{c}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-2\text{Li}_{2}\Big[\dfrac{2\beta}{1+\beta}\Big]-\dfrac{1}{2}\ln^{2}\Big[\dfrac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\Big]\Bigg\}

(22)

where
\beta=\sqrt{1-4M_{W}^{2}/M_{WW}^{2}}
.

2.3
Hard photon
contribution for

H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}\gamma_{H}

We next consider hard-photon
contribution in this subsection.
The corresponding decay
process is
H^{*}(p)\rightarrow W^{+}_{\mu}(q_{1})W^{-}_{\nu}(q_{2})\gamma_{\rho}(q_{3})_{H}

which
\gamma_{H}
is hard photon
in final state. All tree-level Feynman
diagrams
are plotted in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1
:

diagrams for hard photon contribution

The squared amplitude is shown in
terms of Mandelstam variables

s=(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2},t=(q_{2}+q_{3})^{2},u=(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\text{pol}}|\mathcal{M_{\text{hard}}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{4}M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}(M_{WW}^{2}-M_{W}^{2})(M_{W}^{2}-t)^{2}(M_{W}^{2}-u)^{2}}\times

(23)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\times\Bigg\{80M_{W}^{10}-8M_{W}^{8}(7s+12(t+u))+4M_{W}^{6}\Big[3s^{2}+18s(t+u)+4(2t+u)(t+2u)\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt-M_{W}^{4}\Big[s^{3}+(14s^{2}+16tu)(t+u)+s(25t^{2}+74tu+25u^{2})\Big]-stu(s+t+u)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt+M_{W}^{2}\Big[s^{3}(t+u)+s(s+t+u)(3t^{2}+14tu+3u^{2})-(t^{2}-u^{2})^{2}\Big]\Bigg\}.

The Mandelstam invariants follow

The decay rates are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{hard}}=\dfrac{1}{256\pi^{3}M_{WW}^{3}}\int\limits_{4M_{W}^{2}}^{M_{WW}(M_{WW}-2k_{c})}ds\int\limits_{t_{\text{min}}}^{t_{\text{max}}}dt\;\sum\limits_{\text{pol}}|\mathcal{M_{\text{hard}}}|^{2},

(24)

where

\displaystyle t_{\text{max},\text{min}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigg\{M_{WW}^{2}+2M_{W}^{2}-s\pm\sqrt{\Big(1-\dfrac{4M_{W}^{2}}{s}\Big)\Big[(M_{WW}^{2}-s)^{2}\Big]}\Bigg\}.

(25)

Having all the contributions, the total
decay rate is given

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}}^{\textrm{total}}=\Gamma_{\textrm{tree}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{1-loop}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{soft}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{hard}}.

(26)

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented
full one-loop radiative corrections to
off-shell decay
H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}
in
’t Hooft-Veltman within framework of the SM.
One-loop form factors
for decay process are written in terms
of the PV-functions in the standard
notations of
LoopTools
.
As a result, off-shell decay rates can be
computed numerically by using this program.
In phenomenological results, we have shown
decay rates and one-loop corrections as
a function of off-shell Higgs mass.
The corrections are range of

5\%
to
15\%
for the unpolarized
case for
W
bosons and of

-80\%
to
10\%
for the longitudinal
polarization
case for
W
bosons, respectively.
In applications, we have examined
the impacts of
one-loop corrections to
off-shell decay
H^{*}\rightarrow W^{+}W^{-}

in Higgs processes at future
colliders.The signal processes such as

e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow ZH^{*}\rightarrow Z(WW)
and

e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{e}H^{*}\rightarrow\nu_{e}\bar{\nu}_{e}(WW)

and
e^{-}\gamma\rightarrow e^{-}H^{*}\rightarrow e^{-}WW

are examined. We find that the
effects are visible impacts
and these should be taken
into account
at future colliders.

Phase transitions in one-dimensional Riesz gases with long-range interaction
Rodrigue Lelotte

Abstract
We provide numerical evidence for the existence of phase transitions with respect to the temperature in the one-dimensional Riesz gases with non-singular pair interaction, that is particles on the line interacting via the potential − | r | − s -|r|^{-s} , where s ∈ ( − 1 , 0 ) s\in(-1,0) . Our numerics hint for the existence of two distinct phase transitions whose critical temperatures depend on s s , namely a first transition which separates between a fluid and a quasisolid phase reminiscent of the Berezinski–Kosterlitz–Thouless (BKT) transition, and a second transition below which freezing occurs and the system is in a solid phase. We determine the phase diagram with respect to s s and the temperature T T , which we find to be consistent with the known (or expected) results on the 1D Coulomb gas ( s = − 1 s=-1 ), known to be a solid at all temperature, and the Dyson log–gas ( s = 0 s=0 ) which exhibits a BKT transition at T = 1 / 2 T=1/2 and which is believed to be a fluid at all positive temperature.

2.3
The Dyson log–gas

The
Dyson log-gas

Dyson
;
Dyson
;

;

Forrester

at large

g(r)\underset{r\to\infty}{\sim}\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}\beta r^{2}}\quad&\text{for }\,\,\beta<2,\\
1+\frac{\cos(2\pi r)}{2\pi^{2}r^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}r^{2}}\quad&\text{for }\,\,\beta=2,\\
1+c\frac{\cos 2\pi r}{r^{4/\beta}}-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}r^{2}}\quad&\text{for }\,\,\beta>2\end{cases}

(14)

for some universal constant
c>0
. The expansion is rigorous for
\beta=1,2
and
4

Forrester

Forrester
and
(
Forrester

. We see that the decay of

to an oscillating and non-universal decay whose power depends on the temperature. This is a celebrated example of a

;

;

It follows from the expansion (

(

S(k)\sim 2\beta^{-1}|k|\quad\text{ as }k\to 0.

It also follows from the expansion (

Evidence for the existence of a phase transition

In this section, we investigate numerically the 1D Riesz gases for general exponent

3.2.1
Behavior of

In the preceding section, we were attentive to whether or not the pair correlation

converges to a periodic function in the large

not

as shown previously.

On the other hand, it follows from the extension of a heuristical argument of Forrester

— see also

is crystallized, then

must behave like

in the small wavenumber limit

\eta=1-s
is very natural, as it fits with the Dyson log-gas, for which
\eta=1
, and the Coulomb gas for which
\eta=2
. It also fits with the results of obtained by Boursier
Boursier
in the case where

(

in the case of the log-gas, can be extended as follows for any

\epsilon e^{-ikr}

\rho_{\epsilon}(r)-\rho(r)\sim_{k\to 0}-\epsilon e^{ikr}

where

is the density of the original system. We emphasize that this equivalence is only formal and

not rigourous. It says that the system responds in an appropriate manner to the perturbation, that is in such a way as to cancel the perturbation and remain in equilibrium. In fact, this can be viewed as characteristic of a crystalline order. Indeed the crystal should be able to remain stable under perturbation of large enough wavelength
\lambda\gg a
— or small enough wavenumber

, as in (
17
) — where

is the crystal constant.

Now, by letting

where

is the two-point correlation as defined earlier (
4

\rho_{\epsilon}(r)-\rho(r)\sim-\epsilon\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}W_{\epsilon}(r^{\prime})\rho^{(2)}(r,r^{\prime})\sim\epsilon\beta\frac{1}{|k|^{1+s}}S(k).

(19)

Putting (

4
Conclusion

We provided numerical evidence for the existence of two distinct phase transitions with respect to the temperature in 1D Riesz gases. The first transition corresponds to a BKT transition similar to that of the Dyson log-gas. The second one corresponds to the critical temperature below which the system is crystallized. The transition curves interpolate between the known — or at least expected — phase diagrams of the 1D Coulomb gas and the Dyson log-gas. Although a precise quantitative determination on the critical temperatures is delicate, using a set of different criteria we were able to draw a schematic phase diagram of the 1D Riesz gases with respect to the temperature
T
and the exponent
s
.

Interpolant Existence is Undecidable for Two-Variable First-Order Logic with Two Equivalence Relations
Frank Wolter, Michael Zakharyaschev

Abstract
The interpolant existence problem (IEP) for a logic L L is to decide, given formulas φ \varphi and ψ \psi , whether there exists a formula ι \iota , built from the shared symbols of φ \varphi and ψ \psi , such that φ \varphi entails ι \iota and ι \iota entails ψ \psi in L L . If L L enjoys the Craig interpolation property (CIP), then the IEP reduces to validity in L L . Recently, the IEP has been studied for logics without the CIP. The results obtained so far indicate that even though the IEP can be computationally harder than validity, it is decidable when L L is decidable. Here, we give the first examples of decidable fragments of first-order logic for which the IEP is undecidable. Namely, we show that the IEP is undecidable for the two-variable fragment with two equivalence relations and for the two-variable guarded fragment with individual constants and two equivalence relations. We also determine the corresponding decidable Boolean description logics for which the IEP is undecidable.

Introduction

A first-order (
\mathsf{FO}

For many fragments

and

\psi
in
L

An important consequence of the CIP of a logic

is the projective Beth definability property (BDP) of

: if a relation is implicitly definable over a signature
\varrho
in
L
, then it is explicitly definable over
\varrho
in
L
. The BDP is used in ontology engineering for extracting explicit definitions of concepts or nominals from ontologies

;
ten Cate et al. 2013
;
Artale et al. 2021
, developing and maintaining ontology alignments

, and for robust modularisations and decompositions of ontologies

;
Botoeva et al. 2016
. Explicit definitions are used in ontology-based data management to equivalently rewrite ontology-mediated
queries
Seylan et al. 2009
;
Toman and Weddell 2020
;
Franconi et al. 2013
;
Franconi and Kerhet ????
;
Toman and Weddell 2021
.

Unfortunately, there are many prominent fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
that do not enjoy the CIP and BDP: for example, DLs with nominals such as
\mathcal{ALCO}
and/or role inclusions such as
\mathcal{ALCH}

ten Cate et al. 2013
, the guarded and the two-variable fragment of
\mathsf{FO}

Comer 1969
;
Pigozzi 1971
;
Marx and Areces 1998
.
To extend interpolation-based techniques to such formalisms, it has recently been suggested to investigate the interpolant existence problem (IEP) for logics
L
without the CIP: given formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
as an input, decide whether they have a Craig interpolant in
L
. It has been shown that the IEP is decidable for many DLs with nominals and role inclusions
Artale et al. 2023
, Horn-DLs
Fortin et al. 2022
, the two-variable and guarded fragments of
\mathsf{FO}

Jung and Wolter 2021
, and some decidable fragments of first-order modal logics
Kurucz et al. 2023
. These positive results naturally lead to a conjecture that the IEP for
L
is decidable whenever the entailment in
L
is decidable.

In this paper, we disprove this conjecture by showing that the IEP is undecidable for the two-variable
\mathsf{FO}
with two equivalence relations and also for the two-variable guarded fragment of
\mathsf{FO}
with constants and two equivalence relations. In the realm of Description Logic, the former result means that interpolant existence is undecidable for concept inclusions in
\mathcal{ALC}
enriched with Boolean operators on roles and the identity role. As a consequence, we also obtain the undecidability of the explicit definition existence problem (EDEP) for each of these logics
L
, which is the problem of deciding, given formulas
\varphi
,
\psi
and a signature
\varrho
, whether there exists an explicit
\varrho
-definition of
\psi
modulo
\varphi
in
L
. (It is known
Kurucz et al. 2023
that the IEP and EDEP are polynomially reducible to each other.)

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces the fragments of
\mathsf{FO}
we are interested in and summarises the necessary technical tools and results. Section
3
proves the undecidability of the IEP for the two-variable
\mathsf{FO}
with two equivalence relations by reduction of the infinite Post Correspondence Problem. Section
4
shows how this result can be adapted to the two-variable guarded fragment with constants and two equivalence relations and to suitable description logics. Finally, Section
5
discusses a few challenging open problems.

4
Undecidability of the IEP for Guarded and Description Logics

It is not hard to tweak the construction in the previous section to show that the
\mathsf{GF^{2}2E_{c}}
-IEP is undecidable. Observe that the formula
\psi
defined above can be equivalently represented as a formula in
\mathsf{GF^{2}2E_{c}}
and that the
\mathsf{FO^{2}2E}(\varrho)
-bisimulation between
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
is actually a
\mathsf{GF^{2}2E_{c}}(\varrho)
-bisimulation (in this case, it is actually enough to have one copy of the
R
-cycle with an
S
-chain in
\mathfrak{A}
). The only unguarded conjunct of
\varphi
is
\forall x,y\,\big((X_{0}(x)\wedge X_{0}(y))\rightarrow x=y\big)
, which is needed to generate an
R
-cycle with three points. The same result can be achieved using a guarded
\varphi^{\prime}
with two individual constants

and

as follows:

\varphi^{\prime}(x)=R(x,c_{1})\wedge R(c_{1},c_{2})\wedge R(c_{2},x)\wedge\exists y\,\big[S(x,y)\wedge\bigwedge_{A_{i}\in\Gamma}\big(A_{i}(y)\to A_{i}(y)\big)\big].

We then obtain Theorem
4
with
\varphi^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{GF^{2}2E_{c}}
in place of

and
\mathsf{FO^{2}2E}

Theorem 6
.

The
\mathsf{GF^{2}2E_{c}}
-IEP and

These undecidability results can be interpreted as results about description logics with Boolean operators on roles that correspond to the two-variable fragments

;

\mathcal{ALC}^{\cap,\neg,\text{id}}
2E the extension of the basic description logic
\mathcal{ALC}
with the operators
\cap
and
\neg

in any structure
\mathfrak{A}

for

.

The
\mathcal{ALC}^{\cap,\neg,\textit{id}}\mathsf{2E}
-IEP and
\mathcal{ALC}^{\cap,\neg,\textit{id}}\mathsf{2E}

To obtain a DL version of the undecidability for the

5
Open Problems

This paper presents first examples of decidable fragments of first-order logic, for which the interpolant existence problem and the explicit definition existence problem are undecidable. Numerous questions remain open, including the following:

•

Is the IEP decidable for
\mathsf{FO^{2}1E}
, that is,
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with one equivalence relation? Note that the decidability and
coNExpTime
-completeness proofs for
\mathsf{FO^{2}1E}
are significantly less involved than those for
\mathsf{FO^{2}2E}

Pratt-Hartmann 2023
. What happens if we extend
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with a single transitive (rather than equivalence) relation?

•

Is the
\mathsf{FO^{2}2E}
-IEP still undecidable if one drops equality from
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
? Note that, for
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
without equivalence relations and without equality, the IEP is decidable and the known complexity bounds are the same as for the case with equality
Kurucz et al. 2023
.

•

Is the IEP decidable for
\mathcal{ALCQIO}
? What about
\mathsf{C^{2}}
, that is,
\mathsf{FO^{2}}
with counting?

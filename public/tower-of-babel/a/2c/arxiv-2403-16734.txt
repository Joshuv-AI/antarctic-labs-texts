Anderson Acceleration Without Restart: A Novel Method with n n -Step Super Quadratic Convergence Rate
Haishan Ye, Dachao Lin, Xiangyu Chang, Zhihua Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a novel Anderson’s acceleration method to solve nonlinear equations, which does not require a restart strategy to achieve numerical stability. We propose the greedy and random versions of our algorithm. Specifically, the greedy version selects the direction to maximize a certain measure of progress for approximating the current Jacobian matrix. In contrast, the random version chooses the random Gaussian vector as the direction to update the approximate Jacobian. Furthermore, our algorithm, including both greedy and random versions, has an n n -step super quadratic convergence rate, where n n is the dimension of the objective problem. For example, the explicit convergence rate of the random version can be presented as ‖ 𝒙 k + n + 1 − 𝒙 ∗ ‖ / ‖ 𝒙 k − 𝒙 ∗ ‖ 2 = 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − 1 n ) k ​ n ) \left\|{\bm{x}}_{k+n+1}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\|/\left\|{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{kn}\right) for any k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 where 𝒙 ∗ {\bm{x}}_{*} is the optimum of the objective problem. This kind of convergence rate is new to Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods. The experiments also validate the fast convergence rate of our algorithm.

Introduction

This paper focuses on solving a set of nonlinear equations:

{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})=\left(f_{1}({\bm{x}}),\dots,f_{n}({\bm{x}})\right)^{\top}=\bm{0}_{n},

(1)

where
{\bm{F}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
represents a differentiable vector-valued function with its Jacobian
{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}})
defined by
[{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}})]_{ij}=\frac{\partial f_{i}({\bm{x}})}{\partial{\bm{x}}_{j}}
for all
i,j\in[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
The nonlinear equation problem presented in Eq. (
1
) is prevalent across various fields, including natural and social sciences
[
37
,
4
]
.
For instance, solving a nonlinear equation of the Bellman operator in the infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Process reveals the optimal policy
[
2
]
.
Additionally, identifying stationary points in optimization problems serves as another pertinent example
[
38
]
, underscoring the wide-ranging applicability of solving such nonlinear equation systems.

Given the significance of the problem presented in Eq. (
1
), and considering the inherent challenges in obtaining analytical solutions, a wide array of numerical methods has emerged.
Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods, known for their fast convergence rates, are classical solutions to this problem
[
1
,
5
]
.
Anderson’s acceleration, in particular, boasts diverse applications across computational physics
[
37
]
, computational chemistry
[
3
]
, and reinforcement learning
[
33
]
, attesting to its versatility and efficacy.
Similarly, quasi-Newton methods have been foundational in numerous applications, underscoring their importance in computational analyses
[
38
,
32
,
24
]
. In response to these methods’ critical roles, several variants have been developed to enhance convergence rates or ensure numerical stability, further expanding the scope and effectiveness of these computational techniques
[
13
,
14
,
41
,
25
]
.

It is noteworthy that Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods share a close relationship.
Anderson’s acceleration can be considered a type of quasi-Newton method, specifically referred to as the generalized Broyden’s “bad” (type-II) update
[
12
,
36
]
.
Consequently, the original form of Anderson’s acceleration is also termed
AA-II
.
Viewing from Broyden’s “good” (Type-I) update angle, a variant of Anderson’s acceleration has been introduced, designated as type-I Anderson’s acceleration (
AA-I
)
[
41
,
12
]
.

Gay and Schnabel 1978
demonstrated that
AA-I
is capable of achieving a superlinear convergence rate when equipped with full memory and a restart strategy.
Burdakov and Felgenhauer 2005
proposed a marginally more stable version of full memory
AA-I
, generalizing
Gay and Schnabel 1978
’s restart strategy. Employing a non-monotone line search method allows this method to be proven to converge globally
[
8
]
. More recently,
Zhang et al. 2020
have proposed a stable
AA-I
algorithm with the capability of global convergence. The convergence properties of
AA-II
have been extensively explored
[
20
,
34
,
36
]
. The quasi-Newton method is renowned for its ability to achieve a superlinear convergence rate
[
26
,
19
,
6
]
. Lately, a number of studies have elucidated the explicit convergence rates of quasi-Newton methods
[
28
,
30
,
16
,
21
,
40
]
.

Despite decades of study and application, Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods continue to present unresolved challenges in the field
[
12
,
20
,
19
]
.
One of the unsolvable issues is that
AA-I
suffer an inferior convergence rate due to the obligatory restart strategy
[
13
]
.
The restart causes that
AA-I
can not fully exploit the information lying in the previous
n
search directions.
Thus, a novel
AA-I
without restart, which potentially can achieve an
n
-step quadratic convergence rate, is preferred in real applications.
Furthermore, recent studies have elucidated the explicit convergence rates of various quasi-Newton method variants
[
16
,
28
,
39
]
, and several works try to design novel quasi-Newton methods with faster convergence rate
[
22
,
17
]
.
The similar explicit convergence rates of the proposed novel
AA-I
should be determined.
These open questions underscore the ongoing complexity and dynamism of research in this domain, highlighting the potential for significant advancements and discoveries.

This paper provides affirmative solutions to the previously mentioned problems.
We introduce a novel type-I Anderson’s acceleration method, termed Adjusted Anderson’s Acceleration (
AAA
), that achieves numerical stability for
AA-I
without necessitating a restart strategy.
Additionally, our algorithm
AAA
is capable of attaining an
n
-step super quadratic convergence rate (refer to Definition
2.1
).
We further delineate an explicit super quadratic convergence rate of our
AAA
for both greedy and random versions.
Such a convergence rate represents a novel advancement for both Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods.
Finally, our experiments validate that our
AAA
can achieve a super quadratic convergence rate.

2.1
Notation

We denote vectors by lowercase bold letters (e.g.,
{\bm{s}},{\bm{x}}
), and matrices by capital bold letters (e.g.,
{\bm{W}}=[w_{ij}]
).
We use
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
and
{\bm{I}}_{n}

{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

Moreover,
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denotes the
\ell_{2}
-norm (standard Euclidean norm) for vectors, or induced
2

\left\|{\bm{A}}\right\|=\sup_{\left\|{\bm{u}}\right\|=1,{\bm{u}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left\|{\bm{A}}{\bm{u}}\right\|
, and

\left\|{\bm{A}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}
, where
{\bm{A}}=[a_{ij}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
.
We also adopt
\lambda_{i}({\bm{A}})
be its
i
-th largest eigenvalue if
{\bm{A}}

For two real symmetric matrices
{\bm{A}},{\bm{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

{\bm{A}}\succeq{\bm{B}}

We use the standard

n

Definition 2.1

(
n
-step super quadratic convergence)
.

Suppose a sequence
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}
converges to
\bm{x}_{\infty}
that satisfies

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\left\|\bm{x}_{k+n+1}-\bm{x}_{\infty}\right\|}{\left\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}_{\infty}\right\|^{2}}=q_{k},\mbox{ with }q_{k}\to 0\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

We say the sequence
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}
converges with a
n
-step super quadratic convergence rate.

2.2
Two Assumptions

To analyze the convergence properties of our novel algorithms, we first list two standard assumptions in the convergence analysis of quasi-Newton methods.

Assumption 2.2
.

The Jacobian
{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}})
is Lipschitz continuous related to
{\bm{x}}^{*}
with
parameter
M
under induced
2
-norm, i.e.,

\left\|{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}})-{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}^{*})\right\|_{F}\leq M\left\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\right\|.

Such assumption also appears in
[
16
]
, and the condition in
Assumption
2.2
only requires Lipschitz continuity in the direction of the optimal solution
{\bm{x}}^{*}
.
Indeed, that condition is weaker than general Lipschitz continuity for any two points.
It is also weaker than the strong self-concordance recently proposed by
Rodomanov and Nesterov 2021a
,
Rodomanov and Nesterov 2021b
,
Rodomanov and Nesterov 2021c
.

In addition, the Jacobian at the optimum is nonsingular, shown in Assumption
2.3
below.

Assumption 2.3
.

The solution
{\bm{x}}^{*}
of Eq. (
1
) is nondegenerate, i.e.,
{\bm{J}}_{*}:={\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}^{*})
is nonsingular or
{\bm{J}}_{*}^{-1}
exists.

Assumption
2.3
also appears in
[
11
,
26
]
.
Additionally, along with the classical result on quadratic convergence of Newton’s methods, we need some sort of regularity conditions for the objective Jacobian just as listed in Assumption
2.2
.

2.3
Anderson’s Acceleration and Broyden’s Method

Anderson’s Acceleration.

In the original Anderson’s acceleration (
AA-II
)
[
1
]
, for each
k\geq 0
, one solves the the following least square problem with a normalization constraint:

\displaystyle\min_{{\bm{a}}}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{m_{k}}a_{j}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k-m_{k}+j})\right\|

(2)

\displaystyle\mbox{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m_{k}}a_{j}=1,

where the integer
m_{k}
is the memory in iteration
k
.
Letting
{\bm{a}}_{k}=(a_{k,0},\dots,a_{k,m_{k}})^{\top}
be the minimizer of Eq. (
2
), then the Anderson’s acceleration updates
{\bm{x}}_{k}
as

{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{m_{k}}a_{k,j}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k-m_{k}+j}).

By denoting
{\bm{S}}_{k}=[{\bm{s}}_{k-m_{k}},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k-1}]
and
{\bm{Y}}_{k}=[{\bm{y}}_{k-m_{k}},\dots,{\bm{y}}_{k-1}]
with
{\bm{s}}_{i}={\bm{x}}_{i+1}-{\bm{x}}_{i}
and
{\bm{y}}_{i}={\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{i+1})-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{i})
,
assuming that
{\bm{Y}}_{k}
is of full column rank, then the update of
AA-II
can be represented as
[
12
]

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\left({\bm{I}}+({\bm{S}}_{k}-{\bm{Y}}_{k})\left({\bm{Y}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{Y}}_{k}\right)^{-1}{\bm{Y}}_{k}^{\top}\right){\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k})={\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}).

The above update can be regarded as a quasi-Newton-type update with
{\bm{H}}_{k}
being some sort of generalized Broyden’s “bad” (type-II) update
[
5
,
36
]
.
Thus, the original Anderson’s acceleration is also called AA-II
[
41
]
.

Type-I AA (AA-I) is a variant of original Anderson’s acceleration derived from the perspective of Broyden’s “good” (type-I) method
[
12
]
.
The update of
AA-I
is presented as follows with assuming that
{\bm{S}}_{k}
is of full column rank,

{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\left({\bm{I}}+({\bm{Y}}_{k}-{\bm{S}}_{k})\left({\bm{S}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{S}}_{k}\right)^{-1}{\bm{S}}_{k}^{\top}\right)^{-1}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k})={\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}^{-1}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),

where

{\bm{B}}_{k}:={\bm{I}}+({\bm{Y}}_{k}-{\bm{S}}_{k})\left({\bm{S}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{S}}_{k}\right)^{-1}{\bm{S}}_{k}^{\top}.

Broyden’s Method.

Next, we will introduce the Broden’s methods
[
5
]
.
The so-called “good” Broyden’s method is listed as follows:

\text{Broyden's ``good''}:\begin{cases}{\bm{x}}_{k+1}=&{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}^{-1}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\\
{\bm{y}}_{k}=&{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k+1})-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\qquad{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{x}}_{k+1}-{\bm{x}}_{k},\\
{\bm{B}}_{k+1}=&{\bm{B}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}.\end{cases}

The Broyden’s “bad” method is listed as follows:

\text{Broyden's ``bad''}:\begin{cases}{\bm{x}}_{k+1}=&{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\\
{\bm{y}}_{k}=&{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k+1})-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\qquad{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{x}}_{k+1}-{\bm{x}}_{k},\\
{\bm{H}}_{k+1}=&{\bm{H}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{s}}_{k}-{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{y}}_{k}){\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{y}}_{k}}.\end{cases}

For a better understanding of the update rules of Broyden’s method, we turn to a simple linear objective
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})={\bm{J}}{\bm{x}}-{\bm{b}}
for explanation.
Then, both Broyden’s “good” and “bad” methods have
{\bm{y}}_{k}={\bm{J}}{\bm{s}}_{k}
.
Thus,

{\bm{B}}_{k+1}={\bm{B}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{y}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}={\bm{B}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}},

and

{\bm{H}}_{k+1}={\bm{H}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{s}}_{k}-{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{y}}_{k}){\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{y}}_{k}}={\bm{H}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{J}}^{-1}-{\bm{H}}_{k}){\bm{y}}_{k}{\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{y}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{y}}_{k}}.

We further denote that
Broyden’s Update
as:

Definition 2.4
.

Letting
{\bm{B}},{\bm{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
and
{\bm{s}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we define

\text{Broyd}\left({\bm{B}},{\bm{A}},{\bm{s}}\right):={\bm{B}}+\frac{\left({\bm{A}}-{\bm{B}}\right){\bm{s}}{\bm{s}}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}}.

(3)

Then, for the simple linear objective, Broyden’s “good” update can be written as
{\bm{B}}_{k+1}=\text{Broyd}\left({\bm{B}}_{k},{\bm{J}},{\bm{s}}_{k}\right)
, and the “bad” one is written as
{\bm{H}}_{k+1}=\text{Broyd}\left({\bm{H}}_{k},{\bm{J}}^{-1},{\bm{y}}_{k}\right)
.
For the general non-linear equation, the update of
{\bm{B}}_{k}
in the above equation is a Broyden’s update (Eq. (
3
)) with
{\bm{A}}
satisfying
{\bm{A}}{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{y}}_{k}
.

3
Anderson Acceleration From Perspective of Greedy Quasi-Newton

In this section, we will revisit the Anderson acceleration (type-I) from the perspective of the greedy quasi-Newton.
Then we will show how the
AA-I
process approximates the Jacobian matrix gradually for the linear equation case where the Jacobian matrix is a constant matrix.

3.1
Type-I Anderson Acceleration in Quasi-Newton Form

We assume that the problem (
1
) has a constant Jacobian
{\bm{J}}
.
Then the type-I Anderson acceleration has the following update rule in the quasi-Newton form
[
12
,
41
]

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k+1}=

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}^{-1}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k})

(4)

\displaystyle{\bm{s}}_{k}=

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k+1}-{\bm{x}}_{k}

(5)

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}_{k+1}=

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}}{({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}},

(6)

where
{\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}={\bm{I}}-{\bm{P}}_{k}
with
{\bm{P}}_{k}
being the projection matrix of the range of
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k-1}
and
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k}
are linearly independent.
The update rule in Eq. (
6
) is very similar to the Broyden’s type-I update in Eq. (
3
) but with the
{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}
replaced by
\left({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right)^{\top}
.
Note that
AA-I
is first proposed in the work of
Gay and Schnabel 1978
.

3.2
Convergence Rate of AA-I for Linear Equation

In this section, we will only consider the linear equation case with
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})={\bm{J}}{\bm{x}}-{\bm{b}}
whose Jacobian matrix is a constant matrix
{\bm{J}}
.
For the linear equation case, we will show that the matrix
{\bm{B}}_{k}
of
AA-I
will converge to the exact Jacobian
{\bm{J}}
at most
n
steps if all
{\bm{s}}_{k}
’s are all independent.
First, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Letting
{\bm{J}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
be a Jacobian matrix and AA-I update as Eq. (
4
)-(
6
) with
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k}
being independent, then it holds that

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0},

(7)

\displaystyle\mbox{rank}({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}})=\mbox{rank}({\bm{B}}_{0}-{\bm{J}})-k.,

(8)

\displaystyle({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}={\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}.

(9)

Proof.

Note that, by the update rule of
AA-I
, it holds that

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:an_up}}}{{=}}({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k}-({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}.

Furthermore, it holds that

\displaystyle{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\perp{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k-1}.

(10)

This implies that for
i=0,\dots,k-1

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{i}=({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{i}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{i}}{({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}=({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{i}=\bm{0}.

Thus, we can obtain that

\mbox{rank}({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}})=\mbox{rank}({\bm{B}}_{0}-{\bm{J}})-k.

Furthermore, we can also obtain that

\displaystyle({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}=({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})({\bm{I}}-{\bm{P}}_{k})={\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}.

∎

Based on the above proposition, we can obtain the following convergence property which describes how
{\bm{B}}_{k}
approaches the exact Jacobian
{\bm{J}}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{{\bm{x}}_{k}\}
,
\{{\bm{s}}_{k}\}
and
\{{\bm{B}}_{k}\}
be generated by the
AA-I
(Eq. (
4
)-(
6
)).
If
{\bm{s}}_{k}
is independent of
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k-1}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{n-k}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}\lambda_{i}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right)}\right)\cdot\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(11)

Letting
{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}
be the first vector which dependents on
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}-1}
, then it holds that
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1})=\bm{0}
.

Proof.

First, we consider the case that
{\bm{s}}_{k}
is independent of
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k-1}
.
By the update rule of
AA-I
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:an_up}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}}{({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}}{({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2\mathrm{tr}\left(({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}({\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{tr}\left({\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}}-\frac{2\mathrm{tr}\left({\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:perp}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{tr}\left(({\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}.

Now we try to obtain the lower bound of
\frac{\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}
.
Since
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k}
are independent, by Eq. (
8
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\leq n-k.

Furthermore, by Eq. (
7
) and (
10
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2}}\geq\lambda_{n-k}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right).

It also holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}\lambda_{i}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right).

Therefore, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{n-k}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}\lambda_{i}\left(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k})\right)}\right)\cdot\left\|{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Next, we will consider the case that
{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}
be the first vector which is dependent of
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}-1}
.
By Eq. (
7
), we can obtain that for
i=0,\dots,k^{*}-1
, it holds that
({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{i}=\bm{0}
.
Since,
{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}
is dependent on
{\bm{s}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}-1}
.
Thus, we have
({\bm{B}}_{k^{*}}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}=\bm{0}
which implies that
{\bm{B}}_{k^{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}={\bm{J}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}
.
By the update rule of
AA-I
in Eq. (
4
), we can obtain that
{\bm{B}}_{k^{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}=-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}})
.
At the same time, it holds that
{\bm{J}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}={\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1}-{\bm{x}}_{k^{*}})={\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1})-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}})
.
Combining with
{\bm{B}}_{k^{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}={\bm{J}}{\bm{s}}_{k^{*}}
, we can obtain that
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1})=\bm{0}
which implies that
{\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1}
is a solution of
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})=\bm{0}
.
∎

The property that the matrix

Similar properties also hold for the greedy SR1 algorithm (Refer to Theorem 3.5 of

These properties are because of the update of

Remark 3.5
.

The famous conjugate gradient method can achieve a
n
-step quadratic convergence rate with a restart strategy
[
9
]
, that is, restart for each
n
step.
This property relies on the fact that the conjugate gradient method can find the optimal point of a quadratic function at most
n
steps.
However, the problem in practice is commonly of high dimension, i.e.,
n
is large.
Thus, the conjugate gradient with the
n
-step restart strategy are not relevant in a practical context
[
26
]
.

AA-I
with a restart strategy can achieve a superlinear convergence rate
[
13
]
.
However, due to the restarting,
AA-I
can not full exploit the information in the previous
n

In this paper, we try to propose a novel adjusted Anderson acceleration method which can achieve a
n

4
Anderson Acceleration Without Restart

In this section, we will propose our novel Anderson’s acceleration method, which does
not
require a restart strategy to achieve the numerical stability.
First, we give the algorithm description and list the main procedure of our algorithm.
Then, we will show that our algorithm can find the optimum at most
n
steps for the linear equation problem, which is the same as the
AA-I
.
Finally, we give a convergence analysis of our algorithm for the general non-linear equation problems and provide explicit super quadratic convergence rates of our algorithm, including the greedy and random versions.

4.1
Algorithm Description

In order to design a novel quasi-Newton whose approximate Jacobian
{\bm{B}}_{k}
satisfies the properties in Proposition
3.1
but without a restart strategy, we resort to the update rule of the SR1 algorithm.
Accordingly, we propose a new algorithm named Adjusted Anderson Acceleration (
AAA
), whose update combines the
AA-I
update with the one of SR1.
Given matrices
{\bm{B}}
,
{\bm{J}}
and a vector
{\bm{s}}
, our
AAA
takes the following update rule:

\mathrm{AAA}({\bm{B}},{\bm{J}},{\bm{s}})=\begin{cases}{\bm{B}}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}{\bm{s}}^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})^{\top}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}),\qquad&\mbox{if }({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}\neq\bm{0},\\
{\bm{B}},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(12)

For comparison, the SR1 update is defined as follows for given two positive definite matrices
{\bm{H}}
,
{\bm{G}}
and a vector
{\bm{u}}
,

\mathrm{SR1}({\bm{G}},{\bm{H}},{\bm{u}})=\begin{cases}{\bm{G}}-\frac{({\bm{G}}-{\bm{H}}){\bm{u}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}({\bm{G}}-{\bm{H}})}{{\bm{u}}^{\top}({\bm{G}}-{\bm{H}}){\bm{u}}},\qquad&\mbox{if }({\bm{G}}-{\bm{H}}){\bm{u}}\neq\bm{0},\\
{\bm{G}},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(13)

Comparing Eq. (
12
) with Eq. (
13
), we can observe that they are similar to each other.

AAA
only has an extra
{\bm{B}}-{\bm{J}}
term compared with the SR1 update if
{\bm{B}}={\bm{G}}
and
{\bm{J}}={\bm{H}}
.
Due to this similarity, our
AAA
shares some advantages of the SR1 method such as
AAA
does not require a restart strategy.
However, these two updates have some differences.
For example, in the update of Eq. (
12
), the matrices
{\bm{B}}
and
{\bm{J}}
are commonly asymmetric while the SR1 update commonly requires
{\bm{G}}
and
{\bm{H}}
to be positive definite.

Comparing Eq. (
12
) with the update of
AA-I
in Eq. (
6
), we can observe that our
AAA
replaces
{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}
with
{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}
.
This is because it holds that
{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}={\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}
and
{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}=\left[{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}\right]^{\top}
.
And accordingly, Eq. (
6
) can be represented as:

{\bm{B}}_{k+1}={\bm{B}}_{k}+\frac{({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}\left[{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}\right]^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}\left[{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}\right]^{\top}{\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}\cdot{\bm{P}}_{k}^{\perp}.

Thus, our
AAA
can be regarded as a kind of Anderson acceleration method but with some modifications coming from the SR1 update.

Next, we will propose two methods to choose
{\bm{s}}
.
The first method is choose
{\bm{s}}
greedily such that

{\bm{s}}({\bm{B}},{\bm{J}})=\argmax_{{\bm{s}}\in\{{\bm{e}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{e}}_{n}\}}\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}\right\|.

(14)

Accordingly, we call this method as the
greedy

AAA
.
The second method uses a random vector
{\bm{s}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{I}}_{n})
. Accordingly, we call this method as the
random

AAA
.

With the update rule in Eq. (
12
) and two methods for choosing
{\bm{s}}
, we can obtain the following algorithmic procedure of
AAA
for solving problem (
1
):

\begin{cases}&{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{B}}_{k}^{-1}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\\
&\mbox{Choose }{\bm{s}}_{k}\mbox{ greedily or randomly},\\
&{\bm{B}}_{k+1}=\mathrm{AAA}({\bm{B}}_{k},{\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),{\bm{s}}_{k}).\end{cases}

(15)

The detailed algorithm is listed in Algorithm
1
.
Note that Algorithm
1
is mainly for the convergence analysis.
To obtain the computation efficiency, the inverse update of
AAA
should be conducted as follows:

\begin{cases}&{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{C}}_{k}{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),\\
&{\bm{C}}_{k+1}={\bm{C}}_{k}+\frac{{\bm{C}}_{k}\left({\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right)\cdot\left({\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{C}}_{k}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}+\left({\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{C}}_{k}\right)\left({\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}\right)},\quad{\bm{R}}_{k}:={\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{k}.\end{cases}

Remark 4.1
.

In the real implementation of
AA-I
, the
{\bm{J}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}
is commonly replaced by
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k+1})-{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k})
.
However, in the update of
AAA
, it requires to compute
{\bm{J}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}
.
Fortunately, the computation of
{\bm{J}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}
can be obtained efficiently and its computation cost is almost the same to evaluating
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})
(refer to Chapter 8.2 of
Nocedal and Wright 2006
).
In the implementation of the greedy
AAA
, one has to solve the problem (
14
) which seems requiring to compute the
{\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}
explicitly.
We can use the JL Lemma to reduce the computation
[
23
]
.
We use a Gaussian random matrix
{\bm{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
m=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
and compute
{\bm{U}}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})
.
Note that
{\bm{U}}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})
can also be computed efficiently at the cost about
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
times of computing
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}})
(refer to Chapter 8.2 of
Nocedal and Wright 2006
).
Then, we will find a vector
{\bm{s}}^{\prime}
such that maximizes
\left\|{\bm{U}}({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}\right\|
which can be computed very efficiently.
The property of the JL Lemma shows that it holds with a high probability that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})_{:,i}\right\|\leq\left\|{\bm{U}}(({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})_{:,i})\right\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}})_{:,i}\right\|

[
18
]
.
Thus, it holds that
\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}^{\prime}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{6}\max_{{\bm{s}}\in\{{\bm{e}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{e}}_{n}\}}\left\|({\bm{J}}-{\bm{B}}){\bm{s}}\right\|
.
That is, the greedy direction
{\bm{s}}^{\prime}
computed with the JL Lemma approximation is also a good direction.

{\bm{R}}_{k},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.2
.

Given
{\bm{s}}_{0}\dots{\bm{s}}_{k}
, and
{\bm{B}}_{k}
is generated by Algorithm
1
with a constant Jacobian matrix
{\bm{J}}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0},

(17)

\displaystyle\ker({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}})\in\ker({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}).

(18)

Proof.

If
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
, then by Eq. (
16
), it holds that

\displaystyle{\bm{R}}_{k+1}{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}-\frac{{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}-{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}.

If
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}
, then by Eq. (
16
), it holds that

\displaystyle{\bm{R}}_{k+1}{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}.

Furthermore, for
{\bm{s}}_{i}
with
i=0,\dots,k-1
,

\displaystyle{\bm{R}}_{k+1}{\bm{s}}_{i}={\bm{R}}{\bm{s}}_{i}-\frac{{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{i}=\left({\bm{I}}-\frac{{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}\right){\bm{R}}{\bm{s}}_{i}.

Thus, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ker}({\bm{R}}_{k})\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}({\bm{R}}_{k+1}),

\displaystyle{\bm{s}}_{k}\in\mathrm{Ker}({\bm{R}}_{k+1}).

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Given an initial non-singular matrix
{\bm{B}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, update
{\bm{B}}_{k}
as Eq. (
12
) with
{\bm{s}}_{k}
chosen greedily or independently randomly chosen from
\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{I}}_{n})
.
Then our algorithm finds the optimum at most
n
steps. Letting
k_{*}
be the first index such that
({\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}=\bm{0}
, then it holds that
{\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}={\bm{J}}
. For
k<k_{*}
, the approximate Jacobian
{\bm{B}}_{k}
satisfies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)_{+}\left\|{\bm{B}}_{0}-J\right\|_{F}^{2},

(19)

where
(x)_{+}=\max\{0,x\}
.

Proof.

Letting
k_{*}\leq n
be the first index that
{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}=\bm{0}
,
for the greedy method, we have

{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}=\mathop{\arg\max}_{{\bm{s}}\in\{{\bm{e}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{e}}_{n}\}}\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}{\bm{s}}\right\|.

We also have

0\leq\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}{\bm{e}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq n\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}\right\|^{2}=0,

which leads to
{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}=\bm{0}
, that is
{\bm{J}}={\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}
.
For the random method,
{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}
is a random Gaussian vector.
Letting
{\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}-{\bm{J}}={\bm{U}}\mathbf{\Lambda}{\bm{U}}^{\top}
be the spectral decomposition with
\mathbf{\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|({\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}-{\bm{J}}){\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}\right\|^{2}=\left\|{\bm{U}}\mathbf{\Lambda}{\bm{U}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}\right\|^{2}=\left\|\mathbf{\Lambda}{\bm{U}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}u_{i}^{2}=0,

(20)

where
{\bm{u}}={\bm{U}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}=[u_{1},\dots,u_{n}]
is also a Gaussian random vector.
By the Eq. (
20
), we can obtain that
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{0})
holds with probability
1
, which implies that
{\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}={\bm{J}}
.
Thus, for both the greedy and random version of
AAA
, if
{\bm{R}}_{k_{*}}{\bm{s}}_{k_{*}}=\bm{0}
, then it holds that
{\bm{B}}_{k_{*}}={\bm{J}}
.
Then by the update rule of our algorithm, it holds that
{\bm{F}}({\bm{x}}_{k^{*}+1})=\bm{0}
.

For all
k<k_{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:R_up}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}-\frac{{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{tr}\left({\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})^{2}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}\right)}{({\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k})^{2}}-\frac{2\mathrm{tr}\left({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}\right)}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})^{2}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}-2\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})^{2}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})^{2}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}},

where the last inequality is because of the Cauchy’s inequality, that is,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{s}}_{k},({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}\right\rangle^{2}\leq\left\langle{\bm{s}}_{k},{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k},({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}\right\rangle.

For the greedy method
, at step
k
, since
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{i}=\bm{0}
for
i<k
from Proposition
4.2
.
Thus, at step
k
,
{\bm{s}}_{k}
only as
n-k
possible choices and rank
({\bm{R}}_{k})\leq n-k
.
Without loss of generality, we denote these possible choices as
\{{\bm{e}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{e}}_{n-k}\}
.
Then it holds that
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{e}}_{i}=\bm{0}
for all
i\geq n-k+1
.
Accordingly, it holds that
({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})_{i,i}=0
for
i\geq n-k+1
.
Thus, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}=\max_{{\bm{s}}}\frac{{\bm{s}}^{\top}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}){\bm{s}}}{{\bm{s}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}}=\max_{i\in[n-k]}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})_{i,i}\geq\frac{1}{n-k}\mathrm{tr}\left({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}\right)=\frac{1}{n-k}\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Therefore, the greedy choice of
{\bm{s}}_{k}
leads to

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{n-k}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{n-i}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

For the random method
, for all
k\geq 0
,
{\bm{s}}_{k}
’s are independently chosen from
\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{I}}_{n})
.
Suppose
r_{k}:=\mathrm{rank}({\bm{R}}_{k})\geq 1
, i.e.,
{\bm{R}}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
.
We denote
{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}={\bm{U}}_{k}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}{\bm{U}}_{k}^{\top}
as the spectral decomposition of
{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}
with an orthonormal matrix
{\bm{U}}_{k}
and a diagonal matrix
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r_{k}},0,\dots,0)
.
Denoting that
{\bm{v}}_{k}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})^{\top}={\bm{U}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}
, then we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{s}}_{k}}\left[\frac{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{s}}_{k}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{s}}_{k}}\left[\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}\lambda_{i}v_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}v_{i}^{2}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}\lambda_{i}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{v}}_{k}}\left[\frac{v_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}v_{i}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}\lambda_{i}=\frac{\mathrm{tr}({\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k})}{r_{k}}=\frac{\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{r_{k}}.

Therefore,
{\bm{s}}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\bm{I}}_{n})
leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{s}}_{k}}\left[\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{n-k}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Consequently, by the law of total expectation, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{R}}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{n-i}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\left\|{\bm{R}}_{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Comparing Eq. (
19
) with Eq. (

), we can conclude that both AA-I and our algorithm whose
{\bm{B}}_{k}
will converge to the exact Jacobian at most

However, AA-I method exploits the special property of

-th step and the update in Eq. (

) collapses.

AA-I

In contrast, our method does not have such weakness.
For the general case that the Jacobian is not a constant matrix, if
{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
with
{\bm{J}}_{k}
being the Jocobian matrix at

4.3
n
-step Super Quadratic Convergence for General Non-linear Equation

For the notation brevity, we introduce the following notations:

\sigma_{k}:=\left\|{\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}\right\|_{F},r_{k}:=\left\|{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{x}}_{*}\right\|,c:=\left\|{\bm{J}}_{*}^{-1}\right\|,{\bm{J}}_{*}:={\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}_{*}),{\bm{J}}_{k}:={\bm{J}}({\bm{x}}_{k}),{\bm{R}}_{k}:={\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{k}.

First, the sequences
\{{\bm{B}}_{k}\}
and
\{{\bm{s}}_{k}\}
generated by our algorithm satisfy the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Letting
{\bm{B}}_{k}
and
{\bm{s}}_{k}
be generated by Algorithm
1
, then it holds that

({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}=({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}.

(21)

Proof.

By the update rule in Algorithm
1
, and assuming that
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
, we have

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}=

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}+({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:aaa_up}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}-\frac{{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}}{{\bm{s}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}}{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}+({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}.

If
{\bm{R}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}=\bm{0}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}=({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}+({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:aaa_up}}}{{=}}({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{k}){\bm{s}}_{k}+({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}=({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}_{k}.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

Eq. (
21
) shows that
{\bm{s}}_{k}
almost lies in the null space of
{\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}
. This is the key to the
n
-step super quadratic convergence of our algorithm.
Eq. (
21
) reduce to Eq. (
17
) as
{\bm{J}}_{k}
goes to
{\bm{J}}_{*}
.
However, because of
{\bm{s}}_{k}
does not lies in the null space of
{\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}
, Eq. (
18
) can not hold.
Thus, our
AAA
can not obtain an exact approximation of
{\bm{J}}_{*}
at most
n
steps for the general nonlinear equation.

In the following lemmas, we will show how
r_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}
correlate with each other and these lemmas help to obtain the explicit super quadratic convergence rates of our algorithm including both the greedy and random versions.

Lemma 4.8

Lemma 4.9
.

Lemma 4.11

Proof.

{\bm{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\left\|({\bm{B}}_{k+1}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}\right\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:aaa_up_2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left({\bm{B}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}\right){\bm{s}}\right\|+\left\|({\bm{J}}_{k}-{\bm{J}}_{*}){\bm{s}}\right\|.

Repeating the above inequality from the step
i+1
to
k

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\leq

is because of the greedy method and

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

We consider the following elastic net regression (ENR) problem:

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f({\bm{x}}):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}-{\bm{b}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\mu\left(\frac{1}{4}\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{1}\right),

{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha\mu/2}\left({\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta\left({\bm{A}}^{\top}({\bm{A}}{\bm{x}}_{k}-{\bm{b}})+\frac{\mu}{2}{\bm{x}}_{k}\right)\right),

6
Conclusion

This study introduces a new approach to Anderson’s acceleration, designated as
AAA
, marking a novel iteration of type-I Anderson’s acceleration.
It can also be considered a variation of the Broyden’s good method.
Our algorithm achieves numerical stability without the need for a restart strategy.
Unlike the existing
AA-I
variants and quasi-Newton methods that attain a superlinear convergence rate, our
AAA
exhibits an
n
-step super quadratic convergence rate, for which we detail the explicit convergence rates for both greedy and random versions.
These convergence rates introduce new insights into the theories underlying
AA-I
and quasi-Newton methods, aiding in a deeper understanding of the properties of Anderson’s acceleration and quasi-Newton methods. Additionally, our experimental results confirm the accelerated convergence rate of our
AAA
method, showcasing its effectiveness and potential to advance the field of numerical optimization.

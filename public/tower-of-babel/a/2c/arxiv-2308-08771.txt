Competing Lattice Instability and Magnetism on the Surface of Kagome Metals
Hengxin Tan, Binghai Yan

Abstract
Only a few magnetic kagome materials exhibit lattice instabilities among the large kagome material array. In this work, we find that kagome magnets R R Mn 6 Sn 6 ( R R = rare-earth elements) and their non-magnetic counterparts R R V 6 Sn 6 exhibit intriguing interplay between magnetism and lattice dynamics on their surfaces. Notably, R R V 6 Sn 6 surfaces terminated by kagome layers demonstrate pronounced lattice instabilities, manifesting as 3 × 3 \sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3} and 2 × 2 2\times 2 surface charge orders (SCOs). These instabilities are absent on corresponding magnetic materials R R Mn 6 Sn 6 . Here, the SCO is suppressed by magnetism. Otherwise, surface distortions would significantly reduce the spin polarization, elevating the energy via Hund’s rule. Thus, SCOs are energetically unfavorable on magnetic surfaces. The competition of magnetism and lattice instability is further substantiated by observing SCOs on V-substituted R R Mn 6 Sn 6 kagome surfaces, contrasting with their absence on Mn-substituted R R V 6 Sn 6 surfaces. Our findings reveal unexpected surface instability and profound spin-lattice coupling in these kagome magnets, highlighting the complex dynamics hidden within magnetic materials.

According to Hund’s rule which favors high-spin states in magnetic ions for maximum spin polarization, the reduction in spin polarization leads to increased magnetic energy, which outweighs the kinetic energy loss from structural distortions. As a result, the total energy of the surface is increased, mitigating surface distortions.
For TbV

This mechanism contrasts the scenario for the CDW formation of the kagome magnet FeGe. In FeGe, structural distortions lead to kinetic energy gains and increased spin polarization, reducing more magnetic energy

Relaxation for degenerate nonlinear functionals in the onedimensional case
Valeria Chiadò Piat, Virginia De Cicco, Anderson Melchor Hernandez

Abstract
. In this study, we approach the analysis of a degenerate nonlinear functional in one dimension, accommodating a degenerate weight w w . Our investigation focuses on establishing an explicit relaxation formula for a functional exhibiting p p -growth for 1 < p < + ∞ 1<p<+\infty . We adopt the approach developed in [ 6 ] , where some assumptions like doubling or Muckenhoupt conditions are dropped. Our main tools consist of proving the validity of a weighted Poincaré inequality involving an auxiliary weight.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we focus on the study of nonlinear functionals in one dimension, allowing for a degenerate weight
w
. We aim to provide an explicit relaxation formula for a functional that exhibits
p
-growth for
1<p<+\infty
. More precisely, let us set

\displaystyle F(u)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}w{\rm d}x\text{ \ \ if }u\in AC(\overline{\Omega}),\\
&+\infty\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\hskip 2.84544ptu\in X\setminus AC(\overline{\Omega}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is an open bounded set in
\mathbb{R}
,
w
is a nonnegative, locally integrable function, and
X
is a topological space comprising measurable functions which will be introduced later on. We then delve into its relaxation, aiming to provide an explicit expression of the lower semicontinuous envelope of
F
, denoted as
\overline{F}
.

In the last decades, some works have aimed to study the above functional by considering different functional setups; see for instance,
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
]
. In particular, the attention had been given to the case
p=2
. This choice is canonical as it relates to probabilistic problems since in this case
F
is interpreted as a regular Dirichlet form, guaranteeing the existence of right continuous stochastic processes, as treated in
[
1
,
10
]
. It’s noteworthy that the theory of Dirichlet forms is general, with the natural ambient space being
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, where
\mu\coloneqq w(x)\,{\rm d}x
. However, the association between right continuous stochastic processes and
F
usually requires the validity of a Poincaré inequality. Recent extensions have involved the analysis of weighted Sobolev spaces, incorporating geometric aspects
[
2
,
3
]
.

Since the identification of the functional
{\overline{F}}
is subtle, some authors have been used the density of
C^{1}
-functions in weighted Sobolev spaces as an important tool, see for instance,
[
5
,
6
]
. In this approach, however, some additional assumptions on
w
, as described in
[
5
]
, were necessary. For example, to prove the density of
C^{1}
-functions, it is sometimes assumed that
w
satisfies the doubling or Muckenhoupt conditions
[
9
,
12
]
. Differently, in
[
6
]
, have been adopted the case where such requirements on
w
are not satisfied,
p=2
, and where
X
is not fixed a priori.

Let us now describe our approach to relax the functional
F
that extends to the case
1<p<+\infty
the method used in
[
6
]
. We underline that, a priori, we do not known the space
X
because its precise structure depends on the choice of
w
. Specifically, for a fixed
w
, we then construct a weight
\hat{w}_{p}
to define
X
. This function plays a crucial role in compensating for degeneracy present in
w
, and it permits to characterize the domain of
\overline{F}
and analyze it (see Figure
1
, for a precise example of such a function
\hat{w}_{p}
). In our reasoning, the first step is to prove Poincaré inequalities involving
w
and
\hat{w}_{p}
. Specifically, we consider the
p
-norm of the gradient term of a generic function
u
weighted by
w
, while the
p
-norm of
u
itself is weighted by
(\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1}
. These inequalities are referred to as Poincaré inequalities with double weight. Subsequently, assuming that
w
is finitely degenerate (see Definition
2.1
below), we proceed to choose
X=L^{p}((\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
, and we show that
AC
-functions are dense, in a suitable Sobolev space
W\subseteq X
(see formula (
16
) below). It is important to underline that the space
L^{p}(w)
, and
L^{p}((\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
are not comparable, see Remark
2.7
below. As a consequence, we are able to determine the domain of
\overline{F}
performing the relaxation in the strong topology of
X
. The resulting relaxed functional
\overline{F}
maintains the same form as
F
, but its domain consists of functions that are of
L^{p}((\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
-integrable type. Instead, the case where
w
is not finitely degenerate is still an open problem.

This work is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we study the validity of Poincaré inequalities with double weight, see Theorem
2.10
below. In Section
3
, we formulate and prove our relaxation theorem, see Theorem
3.1
below.

2.
Weighted Poincaré inequalities

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
, and for
1<p<\infty
, we let
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1-\frac{1}{p}
. We define

F(u)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,w\,dx\text{ \ \ if }u\in AC(\overline{\Omega})\\
&+\infty\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ \ \ if }u\in X\setminus AC(\overline{\Omega}).\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
X
is an appropriate set of integrable functions, that will be chosen in Section
2
. Further, let
\overline{F}:\,X\to[0,+\infty]
denote the lower semicontinuous envelope of
F
w.r.t. the topology of
X
. We consider a weight
w:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

(1)

w\geq 0\text{ a.e.},\,\ w\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}).

From now on, it is not restrictive to assume that
\Omega=(a,b)
is a bounded open interval, and we consistently interchange
\Omega
and
(a,b)
throughout the text. We denote by
I_{p,\Omega,w}
the set

\displaystyle I_{p,\Omega,w}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega:\,

\displaystyle\exists\,\epsilon>0\text{ such that }w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\in L^{1}\left((x-\epsilon,x+\epsilon)\right)\Big\}.

The set
I_{p,\Omega,w}
is the largest open set in
\Omega
such that
w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
is locally summable. It is noteworthy that this set has already been considered in
[
6
]
for
p=2
. In this work, we are exploring a
p
-version of the results studied in that work. Without loss of generality, we
can express
I_{p,\Omega,w}
as the disjoint union

(2)

I_{p,\Omega,w}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{{N_{p,w}}}(a_{p,i},b_{p,i}),

with
1\leq N_{p,w}\leq+\infty
. Subsequently, for the sake of a lean notation, we set
a_{i}\coloneqq a_{p,i}
,
b_{i}\coloneqq b_{p,i}
,
N_{w}\coloneqq N_{p,w}
, and
I_{\Omega,w}\coloneqq I_{p,\Omega,w}
. Our objective is to characterize the relaxation of the functional
F
concerning
L^{p}(\Omega,(\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
-convergence, where
\hat{w}
is a suitable weight (see (
4
)). To achieve this, we reintroduce the concept of a finitely degenerate weight, as discussed in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

If
I_{\Omega,w}=\,\emptyset
, we put
N_{w}:=0
.

(ii)

If
1\leq\,N_{w}<\,+\infty
we say that
w
is
finitely degenerate
in
\Omega
.

(iii)

If
N_{w}=\,\infty
we say that
w
is
not finitely degenerate
in
\Omega
.

Furthermore, we define the set

(3)

{\rm{Dom}}_{w}:=\Big\{u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}:u\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(I_{\Omega,w}),\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,dx<+\infty\Big\}\,.

As we will see below, this set must be the core of the relaxed functional of
F
with respect to
L^{p}(\Omega,(\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
-convergence.

Remark 2.2
.

We note that, if
w^{-\tfrac{1}{p-1}}\in L^{1}((a,b)),
then, obviously,
w
is finitely degenerate in

\Omega
with
N_{w}=1.
In this case

{\rm{Dom}}_{w}=\{u\in AC([a,b]):\int_{a}^{b}|u^{\prime}|^{p}w\,dx<+\infty\}

(see
[
5
]
) since
AC([a,b])=W^{1,1}([a,b])
.

Lemma 2.3

(Fundamental convergence)
.

Let
(u_{h})_{h}\subset AC([a,b])
such that

(a)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in{\mathbb{N}}}\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u_{h}^{\prime}|^{p}w\,{\rm d}x<+\infty
,

(b)

for every
i=\,1,\dots,N_{w}
there exists
c_{i}
such that
a_{i}<c_{i}<b_{i}
and there exist finite the following limits

\lim_{h\to+\infty}u_{h}(c_{i})=d_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

Then there exists a subsequence
(u_{h_{k}})
and a function

u:\,I_{\Omega,w}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}u_{h_{k}}(x)=u(x)
for every
x\in I_{\Omega,w}
,

(ii)

u\in{\rm{Dom}}_{w}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u_{h_{k}}^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x
.

Proof.

Let us notice that the proof of this Lemma coincides with the one given in
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
. We only need to notice that
L_{\rm loc}^{p}(I_{\Omega,w})\subset L_{\rm loc}^{1}(I_{\Omega,w})
, and the conclusion of our Lemma follows.
∎

2.1.
An auxiliary weight

In what follows, we construct a suitable weight
\hat{w}_{p}
for
1<p<+\infty
for which it is possible to prove a Poincaré inequality involving
w
and
(\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1}
.
Let
w:\,\Omega=(a,b)\to[0,\infty)
be a function satisfying (
1
) and (
2
). We let
{\hat{w}}_{p}:\Omega\to[0,+\infty[
be defined as

(4)

{\hat{w}}_{p}(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a_{i}^{+}}\left(\int_{x}^{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(y)}\,dy\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }x=a_{i}\\
\left(\int_{x}^{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(y)}\,dy\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }{a_{i}}<x\leq{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}}\\
\left(\int_{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}}^{{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(y)}\,dy\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}}\leq x\leq{{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}}\\
\left(\int_{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}^{x}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(y)}\,dy\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }{{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}}\leq x<b_{i}\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to b_{i}^{-}}\left(\int_{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}^{x}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(y)}\,dy\right)^{-1}&\text{ if }x=b_{i}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0&\text{ if }x\in\Omega\setminus\overline{I_{\Omega,w}}\,.\end{cases}

Remark 2.4
.

Let us notice that
\hat{w}_{p}
heavily depends on
p
. In this part,
\hat{w}_{p}
is defined as the inverse of an integral term, which allows us to use its nice properties, such as continuity, that we used to prove the Proposition 2.4.

In the next, we give an explicit example of the previous function
\hat{w}_{p}
.

Figure 1
.
In the first figure on the left hand side, we have the profile of
w(x)=(1-x^{2})^{2}
for
x\in(-2,2)
, while in the right hand side, we have its associated weight
\hat{w}_{2}
. In this case, we note that
N_{w}=3
.

In the relaxation of
F
, we will consider
L^{p}(\Omega,(\hat{w}_{p})^{p-1})
-convergence. Before providing the precise details of how we relax
F
, let us gather some properties of the functions
{\hat{w}}_{p}
in the following proposition. The proof is elementary, taking the definitions into account.

Proposition 2.5
.

(i)

Suppose that
w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
is not locally summable in
\Omega
, that is,
I_{\Omega,w}=\,\emptyset
. Then
{\hat{w}}_{p}\equiv 0
.

(ii)

For each
i=1,\dots,N_{w}
,
{\hat{w}}_{p}
is constant in
[{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}},{{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}}]
, increasing in
[{a_{i}},{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}}]
, decreasing in
[{{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}},b_{i}]
and absolutely continuous in each interval. In particular, the following hold true:

0\,<{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)\leq\,\sup_{y\in(a_{i},b_{i})}{\hat{w}}_{p}(y)<\,\infty\quad\forall\,x\in(a_{i},b_{i})\,,

\inf_{x\in[\alpha,\beta]}w(x)>\,0\text{ for each $x\in[\alpha,\beta]$, $a_{i}<\,\alpha<\,\beta<\,b_{i}$, }

and
{\hat{w}}_{p}(a_{i})=\,0
(respectively
{\hat{w}}_{p}(b_{i})=\,0
) if and only if
w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\notin L^{1}((a_{i},\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2}))
(respectively
w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\notin L^{1}((\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2},b_{i}))
).

(iii)

We have

(5)

({\hat{w}}_{p})^{\prime}={{\left({\hat{w}}_{p}\right)^{2}}\over{w^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}}\quad\text{ a.e. in }\left({a_{i}},{{3a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{4}}\right)\cup\left({{a_{i}+3b_{i}}\over{4}},b_{i}\right)\,.

(iv)

Suppose that
w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a constant
c>\,0
such that

0<\,\frac{1}{c}\leq\,{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)\leq\,c\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\,.

(v)

Suppose that
w
is finitely degenerate in
\Omega
, that is, (
2
) holds with
1\leq\,N_{w}<\,\infty
. Then there exists a constant
c>\,0
such that

0\leq\,{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)\leq\,c\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega\,.

(vi)

Suppose that
w
is not finitely degenerate in
\Omega
, that is, (
2
) holds with
N_{w}=\,\infty
. Then
{\hat{w}}_{p}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(I_{\Omega,w})
.

Remark 2.6
.

Let us consider the following example already considered in
[
6
, Example 4.7]
. Let us take
\alpha>0
such that
\frac{\alpha}{p-1}>1
. Here, we also observe that if
w
is not finitely degenerate in some open set
\Omega
, then it can also happens that
{\hat{w}}_{p}\notin L^{1}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
\Omega=(0,1)
and let
(a_{i},b_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,+\infty,
be a sequence of disjoint open intervals in
(0,1)
and
m_{i}
be a sequence of positive real numbers which will be fixed later on. Let
w:(0,1)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
defined as follows:

\displaystyle w(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}m_{i}(x-a_{i})^{\alpha}&\hskip 2.84544pt\text{if $a_{i}\leq x\leq\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2}$,}\\
m_{i}(b_{i}-x)^{\alpha}&\hskip 2.84544pt\text{if $\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2}\leq x\leq b_{i}$,}\\
0&\hskip 2.84544pt\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let us fix
a_{i}\leq x\leq\frac{3a_{i}+b_{i}}{4}
. Then by definition of
{\hat{w}}_{p}
we have

\displaystyle{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)=\frac{(\alpha_{p}-1)m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(x-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}-1}}{1-\left(\frac{2(x-a_{i})}{b_{i}-a_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{p}-1}},

where
\alpha_{p}\coloneqq\frac{\alpha}{p-1}
. Let us notice that
0\leq\frac{2(x-a_{i})}{b_{i}-a_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and then

\displaystyle(\alpha_{p}-1)m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(x-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}-1}\leq{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)\leq\frac{(\alpha_{p}-1)m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(x-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}-1}}{1-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\alpha_{p}-1}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\hat{w}}_{p}(x)\cong m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(x-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}-1},\ \ a_{i}\leq x\leq\frac{3a_{i}+b_{i}}{4},

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{3a_{i}+b_{i}}{4}}{\hat{w}}_{p}(x){\rm d}x\cong m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}}.

Hence, if we take the set
\{m_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
such that

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{\alpha_{p}}=+\infty

we get that
{\hat{w}}_{p}\notin L^{1}((0,1))
. An example of function
w
for which the latter series diverges can be given in the following manner. Suppose that for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
b_{i}-a_{i}=\frac{1}{2^{i}}
, and we choose
m_{i}\coloneqq 2^{(i+1)\alpha}
. Since
\alpha>0
, we get that
m_{i}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}>2^{i\alpha_{p}}
, and thus (
6
) holds true.

Remark 2.7
.

We recall another example already considered in

2.2.
A Poincaré inequality with a double weight

In what follows, we derive a weighted Poincaré inequality that we use later on. We first state some preliminary lemmas.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let us consider a fixed
u\in{\rm{Dom}}_{w}
,
i=1,\dots,N_{w}
, and let
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p\prime}=1
. Let us take
\eta,x
such that
a_{i}<\eta\leq x\leq{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}
. The following hold true:

(7)

|u(x)-u(\eta)|\,\sqrt[p\prime]{{\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta)}\leq\left(\int_{\eta}^{x}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p}\,;

(8)

|u(\eta)|^{p}({\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta))^{p-1}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(|u(x)|^{p}({\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta))^{p-1}+\int_{a_{i}}^{x}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\,\right).

Let us take
\eta,x
such that
{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}\leq x\leq\eta<b_{i}
. The following hold true:

(9)

|u(x)-u(\eta)|\,\sqrt[p\prime]{{\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta)}\leq\left(\int_{x}^{\eta}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over{p}}\,;

(10)

|u(\eta)|^{p}({\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta))^{p-1}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(|u(x)|^{p}({\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta))^{p-1}+\int_{x}^{b_{i}}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\,\right).

Proof.

.
By definition of
{\rm{Dom}}_{w}
, and by the immersion of
W^{1,1}(I_{\Omega,w})
into
AC(I_{\Omega,w})
, we also have that
u\in AC_{\rm loc}((a_{i},b_{i}))
. Then for every
x,\eta\in]a_{i},{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}]
such that
a_{i}<\eta\leq x\leq{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}
we have

(11)

\begin{split}|u(x)-u(\eta)|=&\left|\int_{\eta}^{x}u^{\prime}(y)\,{\rm d}y\right|\leq\left(\int_{\eta}^{x}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p}\left(\int_{\eta}^{x}w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\eta}^{x}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{p}w(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p}\left(\int_{\eta}^{{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}}w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\,.\end{split}

Let us noticing that, if
a_{i}<\eta\leq\min\{\frac{3a_{i}+b_{i}}{4},x\}
, then (
7
) follows by (
11
) and the definition of
{\hat{w}}
. Furthermore, if
\frac{3a_{i}+b_{i}}{4}\leq\eta\leq\,\frac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{2}
, since we have that

\left(\int_{\eta}^{{{a_{i}+b_{i}}\over{2}}}\frac{1}{w^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(y)}\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1\over p\prime}\leq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt[p\prime]{{\hat{w}}_{p}(\eta)}}\,,

(
7
) still follows by (
11
) and the definition of
{\hat{w}}_{p}
.
Then, since

|u(\eta)|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(|u(x)|^{p}+|u(\eta)-u(x)|^{p}\right)\,,

by (
7
), we then deduce (
8
). The remaining formulas (
9
) and (
10
) follow by arguing in a similar way.
∎

Theorem 3.1

Proof.

Note that by Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
2.8
, we deduce that
D\subseteq{\rm{Dom}}_{w}
. Furthermore, for every

one gets

u\in W^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(I_{\Omega,w})\cap L^{p}(I_{\Omega,w},({\hat{w}}_{p})^{p-1}),\ \ u^{p}({\hat{w}}_{p})^{p-1}\in L^{\infty}(I_{\Omega,w})\,.

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x\leq\overline{F}(u).

By the definition of

Then, thanks to Lemma
2.3
we get up to extracting a subsequence that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x\leq\liminf_{h\to+\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{h}^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x=\lim_{h\to+\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{h}^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x=\overline{F}(u)

and we are done. To conclude, it remains to prove that

\overline{F}(u)\leq\,\displaystyle\int_{I_{\Omega,w}}|u^{\prime}|^{p}\,w\,{\rm d}x,\quad\forall u\in{\rm{Dom}}_{w}\,

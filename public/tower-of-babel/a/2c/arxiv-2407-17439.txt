Abstract
We are concerned with the long-time behavior of classical solutions to the isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . Our main results and innovations can be stated as follows: • Under the assumption that the density ρ ⁡ ( 𝐱 , t ) \rho({\bf{x}},t) verifies ρ ⁡ ( 𝐱 , 0 ) ≥ c > 0 \rho({\bf{x}},0)\geq c>0 and sup t ≥ 0 ‖ ρ ⁡ ( ⋅ , t ) ‖ L ∞ ≤ M \sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M , we establish the optimal decay rates of the solutions. This greatly improves the previous result (Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 234 (2019), 1167–1222), where the authors require an extra hypothesis sup t ≥ 0 ‖ ρ ⁡ ( ⋅ , t ) ‖ C α ≤ M \sup_{t\geq 0}\|\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq M with α \alpha arbitrarily small. • We prove that the vacuum state will persist for any time provided that the initial density contains vacuum and the far-field density is away from vacuum, which extends the torus case obtained in (SIAM J. Math. Anal. 55 (2023), 882–899) to the whole space. • We derive the decay properties of the solutions with vacuum as far-field density. This in particular gives the first result concerning the L ∞ L^{\infty} -decay with a rate ( 1 + t ) − 1 (1+t)^{-1} for the pressure to the 3D compressible Navier–Stokes equations in the presence of vacuum. The main ingredient of the proof relies on the techniques involving blow-up criterion, a key time-independent positive upper and lower bounds of the density, and a regularity interpolation trick.

1.1
Background and motivation

We study the Cauchy problem of the isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\divv(\rho\mathbf{u})=0,\\
(\rho\mathbf{u})_{t}+\divv(\rho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})-\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\text{div}\mathbf{u}+\nabla P(\rho)=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle(\rho,\rho\mathbf{u})(\mathbf{x},0)=(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}\mathbf{u}_{0})({\bf x}),\ \ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.2)

and the far-field behavior

\displaystyle(\rho(\mathbf{x},t),\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t))\rightarrow(\tilde{\rho},\mathbf{0}),\ \ \text{as}\ |\mathbf{x}|\rightarrow\infty,\ t>0,

(1.3)

for a non-negative constant

. Here

,

\displaystyle\mu>0\ \ \text{and}\ \ 2\mu+3\lambda\geq 0.

There is a huge literature on the global existence (well-posedness) of solutions to the multi-dimensional compressible isentropic Navier–Stokes equations. ]

This assumption concerning the small oscillations on the initial perturbation of a non-vacuum state is recently removed by Huang–Li–Xin

1.3
Strategy of the proofs

We shall now explain the major difficulties and techniques in the proof of our main results. It should be pointed out that the methods used in

) of the density. Motivated by

\frac{3}{s}\geq 6
, then (
3.53
) follows from (
3.55
), Sobolev inequality, and Gronwall inequality.
When
s\in(1/2,3/2]
, then
\frac{3}{s}\geq 2
, so (
3.54
) follows from (
3.55
), Gronwall inequality, and (
3.32
).
∎

4
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

With all the
a priori
estimates in Section
3
at hand, we are ready to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The desired (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) follow from (
3.5
) and (
3.32
), respectively.

To show (
1.11
), in virtue of (
3.32
), there exists a positive constant
T_{9}
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varrho(T_{9})\|_{H^{2}}+\|\nabla{\bf u}(T_{9})\|_{H^{2}}\leq\delta,

which together with (
2.8
) and (
3.53
) gives that

\displaystyle\|\varrho(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\bf u}(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla(\varrho,{\bf u})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{s}{1+s}}\|\nabla(\varrho,{\bf u})\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}}^{\frac{1}{1+s}}\leq C\delta^{\frac{s}{1+s}},\ \text{if}\ s\in(0,1/2],

\displaystyle\|\varrho(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\bf u}(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla(\varrho,{\bf u})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\nabla(\varrho,{\bf u})\|_{\dot{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq C\delta^{\frac{1}{3}},\ \text{if}\ s\in(1/2,3/2].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\varrho(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\bf u}(T_{9})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{6}},

(4.1)

provided that
\delta
is properly small. Combining (
4.1
) with (
3.53
) and (
3.54
), then using a family of scaled energy estimates with minimum derivative counts and interpolations among them developed by Guo and Wang
[
16
]
, we deduce (
1.11
). The proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It follows from (
1.1
)
1
and the definition of
\sigma
that

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\sigma+\gamma\sigma\divv{\bf u}=0.

(4.2)

Multiplying (
4.2
) by
6\sigma^{5}
and integrating the resulting equality over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, one has that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{6}}^{6}=-(4\gamma-1)\int\sigma^{6}\divv{\bf u}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\gamma\int\sigma^{5}\divv{\bf u}\mathrm{d}{\bf x},

which combined with (
1.4
), (
1.8
), (
3.3
), and Young inequality yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{6}}^{6}\right|\mathrm{d}t\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{6}}^{6}+\left\|\divv{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}t\leq C,

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{6}}=0.

This along with (
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\rho-1\|_{L^{6}}=0,

(4.3)

which together with (
1.7
), (
1.8
), (
3.3
), and the interpolation inequality indicates (
1.15
).

On the other hand, thanks to (
3.3
), one gets that

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho({\bf x},t)\leq\inf\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}({\bf x})\text{e}^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\divv{\bf u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau}\leq\inf\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}({\bf x})\text{e}^{C\int_{0}^{t}(\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}})\mathrm{d}\tau}\leq\inf\limits_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}({\bf x})\text{e}^{C(1+t)}=0,

as desired (
1.16
). We complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
\Box

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.3
.
To do so, we start with an algebraic decay rate of the total energy.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
t
such that

\displaystyle\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}.

(5.1)

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
)
1
and (
1.1
)
2
by
G^{\prime}(\rho)
and
{\bf u}
, respectively, summing up and integrating the resultant over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\mu\left\|\nabla{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)\left\|\nabla{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

(5.2)

which indicates that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t<\infty}\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big[\mu\left\|\nabla{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)\left\|\nabla{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big]\mathrm{d}t\leq\int\Big(\rho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+2G(\rho_{0})\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}.

(5.3)

Applying the operator
\Delta^{-1}\divv
to (
1.1
)
2
, we arrive at

\displaystyle P(\rho)=\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})+(2\mu+\lambda)\divv{\bf u}-{\mathcal{R}}_{i}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(\rho u^{i}u^{j}),

(5.4)

where
{\mathcal{R}}_{i}
is the usual Riesz transform on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:
{\mathcal{R}}_{i}=-(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x_{i}}
. Taking the
L^{2}
product of (
5.4
) with
P(\rho)
, we infer that

\displaystyle\int P^{2}(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}=

\displaystyle\int\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\int(2\mu+\lambda)\divv{\bf u}P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\int{\mathcal{R}}_{i}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(\rho u^{i}u^{j})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle~I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

(5.5)

We need to estimate each term
I_{i}\ (i=1,2,3)
. It follows from (
1.1
)
1
that

\displaystyle(P(\rho))_{t}+\divv[P(\rho){\bf u}]+(\gamma-1)P(\rho)\divv{\bf u}=0,

which combined with (
1.14
), (
5.3
), the Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorem (see
[
42
, p. 96]
), (
2.6
), Hölder inequality, and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields that

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})(P(\rho))_{t}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\int\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho){\bf u}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle+\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})(\gamma-1)P(\rho)\divv{\bf u}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+C\big(\|\rho{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\Delta^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})\|_{L^{6}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{3}}\|\divv{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+C\big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\rho{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{3}}\|\divv{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+C\big(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{L^{3}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{3}}\|\divv{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+C\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(5.6)

Similarly,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
can be handled as

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|P(\rho)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

(5.7)

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq C\|\rho u^{i}u^{j}\|_{L^{3}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(5.8)

Hence, substituting (
5.6
)–(
5.8
) into (
5.5
) gives that

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\frac{1}{2}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(5.9)

For
t\geq 0
, choose a positive constant
D_{1}
and define the temporal energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)=D_{1}\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}.

By (
1.8
), (
5.3
), and
\|\rho\|_{L^{1}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})P(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\leq

\displaystyle~C\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\divv(\rho{\bf u})\|_{L^{6}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\|\rho{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(\|\sqrt{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{6}\|\sqrt{\rho}{\bf u}\|^{6}_{L^{2}}+\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{1}}\|P(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x},

and

\displaystyle\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\rho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma-1}}(\sqrt{\rho}|{\bf u}|)^{\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma-1}}|{\bf u}|^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{2\gamma-1}}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\int G(\rho)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma-1}}\|\sqrt{\rho}{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma-1}}\|{\bf u}\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{2\gamma-1}}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma-1}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2\gamma}}^{\frac{2\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(P(\rho),\nabla{\bf u})\|^{\frac{2(\gamma-1)}{2\gamma-1}}_{L^{2}}.

(5.10)

Thus, if
D_{1}
is chosen large enough, one has that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)\sim\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)\mathrm{d}{\bf x}.

Taking a linear combination of (
5.2
) and (
5.9
), and using (
5.10
), we see that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
t
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)+C(\mathcal{M}_{1}(t))^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{\gamma-1}}\leq 0.

Solving the above inequality directly yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}},

as desired (
5.1
).
∎

For
\delta>0
, it infers from (
5.1
) and (
5.3
) that there exists a positive constant
T_{10}
such that

\displaystyle\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}+G(\rho)\Big)(T_{10})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\left\|\nabla{\bf u}(T_{10})\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta.

(5.11)

The following Proposition
5.1
(see
[
32
, Theorem 1.3]
) deals with the large-time asymptotic behavior of global classical solutions.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{T_{10}\leq t<\infty}\big[t\big(\left\|\nabla{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|P\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\big]+\int_{T_{10}}^{\infty}t\big(\|\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|P\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\big)\mathrm{d}t\leq C,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{T_{10}\leq t<\infty}\big[t^{2}\big(\|\sqrt{\rho}\dot{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|P\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\big)\big]+\int_{T_{10}}^{\infty}t^{2}\big(\left\|\nabla{\dot{\bf u}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|P\right\|^{4}_{L^{4}}\big)\mathrm{d}t\leq C.

(5.13)

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
By (
1.1
)
1
and the definition of
F
in (
1.4
), one gets that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\rho+\frac{1}{2\mu+\lambda}\rho^{\gamma+1}=-\frac{1}{2\mu+\lambda}\rho F,

(5.14)

Multiplying (
5.14
) by
\gamma t^{2}\rho^{\gamma-1}
indicates that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t^{2}\rho^{\gamma})+\frac{\gamma}{2\mu+\lambda}t^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}=2t\rho^{\gamma}-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu+\lambda}t^{2}\rho^{\gamma}F,

which implies that

\displaystyle t^{2}\rho^{\gamma}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{2\mu+\lambda}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau=T_{10}^{2}\rho^{\gamma}(T_{10})+2\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau\rho^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu+\lambda}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{\gamma}F\mathrm{d}\tau.

(5.15)

By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau\rho^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+C\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+Ct.

(5.16)

It follows from (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.6
), (
5.12
), and (
5.13
) that

\displaystyle\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{\gamma}F\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+C\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\left\|F\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\left\|F\right\|_{L^{6}}\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{L^{6}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+C\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\left\|\rho\dot{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\rho\dot{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{6}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+C\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau\left\|\nabla\dot{\bf u}\right\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2(2\mu+\lambda)}\int_{T_{10}}^{t}\tau^{2}\rho^{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}\tau+Ct.

(5.17)

Substituting (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) into (
5.15
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|P(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t)^{-1},

which together with (
5.1
) and Hölder inequality leads to

\displaystyle\left\|P(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\|P(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|P(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-1+\frac{1}{p\gamma}},\ \ \text{for any}\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,

as desired (
1.17
).

Next, one infers from (
1.17
), (
5.12
), and Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int\rho|{\bf u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\leq\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\|\bf u\right\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-1}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{1}{3\gamma}},

as desired (
1.18
).
\Box

A heterotic integrable deformation of the principal chiral model


Abstract
A novel classically integrable model is proposed. It is a deformation of the two-dimensional principal chiral model, embedded into a heterotic σ \sigma -model, by a particular heterotic gauge field. This is inspired by the bosonic part of the heterotic σ \sigma -model and its recent Hamiltonian formulation in terms of O ( d , d + n ) (d,d+n) -generalised geometry in [ 1 ] . Classical integrability is shown by construction of a Lax pair and a classical ℛ \mathcal{R} -matrix. Latter is almost of the canonical form with twist function and solves the classical Yang-Baxter equation.

The classical bosonic heterotic string in the Hamiltonian formalism

3.1
Integrability of the undeformed model

As starting point we take the principal chiral model on a group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, embedded into the bosonic heterotic
\sigma
-model. In the language of the previous section, this will be characterised by the components of the Maurer-Cartan form on
G
as vielbein

and
B=A=0
in the generalised vielbein (
2.6
). This corresponds to the generalised fluxes (
2.7
)

{f^{c}}_{ab},\qquad H_{abc}=0,\qquad{F^{\gamma}}_{ab}=0,

(3.1)

where
{f^{c}}_{ab}
are structure constants to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. This corresponds to the following equations of motion

\partial_{+}j_{-a}+\partial_{-}j_{+a}=0,\qquad\partial_{+}j_{-}^{a}-\partial_{-}j_{+}^{a}+{f^{a}}_{bc}j_{+}^{b}j_{-}^{c}=0,\qquad\partial_{-}k^{\alpha}=0.

(3.2)

This model is classically integrable, as the currents
k
and the
d
-dimensional principal chiral model with the currents
j
decouple. Classical integrability is shown by providing a
Lax representation
and a classical
\mathcal{R}
-matrix, proving Hamiltonian integrability, i.e. involution of the charges produced from the Lax representation.

The Lax representation of this model is

L_{+}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\big(j_{+}^{a}t_{a}+2k^{\alpha}t_{\alpha}\big),\qquad L_{-}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}j_{-}^{a}t_{a}\qquad\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)^{n},

(3.3)

where
\lambda
is the spectral parameter.
(t_{a},t_{\alpha})
are generators of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)^{n}
which is defined by

[t_{a},t_{b}]={f^{c}}_{ab}t_{c},\qquad[t_{\alpha},t_{\beta}]=0=[t_{a},t_{\alpha}].

A method to show involution of the associated tower of conserved charges is Maillet’s formalism
[
64
,
65
]
. Central for this is the
Lax matrix

\mathcal{L}(\lambda)=L_{1}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}(L_{+}(\lambda)-L_{-}(\lambda))=\frac{\lambda j_{0}^{a}+j_{1}^{a}}{1-\lambda^{2}}t_{a}+\frac{1}{1-\lambda}k^{\alpha}t_{\alpha}

(3.4)

The aim is to construct a so-called classical
\mathcal{R}
-matrix, i.e. an object
\mathcal{R}(\lambda,\mu)\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{1}}(\lambda,\sigma),\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{2}}(\mu,\sigma^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=\left([\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{12}}(\lambda,\mu),\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{1}}(\lambda,\sigma)]-[\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{21}}(\mu,\lambda),\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{2}}(\mu,\sigma)]\right)\delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle{}\quad-\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{12}}(\lambda,\mu)+\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{21}}(\mu,\lambda)\right)\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}).

(3.5)

A bold subscript indicates the place in the tensor product, e.g.
X_{\mathbf{1}}=X\otimes\mathbb{1}
. A sufficient condition for the involution of the conserved charges constructed from the Lax pair is the
classical Yang-Baxter equation
:

[\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{12}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{13}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})]+[\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{12}}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{23}}(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})]+[\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{32}}(\lambda_{3},\lambda_{2}),\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{31}}(\lambda_{3},\lambda_{1})]=0.

(3.6)

The classical
\mathcal{R}
-matrix for the undeformed model (
3.2
) is

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{t_{a}\otimes t^{a}}{\lambda-\mu}\phi^{-1}_{PCM}(\mu)+\frac{t_{\alpha}\otimes t^{\alpha}}{\lambda-\mu}\phi^{-1}_{chiral}(\mu).

(3.7)

with the so-called twist functions

\phi_{PCM}(\lambda)=\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}},\qquad\phi_{chiral}(\lambda)=1-\lambda.

(3.8)

This is close to the
twist function form

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,\mu)=\mathcal{R}^{(0)}(\lambda,\mu)\phi^{-1}(\lambda)
with a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
\mathcal{R}^{(0)}
, like
\mathcal{R}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{t_{a}\otimes t^{a}}{\lambda-\mu}
and a twist function
\phi(\mu)
. This form is a sufficient condition in order for
\mathcal{R}
to be a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (
3.6
) and a typical assumption in the classification of integrable
\sigma
-models
[
66
]
. From this point of view it is noteworthy that this simple model has no
\mathcal{R}
-matrix of this form. Nevertheless, it is easy to see that (
3.7
) solves the classical Yang-Baxter equation, as
\mathfrak{t}_{\alpha}\otimes t^{\alpha}
is a central element of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, and all its contributions drop out of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (
3.6
).

3.3
The Lax pair

In order to show the existence of a Lax pair

\displaystyle L_{-}(\lambda)=a(\lambda)j_{-}^{a}t_{a},\qquad L_{+}=\big(b(\lambda)j_{+}^{a}+c(\lambda)\epsilon\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\alpha}k^{\alpha}\big)t_{a}+\frac{2}{1-\lambda}\bar{k}^{\alpha}\bar{t}_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle 0=\frac{a+b}{2}-ab-\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon^{2}c+h(a-b)),\qquad 0=-(a-b)+ac.

One should check whether the conserved charges from the Lax pair are also in Poisson involution for arbitrary choices of

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\equiv L_{1}=\frac{1}{(1+\lambda)f_{\epsilon,h}(\lambda)}\left[\lambda j_{0}^{a}+\left(f_{\epsilon,h}(\lambda)+\lambda)\right)j_{1}^{a}-\epsilon\ \lambda(1+\lambda)\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}^{a}k_{+}^{\alpha}\right]t_{a}+\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\bar{k}^{\alpha}\bar{t}_{\alpha}.

(3.18)

A corresponding classical
\mathcal{R}
-matrix is

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{12}}(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{t_{a}\otimes t^{a}}{\lambda-\mu}\phi^{-1}_{\epsilon,h}(\mu)+\frac{\bar{t}^{\alpha}\otimes\bar{t}_{\alpha}}{\lambda-\mu}\frac{1}{1-\mu},\qquad\text{with}\quad\phi_{\epsilon,h}(\mu)=\frac{(1+\mu)f_{\epsilon,h}(\mu)}{\mu^{2}}.

(3.19)

Again,
\mathcal{R}
is almost of the form with twist function and solves the classical Yang-Baxter equation (
3.6
) by the same argument as in the undeformed case. This
\mathcal{R}
-matrix could be guessed relatively easily from inspiration from the form of the
\mathcal{R}
-matrix of the principal chiral model. It is easy to see that the
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
-valued part is reproduced for
\epsilon,h\rightarrow 0
, as then
f_{\epsilon,h}(\lambda)\rightarrow(1-\lambda)
. But, as also for the Lax pair, the full
\mathcal{R}
-matrix (
3.19

\mathcal{R}
-matrix (
3.7
) in the non-deformation limit.

One checks that (
3.19

3.18
) fit into Maillet’s formalism, by showing that for the
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
-valued part both the left-hand and right-hand side of Maillet bracket (

) correspond to

\displaystyle{}\qquad\text{\scriptsize$\frac{t^{a}\otimes t^{b}}{(1+\lambda)f_{\epsilon,h}(\lambda)(1+\mu)f_{\epsilon,h}(\mu)}$}\Big({f^{c}}_{ab}\ \delta(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\ \Big((-\lambda\mu)j_{0c}(\sigma)+(\lambda\mu)\ \epsilon\mathcal{A}^{\gamma}_{c}k_{\gamma}(\sigma)

\displaystyle{}\qquad\qquad-\big[(\lambda+\mu)-\epsilon^{2}(\lambda+\mu+2\lambda\mu)-h(\lambda+\mu+\lambda\mu)\big]j_{1c}(\sigma)\Big)

(3.20)

\displaystyle{}\qquad\qquad+\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{\prime}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\ \Big((\lambda+\mu)-\epsilon^{2}(\lambda+\mu+3\mu\lambda-\lambda^{2}\mu^{2})-h(\lambda+\mu+2\mu\lambda)\Big)\Big).

The part valued in
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{n}\otimes\mathfrak{u}(1)^{n}

4.1
Embedding into heterotic supergravity

The usual question for the integrable models and their deformations in context of string theory is, whether they are also supergravity backgrounds. A detailed discussion is left for future research when a similar analysis to the one in this article has been done with the inclusion of fermions.

This question consists of two parts:
1) When is the undeformed model a solution of heterotic supergravity. 2) Is the
A
-deformation a solution to heterotic supergravity, given the undeformed model is?

The principal chiral model.

In comparison to the type II
\sigma
-models, the heterotic principal chiral model
cannot
be completed a string
\sigma
-model by simply choosing a supergroup with vanishing Killing form
[
67
]
.

The WZW-type undeformed model.

A valid starting point for the deformation in heterotic supergravity would be the WZW-type model, i.e. target space geometry corresponding to the fluxes (
3.11
) with
h=1
,
\epsilon=0
. This is clearly a solution of heterotic supergravity, as it only contains NS-NS fields and is a conformal model. WZW-models have been applied in several circumstances in heterotic string theory, for example for the heterotic string on AdS
3

[
68
]
or compactifications on group manifolds with torsion
[
69
]
. Some of these discussions actually require (chiral) gauged WZW-models
[
70
,
71
,
72
]
. For this, the present approach has to be extended from group to coset manifolds as discussed above.

The deformed model.

One can check that an integrable deformation of such a WZW-model will correspond to a solution of heterotic supergravity, given that the two deformation parameters
h
and
\epsilon
in (
3.11
) are related in a certain way.

For example, one of the equations of motion of heterotic supergravity
[
16
]
– in the frame formalism and for constant dilaton – is:

0=R^{(+)}_{ab}-2\alpha^{\prime}\left(\mathrm{tr}(F_{ac}{F_{b}}^{c})-R^{(+)}_{ac}{{R^{(+)}}_{b}}^{c}\right).

(4.2)

Here,
R^{(+)}
is, as usual, the Ricci tensor of the torsionful connection
\Gamma+\frac{1}{2}H
, where
\Gamma
is the Christoffel connection. The choice of fluxes (
3.11
) means that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(F_{ac}{F_{b}}^{c})

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}\kappa_{ab},\qquad R_{ab}^{(+)}=-\chi^{2}\kappa_{ab}

with the Killing form
\kappa
on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\chi^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(h-1)^{2}(2+(h-1)^{2})
for simplicity of notation.

So far, we ignored dimensions. Only for the following analysis, these are briefly reintroduced. In particular,
[\chi^{2}]=[\epsilon]=\left[\alpha^{\prime}\right]^{-1}
. The heterotic supergravity equation (
4.2
) at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime})
is satisfied, given that deformation parameters are related:

\chi^{2}=\frac{1}{4\alpha^{\prime}}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{16\alpha^{\prime 2}}+\epsilon^{2}}\approx 2\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon^{2}+(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1}\mathcal{O}\left((\alpha^{\prime}\epsilon)^{4}\right).

(4.3)

The other heterotic supergravity equations hold in a similar way. Also, by virtue of the Jacobi identity of
\mathfrak{g}
, the anomaly cancellation condition

\mathrm{d}H=0=\mathrm{tr}(F\wedge F)

(4.4)

is automatically fulfilled for fluxes of the deformed model (
3.11
).

4.2
Non-deformation limit.

As discussed briefly in the main text, the objects of the Lax construction, i.e. the Lax pair and the classical
\mathcal{R}
-matrix do not have the correct non-deformation limit. The
\mathfrak{g}
-valued part of the Lax connection (
3.18
) correctly reproduces the one of the principal chiral model, but the full
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(n)^{n})
-valued Lax connection does not reproduce the full undeformed one (
3.3
). A similar problem also arises for the poles and zeros of the twist function.
Whereas
\phi_{\epsilon,h}\rightarrow\phi_{PCM}
and both have double poles at
0
and infinity,
\phi_{\epsilon,h}=(1+\lambda)f_{\epsilon,h}(\lambda)\lambda^{-2}
has three zeros:

\lambda_{1}=-1,\qquad\lambda_{2,3}=-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\left(2\epsilon^{2}+(1+h)\pm\sqrt{8\epsilon^{2}+(h+1)^{2}}\right)

(4.5)

For an analysis of the
h,\epsilon\rightarrow 0
-limit, a concrete
\epsilon
-dependence of
h=\tilde{h}-\frac{3}{2}\epsilon^{2}
resp. also of
\tilde{h}
needs to be specified. As an example, let us assume that
h=h_{0}
has no
\epsilon
-dependence (so
\tilde{h}=h_{0}+\frac{3}{2}\epsilon^{2}
). Then one of the
\lambda_{2,3}
has the expected
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
-limit:

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=-\frac{1+h_{0}}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{3}{1+h_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\overset{\epsilon,h_{0}\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}-\infty,\qquad\lambda_{3}=\frac{1}{1+h_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\overset{\epsilon,h_{0}\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}=1

(4.6)

Latter gives the expected zero in comparison to the twist function of the principal chiral model, but the role and origin of the additional zero at infinity is not clear at this stage. Let us summarise these issues:

deformed Lax pair (
3.18
)

\displaystyle\nrightarrow\text{undeformed Lax pair \eqref{eq:LPairUndeformed}}

deformed
\mathcal{R}
-matrix (
3.19
)

\displaystyle\nrightarrow\text{undeformed $\mathcal{R}$-matrix \eqref{eq:RMatrixUndeformed}}\qquad\text{for}\ \epsilon,h\rightarrow 0

(4.7)

3 zeros of
\phi_{\epsilon,h}

\displaystyle\nrightarrow\text{2 zeros of $\phi_{PCM}$}.

This result might seem puzzling, as for the known integrable deformations the non-deformation limit gives the expected undeformed Lax pairs, see e.g.
[
11
]
. An explanation for this might be the following: It is well-known in perturbation theory that deformations (or perturbations) of partial differential equations do not necessarily correspond to deformations of the solution space. One might interpret this result in this context, as the Lax pair corresponds to a solution of the system. Nevertheless, this question deserves further attention.

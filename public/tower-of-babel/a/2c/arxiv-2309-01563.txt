Evolution of coherent waves driving a single artificial atom Preprint: APS/123-QED
A. V. Vasenin, Sh. V. Kadyrmetov, A. N. Bolgar, A. Yu. Dmitriev, O. V. Astafiev

Abstract
An electromagnetic wave propagating through a waveguide with a strongly coupled superconducting artificial two-level atom exhibits an evolving superposition with the atom. The Rabi oscillations in the atom result from a single excitation-relaxation, corresponding to photon absorption and stimulated emission from/to the field. In this study, we investigate the time-dependent behavior of the transmitted field and extract its spectra. The scattered fields are described using input-output theory. We demonstrate that the time evolution of the propagating fields, due to interaction, encapsulates all information about the atom. Additionally, we deduce the dynamics of the incoherent radiation component from the measured first-order correlation function of the field.

In this work we study the quantum dynamics of propagating electromagnetic waves interacting with an artificial atom strongly coupled to the field. Due to the atom-field superposition produced from this interaction, the scattered wave carries information about the atomic evolution. Figure
1
(a) illustrates a gedankenexperiment, where a rectangular pulse of a coherent field
|\alpha\rangle
with a carrier frequency matching the qubit resonance traverses an infinite waveguide. After a certain time, it causes photon absorption and excites the qubit. As a result, the pulse amplitude and power decrease. However, the continuing pulse stimulates the qubit to emit its excitation, thus increasing the pulse amplitude and power higher than their initial magnitudes. These processes repeat until the pulse ends. Decoherence causes the modulating oscillations to decay. After the pulse passes, the qubit excitation decays into the waveguide vacuum, producing an exponential tail, a phenomenon discussed in Ref.
Astafiev et al. 2010a
;
Abdumalikov et al. 2011
;
Sharafiev et al. 2021
.

Unlike prior methods
Abdumalikov et al. 2011
that utilized repeated pulse sequences to infer qubit states from post-pulse spontaneous emission, we directly observe stimulated photon dynamics between the qubit and the field during pulse drive, extracting Rabi oscillations, qubit population, and the first-order correlation function. The first-order correlation function and instantaneous power density spectra reveal the dynamics of incoherent radiation and confirm the quantum nature of the observed phenomenon. Notably, as the averaged field oscillations diminish, the field correlation function retains its oscillations, highlighting the interplay between coherent and incoherent fluorescence in a non-stationary regime.

Theory. —

The model Hamiltonian for our atom-waveguide system features a two-level atom symmetrically coupled to the left- and right-moving free field modes of a one-dimensional waveguide:

\hat{H}=-\frac{\hbar\omega_{q}}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\sum_{k=l,r}\Biggl[\int d\omega\hbar\omega\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k,\omega}\hat{a}_{k,\omega}\\
+\hbar g\int d\omega\left(\hat{a}_{k,\omega}\hat{\sigma}_{+}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{k,\omega}\hat{\sigma}_{-}\right)\biggr],

(1)

where
\hbar\omega_{q}
denotes the qubit energy splitting,
l
and
r
indices represent left and right modes, and
g
is the atomic coupling constant to the waveguide field modes.

Experimentally, the right-moving mode field is measured. Per the input-output theory for a tightly coupled atom-waveguide system
Gardiner and Collett 1985
;
Fan et al. 2010
;
Peropadre et al. 2013
;
Lalumière et al. 2013
;
Lindkvist and Johansson 2014
in the interaction picture, the field at the right waveguide output
\hat{a}_{o}(t)
corresponds to the left input field
\hat{a}_{i}(t)
as:

\hat{a}_{o}(t)=\hat{a}_{i}(t)+i\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{-}(t).

(2)

We use IQ-mixers which downconvert the signal and produce two field quadratures:
\hat{V}_{I}(t)=V_{0}(\hat{a}_{o}(t)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{o}(t))/2
and
\hat{V}_{Q}(t)=-iV_{0}(\hat{a}_{o}(t)-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{o}(t))/2
. Here,
V_{0}=\sqrt{G\hbar\omega Z_{0}}
, where
G
represents the amplification coefficient and
Z_{0}
is the waveguide impedance. Together, they define the full output field
\hat{V}_{o}(t)=V_{I}(t)+iV_{Q}(t)=V_{0}\hat{a}_{o}(t)
.

For a coherent rectangular pulse driving an atom, the atom state displays Rabi oscillations at frequency
\Omega_{R}(t)=\sqrt{2\Gamma_{1}}\alpha(t)
. These oscillations, notable during the initial pumping
0\leq t\lesssim 1/\Gamma_{1}
, manifest in the averaged output voltage trace:

\left\langle{\hat{V}_{o}(t)}\right\rangle=V_{0}\left(\alpha(t)+i\sqrt{\Gamma_{1}/2}\left\langle{\hat{\sigma}_{-}(t)}\right\rangle\right).

(3)

From equation (
2
), it can be derived that the energy absorbed from the transmitted pulse in the first half of Rabi period nearly matches the single-photon energy. The same applies for the energy emitted in the subsequent half cycle. Conversely, energy reflected in the left direction is minimal. Analytical calculations are detailed in
Sup
.

While
\left\langle{\hat{V}_{o}(t)}\right\rangle
reveals the coherent part of radiation, the non-stationary first-order correlation function exposes the incoherent component of the field:

G^{(1)}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)=V_{0}^{2}\big(\left\langle{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{o}(t_{1})\hat{a}_{o}(t_{2})}\right\rangle\\
-\left\langle{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{o}(t_{1})}\right\rangle\left\langle{\hat{a}_{o}(t_{2})}\right\rangle\big).

(4)

For a classical coherent wave in a transmission line, without qubit interaction, the average field is
\left\langle{\hat{V}_{o}(t)}\right\rangle=V_{0}\alpha(t)
and
G^{(1)}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)=0
, with
|\alpha(t)|^{2}
being the photon rate. With atom interaction,
G^{(1)}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)
becomes:

G^{(1)}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\frac{V_{0}^{2}\Gamma_{1}}{2}\big(\left\langle{\hat{\sigma}_{+}(t_{1})\hat{\sigma}_{-}(t_{2})}\right\rangle\\
-\left\langle{\hat{\sigma}_{+}(t_{1})}\right\rangle\left\langle{\hat{\sigma}_{-}(t_{2})}\right\rangle\big).

(5)

The first-order correlation function highlights the
\hat{a}
operator property to maintain a coherent state
\hat{a}\left|{\alpha}\right\rangle=\alpha\left|{\alpha}\right\rangle
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
to disrupt coherence
Agarwal and Tara 1991
. When evolution operator
\hat{U}(\delta t)\approx 1-ig\delta t(\hat{a}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{+}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{-})
, assuming
\alpha(t)g\delta t\ll 1
, acts on the atom-field wavefunction
\left|{\Psi_{q}(t),\alpha(t)}\right\rangle
, it preserves the coherent state of the field if

\hat{U}(\delta t)\left|{g}\right\rangle\left|{\alpha(t)}\right\rangle\approx(\left|{g}\right\rangle-ig\alpha(t)\delta t\left|{e}\right\rangle)\left|{\alpha(t)}\right\rangle

(6)

and spoils it if

\hat{U}(\delta t)\left|{e}\right\rangle\left|{\alpha(t)}\right\rangle\approx(\left|{e}\right\rangle\left|{\alpha(t)}\right\rangle-ig\delta t\left|{g}\right\rangle\hat{a}^{\dagger}(t)\left|{\alpha(t)}\right\rangle).

(7)

Therefore, it is expected that
G^{(1)}\left(t,t+\tau\right)\approx 0
at
t=\frac{2\pi n}{\Omega_{R}}
and
G^{(1)}\left(t,t+\tau\right)\neq 0
at
t=\frac{\pi(2n-1)}{\Omega_{R}}
, as confirmed by our experiments.

Conclusion. —

In our experiments, we examined a coherent rectangular pulse evolution as it traversed a 1D waveguide with a strongly coupled superconducting artificial atom. We demonstrated that the artificial atom modulates the pulse field with Rabi oscillations. Our findings align closely with simulations based on the input-output theory expression (
2
). The field spectra were interpreted in terms of transitions between dressed qubit levels. Measuring the non-stationary first-order correlation function
G^{(1)}\left(t_{1},t_{2}\right)
provided insights into emitted incoherent radiation dynamics and highlighted the
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
-operator’s inherent property to disrupt a coherent state
\left|{\alpha}\right\rangle
.

Earlier quantum optical experiments mainly observed the exponential tail post-pulse, which encapsulates the energy of a photon over an extended decay time. Yet, within the pulse, the qubit typically absorbs and emits a photon. Given the swift Rabi oscillations, this photon energy is confined to a briefer span, resulting in power oscillations of greater amplitude than the exponential tail. Thus, beyond quantum optics, qubit state measurements also can benefit from measuring modulation of a driving pulse after interaction with the atom.

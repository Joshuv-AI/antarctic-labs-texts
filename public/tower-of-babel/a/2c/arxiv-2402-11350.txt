Non-Heisenbergian Quantum Mechanics
MohammadJavad Kazemi, Ghadir Jafari

Abstract
Relaxing the postulates of an axiomatic theory is a natural way to find more general theories, and historically, the discovery of non-Euclidean geometry is a famous example of this procedure. Here, we use this way to extend quantum mechanics by ignoring the heart of Heisenberg’s quantum mechanics—We do not assume the existence of a position operator that satisfies the Heisenberg commutation relation, [ x ^ , p ^ ] = i ​ ℏ [\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar . The remaining axioms of quantum theory, besides Galilean symmetry, lead to a more general quantum theory with a free parameter l 0 l_{0} of length dimension, such that as l 0 → 0 l_{0}\to 0 the theory reduces to standard quantum theory. Perhaps surprisingly, this non-Heisenbergian quantum theory, without a priori assumption of the non-commutation relation, leads to a modified Heisenberg uncertainty relation, Δ ​ x ​ Δ ​ p ≥ ℏ 2 / 4 + l 0 2 ​ ( Δ ​ p ) 2 \Delta x\Delta p\geq\sqrt{\hbar^{2}/4+l_{0}^{2}(\Delta p)^{2}} , which ensures the existence of a minimal position uncertainty, l 0 l_{0} , as expected from various quantum gravity studies. By comparing the results of this framework with some observed data, which includes the first longitudinal normal modes of the bar gravitational wave detector AURIGA and the 2 ​ S − 1 ​ S 2S-1S transition in the hydrogen atom, we obtain upper bounds on the l 0 l_{0} .

In fact, there is no position operator that satisfies following natural demands

;

;

The operator satisfying all of the conditions but the last condition is obtained by Newton and Wigner
Newton and Wigner 1949
: An eigen-state of Newton-Wigner position operator in one reference frame, which represents a localized state at a point, is not localized in other reference frames. Hence, the Newton-Wigner position operator have no simple covariant meaning
Newton and Wigner 1949
;
Barr et al. 2023
.
.
Secondly, and more importantly, as shown by Hegerfeldt, strictly localized states lead to faster-than-light probability propagation
Hegerfeldt 1974
;
Hegerfeldt and Ruijsenaars 1980
;
Hegerfeldt 1985
.
This means that, in a self-consistent relativistic quantum mechanics, exactly localized states could not exist

;
Barat and Kimball 2003
. Therefore, the position operator is not a well-defined concept if the eigenstates of the position operator represent exactly localized states. Although exactly localized states are not possible, unsharp localization is possible

;
Krekora et al. 2004
;
Krekora et al. 2004
;
Kazemi et al. 2018

;
Kazemi et al. 2018

Kuramochi and Tajima 2023
;
Busch and Loveridge 2011
. Another motivation for avoiding exactly localized states comes from quantum gravity. Although a complete and agreed-upon theory of quantum gravity is still lacking, various approaches to quantum gravity

The aim of the present work is to show how one can establish such a theory of quantum mechanics without a position operator.
Although we avoid the position operator, (at least unsharp) position measurements are routinely performed in quantum experiments, even in the relativistic regime
Sinha et al. 2010
;
Pleinert et al. 2021
;
Korzh et al. 2020
. Therefore, a complete quantum theory must be able to predict the observed position distributions. In fact, a space-time discription of quamtum phenomena is critical for understanding the relativistic aspects of quantum information processes
Caban et al. 2014
;
Peres and Terno 2004
and addressing some fundamental question in quantum theory

;

;
Wagner et al. 2012
;

If yes, how it could be proven without a reference to a position operator?

;
Bialynicki-Birula and Bialynicka-Birula 2012
.
Our starting point is the modern axiomatic formulation of (generalized) quantum theory in which the notion of observable is not limited to self-adjoint operators; an observable-distribution is given by a positive-operator-valued measure (POVM), not necessary a projection-valued measure (PVM) corresponding to a self-adjoint operator
Masanes et al. 2019
;
Busch et al. 2009
;
Holevo 2011
;
Busch et al. 1989
;
Kunjwal et al. 2014
.
A justification for this generalization is provided by Gleason’s theorem
Busch et al. 1996
;
Busch 2003
, which guarantees that this structure is the most general one compatible with the probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics (for more details, see
Busch et al. 1996
). Another justification, based on an operational approach to quantum theory, can be found in
Masanes et al. 2019
.
In fact, the use of POVMs has been previously suggested to describe various observable distributions
which their description via self-adjoint operators is problematic. Examples include the arrival time distribution
Giannitrapani 1997
;
Hegerfeldt et al. 2010
;
Hegerfeldt and Muga 2010
, work distribution
Roncaglia et al. 2014
, screen observable
Werner 1986
, and so on
Busch et al. 2009
. The aim of the present work is to establish a space-resolved quantum mechanics in which the position probability distribution is given by a proper position POVM at the fundamental level. Although the use of POVMs to describe particle localization has been previously suggested
Busch et al. 1989
, as far as we know, this idea has not been fully extended to systematically develop a complete quantum theory—including the derivation of its consequences on uncertainty relations, time evolution, the spectrum of interacting particle systems, and so on. Note that, in conventional quantum mechanics, the position operator not only determines the position probability density but also serves other roles, especially in canonical quantization where it is used to determine the Hamiltonian operator corresponding to a classical Hamiltonian.
We will discuss how a proper position POVM could be consistently used for this purpose as well.

In this work, our focus is on the non-relativistic regime; however, the results shed light on the subject in the general case. The relativistic generalization will be reported in future works. In this regard, the rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we obtain the general position probability density consistent with Galilean symmetry and the general structure of quantum theory. In Section III, we demonstrate that this general position probability density leads to a generalized position-momentum uncertainty relation, as well as a minimum position uncertainty, denoted as
l_{0}
. In Section IV, we discuss the time evolution in the presence of external potential and construct the associated Schrödinger equation without a position operator. Specifically, we study the simple harmonic oscillator in this framework and obtain an upper bound on
l_{0}
by comparing the theoretical results with experimental data of the bar gravitational wave detector AURIGA. In Section V, we systematically generalize this formalism to interacting multi-particle systems, and compute the spectrum of the hydrogen atom in the presence of minimal position uncertainty. Using experimental data on
1S-2S
transition, we derive a more stringent upper bound on
l_{0}
. In Section VI, we summarize the results and provide a discussion on future prospects.

II
Probability density and Galilean symmetry

According to generalized quantum mechanics
Busch et al. 1989
,
we assume that, for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there is an operator
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
such that the position probability density at
\bm{x}
is given by

\rho_{t}(\bm{x})=\langle\psi_{t}|\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}|\psi_{t}\rangle.

(1)

In the conventional Heisenberg quantum mechanics,
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
is a projection operator constructed out of the eigenstates of a position operator, as
|\bm{x}\rangle\langle\bm{x}|
. We do not consider this assumption. Based on the probability theory, it is not necessary that
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
be a projection operator, it is only needed that
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
be a positive operator,
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}\geq 0
, and the set of
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
be normalized, i.e.
\int\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}d^{3}x=\mathbb{1}
. These conditions make
\{\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}|\bm{x}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{3}\}
a POVM.

Now, let’s find the general position probability density for a free spinless particle consistent with non-relativistic space-time symmetries, including spatial and temporal translations, rotation, and Galilean boost. The space and time translation symmetries lead to

\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x+a}}=e^{-i\bm{\hat{P}}.\bm{a}/\hbar}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}e^{i\bm{\hat{P}}.\bm{a}/\hbar}
, and

\hat{Q}^{t+\tau}_{\bm{x}}=e^{i\hat{H}\tau}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}e^{-i\hat{H}\tau/\hbar},

respectively, which for infinitesimal transformations reduce to

\displaystyle[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{P}}]=-i\hbar\nabla\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(2)

and

\displaystyle[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{H}]=+i\hbar\partial_{t}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(3)

where
\hat{\bm{P}}/\hbar
and
\hat{H}/\hbar
are generators of space and time translation, respectively, and
\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}
is time-dependent position POVM in the Heisenberg picture,
\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}\equiv e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}
. The equation (
2
) could be considered as an alternative for the usual position-momentum commutation relation.
Using space and time translations symmetries, the
\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}
could be written in momentum representation as:

\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}\!=\!\!\int\!\!\ Q(\bm{p},\bm{k})e^{i(\bm{p}-\bm{k}).\bm{x}/\hbar}\left[e^{i\hat{H}t/\hbar}|\bm{k}\rangle\langle\bm{p}|e^{-i\hat{H}t/\hbar}\right]d^{3}\bm{p}d^{3}\bm{k},

where,
Q(\bm{p},\bm{k})=\langle\bm{k}|\hat{Q}^{0}_{\bm{0}}|\bm{p}\rangle
.
Similarly, the Galilean boost and rotation symmetries lead to the following commutation relations, respectively:

\displaystyle[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{K}}]=-i\hbar\ t\nabla\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(4)

\displaystyle[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{J}}]=-i\hbar\ \bm{x}\!\times\!\nabla\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}}.

(5)

Here,
\hat{\bm{K}}/\hbar
and
\hat{\bm{J}}/\hbar
are generators of the boost and rotation transformations in Hilbert space.
Then, using the Lie algebra of the projective representation of the Galilean group

;
Ballentine 2014

5
) and (
4

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{k}})Q(\bm{p},\bm{k})=0,

\displaystyle(\bm{p}\times\nabla_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bm{k}\times\nabla_{\bm{k}})Q(\bm{p},\bm{k})=0,

which together ensure the

|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|

\rho_{t}(\bm{x})\!=\!\!\!\!\int\!f(|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|)\tilde{\psi}_{t}(\bm{p})\tilde{\psi}_{t}^{*}(\bm{k})e^{i(\bm{p}-\bm{k}).\bm{x}/\hbar}d^{3}\bm{p}d^{3}\bm{k},

(6)

where
\tilde{\psi}_{t}(\bm{p})
is momentum space wave function,
\langle\bm{p}|\psi_{t}\rangle
, and
f(|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|)
is an isotropic positive-definite kernel.
The kernel

is also called a radial positive definite function. The characterization of radial positive definite functions is a classical result of I. Schoenberg

A function

(7)

III
Modified uncertainty relation

To facilitate a detailed comparison with conventional quantum mechanics, let us compute the moments of the position probability distribution,
\langle x_{i}^{n}\rangle=\int x_{i}^{n}\rho(\bm{x})d^{3}\bm{x}
. Using the identity

\int e^{i(\bm{p}-\bm{k}).\bm{x}/\hbar}d^{3}x=(2\pi\hbar)^{3}\delta(\bm{p}-\bm{k}),

and eq.(
6
) we find that,

\langle x_{i}^{n}\rangle=(2\pi\hbar)^{3}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{n!(i\hbar)^{j}}{(n-j)!j!}\xi_{j}\int x_{i}^{(n-j)}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}x,

where
\xi_{j}\equiv\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial p^{j}_{i}}f(|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|)|_{\bm{p}=\bm{k}}

\psi(\bm{x})
is defined using momentum wave function as
\psi(\bm{x})\equiv(2\pi\hbar)^{-3/2}\int\tilde{\psi}(\bm{p})e^{i\bm{x}.\bm{p}/\hbar}d^{3}p

\psi(\bm{x})
should not be interpreted as a ”
wave function

cannot be defined. Moreover, in the general case, the position probability density is given by eq. (
6

The normalization condition,
\int\rho(\bm{x})d^{3}x=1
, ensures that
\xi_{0}=(2\pi\hbar)^{-3}
. Moreover, using the fact that
f
is a positive kernel, it is easy to prove that
f(x)\leq f(0)

Wells and Williams 2012
, which ensures that
\xi_{1}=0
and
\xi_{2}\leq 0

l_{0}
is defined as
l_{0}=\sqrt{-\xi_{2}(2\pi\hbar)^{3}\hbar^{2}}
, it is easy to see that,

\displaystyle\langle x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int x_{i}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}\bm{x},

\displaystyle\langle x^{2}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{0}^{2}+\int x^{2}_{i}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}\bm{x},

where
l_{0}
is a positive constant with length dimension.
Therefore, it is clear that,

(\Delta x_{i})^{2}=l_{0}^{2}+\int(x_{i}-\langle x_{i}\rangle)^{2}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}x,

(8)

in which
\Delta x_{i}=\sqrt{\langle x_{i}^{2}\rangle-\langle x_{i}\rangle^{2}}
. Note that, according to the fact that the quantity
\int(x_{i}-\langle x_{i}\rangle)^{2}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}x
can be arbitrarily small, the equation (
8
) ensures that

(\Delta x_{i})_{\text{Inf}}=l_{0}
,
where
(\Delta x_{i})_{\text{Inf}}=\text{Inf}\{\Delta^{\psi}x_{i}|\ |\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\}
.

It should be noted that the assumption that an arbitrary sharp localized state exists,
l_{0}=0
, determines the kernels
f
uniquely as a constant function
f(|\bm{p}-\bm{k}|)=f(0)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-3}

Kijowski 1974

6

\rho=|\psi|^{2}
. Moreover, if

, the equation (
7

\bm{J}=\frac{\hbar}{m}\Im[\psi^{*}\nabla\psi]
. In fact, in this case, the current formalism reduces to the standard Heisenberg quantum theory.

The existence of a minimum position uncertainty modifies the usual position-momentum uncertainty relation.
Using properties of Fourier transformation, we know that

\int(x_{i}-\langle x_{i}\rangle)^{2}|\psi(\bm{x})|^{2}d^{3}x\int(p_{i}-\langle p_{i}\rangle)^{2}|\tilde{\psi}(\bm{p})|^{2}d^{3}p\geq\hbar^{2}/4,

which, together with (
8

\Delta x_{i}\Delta p_{i}\geq\sqrt{\hbar^{2}/4+l_{0}^{2}(\Delta p_{i})^{2}}.

(9)

This modified uncertainty relation is the same as the common generalized uncertainty relation at the first order,
\Delta x_{i}\Delta p_{i}\geq\hbar/2(1+2l_{0}^{2}(\Delta p_{i})^{2}/\hbar^{2})
, which appears in a wide range of approaches to quantum gravity
Amati et al. 1989
;
Maggiore 1993a
;
Scardigli 1999
;
Das and Vagenas 2008
;
Hossenfelder 2013
.
Often, such modification is seen as a manifestation of a deformed canonical commutator
Maggiore 1993b
;
Kempf et al. 1995
;
Fadel and Maggiore 2022
which has been related to a deformation of space-time symmetry
Fadel and Maggiore 2022
;
Maggiore 1994
;
Bosso et al. 2023a
. However, it is important to emphasize that we did not assume any modified symmetry or deformed commutation relation. The general form of quantum probability density (
6
) and the modified uncertainty relation (
9
) are derived from the Galilean symmetry.

In the quantum gravity literature, it is often assumed that the value of
l_{0}
is near the Planck scale (
l_{p}=10^{-35}
m). Nonetheless, it is worth mentioning that some theories with varying gravitational coupling, such as those with additional dimensions
Arkani-Hamed et al. 1998

Antoniadis et al. 1998

Hossenfelder et al. 2003

l_{0}
is a free parameter, and we do not presuppose any specific value for it. In principle, the value of
l_{0}
should be determined from a direct comparison of the theoretical result with experimental data
Pikovski et al. 2012

IV
Single particle in an external potential

To find an experimental bound on
l_{0}
, we need to extend the model to non-free particles. Firstly, we consider a single particle in the presence of an external potential and then the model is extended for an interacting many-particle system. For a particle in an external potential, we assume that the position probability density is given by a POVM as
\rho_{t}(\bm{x})=\langle\psi_{t}|\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}|\psi_{t}\rangle
, and the time evolution of the state is given by the following Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi\rangle=\hat{H}|\psi\rangle,

(10)

where
\hat{H}=\hat{\bm{P}}^{2}/2m+\hat{V}
and the potential operator, associated with the classical scalar potential
V(\bm{x})

\hat{V}=\int V(\bm{x})\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}d^{3}x.

(11)

Note that, when
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
are projection operators, this definition reduces to the standard potential operator, i.e.,
\hat{V}=\int V(\bm{x})|\bm{x}\rangle\langle\bm{x}|d^{3}x

l_{0}\neq 0

6

\langle\psi|\hat{V}|\psi\rangle=\int\rho^{\psi}(\bm{x})V(\bm{x})d^{3}x.

(12)

11

It is easy to show that, the same as in the free particle case, the Galilean symmetry implies that

, an experimental limit for the modal energy, i.e., the right-hand side of (

), has been reported by analyzing the residual motion of the first longitudinal mode of the AURIGA gravitational wave bar detector, which is cooled down to
sub-millikelvin
Vinante et al. 2008
.
The measured modal energy,
E_{\text{exp}}\!=\!1.3\!\times\!10^{26}
J,
leads to an upper limit for the minimal position uncertainty as
l_{0}<3.8\times 10^{16}l_{P}

Marin et al. 2013
.
Nevertheless, it should be noted that the direct application of the one-particle theory to the center of mass of a macroscopic object with a very large mass, such as the AURIGA detector with
m=10^{13}M_{p}
, may be questionable—at least, the value of
l_{0}
may not be same for all objects
Bosso et al. 2023b
;
Chevalier et al. 2022
. In principle, for a more accurate consideration, a multi-particle extension of our framework is needed. In the next section, we will develop such an extension, and based on it, we obtain a more justifiable upper limit on
l_{0}
using the observed Hydrogen atom spectrum.

V
Interacting particles system

In this section, we extend the theory to multi-particle systems. For simplicity, we only consider a two-particle system; however, extending the results to an
N
-particle system is straightforward. The Hilbert space of the system is considered, as usual, as the tensor product of one-particle Hilbert spaces. The probability density of a two-particle system in configuration space is given by

\rho_{t}(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})=\langle\psi|\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}|\psi\rangle,

(17)

where,
\{\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}|(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}\}
is a POVM in the two-particle Hilbert space. Similar to the single-particle theory, the non-relativistic space-time symmetries lead to the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle\left[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{H}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+i\hbar\partial_{t}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(18)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{P}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{i}}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(19)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{K}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\ t\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_{\bm{x}_{i}}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(20)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},\hat{\bm{J}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{x}_{i}\!\times\!\nabla_{\bm{x}_{i}}\hat{Q}^{t}_{\bm{x}},

(21)

where,
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})
. It is easy to check that the above commutation relations lead to

\langle\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}|\hat{Q_{\bm{0}}^{0}}|\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}\rangle=f(|\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{k}_{1}|,|\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{k}_{2}|)
,
where
f
is a positive semi-definite kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
. If, as in usual quantum theory, we assume that the separable multi-particle states lead to a separable two-particle probability density—i.e.,
|\psi\rangle=\prod_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\Rightarrow\rho(\bm{x})=\prod_{i}\rho_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
—then, we have

f(|\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{k}_{1}|,|\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{k}_{2}|)=f_{1}(|\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{k}_{1}|)f_{2}(|\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{k}_{2}|),

where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are radial-positive-kernels in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therefore, the two-particle probability density in configuration space is given by

\rho_{t}(\bm{x})\!=\!\!\!\!\int\!\prod_{i}f_{i}(|\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{k}_{i}|)\tilde{\psi}_{t}(\bm{p})\tilde{\psi}_{t}^{*}(\bm{k})e^{i(\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{k}_{i}).\bm{x}_{i}/\hbar}d^{3}\bm{p}_{i}d^{3}\bm{k}_{i},

where
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})
,
\bm{p}=(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})
, and
\bm{k}=(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2})
. Note that we consider a system made of two different particles, which, in principle, could have different quantum behaviors characterized by different kernels.

We assume that the time evolution of the state is given by the Schrödinger equation,

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi\rangle=\hat{H}|\psi\rangle
, in which
\hat{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\hat{\bm{P}}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}+\hat{V}
and the potential operator
\hat{V}
, associated with the classical potential
V(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})
, is defined as follows:

\hat{V}=\int V(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}d^{3}\bm{x}_{1}d^{3}\bm{x}_{2}.

Note that, when
\hat{Q}_{\bm{x}}
are projection operators, this definition reduces to the standard potential operator—i.e.,
\hat{V}=\int V({\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}})|{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}\rangle\langle{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}|d^{3}x_{1}d^{3}x_{2}
.
The Galilean symmetry limits the possible forms of the interaction potential—from
[\hat{\bm{K}},\hat{H}]=i\hbar\hat{\bm{P}}
and Eq.(
20
), or from
[\hat{\bm{P}},\hat{H}]=0
and Eq.(
19
), it is easy to see that,

(\nabla_{\bm{x}_{1}}+\nabla_{\bm{x}_{2}})V(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})=0.

(22)

Moreover,
[\hat{\bm{J}},\hat{H}]=0
together with Eq.(
21
) lead to

(\bm{x}_{1}\times\nabla_{\bm{x}_{1}}+\bm{x}_{2}\times\nabla_{\bm{x}_{2}})V(\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2})=0.

(23)

The equations (
22
) and (
23
) imply that the potential must be a function of
|\bm{x}_{2}-\bm{x}_{1}|
, which is the same condition that arises from Galilean symmetry in conventional quantum mechanics
Weinberg 2015
.

As a crucial example, we investigate the energy levels of the hydrogen atom within the introduced framework. The hydrogen atom may be considered as a two-particle system with interaction potential
V(|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|)=-e^{2}/4\pi\epsilon_{0}|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|
. In momentum representation, the energy eigenvalue problem
E|\Psi\rangle=\hat{H}|\Psi\rangle
can be written as:

\displaystyle E\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{1}}{2m_{1}}+\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{2}}{2m_{2}}\right)\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int\tilde{U}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{k}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{k}_{2})\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2})d^{3}\bm{k}_{1}d^{3}\bm{k}_{2},

where,
\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})=\langle\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}|\Psi\rangle
, and

\tilde{U}\!\!=\!\!f_{1}(|\bm{p}_{1}|)f_{2}(|\bm{p}_{2}|)\!\!\!\int\!\!V(|\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}|)e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}(\bm{p}_{1}.\bm{x}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2}.\bm{x}_{2})}d^{3}\bm{x}_{1}d^{3}\bm{x}_{2}.

It is well-known that, in the case of two interacting particles, working with the total momentum
\bm{p}_{c}=\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2}
and the relative momentum
\bm{p}_{r}=(m_{1}\bm{p}_{2}-m_{2}\bm{p}_{1})/(m_{1}+m_{2})
is useful.
After separation of the external and internal degrees of freedom,
\tilde{\Psi}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})=\tilde{\psi}_{c}(\bm{p}_{c})\tilde{\psi}_{r}(\bm{p}_{r})
, the two-body problem can be split into

E_{c}\psi_{c}(\bm{p}_{c})=\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{c}}{2M}\psi_{c}(\bm{p}_{c})
, and

E_{r}\tilde{\psi}_{r}(\bm{p}_{r})=\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{r}}{2\mu}\tilde{\psi}_{r}(\bm{p}_{r})+\int\tilde{U}_{r}(\bm{p}_{r}-\bm{k}_{r})\tilde{\psi}_{r}(\bm{k}_{r})d^{3}\bm{k}_{r},

(25)

where
\tilde{U}_{r}(\bm{p})=(2\pi\hbar)^{3}f_{1}(|\bm{p}|)f_{2}(|\bm{p}|)\int V(|\bm{x}|)e^{i\bm{p}.\bm{r}/\hbar}d^{3}\bm{r}
,

E=E_{c}+E_{r}
,
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
,
\mu=m_{1}m_{2}/(m_{1}+m_{2})
, and
\bm{r}
represent the relative coordinate,
\bm{x}_{2}-\bm{x}_{1}
. Using the Taylor expansion of
f_{i}
, in the first-order approximation, Eq.(
25
) reduces to

\displaystyle E\psi_{r}(\bm{r})\!=\!-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\!\nabla^{2}\psi_{r}(\bm{r})\!-(1+\frac{l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2}}{2}\nabla^{2})V(r)\psi_{r}(\bm{r}),

where

\psi_{r}(\bm{r})=(2\pi\hbar)^{-3/2}\int\tilde{\psi}_{r}(\bm{p})e^{i\bm{p}.\bm{r}/\hbar}d^{3}p
,
and
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are minimal position uncertainty of the particles, which are given by
l_{i}^{2}=(2\pi\hbar)^{3}\hbar^{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0)
.
Applying the usual perturbation theory, the leading corrections to the
S
-energy levels are given by

\Delta E_{n}=-\frac{e^{2}}{2a_{0}n^{2}}\frac{16\pi(l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2})}{na_{0}^{2}},

in which
a_{0}=4\pi\epsilon_{0}\hbar^{2}/\mu e^{2}
is the Bohr radius. Comparing this result with the high-precision experimental data of hydrogen atom spectroscopy can lead to an upper bound for the minimal position uncertainties. To achieve this, we focus on the extremely narrow
1S-2S
two-photon transition. In recent years, the frequency of the
1S-2S
transition has been accurately measured using a cesium clock with a relative uncertainty of
4.5\times 10^{-15}

Matveev et al. 2013
;
Parthey et al. 2011
.
This relative uncertainty leads to the following upper bound on the minimal position uncertainties:

l_{i}\leq\sqrt{l_{1}^{2}+l_{2}^{2}}\leq 2.8\times 10^{16}l_{p},

which is in agreement with the upper bound obtained in the previous section using AURIGA data.

Finally, it is important to remark that the corrected energy levels in our framework differ from the ones in the deformed commutation relation models
Stetsko and Tkachuk 2006
;
Bouaziz and Ferkous 2010
;
Quesne and Tkachuk 2010
. Especially, in contrast to our result, in some deformed commutation relation models, the hydrogen energy spectrum depends on the center-of-mass momentum
Quesne and Tkachuk 2010
.
In principle, these differences can be used to experimentally distinguish between these models and our non-Heisenbergian quantum mechanics.

VI
Summary and out-looks

Understanding the core content of quantum mechanics requires us to disentangle the hidden logical relationships between the basics assumptions and results of this theory. One of the most important assumptions of Heisenberg quantum mechanics is that there exists a position self-adjoint operator that satisfies the canonical commutation relation. This assumption is usually considered necessary to derive most of the important results of the theory, especially the uncertainty relation. Nonetheless, in this work, by ignoring the position operator, we demonstrate that Heisenberg quantum mechanics could be seen as a special case of a more general self-consistent quantum theory, which has most of fundamental characteristics of the Heisenberg quantum mechanics, such as the Galilean symmetry, probability conservation, position-momentum uncertainty relation, the discrete and bounded from bellow energy spectrum in bound states, and so on. This approach opens an important door to generalize the quantum theory consistent with a given space-time symmetry. It is important to remark that the deformation of commutation relation, as used in GUP literature
Maggiore 1993b
;
Kempf et al. 1995
;
Fadel and Maggiore 2022
, is not consistent with usual space-time symmetries
Fadel and Maggiore 2022
;
Maggiore 1994
;
Bosso et al. 2023a
—the usual canonical commutation relation is a direct result of the Galilean and also Poincare algebras if we consider a self adjoint position operator that correctly transform under special translation. Therefore, it seems that for constructing a non-relativistic quantum theory with a non-zero minimal position uncertainty, the presented formalism is more acceptable than deformed commutation relation approach.

One of the most important extensions of the present work is its relativistic extension. It could provide a way to circumvent the fundamental problems in relativistic quantum mechanics, which arise from the impossibility of defining a completely proper relativistic position operator. In fact, due to the problems mentioned in the Introduction and some other issues
Sebens 2019
;
Kazemi et al. 2018
;
Terno 2014
, despite much effort in recent years
Kowalski and Rembieliński 2011
;
Wagner et al. 2011
;
Wagner et al. 2012
;
Bialynicki-Birula and Bialynicka-Birula 2012
;
Babaei and Mostafazadeh 2017
;
Kazemi et al. 2018
;
Kiessling and Tahvildar-Zadeh 2018
;
Sebens 2019
;
Hawton 2019
;
Hawton 2021
;
Glasgow and Ware 2020
, a complete and agreed-upon formulation of relativistic quantum mechanics with a clear space-time resolution has not yet been proposed. Nonetheless, in relativistic quantum experiments,
the position measurements are routinely performed with high accuracy (just for example see
Sinha et al. 2010
;
Pleinert et al. 2021
;
Korzh et al. 2020
), and thus, a complete quantum theory must be able to predict the observed position distributions
Sebens 2019
;
Krekora et al. 2004
. We believe that a proper relativistic extension of our formulation of quantum theory without a position operator could circumvent the mentioned problems, as has been previously shown by one of the authors in a very special case
Kazemi et al. 2018
. The detailed study of the relativistic extension is left for future works.

Finally, it should be noted that the generalization of quantum theory without a position operator is natural and interesting from the perspective of some non-standard interpretations of quantum theory, such as Bohmian mechanics
Barnea et al. 2018
, Nelson stochastic mechanics
Nelson 1966
, and the many interacting worlds model
Hall et al. 2014
. In these theories, the operators are not fundamental; they are secondarily mathematical objects that are useful for computing statistical observable results
Dürr et al. 2004a
. However, in the formalism of these theories, the position probability density and probability current have fundamental important roles. So, the approach of the present work could shed some new light on the generalization of these alternative formulations of quantum theory, especially in the relativistic regime, which is an interesting open problem
Foo et al. 2022
;
Dürr et al. 2014
;
Gisin 2018
;
Holland 1995
;
Dürr et al. 2004b
.

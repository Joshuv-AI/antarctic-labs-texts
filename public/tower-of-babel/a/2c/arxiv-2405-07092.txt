Belyi Function Decompositions for The Icosahedron of Genus 4
Natalia Amburg, Mariia Kovaleva

Abstract
The icosahedron I 4 I_{4} of genus 4 is a dessin d’enfant embedded in Bring’s curve ℬ \mathcal{B} . The dessin I 4 I_{4} is related in some sense to a regular icosahedron I 0 I_{0} embedded in the complex Riemann sphere. In particular, decompositions of Belyi functions β I 0 : ℂ ​ ℙ 1 → ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \beta_{I_{0}}:\mathbb{CP}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1} and β I 4 : ℬ → ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \beta_{I_{4}}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1} for I 0 I_{0} and I 4 I_{4} have the same lattice. The diagram of β I 0 \beta_{I_{0}} decompositions is already known. In the present paper we find β I 4 \beta_{I_{4}} decompositions. Note that β I 0 \beta_{I_{0}} decomposes into rational functions on ℂ ​ P 1 \mathbb{C}P^{1} , while in case of β I 4 \beta_{I_{4}} we deal with maps between different algebraic curves. Key words : dessins d’enfants, Belyi functions, functional composition, icosahedron of genus 4, Bring’s curve.

1
Introduction

In the early 20th century Joseph Ritt proved his polynomial decomposition theorem. Suppose we have two decompositions of a polynomial
f
into indecomposible polynomials of degrees greater than one

\displaystyle f=g_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ\cdots\circ g_{m}=h_{1}\circ h_{2}\circ\cdots\circ h_{n}\quad g_{i}\neq h_{i}\text{ for some i.}

Then
m=n
, and the degrees of the components are the same but possibly in different order
[
12
]
.
However, the analogous problem for rational functions is more difficult. For example, a Belyi function
\beta_{I_{0}}:\mathbb{C}P^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
for a regular icosahedron
I_{0}
has various decompositions with different number of indecomposable functions (see Figure
4
).
The notion of Belyi function is associated with dessins d’enfants (which means "child’s drawing" in French). The theory of dessins d’enfants was developed by Alexander Grothendieck in 1980s, it connects topological and combinatorial objects with algebraic curves. A dessin d’enfant (or simply dessin) is a type of graph drawn on a topological surface with a single stroke of a pencil. A remarkable fact is that these drawings give us an exact characterisation of the algebraic curves.
In this paper we consider the icosahedron
I_{4}
of genus 4, which has the automorphism group
A_{5}
as well as a regular icosahedron
I_{0}
. Saying in a very brief way, the icosahedron
I_{4}
of genus 4 is a dessin embedded in a genus-4 surfaces, on which
I_{4}
induces the complex structure of Bring’s curve
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{4}
by its Belyi function
\beta_{I_{4}}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
(see Theorem
2.1
). Decompositions of Belyi functions
\beta_{I_{0}}:\mathbb{CP}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and
\beta_{I_{4}}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1}
have the same lattice. To decompose
\beta_{I_{4}}
we consider quotients of
I_{4}
by subgroups of its automorphism group. Also, we find equations of corresponding quotient curves. The full diagram of
\beta_{I_{4}}
decompositions is presented in Figure
5
.

2
Dessin d’enfant

Definition 1

(
Dessin d’enfant
)
.

A dessin d’enfant is a bicolored graph embedded in a compact oriented two-dimensional manifold in such a way that:

•

The edges do not intersect.

•

The complement of the graph is a disjoint union of regions homeomorphic to open disks.

•

The vertices are colored in black and white in such a way that the adjacent vertices have opposite colors.

The genus of a dessin is the genus of the underlying surface.

Remark 2.1
.

In this paper we usually erase white vertices of degree 2 in order to simplify pictures.

Dessins admit an encoding by permutations. Let
M
be a dessin with
m
edges. We label the edges from
1
to
m
and associate
M
with the following pair of permutations
(\sigma,\alpha)
:

•

A cycle of
\sigma\in S_{m}
contains the labels of edges incident with a black vertex, taken in counterclockwise direction around this vertex.

•

The cycles of
\alpha\in S_{m}
correspond, in the same way, to the white vertices.

Remark 2.2
.

[
8
]

The permutation
(\sigma\alpha)^{-1}
represents faces of the corresponding dessin. There are as many cycles in
(\sigma\alpha)^{-1}
as there are faces. Also, the degree of a face is equal to the length of the corresponding cycle.

Example 2.1
.

Figure 1
:
Dessin d’enfant

For the dessin of Figure
1
we obtain the following permutations:

\displaystyle\sigma=(1)(23456),\quad\alpha=(12)(3)(45)(6),\quad(\sigma\alpha)^{-1}=(12643)(5).

(1)

Proposition 2.1
.

[
17
]

A pair of permutations
\sigma,\alpha\in S_{m}
is associated with a dessin iff the permutation group generated by
\sigma,\alpha
is transitive.

Definition 2

(
Belyi pair
)
.

A pair
(X,\beta)
, where
X
is a Riemann surface, and
\beta:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{C}}
is a meromorphic function, is called a Belyi pair if all critical values of
\beta
belong to
\{0,1,\infty\}
.

Theorem 2.1

(
Riemann’s existence theorem
)
.

[
17
]

For any dessin
M
there exists a Belyi pair
(X,\beta)
such that
M
is embedded in
X
as
M=\beta^{-1}([0,1])
in such a way that:

1.

All black vertices of
M
are roots of the equation
\beta(x)=0
, the multiplicity of each root being equal to the degree of the corresponding vertex.

2.

All white vertices of
M
are roots of the equation
\beta(x)=1
, the multiplicity of each root being equal to the degree of the corresponding vertex.

3.

Inside each face of
M
there exists a single pole of
\beta
, the multiplicity of the pole being equal to the degree of the face.

The pair
(X,\beta)
is unique up to an automorphism of the surface
X
.

In other words, dessins are simple
topological encodings of these Belyi pairs.

Definition 3

(
Automorphisms of dessins
)
.

Assume that a Belyi pair
(X,\beta)
corresponds to a dessin
M
. Let as define an automorphism of the dessin
M
as an automorphism
\varphi
of the curve
X
such that
\beta=\beta\circ\varphi
.

3
Icosahedron of Genus 4

Consider a regular icosahedron with white vertices in the middle of edges as a dessin embedded in the sphere and write down the corresponding permutations
(\sigma,\alpha)
.
Following to
[
17
]
, let us define
the icosahedron
I_{4}
of genus 4
as a dessin associated with the pair of permutations
(\sigma^{2},\alpha)
.
The definition is correct by Proposition
2.1
. As you can check,

•

\sigma^{2}
has 12 cycles of length 5
\Rightarrow

I_{4}
has 12 (black) vertices of degree 5

•

\alpha
has 30 cycles of length 2
\Rightarrow

I_{4}
has 30 edges (i.e. 30 white vertices of degree 2)

•

(\sigma^{2}\alpha)^{-1}
has 12 cycles of length 5
\Rightarrow

I_{4}
has 12 faces of degree 5

By Euler’s formula,
I_{4}
is embedded in the surface of genus 4.

Figure 2
:
Great Dodecahedron

There is a nice way to visualize
I_{4}
as the Great Dodecahedron (Figure
2
), which is one of four nonconvex regular polyhedra. Note that vertices and edges of the Great Dodecahedron and a regular icosahedron are the same.
The permutation
(\sigma\alpha)^{-1}
maps an edge incident with a vertex to the next edge in the cyclic order and we obtain triangular faces of a regular icosahedron. While the permutation
(\sigma^{2}\alpha)^{-1}
maps an edge incident with a vertex to the one after the next and we obtain pentagonal faces of
I_{4}
. So, we can consider the Great Dodecahedron as an immersion of the surface of genus 4 into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

3.1
Belyi function for
I_{4}

In 1786 Erland Bring developed a transformation to simplify a generic quintic equation to the form

p(x)=x^{5}+ax+b.

(2)

Note that the roots
x_{i}
of the Bring quintic equation
p(x)=0
satisfy

\begin{cases}x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}=0\\
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}^{2}=0\\
x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+x_{4}^{3}+x_{5}^{3}=0\end{cases}

(3)

In 1884 Felix Klein showed that homogeneous equations (
3
) cut out the curve
\mathcal{B}
of genus 4 in
\mathbb{C}P^{4}
and named it
Bring’s curve
. Also, Klein proved that
I_{4}
is embedded in
\mathcal{B}

[
7
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
17
]

The automorphism group of Bring’s curve
\mathcal{B}
is the symmetric group
S_{5}
, which acts on
\mathcal{B}
by permutations of five projective coordinates.

Example 3.1
.

\displaystyle(12345)\in\text{Aut}(\mathcal{B})\quad(12345)(x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}:x_{4}:x_{5})=(x_{2}:x_{3}:x_{4}:x_{5}:x_{1})

A set of five roots of the equation
p(x)=0
usually gives rise to 120 points on Bring’s curve
\mathcal{B}
. Indeed, the number of permutations of five roots
p(x)
equals to

.

Proposition 3.2
.

A Belyi function
\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{1}
for
I_{4}

\beta_{I_{4}}(x)=1+\frac{25b^{2}}{128a^{5}}\cdot(125b^{2}+\sqrt{5}\prod\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 5}(x_{i}-x_{j})),

(4)

where
a=\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j<k<s\leqslant 5}^{5}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}x_{s},\quad b=-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
.

To prove Proposition
3.2

3.3

discussed in

]

Notation.
Let
M

A Belyi function for

\beta_{H}(x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}:x_{4}:x_{5})=\frac{256a^{5}}{256a^{5}+3125b^{4}},

where
a=\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j<k<s\leqslant 5}^{5}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}x_{s},\quad b=-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
.

Remark 3.1
.

[
17
]

Let
\beta
be a Belyi function for a dessin

is

, where
g(y)=\frac{4y}{(y+1)^{2}}

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

By Remark
3.1
,
\beta_{H}=g\circ\beta_{I_{4}}
, where
\beta_{I_{4}}
is a Belyi function for
I_{4}
, which we want to compute.

\displaystyle\beta_{H}=\frac{4\beta}{(\beta+1)^{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\beta_{1,2}(x)=2\cdot\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\beta_{H}(x)}}{\beta_{H}(x)}-1

Substituting
\beta_{H}
into
\beta_{1,2}

\beta_{1,2}(x)=1+\frac{25b^{2}}{128a^{5}}\cdot(125b^{2}\pm\sqrt{5(256a^{5}+3125b^{4})}).

Note that

is the discriminant of a polynomial
p(x)
of the form (
2

\beta_{1,2}(x)=1+\frac{25b^{2}}{128a^{5}}\cdot(125b^{2}\pm\sqrt{5}\prod\limits_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})).

Belyi pairs
(\mathcal{B},\beta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{B},\beta_{2})
correspond to the same dessin because there exists an automorphism
\varphi

For example, you can take

Corollary 3.1
.

I_{4}
and
I_{4}^{*}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Note, both Belyi pairs
(\mathcal{B},\beta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{B},\beta_{2})
correspond to
I_{4}
(see Proof of Proposition
3.2
). Suppose
\beta
is a Belyi function for a dessin
M
, then
\frac{1}{\beta}
is a Belyi function for
M^{*}
. We will check that

\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}=\beta_{2}.

Indeed,

\beta_{1,2}=2\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{256a^{5}}{256a^{5}+3125b^{4}}}}{\frac{256a^{5}}{256a^{5}+3125b^{4}}}-1=2\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{4^{4}a^{5}}{4^{4}a^{5}+5^{5}b^{4}}}}{\frac{4^{4}a^{5}}{4^{4}a^{5}+5^{5}b^{4}}}-1,\\
\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{256a^{5}}{256a^{5}+3125b^{4}}}}{\frac{256a^{5}}{256a^{5}+3125b^{4}}}=\frac{4^{4}a^{5}+5^{5}b^{4}\pm\sqrt{5^{5}b^{4}(4^{4}a^{5}+5^{5}b^{4})}}{4^{4}a^{5}}=1+t\pm\sqrt{t(1+t)},

where
t=\left(\frac{5}{a}\right)^{5}\left(\frac{b}{4}\right)^{4}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=2+2t+2\sqrt{t(1+t)}-1=1+2t+2\sqrt{t(1+t)},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}=\frac{1}{1+2t+2\sqrt{t(1+t)}}=\frac{1+2t-2\sqrt{t(1+t)}}{(1+2t)^{2}-4(t+t^{2})}=1+2t-2\sqrt{t(1+t)}=\beta_{2}.

∎

3.2
The Automorphism Group of
I_{4}

Proposition 3.4
.

The automorphism group Aut
(I_{4})
of the icosahedron
I_{4}
of genus 4 is the alternating group
A_{5}
, which acts on Bring’s curve
\mathcal{B}
by even permutations of homogeneous coordinates.

Proof.

Consider the Belyi function for
I_{4}

\beta_{I_{4}}(x)=1+\frac{25b^{2}}{128a^{5}}\cdot(125b^{2}+\sqrt{5}\prod\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 5}(x_{i}-x_{j})).

The polynomials
a
and
b
are symmetric. So, the product

\prod\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 5}(x_{i}-x_{j})

is invariant under even permutations and changes the sign under odd permutations.
∎

and
q=(-1,-2i)
on
\mathcal{B}/_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
.
Since

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(p_{1})=p_{2},\quad\phi_{2}(p_{2})=p_{1},

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(q_{1})=q_{1},\quad\phi_{2}(q_{2})=q_{2},

the points
p
and
q
are fixed by
\phi_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\in\text{Aut}(\mathcal{B}/_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}})
, i.e.

\displaystyle p=\phi_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(p),\quad q=\phi_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(q).

So, the point
(-1,2i)
is a fixed point of
\phi_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
. This is a contradiction.

Hence,
\phi_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}:(x,y)\mapsto(\frac{1}{x},-\frac{y}{x^{3}}).

∎

Proposition 7.4
.

\mathcal{B}/_{A_{4}}
is given by the following equation:

y^{2}+xy+y=x^{3}+549x-2202.

Proof.

A curve of genus 1, i.e. an elliptic curve, is a quotient of
\mathbb{C}
by a lattice
\Lambda
. An elliptic curve
E=\mathbb{C}/_{\Lambda}
is also an abelian group. The group action by addition

E\times E\ni(x,y)\mapsto x+y\in E

is a holomorphic map. Being a group, an elliptic curve has a privileged point, the origin
O\in E
.

Consider a point
p\in E
of order 3 such that
p\neq O
. Translation by
p
is an automorphism

\displaystyle\tau_{p}:E\rightarrow E,\quad\tau_{p}:x\mapsto x+p

of order 3 of the Riemann surface
E
. It generates a group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}=\langle\tau_{p}\rangle
, acting freely on
E
, and the quotient
E/_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
is an elliptic curve.

Figure
15
illustrates that the map
I_{4}/_{V_{4}}\rightarrow I_{4}/_{A_{4}}
is an unbranched covering of order 3. Thus,
\mathcal{B}/_{A_{4}}
is a quotient of the elliptic curve
\mathcal{B}/_{V_{4}}
by the action of
\langle\tau_{p}\rangle
where
p\in\mathcal{B}/_{V_{4}}
is a non-zero point of order 3. Note that standard Weierstrass form of
\mathcal{B}/_{V_{4}}
is

y^{2}+xy+y=x^{3}-76x+298.

Searching the LMFDB (
[
15
]
) shows that
(\mathcal{B}/_{V_{4}})/_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
is

y^{2}+xy+y=x^{3}+549x-2202.

∎

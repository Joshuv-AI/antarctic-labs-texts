Analytical aspects of matrix interpolation problems and its applications
Dharm Prakash Singh, Amit Ujlayan

Abstract
In this paper, the m ​ n mn -dimensional space of tensor-product polynomials of two variables, of degree at most ( m − 1 ) + ( n − 1 ) (m-1)+(n-1) , is considered. A theory of two-variate polynomials is developed by establishing the algebra and basic algebraic properties with respect to the usual addition, scalar multiplication, and a newly defined algebraic operation in the considered space. Further, the existence of the considered space is established with respect to the matrix interpolation problem (MIP), P ⁡ ( i , j ) = a i ​ j P(i,j)=a_{ij} for all 1 ≤ i ≤ m 1\leq i\leq m , 1 ≤ j ≤ n 1\leq j\leq n , corresponds to a given matrix ( a i ​ j ) m × n (a_{ij})_{m\times n} in the space of m × n m\times n order real matrices. The poisedness of the MIP is proved and three formulae are presented to construct the respective polynomial in the considered space. After that, using construction formulae, a polynomial map from the space of m × n m\times n order real matrices to the considered space is defined. Some properties of the polynomial map are investigated and some isomorphic structures between the spaces are installed. It is proved that the considered space is isomorphic to the space of m × n m\times n order real matrices with respect to the algebra structure. The polynomials in the considered space with respect to the MIP’s for the given matrices also preserve the geometric properties of the matrices such as transpose, symmetry, and skew-symmetry. Some examples are included to demonstrate and verify the results.

\displaystyle=\alpha\left({\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}\right)\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

\displaystyle=\alpha{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by scalar multiplication}

\displaystyle={\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\alpha\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by scalars distribution}

\displaystyle={\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(\left(\alpha P(x,k)\right)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by scalars multiplication}

\displaystyle={\left(\alpha P(x,y)\right)}\otimes Q(x,y)\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

so the polynomials
\alpha(P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y))
and
{\left(\alpha P(x,y)\right)}\otimes Q(x,y)
are equal, entry-by-entry, and by the definition of polynomial equality, they are equal polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}
. Similarly, the polynomials
\alpha(P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y))
and
P(x,y)\otimes(\alpha Q(x,y))
are equal in
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}
. This completes the proof.

Theorem 2.5
(DP product associativity)

Suppose
P\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{n}
,
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{p}
and
R\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{q}
, then

P(x,y)\otimes\left(Q(x,y)\otimes R(x,y)\right)=\left(P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)\right)\otimes R(x,y)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{P}_{m}^{q}.

Proof:
Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{n}
,
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{p}
and
R\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{q}
. Then,

P(x,y)\otimes\left(Q(x,y)\otimes R(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=P(x,y)\otimes\left({\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\left(Q(x,l)\times R(l,y)\right)}}\right)\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

\displaystyle={\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times\left({\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\left(Q(k,l)\times R(l,y)\right)}}\right)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

\displaystyle={\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{l=1}^{p}}{P(x,k)\times Q(k,l)\times R(l,y)}}\quad\textrm{by distributivity in $\mathbb{R}$}

\displaystyle={\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}{P(x,k)\times Q(k,l)\times R(l,y)}}\quad\textrm{by switching the order of finite sums}

\displaystyle={\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}{R(l,y)\times P(x,k)\times Q(k,l)}}\quad\textrm{by commutativity in $\mathbb{R}$}

\displaystyle={\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\left(R(l,y)\times\left({\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,l)\right)}}\right)\right)}}\quad\textrm{since $R(l,y)$ is independent of the index $k$}

\displaystyle={\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\left(R(l,y)\times\left(P(x,l)\otimes Q(x,l)\right)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

\displaystyle={\sum_{l=1}^{p}{\left(\left(P(x,l)\otimes Q(x,l)\right)\times R(l,y)\right)}}\quad\textrm{by commutativity in $\mathbb{R}$}

\displaystyle=\left(P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)\right)\otimes R(x,y)\quad\textrm{by definition of DP product}

So the polynomials
P(x,y)\otimes\left(Q(x,y)\otimes R(x,y)\right)
and
\left(P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)\right)

\otimes R(x,y)
are equal, entry-by-entry, and by the definition of polynomial equality, they are equal polynomial in
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{q}
. This completes the proof.

Theorem 2.6
(DP product distributivity across addition)

Suppose
P,S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{n}
and
Q,R\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{p}
, then

(i) Left Distributive Law:

P(x,y)\otimes{\left(Q(x,y)+R(x,y)\right)}=P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)+P(x,y)\otimes R(x,y)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}.

(ii) Right Distributive Law:

{\left(P(x,y)+S(x,y)\right)}\otimes Q(x,y)=P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)+S(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}.

Proof:
Let us consider that
P\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{n}
,
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{p}
,
R\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{p}
and
S\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{n}
. Then,

\displaystyle(i)\textrm{ }P(x,y)\otimes{\left(Q(x,y)+R(x,y)\right)}

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(P(x,k)\times{\left(Q(k,y)+R(k,y)\right)}})

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)+P(x,k)\times R(k,y)\right)}}

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}+{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times R(k,y)\right)}}

\displaystyle={}P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)+P(x,y)\otimes R(x,y).

So the polynomials
P(x,y)\otimes{\left(Q(x,y)+R(x,y)\right)}
and
P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)
+
P(x,y)

\otimes R(x,y)
are equal, entry-by-entry, and by the definition of polynomial equality, they are equal polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}
.

\displaystyle(ii)\textrm{ }{\left(P(x,y)+S(x,y)\right)}\otimes Q(x,y)

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}({\left(P(x,k)+S(x,k)\right)\times Q(k,y))}}

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)+S(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}

\displaystyle={}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(P(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}+{\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(S(x,k)\times Q(k,y)\right)}}

\displaystyle={}P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)+S(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y).

So the polynomials
{\left(P(x,y)+S(x,y)\right)}\otimes Q(x,y)
and
P(x,y)\otimes Q(x,y)
+
S(x,y)

\otimes Q(x,y)
are equal, entry-by-entry, and by the definition of polynomial equality, they are equal polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{p}
. This completes the proof.

Concluding remarks

In this paper, the well known

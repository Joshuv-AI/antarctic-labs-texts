Abstract




1.
Introduction

We consider the control problem for differential equation with distributed delay

(1.1)

\dot{x}(t)=x(t-1)+\int_{-1}^{0}x(t+\tau)\varphi(\tau)d\tau+u(t),\ t>0

with the initial conditions

(1.2)

x(t)=x_{0}(t),\ t\in(-1,0);\ x(0)=\xi,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{C},\ x_{0}\in L^{2}(-1,0).

The distribution of delay is defined by the function
\varphi\in L^{2}(-1,0)
and the control function
u
is to be chosen to steer the initial condition
(\xi,x_{0}(t))
to the desired state.

Let

\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}\times L^{2}(-1,0)
be the space of all initial data

\mathcal{M}=\{\vec{x}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\
x(t)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr),\xi\in\mathbb{C},\ x_{0}\in L^{2}(-1,0)\}
.

The equation (
1.1
) always has a solution
x(t)=x(t;\vec{x},u)
and
we deal with
null controlability
problem: given
T>1
and
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{M}
, find the control
function
u
which steers the initial data to zero, i.e.
x(t;\vec{x},u)=0
,
t\in(T-1,T)
.

It is known (see e.g.
[
1
]
) that such control function always exists, and can be given by a bounded operator.
Actually there exist many such functions. We consider the problem of finding the optimal control. In other
we look for the problem

(1.3)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\to\min,\quad u\in U(x_{0},\xi;T),

here
U(x_{0},\xi,T)
stays for all control functions which steers the initial data to zero at the moment
T
.

Such kind of problems were investigated in various settings by many authors.
We refer the reader to (
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
5
]
) to mention a few. We also refer the reader to
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
for comprehensive survey
and description of the current stay of art.

We combine the semigroup techniques from
[
6
]
with the projection techniques, see e.g.
[
2
]
and

[
9
]
. These techniques lead one to the study of the eigenfunction expansions for the corresponding non-selfadjoint operator.
Since the corresponding systems of eigenfunctions do not form a Riesz basis, the expansions diverge, generally speaking.
In
[
2
]
the authors prove the point-wise convergence on the inner intervals of the segment
[T-1,T]
, yet the problem
of construction the optimal control on the whole interval remains open. In this article we consider a simplest model of time delay equation and suggest the reconstruction procedure which converges in the whole
L^{2}(0,T)
. We believe the same procedure can be applied in much more general settings.

We adjust for this case the techniques in
[
4
]
, this techniques cannot be applied directly because the generating function does not satisfy the Muckenhoupt condition. Also it is worth to mention that when constructing the optimal control for a single eigenvector we arrived
to already familiar problem
which appeared in study of the hereditary completeness of sets of exponential functions: given a set of exponents

\Lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that, the corresponding exponential functions
\{\exp(i\lambda t)\}
are incomplete in
L^{2}(0,T)
we need to construct a function which is in the span of
\{\exp(i\lambda t)\}
and is orthogonal to all but one
of these exponential functions. This problem has been considered in
[
3
]
in a very general setting, however the explicit construction
of such functions is a complicated problem. In the present article we restrict ourselves to the simplest model case when
\varphi=0
.

2.
The homogeneous problem

The corresponding homogeneous problem has the form

\dot{x}(t)=x(t-1)+\int_{-1}^{0}x(t+\tau)\varphi(\tau)d\tau,\ t>0

with the initial conditions

\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}x(0)\\
x(\cdot)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)=\vec{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}

This problem admits the unique solution
x(t)=x(t,\vec{x}_{0})
and also generates the natural semigroup

S_{t}=e^{At}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M},\ S_{t}\vec{x}_{0}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}x(t)\\
x(t+\cdot)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr).

The infinitesimal operator of the group is defined by the relation

(2.1)

A:\vec{x}_{0}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}(-1)+\int_{-1}^{0}x_{0}(\tau)\varphi(\tau)d\tau\\
\dot{x}_{0}(\tau)\end{pmatrix}.

Its domain

\mathcal{D}_{A}=\bigl\{(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\
x(t)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr),\ x\in W^{2}_{1}(-1,0),\ \xi=x(0)\bigr\}.

Let

D(z)=-iz+e^{-iz}+\int_{-1}^{0}e^{i\tau z}\varphi(\tau)d\tau

be the characteristic function of the homogeneous problem. Its indicator diagram is
[0,i]
. We denote by

\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}
the set of all zeros of
D
.
It can be easily seen that

\lambda_{n}=-\pi/2+2\pi n+i\log|n|+o(1),n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Here and in what follows we refer the reader to
[
11
]
for facts about entire functions. We denote by
For simplicity we assume
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}

and that
D
has no multiple zeroes, this may be not true for a finite number of zeros only.

We also have

(2.2)

|D(z)|\asymp\begin{cases}|z|,\ \text{for}\ z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\ y<\log x,\ \text{dist}(z,\Lambda)>\epsilon\\
e^{y},\ \text{for}\ z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\ y>\log x,\ \text{dist}(z,\Lambda)>\epsilon.\end{cases}

The propositions below are straightforward (again we refer to
[
11
]
for the proof of completeness and minimality).

Proposition 2.1
.

The spectra of
A
is simple and coincides with
i\Lambda
. The corresponding eigenvectors are of the form

\vec{e}_{\lambda}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
\exp(i\lambda\tau)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr),\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}=\{\vec{e}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be the set of all eigenfunctions.

Proposition 2.2
.

The system
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
is complete and minimal in
\mathcal{M}
.

By
\mathcal{X}_{\Lambda}=\{\vec{x}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{M}
,
\vec{x}_{\lambda}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi_{\lambda}\\
x_{\lambda}(\tau)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
.
we denote the system biorthogonal to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
.

We will use the relations

e^{A\tau}\vec{e}_{\lambda}=e^{i\lambda\tau}\vec{e}_{\lambda},\ e^{A^{*}\tau}\vec{x}_{\lambda}=e^{-i\bar{\lambda}\tau}\vec{x}_{\lambda},\ \lambda\in\Lambda.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following relations take place

(2.3)

\overline{\xi_{\lambda}}=\frac{-i}{D^{\prime}(\lambda)},

(2.4)

\int_{-1}^{0}e^{izt}\overline{x_{\lambda}(t)}dt=\frac{1}{D^{\prime}(\lambda)}\frac{e^{-iz}+\int_{-1}^{0}e^{iz\tau}\varphi(\tau)d\tau+i\lambda}{z-\lambda}.

Since
D(\lambda)=0
, one can rewrite (
2.4
) as

(2.5)

\int_{-1}^{0}e^{izt}\overline{x_{\lambda}(t)}dt=\frac{1}{D^{\prime}(\lambda)}\bigl[\frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-i\lambda t}}{z-\lambda}+\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{e^{-izt}-e^{-i\lambda t}}{z-\lambda}\varphi(t)dt\bigr].

In what follows we use both (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).

Even after being normalised, the system
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
does not form a Riesz basis in
\mathcal{M}
. However
for each
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{M}
we can consider it formal Fourier series

(2.6)

\vec{x}\sim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle\vec{x},\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle\vec{e}_{\lambda}.

3.
Structure of the optimal solution

Denote the operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{M}
as
\mathcal{B}a=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
,
a\in\mathbb{C}
.
The problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) can now be written as

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\dot{\vec{x}}(t)=A\vec{x}(t)+\mathcal{B}u(t),\ t>0,\\
\vec{x}(0)=\vec{x}_{0}.\end{cases}

Its solution has the form

\vec{x}(t)=e^{At}\vec{x}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-\tau)}\mathcal{B}u(\tau)d\tau.

Fix now
T>1
. The admissibility condition
u\in U(T;\vec{x}_{0})
reads as

e^{AT}\vec{x}_{0}=-\int_{0}^{T}e^{A(T-\tau)}\mathcal{B}u(\tau)d\tau.

The set of exponential functions
{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}=\{e^{i\lambda t}\}
is incomplete in
L^{2}(0,T)
. Let

{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}(T)
be the closure of its span in
L^{2}(0,T)
and

{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}(T)^{\perp}=L^{2}(0,T)\ominus{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}(T)
.

We follow the result from
[
2
]
, see also
[
9
]
, Ch.7.4.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(0,T)
solve the problem (
1.3
) for
\vec{x}=\vec{e}_{\lambda}

and

v_{\lambda}(t)=-u_{\lambda}(T-t)
. Then

v_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}(T)

and also

\int_{0}^{T}e^{i\mu t}\overline{v_{\lambda}(t)}dt=\delta_{\lambda,\mu},\ \mu\in\Lambda.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
be such that

(3.2)

0=\int_{0}^{T}e^{At}\mathcal{B}v(t)dt.

Then
v\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}(T)^{\perp}
.

Proof
Given
\lambda\in\Lambda
relation (
3.2
) yields

0=\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{At}\mathcal{B}v(t),\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{B}v(t),e^{A^{*}t}\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{B}v(t),e^{-i\bar{\lambda}t}\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle dt.

It remains to mention that
\mathcal{B}v(t)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}v(t)\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
,

\vec{x}_{\lambda}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi_{\lambda}\\
x_{\lambda}(t)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
, therefore

\langle\mathcal{B}v(t),e^{-i\bar{\lambda}t}\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle=\overline{\xi_{\lambda}}v(t)e^{-i\bar{\lambda}t}

and
\xi_{\lambda}\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
v\in L^{2}(0,T)
be such that

e^{AT}\vec{e}_{\lambda}=\int_{0}^{T}e^{At}\mathcal{B}v(t)dt.

Then

\int_{0}^{T}v(t)e^{-i\bar{\mu}t}dt=\bar{y}_{\lambda}^{-1}e^{i\lambda t}\delta_{\lambda,\mu},\ \mu\in\Lambda.

Proof
is similar to that of the previous lemma. We write

e^{i\lambda t}\delta_{\lambda,\mu}=\langle e^{AT}\vec{e}_{\lambda},\vec{x}_{\mu}\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle e^{At}\mathcal{B}v(t),\vec{x}_{\mu}\rangle dt

and then apply similar arguments. ∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The optimal control
linearly
depends on initial data: if
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are solutions of the problem (
1.3
) for
\vec{x}=\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2}
respectively, then
u_{1}+u_{2}
solves this problem for
\vec{x}=\vec{x}_{1}+\vec{x}_{2}
.

Now in order to solve the general problem we need to reconstruct an arbitrary initial data
\vec{x}\in\mathcal{M}
from its expansion (
2.6
).

4.
Summation method

4.1.
Formulation of the result

In this section we describe linear summation method for the series (
2.6
), i.e we describe the matrices

(w_{n}(\lambda))_{n\in\mathbb{N},\lambda\in\Lambda}
with the following properties:

(4.1)

\displaystyle w_{n}(\lambda)\to 1\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,\ \text{for each}\ \lambda\in\Lambda;

(4.2)

\displaystyle\#\{n;w_{n}(\lambda)\neq 0\}<\infty,\ \text{for each}\ n\in\mathbb{N};

(4.3)

\displaystyle S_{n}\vec{x}\to\vec{x}\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,\ \text{here}\ S_{n}\vec{x}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}w_{n}(\lambda)\langle\vec{x},\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle\vec{e}_{\lambda}.

Given the sequences
\{l_{n}\}
,
\{R_{n}\}
, satisfying the conditions

(4.4)

\displaystyle l_{n},\ R_{n}\to\infty\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

(4.5)

\displaystyle l_{n}^{2}/n\to 0,\ n/R_{n}\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

(4.6)

\displaystyle e^{-\pi l_{n}/2}R_{n}\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.\

Remark 4.1
.

It suffices, for example, to take
l_{n}=|n|^{1/4}
,
R_{n}=n^{4}
.

Consider the function

W_{n}(z)=e^{-l_{n}\pi-il_{n}\log\frac{z-n}{z+n}},\ z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let, for
\lambda\in\Lambda

(4.7)

w_{n}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}W_{n}(\lambda),&|\lambda|<R_{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and the operator
S_{n}
be defined by

(4.8)

\ S_{n}\vec{x}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}w_{n}(\lambda)\langle\vec{x},\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle\vec{e}_{\lambda}.

Then
S_{n}\vec{x}\to\vec{x}
, as
n\to\infty.

4.2.
Preliminary estimates

Lemma 4.3
.

(4.9)

W_{n}(z)\rightrightarrows 1,\ {\text{as}}\ n\to\infty\ {\text{on each compact set}}\ K\subset\mathbb{C}_{+}

Lemma 4.4
.

(4.10)

|W_{n}(z)|<e^{-\pi l_{n}/2},\ z\in\mathbb{C}_{+},|z|=R_{n}.

These lemmas are straightforward.

On the real line we have

W_{n}(t)=\begin{cases}e^{il_{n}\log\bigl|\frac{t-n}{t+n}\bigr|},&|t|<n,\\
e^{il_{n}\log\bigl|\frac{t-n}{t+n}\bigr|d-l_{n}\pi},&|t|>n\end{cases}

Lemma 4.5
.

\sup_{n}\bigl|\int_{1<|t|<n}W_{n}(t)\frac{dt}{t}\bigr|<\infty

Lemma 4.6
.

\sup_{n}\bigl|\int_{|t|>n}W_{n}(t)\frac{dt}{t}\bigr|<\infty

Proof of Lemma
4.5

We have

I_{n}:=\int_{1<|t|<n}W_{n}(t)\frac{dt}{t}=\bigl(\int_{1<|t|<n/l_{n}}+\int_{n/l_{n}<|t|<n}\bigr)=I_{n,1}+I_{n,2}

The estimate of
I_{n,1}
is straightforward. We have for
|t|<n/l_{n}

l_{n}\log\bigl|\frac{t-n}{t+n}|\prec\frac{l_{n}}{n}|t|<<1,

Respectively

I_{n,1}=\int_{1<|t|<n/l_{n}}\bigl[1+O\bigl(\frac{l_{n}}{n}|t|\bigr)\bigr]\frac{dt}{t}=O(1).

When estimating
I_{2,n}
we consider each interval
\pm[n/l_{n},n]
separately.
Consider the integral

I_{n,2}^{\prime}=\int_{n/l_{n}<t<n}e^{il_{n}\log\bigl|\frac{t-n}{t+n}\bigr|}\frac{dt}{t}

By changing of variables

u=\frac{n-t}{n+t};\ \tau=1-u;\ v=\log(1-\tau),

and extracting the main term on each step, we finally arrive to the integral

\int_{1/l_{n}}^{\infty}e^{il_{n}v}\frac{dv}{v},

which is bounded uniformly in
n
. ∎

Proof of Lemma
4.6
goes in the same vien. We
write

\int_{|t|>n}W_{n}(t)\frac{dt}{t}=\bigl(\int_{|t|>nl_{n}}+\int_{n<|t|<nl_{n}}\bigr)W_{n}(t)\frac{dt}{t}

and use the similar reasonings. ∎

Proof of Theorem

Consider instead the adjoint operator

Actually it is more convenient to deal with the conjugate objects:

Let

).

Once again we use the duality reasons

\|\widehat{s_{n}^{(2)}}\|=\sup\bigl\{\bigl|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{s_{n}^{(2)}}(x)h(x)dx\bigr|\ ,\ h\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),\ \|h\|\leq 1\bigr\}.

Let the functions
h_{t}
be defined as in (
4.16
). Consider the function

f(x)=h_{-1}+\int_{-1}^{0}h_{t}(x)\varphi(t)dt.

We have
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
,
\|f\|\prec 1
and

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{s_{n}^{(2)}}(x)h(x)dx=\sum_{\lambda}w_{n}(\lambda)\frac{1+i\lambda}{D^{\prime}(\lambda)}Y(\lambda)f(\lambda).

This expression can be estimated in a similar way as when estimating
\|s_{n,1}\|
.
∎

We have proved the following statement.

Theorem 4.7
.

Given the control problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), let
u_{0}(t)
be the optimal solution defined by
(
1.3
). Let, further,
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
be the corresponding eigenvalues and eigenvectors
and also

\vec{x}_{0}\sim\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle\vec{x}_{0},\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle\vec{e}_{\lambda}

be the formal Fourier expansion of the initial data
\vec{x}_{0}
.

Then

u_{0}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda}w_{n}(\lambda)\langle\vec{x}_{0},\vec{x}_{\lambda}\rangle u_{\lambda}(t),

here
w_{n}(\lambda)
are defined in (
4.7
) and the functions
u_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(0,T)
are defined by the
relations

u_{\lambda}(t)=-\overline{v_{\lambda}(T-t)};\ v_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda),\ \int_{0}^{T}e^{i\mu t}\overline{v_{\lambda}(t)}dt=\delta_{\lambda,\mu},\ \mu\in\Lambda.

The limit exists in
L^{2}(0,T)
sense.

5.
A model case

a.
In this section we consider the simplest model case

(5.1)

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)=x(t-1)+u(t),\ t>0;

(5.2)

\displaystyle x(t)=x_{0}(t),\ t\in(-1,0),\ x(0)=\xi_{0}.

For this case we give an explicit construction of the corresponding functions
v_{\lambda}
for
T\in(1,2)
.
We denote
\delta=T-1
.
The characteristic function has the form

(5.3)

D(z)=-iz+e^{-iz}.

b.
Description of
{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)^{\perp}
. Let
g\in L^{2}(0,T)
,
g\in{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)^{\perp}
. Then the entire function

G(z)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{izt}\overline{g(t)}dt

vanishes on
\Lambda
and the standard reasonings on comparing its growth with that of
D(z)
yield

(5.4)

G(z)=e^{iz}D(z)\Omega(z),

where
\Omega
runs through the set of all functions of the form

(5.5)

\Omega(z)=\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{izt}\overline{\omega(t)}dt,\ x\Omega(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

The later yields

(5.6)

\omega\in W^{2}_{1}(0,\delta),\ \omega(0)=\omega(\delta)=0,

and, by (
5.3
) - (
5.4
), we obtain

g(t)=\begin{cases}\omega^{\prime}(t-1),&t\in(1,1+\delta),\\
\omega(t),&t\in(0,\delta),\\
0,&\rm{otherwise}\end{cases}

This is the description of
{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda)^{\perp}
.

c.
Description of the biorthogonal system. Fix
\lambda\in\Lambda
and let a function

v_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(0,T)
satisfy

\int_{0}^{T}e^{i\mu t}\overline{v_{\lambda}(t)}dt=\delta_{\lambda,\mu},\ \mu\in\Lambda.

Denote

V_{\lambda}(z)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{izt}\overline{v_{\lambda}(t)}dt.

As before we have

V_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1}{e^{i\lambda}D^{\prime}(\lambda)}\frac{e^{iz}D(z)}{z-\lambda}P_{\lambda}(z),

where the function

P_{\lambda}(z)=\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{izt}\overline{p_{\lambda}(t)}dt,\ p_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(0,\delta)

Satisfies

(5.7)

P_{\lambda}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{i\lambda t}\overline{p_{\lambda}(t)}dt=1.

We remind that
D(\lambda)=0
. Therefore

\frac{e^{ix}D(x)}{x-\lambda}=-ie^{ix}-i\frac{e^{i(x-\lambda)}-1}{x-\lambda}=-i\left[e^{ix}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{ixt}e^{-i\lambda t}dt\right]

and, denoting
a_{\lambda}=1/(e^{i\lambda}D^{\prime}(\lambda)
we obtain

v_{\lambda}(t)=-ia_{\lambda}\left[\overline{p_{\lambda}(t-1)}+\int_{t-1}^{t}e^{-i\lambda(t-u)}\overline{p_{\lambda}(u)}du\right]

When
p_{\lambda}
runs through
L^{2}(0,\delta)
the function
v_{\lambda}
runs through all functions in
L^{2}(0,T)

orthogonal to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}\setminus\{e^{i\lambda t}\}
. The condition
v_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\lambda}^{\perp}
leads one to condition on
p_{\lambda}
.

d.
We assume that the functions
\omega
,
p_{\lambda}
are defined on the whole
\mathbb{R}
and vanish outside of

(0,\delta)
. The condition
v_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\Lambda}
takes the form

(5.8)

0=\int_{0}^{T}[\omega(t)+\omega^{\prime}(t-1)]\left[\overline{p_{\lambda}(t-1)}+e^{-i\lambda t}\int_{t-1}^{t}e^{-i\lambda\tau}\overline{p_{\lambda}(\tau)}d\tau\right]dt,

for all
\omega\in W^{2}_{1}(0,\delta)
,
\omega(0)=\omega(\delta)=0
.

When opening the parenthesis we obtain four summands
A_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
. Consider each of them separately.

A_{1}=\int_{0}^{T}\omega(t)\overline{p_{\lambda}(t-1)}dt=0,

since the supports of
\omega(t)
and
p_{\lambda}(t-1)
are disjoint.

Denote
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(t)=e^{-i\lambda t}\chi_{(0,\infty)}(t)
. Then

A_{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\omega(t)\int_{t-1}^{t}\overline{p_{\lambda}(\tau)}e^{-i\lambda(t-\tau)}d\tau=\int_{0}^{\delta}\omega(t)(\overline{p_{\lambda}}*\varepsilon_{\lambda})(t)dt;

A_{3}=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\prime}(t-1)\overline{p_{\lambda}(t-1)}dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\omega(t-1)\overline{p_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t-1)}dt.

Here we assume that
p_{\lambda}^{\prime}
exists. With this assumption we will arrive to equation with respect to
p_{\lambda}^{\prime}

which has smooth solution. This suffices since we already know that there is only one
p_{\lambda}
, satisfying all conditions.

When considering
A_{4}
we use (
5.7
). We have

\displaystyle A_{4}=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\prime}(t-1)e^{-i\lambda t}\int_{t-1}^{t}\overline{p_{\lambda}(\tau)}e^{i\lambda\tau}d\tau dt=\int_{1}^{T}\omega^{\prime}(t-1)e^{-i\lambda t}\int_{t-1}^{\delta}\overline{p_{\lambda}(\tau)}e^{i\lambda\tau}d\tau dt=

\displaystyle e^{-i\lambda}\int_{0}^{\delta}\omega^{\prime}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\left(1-\int_{0}^{t}\overline{p_{\lambda}(\tau)}e^{i\lambda\tau}d\tau\right)dt=e^{-i\lambda}\left[i\lambda\int_{0}^{\delta}\omega(t)e^{-i\lambda t}dt-\int_{0}^{\delta}\omega(t)(\overline{p_{\lambda}}*\varepsilon_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)dt\right].

We collecting these relations together and remark that
\omega
can be any function in
W^{2}_{1}(0,\delta)
, satisfying
(
5.6
). We then arrive to the equation

(5.9)

-\overline{p_{\lambda}}^{\prime}(t)-e^{-i\lambda}(\overline{p_{\lambda}}*\varepsilon_{\lambda})^{\prime}(t)+(\overline{p_{\lambda}}*\varepsilon_{\lambda})(t)-e^{-i\lambda}\varepsilon_{\lambda}^{\prime}(t)=0,\ 0<t<\delta.

The left-hand side of this equation is well-defined for all
t>0
. Denote it by
\alpha(t)
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(s)
and
e^{-\delta s}\mathcal{A}(s)
be the Laplace transform of
\overline{p_{\lambda}}
and
\alpha
respectively.
Taking once again into account that
D(\lambda)=0
, we transform (
5.9
) into equation for
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(s)
:

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(s)(1-s^{2})=(\lambda^{2}-i\lambda\overline{p_{\lambda}(0)})+s\overline{p_{\lambda}(0)}+e^{-\delta s}\mathcal{A}(s).

The last summand in the right-hand side does not influence the values of
\overline{p_{\lambda}}
on
(0,\delta)
.
Therefore

\overline{p_{\lambda}}(t)=[\lambda^{2}-i\lambda\overline{p_{\lambda}}(0)]\sinh t+\overline{p_{\lambda}}(0)\cosh t,\ t\in(0,\delta).

The value
\overline{p_{\lambda}}(0)
should now be chosen to meet (
5.7
). We omit the corresponding calculation.

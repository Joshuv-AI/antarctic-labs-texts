Scattering theory For Quadratic Nonlinear Schrödinger System in dimension six
Chuanwei Gao, Fanfei Meng, Chengbin Xu, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the solutions to the energy-critical quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger system in H ˙ 1 × H ˙ 1 {\dot{H}}^{1}\times{\dot{H}}^{1} , where the sign of its potential energy can not be determined directly. If the initial data u 0 {\rm u}_{0} is radial or non-radial but satisfies the mass-resonance condition, and its energy is below that of the ground state, using the compactness/rigidity method, we give a complete classification of scattering versus blowing-up dichotomies depending on whether the kinetic energy of u 0 {\rm u}_{0} is below or above that of the ground state.

1.
Introduction

We consider the quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\rm u}+A\boldsymbol{\rm u}+\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u})=\boldsymbol{\rm 0},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\boldsymbol{\rm u}(0,x)=\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(
NLS system
)

where
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
,
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}
, and
\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
are all vector-valued complex functions with two components defined as follows

\boldsymbol{\rm u}:=\left(\begin{aligned} u\\
v\end{aligned}\right),~\boldsymbol{\rm u_{0}}:=\left(\begin{aligned} u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{aligned}\right),~\boldsymbol{\rm 0}:=\left(\begin{aligned} 0\\
0\end{aligned}\right),

and
A
is a
2\times 2
matrix,
\boldsymbol{\rm f}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
as follows

A:=\left(\begin{aligned} \Delta\quad&~~~~0\\
0\quad&~~~~\kappa\Delta\end{aligned}\right),~\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):=\left(\begin{aligned} v\overline{u}\\
u^{2}\end{aligned}\right),

(1.1)

u,v:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are unknown functions,
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
is a real number and
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

From a physical point view, (
NLS system
) is related to the Raman amplification in a plasma. This process is a nonlinear instability phenomenon (see
[
3
,
4
,
21
,
22
]
for more details). Solutions to (
NLS system
) preserve the
mass
,
energy
and
momentum
, defined respectively by

\displaystyle M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\equiv M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\equiv E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\overline{u}\nabla u+\frac{1}{2}\overline{v}\nabla v\right){\rm d}x\equiv P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}),

where

\displaystyle(\text{kinetic energy})\quad

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|v\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle(\text{potential energy})\quad

\displaystyle R(\boldsymbol{\rm u}):

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{v}u^{2}{\rm d}x.

The equation (
NLS system
) is invariant under the scaling

\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{\rm u}\left(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x\right)

for any
\lambda>0
.
The critical regularity of Sobolev space is
{\rm\dot{H}}^{s_{c}}
,
i.e.
\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\lambda}\|_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}\|_{{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
where
s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-2
.
Therefore, the equation (
NLS system
) is called mass-subcritical if
d\leqslant 3
, mass-critical if
d=4
, energy-subcritical if
d=5
, and energy-critical if
d=6
. We say (
NLS system
) satisfies the mass-resonance condition if
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
. A distinguished and exclusive feature of this case is that (
NLS system
) is left invariant under the following Galilean transformation

\left(\begin{aligned} u(t,x)\\
v(t,x)\end{aligned}\right)\to\left(\begin{aligned} e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}u(t,x-2t\xi)\\
e^{2ix\cdot\xi}e^{-2it|\xi|^{2}}v(t,x-2t\xi)\end{aligned}\right)

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

From a mathematical point view, even though the fact
A\leqslant 0
(i.e.
\langle A\boldsymbol{\rm u},\boldsymbol{\rm u}\rangle\leqslant 0,~\forall~\boldsymbol{\rm u}\in{\rm\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) superficially suggests that (
NLS system
) is similar to the classical nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\mu|u|^{p-1}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

the truth is quite different. One prominent difference among others is that there is no classification of the so-called focusing (
\mu=1
) and defocusing (
\mu=-1
) in (
NLS system
), which can be seen from the fact the sign of potential energy
R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
can not be judged directly from
\mu
.

In this paper, we consider the energy-critical case of (
NLS system
) in the critical Sobolev space, that is,
d=6
and
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}\in{\rm\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
.
For any
s\geqslant 0
, we use
{\dot{\rm H}}^{s}
to denote Hilbert space
{\dot{H}}^{s}\times{\dot{H}}^{s}

{\rm H}^{s}

For any

to denote

We also denote the Schrödinger group
\verb"S"(t)
as follows

\verb"S"(t):=\left(\begin{aligned} e^{it\Delta}\qquad 0\quad\\
\quad 0\qquad e^{\kappa it\Delta}\end{aligned}\right).

By solution, we mean a function
\boldsymbol{\rm u}\in C_{t}(I,{\rm\dot{H}}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))\cap{\rm L}_{t,x}^{4}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{6})

If the maximal interval of existence

such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)-\verb"S"(t)\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{\pm}\right\|_{{\rm\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}=0.

We mark

For each number

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\leqslant N}\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\phi\left(\frac{\xi}{N}\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{>N}\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\left(1-\phi\left(\frac{\xi}{N}\right)\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{N}\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\left(\phi\left(\frac{\xi}{N}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{2\xi}{N}\right)\right)\hat{\boldsymbol{\rm g}}(\xi),

and similarly

.
We will usually use these multipliers when

and

are

(that is, of the form

in particular, all summations over

Like all Fourier multipliers, the Littlewood-Paley operators commute with the propagator

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein inequalities)
.

For any
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
and

In this section, we are in the position to give the variational characterization for the sharp Gargliardo-Nirenberg inequality. Apart from stating the existence of the ground state, we will also show some relationships between the energy of solution and the energy of ground state, which facilitate us to obtain the finite time blow-up result in Theorem

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(-2\Delta\phi-2\phi\varphi\right)\cdot\overline{\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=1}\lambda^{\alpha}\phi(\lambda^{\beta}x)\right)}

2-3\frac{R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}{H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}\geqslant 2-3C_{GN}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})]^{\frac{1}{2}}=2-2\left(\frac{H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}{H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geqslant 2\left[1-\left(1-\delta^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]:=\delta^{\prime\prime};

and

2-3\frac{R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}{H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}=3\frac{E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})}{H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})}-1\leqslant 3\frac{(1-\delta)E(\boldsymbol{\rm W})}{(1+\delta^{\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})}-1=\frac{(1-\delta)}{(1+\delta^{\prime})}-1:=-\tilde{\delta}^{\prime\prime},

that is,

2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\geqslant\delta^{\prime\prime}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u});

(3.9)

and

2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\leqslant-\tilde{\delta}^{\prime\prime}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}).

(3.10)

So we complete the proof.
∎

3.2.
Energy trapping

For the solution to classical focusing (
1
), generally speaking, we can not determine the sign of its energy. However, under certain circumstance, we may determine the sign of a general function regardless of whether it is a solution to (
1
) or not. For us, we may generalize the above fact to our setting.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\rm v}\in{\rm\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
, if
H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
, then

E(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\geqslant 0.

Proof.

We just need to show
H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})-C_{GN}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})]^{\frac{3}{2}}\geqslant 0
, since

E(\boldsymbol{\rm v})=H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})-R(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\geqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})-C_{GN}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})]^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Considering
0\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
, we define
f(y)=y-C_{GN}y^{\frac{3}{2}}
for
0\leqslant y\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
.
Then
f^{\prime}(y)=1-\frac{3}{2}C_{GN}y^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus
f^{\prime}(y)\geqslant 0
only when
y\leqslant y_{0}:=\frac{4}{9C_{GN}^{2}}
.
The fact
C_{GN}=\frac{R(\boldsymbol{\rm W})}{[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{\frac{3}{2}}}
and
H(\boldsymbol{\rm W}):R(\boldsymbol{\rm W})=2:3
implies

C_{GN}=\frac{2}{3[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad y_{0}=H(\boldsymbol{\rm W}).

Therefore, we obtain
E(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\geqslant f(H(\boldsymbol{\rm v}))\geqslant 0
if
0\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})\leqslant H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
.
∎

Next, we will establish the equivalence relation between the energy and the kinetic energy of the solution to (
NLS system
), that is,
H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\sim E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
under certain restrictions. For the classical (
1
), it is easy to obtain
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\lesssim H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
. Indeed,
for the focusing case, we can use the fact that
R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\geqslant 0
, which is missing in our setting;
for the defocusing case, we can use the associated Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. However, for (
NLS system
), we do not have the natural comparison,
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\lesssim H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
.
Our proof here is different from the argument in
[
18
]
.
To obtain
|R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})|\lesssim_{\kappa}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})]^{\frac{3}{2}}
, the Arithmetic-Geometry Mean-value inequality is used.
Combining with the coercivity of energy, we prove
H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\sim E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\rm v}\in{\rm\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
,

|R(\boldsymbol{\rm v})|\leqslant C(\kappa)[H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})]^{\frac{3}{2}},

(3.11)

where
C(\kappa)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{27\kappa}}>0
.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding, and the Arithmetic-Geometry Mean-value inequality,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\geqslant\sqrt[n]{\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}},\qquad\text{for}~\forall~1\leqslant i\leqslant n,a_{i}\geqslant 0,

we can compute directly, setting
\boldsymbol{\rm v}:=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})^{T}
,

\displaystyle|R(\boldsymbol{\rm v})|=

\displaystyle\left|\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\overline{\tilde{v}}\tilde{u}^{2}{\rm d}x\right|\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|\tilde{v}||\tilde{u}|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\tilde{v}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\leqslant\|\tilde{v}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}\|\tilde{u}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{8}{\kappa}\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\tilde{v}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{8}{\kappa}\left[\frac{\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\tilde{v}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}\right)}{3}\right]^{3}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{8}{27\kappa}}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm v})]^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Choosing
C(\kappa)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{27\kappa}}
, we obtain (
3.11
).
∎

Using Lemma
3.6
and Proposition
3.4
, we can establish the energy trapping result.

Corollary 3.7

(Energy Trapping)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
be a solution to (
NLS system
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0}
and maximal life-span
I_{\max}\ni 0
.
If
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})\leqslant(1-\delta)E(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
and
H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})\leqslant(1-\delta^{\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})
, then

H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))\sim E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)),\qquad\forall~t\in I_{\max}.

(3.12)

Proof.

By (
3.11
) and (
3.7
), we may get the upper bound of energy
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
,

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))\leqslant

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))+|R(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))+C(\kappa)\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(1+C(\kappa)\left[(1-\delta^{\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)).

As for the lower bound of
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u})
, the proof of Proposition
3.4
tells us

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))+\frac{1}{3}\left[2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))+\frac{1}{3}\delta^{\prime\prime}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}(1+\delta^{\prime\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)).

Combining the above two inequalities, we deduce the conclusion (
3.12
).
∎

3.3.
Virial identity and blowing-up

Despite being proved in
[
6
]
, the part of blowing-up result of Theorem

will also be shown for completeness.

We aim to figure out how the mass is distributed in the spatial space. In particular, we try to make clear whether it concentrates at the origin or not,
and how the distribution changes over time.
So we consider a class of initial data satisfying
x\boldsymbol{\rm u_{0}}\in{\rm L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
firstly.
The law of conservation of mass suggests it useless to think about the change of
\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)\|_{{\rm L}^{2}}
over time directly,
so we redistribute the mass of
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
to large radii
and consider the behavior of
\|x\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t)\|_{{\rm L}^{2}}
when
t\in I_{max}
.
For
d=6
in the (
NLS system
) we define a function

I(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|x|^{2}\left(2\kappa|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}\right){\rm d}x.

(3.13)

Remark 3.8
.

The coefficients in (
3.13
) are chosen carefully to match with Propostion
3.4
, one may refer to Remark 4.1 in
[
24
]
for details.
More specifically, just by adjusting the rate of coefficients between
|u|^{2}
and
|v|^{2}
to be

, we can obtain a factor

in (

Lemma 3.9

(Virial identity)
.

If
\boldsymbol{\rm u}
is a solution to (
NLS system
), for any real valued function

,
t\in[0,T_{+}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}))
, then

(1)

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\kappa|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}\right)a(x){\rm d}x=2\kappa\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\overline{u}\nabla u+\overline{v}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla a(x){\rm d}x.

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\kappa|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}\right)a(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\kappa\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(\overline{u_{j}}u_{k}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\overline{v_{j}}v_{k}\right)a_{jk}(x){\rm d}x-2\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(|u|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|v|^{2}\right)\Delta\Delta a(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle-2\kappa\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\overline{v}u^{2}\Delta a(x){\rm d}x.

Set

a(x)=|x|^{2},\qquad a_{j}(x):=\partial_{x_{j}}a=2x_{j},\qquad\nabla a(x)=2x,

As a result, by Lemma

By

), we know

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\kappa}I^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle 2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})-2\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\left[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

is a concave function.

will tend to

in finite time,

\boldsymbol{\rm u}
concentrates at the origin and

has to blow up in finite time.

The coercivity of energy has showed some relations between the potential energy and the kinetic energy of solution in any time

.
But we need some uniform bounds sometimes.

.

For

And then,

\displaystyle 2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))=

\displaystyle 3E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))-H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{0})-H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 3E(\boldsymbol{\rm W})-(1+\tilde{\delta}^{\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})-(1+\tilde{\delta}^{\prime})H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})=-\tilde{\delta}^{\prime}H(\boldsymbol{\rm W}).

.

For

Proof.

First, we introduce the definition of nonlinear profile, which is associated with an initial data and a sequence of time, and then prove that it is well-defined in this subsection.

( if

By the small data theory and Theorem

Proposition 4.7
.

There exists at least one nonlinear profile associated with
(\boldsymbol{\rm v}_{0},\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
.

Proof.

There are two cases to consider: either
|t_{\infty}|<\infty
or
|t_{\infty}|=\infty

If

in any time interval

to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t_{n},\cdot)-\boldsymbol{\rm v}(t_{n},\cdot)\|_{{\dot{\rm H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\verb"S"(t_{n})\boldsymbol{\rm v}_{0}-\verb"S"(t_{n})\boldsymbol{\rm v}_{0}\right\|_{{\dot{\rm H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\int_{t_{\infty}}^{t_{n}}\verb"S"(t_{n}-s)\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(s)){\rm d}s\right\|_{{\dot{\rm H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

2.5

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\boldsymbol{\rm u}(t_{n},\cdot)-\boldsymbol{\rm v}(t_{n},\cdot)\|_{{\dot{\rm H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\int_{t_{n}}^{\infty}\verb"S"(t_{n}-s)\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}(s)){\rm d}s\right\|_{{\dot{\rm H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}

\displaystyle C\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\rm f}(\boldsymbol{\rm u})\right\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}((t_{n},\infty),{\rm L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))}=0.

\displaystyle R^{2}\left(\int_{|x-x(t)|>C(\zeta)\frac{\eta R\lambda(t)}{C(\eta)}}|\nabla u_{c}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle R^{2}\zeta^{\frac{2}{3}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

which implies

\limsup_{t\to\sup I_{c}}\int_{|x|<R}|u_{c}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x=0,\quad\forall~R>0.

(5.2)

A similar way can help us to obtain

\limsup_{t\to\sup I_{c}}\int_{|x|<R}|v_{c}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x=0,\quad\forall~R>0.

(5.3)

Let
a(x)
be a radial smooth function in Lemma
3.9
satisfying

a(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\qquad&|x|\leqslant R,\\
0,\qquad&|x|\geqslant 2R,\end{aligned}\right.

and
|\nabla a(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|}
.
Let

V_{R}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\kappa|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}\right)a(x){\rm d}x.

One one hand, combining (
5.2
) with (
5.3
), we know

\limsup_{t\to\sup I_{c}}V_{R}(t)=\limsup_{t\to\sup I_{c}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\kappa|u_{c}(t,x)|^{2}+|v_{c}(t,x)|^{2}\right)a(x){\rm d}x=0.

(5.4)

On the other hand, by Hardy’s inequality and (
1.5
), we get

\displaystyle|V_{R}^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle=2\kappa\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(2\overline{u}_{c}\nabla u_{c}+\overline{v}_{c}\nabla v_{c})\cdot\nabla a(x){\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}{|x|}\right\|_{{\rm L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\kappa}[H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})]^{2}<[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{2}.

So, Newton-Leibniz’s formula tells us,

V_{R}(t_{1})\lesssim_{\kappa}I_{R}(t_{2})+|t_{1}-t_{2}|[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{2},\qquad\forall~t_{1},t_{2}\in I_{c}.

Let
t_{2}\to T_{+}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})
and using (
5.4
), we obtain

V_{R}(t_{1})\lesssim_{\kappa}|\sup I_{c}-t_{1}|[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{2},\qquad\forall~t_{1}\in I_{c}.

By the conservation of mass and the above estimate, we have

\displaystyle M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0})=

\displaystyle M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t_{1}))\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}2\kappa|u_{c}(t_{1},x)|^{2}+|v_{c}(t_{1},x)|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbb{R}\to\infty}V_{R}(t_{1})\lesssim_{\kappa}|\sup I_{c}-t_{1}|[H(\boldsymbol{\rm W})]^{2},\qquad\forall~t_{1}\in I_{c}.

Let
t_{1}\to T_{+}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})
, we know
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0}\equiv\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
.
And thus, by the uniqueness of the solution to (
NLS system
),
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}\equiv\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
, which is a contradiction with (
1.5
).
∎

\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0}
is radial.
Combining with the fact that
\nabla f(x)=f^{\prime}(r)\frac{x}{r}
for radial function
f(r):=f(x)
when
r=|x|
, we have

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})=

\displaystyle\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(\overline{u}_{c}\nabla u_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{v_{c}}\nabla v_{c}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(\overline{u_{c}(r)}u_{c}^{\prime}(r)\frac{x}{r}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{v_{c}(r)}v_{c}^{\prime}(r)\frac{x}{r}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rm 0},

where we have used the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}g(x){\rm d}x\equiv 0
for any odd function.

We are left to show the remaining case, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0}
is non-radial with the mass-resonance condition.
Under the mass-resonance condition, that is
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
, (
NLS system
) enjoys the Galilean invariance property:

\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t,x)=\left(\begin{aligned} u_{c}(t,x)\\
v_{c}(t,x)\end{aligned}\right)\to\left(\begin{aligned} u_{c}^{\xi}(t,x)\\
v_{c}^{\xi}(t,x)\end{aligned}\right)=:\left(\begin{aligned} e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}u_{c}(t,x-2t\xi)\\
e^{2ix\cdot\xi}e^{-2it|\xi|^{2}}v_{c}(t,x-2t\xi)\end{aligned}\right)

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
.
Then, for
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi}:=(u_{c}^{\xi},v_{c}^{\xi})^{T}
, we can compute

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi})=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{c}^{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\nabla v_{c}^{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|i\xi u_{c}+\nabla u_{c}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\left\|2i\xi v_{c}+\nabla v_{c}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|\Re(\nabla u_{c})-\xi\Im u_{c}\right|^{2}+\left|\xi\Re u_{c}+\Im(\nabla u_{c})\right|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|\Re(\nabla v_{c})-2\xi\Im v_{c}\right|^{2}+\left|2\xi\Re v_{c}+\Im(\nabla v_{c})\right|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|\nabla u_{c}\right|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}|u_{c}|^{2}+2\xi\left(\Re u_{c}\Im(\nabla u_{c})-\Re(\nabla u_{c})\Im u_{c}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left|\nabla v_{c}\right|^{2}+4|\xi|^{2}|v_{c}|^{2}+4\xi\left(\Re v_{c}\Im(\nabla v_{c})-\Re(\nabla v_{c})\Im v_{c}\right){\rm d}x.

Note that
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi})-H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})-|\xi|^{2}M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\big(\Re u_{c}\Im(\nabla u_{c})-\Re(\nabla u_{c})\Im u_{c}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\big(\Re v_{c}\Im(\nabla v_{c})-\Re(\nabla v_{c})\Im v_{c}\big){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\Im(\overline{u_{c}}\nabla u_{c})+\frac{1}{2}\Im(\overline{v_{c}}\nabla v_{c}){\rm d}x=2\xi\cdot P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}).

Because
S_{I_{c}^{\xi}}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi})=S_{I_{c}}(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})=\infty
,
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi}
is a blowing-up solution to (
NLS system
) as well.
Combining with the fact that
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}
has the minimal kinetic energy among all the blowing-up solutions, we obtain

|\xi|^{2}M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})+2\xi\cdot P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})=H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}^{\xi})-H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})\geqslant 0

holds for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
.
Setting
\xi=-\frac{P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})}{M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})}
, we find

0\leqslant|\xi|^{2}M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})+2\xi\cdot P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})=-\frac{|P(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})|^{2}}{M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})}\leqslant 0,

which implies (
6.5
).
∎

Deduced from the property of negative regularity (Theorem
1.13
) and zero momentum (Proposition
6.5
) the movement of spatial center function
x(t)
of soliton-like solutions can be controlled, which can help us use Lemma
3.9
more effectively, combined with the finite mass of soliton-like solution (
\lambda(t)\geqslant 1
).

Corollary 6.6

(Control of spatial center function
x(t)
,
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}
be a soliton-like solution as in Theorem
1.12
.
Then, for any
\eta>0
, there exists
C(\eta)>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{|x-x(t)|\geqslant C(\eta)}|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\lesssim\eta.

Furthermore, the spatial center function
x(t)
satisfies

|x(t)|=o(t),\qquad t\to\infty.

6.2.
Exclusion of soliton-like solution

Now, we use Virial identity to rule out the soliton-like solution.
Its compactness has been described as an almost periodic solution.
From the control of spatial center function
x(t)
, Corollary
6.6
, we know that
x(t)=o(t)
which means
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}
moves slower and slower.
That implies the nonexistence of soliton-like solution for a large time.

Theorem 6.7
.

There exist no soliton-like solutions to (
NLS system
).

Proof.

We argue by contradiction and suppose
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a soliton-like solution to (
NLS system
).
By Definition
1.8
and
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\hookrightarrow{\dot{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
, for any
\eta>0
, there exists
C(\eta)>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{|x-x(t)|\geqslant C(\eta)}\left(|\nabla\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}|^{3}\right){\rm d}x\leqslant\eta.

(6.6)

Using Corollary
6.6
, we know there exists
T_{0}=T_{0}(\eta)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

|x(t)|\leqslant\eta t,\qquad\forall~t\geqslant T_{0}.

(6.7)

Recalling Lemma
3.9
, for
a(x)
be a radial smooth function satisfying

a(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} |x|^{2},\quad&|x|\leqslant R,\\
0,\qquad&|x|\geqslant 2R,\end{aligned}\right.

where
R>0
will be chosen later, we define

V_{R}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\kappa|u_{c}|^{2}+|v_{c}|^{2}\right)a(x){\rm d}x.

By Theorem
1.13
and interpolation theorem, we know
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}:=(u_{c},v_{c})^{T}\in{\rm L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},{\rm L}_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))
.
Then we get

\left|V_{R}^{\prime}(t)\right|=\left|2\kappa\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(2\overline{u_{c}}\nabla u_{c}+\overline{v_{c}}\nabla v_{c}\right)\cdot\nabla a(x){\rm d}x\right|\lesssim_{\kappa}RH(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})\lesssim R,

(6.8)

and

\displaystyle V_{R}^{\prime\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle 8\kappa\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(\overline{u_{c,j}}u_{c,k}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\overline{v_{c,j}}v_{c,k}\right)a_{jk}(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle-2\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(|u_{c}|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|v_{c}|^{2}\right)\Delta\Delta a(x){\rm d}x-2\kappa\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\overline{v_{c}}u_{c}^{2}\Delta a(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\kappa[2H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})-3R(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})]

\displaystyle+O\left(\int_{|x|\geqslant R}|\nabla\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}|^{3}{\rm d}x\right)+O\left(\int_{R\leqslant|x|\leqslant 2R}|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}|^{3}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}.

For
\eta>0
small enough, choosing

R:=C(\eta)+\sup_{T_{0}\leqslant t\leqslant T_{1}}|x(t)|

for any
T_{1}>T_{0}
, by coercivity of energy (Proposition
3.4
), energy trapping (Corollary
3.7
), and (
6.6
), we have

V^{\prime\prime}(t)\gtrsim_{\kappa}E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0}).

(6.9)

Applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus on
[T_{0},T_{1}]
, by (
6.7
), (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), we obtain

(T_{1}-T_{0})E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0})\lesssim_{\kappa}R=C(\eta)+\sup_{T_{0}\leqslant t\leqslant T_{1}}|x(t)|\leqslant C(\eta)+\eta T_{1},\quad\forall~T_{1}>T_{0}.

Setting first
\eta\to 0
and then
T_{1}\to\infty
, we find
E(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c,0})=0
.
Using the conservation of energy and energy trapping again, we know
H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t))=0,~\forall~t\in\mathbb{R}
.
By (
4.22
), we get
H_{c}=0
which is a contradiction with
H_{c}\geqslant\delta_{sd}>0
in (
4.18
).
∎

6.3.
Exclusion of low-to-high frequency cascade

Our last enemy is the low-to-high cascade. An important ingredient is the negative regularity lemma, which indicates that
{\rm u}_{c}
also falls into the
L^{2}
space.

Theorem 6.8
.

There exist no low-to-high frequency cascade to (
NLS system
).

Proof.

We argue by contradiction and suppose
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}:I_{c}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a low-to-high frequency cascade solution to (
NLS system
).
Similar to the proof of Theorem
6.7
, we know
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}\in{\rm L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},{\rm L}_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))
.
Noticing the conservation of mass, we obtain

0\leqslant M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})=M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t)):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x<\infty,

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

On one hand, fixing
t\in\mathbb{R}
and choosing
\eta>0
to be a small number, by Remark
1.10
and Theorem
1.11
, we have

\int_{|\xi|\leqslant C(\eta)\lambda(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}(t,\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi<\eta.

(6.10)

At the same time, since
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}\in{\rm L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},{\dot{\rm}H}_{x}^{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))
, we know

\int_{|\xi|\leqslant C(\eta)\lambda(t)}|\xi|^{-2\varepsilon}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}(t,\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\lesssim 1.

(6.11)

Thus, interpolating (
6.10
) and (
6.11
) yields

\int_{|\xi|\leqslant C(\eta)\lambda(t)}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}(t,\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\lesssim\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}.

(6.12)

On the other hand, by Theorem
1.11
,

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>C(\eta)\lambda(t)}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}(t,\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi\leqslant

\displaystyle[C(\eta)\lambda(t)]^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}}(t,\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi

(6.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle[C(\eta)\lambda(t)]^{-2}H(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle[C(\eta)\lambda(t)]^{-2}H(\boldsymbol{\rm W}).

Combining (
6.12
), (
6.13
), and Plancheral’s theorem, we get the conclusion that

0\leqslant M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c})\lesssim\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}+[C(\eta)\lambda(t)]^{-2},\qquad\forall~t\in\mathbb{R}.

By Definition
1.12
, there exists a time sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=+\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda(t_{n})=+\infty.

,

0\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t_{n}))\lesssim\eta^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}.

Let
\eta\to 0
, we have
M(\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}(t_{n}))\to 0,~n\to\infty
.
Finally, by the conservation of mass, we know
\boldsymbol{\rm u}_{c}\equiv\boldsymbol{\rm 0}
, which is a contradiction with (
1.5
) in Theorem
1.12
.
∎

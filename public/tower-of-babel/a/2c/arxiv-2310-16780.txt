1.
Introduction

In this paper, a
measure preserving system
refers to a quadruple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, where
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space and
T:X\rightarrow X
is an invertible measure preserving transformation. Let
D\in\mathbb{N}
. A tuple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{{\bf t}})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}})
is called a
measurable flow
if
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space,
(T^{\bf t})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}}
is a
D
-parameter group of invertible measure preserving transformations acting on
X
and the mapping
\mathbb{R}^{D}\times X\rightarrow X,\ ({\bf t},x)\mapsto T^{{\bf t}}x
is measurable. Throughout the paper, for any non-empty finite set
A
and any function
f
defined on
A
, we use the notation

\mathbb{E}_{x\in A}f(x)=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{x\in A}f(x)

to denote the average of
f
over
A
, where
|A|
denotes the cardinality of
A
. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
I_{N}=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
.

1.1.
Furstenberg-Bergelson-Leibman conjecture

Following Furstenberg’s work
[
17
]
on the dynamical proof of Szemerédi’s theorem, problems concerning the norm convergence and pointwise convergence of
polynomial ergodic averages have garnered significant attention.
A fundamental question in this area is as follows: Given
d,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
be invertible measure preserving transformations acting on Lebesgue probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
. Is it true that for any
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and any

P_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}[n],1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq l
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}f_{j}(T_{1}^{P_{1,j}(n)}\cdots T_{d}^{P_{d,j}(n)}x)

(1.1)

converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
and almost everywhere as
N\to\infty
?

It was shown by Bergelson and Leibman in
[
3
]
that the polynomial ergodic averages (
1.1
) may fail to converge in
L^{2}(\mu)
when the group generated by
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
is merely assumed to be solvable. In
[
3
, Page 468]
, they conjectured that when
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
generate a nilpotent group, the polynomial ergodic averages (
1.1
) converge both in
L^{2}
-norm and almost everywhere. This conjecture is often referred to as the
Furstenberg-Bergelson-Leibman conjecture
in the literature
[
8
,
23
,
24
,
28
]
.
Walsh
[
32
]
resolved this conjecture for the
L^{2}
-norm, proving the
L^{2}
-norm convergence of the averages (
1.1
) when
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
generate a nilpotent group. Before Walsh’s result, there was an
extensive body of research aimed at establishing
L^{2}
-norm convergence,
including groundbreaking works by Host-Kra
[
20
]
, Ziegler
[
35
]
, Tao
[
30
]
, and others. For more details and references, see the survey articles
[
2
,
14
,
26
]
.

The problem of pointwise convergence for the expression in (
1.1
) is significantly more challenging.
The first major breakthrough in this area
was made by Bourgain, who showed that for any
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{Q(n)}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
5
]
; and for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{a_{1}n}x)f_{2}(T_{1}^{a_{2}n}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
7
]
. For more results on bilinear ergodic averages, one can see
[
13
,
33
,
27
]
.

In 2022, Krause, Mirek and Tao proved that for any
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with
\deg Q\geq 2
and any
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{n}x)f_{2}(T_{1}^{Q(n)}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
28
]
. In 2023, Ionescu, Magyar, Mirek and Szarek
[
23
]
proved that if
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
generate a nilpotent group of step two, then for any
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{Q_{1}(n)}\cdots T_{d}^{Q_{d}(n)}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty
. In 2024, Kosz, Mirek, Peluse and Wright
[
25
]
extended the result of Krause, Mirek and Tao by proving that if
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
are commuting and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with distinct degrees, then for any
f_{1},\ldots f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{Q_{1}(n)}x)\cdots f_{d}(T_{d}^{Q_{d}(n)}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty
.

When
l>2
, the pointwise convergence of the expression in (
1.1
) can only be considered for certain special classes of measure preserving systems at this stage. For instance, when
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T_{1})
is a
K
-system, Derrien and Lesigne proved that for any
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
and any
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{Q_{1}(n)}x)\cdots f_{d}(T_{1}^{Q_{d}(n)}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
10
]
. In 2024, the third author
[
34
]
extended the result of Derrien and Lesigne to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions (
d\geq 1
). When
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T_{1})
is an ergodic distal system, Huang, Shao and Ye proved that for any
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{n}x)\cdots f_{d}(T_{1}^{dn}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
21
]
. When
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is an ergodic distal system and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
are commuting, Donoso and Sun extended the result of Huang, Shao and Ye by proving that for any
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the averages

\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T_{1}^{n}x)\cdots f_{d}(T_{d}^{n}x)

converge almost everywhere as
N\to\infty

[
11
]
.

1.2.
Continuous-time analogues of Furstenberg-Bergelson-Leibman conjecture

In
[
4
]
, Bergelson, Leibman and Moreira introduced the method, which allows ones to derive continuous-time versions of various discrete-time ergodic theorems. For example, combining the mothod with Bourgain’s double recurrence theorem and polynomial ergodic theorem, it was shown that for a measurable flow
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
, any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
P\in\mathbb{R}[t]
, the limits

\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{M}f(T^{a_{1}t}x)g(T^{a_{2}t}x)dt\ \text{and}\ \lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{M}f(T^{P(t)}x)dt

exist for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X

[
4
, Theorem 8.30, 8.31]
.

In 2022, Bourgain, Mirek, Stein and Wright proved the pointwise convergence for certain multi-parameter polynomial ergodic averages
[
8
]
. They showed that for a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, any
P\in\mathbb{Z}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}]
and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{\min\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\in\prod_{j=1}^{k}I_{N_{j}}}f(T^{P(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
. Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}:X\rightarrow X
be invertible, commuting measure preserving transformations acting on Lebesgue probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
. The
Furstenberg-Bellow problem
conjectures that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, any
P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}]
and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{\min\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{{\bf n}\in\prod_{j=1}^{k}I_{N_{j}}}f(T_{1}^{P_{1}({\bf n})}\cdots T_{d}^{P_{d}({\bf n})}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, where
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})

[
8
, Conjecture 1.22]
. This conjecture remains open.

Recently, Kosz, Langowski, Mirek and Plewa provided an affirmative answer to its continuous-time analogues
[
24
]
. The specific statement is as follows: Given
D,k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{\bf t})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}})
be a measurable flow. Then for any
P_{1},\ldots,P_{D}\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}]
and any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{\min\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}\}\to\infty}\frac{1}{M_{1}\cdots M_{k}}\int_{\prod_{j=1}^{k}[0,M_{j}]}f(T^{P_{1}({\bf t})\vec{e}_{1}}\cdots T^{P_{D}({\bf t})\vec{e}_{D}}x)d{\bf t}

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, where for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,D\}
,
\vec{e}_{j}
denotes the
j
-th basis vector of the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Similar to the
Furstenberg-Bergelson-Leibman conjecture
, for measurable flows, we have the corresponding question:

Question 1.1
.

Given
D\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{\bf t})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}})
be a measurable flow. Let
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
P_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}],1\leq i\leq D,1\leq j\leq l
and any
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, does the limit

\lim_{\min\{M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}\}\to\infty}\frac{1}{M_{1}\cdots M_{k}}\int_{\prod_{i=1}^{k}[0,M_{i}]}\prod_{j=1}^{l}f_{j}(T^{P_{1,j}({\bf t})\vec{e}_{1}}\cdots T^{P_{D,j}({\bf t})\vec{e}_{D}}x)d{\bf t}

(1.2)

exist for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
?

In this paper, we will consider some special cases of (
1.2
).

Remark 1.2
.

When
k=1
, Austin
[
1
]
proved that the limit in (
1.2
) exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
. When
D=1
, Potts
[
29
]
showed that the limit in (
1.2
) exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
. For the general case, by combining
[
4
, Theorem 6.14]
and
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
, one can show that the limit in (
1.2
) exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

1.3.
Ergodic theorems without commutativity

In 2023, Frantzikinakis and Host presented the following mean ergodic theorem, which deos not require any commutativity.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
T,S
be invertible measure preserving transformations acting on Lebesgue probability
space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
such that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
has zero entropy. Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with
\deg P\geq 2
. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T^{n}x)g(S^{P(n)}x)

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

The zero entropy assumption on

T
is necessary in the above result. If both
T
and
S
have positive entropy, the above result may fail (See
[
16
, Proposition 1.4]
for details.).

Up to the present, it remains unknown whether the limit in Theorem
1.3
exists almost everywhere.
But in the continuous-time setting, Frantzikinakis showed the following result:
Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(S^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
be two measurable flows, with no commuting restrictions imposed on the
\mathbb{R}
-actions
(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
(S^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{M}f(T^{t}x)g(S^{t^{2}}x)dt

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
(a special case of
[
15
, Theorem 1.11]
).
When
(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
(S^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are commuting
\mathbb{R}
-actions, this result was previously established by Christ, Durcik, Kovač, and Roos in
[
9
]
.

In
[
22
,
33
]
, Theorem
1.3
was extended to the following form:
Let
T,S
be invertible measure preserving transformations acting on Lebesgue probability
space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
such that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
has zero entropy. Then for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},P\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
with
\deg P\geq 2
and any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f_{1}(T^{a_{1}n}x)f_{2}(T^{a_{2}n}x)g(S^{P(n)}x)

(1.3)

exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
. We conjecture that the limit in (
1.3
) exists almost everywhere.
In this paper, we will consider some continuous-time analogues of the limit in (
1.3
).

1.4.
Main results

Before presenting the main results of the paper, we introduce two notations. Let
D\in\mathbb{N}
. For a measurable flow
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{{\bf t}})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}})
, we define
\mathcal{I}((T^{\bf t})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}})
as the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{X}
generated by
\{A\in\mathcal{X}:\text{for}\ \text{each}\ {\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D},T^{{\bf t}}A=A\ (\text{mod}\ \mu)\}
. For a Lebesgue probability space
(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu)
, a sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{Y}
and any
f\in L^{1}(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu)
,
\mathbb{E}_{\nu}(f|\mathcal{F})
denotes the conditional expectation of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
(For the definition, see Subsection
2.2

The following result shows some continuous-time analogues of the limit in (
1.3
) exist almost everywhere.

Theorem A.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(S^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
be two measurable flows,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and
Q\in\mathbb{R}[t]
with
\deg Q\geq 2
. Then for any
f_{1},f_{2},g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, the limit

\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{M}f_{1}(T^{t}x)f_{2}(T^{at}x)g(S^{Q(t)}x)dt

(1.4)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.

In fact, we can show more.

Theorem B.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})

Remark 1.4

(1)

The limit

(2)

When

3

1.5.
A brief overview of the proofs

We now provide a concise outline of the proof structure. Initially, we simplify the problem by reducing bounded measurable functions to a class of typical functions. These typical functions can be regarded as continuous functions defined on specific compact metric spaces. This reduction is facilitated through the application of topological models of measurable flows and relevant maximal inequalities. Subsequently, we transform the continuous-time ergodic averages under consideration into discrete-time ergodic averages, leveraging the continuity properties of the typical functions. Finally, we establish the pointwise convergence of these discrete-time ergodic averages by established ergodic theorems.

To help the reader’s comprehension, we present a detailed sketch of the proof for a special case of Theorem A in Subsection
3.2
.

(A.1)

And the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)

(A.2)

exists almost everywhere.

Proof.

Let
Y=X\times[0,1)^{d}
,
m
be the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1)^{d}
and
\mathcal{D}
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
[0,1)^{d}
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\vartheta(t)=t\ (\text{mod}\ 1)
. Define
S:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times Y\rightarrow Y
by

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d},x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\mapsto S^{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})}(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt:=(T_{1}^{\lfloor t_{1}+z_{1}\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor t_{d}+z_{d}\rfloor}x,\vartheta(t_{1}+z_{1}),\ldots,\vartheta(t_{d}+z_{d})).

Then we get a measurable flow
(Y,\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{D},\mu\times m,(S^{\bf t})_{{\bf t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. Let
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
such that
f\geq 0
almost everywhere. We define
\tilde{f}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by putting
(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\mapsto f(x)
for
(\mu\times m)
-a.e.
(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in Y
. For any
s\in(0,1)
, let
L_{0}(s)=[0,1-s)
and
L_{1}(s)=[1-s,1)
. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu\times m)
-a.e.
(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in Y\backslash(X\times\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in[0,1)^{d}:z_{1}\cdots z_{d}=0\})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{f}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)+z_{1}\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)+z_{d}\rfloor}x)

(A.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\sum_{n\in I_{N},\atop(\vartheta(P_{1}(n)),\ldots,\vartheta(P_{d}(n)))\in L_{i_{1}}(z_{1})\times\cdots\times L_{i_{d}}(z_{1})}f(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}(T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{i_{d}}x)).

By the fact that
f\geq 0
almost everywhere and (
A.3
), for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu\times m)
-a.e.
(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in Y\backslash(X\times\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in[0,1)^{d}:z_{1}\cdots z_{d}=0\})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{f}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(T_{1}^{-i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{-i_{d}}x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}))

(A.4)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\geq\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x).

Note that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}=\prod_{i=1}^{D}S_{i}^{n^{i}},

(A.5)

where
D=\max\{\deg P_{1},\ldots,\deg P_{2}\}
and
S_{1},\ldots,S_{D}
are invertible, commuting measure preserving transformations acting on
(Y,\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{D},\mu\times m)
. By (
A.4
), (
A.5
) and Theorem
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{N\geq 1}\Big|\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}f(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)\Big|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq100}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\left\|\sup_{n\geq 1}\Big|\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{f}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(T_{1}^{-i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{-i_{d}}x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}))\Big|\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu\times m)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq38}}{\leq}

\displaystyle C\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\left\|\tilde{f}((T_{1}^{-i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{-i_{d}}x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu\times m)}

(A.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{d}C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},

where
C
is an absolute constant, depending on
d,P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}
. By the linear property of ergodic averages, (
A.6
) can deduce (
A.1
).

By (
A.1
), we only need to prove that the limit in (
A.2
) exists alomst everywhere for all
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
-functions. Let
g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
with
\left\|g\right\|_{L^{\infty}(X,\mu)}\leq 1
and we define
\tilde{g}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as we defined
\tilde{f}
previously. Clearly, (
A.3
) holds for
g
and
\tilde{g}
. By (
A.5
) and Theorem
2.8
, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{g}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}))

exists
(\mu\times m)
-a.e.
(x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in Y
. So, there exist the sequences
\{z_{i,k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset(0,1),1\leq i\leq d
such that the following hold:

•

For each
1\leq i\leq d
,
z_{i,k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

•

For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
and each
k\geq 1
, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{g}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(x,z_{1,k},\ldots,z_{d,k}))

exists.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{N_{1}\geq N,\atop N_{2}\geq N}\Big|\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N_{1}}}g(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)-\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N_{2}}}g(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)\Big|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}g(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}\tilde{g}(S^{(P_{1}(n),\ldots,P_{d}(n))}(x,z_{1,k},\ldots,z_{d,k}))\Big|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4(2^{d}-1)\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i,k}\hskip 14.22636pt(\text{\eqref{eq99} and Weyl's equidistribution theorem})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

By the above calculation, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n\in I_{N}}g(T_{1}^{\lfloor P_{1}(n)\rfloor}\cdots T_{d}^{\lfloor P_{d}(n)\rfloor}x)

exists almost everywhere.
The proof is complete.
∎

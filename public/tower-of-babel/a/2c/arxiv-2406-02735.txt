Existence and nonexistence of minimizers for classical capillarity problems in presence of nonlocal repulsion and gravity
Giulio Pascale

Abstract
. We investigate, under a volume constraint and among sets contained in a Euclidean half-space, the minimization problem of an energy functional given by the sum of a capillarity perimeter, a nonlocal interaction term and a gravitational potential energy. The capillarity perimeter assigns a constant weight to the portion of the boundary touching the boundary of the half-space. The nonlocal term is represented by a double integral of a positive kernel g g , while the gravitational term is represented by the integral of a positive potential G G . We first establish existence of volume-constrained minimizers in the small mass regime, together with several qualitative properties of minimizers. The existence result holds for rather general choices of kernels in the nonlocal interaction term, including attractive-repulsive ones. When the nonlocal kernel g ⁡ ( x ) = 1 / | x | β g(x)=1/|x|^{\beta} with β ∈ ( 0 , 2 ] \beta\in(0,2] , we also obtain nonexistence of volume constrained minimizers in the large mass regime. Finally, we prove a generalized existence result of minimizers holding for all masses and general nonlocal interaction terms, meaning that the infimum of the problem is realized by a finite disjoint union of sets thought located at “infinite distance” one from the other. These results stem from an application of quantitative isoperimetric inequalities for the capillarity problem in a half-space.

Introduction

The classical liquid drop model for the atomic nucleus in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
n\geq 2
, aims to characterize minimizers of the functional

P(E)+\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x

is a given parameter and

denotes the perimeter of

.
There is a clear competition between the two terms in the energy, since the ball at the same time minimizes the perimeter, by the isoperimetric inequality
[
De 58
]

[

[

]
,
[

The physically relevant case is when

and

This case goes back to Gamow’s liquid drop model for atomic nuclei
[

]

[

In this paper we prove existence and nonexistence results of minimizers in a capillarity context with nonlocal and gravitational terms.
If
E
is a measurable set in the half-space
\{x_{n}>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and
\lambda\in(-1,1)
, we define the weighted perimeter functional

P_{\lambda}(E):=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H),

where
\mathcal{H}^{k}
, with
k\geq 0
, denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
H:=\{x_{n}\leq 0\}
and
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of
E
, see
Section

2
.
Interpreting the perimeter as a measure of the surface tension of a liquid drop, the constant
\lambda
basically represent the relative adhesion coefficient between a liquid drop and the solid walls of the container given by
\{x_{n}>0\}
.
If
m>0
, minimizers for the isoperimetric capillarity problem

(1.1)

\inf\{P_{\lambda}(E)\ :\ E\subset\{x_{n}>0\},\;|E|=m\}

are given by suitably truncated balls lying on the boundary of the half-space, see
[
Mag12
, Theorem 19.21]
.
More precisely, if
B^{\lambda}:=\{x\in B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ \Braket{x,e_{n}}>\lambda\}
,
m>0
and

B^{\lambda}(m):=\frac{m^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}(B^{\lambda}-\lambda\,e_{n}),

minimizers for (
1.1
) are sets of the form

B^{\lambda}(m,x):=B^{\lambda}(m)+x,

with
x\in\{x_{n}=0\}
, see also
[
PP24
, Fig. 1]

If
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
, we define the Riesz-type potential energy

\mathscr{R}(E):=\int_{E}\int_{E}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.

Finally, given a function
G:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, we define the gravity-type potential energy

\mathscr{G}(E):=\int_{E}G(x_{n})\,{\rm d}x.

If
m>0
and we denote

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E):=P_{\lambda}(E)+\mathscr{R}(E)+\mathscr{G}(E),

we consider the minimization problem

(1.2)

\inf\{\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)\ :\ E\subset\{x_{n}>0\},\,|E|=m\}.

In the context of minimization of energies with general Riesz-type potential in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Novaga and Pratelli in
[
NP21
]
showed the existence of minimizers in a generalized sense.
Later on, Carazzato, Fusco and Pratelli in
[
CFP23
]
showed that the ball is the unique minimizer in the small mass regime when the nonlocal kernel

is radial and decreasing.
Pegon in
[

]
proved that, if the kernel
g
decays sufficiently fast at infinity and if the volume is sufficiently large, then minimizers exist and converge to a ball as the volume goes to infinity.
Then, Merlet and Pegon
[
MP22
]
proved that in the planar case minimizers are actually balls in the large mass regime.
In
[
NO22
]
, Novaga and Onoue obtained existence of minimizers for any volume and convergence to a ball as volume goes to infinity, if the Riesz potential decays sufficiently fast and even if the perimeter
P(E)
is replaced by the fractional perimeter
P_{s}(E)
,
0<s<1
.
We refer to
[

,
CN18
,
MW21
,
MS19
,

]
and references therein for further results on variational problems involving nonlocal energies.

The first result in this paper is an existence result in the capillarity context and in the small mass regime, together with a bound on the Fraenkel asymmetry

\alpha_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(m,x)|}{m}\ :\ x\in\{x_{n}=0\}\right\},\qquad{\rm for}\,{\rm any}\;E\subset\{x_{n}>0\}\;{\rm with}\;|E|=m,

and some qualitative properties of volume constrained minimizers.
At the same time, under suitable conditions on the potential energies, existence result extends to all masses.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g
be a
\mathscr{R}
-admissible
q
-growing function,
q\geq 0
, and let
G
be a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function.
There exists a mass
\bar{m}=\bar{m}(n,\lambda,g,G,q)>0
such that, for every
m\in(0,\bar{m})
, there exists a minimizer
E
of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in the class

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\{\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\,{\rm measurable}\ :\ |\Omega|=m\}

and it satisfies

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,m^{\frac{1}{2n}}.

Moreover, if
g
is also infinitesimal, minimizers are indecomposable and, if in addition
g
is symmetric, minimizers are essentially bounded.

Furthermore, if
g
is also
0
-growing, infinitesimal and symmetric and
G
is coercive, minimizers have no holes, i.e., if
E
is a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{A}_{m}
, there is no set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup E)
with
|F|>0
such that

P_{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}(E\cup F)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H).

Finally, if
g
is
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and coercive and
G
is
\mathscr{G}
-admissible and coercive, there exists a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{A}_{m}
for any
m>0
.

Let us make some comments on the definitions present in
Theorem

1.1
, while referring to
Section

2
for their precise enunciation.
The “admissibility” requirements on the kernels just refer to some necessary integrability conditions.
The infinitesimality of
g
and the coercivity of
g
and
G
concern the behavior of these functions as the variable diverge, while the symmetry of
g
is referred to the symmetry with respect to the origin.
The
q
-growing property is satisfied by rather general nonlocal interaction terms, not only by repulsive ones.
Indeed, we point out that classical radial decreasing kernels are
0
-growing, but at the same time attractive-repulsive kernels of the type

(1.3)

g(x)=|x|^{\beta_{1}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta_{2}}},\qquad\beta_{1}>0,\quad\beta_{2}\in(0,n),

are
q
-growing for any
q\geq\beta_{1}
, even if they diverge positively as
|x|\to+\infty
, see
Definition

2.2
and
Remark

2.4
. In particular attractive-repulsive kernels as in (
1.3
) represent a possible choice in the definition of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in
Theorem

1.1
.
Minimization problems for attractive-repulsive functionals have been widely studied in the last years.
Existence and nonexistence results are addressed in
[
BCT18
,
FL18
,
FL21
]
, while stability and uniqueness of minimizers have been respectively studied in
[
BCT24
,
Lop19
]
.
We refer to
[
Car23
,
CP22
,
CPT23
,
CDM16
]
for further results about analogous problems.

We remark that, by a symmetry argument, analyzing the Euler-Lagrange equation of problem (
1.2
), it is possible to verify that the sets
B^{\lambda}(m,x)
are not volume constrained minimizers of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
; actually, the isoperimetric bubbles
B^{\lambda}(m,x)
are not even volume constrained critical points of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
.
It is left as a future project to study quantitative properties of minimizers to (
1.2
), such as the proximity of minimizers from bubbles
B^{\lambda}(m,x)
in terms of the smallness of the mass.
Related results, concerning liquid drops or crystals lying in equilibrium under the action of a potential energy, are addressed in
[
FM11
,
MM16
]
.

For large masses and for suitable choices of repulsive kernels
g
, the repulsive interaction dominates and the variational problem in
Theorem

1.1
does not admit a minimizer.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

g(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta}},\qquad 0<\beta<n,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
For every
\beta\in(0,2]
, there exists
\tilde{m}>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
\beta
,
G
, such that for all
m\geq\tilde{m}
the minimization problem

\inf\{\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)\ :\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,|E|=m\}

has no minimizers.

Therefore, for a general repulsive kernel
g
, existence may fail for masses large enough, since minimizers tend to split in two or more components which then move apart one from the other in order to decrease the nonlocal energy.
To capture this phenomenon, it is convenient to introduce a generalized energy defined as

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(E):=\inf_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{h}(E),

where

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{h}(E):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E^{i})\ :\ E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}E^{i},E^{i}\cap E^{j}=\emptyset\quad{\rm for}\;1\leq i\neq j\leq h\right\}.

Note that in this functional the interaction between different components is not evaluated, which corresponds to consider them “at infinite distance” one from the other.
By considering
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}
instead of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
, we can prove the following generalized existence result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
g
be a
\mathscr{R}
-admissible
q
-growing function,
q\geq 0
, and let
G
be a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function.
For every
m>0
there exists a minimizer of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}
in the class

\mathcal{A}_{m}=\left\{\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\;{\rm measurable}\ :\ |\Omega|=m\right\}.

More precisely, there exist a set
E\in\mathcal{A}
and a subdivision
E=\cup_{j=1}^{h}E^{j}
, with pairwise disjoint sets
E^{j}
, such that

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(E)=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E^{j})=\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}_{m}\right\}.

Moreover, for every
1\leq j\leq h
, the set
E^{j}
is a minimizer of both the standard and the generalized energy for its volume, i.e.

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(E^{j})=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E^{j})=\min\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,\,|\Omega|=|E^{j}|\right\}.

Remark 1.4
.

We note that, if
g
is infinitesimal, then

\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}_{m}\right\}=\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}_{m}\right\}.

The proof can be easily adapted by
[
NP21
, Lemma 3.4]
, with attention given to translating the components of a partition without changing the
n
-th component.
In this case, every minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
is also a minimizer of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}
.
It is left as a future project to study connections between minimizers and generalized minimizers when
g
is not infinitesimal.

Strategy of the proof and comments

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is divided into several steps.
In the spirit of
[
KM14
]
, the existence of minimizers in the small mass regime follows by the direct method of the calculus of variations, see
Theorem

3.1
, once we show that for sufficiently small mass every minimizing sequence of the energy may be replaced by another minimizing sequence where all sets have uniformly bounded diameter, see
Lemma

3.5
.
We remark that we heavily use the quantitative isoperimetric inequality for the capillarity problem proved in
[
PP24
]
, which estimates the Fraenkel asymmetry of a competitor with respect to the optimal sets in terms of the energy deficit.
Note that it is unclear at the moment how to apply stronger isoperimetric inequalities of Fuglede-type
[
CL12
,
Fug89
]
for nearly spherical sets in the present capillarity framework; instead, stronger isoperimetric inequalities have been used as fundamental tools, for example, in
[
AFM13
,
BC14
,
CFP23
]
.
In fact, the classical Fuglede’s method relies on the precise knowledge of the eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, which is not available for
P_{\lambda}
on optimal sets for generic
\lambda\in(-1,1)
.
Moreover, in our case it is in general not possible to globally parametrize
C^{1}
-close boundaries one on the other as normal graphs.

The boundedness result in
Theorem

1.1
follows once we show that minimizers enjoy uniform density estimates at boundary points.
In order to do so, we prove that, under suitable conditions on the Riesz potential, minimizers are
(K,r_{0})
-quasiminimal sets for all masses, see
Definition

3.10
and
Lemma

3.13
.
Indeed quasiminimal sets have well-known topological regularity properties (
Theorem

3.11
), which easily guarantee boundedness, see
Theorem

3.8
.
Note that the lack of symmetry of the problem, due to the presence of gravitational potential and the fact that ambient space is a half-space, forces us to deal with the vertical direction in a separate way, see
Lemma

3.12
.

The absence of holes is based on the combination of some techniques from
[
KM14
]
and
[
NP21
]
.
We firstly prove some density estimates which improve, under suitable hypotheses on
g
, the analogous estimates for quasiminimal sets, by providing bounds independent of the minimizer, see
Lemma

4.2
.
In fact, this allows to prove the boundedness in the vertical direction with a bound independent of the minimizer, see
Lemma

4.8
, and to obtain absence of holes arguing by contradiction, see
Theorem

4.7
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is based on the combination of some techniques from
[
FN21
]
and
[
KM14
]
and exploits some estimates on the diameter and the nonlocal potential energy of minimizers.
We remark that the range of the exponent
\beta
in
Theorem

1.2
is the same as the analogous nonexistence results in the classical setting
[
CNT22
,
FKN16
,
FN21
,
KM14
,
LO14
]
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
is inspired by
[
NP21
]
and exploits the isoperimetric inequality for the capillarity functional
P_{\lambda}
. In our case the argument must be modified to take into account the presence of the gravitational energy and, as before, estimates in the vertical direction must be treated separately. We remark that also the possible choices for the kernels
g
in our
Theorem

1.3
allow for more freedom than those considered in
[
NP21
]
.

2.1.
Sets of finite perimeter

We recall basic definitions and properties regarding sets of finite perimeter, referring to
[
AFP00
,
Mag12
]
for a complete treatment on the subject.
The perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in an open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

(2.1)

P(E,A):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T(x)\,{\rm d}x\ :\ T\in C_{c}^{1}(A;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\,\|T\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

Denoting
P(E):=P(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter if
P(E)<+\infty
.
In such a case, the characteristic function
\chi_{E}
has a distributional gradient
D\chi_{E}
that is a vector-valued Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\int_{E}{\rm div}\,T(x)\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}T\,{\rm d}D\chi_{E},\quad\forall T\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

It can be proved that the set function
P(E,\cdot)
defined in (
2.1
) is the restriction of a nonnegative Borel measure to open sets. The measure
P(E,\cdot)
coincides with the total variation
|D\chi_{E}|
of the distributional gradient, and it is concentrated on the reduced boundary

\partial^{*}E:=\left\{x\in{\rm spt}|D\chi_{E}|\ :\ \exists\,\nu^{E}(x):=-\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))}{|D\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))|}\text{ with }|\nu^{E}(x)|=1\right\}.

Introducing the sets of density
t\in[0,1]
points for
E
defined by

E^{(t)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ \lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=t\right\},

we have that the reduced boundary coincides both with
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{(1)}\cup E^{(0)})
and with the set
E^{(1/2)}
up to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible sets. The vector
\nu^{E}
is called the generalized outer normal of
E
.
Moreover
P(E,\cdot)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
, and the distributional gradient can be written as
D\chi_{E}=-\nu^{E}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial^{*}E
.

2.2.
Preliminary results on the capillarity functional

We recall basic properties on the functional
P_{\lambda}
.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with measure
|E|=m\in(0,+\infty)
.
Note that

P_{\lambda}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\setminus H}1-\lambda\Braket{e_{n},\nu^{E}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1};

in particular
P_{\lambda}(E)>0

[
PP24
, Remark 2.2, Corollary 2.5]
.

The sets
B^{\lambda}(m,x)
uniquely minimize the problem

\inf\{P_{\lambda}(E)\ :\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|E|=m\},

and this is encoded in the isoperimetric inequality
[
Mag12
, Theorem 19.21]
,
[
PP24
, Theorem 3.3]

(2.2)

P_{\lambda}(E)\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

The previous isoperimetric inequality can be strengthened in a quantitative version.
We define the Fraenkel asymmetry

\alpha_{\lambda}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta B^{\lambda}(m,x)|}{m}\ :\ x\in\{x_{n}=0\}\right\},

and the isoperimetric deficit

D_{\lambda}(E):=\frac{P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))}{P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))},

for any
E\subset\{x_{n}>0\}
with volume
|E|=m
.
Then the following sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality holds

Theorem 2.1

(
[
PP24
]
)
.

There exists a constant
c(n,\lambda)>0
such that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
with finite measure there holds

(2.3)

\alpha_{\lambda}(E)^{2}\leq c(n,\lambda)D_{\lambda}(E).

2.3.
Definitions

We provide some definitions for the kernels of Riesz-type potential
\mathscr{R}
and gravity-type potential
\mathscr{G}
.
In particular, in the following
Definition

2.2
and
Definition

2.5
we will impose pointwise requirements on the functions
g
,
G
, i.e., it is understood that we fix pointwise defined representatives for the functions
g
,
G
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is
\mathscr{R}
-admissible if
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
\mathscr{R}(B_{1})<\infty
.

A
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is
q
-growing, for some
q\in[0,\infty)
, if for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and every
\alpha>1
it holds

g(\alpha x)\leq\alpha^{q}g(x).

A
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is infinitesimal if

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}g(x)=0.

A
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is symmetric if

g(-x)=g(x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

A
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)
is coercive if

g(x)\to+\infty\qquad{\rm as}\;|x|\to+\infty.

Given two measurable sets
L
,
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
we let

\mathscr{R}(L,M):=\int_{L}\int_{M}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.

Remark 2.3
.

The functions
\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta}}
, for
\beta\in(0,n)
, are
\mathscr{R}
-admissible,
0
-growing, infinitesimal and symmetric.

Remark 2.4
.

The attractive-repulsive kernels
|x|^{\beta_{1}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta_{2}}}
, for
\beta_{1}>0
and
\beta_{2}\in(0,n)
, are
\mathscr{R}
-admissible
\beta_{1}
-growing symmetric functions.
At the same time they diverge positively as
|x|\to+\infty
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
G:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is
\mathscr{G}
-admissible if
G\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,\infty)
,

(2.4)

\sup_{t\in(0,2)}G(t)<\infty,

and

(2.5)

G(\alpha t)\leq\alpha^{n}G(t),\qquad\forall\alpha>1,\,t>0.

A
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function
G:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is coercive if

G(t)\to+\infty\qquad{\rm as}\;t\to+\infty.

Remark 2.6
.

The identity function
G(t)=t
on
(0,+\infty)
is a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function.

Remark 2.7
.

Conditions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) easily imply

G(t)=G(t\cdot 1)\leq t^{n}G(1)\leq c(G)\,t^{n},\qquad\forall t>1.

3.
Existence of minimizers for small masses

3.1.
Existence

The goal of this Section is to prove the following

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
g
be a
\mathscr{R}
-admissible
q
-growing function and let
G
be a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function.
There exists a mass
\bar{m}=\bar{m}(n,\lambda,g,G,q)>0
such that, for all
m\in(0,\bar{m})
, there exists a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in the class

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\{\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\,{\rm measurable}\ :\ |\Omega|=m\}.

We begin by proving some preparatory lemmas, which estimate the energy of some competitors.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
There exists a constant
c=c(n,\lambda,g,

G)
such that

\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq c\,m,\qquad\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq c\,m

for every
0<m\leq|B^{\lambda}|
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\bar{Q}_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, with
l>0
, the cube
[-l,l]\times\dots\times[-l,l]\times[0,2l]
.
For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
the cube
\bar{Q}_{1}
is the essential union of
(2N)^{n}
disjoint isometric cubes
Q_{\frac{1}{2N}}^{i}
of side
1/N
.
If
\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}}\subset\bar{Q}_{1}
is the cube
\left[-\frac{1}{2N},\frac{1}{2N}\right]\times\dots\times\left[-\frac{1}{2N},\frac{1}{2N}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{1}{N}\right]
, evidently

\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{1})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{(2N)^{n}}\mathscr{R}(Q_{\frac{1}{2N}}^{i})=(2N)^{n}\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}}).

Moreover

2^{n}\sup_{(0,2)}G=|\bar{Q}_{1}|\sup_{(0,2)}G=(2N)^{n}\frac{|\bar{Q}_{1}|}{(2N)^{n}}\sup_{(0,2)}G=(2N)^{n}\int_{\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}}}\sup_{(0,2)}G\,{\rm d}x\geq(2N)^{n}\mathscr{G}(\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}}).

For any
0<r\leq 1
we denote by
N
the integer part of
\frac{1}{2r}
, so that
(2r)^{-1}\leq 2N\leq r^{-1}
.
The above estimates, together with
4rN\geq 1
, imply that

\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{r})\leq(4r)^{n}N^{n}\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}})\leq 2^{n}\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{1})r^{n}

and

\mathscr{G}(\bar{Q}_{r})\leq(4r)^{n}N^{n}\mathscr{G}(\bar{Q}_{\frac{1}{2N}})\leq 4^{n}\left(\sup_{(0,2)}G\right)r^{n}.

If
r=\frac{m^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}\leq 1
, since
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\subset\bar{Q}_{1}
, we get
B^{\lambda}(m)\subset\bar{Q}_{r}
and we conclude that

\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq\mathscr{R}(\bar{Q}_{r})\leq cr^{n}\leq cm

and

\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq\mathscr{G}(\bar{Q}_{r})\leq cr^{n}\leq cm.

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and infinitesimal and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
For every
m\geq 1
there exists
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E|=m
such that
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)\leq cm
for some
c
depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
and
G
.

Proof.

Let us consider the set
E
given by a collection of
N\geq 1
spherical caps
\left\{B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})\right\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
of equal volume
v
and with centers located at
x_{i}=iRe_{1}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, with
R
large enough so that
B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})
are pairwise disjoint.
We choose the number
N
as the smallest integer for which the volume of each spherical cap does not exceed
\min\left\{1,|B^{\lambda}|\right\}
.
In particular
Nv=m
and
N=\left\lceil\frac{m}{\min\{1,|B^{\lambda}|\}}\right\rceil
.
Note that, by
[
PP24
, Lemma 3.1]
, since
v\leq 1\leq m=|E|
,

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}\left(\cup_{i=1}^{N}B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})\right)&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i}))=c(n,\lambda)Nv^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq c(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{m}{\min\{1,|B^{\lambda}|\}}+1\right)v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)\,(m\,v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+v^{\frac{n-1}{n}})\leq c(n,\lambda)\,(m\,1^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+m\,1^{\frac{n-1}{n}})=c(n,\lambda)m.\end{split}

Moreover, let
R
be so large that
g(x-y)<\frac{1}{N}
for every
x\in B^{\lambda}(v,x_{j})
,
y\in B^{\lambda}(v,x_{k})
with
j\neq k
.
Then, by
Lemma

3.2
, since
v\leq 1\leq m=|E|
,

\begin{split}\mathscr{R}(E)&=\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})}\int_{\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})}\int_{B^{\lambda}(v,x_{1})\cup\dots\cup\widehat{B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})}\cup\dots\cup B^{\lambda}(v,x_{N})}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)Nv+N\frac{1}{N}v(m-v)\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)m+(m-v)\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)m,\end{split}

where the
B^{\lambda}(v,x_{1})\cup\dots\cup\widehat{B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})}\cup\dots\cup B^{\lambda}(v,x_{N})
denotes union over all the bubbles except for
B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})
.
Finally, by
Lemma

3.2

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)&=P_{\lambda}(E)+\mathscr{R}(E)+\mathscr{G}(E)\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)|E|+c(n,\lambda,g,G)|E|+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i}))\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)|E|+c(n,\lambda,g,G)|E|+c(n,\lambda,g,G)Nv=c(n,\lambda,g,G)m.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter. Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
q
-growing and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
If
\alpha>1
, then

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\alpha E)\leq\alpha^{2n+q}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E).

Proof.

Note that, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, then
\alpha E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
.
Since
\alpha>1
, by the positivity of
P_{\lambda}
we get

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(\alpha E)&=\alpha^{n-1}P_{\lambda}(E)\leq\alpha^{2n+q}P_{\lambda}(E).\end{split}

Since
g
is
q
-growing, we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{R}(\alpha E)&=\int\int_{(\alpha E)^{2}}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\alpha^{2n}\int\int_{E\times E}g(\alpha(y-x))\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq\alpha^{2n+q}\int\int_{E\times E}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x=\alpha^{2n+q}\mathscr{R}(E).\end{split}

Finally, by (
2.5
) we get

\begin{split}\int_{\alpha E}G(x_{n})\,{\rm d}x&=\alpha^{n}\int_{E}G(\alpha x_{n})\,{\rm d}x\leq\alpha^{2n+q}\int_{E}G(x_{n})\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

∎

The following lemma allows to suitably localize minimizing sequences with sufficiently small volume.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
q
-growing and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
There exists
\bar{m}>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
,
G
and
q
, such that, for every
m\in(0,\bar{m})
and every set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|F|=m
, there exists a set of finite perimeter
L
with

(3.1)

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(L)\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)\qquad{\rm and}\qquad L\subset\bar{Q}_{1}:=[-1,1]\times\dots\times[-1,1]\times[0,2].

Proof.

Throughout the proof we will assume that
\bar{m}<\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{4^{n}}
.
If
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)
, then the assertion of the lemma is proved by choosing
L=B^{\lambda}(m)
.
Then we can assume, by
Lemma

3.2
, that

(3.2)

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)<\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\max\left\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right\}.

By
Lemma

3.2

(3.3)

\begin{split}D_{\lambda}(F)&=\frac{c(n,\lambda)}{m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}(P_{\lambda}(F)-P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m)))\\
&\leq\frac{c(n,\lambda)}{m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}([\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))-\mathscr{R}(F)]+[\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m))-\mathscr{G}(F)])\\
&\leq\frac{c(n,\lambda)}{m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}(\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))+\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m)))\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

By the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (
2.3
)

(3.4)

\alpha_{\lambda}(F)\leq c(n,\lambda)\sqrt{D_{\lambda}(F)}\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}

and, after a suitable translation,

|B^{\lambda}(m)\Delta F|\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}.

Since
|F|=m=|B^{\lambda}(m)|
we also have

|B^{\lambda}(m)\Delta F|=2|F\setminus B^{\lambda}(m)|

and

(3.5)

|F\setminus B^{\lambda}(m)|\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}.

For any
\varrho>0
let
F_{1}=F\cap B_{\varrho}(0)
and
F_{2}=F\setminus B_{\varrho}(0)
.
Note that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\bar{m}
sufficiently small such that, if
\varrho\geq\frac{m^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}R_{\lambda}=:\varrho_{m}
, with
m<\bar{m}
, then

(3.6)

|F_{2}|\leq\varepsilon|F_{1}|.

Indeed, since
B^{\lambda}(|B^{\lambda}|)\subset B_{R_{\lambda}}(0)
, we get
B^{\lambda}(m)\subset B_{\varrho}(0)
.
Moreover, by (
3.5
) and for sufficiently small
\bar{m}
we estimate

\begin{split}|F_{1}|=|F\cap B_{\varrho}(0)|&\geq|F\cap B^{\lambda}(m)|\\
&=|F|-|F\setminus B^{\lambda}(m)|\\
&\geq|B^{\lambda}|\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)-c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}\\
&\geq\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{2}\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right).\end{split}

and

|F\setminus B_{\varrho}(0)|\leq|F\setminus B^{\lambda}(m)|\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{|B^{\lambda}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}|F_{1}|\leq\varepsilon|F_{1}|.

Let us define the monotonically decreasing function
U(\varrho)=|F\setminus B_{\varrho}(0)|
.

We now distinguish two cases.
Let us firstly prove (
3.1
) when we assume that

(3.7)

\Sigma:=P_{\lambda}(F_{1})+P_{\lambda}(F_{2})-P_{\lambda}(F)>\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})\qquad\forall\varrho\in\left(\varrho_{m},\frac{R_{\lambda}}{2}\right)

By (

) we have

U(\varrho_{m})=|F\setminus B^{\lambda}(m)|\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,m^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\varrho_{m}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}

Furthermore, by (
2.2
) and (
3.7
) we have

\begin{split}-2\frac{{\rm d}U(\varrho)}{{\rm d}\varrho}&=\Sigma>\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(F\setminus B_{\varrho}(0))\geq c(n,\lambda)U^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(\varrho).\end{split}

In particular we have

\begin{cases}\quad\frac{{\rm d}U(\varrho)}{{\rm d}\varrho}\leq-c(n,\lambda)U^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(\varrho)\qquad&{\rm for}\,{\rm a.e.}\;\varrho\in\left(\varrho_{m},\frac{R_{\lambda}}{2}\right)\\
\quad U(\varrho_{m})\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\varrho_{m}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}.\qquad&\end{cases}

By ODE comparison we deduce that, if
\bar{m}<1
,

\begin{split}U(\varrho)^{\frac{1}{n}}&\leq U(\varrho_{m})^{\frac{1}{n}}-c(n,\lambda)\,(\varrho-\varrho_{m})\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,\varrho_{m}^{1+\frac{1}{2n}}-c(n,\lambda)\varrho+c(n,\lambda)\varrho_{m}\\
&=c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,m^{\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}}-c(n,\lambda)\,\varrho+c(n,\lambda)\,m^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,m^{\frac{1}{n}}-c(n,\lambda)\,\varrho+c(n,\lambda)\,m^{\frac{1}{n}}\\
&=c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,m^{\frac{1}{n}}-c(n,\lambda)\,\varrho.\end{split}

For
\bar{m}
sufficiently small, it follows that

for
\varrho\geq\frac{R_{\lambda}}{2}
, and we obtain (
3.1
) with
L=F
.

Let us prove (
3.1
) assuming that

(3.8)

\Sigma\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})

holds for some
\varrho_{0}\in\left(\varrho_{m},\frac{R_{\lambda}}{2}\right)
.
Let
m_{1}:=|F_{1}|
,
m_{2}:=|F_{2}|
and

, with
\varepsilon

Let us also denote

.
In particular

By

3.4

(3.9)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(lF_{1})&\leq l^{2n+q}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})+\left(l^{2n+q}-1\right)\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1}).\end{split}

Choosing

, we have

By (

By the definition of

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)&\leq\mathscr{R}(F_{1})+\mathscr{G}(F_{1})+\mathscr{R}(F_{2})+\mathscr{G}(F_{2})-\mathscr{R}(F)-\mathscr{G}(F)+\Sigma-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})+\gamma K\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})\\

By positivity of

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})>P_{\lambda}(F_{2})\geq c(n,\lambda)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})&=P_{\lambda}(F)+\mathscr{R}(F)+\mathscr{G}(F)-P_{\lambda}(F_{1})-\mathscr{R}(F_{1})-\mathscr{G}(F_{1})-P_{\lambda}(F_{2})+P_{\lambda}(F_{2})\\

Remark 3.6
.

Arguing exactly as in (

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

By

there exists a minimizing sequence with uniformly bounded sets.
The lower semicontinuity of

[

, Lemma 3.7]

The following proposition states that, if the nonlocal kernel
g
and the gravitational term
G
are coercive, we have existence of minimizers for
all
values of
m
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
g
be a
\mathscr{R}
-admissible coercive function and let
G
be a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible coercive function.
Then, for every
m>0
, there exists a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in the class

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\{\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H\,{\rm measurable}\ :\ |\Omega|=m\}.

Proof.

Let us consider a minimizing sequence
\left\{E_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{A}_{m}
.
In particular

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E_{i})=C<+\infty.

By
[
PP24
, Corollary 2.5]
it holds

P(E_{i})\leq\frac{2}{1-\lambda}P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\leq\frac{2}{1-\lambda}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E_{i})\leq C,\qquad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

By
[
ANP22
, Lemma 2.10]
and
[
LRV22
, Corollary 3.25]
we get the existence of a constant
\bar{c}=\bar{c}\left(m,n,\sup_{i}P(E_{i})\right)

>0
such that for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
x_{i}\in\left\{x_{n}>0\right\}
with

\left|E_{i}\cap B_{1}(x_{i})\right|\geq\bar{c}.

By the coercivity of
G
we have that
\left(x_{i}\right)_{n}
is uniformly bounded.
Indeed, if by contradiction
\left(x_{i}\right)_{n}\to+\infty
, then

By the lower semicontinuity of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
under strong

convergence, the existence of a minimizer follows if we show that for every

and

there exists

with

In order to prove (

\begin{split}C\geq\mathscr{R}(E_{i})&\geq\int_{E_{i}}\int_{E_{i}\setminus B_{R}(x)}g(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\geq\left(\inf_{|x|>R}g(x)\right)\int_{E_{i}}\left|E_{i}\setminus B_{R}(x)\right|\,{\rm d}x\\
&\geq\left(\inf_{|x|>R}g(x)\right)\int_{E_{i}\cap B_{1}(x_{i})}\left|E_{i}\setminus B_{R}(x)\right|\,{\rm d}x\geq\bar{c}\left(\inf_{|x|>R}g(x)\right)\left|E_{i}\setminus B_{R+1}(x_{i})\right|\end{split}

and

\left|E_{i}\setminus B_{R+1}(x_{i})\right|\leq\frac{C}{\bar{c}}\left(\inf_{|x|>R}g(x)\right)^{-1}\to 0\qquad{\rm as}\quad R\to+\infty.

∎

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\hat{F})<\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)
, then the proof is concluded with
G=\hat{F}
. So we can assume that for any
R>0
there holds
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\hat{F})\geq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)
. Hence for
R
large enough we claim that

(4.3)

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\hat{F})\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\max\left\{m,m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right\}.

Indeed, if
m\geq 1
, estimate (
4.3
) follows by the same computations done in the proof of
Corollary

3.3
.
If instead
m<1
, by
[
PP24
, Lemma 3.1]
we find

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}\left(\cup_{i=1}^{N}B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i})\right)&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i}))=c(n,\lambda)Nv^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq c(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{m}{\min\{1,|B^{\lambda}|\}}+1\right)v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\leq c(n,\lambda)(m\,v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+v^{\frac{n-1}{n}})\leq c(n,\lambda)(m+m^{\frac{n-1}{n}})=c(n,\lambda)m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

Arguing as in
Corollary

3.3
, if
R
is so large that
g(x-y)<\frac{1}{N}
for every
x\in B^{\lambda}(v,x_{j})
,
y\in B^{\lambda}(v,x_{k})
with
j\neq k
, then

\begin{split}\mathscr{R}(E)&\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)m\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)&=P_{\lambda}(E)+\mathscr{R}(E)+\mathscr{G}(E)\leq c(n,\lambda)|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+c(n,\lambda,g,G)|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(v,x_{i}))\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)m^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\end{split}

therefore (
4.3
) holds.

We want to show that if
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, then
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})<\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)
, implying the claim with
G=\tilde{F}
.
By
Lemma

3.4

(4.4)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(lF_{1})&\leq l^{2n+q}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})+(l^{2n+q}-1)\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1}).\end{split}

Choosing
\varepsilon\leq 1
, we have
1\leq l\leq 2^{\frac{1}{n}}
, and by Taylor’s formula we obtain
l^{2n+q}-1=(1+\gamma)^{2+q/n}-1\leq\gamma K
for some
K>0
independent of
\gamma
, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.
By (
4.4
) we arrive at

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})\leq\gamma K\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1}).

By the definition of
\Sigma
and since
\mathscr{R}(F_{1})+\mathscr{R}(F_{2})\leq\mathscr{R}(F)

(4.5)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)&\leq\mathscr{R}(F_{1})+\mathscr{G}(F_{1})+\mathscr{R}(F_{2})+\mathscr{G}(F_{2})-\mathscr{R}(F)-\mathscr{G}(F)+\Sigma-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})+\gamma K\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})+\gamma K\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1}).\end{split}

By positivity of
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{G}
and the isoperimetric inequality, we have
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2})>P_{\lambda}(F_{2})\geq c(n,\lambda)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
.
As in (
3.11
) we obtain
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{1})\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)
.
By (
4.3
), since
\gamma m\leq 2m_{2}
and
\gamma\leq\varepsilon
, (
4.5
) turns into

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})-\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)&\leq-c(n,\lambda)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\gamma K\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F)\leq-c(n,\lambda)m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+C(n,\lambda,g,G,q)\max\left\{m_{2},\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right\}.\end{split}

Since
m_{2}\leq\varepsilon
by (
4.2
), for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small the assertion of the lemma holds with
G=\tilde{F}
. ∎

Next lemma is an improvement of the standard density estimate for quasiminimizers.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible,
q
-growing and infinitesimal, and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible. Then there exists
c=c(n,\lambda,g,G,q)>0
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a minimizer of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
with
|E|=m
,
m>0
.
Then for almost every
x\in E
there holds

|E\cap B_{1}(x)|\geq c\min\{1,m\}.

Proof.

For
r>0
and
x\in E
, let
F_{1}^{r}:=E\setminus B_{r}(x)
and
F_{2}^{r}:=E\cap B_{r}(x)
.
Note that
|F_{1}^{r}|+|F_{2}^{r}|=m
and
|F_{2}^{r}|\leq\omega_{n}r^{n}
.
Then there exists
C>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
,
G
and
q
, such that (
4.2
) holds for all
r\leq r_{1}:=C\min\left\{1,\sqrt[n]{m}\right\}
.
Note that we can choose
C\leq 1
.
Since
E
is a minimizer,
Lemma

4.1
implies that (
4.1
) cannot be satisfied for any
r\leq r_{1}
.
Equivalently, recalling also
[
PP24
, Corollary 2.5]
, for all
r\leq r_{1}
we have

(4.6)

\Sigma^{r}:=P_{\lambda}(F_{1}^{r})+P_{\lambda}(F_{2}^{r})-P_{\lambda}(E)>\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(F_{2}^{r})>\frac{1}{2}P_{\lambda}(F_{2}^{r})\geq\frac{1-\lambda}{4}P(F_{2}^{r}).

At the same time,
for almost every
r
we have

\Sigma^{r}=2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}(x)).

By (
4.6
), for a constant

there holds

(4.7)

2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))>c(n,\lambda)\,(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap B_{r}(x))+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))),

for almost every

Let us now distinguish two cases.
If there exists

, then by the choice of

we get

and the proof is concluded.

Let us assume that

for all

.
Then we rearrange terms in (

) and apply the relative isoperimetric inequality
[

, Proposition 12.37]
to the right-hand side to obtain

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))\geq c(n,\lambda)|E\cap B_{r}(x)|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Let us denote

.
Then
\frac{{\rm d}U(r)}{{\rm d}r}=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E^{(1)}\cap\partial B_{r}(x))
for all

and

\frac{{\rm d}U(r)}{{\rm d}r}\geq c(n,\lambda)U^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(r)\qquad\forall r\in\left(\frac{r_{1}}{2},r_{1}\right).

For
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in E
, we have
U(r)>0
for all
r>0
and ODE comparison in
r\in\left(\frac{r_{1}}{2},r_{1}\right)
implies that

U^{1/n}(r)\geq U^{1/n}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{2}\right)+c(n,\lambda)\left(r-\frac{r_{1}}{2}\right)\geq c(n,\lambda)\left(r-\frac{r_{1}}{2}\right)\qquad\forall r\in\left(\frac{r_{1}}{2},r_{1}\right).

Then the lemma follows as

c(n,\lambda,g,G)\min\{1,m\}=c(n,\lambda)r_{1}^{n}\leq U(r_{1})=|E\cap B_{r_{1}}(x)|=\left|E\cap B_{C\min\left\{1,\sqrt[n]{m}\right\}}(x)\right|\leq|E\cap B_{1}(x)|.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

g(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\beta}},\qquad\beta\in(0,n),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a minimizer for
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
with
|E|=m
,
m>0
.
Then

\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\leq c(n)\,m.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\{\pm e_{n}\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Denote

\begin{split}&E^{+}_{\nu,t}:=E\cap\{\Braket{\nu,x}>t\}\\
&E^{-}_{\nu,t}:=E\cap\{\Braket{\nu,x}<t\}.\end{split}

Let
\nu_{1}:=\frac{\nu_{hor}}{|\nu_{hor}|}
, where
\nu_{hor}
is the orthogonal projection of
\nu
on
\{x_{n}=0\}
.
For any
\varrho\geq 0
, the set

E^{+}_{\nu,t}\cup(E^{-}_{\nu,t}-\varrho\nu_{1})

has measure
m
and, by minimality of
E
,

(4.9)

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E^{+}_{\nu,t}\cup(E^{-}_{\nu,t}-\varrho\nu_{1}))\geq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E).

For any
\varrho>0
and for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}

P_{\lambda}(E^{+}_{\nu,t}\cup(E^{-}_{\nu,t}-\varrho\nu_{1}))=P_{\lambda}(E^{+}_{\nu,t})+P_{\lambda}(E^{-}_{\nu,t})\leq P_{\lambda}(E)+2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{\Braket{\nu,x}=t\}).

For any
\varrho\geq 0
we have

\begin{split}\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}\cup(E^{-}_{\nu,t}-\varrho\nu_{1})}\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}\cup(E^{-}_{\nu,t}-\varrho\nu_{1})}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x=&\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&+2\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y+\varrho\nu_{1}|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

Moreover

\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y+\varrho\nu_{1}|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\to 0

as
\varrho\to\infty
.
Hence, by (
4.9
), letting
\varrho\to\infty
, we get

\begin{split}&P_{\lambda}(E)+2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{\Braket{\nu,x}=t\})+\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\mathscr{G}(E^{+}_{\nu,t})+\mathscr{G}(E^{-}_{\nu,t})\\
&\geq P_{\lambda}(E)+\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\mathscr{G}(E).\end{split}

Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{\Braket{\nu,x}\}=t)&\geq\int_{E^{+}_{\nu,t}}\int_{E^{-}_{\nu,t}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{E}\int_{E}\chi_{\{\Braket{\nu,\cdot}<t\}}(y)\chi_{\{\Braket{\nu,\cdot}>t\}}(x)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

Integrating the last inequality with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by Fubini’s theorem we get

\begin{split}m&\geq\int_{E}\int_{E}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{\{\Braket{\nu,\cdot}<t\}}(y)\chi_{\{\Braket{\nu,\cdot}>t\}}(x)\,{\rm d}t\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x=\int_{E}\int_{E}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{(\Braket{\nu,y},\Braket{\nu,x})}(t)\,{\rm d}t\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{\Braket{\nu,x-y}_{+}}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

Further integrating over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\{\pm e_{n}\}
, since

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Braket{\nu,x-y}_{+}\,{\rm d}\nu=c(n)|x-y|,

by symmetry of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we conclude that

|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|m\geq\int_{E}\int_{E}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Braket{\nu,x-y}_{+}\,{\rm d}\nu\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x=c(n)\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x.

∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Lemma

4.4
and
Lemma

4.5
easily imply

1.2
for
\beta\in(0,1]
and mass
m
sufficiently large.
Then it remains to consider
\beta\in(1,2]
.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a volume constrained minimizer for
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
with
|E|=m
. By
Lemma

4.4
, for
m
large enough, we can assume that
{\rm diam}\,E>4
.
We observe first that

(4.10)

\frac{1}{r^{\beta-1}}\left|\{(x,y)\in E\times E\ :\ |x-y|<r\}\right|=\frac{1}{r^{\beta-1}}\int_{E}|E\cap B_{r}(x)|\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{\omega_{n}r^{n}}{r^{\beta-1}}|E|\xrightarrow[r\to 0]{}0.

Applying the coarea formula on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the Lipschitz function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

and that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\left|\{(x,y)\in E\times E\ :\ |x-y|<r\}\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ |x-y|=r\}}\chi_{E\times E}(x,y)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{2n-1}(x,y)\quad\text{for a.e. $r>0$,}

\begin{split}\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\chi_{E\times E}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}|\nabla f|\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ |x-y|=r\}}\frac{\chi_{E\times E}(x,y)}{r^{\beta-1}}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{2n-1}(x,y)\,{\rm d}r\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{\beta-1}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\left|\{(x,y)\in E\times E\ :\ |x-y|<r\}\right|\,{\rm d}r\\

&\qquad\qquad-\int_{\varepsilon}^{+\infty}(1-\beta)\frac{1}{r^{\beta}}|\{(x,y)\in E\times E\ :\ |x-y|<r\}|\,{\rm d}r.\end{split}

4.10
) and

4.6

\begin{split}\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x&=(\beta-1)\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\{(x,y)\in E\times E\ :\ |x-y|<r\}|\frac{\,{\rm d}r}{r^{\beta}}\\

.

, we get a contradiction for

large enough.

Theorem 4.7

Let

\mathscr{R}

be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible and coercive.
There exists
\bar{m}>0
, depending on

,
\lambda
,

and

, such that, for all

, every minimizer

of

with

We begin with a preparatory lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible,
q
-growing, infinitesimal and symmetric and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible and coercive.
There exist
\bar{m}>0
and
\bar{T}>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
,
G
,

such that, for all
m\in(0,\bar{m})
, every volume constrained minimizer

of
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}
with

satisfies

|E\cap\{x_{n}>\bar{T}\}|=0.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.12
there exists
\bar{T}_{E}<\infty
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
,
G
and
E
with

\bar{T}_{E}:=\sup\{t\ :\ |E\cap\{x_{n}>t\}|>0\}.

Let
x_{E}\in E
such that

(x_{E})_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bar{T}_{E}.

By

there exists

such that, if
\bar{m}<1
, then

|E\cap B_{1}(x_{E})|\geq c\,m.

Therefore

(4.12)

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)\geq P_{\lambda}(E)+\int_{E\cap B_{1}(x_{E})}G\geq P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))+cm\inf_{((x_{E})_{n}-1,(x_{E})_{n}+1)}G.

On the other hand, by

, if

(4.13)

Putting together (
4.12
) and (

) we obtain

\inf_{((x_{E})_{n}-1,(x_{E})_{n}+1)}G\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G,q).

Since

is coercive, then
(x_{E})_{n}
, and in particular also

, is bounded by a constant independent of

∎

Remark 4.9
.

We remark that

.

At the same time,

is

-growing and

Now we are ready to prove

.

Proof of
Theorem

4.7
.

If
\bar{m}
is sufficiently small,
Lemma

4.8
guarantees that there exists
\bar{T}>0
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
g
,
G
, such that

|E\cap\{x_{n}>\bar{T}\}|=0.

Assume that there exists a set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(H\cup E)
with
v:=|F|>0
and such that (
4.11
) holds.
We aim to find a contradiction if
\bar{m}
is chosen suitably small.
Let
\bar{m}\leq|B^{\lambda}|
.
By the minimality of
E
, the isoperimetric inequality (
2.2
), the relative isoperimetric inequality outside convex sets
[
CGR07
,
FM23
]
and since
P_{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))=n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

[
PP24
, Lemma 3.1]
, we find

\begin{split}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))+\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m))&=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(B^{\lambda}(m))\geq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)\geq P_{\lambda}(E)\\
&=P_{\lambda}(E\cup F)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H)\\
&\geq P_{\lambda}(E\cup F)+(1-|\lambda|)P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)\\
&\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}(m+v)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+(1-|\lambda|)\,n\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+(1-|\lambda|)\,n\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\end{split}

which gives, by
Lemma

3.2
,

(4.14)

v\leq\left(\frac{\mathscr{R}(B^{\lambda}(m))+\mathscr{G}(B^{\lambda}(m))}{(1-|\lambda|)\,n\sqrt[n]{\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)\left(\frac{m}{(1-|\lambda|)\,n\sqrt[n]{\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

Since
\bar{m}<c(n,\lambda,g,G)
, also
v
is bounded by a suitable
\bar{v}(n,\lambda,g,G)
.
By
[
NP21
, Lemma 3.5]
there exists a continuous and increasing function
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, with
\varphi(0)=0
, such that for every two sets
F_{1}
,
F_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
one has

\mathscr{R}(F_{1},F_{2})\leq|F_{1}|\,\varphi(|F_{2}|).

Then

\begin{split}\mathscr{R}(E\cup F)-\mathscr{R}(E)&=\mathscr{R}(F,F)+2\mathscr{R}(F,E)\leq v\,\varphi(\bar{v})+2v\,\varphi(\bar{m})\leq c(n,\lambda,g,G)v.\end{split}

Let us prove that also
F
is essentially contained in
\{0<x_{n}\leq\bar{T}\}
.
To this end, assume by contradiction that

(4.15)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\setminus\left(H\cup\partial^{*}E\right)\right)>0.

Let us denote

\begin{split}&\Sigma_{E}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\setminus\left(H\cup\partial^{*}F\right)\right)\\
&\Sigma:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\left(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial^{*}F\right)\setminus H\right)\\
&\Sigma_{F}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\setminus\left(H\cup\partial^{*}E\right)\right)\\
&\Theta_{E}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\cap\partial H\right)\\
&\Theta_{F}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H\right).\end{split}

By (
4.11
) we obtain

\begin{split}\Sigma_{E}+\Sigma-\lambda\Theta_{E}&=P_{\lambda}(E)\\
&=P_{\lambda}(E\cup F)+P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\lambda\Theta_{F}\\
&=\Sigma_{E}+\Sigma_{F}-\lambda\Theta_{E}-\lambda\Theta_{F}+\Sigma+\Sigma_{F}+\lambda\Theta_{F}\\
&=\Sigma_{E}+2\Sigma_{F}+\Sigma-\lambda\Theta_{E}.\end{split}

In particular, we get
\Sigma_{F}=0
, contradicting (
4.15
).
Therefore
F\subset\{0<x_{n}\leq\bar{T}\}
and we also deduce

(4.16)

\mathscr{G}(E\cup F)-\mathscr{G}(E)=\mathscr{G}(F)\leq\int_{F}\sup_{(0,\bar{T})}G\,{\rm d}x=c(n,\lambda,g,G)\,v.

By (
4.11
), we obtain

(4.17)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E\cup F)&=P_{\lambda}(E\cup F)+\mathscr{R}(E\cup F)+\mathscr{G}(E\cup F)\\
&=P_{\lambda}(E)-P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)-\lambda\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}F\cap\partial H)+\mathscr{R}(E\cup F)+\mathscr{G}(E\cup F)\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-(1-|\lambda|)P(F,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H)+\mathscr{R}(E\cup F)+\mathscr{G}(E\cup F)\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-(1-|\lambda|)\,n\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+\mathscr{R}(E)+c\,v+\mathscr{G}(E)+c\,v\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)-(1-|\lambda|)\,n\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}v^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+c\,v<\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E),\end{split}

where the last inequality holds if
v
is sufficiently small, hence by (
4.14
) as soon as
\bar{m}
is sufficiently small.

Let
t\in(0,\infty)
be such that, if
D=\left\{x\in E\cup F,x_{n}<t\right\}
, then
|D|=m
.
Clearly
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(D)\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E\cup F)
, hence (
4.17
) implies
\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(D)<\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)
, contradicting the minimality of
E
.
∎

5.

Let us give the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

If

is a measurable set,

\mathscr{R}
-admissible function,

-admissible function and

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E)&:=P_{\lambda}(E)+\varepsilon_{1}\mathscr{R}(E)+\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{G}(E)\\

.

,

|E|=|B^{\lambda}|

\bar{\varepsilon}:=\frac{m^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{E})=\bar{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\left(P_{\lambda}(E)+\bar{\varepsilon}^{n+1}\int_{E}\int_{E}g(\bar{\varepsilon}(y-x))\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\bar{\varepsilon}\int_{E}G(\bar{\varepsilon}x_{n})\,{\rm d}x\right).

In particular we deduce that

•

for every
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g_{1}
,
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function
G_{1}
and
m>0
, there exist
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
, a
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g_{2}
and a
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function

such that, if

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(E)=P_{\lambda}(E)+\int_{E}\int_{E}g_{1}(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\int_{E}G_{1}(x_{n})\,{\rm d}x

and

is proportional to

and the variational problems are equivalent.

•

for every
\mathscr{R}
-admissible function
g_{2}
,
\mathscr{G}
-admissible function
G_{2}
,

and

, there exist a

and

where

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon,h}(E):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E^{i})\ :\ E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}E^{i},E^{i}\cap E^{j}=\emptyset\quad{\rm for}\;1\leq i\neq j\leq h\right\}.

The goal of this Section is to prove the following version of
Theorem

1.3
, suitably modified for the functional

.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and

be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
For every

(5.1)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E^{j})=\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E^{j})=\min\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,\,|\Omega|=|E^{j}|\right\}.

Note that an analogous version of

3.4
holds.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
be a set of finite perimeter.
Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
q
-growing and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
If
\alpha>1

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(\alpha E)\leq\alpha^{2n+q}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E).

We begin by proving some preparatory lemmas.
The next geometric lemma allows to modify an excessively long and thin set decreasing its energy.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible.
For every
\bar{m}>0
there exists
L(n,\lambda,\bar{m})>0
such that the following holds.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
, and let
a<b
be two numbers with
b>a+2L
and such that

\left|\left\{x\in E\ :\ a\leq x_{1}\leq b\right\}\right|<\bar{m}.

Then there exist two numbers
a^{+}\in[a,a+L]
and
b^{-}\in[b-L,b]
such that, denoting
E^{-}=E\setminus([a^{+},b^{-}]\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}

\times

(0,\infty))
and
m=|E|-|E^{-}|<\bar{m}
, one has

(5.2)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{-})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the claim for bounded sets
E
such that
\partial E\setminus\partial H
is a smooth hypersurface with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{x\in\partial E\setminus H\ :\ \nu^{E}(x)=\pm e_{j}\})=0
for any
j=1,\dots,n
.
Indeed, if
E
is a generic set of finite perimeter satisfying the hypotheses of
Lemma

5.5
, let
E_{i}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}E
be the sequence of sets given by
[
PP24
, Lemma 2.4]
.
For
i
sufficiently large,

\left|\left\{x\in E_{i}\ :\ a\leq x_{1}\leq b\right\}\right|<\bar{m}

holds.
Then there exist
a_{i}^{+}\in[a,a+L]
and
b_{i}^{-}\in[b-L,b]
such that, if we set
E_{i}^{-}=E_{i}\setminus\left([a^{+}_{i},b^{-}_{i}]\times(0,\infty)\right)
and
m_{i}=|E_{i}|-|E^{-}_{i}|
, we obtain

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{-}_{i})\leq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i})-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Up to subsequence,
a_{i}^{+}
and
b_{i}^{-}
converge to certain
a^{+}\in[a,a+L]
and
b^{-}\in[b-L,b]
respectively.
By
[
PP24
, Lemma 2.4]

P_{\lambda}(E_{i})\to P_{\lambda}(E)
.
By the lower semicontinuity of
P_{\lambda}
(
[
PP24
, Lemma 3.7]
), the continuity of
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{G}
under strong
L^{1}
convergence and the properties of
\{E_{i}\}
, if
E^{-}=E\setminus\left([a^{+},b^{-}]\times(0,\infty)\right)
then

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{-})&=P_{\lambda}(E^{-})+\varepsilon_{1}\mathscr{R}(E^{-})+\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{G}(E^{-})\\
&\leq\liminf_{i}(P_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{-})+\varepsilon_{1}\mathscr{R}(E^{-}_{i})+\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{G}(E^{-}_{i}))\\
&\leq\liminf_{i}\left(P_{\lambda}(E_{i})+\varepsilon_{1}\mathscr{R}(E_{i})+\varepsilon_{2}\mathscr{G}(E_{i})-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&=\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

So let us fix
\bar{m}
and consider
a
and
b
as in the claim, with
L
to be determined later.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
let

\sigma(t):=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(E\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ x_{1}=t\right\}\right).

If
c:=\frac{a+b}{2}
, let

\varphi(t):=\int_{t}^{c}\sigma(s)\,{\rm d}s.

We note that there exists
a^{+}\in[a,a+L]\subset[a,c)
such that, if

m_{1}:=\left|\left\{x\in E\ :\ a^{+}<x_{1}<c\right\}\right|,

then

(5.3)

\sigma(a^{+})\leq\frac{1}{8}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{1}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Indeed, assume by contradiction that for every
t\in(a,a+L)
it holds

-\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)>\frac{1}{8}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\varphi(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Then
\varphi|_{(a,a+L)}
is a positive decreasing function satisfying

\begin{cases}&\varphi(a)\leq\bar{m},\\
&|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|>\frac{1}{8}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\varphi(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{cases}

By standard ODE comparison there exists a constant
d>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and
\bar{m}
such that, if
a+d<a+L
, then
\varphi(t)\to 0
as
t\to(a+d)^{-}
. Hence
L
could be chosen so big that
a+d<a+L<c
, and then
\varphi(t)=0
for any
t\in(a+d,c)
.
It follows that there exists
a^{+}
such that (
5.3
) holds.
Similarly, up to choosing a larger
L
, we have the existence of
b^{-}\in[b-L,b]\subset(c,b]
such that, if

m_{2}:=\left|\left\{x\in E\ :\ c<x_{1}<b^{-}\right\}\right|,

then

\sigma(b^{-})\leq\frac{1}{8}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Let
E^{-}:=E\setminus\left(\left[a^{+},b^{-}\right]\times(0,\infty)\right)
and
F:=E\setminus E^{-}
.
Then

|F|=m=m_{1}+m_{2},

and, by isoperimetric inequality (

), there holds

P_{\lambda}(F)\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Hence

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E^{-})&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(F)+2(\sigma(a^{+})+\sigma(b^{-}))\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(F)+\frac{1}{4}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(F)+\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

Since
E^{-}\subset E
, then
\mathscr{R}(E^{-})\leq\mathscr{R}(E)
and
\mathscr{G}(E^{-})\leq\mathscr{G}(E)
, and we deduce (
5.2
). ∎

Let

As in the proof of

5.5
, we note that there exists
b^{-}\in[b-L,b]\subset(0,b]
such that, if

m:=\left|\left\{x\in E\ :\ 0<x_{n}<b^{-}\right\}\right|,

then

\sigma(b^{-})\leq\frac{1}{4}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

If
E^{-}:=E\setminus\left(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\left[0,b^{-}\right]\right)
and
F:=E\setminus E^{-}
,
then

|F|=m

and, by isoperimetric inequality (
2.2
),

P_{\lambda}(F)\geq n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

We establish that

\begin{split}P_{\lambda}(E^{-})&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(F)+2\sigma(b^{-})\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-P_{\lambda}(F)+\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\\
&\leq P_{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

Since
E^{-}\subset E
, then
\mathscr{R}(E^{-})\leq\mathscr{R}(E)
,
\mathscr{G}(E^{-})\leq\mathscr{G}(E)
and we deduce (
5.4
). ∎

We now prove a uniform boundedness result.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
g
be
\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
q
-growing and let
G
be
\mathscr{G}
-admissible. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
.
For every
m\in(0,\infty)
there exist
R>0
and
\bar{h}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
m
,
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{2}
,
g
,
G
and
q
, such that

\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,\,|\Omega|=m\right\}\geq\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,\,|\Omega|=m\right\},

where

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega^{i})\ :\ \Omega=\cup_{i=1}^{\bar{h}}\Omega^{i},\,\Omega^{i}\cap\Omega^{j}=\emptyset,\,{\rm diam}\,\Omega^{i}\leq R\quad\forall 1\leq i\neq j\leq\bar{h}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
M(n,\lambda,m,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},g,G,q)\in\mathbb{N}
be a natural number to be determined later and let us denote
\bar{m}=m/M
.
Let
E\subset R^{n}\setminus H
be a bounded set with
|E|=m
and

(5.5)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)\leq\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}+\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{3}\left(\frac{m}{M^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

This is possible since the infimum is reached by a sequence of bounded sets.
Let
t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{M-1}<t_{M}
be real numbers such that

\left|E\cap\left((t_{i},t_{i+1})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\times(0,\infty)\right)\right|=\bar{m},

for every
0\leq i\leq M-1
and let
L(n,\lambda,\bar{m})
be given by
Lemma

5.5
.
For every
0\leq i\leq M-1
let us define the interval
I_{i}
in the following way.
If
t_{i+1}-t_{i}\leq 2L
we set
I_{i}=\emptyset
, otherwise we apply
Lemma

5.5
with
a=t_{i}
and
b=t_{i+1}
and we set
I_{i}=[a^{+},b^{-}]
.
If
m_{i}=|E\cap(I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\times(0,\infty))|
, then

(5.6)

m_{i}\leq\frac{m}{M^{2}}.

Indeed, if
I_{i}=\emptyset
, then (
5.6
) is clearly true.
If
I_{i}\neq\emptyset
, we set

E^{\prime}=\alpha\left(E\setminus\left(I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\times(0,\infty)\right)\right),

with
\alpha=\left(\frac{m}{m-m_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
.
Note that
\frac{m_{i}}{m}\leq\frac{1}{M}
by construction.
By
Lemma

5.4
and (
5.2
) we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{\prime})&\leq\left(\frac{m}{m-m_{i}}\right)^{2+\frac{q}{n}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}\left(E\setminus(I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\times(0,\infty))\right)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{m_{i}}{m}}\right)^{2+\frac{q}{n}}\left(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right).\end{split}

Moreover, if
M
is large enough,

(5.7)

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{\prime})&\leq\left(1+\left(3+\frac{q}{n}\right)\frac{m_{i}}{m}\right)\left(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{3}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

Estimates (
5.5
) and (
5.7
) imply that

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{\prime})\leq\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}+\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{3}\left(\frac{m}{M^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}-\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}{3}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

and, since
|E^{\prime}|=m
, (
5.6
) holds.

Let

\tilde{E}=E\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{M-1}I_{i}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}\times(0,\infty)\right)

and
\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}m_{i}
, so that
\left|\tilde{E}\right|=m-\mu
.
By
Lemma

5.5
and the subadditivity of power function with exponent less than
1
we get

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\tilde{E})&\leq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}m_{i}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

The set
F:=\left(\frac{m}{m-\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\tilde{E}
has volume
m
.
We can use
Lemma

5.4
to obtain

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(F)&\leq\left(\frac{m}{m-\mu}\right)^{2+\frac{q}{n}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{E})\leq\left(\frac{m}{m-\mu}\right)^{2+\frac{q}{n}}\left(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right).\end{split}

If
\mu
is small enough, which happens as soon as
M
is large enough thanks to (
5.6
), we deduce

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(F)&\leq\left(\frac{m}{m-\mu}\right)^{2+\frac{q}{n}}\left(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E)+C\mu\left(\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}+c\left(\frac{m}{M^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)+(1+C\mu)\left(-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\mu^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right)\\
&\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E).\end{split}

Note that
\tilde{E}
is the union of at most
M+1
sets, each contained in a slab having width at most equal to
2L
by
Lemma

5.5
.
In particular,
F
is the union of at most
M+1
parts and each of them has horizontal width at most equal
3L
.

If we repeat the arguments in the remaining directions, with care to apply also
Lemma

5.6
in the
n
-th direction, we get the boundedness of the pieces in all the
n
directions.
Then there exist
R\in(0,\infty)
,
\bar{h}\in\mathbb{N}
and
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|G|=m
,
G=\cup_{i=1}^{\bar{h}}G_{i}
,
G_{i}\cap G_{j}=\emptyset
, diam
G_{i}\leq R
and
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(G)\leq\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(E)
.
Finally

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(G_{i})\geq\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(G).

∎

Now we are ready to prove
Theorem

5.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

5.3
.

We begin by proving the existence of
h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and of a sequence
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that

(5.8)

\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon,h^{\prime}}(G_{i}).

Let
h^{\prime}(n,\lambda,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},g,G,q)
be an integer to be determined later and consider a sequence
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that

(5.9)

K:=\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}).

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
h(i)\in\mathbb{N}
such that there exists a subdivision
E_{i}=E_{i}^{1}\cup E_{i}^{2}\cup\dots\cup E_{i}^{h(i)}
with

(5.10)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i})>\left(1-\frac{1}{i+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{h(i)}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j}).

Without loss of generality, we can assume
h(i)\to\infty
, so that
h(i)>h^{\prime}
for
i
large enough.
Let us fix a generic
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
For simplicity of notation, let us denote
h=h(i)
and
m_{j}=|E_{i}^{j}|
for every
1\leq j\leq h
.
Let us also assume, without loss of generality, that
m_{j}
is decreasing with respect to
j
.
By (
5.10
) we get

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i})&\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{h}P_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{h}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{j}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt[n]{m_{1}}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}m_{j}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt[n]{m_{1}}}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}.\end{split}

If
i
is large enough, by (
5.9
) we deduce that

m_{1}\geq\left(\frac{n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}{4K}\right)^{n}.

For every such
i
, we define

G_{i}=\alpha\bigcup_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}E_{i}^{j},

with

\alpha=\left(\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{|B^{\lambda}|-\sum_{j>h^{\prime}}m_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq 1+c_{1}\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}m_{j},

where
c_{1}
is a constant depending on
n
,
\lambda
and
K
(that is on
n
,
\lambda
,
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{2}
,
g
and
G
).
Note that also
G_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
.
By
Lemma

5.4
we deduce

(5.11)

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,h^{\prime}}^{\lambda}(G_{i})&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\alpha E_{i}^{j})\leq\alpha^{2n+q}\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{i}^{j})\\
&\leq\left(1+c_{2}(n,\lambda,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},g,G,q)\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}m_{j}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j}).\end{split}

By (
5.10
) we get

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i})&>\left(1-\frac{1}{i+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})\\
&\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{i+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}P_{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})\\
&\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{i+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})+\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}m_{j}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.\end{split}

If
i
is large enough, by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
)

\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{i}^{j})\leq 2K.

By (
5.11
)

(5.12)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,h^{\prime}}^{\lambda}(G_{i})-\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,h}^{\lambda}(E_{i})\leq 2K\left(c_{2}(n,\lambda,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},g,G,q)\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}m_{j}+\frac{1}{i+1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}n|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\sum_{j=h^{\prime}+1}^{h}m_{j}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Now we can define
h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
so that

h^{\prime}\geq\left(\frac{4Kc_{2}(n,\lambda,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},g,G,q)}{n}\right)^{n}.

Since
m_{j}\leq\frac{|B^{\lambda}|}{h^{\prime}}
for
j>h^{\prime}
, if
i
is large enough we get from (
5.12
)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,h^{\prime}}^{\lambda}(G_{i})\leq\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,h}^{\lambda}(E_{i})+\frac{2K}{i+1}.

By (
5.9
) we finally deduce that
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
satisfies (
5.8
).

Let us now show that for every
m>0
there exist
\bar{h}\in\mathbb{N}
, a bounded set
E
with
|E|=m
and a subdivision
E=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}E^{k}
such that

(5.13)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{k})\leq\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,|\Omega|=m\right\}.

Let
R
and
\bar{h}
be as in
Lemma

5.7
. By
Lemma

5.7
there is a sequence of sets
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of volume
m
such that

(5.14)

\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,|\Omega|=m\right\}\geq\lim_{i\to+\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(\Omega_{i}),

where
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}
is defined in
Lemma

5.7
.
For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a partition
\Omega_{i}=\Omega_{i}^{1}\cup\Omega_{i}^{2}\cup\dots\cup\Omega_{i}^{\bar{h}}
with diam
(\Omega_{i}^{j})\leq R
and

(5.15)

\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega_{i}^{j})\leq\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(\Omega_{i})+\frac{1}{i}.

Up to a subsequence there exist
m_{j}\in(0,\infty)
, with
1\leq j\leq\bar{h}
, such that

m_{j}=\lim_{i\to\infty}|\Omega_{i}^{j}|\qquad\forall 1\leq j\leq\bar{h},\qquad\qquad m=\sum_{j=1}^{\bar{h}}m_{j}.

Let us fix
1\leq k\leq\bar{h}
and consider the sets
\{\Omega_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Since their diameters are uniformly bounded by
R
, up to translations we can assume that all the
\Omega_{i}^{k}
are pairwise disjoint and contained in a fixed ball with radius
R
.
Therefore the characteristic functions
f_{i}=\chi_{\Omega_{i}^{k}}
have uniformly bounded supports and are bounded in
BV
.
Up to a subsequence, we can assume that
f_{i}
is weakly
∗
convergent in
BV
, and in particular strongly convergent in
L^{1}
, to a certain function
f
.
Then
f
is the characteristic function of a bounded set
E^{k}
with volume
m_{k}
.
By the lower-semicontinuity of the perimeter under weak
∗

BV
-convergence and the continuity of
\mathscr{R}
and
\mathscr{G}
under strong
L^{1}
convergence, we obtain that

(5.16)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{k})\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega_{i}^{k}).

Up to a translation we can assume that the sets
E^{k}
are pairwise disjoint.
In particular the set
E=\cup_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}E^{k}
is bounded with
|E|=m
.
By (
5.14
), (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) we get

\begin{split}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{k})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega_{i}^{k})\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{h}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega_{i}^{k})\\
&\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,R}(\Omega_{i})\leq\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,\;|\Omega|=m\right\},\end{split}

so (
5.13
) is proved.

We can now conclude the proof of the theorem. Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
as in (
5.8
) and let us consider a subdivision
G_{i}=G_{i}^{1}\cup G_{i}^{2}\cup\dots\cup G_{i}^{h^{\prime}}
such that

(5.17)

\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(G_{i}^{j}).

Up to a subsequence there exist
\mu_{j}>0
,
1\leq j\leq h^{\prime}
, such that

\mu_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}|G_{i}^{j}|\qquad\forall 1\leq j\leq h^{\prime},\;|B^{\lambda}|=\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\mu_{j}.

Let

K_{j}:=\inf\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H,|\Omega|=\mu_{j}\right\}.

By (
5.17
)

(5.18)

\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\}=\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}K_{j}.

By (
5.13
) for every
1\leq j\leq h^{\prime}
there exist
\bar{h}(j)\in\mathbb{N}
, a bounded set
E_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
with
|E_{j}|=\mu_{j}
and a subdivision in pairwise disjoint sets

such that

(5.19)

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{j})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{h}(j)}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E_{j,k})\leq K_{j}.

Since the sets
E_{j}
are bounded, up to translations we can assume that the set

has volume
|B^{\lambda}|
.
Therefore
E
is the disjoint union of all the sets
E_{j,k}
with
1\leq j\leq h^{\prime}
and
1\leq k\leq\bar{h}(j)
.
Let us denote these sets as
E^{l}
with
1\leq l\leq h
and
h=\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\bar{h}(j)
.
By (
5.18
) and (
5.19
) we deduce that

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E)\leq\sum_{l=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(E^{l})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}K_{j}=\inf\left\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(\Omega)\ :\ \Omega\in\mathcal{A}\right\},

that is
E
is a minimizer of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}
and the subdivision
E=\bigcup_{l=1}^{h}E^{l}
is optimal.

The proof of (
5.1
) for a given
1\leq\bar{j}\leq h
easily follows as in the proof of
[
NP21
, Proposition 1.2]
. ∎

Now we are ready to prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

With the notation of
Theorem

1.3
, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H
is a measurable set with
|\Omega|=m
, let

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(\Omega):=\inf_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{h}(\Omega),

\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{h}(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{h}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\Omega^{i})\ :\ \Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}\Omega^{i},\Omega^{i}\cap\Omega^{j}=\emptyset\quad{\rm for}\;1\leq i\neq j\leq h\right\}.

If
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus H

and
\bar{\varepsilon}=\frac{m^{\frac{1}{n}}}{|B^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{n}}}
, the set
\tilde{F}:=\bar{\varepsilon}F
has volume

and by

there exist
\tilde{g}

\mathscr{R}
-admissible and
\tilde{G}

\mathscr{G}
-admissible such that

\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})=\bar{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\left(P_{\lambda}(F)+\bar{\varepsilon}^{n+1}\int_{F}\int_{F}\tilde{g}(y-x)\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x+\bar{\varepsilon}\int_{F}\tilde{G}(x_{n})\,{\rm d}x\right)=:\bar{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\mathscr{F}^{\lambda}_{\bar{\varepsilon}}(F).

Note that
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}(\tilde{F})=\bar{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{\bar{\varepsilon}}^{\lambda}(F)
and that, if

is

-growing, also
\tilde{g}
is

-growing (see

).
By

5.3
there exists

with

which minimizes
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}_{\bar{\varepsilon}}
.
Then the set
\tilde{E}:=\bar{\varepsilon}E
minimizes
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}^{\lambda}
among sets with volume

and
Theorem

1.3
easily follows. ∎

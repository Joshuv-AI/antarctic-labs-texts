The H H and J J functional in the theory of complete exponential sums
James Wright

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce two nonlinear functionals, the H H and J J functional, in the theory of complete exponential sums.

Polynomials in many variables

An advantage of our methods is that it can treat local objects such as
I_{P}(H)
and this allows us (almost for free)
to treat exponential sums for polynomials of many variables.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]

d
in
n

with coefficients in the
p
-adic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

P({\underline{z}})\ =\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq d}c_{\alpha}\,{\underline{z}}^{\alpha}\ \ \ {\rm where}\ \ \ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}.

For
{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}

We consider the oscillatory integral

Theorem 1.5
.

We have

|I_{P}(H)|\ \leq\ C_{d,n}\ p\,H^{-1}.

(8)

Remark

.

\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p^{-n\alpha}N_{a,\alpha}\ =\ \sup_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\,\bigl|\{{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}:\,|Q({\underline{z}})-a|\leq p^{-\alpha}\}\bigr|.

Recall that
P_{\alpha}({\underline{z}})=p^{-\alpha}Q({\underline{z}})
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}=H_{P_{\alpha}}
. Consider any

{\underline{z}}_{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
where
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}=H_{P_{\alpha}}({\underline{z}}_{*})

and let
a_{*}=Q({\underline{z}}_{*})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. We have

S\ :=\ \sup_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}}p^{-n\alpha}N_{a,\alpha}\ \geq\ \bigl|\{{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}:\,|P_{\alpha}({\underline{z}})-P_{\alpha}({\underline{z}}_{*})|\leq 1\}\bigr|

and so

S\ \geq\ \bigl|\{{\underline{z}}\in B_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1}}({\underline{z}}_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}:|\sum_{|\beta|\geq 1}[\partial^{\beta}P({\underline{z}}_{*})/\beta!]({\underline{z}}-{\underline{z}}_{*})^{\beta}|\leq 1\}\bigr|\ =\ |B_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1}}({\underline{z}}_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}|

since
|\partial^{\beta}P({\underline{z}}_{*})/\beta!|\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{|\beta|}

for each
|\beta|\geq 1
.

When
n=1
, we have

|B_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1}}({\underline{z}}_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}|\ >\ p^{-1}\min(1,{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1})

with an improvement to

|B_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1}}({\underline{z}}_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}|=\min(1,{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}^{-1})

when the
p
-adic valuation of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
is an integer. This establishes the lower bound
(
11
) in
Proposition
1.6
.

5.
Comments on the sharpness of the bound in Theorem
1.2

First we observe by example that
\epsilon=1
can occur in (
3
).

Consider the example
P(t)=at^{3}+bt\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
where
|a|=p^{3r-2}
and
|b|=p^{r}
.
We write
b=b_{0}p^{-r}
and
a=a_{0}p^{-3r+2}
where
|b_{0}|=|a_{0}|=1
. We choose any pair
(a_{0},b_{0})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}]^{2}

such that the quadratic congruence
3a_{0}s^{2}+b_{0}\equiv 0
mod
p
is not solvable.

Claim
: Under the above condition, we have
H=p^{r}
where

H\ =\ \inf_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\max\bigl(|b+3at^{2}|,|3at|^{1/2},p^{r-2/3}\bigr).

The size conditions on
a
and
b
imply
H\leq p^{r}
.
To see the reverse inequality, we may assume there is some cancellation in the sum
b+3at^{2}
; that is,

|3at^{2}|=p^{r}
. Otherwise
|b+3at^{2}|\geq p^{r}
and we would be done. Hence we may assume that
|t|=p^{-r+1}
.
If we write
t=p^{r-1}s
where
|s|=1
, we have

|b+3at^{2}|\ =\ p^{r}|b_{0}+3a_{0}s^{2}|\ =\ p^{r}

since
|b_{0}+3a_{0}s^{2}|\leq p^{-1}
means the congruence
3a_{0}s^{2}+b_{0}\equiv 0
mod
p
is solvable.
Hence
H=p^{r}
and
H=H(p^{r-1}s)
for any
|s|=1
. Furthermore,

J(p^{r-1}s)\ =\ \max(|3ap^{r-1}s|^{1/2},|a|^{1/3})\ =\ p^{r-1/2}\ {\rm and}\ |P^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)/3!|\equiv|a|=p^{3r-2}.

Hence (
4
) holds.

Next we have

I_{P}\ =\ \int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(P(z))\,dz\ =\ p^{-M}\,\sum_{x=0}^{p^{M}-1}e^{2\pi i[a_{0}x^{3}+p^{2r-2}b_{0}x]/p^{M}}

where
M=3r-2
. A calculation shows that

I_{P}\ =\ p^{-r}\,\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}e^{2\pi i[a_{0}x^{3}+b_{0}x]/p}\ =\ H^{-1}\,S(a_{0},b_{0})

where
S(a_{0},b_{0})
is an exponential sum over the finite field
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Hence in this case, we have
\epsilon=1
.

Comparing to the euclidean case

The corresponding estimate for the euclidean oscillatory integral

I\ =\ \int_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi iP(x)}\,dx

where
P\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
is

|I|\ \leq\ C_{d}\,H^{-1}\ \ {\rm where}\ \ H=\inf_{x\in[0,1]}H(x)\ =\ \inf_{x\in[0,1]}\max_{k\geq 1}\bigl(|P^{(k)}(x)/k!|^{1/k}\bigr).

A proof
can be found in
[
1
]
, including the corresponding higher dimensional analogue.
In
[
1
]
, sharpness of the above bound was observed in the following sense: for any polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
, there
is a
0<c\leq 1
such that

\min(1,H^{-1})\ =\ \Bigr|\int_{0}^{c}e^{2\pi iP(x)}\,dx\Bigr|.

(13)

This is not too hard to prove but it relies heavily on the ordered structure of the real field
{\mathbb{R}}
.
One can use
(
13
) to compare the bound
|I|\leq C_{d}H^{-1}
to other bounds which are robust
under truncations of the oscillatory integral.

For instance, a very sharp bound for
I
mentioned in the Introduction is due to Phong and Stein
[
4
]
:

\Bigl|\int_{a}^{b}e^{2\pi iP(x)}\,dx\Bigr|\ \leq\ C_{d}\,\max_{\xi}\min_{\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{|a\prod_{\eta\notin{\mathcal{C}}}(\xi-\eta)^{e_{\eta}}|}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{S({\mathcal{C}})+1}}

so that (
6
) is the exact discrete analogue but here
|\cdot|
denotes the usual
archimedean absolute value measuring the distance between the complex roots of
P^{\prime}
instead of the
p
-adic absolute value used in Corollary
1.3
.
The estimate above holds for any
a<b
and hence (
13
) implies

\min_{\xi}\max_{\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}}|a\prod_{\eta\notin{\mathcal{C}}}(\xi-\eta)^{e_{\eta}}|^{1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\ \leq\ C_{d}\,H.

(14)

In the next section, we propose an alternative version of (
13
) which is valid in both archimedean and
nonarchimedean settings.

6.
Proof of the lower bound (
2
) in Theorem
1.1

Here we establish a general lower bound for

I_{P}\ =\ \int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(P(z))\,dz

where
P\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
. The bound (
5
) from
Theorem
1.2
implies

|I_{P}|\ \leq\ C_{d}\,\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})\ \ {\rm where}\ \ J_{P}=\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\max_{2\leq k\leq d}\ (|P^{(k)}(z)/k!|^{1/k}).

To analyse sharpness of this bound, set

\alpha_{P}\ :=\ \sup_{w,b}\ \bigl[\max\bigl(1,J_{wP}\bigr)|I_{wP_{b}}|\bigr]\ \ {\rm where}\ \ P_{b}(z)=P(z)-bz.

Here the supremum is taken over
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
b\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Since the
J
functional does not see linear terms, we see
J_{f_{b}}=J_{f}
. Hence the
above bound implies
\alpha_{P}\leq C_{d}
.
We seek a lower bound for
\alpha_{P}
.

Consider

S_{P}\ :=\ \sup_{a,b\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\bigl|\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}:|P(z)-a-bz|\leq 1\}\bigr|.

Let
z_{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
be such that

J_{P}=J_{P}(z_{*})
. Then for
a=P(z_{*})+bP^{\prime}(z_{*})

and
b=P^{\prime}(z_{*})
,
we have

S_{P}\ \geq\ \bigl|\{z\in B_{J_{P}^{-1}}(z_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}:|\sum_{k\geq 2}[P^{(k)}(z_{*})/k!](z-z_{*})^{k}|\leq 1\}\bigr|\ =\ |B_{J_{P}^{-1}}(z_{*})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}|

since
|P^{(k)}(z_{*})/k!|\leq J_{P}^{k}

for each
k\geq 2
. Hence we see that
S_{P}>p^{-1}\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})
with an improvement to

S_{P}\geq\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})
if
J_{P}=p^{m}
for some integer
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

On the other hand, if
{\mathcal{D}}_{P}^{a,b}=\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}:|P(z)-a-bz|\leq 1\}
, then

{\mathbbm{1}}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{P}^{a,b}}(z)\ =\ {\mathbbm{1}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}(P(z)-a-bz)\ =\ \int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(w(P(z)-a-bz))\,dw

and hence

|{\mathcal{D}}_{P}^{a,b}|\ =\ \int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Bigl[\int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(w(P(z)-a-bz))\,dw\Bigr]\,dz\ =\ \int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(-aw)I_{wP_{b}}\,dw.

Since
J_{wP_{b}}=J_{wP}
, we have

p^{-1}\,\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})\ <\ S_{P}\ \leq\ \alpha_{P}\,\int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\min(1,J_{wP}^{-1})\,dw.

But since
|w|^{1/2}J_{P}\leq J_{wP}
for
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, we have

p^{-1}\,\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})\ <\ \alpha_{P}\,\min(1,J_{P}^{-1})\int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}|w|^{-1/2}\,dw\ \leq\ 4\,\alpha_{P}\,\min(1,J_{P}^{-1}),

implying
(0.25)\,p^{-1}<\alpha_{P}
with an improvement
1/4\leq\alpha_{P}
if
J_{p}=p^{m}
for some

m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We conclude that for every
P\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
, there is a
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and a
b\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
such that

(1/4)\,p^{-1}\,\min(1,J_{wP}^{-1})\ <\ \Bigl|\int_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}{\rm e}(wP_{b}(z))\,dz\Bigr|.

This is our version of (
13
).

For
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
, we apply the above to
P(z)=p^{-m}f(z)
, tracking the value of
b
, to conclude
that there are integers
a,c
such that

(1/4)\,p^{-1}\,\min(1,p^{-m}J_{af}^{-1})\ <\ \bigl|p^{-m}\sum_{x=0}^{p^{m}-1}e^{2\pi i[af_{c}(x)]/p^{m}}\bigr|,

estabishing the lower bound (
2
) in Theorem
1.1
.

7.
Proof of Proposition
1.3

Here we show how Theorem
1.2
implies Proposition
1.3
.

Recall that
P^{\prime}(z)=a\prod_{\xi}(z-\xi)^{e_{\xi}}
. The proof of Corollary
1.3

comes in two steps. First we suppose
\epsilon=0
in Theorem
1.2
. Then

|I_{P}|\leq C_{d}H_{P}^{-1}
and so (
6
) follows from the following bound: for any

z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, there is a root
\xi
of
P^{\prime}
such that

\max_{{\mathcal{C}}\ni\xi}|a\prod_{\eta\notin{\mathcal{C}}}(\xi-\eta)^{e_{\eta}}|^{1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\ \leq\ H_{P}(z)

(15)

holds. Here we do not need any special properties for
P
. The inequality (
15
)
is a general inequality which holds for any polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
.

For the second step, we suppose
\epsilon=1
in Theorem
1.2
. In this case
we have
|I_{P}|\leq C_{d}\sqrt{p}\,p^{-s}
when
p>d
where
p^{s}=H=H(z_{*})=|P^{\prime}(z_{*})|
for some

z_{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Furthermore
p^{s-1/2}=J(z_{*})=|P^{\prime\prime}(z_{*})/2|^{1/2}

and (
4
) holds.

The first step

But first we prove (
15
).

We fix
z

and prove (
15
) for a root

\xi
of
P^{\prime}
with the property

|z-\xi|\ =\ \min_{\eta}|z-\eta|
.
We divvy up the roots
\{\xi_{j}^{k}\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{k},1\leq k\leq t}
of
P^{\prime}
so that

|z-\xi_{1}^{k}|\ =\ |z-\xi_{2}^{k}|\ =\ \cdots\ =|z-\xi_{r_{k}}^{k}|\ =:\ A_{k}

with
0\leq A_{1}<A_{2}<\ldots<A_{t}
. Here we have set
\xi_{1}^{1}=\xi
. We will take special
care with the case
A_{1}=0
where
z=\xi
and
P^{\prime}(z)=0
.

We fix a general subset
{\mathcal{C}}
of the roots of
P^{\prime}
and split it as

{\mathcal{C}}\ =\ C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup\cdots\cup C_{t}\ \ {\rm where}\ \ C_{k}\ =\ \{\xi_{1}^{k},\ldots,\xi_{r_{k}}^{k}\}\cap{\mathcal{C}}.

We set
f_{k}=\sum_{\eta\in C_{k}}e_{\eta}
so that
S({\mathcal{C}})=f_{1}+\cdots+f_{t}
.
When
A_{1}=0
, we have
C_{1}=\{\xi\}
and so
f_{1}=e_{\xi}
.

For each
1\leq k\leq t
, set

F_{k}(z)\ =\ \prod_{j=1}^{r_{k}}(z-\xi_{j}^{k})^{e_{\xi_{j}^{k}}}\ =:\ F_{k}^{1}(z)\,F_{k}^{2}(z)

where

F_{k}^{1}(z)\ =\ \prod_{\xi_{j}^{k}\in C_{k}}(z-\xi_{j}^{k})^{e_{\xi_{j}^{k}}}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ F_{k}^{2}(z)\ =\ \prod_{\xi_{j}^{k}\notin C_{k}}(z-\xi_{j}^{k})^{e_{\xi_{j}^{k}}}.

Therefore
P^{\prime}(z)=a\prod_{k=1}^{t}F_{k}(z)
and if

Q_{{\mathcal{C}}}:=|a\prod_{k=1}^{t}F_{k}^{2}(\xi)|
, then our goal is to prove

Q_{{\mathcal{C}}}^{1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\ \leq\ H_{P}(z)

(16)

which will establish (
15
) since
{\mathcal{C}}
is a general subcollection
of the roots of
P^{\prime}
.

By the formula above for
P^{\prime}
, we have

H_{P}(z)\ \geq\ |P^{\prime}(z)|\ =\ |a\prod_{k=1}^{t}F_{k}^{1}(z)F_{k}^{2}(z)|\ \geq\ A_{1}^{S({\mathcal{C}})}Q_{\mathcal{C}}

(17)

since for any root
\eta
of
P^{\prime}
,
|\xi-\eta|\leq\max(|z-\xi|,|z-\eta|)=|z-\eta|
.

To derive other lower bounds for
H(z)
in terms of
Q_{\mathcal{C}}
,
we consider the derivatives
P^{(1+\rho_{k})}
of
P^{\prime}
where
\rho_{k}:=\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{k}

where
\sigma_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{k}}e_{\xi_{j}^{k}}
.
To do this, set

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(z)\ =\ \prod_{\ell=k+1}^{t}F_{\ell}(z)

for each
1\leq k\leq t-1
and note that

P^{(1+\rho_{k})}(z)/\rho_{k}!\ =\ a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(z)\ +\ a{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(z)

where both
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(z)
are homogeneous functions of degree
d-1-\rho_{k}

(
d={\rm deg}(P)
) in the variables
z-\eta
as
\eta
runs over the distinct roots of
P^{\prime}
.
When
A_{1}=0
, we have
\rho_{1}=\sigma_{1}=e_{\xi}
and so

P^{(1+e_{\xi})}(z)/e_{\xi}!=a{\mathcal{F}}_{1}(z)
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(z)=0
.

When
A_{1}>0
,
each term in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
has a factor
z-\xi_{j}^{\ell}
for some
1\leq\ell\leq k
and so

|a{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(z)|<|a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(z)|
since
|z-\xi_{j}^{\ell}|<|z-\eta_{j^{\prime}}^{\ell^{\prime}}|
for any
k+1\leq\ell^{\prime}
.
Therefore
|P^{(1+\rho_{k})}(z)/\rho_{k}!|=|a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(z)|=|a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\xi)|
since

|\xi-\xi_{j}^{k}|=|\xi-z+z-\xi_{j}^{k}|=|z-\xi_{j}^{k}|
for any
k\geq 2
.

Hence

H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k}}\ \geq\ |P^{(1+\rho_{k})}(z)/\rho_{k}!|\ =\ |a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\xi)|

and if
f^{k}:=\sigma_{k}-f_{k}
, then for all
1\leq k\leq t-1
,

Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ A_{k}^{f^{1}+\cdots f^{k}}\frac{1}{|\prod_{\ell=k+1}^{t}F_{\ell}^{1}(\xi)|}|a{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\xi)|\ \leq\ A_{k}^{f^{1}+\cdots+f^{k}}\frac{1}{A_{k+1}^{f_{k+1}+\cdots+f_{t}}}H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k}}.

The last inequality follows from the previous displayed equation and the fact that
|z-\xi_{j}^{k}|=|\xi-\xi_{j}^{k}|

for every
k\geq 2
as observed before. If
A_{1}=0
, then
f^{1}=0
and we interpret
A_{1}^{f^{1}}=1
.

Therefore since
A_{k}<A_{k+1}<A_{k+2}
, we apply the above inequality for
Q_{\mathcal{C}}

for
k
and
k+1
to conclude that

Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ A_{k+1}^{\rho_{k}-S({\mathcal{C}})}H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k}}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ A_{k+1}^{\rho_{k+1}-S({\mathcal{C}})}H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k+1}}.

(18)

The first inequality with
k=0
incorporates (
17
) if we interpret
\rho_{0}=0
.

We now divide the analysis into cases depending on the size
S({\mathcal{C}})
of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Suppose
\rho_{k}<S({\mathcal{C}})\leq\rho_{k+1}
for some

0\leq k\leq t-1
. Again with the interpretation that
\rho_{0}=0
, we see that any cluster
of roots
{\mathcal{C}}
must have a size lying in one of these intervals. With
\rho_{k}<S({\mathcal{C}})\leq\rho_{k+1}
, we see that the first inequality in (
18
) implies

A_{k+1}^{S({\mathcal{C}})-\rho_{k}}\,Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k}}

and this implies (
16
) when
Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\leq A_{k+1}

and therefore we may assume

A_{k+1}\ <\ Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}.

(19)

When
A_{1}=0
, the reduction to (
19
) when
k=0
is automatic.

But the second inequality in (
18
), together with (
19
), implies

Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ A_{k+1}^{\rho_{k+1}-S({\mathcal{C}})}H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k+1}}\ \leq\ Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{-(\rho_{k+1}-S({\mathcal{C}}))/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\,H_{P}(z)^{1+\rho_{k+1}}

and this unravels to (
16
), completing the proof of (
15
).

The second step

Here we examine the case when
\epsilon=1
. In this case (
3
) implies

|I_{P}|\leq C_{d}\,p^{-s+1/2}
when
p>d\geq 2
where
p^{s}=H=H_{P}(z_{*})=|P^{\prime}(z_{*})|
and

p^{s-1/2}=J_{P}(z_{*})=|P^{\prime\prime}(z_{*})/2|^{1/2}

for some
z_{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
Furthermore (
4
) states

|P^{(k)}(z_{*})/k!|\ \leq\ p^{ks-(k-1)}\ \ {\rm for\ every}\ \ 1\leq k\leq d.

Let
\xi
be a root of
P^{\prime}
with the property

|z_{*}-\xi|\ =\ \min_{\eta}|z_{*}-\eta|
. Our aim is to show

\max_{\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}}|a\prod_{\eta\notin{\mathcal{C}}}(\xi-\eta)^{e_{\eta}}|^{1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\ \leq\ p^{s-1/2}

(20)

when
p\geq 3
.
This
will show (
6
) in the case
\epsilon=1
since
p>d\geq 2
. We follow the argument (and notation)
in the first step but there is one important, additional ingredient to the argument.

We observe that
A_{1}=|z_{*}-\xi|=p^{-s+1}
when
p\geq 3
. To see this, consider the polynomial

g(z)\ :=\ P^{\prime}(z+z_{*})\ =\ P^{\prime}(z_{*})+P^{\prime\prime}(z_{*})z+[P^{\prime\prime\prime}(z_{*})/2]\,z^{2}+\cdots

whose roots are precisely
\{\eta-z_{*}\}
where
\{\eta\}
are the roots of
P^{\prime}
.
The size
|z_{*}-\eta|
of the roots of
g
are ordered by
A_{1}<A_{2}<\cdots<A_{t}

where the
A_{k}
are defined in first step above with
z
replaced by
z_{*}
. The
constant term of
g
has valuation
{\rm ord}_{p}(P^{\prime}(z_{*}))=-s
and
the linear term has valuation
{\rm ord}_{p}(P^{\prime\prime}(z_{*}))=-2s+1
since
p\not=2
.
In general,

{\rm ord}_{p}(P^{(k)}(z_{*})/k!)\geq-ks+(k-1)
by (
4
) and this implies

{\rm ord}_{p}(P^{(k)}(z_{*}))\ \geq\ {\rm ord}_{p}(P^{(k)}(z_{*})/k!)\ \geq\ -ks+(k-1).

We consider the Newton polygon (see
[
2
]
) of
g

in the plane generated by the points

(i,{\rm ord}_{p}(b_{i+1}),\ \ i=0,\ldots,d-1\ \ {\rm where}\ \ g(z)=b_{1}+b_{2}z+\cdots+b_{d}z^{d-1}.

From the conditions detailed above,
{\rm ord}_{p}(b_{1})=-s,\,{\rm ord}_{p}(b_{2})=-2s+1
(since
p\not=2
)
and in general
{\rm ord}_{p}(b_{k})\geq-ks+(k-1)
. Hence we see that the Newton polygon lies above the
line
y=(-s+1)x-s

and
the first part of the polygon lies along this line.

A basic result from algebraic number theory (see for example,
[
2
]
) relates the slopes
of the polygon for
g
with the valuations of the roots of
g
. In particular the largest
(in absolute value) negative slope
-s+1
gives the valuation

{\rm ord}_{p}(z_{*}-\xi)=s-1
of
z_{*}-\xi
;
that is,
A_{1}=|z_{*}-\xi|=p^{-s+1}
as claimed.

We fix a subcollection
{\mathcal{C}}
of the roots of
P^{\prime}
and
follow the argument from the first step with
z
replaced by
z_{*}
.
In particular (
20
) can be expressed as

Q_{\mathcal{C}}^{1/(S({\mathcal{C}})+1)}\ \leq\ p^{s-1/2}.

(21)

The bound (
17
) becomes

p^{s}\ =\ |P^{\prime}(z_{*})|\ \geq\ |a\prod_{k=1}^{t}F_{k}^{1}(z)F_{k}^{2}(z)|\ \geq\ A_{1}^{S({\mathcal{C}})}Q_{\mathcal{C}}

and since
p^{-s+1}=A_{1}
when
p\geq 3
,

(p^{-s+1})^{S({\mathcal{C}})}Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ =\ A_{1}^{S({\mathcal{C}})}Q_{\mathcal{C}}\ \leq\ p^{s}

and this implies (
21
) since
S({\mathcal{C}})\geq 1
.
This completes the second step and the proof of Corollary
1.3
.

A structural sublevel set statement

Here we detail a key sublevel set bound central to the proofs of Theorem

,

Proposition

]
.

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
Q\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
be a polynomials of degree

\phi(t)=c_{0}+c_{1}t+\cdots+c_{d}t^{d}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}[X]
and

\lambda_{+}:=\max(1,\lambda)
where
\lambda=\max_{j}|c_{j}|
.

We define a sequence
\{t_{n}\}
recursively by

t_{n}\ =\ t_{n-1}-\phi(t_{n-1})\phi^{\prime}(t_{n-1})^{-1}

(68)

and recall
\delta_{1}=|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})/2|
and our hypothesis

\delta_{1}<1
. We make the
following claim.

Claim:
For every
n\geq 1
,

(1)_{n}\ \ |t_{n}-t_{n-1}|\ \leq\ |\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|\,\delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1},

(2)_{n}\ \ |\phi(t_{n-1})|\ \leq\ |\phi(t_{0})|\delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1},

(3)_{n}
for
1\leq k\leq L
,
|\phi^{(k)}(t_{n-1})/k!|=|\phi^{(k)}(t_{0})/k!|
.

Note the claim implies that for every
n\geq m
,

|t_{n}-t_{m}|\leq\max_{m<j\leq n}|t_{j}-t_{j-1}|\leq|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|\max_{m<j\leq n}\delta_{1}^{2^{j-1}-1}\to 0\ \ {\rm as}\ m,n\to\infty.

Hence
\{t_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is Cauchy and so
t_{n}\to t
for some
t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Furthermore
|t-t_{0}|\leq|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|
and
(2)_{n}
implies
\phi(t)=0
. Therefore
it remains to prove the claim.

We now proceed with the proof of the claim.
The claim is true for
n=1
and so suppose
(1)_{j},(2)_{j}
and
(3)_{j}
holds for all
1\leq j\leq n
.
Note that
(3)_{n}
implies
\phi^{\prime}(t_{n-1})\not=0
and so
z_{n}
is well-defined.
We begin with proving
(3)_{n+1}
. For
1\leq k\leq L
, we see

\phi^{(k)}(t_{n})\ =\ \phi^{(k)}(t_{n-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{L-k}\frac{\phi^{(k+j)}(t_{n-1})}{j!}(t_{n}-t_{n-1})^{j}\ +\ R_{L-k,k}

(69)

where by
(1)_{n}
,

|R_{L-k,k}|\ \leq\ \lambda_{+}|t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{L-k+1}\ \leq\ \lambda_{+}|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|\delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1}|t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{L-k}

\ =\ \lambda_{+}\delta(\delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1})|\phi^{(L)}(t_{0})/L!||t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{L-k}.

(70)

Furthermore by
(1)_{n}
and
(3)_{n}
, for each
1\leq j\leq L-k
,

|\phi^{(k+j)}(t_{n-1})/j!\,(t_{n}-t_{n-1})^{j}|\ \leq\ \delta_{1}^{j(2^{n-1}-1)}|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|^{j}|\phi^{(k+j)}(t_{0})/(k+j)!|.

By the definition of the
\delta_{\ell},\,1\leq\ell\leq L-1
in the statement of Lemma
8.3
, we have

|(\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1})^{j}\phi^{(k+j)}(t_{0})/(k+j)!|\ =\ |(\delta_{k}\cdots\delta_{k+j-1})\phi^{(k)}(t_{0})/k!|\ \leq\ |\phi^{(k)}(t_{0})/k!|

for
1\leq j\leq L-k
and hence

|\phi^{(k+j)}(t_{n-1})/(k+j)!\,(t_{n}-t_{n-1})^{j}|\ \leq\ \delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1}|\phi^{(k)}(t_{0})/k!|.

(71)

Plugging (
70
) and (
71
) into (
69
), using the hypotheses

\lambda_{+}\delta\leq 1
and
\delta_{1}<1
and the nonarchimedean nature of the
p
-adic absolute value, we see that

|\phi^{(k)}(t_{n})/k!|\ =\ |\phi^{(k)}(t_{0})/k!|

for
1\leq k\leq L
, establishing
(3)_{n+1}
.

For
(2)_{n+1}
, we expand

\phi(t_{n})\ =\ \phi(t_{n-1})+\phi^{\prime}(t_{n-1})(t_{n}-t_{n-1})+\sum_{j=2}^{L}\frac{\phi^{(j)}(t_{n-1})}{j!}\ (t_{n}-t_{n-1})^{j}\ +\ R_{L}

and since

\phi(t_{n-1})+\phi^{\prime}(t_{n-1})(t_{n}-t_{n-1})=0
by definition of
t_{n}
, we have

\phi(t_{n})\ =\ \sum_{j=2}^{L}\frac{\phi^{(j)}(t_{n-1})}{j!}\ (t_{n}-t_{n-1})^{j}\ +\ R_{L}

where

|R_{L}|\ \leq\ \lambda_{+}|t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{L+1}\ \leq\ \lambda_{+}\delta(\delta_{1}^{2^{n-1}-1})|\phi^{(L)}(t_{0})/L!||t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{L}

(72)

as before. For
2\leq j\leq L
, we have by
(1)_{n}
and
(3)_{n}
,

|\phi^{(j)}(t_{n-1})/j!||t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{j}\leq|\phi^{(j)}(t_{0})/j!||\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|^{j-1}\delta_{1}^{2^{n}-2}|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|.

Proceeding as above, using the definition of the
\delta_{\ell}
’s, we have

|\phi^{(j)}(t_{0})/j!||\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|^{j-1}\ =\ (\delta_{2}\cdots\delta_{j-1})|\phi(t_{0})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})/2|,

implying

|\phi^{(j)}(t_{n-1})/j!||t_{n}-t_{n-1}|^{j}\ \leq\delta_{1}\delta_{1}^{2^{n}-2}|\phi(t_{0})|.

We have a similar estimate for the right hand side of (
72
) and so, altogether, we have

|\phi(t_{n})|\ \leq\ |\phi(t_{0})|\delta_{1}^{2^{n}-1},

completing the proof of
(2)_{n+1}
.

Finally for
(1)_{n+1}
, we use
(2)_{n+1}
,
(3)_{n+1}
to see

|t_{n+1}-t_{n}|\ =\ |\phi(t_{n})\phi^{\prime}(t_{n})^{-1}|\ =|\phi(t_{n})\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})^{-1}|\ \leq\ \delta_{1}^{2^{n}-1}|\phi(t_{0})|

which establishes
(1)_{n+1}
,
completing the proof of the claim and hence the proof of Lemma
8.3
.

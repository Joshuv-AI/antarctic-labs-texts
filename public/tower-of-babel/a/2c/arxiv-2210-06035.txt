Volume preserving Gauss curvature flow of convex hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
Yong Wei, Bo Yang, Tailong Zhou

Abstract
. We consider the volume preserving flow of smooth, closed and convex hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space ℍ n + 1 ​ ( n ≥ 2 ) \mathbb{H}^{n+1}(n\geq 2) with the speed given by arbitrary positive power α \alpha of the Gauss curvature. We prove that if the initial hypersurface is convex, then the smooth solution of the flow remains convex and exists for all positive time t ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) t\in[0,\infty) . Moreover, we apply a result of Kohlmann which characterises the geodesic ball using the hyperbolic curvature measures and an argument of Alexandrov reflection to prove that the flow converges to a geodesic sphere exponentially in the smooth topology. This can be viewed as the first result for non-local type volume preserving curvature flows for hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space with only convexity required on the initial data.

1.
Introduction

Let
X_{0}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a smooth embedding such that
M_{0}=X_{0}(M)
is a closed and convex hypersurface in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We consider the smooth family of embeddings
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=&~(\phi(t)-K^{\alpha})\nu(x,t),\\
X(\cdot,0)=&~X_{0}(\cdot),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha>0
,
\nu
is the unit outer normal of
M_{t}=X(M,t)
,
K
is the Gauss curvature of
M_{t}
and

(1.2)

\phi(t)=\frac{1}{|M_{t}|}\int_{M_{t}}K^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

such that the domain
\Omega_{t}
enclosed by
M_{t}
has a fixed volume
|\Omega_{t}|=|\Omega_{0}|
along the flow (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth closed hypersurface in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Denote the principal curvatures of
M
by
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
.

(i).
M
is said to be horospherically convex (or simply called
h
-convex), if its principal curvatures satisfy
\kappa_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
everywhere on
M
. Equivalently,
M
is h-convex if for any point
p\in M
there exists a horosphere enclosing
M
and touching
M
at
p
. We also say that a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is
h
-convex if its boundary
\partial\Omega
is
h
-convex.

(ii).
M
is said to be positively curved (or called having positive sectional curvatures), if its principal curvatures satisfy

\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}>1,\qquad\forall~i\neq j

everywhere on
M
.

(iii).
M
is said to be convex if its principal curvatures satisfy
\kappa_{i}>0
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
everywhere on
M
. We also say that a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is convex if its boundary
\partial\Omega
is convex.

As the main result of this paper, we prove the following convergence result for the flow (
1.1
) with convex initial hypersurfaces:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X_{0}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a smooth embedding such that
M_{0}=X_{0}(M)
is a closed and convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then for any
\alpha>0
, the volume preserving flow (
1.1
) has a unique smooth convex solution
M_{t}
for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
, and the solution
M_{t}
converges smoothly and exponentially as
t\to\infty
to a geodesic sphere of radius
\rho_{\infty}
which encloses the same volume as
M_{0}
.

The volume preserving mean curvature flow

(1.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=(\phi(t)-H)\nu(x,t)

was introduced by Huisken
[
18
]
in 1987 for convex hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and it has been proved that for any smooth convex initial hypersurface, the solution of the flow (
1.3
) converges smoothly to a round sphere. The analogue of the flow (
1.3
) in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
was studied by Cabezas-Rivas and Miquel
[
8
]
in 2007 assuming a stronger condition that the initial hypersurface is
h
-convex. There are further generalizations of the flow (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with the mean curvature
H
replaced by more general curvature functions including powers of the
k
th mean curvature
\sigma_{k}(\kappa),k=1,\cdots,n
(see
[
2
,
5
,
17
,
22
,
30
]
for instance). In all cases, the
h
-convexity is assumed on the initial hypersurfaces. This is mainly because that the
h
-convexity is convenient for the analysis of the curvature evolution equations and so that the tensor maximum principle or the constant rank theorem can be applied to derive that the
h
-convexity is preserved along the flow. Moreover, the
h
-convexity is strong enough geometrically such that the outer radius of the enclosed domain is uniformly controlled by its inner radius (see
[
6
]
), and then the a prior
C^{0}
estimate of the flow can be proved easily. In a recent work
[
1
]
, the first author with Andrews and Chen proved the smooth convergence of volume preserving
k
th mean curvature flows in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
for initial hypersurfaces with positive sectional curvatures. This condition is weaker than
h
-convexity but still stronger than the convexity
\kappa_{i}>0.

An open question in the field is whether the convexity (
\kappa_{i}>0
,
i=1,\cdots,n
) is sufficient to guarantee the smooth convergence of the volume preserving curvature flow in the hyperbolic space. Our result in Theorem
1.2
provides the first affirmative answer to this question. The curve case (
n=1
) of Theorem
1.2
was also treated recently by the first and second authors in
[
31
]
, where the idea is that in this case there is only one curvature and we can calculate the curvature evolution explicitly to derive the convexity preserving immediately. However, the higher dimensional case (
n\geq 2
) as stated in Theorem
1.2
requires on some new ideas. The key step is again to show the convexity preserving, but the tensor maximum principle no longer works when applying to the evolution of the second fundamental form directly. Instead, we shall use the projection method via the Klein model of the hyperbolic space that was described earlier by the first author and Andrews in
[
2
]
. Based on this, we can treat the flow (
1.1
) as an equivalent flow in the Euclidean space and this allows us to derive a time-dependent positive lower bound on the principal curvatures, and a time-dependent upper bound on the Gauss curvature
K
. A continuation argument then implies that the flow exists for all positive time
t>0
.

To study the asymptotical behavior of the flow as time
t\to\infty
, we shall use some machinery from the theory of convex bodies in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. In particular, we use the Blaschke selection theorem to show that for a subsequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
, the enclosed domain
\Omega_{t_{i}}
of
M_{t_{i}}
converges in Hausdorff sense to a limit convex domain
\hat{\Omega}
. We then apply the monotonicity of a certain quermassintegral to show that
\hat{\Omega}
satisfies

(1.4)

\Phi_{0}(\hat{\Omega},\beta)=c\Phi_{n}(\hat{\Omega},\beta)

for any Borel set
\beta
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, where
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{n}
are the hyperbolic curvature measures of
\hat{\Omega}
, and
c
is a constant. A theorem of Kohlmann
[
19
]
which characterises the geodesic ball in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
using the equation (
1.4
) for curvature measures can be used to conclude that
\hat{\Omega}
is a geodesic ball. Moreover, if we denote the center of the inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
as
p_{t}
, then using the Alexandrov reflection argument and the subsequential Hausdorff convergence, we can deduce that for all time
t\to\infty
, the points
p_{t}
converge to a fixed point
p
, which is the center of the ball
\hat{\Omega}
. With the help of this, we can prove the uniform positive bounds for the principal curvatures and then obtain the smooth convergence of the flow to a geodesic sphere.

The convergence result in Theorem
1.2
has an application in the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for quermassintegrals in the hyperbolic space (see §
2
for the definitions). Along the flow (
1.1
), we find that the
(n-1)
th quermassintegral
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
is monotone decreasing in time
t
. As the volume
|\Omega_{t}|
is preserved, the smooth convergence proved in Theorem
1.2
yields the following Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality of quermassintegrals for convex domains in the hyperbolic space:

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is a bounded weakly convex domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Then the following inequality holds:

(1.5)

\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega)\geq\psi_{n}\left(|\Omega|\right),

where
\psi_{n}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a strictly increasing function such that the equality is achieved for geodesic balls. Moreover, the equality holds in (
1.5
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

Here we say that a hypersurface is weakly convex if all principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}\geq 0
. We remark that the inequality (
1.5
) for convex domains in the hyperbolic space was proved also by Brendle, Guan and Li in a preprint
[
7
]
using a different method. The argument we present here provides a new proof of (
1.5
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some preliminary results concerning the geometry of hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space, the evolution equations for geometric quantities along the flow (
1.1
), the quermassintegrals and curvature measures in the hyperbolic space.

2.1.
Hyperbolic space

The hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
can be viewed as a warped product manifold
(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}})
with

g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=d\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is the round metric on unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
D
be the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. The vector field
V=\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho}
is a conformal Killing field satisfying
DV=\cosh\rho g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with a smooth boundary
M=\partial\Omega
. Then
M
is a smooth convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We denote by
g_{ij},h_{ij}
and
\nu
the induced metric, the second fundamental form and the unit outward normal vector of
M
respectively. As
M
is convex, there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Omega
such that
M
is star-shaped with respect to
p_{0}
and can be written as a radial graph
M=\{(\rho(\theta),\theta),~\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}
with respect to
p_{0}
for a smooth function
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Equivalently, the support function of
M
with respect to
p_{0}\in\Omega
defined by

u=\langle V,\nu\rangle=\langle\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho},\nu\rangle

is positive everywhere on
M
. It is well known that (see e.g.
[
16
]
)

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\sigma_{ij},

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}}}(-(\sinh\rho)\rho_{ij}+2(\cosh\rho)\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\cosh\rho\sigma_{ij}),

\displaystyle\nu=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2}/\sinh^{2}\rho}}\left(1,-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\sinh^{2}\rho},\cdots,-\frac{\rho_{n}}{\sinh^{2}\rho}\right),

where
\bar{\nabla}
is the covariant derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with respect to the round metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}=(\sigma_{ij})
and
\rho_{i}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\rho,\rho_{ij}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\rho
. It follows that the Gauss curvature
K
of
M
can be expressed as a function of
\rho
and its first and second derivatives:

(2.1)

K=\frac{\det h_{ij}}{\det g_{ij}}=\frac{\det(-\sinh\rho\rho_{ij}+2\cosh\rho\rho_{i}\rho_{j}+\sinh^{2}\rho\cosh\rho\sigma_{ij})}{(\sinh^{2}\rho+|\bar{\nabla}\rho|^{2})^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(\sinh\rho)^{2n-2}}.

)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k\in\{0,\dots,n-2\}
. Assume that
\Omega
is a compact, connected, locally convex subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with nonempty interior satisfying

(2.13)

\Phi_{k}(\Omega,\beta)=c\Phi_{n}(\Omega,\beta)

for any Borel set
\beta\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H}^{n+1})
, where
c>0
is a constant, then
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

Note that there is no smoothness assumption on the domain
\Omega
in Theorem
2.3
. When the boundary
\partial\Omega
is smooth, the equation (
2.13
) means that the
(n-k)
th mean curvature
\sigma_{n-k}
is a constant on
\partial\Omega
, and the conclusion of Theorem
2.3
in this case reduces to the classical result of Montiel and Ros
[
24
]
.

3.
Klein model and Projection

To investigate the flow (
1.1
) for convex hypersurfaces, it is convenient to project it to the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and apply the Gauss map parametrization as in the work
[
2
, §5]
by the first author and Andrews. We briefly review the argument here and refer the readers to
[
2
]
for details.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}
the Minkowski spacetime, that is the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
endowed with the Minkowski spacetime metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
given by

\langle X,X\rangle~=~-X_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}X_{i}^{2}

for any vector
X=(X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
. The hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is then

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{1,n+1}~|~\langle X,X\rangle=-1,\ X_{0}>0\}.

The Klein model parametrizes the hyperbolic space using the unit disc, which induces a projection from an embedding
X:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to an embedding
Y:M^{n}\rightarrow B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by

(3.1)

X~=~\frac{(1,Y)}{\sqrt{1-|Y|^{2}}}.

Let
\nu\in T\mathbb{H}^{n+1},g^{X}_{ij},h^{X}_{ij},K^{X}
and
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g^{Y}_{ij},h^{Y}_{ij},K^{Y}
denote the unit normal vectors, induced metrics, the second fundamental forms and Gauss curvatures of
X(M^{n})\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
Y(M^{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
respectively. We have the following relations:

(3.2)

\displaystyle h^{X}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{Y}_{ij}}{\sqrt{\left(1-|Y|^{2}\right)\left(1-\langle N,Y\rangle^{2}\right)}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle g^{X}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-|Y|^{2}}\left(g^{Y}_{ij}+\frac{\langle Y,\partial_{i}Y\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{j}Y\rangle}{1-|Y|^{2}}\right).

It follows from (
3.2
) that
X(M^{n})
is convex in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is equivalent to that
Y(M^{n})
is convex in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If we take the determinant on both sides of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), the Gauss curvatures satisfy

(3.4)

K^{X}=\left(\frac{1-|Y|^{2}}{1-\langle N,Y\rangle^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}K^{Y}.

Suppose that
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is a smooth convex solution to the flow (
1.1
). Then the corresponding solution
Y:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfies the evolution equation:

(3.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Y=\sqrt{(1-|Y|^{2})(1-\langle N,Y\rangle^{2})}\Big(\phi(t)-(K^{X})^{\alpha}\Big)N.

Since each
Y_{t}(M^{n})=Y(M^{n},t)\subset B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is convex, we can parametrize
Y_{t}(M^{n})
using the Gauss map and the support function
s(z):=\langle Y_{t}(N^{-1}(z)),z\rangle
, where
N^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to M^{n}
is the inverse map of the Gauss map. Then
Y_{t}(M^{n})
is given by the embedding
Y:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with (see
[
32
, §2]
)

(3.6)

Y(z)=~s(z)z+\bar{\nabla}s

where
\bar{\nabla}
is the gradient with respect to the round metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The derivative of this map is given by

\partial_{i}Y=~\mathfrak{r}_{ik}\sigma^{kl}\partial_{l}z

in local coordinates, where
\mathfrak{r}_{ij}
is given as follows

(3.7)

\mathfrak{r}_{ij}=~\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}s+s\sigma_{ij}.

The eigenvalues
\mathfrak{r}_{i}
of
\mathfrak{r}_{ij}
with respect to the round metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
are the inverse of the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}^{Y}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{r}_{i}=1/{\kappa_{i}^{Y}}
, and are called the principal radii of curvature. The determinant of (
3.7
) gives the Gauss curvature of
Y_{t}(M^{n})
:

(3.8)

K^{Y}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(\mathfrak{r}_{ij})}.

By the identity (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.9)

K^{X}=\left(\frac{1-(s^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2})}{1-s^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}K^{Y}.

The evolution equation (
3.5
) is equivalent to the following scalar parabolic equation:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}s=~\sqrt{(1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2})(1-s^{2})}\Big(\phi(t)-(K^{X})^{\alpha}\Big)

on the round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
for the support function
s(z,t)
of
Y(M^{n},t)
.

We summarize the results as the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
X(M^{n},t)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1},t\in[0,T)
is a smooth convex solution to the flow (
1.1
). Then up to a tangential diffeomorphism, the corresponding solution
Y(M^{n},t)\subset B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and its support function
s(z,t)
satisfy the following equations:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Y=

\displaystyle~\sqrt{(1-|Y|^{2})(1-\langle N,Y\rangle^{2})}\Big(\phi(t)-(K^{X})^{\alpha}\Big)N,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}s=

\displaystyle~\sqrt{(1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2})(1-s^{2})}\Big(\phi(t)-(K^{X})^{\alpha}\Big),

where the Gauss curvature
K^{X}
is related to the Gauss curvature
K^{Y}
via the identities (
3.4
) and (
3.9
).

For simplicity of the notation, we denote
A
and
B
as the following functions:

(3.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} A=A(s,\bar{\nabla}s)&:=\sqrt{(1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2})(1-s^{2})},\\
B=B(s,\bar{\nabla}s)&:=(1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2})^{\frac{n+2}{2}\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(1-s^{2})^{-\frac{n+2}{2}\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then (
3.11
) can be simplified as:

(3.13)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}s=A\phi(t)-B(K^{Y})^{\alpha}.

In §
4.2
, we will estimate the upper bound of the largest eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{r}_{ij}
along the equation (
3.13
) and this implies a positive lower bound on the principal curvatures of the solution
M_{t}
to the original flow (
1.1

along the flow (
3.13

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathfrak{r}_{11}-F^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\mathfrak{r}_{11}=

\displaystyle+A+A_{ss}{s_{1}}^{2}-2A_{ss_{1}}ss_{1}-A_{s}s+A_{s_{1}s_{1}}s^{2}-A_{s_{1}}s_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle-(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\Big(B_{s_{1}s_{1}}{\mathfrak{r}_{11}}^{2}+2B_{ss_{1}}s_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{11}-2B_{s_{1}s_{1}}\mathfrak{r}_{11}s+B_{s}\mathfrak{r}_{11}

\displaystyle+B+B_{ss}{s_{1}}^{2}-2B_{ss_{1}}ss_{1}-B_{s}s+B_{s_{1}s_{1}}s^{2}-B_{s_{1}}s_{1}

The notations such as

We first recall that

\bar{\nabla}_{k}\mathfrak{r}_{ij}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{kj}.

This follows from the Ricci identity for commuting covariant derivatives on

. Differentiating (
3.15

(3.16)

\bar{\nabla}_{(i}\bar{\nabla}_{j)}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}=\bar{\nabla}_{(k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell)}\mathfrak{r}_{ij}+\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\sigma_{ij}-\mathfrak{r}_{ij}\sigma_{k\ell},

(\mathfrak{r}^{ij})=(\mathfrak{r}_{ij})^{-1}

(\mathfrak{r}_{ij})

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}K^{Y}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(\mathrm{det}(\mathfrak{r}_{ij}))^{2}}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathrm{det}(\mathfrak{r}_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mathrm{det}(\mathfrak{r}_{ij})}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K^{Y}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell},

and

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{i}K^{Y}=

\displaystyle-K^{Y}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}-\bar{\nabla}_{j}K^{Y}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}-K^{Y}\bar{\nabla_{j}}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-K^{Y}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}+K^{Y}\left(\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\right)\left(\mathfrak{r}^{pq}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\mathfrak{r}_{pq}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad+K^{Y}\mathfrak{r}^{kq}\mathfrak{r}^{p\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{j}\mathfrak{r}_{pq}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}.

On the other hand, the spatial derivatives of
A(s,\bar{\nabla}s)
can be calculated as

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{i}A=

\displaystyle A_{s}s_{i}+A_{s_{k}}s_{ki}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{s_{k}}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}+A_{s}s_{i}-sA_{s_{k}}\sigma_{ki},

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{j}\bar{\nabla}_{i}A=

\displaystyle A_{s}s_{ij}+A_{ss}s_{i}s_{j}+A_{ss_{\ell}}s_{\ell j}s_{i}+A_{s_{k}s}s_{j}s_{ki}+A_{s_{k}s_{\ell}}s_{ki}s_{\ell j}+A_{s_{k}}s_{kij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{s}(\mathfrak{r}_{ij}-s\sigma_{ij})+A_{ss}s_{i}s_{j}+A_{ss_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{r}_{\ell j}-s\sigma_{\ell j})s_{i}

\displaystyle+A_{s_{k}s}s_{j}(\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-s\sigma_{ki})+A_{s_{k}s_{\ell}}(\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-s\sigma_{ki})(\mathfrak{r}_{\ell j}-s\sigma_{\ell j})

\displaystyle+A_{s_{k}}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{j}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-s_{j}\sigma_{ki}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{s_{k}}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\mathfrak{r}_{ij}+A_{s}\mathfrak{r}_{ij}+A_{ss_{\ell}}s_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{\ell j}+A_{s_{k}s}s_{j}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}+A_{s_{k}s_{\ell}}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}\mathfrak{r}_{\ell j}

\displaystyle-A_{s_{k}s_{l}}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}\sigma_{\ell j}s-A_{s_{k}s_{\ell}}\mathfrak{r}_{\ell j}\sigma_{ki}s-A_{s}s\sigma_{ij}+A_{ss}s_{i}s_{j}-A_{ss_{\ell}}\sigma_{\ell j}ss_{i}

(3.20)

\displaystyle-A_{s_{k}s}\sigma_{ki}s_{j}s+A_{s_{k}s_{\ell}}\sigma_{ki}\sigma_{lj}s^{2}-A_{s_{k}}\sigma_{ki}s_{j},

where
A_{s},A_{s_{k}}
are partial derivatives of
A
with respect to its first and second arguments, and we used (
3.7
) and the Codazzi-type identity (
3.15
). The calculation for
B(s,\bar{\nabla}s)
is similar.

Taking the spatial derivatives to the equation (
3.13
) and using (
3.17
)
-
(
3.20

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}(\partial_{t}s)=

\displaystyle\phi(t)\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}A-(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}B-2\alpha(K^{Y})^{\alpha-1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}K^{Y}\bar{\nabla}_{1}B

\displaystyle-\alpha(K^{Y})^{\alpha-1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}K^{Y}B-\alpha(\alpha-1)(K^{Y})^{\alpha-2}(\bar{\nabla}_{1}K^{Y})^{2}B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(t)\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}A-(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\Big(\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}B-2\alpha\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}B

\displaystyle-\alpha B\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}+\alpha^{2}B\big(\sum_{k,\ell}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\big)^{2}+\alpha B\mathfrak{r}^{kq}\mathfrak{r}^{p\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{pq}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(t)\Big(A_{s_{k}}\bar{\nabla}_{k}{\mathfrak{r}_{11}}+A_{s}\mathfrak{r}_{11}+2A_{ss_{1}}s_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{11}+A_{s_{1}s_{1}}{\mathfrak{r}_{11}^{2}}-2A_{s_{1}s_{1}}\mathfrak{r}_{11}s

\displaystyle-A_{s}s+A_{ss}{s_{1}}^{2}-2A_{ss_{1}}ss_{1}+A_{s_{1}s_{1}}s^{2}-A_{s_{1}}s_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle-(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\Big(B_{s_{k}}\bar{\nabla}_{k}{\mathfrak{r}_{11}}+B_{s}\mathfrak{r}_{11}+2B_{ss_{1}}s_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{11}+B_{s_{1}s_{1}}{\mathfrak{r}_{11}^{2}}-2B_{s_{1}s_{1}}\mathfrak{r}_{11}s

(3.21)

\displaystyle+{\alpha}^{2}B(\sum_{k,\ell}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell})^{2}+\alpha B\mathfrak{r}^{ik}\mathfrak{r}^{j\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{ij}}\bar{\nabla}_{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}}\Big).

Let
F^{k\ell}=\alpha B(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathfrak{r}_{11}-F^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}\mathfrak{r}_{11}=

r^{2}=|Y|^{2}=s^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
s(z,t)
be a smooth solution of the flow (
3.13
). If we choose a local orthonormal frame

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}r^{2}-F^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{\nabla}_{\ell}r^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\phi(t)\Big(sA+A_{s}|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}+A_{s_{k}}s_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-A_{s_{k}}s_{k}s\Big)

(3.23)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\Big((1-n\alpha)sB+B_{s}|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}+B_{s_{k}}s_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-B_{s_{k}}s_{k}s+\alpha B\sum_{k}{\mathfrak{r}_{kk}}\Big),

where
F^{k\ell}=\alpha B(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}

Proof.

Since
r^{2}=s^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}
, using (
3.13
), (
3.17
) and (
3.19
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r^{2}=

\displaystyle 2ss_{t}+2s_{i}s_{it}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2s(A\phi(t)-B(K^{Y})^{\alpha})+2s_{i}\Big(\phi(t)\bar{\nabla}_{i}A-(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\bar{\nabla}_{i}B+\alpha B(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\phi(t)\left(sA+A_{s}|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}+A_{s_{k}}s_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-A_{s_{k}}s_{i}\sigma_{ki}s\right)

\displaystyle-2(K^{Y})^{\alpha}\left(sB+B_{s}|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}+B_{s_{k}}s_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{ki}-B_{s_{k}}s_{i}\sigma_{ki}s-\alpha B\mathfrak{r}^{k\ell}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\mathfrak{r}_{k\ell}s_{i}\right),

∎

Remark 3.4
.

(i). The projection method via Klein model has also been used recently by Chen and Huang
[
10
]
to study the contracting Gauss curvature flows in the hyperbolic space.

(ii). Denote
\mathcal{W}^{X}
the Weingarten matrix of
X(M^{n})\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply that the inverse matrix
\mathcal{W}_{X}^{-1}
of

satisfies

\displaystyle(\mathcal{W}_{X}^{-1})_{ij}=

\displaystyle(h^{-1}_{X})^{jk}g_{ki}^{X}

(3.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h^{-1}_{Y})^{kj}\left(g_{ki}^{Y}+\frac{\langle Y,\partial_{i}Y\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{k}Y\rangle}{(1-|Y|^{2})}\right)\sqrt{\frac{1-\langle N,Y\rangle^{2}}{1-|Y|^{2}}}.

We can also express (
3.4
) using
s,\bar{\nabla}s
and
\mathfrak{r}_{ij}
as follows:

(3.25)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{W}_{X}^{-1})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\left(\sigma^{jq}+\frac{\langle\sigma^{ja}\bar{\nabla}_{a}s,\sigma^{qb}\bar{\nabla}_{b}s\rangle}{1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}}\right)\mathfrak{r}_{qi}\sqrt{\frac{1-s^{2}}{1-s^{2}-|\bar{\nabla}s|^{2}}}.

Since the eigenvalues of

correspond to the reciprocal of the principal curvatures
\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}
of

, the above equation means that in order to estimate the lower bound of

together with the

estimates of

. This observation will be used in the next section. Moreover, we believe that (

) would also be useful for studying general fully nonlinear curvature flows in the hyperbolic space via the projection method described in this section.

4.
A priori estimate

In this section, we first review the
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates of the solution
M_{t}
to the flow (
1.1
), which can be proved using a similar argument as in
[
1
,
2
]
. Then we derive a positive lower bound on the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of the solution
M_{t}
, and prove the upper bound of the Gauss curvature
K
. Both of them may depend on the time
t
.

4.1.
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed and convex hypersurface in the hyperbolic space. The flow (
1.1
) has a unique smooth solution
M_{t}
for at least a short time and
M_{t}
is convex on a possibly shorter time interval. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
M_{t}
a smooth convex solution to the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
on a maximum time interval
[0,T)
, where
T\leq\infty
.

Denote by
\Omega_{t}
as the domain enclosed by
M_{t}
such that
\partial\Omega_{t}=M_{t}
. As the velocity of the flow (
1.1
) only depends on the curvature which is invariant under reflection with respect to a totally geodesic hyperplane, we can argue as in
[
1
, §4]
using the Alexandrov reflection method to show that the inner radius and outer radius of
\Omega_{t}
are uniformly bounded.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M_{t}
be the smooth convex solution to the flow (
1.1
) on the time interval
t\in[0,T)
. Denote
\rho_{-}(t)
,
\rho_{+}(t)
be the inner radius and outer radius of
\Omega_{t}
. Then there exist positive constants
c_{1},\ c_{2}
depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
M_{0}
such that

(4.1)

0<c_{1}\leq\rho_{-}(t)\leq\rho_{+}(t)\leq c_{2}

for all time
t\in[0,T)
.

By (
4.1
), the inner radius of
\Omega_{t}
is bounded below by a positive constant
c_{1}
. This implies that for each
t\in[0,T)
there exists a geodesic ball
B_{c_{1}}(p_{t})
of radius
c_{1}
centered at some point
p_{t}
such that
B_{c_{1}}(p_{t})\subset\Omega_{t}
. A similar argument as in
[
2
, Lemma 4.2]
yields the existence of a geodesic ball with fixed center enclosed by the flow hypersurface on a suitable fixed time interval.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M_{t}
be the smooth convex solution to the flow (
1.1
) on the time interval
[0,T)
. For any
t_{0}\in[0,T)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{W}\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\beta_{1}(T)+(1+n\alpha)\cosh(2c_{2})-\alpha nc^{1+\frac{1}{n\alpha}}\tilde{W}^{\frac{1}{n\alpha}}\biggr)\tilde{W}^{2}.

The coefficient of the hightest order term on the right hand side of (
4.25
) is negative, so the comparison principle implies that
\tilde{W}
is bounded above by a positive constant. In fact, whenever

\tilde{W}\geq\bigg(\frac{2}{\alpha n}\left(\beta_{1}(T)+(1+n\alpha)\cosh(2c_{2})\right)\bigg)^{n\alpha}c^{-n\alpha-1}~=:\beta_{2}(T),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{W}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha n}{2}c^{1+\frac{1}{n\alpha}}\tilde{W}^{2+\frac{1}{n\alpha}}.

Therefore,

(4.26)

\tilde{W}(t)\leq\max\left\{\bigg(W^{-1-\frac{1}{n\alpha}}(t_{0})+\frac{n\alpha+1}{2}c^{1+\frac{1}{n\alpha}}(t-t_{0})\bigg)^{-\frac{n\alpha}{n\alpha+1}},\beta_{2}(T)\right\}

for all time
t\in\left[t_{0},\min\{T,t_{0}+\tau\}\right)
.

For
t_{0}=0
, we obtain from (
4.26
) the upper bound

\tilde{W}(t)\leq\max\left\{\tilde{W}(0),\beta_{2}(T)\right\},\quad\forall t\in[0,\min\{\tau,T\})

and so

(4.27)

K^{\alpha}\leq\sinh(2c_{2})\max\left\{\tilde{W}(0),\beta_{2}(T)\right\},\quad\forall t\in[0,\min\{\tau,T\}).

Next, for
t_{0}=\tau/2
, the estimate (
4.26
) implies

\displaystyle\tilde{W}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{(\frac{n\alpha+1}{2}(t-t_{0}))^{-\frac{n\alpha}{n\alpha+1}}c^{-1},\beta_{2}(T)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\max\left\{(\frac{(n\alpha+1)\tau}{4})^{-\frac{n\alpha}{n\alpha+1}}c^{-1},\beta_{2}(T)\right\}

for
t\in[\tau,\min\{3\tau/2,T\})
, and so

(4.28)

K^{\alpha}\leq\sinh(2c_{2})\max\left\{(\frac{(n\alpha+1)\tau}{4})^{-\frac{n\alpha}{n\alpha+1}}c^{-1},\beta_{2}(T)\right\}

for
t\in[\tau,\min\{3\tau/2,T\})
. Repeating the above argument for
t_{0}=m\tau/2
(
m\geq 2
), we can get the estimate (
4.28
) for
t\in[\frac{(m+1)\tau}{2},\min\{\frac{(m+2)\tau}{2},T\})
, which covers the whole time interval
[0,T)
. Combining (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), we complete the proof of Proposition
4.5
.
∎

5.
Long time existence

In this section, we prove the long time existence of the flow (
1.1
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then
M_{t}
remains convex and exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We will argue by contradiction. Let
[0,T)
be the maximum interval such that the solution of the flow (
1.1
) exists with
T<\infty
. Then combining Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.5
yields that the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
of the solution
M_{t}
satisfy

(5.1)

0<\underline{\kappa}_{0}\leq\kappa_{i}\leq\overline{\kappa}_{0},\quad i=1,\dots,n

for all time
t\in[0,T)
, where the constants
\underline{\kappa}_{0},\overline{\kappa}_{0}
depend on
n,\alpha,M_{0}
and
T
.

To prove the long time existence of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
), we need to derive the higher order regularity estimates. Recall that in
\lx@sectionsign
3
, up to a tangential diffeomorphism, the flow equation (
1.1
) is equivalent the following scalar parabolic equation

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}s=

\displaystyle A\phi(t)-B(K^{Y})^{\alpha}=~A\phi(t)-B(\det\mathfrak{r})^{-\alpha}

on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\mathfrak{r}_{ij}=s_{ij}+s\sigma_{ij}
and
A
,
B
are functions defined in (
3.12
) which depend only on
s
and
\bar{\nabla}s
. Combing Lemma
4.3
and the curvature estimate (
5.1
) gives the
C^{2}
estimates of
s(z,t)
. Denote the right hand side of (
5.2
) by
G(\bar{\nabla}^{2}s,\bar{\nabla}s,s,z,t)
. Then the derivatives of
G
with respect to the first argument are given by

(5.3)

\dot{G}^{ij}=n\alpha B(\det\mathfrak{r})^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\partial(\det\mathfrak{r})^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\partial s_{ij}}

and

(5.4)

\begin{split}\ddot{G}^{ij,k\ell}&=n\alpha B(\det\mathfrak{r})^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\partial^{2}(\det\mathfrak{r})^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\partial s_{ij}\partial s_{k\ell}}\\
&-n\alpha(n\alpha+1)B(\det\mathfrak{r})^{-\alpha-\frac{2}{n}}\frac{\partial(\det\mathfrak{r})^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\partial s_{ij}}\frac{\partial(\det\mathfrak{r})^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\partial s_{k\ell}}.\end{split}

The estimates we have established imply the existence of a constant
C=C(T)>0
, such that
1/CI\leq(\dot{G^{ij}})\leq CI
, that is, the operator
G
is uniformly elliptic on the finite time interval
[0,T)
. Since
(\det\mathfrak{r})^{\frac{1}{n}}
is concave with respect to
s_{ij}
, we see that
G
is also a concave operator. We can apply Theorem 1.1 in
[
28
]
to obtain a
C^{2,\gamma}
estimate on
s
, for a suitable
\gamma\in(0,1)
. See also the arguments in
[
9
,
23
]
for the
C^{2,\gamma}
estimate of the solutions to volume preserving curvature flows. Then by the parabolic Schauder theory (see
[
21
]
), we can deduce all higher order regularity estimates of
s
on
[0,T)
and a standard continuation argument then shows that
T=+\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the curvature estimate (
5.1
) of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
) depends on time
t
and may degenerate as time
t\to\infty
. To study the asymptotical behavior of
M_{t}
as
t\to\infty
, we still need to get an uniform curvature estimate which does not depend on time. This will be obtained in the next two sections.

6.
Monotonicity and Hausdorff convergence

In this section, we prove the monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
, the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of the solution
M_{t}
of (
1.1
) and the convergence of the center of the inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
to a fixed point.

Denote the average integral of the Gauss curvature by

(6.1)

\bar{K}=\frac{1}{|M_{t}|}\int_{M_{t}}{Kd\mu_{t}}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{t})+\frac{1}{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-2}(\Omega_{t})}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t})}.

It follows from the monotonicity (
2.10
) of quermassintegrals with respect to inclusion of convex sets and the estimates on inner radius and outer radius in Lemma
4.1
that
\bar{K}
is uniformly bounded from above and below by positive constants depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
M_{0}
.

6.1.
Monotonicity for
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}

We first show the following monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
along the flow (
1.1
), which will be useful in proving the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M_{t}
be a smooth convex solution of the volume preserving flow (
1.1
). Denote by
\Omega_{t}
the domain enclosed by
M_{t}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
is monotone decreasing in time
t
, and is strictly decreasing unless
\Omega_{t}
is a geodesic ball.

Proof.

From the evolution equation (
2.9
) for the quermassintegrals of
\Omega_{t}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})=n\int_{M_{t}}{K(\phi(t)-K^{\alpha})d\mu_{t}}.

Since
\phi(t)
is defined as in (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{|M_{t}|}\left(\int_{M_{t}}Kd\mu_{t}\int_{M_{t}}K^{\alpha}d\mu_{t}-|M_{t}|\int_{M_{t}}K^{\alpha+1}d\mu_{t}\right)

(6.2)

\displaystyle=-n\int_{M_{t}}{(K-\bar{K})(K^{\alpha}-{\bar{K}}^{\alpha})d\mu_{t}}\leq 0.

Note that equality holds in (
6.1
) if and only if
K
is a constant on
M_{t}
, which means
M_{t}
is a geodesic sphere by the Alexandrov type theorem for hypersurfaces with constant Gauss curvature in the hyperbolic space (see
[
24
]
).
∎

6.2.
Subsequential Hausdorff convergence

In this subsection, we prove that there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
such that the solution
M_{t_{i}}
converges in Hausdorff sense to a geodesic sphere. We first prove the following estimate:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth closed and convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then there exists a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\},t_{i}\to\infty
, such that

(6.3)

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|K-\bar{K}|d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\,\,t_{i}\to\infty.

Proof.

Applying the monotonicity in Lemma
6.1
and the long time existence of the flow (
1.1
), we have

n\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M_{t}}{(K-\bar{K})(K^{\alpha}-{\bar{K}}^{\alpha})d\mu_{t}}dt\leq{\mathcal{A}_{n-1}}(\Omega_{0})<\infty.

Therefore there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
such that

(6.4)

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{(K-\bar{K})(K^{\alpha}-{\bar{K}}^{\alpha})d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0.

If
\alpha\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle(K-\bar{K})(K^{\alpha}-{\bar{K}}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{0}^{1}{((1-s)\bar{K}+sK)^{\alpha-1}ds}\cdot(K-\bar{K})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\int_{0}^{1}{(1-s)^{\alpha-1}ds}\bar{K}^{\alpha-1}(K-\bar{K})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C(K-\bar{K})^{2},

where we used the fact that
\bar{K}
is uniformly bounded and
\alpha\geq 1
. Therefore, (
6.4
) implies that

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{(K-\bar{K})^{2}d\mu_{t_{i}}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\,\,i\to\infty

for a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
and the estimate (
6.3
) follows by the Hölder inequality.

We next prove the estimate (
6.3
) for
0<\alpha<1
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t_{i}}}|K-\bar{K}|d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t_{i}}}|K-\bar{K}|^{1/2}|K-\bar{K}|^{1/2}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t_{i}}}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}{((1-s)\bar{K}^{\alpha}+sK^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}ds}\Big)^{1/2}\cdot\Big|(K^{\alpha}-\bar{K}^{\alpha})(K-\bar{K})\Big|^{1/2}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\underbrace{\int_{M_{t_{i}}}\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}{((1-s)\bar{K}^{\alpha}+sK^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}ds}d\mu_{t_{i}}}_{(I)}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\underbrace{\int_{M_{t_{i}}}(K-\bar{K})(K^{\alpha}-{\bar{K}}^{\alpha})d\mu_{t_{i}}}_{(II)}\Big)^{1/2}.

The second term
(II)
tends to zero as
t_{i}\to\infty
by (
6.4
). We show that the first term
(I)
is bounded for any
0<\alpha<1
. In fact, if
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have
1<1/\alpha\leq 2
. This implies that

\displaystyle(I)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}\int_{0}^{1}{((1-s)\bar{K}^{\alpha}+sK^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}ds}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}\frac{\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha}}{(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})^{2-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\bar{K}^{2\alpha-1}}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha}\bar{K}^{1-\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\leq~C

is uniformly bounded from above, where we used the Hölder inequality in the fourth inequality to get the estimate

\int_{M_{t_{i}}}K^{\alpha}d\mu_{t_{i}}\leq\left(\int_{M_{t_{i}}}Kd\mu_{t_{i}}\right)^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})^{1-\alpha}=\bar{K}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})

for
\alpha<1
and that
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\leq\mathcal{A}_{0}(B_{c_{2}}(0))\leq C
which is due to that the outer radius of
\Omega_{t_{i}}
is bounded from above by
c_{2}
. We repeat the argument for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k})
with
k=2,3,\cdots
, where we have
(k+1)\alpha\geq 1
and
0<k\alpha<1
. Then

\displaystyle(I)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}\frac{(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})^{k}}{(\bar{K}^{\alpha}+K^{\alpha})^{k+1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\bar{K}^{(k+1)\alpha-1}}\int_{M_{t_{i}}}2^{k-1}(\bar{K}^{k\alpha}+K^{k\alpha})d\mu_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{k}}{\alpha}\bar{K}^{1-\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\leq~C

is uniformly bounded, where in the third inequality we used the Hölder inequality again as
k\alpha<1
. Therefore for any
0<\alpha<1
, the term
(I)
is uniformly bounded from above and thus the estimate (
6.3
) follows in this case.
∎

Note that the curvature estimate (
5.1
) of the solution
M_{t}
of the flow (
1.1
) depends on time
t
and may degenerate as time
t\to\infty
. So we can not conclude from (
6.3
) that the solution
M_{t_{i}}
converges to a geodesic sphere as
t_{i}\to\infty
, as we do not have an uniform regularity estimate to guarantee the existence of a smooth limit of
M_{t_{i}}
. However, we can apply the similar idea in the work
[
3
, §6]
by the first author and Andrews in the Euclidean space to prove the following subsequential Hausdorff convergence result:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
M_{t}
be the smooth solution of the flow (
1.1
) starting from
M_{0}
. Then there exists a sequence of times
\{t_{i}\},t_{i}\to\infty
, such that
M_{t_{i}}
converges to a geodesic sphere
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
in Hausdorff sense as
t_{i}\to\infty
, where
p
is the center of the sphere and the radius
\rho_{\infty}
is determined by the fact that
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
encloses the same volume of
M_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
p_{t}
be the center of the inball of
\Omega_{t}
and let
\varphi_{t}:\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be an isometry carrying
p_{t}
to the origin
o\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Clearly, each
\varphi_{t}(M_{t})
is a closed convex hypersurface with an inball centered at the origin and having inner radius
\rho_{-}(t)\geq c_{1}
. For simplicity of the notations, we still denote the transformed solution
\varphi_{t}(M_{t})
as
M_{t}
.

As in §
3
, we project
\Omega_{t}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
onto
B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and get the corresponding
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}\subset B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Since the outer radius of
\Omega_{t}
is uniformly bounded above and so does
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
, the Blaschke selection theorem (see Theorem 1.8.7 of
[
26
]
) implies that there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}
and a convex body
\tilde{\Omega}
such that
\tilde{\Omega}_{t_{i}}
converges to
\tilde{\Omega}
in Hausdorff sense as
t_{i}\to\infty
. Note that the projection yields an one-to-one correspondence between the convex bodies in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and the convex bodies in
B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, then there exists a convex set
\hat{\Omega}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{H}^{n+1})
such that
\Omega_{t_{i}}
converges to
\hat{\Omega}
in Hausdorff sense as
t_{i}\to\infty
. As each
\Omega_{t_{i}}
has inner radius
\rho_{-}(\Omega_{t_{i}})\geq c_{1}
, the limit convex set
\hat{\Omega}
has positive inner radius. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the sequence
t_{i}
is the same sequence such that (
6.3
) holds.

By the continuity of quermassintegrals with respect to the Hausdorff distance in Lemma
2.1
, the Hausdorff convergence of
\Omega_{t_{i}}
to
\hat{\Omega}
implies that

(6.5)

\bar{K}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(\Omega_{t_{i}})+\frac{1}{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-2}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}~\to~\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(\hat{\Omega})+\frac{1}{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-2}(\hat{\Omega})}{\mathcal{A}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})}~=:c

as
t_{i}\to\infty
. We will show that
\hat{\Omega}
satisfies the equation
\Phi_{0}(\hat{\Omega},\cdot)=c\Phi_{n}(\hat{\Omega},\cdot)
for the curvature measures
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{n}
. In fact, by the weak continuity of the curvature measures in Theorem
2.2
, for any bounded continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with compact support, we have that
\int{fd\Phi_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}
converges to
\int{fd\Phi_{0}(\hat{\Omega})}
, and
\int{fd\Phi_{n}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}
converges to
\int{fd\Phi_{n}(\hat{\Omega})}
, as
i\to\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\int{fd\Phi_{0}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}-c\int{fd\Phi_{n}(\Omega_{t_{i}})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{fKd\mathcal{H}^{n}}-\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{cfd\mathcal{H}^{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup|f|\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|K-c|d\mathcal{H}^{n}}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup|f|\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|K-\bar{K}|d\mathcal{H}^{n}}+\sup|f|\int_{M_{t_{i}}}{|\bar{K}-c|d\mathcal{H}^{n}}~\to~0

by the estimates (
6.3
) and (
6.5
).

It follows that
\int{fd\Phi_{0}(\hat{\Omega})}=c\int{fd\Phi_{n}(\hat{\Omega})}
for all bounded continuous functions
f
, and therefore
\Phi_{0}(\hat{\Omega},\cdot)=c\Phi_{n}(\hat{\Omega},\cdot)
as claimed. Then by Theorem
2.3
,
\hat{\Omega}
is a geodesic ball. This means that
\varphi_{t_{i}}(\Omega_{t_{i}})
converges to a geodesic ball of some radius
\rho_{\infty}
centered at the origin. The radius is uniquely determined by the preserving of the volume. Moreover, by an argument of Alexandrov reflection, the evolving domains
\Omega_{t}
cannot leave away from the initial domain
\Omega_{0}
, that is, there exists a geodesic ball
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
of radius
R>0
containing
\Omega_{0}
such that
\Omega_{t}\cap B_{R}\neq\emptyset
for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
, see Lemma 4.1 in
[
1
]
. In particular, this implies that the centers
p_{t_{i}}
of inner ball of
\Omega_{t_{i}}
are located in a compact subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and then there exists a subsequence of
t_{i}
(still denoted by
t_{i}
) such that
p_{t_{i}}
converges to a limit point
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. This concludes that for this subsequence of times
t_{i}
,
\Omega_{t_{i}}
converges to a geodesic ball
B_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
centered at the point
p
without correction of ambient isometry
\varphi_{t_{i}}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
6.3
.
∎

6.3.
Convergence of the center of the inner ball

The argument in
[
3
]
is not sufficient for us to deduce the Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
to the geodesic sphere for all time
t\to\infty
, as we do not have the analogue stability estimate as in
[
26
, Eq.(7.124)]
for the hyperbolic case. However, if we denote
p_{t}
as the center of the inner ball of
\Omega_{t}
, we can prove that
p_{t}
converges to the fixed point
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
for all time
t\to\infty
using the Alexandrov reflection and the subsequential Hausdorff convergence of
M_{t}
proved in Lemma
6.3
.

Recall that
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is the center of the limit geodesic sphere
S_{\rho_{\infty}}(p)
in Lemma
6.3
. Take an arbitrary direction
z\in T_{p}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\gamma_{z}
be the normal geodesic line (i.e.
|\gamma^{\prime}|=1
) through the point
p
with
\gamma_{z}(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{z}(0)=z
, and let
H_{z,s}
be the totally geodesic hyperplane in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
that is perpendicular to
\gamma_{z}
at
\gamma_{z}(s),s\in\mathbb{R}
. We use the notation
H_{z,s}^{+}
and
H_{z,s}^{-}
for the half-spaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
determined by
H_{z,s}
as follows:

H_{z,s}^{+}:=\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\geq s}H_{z,s^{\prime}},\qquad H_{z,s}^{-}:=\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\leq s}H_{z,s^{\prime}}.

For a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, denote

\Omega_{z}^{+}(s)=\Omega\cap H_{z,s}^{+},\qquad\Omega_{z}^{-}(s)=\Omega\cap H_{z,s}^{-}.

The reflection map across
H_{z,s}
is denoted by
R_{\gamma_{z},s}
. We define

\displaystyle S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}~|~R_{\gamma_{z},s}(\Omega_{z}^{+}(s))\subset\Omega_{z}^{-}(s)\},

\displaystyle S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\sup\{s\in\mathbb{R}~|~R_{\gamma_{z},s}(\Omega_{z}^{-}(s))\subset\Omega_{z}^{+}(s)\}.

The Alexandrov reflection argument implies that
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
is non-increasing in
t
for each
z
(see
[
2
, Lemma 6.1]
). By the definitions of
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
and
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})
, we have
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})\leq S_{\gamma_{z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
. Since
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})=-S_{\gamma_{-z}}^{+}(\Omega_{t})
, we also have that
S_{\gamma_{z}}^{-}(\Omega_{t})
is non-decreasing in
t
for each
z
. Note that the paper
[
2
]
deals with the flow with
h
-convex initial hypersurfaces, the argument in Lemma 6.1 of
[
2
]
works for convex solutions as well. The readers may refer to
[
11
,
12
]
for more details on the Alexandrov reflection method.

We need the following lemma in proving the convergence of the center of the inner ball.

Lemma 6.4
.

7.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.3

Finally, we give the proof of Corollary
1.3
using the monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{t})
and the convergence result of the flow (
1.1
). Firstly, if
\Omega
is convex, we evolve the boundary

by the flow

1.1
)
. Then the inequality (
1.5

1.2
and the monotonicity in Lemma

which means that
\Omega

\Omega_{t}
are all geodesic balls.

In general, for weakly convex
\Omega
we can approximate
\Omega
by a family of convex domains
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In fact, if we project the domain
\Omega
into

as in
\lx@sectionsign
2
, the equation (
3.2
) implies that the image

of the projection is also weakly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Hence we can approximate
\hat{\Omega}
by convex domains (e.g., by using mean curvature flow). Since the projection is a diffeomorphism, we find a family of convex domains
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
that approximate
\Omega
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then the inequality (
1.5
) for
\Omega
follows from the one for
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.

To prove the equality case for weakly convex
\Omega
, we employ an argument previously used in
[
15
]
. Suppose that
\Omega
is a weakly convex domain which attains the equality of (
1.5
). Let

. Since there exists at least one point

on a closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
such that all the principal curvatures are strictly larger than 1 at

, the subset

is open and nonempty. We claim that
M_{+}
is closed as well. In fact, pick any
\eta\in C_{0}^{2}(M_{+})
compactly supported in
M_{+}
, let
M_{\varepsilon}
be a smooth family of variational hypersurfaces generated by the vector field

. Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be the domain enclosed by
M_{\varepsilon}
. It is easy to show that

is weakly convex when
|\varepsilon|
is small enough. Hence

\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq\psi_{n}\left(|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\right)

holds for sufficiently small
|\varepsilon|
and with equality holding at

. Thus

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}\Big(\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})-\psi_{n}\left(|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|\right)\Big)=n\int_{M}{\left(K-\psi^{\prime}_{n}(|\Omega|)\right)\eta}d\mu.

Since

Jacob’s ladders, new properties of the function arg ⁡ ζ ⁡ ( 1 2 + i ​ t ) \arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right) and corresponding metamorphoses
Jan Moser

Abstract
. The notion of the Jacob’s ladders, reversely iterated integrals and the ζ \zeta -factorization is used in this paper in order to obtain new results in study of the function arg ⁡ ζ ⁡ ( 1 2 + i ​ t ) \arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right) . Namely, we obtain new formulae for non-local and non-linear interaction of the functions | ζ ⁡ ( 1 2 + i ​ t ) | |\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)| and arg ⁡ ζ ⁡ ( 1 2 + i ​ t ) \arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right) , and also a set of metamorphoses of the oscillating Q-system.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let us denote by
N(T)
the number of zeroes
\beta+i\gamma
of the
\zeta(s)
-function such that

\beta\in(0,1),\ \gamma\in(0,T).

We suppose that
T
is not equal to any
\gamma
. Otherwise, we put

N(T)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}[N(T+\epsilon)+N(T-\epsilon)].

It us well-know that

N(T)=\frac{1}{2\pi}T\ln\frac{T}{2\pi e}+\frac{7}{8}+S(T)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{T}\right),

where

(1.1)

S(T)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right),

and the value of
\arg
is obtained by continuous variation along the straight lines joining the points

2,\ 2+iT,\ \frac{1}{2}+iT,

starting with the value zero. Next, we have the function

(1.2)

S_{1}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}S(t){\rm d}t.

1.2.

Further, let us remind the following facts

\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)=\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|e^{i\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)},

i.e. the functions

(1.3)

\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|,\ \arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)

are parts of the Riemann function

\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right).

The study of these functions have proceeded by isolated ways. Namely:

(a)

the first one studied by Hardy-Littlewood

\int_{T}^{T+U}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}t\sim U\ln T,\dots

(b)

the second one by Backlund, E. Landau, H. Bohr, Littlewood, Titchmarsh - to the fundamental
Selberg’s results (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
).

Let us mention two of the Selberg’s results:

(1.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{T}^{T+H}\{S_{1}(t)\}^{2l}{\rm d}t=c_{l}H+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{\ln T}\right),\\
&T^{a}\leq H\leq T;\ \frac{1}{2}<a\leq 1,\ l\in\mathbb{N},\end{split}

where
l
is arbitrary and fixed, (see
[
4
]
, p. 130), and

(1.5)

S_{1}(t)=\Omega_{\pm}\left\{(\ln t)^{1/3}(\ln\ln t)^{-10/3}\right\},

(see
[
4
]
, p.150).

Remark 1
.

For our purpose it is sufficient to use the formula (
1.4
) in the minimal case

H=T^{1/2+\epsilon},\ \epsilon>0,

where
\epsilon
is sufficiently small (non-principal improvements of the exponent
1/2
are not
relevant for our purpose).

1.3.

To this date, there is no result in the theory of the Riemann zeta-function about the interaction of the
functions (
1.3
), or the functions

(1.6)

\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|,S_{1}(t).

That is, there is nothing known like

F\left(\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|,|S_{1}(\tau)|\right)=0

for a set of values
t,\tau
.

On the other hand, we have developed (see
[
1
]
) the method of
\zeta
-factorization that gives, for example,
the following formula (see
[
1
]
, (1.7))

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{0}\right)\right|}}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}\right)\right|

together with the infinite set of corresponding metamorphoses of the main multiform.

In this paper we use this method to obtain a result of the new type

(1.7)

|S_{1}(\alpha_{0})|\sim\Phi\left\{\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}\right)}{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\beta_{r}\right)}\right|\right\}

together with the infinite set of metamorphoses of the corresponding Q-system from
[
2
]
.

Remark 2
.

A kind of nonlinear and nonlocal interaction of the functions (
1.6
) is expressed by the formula (
1.7
).

2.
Theorem

2.1.

We begin with the Selberg’s formula

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{T}^{T+H}\{S_{1}(t)\}^{2l}{\rm d}t\sim c_{l}H,\ T\to\infty,\\
&H=T^{1/2+\epsilon},\ l\in\mathbb{N},\ \epsilon>0,\end{split}

(comp. (
1.4
) and Remark 1), where
l
is arbitrary and fixed,
\epsilon
is sufficiently small. Now, if we use
our method of transformation (see
[
2
]
, (4.1)–(4.19)) in the case of the formula (
2.1
) then we obtain
(see (
1.1
), (
1.2
)) the following

Theorem
.

Let

(2.2)

[T,T+H]\longrightarrow[\overset{1}{T},\overset{1}{\wideparen{T+H}}],\dots,[\overset{k}{T},\overset{k}{\wideparen{T+H}}],

where

[\overset{r}{T},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T+H}}],\ r=1,\dots,k,\ k\leq k_{0},\ k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

be the reversely iterated segment corresponding to the first segment in (
2.2
) and
k_{0}
be an arbitrary and fixed
number. Then there is a sufficiently big

T_{0}=T_{0}(l,\epsilon)>0

such that for every
T>T_{0}
and every admissible
l,\epsilon,k
there are the functions

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\alpha_{r}=\alpha_{r}(T,l;\epsilon,k),\ r=0,1,\dots,k,\\
&\beta_{r}=\beta_{r}(T;\epsilon,k),\ r=1,\dots,k,\\
&\alpha_{r},\beta_{r}\not=\gamma:\ \zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)=0\end{split}

such that

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right){\rm d}t\right|\sim\\
&\sim\pi(c_{l})^{\frac{1}{2l}}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}(T,l)\right)}{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\beta_{r}(T)\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{l}},\ T\to\infty.\end{split}

Moreover, the sequences

\{\alpha_{r}\}_{r=0}^{k},\ \{\beta_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}

have the following properties

(2.5)

\begin{split}&T<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\dots<\alpha_{k},\\
&T<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\dots<\beta_{k},\\
&\alpha_{0}\in(T,T+H),\ \alpha_{r},\beta_{r}\in(\overset{r}{T},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T+H}}),\\
&r=1,\dots,k,\end{split}

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\alpha_{r+1}-\alpha_{r}\sim(1-c)\pi(T),\ r=0,1,\dots,k-1,\\
&\beta_{r+1}-\beta_{r}\sim(1-c)\pi(T),\ r=1,\dots,k-1,\end{split}

where

\pi(T)\sim\frac{T}{\ln T},\ T\to\infty

is the prime-counting function and
c
is the Euler’s constant.

Remark 3
.

Let us notice that the asymptotic behavior of the sets

(2.7)

\{\alpha_{r}\}_{r=0}^{k},\ \{\beta_{r}\}_{r=1}^{k}

is as follows: at
T\to\infty
the points of every set in (
2.7
) recede unboundedly each from other and
all together recede to infinity. Hence, at
T\to\infty
each set in (
2.7
) looks like one-dimensional
Friedmann-Hubble universe.

2.2.

Let us denote the mean-value of the function

\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right),\ t\in[0,T]

by the symbol

\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,T]}.

Let us mention that the function under consideration has an infinite set of first-order discontinuities. Since

\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right){\rm d}t=\alpha_{0}(T)\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,\alpha_{0}(T)]},

then we obtain from (
2.4
) the following

Corollary 1
.

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\left|\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,\alpha_{0}(T)]}\right|\sim\\
&\sim\frac{\pi(c_{l})^{\frac{1}{2l}}}{\alpha_{0}(T)}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}(T,l)\right)}{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\beta_{r}(T,l)\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{l}},\\
&\alpha_{0}(T)\in(T,T+H),\ T\to\infty.\end{split}

Let us remind that the following Littlewood’s estimate (comp.
[
5
]
, p. 189)

S_{1}(t)=\mathcal{O}(\ln t),\ t\to\infty

holds true. Hence, we have (see (
1.1
), (
1.2
)) the estimate

(2.9)

\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,T]}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln T}{T}\right),\ T\to\infty.

Remark 4
.

Consequently, we have obtained in the direction of the estimate (
2.9
) the explicit asymptotic formula (
2.8
) for the mean-value

\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,T]}

on the infinite subset

\{\alpha_{0}(T)\},\alpha_{0}(T)\in(T,T+T^{1/2+\epsilon}),\ T\to\infty.

3.
Reduction of the integral in (
2.4
)

3.1.

Now, we use the Selberg’s
\Omega
-theorem (
1.5
) to transform our formula (
2.4
). It follows from (
1.5
) that
there are two sequences

\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\ \{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\ a_{n},b_{n}\to\infty

such that

(3.1)

\begin{split}&S_{1}(a_{n})>A(\ln a_{n})^{1/3}(\ln\ln a_{n})^{-10/3},\\
&S_{1}(b_{n})<-B(\ln a_{n})^{1/3}(\ln\ln a_{n})^{-10/3};\\
&A,B>0.\end{split}

Since

S_{1}(t),\ t>0

is the continuous function then by (
3.1
) there is (Bolzano-Cauchy) the sequence

(3.2)

\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\ S_{1}(\mu_{n})=0,\ \mu_{n}\to\infty,

where
\mu_{n}
is the odd-order root of the equation

(3.3)

S_{1}(t)=0,\ t>0.

Remark 5
.

We may suppose, of course, that the sequence (
3.2
) is
complete
one in the usual sense, the interval

(\mu_{n},\mu_{n+1})

does not contain any other odd-order root of the equation (
3.3
).

Remark 6
.

There is no need to discuss (for our purpose) the question about even-order roots of the equation (
3.3
).

Hence, we have: if

(3.4)

\bar{k}=\bar{k}[\alpha_{0}(T)]:\ \mu_{\bar{k}}<\alpha_{0}(T)<\mu_{\bar{k}+1}

and (of course, see (
2.4
), (
3.3
))

S_{1}[\alpha_{0}(T)]\not=0,

then

(3.5)

\begin{split}&S_{1}[\alpha_{0}(T)]=\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}S(t){\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\mu_{\bar{k}}}+\int_{\mu_{\bar{k}}}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}=\int_{\mu_{\bar{k}}}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}S(t){\rm d}t.\end{split}

Consequently, we have from (
2.4
) by (
3.4
), (
3.5
) the following

Corollary 2
.

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mu_{\bar{k}}}^{\alpha_{0}(T)}\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right){\rm d}t\right|\sim\\
&\sim\pi(c_{l})^{\frac{1}{2l}}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}(T,l)\right)}{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\beta_{r}(T)\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{l}},\ T\to\infty.\end{split}

3.2.

Next, we obtain from (
3.6
), (comp. (
2.8
)), the following

Corollary 3
.

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\left|\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[\mu_{\bar{k}},\alpha_{0}(T)]}\right|\sim\\
&\sim\frac{\pi(c_{l})^{\frac{1}{2l}}}{\alpha_{0}(T)-\mu_{\bar{k}}}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\alpha_{r}(T,l)\right)}{\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\beta_{r}(T)\right)}\right|^{-\frac{1}{l}},\end{split}

and, of course, (see (
2.8
), (
3.7
))

\begin{split}&\left|\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[\mu_{\bar{k}},\alpha_{0}(T)]}\right|\sim\\
&\sim\frac{\alpha_{0}(T)}{\alpha_{0}(T)-\mu_{\bar{k}}}\left|\left.\langle\arg\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\rangle\right|_{[0,\alpha_{0}(T)]}\right|,\ T\to\infty.\end{split}

4.
On infinite set of metamorphoses of the Q-system that is generated by the factorization
formula (
2.4
)

4.1.

Let us remind the Riemann-Siegel formula

(4.1)

Z(t)=2\sum_{n\leq\tau(t)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(t)-t\ln n\}+\mathcal{O}(t^{-1/4}),

where

\begin{split}&Z(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right),\ \tau(t)=\sqrt{\frac{t}{2\pi}},\\
&\vartheta(t)=-\frac{t}{2}\ln\pi+\mbox{Im}\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{t}{2}\right),\end{split}

(see
[
5
]
, pp. 79, 239). Next, we have introduced (see
[
2
]
, (2.1)) the following oscillatory
Q-system (based exactly on the Riemann-Siegel formula (
4.1
))

(4.2)

\begin{split}&G(x_{1},\dots,x_{k};y_{1},\dots,y_{k})=\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{Z(x_{r})}{Z(y_{r})}\right|=\\
&=\prod_{r=1}^{k}\left|\frac{\sum_{n\leq\tau(x_{r})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(x_{r})-x_{r}\ln n\}+R(x_{r})}{\sum_{n\leq\tau(y_{r})}\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\{\vartheta(y_{r})-y_{r}\ln n\}+R(y_{r})}\right|,\\
&(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in M_{k}^{1},\ (y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in M_{k}^{2},\\
&R(t)=\mathcal{O}(t^{-1/4}),\ k\leq k_{0}\in\mathbb{N},\end{split}

where

(4.3)

\begin{split}&M_{k}^{1}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in(T_{0},+\infty)^{k},\ T_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{k}\},\\
&M_{k}^{2}=\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in(T_{0},+\infty)^{k},\ T_{0}<y_{1}<\dots<y_{k}\},\\
&x_{r},y_{r}\not=\gamma:\ \zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)=0,\ r=1,\dots,k.\end{split}

4.2.

Next, we have obtained (see
[
2
]
, (3.1)) the following spectral formula

(4.4)

\begin{split}&Z(t)=2\sum_{n\leq\tau(x_{r})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\left\{t\ln\frac{\tau(x_{r})}{n}-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right\}+\\
&+\mathcal{O}(x_{r}^{-1/4}),\ \tau(x_{r})=\sqrt{\frac{x_{r}}{2\pi}},\\
&t\in[x_{r},x_{r}+V],\ V\in(0,\sqrt[4]{x_{r}}],\end{split}

(and similarly for
x_{r}\longrightarrow y_{r}
), where

T_{0}<x_{r},y_{r},\ r=1,\dots,k.

Remark 7
.

The spectral formula (
4.4
) is, of course, a variant of the Riemann-Siegel formula (
4.1
).

Remark 8
.

We call the expressions

(4.5)

\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}\cos\left\{t\omega_{n}(x_{r})-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right\},\dots

as the local Riemann’s oscillators with:

(a)

the amplitudes

\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}},

(b)

the incohorent local phase constants

\left\{-\frac{x_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right\},\ \left\{-\frac{y_{r}}{2}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right\},

(c)

the non-synchronized local times

t=t(x_{r})\in[x_{r},x_{r}+V],\dots

(d)

the local spectrum of the cyclic frequencies

\begin{split}&\{\omega_{n}(x_{r})\}_{n\leq\tau(x_{r})},\ \omega_{n}(x_{r})=\ln\frac{\tau(x_{r})}{n},\\
&\{\omega_{n}(y_{r})\}_{n\leq\tau(y_{r})},\ \omega_{n}(y_{r})=\ln\frac{\tau(y_{r})}{n}.\end{split}

Remark 9
.

The Q-system (
4.2
) represents a complicated oscillating process generated by oscillations of
big number of the local Riemann’s oscillators (
4.5
).

4.3.

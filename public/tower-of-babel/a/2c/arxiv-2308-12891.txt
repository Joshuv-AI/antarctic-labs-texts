A class of Discontinuous Galerkin methods for nonlinear variational problems
G. Grekas, K. Koumatos, C. Makridakis, A. Vikelis

Abstract
In the context of Discontinuous Galerkin methods, we study approximations of nonlinear variational problems. We propose element-wise nonconforming finite element methods to discretize the continuous minimisation problem. Using Γ \Gamma -convergence arguments we show that for convex energies the discrete minimisers converge to a minimiser of the continuous problem as the mesh parameter tends to zero, under the additional contribution of appropriately defined penalty terms at the level of the discrete energies. In addition, we provide error analysis in the case where the solution of the continuous minimization problem is smooth enough. Under appropriate assumptions we show local existence, uniqueness and corresponding optimal order error estimates for the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations of our scheme. We finally substantiate the feasibility of our methods by numerical examples.



1
Introduction

In this paper we propose and analyse Discontiuous Galerkin (DG) methods for nonlinear variational problems of a rather general form.
We formulate Discontiuous Galerkin methods in a systematic and consistent way and we analyse the convergence properties of the discretisations
by appropriately restricting the energy class.
Indeed, we consider the problem of minimising the total energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[u]=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u(x))dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,u(x)dx,

(1.1)

with
u
in appropriate Sobolev spaces incorporating boundary conditions.

W
is the strain energy function and
f
a given external field. Local minima of this problem satisfy appropriate Euler-Lagrange equations when certain smoothness requirements are met which are nonlinear. Despite the progress in Discontinuous Galerkin methods, appropriate formulations of the nonlinear principal part of equations of the form (
1.1
) are, to the best of our knowledge, quite limited. This paper is devoted to the systematic derivation of such formulations and their mathematical analysis.

Problems in the calculus of variations seeking to minimise functionals of the form
(
1.1
) are important and arise in several different applications. Nevertheless, many computational challenges remain largely open. The analytical issues are quite subtle and the numerical analysis, even at the scheme design level, requires new approaches.

Our focus in the present paper is the design of methods that are consistent with structural properties. We theoretically verify the convergence of the methods by considering two alternative approaches. First, without smoothness assumptions on the minimisers, we show that the
\Gamma
-convergence framework is applicable, considering as a working hypothesis that
W
is convex. Subsequently, we provide error analysis in the case where the solution of the continuous minimisation problem is assumed to be smooth enough. Finally we provide test examples showing that the theoretical predictions are indeed experimentally confirmed.

Our Contributions.

We start in Section
2
by systematically defining the discrete method.
Our approach recovers the standard interior penalty method in the case
of linear elliptic problems. Its final form is given at the energy
level, see (
2.11
), and includes terms involving energies of elementwise gradients and explicit jump terms, as is typical with discontinuous Galerkin methods. A penalty term is included to ensure coercivity and therefore discrete stability. In Section
2
we present other available DG methods for the approximation of (
1.1
).
Notably, these methods either are defined implicitly through lifting operators, or are defined on the first variations and lack a parent approximate energy. Our method fills this gap in the literature, since it is designed elementwise; thus its structure follows the typical paradigm of DG methods and in addition it is defined at the energy level approximating (
1.1
).

Section
3
is devoted to the analysis of the method using the tool of
\Gamma-
convergence in the present DG setting. We show that the discrete energies
\Gamma-
converge to (
1.1
) as the mesh parameter
h
tends to zero. In the proofs we assume that the energy
W
is convex and satisfies certain growth conditions, (
3.2
).
Then we show the convergence of discrete minimisers to the solution of the original problem provided that the penalty terms are appropriately chosen. Towards this goal, equi-coercivity, the
\liminf
and the

\limsup
inequalities are derived for the discrete energy functional. As in other works,
[
BO09
,
DPE10
]
,
related to
\Gamma
-convergence analysis for DG methods, the analysis is challenging due to the fact
that discrete functions are totally discontinuous while the space of continuous minimisers is a
subspace of
H^{1}\,.
See
[
BBN17
,
BNN21
,
GKMR22
]
for recent
\Gamma
-convergence results using conforming or non-conforming finite elements approximating nonlinear fourth order problems.
Although we follow,
[
BO09
,
DPE10
]
, in reconstructing discrete gradients, in key technical compactness and lower semicontinuity arguments, our analysis
introduces new ideas to tackle the problem at hand. As a consequence, we show that the element-wise terms introduced in our formulation, indeed contribute to the consistency and stability of the method, and furthermore, the present analysis reveals the subtle form of the penalty terms which are required for convergence.

Section 4 is devoted to error analysis in the case where the solution of the continuous minimization problem is assumed to be smooth enough. We show that fixed point arguments initially introduced for the dynamic problem and then applied to the stationary Euler-Lagrange equations,
[
Mak93
,
OS07
]
,
are applicable to our scheme. Such arguments yield local existence and uniqueness and corresponding optimal order error estimates for the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations. An interesting new feature of our analysis is that we are able to complete the proofs by assuming only natural Gårding-type inequalities for the stationary problem, thereby extending the analysis of
[
OS07
]
in which the analysis of the elliptic problem was based on strong coercivity assumptions, see Section 4 for details.

We conclude with numerical experiments in Section
5
, showing that the proposed method can lead to reliable predictions of the problem at hand and is in agreement with the theoretical results.

2
Design of the DG method

To motivate the design of the methods we consider first
a smooth stored energy function
W
and assume that
f=0.

We consider the minimisation problem on an appropriate Banach space
X
(typically a Sobolev space) containing functions

u\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\supseteq\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

\displaystyle\text{find a minimiser in ${X}$ of : }

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\,W(\nabla u)\,dx\,.

Temporarily, to avoid problems with boundary conditions, we assume that elements of
{X}
are of the form
Fx+v(x)
where
v\in V,
a subspace of
X
with periodic boundary conditions,
F\in R^{N\times d}
and
\Omega=[0,1]^{d}
.
If such a minimiser exists, then employing the summation convention,

\displaystyle{\langle}D{\mathcal{E}}(y),v{\rangle}=\int_{\Omega}S_{i\alpha}(\nabla y(x))\,\partial_{\alpha}v^{i}(x)\,dx=0,\qquad v\in V\,,

where the Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor is defined through

\displaystyle S_{i\alpha}(\zeta):=\frac{\partial W(\zeta)}{\partial{\zeta_{i\alpha}}}\,.

We will use the notation

\displaystyle S=\partial_{\zeta}\,W\,.

We observe now that

a key structural property

of the above problem is that
affine maps of the form
Fx+b
are critical points of the energy functional.
Indeed,
the energy
\mathcal{E}
satisfies

{\langle}D\mathcal{E}(y_{F}),v{\rangle}=0,\quad y_{F}(x)=Fx\,,

for all
v\in
as the Gauss-Green Theorem implies that

\begin{split}{\langle}D\mathcal{E}(y_{F}),v{\rangle}=\partial_{\zeta}\,W(F)\cdot\int_{\Omega}\nabla vdx&=S(F)\cdot\int_{\Omega}\nabla vdx\\
&=S_{i\alpha}(F)\int_{\partial\Omega}\,n_{\alpha}v^{i}dx=0\,\end{split}

due to periodicity in
X.

We would like our numerical methods to satisfy a discrete analogue of this fundamental property. It is
easy to see that for standard
C^{0}
finite elements the above argument goes through and
y_{F}
is a
critical point of the discrete functional. However, we would like to consider the case of
discontinuous discrete spaces.
Among other reasons, standard finite elements result in a method which might be “too rigid” in certain applications due to the continuity requirement.
The discrete version of the above property was called
patch test consistency
in the early literature of finite elements.
The next observation is crucial when considering discontinuous elements.
Assume that
\Omega
is split in two pieces
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2},
their interface is denoted by
\Gamma
and the test function
v
has a possible
discontinuity at
\Gamma.

Then

\begin{split}{\langle}D\mathcal{E}(y_{F}),v{\rangle}=&\partial_{\zeta}\,W(F)\cdot\int_{\Omega_{1}}\nabla vdx+\partial_{\zeta}\,W(F)\cdot\int_{\Omega_{2}}\nabla vdx\\
&=S(F)\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega_{1}}\nabla vdx+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\nabla vdx\right]\\
&=S_{i\alpha}(F)\left[\ \int_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\,n_{\Omega_{1},\,\alpha}v^{i}dS+\int_{\partial\Omega_{2}}\,n_{\Omega_{1},\,\alpha}v^{i}dS\right]\\
&=S_{i\alpha}(F)\left[\ \int_{\partial\Omega}\,n_{\alpha}v^{i}dS+\int_{\Gamma}\,\Big[n_{\Omega_{1},\,\alpha}v^{i}+n_{\Omega_{2},\,\alpha}v^{i}\Big]dS\right]\\
&=S_{i\alpha}(F)\int_{\Gamma}\,\Big[n_{\Omega_{1},\,\alpha}v^{i}+n_{\Omega_{2},\,\alpha}v^{i}\Big]dS\\
&=S(F)\cdot\int_{\Gamma}\ {[\![v\otimes n]\!]}\,dS.\end{split}

It is clear that the discontinuity of the function
v
induces a violation of the key property
{\langle}D\mathcal{E}(y_{F}),v{\rangle}=0\,.

On the other hand, this calculation motivates a new definition, see
[
MMR14
]
, of the “discrete” functional which we call discontinuous interface coupling on
\Gamma:

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{dg}(y)=&\int_{\Omega_{1}}\,W(\nabla y)dx+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\,W(\nabla y)dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Gamma}\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla y\}\!\!\}})\cdot{[\![y\otimes n]\!]}\,dS\,.\end{split}

The new energy is consistent. Indeed, it is a simple matter to check that

\int_{\Gamma}D\big[S({\{\!\!\{\nabla w\}\!\!\}}){\cdot}{[\![w\otimes n]\!]}\,\big]\,v=\int_{\Gamma}{\Bigl(}\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla w\}\!\!\}})\,{[\![w\otimes n]\!]}{\Bigl)\cdot}{\{\!\!\{\nabla v\}\!\!\}}+S({\{\!\!\{\nabla w\}\!\!\}}){\cdot}{[\![v\otimes n]\!]},

which reduces to
{S(F)\cdot{[\![v\otimes n]\!]}}
when
w=y_{F}.
Repeating then the arguments above we conclude that
{\langle}D\mathcal{E}_{dg}(y_{F}),v{\rangle}=0\,.

2.1
New DG finite element energy minimisation methods.

Next, motivated by the above discussion we shall define the discrete energy functional on appropriate
discontinuous Galerkin spaces. First we introduce the necessary notation, before the DG method is defined. The section concludes with some remarks on its relationship to
other known methods for energy minimisation as well as to classical DG methods for linear problems.

2.1.1
Finite element spaces and discrete gradients

For simplicity, let us assume
that
\Omega
is a polytope domain, and consider a triangulation
T_{h}
of
\Omega
of mesh size
h>0
. Next we will require the partitions of the domain
to be shape regular
[
BS07
]
, i.e., there exists
c>0
such that

\rho_{K}\geq\frac{h_{K}}{c}\text{ for all }K\in T_{h},

where
\rho_{K}
is the diameter of the largest ball inscribed in
K
. To simplify matters, we assume that no hanging nodes are present.
The methods and proofs can be extended to allow hanging nodes under appropriate assumptions.

We seek discontinuous Galerkin approximations to (
3.1
). To this end, we introduce the space of piecewise polynomial functions

V^{q}_{h}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,:\,{\left.\kern-1.2ptv\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K}}\in\mathbb{P}_{q}(K),K\in T_{h}\right\},

(2.2)

where
\mathbb{P}_{q}(K)
denotes the space of polynomial functions on
K
of degree
q\in\mathbb{N}
which is henceforth fixed. Moreover, we adopt the following standard notation:
the
trace
of
functions in
V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
belongs to the space

\displaystyle T(E_{h}):=\Pi_{e\in E_{h}}\mathbb{P}_{q}(e),

(2.3)

where
E_{h}
is the set of mesh edges.
The
average
and
jump
operators over

T(E_{h})
are defined by:

\displaystyle\{\kern-3.125pt\{\cdot\}\kern-3.125pt\}\colon

\displaystyle T(E_{h})\mapsto L^{p}(E_{h})

(2.4)

\displaystyle\{\kern-3.125pt\{w\}\kern-3.125pt\}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}({\left.\kern-1.2ptw\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{+}}}}+{\left.\kern-1.2ptw\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{-}}}}),&\quad\text{for }e\in E_{h}^{i}\\
w,&\quad\text{for }e\in E_{h}^{b},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket\colon

\displaystyle T(E_{h})\mapsto L^{p}(E_{h})

(2.5)

\displaystyle\llbracket v\rrbracket:={\left.\kern-1.2ptv\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{+}}}}-{\left.\kern-1.2ptv\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{-}}}},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }e\in E_{h}^{i}.

We often apply the jump operator on dyadic products
v\otimes n
, for
v\colon\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
n\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and we write

\displaystyle\llbracket v\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket:={\left.\kern-1.2ptv\otimes n_{e^{+}}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{+}}}}+{\left.\kern-1.2ptv\otimes n_{e^{-}}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K_{e^{-}}}},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }e\in E_{h}^{i}

(2.6)

where
K_{e^{+}}
,
K_{e^{-}}
are the elements that share the internal edge
e
with corresponding outward pointing normals

n_{e^{+}},n_{e^{-}}
, and
E_{h}^{i}
,
E_{h}^{b}
denote respectively the set of internal and boundary edges.

The space
V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
is equipped with the norm

\|v\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}=\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+|v|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})},

where the seminorm
|\cdot|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}
is defined by

|v|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}:=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla v|^{p}+\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket v\rrbracket|^{p}.

(2.7)

We note that in the above expression
\nabla v
denotes the gradient, in the classical sense, of the polynomial function
v
over the set
K
. As a function in
SBV(\Omega)
, i.e. in the class of special functions of bounded variation
[
AFP00
]
,

v\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
, admits a distributional gradient which is a Radon measure without a Cantor part and decomposes as

\displaystyle Dv=\nabla v\mathcal{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Omega+\llbracket v\rrbracket\otimes n_{e}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E_{h}

(2.8)

where
\nabla v
denotes the approximate gradient. To avoid confusion, we denote the above approximate gradient by
\nabla_{h}v
, i.e. we write
\nabla_{h}:V^{k}_{h}\to V^{k-1}_{h}
for the operator defined by

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(\nabla_{h}v\right)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K}}=\nabla({\left.\kern-1.2ptv\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{K}}).

In particular, it holds that

\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}W(\nabla u_{h}).

A crucial role in the analysis is played by the lifting operator defined below.

Definition 2.1
.

The (global) lifting operator
R_{h}:T(E_{h})\to V^{q-1}_{h}(\Omega)
is defined by its action on elements of
V^{q-1}_{h}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(\varphi):w_{h}\,dx=\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{w_{h}\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket\varphi\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds,\quad\forall w_{h}\in V^{q-1}_{h}(\Omega).

(2.9)

Through the lifting operator we may also define the discrete gradient
G_{h}
for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
as

\displaystyle G_{h}(u_{h})=\nabla_{h}u_{h}-R_{h}(u_{h}).

(2.10)

In general, the lifting operator maps into polynomials of degree
l\geq 0
,
[
DPE10
, Section 2.3]
. For convenience we choose
l=k-1
although this is not essential.

2.1.2
Discrete energy functionals

Motivated by the discussion in the beginning of this section, one is led to define the discrete energy over totally discontinuous finite element spaces through

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{dg}[u_{h}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h}(x))

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\cdot{[\![u_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds+\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}}),

where as is typical with discontinuous Galerkin formulations
\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}})
is an appropriate penalty term, i.e., a functional which penalises the jumps
{[\![u_{h}]\!]}
and it will be specified in the sequel.

Consider now
\mathrm{P}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to V^{q-1}_{h}
the standard (local)
L^{2}
projection so that for
V\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{P}V\right):w=\int_{\Omega}V:w,\quad\forall\,w\in V^{q-1}_{h}.

In particular, since
\nabla_{h}u_{h}
is in the finite-dimensional space, it holds that
DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
We can consider a slight variation of the energy (
2.11
), including the projection operator on the interface terms,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}[u_{h}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h}(x))

(2.12)

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ {\{\!\!\{\mathrm{P}\,S(\nabla u_{h})\}\!\!\}}\cdot{[\![u_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds+\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}}).

The energy (
2.12
) is still consistent in the sense discussed in the beginning of
Section
2
since
{\{\!\!\{\mathrm{P}\,S(F)\}\!\!\}}=S({\{\!\!\{F\}\!\!\}})=S(F\,)
given that
F
is constant. For piecewise linear elements (
q=1
) there holds
\mathrm{P}\,S(\nabla u_{h})=S(\nabla u_{h})\,.
In addition, for high-order elements, notice that
\mathrm{P}v
can be computed elementwise due to the discontinuity of the functions of
V^{q-1}_{h}
across elements. Thus the increase in the computational costs
of (
2.12
) compared to (
2.11
) is minimal.

2.1.3
Relationship to known discrete functionals

The following functional can be defined on
V^{q}_{h}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{lo}[u_{h}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h}(x)-R_{h}(u_{h}))+\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}})

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))+\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}}).

This consists a natural choice, given that

is considered as the discrete gradient.
This method was introduced by Ten Eyck and Lew (2006)
[
TEL06
]
and further analysed in

[
OS07
]
,
[
BO09
]
.
Although lifting operators are a typical analysis tool for DG methods, in the present context, the evaluation of this functional requires the
computation of
R_{h}(u_{h})
(and thus of
G_{h}(u_{h})
) which appears implicitly in

Computationally this reduces to the solution of local problems only, and it is not expensive.
Early contributions on the relevance of DG methods for nonlinear variational problems include

[
GP99
]
. In this work the discrete functional involved only jump terms on the interface and thus for quadratic potentials the discrete energy is not consistent with
typical DG formulations.
It is interesting that functionals such as (

), (

) including typical consistent discontinuous Galerkin terms in explicit form and being at the same time appropriate for nonlinear energy minimisation problems were not available up to now.

3
Convergence of Discrete Minimizers

For
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a bounded, polytope domain, consider the minimisation problem

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u(x))\,dx,\quad u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\,\,u=u_{0},\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

(3.1)

We assume throughout that
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is of class
C^{1}
and satisfies

-1+|\xi|^{p}\lesssim W(\xi)\lesssim 1+|\xi|^{p},\quad p>1.

(3.2)

Hence, we naturally pose the problem of minimising (
3.1
) over the
set of admissible functions

\mathbb{A}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{N}\,:\,u=u_{0}\,\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mbox{ a.e. on $\partial\Omega$}\right\}.

Given (
3.2
), it is known that (
3.1
) admits minimisers of class
W^{1,p}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
as long as
W
is quasiconvex.

Employing the aforementioned ideas for the discretization of energy functionals in the DG setting, see eq. (
2.12
), and applying Dirichlet boundary conditions through Nitsche’s
approach, for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
our discrete energy functional has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h})dx-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{{\mathrm{P}}DW(\nabla u_{h})\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}),

where
\alpha
will be chosen large enough and the penalty
{\rm Pen}(u_{h})
is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle:=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(3.4)

where for the boundary faces we use the notation

\displaystyle\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket=u_{h}^{-}-u_{0},\text{ for }e\in E_{h}^{b}=E_{h}\cap\partial\Omega,

(3.5)

and
u_{0}
are the boundary conditions encoded in the continuous function space
\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
of eq. (
3
).
The above penalty term seems complicated but it is an appropriate
L^{p}
modification of the typical
L^{2}
penalty

\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}.

As it will be evident from the proofs, by assuming that
W\in C^{2}
and
by imposing further restrictions on the growth of its derivatives, one may also choose
the penalty term as

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle:=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

(3.6)

which reduces precisely to the standard
L^{2}
penalty term for
p=2
.
We note that the above penalty terms are required to absorb terms in the proofs of Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
which appear due to the nonlinearity of
W
and cannot be
handled by the typical
L^{2}
penalty term.

Recall that
DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Also, as
R_{h}(u_{h})\in V^{q-1}_{h}
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):\mathrm{P}DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{{\mathrm{P}}DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{{\mathrm{P}}DW(\nabla u_{h})\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds.

Hence, we can re-write (
3.3
) in the compact form

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\left[W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})\right]dx+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

(3.7)

3.1
Preliminary results.

Previously we have defined the lifting operator
R_{h}
in eq (
2.9
).
In particular, for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
we have that

\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):w_{h}\,dx=\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{w_{h}\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds.

Moreover, we note the following lemma,
[
BO09
]
, providing an estimate of
\|R_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}}
in terms of the
W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})
seminorm.

Lemma 3.1

(Bound on global lifting operator)
.

For all
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\leq C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p},

(3.8)

where the constant
C_{R}
is independent of
h
.

So far, we have defined
\nabla_{h}
as the approximate gradient of the
distributional gradient, eq. (
2.8
). Now the relation of the discrete
gradient
G_{h}(u_{h})
, eq. (
2.10
), to the distributional
gradient of an SBV function is revealed by the following: for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
,

\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
and
\Omega=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
, we
compute

\displaystyle\langle Du_{h},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\cdot{\rm div}\,\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{1}}\nabla u_{h}:\varphi+\int_{K_{1}}\nabla u_{h}:\varphi-\int_{e}\varphi:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varphi:\nabla u_{h}-\int_{e}\varphi:\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla u_{h}\mathcal{L}^{d}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Omega+\llbracket u_{h}\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}e,\varphi\rangle.

That is,
G_{h}(u_{h})
acts on
\varphi\in V^{q-1}_{h}(\Omega)
in the same way that the Radon measure
Du_{h}
acts on
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
and
G_{h}(u_{h})
can be interpreted as a discrete version of the distributional gradient of the
SBV
function
u_{h}
. Hence, the following lemma comes as no surprise, where eq. (
3.8
) is employed for the proof,
[
DPE10
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
it holds that

\|G_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

Moreover, whenever
u_{h}\rightarrow u
in

L^{p}(\Omega)
and
|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}G_{h}(u_{h}):\varphi=-\int_{\Omega}u\cdot{\rm div}\,\varphi,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(3.9)

In particular, if
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, it holds that

G_{h}(u_{h})\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega).

Next Poincaré type inequalities for DG spaces are presented. Similar results have been shown in
[
BO09
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Poincaré inequality for DG spaces.)
.

Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}(\Omega)
and
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
with
|\Gamma|>0
, then for all
h\leq 1
it holds that

\displaystyle\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

(3.10)

Proof.

Let
w_{h}
be the approximation of
u_{h}
from the continuous finite element space

V_{h}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega)
through the reconstruction operator
Q_{h}:V_{h}\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, i.e.
w_{h}=Q_{h}u_{h}
,
defined in
[
BO09
, Section 3.2]
. The
following inequality results from the local estimates given in
[
BO09
, Theorem 3.1]
:

\displaystyle\lVert u_{h}-w_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}{(\Omega)}}+\lVert u_{h}-w_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}{(\Gamma)}}+\lVert\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}{(\Omega)}}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

(3.11)

Standard Poincaré inequalities
for functions in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
[
Zie89
]
,
together with inequality (
3.11
) give the bound

\displaystyle\lVert w_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla w_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\left|\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\int_{\Gamma}w_{h}ds\right|^{p}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}

\displaystyle+\lVert w_{h}-u_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}+\lVert u_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}+\lVert u_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}

Then, it is straightforward to deduce inequality (
3.10
) again with the help of eq. (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\lVert u_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert u_{h}-w_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert w_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}+\lVert u_{h}\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}.

∎

3.2
\Gamma
-convergence for convex energies

We may now state our main result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be convex and of class
C^{1}
, satisfying the coercivity and growth condition
(
3.2
). Suppose that the penalty parameter
\alpha
from (
3.3
) is large
enough and
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
is a sequence of minimisers of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, eq. (
3.3
), i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\min_{V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}_{h}.

Then there exists
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and

\mathcal{E}(u)=\min_{\mathbb{A}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is a direct consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{h}

\Gamma
-converges to
\mathcal{E}
with respect to the strong topology of
L^{p}(\Omega)
. The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence result. We start by proving compactness in an appropriate topology for sequences of bounded energy.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
it holds that

\|u_{h}\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\lesssim 1+\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h}).

In particular, if

\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C

there exists
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and

G_{h}(u_{h})\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega).

Proof.

For
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)

and using either penalty term (
3.4
) or (
3.6
) we first estimate the second term in the discrete energy (
3.7
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}C\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(2^{\frac{1}{p-1}}C\int_{\Omega}1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

by Lemma
3.1
and the growth of
W
which, combined with convexity, implies that

|DW(\xi)|\lesssim 1+|\xi|^{p-1}.

(3.12)

The above inequality follows from
[
Dac07
, Proposition 2.32]
.
Hence, by Young’s inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}C_{R}\delta^{1-p}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\frac{p-1}{p}2^{\frac{1}{p-1}}C\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right).

(3.13)

The coercivity assumption on
W
says that

W(\xi)\geq-C_{0}+C_{1}|\xi|^{p}.

Thus, choosing
\delta^{-1}=\frac{2}{C_{1}}\frac{p-1}{p}2^{\frac{1}{p-1}}C
, by (
3.2
) we find that

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}-C-C(p,R)\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

where

C(p,R)=\frac{1}{p}C_{R}\delta^{1-p}=\frac{1}{p}C_{R}\left(\frac{2}{C_{1}}\frac{p-1}{p}2^{\frac{1}{p-1}}C\right)^{p-1}.

Next, note that since

|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\geq\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

(3.14)

the penalty term in (
3.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that the conjugate exponents
(p-1)/p
and
1/p
add up to
1
. Then, provided that
\alpha>C(p,R)
, we deduce that

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}-C+(\alpha-C(p,R))\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

and hence that

|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\lesssim 1+\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h}).

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C
,
||u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}
is uniformly bounded which implies from the Poincaré inequality (
3.10
) that
||u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime}
.
From compact embeddings for broken Sobolev spaces,
[
DPE10
, Theorem 6.1]
, together with Lemma
3.2
, we infer Lemma
3.4
. In particular, we find that

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }G_{h}(u_{h})\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega),

for some
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. To prove
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
note that

||u-u_{0}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq||u_{h}-u_{0}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+||u-u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

As
h\rightarrow 0
the first term of the right hand side converges to zero due to the assumption that
\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C
and

||u-u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
from the compact embedding in
[
BO09
, Lemma 8]
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Regarding the penalty term (
3.6
), provided that
p>2
and using (
3.14
), we find that

|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}=\left(|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\geq\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Hence, the penalty term in (
3.6
) satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

due to the conjugacy of the exponents and Lemma
3.4
also follows in this case.

We next prove the
\liminf
-inequality, providing a lower bound for the sequence
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
W
is convex,
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
and
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
satisfy
u_{h}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then,

\liminf_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\geq\mathcal{E}(u).

Proof.

Given
u_{h}
,
u
as in the statement, suppose that
\liminf\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C
as otherwise there is nothing to prove. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the limit
is attained and that

\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C.

By Lemma
3.4
we may also assume that
\|u_{h}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq C
and that

G_{h}(u_{h})\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega).

(3.15)

Note that since
W
is convex and
G_{h}(u_{h})\rightharpoonup\nabla u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))\geq\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u)=\mathcal{E}(u).

(3.16)

We thus add and subtract the term

\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))

in the discrete energy (
3.7
) to find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))+\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-W(G_{h}(u_{h}))

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=:I_{h}+II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

By (
3.16
) it suffices to show that

\liminf_{h\to 0}\left[II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})\right]\geq 0.

(3.17)

We begin by estimating
III_{h}
. By Hölder’s inequality we deduce that

|III_{h}|\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

By invoking the growth of
DW
in (
3.12
) we infer that

\displaystyle|III_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}|1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p-1}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle=C\|R_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(1+\|\nabla_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left(C_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(1+\|\nabla_{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.1
. Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle|III_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left(C_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(1+|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\,C\,\left(C_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

Next, we estimate the term
II_{h}
. Note that the convexity and growth of
W
, see

[
Dac07
, Proposition 2.32]
, imply the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|W(\xi_{1})-W(\xi_{2})|\leq C\left(1+|\xi_{1}|^{p-1}+|\xi_{2}|^{p-1}\right)|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

(3.19)

Therefore, by Hölder’s inequality, the relation
R_{h}(u_{h})=\nabla_{h}u_{h}-G_{h}(u_{h})
, and Lemma
3.1
we find that

\displaystyle|II_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p-1}+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p-1}\right)|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-G_{h}(u_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p-1}+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\|R_{h}(u_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\,C_{R}\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By Lemma
3.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|II_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq C\,C_{R}\left(1+|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,C\,C_{R}{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

Using (
3.18
) and (
3.20
), we get that

II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})\geq\left(\alpha-2CC_{R}\right){\rm Pen}(u_{h})

and choosing
\alpha>2CC_{R}
we infer (
3.17
) which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If instead we assume the penalty term (
3.6
)
we can prove Lemma
3.5
with the additional assumption that
W\in C^{2}
and

|D^{2}W(\xi)|\lesssim 1+|\xi|^{p-2},\text{ }p\geq 2.

Note this is a genuine assumption and not a consequence of convexity, see Appendix C in
[
KLST20
]
for
an example.
For the proof re-write the discrete energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-W(G_{h}(u_{h}))-DW(G_{h}(u_{h})):R_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(DW(G_{h}(u_{h})-DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h}))\right):R_{h}(u_{h})+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=:I_{h}+II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

As before the convexity of
W
implies that

\liminf_{h\to 0}I_{h}=\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))\geq\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u)=\mathcal{E}(u).

(3.21)

Moreover, using the convexity of
W
again, we have that

W(\xi_{1})-W(\xi_{2})-DW(\xi_{2}):(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\geq 0,

and recalling that
R_{h}(u_{h})=\nabla_{h}u_{h}-G_{h}(u_{h})
, we deduce that

II_{h}=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-W(G_{h}(u_{h}))-DW(G_{h}(u_{h})):R_{h}(u_{h})\geq 0.

(3.22)

As for the term
\left(DW(G_{h}(u_{h})-DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h}))\right):R_{h}(u_{h})
, we absorb it into the penalty term. Indeed, by assuming a
(p-2)
-growth on
D^{2}W
, we infer that

|DW(\xi_{1})-DW(\xi_{2})|\leq C\left(1+|\xi_{1}|^{p-2}+|\xi_{2}|^{p-2}\right)|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|

and, since
R_{h}(u_{h})=\nabla_{h}u_{h}-G_{h}(u_{h})
, that

\displaystyle|III_{h}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p-2}+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p-2}\right)|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

However, by Lemma
3.2
, we find that

\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right)\lesssim 1+|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}

whereas, by Lemma
3.1
, that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\leq C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}.

Thus,

|III_{h}|\lesssim\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)\left(\sum_{e}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}={\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

(3.23)

Combining (
3.16
)–(
3.23
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{E}(u)+(\alpha-C){\rm Pen}(u_{h})\geq\mathcal{E}(u),

for
\alpha>0
large enough.

The remaining ingredient in the proof of Theorem
3.1
is providing a recovery sequence which is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
if
q\geq 1
and
h<1
there exists
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
such that
u_{h}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq\mathcal{E}(u).

Proof.

Here we show that for

a recovery sequence

\displaystyle||u-u_{h}||^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\sum_{e\in E^{b}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|u_{h}-u_{0}|^{p}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0

(3.24)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\mathcal{E}(u).

(3.25)

First note that

and one can create a sequence

with the properties

\displaystyle||u-u_{\delta}||_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\delta\quad\text{and}\quad|u_{\delta}|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta}|u|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

(3.26)

Choosing the sequence

, where

we recall the error estimates for all

\displaystyle|u_{\delta}-u_{h,\delta}|_{W^{i,p}(K)}\lesssim h_{K}^{2-i}|u_{\delta}|_{W^{2,p}(K)},\text{ }i=0,1,2,

(3.27)

\displaystyle|u_{\delta}-u_{h,\delta}|_{L^{p}(e)}\lesssim h_{K}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}|u_{\delta}|_{W^{2,p}(K)}.

(3.28)

In the following, assuming that

, the above error estimates are combined with eq. (

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in T_{h}}|u_{\delta}-u_{h,\delta}|_{W^{1,p}(K)}^{p}\lesssim h^{p}|u_{\delta}|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\lesssim\frac{h^{p}}{\delta^{p}}|u|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p},

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}||\llbracket u_{\delta}-u_{h,\delta}\rrbracket||_{L^{p}(e)}^{p}\lesssim h^{p}\sum_{e\in E_{h}}|u_{\delta}|^{p}_{W^{2,p}(K_{e})}\lesssim\frac{h^{p}}{\delta^{p}}|u|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p},

(3.30)

here

. For the boundary terms applying

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E^{b}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|u_{h,\delta}-u_{0}|^{p}\lesssim\sum_{e\in E^{b}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}\left(|u_{h,\delta}-u_{\delta}|^{p}+|u_{\delta}-u|^{p}\right)

(3.31)

For
\beta\in(\frac{p-1}{p},1)

\delta=h^{\beta}

)

as
h\rightarrow 0
. Here
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
(

is continuous by construction), which means
\llbracket u\rrbracket=0
a.e. on every

and as

\displaystyle\lVert u-u_{h,\delta}\rVert^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq\lVert u-u_{\delta}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\lVert u_{\delta}-u_{h,\delta}\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}\rightarrow 0.

(3.32)

Therefore, we found a sequence in

such that eq. (
3.24
) holds.
Next for the convergence of the discrete functional,

3.25

G_{h}(u_{h})\to\nabla u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega).

(3.33)

Indeed, recalling that

,
\llbracket u\rrbracket=0
a.e. and using Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|G_{h}(u_{h})-\nabla u|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-\nabla u|^{p}+\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-\nabla u|^{p}+\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K}\int_{K}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-\nabla u|^{p}+\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}-u\rrbracket|^{p}

\displaystyle=|u_{h}-u|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\to 0.

As in Lemma
3.5
, we can write the discrete energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))+\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-W(G_{h}(u_{h}))

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(u_{h}):DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h})+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=:I_{h}+II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

Remark 3.3
.

If instead we assume the penalty term (

), we re-write the discrete energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}u_{h})-W(G_{h}(u_{h}))-DW(G_{h}(u_{h})):R_{h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(DW(G_{h}(u_{h})-DW(\nabla_{h}u_{h}))\right):R_{h}(u_{h})+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=:I_{h}+II_{h}+III_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

As before

I_{h}=\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))\to\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u)=\mathcal{E}(u)

(3.35)

and it remains to show that

,

, and

vanish in the limit

. For

, the
(p-2)

\displaystyle|II_{h}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)D^{2}W(G_{h}(u_{h})+tR_{h}(u_{h}))R_{h}(u_{h}):R_{h}(u_{h})\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}+|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}

3.5

3.2
and Lemma

Then, since by Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}\leq C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p},

we deduce that

\displaystyle|II_{h}|\lesssim\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)\left(\sum_{ei}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\lesssim{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

For the term

We conclude by demonstrating the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose that
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
is given such that

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\inf_{V^{q}_{h}}\mathcal{E}_{h}.

Note that for any
v\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
, by Lemma
3.6
we can find a sequence
v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
such that

v_{h}\to v\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and
\limsup\mathcal{E}_{h}(v_{h})\leq\mathcal{E}(v)
. In particular, up to a subsequence,

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(v_{h})\leq\limsup\mathcal{E}_{h}(v_{h})\leq\mathcal{E}(v)<\infty.

Hence, by Lemma
3.4
, there exists
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
such that

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and, for any
v\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{E}(u)\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq\limsup\mathcal{E}_{h}(v_{h})\leq\mathcal{E}(v),

where
v_{h}
is the recovery sequence for

whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma
3.6
. That is,

\mathcal{E}(u)=\inf_{\mathbb{A}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}

completing the proof.

4
A priori estimates

In this section we shall focus on a priori error estimates between the exact solution
u
and the solution
u_{h}
of the discrete
Euler-Lagrange equations
.
Under certain assumptions on
u
and on the energy
W
we will show that
u_{h}
exists and satisfies optimal-order a priori error bounds. To this end, we introduce some further
notation. As pointed out in (
2.14
) the
first variation of (
2.11
) is

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}):=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\langle}D\mathcal{E}_{dg}[u_{h}],v_{h}{\rangle}=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}S(\nabla u_{h}(x))\nabla v_{h}(x)

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\cdot{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ \Bigl(\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\,{[\![u_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}{\Bigl)\cdot}{\{\!\!\{\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}}ds

\displaystyle+{\langle}D\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}}),v_{h}{\rangle}.

Notice that critical points
u_{h}
of the Euler-Lagrange equations satisfy

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})={\langle}f,v_{h}{\rangle}\qquad\text{for all }v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)\,.

(4.2)

The minimiser of the continuous problem will satisfy (employing the summation convention),

\displaystyle a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}S_{i\alpha}(\nabla y(x))\,\partial_{\alpha}v^{i}(x)\,dx={\langle}f,v{\rangle}\qquad\text{for all }v\in V\,.

(4.3)

We shall need the derivative of the Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor
S=\partial_{\zeta}\,W=\{A_{i\alpha j\beta}\}
defined by

\displaystyle A_{i\alpha j\beta}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\zeta_{j\beta}}}S_{i\alpha}(\zeta)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{\zeta_{i\alpha}}\partial{\zeta_{j\beta}}}W(\zeta)\,.

(4.4)

Dealing with smooth solutions of the dynamic and the static problem,

[
DH85
,
Mak93
,
OS07
]
, a coercivity assumption on
A_{i\alpha j\beta}
was instrumental:

\sum_{i,\alpha,j,\beta}A_{i\alpha j\beta}(\eta)\zeta_{\alpha}\zeta_{\beta}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq M_{1}|\zeta|^{2}|\xi|^{2}\,,\qquad\text{for }M_{1}>0\,,

(4.5)

and for all
\zeta,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,.
In addition
A_{i\alpha j\beta}
is assumed to be symmetric. Hypothesis (
4.5
) allows the inclusion of
physically relevant energies,
[
DH85
]
, and it will be assumed in the sequel. It implies a Gårding type inequality

\sum_{i,\alpha,j,\beta}\int_{\Omega}A_{i\alpha j\beta}(\nabla u(x))\,\partial_{\beta}v^{j}(x)\partial_{\alpha}v^{i}(x)\,dx\geq M_{0}(u)\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}-\mu_{0}(u)\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(4.6)

which is the key assumption in our analysis.

4.1
Error Analysis

In this section we perform the convergence analysis of our discontinuous Galerkin finite scheme for the penalty term (
3.6
) whenever

. The general case

depends on generalised Gårding inequalities, requires more delicate analysis, and is beyond the scope of this paper; see Remark
4.1

As mentioned, we follow the general plan introduced in
[
Mak93
,
OS07
]
considering appropriate linearisation problems and fixed point arguments. Note that, in comparison to form (
4.1
), the method considered in
[
OS07
]
was similar but did not include the term involving
\partial_{\zeta}S.
As a result, the additional technical aspects in the analysis are minor, as discussed below, and can be handled using standard estimates.
In fact, one can show by following the arguments of
[
OS07
]
that the linearised form satisfies a discrete Gårding-type inequality, see
[
OS07
, Lemma 4.1]
. We build on this result, (which implies (
4.18
)), and we show
the discrete inf-sup condition Lemma 4.1 without additional assumptions.
[
OS07
, Lemma 4.2, Lemma 4.3]
are used as well. Lemma 4.1. provides what is needed to complete the fixed point argument, see below for details. The result of
[
OS07
]
in the stationary case assumes a stronger coercivity, ((
4.6
) with
m_{0}=0
), see
[
OS07
, Section 5, p.1382]
. We briefly describe the main steps of the analysis and we provide details of the proof of the Lemma 4.1 below.

Remark 4.1
.

The starting point of the
L^{2}-
analysis considered herein, as well as in the previous works,
[
OS07
]
, is the Gårding inequality (
4.6
). Generalising (
4.6
) to
L^{p}
setting is not straightforward, see e.g.
[
KV21
]
and its references. The form of the Gårding inequality might depend on quantities
of the form
\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

It is possible, although technically more demanding, to extend our analysis under the validity of such assumptions. This will be the subject of a forthcoming work.

Let
W_{h}=I_{h}u
be an appropriate conforming finite element projection of
u
(which is assumed to be continuous and satisfies typical optimal order error bounds).
Then, the consistency error is defined as

\displaystyle a_{h}(W_{h},v_{h})={\langle}f,v_{h}{\rangle}+{\langle}R_{h},v_{h}{\rangle}

(4.7)

where
{\langle}R_{h},v_{h}{\rangle}
is the consistency error satisfying optimal order error bounds.
Then,

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})-a_{h}(W_{h},v_{h})=-{\langle}R_{h},v_{h}{\rangle}\,.

(4.8)

Next we shall evaluate the left hand side of this equation:

\displaystyle a_{h}(W_{h},

\displaystyle v_{h})-a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}(S(\nabla W_{h}(x))-S(\nabla u_{h}(x)))\nabla v_{h}(x)

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\Big(\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla W_{h}\}\!\!\}})-\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\Big)\cdot{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ \Bigl((\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla W_{h}\}\!\!\}}-\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\,{[\![W_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}{\Bigl)\cdot}{\{\!\!\{\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}}ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ \Bigl(\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\,{[\![(W_{h}-u_{h})\otimes n_{e}]\!]}{\Bigl)\cdot}{\{\!\!\{\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}}ds

\displaystyle+{\langle}D\text{Pen}\,({W_{h}-u_{h}}),v_{h}{\rangle}.

Since
W_{h}
is continuous, the third term on the right hand side vanishes. Thus, we conclude,

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{h};u_{h}-W_{h},v_{h})=-{\langle}R_{h},v_{h}{\rangle}\,,

(4.9)

where we have set,

\displaystyle\overline{A}(\nabla w\ ;\ \nabla u)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\zeta}S(\nabla w+\tau(\nabla u-\nabla w))d\tau\,,

(4.10)

and

\displaystyle A_{h}(\varphi\ ;

\displaystyle w_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}\overline{A}(\nabla W_{h}\ ;\ \nabla\varphi)\nabla w_{h}(x)\nabla v_{h}(x)

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\Big(\overline{A}({\{\!\!\{\nabla W_{h}\}\!\!\}}\ ;\ {\{\!\!\{\nabla\varphi\}\!\!\}}){\{\!\!\{\nabla(w_{h})\}\!\!\}})\Big)\cdot{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ \Bigl(\partial_{\zeta}S({\{\!\!\{\nabla\varphi\}\!\!\}})\,{[\![w_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}{\Bigl)\cdot}{\{\!\!\{\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}}ds

\displaystyle+{\langle}D\text{Pen}\,({w_{h}}),v_{h}{\rangle}.

Therefore for fixed
\varphi,

is a bilinear form (with respect to
w_{h},v_{h}
). This form is closely related to the standard symmetric interior penalty DG
bilinear form. To derive the estimates one needs to define a set

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{\delta}(u)=\{\Phi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})\ :\|\Phi-\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}<\delta\,\}\,,

(4.11)

where
u
is the (assumed smooth) minimiser of the continuous problem and

is a fixed number which guarantees that
\Phi\in\mathbf{Z}_{\delta}(u)
satisfy
\Phi(x)\in\mathcal{O}.

Without being very precise, if a function
\phi\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
and its piecewise derivative is in
\mathbf{Z}_{\delta}(u)
then the coercivity of the principal part of
A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})

will follow in view of (

) and of the fact

will be close to
\nabla u

\displaystyle\text{for $h<h_{0},$ if
$\Phi\in\mathbf{Z}_{\delta}(W_{h})$ then $\Phi(x)\in\mathcal{O}.$}

(4.12)

Next, define the set
\mathcal{J}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\{v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)\ :

\displaystyle\|v_{h}-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq C_{\star}(u)h^{r}

\displaystyle\text{ and in piecewise sense }\nabla v_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\delta}(W_{h})\,\}\,,

where we use the notation

The next result is essential. The assumption that
\Omega
is convex can be relaxed:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\Omega

\displaystyle\sup_{v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)}\frac{A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}\geq\gamma_{0}\,\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}

Proof.

We adopt in our setting an original idea of
[

]

[

]

We first consider the auxiliary bilinear forms

\displaystyle D_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\Big(\partial_{\zeta}S(\nabla u)){\{\!\!\{\nabla(w_{h})\}\!\!\}})\Big)\cdot{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}ds

\displaystyle A_{h}

\displaystyle(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})=A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},w_{h})+A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},P_{D}z)

\displaystyle+A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)-A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)+A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)-\mu_{1}{\langle}w_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}

\displaystyle=A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},w_{h})+\mu_{1}{\langle}w_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}+A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h}-\overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)

\displaystyle+\Big[A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)-A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)\Big]+A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},z)-\mu_{1}{\langle}w_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}

\displaystyle+\mu_{1}{\langle}\overline{w}_{h},z-P_{D}z{\rangle}

\displaystyle=\left[A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},w_{h})+\mu_{1}{\langle}w_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}+A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h}-\overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)\right]

\displaystyle+\Big[A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)-A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)\Big]+\Big[A(u\ ;\ \overline{w}_{h},z)-\mu_{1}{\langle}\overline{w}_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}\Big]

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}{\langle}w_{h}-\overline{w}_{h},w_{h}{\rangle}+\mu_{1}{\langle}\overline{w}_{h},z-P_{D}z{\rangle}\,=:T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}+T_{5}.

Select now
z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as the solution of

\displaystyle A(u\ ;\ v,z)=\mu_{1}{\langle}v,w_{h}{\rangle}\qquad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\,,

(4.16)

and then
T_{3}=0
. We claim that

\displaystyle A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})\geq C_{0}\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{2}.

(4.17)

Indeed, following the arguments of
[
OS07
, Lemma 4.1]
we can show that

\begin{split}A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},w_{h})\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon^{2})\sum_{K}&\int_{K}|\nabla w_{h}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}\\
&+\alpha\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}-\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

(4.18)

Additionally, (compare to
[
OS07
, Lemma 4.2]
) we have

\displaystyle A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h}-\overline{w}_{h},P_{D}z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{h}-\overline{w_{h}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\|P_{D}z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{h}-\overline{w_{h}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|w_{h}-\overline{w_{h}}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}.

Next, concerning the second term
T_{2}
, we use
[
OS07
, Lemma 4.3]
to get that

\displaystyle-T_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\varphi-\nabla u\|_{\infty}\|\overline{w_{h}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|P_{D}z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\delta\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

For the last two terms,
T_{4}
and
T_{5},
we have that

\displaystyle\big|\mu_{1}\langle w_{h}-\overline{w_{h}},w_{h}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{1}^{2}\|w_{h}-\overline{w_{h}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}+\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\big|\mu_{1}{\langle}\overline{w}_{h},

\displaystyle z-P_{D}z{\rangle}\big|\lesssim\|\overline{w_{h}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mu_{1}^{2}\|z-P_{D}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2}\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}+h\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+h^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{2}\,.

Combining all the above we finally conclude that

\displaystyle A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon^{2})\sum_{K}\int_{K}|\nabla w_{h}|^{2}+(\alpha-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-h^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}-h)\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\int_{e}|\llbracket w_{h}\rrbracket|^{2}

\displaystyle+(\mu_{1}-1-h)\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-(\delta+h^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{2}.

Choose now for example
\mu_{1}>1,\,\varepsilon<1
and
\alpha>1/\varepsilon^{2}
and then

\displaystyle A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ w_{h},v_{h})\gtrsim(1-\delta-h^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{2},

and by choosing
\delta<1/2-\varepsilon^{2}
(for a possibly smaller
\varepsilon
) and
h
small enough we prove our claim.
We recall here that by using standard properties of conforming finite elements and the elliptic regularity of (
4.16
) we know that

\displaystyle\|z-P_{D}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq Ch^{2}\|z\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{2}\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{2}\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\,.

Furthermore, we have,

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\|P_{D}z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\|w_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

Combining the above bounds we complete the proof by assuming that
h\leq h_{1},
for an appropriate
h_{1}>0.

∎

Next, following
[
Mak93
,
OS07
]
, we show that the map
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{J}\to V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle A_{h}(\phi;\mathcal{N}(\phi)-W_{h},v_{h})=A_{h}(u;u-W_{h},v_{h}),

(4.19)

has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{J}.
To this aim, we first prove that
\mathcal{N}
maps
\mathcal{J}
into itself. For
v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
, by continuity of the bilinear form, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ \mathcal{N}(\phi)-W_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}=\frac{A_{h}(u\ ;\ u-W_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}\lesssim\frac{\|u-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}},

and so by taking the supremum for all
v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
and applying Lemma
4.1
we infer that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\phi)-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\lesssim\|u-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq C(u)h^{r},

where in the last inequality we used the properties of the approximation operator
I_{h}
. Hence
\mathcal{N}(\phi)\in\mathcal{J}
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{N}
is a contraction. Indeed, for
\phi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{J}
we have that

\displaystyle A_{h}(\phi;\mathcal{N}(\phi)-W_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=A_{h}(u;u-W_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle A_{h}(\psi;\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=A_{h}(u;u-W_{h},v_{h}),

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{q}(\Omega)
. By subtracting the second from the first line we have that

\displaystyle A_{h}(\phi;\mathcal{N}(\phi)-\mathcal{N}(\psi),v_{h})=A_{h}(\psi;\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h},v_{h})-A_{h}(\phi;\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h},v_{h}),

hence we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{A_{h}(\varphi\ ;\ \mathcal{N}(\phi)-\mathcal{N}(\psi),v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{h}(\psi;\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h},v_{h})-A_{h}(\phi;\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\phi-\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

Taking again the supremum over
v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
and applying Lemma
4.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}(\psi)-\mathcal{N}(\psi)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\phi-\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{N}(\psi)-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\ast}(u)h^{r}\|\nabla\phi-\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c(h)\|\phi-\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})},

where we used the fact that
\mathcal{N}(\psi)\in\mathcal{J}
and a proper inverse inequality. This proves our claim and hence
\mathcal{N}
has a unique fixed point
\phi_{*}=\mathcal{N}(\phi_{*})
which solves the discrete problem. Furthermore, for
u_{h}:=\phi_{\ast}=\mathcal{N}(\phi_{*})
belongs to
\mathcal{J},
and satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq\|u_{h}-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}+\|u-W_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,T_{h})}\leq\tilde{C}(u)h^{r}\,.

Schur-Weyl dualities for shifted quantum affine algebras and Ariki-Koike algebras
*

Abstract
. We establish a Schur-Weyl duality between a shifted quantum affine algebra and an Ariki-Koike algebra. Then, we realize a cyclotomic q q -Schur algebra in the context of the Schur-Weyl duality.



0.
Introduction

0.1.
Let
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
be the Ariki-Koike algebra (cyclotomic Hecke algebra) associated with the complex reflection group

\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{n}
of type
G(r,1,n)
over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
with parameters

q,Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
We denote by
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra associated with
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
introduced in
[
DJM98
]
,
where
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
is an
r
-tuple of positive integers.

By
[
DJM98
]
, it is known that
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is a quasi-hereditary cover of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
,
equivalently
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}\operatorname{-mod}
is a highest weight cover of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}\operatorname{-mod}

in the sense of
[
Rou08
]
if
\mathbf{m}
is large enough.
Moreover, in
[
RSVV16
]
and
[
Los16
]
independently,
it is known that

\mathscr{S}_{n,r}\operatorname{-mod}
is equivalent to a certain full subcategory of an affine parabolic category

\mathbf{O}
of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{N}
,
and also equivalent to the category
\mathcal{O}
of rational Cherednik algebra of type
G(r,1,n)
with corresponding parameters
as highest weight covers of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}\operatorname{-mod}
.
In the degenerate cases,
they are studied in
[
BK08
]

which also contains some connection with finite
W
-algebras.

In this paper,
we establish a Schur-Weyl duality between
a shifted quantum affine algebra and an Ariki-Koike algebra,
then we realize the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra via this Schur-Weyl duality.

0.2.
In the case where
r=1
,
the algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n,1}
is the Iwahori-Hecke algebra associated with the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
and
\mathscr{S}_{n,1}(m)
is the classical
q
-Schur algebra given in
[
DJ89
]
.
It is well-known that the
q
-Schur algebra appears in the context of quantum Schur-Weyl duality established in
[
Jim86
]

(
[
BLM90
]
and
[
Du95
]
in the case where
q
is a root of unity).
In particular, the
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,1}(m)
is a quotient algebra of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})

associated with the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
.

0.3.
In the case where
r>1
, the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is defined
as the endomorphism ring of a certain
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-module which is a natural generalization
of the classical
q
-Schur algebras.
There is a nice survey
[
Mat04
]
on the representation theory of
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
.
Through the study of representations of
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
,
it had been expected that there exists a certain quantum group
in the background of the representation theory of cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebras.

In
[
DR99
]
, it is proven that the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})

has upper and lower Borel subalgebras,
and
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is a product of them.
Moreover,
the upper (resp. lower) Borel subalgebra of
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is isomorphic to
the upper (resp. lower) Borel subalgebra of the classical
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,1}(m)

which is a quotient of the upper (resp. lower) Borel subalgebra of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})

through the quantum Schur-Weyl duality if
\mathbf{m}
is large enough.
A difference between the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
(
r>1
)
and the classical
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,1}(m)
appears in the commutation relations
for upper and lower Borel subalgebras (see
[
Wad11
]
for details).
We will clarify that this difference corresponds to a shift of the shifted quantum affine algebra
in this paper.

In
[
Wad16
]
,
by using the structures of
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
explained in the above,
the author introduces the deformed current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x]
and
the
(q,\mathbf{Q})
-current algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])

associated with the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
,
and proves that the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is realized as a quotient of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])
.
The deformed current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x]
is a certain deformation of the polynomial current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}[x]=\mathfrak{gl}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}[x]
with parameters
\mathbf{Q}=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
,
and The
(q,\mathbf{Q})
-current algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])

is a
q
-analogue of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x]
.
The representations of deformed current Lie algebras
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x]
and also
\mathfrak{sl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x]

are studied in
[
Wad18
]
.

In
[
FT19
]
, Finkelberg and Tsymbaliuk introduce the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}=U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
as a certain shift of quantum loop algebra
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
.
Then, in
[
KW21
]
, the authors prove that the
(q,\mathbf{Q})
-current algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])

is a subalgebra of the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}

with the special shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
,
and study the representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])

by using the connection with the shifted quantum affine algebras.
The representations of shifted quantum affine algebras in more general setting are studied in
[
Her23
]
.

0.4.
In this paper,
we establish the Schur-Weyl duality for the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}

with the special shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
and the Ariki-Koike algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}

(see (
8.3.1
) for the shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
).
Then, we realize the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
through this Schur-Weyl duality.
More precisely,
we construct the
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}
and its submodule
\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}

via a shift of the quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality.
We denote the representation corresponding to the action of
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}

on
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
by
\rho_{n,r}:U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}})
,
and also denote the action of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
by
\sigma_{n,r}:\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}})
.
Then, we have the following.

Theorem 0.5

(Theorem
8.9
)
.

Assume that
q
is not a root of unity and
m_{k}\geq n
for
k=1,2,\dots,r
.
The shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
and the Ariki-Koike algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}

satisfy the double centralizer property through the bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
, namely we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\rho_{n,r}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}}(V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}),\quad\operatorname{Im}\sigma_{n,r}=\operatorname{End}_{U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}}(V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}).

In particular, we have the surjective homomorphism

\displaystyle\rho_{n,r}:U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}}(V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}})\cong\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m}).

In fact, the statements in this theorem also hold even the case where
q
is a root of unity
by taking a certain
\mathbb{A}
-form
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\mathbb{A}}
of
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
and its specialization,
where
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{Z}[\hat{q},\hat{q}^{-1},\hat{Q}_{0},\dots,\hat{Q}_{r-1}]
with indeterminate variables

\hat{q},\hat{Q}_{0},\dots,\hat{Q}_{r-1}
.
We give these treatments in the paragraph
8
.
The
\mathbb{A}
-form
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\mathbb{A}}
in here is enough to consider the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra at a root of unity,
but we do not whether
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\mathbb{A}}
is the “correct” integral form of the shifted quantum affine algebra or not,
and it seems not.
(see Remark
8
).

By Theorem
0.5
,
we have the surjective homomorphism

\rho_{n,r}:U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\rightarrow\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})

if
\mathbf{m}
is large enough.
This surjection is an extension of the surjection from the
(q,\mathbf{Q})
-current algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m}^{\langle\mathbf{Q}\rangle}[x])

to
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
given in
[
Wad16
]
.
However, the argument in this paper is quite different from that in
[
Wad16
]
.
In
[
Wad16
]
, we construct the surjection directly.
On the other hand, in this paper,
we obtain the surjection through the Schur-Weyl duality,
and we clarify how the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})

appears in the representation theory of the shifted quantum affine algebra
through the Schur-Weyl duality.

0.6.
In our argument,
we identify the set
\varLambda_{n}(m)
of compositions of
n
having
m
-parts
with the set
\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
of
r
-compositions of
n
having
\mathbf{m}
-parts,
and we regard them as a subset of the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
(see §
1
).

In §
3
,
we review and study on cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebras.
In particular,
we consider the cyclic right
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules

M^{\mu}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
for
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
(see (
3.1.2
) for definitions).
The cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}}(\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})}M^{\mu})

as the endomorphism ring of the direct sum of cyclic modules
M^{\mu}
(
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
).
The cyclic module
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
(
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
) has a supporting role to consider the connection with Schur-Weyl dualities explained in the below.
We give the defining relations of these cyclic modules
M^{\mu}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}

in Theorem
3.8
,
and we have the surjective homomorphism
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}\rightarrow M^{\mu}
as right
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules
by using these defining relations.

It seems that these defining relations had been unknown,
and may be useful in the study of Ariki-Koike algebras and cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebras themselves.

0.7.
We briefly explain how to construct
the
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}
and its submodule
\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}

used in Theorem
0.5
.

We start with the quantum Schur-Weyl duality in
[
Jim86
]
.
Let
V
be the vector representation of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
,
and we consider the tensor space
V^{\otimes n}
.
Then, the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
acts on
V^{\otimes n}
through the coproduct of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
,
and the Iwahori Hecke algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n}
associated with the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
V^{\otimes n}

as a
q
-analogue of the place permutations of tensor products.
Then, the algebras
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
and
\mathscr{H}_{n}
satisfy the double centralizer property,
so-called quantum Schur-Weyl duality,
through the tensor space
V^{\otimes n}

by
[
Jim86
, Proposition 3]
.

We consider the affinization of the quantum Schur-Weyl duality
given in
[
GRV94
]
and
[
CP96
]
independently.
Let
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
be the quantum affine algebra of type
A_{m-1}^{(1)}
.
For
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
there exists an algebra homomorphism,
so-called evaluation homomorphism,

\mathbf{{ev}}_{\gamma}:U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\rightarrow U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
.
We regard the vector representation
V
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
as
the
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
-module through the evaluation homomorphism
\mathbf{{ev}}_{1}
at
\gamma=1
,
and we consider the affinization
\widehat{V}=V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]
of
V

in the sense of
[
Kas02
, §4.2]
.
Then, the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
acts on the tensor space
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}

through the coproduct of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
,
and the affine Hecke algebra
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
of type
GL_{n}
also acts on
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}

from right.
Then, these actions on
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}
commute with each other,
and the functor

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}}-:\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\operatorname{-mod},\quad M\mapsto\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}}M

gives an equivalence between the category of finite dimensional
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
-modules
and the corresponding subcategory of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\operatorname{-mod}

by
[
GRV94
, Theorem 6.8]
and
[
CP96
, Theorem 4.2]
.
We call it the quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality.

Note that the Ariki-Koike algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
is a quotient of the affine Hecke algebra
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
,
and we put
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}=\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}}\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
.
Then, the space
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
has the
(U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m}),\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule structure.
The
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
-module
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
has the weight space decomposition

\widetilde{V}_{n,r}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m)}\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}
,
and this is also a decomposition as right
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules.
By using the defining relation of
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
and comparing dimensions,
we see that
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
as right
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules
for each
\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m)
,
where we identify the set
\varLambda_{n}(m)
with
\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}
be the
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-submodule of
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}

such that
\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}\cap\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}
coincides with the kernel of the surjection

\widetilde{M}^{\mu}\rightarrow M^{\mu}
under the isomorphism
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}\cong\widetilde{M}^{\mu}

for each
\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m)
.
As a consequence, we have the isomorphism

\widetilde{V}_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})}M^{\mu}
as right
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules.

Unfortunately,
the space
\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}
is not a
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
-submodule of
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
(Proposition
6.7
),
and
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
does not act on
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}
.
Then, we need to consider the shifts of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
and
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
as follows.
Since the action of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
on
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
is level zero,
the quantum loop algebra
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
also acts on
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
through the isomorphism given in
[
Dri87
]
and
[
Bec94
]
.
We consider the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
with the shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
corresponding to
\mathbf{m}

(see (
8.3.1
) for the shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
),
and consider the shift homomorphism
\iota_{[0,\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\bm{\eta}}:U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\rightarrow U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
.
(Correctly, we need to take a certain quotient of
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
associated with
\bm{\eta}
,
or replace
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
with
U_{q,[0]}
, see Remark
7
.
We do not care in this introduction since it is not so much trouble.)
By
[
FT19
]
, it is known that
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
is a coideal subalgebra of
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})

through the shift homomorphism
\iota_{[0,\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\bm{\eta}}
.
In particular, we have the algebra homomorphism

\Delta_{\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}},0}:U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\rightarrow U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}\otimes U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})

induced from Drinfeld-Jimbo coproduct of
U_{q}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
.
We also take a one-dimensional
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
-module
L_{\bm{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathbf{Q}}}
with respect to
parameters
\mathbf{Q}=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})

(see (
8.3.2
)).
Put
V_{n,r}=L_{\bm{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathbf{Q}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
.
Then,
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
acts on
V_{n,r}
through the algebra homomorphism
\Delta_{\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}},0}
,
and
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
also acts on
V_{n,r}
from right via the action on
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
.
As a consequence, we obtain the
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}
.
We can also take an
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-submodule
\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
of
V_{n,r}
in a similar manner
as in the case of
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
,
and we see that
\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
is also a
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
-submodule of
V_{n,r}

(Corollary
8.8
).
Then, we obtain the Schur-Weyl duality in Theorem
0.5

through the
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
.

0.8.
The construction of the
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
is a bit technical.
However, in the case where
q=1
, we have more natural interpretation of the bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
as follows.

In §
9
,
we introduce the shifted loop Lie algebra
L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
with a shift
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m-1}
.
This is a shift of the loop Lie algebra
L\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
associated with
\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
.
In particular, we have the shift homomorphism

\iota_{[\mathbf{b}]^{\sharp}}^{\bm{\eta}}:L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
(Proposition
9.5
).
We can regard the universal enveloping algebra
U(L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
of
L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}

as the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}
at
q=1
by taking a certain form
\widetilde{U}_{q,\bm{\eta},[\mathbf{b}]}
of
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}
.

We denote by
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{q=1}
the Ariki-Koike algebra at
q=1
,
and we denote by
U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}

the universal enveloping algebra of the shifted loop Lie algebra
L_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
with the shift
\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}
.
Then, we can construct the
(U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{q=1})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
in a similar way as in the above.
On the other hand, the algebra
U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
is a Hopf subalgebra of
U(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
through the shift homomorphism

\iota_{[\mathbf{b}]^{\sharp}}^{\bm{\eta}}:L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}\rightarrow L\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
.
Put
\mathbb{V}=V_{1,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{1,r}}
the bimodule in the case where
n=1
.
We consider the tensor space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
,
and the algebra
U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
acts on
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
through the coproduct of
U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
.
We can also define the right action of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{q=1}
on
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
in a natural way.
Then, we can prove that
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}\cong V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
as
(U_{[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{q=1})
-bimodules,
and we obtain the statements corresponding to Theorem
0.5
at
q=1

through the tensor space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}

(Theorem
10.8
).

By the above argument at
q=1
,
we can regard
(U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]},\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}

as a
q
-analogue of the tensor space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
.
However, we can not consider the tensor space
\mathbb{V}^{\otimes n}
as the
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
-module directly
since the shifted quantum affine algebra
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}_{\mathbf{m}}]}
does not have a Hopf algebra structure although
it is a coideal subalgebra of
U(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
.
Then, as explained in the above,
we need a bit technical steps to construct the bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
,
but it seems that the bimodule
V_{n,r}/\mathcal{I}_{V_{n,r}}
is natural one in the representation theory of shifted quantum affine algebras.

1.
Notation

In this section, we give some notation used in this paper.

1.1.
For a condition
X
,
we put
\delta_{(X)}=1
if
X
is true, and
\delta_{(X)}=0
if
X
is false.
For integers
i
and
j
, put
\delta_{i,j}=\delta_{(i=j)}
.

For an integer
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we put
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq a}=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}\mid b\geq a\}
.
It is similar for
\mathbb{Z}_{>a}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq a}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{<a}
.

For an algebra
A
, we denote the opposite algebra of
A
by
A^{\operatorname{opp}}
.
We also denote the category of finitely generated left
A
-modules by
A\operatorname{-mod}
.

1.2.
For an
r
-tuple of positive integers
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
,
put

\displaystyle\varGamma(\mathbf{m})=\{(i,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid 1\leq i\leq m_{k},\,1\leq k\leq r\}.

We identify the set
\varGamma(\mathbf{m})
with the set
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
,
where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}
,
by the bijection

\displaystyle\xi:\varGamma(\mathbf{m})\rightarrow\{1,2,\dots,m\},\quad(i,k)\mapsto\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}m_{p}+i.

Then we can regard the set
\varGamma(\mathbf{m})
as a partion of the set
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
to
r
-parts.

1.3.
For an
r
-tuple of positive integers
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
,
and the integer
m=m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}
,
put

\displaystyle\varLambda_{n}(m)=\{\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}=n\},

\displaystyle\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})=\left\{\mu=(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\dots,\mu^{(r)})\mid\begin{array}[]{l}\mu^{(k)}=(\mu_{1}^{(k)},\mu_{2}^{(k)},\dots,\mu_{m_{k}}^{(k)})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m_{k}}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{i}^{(k)}=n\end{array}\right\}.

An element of
\varLambda_{n}(m)
is called a composition of
n
with
m
parts,
and an element of
\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
is called an
r
-composition.
For
\mu=(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\dots,\mu^{(r)})\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
,
we denote by
|\mu^{(k)}|
the sum of integers
\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}\mu_{i}^{(k)}
.

We identify the set
\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
with the set
\varLambda_{n}(m)
by the bijection

(1.3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})\rightarrow\varLambda_{n}(m),\\
&(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)}\dots,\mu^{(r)})\mapsto(\mu_{1}^{(1)},\mu_{2}^{(1)},\dots,\mu_{m_{1}}^{(1)},\mu_{1}^{(2)},\dots,\mu_{m_{2}}^{(2)},\dots,\mu_{1}^{(r)},\dots,\mu_{m_{r}}^{(r)}).\end{split}

Let
P=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_{i}
be the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
.
Put
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}
(
1\leq i\leq m-1
),
then
\{\alpha_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1\}
gives the set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
(resp.
\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
).
We regard the set
\varLambda_{n}(m)
as a subset of the weight lattice
P

by the injection

(1.3.2)

\displaystyle\varLambda_{n}(m)\rightarrow P,\quad(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{m})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\varepsilon_{i}.

We also regard the set
\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
as a subset of the weight lattice
P

through the bijection (
1.3.1
) and the injection (
1.3.2
).

1.4.
For
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{m})\in\varLambda_{n}(m)=\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})

and
1\leq i\leq m
,
put

\displaystyle N_{i}^{\mu}=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\dots+\mu_{i}.

For
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
and
0\leq k\leq r
, put

\displaystyle a_{k}^{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\mu^{(j)}|.

For
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
and
1\leq j\leq n
,
we define the integer
c_{j}^{\mu}
by

(1.4.1)

\displaystyle c_{j}^{\mu}=k\text{ if }a_{k-1}^{\mu}<j\leq a_{k}^{\mu}.

From the definition, we see that

\displaystyle c_{j}^{\mu}\leq c_{j^{\prime}}^{\mu}\text{ if }j\leq j^{\prime}\text{ and }c_{N_{i}^{\mu}+1}^{\mu}=c_{N_{i}^{\mu}+2}^{\mu}=\dots=c_{N_{i}^{\mu}+\mu_{i+1}}^{\mu}.

2.
Ariki-Koike algebras and affine Hecke algebras

In this section,
we review the definition and some known basic properties of Ariki-Koike algebras.
We also introduce some elements of the Ariki-Koike algebra which are used in the subsequence argument.

2.1.
Let
R
be a commutative ring.
We take parameters
q,Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1}\in R^{\times}
,
where
R^{\times}
is the set of invertible elements in
R
,
and put
\mathbf{Q}=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r-1})
.
The Ariki-Koike algebra (cyclotomic Hecke algebra)
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}=\mathscr{H}_{n,r}(q,\mathbf{Q})

associated with the complex reflection group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{n}
of type
G(r,1,n)

is the associative algebra over
R
generated by
T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}

with the following defining relations:

\displaystyle(T_{0}-Q_{0})(T_{0}-Q_{1})\dots(T_{0}-Q_{r-1})=0,\quad(T_{i}-q)(T_{i}+q^{-1})=0\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),

\displaystyle T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}=T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}T_{0},\quad T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-2),

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}\quad(|i-j|>1).

We remark that there exists an isomorphism of algebras

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{n,r}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}\text{ such that }T_{i}\mapsto T_{i}\,(0\leq i\leq n-1).

2.2.
The subalgebra of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
generated by
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{n-1}
is isomorphic to the Iwahori-Hecke algebra

\mathscr{H}_{n}
associated with the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of
n
letters.

For
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
,
we denote the adjacent transposition by
s_{i}=(i,i+1)
.
For
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we denote the length of
w
by
\ell(w)
.
Let
\{T_{w}\mid w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathscr{H}_{n}
,
namely
T_{w}=T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\dots T_{i_{l}}
when
w=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\dots s_{i_{l}}
is a reduced expression.

For
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{m})\in\varLambda_{n}(m)
, let
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
be the parabolic subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
corresponding to
\mu
,
namely
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{\mu_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\mu_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\mu_{m}}

is the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
generated by

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\{s_{(\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i-1})+1},s_{(\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i-1})+2},\dots,s_{(\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{i-1})+\mu_{i}-1}\}.

Put
\mathfrak{S}^{\mu}=\{y\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\mid\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\}
,
then it is well-known that
\mathfrak{S}^{\mu}
gives the set of distinguished coset representatives of the coset
\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Moreover, for
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we can uniquely write

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle T_{w}=T_{x}T_{y}\text{ for some }x\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}\text{ and }y\in\mathfrak{S}^{\mu}.

2.3.
For
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
, put

\mathbb{T}_{i,j}=T_{i}T_{i+1}\dots T_{j}
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j,i}=T_{j}T_{j-1}\dots T_{i}
.
For convenience,
we also put
\mathbb{T}_{i,i-1}=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,i}=1
for
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
.

For
i=1,2,\dots,n
,
we define an element
L_{i}\in\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
by
L_{i}=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,1}T_{0}\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1}
.
We see that
T_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n-1
) and
L_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq n
) are invertible elements of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}

by definitions.
The following relations are well-known,
and one can check them by direct calculation using the defining relations of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have the following.

(i)

L_{i}
and
L_{j}
commute with each other for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

(ii)

T_{i}
and
L_{j}
commute with each other if
j\not=i,i+1
.

T_{i}L_{i}=L_{i+1}T_{i}-(q-q^{-1})L_{i+1}
,

T_{i}L_{i+1}=L_{i}T_{i}+(q-q^{-1})L_{i+1}
.

(iii)

T_{i}
commutes with both
L_{i}L_{i+1}
and
L_{i}+L_{i+1}
.

(iv)

For any
a\in R
,

(L_{1}-a)(L_{2}-a)\dots(L_{i}-a)
and
T_{j}
commute with each other if
i\not=j
.

(v)

L_{i+1}^{t}T_{i}=T_{i}L_{i}^{t}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}L_{i}^{s}L_{i+1}^{t-s}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

L_{i}^{t}T_{i}=T_{i}L_{i+1}^{t}-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{s=1}^{t}L_{i}^{t-s}L_{i+1}^{s}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

(vi)

L_{i}^{k}L_{i+1}^{l}T_{i}=\begin{cases}T_{i}L_{i}^{l}L_{i+1}^{k}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{s=1}^{l-k}L_{i}^{l-s}L_{i+1}^{k+s}&\text{ if }k\leq l,\\
T_{i}L_{i}^{l}L_{i+1}^{k}-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{s=1}^{k-l}L_{i}^{k-s}L_{i+1}^{l+s}&\text{ if }k>l\end{cases}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

We have the following theorem for an
R
-free basis of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
proved in
[
AK94
, Theomre 3.10]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
AK94
, Theomre 3.10]
)
.

The algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
is an
R
-free module with an
R
-free basis

\{T_{w}L_{1}^{k_{1}}L_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots L_{n}^{k_{n}}\mid w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},\,0\leq k_{i}\leq r-1\,(1\leq i\leq n)\}
.
The set
\{L_{1}^{k_{1}}L_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots L_{n}^{k_{n}}T_{w}\mid w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},\,0\leq k_{i}\leq r-1\,(1\leq i\leq n)\}

also gives an
R
-free basis of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
.

2.6.
The affine Hecke algebra
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
of type
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
is the associative algebra
over
R
generated by
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{n-1}
and
X_{1}^{\pm},X_{2}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}

with the following defining relations:

\displaystyle(T_{i}-q)(T_{i}+q^{-1})=0\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),

\displaystyle T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-2),\quad T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}\quad(|i-j|>1),

\displaystyle X_{i}^{+}X_{i}^{-}=X_{i}^{-}X_{i}^{+}=1,\quad X_{i}^{+}X_{j}^{+}=X_{j}^{+}X_{i}^{+}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n),

\displaystyle T_{i}X_{i}^{+}T_{i}=X_{i+1}^{+}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),\quad T_{i}X_{j}^{+}=X_{j}^{+}T_{i}\quad(j\not=i,i+1).

By the defining relations of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}

together with
the definition of
L_{j}
and Lemma
2.4
(ii),
we see that there exists the surjective algebra homomorphism

(2.6.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{H}_{n}}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{n,r}\text{ such that }T_{i}\mapsto T_{i},\quad X_{j}^{+}\mapsto L_{j},\quad X_{j}^{-}\mapsto L_{j}^{-1}.

2.7.
For later discussion,
we introduce some modifications of the elements
L_{i}^{k}\in\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
as follows.
For
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq r
,
we define
L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}\in\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
by

\displaystyle L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,1}(T_{0}-Q_{0})(T_{0}-Q_{1})\dots(T_{0}-Q_{k-1})\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1}.

By the defining relations of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
,
we see that
L_{i}^{\langle r\rangle}=0
for any
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
We have
T_{i}L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}T_{i}=L_{i+1}^{\langle k\rangle}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
1\leq k\leq n
.
We also have

(2.7.1)

\displaystyle L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}

\displaystyle=L_{i}^{\langle l\rangle}(\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1})^{-1}(T_{0}-Q_{l})(T_{0}-Q_{l+1})\dots(T_{0}-Q_{k-1})\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1}

for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq l<k\leq r
.
We note that
L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}
and
L_{j}^{\langle l\rangle}
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
1\leq k,l\leq r
) do not commute in general.

Remark 2.8.
In the case where
q=1
, we have
T_{i}^{2}=1
for
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
.
Then we have
\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,1}=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,1}\mathbb{T}_{1,i-1}=1
.
This implies that, if
q=1
,

\displaystyle L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}=(L_{i}-Q_{0})(L_{i}-Q_{1})\dots(L_{i}-Q_{k-1})

for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq r
.

In the last of this section, we give some technical lemmas for the elements
L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}
.
We give a proof of these lemmas in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq r
,
we have

\displaystyle L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{i}^{k}+\sum_{0\leq p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{i}\leq k-1}L_{1}^{p_{1}}L_{2}^{p_{2}}\dots L_{i}^{p_{i}}h_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{i})}

for some
h_{(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{i})}\in\mathscr{H}_{[1,i]}
, where

\mathscr{H}_{[1,i]}
is the subalgebra of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
generated by
\{T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{i-1}\}

with
\mathscr{H}_{[1,1]}=\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

For
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
1\leq l\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq r
, we have the following.

(i)

If
l\not=i,i+1
, we have

T_{i}L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}T_{i}
.

(ii)

T_{i}L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{i+1}^{\langle k\rangle}T_{i}-(q-q^{-1})L_{i+1}^{\langle k\rangle}
.

(iii)

T_{i}L_{i+1}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}T_{i}+(q-q^{-1})L_{i+1}^{\langle k\rangle}
.

(iv)

\mathbb{T}_{i,j}L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}=\begin{cases}L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{i,j}&\text{ if }l>j+1,\\
\displaystyle L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{i,j}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-i+1}L_{i+p}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{i,i+p-2}\mathbb{T}_{i+p,j}&\text{ if }l=j+1,\\
L_{l+1}^{\langle k\rangle}\big(\mathbb{T}_{i,j}-(q-q^{-1})\mathbb{T}_{i,l-1}\mathbb{T}_{l+1,j}\big)&\text{ if }j+1>l\geq i,\\
L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{i,j}&\text{ if }i>l.\end{cases}

(v)

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j,i}L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}=\begin{cases}L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j,i}&\text{ if }l>j+1,\\
L_{l-1}^{\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j,i}+(q-q^{-1})L_{j+1}^{\langle k\rangle}(\mathbb{T}_{l,j})^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{l-2,i}&\text{ if }j+1\geq l>i,\\
L_{j+1}^{\langle k\rangle}(\mathbb{T}_{i,j})^{-1}&\text{ if }l=i,\\
L_{l}^{\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j,i}&\text{ if }i>l.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.11
.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq r
, we have the following.

(i)

(a)

If
i>j
, we have

L_{i}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}
.

(b)

If
i=j
, we have

\displaystyle L_{j}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j}-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}(L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j-p}-L_{j-p}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j})(\mathbb{T}_{j-p+1,j-1})^{-1}\mathbb{T}_{j-p,j-1}.

(c)

If
i<j
, we have

\displaystyle L_{i}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}+(q-q^{-1})(L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}-L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j})(\mathbb{T}_{i+1,j-1})^{-1}\mathbb{T}_{i,j-1}.

(ii)

(a)

If
i>j
, we have

L_{i}^{-1}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}^{-1}
.

(b)

If
i=j
, we have

\displaystyle L_{j}^{-1}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}

\displaystyle=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}L_{j-p}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j-p}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}_{j-p+1,\,j-1})^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-p-1,\,1}\mathbb{T}_{1,\,j-1}.

(c)

If
i<j
, we have

\displaystyle L_{i}^{-1}L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}

\displaystyle=L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}^{-1}-(q-q^{-1})L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{j}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,\,i+1}\mathbb{T}_{i,\,j-1}

\displaystyle\quad+(q-q^{-1})L_{i}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,\,i+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-1,\,1}\mathbb{T}_{1,\,j-1}

\displaystyle\quad-(q-q^{-1})^{2}\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}L_{i-p}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{i-p}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,\,i+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{i-p-1,\,1}(\mathbb{T}_{i-p+1,\,i-1})^{-1}\mathbb{T}_{1,\,j-1}.

Lemma 2.12
.

For
1\leq j\leq n
and

,
we have

\displaystyle(L_{j}-Q_{k})L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}=L_{j}^{\langle k+1\rangle}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}L_{p}^{\langle k\rangle}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,\,p+1}L_{p+1}\mathbb{T}_{p,j-1}.

3.
Cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebras

In this section,
we review the definition and some known basic properties of cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebras.
Then, we give a defining relation of cyclic
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-module
M^{\mu}
(resp.
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
)
in Theorem
3.8
.
These defining relations are important to construct the Schur-Weyl duality in the subsequence sections.

3.1.
For
\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
, we define the elements
x_{\mu}
and
m_{\mu}
of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
by

\displaystyle x_{\mu}=\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}q^{\ell(w)}T_{w},\quad m_{\mu}=\big(\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}}q^{\ell(w)}T_{w}\big)\big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\prod_{i=1}^{a_{k}^{\mu}}(L_{i}-Q_{k})\big).

It is well-known (e.g.
[
Mat99
, Lemma 3.2]
) that

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle x_{\mu}T_{w}=q^{\ell(w)}x_{\mu}\text{ and }m_{\mu}T_{w}=q^{\ell(w)}m_{\mu}\text{ for }w\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}.

We denote the right ideal of
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
generated by
m_{\mu}
(resp.
x_{\mu}
) by
M^{\mu}
(resp.
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
),
namely we have

(3.1.2)

\displaystyle M^{\mu}=m_{\mu}\mathscr{H}_{n,r}\text{ and }\widetilde{M}^{\mu}=x_{\mu}\mathscr{H}_{n,r}.

Then, the cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})
associated with
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n,r}^{\operatorname{opp}}}\big(\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})}M^{\mu}\big).

From the definitions, we see that
\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})}M^{\mu}
is an
(\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m}),\mathscr{H}_{n,r})
-bimodule.
The cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra has the following property.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
DJM98
, Theorem 6.12, Corollary 6.18]
,
[
Mat04
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

Suppose that
R
is a field, and
m_{k}\geq n
for
k=1,2,\dots,r
,
then we have the following.

(i)

The cyclotomic
q
-Schur algebra
\mathscr{S}_{n,r}(\mathbf{m})

4.
Quantum Schur-Weyl duality

In this section, we recall the quantum Schur-Weyl duality given in
[
Jim86
]
,
and also recall the connection with the classical
q
-Schur algebras.

4.1.
The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
associated with the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}

is an associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with a parameter
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}

generated by
E_{i},F_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq m-1
) and
K_{j}^{\pm}
(
1\leq j\leq m
)
with the following defining relations:

\displaystyle K_{i}^{+}K_{i}^{-}=K_{i}^{-}K_{i}^{+}=1,\quad K_{i}^{+}K_{j}^{+}=K_{j}^{+}K_{i}^{+},

\displaystyle K_{i}^{+}E_{j}K_{i}^{-}=q^{\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i,j+1}}E_{j},\quad K_{i}^{+}F_{j}K_{i}^{-}=q^{-(\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i,j+1})}F_{j},

\displaystyle E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{i,j}\frac{K_{i}^{+}K_{i+1}^{-}-K_{i}^{-}K_{i+1}^{+}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle E_{i\pm 1}E_{i}^{2}-(q+q^{-1})E_{i}E_{i\pm 1}E_{i}+E_{i}^{2}E_{i\pm 1}=0,\quad E_{i}E_{j}=E_{j}E_{i}\text{ if }j\not=i\pm 1,

\displaystyle F_{i\pm 1}F_{i}^{2}-(q+q^{-1})F_{i}F_{i\pm 1}F_{i}+F_{i}^{2}F_{i\pm 1}=0,\quad F_{i}F_{j}=F_{j}F_{i}\text{ if }j\not=i\pm 1.

The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
has the coproduct
\Delta:U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\rightarrow U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})

defined by

\displaystyle\Delta(E_{i})=E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{+}K_{i+1}^{-}+1\otimes E_{i},\quad\Delta(F_{i})=F_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}^{-}K_{i+1}^{+}\otimes F_{i},

\displaystyle\Delta(K_{j}^{\pm})=K_{j}^{\pm}\otimes K_{j}^{\pm}.

4.2.
Let
V
be an
m
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
with a basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}\}
.
We define the left action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
on
V
by

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot v_{j}=\delta_{j,i+1}v_{j-1},\quad F_{i}\cdot v_{j}=\delta_{j,i}v_{j+1},\quad K_{i}^{\pm}\cdot v_{j}=q^{\pm\delta_{j,i}}v_{j}.

The
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module
V
is called the
q
-vector representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
.
The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
acts on the tensor space
V^{\otimes n}
through the coproduct
\Delta
.
We denote by
\rho:U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\otimes n})
the representation corresponding to this action.

On the other hand,
we define a linear transformation
\mathcal{T}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V\otimes V)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=\begin{cases}qv_{i}\otimes v_{j}&\text{ if }i=j,\\
v_{j}\otimes v_{i}&\text{ if }i<j,\\
v_{j}\otimes v_{i}+(q-q^{-1})v_{i}\otimes v_{j}&\text{ if }i>j,\end{cases}

and we define the right action of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra
\mathscr{H}_{n}
associated with
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
by

\displaystyle T_{i}=(\operatorname{id}_{V})^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\mathcal{T}\otimes(\operatorname{id}_{V})^{\otimes(n-i-1)}

for
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
.
We denote the representation corresponding to this action by
\sigma:\mathscr{H}_{n}^{\operatorname{opp}}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\otimes n})
.
Then we have the quantum Schur-Weyl duality obtained in
[
Jim86
]
.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
Jim86
, Proposition 3]
)
.

The actions of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
and of
\mathscr{H}_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
commute with each other.
If
q
is not a root of unity,
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\rho=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n}^{\operatorname{opp}}}(V^{\otimes n})\text{ and }\operatorname{Im}\sigma=\operatorname{End}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})}(V^{\otimes n}).

Moreover, if
m\geq n
, the homomorphism
\sigma
is injective,
and we have the isomorphism
\mathscr{H}_{n}\cong\operatorname{End}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})}(V^{\otimes n})
.

4.4.
Suppose that
q
is not a root of unity.
We have the following weight space decomposition of
V^{\otimes n}
as the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module:

\displaystyle V^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m)}V^{\otimes n}_{\mu},\quad V^{\otimes n}_{\mu}=\{v\in V^{\otimes n}\mid K_{j}^{+}\cdot v=q^{\mu_{j}}v\,\,(1\leq j\leq m)\}.

Since the action of
\mathscr{H}_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
commute with the action of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
,
the weight space
V^{\otimes n}_{\mu}
is an
\mathscr{H}_{n}
-submodule of
V^{\otimes n}
.
Moreover, from definitions, we see that

\displaystyle\{v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n}}\mid\sharp\{k\mid j_{k}=i\}=\mu_{i}\,,1\leq i\leq m\}

gives a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
V^{\otimes n}_{\mu}
,
and the right
\mathscr{H}_{n}
-module
V_{\mu}^{\otimes n}
is generated by

(4.4.1)

\displaystyle v_{\mu}=\underbrace{v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{1}}_{\mu_{1}}\otimes\underbrace{v_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{2}}_{\mu_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\underbrace{v_{m}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{m}}_{\mu_{m}}.

We can easily check that
v_{\mu}T_{i}=qv_{\mu}
if
s_{i}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mu}
.
Then, we have the surjective
\mathscr{H}_{n}
-homomorphism

\varphi:M^{\mu}\rightarrow V^{\otimes n}_{\mu}
such that
x_{\mu}\mapsto v_{\mu}
by Theorem
3.8
,
where we note that
m_{\mu}=x_{\mu}
, thus
M^{\mu}=\widetilde{M}^{\mu}
, in the case of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra

\mathscr{H}_{n}
associated with
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We can also check that

(4.4.2)

\displaystyle\sharp\mathfrak{S}^{\mu}=\sharp\{v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n}}\mid\sharp\{k\mid j_{k}=i\}=\mu_{i}\,,1\leq i\leq m\}

(see, e.g.
[
DDPW08
, §9.1]
),
and we see that the homomorphism
\varphi:M^{\mu}\rightarrow V^{\otimes n}_{\mu}
is an isomorphism.
As a consequence,
we have the algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\rho=\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n}^{\operatorname{opp}}}(V^{\otimes n})\cong\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{H}_{n}^{\operatorname{opp}}}\big(\bigoplus_{\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m)}M^{\mu}\big)=\mathscr{S}_{n}(\mathbf{m})

which is a quotient of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
.

Remark 4.5.
The results in this section also hold over an any field
F
and any parameter

by replacing
U_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
with the restricted quantum group associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
over

which is the specialized algebra of the Lusztig’s integral form using divided powers.
This fact is pointed out in
[
Du95
]
by using the geometric realization of

given in
[
BLM90
]
.

5.
Quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality

In this section,
we recall the quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality given in

[
GRV94
]
and
[
CP96
]
independently.

5.1.
The quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
is an associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with a parameter

q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}

generated by
e_{i},f_{i},k_{i}^{\pm}
(
0\leq i\leq m-1
) with the following defining relations:

\displaystyle k_{i}^{+}k_{i}^{-}=k_{i}^{-}k_{i}^{+}=1,\quad k_{i}^{+}k_{j}^{+}=k_{j}^{+}k_{i}^{+},\quad k_{i}^{+}e_{j}k_{i}^{-}=q^{a_{ij}}e_{j},\quad k_{i}^{+}f_{j}k_{i}^{-}=q^{-a_{ij}}f_{j},

\displaystyle e_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{i,j}\frac{k_{i}^{+}-k_{i}^{-}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle e_{j}e_{i}^{2}-(q+q^{-1})e_{i}e_{j}e_{i}+e_{i}^{2}e_{j}=0\text{ if }a_{ij}=-1,\quad e_{i}e_{j}=e_{j}e_{i}\text{ if }a_{ij}=0,

\displaystyle f_{j}e_{i}^{2}-(q+q^{-1})f_{i}f_{j}f_{i}+f_{i}^{2}f_{j}=0\text{ if }a_{ij}=-1,\quad f_{i}f_{j}=f_{j}f_{i}\text{ if }a_{ij}=0,

where
(a_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}
is the Cartan matrix of type
A_{m-1}^{(1)}
.

The quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
has the coproduct

\Delta:U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\rightarrow U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\otimes U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
defined by

\displaystyle\Delta(e_{i})=e_{i}\otimes k_{i}^{+}+1\otimes e_{i},\quad\Delta(f_{i})=f_{i}\otimes 1+k_{i}^{-}\otimes f_{i},\quad\Delta(k_{i}^{\pm})=k_{i}^{\pm}\otimes k_{i}^{\pm}.

We call it Drinfeld-Jimbo coproduct of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
.

5.2.
For
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
there exists an algebra homomorphism

\mathbf{{ev}}_{\gamma}:U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\rightarrow U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
,
so-called an evaluation homomorphism at
\gamma
,
such that

\displaystyle e_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\quad f_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\quad k_{i}^{+}\mapsto K_{i}^{+}K_{i+1}^{-}\quad(1\leq i\leq m-1),

\displaystyle k_{0}^{+}\mapsto K_{1}^{-}K_{m}^{+},

\displaystyle e_{0}\mapsto\gamma q^{-1}(K_{1}^{+}K_{m}^{+})[F_{m-1},[F_{m-2},\dots,[F_{2},F_{1}]_{q^{-1}}\dots]_{q^{-1}}]_{q^{-1}},

\displaystyle f_{0}\mapsto(-1)^{m}\gamma^{-1}q^{m-1}(K_{1}^{-}K_{m}^{-})[E_{m-1},[E_{m-2},\dots,[E_{2},E_{1}]_{q^{-1}}\dots]_{q^{-1}}]_{q^{-1}}.

For a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module
M
,
we regard
M
as a
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
-module through the homomorphism
\mathbf{{ev}}_{\gamma}
,
and we denote it by
M^{\mathbf{{ev}}_{\gamma}}
.

Recall that
V
is the vector representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
considered in the previous section.
Let
\widehat{V}=V^{\mathbf{{ev}}_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]
be the affinization of
V^{\mathbf{{ev}}_{1}}
in the sense of
[
Kas02
, §4.2]
,
namely the generators
e_{i}
,
f_{i}
and
k_{i}^{\pm}
(
0\leq i\leq m-1
) act on
\widehat{V}

by
e_{i}\otimes x^{\delta_{i,0}}
,
f_{i}\otimes x^{-\delta_{i,0}}
and
k_{i}^{\pm}\otimes 1
respectively.
Then, we have that

(5.2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&e_{0}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=\delta_{j,1}v_{m}\otimes x^{k+1},\quad f_{0}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=\delta_{j,m}v_{1}\otimes x^{k-1},\\
&k_{0}^{\pm}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=q^{\pm(\delta_{m,j}-\delta_{1,j})}v_{j}\otimes x^{k},\\
&e_{i}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=\delta_{i+1,j}v_{j-1}\otimes x^{k},\quad f_{i}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=\delta_{i,j}v_{j+1}\otimes x^{k},\\
&k_{i}^{\pm}\cdot(v_{j}\otimes x^{k})=q^{\pm(\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i+1,j})}v_{j}\otimes x^{k}\quad(1\leq i\leq m-1).\end{split}

The quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
acts on the tensor space
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}
through the coproduct
\Delta
.

On the other hand,
we define the right action of
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
on
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}
as follows.
We identify the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}=(V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}])^{\otimes n}

with
V^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]

by the natural way.
Then, we define a linear transformation
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\widehat{V}\otimes\widehat{V})
by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{T}}((v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}})\otimes x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}})\otimes x_{1}^{k_{2}}x_{2}^{k_{1}}+(q-q^{-1})(v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}})\otimes\frac{x_{1}^{k_{2}}x_{2}^{k_{1}}-x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}}{x_{1}x_{2}^{-1}-1},

and we define the right action of
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
on
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}
by

\displaystyle T_{i}=(\operatorname{id}_{\widehat{V}})^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{T}}\otimes(\operatorname{id}_{\widehat{V}})^{\otimes(n-i-1)}\quad(1\leq i\leq n-1),

and the action of
X_{j}^{\pm}
(
1\leq j\leq n
) is given as the multiplication by
x_{j}^{\pm}
.
Then, we have the following quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
GRV94
, Theorem 6.8]
,
[
CP96
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

The actions of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})
and of
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
on
\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}
commute with each other.
If
q
is not a root of unity and
m\geq n
, then the functor

\displaystyle\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}}-:\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\operatorname{-mod},\quad M\mapsto\widehat{V}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}}M

gives an equivalence between the category of finite dimensional
\widehat{\mathscr{H}}_{n}
-modules
and the corresponding subcategory of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m})\operatorname{-mod}
.

6.

(U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{m}),\mathscr{H}_{n,r})

6.1.
In this section, we denote

6.3.
From the definitions, we have that

\displaystyle\left\{(v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n}})\otimes x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}\otimes 1\mid\begin{matrix}1\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\leq m,\\

By (

,
and
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}
has a
\mathbb{C}
-basis

(6.3.2)

\displaystyle\left\{(v_{j_{1}}\otimes v_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n}})\otimes x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}\otimes 1\mid\begin{matrix}\sharp\{k\mid j_{k}=i\}=\mu_{i}\,\,(1\leq i\leq m)\\
0\leq k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\leq r-1\end{matrix}\right\}.

On the other hand, the decomposition (

\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules

For

As a consequence, we have the following lemma.

For each

\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-modules
from
\widetilde{M}^{\mu}

\widetilde{V}_{n,r}^{\mu}

6.3.4

6.5.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{\widetilde{V}_{n,r}}
be the
\mathscr{H}_{n,r}
-submodule of
\widetilde{V}_{n,r}
generated by

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{v}_{\mu}\cdot L_{j}^{\langle c_{j}^{\mu}\rangle}\mid\mu\in\varLambda_{n}(m),\,1\leq j\leq n\}.

We remark that the

7.
Shifted quantum affine algebras

In this section,
we review the definition and fundamental properties of shifted quantum affine algebras given in
[
FT19
]
.

Definition 7.1

(
[
FT19
, §5.1]
)
.

For
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{m-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}
,
we define the shifted quantum affine algebra

U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}=U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{m})
by the following generators and defining relations:

generators:

e_{i,t},\,f_{i,t},\,\psi_{i,s_{i}}^{+},\,(\psi^{+}_{i,-b_{i}})^{-1},\,\psi_{i,s}^{-},\,(\psi_{i,0}^{-})^{-1}

(
1\leq i\leq m-1
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
,
s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-b_{i}}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
)

defining relations:

(U1)

\displaystyle\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+}(\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+})^{-1}=1=(\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+})^{-1}\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+},\quad\psi_{i,0}^{-}(\psi_{i,0}^{-})^{-1}=1=(\psi_{i,0}^{-})^{-1}\psi_{i,0}^{-},

\displaystyle[\psi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z),\psi_{j}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(w)]=0\quad(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}),

(U2)

\displaystyle(z-q^{a_{ij}}w)e_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)=(q^{a_{ij}}z-w)e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z),

(U3)

\displaystyle(q^{a_{ij}}z-w)f_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)=(z-q^{a_{ij}}w)f_{j}(w)f_{i}(z),

(U4)

\displaystyle(z-q^{a_{ij}}w)\psi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z)e_{j}(w)=(q^{a_{ij}}z-w)e_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z)\quad(\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}),

(U5)

\displaystyle(q^{a_{ij}}z-w)\psi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z)f_{j}(w)=(z-q^{a_{ij}}w)f_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(z)\quad(\varepsilon\in\{\pm\}),

(U6)

\displaystyle[e_{i}(z),f_{j}(w)]=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{q-q^{-1}}\delta\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)(\psi_{i}^{+}(z)-\psi_{i}^{-}(z)),

(U7)

\displaystyle e_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)=e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z)\text{ if }j\not=i,i\pm 1,

\displaystyle e_{i\pm 1}(w)\big(e_{i}(z_{1})e_{i}(z_{2})+e_{i}(z_{2})e_{i}(z_{1})\big)+\big(e_{i}(z_{1})e_{i}(z_{2})+e_{i}(z_{2})e_{i}(z_{1})\big)e_{i\pm 1}(w)

\displaystyle\quad=[2]\big(e_{i}(z_{1})e_{i\pm 1}(w)e_{i}(z_{2})+e_{i}(z_{2})e_{i\pm 1}(w)e_{i}(z_{1})\big),

(U8)

\displaystyle f_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)=f_{j}(w)f_{i}(z)\text{ if }j\not=i,i\pm 1,

\displaystyle f_{i\pm 1}(w)\big(f_{i}(z_{1})f_{i}(z_{2})+f_{i}(z_{2})f_{i}(z_{1})\big)+\big(f_{i}(z_{1})f_{i}(z_{2})+f_{i}(z_{2})f_{i}(z_{1})\big)f_{i\pm 1}(w)

\displaystyle\quad=[2]\big(f_{i}(z_{1})f_{i\pm 1}(w)f_{i}(z_{2})+f_{i}(z_{2})f_{i\pm 1}(w)f_{i}(z_{1})\big),

where
(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m-1}
is the Cartan matrix of type
A_{m-1}
,
and we consider the generating series

\displaystyle e_{i}(z)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{i,t}z^{-t},\quad f_{i}(z)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i,t}z^{-t},

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{+}(z)=\sum_{t\geq-b_{i}}\psi_{i,t}^{+}z^{-t},\quad\psi_{i}^{-}(z)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\psi_{i,-t}^{-}z^{t},\quad\delta(z)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{t}.

Remark 7.2.
In the above definition,
we consider a shift of only the positive part.
In
[
FT19
, §5.1]
,
the definition of shifted quantum affine algebras are given for any shifts of both positive and negative parts.
However, they are isomorphic to the above algebras
U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}
for some
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}

(see the comments before remark 5.2 in
[
FT19
, §5.1]
).

7.3.
We define the elements
h_{i,\pm t}\in U_{q,[\mathbf{b}]}
(
1\leq i\leq m-1
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
) by

\displaystyle(\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+}z^{b_{i}})^{-1}\psi_{i}^{+}(z)=\exp\big((q-q^{-1})\sum_{t>0}h_{i,t}z^{-t}\big),

\displaystyle(\psi_{i,0}^{-})^{-1}\psi_{i}^{-}(z)=\exp\big(-(q-q^{-1})\sum_{t>0}h_{i,-t}z^{t}\big).

Then, the relations (U4) and (U5) are replaced by

(U4’)

\displaystyle\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+}e_{j,s}(\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+})^{-1}=q^{a_{ij}}e_{j,s},\quad\psi_{i,0}^{-}e_{j,s}(\psi_{i,0})^{-1}=q^{-a_{ij}}e_{j,s},\quad[h_{i,t},e_{j,s}]=\frac{[ta_{ij}]}{t}e_{j,s+t},

(U5’)

\displaystyle\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+}f_{j,s}(\psi_{i,-b_{i}}^{+})^{-1}=q^{-a_{ij}}f_{j,s},\quad\psi_{i,0}^{-}f_{j,s}(\psi_{i,0})^{-1}=q^{a_{ij}}f_{j,s},\quad[h_{i,t},f_{j,s}]=-\frac{[ta_{ij}]}{t}f_{j,s+t}

\displaystyle\psi_{i,s}^{+}-\psi_{i,s}^{-}=(q-q^{-1})[e_{i,s},f_{i,0}]\quad(-b_{i}\leq s\leq 0)

\psi_{i,s_{i}}^{+}

\psi_{i,0}^{-}

9.
Shifted loop Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the shifted loop Lie algebras.
Then, we see that the universal enveloping algebra of the shifted loop Lie algebra
can be regarded as the shifted quantum affine algebra at
q=1

by taking a certain form of the shifted quantum affine algebra.

We consider only the shifted loop Lie algebra associated with the special linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{m}
in this paper.
We can also consider the shifted loop Lie algebra for an arbitrary finite dimensional simple Lie algebra
in a similar manner.

Definition 9.1
.

For
\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m-1})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m-1}

and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m-1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m-1}
,
we define the shifted loop Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\bm{\eta},[\mathbf{b}]}=L_{[\mathbf{b}]}^{\bm{\eta}}\mathfrak{sl}_{m}

by the following generators and defining relations:

generators:

E_{i,t}
,
F_{i,t}
,
H_{i,t}

(1\leq i\leq m-1,\,t\in\mathbb{Z})

defining relations:

(L1)

\displaystyle[H_{i,t},H_{j,s}]=0,

(L2)

\displaystyle[H_{i,t},E_{j,s}]=a_{ij}E_{j,s+t},\quad[H_{i,t},F_{j,s}]=-a_{ij}F_{j,s+t},

(L3)

\displaystyle[E_{i,t},F_{j,s}]=\delta_{i,j}\big(H_{i,s+t}+\delta_{(b_{i}>0)}(-\eta_{i}^{-1})^{b_{i}}H_{i,s+t+b_{i}}\big),

(L4)

\displaystyle[E_{i,t},E_{j,s}]=[F_{i,t},F_{j,s}]=0\text{ if }j\not=i\pm 1,

\displaystyle[E_{i,t},E_{i\pm 1,s}]=[E_{i,t+1},E_{i\pm 1,s-1}],\quad[F_{i,t},F_{i\pm 1,s}]=[F_{i,t+1},F_{i\pm 1,s-1}],

(L5)

\displaystyle[E_{i,s},[E_{i,t},E_{i\pm 1,u}]]=[F_{i,s},[F_{i,t},F_{i\pm 1,u}]]=0.

Remark 9.2.
In the case where

\mathfrak{g}_{\bm{\eta},[0]}\cong L\mathfrak{sl}_{m}

9.3.
For
1\leq i,j\leq m

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,i}L_{i}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=i}^{j-2}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,p+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{p-1,i}L_{p+1}+(q-q^{-1})\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-2,i}L_{j}.

Then, the formula (
A.4.1
) implies that

\displaystyle(L_{j}-Q_{k})L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}

\displaystyle=(L_{j}-Q_{k})\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}

\displaystyle=\big\{\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}L_{1}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,p+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{p-1,1}L_{p+1}\big\}L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}-Q_{k}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}(L_{1}-Q_{k})L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,p+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{p-1,1}L_{p+1}L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}.

By using Lemma
2.4
(ii) and Lemma
2.11
,
we have

\displaystyle(L_{j}-Q_{k})L_{j}^{\langle k\rangle}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,1}L_{1}^{\langle k+1\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,j-1}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,p+1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{p-1,1}L_{1}^{\langle k\rangle}\mathbb{T}_{1,p-1}L_{p+1}\mathbb{T}_{p,j-1}

\displaystyle=L_{j}^{\langle k+1\rangle}+(q-q^{-1})\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{j-1,p+1}L_{p}^{\langle k\rangle}L_{p+1}\mathbb{T}_{p,j-1}.

Apply Lemma
2.10
(v) to the right-hand side,

.

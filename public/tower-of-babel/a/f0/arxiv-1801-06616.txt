Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
L
be a finitely generated extension field
of
k
.
L

(or

It is obvious that “

\Rightarrow

-rational”

\Rightarrow
“

The Lüroth problem asks, under what situation, the converse is true,
i.e. “

-unirational”
\Rightarrow
“

-rational”.
The Lüroth problem is a famous problem in algebraic geometry.
For a survey of it and related rationality problems
(e.g. Noether’s problem), see, for examples,
[

]
and
[

]
.

Throughout this paper, we assume, unless otherwise specified,
that
k
is a field with char
k\neq 2

and
k
is not an algebraically closed field.

Proof.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(x+\frac{b}{x}\right),\ t_{2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a}}\left(x-\frac{b}{x}\right),

Appendix: a geometric interpretation

After the first version of this paper posted in the arXiv,
J.-L. Colliot-Thélène gave the authors how
the presence of a smooth
k
-point implies the
k
-rationality
of
X

when a base field
k
is perfect infinite with char
k\neq 2
.

In this section, we assume that
a base field
k
is perfect infinite with char
k\neq 2
.
The intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbbm{P}^{n}
where
n
is an even integer
and
n\geq 4
was studied by Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer
in
[
CTSSD
]
by very powerful methods.
In particular, the theorem
[
CTSSD
, Theorem 2.4]

(see Theorem
4.1
below) is a convenient tool
to proving the
k
-rationality when a smooth
k
-point exists.
We will give an alternative proof of the equivalence of
(i), (ii), (iii), (iv), (v)
of Theorem
1.8
by using the theorem
[
CTSSD
, Theorem 2.4]
.

We first explain a terminology used in
[
CTSSD
]
.
Let
\overline{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
.
If
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
are linear subspaces of
\mathbbm{P}^{n}_{\overline{k}}
,
define
{\rm Span}\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}
to be the linear subspace
spanned projectively by
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
in
\mathbbm{P}^{n}_{\overline{k}}
.
The special case of
[
CTSSD
, Theorem 2.4]
when

n=4
may be formulated as follows.

Theorem 4.1

(Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
CTSSD
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

Let
k
be a perfect infinite field with
char

k\neq 2
,

X
be a projective surface in
\mathbbm{P}^{4}_{k}
and

\overline{X}:=X\times_{k}\overline{k}
such that
\overline{X}

is an integral scheme over
\overline{k}
, pure of dimension two,
and
X
is an intersection of two quadrics defined over
k
.
Assume that
X
contains a smooth
k
-point
P_{0}
and
\overline{X}

contains a pair of lines
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
satisfying that

(i)

\Pi_{1}\cap\Pi_{2}=\emptyset
,
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
are permuted by

{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
; and

(ii)

P_{0}\notin{\rm Span}\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}
,

{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}\not\subset\overline{X}
and

{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{2}\}\not\subset\overline{X}
.
Then
X
is
k
-rational.

Alternative proof of

{\rm(i)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(ii)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(iii)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(iv)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(v)}

of Theorem
1.8
when
k
is perfect infinite.

The implications
{\rm(i)}\Rightarrow{\rm(ii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(iii)}

are standard.
The equivalence
{\rm(iv)}\Leftrightarrow{\rm(v)}

follows from the same argument given in Section
2
.
We will give an alternative proof of

{\rm(iii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(v)}
and

{\rm(v)}\Rightarrow{\rm(i)}
.

Proof of
{\rm(iii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(v)}
of Theorem
1.8
.
———

Since
X
is
k
-unirational, there is a dominant rational map

f:\mathbbm{P}^{m}_{k}\dashrightarrow X
over
k

(
m
is some positive integer).
Because
k
is an infinite field,
the image of
f
contains infinitely many
k
-points in
X
.

Alternatively, these implications can be found in the proof
of
{\rm(iii)}\Rightarrow{\rm(v)}
of Theorem
1.8
,
because we have infinitely many choices of
a_{0},b_{0}\in k

with
h(a_{0},b_{0})\neq 0
.

Proof of
{\rm(v)}\Rightarrow{\rm(i)}
of Theorem
1.8
.
———

Let
P_{0}
be a smooth
k
-point on
X
.
Recall that
P_{0}
must lie on
X_{0}
and hence its
T_{5}
-coordinate is not zero
(see the first paragraph of Proof of Theorem
1.8
in Section
2
).
Define two lines
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
on
\overline{X}
as follows:

\Pi_{1}
is defined by the equations
T_{1}-\sqrt{a}T_{2}=T_{3}-\sqrt{a}T_{4}=T_{5}=0
,

\Pi_{2}
is defined by the equations
T_{1}+\sqrt{a}T_{2}=T_{3}+\sqrt{a}T_{4}=T_{5}=0
.
Clearly
\Pi_{1}\cap\Pi_{2}=\emptyset
and
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
are permuted by

{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
.
Moreover,
P_{0}\notin{\rm Span}\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}
,
because both
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
lie on the hyperplane
T_{5}=0
,
while
P_{0}
is a
k
-point on

whose
T_{5}
-coordinate is not zero.

Finally we will show that
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}\not\subset\overline{X}
.
Suppose that
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}\subset\overline{X}
.
We will find a contradiction.

Note that the projective subvarieties
\Pi_{1}
,
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}
,

\overline{X}
in
\mathbbm{P}^{4}_{\overline{k}}
are irreducible (integral).

Since
\Pi_{1}

,

dim
\Pi_{1}=1
.
On the other hand, as we suppose that

{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}\subset\overline{X}

we have dim
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}\leq

.
It follows that both dimensions of

are equal to

Thus
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{1}\}=\overline{X}

Consequently

By the same way,
{\rm Span}\{P_{0},\Pi_{2}\}\not\subset\overline{X}
.

Now we may apply Theorem

to conclude that

-rational.

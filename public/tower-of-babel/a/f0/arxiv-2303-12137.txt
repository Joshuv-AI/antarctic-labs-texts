Abstract
. For α > − 1 \alpha>-1 and 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty , we study weighted Bergman spaces ℬ α p \mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha} of harmonic functions on the real hyperbolic ball and obtain an atomic decomposition of these spaces in terms of reproducing kernels. We show that an r r -separated sequence { a m } \{a_{m}\} with sufficiently large r r is an interpolating sequence for ℬ α p \mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha} . Using these we determine precisely when a Bergman space ℬ α p \mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha} is included in another Bergman space ℬ β q \mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta} .

1.
Introduction

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\langle x,y\rangle=x_{1}y_{1}+\dots+x_{n}y_{n}
be
the Euclidean inner product and
\lvert x\rvert=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}

be the corresponding norm.
Let
\mathbb{B}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lvert x\rvert<1\}
be the
unit ball and
\mathbb{S}=\partial\mathbb{B}
be the unit sphere.
The hyperbolic ball is
\mathbb{B}
endowed with the hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}=\frac{4}{(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}^{2}.

The Laplacian
\Delta_{h}
and the gradient
\nabla^{h}
with respect
to the hyperbolic metric are given by (we refer the reader to

[
12
, Chapter 3]
for more details about
\Delta_{h}
and
\nabla^{h}
)

(\Delta_{h}f)(a)=\Delta(f\circ\varphi_{a})(0)\qquad(f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B})),

and

(\nabla^{h}f)(a)=-\nabla(f\circ\varphi_{a})(0)\qquad(f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{B})),

where
\Delta=\partial^{2}/\partial x_{1}^{2}+\dots+\partial^{2}/\partial x_{n}^{2}

and
\nabla=\big(\partial/\partial x_{1},\dots,\partial/\partial x_{n}\big)

are the usual Euclidean Laplacian and gradient.
Here
\varphi_{a}
is the canonical Möbius transformation mapping

\mathbb{B}
to
\mathbb{B}
and exchanging
a
and
0
given
in (
9
).
It is easy to show that

\Delta_{h}f(a)=(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{2}\Delta f(a)+2(n-2)(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})\langle a,\nabla f(a)\rangle,

and

(1)

\nabla^{h}f(a)=(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})\nabla f(a).

A twice continuously differentiable function

f\colon\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{C}
is called hyperbolic harmonic or

\mathcal{H}
-harmonic on
\mathbb{B}
if
\Delta_{h}f\equiv 0
.
We denote the space of all
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions
by
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B})
.

Let
\nu
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
normalized so
that
\nu(\mathbb{B})=1
.
For
\alpha>-1
, define the weighted measure
d\nu_{\alpha}(x)
by

d\nu_{\alpha}(x)=(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{\alpha}\,d\nu(x).

For
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
, we denote the Lebesgue space with
respect to
d\nu_{\alpha}
by
L^{p}_{\alpha}=L^{p}(d\nu_{\alpha})
.
The subspace
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
consisting of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
functions is called the weighted
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman space,

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}=\Bigl\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}):\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(x)<\infty\Bigr\}.

These are Banach spaces when
1\leq p<\infty
, and complete metric spaces
with respect to the metric
d(f,g)=\|f-g\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}
when
0<p<1
.

Point evaluation functionals are bounded on all
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}

and in particular
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.
Therefore for every
x\in\mathbb{B}
, there exists

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
such that

(2)

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(y)\overline{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(y)\qquad(f\in\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}).

The reproducing kernel
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric
in its variables, is real valued (so conjugation in (
2
)
can be deleted) and is
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic with respect to each variable.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{B}
, let
\rho(a,b)=\lvert\varphi_{a}(b)\rvert
be the
pseudo-hyperbolic metric, and for
0<r<1
, let

E_{r}(a)=\{x\in\mathbb{B}:\rho(x,a)<r\}
be the pseudo-hyperbolic ball.
For
0<r<1
, a sequence
\{a_{m}\}
of points of
\mathbb{B}
is called

r
-separated if
\rho(a_{k},a_{m})\geq r
when
k\neq m
.
An
r
-separated sequence
\{a_{m}\}
is called an
r
-lattice if

\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{r}(a_{m})=\mathbb{B}
, that is, if
\{a_{m}\}
is maximal.

In
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, it is shown by Coifman and Rochberg that
if
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r
-lattice with
r
sufficiently small, then every

holomorphic
Bergman function
f\in A^{p}
on the unit ball of

\mathbb{C}^{n}
(more generally on a symmetric Siegel domain of type two) can be
represented in the form
f(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}\tilde{B}(z,a_{m})
, where

\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
and
\tilde{B}(z,a_{m})
is determined by
B(\cdot,a_{m})
,
the reproducing kernel at the point
a_{m}
.
This representation is called atomic decomposition,
B(\cdot,a_{m})
being
the atoms.
They further showed that a similar decomposition holds also for
(Euclidean)
harmonic
functions on the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This last result is extended in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
to
harmonic Bergman spaces on bounded symmetric domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Our first aim in this work is to show that an if
\{a_{m}\}
is an

r
-lattice with small enough
r
, then an analogous series representation
in terms of the reproducing kernels holds also for
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
Bergman spaces
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.
For atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
Hardy
spaces
on the real hyperbolic ball, see
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
.
Pick
s
large enough to satisfy

(3)

\begin{split}\alpha+1&<p(s+1),\quad\text{if $p\geq 1$}\\
\alpha+n&<p(s+n),\quad\text{if $0<p<1$}.\end{split}

There is an
r_{0}<1/8
depending only on
n,\alpha,p,s

such that if
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r
-lattice with

r<r_{0}
, then for every
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
, there exists

\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
such that

(4)

f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B}),

where the series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}

and in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
, and the norm
\|\{\lambda_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}

is equivalent to the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

The decomposition above can be written in other forms.
Theorem
1.1
remains true if (
4
) is replaced with

(5)

f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}\frac{\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})}{\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(\cdot,a_{m})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B}).

This follows from the fact that

\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(\cdot,a_{m})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p-(s+n)}

by (
18
) below, and can be verified with only minor changes
in the proof given in Section
3
.
Also,
\mathcal{R}_{s}(a_{m},a_{m})\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(s+n)}

by (
17
), and (
4
) can be replaced with

f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})}{\mathcal{R}_{s}(a_{m},a_{m})}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B}).

We next consider the interpolation problem.
If
\{a_{m}\}
is
r
-separated and
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
, then
the sequence (see Proposition
3.3
below)

\big\{f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p}\big\}

is in
\ell^{p}
.
If the converse holds, that is, if for every
\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
,
one can find an
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
such that

f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p}=\lambda_{m}
,
then
\{a_{m}\}
is called an interpolating sequence for

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
.
We show that if the separation constant
r
is large enough,
then
\{a_{m}\}
is an interpolating sequence.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
.
There is an
r_{0}
with
1/2<r_{0}<1
depending only on
n,\alpha,p

such that if
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r
-separated
sequence with
r>r_{0}
, then for every
\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
, there
exists
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
such that

f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p}=\lambda_{m},

and the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}

is equivalent to the norm
\|\{\lambda_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}
.

The
holomorphic
analogue of the above theorem is proved in

[
2
]
for the unit ball and polydisc, and in
[
10
]
for
more general domains of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
For
harmonic
Bergman spaces on the upper half-space
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an analogous result is proved in
[
5
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
b^{p}_{\alpha}
be the weighted (Euclidean) harmonic Bergman spaces
on
\mathbb{B}
.
The proof of the interpolation theorem we give in Section
4

below works also for the harmonic case and Theorem
1.3
is true
when
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
replaced with
b^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Finally, we determine precisely when a Bergman space
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}

is contained in an another Bergman space
\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
and
0<p,q<\infty
.

(a)

If
q\geq p
, then

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{\alpha+n}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+n}{q}

(b)

If
q<p
, then

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\frac{\alpha+1}{p}<\frac{\beta+1}{q}

In both cases the inclusion

i\colon\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
is continuous.

For holomorphic Bergman spaces on the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
a similar theorem has been proved in
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
.
However this source uses gap series formed by using the so-called
Ryll-Wojtaszczyk polynomials (see
[
11
]
).
We do not know whether such type of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions
exist on the real hyperbolic ball.
Our proof is based on the above atomic decomposition and interpolation
theorems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some facts about Möbius
transformations and
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman spaces
that will be used in the sequel.
These are all known and we try to provide a suitable
reference.
If the proof is short we include it for the convenience
of the reader.

2.1.
Notation

We denote positive constants whose exact values are inessential
with
C
.
The value of
C
may differ from one occurrence to another.
We write
X\lesssim Y
if
X\leq CY
, and
X\sim Y
if both

X\leq CY
and
Y\leq CX
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

[x,y]:=\sqrt{1-2\langle x,y\rangle+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}}.

It is symmetric in its variables and the following equality holds

(6)

[x,y]^{2}=\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}+(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2}).

If either of the variables is
0
, then
[x,0]=[0,y]=1
; otherwise

(7)

[x,y]=\Bigl\lvert\lvert y\rvert x-\frac{y}{\lvert y\rvert}\Bigr\rvert=\Bigl\lvert\frac{x}{\lvert x\rvert}-\lvert x\rvert y\Bigr\rvert,

and so

(8)

1-\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert\leq[x,y]\leq 1+\lvert x\rvert\lvert y\rvert\qquad(x,y\in\mathbb{B}).

2.2.
Möbius transformations and pseudo-hyperbolic metric

For details about the facts listed in this subsection we refer
the reader to
[
1
]
or
[
12
]
.

A Möbius transformation of
\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
is a
finite composition of reflections (inversions) in spheres or planes.
We denote the group of all Möbius transformations
mapping
\mathbb{B}
to
\mathbb{B}
by
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{B})
.
For
a\in\mathbb{B}
, the mapping

(9)

\varphi_{a}(x)=\frac{a\lvert x-a\rvert^{2}+(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(a-x)}{[x,a]^{2}}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B})

is in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{B})
, exchanges
a
and
0
, and
satisfies
\varphi_{a}\circ\varphi_{a}=\text{Id}
.
The group
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{B})
is generated by

\{\varphi_{a}:a\in\mathbb{B}\}
and orthogonal transformations.
A very useful identity involving
\varphi_{a}
is
(
[
12
, Eqn. 2.1.7]
)

(10)

1-\lvert\varphi_{a}(x)\rvert^{2}=\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})}{[x,a]^{2}},

and the Jacobian
J_{\varphi_{a}}
satisfies (
[
12
, Theorem 3.3.1]
)

(11)

\lvert J_{\varphi_{a}}(x)\rvert=\frac{(1-\lvert\varphi_{a}(x)\rvert^{2})^{n}}{(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{n}}.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
a,x\in\mathbb{B}
, the following equality holds

[\varphi_{a}(x),a]=\frac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{[x,a]}.

Proof.

Replacing
x
in (
10
) with
\varphi_{a}(x)
and noting that

\varphi_{a}\circ\varphi_{a}=\text{Id}
shows

[\varphi_{a}(x),a]^{2}=\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert\varphi_{a}(x)\rvert^{2})}{1-\lvert x\rvert^{2}}.

Using (
10
) again gives the result.
∎

For
a,b\in\mathbb{B}
, the pseudo-hyperbolic metric

\rho(a,b)=\lvert\varphi_{a}(b)\rvert
satisfies

(12)

\rho(a,b)=\frac{\lvert a-b\rvert}{[a,b]},

by (
10
) and (
6
).
It is bounded
0\leq\rho(a,b)<1
, and is Möbius invariant
in the sense that
\rho(\psi(a),\psi(b))=\rho(a,b)

for every
\psi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{B})
.
It satisfies not only the triangle
inequality (
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.3]
), but the following strong
triangle inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
a,b,x\in\mathbb{B}
, the following inequalities hold

\frac{\big\lvert\rho(a,x)-\rho(b,x)\big\rvert}{1-\rho(a,x)\rho(b,x)}\leq\rho(a,b)\leq\frac{\rho(a,x)+\rho(b,x)}{1+\rho(a,x)\rho(b,x)}.

Proof.

By (
10
) and (
8
), we have

\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert b\rvert^{2})}{(1+\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert)^{2}}\leq 1-\lvert\varphi_{a}(b)\rvert^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert b\rvert^{2})}{(1-\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert)^{2}},

which after simplification implies

\frac{\big\lvert\lvert a\rvert-\lvert b\rvert\big\rvert}{1-\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}\leq\lvert\varphi_{a}(b)\rvert\leq\frac{\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert}{1+\lvert a\rvert\lvert b\rvert}.

Since
\rho(a,b)=\lvert\varphi_{a}(b)\rvert
and
\rho(a,0)=\lvert a\rvert
,

\rho(b,0)=\lvert b\rvert
, this proves the lemma when
x=0
.
To obtain the general case apply this special case with
a,b
replaced
by
\varphi_{x}(a),\varphi_{x}(b)
, write
0=\varphi_{x}(x)
, and
use the Möbius invariance of
\rho
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
,

1-\rho(x,y)\leq\frac{1-\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{[x,y]}\leq 1+\rho(x,y).

Proof.

The lemma clearly holds when
x=0
.
Otherwise, let
x^{*}:=x/\lvert x\rvert^{2}
be the inversion of

x
with respect to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}
.
Multiply the triangle inequality

\lvert x^{*}-y\rvert-\lvert y-x\rvert\leq\lvert x^{*}-x\rvert\leq\lvert x^{*}-y\rvert+\lvert y-x\rvert

with
\lvert x\rvert
.
Noting that

\lvert x\rvert\lvert x^{*}-y\rvert=[x,y]

by (
7
), and

\lvert x\rvert\lvert x^{*}-x\rvert=1-\lvert x\rvert^{2}
,
we deduce

[x,y]-\lvert x\rvert\lvert y-x\rvert\leq 1-\lvert x\rvert^{2}\leq[x,y]+\lvert x\rvert\lvert y-x\rvert.

The lemma follows from the facts that

\lvert y-x\rvert=\rho(x,y)[x,y]
by (
12
),
and
\lvert x\rvert<1
.
∎

The following lemma is a slight modification of
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
and
immediately follows from Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
,

\frac{1-\rho(x,y)}{1+\rho(x,y)}\leq\frac{1-\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert y\rvert^{2}}\leq\frac{1+\rho(x,y)}{1-\rho(x,y)}.

The next lemma is proved in
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
a,x,y\in\mathbb{B}
,

\frac{1-\rho(x,y)}{1+\rho(x,y)}\leq\frac{[x,a]}{[y,a]}\leq\frac{1+\rho(x,y)}{1-\rho(x,y)}.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lvert x\rvert<r\}
.
The pseudo-hyperbolic ball

E_{r}(a)=\{x\in\mathbb{B}:\rho(x,a)<r\}=\varphi_{a}(\mathbb{B}_{r})
is
also a Euclidean ball with (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.2]
)

(13)

\text{center}=\frac{(1-r^{2})a}{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}r^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{radius}=\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})r}{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}r^{2}}.

The measure
d\tau(x)=(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{-n}d\nu(x)

is the invariant measure on
\mathbb{B}
and
the volume
\tau(E_{r}(a))=\tau(\mathbb{B}_{r})
is independent
of
a\in\mathbb{B}
.

2.3.
Separated sequences and lattices

A sequence
\{a_{m}\}
of points of
\mathbb{B}
is called

r
-separated if
\rho(a_{k},a_{m})\geq r
for every
k\neq m
.
If, in addition,
\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{r}(a_{m})=\mathbb{B}
, then

\{a_{m}\}
is called an
r
-lattice.
As is explained in
[
6
, p. 18]
, for every
0<r<1
, there
exists an
r
-lattice and every
r
-separated sequence can be
completed to an
r
-lattice.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{a_{m}\}
be an
r
-lattice.
There exists a sequence
\{E_{m}\}
of disjoint sets such that

\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{m}=\mathbb{B}
and

(14)

E_{r/2}(a_{m})\subset E_{m}\subset E_{r}(a_{m}).

Proof.

Let
E_{1}=E_{r}(a_{1})\backslash\bigcup_{m=2}^{\infty}E_{r/2}(a_{m})

and given
E_{1},\dots,E_{m-1}
, let

E_{m}=E_{r}(a_{m})\backslash\biggl(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m-1}E_{i}\ \bigcup\bigcup_{i=m+1}^{\infty}E_{r/2}(a_{m})\biggr).\qed

The next lemma follows from a standard invariant volume argument.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0<r,\delta<1
and
\{a_{m}\}
be
r
-separated.
There exists
N
depending only on
n,r,\delta
such that
every
x\in\mathbb{B}
belongs to at most
N
of the balls

E_{\delta}(a_{m})
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in\mathbb{B}
belongs to
N_{x}
of the balls
E_{\delta}(a_{m})
.
Set

s:=\frac{\delta+r/2}{1+\delta r/2}<1,

where the last inequality follows from
(1-\delta)(1-r/2)>0
.
If
\rho(x,a_{m})<\delta
, then by Lemma
2.2
,

E_{r/2}(a_{m})\subset E_{x}(s)
.
Since the balls
E_{r/2}(a_{m})
are disjoint and

\tau
is the invariant measure, it follows that

N_{x}\,\tau(\mathbb{B}_{r/2})\leq\tau(\mathbb{B}_{s})
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<r<1
.

(a)

If
\{a_{m}\}
is
r
-separated and
\gamma>n-1
, then

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma}<\infty
.

(b)

If
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r
-lattice, then

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma}<\infty
if and only if

\gamma>n-1
.

Proof.

To see part (a), we note that

(15)

\int_{E_{r/2}(a_{m})}(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\gamma-n}\,d\nu(y)\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma},

where the implied constants depend only on the fixed parameters

n,\gamma,r
and are independent of
a_{m}
.
This is true because for
y\in E_{r/2}(a_{m})
, we have

(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})
by
Lemma
2.4
and

\nu(E_{r/2}(a_{m}))\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{n}
by (
13
).
Since the balls
E_{r/2}(a_{m})
are disjoint, we obtain that

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{B}}(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\gamma-n}\,d\nu(y).

If
\gamma>n-1
, then the above integral is finite.

For part (b), let
E_{m}
be as given in Lemma
2.6
.
By (
14
), we similarly have

(16)

\int_{E_{m}}(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\gamma-n}\,d\nu(y)\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma}

and therefore

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\gamma}\sim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\gamma-n}\,d\nu(y)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\gamma-n}\,d\nu(y).

The last integral is finite if and only if
\gamma>n-1
.
∎

The following upper estimates of the reproducing kernels

For

On the diagonal

The following estimate of the integrals of powers of the

If

For

for suitable

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

and

Proof.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b) follows from part (a) of Lemma
2.9
,
(b)
\Rightarrow
(a) is clear,
(a)
\Rightarrow
(d) is proved in
[

, Theorem 1.1]

∎

We denote by

\mathbb{S}

Lemma 2.11

Let

3.
Atomic Decomposition

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
.
The main problem is to show that the operator
U
defined in
(
20
) below is onto under the assumptions of the theorem and
we do this through a couple of propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
, choose
s
so that (
3
) holds
and let
0<r<1
.
If
\{a_{m}\}
is
r
-separated, then the operator

U\colon\ell^{p}\to\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
mapping
\lambda=\{\lambda_{m}\}
to

(20)

U\lambda(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B})

is bounded.
The above series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}
,
and also in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof we suppress the constants that depend on the
fixed parameters
n,\alpha,p,s
and
r
.

We begin with the case
0<p\leq 1
.
We first show that for
\lambda\in\ell^{p}
, the series in (
20
)
converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}
which
will imply that
U\lambda
is
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic on
\mathbb{B}

since each
\mathcal{R}_{s}(\cdot,a_{m})
is
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic.
By Lemma
2.9
(a), if
\lvert x\rvert\leq R<1
, then

\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert\lesssim 1
since
[x,a_{m}]\geq 1-\lvert x\rvert

by (
8
) and using also Lemma
2.12
(i) we deduce

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\\
\leq\Bigl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{p(s+n)-(\alpha+n)}\Bigr)^{1/p}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p},

where in the last inequality we use
p(s+n)-(\alpha+n)>0
by (
3
).

To show
\|U\lambda\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|\lambda\|_{\ell^{p}}
, we again
use Lemma
2.12
(i) and obtain

\displaystyle\|U\lambda\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\biggl\lvert\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\biggr\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{p(s+n)-(\alpha+n)}\int_{\mathbb{B}}\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p},

where the last inequality follows from (
18
) by (
3
).
This also shows that the series in (
20
) converges
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

We now consider the case
1<p<\infty
.
Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
p
.
The series in (
20
) converges absolutely and uniformly
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}
because we again have

\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert\lesssim 1
and by Hölder’s inequality,

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\leq\|\{\lambda_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{p^{\prime}(s+n-(\alpha+n)/p)}\Bigr)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

The last sum is finite by Lemma
2.8
since the inequality

p^{\prime}(s+n-(\alpha+n)/p)>n-1
is equivalent to (
3
).
Thus
U\lambda
is
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic on
\mathbb{B}
.

To show
\|U\lambda\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|\lambda\|_{\ell^{p}}
,
following
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
, we use the projection
theorem.
Let

g(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\chi_{E_{r/2}(a_{m})}(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{B}),

where

E_{r/2}(a_{m})
.
We have
\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\sim\|\lambda\|_{\ell^{p}}
since

\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(\alpha+n)}\nu_{\alpha}(E_{r/2}(a_{m}))\sim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{p}.

Next, with

as in (

\displaystyle Q_{s}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{g(y)\,d\nu_{s}(y)}{[x,y]^{s+n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(\alpha+n)/p}\int_{E_{r/2}(a_{m})}\frac{(1-|y\rvert^{2})^{s}}{[x,y]^{s+n}}\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}}{[x,a_{m}]^{s+n}},

where in the last line we use the fact that

for

2.5
,
and then (

This shows that

by Lemma
2.9
(a).
Since

is bounded by Lemma

we conclude

\|U\lambda\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|Q_{s}g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\sim\|\lambda\|_{\ell^{p}}.\qed

To verify that the above operator

is
onto under the additional assumption that

is an
r
-lattice

small enough, we need to consider a second operator.

.

Let

Then for all

and all

where

if

and

if

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
0<p<\infty
,
0<r<1
and
\{a_{m}\}
be
r
-separated.
The operator

(21)

Tf=\big\{f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p}\big\}

Applying Lemma
3.2
with

and noting that

(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})
for

\lvert f(a_{m})\rvert^{p}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\alpha+n}\lesssim\int_{E_{r/2}(a_{m})}\lvert f(y)\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(y).

Since

are disjoint, we deduce

\|Tf\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert f(a_{m})\rvert^{p}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{\alpha+n}\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{r/2}(a_{m})}\lvert f(y)\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(y)\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}^{p}.\qed

We need a slightly modified version of the above operator
T
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
, choose
s
so that (
3
) holds
and let
0<r<1
.
If
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r

\{E_{m}\}
is the associated sequence
as given in Lemma

, then the operator

\hat{T}\colon\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\to\ell^{p}

\hat{T}f=\bigl\{f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p-(s+n)}\,\nu_{s}(E_{m})\bigr\}.

is bounded.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
3.3
since by (
16
),

\nu_{s}(E_{m})\sim(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
0<p<\infty
, choose
s
so that (
3
) holds.
There exists a constant
C>0
depending only on

such that
if
\{a_{m}\}
is an
r

, then

\|I-U\hat{T}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\leq Cr
.

In the proofs of the previous propositions the constants
depended on the separation constant
r
.
In the proof of Proposition
3.5
, we will be careful
that the suppressed constants are independent of
r
.
However we will frequently use the fact that

is bounded above by
1/8
.
We prove the cases
p\geq 1
and

separately.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
when
p\geq 1
.

Writing
\nu_{s}(E_{m})=\int_{E_{m}}d\nu_{s}(y)
, by (
20
)
and (
22
),

U\hat{T}f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}f(a_{m})\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\,d\nu_{s}(y).

By (
3
) and Lemma
2.10
we have
P_{s}f=f
and so

f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}f(y)\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)\,d\nu_{s}(y).

Combining these shows

(23)

\begin{split}(I-U\hat{T})f(x)&=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\bigr)f(y)\,d\nu_{s}(y)\\
&+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\big(f(y)-f(a_{m})\big)\,d\nu_{s}(y)=:h_{1}(x)+h_{2}(x).\end{split}

We first estimate
h_{1}
.
Let
y\in E_{m}
.
By the mean value theorem of calculus, there exists
\tilde{y}
lying
on the line segment joining

a_{m}
and
y
such that

\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})=\big\langle\,y-a_{m},\nabla_{2}\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,\tilde{y})\,\big\rangle,

where
\nabla_{2}
means the gradient is taken with respect to the
second variable.
Observe that, because
r
is bounded above by
1/8
, there are
constants independent of
r
such that for
y\in E_{m}\subset E_{r}(a_{m})
,
by Lemma
2.5

[y,a_{m}]\sim[y,y]=1-\lvert y\rvert^{2}
.
Thus by (

\lvert y-a_{m}\rvert=\rho(y,a_{m})[y,a_{m}]<r[y,a_{m}]\lesssim r(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2}).

Next, since
a_{m}
and
y
are both in the ball
E_{r}(a_{m})
,
so is
\tilde{y}
.
Hence
\rho(y,\tilde{y})<1/4
and by Lemma
2.5

[x,y]\sim[x,\tilde{y}]

for every
x\in\mathbb{B}
with the constants again not depending on
r
.
Therefore by Lemma
2.9
(b) and the
symmetry of
\mathcal{R}_{s}(\cdot,\cdot)

\big\lvert\nabla_{2}\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,\tilde{y})\big\rvert\lesssim\frac{1}{[x,\tilde{y}]^{s+n+1}}\sim\frac{1}{[x,y]^{s+n+1}}.

Combining these we see that for
y\in E_{m}
and
x\in\mathbb{B}

(24)

\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert\lesssim\frac{r(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})}{[x,y]^{s+n+1}}\lesssim\frac{r}{[x,y]^{s+n}},

where in the last inequality we use

Thus

\lvert h_{1}(x)\rvert\lesssim r\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}\frac{\lvert f(y)\rvert}{[x,y]^{s+n}}\,d\nu_{s}(y)=r\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\lvert f(y)\rvert}{[x,y]^{s+n}}\,d\nu_{s}(y)=rQ_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(x),

\|h_{1}\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim r\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}

We now estimate

.
Let
y\in E_{m}
.
As above, we have
P_{s}f=f

Since

\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(y,z)-\mathcal{R}_{s}(a_{m},z)\rvert\lesssim\frac{r}{[y,z]^{s+n}},

for all

.

\lvert f(y)-f(a_{m})\rvert\lesssim r\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{\lvert f(z)\rvert}{[y,z]^{s+n}}\,d\nu_{s}(z)=rQ_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(y),

\lvert h_{2}(x)\rvert\lesssim r\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}\lvert\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})\rvert\,Q_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(y)\,d\nu_{s}(y)\lesssim r\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{m}}\frac{Q_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(y)}{[x,a_{m}]^{s+n}}\,d\nu_{s}(y),

where in the last inequality we use Lemma
2.9
(a).
By Lemma
2.4
again, we have
[x,a_{m}]\sim[x,y]
for

since

.
Hence

\lvert h_{2}(x)\rvert\lesssim r\int_{\mathbb{B}}\frac{Q_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(y)}{[x,y]^{s+n}}\,d\nu_{s}(y)=rQ_{s}\circ Q_{s}(\lvert f\rvert)(x),

and it follows from Lemma
2.10

\|h_{2}\|_{L^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim r\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

We conclude that

\|(I-U\hat{T})f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}\leq Cr\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}
,
with

depending only on

.
This finishes the proof when

∎

\displaystyle(I-U\hat{T})f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\kappa_{m}}\int_{E_{mk}}\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{mk})\bigr)f(y)\,d\nu_{s}(y)

Let

First, as is shown in (

Lemma 4.3

Let

(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{c}\int_{\mathbb{B}\backslash E_{r}(a)}\,\frac{(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{b}}{[a,y]^{n+b+c}}\,d\nu(y)<\varepsilon.

Proof.

The change of variable

with the facts that

\varphi_{a}(\mathbb{B}_{r})=E_{r}(a)

\lvert J_{\varphi_{a}}\rvert

K:=(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{c}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}\backslash E_{r}(a)}\frac{(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{b}d\nu(y)}{[a,y]^{n+b+c}}=(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{c}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{r}}\frac{(1-\lvert\varphi_{a}(z)\rvert^{2})^{b+n}\,d\nu(z)}{[a,\varphi_{a}(z)]^{n+b+c}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{n}}.

Applying (

and

K=\int_{\mathbb{B}\backslash\mathbb{B}_{r}}\frac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{b}}{[a,z]^{n+b-c}}\,d\nu(z)=n\int_{r}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t^{2})^{b}\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{\lvert ta-\zeta\rvert^{n+b-c}}\,dt,

where in the second equality we integrate in polar coordinates and

[a,t\zeta]=\lvert ta-\zeta\rvert
by (

By Lemma
2.11
and the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{S}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{\lvert ta-\zeta\rvert^{n+b-c}}\leq Cg(t):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{1+b-c}},&\text{if $1+b-c>0$};\\
1+\log\dfrac{1}{1-t^{2}},&\text{if $1+b-c=0$};\\
1,&\text{if $1+b-c<0$},\end{cases}

where the constant

depends only on

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
b>n-1
and
c>0
.
For
1/2<r<1
, there exists
C(r)>0
(depending also on

and
c

such that if
\{a_{m}\}
is
r
-separated, then

(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\lvert a_{k}\rvert^{2})^{b}}{[a_{m},a_{k}]^{b+c}}\leq C(r),

for every
m=1,2,\dots
.
Moreover, one can choose
C(r)
to be arbitrarily small by making
r

sufficiently close to
1
.

Proof.

By the Lemmas
2.4
,
2.5
and (
13
),
there exists
C>0
depending only on
n,b,c
such that

\frac{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{b}}{[x,a]^{b+c}}\leq C\int_{E_{1/4}(a)}\frac{(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{b-n}}{[x,y]^{b+c}}\,d\nu(y),

for all
a,x\in\mathbb{B}
.
If
\{a_{m}\}
is
r
-separated with
r>1/2
, then the balls
E_{1/4}(a_{m})

are disjoint and therefore

(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\lvert a_{k}\rvert^{2})^{b}}{[a_{m},a_{k}]^{b+c}}\leq C(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{c}\int_{\bigcup^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}E_{1/4}(a_{k})}\frac{(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{b-n}}{[a_{m},y]^{b+c}}\,d\nu(y).

Observe next that

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{mk}\gamma_{m}^{p}=(1-\lvert a_{k}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq k\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\,\frac{(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\alpha+n)/p+(n-1)/p^{\prime}}}{[a_{m},a_{k}]^{s+n}}.

To apply Lemma
4.4
we check that

(\alpha+n)/p+(n-1)/p^{\prime}=(\alpha+1)/p+n-1>n-1
, and

s+n-\bigl((\alpha+n)/p+(n-1)/p^{\prime}\bigr)=s+1-(\alpha+1)/p>0

by (
3
).
Thus there exists
C_{2}(r)
such that

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{mk}\gamma_{m}^{p}\leq(1-\lvert a_{k}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\frac{C_{2}(r)}{(1-\lvert a_{k}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p-(n-1)/p^{\prime}}}=C_{2}(r)\gamma_{k}^{p}.

We deduce that
L_{A}
is bounded and
\|L_{A}\|\leq\max\{C_{1}(r),C_{2}(r)\}
.
Therefore
\|T\hat{U}-I\|\leq C\max\{C_{1}(r),C_{2}(r)\}
and
since both
C_{1}(r)
and
C_{2}(r)
can be made arbitrarily small by making

r
close enough to
1
, we conclude that

\|T\hat{U}-I\|
can be made small.
This finishes the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Let
R_{\alpha}(x,y)
be the reproducing kernel of the (Euclidean)
harmonic Bergman space
b^{p}_{\alpha}
.
In the above proof of the interpolation theorem, the only facts we
used about the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic reproducing kernels

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
are the estimates given in
Lemma
2.9
, (
17
) and (
18
).
However, it is well-known that exactly same estimates hold also for
the kernels
R_{\alpha}
.
It is also well-known that a sub-mean value inequality similar to
Lemma
3.2
holds for harmonic functions.
Thus, the above proof, without any change, works also in the harmonic
case and the interpolation theorem is valid if
B^{p}_{\alpha}

is replaced with
b^{p}_{\alpha}
.

5.
Inclusion Relations

In this section we prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first prove part (a).
Suppose
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
.
Since point evaluations are bounded on Bergman spaces, by the
closed graph theorem, the inclusion

i\colon\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
is continuous.
For every
s>-1
and
a\in\mathbb{B}
, the function

\mathcal{R}_{s}(a,\cdot)
is bounded on
\mathbb{B}
by
Lemma
2.9
(a) and (
8
), so belongs to every
Bergman space.
By (
18
), for large enough
s
, we have

\frac{\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(a,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}}}{\|\mathcal{R}_{s}(a,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}}\sim(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)/q-(\alpha+n)/p},

and the right-hand side is bounded as
\lvert a\rvert\to 1^{-}

only if
(\beta+n)/q\geq(\alpha+n)/p
.

Suppose now that

(36)

\frac{\alpha+n}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+n}{q}.

Pick
s
large enough so that (
3
) holds both for

\alpha,p
and
\beta,q
.
Let
\{a_{m}\}
be an
r
-lattice with
r<r_{0}
, where

r_{0}
is as asserted in the atomic decomposition theorem.
Then for every
f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
, there exists

\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
with

\|\{\lambda_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}\sim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}

such that

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\alpha+n)/p}\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)/q-(\alpha+n)/p}\,(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{s+n-(\beta+n)/q}\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(x,a_{m}).

The sequence

\{\beta_{m}\}=\bigl\{\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)/q-(\alpha+n)/p}\bigr\}

is also in
\ell^{p}
by (
36
) and so
\{\beta_{m}\}\in\ell^{q}
by
Lemma
2.12
(i).
It follows from Proposition
3.1
that
f\in\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}

and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|\{\beta_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{q}}\leq\|\{\beta_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\|\{\lambda_{m}\}\|_{\ell^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

We next prove part (b).
Note first that in this case
p/q>1
and the conjugate
exponent of
p/q
is
p/(p-q)
.

To see the if part, suppose

(37)

\frac{\alpha+1}{p}<\frac{\beta+1}{q}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{B}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{q}\,d\nu_{\beta}(x)\leq\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}\,d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\biggr)^{q/p}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{B}}(1-\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{(\beta-\alpha\frac{q}{p})\frac{p}{p-q}}\,d\nu(x)\biggr)^{(p-q)/p},

and since the exponent
(\beta-\alpha\frac{q}{p})\frac{p}{p-q}>-1
by (
37
),
we obtain
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}}
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
.
Let
r_{0}
be as asserted in the interpolation theorem
for
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
and let
\{a_{m}\}
be an

r
-lattice with
r>r_{0}
.
Then for every
\{\lambda_{m}\}\in\ell^{p}
there exists a function

f\in\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
such that

f(a_{m})=\lambda_{m}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{-(\alpha+n)/p}.

Since
f
is also in
\mathcal{B}^{q}_{\beta}
the sequence

\{f(a_{m})(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)/q}\}
is in
\ell^{q}

by Proposition
3.3
, and so

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\lambda_{m}\rvert^{q}(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)-(\alpha+n)q/p}<\infty.

This shows that the series

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lvert\beta_{m}\rvert(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)-(\alpha+n)q/p}
converges
for every
\{\beta_{m}\}\in\ell^{p/q}
.
By the duality in Lemma
2.12
(ii), we conclude that the
sequence
\{(1-\lvert a_{m}\rvert^{2})^{(\beta+n)-(\alpha+n)q/p}\}

is in
\ell^{p/(p-q)}
and by Lemma
2.8
(b), this holds
only if

\Bigl((\beta+n)-(\alpha+n)\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)\frac{p}{p-q}>n-1,

which is equivalent to (
37
).
∎

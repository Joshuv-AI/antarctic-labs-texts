Abstract
. In this paper we develop a geometric framework to address asymptoticity and nonexpansivity in topological dynamics when the acting group is second countable and locally compact. As an application, we show extensions of Schwartzman’s theorem in this context. Also, we get new results when the acting groups is ℤ d {\mathbb{Z}}^{d} : any half-space of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} contains a vector defining a (oriented) nonexpansive direction in the sense of Boyle and Lind. Finally, we deduce rigidity properties of distal Cantor systems.

Introduction

Let

be a self-homeomorphism of an infinite compact metric space

-asymptotic pairs. Considering

\epsilon
-asymptotic pairs

This means that there are two different points that are

n\leq 0

. Another way to state the same result is by saying that in an infinite compact metric space it is impossible to construct positively expansive homeomorphisms. This result has been recovered independently and in different flavours by several authors (see for instance

Motivated by the study of subactions of higher rank abelian actions, M. Boyle and D. Lind obtained in
[
7
]
a major result in the spirit of Schwarztman’s theorem. They introduced the notion of

along a subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, by considering only the action of elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
that lie within a given bounded distance of the subspace. They showed (see also
[
12
]
) that for an infinite compact metric space

, there exist a half-space
H\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and different points
x,y
in
X
that stay arbitrarily close along their

-orbit (see Section
5
). Such half-space is called

since the action of its border is nonexpansive.

Let us point out that unlike Schwartzman’s result, where the two half-spaces of
{\mathbb{Z}}
are nonexpansive, here only one nonexpansive half-space is guaranteed to exist. Actually, not any half-space is nonexpansive for an arbitrary
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
system. Explicit examples like Ledrappier’s
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
subshift illustrate that the set of such half-spaces can even be finite. A motivation for studying the set of nonexpansive half-spaces is that many dynamical properties of the subactions vary nicely along the connected components of the set of expansive directions and a bifurcation phenomenon may occur when passing from one component to another.

To complete their result, Boyle and Lind, and later M. Hochman, realized any nonempty closed set of half-spaces as the set of nonexpansive ones of some
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-action. In particular, Hochman succeeded in realizing the challenging case where this set consists of a single half-space whose border line has an irrational slope
[
17
]
.

The notions of nonexpansive half-spaces and asymptotic points have turned out to be the key notions to tackle various problems. For instance, in dynamics, they appear as fundamental objects for studying expansive maps (e.g.,
[
21
]
), their centralizer and cellular automata (e.g.,
[
25

8
]

[
19

). For
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions, they are main notions to explore directional invariants like directional entropy (e.g.,
[
26

). They also appear in combinatorics when addressing the long standing Nivat conjecture (e.g.,
[
11
]
). Despite the breadth of possible applications, no similar concept has been proposed for general group actions.

The definition and the proofs in
[
7
]
seem to rely heavily on the linear structure of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, so extensions of these results to general group actions were open for a long time. In this work, we develop a framework using basic elements of geometric group theory that allows us to give a definition of nonexpansiveness for actions of countable groups and more generally for second countable and locally compact topological groups.
The notion of half-space is replaced by that of horoball (see
Section

2
for the precise definition). Horoballs are sublevel sets of horofunctions, which are the analogous to linear forms on general metric spaces. They depend on the choice of a proper right-invariant distance on the acting group. We warn the reader that, depending on the group and the metric, horoballs can contain arbitrary large balls, like in the case of finitely generated groups, or can even be empty, like in the case of infinite sum of finite groups (see
2.1
and the discussion around it). Anyway, using this framework, we show the following extension of Boyle and Lind’s results, which is meaningful for a large class of groups including the finitely generated ones.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,T,G)
be an infinite topological dynamical system and
G
be an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
there exist different points
x,y\in X
and a horoball
H
such that
{\rm dist}(T_{g}x,T_{g}y)\leq\epsilon
for all
g\in H
.

Actually,
1.1
is derived from the following more general theorem, which we believe is of independent interest and might be useful for other problems in topological dynamics.

Theorem 1.2

(Robinson Crusoe theorem)
.

Let
(X,T,G)
be a topological dynamical system and
G
be an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance. Let
O\subsetneq X
be an open, not closed and
G
-invariant (
T_{g}(O)=O,\forall g\in G
) subset of
X
. Then, for any neighborhood
U
of the boundary of
O
there exists a horoball
H
in
G
such that

O\cap\bigcap_{g\in H}T_{g}^{-1}(U)\neq\emptyset.

We name this theorem Robinson Crusoe because of the following interpretation. Assume that Robinson Crusoe is isolated on an island and his movement is deterministic, i.e., given by a
{\mathbb{R}}
-flow. The island defines a
{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant open set
O
in the phase space. The theorem claims that there exists a trajectory of Robinson Crusoe that stays on the beach (the border of the island) for all positive (or negative) times. This can be seen as a containment result:
O
is not repulsed by its border for positive or negative times.

From
1.2
we deduce in
4.3
the existence of
\epsilon
-asymptotic pairs relatively to a factor map, and from this
1.1
follows immediately. When
G={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is endowed with the Euclidean metric, we recover the theorem of Boyle and Lind on the existence of nonexpansive half-spaces.

Pursuing extensions of Schwartzman’s result, we also provide a directed version of Robinson Crusoe theorem where the set of horoballs can be restricted to a smaller family, as long as the action satisfies a non-repulsive condition along a semi-group (
3.4
). Although this technical condition restricts the actions, the family of systems that satisfy it is rather broad. We deduce in
4.4
a directed version of asymptotic pairs for infinite groups admitting a proper right-invariant distance such that the inversion map
g\mapsto g^{-1}
is an isometry. As a consequence, for
G={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
we obtain the following new restriction on the nonexpansive half-spaces of a given action (see
5.6
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,T,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
be an infinite topological dynamical system.
Then, the intersection of all its nonexpansive open half-spaces is empty.

Whereas
1.1
states that there exists at least one nonexpansive half-space for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions,
1.3
establishes that the half-space cannot be unique. In particular, when
d=1
, these recover Schwartzman’s result. Thus, Theorems

and
1.3
constitute the first known common generalization of Schwartzman’s and Boyle and Lind’s results.

Finally, we apply Robinson Crusoe theorem to Cantor dynamics to get restrictions on distal systems and distal factors for a class of groups containing finitely generated groups: they have to be equicontinuous (
6.2
). This recovers results in
[
4
]
and in
[
23
]
for subshifts.

Proposition 2.1

Let

\inf_{g\in G}j(g)=-\infty

Proof.

Let

To prove the second statement of the lemma, assume that

3.
Robinson Crusoe theorem

In this section we prove our main abstract theorems that will allow us in the next sections to establish some extensions of Schwartzman’s theorem and other applications.
We recall that a
topological dynamical system

(X,T,G)
is given by a continuous left-action
T\colon G\times X\to X
of a group

on a compact metric space

equipped with a distance
{\rm dist}
. This provides a family of self-homeomorphisms
\{T_{g}:g\in G\}
of

such that
T_{g}\circ T_{h}=T_{gh}
for any
g,h\in G
and the maps
T_{g}
depend continuously (for the uniform topology) on the element
g

is

(or simply

when there is no ambiguity) if

for all
g\in G

The next theorem is

from the introduction.

Theorem 3.1

(Robinson Crusoe theorem)
.

Let
(X,T,G)
be a topological dynamical system and
G
be an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance. Let

be an open, not closed and
G
-invariant (

) subset of

. Then, for any neighborhood
U
of the boundary of

there exists a horofunction

such that its associated horoball
H
in
G
satisfies

Equivalently,

states that for any closed invariant subset

which remains close to

under the action along a horoball

Notice that the distance (or even the semi-distance) on the group

can be chosen independently of the action.
The theorem is worthwhile for a distance where any horoball is large, like in the
\ell_{1}
distance in a finitely generated group.

The idea of the proof for
G={\mathbb{Z}}
is as follows. By contradiction, assume no backward or forward
orbit stays in a neighborhood of the border of

. A compactness argument provides a finitary version as follows: there exists a constant

such that no point in

can have

\partial O
. Since

is not closed and invariant, any point close enough to the border

will then contradict the constant

. The proof for the general case is similar but by considering the compact set of horoballs instead of the half-spaces
(0,+\infty)
or
(-\infty,0)
. The geometrical fact that the horoballs are locally tangent to a ball at the identity (
2.3
) helps to go from the finitary version to the infinite one.

Observe that the claim is obvious if

This allows defining the non-negative constant

.

4.
Extensions of Schwartzman theorem

In this section we apply the Robinson Crusoe theorems to get several extensions of a classical result due to Schwartzman in his Ph.D. Thesis
[
28
]
to general group actions. Recall that Schwartzman’s theorem states that given a homeomorphism
T\colon X\to X
of an infinite compact metric space
X
and
\epsilon>0
there exist positive (resp. negative)
\epsilon
-asymptotic pairs. That is, different points
x,y\in X
such that
{\rm dist}(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)\leq\epsilon
for all
n\geq 0
(resp.
n\leq 0
).
This is equivalent to say that there is no positively (or negatively) expansive homeomorphisms on an infinite compact space.
This result can be seen as a version in topological dynamics of the classical Morse-Hedlund’s theorem on the complexity of an infinite subshift
[
24
]
.

A crucial issue in extending Schwartzman’s theorem to general group actions is to define a good notion of asymptoticity that extends the one of asymptotic pair for
{\mathbb{Z}}
-symbolic systems. We propose a notion using the elements of geometric group theory that we developed in the last sections.
We recall that a factor map between topological dynamical systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
is a continuous and onto map
\pi\colon X\to Y
such that
S_{g}\circ\pi=\pi\circ T_{g}
for all
g\in G
.

Definition 4.1
.

For two dynamical systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
, a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
,
\epsilon>0
and a horoball
H
, we call an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair relative to
\pi
any pair
(x,y)
of two different points
x,y\in X
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
and
{\rm dist}(T_{g}x,T_{g}y)\leq\epsilon
for all
g\in H
.

When the system
Y
is trivial we simply say that
(x,y)
is an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair.

We might naively expect from Schwartzman’s theorem that any group action admits
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pairs for each horoball
H
. But this is false even for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-actions, as shown by Ledrappier’s shift example.

Example 4.2
.

(Ledrappier’s example)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

and the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-action generated by the horizontal and vertical shifts.
If

is not an outgoing vector normal to a face of the unit simplex in

, then for each

, the coordinates of

within
H_{v}=\{z:\langle z,v\rangle<0\}
determine all the coordinates of
x
. So, there is no
(\epsilon,H_{v})
-asymptotic pair for any small enough
\epsilon>0
. Conversely, for each of the three normal outgoing vectors
v
of the face of the unit simplex, there is an asymptotic pair for the half-space
H_{v}
. See
[
7
, Example 2.7]
for details.

Nevertheless, an important step in extending Schwartzman’s theorem to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions has been done by Boyle and Lind through the notion of nonexpansive direction
[
7
]
. In contrast with the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-case, their result provides the existence of
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pairs only for some horoball
H
. Technically, it ensures the existence of some nonexpansive directions from which the former claim can be easily deduced.

4.1.
Existence of
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pairs

Thanks to
3.1
we propose a first extension of Schwartzman’s theorem (more in the spirit of Boyle and Lind’s theorem) to any countable group and more generally to any second countable and locally compact group. Moreover, our extension is relative to a factor map.

A factor map
\pi
is
bounded to one
(resp.
constant to one
) if the cardinality of the
\pi
-fibers is uniformly bounded (resp. equals to a constant).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
be topological dynamical systems, where
G
is an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance, and let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map. Then, either

•

\pi
is bounded to one,

•

or for any
\epsilon>0

there exist a horoball
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
and an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair
(x,y)\in X^{2}
relative to
\pi
.

In particular, when
(Y,S,G)
is trivial and
X
is infinite, there exists an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair
(x,y)\in X^{2}
.

Note that in the case of a trivial
(Y,S,G)
,
4.3
recovers
1.1
announced in the introduction.

Interestingly, in the context of symbolic dynamics,
4.3
provides the existence of an asymptotic pair for any infinite subshift. More precisely, for an infinite closed subset
X
of
A^{G}
(where
A
is a finite set and
G
is a countable group), invariant by the natural shift action
T
induced by translations on
G
, there exists a horoball
H
and two points
x\neq y\in X
that coincide when restricted to
H
.

Remark that
4.3
does not say that its items are mutually exclusive. In fact, many situations can arise: there exist factor maps that are bounded to one but do not identify asymptotic pairs (e.g., consider an irrational rotation on the complex circle with the factor map
z\mapsto z^{n}
).
On the other hand, an example of an unbounded to one factor map identifying asymptotic points is also given by a dynamic on the circle. For instance, consider a Denjoy’s homeomorphism. It preserves a Cantor set
C
. It is standard that it has positively asymptotic pairs: namely the extreme points of a connected component of the complementary of
C
. Moreover, the homeomorphism factorizes onto an irrational rotation by a factor map identifying these connected components. Finally, recall that there exist Denjoy’s examples where the restrictions on the Cantor set
C
provide factor maps that are both bounded to one and identify the asymptotic pairs.

Let us stress that
4.3
is intrinsic to the system and does not depend on the embedding into another space with some constraints (like the dimension, connectivity, …). This differs, for instance, from what the usual study of minimal sets of homeomorphisms (like the one of Denjoy homeomorphisms) would suggest.

Proof.

Consider the system
(R_{\pi},T^{(2)},G)
, where
R_{\pi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
and
T^{(2)}
is the diagonal action
T^{(2)}_{g}(x,y)=(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))
. Let
O=R_{\pi}\setminus\Delta_{X}
, where
\Delta_{X}
is the diagonal on
X
. Clearly,
O
is open and
G
-invariant. If
O
is closed, then there exists
\delta>0
such that
{\rm dist}(x,y)\geq\delta
for any
(x,y)\in O
. Then the compactness of

implies that
\pi
is bounded to one.

Now assume
O
is not closed. This implies that
\emptyset\neq\partial O\subseteq\Delta_{X}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, set
U=\{(x,y)\colon{\rm dist}(x,y)<\epsilon\}
an open neighborhood of
\partial O
.
By

, for any

there exist a horoball
H
in
{\mathcal{H}}
and different points

with

.
∎

Similarly, the application of the Directed Robinson Crusoe theorem provides a directed version of
4.3
. This is a second extension of Schwartzman’s theorem. For expository reasons, we state a version which is valid for groups admitting non periodic elements and
where the inverse map is an isometry, i.e.,
d(x^{-1},y^{-1})=d(x,y)
.
This is the case, for instance, for the
\ell_{1}
distance of a finitely generated group. The inverse map is also an isometry for any distance

that is
bi-invariant
, i.e., that is both left and right-invariant:

for any

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map between the topological dynamical systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
, where
G
is an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance and where the inverse map is an isometry. Assume that
\pi
is not bounded to one.
Let
k\in G
be an element generating an unbounded subgroup
\langle k\rangle
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
a horoball
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

•

the horoball

does not contain
k
;

•

there exists an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair
(x,y)
relative to
\pi
.

Observe that the first item implies a strong restriction on the horoballs. For instance, if
G={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, the set of
\ell_{2}
horoballs that do not contain a given point

forms a half circle of directions when identified with their normal outgoing vectors (see
Section

5
for further discussion).

Also, notice that following the proof (given below), we can relax the hypothesis on the isometric action of the inverse map
by only considering right-invariant distances. But the price to pay is to get the following more intricate statement.

Theorem 4.5

(
4.4

.

Let

Actually, we believe the correct general setting on the distance to get a similar conclusion is that the inverse map

Proof of
4.4
.

The proof starts similarly to that of
4.3
. We keep the same notations: i.e.,
we consider the system
(R_{\pi},T^{(2)},G)
, where
R_{\pi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
and
T^{(2)}
is the diagonal action
T^{(2)}_{g}(x,y)=(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))
. The set
O
denotes
R_{\pi}\setminus\Delta_{X}
, where
\Delta_{X}
is the diagonal in
X\times X
.
The set
O
is open and
G
-invariant and the same proof as in
4.3
shows it is not closed.

Moreover, for any
\delta>0
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that if
(x,y)\in O
and
{\rm dist}(x,y)\leq\epsilon
then
{\rm dist}((x,y),\partial O)\leq\delta
. Indeed, for the sake of a contradiction, assume this is not true. Taking a subsequence if needed, we may consider a sequence
((x_{n},y_{n}))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
O
with
(x_{n},y_{n})\to(z,z)
when
n\to\infty
for some
z\in X
and
{\rm dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),\partial O)>\delta
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. But, by definition,
(z,z)\in\partial O
, which contradicts the fact that
{\rm dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),\partial O)>\delta
.

Since the group
\langle k\rangle
is unbounded, both semigroups
\langle k\rangle_{+}
and
\langle k^{-1}\rangle_{+}
are unbounded.
We claim that
O
is not pointwise
\{k^{-1}\}
-repulsed by its border.
Indeed, for
\delta>0
let
\epsilon>0
be such that
(x,y)\in O
and
d(x,y)\leq\epsilon
implies

. Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
be distinct open balls of diameter
\epsilon/2
whose union covers
X
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1}
be distinct points with
\pi(x_{1})=\pi(x_{2})=\cdots=\pi(x_{m+1})
. Let
F=\{(x_{i},x_{j}):i\neq j\}\subseteq O
. Then, by the pigeonhole principle, for any
g\in\langle k^{-1}\rangle_{+}\subseteq G
there exist

and

such that

and

belong to a same ball

. This means that
{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x_{i}),T_{g}(x_{j}))\leq\epsilon
, which implies that
(T_{g}(x_{i}),T_{g}(x_{j}))
is

. The claim is proved.

So the set

is non pointwise
\{k^{-1}\}
-repulsed by its border. Set
G_{k}=\{g\in G:k^{-1}\in B_{d(1,g)}(g)\}
. It can be checked that

and

are unbounded since they contain unbounded subsets of

and
\langle k\rangle_{+}
respectively. Moreover, by definition

. By the directed Robinson Crusoe theorem

applied to

, for any

there exist a horoball

in

and different points

with

such that for all

.

Write
\displaystyle H=\{\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{g_{n}}<0\}
for a sequence

in

going to infinity. Since

we have that for all

Since the inverse map is an isometry, this means that

.
The proof is complete.
∎

The Robinson Crusoe theorems are valid for second countable, locally compact groups (like
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
or Lie groups).
But in some cases, they hold for trivial reasons. For instance, in the case of a
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-flow, the notion of

-asymptotic pair is trivial: any non fixed point with any small translation of it by the flow give an asymptotic pair. This problem already occurs in the definition of expansive flows (see
[

]
).
This leads to the following question:

Question 4.6
.

Provide nontrivial versions of
4.3
and
4.4
for flows of (connected) second countable, locally compact groups actions.

4.2.
Structure of the set of asymptotic pairs

The structure of the set of pairs that are
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic for some horoball
H
and
\epsilon>0
depends very much on the particular dynamical system we are working with. It can be really small, for instance finite, and even restricted to only two orbits (e.g., Sturmian subshifts, Denjoy examples of homeomorphism of the circle). Hence it does not form, in general, a generic set in the topological or measurable frameworks. At the opposite, it can also be really large (e.g., in the fullshift there is a Cantor set of such pairs).

However, in some cases, the asymptotic pairs reflect features of the global structure of the ambient space.
For instance, for a product dynamical system, it is simple to check that the set of asymptotic pairs also has a product structure. Less directly, we have the following result for spaces with nontrivial connected components.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
(X,T,G)
be a topological dynamical system with
\dim X>0
and
G
be an infinite group with a proper right-invariant distance. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
there exist a horoball
H
in
G
and a connected set
F\subseteq X
such that

0<\textrm{diam}(T_{g}(F))<\epsilon
for every
g\in H\cup\{1_{G}\}
.

In particular, this result shows, in the case of a dynamic on a connected set, that it admits a (stable) set

W^{s}_{\epsilon,H}(x)=\{y\in X:\forall h\in H,\ {\rm dist}(T_{h}(x),T_{h}(y))<\epsilon\}

of positive topological dimension for some
x\in X
and some horoball
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
. In differentiable dynamics, this leads to the meaningful notion of stable manifold.
Let us also point out that this result is the starting point in Mañé’s proof that the minimal components of expansive
{\mathbb{Z}}
-dynamics are zero-dimensional
[
21
]
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
denote the set of all closed connected subsets of
X
. Since
X
has positive dimension, basics on dimension theory imply that
{\mathcal{X}}
is nonempty. It is also classical that
{\mathcal{X}}
is compact for the Hausdorff metric.
Let
O\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
denote the set of positive diameter connected subsets of
X
. It is clearly invariant, open and non closed. Then,
3.1
gives us immediately the desired conclusion.
∎

5.
Set of nonexpansive horoballs for a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action

In
Section

4
we have shown the existence of horoballs admitting
\epsilon
-asymptotic pairs. Here we try to obtain more information about the set of such horoballs.
For this we follow the same path initiated by Boyle and Lind in
[
7
]
. In their work the authors consider the notion of
nonexpansive direction
which corresponds in our context to the border of horoballs admitting
\epsilon
-asymptotic pairs for the
\ell_{2}
distance in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. In this case, horoballs are open half-spaces and their border are hyperplanes, i.e., directions for
d=2
. Remark that by only considering the border of a half-space, we lose information about its orientation, as the half-space and its opposite share the same border. This is an important difference between our approach and that of nonexpansive directions.

Boyle and Lind showed in
[
7
]
that the set of nonexpansive directions (under their definition) is a closed subset within the set of all the directions. Furthermore, their work, together with Hochmans’s
[
17
]
shows that any nonempty closed set can be realized as the set of nonexpansive direction of some
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-action.

In this section we restrict ourselves to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions and we propose a notion of nonexpansive horoballs (see
5.1
, similar to the one of
[
30
]
). The result of
[
7
]
about closeness of the set of nonexpansive directions can be easily adapted to the context of
\ell_{2}
horoballs. We use this fact to obtain a restriction on the set of nonexpansive horoballs of a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action: the intersection of all its nonexpansive horoballs has to be empty (
5.6
). This is an extension of Schwartzman’s result.

Definition 5.1
.

For dynamical systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
, a factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
and
\epsilon>0
, a horoball
H
is said to be
(\epsilon,\pi)
-nonexpansive if there exist two different points
x,y\in X
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
and
{\rm dist}(T_{g}x,T_{g}y)\leq\epsilon
for all
g\in H
(i.e.,
(x,y)
is an
(\epsilon,H)
-asymptotic pair relative to
\pi
).

When
Y
is the trivial system, we simply call the horoball
\epsilon
-nonexpansive.

At the opposite, a horoball
H
that is not
(\epsilon,\pi)
-nonexpansive, satisfies the following property: for every
x,y
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
,

\sup_{g\in H}{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))\leq\epsilon\text{ implies }x=y.

In this sense, the horoball
H
determines the points of
X
. For this reason, C. Zinoviadis in
[
30
]
uses the term
nondeterministic
instead of nonexpansive.

For
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-actions, we set the collection of
(\epsilon,\pi)
-nonexpansive
\ell_{2}
horoballs (identified with their normal outgoing vectors) as

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)

\displaystyle=\{v\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}:\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\langle x,v\rangle<0\}\textrm{ is }(\epsilon,\pi)\textrm{-nonexpansive}\},

(4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi).

Also, for the trivial factor we simply denote these sets by
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon)
and
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X)
.
To avoid confusion between the notions, we refer to the elements of
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)
(resp.
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X)
) as
\pi
-nondeterministic
(resp.
nondeterministic
) vectors.
Similar arguments as the ones in
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
show the closeness of the set of nondeterministic vectors for the usual topology on the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(X,T,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
be a dynamical system and
\pi\colon X\to Y
a factor map onto the system
(Y,S,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
the sets
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
and
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)
are closed subsets of the unit sphere.

For the reader’s convenience, we provide a proof that
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
is closed.

Proof.

First, we assume that for any pair of different points
x,y
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
, there exists a
g\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))>\epsilon
.
Otherwise we have
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)={\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
and there is nothing to prove.

For
v\in\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
, there are two points
x,y
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
,
and
{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))<\epsilon
, for any
g
in the half-space
H_{v}=\{g:\langle v,g\rangle<0\}
. By the initial assumption, we may pick
\tilde{g}
, minimizing the distance to
H_{v}
such that
{\rm dist}(T_{\tilde{g}}(x),T_{\tilde{g}}(y))>\epsilon
. Hence
{\rm dist}(T_{g\tilde{g}}(x),T_{g\tilde{g}}(y))<\epsilon
for any
g\in H_{v}
.

We deduce the next characterization:

v\in\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
if and only if there exist
x,y\in X
such that,
\pi(x)=\pi(y),{\rm dist}(x,y)>\epsilon
,
and
{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))<\epsilon
, for any
g
such that
\langle v,g\rangle<0
.
Note that the converse part is trivial.
From this characterization, it is straightforward to check that
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
is closed.

The closeness of
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)
follows from that of
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
since it is the intersection of a nested sequence of such sets.
∎

More interestingly,
4.3
provides conditions that ensure the nonemptiness of the set
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
for every
\epsilon>0
. The former argument then implies that the set
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)
is nonempty. This shows in particular that the horoball given by
4.3
may be taken uniformly in
\epsilon>0
for the
\ell_{2}
distance. This leads to the following notion of nonexpansivity along a subset (similar to
[
7
]
), which becomes relevant when considering horoballs.

Definition 5.3
.

A topological dynamical system
(X,T,G)
with
G
countable is said to be expansive along a subset
H
if there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{g\in H}{\rm dist}(T_{g}x,T_{g}y)\leq\epsilon_{0}\text{ implies }x=y.

If
H
fails to meet this condition, the system is said to be non
H
-expansive.

Observe that this notion is meaningless in the context of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
flows since any pair of points
(x,T_{\epsilon}x)
for small enough
\epsilon>0
shows that such flow is non
H
-expansive for any horoball.

With this definition
4.3
can be stated as follows (in the spirit of Schwartzman’s result).

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(X,T,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
be an infinite topological dynamical system. Then, it is non
H
-expansive for some
\ell_{2}
horoball
H
.

In addition, the closeness property together with
4.4
(the directed version on the existence of asymptotic pairs) enables us to guarantee the existence of a nonexpansive horoball (for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
systems) within any given half-space.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map between the topological dynamical systems
(X,T,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
and
(Y,S,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. Assume that
\pi
is not bounded to one. Then, any closed half-space
H\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
contains a
\pi
-nondeterministic vector, i.e.,

\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\pi)\cap H\neq\emptyset.

When
X
is infinite and
Y
is the trivial factor, we obtain that any closed half-space contains a nondeterministic vector.

Proof.

We endow
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with the
\ell^{2}
distance. Let
H\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a closed half-space and write
H=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\langle x,c\rangle\geq 0\}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
(g_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence going to infinity such that the distance of
g_{n}
to the ray
\{tc:t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
goes to 0. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
denote the set of horoballs that do not contain
g_{n}
. Identifying a horoball with its outward unit normal vector, we may identify
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
with the set
S_{n}=\{v\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}:\langle g_{n},v\rangle\geq 0\}
. Let
\epsilon>0
.
By
4.4
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we may find a vector
v_{n}\in\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)\cap S_{n}
. Taking a subsequence if needed, we may assume that
v_{n}\to v
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
is closed by
5.2
, we get
v\in\operatorname{ND}_{\ell_{2}}(X,\epsilon,\pi)
. We may write
g_{n}=t_{n}c+\epsilon_{n}
with
\|\epsilon_{n}\|\to 0
and
t_{n}\nearrow\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
From
\langle v_{n},g_{n}\rangle\geq 0
we obtain that
\langle v,c\rangle\geq 0
, and hence
v
belongs to
H
. As
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, using again
5.2
, we obtain the desired conclusion.

∎

A special case of
5.5
, for
d=2
and

the trivial system, was announced by P. Guillon, J. Kari and C. Zinoviadis in 2015
[
15
]
, but to the best of our knowledge, the proof was not communicated to the community. An immediate corollary of

is the following restriction on the set of nonexpansive half-spaces.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be a factor map between the topological dynamical systems

6.
Distality in Cantor dynamics

In this section we illustrate further applications of Robinson Crusoe theorem (
3.1
) in the context of zero dimensional dynamics, i.e., when the phase space
X
is a zero dimensional space.

Recall that for a topological dynamical system
(X,T,G)
a point
x\in X
is
almost periodic
if for every neighborhood
U
of
x
the set of return times
N(x,U)=\{g\in G:T_{g}(x)\in U\}
is
syndetic
(i.e., there exists a compact set
K\subseteq G
such that
KN(x,U)=G
). Equivalently,
x
has a minimal orbit closure, i.e.,
(\overline{Gx},G)
is minimal. The topological dynamical system
(X,T,G)
is
pointwise almost periodic
if every
x\in X
is almost periodic.

The following result was obtained in
[
4
]
(stated for finitely generated groups). Here we give a proof for any countable group whose horofunctions satisfy the conditions of
2.1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(X,T,G)
be a dynamical system with
X
a zero dimensional space and
G
a countable group. Assume that the group admits a right-invariant and proper metric so that each horoball
H
contains arbitrarily large balls. Then the following statements are equivalent,

(1)

the action of
G
is pointwise almost periodic;

(2)

the relation
R=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in\overline{Gx}\}
is closed.

We notice that the conclusion of
6.1
is still true when the acting group
G
is finite.

Proof.

The implication (
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
) is valid for any topological
G
-action. By contradiction, assume there exists
x\in X
that is not almost periodic. Then, there exists an almost periodic point
y\in\overline{Gx}
such that
x\notin\overline{Gy}
. Let
(g_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq G
be a sequence such that
T_{g_{n}}(x)
converges to
y
as
n
goes to infinity. It follows that
(T_{g_{n}}(x),x)\in R
converges to
(y,x)\in R
, a contradiction.

We also prove (
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
) by contradiction. Assume that
R
is not closed. Then there exists a sequence
((x_{n},y_{n}))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
R
converging to
(x,y)\notin R
. Let
U
be a clopen subset such that
\overline{Gx}\subseteq U
and
y\notin U
. Let
O
be the set
\{y^{\prime}\in\overline{Gx}^{c}:\overline{Gy^{\prime}}\cap U^{c}\neq\emptyset\}
. One checks that
O
is a
G
-invariant open set, which is not closed since
x_{n}
belongs to
O
for large enough
n
. Moreover,
U
is also a neighborhood of
\partial O
. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that there exist
y^{\prime}\in O
and a horoball
H
such that
T_{g}(y^{\prime})\in U
for every
g\in H
. Since each horoball contains arbitrarily large balls, the set of return times
N(y",U^{c})
of some point
y"\in U^{c}
in the orbit of
y^{\prime}
, cannot be syndetic. This is a contradiction.
∎

It follows from
2.1
that any finitely generated group fulfills the assumptions of
6.1
. It would be interesting to exhibit other groups or families of groups that satisfy the assumptions of
6.1
. For now, we do not rule out that this family consists only of finitely generated groups.

Let
\pi\colon(X,T,G)\to(Y,S,G)
be a factor map between the topological dynamical systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
. We say that
\pi
is
equicontinuous
(or that
X
is an equicontinuous extension of
Y
) if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
{\rm dist}(x,y)<\delta
and
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
implies that
{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))<\epsilon
for all
g\in G
. The factor map
\pi
is
distal
(or
X
is a distal extension of
Y
) if
\displaystyle\inf_{g\in G}{\rm dist}(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))>0
whenever
\pi(x)=\pi(y)
and
x\neq y
.
If the factor
\pi
is trivial one says that
(X,T,G)
is equicontinous, or distal, respectively.

Two points
x,y\in X
are
\pi
-regionally proximal
if for any
\epsilon>0
there exist points
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in X
and
g\in G
such that
\pi(x^{\prime})=\pi(y^{\prime})
,
{\rm dist}(x,x^{\prime})<\epsilon
,
{\rm dist}(y,y^{\prime})<\epsilon
and
{\rm dist}(T_{g}x^{\prime},T_{g}y^{\prime})<\epsilon
. We let
{\bf RP}(\pi)
denote the set of
\pi
-regionally proximal points.
It is well known (see for instance
[
3
, Chapter 7, Theorem 2]
) that a factor map is equicontinuous if and only if
{\bf RP}(\pi)=\triangle_{X}
and that if a factor map is equicontinuous, then it is a distal factor.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,T,G)\to(Y,S,G)
be a factor map, where
G
is a countable group,
X
is zero dimensional and
(Y,S,G)
is a pointwise almost periodic system. Assume that the group admits a right-invariant and proper metric so that each horoball
H
contains arbitrarily large balls.
Then, the factor
\pi
is distal if and only if it is equicontinuous.

Proof.

We show the nontrivial implication. Assume that
\pi
is distal and consider the zero dimensional system
(R_{\pi},T^{(2)},G)
, where
R_{\pi}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\pi(x)=\pi(y)\}
and
T^{(2)}
is the diagonal action
T^{(2)}_{g}(x,y)=(T_{g}(x),T_{g}(y))
.
We claim that the distality of
\pi
implies that
(R_{\pi},G)
is pointwise almost periodic. To show this, we need to use the machinery of the enveloping semigroup (to avoid details about this theory, we refer to the book
[
3
, Chapters 3 and 6]
). Let
E(X,T,G)
and
E(Y,S,G)
denote the enveloping semigroup of the systems
(X,T,G)
and
(Y,S,G)
respectively. Since
\pi(x)
is an almost periodic point, we can find
u\in E(Y,S,G)
a minimal idempotent such that
u\pi(x)=\pi(x)
. We can take a minimal idempotent
v\in E(X,T,G)
such that
\pi_{\ast}(v)=u
, where
\pi_{\ast}\colon E(X,T,G)\to E(Y,S,G)
is the natural induced semigroup homomorphism (see
[
3
, Chapter 3, Theorem 7]
). Then,
v(x,y)=(vx,vy)
and, since
u\pi(x)=\pi(x)
, we obtain
\pi(vx)=\pi(x)=\pi(y)=\pi(vy)
. Noting that
(x,vx)
and
(y,vy)
are proximal pairs (i.e.,
\inf_{g\in G}{\rm dist}(x,vx)=\inf_{g\in G}{\rm dist}(y,vy)=0
), we obtain that
x=vx
and
y=vy
. Hence,
(x,y)=v(x,y)
is a minimal point.

By
6.1
, the orbit relation
R=\{((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))\in R_{\pi}\times R_{\pi}:(x_{2},y_{2})\in\overline{G(x_{1},y_{1})}\}
is closed. From this, it is classical to deduce that
{\bf RP}(\pi)=\triangle_{X}
, we provide the argument for completeness. Let
(x,y)\in{\bf RP}(\pi)
and let
(x_{n},y_{n})\in R_{\pi}
and
g_{n}\in G
such that
(x_{n},y_{n})\to(x,y)
and
(T_{g_{n}}x_{n},T_{g_{n}}y_{n})\to(z,z)
, for
z\in X
and a sequence
(g_{n})
in
G
. Since
((T_{g_{n}}x_{n},T_{g_{n}}y_{n}),(x_{n},y_{n}))\in R
, and this relation is closed, we obtain that
((z,z),(x,y))\in R
. This implies that
x=y
.
We conclude that
\pi
is an equicontinuous extension.

∎

When
(Y,S,G)
is the trivial system, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3

Let
(X,T,G)
be a topological dynamical where

G

For a group
G

.

[

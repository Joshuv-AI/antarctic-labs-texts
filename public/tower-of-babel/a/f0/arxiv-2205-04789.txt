Regularization by random translation of potentials for the continuous PAM and related models in arbitrary dimension
Florian Bechtold

Abstract
. We study a regularization by noise phenomenon for the continuous parabolic Anderson model with a potential shifted along paths of fractional Brownian motion. We demonstrate that provided the Hurst parameter is chosen sufficiently small, this shift allows to establish well-posedness and stability to the corresponding problem - without the need of renormalization - in any dimension. We moreover provide a robustified Feynman-Kac type formula for the unique solution to the regularized problem building upon regularity estimates for the local time of fractional Brownian motion as well as non-linear Young integration.

1.1.
Formulation of the main result

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\eta\;\geqslant\;0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
V\in H^{-\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

Corollary 1.2

(Regularized PAM)
.

Consider spatial white noise on the

2.1.
Solutions to the mollified equation converge

Let

\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u^{\epsilon}-(\widetilde{V^{\epsilon}})u^{\epsilon},\qquad u^{\epsilon}(0)=f,

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}(t,x)

Lemma 2.2

(Identification of Riemann integral as Sewing)
.

For
\delta>0

(\mathcal{I}A^{t})

\int_{0}^{t}V(W_{t-s}-w^{H}_{s})ds=(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{t}

Proof.

Remark first that by Lemma
2.1

\epsilon>0

\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\;\leqslant\;C_{T}|t-s|^{1/2+\epsilon}.

Moreover, by Young’s inequality in Besov spaces (

\left\lVert V*L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\delta}}\lesssim\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{1+\delta}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

Moreover, we have for almost every

. We therefore obtain for

that

\displaystyle|(\delta A^{t})_{s,u,r}|

\displaystyle=|V*L_{u,r}(W_{t-s})-V*L_{u,r}(W_{t-u})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{1+\delta}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}|r-u|^{1/2+\epsilon}|u-s|^{1/2-\epsilon/2}.

We conclude that

does indeed admit a Sewing on

|A^{t}_{s,u}-(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{s,u}|=O(|u-s|^{1+\epsilon/2}).

Next, observe that the germ

\widetilde{A}^{t}_{s,u}:=\int_{s}^{u}V(W_{t-v}-w^{H}_{v})dv

wherefore we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{u}V(W_{t-v}-w^{H}_{v})dv-(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{s,u}\right|\;\leqslant\;|\widetilde{A}^{t}_{s,u}-A^{t}_{s,u}|+|A^{t}_{s,u}-(\mathcal{I}A^{t})_{s,u}|\lesssim|u-s|^{1+\delta(1-\epsilon/2)}

Remark 2.3

Remark that in the above statement, we did not exploit any regularization from the local time, but instead demanded regularity of the potential

Lemma 2.4

(Convergence of mollifications)
.

For
\eta\;\geqslant\;0

\displaystyle\left\lVert V*L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\delta}}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{-\eta}}\left\lVert L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{H^{1+\eta+\delta}}

Proof.

Local time and occupation times formula

We recall for the reader the basic concepts of occupation measures, local times and the occupation times formula. A comprehensive review paper on these topics is
[

Definition 3.1
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable path. Then the occupation measure at time
t\in[0,T]
, written
\mu^{w}_{t}
is the Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\mu^{w}_{t}(A):=\lambda(\{s\in[0,t]:\ w_{s}\in A\}),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\lambda
denotes the standard Lebesgue measure.

The occupation measure thus measures how much time the process
w
spends in certain Borel sets. Provided for any
t\in[0,T]
, the measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we call the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative local time of the process
w
:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable path. Assume that there exists a measurable function

such that

\mu^{w}_{t}(A)=\int_{A}L^{w}_{t}(z)dz,

A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

Note that by the definition of the occupation measure, we have for any bounded measurable function

\int_{0}^{t}f(w_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mu^{w}_{t}(dz).

The Sewing Lemma

We recall the Sewing Lemma due to
[
Gub04
]
(see also
[
FH14
, Lemma 4.2]
). Let
E
be a Banach space,
[0,T]
a given interval. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-th simplex of
[0,T]
, i.e.
\Delta_{n}:=\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})|0\;\leqslant\;t_{1}\dots\;\leqslant\;t_{n}\;\leqslant\;T\}
. For a function
A:\Delta_{2}\to E
define the mapping
\delta A:\Delta_{3}\to E
via

(\delta A)_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}.

Provided
A_{t,t}=0
we say that for
\alpha,\beta>0
we have
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
if
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty
, where

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}:=\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}}\frac{\left\lVert A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}},\qquad\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}:=\sup_{(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{3}}\frac{\left\lVert(\delta A)_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\beta}}

and
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}:=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}
. For a function
f:[0,T]\to E
, we denote
f_{s,t}:=f_{t}-f_{s}
. Moreover, if for any sequence
(\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t]))_{n}
of partitions of
[s,t]
whose mesh size goes to zero, the quantity

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}

converges to the same limit, we note

(\mathcal{I}A)_{s,t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}.

Appendix C
Exponential moments for the Hölder modulus of continuity of Brownian motion

For the sake of completeness, we provide a sketch of the proof that the
\gamma
-Hölder modulus of continuity of Brownian motion is exponentially integrable for
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
. Refer also to
[
HV08
]
for more refined integrability statements.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B
be a standard Brownian motion. Then for any
\gamma<1/2
and
a>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp{\left(a\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,T]}\frac{|B_{t}-B_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\right)}\right]<\infty.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, set
T=1
. Remark first that we have for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{E}[|B_{t}-B_{s}|^{k}]\;\leqslant\;|t-s|^{k/2}(k-1)!!

We follow the classical proof of Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem (refer for example to
[
SP12
, Theorem 10.1]
). For
D_{m}:=2^{-m}\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,1)
and
D=\cup_{m}D_{m}
set

\Delta_{m}=\{(s,t)\in D_{m}\times D_{m}:\ |t-s|\;\leqslant\;2^{-m}\}.

We then have for
\sigma_{j}:=\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{j}}|B(t)-B(s)|
the bound

\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{j}^{k}]\;\leqslant\;\sum_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{j}}\mathbb{E}[|B_{t}-B_{s}|^{k}]\;\leqslant\;2\cdot 2^{j(1-k/2)})(k-1)!!

Following further the proof of Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem as in
[
SP12
, Theorem 10.1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{s\neq t\in D}\frac{|B_{t}-B_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\right)^{k}\right]^{1/k}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{1+\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j\gamma}\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{j}^{k}]^{1/k}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{1/k}\cdot 2^{1+\gamma}((k-1)!!)^{1/k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j\gamma}(2^{-j(1/2-1/k)}).

Now let
k_{0}
be the smallest natural such that
\gamma<1/2-1/k_{0}
. We then obtain for any
k\;\geqslant\;k_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{s\neq t\in D}\frac{|B_{t}-B_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\right)^{k}\right]^{1/k}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{1/k}\cdot 2^{1+\gamma}((k-1)!!)^{1/k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j(\gamma+1/k-1/2)}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2^{1/k}\cdot 2^{1+\gamma}((k-1)!!)^{1/k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{j(\gamma+1/k_{0}-1/2)}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C2^{1/k}\cdot 2^{1+\gamma}((k-1)!!)^{1/k}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{a^{k}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,1]}\frac{|B_{t}-B_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\right)^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{1+\gamma}Ca)^{k}}{k!!}<\infty,

which proves the claim.
∎

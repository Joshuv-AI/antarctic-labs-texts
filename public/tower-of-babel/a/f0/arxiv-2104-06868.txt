Forward-backward stochastic differential equations driven by G-Brownian motion
Huan Lu, Yongsheng Song

Abstract

1
Introduction

In recent decades, forward-backward stochastic differential equations(FBSDEs, for short) in the Wiener probability space, which has a general form as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})dW_{t},\\
dY_{t}=-f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})dt+Z_{t}dW_{t},\\
X_{0}=x,\quad Y_{T}=g(X_{T}),\end{array}\right.

has been widely studied, where
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denotes the standard Brownian motion. Many results, such as the well-posedness and regularity of their solutions, have been obtained by various means. There are three main methodologies to discuss the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to these FBSDEs: small time duration, the method of continuation, and the four-step scheme.

The fully coupled FBSDEs of the case
\sigma(t,x,y,z)=\sigma_{0}(t,x,y)
, was first researched by Antonelli(
[
1
]
) in the early 1990s. By the Lipschitz property of the coefficients, Antonelli construct a contraction map when the time duration
T
is small enough, which implies the unique solvability of FBSDEs.

The method of continuation was introduced in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
to discuss the solvability of fully coupled nonlinear FBSDEs. In these works, they introduced a so-called G-monotone conditions of coefficients, by which they can easily get the uniqueness of the solution if it exists. They then use this method to connect a family of FBSDEs, which have the same solvability, to get the existence of those solutions.

Unlike the two methods above, Ma, Protter and Yong (
[
5
]
) gave the solvability of FBSDEs based on the results of PDEs. Their approach is called the four-step scheme, which is extended to wider situations by Delarue (
[
6
]
), where he supposed that
\sigma
is independent of
z
and is uniformly elliptic. More specifically, let
u
be a solution to the PDE associated with the FBSDE, then we can solve the forward SDE by replacing
Y_{t}
and
Z_{t}
with
u(t,X_{t})
and
u_{x}(t,X_{t})\sigma(t,X_{t},u(t,X_{t}))
, respectively. In this way,
(X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})
is a solution to the FBSDE, where
Y_{t}=u(t,X_{t})
and
Z_{t}=u_{x}(t,X_{t})\sigma(t,X_{t},u(t,X_{t}))
.

Notice that the assumptions appearing in these three means cannot imply each other. For instance, method of continuation requires the G-monotone of coefficients, while Antonelli only needed the Lipschitz condition; both Antonelli and Delarue required
\sigma
to be independent of
z
, but Delarue needed more restrictions on
\sigma
to get the solvability for arbitrary time duration
T
. Therefore, there is no unified approach to solve the problem.

We have already known that there is a close connection between PDEs and FBSDEs. Briefly speaking, the solution to a non-degenerate linear second-order parabolic partial differential equation can be expressed by a diffusion process, while the solution to quasi-linear ones can be represented by the solutions to FBSDEs. Nevertheless, in the linear expectation framework, there is no probabilistic representation for the solutions to fully nonlinear PDEs, because linear expectation cannot reflect the nonlinearity of these PDEs. Thanks to the G-expectation and G-Brownian motion introduced by Peng (
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
), the solutions to these PDEs have probabilistic representation with the help of FBSDEs in the G-expectation framework. In addition, FBSDEs can be obtained by applying stochastic maximum principle to optimal control problems. When the optimal control problems are considered in the G-expectation framework, which usually occurs in economics and finance, the associated FBSDEs should be driven by G-Brownian motion.

However, in the G-expectation framework, things go more complex. The solvability of SDEs and BSDEs are given by Peng (
[
8
,
10
]
), Bai and Lin (
[
11
]
), and Hu, Ji, Peng and Song (
[
12
]
) respectively in recent years. To the best of our knowledge, there are few researchers studying FBSDEs under the G-expectation framework, named forward-backward stochastic differential equations driven by G-Brownian motion (FBGSDEs, for short), much less for those fully coupled ones.

Motivated by the aforementioned reasons, in this paper, we will discuss the solvability of FBGSDEs. Specifically, given a G-expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
and let
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a
d
-dimensional G-Brownian motion on it, we study whether there is a unique solution
(X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},K_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
to the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dt+h(t,X_{t},Y_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}+\sigma(t,X_{t},Y_{t})dB_{t},\\
dY_{t}=-f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})dt-g(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})d\langle B\rangle_{t}+Z_{t}dB_{t}+dK_{t},\\
X_{0}=x,\quad Y_{T}=\Phi(X_{T}),\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
X,Y,Z,K
take values in
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},\mathbb{R}^{m}
, while
b,h,\sigma,f,g,\Phi
take values in
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n\times d},\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively. For simplify, we only consider the case of
d=m=n=1
, and the multidimensional case is similar. The main difficulties in our work are the lack of dominated convergence theorem in the G-expectation framework and the asymmetry of G-martingale. In order to overcome these obstacles, in our paper, we use uniformly convergence and the general Doob’s type inequality which needs higher moment estimations.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we shall present some preliminaries for the G-expectation framework and introduce the stochastic calculus on it. In section
3
, we give the solvability of FBGSDEs (
1
) on a small time duration, where we will also give a main estimation which plays a key role in our proof of the main theorem in this paper. In section
4
, some results of PDEs will be presented, and the existence and uniqueness of solutions to FBGSDEs (
1
) for arbitrary
T
, with non-degenerate diffusion, is provided, which is the main work of this paper.

2.1
Sublinear expectation space and G-expectation

Let
\Omega
be a given set, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a vector lattice of real valued functions defined on
\Omega
, which satisfies

(i)

c\in\mathcal{H}
, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

(ii)

|X|\in\mathcal{H}
, for any
X\in\mathcal{H}
.

In this article, the space
\mathcal{H}
will be used as the space of random variables.

Definition 2.1
.

A sublinear expectation is a function
\hat{\mathbb{E}}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
, satisfying

(i)

\hat{\mathbb{E}}X\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}Y
, for any
X\leq Y
,

(ii)

\hat{\mathbb{E}}c=c
, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

(iii)

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X+Y]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}X+\hat{\mathbb{E}}Y
,

(iv)

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\lambda X]=\lambda\hat{\mathbb{E}}X
, for any
\lambda\geq 0
.

We then call the triple
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
a sublinear expectation space.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
be a sublinear expectation on
\mathcal{H}
, then there exists a family of linear expectations
\{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\colon\theta\in\Theta\}
, such that

\hat{\mathbb{E}}X=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}X.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
be a sublinear expectation space, a random vector
Y\in\mathcal{H}^{m}
is said to be independent of another random vector
X\in\mathcal{H}^{n}
, if

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X,Y)]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(x,Y)]\left|{}_{x=X}\right.],\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in C_{l,lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
be a sublinear expectation space, random vectors
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}^{n}
are said to be identically distributed, denoted by
X\overset{d}{=}Y
, if

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X)]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(Y)],\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in C_{l,lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 2.5
.

We say
\tilde{X}
is an independent copy of
X
, if
\tilde{X}\overset{d}{=}X
and
\tilde{X}
is independent of
X
. Notice that
\tilde{X}
is independent of
X
does not imply
X
is independent of
\tilde{X}
.

Definition 2.6

(G-normal distribution)
.

Given a sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
, random vector
X\in\mathcal{H}^{d}
is called G-normally distributed, if

aX+b\tilde{X}\overset{d}{=}\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}X,\quad\text{for any }a,b\geq 0,

where
\tilde{X}
is an independent copy of
X
.

Now we can define function

\displaystyle G\colon S_{d}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\langle AX,X\rangle],

where
S_{d}
denotes the set of all
d\times d
symmetric matrices. Since
G
is a monotone, subadditive and bounded operator on
S_{d}
, there exists a bounded, convex and closed subset
\Gamma\subset S_{d}^{+}
, such that

G(A)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\rm{tr}(\gamma A).

(2)

Especially, we let
G
be non-degenerate in this paper, i.e., there exits
\lambda>0
, such that
G(A)-G(B)\geq\lambda\|A-B\|_{HS}
, for any
A\geq B
.

Remark 2.7
.

In the case of
d=1
,
G(a)=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\sigma}^{2}a^{+}-\frac{1}{2}\underline{\sigma}^{2}a^{-}
, where
\overline{\sigma}^{2}=\hat{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}
,
\underline{\sigma}^{2}=-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X^{2}]
. And
G(a)-G(b)\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{\sigma}^{2}(a-b)
, for any
a\geq b
.

Definition 2.8

(G-expectation)
.

Let
\Omega_{T}=C_{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
valued continuous functions on
[0,T]
with
\omega_{0}=0
, endowing with the supremum norm, and let
B_{t}(\omega)=\omega_{t}
be the canonical process. Set
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{0}=\{\varphi(B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}})\colon

t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\in[0,T],\varphi\in C_{l,lip}(\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}),n\geq 1\}
, for any
X=\varphi(B_{t_{1}}-B_{t_{0}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{0}
, we define G-expectation as follows,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(B_{t_{1}}-B_{t_{0}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(\sqrt{t_{1}-t_{0}}\xi_{1},\cdots,\sqrt{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\xi_{n})],

in which
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}
are identically distributed d-dimensional G-normally distributed random vectors, and
\xi_{i+1}
is independent of
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{i})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
. We call
(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{H}_{T}^{0},\tilde{\mathbb{E}})
the G-expectation space.

Definition 2.9

(conditional expectation)
.

Under the same notations above, for
X=\varphi(B_{t_{1}}-B_{t_{0}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})
, we can define its conditional expectation as follows,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{t_{i}}[\varphi(B_{t_{1}}-B_{t_{0}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(B_{t_{1}}-B_{t_{0}},B_{t_{2}}-B_{t_{1}},\cdots,B_{t_{i}}-B_{t_{i-1}}),

where

\tilde{\varphi}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i})=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i},B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_{i}},\cdots,B_{t_{n}}-B_{t_{n-1}})].

Remark 2.10
.

Without loss of generality, we write
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
as
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
in the rest of the paper, and the canonical process
\{B_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is the so-called G-Brownian motion. We refer readers to
[
10
]
for more details of this part.

3.1
Existence and uniqueness

Definition 3.1
.

We call the quadruple
(X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},K_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}

Since the conclusions of this paper are inspired mainly by the idea of
[
6
]
, and heavily depends on the results of fully nonlinear PDEs, there are still a lot to improve in this paper, we list some of them here.

•

We only consider the case that

are independent of

in this paper, for the situation of

and

, do Theorem

and Theorem

hold true?

•

We require all the coefficients are continuous differentiable with their derivatives satisfying Lipschitz conditions, can this assumption be relaxed to the Lipschitz conditions of coefficients?

•

What if
\sigma
does not satisfy uniformly elliptic condition, or can we get a similar conclusion without using those results of PDEs?

In fact, the second problem is influenced by the results of PDEs we quote in this paper. If the
C^{1}
norm of

, which is the solution to PDE (
11
), is bounded by a constant only depending on the boundary of first derivatives of coefficients, which means it is independent of the second derivatives of coefficients, then Assumption
4.6
can be weaker as we wish. However, the first problem is much more complex, since we need a higher moment control in the Doob’s inequality (Proposition
2.12
) under G-expectation. As a result, it is not easy to construct a contractive map
\mathcal{I}
as before, as long as

or

depends on

. Actually, if
b
or
h
depends on

, Lemma

may not hold any longer, because the function

or

does not satisfy the inequality appearing in (iv) of Definition

, and we cannot apply Theorem

to prove Lemma

. Consequently, we cannot use the results of PDEs to construct solutions of a family of FBGSDEs to approach the solution of FBGSDE (

).

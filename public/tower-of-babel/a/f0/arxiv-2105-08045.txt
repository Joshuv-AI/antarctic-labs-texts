Gravity Waves and Primordial Black Holes in Scalar Warm Little Inflation
Mar Bastero-Gil, Marta Subías Díaz-Blanco

Abstract
In warm inflation, dissipation due to the interactions of the inflaton field to other light degrees of freedom leads naturally to the enhancement of the primordial spectrum during the last 10-20 efolds of inflation. We study this effect in a variant of the Warm Little Inflaton model, where the inflaton couples to light scalars, with a quartic chaotic potential. These large fluctuations on re-entry will form light, evaporating Primordial Black Holes, with masses lighter than 10 6 10^{6} g. But at the same time they will act as a source for the tensors at second order. The enhancement is maximal near the end of inflation, which result in a spectral density of Gravitational Waves (GW) peaked at frequencies f ∼ O ⁡ ( 10 5 − 10 6 ) f\sim O(10^{5}-10^{6}) Hz today, and with an amplitude Ω G ​ W ∼ 10 − 9 \Omega_{GW}\sim 10^{-9} . Although the frequency range is outside the reach of present and planned GW detectors, it might be reached in future high-frequency gravitational waves detectors, designed to search for cosmological stochastic GW backgrounds above MHz frequencies.

Basics of Warm inflation: the “Scalar” WLI model

Appendix B
Fluctuations in WI and the growing mode function

In order to get the amplitude of scalar primordial spectrum on superhorizon scales, we work with the comoving curvature perturbation at linear order
{\cal R}
. In WI we have a mixture of two fluids, radiation and inflaton field, and for each one we can define

{\cal R}_{\alpha}=-\frac{H}{h_{\alpha}}\Psi_{\alpha}^{GI}\,,

(42)

with
h_{\alpha}=\rho_{\alpha}+p_{\alpha}
,
\rho_{\alpha}
,
p_{\alpha}
being the energy density and pressure of the background fluid, and
\Psi_{\alpha}^{GI}
the gauge invariant (GI) momentum perturbation. The total comoving curvature perturbation is given by

{\cal R}=\frac{h_{\phi}}{h_{\phi}+h_{r}}{\cal R}_{\phi}+\frac{h_{r}}{h_{\phi}+h_{r}}{\cal R}_{r}\,.

(43)

During slow-roll, one can show that the momentum perturbations fulfil the relation
growing2
;
reexamination
:

\Psi_{r}^{GI}\simeq Q\Psi_{\phi}^{GI}\,,

(44)

on super-horizon scales, and using the slow-roll Eq. (
4
) we simply have:

{\cal R}\simeq-\frac{(1+Q)H}{h_{r}+h_{\phi}}\Psi_{\phi}^{GI}\simeq{\cal R_{\phi}}\,,

(45)

which means that the primordial perturbations are adiabatic and there are no entropy perturbations. The GI inflaton momentum perturbation is given by
\Psi_{\phi}^{GI}=-\dot{\phi}\delta\phi^{GI}
,
\delta\phi^{GI}
being the GI inflaton fluctuation, and using
h_{\phi}=\dot{\phi}^{2}
we have

{\cal R}_{\phi}=\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}}\delta\phi^{GI}\,.

(46)

The spectrum is then given by:

P_{\cal R}(k)=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}|{\cal R}_{k}|^{2}\simeq\left(\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}^{2}}\right)^{2}P_{\phi}(k)\,,

(47)

where
{\cal R}_{k}
is the perturbation in Fourier space,
P_{\phi}(k)
the inflaton spectrum.

Gauge invariant perturbations at first order are built combining field, momentum and energy density perturbations with metric ones. At linear order, the FLRW metric is given by:

ds^{2}=-(1+2\alpha)dt^{2}-2a\partial_{i}\beta dx^{i}dt+a^{2}(t)[\delta_{ij}(1+2\varphi)+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\gamma]dx^{i}dx^{j}\,.

(48)

We work in the longitudinal, shear-free gauge, in which
\chi=a(\beta+a\dot{\gamma})=0
and
\alpha=-\varphi
. The gauge invariant momentum, field and radiation energy density perturbations at linear order are given by:

\displaystyle\Psi^{GI}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi-(\rho+p)\varphi/H\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\delta\phi^{GI}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\phi-\frac{\dot{\phi}}{H}\varphi\,,

(50)

\displaystyle\delta\delta\rho_{r}^{GI}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{r}-\frac{\dot{\rho}_{r}}{H}\varphi\,.

(51)

Instead of writting directly with the evolution equations for the GI perturbations, we found numerically more convenient to work with the perturbed Eqs. for field and radiation energy density, including the metric perturbations, and obtain the GI curvature perturbation from Eq. (
43
). The equations for the coupled system of inflaton, radiation and metric perturbations can be found in
growing2
;
growi<ng3
. Metric perturbations in the longitudinal gauge are given by combining the Einstein Eqs. at linear order:

\displaystyle(H\alpha-\dot{\varphi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m_{P}^{2}}\Psi\,,

(52)

\displaystyle-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\varphi+3H(H\alpha-\dot{\varphi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m_{P}^{2}}\delta\rho\,,

(53)

with
\alpha=-\varphi
, where
\delta\rho
(
\delta\Psi
) is the total energy (momentum) density perturbation. The Eqs. for the radiation fluctuations are given by (in Fourier space):

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\rho}_{r}+4H\delta\rho_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3H(1+w_{r})\rho_{r}\dot{\varphi}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Psi_{r}+\delta Q_{r}+Q_{r}\alpha\,,

(54)

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}_{r}+3H\Psi_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-w_{r}\delta\rho_{r}-(1+w_{r})\rho_{r}\alpha-\Upsilon\dot{\phi}\delta\phi\,,

(55)

where
w_{r}=1/r
,
Q_{r}=\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}
is the source term for the radiation that we have in Eq. (
2
), and
\delta Q_{r}
its perturbation

\delta Q_{r}=\delta\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}+2\Upsilon\dot{\phi}\delta\dot{\phi}-2\alpha\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}\,.

(56)

Finally, the Eq. for the inflaton field perturbation is given by a Langevin-like equation, including the stochastic Gaussian noises, quantum
\xi_{q}
and thermal
\xi_{T}

\delta\ddot{\phi}+3H\delta\dot{\phi}+\left(\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+V_{\phi\phi}\right)\delta\phi=\sqrt{2}H\xi_{q}+\sqrt{2\Upsilon T}\xi_{T}-\delta\Upsilon\dot{\phi}+\dot{\phi}(3(H\alpha-\dot{\varphi})+\dot{\alpha})+(2\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi})\alpha-\Upsilon(\delta\phi-\alpha\dot{\phi})\,,

(57)

where
\langle\xi_{\alpha}(k,t)\xi_{\alpha}(k^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta^{(3)}(k-k^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. In order to get the comoving curvature spectrum we have to integrate the system of Eqs. (
54
), (
55
), (
57
) for different realisations of the noise, and take the average:

P_{\cal R}=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\langle|{\cal R}|^{2}\rangle_{\xi}\,.

(58)

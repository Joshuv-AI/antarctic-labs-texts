Rigidity results for Riemann solitons
Willian Tokura, Marcelo Barboza, Elismar Batista, Ilton Menezes

Abstract
In this paper we take a look at conditions that make a Riemann soliton trivial, compacity being one of them. We also show that the behaviour at infinity of the gradient field of a non-compact gradient Riemann soliton might cause the soliton to be an Einstein manifold. Finally, we obtain scalar curvature estimates for complete shrinking or steady gradient Riemann solitons whose scalar curvature is bounded from below at infinity.

The Riemann flow, which was introduced by Udriste
[
1

2
]
, is the flow associated with the evolution equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t)=-2Rm_{g(t)},

(1)

for Riemannian metrics on a given differentiable manifold

is the Riemann curvature
(0,4)
-tensor of the metric

that, for symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors

and

is defined by (see
[
3
]

\displaystyle(A\odot B)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})

\displaystyle=A(X_{1},X_{3})B(X_{2},X_{4})+A(X_{2},X_{4})B(X_{1},X_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad-A(X_{1},X_{4})B(X_{2},X_{3})-A(X_{2},X_{3})B(X_{1},X_{4}).

Short-time existence and uniqueness of the flow (
1
) is
guaranteed by Hirica and Udriste on compact manifolds in
[
4
]
,
where the authors utilize the Ricci flow to construct short-time solutions of

Just like it happens with Ricci solitons, critical metrics for the Riemann flow
are self similar solutions of (

time from a given Riemannian metric on the manifold by diffeomorphisms and

Definition 1

A Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geqslant 3
, is a Riemann soliton if
there exist a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and a real number

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

Rm+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}g\odot g=\lambda G,

(2)

where

is the Lie derivative of

in the direction of

We write the soliton in (

(M^{n},g,X,\lambda)

for the sake of simplicity. It may happen that

for some

, in which case we say that
(M^{n},g,\nabla f,\lambda)
is a
gradient Riemann soliton. Notice that equation (
2
)
for gradient Riemann solitons reads:

Rm+\nabla^{2}{f}\odot g=\lambda G.

(3)

A Riemann soliton is classified into one of three types according to the sign
of
\lambda
, expanding if
\lambda<0
, steady if
\lambda=0
and,
finally, shrinking if
\lambda>0
. Also, a Riemann soliton is called trivial
if either
\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0
or
\nabla^{2}f=0
.

One might start picturing Riemann solitons by noticing that the trivial ones
satisfy

, thus having constant sectional
curvature. Also, in
[
6

Catino and Mastrolia define what

-space forms. Interestingly,

Example 2

The Gaussian soliton on

Example 4

Let

Lemma 5

The Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
admits a Riemannian soliton structure

(M^{n},g,X,\lambda)
if and only if
(M^{n},g)
has null Weyl tensor and admits
an almost Ricci soliton structure with soliton vector field
(n-2)X

satisfying

Rc_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jk}=\left[\frac{(n-2)\lambda}{2}+\frac{S}{2(n-1)}\right]g_{jk},

(4)

where
Rc
is the Ricci tensor and
S
the scalar curvature of

(M^{n},g)
.

Proof of Lemma
5
1

From the fundamental equation (
2
), we have

\begin{split}Rm_{ijkl}+\frac{1}{2}[g_{il}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jk}+g_{jk}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{il}-g_{ik}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jl}-g_{jl}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{ik}]=\lambda[g_{il}g_{jk}-g_{ik}g_{jl}],\end{split}

which by contraction over
i
and

Rc_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jk}=[(n-1)\lambda-div(X)]g_{jk},

(5)

or equivalently

Rc_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jk}=\left[\frac{(n-2)\lambda}{2}+\frac{S}{2(n-1)}\right]g_{jk}.

(6)

Now, substituting equation (
6
) into the decomposition of the Riemann
tensor (see
[
3
]

Rm_{ijkl}=\left[\frac{1}{n-2}\left(Rc_{g}-\frac{S}{2(n-1)}g\right)\odot g\right]_{ijkl}+W_{ijkl},

(7)

and using the Riemann soliton equation (

), we deduce
that
W_{ijkl}=0
.

On the other hand, if
(M^{n},g)
has null Weyl

and admits an almost Ricci soliton structure

Rc_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)_{jk}=\left[\frac{(n-2)\lambda}{2}+\frac{S}{2(n-1)}\right]g_{jk}.

Then from (

\begin{split}Rm_{ijkl}&=\left[\frac{1}{n-2}\left(Rc_{g}-\frac{S}{2(n-1)}g\right)\odot g\right]_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(-\mathcal{L}_{X}g+\lambda g\right)\odot g\right]_{ijkl}.\end{split}

Therefore,
(M^{n},g)
admits a Riemann solitons structure

Rm_{ijkl}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}g\odot g\right)_{ijkl}=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}g\odot g\right)_{ijkl},

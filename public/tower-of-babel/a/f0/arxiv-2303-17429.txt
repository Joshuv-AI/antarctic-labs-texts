Abstract:
Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated, infinite group
\Gamma
. We show that
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property if and only if for every
\alpha<1
, there is a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(g)]>\alpha\deg_{{\mathcal{G}}}(g)
for every vertex
g
and with the two-point function
\tau(g,h)=\mathbb{P}\big[g\leftrightarrow h\big]
vanishing as
d(g,h)\to\infty
. On the other hand, we show that
\Gamma
has Kazhdan’s property (T) if and only if there exists a threshold
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
implies that the two-point function is uniformly bounded away from zero. These results in particular answer questions raised by Lyons (
J. Math. Phys.

41
1099-1126 (2000)) about characterizations of properties of groups beyond amenability through group-invariant percolations.

The method of proof is new and is based on a construction of percolations with suitable dependence structures built from invariant point processes on spaces with measured walls. This construction furthermore leads to quantitative bounds on the two-point functions, exhibiting in particular exponential decay of the two-point function in several prominent examples of Haagerup groups, including co-compact Fuchsian groups, co-compact discrete subgroups of
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and lamplighters over free groups. This method also allows us to extend the aforementioned characterization of property (T) to the setting of relative property (T) and provide an application to Bernoulli percolation at the uniqueness threshold.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
be the right Cayley graph with respect to a finite and symmetric set of generators and equipped with the word metric
d
– we assume all finitely generated groups to be countably infinite and all appearing Cayley graphs to be right Cayley graphs with respect to finite, symmetric generating sets. Note that left multiplication by elements of
\Gamma
induces a left action on
{\mathcal{G}}
by graph automorphisms. In this article, we study the relationship between the geometry of this graph and the behavior of its
\Gamma
-invariant, random subgraphs, which are probabilistic objects known as
group-invariant percolations
.

More precisely, a probability measure on subsets of
E
is called a
bond percolation
on
{\mathcal{G}}
. This probability measure is called
\Gamma
-invariant
if it is invariant under the action of
\Gamma
. Denote the percolation configurations by
\omega
and the vertex corresponding to the identity in
\Gamma
by
o
. A
cluster
of
\omega
is a connected component of the induced subgraph, i.e. the subgraph consisting of all vertices and all edges which are in
\omega
. We will write

\deg_{\omega}(o)
for the degree of
o
as a vertex in this induced subgraph and
\deg(o)
for the usual degree of
o
in the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
. The most important example of a group-invariant percolation is
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation
in which edges are kept independently at random with fixed probability
p\in[0,1]
. It is a classical result of Newman and Schulman
[
49
]
that the number of infinite clusters in this model is constant a.s. with this constant being equal to either
0,1
or
\infty
. The
critical parameter

p_{c}=p_{c}({\mathcal{G}})
is defined as the infimum over all
p\in[0,1]
such that in
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation, there exists an infinite cluster a.s. Similarly, the
uniqueness threshold

p_{u}=p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})
is the infimum over all
p\in[0,1]
such that in
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation there is a unique infinite cluster a.s.

In a seminal paper, Benjamini, Lyons, Peres and Schramm
[
6
]
initiated the study of group-invariant percolations, relating these models with the geometry of the underlying graph and also showing that these are useful tools for understanding classical models such as Bernoulli percolation. Concretely, developing and using the mass-transport principle, the following characterization of amenability of the underlying group was obtained there:

Theorem 1

(Group-invariant percolation and amenability, cf.
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
is amenable if and only if for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with

(1.1)

\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)

and with no infinite clusters.

In a beautiful survey from 2000
[
39
, Sec. 10, p. 1122-1123]
, Lyons suggested the problem of searching for characterizations of other classes of groups through group-invariant percolation as part of a program aiming to understand the interplay between geometric group theory and probability. In fact, it was noted there that previous characterizations had been limited to
amenability only
. In particular, Lyons
[
39
, Sec. 10, p. 1123]
also asked whether Kazhdan’s property (T) can be characterized through group-invariant percolation.

The goal of this article is to answer these questions affirmatively by developing a novel and unifying approach to go beyond amenability. This approach leads to characterizations of the
Haagerup property
and of
Kazhdan’s property (T)
, which unlike amenability, are not quasi-isometry invariant. These properties are of utmost importance in geometric group theory and operator algebras; most notably, the Baum-Connes conjecture has been verified for groups with the Haagerup property
[
27
]
, while Kazhdan’s property (T) provides an obstruction to extending this proof to the general conjecture (we refer to Section
1.4
for basic definitions, examples and background regarding these groups).

Our approach combines probabilistic and geometric input, is constructive and provides the following additional information and applications along the way:

•

We provide the first probabilistic characterizations of the Haagerup property and Kazhdan’s property (T) through invariant percolation. In particular, these are given genuinely probabilistic meanings.

The key new feature of these characterizations is that they highlight the role of the competitive relationship between two properties a percolation may have, namely
large marginals
(in the sense of (
1.1
) for some
\alpha
close to
1
) and
two-point function decay
(a natural measurement of connectivity, cf. (
1.3
)). More precisely, we show that:

–

The Haagerup property is equivalent to the existence of invariant bond percolations with arbitrarily large marginals and two-point function vanishing at infinity (see Theorem
1.1
for the precise statement and Remark
1
-
2
for the
probabilistic
, resp.
geometric
, meaning of the Haagerup property implied by Theorem
1.1
).

1
) clearly implies vanishing connectivity, the converse does not hold: in the case of the free group on two generators, which is the simplest example of a non-amenable group with the Haagerup property and where the Cayley graph is the four-regular tree, we have
p_{c}<1
and for
p\in(p_{c},1)
, there are infinitely many infinite clusters (so
p_{u}=1
), but the two-point function decays exponentially, see Example
2.2.2
for details.

–

Kazhdan’s property (T) is equivalent to the existence of a non-trivial
threshold
such that every invariant bond percolation with marginals above this threshold has an infinite cluster with a
uniformly positive
probability of reaching
any given vertex
(see Theorem
1.2
for the precise statement and Remark
3
-
4
for its probabilistic, resp. geometric, interpretation).

We note that, by Theorem
1
, non-amenability is equivalent to the existence of a non-trivial threshold such that every invariant bond percolation with marginals above this threshold ”just” has some infinite cluster with positive probability. This comparison underlines the fact that Kazhdan’s property (T) is a particularly strong form of non-amenability. We may also point out that neither Theorem
1.1
nor Theorem
1.2
follow from the other one, which is due to the fact that there are groups which have neither Haagerup’s nor Kazhdan’s property. In the present context, this is related to the fact that the two-point functions may fail to vanish at infinity in (at least) two qualitatively different ways: they could be
uniformly
bounded away from zero (a phenomenon known as
long-range order
), or they could vanish along
some
direction(s), but remain uniformly bounded away from zero along other directions (known as
connectivity decay
) – we refer to Remark
7
, Remark
8
and Remark
10
for details and concrete examples of groups exhibiting such behavior.

We also extend this result to a characterization of groups with
relative property (T)
(see Theorem
4.3
) and provide an application to Bernoulli percolation at the uniqueness threshold
p_{u}
for these groups (see Theorem
4.4
).

•

The methods used in the proofs are new: they are necessarily different from the literature on the amenable case because the approach of removing boundaries of finite subsets (Følner sets) is not available. In particular, we do not use the mass-transport principle, which is a powerful tool for analyzing quantities of the form
\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\tau(o,g)
, but less useful for treating pointwise behavior of
\tau(o,g)
,
g\in\Gamma
. The present ideas are also quite different from the dynamical characterizations of Kazhdan’s property due to Glasner and Weiss
[
22
]
and related works
[
54
,
13
]
(and their analogues for the Haagerup property – see Section
1.4
for precise statements), namely the present ideas are less abstract, in particular they highlight the role of the geometry of the Cayley and are inherently quantitative (see below). Indeed, we introduce a new construction of (dependent) percolations built from invariant Poisson point processes on suitably chosen infinite measure spaces. This method, which we will outline in Section
1.3
, is robust (as will be demonstrated by many subsequent applications) and also unifying in the sense that it covers the constructive part of the proof of Theorem
1
as given in
[
6
]
.

•

Another advantage of this constructive approach is that it will yield
quantitative information
about the decay of the two-point function and link this information with the underlying geometry (see Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
for precise statements). In particular, we will obtain estimates of the rate of decay for particularly prominent examples of groups with the Haagerup property, namely co-compact Fuchsian groups (see Theorem
1.4
) and lamplighters over free groups (see Theorem
1.3
). Consequently, the approach will provide a framework for several natural percolation models on groups with the Haagerup property, including Bernoulli percolation on trees (naturally associated to
groups acting on trees
), Poisson hyperplane percolation (naturally defined on
co-compact discrete subgroups of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n}
)
) and percolations on amenable groups defined using Følner sets (see Proposition
3.1
, Theorem
5.4
and Proposition
5.7
). These as well as other examples will be highlighted throughout this article.

Finally, let us conclude this summary with the comment that while the focus of the present article is on group-invariant percolation on Cayley graphs, some of the ideas developed here are extended by the authors
[
46
]
to the setting of general locally finite, connected graphs and their random subgraphs, where they are used to characterize coarse embeddabiility into Hilbert space and the (non-equivariant)
L^{1}
-compression exponents through percolation despite the possible lack of symmetry. In the remaining part of this introduction, we will now provide a detailed description of the results outlined above.

1.1.
Haagerup and Kazhdan properties and invariant percolations.

For a bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
, define the
two-point function

(1.2)

\tau\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to[0,1]\,,\,\tau(g,h):=\mathbb{P}\big[g\leftrightarrow h\big]\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\big[\omega\colon g\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\,h\big]

to be the probability that
g
and
h
are in the same cluster of the percolation configuration. Clearly, if a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation has no infinite clusters, then
the two-point function
\tau(o,\cdot)
vanishes at infinity, written
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
and meaning that

(1.3)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{g,h \in\Gamma}{d(g,h)>r}}\,\tau(g,h)=0.

As mentioned above, recall that this property does not imply having no infinite clusters. Our first main result shows that two-point function decay in the presence of large marginals is possible if and only if the underlying group has the Haagerup property: recall that
\Gamma
has the
Haagerup property
(or is
a-T-menable
in the terminology of Gromov
[
23
]
) if it admits an affine isometric action on a Hilbert space, which is metrically proper (see Section
1.4
).

Theorem 1.1

(
Group-invariant percolation and the Haagerup property).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property if and only if for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
.

We point out two related results: first, there is a
quantitative version
specifying the decay of the two-point function which also yields a geometric meaning (see Theorem
3.6
and its consequences in Section
3.4
). Second, Theorem
1.1
implies a sufficient condition for the Haagerup property in terms of Bernoulli percolation on invariant random spanning subgraphs of
{\mathcal{G}}
(see Theorem
3.8
). Let us now underline the probabilistic and geometric content of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark
1

(
Percolation interpretation of the Haagerup property)

Theorem
1.1
provides the following probabilistic interpretation of the Haagerup property: picture the Cayley graph as an infinite, connected network and consider the task of designing an invariant, random strategy of removing edges in order to disconnect the graph. Consider the strategy successful if vertices, which are far apart, are disconnected with high probability, say tending to one as the distance tends to infinity. As a reasonable constraint, we do not allow arbitrary removal of edges, but associate a cost to the removal in such a way that we allow to remove, on average, at most
1-\alpha
percent of the edges at each vertex for some
\alpha<1
. Theorem
1.1
asserts that there exists a successful strategy at arbitrarily small costs if and only if the group associated to the Cayley graph has the Haagerup property.

Remark
2

(
Geometric interpretation of the Haagerup property and non-QI-invariance)

Theorem
1.1
also entails the following concrete insight about the geometry of groups with the Haagerup property: their Cayley graphs are such that vertices at large distances can be separated efficiently, i.e. in the probabilistic sense of Remark
1
. We emphasize a distinction between the intuitive sense of the word geometric and the sense in which it is frequently used in geometric group theory, where a property is called
geometric
if it is invariant under quasi-isometries (
QI-invariant
), see for instance
[
5
, Section 3.6]
. The Haagerup property is not QI-invariant as shown by Carette
[
9
]
. From this perspective, Theorem
1.1
and the underlying geometric intuition described above may be surprising. Informally speaking, relaxing the static setting of the Cayley graph itself to the setting of invariant random subgraphs provides the appropriate amount of freedom to being able to capture the Haagerup property in terms of geometric properties of the graph.

We now proceed to an important,
strong negation
of the Haagerup property: recall that
\Gamma
has
property (T)
if there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a finite subset
K\subset\Gamma
such that every unitary representation
\pi\colon\Gamma\to B(H)
for which there exists a unit vector
\varphi
with

(1.4)

\sup_{s\in K}\,\|\pi(s)\varphi-\varphi\|<\varepsilon,

has a non-zero invariant vector. While not immediate from this definiton, property (T) is a strong negation of both amenability and the Haagerup property. This will be underscored by the following probabilistic characterization of countable Kazhdan groups (every countable Kazhdan group is finitely generated, see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem 1.3.1]
), which is our second main result (see Theorem
4.1
).

Theorem 1.2

(
Group-invariant percolation and Kazhdan’s property (T)).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
has property (T) if and only if there exists
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
implies that
\tau(o,\cdot)
is bounded away from zero, i.e.

(1.5)

\inf_{g,h\in\Gamma}\tau(g,h)>0.

Theorem
1.2
answers affirmatively the question whether property (T) can be characterized through group-invariant percolations raised by Lyons in
[
39
, p. 1123]
. It is important to point out two aspects: First, the direct consequence (i.e., necessity) of Kazhdan’s property was known and is due to Lyons and Schramm
[
43
]
. Second, the threshold
\alpha^{*}
is not elusive and quantitative estimates in terms of Kazhdan pairs are available (see Section
4.1
, in particular Theorem
4.1
and Remark
9
, for precise statements). The proof of the converse direction (i.e., sufficiency) is new with the argument relying heavily on the method developed here.

As in the case of the Haagerup property, the next two remarks put Theorem
1.2
into the respective context.

Remark
3

(
Percolation interpretation of Kazhdan’s property (T))

Theorem
1.2
provides the following probabilistic interpretation of property (T): picture the Cayley graph again as an infinite, connected network and consider the task of designing an invariant, random strategy to disconnect the graph with the constraint that, on average, at most
1-\alpha
percent of the edges may be removed at each vertex. Theorem
1.2
asserts that every strategy which is
too cheap
(i.e. costs less than
1-\alpha^{*}
) is bound to fail: for such a strategy, pairs of vertices will remain connected with uniformly positive probability, irrespective of their distance. To further interpret this condition, we recall a result of Lyons and Schramm
[
43
, Theorem 4.1]
that an invariant, ergodic and insertion-tolerant bond percolation satisfying (
1.5
) always has a
unique
infinite cluster. Thus Theorem
1.2
shows that, on a Kazhdan group,
every
such percolation with sufficiently large marginals has a unique infinite cluster, suggesting that Cayley graphs of Kazhdan groups are Cayley graphs with a strong tendency towards forming a unique infinite cluster. This intuition is complemented by a result obtained by Hutchcroft and Pete
[
29
]
in a recent breakthrough regarding the ergodic-theoretic cost of Kazhdan groups, which states that Cayley graphs of Kazhdan groups admit invariant percolations with arbitrarily small marginals and with a unique infinite cluster. To summarize, Theorem
1.2
asserts that Cayley graphs of Kazhdan groups are distinguished by the property of being very robust against all attempts of disconnecting them. Interestingly, this robustness property is reminiscent of similar features of expander graphs in the setting of finite graphs.

Remark
4

(
Geometric interpretation of Kazhdan’s property (T) and non-QI-invariance)

Theorem
1.2
also entails concrete insight about the geometry of Kazhdan groups: their Cayley graphs are
highly connected
at all distances and they are thus able to withstand any cheap attempt of disconnecting them (in the probabilistic sense of Remark
3
). We also note that, like the Haagerup property, property (T) is not QI-invariant, see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem 3.6.5]
. This fact may lead to the perception that property (T) is not reflected in the geometric structure of the Cayley graph in a meaningful or intuitive way – from this perspective, Theorem
1.2
and the underlying geometric intuition described above may be surprising (to elaborate on that point, property (T) is by definition a property of the complete unitary representation theory of the group, whose encoding in the Cayley graph is unclear and seems to be quite challenging to decipher, if at all possible). Our approach of adding a natural source of randomness thus seems to be very useful to identify rigorously features of the geometry of Kazhdan groups.

Further results related to property (T):
In this context, we consider groups with relative property (T) (see Section
4.2
for the definition), extend Theorem
1.2
to this setting (see Theorem
4.3
) and provide an application of the corresponding threshold to a well-known problem in Bernoulli percolation: namely, we prove that the uniqueness threshold does not belong to the uniqueness phase in Bernoulli bond percolation on any Cayley graph of an infinite group which has property (T) relative to an infinite, normal subgroup (see Theorem
4.4
). We note that a more general result has appeared in
[
20
]
, but the argument we give here is very different: our argument is probabilistic and based on the aforementioned threshold, an approach to ruling out a unique infinite cluster at specific values of the survival parameter due to Benjamini, Lyons, Peres and Schramm
[
7
]
and ideas described by Hutchcroft and Pete in
[
29
]
.

In this context, we also provide an estimate of a similar threshold appearing for groups which do not have the Haagerup property, also known as the
weak
Kazhdan property (see Proposition
4.5
). Under an additional symmetry assumption, our probabilistic argument for non-uniqueness at the uniqueness threshold also applies to non-Haagerup groups using that corresponding threshold (see Corollary
4.6
).

Let us conclude by pointing out that although we state our results for bond percolations, the analogues for
site percolations

– i.e. random subgraphs induced by an invariant random subset of vertices –
are straightforward (for the Haagerup property, see Theorem
3.9
).

1.2.
Decay rate of two-point functions for concrete examples of Haagerup groups.

The construction we develop is widely applicable and yields natural models as well as exponential decay in settings of particular interest. This is emphasized in the final part of the article by an in-depth discussion of three classes of examples which have historically been of great relevance for both probability theory and geometric group theory (several more examples are described along the way, see Example
3.1
, Example
3.2
, Example
5.2.1
and Example
5.2.2
).

1.2.1.
Lamplighters over free groups.

The first class of examples we treat are so-called
lamplighter groups
over free groups. These are restricted wreath products of the form
H\wr\mathbb{F}_{r}
, where the
base group

\mathbb{F}_{r}
is a free group of rank
r\in\mathbb{N}
and the
lamp group

H
is finite (see Section
5.1
for precise definitions). It is a non-trivial fact
[
15
]
that these groups have the Haagerup property. More generally, stability of the Haagerup property under wreath products was established in
[
16
]
. Lamplighter groups, especially ones of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, have received substantial attention in the theory of random walks starting with
[
33
]
. Let us mention the breakthrough results about identification of the Poisson boundary
[
18
,
19
]
for
d\geq 5
and
[
42
]
for
d=3,4
. See also
[
34
]
for an investigation of the Poisson boundary of random walks on lamplighters over free groups. For Bernoulli percolation, Lyons and Schramm
[
43
]
established that any Cayley graph of a lamplighter group over a finitely generated base group with non-trivial, finite lamp group has non-trivial uniqueness phase, i.e.
p_{u}<1
. In particular, this implies that the two-point function of Bernoulli percolation is bounded away from zero for sufficiently large marginals. On the other hand, in the setting of invariant percolations, we prove the following result (see Theorem
5.1
).

Theorem 1.3

(
Large marginals and exponential decay).

Let
H
be a finite group, let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be any Cayley graph of
H\wr\mathbb{F}_{r}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and with two-point functions having exponential decay, i.e.

(1.6)

{\rm e}^{-\beta|g|}\leq\tau(o,g)\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma|g|}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,g\in\Gamma,

where
\beta,\gamma>0
such that
\beta/\gamma\leq C
.

Remark
5

(
On the geometry of lamplighters)

A distinguishing feature of Theorem
1.3
is given by the fact that the geometry of the wreath product
H\wr\mathbb{F}_{r}
is considerably different from the geometry of the base free group: any Cayley graph of
H\wr\mathbb{F}_{r}
has one end and even
p_{u}<1

[
43
]
(in contrast to
\mathbb{F}_{r}
). To elaborate on that point, without using our construction (see Theorem
3.6
), even finding an invariant percolation model with large marginals and two-point function vanishing at infinity (not necessarily exponentially) seems to be challenging for these graphs.

1.2.2.
Co-compact Fuchsian groups

The second class of examples we treat are
planar
Cayley graphs. We are particularly interested in one-ended, nonamenable examples, i.e. Cayley graphs which embed in a nice way into the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, see
[
8
]
. Notably, these include the canonical Cayley graphs of co-compact Fuchsian groups, an example considered in percolation theory first in
[
36
]
. For important results about percolation in the planar hyperbolic setting, Bernoulli as well as general invariant, we refer to
[
8
,
36
,
37
,
41
]
. In this context, let us mention that several fundamental conjectures about Bernoulli percolation were recently established for all Cayley graphs of Gromov hyperbolic groups in
[
28
]
.

In the planar hyperbolic setting (and more generally for groups with a proper, co-compact action on
n
-dimensional real hyperbolic space), our construction yields a natural percolation model defined by removing edges along boundaries of half-spaces of the natural Poisson process (see Section
5.2
for details). These exhibit exponential decay, from which we addtionally derive the number of infinite clusters in the percolations guaranteed by Theorem
1.1
for many examples, including canonical Cayley graphs of co-compact Fuchsian groups (see Theorem
5.4
).

Theorem 1.4

(
Large marginals, exponential decay and infinitely many infinite clusters).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a planar, nonamenable Cayley graph with one end. Then for every
\alpha<1
, there exists an
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
, such that
\tau(o,\cdot)
has exponential decay (i.e., (
1.6
) holds) and such that it has infinitely many infinite clusters.

1.2.3.
The special case of amenable groups.

Recall that amenable groups have the Haagerup property
[
4
]
. As mentioned above, our construction is unifying in the sense that it covers the constructive part of the proof of Theorem
1
from
[
6
]
in the case of an amenable group. More precisely, we obtain the following result (see Proposition
5.7
).

Proposition 1.5

(
Recovering the amenable case).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated amenable group
\Gamma
. There exists an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls such that the construction used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
yields, for every
\alpha<1
, a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and with no infinite clusters.

We note that information about the decay rate of the two-point function does not suffice to characterize amenability (indeed, as mentioned before and discussed in Example
2.2.2
, supercritical Bernoulli bond percolation on a regular tree of degree at least three shows that even exponential decay of the two-point function does not imply finiteness of percolation clusters). However, also in the amenable setting a close connection with positive definite functions, which are not two-point functions, exists. Essentially the idea is that for any percolation with only finite clusters, there is a canonical way to associate a random positive definite function to the cluster of the identity. This approach and a characterization of amenable groups via such positive definite functions were first described in
[
45
, Theorem 1.6]
, which served as the motivation of the current work. These positive definite functions provide a large class of probabilistic examples of functions arising naturally in the study of Schur multipliers of
C^{*}
-algebras associated to
\Gamma
, which implement certain operator algebraic approximation properties. Their specific form was then used to provide asymptotic decompositions and establish limiting behavior for sequences of such multipliers by means of the group-invariant compactification constructed in
[
47
]
; we refer the interested reader to
[
45
]
for details.

1.3.
Novel ideas of the proofs.

Let us underline the key ideas involved in the proofs and the main technical contributions of the present work. To start off, we point out that while the Haagerup property has played a major role in operator algebras and geometric group theory, it does not seem to have appeared in concrete probabilistic contexts. In particular, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, there are no applications of the Haagerup property to percolation, cf.
[
41
]
. Two observations may explain this:

(i)

the Haagerup property is a general property, enjoyed by many groups, whose “common geometric features” are not at all apparent (cf. Section
1.4
below).

(ii)

the Haagerup property has several equivalent reformulations (see e.g.
[
12
]
), most of which are quite abstract and may thus seem unrelated to intuitive probabilistic behavior.

To elaborate on the second point, while the proof of any characterization of the Haagerup property may use the reformulation most useful for each direction of the equivalence, the first difficulty clearly lies in formulating a suitable statement. Now the first main contribution of the present work is not only to provide such a statement, but one which fits into a natural probabilistic framework and leads to several ramifications.

More specifically, the properties, which percolations on groups with the Haagerup property should or should not have, are far from clear. However, one possibility to arrive at the statement of Theorem
1.1
is to observe that the ”if” direction is a consequence of a classical observation by Aizenman and Newman
[
2
]
that the two-point function of an invariant percolation is positive definite, combined with a particular formulation of the Haagerup property in terms of positive definite functions. Note that there is a priori no reason for the converse to be true and that there would be two general approaches towards proving it, either through abstract reasoning or a construction. In this context, recall that a constructive approach was used for amenability
[
6
]
utilizing the existence of Følner sets and the mass-transport principle, but in the present setting, Følner sets are not available and the mass-transport principle would be useful for quantities of the from
\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\tau(o,g)
instead of the desired pointwise behavior of
\tau(o,g)
,
g\in\Gamma
. Nonetheless, a constructive approach is more desirable from a probabilistic point of view. In fact, we are interested not in “some” construction, but one in which the probabilistic and geometric mechanisms converge, and the ways in which different formulations of the Haagerup property are linked, become evident. While these requirements are somewhat at odds with the need for a “flexible” construction (cf. (i) above), there is an important reason for asking for these additional difficulties to be addressed, namely that such an approach is likely to find further applications.

Let us now describe the explicit construction put forward in this article. While a construction with built-in independence is desirable for its tractability, Bernoulli percolation, except for very specific examples, will not have the properties in Theorem
1.1
. In general, we therefore have to introduce (strong) dependencies between edges. It is nonetheless instructive to look at the basic example where Bernoulli percolation suffices, which is that of a free group. Essentially, the reason independent percolation suffices here is that every edge plays the role of a “wall”, whose removal breaks the graph into two disconnected components. This simple observation leads to a proof of Theorem
1.1
in the special case of groups acting properly on trees (cf. Proposition
3.1
), an important subclass of groups with the Haagerup property. The next important observation is that it is not the tree-structure, but the ”wall-structure”, which makes this argument work. This is demonstrated by our proof of Theorem
1.1
for groups acting properly on
spaces with walls

[
26
]
(cf. Proposition
3.3
). Here a countable set of ”walls” is deleted independently, and the resulting random set of walls is used to define percolations with more intricate dependence structure. In both of these constructions, the probabilistic and geometric mechanisms at play are clearly visible. To prove Theorem
1.1
, we then develop a machinery which yields similar behavior for all groups with the Haagerup property: our starting point are infinite measure spaces called
spaces with measured walls

[
11
]
, which are quadruplets
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
where
X
is a set,
{\mathcal{W}}
is a set of partitions of
X
into two classes (a partition is called a
wall
),
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
{\mathcal{W}}
and
\mu
is a measure on
{\mathcal{B}}
such that for every two distinct points
x
and
y
in
X
, the set
{\mathcal{W}}(x,y)
of walls separating
x
and
y
is
{\mathcal{B}}
-measurable and
\mu({\mathcal{W}}(x,y))<\infty
. We will be interested mainly in
invariant actions
on spaces with measured walls, which are actions on the set
X
which extend to a measure-preserving action on
({\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
(see Section
3.3
for details). Now, in the continuum setup, the next key new idea is to employ invariant (Poisson) point processes on spaces with measured walls, i.e. the continuous analogue of deleting walls independently. The resulting random countable set of walls is then used to build invariant bond percolations with suitable dependence structures (see Theorem
3.5
) satisfying the required properties of large marginals and vanishing connectivity. This construction is the second main contribution of the present work. In fact, this approach provides a generalized machinery to build invariant percolations, with explicit control of marginals and two-point function decay, from invariant actions on spaces with measured walls (see Theorem
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
for details). In this general form, the construction addresses all the aforementioned difficulties and, indeed, can be successfully applied in several further situations, as we will describe below.

Regarding the characterization of property (T) given in Theorem
1.2
, the situation is reversed compared to that of the Haagerup property. Here, the implication of Kazhdan’s property (T) follows from the aforementioned observation that two-point functions are positive definite (this is the direction which was previously known
[
43
]
– here a different, quantitative proof is given based on
[
30
]
). Note that property (T), which is essentially designed to be a very strong version of non-amenability, has strong
consequences
for probabilistic models, including percolation. In fact, it is in part due to this reason that property (T) has played a notable role in percolation, cf.
[
41
]
. Now the fact that the resulting condition for percolation actually
suffices to characterize property (T)
is the new contribution and the tool for deducing this new direction is also our construction of building percolations using spaces with measured walls (see Section
4.1
).

Let us emphasize that the current method is quite robust, allowing us to go beyond the two characterizations of the Haagerup and Kazhdan properties in several ways. First, it is more general as shown by the aforementioned characterization of relative property (T) (Theorem
4.3
), the subsequent application of the method to Bernoulli percolation at the uniqueness threshold for such groups (Theorem
4.4
) and the fact that we also recover the amenable case (see Proposition
5.7
). Secondly, this approach yields much more information, as underscored by the
aforementioned generalized quantitative estimates on the two-point functions (Theorem
3.5
- Corollary
3.7
) which are leveraged to analyze the prominent examples from Section
1.2.1
and Section
1.2.2
.
In fact, the key ingredient in the proofs of Theorem
1.3
, and in the proof of the part of Theorem
1.4
which does not deal with the number of infinite clusters, is also the aforementioned construction. For these applications, we then use the existence of measure definite functions with linear growth for these groups. Existence of such functions is known in the second case, and has been proved in the first case in
[
16
]
(we present a simplified, self-contained argument here). The number of infinite clusters in Theorem
1.4
is then derived by combining three additional ingredients: non-amenability in the form of Theorem
1
, a result about group-invariant percolation in the hyperbolic plane
[
8
]
and the observation that the Harris-FKG-inequality of the underlying Poisson process passes to such an inequality also for the percolations we construct. We refer to Section
5
for details.

1.4.
Background on the Haagerup and Kazhdan properties and discussion.

A locally compact, second countable group
G
has the
Haagerup property
, or is
a-T-menable
in the terminology of Gromov
[
23
]
, if it admits a continuous, affine, isometric action on a Hilbert space
H
, which is metrically proper (i.e. for every bounded
B\subset H
, the set
\{g\in G\colon gB\cap B\neq\emptyset\}\subset G
has compact closure), see e.g.
[
12
]
. Similarly, the group
G
has
Kazhdan’s property (T)
if every continuous, affine, isometric action on a Hilbert space has a fixed point (for finitely generated groups, this definiton is equivalent to the one given in (
1.4
), see e.g.
[
5
,
12
]
). There are alternative characterizations of both properties in terms of positive and negative definite functions, which are classical and which we review in Section
2.1
, resp. Section
4.1
.

The origins of the Haagerup property can be traced back to seminal work of Haagerup
[
25
]
on approximation properties of group
C^{*}
-algebras of free groups. Groups with the Haagerup property form a large class with classical examples provided by compact groups, amenable groups, free groups, Coxeter groups, groups acting properly on trees and isometry groups of real hyperbolic spaces, see e.g.
[
12
]
. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for more examples, background as well as a survey of various results about the Haagerup property. On the other hand, groups with property (T) form a quite special class of groups, the main examples being
{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
(and
{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
) for
n\geq 3
. For more examples, background and a survey of various striking results about property (T), see
[
5
]
. Two measure-theoretic characterizations of the Haagerup and Kazhdan properties were previously known. On the one hand, Jolissaint
[
12
, Theorem 2.1.3]
showed that a locally compact, second countable group has the Haagerup property if and only if it has a measure preserving action on a standard probability space which is strongly mixing and contains a Følner sequence. This result is an analogue of the characterization of property (T), due to Schmidt
[
54
]
and Connes and Weiss
[
13
]
, which states that
G
has property (T) if and only if every measure preserving ergodic action is strongly ergodic. On the other hand, answering a question posed in
[
12
]
, Glasner
[
21
, Theorem 13.21]
showed that a countable group has the Haagerup property if and only if the closure of the set of
\Gamma
-invariant, mixing probability measures on
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
is convex. This characterization is also an analogue of the well-known probabilistic characterization of property (T) due to Glasner and Weiss
[
22
]
, which states that a countable group
\Gamma
has property (T) if and only if in the set of
\Gamma
-invariant probability measures on
\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}
, the set of ergodic measures is closed, equivalently, is not dense. In comparison with these existing results, perhaps distinguishing features of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are provided by the probabilistic meaning entailed and the concrete insight provided about the geometry of the group, see Remarks
1
-
4
. On a similar note, recall that the Haagerup property is a generalization of amenability
[
4
,
12
]
, while property (T) is a strong negation – both of these facts are illustrated in a new and geometric way by Theorem
1.1
, resp. Theorem
1.2
, in combination with Theorem
1
.

2.1.
The Haagerup property, positive definite and conditionally negative definite functions.

We start with some background on the Haagerup property, which, by definition, as mentioned in the introduction, is satisfied by
a locally compact, second countable group
G
if it admits a continuous, affine, isometric action on a Hilbert space, which is metrically proper, see
[
12
]
.
In the current section, we collect the most relevant characterizations of this property for finitely generated
groups
\Gamma
. We first recall the following standard definitions.

•

Positive definiteness:
A function
k\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
positive definite kernel
if the matrix
[k(g,h)]_{g,h\in F}
is positive for any finite subset
F\subset\Gamma
, i.e.

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}k(g_{i},g_{j})\geq 0

for every
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\Gamma
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. It is called
invariant
if it is invariant under the diagonal action of
\Gamma
. A function
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to \mathbb{C}
is called
positive definite
if
k_{\varphi}(g,h)\coloneqq\varphi(g^{-1}h)
is a positive definite kernel. It is called
normalized
if
\varphi(e)=1
.

•

Conditional negative definiteness:
A function
k\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
is called a
conditionally negative definite kernel
if
k(g,g)=0
and
k(g,h)=k(h,g)
for every
g,h\in\Gamma
and

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}k(g_{i},g_{j})\leq 0

for every
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\Gamma
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=0
. It is called
invariant
if it is invariant under the diagonal action of
\Gamma
. A function
\psi\colon\Gamma\to [0,\infty)
is called
conditionally negative definite
if
k_{\psi}(g,h)\coloneqq\psi(g^{-1}h)
is a conditionally negative definite kernel.

•

Measure definiteness:
The notion of measure definiteness will be needed in Section
3.4
.
Following
[
52
, Definition 0.1.1]
, we say that a function
k\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
is a
measure definite kernel
if there exists a measure space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
and a map
S\colon\Gamma\to{\mathcal{B}}
,
g\mapsto S_{g}
such that

k(g,h)=\mu(S_{g}\Delta S_{h})

for every
g,h\in\Gamma
. Similarly, a function
\psi\colon\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
is called
measure definite
if the kernel
k_{\psi}(g,h)=\psi(g^{-1}h)
is measure definite.

According to a well-known result of Schoenberg, a function
\psi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is conditionally negative definite if and only if for every
t\geq 0
, the map
g\mapsto\exp(-t\psi(g))
is positive definite.
The origins of the Haagerup property can be traced back to the seminal work of Haagerup
[
25
]
on group
C^{*}
-algebras of free groups, where, as a key ingredient, it was shown that the word-length function

|\cdot|
on the finitely generated free group
\mathbb{F}_{r}
(w.r.t. the standard set of generators) is conditionally negative definite. Therefore, by Schoenberg’s correspondence, Haagerup’s result can be restated by saying that, for each
t\geq 0
, the map
\mathbb{F}_{r}\ni g\mapsto\exp(-t|g|)
is positive definite. Note that if

\varphi_{j}(g):=\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{j}|g|\Big)\qquad j\geq 1,g\in\Gamma,

then each
\varphi_{j}
vanishes at infinity and the sequence converges pointwise to
1
.
In this vein, below we summarize the characterizations of the Haagerup property in terms of positive and conditionally negative definite functions that are relevant for our purposes:

Theorem 2.1

(Characterizations of the Haagerup property, cf.
[
12
, Theorem 2.1.1]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Gamma
has the Haagerup property;

(ii)

there exists a conditionally negative definite function
\psi
on
\Gamma
that is proper (the latter condition means that
\lim_{|g|\to\infty}\psi(g)=\infty
).

(iii)

there exists a sequence of normalized, positive definite functions on
\Gamma
which vanish at infinity and converge pointwise to
1
.

There are further characterizations of the Haagerup property – for example, it is also known that
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property if and only if it admits a mixing (or
C_{0}
) representation containing almost invariant vectors
[
12
]
. Since
\Gamma
is amenable if and only if its left-regular representation admits almost invariant vectors, it follows that amenable (e.g. abelian or compact) groups satisfy the Haagerup property.

2.2.
Group-invariant percolations.

We first introduce some graph-theoretic terminology. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V({\mathcal{G}}),E({\mathcal{G}}))

and symmetric edge set

between two vertices

is defined to be the length of a shortest path in

which connects
u
and
v
and is denoted by

. A

is a connected graph without cycles and a

is a graph all of whose connected components are trees. The

of

{\mathcal{G}}

{\mathcal{G}}

An

. It is

. The graph

if
\inf|\partial K|/|K|=0
, where

Given the Cayley graph

\omega

for the cluster containing

. We write

for the degree of
g\in\Gamma
as a vertex in the configuration
\omega
. For

, the

\tau(g,h):=\mathbb{P}\big[g\leftrightarrow h\big]

is defined to be the probability that
g
and

are in the same cluster of the percolation configuration.

2.2.1.
Positive definite functions in invariant percolations.

The main link between the objects defined in Section

Lemma 2.2

(Two-point functions of invariant percolations are positive definite functions)

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma

\mathbb{P}
be a

As pointed out in the introduction, this observation is due to Aizenman and Newman

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}\tau(g_{i},g_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}\mathbb{E}\big[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{g_{i}\leftrightarrow g_{j}\}}\big]=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\ \sum_{i,j}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{g_{i}\leftrightarrow g_{j}\}}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\ \sum_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}}\,\sum_{\{g_{i},g_{j}\}\subset C}\overline{a_{i}}a_{j}\biggr]=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\ \sum_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}}\,\Bigl|\sum_{g_{i}\in C}a_{i}\Bigr|^{2}\biggr]\geq 0,

2.2.2.

As introduced in Section

is the bond percolation in which every edge is kept independently at random with fixed probability

. By a result of Newman and Schulman
[

(see also

or

is defined as the infimum over all

such that in

p_{u}=p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})
is the infimum over all
p\in[0,1]
such that in

-Bernoulli bond percolation, there is a unique infinite cluster almost surely. By the well-known

, increasing events are positively correlated in any Bernoulli bond percolation
[

.

Example 2.3
(Two-point function vanishing at infinity vs. finite clusters) As mentioned in the introduction, vanishing at infinity of the two-point function does not necessarily imply having no infinite clusters. Indeed, let
T
denote the
3
-regular tree. It follows that
p_{c}(T)<1
, see e.g.
[
41
, Theorem 5.15]
. Therefore supercritical
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation
\mathbb{P}_{p}
, that is to say with
p\in(p_{c},1)
, produces an infinite cluster a.s. To see that there are infinitely many infinite clusters, it therefore suffices to rule out the possibility that there is a unique infinite cluster. If there was a unique infinite cluster, then for every two vertices
u
and
v
the Harris-FKG-Inequality and transitivity would imply that
\mathbb{P}_{p}(u\leftrightarrow v)\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(|C(u)|=\infty,|C(v)|=\infty)\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(|C(u)|=\infty)^{2}>0
. This contradicts the fact that the two-point function of
\mathbb{P}_{p}
clearly vanishes at infinity.

We now define aonther threshold
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}
pertinent to Bernoulli percolation. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a countable, locally finite, connected graph
{\mathcal{H}}=(V,E)
. The need to go beyond Cayley graphs at this stage is due to the fact that we want to relate the Haagerup property to phase transitions in Bernoulli percolation performed on
configurations
of invariant percolations, which clearly need not satisfy any form of transitivity. This parameter was introduced by Schonmann
[
55
, Section 3.1]
as a natural critical point associated with Bernoulli percolation. Following the notation in
[
55
, Section 3.1]
, we define

p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}\coloneqq p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}})\coloneqq\sup\bigg\{p\in[0,1]\colon\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{u,v \in V}{d(u,v)>r}}\tau_{p}(u,v)=0\bigg\},

where
\tau_{p}
denotes the two-point function of
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
{\mathcal{H}}
. A related parameter, also introduced by Schonmann
[
55
, Section 3.1]
, is defined as

\displaystyle\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}\coloneqq\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{p\in[0,1]\colon\inf_{u,v\in V}\tau_{p}(u,v)>0\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigg\{p\in[0,1]\colon\inf_{u,v\in V}\tau_{p}(u,v)=0\bigg\}.

It is immediate from the definitions that

(2.1)

p_{c}({\mathcal{H}})\leq p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}})\leq\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}}).\vskip 2.84526pt

If
{\mathcal{H}}
is quasi-transitive, an application of the Harris-FKG-Inequality as in Example
2.2.2
shows that
\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}\leq p_{u}
. Thus, for quasi-transitive
{\mathcal{H}}
we may extend (
2.1
) as follows

(2.2)

p_{c}({\mathcal{H}})\leq p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}})\leq\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}({\mathcal{H}})\leq p_{u}({\mathcal{H}}).\vskip 2.84526pt

By Lyons and Schramm
[
43
, Theorem 4.1]
,
p_{u}=\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}
for Cayley graphs. We note that for every tree
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=\overline{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=p_{u}=1
. For a further discussion of these parameters see
[
55
]
.

2.3.
Point processes.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
be a measurable space. A measure on
X
is called
s
-finite
if it is a countable sum of finite measures. Note that every
\sigma
-finite measure is
s
-finite. Let
\mathbf{N}\coloneqq\mathbf{N}(X)
denote the space of all measures on
X
which can be written as countable sums of finite,
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-valued measures. We equip
\mathbf{N}
with the
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(X)
generated by the collection of all sets of the form

\{\mu\in\mathbf{N}(X)\colon\mu(B)=k\} \quad B\in{\mathcal{B}},k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

A
point process
on
X
is a random element of
(\mathbf{N},\mathcal{N})
. A point process
\eta
on
X
is called
proper
if there exist random elements
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
and an
\overline{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
-valued random variable
M
such that

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\xi_{i}\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Let
\lambda
be an
s
-finite measure. A
Poisson process
with
intensity measure

\lambda
is a point process
\eta
on
X
with the following two properties:

(i)

for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
the distribution of
\eta(B)
is Poisson with parameter
\lambda(B)
, that is to say

\mathbb{P}[\eta(B)=k]={\rm e}^{-\lambda(B)}\frac{\lambda(B)^{k}}{k!}\quad\text{for every } k\in\mathbb{N}_{0};

(ii)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every collection of pairwise disjoint sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, the random variables
\eta_{p}(B_{1}),\ldots,\eta_{p}(B_{n})
are independent.

We need the standard fact that such processes exist.

Theorem 2.4

(Existence of the Poisson process, cf.
[
38
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

Let
\lambda
be an
s
-finite measure on a measurable space
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
, then there exists a Poisson process on
X
with intensity measure
\lambda
.

A useful property of Poisson processes is that they satisfy a version of the Harris-FKG-Inequality, which we now recall
[
38
, Section 20.3]
: Given
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, a measurable real-valued function
f
on
\mathbf{N}(X)
is called
increasing on

B
if

f(\mu+\delta_{x})\geq f(\mu)\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\mu\in\mathbf{N}(X)\,\,\mbox{and all}\,\,x\in B,

and is called
decreasing on

B
if
(-f)
is increasing on
B
.

Theorem 2.5

(Harris-FKG-Inequality for the Poisson process, cf.
[
38
, Theorem 20.4]
)
.

Let
\eta
be a proper Poisson process on a measurable space
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
with
\sigma
-finite intensity measure
\lambda
and law
\mathbb{P}_{\eta}
. Let
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
and let
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\eta})
be increasing on
B
and decreasing on
X\setminus B
, then

\mathbb{E}\big[f(\eta)f^{\prime}(\eta)\big]\geq\mathbb{E}\big[f(\eta)\big]\,\mathbb{E}\big[f^{\prime}(\eta)\big].

3.
Characterization of the Haagerup property: Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
and provide our general construction. Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
3.3
. Before considering this general case, we demonstrate our result for two sub-classes of groups with the Haagerup property: groups acting properly on trees, see Section
3.1
, and groups acting properly on spaces with walls, see Section
3.2
. In these cases, the construction is more concrete and they can thus serve as useful intuition. Moreover, they provide interesting examples, which are illustrated along the way. In Section
3.4
, we state the generalized version of our construction. We then provide the sufficient criterion for the Haagerup property in terms of Bernoulli percolation, see Section
3.5
. In the final Section
3.6
, we demonstrate the modifications required to obtain the analogous results for site percolation.

3.1.
Percolation and groups acting on trees.

Let
T
be a locally finite tree. We say that
\Gamma

acts properly on
T
if it acts on
T
via automorphisms and such that
|\{g\in\Gamma\colon gF\cap F\neq\emptyset\}|<\infty
for every finite
F\subset T
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
acts properly on a locally finite tree, then for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
T=(V(T),E(T))
be a locally finite tree on which
\Gamma
acts properly and fix a root
x_{0}\in V(T)
. For
p\in[0,1]
, let
\mathbb{P}_{p}
denote
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
T
. If
p<1
we have

(3.1)

\lim_{d(x_{0},x)\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[x_{0}\leftrightarrow x\big]=0

because the removal of any edge in the unique path between
x_{0}
and
x
disconnects the two vertices.

Let
\xi_{p}
denote the configuration of a
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
T
. We define the configuration
\omega_{p}
of a bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
as follows: For each edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, let
e
be in
\omega_{p}
if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

gx_{0}=hx_{0}
,

(ii)

gx_{0}\neq hx_{0}
but
gx_{0}
is connected to
hx_{0}
in
\xi_{p}
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{p}
denote the law of
\omega_{p}
. We first claim that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
. To see this, consider any cylinder event

C=\big\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}\colon\omega(e_{1}),\ldots,\omega(e_{n})=1\,,\,\omega(e_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\omega(e_{m}^{\prime})=0\big\},

where
n,m\geq 1
and
e_{1}=[\gamma_{1},h_{1}],\ldots,e_{n}=[\gamma_{n},h_{n}],e_{1}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{1}^{\prime},h_{1}^{\prime}],\ldots,e_{m}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{m}^{\prime},h_{m}^{\prime}]\in E
. Let
g\in\Gamma
, then

gC=\big\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}\colon\omega(ge_{1}),\ldots,\omega(ge_{n})=1\,,\,\omega(ge_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\omega(ge_{m}^{\prime})=0\big\}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{p}[gC]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{p}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g\gamma_{i}x_{0}\leftrightarrow gh_{i}x_{0}\text{ in }\xi_{p}\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
g\gamma_{j}^{\prime}x_{0}\not\leftrightarrow gh_{j}^{\prime}x_{0}\text{ in }\xi_{p}\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{p}\circ g\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{i}x_{0}\leftrightarrow h_{i}x_{0}\text{ in }\xi_{p}\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}x_{0}\not\leftrightarrow h_{j}^{\prime}x_{0}\text{ in }\xi_{p}\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{P}_{p}[C],

where we have used the fact that
\Gamma
acts on
T
via automorphism and the fact that the
\mathbb{P}_{p}
is
{\rm Aut}(T)
-invariant. This proves
\Gamma
-invariance of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
.

We note that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
clearly has marginals arbitrarily close to
1
for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. This implies that
\mathbf{E}_{p}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. Hence it suffices to prove that the two-point function
\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}[o\leftrightarrow g]
of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
satisfies
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
for every
p<1
. To show this, simply observe that on the event
\{o\leftrightarrow g\}
the unique path which joins
x_{0}
to
gx_{0}
must be contained in
\xi_{p}
. Thus

\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}\big[o\leftrightarrow g\big]\leq\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[x_{0}\leftrightarrow gx_{0}\big].

Moreover, since the action of
\Gamma
on
T
is proper, we have that
\lim_{g\to\infty}d(x_{0},gx_{0})=\infty
. Combined with Equation (
3.1
) we therefore obtain

\lim_{g\to\infty}\tau_{p}(o,g)\leq\lim_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[x_{0}\leftrightarrow gx_{0}\big]=0,

which concludes the proof. ∎

Example 3.2
(Bernoulli-percolation-proof that tree distances are conditionally negative definite)
Let us comment on the particular example when
\Gamma
is a finitely generated free group acting on its canonical Cayley graph. In this case it is easy to see that the two-point function of
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation is given by

\tau_{p}(g,h)=p^{d(g,h)}={\rm e}^{\log(p)d(g,h)}.

Consider the distance function
|g|=d(o,g)
on
\Gamma
. Since
pa\mapsto\log(p)
is a bijection from
(0,1)
to
(-\infty,0)
, we obtain that the function
g \mapsto{\rm e}^{-\lambda|g|}
is positive definite for every
\lambda>0
. By Schoenberg’s theorem, the distance function is conditionally negative definite, see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem C.3.2]
. This recovers a fundamental result due to Haagerup
[
25
, Lemma 1.2]
using a short probabilistic proof which involves only the most natural percolation model, Bernoulli percolation. Let us point out that an even easier proof was already known, which involves showing that the distance function is measure definite, which immediately implies conditional negative definiteness – measure definiteness in turn may be seen by writing down explicitly an embedding of the Cayley tree into an
L^{1}
-space or by writing the distance as a wall distance; these ideas appear for example in
[
11
,
16
,
40
]
. Our purpose of presenting the above probabilistic proof is to underline a motivating idea, which as we will explore, will generalize to a much broader class of groups with the Haagerup property for which the distance function may be far from being conditionally negative definite.

3.2.
Percolation and groups acting on spaces with walls.

The following terminology was introduced by Haglund and Paulin in
[
26
]
. Let
X
be a set and let
{\mathcal{W}}
be a set of partitions of
X
into two classes. A partition
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
is called a
wall
and we say that
W

separates
two distinct points
x
and
y
in
X
if the points belong to different classes in
W
. The pair
(X,{\mathcal{W}})
is a
space with walls
if, for every two distinct points
x
and
y
in
X
, the set

{\mathcal{W}}(x,y)\coloneqq\{W\in{\mathcal{W}}\colon W\text{ separates }x\text{ and }y\}

is finite. In this case
w(x,y)\coloneqq|{\mathcal{W}}(x,y)|
is called the
number of walls separating
x
and
y
. We say that a finitely generated group
\Gamma

acts properly
on a space with walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}})
if
\Gamma
acts on
X
, preserves the family of walls and for some
x_{0}\in X
(and hence for all
x\in X
) the function
\Gamma\to\mathbb{N}_{0}\,,\,g\mapsto w(x_{0},gx_{0})
is
proper
, that is to say
\lim_{g\to\infty}w(x_{0},gx_{0})=\infty.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
acts properly on a space with walls, then for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
act properly on the space with walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}})
and fix a point
x_{0}\in X
. Let
p\in(0,1)
and let
\{Z_{W}\}_{W\in{\mathcal{W}}}
be iid Bernoulli-
p
-distributed random variables defined on some probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
. We define the configuration
\omega_{p}
of a bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
as follows: For each edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, let
e
be contained in
\omega_{p}
if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

gx_{0}=hx_{0}
,

(ii)

gx_{0}\neq hx_{0}
and for every
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0})
we have
Z_{W}=1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{p}
denote the law of
\omega_{p}
. We again claim that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation: First observe that the maps
\{B,B^{c}\}=W\mapsto gW=\{gB,gB^{c}\}
define an action of
\Gamma
on the set of walls and note that
{\mathcal{W}}(gx,gy)=g{\mathcal{W}}(x,y)
for every
g\in\Gamma,x,y\in X
. Consider any cylinder event

C=\big\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}\colon\omega(e_{1}),\ldots,\omega(e_{n})=1\,,\,\omega(e_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\omega(e_{m}^{\prime})=0\big\},

where
n,m\geq 1
and
e_{1}=[\gamma_{1},h_{1}],\ldots,e_{n}=[\gamma_{n},h_{n}],e_{1}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{1}^{\prime},h_{1}^{\prime}],\ldots,e_{m}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{m}^{\prime},h_{m}^{\prime}]\in E
. Then

\mathbf{P}_{p}[C]=\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}Z_{W}=1\text{ for all }W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{i}x_{0},h_{i}x_{0})\text{ and all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\text{ there exists }W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{j}^{\prime}x_{0},h_{j}^{\prime}x_{0})\text{ such that }Z_{W}=0\end{array}\right].

We define
{\mathcal{V}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{i}x_{0},h_{i}x_{0})
, which implies

\mathbf{P}_{p}[C]=p^{|{\mathcal{V}}|}\mathbf{P}_{p}\Big[\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\text{ there exists }W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{j}^{\prime}x_{0},h_{j}^{\prime}x_{0})\setminus{\mathcal{V}}\text{ such that }Z_{W}=0\Big].

By splitting this event into cases according to which edges
e_{j}^{\prime}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, are separated by the same wall
W
with
Z_{W}=0
, we may decompose the second event as a sum of products of terms of the form
(1-p^{k})
,
k
being the cardinality of the appearing set of walls in the decomposition. For
g\in\Gamma
, we now observe that the push-forward under
g
of the above decomposition gives a similar decomposition of the event
gC
, where all appearing cardinalities agree. This proves that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

We observe that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
has marginals arbitrarily close to
1
for
p
sufficiently close to
1
: Consider any edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{p}\big[[g,h]\in\omega_{p}\big]

\displaystyle={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{gx_{0}=hx_{0}\}}+{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{gx_{0}\neq hx_{0}\}}\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{W}=1\text{ for all }W\in{\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0})\big]

\displaystyle={\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{gx_{0}=hx_{0}\}}+{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\{gx_{0}\neq hx_{0}\}}p^{w(gx_{0},hx_{0})},

which converges to
1
as
p\to 1
. Hence
\mathbf{E}_{p}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. It remains to show that the two-point function
\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}[o\leftrightarrow g]
of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
satisfies
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
. To see this let
g\in\Gamma
be arbitrary, let
o=h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{n}=g
be any path of vertices joining
o
to
g
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and let
W
be any wall separating
x_{0}
and
gx_{0}
. Let
B\sqcup B^{c}=X
be the partition determined by
W
and assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}\in B
. By assumption
gx_{0}\in B^{c}
and thus

i\coloneqq\max\{j\colon 0\leq j\leq n-1\text{ and }h_{j}x_{0}\in B\}

is well defined and satisfies
h_{i}x_{0}\in B
as well as
h_{i+1}x_{0}\in B^{c}
. In other words the wall
W
also separates
h_{i}x_{0}
and
h_{i+1}x_{0}
. With this observation, we now prove the claim: Assume that
o
is connected to
g
in
\omega_{p}
, then there exists some path of open edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
joining
o
to
g
in
{\mathcal{G}}
. The above argument shows that for every wall
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(x_{0},gx_{0})
there exists an edge
e=[h,\gamma]\in\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
such that
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(hx_{0},\gamma x_{0})
. By definition,
e
being open implies that
Z_{W}=1
. Therefore we obtain that on the event
\{o\leftrightarrow g\}
, we must have
Z_{W}=1
for every
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(x_{0},gx_{0})
. Thus

\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}\big[o\leftrightarrow g\big]\leq\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{W}=1\text{ for every }W\in{\mathcal{W}}(x_{0},gx_{0})\big]=p^{w(x_{0},gx_{0})}.

Since
\Gamma
acts properly, it follows that

\lim_{g\to\infty}\tau_{p}(o,g)\leq\lim_{g\to\infty}p^{w(x_{0},gx_{0})}=0,

which concludes the proof. ∎

Example 3.4
(Wall structures for Euclidean lattices and trees)
We give two familiar examples of spaces with walls:

\bullet
A locally finite tree
T
has a natural structure of space with walls by declaring
{\mathcal{W}}
to consist of partitions into two connected components which are obtained by removing an edge. A group acts properly on
(T,{\mathcal{W}})
if and only if it acts properly on the tree
T
.

\bullet
The graph
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
can be split into two discrete half-spaces orthogonal to the horizontal axis, resp. vertical axis, by cutting for fixed
x\in\mathbb{Z}
along all edges of the form
[(x,y),(x+1,y)]
, resp. by cutting for fixed
y\in\mathbb{Z}
along all edges of the form
[(x,y),(x,y+1)]
. This defines a family of walls on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that the number of walls separating two points is equal to their graph distance. Clearly the same applies in the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

3.3.
Invariant point processes and groups acting on spaces with measured walls.

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
. As mentioned in Section
1.3
, a key idea will be to introduce invariant Poisson point processes on suitable infinite measure spaces. These measure spaces generalize the spaces used appearing in Section
3.1
and Section
3.2
in such a way that the main intuition behind the constructions of the previous two sections is still valid. For this purpose, the appropriate generalization turns out to be the concept of a space with measured walls, which is due to Cherix, Martin and Valette
[
11
]
, and which we now recall.

Let
X
be a set,
{\mathcal{W}}
a set of walls on
X
,
{\mathcal{B}}
a
\sigma
-algebra on
{\mathcal{W}}
and
\mu
a measure on
{\mathcal{B}}
. The
4
-tuple
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
is a
space with measured walls
if, for every two distinct points
x
and
y
in
X
, the set
{\mathcal{W}}(x,y)
of walls separating
x
and
y
is
{\mathcal{B}}
-measurable and
w(x,y)\coloneqq\mu({\mathcal{W}}(x,y))<\infty
. Note that if
\mu
is a counting measure, then we recover a space with walls. We say that a finitely generated group
\Gamma

acts properly
on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
if
\Gamma
acts on
X
, preserves the family of walls, preserves the function
w(\cdot,\cdot)
and for some
x_{0}\in X
(and hence for all
x\in X
) the function
\Gamma\to[0,\infty)\,,\,g\mapsto w(x_{0},gx_{0})
is proper. This again extends the definition of a proper action on a space with walls in the case that
\mu
is a counting measure. Note that

(3.2)

\Gamma\times\Gamma\to[0,\infty)\,,\,D(g,h)\coloneqq w(gx_{0},hx_{0})

defines a pseudo-distance on
\Gamma
which we call the
wall distance
.

It was proved in
[
11
, Proposition 1]
that a locally compact group which acts properly on a space with measured walls has the Haagerup property. Moreover it was proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1.(1)]
that a countable discrete group with the Haagerup property admits a proper action on a space with measured walls. In fact, building on the ideas developed in
[
52
]
, the proof given in
[
11
]
provides explicitly a space with measured walls and a corresponding proper action, see also
[
12
, Proposition 7.5.1]
. Namely, for a finitely generated group
\Gamma
, we may consider the following:

•

Let
X=\Gamma
on which
\Gamma
acts via left-multiplication and let

\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}\setminus\{(0,0,\ldots),(1,1,\ldots)\},

the set of all non-empty and non-full subsets of
\Gamma
, equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
. Then
\Gamma
has a natural measurable action on
\Omega
defined by
g\omega(x)=\omega(g^{-1}x)
.

•

For
\omega\in\Omega
define
W_{\omega}=\{\omega,\omega^{c}\}
and let
{\mathcal{W}}=\{W_{\omega}\colon\omega\in\Omega\}
. Clearly
{\mathcal{W}}
defines a family of walls on
X
which is preserved by the action of
\Gamma
.

•

The set
{\mathcal{W}}
can be identified with the quotient of
\Omega
by the fixed-point free involution
\omega\mapsto\omega^{c}
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the direct image of
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
.

•

For
g\in\Gamma
define
S_{g}=\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\omega(g)=1\}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
. Let
\psi
be a conditionally negative definite function on
\Gamma
. By
[
52
, Proposition 1.4]
, there exists a regular Borel measure
\nu
on
\Omega
such that

\sqrt{\psi(g^{-1}h)}=\nu(S_{g}\Delta S_{h})\quad\text{for all }g,h\in\Gamma.

Moreover, it follows from the proof of
[
52
, Theorem 2.1]
that
\nu
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Let
\mu
denote the direct image of
\nu
on
{\mathcal{W}}
. In particular

\mu({\mathcal{W}}(g,h))=\nu(S_{g}\Delta S_{h})=\sqrt{\psi(g^{-1}h)}<\infty\quad\text{for all }g,h\in\Gamma.

Then
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
defines a space with measured walls. Moreover, the induced
\Gamma
action on the set of walls is measurable, leaves
\mu
invariant and satisfies
{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma g,\gamma h)=\gamma{\mathcal{W}}(g,h)
. In particular the function
w(\cdot,\cdot)
is preserved. Now if
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property then there exists a proper, conditionally negative definite function
\psi
on
\Gamma
. In this case,
\Gamma
clearly acts properly on
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property, then for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a proper, conditionally negative definite function on
\Gamma
and consider the proper action of
\Gamma
on the space with measured walls
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
described above. Let
p\in(0,1)
and let
\eta_{p}
be a Poisson process on
{\mathcal{W}}
with intensity measure
(1-p)\mu
defined on some probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
. Existence follows from Theorem
2.4
because the measure
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite. We define the configuration
\omega_{p}
of a bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
as follows: For each edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, let
e
be contained in
\omega_{p}
if and only if
\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(g,h))=0
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{p}
denote the law of
\omega_{p}
. We claim that
\mathbf{P}_{p}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation. We first show that, for every
g\in\Gamma
, the process
\eta_{p}\circ g
has the same law under
\mathbb{P}
as
\eta_{p}
. To shorten notation, denote the intensity measure by
\lambda\coloneqq(1-p)\mu
and recall that
\lambda
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Now let
n\geq 1
and consider pairwise disjoint
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Then

\big(\eta_{p}(B_{1}),\ldots,\eta_{p}(B_{n})\big)\overset{d}{=}{\rm Poi}\big(\lambda(B_{1})\big)\otimes\ldots\otimes{\rm Poi}\big(\lambda(B_{n})\big).

Moreover,
gB_{1},\ldots,gB_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}
are pairwise disjoint with
\lambda(gB_{i})=\lambda(B_{i})
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
by
\Gamma
-invariance of
\lambda
. It follows that

\big(\eta_{p}\circ g(B_{1}),\ldots,\eta_{p}\circ g(B_{n})\big)=\big(\eta_{p}(gB_{1}),\ldots,\eta_{p}(gB_{n})\big)\overset{d}{=}{\rm Poi}\big(\lambda(B_{1})\big)\otimes\ldots\otimes{\rm Poi}\big(\lambda(B_{n})\big).

By
[
38
, Proposition 2.10 (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)]
the point process
\eta_{p}\circ g
has the same law as
\eta_{p}
. Now consider any cylinder event

C=\big\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}\colon\omega(e_{1}),\ldots,\omega(e_{n})=1\,,\,\omega(e_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\omega(e_{m}^{\prime})=0\big\},

where
n,m\geq 1
and
e_{1}=[\gamma_{1},h_{1}],\ldots,e_{n}=[\gamma_{n},h_{n}],e_{1}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{1}^{\prime},h_{1}^{\prime}],\ldots,e_{m}^{\prime}=[\gamma_{m}^{\prime},h_{m}^{\prime}]\in E
. Then

\mathbf{P}_{p}[C]=\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{i},h_{i}))=0\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{j}^{\prime},h_{j}^{\prime}))>0\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{array}\right]

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{p}[gC]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(g\gamma_{i},gh_{i}))=0\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(g\gamma_{j}^{\prime},gh_{j}^{\prime}))>0\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{p}\circ g({\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{i},h_{i}))=0\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\eta_{p}\circ g({\mathcal{W}}(\gamma_{j}^{\prime},h_{j}^{\prime}))>0\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{array}\right],

since
{\mathcal{W}}(g\gamma,gh)=g{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma,h)
for any
\gamma,h\in\Gamma
. This proves
\Gamma
-invariance of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
.

Again,
\mathbf{P}_{p}
has marginals arbitrarily close to
1
for
p
sufficiently close to
1
: Let
e=[g,h]\in E
, then

\mathbf{P}_{p}\big[[g,h]\in\omega_{p}\big]=\mathbb{P}\big[\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(g,h))=0\big]={\rm e}^{-(1-p)\mu({\mathcal{W}}(g,h))}

which, since
0\leq\mu({\mathcal{W}}(g,h))<\infty
, converges to
1
as
p\to 1
. To conclude the proof, it remains to show that the two-point function
\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}[o\leftrightarrow g]
of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
satisfies
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
. Let
g\in\Gamma
and recall from the proof of Proposition
3.3
that, given any path of edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
joining
o
to
g
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and any wall
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(o,g)
, there exists
e\in\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
such that
W
also separates the endpoints of the edge
e
. Writing
{\mathcal{W}}(e)
for the set of walls separating the endpoints of an edge
e
, it follows that

{\mathcal{W}}(o,g)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{W}}(e_{i}).

We obtain that on the event that
o
and
g
are in the same cluster of
\omega_{p}
, we have
\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(o,g))=0
. Thus

\tau_{p}(o,g)=\mathbf{P}_{p}\big[o\leftrightarrow g\big]\leq\mathbb{P}\big[\,\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(o,g))=0\big]={\rm e}^{-(1-p)\mu({\mathcal{W}}(o,g))}.

Since the action of
\Gamma
is proper, it follows that

\lim_{g\to\infty}\tau_{p}(o,g)\leq\lim_{g\to\infty}{\rm e}^{-(1-p)\mu({\mathcal{W}}(o,g))}=0,

which concludes the proof. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Suppose that for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
. Choose a sequence
(\mathbb{P}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolations on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that the two point function
\tau_{i}(o,\cdot)
of each
\mathbb{P}_{i}
vanishes at infinity and such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{i}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\deg(o).

Then

is a sequence of positive definite functions, see Lemma
2.2
, which vanish at infinity and which converge pointwise to

. That is to say

has the Haagerup property. The converse direction is exactly Theorem

above. ∎

3.4.
Quantitative estimates and a generalized construction with invariant point processes.

Our next result is a general version of the construction used in the proof of Theorem
3.5
. We are primarily interested in such a generalization because there exist many known actions of groups on spaces with measured walls which are well understood or have useful geometric features. Our general construction applies to all of these. We first need to introduce the concept of invariance for actions on spaces with measured walls. We say that
\Gamma
has an
invariant action
on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
, if
\Gamma
acts on
X
and the maps

{\mathcal{W}}\to{\mathcal{W}}\,,\,\{B,B^{c}\}=W\mapsto gW=\{gB,gB^{c}\}

define a
{\mathcal{B}}
-measurable
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathcal{W}}
for which the measure
\mu
is invariant.

Theorem 3.6

(
Quantitative estimate on two-point function).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Suppose that
\Gamma
has an invariant action on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
and fix
x_{0}\in X
. For
p\in(0,1)
, define

\alpha_{p}\coloneqq\exp\Bigl(-(1-p)\max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0})\Bigr)\in(0,1).

Then there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]\geq\alpha_{p}\deg(o)
and

\exp\Bigl(-(1-p)\max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0})|g|\Bigr)\leq\tau(o,g)\leq\exp\bigl(-(1-p)w(x_{0},gx_{0})\bigr)\quad\text{for every }g\in\Gamma.

Proof.

We first show that
\Gamma
admits an invariant action on a space with measured walls
(X_{0},{\mathcal{W}}_{0},{\mathcal{B}}_{0},\mu_{0})
such that the measure
\mu_{0}
is
s
-finite and satisfies
\mu_{0}({\mathcal{W}}_{0}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))=\mu({\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))
for all
g,h\in\Gamma
. Consider the set of walls

{\mathcal{W}}_{0}\coloneqq\bigcup_{g,h\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0})\subset{\mathcal{W}}.

Note that
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
as a countable union of
{\mathcal{B}}
-measurable sets. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
be the restriction of
{\mathcal{B}}
to
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
and let
\mu_{0}
be the restriction of
\mu
to
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
. Then
(X,{\mathcal{W}}_{0},{\mathcal{B}}_{0},\mu_{0})
is a space with measured walls because for every two distinct points
x
and
y
in
X
the set
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(x,y)
of walls separating
x
and
y
is given by

{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(x,y)={\mathcal{W}}(x,y)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{0}

and satisfies
\mu_{0}({\mathcal{W}}_{0}(x,y))=\mu({\mathcal{W}}(x,y)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{0})\leq\mu({\mathcal{W}}(x,y))<\infty
. Moreover, the measure
\mu_{0}
is
s
-finite because
\mu_{0}({\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))<\infty
for every
g,h\in\Gamma
. Finally, for every
\gamma,g,h\in\Gamma
and every
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0})
we have that
\gamma W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\gamma gx_{0},\gamma hx_{0})
, thus
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant set and therefore the action of
\Gamma
restricts to an invariant action of
\Gamma
on
(X,{\mathcal{W}}_{0},{\mathcal{B}}_{0},\mu_{0})
as claimed.

Let
p\in(0,1)
. Let
\eta
be a Poisson process on
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}
with intensity measure
(1-p)\mu_{0}
defined on some probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbf{P})
, see Theorem
2.4
. Define the configuration
\omega
of a bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
as follows: For each edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, let
e
be contained in
\omega
if and only if
\eta({\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))=0
. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the law of
\omega
. Since the intensity measure of
\eta
is
\Gamma
-invariant, exactly the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
shows that
\mathbb{P}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation. Now for every edge
e=[g,h]\in E
, we have that

\mathbb{P}[e\in\omega]=\mathbf{P}\big[\eta_{p}({\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))=0\big]={\rm e}^{-(1-p)\mu_{0}({\mathcal{W}}(gx_{0},hx_{0}))}={\rm e}^{-(1-p)w(gx_{0},hx_{0})}

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\sum_{g\sim o}\mathbb{P}\big[[o,g]\in\omega\big]

\displaystyle\geq\deg(o)\min_{o\sim g} \mathbb{P}\big[[o,g]\in\omega\big]

\displaystyle=\deg(o)\exp\Bigl(-(1-p)\max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0})\Bigr).

Moreover, again by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, it follows that

\tau(o,g)\leq\mathbf{P}\big[\eta({\mathcal{W}}(o,g))=0\big]=\exp\bigl(-(1-p)w(x_{0},gx_{0})\bigr)\quad\text{for every }g\in\Gamma.

For the lower bound, let
g\in\Gamma
and choose a shortest path
e_{1},\ldots,e_{|g|}
of edges joining
o
to
g
in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Recall that
{\mathcal{W}}(e_{i})
denotes the set of walls separating the endpoints of the edge
e_{i}
and define

{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{|g|}{\mathcal{W}}(e_{i}).

By the union bound and invariance

\mu_{0}({\mathcal{V}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{|g|}\mu_{0}({\mathcal{W}}(e_{i}))\leq|g| \max_{e\in E}\mu({\mathcal{W}}(e))= |g| \max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0}).

Finally, note that
\{o\leftrightarrow g\}\supset\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{|g|}\in\omega\}=\{\eta({\mathcal{V}})=0\}
and thus

\tau(o,g)=\mathbb{P}\big[o\leftrightarrow g\big]\geq\mathbf{P}\big[\eta({\mathcal{V}})=0\big]={\rm e}^{-(1-p)\mu({\mathcal{V}})}\geq\exp\Bigl(-(1-p)|g| \max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0})\Bigr).

The proof of Theorem
3.6
is thus complete. ∎

3.4.1.
Consequence of Theorem
3.6
.

Our general construction result Theorem
3.6
entails significant quantitative information as we will now explain. We start with the description of the relationship between the growth of wall distances and the decay of the two-point functions of the associated percolations. To state this precisely, we first set up the relevant terminology: Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group equipped with a word length
|\cdot|
and let
\rho\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
be a function.

•

Let
D
be a left-invariant pseudo-distance on
\Gamma
. Following
[
16
, Definition 7.1]
, we say that
\rho
is a
compression function
for
D
if
D(o,g)\geq\rho(|g|)
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

•

If
\Gamma
has an invariant action on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
such that for some
x_{0}\in X
, the
wall distance

D(g,h)=w(gx_{0},hx_{0})
admits
\rho
as a compression function, we say that the action has
\rho
-growth
. If
\rho
is linear, we say that the action has
linear growth
.

•

Let
C>0
be a constant and let
\tau
be the two-point function of a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation. We say that
\tau
has
(C,\rho)
-exponential decay
if there exist
\beta,\gamma>0
such that
\beta/\gamma\leq C
and

{\rm e}^{-\beta|g|}\leq\tau(o,g)\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma\rho(|g|)}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,g\in\Gamma.

If
\rho
is linear, we say that
\tau
has
exponential decay
.

Note that wall distances associated to an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls and measure definite kernels on
\Gamma
can have at most linear growth (by Example
3.1
, resp. Example
3.2
(ii), finitely generated free groups, resp.
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\in\mathbb{N}
, admit wall distances with linear growth).

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
and let
\rho\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
be a function with
\{\rho=0\}=\{0\}
. Consider the following conditions:

(i)

There exists an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls with
\rho
-growth;

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and with
(C,\rho)
-exponential decay.

Then condition (i) implies condition (ii).

The geometric meaning of Corollary
3.7
is explained in the following remark.

Remark
6

(Connection with the equivariant
L^{1}
-compression exponent)

Condition (i) in Corollary
3.7
is related to the
equivariant
L^{1}
-compression exponent

\alpha_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}(\Gamma)
of the group
\Gamma
, which is defined to be the supremum over all
\alpha\in[0,1]
, such that there exists an isometric action of
\Gamma
on an
L^{1}
-space
E
together with a
\Gamma
-equivariant map

with

\|f(g)-f(h)\|_{E}\geq|g^{-1}h|^{\alpha}\vskip 2.84526pt

for all
g,h\in\Gamma
. For background on this exponent, see
[
24
,
48
]
. As observed in
[
16
, Section 7]
, the results in
[
10
]
imply that

(3.3)

\|f(g)-f(h)\|_{E}\vskip 2.84526pt

is a wall distance associated to an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls. Thus
\alpha_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}(\Gamma)
may equivalently be realized as the supremum over all
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that there exists an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls with
\rho_{\alpha}
-growth for the particular choice
\rho_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha}
. In the special case
\rho=\rho_{\alpha}
, condition (i) therefore amounts to requiring
\alpha_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}(\Gamma)\geq\alpha.
In particular, Corollary
3.7
yields a consequence of ”good” equivariant

-compression for the decay rates of two-point functions of invariant bond percolation models.

Proof of Corollary
3.7
:
Let
\Gamma
have an invariant action on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
with
\rho
-growth, i.e. for some
x_{0}\in X
we have that
w(x_{0},gx_{0})\geq\rho(|g|)
for all
g\in\Gamma
. Note that
C\coloneqq\max_{g\sim o}\,w(x_{0},gx_{0})\in(0,\infty)
and let
p\in(0,1)
. By Theorem

, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]\geq{\rm e}^{-(1-p)C}\deg(o)
and

for every

. Define

Since

3.5.
Haagerup property and Bernoulli percolation.

In this section, we prove a sufficient criterion for the Haagerup property, which is inspired by the amenability criterion given in
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
. For the definition of
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}
we refer to Section
2
.

Theorem 3.8

(
Sufficient criterion using Bernoulli percolation).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant random spanning subgraph of
{\mathcal{G}}
with
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=1
a.s.;

(ii)

there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant random nonempty connected subgraph of
{\mathcal{G}}
with
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=1
w.p.p.

Moreover, if
\Gamma
satisfies
(i)
, or equivalently
(ii)
, then
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property.

Proof.

The implication ”(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)” is obvious.

”(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)”: We use a standard way of efficiently augmenting an invariant percolation with a unique (necessarily infinite) cluster to an invariant random spanning subgraph, see e.g.
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
for another application of this strategy. Let
\omega
be a
\Gamma
-invariant random nonempty connected subgraph of
{\mathcal{G}}
with
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=1
with positive probability. Conditioning on this event, we may assume that
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=1
a.s. Now we augment the configuration
\omega
as follows: For every vertex which is not already in
\omega
, choose an edge uniformly among those edges connecting it to a vertex closer to
\omega
in the graph distance of the underlying Cayley graph
{\mathcal{G}}
. The resulting configuration, denoted by
\xi
, is clearly spanning and invariant. Moreover,
\xi\setminus\omega
is a forest. Therefore the property ”
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}(\xi)=1
a.s.” is trivially inherited from the assumption that
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}(\omega)=1
a.s.

Finally, let us prove that (i) (and therefore (ii)) implies the Haagerup property. Therefore, let us assume that condition (i) holds. Let
\omega
be a
\Gamma
-invariant random spanning subgraph of
{\mathcal{G}}
with
p_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm conn}}=1
a.s. Let
p\in(0,1)
and let
\tau_{p}
denote the two-point function of the bond percolation
\xi_{p}
obtained by performing
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
\omega
. As the two-point function of a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation,
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)
defines a positive definite function. Recalling Theorem
2.1
, it is therefore sufficient to show that
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)
vanishes at infinity and converges pointwise to
1
as
p\to 1
. To that end, fix
g\in\Gamma
. Fixing
\omega
, we see that

\lim_{p\to 1}\,\mathbb{P}\Big[o\xleftrightarrow{\xi_{p}}g\,\big|\,\omega\Big]=1

because
o
and
g
are joined by some finite path in the spanning configuration
\omega
, which, with probability converging to one is fully contained in
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation as
p
converges to
1
. The bounded convergence theorem yields that indeed
\lim_{p\to 1}\tau_{p}(o,g)=1
.
We now verify that
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)
vanishes at infinity. To see this, it suffices to show that for every sequence
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\Gamma
with
g_{n}\to\infty
, we have that
\tau_{p}(o,g_{n})\to 0
. Fix such a sequence. Then conditional on the configuration
\omega
, the probability that
o
and
g_{n}
are in the same cluster of
\xi_{p}
converges to zero a.s. as
n\to\infty
by assumption. The claim follows again from the bounded convergence theorem.
∎

3.6.
Haagerup property and site percolation.

Theorem 3.9

(
Group-invariant site percolation and the Haagerup property).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property if and only if for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant site percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{P}[o\in\omega]>\alpha
and
\tau(o,\cdot)\in C_{0}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

The ”if” direction follows exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
above.

To prove the converse, assume that
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property. By Theorem
1.1
there exists a sequence
(\mathbb{P}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolations such that the corresponding two-point functions
\tau_{i}(o,\cdot)
vanish at infinity for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{i}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\deg(o).

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots
denote configurations with law
\mathbb{P}_{i}
defined on some common probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, define the configuration
\xi_{i}
of a site percolation as follows: Given the configuration
\omega_{i}
, remove all vertices which are elements of the vertex boundary of some cluster of
\omega_{i}
. More formally, for a subset
U\subset V
, let

\partial_{V}(U)=\{x\in V\setminus U\colon\text{there exists }y\in U\text{ with }x\sim y\}

denote the
vertex boundary
of
U
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the collection of all clusters of
\omega_{i}
and define, for
x\in V
, that
x\in\xi_{i}
if and only if

x\in V\setminus\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\partial_{V}(C).

Let
\mathbf{P}_{i}
denote the law of
\xi_{i}
. Because the law of
\omega_{i}
is
\Gamma
-invariant,
\mathbf{P}_{i}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant site percolation. Now note that

\{o\in\xi_{i}\}\supset\bigl\{[o,g]\in\omega_{i}\text{ for all }g\sim o\bigr\}

because if all edges incident to
o
are contained in
\omega_{i}
, then
o
and all its neighbors belong to the same cluster and therefore
o
is not an element of the vertex boundary of any cluster. This implies that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{i}[o\in\omega]=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[o\in\xi_{i}]\geq\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl[\,[o,g]\in\omega_{i}\text{ for all }g\sim o\bigr]=1.

On the other hand, note that if
o
and
g
are not in the same cluster of
\omega_{i}
, then the vertex boundary of the cluster of
o
is not contained in
\xi_{i}
and therefore separates
o
and
g
in
\xi_{i}
. Taking complements yields that

\{o\leftrightarrow g\text{ in }\xi_{i}\}\subset\{o\leftrightarrow g\text{ in }\omega_{i}\}.

Hence the two-point function of
\mathbf{P}_{i}
is bounded by
\tau_{i}
, thus also decaying at infinity. It follows that
\mathbf{P}_{i}
, for
i
large enough, has the desired properties. ∎

4.
Characterization of Kazhdan’s property (T): Proof of Theorem
1.2

The main theme of this section is the appearance of thresholds. These thresholds are such that invariant bond percolations with expected degree exceeding the threshold do not exhibit decay of the two-point function. Failure to decay at infinity may occur in two (in some sense three) qualitatively distinct ways, which depend on the strength of Kazhdan’s property (T) the group has, i.e. whether it has actual property (T), relative property (T) or only fails to have the Haagerup property. This section is divided into three subsections corresponding to each of these cases and we refer to Remarks
7
-
8
and Remark
10
for the comparison. In Section
4.1
, we provide the threshold for Kazhdan groups and prove the characterization of Kazhdan’s property (T) announced in Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4.2
, we extend this result to characterize relative property (T) and provide the application of this threshold to Bernoulli percolation. In Section
4.3
, we study the threshold appearing for groups which fail to have the Haagerup property and provide another application to Bernoulli bond percolation, which applies to Cayley graphs satisfying a weak form of spherical symmetry.

4.1.
Kazhdan’s property (T).

We start by recalling the definition of Kazhdan’s property (T): If
(\pi,H)
is a unitary representation of
\Gamma
,
\varphi\in H
is an
invariant vector
if
\pi(s)\varphi=\varphi
for every
s\in\Gamma
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
K\subset\Gamma
finite, then a unit vector
\varphi\in H
is called
(\varepsilon,K)
-invariant
if

\sup_{s\in K}\|\pi(s)\varphi-\varphi\|<\varepsilon.

The pair
(\varepsilon,K)
is called a
Kazhdan pair
for
\Gamma
if every unitary representation of
\Gamma
which has an
(\varepsilon,K)
-invariant vector also has a non-zero invariant vector. The group
\Gamma
has
property (T)
if there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a finite set
K\subset\Gamma
such that
(\varepsilon,K)
is a Kazhdan pair. The goal of this subsection is to prove the following characterization of property (T) in terms of invariant percolations.

Theorem 4.1

(
Group-invariant percolation and Kazhdan’s property (T)).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma
has property (T) if and only if there exists a threshold
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
implies that
\tau
is bounded away from zero, i.e.

\inf_{g,h\in\Gamma}\tau(g,h)>0.

In fact, the threshold
\alpha^{\star}
satisfies

\alpha^{*}\leq\inf_{(\varepsilon,K)}\bigg[1-\varepsilon^{2}/\big(4\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\big)\bigg],

where the infimum runs over all Kazhdan pairs
(\varepsilon,K)
for
\Gamma
and
d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq\max\big\{d(o,g)\colon g\in K\big\}
.

Remark
7

(
Connectivity decay vs. long-range order)

A two-point function
\tau
may fail to vanish at infinity in two qualitatively distinct ways. Following Lyons and Schramm
[
43
]
, we say that
\tau
exhibits
connectivity decay
, if

(4.1)

\inf_{g,h\in\Gamma}\tau(g,h)=0,

and otherwise say that
\tau
exhibits
long-range order
, i.e.

(4.2)

\inf_{g,h\in\Gamma}\tau(g,h)>0.

Connectivity decay is a particularly interesting case because it implies that
\tau(o,g)
decays to zero in at least one direction
g\to\infty
, while it stays bounded away from zero in some other direction. This is not exceptional behavior. In fact, it already appears in Bernoulli bond percolation
[
43
, Remark 1.3]
: take the canonical Cayley graph of the free product
\mathbb{Z}^{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, i.e. the Cayley graph corresponding to the presentation
\langle a,b,c\mid ab=ba,c=c^{-1}\rangle
. Then
p_{u}=1
, because removal of any edge corresponding to the generator
c
breaks the graph into two connected components. In particular,
\tau_{p}
does not exhibit long-range order for
p<1
. On the other hand, if
p>1/2=p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
, then there is a unique infinite cluster in every
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
fiber (the set of pairs
g,h
such that
g^{-1}h
is in the group generated by
a
and
b
). Thus
\tau_{p}
, for
p\in(1/2,1)
, exhibits connectivity decay.

Remark
8

(
Haagerup vs. Kazhdan’s property)

The distinction between connectivity decay and long-range order described in Remark
7
corresponds to the fact that Theorem
1.1
is not the negation of Theorem
1.2
, nor vice-versa. Long-range order is the strongest form of non-vanishing at infinity, which quite remarkably is exhibited by invariant bond percolations with large marginals on Kazhdan groups irrespective of the specific model. Examples of groups which satisfy neither the Haagerup nor Kazhdan’s property, together with an explanation of how this manifests in the distinction between connectivity decay and long-range order (as a condition on all models) are given in Remark
10
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
:

The proof naturally splits into two parts, namely showing that the existence of a long-range order threshold is both necessary and sufficient.

(
``\Leftarrow"
Long-range order threshold is sufficient for property (T): )
Let us recall
that the sufficiency part of Theorem
4.1
is new and its proof will rely on our construction used in the proof of Theorem
3.5
. More precisely, assume that there exists a threshold
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
, the two-point function is bounded away from zero. Recall that if every conditionally negative definite function
\psi
on
\Gamma
is bounded, then
\Gamma
has property (T) (this is classical, see e.g.
[
12
]
). Let
\psi
be a conditionally negative definite function. As in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, we may construct
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolations
\mathbf{P}_{p}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
, for
p\in(0,1)
, such that

\lim_{p\to 1}\mathbf{E}_{p}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\deg(o)

and such that

\tau_{p}(o,g)\leq{\rm e}^{-(1-p)\sqrt{\psi(g)}}\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,g\in\Gamma,p\in(0,1).

Then, for a fixed
p
sufficiently close to
1
, the two-point function
\tau_{p}
of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
is bounded away from zero. Hence
\sqrt{\psi}
, and thus
\psi
, must be bounded. It follows that
\Gamma
has property (T). ∎

(
``\Rightarrow"
Long-range order threshold is necessary for property (T): )
As mentioned before, the part of Theorem
4.1
concerning the qualitative consequence of property (T) is due to Lyons and Schramm
[
43
, Remark 6.7]
and was applied there to show that
p_{u}<1
, and to prove non-uniqueness at
p_{u}
, for Cayley graphs of Kazhdan groups
[
43
, Corollary 6.6]
. There it was noted that this qualitative implication may be deduced from a classical fact that if
\Gamma
has property (T), then every sequence of normalized, positive definite functions on
\Gamma
which converges pointwise to
1
, converges to
1
uniformly on
\Gamma
(see e.g.
[
12
]
). In
[
30
, Theorem 5.6]
, it was shown that for a group with property (T) and a Kazhdan pair
(\varepsilon,K)
with
K
a generating set, every invariant, insertion-tolerant, ergodic bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on the Cayley graph with respect to
K
, which satisfies
\inf_{e}\mathbb{P}\big(e\in\omega\big)\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
, has a unique infinite cluster. Based on this method, and for the convenience of the reader, we will provide a quantitative proof of the consequence of property (T), i.e. of the assertion that

(4.3)

\beta=\inf_{(\varepsilon,K)}\bigg[1-\varepsilon^{2}/\big(4\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\big)\bigg],

with the infimum running over all Kazhdan pairs
(\varepsilon,K)
for
\Gamma
and
d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq\max\{d(o,g)\colon g\in K\}
, provides a threshold for long-range order. We point out that there is a minor difference to the argument from
[
30
]
, namely that we will consider an a-priori fixed Cayley graph of
\Gamma
and not one built using the set
K
, thereby getting slightly different constants.

We recall the following lemma, which follows from
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
30
, Proposition 5.3]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group with property (T), let
(\varepsilon,K)
be a Kazhdan pair for
\Gamma
, let
\delta>0
and let
\varphi
be a normalized, positive definite function. Then

\min_{g\in K}\,{\rm Re}(\varphi(g))\geq 1-\frac{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2},

where
{\rm Re}(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
denotes the real part, implies that

\inf_{g\in\Gamma}\,{\rm Re}(\varphi(g))\geq 1-2\delta^{2}.

Now assume that
\Gamma
has property (T). Let
(\varepsilon,K)
be any Kazhdan pair for
\Gamma
and recall that
d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq\max\{d(o,g)\colon g\in K\}.
Let
\mathbb{P}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
with two-point function
\tau
and define

m\coloneqq\inf_{e\in E}\mathbb{P}[e\in\omega]=\min_{o\sim g}\tau(o,g).

For every
g\in\Gamma
, we then have the lower bound
\tau(o,g)\geq 1-d(o,g)(1-m)
: to see this, let
(o,g)
denote a shortest path of edges joining
o
to
g
, then

\displaystyle\tau(o,g)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big[o\leftrightarrow g\big]\geq\mathbb{P}\big[(o,g)\subset\omega\big]=\mathbb{P}\bigl[e\in\omega\text{ for all }e\in(o,g)\bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{e\in(o,g)}\mathbb{P}[e\notin\omega]

\displaystyle\geq 1-d(o,g)(1-m).

In particular, it follows that
\min_{g\in K}\tau(o,g)\geq 1-d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}(1-m).
Hence

(4.4)

m>1-\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}/(2d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}})

for some
\delta>0
implies that

\min_{g\in K}\tau(o,g)>1-\frac{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Rewriting

\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\sum_{o\sim g}\tau(o,g)\leq m+(\deg(o)-1)

shows that

(4.5)

\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\biggl(1-\frac{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}}\biggr)\deg(o)=\deg(o)-\frac{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}},

implies (
4.4
). Thus every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation satisfying (
4.5
), also satisfies (
4.4
). Now suppose that (
4.5
) holds and
\delta\in(0,1/\sqrt{2})
, then Lemma
4.2
implies

\inf_{g,h}\tau(g,h)=\inf_{g\in\Gamma}\tau(o,g)>1-2\delta^{2}>0.

This shows that
\alpha^{*}
can be taken to be

1-\frac{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{2\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}}<1,

as claimed. Letting
\delta\to 1/\sqrt{2}
, we in particular see that the optimal threshold is less than or equal to

(4.6)

\inf\biggl\{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}}\colon(\varepsilon,K)\,\,\mbox{is a Kazhdan pair for}\,\,\Gamma\biggr\},

i.e. the threshold
\beta
defined in (
4.3
). This concludes the proof of the direct implication. ∎

Remark
9

(
Marginals and expected degree)

We have presented a threshold for the expected degree to keep the exposition consistent. As seen in the proof, such a threshold is essentially equivalent to a threshold for marginals. The lower bound on the expected degree may e.g. be replaced by the assumption that each individual edge is present with probability at least
1-(\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2})/(2d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}})
. If
S
denotes the generating set used to define
{\mathcal{G}}
, then
d_{S,{\mathcal{G}}}=1
and it is a standard fact that
(\varepsilon_{S},S)
is a Kazhdan pair for
\Gamma
for suitable
\varepsilon_{S}>0
. Letting
\delta\to 1/\sqrt{2}
, it follows that every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation which contains each individual edge with probability at least
1-\varepsilon_{S}^{2}/4
, has a two-point function bounded away from zero. This recovers the bound in
[
30
, Corollary 5.4 (ii)]
. Moreover, it shows that the optimal threshold for the expected degree is less than or equal to
1-\varepsilon_{S}^{2}/(4\deg(o))
.

4.2.
Relative property (T) and non-uniqueness at
p_{u}
.

In this section, we find an extension of Theorem
4.1
to groups which have relative property (T) and provide an application to the question of uniqueness in Bernoulli bond percolation. To start off, recall the definition of relative property (T): Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let
H
be a subgroup. The pair
(\Gamma,H)
has
relative property (T)
if every unitary representation of
\Gamma
on a Hilbert space which almost has invariant vectors, has a non-zero
H
-invariant vector, see
[
5
, Definition 1.4.3]
. The notion of relative property (T) for a pair was considered implicitly in Kazhdan’s work
[
35
]
and explicitly by Margulis
[
44
]
. A well-known example is the pair
(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}^{2})
, which has relative property (T) but
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
does not have property (T). Characterizations of property (T) can often be extended to formulations for relative property (T), see e.g.
[
3
,
32
,
14
]
. In this vein, using our construction from Theorem
3.6
we provide the following extension of Theorem
4.1
to the situation of relative property (T) for the purpose of then providing an application of that “long-range order” threshold to Bernoulli percolation in Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 4.3

(
Group-invariant percolation and relative property (T)).

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, let
H
be a subgroup and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a Cayley graph of
\Gamma
. Then the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T) if and only if there exists a threshold
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
implies that
\tau
is bounded away from zero on each left
H
-coset, i.e. for every
g\in\Gamma

\inf_{h\in H}\tau(g,gh)>0.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a threshold
\alpha^{*}<1
such that for every
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
,
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
implies that
\tau
is bounded away from zero on each left
H
-coset. We recall that the methods of
[
52
,
11
]
yield the following characterization of relative property (T) in terms of invariant actions on spaces with measured walls, as observed in
[
31
, Theorem 3.3.6]
: Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and let
H
be a subgroup. Then the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T) if and only if for every invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
, the
H
-orbits are bounded in the sense that for every
x_{0}\in X

\sup_{h\in H}w(x_{0},hx_{0})=\sup_{h\in H}\mu({\mathcal{W}}(x_{0},hx_{0}))<\infty.

We may thus show that the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T) by verifying that every invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls has bounded
H
-orbits. To that end, suppose that
\Gamma
has an invariant action on a space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
and let
x_{0}\in X
. By Theorem
3.6
, for suitable
p\in(0,1)
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}\deg(o)
and

(4.7)

\tau(o,g)\leq\exp\bigl(-(1-p)w(x_{0},gx_{0})\bigr)\quad\mbox{for every}\,\,g\in\Gamma.

It follows from our assumption that

(4.8)

\inf_{h\in H}\tau(o,h)>0.

Combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) shows that

\sup_{h\in H}w(x_{0},hx_{0})<\infty,

i.e. the
H
-orbit of
x_{0}
is bounded. Since
x_{0}\in X
was arbitrary, all
H
-orbits are bounded and it follows that the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T).

Conversely, suppose there exists a sequence
\mathbb{P}_{n}
of
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolations on
{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]=\deg(o)

and such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
g_{n}\in\Gamma
with

\inf_{h\in H}\tau_{n}(g_{n},g_{n}h)=0.

Then the sequence
\tau_{n}(o,\cdot)=\tau_{n}(g_{n},g_{n}\cdot)
is a sequence of positive definite functions converging pointwise to
1
, but not uniformly on
H
. Since a pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T) if and only if every sequence of positive definite functions on
\Gamma
which converges pointwise to
1
, converges to
1
uniformly on
H
(see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
), we conclude that the pair
(\Gamma,H)
does not have relative property (T). ∎

Remark
10

(
Groups without the Haagerup property and without property (T))

Returning to the discussion in Remark
8
, we now elaborate on the subtle behavior of groups in between groups with the Haagerup and Kazhdan properties: recall that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
does not have property (T), but does have relative property (T) with respect to an infinite subgroup. Theorem
4.3
thus shows that there are groups such that invariant bond percolations with sufficiently large marginals fail to vanish at infinity by being bounded away from zero only in a specific direction. Even more curiously, the existence of groups without the Haagerup property and without property (T) with respect to any infinite subgroup (see e.g.
[
14
]
) implies that there are groups such that invariant bond percolations with sufficiently large marginals fail to vanish at infinity, but the directions in which they are bounded away from zero are necessarily not the same for all such models.

Recall that for Cayley graphs of groups with property (T), Bernoulli percolation at the uniqueness threshold does not have a unique infinite cluster. This is due to Peres, see Lyons and Schramm
[
43
, Corollary 6.6]
.
The fact that this also holds for groups
\Gamma
which admit an infinite normal subgroup
H
such that the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T) has appeared in the work of Gaboriau and Tucker-Drob
[
20
]
. Note that this in particular implies
p_{u}<1
for these Cayley graphs. We will give alternative proofs of these results using Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
. Due to the fact that these proofs only use invariant percolations, we believe them to be of interest and in this vein,
we will include a proof of the strongest result.

Theorem 4.4

(
Relative property (T) implies non-uniqueness at
p_{u}
).

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let
H
be an infinite, normal subgroup such that the pair
(\Gamma,H)
has relative property (T). Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be any Cayley graph of
\Gamma
. Then
p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})
-Bernoulli bond percolation does not have a unique infinite cluster a.s.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{p_{u}}
denote the configuration of
p_{u}\coloneqq p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
. Suppose that there is a unique infinite cluster
C_{\infty}
in
\omega_{p_{u}}
a.s. We use a construction of Benjamini, Lyons, Peres and Schramm
[
7
]
: For each
g\in\Gamma
let
C(g)
denote the set of vertices in
C_{\infty}
which are closest to
g
in the graph distance. Let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
denote the configuration of an
\varepsilon
-Bernoulli bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
which is independent of
\omega_{p_{u}}
. Now define a bond percolation
\xi_{\varepsilon}
as follows: Let the edge
[g,h]
be contained in
\xi_{\varepsilon}
if and only if
\max\{d(g,C_{\infty}),d(h,C_{\infty})\}<1/\varepsilon
holds and additionally
C(g)
and
C(h)
are contained in the same connected component of
\omega\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}
. It follows from the bounded convergence theorem that
\xi_{\varepsilon}
has marginals arbitrarily close to
1
when
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, see
[
7
, Section 3]
. Now Theorem
4.3
implies that for some fixed, sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we have that
\tau_{\xi_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded away from zero on (left)
H
-cosets. We claim that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(4.9)

c\,\tau_{\xi_{\varepsilon}}(g,h)\leq\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(g,h)\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,g,h\in\Gamma.

To see this, observe that
\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}
is a Bernoulli bond percolation with parameter
(1-\varepsilon)p_{u}>0
. In particular

c_{1}\coloneqq\inf_{u\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(o)}\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(o,u)>0.

By transitivity, for every
g\in\Gamma
,

c_{1}=\inf_{u\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(g)}\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(g,u).

Let
g,h\in\Gamma
,
u\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(g)
and
v\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(h)
. Applying the Harris-FKG-Inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(g,h)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(g\xleftrightarrow{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}u,u\xleftrightarrow{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}v,v\xleftrightarrow{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}h\Bigr)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}^{2}\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(u,v).

Now by the definition of
\xi_{\varepsilon}
we have that

\Bigl\{g\xleftrightarrow{\xi_{\varepsilon}}h \Bigr\}\subset\Bigl\{B_{1/\varepsilon}(g)\xleftrightarrow{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}B_{1/\varepsilon}(h)\Bigr\}.

Applying monotonicity, the union bound and inequality (
4.10
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi_{\varepsilon}}(g,h)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl[B_{1/\varepsilon}(g)\xleftrightarrow{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}B_{1/\varepsilon}(h)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(g)\\
v\in\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(h)\end{subarray}}\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(o)|^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(g,h).

This proves the inequality (
4.9
) with the constant
c=(|\partial B_{1/\varepsilon}(o)|/c_{1})^{-2}
. In particular, the fact that
\tau_{\xi_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded away from zero on
H
-cosets implies that the same holds for
\tau_{\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}}
. To finish the proof, we adapt the proof strategy of Hutchcroft and Pete in
[
29
]
to our setting: First observe that the
H
-cosets define a partition of the Cayley graph
{\mathcal{G}}
into isomorphic subgraphs, say all isomorphic to
{\mathcal{G}}_{H}
which is infinite with transitive, unimodular and closed automorphism subgroup
H
. Since
H
is infinite normal, the graphs of neighboring
H
-cosets are connected by infinitely many edges, see
[
29
]
. Now the percolation which
\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}
induces on the graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{H}
, is insertion tolerant,
H
-invariant and ergodic. By
[
43
, Theorem 4.1]
, there is a unique infinite cluster a.s. in each
H
-coset. We can now apply
sprinkling
as in
[
29
, Section 3]
: For every
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, adding an independent
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Bernoulli bond percolation to
\omega_{p_{u}}\setminus\gamma_{\varepsilon}
a.s. connects the clusters in neighboring
H
-cosets. Hence the resulting
\Gamma
-invariant percolation has a unique infinite cluster a.s. However, it is also
\bigl((1-\varepsilon)p_{u}+(1-(1-\varepsilon)p_{u})\varepsilon^{\prime}\bigr)
-Bernoulli bond percolation. For
\varepsilon^{\prime}
sufficiently small, this contradicts the definition of
p_{u}
. Therefore
\omega_{p_{u}}
cannot have a unique infinite cluster and the proof of Theorem
4.4
is complete. ∎

4.3.
Weak Kazhdan property and a quantitative converse to Theorem
1.1
.

In this subsection, we consider groups which do not have the Haagerup property. Following Glasner
[
21
, Definition 13.16]
, we call such groups
weakly Kazhdan
or say they have
property
(T_{w})
. Consider now a Cayley graph
{\mathcal{G}}
of a group
\Gamma
with property
(T_{w})
. Then Theorem
3.5
implies the existence of a threshold
\alpha^{*}_{w}\coloneqq\alpha^{*}_{w}({\mathcal{G}})<1
such that whenever
\mathbb{P}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha^{*}_{w}\deg(o)
, then
\tau(o,\cdot)
does not vanish at infinity. We now give an upper bound on
\alpha^{*}_{w}
in terms of another family of parameters associated with weakly Kazhdan groups. Since
\Gamma
does not have the Haagerup property, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
K\subset\Gamma
finite with the following property:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\text{every normalized positive definite function}\ \varphi\ \text{on}\ \Gamma\ \text{satisfying}

\displaystyle\min_{g\in K} \varphi(g)\geq 1-\varepsilon\ \text{does not vanish at infinity}.

Define the parameters

\displaystyle d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq\max\big\{d(o,g)\colon g\in K\big\},

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon,K,{\mathcal{G}}}\coloneqq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}},\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\kappa_{w}({\mathcal{G}})\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{\alpha_{\varepsilon,K,{\mathcal{G}}}\colon\mbox{the pair $(\varepsilon,K)$ satisfies \eqref{eq-WeakKazhdan}}\bigg\}.

Proposition 4.5

(
Estimate of the threshold for weak Kazhdan groups).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
which has property
(T_{w})
. Then
\alpha^{*}_{w}({\mathcal{G}})\leq\kappa_{w}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
K\subset\Gamma
finite such that every normalized positive definite function
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to[0,1]
which satisfies

(4.12)

\min_{g\in K} \varphi(g)\geq 1-\varepsilon,

does not vanish at infinity. Let
\mathbb{P}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E)
. It follows by the same computation as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
that

\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha_{\varepsilon,K,{\mathcal{G}}}\deg(o)=\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{\deg(o)d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}}\Big)\deg(o)

implies that
\tau
satisfies (
4.12
) and hence does not vanish at infinity. The claim follows. ∎

Remark
11

(
Marginals and expected degree)

The lower bound on the expected degree may again be replaced by the assumption that each individual edge is present with probability strictly above
\inf_{(\varepsilon,K)}1-\varepsilon/d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}
, where the infimum is over all pairs
(\varepsilon,K)
satisfying (
4.11
).

Remark
12

(
Threshold for site percolation)

In the setting of site percolation, Corollary
3.9
implies the existence of a possibly different threshold
\alpha^{*}_{w,{\rm site}}\coloneqq\alpha^{*}_{w,{\rm site}}({\mathcal{G}})<1
such that whenever
\mathbb{P}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant site percolation satisfying
\mathbb{P}[o\in\omega]>\alpha^{*}_{w,{\rm site}}
, then
\tau(o,\cdot)
does not vanish at infinity. It follows from essentially the same argument as in above proof of Proposition
4.5
that
\alpha^{*}_{w,{\rm site}}\leq\inf_{(\varepsilon,K)}1-\varepsilon/d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}
with the infimum taken over all pairs
(\varepsilon,K)
satisfying (
4.11
).

As pointed out by Lyons
[
39
]
, the distinction between connectivity decay and long-range order also reveals a lack of spherical symmetry in Cayley graphs (where a Cayley graph is called
spherically symmetric
if for all vertices with
d(o,g)=d(o,h)
, there exists an automorphism which fixes
o
and maps
g
to
h
). As a final application of the results in this section, we show that a rather weak form of spherical symmetry suffices to guarantee non-uniqueness at
p_{u}
(and
p_{u}<1
) for weak Kazhdan groups.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
which has property
(T_{w})
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies the following spherical symmetry assumption:

(Sym)

For every
p\in(0,1)
, the two-point function
\tau_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
of
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation vanishes at infinity if and only if it exhibits connectivity decay.

Then
p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})<1
. Moreover,
p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})
-Bernoulli bond percolation does not have a unique infinite cluster.

Proof.

The assertion that
p_{u}<1
follows directly from the threshold: Indeed, Proposition
4.5
implies that
\tau_{p}(o,\cdot)
does not vanish at infinity for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. Then (Sym) implies that it exhibits long-range order, i.e. is bounded away from zero. By
[
43
, Theorem 4.1]
, this implies that the corresponding
p
-Bernoulli bond percolation has a unique infinite cluster. The assertion that there is not a unique infinite cluster at the uniqueness threshold follows from the same argument as employed in the first part of the proof of Theorem
4.4
, replacing the use of Theorem
4.3
with Proposition
4.5
, and then upgrading non-vanishing at infinity to long-range order through (Sym). ∎

Note also that in the setting of Corollary
4.6
, Remark
11
implies that
p_{u}({\mathcal{G}})\leq\inf_{(\varepsilon,K)}1-\varepsilon/d_{K,{\mathcal{G}}}
with the infimum taken over all pairs
(\varepsilon,K)
satisfying (
4.11
).

5.
Decay of two-point functions on examples of Haagerup groups: Proofs of Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
1.5
.

In this section we will prove the results outlined in Section
1.2
. In particular, Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
1.5
will be proved in Section
5.1
, Section
5.2
and Section
5.3
respectively.

5.1.
Lamplighters over free groups: Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Here we will prove the following result, stated earlier in Theorem
1.3
:

Theorem 5.1

(
Large marginals and exponential decay).

Let
H
be a finite group, let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be any Cayley graph of
H\wr\mathbb{F}_{r}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and with

{\rm e}^{-\beta|g|}\leq\tau(o,g)\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma|g|}\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,g\in\Gamma,

where
\beta,\gamma>0
such that
\beta/\gamma\leq C
.

We proceed with the proof of Theorem
5.1
in the following three steps: we first recall the wreath product construction (see Section
5.1.1
) and then recall a way of defining a wall structure on wreath products over free groups due to
[
15
]
(see Section
5.1.2
). It was observed in the follow-up article
[
16
]
that the corresponding wall-distance can be quantified. In our probabilistically motivated set-up, we give a slightly different, self-contained estimate for the convenience of the reader. We finish with an application of Corollary
3.7
to obtain the desired result.

5.1.1.
Wreath products of finitely generated groups.

Let
H
and
G
be any two groups. The (standard, restricted)
wreath product

H\wr G
is the semidirect product
H^{(G)}\rtimes G
, where

H^{(G)}=\bigotimes_{x\in G}H

denotes the direct sum of copies of
H
on which
G
acts by left translation.
In other words, the elements of
H\wr G
are pairs
(\varphi,x)
where
x\in G
and where
\varphi \colon G\to H
is a function with
f(y)
equal to the identity
e_{H}
of
H
for all but finitely many
y\in G
. The group operation is given by

(\varphi_{1},x_{1})(\varphi_{2},x_{2})=(\varphi_{1}(x_{2}\varphi_{2}),x_{1}x_{2}),

where
x\varphi(y)=\varphi(x^{-1}y)
and
(\varphi\psi)(y)=\varphi(y)\psi(y)
.

It is well-known that if
G
and
H
are finitely generated, then
H\wr G
is finitely generated. In fact, given a finite generating set
S
of
G
and a finite generating set
T
of
H
, the
canonical generating set
of
H\wr G
is defined as follows: For every
t\in T
let
\varphi_{t}\in H^{(G)}
be the function with
\varphi_{t}(e_{G})=t
and
\varphi_{t}(x)=e_{H}
for all
x\neq e_{G}
. Moreover, let
e_{H^{(G)}}\colon G\to H
be the function which is equal to identity everywhere. Then

\Bigl\{(e_{H^{(G)}},s)\colon s\in S\Bigr\}\sqcup\Bigl\{(\varphi_{t},e_{G})\colon t\in T\Bigr\}

is a finite generating set for
H\wr G
, see e.g.
[
43
]
.

5.1.2.
Wreath products over free groups.

In the following we restrict our attention to one particular example, the wreath product
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the classical
lamplighter group
over the free group on two generators. This is only for simplicity of the exposition and all arguments extend in straightforward manner to the general setting of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
denote the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
with respect to the canonical generating set. We can picture this group as the
4
-regular tree with a lamp attached to each vertex. An element
(\varphi,x)\in\Gamma
is then interpreted as lamps being switched on at the support of
\varphi
, denoted by
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
, and a marker standing at position
x
. If
a,b
denote the canonical generators for
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then the generators

\Bigl\{(\mathbf{0},a),(\mathbf{0},a^{-1}),(\mathbf{0},b),(\mathbf{0},b^{-1})\Bigr\},

where
\mathbf{0}(x)=0
for all
x
, correspond to moving the marker in the direction of the appearing generator while leaving the configuration of lamps untouched. The remaining generator
({\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}},e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
corresponds to switching the state of the lamp at the current position of the marker. For this Cayley graph it is well-known how to describe the graph distance: Given two elements
(\varphi,x)
and
(\psi,y)
of
\Gamma
, a shortest path in
{\mathcal{G}}
is such that the marker starts at position
x
, visits the position of all lamps which need to be switched and switches these configurations accordingly, and finally moves to position
y
. Note that the positions of the lamps which need to be switched are given by
{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi)
and that
|{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi)|
many switches have to be made. Therefore

d((\varphi,x),(\psi,y))=|{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi)|+\ell(x,{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi),y),

where
\ell(x,{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi),y)
denotes the length of a shortest path in the
4
-regular tree which starts at
x
, visits
{\rm supp}(\varphi\psi)
completely and ends at
y
. In other words, the distance is essentially determined by the solution to the above
travelling salesman problem
in the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. When
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is replaced with a different finitely generated group and the tree is replaced with the corresponding Cayley graph, this description remains correct. However, the particularly nice connection between
walks in the base graph
and
walls in the lamplighter graph
we describe next makes use of the tree-structure.

The following description of a proper, left-invariant structure of space with walls on
\Gamma
is from
[
15
]
. We adapt their notation to our more restrictive setting in order to readily obtain quantitative estimates of the associated wall distance. Let
T_{4}
denote the canonical Cayley graph of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, i.e. the
4
-regular tree, whose root is given by the vertex corresponding to
e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
. Note that every edge
e\in E(T_{4})
determines a wall by splitting
T_{4}
into the two connected components of
T_{4}\setminus\{e\}
. For our purposes it is convenient to consider the set
E^{\to}(T_{4})
of oriented edges of
T_{4}
. For every oriented edge
e
, let
A_{e}
denote the connected component of
T_{4}\setminus\{e\}
which it points away from. Given a finitely supported function
\psi\colon A_{e}^{c}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, define

E(e,\psi)\coloneqq\Bigl\{(x,\varphi)\in\Gamma\colon x\in A_{e},\varphi_{|A_{e}^{c}}=\psi\Bigr\}.

A family of walls on
\Gamma
is then defined by

{\mathcal{W}}\coloneqq\Bigl\{\{E(e,\psi),E(e,\psi)^{c}\}\colon e\in E^{\to}(T_{4}),\psi\colon A_{e}^{c}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}\,\,\mbox{finitely supported}\Bigr\}.

It was proven in
[
15
, Theorem 1]
that
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}})
is a space with walls on which
\Gamma
acts properly via left translations. We are interested in a quantitative version, which is given by the following lemma and was also observed in a more general context in
[
16
, Proposition 7.2]
.

Lemma 5.2

(
Estimate of the wall-distance for lamplighters over free groups).

Let
\Gamma\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Consider the proper action of
\Gamma
on the space with walls
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}})
with

{\mathcal{W}}\coloneqq\Bigl\{E(e,\psi)\colon e\in E^{\to}(T_{4}),\psi\colon A_{e}^{c}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}\,\,\mbox{finitely supported}\Bigr\}.

Set
o\coloneqq(\mathbf{0},e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
and consider the canonical Cayley graph of
\Gamma
. Then, for every
(\varphi,x)\in\Gamma
,

w(o,(\varphi,x))\geq\frac{1}{4}|(\varphi,x)|-\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

Let
(\varphi,x)\in\Gamma
and recall from above that

|(\varphi,x)|=d(o,(\varphi,x))=|{\rm supp}(\varphi)|+\ell(e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},{\rm supp}(\varphi),x).

To shorten notation, set
\ell=\ell(e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},{\rm supp}(\varphi),x)
. Let
\rho=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
be a shortest path of edges from
e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
to
x
which visits
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
completely. Note that every position in
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
except the origin has to be visited by
\rho
, thus
|{\rm supp}(\varphi)|\leq 1+\ell.

In particular

(5.1)

|(\varphi,x)|\leq 2\ell+1.

We claim that

(5.2)

\ell\leq 2w(o,(\varphi,x)).\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

To see this, we describe a subset
\rho^{\prime}
of
\rho
, consisting of at least half of the original edges, such that for each in
\rho^{\prime}
there is a unique wall separating
o
and
(\varphi,x)
: Let
e_{i}
be an edge in
\rho
which appears for the first time. It is clear from the assumptions on
\rho
that each edge can appear at most twice and we treat the resulting two cases separately.

(i)

Assume that
e_{i}
appears a second time, say at
e_{j}
with
i\leq j\leq\ell
. Since the walk takes place on a tree, it follows that every edge
e_{k}
with
i\leq k\leq j
appears exactly twice. Treating each
e_{k}
as an oriented edge pointing in the direction of the next appearing edge in
\rho
or towards
x
in case
i=\ell-1
, we see that this case describes the following situation: The path
\rho
starts at endpoint
y
of
e_{i}
, proceeds in the direction of
e_{i}
and explores a part of the corresponding branch of the tree, before returning to
y
and leaving in the direction of one of the remaining branches originating from
y
. In particular the edge
e_{i}
points away from the final position
x
of the marker. The same holds for every
e_{k}
with
i\leq k\leq j
which appears for the first time. Now fix any such edge
e_{k}
. Clearly the only reason for its appearance can be that there is an element
z\in{\rm supp}(\varphi)
which lies in the direction it is pointing. Recalling that
A_{k}\coloneqq A_{e_{k}}
denotes the connected component of
T_{4}\setminus\{e_{k}\}
which
e_{k}
points away from, we therefore have that
x\in A_{k}
and that
z\in{\rm supp}(\varphi_{|A_{k}^{c}})
. It follows that the set

E(e_{k},\varphi_{|A_{k}^{c}})=\Bigl\{(v,\psi)\in\Gamma\colon v\in A_{k},\psi_{|A_{k}^{c}}=\varphi_{|A_{k}^{c}}\Bigr\}

contains
(x,\varphi)
, but not
o=(\mathbf{0},e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
because
\varphi_{|A_{k}^{c}}(z)=1\neq\mathbf{0}(z)
. We have thus exhibited for half of the edges
e_{i},\ldots,e_{j}
a unique wall separating
o
and
(\varphi,x)
.

(ii)

Assume that
e_{i}
appears exactly once. We again treat
e_{i}
as an oriented edge pointing towards the next appearing edge in
\rho
or towards
x
in case
i=\ell-1
. Since the path started at the identity and since
e_{i}
appears for the first time,
e_{i}
points away from
e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
, i.e.
e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\in A_{i}\coloneqq A_{e_{i}}
. Since
e_{i}
is not traversed twice, the final position
x
of the marker lies in
A_{e_{i}}^{c}
. In particular

E(e_{i},\mathbf{0}_{|A_{i}^{c}})=\Bigl\{(v,\psi)\in\Gamma\colon v\in A_{i},\psi_{|A_{i}^{c}}=\mathbf{0}_{|A_{k}^{c}}\Bigr\}

contains
o=(\mathbf{0},e_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
, but not
(x,\varphi)
because
x\notin A_{i}
. We have thus exhibited for this edge a unique wall separating
o
and
(\varphi,x)
.

Applying the above procedure iteratively proves Inequality (
5.2
). Combined with Inequality (
5.1
), this concludes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
Combining Lemma
5.2
with Corollary
3.7
yields the desired result. ∎

5.2.
Isometry groups of hyperbolic space: Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In this section we discuss a relevant class of examples in which the percolations constructed using Theorem
3.6
can be understood in a precise geometric sense. These are finitely generated groups with a proper isometric action on real hyperbolic space. In dimension two, we use the additional geometric information to describe the number of infinite clusters completely, i.e. we prove Theorem
1.4
which, as a special case, yields that canonical Cayley graphs of co-compact Fuchsian groups admit invariant bond percolations with marginals arbitrarily close to
1
but with exponential decay of the two-point function and infinitely many infinite clusters a.s.

5.2.1.
Hyperbolic space as a space with measured walls.

The following observations are due to
[
11
, Section 3]
building on work of Robertson
[
51
]
, see also
[
10
, Example 3.7]
for the notation we adopt here. Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be real
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space and let
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
be its isometry group. Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
denote the set of all closed or open geometric half-spaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with boundary an isometric copy of
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
. Each half-space defines a wall by partitioning the space into the half-space itself and its complement. The group
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is unimodular, acts transitively on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
and the stabilizer of a given wall is unimodular. Thus
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
carries a non-zero
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
-invariant Borel measure
\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
. Note that the set
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,w)
of walls separating two points
z,w\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is relatively compact, which implies
\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,w))<\infty
. Hence the
4
-tuple
(\mathbb{H}^{n},{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}},{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}),\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})
defines a space with measured walls. By Crofton’s formula
[
51
, Proposition 2.1]
, there exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that, for every
z,w\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(5.3)

\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}({\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(z,w))=\lambda d(z,w),

where
d(z,w)
denotes the hyperbolic distance.

Remark
13

(
Complex hyperbolic space)

It is much more difficult to define a structure of space with measured walls on
n
-dimensional complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that it is invariant under the action of the corresponding isometry group; the difficulties are described e.g. in
[
51
]
. The fact that this is possible is a result of Chatterji, Drutu and Haglund
[
10
, Corollary 6.28]
: In fact,
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
may be equipped with an invariant structure of space with measured walls such that the corresponding wall distance is the square-root of the hyperbolic distance and such that all walls are convex with respect to the wall distance.

Example 5.3
(
Exponential decay for discrete, co-compact subgroups of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
)
From the above considerations, we obtain many examples of groups for which the Haagerup property is witnessed by two-point functions of invariant percolations with exponential decay. More precisely, this is true for every finitely generated group
\Gamma
which has an isometric action on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
we have
d(z_{0},gz_{0})\geq c|g|
for all
g\in\Gamma
. Particular examples are provided by discrete subgroups of
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
which act co-compactly (by the Milnor-Schwarz Lemma, see e.g.
[
53
, Theorem 1.18]
).

5.2.2.
Hyperplane percolation.

We now focus on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a graph embedded in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
such that the automorphisms of
{\mathcal{G}}
extend to isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Let
\eta
be a Poisson process on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
with intensity measure
\gamma\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
for some
\gamma>0
. Define a bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
as in Theorem
3.6
, i.e. for every half-space contained in
\eta
, delete all edges which intersect its boundary. We refer to this bond percolation as the
hyperplane percolation
on
{\mathcal{G}}
induced by
\eta
. Note that
{\rm Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
-invariance of
\eta
implies
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
-invariance of the corresponding hyperplane percolation.

Theorem 5.4

(
Large marginals, exponential decay and infinitely many infinite clusters).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a transitive, nonamenable, planar graph with one end. Then for every
\alpha<1
, there exists an
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
, such that
\tau(o,\cdot)
has exponential decay and such that it has infinitely many infinite clusters a.s.

Remark
14

(
Co-compact Fuchsian groups)

Theorem
5.4
applies to planar Cayley graphs of nonamenable one-ended groups. In particular it applies to the examples considered by Lalley
[
36
,
37
]
, namely planar Cayley graphs of co-compact Fuchsian groups. For these groups, it was shown in
[
8
,
36
]
that Bernoulli bond percolation exhibits a double phase transition. In this sense, these groups behave quite differently from infinitely ended ones, which all have uniqueness threshold
p_{u}=1
. With respect to general invariant bond percolations, this difference between one-ended and infinitely-ended nonamenable planar Cayley graphs disappears in the sense that there exist invariant percolations with marginals arbitrarily close to
1
but with infinitely many infinite clusters a.s.

Example 5.5
(
Estimate of the exponential decay rate for co-compact Fuchsian groups
)
We specialize Example
5.2.1
to the setting of the hyperbolic plane: Consider a co-compact Fuchsian group
\Gamma
. Recall that there exists a compact fundamental polygon
T\subset\mathbb{H}_{2}
for
\Gamma
, i.e. a compact, connected subset, bounded by finitely many geodesics and such that the translates
\{gT\}_{g\in\Gamma}
tesselate
\mathbb{H}_{2}
. In particular there are finitely many group elements for which
gT
and
T
intersect. These are called
side-pairing transformations
and constitute a generating set for
\Gamma
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the corresponding Cayley graph, which can be identified with the dual graph of the tiling
\{gT\}
. Since the tiling is locally finite, we have that for every radius
r>0
, there exists a constant
0<D(r)<\infty
such that no hyberbolic ball of radius
r
intersects more than
D(r)
tiles. Now pick
z_{0}\in T\setminus\partial T
and set
\rho\coloneqq\inf_{g,h\in\Gamma}d(gz_{0},hz_{0})
. It follows by elementary arguments that

\frac{\rho}{2D(\rho)}|g|\leq d(z_{0},gz_{0})\leq{\rm diam}(T)|g|\quad\mbox{for all} \,\,g\in\Gamma\setminus\{o\},

see e.g.
[
37
, Proposition 1.10]
.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

By
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
, the assumptions guarantee that
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
is discrete, hence unimodular, and that
{\mathcal{G}}
can be embedded in the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in such a way that the action of
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
extends to an isometric action on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Moreover, the embedding may be taken such that the dual graph is quasi-transitive, hence the embedding is a quasi-isometry. Identify
{\mathcal{G}}
with its embedding. Let
p\in(0,1)
, let
\eta_{p}
be a Poisson process on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
with intensity measure
(1-p)\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
and let
\xi_{p}
denote the induced hyperplane process on
{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{p}
denote the law of
\xi_{p}
, which defines an
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
-invariant bond percolation. Since the embedding is a quasi-isometry, it follows from Crofton’s formula, see Equation (
5.3
), that
\tau_{\xi_{p}}(o,\cdot)
has exponential decay and that

\lim_{p\to 1}\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\xi_{p}}(o)]=\deg(o).

Hence it suffices to show that
\xi_{p}
has infinitely many infinite clusters for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. By
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, the number of infinite clusters of
\xi_{p}
is a.s. constant and equal to
0,1
or
\infty
. Note that since
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
is transitive and unimodular, nonamenability of
{\mathcal{G}}
implies nonamenability of
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
by
[
6
, Theorem 5.3]
. The generalization
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
of Theorem
1
to general automorphism groups of graphs implies that any invariant percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
with marginals sufficiently close to
1
has infinite clusters with positive probability. Therefore it suffices to rule out the possibility of a unique infinite cluster. Note that this seems obvious because we already know that the two-point function of
\xi_{p}
vanishes at infinity. However, we cannot directly apply the standard Harris-FKG-Inequality because the percolation
\xi_{p}
may be highly dependent. We now present two alternative proofs, one specific to the setting of hyperbolic geometry and one based on the Harris-FKG-Inequality for the Poisson process, which therefore applies quite generally and yields some additional information about the infinite clusters of the percolations in Theorem
3.5
, see Remark
15
below.

Completing the proof using the hyperbolic setting.
This proof uses only the following result.

Theorem 5.6

(
[
41
, Theorem 8.48 & 8.49]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a quasi-transitive graph embedded in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
in such a way that the embedding is a quasi-isometry. Let
\omega
be the configuration of an
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
-invariant percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
. If
\omega
has a unique infinite cluster a.s., then a.s. the set of points
z
in the ideal boundary
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}
for which there is a path in
\omega
with limit
z
is dense in
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

This is a generalization of results of Benjamini and Schramm
[
8
, Theorem 4.1 & Lemma 4.3]
, stated in
[
41
]
. Note that it applies not just in dimension two. However, in general there might be a lack of ergodicity in dimensions three and higher. Looking back at our model, assume that
\xi_{p}
has a unique infinite cluster a.s. and observe that
\eta_{p}
is a.s. non-zero, hence the set of points
z
in the ideal boundary for which there is a path in
\xi_{p}
with limit
z
is a.s. not dense in
\partial\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Theorem
5.6
then implies that
\xi_{p}
does not a.s. have a unique infinite cluster.

An alternative proof using the Harris-FKG-Inequality for the Poisson process.
By
[
38
, Corollary 3.7]
, we may assume that our Poisson process is proper. Moreover, the intensity measure
\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
is
\sigma
-finite. To see this, take for example a countable dense set
D
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and consider the countable covering of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
by the compact, thus of finite
\mu_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
-measure, sets
\{\overline{{\mathcal{W}}(z,w)}\colon z\neq w\in D\}
. For
g\in\Gamma
, let
A_{g}
denote the event that the cluster of vertex
g
in the hyperplane process
\xi_{p}
is the unique infinite cluster. Since adding hyperplanes to the process
\eta_{p}
can only result in removing more edges, the function
{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A_{g}}
is decreasing on all of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
. By Theorem
2.5
, for every
g,h\in\Gamma
,

\tau_{\xi_{p}}(g,h)\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}[A_{g}\cap A_{h}]= \mathbb{E}[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A_{g}}(\eta_{p}){\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A_{h}}(\eta_{p})]\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}[A_{g}]\,\mathbb{P}_{p}[A_{h}]=\mathbb{P}_{p}[A_{o}]^{2}.

Now suppose there is a unique infinite cluster
\mathbb{P}_{p}
-as., then
\mathbb{P}_{p}[A_{o}]
is positive and thus
\tau_{\xi_{p}}
is bounded away from zero. This contradicts the fact that
\tau_{\xi_{p}}
vanishes at infinity. ∎

Remark
15

(
Ruling out a unique infinite cluster in general)

The Poisson processes in the proof of Theorem
3.5
also have
\sigma
-finite intensity measure. By a slight modification of the above argument, we obtain that it is impossible that these percolations have a unique infinite cluster with positive probability. It follows that the percolations appearing in the statement of Theorem
3.5
may always be assumed to have no unique infinite cluster a.s. However, in the general context there is neither ergodicity nor planar hyperbolic geometry, hence the number of infinite clusters could be a non-trivial random variable with values in
\{0,2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}
.

5.3.
Spaces with measured walls for amenable groups: Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Recall that amenable groups have the Haagerup property
[
4
]
. In this section, we show that under the additional assumption that
\Gamma
is amenable, there exists a natural space with measured walls which admits a proper, invariant
\Gamma
-action, such that our constructive proof of Theorem
3.5
recovers the corresponding statement of Theorem
1
from
[
6
]
– let us point out that Theorem
1
may also be proved by the Rokhlin lemma of Ornstein and Weiss
[
50
]
and results of Adams and Lyons
[
1
]
and that
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
provides a more general statement.

Proposition 5.7

(Recovering the amenable case)
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated amenable group
\Gamma
. Consider the proper, invariant action of
\Gamma
on the space with measured walls
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
described in
[
11
, Proof of Theorem I(5)]
. For
p\in(0,1)
, let
\eta_{p}
be a Poisson process on
{\mathcal{W}}
with intensity
(1-p)\mu
and let
\omega_{p}
denote the associated invariant bond percolation on
{\mathcal{G}}
as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
. Then
\omega_{p}
has no infinite cluster.

Proof.

We first need to introduce the space with measured walls from
[
11
]
. To that end, we recall a notion of product for spaces with measured walls from
[
11
, Definition 5]
: Let
(X_{n},{\mathcal{W}}_{n},{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of spaces with measured walls. Distinguish a point
x_{n}^{0}\in X_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now define the set

X\coloneqq\Biggl\{x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\colon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}(x_{0}^{(n)},x_{n})<\infty\Biggr\}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
p_{n}\colon X\to X_{n}
denote the projection onto the
n
’th coordinate. Note that if
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{A,A^{c}\}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
, then

W(\{A,A^{c}\}) \coloneqq p_{n}^{-1}\bigl(\{A,A^{c}\}\bigr)=\Bigl\{ \bigl\{ x\in X\colon x_{n}\in A\bigr\},\bigl\{ x\in X\colon x_{n}\in A^{c}\bigr\}\Bigr\}

defines a wall on
X
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}
denote the (disjoint) union of such walls, which can be identified with the coproduct of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
p_{n}^{-1}({\mathcal{B}}_{n})
and let
\mu
be the unique measure on
{\mathcal{W}}
whose restriction to
p_{n}^{-1}({\mathcal{B}}_{n})
coincides with
\mu_{n}
. Then
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
is a space with measured walls which is called the
product
of the
pointed
spaces
(X_{n},{\mathcal{W}}_{n},{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\mu_{n},x_{n}^{0})
.

Now let
(K_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of non-empty finite subsets of
\Gamma
increasing to
\Gamma
. Assuming amenability, there exists a
Følner sequence
for
(K_{n})
, i.e. a sequence
(A_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of non-empty finite subsets of
\Gamma
such that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in K_{n}
,

\frac{|gA_{n}\Delta A_{n}|}{|A_{n}|}<2^{-n}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, form the space with measured walls
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}}_{n},{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\mu_{n})
, where the set of walls is

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}=\Bigl\{ \bigl\{gA_{n}^{-1},(gA_{n}^{-1})^{c}\bigr\}\colon g\in\Gamma \Bigr\},

the
\sigma
-field
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
is the power set and
\mu
is counting measure normalized by
n/|A_{n}|
, see
[
11
, Example 1]
. Observe that the corresponding wall distance is given by

w_{n}(g,h)=\frac{n}{|A_{n}|}|gA_{n}\Delta hA_{n}|,

which is clearly bounded by
n
. In order to obtain an action on a space with unbounded wall distance we therefore need to
pack up
(a formulation due to
[
11
]
) the sequence of spaces described above. To that end, distinguish the point
x_{0}^{n}=o
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(X,{\mathcal{W}},{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
be the product of the pointed spaces with measured walls
(\Gamma,{\mathcal{W}}_{n},{\mathcal{B}}_{n},\mu_{n},o)
. It is then not difficult to check that
\Gamma
acts on
X
by the diagonal action, see
[
11
, Proof of Theorem I(5)]
. Distinguish the point
x_{0}=(o,o,\ldots)\in X
.

With these technical preliminaries established, we are now ready to conclude the proof: Since
\omega_{p}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation, it suffices to prove that the cluster of the identity is finite a.s. Consider the set of walls

{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigl\{p_{n}^{-1}\bigl(\bigl\{gA_{n}^{-1},(gA_{n}^{-1})^{c}\bigr\}\bigr)\colon g\in\Gamma\,\,\mbox{such that}\,\,o\in gA_{n}^{-1}\ \Bigr\}.

In the particular setting described here, we claim that
{\mathcal{V}}
may be interpreted as a particular set of all walls which separate
o
from
\infty
in
{\mathcal{G}}
: Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in\Gamma
such that
o\in gA_{n}^{-1}
. If the wall

W=p_{n}^{-1}\bigl(\bigl\{gA_{n}^{-1},(gA_{n}^{-1})^{c}\bigr\}\bigr)

satisfies
W\in{\rm supp}(\eta_{p})
, then, in the configuration
\omega_{p}
, the edge boundary of a finite neighborhood of
o
in
{\mathcal{G}}
is completely removed. Therefore on the event that
\eta_{p}({\mathcal{V}})>0
, the cluster of
o
in
\omega_{p}
is finite. But now, since
\eta_{p}
is a Poisson process with intensity
(1-p)\mu
, the fact that

\displaystyle\mu({\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\Bigl(\Bigl\{\bigl\{gA_{n}^{-1},(gA_{n}^{-1})^{c}\bigr\}\colon g\in\Gamma\,\,\mbox{such that}\,\,o\in gA_{n}^{-1}\Bigr\}\Bigr)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{|A_{n}|}|A_{n}|=\infty,

already implies that
\mathbb{P}(\eta_{p}({\mathcal{V}})>0)=1
. Thus
\omega_{p}
has no infinite cluster a.s. ∎

5.4.
Outlook.

In light of Remark
6
it is natural to ask whether the equivariant
L^{1}
-compression exponent can be characterized through group-invariant percolation, say by Corollary
3.7
(ii) with
\rho=\rho_{\alpha}
. Notably, this would provide the first probabilistic characterization of this exponent. The following intermediate result is proved in
[
46
]
: recall that
\psi\colon\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
is
measure definite function
if there exists a measure space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{B}},\mu)
and
S\colon\Gamma\to{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\psi(g^{-1}h)=\mu(S_{g}\Delta S_{h})
.

Theorem 5.8

(
Quantitative two-point function decay and compression functions).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the Cayley graph of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
and let
\rho\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
be a function with
\{\rho=0\}=\{0\}
. Then each of the following conditions implies the next one:

(i)

There exists an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls with
\rho
-growth;

(ii)

There exists
C>0
such that for every
\alpha<1
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant bond percolation
\mathbb{P}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\omega}(o)]>\alpha\deg(o)
and with
(C,\rho)
-exponential decay;

(iii)

There exists a measure definite function on
\Gamma
which admits
\rho
as a compression function.

Moreover, if
\Gamma
is amenable or if the measure definite function in (iii) can be assumed to be the square-root of a conditionally negative definite function, then all three conditions are equivalent.

Remark
16

(Connection with the equivariant
L^{1}
-compression exponent, continued)

Condition (iii) in Theorem
5.8
may be interpreted as ”almost” condition (i), as we will now explain: observe that every measure definite function
\psi
on
\Gamma
is of the form

(5.4)

\psi(g^{-1}h)=\|f(g)-f(h)\|_{E}\vskip 2.84526pt

for an
L^{1}
-space
E
and a map
f\colon\Gamma\to E
. While this is a formally weaker requirement than being of the form (
3.3
), it is an open question whether the two coincide, see
[
16
]
. In particular, proving that these notions coincide provides one possible way of obtaining the desired probabilistic characterization of the equivariant
L^{1}
-compression exponent (in this vein, note that Theorem
5.8
does entail the desired characterization for amenable groups). On a related note, since the square-root of any conditionally negative definite kernel is of the form (
3.3
), as described in Section
3.3
, condition (iii) with
\rho=\rho_{\alpha}
always implies condition (i) with
\rho=\rho_{\alpha/2}
.

Remark
17

Let us comment on the proof of Theorem
5.8
. Suppose that the implication ”(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)” holds. Since the square-root of a conditionally negative definite kernel is the wall distance associated to an invariant action of
\Gamma
on a space with measured walls, condition (iii) implies condition (i) under the first additional assumption. Under the other additional assumption of amenability, the fact that every measure definite function is the wall distance associated to an
invariant
action on a space with measured walls is due to
[
16
, Proposition 2.8]
. Hence, only ”(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)” requires justification. A preliminary version of this article featured a proof, however, this proof does not use the underlying group structure and, after completion, the authors realized that it is in fact an instance of a more general phenomenon. In the more general form it can be applied to provide a characterization of the (non-equivariant)
L^{1}
-compression exponent using the strategy presented above and building on the ideas developed in this article, see
[
46
]
. Since the implication ”(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)” in Theorem
5.8
is not used in this article, the proof may be found in the follow-up work
[
46
, Theorem 3.3]
.

We do not know whether the conditions in Theorem
5.8
are in fact equivalent. There are prominent examples of non-amenable groups which satisfy all three conditions: to name two, these include
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{F}_{2}
by Theorem
5.1
and co-compact Fuchsian groups by Theorem
5.4
.

Affine Deligne-Lusztig Varieties and Quantum Bruhat Graph
Arghya Sadhukhan

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties X w ​ ( b ) X_{w}(b) and their certain union X ⁡ ( μ , b ) X(\mu,b) inside the affine flag variety of a reductive group. Several important results in the study of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties have been established under the so-called superregularity hypothesis. Such results include a description of generic Newton points in Iwahori double cosets of loop groups, covering relation in associated Iwahori-Weyl group and dimension formula for X ⁡ ( μ , b ) X(\mu,b) . We show that one can considerably weaken the superregularity hypothesis and sometimes completely eliminate it, thus strengthening these existing results.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field and
\breve{F}
be the completion of its maximal unramified extension. Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius automorphism of
\breve{F}
over
F
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be a split connected reductive group over
F
. Let
\breve{}\mathcal{I}
be the standard Iwahori subgroup of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Let
w
be an element in the Iwahori-Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
and
[b]
be a
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
G(\breve{F})
. The affine Deligne-Lusztig variety associated to this pair
(w,[b])
is defined to be a locally closed subscheme of the affine flag variety given by

\displaystyle X_{w}(b)=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{I}\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})/\breve{}\mathcal{I};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\breve{}\mathcal{I}\}.

Let
\mu
be a conjugacy class of coweights of
\breve{G}
over the algebraic closure
\bar{F}
and let
\Adm(\mu)
be the set of
\mu
-admissible elements of
\widetilde{W}
; see
section

2.4
for more details. Then we define

X(\mu,b)=\coprod\limits_{w\in\Adm(\mu)}X_{w}(b).

This is a closed subscheme of the affine flag variety and it serves as the group-theoretic model for the Newton stratum corresponding to
[b]
in the special fiber of a Shimura variety giving rise to the
datum
(\mathbf{G},\mu)
.

First introduced by Rapoport in
[
25
]
, the notion of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties plays an important role in arithmetic geometry and the Langlands program. A fundamental problem in the study of
X_{w}(b)
is the non-emptiness pattern, i.e. for which choice of
(w,[b])
,
X_{w}(b)
is nonempty. Note that
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathcal{N}_{b,w}:=[b]\cap\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is nonempty; the latter is the set of geometric points of a locally closed subscheme of
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
; namely, the Newton stratum associated to
[b]
.

Let
B(G)
be the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. The set
B(G)
is equipped with a
natural partial order defined in terms of the Kottwitz map
\kappa
and the Newton map
\nu
. Understanding the non-emptiness pattern of affine Deligne Lusztig varieties associated to
w\in\widetilde{W}
naturally lead us to investigate the sub-poset
B(G)_{w}=\{[b]:X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset\}
. This set contains a unique maximal element, which we denote by
[b_{w}]
. Recent work by Milićević and Viehmann in
[
22
]
show that whenever the dimension of
X_{w}(b_{w})
agrees with its virtual dimension in the sense of
[
9
]
, the associated Newton strata inside
\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}
exhibit well-behaved geometry. Furthermore, their theorem gives a condition that can be checked from knowledge of the Newton point
\nu([b_{w}])
for this maximal element of the
B(G)_{w}
, but it provides important conclusion about the shape of the entire poset. In light of these results, it becomes important to understand the maximal Newton point
\nu([b_{w}])
for elements
w\in\widetilde{W}
.

An explicit description of
\nu([b_{w}])
is given by Milićević in
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
for elements
w\in\widetilde{W}
that are
suitably far from walls of any Weyl chamber
. To quantify the later condition, let us define for any dominant coweight
\lambda
its depth as

\text{depth}(\lambda)=\min\{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle:\alpha~\text{is a simple root}\}.

This quantity estimates how far
\lambda
is from the walls of any Weyl chamber. Then a formula for
\nu([b_{w}])
obtained in loc. sit. under ‘superregularity’ hypothesis on the dominant translation part
\lambda
of
w
, which roughly says that the depth of
\lambda
is
quadratically large
with respect to the semisimple rank of
\mathbf{G}
. To establish this result, Milićević first derives a characterization of the covering relation in
\widetilde{W}
, again under certain superregularity hypothesis.

This characterization is of independent interest, and more recently it has been utilized by He and Yu in
[
12
]
to deduce a partial description of admissible sets in
\widetilde{W}
. This latter description is a crucial ingredient in proving the main result in their paper, which provides a formula for the dimension of
X(\mu,b)
. However, due to this reliance on the result about covering relation, such dimension formula is established in loc. sit. under the hypothesis that
\mu
is superregular.

1.2.
Main results

The main purpose of this article is to show that the aforementioned results in the study of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties can be improved by weakening the hypothesis required to prove them.

1.2.1.

An uniform bound is given in
[
21
, Corollary 3.3]
in the quasi-simple case, which says that the description of
\nu([b_{w}])
provided in the article is valid whenever the following depth hypothesis is satisfied on
\lambda
:

depth(\lambda)\geq\begin{cases}8\ell(w_{0}),&\text{if $\mathbf{G}~\text{is of classical type}$;}\\
16\ell(w_{0}),&\text{if $\mathbf{G}~\text{is of exceptional type}$.}\\
\end{cases}

Here
\ell(w_{0})
is the length of the longest element
w_{0}
in the associated finite Weyl group
W
. However, it is noted in loc. sit. that this restriction on the lower bound arises merely from the proof method and is rather superficial, in the sense that depending on the element
w
one can often get the same formula for
\nu([b_{w}])
under a much weaker hypothesis. In this paper, we show that one can indeed weaken this hypothesis considerably. For simplicity, we focus on the quasi-simple groups in the statement of this result.

Theorem A

(
Theorem

4.1
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a quasi-simple split group of rank
n
. Let
w=ut^{\lambda}v
be an element of its Iwahori-Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
such that
\text{depth}(\lambda)>\Xi
, where
\Xi
is a linear expression of
n
. Then the maximal Newton point associated to
w
is given by
\nu([b]_{w})=\lambda-\text{weight}(v^{-1},u)
.

Here the precise expression of
\Xi
depends on the group
\mathbf{G}
. We refer to
section

2.7
for the definition of
weight
and to the statement of
theorem

4.1
and its subsequent remark for the explicit form of
\Xi
and relevant discussion on how to handle the case when
\mathbf{G}
is a split connected reductive group which is not necessarily quasi-simple.

1.2.2.

Our second main result characterizes the covering relation of Bruhat order for most of the elements in
\widetilde{W}
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

6.1
)
.

Assume that
w=ut^{\lambda}v
be an element of
\widetilde{W}
such that
\text{depth}(\lambda)
is bigger than a certain constant. Let
r_{\beta}=t^{mu\alpha^{\vee}}s_{u\alpha}
be an affine reflection for some positive root
\alpha
and integer
m
, and let
w^{\prime}=r_{\beta}w
. Then
w\gtrdot w^{\prime}
is a covering relation, i.e.
w\geq w^{\prime}
and
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})+1
, if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

m=0
and
\ell(us_{\alpha})=\ell(u)-1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=us_{\alpha}t^{\lambda}v
.

(2)

m=1
and
\ell(us_{\alpha})=\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=us_{\alpha}t^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}v
.

(3)

m=\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
and
\ell(s_{\alpha}v)=\ell(v)+1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=ut^{\lambda}s_{\alpha}v
.

(4)

m=\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-1
and
\ell(s_{\alpha}v)=\ell(v)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=ut^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}v
.

We refer to the statement of
Theorem

6.1
and its subsequent remark for the precise constant lower bound mentioned above. We remark that the same characterization was first proved in
[
18
, Proposition 4.1]
under the assumption that the depth of the relevant coweight is at least
2|W|+2
. Later it was improved in
[
21
, Proposition 4.2]
, where the depth hypothesis is relaxed to a quadratic lower bound in the quasi-simple case as follows:

depth(\lambda)\geq\begin{cases}2\ell(w_{0})+2,&\text{if $\mathbf{G}~\text{is not of type }G_{2}$;}\\
3\ell(w_{0})+3,&\text{if $\mathbf{G}~\text{is of type }G_{2}$.}\\
\end{cases}

1.2.3.

In
section

7
, we improve upon a result about dimension of
X(\mu,b)
. Based on recent discovery of the dimension formula for single affine Deligne-Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
for “sufficiently large”
w
in
[
13
]
, He and Yu provide a formula in
[
12
]
for the dimension of
X(\mu,b)
under the assumption that
\mu
is superregular. We show that this formula in fact holds true for all
dominant regular
\mu
.

Theorem C
.

Suppose that
G
is a split connected reductive group. Let
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
be dominant regular. Assume that
[b]\in B(G,\mu)
with
\mu\geq\nu([b])+2\rho^{\vee}
. Then

\dim X(\mu,b)=\langle\rho,\mu-\nu([b])\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\text{def}_{\textbf{G}}(b)+\frac{1}{2}(\ell(w_{0})-\ell_{R}(w_{0})).

We refer the reader to
section

2.4
and
section

2.5
for relevant definitions.

1.3.
Strategy

We now discuss the outline of proofs of the above results. We refer to
section

2
for relevant definitions.

Let us start by summarising our approach to prove theorem A. By employing certain tools from the theory of Demazure product, we show in
section

3.2
that one can recast the problem of computing the maximal Newton point associated with an arbitrary element of
\widetilde{W}
to that of an element lying in the dominant chamber. Hence, we focus exclusively on such element
w
. To do so, we repeatedly apply an observation about Bruhat order on
\widetilde{W}
from
[
25
]
until we reach a dominant translation element below
w
. In order to carry out this procedure, we impose a certain lower bound on the depth of the coweight associated to
w
in
section

4.1
. We also show that under this depth hypothesis, the Newton point of
[b_{w}]
is given by a translation element below it. We finally confirm that this is indeed the translation element produced in the process alluded to before. This is achieved by proving a technical lemma, which employs techniques about downward Demazure product, and as such brings in another restriction in the depth hypothesis. Taking into account these two restrictions on the depth, we finally prove the theorem by appealing to the fact that the result is already known under superregularity hypothesis in
[
21
]
.

To prove theorem B, we again (easily) reduce to the case of considering elements lying in the dominant chamber. Then we estimate a series of relevant quantities to assert that the coweight associated with the dominant translation part of the plausible cocover has to remain in the closure of one of the two specific Weyl chambers in order for covering relation to hold. Finally, we deal with each of these two cases separately. We note that a crucial aspect in which our proof differs from that in
[
21
]
is that we handle the cases where this coweight can be possibly singular.

We remark that in the treatment of
[
21
]
, the covering relation in
\widetilde{W}
is in fact the starting point towards obtaining the formula of maximal Newton point. The strategy in loc. sit. is to apply the characterization of covering relation on
w
and its subsequent cocovers repeatedly, until one reaches the maximal translation element below
w
. One then carefully keeps track of the cocovers of a given element
w
to quantify a depth hypothesis under which this process work. Hence, it is plausible that one may use an improved version of the covering relation characterization (as the one established in theorem B) and carry out a similar analysis to obtain the maximal Newton point formula under a weaker hypothesis on the depth,
which may even be a constant lower bound hypothesis
. However, we do not follow this approach here. While we prove theorem A only under a linear bound hypothesis on depth, our approach exclusively outlines techniques from Demazure product and
it highlights the fact that the simple observation in
lemma

4.3
lies at the heart of this matter
. We do point out some speculative assumptions about how one can improve theorem A, see
remark

4.9
.

Let us now briefly discuss our strategy for proving theorem C. The key ingredient in eliminating superregularity condition here is the additivity property of admissible sets established in
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
. This is one of the crucial results used in proving Kottwitz-Rapoport conjecture on non-emptiness of
X(\mu,b)
in loc. sit. We use this additivity to show that certain steps in the existing proof in
[
12
]
can be carried out in a way that superregularity is no longer required. We point out that the proof philosophy has a resemblance to that of theorem A, in the sense that we leverage the results already established in loc. sit. under superregularity hypothesis.

We denote by

be the subgroup of
\widetilde{W}

•

Let

•

2.5.
Virtual dimension of affine Deligne-Lusztig variety

Note that any element
w\in\widetilde{W}
can be written in a unique way as
w=ut^{\lambda}v
with
\lambda

u,v\in W

Let
\mathbf{J}_{b}
be the reductive group over

\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)=\{g\in\breve{G};gb\sigma(g)^{-1}=b\}.
Then the

\rm{def}_{\mathbf{G}}(b)=\rank_{F}\mathbf{G}-\rank_{F}\mathbf{J}_{b}.
Here for a reductive group

Following
[

d_{w}(b)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\ell(w)+\ell(\eta(w))-\rm{def}_{\mathbf{G}}(b)\big)-\langle\rho,\nu([b])\rangle.

The justification of defining such an expression lies in a result proved by He in

2.6.
Demazure product and its variations

We recall three operations
*,\rhd,\lhd:\widetilde{W}\times\widetilde{W}\rightarrow\widetilde{W}
. Here
*
is the
Demazure product
and
\lhd,\rhd
are the left and right
downward Demazure products
, respectively. We describe these operations in form of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
7
, Section 2.2]

Let
x,y\in\widetilde{W}
.

(1)

The subset
\{uv:u\leq x,v\leq y\}
contains an unique maximal element, which we denote by
x*y
. Moreover,
x*y=u^{\prime}y=xv^{\prime}
for some
u^{\prime}\leq x
and
v^{\prime}\leq y
and
\ell(x*y)=\ell(u^{\prime})+\ell(y)=\ell(x)+\ell(v^{\prime})
.

(2)

The subset
\{uy:u\leq x\}
contains a unique minimal element, which
we denote by
x\rhd y
. Moreover,
x\rhd y=u^{\prime\prime}y
for some
u^{\prime\prime}\leq x
with
\ell(x\rhd y)=\ell(y)-\ell(u^{\prime\prime})
.

(3)

The subset
\{xv:v\leq y\}
contains a unique minimal element, which
we denote by
x\lhd y
. Moreover,
x\lhd y=xv^{\prime\prime}
for some
v^{\prime\prime}\leq y
with
\ell(x\lhd y)=\ell(x)-\ell(v^{\prime\prime})
.

The Demazure product is related to product of closure of two Iwahori double cosets in
\breve{G}
. More precisely, for any

w\in\widetilde{W},\overline{\breve{I}w\breve{I}}=\bigcup_{w^{\prime}\leq w}\breve{I}w^{\prime}\breve{I}
is a closed admissible subset of
G
in the sense of
[
11
]
. Then for any
x,y\in\widetilde{W}
, we have

\overline{\breve{I}x\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}y\breve{I}}=\bigcup_{u\leq x}\breve{I}x\breve{I}\cdot\bigcup_{v\leq y}\breve{I}y\breve{I}=\bigcup_{w\leq x*y}\breve{I}w\breve{I}=\overline{\breve{I}(x*y)\breve{I}}.

The above equation also implies that
*
is an associative binary operation on
\widetilde{W}
. We will need the following results about these operations.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
8
, Lemma 2 and Lemma 3]

(1)

If
w\geq w^{\prime}
,
v\leq v^{\prime}
are elements of
\widetilde{W}
, then
w\rhd v\leq w^{\prime}\rhd v^{\prime}
.

(2)

For any three elements
x,y,z\in\widetilde{W}
, we have
x\rhd(y\rhd z)=(x*y)\rhd z
. In other words, the action
(\widetilde{W},*)\times W\rightarrow\widetilde{W},(x,y)\rightarrow x\rhd y
is a left action of the monoid
(\widetilde{W},*)
.

2.7.
Quantum Bruhat graph

We recall the quantum Bruhat graph introduced by Brenti, Fomin and Postnikov in
[
6
]
. By definition, a
quantum Bruhat graph

\Gamma_{\Phi}
is a directed graph with

•

vertices given by the elements of
W
;

•

upward edges
w\rightharpoonup ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1
;

•

downward edges
w\rightharpoondown ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
.

Note that
\ell(s_{\alpha})\leq\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1
for any
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, so we have
\ell(ws_{\alpha})\geq\ell(w)-\ell(s_{\alpha})\geq\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1.
Therefore the condition for downward edges can be rephrased to saying that

\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-\ell(s_{\alpha})~\text{with}~\ell(s_{\alpha})=\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1.

Following
[
19
]
, we call
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
to be a
quantum root
if
\ell(s_{\alpha})=\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1
. We have the following description of quantum roots.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
19
, Lemma 4.2]

We have that
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
is a quantum root if and only if

(1)

\alpha
is a long root, or

(2)

\alpha
is a short root, and if
\alpha=\sum\limits_{\alpha_{i}\in\Delta}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
then we have
c_{i}=0
for any long simple root
\alpha_{i}
.

Here for simply laced root systems we consider all roots to be long. Thus in a simply laced type, all roots are quantum. Examples of quantum root, in general, include the simple roots as well as the highest root.

The weight of an upward edge is defined to be
0
and the weight of a downward edge
w\rightharpoondown ws_{\alpha}
is defined to be
\alpha^{\vee}
. The
weight of a path
in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
is defined to be the sum of weights of the edges in the path. For any
x,y\in W
, we denote by
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)
the minimal length among all paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
24
, Theorem 2]
,
[
6
, Lemma 6.7]

Let
x,y\in W
. Then

(1)

There exists a directed path in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
.

(2)

All the shortest paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
have the same weight, which we denote by
\text{wt}(x,y)
.

(3)

Any path in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
has weight
\geqslant\text{wt}(x,y)
.

3.
Some combinatorial property

3.1.
Monotonicity of weight function

Let us define the function
\text{wt}:W\rightarrow X_{*}(T)
by
\text{wt}(x):=\text{wt}(x,1)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in W
; if
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
in Bruhat order, then
\text{wt}(x_{1})\leq\text{wt}(x_{2})
in dominance order.

Proof.

Assume that
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)^{+}
is regular. We first note that
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
implies
t^{\lambda}x_{1}\geqslant t^{\lambda}x_{2}
. It suffices to show this when
x_{2}
is a cover of
x_{1}
, i.e.
x_{2}=x_{1}s_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\ell(x_{2})=\ell(x_{1})+1
. Then
t^{\lambda}x_{2}=t^{\lambda}x_{1}\cdot s_{\alpha}
, hence the two elements in question are comparable. Now,
eq.

2.1
shows that

\ell(t^{\lambda}x_{2})=\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(x_{2})=\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(x_{1})-1=\ell(t^{\lambda}x_{1})-1.

Hence
t^{\lambda}x_{1}\geqslant t^{\lambda}x_{2}
.

Recall the description of maximal Newton point associated to
w\in\widetilde{W}
given in
[
27
]
.

(3.1)

\nu_{w}=\max\{\nu(u):u\leq w,u\in\widetilde{W}\}.

By applying this, we get
\nu_{t^{\lambda}x_{1}}\geqslant\nu_{t^{\lambda}x_{2}}
. Let us now assume that
\lambda
is actually superregular; hence the formula for maximal Newton point in
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
applies in our case and it gives
\nu_{t^{\lambda}x_{i}}=\lambda-\text{wt}(x_{i}^{-1})=\lambda-\text{wt}(x_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Thus we get
\lambda-\text{wt}(x_{1})\geq\lambda-\text{wt}(x_{2})
, whence
\text{wt}(x_{1})\leqslant\text{wt}(x_{2})
.
∎

3.2.
Reduction to dominant chamber alcoves

We will now reduce our calculation of maximal Newton point of an arbitrary element of
\widetilde{W}
to that of a suitable element lying in the dominant chamber.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\lambda
is dominant regular, then
[b_{ut^{\lambda}v}]=[b_{t^{\lambda}(v\lhd u)}]
.

Proof.

Note that by
eq.

2.1
,
\ell(ut^{\lambda}v)=\ell(u)+\ell(t^{\lambda}v)
, hence
ut^{\lambda}v=u*(t^{\lambda}v)
. Thus
\overline{\breve{I}ut^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}=\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}
.

Now recall that
\breve{I}
is
\sigma
-stable, and since
\mathbf{G}
is split,
\sigma
acts trivially on
\widetilde{W}
too. Hence
\sigma
fixes
\overline{\breve{I}w\breve{I}}
for any
w\in\widetilde{W}
. Therefore, if
w=w_{1}w_{2}
is an element with
w_{1}\in\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}},w_{2}\in\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}
, then
w_{1}^{-1}\cdot_{\sigma}w=w_{2}w_{1}
. This shows that
\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}})\subset\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}})
. One obtains the other inclusion in a similar way. This shows that
\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}})=\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}(\overline{\breve{I}t^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}\cdot\overline{\breve{I}u\breve{I}}).

Hence, we conclude that
\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}ut^{\lambda}v\breve{I}}=\breve{G}\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}(t^{\lambda}v)*u\breve{I}}.

Since
G\cdot_{\sigma}\overline{\breve{I}w\breve{I}}
is the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes intersecting
\overline{\breve{I}w\breve{I}}
, this shows that

(3.2)

[b_{ut^{\lambda}v}]=[b_{(t^{\lambda}v)*u}].

Thus it suffices to show that

(a) if
\lambda
is dominant regular, then
(t^{\lambda}v)*u=t^{\lambda}(v\lhd u)
.

By
lemma

2.4
, we just need to argue the case when
u
is a simple reflection
s\in\mathbb{S}
. Then this statement is equivalent to the assertion that
\ell(vs)=\ell(v)-1
if and only if
\ell(t^{\lambda}vs)=\ell(t^{\lambda}v)+1
. This follows directly from a computation using
eq.

2.1
. Hence we are done.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
x,y\in W
. Then
\text{wt}(x,y)=\text{wt}(x^{-1}\lhd y)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)^{+}
is superregular. Applying the formula established in
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
to
w:=xt^{\lambda}y
and
w^{\prime}:=t^{\lambda}(y\lhd x)
, we get that
\nu_{w}=\lambda-\text{wt}(y^{-1},x)
and
\nu_{w^{\prime}}=\lambda-\text{wt}((y\lhd x)^{-1})=\lambda-\text{wt}(y\lhd x)
. Since
\nu_{w}=\nu_{w^{\prime}}
by
proposition

3.2
, we get
\text{wt}(y^{-1},x)=\text{wt}(y\lhd x)
. Now replacing the pair
(x,y)
by
(y,x^{-1})
, we get the conclusion.
∎

3.3.
Reformulation of weight in quantum Bruhat graph

We now give another interpretation of weight of a shortest path in the quantum Bruhat graph.

Definition 3.4
.

For an element
x\in W
, we say that
x=s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k}}
is a
reduced quantum reflection decomposition
if

(1)

\{\beta_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}
is a collection of (not necessarily simple) quantum roots, i.e.
l(s_{\beta_{i}})=\langle 2\rho,\beta_{i}^{\vee}\rangle-1.

(2)

\ell(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell(s_{\beta_{i}})
.

(3)

Subject to the first two conditions,
k
is minimal.

In this case, we say that
k
is the minimal reduced length of
x
in terms of reflections associated to quantum roots, and write
\ell_{\downarrow}(x)=k
. Note that
d_{\Gamma}(x,1)=\ell_{\downarrow}(x)
.

The choice of notation is suggested from the fact that

{Reduced quantum reflection decompositions for
x
}

↕

{Shortest paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
1
that uses
only downwards edges
}

By
[
22
, Proposition 4.11]
, the weight of any path in the later set is equal to
\text{wt}(x)
. Therefore, the problem of computing
\text{wt}(x)
reduces to one of finding a suitable decomposition for
x
: if
x=s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k}}
is such a decomposition then
\text{wt}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
. In view of
corollary

3.3
, we can thus reformulate
\text{wt}(x,y)
in terms of a decomposition of
x^{-1}\lhd y
.

4.2.
Proof of the easier inequality

We need the following result about Bruhat order on

Lemma 4.3
.

[
25
, Remark 3.9]

Let
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
be dominant. Let
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\lambda-\beta^{\vee}
is dominant. Then
t^{\lambda-\beta^{\vee}}\leq t^{\lambda}s_{\beta}\leq t^{\lambda}
in
\widetilde{W}
.

We start by establishing the easier inequality.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
w=t^{\lambda}x\in\widetilde{W}
such that
\text{depth}(\lambda)\geq\widetilde{}\mathcal{M}
. Then
w\geq t^{\lambda-\text{wt}(x)}
and therefore
\nu_{w}\geq\lambda-wt(x)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x=s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l}}
is a reduced quantum reflection decomposition with
\ell_{\downarrow}(x)=l>1
, and therefore
\text{wt}(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
. Let us first note that:

(a)
s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k}}
is a reduced quantum reflection decomposition for
x_{k}:=xs_{\beta_{l}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}
for all
k\leq l
.

Suppose otherwise; then we can find a reduced quantum reflection decomposition of the form
x_{k}=s_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{j}}
with
j<k
. But then
x=x_{k}s_{\beta_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l}}=s_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots s_{\gamma_{j}}s_{\beta_{k+1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l}}
satisfies length additivity:

\ell(x)=\ell(x_{k})+\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{l}\beta_{i}^{\vee}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\gamma_{i}^{\vee}+\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{l}\beta_{i}^{\vee}.

Note that all associated roots in this new decomposition of
x
are quantum, and it has
j+l-k<l
factors, hence it contradicts the fact that
\ell_{\downarrow}(x)=l
. So statement (a) is proved.

To prove the proposition, we induct on
\ell_{\downarrow}(x)
. We note our depth hypothesis ensures that
\lambda-\beta^{\vee}
is dominant for all
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
: for
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
, we have
\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-\beta^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-\langle\alpha_{i},\beta^{\vee}\rangle>0
, since maximum value of
\langle\alpha^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\rangle
equals
2
in every Cartan type, except for
G_{2}
- in which case it equals
3
, cf.
[
5
, Chapter 6, Section 1, no. 3]
. Therefore
lemma

4.3
covers the base case, i.e. when
x=s_{\beta}
for some
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
.

Let us now apply induction hypothesis to
xs_{\beta_{l}}=s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l-1}}
. Since
\text{wt}(xs_{\beta_{l}})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
by virtue of statement (a), this gives

(4.1)

t^{\lambda}s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l-1}}\geq t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}}.

To perform the induction step, we apply a property of Bruhat order as described in
eq.

2.3
. We set

w_{1}=t^{\lambda}x,w_{2}=t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}}s_{\beta_{l}},v=s_{\beta_{l}}

and check the required length additivity. Note that
\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
is dominant under our depth hypothesis: for
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
,

\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-\langle\alpha_{i},\text{wt}(xs_{\beta_{l}})\rangle>\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-\mathcal{M}\geq 0,

therefore the length formula in
eq.

2.1
applies. We see that

(1)

\ell(w_{1}v)=\ell(t^{\lambda}s_{\beta_{1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{l-1}})=\ell(t^{\lambda})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\ell(s_{\beta_{i}})=\ell(t^{\lambda})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\ell(s_{\beta_{i}})+\ell(s_{\beta_{l}})=\ell(w_{1})+\ell(v).

(2)

\ell(w_{2}v)=\ell(t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}})=\ell(t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}})-\ell(s_{\beta_{l}})+\ell(s_{\beta_{l}})=\ell(w_{2})+\ell(v).

In presence of
eq.

4.1
, we therefore get
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
, i.e.
t^{\lambda}x\geq t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}}s_{\beta_{l}}
. Finally, we note that
\lambda-\text{wt}(x)=\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
is dominant (by similar argument as before) and thus
lemma

4.3
applies to give
t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l-1}\beta_{i}^{\vee}}s_{\beta_{l}}\geq t^{\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i}^{\vee}}
. Combining these inequalities, we get
t^{\lambda}x\geq t^{\lambda-\text{wt}(x)}
. Appealing to
eq.

3.1
, we thus get
\nu_{w}\geq\lambda-\text{wt}(x)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
w=t^{\lambda}x\in\widetilde{W}
such that
\text{depth}(\lambda)>\widetilde{}\mathcal{M}
. Then
\nu_{w}=\max\{\gamma^{+}:t^{\gamma}\leq w\}
.

Proof.

By
eq.

3.1
, we can assume that
\nu_{w}=\nu(t^{y\mu}z)
for some
t^{y\mu}z\in\widetilde{W},w\geq t^{y\mu}z
. Suppose that

, therefore the order of
z
equals
m\geq 2
. Then
\nu(t^{y\mu}z)=(\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}z^{i}(y\mu))^{+}
. Note that
\zeta\cdot\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}z^{i}(y\mu)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}z^{i}(y\mu)
for all

, so the element
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}z^{i}(y\mu)
lies on the wall of some Weyl chamber, cf.
[

. Therefore
(\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}z^{i}(y\mu))^{+}
lies on the wall of

, and hence

is singular.

By

4.4
, we can assume that
\nu_{w}=\lambda-\epsilon
, with
\epsilon\leq\text{wt}(x)\leq\text{wt}(w_{0})
. Hence, there is some

, and thus

, which is a contradiction to hypothesis on

. Therefore,
\nu_{w}
cannot be singular and hence

.

4.3.
Toward computing a downward Demazure product

We need to understand the largest translation dominated by
w
to determine its associated generic Newton point
\nu_{w}
. In view of
proposition

4.4
, let us denote this translation element by
t^{y\gamma}
for some
y\in W
and
\gamma\geq\lambda-\text{wt}(x)
. Of course, this would be equality if
\lambda
is superregular. We want to leverage this result to establish such equality with some linear bound on depth here. The following lemma lay the path towards proving that.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}
is dominant, we have
t^{\lambda}x\geq t^{y\gamma}
if and only if
t^{\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}}\geq t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{y\gamma}
.

Proof.

Note that if
\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}
is dominant,
\ell(t^{\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}})=\langle 2\rho,\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}\rangle=\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(t^{-2\rho^{\vee}})
; also, since
\lambda\geq\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}
are dominant coweights,
t^{\lambda}\geq t^{\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}}
, cf.
[
25
, proof of Proposition 3.5]
. Hence,
t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{\lambda}=t^{\lambda-2\rho^{\vee}}
.

Now, it suffices to show the following:

(a) If
a,b
are two elements in a Coxeter group and
s
is a simple reflection, then
a\geq b
if and only if
s\rhd a\geq s\rhd b
.

This follows from the well known lifting property of Bruhat order.
Note that we only need to consider the case when
s\rhd a=sa
, i.e.
a\geq sa
. Then there are two further cases.

(1)

s\rhd b=b
: in this case,
sb\geq b
. Hence
a\geq b
is equivalent to
sa\geq b
, and hence
s\rhd a\geq s\rhd b
.

(2)

s\rhd b=sb
: in this case,
b\geq sb
. Hence
a\geq b
is equivalent to
sa\geq sb
, and hence
s\rhd a\geq s\rhd b
.

∎

In
section

4.4
we will focus on computing the downward Demazure product that appears in
lemma

4.6
. Before proceeding any further to do that, let us first sketch what the answer should look like. We first apply
lemma

2.4
with
w=t^{-2\rho^{\vee}},w^{\prime}=w_{0}
and
v=v^{\prime}=t^{y\mu}
; if we require that
\lambda
is of large enough depth (in a sense made precise below) so that
\gamma
is dominant regular, we get
w_{0}\rhd yt^{\gamma}y^{-1}=t^{\gamma}y^{-1}\geq t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{y\gamma}
. We next apply
lemma

2.4
with
v^{\prime}=t^{y\gamma},v=t^{\gamma}y^{-1}
and
w=w^{\prime}=t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}
. If we now require
\lambda
to be of sufficiently large depth so that
\gamma-2\rho^{\vee}
is also dominant, we get
t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{y\gamma}\geq t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{\gamma}y^{-1}=t^{\gamma-2\rho^{\vee}}y^{-1}
. Combining these, we see that whenever
\lambda
has “large” depth (to be specified later) we have

t^{\gamma}y^{-1}\geq t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{y\gamma}\geq t^{\gamma-2\rho^{\vee}}y^{-1}.

Therefore, it is natural to expect that this downward Demazure product depends only on

whenever

has a “large” depth. In the next section, we quantify this depth condition and show that we indeed get the desired conclusion.

4.4.1.
Relating relevant coweights

We first determine how the coweights associated to translation part of these elements are related. By (D1),
t^{y\gamma}=a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}t^{a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}b_{2}

v\in W
such that
\gamma=v(a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})
. We will now show that
v=1
.

Assume the contrary and let
\beta\in\text{Inv}(v)
. Now,
\mu_{2}=v^{-1}\gamma-a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}
is dominant, therefore

\langle\beta,v^{-1}\gamma-a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}\rangle\geq 0.

By (D3), we have
t^{2\rho^{\vee}}\geq a_{1}t^{\mu_{1}}b_{1}
, thus
2\rho^{\vee}\geq\mu_{1}
; by a geometric characterization of dominance order, e.g. see
[

, this gives

\mu_{1}=\sum\limits_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\zeta\cdot 2\rho^{\vee}~\text{for some}~a_{\zeta}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}~\text{such that}~\sum\limits_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}=1.

Substituting this expression of
\mu_{1}
in the pairing above and using its

\langle v\beta,\gamma\rangle-\sum_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\langle\zeta^{-1}b_{1}a_{2}\beta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0.

Let
\beta^{\prime}=-v\beta\in\Phi^{+}
. Note that
\zeta^{-1}b_{1}a_{2}\beta\geq-\theta
for any element
\zeta\in W
, and therefore
\langle\zeta^{-1}b_{1}a_{2}\beta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle\geq-\langle\theta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle
. Putting all these together, we obtain

\langle\beta^{\prime},\gamma\rangle=-\sum_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\langle\zeta^{-1}b_{1}a_{2}\beta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle\leq\sum\limits_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\langle\theta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle=\mathcal{S}.

Now recall that
\gamma=\nu_{w}=\lambda-\epsilon
is dominant with
\epsilon\leq\text{wt}(x)\leq\text{wt}(w_{0})
. Choose a simple root

. Then we get

, and therefore
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\leq\mathcal{S}+\langle\alpha,\epsilon\rangle\leq\mathcal{S}+\widetilde{}\mathcal{M}
. Since this yields a contradiction to the depth hypothesis of
\lambda
, we are done. Therefore

and
\gamma=a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
.

4.4.2.
Showing that certain coweights are regular

Note that
\gamma
is regular, since for any simple root
\alpha_{i}
we have
\langle\alpha_{i},\gamma\rangle=0\implies\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\epsilon\rangle\leq\widetilde{}\mathcal{M}
, thereby contradicting the depth hypothesis imposed on
\lambda
. Hence, knowing that the translation parts in

are equal allows us to relate the finite Weyl group components from both sides. Namely, we have
y=a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}=b_{2}^{-1}
. We can simplify these relations further in the following way. We observe that
\mu_{2}
is regular, since otherwise we can find a simple root
\alpha
such that
\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle=\sum_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\langle\alpha,\zeta\cdot a_{2}^{-1}b_{1}^{-1}2\rho^{\vee}\rangle=\sum_{\zeta\in W}\langle b_{1}a_{2}\zeta\alpha,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle\leq\sum_{\zeta\in W}a_{\zeta}\langle\theta,2\rho^{\vee}\rangle=\mathcal{S}
; but this would yield contradiction to the imposed depth condition as before.

4.4.3.
Showing that the product describes a dominant chamber alcove

We can now show that
a_{2}=1
. Recall that
t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{\gamma y}=a_{2}t^{\mu_{2}}b_{2}
. Let us rewrite
t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}=w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}=w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}
and apply
w_{0}\rhd
to this equation. This gives

w_{0}\rhd\{(w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0})\rhd t^{y\gamma}\}=w_{0}\rhd(a_{2}t^{\mu_{2}}b_{2}).

We now apply
lemma

2.4
on the left hand side, and use the fact

is regular in the right hand side. We get

\{w_{0}*(w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0})\}\rhd t^{y\gamma}=(w_{0}\rhd a_{2})t^{\mu_{2}}b_{2}.

Note that
w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}=w_{0}*t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}
, and hence we can use associativity of operation
*
to rewrite the element in the parenthesis on the left hand side as
w_{0}*(w_{0}*t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0})=(w_{0}*w_{0})*t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}=w_{0}*t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}=w_{0}t^{2\rho^{\vee}}w_{0}=t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}
. Therefore, we finally deduce

t^{-2\rho^{\vee}}\rhd t^{y\gamma}=t^{\mu_{2}}b_{2}.

This proves our claim.

Therefore, the relations arising from (D1) are

(4.2)

\gamma=b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},y=a_{1}b_{1}=b_{2}^{-1}.

Let us now utilize (D2). It says that we have length additivity in the decomposition

, i.e.

\langle\gamma,2\rho\rangle=\ell(a_{1})+\langle\mu_{1},2\rho\rangle-\ell(b_{1})+\langle\mu_{2},2\rho\rangle-\ell(b_{2}).

By use of

, the last equation reduces to

\ell(a_{1}b_{1})=\ell(a_{1})-\ell(b_{1})+\langle\mu_{1}-b_{1}^{-1}\mu_{1},2\rho\rangle.

Therefore we are led to consider the following subset of

\Omega_{y}=\{at^{\mu}b\in\widetilde{W}:y=ab,at^{\mu}b\leq t^{2\rho^{\vee}},\ell(ab)=\ell(a)-\ell(b)+\langle\mu-b^{-1}\mu,2\rho\rangle\}.

Note that the definition of

Finishing the proof

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

By combining

4.6
and

, we observe that our standing assumption

implies

Let us now choose

to be large enough so that

(1)

\ell(t^{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot t^{\gamma-\mu_{y}}y^{-1})=\ell(t^{\lambda^{\prime}+\gamma-\mu_{y}}y^{-1})=\langle 2\rho,\lambda^{\prime}+\gamma-\mu_{y}\rangle-\ell(y^{-1})=\langle 2\rho,\lambda^{\prime}\rangle+\langle 2\rho,\gamma-\mu_{y}\rangle-\ell(y^{-1})=\ell(t^{\lambda^{\prime}})+\ell(t^{\gamma-\mu_{y}}y^{-1}).

in this situation, and get

t^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-2\rho^{\vee}}x\geq t^{\gamma+\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{y}}y^{-1}.

Now, we use the ‘only if’ part of

4.6
to deduce from the previous equation

t^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}x\geq t^{y(\gamma+\lambda^{\prime})}.

Therefore, by

\nu_{t^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}x}\geq\nu_{t^{y(\gamma+\lambda^{\prime})}}

applies to elements of

\text{wt}(w_{0})=4\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+8\alpha_{2}^{\vee}+10\alpha_{3}^{\vee}+14\alpha_{4}^{\vee}+12\alpha_{5}^{\vee}+8\alpha_{6}^{\vee}+6\alpha_{7}^{\vee}+2\alpha_{8}^{\vee}.

(8)

Type
F_{4}

w_{0}=s_{2\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{2}+4\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{4}}s_{\alpha_{2}+2\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{4}}s_{\alpha_{2}+2\alpha_{3}}s_{\alpha_{2}}

(9)

Type
G_{2}
: here
w_{0}=s_{3\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}

\text{wt}(w_{0})=2\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+2\alpha_{2}^{\vee}.

Remark 5.1
.

We note that the weights of longest element found above appear in a rather different context, cf.

6.
Covering relation in affine Weyl group

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
W
is an irreducible finite Weyl group. Assume that

(6.1)

depth(\lambda)\geq\begin{cases}3,&\text{if $W\text{is of simply laced type}$;}\\
4,&\text{if $W\text{is of non-simply laced type but not of type}~G_{2}$;}\\
6,&\text{if $W\text{is of type}~G_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Then
w:=t^{\lambda}y\gtrdot w^{\prime}:=t^{m\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}w
if and only if one of the following holds.

(1)

m=1
and
\ell(s_{\alpha})=\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=s_{\alpha}t^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}y
.

(2)

m=\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
and
\ell(s_{\alpha}y)=\ell(y)+1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=t^{\lambda}s_{\alpha}y
.

(3)

m=\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-1
,
\ell(s_{\alpha}y)=\ell(y)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
; in this case,
w^{\prime}=t^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}y
.

Remark 6.2
.

We explain how
theorem

6.1
implies theorem B. Let us first note that we can again reduce to the case of irreducible factors as in
remark

4.2
. Notice that if
\lambda
is regular,
\ell(ut^{\lambda}v)=\ell(u)+\ell(t^{\lambda}v)
by
eq.

2.1
. Choose reduced expressions
u=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}
and
t^{\lambda}v=s_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{j_{k}}\zeta
, where
s_{i_{p}},s_{j_{q}}\in\mathbb{S}
for
1\leq p\leq l,1\leq q\leq k
and
\zeta\in\Omega
. Then
ut^{\lambda}v=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}s_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{j_{k}}\zeta
is a reduced expression, and a cocover of
ut^{\lambda}v
must be of the form of either
s_{i_{1}}\cdots\widehat{s_{i_{m}}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}s_{j_{1}}\cdots s_{j_{k}}\zeta
for some
p\in[1,l]
or
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}s_{j_{1}}\cdots\widehat{s_{j_{n}}}\cdots s_{j_{k}}\zeta
for some
q\in[1,k]
. In other words, any cocover of
ut^{\lambda}v
is obtained by

•

(C1) either multiplying a cocover of
u
with
t^{\lambda}v
, or

•

(C2) multiplying
u
with a cocover of
t^{\lambda}v
.

The procedure listed in (C1) is easy to describe. A cocover of
u
is of the form
us_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\ell(us_{\alpha})=\ell(u)-1
. This corresponds to case (1) in theorem B. We claim that the three other cases there, i.e. (2)-(4), come from procedure described in (C2), and hence they correspond to those listed in
theorem

6.1
. Clearly, the third and fourth case in theorem B corresponds respectively to the second and third case in
theorem

6.1
. Finally, suppose that
w^{\prime}:=s_{a}t^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}}v
is a cocover of
w:=t^{\lambda}v
such that
uw^{\prime}
is a cocover of
uw
. By
eq.

2.1
,

\ell(uw^{\prime})=\ell(us_{a})+\ell(t^{\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}})-\ell(v)=\ell(us_{a})+\langle 2\rho,\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-\ell(v).

We use dominance of
\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}
in the last equality; this is true as
\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-a^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0
for any simple root
a_{i}
- since the maximum value of
\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
equals
2
in every Cartan type, except for
G_{2}
- in which case it equals
3
, cf.
[
5
, Chapter VI, Section 3, no. 1]
. Note that

(6.2)

\ell(us_{\alpha})\leq\ell(u)+\ell(s_{a})\leq\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1.

Since
\ell(ut^{\lambda}v)=\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle-\ell(v)
, the cocover condition
uw\gtrdot uw^{\prime}
gives

\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle-\ell(v)-1=\ell(us_{a})+\langle 2\rho,\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-\ell(v)\leq\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1+\langle 2\rho,\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-\ell(v).

Hence we deduce that all of the inequalities in
eq.

6.2
must be equality, and thus we get
\ell(us_{\alpha})=\ell(u)+\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1
. This is exactly the situation listed in the second item in theorem B.

We will only focus on proving the necessity of the above conditions. For the sufficiency part, the argument in
[
21
, Section 4]
toward the end of proposition
4.2
can be applied.

We divide our discussion of the proof in the following subsections.

6.1.
Some useful inequalities

In this subsection, we lay out an estimate of some relevant quantities that we shall use in the next subsection. We shall resume the assumption on the depth of
\lambda
stated in
theorem

6.1
throughout our discussion after the first lemma below.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
w\gtrdot w^{\prime}
. Then we must have
w^{\prime}=t^{m\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}w
for some affine root
\alpha+m
with
1\leq m\leq\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}
be a reduced expression, where
s_{i_{j}}\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. This describes a reduced gallery from
\mathbf{a}
to
w\mathbf{a}
via the chain of adjacent alcoves
\mathbf{a}\rightarrow s_{i_{1}}\mathbf{a}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}\mathbf{a}
. Since the gallery is reduced, it cannot cross any hyperplane twice - hence all the alcoves lie in the dominant chamber. Let
r_{j}
be the affine reflection with respect to the common wall between
\mathbf{a}_{j-1}:=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{a}_{j}:=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j}}\mathbf{a}
, that is
r_{j}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}s_{i_{j}}s_{i_{j-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
.

Now suppose that
w^{\prime}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots\widehat{s_{i_{k}}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}
is a cocover; as before, let
\{\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}:1\leq j\leq l-1\}
be the collection of alcoves describing the reduced gallery corresponding to this expression of
w^{\prime}
and let
r_{j}^{\prime}
be the associated affine reflections. Then we see that
r_{j}=r_{j}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{a}_{j}=\mathbf{a}_{j}^{\prime}
for
1\leq j\leq k-1
, and the rest of the gallery for
w^{\prime}
is the reflection of portion of the gallery for
w
from
\mathbf{a}_{k+1}
onward with respect to the hyperplane corresponding to
r_{k}
. This is because for
j\geq k
we have

\mathbf{a}_{j-1}^{\prime}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{j}}\mathbf{a}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}}s_{i_{k}}s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}(s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j}}\mathbf{a})=r_{k}(\mathbf{a}_{j}).

In particular,
w^{\prime}=r_{k}w
.

Therefore, to create a cocover of
w
, we must reflect
w\mathbf{a}
with respect to some hyperplane
H_{\alpha-m}
lying between
\mathbf{a}
and itself. Since the number of hyperplanes between the alcove
w\mathbf{a}
and the wall
H_{\alpha}
is
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
, this restricts the possibility of
m
to asserted values above.
∎

Now,
w^{\prime}=t^{m\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}w=s_{\alpha}t^{\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}}y
. By
lemma

6.3
, the coweight associated to the translation part of
w^{\prime}
belongs to following list of coweights:

(6.3)

\lambda-\alpha^{\vee},\lambda-2\alpha^{\vee},\cdots,\lambda-(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-1)\alpha^{\vee}=s_{\alpha}(\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}),\lambda-\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\alpha^{\vee}=s_{\alpha}(\lambda).

Let us now define

k=k_{\lambda,\alpha}:=\text{max}\{m:\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}^{+}},1\leq m\leq\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We now give a lower bound for
k
.

Lemma 6.4
.

We have that

k\geq\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $W\text{is of simply laced type}$;}\\
2,&\text{if $W\text{is of non-simply laced type}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By the definition of
k
, there is a real number
\widetilde{m}
with
k\leq\widetilde{m}<k+1
such that
\lambda-\widetilde{m}\alpha^{\vee}
lies on at least one of the walls of
\mathcal{C}^{+}
. It follows that for some simple root
\alpha_{i}
, we have
\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-\widetilde{m}\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0
. Therefore,
\widetilde{m}=\frac{\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}
- hence giving
k=\lfloor\frac{\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\rfloor
. Recalling the estimate about maximum value of
\langle\alpha^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\rangle
in each Cartan type, we get the desired bound on

from the depth condition on
\lambda
.
∎

Our next lemma estimates the length of the translation elements corresponding to the coweights in the list (6.1). This proof closely follows a part of the proof of
[
21
, Proposition 4.2]
. We include it here for completeness’ sake.

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that for some integer
m\in[1,\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle]
,
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
lies in the closure of a chamber different from
\mathcal{C}^{+}
or
\mathcal{C}_{s_{\alpha}}
. Then
\ell(t^{\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}})\leq\ell(t^{\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}})
.

Proof.

Following
[
18
]
, define the function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by linearly extending the function
\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined by

\widetilde{f}(m)=\ell(t^{\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}}).

More precisely,
f
is the function associated to the graph obtained by joining
\widetilde{f}(m)
and
\widetilde{f}(m+1)
by the line passing through them for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. It is easy to see that
f
is a convex function, cf.
[
18
]

m
as long as
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
is in the same chamber. Since
\lambda,\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}
are both dominant due to the imposed depth hypothesis, we see that
f(1)=\langle 2\rho,\lambda-\alpha^{\vee}\rangle,f(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle)=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle
.

We now show that
f
is decreasing around
1
and increasing around
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
. For example, if
m\in(0,\frac{3}{2})
and
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
then by our earlier discussion about maximum value of
\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
we have

\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-m\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}\text{depth}(\lambda)-3,&\text{if $W\text{is not of type}~G_{2}$;}\\
\text{depth}(\lambda)-\frac{9}{2},&\text{if $W\text{is of type}~G_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Hence, our depth hypothesis ensures that
\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0
. Therefore,
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
is in
\overline{\mathcal{C}^{+}}
whenever
m\in(0,\frac{3}{2})
, and thus

(6.4)

f(m)=\langle 2\rho,\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle-m\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle

is clearly decreasing in this neighbourhood. Similarly, we note that if
m\in(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-\frac{1}{2},\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle+\frac{1}{2})
and
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{i},s_{\alpha}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\rangle-(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-m)\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}\text{depth}(\lambda)-1,&\text{if $W\text{is not of type}~G_{2}$;}\\
\text{depth}(\lambda)-\frac{3}{2},&\text{if $W\text{is of type}~G_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Again, our depth hypothesis ensures that
\langle\alpha_{i},s_{\alpha}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})\rangle>0
. Hence
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathcal{C}_{s_{\alpha}}
whenever
m\in(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-\frac{1}{2},\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle+\frac{1}{2})
, and thus

(6.5)

f(m)=\langle 2\rho,s_{a}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})\rangle=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle-(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-m)\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\langle 2\rho,s_{a}(\lambda)\rangle+m\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle

is clearly increasing in the neighbourhood.

Since the intervals
(0,\frac{3}{2})
and
(\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle-\frac{1}{2},\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle+\frac{1}{2})
are disjoint, by convexity of

we infer the global shape of the graph of
f
. Namely,
f(m)
steadily decreases and is defined by
eq.

6.4

\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}^{+}}
; as
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
traverses through other chambers with value of
m
increasing, it (weakly) decreases further until it reaches local minimum, then it starts increasing; and finally, once
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
enters
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s_{\alpha}}}

is defined by
eq.

6.5
and steadily increases. Therefore, if we define

k^{\prime}=k^{\prime}_{\lambda,\alpha}:=\text{min}\{m:\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s_{\alpha}}},1\leq m\leq\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle,m\in\mathbb{Z}\},

we must have
f(m)\leq\text{max}\{(f(k),f(k^{\prime})\}
for all

. Since
\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}=s_{\alpha}(\lambda-k^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee})
by symmetry, we have
f(k)=f(k^{\prime})=\ell(t^{\lambda-k\alpha^{\vee}})
. Hence we are done.
∎

Recall from
lemma

6.3
that
m\leq\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle
. We now define
z\in W
to be such that
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{z}}
. Note that if
\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}
is singular,
z
is not uniquely specified by this condition. However, the precise choice of
z
would be immaterial in what follows, and henceforth pick one such
z
satisfying the above condition. Let us denote the left and right weak Bruhat order by
\prec_{\text{left}}
and
\prec_{\text{right}}
respectively.

Lemma 6.6
.

We have
z\prec_{\text{right}}s_{\alpha}
. As a consequence,
\ell(s_{\alpha}z)+\ell(z)=\ell(s_{\alpha})
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\text{Inv}(z^{-1})\subset\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha})
, because this is equivalent to
z^{-1}\prec_{\text{left}}s_{\alpha}
, which in turn is equivalent to the claim above.

We start by noting that for
\beta\in\text{Inv}(z^{-1})
, we have
\langle\beta,\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\leq 0
. This follows from rewriting
\langle\beta,\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\langle z^{-1}\beta,z^{-1}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})\rangle
and noting that
z^{-1}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})
is a dominant coweight and
z^{-1}\beta\in-\Phi^{+}
. Therefore we get
\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle\leq m\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
. Since
\lambda
dominant regular,
\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle>0
and hence
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0
as well. Dividing out both side of the inequality by
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
, we get
m\geq\frac{\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle}{\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}
. Combining this with
lemma

6.3
, we obtain

\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\geq\frac{\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle}{\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}.

But then
\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\geq\langle\beta,\lambda\rangle
, whence
\langle s_{\alpha}(\beta),\lambda\rangle=\langle\beta-\langle\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\leq 0
. Since
\lambda
is dominant regular, this gives
s_{\alpha}(\beta)\in-\Phi^{+}
, hence
\beta\in\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha})
. Thus we have shown
\text{Inv}(z^{-1})\subset\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha})
.

Finally, let us note that

\ell(s_{\alpha}z)+\ell(z)=\ell(s_{\alpha})-\ell(z)+2|\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha})^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z^{-1})|+\ell(z)=\ell(s_{\alpha})+2|\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha})^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z^{-1})|=\ell(s_{\alpha}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

6.2.
Two distinct possibilities

In this subsection we further pin down the possible values of
z
. Recall that
z^{-1}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})
is dominant; let us temporarily denote this coweight by
\mu
. Let

be such that

. Abbreviate
\zeta=z^{-1}y

has an unique factorization as
\zeta=\zeta_{J}\zeta^{J}
with
\zeta_{J}\in W_{J}
and
\zeta^{J}\in~^{J}W
. Note that

(6.6)

w^{\prime}=s_{\alpha}t^{\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee}}y=s_{\alpha}zt^{\mu}z^{-1}y=s_{\alpha}zt^{\mu}\zeta_{J}\zeta^{J}=s_{\alpha}z\zeta_{J}t^{\mu}\zeta^{J}.

Lemma 6.7
.

The alcove
t^{\mu}\zeta^{J}\mathbf{a}
is in the dominant chamber.

Proof.

Define
\bar{\omega}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\omega_{i}^{\vee}}{n_{i}}
, where
n_{i}
is the coefficient of
\alpha_{i}
in the highest root
\theta
. Since
\mu+\zeta^{J}\bar{\omega}
is centroid of the alcove
t^{\mu}\zeta^{J}\mathbf{a}
, we can make the following observation:
t^{\mu}\zeta^{J}\mathbf{a}
is in the dominant chamber if and only if
\mu+\zeta^{J}\bar{\omega}
is dominant.

We now show that the latter statement holds true. Note that if
\alpha_{i}\in J
, then
\langle\alpha_{i},\mu+y^{J}\bar{\omega}\rangle=\langle(y^{J})^{-1}\alpha_{i},\bar{\omega}\rangle>0
, since by definition
s_{i}y^{J}>y^{J}\implies(y^{J})^{-1}s_{i}>(y^{J})^{-1}\implies(y^{J})^{-1}\alpha_{i}\in\Phi^{+}
. Now let
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta\setminus J

\langle\alpha_{i},\mu\rangle\geq 1
; since
(y^{J})^{-1}\alpha_{i}\geq-\theta
, we have
\langle\alpha_{i},\mu+y^{J}\bar{\omega}\rangle\geq 1+\langle-\theta,\bar{\omega}\rangle\geq 1-1=0
. This proves the claim.
∎

Applying the previous lemma, we get from

6.6

\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(s_{\alpha}z\zeta_{J})+\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle-\ell(\zeta^{J}).

Therefore the cocover condition gives

\displaystyle\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle-\ell(y)-1=\ell(s_{\alpha}z\zeta_{J})+\langle 2\rho,\mu\rangle-\ell(\zeta^{J}).

In other words, we get

(6.7)

\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(t^{\mu})=1+\ell(s_{\alpha}z\zeta_{J})+\ell(y)-\ell(\zeta^{J}).

Write

. Note that

\ell(y)=\ell(z\cdot\zeta_{J}\zeta^{J})=\ell(z)+\ell(\zeta_{J})+\ell(\zeta^{J})-2|\text{Inv}(z)\cap\text{Inv}((\zeta^{J})^{-1}\zeta_{J}^{-1})|.

Similarly,

\ell(s_{a}z\zeta_{J})=\ell(s_{\alpha}z)-\ell(\zeta_{J})+2|\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})|

Hence we can rewrite the right hand side of

\displaystyle 1+\ell(s_{\alpha}z)+\ell(z)+2\{|\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})|-|\text{Inv}(z)\cap\text{Inv}((\zeta^{J})^{-1}\zeta_{J}^{-1})|\}.

Since
\zeta_{J}^{-1}\prec_{\text{left}}(\zeta^{J})^{-1}\zeta_{J}^{-1}
, we have that
\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})\subset\text{Inv}((\zeta^{J})^{-1}\zeta^{-1})
. Combining this with the fact that
|A|-|B|\leq|A\setminus B|
for two sets

, we therefore conclude that

(6.8)

\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(t^{\mu})\leq 1+\ell(s_{\alpha}z)+\ell(z)+2|\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})|.

Lemma 6.8
.

We have
\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The argument here is similar to the proof of

. Suppose
\beta\in\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(\zeta_{J}^{-1})\subset\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}\cap\text{Inv}(z)^{c}\cap\Phi_{J}^{+}
. Thus
\langle\beta,z^{-1}(\lambda-m\alpha^{\vee})\rangle=0
, hence
\langle z\beta,\lambda\rangle=m\langle z\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle
; since

is a positive root, both sides are positive and

Therefore,
\langle z\beta,\lambda\rangle-\langle z\beta,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle=\langle z\beta,s_{\alpha}(\lambda)\rangle\leq 0
. Since
\beta\in\text{Inv}(s_{\alpha}z)^{c}
, we have

. Hence we have
\langle z\beta,s_{\alpha}(\lambda)\rangle=\langle s_{\alpha}(z\beta),\lambda\rangle\leq 0
, but that is a contradiction since

is dominant regular. This shows that the purported set must be empty and we are done.
∎

Therefore, combining

with

and

\ell(t^{\lambda})-\ell(t^{\mu})\leq 1+\ell(s_{\alpha})

. By

Combining this with

Let us choose
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
such that
\text{depth}(\lambda)\geq 2n
. By
proposition

4.4
, we then have
t^{\lambda}x\geq t^{\lambda-\text{wt}(x)}
. An easy computation shows that

t^{\lambda}x\varpi_{j}^{\vee}-\varpi_{j}^{\vee}=\lambda-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\langle\beta_{i},\varpi_{j}^{\vee}\rangle\beta_{i}^{\vee}=\lambda-\sum\limits_{\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{j}}\beta_{i}^{\vee}.

We set
\zeta_{j}:=\lambda-\sum\limits_{\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{j}}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
. Note that at most
n
coroots are subtracted in the expression of
\zeta_{j}
, and since the maximum value of
\langle\alpha,\beta_{i}^{\vee}\rangle
is
2
for any simple root
\alpha
, the depth hypothesis on
\lambda
ensures that
\zeta_{j}
is dominant. Applying
lemma

8.6
, we get that
\lambda-\text{wt}(x)\in P_{\zeta_{j}}
. Since
\lambda-\text{wt}(x)
is dominant, we conclude that
\lambda-\text{wt}(x)\leq\zeta_{j}
, i.e.

(8.3)

\text{wt}(x)\geq\sum\limits_{\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{j}}\beta_{i}^{\vee}.

Since
eq.

8.3
is valid for any
j=1,\cdots,n
, we get that

\text{wt}(x)\geq\bigvee\limits_{j=1}^{n}(\sum\limits_{\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{j}}\beta_{i}^{\vee}),

where
\bigvee
stands for the join operation. It is easy to see that that this join is equal to
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{\vee}
. Hence we are done.
∎

Remark 8.9

We now give some explicit examples of elements in irreducible Weyl groups of non-simply laced type, for which the conclusions of

and

fail.

(i) Let

and let

x=s_{2}s_{3}s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}s_{1}s_{2}

(a)

(ii) Now, let

Proof of Proposition 9.1.

We need to show that the properties (i)-(iii) in

8.2
are satisfied by
wt

s_{\alpha}xs_{\alpha}=s_{s_{\alpha}(\beta_{1})}\cdots s_{s_{\alpha}(\beta_{k})}

Finally, we argue that property (iii) is satisfied by

for any

s_{\alpha}x>x

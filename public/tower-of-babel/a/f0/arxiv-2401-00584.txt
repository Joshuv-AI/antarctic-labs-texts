Representing maps for semibounded forms and their Lebesgue type decompositions
S. Hassi, H.S.V. de Snoo

Abstract
. For a semibounded sesquilinear form 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} in a Hilbert space ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} there exists a representing map Q Q from ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} to another Hilbert space 𝔎 {\mathfrak{K}} , such that 𝔱 ⁡ [ φ , ψ ] − c ⁡ ( φ , ψ ) = ( Q ​ φ , Q ​ ψ ) {\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)\!=\!(Q\varphi,Q\psi) , φ , ψ ∈ dom ​ 𝔱 \varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}} , with c ∈ ℝ c\in{\mathbb{R}} a lower bound of 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} . Representing maps offer a simplifying tool to study general semibounded forms. By means of representing maps closedness, closability, and singularity of 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} are immediately translated into the corresponding properties of the operator Q Q , and vice versa. Also properties of sum decompositions 𝔱 = 𝔱 1 + 𝔱 2 {\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2} of a nonnegative form 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} with two other nonnegative forms 𝔱 1 {\mathfrak{t}}_{1} and 𝔱 2 {\mathfrak{t}}_{2} in ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} can be analyzed by means of associated nonnegative contractions K ∈ 𝐁 ⁡ ( 𝔎 ) K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}}) . This helps, for instance, to establish an explicit operator theoretic characterization for the summands 𝔱 1 {\mathfrak{t}}_{1} and 𝔱 2 {\mathfrak{t}}_{2} to be, or not to be, mutually singular. Such sum decompositions are used to study characteristic properties of the so-called Lebesgue type decompositions of semibounded forms 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} , where 𝔱 1 {\mathfrak{t}}_{1} is closable and 𝔱 2 {\mathfrak{t}}_{2} singular; in particular, this includes the Lebesgue decomposition of a semibounded form due to B. Simon. Furthermore, for a semibounded form 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} with its representing map Q Q it will be shown that the corresponding semibounded selfadjoint relation Q ∗ ​ Q ∗ ⁣ ∗ + c Q^{*}Q^{**}+c is uniquely determined by a limit version of the classical representation theorem for the form 𝔱 {\mathfrak{t}} , being studied by W. Arendt and T. ter Elst in a sectorial context. Via representing maps a full treatment is given of the convergence of monotone sequences of semibounded forms.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, the sesquilinear forms in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
and assume that
{\mathfrak{t}}
is semibounded.
Then with
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
a lower bound of
{\mathfrak{t}}
there exists a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
,
i.e., a linear operator
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
such that

(1.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)=(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q.

Here
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
stands for the space of all linear relations from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
Without loss of generality it may be assumed that
the representation in (
1.1
) is minimal in the sense that
{\rm ran\,}Q
is dense in
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
With such a representing map several notions for linear operators
are connected to similar notions for semibounded forms:
think of closability and singularity.
For instance, a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable
if and only if the representing map
Q
is closable and, in this case,
the closure
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}
of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by

\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)=(Q^{**}\varphi,Q^{**}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q,

where the operator
Q^{**}
is the closure of
Q
.
Likewise, a nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is singular
if and only if the representing map
Q
is singular.
A short overview of representing maps is given in Section
2
.
Recall that representing maps have
Lebesgue type decompositions based on
a decomposition of Hilbert spaces going back
to de Branges and Rovnyak;
general linear relations were decomposed along
these lines in
[
12
]
.
In the present paper the representing maps will be used
to decompose semibounded
forms in semibounded closable and nonnegative singular forms
and to offer a new interpretation of the
classical representation theorem for forms.
Furthermore, this interpretation will play
a role when considering the convergence of monotone sequences of forms.
The contents of the present paper will now be described.

The first topic is the Lebesgue decomposition of a semibounded form

{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
. In general a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
need not be closed
or closable, so that the interest is in decomposing
{\mathfrak{t}}
into a sum where the summands
are closable and singular, respectively. In particular, there exist forms

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg},{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(1.2)

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing},\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing},

where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is semibounded and closable
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}
is nonnegative and singular.
Moreover, this decomposition is uniquely determined since
the component
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is the maximal closable part of
{\mathfrak{t}}

and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}={\mathfrak{t}}-{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
.
This so-called Lebesgue decomposition goes back to Simon
[
21
]
.
Here the decomposition will be shown by means of the Lebesgue decomposition
of the representing map
Q
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in (
1.1
). The regular part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}

of a form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
will appear when discussing the representation
of general forms by means of nonnegative selfadjoint relations (see Section
6
)
or describing the limits of monotone sequences of forms (see Section
7
).

The second topic is a description of all Lebesgue type decompositions
of a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
as

(1.3)

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2},\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2},

where the form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded and closable
while the form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nonnegative and singular.
In
[
15
]
there is a treatment of singular forms with applications.
Note that the Lebesgue decomposition in (
1.2
) is an example
of a Lebesgue type decomposition.

The first step to study form sums (
1.3
) is taken in Section
4
, where the interest is
in sum decompositions (
1.3
) of a nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}

with nonnegative forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
The sum decompositions are characterized by means of nonnegative contractions
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is the Hilbert space associated with the representing map
Q
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The main results for the parametrization can be found in Theorem
4.1
,
while the question of minimality of representation for the sum
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
,
assuming minimality of representations of the summands
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
,
is dealt with in Theorem
4.3
.
It appears that the minimality of the parametric representation for the sum

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
is intimately connected with the interaction between the components
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
Following the measure theoretic analog for the mutual singularity of pairs of nonnegative measures,
the parallel sum
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
of nonnegative forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1},{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
is treated
and described by means of the contraction
K
in Proposition

This leads to a classification of form sums

in two main categories:
those for which
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}=0

and the opposite case
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\neq 0

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
interact with each other.
The Lebesgue decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}
is of the first category,

[
10

K
is an orthogonal projection.
The other case, where
K
is not an orthogonal projection means non-minimal

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
A deeper operator theoretic treatment to study these phenomena is presented in
[
12
]

The second step to study form sums (
1.3
) can be found in Section

) the form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}

In fact, the general case where

is semibounded will be presented,

Lebesgue type decompositions for a pair of nonnegative forms were introduced
in
[
10
]
; in the present paper the emphasis is on single forms.

The third topic is the representation of semibounded forms
via semibounded selfadjoint relations.
This is treated in Section
6
.
Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form
with the representation (
1.1
).
If
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closed then the well-known representation theorem
asserts that there is a semibounded selfadjoint relation
H
in
{\mathfrak{H}}

with
{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and having the same lower bound as
{\mathfrak{t}}
,
such that for all elements

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

In Theorem

is not necessarily closed, that there exists a unique semibounded selfadjoint relation

namely

{\rm dom\,}A\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

for each element
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in A

\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
for which

(1.4)

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi]\to(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

where
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
means

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}]\to 0
.
The connection with the representation for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
can be
found in Theorem

.
The approach via representing maps gives a simple proof of all these facts.
The representation (

) for a general semibounded form

coincides with the one of Arendt and ter Elst
[
3
]
.

2.
Representing maps for semibounded sesquilinear forms

This section is a summary of the facts concerning semibounded sesquilinear forms in
a Hilbert space. There is a special emphasis on the notion of a representing map for
a semibounded sesquilinear form.

A sesquilinear form
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}[\cdot,\cdot]
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
over
{\mathbb{C}}

with an inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
is a mapping from
{\mathfrak{D}}\times{\mathfrak{D}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
,
where
{\mathfrak{D}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
is a linear subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
which is linear in the first entry and antilinear in the second entry.
In what follows, sesquilinear forms over
{\mathbb{C}}
will be often called just forms,
and the class of forms
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
will be denoted by
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
Also the diagonal
{\mathfrak{t}}[\cdot]
will be used:

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi]={\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\varphi]
,
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is called symmetric
if
{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=\overline{{\mathfrak{t}}[\psi,\varphi]}
for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Equivalently,
{\mathfrak{t}}
is symmetric if and only if
{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi]\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1},{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be forms
with domains
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
Then the sum
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
, defined by

({\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2})[\varphi,\psi]={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi],\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\cap{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2},

belongs to
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
In particular,
{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]+c(\varphi,\psi)
,
with
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, defines a form, denoted by
{\mathfrak{t}}+c
.
The inequality
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
for forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1},{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is defined by

(2.1)

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1},\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}[\varphi]\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}[\varphi],\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}.

The inclusion
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
means that
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}

and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
agrees with
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
on
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
In this case
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
is called a restriction of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
,
while
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
is called an extension of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
In particular,
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
implies
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
.
All these elementary facts can be found in
[
6
,
14
,
15
]
.

A form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
with domain
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
is called bounded if

|{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi]|\leq a\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

for some
a\geq 0
. The form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

is called bounded from below by
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
if

(2.2)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi]\geq c\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

and nonnegative if
{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi]\geq 0
,
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
A semibounded form is automatically symmetric.
The sum of forms which are semibounded from below
is also semibounded from below.
The lower bound
m({\mathfrak{t}})\in{\mathbb{R}}
of
{\mathfrak{t}}

is the greatest of all numbers
c
in (
2.2
):

m({\mathfrak{t}})=\inf\left\{\,\frac{{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\varphi]}{(\varphi,\varphi)}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\,\right\}.

For
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
define the form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
as follows

({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi,\psi]={\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Then
{\mathfrak{t}}-c\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a nonnegative form
with
{\rm dom\,}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The kernel
{{\rm ker\,}\,}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)
of
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
is defined by

{{\rm ker\,}\,}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)=\{\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}:\,({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi,\varphi]=0\}.

By Cauchy’s inequality one sees that
{{\rm ker\,}\,}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)

is a linear subspace of
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.

A basic tool in the present paper is the following concept
of a representing map for forms;
for individual forms see
[
17
]
,
[
22
]
,
and for a pair of nonnegative forms see also
[
10
, (4.6)]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form
and let
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
A representing map for the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
is a linear operator

Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, with
{\rm dom\,}Q={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
,
such that

(2.3)

({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi,\psi]=(Q\varphi,Q\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q.

In what follows, it is important to observe that
for every semibounded form there exists a representing map.
For a proof of this result, see
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form.
Then for each
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
there exists a
representing map
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
for the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.
Moreover, this representing map is uniquely defined in the following sense:
if
Q^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
is an another representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c

with
{\rm dom\,}Q^{\prime}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
,
then there exists a partial isometry
V\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})

with initial space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q
and final space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q^{\prime}
,
such that
Q^{\prime}=VQ
.

Definition 2.3
.

The representing map
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

in Lemma
2.2
is called minimal when
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q={\mathfrak{K}}
.

If
Q
is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to
{\mathfrak{K}}
,
then by replacing
{\mathfrak{K}}
by
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q
one gets
a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
whose range is dense.
When the representing maps
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c

both are taken to be operators with dense ranges,
the partial isometry in Lemma
2.2
becomes a unitary operator.

At this point it should be stressed that representing maps
Q

in (
2.3
) depend on the choice of the bound
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
By indicating this in the notation
Q_{c}
one has, in particular,

{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}={{\rm ker\,}\,}({\mathfrak{t}}-c).

Thus, for instance,
{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}=\{0\}
for all
c<m({\mathfrak{t}})
,
while in general
{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}\neq\{0\}
when
c=m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
However, observe that, by definition,

{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}={\rm dom\,}Q_{m({\mathfrak{t}})}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},\quad c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}}).

Furthermore, it follows from (
2.3
) that

(2.4)

\inf\left\{\,\frac{\|Q_{c}\varphi\|^{2}}{\|\varphi\|^{2}}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\,\right\}=m({\mathfrak{t}})-c.

Of course, one could always take
c=m({\mathfrak{t}})
,
but the more general choice
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
allows
some useful flexibility.

The following notions and definitions are quite standard
for a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
; see, e.g.,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
.
A sequence
\varphi_{n}
in
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
is said
to converge to
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}
in the sense of
{\mathfrak{t}}
, denoted by

\varphi_{n}\to_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\varphi
, if

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}]\to 0,

where the convergence
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi

takes place in
{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form.
Then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is said to be

(a)

closable if for any sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
:

\hskip 28.45274pt\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}0\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}]\to 0.

(b)

closed if for any sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
:

\hskip 28.45274pt\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}-\varphi]\to 0.

Closability and closedness of a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}

are
shift invariant
, i.e.,
for all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
the forms
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}-a
are
simultaneously closable or closed, respectively.
The connection between these notions for forms and for the corresponding
linear representing maps is now easily established;
cf.
[
14
, Ch. VI, Examples 1.13, 1.23]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form
and let
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}

as in (
2.3
) with
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
Then the following statements hold:

(a)

{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable if and only if
Q_{c}
is closable;

(b)

{\mathfrak{t}}
is closed if and only if
Q_{c}
is closed.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
is equivalent to

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi

while
Q_{c}\varphi_{n}
converges in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
.
∎

Assume that the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

has a semibounded form extension
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, i.e.,
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}
.
Let
Q_{c}
and
Q_{c}^{\prime}
be the representing maps for
{\mathfrak{t}}

and
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}
for
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
If
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\prime}
is closed, then
Q_{c}^{\prime}
is closed by Lemma
2.5
,
which implies that
Q_{c}
is closable;
thus
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable by Lemma
2.5
.
Therefore, if the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
has a closed extension, then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable.
A converse to this statement is contained in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form.
If
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable, then
{\mathfrak{t}}
has a closure

\bar{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

(
the smallest closed extension
)
defined as follows:
the domain
{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by all
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}

for which there exists a sequence

\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
with
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
, and in this case

(2.5)

\bar{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi_{n}]\quad\mbox{for any}\quad\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi,\quad\psi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\psi.

The forms
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}
have the same lower bound.

In fact, if
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a closable representing map
for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
:

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c},

then
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by

(2.6)

\bar{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}^{**}\varphi,Q_{c}^{**}\psi)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**},

where
Q_{c}^{**}
denotes the closure of
Q_{c}
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable, also
Q_{c}
is closable by Lemma
2.5
, i.e.,

Q_{c}^{**}
is a closed operator.
Hence, the form
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}
defined by (
2.6
) is closed
and it clearly extends
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Since
Q_{c}^{**}
is the smallest closed linear
operator extension of
Q_{c}
,
it follows that
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}
is the smallest closed form-extension of
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
By construction one has
{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**}
,
so that
{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by all
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}

for which there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

with
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
and (
2.5
).
∎

If the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded and closable,
then for any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
also the form
{\mathfrak{t}}-a\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is
semibounded and closable. Moreover, in this case one has

\overline{{\mathfrak{t}}-a}=\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}-a,\quad a\in{\mathbb{R}};

cf. Lemma
2.6
.
It should be stressed that the closure process in Lemma
2.6

depends neither on the choices of the sequences

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
and
\psi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\psi

in (
2.14
), (
2.5
), nor on the choices of

c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and of
Q_{c}
in the representation (
2.3
).
The notion of a core is closely connected to Lemma
2.6
.
Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded and closed.
Then a
core for

{\mathfrak{t}}
is a subset
{\mathfrak{D}}
of
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
such that
the (form) closure of the restriction
{\mathfrak{t}}{\upharpoonright\,}{\mathfrak{D}}
is equal to
{\mathfrak{t}}
;
cf.
[
14
, p. 317]
.
Likewise, let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a closed linear operator.
Then a
core for

T
is a subset
{\mathfrak{D}}
of
{\rm dom\,}T
such that
the (graph) closure of
\{\{\varphi,T\varphi\}:\,\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}\}
is equal to
T
;
cf.
[
14
, p. 166]
.
Therefore, if the closed semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and the closed linear operator
Q_{c}
with
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
are connected by

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]-c(\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c},

and
{\mathfrak{D}}
is a subset of
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}
, then

{\mathfrak{D}}
is a core for
{\mathfrak{t}}
if and only if
{\mathfrak{D}}
is a core for
Q_{c}
;
cf. Lemma
2.6
.

The translation invariance of the closability of forms
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is quite natural.
The situation is different in the case of nonnegative singular forms.
For instance, it will follow from the definition below that
if
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nonnegative and singular,
then
{\mathfrak{t}}+c
is not singular for any
c>0
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be nonnegative.
Then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is said to be singular
if for every
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

there is a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
such that

(2.7)

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi,\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}]\to 0.

Clearly, an equivalent definition is that for every
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

there is a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
such that

(2.8)

\varphi_{n}\to 0,\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}-\varphi]\to 0.

For a discussion of singular nonnegative forms, see for instance
[
15
]
.
In
[
21
]
the singular part of a form was defined via the Lebesgue
decomposition as the difference between the form and its regular part;
see Section
3
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be nonnegative
and let
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}
, so that

(2.9)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q\varphi,Q\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q,

and, consequently,

(2.10)

m({\mathfrak{t}})=\inf\left\{\,\frac{\|Q\varphi\|^{2}}{\|\varphi\|^{2}}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q\,\right\}.

Then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is singular if and only if
Q
is singular, and in this case

m({\mathfrak{t}})=0
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{t}}
is nonnegative it follows that
m({\mathfrak{t}})\geq 0
.
Thus by taking
c=0
in (
2.3
) one sees that (
2.9
) holds
for some representing map
Q
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
Furthermore, now (
2.10
) is a consequence of (
2.4
).

According to the definition in (
2.7
) the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is singular
if and only if for every
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q

there is a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
such that

(2.11)

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi,\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}]=\|Q\varphi_{n}\|^{2}\to 0.

This condition means that
{\rm dom\,}Q\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q^{**}
,
i.e.,
Q
is a singular operator. Moreover, it follows from (
2.11
)
and (
2.10
) that
m({\mathfrak{t}})=0
. Conversely, if
Q
is singular, it follows
from (
2.9
) that the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is nonnegative and singular.
∎

It is clear that a null form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, i.e.,
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={{\rm ker\,}\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
,
is nonnegative and simultaneously, closable and singular.
Conversely, if
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a nonnegative form
which is both regular and singular, then it follows
from Lemma
2.8
that
{\mathfrak{t}}=0
.

Recall that for linear operators
Q_{1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{1})
and
Q_{2}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{2})

one says that
Q_{1}
is
contractively dominated
by
Q_{2}
,
in notation
Q_{1}\prec_{c}Q_{2}
, if

(2.12)

{\rm dom\,}Q_{2}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|Q_{1}\varphi\|\leq\|Q_{2}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{2},

see
[
11
, Definition 8.1, Lemma 8.2]
.
If for
i=1,2
one has

{\mathfrak{t}}_{i}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{i}\varphi,Q_{i}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{i}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}_{i}={\rm dom\,}Q_{i}

then clearly

(2.13)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q_{1}\prec_{c}Q_{2}.

This equivalence will be tacitly used elsewhere in the paper.
In particular, one sees that if
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
are closable, then

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{1}\leq\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{2},

as
Q_{1}\prec_{c}Q_{2}
implies that
(Q_{1})^{**}\prec_{c}(Q_{2})^{**}
;
cf.
[
11
]
,
[
13
, (2.4)]
.

Finally some remarks will be made concerning the definitions
of closable, closed, and singular forms in a Hilbert space.

Remark 2.9
.

Assume that the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded
and let
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a representing map,
with
c\leq\gamma
.
If
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
and
\psi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\psi
,
then
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
and
\psi_{n}\to\psi
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
,
while
Q_{c}\varphi_{n}
and
Q_{c}\psi_{n}
converge
in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
to, say,
h
and
k
in
{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{**}
.
This implies that

(2.14)

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi_{n}]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(h,k).

Assume that
{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}
is not trivial.
Let
\omega\in{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}
with
\omega\neq 0
.
Then there is a sequence
\chi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}

such that
\chi_{n}\to 0
and
Q_{c}\chi_{n}\to\omega
.
Therefore
\varphi_{n}+a\chi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi

for all
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}+a\chi_{n},\psi_{n}]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(h,k)+a(\omega,k).

Thus it follows that for any
\omega\in{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}
which is not orthogonal
to
k
the limit in (
2.14
) can take every possible value in
{\mathbb{C}}
, as can be seen from

{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{**}=({\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{**}\ominus{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**})\oplus{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}.

Consequently, the limit in (
2.14
) depends on the choice of the sequences

\varphi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
.
Hence, in general, there does not exist a well defined extension for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

Finally, notice that the alternative characterization of singularity in (
2.8
), i.e.
for every
\chi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
there is a sequence
\chi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
, such that

\chi_{n}\to 0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\chi_{n}-\chi]\to 0,

is equivalent to saying that
{\rm ran\,}Q\subset{\rm mul\,}Q^{**}
.

3.
Lebesgue decompositions of semibounded forms

It will be shown that for a semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
there exists a sum decomposition

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
,
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
, where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is closable
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is singular. Moreover, it will be shown that this decomposition is unique
by the property that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
is the largest of all closable forms below
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
This so-called Lebesgue decomposition and its uniqueness go back to Simon
[
21
]
;
the present proofs are based on the Lebesgue decomposition of the representing map of
the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded,
let
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
, and assume that
{\mathfrak{t}}
has the representation

(3.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q,

where
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.
The closure of
Q
is a closed linear relation
Q^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
and
let
P_{0}
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}Q^{**}
.
Then the sum decomposition

(3.2)

Q=Q_{\rm reg}+Q_{\rm sing},\quad Q_{\rm reg}=(I-P_{0})Q,\quad Q_{\rm sing}=P_{0}Q,\\

where

{\rm dom\,}Q_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}Q_{\rm sing}={\rm dom\,}Q
,
is the so-called Lebesgue decomposition of the representing
Q
; cf.
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.
Here
Q_{\rm reg}
is a closable operator
and
Q_{\rm sing}
is a singular operator.
Recall that
Q^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
has a similar decomposition

Q^{**}=(Q^{**})_{\rm reg}+(Q^{**})_{\rm sing},\quad(Q^{**})_{\rm reg}=(I-P_{0})Q^{**},\quad(Q^{**})_{\rm sing}=P_{0}Q^{**},\\

where

{\rm dom\,}(Q^{**})_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}(Q^{**})_{\rm sing}={\rm dom\,}Q^{**}
.
Here
(Q^{**})_{\rm reg}
is the usual orthogonal operator part
of the closed linear relation
Q^{**}
.
It is important to observe that

(Q_{\rm reg})^{**}=(Q^{**})_{\rm reg},\quad{\rm dom\,}(Q_{\rm reg})^{**}={\rm dom\,}Q^{**}.

For more details, see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded.
Then
{\mathfrak{t}}
has the sum decomposition

(3.3)

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing},\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing},

with a closable semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

and a singular nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
In fact, let
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and assume
that
{\mathfrak{t}}
has the representation (
3.1
).
Then

(3.4)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{{\rm reg}}\varphi,Q_{{\rm reg}}\psi),\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{{\rm sing}}\varphi,Q_{{\rm sing}}\psi),

with
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q
.
Moreover, the closure
\overline{{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{\rm reg}}

of the closable part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is given by

(3.5)

\overline{{{\mathfrak{t}}}_{\rm reg}}\,[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+((Q_{{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi,(Q_{{\rm reg}})^{**}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\overline{{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}}={\rm dom\,}Q^{**}.

Proof.

Since
P_{0}
in (
3.2
) is an orthogonal projection, it is clear from (
3.4
)
that
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}
; cf. (
3.1
).
Since
Q_{\rm reg}
is a closable operator and
Q_{\rm sing}
is a singular operator
it also follows from (
3.4
) that the forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}

are regular and singular, respectively; cf. Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.8
.
Finally, (
3.5
) is a direct consequence of the definition of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
;
cf. Lemma
2.6
.
∎

The decomposition of the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in (
3.3
)
into the regular part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
and the singular part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}

in (
3.4
) is called the
Lebesgue decomposition
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
It will be shown in the following theorem that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
in
(
3.4
) does not depend on the choice of
c
and
Q
in (
3.1
),
and neither does
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}={\mathfrak{t}}-{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded.
Then
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
as defined in Theorem
3.1

satisfies
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form and
assume that
{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
, then

(3.6)

{\mathfrak{u}}_{\rm reg}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}.

In particular, if the form
{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded
and
{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
, then

{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}.

Consequently,
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is the largest
of all closable semibounded forms below
{\mathfrak{t}}
.

Proof.

By assumption
{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a semibounded form with
{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
, i.e.,

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{u}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{u}}[\varphi]\leq{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi],\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Let the lower bound of
{\mathfrak{u}}
be
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Thanks to the inequality
{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
, it follows that
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
By Lemma
2.2
the form
{\mathfrak{u}}
(having lower bound
c
)
has a representation of the form

{\mathfrak{u}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(R_{c}\varphi,R_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{u}}={\rm dom\,}R_{c},

with a representing map
R_{c}
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime}
.
Since
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
, it follows from the same lemma that the form
{\mathfrak{t}}

(with lower bound
\gamma
) has a representation of the form

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c},

where
Q_{c}
is a representing map from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
.
As in Theorem
3.1
one sees that

{\mathfrak{u}}_{\rm reg}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(R_{c,{\rm reg}}\varphi,R_{c,{\rm reg}}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}R_{c},

and, likewise, it follows from Theorem
3.1
that

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c,{\rm reg}}\varphi,Q_{c,{\rm reg}}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}.

Using the representations of
{\mathfrak{u}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}
one sees that the inequality
{\mathfrak{u}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}

is equivalent to

{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}\subset{\rm dom\,}R_{c}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|R_{c}\varphi\|\leq\|Q_{c}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}.

As a consequence from
[
11
, Theorem 8.3]
, one finds that

(3.7)

R_{c,{\rm reg}}\prec Q_{c,{\rm reg}},

see (
2.12
).
Now, using the above representations of
{\mathfrak{u}}_{\rm reg}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
,
it follows that the inequality (
3.6
) holds; cf. (
2.13
).
Since
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is closable, it is clear that

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
has the stated maximality property.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The following useful observation goes back to
[
21
]
.
Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded,
let the form
{\mathfrak{b}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be symmetric
with
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{b}}
, and assume that for some
\alpha\geq 0

|{\mathfrak{b}}[\varphi]|\leq\alpha\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Then the sum
{\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

is semibounded (with
{\rm dom\,}({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
).
Moreover, if
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable, then
{\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}}
is closable,
cf.
[
14
, p. 320]
and
[
6
, Theorem 5.1.16]
,
and in this case

(3.8)

({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm reg}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{b}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm sing}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}.

To see this, note that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
implies

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{b}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}}
,
and, since the left-hand side is closable, one obtains

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}+{\mathfrak{b}}\leq({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm reg}
by Theorem
3.2
.
Likewise
({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm reg}-{\mathfrak{b}}
is closable
and
({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm reg}-{\mathfrak{b}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}
, so that

({\mathfrak{t}}+{\mathfrak{b}})_{\rm reg}-{\mathfrak{b}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
by Theorem
3.2
.
Combining these inequalities gives the first identity in (
3.8
)
and the second identity in (
3.8
) is clear from the first one.

It follows from (
3.2
) that

{\rm ran\,}Q_{\rm reg}\subset{\rm ran\,}(I-P_{0})
and
{\rm ran\,}Q_{\rm sing}\subset{\rm ran\,}P_{0}
.
If the representing map
Q
is minimal:
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q={\mathfrak{K}}
, then

{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{\rm reg}={\rm ran\,}(I-P_{0})
and
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{\rm sing}={\rm ran\,}P_{0}
.
Clearly, the regular and singular components in the Lebesgue decomposition
of
Q
have only a trivial intersection of the ranges.
The Lebesgue decomposition is an example of a more general decomposition
of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
is closable and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
is singular.
In fact, by considering orthogonal Lebesgue type decompositions of the
representing map
Q
one may easily construct such decompositions in the same way
as the Lebesgue decomposition. Moreover, also in these cases the ranges
of the components of
Q
have only a trivial intersection; cf. Corollary
5.4
.
One then says that the corresponding forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}

are mutually singular.
However not every decomposition
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
is closable and

{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
is singular, can be obtained via an orthogonal Lebesgue type decomposition of the
representing map
Q
.
The required class of decompositions is much wider and
only in this more general situation one meets components
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}

that are not mutually singular; cf. Theorem
5.2
.
In order to characterize all such decompositions it is helpful to first
investigate the sum decompositions of nonnegative forms into nonnegative forms
in Section
4
. These will then be used to study Lebesgue type decompositions
of semibounded forms in Section
5
.

4.
Sum decompositions of nonnegative forms

In this section the interest will be in the sum decomposition
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}

of a nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
with nonnegative forms

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

with the additional property that
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.
The parametrization of such decompositions will be given by means of the
representing map of the sum.
The minimality of the representation of such a sum will be characterized.
Furthermore, the interaction of the components
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}

in the sum decomposition will be described in terms of
the parallel sum of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.

First a characterization will be presented of all possible sum decompositions
of a nonnegative form. The construction in Theorem
4.1
is based on the fact that
associated with a sum decomposition of nonnegative forms are natural inequalities
that arise from this decomposition; cf.
[
17
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a nonnegative form
and assume that
{\mathfrak{h}}
has the representation

(4.1)

{\mathfrak{h}}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q,

where
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a representing map.

Let
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
be a nonnegative contraction
and define the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q
by

(4.2)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=((I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi),\quad{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi).

Then the form
{\mathfrak{h}}
has the sum decomposition

(4.3)

{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2},\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2},

where
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
are nonnegative.

Conversely, let the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in (
4.1
)
have a sum decomposition (
4.3
),
where
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
are nonnegative.
Then there exists a nonnegative contraction
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})

such that (
4.2
) holds.

Proof.

Assume that
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
is a nonnegative contraction
and let
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be defined by (
4.2
).
Due to the identity

((I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}})^{*}(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}+(K^{{\frac{1}{2}}})^{*}K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}=K+(I-K)=I_{\mathfrak{K}},

it is clear from (
4.2
)
that
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}={\mathfrak{h}}
. Hence,
K
generates a sum decomposition
of the form
{\mathfrak{h}}
in (
4.1
).

Conversely, let the form
{\mathfrak{h}}
have the decomposition
as in (
4.3
).
According to Lemma
2.2
there exist representing maps

Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to Hilbert spaces

{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
, respectively, with domains equal to
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
,
such that

(4.4)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}},\quad{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}},

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
.
It follows from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) that

(4.5)

\begin{split}(Q\varphi,Q\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}}&={\mathfrak{h}}[\varphi,\psi]={\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]\\
&=(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}}+(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}},\end{split}

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
.
The identity (
4.5
) shows that there are natural
inequalities: for all
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
one has

(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\varphi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}}\leq\big(Q\varphi,Q\varphi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}},\quad(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\varphi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}}\leq\big(Q\varphi,Q\varphi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}}.

Hence, the linear relations
C_{1}
from
{\mathfrak{K}}
to
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}

and
C_{2}
from
{\mathfrak{K}}
to
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
, defined by

C_{1}=\big\{\{Q\varphi,Q_{1}\varphi\}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}\big\},\quad C_{2}=\big\{\{Q\varphi,Q_{2}\varphi\}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}\big\},

are actually contractive operators from
{\rm ran\,}Q
onto
{\rm ran\,}Q_{1}

and
{\rm ran\,}Q_{2}
, respectively. Thus they can be uniquely extended to
all of
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q
and these extensions are denoted again by
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
,
respectively. Finally extend these operators trivially to all of
{\mathfrak{K}}

and give these extensions the same notation.
Now define the operators
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
by

K_{1}=C_{1}^{*}C_{1}^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad K_{2}=C_{2}^{*}C_{2}^{**}.

Then
K_{1},K_{2}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
are nonnegative contractions and they satisfy

(4.6)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=\big(C_{1}Q\varphi,C_{1}Q\psi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}}=\big(K_{1}Q\varphi,Q\psi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}}

and

(4.7)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=\big(C_{2}Q\varphi,C_{2}Q\psi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}}=\big(K_{2}Q\varphi,Q\psi\big)_{{\mathfrak{K}}}

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
.
By combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) with (
4.5
),
it follows that

K_{1}+K_{2}=P_{{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q},

where the right-hand side stands for the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q
.
Now let
K=K_{2}
, then the second identity in (
4.2
) follows from (
4.7
).
Note that

I_{\mathfrak{K}}-K=P_{{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q}-K_{2}+(I-P_{{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q})=K_{1}+(I-P_{{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q}),

which leads to the identity

((I_{\mathfrak{K}}-K)Q\varphi,Q\psi)=(K_{1}Q\varphi,Q\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}.

Thus the first identity in (
4.2
) follows from (
4.6
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

If the representing map
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
in (
4.1
) is minimal, then the contraction
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
in (
4.2
) is unique
(see for a similar feature
[
12
, Remark 3.7]
).
To see this directly, assume that
K
and
{{\widetilde{K}}}
are nonnegative contractions
in
{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
which satisfy (
4.2
).
Then it follows that ,

{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi)=({{\widetilde{K}}}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,{{\widetilde{K}}}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}.

Hence
((K-{{\widetilde{K}}})Q\varphi,Q\psi)=0
for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}
.
By the assumption
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q={\mathfrak{K}}
one concludes that
K-{{\widetilde{K}}}=0
.

Let
Q_{1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{1})
and
Q_{2}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{2})
be linear operators;
here
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
are Hilbert spaces.
The corresponding
column operator

{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})

from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to the orthogonal sum
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
is defined by

{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{1}\\
Q_{2}\end{pmatrix}:{\rm dom\,}Q_{1}\cap{\rm dom\,}Q_{2}\to{\mathfrak{K}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\\
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The column operator is used to describe the representing map for a sum
of nonnegative forms. Let the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

be nonnegative with
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.
For
i=1,2
let
Q_{i}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{i})
be the representing maps for the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}

with
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{1}={\rm dom\,}Q_{2}
, such that

(4.8)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\psi),\quad{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\psi),

where
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}={\rm dom\,}Q_{1}={\rm dom\,}Q_{2}
.
Then
{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
is a representing map for the form
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
,
as follows with
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
from

(4.9)

\begin{split}({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2})[\varphi,\psi]&=(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\psi+(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\psi)\\
&=({\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})\varphi,{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})\psi).\end{split}

If in (
4.8
) both
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are minimal
in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
, respectively,
it does not necessarily follow that
{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})
is minimal
in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
.
A characterization of minimality in the context of Theorem
4.1

will be given in Theorem
4.3
.
For this purpose let
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
be a nonnegative contraction and
define the Hilbert spaces
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
by

{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}(I-K)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}K,

each provided with the inner product of
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
They satisfy
{\mathfrak{K}}={\mathfrak{K}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
with overlapping space

{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}(I-K)K
; for a treatment of the corresponding
de Branges-Rovnyak decompositions, see
[
12
]
.
With the representations in (
4.2
)
one defines
Q_{1}=(I-K)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q
and
Q_{2}=K^{\frac{1}{2}}Q
. Then
{\rm col\,}(Q_{1},Q_{2})

is a representing map in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\times{\mathfrak{K}}_{2})
,
so that for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q

(4.10)

{\mathfrak{h}}[\varphi,\psi]=\left(\begin{pmatrix}(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\\
K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\end{pmatrix}Q\varphi,\begin{pmatrix}(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\\
K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\end{pmatrix}Q\psi\right).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be nonnegative
with the representation (
4.1
),
where
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a minimal representing map, i.e.,
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q={\mathfrak{K}}
.
Let
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
be a nonnegative contraction
and assume that with respect to
K
the form
{\mathfrak{h}}
has the representation (
4.10
).
Then
(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q
is an operator from
{\mathfrak{H}}
into
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}
with dense range
and
K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q
is an operator from
{\mathfrak{H}}
into
{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
with dense range.
Moreover, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

the representation (
4.10
) is minimal;

(ii)

K
is an orthogonal projection.

Proof.

The representation (
4.10
) for the form
{\mathfrak{h}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

follows from (
4.2
) and (
4.9
).
By assumption
Q
has dense range in
{\mathfrak{K}}
, which implies that
(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q

has dense range in
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\mathfrak{K}}_{1}

and similarly
K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q
has dense range in
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
.

Recall from
[
12
]
that
W={\rm col\,}((I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}},K^{{\frac{1}{2}}})

is an isometry from
{\mathfrak{K}}
into the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}
.
Moreover, the isometry
W
is surjective
if and only if
K
is an orthogonal projection; cf.
[
12
, Proposition 2.8]
.
Finally observe that due to
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q={\mathfrak{K}}
the mapping
W
is surjective
if and only if the representing map
{\rm col\,}((I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q,K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q)
is minimal.
∎

Next the interaction between the components in a sum decomposition
will be considered. This interaction is measured in terms
of parallel sums.
To explain this, assume that
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
are nonnegative
with
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.
The
parallel sum
of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
is a nonnegative form
defined for
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
by

(4.11)

({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2})[\varphi]=\inf\left\{{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[h+\varphi]+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[h]:\,h\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}\right\},

and polarization.
The definition in (
4.11
) and a proof that it is actually
a nonnegative form can be found in
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
.
Likewise, for nonnegative operators
A,B\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
one defines
the
parallel sum

A:B\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
for
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{K}}
by

(4.12)

((A:B)\varphi,\varphi)=\inf\left\{(A(h+\varphi),h+\varphi)+(Bh,h):\,h\in{\mathfrak{K}}\right\},

and polarization, cf.
[
8
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1},{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be nonnegative with sum
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.
Let
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a minimal representing map for the sum
{\mathfrak{h}}
:

{\mathfrak{h}}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}.

Let
K\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}})
be the nonnegative contraction for which:

{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=((I-K)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q\varphi,(I-K)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q\psi),\quad{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(K^{\frac{1}{2}}Q\varphi,K^{\frac{1}{2}}Q\psi),

where
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q
.
Then the parallel sum
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
has the representation

(4.13)

({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2})[\varphi,\psi]=\left(((I-K):K)Q\varphi,Q\psi\right),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{h}}={\rm dom\,}Q.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q
and consider
the quadratic form defined by (
4.11
).
It follows from the definition in (
4.11
)
and the representations (
4.2
) in Theorem
4.1
that

(4.14)

\begin{array}[]{rl}({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2})[\varphi]&=\inf\big\{\|(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q(h+\varphi)\|^{2}+\|K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Qh\|^{2}:\,h\in{\rm dom\,}Q\big\}\\
&\geq\inf\big\{\|(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(Q\varphi-g)\|^{2}+\|K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}g\|^{2}:\,g\in{\mathfrak{K}}\big\}\\
&=\inf\left\{\left((I-K)f,f\right)+\left(Kg,g\right):\,f+g=Q\varphi:\,f,g\in{\mathfrak{K}}\right\}\\
&=\left(((I-K):K)Q\varphi,Q\varphi\right).\end{array}

The last equality follows from the definition in (
4.12
).

The reverse inequality in (
4.14
) follows from
the denseness of
{\rm ran\,}Q
in
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
To see this let
\varepsilon>0
and select
g\in{\mathfrak{K}}
such that

\|(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(Q\varphi-g)\|^{2}+\|K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}g\|^{2}<\left(((I-K):K)Q\varphi,Q\varphi\right)+\varepsilon.

Since
{\rm ran\,}Q
is dense in
{\mathfrak{K}}
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|g-Qh_{n}\|=0
for some sequence
h_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}Q
.
It follows that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
h\in{\rm dom\,}Q
such that

\|(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(Q(\varphi-h)\|^{2}+\|K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Qh\|^{2}<\left(((I-K):K)Q\varphi,Q\varphi\right)+2\varepsilon.

Taking the infimum over all
h\in{\rm dom\,}Q
this leads to

\begin{split}&\inf\big\{\|(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q(\varphi-h)\|^{2}+\|K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Qh\|^{2}:\,h\in{\rm dom\,}Q\big\}\\
&\hskip 184.9429pt\leq\left(((I-K):K)Q\varphi,Q\varphi\right).\end{split}

Therefore, equality prevails in (
4.14
)
and this implies the statement by the polarization formula
for forms.
∎

Thus the representing map for the form
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
is given by

((I-K):K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q
, which involves the parallel sum of the operators

I-K
and
K
.
This result is a special case of the functional calculus developed in
[
17
]
.
The parallel sum
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
in (
4.13
) measures the
interaction of the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.
The forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
are called
mutually singular

if
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}:{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}=0
; cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.10]
, in which case
there is no interaction
between the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
4.4

the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
are mutually singular;

(ii)

K
is an orthogonal projection.

Proof.

It follows from (
4.14
) that
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
are mutually singular
if and only if
((I-K):K)^{\frac{1}{2}}Q=0
.
Since the representing map
Q
in Proposition
4.4

is assumed to be minimal,
this is equivalent to
((I-K):K)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0
. Observe that by definition

(I-K):K=K-K((I-K)+K)^{-1}K=(I-K)K,

cf.
[
8
]
, from which the assertion follows.
∎

Now return to the context of Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
.
The sum decompositions (
4.3
) of a form
{\mathfrak{h}}

can be classified into two different categories:
the forms
{\mathfrak{h}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{2}
are mutually singular
(precisely when
K
is an orthogonal projection)
or they are not mutually singular (precisely when
K
is not an orthogonal projection);
see the remark following Theorem 2.5 in
[
21
]
.

5.
Lebesgue type decompositions of semibounded forms

The Lebesgue decomposition of a semibounded form

{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in Section
3

provides a sum decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}
into a closable part

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
and a singular part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}
.
Recall that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is the largest of all closable
semibounded forms below
{\mathfrak{t}}
. By means of the
sum decompositions in Section
4
it is now possible
to describe the general situation.

Based on the Lebesgue decomposition in Section
3

the following definition is quite natural.

Definition 5.1
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded.
Then a sum decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}
, given by

(5.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2},\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2},

is called a Lebesgue type sum decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}

if
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded and closable,
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nonnegative and singular.

The following characterization of all Lebesgue type decompositions
of a semibounded form is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
,
Lemma
2.5
, and Lemma
2.8
,
by a reduction to nonnegative forms.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded,
let
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
, and assume that
{\mathfrak{t}}
has the representation

(5.2)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q,

where
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.

Let
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
be a nonnegative contraction which satisfies
the conditions

(5.3)

{\rm clos\,}\{k\in{\rm\overline{ran}\,}(I-K):\,(I-K)^{\frac{1}{2}}k\in{\rm dom\,}Q^{*}\}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}(I-K),

(5.4)

{\rm ran\,}K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\cap{\rm dom\,}Q^{*}\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q^{*},

and define the forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
by

(5.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+((I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,(I-K)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi),\\
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=(K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\varphi,K^{{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\psi).\end{array}

Then the sum
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
in (
5.1
)
is a Lebesgue type decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1
.

Conversely, let the sum
{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
in (
5.1
)
be a Lebesgue type decomposition of
{\mathfrak{t}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1
.
Then there exists a nonnegative contraction
K\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})

such that (
5.3
), (
5.4
),
and (
5.5
) are satisfied.

Proof.

According to (
5.2
) the form
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{t}}-c\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nonnegative
with representing map
Q
. Assume

6.
Representation theorems for semibounded forms

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form.
If
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closed then the well-known representation theorem (see
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
)
asserts that there is a semibounded selfadjoint relation
H
in
{\mathfrak{H}}

with the same lower bound as
{\mathfrak{t}}
, such that for all elements
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

In this section it will be shown that for an arbitrary semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

a similar observation can be made when the above identity is
interpreted in a limiting sense.
Moreover, the regular part
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
is represented by the same semibounded selfadjoint
relation. This version of the representation of semibounded forms goes back
to Arendt and ter Elst (in the setting of sectorial forms); see

[
3
, Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.10]
. The representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

allows a simple and direct exposition of the arguments.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded.
Then the following statements hold:

(A)

There exists a semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

which is bounded from below by
m({\mathfrak{t}})
, such that

{\rm dom\,}S_{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S_{\mathfrak{t}}

(6.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

If
H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is any symmetric relation, such that

{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H

the statement (
6.1
) holds,
then
H\subset S_{\mathfrak{t}}
.

(B)

There exists a semibounded selfadjoint extension
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}

which is bounded from below by
m({\mathfrak{t}})
,
such that for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}

there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
for which

(6.2)

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi]\to(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

If
H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is any selfadjoint relation, such that
for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H

there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
for which
the statement (
6.2
) holds, then
H={{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
.

(C)

For all
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
the symmetric relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
and
the selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
admit the representations

(6.3)

S_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c\quad\mbox{and}\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c,

where
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.
In particular, the relations
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}

do not depend
on the choice of
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and the representing map
Q_{c}
.

Proof.

(A)
Fix
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and let
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

be some representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
:

(6.4)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi)+c(\varphi,\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Define
S_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c
. Then clearly
S_{c}\geq c
and
{\rm dom\,}S_{c}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Now let
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}
.
Then
\{Q_{c}\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in Q_{c}^{*}
and
if
\{\psi,Q_{c}\psi\}\in Q_{c}
, then

(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi)=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)
. From (
6.4
) one gets

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)+c(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi^{\prime}+c\varphi,\psi).

Therefore,
S_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c
satisfies (
6.1
).

Next let
H
with
{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
satisfy (
6.1
).
If
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H
and
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
then using (
6.4
) one obtains

(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi)+c(\varphi,\psi)\;\Leftrightarrow\;(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi).

Since this holds for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}
, one concludes that

\{Q_{c}\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}.

Therefore,
H-c\subset Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}

\Leftrightarrow

H\subset S_{c}
.

(B)
Define
A_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c
and let
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in A_{c}
,
so that
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}
.
Then

\{\varphi,g\}\in Q_{c}^{**}
and
\{g,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}

for some
g\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
.
Hence there exists a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

such that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
{\mathfrak{H}}

and
Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\to g
in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
.
Then
(Q_{c}\varphi_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence and

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}]=\|Q_{c}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\|_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}^{2}+c\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}}^{2}\to 0,\quad n,m\to\infty.

Thus,
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
.
On the other hand, for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
one has

(6.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi_{n},\psi]=\lim_{n\to\infty}(Q_{c}\varphi_{n},Q_{c}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}=(g,Q_{c}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}.

It follows from
\{g,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}
and
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}
that

(6.6)

(g,Q_{c}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}.

By combining (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), one obtains for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}}+c\lim_{n\to\infty}(\varphi_{n},\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}.

Thus
A_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c
satisfies (
6.2
).

Next, let
H=H^{*}
be such that for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H

there exist
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
satisfying (
6.2
).
Then
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi
implies that

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|Q_{c}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\|_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}\to 0.

Thus
(Q_{c}\varphi_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
and

Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\to g
for some
g\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
.
Hence,

\{\varphi,g\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\{\varphi_{n},Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\}\in Q_{c}^{**}
.
Now from the second condition in (
6.2
)
one gets for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

\lim_{n\to\infty}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi_{n},\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}}-c\lim_{n\to\infty}(\varphi_{n},\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}.

Thus for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}
,

(g,Q_{c}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(Q_{c}\varphi_{n},Q_{c}\psi)_{{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi_{n},\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}.

Hence
\{g,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}
and, since
\{\varphi,g\}\in Q_{c}^{**}
,
one has

\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}\in Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}.

Thus
H-c\subset Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}

or, equivalently,
H\subset A_{c}
. Since both relations are selfadjoint,
this gives
H=A_{c}
.

(C)
The proofs of (A) and (B) show that
S_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c
and
A_{c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c

satisfy the desired conditions with a choice of
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})

and a representing map
Q_{c}
for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.

On the other hand, if
Q_{c}^{\prime}=VQ_{c}
with a unitary map
V
from
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}
onto
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}
, then

Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c=(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{*}Q_{c}^{\prime}+c;\quad Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c=(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{*}(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{**}+c.

Thus,
S_{c}
and
A_{c}
do not depend on the choice of
Q_{c}
.

Finally, if
c^{\prime}\leq\gamma
and
Q_{c^{\prime}}

is some representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c^{\prime}
, then the proof of (A) shows that

S_{c^{\prime}}=Q_{c^{\prime}}^{*}Q_{c^{\prime}}+c^{\prime}

also satisfies the conditions in (A) and, thus,
S_{c^{\prime}}\subset S_{c}
.
By symmetry, one also has
S_{c}\subset S_{c^{\prime}}
. Therefore,
S_{c^{\prime}}=S_{c}
.
This proves that
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
in (
6.3
) does not depend on
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
In particular,
S_{\mathfrak{t}}=S_{c}\geq c
for each
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
,
so that
S_{\mathfrak{t}}\geq m({\mathfrak{t}})
. A similar reasoning applies to
A_{c}
:

{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c
for all
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and thus

{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}\geq m({\mathfrak{t}})
.

This completes the proof.
∎

According to Theorem

any semibounded form

generates a semibounded (symmetric) relation

and a semibounded selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
in

.
Moreover, it follows from (

) that they satisfy the following identities:

(6.7)

{\rm mul\,}S_{\mathfrak{t}}=({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp},\quad{\rm mul\,}{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp},

The semibounded relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}

in Theorem

is selfadjoint with
{\rm\overline{dom}\,}{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
;
its orthogonal operator part
({{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}

is given by
({{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}=(I-P_{\rm reg}){{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
,
where
P_{\rm reg}
is the orthogonal projection
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Therefore, in (

(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)

by
(({{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)

so that (

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{t}}\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi_{n},\psi]\to(({{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

where
({{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}
is a semibounded selfadjoint operator
in
{\mathfrak{H}}\ominus{\rm mul\,}{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
In general, the inclusion

S_{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}

for instance, this is the case when the form

is defined by a semibounded

When the form

Corollary 6.2
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded and closed.
Then there exists a semibounded
selfadjoint relation
A_{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
with lower bound
m({\mathfrak{t}})
,
such that
{\rm dom\,}A_{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in A_{\mathfrak{t}}

(6.8)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

If
H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a selfadjoint relation, such that

{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
and for each
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H

the statement (
6.8
) holds,
then
H=A_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Moreover, for all
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
the selfadjoint relation
A_{\mathfrak{t}}
is given by

(6.9)

A_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c,

where the representing map
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
is closed.

Note that it follows from (
6.7
) or (
6.9
) that the following identities

{\rm mul\,}A_{\mathfrak{t}}=({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm\overline{dom}\,}A_{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

hold.
Recall that the identity (
6.8
) can be written
for each
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}A_{\mathfrak{t}}
as

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=((A_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

where the orthogonal operator part
(A_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm reg}

is a semibounded selfadjoint operator in
{\mathfrak{H}}\ominus{\rm mul\,}A_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
It is now simple to see that
any semibounded selfadjoint relation in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is connected
to a closed semibounded form; cf.
[
6
]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a selfadjoint relation with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
. Then the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, defined by

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}(A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=((A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi,(A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi)+c(\varphi,\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{A},

is closed, independent of
c\leq\gamma
, and has lower bound
m({\mathfrak{t}})=\gamma
.
Moreover, the unique semibounded selfadjoint relation in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
associated with
{\mathfrak{t}}
is
A
.

Proof.

Note that the mapping, defined by

\varphi\mapsto(A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}(A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}},

takes
{\rm dom\,}(A_{\rm reg}-c)^{\frac{1}{2}}
into the Hilbert space

{\mathfrak{H}}\ominus{\rm mul\,}A
with dense range.
When
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}A
, it follows that

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(A_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)
.
∎

In Corollary
6.2
it has been shown that for every closed
semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

there is a unique semibounded selfadjoint relation
H=A_{\mathfrak{t}}

in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
such that (
6.8
) holds.
Furthermore, according to Lemma
6.3
,
for each semibounded selfadjoint relation
H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

there is a closed semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

such that
H=A_{\mathfrak{t}}
holds. In the following, the notation

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is used to denote this one-to-one correspondence.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded forms
with representations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{1}\varphi,Q_{1}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}={\rm dom\,}Q_{1},\\
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{2}\varphi,Q_{2}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}={\rm dom\,}Q_{2},\end{array}\right.

with representing maps
Q_{1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{1})
and
Q_{2}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{2})
.
Recall that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
if and only if
Q_{1}\prec_{c}Q_{2}
; see (
2.1
), (
2.12
), and (
2.13
).
Now assume, in addition, that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}
are closed forms or, equivalently, that
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are
closed operators, so that the corresponding semibounded selfadjoint relations
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

are given by

H_{1}=Q_{1}^{*}Q_{1}+c,\quad H_{2}=Q_{2}^{*}Q_{2}+c.

Recall from
[
6
, Definition 5.2.8]
that two nonnegative relations

H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
satisfy
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
when

{\rm dom\,}H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset{\rm dom\,}H_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(H_{1,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|\leq\|(H_{2,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|,\,\,f\in{\rm dom\,}H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

According to (
2.1
) and
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
one has the equivalent statements

{\mathfrak{t}}_{1}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q_{1}\prec_{c}Q_{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad H_{1}\leq H_{2}.

In particular, one has for semibounded selfadjoint relations

H
and
K
in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
that
H\leq K
is equivalent to

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)\leq{\mathfrak{t}}(K)
(here the above notation has been used).

When the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in Theorem
6.1

is closable the results simplify in the sense that the description in (B)
of that theorem can be stated directly.
The result is straightforward; the main tool is Lemma
2.6

in conjunction with the representations (
6.3
) in Theorem
6.1
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded and closable.
Let
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the closure of
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Then the semibounded selfadjoint relation
A_{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

is an extension of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}

(
introduced in Theorem
6.1
)

and it has the same lower bound
m({\mathfrak{t}})
.
For all
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
the semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
and
the selfadjoint relation
A_{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}
admit the representations

S_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}+c\quad\mbox{and}\quad A_{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}+c,

where
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
.
In particular,
A_{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}
coincides
with the selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
in Theorem
6.1
.

Now return to the general context of Theorem
6.1
,
where
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
Fix
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and let
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be some representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
,
so that

(6.10)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

In the general situation of Theorem
6.1

the representing map
Q_{c}
need not be closable.
However, the product
Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}
makes sense and is
a nonnegative selfadjoint relation in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
To proceed one turns to the Lebesgue decomposition of
Q_{c}
:

Q_{c}=Q_{c,\rm reg}+Q_{c,\rm sing}.

The closable component
Q_{c,\rm reg}

defines a closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
which in the following will be denoted by

{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}.

It is clear that the closable form
{\mathfrak{r}}
and its closure
{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}
have the representations

(6.11)

{\mathfrak{r}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c,\rm reg}\varphi,Q_{c,\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{r}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

and

(6.12)

{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+((Q_{c,\rm reg})^{**}\varphi,(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{**}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{r}}.

Observe that one may also apply Theorem
6.1
to the closable form
{\mathfrak{r}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
; it turns out
that the semibounded selfadjoint relations generated by
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{r}}
coincide.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let the form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded,
let
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
, and let
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
have a representing map
Q_{c}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
such that (
6.10
) holds.
Let
{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
so that (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) are satisfied.
Then the relations
S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}
and
{{{\widetilde{A}}}}_{\mathfrak{r}}

(
corresponding to
S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}
and
{{{\widetilde{A}}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}

introduced for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
in Theorem
6.1
)

are given by

(6.13)

S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}Q_{c,\rm reg}+c\quad\mbox{and}\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{**}+c,

and they satisfy

(6.14)

S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}.

In particular, the relations
S_{\mathfrak{r}}
and
A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}

in (
6.13
) and (
6.14
) do not depend
on the choice of
c\leq m({\mathfrak{t}})
and the representing map
Q_{c}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}
in (
6.14
) is equivalent to

Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}\subset(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}Q_{c,\rm reg}
and the equality

{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}
is equivalent to
Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}=(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{**}
.
This last inclusion and equality are both easily established by means of the formula

Q_{c,\rm reg}=(I-P)Q_{c}
, where
P
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}=({\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{*})^{\perp}
;
for details, see
[
13
, Appendix]
.
The equality
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}
holds by Corollary
6.4
.
The remaining statements follow from Theorem
6.1
.

7.
Monotone sequences of semibounded forms

Nondecreasing sequences of semibounded forms
have a limit and, likewise, nonincreasing sequences
of semibounded forms with a common lower bound have a limit;
see for instance
[
14
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
21
]
.
In this section the convergence of monotone sequences
of semibounded forms will be considered in connection with
the convergence of the corresponding representing maps
as in Section
6
.

First some general facts are established.
Let
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of semibounded forms
whose lower bounds are uniformly bounded:

\gamma\leq m(t_{n})\quad\mbox{for some}\quad\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},

and let
c\leq\gamma
. Then
there exists a sequence of representing maps

Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
such that

(7.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}[\varphi,\psi]=c+(Q_{n}\varphi,Q_{n}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}={\rm dom\,}Q_{n}.

Conversely, each sequence of linear operators

Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})

defines via (
7.1
) a sequence
of semibounded forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

such that
m({\mathfrak{t}}_{n})\geq c
.
Now assume that the sequence of
semibounded forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
satisfies

(7.2)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{n},\quad m\leq n.

In this case one can take
\gamma=m({\mathfrak{t}}_{1})
and the
representing maps
Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
in (
7.1
)
satisfy

(7.3)

Q_{m}\prec_{c}Q_{n},\quad m\leq n.

Conversely, if
Q_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
satisfies (
7.3
) then the
semibounded forms
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in (
7.1
) satisfy (
7.2
).
It is clear from (
7.1
) that

\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm dom\,}Q_{n},

and, moreover, for an element
\varphi
in this set one has

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}[\varphi]<\infty\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|Q_{n}\varphi\|<\infty.

Recall that if (
7.3
) is satisfied, then there exists a linear operator

Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, which satisfies

(7.4)

Q_{n}\prec_{c}Q\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|Q_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|Q\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q,

where
{\rm dom\,}Q
is given by

{\rm dom\,}Q=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}Q_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|Q_{n}\varphi]\|<\infty\right\},

see
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
.
The linear operator
Q\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

serves as a representing map for the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
defined by

(7.5)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c+(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q.

Hence the following lemma, going back to Simon (see
[
21
]
and
[
18
]
)
is now straightforward.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of semibounded forms,
represented as in (
7.1
). Assume that the sequence
satisfies

(7.6)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{m}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{n},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a unique semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, represented in (
7.5
),
such that

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}[\varphi]<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}[\varphi]\nearrow{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi],\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}.

Furthermore, let
\,{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded form. Then there is the implication

{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\leq{\mathfrak{u}},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\mathfrak{t}}\leq{\mathfrak{u}}.

The following statements hold:

(a)

if
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}
is closable for all

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable;

(b)

if
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}
is closed for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
then
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closed.

If
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}
is a bounded everywhere defined form
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\mathfrak{t}}

is a bounded everywhere defined form.

In the limit procedure of Lemma
7.1
the properties of
being closable and closed are preserved, respectively. This observation
will be used in the following discussion of the regular parts of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}

and
{\mathfrak{t}}
in
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
It follows from (
7.1
) and (
7.5
) that
{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}={\mathfrak{t}}_{n,{\rm reg}}

and
{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{{\rm reg}}
have the representations

(7.7)

{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi,\psi]=c+(Q_{n,\rm reg}\varphi,Q_{n,\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}={\rm dom\,}Q_{n},

and

(7.8)

{\mathfrak{r}}[\varphi,\psi]=c+(Q_{\rm reg}\varphi,Q_{\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{r}}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q.

The assumption
Q_{m}\prec_{c}Q_{n}
in (
7.3
) implies that

(7.9)

Q_{m,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}Q_{n,{\rm reg}},\quad m\leq n,

and, likewise, the inequality
Q_{n}\prec_{c}Q
in (
7.4
) gives

(7.10)

Q_{n,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}Q_{\rm reg};

cf. (
3.7
).
In particular,
Q_{\rm reg}
is an upper bound for
Q_{n,{\rm reg}}
.
By (
7.9
) and Lemma
7.1

it follows from the closability of the operators
Q_{n,{\rm reg}}
that there exists
a closable operator
Q_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
such that its domain
is given by

(7.11)

{\rm dom\,}Q_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}Q_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|Q_{n,{\rm reg}}\varphi]\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(7.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Q_{n,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}Q_{\rm r}\prec Q_{\rm reg},\\
\|Q_{n,{\rm reg}}\varphi\|\nearrow\|Q_{\rm r}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{\rm r}.\end{array}\right.

The closable operator
Q_{\rm r}
serves as a representing map
for the closable semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
defined by

(7.13)

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{\rm r}\varphi,Q_{\rm r}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{\rm r}.

Since the regular parts
Q_{n,{\rm reg}}
and
Q_{\rm r}
are closable,
the semibounded forms
{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}
are closable, and one
obtains from (
7.7
) that

(7.14)

\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi,\psi]=c+((Q_{n,\rm reg})^{**}\varphi,(Q_{n,\rm reg})^{**}\psi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}={\rm dom\,}(Q_{n,\rm reg})^{**}

and from (
7.13
) that

(7.15)

({\rm clos\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\rm r}})\,[\varphi,\psi]=c+((Q_{\rm r})^{**}\varphi,(Q_{\rm r})^{**}\psi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}({\rm clos\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r})={\rm dom\,}(Q_{\rm r})^{**}
.
It follows from the inequalities (
7.9
) that

(7.16)

(Q_{m,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**},\quad m\leq n,

and from (
7.10
) that

(7.17)

(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}(Q_{{\rm r}})^{**}.

By (
7.16
) and Lemma
7.1

it follows that there exists a closed operator
S_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime\prime})

such that its domain is given by

(7.18)

{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi]\|<\infty\right\},

and which satisfies

(7.19)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}S_{\rm r}\prec_{c}(Q_{\rm reg})^{**},\\
\|(Q_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|\nearrow\|S_{\rm r}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}.\end{array}\right.

The closed operator
S_{\rm r}
serves
as a representing map for the closed semibounded form

{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
defined by

(7.20)

{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}[\varphi,\psi]=c+(S_{\rm r}\varphi,S_{\rm r}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}={\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}.

It is clear from the above that
S_{\rm r}\prec(Q_{\rm r})^{**}
.

The above facts together with Lemma
7.1
now lead to the following theorem,
that ultimately describes the limit behaviour of the semibounded selfadjoint
relations corresponding to
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}
as described in Theorems
6.1
,
6.5
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of semibounded forms,
represented in (
7.1
), which satisfies (
7.6
).
Let the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
represented in (
7.5
), be the limit of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(A)

The regular parts
{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})

), satisfy

(7.21)

{\mathfrak{r}}_{m}\leq{\mathfrak{r}}_{n},\quad m\leq n,\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\rm reg}},

where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
is represented in (

).

{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
represented in (

(7.22)

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi]<\infty\right\},

and which satisfies

(7.23)

{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi]\nearrow{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}[\varphi],\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}.

(B)

The closures
\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
of the regular parts

,
represented in (

), satisfy

(7.24)

\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{m}\leq\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\quad m\leq n,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\leq{\rm clos\,}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r},

where
{\rm clos\,}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}
is represented in (
7.15
).
Moreover, there exists a closed semibounded form
{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{F}}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
represented in (

),
such that

(7.25)

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi]<\infty\right\},

and which satisfies

(7.26)

\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}\leq{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}\leq{\rm clos\,}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\mathfrak{r}}_{n}[\varphi]\nearrow{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}[\varphi],\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}.

({\rm clos\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r})\,[\varphi,\psi]={\mathfrak{s}}_{\rm r}[\varphi,\psi],\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}({\rm clos\,}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm r}).

{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}_{n}}\to A_{\mathfrak{s}}

Numerical convergence of discrete extensions in a space-time finite element, fictitious domain method for the Navier–Stokes equations
Mathias Anselmann ⋆,1, Markus Bause ⋆ ⋆ Helmut Schmidt University, Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, Holstenhofweg 85, 22043 Hamburg, Germany

Abstract
A key ingredient of our fictitious domain, higher order space-time cut finite element (CutFEM) approach for solving the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on evolving domains (cf. [ 1 ] ) is the extension of the physical solution from the time-dependent flow domain Ω f t \Omega_{f}^{t} to the entire, time-independent computational domain Ω \Omega . The extension is defined implicitly and, simultaneously, aims at stabilizing the discrete solution in the case of unavoidable irregular small cuts. Here, the convergence properties of the scheme are studied numerically for variations of the combined extension and stabilization.

Mathematical problem and numerical scheme

For an evolving flow domain
\Omega_{f}^{t}
, with
t\in[0,T]
, and
I:=(0,T]
we consider the incompressible Navier–Stokes system (cf. Fig.
1
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{f}\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=0\hskip 12.91663pt\text{in }\Omega_{f}^{t}\times I\,,

that is equipped with the initial condition
\boldsymbol{v}(0)=\boldsymbol{v}_{0}
in
\Omega_{f}^{0}
and the Dirichlet boundary condition
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{g}
on
\Gamma_{f}^{t}\times I
for the time-dependent boundary
\Gamma_{f}^{t}
of the flow domain
\Omega_{f}^{t}
.
For flow problems with inflow and outflow boundaries we refer to cf.
[
1
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{K\}
be a family of regular decompositions of the computational domain
\Omega=\Omega_{f}^{t}\cup\overline{\Omega_{r}^{t}}
. By
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
we denote an inf-sup stable pair of time-independent bulk finite element spaces on
\Omega
for the velocity and pressure.

Figure 1
:
Prototype flow domain
\Omega_{f}^{t}
with enclosed rigid body
\Omega_{r}^{t}
, computational background domain
\Omega
.

For
\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
, with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})
,
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h},\xi_{h})
, we define
A_{h}:(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\times(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
L_{h}:(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle A_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\begin{aligned} &\langle(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla})\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}+\nu\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}-\langle p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}+\langle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\xi\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}\\
&-\langle\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}-p_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}+B_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})+S_{F_{h}^{t}}(´\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})\,,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle L_{h}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h};\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g})

\displaystyle:=\langle\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}+B_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})\,.

The bilinearform
B_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}:\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{f}^{t})\times(\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
enforces Dirichlet boundary conditions weakly by Nitsche’s method,

B_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}):=-\langle\boldsymbol{w},\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}+\xi_{h}\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}^{t}}+\gamma_{1}\nu\langle h^{-1}\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}+\gamma_{2}\langle h^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\rangle_{\Gamma_{f}^{t}}\,,

for
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{f}^{t})
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
. Here,

\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
are numerical (tuning) parameters for the penalization. The linear form
S_{F_{h}^{t}}
is introduced and analyzed for the Stokes problem in
[
3
]
. Its impact is twofold. On the one hand it extends the solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
from the fluid domain
\Omega_{f}^{t}
to the rigid domain
\Omega_{r}^{t}
such that the solution is defined on the whole computational domain
\Omega=\Omega_{f}^{t}\cup\overline{\Omega_{r}^{t}}
. On the other hand,
S_{F_{h}^{t}}
stabilizes the solution in the case of small cut cell scenarios. For the definition of
S_{F_{h}^{t}}
, we define a stabilization zone
\Omega_{s}^{t}\subset\Omega
such that the enclosure
\Omega_{r}^{t}\subset\Omega_{s}^{t}
is satisfied, and a corresponding submesh
\mathcal{T}_{h,s}^{t}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}
of all cells that cover
\Omega_{s}^{t}
completely. In Sec. 2 we will study numerically the effect of extending the stabilization into the fluid domain, i.e. of widening the domain
\Omega_{s}^{t}
.
The set of all faces that are common to two cells of
\mathcal{T}_{h,s}^{t}
is denoted by
F_{h}^{t}
.
With numerical parameters
\gamma_{v}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{v}({\nu}^{-1}+\nu)h^{-2}>0
and
\gamma_{p}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{p}{\nu}^{-1}>0
, the form
S_{F_{h}^{t}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\small S_{F_{h}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})=\sum_{F\in F_{h}^{t}}\gamma_{v}\langle\mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h|K_{1}}-\mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{v}_{h|K_{2}},\mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h|K_{1}}-\mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h|K_{2}}\rangle_{\omega_{F}}+\gamma_{p}\langle\mathcal{E}p_{|K_{1}}-\mathcal{E}p_{|K_{2}},\mathcal{E}\xi_{|K_{1}}-\mathcal{E}\xi_{|K_{2}}\rangle_{\omega_{F}}\,.

Here,
\mathcal{E}
denotes the canonical patchwise extension of the discrete functions. We refere to
[
3
,
1
]
for its definition.

Putting
A_{h}^{s}\coloneqq A_{h}+S_{F_{h}^{t}}
and applying a discontinuous Galerkin time discretization with piecewise polynomials of order
k
(cf.
[
1
]
) leads to finding, in each subintervall
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n})
, functions
(\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};\boldsymbol{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};Q_{h})
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau,h},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\tau,h}\rangle_{\Omega_{f}^{t}}+A_{h}^{s}(\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau,h},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\tau,h})\mathrm{d}t+\langle[\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau,h}]_{t_{n-1}},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}^{+})\rangle_{\Omega}=\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}L_{h}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\tau,h};\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g})\mathrm{d}t

for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I;\boldsymbol{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};Q_{h})
, with
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\tau,h}=(\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\tau,h},\xi_{\tau,h})
.

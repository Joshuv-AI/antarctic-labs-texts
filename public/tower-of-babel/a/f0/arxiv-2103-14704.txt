Steinberg slices and group-valued moment maps
Ana Bălibanu

Abstract
. We define a class of transversal slices in spaces which are quasi-Poisson for the action of a complex semisimple group G G . This is a multiplicative analogue of Whittaker reduction. One example is the multiplicative universal centralizer ℨ \mathfrak{Z} of G G , which is equipped with the usual symplectic structure in this way. We construct a smooth relative compactification ℨ ¯ \overline{\mathfrak{Z}} by taking the closure of each centralizer fiber in the wonderful compactification of G G . By realizing this relative compactification as a transversal in a larger quasi-Poisson variety, we show that it is smooth and log-symplectic.



Introduction

Let
G
be a simply-connected, complex semisimple group, and let
G_{\ad}
be its adjoint form. The group
G_{\ad}
acts on
G
by conjugation, and
G
contains a transversal slice
\Sigma
for this action which was introduced by Steinberg
[
Ste
]
. The resulting (multiplicative) universal centralizer is the smooth affine variety

\mathfrak{Z}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(a,h)\in G_{\ad}\times\Sigma\mid a\in G_{\ad}^{h}\right\}.

This family of centralizers first appeared in work of Lusztig
[
Lus
, Section 8.6]
. When
G
is simply-laced, Bezrukavnikov, Finkelberg, and Mirkovic
[
BFM
]
showed that its coordinate ring is isomorphic to the equivariant
K
-theory of the affine Grassmannian of the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
—therefore, in this case
\mathfrak{Z}
is an example of a Coulomb branch as defined by Nakajima
[
Nak
]
.

The natural symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{Z}
is inherited from the nondegenerate quasi-Poisson structure on the double
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G_{\ad}\times G
as described, up to a finite central quotient, by Finkelberg and Tsymbaliuk
[
FiTs
]
. We construct a relative compactification

\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(a,h)\in\overline{G_{\ad}}\times\Sigma\mid a\in\overline{G_{\ad}^{h}}\right\}

of
\mathfrak{Z}
, by taking the closure of each centralizer fiber inside the wonderful compactification
\overline{G_{\ad}}
. We show that
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
is smooth, and that the symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{Z}
extends to a log-symplectic Poisson structure on
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
. When
G
is simply-laced, the relative compactification
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
can be obtained from the Coulomb branch definition given in
[
BFM
]
through a Rees construction which comes from the Vinberg monoid. Therefore, in this case
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
is an example of a Rees algebra approach to compactified Couloumb branches suggested by Braverman, Finkelberg, and Nakajima
[
BFN
, Remark 3.7]
.

The additive setting

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
and fix a regular
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
. The regular nilpotent element
e
is contained in a unique Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
, and we write
\mathfrak{u}
for its nilpotent radical and
U
for the corresponding subgroup of
G
.

Suppose that
M
is a Poisson manifold with a Hamiltonian action of
G
and, identifying the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with its dual via the Killing form isomorphism, let
\nu\colon M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the associated moment map. The
Whittaker reduction
of
M
is the Poisson manifold

\nu^{-1}(f+\mathfrak{b})/U

obtained by Hamiltonian reduction with respect to the action of
U
at a point corresponding to a regular character in
\mathfrak{u}^{*}
.

On the other hand, one can consider the principal slice of regular elements

\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f+\mathfrak{g}^{e}\subset\mathfrak{g},

which was defined by Kostant
[
Kos
]
and which is contained in the space
f+\mathfrak{b}
. Because
\mathcal{S}
meets every regular adjoint orbit exactly once and transversally, it is a Poisson transversal for the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. This implies that the preimage
\nu^{-1}(\mathcal{S})
is a Poisson transversal in
M
, and in this way inherits a natural Poisson structure.

These two constructions are related through the Kostant Cross-section Theorem
[
Kos
, Theorem 8]
, which states that
\mathcal{S}
is a slice for the free action of
U
on
f+\mathfrak{b}
. The induced isomorphism of Poisson manifolds

\nu^{-1}(\mathcal{S})\cong\nu^{-1}(f+\mathfrak{b})/U,

shows that classical Whittaker reduction coincides with the preimage of the Kostant section
\mathcal{S}
under the moment map.

This approach is used in
[
Bal
]
to construct a canonical relative compactification of the universal centralizer

\mathcal{Z}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(a,x)\in G_{\ad}\times\mathcal{S}\mid a\in G_{\ad}^{x}\right\}.

This is a symplectic variety obtained by Whittaker reduction relative to the
G\times G
-action on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G_{\ad}
. The canonical symplectic structure on
T^{*}G_{\ad}
extends to a log-symplectic structure on the logarithmic cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}
. It is shown in
[
Bal
]
that the universal centralizer
\mathcal{Z}
has a smooth, log-symplectic relative compactification

\overline{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(a,x)\in\overline{G_{\ad}}\times\mathcal{S}\mid a\in\overline{G_{\ad}^{x}}\right\},

which is the Whittaker reduction of
T^{\ast}_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}
. In view of the discussion above, there is a commutative diagram of moment maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}G_{\ad}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{\ast}_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\nu}}

The varieties
\mathcal{Z}
and
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}
are simply the preimages of the principal slice
\mathcal{S}\times(-\mathcal{S})
under
\nu
and
\overline{\nu}
, and their Poisson structures are therefore also obtained via restriction in this way.

Summary of results

We give a multiplicative analogue of these results by considering manifolds which are quasi-Poisson relative to the action of
G
. These can be viewed as deformations of ordinary Poisson structures in which the Jacobi identity is twisted by a canonical trivector field induced by the group action. They were introduced in a series of papers by Alekseev, Malkin, and Meinrenken
[
AMM
]
, Alekseev and Kosmann-Schwarzbach
[
AKS
]
, and Alekseev, Kosmann-Schwarzbach, and Meinreken
[
AKSM
]
. These manifolds come equipped with group-valued momentum maps, and they are foliated by nondegenerate leaves.

The geometry of quasi-Poisson structures makes the multiplicative setting more subtle than the additive case described above. In this setting there is no naïve analogue of Whittaker reduction, because one cannot generally perform quasi-Hamiltonian reduction with respect to the action of a subgroup. This is because a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold is generally not quasi-Poisson for the action of a subgroup of
G
. To fix this issue, we introduce a multiplicative counterpart of Whittaker reduction which uses transversal slices. Kostant’s principal slice
\mathcal{S}
is replaced by the Steinberg cross-section
\Sigma
of
G
, and we prove the following theorem, as Corollary
2.5
:

Theorem
.

Let
M
be a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold with group-valued moment map
\Phi\colon M\longrightarrow G
. The Steinberg slice

M_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Phi^{-1}(\Sigma)

is a smooth submanifold of
M
. The quasi-Poisson structure on
M
pulls back to a Poisson structure on
M_{\Sigma}
whose symplectic leaves are the intersections of
M_{\Sigma}
with the nondegenerate leaves of
M
.

The natural Poisson structure on
M_{\Sigma}
is “transverse” to the quasi-Poisson structure on
M
, in the sense that it intersects every nondegenerate leaf transversally and symplectically. We use this approach to construct multiplicative analogues of several Whittaker-type algebraic varieties. We also define the notion of
log-nondegenerate
quasi-Poisson structures, which are a multiplicative analogue of log-symplectic structures. In Proposition
2.27
we show that the log-nondegeneracy condition behaves well with respect to Steinberg slices:

Proposition
.

If
M
is a log-nondegenerate quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold, the Poisson structure induced on the Steinberg slice
M_{\Sigma}
is log-symplectic.

We then apply this framework to the multiplicative universal centralizer
\mathfrak{Z}
. This variety sits as a Steinberg slice in the double
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}=G_{\ad}\times G
, which is the quasi-Poisson analogue of the cotangent bundle of
G
. In fact, using the identification
T^{*}G_{\ad}\cong G_{\ad}\times\mathfrak{g}
, the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G_{\ad}
is a bundle of Lie algebras over
G_{\ad}
and
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
is the simply-connected group scheme which integrates it. In Proposition
4.3
we extend the group scheme
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
to a group scheme over
\overline{G_{\ad}}
, which we call the
logarithmic double
.

Proposition
.

The double
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
extends to a group scheme
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
over
\overline{G_{\ad}}
which integrates the bundle of Lie algebras
T^{\ast}_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}
. The nondegenerate quasi-Poisson structure on
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
extends to a log-nondegenerate quasi-Poisson structure on
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
.

This produces a commutative diagram of group-valued moment maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times G.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mu}}

We write
\iota\colon G\longrightarrow G
for the inversion map, and we prove the following theorem, as Theorem
5.4
:

Theorem
.

There is an isomorphism of varieties

\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}\cong\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma\times\iota(\Sigma))

between the compactified universal centralizer
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
and the Steinberg slice
\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma\times\iota(\Sigma))
of the logarithmic double
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
. Therefore
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
is a smooth, log-symplectic Poisson variety whose open dense symplectic leaf is
\mathfrak{Z}
.

Outline

In Section
1
we review quasi-Poisson manifolds as developed in
[
AKSM
]
. We also outline how they fit into the framework of twisted Dirac structures, as described in
[
BuCr1
]
and
[
BuCr2
]
. In Section
2
we develop a theory of Steinberg slices in quasi-Poisson manifolds. We give several examples of these slices, including a multiplicative analogue of the twisted cotangent bundle of the base affine space. Then we define the notion of log-nondegeneracy for quasi-Poisson manifolds, and we show that Steinberg slices in log-nondegenerate quasi-Poisson manifolds are log-symplectic.

In Section
3
we recall the multiplicative universal centralizer
\mathfrak{Z}
, which is a Steinberg slice in the double
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
, and we review some facts about the geometry of the wonderful compactification
\overline{G_{\ad}}
. In Section
4
we use the Vinberg monoid to construct the smooth group scheme

\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}

\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}

5
we realize the relative compactification
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
as a Steinberg slice in
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
, equipping it with a log-symplectic Poisson structure. We give an explicit description of its stratification by symplectic leaves.

1.
Quasi-Hamiltonian and quasi-Poisson structures

We recall the basics of quasi-Hamiltonian and quasi-Poisson manifolds below, and we refer to
[
AKSM
]
for more details. We then explain how to view quasi-Poisson manifolds as twisted Dirac manifolds, following
[
BuCr1
]
and
[
BuCr2
]
. We will use this formalism in Section
2
.

1.1.
Quasi-Poisson manifolds

Let
G
be a simply-connected, semisimple complex group, let
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra, and write
(\cdot,\cdot)
for the Killing form. Under the isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
induced by this form, the
Cartan
3
-tensor

\varphi\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}
is the dual of the invariant trilinear function
\eta\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by

\eta(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{12}(x,[y,z])\qquad\text{for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
which is orthonormal relative to the Killing form. Then

\varphi=\frac{1}{12}C_{ijk}e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}

where
C_{ijk}=(e_{i},[e_{j},e_{k}])
are the structure constants. Here and throughout the paper we adopt the convention of summing over repeated indices.

If
G
acts on a complex manifold
M
, we write
\xi_{M}
for the polyvector field induced by the infinitesimal action of an element
\xi\in\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, the Cartan
3
-tensor
\varphi
generates a trivector field
\varphi_{M}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{3}TM)
. A
quasi-Poisson
structure on the manifold
M
is a
G
-invariant section
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
such that

(1.1)

[\pi,\pi]=\varphi_{M},

where the bracket on the left is the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket. In the special case where
G
is abelian, the Cartan
3
-tensor is trivial, and a quasi-Poisson structure on
M
is simply a
G
-invariant Poisson structure.

Example 1.2
.

[
AKSM
, Section 3]

The group
G
, equipped with the conjugation action, has a natural quasi-Poisson bivector

\pi_{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}e_{i}^{R}\wedge e_{i}^{L}.

Here
e_{i}^{L}
and
e_{i}^{R}
are the invariant vector fields on
G
corresponding to left- and right-multiplication. The bivector
\pi_{G}
is tangent to the conjugacy classes, and it induces a quasi-Poisson structure on each one.

If
(M_{1},\pi_{1})
and
(M_{2},\pi_{2})
are quasi-Poisson
G
-manifolds, a
G
-equivariant map
f\colon M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
is called
quasi-Poisson
if the bivectors
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are
f
-related. A quasi-Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
is
Hamiltonian
if it has a
G
-equivariant group-valued moment map

\Phi\colon M\longrightarrow G

which satisfies a differential equation analogous to the usual moment map condition
[
AKSM
, Definition 2.2]
. In particular,
\Phi
is a quasi-Poisson map when
G
is equipped with the bivector
\pi_{G}
. In what follows all quasi-Poisson manifolds will be Hamiltonian, so we will suppress this adjective.

Example 1.3
.

[
AKSM
, Example 5.3]
Consider the
internal fusion double

D(G)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G\times G
. The group
G\times G
acts on
D(G)
by

(g,h)\cdot(u,v)=\left(guh^{-1},hvg^{-1}\right)

for
(g,h)\in G\times G
and
(u,v)\in D(G)
. Let
\{e_{i}^{1},e_{i}^{2}\}
be the induced orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. The manifold
D(G)
has a quasi-Poisson structure

\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{i}^{1L}\wedge e_{i}^{2R}+e_{i}^{1R}\wedge e_{i}^{2L}\right).

The associated moment map is

\displaystyle D(G)\longrightarrow G\times G

\displaystyle(u,v)\longmapsto(uv,u^{-1}v^{-1}).

In the subsequent sections we will often use the reparametrization of
D(G)
given by setting
a=u
and
b=vu
. This is analogous to the left-trivialization of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G
. In these coordinates the
G\times G
-action is

(1.4)

(g,h)\cdot(a,b)=\left(gah^{-1},hbh^{-1}\right).

At the point
(a,b)
the quasi-Poisson bivector becomes

\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{i}^{1L}\wedge e_{i}^{2R}+e_{i}^{2L}\wedge e_{i}^{2R}+e_{i}^{1R}\wedge(\Ad_{a^{-1}}e_{i})^{2L}\right).

Using the fact that
\Ad_{a^{-1}}
is an orthogonal transformation relative to the Killing form and summing once again over repeated indices, the last term simplifies to

e_{i}^{1R}\wedge(\Ad_{a^{-1}}e_{i})^{2L}=(\Ad_{a^{-1}}e_{i})^{1L}\wedge(\Ad_{a^{-1}}e_{i})^{2L}=e_{i}^{1L}\wedge e_{i}^{2L}.

Therefore the quasi-Poisson structure in these coordinates is

(1.5)

\displaystyle\pi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{i}^{1L}\wedge\left(e_{i}^{2L}+e_{i}^{2R}\right)+e_{i}^{2L}\wedge e_{i}^{2R}\right),

and the associated moment map is

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mu\colon

\displaystyle D(G)\longrightarrow G\times G

\displaystyle(a,b)\longmapsto(aba^{-1},b^{-1}).

Quasi-Poisson structures are not compatible with restriction to the action of a subgroup—that is, a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold is not in general quasi-Poisson for the action of a subgroup of
G
. An exception to this is the case of diagonal subgroups, for which there is a procedure called
internal fusion

[
AKSM
, Section 5]
which we now describe.

Suppose that
(M,\pi)
is a quasi-Poisson
G\times G\times H
-manifold with group-valued moment map

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G\times G\times H

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\Phi_{1}(m),\Phi_{2}(m),\Psi(m)).

Define a
2
-tensor

(1.7)

\psi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}e_{i}^{1}\wedge e_{i}^{2}\in\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}),

and consider the modified bivector

\pi_{\text{fus}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi+\psi_{M}.

Let
\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}
denote the pointwise product of the components of
\Phi
. Then the triple

(M,\pi_{\text{fus}},(\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2},\Psi))

is a quasi-Poisson
G\times H
-manifold relative to the diagonal action of
G
.

Fusion equips the category of quasi-Poisson
G
-manifolds with a monoidal structure. Given two quasi-Poisson
G
-manifolds
(M_{1},\pi_{1},\Phi_{1})
and
(M_{2},\pi_{2},\Phi_{2})
, their direct product
M_{1}\times M_{2}
is naturally a quasi-Poisson manifold for the action of
G\times G
. Fusing the two sides of the
G
-action, we obtain a new quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold denoted

M_{1}\circledast M_{2},

with bivector
(\pi_{1}+\pi_{2})_{\text{fus}}
and moment map
\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}.

1.2.
Nondegenerate quasi-Poisson structures

Let
(M,\pi,\Phi)
be a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold. The bivector
\pi
induces a morphism of vector bundles

\displaystyle\pi^{\#}\colon T^{*}M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow TM

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi(\alpha,-)

from the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
to the tangent bundle
TM
. The action of
G
differentiates to an infinitesimal action map

\rho\colon M\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow TM.

The quasi-Poisson manifold
M
is called
nondegenerate
if the bundle map

(1.8)

\displaystyle\pi^{\#}\oplus\rho\colon T^{*}M\oplus\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow TM

\displaystyle(\alpha,\xi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi^{\#}(\alpha)+\rho(\xi)

is surjective. For example, the double
D(G)
defined in Example
1.3
is nondegenerate.

Let
\theta^{L}
and
\theta^{R}
be the left- and right-invariant Maurer–Cartan forms on
G
. These are
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-forms defined as follows: if
L_{h},R_{h}
are the differentials of left- and right-multiplication by the element
h\in G
, then for any
v\in T_{h}G

\theta^{L}_{h}(v)=L_{h^{-1}}v\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta^{R}_{h}(v)=R_{h^{-1}}v.

The bi-invariant
3
-form on
G
induced by
\eta\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which by abusing notation we denote by the same symbol, is

(1.9)

\eta=\frac{1}{12}\left(\theta^{L},[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]\right)=\frac{1}{12}\left(\theta^{R},[\theta^{R},\theta^{R}]\right)\in\Gamma(\wedge^{3}T^{*}G).

Every nondegenerate quasi-Poisson manifold
(M,\pi,\Phi)
carries a (possibly degenerate, non-closed)
2
-form
\omega
which satifies the following properties:

\displaystyle\text{(Q1)}\quad d\omega=-\Phi^{*}\eta;

\displaystyle\text{(Q2)}\quad\iota_{\xi_{M}}\omega=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{*}(\theta^{L}+\theta^{R},\xi)\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathfrak{g};

\displaystyle\text{(Q3)}\quad\ker\omega_{m}=\left\{\xi_{M}(m)\mid\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ such that }\Ad_{\Phi(m)}\xi=-\xi\right\}.

This
2
-form gives
M
the structure of a
quasi-Hamiltonian
G
-space
in the sense of
[
AMM
]
. We write
\theta_{i}^{L},\theta_{i}^{R}\in\Gamma(T^{*}G)
for the components of
\theta^{L}
and
\theta^{R}
in the basis
\{e_{i}\}
. At every point these
1
-forms are a dual basis to the left- and right-invariant vector fields, so that

\theta_{i}^{L}(e_{j}^{L})=\theta_{i}^{R}(e_{j}^{R})=\delta_{ij}.

Define
C\colon TM\longrightarrow TM
to be the morphism of vector bundles

(1.10)

C\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{Id}-\frac{1}{4}\Phi^{*}(\theta_{i}^{L}-\theta_{i}^{R})\otimes e_{iM}.

Then
\omega
and
\pi
satisfy the compatibility condition

(1.11)

\pi^{\#}\circ\omega^{\flat}=C,

where
\omega^{\flat}\colon TM\longrightarrow T^{*}M
is the vector bundle map given by contraction with
\omega
.

Example 1.12
.

The quasi-Hamiltonian
2
-form corresponding to the nondegenerate quasi-Poisson manifold
D(G)
from Example
1.3
is

\omega=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta_{i}^{1L}\wedge\theta_{i}^{2R}+\theta_{i}^{1R}\wedge\theta_{i}^{2L}\right).

Remark 1.13
.

If the action of
G
is trivial, the quasi-Poisson manifold
M
is nondegenerate if and only if
\pi^{\#}
is an isomorphism—that is, if and only if
\pi
is a nondegenerate Poisson structure. In this case
\omega
is exactly the corresponding symplectic form.

Even when
\pi
is degenerate, the image of (
1.8
) is an integrable generalized distribution. Its integral submanifolds, which are
G
-stable, are called the
nondegenerate leaves
of
M
, because
\pi
gives each the structure of a nondegenerate quasi-Poisson manifold. In particular, each nondegenerate leaf
S
is equipped with a quasi-Hamiltonian
2

\omega_{S}
.

Example 1.14
.

The nondegenerate leaves of the quasi-Poisson structure
(G,\pi_{G})
defined in Example
1.2
are the conjugacy classes.

There is an analogue of Hamiltonian reduction for quasi-Poisson manifolds. Let
(M,\pi,\Phi)
be a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold and fix a conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}\subset G
. If the action of
G
on
\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
is locally-free, then the quotient

M\!\sslash_{\mathcal{O}}\!G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G

has a natural Poisson structure whose symplectic leaves are precisely the reductions of the nondegenerate leaves of
M
. When
\mathcal{O}=\{1\}
is the identity element, we denote this quotient simply by
M\!\sslash\!G
.

1.3.
Twisted Dirac structures

Fix a closed
3
-form
\phi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{3}T^{*}M)
. A vector subbundle

L\subset TM\oplus T^{*}M

is called a
\phi
-twisted Dirac structure
on
M
if it satisfies the following two conditions:

\bullet

L
is Lagrangian with respect to the symmetric pairing on
\Gamma(TM\oplus T^{*}M)
given by

\langle(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\rangle=\beta(X)+\alpha(Y);

\bullet

\Gamma(L)
is closed under the
\phi
-twisted Courant bracket
on

defined by

\llbracket(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)\rrbracket_{\phi}=([X,Y],\mathcal{L}_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}d\alpha+\iota_{X\wedge Y}\phi).

The projection of

onto the first summand is an integrable generalized distribution, and induces a foliation of

by

. Each presymplectic leaf

carries a (potentially degenerate, non-closed)

-form

such that

.

Example 1.15
.

(1)

A symplectic structure
\omega
on a manifold

corresponds to the
0
-twisted Dirac structure

given by the graph of

. Conversely, a

-twisted Dirac structure

is induced by a symplectic form if and only if

is transverse to both

and

viewed as subbundles of

.

(2)

Similarly, a Poisson structure

on

corresponds to the

-twisted Dirac structure

given by the graph of

. Its projection onto the first coordinate is the distribution whose integral submanifolds are the symplectic leaves of

. Conversely, a
0
-twisted Dirac structure

is induced by a Poisson bivector if and only if

is transverse to

.

(3)

[
BuCr1
, Theorem 3.16]
A Hamiltonian quasi-Poisson structure

on a
G
-manifold

corresponds to the
-\Phi^{*}\eta
-twisted Dirac structure

L=\left\{\left(\pi^{\#}(\alpha)+\rho(\xi),\,C^{*}(\alpha)+\Phi^{*}\sigma(\xi)\right)\mid\alpha\in T^{*}M,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\right\}\subset TM\oplus T^{*}M.

Here

is as defined in (

) and
\sigma
is given by

(1.16)

\displaystyle\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}G)

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi^{L}+\xi^{R}\right)^{\vee},

where

is the dual of the vector field

under the isomorphism

induced by the Killing form.

This Dirac structure has the property that
\ker\Phi_{*}\cap L=0
. The associated presymplectic foliation, given by projecting

onto

, is exactly the foliation of

by quasi-Hamiltonian leaves described in Section

.

Let
(M,L_{M})
be a Dirac manifold and let
f\colon M\longrightarrow N
be a smooth map to a manifold
N
. The
pushforward
of the Dirac structure
L_{M}
, if it is well-defined, is the distribution

f_{*}L_{M}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(f_{*}X,\beta)\in TN\oplus T^{*}N\mid(X,f^{*}\beta)\in L_{M}\}.

If this distribution defines a smooth vector bundle on
N
, it is a Dirac structure. When
(M,L_{M})
and
(N,L_{N})
are Dirac manifolds, a map
f\colon M\longrightarrow N
is
forward-Dirac
if

L_{N}=f_{*}L_{M}.

This notion generalizes the pushforward of vector fields, and all Poisson and quasi-Poisson maps are forward-Dirac. In particular, if
(M,\pi,\Phi)
is a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold, then the group-valued moment map
\Phi
is forward-Dirac when
M
and
G
are viewed as Dirac manifolds. Moreover,
[
BuCr1
, Theorem 3.16]
shows that every
\phi
-twisted Dirac manifold
(M,L)
equipped with a forward-Dirac map
\Phi\colon M\longrightarrow G
which satisfies

(1.17)

\phi=-\Phi^{*}\eta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ker\Phi_{*}\cap L=0

is a quasi-Poisson manifold. (In
[
BuCr2
]
, such a map is called
strong
forward-Dirac.)

Conversely, let
(N,L_{N})
be a Dirac manifold and let
f\colon M\longrightarrow N
be a smooth map from a manifold
M
. The
pullback
of the Dirac structure
L_{N}
is the Lagrangian distribution

f^{*}L_{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(X,f^{*}\beta)\in TM\oplus T^{*}M\mid(f_{*}X,\beta)\in L_{N}\}.

If this distribution defines a smooth vector bundle on
M
, it is a Dirac structure. When
(M,L_{M})
and
(N,L_{N})
are Dirac manifolds, a map
f\colon M\longrightarrow N
is called
backward-Dirac
if

L_{M}=f^{*}L_{N}.

This is a generalization of the pullback of differential forms—symplectomorphisms, for instance, are backward-Dirac. We give the following important example of a Dirac pullback, which we will use repeatedly in the next section.

Lemma 1.18
.

Suppose that
(N,L)
is a
\phi
-twisted Dirac manifold. If
\imath\colon Z\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow N
is a submanifold which is transverse to the foliation of
N
by presymplectic leaves, then

\imath^{*}L=\{(X,\imath^{*}\beta)\in TZ\oplus T^{*}Z\mid(\imath_{*}X,\beta)\in L\}

is a
\imath^{*}\phi
-twisted Dirac structure on
Z
.

Proof.

By
[
Bur
, Section 1.5.1]
, the pullback
\imath^{*}L
defines a Lagrangian distribution in
TZ\oplus T^{*}Z
. Let
p_{T}
be the projection of
TN\oplus T^{*}N
onto the first summand. The condition that
Z
is transverse to the presymplectic foliation is equivalent to

p_{T}(L)_{|Z}+TZ=TM_{|Z}.

Then we have

\displaystyle L_{|Z}\cap TZ^{\perp}

\displaystyle=p_{T}(L)_{|Z}^{\perp}\cap TZ^{\perp}

\displaystyle=(p_{T}(L)_{|Z}+TZ)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=0,

where the first equality follows from the fact that
L
is Lagrangian. In particular
L_{|Z}\cap TZ^{\perp}
has constant rank, so
[
Bur
, Proposition 1.10 and Example 1.11]
implies that the Lagrangian distribution
\imath^{*}L
is a smooth subbundle of
TZ\oplus T^{*}Z
.

The
\phi
-twisted Courant bracket on
TN\oplus T^{*}N
restricts to a
\imath^{*}\phi
-twisted Courant bracket on
TZ\oplus T^{*}Z
. Since the Lagrangian distribution
\imath^{*}L
is a smooth vector bundle, its space of sections is closed under this bracket and
\imath^{*}L
is therefore a
\imath^{*}\phi
-twisted Dirac structure on
Z
.
∎

Note that any isomorphism which is forward-Dirac is also backward-Dirac, and vice-versa. To conclude we prove two simple “push–pull” lemmas which will be useful in the next section.

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
A,B,C,D
be manifolds which fit into the diagram

(1.20)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}

Suppose that
B
has a Dirac structure

and that all Dirac pullbacks and pushforwards along the diagram are well-defined smooth vector bundles. If (
1.20
) is Cartesian, then

\rho_{*}\psi^{*}L_{B}=\tau^{*}\sigma_{*}L_{B}.

Proof.

Computing, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{*}\psi^{*}L_{B}

\displaystyle=\{(\rho_{*}a,\gamma)\in TC\oplus T^{*}C\mid(a,\rho^{*}\gamma)\in\psi^{*}L_{B}\}

\displaystyle=\{(\rho_{*}a,\gamma)\in TC\oplus T^{*}C\mid\exists\beta\in T^{*}B\text{ such that }\psi^{*}\beta=\rho^{*}\gamma\text{ and }(\psi_{*}a,\beta)\in L_{B}\}

and

\displaystyle\tau^{*}\sigma_{*}L_{B}

\displaystyle=\{(c,\tau^{*}\delta)\in TC\oplus T^{*}C\mid(\tau_{*}c,\delta)\in\sigma_{*}L_{B}\}

\displaystyle=\{(c,\tau^{*}\delta)\in TC\oplus T^{*}C\mid\exists b\in TB\text{ such that }\sigma_{*}b=\tau_{*}c\text{ and }(b,\sigma^{*}\delta)\in L_{B}\}

Suppose now that
(\rho_{*}a,\gamma)\in\rho_{*}\psi^{*}L_{B}
. Then there exists
\beta\in T^{*}B
such that

\psi^{*}\beta=\rho^{*}\gamma\quad\text{ and }\quad(\psi_{*}a,\beta)\in L_{B}.

Since the pullbacks of
\beta
and
\gamma
to
A
agree, and since the diagram (
1.20
) is Cartesian, there exists a covector
\delta\in T^{*}D
such that

\sigma^{*}\delta=\beta\quad\text{ and }\quad\tau^{*}\delta=\gamma.

Let
b=\psi^{*}a
. Then
\sigma_{*}b=\tau_{*}\rho_{*}a
and

(b,\sigma^{*}\delta)=(\psi_{*}a,\beta)\in L_{B}.

Therefore
(\rho_{*}a,\gamma)\in\tau^{*}\sigma_{*}L_{B},
and we see that

\rho_{*}\psi^{*}L_{B}\subset\tau^{*}\sigma_{*}L_{B}.

Since the two sides of this inclusion are vector bundles of the same rank over
C
, they are equal.
∎

Lemma 1.21
.

Let
A,B,C
be Dirac manifolds and let
\sigma\colon A\longrightarrow C
be an isomorphism which factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

where
\psi
is backward-Dirac and
\rho
is forward-Dirac. Then
\sigma
is both backward- and forward-Dirac.

Proof.

Write
L_{A}
,
L_{B}
, and
L_{C}
for the Dirac structures on
A
,
B
, and
C
respectively. Since
\psi
is backward-Dirac and
\rho
is forward-Dirac, we have

L_{A}=\psi^{*}L_{B}\quad\text{ and }\quad L_{C}=\rho_{*}L_{B}.

Since
\sigma
is an isomorphism, it is sufficient to prove that it is forward-Dirac—that is, to show that
L_{C}=\sigma_{*}L_{A}.

Suppose that
(\sigma_{*}a,\gamma)\in\sigma_{*}L_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle(a,\sigma^{*}\gamma)\in L_{A}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad(a,\psi^{*}\rho^{*}\gamma)\in L_{A}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\psi_{*}a,\rho^{*}\gamma)\in L_{B}\quad\text{since $L_{A}=\psi^{*}L_{B}$}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\rho_{*}\psi_{*}a,\gamma)\in L_{C}\quad\text{since $L_{C}=\rho_{*}L_{B}$}

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\sigma_{*}a,\gamma)\in L_{C}.

Therefore
\sigma_{*}L_{A}\subset L_{C}
. Since
\sigma_{*}L_{A}
and
L_{C}
are vector bundles of the same rank over

, this inclusion implies equality. Therefore

is forward-Dirac. ∎

2.
Steinberg slices

In this section we show that any quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold
(M,\pi,\Phi)
has a distinguished submanifold
M_{\Sigma}
which intersects each nondegenerate leaf transversally and symplectically. This submanifold, which we call the
Steinberg slice
of
M
, is the preimage of the Steinberg cross-section of
G
under the moment map
\Phi
. It carries a Poisson structure whose symplectic leaves are its intersections with the nondegenerate leaves of
M
.

2.1.
Construction of
M_{\Sigma}

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
G
corresponding to a maximal torus
T
, and let
c\in W
be a Coxeter element—that is,
c
is the product of the simple reflections, which is unique up to conjugation. Write
\dot{c}\in N_{G}(T)
for a fixed group representative of
c
.

Fix a pair of opposite Borel subgroups
B
and
B^{-}
containing
T
, and let
U
and
U^{-}
be their unipotent radicals. The
Steinberg cross-section
of
G
, which was introduced in
[
Ste
]
, is the closed subvariety

\Sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}U\dot{c}\cap\dot{c}\,U^{-}\subset G.

It is an affine space which consists entirely of regular elements. Its dimension is equal to the length of
c
as an element of the Weyl group, which is the rank of
G
.

Since
G
is simply-connected,
\Sigma
intersects every regular conjugacy class in
G
exactly once and transversally
[
Ste
, Theorem 1.4]
. If
\Xi\colon G\longrightarrow T/W
is the quotient map induced by the Chevalley isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[G]^{G}\cong\mathbb{C}[T]^{W}
, then the composition

(2.1)

\Sigma\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow G\xlongrightarrow{\Xi}T/W

is an isomorphism of affine varieties.

Lemma 2.2
.

The pullback of the twisted Dirac structure
L_{G}
to the cross-section
\Sigma
is the zero Poisson structure.

Proof.

Let
\jmath\colon\Sigma\longrightarrow G
be the inclusion map. Since
\Sigma
is transverse to the conjugacy classes of
G
, by Lemma
1.18
the Dirac structure
L_{G}
pulls back to a
\jmath^{*}\eta
-twisted Dirac structure

L_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\jmath^{*}L_{G}=\{(0,\jmath^{*}\alpha)\in T\Sigma\oplus T^{*}\Sigma\mid(0,\alpha)\in L_{G}\}

on
\Sigma
.

For any
h\in\Sigma
, the image of

\theta^{R}_{h}=(R_{h^{-1}})_{*}\colon T_{h}\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

is contained in
\mathfrak{b}=\text{Lie}(B)
, and
(\mathfrak{b},[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}])=0
. In view of (
1.9
) we have
\jmath^{*}\eta=0
, so the Dirac structure
L_{\Sigma}
is non-twisted.

Moreover, since
L_{\Sigma}
is a Lagrangian subbundle of
T\Sigma\oplus T^{*}\Sigma
, it follows that the map
\jmath^{*}
has full rank and therefore

L_{\Sigma}=\{(0,\alpha)\in T\Sigma\oplus T^{*}\Sigma\mid\alpha\in T^{*}\Sigma\}

corresponds to the zero Poisson structure.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

and

be complex groups. Suppose that

is simply-connected and semisimple, and let
\Sigma
be the Steinberg cross-section of

. Let
(M,\pi)
be a quasi-Poisson

-manifold with group-valued moment map

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H\times K

\displaystyle m

(a)

.

to

is quasi-Poisson for the action of

, with group-valued moment map

.

are the connected components of

, where

varies over all nondegenerate leaves of

; the quasi-Hamiltonian

.

Proof.

(a) Let

and

, and write

for the conjugacy class of

. Because

Therefore, since

is transverse to

, it is transverse to

. It follows that

is a smooth submanifold of

.

(b) Let

be the inclusion, and let

-form on the product

. Since

is transverse to the conjugacy classes of

It follows that

is transverse to the

-orbits on

, and therefore also to the presymplectic leaves of the Dirac structure

.

Let
\imath\colon M_{H,\Sigma}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M

, transversality implies that the

-twisted Dirac structure on

pulls back to a

-twisted Dirac structure

on

.

2.2.

We may also take the preimage of the Steinberg cross-section through only one component of the moment map (

.

Example 2.8
.

By letting

and

The corresponding group-valued moment map is

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G

\displaystyle(a,h)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow aha^{-1}.

Remark 2.9

Consider the affine space

The one-sided slice

Lemma 2.10

Let

be a nondegenerate quasi-Poisson

-form

2.3.
Log-nondegenerate quasi-Poisson structures

Once again let
(M,\pi,\Phi)
be a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold, and let

be a
G
-stable divisor with simple normal crossings. The

is the sheaf of logarithmic vector fields on

—that is, vector fields which are tangent to the divisor
D
. Because

has simple normal crossings, this sheaf is locally free. The associated vector bundle
T_{D}M
is called the

of

, and its dual

is the

Suppose for the rest of this section that the bivector field

is logarithmic. Then it corresponds to a natural morphism of vector bundles

\pi^{\#}_{D}\colon T^{*}_{D}M\longrightarrow T_{D}M

from the log-cotangent bundle of

2
-form
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T^{*}_{D}M)
corresponds to

\omega_{D}^{\flat}\colon T_{D}M\longrightarrow T_{D}^{*}M.

Since the action of
G
on
M
stabilizes the divisor
D
, there is also a logarithmic infinitesimal action map

\rho_{D}\colon M\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow T_{D}M.

Definition 2.19
.

The quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold

is

if the morphism of vector bundles

(2.20)

\displaystyle\pi^{\#}_{D}\oplus\rho_{D}\colon T^{*}_{D}M\oplus\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{D}M

\displaystyle(\alpha,\xi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi_{D}^{\#}(\alpha)+\rho_{D}(\xi)

is surjective.

Remark 2.21
.

The pullback of

to the open dense locus

; similarly, the pullback of

to

is

. Therefore, along

the morphism
(
2.20
)
agrees with the morphism of vector bundles (
1.8
). In particular, if

is log-nondegenerate then

is its unique open dense nondegenerate leaf.

Viewed as an automorphism of the tangent sheaf of

, the map defined in (

) takes logarithmic vector fields to logarithmic vector fields. Therefore it defines a morphism of vector bundles

C_{D}\colon T_{D}M\longrightarrow T_{D}M.

Using this and
[

, Theorem 10.3]
, we give an equivalent condition for log-nondegeneracy.

Proposition 2.23

The quasi-Poisson manifold

is log-nondegenerate if and only if there exists a logarithmic
2
-form
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}T_{D}^{*}M)

\pi_{D}^{\#}\circ\omega_{D}^{\flat}=C_{D}.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) First suppose that
\pi
is log-nondegenerate. Then its restriction to

is a nondegenerate quasi-Poisson bivector

. By
[

is the restriction of (

Taking duals, we also obtain

Remark 2.26

Let

Note that

Lemma 3.1

Proof.

be the Jordan decomposition of

Write

which is the image of

, and let

The center

∎

3.2.
The wonderful compactification

Let

be the rank of
G
. The

is a canonical, smooth,
G\times G
-equivariant compactification of

which was introduced by de Concini and Procesi
[

]
. We recall some of its structure theory, following
[

]
. It is a smooth projective variety which contains
G_{\ad}
as an open dense subset and on which
G
acts by extensions of the left- and right-multiplication. The boundary

is a simple normal crossing divisor with irreducible components

.

The

orbits on
\overline{G_{\ad}}
are in bijection with subsets of the simple roots in the sense that, for any
I\subset\{1,\ldots,l\}
, the closure of the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{I}
is the corresponding partial intersection of divisor components

\overline{\mathcal{O}_{I}}=\bigcap_{i\not\in I}D_{i}.

In particular, the closure of each orbit is smooth.

The subset

determines a “positive” parabolic subgroup
P_{I}
, generated by the “positive” Borel
B
and the simple root spaces indexed by
I
. Write
P_{I}^{-}
for the opposite parabolic and
L_{I}
for their common Levi component. Let
U^{\pm}_{I}\subset P^{\pm}_{I}
be the unipotent radicals, and denote by
\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}_{I}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{I}^{\pm}
, and
\mathfrak{l}_{I}
the Lie algebras of these subgroups. Each orbit
\mathcal{O}_{I}
has a distinguished basepoint

whose
G\times G
-stabilizer is

(3.6)

\text{Stab}_{G\times G}(z_{I})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(us,vt)\in P_{I}\times P_{I}^{-}\mid u\in U_{I},v\in U_{I}^{-},s,t\in L_{I},st^{-1}\in Z_{L_{I}}\right\}.

It follows that
\mathcal{O}_{I}
is a fiber bundle over the product of partial flag varieties
G/P_{I}\times G/P_{I}^{-}
, with fiber isomorphic to the adjoint group
L_{I}/Z_{L_{I}}
. This extends to a smooth fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{L_{I}/Z_{L_{I}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{O}_{I}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/P_{I}\times G/P_{I}^{-}}

whose fiber is the wonderful compactification of
L_{I}/Z_{L_{I}}
.

The wonderful compactification
\overline{G_{\ad}}
is
log-homogeneous
in the sense of
[
Bri
]
—that is, the logarithmic infinitesimal action map

\text{act}_{D}\colon\overline{G_{\ad}}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow T_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}

is surjective. Let
\kappa
be the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
. In the short exact sequence of vector bundles over
\overline{G_{\ad}}

the kernel

is Lagrangian relative to the form

on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}

[
Bri
, Example 2.5]
. It follows that

(3.7)

\ker(\text{act}_{D})\cong T^{\ast}_{D}\overline{G_{\ad}}.

This identifies the log-cotangent bundle

with a subbundle of the trivial bundle
\overline{G_{\ad}}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}
, extending the embedding

\displaystyle T^{*}G_{\ad}\cong G_{\ad}\times\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow G_{\ad}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle(a,x)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow({a,\Ad_{a}x,x}).

Under (

), the fiber of the log-cotangent bundle at the orbit basepoint

is

T^{*}_{D,z_{I}}\overline{G_{\ad}}\cong\mathfrak{p}_{I}\times_{\mathfrak{l}_{I}}\mathfrak{p}^{-}_{I}.

Remark 3.8
.

Via (

is a bundle of Lie algebras over

. In analogy with Remark

(z_{I},x,y)\in\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
, we obtain

\overline{\pi}_{D}^{\#}(\theta_{\alpha}^{1L})=\begin{cases}E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R},\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ if }E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{I}^{-}\backslash\mathfrak{l}_{I}\\
E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R}+E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R},\quad\text{ if }E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}_{I}\end{cases}

and

\overline{\pi}_{D}^{\#}(\theta_{\alpha}^{1R})=\begin{cases}E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R},\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ if }E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{I}\backslash\mathfrak{l}_{I}\\
E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R}+E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R},\quad\text{ if }E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}_{I}.\end{cases}

This implies that the image of
\overline{\pi}^{\#}_{D}
contains a subspace of dimension
\dim G
which is parallel to the fiber. It follows that, at the point
(z_{I},x,y)
,

\dim\left(\im(\overline{\pi}^{\#}_{D}\oplus\rho_{D})\right)=2\dim G.

Therefore this morphism of vector bundles is surjective.∎

5.
The relative compactification of
\mathfrak{Z}

Consider the relative compactification

\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}=\left\{(a,h)\in\overline{G_{\ad}}\times\Sigma\mid a\in\overline{Z_{\ad}(h)}\right\}.

By realizing
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
as a Steinberg slice in
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
, we will use the results of the previous sections to show that it is a smooth algebraic variety whose boundary is a simple normal crossing divisor, and that the symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{Z}
defined (up to a finite central quotient) in Example
2.6
extends to a log-symplectic structure on
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
. We will then describe the symplectic leaves of this structure.

5.1.
Construction of
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}

We begin by characterizing the image and fibers of the compactified moment map
\overline{\mu}.
In Section
2
we defined the quotient map
\Xi\colon G\longrightarrow T/W
, whose fibers are the closures of the regular conjugacy classes. In view of diagram (
4.7
), the map
\overline{\mu}
is proper, and we have the following description of its image.

Lemma 5.1
.

The image of
\overline{\mu}
is the closed subvariety

\Delta\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(g,h)\in G\times G\mid\Xi(g)=\Xi(h^{-1})\right\}

consisting of pairs of elements
(g,h)\in G\times G
with the property that
g
and
h^{-1}
lie in the closure of the same conjugacy class.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mu}
is proper, its image is closed, so it is the closure of the image of
\mu
. As in (
2.7
), the image of
\mu
is the collection of pairs

\left\{(g,h)\in G\times G\mid g\text{ is conjugate to }h^{-1}\right\}.

The closure of this set is precisely
\Delta
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The variety
\Delta
is normal.

Proof.

Because
\Delta
is the image of
\overline{\mu}
, it is irreducible of dimension

2\dim G-l.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in\mathbb{C}[G]^{G}
be a set of generators for the algebra of conjugation-invariant functions on
G
. Then

\Delta=\left\{(g,h)\in G\times G\mid f_{i}(g)=f_{i}(h^{-1})\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq l\right\}.

In particular,
\Delta
is the vanishing locus of exactly
l
algebraically independent functions on
G\times G
. Therefore it is a complete intersection, and in particular it is Cohen–Macaulay.

The regular locus

\Delta^{\text{r}}=\left\{(g,h)\in\Delta\mid g\text{ and }h\text{ are regular}\right\}

is a smooth open subset of
\Delta
because the differentials
df_{1},\ldots,df_{l}
are linearly independent at every point of
G^{\text{r}}

[
Ste
, Theorem 1.5]
. Moreover, the complement of
\Delta^{\text{r}}
in
\Delta
has codimension at least two
[
Ste
, Theorem 1.3]
. Since
\Delta
is a Cohen–Macaulay variety with no singularities in codimension one, by Serre’s criterion it is normal.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

The fibers of
\overline{\mu}
are connected.

Proof.

A general fiber of
\overline{\mu}
is the closure in
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
of a general fiber of
\mu
, which is connected by Lemma
3.1
. Since
\overline{\mu}
is proper, by Stein factorization it decomposes as a composition

\overline{\mu}=f\circ g

where
f
is a finite morphism and
g
has connected fibers.

Because the general fiber of
\overline{\mu}
is connected,
f
is a birational map. Moreover, since the image of
f
is normal by Lemma
5.2
, it follows from Zariski’s main theorem that all the fibers of
f
are connected. Therefore the fibers of
\overline{\mu}
are also connected.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The variety
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
is smooth and has a natural log-symplectic Poisson structure whose open dense symplectic leaf is
\mathfrak{Z}
.

Proof.

By Propositions
4.8
and
4.10
,
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
is a log-nondegenerate quasi-Poisson variety whose open dense leaf is the double
\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
. There is a commutative diagram of moment maps

(5.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times G.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mu}}

Two elements of
\Sigma
are in the closure of the same conjugacy class if and only if they are equal. It follows from Lemma
5.1
that

\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma\times\iota(\Sigma))=\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta}).

Since
\Sigma\times\iota(\Sigma)
is a Steinberg cross-section in
G\times G
, Theorem
2.3
implies that the preimage
\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})
is a smooth subvariety of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
with a natural Poisson structure whose symplectic leaves are the intersections of
\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})
with the nondegenerate leaves of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
. This Poisson structure is log-symplectic by Proposition
2.27
. It remains only to show that
\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
.

By Lemma
5.3
, the variety
\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})
is connected. Since it is also smooth, it is irreducible, and therefore it is the closure in
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
of
\mu^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})\subset\mathbf{D}_{G_{\ad}}
. In particular, for any
h\in\Sigma
,

\overline{\mu}^{-1}(h,h^{-1})=\overline{\mu^{-1}(h,h^{-1})}\cong\overline{Z_{\ad}(h)}\subset\overline{G_{\ad}}.

It follows that

\overline{\mu}^{-1}(\Sigma_{\Delta})=\left\{(a,h,h^{-1})\in\overline{G_{\ad}}\times G\times G\mid h\in\Sigma,a\in\overline{Z_{\ad}(h)}\right\}\cong\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}.

We obtain a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma,}

which is the pullback of (
5.5
) along the embedding
\Sigma\cong\Sigma_{\Delta}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow G\times G
. Since the horizontal arrow in this diagram is the restriction of a backward-Dirac map, it is a Poisson morphism. In particular,
\mathfrak{Z}
sits inside
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
as the unique open dense symplectic leaf.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Suppose that
G
is simply-laced and let
G^{\vee}
be its Langlands dual group. Write
\mathcal{K}
for the field
\mathbb{C}((t))
of Laurent series,
\mathcal{O}
for its ring of integers, and

\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})/G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})

for the affine Grassmannian of
G^{\vee}
. In
[
BFM
, Proposition 2.8 and Theorem 2.15]
, the authors prove that there is a natural isomorphism Poisson algebras

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{Z}]\cong K^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}})

between the coordinate ring of the multiplicative universal centralizer and the equivariant
K
-theory of
\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
. Here the right-hand side has a ring structure given by convolution, and its Poisson bracket comes from the one-parameter deformation
K^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}})
induced by the loop rotation.

The algebra
K^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}})
has a natural filtration indexed by the weight lattice
\Lambda
of
G
, which is induced by the support in
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
-orbit closures. Using the Vinberg monoid realization of the wonderful compactification, one easily shows (see
[
Bal
, Section 5]
) that the relative compactification
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
can also be obtained via relative Proj as

\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}\cong\text{Proj}_{\Sigma}\left(\text{Rees}_{\Lambda}(K^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathbf{Gr}_{G^{\vee}})\right).

Therefore, in the simply-laced case, our relative compactification
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
is an example the Rees algebra approach to compactified Coulomb branches illustrated in
[
BFN
, Remark 3.7]
.

5.2.
Symplectic leaves

By Proposition
2.27
, the symplectic leaves of
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
are the connected components of the intersections of
\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}
with the nondegenerate leaves of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
. Therefore we first describe the nondegenerate leaves of the quasi-Poisson variety
(\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}},\overline{\pi},\overline{\mu})
. For this we need to analyze the image of the (non-logarithmic) bundle map

\overline{\pi}^{\#}\oplus\rho\colon T^{*}\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow T\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}.

Fix an index set
I\subset\{1,\ldots,l\}
, and write

\mathfrak{c}_{I}\colon P_{I}\longrightarrow P_{I}/[P_{I},P_{I}]=:A_{I}

for the quotient of
P_{I}
by its derived subgroup. The torus
A_{I}
is the “universal torus” associated to the standard parabolic
P_{I}
. We first give a criterion for when two points in the fiber of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
above
z_{I}\in\overline{G_{\ad}}
are in the same nondegenerate leaf.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-}
. Then
(z_{I},x,y)
and
(z_{I},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are in the same nondegenerate leaf of
(\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}},\overline{\pi},\overline{\mu})
if and only if

\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x)=\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x^{\prime}).

Remark 5.8
.

The value of
\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x)
depends only on the
L_{I}
-component of the element

x\in P_{I}=L_{I}\ltimes U_{I}.

Since points in
P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-}
are pairs with the same Levi component, the proposition could instead be stated in an equivalent way relative to the second coordinate and the negative parabolic
P_{I}^{-}
.

Proof.

In order to determine the intersection of the fiber
\{z_{I}\}\times(P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-})
with each nondegenerate leaf, we will find which vectors in the image of
\overline{\pi}^{\#}\oplus\rho
are tangent to the fibers of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
.

By (
3.6
), the kernel of the infinitesimal action map

\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow T_{z_{I}}\overline{G_{\ad}}

is the subalgebra of pairs

\{(u+s,v+t)\in\mathfrak{p}_{I}\times\mathfrak{p}_{I}^{-}\mid u\in\mathfrak{u}_{I},v\in\mathfrak{u}_{I}^{-},s,t\in\mathfrak{l}_{I},s-t\in Z_{\mathfrak{l}_{I}}\}.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Proposition
4.10
. Viewed as a section of
\wedge^{2}T\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
, at the point
(z_{I},x,y)
the value of the bivector
\overline{\pi}
is

\displaystyle\overline{\pi}=\sum_{E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{I}^{-}\backslash Z_{\mathfrak{l}_{I}}}E_{\alpha}^{1L}\wedge(E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R})

\displaystyle+\sum_{E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}_{I}\backslash Z_{\mathfrak{l}_{I}}}E_{\alpha}^{1R}\wedge(E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{0}}(E_{\alpha}^{2L}\wedge E_{\alpha}^{2R}+E_{\alpha}^{3R}\wedge E_{\alpha}^{3L}).

Therefore, the vectors in the image of
\overline{\pi}^{\#}\oplus\rho
which are parallel to the fiber of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
at
z_{I}\in\overline{G_{\ad}}
are given by the span of

\displaystyle\left\{E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R}\mid E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{u}_{I}^{-}\right\}\cup

\displaystyle\left\{E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R}\mid E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{u}_{I}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\cup\left\{E_{\alpha}^{2L}+E_{\alpha}^{2R}+E_{\alpha}^{3L}+E_{\alpha}^{3R}\mid E_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{l}_{I}\backslash Z_{\mathfrak{l}_{I}}\right\}.

At each point this is the tangent space to the fibers of the smooth morphism

\displaystyle P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A_{I}

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x).

Since these fibers are connected, it follows that two points
(z_{I},x,y)
and
(z_{I},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are in the same nondegenerate leaf if and only if they have the same image under this map.
∎

Let
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}
be the preimage of
\mathcal{O}_{I}\subset\overline{G_{\ad}}
under the structure map

\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}\longrightarrow\overline{G_{\ad}}.

Since both
\pi
and
\rho
are tangent to the boundary of
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}}
, each orbit preimage
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}

is a union of nondegenerate leaves. To extend the criterion of Proposition
5.7
to this preimage, we define the following data.

For any
a\in\overline{G_{\ad}}
, there exist group elements
g,h\in G
such that
a=gz_{I}h^{-1}
. We associate to this point a corresponding “positive” parabolic subgroup

P_{a}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}gP_{I}g^{-1},

which, in view of (
3.6
), depends only on the point
a\in\overline{G_{\ad}}
. There is a canonical identification of tori

P_{a}/[P_{a},P_{a}]\cong P_{I}/[P_{I},P_{I}]=A_{I},

and we denote the corresponding quotient map by

\mathfrak{c}_{a}\colon P_{a}\longrightarrow P_{a}/[P_{a},P_{a}]\cong A_{I}.

The preimage
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}
is a locally trivial
G\times G
-equivariant fiber bundle over
\mathcal{O}_{I}
. In other words, there is an isomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G\times G)\times_{\text{Stab}_{G\times G}(z_{I})}(P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{I},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Moreover, the map

\displaystyle(G\times G)\times_{\text{Stab}_{G\times G}(z_{I})}(P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A_{I}

\displaystyle[(g,h):(x,y)]

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x)

is well-defined. Composing it with the isomorphism above, we get a smooth morphism

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A_{I}

\displaystyle(a,x,y)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathfrak{c}_{a}(x).

In the following proposition we show that its fibers are precisely the nondegenerate quasi-Poisson leaves in
\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Two points
(a,x,y),(b,w,z)\in\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}},I}
are in the same nondegenerate leaf of
\overline{\pi}
if and only if

\mathfrak{c}_{a}(x)=\mathfrak{c}_{b}(w).

Proof.

There exist points

(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),(w^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in P_{I}\times_{L_{I}}P_{I}^{-}

such that
(a,x,y)
is
G\times G
-conjugate to
(z_{I},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and
(b,w,z)
is
G\times G
-conjugate to
(z_{I},w^{\prime},z^{\prime})
. Since the nondegenerate leaves of
(\mathbf{D}_{\overline{G_{\ad}}},\overline{\pi})
are
G\times G
-stable,
(a,x,y)
and
(b,w,z)
are in the same leaf if and only if their translates
(z_{I},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
and
(z_{I},w^{\prime},z^{\prime})
are in the same leaf. By Proposition
5.7
, this occurs if and only if

\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x^{\prime})=\mathfrak{c}_{I}(w^{\prime}).

But now
\mathfrak{c}_{a}(x)=\mathfrak{c}_{I}(x^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{c}_{b}(w)=\mathfrak{c}_{I}(w^{\prime})
, and the statement follows.
∎

The orbit stratification on

By Theorem

is a union of symplectic leaves, and Proposition

has the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 5.10
.

The symplectic leaves of

are the fibers of the smooth morphism

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{Z}}_{I}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A_{I}

\displaystyle(a,h)

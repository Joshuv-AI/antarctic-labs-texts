On certain analytic functions defined by differential inequality
Prachi Prajna Dash ‡ , Jugal Kishore Prajapat †

Abstract
. For the family of analytic functions f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) in the open unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} with f ⁡ ( 0 ) = f ′ ​ ( 0 ) − 1 = 0 f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0 , satisfying the differential equation z ​ f ′ ​ ( z ) − f ⁡ ( z ) = 1 2 ​ z 2 ​ ϕ ​ ( z ) , | ϕ ⁡ ( z ) | ≤ 1 , zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}z^{2}\phi(z),\quad|\phi(z)|\leq 1, we obtain radii of convexity, starlikeness, and close-to-convexity of partial sums of f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) . 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the family of analytic functions in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
satisfying
f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1
, and let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of univalent functions in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
E\subset\mathcal{A}
and
P
denote the property of the image of
f(z)\in E
. The largest
\gamma_{f}
such that
f(z)\in E
has a property
P
in each disk
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|<r\}
for
0<r\leq\gamma_{f}
is known as the radius of the property
P
of
f(z)
. The number
\gamma:=\mbox{inf}\{\gamma_{f}:\;f(z)\in E\}
is the radius of the property
P
of the class
E
. If
\mathcal{G}
is the class of all
f(z)\in E
characterized by the property
P
, then
\gamma
is called the
\mathcal{G}
-radius of the class
E
.

A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be convex if it maps
\mathbb{D}
conformally onto a convex domain. A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is convex if and only if
\Re\left(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
for all
z
in
\mathbb{D}
. The class of these functions is denoted by
\mathcal{K}.
A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be starlike if it maps
\mathbb{D}
conformally onto a starlike domain. A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is starlike if and only if
\Re\left(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\right)>0
for all
z
in
\mathbb{D}
. The class of these functions is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
. Note that
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}\subset\mathcal{S}
. An analytic function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
is close-to-convex in
\mathbb{D}
if
\mathbb{C}\backslash f(\mathbb{D})
can be represented as a union of non-crossing half lines. The class of these functions is denoted by
\mathcal{C}
. For every convex function
g(z)
, a function
f(z)\in\mathcal{C}
satisfies the inequality
\Re\left(f^{\prime}(z)/g^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
for
z
in
\mathbb{D}
.

A function
f(z)
is subordinate to the function
g
, written as
f(z)\prec g(z)
, if there exists a Schwarz function
w(z)
, with
w(0)=0
and
|w(z)|\leq|z|
, such that
f(z)=g(w(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
g(z)\in\mathcal{S}
, then the following relation:

f(z)\prec g(z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad[f(0)=g(0)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;f(\mathbb{D})\subset g(\mathbb{D})],

holds true. The convolution (or Hadamard product) of two analytic functions
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
and
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
is defined by

(f*g)(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\,b_{k}\,z^{k}.

The convolution has the algebraic properties of ordinary multiplication. Rusheweyh and Sheil-Small
[
9
]
proved an important conjecture known as the
Pólya-Schoenberg Conjecture
that the class
\mathcal{K}
is preserved under convolution. They also proved that the classes
\mathcal{S^{*}}
and
\mathcal{C}
are closed under convolution with the class
\mathcal{K}
. Many other convolution problems were studied by Rusheweyh in
[
8
]
and have found many applications in various fields. Finding classes of univalent functions that are preserved under convolution and studying their properties is an important research problem. Recently, Peng and Zhong
[
11
]
proved that
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
that satisfy the differential equation

(1.1)

zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}z^{2}\phi(z),\quad|\phi(z)|\leq 1,

are closed under convolution. Let
\Omega
be the subset of
\mathcal{A}
that consists of all functions satisfying (
1.1
). Note that the functions
f\in\Omega
satisfy the inequality

(1.2)

|zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)|<\dfrac{1}{2},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The functions in
\Omega
are univalent and starlike in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
11
,
4
]
). Obradović and Peng
[
5
]
gave certain sufficient conditions for functions belonging to the class
\Omega
. Also, Wani and Swaminathan
[
1
]
obtained certain sufficient conditions and subordination properties for functions belonging to the class
\Omega
.

To obtain an example of a function in
\Omega
, let
f_{\mu}(z)\in\mathcal{A}
such that

zf^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)-f_{\mu}(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}z^{2}\phi_{\mu}(z),

where
\phi_{\mu}(z)=\dfrac{\mu+z}{1+\mu z},\;\mu\in[-1,1]
. Then

zf^{\prime}_{\mu}(z)-f_{\mu}(z)=\dfrac{1}{2}z^{2}\left(\mu+(1-\mu^{2})\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\mu)^{k-1}\,z^{k}\right),

now comparing the coefficients on both sides, we obtain that
f_{\mu}(z)\in\Omega
, where

(1.3)

f_{\mu}(z)=z+\dfrac{\mu}{2}z^{2}+\dfrac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-\mu)^{k-1}}{k+1}\,z^{k+2},\quad\mu\in[-1,1].

For
\lambda>0
, let
\Omega_{\lambda}
denote the set of functions in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying the differential equation

(1.4)

zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)=\lambda z^{2}\phi(z),\quad|\phi(z)|\leq 1.

Note that the functions
f(z)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
satisfy the inequality

(1.5)

|zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)|<\lambda,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The class
\Omega_{\lambda}
is non-empty as the identity function satisfies the inequality (
1.5
). Note that
\Omega_{1/2}=\Omega
and
\Omega_{\lambda_{1}}\subseteq\Omega_{\lambda_{2}}
when
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
. Consequently, the functions in
\Omega_{\lambda}
are univalent and starlike in
\mathbb{D}
for
\lambda\in\left(0,1/2\right]
. We observe that the function

(1.6)

f(z)=z+\dfrac{\lambda z^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{\lambda z^{3}}{4},\qquad\lambda>0,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

belongs to the class
\Omega_{\lambda}
.

A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
has the power series representation

(1.7)

f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}\,z^{k},\qquad\left(a_{k}=\dfrac{f^{(k)}(z)}{k!},\;k=2,\,3,\dots,\;z\in\mathbb{D}\right),

and the
n
-th partial sum of
f(z)
is the polynomial

(1.8)

s_{n}(z;f)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{n}a_{k}\,z^{k}.

In 1928, Szegö demonstrated that
s_{n}(z;f)
for
f(z)\in\mathcal{S}
are univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{1/4}
, and this radius cannot be extended to a larger value
[
3
]
. Later, in 1941, Robertson
[
6
]
established that
s_{n}(z;f)
remains univalent in the disk
|z|<1-(3\log n)/n
for all
n\geq 5
. It is true that the Koebe function is extremal in many subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
, but Bshouty and Hergartner
[
2
]
showed in 1991 that it is not extremal for the radius of univalence of
s_{n}(z;f)
for
f(z)\in\mathcal{S}
. This result was resolved using the convolution theorem by Ruscheweyh and others
[
9
]
for various geometric subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
. However, determining the exact largest radius of univalence of
s_{n}(z;f)
for
f(z)\in\mathcal{S}
remains an open problem (see
[
7
, page 246]
).

We shall use the following results.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
11
,
4
]

If
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\Omega
, then

(a)

|z|-\dfrac{|z|^{2}}{2}\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+\dfrac{|z|^{2}}{2}
;

(b)

1-|z|\,\leq\,|f^{\prime}(z)|\,\leq\,1+|z|
;

(c)

|a_{k}|\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{2(k-1)},\;k\geq 2
.

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},
equality occurs in (a) and (b) if and only if
f(z)=z+\dfrac{1}{2}e^{i\psi}z^{2},
where
\psi\in\mathbb{R}
, and equality holds for (c) if
f(z)=z+\dfrac{1}{2(k-1)}z^{k}
, where
k=2,3,\dots
.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
1
]

If
f(z)\in\Omega
, then

\bigg|\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-1\bigg|\leq\dfrac{r}{2-r},\quad|z|=r<1,

for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
10
]

Let
q(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{K}
. If
p(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies the subordination
p(z)\prec q(z)
, then
|a_{n}|\leq|b_{1}|
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\Omega
and
\rho_{n}(z;f)=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
. Then, for
n\geq 2
and
|z|=r<1
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle|\rho_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\,-\,\dfrac{r}{2}\,\mbox{ln}(1-r)-\dfrac{r^{2}}{2},

(1.10)

\displaystyle|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\,\dfrac{2r^{2}-r}{2(1-r)}-\dfrac{\mbox{ln}(1-r)}{2},

(1.11)

\displaystyle|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\,\dfrac{r^{2}(3-2r)}{2(1-r)^{2}}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
1.1
(c)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\rho_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\;\dfrac{1}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\dfrac{r^{k+n}}{k+n-1}\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\dfrac{r^{k+2}}{k+1}=-\dfrac{r}{2}\ln(1-r)-\dfrac{r^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\;\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(k+n)\,r^{k+n-1}}{k+n-1}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{k+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{r^{k+1}}{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\dfrac{2r^{2}-r}{2(1-r)}-\dfrac{\ln(1-r)}{2},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|

\displaystyle\leq\;\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(k+n\right)r^{k+n-1}\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k+2)r^{k+1}=\dfrac{r^{2}(3-2r)}{2(1-r)^{2}}.

∎

2.
Radius properties of the Class
\Omega

The functions in
\Omega
are convex in
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}

[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
. However, the partial sums of
f(z)\in\Omega
need not be convex in
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
. For example, if
s_{3}(z;f)=z+\dfrac{\mu}{2}z^{2}+\dfrac{1-\mu^{2}}{4}z^{3}
of
f_{\mu}
given by (
1.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Re\left(1+\dfrac{z\,s_{3}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)\geq 1-\left|\dfrac{\mu z+\dfrac{3}{2}(1-\mu^{2})z^{2}}{1+\mu z+\dfrac{3}{4}(1-\mu^{2})z^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq 1-\dfrac{|\mu||z|+\dfrac{3}{2}(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}{1-|\mu||z|-\dfrac{3}{4}(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-2|\mu||z|-\dfrac{9}{4}(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}{1-|\mu||z|-\dfrac{3}{4}(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}.

Note that
1-|\mu||z|-\dfrac{3}{4}(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}>0
in
|z|<0.964
if
\mu=0.9
, but
\Re\left(1+\dfrac{z\,s_{3}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)\not>0
for
\mu=0.9
and
|z|\geq 0.4969
. Hence,
s_{3}(z;f)
is not convex in
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
. In the next result, we obtain the radius of convexity for the partial sums of
f\in\Omega
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\Omega
, and
r_{\mathcal{K}}
denote the positive root of

(2.1)

(1-r)^{2}\ln(1-r)+2-7r+4r^{2}=0.

Then
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex in the disk
|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
1.1
(c)
, we obtain

\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{2}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)\geq 1-\dfrac{2|a_{2}||z|}{1-2|a_{2}||z|}\geq 1-\dfrac{|z|}{1-|z|},

which is positive for
|z|<1/2
. Thus,
s_{2}(z;f)
is convex in the disk
|z|<1/2:=r_{1}
. Further, using Lemma
1.1
(c)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{3}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Re\left(\dfrac{1+4a_{2}z+9a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right)=2-\Re\left(\dfrac{1-3a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2-\left|\dfrac{1-3a_{3}z^{2}}{1+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}}\right|\geq 2-\dfrac{1+3|a_{3}||z|^{2}}{1-2|a_{2}||z|-3|a_{3}||z|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2-\dfrac{4+3|z|^{2}}{4-4|z|-3|z|^{2}}=\dfrac{4-8|z|-9|z|^{2}}{4-4|z|-3|z|^{2}}\;,

which is positive for
|z|<\min\{r_{2},r_{2.1}\}=r_{2}
, where
r_{2}\approx 0.3568
is the unique root of
9r^{2}+8r-4=0
and
r_{2.1}\approx 0.6667
is the unique root of
3r^{2}+4r-4=0
in
(0,1)
. Thus,
s_{3}(z;f)
is convex in the disk
|z|<r_{2}
.

Notice that the radii are decreasing as
n
increases. Next, we consider the general case
n\geq 2
. To prove that
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex in
|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}},
it is sufficient to prove that for
0<|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}},

s^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\neq 0
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z;f)}{s_{n}^{\prime}(z;f)}\right)}

\displaystyle=\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+\dfrac{\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)-z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{f^{\prime}(z)-\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)-\dfrac{\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right||\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|+|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}{|f^{\prime}(z)|-|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}>0,

where
\rho_{n}(z;f)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n+1}a_{k}z^{k}
. If
f\in\Omega
, then for
r\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},

(2.3)

\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\dfrac{r}{1-r}

(see
[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
). Hence,

\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r},\qquad|z|\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}.

To prove the inequality (
2.2
), it is sufficient to show that

(2.4)

|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|<|f^{\prime}(z)|,\qquad|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}}.

and

(2.5)

\dfrac{\Big|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\Big||\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|+|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}{|f^{\prime}(z)|-|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}\leq\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r},\qquad|z|=r<r_{\mathcal{K}},

where
r_{\mathcal{K}}
is the radius of convexity of
s_{n}(z;f)
.

Note that
\dfrac{\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{f^{\prime}(z)}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
since
f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0
(by local mapping theorem and as
f\in\mathcal{S}
) for
0<|z|<1
. Further, by the use of the maximum modulus theorem, if (
2.4
) holds for
|z|=r_{\mathcal{K}},
then the same inequality holds for
0<|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}}
when
s^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\neq 0
for
0<|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}}
. Consequently,
1+\dfrac{zs^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{s^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}
is analytic on
|z|\leq r_{\mathcal{K}}
, and if
\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{s^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}\right)\geq 0
on
|z|=r_{\mathcal{K}}
, then
s_{n}(z;f)
is convex in
|z|<r_{\mathcal{K}}
by the maximum modulus theorem. But the inequality
\Re\left(1+\dfrac{zs^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)}{s^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)}\right)\geq 0
on
|z|=r_{\mathcal{K}}
follows from (
2.5
). If
|z|=r
, then by using Lemma
1.4
and Lemma
1.1
(b)
, we obtain

|f^{\prime}(z)|-|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|\geq 1-r-\left(\dfrac{2r^{2}-r}{2(1-r)}-\dfrac{\ln(1-r)}{2}\right)=\dfrac{2-3r}{2(1-r)}+\dfrac{\ln(1-r)}{2},

which is positive when
|z|<r_{3},
where
r_{3}\approx 0.5471.
Hence (
2.4
) holds when
r<r_{3}\approx 0.5471.

Now by using inequalities (
1.10
), (
1.11
), (
2.3
) and Lemma
1.1
(b)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r}-\dfrac{\left|\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right||\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|+|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}{|f^{\prime}(z)|-|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)|}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r}-\dfrac{\dfrac{r}{1-r}\left|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|+\left|z\rho^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|}{1-r-\left|\rho^{\prime}_{n}(z;f)\right|}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r}-\dfrac{\dfrac{r}{(1-r)}\left(\dfrac{2r^{2}-r}{2(1-r)}-\dfrac{\ln(1-r)}{2}\right)+\dfrac{r^{2}(3-2r)}{2(1-r)^{2}}}{1-r-\dfrac{2r^{2}-r}{2(1-r)}+\dfrac{\ln(1-r)}{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-2r}{1-r}-\dfrac{2r^{2}-r(1-r)\ln(1-r)}{(1-r)(2-3r+(1-r)\ln(1-r))},

which is positive for
|z|<r_{4},
where
r_{4}\approx 0.3181
is the unique root of (
2.1
) in
(0,(\sqrt{5}-1)/2)
. Thus
s_{n}(z;f),\,n\geq 2
is convex in the disk
|z|<\min\{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4}\}=r_{4}:=r_{\mathcal{K}}
.
∎

The functions in
\Omega
are starlike in
\mathbb{D}

[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, but the partial sums of
f(z)\in\Omega
may not be starlike in
\mathbb{D}
. For example, if we consider
s_{3}(z;f)
of
f_{\mu}
given by (
1.3
), then we obtain

\displaystyle\Re\left(\dfrac{zs^{\prime}_{3}(z;f)}{s_{3}(z;f)}\right)\geq 1-\left|\dfrac{2\mu z^{2}+2(1-\mu^{2})z^{3}}{4z+2\mu z^{2}+(1-\mu^{2})z^{3}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq 1-\dfrac{2|\mu||z|+2(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}{4-2|\mu||z|-(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{4-4|\mu||z|-3(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}{4-2|\mu||z|-(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}}.

Note that
4-2|\mu||z|-(1-\mu^{2})|z|^{2}>0
in
\mathbb{D}
if
\mu=0.7
, but
\Re\left(\dfrac{zs_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}(z;f)}\right)\not>0
for
\mu=0.7
and
|z|\geq 0.9428
. Hence,
s_{3}(z;f)
is not starlike in
\mathbb{D}
. In the following result, we obtain the radius of starlikeness for the partial sums of
f\in\Omega
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\Omega
, and
r_{\mathcal{S}^{*}}
denote the positive root of

(2.6)

3(1-r)(2-r)\ln(1-r)+4-4r-3r^{2}+2r^{3}=0.

Then
s_{n}(z;f)
is starlike in the disk
|z|<r_{\mathcal{S}^{*}}
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
1.1
(c)
, we obtain

\Re\left(\dfrac{zs_{2}^{\prime}(z;f)}{s_{2}(z;f)}\right)=\Re\left(\dfrac{z+2a_{2}z^{2}}{z+a_{2}z^{2}}\right)\geq 1-\dfrac{|a_{2}||z|^{2}}{|z|-|a_{2}||z|^{2}}\geq\dfrac{2(1-|z|)}{2-|z|},

which is positive for
|z|<1
. Thus,
s_{2}(z;f)
is startlike in
|z|<1:=r_{5}
. Further, using Lemma
1.1
(c)
, we obtain

\Re\left(\dfrac{zs_{3}^{\prime}(z;f)}{s_{3}(z;f)}\right)=\Re\left(\dfrac{z+2a_{2}z^{2}+3a_{3}z^{3}}{z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}}\right)\geq\dfrac{4-4|z|-3|z|^{2}}{4-2|z|-|z|^{2}},

which is positive for
|z|<r_{6},
where
r_{6}\approx 0.6667
is the unique root of
3r^{2}+4r-4=0
in
(0,1)
. Thus,
s_{3}(z;f)
is starlike in
|z|<r_{6}
.

Notice that the radii are decreasing as
n
increases. Next, we consider the general case
n\geq 2
. To prove that

Particle approximation for a conditional McKean–Vlasov stochastic differential equation
Kai Du, Yunzhang Li, Yuyang Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we construct a type of interacting particle systems to approximate a class of stochastic different equations whose coefficients depend on the conditional probability distributions of the processes given partial observations. After proving the well-posedness and regularity of the particle systems, we establish a quantitative convergence result for the empirical measures of the particle systems in the Wasserstein space, as the number of particles increases. Moreover, we discuss an Euler–Maruyama scheme of the particle system and validate its strong convergence. A numerical experiment is conducted to illustrate our results. AMS subject classification : 60G25, 60G35, 60H35 Key words : conditional McKean–Vlasov SDE, conditional propagation of chaos, synchronous coupling, error estimates in Wasserstein space, Euler–Maruyama scheme.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with a conditional McKean–Vlasov stochastic differential equation (CMVSDE) in a completed probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{t}&=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s},\pi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{1}(X_{s},\pi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{2}(X_{s},\pi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\\[8.5359pt]
Y_{t}&=\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s},\pi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+B_{t},\qquad\quad\text{with }\ \pi_{s}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}\big[{X}_{s}\in\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}\big],\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}=\sigma\{Y_{s}:s\leq t\}
, and
(B,W)
is a standard Brownian motion.
The process
Y
serves as observations, resulting in a conditional law
\pi
of
X
that is involved in the dynamics of
X
.
We remark that when
h
vanishes, the equation (
1.1
) becomes the so-called McKean–Vlasov SDE with common noise which has been extensively studied in the literature, see e.g.
[
8
,
15
,
5
]
, and
[
12
]
for some recent progress.
As
h
emerges and relies on
X
, the increased reliance of the observation process
Y
on the state
X
substantially amplifies the nonlinearity within the dynamics.
Very recently, the well-posedness of (
1.1
) is obtained by Buckdahn, Li, and Ma
[
4
]
by employing Banach’s fixed point theorem and a localization technique.
The study on (
1.1
) is motivated by applications in mathematical finance and stochastic control.
For instance, Ma, Sun, and Zhou
[
24
]
formulated a linear form of CMVSDE (
1.1
) based on the Kyle–Back equilibrium model;
Buckdahn, Li, and Ma
[
3
]
studied a control problem arising in the mean-field game, in which the coefficients of the controlled dynamics linearly depend on the conditional law of the state.

In this paper, we aim to construct a particle system to approximate CMVSDE (
1.1
).
To approximate the conditional law
\pi
in an appropriate way,
we draw upon the methodology from nonlinear filtering theory and introduce the reference probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
by the Girsanov transformation:

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\Big|_{t}=\frac{1}{L_{t}}:=\exp\bigg(-\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s},\pi_{s})\mathrm{d}Y_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left|h(X_{s},\pi_{s})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg).

As a result, the observation process
Y
is a standard Brownian motion under
\mathbb{Q}
,
and by Bayes’ formula (or the Kallianpur–Striebel formula), one can write the conditional law
\pi
defined in (
1.1
) as

\pi_{s}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}[{X}_{s}\in\cdot\,|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[{L}_{s}\bm{1}_{{X}_{s}\in\,\cdot}\,|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[{L}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}]}.

(1.2)

Based on this transformation, we introduce a particle system
\{(X^{i,N},L^{i,N})\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
(\Omega,\mathbb{Q},\mathcal{F})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{t}^{i,N}&=x+\int_{0}^{t}(b-\sigma_{2}h)(X_{s}^{i,N},\pi_{s}^{N})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{1}(X_{s}^{i,N},\pi_{s}^{N})\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{2}(X_{s}^{i,N},\pi_{s}^{N})\mathrm{d}Y_{s},\\[8.5359pt]
L_{t}^{i,N}&=1+\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s}^{i,N},\pi_{s}^{N})L_{s}^{i,N}\mathrm{d}Y_{s},\\[8.5359pt]
\pi_{s}^{N}&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{L}^{i,N}_{s}\delta_{X_{s}^{i,N}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{L}^{i,N}_{s}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{w}_{s}^{i}\,\delta_{{X}_{s}^{i,N}}\quad\text{with}\quad{w}^{i}_{s}=\frac{{L}^{i,N}_{s}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{L}^{i,N}_{s}},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where
\{W^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are independent Brownian motions under
\mathbb{Q}
and independent of
Y
.
Intuitively, the process
L^{i}
plays a role of a likelihood weight assigned to the path
X^{i}
that represents the probability of that path being sampled from the conditional law.
We can illustrate the validity of this model from two perspectives.
First, if the coefficient
h
is a constant, then
\pi_{t}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i,N}}
is an unweighted empirical measure and converges, as
N\to\infty
, to the conditional law of a McKean–Vlasov process with common noise (cf.
[
8
]
).
Second, if the coefficients are all independent of the distribution
\pi
, then (
1.3
) becomes a standard model for particle filter, such as in
[
7
]
, Crisan, Gaines and Lyons construct a special sequence of branching particle systems to the solution of the Zakai equation; and in
[
9
]
, Crisan and Lyons studies use branching particle systems to study the solution of Kushner–Stratonovitch equation; and in
[
29
]
, some particle stability results are obtained by Whiteley.
However, in its full generality, the system (
1.3
) is highly coupled and nonlinear;
in particular, it is not Lipschitz even the coefficients are all bounded and smooth.
As an indispensable intermediate step,
we prove the uniqueness and existence of strong solutions to the particle system (
1.3
) by combining the multiplier method, the tightness argument, and the Yamada–Watanabe theorem.

In this paper, we establish the conditional propagation of chaos (CPoC) property of the particle system (
1.3
). Specifically, we prove that, as
N
increases, the weighted empirical measure
\pi_{t}^{N}
converges to the conditional law
\pi_{t}
in our target equation (
1.1
);
moreover, we provide a quantitative characterization for the convergence rate in terms of the Wasserstein distance.
We remark that there are many relevant results in the literature.
For instance, Zheng
[
30
]
applied CPoC to solve a class of quasilinear equations of parabolic type;
Kurtz and Xiong
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
constructed a sequence of weighted empirical measures from a particle system to approximate a class of nonlinear SPDE and verify the convergence in the weak topology;
Coghi and Flandolli
[
6
]
established the CPoC property of the particle system with common space-dependent noise;
and Erny, Löcherbach, and Loukianova
[
10
]
considered jumping particle systems, among others.
To our knowledge, our results differ significantly from existing ones in at least two aspects.
First, the particle system (
1.3
) is not covered by the existing models and its well-posedness result obtained here is new.
Second, we derive a quantitative CPoC in terms of the Wasserstein distance, whereas a related paper
[
17
]
proved the convergence in law without convergence rate and under stronger regularity requirements on the coefficients.
Technically, we develop a multiplier method to deal with the well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of non-Lipschitz particle systems.
In contrast to the localization technique used in
[
4
,
17
]
, our method stands out in its capacity to notably streamline calculations but also to generate new quantitative estimates.

It is worth noting that the CMVSDE (
1.1
) is closely related to the optimal control problem with partial observation (see e.g.
[
1
,
25
,
27
,
28
,
22
]
).
In particular, in recent years, there has been growing interest in the problem of mean-field control (MFC) and mean-field game (MFG) with partial observation (see e.g.
[
20
,
21
,
19
,
26
,
2
,
11
]
).
What’s more, noticing that
\pi_{t}
in (
1.1
) satisfies the Zakai equation, our approximation result may be helpful to compute some stochastic control problem driven by Zakai equation (see e.g.
[
1
,
23
]
) since our work is associated with particle filters, which use particle systems to approximate the solutions in nonlinear filtering problems.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we present the main results including well-posedness and CPoC property of (
1.3
), and error bound of the fully discretization scheme. Auxiliary lemmas are proved in Section 3. Section 4 is devoted to the proof of the main results. The analysis is confirmed by numerical examples in Section 5.

For convenience, in what follows we shall assume
\sigma_{2}=0
in (
1.1
). The case of
\sigma_{2}\neq 0
is known as the “correlated noise case” in the nonlinear filtering theory, which is well-understood and without substantial difficulties, although technically slightly more tedious (see e.g.
[
4
]
). We prefer not to pursue such complexity in this paper but focus on the conditional McKean-Vlasov nature instead, that is, we set that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma
and
\sigma_{2}=0
.

2
Main results

Throughout this paper, we assume that the coefficients
\phi=(b,\sigma,h):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is bounded and globally Lipschitz continuous: there is a constant
K\geq 0
such that

|\phi(x,\mu)|\leq K\quad\text{and}\quad|\phi(x,\mu)-\phi(y,\mu^{\prime})|\leq K(|x-y|+W_{1}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})),

where
W_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the
1
-Wasserstein distance of probability measure:

W_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf\big\{\mathbb{E}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|:\mathrm{law}(\xi_{1})=\mu_{1},\,\mathrm{law}(\xi_{2})=\mu_{2}\big\}.

2.1
Conditional propagation of chaos

First, we establish the well-posedness of the particle system (
1.3
).

Theorem 2.1
.

The particle system (
1.3
) admits a unique strong solution
\{(X^{i,N},L^{i,N})\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

Next we use the particle system (
1.3
) to approximate the signal
X
in (
1.1
) and the conditional measure
\pi
in (
1.2
). To do this, we apply the synchronous coupling technique to generate
\{\bar{X}^{i},\bar{L}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, which is the solution to SDE (
1.1
) with the driven terms replaced by the Brownian motion
\{W^{i},Y\}_{i=1}^{N}
appearing in the particle system (
1.3
).
Since
\{W^{i},Y\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a standard Brownian motion, we know that for all
1\leq i\leq N
, the conditional distribution of the synchronous coupling signal
\mathscr{L}^{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{X}^{i}_{t}|\mathcal{F}^{Y}_{t})
is identical to the conditional distribution of the original signal
\pi
defined in (
1.2
).
For any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we define for all
s\in[0,T]

\bar{\pi}_{s}^{N}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{s}^{i}\delta_{\bar{X}_{s}^{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{s}^{i}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{w}_{s}^{i}\,\delta_{\bar{X}_{s}^{i}}\quad\text{with}\quad\bar{w}^{i}_{s}=\frac{\bar{L}^{i}_{s}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}^{i}_{s}}.

(2.1)

We have the following approximation theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a constant
C=C(K,T,n)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i,N}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Psi_{t}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},\quad\quad\forall i=1,2,...,N,

where
n
is the dimension of synchronous coupling signal
\bar{X}^{i}
and the multiplier is defined by

\Psi_{t}=\exp\bigg\{-\alpha N\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k,N}\big|^{2}\Big)\right]\mathrm{d}s\bigg\},

with
\alpha=\alpha(K,T)
and the random normalized weights
{w}^{i}_{s}
and
\bar{w}^{i}_{s}
given by (
1.3
) and (
2.1
).

Remark 2.1
.

If
h
is a constant or all the coefficients are independent of
\pi
, then multiplier
\Psi_{t}
can be just chosen as
\Psi_{t}=e^{-\alpha t}
with
\alpha=\alpha(K,T)>0
. Then we can get that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i,N}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},\quad\quad\forall i=1,2,...,N,

where
C=C(K,T,n)
.

In view of Theorem
2.2
, we can get the mean convergence rate of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}|^{2}]
as follows by estimating multipliers.

Corollary 2.1
.

For all
i=1,2,...,N
and
\beta>0
, there exists
C=C(\beta,K,T,n)
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max\limits_{t\in[0,\tau_{\beta}\wedge T]}\big|X_{t}^{i,N}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},

where the stopping time is defined as

\tau_{\beta}:=\inf\bigg\{t:N\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k,N}\big|^{2}\Big)\right]\mathrm{d}s>\beta\bigg\},

with

\mathbb{Q}[\tau_{\beta}>T]>1-\frac{C}{\beta}.

Furthermore, it holds that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi^{N}_{t},\pi_{t}\big)^{2}\Big]+\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i,N}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq C\bigl|\ln(N)\bigr|^{-\frac{1}{2p}},\quad\quad\forall p>1,

where
C=C(K,T,n,p)
,
\pi_{t}^{N}
is the empirical measure defined in (
1.3
) and
\pi_{t}
is the target measure defined in (
1.2
).

Remark 2.2
.

The convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(|\ln(N)|^{-\frac{1}{2}+})
results from the fact that the inverse of multiplier
\Psi^{-1}
is not bounded, and even not integrable in general, which makes the estimation of convergence rate become intractable. However, we observe that
\ln(\Psi^{-1})
is integrable, which allows us to get the convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(|\ln(N)|^{-\frac{1}{2}+})
. Although this convergence rate is much slower compared to the classical result, it seems to be the first result in regard to the strong convergence rate for the mean-field particle filters. Moreover, our numerical scheme works very well in some examples even in the cases when coefficients are not bounded (see Section
5
).

2.2
Sequential importance sampling: full discretization

Next, we use the sequential importance sampling to fully discretize the SDE (
1.1
), which is a Monte Carlo algorithm based on the CPoC property and the Euler scheme. To approximate the random weight
\{\bar{L}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, it is equivalent to consider the random normalized weight
\{\bar{w}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
define in (
2.1
). By Ito’s formula, the dynamic of
\bar{w}_{t}^{i}
is governed by

\mathrm{d}\bar{w}_{t}^{i}=\bar{w}_{t}^{i}\,H\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\bar{w}_{t}^{i}\,M^{\text{T}}\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N}\right)\mathrm{d}Y_{t},\qquad\bar{w}^{i}_{0}=1,

(2.2)

where the coefficient
(M,H):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}
is the function satisfying

M(x,\pi)=h(x,\pi)-\int h(x,\pi)\pi(\mathrm{d}x),

and

H(x,\pi)=\Big|\int h(x,\pi)\pi(\mathrm{d}x)\Big|^{2}-h^{\text{T}}(x,\pi)\int h(x,\pi)\pi(\mathrm{d}x).

Then the full discretization for the synchronous coupling signal
\bar{X}^{i}
and for the random normalized weight
\bar{w}^{i}
in (
2.2
) is:
\forall 1\leq i\leq N
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{i,\Delta}=b\big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i},\qquad X_{0}^{i,\Delta}=x,\\[8.5359pt]
&\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{i,\Delta}=w_{t}^{i,\Delta}H\big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)\mathrm{d}t+w_{t}^{i,\Delta}M^{\text{T}}\big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)\mathrm{d}Y_{t},\qquad w^{i,\Delta}_{0}=1,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

where

•

\Delta\in(0,T)
is the size of time step, and
\theta:[0,T]\times(0,T)\rightarrow[0,T]
is a measurable function such that

\theta(t,\Delta)=\Big\lfloor\frac{t}{\Delta}\Big\rfloor\Delta,

with
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
being the floor function;

•

the empirical measure with random weights is defined by

\pi_{t}^{N,\Delta}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}w_{t}^{i,\Delta}\,\,\delta_{X_{t}^{i,\Delta}}\,.

We can prove the following approximation results.

Theorem 2.3
.

For all
\Delta\in(0,T)
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{s\in[0,T]}\big|\bar{X}_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq C\Big\{\big[\ln\big(\Delta^{-1}\big)\big]^{-\frac{1}{2p}}+\big[\ln\big(N\big)\big]^{-\frac{1}{2p}}\Big\},\quad\quad\forall p>1,

where
C=C(K,T,n,p)
.
In particular, if we take
\Delta=\frac{T}{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\big|\bar{X}_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq C\big[\ln\big(N\big)\big]^{-\frac{1}{2p}},\quad\quad\forall p>1,

where
C=C(K,T,n,p)
.

3
Auxiliary lemmas

3.1
Estimates for the weight of empirical measure

Suppose
\{\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a solution of the following stochastic equation: for all
1\leq i\leq N
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}=b(\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\tilde{\pi}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\tilde{\pi}_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i},\qquad\tilde{X}_{0}^{i}=x,\\
\mathrm{d}\tilde{L}_{t}^{i}=h(\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\tilde{\pi}_{t})\tilde{L}_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}Y_{t},\qquad\tilde{L}_{0}^{i}=1,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
\tilde{\pi}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a given random probability measure. For simplicity, we write
(X^{i},L^{i},w^{i})
instead of
(X^{i,N},L^{i,N},w^{i,N})
in (
1.3
) and denote

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}^{N}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{L}^{i}\delta_{\tilde{X}^{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{L}^{i}},\quad z^{i}=\ln(w^{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{z}^{i}=\ln(\tilde{w}^{i}).

(3.2)

Then we have the following estimate of 1-Wasserstein measure.

Lemma 3.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\pi^{N},\tilde{\pi}^{N}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\big|\big|X^{i}\big|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}^{i}\big|X^{i}-\tilde{X}^{i}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}w^{i}\vee\tilde{w}^{i}\big|z^{i}-\tilde{z}^{i}\big|\big|X^{i}\big|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}^{i}\big|X^{i}-\tilde{X}^{i}\big|.

Proof.

Define

\underline{\pi}^{N}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{L}^{i}\delta_{X^{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{L}^{i}}.

By triangle inequality of Wasserstein space and Kantorovich–Rubinstein formula, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\pi^{N},\tilde{\pi}^{N}\big)\leq

\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\pi^{N},\underline{\pi}^{N}\big)+W_{1}\big(\underline{\pi}^{N},\tilde{\pi}^{N}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{\phi\in\text{Lip}(1)}\bigg\{\bigg|\int\phi(x)\pi^{N}(\mathrm{d}x)-\int\phi(x)\underline{\pi}^{N}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\inf_{\phi\in\text{Lip}(1)}\bigg\{\bigg|\int\phi(x)\underline{\pi}^{N}(\mathrm{d}x)-\int\phi(x)\tilde{\pi}^{N}(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\big|\big|X^{i}\big|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}^{i}\big|X^{i}-\tilde{X}^{i}\big|,

where the notation
\text{Lip}(1)
is the space of all Lipschitz functions with Lipschitz constant 1.

Using the fact
|e^{x}-e^{y}|\leq e^{x\vee y}|x-y|
, it yields that

\displaystyle\big|w^{i}-\tilde{w}^{i}\big|\big|X^{i}\big|\leq w^{i}\vee\tilde{w}^{i}\big|z^{i}-\tilde{z}^{i}\big|\big|X^{i}\big|,

then the proof of Lemma
3.1
is completed.
∎

Recalling the definitions of
M
and
H
in (
2.2
), it is easy to see that
M
and
H
are bounded functions. Using Ito’s formula, we can get

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}w_{t}^{i}=w_{t}^{i}H(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\mathrm{d}t+w_{t}^{i}M^{\text{T}}(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\mathrm{d}Y_{t}.

(3.3)

By Ito’s formula (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}z_{t}^{i}=R(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\mathrm{d}t+M^{\text{T}}(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\mathrm{d}Y_{t},

where

R(x,\pi)=H(x,\pi)-\frac{1}{2}|M(x,\pi)|^{2}.

Recalling the boundedness and Lipshitz continuity of
h
, we can observe that
R
and
M
is bounded and

\displaystyle\left|R(x,\pi)-R(x^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\right|+\left|M(x,\pi)-M(x^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\right|\leq C\left(|x-x^{\prime}|+W_{1}\big(\pi,\pi^{\prime}\big)\right).

(3.4)

where
C=C(K)
is a constant.

The following estimate is significant for our proof of uniqueness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Denote

\Gamma_{t}=\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s\bigg),

then for all
t\in[0,T]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\big|X_{t}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(C+\gamma_{s}\big)\big|X_{s}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{s}\Big(X_{s}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Big(\sigma(X_{s}^{i},\pi_{s}^{N})-\sigma(\tilde{X}_{s}^{i},\tilde{\pi_{s}})\Big)\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{i}.

and

\displaystyle\big|z_{t}^{i}-\tilde{z}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\big|X_{s}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}+W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(C+\gamma_{s}\big)\big|z_{s}^{i}-\tilde{z}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\,\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{s}\Big(z_{s}^{i}-\tilde{z}_{s}^{i}\Big)\Big(M(X_{s}^{i},\pi_{s}^{N})-M(\tilde{X}_{s}^{i},\tilde{\pi_{s}})\Big)^{\text{T}}\mathrm{d}Y_{s},

where
C=C(K)
is a constant.

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote

\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}=X_{t}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\quad\Delta\tilde{b}_{t}^{i}=b(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})-b(\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\tilde{\pi_{t}}),\quad\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{i}=\sigma(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})-\sigma(\tilde{X}_{t}^{i},\tilde{\pi_{t}}),

Using Itô’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\big|\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{i}\Gamma_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i}+2\Big(\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Delta\tilde{b}_{t}^{i}\Gamma_{t}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\Tr\left(\left(\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{i}\right)^{\text{T}}\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{t}^{i}\right)\Gamma_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\gamma_{t}\big|\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}\mathrm{d}t.

With the fact that
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\big|\Delta\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}+\big|\Delta\tilde{b}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}+\Tr\left(\left(\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{s}^{i}\right)^{\text{T}}\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{s}^{i}\right)\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\Delta\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Delta\tilde{\sigma}_{s}^{i}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{i}+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}\big|\Delta\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.5)

Combining with (
3.1
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
(b,\sigma)
, we complete our first estimate in Lemma
3.2
. The estimate of
\big|z_{t}^{i}-\tilde{z}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{t}
is similar because of the Lipschtiz continuity of
(R,H)
.
∎

Next lemma shows that
\pi_{t}^{N}
is continuous in probability.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that there exists
C=C(K,T)
satisfying for all
1\leq i\leq N

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{4}+N^{4}\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big]\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{4}+N^{4}\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big]\leq C|t-s|^{2}.

Then for
\tilde{\pi}^{N}
defined in (
3.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{N},\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{N}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq C\,|t-s|,\quad\forall\,s,t\in[0,T],

where
C=C(K,T)
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{N},\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{N}\big)\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|.

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{N},\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{N}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\bigg]+C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{i}-\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{j}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{j}\big|^{2}\bigg]+C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{j}-\tilde{w}_{s}^{j}\big|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\tilde{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{j}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{j}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\tilde{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\tilde{w}_{t}^{j}-\tilde{w}_{s}^{j}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.6)

By our conditions, we can conclude the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.2
Wasserstein convergence of weighted empirical measures

The convergence of
unweighted empirical measures in the Wasserstein distance has been extensively investigated in the literature. Here we follow the idea of J. Horowitz and R. Karandikar (
[
13
]
), which helps us to obtain the following mean convergence rate of the Wasserstein space first.

Lemma 3.4
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[W_{1}\left(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}\right)^{4}\right]\leq CN^{-\frac{16}{7n+8}},

where
C=C(K,T,n)
,
n
is the dimension of the signal process,
\pi_{t}^{N}
is the empirical measure defined in (
1.3
) and
\pi_{t}
is the original measure defined in (
1.2
).

Proof.

We write
\Phi_{d}\sim N(0,d^{2}I)
to indicate
\Phi_{d}
is the normal distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with mean
0
and variance
d^{2}I
.
\phi_{d}
is the probability density function corresponding to
\Phi_{d}
. For any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, let
\mu^{d}=\Phi_{d}*\mu
be the convolution of
\Phi_{d}
and
\mu
.
Consequently, we can observe that

\displaystyle W_{1}\left(\mu,\mu_{d}\right)\leq Cd.

(3.7)

To see this, let
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
be the independent random variables with laws
\mu
and
\phi_{d}
respectively, then
(\xi_{1},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})
is a coupling of
\mu
and
\mu_{d}
, and

W_{1}\left(\mu,\mu_{d}\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|\phi_{d}\mathrm{d}x=Cd.

Hence, by triangle inequality of Wasserstein space, we have

\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}\big)^{4}\leq 27W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)^{2}+27W_{1}\big(\pi_{t},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)^{2}+27W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d}\big)^{2}.

(3.8)

Let
f_{t}^{N,d}
and
f_{t}^{d}
be the probability density functions of
\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d}
and
\pi^{d}_{t}
. By Kantorovich-Rubinstein theorem and Holder’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\psi\in\text{Lip}(1)}\bigg\{\int\psi(x)f_{t}^{N,d}(x)\mathrm{d}x-\int\psi(x)f_{t}^{d}(x)\mathrm{d}x\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\int|x|\big|f_{t}^{N,d}(x)-f_{t}^{d}(x)\big|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int\big(|x|^{3n+7}+1\big)\big|f_{t}^{N,d}(x)-f_{t}^{d}(x)\big|^{4}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)^{4}\Big]\leq C\int\big(|x|^{3n+7}+1\big)\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|f_{t}^{N,d}(x)-f_{t}^{d}(x)\big|^{4}\Big]\mathrm{d}x.

(3.9)

By definition, we can actually get the expression of
f_{t}^{N,d}(x)
and
f_{t}^{d}(x)
:

\displaystyle f_{t}^{N,d}(x)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i}\big)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad f_{t}^{d}(x)=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]},

where we use the fact that for all
i\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big].

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\big|f_{t}^{N,d}(x)-f_{t}^{d}(x)\big|^{4}\leq

\displaystyle 8\,\bigg|\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}}\bigg|^{4}

\displaystyle+8\bigg|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\right)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]\bigg|^{4}\bigg|\Big(\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}}\Big)^{-1}-\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]\Big)^{-1}\bigg|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}+A_{2}.

(3.10)

Firstly, the term
A_{1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[A_{1}\big]\leq C\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i})-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]\Big|^{8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{-8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.11)

Notice for all
1\leq i\leq N
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\big|^{-8}\bigg]\leq C.

Then by Jensen’s inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{-8}\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\big|^{-8}\bigg]\leq C.

(3.12)

Note that
\{(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{L}_{t}^{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
are the i.i.d. random variables under
\mathbb{P}\big[\cdot\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]
. We denote

g_{d}\left(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\right)=\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i}\big)-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big],

then we can deduce that
\{g_{d}(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{L}_{t}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
are the i.i.d. r.v.’s under
\mathbb{P}\big[\cdot\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i})-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]\Big|^{8}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N^{8}}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\leq N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\prod\limits_{m=1}^{4}\Big|g_{d}\big(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{i_{m}},\bar{L}_{t}^{i_{m}}\big)\Big|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\bigg].

(3.13)

The right hand side in (
3.2
) has the upper bound estimates:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\prod\limits_{m=1}^{4}\Big|g_{d}\big(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{i_{m}},\bar{L}_{t}^{i_{m}}\big)\Big|^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\bigg]\leq\prod\limits_{m=1}^{4}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|g_{d}\big(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{i_{m}},\bar{L}_{t}^{i_{m}}\big)\big|^{8}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]^{\frac{1}{4}}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left|g_{d}\left(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{1},\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\right)\right|^{8}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right].

According to (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi_{d}(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{i})-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big]\Big|^{8}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\bigg]\leq\frac{C}{N^{4}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|g_{d}\left(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{1},\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\right)\big|^{8}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\Big].

(3.14)

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.14
), we can get the upper bound of
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[A_{1}]
with the help of Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[A_{1}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\big|g_{d}\left(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{1},\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\right)\big|^{4}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]\Big|^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\big|g_{d}\big(x,\bar{X}_{t}^{1},\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|^{8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\Big|^{8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.15)

Next, we estimate
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[A_{2}]
by Cauchy’s inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[A_{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}}-\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]}\bigg|^{8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]\Big|^{8}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{16}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]\Big|^{-16}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{-16}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\big|^{8}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.16)

By the similar calculation that deduces (
3.12
) and (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]\Big|^{-16}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{-16}\bigg]\leq C,

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\Big|\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\big]-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\Big|^{16}\bigg]\leq\frac{C}{N^{8}}.

(3.18)

In view of (
3.2
), (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[A_{2}\big]\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\big|^{8}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.19)

By (
3.2
), (
3.2
) and (
3.19
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|f_{t}^{N,d}(x)-f_{t}^{d}(x)\big|^{4}\Big]\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\big|^{8}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

According to (
3.9
) and Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)^{4}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\int\big(|x|^{3n+7}+1\big)\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\big|^{8}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}}\left(\int\left(|x|^{6n+16}+1\right)\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\right)\right|^{8}\right]\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.20)

By simple calculation, we can check that

\displaystyle\phi_{d}(x)^{8}=Cd^{-7n}\phi_{\frac{d}{2\sqrt{2}}}(x).

Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\int\big(|x|^{6n+16}+1\big)\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\phi_{d}\left(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\right)\right|^{8}\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,Cd^{-7n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|^{8}\int\big(|x|^{6n+16}+1\big)\phi_{\frac{d}{2\sqrt{2}}}\big(x-\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big)\mathrm{d}x\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,Cd^{-7n}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\bar{L}_{t}^{1}\big|^{8}\int\big(|x|^{6n+16}+\big|\bar{X}_{t}^{1}\big|^{6n+16}+1\big)\phi_{\frac{d}{2\sqrt{2}}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\Big]\leq Cd^{-7n}.

In view of (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N,d},\pi^{d}_{t}\big)^{4}\Big]\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}d^{\frac{7}{2}n}}.

(3.21)

Choosing
d=N^{-\frac{4}{7n+8}}
and combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}\big)^{4}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{16}{7n+8}}.

At last, we notice constant
C=C(K,T,n)
, so we can conclude that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}\big)^{4}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{16}{7n+8}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.1
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We first give the proof of uniqueness of solution to SDE (

Theorem 4.1
.

Given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\},\{W_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},Y)
, suppose that
\{(X_{t}^{i},L_{t}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{(\hat{X}_{t}^{i},\hat{L}_{t}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
are two strong solutions to the SDE (
1.3

i=1,2,...,N
,

(X_{t}^{i},L_{t}^{i})=(\hat{X}_{t}^{i},\hat{L}_{t}^{i}),\quad\quad\mathbb{Q}-a.e.

Proof.

Denote

\hat{\pi}_{t}^{N}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{L}_{t}^{i}\delta_{\hat{X}_{t}^{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{L}_{t}^{i}}

and define the function

\Phi_{t}=\exp\bigg(-\alpha N\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\hat{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big]\mathrm{d}s\bigg),

where
\alpha>0
is an undetermined constant.
Denote

\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}=X_{t}^{i}-\hat{X}_{t}^{i},\quad\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{i}=z_{t}^{i}-\hat{z}_{t}^{i},\quad\Delta\hat{\sigma}_{t}^{i}=\sigma(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})-\sigma(\hat{X}_{t}^{i},\hat{\pi}_{t}^{N}),\quad\Delta\hat{M}_{t}^{i}=M(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})-M(\hat{X}_{t}^{i},\hat{\pi}_{t}^{N}).

Utilizing Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\hat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right)^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\Phi_{s}\Big(\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Delta\hat{\sigma}_{s}^{i}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{i}.

(4.1)

According to Lemma
3.1
, it holds that

\displaystyle W_{1}\Big(\pi_{t}^{N},\hat{\pi}_{t}^{N}\Big)^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq 2\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|X_{t}^{i}\big|\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{i}\big|\big|w_{t}^{i}\vee\hat{w}_{t}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\Phi_{t}+2\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\hat{w}_{t}^{i}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\Phi_{t}.

(4.2)

By Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|X_{t}^{i}\big|\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{i}\big|\big|w_{t}^{i}\vee\hat{w}_{t}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\big|X_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\big|w_{t}^{k}\vee\hat{w}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\hat{w}_{t}^{i}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\big|\bigg)^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\big|\hat{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t}.

(4.4)

Plugging (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) into (
4.1
) and summing over
i
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\right|^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(1+N\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\big|\hat{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\bigg)\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\Delta\hat{X}_{s}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\Delta\hat{\sigma}_{s}^{i}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{i}

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\hat{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\big|X_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\hat{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\bigg]\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

(4.5)

Similarly, it yields from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+N\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\hat{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta\hat{z}_{s}^{i}\Gamma_{s}\Big(\Delta\hat{M}_{s}^{i}\Big)^{\text{T}}\mathrm{d}Y_{s}

\displaystyle+\big(C-\alpha\big)\int_{0}^{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\hat{w}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\bigg]\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

(4.6)

Combining (
4.1
) and (
4.1
) and taking

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|\Delta\hat{X}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t}+\big|\Delta\hat{z}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Phi_{t}\Big]\leq 0.

(4.7)

Since

Let us recall the classic result of the equivalence of the weak solution of a functional SDE and the solution to the corresponding local martingale problem. More details can be found in

.

Theorem 4.2
.

The existence of the weak solution
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q},\{X_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{L_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{W_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},Y)
to the SDE (
1.3
) is equivalent to the existence of a probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfying that for all
f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle f(X_{t},L_{t})-f(X_{0},L_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}(Af)(s,X,L)\mathrm{d}s\in\mathcal{M}^{c,loc}(\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}),

(4.8)

where

•

X=(X^{1},\cdots,X^{N})
and
L=(L^{1},\cdots,L^{N})
;

•

\mathcal{M}^{c,loc}(\tilde{\mathbb{Q}})
is the family of all the continuous local martingales under the probability
\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}
;

•

the operator
A
is defined by

\displaystyle(Af)(t,X,L)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}b^{\text{T}}(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(X_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Tr\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(X_{t},L_{t})\sigma\sigma^{\text{T}}(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big|h(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N})\big|^{2}\big|L_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial l_{i}^{2}}(X_{t},L_{t})

with

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i,1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i,n}}\right)^{\text{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}_{i}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}_{i,1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i,1}\partial x_{i,n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i,n}\partial x_{i,1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}_{i,n}}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

and

\pi_{t}^{N}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{t}^{i}\delta_{X_{t}^{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{t}^{i}}.

Now we show the existence of the strong solution.

Theorem 4.3
.

There exists a weak solution
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q},\{X_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{L_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\{W_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},Y)

1.3

4.2

First we can construct a series of stochastic process

\forall\,t\in[{jT/2^{k}},{(j+1)T/2^{k}}]
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{t}^{i,k}=X_{jT/2^{k}}^{i,k}+b\big(X_{jT/2^{k}}^{i,k},\pi^{N,k}_{jT/2^{k}}\big)\Big(t-\frac{j}{2^{k}}T\Big)+\sigma\big(X_{jT/2^{k}}^{i,k},\pi^{N,k}_{jT/2^{k}}\big)\Big(W^{i}_{t}-W^{i}_{jT/2^{k}}\Big),\\

\end{aligned}\right.

is uniformly integrable. So we can get that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\big|\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{X}_{r}^{i}\big|\Big]\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\big|\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{X}_{r}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{4}}\rightarrow 0,

and

\displaystyle\max\limits_{0\leq r\leq t}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi_{r}^{N,k_{n}},\pi_{r}^{N}\big)\Big]\leq C\max\limits_{0\leq r\leq t}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi_{r}^{N,k_{n}},\pi_{r}^{N}\big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0.

(4.17)

By the similar calculation that deduces (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\big|(b,\sigma\sigma^{\text{T}})(\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}})-(b,\sigma\sigma^{\text{T}})(\hat{X}_{r}^{i},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N})\big|\Big]\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}\Big|\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{X}_{r}^{i}\Big|\mathrm{d}r\bigg]+C\,\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\bigg[\int_{s}^{t}W_{1}\big(\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N}\big)\mathrm{d}r\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\left|\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{X}_{r}^{i}\right|\right]+C\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N}\big)\Big].

(4.18)

It follows from (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{s}^{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\big|b(\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}},\pi_{r}^{N,k_{n}})-b(\hat{X}_{r}^{i},\pi_{r}^{N})\big|+\big|\sigma\sigma^{\text{T}}(\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}},\pi_{r}^{N,k_{n}})-\sigma\sigma^{\text{T}}(\hat{X}_{r}^{i},\pi_{r}^{N})\big|\Big]\mathrm{d}r\rightarrow 0.

Similarly, by the integrable of
|L_{t}^{i}|^{4}
and boundedness of
h
, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\left||h|^{2}(\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}})-|h|^{2}(\hat{X}^{i}_{r},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N})\right|\Big|\hat{L}_{r}^{i}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\big|\hat{L}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}\big|^{2}-\big|\hat{L}_{r}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big|\Big]\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\big|h(\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}})-h(\hat{X}^{i}_{r},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N})\big|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\big|\hat{L}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{L}_{r}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\big|\hat{X}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{X}_{r}^{i}\big|^{2}+\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\big|\hat{L}_{r}^{i,k_{n}}-\hat{L}_{r}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\max\limits_{s\leq r\leq t}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N,k_{n}},\hat{\pi}_{r}^{N}\big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\}.

By the uniform convergence of
T^{i,k_{n}}
and (

.

Finally, the weak existence of the solution to SDE (

(\hat{X}^{(k_{n})},\hat{L}^{k_{n}})
and

∎

By the results of Theorem

4.2
Proof of particle approximation

The following lemma guarantees the well-posedness of the limiting process.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a probability space
({\Omega},\mathcal{{F}},\mathbb{{Q}},\{\mathcal{{F}}_{t}\},\{{W}_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},{Y})
such that the following mean-field SDE:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{X}_{t}^{i}=b\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i},\qquad\bar{X}_{0}^{i}=x,\\[8.5359pt]
\mathrm{d}\bar{L}_{t}^{i}=h(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t})\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}Y_{t},\qquad\bar{L}_{0}^{i}=1,\ \ \ i=1,2,...,N,\ \ \ t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

(4.19)

has a unique solution
\{(\bar{X}^{i},\bar{L}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in this probability space, where
\{W_{t}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},Y
are the independent Brownian motions in this probability space with the natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\{(W^{i}_{s},Y_{s}):s\leq t,\,\,1\leq i\}
, and
\pi_{t}
is the random probability measure such that for all
i\geq 1
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}\pi_{t}=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi(\bar{X}_{t}^{i})|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]},\qquad\forall\,\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4.20)

The proof of lemma
4.1
is not difficult to obtain by similar arguments in
[
4
]
or
[
16
]
. We give some remarks here about the limiting process (
4.19
). In fact, the solution
\{(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\bar{L}_{t}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of (
4.19
) has the same distribution under
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y})
. Therefore
\forall\,i\geq 1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}\pi_{t}=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}\phi(\bar{X}_{t}^{i})|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\bar{L}_{t}^{i}|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right]},\quad\forall\,\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This symmetry is used in our following proof.

What’s more, we point out that we can construct the weak solution of the partially observed system from the solution of (
4.19
). Given
i\geq 1
, we denote

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}=L_{T}^{i}.

Using Girsanov theorem, we can easily check that
(\bar{X}^{i},Y)
is the solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{X}_{t}^{i}=b\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{i},\quad\bar{X}_{0}^{i}=x,\\
\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=h\left(\bar{X}_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad Y_{0}=0,\ \ \ t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

(4.21)

where
W_{t}^{i}
,
B_{t}
are independent standard Brownian motion under
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}^{i})
. By Kallianpur-Striebel formula, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}\pi_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{i}}\left[\phi(\bar{X}_{t}^{i})|\mathcal{F}_{t}^{Y}\right],\quad\forall\,\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

In other words,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}^{i},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\},\bar{X}_{t}^{i},Y_{t},W_{t}^{i},B_{t})
is a weak solution of the conditional McKean-Vlasov SDE (
4.21

Now we begin our proof of particle approximation.
For a given
N
, we simply denote
\{(X^{i},L^{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
instead of

Lemma 4.2
.

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi_{t}^{N},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N}\big)^{2}\Psi_{t}\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},

where
C=C(K,T,n)
is the constant.

Proof.

With the similar calculation in (
4.2
)-(
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle W_{1}\Big(\pi_{t}^{N},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N}\Big)^{2}\Psi_{t}\leq 2\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big(\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Psi_{t}+2\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Psi_{t}.

(4.22)

By Ito’s formula,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Psi_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Psi_{t}\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\big(X_{t}^{j}\big)^{\text{T}}b_{t}^{j}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2\Psi_{t}\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}H_{t}^{j}+\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\bar{H}_{t}^{j}\Big)\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+2\Psi_{t}\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\Delta\bar{R}_{t}^{k}+\big(\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big)^{T}\Delta\bar{b}_{t}^{k}\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\Psi_{t}\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\big|\sigma_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\Psi_{t}\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\big|w_{t}^{j}M_{t}^{j}+\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\bar{M}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}\Psi_{t}+\mathrm{d}V_{t},

Denote

By BDG inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|B_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{t}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big)^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{C}{N^{4}}.

(4.23)

By the Lipschitz continuity and boundedness of

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[B_{s}^{j}\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big]\mathrm{d}s+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[B_{s}^{j}W_{1}\big(\pi_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}\big)^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\alpha N\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|\tilde{X}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\bigg]\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)B_{s}^{j}\bigg]\mathrm{d}s.

(4.24)

By the triangle inequality of Wasserstein distance,

\displaystyle W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}\right)^{2}\leq 2W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\bar{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right)^{2}+2W_{1}\left(\bar{\pi}_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}\right)^{2}.

(4.25)

Taking

4.2

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}N\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}B_{s}^{j}W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}\big)^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq CN\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{s\in[0,t]}\big|B_{s}^{j}\big|^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{s\in[0,t]}W_{1}\big(\bar{\pi}_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}\big)^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then it follows from (
4.22

4.23

that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\Big(\pi_{t}^{N},\bar{\pi}_{t}^{N}\Big)^{2}\Psi_{t}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}B_{t}^{j}\Big(\big|\Delta\bar{z}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|\Delta\bar{X}_{t}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big]\leq CN^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},

the proof is completed.
∎

.

(4.31)

Consequently, by Cauchy’s inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\right\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big(\big|X_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big)\mathbbm{1}_{\big\{\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\big\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}\big|^{2q}+\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2q}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbbm{1}_{\big\{\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\big\}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{Q}\Big(\min_{t\in[0,T]}\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.32)

With the help of Chebyshev’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\Big[\min_{t\in[0,T]}\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}\bigg[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\{N\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\big|w_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\big(1+|X_{t}^{i}|^{2}\big)\Big\}>\beta\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}N\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\big|w_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{t}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big].

Using BDG inequality and Gronwall’s inequality, we have the following standard estimates

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\Big[\max\limits_{s\in[0,T]}\Big\{\big|w_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}+\big|\bar{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{2}\Big\}\Big]+\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\Big[\max\limits_{s\in[0,T]}\Big\{\big|w_{s}^{i}\big|^{4}+\big|\bar{w}_{s}^{i}\big|^{4}\Big\}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}},\quad\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\Big[\max\limits_{s\in[0,T]}\big|X_{s}^{j}\big|^{4}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C.

(4.33)

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\Big[\min_{t\in[0,T]}\Psi_{t}<e^{-\beta}\Big]\leq\frac{C}{\beta}.

(4.34)

By the definition of
\tau_{\beta}
and (
4.31
), we get

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,\tau_{\beta}\wedge T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\Psi_{t}\geq e^{-\beta}\right\}}\Big]\leq Ce^{\beta}N^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}},

and by (
4.34
), we have

\mathbb{Q}(\tau_{\beta}>T)\geq 1-\frac{C}{\beta}.

Then we prove the first estimate in Corollary (
2.1
).

Next, by inequality (
4.31
), (
4.2
) and (
4.34
), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\right|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\Psi_{t}\leq e^{-\alpha_{N}}\right\}}\Big]+\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\Psi_{t}>e^{-\alpha_{N}}\right\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\beta}N^{-\frac{8}{7n+8}}+C\beta^{-\frac{1}{2p}}.

If we take
\beta=\Lambda\ln(N)
, where
\Lambda=\Lambda(K,T)
is a constant small enough, then we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\big|X_{t}^{i}-\bar{X}^{i}_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]\leq C\big[\ln\big(N\big)\big]^{-\frac{1}{2p}},\quad\quad\forall p>1.

Similarly, by Lemma
4.2
, we have

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq C\big[\ln\big(N\big)\big]^{-\frac{1}{2p}},\quad\quad\forall p>1,

then the proof is completed.
∎

We define

z_{t}^{i,\Delta}=\ln\big(w_{t}^{i,\Delta}\big).

With the help of Ito’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}z_{t}^{i,\Delta}=R\Big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N}\Big)+M^{\text{T}}\Big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N}\Big)\mathrm{d}Y_{t}.

Denote multiplier

\Psi_{t}^{\Delta}=\exp\bigg\{-\alpha N\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\frac{1}{N^{2}}+\Big(\big|w_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}+\big|w_{s}^{k,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\big|X_{s}^{k}\big|^{2}\Big)\right]\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}.

By the boundedness of
b
and
\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|X_{s}^{i}-X_{\theta(s,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big]+2\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|X_{\theta(s,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\left|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\right|^{2}\right]+C\Delta.

(4.35)

With the similar calculation that deduces Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[W_{1}\big(\pi_{s}^{N,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(s,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq C\Delta.

Similarly, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\pi_{\theta(s,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\right)^{2}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[W_{1}\left(\pi_{s}^{N},\pi_{s}^{N,\Delta}\right)^{2}\right]+C\Delta.

(4.36)

Combing (
4.3
) and (
4.36
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\Big|f\big(X_{t}^{i},\pi_{t}^{N}\big)-f\big(X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)\Big|^{2}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|X_{t}^{i}-X_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}+W_{1}\big(\pi_{t}^{N},\pi_{\theta(t,\Delta)}^{N,\Delta}\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\big|X_{t}^{i}-X_{t}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}+W_{1}\big(\pi_{t}^{N},\pi_{t}^{N,\Delta}\big)^{2}\Big]+C\Delta,

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\max\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\big|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\big|^{2}\Psi_{s}^{\Delta}\Big]\leq C\Delta,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\max\limits_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,\Delta}\right|^{2}\right]\leq C\left|\ln\left(\Delta^{-1}\right)\right|^{-\frac{1}{2p}}.

5
Numerical Experiment

Although our theoretical error bound is
\mathcal{O}([\ln(N)]^{-\frac{1}{2}+})
, the numerical scheme works very well in our numerical experiments (even in the cases when coefficients are not bounded).
Here we exhibit an example in which the solution can be written down explicitly.
Let us consider the following CMVSDE in the completed probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{Q},\mathcal{F})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{t}&=2x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b\left(s,X_{s},\pi_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{1}\left(s,X_{s},\pi_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{2}\left(s,X_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}Y_{s},\\
L_{t}&=1+\int_{0}^{t}h\left(s,X_{s},\pi_{s}\right)L_{s}\mathrm{d}Y_{s},\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
(W,Y)
is the standard
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued Brownian motion under
\mathbb{Q}
, and the mean-field term
\pi_{s}:=\mathscr{L}^{\mathbb{P}}(X_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y})
with
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}=L_{T}
, and the coefficients are defined by

b(\omega,s,x,\pi)=b_{0}\left[x+\pi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)-\mathcal{R}\left(\omega,s\right)\right],

\sigma_{1}(\omega,s,x,\pi)=d_{0}\left[x+\pi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)-\mathcal{S}\left(\omega,s\right)\right],

\sigma_{2}(\omega,s,x)=c_{0}\,x_{0}\,e^{b_{0}s}\left[x-\mathcal{T}\left(\omega,s\right)\right],

h(\omega,s,x,\pi)=c_{0}\left[x+\pi\left(\mathcal{L}\right)-\mathcal{U}\left(\omega,s\right)\right],

with
(b_{0},c_{0},d_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\mathcal{L}
being the linear function
\mathcal{L}(x)=x
such that

\pi_{s}\left(\mathcal{L}\right)=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{L}\left(X_{s}\right)\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[X_{s}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[X_{s}L_{s}\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}\big]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big[L_{s}\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}^{Y}\big]},

and the stochastic processes being defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\left(\omega,s\right)

\displaystyle=x_{0}e^{b_{0}s}\bigg[1+\exp\Big(c_{0}x_{0}\int_{0}^{s}e^{br}\mathrm{d}Y_{r}\left(\omega\right)-\frac{1}{4b_{0}}c^{2}_{0}x_{0}^{2}(e^{2b_{0}s}-1)\Big)\bigg],

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(\omega,s\right)

\displaystyle=x_{0}e^{b_{0}s}\bigg[1+2\exp\Big(c_{0}x_{0}\int_{0}^{s}e^{br}\mathrm{d}Y_{r}\left(\omega\right)-\frac{1}{4b_{0}}c_{0}^{2}x_{0}^{2}(e^{2b_{0}s}-1)\Big)\bigg],

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\left(\omega,s\right)

\displaystyle=x_{0}\exp\left[\left(b_{0}-\frac{1}{2}d_{0}^{2}\right)s+d_{0}W_{s}\left(\omega\right)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\left(\omega,s\right)

\displaystyle=2x_{0}e^{b_{0}s}\exp\left[c_{0}x_{0}\int_{0}^{s}e^{b_{0}r}\mathrm{d}Y_{r}\left(\omega\right)-\frac{1}{4b_{0}}c^{2}_{0}x_{0}^{2}\Big(e^{2b_{0}s}-1\Big)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+x_{0}\exp\bigg[\Big(b_{0}-\frac{1}{2}d_{0}^{2}\Big)s+d_{0}W_{s}\left(\omega\right)\bigg].

It is straightforward to check that (
5.1
) has a unique solution

X_{t}=x_{0}\exp\left[\Big(b_{0}-\frac{1}{2}d_{0}^{2}\Big)t+d_{0}W_{t}\right]+x_{0}\exp\left[c_{0}x_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{b_{0}s}\mathrm{d}Y_{s}-\frac{1}{4b_{0}}c_{0}^{2}x_{0}^{2}\left(e^{2b_{0}t}-1\right)+b_{0}t\right].

Now we use the Euler scheme (
2.3
) to compute the partially observable mean-field SDE (
5.1
). In our numerical experiments, we take
T=0.1
,
b_{0}=d_{0}=c_{0}=x_{0}=1
, and
\Delta=10^{-5}
. We sample N particles and repeat the simulation independently
N_{\text{compute}}=100
times, where the particle number
N
is taken from the set
\mathscr{S}:=\{10k-5:\,\,1\leq k\leq 10\}
. Finally, for each
N\in\mathscr{S}
, we record the error

e_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq T/\Delta}\bigg\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left|X_{n\Delta}^{i}-X_{n\Delta}^{i,\Delta}\right|^{2}\bigg\}

for
N_{\text{compute}}=100
times and compute the mean of the error
e_{N}
. In Figure
1
, we give our computed results and we can observe that the algorithm has the
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{N})
-convergence rate in this example.

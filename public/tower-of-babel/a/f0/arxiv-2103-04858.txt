Abstract




Abstract
We derive large deviations principles for the distribution of the empirical measure of the equilibrium measure for the Generalized Gibbs ensembles of the classical Toda chain introduced in
[
9
]
. We deduce its almost sure convergence and characterize its limit in terms of the limiting measure of Beta-ensembles. Our results apply to general smooth potentials.

1.
Introduction

In a breakthrough paper
[
9
]
, Herbert Spohn introduced the generalized Gibbs ensembles of the classical Toda chain as invariant measures of the dynamics of the classical Toda lattice and showed how to analyze it thanks to a beautiful comparison with Dumitriu-Edelman tri-diagonal representations of
\beta
-ensembles. Thanks to this comparison, it is shown in
[
9
]
that
the empirical measure of the eigenvalues of Toda Lax matrices for these Generalized Gibbs ensembles converges towards a probability measure related with the equilibrium measure for
\beta
ensembles.
One of the key tool of Herbert Spohn analysis is the uses of transfer matrices which restricts him to polynomial potentials. We refer the interested reader to subsequent developments in
[
7
,
8
,
6
]
.

The main goal of this article is to generalize some of the results of Spohn
[
9
]
by using large deviations theory, which will allow us to consider more general potentials. More precisely, we will show the convergence of the free energy and of the empirical measure of the eigenvalues of Toda Lax matrices following these Generalized Gibbs. We will characterize the limits in terms of related to
\beta
-ensembles as in
[
9
]
but for general continuous potentials instead of polynomial functions. Moreover, we will derive large deviation principles allowing to study a priori tri-diagonal matrices with more general coefficients. However, in this generality, the rate functions and the limits will not be explicit as the comparison with
\beta
-ensembles is not possible.

More precisely, the Hamiltonian of the Toda chain on sites
j=1,\ldots,N
is given by

H=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\frac{1}{2}p_{j}^{2}+e^{-r_{j}}),\quad r_{j}=q_{j+1}-q_{j}

with the periodic conditions
q_{N+j}=q_{j}
. The equations of motion are then given by

\frac{d}{dt}q_{j}=p_{j},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}p_{j}=e^{-r_{j-1}}-e^{-r_{j}}\,.

(1)

Let
L_{N}
be the Lax matrix given by the tri-diagonal matrix with entries

(L_{N})_{j,j}=p_{j}\text{ and }(L_{N})_{j,j+1}=(L_{N})_{j+1,j}=e^{-r_{j}/2}

(2)

with periodic boundary conditions
(L_{N})_{1,N}=(L_{N})_{N+1,N}
and
(L_{N})_{N,1}=(L_{N})_{N,N+1}
, then for all integer number
n
,

Q^{n}_{N}={\rm Tr}(L_{N}^{n})

is conserved by the dynamics (
1
) as well as
\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}
. It is therefore natural to consider that the finite
N
Toda chain is distributed according to the Gibbs measure with density
e^{-{\rm Tr}(W(L_{N}))}
with respect to

\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-Pr_{i}}dr_{i}dp_{i}
,
where
P>0
controls the pressure of the chain.
W
is a potential to be chosen later, which can be a polynomial or a general measurable function from
{\mathbb{R}}
into
{\mathbb{R}}
. We will assume it goes to infinity faster than
x^{2}
: namely there exists
a>0
and a finite constant
C
such that for

W(x)\geq ax^{2}+C\,.

(3)

This assumption is used to compare our distribution to the case where
W(x)=x^{2}
in which case the entries of the Lax matrix
L_{N}
are independent.
We can without loss of generality assume
a=\frac{1}{2}
up to rescaling and therefore put
W(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+V
. In the following we will denote

d{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,P}(p,r)=\frac{1}{\mathbb{Z}_{N,{\mathbb{T}}}^{V,P}}\exp\{-{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N}))-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(L_{N}^{2})\}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-Pr_{i}}dr_{i}dp_{i}

(4)

where
\mathbb{Z}_{N,{\mathbb{T}}}^{V,P}
is the partition function of the Toda Gibbs measure :

\mathbb{Z}_{N,{\mathbb{T}}}^{V,P}=\int\exp\{-{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N}))-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(L_{N}^{2})\}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-Pr_{i}}dr_{i}dp_{i}\,.

(5)

We denote
in short
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{P}
for
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{0,P}
.
Our goal in this article is to study the empirical measure of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
of
L_{N}

denoted by

\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}\,.

We shall call
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}
the spectral measure of
L_{N}
, or the empirical density of states of the Lax matrix following
[
9
]
.
Our main result is a large deviation principle for the distribution of
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}
under
d{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,P}
, from which we deduce the almost sure convergence of
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}
under
d{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,P}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P>0
and assume that
V
is continuous. Assume that there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{V(x)}{x^{2k}}=a\,,

(6)

with
a>0
if
k>1
,
a>-1/2
if
k\leq 1
.
Then,

(1)

the law of
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}
under
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,P}
satisfies a large deviation principle in the scale
N
with good rate function
T_{P}^{V}
,

(2)

T_{P}^{V}
achieves its minimal value at a unique probability measure
\nu_{P}^{V}
,

(3)

As a consequence
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}
converges almost surely and in
L^{1}
towards
\nu_{P}^{V}
.

\nu^{V}_{P}
corresponds to the density of states of the Lax matrix in
[
9
]
.
Moreover, following

[
9
]
, we can identify the equilibrium measure

\nu_{P}^{V}
using the equilibrium measure for Coulomb gases in dimension one
at temperature of order of the dimension. More precisely,
for a probability measure
\mu
on the real line, define the function

f_{P}^{V}(\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\int(W(x)+W(y)-2P\ln|x-y|)d\mu(x)d\mu(y)+\int\ln\frac{d\mu}{dx}d\mu(x)

if
\mu\ll dx
, whereas
f_{P}^{V}
is infinite otherwise.
f_{P}^{V}
achieves its minimal value at a unique probability measure
\mu_{P}^{V}\ll dx
which satisfies the non-linear equation

W(x)-2P\int\ln|x-y|d\mu_{P}^{V}(y)+\ln\frac{d\mu_{P}^{V}}{dx}=\lambda_{P}^{V}\qquad a.s

(7)

where
\lambda_{P}^{V}
is a finite constant. We show in section
3
that
\mu_{P}^{V}
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure with density which is almost surely differentiable with respect to
P
.
We then show that

Theorem 1.2
.

For any bounded continuous function
f
on the real line,

\int f(x)d\nu_{P}^{V}(x)=\partial_{P}(P\int f(x)d\mu_{P}^{V}(x))

This result was already shown in
[
9
]
when
V
is a polynomial.
Moreover, our result allows to derive large deviations principles for a general variance profile. Namely let
L_{N}^{\sigma}
be a tri-diagonal symmetric matrix with independent Gaussian variables on the diagonal and independent chi distributed variables above the diagonal with parameter
\sigma(\frac{i}{N}),1\leq i\leq N
. Let
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,\sigma}
be the distribution with density
e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N}^{\sigma}))}/Z
with respect to the distribution of
L_{N}^{\sigma}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
V
is continuous and satisfies (
6
). Then, if
\sigma
is bounded continuous,

(1)

the law of
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}^{\sigma}}
under
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{V,\sigma}
satisfies a large deviation principle in the scale
N
with good rate function
T^{V}_{\sigma}
,

(2)

T^{V}_{\sigma}
achieves its minimal value at a unique probability measure
\nu_{\sigma}^{V}=\int_{0}^{1}\nu_{\sigma(P)}^{V}dP
,

(3)

As a consequence,
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}^{\sigma}}
converges almost surely and in
L^{1}
towards
\nu_{\sigma}^{V}
.

Our strategy is to prove first a large deviations principle in the case when
W
is quadratic:
L_{N}
has then independent entries (modulo the symmetry constraint) under
{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}
. We then derive large deviations principle for more general bounded continuous potentials by using Varadhan’s Lemma, see section
2
.

Indeed, in the case where
V
vanishes,
the random variables
(p_{j},r_{j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}
are independent,
(L_{N})_{j,j}
are standard Gaussian
N(0,1)
variables and
\sqrt{2}(L_{N})_{j,j+1}
follows a
\chi_{2P}
distribution with density with respect to Lebesgue measure given by

\chi_{2P}(x)=\frac{2^{1-P}x^{2P-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}}{\Gamma(P)}\mathbf{1}_{x>0}.

(8)

The central observation is that the eigenvalues of such a matrix, if the parameter
P
was replaced by
2Pi/N
, would follow a
2P/N
- ensembles by Dumitriu-Edelman
[
3
]
.
We then relate the free energy, the rate function of the equilibrium measure of the Toda chain with Coulomb gases in section
3
. In section
4
, we study the case of general potentials. The proof is nearly independent from the quadratic case, but requires additional arguments in particular because the eigenvalues of the Toda matrix are not simple functions of the empirical measure of the entries.

2.
Large deviation principles for tri-diagonal matrices

In this section, we consider a tri-diagonal matrix
M_{N}
with entries

(M_{N})_{j,j}=a_{j}\text{ and }(M_{N})_{j,j+1}=(M_{N})_{j+1,j}=b_{j}

(9)

with periodic boundary conditions, the random variables
(a_{i},b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
being iid, with
(a_{1},b_{1})
with law
Q_{a}\otimes Q_{b}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We denote by
\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}
the spectral measure of
M_{N}
and prove the existence of a large deviations principle for the distribution of
\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}
.
In
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
, the author proves a large deviations principle for moments
\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}(x^{k})
by noticing that

\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}(x^{k})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{k}(a_{j},b_{j},|i-j|\leq k)

and using the large deviations principle for Markov chains, see e.g
[
2
, Theorem 3.1.2]
, as well as the contraction principle. Here
f_{k}(a_{j},b_{j},|i-j|\leq k)=(M_{N}^{k})_{ii}
is an homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
.
This could be used to deduce the existence of a large deviation principle for
\hat{\mu}
for the weak topology after approximations, but the rate function would not be particularly explicit. We prefer to develop a more straightforward sub-additivity argument and prove separately the existence of a weak large deviation principle and exponential tightness, see e.g
[
2
, Lemma 1.2.18]
.

2.1.
Exponential tightness

In this section we assume that

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists
\gamma>0
such that

D_{\gamma}:=\int e^{\gamma x^{2}}dQ_{a}(x)\times\int e^{\gamma y^{2}}dQ_{b}(y)<\infty\,.

We equip the set of probability measures on the real line
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
with the weak topology.
We then show that

Lemma 2.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the sequence
(\hat{\mu}^{N}_{M})_{N\geq 0}
is exponentially tight, namely for each
L\geq 0
there exists a compact set
K_{L}
,
K_{L}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}):\int x^{2}d\mu(x)\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}(L+\ln D_{\gamma})\}
, such that

\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in K_{L}^{c})<-L.

(10)

Proof.

For
N\geqslant 1
, notice that

\displaystyle\int x^{2}\text{d}\hat{\mu}^{N}_{M}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}(M_{N}^{2})

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left((M_{N})_{j,j}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{2}(M_{N})_{j,j+1}\right)^{2}.

As a consequence, Tchebychev’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\text{d}\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}(x)>L\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\gamma NL}\mathbb{E}[e^{\frac{1}{2}N\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\text{d}\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}(x)}]\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\gamma NL}D_{\gamma}^{N}\,.

The conclusion follows.

∎

2.2.
Weak large deviation principle

We next establish a weak large deviation principle, based on the general ideas developed in
[
2
]
, see Lemma 6.1.7.
To this end, we use the following distance on
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
:

d(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\|f\|_{\text{BV}}\leqslant 1,|f|_{\text{Lip}}\leqslant 1}\left\{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}fd\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}}fd\nu\right|\right\},

(12)

where
\|f\|_{\text{BV}}
is the total variation of
f

given by

\|f\|_{\text{BV}}=\sup\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})|,

where the supremum holds over all increasing sequences

(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
\|f\|_{L}
is the Lispchitz norm of
f
. If
f
is
C^{1}
and we put without loss of generality
f(0)=0
,
\|f\|_{BV}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|f^{\prime}(y)|dy
and
\|f\|_{L}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
. The distance
d
is smaller than the Wasserstein distance where one takes the supremum over all functions whose sum of their
L^{\infty}
and Lipschitz norm are bounded by one and is easily seen to be as well compatible with the weak topology.
Then, we shall prove that

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\mu
in
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
,
there exists a limit

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right).

(13)

We denote this limit by
-J_{M}(\mu)
.

Proof.

The advantage of the distance
d
is the following control: For any symmetric
N\times N
matrices

A
and
B
with spectral measures
\hat{\mu}_{A}
and
\hat{\mu}_{B}
, we have:

d(\hat{\mu}_{A},\hat{\mu}_{B})\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{{\rm rank}(A-B)}{N},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}|A(i,j)-B(i,j)|\right\}.

(14)

Indeed, for any function
f
with bounded variation we have thanks to Weyl interlacing property, see e.g.
[
5
, (1.17)]
,

\left|\int fd\hat{\mu}^{N}_{A}-\int fd\hat{\mu}^{N}_{B}\right|\leq\frac{1}{N}{\rm rank}({A-B})\,.

(15)

Moreover, one can check that if
f
is
C^{1}

\displaystyle\int fd\hat{\mu}_{A}-\int fd\hat{\mu}_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left((A-B)f^{\prime}(\alpha A+(1-\alpha)B)\right)d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(A-B)_{ij}f^{\prime}(\alpha A+(1-\alpha)B)_{ji}\right)d\alpha

which implies since for all indices
i,j
,
|f^{\prime}(\alpha A+(1-\alpha)B)_{ji}|\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
that

\left|\int fd\hat{\mu}_{A}-\int fd\hat{\mu}_{B}\right|\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|(A-B)_{ij}|

(16)

Since
C^{1}
functions with bounded
L^{\infty}
norm are dense in Lipschitz functions, we deduce (
14
) from (
15
) and (
16
).
We are now ready to prove
Lemma
2.3
. To this end we shall approximate our matrix
M_{N}
by a diagonal block matrix with independent blocks.
Let
q\geqslant 1
. For
N\geqslant 1
we decompose
N=k_{N}q+r_{N}
with
r_{N}\in\left\{0,\ldots,q-1\right\}
and set
M_{N}=M_{N}^{q}+R_{N}^{q}
, where
M_{N}^{q}
is the diagonal block matrix

M_{N}^{q}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{q}^{1}&&&\\
&\ddots&&\\
&&M_{q}^{k_{N}}&\\
&&&B\end{bmatrix},

(17)

where for all

i\in\left\{1,\ldots,k_{N}\right\}

M_{q}^{i}
has the same distribution than
M_{q}
and
B
the same distribution than
M_{r_{N}}
. The
M_{q}^{i}
are independent, and are independent from
B
.

R_{N}^{q}
is the self-adjoint matrix with null entries except
R_{N}^{q}(1,N)=R_{N}^{q}(N,1)=b_{N}
,
R_{N}^{q}(k_{N}q+1,N)=R_{N}^{q}(N,k_{N}q+1)=-b_{N}
,
and those given, for
k\in\{1,\ldots,k_{N}\}
,
R_{N}^{q}(kq+1,kq)=R_{N}^{q}(kq,kq+1)=b_{kq}
,
R_{N}^{q}((k-1)q+1,kq))=R_{N}^{q}(kq,(k-1)q+1)=-b_{kq}
. Therefore

{\rm rank}(R_{N}^{q})\leqslant 2k_{N}+2\leqslant 4k_{N}
.
By (
14
), we deduce that

d(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}},\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}^{q}})\leqslant\frac{4}{q}.

(18)

Moreover, we can write

\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}^{q}}
as the sum

\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}^{q}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}\frac{q}{N}\hat{\mu}_{M^{i}_{q}}+\frac{r_{N}}{N}\hat{\mu}_{B}\,.

Therefore, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\delta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M^{1}_{q}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)^{k_{N}}\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{r_{N}}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\forall\ i\in\{1,\ldots,k_{N}\},\ \hat{\mu}_{M^{i}_{q}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta),\ \hat{\mu}_{B}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}^{q}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta+\frac{4}{q})\right),

where we used the convexity of balls and (
18
). As a consequence,

u_{N}(\delta)=-\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)

satisfies

u_{N}(\delta+4/q)\leqslant k_{N}u_{q}(\delta)+u_{r_{N}}(\delta).

It is easy (and classical) to deduce the convergence of
u_{N}/N
when
N
and then
\delta
goes to infinity, and then as
\delta
goes to zero. Indeed let
\delta>0
be given and choose
q
large enough so that
\frac{4}{q}<\delta
. Then, since
\delta\rightarrow u_{N}(\delta)
is decreasing,

\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leqslant\frac{u_{N}(\delta+4/q)}{N}\leqslant\frac{u_{q}(\delta)}{q}+\frac{u_{r_{N}}(\delta)}{N}\,.

(19)

Since
\frac{u_{r_{N}}(\delta)}{N}\leqslant\frac{\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant q-1}u_{i}(\delta)}{N}
goes to zero when
N\to\infty
, we conclude that

\limsup_{N}\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leqslant\frac{u_{q}(\delta)}{q}\,.

Since this is true for all
q
large enough,
we get

\limsup_{N}\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leqslant\liminf_{N}\frac{u_{N}(\delta)}{N}\,.

Since the left and right hand sides decrease as
\delta
goes to zero, we conclude that

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\right)\,,

and the conclusion follows.
∎

2.3.
Full large deviation principle

As a consequence of Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we have by
[
2
, Theorem 1.2.18]
the following large deviation theorem:

Theorem 2.4
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the law of
\hat{\mu}_{M}
satisfies a large deviation principle in the scale
N
with a good rate function
J_{M}
. Moreover,
J_{M}
is convex.
In other words,

•

J_{M}:\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
has compact level sets
\{\mu:J_{M}(\mu)\leq L\}
for all
L\geq 0
. Moreover,
J_{M}
is convex.

•

For any closed set
F\subset\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in F)\leq-\inf_{F}J_{M}\,,

whereas for any open set
O\subset\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}\in O)\geq-\inf_{O}J_{M}\,.

Proof.

J_{M}
exists and is defined by Lemma
2.3
. The lower semi-continuity of
J_{M}
follows from
[
2
, Theorem 4.1.11]
. We then deduce that the level sets of
J_{M}
are compact by the exponential tightness, see
[
2
, Lemma 1.2.18 (b)]
.

In the spirit of
[
2
, Lemma 4.1.21]
, we show that
J_{M}
is convex. Let
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
. Since
\hat{\mu}_{M_{2N}}
can be decomposed as the independent sum of
\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}}
divided by 2 plus an error term of smaller than
4/N
by (
15
), we have for all
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0

\mathbb{P}\left(d(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}},\mu_{1})<\delta_{1}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(d(\hat{\mu}_{M_{N}},\mu_{2})<\delta_{2}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(d(\hat{\mu}_{M_{2N}},\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2})<\delta_{3}\right).

(20)

for any
\delta_{3}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})+\frac{4}{N}
. Taking the logarithm, dividing by
2N
and letting
N
go to infinity,
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and then
\delta_{3}
to zero, we conclude that

J_{M}\left(\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\bigg(J_{M}(\mu_{1})+J_{M}(\mu_{2})\bigg),

(21)

The second point, namely that a weak large deviation principle and exponential tightness implies a full large deviation principle, is classical, see
[
2
, Lemma 1.2.18]
.
∎

2.4.
Large deviation principle for the Toda-Chain with quadratic potential

In the case of the Toda chain with Gaussian potential
W(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
, that is
V=0
, with entries following
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{P}
, we take
Q_{a}
to be the standard Gaussian law and
Q_{b}
to be the chi distribution
\sqrt{2}^{-1}\chi_{2P}
given in (
8
). We let
L_{N}(P)
the tridiagonal matrix whose entries follow
{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}
.
These entries clearly satisfy Assumption
2.1
and therefore we have

Corollary 2.5
.

For any
P>0
,
the law of
\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}(P)}
satisfies a large deviation principle in the scale
N
with convex good rate function
T_{P}
.

For further use, we show that

Lemma 2.6
.

For each
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
, the map
s\in(0,+\infty)\mapsto T_{s}(\mu)
is lower semi-continuous.

Proof.

We first show that we can couple
the matrices

(L_{N}(s),L_{N}(s+h))_{N}
, where
L_{N}(s)
follows
{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{s}
so that there exists a finite constant
c
and a function
A(h)
going to infinity as

is bounded uniformly by the previous estimate,
this already gives
the existence of a finite constant
D
such that

D(\mu_{P}^{V},\mu_{R}^{V})\leq D\sqrt{|P-R|}\,.

We next improve this bound to show the Lipschitz property. If

has finite
\|.\|_{1/2}
norm, we are done by using (
29
). Because this is unclear,
we introduce another probability measure
\nu
in (
32
) so that

\displaystyle PD(\mu_{P}^{V},\mu_{R}^{V})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(P-R)\int\int\ln|x-y|d(\mu_{R}^{V}-\nu)(x)d\Delta\mu(y)

(33)

\displaystyle+2(P-R)\int\int\ln|x-y|d\nu(x)d\Delta\mu(y)

We choose
\nu
such that
\phi_{\nu}(x)=\int\ln|x-y|d\nu(y)
is bounded and differentiable with bounded derivative, for instance the Cauchy transform with coefficient 1 for which

\phi^{\prime}_{\nu}(x)=PV\int\frac{1}{x-y}\frac{1}{\pi(y^{2}+1)}dy=\frac{1}{x^{2}+1}

Because

\|f\|_{1/2}^{2}=\int t|\hat{f}_{t}|^{2}dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int t^{2}|\hat{f}_{t}|^{2}dt+\int|\hat{f}_{t}|^{2}dt\right)=\frac{1}{2}(\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\infty})

is bounded and hence there exists a finite constant

such that

\left|\int\int\ln|x-y|d\nu(x)d\Delta\mu(y)\right|\leq CD(\mu_{P}^{V},\mu_{R}^{V})\,.

Moreover we notice that by taking the Fourier transform of the logarithm and using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\left|\int\int\ln|x-y|d(\mu_{R}^{V}-\nu)(x)d\Delta\mu(y)\right|=\left|\int\frac{1}{t}\widehat{(\mu_{R}^{V}-\nu)}_{t}\overline{\widehat{\Delta\mu}_{t}}dt\right|\leq D(\mu^{V}_{R},\nu)D(\mu^{V}_{R},\mu^{V}_{\nu})\,.

Now

is bounded uniformly because
\mu^{V}_{R}
and
\nu

Hence, we conclude from (

such that

from which the conclusion follows.

( or

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left(V(L_{N})-V(\tilde{L}_{N})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{k}\left(\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left((\Delta L_{N})^{2k}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left((\Delta L_{N})^{2k}\right)\right)^{1/2k}(\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}(L_{N}^{2k}))^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}\right)

\displaystyle C(M,K)N^{-\frac{1}{2k}}

. Note that in the above right hand side

.
Moreover, by (

has rank two.
We fix a probability measure
\mu
and first prove that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\int 1_{d(\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}},\mu)<\delta}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}\geq\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln{\int 1_{d(\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}},\mu)<\delta}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}}

(47)

for which we can assume that the right hand side does not equal
-\infty
.
Then, we have by the previous remark

\displaystyle\int 1_{d(\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}},\mu)<\delta}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-C(M,K)N^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}}\int 1_{\tilde{B}_{N}^{M,K}\cap\{d(\hat{\mu}_{\tilde{L}_{N}},\mu)<\delta-\frac{2}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}e^{-C(M,K)N^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}}\int 1_{\{{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})\leq NM\}\cap\{d(\hat{\mu}_{\tilde{L}_{N}},\mu)<\delta-\frac{2}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}e^{-C(M,K)N^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}}\left\{\int 1_{\{d(\hat{\mu}_{\tilde{L}_{N}},\mu)<\delta-\frac{2}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}-e^{-NM}\right\}

where in the second line we integrated over
b_{N}\leq K
and in the last line we used that

\int 1_{\{{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})\geq NM\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}\leq e^{-NM}\,.

We next choose
M
so that this term is smaller than the first term (which we assumed bounded below by

for some finite

).
We deduce that (
47

To prove the converse inequality, we notice that there exists one
b_{i}
bounded by
K
with probability greater than

under
{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}
, with

which goes to
+
infinity when

does. By symmetry with respect to the order of the indices, we may assume it is

. Therefore, because

is bounded below by some finite constant

, setting

\displaystyle\int 1_{d(\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}},\mu)<\delta}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}\leq e^{-Na^{\prime}(K)}+N\int 1_{\{b_{N}\leq K\}\cap\{d(\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}},\mu)<\delta\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq e^{-Na^{\prime}(K)}+Ne^{-N(M-c_{V})}+Ne^{C(M,K)N^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}}\int 1_{B_{N}^{M,K}\cap\{d(\hat{\mu}_{\tilde{L}_{N}},\mu)<\delta+\frac{2}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq e^{-Na^{\prime}(K)}+Ne^{-N(M-c_{V})}+Ne^{C(M,K)N^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}}\int 1_{\{d(\hat{\mu}_{\tilde{L}_{N}},\mu)<\delta+\frac{2}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}

which gives the converse bound, letting

going to infinity, provided

∎

Lemma 4.4

(48)

Proof.

We use the notations of Lemma

By Jensen’s inequality we have

\frac{1}{N}\ln\frac{Z_{N,q}^{V}}{Z_{N}^{V}}=\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[e^{{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N})-V(L^{q}_{N}))}\bigg]\geqslant\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N})-V(L^{q}_{N}))\bigg]\,.

\qquad\qquad+C\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left((L_{N}-L^{q}_{N})^{2k}\right)\bigg]^{1/2k}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}(L_{N}^{2k})\bigg]^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}

Now,

has non zero entries only at the sites
(i,i+1)

(i+1,i)

, as well as
(N,1)
and
(1,N)
. We take

We next remark that since the application

L_{i,i+1}^{2k}\leq((L_{N}^{2})_{i,i})^{k}\leq(L_{N}^{2k})_{i,i}\,.

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}\left((L_{N}-L^{q}_{N})^{2k}\right)\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{N}C_{k}\sum_{i\in J}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[(L)_{i,i}^{2k}\bigg]=C_{k}\frac{k_{N}}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}(L_{N})^{2k}\bigg]\,.

But (
46

is bounded by some finite constant independent of
N
. We therefore deduce (
49
) from (
50

We next prove the subadditivity property.

where we set

.
As before, noticing that

and
L_{N}-L_{N}^{q}
, we find a finite constant

such that, on

\frac{1}{N}\left|{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N})-V(L^{q}_{N}))\right|\leq C\bigg(\left(\frac{k_{N}}{N}C_{k}A\right)^{1/2k}L^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}+\frac{k_{N}}{N}C_{k}A\bigg)\,.

Therefore if we set
\mathcal{K}_{L}^{q}=\{\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}^{q}}(V)\leq L\}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}\left(\{\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\}\cap\mathcal{K}_{L}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{e^{-\varepsilon(q)N}}{Z^{V}_{N,q}}\int_{K_{A}\cap\mathcal{K}_{L-\varepsilon(q)}^{q}}\mathbf{1}_{\hat{\mu}_{L_{N}^{q}}\in B_{\mu}(\delta-4/q)}e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(L_{N}^{q}))}d{{\mathbb{T}}}^{P}_{N},

4.3.
Convergence of the free energy and large deviation principle

In the case where
V(x)=ax^{2k},a>0
, the previous two sections showed that a large deviation principle holds for the empirical measure of the eigenvalues of
L_{N}
under
{\mathbb{T}}^{V,P}_{N}
with good rate function

T^{V}_{P}(\mu)=-\inf_{W\in C^{o}_{b}}\{\int Wd\mu+F^{V+W,P}_{\mathbb{T}}-F^{V,P}_{\mathbb{T}}\}

(55)

where

F^{V,P}_{\mathbb{T}}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\int e^{-{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})}d{\mathbb{T}}^{P}_{N}\,.

To identify
T^{V}_{P}
and its minimal value our goal is to show that

Lemma 4.5
.

For
V(x)=x^{2k}+W(x)
with
W\in C_{0}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
, for all
P>0

\int_{0}^{1}F^{V,sP}_{\mathbb{T}}ds=F_{C}^{V,P}\,.

(56)

As a consequence, the unique minimizer of
T^{V}_{P}
is given by
\nu_{P}^{V}=\partial_{P}(P\mu_{P}^{V})
with
\mu^{V}_{P}
the equilibrium measure for the Coulomb gas.

Proof.

We first prove (
56
). Clearly, for all bounded continuous functions
W,W^{\prime}
, uniformly in
P
,

|F^{x^{2k}+W,P}_{{\mathbb{T}}}-F^{x^{2k}+W^{\prime},P}_{{\mathbb{T}}}|\leq\|W-W^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\mbox{ and }|F^{x^{2k}+W,P}_{C}-F^{x^{2k}+W^{\prime},P}_{C}|\leq\|W-W^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\,.

Therefore it is enough to prove (
56
) for
W\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. We prove that for
W\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,

F^{V,P}_{{\mathbb{T}}}=\partial_{P}(PF^{V,P}_{C})\,.

(57)

Let us consider the tridiagonal matrix
C_{P}^{N}
of the Coulomb model with distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\frac{2P}{N}}_{N}
we decompose, for
\varepsilon>0
this matrix as

C_{P}^{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}&R_{N}\cr R_{N}^{T}&C_{P_{N}^{\varepsilon}}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\end{array}\right)

where
M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}
is a
\lfloor N\varepsilon\rfloor\times\lfloor N\varepsilon\rfloor
tri-diagonal symmetric matrix with standard independent Gaussian on the diagonal and chi distributed variables above the diagonal with parameters
2\frac{i}{N}P,N-\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor\leq i\leq N-1
,
C_{P_{N}^{\varepsilon}}^{N_{\varepsilon}}
is a
N_{\varepsilon}=N-\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor
square tridiagonal Coulomb matrix with parameter
2P_{N}^{\varepsilon}/N
with
P_{N}^{\varepsilon}=N_{\varepsilon}N^{-1}P=(1-\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor/N)P
,
and
R_{N}
has only one non-zero entry
r
at position
(\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor,\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor+1)
. Our first goal is to show that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon N}\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(F_{C}^{V,P}-F_{C}^{V,P-\varepsilon})+F_{C}^{V,P-\varepsilon}\,.

(58)

We will then complete the argument by showing that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon N}\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]=F^{V,P}_{\mathbb{T}}

(59)

We next turn to the proof of (
58
). Let us denote

\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}&0\cr 0&C_{P_{N}^{\varepsilon}}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\end{array}\right)\,.

We now show that

{\rm Tr}((C_{P}^{N})^{{2k}})\geq{\rm Tr}((\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})^{{2k}})\,.

(60)

Indeed, by Klein’s lemma
[
1
]
,
B\mapsto{\rm Tr}(B)^{{2k}}
is convex on the set of symmetric matrices. Moreover
\nabla{\rm Tr}(B)^{{2k}}=(2k(B)^{{2k-1}})_{ij}
.
As a consequence, for any symmetric matrices
A,B

{\rm Tr}((A+B)^{2k})-{\rm Tr}((B)^{2k})\geq{\rm Tr}(2kB^{2k-1}A)\,.

We apply the above inequality with
A=C_{P}^{N}-\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}
and
B=\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}
and notice that the entry
\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor,\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor+1
of
(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})^{2k-1}
vanishes so that
{\rm Tr}((\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})^{2k-1}(C_{P}^{N}-\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))=0
. Moreover, if
W
is
C^{1}_{b}
,

|{\rm Tr}(W(C_{P}^{N}))-{\rm Tr}(W(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))|\leq\int_{0}^{1}|{\rm Tr}(W^{\prime}(\alpha C_{P}^{N}+(1-\alpha)\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})(C^{P}_{N}-\tilde{C}^{P}_{N}))|d\alpha\leq\|W^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|r|

Consequently, using the independence of
r
and
\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}
and the fact that
C_{W}=\mathbb{E}[e^{+\|W^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|r|}]
is finite since
r
has sub-Gaussian distribution, we deduce from (
60
) that

\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(C_{P}^{N}))}]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))+\|W^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|r|}]\leq C_{W}\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]\,.

(61)

As a consequence

\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(C_{P}^{N}))}]\leq C_{W}\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(C_{P_{N}^{\varepsilon}}^{N_{\varepsilon}}))}]

which gives the desired lower bound:

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]\geq F_{C}^{P,V}-(1-\varepsilon)F_{C}^{P(1-\varepsilon),V}

(62)

where we used that Theorem
3.1
is valid for
P_{N}^{\varepsilon}\to(1-\varepsilon)P
.
To get the complementary lower bound we restrict ourselves to

\{|r|\leq\frac{1}{N}\}\cap\{\|\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}\|_{\infty}\leq(NM)^{1/2k}\}

On this set

{\rm Tr}(V(C_{P}^{N}))-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))
goes to zero uniformly for all
M
. On the other hand the probability of the set
\{|r|\leq\frac{1}{N}\}
is of order
1/N
. Again by independence we deduce that there exists a function
o(N)
such that
o(N)/N
goes to zero (and eventually changing from line to line) such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(C_{P}^{N}))}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{o(N)}\mathbb{E}[1_{\{\|\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}\|_{\infty}\leq(NM)^{1/2k}\}\cap\{|r|\leq\frac{1}{N}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]

(63)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{o(N)}\left(\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]-\mathbb{E}[1_{\{\|\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}\|_{\infty}\geq(NM)^{1/2k}\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{o(N)}\left(\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]-\mathbb{E}[1_{\{{\rm Tr}((\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})^{2k})\geq MN\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]\right)\,.

But we can show exactly as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
that for
M
large enough

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[1_{\{{\rm Tr}((\tilde{C}_{P}^{N})^{2k})\geq MN\}}e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]}{\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}(V(\tilde{C}_{P}^{N}))}]}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

yielding the desired lower bound and therefore (
58
).

To prove (
59
), we proceed by approximation. We notice that if we denote by
D_{T}^{\varepsilon}
the density of the distribution of
M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}
with respect to the distribution of a Toda matrix
\tilde{L}_{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}
with parameter
P
to which we removed the extreme entries at
(1,\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor)
and

(\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor,1)
,
then we get

D_{T}^{\varepsilon}=\prod_{i=1}^{N\varepsilon}b_{i}^{-2P(\frac{i}{N})}

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{-\varepsilon^{2}NM}\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}1_{-2P\sum_{i=1}^{\varepsilon N}\frac{i}{N}\ln b_{i}\geq-\varepsilon^{2}NM}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\varepsilon^{2}NM}\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(\tilde{L}_{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}](1-\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\lfloor N\varepsilon\rfloor}^{V,P}(-2P\sum_{i=1}^{\varepsilon N}\frac{i}{N}\ln b_{i}\leq-\varepsilon^{2}NM))

On the other hand

\{2P\sum_{i=1}^{\varepsilon N}\frac{i}{N}\ln b_{i}\geq\varepsilon^{2}NM\}\subset\{P\frac{1}{N\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{\varepsilon N}b_{i}^{2}\geq M\}\subset\{\frac{1}{N\varepsilon}{\rm Tr}((\tilde{L}_{\lfloor N\varepsilon\rfloor})^{2})\geq M/P\}

has exponentially small probability under
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\lfloor N\varepsilon\rfloor}^{V,P}
for
M
large enough. This shows that there exists a finite constant
M
such that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N\varepsilon}\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-{\rm Tr}V(M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor})}]\geq F^{V,P}_{\mathbb{T}}+M\varepsilon

Similarly, we can see that the density
\tilde{D}_{T}^{\varepsilon}=\prod_{i=1}^{N\varepsilon}b_{i}^{2P(\frac{i}{N}-\varepsilon)}
of the law a Toda matrix
\tilde{L}_{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}

with respect to
M_{P}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}
is bounded below by
-\varepsilon^{2}NM
on
\{\sum_{i=1}^{\varepsilon N}(\varepsilon-\frac{i}{N})\ln b_{i}\leq\varepsilon^{2}NM\}
so that we get similarly a finite constant
M^{\prime}
such that

We finally show that (

Indeed, by (

Indeed, even if we do not have the coupling of Corollary

Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}V(L_{N})-\frac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}V_{\varepsilon}(L_{N})\right|

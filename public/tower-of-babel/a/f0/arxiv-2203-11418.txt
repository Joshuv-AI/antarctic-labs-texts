Abstract
. In this paper, we improve the global existence result in [ 9 ] slightly. More precisely, the global existence of strong solutions to the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity and diffusivity is obtained under the assumption of initial data ( v 0 , T 0 ) ∈ H 1 (v_{0},T_{0})\in H^{1} with ∂ z v 0 ∈ L 4 \partial_{z}v_{0}\in L^{4} . Moreover, we prove that the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation strongly converge to the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity and diffusivity, in the cases of H 1 H^{1} initial data, H 1 H^{1} initial data with additional regularity ∂ z v 0 ∈ L 4 \partial_{z}v_{0}\in L^{4} and H 2 H^{2} initial data, respectively, as the aspect ration parameter λ \lambda goes to zero, and the rate of convergence is of the order O ⁡ ( λ η / 2 ) O(\lambda^{{\eta}/2}) with η = min ⁡ { 2 , β − 2 , γ − 2 } ​ ( 2 < β , γ < ∞ ) \eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}(2<\beta,\gamma<\infty) . The convergence result implies a rigorous justification of the hydrostatic approximation.



1.
Introduction

The primitive equations are considered as the fundamental model in geophysical flows (see, e.g.,
[
47
,
38
,
43
,
36
,
46
]
). In large-scale ocean dynamics, an important feature is that the vertical scale of ocean is much smaller than the horizontal scale, which means that we have to use the hydrostatic approximation to simulate the motion of ocean in the vertical direction. Owing to this fact and the high accuracy of hydrostatic approximation, the three-dimensional viscous primitive equations of ocean dynamics can be formally derived from the three-dimensional Boussinesq equations with rotation (see
[
30
,
11
]
).

The small aspect ratio limit from the Navier-Stokes equations to the primitive equations was studied first by Azérad-Guillén
[
1
]
in a weak sense, then by Li-Titi
[
32
]
in a strong sense with error estimates, and finally by Furukawa
et al.
[
15
]
in a strong sense but under relaxing the regularity on the initial condition. Subsequently, the strong convergence of solutions of the scaled Navier-Stokes equations to the corresponding ones of the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity was obtained by Li-Titi-Yuan
[
34
]
. Furthermore, the rigorous justification of the hydrostatic approximation from the scaled Boussinesq equations to the primitive equations with full viscosity and diffusivity was obtained by Pu-Zhou
[
39
]
.

From a physical point of view, fluid flow is strongly influenced by effect of stratification (see, e.g.,
[
38
,
36
,
46
]
). An important observation for effect of stratification is that the density of a fluid changes with depth. In some mathematical studies, considering the hydrodynamic equations with density stratification term can often obtain better results (see, e.g.,
[
12
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
). These two facts show that density stratification term is of great significance both physically and mathematically. The rigorous mathematical derivation for the governed equations describing the motion of stable stratified fluid, i.e., the viscous primitive equations with density stratification, is due to the work of Pu-Zhou
[
40
]
. Based on the ideas that follow from Li-Titi
[
32
]
, Li-Titi-Yuan
[
34
]
, and Pu-Zhou
[
40
]
, We study the hydrostatic approximation of the Boussinesq equations with rotation in a thin domain.

1.1.
The scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation in a thin domain

Let
\varOmega_{\lambda}=M\times(-\lambda,\lambda)
be a
\lambda
-dependent domian, where
M=(0,1)\times(0,1)
. Here,
\lambda=H/L
is called the aspect ratio, measuring the ratio of the vertical and horizontal scales of the motion, which is usually very small. Say, for large-scale ocean circulation, the ratio
\lambda\sim 10^{-3}\ll 1
.

Denote by
\nabla_{h}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})
the horizontal gradient operator. Then the horizontal Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}
is given by

\Delta_{h}=\nabla_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}=\partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy}.

Let us consider the anisotropic Boussinesq equations with rotation defined on
\varOmega_{\lambda}

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi-\theta\vec{k}+f_{0}\vec{k}\times v=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}u+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}u,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=\kappa_{h}\Delta_{h}\theta+\kappa_{z}\partial_{zz}\theta,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the three dimensional velocity field
u=(v,w)=(v_{1},v_{2},w)
, the pressure
\pi
and temperature
\theta
are the unknowns.
f_{0}
is the Coriolis parameter and
\vec{k}=(0,0,1)
is unit vector pointing to the
z
-direction.
\mu_{h}
and
\mu_{z}
represent the horizontal and vertical viscosity coefficients, while
\kappa_{h}
and
\kappa_{z}
represent the horizontal and vertical heat diffusion coefficients, respectively.

In fact, the anisotropic Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.1
) have an elementary exact solution

(u,\theta,\pi)=\left(0,\bar{\theta}(z),\bar{\pi}(z)\right)=\left(0,{N^{2}}z,\frac{N^{2}}{2}z^{2}\right),

satisfying the hydrostatic approximation

\frac{d\bar{\pi}(z)}{dz}-\bar{\theta}(z)=0.

Here,
N>0
is called the buoyancy or Brunt-Väisälä frequency and denotes the strengthen of stable stratification, which implies that the density of a fluid decreases with height and lighter fluid is above heavier fluid. Assume that

\displaystyle p(x,y,z,t)=\pi(x,y,z,t)-\bar{\pi}(z),

\displaystyle T(x,y,z,t)=\theta(x,y,z,t)-\bar{\theta}(z).

Then the anisotropic Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.1
) become

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+(v\cdot\nabla_{h})v+w\partial_{z}v+\nabla_{h}p+f_{0}\vec{k}\times v=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}v+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}v,\\
\partial_{t}w+v\cdot\nabla_{h}w+w\partial_{z}w+\partial_{z}p-T=\mu_{h}\Delta_{h}w+\mu_{z}\partial_{zz}w,\\
\partial_{t}T+v\cdot\nabla_{h}T+w\partial_{z}T+N^{2}w=\kappa_{h}\Delta_{h}T+\kappa_{z}\partial_{zz}T,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v+\partial_{z}w=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Firstly, we transform the anisotropic Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.2
), defined on the
\lambda
-dependent domain
\varOmega_{\lambda}
, to the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation defined on a fixed domain. To this end, we introduce the following new unknowns

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}=(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda}),~v_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=v(x,y,\lambda z,t),

\displaystyle w_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}w(x,y,\lambda z,t),~p_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=p(x,y,\lambda z,t),

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=\lambda T(x,y,\lambda z,t),~\bar{\pi}_{\lambda}(z)=\bar{\pi}(\lambda z),~\bar{\theta}_{\lambda}(z)=\lambda^{2}\bar{\theta}(\lambda z),

for any
(x,y,z)\in\varOmega=:M\times(-1,1)
and for any
t\in(0,\infty)
. Then the last two scalings allow us to write the pressure and temperature non-dimensionally as

\bar{\pi}_{\lambda}(z)+p_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=\bar{\pi}(\lambda z)+p(x,y,\lambda z,t)=\pi(x,y,\lambda z,t)

and

\bar{\theta}_{\lambda}(z)+\lambda T_{\lambda}(x,y,z,t)=\lambda^{2}(\bar{\theta}(\lambda z)+T(x,y,\lambda z,t))=\lambda^{2}\theta(x,y,\lambda z,t),

respectively.

Suppose that
\mu_{h}=\kappa_{h}=1
,
\mu_{z}=\lambda^{\beta}
, and
\kappa_{z}=\lambda^{\gamma}
, with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
. Under these scalings, the anisotropic Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.2
) defined on
\varOmega_{\lambda}
can be written as the following scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\lambda}+(v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}v_{\lambda}+\nabla_{h}p_{\lambda}+f_{0}\vec{k}\times v_{\lambda}=\Delta_{h}v_{\lambda}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\partial_{zz}v_{\lambda},\\
\lambda(\partial_{t}w_{\lambda}+v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}w_{\lambda})+\frac{1}{\lambda}(\partial_{z}p_{\lambda}-T_{\lambda})=\lambda\Delta_{h}w_{\lambda}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\partial_{zz}w_{\lambda},\\
\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\partial_{t}T_{\lambda}+v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}T_{\lambda}\right)+\lambda N^{2}w_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Delta_{h}T_{\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma-3}\partial_{zz}T_{\lambda},\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{\lambda}+\partial_{z}w_{\lambda}=0,\end{cases}

(1.3)

defined on the fixed domain
\varOmega
.

Set
\lambda^{2}N^{2}=1
, i.e.,
N\sim 1/\lambda
, which means that the stratification effect is very strong as the aspect ratio
\lambda
tends to zero. In such a case, the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\lambda}-\Delta_{h}v_{\lambda}-\lambda^{\beta-2}\partial_{zz}v_{\lambda}+(v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}v_{\lambda}+\nabla_{h}p_{\lambda}+f_{0}\vec{k}\times v_{\lambda}=0,\\
\lambda^{2}\left(\partial_{t}w_{\lambda}-\Delta_{h}w_{\lambda}-\lambda^{\beta-2}\partial_{zz}w_{\lambda}+v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}w_{\lambda}\right)+\partial_{z}p_{\lambda}-T_{\lambda}=0,\\
\partial_{t}T_{\lambda}-\Delta_{h}T_{\lambda}-\lambda^{\gamma-2}\partial_{zz}T_{\lambda}+v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}\partial_{z}T_{\lambda}+w_{\lambda}=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{\lambda}+\partial_{z}w_{\lambda}=0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Next, we supply the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
) with the following boundary and initial conditions

\displaystyle v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},p_{\lambda}~\textnormal{and}~T_{\lambda}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,

(1.5)

\displaystyle(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})|_{t=0}=(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0}),

(1.6)

where
(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0})
is given. Moreover, we also equip the system (
1.4
) with the following symmetry condition

v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},p_{\lambda}~\textnormal{and}~T_{\lambda}~\textnormal{are even,~odd,~even and odd with respect to}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

(1.7)

Noting that the above symmetry condition is preserved by the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
), i.e., it holds provided that the initial data satisfies this symmetry condition. Due to this fact, throughout this paper, we always suppose that

v_{0},w_{0}~\textnormal{and}~T_{0}~\textnormal{are periodic in}~x,y,z,~\textnormal{and are even,~odd~and odd in}~z,~\textnormal{respectively}.

(1.8)

In this paper, we will not distinguish in notation between spaces of scalar and vector-valued functions. Namely, we will use the same notation to denote both a space itself and its finite product spaces. For simplicity, we denote by notation
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p}
the
L^{p}(\varOmega)
norm.

The weak solutions of the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
) are defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
(u_{0},T_{0})=(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0})\in L^{2}(\varOmega)
, with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
. We say that a space periodic function
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
is a weak solution of the system (
1.4
), subject to boundary and initial conditions (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (
1.7
), if

(i)
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\varOmega))\cap L^{2}_{loc}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\varOmega))
and

(ii)
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
satisfies the following integral equality

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\bigg\{\left[-v_{\lambda}\cdot\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}-\lambda^{2}w_{\lambda}\partial_{t}\varphi_{3}-T_{\lambda}\partial_{t}\psi-T_{\lambda}\varphi_{3}+w_{\lambda}\psi+f_{0}(\vec{k}\times v_{\lambda})\cdot\varphi_{h}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\left[\nabla_{h}v_{\lambda}:\nabla_{h}\varphi_{h}+\lambda^{\beta-2}(\partial_{z}v_{\lambda})\cdot\partial_{z}\varphi_{h}+\lambda^{2}\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}\varphi_{3}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\left[\lambda^{\beta}(\partial_{z}w_{\lambda})\partial_{z}\varphi_{3}+\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi+\lambda^{\gamma-2}(\partial_{z}T_{\lambda})\partial_{z}\psi\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\left[(u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla)v_{\lambda}\cdot\varphi_{h}+\lambda^{2}(u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla w_{\lambda})\varphi_{3}+(u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla T_{\lambda})\psi\right]\bigg\}dxdydzdt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\int_{\varOmega}\left(v_{0}\cdot\varphi_{h}(0)+\lambda^{2}w_{0}\varphi_{3}(0)+T_{0}\psi(0)\right)dxdydz,

for any spatially periodic function
(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{3},\psi)
, with
\varphi_{h}=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
, such that
\nabla\cdot\varphi=0
and
(\varphi,\psi)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\varOmega}\times[0,\infty))
.

Remark 1.2
.

The proof of the existence of weak solutions to the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
) follows from the
similar argument in Lions-Temam-Wang
[
30
, Part IV]
. Specifically, for any initial data

(u_{0},T_{0})=(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0})\in L^{2}(\varOmega)
, with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
, we can prove that there exists a global weak solution

(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
of the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
), subject to boundary and initial conditions
(
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (
1.7
). Moreover, by the similar argument as Lions-Temam-Wang

[
30
, Part IV]
, we can also show that it has a unique local strong solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})

for initial data
(u_{0},T_{0})=(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0})\in H^{1}(\varOmega)
, with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
.

Remark 1.3
.

Similar to the theory of three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, e.g., see Temam
[
45
, Ch.III, Remark 4.1]
and Robinson
et al.
[
42
, Theorem 4.6]
, we can prove that the weak solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
satisfies the following energy inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert w_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}v_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}w_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}T_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right).

(1.9)

for a.e.
t\in[0,\infty)
, as long as the weak solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
is obtained by Galerkin method.

1.2.
The limiting system of the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation

Now we discuss the limiting system of the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
).

When
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
, taking the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in system (
1.4
), then this system formally converges to the following primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity and diffusivity

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\Delta_{h}v+(v\cdot\nabla_{h})v+w\partial_{z}v+\nabla_{h}p+f_{0}\vec{k}\times v=0,\\
\partial_{z}p-T=0,\\
\partial_{t}T-\Delta_{h}T+v\cdot\nabla_{h}T+w\partial_{z}T+w=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v+\partial_{z}w=0.\end{cases}

(1.10)

The term
w
in the third equation of system (
1.10
) is called the density stratification term, providing additional dissipation for this system. Moreover, we supply the limiting system (
1.10
) with the same boundary and initial conditions (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (
1.7
) as the system (
1.4
). In studying the well-posedness of system (
1.10
), we observe that it is not necessary to give the initial condition for vertical velocity
w
, since there is no dynamic equation for vertical velocity in the system. So we say that the system (
1.10
) satisfies the initial condition (
1.6
) just for convenience. Note that the initial value
w_{0}
for vertical velocity
w_{\lambda}
is uniquely determined by the divergence-free condition and symmetry condition (
1.7
). Hence it can be represented as

w_{0}(x,y,z)=-\int^{z}_{0}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{0}(x,y,\xi)d\xi,

(1.11)

for any
(x,y)\in M
and
z\in(-1,1)
. Therefore, only the initial conditions of horizontal velocity and temperature are given throughout the paper.

We remark that the limiting system of the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
) is the primitive equations with full viscosity and diffusivity when
\beta=\gamma=2
. This case was studied by the authors (see
[
40
]
). In consequence, the aim of this paper is to prove that the scaled Boussinesq equations with rotation (
1.4
) strongly converge to the primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity and diffusivity (
1.10
), in the cases of
H^{1}
initial data,
H^{1}
initial data with additional regularity
\partial_{z}v_{0}\in L^{4}
and
H^{2}
initial data, respectively, as the aspect ration parameter tends to zero. These convergence results are briefly described as follows.

•

For
H^{1}
initial data, the system (
1.10
) corresponding to (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) has a unique local strong solution
(v,T)
(see
[
9
]
). Based on this local well-posedness result and Remark
1.2
, we obtain the local strong convergence theorem (see Theorem
2.1
).

•

For
H^{1}
initial data with additional regularity
\partial_{z}v_{0}\in L^{4}
, the global existence of strong solutions to the system (
1.10
) with (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) is proved (see Theorem
2.2
). Compared with
[
9
]
, the condition
(v_{0},T_{0})\in L^{\infty}
is removed by establishing
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{4}_{x}
estimate on the vertical derivative of horizontal velocity that does not depend on
\left\lVert(v_{0},T_{0})\right\rVert_{\infty}
. Consequently, the improved result and Remark
1.2
yield the global strong convergence theorem (see Theorem
2.4
).

•

For
H^{2}
initial data, there exists a unique global strong solution
(v,T)
to the system (
1.10
) subject to (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) (see
[
8
]
). According to the energy estimate on the global strong solutions and Remark
1.2
, we establish the corresponding global strong convergence result (see Theorem
2.5
).

As can be seen from above, the well-posedness results of primitive equations with only horizontal viscosity and diffusivity (
1.4

(u,\theta,\pi)=\left(0,\bar{\theta}(z),\bar{\pi}(z)\right)=\left(0,{N^{2}}z,\frac{N^{2}}{2}z^{2}\right),

which satisfies the hydrostatic approximation

\frac{d\bar{\pi}(z)}{dz}-\bar{\theta}(z)=0.

2.
Main results

Now we are to state the main results of this paper. Assume that initial data
(v_{0},T_{0})\in H^{1}(\varOmega)
. Then it deduces from (
1.11
) that
(v_{0},w_{0},T_{0})\in L^{2}(\varOmega)
, which implies that the system (
1.4
) subject to (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) has a global weak solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
by Remark
1.2
, and the system (
1.10
) exists a unique local strong solution
(v,T)
(see
[
9
]
). Denote by
t^{*}_{0}
the maximal existence time of the local strong solution
(v,T)
to the system (
1.10
). For this case, we have the following strong convergence theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given a periodic function pair
(v_{0},T_{0})\in H^{1}(\varOmega)
with
\int^{1}_{-1}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{0}dz=0
. Suppose that
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
is a global weak solution of the system (
1.4
), satisfying the energy inequality (
1.9
), and that
(v,T)
is the unique local strong solution of the system (
1.10
), with the same boundary and initial conditions (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and symmetry condition (
1.7
). Let

(V_{\lambda},W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})=(v_{\lambda}-v,w_{\lambda}-w,T_{\lambda}-T).

Then the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<t^{*}_{0}}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(t)+\int^{t^{*}_{0}}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t^{*}_{0}}_{0}\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\lambda^{\eta}(t^{*}_{0}+1)e^{C(t^{*}_{0}+1)}\left[1+\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}\right],

where
\eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}
with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
, and

is a positive constant that does not depend on
\lambda
. As a result, we have the following strong convergences

\displaystyle(v_{\lambda},\lambda w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})\rightarrow(v,0,T),~in~L^{\infty}\left([0,t^{*}_{0});L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{h}v_{\lambda},\lambda^{(\beta-2)/2}\partial_{z}v_{\lambda},\lambda\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda},w_{\lambda}\right)\rightarrow(\nabla_{h}v,0,0,w),~in~L^{2}\left([0,t^{*}_{0});L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda},\lambda^{\beta/2}\partial_{z}w_{\lambda},\lambda^{(\gamma-2)/2}\partial_{z}T_{\lambda}\right)\rightarrow(\nabla_{h}T,0,0),~in~L^{2}\left([0,t^{*}_{0});L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

and the rate of convergence is of the order

.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left({\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}+\left\lVert(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\right)ds\leq\mathcal{J}_{2}(t),

3.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
v_{0}
,

\displaystyle(v,T)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,t^{*}_{0});H^{1}(\varOmega)\right)\cap C\left([0,t^{*}_{0});L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

where

(ii) The local strong solution

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(v,T)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left({\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}+\left\lVert(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\right)ds\leq C,

Proposition 3.2
.

Given a periodic function pair

Suppose that

1.4

1.9

(v,T)
is the unique local strong solution of the system (
1.10

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\varOmega}\left(v_{\lambda}\cdot v+\lambda^{2}w_{\lambda}w+T_{\lambda}T\right)dxdydz\right)(r)+\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}f_{0}(\vec{k}\times v_{\lambda})\cdot vdxdydzdt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\left[\nabla_{h}v_{\lambda}:\nabla_{h}v+\lambda^{\beta-2}(\partial_{z}v_{\lambda})\cdot\partial_{z}v+\lambda^{2}\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}w\right]dxdydzdt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\left[\lambda^{\beta}(\partial_{z}w_{\lambda})\partial_{z}w+\nabla_{h}T_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{h}T+\lambda^{\gamma-2}(\partial_{z}T_{\lambda})\partial_{z}T\right]dxdydzdt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\left\lVert w(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\left(\int^{z}_{0}\partial_{t}v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)\cdot\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}dxdydzdt

for any

With the help of Proposition

Lemma 3.3

The following inequalities hold

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\left(\int^{1}_{-1}\varphi(x,y,z)dz\right)\left(\int^{1}_{-1}\psi(x,y,z)\phi(x,y,z)dz\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\varphi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\right)\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\right)\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{2},

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\left(\int^{1}_{-1}\varphi(x,y,z)dz\right)\left(\int^{1}_{-1}\psi(x,y,z)\phi(x,y,z)dz\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\psi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\right)\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\left(\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\phi\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\right)\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{2},

for every

such that the right-hand sides make sense and are finite, where

is a positive constant.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(t+1)e^{C(t+1)}\left[\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}+\lambda^{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}\right],

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\left[-f_{0}(\vec{k}\times v)\cdot v_{\lambda}\right]dxdydzdt.

(3.3)

Replacing

with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert v(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)+\int^{r}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{\lambda}(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert w_{\lambda}(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{\lambda}(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{r}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}v_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}w_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\lambda^{2}\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\varOmega}\left[-\left(\int^{z}_{0}\partial_{t}v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)\cdot\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right]dxdydzdt

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{11}:

From (

) and (

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert V_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert W_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\Phi_{\lambda}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C(t+1)e^{C(t+1)}\left[\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}+\lambda^{2}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}\right].

Based on Proposition

Proof of Theorem

By the Proposition

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\lambda^{\eta}(t^{*}_{0}+1)e^{C(t^{*}_{0}+1)}\left[1+\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}\right],

is the maximal existence time of local strong solution
(v,T)
to the system (
1.10
). Here

is a positive constant that does not depend on
\lambda
. The above estimate implies that

\displaystyle(v_{\lambda},\lambda w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})\rightarrow(v,0,T),~in~L^{\infty}\left([0,t^{*}_{0});L^{2}(\varOmega)\right),

With the help of Proposition

Proof of Theorem

Thanks to the energy estimate (

\int^{t^{*}_{0}}_{t^{*}_{0}/4}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\leq C,

\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}(t_{0})+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}(t_{0})\leq C/t^{*}_{0}.

(4.2)

Moreover, the following the energy estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}\left(\left\lVert(v,T)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}\right)(s)+\int^{t_{0}}_{0}{\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t_{0}}_{0}{\left({\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}+\left\lVert(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\right)}ds\leq C.

According to Proposition
4.2

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,y,z)\in\overline{\varOmega}}|v(x,y,z,t_{0})|+\sup_{(x,y,z)\in\overline{\varOmega}}|T(x,y,z,t_{0})|

note that the divergence-free condition is used. Differentiating the above equation with respect to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial_{z}v)-\Delta_{h}(\partial_{z}v)+(\partial_{z}v\cdot\nabla_{h})v-(\nabla_{h}\cdot v)\partial_{z}v+\nabla_{h}T+f_{0}\vec{k}\times(\partial_{z}v)

\displaystyle+(v\cdot\nabla_{h})\partial_{z}v-\left(\int^{z}_{0}\nabla_{h}\cdot v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)\partial_{zz}v=0.

(4.4)

Taking the

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{z}v\right\rVert^{4}_{4}+\int_{\varOmega}\left(|\partial_{z}v|^{2}|\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v|^{2}+2|\partial_{z}v|^{2}|\nabla_{h}|\partial_{z}v||^{2}\right)dxdydz

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{\varOmega}\left[T|\partial_{z}v|^{2}(\nabla_{h}\cdot\partial_{z}v)+T(\partial_{z}v)\cdot\left(\nabla_{h}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}\right)\right]dxdydz,

\displaystyle\int_{\varOmega}\left[\left(v\cdot\nabla_{h}\right)\partial_{z}v-\left(\int^{z}_{0}\nabla_{h}\cdot v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\right)\partial_{zz}v\right]\cdot|\partial_{z}v|^{2}\partial_{z}vdxdydz=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\varOmega}\left[(\nabla_{h}\cdot v)\partial_{z}v-(\partial_{z}v\cdot\nabla_{h})v\right]\cdot|\partial_{z}v|^{2}\partial_{z}vdxdydz

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\int_{M}\left(\int^{1}_{-1}(|\nabla_{h}v|+|\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v|)dz\right)\left(\int^{1}_{-1}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}|\partial_{z}v^{2}|dz\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\left(\int_{\varOmega}|\partial_{z}v|^{4}dxdydz\right)(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v\right\rVert_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v\right\rVert_{2})\left\lVert\partial_{z}v\right\rVert^{2}_{4}\left(\int_{\varOmega}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}|\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v|^{2}dxdydz\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\left(1+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)\left\lVert\partial_{z}v\right\rVert^{4}_{4}+\frac{3}{8}\int_{\varOmega}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}|\nabla_{h}\partial_{z}v|^{2}dxdydz,

(4.6)

note that the boundary condition (
1.5
) and symmetry condition (

) are used. Applying the Hölder inequality, Young inequality and Sobolev imbedding theorem, the last integral term on the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{\varOmega}\left[T|\partial_{z}v|^{2}(\nabla_{h}\cdot\partial_{z}v)+T(\partial_{z}v)\cdot\left(\nabla_{h}|\partial_{z}v|^{2}\right)\right]dxdydz

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s<t^{*}_{0}}\left\lVert\partial_{z}v\right\rVert_{4}(s)\leq\exp\left\{C\int^{t^{*}_{0}}_{0}\left(1+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)ds\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\times\left[\left\lVert\partial_{z}v_{0}\right\rVert^{4}_{4}+C\int^{t^{*}_{0}}_{0}\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}ds\right]^{1/4}\leq Ce^{C(1+t^{*}_{0})}\left(t^{*}_{0}+\left\lVert\partial_{z}v_{0}\right\rVert^{4}_{4}\right)^{1/4}.

Recall that
(v(x,y,z,t_{0}),T(x,y,z,t_{0}))\in L^{\infty}(\varOmega)
, it follows from Proposition

that

\displaystyle\sup_{t_{0}\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert(v,T)\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}{\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}{\left({\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}(\varOmega)}}+\left\lVert(\partial_{t}v,\partial_{t}T)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\varOmega)}\right)}ds\leq\mathcal{J}_{1}(t),

The proof Theorem

, where

. Here

. Obviously, the local strong convergences in Theorem

(ii) The global strong solution

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C_{0}\left(\lambda^{\beta-2}+\lambda^{2\beta-4}\right)\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+C_{0}\left(\lambda^{\beta}+\lambda^{2\beta-2}\right)\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C_{0}\left(\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right).

Noting that the fact that
(V_{\lambda},W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})|_{t=0}=0
, and applying the Gronwall inequality to the above inequality, it follows from Proposition
5.1
and
5.2
that

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\exp\bigg\{C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left[\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+(1+\lambda^{2})\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+(1+\lambda^{2})\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left[\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{4}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+(1+\lambda^{4})\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left[(1+\lambda^{2})+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left[\left\lVert\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla T\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+(1+\lambda^{4})\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla^{2}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\times\bigg\{C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{t}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{4}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds+C^{\prime}\left(\lambda^{\gamma-2}+\lambda^{2\gamma-4}\right)\int^{t}_{0}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\left(\lambda^{\beta-2}+\lambda^{2\beta-4}\right)\int^{t}_{0}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}ds+C^{\prime}\left(\lambda^{\beta}+\lambda^{2\beta-2}\right)\int^{t}_{0}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+C^{\prime}\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}v\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}T\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C^{\prime}\lambda^{\eta}(t+1)\left[\mathcal{J}_{4}(t)+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(t)+\mathcal{J}_{5}(t)+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{5}(t)+\mathcal{J}_{4}(t)\mathcal{J}_{5}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\times\exp\left\{C^{\prime}(t+1)\left[\mathcal{J}_{4}(t)+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(t)+\mathcal{J}_{5}(t)+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{5}(t)+1\right]\right\},

where
\eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}
with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
. This completes the proof of Proposition
5.3
.
∎

By finding a small positive constant to eliminate the effect of the smallness condition in Proposition
5.3
, we establish the
H^{1}
estimate on difference function
(V_{\lambda},W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}
be the maximal existence time of the strong solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
to the system (
1.4
) corresponding to (
1.5
)-(
1.7
). Then, for any finite time
\mathcal{T}>0
, there exists a small positive constant
\lambda(\mathcal{T})=\left(\frac{3\ell^{2}_{0}}{8\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})}\right)^{1/\eta}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}>\mathcal{T}
provided that
\lambda\in

(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
. Furthermore, the system (
5.2
)-(
5.5
) has the following energy estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t)+\int^{\mathcal{T}}_{0}{\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{\mathcal{T}}_{0}{\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\lambda^{\eta}\left(\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})\right),

where
\eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}
with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
. Here
\mathcal{J}_{5}(t)
and
\mathcal{J}_{6}(t)
are nonnegative continuously increasing functions that do not depend on
\lambda
.

Proof.

For any finite time
\mathcal{T}>0
, setting
\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}=\min\{\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda},\mathcal{T}\}
, then from Proposition
5.2
it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(t)+\int^{\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}}_{0}{\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}}_{0}{\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)}dt\leq\lambda^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T}),

(5.12)

where

\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})=C(\mathcal{T}+1)e^{C(\mathcal{T}+1)\left(\mathcal{J}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(\mathcal{T})\right)}\left[\mathcal{J}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\left(\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\left\lVert T_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}\right].

Here
\eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}
with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
, and
C
is a positive constant that does not depend on
\lambda
.

Let
\ell_{0}
be the constant from Proposition
5.3
. Define

t^{\delta}_{\lambda}:=\sup\left\{t\in(0,\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda})\bigg|\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)\leq\ell^{2}_{0}\right\}.

By virtue of Proposition
5.3
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\leq\lambda^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T}),

(5.13)

for any
t\in[0,t^{\delta}_{\lambda})
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\leq C(\mathcal{T}+1)\left[\mathcal{J}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}_{4}(\mathcal{T})\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\exp\left\{C(\mathcal{T}+1)\left[\mathcal{J}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{4}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}^{2}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+1\right]\right\}.

Choosing
\lambda(\mathcal{T})=\left(\frac{3\ell^{2}_{0}}{8\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})}\right)^{1/\eta}
, it deduces from (
5.13
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(s)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\left\lVert\nabla\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\nabla\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)ds\leq\frac{3\ell^{2}_{0}}{8},

for any
t\in[0,t^{\delta}_{\lambda})
and for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
, which gives

\sup_{0\leq t<t^{\delta}_{\lambda}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\right)(t)\leq\frac{3\ell^{2}_{0}}{8}<\ell^{2}_{0}.

(5.14)

According to the definition of
t^{\delta}_{\lambda}
, (
5.14
) implies that
t^{\delta}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}
. In consequence, the estimate (
5.13
) holds for
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda})
and for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
.

We claim that
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}>\mathcal{T}
for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
. If
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}\leq\mathcal{T}
, then it is obvious that

\limsup_{t\rightarrow(\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda})^{-}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert_{2}\right)=\infty.

Otherwise, the strong solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
to the system (
1.4
) can be extended beyond the maximal existence time
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}
. However, the above result contradicts to (
5.13
). This contradiction leads to
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}>\mathcal{T}
, and hence
\mathcal{T}^{\delta}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, combining (
5.12
) with (
5.13
) yields the energy estimate in Proposition
5.4
. This completes the proof.
∎

Based on Proposition
5.4
, we give the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

For any finite time
\mathcal{T}>0
, by virtue of Proposition
5.4
, there exists a small positive constant
\lambda(\mathcal{T})=\left(\frac{3\ell^{2}_{0}}{8\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})}\right)^{1/\eta}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\lambda}>\mathcal{T}
provided that
\lambda\in(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
, which implies that the system (
1.4
) with (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) has a unique strong solution
(v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda},T_{\lambda})
on the time interval
[0,\mathcal{T}]
as long as
\lambda\in(0,\lambda(\mathcal{T}))
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{7}(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{J}_{5}(\mathcal{T})+\mathcal{J}_{6}(\mathcal{T})
, then the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}\left(\left\lVert(V_{\lambda},\lambda W_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda})\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)(t)+\int^{\mathcal{T}}_{0}{\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{h}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\beta-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}V_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int^{\mathcal{T}}_{0}{\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{h}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\nabla_{h}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\beta}\left\lVert\partial_{z}W_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda^{\gamma-2}\left\lVert\partial_{z}\Phi_{\lambda}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)}dt\leq\lambda^{\eta}\mathcal{J}_{7}(\mathcal{T}),

where
\eta=\min\{2,\beta-2,\gamma-2\}
with
2<\beta,\gamma<\infty
, and
\mathcal{J}_{7}(t)
is a nonnegative continuously increasing function that does not depend on
\lambda
. Finally, it is clear that the strong convergences stated in Theorem
2.5
are the direct consequences of the above estimate. The theorem is thus proved.

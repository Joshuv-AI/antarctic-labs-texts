Algebraically generated groups and their Lie algebras
Hanspeter Kraft and Mikhail Zaidenberg

Abstract
. The automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( X ) \Aut(X) of an affine variety X X is an ind-group. Its Lie algebra is canonically embedded into the Lie algebra Vec ⁡ ( X ) \VF(X) of vector fields on X X . We study the relations between subgroups of Aut ⁡ ( X ) \Aut(X) and Lie subalgebras of Vec ⁡ ( X ) \VF(X) . We show that a subgroup G ⊆ Aut ⁡ ( X ) G\subseteq\Aut(X) generated by a family of connected algebraic subgroups G i G_{i} of Aut ⁡ ( X ) \Aut(X) is algebraic if and only if the Lie algebras Lie ⁡ G i ⊆ Vec ⁡ ( X ) \Lie G_{i}\subseteq\VF(X) generate a finite dimensional Lie subalgebra of Vec ⁡ ( X ) \VF(X) . Extending a result by Cohen-Draisma [ CD03 ] we prove that a locally finite Lie algebra L ⊆ Vec ⁡ ( X ) L\subseteq\VF(X) generated by locally nilpotent vector fields is algebraic, i.e. L = Lie ⁡ G L=\Lie G for an algebraic subgroup G ⊆ Aut ⁡ ( X ) G\subseteq\Aut(X) . Along the same lines we prove that if a subgroup G ⊆ Aut ⁡ ( X ) G\subseteq\Aut(X) generated by finitely many connected algebraic groups is solvable, then it is an algebraic group. We also show that a unipotent algebraic subgroup U ⊆ Aut ⁡ ( X ) U\subseteq\Aut(X) has derived length ≤ dim X \leq\dim X . This result is based on the following triangulation theorem: Every unipotent algebraic subgroup of Aut ⁡ ( 𝔸 n ) \Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n}) with a dense orbit in 𝔸 n {\mathbb{A}}^{n} is conjugate to a subgroup of the de Jonquières subgroup. Furthermore, we give an example of a free subgroup F ⊆ Aut ⁡ ( 𝔸 2 ) F\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2}) generated by two algebraic elements such that the Zariski closure F ¯ \overline{F} is a free product of two nested commutative closed unipotent ind-subgroups. To any affine ind-group 𝔊 \mathfrak{G} one can associate a canonical ideal L 𝔊 ⊆ Lie ⁡ 𝔊 L_{\mathfrak{G}}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G} . It is linearly generated by the tangent spaces T e ​ X T_{e}X for all algebraic subsets X ⊆ 𝔊 X\subseteq\mathfrak{G} which are smooth in e e . It has the important property that for a surjective homomorphism φ : 𝔊 → ℌ \varphi\colon\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{H} the induced homomorphism d ​ φ e : L 𝔊 → L ℌ d\varphi_{e}\colon L_{\mathfrak{G}}\to L_{\mathfrak{H}} is surjective as well. Moreover, if ℌ ⊆ 𝔊 \mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G} is a subnormal closed ind-subgroup of finite codimension, then L ℌ L_{\mathfrak{H}} has finite codimension in L 𝔊 L_{\mathfrak{G}} .

1.
Introduction and main results

The introduction contains the necessary preliminaries and definitions, gives some background material and describes the main results of the paper. The details then follow in the subsequent sections.

1.1.
Notation

Our base field
{\Bbbk}
is algebraically closed and of characteristic zero.
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
stands for affine
n
-space over
{\Bbbk}
, and
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}:={\Bbbk}^{+}
and
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}:={\Bbbk}^{*}:={\Bbbk}\setminus\{0\}
denote the
additive
and the
multiplicative
groups of
{\Bbbk}
. An algebraic group is always an
affine algebraic group
, and
a
G
-variety
X
is an affine variety with an action of
G

such that the corresponding map
G\times X\to X
is a morphism.

For every

G
-variety
X
there is a canonical
(anti-)
homomorphism of Lie algebras
\xi\colon\Lie G\to\VF(X)
,
where
\VF(X)
stands for the Lie algebra of (algebraic) vector fields on
X
. The construction of this
anti-homomorphism goes as follows. For any
x\in X
let
\theta_{x}\colon G\to X
be the orbit map
g\mapsto gx
, and denote by

d\theta_{x}\colon\Lie G:=T_{e}G\to T_{x}X

its differential in
e\in G
. If
A\in\Lie G
, then the corresponding vector field
\xi_{A}
is given by

\xi_{A}(x):=d\theta_{x}(A)\in T_{x}X
. If
L\subseteq\VF(X)
denotes the image of
\Lie G
, then one has
T_{x}Gx=L(x)
for any
x\in X
.
Moreover, the differential
(d\theta_{x})_{g}\colon T_{g}G\to T_{gx}Gx
of the orbit map
\theta_{x}
is surjective in every
g\in G
.
(For a reference one might look at
[
Kra16
, Appendix A.4]
.)

For a
G
-variety
X
we have linear actions of
G
on the coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}(X)
and on the vector fields
\VF(X)
,
and these representations are
locally finite and rational
, i.e. every element is contained in
a finite dimensional
G
-invariant subspace and the linear action of
G
on any finite dimensional
G
-invariant subspace is rational. Moreover, the homomorphism
\xi\colon\Lie G\to\VF(X)
is
G
-equivariant, see
[
FK18
, Sect. 7.3]
.

1.2.
Automorphism groups and vector fields

For an affine variety
X
the group of regular automorphisms
\Aut(X)
is an
affine ind-group
. We refer to the paper
[
FK18
]

for an introduction to ind-varieties and ind-groups and for basic concepts, cf.
[
Sha81
,
Kum02
]
.
For an affine ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
the tangent space
T_{e}\mathfrak{G}
carries a natural structure of a Lie algebra which will be denoted by
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
. In case of

\Aut(X)
there is a canonical embedding
\xi\colon\Lie\Aut(X)\hookrightarrow\VF(X)
which is an anti-homomorphism of Lie algebras. It is constructed in a similar way as explained above for an algebraic group
G
, see
[
FK18
, Proposition 7.2.4]
. It has the following property:

If
A\in\Lie\Aut(X)
and
\xi_{A}\in\VF(X)
the corresponding vector field, then one has, for every
x\in X
,

\xi_{A}(x)=d(\theta_{x})_{e}(A)\ \text{ where\ }\ \theta_{x}\colon\Aut(X)\to X\text{ is the orbit map in }x.

The group
\mathfrak{G}
acts on
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
by the adjoint action:
\Ad(g):=d(\Int g)_{e}
. For
\mathfrak{G}=\Aut(X)
this action is induced by the action on all vector fields
\VF(X)
which is given by

\Ad g(\delta)(gx):=d\mu_{g}\,\delta(x),\ \text{ i.e. }\ \Ad g(\delta)=d\mu_{g}\circ\delta\circ\mu_{g^{-1}}

where
\mu_{g}\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}X
denotes the multiplication map
x\mapsto gx
. Here we consider a vector field
\delta\in\VF(X)
as a section of the “tangent bundle”
\mathcal{T}_{X}\to X
, and then
\Ad g(\delta)
is defined by the following diagram:

\begin{CD}\mathcal{T}_{X}@<{d\mu_{g}}<{}<\mathcal{T}_{X}\\
@A{\Ad g(\delta)}A{}A@A{}A{\delta}A\\
X@>{\mu_{g}^{-1}}>{}>X\end{CD}

It is easy to see that the embedding
\xi\colon\Lie\Aut(X)\hookrightarrow\VF(X)
defined above is
\Aut(X)
-equivariant:

\xi_{\Ad(g)A}=\Ad g(\xi_{A})\ \text{ or }\ \xi_{\Ad(g)A}(gx)=d\mu_{g}\,\xi_{A}(x).

Ind-varieties and ind-groups carry a natural topology,
called
Zariski-topology
or
ind-topology
. All topological notation in this paper will be with respect to this topology.

A subset
Y\subseteq\mathfrak{V}
of an ind-variety
\mathfrak{V}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{V}_{k}
is called
bounded
if it is contained in
\mathfrak{V}_{k}
for some
k
. It is called
algebraic
if it is bounded and locally closed.
If
X
is a
G
-variety, then the canonical map
G\to\Aut(X)
is a homomorphism of ind-groups, and the image of
G
is closed and algebraic,
see
[
FK18
, Proposition 2.7.1]
. In particular, every algebraic subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is closed and thus a linear algebraic group. It follows that
\Lie G\subseteq\Lie\Aut(X)
is a Lie subalgebra and that
\Lie G
is canonically embedded into the vector fields
\VF(X)
.

Convention.
In the following our ind-groups and ind-varieties will always be
affine
ind-groups and
affine
ind-varieties. By abuse of notation, we will constantly identify, for an algebraic group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
, the Lie algebra
\Lie G
with its image
\xi(\Lie G)\subseteq\VF(X)
, although the map
\xi
is an anti-homomorphism.

1.3.
Algebraically generated groups

A first result showing a very strong relation between the Lie algebra of an ind-group and the group itself is the following, see
[
FK18
, Proposition 2.2.1(3) and (4)]
.

Proposition 1.3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be an ind-group. Then the connected component
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
is an algebraic group if and only if
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is finite dimensional.

We will prove a similar statement for so-called algebraically generated groups, see
[
AFK13
]
.
Let
(G_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of connected algebraic subgroups of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
.
The subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
generated by the
G_{i}
will be called
algebraically generated
(Definition
2.1.1
).

Let
L(G)\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
be the Lie subalgebra generated by the Lie algebras
\Lie G_{i}
.
We will see in Theorem
2.3.1
that
L(G)

is invariant under the action of
G
and of the closure
\overline{G}
. This implies that

L(G)\subseteq\Lie\overline{G}
is an ideal.

Question 1
.

Do we have
L(G)=\Lie\overline{G}
?

Our result in this direction is the following, see Theorem
2.5.1
.

Theorem A
.

The subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic group if and only if
L(G)
is finite dimensional, and in this case we have
\Lie G=L(G)
.

See also Theorem
2.3.1
.
Our example in section
6
gives an infinite dimensional
L
where Question
1
above has a positive answer, see Remark
6.4.3
(1).

Theorem A above is an important ingredient in the proof of the following result.

Theorem B
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be a solvable subgroup generated by a family of connected algebraic subgroups. If the family is finite, then
G
is an algebraic group. In general,
G
is nested, i.e. a filtered union of algebraic subgroups, and is of the form
U_{G}\rtimes T
where
T
is a torus and
U_{G}
a nested unipotent group.

In particular, if
G
is generated by a family of unipotent algebraic groups, then
G=U_{G}
is a nested unipotent group.

The proof of Theorem B will be given in Section
5.1
, see Theorem
5.1.1
.

Remarks 1.3.2
.

(1)

Clearly, a connected nested ind-group is algebraically generated.

(2)

If
G
is an algebraic group and
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\subseteq G
a finite set of connected closed subgroups, then the subgroup
H
generated by the
H_{i}
is closed. (Indeed, there is a sequence
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}
such that the product
P:=H_{i_{1}}H_{i_{2}}\cdots H_{i_{N}}
is dense (and constructible) in
\overline{H}
. It then follows that
PP=\overline{H}
, hence
H=\overline{H}
.)

As a consequence we see that a subgroup of a nested ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
generated by finitely many connected algebraic groups is an algebraic group.

Note that this statement does not hold for non-connected subgroups. E.g. the two matrices

A:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&\phantom{-}0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and

B:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-\frac{1}{2}\\
2&\phantom{-}0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
have both order 4, and the product
AB=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-2&\phantom{-}0\\
\phantom{-}0&-\frac{1}{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
has infinite order. Thus
\overline{\langle A,B\rangle}=N
, the normalizer of the diagonal torus, but
\langle A,B\rangle\subseteq\SL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})
. It follows that the subgroup of
\SL_{2}({\Bbbk})
generated by the finite groups
\langle A\rangle
and
\langle B\rangle
is not closed.

(3)

If the ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
verifies
Tits
’ alternative, then the assumption of solvability of
G
in Theorem B can be replaced by the weaker assumption that
G
has no nonabelian free subgroup; see e.g.
[
AZ21
]
and the literature therein for a discussion of
Tits
’ alternative in ind-groups of type
\Aut(X)
.

1.4.
Solvability and triangulation

The de Jonquières subgroup
\Jonq(n)\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
consists of the automorphisms of the form

\varphi=(a_{1}x_{1}+f_{1},a_{2}x_{2}+f_{2}(x_{1}),\ldots,a_{n}x_{n}+f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}))

where
a_{i}\in{{\Bbbk}^{*}}
and
f_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}]
.
It is known that the unipotent elements of
\Jonq(n)
form a solvable subgroup of derived length
n
which is not nilpotent for
n>1
(see Remarks
5.2.1
and
5.3.3
). More generally, we have the following result.

Theorem C
.

A nested unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
is
solvable of derived length
\leq\max\{\dim Ux\mid x\in X\}\leq\dim X
.

It is known that the derived length of a nilpotent (respectively, a solvable) connected Lie group
G
acting faithfully on a Hausdorff topological space
M
is bounded above by
\dim M
(respectively, by
\dim M+1
),
see
[
ET79
, Theorem 1.2]
. Up to passing to a finite index subgroup, the former estimate works as well in the case of a finitely generated nilpotent group acting faithfully on a quasi-projective variety
X
defined over a field of characteristic zero
[
Abb22
, Theorem B]
.

The proof of Theorem C will be given in Section
5.3
, see Theorem
5.3.1
. It is based on the following important triangulation result, see Theorem
5.2.2
.

Theorem D
.

Assume that a nested unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
has a dense orbit in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
. Then
U
acts transitively on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and
U
is triangulable, i.e., it is conjugate to a subgroup of the de Jonquières subgroup.

In
[
Bas84
]

Bass
gave an example of a
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
which is not triangulable, i.e. the image
A\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{3})
of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
is not conjugate to a subgroup of
\Jonq(3)
. The reason is that the fixed points set
({\mathbb{A}}^{3})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}}
is a hypersurface with an isolated singularity. This is not possible for a triangulable action, because for such an action the fixed point set has the form
X\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. With the same idea one can construct non-triangulable
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-actions in any dimension, see
[
Pop87
]
.

The
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action of
Bass
corresponds to the locally nilpotent vector field
\delta:=(xz+y^{2})(-2y\partial_{x}+z\partial_{y})
, and the image
A
of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
contains the famous Nagata automorphism

\eta=(x-2y(xz+y^{2})-z(xz+y^{2})^{2},y+z(xz+y^{2}),z).

Due to the celebrated
Shestakov-Umirbaev
Theorem (
[
SU03
]
) this
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-subgroup is not contained in the tame subgroup of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{3})
. However, it becomes tame in
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
, see
[
Smi89
]
, but it is still non-triangulable in
{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
, see
[
Fre06
, Lemma 3.36]
.
The question arises if this action is
stably triangulable
, i.e. becomes triangulable in
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
for a suitable
d\geq 1
. In this context we have the following negative answer.

Proposition 1.4.1
.

Consider a
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and assume that the fixed point set is a hypersurface with an isolated singularity. Then the action is not stably triangulable.

Proof.

(a)
Denote by
F\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
the fixed point set and by
p\in F
the isolated singularity. For the quotient morphism
\pi\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))
has dimension
\geq 1
and thus is not contained in the singularities of
F
. If we extend the action to
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
where
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
acts trivially on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
, then
({\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d})/\!\!/{\mathbb{G}}_{a}={\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
and the quotient morphism is equal to
\tilde{\pi}:=\pi\times\id
. Moreover, the fixed point set is
\tilde{F}:=F\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
, and the singularities of
\tilde{F}
are
{\tilde{F}}_{\text{sing}}:=\{p\}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
. It follows that
{\tilde{\pi}}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}({\tilde{F}}_{\text{sing}}))
is not contained in
{\tilde{F}}_{\text{sing}}
.

(b)
Now assume that the action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d}
is triangulable, so that the corresponding vector field is equivalent to one of the form

\tilde{\delta}=f_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+f_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}+\cdots+f_{m}\partial_{x_{m}}

where
m:=n+d
and
f_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}]
. The fixed point set
\tilde{F}
is an irreducible hypersurface defined by an invariant (irreducible) function
h
, and so
h
must divide all the
f_{i}
. Since the singular points of
\tilde{F}
form a subvariety of dimension
d
this implies that
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{n}=0
. In fact, if
h
would depend on less than
n
variables, then the zero set
\{h=0\}\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
would have the form
X\times{\mathbb{A}}^{d+1}
and so the singular set of
\tilde{F}
would have at least dimension
d+1
.

Let
f_{r+1}
be the first non-zero coefficient of
\tilde{\delta}
. Then
r\geq n
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}
are invariants. Since
h
divides
f_{r+1}
we see that
h
only depends on the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
. It follows that the linear
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-invariant morphism
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
, maps the fixed point set
\tilde{F}
to the hypersurface
F_{r}:=\{h=0\}\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{r}
, and

\tilde{F}=F_{r}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{m-r}
. This shows that
\varphi^{-1}(\varphi({\tilde{F}}_{\text{sing}}))={\tilde{F}}_{\text{sing}}
. Since the invariant map
\varphi
factors through the quotient map
\tilde{\pi}
this contradicts what we have seen in (a).
∎

Remarks 1.4.2
.

(1)

The non-triangulable
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-actions on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
constructed in
[
Pop87
]
are not stably triangulable.

(2)

The Nagata automorphism
\eta
is not contained in a unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{3})
which has a dense orbit in
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
, by our Theorem D.

(3)

If we consider the diagonal action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
where
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
acts on
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
as in
Bass
’ example and on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
by translation, then this new action is triangulable. Indeed, we have the following result.

Let
U
be a unipotent group acting on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
. Then the diagonal action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times U
where
U
acts by left multiplication on
U
is triangulable.

Indeed, consider the isomorphism
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times U\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\times U
given by
(x,u)\mapsto(u^{-1}x,u)
. This morphism is
U
-equivariant with respect to the diagonal action on the left hand side and the action on
U
by left multiplication (and the trivial action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
) on the right hand side. Since the action on
U
is triangulable by our Theorem D above the claim follows.

Likewise, given any
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
R
and a locally nilpotent derivation
\partial
of
R
, its extension
\overline{\partial}
to
R[x]
by letting
\overline{\partial}(x)=1
is conjugate to the locally nilpotent
R
-derivation
\delta
of
R[x]
defined by
\delta(x)=1
.

1.5.
Locally finite subsets

Let
V
be a vector space over
{\Bbbk}
.
We denote by
\Ll(V)
the algebra of linear endomorphisms of
V
.
An endomorphism
\lambda\in\Ll(V)
is called
locally finite
if the linear span
\langle\lambda^{j}(v)\mid j\in{\mathbb{N}}\rangle

is finite dimensional for any
v\in V
.
It is called
semisimple
if there is a basis of eigenvectors, and
locally nilpotent
if for any
v\in V
there is an
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lambda^{m}(v)=0
.
Every locally finite endomorphism
\lambda
has a uniquely defined
additive Jordan-decomposition

\lambda=\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{n}

where
\lambda_{s}
is semisimple,
\lambda_{n}
locally nilpotent, and
\lambda_{s}\circ\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n}\circ\lambda_{s}
.

A subset
S\subseteq\Ll(V)
is called
locally finite
if every
v\in V

is contained in a finite dimensional subspace
W\subseteq V
which is invariant
under all elements from
S
. If
S\subseteq\GL(V)
, then
S
is locally finite if
and only if the group
\langle S\rangle
generated by
S
is locally finite.

Note that a locally finite subspace
A\subseteq\Ll(V)
is not necessarily finite dimensional.
In fact, let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
be a basis of
V:={\Bbbk}^{\infty}
and define
\lambda_{k}\in\Ll(V)
by
\lambda_{k}(e_{j}):=\delta_{kj}e_{j}
.
Then
A:=\bigoplus_{k}{\Bbbk}\lambda_{k}
is an infinite dimensional locally finite subspace.

Definition 1.5.1
.

Let
X
be an affine variety. A morphism
\varphi\colon X\to X
is called

locally finite
if the induced endomorphism
\varphi^{*}\in\Ll(\mathcal{O}(X))

is locally finite. It is called
semisimple
if
\varphi^{*}
is semisimple, and
locally nilpotent
if
\varphi^{*}
is locally nilpotent.

A subgroup
H\subseteq\Aut(X)
is called
locally finite
if the image of
H

in
\Ll(\mathcal{O}(X))
is locally finite.

Note that a subgroup
H\subseteq\Aut(X)
is locally finite if and only if the
closure
\overline{H}\subseteq\Aut(X)
is an algebraic group. If
g\in\Aut(X)
is locally finite,
then the closed commutative subgroup
\overline{\langle g\rangle}
has the form
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{p}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{q}\times F
where
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
q\in\{0,1\}
and
F
is a finite cyclic group.

There are many examples of subgroups
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
that are not locally finite while being generated by locally finite elements.
We will discuss such an example in Section
6
. We also refer to the interesting discussions in

[
FK18
, 9.4.3–9.4.5]
and
[
PR22
]
of subgroups
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
which consist of locally finite elements.

1.6.
Locally finite vector fields

Recall that the vector fields
\VF(X)
are the derivations
\Der(\mathcal{O}(X))
of
\mathcal{O}(X)
. Since
\Der(\mathcal{O}(X))\subseteq\Ll(\mathcal{O}(X))
we can talk about
locally finite vector fields
and
locally finite subspaces
of
\VF(X)
.

Example 1.6.1
.

If
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is an algebraic subgroup, then its Lie algebra
\Lie(G)\subseteq\VF(X)
is locally finite. These Lie algebras are called
algebraic
.

In contrast to the general situation we have the following finiteness result.

Lemma 1.6.2
.

Let
L\subseteq\VF(X)
be a locally finite subspace. Then
L
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

If
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
is a set of generators, then every vector field
\delta
is determined by the values
\delta(f_{1}),\ldots,\delta(f_{m})
.
It follows that for any subspace
L\subseteq\VF(X)
the linear map
L\to\mathcal{O}(X)^{m}
,
\delta\mapsto(\delta(f_{1}),\ldots,\delta(f_{m}))
is injective.
If
L
is locally finite, then the image of this map is contained in the finite dimensional subspace
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}Lf_{i}
, and the claim follows.
∎

The fact that a vector field is determined by its values on a finite generating set of
\mathcal{O}(X)
has the following important consequences.

Lemma 1.6.3
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
be a finite dimensional subspace generating
\mathcal{O}(X)
,
and let
\delta\in\VF(X)
be a vector field. If
V
is
\delta
-invariant,
then the following holds.

(1)

\delta
is locally finite.

(2)

\delta
is semisimple if and only if
\delta|_{V}\in\gl(V)
is semisimple.

(3)

\delta
is locally nilpotent if and only if
\delta|_{V}\in\gl(V)
is nilpotent.

(4)

If
\delta=\delta_{s}+\delta_{n}
is the Jordan decomposition in
\Ll(\mathcal{O}(X))
, then
\delta_{s},\delta_{n}\in\VF(X)
.

(5)

V
is
\delta_{s}
- and
\delta_{n}
-invariant, and
\delta|_{V}=\delta_{s}|_{V}+\delta_{n}|_{V}\in\gl(V)
is the usual Jordan decomposition in
\gl(V)
.

Proof.

Denote by
V^{(n)}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
the linear subspace generated by the
n
-fold products of elements from
V
.
Then the following is obvious: (a)
V^{(n)}
is
\delta
-invariant; (b) if
\delta|_{V}
is semisimple resp. nilpotent, then so is
\delta|_{V^{(n)}}
. This proves (1)–(3).
The claim (4) follows from [FK18, Proposition 7.6.1], and (5) follows from
(2) and (3).
∎

Question 2
.

Let
L_{i}\subseteq\VF(X)
(

.

Let

be the Lie subalgebra generated by a family of locally finite Lie subalgebras

,

.
Assume that each

Remark 1.6.4
.

For a locally finite vector field

there is a uniquely determined minimal algebraic group

. Moreover,

1.7.
The adjoint action on
\VF(X)

For any vector field
\delta
we have the
adjoint action

\ad\delta
on
\VF(X)
defined in the usual way:

\ad\delta(\eta):=[\delta,\eta]=\delta\circ\eta-\eta\circ\delta.

Lemma 1.7.1
.

Let
\delta\in\VF(X)
be a locally finite vector field with Jordan decomposition
\delta=\delta_{s}+\delta_{n}
.
Then
\ad\delta
acting on
\VF(X)
is locally finite, and
\ad\delta=\ad\delta_{s}+\ad\delta_{n}
is the Jordan decomposition.

Proof.

Let
V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
be a finite dimensional
\delta
-invariant subspace which generates
\mathcal{O}(X)
. Then we have an inclusion

\VF(X)\hookrightarrow\Ll(V,\mathcal{O}(X)),\hskip 9.24994pt\mu\mapsto\mu|_{V}.

Given
\mu\in\VF(X)
we choose a finite dimensional
\delta
-invariant subspace
W
which contains
\mu(V)
.
In
\Ll(\mathcal{O}(X))
we have

(\ad\delta)^{m}\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\delta^{m-i}\mu\,\delta^{i}.

Hence
(\ad\delta)^{m}\mu
sends
V
to
W
for all
m\geq 0
, and so the linear span
\langle(\ad\delta)^{m}\mu\mid m\geq 0\rangle\subseteq\VF(X)
is finite dimensional.
This shows that
\ad\delta
is locally finite.

If
\delta
is locally nilpotent, then
(\delta|_{V})^{m}=0
and
(\delta|_{W})^{m}=0
for a suitable
m>0
. The above formula implies that
(\ad\delta)^{2m-1}\mu=0
, hence
\ad\delta
is locally nilpotent.

Next, assume that
\delta
is semisimple. Then we have eigenspace decompositions
V=\bigoplus_{a}V_{a}
and
\mathcal{O}(X)=\bigoplus_{b}\mathcal{O}(X)_{b}
. As a consequence, we get a decomposition

\Ll(V,\mathcal{O}(X))=\bigoplus_{a,b}\Ll(V_{a},\mathcal{O}(X)_{b})

into eigenspaces of
\ad\delta
, and so
\ad\delta
is semisimple.

Finally,
\ad\delta_{s}
and
\ad\delta_{n}
commute, because
[\delta_{s},\delta_{n}]=0
, and thus
\ad\delta=\ad\delta_{s}+\ad\delta_{n}
is the Jordan decomposition.
∎

1.8.
Toral subalgebras

Definition 1.8.1
.

A finite dimensional Lie subalgebra
S\subseteq\VF(X)
is called
toral
if it consists of semisimple elements.
It follows that
S
is commutative
[
Hum72
, 8.1, Lemma]
and thus locally finite.
In particular, we obtain a
weight decomposition

\mathcal{O}(X)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S^{*}}\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}
where

\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}:=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(X)\mid\tau f=\alpha(\tau)\cdot f\text{ for }\tau\in S\}.

If
T\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a torus, then
\Lie T\subseteq\VF(X)
is a toral subalgebra,
but it is not true that every toral subalgebra is of this form. Indeed, if
T\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a two dimensional torus,
then the one dimensional tori
S\subseteq T
are the kernels of certain characters
\chi\in X(T)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

whereas every one dimensional subspace of
\Lie T
is a toral subalgebra. The best we can get is the following result.

Lemma 1.8.2
.

For a toral Lie subalgebra
S\subseteq\VF(X)
there exists a uniquely defined smallest torus
T\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
\Lie T\supseteq S
.
Moreover, if a subspace
M\subseteq\VF(X)
is invariant under the adjoint action of
S
, then
M
is invariant under
T
.

Proof.

(a)
One easily verifies that the weight decomposition of
\mathcal{O}(X)
has the following properties, cf.

[
FK18
, Proposition 7.6.1
and its proof]
:

\bullet

\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}\cdot\mathcal{O}(X)_{\beta}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha+\beta}
;

\bullet

the set of weights
\Lambda:=\{\gamma\mid\mathcal{O}(X)_{\gamma}\neq 0\}\subseteq S^{*}
generates a free subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{i}
.

This defines a faithful action of the torus
T:=({\mathbb{G}}_{m})^{k}
on
\mathcal{O}(X)
in the following way: For
\alpha=\sum_{i}n_{i}\mu_{i}\in\Lambda
,

f\in\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}
and
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in T
we put

t\,f:=t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{k}^{n_{k}}\cdot f.

The corresponding action of
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in\Lie T={\Bbbk}^{m}
is then given by the vector field
\delta_{c}
where

\delta_{c}|_{\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}}=\text{scalar multiplication with }\textstyle\sum_{i}n_{i}c_{i}\text{ for }\alpha=\sum_{i}n_{i}\mu_{i}\in\Lambda.

It follows that
\Lie T
contains
S
and that every torus with this property contains
T
, see

[
FK18
, proof of Proposition 7.6.1(2)]
.

(b)
With respect to the adjoint action of
S
on
\VF(X)
we also have a weight space decomposition

\VF(X)=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in S^{*}}\VF(X)_{\gamma}
where

\VF(X)_{\gamma}:=\{\delta\in\VF(X)\mid(\ad\tau)\delta=\gamma(\tau)\cdot\delta\text{ for }\tau\in S\},

see Lemma
1.7.1
. Equivalently, we have

\VF(X)_{\gamma}:=\{\delta\in\VF(X)\mid\delta(\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha+\gamma}\text{ for }\alpha\in S^{*}\}.

It follows that if
\VF(X)_{\gamma}\neq 0
, then
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
. In fact, if
\delta\in\VF(X)_{\gamma}
,
\delta\neq 0
, then there is an
\alpha\in\Lambda
and a non-zero
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)_{\alpha}
such that
\delta f\neq 0
. Thus
\alpha
and
\alpha+\gamma
belong to
\Lambda
, and so
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
.
Hence, the subspaces
\VF(X)_{\gamma}
are weight spaces of the action of
T
on
\VF(X)
, and thus every
S
-invariant subspace of
\VF(X)
is invariant under
T
.
∎

1.10.
A free subgroup of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and its closure

In Section
6
we provide an example of a
nonabelian free subgroup
F\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}
of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
generated by two locally finite elements whose product is not locally finite.
In particular,
F
is not locally finite. We compute its closure
\mathfrak{F}:=\overline{F}\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and describe the
Lie algebra
\Lie\mathfrak{F}
. It occurs that
\mathfrak{F}
coincides with the double centralizer of
F

(Lemma
6.1.1
) and is a free product of two abelian nested unipotent ind-subgroups
\mathfrak{J}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{-}
(Theorem
6.4.2
(3)).
Furthermore,
any algebraic subgroup
G
of
\mathfrak{F}
is abelian and unipotent and is conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathfrak{J}
or
\mathfrak{J}^{-}

(Theorem
6.4.2
(4)).

The construction shows that
\mathfrak{F}
contains two algebraic subgroups
U
and
V
, both isomorphic to
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
, with the following properties:

\langle U,V\rangle=U*V,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathfrak{F}=\overline{\langle U,V\rangle}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\Lie\mathfrak{F}=\langle\Lie U,\Lie V\rangle_{\text{\tiny Lie}},

see Lemma
6.2.1
.
This is an instance where Question
1
has a positive answer.

Yet another instance is the following. Consider the ind-group
\mathfrak{G}:=\Aut_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{K}[x,y])
where
\mathcal{K}:={\Bbbk}[z]
,
and let
\mathfrak{G}^{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be the subgroup consisting of tame automorphisms
of
{\Bbbk}[x,y,z]
. Then
\mathfrak{G}
is connected and closed in
\Aut_{\Bbbk}({\Bbbk}[x,y,z])
,
\mathfrak{G}^{t}\subsetneqq\mathfrak{G}
is closed and

\Lie\mathfrak{G}^{t}=\Lie\mathfrak{G}
, see
[
FK18
, Theorem 17.3.1]
. The group
\mathfrak{G}^{t}
is generated by the affine and the de Jonquières
transformations which fix

(cf.
[
FK18
, Remark 17.3.10]
).
Since the de Jonquières group is nested (Remark
5.2.1
), it is algebraically generated (Remark
1.3.2
(1)), and so
\mathfrak{G}^{t}
is algebraically generated.
By the
Umirbaev-Shestakov
Theorem, the

U
of
\mathfrak{G}
corresponding to the locally nilpotent Nagata derivation is not contained in

.
However, since the algebraically generated group
\mathfrak{H}:=\langle\mathfrak{G}^{t},U\rangle
is contained in

and

1.11.
Ind-group actions and path-connected subsets

The theory of algebraic group actions on affine varieties can be generalized, to some extent, to ind-groups acting on affine ind-varieties,
see
[
FK18
]
. If
\mathfrak{G}
is an ind-group, then a
\mathfrak{G}
-variety

X
is a variety with a
\mathfrak{G}
-action such that the map
\mathfrak{G}\times X\to X

is a morphism of ind-varieties.

A subset
M\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
of an ind-variety
\mathfrak{X}
is called
path-connected
if for any two points
x,y\in M

there is an irreducible variety
Y
and a morphism
\varphi\colon Y\to M
such that
x,y\in\varphi(Y)\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
,
cf.
[
Ram64
, p. 26]
.
One can always assume that
Y
is an irreducible curve, because any two points in an irreducible variety are contained in an irreducible curve,
cf.
[
FK18
, Section 1.6]
. A connected ind-variety is not necessarily path-connected (
[
FK18
, Example 1.6.5]
), but a connected ind-group is path-connected (
[
FK18
, Proposition 2.2.1]
).

Note that a path-connected subset of an algebraic variety is not necessarily closed; take
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and remove a ball around the origin. However, we will see that a path-connected subgroup of an algebraic group is closed, cf. Corollary
2.4.2
.

In contrast to the finite-dimensional case, a strict closed subgroup of a path-connected ind-group may have the same
Lie algebra, see
[
FK18
, Theorem 17.3.1]
and the example in Section
1.10
.
This disproves
[
Sha81
, Theorems 1 and 2]
.
However, a homomorphism of a path-connected ind-group to an ind-group is uniquely determined by the induced homomorphism
of their Lie algebras, see
[
FK18
, Proposition 7.4.7]
.

In the last section
7
we define a canonical Lie subalgebra
L_{\mathfrak{G}}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
for an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
and show that for a surjective homomorphism
\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{H}
the corresponding homomorphism
L_{\mathfrak{G}}\to L_{\mathfrak{H}}
is also surjective. We do not know if this holds for
\Lie\mathfrak{G}\to\Lie\mathfrak{H}
. We also study subgroups
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
of finite codimension and show that under additional assumptions
L_{\mathfrak{H}}
has finite codimension in
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

2.
Algebraically generated subgroups

2.1.
Orbits of algebraically generated subgroups

Let
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{G}_{k}
be an ind-group.
The following concept was introduced in
[
AFK13
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

A subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is called
algebraically generated
if there is
a family
(G_{i})_{i\in I}
of connected algebraic subgroups
G_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}

generating
G
as an abstract group:
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
.

It is clear that an algebraically generated subgroup is path-connected (section
1.11
), hence connected. The following result
can be found in
[
AFK13
, Prop. 1.2 and 1.3]
.
For the reader’s convenience we provide a short argument.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

Let
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)
be an algebraically generated subgroup.
For every
x\in X
the
G
-orbit
Gx\subseteq X
is open in its closure
\overline{Gx}
.
Moreover, there is a finite sequence
j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}\in I
such that

Gx=G_{j_{1}}\cdots G_{j_{n}}x
for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

(a) Choose a sequence
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}
such that
\dim\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x}
is maximal. Since

\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x}
is irreducible, it
follows that
G_{j_{1}}\cdots G_{j_{\ell}}x\subseteq\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x}
for every sequence

j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell}\in I
. Hence
\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x}=\overline{Gx}
, and there is an open dense set
U\subseteq\overline{Gx}
contained in
G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x
.
This implies that
Gx=GU
is open in
\overline{Gx}
.

(b) For the second claim we first remark that for every
x\in X
with

\dim Gx
maximal there is an open set
U_{x}\subseteq X
containing

x
and a sequence
\boldsymbol{i}_{x}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
, depending on

x
, such that
\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}y}=\overline{Gy}
for all
y\in U_{x}
. Indeed, if
d:=\dim Gx
is
maximal and
Gx\subseteq\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}x}
,
then
\dim\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}y}=d
for all
y

in an open neighborhood of
x
. Now the union
U:=\bigcup_{x}U_{x}
is a
G
-invariant open set, hence covered by finitely many

U_{x_{j}}
. By joining the finitely many sequences
\boldsymbol{i}_{x_{j}}

corresponding to the points
x_{j}
, we obtain a sequence

(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\ell})
such that
\overline{G_{j_{1}}\cdots G_{j_{\ell}}y}=\overline{Gy}
for all
y\in U
. A standard
argument implies that
G_{j_{\ell}}G_{j_{\ell-1}}\cdots G_{j_{2}}G_{j_{1}}G_{j_{2}}\cdots G_{j_{\ell}}y=Gy
for all

y\in U
. Since
X\setminus U
is a closed
G
-invariant subvariety
of smaller dimension, the claim follows by induction.
∎

Remark 2.1.3
.

A similar result holds for the action of a connected ind-group

,
see

2.2.
The multiplication map

In the following lemma we use the adjoint action
\Ad
of
\mathfrak{G}
on its Lie algebra
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
, cf.
[
FK18
]
. Moreover, for
g\in\mathfrak{G}
, we denote by
\rho_{g}\colon\mathfrak{G}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{G}
the right multiplication with
g
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be connected algebraic subgroups
and consider the “multiplication map”
\varphi\colon G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m}\to\mathfrak{G}
.
Fix
g_{i}\in G_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, and set
g:=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}
.
Then the image of the composition

d(\rho_{g^{-1}})_{g}\circ d\varphi_{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})}\colon T_{g_{1}}G_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{g_{m}}G_{m}\to\Lie\mathfrak{G}

is given by
\sum_{i}\Ad h_{i}(\Lie G_{i})
where
h_{1}=e
and
h_{i}:=g_{1}\cdots g_{i-1}
for
i>1
.

Proof.

The restriction of the composition
\rho_{g^{-1}}\circ\varphi
to

\{g_{1}\}\times\cdots\times\{g_{i-1}\}\times G_{i}\times\{g_{i+1}\}\times\cdots\times\{g_{n}\}\subseteq G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}

is given by

\varphi_{i}\colon G_{i}\to\mathfrak{G},\hskip 9.24994pth\mapsto g_{1}\cdots g_{i-1}hg_{i+1}\cdots g_{n}g^{-1}=h_{i}(hg_{i}^{-1})h_{i}^{-1}

i.e.
\varphi_{i}=\Int h_{i}\circ\rho_{g_{i}^{-1}}
, and so the image of
T_{g_{i}}G_{i}
under
d(\varphi_{i})_{g_{i}}
is equal to
\Ad h_{i}(\Lie G_{i})
.
∎

In case of an automorphism group
\mathfrak{G}=\Aut(X)
we can reformulate the lemma above in terms of vector fields. Recall that the action of
\Aut(X)
on the vector fields is given by
\Ad g(\delta)(gx)=d\mu_{g}\,\delta(x)
, see section
1.2
.

If
L\subseteq\VF(X)
is a subspace, then
L(x)\subseteq T_{x}X
denotes the image of
L
under the evaluation map
\ev_{x}\colon\VF(X)\to T_{x}X
.

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}\subseteq\Aut(X)
be connected algebraic subgroups. Set
L_{i}:=\Lie G_{i}\subseteq\VF(X)
and consider the “orbit map”

\theta_{x}\colon G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m}\to X,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbf{g}:=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\mapsto\mathbf{g}x:=g_{1}\cdots g_{m}x.

Then the image of the differential
(d\theta_{x})_{\mathbf{g}}\colon T_{g_{1}}G_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{g_{m}}G_{m}\to T_{\mathbf{g}x}X
is given by

\sum_{i}d\mu_{h_{i}}L_{i}(g_{i}\cdots g_{m}x)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2.1
above the image of the differential
(d\theta_{x})_{\mathbf{g}}
is given by
\sum_{i}\Ad h_{i}(L_{i})(\mathbf{g}x)
. Since
\Ad h_{i}(L_{i})(\mathbf{g}x)=d\mu_{h_{i}}L_{i}(g_{i}\cdots g_{m}x)
the claim follows.
∎

2.3.
Lie algebras of algebraically generated groups

For an algebraically generated subgroup
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
of the ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
we define

L(G):=\langle\Lie G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle_{\text{\rm Lie}}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}

to be the Lie subalgebra
generated by the
\Lie G_{i}
. If
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic subset and
g\in Z
we can use the right multiplication with
g^{-1}
to send
T_{g}Z
into
T_{e}\mathfrak{G}=\Lie\mathfrak{G}
:
d\rho_{g^{-1}}\colon T_{g}Z\hookrightarrow\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.
Note that for an algebraic subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
this image of
T_{g}G
is equal to
\Lie G
for any
g\in G
.

Theorem 2.3.1
.

Let
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be an algebraically generated subgroup.

(1)

L(G)
is stable under the adjoint actions of
G
and of
\overline{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
.
In particular,
L(G)
is an ideal in
\Lie\overline{G}
.

Assume in addition that the index set
I
is countable and that the base field
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable.

(2)

If
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic subset contained in
G
, then the image of

under

for any

(3)

If

is an algebraic subgroup contained in
G
, then
\Lie H\subseteq L(G)
. In particular,
L(G)

depends only on
G

G_{i}
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

We do not know if the additional assumptions for the statements (2) and (3) are necessary.

Proof.

(1)
It suffices to show that
L(G)
is invariant under all the
G_{i}
. Since
G_{i}
is connected, we are reduced to prove that
L(G)
is invariant under the adjoint action of
\Lie G_{i}
(see
[

) which holds by construction.

(2)
We first show that
Z\subseteq G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}
for a suitable sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
. We can clearly assume that
Z
is irreducible. Since the index set

is countable the group

is the union of countably many products
G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}

is the union of countably many constructible subsets

. Since the base field
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable we get
Z=Z\cap G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}\subseteq G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}
for a suitable sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
(
[
FK18
, Lemma 1.3.1]
).

Now consider the multiplication map
\varphi\colon G_{i_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{i_{m}}\to\mathfrak{G}

\varphi^{\prime}\colon\varphi^{-1}(Z)\to Z
is smooth on a non-empty open set

where we can assume that

is surjective for all

it follows from Lemma

that the image of the tangent space

in
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is contained in

\sum_{k}\Ad h_{i_{k}}(\Lie G_{i_{k}})\subseteq L(G)
where the latter inclusion follows from (1).

(3)
This is an immediate consequence of (2).
∎

Now consider the case where
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)
and so

.
If
Y\subseteq X
is a
G

for all

and so

Choosing

Proposition 2.3.3
.

We have
T_{x}Gx=L(G)(x)
for all
x\in X
. In particular, any vector field in
L(G)

is tangent to the
G
-orbits.

Proof.

We already know that
L(G)(x)\subseteq T_{x}Gx

\theta_{x}\colon G_{j_{1}}\times\cdots\times G_{j_{n}}\to Gx\subseteq X,

see Proposition

the differential

is also surjective. Thus we get

by Lemma

By Theorem

(1)

d\mu_{h_{j}}L_{j}(g_{j}\cdots g_{n}x)\subseteq d\mu_{h_{j}}L(G)(g_{j}\cdots g_{n}x)=L(G)(\mathbf{g}x),

and the claim follows.
∎

2.4.
Ramanujam’s alternative

The following result is due to
Ramanujam
(
[
Ram64
, Theorem on p. 26]
).

Proposition 2.4.1
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be a path-connected subgroup of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

G
is an algebraic subgroup;

(ii)

G
contains algebraic subsets of arbitrary large dimension.

Proof.

Assume that we are not in case (ii), and let
Y\subseteq G
be an irreducible algebraic subset
of maximal dimension
d
. We may assume that
e\in Y
. We claim that
G\subseteq\overline{Y}=\overline{G}
.

Since
G
is path-connected we can find, for any
g\in G
, an irreducible variety
C_{g}
and a morphism

\varphi_{g}\colon C_{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
such that
\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\subseteq G
and
e,g\in\varphi_{g}(C_{g})
. Clearly,

\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\cdot Y\subseteq G
, and the closure
\overline{\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\cdot Y}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an irreducible subvariety which contains
\overline{Y}
and
g
. The constructible set
\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\cdot Y
contains a subset
U_{g}
which is open and dense in its closure
\overline{\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\cdot Y}
. Thus
\dim U_{g}\leq d
, and so
\overline{U_{g}}=\overline{\varphi_{g}(C_{g})\cdot Y}=\overline{Y}
. Hence,
G\subseteq\overline{Y}=\overline{G}
as claimed.

As a consequence,
\overline{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic subgroup. Since
Y\subseteq G

is open and dense in
\overline{G}
it follows that
\overline{G}=Y^{-1}\cdot Y\subseteq G
,
and so (i) holds.
∎

Corollary 2.4.2
.

A path-connected subgroup of an algebraic group is a closed subgroup.

2.5.
The finite dimensional case

The next result shows that an algebraically generated subgroup of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic group if and only if the Lie algebra
L(G)
is finite dimensional.
It also proves Theorem A from Section
1.3
of the introduction.

Theorem 2.5.1
.

Let
G=\langle G_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be an algebraically generated group. Then the following two statements are equivalent.

(i)

G
is an algebraic subgroup of
\mathfrak{G}
;

(ii)

The Lie algebra
L(G)
is finite dimensional.

In this case
\Lie G=L(G)
. In particular,
L(G)
is locally finite and algebraic.

Proof.

(a) Assume that
G
is an algebraic group. Since
G_{i}\subseteq G
is a closed subgroup for any
i
, we have
\Lie G_{i}\subseteq\Lie G
, and so
L(G)\subseteq\Lie G
is finite dimensional.
Moreover, there is a finite sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
such that the multiplication map
\varphi\colon G_{i_{1}}\times\cdot\times G_{i_{m}}\to G
is surjective. Then Lemma
2.2.1
applied to
g_{1}=g_{2}=\cdots=g_{m}=e
shows that
\Lie G\subseteq\sum_{i}\Lie G_{i}
, and so
L(G)=\Lie G
.

(b) Now assume that
L(G)
is finite dimensional, and consider the multiplication map
\varphi\colon G_{i_{1}}\times\cdot\times G_{i_{m}}\to\mathfrak{G}
. There is an open dense subset
U\subseteq G_{i_{1}}\times\cdot\times G_{i_{m}}
such that
\varphi(U)\subseteq\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is open and contained in

(\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}})_{\text{\it reg}}
, and that
\varphi|_{U}\colon U\to\varphi(U)
is smooth. It follows from Lemma
2.2.1
that
T_{g}\varphi(U)\subseteq L(G)
for all
g\in\varphi(U)
and so
\dim\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}}\leq\dim L(G)
. Hence there is a sequence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
such that
\dim\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}}
is maximal which implies that all
G_{i}
are contained in
\overline{G_{i_{1}}\cdots G_{i_{m}}}
, and so
G
is an algebraic subgroup.
∎

3.
Integration of vector fields

3.1.
Integration of locally finite Lie algebras

The next result is our Theorem E from the introduction, see Section
1.9
.

Theorem 3.1.1
.

Let
L\subseteq\VF(X)
be a Lie subalgebra.

(1)

If
L
is finite dimensional and generated by locally nilpotent vector fields, then
L
is algebraic, i.e. there is a connected algebraic subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
L=\Lie G
.

(2)

If
L
is locally finite, then there exists a unique minimal connected algebraic subgroup

G\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
L\subseteq\Lie G
.

Proof of (1).

Assume that
L
is finite dimensional and generated by locally nilpotent vector fields
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{m}
.
By
[
FK18
, Proposition 7.6.1]
there are (uniquely determined) algebraic subgroups
U_{i}\subseteq\Aut(X)
,

U_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
, such that
\Lie U_{i}={\Bbbk}\eta_{i}
. Now Theorem
2.5.1
implies that
G:=\langle U_{i}\mid i=1,\ldots,m\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)

is an algebraic group with
\Lie G=L
.
∎

Example 3.1.2
.

Assume that
L\subseteq\VF(X)
is a locally finite semisimple Lie subalgebra. Then there is a semisimple group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
L=\xi(\Lie G)
. In particular,
L
is algebraic.

In fact, the semisimple Lie algebra
L
is generated by the root subspaces with respect to some Cartan subalgebra
(see
[
Hum72
, Proposition 8.4(f)]
), hence by nilpotent elements. Lemma
1.6.3
(3) shows that these elements are locally nilpotent in
\VF(X)
, and thus
L
is generated by locally nilpotent vector fields.

The proof of (2) needs some preparation. It will be given at the end of section
3.2
.
Let
L=L_{0}\ltimes R
be the Levi decomposition where
L_{0}
is semisimple and
R:=\rad L
is the solvable radical (see
[
Pro07
, 10.1.6, pp. 305–306]
.
The example above shows that
L_{0}=\Lie G_{0}
for a semisimple subgroup
G_{0}\subseteq\Aut(X)
.
It remains to find a solvable subgroup
Q\subseteq\Aut(X)
normalized by
G_{0}
such that
\Lie Q\supseteq R
. Then the product
G:=G_{0}Q
has the required property.

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Let
M\subseteq\VF(X)
be a locally finite solvable Lie subalgebra. Assume that
M
contains with every element
\mu

its semisimple and locally nilpotent parts
\mu_{s}
and
\mu_{n}
.
Then the locally nilpotent elements from
M
form a Lie subalgebra
M_{n}
, and
M=S\ltimes M_{n}
where
S
is any maximal toral subalgebra of
M
(see Definition
1.8.1
).

Proof.

Since
M
is locally finite we may assume that
M\subseteq\gl(V)
where
V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
is a finite dimensional subspace generating
\mathcal{O}(X)
.
Since
M
is solvable there is a basis of
V
such that
M
is contained in the upper triangular matrices

\mathfrak{b}_{d}\subseteq\gl_{d}
where
d:=\dim V
(Lie’s Theorem). Clearly, the nilpotent elements from
M
belong to the subalgebra

\mathfrak{n}_{d}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}_{d}
of upper triangular matrices with zeros along the diagonal, and so
M_{n}:=M\cap\mathfrak{n}_{d}
is the ideal of
M
consisting of the nilpotent elements.
By Lemma
1.6.3
every nilpotent element of
M
is also locally nilpotent viewed as a vector field on
X
, and vice versa. If
M=M_{n}
then
S=0

and
M=S\ltimes M_{n}
, as desired.

In the general case,
choose a maximal toral subalgebra
S\subseteq M
, and consider the corresponding weight space decomposition

M=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S^{*}}M_{\alpha}
where
S^{*}
is the group of characters of
S
and

M_{\alpha}:=\{\mu\in M\mid\ad\tau(\mu)=[\tau,\mu]=\alpha(\tau)\cdot\mu\text{ for }\tau\in S\}.

Similarly, we get a decomposition
\mathfrak{b}_{d}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S^{*}}(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{\alpha}
.
Using the identity
[s,a\cdot b]=[s,a]\cdot b+a\cdot[s,b]
we get

(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{\alpha}\cdot(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{\beta}\subseteq(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{\alpha+\beta}
which implies that
M_{\alpha}
consists of nilpotent elements for
\alpha\neq 0
.
Hence,
\bigoplus_{\alpha\neq 0}M_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{d}
,
while
M_{0}=\cent_{M}(S)\supseteq S
.

Let
\mu\in M_{0}
have Jordan decomposition
\mu=\mu_{s}+\mu_{n}
. Since
S
commutes with
\mu

it also commutes with
\mu_{s}
and
\mu_{n}
, and so
\mu_{s}\in M_{0}
and
\mu_{n}\in M_{0}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{d}
. Since
S
is a maximal toral subalgebra, we have
\mu_{s}\in S
, and so

M_{0}=S\oplus(M_{0}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{d})
. As a consequence we see that
M_{n}=(M_{0}\cap\mathfrak{n}_{d})\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\neq 0}M_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{d}
and
M=S\ltimes M_{n}
.
∎

From the first part of the proof we get the following result.

Corollary 3.1.4
.

If a locally finite solvable Lie subalgebra
M\subseteq\VF(X)
is generated by locally nilpotent elements, then it consists of locally nilpotent elements.

3.2.
Jordan-saturated subspaces

The assumption in Lemma
3.1.3
above leads to the following definition.
A locally finite subspace
W\subseteq\VF(X)
is called

(shortly
J

with every element
\eta

\eta_{s}
and
\eta_{n}
.
Clearly, for any algebraic subgroup

the Lie algebra

-saturated.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let

-saturated Lie subalgebra
\tilde{R}\subseteq\VF(X)

containing

has the following properties:

(1)

\tilde{R}
is locally finite and solvable.

(2)

If a subspace
V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
is
R
-invariant, then it is invariant under
\tilde{R}
.

(3)

If an automorphisms
\varphi\in\Aut(X)
normalizes
R
, then it normalizes
\tilde{R}
.

Notice that any algebraic Lie subalgebra of
\VF(X)
which contains
R
also contains
\tilde{R}
.

Proof.

As before, we fix a finite dimensional
R
-invariant subspace
V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
which generates
\mathcal{O}(X)
.
Choosing a suitable basis of
V
we may assume that the image of
R
in
\gl(V)=\gl_{d}
is contained in the upper triangular matrices
\mathfrak{b}_{d}
.

If
\eta=\eta_{s}+\eta_{n}
is the Jordan-decomposition of an element
\eta\in R
, then every finite dimensional
R
-invariant subspace

V\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
is invariant under
\eta_{s}
and
\eta_{n}
, hence invariant under the Lie subalgebra
R_{1}
generated by
R+{\Bbbk}\eta_{n}
. Thus
R_{1}
is locally finite.
The Jordan-decomposition of
\eta
carries over to the image of
\eta
in
\gl(V)=\gl_{d}
. Hence the images of
\eta_{s}
and
\eta_{n}
also belong to
\mathfrak{b}_{d}

which shows that the image of
R_{1}
is contained in
\mathfrak{b}_{d}
.

This procedure of adding the locally nilpotent part and forming the Lie algebra has to end with a locally finite and solvable Lie subalgebra

\tilde{R}
containing
R
with the properties (1) and (2).

Finally, assume that
\varphi\in\Aut(X)
stabilizes
R
, and let
\eta\in R
. Then
\varphi(\eta)=\varphi(\eta_{s})+\varphi(\eta_{n})
is the Jordan
decomposition of
\varphi(\eta)\in R
, hence
\varphi(\eta_{s})
and
\varphi(\eta_{n})
belong to
\tilde{R}
.
Now it follows from the construction that
\tilde{R}
is invariant under
\varphi
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(2).

Let
L=L_{0}\ltimes R

4.
Nested ind-groups and nested subgroups

4.1.
Nested groups and nested Lie algebras

For the following concept see
[
FK18
, Section 9.4]
and
[
KPZ17
]
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

An ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
is called
nested
if it admits an admissible filtration consisting of algebraic subgroups.
A Lie algebra
L
is called
nested
if there exists a sequence

L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq L
of finite dimensional Lie algebras
L_{i}
such that
L=\bigcup_{i}L_{i}
.

Lemma 4.1.2
.

(1)

The Lie algebra
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
of a nested ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
is nested.

(2)

Every filtration of a nested ind-group by algebraic groups is admissible.

(3)

A connected nested ind-group admits a filtration by connected algebraic subgroups. In particular, it is algebraically generated (Definition
2.1.1
).

(4)

Assume that the base field
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable. If an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
has a filtration by algebraic subgroups, then
\mathfrak{G}
is nested.

Proof.

(1) This is clear.

(2) Let
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{G}_{k}
be a nested ind-group, i.e. the
\mathfrak{G}_{k}
are algebraic subgroups, and let
G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be a sequence of algebraic subgroups such that
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}
. Then each
G_{i}
is contained in some
\mathfrak{G}_{k}
, and
\mathfrak{G}_{k}=\bigcup_{i}(\mathfrak{G}_{k}\cap G_{i})
is a countable union of closed subgroups. It follows from Lemma
4.1.3
below that
\mathfrak{G}_{k}=\mathfrak{G}_{k}\cap G_{i}
for some
i
and so
\mathfrak{G}_{k}\subseteq G_{i}
.

(3) If
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{G}_{k}
is connected, then
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{G}_{k}^{\circ}
by
[
FK18
, Proposition 2.2.1]
.

(4) If
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable, then every filtration by closed algebraic subsets is admissible, see
[
FK18
, Theorem 1.3.3]
.
∎

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

For any algebraic group
G
there exists a finite set
\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\}\subseteq G
such that
G=\overline{\langle g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}
.

Outline of Proof.

The statement is clear for a unipotent group
U
since
\overline{\langle u\rangle}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
for a unipotent element
u\neq e
. It is also clear for a torus
T
since there is always an element
t\in T
such that
T=\overline{\langle t\rangle}
. Finally, for a semisimple group
G
we fix a maximal torus
T
, choose a “dense” element
t\in T
and unipotent elements in every root subgroup of
G
.
∎

Definition 4.1.4
.

A subgroup
G
of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
is called
quasi-nested
if there exists a sequence
G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
of bounded subgroups of
\mathfrak{G}
(see section
1.2
) such that
G=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}
. It is called
nested
if the
G_{i}
are algebraic subgroups.

The situation here is subtle. Every nested subgroup
G
of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
has a natural structure of a (nested) ind-group given by the filtration by algebraic subgroups, and thus
\Lie G
is well defined and is a subalgebra of
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
. But in general
G
is not a closed ind-subgroup as shown by the following example.

Example 4.1.5
.

The subgroup
\mu_{2}\subseteq{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
of elements of order a power of
2
is nested by the finite subgroups
\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
. But it is not closed.

Remarks 4.1.6
.

(a)

A quasi-nested subgroup
G=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
defines a nested subgroup, namely
\bigcup_{i}\overline{G_{i}}
.

(b)

Every subgroup of a nested ind-group is quasi-nested.

(c)

Let
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{H}
be a homomorphism of ind-groups. If
\mathfrak{G}
is nested, then the image
\varphi(\mathfrak{G})\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
is a nested subgroup.

(d)

If the subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is quasi-nested,
G=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}
, then one can always assume that the subgroups
G_{i}\subseteq G
are closed in
G
.

Proposition 4.1.7
.

Assume that the base field
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable.
Then a path-connected quasi-nested subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is nested.

Proof.

Let
G=\bigcup G_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
where the
G_{i}
are closed in
G
, see Remark
4.1.6
(d). Define
G_{i}^{\circ}\subseteq G_{i}
to be the path-connected component of
e
, i.e. the set of elements of
G_{i}
which can be connected to
e\in G_{i}
. Then
G_{i}^{\circ}
is a path-connected bounded subgroup, hence an algebraic group by Corollary
2.4.2
.

We claim that
G=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}^{\circ}
. For any
g\in G
there is an irreducible variety
Y
and a morphism
\varphi\colon Y\to\mathfrak{G}
such that
e,g\in\varphi(Y)\subseteq G
. Since the subgroups
G_{i}\subseteq G
are closed, the inverse images
\varphi^{-1}(G_{i})\subseteq Y
are closed, and
\bigcup_{i}\varphi^{-1}(G_{i})=Y
. Since
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable there is an
i
such that
Y=\varphi^{-1}(G_{i})
. Hence
\varphi(Y)\subseteq G_{i}^{\circ}
and so

4.2.
Integration of nested Lie algebras

If

is a nested subgroup, then

is a nested Lie algebra filtered by locally finite subalgebras.
The converse of this is also true.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
L=\bigcup_{i}L_{i}\subseteq\VF(X)
be a nested Lie subalgebra.
Then the following hold.

(1)

If
L
is generated by locally nilpotent vector fields, then
there exists a nested unipotent subgroup

G=\bigcup_{i}G_{i}\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
L=\bigcup_{i}\Lie G_{i}
.

(2)

Assume that all
L_{i}
are locally finite.
Then there exists a smallest nested subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
such that
L\subseteq\Lie G\subseteq\VF(X)
.
Moreover, a closed ind-subgroup
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\Aut(X)
contains
G
if and only if
L\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

Proof.

(1) Any finite collection
\mathcal{S}\subseteq L
of locally nilpotent vector fields
generates a Lie subalgebra, say,
M_{\mathcal{S}}
of some
L_{j}
. According to Theorem
3.1.1
(1),

M_{\mathcal{S}}=\Lie G_{\mathcal{S}}
for an algebraic subgroup

G_{\mathcal{S}}
of
\Aut(X)
. On the other hand,
L_{i}
is contained in some
M_{i}:=M_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}
, hence
L=\bigcup_{i}M_{i}
.
We may suppose that
M_{i}\subseteq M_{i+1}
, and so
G_{i}\subseteq G_{i+1}
where

. The union

is a nested subgroup of
\Aut X
such that

L=\bigcup_{i}\Lie G_{i}
.

(2) Let
G_{i}\subseteq\Aut(X)
be the smallest algebraic subgroup with
\Lie G_{i}\supseteq L_{i}
(see Theorem
3.1.1
(2)).
Then
G_{i}\subseteq G_{i+1}
for all

and so

is a nested subgroup with the required properties.

Now assume that

and define

. Then

∎

5.1.
Solvable subgroups

This section is devoted to the proof of our Theorem B from the introduction.

Theorem 5.1.1
.

Let
G=\langle H_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
be a solvable algebraically generated group. If the family
I
is finite, then
G
is a solvable algebraic group
with Lie algebra
\Lie G=L(G)=\langle\Lie H_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
. In general,

is nested and is of the form

where

is a torus and

is a nested unipotent group consisting of the unipotent elements of
G
.

Corollary 5.1.2
.

A solvable subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
generated by unipotent elements is quasi-nested and consists of unipotent elements.

Proof of Corollary.

If
G
is generated by the unipotent elements
(u_{i})_{i\in I}
, then the closure
\overline{G}\subseteq\Aut(X)
contains
the subgroup
\widetilde{G}
generated by the unipotent subgroups
\overline{\langle u_{i}\rangle}
.
We will see in the following Lemma
5.1.3
(2) that
\overline{G}
is also solvable.
It then follows from the theorem above that
\widetilde{G}=U_{\widetilde{G}}\rtimes T
is nested, hence

is quasi-nested and is contained in

.
∎

The proof of Theorem
5.1.1
needs some preparation.
Given a solvable group
G
we denote by
G^{(i)}
the members of the derived series of
G
and by
d(G)
the derived length:

G^{(0)}:=G\supseteq G^{(1)}:=(G,G)\supseteq G^{(2)}:=(G^{(1)},G^{(1)})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G^{(d)}=\{e\}.

If
G
is nilpotent, then
G_{i}
stands for the members of the lower central series of
G
and
n(G)
for its nilpotency class:

G_{0}:=G\supseteq G_{1}:=(G,G)\supseteq G_{2}:=(G,G_{1})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{n}=\{e\}.

In the following lemma we collect some important facts about closures of solvable and nilpotent groups.
Statement (2) can be found in
[
FP18
, Lemma 2.3(3)]
.

Lemma 5.1.3
.

Let
G,H\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be subgroups of an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
.

(1)

We have
(\overline{G},\overline{H})\subseteq\overline{(G,H)}
,

\overline{G}^{(i)}\subseteq\overline{G^{(i)}}
and
\overline{G}_{i}\subseteq\overline{G_{i}}
.

(2)

If
G
is solvable, then so is
\overline{G}
, and
d(\overline{G})=d(G)
.

(3)

If
G
is nilpotent, then so is
\overline{G}
, and
n(\overline{G})=n(G)
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
\gamma\colon\mathfrak{G}\times\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}
be the commutator map,
(g,h)\mapsto ghg^{-1}h^{-1}
.
Then
\gamma(\overline{G}\times\overline{H})\subseteq\overline{\gamma(G\times H)}
, and so

(\overline{G},\overline{H})\subseteq\langle\overline{\gamma(G\times H)}\rangle\subseteq\overline{(G,H)}
.
Now the remaining inclusions follow.

(2)
By (1) we have
G^{(i)}\subseteq\overline{G}^{(i)}\subseteq\overline{G^{(i)}}
. Hence
\overline{G}^{(d(G))}=\{e\}
and

\overline{G}^{(i)}\neq\{e\}
for
i<d(G)
.

(3)
By (1) we have
G_{i}\subseteq\overline{G}_{i}\subseteq\overline{G_{i}}
. Hence
\overline{G}_{n(G)}=\{e\}
and

\overline{G}_{i}\neq\{e\}
for
i<n(G)
.
∎

Lemma 5.1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be an ind-group and
\mathfrak{H}
be a closed subgroup of
\mathfrak{G}
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

[\Lie\mathfrak{H},\Lie\mathfrak{G}]\subseteq\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{G}
is solvable, then so is
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

(3)

If
\mathfrak{G}
is nilpotent, then so is
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

Proof.

(Cf.
[
FK18
, Section 7.5]
)

(1) For a fixed
h\in\mathfrak{H}
consider the morphism

\gamma_{h}\colon\mathfrak{G}\to(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G}),\hskip 9.24994pt\gamma_{h}(g):=hgh^{-1}g^{-1}.

The differential
(d\gamma_{h})_{e}\colon\Lie\mathfrak{G}\to\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})}
is given by
(d\gamma_{h})_{e}=\Ad(h)-\id
.
Now fix
A\in\Lie\mathfrak{G}
and consider the morphism

\alpha_{A}\colon\mathfrak{H}\to\Lie\mathfrak{G},\hskip 9.24994pt\alpha_{A}(h):=\Ad(h)A-A=d\gamma_{h}(A).

The differential at
e
of this map is
-\ad(A)
; it sends
\Lie\mathfrak{H}
onto
[\Lie\mathfrak{H},A]
.
By the preceding,
(d\gamma_{h})_{e}(A)\in\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})}
for any
h\in\mathfrak{H}
and
A\in\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.
Hence,
[\Lie\mathfrak{H},A]\subseteq\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{G})}
for any
A\in\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.
This yields (1).

(2) Now assume that
\mathfrak{G}
is solvable. From the derived series for
\mathfrak{G}
we get the normal series

\mathfrak{G}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{G}\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(1)}}\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(2)}}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(n)}}=\{e\}

of closed subgroups with the property that the factor groups are all commutative (see Lemma
5.1.3
(1)). Passing to the series

\Lie\mathfrak{G}\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(1)}}\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(2)}}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(n)}}=0

we get from (1)

\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i+1)}}=\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{G}^{(i)},\mathfrak{G}^{(i)})}=\Lie\overline{(\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i)}},\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i)}})}\supseteq[\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i)}},\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i)}}].

Thus the factors
\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i)}}/\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}^{(i+1)}}
are all commutative, which implies that
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is solvable.

(3) Assume now that
\mathfrak{G}
is nilpotent. From the lower central series for
\mathfrak{G}
we get the series of closed subgroups

\mathfrak{G}_{0}=\mathfrak{G}\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{2}}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{n}}=\{e\}

and the corresponding series

\Lie\mathfrak{G}\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{1}}\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{2}}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{n}}=\{0\}.

By virtue of (1) we have

\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{i+1}}=\Lie\overline{(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{i})}=\Lie\overline{(\overline{\mathfrak{G}},\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{i}})}\supseteq[\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}},\Lie\overline{\mathfrak{G}_{i}}].

Thus
(*)
is a central series and so
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is nilpotent.
∎

Let

It is known that a path-connected ind-group

Lemma 5.1.6
.

Let

In the derived series

Proof of Theorem

(a) We first consider the case where

5.2.
Triangulation in
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})

By definition, an automorphism
\varphi
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
belongs to the de Jonquières subgroup
\Jonq(n)
(see Section
1.4
) if and only if the comorphism
\varphi^{*}
stabilizes the flag

{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n}]\supseteq{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]\supseteq\cdots\supseteq{\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2}]\supseteq{\Bbbk}[x_{1}].

Equivalently,
\varphi
stabilizes the coflag

\mathcal{F}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n-2}\to\cdots\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

which means that
\varphi
induce an automorphism on each
{\mathbb{A}}^{k}
such that the projections
{\mathbb{A}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{k-1}
,
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mapsto(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
are
\varphi
-equivariant.

Remark 5.2.1
.

\Jonq(n)
is a connected, nested solvable group of the form
\Jonq(n)=\Jonq(n)_{u}\rtimes T
where
T:={{\Bbbk}^{*}}^{n}
is the standard maximal torus and
\Jonq(n)_{u}
is the subgroup of unipotent elements, cf.
[
FK18
, Section 15.1]
.
\Jonq(n)
has derived length
d(\Jonq(n))=n+1
(
[
FP18
, Lemma 3.2]
) and thus
d(\Jonq(n)_{u})=n
, because
(\Jonq(n),\Jonq(n))=\Jonq(n)_{u}
.

Theorem 5.2.2
.

Let
U\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
be a nested unipotent subgroup. If
U
has a dense orbit on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, then
U
acts transitively on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, and
U
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Jonq(n)_{u}
.

Proof.

The first statement follows Corollary
4.1.8
and the fact, that
U
-orbits are closed.

For the second claim
we have to construct a
U
-stable coflag
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}\to\cdots\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
where the projections are of the form
{\mathbb{A}}^{k}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{k-1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\overset{\text{pr}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{A}}^{k-1}
.

Let
U_{x}\subseteq U
be the isotropy group of some point
x\in{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
. By Lemma
5.2.3
below we can find an element
a\in\Norm_{U}U_{x}\setminus U_{x}
. Then the subgroup
A:=\overline{\langle a\rangle}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
and normalizes
U_{x}
. Then there is a right action of
A
on
U/U_{x}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
which commutes with the
U
-action. Thus we get an action of
U
on the quotient
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/A:=\Spec\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})^{A}
such that the quotient map
p\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/A
is
U
-equivariant. It follows that
U
has a dense orbit in
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/A
and so
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/A\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}
, because orbits of unipotent groups are closed and isomorphic to affine spaces, cf.
[
FK18
, Theorem 11.1.1]
.

Moreover, the
A
-action on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
admits a local slice, hence a local section of
p
, see
[
Fre06
, Section 1.4, Principle 1(c)]
. Since
U
acts transitively on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and
p
is
U
-equivariant there exist local sections of
p
in any

and thus

is a principal
A
-bundle. By a theorem of Serre
[
Ser58
, Section 5.1]
we have
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})=0
for any affine variety
X
, and hence this bundle is trivial:

\begin{CD}{\mathbb{A}}^{n}@>{\simeq}>{}>{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\\
@V{}V{p}V@V{}V{\pr}V\\
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}/\!\!/A@>{\simeq}>{}>{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}\end{CD}

Thus, we have constructed a

-equivariant projection
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}
, and the claim follows by induction.
∎

Lemma 5.2.3
.

Let
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
be a nested unipotent subgroup. For any point

the normalizer
\Norm_{U}U_{x}
strictly contains the isotropy group

.

Proof.

The claim of the lemma is well-known for a unipotent algebraic group

since such a group is nilpotent.
In general,
U=\bigcup_{k}U_{k}
with closed unipotent algebraic subgroups

such that
U_{k}\subseteq U_{k+1}
, and, similarly,
U_{x}=\bigcup_{k}(U_{k})_{x}
with closed unipotent algebraic subgroups
(U_{k})_{x}=U_{x}\cap U_{k}
. Corollary

implies that we can find a

such that

(U_{k})_{x}
has the same orbits on

as

for all

.

Now we use the following general fact. If a group
G

, and if

, then

belongs to the normalizer of the isotropy group

if and only if the isotropy group of

, i.e. if and only if

Assume that

belongs to the normalizer of

, and so

Question 6
.

Let

5.3.
Nested unipotent subgroups of
\Aut(X)
are solvable

We know from Theorem B that a solvable subgroup

G
of
\Aut(X)
generated by a family of unipotent
algebraic groups is a nested unipotent group.
The next result shows that the converse holds as well.

Theorem 5.3.1
.

A nested unipotent subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
is
solvable of derived length
\leq\max\{\dim Ux\mid x\in X\}\leq\dim X
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case of a unipotent algebraic subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
. Then every orbit
O=Ux
is closed and isomorphic to an affine space. Therefore, by Theorem
5.2.2
and Remark
5.2.1
, the image of
U
in
\Aut(O)
has derived length
\leq\dim O
. If
m:=\max\{\dim Ux\mid x\in X\}
, then the
m
th member
U^{(m)}
of the derived series of
U
acts trivially on every orbit, hence on
X
, and so
U^{(m)}=\{e\}
.
∎

.

(1)

A solvable algebraically generated subgroup

is of derived length
\leq\dim X+1
.

(2)

Let

.

Remark 5.3.3
.

A unipotent algebraic group is nilpotent, but the nilpotency class of unipotent subgroups of
\Aut(X)
might not be bounded, and thus a nested unipotent subgroup is not necessarily nilpotent.

For example,
\mathcal{J}_{n}:=\Jonq(n)_{u}

. For

is neither, by Lemma

(3).
Consider, for instance, the case

, and define

It is easily seen that the

of the lower central series of

does not vanish, and so
(\Lie\mathcal{J}_{2})_{d}\neq 0
for any

.

\mathfrak{F}
is a free product of two nested closed abelian unipotent ind-subgroups
\mathfrak{J}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{-}\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
. In particular,
\mathfrak{F}
is torsion free.

\bullet

As a Lie algebra,
\Lie\mathfrak{F}
is generated by
\Lie U
and
\Lie V
where
U:=\overline{\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}

and
V:=\overline{\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
.

\bullet

Any algebraic subgroup of
\mathfrak{F}
is abelian and unipotent and is conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathfrak{J}
or
\mathfrak{J}^{-}
.

6.1.
Notation and first results

Let

T:=\{(ax,by)\mid a,b\in{\Bbbk}^{*}\}\subseteq\GL_{2}({\Bbbk})\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

denote the standard 2-dimensional torus, and let

\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\{\mathbf{g}\in\Aut(X)\mid\Jac(\mathbf{g})=1\}

where the
Jacobian
of
\mathbf{g}=(f,h)
is defined in the usual way:

\Jac(\mathbf{g})=\det\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}&\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\\
\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}&\frac{\partial h}{\partial y}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
Furthermore, we set

\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\{\mathbf{g}\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid\mathbf{g}(0)=0\},

the group of automorphisms fixing the origin, and

\SAut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\cap\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}).

There is a canonical homomorphism
D\colon\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\to\GL_{2}({\Bbbk})
defined by
D(\mathbf{g}):=(d\mathbf{g})_{0}
.
If
\mathbf{g}=(f,h)
, then
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
if and only if
f(0)=h(0)=0
, i.e.
f
and
h
have no constant terms, and
D(\mathbf{g})=(f_{1},h_{1})
, the endomorphism given by the linear terms of
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
.
We will be interested in the kernel of
D
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}:=\ker D

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid(d\mathbf{g})_{0}=\id\}

\displaystyle=\{(f,h)\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid(f_{1},h_{1})=(x,y)\}.

We get a split exact sequence of ind-groups

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>\mathfrak{A}@>{}>{}>\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})@>{D}>{}>\GL_{2}({\Bbbk})@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

Thus
\mathfrak{A}
is a closed path-connected ind-subgroup of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
which contains

\mathbf{u}=(x+y^{2},y)
and
\mathbf{v}=(x,y+x^{2})
, and so
\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
.

Let
S:=\{(\zeta x,\zeta^{2}y)\mid\zeta^{3}=1\}\subseteq T\cap\SLtwo\subseteq\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be the cyclic diagonal subgroup of order 3.
The elements of
S
commute with
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
and thus with all elements from
F
and hence from
\mathfrak{F}=\overline{F}
.

Lemma 6.1.1
.

One has
S=\Cent_{\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})}(F)
.

Proof.

Note that any
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
commuting with
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
, and therefore with
U
and
V
,
fixes the origin which is the unique common fixed point of
U
and
V
. It also leaves invariant the set of orbits of
U
and of
V
,
i.e. the pencils of horizontal and vertical lines. Hence,
\mathbf{g}\in T
, and an easy calculation shows that, indeed,
\mathbf{g}\in S
.
∎

Considering the action of
S
on
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
by conjugation, one sees that
\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
\mathfrak{A}
are stable under this action, and thus

\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{S}:=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathfrak{A}\mid\mathbf{g}\circ s=s\circ\mathbf{g}\text{ for all }s\in S\}.

Moreover,
S
also acts on the Lie algebras
\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
\Lie\mathfrak{A}
, and so

(1)

\Lie\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{A}^{S}\subseteq(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}.

6.2.
The Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{F}

The
divergence
\Div
of a vector field
\delta=f\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+h\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

is defined as
\Div\delta=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial h}{\partial y}
.
For the Lie algebras of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
,
\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
we have the following description:

\bullet

\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}\VF^{c}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\{\delta\in\VF({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid\Div\delta\in{\Bbbk}\}
,

\bullet

\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}\VF^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\{\delta\in\VF({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid\Div\delta=0\}
,

\bullet

\Lie\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\xrightarrow{\sim}\VF_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}):=\{\delta\in\VF^{c}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid\delta(0)=0\}
,

The first two are given in
[
FK18
, Proposition 15.7.2]
, and the last is an immediate consequence. The split exact sequence
(*)
from the previous section
6.1
implies that

\displaystyle\Lie\mathfrak{A}

\displaystyle=\ker(dD_{e}\colon\VF_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\to\gl_{2})

\displaystyle=\{\delta=f{\frac{\partial}{\partial x}}+h{\frac{\partial}{\partial y}}\in\VF_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid f_{1}=h_{1}=0\}.

Now consider the following vector fields from
\VF^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
,

\partial_{i,j}:=(j+1)x^{i+1}y^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-(i+1)x^{i}y^{j+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

where

(i,j)\in\Lambda:=\{(k,l)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\,\mid k,l\geq-1,k+l\geq 0\}.

The torus
T
acts by conjugation on
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and on
\VF({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
, and the
\partial_{i,j}
are
eigenvectors of weight
(i,j)
. Moreover, the action of
T
on
\VF^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is multiplicity-free, and we have the weight decompositions

\VF^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\Lambda}{\Bbbk}\partial_{i,j}\text{ \ and \ }\Lie\mathfrak{A}=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\Lambda,i+j>0}{\Bbbk}\partial_{i,j}.

Note that
\partial_{i,j}
is fixed by the finite subgroup
S\subseteq T
if and only if
i-j\equiv 0\mod 3
.
Setting
\Lambda_{0}:=\{(k,l)\in\Lambda\mid k-l\equiv 0\mod 3,(k,l)\neq(0,0)\}
we get

(2)

\Lie\mathfrak{A}^{S}\subseteq(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\Lambda_{0}}{\Bbbk}\partial_{i,j}.

The next lemma shows that
\Lie\mathfrak{F}=\Lie\mathfrak{A}^{S}=(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}
. In contrast to the situation of algebraic groups,
this does not automatically imply that
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{A}^{S}
. In fact,
Furter-Kraft
give an example of a closed ind-subgroup
of a path-connected ind-group which has the same Lie algebra, but is strictly smaller (see
[
FK18
, Theorem 17.3.1]
).
In order to show that
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{A}^{S}
(see Theorem
6.4.2
) we therefore need an additional argument.

Lemma 6.2.1
.

The Lie algebra
(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}
is generated by
\Lie U
and
\Lie V
, hence
\Lie\mathfrak{F}=\Lie\mathfrak{A}^{S}=(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}
.

Proof.

We have
\Lie U={\Bbbk}\partial_{-1,2}
and
\Lie V={\Bbbk}\partial_{2,-1}
. An easy calculation shows that

\ad\partial_{-1,2}(\partial_{i,j})=a\,\partial_{i-1,j+2}\,(i\geq 0)\text{ and }\ad\partial_{2,-1}(\partial_{i,j})=b\,\partial_{i+2,j-1}\,(j\geq 0)

with non-zero constants
a,b\in{\Bbbk}
. This implies that the Lie algebra generated by
\partial_{-1,2}
and
\partial_{2,-1}

contains all
\partial_{i,j}
with
(i,j)\in\Lambda_{0}
, hence
\langle\Lie U,\Lie V\rangle_{\text{Lie}}\supseteq(\Lie\mathfrak{A})^{S}
,
see formula (
2
). Since
\Lie U,\Lie V\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{F}
the claim follows from the inclusions in (
1
).
∎

It is easy to see that the vector field
\partial_{i,j}
is locally nilpotent if and only if
i=-1
or
j=-1
. In these cases,
there are unique algebraic subgroups
U_{-1,k}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
and
U_{k,-1}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
with
\Lie U_{-1,k}={\Bbbk}\partial_{-1,k}
and
\Lie U_{k,-1}={\Bbbk}\partial_{k,-1}
.
Note that
U_{-1,2}=U=\overline{\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle}
and
U_{2,-1}=V=\overline{\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle}
.
Moreover, the subgroups
U_{-1,k}
and
U_{-1,l}
commute as well as the vector fields
\partial_{-1,k}
and
\partial_{-1,l}
.

6.3.
De Jonquières subgroups and amalgamated products

This and the subsequent sections are largely inspired by
[
AZ13
, Section 4]
.
Consider the de Jonquières group

\Jonq:=\{(ax+h(y),cy+d)\mid a,c\in{\Bbbk}^{*},d\in{\Bbbk},h\in{\Bbbk}[y]\}

and its subgroups

\displaystyle\Jonq_{0}:=\{(ax+yf(y),by)\mid a,b\in{\Bbbk}^{*},f\in{\Bbbk}[y]\}=\Jonq\cap\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{0}:=\{(x+yf(y),y)\mid f\in{\Bbbk}[y]\}\subseteq\Jonq_{0}.

\mathfrak{J}_{0}
is a commutative closed unipotent ind-subgroup, isomorphic to
{\Bbbk}[y]^{+}
.
It admits a filtration by closed unipotent subgroups
\prod_{i=1}^{d}U_{-1,i}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. Note that
\Jonq_{0}=\mathfrak{J}_{0}\rtimes T
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

The group
\mathfrak{A}^{S}
is the free product
(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}*(\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{-})^{S}
where
\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{-}:=\tau\circ\mathfrak{J}_{0}\circ\tau
and
\tau:=[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}]
.

Proof.

(1)
The amalgamated product structure
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})=\Jonq*_{\mathfrak{B}}\Aff_{2}
where
\mathfrak{B}:=\Jonq\cap\Aff_{2}

implies that every element
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\setminus\mathfrak{B}
can be expressed in one of the following 4 forms, called
the type
of
\mathbf{g}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=j_{1}a_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=j_{1}a_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}a_{n},

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=a_{1}j_{1}a_{2}\cdots j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=a_{1}j_{1}a_{2}\cdots j_{n}a_{n+1}

where
j_{k}\in\Jonq\setminus\mathfrak{B}
and
a_{k}\in\Aff_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{B}
.
The length of the expression, its type and the degrees of the
j_{k}
are uniquely determined by
\mathbf{g}
.

(2)
Let now
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
. Using
\Jonq=\Jonq_{0}\rtimes\Trans_{2}
and

\Aff_{2}=\GL_{2}\rtimes\Trans_{2}
where
\Trans_{2}\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{2}
is the group of translations, one can assume that
j_{k}\in\Jonq_{0}\setminus B

and
a_{k}\in\GL_{2}\setminus B
where
B\subseteq\GL_{2}
is the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices.
Since
\GL_{2}\setminus B=U\tau B=B\tau U
where
U\subseteq B
is the unipotent radical, we see that any
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
has a presentation in one of the 4 forms

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots\tau j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}\tau,

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n}\tau

where
j_{k}\in\Jonq_{0}\setminus B
. Again, the length, the type and the degrees of the
j_{k}
are uniquely determined by
\mathbf{g}
.

(3)
Now we remark that

\Jonq_{0}=\mathfrak{J}_{0}\rtimes T
and

T\tau=\tau T
. It follows that
we can reach one of the following forms

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots\tau j_{n}t,\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}\tau t,

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n}t,\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n}\tau t

where
j_{k}\in\mathfrak{J}_{0}
and
t\in T
. We claim that this form is uniquely determined by
\mathbf{g}
.
Since the type and the length are given by
\mathbf{g}
, it suffices to compare the similar decompositions

\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}t=j_{1}^{\prime}\tau j_{2}^{\prime}\cdots j_{n}^{\prime}t^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}\tau t=j_{1}^{\prime}\tau j_{2}^{\prime}\cdots j_{n}^{\prime}\tau t^{\prime}.

It follows that

\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}t=j_{1}^{-1}j_{1}^{\prime}\tau j_{2}^{\prime}\cdots j_{n}^{\prime}t^{\prime}\text{ \ resp. \ }\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}\tau t=j_{1}^{-1}j_{1}^{\prime}\tau j_{2}^{\prime}\cdots j_{n}^{\prime}\tau t^{\prime},

hence
j_{1}^{\prime}=j_{1}
. Now the claim follows by induction.

(4)
Using the equalities
st=ts
and
s\tau s=\tau
for
s\in S
and
t\in T
we find for an
S
-invariant
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})

of the first form
\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}t
in (3) above that

\mathbf{g}=s\mathbf{g}s^{-1}=\begin{cases}(sj_{1}s^{-1})\tau(s^{-1}j_{2}s)\tau(sj_{3}s^{-1})\cdots(sj_{n}s^{-1})t&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\\
(sj_{1}s^{-1})\tau(s^{-1}j_{2}s)\tau(sj_{3}s^{-1})\cdots(s^{-1}j_{n}s^{-1})t&\text{if $n$ is even.}\end{cases}

In the first case, we get that each
j_{k}
is
S
-invariant. In the second case, we obtain
sj_{n}s=j_{n}
, a contradiction.
Looking at the other forms of
\mathbf{g}
in (3) we see that for an
S
-invariant
\mathbf{g}
the number of
\tau
’s must be even and each
j_{k}
is
S
-invariant, i.e.
j_{k}\in(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}^{S}
.

(5)
Finally, assume that
\mathbf{g}\in\mathfrak{A}^{S}
. If
\mathbf{g}
is given in one of the forms of (3), then
t=\id
, because

d\mathbf{g}_{0}=\id
and
(dj_{k})_{0}=\id
for
j_{k}\in(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}
. Since the number of
\tau
’s is even,
we see that such an automorphism
\mathbf{g}
is a product of the form
\cdots j_{k}j_{k}^{\prime}j_{k+1}j_{k+1}^{\prime}\cdots
where
j_{k}\in(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}

and
j_{k}^{\prime}\in(\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{-})^{S}=(\tau\mathfrak{J}_{0}\tau)^{S}=\tau(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}\tau
.
∎

We have seen in the proof above that every
\mathbf{g}\in\Aut_{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
has a unique presentation in one of the 4 forms

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots\tau j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=j_{1}\tau j_{2}\cdots j_{n}\tau,

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n}\tau

where
j_{k}\in\Jonq_{0}\setminus B
. As usual, we define the
degree
of an element
(f,h)\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
as
\deg(f,h):=\max\{\deg f,\deg h\}
.
The following lemma is known, see e.g.
[
Kam79
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 6.3.2
.

If
\mathbf{g}
is as above, then
\deg\mathbf{g}=\deg j_{1}\cdot\deg j_{2}\cdots\deg j_{n}
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the cases
\mathbf{g}=\tau j_{1}\tau\cdots\tau j_{n}=(f_{n},h_{n})
. We now prove by induction that
\deg f_{k}>\deg h_{k}

and that
\deg f_{k+1}=\deg f_{k}\cdot\deg j_{k+1}
. Clearly,
\deg f_{1}>\deg h_{1}=1
, because
j_{1}\notin B
. If
j_{k+1}=(x,y+p(x)
, then

(f_{k+1},h_{k+1})=\tau\circ j_{k+1}\circ(f_{k},h_{k})=(h_{k}+p(f_{k}),f_{k}).

Since
\deg j_{k+1}=\deg p>1
and
\deg h_{k}<\deg f_{k}
we get
\deg f_{k+1}=\deg p\cdot\deg f_{k}>\deg f_{k}
, hence the claim.
∎

6.4.
The fixed points under
S

Recall that
\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
denotes the closure of the free group
F
generated by
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
.

Proposition 6.4.1
.

(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{A}^{S}=(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}*(\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{-})^{S}
.

Proof.

We have seen in Section
6.3
that
\mathfrak{J}_{0}\simeq{\Bbbk}[y]^{+}
admits a filtration by the closed unipotent subgroups
\prod_{i=2}^{d}U_{-1,i}
,
d\geq 2
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{S}=\{(x+y^{2}f(y^{3}),y)\mid f\in{\Bbbk}[y]\}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\Bbbk}[y]^{+}
, and that
\mathfrak{J}_{0}^{S}
admits a filtration by the closed unipotent subgroups
\prod_{j=1}^{k}U_{-1,3j-1}
.
Note that this product contains any automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
of the form
(x+\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1},y)
.
We now show by induction that
U_{-1,3j-1}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
.

Assume that this holds for
j\leq k
. Set

\varphi:=\mathbf{u}\circ\mathbf{v}=(x+y^{2},y)\circ(x,y+x^{2})=\\
=(x+y^{2}+2x^{2}y+x^{4},y+x^{2})\in F=\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle

and consider the product

\varphi\circ(x-\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1},y)=:(f_{k},h_{k})\in F\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{k}U_{-1,3j-1}.

Then

\textstyle f_{k}(0,y)=-\sum_{1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1}+y^{2}+2y(\sum_{1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1})^{2}+(\sum_{1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1})^{4}.

In particular, the exponents of
y
in
f_{k}(0,y)
are all
\equiv 2\mod 3
.
Setting
c_{1}=1
the second power of
y
disappears and we get

\textstyle f_{k}(0,y)=-\sum_{2}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1}+2y(\sum_{1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1})^{2}+(\sum_{1}^{k}c_{j}y^{3j-1})^{4}.

Hence, for
j\leq k
, the coefficient
d_{j}
of
y^{3j-1}
has the form

d_{j}=-c_{j}+2\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=j}c_{j_{1}}c_{j_{2}}+\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}=j+1}c_{j_{1}}c_{j_{2}}c_{j_{3}}c_{j_{4}}.

It follows that for any
j\leq k
the system of equations
d_{1}=d_{2}=\cdots=d_{j}=0
admits a unique solution
in positive integers
c_{i}
which does not depend on
k
(
c_{2}=2,c_{3}=9,c_{4}=52,c_{5}=340,c_{6}=2394,\ldots
).
Moreover, we see that the coefficient
d_{k+1}

of
y^{3k+2}
is positive. But then

(t^{-3k-2}x,t^{-1}y)\circ(f_{k},h_{k})\circ(t^{3k+2}x,ty)\overset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}(x+d_{k+1}y^{3k+2},y)\in U_{-1,3k+2}.

Since
\mathfrak{F}
is closed and stable under the action of the torus
T
we get
U_{-1,3k+2}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
, and the claim follows.
∎

Summing up we have the following result.

Theorem 6.4.2
.

Let
F\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
be the subgroup generated by
\mathbf{u}:=(x+y^{2},y)
and
\mathbf{v}:=(x,y+x^{2})
, and denote by
\mathfrak{F}:=\overline{F}

its closure in
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
. Furthermore, define
\mathfrak{A}:=\{\mathbf{g}\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})\mid\mathbf{g}(0)=0,d\mathbf{g}_{0}=\id\}
.

(1)

F
is a free group in the two generators
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
, containing elements which are not locally finite.

(2)

\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{A}^{S}=\Cent_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
where
S:=\{(\zeta x,\zeta^{2}y)\mid\zeta^{3}=1\}\subseteq T
is the centralizer of
F
in
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
.

(3)

\mathfrak{F}
is a free product
\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}
where
\mathfrak{J}:=\{(x+y^{2}f(y^{3}),y)\mid f\in{\Bbbk}[y]\}=(\mathfrak{J}_{0})^{S}
and

\mathfrak{J}^{-}=\{(x,y+x^{2}f(x^{3}))\mid f\in{\Bbbk}[x]\}=\tau\mathfrak{J}\tau
. In particular,
\mathfrak{J}
is a commutative nested unipotent closed ind-subgroup of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
isomorphic to
{\Bbbk}[y]^{+}
.

(4)

Every algebraic subgroup of
\mathfrak{F}
is commutative and unipotent and is conjugate to a subgroup of
\mathfrak{J}

or of
\mathfrak{J}^{-}
.

Proof.

(1)
The first part is clear. For the second we
notice that
\deg(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v})^{k}
tends to infinity for
k\to\infty
. Hence,
\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}\in\mathfrak{F}
is not locally finite.

(2) & (3)
This is Proposition
6.4.1
and Lemma
6.1.1
.

(4)
For an algebraic subset
X\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
the degrees of the elements from
X
are bounded above. It follows from Lemma
6.3.2
that the elements of
X
have bounded length in
\mathfrak{F}
. Hence
a famous result of
Serre
’s (see
[
Ser03
, Theorem 8]
) implies that
an algebraic subgroup of the free product
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}
is
conjugate to a subgroup of one of the factors.
∎

Remarks 6.4.3
.

(1)

According to Lemma 3.2.1 the subgroup
\mathfrak{F}\subseteq\Aut(X)
is the closure of the subgroup generated by
U
and
V
,
and
\Lie\mathfrak{F}
is the Lie algebra generated by
\Lie U
and
\Lie V
. Thus our example is a positive instance of our Question
1
.

(2)

The free product
U*V
is strictly contained in
\mathfrak{F}
, because, by Lemma

, the degree of an element from
U*V
is a power of 2, and so
U*V
cannot contain all the groups
U_{-1,3j-1}
. Even more, by the same lemma, every algebraic subgroup of
U*V
is of bounded length, hence conjugate to either
U
or
V
by
Serre
’s result
[
Ser03
, Theorem 8]
).

(3)

The theorem above shows that there is an ind-structure on the free product of the two ind-groups
\mathfrak{J}

and
\mathfrak{J}^{-}
. However, this structure
is not universal
, i.e. it is not true that
given two homomorphisms of ind-groups
\mathfrak{J}\to\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{-}\to\mathfrak{G}
, then the induced homomorphism
\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}\to\mathfrak{G}
is a homomorphism of ind-groups.

In order to see this consider the two projections
\mathfrak{J}\to U\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
and
\mathfrak{J}^{-}\to V\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
. We claim that the induced homomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}\to\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is not an ind-homomorphism. In fact, the image of
\varphi
is
U*V\subseteq\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}
and
\varphi
induces the identity on
U*V
. Since
U*V
is dense in
\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}
it follows that
\varphi
is the identity on
\mathfrak{J}*\mathfrak{J}^{-}
, a contradiction.

7.
A canonical Lie algebra for an ind-group

For an ind-variety
\mathfrak{V}
the tangent space
T_{x}\mathfrak{V}
in a point
x\in\mathfrak{V}
can be defined as the limit

\varinjlim T_{x}X
where
X
runs through all algebraic subsets of
\mathfrak{V}
containing
x
. There is a canonical subspace of
T_{x}\mathfrak{V}
generated by those
T_{x}X
where
X
is smooth in
x
:

T^{(s)}_{x}\mathfrak{V}:=\Span(T_{x}X\mid X\subseteq\mathfrak{V}\text{ algebraic and }x\in X_{\text{\it reg}}).

If
\mathfrak{V}
is
strongly smooth in
x
, i.e. there is an admissible filtration
\mathfrak{V}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{V}_{k}
such that each
\mathfrak{V}_{k}
is smooth in
x
, then
T^{(s)}_{x}\mathfrak{V}=T_{x}\mathfrak{V}
.
In general, we could not prove any useful results for this subspace, but for ind-groups it turns out that it has very remarkable properties.

7.1.
Definition and first properties

Definition 7.1.1
.

For an ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
we define

L_{\mathfrak{G}}:=\Span(T_{e}X\mid X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}\text{ algebraic and }e\in X_{\text{\it reg}})\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}.

The next proposition shows that
L_{\mathfrak{G}}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is a canonical Lie subalgebra.

Proposition 7.1.2
.

(a)

L_{\mathfrak{G}}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
is stable under the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{G}
, i.e.
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is
an ideal in
\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

(b)

For every algebraic subgroup
G\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
we have
\Lie G\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

(c)

L_{\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}=L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

(d)

L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is finite dimensional if and only if
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
is an algebraic group, and in this case we have
L_{\mathfrak{G}}=\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

Proof.

(a) If
X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is an algebraic subset with
e\in X_{\text{\it reg}}
and if
g\in\mathfrak{G}
, then
X^{\prime}:=gXg^{-1}
is
an algebraic subset and
e\in X^{\prime}_{\text{\it reg}}
. Moreover,
\Ad g(T_{e}X)=T_{e}X^{\prime}\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
, and the claim follows.

(b) and (c) are clear.

(d) If
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
is an algebraic group, then the claims follow from (b) and (c). If
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is finite dimensional, then so is
L_{\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}
by (c).
As a consequence, the dimension of an irreducible subvariety
X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
is bounded by
\dim L_{\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}=\dim L_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
and the claim follows from Proposition
2.4.1
.
∎

The following results show that one has
L_{\mathfrak{G}}=\Lie\mathfrak{G}
in many cases, but we have no example where
L_{\mathfrak{G}}\neq\Lie\mathfrak{G}
.

Corollary 7.1.3
.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{G}
be algebraically generated by a family
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of connected algebraic subgroups. Then
L(\mathfrak{G})\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is an ideal.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{G}
is strongly smooth in
e
, then
L_{\mathfrak{G}}=\Lie(G)
.
This holds, in particular, for a nested ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
.

Recall that

\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n}):=\{\alpha\in\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})\,|\,{\rm jac}(\alpha)=1\}

is a normal subgroup of
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
with Lie algebra
\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})=\VF^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
,
the algebra of polynomial vector fields on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
with zero divergence (see Section
6.2
).

Corollary 7.1.4
.

We have
L_{\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})}=\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
and
L_{\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})}=\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
.

Note that
\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{2})
is not strongly smooth in
e
(see
[
FK18
, Corollary 14.1.2]
) and so we cannot apply Corollary
7.1.3
(2).

Proof.

By
[
Sha81
, Lemma 3]
, the Lie algebra

\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})=[\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n}),\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})]

is simple, and by Proposition
7.1.2
(a)
L_{\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})}
is a (non-zero) ideal in
\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
.
This proves the first claim.

The second claim is a consequence of Proposition
7.1.2
(b) due to the fact that

\Lie\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})=\Lie\SAut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})\oplus\Lie{\Bbbk}^{*},

where
{\Bbbk}^{*}\subseteq\Aut({\mathbb{A}}^{n})

is realized as the subgroup of the scalar multiplications
\lambda\cdot\id
,

\lambda\in{\Bbbk}^{*}
, cf.
[
FK18
, Prop. 15.7.2]
.
∎

7.2.
Functorial properties

Proposition 7.2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{G}
be a homomorphism of ind-groups.

(1)

Then
d\varphi_{e}(L_{\mathfrak{H}})\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
. In particular,
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is invariant under every ind-group automorphism
\mathfrak{G}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{G}
.

(2)

Assume that
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable. If
\varphi
is surjective, then the differential
d\varphi_{e}\colon L_{\mathfrak{H}}\to L_{\mathfrak{G}}
is surjective.

Proof.

(a) Let
Y\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
be an irreducible algebraic subset such that
e\in Y_{\text{\it reg}}
.
There is an open dense subset
U\subseteq Y_{\text{\it reg}}
such that
\varphi(U)\subseteq\overline{\varphi(Y)}_{\text{\it reg}}

and that
\varphi|_{U}\colon U\to\varphi(U)
is smooth in every point
u\in U
. In particular, for all
u\in U
, the image
\varphi(Y\!u^{-1})
is smooth in
e
, hence the image of
T_{e}\,Y\!u^{-1}
is contained in
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
for all
u\in U
. Now the claim follows from Lemma
7.2.3
below, because
U
is dense in
Y_{\text{\it reg}}
.

(b) Let
X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be an irreducible algebraic subset such that
e\in X_{\text{\it reg}}
. We have to show that
T_{e}X\subseteq d\varphi_{e}(L_{\mathfrak{H}})
.
Since
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable there exists an irreducible closed algebraic subset
Y\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
such that
\varphi(Y)=X
(
[
FK18
, Prop. 1.3.2]
). As in (a) we choose a dense open
U\subseteq Y
such that
\varphi(U)
is an open subset of
X_{\text{\it reg}}
and that
\varphi|_{U}\colon U\to\varphi(U)
is smooth.
Then
d\varphi_{e}(T_{e}\,Y\!u^{-1})=T_{e}\,X\varphi(u)^{-1}
for all
u\in U
. It follows that
T_{e}\,Xv^{-1}\subseteq d\varphi_{e}(L_{\mathfrak{H}})
for all
v\in\varphi(U)
, and hence for all
v\in X_{\text{\it reg}}
, again by Lemma
7.2.3
below.
∎

Remark 7.2.2
.

The assumption that
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable is necessary, as seen from the following example. Take the bijective homomorphism
{\Bbbk}^{+}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
where
{\Bbbk}^{+}
is endowed with the discrete topology.

Lemma 7.2.3
.

Let
Y\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
be an irreducible algebraic subset. Then

\Span(T_{e}\,Y\!g^{-1}\mid g\in Y)\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{H}
is finite dimensional, and

\Span(T_{e}\,Y\!g^{-1}\mid g\in Y_{\text{\it reg}})=\Span(T_{e}\,Y\!g^{-1}\mid g\in U)

for any dense subset
U\subseteq Y_{\text{\it reg}}
.

Proof.

Set
X:=\overline{YY^{-1}}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
. Then

T_{e}\,Y\!g^{-1}\subseteq T_{e}X
for all
g\in Y
which implies the first claim.

For the second claim let
\Grass_{n}T_{e}X
denote the Grassmannian of
n
-dimensional subspaces of
T_{e}X
where
n:=\dim Y
, and consider the morphism
\mu\colon Y_{\text{\it reg}}\to\Grass_{n}T_{e}X
given by
g\mapsto T_{e}\,Y\!g^{-1}
. Since
\Grass_{n}V\subseteq\Grass_{n}T_{e}X
is closed for any subspace
V\subseteq T_{e}X
it follows that a dense subset of
Y_{\text{\it reg}}
has the same span as
Y_{\text{\it reg}}
.
∎

Question 7
.

Assume we have a surjective homomorphism
\mathfrak{G}\to G
where
\mathfrak{G}
is a path-connected ind-group,

G
an algebraic group and
{\Bbbk}
uncountable.
Does
\mathfrak{G}
contain an algebraic
subgroup which is sent surjectively onto
G
?

7.3.
Subgroups of finite codimension

Let
\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{d}\mathfrak{G}_{d}
be an ind-group.

Definition 7.3.1
.

We say that a closed subgroup
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
is
of finite codimension
if

\mathfrak{G}_{d}\cdot\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{G}
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

For instance, if
\mathfrak{G}
is connected and acts on an algebraic variety
X
, then the stabilizer

\mathfrak{G}_{x}
of any point
x\in X
is of finite codimension. Indeed, by
[
FK18
, Prop. 7.1.2]
the orbit
\mathfrak{G}x
is an irreducible subvariety of
X
and
\mathfrak{G}x=\mathfrak{G}_{d}x

for some
d
. The latter implies
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{G}_{d}\cdot\mathfrak{G}_{x}
.

We do not know if every subgroup
\mathfrak{H}
of finite codimension of a connected ind-group
\mathfrak{G}

is the stabilizer of a point of a
\mathfrak{G}
-variety, i.e. if
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{H}
has a canonical structure
of an algebraic variety.

Example 7.3.2
.

The connected component

\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{G}
if and only if it has finite index in
\mathfrak{G}
. Indeed, a closed algebraic subset
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{G}

can only meet finitely many of the components of
\mathfrak{G}
. Similarly, if
\mathfrak{H}
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{G}
, then the image of
\mathfrak{H}
in
\mathfrak{G}/\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
has finite index.

One could expect that for a subgroup
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
of finite codimension the Lie algebra
\Lie\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}

has finite codimension as well. What we can show is the following result.

Proposition 7.3.3
.

Assume that the base field
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable.
Let
\mathfrak{G}
be an ind-group and
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
a closed subgroup of finite codimension.
If
\mathfrak{H}
is subnormal in
\mathfrak{G}
, i.e. there is a finite series
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\ell}=\mathfrak{G}

such that
\mathfrak{H}_{i}
is normal in
\mathfrak{H}_{i+1}
, then
L_{\mathfrak{H}}
has finite codimension in
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

The proof needs some preparation. For an algebraic subset
X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
we define

M_{X}:=\Span(T_{e}\,X\!g^{-1}\mid g\in X)\subseteq\Lie\mathfrak{G}.

We have seen in Lemma
7.2.3
that
M_{X}
is finite dimensional. Moreover, if
X
is smooth,
then
M_{U}=M_{X}
for an open dense subset
U
of
X
, and
M_{X}\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

Lemma 7.3.4
.

Assume that
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable.
Let
\mathfrak{G}
be an ind-group and
\mathfrak{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
a closed normal subgroup. If there exists a
closed irreducible algebraic subset
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
such that
Z\cdot\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{G}
,
then
L_{\mathfrak{G}}\subseteq M_{Z}+L_{\mathfrak{H}}
. In particular,
L_{\mathfrak{H}}
has finite codimension in
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
X\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
be a closed irreducible algebraic subset. Since
{\Bbbk}
is uncountable we can find an irreducible algebraic subset

Y\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
such that
Z\cdot Y\supseteq X
. Thus
X
is contained in the image of the multiplication morphism

\mu\colon Z\times Y\to\mathfrak{G}
,
\mu(z,y):=z\cdot y
. For every
g:=z\cdot y

where
(z,y)\in Z\times Y
we have the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}Z\times Y@>{\mu}>{}>\mathfrak{G}\\
@V{\rho_{z^{-1}}\times\Int z\,\circ\,\rho_{y^{-1}}}V{\simeq}V@V{\simeq}V{\rho_{g^{-1}}}V\\
Zz^{-1}\times z(Yy^{-1})z^{-1}@>{\mu}>{}>\mathfrak{G}\end{CD}

which induces the commutative diagram of tangent maps

\begin{CD}T_{z}Z\oplus T_{y}Y@>{d\mu_{(z,y)}}>{}>T_{g}\mathfrak{G}\\
@V{d\rho_{z^{-1}}\times\Ad z\,\circ\,d\rho_{y^{-1}}}V{\simeq}V@V{\simeq}V{d\rho_{g^{-1}}}V\\
T_{e}(Zz^{-1})\oplus T_{e}(z(Yy^{-1})z^{-1})@>{d\mu_{(e,e)}}>{}>T_{e}\mathfrak{G}\end{CD}

(2) There exists an irreducible open subset
U\subseteq\mu^{-1}(X)_{\text{\it reg}}
such that
\mu(U)\subseteq X_{\text{\it reg}}

is open and dense, and that
\mu|_{U}\colon U\to X
is smooth. Replacing
Y
by
\overline{\pr_{Y}(U)}
if necessary
we can assume that
\pr_{Y}(U)\subseteq Y_{\text{\it reg}}
. For
g=z\cdot y
where
(z,y)\in U

it follows from the diagram above that

T_{e}\,X\!g^{-1}\subseteq T_{e}\,Zz^{-1}+\Ad z(T_{e}\,Y\!y^{-1})\subseteq M_{Z}+\Ad z(L_{\mathfrak{H}}),

hence
M_{X_{\text{\it reg}}}\subseteq M_{Z}+L_{\mathfrak{H}}
by Lemma
7.2.3
. Since the
M_{X_{\text{\it reg}}}\subseteq L_{\mathfrak{G}}
generate
L_{\mathfrak{G}}
the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.3.3
.

We can clearly assume that
\mathfrak{H}
is normal in
\mathfrak{G}
.

(1) We first claim that
\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
. The ind-group
\mathfrak{G}
is a countable disjoint union

\mathfrak{G}=\bigcup_{j\in J}g_{j}\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}=\bigcup_{j\in J}\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}g_{j}
where the components are open, closed, and connected, cf.
[
FK18
, Section 2.2]
.
Similarly we get a countable disjoint union of open and closed subsets of
\mathfrak{H}
in the form
\mathfrak{H}=\bigcup_{i\in I}h_{i}(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ})
.

Assume that
Z\cdot\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{G}
for some algebraic subset
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
. Then
Z=\bigcup_{j\in J_{0}}Z_{j}g_{j}
for a finite subset
J_{0}\subseteq J
and algebraic subsets
Z_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
. It follows that

\mathfrak{G}=Z\cdot\mathfrak{H}=\bigcup_{j\in J_{0}}Z_{j}g_{j}\cdot\mathfrak{H}\text{ \ and \ }Z_{j}g_{j}\cdot\mathfrak{H}=\bigcup_{i\in I}Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i}\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}).

Each
Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i}\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ})
belongs to a connected component of
\mathfrak{G}
, and
Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i}\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ})\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
if and only if
g_{j}h_{i}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
. Thus

\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}=\bigcup_{j\in J_{0},\,g_{j}h_{i}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i}\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ})=(\bigcup_{j\in J_{0},\,g_{j}h_{i}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i})\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}).

By construction,
\bigcup_{j\in J_{0},\,g_{j}h_{i}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}Z_{j}g_{j}h_{i}
is a finite union of algebraic subsets and thus contained in
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}_{k}
for some
k
where
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}=\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}_{k}
is an admissible filtration.

(2)
Since
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
is connected we can assume that the
\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}_{k}
are irreducible (
[
FK18
, Prop. 1.6.3 and Prop. 2.2.1(2)]
) and so
Z^{\prime}\cdot(\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ})=\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
with an irreducible algebraic subset
Z^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}
. Now Lemma
7.3.4
and Proposition
7.1.2
(c) imply that
L_{\mathfrak{H}}=L_{\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}
has finite codimension in
L_{\mathfrak{G}}=L_{\mathfrak{G}^{\circ}}
.
∎

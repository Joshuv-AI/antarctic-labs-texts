Malliavin Calculus for rough stochastic differential equations
Fabio Bugini, Michele Coghi, Torstein Nilssen

Abstract
. In this work we show that rough stochastic differential equations (RSDEs), as introduced in [ 15 ] , are Malliavin differentiable. We use this to prove existence of a density when the diffusion coefficients satisfies standard ellipticity assumptions. Moreover, when the coefficients are smooth and the diffusion coefficients satisfies a Hörmander condition, the density is shown to be smooth. The key ingredient is to develop a comprehensive theory of linear rough stochastic differential equations, which could be of independent interest.



Keywords.
Malliavin caluculus, rough paths, linear rough-stochastic equations, Hörmander theorem.

60H07, 60H10, 60L20.

1.
Introduction

On a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
, we consider the following mixed rough path and stochastic differential equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
t\in[0,T]
,

(1)

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}+\beta(X_{t})d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here,
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an
m-
dimensional
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
-Brownian motion and
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})
is a deterministic geometric
\alpha
-rough path, with
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\right]
.
The coefficients of the equation are
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{d})\equiv\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
and
\beta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A solution theory for equation (
1
) can be found in
[
15
]
: Equation (
1
) admits a unique solution
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, under the assumptions on the coefficients one would expect from considering the rough path and the stochastic equation separately;
When
\beta\equiv 0
equation (
1
) is a classical stochastic differential equation (SDE) which is well-posed if the coefficents are Lipschitz. On the other hand, when
b,\sigma\equiv 0
, rough path theory (see
[
23
]
) tells us that equation (
1
) is well-posed when
\beta
is three times differentiable, see also
[
14
]
for a general introduction on rough paths.

In this paper, we study the Malliavin differentiability of the solution
X
and, consequently, the existence (and smoothness) of the density with respect to the Lebesgue measure of the law of
X_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
.

One motivation for studying equations of the type (
1
) or (
2
) comes from McKean-Vlasov models with common noise.
When the deterministic rough path
\mathbf{Z}
in equation (
1
) is replaced by a stochastic process
W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, independent of
B
, we obtain the following so called doubly stochastic differential equation

(2)

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}+\beta(X_{t})dW_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Consider a system of
N
(interacting) particles subject to both independent Brownian motions
B^{1},\dots,B^{N}
(one for each particle) and a common noise
W
. The empirical measure of the system is then expected to converge, when
N\to\infty
, to a random measure
\mu_{t}=\mathcal{L}(X_{t}\mid\sigma(W_{s},\ s\leq t))
, which is the conditional law of a process
X
solution to (
2
) given the common noise
W
. This is a phenomenon of conditional propagation of chaos, see
[
21
]
or
[
6
]
for a case without independent noise. In the case with interaction, some (or all) the coefficients of (
2
) might depend on
\mu
itself, this case is beyond the scope of the current work. An important generalization of mean field theory is the theory of mean field games with common noise, which arises when the dynamics of the particles (in this case players or agents) are controlled and each agent tries to maximise a value function
[
3
]
.

Let us emphasize that (
1
) is more general than (
2
), since, in general
\mathbf{Z}
can be sampled form any stochastic process
W
with continuous trajectories of Hölder regularity in
(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, for which there exists a rough path lift. In particular
\mathbf{Z}
can be sampled from a fractional Browinan motion, a more general Gaussian process or a Brownian bridge.

A second motivation for studying equations of the type (
2
) comes from filtering theory: in many applications one wants to have information about a stream of data called the signal, say the process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, but only has access to the process
(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, called the observation. See
[
2
]
for a general introduction on stochastic filtering. Also in this context, the object of interest is the so called filtering measure, which is the distribution of
X
given the observation
W
. As it turns out, under suitable assumptions and the application of a Girsanov tranformation,
X
can be seen as a diffusion process which depends on the observation
W
and on other independent fluctuations, as described by equation (
2
). One fundamental question is the robustness of the model, or how the filtering measure depends on the observation or even if this dependence is continuous. Notoriously, the solution of a rough differential equation depends continuously on the driver and rough paths can be employed to construct robust filters, as in
[
9
]
or
[
8
]
.

Another motivation comes from linear rough PDEs as follows.
At least at the formal level, if we define
u_{t}:=\mathcal{L}(X_{t})
, then
u
satisfies the Fokker-Planck equation

(3)

\mathrm{d}u=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\ u\mathrm{d}t+\nabla\cdot(\beta u)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t},\qquad u_{0}=\delta_{x_{0}}

where
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator of

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}b^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a^{i,j}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}x_{j}},

and
a(x):=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\top}
.
If one shows that any solution of (
3
) can be represented by (
1
) by the Feynman-Kac formula (in a similar vein of
[
11
]
), then the results of the present paper could be used to show smoothing properties in the solution of (
3
).

The central result of our paper is the following

Theorem

(see Theorem
4.2
)
.

If
b
and
\sigma
are differentiable and bounded and
\beta
is three times differentiable and bounded, then the solution
X
to equation (
1
) is Malliavin differentiable.

Using the notation from
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
we will actually prove that
X
belongs to the space
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
for every
p\geq 1
.
The strategy of the proof is based on an approximation argument as follows. We replace
\mathbf{Z}
in equation (
1
) with the canonical lift of a smooth path, say
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, where
(\mathbf{Z}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\mathbf{Z}
in the rough path metric.
When equation (
1
) is driven by
\mathbf{Z}^{n}
, its solution,
X^{n}
, is well known to be Malliavin differentiable and its derivative satisfies a linearized equation.
Provided we can show good a priori estimates on this linearized equation,
Malliavin differentiability of
X
now follows by
L^{2}(\Omega)
stability of
\mathbf{Z}\mapsto X
in the rough path metric coupled with the fact that the Malliavin derivative is a closed operator.

Towards this goal, we will give a meaning and prove well-posedness to solutions of equations of the form

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dY_{t}&=dF_{t}+G_{t}Y_{t}dt+S_{t}Y_{t}dB_{t}+f_{t}Y_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Y_{0}&=\xi,\end{aligned}

on any finite dimensional Hilbert space.
Indeed, as in the classical case, we expect the Malliavin derivative
Y^{\theta}_{t}=\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
,
t\geq\theta\in[0,T]
, to solve the following linear rough stochastic equation,

(5)

\displaystyle dY^{\theta}_{t}=Db(X_{t})Y^{\theta}_{t}dt+D\sigma(X_{t})Y^{\theta}_{t}dB_{t}+D\beta(X_{t})Y^{\theta}_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\ t\in[\theta,T]

\displaystyle Y^{\theta}_{\theta}=\sigma(X_{\theta}).

In equation (
4
) the coefficients
G
and
S
are random linear vector fields and
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega;W)
. The linear term,
f
, and the forcing term,
F
, are assumed to be
stochastically controlled vector fields
, as introduced in
[
16
]
; This is a generalization of controlled paths in the sense of Gubinelli
[
17
]
(see Section
3
below).

Since the vector fields of (
4
) are linear, hence not bounded, we cannot apply the well-posedness results of
[
15
]
. Our main technical contribution is thus the following.

Theorem

(see Theorem
3.11
)
.

Equation (
4
) admits a unique solution. Moreover, the solution is continuous in all the inputs
(F,G,S,f,\mathbf{Z},\xi)
.

The solution of (
4
) is understood in the sense of Davie
[
10
]
(see Definition
3.6
below) as in
[
15
]
. A priori estimates are proved using techniques introduced in
[
1
]
and stochastic sewing techniques.
When the coefficients of equation (
1
) are smooth, we establish (infinite) Malliavin differentiability for the solutions.

Next, we apply these results to study the density of (
1
) as follows. In the regularity class of
[
15
]
and under a uniform ellipticity condition on
\sigma
we can show that the solution of (
1
) admits a density with respect to Lebesgue measure.
Smoothness of the density is shown when the coefficients are smooth and satisfy an appropriate Hörmander condition as described in the following.

Define the space of vector fields
\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\{\sigma^{k}\mid k=1,\dots,m\}
and recursively

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i+1}:=\mathcal{S}_{i}\cup\{[F,V]\mid F\in\{b,\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{m},\beta^{1},\dots,\beta^{n}\},V\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N}

as well as
\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{S}_{i}
. Here, we have used the commutator notation between two vector fields
F,G
, viz

[F,G](x)=DG(x)F(x)-DF(x)G(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define
\mathcal{S}_{i}(x)=\text{span}\{V(x)|V\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\}
and
\mathcal{S}(x)=\operatorname{span}\{V(x)\mid V\in\mathcal{S}\}
.

Our Hörmander condition reads as follows.

Assumptions 1.1

(Hörmander condition)
.

Assume
b\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and
\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
We say that
Hörmander’s condition
is satisfied if
\mathcal{S}(x)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Our main results are summarized in the following theorem. See Theorems
5.5
and
5.9
for precise results and Definitions
5.1
and
5.6
for the definition of true roughness and
\theta
-Hölder roughness. Heuristically, an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous path,
Z
, is truly rough if it is
not

2\alpha
-Hölder continuous at every time and this notion yields a suitable rough path version of the Doob-Meyer decomposition. The notion of
\theta
-Hölder roughness is a further quantitative measure on this roughness and yields a suitable rough path version of Norris lemma.
Both properties are shared, for instance, by the Brownian motion and more in general by a class of Gaussian processes which include the fractional Brownian motion.

Theorem
.

Suppose the coefficients of (
1
) satisfy Assumption
1.1
and
Z
is truly rough.
Then, for each fixed
t
, the solution
X_{t}
has a density w.r.t. Lebesgue measure.

If, in addition,
Z
is
\theta
-Hölder rough, then the density is smooth.

Note that the roughness of
Z
spans the diffusivity in more directions via each of the vector fields
\beta^{1},\dots,\beta^{n}
than would be the case if
Z
was a smooth path. To see this, consider the case when
Z_{t}^{j}=t
for each
j=1,\dots,n
for which we have classical diffusion

dX_{t}=\big(b(X_{t})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta^{j}(X_{t})\big)dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}.

In this setting the first 2 sets of vector fields would be

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{0}=\{\sigma^{k}\mid k=1,\dots,m\}=\mathcal{S}_{0}

and

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\cup\{[F,V]\mid F\in\{b+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta^{j},\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{m}\},V\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\}

which clearly gives
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(x)\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}(x)
.

Remark 1.2
.

It is worth noting that, in accordance with the classical case, when the diffiusion coeffient
\sigma
is uniformly elliptic, Hörmander condition is immediately satisfied and there exists a density. In this case the coefficients of the equation don’t need to be smooth (this is apparent from the proof of existence of densities).

Notation and Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We write
a\lesssim b
to express that there exists a non-negative constant
C\geq 0
such that
a\leq Cb
. If such a constant depends on some parameters
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N}
we write
a\lesssim_{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N}}b
.
For any
\pi=\{a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=b\}
partition of
[a,b]
, we define its mesh size as

|\pi|:=\max_{i=0,\dots,N-1}|t_{i+1}-t_{i}|.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
x^{i}
its
i
-th component (
i=1,\dots,d)
.

Let
(W,|\cdot|_{W})
and
(\bar{W},|\cdot|_{\bar{W}})
be two Banach spaces. We denote by
\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
the space of linear and continuous functions from
W
to
\bar{W}
. The latter is a Banach space, if endowed with the norm
|T|_{\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})}:=\sup_{x\in W,|x|_{W}\leq 1}|Tx|_{\bar{W}}
. For any
T\in\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
and for any
x\in W
, we have that

|Tx|_{\bar{W}}\leq|T|_{\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})}|x|_{W}.

If
W
and
\bar{W}
are finite dimensional Hilbert spaces, say
W\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{W}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we often identify the tensor product
W\otimes\bar{W}
with the matrix space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
.
Given any other finite dimensional Hilbert space
(V,|\cdot|_{V})
, the following identifications hold:

\mathscr{L}(W\otimes\bar{W},V)\equiv\mathscr{L}(W,\mathscr{L}(\bar{W},V))\equiv\mathscr{L}^{2}(W\times\bar{W},V),

where
\mathscr{L}^{2}(W\times\bar{W},V)
denotes the space of bilinear and continuous maps from
W\times\bar{W}
to
V
.

Given any function
F:W\to\bar{W}
, we denote by
|F|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in W}|F(x)|_{\bar{W}}
. If
|F|_{\infty}<+\infty
, the function
F
is said to be bounded. Moreover, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
{C}^{n}_{b}(W;\bar{W})
the space of bounded and continuous functions from
W
to
\bar{W}
which are
n
-times continuously differentiable with bounded derivatives.
If
F
is differentiable in
W
, we consider its derivative as a map
DF:W\to\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
.
The space
{C}^{n}_{b}(W;\bar{W})
is a Banach space if endowed with the norm

|F|_{{C}_{b}^{n}}:=|F|_{\infty}+|DF|_{\infty}+\dots+|D^{n}F|_{\infty},

where
D^{n}F
denotes the
n
-th derivative of
F
.
Any function
F\in{C}^{1}_{b}(W,\bar{W})
satisfies the following mean value theorem:

F(x+h)-F(x)=\int_{0}^{1}DF(x+\theta h)d\theta\ h

for any
x,h\in W
, where the integral can be intended as a componentwise integral, namely

F^{i}(x+h)-F^{i}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial F^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(x+\theta h)d\theta\big)h^{j}\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Similarly, for any
F\in{C}^{2}_{b}(W,\bar{W})
and for any

,

F(x+h)-F(x)-DF(x)h=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)D^{2}F(x+\theta h)d\theta\ h\otimes h.

Given a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and an integrability esponent
p\in[1,\infty)
, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{p;W}
- or simply
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, if clear from the context - the usual norm on the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega;W)
of
W
-valued random variables. A
W
-valued stochastic process
X=(X_{t})_{t}
is said to be
L^{p}
-integrable if any
X_{t}
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega;W)
. If
W=\mathbb{R}
, we sometimes write
L^{p}(\Omega)
instead of
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. For a given sub-
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, the space
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{G};W)
is the space of
\mathcal{G}
-measurable random variables in

.
The norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denote the essential supremum norm.

2.2.
Rough paths theory

Let
(E,|\cdot|_{E})
be any Banach space. If clear from the context, we denote
|\cdot|_{E}
simply by
|\cdot|
.
For any
T>0
we define

\Delta_{[0,T]}:=\{(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}\ |\ s\leq t\}

and

\Delta^{2}_{[0,T]}:=\{(s,u,t)\in[0,T]^{3}\ |\ s\leq u\leq t\}.

For any path
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto X_{t}\in E
, we define its increment as the two parameter function

\delta X_{s,t}:=X_{t}-X_{s}\qquad(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}.

Similarly, given a two-parameter function
A:\Delta_{[0,T]}\to E
,
(s,t)\mapsto A_{s,t}
, we define

\delta A_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}\qquad(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}^{2}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. An
\alpha
-Hölder continuous path with values in
E
is a map
X:[0,T]\to E
for which there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\delta X_{s,t}|_{E}\leq C|t-s|^{\alpha}\qquad\text{for any $(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}$.}

We write
X\in{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];E)
and we denote by

|\delta X|_{\alpha;E}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|_{E}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

Similarly, an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous two-parameter path with values in
E
is a map
A:\Delta_{[0,T]}\to E
for which there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|A_{s,t}|_{E}\leq C|t-s|^{\alpha}\qquad\text{for any $(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}$.}

We write
A\in{C}_{2}^{\alpha}(\Delta_{[0,T]};E)
and we denote by

|A|_{\alpha;E}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\frac{|A_{s,t}|_{E}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

If
X\in{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];E)
, then
\delta X\in{C}_{2}^{\alpha}(\Delta{[0,T]};E)
. The converse is a priori not true.
If clear from the context, we write
|\cdot|_{\alpha}
instead of
|\cdot|_{\alpha;E}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
\alpha
-Hölder rough path on
[0,T]
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a pair
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})
such that

a)

Z\in{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

b)

\mathbb{Z}\in{C}_{2}^{2\alpha}(\Delta_{[0,T]};\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

c)

(Chen’s relation) for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}^{2}
, it holds

\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}-\mathbb{Z}_{s,u}-\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}=\delta Z_{s,u}\otimes\delta Z_{u,t}

or, equivalently,
\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}^{ij}-\mathbb{Z}_{s,u}^{ij}-\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}^{ij}=\delta Z_{s,u}^{i}\delta Z_{u,t}^{j}
for any
i,j=1,\dots,n
.

We write
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we define

\displaystyle|\mathbf{Z}|_{\alpha}:=|\delta Z|_{\alpha}+|\mathbb{Z}|_{2\alpha}.

Given any pair of rough paths
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z}),\ \bar{\mathbf{Z}}=(\bar{Z},\bar{\mathbb{Z}})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define their distance as

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(\mathbf{Z},\bar{\mathbf{Z}})

\displaystyle:=|\delta Z-\delta\bar{Z}|_{\alpha}+|\mathbb{Z}-\bar{\mathbb{Z}}|_{2\alpha}=

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{|\delta Z_{s,t}-\delta\bar{Z}_{s,t}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{|\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}-\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{s,t}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}.

Let us now consider a smooth path
Z\in{C}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We denote by
\dot{Z}
its time derivative. It is possible to show that such a path can be canonically lifted to a
\alpha
-rough path for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, by defining

\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}\delta Z_{s,r}\otimes dZ_{r}=\int_{s}^{t}\delta Z_{s,r}\otimes\dot{Z}_{r}\ dr\qquad\text{for any $(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}$}.

We write
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{L}({C}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and we call it a smooth rough path.

Definition 2.3
.

A weakly geometric
\alpha
-rough path with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an element
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
,

Sym(\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}):=\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}+\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}^{\top}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\delta Z_{s,t}\otimes\delta Z_{s,t}.

We write
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.4
.

A geometric
\alpha
-rough path with values in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an element of the closure of
\mathscr{L}({C}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
in
(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}),\rho_{\alpha})
. Namely,
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})
is a geometric rough path - and we write
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{0,\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
- if and only if it belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
and there exists a sequence
(\mathbf{Z}^{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{L}({C}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
of smooth rough paths such that

\rho_{\alpha}(\mathbf{Z}^{n},\mathbf{Z})\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to+\infty$.}

2.3.
Preliminaries on Malliavin Calculus

For a complete discussion about Malliavin calculus and its application to stochastic differential equations we refer to
[
25
]
.
The general framework to develop Malliavin calculus consists in a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, a separable real Hilbert space
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
and an isonormal Gaussian process on
H
. The latter
is a family
\{W(h),h\in H\}
of real-valued centered Gaussian random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that

\mathbb{E}(W(h)W(g))=\langle h,g\rangle_{H}

for any
h,g\in H
. The mapping
H\ni h\mapsto W(h)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
provides a linear isometry of
H
onto a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

We are interested in developing Malliavin calculus on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
, on which it is defined an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion
B=((B^{1}_{t},\dots,B^{m}_{t}))_{t\in[0,T]}
.
Therefore it is classical to choose

H=L^{2}(\mathcal{T},\mathscr{B},\mu),

where
(\mathcal{T},\mathscr{B},\mu)
is the finite measure space defined by

\mathcal{T}:=[0,T]\times\{1,\dots,m\},\quad\mathscr{B}:=\mathcal{B}([0,T])\otimes\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,m\}),\quad\mu:=\lambda_{|_{[0,T]}}\otimes\#

with
\lambda
denoting the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure,
\#
denoting the counting measure on the discrete set
\{1,\dots,m\}
and
\mathcal{B}([0,T])
the Borel
\sigma
-field on
[0,T]
. Standard functional analysis results lead to the following identifications (i.e. the spaces are isometrically isomorphic):

•

L^{2}(\mathcal{T})\equiv L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

•

L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\equiv L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

•

L^{2}(\mathcal{T})^{\otimes{k}}\equiv L^{2}([0,T]^{k};(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{k}),\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.

The scalar product on
L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
can therefore be written as

\langle h,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T})}=\int_{\mathcal{T}}h_{\tau}g_{\tau}\mu(d\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{T}h_{t}^{j}g_{t}^{j}dt.

and, for any
h\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, we define

W(h):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{T}h^{j}_{r}dB^{j}_{r},

where the integral of a deterministic function on
[0,T]
against a Brownian motion is intended as a Wiener integral. The map
W:L^{2}(\mathcal{T})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies all the requirements to be isonormal Gaussian process on
L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
.
In this setting, for any fixed
p\in[1,\infty)
, the Malliavin derivative operator is a linear operator

\mathbb{D}:\mathbb{D}^{1,p}\subseteq L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})).

The case
p=2
is particularly interesting, since the Malliavin derivative of a random variable can be interpreted as a
\lambda
-a.e. defined stochastic process taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Namely, for any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
,
\mathbb{D}F
is an element of
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and, for
\lambda
-almost every
t\in[0,T]
and for any
k=1,\dots,m
, we write

\mathbb{D}_{t}F:=\mathbb{D}F(t,\omega).

If we consider a random vector
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{d})
such that
X^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for any
i=1,\dots,d
, we often define its Malliavin derivative as

\mathbb{D}X:=(\mathbb{D}X^{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}X^{d})^{T}\in[L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m})]^{d}\equiv L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})

and we identify it with the
\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
-valued process given by
\mathbb{D}_{t}X=((\mathbb{D}_{t}X^{i})^{k})_{i=1,\dots,d;k=1,\dots,m}
.
Similarly, the
k
-th order Malliavin derivative operator is defined as a map

\mathbb{D}^{k}:\mathbb{D}^{k,p}\subseteq L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T]^{k};(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{k}))\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1},\ p\in[1,\infty).

Given
F\in\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
, its Malliavin derivative
\mathbb{D}^{k}F
can be seen as an element of
L^{2}([0,T]^{k}\times\Omega;(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{k})
and we write

\mathbb{D}^{k}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}}F:=\mathbb{D}^{k}F((t_{1},\dots,t_{k}),\omega)\qquad(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\in[0,T]^{k}.

Given a random vector
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{d})
such that
X^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
for any
i=1,\dots,d
, we consider its
k
-th Malliavin derivative as

\mathbb{D}^{k}X:=(\mathbb{D}^{k}X^{1},\dots,\mathbb{D}^{k}X^{d})^{\top}\in L^{2}([0,T]^{k}\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times km}).

2.5.
Rough Itô formula for functions of polynomial growth

Throughout this section we fix a weakly geometric rough path
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}_{g}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
.
We prove an Itô-type formula for
rough Itô processes
and functions of polynomial growth. The idea is based on the rough Itô’s formula for
C^{2}
-bounded functions proved in
[
15
, Section 4.5]
, which cannot be directly applied in our case, as we need a chain rule for the product of two rough Itô processes, which is not bounded.

As a first step to prove the rough Itô formula we must show that the composition of a
C^{2}
function with polynomial growth with a stochastic controlled path is still a stochastic controlled path.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
U,W
be finite dimensional Hilbert spaces. Let
f\in C^{2}(U;W)
such that there exists
c>0
and
q\geq 1
such that for
k=1,2

|D^{k}f(x)|\leq c(1+|x|^{q}).

Moreover, assume that, for every
p\geq 2
, and that

(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{Z}L^{p}(U),\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X\|_{p}<\infty.

Then,

(f(X),Df(X)X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\alpha}_{Z}L^{p}(W)

Proof.

We start the proof by expanding the increment of
f(X_{t})
as follows

\delta f(X)_{s,t}=Df(X_{s})X_{s}^{\prime}\delta Z_{s,t}+R^{f}_{s,t},

where, for
R^{X}_{s,t}:=\delta X_{s,t}-X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}
,

R^{f}_{s,t}:=Df(X_{s})R^{X}_{s,t}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(Df(X_{s}+\theta\delta X_{s,t})-Df(X_{s})\right)\mathrm{d}\theta\delta X_{s,t}.

We must prove that Definition
2.7
is satisfied by
f(X)
. Since
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(X^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted, so are also the processes
(f(X_{t}))_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(Df(X_{t})X^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.
We show that
\delta f(X)\in C^{\alpha}_{2}L^{p}(W)
by using (
2.13
) and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\|\delta f(X)_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\int_{0}^{1}Df(X_{s}+\theta\delta X_{s,t})\mathrm{d}\theta\delta X_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq c\|(1+|X_{s}|^{q}+|\delta X_{s,t}|^{q}\|_{{2p}}\|\delta X_{s,t}\|_{2p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X_{u}\|_{{2pq}}^{q}\|X\|_{\alpha;2p}|t-s|^{\alpha}.

This shows that
f(X)
satisfies point a) in Definition
2.7
.
With similar arguments one shows that

\displaystyle\|\delta(Df(X)X^{\prime})_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X_{u}\|_{{2pq}}^{q}+\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X^{\prime}_{u}\|_{{2pq}}^{q})(\|X^{\prime}\|_{\alpha;2p}+\|X\|_{\alpha;2p})|t-s|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Df(X_{t})X^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X_{u}\|_{{2pq}}^{q})\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X^{\prime}_{u}\|_{{2p}}

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}R^{f}_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X_{u}\|_{{2pq}}^{q})(\|X\|_{\alpha;4p}^{2}+\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{x}\|_{2\alpha;2p})|t-s|^{2\alpha}.

This complete the proof that
f(X)
satisfies Definition
2.7
points b) and c).
∎

We are now ready to introduce the definition of rough Itô process on a finite dimensional Hilbert space
U
.

Definition 2.14
.

Assume that

\displaystyle(b_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in U,\qquad(\sigma_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},U)

are progressive processes such that, for every
p\geq 2
,

(10)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|b_{t}\|_{p},\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{t}\|_{p}<\infty.

Moreover, for every
p\geq 2
, let
(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p}(\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},U))
such that

(11)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}<\infty.

For every
p\geq 2
assume that
X_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;U)
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable.

We say that
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,t]}\in U
is a
rough Itô process
if it is a continuous and adapted process such that

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})_{s}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{s}.

Proposition 2.15
.

If
X
is a rough Itô process, then

X\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p}(U)
and

(12)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}\|_{p}<\infty;

Proof.

It follows from the definition of rough stochastic integral and keeping in mind assumption (
11
) that

. The bounds (
12
) follow from standard considerations using Jensen and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities on the drift and the diffusion terms and

on the rough integral.
∎

We are now ready to state and proof the rough Itô formula for functions of polynomial growth.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
U
be a finite dimensional Hilbert spaces and
f\in C^{3}(U;W)
such that, there exists
q\geq 1
and
c>0
such that, for
k=1,2,3

|D^{k}f(x)|\leq c(1+|x|^{q}),\qquad x\in U.

Assume that
X
is a rough Itô process on
U
.

Then, for every
p\geq 2
,
(Y,Y^{\prime}):=(Df(X)X^{\prime},D^{2}f(X)(X^{\prime})^{2}+Df(X)X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p}(\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},W))
and the rough Itô formula holds: For every
t\in[0,T]
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f(X_{t})-f(X_{0})=&\int_{0}^{t}Df(X_{u})b_{u}\mathrm{d}u+\int_{0}^{t}Df(X_{u})\sigma_{u}\mathrm{d}B_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}(Y,Y^{\prime})_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\operatorname{trace}(\sigma_{u}\sigma^{\top}_{u}D^{2}f(X_{u}))\mathrm{d}u.\end{aligned}

Proof.

First note that
(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p}(\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},W))
thanks to Proposition (
2.13
) applied to the function
U\times\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},U)\ni(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto Df(x)x^{\prime}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},W)
, which is
2
-times differentiable with derivatives of polynomial growth.

We apply now Taylor expansion to compute the increment of
f(X)
. Let
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, we have
\delta f(X)_{s,t}=A_{s,t}+R_{s,t}
,
where

\displaystyle A_{s,t}

\displaystyle:=Df(X_{s})\delta X_{s,t}+\frac{1}{2}D^{2}f(X_{s})(\delta X_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle R_{s,t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}D^{3}f(X_{s}+\xi\theta\zeta\delta X_{s,t})\mathrm{d}\xi\theta\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\zeta(\delta X_{s,t})^{\otimes 3}.

Using the polynomial growth property of

and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have that

. Indeed, there exists

such that

\|R_{s,t}\|_{2}\leq c(1+\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\|X\|_{{4q}}^{q})\|\delta X\|_{\alpha;12}^{3}|t-s|^{3\alpha}.

This immediately implies that

(14)

\delta f(X)_{s,t}=L^{2}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi_{n}}A_{u,v},

where
\pi_{n}
is a sequence of partitions of
[s,t]
whose mesh goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

We now define
I_{t}
as the right-hand side of (
13
) evaluated for
s=0
and
t=t
. By definition
(I_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted stochastic process with continuous paths and
I_{t}\in L^{p}(W)
for every
t\in[0,T]
and
p\geq 2
. We want to now apply the uniqueness part of the stochastic sewing lemma to see that
\delta I_{s,t}
is the
L^{2}
limit of the partial sums over

and thus coincides with
\delta f(X)_{s,t}
almost surely.
Using the definition of
I
,
A
,
(Y,Y^{\prime})
expanding
\delta X_{s,t}
and adding and subtracting the relevant terms, we have that

(15)

\displaystyle\delta I_{s,t}-A_{s,t}=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\delta Df(X)_{s,u}b_{u}\mathrm{d}u+\int_{s}^{t}\delta Df(X)_{s,u}\sigma_{u}\mathrm{d}B_{u}

(16)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}(Y,Y^{\prime})_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}-Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}-Y_{s}^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}

(17)

\displaystyle-Df(X_{s})\left(\int_{s}^{t}(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}-X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}-X^{\prime\prime}_{s}\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}f(X_{s})\left((\delta X_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}-2(X^{\prime}_{s})^{2}\operatorname{Symm}(\mathbb{Z}_{s,t})\right)

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\operatorname{trace}(\sigma_{u}\sigma^{\top}_{u}D^{2}f(X_{u}))\mathrm{d}u.

By standard arguments of stochastic calculus one sees that
\|\eqref{eq: expansion 1}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}
and
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}\eqref{eq: expansion 1}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\alpha}
. It is immediate to notice that terms (
16
) and (
17
) are rough stochastic integrals minus their local expansions. By Proposition
2.8
, we immediately have

\|\eqref{eq: expansion 2}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{2\alpha},\qquad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}\eqref{eq: expansion 2}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{3\alpha},

and similar bounds hold for (

), using Hölder inequality.

For term (
18
) we have

\displaystyle(\delta X_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}-2(X^{\prime}_{s})^{2}\operatorname{Symm}(\mathbb{Z}_{s,t})=(\delta X_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}-(X^{\prime}_{s})^{2}(\delta Z_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{u}\mathrm{d}B_{u}\right)^{\otimes 2}+\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{\prime}_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\right)^{\otimes 2}+\bar{R}_{s,t}-(X^{\prime}_{s})^{2}(\delta Z_{s,t})^{\otimes 2},

where

is such that

\|\bar{R}_{s,t}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{2\alpha},\qquad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}\bar{R}_{s,t}\|_{2}\lesssim|t-s|^{3\alpha}.

Using again Hölder inequality and the fact that

have all moments bounded uniformly in time, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{\prime}_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\right)^{\otimes 2}-(X^{\prime}_{s})^{2}(\delta Z_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|\left(\int_{s}^{t}\delta X^{\prime}_{s,u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\right)\otimes\left(\int_{s}^{t}\delta X^{\prime}_{u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\right)\|_{2}+\|X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}\otimes\left(\int_{s}^{t}\delta X^{\prime}_{s,u}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{u}\right)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim|t-s|^{3\alpha}.

In order to have the required bounds on

, we are only left with computing the following

\displaystyle S_{s,t}:=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\operatorname{trace}(\sigma_{u}\sigma_{u}^{\top}D^{2}f(X_{u}))\mathrm{d}u-D^{2}f(X_{s})\left(\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{u}\mathrm{d}B_{u}\right)^{\otimes 2}

From standard stochastic calculus, the polynomial growth of

and the assumptions on the moments of

, one readily obtains

Putting all previous estimates together, we have that there exists

such that

By the uniqueness part of the stochastic sewing lemma, Theorem

, we have that
\delta I_{s,t}
coincides with the

limit in (

) and it is almost surely equal to

.
∎

3.
Linear rough stochastic differential equations

In this section we develop a theory of RSDEs with linear coefficients on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
. We consider an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
and a deterministic rough path
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
. We assume that solutions to linear RSDEs live in the finite dimensional real Hilbert spaces which we denote by
W
and
\bar{W}
, typically they are
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
for integers
d
and
m
.

Definition 3.1
.

A stochastic linear vector field from
W
to
\bar{W}
is a progressive
\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
-valued stochastic process
G=(G_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

\|G\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|G_{t}(\omega)|_{\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})}<+\infty.

Definition 3.2

(Stochastic controlled linear vector fields)
.

Let
Z\in{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a deterministic
\alpha
-Hölder continuous path with
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let
p\in[2,\infty)
,
q\in[p,\infty]
and let
\gamma\in(0,1]
. A pair
(f,f^{\prime})
is said to be a
L^{p,q}
-integrable stochastic controlled linear vector field from
W
to
\bar{W}
with
\gamma
-Hölder regularity - and we write
(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathbf{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L_{lin}^{p,q}(W,\bar{W})
- if

a)

f=(f_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an adapted
\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
-valued stochastic process such that

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f_{t}|_{\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})}<+\infty

and

\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,q}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|\|f_{t}-f_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}<+\infty;

b)

f^{\prime}=(f^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is an adapted
\mathscr{L}(W,\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\bar{W}))
-valued stochastic process such that

\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\omega\in\Omega}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|f^{\prime}_{t}|_{\mathscr{L}(W,\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\bar{W})}<+\infty

and

\|\delta f^{\prime}\|_{\gamma;p,q}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|\|f^{\prime}_{t}-f^{\prime}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}<+\infty;

c)

denoting by
R_{s,t}^{f}:=f_{t}-f_{s}-f^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}
for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
, it holds

\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{f}\|_{2\gamma;q}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(R_{s,t}^{f})\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{2\gamma}}<+\infty.

For such a stochastic controlled linear vector field we write

\displaystyle\|(f,f^{\prime})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\|(f,f^{\prime})\|_{\mathbf{D}_{Z}^{2\gamma}L_{lin}^{p,q}}

\displaystyle:=\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,q}+\|\delta f^{\prime}\|_{\gamma;p,q}+\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{f}\|_{2\gamma;q}.

The following is a stability result for stochastic controlled paths under the composition with stochastic controlled linear vector fields.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Z\in{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
p\in[2,\infty),\ \gamma\in(0,1]
. Let
(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\gamma}L^{p}(W)
such that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty
and let
(f,f^{\prime})\in\mathbf{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L_{lin}^{p,\infty}(W,\bar{W})
. Then

(f,f^{\prime})Y:=(f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot},f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}Y^{\prime}_{\cdot})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\gamma}L^{p}(\bar{W}).

Proof.

For any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
,
f_{t}Y_{t}
and
f^{\prime}_{t}Y_{t}+f_{t}Y^{\prime}_{t}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and

(20)

\|f^{\prime}_{t}Y_{t}+f_{t}Y^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty.

It is also easy to verify that

(21)

\displaystyle\|\delta(f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot})_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{t}\delta Y_{s,t}\|_{p}+\|\delta f_{s,t}Y_{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|f\|_{\infty}\|\delta Y\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}\big)|t-s|^{\gamma}

and, similarly,

(22)

\displaystyle\|\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}Y^{\prime}_{\cdot})_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\delta Y\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\delta f^{\prime}\|_{\gamma;p,\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+\|f\|_{\infty}\|\delta Y^{\prime}\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}\big)|t-s|^{\gamma}.

Moreover, by linearity we can write

(23)

\displaystyle R_{s,t}^{f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}}

\displaystyle:=f_{t}Y_{t}-f_{s}Y_{s}-(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}Y^{\prime}_{s})\delta Z_{s,t}

\displaystyle=f_{t}Y_{t}-f_{s}Y_{t}+f_{s}(Y_{t}-Y_{s}-Y^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t})-f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\delta f_{s,t}\delta Y_{s,t}+f_{s}R^{Y}_{s,t}+R^{f}_{s,t}Y_{s}.

Let
\bar{p}\in(1,2]
be the conjugate exponent of
p
. By means of the conditional Hölder inequality we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta f_{s,t}\delta Y_{s,t})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}_{s}(|\delta f_{s,t}\delta Y_{s,t}|)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathbb{E}(\|\delta f_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{\bar{p}}^{p}\|\delta Y_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\|\delta f_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{\bar{p}}\|_{\infty}\|\delta Y_{s,t}\|_{p}\leq\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,\infty}\|\delta Y\|_{\gamma;p}|t-s|^{2\gamma},

and we also have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(f_{s}R^{Y}_{s,t})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{Y}\|_{2\gamma;p}|t-s|^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(R^{f}_{s,t}Y_{s})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{f}\|_{2\gamma;p}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}|t-s|^{2\gamma}.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

From the proof of Proposition
3.3
, it is clear that we have stability of the composition only if we take a
L^{p,q}
-integrable stochastic controlled linear vector field with
q=\infty
. Otherwise, due to the application of the (possibly conditional) Hölder inequality, we lose some integrability and we can only conclude that
(f,f^{\prime})Y\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\gamma}L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\bar{W})
.

The following lemma gives an example on how it is possible to construct stochastic linear vector fields, starting from solutions of RSDEs.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, be such that
|\mathbf{Z}|_{\alpha}\leq C
for some
C>0
, and let
b\in{C}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times m})
and
\beta\in{C}^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times n})
. Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the solution of the following RSDE, as introduced in Definition
2.11
:

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}+(\beta(X_{t}),D\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t}))d\mathbf{Z}_{t}.

Then
(Db(X_{t}))_{t\in[0,T]}
and
(D\sigma(X_{t}))_{t\in[0,T]}
are stochastic linear vector fields from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively. Moreover, for any
p\in[2,\infty)
,

(D\beta(X_{\cdot}),D^{2}\beta(X_{\cdot})\beta(X_{\cdot}))\in\mathbf{D}^{2\alpha}_{Z}L_{lin}^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}))

and there exists a positive constant
K_{C}=K_{C}(|b|_{\infty},|\sigma|_{\infty},|\beta|_{\mathcal{C}^{3}_{b}},C,\alpha,p,T)
such that

(24)

\|(D\beta(X_{\cdot}),D^{2}\beta(X_{\cdot})\beta(X_{\cdot}))\|_{\mathbf{D}^{2\alpha}_{Z}L_{lin}^{p,\infty}}\leq K_{C}.

Proof.

The first part of the statement easily follows from the assumptions on
b
and
\sigma
and from the fact that
X
is continuous and adapted.
By definition of Fréchet derivative,
D\beta:W\to\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W})
, while
D^{2}\beta
is a map from
W
to
\mathscr{L}(W,\mathscr{L}(W,\bar{W}))
. Being
X
adapted, we have that
f
and
f^{\prime}
are adapted. Recall that, from the general theory about RSDEs,
(X,\beta(X))\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
p\in[2,\infty)
. For any
t\in[0,T]
and
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, we have
|f_{t}|\leq|D\beta|_{\infty}
and

|f^{\prime}_{t}|=|D^{2}\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t})|\leq|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}|\beta|_{\infty}<+\infty.

Let now
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|f_{t}-f_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|D\beta(X_{t})-D\beta(X_{s})|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}=\mathbb{E}(|D\beta(X_{t})-D\beta(X_{s})|^{p}|\mathcal{F}_{s})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}\|X_{t}-X_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\leq|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}\|\|\delta X_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}\|\delta X\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}|t-s|^{\alpha}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.},

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}_{t}-f^{\prime}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|D^{2}\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t})-D^{2}\beta(X_{s})\beta(X_{s})|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\big(|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}|\beta|_{\infty}+|D^{3}\beta|_{\infty}|\beta|_{\infty}\big)\|\delta X\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}|t-s|^{\alpha}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

The previous estimates prove that both
\|\delta f\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}
and
\|\delta f^{\prime}\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}
are finite. By linearity and applying again the mean value theorem, we can write

\displaystyle R^{f}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=D\beta(X_{t})-D\beta(X_{s})-D^{2}\beta(X_{s})X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}

\displaystyle=D\beta(X_{t})-D\beta(X_{s})-D^{2}\beta(X_{s})\delta{X}_{s,t}+D^{2}\beta(X_{s})(\delta{X}_{s,t}-X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)D^{3}\beta(X_{s}+\theta\delta{X}_{s,t})d\theta(\delta{X}_{s,t})^{\otimes 2}+D^{2}\beta(X_{s})R^{X}_{s,t}.

By applying the conditional Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)D^{3}\beta(X_{s}+\theta\delta{X}_{s,t})d\theta(\delta{X}_{s,t})^{\otimes 2})|

\displaystyle\leq|D^{3}\beta|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{s}(|\delta X_{s,t}|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq|D^{3}\beta|_{\infty}\|\delta{X}\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}^{2}|t-s|^{2\alpha}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.},

and by definiton of stochastic controlled path

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{s}(D^{2}\beta(X_{s})R^{X}_{s,t})|

\displaystyle=|D^{2}\beta(X_{s})\mathbb{E}_{s}(R^{X}_{s,t})|\leq|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{X}\|_{2\alpha;\infty}|t-s|^{2\alpha}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

Thus we deduce that

\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(R^{f}_{s,t})\|_{\infty}\leq(|D^{3}\beta|_{\infty}\|\delta{X}\|_{\alpha;p,\infty}^{2}+|D^{2}\beta|_{\infty}\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{X}\|_{2\alpha;\infty})|t-s|^{2\alpha}

In conclusion, (
24
) follows by applying the a priori estimate contained in
[
15
, Proposition 4.6]
to the previous computations.
∎

From this point till the end of this section, let

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=dF_{t}+G_{t}Y_{t}dt+S_{t}Y_{t}dB_{t}+(f_{t},f^{\prime}_{t})Y_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T].

Definition 3.6

(Solution in the sense of Davie)
.

Let

for any

\displaystyle Y_{s,t}^{\natural}

\displaystyle:=\delta Y_{s,t}-\delta F_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr-\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}-f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}+

\displaystyle-(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}.

Remark 3.7
.

There is no loss of generality in assuming

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a continuous and adapted
W
-valued stochastic process such that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty
and satisfying condition

of Definition
3.6
.
Then

(Y_{\cdot},f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+F^{\prime}_{\cdot})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L^{p}(W).

Proof.

Let
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. Both
Y_{t}
and
f_{t}Y_{t}+F^{\prime}_{t}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable. Moreover,

\|f_{t}Y_{t}+F^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|F^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty.

From the Davie-type expansion in Definition
3.6
and applying Bochner inequality for the Bochner integral and BDG inequality for the Itô integral, we have

(26)

\displaystyle\|\delta Y_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\delta F_{s,t}\|_{p}+\|\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr\|_{p}+\|\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}\|_{p}+\|f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle+\|(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}\|_{p}+\|Y^{\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\|\delta F\|_{\gamma;p}|t-s|^{\gamma}+\|G\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}|t-s|+\|S\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}+

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{\infty}|\delta Z|_{\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}|t-s|^{\alpha}+

\displaystyle+\big((\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty})\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|F^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}\big)|\mathbb{Z}|_{2\alpha}|t-s|^{2\alpha}+

\displaystyle+\|Y^{\natural}\|_{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1};p}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon_{1}},

from which we deduce that
\|\delta Y\|_{\gamma;p}<+\infty
. Moreover, thanks to the martingale property of the Itô integral and the contraction property of the conditional expectation,

(27)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta Y_{s,t}-(f_{s}Y_{s}+F^{\prime}_{s})\delta Z_{s,t})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta F_{s,t}-F^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t})\|_{p}+\|\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr\|_{p}+\|(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}\|_{p}+\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y^{\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,T,\varepsilon_{2}}\big[\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R^{F}\|_{2\gamma;p}+\|G\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|^{2}_{\infty})|\mathbb{Z}|_{2\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|F^{\prime}_{t}\|_{p}|\mathbb{Z}|_{2\alpha}+\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}Y^{\natural}\|_{1+\varepsilon_{2};p}\big]|t-s|^{2\gamma}.

Finally, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\|\delta(f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+F^{\prime}_{\cdot})_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq(\|f\|_{\infty}\|\delta Y\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\delta f\|_{\gamma;p,\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}+\|\delta F^{\prime}\|_{\gamma;p})|t-s|^{\gamma}.

∎

Solutions to linear RSDEs can be characterized according to the following proposition.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a continuous and adapted
W
-valued stochastic process such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty
and let
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0};W)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

Y
is an
L^{p}
-integrable solution in the sense of Davie to (
25
) starting from
\xi
;

(ii)

(f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot},f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f^{2}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}F^{\prime}_{\cdot})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L^{p}(\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},W))
and,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and for any
t\in[0,T]
,

Y_{t}=\xi+F_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr+\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}(f_{r}Y_{r},f^{\prime}_{r}Y_{r}+f_{r}^{2}Y_{r}+f_{r}F^{\prime}_{r})d\mathbf{Z}_{r}.

In particular, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
it holds that

\|Y^{\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y^{\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\
The fact that
(f_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot},f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f^{2}_{\cdot}Y_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}F^{\prime}_{\cdot})\in\mathscr{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L^{p}(\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},W))
follows combining Lemma
3.8
and Proposition
3.3
. For any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
, we denote by

A_{s,t}=f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}+(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}=f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}+(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}Y^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}.

According Proposition
2.8
it makes sense to define

\displaystyle R_{s,t}

\displaystyle:=\delta Y_{s,t}-\delta F_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr-\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r},f^{\prime}_{r})Y_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r}

\displaystyle=A_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r},f^{\prime}_{r})Y_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r}+Y_{s,t}^{\natural}

The previous equality leads to
to
\|R_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}
and
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(R_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}
. On noting that
\delta R\equiv 0
(i.e.
R
is additive), this is enough to get that
R_{s,t}=0
for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. In particular, for any
t\in[0,T]
and recalling that
F_{0}=0
, it holds

Y_{t}-\xi=\delta Y_{0,t}=F_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr+\int_{0}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}(f_{r},f^{\prime}_{r})Y_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

and the conclusion follows by the a.s. continuity of the involved processes.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)\
Trivially one has
Y_{0}=\xi
and we can write

\displaystyle Y_{s,t}^{\natural}

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{s,t}-\delta F_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr-\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}-f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}-(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}(f_{r},f^{\prime}_{r})Y_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r}-A_{s,t}.

From stochastic sewing lemma we conclude that
\|Y_{s,t}^{\natural}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}
and
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y_{s,t}^{\natural})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}
.
∎

(A priori estimates)

Let

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T}\big(\|(F,F^{\prime})\|_{\mathscr{D}^{2\gamma}_{Z}L^{p}}+(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|Y^{\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\big)e^{t/\lambda}|t-s|^{\gamma}.

By means of Proposition
A.5
we can deduce an estimate for

. Indeed, by construction, we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta Y^{\natural}_{s,u,t})\|_{p}

Theorem 3.11

(Well-posedness)
.

Assume
p\gamma>1
. Then, for any
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0};W)
, there exists a unique up to indistinguishability
L^{p}
-integrable solution to (
25
) starting from
\xi
.

Proof.

Uniqueness.
Let
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be an
L^{p}
-integrable solution of (
25
) starting from
\xi=0
and with forcing term
(F,F^{\prime})=(0,0)
. Being
Y
continuous and applying Theorem
3.10
, it follows that,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and for any
t\in[0,T]
,
Y_{t}=0
.
The proof of uniqueness can be concluded due to the linearity of the equation.

Existence.
We run a Picard’s iteration argument in three steps. For convenience, let us fix a positive constant
M>0
such that

|\mathbf{Z}|_{\alpha}+\|G\|_{\infty}+\|S\|_{\infty}+\|(f,f^{\prime})\|_{\infty}+\|(f,f^{\prime})\|_{\mathbf{D}_{Z}^{2\gamma}L^{p,\infty}_{lin}}\leq M.

Step 1. (Construction of the iterated processes)
Let us define three initial (and trivially continuous, adapted, and
L^{p}
-integrable) stochastic processes

\displaystyle Y^{-2}_{\cdot}=Y^{-1}_{\cdot}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad Y^{0}_{\cdot}=\xi+F_{\cdot}

By recursion on
n\geq 0
, it is possible to construct a sequence of continuous and adapted
W
-valued stochastic processes
Y^{n+1}=(Y^{n+1}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

with
Y^{n+1}_{0}=\xi
such that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{n+1}_{t}\|_{p}
is finite and the two-parameter process

Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}:=\delta Y^{n+1}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}^{n}\qquad(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}

satisfies
\|Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}
and
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}
, where

A^{n}_{s,t}:=\delta F_{s,t}+\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y^{n}_{r}dr+\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}^{n}dB_{r}+f_{s}Y_{s}^{n}\delta Z_{s,t}+(f^{\prime}_{s}Y^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y^{n-1}_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}.

Indeed, given
n\geq 0
and given the processes
Y^{n}=(Y^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},Y^{n-1}=(Y^{n-1}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
Y^{n-2}=(Y^{n-2}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfying the required recursive properties, one can show that
A^{n}_{t,t}=0
,
A^{n}_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and

\|A^{n}_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\gamma}
, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. Moreover, for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}^{2}
we deduce

\displaystyle\delta A_{s,u,t}^{n}=

\displaystyle=-\delta(f_{\cdot}Y^{n}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\delta{Z}_{u,t}+(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}^{n}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}^{n-1}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\delta{Z}_{s,u}\delta{Z}_{u,t}-\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y^{n}_{\cdot}+f^{2}_{\cdot}Y^{n-1}_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}F^{\prime}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}

\displaystyle=-\big(R_{s,u}^{f}Y^{n}_{s}+f_{s}(\delta Y^{n}_{s,u}-f_{s}Y^{n-1}_{s}\delta{Z}_{s,u}-F^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,u})+\delta{f}_{s,u}\delta Y^{n}_{s,u}\big)\delta{Z}_{u,t}-\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y^{n}_{\cdot}+f^{2}_{\cdot}Y^{n-1}_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}F^{\prime}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}

\displaystyle=-\big(R_{s,u}^{f}Y^{n}_{s}+f_{s}(\int_{s}^{u}G_{r}Y^{n-1}_{r}dr+\int_{s}^{u}S_{r}Y_{r}^{n-1}dB_{r}+(f^{\prime}_{s}Y^{n-1}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y^{n-2}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,u}+Y^{n,\natural}_{s,u}+R^{F}_{s,u})

\displaystyle+\delta{f}_{s,u}\delta{Y^{n}}_{s,u}\big)\delta{Z}_{u,t}-\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}Y^{n}_{\cdot}+f^{2}_{\cdot}Y^{n-1}_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}F^{\prime}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\mathbb{Z}_{u,t},

where we set
Y^{0,\natural}\equiv 0
.
It is standard to show that

\displaystyle\|\delta A^{n}_{s,u,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma},\qquad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta A^{n}_{s,u,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

and it also straightforward to see that

\|\sup_{\tau\in[\frac{s+t}{2},t]}|\delta A^{n}_{s,\frac{s+t}{2},\tau}|\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}.

Therefore,
A^{n}
satisfies the assumptions of the stochastic sewing lemma and, denoting by
\mathcal{A}^{n}=(\mathcal{A}^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the corresponding process, we define

Y^{n+1}_{t}:=\xi+\mathcal{A}^{n}_{t}\qquad t\in[0,T].

Such a process is by construction continuous, adapted and
L^{p}
-integrable, with
Y^{n+1}_{0}=\xi
. Observing that
Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}=\delta Y^{n+1}_{s,t}-A^{n}_{s,t}=\delta\mathcal{A}^{n}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}^{n}
, it holds

\|Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. Moreover, we get
\|\delta Y^{n+1}\|_{\gamma;p}<+\infty
and, consequently,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{n+1}_{t}\|_{p}\leq\|\xi\|_{p}+\|\delta Y^{n+1}\|_{\gamma;p}T^{\gamma}<+\infty.

Step 2. (A sequence of auxiliary processes)
Let us define a new sequence of
W
-valued stochastic processes in the following way:

\displaystyle J^{-1}_{\cdot}\equiv 0,\qquad J^{n}_{\cdot}:=Y^{n}_{\cdot}-Y^{n-1}_{\cdot}\qquad n\geq 0.

By construction, every
J^{n}
is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. continuous, adapted and it satisfies
\|\delta{J}^{n}\|_{\gamma;p}<+\infty
and
\sup_{t}\|J^{n}_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty
. In particular, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
and for any
n\geq 0
, by means of the linearity of the coefficients and of the integrals we can write

(34)

\displaystyle\delta J^{n+1}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\delta Y^{n+1}_{s,t}-\delta Y^{n}_{s,t}=

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}J_{r}^{n}dr+\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}J_{r}^{n}dB_{r}+f_{s}J_{s}^{n}\delta Z_{s,t}+(f^{\prime}_{s}J^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}J^{n-1}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}+J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t},

where we denote by

J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}:=Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}-Y^{n,\natural}_{s,t}\qquad\text{for $n\geq 0$}.

By construction,
\|J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}
and
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}
, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
and for any
n\geq 0
.
Moreover,
J^{n}_{0}=0
for any
n\geq 1
.
This last fact implies, taking into account Proposition
A.3
, that

(35)

(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta{J}^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}\qquad\text{for any $n\geq 1$.}

Arguing in a very similar way to what is done in the proof of Theorem
3.10
, we can show that, for any
n\geq 2
and up to choosing
\lambda>0
sufficiently small,

(36)

(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta J^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T}\lambda^{\gamma n}.

Indeed, from (
34
) we deduce

(37)

\displaystyle(|\delta{J}^{n+1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}\lesssim(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda},

for any
n\geq 0
and with the implicit constant depending on
M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T
. For any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}^{2}
and for any
n\geq 0
it holds that

\displaystyle\delta J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,u,t}

\displaystyle=\delta(f_{\cdot}J^{n}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\delta Z_{u,t}-(f^{\prime}_{s}J^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}J^{n-1}_{s})\delta Z_{s,u}\delta Z_{u,t}+\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}J^{n}_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}^{2}J^{n-1}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}

\displaystyle=\big(\delta(f_{\cdot}J^{n}_{\cdot})_{s,u}-(f^{\prime}_{s}J^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}J^{n-1}_{s})\delta Z_{s,u}\big)\delta Z_{u,t}+\delta(f^{\prime}_{\cdot}J^{n}_{\cdot}+f_{\cdot}^{2}J^{n-1}_{\cdot})_{s,u}\mathbb{Z}_{u,t}

and, provided that
n\geq 1
, we can write

\displaystyle\delta(f_{\cdot}J^{n}_{\cdot})_{s,u}-(f^{\prime}_{s}J^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}J^{n-1}_{s})\delta Z_{s,u}=

\displaystyle=f_{s}(\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}J^{n-1}_{r}dr+\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}J^{n-1}_{r}dB_{r}+(f^{\prime}_{s}J^{n-1}_{s}+f^{2}_{s}J^{n-2}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,u}+J^{n,\natural}_{s,u})+R^{f}_{s,u}J_{s}^{n}+\delta{f}_{s,u}\delta{J}^{n}_{s,u}.

Assuming
|t-s|\leq\lambda
, this leads to

\displaystyle(|\delta{J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,u,t}}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,T}(|\delta J^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta J^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta J^{n-1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}

and

\displaystyle(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta{J^{n+1,\natural}}|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,T}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|J^{n-2}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|J^{n,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta{J}^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}.

Hence, we apply again Proposition
A.5
to
J^{n+1,\natural}
to obtain

\displaystyle(|J^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}

\displaystyle=(|\Lambda(J^{n+1,\natural})|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,p}\lambda^{\gamma}(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta{J}^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|\delta{J}^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,T,M}\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-2}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+

\displaystyle+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta{J}^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta J^{n-1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|\delta J^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda},

for any
n\geq 1
. Recalling (
35
) and (
37
), for any
n\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle(|J^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,p,T,M}\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-2}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+

\displaystyle+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta{J}^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta J^{n-1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,p,T,M}\lambda^{\gamma}\big((|J^{n-2}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta J^{n-1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\big)+\lambda^{\gamma}(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+\lambda^{\gamma}(|\delta{J}^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}.

Therefore, putting everything together we conclude that, up to choosing
\lambda>0
sufficiently small,

(|J^{n-1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta J^{n}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n+1,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma}\big((|J^{n-2}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta J^{n-1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}+(|J^{n,\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\big)

for any
n\geq 2
, where the implicit constant depends on
\alpha,\gamma,p,T
and
M
. This proves (
36
).

Step 3. (Construction of the solution)
We construct the solution as the limit of Picard’s iterations. Recall that
p\in[2,\infty)
is such that
p\gamma>1
.
Let us assume we chose
\lambda>0
sufficiently small so that
\lambda^{\gamma}<\frac{1}{2}
. For any
n\geq 1
and for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
, it holds

\|J^{n}_{t}-J^{n}_{s}\|_{p}\leq\|\delta J^{n}\|_{\gamma;p}|t-s|^{\gamma}.

We can therefore apply Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion (cf.
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
) and we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|J^{n}_{t}|\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,p,T}\|\delta J^{n}\|_{\gamma;p}.

By Markov’s inequality, for any
n\geq 1
we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{n+1}-Y_{t}^{n}|\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{np}\|\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|J^{n+1}_{t}|\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,p,T}2^{np}\|\delta J^{n+1}\|_{\gamma;p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{pT/\lambda}2^{np}(|\delta J^{n+1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}^{p}

and, by means of (
36
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}2^{np}(|\delta J^{n+1}|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,\gamma,p,T}\lambda^{\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}(2\lambda^{\gamma})^{np}<+\infty,

due to the assumption on
\lambda
.
By a standard Borel-Cantelli argument, we obtain the existence of a
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. continuous and adapted process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
defined as

(38)

Y_{t}:=\lim_{n\to+\infty}Y^{n}_{t}\qquad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

By construction,
Y_{0}=\xi
.
Moreover, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|_{p}+\|\lim_{n\to+\infty}Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}^{0}\|_{p}=\|\xi\|_{p}+\|\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}J^{k+1}_{t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|_{p}+e^{T/\lambda}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(|J^{k+1}_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T}\|\xi\|_{p}+e^{T/\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{\gamma(k+2)}<+\infty,

and

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}-Y_{t}^{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\lim_{N\to+\infty}Y^{N}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}\|_{p}=\|\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}Y_{t}^{k+1}-Y_{t}^{k}\|_{p}\leq e^{T/\lambda}\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}(|J_{\cdot}^{k+1}|)_{p;\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma,p,T,M}e^{T/\lambda}\sum_{k=n}^{+\infty}\lambda^{\gamma(k+2)}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as $n\to+\infty$.}

In particular, this shows that the convergence in (
38
) is also a convergence in
L^{p}(\Omega;W)
.
As a consequence, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
it holds that

\displaystyle Y^{\natural}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\delta Y_{s,t}-\delta F_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}dr-\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}dB_{r}-f_{s}Y_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}-(f^{\prime}_{s}Y_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\big(\delta Y^{n+1}_{s,t}-\delta F_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}G_{r}Y_{r}^{n}dr-\int_{s}^{t}S_{r}Y_{r}^{n}dB_{r}-f_{s}Y_{s}^{n}\delta Z_{s,t}-(f^{\prime}_{s}Y^{n}_{s}+f_{s}^{2}Y^{n-1}_{s}+f_{s}F^{\prime}_{s})\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}Y^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t},

where the limit is taken in
L^{p}
.
Taking into account (
36
), for any
n\geq 3
we apply the stochastic sewing lemma to
J^{n,\natural}
and, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
we get

\|J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}=\|\Lambda(J^{n+1,\natural})_{s,t}\|_{p}\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T,\lambda}\lambda^{\gamma(n-1)}|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}

and

\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(J^{n+1,\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}=\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\Lambda(J^{n+1,\natural})_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T,\lambda}\lambda^{\gamma(n-1)}|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

where the implicit constants do not depend on
n
. Here
\Lambda(\cdot)
denotes the sewing map defined in Proposition
A.5
.
Thus, we can pass the two previous inequalities to the limit in
L^{p}
and we can conclude that, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}

\displaystyle\|Y^{\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|Y^{3,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}+\|L^{p}\text{-}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{k=3}^{n}J^{k+1,\natural}_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T,\lambda}\big(\|Y^{3,\natural}\|_{\alpha+\gamma;p}+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\lambda^{\gamma(k-1)}\big)|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}

and, similarly,

\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(Y^{\natural}_{s,t})\|_{p}\lesssim_{M,\alpha,\gamma,p,T,\lambda}|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}.

∎

Theorem 3.13

(Stability)
.

The proof is very similar to the one of Theorem

\|AB-\bar{A}\bar{B}\|_{p}\leq\|A\|_{2p}\|B-\bar{B}\|_{2p}+\|A-\bar{A}\|_{2p}\|\bar{B}\|_{2p}.

According to Theorem

and

(|\bar{Y}_{\cdot}|)_{2p;\lambda_{0}}+(|\delta\bar{Y}|)_{\gamma;2p;\lambda_{0}}+(|\bar{Y}^{\natural}|)_{\alpha+\gamma;2p;\lambda_{0}}+(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\bar{R}^{\bar{Y}}|)_{2\gamma;2p;\lambda_{0}}\lesssim_{M_{p},\alpha,\gamma,2p,T}1,

for that particular choice of
\lambda_{0}
and so for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]
(cf. Remark
A.2

For any
t\in[0,T]
, let us define

\tilde{Y}_{t}:=Y_{t}-\bar{Y}_{t}.

For simplicity, we also denote by

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{t}

\displaystyle=Z_{t}-\bar{Z}_{t}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}-\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{s,t}

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{t}

4.
Malliavin differentiability of the solutions

In this section we consider a geometric rough path
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{0,\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
, and some coefficients vector fields
b\in{C}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma\in{C}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and
\beta\in{C}^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
For a fixed
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the solution of the following RSDE:

(41)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}+(\beta(X_{t}),D\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t}))d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle X_{0}=x_{0}.

Our aim is to prove the Malliavin-differentiability of such a solution, under those standard regularity assumptions on the coefficients. We rely on the following result, coming from general Malliavin calculus theory (cf.
[
25
, Lemma 1.2.3]
and
[
25
, Proposition 1.5.5]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Assume that, for a certain
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, there exists
(F_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
such that
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\big(\int_{0}^{T}\dots\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbb{D}^{k}_{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}}F_{n}|^{2}d\theta_{1}\dots d\theta_{k}\big)<+\infty.

Then
F\in\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
and
\mathbb{D}^{k}F_{n}\rightharpoonup\mathbb{D}^{k}F
in
L^{2}([0,T]^{k}\times\Omega;(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\times k})
. Moreover, if for some
p>2
it holds that
F\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{D}^{l}F\in L^{p}([0,T]^{l}\times\Omega;(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\times l})
for
l=1,\dots,k
, then
F\in\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
.

The following shows how it is possible to apply Malliavin calculus to RSDEs, to obtain the Malliavin differentiability of solutions.
Moreover, we also get that the Malliavin derivative of these solutions is itself the solution to a linear RSDE.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be as in (
41
) and let us fix an arbitrary
t\in[0,T]
. Then, for any
i=1,\dots,d

X_{t}^{i}\in\bigcap_{p\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{1,p}\quad\text{}

Moreover, for
\lambda

\theta\in[0,T]

\mathbb{P}

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}=\begin{cases}Y^{\theta}_{t}&\text{if $\theta\leq t$}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}

(42)

\displaystyle Y^{\theta}_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{\theta})+\int_{\theta}^{s}Db(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}dr+\int_{\theta}^{s}D\sigma(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}dB_{r}+

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta}^{s}(D\beta(X_{r}),D^{2}\beta(X_{r})\beta(X_{r}))Y^{\theta}_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r}\qquad s\in[\theta,T].

Proof.

We argue by approximation, taking into account Lemma
4.1
. Let
(Z^{n})_{n}\subseteq C^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
be any sequence of smooth rough paths whose rough path lifts
(\mathbf{Z}^{n}=(Z^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n}))_{n}

\mathbf{Z}

as

is geometric, and the argument of this proof does not depend on the choice of such a sequence. By construction there is a constant

(43)

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by

(44)

\displaystyle dX^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X^{n}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{n}_{t})dB_{t}+(\beta(X^{n}_{t}),D\beta(X^{n}_{t})\beta(X^{n}_{t}))d\mathbf{Z}^{n}_{t}\qquad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle=x_{0}.

[
15

in

. According to Lemma

X^{n,i}_{t}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
,
\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X^{n}_{t}=Y^{n,\theta}_{t}

\lambda
-a.e.

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X^{n}_{t}=0

\lambda
-a.e.

3.5

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\int_{0}^{t}|\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}^{n,i}|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}^{2}d\theta\big)

Consider the following stochastic linear vector fields

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:=Db(X_{\cdot})\qquad G^{n}:=Db(X^{n}_{\cdot}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle:=D\sigma(X_{\cdot})\qquad S^{n}:=D\sigma(X^{n}_{\cdot}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle(f,f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\theta^{n}_{p,\gamma}:=

\displaystyle+\|\delta(f^{n}-{f})\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\delta((f^{n})^{\prime}-{f}^{\prime})\|_{\gamma;p}+\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}(R^{n,{f^{n}}}-{R}^{{f}})\|_{2\gamma;p},

Theorem 4.4

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the solution of (

\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}))

and for any

X_{t}^{i}\in\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}}\bigcap_{p\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{k,p}.

Let
t\in[0,T]
and
i=1,\dots,d
. By strong induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}

we prove that

X_{t}^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{k,p}\quad\text{for any $p\geq 2$}.

Operatively, we first prove that
X_{t}^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{k,2}
, arguing by approximation. Then, knowing that lower order Malliavin derivatives satisfy suitable linear RSDEs, we prove
X_{t}^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
for any
p>2
.
Let us fix any approximating sequence
(Z^{n})_{n}
of smooth paths, whose
canonical rough path lifts converge to
\mathbf{Z}
in the
\alpha
-rough path metric as

Lemma 4.5

For any

5.
Hörmander-type results

5.1.
Existence of densities under Hörmander condition

In this section we prove the existence of densities for the law of the solution of equation (
9
) under Hörmander condition.

For a random vector
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{d})
, such that
X^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
for every
i=1,\dots,d
, we define the Malliavin matrix as

(45)

\gamma_{X}:=(\langle\mathbb{D}_{\cdot}X^{i},\mathbb{D}_{\cdot}X^{j}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,T])})_{i,j=1,\dots,d}.

From classical Malliavin theory, if
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{d})
is such that
X^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
, for every
i=1,\dots,d
and for some
p>1
, then the law of
X
admits a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure when the Malliavin matrix
\gamma_{X}
is a.s. invertible, see
[
25
, Theorem 2.1.2]
. Moreover, the density is smooth whenever
X
is Malliavin smooth and the inverse of
\gamma_{X}
has moments of all orders, see
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
24
, Theorem 2.1.4]
.

When we work with
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
solution to (
9
), it is convenient to introduce the reduced Malliavin matrix of
X_{t}
. In order to define it, we first formally derive equation (
9
) in the initial condition.
Let
J=(J_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the continuous and adapted
\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued stochastic process which solves the following linear RSDE:

(46)

\displaystyle dJ_{t}=Db(X_{t})J_{t}dt+D\sigma(X_{t})J_{t}dB_{t}+(D\beta(X_{t}),D^{2}\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t}))J_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle J_{0}=id_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Moreover, let us denote by
I=(I_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the
\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued solution of the following linear RSDE:

(47)

\displaystyle dI_{t}=-I_{t}(Db(X_{t})-D\sigma^{2}(X_{t}))dt-I_{t}D\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}-I_{t}(D\beta(X_{t}),D^{2}\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t}))d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle I_{0}=id_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Notice that both equation (
46
) and (
47
) are well-posed by Theorem
3.11
.
As an application of the rough Itô formula, equation (
13
), we have that
I_{t}
is the matrix inverse of
J_{t}
. Indeed,
for any
t\in[0,T]
we have,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle d(IJ)_{t}

\displaystyle=I_{t}dJ_{t}+dI_{t}J_{t}+d\langle I,J\rangle_{t}=

\displaystyle=I_{t}Db(X_{t})J_{t}dt+I_{t}D\beta(X_{t}))J_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t}+I_{t}D\sigma(X_{t})J_{t}dB_{t}

\displaystyle-I_{t}(Db(X_{t})-D\sigma(X_{t})^{2})J_{t}dt+D\beta(X_{t})J_{t}d\mathbf{Z}_{t}-I_{t}D\sigma(X_{t})J_{t}dB_{t}

\displaystyle-I_{t}D\sigma(X_{t})^{2}J_{t}=0.

We define the reduced Malliavin matrix of
X_{t}
as

(48)

C_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}I_{s}\sigma(X_{s})\sigma^{\top}(X_{s})I_{s}^{\top}\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in[0,T].

As in the classical case, we have
\gamma_{X_{t}}=J_{t}C_{t}J_{t}^{\top}
. Indeed, we first multiply equation (
46
) to the right by
I_{\theta}\sigma(X_{\theta})
, for
\theta\in[0,t]
, to obtain that
J_{t}I_{\theta}\sigma(X_{\theta})
solves the same linear equation as the Malliavin derivative
\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}
, equation (
42
). By uniqueness of solutions to linear RSDEs, Theorem
3.11
, we have that,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}=J_{t}I_{\theta}\sigma(X_{\theta}),\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T.

Now, by definition, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\gamma_{X_{t}}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{D}_{s}X_{t}(\mathbb{D}_{s}X_{t})^{\top}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}J_{t}I_{s}\sigma(X_{s})\sigma(X_{s})^{\top}I_{s}^{\top}J_{t}^{\top}\mathrm{d}s=J_{t}C_{t}J_{t}^{\top}.

In order to prove that the Malliavin matrix is invertible, and the inverse has moments of all order, it is enough to prove the same properties for the reduced Malliavin matrix.

Before diving into the proof of existence and smoothness of densities, we need an extra assumption on the rough path driving the equation. In short, if the rough path is just the lift of a (relatively) smooth process, it could be assimilated to a drift and it would help much less in spreading the diffiusion in all directions. Instead, we will work under the assumption that the rough path is truly rough in the sense of the following definition (see
[
14
, Definition 6.3]
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
. Let
s\in[0,T)
, we say that
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
rough at time s
if for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\overline{\lim}_{t\downarrow s}\frac{|v\cdot\delta Z_{s,t}|}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}=\infty.

If
\mathbf{Z}
is rough at
s
for every
s
in a dense subset of
[0,T]
we say that
\mathbf{Z}
is
truly rough
.

It is classical that, if a rough path is truly rough, then the Gubinelli derivative of any controlled path is unique, see
[
14
, Proposition 6.4]
. This is the case also for stochastically controlled path, as shown in the following proposition, which works for any Banach space
W
. However, we will only apply it in the finite dimensional case.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
truly rough. If
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p,q}(W)
, with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and
X^{\prime}
has continuous sample paths, then

(1)

If
\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta X\in C^{2\alpha}L^{q}(W)
then
X^{\prime}
is indistinguishable from
0
.

(2)

(Uniqueness of Gubinelli derivative) If
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
are both Gubinelli derivatives of
X
, then they are indistinguishable.

Proof.

In order to prove the first statement, we first notice that

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|X^{\prime}_{s}Z_{s,t}\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|\mathbb{E}_{s}[X_{s,t}-R^{X}_{s,t}]\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}<+\infty.

Let
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
and
W^{\ast}
be the dual of
W
. We take
w^{\ast}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(W^{\ast})
, with
\|w^{\ast}\|_{q^{\prime}}=1
.
Let us now apply the definiton of rough at time
s\in[0,T)
with the vector
v:=\mathbb{E}[(Y^{\prime})^{\ast}w^{\ast}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\overline{\lim}_{t\downarrow s}\frac{|v\cdot Z_{s,t}|}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}=\overline{\lim}_{t\downarrow s}\frac{|\mathbb{E}[w^{\ast}(X^{\prime}_{s}Z_{s,t})]|}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}\leq\|w\|_{q^{\prime}}\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|X^{\prime}_{s}Z_{s,t}\|_{q}}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}<+\infty.

This implies that
v=0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hence

\|X^{\prime}_{s}\|_{q}=\sup_{\|w^{\ast}\|_{q^{\prime}}=1}|\mathbb{E}[(X^{\prime}_{s})^{\ast}w^{\ast}]|=0.

Hence,
X^{\prime}_{s}=0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for
s
in a dense subset of
[0,T]
, without loss of generality, we can assume that the dense is countable and, by continuity, we have that
X^{\prime}
is indistinguishable from
0
.

The second statement is a direct consequence of the first. Indeed, assume that there are two Gubinelli derivatives for
Y
, say
Y^{1}
,
Y^{2}
, then

0=(X^{1}-X^{2})_{s}Z_{s,t}+R_{s,t}

for some
R\in C^{2\alpha}_{2}L^{q}(W)
. This implies that
X^{1}-X^{2}
is the Gubinelli derivative of
0
, which is
0
, by uniqueness.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Proposition
5.2
is a slight generalization of
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
. Notice that in that paper they only assume that
X
is a martingale, whereas, under assumptions,
X
can also be the sum of a martingale and a finite variation process.

Moreover, the assumption of path continuity for
X^{\prime}
is needed if we want uniqueness up to indistinguishability. Without uniqueness one obtains that any two Gubinalli derivatives coincide on a dense subset of times, up to a null set, which possibly depends on time.

As an immediate consequence, we have uniqueness in the decomposition of a path as the sum of a stochastic integral, a rough integral and a Lebesgue integral. Assume
W
is a finite dimensional Banach space.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
truly rough, with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
. Let
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a rough Itô process with coefficients
b,\sigma
. Assume that
X\equiv 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, then

b\equiv 0
,
\sigma\equiv 0
and
(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})\equiv 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

Proof.

We define the path

(49)

Y_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}=-\int_{0}^{t}(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})_{s}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{s}.

Then
(Y,X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{\mathbf{Z}}^{2\alpha}L^{p}(U)
for every
p\geq 0
. However,
\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta Y\in C^{2\alpha}_{2}L^{p}(U)
. By Proposition
5.2
we have that
(X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime})\equiv(0,0)
, which also implies that the rough integral and hence each term in (
49
) is
0
. Since the central term in (
49
) is
0
, by classical stochastic calculus also
b
and
\sigma
vanish.
∎

We are now ready to prove existence of densities under Hörmander condition.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{C}^{0,\alpha}_{g}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a truly rough geometric rough path.
Assume that the coefficients
b,\sigma,\beta
satisfy Hörmander’s condition
1.1
. The solution
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
to equation (
9
) admits a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of the classical proof for SDEs (see
[
25
, Theorem 2.3.2]
). The goal is to apply
[
25
, Theorem 2.1.2]
by showing that the Malliavin matrix
\gamma_{X_{t}}
, defined in (
45
), is almost surely invertible. Equivalently, we check that the reduced Malliavin matrix
C_{t}
is almost surely invertible.

First we notice that we can actually replace the spaces
\{\mathcal{S}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{0}
and

(50)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i+1}:=&\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\cup\{[F,V]\mid F\in\{\sigma^{1},\dots,\sigma^{m},\beta^{1},\dots,\beta^{n}\},V\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\}\\
&\cup\left\{[b,V]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}[\sigma^{k},[\sigma^{k},V]]\mid V\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\right\},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N}.\end{aligned}

Indeed, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{V(x)\mid V\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}\}
spans the same space as
\{V(x)\mid V\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\}
.

Fix
t>0
. Assume that there exist
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle 0=\xi^{\top}C_{t}\xi:=\xi^{\top}\left(\int_{0}^{t}I_{s}\sigma\sigma^{\top}(X_{s})I_{s}^{\top}\mathrm{d}s\right)\xi=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}|\xi^{\top}I_{s}\sigma^{k}(X_{s})|^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\xi^{\top}I_{s}V(X_{s})|=0,\qquad\forall V\in\mathcal{S}_{0}.

If we can prove that, for every

,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

(51)

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\xi^{\top}I_{s}V(X_{s})|=0,\qquad\forall V\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}.

we can then take

It is only left to prove (

Let

be a smooth bounded vector field. We define

(52)

W_{t}(F):=\xi^{\top}I_{t}F(X_{t}).

It follows from the rough Itô formula
[

that

is a rough Itô process satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
2.16

By Proposition

(53)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}W_{t}(F)

\displaystyle=\left(W_{t}([b,F])+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}W_{t}([\sigma^{k},[\sigma^{k},F]])\right)\mathrm{d}t+W_{t}([\sigma,F])\mathrm{d}B_{t}+W_{t}([\beta,F])\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t},

5.2.
Smoothness of densities under Hörmander condition

In order to obtain smoothness of densities, it is crucial to obtain an analogous of Norris’s Lemma for a stochastic controlled path.

The starting point is to give a quantitative definition of truly rough (see Definition
5.1
). We have the following from
[
14
, Definition 6.7]

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
.
We say that
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\theta
-Hölder rough for
\theta\in(0,1)
on scale
\epsilon_{0}>0
if there exists a constant

such that for every

,
s\in[0,T]
and
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
there exists

with

|t-s|<\epsilon,\quad|v\cdot Z_{s,t}|\geq L\epsilon^{\theta}|v|.

Notice that, if
\mathbf{Z}
is
\theta
-Hölder rough for some
\theta<2\alpha
then it is truly rough. In particular this implies that every stochastic controlled path
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{\mathbf{Z}}^{2\alpha}L^{p,q}(W)
has a unique Guibinelli derivative
X^{\prime}
.

However, the notion of
\theta
-rough allows us to give the following quantitative estimate of the Gubinelli derivative in term of the path and the remainder

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and
\theta<2\alpha
. If
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\theta
-Hölder rough on scale
\epsilon_{0}>0
with modulus
L>0
and
(X,X^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p,q}(W)
for every

then

\forall\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}],\qquad L\epsilon^{\theta}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{\prime}_{t}|\leq\sup_{|t-s|\leq\epsilon}\left|\mathbb{E}_{s}\delta X_{s,t}+\mathbb{E}_{s}R_{s,t}\right|

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
Moreover, for every
\bar{\alpha}\in(\frac{\theta}{2},\alpha-\frac{1}{2q}]

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{\prime}_{t}|\leq\frac{1}{L}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|+\sup_{|t-s|\leq\epsilon_{0}}|\mathbb{E}_{s}\delta X_{s,t}|)\left(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R|_{2\bar{\alpha}}^{\theta/2\bar{\alpha}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{-\theta/2\bar{\alpha}}\vee\epsilon_{0}^{-\theta}\right).

Proof.

Notice that, if
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|=0
, then
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\prime}|=0
by Proposition
5.2
and the statement becomes trivial. We thus give the proof under the assumption that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|>0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

In the proof we keep track of the space whenever we take the relevant norm (unless it is the usual absolute value in
\mathbb{R}
).

We first prove the first statement.
Let
s\in[0,T]
and
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}]
. Moreover, in the following we can fix
\omega\in\Omega
as the argument is completely deterministic. We take
w^{\ast}\in W^{\ast}
and, by calling
X^{\ast}\in\mathscr{L}(W,\mathbb{R}^{n})
the adjoint operator of
X
, we notice that
(X_{s})^{\ast}w^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. There exists a random time
\bar{t}=\bar{t}(s,\epsilon,w^{\ast})>0
such that
|\bar{t}-s|\leq\epsilon
and

(54)

L\epsilon^{\theta}|(X^{\prime}_{s})^{\ast}w^{\ast}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}<|(X^{\prime}_{s})^{\ast}w^{\ast}\cdot\delta Z_{s,\bar{t}}|\leq|w^{\ast}(X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,\bar{t}})|\leq\sup_{t:|t-s|\leq\epsilon}|w^{\ast}(X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t})|.

We take the supremum in

and

in (

\displaystyle L\epsilon^{\theta}\sup_{s}|X^{\prime}_{s}|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)}

\displaystyle=L\epsilon^{\theta}\sup_{s}\sup_{|w^{\ast}|_{W^{\ast}}\leq 1}|(X^{\prime}_{s})^{\ast}w^{\ast}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq L\epsilon^{\theta}\sup_{s,t:|t-s|\leq\epsilon}\sup_{|w^{\ast}|_{W^{\ast}}\leq 1}|w^{\ast}(X^{\prime}_{s}\delta Z_{s,t})|

\displaystyle\leq L\epsilon^{\theta}\sup_{|t-s|\leq\epsilon}|X_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}|_{W}.

In order to conclude the proof notice that, by the definition of stochastic controlled path, we have,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\sup_{|t-s|\leq\epsilon}|X_{s}\delta Z_{s,t}|_{W}=\sup_{|t-s|\leq\epsilon}\left|\mathbb{E}_{s}\delta X_{s,t}+\mathbb{E}_{s}R_{s,t}\right|_{W}.

The second statement follows from the first by choosing

\epsilon:=|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R|_{2\bar{\alpha}}^{-1/2\bar{\alpha}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|_{W}^{1/2\bar{\alpha}}\wedge\epsilon_{0}.

Notice that, as the previous argument is completely deterministic, we can actually choose
\epsilon
random.
Notice that, by Kolmogorov continuity theorem (see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
), if

\||\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R|_{2\bar{\alpha}}\|_{q}\leq C\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}R\|_{2\bar{\alpha};q}<\infty

so that
\epsilon(\omega)\in[0,\epsilon_{0}]
is well defined,
\omega
-a.s.
∎

\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]

Step 1

Appendix A
Weighted norms

We introduce a new class of “weighted” Hölder norms, which can be seen as the as the stochastic counterpart of the ones introduced in

The definition of these norms, and consequently all the related results, depends on the choice of a parameter
\lambda\in(0,+\infty)

(W,|\cdot|)

Definition A.1

(Weighted norms)
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]

Y=(Y_{t})_{[0,T]}

-integrable stochastic process. We define

Let
A=(A_{s,t})_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}}
be a
L^{p}
-integrable two-paramenter stochastic process. We define

\displaystyle(|A|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\underset{|t-s|\leq\lambda}{0\leq s<t\leq T}}\ \frac{\|A_{s,t}\|_{p}}{e^{t/\lambda}|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

Similarly, we can also consider a
L^{p}
-integrable three-parameter stochastic process
\Xi=(\Xi_{s,u,t})_{(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}^{2}}
and we can define

(|\Xi|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda}:=\sup_{\underset{|t-s|\leq\lambda}{0\leq s<u<t\leq T}}\ \frac{\|\Xi_{s,u,t}\|_{p}}{e^{t/\lambda}|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

Remark A.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
be such that
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
. For any
t\in[0,T]
, it holds

\frac{\|Y_{t}\|_{p}}{e^{t/\lambda_{1}}}=\frac{\|Y_{t}\|_{p}}{e^{t/\lambda_{2}}}e^{(t/\lambda_{2}-t/\lambda_{1})}\leq\frac{\|Y_{t}\|_{p}}{e^{t/\lambda_{2}}}\leq(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda_{2}}

and, therefore,
(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda_{1}}\leq(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda_{2}}
. With the same technique, one case also prove that
(|\cdot|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda_{1}}\leq(|\cdot|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda_{2}}
.

The following result shows the link between the weighted norm of a process and the weighted norm of its increment. The presence of that
\lambda^{\alpha}
factor will appear as a fundamental motivation for choosing this kind of norms to handle linear RSDEs.

Proposition A.3

(Comparison result)
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
be fixed. Then, for any
L^{p}
-integrable stochastic process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, it holds

(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}\leq\|Y_{0}\|_{p}+e^{2}\lambda^{\alpha}(|\delta Y|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda}.

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T]
. Let
N=N(t)\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
be such that
(N-1)\lambda<t\leq N\lambda
and consider the partition
\pi^{[0,t],\lambda}=\{t_{0}=0,t_{1}=\lambda,\dots,t_{N-1}=(N-1)\lambda,t_{N}=t\}
of the interval
[0,t]
. Notice that
|\pi^{[0,t],\lambda}|\leq\lambda
. Then, for any
t\in[0,T]
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|Y_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(Y_{t_{k+1}}-Y_{t_{k}})\|_{p}\leq\|Y_{0}\|_{p}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|Y_{t_{k+1}}-Y_{t_{k}}\|_{p}=

\displaystyle=\|Y_{0}\|_{p}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\|Y_{t_{k+1}}-Y_{t_{k}}\|_{p}}{e^{t_{k+1}/\lambda}|t_{k+1}-t_{k}|^{\alpha}}e^{t_{k+1}/\lambda}|t_{k+1}-t_{k}|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|Y_{0}\|_{p}e^{t/\lambda}+(|\delta Y|)_{\alpha;p;\lambda}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{t_{k+1}/\lambda}\right)\lambda^{\alpha}.

By construction
(N+1)\lambda<t+2\lambda
. Therefore, by noticing that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{t_{k+1}}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{(k+1)\lambda}{\lambda}}=\frac{e^{N+1}-e}{e-1}\leq e^{N+1}<e^{\frac{t+2\lambda}{\lambda}}=e^{2}e^{\frac{t}{\lambda}}

we reach the conclusion.
∎

Remark A.4
.

Let us consider a
L^{p}
-integrable stochastic process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\lambda>0
. By definition of weighted norm, it is straightforward to see that

(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}\leq e^{T/\lambda}(|Y_{\cdot}|)_{p;\lambda}.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1]
. As a general fact
(|\cdot|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\gamma;p}
, being
e^{t/\lambda}\geq 1
for any
t\in[0,T]
. By definition, given an
L^{p}
-integrable stochastic process
A=(A_{s,t})_{[s,t]\in\Delta_{[0,T]}}
it holds that

(|A|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}\leq\|A\|_{\gamma;p}.

If
A
is additive (i.e.
\delta A=0
), we also get that

\|A\|_{\gamma;p}\leq e^{2}e^{T/\lambda}(|A|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}.

Indeed, the inequality follows by applying the same argument of the proof of Proposition
A.3
over any time interval
[s,t]
and by writing
A_{s,t}
as a telescopic sum of increments of
A
along
\pi^{[s,t],\lambda}
. In particular, the norm
(|\cdot|)_{p;\lambda}+(|\delta\cdot|)_{\gamma;p;\lambda}
is equivalent to the standard norm on
{C}^{\gamma}L^{p}(W)
.

By means of the stochastic sewing lemma, it is also possible to deduce an estimate involving weighted norms of two-parameter processes. The following result shows that we can use stochastic sewing, not only to construct the rough stochastic integral, but also to deduce some useful bounds.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty),\ \alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}],\ \gamma\in(\frac{1}{3},\alpha]
and
\lambda>0
. Let
A=(A_{s,t})_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}}
be a
L^{p}
-integrable stochastic process such that
A_{t,t}=0
,
A_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable,

\|\delta A_{s,u,t}\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\delta A_{s,u,t})\|_{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,T]}
.
By applying Theorem
2.6
with
p=q
and being
\frac{1}{3}<\gamma\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
, the following sewing map is well defined:

\Lambda(A):\Delta_{[0,T]}\ni(s,t)\longmapsto\Lambda(A)_{s,t}:=\delta\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}\in L^{p}(\Omega;W).

Then there exist some constants
c_{\alpha,\gamma},c^{\prime}_{\alpha,\gamma,p}>0
such that

(62)

(|\Lambda(A)|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}\leq c_{\alpha,\gamma}\lambda^{\gamma}(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta A|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}+c^{\prime}_{\alpha,\gamma,p}(|\delta A|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}.

Proof.

Let
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
such that
|t-s|\leq\lambda
. For any
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\pi^{h}=\{s=t_{0}^{h}<t_{1}^{h}<\dots<t_{2^{h}}^{h}=t\}
be the
h
-th dyadic partition of
[s,t]
and denote by
u_{i}^{h}
the middle point of any interval
[t_{i}^{h},t_{i+1}^{h}]
,
i=0,\dots,2^{h}-1
. Then Theorem
2.6
and an application of BDG inequality - as shown in
[
22
, Section 2]
- lead to

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(A)_{s,t}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\delta\mathcal{A}_{s,t}-A_{s,t}\|_{p}=\|L^{p}\text{-}\lim_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{h}-1}A_{t_{i}^{h},t_{i+1}^{h}}-A_{t^{0}_{0},t^{0}_{1}}\|_{p}=

\displaystyle=\|L^{p}\text{-}\lim_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k+1}-1}A_{t_{i}^{k+1},t_{i+1}^{k+1}}-\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}A_{t_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}}\big)\|_{p}=

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\|\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}-\delta A_{t_{i}^{k},u_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}\|\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}^{k}}(\delta A_{t_{i}^{k},u_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}})\|_{p}+2c_{p}^{1/p}\big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}\|\delta A_{t_{i}^{k},u_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}}\|_{p}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

To conclude we can now note that, for every
h\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}\|\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}^{k}}(\delta A_{t_{i}^{k},u_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta A|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}e^{t_{i+1}^{k}/\lambda}|t_{i+1}^{k}-t_{i}^{k}|^{\alpha+2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta A|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}e^{t/\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}(2^{-k}|t-s|)^{\alpha+2\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma}(|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\delta A|)_{\alpha+2\gamma;p;\lambda}e^{t/\lambda}\lambda^{\gamma}|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}\|\delta A_{t_{i}^{k},u_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}}\|_{p}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}(|\delta A|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}^{2}e^{2t_{i+1}^{k}/\lambda}|t_{i+1}^{k}-t_{i}^{k}|^{2(\alpha+\gamma)}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(|\delta A|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}e^{t/\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\big(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}(2^{-k}|t-s|)^{2(\alpha+\gamma)}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\gamma}(|\delta A|)_{\alpha+\gamma;p;\lambda}e^{t/\lambda}|t-s|^{\alpha+\gamma}.

∎

Appendix B
Malliavin calculus for smooth RSDEs

We recollect some result concerning the application of Malliavin calculus to rough stochastic differential equations driven by smooth rough paths. Solutions to the latter, as one may expect, can also be seen as solutions to purely stochastic differential equations.
In our context it is always convenient to take into account the double nature of such solutions, since we want to apply Malliavin calculus but also to use estimates and stabililty results coming from rough stochastic equations.
Throughout the entire section
Z:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth path and
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
is its canonical rough path lift. Recall that
\mathbf{Z}\in\bigcap_{\frac{1}{3}<\delta\leq 1}\mathscr{C}^{\delta}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The following allows us to treat RSDEs driven by smooth rough paths as SDEs.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and let
p\in[2,\infty),\ q\in[p,\infty]
. Let
(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathscr{D}_{Z}^{2\alpha}L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
Y
is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. continuous and
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y_{t}\|_{p}<+\infty
. Then,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{0}^{t}(Y_{r},Y^{\prime}_{r})d\mathbf{Z}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}dZ_{r}.

Proof.

The conclusion simply follows from the uniqueness part of Theorem
2.6
. Indeed, it is sufficient to observe that the continuous, adapted and
L^{p}
-integrable stochastic process
\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{r}dZ_{r}=(\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}dZ_{r})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfies
\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}dZ_{r}\big|_{t=0}=0
and

\|\|\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}dZ_{r}-Y_{s}\delta{Z}_{s,t}-Y^{\prime}_{s}\mathbb{Z}_{s,t}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\|_{q}\lesssim|t-s|^{1+\varepsilon},

for a certain
\varepsilon>0
and for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{[0,T]}
. Recall that
\|\mathbb{E}_{s}(\cdot)\|_{q}\leq\|\|\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{s}\|_{p}\|_{q}
.
∎

Let us consider some coefficients vector fields
b\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and
\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathscr{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
We denote by
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the unique solution of the following RSDE driven by the smooth rough path
\mathbf{Z}
:

(63)

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t})dB_{t}+(\beta(X_{t}),D\beta(X_{t})\beta(X_{t}))d\mathbf{Z}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle X_{0}

\displaystyle=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By applying Proposition
B.1
, one can easily deduce that
X
is indistinguishable from
\bar{X}=(\bar{X}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, being
\bar{X}
the solution of the following stochastic differential equation:

\displaystyle d\bar{X}_{t}

\displaystyle=(b(\bar{X}_{t})+\beta(\bar{X}_{t})\dot{Z}_{t})dt+\sigma(\bar{X}_{t})dB_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle X_{0}

\displaystyle=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The fact that
X
can also be seen as the solution of a pure SDE is a key point of our work, since Malliavin calculus classically applies to SDEs (see, for example,
[
25
, Section 2.2]
). In the following two lemmas we state the main results concerning Malliavin calculus and its application to smooth RSDEs.

Lemma B.2
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
and for any
i=1,\dots,d
,

X_{t}^{i}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.

Moreover, for
\lambda
-a.e.
\theta\in[0,T]
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\mathbb{D}_{\theta}X_{t}=\begin{cases}Y_{t}^{\theta}&\text{if $\theta\leq t$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

\displaystyle dY^{\theta}_{r}=Db(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}dr+D\sigma(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}dB_{r}+(D\beta(X_{r}),D^{2}\beta(X_{r})\beta(X_{r}))Y^{\theta}_{r}d\mathbf{Z}_{r},

\displaystyle r\in[\theta,T]

\displaystyle Y^{\theta}_{\theta}=\sigma(X_{\theta}).

See
[
25

. Notice that the function

satisfies the assumptions (h1)-(h2) required on the drift coefficient in that paper. The conclusion follows by noting that

is (indistinguishable from) the solution of the following SDE:

\displaystyle dY^{\theta}_{r}=(Db(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}+D\beta(X_{r})\dot{Z}_{t})dr+D\sigma(X_{r})Y^{\theta}_{r}dB_{r},\quad r\in[\theta,T]

\displaystyle Y^{\theta}_{\theta}=\sigma(X_{\theta}).

∎

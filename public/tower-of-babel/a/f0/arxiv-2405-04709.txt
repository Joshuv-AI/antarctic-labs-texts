Quillen’s Fundamental Spectral Sequences Revisited
Hossein Faridian

Abstract
. Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences relate André-Quillen homology and cohomology to Tor and Ext functors. The five-term exact sequences arising from these spectral sequences are leveraged to characterize regular and complete intersection local rings. Despite their immense importance in the theory of André-Quillen homology and cohomology, these spectral sequences are not treated in the literature as they merit. A sketchy argument with gaps for a special case of the first spectral sequence appears in an unpublished manuscript of Quillen, while the second spectral sequence is only stated without proof in another paper of Quillen. A rigorous proof of the spectral sequences requires a delicate investigation of the subtle structures involved. The goal of this expository article is to present a comprehensive and detailed proof of Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences in a readable and well-structured manner.

1.
Introduction

In the late 60’s, M. André and D. Quillen independently introduced a (co)homology theory for commutative algebras that now goes by the name of André-Quillen (co)homology. The theory was later developed for schemes and more generally for ringed topoi by L. Illusie. André uses ad hoc definitions and constructions, Quillen deploys his model category theory, and Illusie utilizes Grothendieck topos theory; see
[
An
]
,
[
Qu3
]
, and
[
Il
]
. The theory of André-Quillen (co)homology has countless applications in local algebra, algebraic geometry, and number theory: characterization of regular and complete intersection local rings, characterization of separable field extensions, characterization of unramified extensions of number fields, and deformation theory of schemes, to name a few. For more applications of the theory, refer to
[
MR
]
,
[
Av1
]
,
[
Iy
]
,
[
BI
]
, and
[
GS
]
.

To get a flavor of the theory, let
R
be a commutative ring,
A
a commutative
R
-algebra, and
M
an
A
-module. A derivation of
A
over
R
with coefficients in
M
is an
R
-homomorphism
D:A\rightarrow M
that satisfies the Leibniz rule, i.e.
D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a)
for every
a,b\in A
. The set of all derivations of
A
over
R
with coefficients in
M
, denoted by
\operatorname{Der}_{R}(A,M)
, is an
A
-submodule of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,M)
. In addition, the induced functor
\operatorname{Der}_{R}(A,-):\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is representable, i.e. there exists an
A
-module
\Omega_{A|R}
, the so-called module of differentials of
A
over
R
, and a universal derivation
d_{A|R}:A\rightarrow\Omega_{A|R}
that induces a natural
A
-isomorphism
\operatorname{Der}_{R}(A,M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\Omega_{A|R},M\right)
. The module of differentials
\Omega_{A|R}
can be concretely described as
\Omega_{A|R}=\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{2}
where
\mathfrak{k}=\operatorname{Ker}(\mu)
in which
\mu:A\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow A
is given by
\mu(a\otimes b)=ab
for every
a,b\in A
. In addition, the universal derivation
d_{A|R}:A\rightarrow\Omega_{A|R}
is given by
d_{A|R}(a)=(1\otimes a-a\otimes 1)+\mathfrak{k}^{2}
for every
a\in A
. Now assume that
R\rightarrow S\rightarrow T
are two homomorphisms of commutative rings, and
M
is a
T
-module. Then one has the following exact sequences:

\Omega_{S|R}\otimes_{S}M\rightarrow\Omega_{T|R}\otimes_{T}M\rightarrow\Omega_{T|S}\otimes_{T}M\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{S}(T,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{R}(T,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{R}(S,M)

Historically speaking, people grew interested in extending these sequences beyond the above-displayed three terms. Grothendieck extended the second sequence to a six-term exact sequence by the Exalcomm functor, Lichtenbaum and Schlessinger extended it further to a nine-term exact sequence, and finally, André and Quillen were able to extend the sequences indefinitely; see
[
Gr
]
,
[
LS
]
,
[
An
]
,
[
Qu1
]
, and
[
Qu3
]
. Viewing derivations and differentials as functors of commutative algebras, one requires to have some sort of derived functors in order to extend the sequences. Applying the general theory of derived functors in model categories to the category of simplicial commutative algebras, one can construct the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\in\operatorname{Obj}\left(\mathcal{D}(A)\right)
as the image of the identity map
1^{A}:A\rightarrow A
under the left derived functor of the abelianization functor followed by the right derived functor of the normalization functor. The cotangent complex is in turn used to define the André-Quillen homology and cohomology modules as
H_{i}^{AQ}\left(A|R;M\right)=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)
and
H_{AQ}^{i}\left(A|R;M\right)=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)
for every
i\geq 0
. These modules enable one to extend the above-mentioned sequences to the following exact sequences:

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}\left(S|R;M\right)\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}\left(T|R;M\right)\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}\left(T|S;M\right)\rightarrow H_{1}^{AQ}\left(S|R;M\right)\rightarrow

\displaystyle H_{1}^{AQ}\left(T|R;M\right)\rightarrow H_{1}^{AQ}\left(T|S;M\right)\rightarrow\Omega_{S|R}\otimes_{S}M\rightarrow\Omega_{T|R}\otimes_{T}M\rightarrow\Omega_{T|S}\otimes_{T}M\rightarrow 0

and

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{S}(T,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{R}(T,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{R}(S,M)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{1}\left(T|S;M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{1}\left(T|R;M\right)\rightarrow

\displaystyle H_{AQ}^{1}\left(S|R;M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}\left(T|S;M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}\left(T|R;M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}\left(S|R;M\right)\rightarrow\cdots

Upon further development of the theory, Quillen constructed two spectral sequences that relate André-Quillen homology and cohomology to Tor and Ext functors. The five-term exact sequences resulting from these spectral sequences are as follows:

\operatorname{Tor}_{3}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{3}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{2}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(A,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A),M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{3}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{3}(A,M)

These sequences are used to characterize regular local rings as well as complete intersection local rings as follows.

.

.

The latter theorem is leveraged by L. L. Avramov to prove the ascent-descent property of complete intersection local rings, and then to solve their long-sought localization problem; see
[

.

Despite their focal role in the theory of André-Quillen (co)homology, Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences are not treated the way they deserve. To shed some light on this, we point out that a sketchy argument with gaps for a special case of the first spectral sequence appears in an unpublished manuscript of Quillen (see
[
Qu1
, Theorem 6.8]
), while the second spectral sequence is only stated without proof in another paper of Quillen (see
[
Qu3
, Theorem 6.8]
). In order to construct the first spectral sequence, Quillen forms a filtration from which the spectral sequence is obtained. Moreover, he proves his Connectivity Lemma to establish its convergence. However, it is not obvious how the Connectivity Lemma implies convergence by looking at the standard theorems of convergence in the literature such as
[
Mc
, Theorem 2.6]
,
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.14]
, and
[
We
, Theorem 5.5.1]
. To address this defect, we prove a general theorem that allows one to use the Connectivity Lemma to settle the convergence of the spectral sequences. Also, a spectral sequence arising from a filtration is generally described by its first page, while Quillen’s spectral sequences are given by their second pages. A delicate reparametrization is required to reconcile the two which is not addressed in
[
Qu1
, Theorem 6.8]
. The proof of the Connectivity Lemma in
[
Qu1
, Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 8.8]
is not well-articulated, so we use the ideas developed in
[
An
, Chapters XI, XII, and XIII]
and assemble them to provide an easier and more readable proof. When we get to the second spectral sequence, there is no proof in the literature. One might be tripped up to think that a straightforward dual argument works, but it is not clear what filtration should be used to form the second spectral sequence. We thus include a complete treatment of the second spectral sequence. After establishing the spectral sequences, we deal with their corresponding five-term exact sequences. However, one needs a generalized version of five-term exact sequences which is only stated in
[
Ro
, Corollaries 10.32 and 10.34]
whose statement is not even correct. We include the correct version and use it effectively. In the determination of the terms of the five-term exact sequences, there is a subtlety that should be noted about the fact that the symmetric power functor does not commute with the normalization functor, so one needs some sort of homotopy preservation result which we tackle. All in all, we present a complete, neat, and readable account of Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences.

2.
Spectral Sequences Arising from Filtrations

It is folklore that filtrations yield spectral sequences; see
[
Mc
, Theorem 2.6]
,
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.14]
, and
[
We
, Theorem 5.5.1]
. In this regard, Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences arise from certain filtrations on chain complexes. However, one needs stronger versions of these theorems in order to establish the convergence of Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. In this section, we prove a theorem on the convergence of spectral sequences arising from filtrations which generalizes the existing theorems in the literature and fits the bill for Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. First we recall the basics of spectral sequences and chain complexes in order to set up the language and notation.

Let
R
be a ring. A
spectral sequence

E
of left
R
-modules consists of the following data: A left
R
-module
E_{p,q}^{r}
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\geq 1
; An
R
-homomorphism
d_{p,q}^{r}:E_{p,q}^{r}\rightarrow E_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\geq 1
such that each composition
E_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\xrightarrow{d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}}E_{p,q}^{r}\xrightarrow{d_{p,q}^{r}}E_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
is zero; and an
R
-isomorphism
E_{p,q}^{r+1}\cong\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{r}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)}
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\geq 1
.

A spectral sequence
E
of left
R
-modules is said to be
first quadrant
if
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
for every
p<0
or
q<0
, and is said to be
third quadrant
if
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
for every
p>0
or
q>0
.

Given a spectral sequence
E
of left
R
-modules, for any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\geq 2
, there is a chain

B_{p,q}^{2}\subseteq B_{p,q}^{3}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq B_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq Z_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq Z_{p,q}^{3}\subseteq Z_{p,q}^{2}

of submodules of
E_{p,q}^{1}
such that
E_{p,q}^{r}\cong\frac{Z_{p,q}^{r}}{B_{p,q}^{r}}
; see
[
We
, 5.2.8]
or
[
Ro
, paragraph after Example 10.12]
. Set
Z_{p,q}^{\infty}=\bigcap_{r=2}^{\infty}Z_{p,q}^{r}
,
B_{p,q}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{r=2}^{\infty}B_{p,q}^{r}
, and
E_{p,q}^{\infty}=\frac{Z_{p,q}^{\infty}}{B_{p,q}^{\infty}}
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. The spectral sequence
E
is said to be
convergent
to a family
H=\{H_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of left
R
-modules if for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{n}
has a finite filtration

0=U^{\alpha}\subseteq U^{\alpha+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U^{\beta}=H_{n}

such that
\frac{U^{p}}{U^{p-1}}\cong E_{p,n-p}^{\infty}
for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. When we speak of spectral sequences, we implicitly mean convergent spectral sequences and write
E_{p,q}^{1}\Rightarrow H_{p+q}
or
E_{p,q}^{2}\Rightarrow H_{p+q}
accordingly.

An
R
-
complex

X
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed sequence

X:\cdots\rightarrow X_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{i+1}^{X}}X_{i}\xrightarrow{\partial_{i}^{X}}X_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots

of left
R
-modules and
R
-homomorphisms such that
\partial_{i}^{X}\partial_{i+1}^{X}=0
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
i
th
homology
of
X
is the left
R
-module
H_{i}(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{X}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{X}\right)}
.

Given an
R
-complex
X
, a
filtration
of
X
is a sequence
\{F^{p}\}_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
of subcomplexes of
X
such that
F^{p-1}
is a subcomplex of
F^{p}
for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Given a filtration

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X

of
X
, there is an induced filtration

\cdots\subseteq F_{n}^{p-1}\subseteq F_{n}^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{n}

of
X_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, if for any
p,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set
U_{n}^{p}=\operatorname{Im}\left(H_{n}(\iota^{p})\right)
where
\iota^{p}:F^{p}\rightarrow X
is the inclusion morphism, then we get an induced filtration

\cdots\subseteq U_{n}^{p-1}\subseteq U_{n}^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq H_{n}(X)

of
H_{n}(X)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
; see
[
Ro
, paragraph after Lemma 10.13]
.

We need a general theorem on the convergence of the spectral sequence arising from a filtration that is strong enough to derive Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. We first need a lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a ring,
M
a left
R
-module, and
N
,
K
, and
L
three submodules of
M
. If
L\subseteq K
, then we have:

\textstyle\frac{N+K}{N+L}\cong\frac{K}{(K\cap N)+L}

Proof.

Since
L\subseteq K
, we have
K=K+L
, so we get
\textstyle\frac{N+K}{N+L}=\frac{N+K+L}{N+L}\cong\frac{K}{K\cap(N+L)}
. On the other hand, since
K\supseteq L
, we have
K\cap(N+L)=(K\cap N)+(K\cap L)=(K\cap N)+L
. As a result, we get
\textstyle\frac{N+K}{N+L}\cong\frac{K}{K\cap(N+L)}=\frac{K}{(K\cap N)+L}
.
∎

The following theorem and its corollaries generalize
[
Mc
, Theorem 2.6]
,
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.14]
, and
[
We
, Theorem 5.5.1]
. The author uses some ideas from
[
St
, Sections 12.23 and 12.24 on Spectral Sequences from Filtered Complexes]
in the sequel.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
R
be a ring, and
X
an
R
-complex equipped with a filtration

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X

such that the induced filtration of
H_{n}(X)
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
r\geq 0
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
I_{p,q}^{r}:=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
and
J_{p,q}^{r}:=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
. If

\left(\textstyle\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}=\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}

and

\left(\textstyle\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}=\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}

for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a spectral sequence as follows:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{1}=H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(X)

Proof.

First, we construct the spectral sequence. Let
r\geq 0
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\subseteq F_{p+q-1}^{p-r+1}
, it is clear that
I_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq I_{p,q}^{r-1}
. Similarly, as
F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\subseteq F_{p+q+1}^{p+r}
, we see that
J_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq J_{p,q}^{r+1}
. As a consequence, we have:

J_{p,q}^{0}\subseteq J_{p,q}^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{p,q}^{1}\subseteq I_{p,q}^{0}=F_{p+q}^{p}

Let
r\geq 1
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. We show that
J_{p,q}^{r}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}\left(I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)
. To see this, let
y\in J_{p,q}^{r}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
. Then there is an element
x\in F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}
such that
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)
. But then
\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)=y\in F_{p+q}^{p}
, so
x\in\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q}^{p}\right)
. Hence
x\in\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q}^{p}\right)\cap F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}=I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}
. As a result,
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}(x)\in\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}\left(I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)
. Conversely, let
y\in\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}\left(I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)
. Then there is an element
x\in I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}
such that
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}(x)
. But
x\in I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}=\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q}^{p}\right)\cap F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}
, so
x\in F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}
and
\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)\in F_{p+q}^{p}
. As a result,
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}(x)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)\in\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}=J_{p,q}^{r}
. Therefore,
J_{p,q}^{r}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r-1}}\left(I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{r}:=\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}

Furthermore, define a map
\phi_{p,q}^{r}:I_{p,q}^{r}\rightarrow\frac{I_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}}{J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}}
by setting
\phi_{p,q}^{r}(x)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)+J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}
for every
x\in I_{p,q}^{r}
. For well-definedness, we note that if
x\in I_{p,q}^{r}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
, then
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\in F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}
, so since
\partial_{p+q-1}^{X}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\right)=0\in F_{p+q-2}^{p-2r}
, we get
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\in\left(\partial_{p+q-1}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-2}^{p-2r}\right)\cap F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}=I_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
. Hence
\phi_{p,q}^{r}
is well-defined. Moreover, it is clear that
\phi_{p,q}^{r}
is an
R
-homomorphism. If
x\in J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
, then we can write
x=u+v
for some
u\in J_{p,q}^{r}
and
v\in I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
. Since
u\in J_{p,q}^{r}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
, we have
u=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(w)
for some
w\in F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}
, so
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(u)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(w)\right)=0
. As
v\in I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}
, we have
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(v)=\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p-1}}(v)\in\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p-1}}\left(I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\right)=J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
. Consequently,
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(u)+\partial_{p+q}^{X}(v)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(v)\in J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
, so
\phi_{p,q}^{r}(x)=0
. This shows that
J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\left(\phi_{p,q}^{r}\right)
, so
\phi_{p,q}^{r}
induces an
R
-homomorphism

\textstyle d_{p,q}^{r}:=\overline{\phi_{p,q}^{r}}:\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}\rightarrow\frac{I_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}}{J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}}

given by

d_{p,q}^{r}\left(x+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\right)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)+J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}

for every
x\in I_{p,q}^{r}
. Since
\partial_{p+q}^{X}\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}=0
, we see that
d_{p,q}^{r}d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}=0
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Considering
d_{p,q}^{r}:\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}\rightarrow\frac{I_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}}{J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}}
, we show that:

\textstyle\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{r}\right)=\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}

To see this, let
x+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{r}\right)
. Then
x\in I_{p,q}^{r}
with
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\in J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}
, so we can write
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)=u+v
for some
u\in J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}
and
v\in I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}
. As
u\in J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}=\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p-1}}\left(I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\right)
, we have
u=\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p-1}}(y)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(y)
for some
y\in I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
. Now
x\in I_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq F_{p+q}^{p}
and
y\in I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\subseteq F_{p+q}^{p-1}\subseteq F_{p+q}^{p}
, so
x-y\in F_{p+q}^{p}
. Also,
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x-y)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)-u=v\in I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}\subseteq F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}
, so
x-y\in\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}\right)
. Therefore,
x-y\in\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}=I_{p,q}^{r+1}
, so
x-y=z
for some
z\in I_{p,q}^{r+1}
. Thus
x=z+y\in I_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
, so
x+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\in\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}
. Conversely, let
x\in I_{p,q}^{r+1}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}
. Then
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\in F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}
, so since
\partial_{p+q-1}^{X}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\right)=0\in F_{p+q-2}^{p-2r}
, we get
\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)\in\left(\partial_{p+q-1}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-2}^{p-2r}\right)\cap F_{p+q-1}^{p-r-1}=I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}
. As a result,
d_{p,q}^{r}\left(x+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\right)=\partial_{p+q}^{X}(x)+J_{p-r,q+r-1}^{r}+I_{p-r-1,q+r}^{r-1}=0
, so
x+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{r}\right)
. Therefore, the claim is proved.

Similarly, considering
d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}:\frac{I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}}{J_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}+I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}}\rightarrow\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}
, we show that:

\textstyle\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)=\frac{J_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}

To see this, let
y\in I_{p,q}^{r}
be such that
y+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\in\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)
. Then there is an element
x\in I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\subseteq F_{p+q+1}^{p+r}
such that:

y+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}=d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\left(x+J_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}+I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}

That is,
y-\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)\in J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
, so we can write
y-\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)=u+v
for some
u\in J_{p,q}^{r}\subseteq J_{p,q}^{r+1}
and
v\in I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
. But
x\in I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}
, so
\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r}}(x)\in\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r}}\left(I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)=J_{p,q}^{r+1}
. As a result,
\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)+u\in J_{p,q}^{r+1}
, so
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)+u+v\in J_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}
. Conversely, let
y\in J_{p,q}^{r+1}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r}}\left(I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)
. Then
y=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p+r}}(x)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)
for some
x\in I_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}
. Thus we have:

y+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}(x)+J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}=d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\left(x+J_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}+I_{p+r-1,q-r+2}^{r-1}\right)

Therefore, the claim is proved.

We now note that since
F_{p+q}^{p-1}\subseteq F_{p+q}^{p}
, we have:

I_{p,q}^{r}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}

Using this and the fact that
I_{p,q}^{r+1}\subseteq I_{p,q}^{r}
, we see that:

I_{p,q}^{r+1}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}=I_{p,q}^{r+1}\cap I_{p,q}^{r}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=I_{p,q}^{r+1}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=I_{p-1,q+1}^{r}

Thus in view of the fact that
J_{p,q}^{r+1}\subseteq I_{p,q}^{r+1}
, we can use Lemma
2.1
to get the following isomorphisms:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{r+1}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}}{J_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r}}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}}{J_{p,q}^{r+1}+\left(I_{p,q}^{r+1}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}\right)}\cong\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}\cong\frac{\frac{I_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}}{\frac{J_{p,q}^{r+1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{r-1}}}=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{r}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+r,q-r+1}^{r}\right)}

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. We note that
I_{p,q}^{1}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p}}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-1}\right)
and
J_{p,q}^{1}=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p}}\left(I_{p,q+1}^{0}\right)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p}}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p}\right)
. Consider the following
R
-homomorphisms:

\textstyle\frac{F_{p+q+1}^{p}}{F_{p+q+1}^{p-1}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{p+q+1}^{\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}}}\frac{F_{p+q}^{p}}{F_{p+q}^{p-1}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{p+q}^{\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}}}\frac{F_{p+q-1}^{p}}{F_{p+q-1}^{p-1}}

Then using Lemma
2.1
, we see that:

\textstyle H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}}\right)}=\frac{\frac{\left(\partial_{p+q}^{F^{p}}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-1}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{F_{p+q}^{p-1}}}{\frac{\partial_{p+q+1}^{F^{p}}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{F_{p+q}^{p-1}}}\cong\frac{I_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}\cong\frac{I_{p,q}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+\left(I_{p,q}^{1}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}\right)}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}=E_{p,q}^{1}

Therefore, we obtain a spectral sequence with
E_{p,q}^{1}\cong H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)
for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Next, we establish the convergence of the spectral sequence. Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
. By definition, we have
Z_{p,q}^{2}=\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{1}\right)=\frac{I_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}
and
B_{p,q}^{2}=\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+1,q}^{1}\right)=\frac{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}
as submodules of
E_{p,q}^{1}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}
. As we observed above, we have:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{2}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{2}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{2}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+\left(I_{p,q}^{2}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)}\cong\frac{I_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}\cong\frac{\frac{I_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}{\frac{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{1}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+1,q}^{1}\right)}=\frac{Z_{p,q}^{2}}{B_{p,q}^{2}}

We note that since
I_{p,q}^{3}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=I_{p-1,q+1}^{2}\subseteq I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\subseteq I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}=F_{p+q}^{p-1}
, we have:

\begin{split}\left(I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\right)\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}&=\left(I_{p,q}^{3}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)+\left(I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)=\left(I_{p,q}^{3}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}\right)+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\\
&=I_{p-1,q+1}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}=I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\end{split}

Now the submodule
\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{2}\right)
of
E_{p,q}^{2}
is mapped under the above isomorphism as follows:

\begin{split}\operatorname{Ker}\left(d_{p,q}^{2}\right)&=\textstyle\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}=\textstyle\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+\left(\left(I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\right)\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)}\cong\textstyle\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}\cong\textstyle\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}\\
&\cong\textstyle\frac{\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}{\frac{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}=\textstyle\frac{Z_{p,q}^{3}}{B_{p,q}^{2}}\end{split}

Hence
Z_{p,q}^{3}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}
. Similarly, since
J_{p,q}^{3}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}\subseteq I_{p,q}^{2}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}=I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\subseteq I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}=F_{p+q}^{p-1}
, we have:

\begin{split}\left(J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\right)\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}&=\left(J_{p,q}^{3}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)+\left(I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)=\left(J_{p,q}^{3}\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}\right)+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\\
&=I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\end{split}

As a result, the submodule
\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+2,q-1}^{2}\right)
of
E_{p,q}^{2}
is mapped under the above isomorphism as follows:

\begin{split}\textstyle\operatorname{Im}\left(d_{p+2,q-1}^{2}\right)&=\textstyle\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}=\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+\left(\left(J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}\right)\cap I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}\right)}\cong\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}\cong\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}\\
&\textstyle\cong\frac{\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}{\frac{J_{p,q}^{2}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}}=\frac{B_{p,q}^{3}}{B_{p,q}^{2}}\end{split}

Hence
B_{p,q}^{3}=\frac{J_{p,q}^{3}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}
. Continuing in this way, we see that:

Z_{p,q}^{r}=\textstyle\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}=\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad B_{p,q}^{r}=\textstyle\frac{J_{p,q}^{r}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+I_{p-1,q+1}^{0}}=\frac{J_{p,q}^{r}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}

On the other hand, using the hypothesis, we have:

\textstyle Z_{p,q}^{\infty}=\bigcap_{r=2}^{\infty}Z_{p,q}^{r}=\bigcap_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{I_{p,q}^{r}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}=\frac{\left(\bigcap_{r=2}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}=\frac{\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}

and

\textstyle B_{p,q}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{r=2}^{\infty}B_{p,q}^{r}=\bigcup_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{J_{p,q}^{r}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}=\frac{\left(\bigcup_{r=2}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}=\frac{\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{J_{p,q}^{1}+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}

Therefore, using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{\infty}=\frac{Z_{p,q}^{\infty}}{B_{p,q}^{\infty}}\cong\frac{\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}{\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}}\cong\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}}{\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p-1}\right)+\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)}

Let
p,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\iota^{p}:F^{p}\rightarrow X
be the inclusion morphism, and consider the induced
R
-homomorphism
H_{n}(\iota^{p}):H_{n}(F^{p})\rightarrow H_{n}(X)
. Then it is clear that:

\textstyle U_{n}^{p}:=\operatorname{Im}\left(H_{n}(\iota^{p})\right)=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p}}\right)+\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)}{\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)}

By the hypothesis, there is a finite filtration
0=U_{n}^{\alpha}\subseteq U_{n}^{\alpha+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{n}^{\beta}=H_{n}(X)
of
H_{n}(X)
where
\alpha
and
\beta
depend on
n
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p-1}}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p}}\right)
, so using Lemma
2.1
, we have:

\begin{split}\textstyle\frac{U_{n}^{p}}{U_{n}^{p-1}}&\cong\textstyle\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p}}\right)+\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)}{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p-1}}\right)+\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)}\cong\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p}}\right)}{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p-1}}\right)+\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)\cap\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p}}\right)\right)}\textstyle\cong\frac{\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}}{\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p-1}\right)+\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}\right)}\cong E_{p,n-p}^{\infty}\end{split}

This means that the spectral sequence is convergent to
H_{n}(X)
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
R
be a ring,
X
an
R
-complex equipped with a filtration

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X,

and
\iota^{p}:F^{p}\rightarrow X
the inclusion morphism for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. If for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are integers
\alpha(n)
and
\beta(n)
such that
H_{n}(F^{p})=0
for every
p\leq\alpha(n)
, and
H_{n}(\iota^{p}):H_{n}(F^{p})\rightarrow H_{n}(X)
is an isomorphism for every
p\geq\beta(n)
, then there exists a spectral sequence as follows:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{1}=H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(X)

Proof.

Set
U_{n}^{p}=\operatorname{Im}\left(H_{n}(\iota^{p})\right)
for every
p,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The hypothesis implies that
U_{n}^{p}=0
for every
p\leq\alpha(n)
, and
U_{n}^{p}=H_{n}(X)
for every
p\geq\beta(n)
. Thus there is a finite filtration of
H_{n}(X)
as follows:

0=U_{n}^{\alpha(n)}\subseteq U_{n}^{\alpha(n)+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{n}^{\beta(n)}=H_{n}(X)

For any
r\geq 0
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
I_{p,q}^{r}
and
J_{p,q}^{r}
as in Theorem
2.2
. It is clear that
\left(\textstyle\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}\supseteq\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}
and
\left(\textstyle\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}\subseteq\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)+F_{p+q}^{p-1}
. We establish the reverse inequalities. Fix
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p+q=n
.

Let
x\in\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}
. Then
x\in F_{n}^{p}
and
\partial_{n}^{X}(x)\in F_{n-1}^{p-r}
for every
r\geq 0
. There is an
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
p-r_{0}\leq\alpha(n-1)
. Then the hypothesis implies that
H_{n-1}\left(F^{p-r_{0}}\right)=0
, so
\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n-1}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}\right)=\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}\right)
. Consider the following
R
-homomorphisms:

F_{n}^{p-r_{0}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{n}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}}F_{n-1}^{p-r_{0}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{n-1}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}}F_{n-2}^{p-r_{0}}

We have
\partial_{n}^{X}(x)\in F_{n-1}^{p-r_{0}}
and
\partial_{n-1}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}(x)\right)=\partial_{n-1}^{X}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}(x)\right)=0
, so
\partial_{n}^{X}(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n-1}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}\right)=\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}\right)
. Thus there is an element
y\in F_{n}^{p-r_{0}}
such that
\partial_{n}^{X}(x)=\partial_{n}^{F^{p-r_{0}}}(y)=\partial_{n}^{X}(y)
. But then
x-y\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)
, so
x-y=z
for some
z\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)
. Now,
x\in F_{n}^{p}
and
y\in F_{n}^{p-r_{0}}\subseteq F_{n}^{p}
, so
z=x-y\in F_{n}^{p}
, whence
z\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}
. On the other hand,
y\in F_{n}^{p-r_{0}}\subseteq F_{n}^{p-1}
. Consequently,
x=z+y\in\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}\right)+F_{n}^{p-1}
. It follows that
\left(\textstyle\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{n}^{p-1}=\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}\right)+F_{n}^{p-1}
.

Let
y\in\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}
. Then
y\in F_{n}^{p}
and
y=\partial_{n+1}^{X}(x)
for some
x\in X_{n+1}
. There is an
r_{0}\geq 1
such that
p+r_{0}-1\geq\beta(n)
. Then the hypothesis implies that
H_{n}\left(\iota^{p+r_{0}-1}\right):H_{n}\left(F^{p+r_{0}-1}\right)\rightarrow H_{n}(X)
is an isomorphism. We have
y\in F_{n}^{p}\subseteq F_{n}^{p+r_{0}-1}
and
\partial_{n}^{F^{p+r_{0}-1}}(y)=\partial_{n}^{X}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}(x)\right)=0
, so
y\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{n}^{F^{p+r_{0}-1}}\right)
. But then
H_{n}\left(\iota^{p+r_{0}-1}\right)\left(y+\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{F^{p+r_{0}-1}}\right)\right)=y+\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)=0
as
y\in\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)
. Thus
y\in\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{F^{p+r_{0}-1}}\right)
, so
y\in\partial_{n+1}^{X}\left(F_{n+1}^{p+r_{0}-1}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}=J_{p,q}^{r_{0}}\subseteq\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}
. It follows that
\left(\textstyle\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}\right)+F_{n}^{p-1}=\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{n+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{n}^{p}\right)+F_{n}^{p-1}
.

Therefore, the result follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
R
be a ring, and
X
an
R
-complex equipped with a filtration

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X

such that the induced filtration of
H_{n}(X)
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}^{p}=0
and
\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}^{p}=X_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a spectral sequence as follows:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{1}=H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(X)

Proof.

For any
r\geq 0
and
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
I_{p,q}^{r}
and
J_{p,q}^{r}
as in Theorem
2.2
. Using the hypothesis, we have:

\textstyle\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}I_{p,q}^{r}=\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}\left(\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)=\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{r=0}^{\infty}F_{p+q-1}^{p-r}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}=\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{p+q}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}

and

\textstyle\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}J_{p,q}^{r}=\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}\right)=\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\left(\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}F_{p+q+1}^{p+r-1}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}=\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{p+q+1}^{X}\right)\cap F_{p+q}^{p}

Therefore, the result follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
R
be a ring, and
X
an
R
-complex equipped with a filtration

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X

such that the induced filtration of
X_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exists a spectral sequence as follows:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{1}=H_{p+q}\left(\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(X)

Proof.

For any
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\iota^{p}:F^{p}\rightarrow X
be the inclusion morphism, and set
U_{n}^{p}=\operatorname{Im}\left(H_{n}(\iota^{p})\right)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By the hypothesis,
X_{n}
has a finite filtration as follows:

0=F_{n}^{\alpha(n)}\subseteq F_{n}^{\alpha(n)+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{n}^{\beta(n)}=X_{n}

If
p<\alpha(n)
, then
F_{n}^{p}=0
, so
H_{n}(F^{p})=0
, whence
U_{n}^{p}=0
. Let
\gamma(n)=\max\left\{\beta(n-1),\beta(n),\beta(n+1)\right\}
. If
p\geq\gamma(n)
, then
F_{n-1}^{p}=X_{n-1}
,
F_{n}^{p}=X_{n}
, and
F_{n+1}^{p}=X_{n+1}
, so
H_{n}(F^{p})=H_{n}(X)
, whence
U_{n}^{p}=H_{n}(X)
. This implies that there is a finite filtration of
H_{n}(X)
as follows:

0=U_{n}^{\alpha(n)}\subseteq U_{n}^{\alpha(n)+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{n}^{\gamma(n)}=H_{n}(X)

On the other hand, it is clear that
\bigcap_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}^{p}=0
and
\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}^{p}=X_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, the result follows from Corollary
2.4
.
∎

3.
Connectivity Lemma

The key point to prove the convergence of Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences is a deep result of Quillen known as the
“Connectivity Lemma”
which allows one to deploy Corollary
2.3
. This result appears in
[
Qu3
, Theorem 6.12]
without proof. Also, it is discussed in Quillen’s unpublished notes
[
Qu1
, Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 8.8]
, where the argument is confusing and contains gaps to be filled. On the other hand, it is presented in
[
An
, Chapter XIII, Corollaire 4]
whose statement is a special case of Quillen’s original version. In this section, we provide a proof of Quillen’s original version using the ideas developed in
[
An
, Chapters XI, XII, and XIII]
. Our argument is complete, clear, and contains some new elements different from the references mentioned.

We first recall the notions of simplicial objects, their homotopy groups, simplicial resolutions, and the bar construction in the following remark.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A simplicial object over
\mathcal{C}
is a family
A=\{A_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
together with face morphisms
d_{n,i}^{A}:A_{n}\rightarrow A_{n-1}
for every
n\geq 1
and
0\leq i\leq n
, and degeneracy morphisms
s_{n,i}^{A}:A_{n}\rightarrow A_{n+1}
for every
n\geq 0
and
0\leq i\leq n
, that satisfy the following relations for every
n\geq 0
and
0\leq i,j\leq n
:

(i)

d_{n,i}^{A}d_{n+1,j}^{A}=d_{n,j-1}^{A}d_{n+1,i}^{A}
if
i<j
.

(ii)

s_{n,i}^{A}s_{n-1,j}^{A}=s_{n,j+1}^{A}s_{n-1,i}^{A}
if
i\leq j
.

(iii)

d_{n,i}^{A}s_{n-1,j}^{A}=\begin{cases}s_{n-2,j-1}^{A}d_{n-1,i}^{A}&\text{if }i<j\\
1^{A_{n-1}}&\text{if }i=j\text{ or }j+1\\
s_{n-2,j}^{A}d_{n-1,i-1}^{A}&\text{if }i>j+1\end{cases}

Given two simplicial objects
A=\{A_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
B=\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
over
\mathcal{C}
, a morphism
\tau:A\rightarrow B
of simplicial objects is a collection
\tau=(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of morphisms
\tau_{n}:A_{n}\rightarrow B_{n}
of
\mathcal{C}
for every
n\geq 0
, such that the following diagram is commutative for every
n\geq 1
and
0\leq i\leq n
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{n-1,i}^{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{n,i}^{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{n-1,i}^{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{n,i}^{B}}

It is immediate that we have a category
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}
of simplicial objects over
\mathcal{C}
. As a matter of fact,
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}
is a functor category. Let
\Delta
denote the simplex category described by

\operatorname{Obj}(\Delta)=\left\{[n]=\{0,1,2,...,n\}\;|\;n\geq 0\right\}

and

\operatorname{Mor}_{\Delta}\left([n],[m]\right)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{et}}\left([n],[m]\right)\;|\;f\textrm{ is order-preserving}\right\}

for every
[n],[m]\in\operatorname{Obj}(\Delta)
. Then it is folklore that
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
. In other words, a simplicial object over
\mathcal{C}
is nothing but a contravariant functor
\Delta\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. In practice, we speak of simplicial sets, simplicial modules, or simplicial commutative algebras when we consider simplicial objects over the categories of sets, modules, or commutative algebras.

Given a ring
R
, one has the normalization functor
\operatorname{Nor}:\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(R)
and the Dold-Kan functor
\operatorname{DK}:\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(R)\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
; see
[
We
, Definition 8.3.6, and 8.4.4]
or
[
Fa
, Definitions 4.5 and 4.9]
. The Dold-Kan Correspondence Theorem asserts that there is an adjoint equivalence of categories
(\operatorname{DK},\operatorname{Nor}):\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(R)\leftrightarrows\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
; see
[
We
, Theorem 8.4.1 and Exercise 8.4.2]
.

Given a simplicial set that satisfies the so-called Kan condition, one can define its homotopy groups; see
[
We
, Chapter 8, Section 8.3]
. It is folklore that simplicial modules and simplicial commutative algebras satisfy the Kan condition, so one can define their homotopy groups. In particular, if
R
is a commutative ring and
M
is a simplicial
R
-module, then for any
n\geq 0
, the
n
th homotopy group of
M
is given by
\pi_{n}(M)=H_{n}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\right)\cong H_{n}\left(C_{R}(M)\right)
where
C_{R}(M)
is the Moore complex associated with
M
. The same description applies to the homotopy groups of a simplicial commutative
R
-algebra; see
[
We
, Definition 8.3.6 and Lemma 8.3.7]
and
[
Fa
, Remark 4.6]
. We note that it follows from the Dold-Kan Correspondence Theorem that the normalization functor is exact, so if

0\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence of simplicial
R
-modules, then we get the following natural exact sequence of homotopy groups:

\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{i}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\pi_{i}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{i}(M^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow\pi_{i-1}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\pi_{i-1}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{i-1}(M^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow

\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{0}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{0}(M^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow 0

We further note that there is an adjoint pair
(\pi_{0},\operatorname{Diag}):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)\leftrightarrows\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
of functors in which
\operatorname{Diag}:\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
is the diagonal functor that assigns to each left
R
-module
M
, the constant simplicial left
R
-module
M
, and to each
R
-homomorphism
f:M\rightarrow N
, the morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
of constant simplicial left
R
-modules.

Given a commutative ring
R
, a deep result of Quillen states that the category of simplicial commutative
R
-algebras is a model category; see
[
Qu2
, Chapter II, Section 3]
, also see
[
Fa
]
for a purely algebraic proof. Accordingly, given a commutative
R
-algebra
A
, there exists a cofibrant replacement
A_{\textrm{c}}\rightarrow A
for
A
in the category of simplicial commutative
R
-algebras, which is referred to as a
“simplicial algebra resolution”
of
A
. In particular, there is an
R
-algebra epimorphism
\varepsilon:(A_{\textrm{c}})_{0}\rightarrow A
with
\varepsilon d_{1,0}^{A_{\textrm{c}}}=\varepsilon d_{1,1}^{A_{\textrm{c}}}
that induces

\pi_{n}(A_{\textrm{c}})=\begin{cases}A&\textrm{if }n=0\\
0&\textrm{if }n>0.\end{cases}

More explicitly, there exists a concrete construction of a simplicial algebra resolution
R\langle X\rangle\rightarrow A
for
A
in which
R\langle X\rangle
is a polynomial algebra in each degree; see
[
Iy
, Section 4]
. We make extensive use of such a resolution in the sequel.

Given a commutative ring
R
, an
R
-algebra
A
, a right
A
-module
M
, and a left
A
-module
N
, the bar construction
\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)
is a simplicial
R
-module such that for any
n\geq 0
, we have

\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n}=M\otimes_{R}T^{n}_{R}(A)\otimes_{R}N

where
T^{n}_{R}(A)
is the
n
-fold tensor product of
A
, and for any
0\leq i\leq n
, the face morphism
d_{n,i}^{\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)}:\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n-1}
is given by

d_{n,i}^{\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})=a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}

and the degeneracy morphism
s_{n,i}^{\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)}:\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n+1}
is given by

s_{n,i}^{\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})=a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}

for every
a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}\in\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)_{n}
. If
A
is a flat
R
-algebra, and either
M
or
N
is a flat
R
-module, then one has
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(M,N)\cong\pi_{i}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{R}(M,A,N)\right)
for every
i\geq 0
; see
[
We
, 8.6.12 and Example 8.7.2]
for a more general version.

We also need the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R
be a ring,
\{a_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Phi}
a family of elements of
R
such that any finitely many distinct elements of it form a regular sequence on
R
in some order, and
\mathfrak{a}=\langle a_{\alpha}\;|\;\alpha\in\Phi\rangle
. Then
\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}
is a free
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}
-module with a basis
\mathfrak{B}=\{a_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{s}}+\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}\;|\;\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{s}\in\Phi\}
for every
s\geq 1
. As a result, there is a natural isomorphism
\operatorname{Sym}_{\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}}^{s}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\right)\cong\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}
for every
s\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
s\geq 1
. It is clear that
\mathfrak{a}^{s}=\langle a_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{s}}\;|\;\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{s}\in\Phi\rangle
, so
\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}
is generated by
\mathfrak{B}
as an
R
-module, hence as an
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}
-module. On the other hand, if
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(r_{j}+\mathfrak{a}\right)\left(a_{\alpha_{1j}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{sj}}+\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}\right)=0
for some
r_{1},...,r_{m}\in R
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}a_{\alpha_{1j}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{sj}}\in\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}
. Let
a_{1},...,a_{n}
be the distinct elements that appear among
a_{\alpha_{ij}}
’s for every
1\leq i\leq s
and
1\leq j\leq m
, and also among the generators of
\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}
in the expression of
\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}a_{\alpha_{1j}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{sj}}
. Allowing some coefficients and exponents to be zero if necessary, we can rewrite the latter inclusion as
\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}=s}r_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}a_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{i_{n}}\in\langle a_{1},...,a_{n}\rangle^{s+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}
where every
r_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}\in R
. Assuming
a_{1},...,a_{n}
are in the right order to form a regular sequence on
R
, we deduce from
[
BH
, Theorem 1.1.7]
that every
r_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}\in\langle a_{1},...,a_{n}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. But it is obvious that
r_{1},...,r_{m}
are among
r_{i_{1},...,i_{n}}
’s, so
r_{1},...,r_{m}\in\mathfrak{a}
. That is,
\mathfrak{B}
is linearly independent.

For the next part, we note that by using the universal property of symmetric powers, we can define an
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}
-epimorphism
\zeta:\operatorname{Sym}_{\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}}^{s}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\right)\rightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}
by setting
\zeta\left(\left(a_{1}+\mathfrak{a}^{2}\right)\ast\cdots\ast\left(a_{s}+\mathfrak{a}^{2}\right)\right)=a_{1}\cdots a_{s}+\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}
for every
a_{1},...,a_{s}\in\mathfrak{a}
. On the other hand, since
\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}
is a free
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}
-module, we can define an
\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}
-homomorphism
\xi:\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{s}}{\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}}^{s}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\right)
by setting
\xi\left(a_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{\alpha_{s}}+\mathfrak{a}^{s+1}\right)=\left(a_{\alpha_{1}}+\mathfrak{a}^{2}\right)\ast\cdots\ast\left(a_{\alpha_{s}}+\mathfrak{a}^{2}\right)
for every
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{s}\in\Phi
. By checking on the basis elements, we observe that
\xi\zeta=1^{\operatorname{Sym}_{\frac{R}{\mathfrak{a}}}^{s}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{a}^{2}}\right)}
, so
\zeta
is injective, hence an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a ring,
M
a simplicial right
R
-module, and
N
a simplicial left
R
-module. Then the following natural isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules holds for every
i\geq 0
:

\pi_{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)

Proof.

In light of
[
Fa
, Corollaries 5.7 and 5.10]
, we have following natural isomorphisms for every
i\geq 0
:

\pi_{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\right)\cong H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\boxtimes_{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)\cong H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
R
be a ring,
M
a simplicial right
R
-module, and
N
a simplicial left
R
-module. Suppose that either
M_{i}
is a flat right
R
-module for every
i\geq 0
, or
N_{i}
is a flat left
R
-module for every
i\geq 0
. Then the following natural isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules holds:

\pi_{0}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\pi_{0}(M)\otimes_{R}\pi_{0}(N)

Proof.

Suppose that
M_{i}
is a flat right
R
-module for every
i\geq 0
. For any
i\geq 0
,
\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)_{i}
is a direct summand of
M_{i}
by
[
We
, Lemma 8.3.7]
, so we conclude that
\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)_{i}
is a flat right
R
-module. As a result, by
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.90]
, there is a first quadrant spectral sequence as follows:

E_{p,q}^{2}=\bigoplus_{i+j=q}\operatorname{Tor}_{p}^{R}\left(H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\right),H_{j}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)

As a result, we have the low-degree isomorphism
E_{0,0}^{2}\cong H_{0}
. However, since
H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\right)=0
for every
i<0
and
H_{j}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)=0
for every
j<0
, we have:

E_{0,0}^{2}=\bigoplus_{i+j=0}\operatorname{Tor}_{0}^{R}\left(H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\right),H_{j}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\right)\otimes_{R}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)=\pi_{0}(M)\otimes_{R}\pi_{0}(N)

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.3
, we have
H_{0}=H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(M)\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(N)\right)\cong\pi_{0}(M\otimes_{R}N)
. Therefore, the result follows. The other case is similarly established.
∎

The following corollary generalizes
[
An
, Chapter VIII, Lemme 18]
by replacing
“projectivity”
with
“flatness”
. The argument here is different as well.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
R
be a ring,
M
a simplicial right
R
-module,
N
a simplicial left
R
-module, and
m,n\geq 0
. Suppose that either
M_{i}
is a flat right
R
-module for every
i\geq 0
, or
N_{i}
is a flat left
R
-module for every
i\geq 0
. If
\pi_{i}(M)=0
for every

and
\pi_{i}(N)=0
for every

, then
\pi_{i}(M\otimes_{R}N)=0
for every

.

Proof.

Suppose that

is a flat right

. As in the proof of Corollary

, there is a first quadrant spectral sequence as follows:

If

, then in view of

or

for every

. The other case is similarly established.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}{\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s}}}

Secondly, we show that
\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(A,\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)\right)=0

\mathfrak{H}_{n}

A

\pi_{0}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{j-1}\right)\cong\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{H})\otimes_{A}\pi_{0}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{j-1}\right)=0

, the natural

Since this short exact sequence is natural, we obtain the following short exact sequence of simplicial
A

:

. For any

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{j+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{j}\rightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{H}^{j}}{\mathfrak{H}^{j+1}}\rightarrow 0

\textstyle 0=\pi_{0}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{j}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{0}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{H}^{j}}{\mathfrak{H}^{j+1}}\right)\rightarrow 0,

. We note that by Remark
3.1
, the functor

\textstyle\pi_{0}\left(\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)\cong\pi_{0}\left(\bigoplus_{j=0}^{s-1}\frac{\mathfrak{H}^{j}}{\mathfrak{H}^{j+1}}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{s-1}\pi_{0}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{H}^{j}}{\mathfrak{H}^{j+1}}\right)=0

We observe that

\textstyle\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(A,\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}(A,A)\otimes_{A}\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}

\textstyle\pi_{0}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(A,\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)\right)\cong\pi_{0}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}(A,A)\otimes_{A}\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)\cong\pi_{0}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}(A,A)\right)\otimes_{A}\pi_{0}\left(\frac{A\langle X\rangle}{\mathfrak{H}^{s}}\right)=0

, and the result holds for less than

. As

-modules. By the base case, we have

\textstyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\left(A,\frac{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}{\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s}}\right)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\left(A,\kappa_{s}\right)}\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\left(A,\frac{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}{\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}}\right)

i\geq 0
:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow 0

Using what was proved in the previous paragraph, we get the following exact sequence for every
i\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq s-2
:

\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)=0

We have for every
i\geq 0
:

C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i}=\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)_{i}=\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}T^{i}_{A}\left(A\langle X\rangle\right)\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}

Since
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
is a projective, hence flat
A
-module and
\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{H})=0
, by Corollary
3.4
, we have
\pi_{0}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}T_{A}^{i}\left(A\langle X\rangle\right)\right)\cong\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{H})\otimes_{A}\pi_{0}\left(T_{A}^{i}\left(A\langle X\rangle)\right)\right)=0
, i.e.
\pi_{j}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}T_{A}^{i}\left(A\langle X\rangle\right)\right)=0
for every
j\leq 0
and
i\geq 0
. On the other hand,
\pi_{j}(\mathfrak{H}^{s-1})=0
for every
j\leq s-2
by the induction hypothesis. Since
\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s}
is a projective, hence flat
A
-module, it follows from Corollary
3.5
that

\pi_{j}\left(C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i}\right)=\pi_{j}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}T^{i}_{A}\left(A\langle X\rangle\right)\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)=0

for every
j\leq 0+s-2+1=s-1
and
i\geq 0
. Now consider the following short exact sequence of simplicial
A
-modules for every
i\geq 0
:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i}\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow 0

We get the following exact sequence for every
i\geq 0
and
k\leq s-2
:

0=\pi_{k+1}\left(C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k+1}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)

\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)

So far, we have obtained the following two exact sequences for every
i\geq 1
and
k\leq s-2
:

\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\pi_{k+1}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)

These sequences conspire to show that if
i\geq 1
and
k\leq s-2
, then the following implication holds:

\pi_{k}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)=0\Longrightarrow\pi_{k+1}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)=0

If
i\geq 0
, then
\pi_{0}\left(C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i+1}\right)=0
, so the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i+1}\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow 0

gives the following exact sequence:

0=\pi_{0}\left(C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{i+1}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{0}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)\rightarrow 0

Thus
\pi_{0}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{i+1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)=0
for every
i\geq 0
. Using the aforementioned implication repeatedly, we end up with

\pi_{i}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)=0

for every
i\leq s-1
. Consider the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{0}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{0}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow 0

We have

\begin{split}\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{0}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)&=C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)_{0}=\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}T^{0}_{A}\left(A\langle X\rangle\right)\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\\
&=\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}A\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\cong\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\end{split}

and
\operatorname{Tor}_{0}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\cong\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}
, so we obtain the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\rightarrow 0

By the base case,
\pi_{0}(\mathfrak{H})=0
, i.e.
\pi_{k}(\mathfrak{H})=0
for every
k\leq 0
, and by the induction hypothesis,
\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)=0
for every
k\leq s-2
. As
\mathfrak{H}_{n}
is a projective, hence flat
A
-module, it follows from Corollary
3.5
that
\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)=0
for every
k\leq 0+s-2+1=s-1
. Therefore, we get the following exact sequence for every
k\leq s-1
:

0=\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(\partial_{1}^{C_{A}\left(\operatorname{Bar}_{A}\left(\mathfrak{H},A\langle X\rangle,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)}\right)\right)=0

Hence
\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)=0
for every
k\leq s-1
. We have the following short exact sequence of
A
-modules:

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\left(\frac{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}{\mathfrak{H}_{n}},\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{n}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{n}\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}\rightarrow 0

But
\frac{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}{\mathfrak{H}_{n}}\cong A
and
\mathfrak{H}_{n}\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}=\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s}
, so we get the following short exact sequence of
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\left(A,\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{n}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle_{n}}\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{n}^{s}\rightarrow 0

Since this short exact sequence is natural, we get the following short exact sequence of simplicial
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(A,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{s}\rightarrow 0

Therefore, using what was proved in the previous paragraph, we get the following exact sequence for every
k\leq s-1
:

0=\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}\otimes_{A\langle X\rangle}\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{s}\right)\rightarrow\pi_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{A\langle X\rangle}\left(A,\mathfrak{H}^{s-1}\right)\right)=0

Hence
\pi_{k}\left(\mathfrak{H}^{s}\right)=0
for every
k\leq s-1
.
∎

4.
Fundamental Spectral Sequences

In this section, we provide complete proofs of Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. We start with a remark on cotangent complex and André-Quillen homology and cohomology.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and
A
a commutative
R
-algebra. Then there is a Quillen adjunction as follows:

\left(\Omega_{-|R}\otimes_{-}A,A\ltimes-\right):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}\mathpzc{om}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(R)/A\leftrightarrows\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)

See
[
Qu1
, Proposition 1.7]
and
[
GS
, Proposition 4.27]
. By the general theory of derived functors in model categories, the left derived functor of
\Omega_{-|R}\otimes_{-}A:\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}\mathpzc{om}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(R)/A\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
exists; see
[
Hi
, Theorem 8.5.8]
and
[
Ho
, Lemma 1.3.10]
. Also, the right derived functor of
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(-):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
exists. Let
\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(A)
be the inclusion functor, and consider the following functors:

\operatorname{Ho}\left(\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{C}\mathpzc{om}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(R)/A\right)\xrightarrow{\textrm{L}\left(\Omega_{-|R}\otimes_{-}A\right)}\operatorname{Ho}\left(\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\right)\xrightarrow{\textrm{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(-)}\operatorname{Ho}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)\right)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{I})}\mathcal{D}(A)

Then the cotangent complex of
A
over
R
is defined as follows:

\mathbb{L}^{A|R}:=\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{I})\left(\textrm{R}\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textrm{L}\left(\Omega_{-|R}\otimes_{-}A\right)\left(1^{A}\right)\right)\right)

More concretely, if
\rho_{A}:A_{\textrm{c}}\rightarrow A
is any simplicial algebra resolution of
A
, then we have:

\mathbb{L}^{A|R}=\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{A_{\textrm{c}}|R}\otimes_{A_{\textrm{c}}}A\right)

Now if
M
is an
A
-module, then for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
i
th André-Quillen homology of
A
over
R
with coefficients in
M
is defined as

H_{i}^{AQ}(A|R;M):=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)=H_{i}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\otimes_{A}M\right),

and the
i
th André-Quillen cohomology of
A
over
R
with coefficients in
M
is defined as

H_{AQ}^{i}(A|R;M):=\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)=H_{-i}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)\right).

Now we are ready to present Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. Note that a sketchy argument with gaps for a special case of the first spectral sequence appears in an unpublished manuscript of Quillen; see
[
Qu1
, Theorem 6.8]
. Also, the second spectral sequence is only stated without proof in another
paper of Quillen; see
[
Qu3
, Theorem 6.8]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R
be a ring,
A
a commutative
R
-algebra, and
M
an
A
-module. Let
\rho:R\langle X\rangle\rightarrow A
be a simplicial algebra resolution of
A
. Suppose that the natural
R
-algebra epimorphism
\mu_{A}:A\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow A
, given by
\mu_{A}(a\otimes b)=ab
for every
a,b\in A
, is an isomorphism. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

There is a first quadrant spectral sequence as follows:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{p+q}^{R}(A,M)

(ii)

There is a third quadrant spectral sequence as follows:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-p-q}(A,M)

Proof.

We first note that the spectral sequences are independent of the simplicial algebra resolution chosen since
\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A=\textrm{L}\left(\Omega_{-|R}\otimes_{-}A\right)\left(1^{A}\right)
. Consider the morphism
\varphi:R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow A
of simplicial
A
-algebras, given by
\varphi_{n}(p\otimes a)=\rho_{n}(p)a
for every
n\geq 0
,
p\in R\langle X\rangle_{n}
, and
a\in A
. Set
\mathfrak{K}=\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)
regarded as a simplicial ideal in the simplicial
A
-algebra
R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A
. This gives a filtration

\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}\subseteq\mathfrak{K}^{s}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak{K}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{K}\subseteq R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A

of the simplicial
A
-module
R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A
. Since the normalization functor is exact, after applying the normalization functor and making the required identifications, we can assume that there is a filtration

\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K})\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right)

of the
A
-complex
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right)
. Furthermore, we observed in the proof of Theorem
3.6
that for any
s,n\geq 0
,
\frac{\mathfrak{K}_{n}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}_{n}^{s+1}}
is a projective
A
-module, so its direct summand
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\right)_{n}
is a projective
A
-module as well; see
[
We
, Lemma 8.3.7]
or
[
Fa
, Remark 4.6]
. Thus for any
s\geq 0
, the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{K}^{s}\rightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\rightarrow 0
of simplicial
A
-modules yields the degreewise split short exact sequence

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\right)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes. Then:

(i): For any
s\geq 0
, the degreewise split short exact sequence

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\right)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes gives the degreewise split short exact sequence

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes. After performing the required identifications, we can assume that
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\otimes_{A}M\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M
and
\frac{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M}{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\otimes_{A}M}\cong\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{s}}{\mathfrak{K}^{s+1}}\right)\otimes_{A}M
. This gives a filtration

\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s+1})\otimes_{A}M\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{2})\otimes_{A}M\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K})\otimes_{A}M\subseteq\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right)\otimes_{A}M

of the
A
-complex
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right)\otimes_{A}M
. Setting
F^{p}=\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-p})\otimes_{A}M
for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, the above filtration takes the following standard form:

\cdots\subseteq F^{p-1}\subseteq F^{p}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F^{-1}\subseteq F^{0}=\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right)\otimes_{A}M

We next show that
H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)=0
for every
s\geq 0
and
k\leq s-1
. Fix
s\geq 0
. Following the ideas of
[
An
, Chapter II, Lemme 18]
, we show that
H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}M
for every
A
-module
M
and
k\leq s-1
. For
k<0
, both sides are zero, so we may assume that
0\leq k\leq s-1
. We argue by induction on
k
. Let
k=0
. Then we have:

\begin{split}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)&=\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M}\right)=\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})}\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})}\right)\otimes_{A}M\\
&=H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}M\end{split}

Now suppose that
k\geq 1
, and the result holds for
k-1
. Let
0\rightarrow N^{\prime}\rightarrow N\rightarrow N^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of
A
-modules. Since
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})
is an
A
-complex of projective, hence flat modules, by
[
CFH
, Example 2.4.17]
, we get the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes, which gives the following exact sequence:

H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime}\right)\rightarrow H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N\right)\rightarrow H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime\prime}\right)\rightarrow H_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime}\right)

By the induction hypothesis,
H_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}M
. Since
k-1<k\leq s-1
, Theorem
3.6
implies that
H_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)=\pi_{k-1}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})=0
, so
H_{k-1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)=0
. Therefore, the above exact sequence turns into the following exact sequence:

H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime}\right)\rightarrow H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N\right)\rightarrow H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}N^{\prime\prime}\right)\rightarrow 0

This in particular shows that the functor
H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}-\right):\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is right exact. On the other hand, the covariant functor
-\otimes_{A}M:\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is right exact, so there is a natural
A
-homomorphism
\chi_{M}:H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)
. Moreover, if
F
is a flat
A
-module, then the functor
-\otimes_{A}F:\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is exact, so we conclude that
\chi_{F}
is an isomorphism; see
[
CFH
, 2.2.19]
. Now let
F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence in which
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are flat
A
-modules. Then we get the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}F_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}F_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\right)\otimes_{A}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}F_{1}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}F_{0}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{k}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{s})\otimes_{A}M\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{F_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\frac{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{p-1})}{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{p})},M\right)\rightarrow\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}
that makes the above diagram commutative. In particular,
\frac{F^{p}}{F^{p-1}}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\frac{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{p-1})}{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{p})},M\right)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p}}\right),M\right)
. Hence we have the following spectral sequence:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{1}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p}}\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right),M\right)\right)

We now reparametrize the spectral sequence as follows. Consider the following differential:

\textstyle H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p}}\right),M\right)\right)=E_{p,q}^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{p,q}^{1}}E_{p-1,q}^{1}=H_{p+q-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-2}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}\right),M\right)\right)

We find
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
such that

and
p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=p+q
. Thus
p^{\prime}=2p+q-1
and
q^{\prime}=-p+1
. Then
H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p}}\right),M\right)\right)
is transformed into
H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}+1}}\right),M\right)\right)
, and
H_{p+q-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{p-2}}{\mathfrak{K}^{p-1}}\right),M\right)\right)
is transformed into
H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}\right),M\right)\right)
. Since
d_{p,q}^{1}
is induced by
\partial_{p+q}^{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right),M\right)}
, we see that
d_{p,q}^{1}
is transformed into the following homomorphism which is induced by
\partial_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right),M\right)}
:

\textstyle H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}+1}}\right),M\right)\right)\rightarrow H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}\right),M\right)\right)

Looking carefully at the indices, we notice that this homomorphism should be
d_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2}
. In other words, we have:

\textstyle H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}+1}}\right),M\right)\right)=E_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2}\xrightarrow{d_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2}}E_{p^{\prime}-2,q^{\prime}+1}^{2}=H_{p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}-1}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q^{\prime}}}\right),M\right)\right)

Reverting the indices back to
p
and
q
, we get the following spectral sequence:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right),M\right)\right)

For any
q\leq 0
, there is an isomorphism
\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\cong\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}}{\mathfrak{K}^{2}}\right)\cong\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)
of simplicial
A
-modules which gives the following isomorphism of
A
-complexes:

\textstyle\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right),M\right)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)

Moreover,
\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(\rho):\operatorname{Nor}_{R}\left(R\langle X\rangle\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{R}(A)=A
is a projective resolution of
A
as an
R
-module, so we have

\begin{split}H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(R\langle X\rangle\otimes_{R}A\right),M\right)\right)&\cong H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}\left(R\langle X\rangle\right)\otimes_{R}A,M\right)\right)\\
&\cong H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}\left(R\langle X\rangle\right),\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A,M)\right)\right)\\
&\cong H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{R}\left(R\langle X\rangle\right),M\right)\right)\\
&=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-p-q}(A,M)\end{split}

for every
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p+q\leq 0
. Thus we get the following spectral sequence:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-p-q}(A,M)

We finally show that the spectral sequence is third quadrant. If
q>0
, then
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)=0
, so
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
. For any
q\leq 0
, the degreewise split short exact sequence

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes gives the degreewise split short exact sequence

\textstyle 0\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q}),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}),M\right)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes which in turn yields the following exact sequence:

\textstyle H_{p+q+1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}),M\right)\right)\rightarrow H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right),M\right)\right)

\rightarrow H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q}),M\right)\right)

If
p>0
, then
-p-q-1\leq-q
and
-p-q\leq-q-1
, so
H_{p+q+1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}),M\right)\right)=0=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(\mathfrak{K}^{-q}),M\right)\right)
, whence the above exact sequence implies that:

\textstyle E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\cong H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{K}^{-q}}{\mathfrak{K}^{-q+1}}\right),M\right)\right)=0

Hence the spectral sequence is third quadrant.

∎

5.
Five-Term Exact Sequences

In this section, we present the five-term exact sequences resulting from the Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences. To this end, we need some generalizations of the five-term exact sequence of a general spectral sequence that seem to appear in
[
Ro
, Corollaries 10.32 and 10.34]
. However, the statements there are incorrect and no proof is presented. The correct statements with proofs are offered in
[
DFT
, Lemma 5.4]
. We include an even stronger statement here for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E^{2}_{p,q}\Rightarrow H_{p+q}
be a spectral sequence. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If the spectral sequence is first quadrant and there is an integer
n\geq 1
such that
E^{2}_{p,q}=0
for every
p\geq 2
and
q\leq n-2
, then there is a five-term exact sequence as follows:

H_{n+1}\rightarrow E^{2}_{2,n-1}\rightarrow E^{2}_{0,n}\rightarrow H_{n}\rightarrow E^{2}_{1,n-1}\rightarrow 0

(ii)

If the spectral sequence is third quadrant and there is an integer
n\geq 1
such that
E^{2}_{p,q}=0
for every
p\leq-2
and
q\geq 2-n
, then there is a five-term exact sequence as follows:

0\rightarrow E^{2}_{-1,1-n}\rightarrow H_{-n}\rightarrow E^{2}_{0,-n}\rightarrow E^{2}_{-2,1-n}\rightarrow H_{-n-1}

Proof.

For a proof, refer to
[
DFT
, Lemma 5.4]
. However, note that in the statement of
[
DFT
, Lemma 5.4 (i)]
, the vanishing
E^{2}_{p,q}=0
is assumed for every
q\leq n-2
. But the proof shows that the weaker assumption that
E^{2}_{p,q}=0
for every
p\geq 2
and
q\leq n-2
suffices. The same holds for
[
DFT
, Lemma 5.4 (ii)]
.
∎

The following lemma is well-known; see for example

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(M)^{(\Delta^{1})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa^{\mathcal{F}(M)}\iota_{1}^{\mathcal{F}(M)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(g)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa^{\mathcal{F}(M)}\iota_{2}^{\mathcal{F}(M)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(\phi)\theta}

This shows that
\mathcal{F}(f)\sim_{l}\mathcal{F}(g)
. But
\mathcal{F}(M)
is cofibrant, so we deduce that
\mathcal{F}(f)\sim_{r}\mathcal{F}(g)
; see
[
Hi
, Proposition 7.4.5]
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}(f)\sim\mathcal{F}(g)
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
R
and
S
be two rings, and
\mathcal{F}:\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(S)
a covariant functor that is extended from a preadditive covariant functor
\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(S)
. If
f:M\rightarrow N
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
,
M
and
N
are cofibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(R)
, and
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
\mathcal{F}(N)
are cofibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(S)
, then
\mathcal{F}(f):\mathcal{F}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(N)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(S)
.

Proof.

Since
f:M\rightarrow N
is a homotopy equivalence, there is a morphism
g:N\rightarrow M
such that
gf\sim 1^{M}
and
fg\sim 1^{N}
. Now the previous proposition implies that
\mathcal{F}(g)\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{F}(gf)\sim\mathcal{F}(1^{M})=1^{\mathcal{F}(M)}
and
\mathcal{F}(f)\mathcal{F}(g)=\mathcal{F}(fg)\sim\mathcal{F}(1^{N})=1^{\mathcal{F}(N)}
. Hence
\mathcal{F}(f)
is a homotopy equivalence.
∎

Now we are ready to handle the five-term exact sequences of the Quillen’s fundamental spectral sequences.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
R
be a ring,
A
an
R
-algebra, and
M
an
A
-module. Suppose that the natural
R
-algebra epimorphism
\mu_{A}:A\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow A
, given by
\mu_{A}(a\otimes b)=ab
for every
a,b\in A
, is an isomorphism. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

H_{0}^{AQ}(A|R;M)=0=H_{AQ}^{0}(A|R;M)
.

(ii)

H_{1}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,M)
and
H_{AQ}^{1}(A|R;M)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(A,M)
.

(iii)

There are natural exact sequences of
A
-modules as follows:

\operatorname{Tor}_{3}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{3}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{2}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(A,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A),M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{3}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{3}(A,M)

Proof.

(i): By the hypothesis,
\mu_{A}
is an isomorphism, so
\mathfrak{k}=\operatorname{Ker}(\mu_{A})=0
, whence
\Omega_{A|R}=\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{2}=0
. Using
[
Qu1
, Proposition 3.8]
, we get
H_{0}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\cong\Omega_{A|R}\otimes_{A}M=0
and
H_{AQ}^{0}(A|R;M)\cong\operatorname{Der}_{R}(A,M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\Omega_{A|R},M\right)=0
.

(ii): Consider the following natural first quadrant spectral sequence from Theorem
4.2
:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{p+q}^{R}(A,M)

By
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.31]
, there is a natural five-term exact sequence as follows:

H_{2}\rightarrow E_{2,0}^{2}\rightarrow E_{0,1}^{2}\rightarrow H_{1}\rightarrow E_{1,0}^{2}\rightarrow 0

But we have:

E_{1,0}^{2}=H_{1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{0}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(A)\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{1}\left(A\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{1}(M)=0

Similarly, we see that
E_{2,0}^{2}=0
. Thus from the above sequence, we get the following natural isomorphism:

\begin{split}H_{1}^{AQ}(A|R;M)&=H_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\\
&=H_{1}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{1}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)=E_{0,1}^{2}\cong H_{1}=\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,M)\end{split}

For the second part, consider the following natural third quadrant spectral sequence from Theorem
4.2
:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-p-q}(A,M)

By
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.33]
, there is a natural five-term exact sequence as follows:

0\rightarrow E_{-1,0}^{2}\rightarrow H_{-1}\rightarrow E_{0,-1}^{2}\rightarrow E_{-2,0}^{2}\rightarrow H_{-2}

But we have:

\begin{split}E_{-1,0}^{2}&=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{0}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(A),M\right)\right)\\
&=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(A,M\right)\right)\cong H_{-1}(M)=0\end{split}

Similarly, we see that
E_{-2,0}^{2}=0
. Thus from the above sequence, we get the following natural isomorphism:

\begin{split}H_{AQ}^{1}(A|R;M)&=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)\right)=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right),M\right)\right)\\
&=H_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{1}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)=E_{0,-1}^{2}\cong H_{-1}=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}(A,M)\end{split}

(iii): Consider the following natural first quadrant spectral sequence from Theorem
4.2
:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{p+q}^{R}(A,M)

We note that
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
for every
p\geq 2
and
q\leq 0
. Indeed, if
q<0
, then
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)=0
, so
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
. Moreover, if
q=0
, then since
p\geq 2
, we have:

E_{p,0}^{2}=H_{p}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{0}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{p}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(A)\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{p}(A\otimes_{A}M)\cong H_{p}(M)=0

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that there is a natural five-term exact sequence as follows:

H_{3}\rightarrow E_{2,1}^{2}\rightarrow E_{0,2}^{2}\rightarrow H_{2}\rightarrow E_{1,1}^{2}\rightarrow 0

We note that:

\begin{split}E_{1,1}^{2}&=H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{1}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\\
&=H_{2}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\otimes_{A}M\right)=H_{2}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\end{split}

Similarly, we have
E_{2,1}^{2}=H_{3}^{AQ}(A|R;M)
. Moreover,
H_{2}=\operatorname{Tor}_{2}^{R}(A,M)
and
H_{3}=\operatorname{Tor}_{3}^{R}(A,M)
. Therefore, we are left to determine the following term:

E_{0,2}^{2}=H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)

We express this term in terms of exterior powers. By
[
CFH
, Theorems 5.1.12 and 5.2.15]
, there is a semi-projective resolution
P\rightarrow\mathbb{L}^{A|R}
such that
P_{n}
is a free
A
-module for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P_{n}=0
for every
n<\inf\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)
. Since
\mu_{A}
is an isomorphism,
\mathfrak{k}=\operatorname{Ker}(\mu_{A})=0
, so
H_{0}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)\cong\Omega_{A|R}=\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{2}=0
, whence
\inf\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)\geq 1
. Hence
P_{n}=0
for every
n<1
. Therefore, we have the following degreewise split short exact sequence of connective
A
-complexes:

0\rightarrow\Sigma^{-1}P\rightarrow\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0

Since the Dold-Kan functor
\operatorname{DK}:\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is an equivalence of categories, it is a fortiori exact. Moreover, it is clear from the definition that it preserves degreewise split short exact sequences. As a result, we get the following degreewise split short exact sequence of simplicial
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{DK}\left(\Sigma^{-1}P\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{DK}\left(\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{DK}(P)\rightarrow 0

Setting
U=\operatorname{DK}\left(\Sigma^{-1}P\right)
,
V=\operatorname{DK}\left(\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)\right)
, and
W=\operatorname{DK}(P)
for convenience, the above short exact sequence takes the following form:

0\rightarrow U\rightarrow V\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0

We further note that for any
n\geq 0
,
P_{n}
is a free
A
-module, so it is obvious that
U_{n}
,
V_{n}
, and
W_{n}
are free
A
-modules. Now fix
n\geq 0
. Since
V_{n}=U_{n}\oplus W_{n}
, we get:

\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})=\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n}\oplus W_{n})\cong\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})

Thus
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})
is a
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})
-module via the canonical injection
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})
. Therefore, as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we have the following natural isomorphisms for every
i\geq 0
:

\begin{split}H_{i}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)\right)&\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})}\left(A,\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})}\left(A,\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})\right)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})}\left(A,\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(U_{n})\right)\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})\end{split}

It follows that:

H_{i}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)\right)\cong\begin{cases}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})&\textrm{if }i=0\\
0&\textrm{if }i>0\end{cases}

This means that the following augmented sequence is exact:

K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)^{+}:\cdots\rightarrow K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)_{2}\rightarrow K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)_{1}\rightarrow K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)_{0}

\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0

Writing
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(W_{n})=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{j}(W_{n})
, we have

\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}H_{i,j}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)^{+}\right)\cong H_{i}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)^{+}\right)=0

for every
i\geq 0
, so
H_{i,j}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)^{+}\right)=0
for every
i,j\geq 0
. In particular,
H_{i,2}\left(K^{A}\left(U_{n},\operatorname{Sym}_{A}(V_{n})\right)^{+}\right)=0
for every
i\geq 0
, so we get the following natural exact sequence of
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U_{n}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{0}(V_{n})\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{1}U_{n}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{1}(V_{n})\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{0}U_{n}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0

But this sequence is isomorphic to the following natural exact sequence of
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U_{n}\rightarrow U_{n}\otimes_{A}V_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W_{n})\rightarrow 0

As a result, we get the following exact sequence of simplicial
A
-modules:

0\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\rightarrow U\otimes_{A}V\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\rightarrow 0

Applying the normalization functor, we obtain the following exact sequence of
A
-complexes:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\rightarrow 0

For any
n\geq 0
, the sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)_{n}\rightarrow 0

is a right-bounded exact
A
-complex of projective modules, so it is split exact, thereby the sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)_{n}\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)_{n}\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)_{n}\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)_{n}\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow 0

is split exact. Hence we get the following exact sequence of
A
-complexes:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow 0

We now note that by
[
CFH
, Theorem 4.3.30]
,
\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)
is a contractible
A
-complex, so we have

H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(V)\right)=H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{DK}\left(\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)\right)\right)\right)\cong H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Cone}\left(1^{\Sigma^{-1}P}\right)\right)=0

for every
i\geq 0
, i.e.
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(V)
is an exact
A
-complex. Since
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)
is an
A
-complex of projective, hence flat modules, it follows from
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.90]
that
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(V)
is an exact
A
-complex. But then by Lemma
3.3
,

H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U\otimes_{A}V)\right)=\pi_{i}(U\otimes_{A}V)\cong H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\otimes_{A}\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(V)\right)=0

for every
i\geq 0
, so
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U\otimes_{A}V)
is an exact
A
-complex. As
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U\otimes_{A}V)
is an
A
-complex of projective, hence flat modules, we conclude from
[
Ro
, Theorem 10.90]
that
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U\otimes_{A}V)\otimes_{A}M
is an exact
A
-complex. On the other hand, since
\pi_{i}(V)=H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(V)\right)=0
for every
i\geq 0
, we conclude that the unique morphism
0\rightarrow V
is a weak equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. As the source and the target of the latter morphism consists of free, hence projective modules, they are both cofibrant and thus bifibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, so by Whitehead Homotopy Lemma (see
[
Hi
, Theorem 7.5.10]
),
0\rightarrow V
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. Since the functor
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(-):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
is extended from a preadditive covariant functor
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(-):\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, we conclude from Corollary
5.3
that
0=\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(0)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. But the source and the target of the latter morphism consists of free, hence projective modules, so they are both cofibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, whence
0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)
is a weak equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. As a result,
0=\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(0)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)
is a weak equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, i.e. a quasi-isomorphism. Again the source and the target of the latter morphism consists of projective modules, so they are both cofibrant in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, thereby
0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
. Consequently,
0=0\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, hence a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, we have
H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)=0
for every
i\geq 0
, i.e.
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M
is an exact
A
-complex. Now consider the following exact sequence of
A
-complexes:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow 0

We just observed that
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U\otimes_{A}V)\otimes_{A}M
and
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\otimes_{A}M
are exact
A
-complexes, so we infer that:

H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)

Since
P\rightarrow\mathbb{L}^{A|R}
is a quasi-isomorphism,
W=\operatorname{DK}(P)\rightarrow\operatorname{DK}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)
is a weak equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. But

\operatorname{DK}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)=\operatorname{DK}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\cong\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A,

so there is a weak equivalence
W\rightarrow\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A
in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. As the source and the target of the latter morphism consists of free, hence projective modules, they are both cofibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, so
W\rightarrow\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, whence
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. But the source and the target of the latter morphism consists of free, hence projective modules, so they are both cofibrant in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
, whence
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)
is a weak equivalence in
\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
. As a result,
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)
is a weak equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, i.e. a quasi-isomorphism. Again the source and the target of latter morphism consists of projective modules, so they are both cofibrant in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, thereby
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
. As a consequence,

\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M

is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, hence a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, we have:

E_{0,2}^{2}=H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(W\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)

Now let
\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)
denote the category of skew-commutative
A
-algebras, and
\sharp_{1}:\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
be the the projection functor onto degree one summand. Deploying the adjoint pairs of functors
\left(\bigwedge_{A}-,\sharp_{1}\right):\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\leftrightarrows\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)
,
\left(\pi_{0},\operatorname{Diag}\right):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\leftrightarrows\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)
,
\left(\bigwedge_{A}-,\sharp_{1}\right):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)\leftrightarrows\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)
, and
\left(\pi_{0},\operatorname{Diag}\right):\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)\leftrightarrows\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)
successively, we get the following natural isomorphisms for every skew-commutative
A
-algebra
B
:

\begin{split}\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}\pi_{0}(U),B\right)&\cong\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)}\left(\pi_{0}(U),B_{1}\right)\cong\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{M}\mathpzc{od}(A)}\left(U,B_{1}\right)\cong\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathpzc{s}\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}U,B\right)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Mor}_{\mathcal{S}\mathpzc{kw}\mathcal{A}\mathpzc{lg}(A)}\left(\pi_{0}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}U\right),B\right)\end{split}

An application of Yoneda Lemma (see
[
Aw
, Corollary 8.5]
) implies that
\bigwedge_{A}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)=\bigwedge_{A}\pi_{0}(U)\cong\pi_{0}\left(\bigwedge_{A}U\right)=H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}U\right)\right)
, so in particular,
\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\right)
. Thus we get:

\begin{split}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)&=\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M}\right)=\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)}\otimes_{A}M\right)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)}\right)\otimes_{A}M=H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\\
&\cong\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)\otimes_{A}M\end{split}

Also, using (ii), we have:

\begin{split}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)&\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{DK}\left(\Sigma^{-1}P\right)\right)\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\Sigma^{-1}P\right)=H_{1}(P)\cong H_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\right)\cong H_{1}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R}\otimes_{A}A\right)\\
&=H_{1}^{AQ}(A|R;A)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)\end{split}

Putting everything together, we get:

\begin{split}E_{0,2}^{2}&=H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong H_{2}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\\
&\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\otimes_{A}M\right)\cong\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)\otimes_{A}M\cong\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)\otimes_{A}M\end{split}

All in all, we get the following exact sequence:

\operatorname{Tor}_{3}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{3}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)\otimes_{A}M\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{2}^{R}(A,M)\rightarrow H_{2}^{AQ}(A|R;M)\rightarrow 0

For the second part, consider the following third quadrant spectral sequence from Theorem
4.2
:

E_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p+q}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{-p-q}(A,M)

We note that
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
for every
p\leq-2
and
q\geq 0
. Indeed, if
q>0
, then
\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{-q}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle|R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)=0
, so
E_{p,q}^{2}=0
. Moreover, if
q=0
, then since
p\leq-2
, we have:

\begin{split}E_{p,0}^{2}&=H_{p}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{0}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)=H_{p}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(A),M\right)\right)\\
&=H_{p}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A,M)\right)\cong H_{p}(M)=0\end{split}

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
5.1
that there is a natural five-term exact sequence as follows:

0\rightarrow E_{-1,-1}^{2}\rightarrow H_{-2}\rightarrow E_{0,-2}^{2}\rightarrow E_{-2,-1}^{2}\rightarrow H_{-3}

We note that:

\begin{split}E_{-1,-1}^{2}&=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{1}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\\
&=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right),M\right)\right)\\
&=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\mathbb{L}^{A|R},M\right)\right)\\
&=H^{2}_{AQ}(A|R;M)\end{split}

Similarly, we have
E_{-2,-1}^{2}=H_{AQ}^{3}(A|R;M)
. Moreover,
H_{-2}=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(A,M)
and
H_{-3}=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{3}(A,M)
. Therefore, we are left to determine the following term:

E_{0,-2}^{2}=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)

We express this term in terms of exterior powers. Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-complexes obtained above. For any
n\geq 0
, the sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)_{n}\rightarrow 0

is split exact, so the sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)_{n},M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)_{n},M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)_{n},M\right)

\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)_{n},M\right)\rightarrow 0

is split exact. Hence we get the following exact sequence of
A
-complexes:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right),M\right)

\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right),M\right)\rightarrow 0

As
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right)
is an exact
A
-complex of projective modules, we conclude from a dual version of [Ro, Theorem 10.90] that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\otimes_{A}V\right),M\right)
is an exact
A
-complex. On the other hand, we observed that
0\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, so
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(0,M)=0
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, hence a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, we have
H_{i}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right),M\right)\right)=0
for every
i\geq 0
, i.e.
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(V)\right),M\right)
is an exact
A
-complex. Now it follows from the above exact sequence that:

H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right),M\right)\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right),M\right)\right)

We observed that
\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right)
is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, so

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right),M\right)

is a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}(A)
, hence a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, we have:

E_{0,-2}^{2}=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\cong H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right),M\right)\right)

We observed that
\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\right)
, so we get:

\begin{split}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right),M\right)\right)&=\operatorname{Ker}\left(\partial_{0}^{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right),M\right)}\right)=\operatorname{Ker}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)},M\right)\right)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Coker}\left(\partial_{1}^{\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)}\right),M\right)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right)\right),M\right)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(U\right)\right),M\right)\end{split}

Also, we observed that
H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A)
. Putting everything together, we get:

\begin{split}E_{0,-2}^{2}&=H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}\left(\Omega_{R\langle X\rangle\mid R}\otimes_{R\langle X\rangle}A\right)\right),M\right)\right)\cong H_{-2}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Sym}_{A}^{2}(W)\right),M\right)\right)\\
&\cong H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}U\right),M\right)\right)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}H_{0}\left(\operatorname{Nor}_{A}(U)\right),M\right)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A),M\right)\end{split}

All in all, we get the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{2}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(A,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{A}\left(\textstyle\bigwedge_{A}^{2}\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(A,A),M\right)\rightarrow H_{AQ}^{3}(A|R;M)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{3}(A,M)\qed

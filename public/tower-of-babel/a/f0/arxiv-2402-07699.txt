On scalable K K -frames and a version of Lax-Milgram theorem
F. Javadi 1 and M. J. Mehdipour 1

Abstract
In this paper, we first prove a theorem by a little modification on the Lax-Milgram theorem. Then, using K K -frames, we obtain lower and upper bounds for the results obtained from this theorem. Also, we present some methods for the characterization of scalable K K -frames. Finally, we introduce piecewise scalable K K -frames and give necessary and sufficient conditions for a K K -frame to be piecewise scalable.



Keywords
: Lax-Milgram theorem, parseval
K
-frame, scalable
K
-frame, piecewise scalable
K
-frame.

Introduction

Let
H
denote a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space and

{\cal L}(H)
be the space of all bounded linear operators from
H
into
H
.
A sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
frame
, if there exist constants
A,B>0
such that for every
f\in H
,

(1.1)

A\,\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,f_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\,\|f\|^{2};

see [7] for more details.
If
A=B=1
,
then we call
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
a
Parseval frame
.
A frame
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a

scalable frame
if there exist constants
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{\infty}(J)
such that
\{c_{j}f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Parseval frame. So for every
f\in H
, we have

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}.

In this paper, we always assume that
K\in\mathcal{L}(H)
. A sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
K
-frame
,
if there exist constants
A,B>0
such that for every
f\in H
,

(1.2)

A\,\|K^{*}f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,f_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\,\|f\|^{2}.

If

\|K^{*}f\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,f_{j}\rangle|^{2},

then
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
Parseval
K
-frame
on
H
.
The operator
T:H\rightarrow\ell^{2}(J)
defined by

Tf=\{\langle f,f_{j}\rangle\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}},

is called
analysis operator of

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
The operator
S:H\rightarrow H
defined by

Sf=T^{*}Tf=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle f_{j},

is called
frame operator
of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
; see [9, 10, 11, 12, 15].

It’s clear that if
K
is the identity map on
H
, then every
K
-frame is an ordinary frame. Hence,
K
-frames arise naturally as a generalization of the ordinary frames. Parseval

K
-frames are one of the most important types of
K
-frames. They are applied in signal communication. It is natural to ask how to make a Parseval

K
-frame from a given
K
-frame. Kutyniok
et. al [13] solved this question for frames and introduced the conception of scalable frames. This notion has been investigated by some authors [2,4,5,6,14]. These results cause us to investigate the conception of scalable
K
-frames.

Several authors studied scalable frames and piecewise scalable frames. For example, Cahill and Chen [2] investigated the following question: Under what conditions is a frame scalable? Casazza et. al [3] generalized the notion of scalable frames and introduced
piecewise scalable frames. They also characterized them. In this paper, we continue these investigations.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we prove an analogue of Lax-Milgram theorem and give an application of it for
K
-frames.
In Section 3, we construct a scalable
K
-frame by a given scalable and a bounded operator.
In Section 4, we introduce piecewise scalable
K
-frame and characterize them.

2
Generalization of Lax-Milgram Theorem

Let
{\cal H}
be a real Hilbert space and

\sigma:{\cal H}\times{\cal H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bilinear map.
For
f_{0}\in{\cal H}
and a non-empty closed convex subset
C
of
{\cal H}
,
we define the function
J_{f_{0},\sigma}:C\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(v,v)-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle.

Let us recall that
\sigma
is called
coercive
if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle\sigma(v,v)\geq\alpha\,\|v\|^{2},

and is called
continuous
if there exists
\beta>0
such that for every
u,v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle|\sigma(u,v)|\leq\beta\,\|u\|\,\|v\|.

Also,
a self-adjoint operator
A:{\cal H}\rightarrow{\cal H}
is called
coercive
if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle\langle Av,v\rangle\geq\alpha\,\|v\|^{2}.

The following result is a little modification of the Stampacchia and the Lax-Milgram theorems [1].

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a continuous coercive bilinear map on
{\cal H}
, and
C
be a non-empty closed convex subset of
{\cal H}
. Then for every bounded linear functional
L
on
{\cal H}
,
there exist unique
u_{0}\in C
and
f_{0}\in{\cal H}
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle\sigma(u_{0},v-u_{0})\geq\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle\quad\emph{and}\quad L(v)=\langle f_{0},v\rangle.

Moreover, if
\sigma
is symmetric,
then
u_{0}
is characterized by the property

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma}(u_{0})=\min_{v\in C}J_{f_{0},\sigma}(v).

Proof.

Let
L
be a bounded linear functional on
{\cal H}
.
Using the Riesz
representation theorem,
there exists a unique element
f_{0}\in{\cal H}
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}
,

\displaystyle L(v)=\langle f_{0},v\rangle.

Put
\tilde{L}:=LKK^{*}
.
Then there exists
f_{0}^{\prime}\in{\cal H}
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}
, we have

\tilde{L}(v)=\langle f_{0}^{\prime},v\rangle
.
So for every
v\in{\cal H}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle f_{0}^{\prime},v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(v)=LKK^{*}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(KK^{*}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{0},KK^{*}v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle.

For every
u\in{\cal H}
,
the linear functional
v\mapsto\sigma(u,v)
is continuous.
So,
we find some unique element in
{\cal H}
, denoted by
\Lambda u
,
such that
\sigma(u,v)=\langle\Lambda u,v\rangle
for every
v\in{\cal H}
.
Then the operator
\Lambda
is linear and there exists a constant
\beta>0
such that for every
u\in{\cal H}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle\Lambda u,u\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sigma(u,u)|\leq\beta\,\|u\|^{2}.

Since
\sigma
is coercive, there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that for every
u\in{\cal H}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda u,u\rangle=\sigma(u,u)\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}.

Set
\gamma:=\alpha/\beta^{2}
and define the map
T
on
C
by

\displaystyle T(v)=P_{C}(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda v+v),

where
P_{C}
is the projection onto the closed convex set
C
. Note that every projection on
C
is not increase distance;
see Proposition 5.3 in [1].
Thus

\displaystyle\|Tv_{1}-Tv_{2}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P_{C}(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda v_{1}+v_{1})-P_{C}(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda v_{2}+v_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda v_{1}+v_{1})-(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda v_{2}+v_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}-v_{2})-\gamma(\Lambda v_{1}-\Lambda v_{2})\|.

So, using (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|Tv_{1}-Tv_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}-v_{2})\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle\Lambda v_{1}-\Lambda v_{2},v_{1}-v_{2}\rangle+\gamma^{2}\|\Lambda v_{1}-\Lambda v_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}-v_{2})\|^{2}(1-2\gamma\alpha+\gamma^{2}\beta^{2}).

Hence
T
is a strict contraction.
In view of Theorem 5.7 in [1],

T
has a unique fixed point,
i.e.,
there exists a unique
u_{0}\in C
such that

\displaystyle u_{0}=T(u_{0})=P_{C}(\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda u_{0}+u_{0}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\langle\gamma KK^{*}f_{0}-\gamma\Lambda u_{0}+u_{0}-u_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle\leq 0;

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda u_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle\geq\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle.

Therefore,
\sigma(u_{0},v-u_{0})\geq\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle
.

Now, let
\sigma(u,v)
be symmetric.
Then the mapping

(u,v)\mapsto\sigma(u,v)=\langle\Lambda u,v\rangle

defines an inner product on
{\cal H}
.
With this inner product,
{\cal H}
is also a Hilbert space.
By
the Riesz theorem, there exists a unique element
g_{0}\in{\cal H}
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(v)=\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle=\sigma(g_{0},v).

The rest of the proof is similar to the proof of Theorem Stampacchia; see Theorem 5.6 in [1].
So we omit it.
∎

Let
\sigma(u,v)
be a continuous bilinear on
{\cal H}
.
Then for every
v\in{\cal H}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(v,v)\geq 0.

Thus the function
v\mapsto\sigma(v,v)
is convex.
This fact together with Theorem
2.1
proves the following result.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\sigma(u,v)
be a continuous coercive bilinear on
{\cal H}
.
Then for every bounded linear functional
L
on
{\cal H}
,
there exist unique
u_{0},f_{0}\in{\cal H}
such that for every
v\in{\cal H}

\displaystyle\sigma(u_{0},v-u_{0})=\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v-u_{0}\rangle\quad\emph{and}\quad L(v)=\langle f_{0},v\rangle.

Moreover, if
\sigma
is symmetric,
then
u_{0}
is characterized by the property

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma}(u_{0})=\min_{v\in C}J_{f_{0},\sigma}(v).

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K
-frame for
{\cal H}
with the frame operator
S
.
Define the bilinear form
\sigma_{S}:{\cal H}\times{\cal H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(u,v)=\langle Su,v\rangle.

Then
\sigma_{S}
is bounded, coercive and symmetric.
So Corollary
2.2
yields the following:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K
-frame for
{\cal H}
with the frame operator
S
.
Then for every bounded linear functional
L
on
{\cal H}
, there exist unique
u_{0},f_{0}\in{\cal H}
for which

\displaystyle\langle Su_{0},v\rangle=\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\quad\emph{and}\quad L(v)=\langle f_{0},v\rangle

for all
v\in H
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(u_{0})=\min_{v\in C}J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v).

In the following, using
K
-frames, we get lower and upper bounds for
\min_{v\in{\cal H}}J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K
-frame for
{\cal H}
with the frame operator
S
and
K
-frame constants
A<B
. If
f_{0}\in{\cal H}
, then

\displaystyle-\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{2A}\leq\min_{v\in{\cal H}}J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)\leq-\frac{7}{32}\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{B\|f_{0}\|^{2}}\leq-\beta\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2},

for some
\beta>0
.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K
-frame for
\cal{H}
.
By (
1.2
), for every
v\in\cal{H}
,
we have

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\langle Sv,v\rangle-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{A}{2}\|K^{*}v\|^{2}-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\bigg).

Apply Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to conclude that

\displaystyle\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle=\langle K^{*}f_{0},K^{*}v\rangle\leq\|K^{*}f_{0}\|\|K^{*}v\|.

So we have

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)\geq\bigg(\frac{A}{2}\|K^{*}v\|^{2}-\|K^{*}f_{0}\|\|K^{*}v\|\bigg).

The minimum of the function
v\mapsto\frac{A}{2}\|K^{*}v\|^{2}-\|K^{*}f_{0}\|\|K^{*}v\|
is attained in point
\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|}{A}
, and hence

\displaystyle\min_{v\in{\cal H}}J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{A}{2}\frac{1}{A^{2}}\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}-\|K^{*}f_{0}\|\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|}{A}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{2A}.

In view of (
1.2
), for every
v\in\cal{H}
we have

\displaystyle J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\langle Sv,v\rangle-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{B}{2}\|v\|^{2}-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\bigg).

Thus

\displaystyle\min_{v\in{\cal H}}J_{f_{0},\sigma_{S}}(v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\min_{v\in{\cal H}}\bigg(\frac{B}{2}\|v\|^{2}-\langle KK^{*}f_{0},v\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{B\alpha^{2}}{2}\|f_{0}\|^{2}-\alpha\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0,

whenever

\displaystyle 0<\alpha<\frac{2}{B}\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{\|f_{0}\|^{2}}.

The minimum of the function
\alpha\mapsto\frac{B\alpha^{2}}{2}\|f_{0}\|^{2}-\alpha\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}
is attained in point
\alpha=\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{4B\|f_{0}\|^{2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{B}{2}\,\alpha^{2}\|f_{0}\|^{2}-\alpha\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{B}{2}\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{4}}{16B^{2}\|f_{0}\|^{4}}\|f_{0}\|^{2}-\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{4B\|f_{0}\|^{2}}\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{2}}{16B\|f_{0}\|^{2}}-\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{4}}{4B\|f_{0}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{7}{32}\frac{\|K^{*}f_{0}\|^{4}}{B\|f_{0}\|^{2}},

as claimed.
∎

3
Construction of Scalable
K
-Frames

Throughout this section,
H
denotes a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space. First, we give a slight generalization of the concept of scalable frames for
K
-frames;
in fact, a
K
-frame
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq H
is called a
K_{s}
-frame with scalling
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{\infty}(J)
,
briefly
K_{s}
-frame and denoted by
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
if
\{c_{j}f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
Parseval
K
-frame
, i.e.,
for every
f\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}=KK^{*}f;

or equivalently,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
U,V\in\mathcal{L}(H)
and
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
H
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a scalable frame,
then
\{Uf_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
U_{s}
-frame with the same scalling of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

(ii)

If
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
U_{s}
-frame,
then sequence
\{Vf_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
VU_{s}
-frame with the same scalling of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

(iii)
If
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{Uf_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
are
K_{s}
-frames with the same scallings,
then

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
UK_{s}
-frame with the same scalling of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

(iv)
If
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame and
N\geq 1
,
then

\{K^{N}f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}^{N+1}
-frame with the same scalling of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
and
\{c_{j}\}
be a sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(J)
. Then for every
f\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}Tf_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}Tf_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}Tf_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle T^{*}f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}\bigg).

This proves (i) and (ii) if we set
T=U
and
T=V
, respectively.

Let
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
(Uf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be
K_{s}
-frames. Then

\displaystyle(UK)(UK)^{*}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}Uf_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}Uf_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle KK^{*}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}.

So,
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
UK_{s}
-frame.
Thus (iii) holds.

Let
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K_{s}
-frame.
Then for every
f\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,c_{j}K^{N}f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle(K^{N})^{*}f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}({K^{N}})^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(K^{N+1})^{*}f)\|^{2}.

Therefore,
(K^{N}f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
K^{N+1}_{s}
-frame.
That is, (iv) is true.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
and
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K_{s}
-frame.
If
TK=KT
and
T^{*}
is isometry,
then
(Tf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame.

Proof.

Let
(f_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K_{s}
-frame.
Then
for every
f\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,c_{j}Tf_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle T^{*}f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}T^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(TK)^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(KT)^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T^{*}(K^{*}f)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

Thus
(Tf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be an invertible operator,

\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
K
-frame for
H
and
\{c_{j}\}\in\ell^{\infty}(J)
.
Then
(Tf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame
if and only if
the frame operator for
\{c_{j}Tf_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
(T^{-1}K)(T^{-1}K)^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be the operator frame for
\{c_{j}f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then for every
f\in H
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}Tf_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}Tf_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle T^{*}f,c_{j}f_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}f_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle TST^{*}f.

On the other hand,
(Tf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame.
Thus

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,c_{j}Tf_{j}\rangle\,c_{j}Tf_{j}=KK^{*}f.

By using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
),

(Tf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame
if and only if
S=(T^{-1}K)(T^{-1}K)^{*}
.
∎

4
Piecewise Scalable
K
-Frames

A
K
-frame
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for the
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
piecewise scalable
K
-frame

(briefly,
K_{s}^{p}
-frame)
if there exist an orthogonal projection
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and constants
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
such that
for every

f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}+b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

We denote this notion by
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
. In the following, let
P
be an orthogonal projection on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and we set

\displaystyle X:=P(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\hbox{and}\quad Y:=(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

One can prove that if
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then
(Pf_{j},a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
and
((I-P)f_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
are
K_{s}
-frames for
X
and
Y
, respectively. To prove other our results, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
KP=PK
.
If
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a Parseval
K
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\{Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a Parseval
K
-frame for
X
.

Proof.

For every
f\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle Pf,f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}Pf\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|PK^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

Also, note that if
PK=KP
,
then
\hbox{ran}(K^{*})\subseteq\hbox{ran}(P)
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
KP=PK
.
Then
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame
if and only if

(i)

Both
(Pf_{j},a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
and
((I-P)f_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
are
K_{s}
-frame for
X
and
Y
, respectively.

(ii)

For every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle=0.

Proof.

First, note that if (i) holds, then for every
f\in H
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}+b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|PK^{*}f\|+\|(I-P)K^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}f\|^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle.

Assume now that
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame.
From Lemma
4.1
and the fact

\displaystyle P(a_{j}Pf_{j}+b_{j}(I-P)f_{j})=a_{j}Pf_{j},

we infer that
\{a_{j}Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a Parseval
K
-frame for
X
.
Therefore,
\{b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a Parseval
K
-frame for
Y
.
That is, (i) is proved.
The converse follows at once from (
4.1
).
∎

(ii)

In the next result, we let
T_{1}
and

be analysis operators of
\{Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
PK=KP
.
If there exists a proper non-empty subset
I
of
\{1,...,n\}
such that
(Pf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in I}
and
((I-P)f_{j},d_{j})_{j\in I^{c}}
are
K_{s}
-frames for

X
and
Y
, respectively, then there exist constants
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

such that
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a proper non-empty subset of
\{1,...,n\}
such that
(Pf_{j},c_{j})_{j\in I}
and
((I-P)f_{j},d_{j})_{j\in I^{c}}
are
K_{s}
-frames for

X
and
Y
, respectively.
Set

\displaystyle a_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{j},&\hbox{$j\in I$;}\\
0,&\hbox{$j\in I^{c}$}\end{array}\right.\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad b_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$j\in I$;}\\
d_{j},&\hbox{$j\in I^{c}$.}\end{array}\right.

Then for every
f\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i\in I^{c}}|\langle f,a_{j}Pf_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

So
(Pf_{j},a_{j})_{j\in I}
is a
K_{s}
-frame for

X
.
Similarly,
one can prove that
((I-P)f_{j},b_{j})_{j\in I^{c}}
is a
K_{s}
-frame for

Y
. But,
a_{j}b_{j}=0
for all
j=1,...,n
.
Then by Theorem
4.2
,
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
PK=KP
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(a)

(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame.

(b)
If
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are diagonal operators on
\ell^{2}(n)
with diagonal elements
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, respectively, then

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}D_{1}^{2}T_{1}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muX},~~T_{2}^{*}D_{2}^{2}T_{2}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muY}~~\emph{and}~~T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=0.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By Theorem
4.2
(i),
\{a_{j}Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

and
\{b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are
K_{s}
-frame for
X
and
Y
, respectively.
It follows that

T_{1}^{*}D_{1}^{2}T_{1}=S_{1}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muX}\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad T_{2}^{*}D_{2}^{2}T_{2}=S_{2}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muY},

where
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are the frame operators of
\{a_{j}Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, respectively.
On the other hand,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}(f),f\rangle=\bigg\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle,f\bigg\rangle

for every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Another application of Theorem
4.2
(ii),
shows that

\displaystyle\langle T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}(f),f\rangle=0

for every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So (a)
\Rightarrow
(b).

To complete the proof, note that if
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}^{2}T_{1}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muX}
and
T_{2}^{*}D_{2}^{2}T_{2}=KK^{*}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muY}
,
then
\{a_{j}Pf_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
are
K_{s}
-frame for
X
and
Y
, respectively.
Also, if
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=0
, then by (
4.3
),
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\langle f,Pf_{j}\rangle\langle f,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle=0.

Hence (b)
\Rightarrow
(a).
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame.
Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an orthogonal projection and
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unitary operator such that

\displaystyle UP=QU\quad\emph{and}\quad UK=KU.

Then
(Uf_{j},Q,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}-
frame.

Proof.

Let
(f_{j},P,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
be a
K^{p}_{s}
-frame. Then for every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}Q\,Uf_{j}+b_{j}(I-Q)\,Uf_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle f,a_{j}U\,Pf_{j}+b_{j}U\,(I-P)f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle U^{*}f,a_{j}Pf_{j}+b_{j}(I-P)f_{j}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}\,U^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|U^{*}\,K^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U\,U^{*}K^{*}f,K^{*}f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{*}f\|^{2}.

So,
(Uf_{j},Q,a_{j},b_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a
K^{p}_{s}-
frame.
∎

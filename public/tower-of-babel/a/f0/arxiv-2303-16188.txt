Symmetric Rank- k k Methods
Chengchang Liu, Cheng Chen, Luo Luo

Abstract
This paper proposes a novel class of block quasi-Newton methods for convex optimization which we call symmetric rank- k k (SR- k k ) methods. Each iteration of SR- k k incorporates the curvature information with k k Hessian-vector products achieved from the greedy or random strategy. We prove that SR- k k methods have the local superlinear convergence rate of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( 1 − k / d ) t ⁡ ( t − 1 ) / 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}\big((1-k/d)^{t(t-1)/2}\big) for minimizing smooth and strongly convex function, where d d is the problem dimension and t t is the iteration counter. This is the first explicit superlinear convergence rate for block quasi-Newton methods, and it successfully explains why block quasi-Newton methods converge faster than ordinary quasi-Newton methods in practice. We also leverage the idea of SR- k k methods to study the block BFGS and block DFP methods, showing their superior convergence rates.

Introduction

We study quasi-Newton methods for solving the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f({\bf x}),

(1)

where
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is smooth and strongly convex.
Quasi-Newton methods
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

are widely recognized for their fast convergence rates and efficient updates, which attracts growing attention in many fields such as statistics
[

,

,
3
]
, economics
[

,
32
]
and machine learning
[

,

.
Unlike standard Newton methods which need to compute the Hessian and its inverse, quasi-Newton methods go along the descent direction by the following scheme

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t+1}={\bf x}_{t}-{\bf G}_{t}^{-1}\nabla f({\bf x}_{t}),

where
{\bf G}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
is an estimator of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}f({\bf x}_{t})
.
The most popular ways to construct the Hessian estimator are the Broyden family updates, including the the Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) method
[
7
,
6
,
49
]
, the Davidon–Fletcher–Powell (DFP) method
[

,

]
, and the symmetric rank-one (SR1) method
[

The classical quasi-Newton methods with Broyden family updates
[
7
,
6
]
find the Hessian estimator
{\bf G}_{t+1}
for the next round by the secant equation

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{t+1}({\bf x}_{t+1}-{\bf x}_{t})=\nabla f({\bf x}_{t+1})-\nabla f({\bf x}_{t}).

(2)

These methods have been proven to exhibit local superlinear convergence in 1970s
[
42
,
13
,
8
,
11
]
,
and their non-asymptotic superlinear rates were established in recent years
[
47
,
46
,
54
,
25
,
26
]
.
For example, Rodomanov and Nesterov
[
47
]
showed classical BFGS method enjoys the local superlinear rates of
{\mathcal{O}}\big((d\varkappa/t)^{t/2}\big)
, where
\varkappa>1
is the condition number. They also improved this result to
{\mathcal{O}}\big(({\rm exp}\left(d\ln(\varkappa)/t\right)-1)^{t/2}\big)
in consequent work
[
46
]
.
Later, Ye et al.
[
54
]
showed classical SR1 method converges with a local superlinear rate of
{\mathcal{O}}\big((d\ln(\varkappa)/t)^{t/2}\big)
.

Some recent work
[
18
,
45
,
33
,
22
]
proposed another type of quasi-Newton methods, which construct the Hessian estimator by the following equation

{\bf G}_{t+1}{\bf u}_{t}=\nabla^{2}f({\bf x}_{t+1}){\bf u}_{t},

(3)

where
{\bf u}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is chosen by greedy or randomized strategy.
Rodomanov and Nesterov
[
45
]
established a local superlinear rate of
{\mathcal{O}}\big((1-{1}/{(\varkappa d)})^{t(t-1)/2}\big)
for greedy quasi-Newton methods with Broyden family updates.
Later, Lin et al.
[
33
]
provided a condition-number free superlinear rate of
{\mathcal{O}}\big((1-{1}/{d})^{t(t-1)/2}\big)
for the greedy and randomized quasi-Newton methods with the specific BFGS and SR1 updates.

Block quasi-Newton methods construct the Hessian estimator along multiple directions at each iteration.
The study of these methods dates back to 1980s. Schnabel
[
48
]
proposed the first block BFGS method by extending equation (
2
) to multiple secant equations, i.e.,

{\bf G}_{t+1}({\bf x}_{t+1}-{\bf x}_{t+1-j})=\nabla f({\bf x}_{t+1})-\nabla f({\bf x}_{t+1-j})

for all
j=1,\dots,k
.
However, the resulting update of this method cannot guarantee that the Hessian estimator is symmetric, and it requires additionally modification to ensure the positive definiteness
[
48
,
41
,
15
,
31
]
.
Another line of research in 2010s
[
15
,
19
,
18
,
29
,
4
]
consider the variants of block BFGS methods that generalizes condition (
3
) to

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{t+1}{\bf U}_{t}=\nabla^{2}f({\bf x}_{t+1}){\bf U}_{t},

(4)

where
{\bf U}_{t}=[{\bf u}_{t}^{(1)},\cdots,{\bf u}_{t}^{(k)}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
indicates multiple directions constructed by deterministic or randomized ways.
Since the Hessian-matrix product
\nabla^{2}f({\bf x}_{t+1}){\bf U}_{t}
can be efficiently achieved by accessing the Hessian-vector products
\{\nabla^{2}f({\bf x}_{t+1}){\bf u}_{t}^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{k}
in parallel
[
15
]
, these block BFGS methods usually exhibit better empirical performance than the ordinary quasi-Newton methods without block fashion updates.
Gao and Goldfarb
[
15
]
, Kovalev et al.
[
29
]
provided asymptotic local superlinear rates for block BFGS methods based on the condition (
4
),
but the advantage of block quasi-Newton methods over ordinary quasi-Newton methods is still unclear in theory.
This naturally leads to the following question:

Can we provide a block quasi-Newton method with explicit superior convergence rate over the ordinary ones?

In this paper, we give an affirmative answer by proposing symmetric rank-
k

(SR-
k
)
methods.
We design a novel block update to construct the Hessian estimator
{\bf G}_{t+1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
that satisfies equation (
4
).
We also provide the randomized and greedy strategies to determine directions in
{\bf U}_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
for SR-
k
methods, which leads to the explicit local superlinear convergence rate of
{\mathcal{O}}\big((1-{k}/{d})^{t(t-1)/2}\big)
, where
k
is the number of directions used to approximate Hessian at per iteration.
For the case of
k=1
, our methods reduce to randomized and greedy SR1 methods
[
33
]
.
For the case of
k\geq 2
, it is clear that SR-
k
methods have faster superlinear rates than existing greedy and randomized quasi-Newton methods
[
33
,
45
]
.
For the case of
k=d
, it recovers the quadratic convergence rate like standard Newton’s method.
We also follow the design of SR-
k
to propose the variants of randomized block BFGS methods
[
19
,
18
,
29
]
and a randomized block DFP method, resulting the faster explicit superlinear rates.

Preliminaries

We first introduce the notations used in this paper. We use
\{{\bf e}_{1},\cdots,{\bf e}_{d}\}
to present the standard basis in space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
{\bf I}_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
be the identity matrix.
We use
{\bf S}^{{\dagger}}
to denote the Moore-Penrose inverse of a matrix
{\bf S}
.
We denote the trace of a square matrix by
{\rm tr}(\cdot)
.
We use
\|\cdot\|
to present the spectral norm of a matrix and the Euclidean norm of a vector. Given a positive definite matrix
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
, we denote the corresponding weighted norm as
\|{{\bf x}}\|_{\bf A}\triangleq({{\bf x}}^{\top}{\bf A}{{\bf x}})^{1/2}
for some
{{\bf x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We use the notation
\left\|{{\bf x}}\right\|_{\bf{z}}
to present
\|{{\bf x}}\|_{\nabla^{2}f({\bf{z}})}
for positive definite Hessian
\nabla^{2}f({\bf{z}})
, if there is no ambiguity for the reference function
f(\cdot)
.
We also define

{\bf E}_{k}({\bf A})\triangleq[{\bf{e}}_{i_{1}};\cdots;{\bf{e}}_{i_{k}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
,
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
are the indices for the largest
k
diagonal entries of matrix
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
.

Throughout this paper, we suppose the objective in problem (
1
) satisfies the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume the objective
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
L
-smooth, i.e., there exists some constant
L\geq 0
such that
\|\nabla f({{\bf x}})-\nabla f({{\bf y}})\|\leq L\|{{\bf x}}-{{\bf y}}\|
for any
{{\bf x}},{{\bf y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Assumption 2.2
.

We assume the objective
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
\mu
-strongly convex, i.e., there exists some constant
\mu>0
such that

\displaystyle f(\lambda{{\bf x}}+(1-\lambda){{\bf y}})\leq\lambda f({{\bf x}})+(1-\lambda)f({{\bf y}})-\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)\mu}{2}\|{{\bf x}}-{{\bf y}}\|^{2}.

for any
{{\bf x}},{{\bf y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

We define the condition number as
\varkappa\triangleq L/\mu
.
The following proposition shows the twice differentiable function has bounded Hessian under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2

[
40
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose the objective
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is twice differentiable and satisfies Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, then it holds
\mu{\bf I}_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}f({\bf x})\preceq L{\bf I}_{d}

for any
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

We also impose the assumption of strongly self-concordance
[
45
,
33
]
as follows.

Assumption 2.4
.

We assume the objective
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
M
-strongly self-concordant, i.e., there exists some constant
M\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f({\bf y})-\nabla^{2}f({\bf x})\preceq M\|{\bf y}-{\bf x}\|_{\bf z}\nabla^{2}f({\bf w})

holds for all
{\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf w},{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Noticing that the strongly convex function with Lipschitz continuous Hessian is strongly self-concordant
[
45
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose the objective
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies Assumption
2.2
and has
L_{2}
-Lipschitz continuous Hessian, i.e., there exists some constant
L_{2}\geq 0
such that
\|\nabla^{2}f({\bf x})-\nabla^{2}f({\bf y})\|\leq L_{2}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|
holds
for all
{\bf x}
,
{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then the function
f(\cdot)
is
M
-strongly self-concordant with
M={L_{2}}/{\mu^{3/2}}
.

3
Symmetric Rank-
k
Updates

We propose the symmetric rank-
k
(SR-
k
) update for matrix approximation as follows.

Definition 3.1

(SR-
k
Update)
.

Let
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
and
{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
be two positive-definite matrices with
{\bf A}\preceq{\bf G}
.
For any matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
, we define

\displaystyle{\text{\rm SR-$k$}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U})\triangleq{\bf G}-({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big)^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}).

(5)

Noticing that the formula of
SR-
k
update contains a term of Moore-Penrose inverse, since the matrix
{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}
is possibly singular even if matrices
{\bf A},{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
, and
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
are full rank.
This leads to its design and analysis be quite different from other block-type updates (i.e., block BFGS/DFP updates) we study in Section
5
.

In this section,
we always use
{\bf G}_{+}
to denote the output of
SR-
k
update such that

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}\triangleq{\text{SR-$k$}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U}).

(6)

We provide the following lemma to show that
SR-
k
update does not increase the deviation from target matrix
{\bf A}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given any positive-definite matrices
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
and
{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
with
{\bf A}\preceq{\bf G}\preceq\eta{\bf A}
for some
\eta\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\bf A}\preceq{\bf G}_{+}\preceq\eta{\bf A}.

(7)

Proof.

According to the update rule (
5
), we have

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}-{\bf A}=({\bf G}-{\bf A})-({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})

\displaystyle=\left({\bf I}_{d}\!-\!({\bf G}\!-\!{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}\!-\!{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}\right)({\bf G}-{\bf A})\left({\bf I}_{d}\!-\!{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}\!-\!{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}\!-\!{\bf A})\right)

\displaystyle\succeq{\bf 0},

which indicates
{\bf G}_{+}\succeq{\bf A}
.
On the other hand, the condition
{\bf G}\preceq\eta{\bf A}
indicates

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}

\displaystyle\preceq\eta{\bf A}-({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})\preceq\eta{\bf A},

where the last inequality is by the condition
{\bf G}\succeq{\bf A}
.
Hence, we finish the proof.
∎

To evaluate the convergence of SR-
k
update, we introduce the quantity
[
33
,
54
]

\displaystyle\tau_{\bf A}({\bf G})\triangleq{\rm tr}({\bf G}-{\bf A}),

(8)

to describe the difference between the target matrix
{\bf A}
and the current estimator
{\bf G}
.

In the remainder of this section, we aim to establish the convergence guarantee

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})]\leq\left(1-\frac{k}{d}\right)\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})

(9)

for approximating the target matrix
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
by
SR-
k
iteration (
6
) with the appropriate choice of (random) matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
.
Note that if we take
k=1
, the equation (
9
) will reduce to
{\mathbb{E}}[\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})]\leq\left(1-{1}/{d}\right)\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})
, which corresponds to the convergence result of randomized and greedy SR1 updates
[
33
]
.

Observe that we can split
\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})
as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})&\!\overset{\eqref{eq:srk},\,\eqref{iter:srk}}{=}{\rm tr}\big({\bf G}-{\bf A}-({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})\big)\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\underbrace{{\rm tr}({\bf G}-{\bf A})}_{\text{Part}~{\rm I}}-\underbrace{{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})\big)}_{\text{Part}~{\rm II}}.\end{split}

(10)

Since Part I is equal to
\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})
and Part II is nonnegative, the
SR-
k
update can reduce the estimation error with regard to
\tau_{\bf A}(\cdot)
.
To obtain (
9
), we only need to prove

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U})^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})\big)\big]\geq\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf G}-{\bf A}).

We first provide the following lemma to bound Part II (in the view of
{\bf R}={\bf G}-{\bf A}
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For symmetric positive semi-definite matrix
{\bf R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
and full rank matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
with
k\leq d
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big({\bf R}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf U}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)\geq{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big).

(11)

Proof.

Let
{\bf U}={\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}
be the reduced SVD of
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
, where
{\bf Q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
,
{\bf V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
are (column) orthonormal (i.e.
{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf Q}={\bf I}_{k}
and
{\bf V}^{\top}{\bf V}={\bf I}_{k}
) and
{\bf\Sigma}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
is diagonal, then the right-hand side of inequality (
11
) can be written as

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big({\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}^{2}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{-1}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)={\rm tr}\big({\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}\big).

Consequently, we upper bound the left-hand side of inequality (
11
) as follows

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big({\bf R}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf U}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)={\rm tr}\big({\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big({\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}\big)

\displaystyle~~~+{\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}\big)^{1/2}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}\big)^{1/2}\big)

\displaystyle\geq{\rm tr}\big({\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big(({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma})^{-1}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\big({\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big(({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma})^{-1}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\big({\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\big)={\rm tr}\big({\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}\big),

where the inequality is due to
{\bf Q}
is column orthonormal (thus
{\bf I}_{d}-{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}\succeq{\bf 0}
) and
{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\left({\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf Q}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\top}\right)^{{\dagger}}{\bf V}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf R}
is positive semi-definite.
We connect above results to finish the proof.
∎

We provide two strategies for selecting matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
of the
SR-
k
update:

(a)

For the randomized strategy, we construct matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
by sampling each of its entries
according to the standard normal distribution independently, i.e.,
[{\bf U}]_{ij}\overset{{\rm i.i.d}}{\sim}{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
for all
i\in[d]
and
j\in[k]
.

(b)

For the greedy strategy, we construct matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
as
{\bf U}={\bf E}_{k}({\bf G}-{\bf A})
,
where
{\bf E}_{k}(\cdot)
follows the definition in Section
2
.

The following lemma indicates that the term of
{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}{\bf R}\big)
in inequality (
11
) can be further lower bounded when we choose
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
by the above randomized or greedy strategy, which guarantees a sufficient decrease of
\tau_{{\bf A}}(\cdot)
for
SR-
k
updates.

Lemma 3.4
.

SR-
k
updates with randomized and greedy strategies have the following properties:

(a)

If
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
is chosen as
[{\bf U}]_{ij}\overset{{\rm i.i.d}}{\sim}{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
, then we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)\big]=\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf R})

(12)

for any matrix
{\bf R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
.

(b)

If
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
is chosen as
{\bf U}={\bf E}_{k}({\bf R})
, then we have

\displaystyle{{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)}\geq\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf R})

(13)

for any matrix
{\bf R}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
.

Proof.

We first consider the randomized strategy such that each entry of the matrix
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
is independently sampled from
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
.
We use
{\mathcal{V}}_{d,k}
to present the Stiefel manifold which is the set of all
d\times k
column orthogonal matrices and denote
{\mathcal{P}}_{k,d-k}
as the set of all
m\times m
orthogonal projection matrices of rank
k
.

According to Theorem 2.2.1 (iii) and Theorem 2.2.2 (iii) of Chikuse
[
10
]
, the random matrix
{\bf Z}={\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1/2}

is uniformly distributed on
{\mathcal{V}}_{d,k}
and the random matrix

{\bf Z}{\bf Z}^{\top}={\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}

is uniformly distributed on
{\mathcal{P}}_{k,d-k}
.
Then, applying Theorem 2.2.2 (i) of Chikuse
[
10
]
on matrix
{\bf Z}{\bf Z}^{\top}
achieves

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\right]=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}.

(14)

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)\right]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\big({\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\right]{\bf R}\big)\overset{\eqref{eq:EP}}{=}\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf R}).

Then we consider the greedy strategy such that
{\bf U}={\bf E}_{k}({\bf R})
.
We use
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
to denote
k
largest diagonal entries of
{\bf R}
with
r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq\cdots\geq r_{k}
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\geq\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf R}).

(15)

We let
{\bf{u}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be the
i
-th column of
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
, then we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}\big)={\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf U}\big)={\rm tr}\big({\bf I}_{k}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf U}\big)={\rm tr}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf U}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\bf u}_{i}^{\top}{\bf R}{\bf u}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\!\!\overset{\eqref{eq:bigeq}}{\geq}\frac{k}{d}{\rm tr}({\bf R}).

Hence, we finish the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5

Now, we formally present the convergence result (

) for

k
update.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
{\bf G}_{+}={\text{\rm SR-$k$}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U})

with
{\bf G},{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
such that
{\bf G}\succeq{\bf A}
and select

Proof.

Applying Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
by taking

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})]

\displaystyle~~~\overset{\eqref{eq:tauAG+}}{=}~~~\tau_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})-{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}\big({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big)^{{\dagger}}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A})\big)\big]

∎

Theorem

We first show that block BFGS and DFP updates also have the non-increasing deviation from the target matrix.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given any positive-definite matrices
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}

{\bf G}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}

Proof.

We first consider the block BFGS update
{\bf G}_{+}={\text{\rm BlockBFGS}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U})
.
According to the Woodbury identity
[

]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf G}_{+}^{-1}=&\,{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\\

(23)

The condition

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}^{-1}\overset{\eqref{update:invRaBFGS}}{\preceq}

\displaystyle{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}

\displaystyle+\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}\big){\bf A}^{-1}\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf A}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)={\bf A}^{-1}

and

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}^{-1}\!\!

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{update:invRaBFGS}}{\succeq}\!\!{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\!\!+\!\eta^{-1}\!\big({\bf I}_{d}\!-\!{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf A}\big)\!{\bf A}\!^{-1}\!\big({\bf I}_{d}\!-\!{\bf A}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)

Theorem 5.3
.

Consider the randomized block update

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{+}={\text{\rm BlockBFGS}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U})\qquad\text{or}\qquad{\bf G}_{+}={\text{\rm BlockDFP}}({\bf G},{\bf A},{\bf U}),

(25)

where
{\bf G},{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
and
{\bf G}\succeq{\bf A}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sigma_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{k}{d\varkappa}\right)\sigma_{\bf A}({\bf G}).

(26)

Proof.

The condition
\mu{\bf I}_{d}\preceq{\bf A}\preceq L{\bf I}_{d}
means we have

\displaystyle\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}\succeq\frac{1}{L}\cdot\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\mu}\cdot{\bf I}_{d}-{\bf A}^{-1}\succeq{\bf 0},

(27)

which leads to the following inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm tr}\big(\!\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)-{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\\
&={\rm tr}\big(\!\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big)\overset{\eqref{eq:Aneq}}{\geq}\frac{1}{L}{\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big)\\

&\!\!\overset{\eqref{eq:Aneq}}{\geq}\frac{\mu}{L}{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}^{-1}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big).\end{split}

(28)

For the block BFGS update, we set
{\bf{P}}={\bf A}^{1/2}{\bf U}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\big({\bf A}^{-1}-{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)}{\bf G}{\bf U}\\
&={\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1/2}\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf A}^{1/2}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}^{1/2}\big){\bf A}^{-1/2}{\bf G}{\bf U}\\
&={\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1/2}\big({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf{P}}\left({\bf{P}}^{\top}{\bf{P}}\right)^{-1}{\bf{P}}^{\top}\big){\bf A}^{-1/2}{\bf G}{\bf U}\succeq{\bf 0},\end{split}

(29)

which implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm tr}\big({\bf U}{\bf G}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1}\big)-{\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)\\
&={\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)-{\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)\\
&={\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1}{\bf G}{\bf U}-{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)\big)\\
&=\text{tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)\!^{-1/2}{\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf G}{\big({\bf A}\!^{-1}\!-\!{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}\!{\bf U}^{\top}\big)}{\bf G}{\bf U}\!\left({\bf U}^{\top}\!{\bf G}{\bf U}\right)\!^{-1/2}\big)\!\overset{\eqref{eq:bfgs-proof-psd}}{\geq}\!0.\end{split}

(30)

The equation (
21
) implies

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}_{+}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle~\overset{\eqref{update:RaBFGS}}{=}{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)-{\rm tr}\big({\bf G}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1}\big)+{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}\left({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:trineq}}{\leq}{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)-\big({\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}\big)-{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\big).

Taking expectation on both sides of above result, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}[\sigma_{{\bf A}}({\bf G}_{+})]\overset{\eqref{eq:measurebfgs}}{\leq}\sigma_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})-{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm tr}\big(\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}-{\bf A}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\big]\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:trineq2}}{\leq}\sigma_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\mu}{L}{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}^{-1}({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\right]\\
&~=\sigma_{{\bf A}}({\bf G})-\frac{k}{d\varkappa}\sigma_{\bf A}({\bf G})=\left(1-\frac{k}{d\varkappa}\right)\sigma_{\bf A}({\bf G}),\end{split}

(31)

where the last line is achieved by applying Lemma
3.4
(a) with
{\bf R}={\bf A}^{-1}({\bf G}-{\bf A})
.

For the block DFP update, the equation (
22
) indicates

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}_{+}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:dfp-update}}{=}{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)+{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)\\
&~~~~~~+{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\\
&~~~~~~-{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf A}^{-1}\big)-{\rm tr}\big({\bf G}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\\
&~=~{\rm tr}\big(({\bf G}-{\bf A}){\bf A}^{-1}\big)-\big({\rm tr}\big(({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U})^{-1}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf G}{\bf U})\big)-{\rm tr}\big({\bf A}{\bf U}\big({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf A}{\bf U}\big)^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}\big)\big).\end{split}

Take the expectation on both sides of above equation, then we can follow the analysis of the block BFGS update from the step of (
31
) to prove desired result for block DFP update. Hence, we finish the proof.
∎

Appendix A
An Elementary Proof of Lemma
3.4
(a)

Before proving Lemma
3.4
(a), we first provide the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume matrix
{\bf P}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times r}
is column orthonormal with
r\leq d
and the
d
-dimensional random vector
{\bf{v}}
is distributed according to
\mathcal{N}_{d}({\bf{0}},{\bf P}{\bf P}^{\top})
, then we have
{\mathbb{E}}\left[{{\bf{v}}{\bf{v}}^{\top}}/{({\bf{v}}^{\top}{\bf{v}})}\right]={\bf P}{\bf P}^{\top}/r
.

Proof.

The distribution
{\bf{v}}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d}({\bf{0}},{\bf P}{\bf P}^{\top})
implies there exists a
r
-dimensional normal distributed random vector
{{\bf w}}\sim\mathcal{N}_{r}({\bf{0}},{\bf I}_{r})
such that
{\bf{v}}={\bf P}{{\bf w}}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{{\bf{v}}{\bf{v}}^{\top}}{{\bf{v}}^{\top}{\bf{v}}}\right]=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{({\bf P}{{\bf w}})({\bf P}{{\bf w}})^{\top}}{({\bf P}{{\bf w}})^{\top}({\bf P}{{\bf w}})}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{{\bf P}{{\bf w}}{{\bf w}}^{\top}{\bf P}^{\top}}{{{\bf w}}^{\top}{\bf P}^{\top}{\bf P}{{\bf w}}}\right]={\bf P}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{{{\bf w}}{{\bf w}}^{\top}}{{{\bf w}}^{\top}{{\bf w}}}\right]{\bf P}^{\top}=\frac{1}{r}{\bf P}{\bf P}^{\top},

where the last step is because
{{\bf w}}/\left\|{{\bf w}}\right\|_{2}
is uniform distributed on the
r
-dimensional unit sphere and its covariance matrix is
{\bf I}_{r}/r
.
∎

Now we prove statement (a) of Lemma
3.4
.

Proof.

We only need to prove equation (
14
) by using induction on
k
.
The induction base
k=1
have been verified by Lemma
A.1
.
Now we assume equation (
14
)
holds for any
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times k}
such that each of its entries are independently distributed according to
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
.
We define the random matrix

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf U}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}&{\bf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times(k+1)},

where
{\bf{v}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}_{d}({\bf{0}},{\bf I}_{d})
is independent distributed to
{\bf U}
. We define
{\bf B}={\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}
, then we use block matrix inversion formula to rewrite
(\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}\hat{{\bf U}})^{-1}
as follows

\displaystyle(\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}\hat{{\bf U}})^{-1}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}^{\top}\\
{\bf{v}}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}&{\bf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U}&{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf v}\\
{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf U}&{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf v}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf S}&-({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf v}({\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v})^{-1}\\
-({\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v})^{-1}{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}&~({\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v})^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

where
{\bf S}=({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}+({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}{\bf U}^{\top}{\bf v}({\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v})^{-1}{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf U}({\bf U}^{\top}{\bf U})^{-1}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf U}}(\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}\hat{{\bf U}})^{-1}\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}&{\bf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}^{\top}\\
{\bf{v}}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}&{\bf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}{\bf U}^{\top}\\
{\bf{v}}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle={\bf B}+\frac{{\bf B}{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf B}}{{\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v}}-\frac{{\bf B}{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}}{{\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v}}-\frac{{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}{\bf B}}{{\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v}}+\frac{{\bf v}{\bf v}^{\top}}{{\bf v}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf v}}

\displaystyle={\bf B}+\frac{({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf{v}}{\bf{v}}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B})}{{\bf{v}}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf{v}}}.

Since the rank of the projection matrix
{\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}
is
d-k
, we can write
{\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}
, where
{\bf Q}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times(d-k)}
is column orthonormal.
Thus, we achieve

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{{\bf U}}(\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}\hat{{\bf U}})\hat{{\bf U}}^{\top}\big]=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}+{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf U}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf{v}}}\left[\frac{({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf{v}}{\bf{v}}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B})}{{\bf{v}}^{\top}({\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}){\bf{v}}}\,\Big|\,{\bf U}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}+{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf U}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf{v}}}\left[\frac{({\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf{v}})({\bf{v}}^{\top}{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top})}{({\bf{v}}^{\top}{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top})({\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf{v}})}\,\Big|\,{\bf U}\right]\right]=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}+\frac{1}{d-k}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf U}}[{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}+\frac{1}{d-k}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bf U}}[{\bf I}_{d}-{\bf B}]=\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}+\frac{1}{d-k}\left({\bf I}_{d}-\frac{k}{d}{\bf I}_{d}\right)=\frac{k+1}{d}{\bf I}_{d},

which completes the induction.
In above derivation, the first and the fifth equalities come from the inductive hypothesis; third equality is achieved by applying Lemma
A.1
with
{\bf P}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}
and
r=d-k
, and the fact
{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top}{\bf{v}}\sim\mathcal{N}_{d}({\bf{0}},{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{\top})
.
∎

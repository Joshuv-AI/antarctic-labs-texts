Abstract
. We prove a regularization by noise phenomenon for semilinear SPDEs driven by multiplicative cylindrical Brownian motion and singular diffusion coefficient, addressing an open problem in [ CH23 ] . The analysis is based on a combination of infinite dimensional generalizations of arguments in [ BH23 ] as well as careful maximal regularity analysis for semilinear SPDEs and Volterra-sewing techniques developed in [ HT21 ] .



1.
Introduction

We investigate existence of solutions to parabolic SPDEs of the form

(1.1)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v_{t}(x)=\Delta v_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(v_{t}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}(x)+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w_{t},\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}

where
\sigma
is singular but integrable function,
W:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a space-time stochastic process on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
which is white in time, and
w:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous sample path of a stochastic process. Such type of SPDEs are often called stochastic heat equation with multiplicative space-time noise. In the spirit of "pathwise regularization by noise" (see e.g.
[
CG16
,
GG22
,
HP21
]
), we will in this article investigate the interplay between conditions on the additive continuous path
w
and on the nonlinear coefficient
\sigma
, under which (
1.1
) is well posed.

In recent years much improvement has been made in showing how the addition of stochastic processes to otherwise ill-posed (i.e. non-existing and/or non-unique equations) ODEs makes the equations well posed. While this fact has been well known since the early discoveries of Zvonkin
[
Zvo74
]
, showing that the addition of a Brownian motion establishes (probabilistic) uniqueness for certain non-unique ODEs, the research in this direction over the last ten years has been blooming, with the availability of several new tools and techniques that go beyond the Markovian setting. In particular, in
[
CG16
]
Catellier and Gubinelli studied pathwise well-posedness of SDEs of the form

(1.2)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{t}=b(x_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w_{t}

where the stochastic process
w
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
. The authors showed that this equation has a pathwise unique solution for any
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}
for
\alpha>1-\frac{1}{2H}
where
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
denotes the Besov space of regularity
\alpha
and integrability indices
p
and
q
.
One of the novel insights from that article is the importance of the regularity of the
averaged field
, defined as the integral function

A_{t}(x)=\int_{0}^{t}b(x+w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

and how this plays a central role for proving existence and uniqueness of equations of the form (
1.2
).
Another inspirational insight from this article is that it is the irregularity, or roughness, of the trajectories of
w
that seems to provide a regularizing effect for the SDE.
This interpretation of the phenomena has later been highlighted in more detail through the connection with occupation measures and local times (see
[
HP21
,
GG23
]
), through the local time formula

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{t}b(x-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=b\ast L_{t}(x),

where
L
denotes the local time associated to the path
w
, see Section
2
for more details.
These techniques have later been extended upon in different directions, improved, and discussed in a great number of articles, see e.g.
[
HP21
,
GG22
,
Bec22
,
BW23
,
Lê20
,
ABLM22
,
CH23
,
GH22
]
.

One direction of the
regularization by noise
program that has received less attention is the problem of regularization when the SDE has multiplicative noise. That is, one considers a classical SDE controlled by a stochastic process
\beta
of the form

(1.4)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{t}=\sigma(x_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta_{t}+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w_{t},

and
w
plays the role of a potentially regularizing path. Again, in this problem we are looking for a class of paths
w
with the property that that it restorers existence and uniqueness to (
1.4
) for non-Lipschitz
\sigma
, in cases when it is known that the equation is ill-posed if
w\equiv 0
.
To the best of our knowledge, this problem was initially studied by Galeati and one of the authors of the current article in
[
GH22
]
, where the case when
\beta
was a fractional Brownian motion with
H>\frac{1}{2}
was studied. It is there proved that the regularizing effect that the noise
w
may have on the equation also depends on the roughness of the stochastic process
\beta
. The closer
H
was to
\frac{1}{2}
, the better regularizing properties of
w
is required in order to obtain existence and uniqueness of (
1.4
). The analysis is also there strongly influenced by the concept of averaged fields, as defined in (
1.3
), and inspired by the techniques of pathwise regularization by noise. However, the equations them self where considered as truly stochastic, and thus techniques mixing pathwise and probabilistic considerations where used to obtain stochastic strong solutions.
Since the analysis there relied upon the condition
H>\frac{1}{2}
, the case when
\beta
is a multiplicative Brownian motion was excluded, and thus regular Itô SDEs could not be considered. However, in a more recent article
[
BH23
]
, Hofmanová and the other author of the current article has managed to prove (stochastic) weak existence of solutions to (
1.4
) in the case when
\beta
is a standard Brownian motion, and
w
is a regularizing path. However, this also comes at the price of being able to treat only singularities of the form
\sigma\in L^{p}
, as opposed to distributional
\sigma
in
[
GH22
]
. The approach taken there consists again of a combination of pathwise techniques based on averaged fields as in (
1.3
) and local times and classical theory of weak solutions to stochastic equations. In particular, deriving an a-priori bound on Hölder scale based on smooth approximations in combination with certain regularity estimates of averaged fields allows to extract a convergent sub-sequence after which an identification of the limit is done.

Regularization by noise for stochastic equations with multiplicative noise has also been studied in
[
CH23
]
. There, stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) with multiplicative spatial noise where considered (such as the Parabolic Anderson Model), and regularization by noise where proved for equations of the form

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(x)=\Delta v_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(v_{t}(x))\xi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w_{t},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}.

Here
\sigma
is a nonlinear function,
\xi
is a stochastic spatial white noise and
w
is a continuous path (only depending on time), providing the regularizing effect. Pathwise existence and uniqueness were obtained there, even for highly singular
\sigma
, but the techniques could not be generalized to allow for a time dependence in the multiplicative noise
\xi
.

In the current article the goal is to prove a regularization by noise phenomena for SPDEs with multiplicative
space time
noise. In particular, considering SPDEs of the form

(1.5)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(x)=\Delta v_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(v_{t}(x))\xi_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w_{t},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}.

Here
\sigma
is again a nonlinear function,
\xi
is a stochastic space-time noise (to be specified) and
w
is again a real valued continuous path (only depending on time). In spirit of the regularization by noise program, we will combine the techniques of

[
BH23
]
and
[
CH23
]
in order to prove stochastic weak existence of this equation when
\sigma
is a potentially singular and the multiplicative noise is fully depending on space and time, providing an answer to one open problem left in the conclusion of
[
CH23
, Sec. 6]
.

Our analysis is based on arguments related to infinite dimensional stochastic equations. To this end, we write the SPDE in (
1.5
) in its mild form (see e.g.
[
DPZ92
]
), to obtain

v_{t}=P_{t}v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma(v_{s})\xi_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+w_{t},\quad t\in[0,T].

where
\{P_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is the semi-group generated by the heat equation.
Using the translation trick
u=v+w
, then formally
v
solves

(1.6)

u_{t}=P_{t}v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma(u_{s}+w_{s})\xi_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Due to the dependence of
P
on the difference
t-s
, we may view this equation as an infinite dimensional Volterra equation.
In our analysis, we will see the space time noise
\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\equiv W_{t}
where
W
is a cylindrical Wiener process on a separable Hilbert space.
We begin to show how the integral appearing in Equation (
1.6
) can truly be seen as an infinite dimensional Itô integral in Section
3
. We then continue in Section
4
with a discussion of the regularity of averaged fields in the presence of a Volterra integral kernel. In Section
5
we prove two different a-priori bounds of the integral equation in (
1.6
), which is used in combination with the Aubin-Lions lemma to prove tightness. After obtaining potential solution candidates, we provide in Section
6
an identification of the limiting equation based on martingale techniques, concluding our results.

1.2.
Main results

Before presenting the main findings, we will briefly discuss the infinite dimensional setting of the Brownian noise we consider here, and the standing assumptions on the structure of the singular coefficients
\sigma
.

Let
\Delta
be the Laplacian endowed with periodic boundary conditions on the one dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
.
Let
W
be a cylindrical Wiener process on a separable Hilbert space
L^{2}_{x}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, i.e. formally

W_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{k}\beta_{t}^{k}

where
(e_{k})_{k}
is an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}_{x}

and
(\beta^{k})_{k}
a sequence of independent Brownian motions.
We assume
\sigma:L^{2}_{x}\to\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{x})
to be of the form

(1.8)

(\sigma(u))(\cdot)=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}(u)\langle e_{k},\cdot\rangle,\quad u\in L^{2}_{x}

for some measurable functions
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. We will denote throughout the remainder of the paper

\Sigma^{2}(x):=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Our main assumption later on will be
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
for some
p\geq 1
,
and as an example we consider the case where locally

|\Sigma^{2}(x)|\simeq\frac{1}{|x|^{\gamma}}

for some
\gamma<1
. Remark in particular that in this setting, we have for some function
u\in L^{2}_{x}
that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm satisfies

(1.9)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\sigma(u)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}&:=\sum_{k}\left\lVert\sigma_{k}(u)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sigma_{k}^{2}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

We now provide a definition of the solution concept we use here. This is indeed based on classical stochastic weak solutions, but adjusted to the "pathwise regularization" program that will be used here.
It is worth noting here that the perturbation noise
w
that enters the equation (
1.6
) is one-dimensional. This is both to keep the analysis clear but also to ensure that the noise is simply compatible with our later infinite dimensional analysis of the stochastic equations.

Definition 1
.

Let
w^{H}
be a
1
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H
on
(\Omega^{H},\mathcal{F}^{H},\mathbb{P}^{H})
. Denote by
B^{H}
the set of full probability on which the path
w^{H}(\omega^{H})
admits a local time
L(\omega^{H})
. Let
\sigma
be defined as in (
1.8
). We say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t},(W_{t})_{t},(u_{t})_{t})
is a weak solution to

(1.10)

du_{t}=\Delta u_{t}dt+\sigma(X_{t}-w^{H}_{t})dW_{t},\qquad u_{0}\in L^{2}_{x}

if
(W_{t})_{t}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t}
adapted cylindrical Brownian motion on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,
(u_{t})_{t}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t}
progressively measurable process and if there exists a measurable set
S^{H}\subset B^{H}
with
\mathbb{P}^{H}(S^{H})=1
such that for all
\omega^{H}\in S^{H}
, the germ
A_{s,t}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma^{2}*L_{s,t}(\omega^{H}))(u_{s})dx
admits a sewing in
L^{1}(\Omega)
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}(u_{s}-w^{H}_{s}(\omega^{H}))ds\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}(u_{s}-w^{H}_{s}(\omega^{H}))dxds\right]:=\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A_{T})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty.

Furthermore, for all
\omega^{H}\in S^{H}
,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, the solution satisfies

u_{t}=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta u_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s}-w^{H}_{s})dW_{s},

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where the stochastic integral is understood in the sense of Lemma
9
.

Throughout the remainder of the paper, we will make the following main assumption on the relation between the parameters
p
and
H
.

Assumption 1
.

We assume
p\geq 1
,
\gamma_{0}\in(1/2,1)
and
H\in(0,1)
satisfy the following conditions

(1.11)

2H<\left(1+\frac{1}{(p\wedge 4/3)}\right)^{-1},\qquad\gamma_{0}<1-\left(4+\frac{1}{p/4\wedge 1/3}\right)^{-1}.

We will now present the main theorem to be proven in this article.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
H\in(0,1)
,
\gamma_{0}\in(1/2,1)
and
p\geq 1
satisfy Assumption
1
, and suppose
u_{0}\in H^{\gamma_{0}}
. Then for any
\sigma
of the form (
1.8
) such that
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
the problem (
1.10
) admits a weak solution
u
in the sense of Definition
1
. Moreover, for some
m\geq 2
, we have
\mathbb{P}^{H}
-almost surely

u\in L^{m}(\Omega;C^{\gamma_{0}}([0,T];L^{2}_{x}))\cap L^{m}(\Omega,L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\gamma_{0}}))

As a particular application and immediate consequence, we illustrate Theorem
2
with a corollary in the case when
w
is a fractional Brownian motion and
\sigma
is singular in the sense that
|\Sigma(x)|\simeq\frac{1}{|x|^{\gamma}}
for some
0<\gamma<1
:

Corollary 3
.

For
p\geq 1
and
H\in(0,1)
suppose Assumption
1
holds. Given the setting of Theorem
2
, suppose
\sigma
is a singular function, such that
|\Sigma^{2}(x)|\simeq\frac{r(x)}{|x|^{\gamma}}
, where
r(x)\equiv 1
on some subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}
of finite size, and decays exponentially outside this set. Then if
\gamma<\frac{1}{p}
, then there exists a weak solution to (
1.5
) for
\mathbb{P}
-almost all samples paths of the Brownian motion

with Hurst parameter
H
.

Remark 4
.

Our two results above are stated under the assumption that the continuous path
w^{H}
is sampled from a fractional Brownian motion. However, this assumption can easily be generalized to any continuous path with a sufficiently regular local time, see in particular Section
2
and the results therein. As the fractional Brownian motion is a standard representation of such regularizing paths and a familiar Gaussian process to work with, we choose to do all our analysis here with this process.

2.
Pathwise regularization by noise

We provide a brief overview of the concept of averaging operators as introduced in
[
CG16
]
, which will be a central object in the remainder of the article. This section is to be accompanied with the concept of local times, which is briefly presented in the appendix
A.1
.

While the study of potential regularizing effects of perturbations by stochastic processes has received much attention in the past decade (see e.g.
[
CG16
,
GG23
,
HP21
]
and several references mentioned in the introduction), we will for the reader’s convenience begin by citing a result on the regularity of averaging operators associated with fractional Brownian motion. This process with its regularizing properties will serve as our main example throughout the remainder of the article, and will be denoted by
w
throughout.

A particularly interesting relation is that
\mathbb{P}
-almost any realization of the fractional Brownian motion
\{w_{t}\}_{t}
admits a local time
L
(since
H<1
, and the noise is one dimensional) and we have the relation

T^{-w}_{t}f:(t,x)\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}f(x-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=(f*L_{t})(x).

For more information about local times and their relation to the averaged field above, see e.g.
[
HP21
]
.

The following result represents a slight adaptation of
[
HP21
, Theorem 3.1]
to our purposes taken from
[
BH23
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 5

(Regularity of averaging operators)
.

Let
w
be
1
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exists a
\Omega
-nullset
\mathcal{N}
such that for any
\omega\in\mathcal{N}^{c}
,
w(\omega)
admits a local time
L
and for any
(\lambda,\gamma)
satisfying

(2.1)

\lambda<1/(2H)-1/(p\wedge 2),\qquad\gamma<1-(\lambda+1/2)H

we have
T^{-w}f\in C^{\gamma}_{t}C^{\lambda}_{x}
provided
f\in L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover we have for
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R})
the stability property

(2.2)

\left\lVert T^{-w}(f_{1}-f_{2})\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}_{t}C^{\lambda}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert f_{1}-f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}.

In the following example we illustrate how this assumption can be used in combination with Theorem
1
to derive useful regularity bounds that will be applied later.

Example 6
.

Let
\Sigma^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
and let
w
be a
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
one-dimensional fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H
, where
p
and
H
satisfies Assumption
1

and there exists a local time for
\Omega
-almost any realization of
w
. By Theorem
2.2
, we can find a
\Omega
-null set
\mathcal{N}
independent of
\Sigma^{2}
such that for any
\omega\in\mathcal{N}^{c}
we have for some
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in(1/2,1)
the inequalities

(2.3)

\left\lVert(T^{-w}_{s,t}\Sigma^{2})\right\rVert_{C^{0}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{0}},\qquad\left\lVert(T^{-w}_{s,t}\Sigma^{2})\right\rVert_{C^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{1}}

hold for any
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
. Moreover,
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
satisfy
\gamma_{0}/2+\gamma_{1}>1
. Indeed, remark that the condition
2H<(1+\frac{1}{p\wedge 2})^{-1}
ensures
T^{-w}_{s,t}\Sigma^{2}\in C^{1}_{x}
whereas
2H<(1+\frac{1}{4/3})^{-1}
ensures that
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
may be chosen such that
\gamma_{0}/2+\gamma_{1}>1
. The maximal
\gamma_{0}
we may choose that satisfies these two conditions is

(2.4)

\gamma_{0}<1-\left(4+\frac{1}{p/4\wedge 1/3}\right)^{-1}.

Remark 7
.

In the remainder of the article we will treat
w
as a fixed realization of a fractional Brownian motion, for which we have an associated local time that is well behaved (according to the above Theorem and assumption). We will therefore not make any probabilistic considerations with respect to the trajectory
w
, and keep all probabilistic estimates related to the infinite dimensional noise
W
, presented in more detail ion the next section.

3.
Itô integrals in the presence of regularizing paths

The following section is essentially an adaption of
[
BH23
, Section 3]
to our infinite-dimensional setting.
Before addressing the proof of Theorem
2
, let us first remark that even in the case of a singular diffusion coefficient
\sigma
, with
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
, it is a-priori unclear why for progressively measurable
u\in C_{t}L^{2}_{x}
the infinite dimensional stochastic integral

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}

appearing in Definition
1
should even be a well-defined object. Indeed, as
\sigma
is neither bounded nor of linear growth, it is at first sight unclear why in the below Itô type isometry the right hand side should be finite;

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}(u_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right].

We remark again here that
w
is now seen as a realization of a fractional Brownian motion, and thus a deterministic path.
Already at this stage, we will therefore need to harness the regularizing properties of averaging operators as outlined in Subsection
1.2
. More precisely, let
\sigma_{\epsilon}
denote a cut-off mollification of
\sigma
, by which we mean

(3.1)

(\sigma_{\epsilon}(x))(\cdot)=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k,\epsilon}(x)\langle e_{k},\cdot\rangle,

where
\sigma_{k,\epsilon}=(\sigma_{k}*\rho^{\epsilon})\varphi_{\epsilon}
and
\rho^{\epsilon}
is a sequence of mollifiers and
\varphi_{\epsilon}
is a smooth positive cut-off function. Let us denote
\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}(x):=\sum_{k}\sigma_{k,\epsilon}^{2}(x)
. It can be easily verified that for
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}
and each
\epsilon>0
, there exists two constants constant
c_{\epsilon}>0
and
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(x)\lesssim c_{\epsilon}^{2}
for a.a.
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(3.2)

\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u)\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}\leq c_{\epsilon}^{2},\qquad\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u)-\sigma_{\epsilon}(v)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}\leq C_{\epsilon}\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}.

For any
\epsilon>0
, we therefore have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right]\lesssim c_{\epsilon}^{2}T,

meaning that for any progressively measurable
u\in C_{t}L^{2}_{x}
, the stochastic integral is well defined.
In Lemma
8
below, we first show that under certain regularity assumptions on
u
, we have the identification

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{0,T}],

where
A^{\epsilon}_{0,T}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{0,T})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, and
\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon}
denotes the sewing of
A^{\epsilon}
, and we recall that
L
denotes the local time associated to
w
. In a second step (Lemma
9
), we exploit the gain of regularity due to the local time to show that the above sewing is stable in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
, i.e.
(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})\to(\mathcal{I}A)
, where
A=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma^{2}*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. This allows to conclude that the sequence

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right)_{\epsilon},

of stochastic integrals is Cauchy, and thus to deduce the existence of a limit we will denote by
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}
. Note moreover that thanks to the approximation procedure employed in the construction of
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}
, properties such as adaptedness naturally carry over.

Lemma 8

(Identification)
.

Let
w
be a fractional Brownian motion. Let
\omega\in\Omega
such that
w(\omega)
is locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for
\alpha<H
. Let
\sigma
be of the form of (
1.8
) and
\sigma_{\epsilon}
a corresponding cut-off mollification defined in (
3.1
). Let
m\geq 2
and
u
be a stochastic process satisfying

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m}=\sup_{s\neq t\in[0,T]}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert u_{t}-u_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}]}{|t-s|^{m\gamma_{0}/2}}<\infty,

for some
\gamma_{0}>0
. Then the germ

A_{s,t}^{\epsilon}=\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{s}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{s}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

admits a sewing
\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon}
in
L^{m/2}(\Omega)
and we have for any
t\in[0,T]

\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{t}-\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}=0.

Assume moreover the setting of Assumption
1
. Then we have the bound

(3.3)

\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}),

where
p^{\prime}
is the Young convolutional conjugate of
p
.

Proof.

We first verify that the germ
A^{\epsilon}
does admit a sewing (remark that we do not require regularization from the local time in this setting as
\sigma_{\epsilon}
is assumed to be smooth and bounded). Indeed, from (
3.2
), using the simple identity
a^{2}-b^{2}=(a+b)(a-b)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\delta A^{\epsilon})_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{u}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{u}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}-\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{s}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}\left\lVert\left\lVert u_{u}-u_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}(t-u)\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{1+\gamma_{0}/2}.

By application of the Sewing Lemma (e.g.
[
FH14
]
), it follows that

\|(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}-A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}\|\lesssim c_{\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{1+\gamma_{0}/2}.

Furthermore, the germ

\tilde{A}^{\epsilon}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

trivially admits a sewing as
\delta\tilde{A}^{\epsilon}=0
and therefore
(\mathcal{I}\tilde{A}^{\epsilon})_{s,t}=\tilde{A}^{\epsilon}_{s,t}
. We also observe that

(3.4)

\left\lVert A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}-\tilde{A}^{\epsilon}_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}=\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{s}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}-\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}\\
\leq 2c_{\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{1+\gamma_{0}/2}.

Combining our two estimates from above, this allows us to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}-\left(\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{s,t}-A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}-\tilde{A}^{\epsilon}_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim c_{\epsilon}C_{\epsilon}\|u\|_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{1+\gamma_{0}/2}.

Hence, the function

t\to\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(X_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r-(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)},

is a constant, starting in zero, which leads to our conclusion. Towards the second point, note that under the additional Assumption
1
, we apply the Young convolution inequality in combination with elementary estimates to obtain the alternative bound

A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{s}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{s,t}(u_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{0}},

as well as

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\delta A^{\epsilon})_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left((\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{u,t})(u_{s})-(\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{u,t})(u_{u})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}*L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{C^{1}_{x}}\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u_{s}-u_{u}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}W^{1,p^{\prime}}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{0}/2},

where we have used the Jensen’s inequality, and
p^{\prime}
is the Young conjugate of
p
.
From this we directly infer (
3.3
).
∎

In the next Lemma, we show that the robustified a-priori bound (
3.3
) can serve to extend the definition of the stochastic integral to singular diffusion coefficients
\sigma
with the property that
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\sigma
be of the form (
1.8
) and
\sigma_{\epsilon}
denote a corresponding cut-off mollification given by (
3.1
). Suppose the setting of Assumption
1
holds. Let
u
and
\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon}
be as in Lemma
8
above and assume additionally that
\gamma_{0}
satisfies (
2.4
). Then it holds that

(3.5)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}({L^{2}_{x}})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right]=\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{t}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

and for
m\geq 2
the following version of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality holds

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}\right]\lesssim\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon})_{T}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}.

In particular, the sequence
\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right)_{\epsilon}
is Cauchy in
L^{m}(\Omega,C([0,T],L^{2}_{x}))
, whose limit we denote by

t\to I_{t}\sigma(u-w)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}.

By construction, we have the Itô isometry

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right]=\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A)_{t}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\qquad where\qquad A_{s,t}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Sigma^{2}*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

The construction is independent of the chosen cut-off mollification and is adapted to the filtration generated by
(u,W)
. Moreover the so constructed integral is linear in the sense that for two functions
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, we have

I_{t}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})(u-w)=I_{t}\sigma_{1}(u-w)+I_{t}\sigma_{2}(u-w).

Finally, we have the a-priori bound

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}\right]\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m/2}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}),

where
p^{\prime}
is the Young convolutional conjugate of
p
.

Proof.

The above (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) are immediate consequences of the classical Itô isometry and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality available in this setting as well as the previous Lemma
8
. Moreover, for
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we have similarly

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}(\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})-\sigma_{\epsilon^{\prime}}(u_{r}-w_{r}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}\right]\lesssim\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{T}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

where
(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})
denotes the Sewing of the germ

A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}_{s,t}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{k}((\sigma_{k,\epsilon}-\sigma_{k,\epsilon^{\prime}})^{2}*L_{s,t})(u_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

For notational ease, we define
\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}=\sum_{k}(\sigma_{k,\epsilon}-\sigma_{k,\epsilon^{\prime}})^{2}
. Note that by Vitali’s convergence theorem
\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\to 0
.
Recalling the available regularity of the averaging operator in (
2.3
) of Assumption
1
, we obtain immediately by Young’s convolutional inequality

\displaystyle|A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}_{s,t}|\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}*L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{1}},

where
p^{\prime}
is the Young convolution conjugate of
p
. In addition, it is readily seen that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(\delta A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{s,u,t}|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}|(T_{u,t}^{-w}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})(u_{s})-(T_{u,t}^{-w}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})(u_{u})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert(T_{u,t}^{-w}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{C^{1}_{x}}\left\lVert u_{u}-u_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}|t-u|^{\gamma_{1}}\left\lVert u_{u}-u_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}.\end{aligned}

We have by Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\delta A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert(\delta A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}|t-u|^{\gamma_{1}}|u-s|^{\gamma_{0}/2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}

As by Assumption
1

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{0}/2>1
, the above shows that
A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}
admits a sewing
\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon,\epsilon}
for which we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})_{T}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\left\lVert A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert(\delta A^{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2+\gamma_{1}}_{t}L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}.

We conclude that the sequence
\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right)_{\epsilon}
is Cauchy in
L^{m}(\Omega,C_{t}L^{2}_{x})
, allowing to define the corresponding limit as the stochastic integral. Remark moreover that this construction is independent of the sequence of chosen cut-off mollifications, which is immediate by replacing
\sigma_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
by
\sigma
in the above considerations. Adaptedness follows from the fact that the sequence of approximations is adapted by classical Itô theory. Linearity follows from the fact that cut-off mollifications are linear, i.e.
(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})_{\epsilon}=\sigma_{1,\epsilon}+\sigma_{2,\epsilon}
as well as the fact that the classical Itô integral is linear. The last assertion follows as in Lemma
8
.

4.
Regularity of averaged fields and Volterra sewing

As is common in the study of (semi-linear) SPDEs, we will crucially rely on some space-time regularity trade-offs. In the sequel we let
P:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{x})
denote the heat-semigroup. This operator plays a crucial role, as it allows us to gain spatial regularity at the cost of additional time singularities of Volterra type appearing in the integral. In order to benefit from such regularity trade-offs, we need to understand how singularities of Volterra type can be treated in the robustified sewing setting we are concerned with here. This is precisely the content of this section. We will mainly invoke tools developed in
[
HT21
]
to accommodate the Volterra structure. See also
[
CH23
]
where a similar construction has been used in the case of space-(only)-noise.
The following lemma is an adaption of the Volterra sewing lemma from
[
HT21
]
to the non-linear Young setting and to the specific setting in the current article.

Lemma 10

(Non-linear Young-Volterra integral)
.

Let
E
be a Banach space. Let
\eta\in(0,1)
and suppose
A:[0,T]^{2}\rightarrow E
is such that for
\gamma>\eta
and
\alpha,\rho\in(0,1)
satisfying
\gamma+\alpha\rho>1
, then we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{s,t\in[0,T]}\frac{\|A_{s,t}\|_{E}}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}<\infty

\displaystyle\|\delta A\|_{\gamma+\rho\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{s<u<t\in[0,T]}\frac{\|\delta A_{s,u,t}\|_{E}}{|t-s|^{\gamma+\alpha\rho}}<\infty.

Then the Volterra Non-linear Young integral defined by

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}:=\lim_{|\mathcal{P}|\rightarrow 0}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}}(t-u)^{-\eta}A_{u,v}

is an element of
C^{\gamma-\eta-\delta}_{t}E
for all
\delta>0
. Furthermore, for
0<s<t<T
and
\delta>0
the following bound holds:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma-\eta-\delta}(\|A\|_{\gamma}+\|\delta A\|_{\gamma+\alpha\rho})

Suppose in addition that for any
t\in[0,T]
, the limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}A_{t,t+\epsilon}

exists as an element in
E
, in which case we denote it by
(\partial_{t}A)_{t}
. If moreover
t\to(\partial_{t}A)_{t}
is continuous we have

\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}=\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}(\partial_{t}A)_{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.

Proof.

This proof is a simple application of
[
HT21
, Lem. 22]
to our specific non-linear Young integrand
A
. We therefore try to keep the proof short and advice the reader to consult the reference for further details of the lemma. We introduce a new parameter
t\leq\tau\leq T
and define

A^{\tau}_{s,t}=(\tau-s)^{-\eta}A_{s,t}.

According to
[
HT21
, Lem. 22]
, we first need to check that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}^{\tau}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tau-t|^{-\eta}|t-s|^{\gamma}\wedge|\tau-s|^{\gamma-\eta}

\displaystyle\|(A^{\tau}-A^{\tau^{\prime}})_{s,t}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{\theta}|\tau^{\prime}-t|^{-\eta-\theta}|t-s|^{\gamma}\wedge|\tau-s|^{\gamma-\eta}

The first bound follows immediately from definition of
A^{\tau}_{s,t}=(\tau-s)^{-\eta}A_{s,t}
and the assumed bound on
A
together with the fact that
|\tau-s|^{-\eta}\leq|\tau-t|^{-\eta}
and that
|t-s|^{\gamma}\leq|\tau-s|^{\gamma}
.

For the second estimate, we need to use that for any
\theta\in[0,1]
we have

(4.5)

|(\tau-s)^{-\gamma}-(\tau^{\prime}-s)^{-\gamma}|\lesssim|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{\theta}|\tau^{\prime}-s|^{-\eta-\theta},

and the estimate follows in the same way as for the first.
Next we need to check that for
s\leq u\leq t
there exists
\beta>1
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for any
\theta\in[0,1]
we also have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\delta A_{s,u,t}^{\tau}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tau-t|^{-\kappa}|t-s|^{\beta}\wedge|\tau-s|^{\beta-\kappa}

\displaystyle\|\delta(A^{\tau}-A^{\tau^{\prime}})_{s,u,t}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{\theta}|\tau^{\prime}-t|^{-\kappa-\theta}|t-s|^{\beta}\wedge|\tau-s|^{\beta-\kappa}

These estimates will again follow by some simple bounds related to the singularity
(\tau-s)^{-\eta}
together with the assumed bound on
\delta A_{s,u,t}
. More precisely, it is readily checked that

\delta A_{s,u,t}^{\tau}=[(\tau-s)^{-\eta}-(\tau-u)^{-\eta}]A_{u,t}+(\tau-s)^{-\eta}\delta A_{s,u,t}.

We will bound the two terms on the right hand side separately, and begin with the first term. There we use (
4.5
) to see that

\|[(\tau-s)^{-\eta}-(\tau-u)^{-\eta}]A_{u,t}\|_{E}\lesssim|u-s|^{\theta}|\tau-u|^{-\eta-\theta}\|A\|_{\gamma}|t-u|^{\gamma}.

while for the second term we have

\|(\tau-s)^{-\eta}\delta A_{s,u,t}\|_{E}\lesssim|\tau-s|^{-\eta}|t-s|^{\gamma+\rho\alpha}.

Therefore, choosing
\theta=\rho\alpha
we see that

\|\delta A_{s,u,t}^{\tau}\|_{E}\lesssim_{T}|\tau-u|^{-\eta-\rho\alpha}|t-s|^{\gamma+\rho\alpha}\wedge|\tau-s|^{\gamma-\eta}.

Thus, for
\beta=\gamma+\rho\alpha>1
and
\kappa=\eta+\rho\alpha
we see that the first bound in (
4.6
) is satisfied. Furthermore, it is clear that
\beta-\kappa=\gamma-\eta
.

For the second inequality in (
4.6
) we apply the very similar techniques, but now invoking the following bound for the singular kernel with
s\leq u\leq t\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
we have for any
\theta,\zeta\in[0,1]

|(\tau-t)^{-\eta}-(\tau^{\prime}-t)^{-\eta}-(\tau-s)^{-\eta}+(\tau^{\prime}-s)^{-\eta}|\lesssim|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{\theta}|\tau^{\prime}-t|^{-\eta-\theta-\zeta}|t-s|^{\zeta}.

With this inequality, using again that

\delta(A^{\tau}-A^{\tau^{\prime}})_{s,u,t}\\
=[(\tau^{\prime}-s)^{-\eta}-(\tau-s)^{-\eta}-(\tau^{\prime}-u)^{-\eta}-(\tau-u)^{-\eta}]A_{u,t}+[(\tau-s)^{-\eta}-(\tau^{\prime}-s)^{-\eta}]\delta A_{s,u,t}.

we conclude by following the exact same steps as for the first bound in (
4.6
).
We conclude that the conditions in (
4.6
) holds with
\beta=\gamma+\alpha\rho
and
\kappa=\eta+\alpha\rho
, and it follows by
[
HT21
, Lem. 22]
in combination with
[
HT21
, Rem. 19]
that
\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}
exists as an element of
C^{\gamma-\eta-\delta}_{t}E
for any small
\delta>0
. Finally, it identification of the Volterra sewing with a Volterra integral in the case of a differentiable germ
A
is obtained analogously to Lemma
8
.
∎

Corollary 11
.

Let
\sigma
be of the form (
1.8
) and
\sigma_{\epsilon}
denote a corresponding cut-off mollification given by (
3.1
). Suppose the setting of Assumption
1
holds. Let
u
and
\mathcal{I}A^{\epsilon}
be as in Lemma
8
above and assume additionally that
\gamma_{0}
satisfies (
2.4
). Then for any
\gamma_{0},\eta\geq 0
satisfying
\gamma_{0}-\eta>\delta>0
for some

the following inequality holds:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}-\eta-\delta}\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}),

Proof.

As observed in the proof of Lemma
8
already, the germ

A_{s,t}^{\epsilon}:=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{s}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

\left\lVert A^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{\gamma_{0}/2}+\left\lVert\delta A^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{\gamma_{0}/2+\gamma_{1}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}).

It then follows directly by Lemma

that the corresponding non-linear Volterra integral exists and enjoys the bound

\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}-\eta-\delta}\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}})

Moreover, since
\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}
is smooth,

(\partial_{t}A^{\epsilon})_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}(u_{t}-w_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

and therefore, by the last part of Lemma
10

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\eta}A_{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{m/2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

concluding the claim.

∎

5.
Tightness

Combing the stochastic integral considerations of the two previous sections, we are now in place to establish a-priori bounds for solutions to the mollified problem. More precisely, let
\sigma
be of the form (
1.8
) such that
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
and let
\sigma_{\epsilon}
be a cut-off mollification as in (
3.1
). Since
\sigma_{\epsilon}
satisfies (
3.2
) for any
\epsilon>0
fixed, the equation

(5.1)

u^{\epsilon}_{t}=P_{t}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s},\qquad u_{0}\in H^{1/2}_{x}

classically admits a unique solution
u^{\epsilon}\in C^{1/2-\delta}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}
for any
\delta>0
and
m\in[1,\infty)
(refer for example to
[
DPZ14
]
).

Towards the aim of proving existence of the limiting solution to (
5.1
), we must establish two distinct a-priori bounds that will play central parts in the subsequent application of the Aubin-Lions lemma. These two bounds follow in the two next subsections.

5.1.
A first a priori bound

Using the machinery developed in the previous sections, we first establish an a-priori bound for
u^{\epsilon}
in
C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}
uniformly in
\epsilon>0
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\sigma
be of the form (
1.8
) such that
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
and
\sigma_{\epsilon}
denote a corresponding cut-off mollification given by (
3.1
). Suppose the setting of Assumption
1
holds. Let
u^{\epsilon}
denote the unique solution to (
5.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}
. Then for
\gamma_{0}
satisfying (
2.4
), we have for all
m\geq 2

\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}+\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m}.

Proof.

As
u^{\epsilon}
solves (
5.1
), we write the equation on its mild form and obtain

(5.2)

u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u^{\epsilon}_{s}=(P_{t}-P_{s})u_{0}+\int_{s}^{t}P_{t-r}\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}+w_{r})\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}(P_{t-s}-1)P_{s-r}\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}+w_{r})\xi_{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.

Concerning the first term involving the initial condition, we have by Lemma
26
in the appendix that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P_{t}-P_{s})u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{-\gamma_{0}/2}(P_{t}-P_{s})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{x})}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}/2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}.

Concerning the middle term of (
5.2
), we have by Itô’s isometry in the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{x}

(5.3)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\int_{s}^{t}P_{t-r}\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert P_{t-r}\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right].\end{split}

Concerning the last term of (
5.2
), we proceed similarly using additionally the Schauder estimate of Lemma
26
in the Appendix, we have

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{s}(P_{t-r}-P_{s-r})\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert^{m}_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert(P_{t-r}-P_{s-r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\left\lVert\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{m/2}\right]\\
&\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\gamma_{0}}\left\lVert\sigma^{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{m/2}\right]\\
&=(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\gamma_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{m/2}\right].\end{split}

Combining our findings above shows that

(5.5)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}^{m}&\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}^{m}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right]\\
&+(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\gamma_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right].\ \end{split}

Using the a-prioi bound (
3.3
) obtained in the end of Lemma
8
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m/2}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m/2}).

Moreover, using Corollary
11
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\gamma_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m/2}\|L\|_{C^{\gamma_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m/2})

More precisely, we are allowed to use Corollary
11
by exploiting that thanks to the strict inequality on
H
in (
1.11
) of Assumption
1
, we also have

\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}\ast L_{s,t}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}|t-s|^{\gamma_{0}+\delta},

for some small
\delta
, meaning the germ

A^{\epsilon}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u^{\epsilon}_{s}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,

in Corollary
11
actually enjoys local regularity
\gamma_{0}+\delta
, and thus enough to compensate the Volterra singularity of order
\gamma_{0}
as demanded in Corollary
11
.
Going back to (
5.5
), we can therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}_{t}-u^{\epsilon}_{s}\right\rVert^{m}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}m/2}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}+\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m/2}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{m/2}_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}})\right)

which in particular implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{m}_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}+\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m/2}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{m/2}_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{x}}+\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m/2}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{m/2}_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}).

where in the last step, we used lower semi continuity of the norm and the fact that
\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}\to\Sigma^{2}
by Vitali’s convergence theorem. The claim now follows directly.
∎

Remark 13
.

Since Lemma
12
holds for any
m\geq 2
, we can combine it with the Kolmogorov continuity theorem to conclude that also
u^{\epsilon}
is also pathwise continuous, taking values in

. Furthermore, we have that

5.2.
A second a-priori bound

In this section, we set out to establish a-priori bounds of the form

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}^{m}<\infty

for
m
sufficiently large. Towards this end, we exploit modified bounds on the stochastic convolution based on the estimates derived in
[
DPZ14
, Proposition 7.9]

Lemma 14
.

Let
W
be a cylindrical Wiener process on
L^{2}_{x}
, and let
H^{\lambda}_{x}
denote the
L^{2}_{x}
based fractional Sobolev spaces of regularity
\lambda
. Consider the stochastic convolution

W^{\phi}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\phi(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s},

for some
\phi\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x}))
.
Then for
m\geq 2
and
1/m<\alpha<1
it holds that for any
\lambda<1-(2\alpha)
we have

(5.6)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert W^{\phi}_{t}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}\leq c_{r,T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\lambda}\left\lVert\phi(r)\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Remark 15
.

The gain of up to almost one derivative in space (expressed by the condition
\lambda<1
obtained asymptotically for
m\to\infty
) is analogous to the stochastic convolution estimate obtained in
[
Bec23
, Theorem 2.1]
, see also
[
GHN21
]
.

Proof.

The proof follows along the lines of
[
Bec23
, Theorem 2.1]
, but for the sake of completeness let us sketch the proof to the above claim and explain the parameter regimes we obtain in our setting. The proof follows the classical factorization Lemma. Let
r\leq s\leq t
and
0<\alpha<1
. Starting from the identity

\int_{r}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-r)^{-\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\pi}{\sin{\pi\alpha}},

we obtain, thanks to Fubini, the factorization

W^{\phi}_{t}=\frac{\sin{\pi\alpha}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}P_{t-s}Y_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\qquad\mathrm{with}\qquad Y_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\phi(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we therefore have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert W^{\phi}_{t}\right\rVert_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}^{m}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{(\alpha-1)\frac{m}{m-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1-1/m}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert Y_{s}\right\rVert^{2r}_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert Y_{s}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where we exploited
m>1/\alpha
in the last inequality. Concerning the remaining expression, we can make use of Itô’s isometry and the Schauder estimate
26
to obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert Y_{s}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}P_{s-r}\phi(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{\lambda}_{x}}^{m}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha}\left\lVert P_{s-r}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}_{x},H^{\lambda}_{x})}^{2}\left\lVert\phi(r)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\lambda}\left\lVert\phi(r)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}_{x})}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Combining the two estimates in (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) allows us to conclude that (
5.6
) holds.
∎

Invoking this Lemma, the a-priori bound is obtained in a straightforward fashion: Replace again the right hand side Lebesgue integral by a stochastic sewing that is robust and will be uniformly bounded thanks to the a-priori estimate obtained in Section
5.1
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
\sigma
be of the form (
1.8
) such that
\Sigma^{2}\in L^{p}_{x}
and
\sigma_{\epsilon}
denote a corresponding cut-off mollification given by (
3.1
). Suppose the setting of Assumption
1
holds. Let
u^{\epsilon}
denote the unique solution to (
5.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}
. Then for
\gamma_{0}
satisfying (
2.4
), we have for
m
sufficiently large

(5.9)

\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C_{t}H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}^{m}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}^{m}+\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert^{m}_{L^{p}_{x}}.

Proof.

As
u^{\epsilon}
solves the stochastic integral equation

u^{\epsilon}_{t}=P_{t}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s},

we can apply Lemma
14
to obtain that for

the following bound holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}\sigma_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}_{s}-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-2\alpha-\gamma_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Sigma_{\epsilon}^{2}(u_{r}-w_{r})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{m/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Similar to the proof of Lemma

, note that since the condition on

in Assumption

for some small

. This means in particular that the germ

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon}\right\rVert^{m/2}_{L^{p}_{x}}(1+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m/2})\int_{0}^{T}s^{(\delta-3\alpha)m/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

Extraction of convergent subsequence

Summarizing the results of the two previous subsections, we have for sufficiently large

and
\gamma_{0}
satisfying

that

\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{m}_{\omega}L^{2}_{x}}^{m}+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}C^{\gamma_{0}/2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{m}+\left\lVert u^{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{m}_{\omega}L^{\infty}_{t}H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}^{m}\lesssim\left\lVert\Sigma^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}}^{m}+\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert^{m}_{H^{\gamma_{0}}_{x}}.

6.
Identification of the limit

After obtaining a potential solution candidate
(\bar{u},\bar{W})
in the previous Corollary
17
, it remains to verify that the solution indeed satisfy the infinite dimensional equation

(6.1)

\bar{u}_{t}=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta\bar{u}_{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\bar{u}_{r}-w_{r})d\bar{W}_{r}.

Let us recall that the stochastic integral on the right hand side of (
6.1
) is well-defined thanks to the a priori bound (
5.11
) and Lemma
9
. While
the convergence of the linear part is immediate, the main challenge will consist in establishing the convergence of the stochastic integral term, i.e. showing that

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})d\bar{W}^{\epsilon}_{r}\to\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\bar{u}_{r}-w_{r})d\bar{W}_{r}

Note that as we are working in a robustified sewing setting of Lemma
9
and not with classical
L^{2}_{\omega}L^{2}_{t}
based Itô theory, the martingale representation theorem usually employed at this stage is unavailable to us. Instead, we use the more direct approach of the stochastic compactness method (
[
Hof13
, Lemma A.1]
,
[
BFH18
]
) already adapted in
[
BH23
]
to the finite dimensional setting. In the following, we demonstrate that the arguments developed in
[
BH23
]
essentially also extend to our infinite dimensional setting with some care. Throughout this section, let
(e_{j})_{j}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. One can readily check that the three processes

(6.2)

\begin{split}t&\to M^{j,\epsilon}_{t}:=\langle\left(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{t}-u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\Delta\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right),e_{j}\rangle=\langle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\epsilon}(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})d\bar{W}_{r},e_{j}\rangle,\\
t&\to(M^{j,\epsilon}_{t})^{2}-\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sigma_{k,\epsilon}(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})e_{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,\\
t&\to M^{j,\epsilon}_{t}\langle\bar{W}^{\epsilon}_{t},e_{i}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\sigma_{i,\epsilon}(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r}),e_{j}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r,\end{split}

are all martingales with respect to
(\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t}
. Note that since
\sigma_{\epsilon}
is smooth, we have by Lemma
8
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\langle\sigma_{i,\epsilon}(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r}),e_{j}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=(\mathcal{I}a^{i,j,\epsilon})_{t},

where

\displaystyle a^{i,j,\epsilon}_{s,t}=\langle(\sigma_{i,\epsilon}*L_{s,t})(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{s}),e_{j}\rangle.

Note that (
6.2
) being martingales is equivalent to having that for any bounded continuous functional
\phi
on
C([0,s],L^{2})\times C([0,s],H^{-1-\delta})
then

(6.3)

\begin{split}\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bar{W}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]})(M^{j,\epsilon}_{t}-M^{j,\epsilon}_{s})]&=0,\\
\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bar{W}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]})((M^{j,\epsilon}_{t})^{2}-(M^{j,\epsilon}_{s})^{2}-\sum_{k}\int_{s}^{t}|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sigma_{k,\epsilon}(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}_{r}-w_{r})e_{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r]&=0,\\
\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(\bar{u}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]},\bar{W}^{\epsilon}|_{[0,s]})(M^{j,\epsilon}_{t}\langle\bar{W}_{t}^{\epsilon},e_{i}\rangle-M^{j,\epsilon}_{s}\langle\bar{W}_{s}^{\epsilon},e_{i}\rangle-(\mathcal{I}a^{i,j,\epsilon})_{s,t})]&=0.\end{split}

We next intend to pass to the limit in (
6.3
). Note that by almost sure convergence of Corollary
17
and (
5.10

A.1.
Local time and occupation times formula

We recall for the reader the basic concepts of occupation measures, local times and the occupation times formula. For a comprehensive review paper on these topics, see
[
GH80
]
.

Definition 22
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable path. Then the occupation measure at time
t\in[0,T]
, written
\mu^{w}_{t}
, is the Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\mu^{w}_{t}(A):=\lambda(\{s\in[0,t]:\ w_{s}\in A\}),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\lambda
denotes the standard Lebesgue measure.

The occupation measure thus measures how much time the process
w
spends in certain Borel sets. Provided for any
t\in[0,T]
, the measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we call the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative local time of the process
w
:

Definition 23
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable path. Assume that there exists a measurable function
L^{w}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\mu^{w}_{t}(A)=\int_{A}L^{w}_{t}(z)dz,

for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t\in[0,T]
. Then we call
L^{w}
local time of
w
.

Note that by the definition of the occupation measure, we have for any bounded measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
that

(A.1)

\int_{0}^{t}f(w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mu^{w}_{t}(dz).

The above equation (
A.1
) is called occupation times formula. Remark that in particular, provided
w
admits a local time, we also have for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(A.2)

\int_{0}^{t}f(x-w_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-z)\mu^{w}_{t}(dz)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-z)L^{w}_{t}(z)dz=(f*L^{w}_{t})(x).

A.2.
The Sewing Lemma

We recall the Sewing Lemma due to
[
Gub04
]
(see also
[
FH14
, Lemma 4.2]
). Let
E
be a Banach space,
[0,T]
a given interval. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-th simplex of
[0,T]
, i.e.
\Delta_{n}:\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})|0\leq t_{1}\dots\leq t_{n}\leq T\}
. For a function
A:\Delta_{2}\to E
define the mapping
\delta A:\Delta_{3}\to E
via

(\delta A)_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}

Provided
A_{t,t}=0
we say that for
\alpha,\beta>0
we have
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
if
\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty
where

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}:=\sup_{(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}}\frac{\left\lVert A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}},\qquad\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}:=\sup_{(s,u,t)\in\Delta_{3}}\frac{\left\lVert(\delta A)_{s,u,t}\right\rVert_{E}}{|t-s|^{\beta}}\qquad\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha,\beta}:=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\alpha}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}

For a function
f:[0,T]\to E
, we note
f_{s,t}:=f_{t}-f_{s}

Moreover, if for any sequence
(\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t]))_{n}
of partitions of
[s,t]
whose mesh size goes to zero, the quantity

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}

converges to the same limit, we note

(\mathcal{I}A)_{s,t}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{[u,v]\in\mathcal{P}^{n}([s,t])}A_{u,v}.

Lemma 24

(Sewing)
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1<\beta
. Then for any
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
,
(\mathcal{I}A)
is well defined. Moreover, denoting
(\mathcal{I}A)_{t}:=(\mathcal{I}A)_{0,t}
, we have
(\mathcal{I}A)\in C^{\alpha}([0,T],E)
and
(\mathcal{I}A)_{0}=0
and for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\beta
we have

\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}A)_{t}-(\mathcal{I}A)_{s}-A_{s,t}\right\rVert_{E}\leq c\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\beta}|t-s|^{\beta}.

We say the germ
A
admits a sewing
(\mathcal{I}A)
and call
\mathcal{I}
the sewing operator.

Let us finally cite a result allowing to commute limits and sewings.

Lemma 25

(Lemma A.2
[
Gal21
]
)
.

For
0<\alpha\leq 1<\beta
and
E
a Banach space, let
A\in C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
and
(A^{n})_{n}\subset C^{\alpha,\beta}_{2}(E)
such that for some
R>0
,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert\delta A^{n}\right\rVert_{\beta}\leq R
and such that
\left\lVert A^{n}-A\right\rVert_{\alpha}\to 0

\left\lVert\mathcal{I}(A-A^{n})\right\rVert_{\alpha}\to 0.

Auxiliary Lemmata

Lemma 26

(A Schauder estimate)
.

For any
s\leq t
and
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\rho/2+\theta\geq 0
we have that

(A.3)

\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\rho/2}(P_{t}-P_{s})\right\rVert_{L(H,H)}\lesssim(t-s)^{\theta}s^{-(\theta+\rho/2)}

Proof.

We have for
k\in\mathbb{Z}

(-\Delta)^{\rho/2}(P_{t}-P_{s})e_{k}=k^{\rho}(e^{-k^{2}t}-e^{-k^{2}s})e_{k}.

We note that

|e^{-k^{2}t}-e^{-k^{2}s}|=|\int_{k^{2}s}^{k^{2}t}-e^{-x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x|\leq k^{2}(t-s),\qquad|e^{-k^{2}t}-e^{-k^{2}s}|\leq 2e^{-k^{2}s}

Interporlating the above bounds yields

k^{\rho}(e^{-k^{2}t}-e^{-k^{2}s})\lesssim(t-s)^{\theta}(k^{2}s)^{\rho/2+\theta}e^{-k^{2}s(1-\theta)}s^{-(\rho/2+\theta)}\lesssim(t-s)^{\theta}s^{-(\rho/2+\theta)}

Thus, for any
v\in H
we have

\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\rho/2}(P_{t}-P_{s})v\right\rVert_{H}=(t-s)^{\theta}s^{-(\rho/2+\theta)}\left(\sum_{k}\langle v,e_{k}\rangle^{2}\right)^{1/2}=(t-s)^{\theta}s^{-(\rho/2+\theta)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H}

yielding the claim.
∎

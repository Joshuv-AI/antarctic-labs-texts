Improved approximation ratio for covering pliable set families
Zeev Nutov

Abstract
A classic result of Williamson, Goemans, Mihail, and Vazirani [STOC 1993: 708–717] states that the problem of covering an uncrossable set family by a min-cost edge set admits approximation ratio 2 2 , by a primal-dual algorithm with a reverse delete phase. Recently, Bansal, Cheriyan, Grout, and Ibrahimpur [ICALP 2023: 15:1–15:19] showed that this algorithm achieves approximation ratio 16 16 for a larger class of set families, that have much weaker uncrossing properties. In this paper we will refine their analysis and show an approximation ratio of 10 10 . This also improves approximation ratios for several variants of the Capacitated k k -Edge Connected Spanning Subgraph problem.

Agrawal, Klein and Ravi

A classic result of Goemans and Williamson

In fact, one of the main achievements of the Goemans and Williamson paper was defining

Slightly later, Williamson, Goemans, Mihail, and Vazirani

further extended this result to the more general class of uncrossable families,
by adding to the algorithm a novel reverse-delete phase;
a set family

They posed an open question of extending this algorithm to
a larger class of set families and combinatorial optimization problems.
However, for 30 years, the class of uncrossable set families remained the most general generic class
of set families for which the WGMV algorithm achieves a constant approximation ratio.

Recently, Bansal, Cheriyan, Grout, and Ibrahimpur
[
4

analyzed the performance of the WGMV algorithm
for a class of set families that arise in several variants of capacitated network design problems.

Definition 1

A set family

Bansal, Cheriyan, Grout, and Ibrahimpur

Proof of Theorem
2

Here we prove Theorem
2
. We start with stating some simple properties of pliable families.
One can see that if an edge
e
covers one of the sets
A\cap B,A\cup B,A\setminus B,B\setminus A

then it also covers one of
A,B
. This implies the following.

Lemma 3
.

If
{\cal F}
is pliable or is
\gamma
-pliable, then so is
{\cal F}^{I}
, for any edge set
I
.

An
{\cal F}
-core
is an inclusion minimal member of
{\cal F}
; let
{\cal C}_{\cal F}
denote the family of
{\cal F}
-cores.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{\cal F}
be a pliable set family and let
A\in{\cal F}
and
C\in{\cal C}_{\cal F}
.
Then either
C\subseteq A
, or
C\cap A=\emptyset
, or
A\setminus C,A\cup C\in{\cal F}
.
Consequently, the members of
{\cal C}_{\cal F}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof.

Since
C\in{\cal C}_{\cal F}
,
we can have
C\cap A\in{\cal F}
only if
C\subseteq A
, and
we can have
C\setminus A\in{\cal F}
only if
C\cap A=\emptyset
.
In any other case, we must have
A\setminus C,A\cup C\in{\cal F}
, since
{\cal F}
is pliable.
∎

We now describe the algorithm.
Consider the following LP-relaxation
(P)
for
Set Family Edge Cover
and its dual program
(D)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllllll}&&\min&\ \displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c_{e}x_{e}&&\max&\ \displaystyle\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\\
&\mbox{\bf(P)}&\ \mbox{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\delta(S)}x_{e}\geq 1\ \ \ \ \ \forall S\in{\cal F}&\mbox{\bf(D)}&\ \mbox{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{\delta(S)\ni e}y_{S}\leq c_{e}\ \ \ \ \ \forall e\in E\\
&&&\ \ x_{e}\geq 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall e\in E&&&\ \ y_{S}\geq 0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall S\in{\cal F}\end{array}

Given a solution
y
to
(D)
, an edge
e\in E
is
tight

if the inequality of
e
in
(D)
holds with equality.
The algorithm has two phases.

Phase 1
starts with
I=\emptyset
an applies a sequence of iterations.
At the beginning of an iteration, we compute the family
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{{\cal F}^{I}}
of
{\cal F}^{I}
-cores.
Then we raise the dual variables of the
{\cal F}^{I}
-cores
uniformly (possibly by zero), until some edge
e\in E\setminus I
becomes tight, and add
e
to
I
.
Phase I terminates when
{\cal C}_{{\cal F}^{I}}=\emptyset
, namely when
I
covers
{\cal F}
.

Phase 2
applies on
I
“reverse delete”, which means the following.
Let
I=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{j}\}
, where
e_{i+1}
was added after
e_{i}
.
For
i=j
downto
1
, we delete
e_{i}
from
I
if
I\setminus\{e_{i}\}
still covers
{\cal F}
.
At the end of the algorithm,
I
is output.

It is easy to see that the produced dual solution is feasible, hence

\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\leq{\sf opt}
, by the Weak Duality Theorem.
We prove that at the end of the algorithm

\sum_{e\in I}c(e)\leq 10\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\ .

As any edge in
I
is tight, the last inequality is equivalent to

\sum_{e\in I}\sum_{\delta_{l}(S)\ni e}y_{S}\leq 10\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\ .

By changing the order of summation we get:

\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}d_{I}(S)y_{S}\leq 10\sum_{S\in{\cal F}}y_{S}\ .

It is sufficient to prove that at any iteration the increase at the left hand side is at most
the increase in the right hand side.
Let us fix some iteration, and let
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{{\cal F}^{I}}
be the family of cores among
the members of
{\cal F}
not yet covered. The increase in the left hand side is

\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)
,
where
\varepsilon
is the amount by which the dual variables were raised in the iteration,
while the increase in the right hand side is

\varepsilon\cdot 10|{\cal C}|
. Consequently, it is sufficient to prove that

\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 10|{\cal C}|
.
As the edges were deleted in reverse order, the set
I^{\prime}
of edges in
I
that were
added after the iteration (and “survived” the reverse delete phase),
form an inclusion minimal edge-cover of the family
{\cal F}^{\prime}
of members
in
{\cal F}
that are uncovered at the beginning of the iteration.
Note also that
\bigcup_{C\in{\cal C}}\delta_{I}(C)\subseteq I^{\prime}
.
Hence to prove approximation ratio
10
, it is sufficient to prove the following purely combinatorial statement,
in which due to Lemma
3

|I|=|{\cal L}|
)

\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 2(2|C|-1)
,
and
\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 4(|C|-1)
if
{\cal F}
is symmetric.

Suppose now that every hollow chain has length
\ell
.
Then the contribution of the edges
a_{0}b_{0},\ldots,a_{\ell}b_{\ell}
of each chain is at most
2(\ell+1)
.
If we “shortcut” every maximal hollow chain in
{\cal T}
,
we obtain a tree with at most
2|{\cal C}|-1
edges.
However, every such edge might be a shortcut of a hollow chain,
and thus may contribute
2(\ell+1)
to
\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)
.
As the number of edges after the shortcuts is at most
2|{\cal C}|-1
, we get
\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 2(\ell+1)(2|{\cal C}|-1)
.
Bansal et. al.
[
4
]
showed that the maximum possible length of a hollow chain is
3
,
which gives the bound
\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 8\cdot(2|{\cal C}|-1)<16\cdot|{\cal C}|
.

Let
U=\cup_{C\in{\cal C}}C
. To improve this bound of
[
4
]
,
we will show that for any hollow chain with
\ell=2,3
,
among the relevant nodes
a_{0},b_{0},\ldots,a_{\ell},b_{\ell}
at most
3
belong to
U
.
This reduces the bound on the contribution of every hollow chain from
8
to
5
,
and gives the bounds

\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 5\cdot(2|{\cal C}|-1)
and

\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}d_{I}(C)\leq 5\cdot 2(|{\cal C}|-1)
if
{\cal F}
is symmetric.
Specifically, in the next section we will prove the following.

To prove Lemma

Property
(\gamma)
:

For any sets
S_{1}\subset S_{2}
in
{\cal F}
,
if an inclusion minimal set
C
of
{\cal F}
crosses each of
S_{1},S_{2}

then the set
S_{2}\setminus(S_{1}\cup C)

In what follows, note that every

Lemma 10
.

Let
A
be the child of a hollow set
S

S\setminus A
contains no set in
{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
S\setminus A
contains a set
B\in{\cal F}
.
Then
B
contains some core
C\in{\cal C}
. Let
S_{C}\in{\cal L}
be the set that owns
C
.
Then we must have
S_{C}\subseteq S
and
S_{C}\cap A=\emptyset
.
Consequently,
S
has at least
2
children in
{\cal L}
, contradicting that
S
is hollow.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
C\in{\cal C}
and let

.
If
(C\cap S_{i})\setminus S_{0}\neq\emptyset

C
and
S_{i}
cross.

Proof.

We need to show that

•

•

•

It remains to show that

Suppose to the contrary that
C\subseteq S_{i}
.
Let

be the set that owns
C

Since

S_{C}
is a descendant of
S_{i}
.
Since

S_{C}
is not a descendant of
S_{0}

Thus

Subelliptic estimates for the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -problem on complex algebraic surfaces with isolated singularities
Dariush Ehsani

Abstract
We obtain subelliptic estimates for the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -problem on complex algebraic surfaces embedded in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with isolated singularities. W ϵ W^{\epsilon} Sobolev norms of a form, f f , for 0 < ϵ < 1 0<\epsilon<1 are estimated in terms of weighted L 2 L^{2} norms of ∂ ¯ ​ f \bar{\partial}f and ∂ ¯ ∗ ​ f \bar{\partial}^{\ast}f , with weights which vanish at the singularities, as well as weighted L 2 L^{2} norms of f f , with weights which blow up at the singularities.



1
Introduction

We let
X
be an algebraic surface over
\mathbb{C}

embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with isolated singularities.
The main goal of this article is to obtain subelliptic estimates
for the
\bar{\partial}
-problem on
X
. Subelliptic estimates
are an important topic in the theory of the

\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem, and in particular
provide regularity of the solution to the

\bar{\partial}
-problem. Whereas on smooth domains
where such estimates are related to the geometry of
the boundary, on complex spaces with isolated
singularities little is known about the regularity of the

\bar{\partial}
-problem. There has however been significant progress
made in the study of the
L^{2}
-cohomology for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator,

[
9
]
(see also
[
11
]
).

A study of
subellitptic estimates on complex spaces with isolated
singularities was initiated in
[
3
]

with the example
z^{2}=xy
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
The current article builds off the idea of the work on
that example but achieves considerably more
generality. Namely, we obtain results which apply to all complex algebraic surfaces with isolated
singularities.

We work with some of the following simplifications, without any loss
of generality. As the theory of the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem is
well established on smooth manifolds, we will work in a
neighborhood of an isolated singularity. We assume the
singularity lies at the origin and
D^{n}(1)\cap(X-0)
,
where
D^{n}(1)
is the ball of radius 1,
contains no other singularities.

For our main result, we let
f
be a
(p,q)
-form
(
0\leq p,q\leq 2
) with support in

D^{n}(1)\cap X
and with

f\in\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial})\cap\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial}^{\ast})
,
that is

\bar{\partial}f\in L_{(p,q+1)}^{2}(X)
and

\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\in L_{(p,q-1)}^{2}(X)
.
For some
0<\epsilon\leq 1
,
we also suppose

(r^{\epsilon}\log(r))^{-1}f\in L_{(p,q)}^{2}(X)
,
where
r=|z|
. We will often drop the
designation of the form type in the notation
of the Sobolev spaces; thus,
L^{2}(X)
will also stand,
for instance, for
L^{2}_{(0,1)}(X)
where appropriate.
We
establish the

Main Theorem
.

For
f
as above we have

f\in W^{\epsilon}(X)
with the estimates

\|f\|_{W^{\epsilon}(X)}\lesssim\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r^{\epsilon}\log(r)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

The intermediate Sobolev norms are defined
by interpolation
(see
[
1
]
), and the first step in the proof
is to establish
W^{1}(X)
estimates (so that interpolation
can follow). The works of

[
4
]
and the related
[
8
]

are essential in our use of
coordinates which are particularly
helpful in establishing estimates for the
intermediate norms.

The author wishes to acknowledge and express
sincere gratitude for
many fruitful mathematical exchanges with
Jean Ruppenthal. These discussions began
during the author’s employment
with the Complex Analysis Group
at the University of Wuppertal
and contributed in essential ways towards the completion
of this work.

Sobolev 1 estimate

If we first assume that a
smooth form,
f
, is supported in
a neighborhood of the origin, but away from the singularity at 0,
we can follow the establishment of the
Morrey-Kohn-Hömander identity
(see for instance
[
2
]

Proposition 4.3.1 and Proposition 5.1.1), using
integration by parts to show

\|f\|_{W^{1}(X)}^{2}\leq C_{1}\left(\|\bar{\partial}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\right)+C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

(2.1)

The constants,

C_{1}
and

for instance, it is not known beforehand that
a finite number of charts
suffices to cover a neighborhood

derivatives of local cutoffs subordinate to
the charts.

Our strategy is to cover

,
with neighborhoods such that
each neighborhood is contained
in a chart (to which we will refer here as a
resolution chart
)
obtained by a resolution of the singularity
at the origin.
A resolution
leads to a finite number of charts which cover

U=D^{n}(1)\cap(X-0)
.
The charts are of the form

\displaystyle z_{1}=u^{n_{1}}v^{m_{1}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle z_{2}=f_{2}(z_{1})+u^{n_{2}}v^{m_{2}},

\displaystyle z_{i}=f_{i}(z_{1})+u^{n_{i}}v^{m_{i}}g_{i}(u,v),

where
n_{i}\geq n_{j}
,
m_{i}\geq m_{j}
for

i>j
, with
n_{1}m_{i}-n_{i}m_{1}\neq 0
for

i\neq 1
,

f_{i}
and
f_{i}^{\prime}
are holomorphic functions
of
u
and
v
, and
f_{i}=o(z_{1})
, and
the

are local units (are holomorphic with
non-zero constant terms in their series expansions),
see
[
4
]
.
We refer the reader to
[
5
]

Set

From
[
4
]

h:N\times(0,1]\rightarrow D^{n}(1)\cap(X-0).

(2.3)

with neighborhoods, and in each neighborhood

\partial U

The covering of

\partial U
is chosen so that
each neighborhood and its trace along flow lines to the origin

the singularity at 0 with
repeated quadratic transformations, and so the traces along
flow lines of the covering of
\partial U
constitutes a covering
of
U
, written as

\|f\|_{W^{1}(U)}\simeq\sum_{i}\|f\|_{W^{1}(U_{i})},

\|f\|_{W^{1}(U)}\simeq\sum_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\|L_{k}^{i}f\|_{L^{2}({U}_{ij})}+\|\overline{L}_{k}^{i}f\|_{L^{2}({U}_{ij})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(U)}.

(2.4)

Using a family of smooth cutoff functions,

subordinate

We can thus write

\|f\|_{W^{1}(U)}\simeq\sum_{i,j}\|\varphi_{i}f\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}.

In what follows, we shall write

The

We now return to (

In order to obtain estimates for

We obtain estimates for the intermediate spaces in terms of

Lemma 4.1
.

W^{1}(U)
is dense in
L^{2}(U)
.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
denote
U_{\varepsilon}=h(N\times(\varepsilon,1))

The lemma follows from the density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(U_{\varepsilon})

in
L^{2}(U_{\varepsilon})
(
U_{\varepsilon}
is a complex

L^{2}(U_{\varepsilon})
in

L^{2}(U_{\varepsilon})
to one in
L^{2}(U)
.
∎

Using the density of
W^{1}(U)
in
L^{2}(U)

a positive, self-adjoint operator,

,
such that

(u,v)_{W^{1}(U)}=(\Lambda u,\Lambda v)_{L^{2}(U)}\qquad\forall u,v\in W^{1}(U).

Intermediate Sobolev norms can be defined
in terms of powers of the operator

[
6
]
(Section 2.1). We denote these spaces by

\|f\|_{W^{\epsilon}(U)}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(U)}+\|\Lambda^{\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(U)}.

There are some techniques which allow one to interpolate between the spaces.
Two methods, the ”

-method,” can be

From Theorem 15.1 in

6
]

-method, and give equivalent norms. Furthermore,
we can use a norm described as in
[
1
]
to calculate the
K
-method

To calculate the

\|f\|_{W^{\epsilon}(U)}

I(u)=\\

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\|D_{\tau}^{i}u^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}t^{1-2\epsilon}drdt\simeq\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{x^{i}}u^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}rt^{1-2\epsilon}drdt

and proceeding as above leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{x^{i}}u^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}rt^{1-2\epsilon}drdt\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{x^{i}}f^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}rr^{2-2\epsilon}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|r^{-\alpha_{i}}\partial_{x^{i}}f^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}r^{2-2\epsilon}dr.

Thus the second integral on the right-hand side of
(
4.3
)
inserted into (
4.2
)
is bounded by

\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{i}\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}r^{2-2\epsilon}dr.

Similar estimates for the second integral on the
right hand side of (
4.2
) yield

\int_{0}^{\delta}t^{1-2\epsilon}\left\|u^{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{ij})}dt\lesssim\|r^{1-\epsilon}f^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij})}^{2}.

Putting all this together in (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t^{1-\epsilon}u(t)\big\|^{2}_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}t^{-1}dt\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{r}f^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}r^{2-2\epsilon}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{i}\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij}^{1})}^{2}r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}r^{2-2\epsilon}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|r^{1-\epsilon}f\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}+\|r^{-\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij})}.

In the case the metric is isometric
to (
3.3
), we proceed in a
similar manner. We have

\sum_{j}\int_{{U}_{ij}}\bigg(|L_{k}^{i}u^{i}|^{2}+|\overline{L}_{k}^{i}u^{i}|^{2}\bigg)dV^{i}\simeq\sum_{k=1}^{4}\int_{{U}_{ij}}|D_{k}^{i}u^{i}|^{2}dV^{i},

where
D_{k}^{i}
are the vector fields in
(
3.7
).
Let us handle the cases

D_{k}^{i}=\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\partial_{s^{i}}
and

D_{k}^{i}=\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\frac{1}{r^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}
.
We first estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\int

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}\right|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}t^{1-2\epsilon}dsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}drdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\int|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})rt^{1-2\epsilon}dsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}drdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{t}^{1}\int|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime}-t)t^{1-2\epsilon}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}dr^{\prime}dt.

As above, we change the order of integration and
estimate

\int\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime}-t)t^{1-\epsilon}dtdr^{\prime}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}\\
+\int\int_{\delta}^{1}\int_{0}^{\delta}|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime}-t)dtdr^{\prime}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}.

We use

\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime}-t)t^{1-\epsilon}dtdr^{\prime}\lesssim\\
\int_{0}^{\delta}|\partial_{s^{i}}f^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime})^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime})^{3-2\epsilon}dr^{\prime}

and

\int_{\delta}^{1}\int_{0}^{\delta}|\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime}-t)t^{1-\epsilon}dtdr^{\prime}\lesssim\\
\int_{\delta}^{1}|\partial_{s^{i}}f^{i}|^{2}((r^{\prime})^{b_{i}}+s^{i})(r^{\prime})^{3-2\epsilon}dr^{\prime}.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\int\left|\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\partial_{s^{i}}u^{i}\right|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})

\displaystyle r^{2\alpha_{i}+1}t^{1-2\epsilon}dsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}drdt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{s^{i}}f^{i}|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})r^{3-2\epsilon}drds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\partial_{s^{i}}f^{i}\right|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})r^{2\alpha_{i}+3-2\epsilon}drdsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\int\left|\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\partial_{s^{i}}f^{i}\right|^{2}r^{2-2\epsilon}dV^{i}.

Similarly, in the case

D_{k}^{i}=\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\frac{1}{r^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}

we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\int

\displaystyle|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})^{-1}rt^{1-2\epsilon}dsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}drdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{t}^{1}\int|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}\frac{(r^{\prime}-t)}{(r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}t^{1-2\epsilon}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}dr^{\prime}dt.

As above, we change the order of integration and
estimate

\int\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}\frac{(r^{\prime}-t)}{(r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}t^{1-2\epsilon}dtdr^{\prime}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}\\
+\int\int_{\delta}^{1}\int_{0}^{\delta}|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}\frac{(r^{\prime}-t)}{(r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}t^{1-2\epsilon}dtdr^{\prime}ds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}.

We use

\frac{(r^{\prime}-t)}{(r^{\prime}-t)^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}\lesssim\frac{r^{\prime}}{(r^{\prime})^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}

(for
r^{\prime}>t
and
0<b_{i}<1
)
in both integrals to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}\int|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}u^{i}|^{2}(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})^{-1}

\displaystyle rt^{1-2\epsilon}dsd\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}drdt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{\varphi^{i}}f^{i}|^{2}\frac{r}{r^{b_{i}}+s^{i}}r^{2-2\epsilon}drds^{i}d\varphi^{i}d\theta^{i}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\left|\frac{1}{r^{\alpha_{i}}}\frac{1}{(r^{b_{i}}+s^{i})}\partial_{\varphi^{i}}f^{i}\right|^{2}r^{2-2\epsilon}dV^{i}.

The other vector fields in (
3.7
) are handled
similarly and we obtain in the case the metric over

U_{i}
is quasi-isometric to (
3.3
)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|t^{1-\epsilon}u(t)\big\|^{2}_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}t^{-1}dt\lesssim\|r^{1-\epsilon}f\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}+\|r^{-\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij})}

as above.

For the second integral in (
4.1
)
we use

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{i}(t)=

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}f^{i}_{t})\eta(t)+f^{i}_{t}\eta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{r}f^{i}_{t})\eta(t)+f^{i}_{t}\eta^{\prime}(t).

Therefore,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|t^{1-\epsilon}\partial_{t}u^{i}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}t^{-1}dt\lesssim\sum_{ij}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{{U}_{ij}}|\partial_{r}f^{i}_{t}|^{2}t^{1-2\epsilon}dV^{i}dt\\
+\sum_{ij}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{U_{ij}}|f^{i}_{t}|^{2}t^{1-2\epsilon}dV^{i}dt.

(4.5)

Using a change of coordinates

r=r^{\prime}+t
as above
in the first integral above yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{U_{ij}}|\partial_{r}f^{i}_{t}|^{2}t^{1-2\epsilon}dV^{i}dt\lesssim

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{r}f^{i}|^{2}r^{2-2\epsilon}dV^{i}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|r^{1-\epsilon}f^{i}\|_{W^{1}(U_{ij})}+\|r^{-\epsilon}f^{i}\|_{L^{2}(U_{ij})}.

Summing over
U_{ij}
shows the first integral on the
right-hand side of (
4.5
) is bounded by

\|r^{1-\epsilon}f\|_{W^{1}(X)}+\|r^{-\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

Estimates for the second term on the right of
(
4.5
) follow as
those above, and
in terms of integrals over
X
, we have

\int_{0}^{\delta}\left\|t^{1-\epsilon}\partial_{t}u^{i}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}t^{-1}dt\lesssim\|r^{1-\epsilon}f\|_{W^{1}(X)}+\|r^{-\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

∎

5
Approximation by smooth forms

Theorem
4.2

was proved under the condition of smoothness
of the function to be
estimated. We use an approximation argument in this
section to broaden the class of functions (or forms)
to which the theorem applies.

Let us define the weighted
L^{2}
norms

L^{2,-C}(X):=\{f\in L^{2}(X):r^{-C}f\in L^{2}(X)\}

for
C>1
. The motivation for the
weights,
r^{-C}
, for
C>1
comes from the

L^{2}
norms on the right-hand side of
(
2.6
).
Recall that weighted norms for forms are defined by
the norm for functions applied component-wise.
Thus, for instance,
L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)
is defined
to consist of those
(p,q)
-forms such that
each component is in
L^{2,-C}(X)
.

We use the cutoffs
\mu_{k}
from Section
2
.
Recall

\mu_{k}=\mu_{k}(|z|)
is a smooth function
such that
\mu_{k}=0
for

|z|<e^{-e^{k+1}}
and

\mu_{k}=1
for
|z|>e^{-e^{k}}
,
and with the property

|d\mu_{k}(z)|\lesssim\frac{\chi_{k}(|z|^{2})}{|z|\log|z|},

(5.1)

where
\chi_{k}
is the characteristic function of

[e^{-e^{k+1}},e^{-e^{k}}]
. With

f_{k}:=\mu_{k}f
we have

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
C>1
and
f
with support near the origin
be such that

f\in L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)
,

\bar{\partial}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q+1)}(X)
, and
\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q-1)}(X)
. Then

r^{-C}f_{k}\rightarrow r^{-C}f\ \ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ L^{2}_{(p,q)}(X),

(5.2)

and

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}f_{k}\rightarrow\bar{\partial}f

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }L^{2}_{(p,q+1)}(X)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f_{k}\rightarrow\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }L^{2}_{(p,q-1)}(X).

Furthermore,

f_{k}\overset{W^{1}}{\rightarrow}f.

(5.4)

Proof.

The limit (
5.2
) is clear. To show (
5.3
) we
estimate

\displaystyle\|\bar{\partial}\mu_{k}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq

\displaystyle\|\mu_{k}\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\bar{\partial}\mu_{k}\wedge f\|_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mu_{k}\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\chi_{k}(r^{2})}{r\log(r)}f_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(X)},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mu_{k}\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|r^{-C}f_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}

using (
5.1
).

Now letting
k\rightarrow\infty
we have

\|\mu_{k}\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}}

from the dominated convergence theorem.
Thus
\|\bar{\partial}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}}

and a similar
argument shows
\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f_{k}\overset{L^{2}}{\rightarrow}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f
.
When combined with
(
2.6
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) yield (
5.4
).
∎

In Section
2
, we showed

\|\varphi\|_{W_{1}(X)}^{2}\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r\log(r)}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}

for smooth forms,
\varphi
.
Now with
f\in L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)
for
C>1
,

\bar{\partial}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q+1)}(X)
, and

\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q-1)}(X)
,
and with

f_{k}=\mu_{k}f
as above, we define smooth

f_{k}^{\delta}
with the use of mollifiers
so that

\displaystyle r^{-C}f_{k}^{\delta}\overset{L^{2}}{\rightarrow}

\displaystyle r^{-C}f_{k}

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}f_{k}^{\delta}\overset{L^{2}}{\rightarrow}

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}f_{k}

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f_{k}^{\delta}\overset{L^{2}}{\rightarrow}

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f_{k}

(as
\delta\rightarrow 0
).

Letting
\varphi=f_{k}^{\delta}
in the above estimates
and letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
,
gives

\|f_{k}\|_{W^{1}(X)}^{2}\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r\log(r)}f_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

We can now apply Lemma

5.1
and let
k\rightarrow\infty
. We obtain
for
f\in L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)
(
0\leq p,q\leq 2
), with

\bar{\partial}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q+1)}(X)
, and

\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q-1)}(X)
,
that
f\in W^{1}_{(p,q)}(X)
with estimates

\|f\|_{W^{1}(X)}\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r\log(r)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}.

(5.5)

Combining (
5.5
) with
Theorem
4.2
, we have

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
C>1
.
For

f
supported near the origin such that

f\in L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)

and

f\in L^{2}_{(p,q)}(X)\cap\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial})\cap\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial}^{\ast})

we have

\|f\|_{W^{\epsilon}(X)}\lesssim\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r^{\epsilon}\log(r)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}

for

0<\epsilon\leq 1
.

If we fix
\epsilon
, we can replace the
hypothesis that

f\in L^{2,-C}_{(p,q)}(X)
with
\frac{1}{r^{\epsilon}\log(r)}f\in L^{2}_{(p,q)}(X)

as in the Main Theorem. The theorem can easily be extended to
the case of forms whose support contains
multiple singularities of
X
.

As a final corollary we relate

\left\|\frac{1}{r^{\epsilon}\log(r)}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}

estimates to
L^{p}
-estimates.
With

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1

we have for a function,
f\in L^{2p}(X)
with support near 0,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{r^{\epsilon}\log(r)}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|r^{-\delta}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\int_{U_{i}}|r^{-2p^{\prime}\delta}|dV^{i}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{U_{i}}|f^{i}|^{2p}dV^{i}\right)^{1/p}

for
\delta>\epsilon
.
Using
(
3.4
) for each

U_{i}
the integration of

r^{-2p^{\prime}\delta}
converges for

p^{\prime}<2/\delta
, which corresponds to

p>2/(2-\delta)
.

We apply the above with a
\delta>1
.
We conclude that with
f\in L^{2p}(X)
for

p>2
, we have that
f
is also in
L^{2,-C}(X)

for some
C>1
. If in addition,

\bar{\partial}f\in L^{2}(X)
, and
\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\in L^{2}(X)
,
then Theorem
5.2
applies, and,
since
\|r^{-\epsilon}f\|\lesssim\|r^{-\delta}f\|
, we have the
following

Corollary 5.3
.

For a form,

f
, supported near the origin such that

f\in L^{p}(X)\cap\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial})\cap\mbox{dom}(\bar{\partial}^{\ast})
with
p>4
,

\|f\|_{W^{\epsilon}(X)}\lesssim\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|r^{1-\epsilon}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}

for

0<\epsilon\leq 1
.

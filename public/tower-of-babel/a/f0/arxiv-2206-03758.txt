Cohen-Macaulay type of orders, generators and ideal classes
Stefano Marseglia

Abstract
. In this paper we study the (Cohen-Macaulay) type of orders over Dedekind domains in étale algebras. We provide a bound for the type, and give formulas to compute it. We relate the type of the overorders of a given order to the size of minimal generating sets of its fractional ideals, generalizing known results for Gorenstein and Bass orders. Finally, we give a classification of the ideal classes with multiplicator ring of type 2 2 , with applications to the computations of the conjugacy classes of integral matrices and the isomorphism classes of abelian varieties over finite fields.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification

16H10, 11Y40, 13H10, 15B36, 14K15.

1.
Introduction

Cohen-Macaulay rings play a very important role in algebraic geometry.
The associated invariants, such as the (Cohen-Macaulay) type, have been widely studied in the literature.
On the other hand, it seems that a large class of Cohen-Macaulay rings coming from number theory, namely orders in number fields, has been so far widely neglected.
In this paper we work on filling this gap, that is, we study orders in terms of their type.

The typical example of an order is the ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
of a number field
K
.
The order
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
is a central object of study for number theorists since the work of Dedekind and Noether.
For example, we know that all the completions of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
at its maximal ideals have type
1
.
In fact, having (local) type
1
characterizes a larger class of Cohen-Macaulay rings, the so-called Gorenstein rings.
The study of Gorenstein rings started with the seminal ‘ubiquity’ paper by Bass
[
Bas63
]
.
Gorenstein orders in
K
share several properties with
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.
For example, every fractional ideal with Gorenstein multiplicator ring
S
is invertible, or, equivalently, locally principal.
On the other hand, much less is known about non-Gorenstein orders, that is, when there are maximal ideals for which the local type is strictly greater than
1
.

We will now overview the structure of the paper and highlight the main results.
We will work in a more general context than orders in number fields.
We refer the reader to Section
2
for details and missing definitions.
Let
Z
be a Dedekind domain with field of fractions
Q
.
Let
K
be an étale algebra over
Q
.
Let

be an order in
K
.
In analogy with the ring of rational integers

, the maximal ideals of

will be called primes.
Let

be such a prime.
In Section
3
, we will study the type of
S
at
{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is defined as the type of the completion

of

at
{\mathfrak{p}}
, that is,

where

is the residue field of
\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We will also use a global notion of type by setting

\mathrm{type}({S})=\max_{\mathfrak{p}}\left\{\mathrm{type}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}({S})\right\},

where
{\mathfrak{p}}
runs over the primes of
S
.
This is finite quantity because the local types are almost always

.
See Proposition

.
We will produce formulas to compute these types in terms of the trace dual

of

In fact, the presence of such a non-degenerate trace will be crucial for our investigations in the rest of the paper.

The first main results of the paper about the type are summarized here in Main Theorem

In Example

It is known that, among all fractional

The following proposition allows us to compare the size of minimal generating sets of the fractional ideals of an order with the type of some specific overorders.

Proposition 4.5

In Section

, we discussed Gorenstein orders.
A generalization, which takes into account all the overorders, is the following.
An order which is either maximal or satisfies the equivalent conditions of Proposition

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
S
be a non-maximal order. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

For every overorder

of

.

(2)

Every overorder

of

(3)

Every fractional

-ideal.

(4)

The

.

The equivalence of the first three statement is a direct consequence of Proposition

.
The equivalence of the last four statements can be found in

.
∎

Proposition
3.4
tells us that the type measures the deviation of an order from being Gorenstein.
Following this philosophy, a measure of how an order fails to being Bass can be given in terms of the types of all the overorders.
More precisely, we have the following generalization of Proposition
4.6
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
S
be a non-maximal order,
\mathcal{S}
be the set of overorders
T
of
S
, and
d
be a positive integer.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

1+\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\mathrm{type}({T})\right\}=d
.

(2)

\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\dim_{T/{\mathfrak{P}}}\left(({\mathfrak{P}}:{\mathfrak{P}})/{\mathfrak{P}}\right):{\mathfrak{P}}\text{ a prime of }T\right\}=d.

(3)

g_{S}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}/S)=d-1
.

(4)

\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\dim_{T/{\mathfrak{P}}}\left({\mathcal{O}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{P}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\right):{\mathfrak{P}}\text{ a prime of }T\right\}=d
.

(5)

\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{g(T)\right\}=d
.

(6)

g(S)=d
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a non-maximal overorder in
\mathcal{S}
. Then

\mathrm{type}({T})+1=\max\left\{\dim_{T/{\mathfrak{P}}}\frac{({\mathfrak{P}}:{\mathfrak{P}})}{{\mathfrak{P}}}:{\mathfrak{P}}\text{ a prime of }T\right\},

by Corollary
3.6
.
This implies the equivalence of
1
and
2
.
Corollary
4.4
gives the equivalence of
4
and
5
.
Note that for every
T\in\mathcal{S}
, every fractional
T
-ideal

is also a fractional
S
-ideal.
So we have
g_{T}(I)\leq g_{S}(I)
.
Hence
g(S)=\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{g(T)\right\}
.
In particular,
5
and
6
are equivalent.

We now show that
3
is equivalent to
6
.
Denote by
M
the
S
-module
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/S
.
By Lemma
2.14
we have that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
only for finitely many primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
S
.
By Nakayama’s Lemma and an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have

g_{S}(M)=\max\left\{\dim_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\dfrac{M}{{\mathfrak{p}}M}\right):\ {\mathfrak{p}}\text{ a prime of }S\right\}.

Observe that for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of

we have

(8)

\dfrac{M}{{\mathfrak{p}}M}\simeq\dfrac{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}{S+{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}.

Moreover, we have a short exact sequence of

-vector spaces

(9)

0\to\dfrac{S+{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}{{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\to\dfrac{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}{{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\to\dfrac{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}{S+{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\to 0.

As shown in Equation (
6
), we have that
(S+{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K})/{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
has dimension

over
S/{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Hence by Equations (
8
) and (
9
), we get

\dim_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\dfrac{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}{{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\right)=\dim_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\dfrac{M}{{\mathfrak{p}}M}\right)+1.

By Corollary
4.4
, taking the maximum over the primes of

as required.

To conclude we show that
1
and

are equivalent, that is, that we have an equality

g(S)=1+\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\mathrm{type}({T})\right\}.

By Proposition
4.5
, we obtain

(10)

\begin{split}g(S)&=1+\max\left\{\mathrm{type}({S+{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}):\ {\mathfrak{p}}\text{ a prime of }S\right\}\\
&\leq 1+\max_{T\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\mathrm{type}({T})\right\}.\end{split}

For the other inequality, pick any non-maximal
T\in\mathcal{S}
.
For any prime
{\mathfrak{P}}
of

, we have

(11)

\dim_{T/{\mathfrak{P}}}\frac{({\mathfrak{P}}:{\mathfrak{P}})}{{\mathfrak{P}}}\leq g_{T}(({\mathfrak{P}}:{\mathfrak{P}}))\leq g(T)\leq g(S),

where the first inequality follows from Lemma

, the second is clear and the third follows from the discussion above.
Combining Equation (

3.6

\mathrm{type}({T})+1\leq g(S),

as required to complete the proof.
∎

5.
Ideal classes

Let
Z
be a Dedekind domain with field of fractions
Q
and let
K
be a non-zero
Q
-étale algebra. Pick an order
S
in
K
.
Recall that two fractional
S
-ideals
I
and
J
are
isomorphic
as
S
-modules if and only if there exists
\alpha\in K^{\times}
such that
\alpha I=J
.
The set of isomorphism classes of invertible fractional
S
-ideal, the
Picard group
of
S
, which we denote
\Pic(S)
, has a natural group structure induced by fractional ideal multiplication.
Similarly, the set of isomorphism classes of all fractional
S
-ideals forms a commutative monoid that we denote by
\ICM(S)
and call the
ideal class monoid
of
S
.
The ideal class monoid has a natural partitioning

\ICM(S)=\bigsqcup_{T}\ICM_{T}(S),

where the disjoint union is taken over the overorders
T
of
S
, and
\ICM_{T}(S)
consists of the classes with multiplicator ring
T
.

We say that two fractional
S
-ideals
I
and
J
are
weakly equivalent
if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\simeq J_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
S
.
This notion was introduced in
[
DTZ62
]
for orders in number fields.
Denote by the
\mathcal{W}(S)
the monoid of weak equivalence classes of fractional
S
-ideals.
As we will see in the next proposition, the multiplicator ring of a fractional
S
-ideal is an invariant of its weak equivalence class.
Hence we have a partitioning

\mathcal{W}(S)=\bigsqcup_{T}\mathcal{W}_{T}(S),

where the disjoint union is taken over the over-orders
T
of
S
, and
\mathcal{W}_{T}(S)
is the subset of
\mathcal{W}(S)
consisting of the classes with multiplicator ring equal to
T
.

In the next proposition, we summarize some useful facts about isomorphism and weak equivalence classes, and how invertible ideals connect the two notions.
More details can be found in
[
Mar20a
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be an order.

(1)

Let
I
and
J
be two fractional
S
-ideals. Then the following are equivalent

•

I
and
J
are weakly equivalent.

•

1\in(I:J)(J:I)
.

•

I
and
J
have the same multiplicator ring, say
T
, and there exists an invertible fractional
T
-ideal
L
such that
I=LJ
.

(2)

Let
T
be an overorder of
S
.
Let
T_{0}
be the smallest overorder of
T
such that the extension to
T_{0}
of every fractional
T
-ideal with multiplicator ring
T
is invertible.
Put
{\mathfrak{f}}_{0}=(T:T_{0})
.
Then every class in
\mathcal{W}_{T}(S)
admits a representative

such that

{\mathfrak{f}}_{0}\subseteq I\subseteq T_{0}.

(3)

Fix an overorder
T
of
S
.
Then there is a free action of
\Pic(T)
on
\ICM_{T}(S)
with quotient
\mathcal{W}_{T}(S)
.

Proof.

The statements for orders over
\mathbb{Z}
in a non-zero étale

-algebras can be found in
[
Mar20a
, Prop 4.1, Prop. 5.1, Thm. 4.6]
.
The same proofs are valid also for the more general case under consideration in this paper.
Possibly, the only fact used in proving

that is not obvious in our more general setting is that
the extension map induces a surjective group homomorphism from
\Pic(S)
onto
\Pic(T)
.
The proof of this for the case

can be found in
[

, Sec. 2]
, and it works in the same way in the general case, as stated in
[

, Eq. (4.3.1)]
.
∎

Remark 5.2

We start by recalling a lemma from linear algebra.

It follows that the multiplication map

Assume that

If we write

then
\left\{u\beta_{k}+{\mathfrak{p}}S^{t}\right\}
is a finite set of generators over
k
for
W
.
By Nakayama’s Lemma
we obtain that
\left\{u\beta_{k}\right\}
gives a set of generators over
\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\hat{S}^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
By identifying

with its image in

we see that

, which is equivalent to
\hat{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}=u\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
By

as an

-module.
If

.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
S
be a non-Gorenstein order.
Then the following are equivalent:

•

\mathrm{type}({S})=2
.

•

\max\left\{g_{S}(I):\text{ fractional $S$-ideal $I$ with $S=(I:I)$}\right\}=2

Proof.

Recall that for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
S
, we have
g_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})=\dim_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}(I/{\mathfrak{p}}I)
for every fractional
S
-ideal
I
by Nakayama’s Lemma.
Define

m=\max\left\{g_{S}(I):\text{ fractional $S$-ideal $I$ with $(I:I)=S$}\right\}.

Assume that
\mathrm{type}({S})=2
.
By Lemma
4.2
this is equivalent to
g_{S}(S^{t})=2
.
Since
(S^{t}:S^{t})=S
we then have
m\geq 2
.
Now, let
I
be any fractional
S
-ideal with
(I:I)=S
.
By Theorem
6.2
we have
g_{S_{\mathfrak{p}}}(I_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq 2
, and hence by Lemma
4.2
, we also have
g_{S}(I)\leq 2
.
Since
I
was arbitrary we get also
m\leq 2
.
Therefore
m=2
, as required.

For the converse, assume that
m=2
.
Since
S
is not Gorenstein, we have that
g_{S}(S^{t})>1
and hence
g_{S}(S^{t})=2
.
Hence we get that
\mathrm{type}({S})=2
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
S
be an order and let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{r}
be distinct primes of
S
.
Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
be fractional
S
-ideals contained in
S
, possibly with repetitions.
Let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
be non-negative integers such that

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m_{i}}\subseteq I_{i,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}

for
1\leq i\leq r
, where
I_{i,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
denotes the localization of
I_{i}
at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.
Put

J:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\left(I_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m_{i}}\right)\prod_{j\neq i}{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{m_{j}}\right).

Then for each
1\leq k\leq r
we have

J_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}=I_{k,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}.

Proof.

Fix an index
k
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
, by Lemma
2.11
, we have

\left(I_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m_{i}}\right)_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}=\begin{cases}I_{k,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}},&\text{ if }i=k\\
S_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}},&\text{ if }i\neq k\end{cases}

and

\left(\prod_{j\neq i}{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{m_{j}}\right)_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}=\begin{cases}S_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}},&\text{ if }i=k\\
{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}^{m_{k}},&\text{ if }i\neq k.\end{cases}

Hence

\displaystyle J_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(I_{k}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}^{m_{k}}\right)\prod_{j\neq k}{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{m_{j}}\right)_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}+\sum_{i\neq k}\left(\left(I_{i}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m_{i}}\right)\prod_{j\neq i}{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{m_{j}}\right)_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=I_{k,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}S_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}+S_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}^{m_{k}}

\displaystyle=I_{k,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}^{m_{k}}

\displaystyle=I_{k,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}},

as required.
∎

7.
Comparison with other notions of close-to-being Gorenstein

In the literature there are several conditions that a ring can satisfy that make it close-to-being Gorenstein.
In this section, when applicable, we will study them in terms of their type.
As usual, we will denote by
Q
the field of fractions of a Dedekind domain, and by
K
a non-zero étale
Q
-algebra.

7.1.
Nearly Gorenstein

Let
R
be a ring and
M
an
R
-module.
The
trace ideal
of
M
is defined as

\tr M=\sum_{f\in\Hom_{R}(M,R)}f(M).

See for example
[
DKT21a
, Def. 2.1]
.
Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with canonical module
C
.
Then
R
is called
nearly Gorenstein
if
\tr C
contains
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Such a notion was introduced and studied in
[
HHS19
]
.

If
S
is an order in
K
then by Lemma
2.1
we have an identification

\tr S^{t}=\sum_{f\in(S:S^{t})}f\cdot S^{t}=S^{t}(S:S^{t}).

Since we are going to work locally, in order to lighten the notation, we set
R=\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{p}}\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a prime of
S
.
In particular,
R^{t}
is the canonical module of
R
and
\mathrm{type}({R})=\mathrm{type}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}({S})
by Proposition
3.1
.
Using this notation,
R
is nearly Gorenstein if and only if

{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R^{t}(R:R^{t}).

Observe that the inclusion is strict if and only if
R^{t}(R:R^{t})=R
, that is,
\mathrm{type}({R})=1
, or equivalently
R
is Gorenstein by Proposition
3.4
.

If
R
is nearly Gorenstein, but not Gorenstein, then the type of
R
is

\mathrm{type}({R})=\dim_{R/{\mathfrak{m}}}\frac{R^{t}}{(R^{t})^{2}(R:R^{t})}.

By Lemma
2.4
.
3
, we get the following equalities of
R/{\mathfrak{m}}
-vector spaces

\frac{R^{t}}{(R^{t})^{2}(R:R^{t})}=\frac{R^{t}}{(R:R^{t})^{t}(R:R^{t})}=\frac{R^{t}}{T^{t}},

where
T
is the multiplicator ring of
(R:R^{t})
.
Similarly, by multiplying
m=R^{t}(R:R^{t})
by
R^{t}
, we get that
{\mathfrak{m}}R^{t}=T^{t}
.
Hence
(R:{\mathfrak{m}})=T
, which by Lemma
2.2
leads to
T=({\mathfrak{m}}:{\mathfrak{m}})
.
We summarize these observations in the following proposition.

Proposition 7.1
.

If
R
is nearly Gorenstein, but not Gorenstein, then

\mathrm{type}({R})=\dim_{R/{\mathfrak{m}}}\frac{R^{t}}{(R^{t})^{2}(R:R^{t})}.

Moreover, the multiplicator ring
({\mathfrak{m}}:{\mathfrak{m}})
of
{\mathfrak{m}}
equals the multiplicator ring of
(R:R^{t})
.

7.2.
Almost Gorenstein and Weakly almost Gorenstein

Let
R
be a local Cohen-Macaulay ring
R
of Krull dimension
1
with normalization
\overline{R}
and conductor
{\mathfrak{f}}=(R:\overline{R})
.
We say that
R
is
almost Gorenstein
if

\ell_{R}\left(\frac{\overline{R}}{R}\right)=\ell_{R}\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)+\mathrm{type}({R})-1,

where
\ell_{R}(-)
is the length as an
R
-module.
The study of almost Gorenstein rings, where this definition was given, started with
[
BF97
]
.
It was extended in
[
GMP13
]
and
[
GTT15
]
.
It is known that almost Gorenstein rings are nearly Gorenstein, see
[
HHS19
, Prop. 6.1]
.

Let
S
be an order in
K
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
a prime of
S
.
We work locally, and again we simplify the notation by
setting
R=\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{p}}\hat{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\overline{R}=\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{K,{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
{\mathfrak{f}}=(R:\overline{R})
.

We can rephrase the definition of the type of
R
using the length as

\mathrm{type}({R})=\ell_{R}\left(\frac{R^{t}}{{\mathfrak{m}}R^{t}}\right)=\ell_{R}\left(\frac{(R:{\mathfrak{m}})}{R}\right),

where the second equality follow from Lemma
2.4
.
3
and
4
.
Using that the length is an additive function on short exact sequences,
we get the following proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

The order
R
is almost Gorenstein if and only if

\ell_{R}\left(\frac{\overline{R}}{R}\right)=\ell_{R}\left(\frac{(R:{\mathfrak{m}})}{{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)-1.

Related to the definition of almost Gorenstein, there is the notion of
weakly almost Gorenstein rings
introduced in
[
DKT21b
]
.
In Krull dimension one, the two notions coincide, see
[
DKT21b
, Thm. 1.3]
.

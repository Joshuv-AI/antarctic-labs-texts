Characteristic functions and Colligations
Neeru Bala, Santanu Dey and Reshmi M. N

Abstract
. The characteristic function of row contractions and liftings of row contractions are complete invariants up to unitary equivalence for row contractions and liftings of row contractions, respectively. We provide alternate proofs for these using colligations. A class of co-isometric observable colligations with input spaces of finite dimension is characterized. Blaschke factor based transformations of the characteristic function of lifting are studied.

1.
Introduction

One of the well studied classes of operators on Hilbert spaces is the class of normal operators and the spectral theorem for normal operators plays a vital role in its prominence. For a bounded linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space, the resolvent function is
(T-\lambda I)^{-1}
(for complex
\lambda
where the operator
(T-\lambda I)^{-1}
is defined). The spectrum of an operator and the spectral theorem is directly related to the resolvent function, which is an operator valued analytic function. The resolvent function is very important in the study of normal operators, but it provides relatively less information about the structure of non-normal operators. Nagy and Foias
[
8
]
associated a new operator valued analytic function to contractions on Hilbert spaces, called the characteristic function, that gives valuable information about the structure of the contraction. Since we can multiply every non-zero operator on a Hilbert space with suitable constant to obtain a non-zero contraction on the space, Nagy-Foias characteristic function is useful in studying non-normal operators. The book by Nagy and Foias
[
8
]
is a great reference for the results related to contraction and dilation theory. Further, Popescu
[
10
,
11
]
explored the characteristic function in the non-commuting setup for row contractions.

Let
H_{T}
be a Hilbert space such that
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H_{T})
. Then
\underline{T}=(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{d})
is called a
row contraction
, if
\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\leq I
, which is equivalent to say that the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&T_{2}&\ldots&T_{d}\end{bmatrix}
is a contraction. The characteristic function
M_{T}:\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{T}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,T}
of the row contraction
\underline{T}
is defined by

(1.1)

{M_{T}}=-I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{T}+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{*,T})({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{T}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{T}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{T}^{*}))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{T}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{T}),

where
\mathcal{D}_{T}
and
\mathcal{D}_{*,T}
are the defect spaces associated to
\underline{T}
. The articles

[
10
,
11
,
12
]
are a few references on the fundamentals of dilation theory for row contractions.

For a row contraction
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
on
H_{C}
, we say
\underline{E}
is a
lifting
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
on
H_{E}=H_{C}\oplus H_{A}
, if

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,d

where
B_{i}
and
A_{i}
are some operators. Contractive liftings is crucial in the study of non-commutative theory. The book by Foias and Frazho
[
6
]
is an excellent source of topics that demonstrate the theoritical and practical aspects of contractive lifting.
S. Dey and R. Gohm
[
3
]
associated a characteristic function to contractive liftings and studied its properties in various settings. Also, different subclasses of contractive liftings are studied in
[
3
,
5
,
4
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A contractive lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
is called
minimal,
if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{E}=\overline{span}\{E_{\alpha}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\;\;\text{for all }\,\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}\}.

The paper is organised as follows: In section
2
, we fix some notations and recall the results that are used in subsequent sections. In section
3
, we show that, if Popescu characteristic function (
1.1
) coincides for two different completely non-co-isometric row contractions, then the row contractions are unitarily equivalent. This is already proved by Popescu
[
11
]
, but here we give a different proof using colligation matrix. In section
4
, we show that the characteristic function for minimal contractive liftings coincides if and only if the liftings are unitarily equivalent, again using colligation matrix. The characteristic functions of some examples of liftings are computed and the corresponding colligations are derived in section
5.
Some properties of characteristic functions of liftings are discussed.

Let
\underline{A}
be a row contraction with finite dimensional defect space
\mathcal{D}_{A}.
A class of co-isometric observable colligations with basic operator
\underline{A}^{*}
and the dimension of the input space equal to the dimension of
\mathcal{D}_{A},
is characterized in section
6
. For a contraction
T
on a Hilbert space and any
a
in the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, define the operator
T_{a}:=(T-aI)(I-\overline{a}T)^{-1}.
If
E
is a minimal contractive lifting of a row contraction
C,
it is shown in section
7
that
E_{a}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
C_{a}.
The symbol
\theta_{C_{a},E_{a}}(\lambda)
of the characteristic function of lifting
E_{a}
coincides with
\theta_{C,E}(\frac{\lambda+a}{1+\overline{a}\lambda}).

2.
Preliminaries

The full Fock space on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{d}):=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{d}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes 2}\oplus\cdots\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes n}\oplus\cdots,

where
e_{0}=1\oplus 0\oplus 0\oplus\cdots
is called the
vaccum vector
. To simplify the notation, we use
\Gamma
, for
\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. We denote the standard ordered basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
by
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d}\}
. For
x\in\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, the operators

L_{i}x=e_{i}\otimes x,\text{ and }R_{i}x=x\otimes e_{i},\,i=1,2,\ldots,d,

are called the
left creation
and
right creation
operators, respectively. We use the notation
\Lambda
for the set
\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}:=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\bigcup}}\Lambda^{n}
, where
\Lambda^{0}=\{0\}
. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
,
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdot+\alpha_{n}
.

For a row contraction
\underline{T}=(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{d})
on
H_{T}
, the
defect operators
and
defect spaces
associated with
\underline{T}
are defined by

\displaystyle D_{T}

\displaystyle=(I-\underline{T}^{*}\underline{T})^{1/2}:\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}H_{T}\rightarrow\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}H_{T},

\displaystyle D_{*,T}

\displaystyle=(I-\underline{T}\,\underline{T}^{*})^{1/2}:H_{T}\rightarrow H_{T},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{ran}}\,D_{T},\,\mathcal{D}_{*,T}=\overline{\text{ran}}\,D_{*,T},

respectively. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
,
T_{\alpha}=T_{\alpha_{1}}\ldots T_{\alpha_{n}}
. We say the row contraction
\underline{T}
is
completely non-co-isometric
(or c.n.c. in short), if

H_{T}^{1}=\left\{h\in H_{T}:\underset{|\alpha|=n}{\sum}\|T_{\alpha}^{*}h\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}=\{0\}.

It is easy to observe that
D_{*,T}
is an injective operator, if
\underline{T}
is a c.n.c. row contraction.

A contractive colligation on
d
-variable is an operator

W=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{1}&B_{1}\\
A_{2}&B_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
A_{d}&B_{d}\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}:H_{1}\oplus H_{2}\rightarrow H_{1}^{d}\oplus H_{3},

where
H_{1},H_{2}
and
H_{3}
are Hilbert spaces. The operator
A is called the
basic operator
of the colligation and the function

(2.1)

\theta_{W}=D+C({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{1}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{1}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes A))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{1}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes B)

is called the associated
transfer function
of the colligation. The spaces
H_{1},H_{2}
and
H_{3}
are called
state space, input space
and
output space,
respectively. Note that
\theta_{W}\in\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{B}(H_{2},H_{3})
. We say
W
is
observable
, if the observability operator
\mathcal{O}_{C,A}:H_{1}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes H_{3}
defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{C,A}=C({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{1}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{1}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes A))^{-1}

is an injective operator.

For two Hilbert spaces
H_{1},H_{2}
, let
\mathcal{M}_{nc,d}(H_{1},H_{2})
consists of functions
\theta\in\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{B}(H_{1},H_{2})
for which
M_{\theta}f=\theta f
is a well defined bounded linear operator from
\Gamma\otimes H_{1}
to
\Gamma\otimes H_{2}
. From
[
2
]
, we know that
M_{\theta}(L_{i}\otimes I_{H_{1}})=(L_{i}\otimes I_{H_{2}})M_{\theta}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{nc,d}(H_{1},H_{2})=\{\theta\in\mathcal{M}_{nc,d}(H_{1},H_{2}):\|M_{\theta}\|\leq 1\}.

For a contractive colligation matrix
W,
we know that
\theta_{W}\in\mathcal{S}_{nc,d}
, by
[
2
]
.

We use the notion of unitary equivalence of two colligation matrices defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

[
2
, Page 527-528]

Let

W=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}:H_{1}\oplus H_{2}\rightarrow H_{1}^{d}\oplus H_{3}

and

W^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&B^{\prime}\\
C^{\prime}&D^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}:H_{1}^{\prime}\oplus H_{2}\rightarrow H_{1}^{\prime d}\oplus H_{3}

be two colligations. These colligations are unitarily equivalent if there exist a unitary operator
U:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{1}^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U&0\\
0&I_{H_{3}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&B^{\prime}\\
C^{\prime}&D^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}U&0\\
0&I_{H_{2}}\end{bmatrix}.

The following result gives the invariance of transfer function under unitarily equivalent colligation matrices.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
2
, Corollary 3.9]

Any two observable, co-isometric colligation matrices
W
and
W^{\prime}
for same associated transfer function
\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{nc,d}(H_{1},H_{2})
are unitarily equivalent.

3.
Characteristic function for c.n.c. row contraction

In this section, our aim is to show that two c.n.c. row contractions with same characteristic function (in the sense of Popescu
[
11
]
) are unitarily equivalent.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be Hilbert spaces.
A bounded operator
M:\Gamma\otimes H_{1}\to\Gamma\otimes H_{2}
is said to be
multi-analytic
if

M_{C,E}(L_{i}\otimes I)=(L_{i}\otimes I)M_{C,E}

for all
i=1,\ldots,d.
Such an operator is determined its symbol
\theta:=M|_{e_{0}\otimes H_{1}}.

First we recall the characteristic function for a c.n.c. row contraction defined by Popescu
[
11
]
.
Let
\underline{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{d})
be a c.n.c. row contraction on
H_{A}
. Then the
characteristic function

M_{A}:\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{A}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
of the row contraction
\underline{A}
is defined by the multi-analytic operator

(3.1)

M_{A}=-I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{*,A})(I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}),

and its symbol
\theta_{A}:\mathcal{D}_{A}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
is
\theta_{A}=M_{A}|_{e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{A}}.
Then

(3.2)

\theta_{A}=-e_{0}\otimes\underline{A}+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{*,A})({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}).

We can identify
\mathcal{D}_{A}
with closed linear span of elements of the form
e_{0}\otimes D_{A}h
for
h\in H
. Then
\theta_{A}
can be written as

\theta_{A}=-\underline{A}+D_{*,A}(I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}).

It is easy to see that
\theta_{A}
is also the transfer function for the following colligation matrix

(3.3)

\displaystyle W_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}\\
D_{*,A}&-\underline{A}\end{bmatrix}.

Also
\theta_{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{nc,d}
for a c.n.c. row contraction
\underline{A}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that
W_{A}
is co-isometric (that is
W_{A}W_{A}^{*}=I
) for any row contraction
\underline{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{d})
.

We begin with a simple observation about the colligation matrix
W_{A}
defined in Equation (
3.3
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\underline{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{d})
be a c.n.c. row contraction. Then
W_{A}
is observable.

Proof.

Since
A
is a c.n.c. row contraction, we have
D_{*,A}
is an injective operator. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{A^{*},D_{*,A}}:\mathcal{D}_{A}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
, defined by

\mathcal{O}_{A^{*},D_{*,A}}:=D_{*,A}(I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1},

is injective and hence
W_{A}
is observable.
∎

We say that two multi-analytic operators
M_{1}:\Gamma\otimes H_{1}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\tilde{H}_{1}
and
M_{2}:\Gamma\otimes H_{2}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\tilde{H}_{2}

coincide,
if there exist unitary operators
U:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
and
\tilde{U}:\tilde{H}_{1}\rightarrow\tilde{H}_{2}
such that

(3.4)

(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\tilde{U})M_{1}=M_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes U).

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be the symbols of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
respectively.
The Equation (
3.4
) is equivalent to

(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\tilde{U})\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}U.

In this case we also say that
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}

coincides.

If
\underline{A}
and
\underline{A}^{\prime}
be two unitarily equivalent c.n.c. row contractions, then using direct computation, we know that
\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{A^{\prime}}
coincides (cf.
[
11
]
). For the converse, Popescu used the functional model. Here we present an alternate proof for the converse using colligation matrix.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\underline{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots A_{d})
and
\underline{A}^{\prime}=(A_{1}^{\prime},A_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{d}^{\prime})
be two c.n.c. row contractions on
H_{A}
and
H_{A^{\prime}}
. If
\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{A^{\prime}}
coincide then
\underline{A}
and
\underline{A}^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{A^{\prime}}
coincide. Then there exist two unitaries
U_{1}:\mathcal{D}_{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{A^{\prime}}
and
U_{2}:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,A^{\prime}}
such that
(I_{\Gamma}\otimes U_{2})\theta_{A}=\theta_{A^{\prime}}U_{1}
.

Using Equation (
3.3
), we know that
(I_{\Gamma}\otimes U_{2})\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{A^{\prime}}U_{1}
are the transfer functions of the following colligation matrices,

\displaystyle W_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}\\
U_{2}D_{*,A}&-U_{2}\underline{A}\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }W_{A^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\underline{A}^{\prime}}^{*}&D_{{\underline{A}^{\prime}}}U_{1}\\
D_{*,{\underline{A}^{\prime}}}&-{\underline{A}^{\prime}}U_{1}\end{bmatrix},

respectively. Using Lemma
2.2
, Remark
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
,
W_{A}
and
W_{A^{\prime}}
are unitarily equivalent. Thus there exists unitary
S:H_{A}\rightarrow H_{A^{\prime}}
such that

(1)

(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}S)\underline{A}^{*}=\underline{A}^{\prime*}S
,

(2)

U_{2}D_{*,A}=D_{*,{A^{\prime}}}S
,

(3)

\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}S\right)D_{A}=D_{{A^{\prime}}}U_{1}
,

(4)

U_{2}\underline{A}=\underline{A}^{\prime}U_{1}
.

From above equations, we get that
U_{2}=S|_{\mathcal{D}_{*,A}}
,
U_{1}=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}S\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}
. Hence
\underline{A}
and
\underline{A}^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

From the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we observe that, if
\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)\underline{A}^{*}=\underline{A}^{\prime*}U
for some unitary operator
U:H_{A}\rightarrow H_{A^{\prime}}
, then
\left(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\tilde{U}_{1}\right)\theta_{A}=\theta_{A^{\prime}}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}\tilde{U}_{2}\right)
, where
\tilde{U}_{1}=U|_{\mathcal{D}_{*,A}}
and
\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}\tilde{U}_{2}=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}.

4.
Lifting of row contraction

In this section, we deal with characteristic function of contractive liftings, which is defined by S. Dey and R. Gohm
[
3
]
.

We know from
[
3
]
, that the contractive liftings we are concerned with has the following structure.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
3
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
E_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(H_{E})
and

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}

on
H_{E}=H_{C}\oplus H_{A}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,d
. Then
\underline{E}=(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{d})
is a row contraction if and only if
\underline{C}
and
\underline{A}
are row contractions and there exists a contraction
\gamma:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
such that
\underline{B}^{*}=D_{C}\gamma D_{*,A}.

Following the terminology of
[
3
]
, we say that the contraction
\gamma:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
is
resolving
, if for every
h\in H_{A}
,

(4.1)

\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}h=0,\,\forall\,\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}\text{ implies }D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}h=0,\,\forall\,\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}.

Definition 4.2
.

The lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
is called a
reduced lifting
, if
\underline{A}
is a c.n.c. row contraction and the function
\gamma
in Proposition
4.1
is resolving.

By the remark following
[
4
, Proposition 3.8]
, it is important to note that both reduced liftings and minimal liftings are same. For a Hilbert space
H
and
i=1,\ldots,d,
define the function
i^{H}_{j}:H\to\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}H
by
i^{H}_{j}(h)=(0,\ldots,0,h,0,\ldots,0)
where
h
is in the
j^{th}
component.
For a minimal lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
, S. Dey and R. Gohm
[
3
]
defined a characteristic function
M_{C,E}:\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
with symbol
\theta_{C,E}:\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
. Identifying the space
e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}
with
\mathcal{D}_{E}
, from
[
3
]
, we have the following expression for
\theta_{C,E}
:
For
h_{c}\in H_{C}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}(e_{0}\otimes D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{c}))=e_{0}\otimes(D_{C}h_{C}-\gamma D_{*,A}B_{j}h_{c})-\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}B_{j}h_{c},

and for
h_{a}\in H_{A}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}(e_{0}\otimes D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{a}))=-e_{0}\otimes\gamma\underline{A}D_{A}i^{H_{A}}_{j}(h_{a})+\underset{j=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}e_{j}\otimes\underset{\alpha}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}P_{j}D_{A}^{2}i^{H_{A}}_{j}(h_{a}).

Note that
\theta_{C,E}(e_{0}\otimes D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{a}))=(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}(e_{0}\otimes D_{A}i^{H_{A}}_{j}(h_{a}))
for
h_{a}\in H_{A}
, where
\theta_{A}
is as defined in Equation (
3.2
).

First, we develop another expression for
\theta_{C,E}
which would be useful for the computation later in this article.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
be a row contraction on
H_{C}
and
\underline{E}=(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{d})
be a minimal lifting of
\underline{C}
on
H_{E}=H_{C}\oplus H_{A}
, where

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d.

Then

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}{\sigma},

where
\sigma:\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,\gamma}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{A}
is a unitary operator defined by

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sigma D_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

In
[
1
]
, it is shown that the map
\sigma
is a unitary.
For
h_{a}\in H_{A}
and
j=1,\ldots,d
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}{\sigma}D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{a})=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
i^{H_{A}}_{j}(h_{a})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
D_{A}i^{H_{A}}_{j}(h_{a})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}D_{A}i^{H_{a}}_{j}(h_{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{a}).

For
h_{c}\in H_{C}
and
j=1,\ldots,d
, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{c})

\displaystyle=D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\gamma D_{*,A}\underline{B}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}\underline{B}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})

\displaystyle=D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\gamma D_{*,A}D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})

\displaystyle=D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\gamma(I-\underline{A}\underline{A}^{*})\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})

\displaystyle=(I-\gamma\gamma^{*})D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\left[-\gamma\underline{A}\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})+\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})\right]

\displaystyle=D_{*,\gamma}^{2}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-\left[-\gamma\underline{A}+\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha-1}^{*}D_{A}\right](e_{0}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c}))

\displaystyle=D_{*,\gamma}^{2}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c}).

From above calculation, we get

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}{\sigma}D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{c})=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})\\
0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{*,\gamma}^{2}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})-(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}i^{H_{C}}_{j}(h_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}D_{E}i^{H_{E}}_{j}(h_{c}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The following result illustrates that the characteristic function
\theta_{C,E}
can also be viewed as transfer function for some colligation matrix.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
be a row contraction on
H_{C}
and
\underline{E}=(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{d})

\underline{C}
on

B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d.

Then
\theta_{C,E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}{\sigma}
is the transfer function of the following colligation matrix

V=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma\\
\gamma D_{*,A}&(D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma)\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, note that

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}&(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}\end{bmatrix}{\begin{bmatrix}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma\\
P_{D_{A}}\sigma\end{bmatrix}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}{P_{D_{A}}\sigma}

\displaystyle D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A})({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1}

\displaystyle(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{T}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma).

We know that

D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A})({I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}}}-(\underline{R}\otimes I_{\Gamma\otimes H_{A}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\underline{A}^{*}))^{-1}(\underline{R}\otimes I_{H_{T}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma)

is the transfer function of the colligation

\displaystyle V=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma\\
\gamma D_{*,A}&(D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma)\end{bmatrix}.

This proves the result.
∎

We say two multi-analytic operators

The following result conveys that if characteristic function of two minimal liftings are equivalent then the liftings are unitarily equivalent. Here we want to mention that the proof uses colligation matrix based techniques and not the usual functional model theory.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
be a row contraction. Suppose
\underline{E}
and
\underline{E^{\prime}}
be two minimal liftings of
\underline{C}
such that
\theta_{C,E}
and
\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}
are equivalent. Then
\underline{E}
and
\underline{E^{\prime}}
are unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

Since
\theta_{C,E}
and
\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}
are equivalent, we have a unitary
v:\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}
such that
\theta_{C,E}=\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}v
.

By Lemma
4.4
,
\theta_{C,E}
and
\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}v
are transfer functions of the colligation matrices

\displaystyle W_{1}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma\\
\gamma D_{*,A}&\left(D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma\right)\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }

\displaystyle W_{2}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A^{\prime}}^{*}&D_{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v\\
\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}&\left(D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}P_{D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v-\gamma^{\prime}\underline{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v\right)\end{bmatrix},

respectively. By Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
4.5
, we know that
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are unitarly equivalent. Thus there exist
U:H_{A}\rightarrow H_{A^{\prime}}
such that

(1)

{A^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}=UA_{i}^{*}U^{*}
,

(2)

\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}=\gamma D_{*,A}U^{*}

(3)

D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}P_{D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v-\gamma^{\prime}\underline{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v=D_{*,\gamma}P_{D_{*,\gamma}}\sigma-\gamma\underline{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma
,

(4)

D_{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A^{\prime}}}\sigma^{\prime}v=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}{U^{*}}\right)D_{A}P_{D_{A}}\sigma
.

From Equation (
2
), we have
\underline{B}^{\prime}=D_{*,A^{\prime}}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}=\left(\gamma D_{*,A}U^{*}\right)^{*}D_{C}=UD_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}=U\underline{B}
. Hence

\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&U\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\underline{C}&0\\
\underline{B}&\underline{A}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{C}&0\\
\underline{B^{\prime}}&\underline{A^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\end{bmatrix}.

Hence
\underline{E}
and
\underline{E}^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent. From above equality, we also have

D_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\end{bmatrix}D_{E^{\prime}}\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\end{bmatrix},

and as a result

v=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\end{bmatrix}|_{\mathcal{D}_{E}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The converse of the above result was proved in
[

]
, but here we present a simple proof of it.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots C_{d})
be a row contraction on
H_{C}
. If
\underline{E}
and
\underline{E}^{{}^{\prime}}
be two equivalent minimal liftings of
\underline{C}
on
H_{E}=H_{C}\oplus H_{A}
and
H_{E^{{}^{\prime}}}=H_{C}\oplus H_{A^{{}^{\prime}}}
respectively, then
\theta_{C,E}
and
\theta_{C,E^{{}^{\prime}}}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\underline{E}
and
\underline{E}^{{}^{\prime}}
be two unitarily equivalent minimal liftings of
\underline{C}.
Then there exists a unitary
U:H_{A}\rightarrow H_{A^{{}^{\prime}}}
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle UB_{i}=B_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\text{ and }UA_{i}=A_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}U.

Equivalent to say that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&U\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\underline{C}&0\\
\underline{B}&\underline{A}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{C}&0\\
\underline{B}^{\prime}&\underline{A}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\end{bmatrix}.

By simply computing

and using the above equation, we have

\displaystyle D_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\end{bmatrix}D_{E^{\prime}}\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}\end{bmatrix}.

Similarly, evaluating
I-\underline{A}\underline{A}^{*},\,I-\underline{A}^{*}\underline{A}
and using Equation (
4.3
), we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle D_{A}=

\displaystyle\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)D_{A^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}},

(4.5)

\displaystyle D_{*,A}=

\displaystyle U^{*}D_{*,A^{{}^{\prime}}}U|_{\mathcal{D}_{*,A}}.

From Equations (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and the representations
\underline{B}=D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}
,

), we have

\displaystyle D_{*,A^{{}^{\prime}}}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}=

\displaystyle UD_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{*,A^{{}^{\prime}}}U\gamma^{*}D_{C}.

Using the fact that
A^{{}^{\prime}}
is a c.n.c. row contraction, we get
U\gamma^{*}={\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}
, and consequently

(4.6)

\displaystyle\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}U|_{D_{*,A}}.

Thus, we get
D_{*,\gamma}=D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}.

From Equations (
4.3
) and (
4.6
),

\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\underline{A^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma D_{E}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\gamma^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}D_{C}&0\\
-\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\underline{A^{\prime}}^{*}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}&\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)D_{A^{\prime}}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}&D_{A^{{}^{\prime}}}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}{\gamma^{\prime}}^{*}D_{C}&D_{A^{{}^{\prime}}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)|_{\mathcal{D}_{A}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\
0&\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\end{bmatrix}\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}D_{E^{{}^{\prime}}}\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{H_{C}}&0\\

.

Hence

B
is injective, which gives that the operator
C(I-R\otimes\underline{A}^{*})^{-1}(R\otimes B)
is injective. Hence
u=0
and consequently
\theta
is injective.

By Lemma
6.1
there exist a row contraction
\underline{C}^{\prime}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
with
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{C^{\prime}})=\dim(\mathcal{Y})
.
Using the construction given in
[
4
]
corresponding to the transfer function
\theta
we can construct a minimal lifting
\underline{E}^{\prime}
of the row contraction
\underline{C}^{\prime}
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}})=\dim(\mathcal{U})
. Since
\theta
is injective, we know that the symbol
\theta_{C^{\prime},E^{\prime}}
characteristic function of the lifting is equivalent to the transfer function
\theta
.

By Lemma
4.4
, there exist a co-isometric observable colligation
V
with basic operator
\underline{A^{\prime}}^{*}
and transfer function
\theta_{C,E}=\theta
, where

V=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{\prime*}&D_{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A}^{\prime}}\sigma\\
\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}&(D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}P_{D_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}}\sigma-\gamma^{\prime}\underline{A^{\prime}}P_{D_{A}^{\prime}}\sigma)\end{bmatrix}.

Observe that
V
and
W
are two co-isometric observable colligations with same transfer function. Hence
V
and
W
are unitarily equivalent. Thus there exist a unitary
U:H_{A}\rightarrow H_{A^{\prime}}
such that,

(6.1)

UA^{*}_{i}=A^{\prime*}_{i}U\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,d.

Consequently,

\begin{bmatrix}A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{d}\end{bmatrix}=U^{*}\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,A^{\prime}_{d}\end{bmatrix}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right),

D_{A}=(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*})D_{A^{\prime}}(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U)
, and
D_{*,A}=U^{*}D_{*,A^{\prime}}U
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{A^{\prime}})=\dim(\mathcal{D}_{A})
which is equal to the
\dim(\mathcal{U})
. Since
\theta_{C,E}=\theta
,
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}
and
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{A^{\prime}})=\dim(\mathcal{U})
, we get
\mathcal{D}_{A^{\prime}}=\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}
. But
\sigma
is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}
to
\mathcal{D}_{A^{\prime}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}
, which implies

\mathcal{D}_{*,\gamma^{\prime}}=\{0\}
. Hence
\gamma^{\prime}
is a co-isometry and

V=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{\prime*}&D_{A^{\prime}}\sigma\\
\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}&-\gamma^{\prime}\underline{A^{\prime}}\sigma.\end{bmatrix}

By unitarily equivalence of
V
and
W
, Equation (
6.1
), we have

(1)

\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U\right)B=D_{A^{\prime}}\sigma
, equivalently
B=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)D_{A^{\prime}}\sigma=D_{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma,

(2)

C=\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}U
, equivalently
C=\gamma^{\prime}UD_{*,A},

(3)

D=\gamma^{\prime}\underline{A^{\prime}}\sigma=\gamma^{\prime}U\underline{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma.

Therefore

W=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma\\
\gamma^{\prime}UD_{*,A}&-\gamma^{\prime}U\underline{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma\end{bmatrix}.

Define
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}U
and
\tilde{\sigma}=\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma
. Then
\gamma
is a co-isometric row contraction from
\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
to
\mathcal{Y}
and
\tilde{\sigma}
is a unitary from
\mathcal{U}
to
\mathcal{D}_{A}
. Hence

W=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma\\
\gamma D_{*,A}&-\gamma\underline{A}\left(\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\oplus}}U^{*}\right)\sigma\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\gamma\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\underline{A}^{*}&D_{A}\tilde{\sigma}\\
D_{*,A}&-\underline{A}\tilde{\sigma}\end{bmatrix}.

This completes the proof.
∎

.

7.
Tranformations of characteristic function

Recall that, if
T
is a contraction on a Hilbert space
H
, then for any
a
in the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, the operator
T_{a}:=(T-aI)(I-\overline{a}T)^{-1}
is also a contraction on
H
. Let
S_{T}:=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}(I-\overline{a}T)^{-1}
then by equations
(1.8)
on page 246 of
[
8
]
, there exist unitaries
Z_{T}:\mathcal{D}_{T_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{T}
and
Z_{*T}:\mathcal{D}_{*,T_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,T}
such that

(7.1)

\displaystyle Z_{T}D_{T_{a}}

\displaystyle=D_{T}S_{T}

(7.2)

\displaystyle Z_{*T}D_{*,T_{a}}

\displaystyle=D_{*,T}S^{*}_{T}.

Let
\theta_{T_{a}}
and
\theta_{T}
be the symbols of characteristic functions of
T_{a}
and
T
respectively. Then

(7.3)

Z_{*T}\theta_{T_{a}}(\lambda)Z^{-1}_{A}=\theta_{T}(\mu)\text{ for }\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

where
\mu=\frac{\lambda+a}{1+\overline{a}\lambda}.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let

E=\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}
, be a minimal contractive lifting of
C
. Then for
a\in\mathbb{D}
,
E_{a}=(E-aI)(I-\overline{a}E)^{-1}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
C_{a}
and

E_{a}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{a}&0\\
S_{A}BS_{C}&A_{a}\end{bmatrix}

where

S_{C}=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}
and
S_{A}=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}
.

Proof.

By Equations (
7.1
) and (
7.2
), there exist unitaries
Z_{C}:\mathcal{D}_{C_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
,
Z_{*C}:\mathcal{D}_{*,C_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,C}
,
Z_{A}:\mathcal{D}_{A_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{A}
and
Z_{*A}:\mathcal{D}_{*,A_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
such that

(7.4)

\displaystyle Z_{C}D_{C_{a}}

\displaystyle=D_{C}S_{C}\>\>\text{and}\>\>Z_{*C}D_{*,C_{a}}=D_{*,C}{S^{*}_{C}}

(7.5)

\displaystyle Z_{A}D_{A_{a}}

\displaystyle=D_{A}S_{A}\>\>\text{and}\>\>Z_{*A}D_{*,A_{a}}=D_{*,A}{S^{*}_{A}}.

Then

\displaystyle E_{a}

\displaystyle=(E-aI)(I-\overline{a}E)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C-aI&0\\
B&A-aI\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}&0\\
(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}\overline{a}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}&(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}(C-aI)(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}&0\\
B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}+(A-aI)(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}\overline{a}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}&(A-aI)(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C_{a}&0\\
\tilde{B}&A_{a}\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}+(A-aI)(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}\overline{a}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[1+\overline{a}(A-aI)(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}]B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[(I-\overline{a}A)+\overline{a}(A-aI)](I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(I-\overline{a}A+\overline{a}A-|a|^{2}I)(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1-|a|^{2})I(I-\overline{a}A)^{-1}B(I-\overline{a}C)^{-1}

\displaystyle=S_{A}BS_{C}.

Hence
E_{a}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{a}&0\\
S_{A}BS_{C}&A_{a}\end{bmatrix}
is a contractive lifting of
C_{a}
. Note that
(\frac{\lambda-a}{1-\bar{a}\lambda})^{n}
has the Taylor series expansion
(\frac{\lambda-a}{1-\bar{a}\lambda})^{n}=\underset{v=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}c_{v}(a,n)\lambda^{v}
with radius of convergence greater than 1. Then for the contraction
E_{a}
, we have

\displaystyle{E^{n}_{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}c_{v}(a,n)E^{v}

\displaystyle\bigvee E^{n}_{a}

\displaystyle{H_{C}}\subset\bigvee E^{n}{H_{C}}.

Also, we have
({E_{a}})_{-a}=E
for
a\in\mathbb{D}.
Then
\bigvee E^{n}_{a}H_{C}=\bigvee E^{n}{H_{C}}=H_{E}
. Hence
E_{a}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
C_{a}
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

be a minimal contractive lifting

),

\displaystyle D_{*,A_{a}}\gamma^{*}_{a}D_{C_{a}}

\displaystyle=S_{A}BS_{C}=S_{A}D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C}S_{C}

Since

(7.6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{a}

\displaystyle=Z^{*}_{C}\gamma Z_{*A}

(7.7)

\displaystyle D_{*,\gamma_{a}}

\displaystyle=Z^{*}_{C}D_{*,\gamma}Z_{C}.

Powers of the edge ideals and matchings in hypergraphs
Fahimeh Khosh-Ahang Ghasr

Abstract
. In this work, some combinatorial lower bound for regularity of powers of the edge ideal of a uniform hypergarph is gained. A family of hypergraphs whose regularity of edge ideal attains this bound and has a significant difference from the lower bounds heretofore obtained have also been introduced.

Introduction

In what follows
t,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 2
,
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring over the field
K

S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
are monomials in

S_{k}
for both of the monomial
S_{k}
and
\{x_{i}\ :\ x_{i}\mid S_{k}\}

x_{i}
and the variable

\mathcal{H}
stands for a simple hypergraph with
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
. Recall a hypergraph is

So there is a correspondence between

For a minimal graded free resolution

In the last few decades, studying the regularity of square-free monomial ideals because of their connections to other fields such as combinatorics, algebraic topology and computational algebra, has been extensively expanded. In particular, many researchers in combinatorial commutative algebra are interested in computing or bounding the regularity of the edge ideal of graphs and hypergraphs and their powers (see
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
for some surveys in this context).

In this paper, we firstly investigate free resolutions of powers of a square-free monomial ideal supported on a simplicial complex. Then we use the gained results in Section 2 for a simplicial complex introduced in
[
8
]
for powers of the edge ideal of a hypergraph. These yield to characterize or bound the graded Betti numbers and regularity of
R/I^{t}
in Section 3.
For instance in Corollary
3.5
a combinatorial characterization is gained for vanishing of

in terms of the number of matchings of size
i
in a
d
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
. The needed definitions of some kinds of matchings are presented in Definition
3.1
. Moreover the main result of this note is the following result:

Theorem A
.

(1)

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph which has a self semi-induced matching, say
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{i}\}
, of type
(i,j)
. Then for each
\ell
with
1\leq\ell\leq i
we have
\beta_{i,|S_{\ell}|(t-1)+j}(R/I^{t})\neq 0
and so

\mathrm{reg}(R/I^{t})\geq|S_{\ell}|(t-1)+(|\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq i}S_{k}|-i).

(2)

Let
s
be the number of all self semi-induced matchings in
\mathcal{H}
of type
(i,j)
. Then

\beta_{i,j}(R/I)\geq s,

and if moreover
\mathcal{H}
is
d
-uniform, then for all integers

\beta_{i,d(t-1)+j}(R/I^{t})\geq si.

(3)

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
d
-uniform hypergraph. Then for all

d(t-1)+(d-1)(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}\leq d(t-1)+(s.s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\mathrm{reg}(R/I^{t}).

The above result is indeed a generalization of some parts of
[
10
, Theorem 6.5]
,
[
12
, Lemma 2.2 and Proposition 2.5]
,
[
16
, Theorems 3.5(1), 3.7(1) and Corollary 3.6]
for arbitrary powers of the edge ideal of a hypergraph. Also, it gives a lower bound better than
[
3
, Theorem 3.7(1) and Corollary 3.8(1)]
and
[
17
, Corollary 3.9]
for the regularity of powers of the edge ideals of uniform hypergraphs, since there are examples of
d
-uniform hypergraphs achieving our bound such that

is reasonably larger that

as presented in Example

3.
Powers of the edge ideal of a hypergraph

To start, we recall the following definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

(See
[
5
]
and
[
16
, Definitions 2.1 and Notation 2.3]
.)
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{i}\}
be a family of edges of
\mathcal{H}
. We define the type of
\mathcal{S}
as
(i,j)
, where
i
is the cardinality of
\mathcal{S}
and

.

(1)

\mathcal{S}
is called a

\mathcal{H}
if for each
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
with
1\leq\ell<\ell^{\prime}\leq i
,
S_{\ell}\cap S_{\ell^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.

(2)

\mathcal{S}
is called a
self matching
in
\mathcal{H}
if for all
k
with
1\leq k\leq i
,
S_{k}\nsubseteq\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq i,\ell\neq k}S_{\ell}
.

(3)

\mathcal{S}
is called a
semi-induced matching
in
\mathcal{H}
if for each
S\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{S_{1},\dots,S_{i}\}
,
S\nsubseteq\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq i}S_{\ell}
.

(4)

\mathcal{S}
is called a
self semi-induced matching
in
\mathcal{H}
if
\mathcal{S}
is both self matching and semi-induced matching.

(5)

\mathcal{S}
is called an
induced matching
in
\mathcal{H}
if
\mathcal{S}
is both matching and semi-induced matching.

(6)

We use the following notions:

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{i\ :\ \textrm{there is a matching of size }i\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\};

\displaystyle(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{i\ :\ \textrm{there is an induced matching of size }i\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\};

\displaystyle(i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{j-i\ :\ \textrm{there is an induced matching of type }(i,j)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\};

\displaystyle(s.s.i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{i\ :\ \textrm{there is a self semi-induced matching of size }i\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\};

\displaystyle(s.s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{j-i\ :\ \textrm{there is a self semi-induced matching of type }(i,j)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\};

\displaystyle(s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\{j-i\ :\ \textrm{there is a semi-induced matching of type }(i,j)\textrm{ in }\mathcal{H}\}.

The following observations may be helpful in the sequel.

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose that
d
is the maximum size of edges in
\mathcal{H}
.

(1)

The following inequalities are straightforward:

(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\min\{m_{\mathcal{H}},(s.s.i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}\},\ \ (i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq(s.s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq(s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Also, if
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{i}\}
is an induced matching of type
(i,j)
in
\mathcal{H}
, then

j-i=|\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq i}S_{\ell}|-i=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq i}|S_{\ell}|-i\leq(d-1)i\leq(d-1)(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}.

Therefore
(i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq(d-1)(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}

(d-1)(i.m)_{\mathcal{H}}=(i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq(s.s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq(s.i.m)^{\prime}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Thus in view of

(2)

The matrix

(3)

Lemma 3.3

Suppose that

Proof.

Assume on the contrary that

. This means
S_{r}\subseteq S_{u_{j_{1}}}\cup\dots\cup S_{u_{j_{s}}}
. This contradicts to
\mathcal{S}
is a self semi-induced matching.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
d
-uniform hypergraph,
i>1,j=2di
and

\mathcal{L}_{i,j}=\{e\ :\ e=\overline{e_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}}}\in\mathrm{Ker}\overline{\partial}_{i}\setminus\mathrm{Im}\overline{\partial}_{i+1},\mathrm{Sum}(\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}]))=j\}.

Then
\beta_{i,j}(R/I^{2})=|\mathcal{L}_{i,j}|
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.3
, it is enough to show that the assumptions of Parts 1 and 2 in Lemma
2.3
hold, when
t=2
,
i>1
and
j=2di
. To this end, suppose that
\tau=\{S^{\textbf{b}_{\ell_{1}}},\dots,S^{\textbf{b}_{\ell_{i}}}\}
is a face of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(I)
. By means of
[
7
, Definition 3.1]
, one may assume that
\tau
has one of the following forms:

\tau=\{S_{1}^{2},S_{1}S_{2},\dots,S_{1}S_{i}\}\text{ or }\tau=\{S_{\ell_{1}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,S_{\ell_{i}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i}}\},

when
\ell_{k}<\ell^{\prime}_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq i
. Now in each case we investigate the degree of
e_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}])=(c_{k})
. In view of Remark
3.2
(3), in the first case,
c_{k}=2
if
x_{k}\in S_{1}
,
c_{k}=1
if
x_{k}\in\bigcup_{2\leq\ell\leq i}S_{\ell}\setminus S_{1}
and else
c_{k}=0
. So

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(e_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}})

\displaystyle=\sum c_{k}

\displaystyle=2d+|\bigcup_{2\leq\ell\leq i}S_{\ell}\setminus S_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2d+(i-1)d

\displaystyle=(i+1)d<2di.

Hence
\tau
should be in the form of the second one. Now, in the second case
c_{k}=2
if
x_{k}\in\bigcup_{1\leq r\leq i}(S_{\ell_{r}}\cap S_{\ell^{\prime}_{r}})
,
c_{k}=1
if
x_{k}\in\bigcup_{1\leq r\leq i}((S_{\ell_{r}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{r}})\setminus(S_{\ell_{r}}\cap S_{\ell^{\prime}_{r}}))
and else
c_{k}=0
. Hence

\mathrm{deg}(e_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}})=\sum c_{k}\leq 2di,

and the equality holds when
(S_{\ell_{k}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}})
s are disjoint for all
1\leq k\leq i
. So,
for all
1\leq k\leq i
, the rows associated to the vertices in
S_{\ell_{k}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}}
will be zero in
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\widehat{\ell_{k}},\dots,\ell_{i}])
, while they have entries 1 or 2 in
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}])
. Thus
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\widehat{\ell_{k}},\dots,\ell_{i}])\neq\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}])
,
for all
1\leq k\leq i
. This shows that the assumption of Lemma
2.3
(1) holds and so
\beta_{i,j}(R/I^{2})\leq|\mathcal{L}_{i,j}|
.

Now suppose that
\tau=\{S^{\textbf{b}_{\ell_{1}}},\dots,S^{\textbf{b}_{\ell_{i+1}}}\}
is a face of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(I)
with

\mathrm{Sum}(\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}]))=2di,\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i+1}])=\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}]).

By means of the above explanation, we should have

\tau=\{S_{\ell_{1}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,S_{\ell_{i+1}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i+1}}\},

when
\ell_{k}<\ell^{\prime}_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq i+1
and
(S_{\ell_{k}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}})
s are disjoint for all
1\leq k\leq i
and
S_{\ell_{i+1}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i+1}}\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq r\leq i}(S_{\ell_{r}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{r}})
. Suppose that
1\leq k\leq i
. Then in view of the structure of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(I)
,
S_{\ell_{k}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}}\nmid S_{\ell_{i+1}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i+1}}
. So there exists a variable
x_{r_{k}}
such that

\bullet
either
x_{r_{k}}\in S_{\ell_{k}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}}
but
x_{r_{k}}\not\in S_{\ell_{i+1}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i+1}}
,

\bullet
or
x_{r_{k}}\in S_{\ell_{k}}\cap S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}}
but
x_{r_{k}}\not\in S_{\ell_{i+1}}\cap S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i+1}}
.

Hence

\bullet
either the
r_{k}
th row in
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}])
is one or two, while the
r_{k}
th row in

\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\widehat{\ell_{k}},\dots,\ell_{i+1}]),

is zero,

\bullet
or the
r_{k}
th row in
\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}])
is two, while the
r_{k}
th row in

\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\widehat{\ell_{k}},\dots,\ell_{i+1}]),

is one.

Therefore in each case we have

\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\widehat{\ell_{k}},\dots,\ell_{i+1}])\neq\mathrm{Max}(AB[\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{i}]).

Thus by Lemma
2.3
(2),
\beta_{i,j}(R/I^{2})\geq|\mathcal{L}_{i,j}|
. These complete the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
d
-uniform hypergraph and
i>1
such that
\beta_{i,2di}(R/I^{2})\neq 0
. Then
\mathcal{H}
should have
2^{i}
matchings of size
i
.

Proof.

If
\beta_{i,2di}(R/I^{2})\neq 0
, then in view of Proposition
3.4
,
\mathcal{L}_{i,2di}\neq\emptyset
. So, there exists an element
e_{\tau}
in
\mathcal{L}_{i,2di}
of degree
2di
. As one can see in the proof of Proposition
3.4
,
\tau
should be in the form of
\{S_{\ell_{1}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{1}},\dots,S_{\ell_{i}}S_{\ell^{\prime}_{i}}\}
, when
\ell_{k}<\ell^{\prime}_{k}
and
(S_{\ell_{k}}\cup S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}})
s are disjoint for all
1\leq k\leq i
. Hence a set consisting precisely one edge from each set
\{S_{\ell_{k}},S_{\ell^{\prime}_{k}}\}
for
1\leq k\leq i
, will be a matching of size
i
in
\mathcal{H}
. Clearly, there exist
2^{i}
such sets.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(1)

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{i}\}

\overline{e_{\tau_{\ell}}}\in(\mathrm{Ker}\overline{\partial}_{i}\setminus\mathrm{Im}\overline{\partial}_{i+1})_{|S_{\ell}|(t-1)+j}.

Firstly note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(e_{\tau_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}(S_{\ell}^{t-1}\mathrm{lcm}(S_{1},\dots,S_{i}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}(S_{\ell}^{t-1})+\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{lcm}(S_{1},\dots,S_{i}))

\displaystyle=|S_{\ell}|(t-1)+|\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq i}S_{k}|=|S_{\ell}|(t-1)+j.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\ell=1
and for
1\leq r\leq i
,
\textbf{b}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}_{m,t}
is the vector associated to the
r
th element of
\tau_{1}
. Now, in view of Remark
2.2
(1), to prove
\overline{e_{\tau_{1}}}\in\mathrm{Ker}\overline{\partial}_{i}
it is enough to show that for all
1\leq k\leq i
,

\mathrm{Max}(AB[1,\dots,i])\neq\mathrm{Max}(AB[1,\dots,\widehat{k},\dots,i]).

Suppose that
2\leq k\leq i
(resp.
k=1

\mathcal{S}
is a self-matching, there is a vertex
x_{u_{k}}\in S_{k}\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq i,\ell\neq k}S_{\ell}
. Now, in view of Remark

(3), if
AB[1,\dots,i]=(c_{r,r^{\prime}})_{n\times i}
, then
c_{u_{k},k}=1

u_{k}
th component in
\mathrm{Max}(AB[1,\dots,\widehat{k},\dots,i])

).

\overline{e_{1,\dots,i}}\in\mathrm{Im}\overline{\partial}_{i+1}

\tau_{1}

\mathrm{Max}(AB[1,\dots,i])=\mathrm{Max}(AB[1,\dots,i+1]).

\tau^{\prime}=\{S_{1}^{t},S_{1}^{t-1}S_{2},\dots,S_{1}^{t-1}S_{i},S_{1}^{t-1}S_{i+1}\},

One can check that

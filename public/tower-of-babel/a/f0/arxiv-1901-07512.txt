Compressive Sensing with a Multiple Convex Sets Domain
Hang Zhang, Afshin Abdi, Faramarz Fekri

Abstract
In this paper, we study a general framework for compressive sensing assuming the existence of the prior knowledge that 𝐱 ♮ \mathbf{x}^{\natural} belongs to the union of multiple convex sets, 𝐱 ♮ ∈ ⋃ i 𝒞 i \mathbf{x}^{\natural}\in\bigcup_{i}{\cal C}_{i} . In fact, by proper choices of these convex sets in the above framework, the problem can be transformed to well known CS problems such as the phase retrieval, quantized compressive sensing, and model-based CS. First we analyze the impact of this prior knowledge on the minimum number of measurements M M to guarantee the uniqueness of the solution. Then we formulate a universal objective function for signal recovery, which is both computationally inexpensive and flexible. Then, an algorithm based on multiplicative weight update and proximal gradient descent is proposed and analyzed for signal reconstruction. Finally, we investigate as to how we can improve the signal recovery by introducing regularizers into the objective function.



Introduction

In the traditional
compressive sensing
(CS)

Foucart & Rauhut, n.d.
;
Eldar & Kutyniok, 2012
,
sparse signal
\mathbf{x}
is reconstructed via

\min_{\mathbf{x}}~\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1},~~\textup{~s.t.~}~~\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x},

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
denotes the measurement vector and

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is the measurement matrix.

In this paper, we assume the existence of extra prior knowledge
that
\mathbf{x}
lies in the union of some convex sets,

\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
where
L
denotes the number of constraint sets and
{\cal C}_{i}
is the
i
-th
convex constraint set.
Therefore, we now wish to solve

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1},\ \textup{~s.t.~}~~\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}\mathscr{C}_{i}

(1.2)

This ill-posed inverse problem (i.e., given measurement
\mathbf{y}
,
solving for
\mathbf{x}
) turns out to be a rather general form of
CS.
For example,
setting
\bigcup\mathscr{C}_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
simplifies our
problem to the traditional CS problem.
In the following, we will further show that
by appropriate choices of these convex sets, Eq. (
1.2
)
can be transformed to the
phase retrieval

Candes et al. , 2011
;
Chen & Candes, 2015
,

quantized compressive sensing

Dai et al. , 2009
,
or
model-based CS

Baraniuk et al. , 2010

Relation with other problems

Phase retrieval

Consider the noiseless phase retrieval problem in which the measurements are given by

\displaystyle y_{i}=\left|\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},~\mathbf{x}\rangle\right|^{2},~~1\leq i\leq l,\vskip 2.84526pt

(1.3)

where
y_{i}
is the
i
-th measurement and
\mathbf{a}_{i}
denotes
the
corresponding coefficients. Considering the first measurement, the constraint

\sqrt{y_{1}}=|\langle\mathbf{a}_{1},~\mathbf{x}\rangle|
can be represented
via
\mathbf{x}\in{\cal B}_{+}^{(1)}\bigcup{\cal B}_{-}^{(1)}
where

{\cal B}_{+}^{(1)}=\{\mathbf{x}:~\langle\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{x}\rangle=\sqrt{y_{1}}\}
and

{\cal B}_{-}^{(1)}=\{\mathbf{x}:~\langle\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{x}\rangle=-\sqrt{y_{1}}\}
.
Following these steps,
the constraints

\{y_{i}=\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}\}_{i=1}^{l}

can be transformed to

\mathbf{x}\in\bigcap_{i}\big({\cal B}_{+}^{(i)}\bigcup{\cal B}_{-}^{(i)}\big)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{l}}\mathscr{C}_{j}
,
for some appropriately defined
C_{j}
’s given by the
intersection of different
\mathscr{B}_{\pm}^{(i)}
.
Setting sensing matrix

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{0}
will restore the phase retrieval to our setting.

Quantized compressive sensing

In this scenario, the measurements are quantized, i.e.,

\displaystyle y_{i}=Q(\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},~\mathbf{x}\rangle),~1\leq i\leq L,\vskip 2.84526pt

(1.4)

where
Q(\cdot)
is the quantizer.
Since
Q^{-1}(\cdot)
is an interval on real line,
\mathscr{C}_{i}
would be a
convex set and the quantized CS can be easily transformed to
Eq. (
1.2
).

Model-based compressive sensing

These lines of works
Baraniuk et al. , 2010
;
Duarte & Eldar, 2011
;
Silva et al. , 2011

are the most similar work to our model, where they consider

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\natural},~~\mathbf{x}^{\natural}\in\bigcup_{i}{\cal L}_{i}.\vskip 2.84526pt

(1.5)

Here,
{\cal L}_{i}
is assumed to be a linear space whereas
the only assumption we make on the models is being a convex set.
Hence, their model can be regarded as a special case of our problem.

In
Baraniuk et al. , 2010
, the author studied the minimum number of measurements

M
under different models, i.e., shape of
{\cal L}_{i}
, and modified
CoSaMP algorithms
Foucart & Rauhut, n.d.
to reconstruct signal.
In
Duarte & Eldar, 2011
, the authors expanded the signal onto different basis
and transformed model-based CS to be
block-sparse CS. In
Silva et al. , 2011
, the author
studied model-based CS with incomplete sensing matrix information and
reformulated it as a matrix completion problem.

Our contribution:

Statistical Analysis

We analyze the minimum number of measurements
to ensure uniqueness of the solution.
We first show that the conditions for the uniqueness
can be represented as

\min_{u\in E}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}\|_{2}>0
, for an appropriate set
E
.
Assuming the entries of the sensing matrix
\mathbf{A}
are i.i.d. Gaussian,
we relate the probability of uniqueness to the number of measurements,
M
.
Our results show that depending on the structure of
{\cal C}_{i}
’s,
the number of measurements can be reduced significantly.

Optimization Algorithm

We propose a novel formulation and the associated
optimization algorithm to reconstruct
the signal
\mathbf{x}
.
First, note that existing algorithms on e.g., model-based CS
are not applicable to our problem as they rely heavily on the structure of
constraint sets.
For example, a key idea in model-based CS is
to consider expansion of
\mathbf{x}
onto the basis of each
\mathscr{C}_{i}
and
then rephrase the constraint as the block sparsity on the representation of
\mathbf{x}

on the union of bases.
However, such an approach may add complicated constraints on the coefficients of
\mathbf{x}

in the new basis,
as the sets
{\cal C}_{i}
’s are not necessarily simple subspaces.

Note that although
{\cal C}_{i}
’s are assumed to be convex,
their union
\bigcup_{i}{\cal C}_{i}
is not necessarily a convex set,
which makes the optimization problem Eq. (
1.2
) hard to solve.
By introducing an auxiliary variable,
\mathbf{p}
, we convert the non-convex
optimization problem to a biconvex problem.
Using
multiplicative weight update

Arora et al. , 2012

from online learning theory
Shalev-Shwartz et al. , 2012
, we
design an algorithm with convergence speed of

{\cal O}(T^{-1/2})
to a local minimum. Further,
we investigate improving the performance of the algorithm by
incorporating appropriate regularization.
Compared to the naive idea of solving
L
simultaneous optimization problems

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}~\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1},~\textup{~s.t.~}~\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x},\quad\mathbf{x}\in{\cal C}_{i},\vskip 2.84526pt

(1.6)

and choosing the best solution out of
L
results,
our method is computationally less-expensive and more flexible.

Statistical Property

In this section, we will find the minimum number of measurements

to
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}^{\natural}

\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0}

{definition}

The tangent cone

for

is defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\mathbf{x}}\triangleq\{\mathbf{e}:~\ \|\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{e}\|_{1}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1},~\exists~t\geq 0\}.\vskip 2.84526pt

(3.1)

Geometric interpretation of

is that it contains all directions that lead to smaller
\|\cdot\|_{1}
originating
from
\mathbf{x}
. In the following analysis,
we use
\mathscr{T}
as a compact notation for
\mathscr{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{\natural}}
.
Easily we can prove that
\mathbf{d}\in{\cal T}
.

{definition}

The Gaussian width
\omega(\cdot)
associated with
set
U
is defined as

\omega(U)\triangleq\mathbb{E}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in U}\left\langle\mathbf{g},~\mathbf{x}\right\rangle,\mathbf{g}\sim\mathscr{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})

.

Define cone
\widetilde{{\cal C}}_{i,j}

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal C}}_{i,j}\triangleq\ \left\{\mathbf{z}~\big|~\mathbf{z}=t(\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}),~\exists~t>0,~\mathbf{x}_{1}\in{\cal C}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in\ {\cal C}_{j}\right\},\vskip 2.84526pt

(3.2)

which denotes the cone consisting of all vectors

that are parallel

\mathbf{x}_{1}-\mathbf{x}_{2}

\mathbf{x}_{1}\in{\cal C}_{i}
, and
\mathbf{x}_{2}\in{\cal C}_{j}

Then we define event

\displaystyle{\cal E}\triangleq\ \left\{\bigcup_{i,j}\left(\textup{null}(\mathbf{A})\bigcap{\cal T}\bigcap\widetilde{{\cal C}}_{i,j}\right)=\{\mathbf{0}\}\right\}.\vskip 2.84526pt

(3.3)

{lemma}

[]

We can guarantee the correct recovery of

Proof.

This proof is fundamentally same as

Zhang et al. , Nov.

;

Setting

Then we prove that

Assume that there exists non-zero

We can show that signal

Since a direct computation of the probability of event

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in{\cal T}\bigcap\left(\bigcup_{i,j}\widetilde{{\cal C}}_{ij}\right)}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|_{2}>0.\vskip 2.84526pt

Using Gordon’s escape from mesh theorem

{theorem}

[]

{remark}

If we fix

Computational Algorithm

Apart from the statistical property, another important aspect of
Eq. (
2.2
) is to design an efficient algorithm.
One naive idea is to consider and solve
L
separate optimization problems

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}=\textup{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}}~\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1},~\textup{~s.t.~}~~\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x},~~\mathbf{x}\in{\cal C}_{i},\vskip 2.84526pt

(4.1)

and then selecting the best one, i.e., the sparsest reconstructed signal
among all
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}
’s.
However, this method has two drawbacks:

•

It requires solving
L
separate optimization problems, which in many
applications might be prohibitively large and difficult to handle, but the
proposed method is based on one single optimization procedure.

•

It is inflexible. For example, some prior
knowledge of which
{\cal C}_{i}

the true signal
\mathbf{x}^{\natural}
is more likely to reside might be available.
The above method cannot incorporate such priors.

To overcome the above drawbacks, we
(i)
reformulate Eq. (
2.2
)
to a more tractable objective function,
and
(ii)
propose a computationally efficient algorithm to solve it.
In the following, we assume that
\mathbf{x}
is bounded in the sense that
for a constant
R
,
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq R
.

Reformulation of the objective function

We introduce an auxiliary variable
\mathbf{p}

and rewrite the Lagrangian form in
Eq. (
2.2
) as

\min_{\mathbf{x}}\min_{\mathbf{p}\in\Delta_{L}}\sum_{i}~p_{i}\ \bigg(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}+\widetilde{\mathbbm{1}}(\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{C}_{i})+\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{2}+\dfrac{\lambda_{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{2}}{2}\bigg),

(4.2)

where
\Delta_{L}
is the simplex
\{p_{i}\geq 0,~\sum_{i}p_{i}=1\}
,

\widetilde{\mathbbm{1}}(\cdot)
is the truncated indicator function, which is

0
when its argument is true and is some large finite number
C
otherwise,
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
are the Lagrange multipliers.
The term
\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{2}
is used to penalize for
the constraint
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
while

\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}_{2}
corresponds to the energy constraint

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\leq R
.
It can be easily shown that solving Eq. (
4.2
)
for large enough
C
ensures

\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{i}^{L}\mathscr{C}_{i}
.

Non-convex Proximal Multiplicative Weighting Algorithm

1:

•

: Initialize all variables with uniform weight

p_{i}^{(0)}=L^{-1}
and
\mathbf{x}^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}
.

2:

•
For time

to
T
:

We update

and

as

\displaystyle p_{i}^{(t+1)}\propto p_{i}^{(t)}e^{-\eta_{p}^{(t)}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})}\

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}=\mathsf{prox}_{\eta^{(t)}_{w}\|\cdot\|_{1}}\ \bigg[\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta^{(t)}_{x}\sum_{i}p_{i}^{(t)}\left(\nabla_{x}h_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\mathbf{y})\ +\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\right)\bigg],

where

\mathbf{p}^{(t)}

•

Regularization for
\mathbf{p}

Another drawback of the naive method is that
they cannot exploit the prior knowledge. For example,
if we know that the true
\mathbf{x}^{\natural}
is most likely to reside in set
{\cal C}_{1}
.
With the naive method,
we cannot use this information but separately solve
Eq. (
2.2
) for all
L
sets.
In the sequel, we will show that
our formulation Eq. (
4.2
)
can incorporate such prior knowledge by adding
regularizers for
\mathbf{p}
, and bring certain
performance improvement.

Note that we can interpret
p_{i}
, the
i
-th element of
\mathbf{p}

in Eq. (
4.5
) as
the likelihood of
\mathbf{x}^{\natural}\in{\cal C}_{i}
.
Without any prior knowledge about
which set
{\cal C}_{i}
the true signal
\mathbf{x}^{\natural}
resides,
variable
\mathbf{p}
is uniformly distributed among
all possible distributions
\Delta_{L}
.
When certain prior information is available, its
distribution is skewed towards certain distributions,
namely
\mathbf{q}
.

In this paper, we adopt
\|\cdot\|^{2}_{2}
to regularize

\mathbf{p}
towards
\mathbf{q}
and write the modified function

\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x})
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x})={\cal L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x})+\ \dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|^{2}_{2},\vskip 2.84526pt

(4.10)

where
\lambda_{3}>0
is a constant used to
balance
{\cal L}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x})
and
\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\|^{2}_{2}
.
Based on different applications, other norms such as KL-divergence or

l_{1}
norm can be used as the regularizer.

Then we substitute the update equation Eq. (
4.3
)
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}=\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}\left(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\eta^{(t)}_{p}\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\right),\vskip 2.84526pt

(4.11)

where

\mathbf{g}^{(t)}=\nabla_{\mathbf{p}^{(t)}}\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})+\lambda_{3}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{q})
,
and

\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})
denotes the vector whose

i
th element is
f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})
.
Similar as above, we obtain the following theorems.

{theorem}

[]

Provided that
\|\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\|_{2}\leq R_{g}
,
by setting
\eta_{x}^{(t)}=\eta_{x}\leq L_{h}^{-1}

and
\eta_{p}^{(t)}=(\lambda t)^{-1}

we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bigg|\dfrac{\min_{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{t}\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})}{T}-\dfrac{\sum_{t}\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})}{T}\bigg|\\

\leq~&\dfrac{R_{g}^{2}\log T}{2\lambda_{3}T}+\dfrac{R^{2}}{2\eta_{x}T},\end{aligned}\vskip 2.84526pt

(4.12)

Comparing with Thm.

,
Thm.
4.3
implies that
the regularizers improve the optimal

{\cal O}(T^{-1/2})
to

.
Therefore, our framework can exploit the prior information
to improve the recovery performance whereas the naive
method of iterative computation fails to achieve as such.

{theorem}

Provided that

Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the
compressive sensing with a multiple convex-set domain.
First we analyzed the impact of prior knowledge

\mathbf{x}\in\bigcup_{i}{\cal C}_{i}
on the minimum number of measurements
M

to guarantee uniqueness of the solution.
We gave an illustrative example and
showed that significant savings in
M
can be achieved.
Then we formulated a universal objective
function and develop an algorithm for the signal reconstruction.
We show that in terms of the speed of convergence
to local minimum,
our proposed algorithm based on
multiplicative weight update

and
proximal gradient descent
can achieve the optimal
rate of
{\cal O}(T^{-1/2})
.
Further, in terms of

T^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\|^{2}_{2}
, the optimal speed
increases to
{\cal O}(T^{-1})
.
Moreover, provided that we have a prior knowledge about
\mathbf{p}
,
we show that we can improve
the optimal recovery performance
by
\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
regularizers, and hence increasing
the above convergence rate from

{\cal O}(T^{-1/2})
to
{\cal O}\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right)
.

=~&D_{KL}(\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}||\mathbf{p}^{(t)})+\ \underbrace{\sum_{i}\log\left(e^{-\eta^{(t)}_{p}p^{(t)}_{i}{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})}\right)+\ \eta^{(t)}_{p}\left\langle\mathbf{p}^{(t)},f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right\rangle}_{0}=\ D_{KL}(\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}||\mathbf{p}^{(t)}),\end{aligned}\vskip 2.84526pt

(C.9)

where in
(i)
we use

\sum_{i}p_{i}x_{i}\geq\prod_{i}x_{i}^{p_{i}}
such that

\sum_{i}p_{i}=1,p_{i}\geq 0
.

∎

Appendix D
Proof of
subsection 4.3

Proof.

Define
\mathbf{p}^{*}
and
\mathbf{x}^{*}
as

\mathbf{p}^{*}=\textup{argmin}_{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{t}\mathscr{LR}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\right),~~~\mathbf{x}^{*}=\textup{argmin}_{\mathbf{x}}\sum_{t}\mathscr{LR}\left(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}\right).

(D.1)

respectively
First we define
{\cal T}^{t}_{1}
and
{\cal T}^{t}_{2}
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\cal T}^{t}_{1}&=\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})-\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{*},\mathbf{x}^{(t)});\\
{\cal T}^{t}_{2}&=\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})-\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{*}),\end{aligned}\vskip 2.84526pt

(D.2)

respectively. Then our goal becomes
bounding
|\sum_{t}{\cal T}^{t}_{1}|+|\sum_{t}{\cal T}^{t}_{2}|
,
For term
|\sum_{t}{\cal T}^{t}_{2}|
, the analysis stays the same
as
Appendix B
.
Here we focus on bounding
\sum_{t}{\cal T}_{1}^{t}
, which proceeds as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{t}{\cal T}_{1}^{t}=\ \sum_{t}\left(\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})-\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})\right)=\ \sum_{t}\left\{-\left\langle\underbrace{\nabla_{t}\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})}_{\mathbf{g}^{(t)}},~\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{(t)}\right\rangle-\ \dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}\right\}\\
=~&\sum_{t}\left\{\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{(t)},\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle-\ \dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}\right\}\end{aligned}\vskip 2.84526pt

(D.3)

Then we consider the distance
\|\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}
which is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}=\ \left\|\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\eta_{p}^{t}\mathbf{g}^{(t)})-\mathbf{p}\right\|^{2}_{2}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\ \left\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\eta_{p}^{(t)}\mathbf{g}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\right\|^{2}_{2}\\

(i)

projection,
which gives us

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{g}^{(t)},\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\right\rangle\leq\dfrac{\eta_{p}^{(t)}\|\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\|^{2}_{2}}{2}+\ \dfrac{\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}-\|\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}}{2\eta_{p}^{(t)}}\vskip 2.84526pt

(D.5)

\eta_{p}^{(t)}=(\lambda t)^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{t}{\cal T}_{1}^{t}\leq\ \dfrac{R_{g}^{2}\log T}{2\lambda_{3}}+\ \dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\sum_{t}t(\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}-\|\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2})-\ \dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\sum_{t}\|\mathbf{p}^{(t)}-\mathbf{p}\|^{2}_{2}\\

(D.6)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\sum_{t}{\cal T}_{1}^{t}+{\cal T}_{2}^{t}}{T}\leq\ \dfrac{R_{g}^{2}\log T}{2\lambda_{3}T}+\dfrac{R^{2}}{2\eta_{x}T},\vskip 2.84526pt

(D.7)

which completes the proof.

∎

Appendix E
Proof of

Proof.

Following the same procedure in

Appendix C
, we
can bound

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}^{(t)}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\dfrac{2}{L_{h}}\ \left[\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t)})-\mathscr{LR}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)})\right]\\

Abstract
. In this paper we investigate the Margulis-Ruelle inequality for general Riemannian manifolds (possibly noncompact and with boundary) and show that it always holds under integrable condition.



1.
Introduction

In the theroy of differentiable dynamical systems, Margulis-Ruelle inequality
[
6
]
is a fundamental formulation. This inequality establishes the bridge between the chaotic index metric entropy and the stable index Lyapunov exponent, being employed especially frequently in the study of hyperbolic behaviors in various settings (uniformly, nonuniformly or partially hyperbolic systems).

Let
f
be a continuous map on a metric space
M
. For
f
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
and measurable partition
\xi
of
M
with partition entropy

H_{\mu}(\xi)=\int\log\mu^{-1}(\xi(x))d\mu<\infty
,
the metric entropy of
f
with respect to
\xi
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(f,\xi)=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{-i}(\xi))

and the metric entropy
h_{\mu}(f)
of
f
with respect to
\mu
is defined as the supremum of
(
1.1
) over all
\xi
with finite partition entropy.

For
f
differentiable on a Riemannian manifold
M
, relative to a direction
v\in T_{x}M
with
x\in M
, Lyapunov exponent along
v
is given by the limit

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|D_{x}f^{n}v\|,

which exists (possibly -
\infty
), for almost every point
x
of every
f
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
by Oseledets theorem
[
4
,
1
]
if
\int\log^{+}\|D_{x}f\|d\mu<\infty
, where
\log^{+}t=\max\{\log t,0\}
. Moreover, the finite dimension of
M
brings about exactly the same number of values of the limit (
1.2
), which we denote as
\lambda_{i}(f,x)

(1\leq i\leq\dim M)
.

When the manifold
M
is compact and without boundary, the above two mechanisms could be linked together as the layout of Margulis-Ruelle inequality
[
6
]
: the metric entropy of
\mu
is not beyond the sum of positive Lyapunov exponents:

(1.3)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(f)\leq\int\sum_{\lambda_{i}(f,x)>0}\lambda_{i}(f,x)\,d\mu.

However, for general manifolds, the availability of (
1.3
) is unkown yet. There are many spaces which are expected to be taken into account, but not in the previous context, such as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Lie groups
\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, billiard tables, moduli spaces, etc, where both noncompactness and boundary lead to the actual obstructions.
As the case exhibits, if
M
is noncompact, the inequality (
1.3
) possibly doesn’t hold
[
5
]
. With boundary permissible, (
1.3
) was dealt with in (
[
2
]
), involving systems on manifolds with finite capacity. Regarding the two aspects, in this paper, we investigate this problem in a general situation, i.e., for manifolds which are possibly both noncompact
and with boundary.

Let
M
be a Riemannian mainfold,
f
a
C^{1}
map from an open subset
U\subset M
to
M\setminus\partial M
, and Borel probability measure
\mu
on
M\setminus\partial M
is
f
-invariant:
\mu(f^{-1}(B))=\mu(B)
for any Borel set
B\subset M\setminus\partial M
. The general setting is as follows:

(A)
Distortion on
f
: there exist
0<\alpha<1
,
C,a>1
such that for any
x\in U,\,\,y\in B(x,\min\{1,\,\,d^{a}(x,\partial M)\})
,

\frac{\big|\|D_{x}f\|-\|D_{y}f\|\big|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}\leq Cd_{0}(x,\partial M)^{-a},

where
d_{0}(x,\partial M)=\min\{d(x,\partial M),d^{-1}(x,x_{0})\}
for any preassigned
x_{0}\in M
.

(B)
Integrability on
\mu
:

\int_{U}\max\{\log^{+}\|D_{x}f\|,\,\,|\log d_{0}(x,\partial M)|,\,\,|\log\rho_{b}(x)|,\,\,\log N_{b}(x)\,\}\,d\mu<\infty,

where
\rho_{b}(x)
for some
b\geq 1
is the regular radius given by the minimum of
\varrho_{b}(y)
with
d_{0}(y,\partial M)\geq d_{0}(x,\partial M)
and
\varrho_{b}(y)=\max\{0<r\leq 1:\,\|D_{w}\exp_{x_{1}}\|\leq b
,
\|D_{x_{2}}\exp_{x_{1}}^{-1}\|\leq b,\quad\forall\,\,x_{i}\in B(y,r),\,\,\,i=1,2,\,\,w=\exp^{-1}_{x_{1}}(x_{2})\}
;
N_{b}(x)
is the regular tankage given by the minimal cardinality among covers of
\{y:d_{0}(y,\partial M)\geq d_{0}(x,\partial M)\}
by balls with regular radius.

Remark
.

The properties on
d_{0}
is independent of the choice of
x_{0}
. In fact,
d^{-1}(x,x_{0})
represents the distance of
x
relative to the infinity: “away from
x_{0}
” is equavlent to “close to the infinity”.

Remark
.

(A)(B) are naturally satisfied if
M
is compact and without boundary.
In (A), we adopt
\|D_{x}f\|-\|D_{y}f\|
instead of
\|D_{x}f-D_{y}f\|
, which avoids the assumptions on the local trivialization of manifolds as in
[
2
]
since we just compare real numbers
\|D_{x}f\|
and
\|D_{y}f\|
. In (B), the first one is a natrual property guaranteeing the exisitence of Lyapunov
exponents in Oseledets theorem, the next three could be verified in ususl analytic settings with some nondegenerate polynomial estimates, for example, for any measure absolute to Lebesgue measure such that

•

the density decreases polynomially of order bigger than
\dim M
near infinity and increases polynomially of order smaller than
\dim M
near boundary;

•

the regular radius decreases polynomially and the regular tankage increases polynomially near infinity and boundary.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and
f
a
C^{1}
map from an open subset
U\subset M
to
M\setminus\partial M
satisfying (A). Then for any
f
-invariant Borel probability measure satisfying
(B)
, Margulis-Ruelle inequality holds true.

The difficulties to establish (
1.3
) mainly arise from four aspects: firstly, the norm of derivarives may grow to the infinity; secondly, the regular neighborhoods on which
f
behaves like diffeomorphisms may be destroyed by the boundary; thirdly, the distortion of manifold has no uniform bounds; and fourthly, the tankage may increase very largely when approaching the infinity and boundary. To overcome these difficulties, we take advantage of partitions which are adaptive to the above items and also we show the finiteness of such partitions’ entropy in Section
2
for the feasibility of later dynamical entropy computation. In Section
3
, we carry on analysis of entropy for evolutions, with respect to the (singular) part near boundary and infinity, and the (regular) part away from boundary and infinity. In the present situation, we adopt infinite countable partition instead of finite partition as in
[
2
]
, which will make the integrable condition of Theorem
1.1
more directly applicable to the proof of (
1.3
).

2.
Construction of partitions and partition entropy

For convenience, we consider the minimum between
d_{0}
and
1
, which we denote by
d_{*}
. Notice that the properties in (A)(B) concerning
d_{0}
also hold for
d_{*}
.

Consider iterations
f^{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Associated to the influences of noncompactness and boundary, we define for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle A_{n}=\Big\{x\in U:

\displaystyle\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}\|D_{f^{j}(x)}f\|^{*}\leq 2^{n},\,\,\,\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{*}(f^{j}(x),\partial M)\geq 2^{-n},

\displaystyle\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}\rho_{b}(f^{j}(x))\geq 2^{-n},\,\,\,\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}N_{b}(f^{j}(x))\leq 2^{n}\Big\},

where
t^{*}=\max\{t,\,1\}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

It holds that
A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots
and
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(A_{n})=1
by (B). We are going to construct partitions with box-like elements whose sizes are given differently according to
A_{n}
.

Denote
\operatorname{d}=\dim M
. Relative to any
x\in M\setminus\partial M
, under an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{\operatorname{d}}\}
of
T_{x}M
, a box centered at
y
with sides
2a_{i}>0

(1\leq i\leq\operatorname{d})
is understood as

\exp_{x}(\Gamma(\exp^{-1}_{x}(y);a_{1},\cdots,a_{\operatorname{d}})):=\exp_{x}(\{\exp^{-1}_{x}(y)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\operatorname{d}}t_{i}a_{i}e_{i},\quad-1\leq t_{i}\leq 1\}).

In particular, for any
a>0
, we denote
\Gamma_{x}(a)
as the cube
\exp_{x}(\Gamma(0;a,\cdots,a))
and for any integer
l\geq 1
,
\mathcal{Y}(x,a,l)

is defined as a partition of
\Gamma_{x}(a)
consisting of subcubes

\exp_{x}(\{\sum_{1\leq i\leq\operatorname{d}}\frac{k_{i}a}{2^{l}}e_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\operatorname{d}}t_{i}\frac{a}{2^{l}}e_{i}:\,\,-1\leq t_{i}\leq 1\}),\,\,1-2^{l}\leq k_{i}\leq 2^{l}-1,\,\,\frac{k_{i}+1}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

For any
\operatorname{\varepsilon}>0
and a subset
M_{1}\subset M
, denote
F(M_{1},\operatorname{\varepsilon})
as a subset of
M_{1}
with maximal cardinality in sense that any
x,y\in M_{1}
satisfy
d(x,y)>\operatorname{\varepsilon}
. As a matter of fact,
M_{1}\subset\cup_{x\in F(M_{1},\operatorname{\varepsilon})}\bar{B}(x,\operatorname{\varepsilon})
, where
\bar{B}(x,\operatorname{\varepsilon})
is the closure of ball
B(x,\operatorname{\varepsilon})
. Noting that
\exp_{x}^{-1}(B(x,\rho_{b}(x))=B_{\mathbb{R}^{\operatorname{d}}}(0,\rho_{b}(x))
, by the control in regular scale there exists a constant
C_{0,1}>0
such that for any
x\in M\setminus\partial M
,
0<r\leq\rho_{b}(x)
, one has

\#F(B(x,r),\operatorname{\varepsilon})\leq C_{0,1}(\lceil\frac{r}{\operatorname{\varepsilon}}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}},\quad\forall\,\operatorname{\varepsilon}>0.

Choose
l_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

l_{1}>a,\quad\frac{1}{2^{n(l_{1}-2m)}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}2^{n+1}},\quad C\big(\frac{1}{2^{n(l_{1}-2m)}}\big)^{\alpha}2^{an}<2^{\frac{1}{m}}-1,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

and define

\displaystyle\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}2^{nl_{1}}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In order to cover
A_{n}
, we take
F(A_{n},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})=\{x_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,x_{k_{n}}^{(n)}\}
with
k_{n}=\#F(A_{n},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})
. It holds that
\cup_{1\leq i\leq k_{n}}\Gamma_{x_{i}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\supset A_{n}
since
\Gamma_{x_{i}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\supset\bar{B}(x_{i}^{(n)},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})
. Moreover,

\Gamma_{x_{i}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\subset\bar{B}(x_{i}^{(n)},\,\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})=\bar{B}(x_{i}^{(n)},\,\frac{1}{2^{nl_{1}}}),\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq k_{n}.

We point out that the closure of any ball
B
centered at
x\in A_{n}
with radius
\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n}
intersects at most
(4\lceil b\operatorname{d}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}
such
\Gamma_{x_{i}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})
. Indeed, if not, then the number of
x_{i}^{(n)}
in
\bar{B}(x,2\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\,\subset\Gamma_{x}(2\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})

(\subset\bar{B}(x,2\operatorname{d}\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\subset B(x,\frac{1}{2^{n}})\subset B(x,\rho_{b}(x)))
is more than
(4\lceil b\operatorname{d}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}
, which leads to at least two of them staying in the same element of the equi-partition of

by cubes with sides

, thus their distance on

not bigger than

, that contradicts the choice of
x_{i}^{(n)}
.

To obtain a partition of
A_{n}
, we define

\displaystyle B_{n;1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}\cap\Gamma_{x_{1}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n}),

\displaystyle B_{n;2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}\cap\Gamma_{x_{2}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\setminus B_{n;1},

\displaystyle B_{n;3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}\cap\Gamma_{x_{3}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\setminus(\cup_{1\leq i\leq 2}B_{n;i}),

\displaystyle B_{n;k_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}\cap\Gamma_{x_{k_{n}}^{(n)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{n})\setminus(\cup_{1\leq i\leq k_{n}-1}B_{n;i}).

For
s\geq n+1
, taking
F(A_{s}\setminus A_{s-1},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s})=\{x_{1}^{(s)},\cdots,x_{k_{s}}^{(s)}\}
with
k_{s}=\#F(A_{s}\setminus A_{s-1},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s})
, define

\displaystyle B_{s;1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{s}\setminus A_{s-1})\cap\Gamma_{x_{1}^{(s)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}),

\displaystyle B_{s;2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{s}\setminus A_{s-1})\cap\Gamma_{x_{2}^{(s)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s})\setminus B_{s;1},

\displaystyle B_{s;k_{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{s}\setminus A_{s-1})\cap\Gamma_{x_{k_{s}}^{(s)}}(\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s})\setminus(\cup_{1\leq i\leq k_{s}-1}B_{s;i}).

Then we may get two measurable partitions

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{B_{s,i}:\,\,1\leq i\leq k_{s},\,s\geq n\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{P}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{E_{s}=\cup_{1\leq i\leq k_{s}}B_{s,i}:\,s\geq n\Big\}.

In fact, one may see that

and

for

.

We continue by using

to partition each

into smaller sets. To be precise, denote
\mathcal{Y}(x_{i}^{(s)},\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s},l)=\{\Gamma_{1}^{(s,i,l)},\Gamma_{2}^{(s,i,l)},\cdots,\Gamma_{2^{l\operatorname{d}}}^{(s,i,l)}\}

\displaystyle B_{s;i,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{s;i}\cap\Gamma_{1}^{(s,i,l)},\,

\displaystyle B_{s;i,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{s;i}\cap\Gamma_{2}^{(s,i,l)}\setminus B_{s;i,1},

\displaystyle B_{s;i,2^{l\operatorname{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{s;i}\cap\Gamma_{2^{l\operatorname{d}}}^{(s,i,l)}\setminus(\cup_{1\leq j\leq 2^{l\operatorname{d}}-1}B_{s;i,j}).

Then, for any

, one can obtain a partition as the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}=

\displaystyle\Big\{B_{s;i,j}:\,\,1\leq i\leq k_{s},\,1\leq j\leq 2^{l\operatorname{d}},\,\,s\geq n\Big\}.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{\tilde{P}}\prec\mathcal{P}\prec\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}\,\mod 0\,
for any

. For convenience of statement, we denote
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}=\mathcal{P}
.

In the above construction,
n
stands for the regularity relative to the bounday and infinity;
l
is used to make the size of partitions arbitrarily small. The choice of
l
will be confirmed in the estimate of entropy with respect to evolutions.

Note that
A_{s}
is covered by at most
2^{s}
balls with regular size (which is
\leq 1
) and each of them can be further covered by at most
C_{0,1}(\lceil\frac{4}{\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}
balls with radius
\frac{\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}}{4}
(each such ball is contained in a ball centered at
A_{s}
with radius
\frac{\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}}{2}
), thus
k_{s}\leq 2^{s}\cdot C_{0,1}(\lceil\frac{4}{\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}
. Moreover, each element of
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}
is partitioned by

cubes in the constructoin of
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}
. Thus, for any
s\geq n
,

\displaystyle\#\Big\{B_{s;i,j}:\,\,1\leq i\leq k_{s},\,1\leq j\leq 2^{l\operatorname{d}}\Big\}\leq 2^{s}\cdot C_{0,1}(\lceil\frac{4}{\operatorname{\varepsilon}_{s}}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}\cdot 2^{l\operatorname{d}}

\displaystyle=C_{0,1}(\lceil 4\sqrt{\operatorname{d}}\rceil)^{\operatorname{d}}2^{s(1+l_{1}\operatorname{d})+l\operatorname{d}}:=C_{0}2^{s(1+l_{1}\operatorname{d})+l\operatorname{d}}.

Before going any further, we first show the finiteness of partition entropy
H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})
so that the constructed partitions are feasible in the computation of dynamical entropy.

Lemma 2.1
.

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})<\infty
for any integer
l\geq 0
.

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle p(x)=\max\Big\{

\displaystyle\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}\|D_{f^{j}(x)}f\|^{*},\,\,\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{*}^{-1}(f^{j}(x),\partial M),

\displaystyle\,\,\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}\rho^{-1}_{b}(f^{i}(x)),\,\,\Pi_{j=0}^{m-1}N_{b}(f^{j}(x))\quad\Big\}.

Note that if
x\in B_{s;i,j}
with
s\geq n+1
, then
x\notin A_{s-1}
, so
p(x)>2^{s-1}
. It follows that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int\log p(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=n}^{+\infty}\int_{E_{s}}\log p(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{n}}\log p(x)d\mu(x)+\sum_{s=n+1}^{+\infty}\int_{E_{s}}\log 2^{s-1}d\mu(x).

Together with (B), we deduce

\sum_{s=n}^{+\infty}\mu(E_{s})s<+\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=n}^{+\infty}\mu(E_{s})\log(C_{0}2^{s(1+l_{1}\operatorname{d})+l\operatorname{d}})<+\infty.

∎

3.
Estimates on entropy for dynamical evolutions

This section is devoted to the estimate of metric entropy of
g
by using the constructed partitions
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the entropy of
\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}
is finite, thus

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(g,\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=0}^{t-1}g^{-i}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\Big(H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}H_{\mu}(g^{-i}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\mid\bigvee_{j=0}^{i-1}g^{-j}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}H_{\mu}(g^{-i}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\mid\bigvee_{j=0}^{i-1}g^{-j}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(g^{-1}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\mid\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}).

Furthermore, it holds that

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(g^{-1}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\mid\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(g^{-1}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\vee g^{-1}(\mathcal{\tilde{P}})\mid\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(g^{-1}(\mathcal{\tilde{P}})\mid\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})+H_{\mu}(g^{-1}(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)})\mid\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}\vee g^{-1}(\mathcal{\tilde{P}}))

\displaystyle\sum_{s=L+1}^{+\infty}\int\big[-\frac{\mu(g^{-1}(E_{s})\cap\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x))}{\mu(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x))}\log\frac{\mu(g^{-1}(E_{s})\cap\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x))}{\mu(\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(0)}(x))}\big]d\mu(x)

\displaystyle 1.

For the expression of
\operatorname{I}

\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}
stays below in the form of conditional entropy. In order to make

\mathcal{P}_{n}^{(l)}

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(f)=\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{1}{m}h_{\mu}(f^{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{1}{m}\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}(f^{m},x)>0}\lambda_{i}(f^{m},x)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{i}(f,x)>0}\lambda_{i}(f,x)\,d\mu.

This completes the proof of Theorem

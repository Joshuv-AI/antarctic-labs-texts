We consider backward fractional Kolmogorov equations with singular Besov drift of low regularity and singular terminal conditions. To treat drifts beyond the socalled Young regime, we assume an enhancement assumption on the drift and consider paracontrolled terminal conditions.
Our work generalizes previous results on the equation from
[
CC18
,
KP22
]
to the case of
singular paracontrolled terminal conditions
and simultaneously treats singular and non-singular data in one concise solution theory. We introduce a paracontrolled solution space, that implies parabolic time and space regularity on the solution without introducing the socalled ”modified paraproduct” from
[
GP17
]
. The tools developed in this article apply for general linear PDEs that can be tackled with the paracontrolled ansatz.

Keywords: fractional Laplace operator, paracontrolled distributions, singular terminal conditions

Introduction

Kolmogorov equations are second order parabolic differential equations. Their connection to stochastic processes was already investigated by Kolmogorov in the seminal work
[
Kol31
]
. There exist analytic and probabilistic methods to study Kolmogorov equations. We refer to the books
[
KZR99
,
DP04
,
Kry08
,
BKRS15
]
for an overview on Kolmogorov equations in both finite and infinite dimensional spaces. In the finite dimensional setting, Kolmogorov equations with bounded and measurable coefficients and uniformly elliptic diffusion coefficients can be treated as a special case of the infinite dimensional Dirichlet form methods of
[
MR95
]
, see also
[
KZR99
, Section 2.4.1]
and the connection to the martingale problem in
[
KZR99
, Section 6.1.2]
. We remain in the finite-dimensional setting, but consider distributional drifts in Besov spaces. Besov spaces play well with the paracontrolled calculus that defines products of distributions, cf. the Littlewood-Paley theory in
[
BCD11
]
. Previous articles that consider distributional drifts are
[
FRW03
,
FIR17
]
, as well as
[
CC18
]
in the setting of rougher distributional drifts. Heat kernel estimates for the solution to the Kolmogorov equation were established in
[
ZZ17
,
PvZ22
]
.

In the article
[
KP22
]
, the Laplace operator is replaced by a generalized fractional Laplacian. We extend our previous results on the equation from
[
KP22
]
to allow for irregular terminal conditions. That is, we consider the fractional parabolic Kolmogorov backward equation

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{t}-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}+V\cdot\nabla\big)u=f,\quad u(T,\cdot)=u^{T},

on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
generalizes the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
for
\alpha\in(1,2]

and
V
is a vector-valued Besov drift with negative regularity
\beta\in(\frac{2-2\alpha}{3},0)
, i.e.
V\in C([0,T],(B^{\beta}_{\infty,\infty})^{d})=C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. Since
V
is a distribution, we need to be careful with well-definedness of the product
V\cdot\nabla u
.
The regularity obtained from
-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
suggests that
u(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha+\beta}
if right-hand side
f
and terminal condition
u^{T}
are regular enough. Therefore we have
\nabla u(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha+\beta-1}
. Since the product
V(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla u(t,\cdot)
is well-defined if and only if the sum of the regularities of the factors is strictly positive, we obtain the condition
\alpha+2\beta-1>0
, equivalently
\beta>(1-\alpha)/2
. We call this the
Young regime
, in analogy to the regularity requirements that are needed for the construction of the Young integral. However, we go beyond the Young regime, considering also the so-called
rough regime

\beta\in(\frac{2-2\alpha}{3},\frac{1-\alpha}{2}]
. In the rough case, we employ paracontrolled distributions (cf.
[
GIP15
]
) to solve the equation. The idea is to gain some regularity by treating
u
as a perturbation of the solution of the linearized equation with additive noise,
\partial_{t}w=\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}w-V
. The techniques work as long as the nonlinearity
V\cdot\nabla u
is of lower order than the linear operator
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
, i.e. for
\alpha>1
or equivalently
(2-2\alpha)/3<(1-\alpha)/2
. The price one has to pay to go beyond the Young regime is a stronger assumption on
V
. That is, we assume that certain resonant products involving
V
are a priori given. Those play the role of the iterated integrals in rough paths theory (cf.
[
FH20
]
). We then enhance
V
by that resonant product component and call the enhancement
\mathscr{V}
.

In
[
CC18
,
KP22
]
, only regular terminal conditions were considered, i.e.
u^{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha+\beta}
in the Young regime and
u^{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{2(\alpha+\beta)-1}
in the rough regime. The right-hand side
f
can either be an element of
C_{T}L^{\infty}
or
f=V^{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
. There are techniques available to treat less regular terminal conditions, cf.
[
GP17
, Section 6]
. With the help of those techniques, one can allow for terminal conditions
u^{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{(1-\gamma)\alpha+\beta}
in the Young case and
u^{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{(2-\gamma)\alpha+2\beta-1}
in the rough case for
\gamma\in[0,1)
, obtaining a solution
u_{t}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha+\beta}
for
t<T
and blow-up
\gamma
for
t\to T
. In this work, consider moreover
singular paracontrolled
right-hand sides
f
as well as
singular paracontrolled terminal condition

u^{T}
, which includes all cases mentioned above. Moreover, we can consider
f
and
u^{T}
more generally as elements of Besov spaces
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}=B_{p,\infty}^{\theta}
with integrability parameter
p\in[1,\infty]
. Examples for terminal conditions that we cover in the rough case include the Dirac measure, that is
u^{T}=\delta_{0}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}_{1}
, and
u_{T}=V(T,\cdot)
. To be more precise, in the rough regime, we assume paracontrolled right-hand sides and terminal conditions,

\displaystyle f=f^{\sharp}+f^{\prime}\varolessthan V,\quad u^{T}=u^{T,\sharp}+u^{T,\prime}\varolessthan V_{T},

with
u^{T,\prime},f^{\prime}_{t}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha+\beta-1}_{p}
and remainders
f^{\sharp}_{t}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha+2\beta-1}_{p}
,
u^{T,\sharp}\in\mathscr{C}^{(2-\gamma)\alpha+2\beta-1}_{p}
for
\gamma\in[0,1)
. For
f^{\prime}_{t}
and
f^{\sharp}_{t}
we also allow a blow-up
\gamma
for
t\to T
.
We prove existence and uniqueness of mild solutions of the Kolmogorov backward equation for singular paracontrolled data
(f,u^{T})
. The paracontrolled solution is an element of the solution space with blow-up
\gamma
at terminal time
T
.
As a byproduct, we prove a new commutator estimate for the
(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})
-semigroup, cf.
Lemma

3.4
, that allows to gain not only space regularity, but also time regularity. Thanks to
Lemma

3.4
there is no need for the so-called “modified paraproduct” from
[
GP17
, Section 6.1]
. Moreover, we prove continuity of the Kolmogorov solution map
and a uniform bound for the solutions considered on subintervals of
[0,T]
for bounded sets of data.

It is important to mention that the techniques we develop in this article are not limited to that particular equation and can be used to treat other linear singular PDEs with the paracontrolled approach. In this sense we see the Kolmogorov PDE as a model example.

The work is structured as follows. In
Section

2
we introduce the generalized fractional Laplacian
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
and its semigroup. We prove semigroup and commutator estimates.
In
Section

3
we introduce the solution spaces and prove generalized Schauder and commutator estimates thereon.
Finally, we solve the Kolmogorov equation with singular paracontrolled data
(f,u^{T})
in
Section

4
and prove continuity of the solution map, as well as a uniform bound for the solutions on subintervals.

2
Preliminaries

Below we introduce some technical ingredients about Besov spaces and paraproducts, that we will need in the sequel.
We study estimates for the generalized fractional Laplacian and its semigroup, as well as, commutator estimates involving the paraproducts and the fractional semigroup.

Let
(p_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
be a smooth dyadic partition of unity, i.e. a family of functions
p_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
j\geqslant-1
, such that

1.)

p_{-1}
and
p_{0}
are non-negative radial functions (they just depend on the absolute value of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
), such that the support of
p_{-1}
is contained in a ball and the support of
p_{0}
is contained in an annulus;

2.)

p_{j}(x):=p_{0}(2^{-j}x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j\geqslant 0
;

3.)

\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}p_{j}(x)=1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; and

4.)

\operatorname{supp}(p_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(p_{j})=\emptyset
for all
\lvert i-j\rvert>1
.

We then define the Besov spaces for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{p,q}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\lVert u\rVert_{B^{\theta}_{p,q}}=\big\lVert(2^{j\theta}\lVert\Delta_{j}u\rVert_{L^{p}})_{j\geqslant-1}\big\rVert_{\ell^{q}}<\infty\},

(2.1)

where
\Delta_{j}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(p_{j}\mathcal{F}u)
are the Littlewood-Paley blocks, and the Fourier transform is defined with the normalization
\hat{\varphi}(y):=\mathcal{F}\varphi(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle}dx
(and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi(x)=\hat{\varphi}(-x)
); moreover,
\mathcal{S}
are the Schwartz functions and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are the Schwartz distributions.

Let
C^{\infty}_{b}=C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
denote the space of bounded and smooth functions with bounded partial derivatives.
For
q=\infty
, the space
B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
has the unpleasant property that
C^{\infty}_{b}\subset B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
is not dense.
Therefore, we rather work with the following space:

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}:=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\mid\lim_{j\to\infty}2^{j\theta}\lVert\Delta_{j}u\rVert_{L^{p}}=0\},

(2.2)

for which
C^{\infty}_{b}
is a dense subset (cf.
[
BCD11
, Remark 2.75]
). We also use the notation
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=(\mathscr{C}^{\theta})^{d}=\mathscr{C}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathscr{C}^{\theta-}:=\bigcap_{\gamma<\theta}\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\theta+}=\bigcup_{\gamma>\theta}\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}
. Furthermore, we introduce the notation
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}:=B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, where
\mathscr{C}^{\theta}:=\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\infty}
with norm denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\theta}:=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}}
.

For
1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant\infty
,
1\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{p_{1},q_{1}}
is continuously embedded in
B_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{s-d(1/p_{1}-1/p_{2})}
(cf.
[
BCD11
, Proposition 2.71]
). Furthermore, we will use that for
u\in B^{s}_{p,q}
and a multi-index
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\lVert\partial^{n}u\rVert_{B^{s-\lvert n\rvert}_{p,q}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{B^{s}_{p,q}}
, which follows from the more general multiplier result from
[
BCD11
, Proposition 2.78]
.

We recall from Bony’s paraproduct theory (cf.
[
BCD11
, Section 2]
) that in general for
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\theta}
and
v\in\mathscr{C}^{\beta}
with
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the product
uv:=u\varolessthan v+u\varogreaterthan v+u\varodot v
, is well defined in
\mathscr{C}^{\min(\theta,\beta,\theta+\beta)}
if and only if
\theta+\beta>0
. Denoting
S_{i}u=\sum_{j=-1}^{i-1}\Delta_{j}u
, the paraproducts are defined as follows

\displaystyle u\varolessthan v:=\sum_{i\geqslant-1}S_{i-1}u\Delta_{i}v,\quad u\varogreaterthan v:=v\varolessthan u,\quad u\varodot v:=\sum_{\lvert i-j\rvert\leqslant 1}\Delta_{i}u\Delta_{j}v.

Here, we use the notation of
[
MP19
,
MW17
]
for the para- and resonant products
\varolessthan,\varogreaterthan
and
\varodot
.

In estimates we often use the notation
a\lesssim b
, which means, that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
a\leqslant Cb
. In the case that we want to stress the dependence of the constant
C(d)
in the estimate on a parameter
d
, we write
a\lesssim_{d}b
.

The paraproducts satisfy the following estimates for
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\leqslant 1
and
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
PvZ22
, Theorem A.1]
and
[
BCD11
, Theorem 2.82, Theorem 2.85]
)

\displaystyle\lVert u\varodot v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta+\beta}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta+\beta>0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lVert u\varolessthan v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta>0,

\displaystyle\lVert u\varolessthan v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta+\theta}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}},\qquad\text{if }\theta<0.

So if
\theta+\beta>0
, we have
\lVert uv\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\gamma}_{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p_{1}}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p_{2}}}
for
\gamma:=\min(\theta,\beta,\theta+\beta)
.

We define the generalized fractional Laplacian
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
via Fourier analysis as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and let
\nu
be a symmetric (i.e.
\nu(A)=\nu(-A)
), finite and non-zero measure on the unit sphere
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}\varphi)\qquad\text{for $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}$,}

(2.4)

where

\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}(z):=\int_{S}\lvert\langle z,\xi\rangle\rvert^{\alpha}\nu(d\xi).

For
\alpha=2
, we set
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}:=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta
.

Remark 2.2
.

If we take
\nu
as a suitable multiple of the Lebesgue measure on the sphere, then
\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}(z)=|2\pi z|^{\alpha}
and thus
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
is the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
.

Assumption 2.3
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the measure
\nu
from
Definition

2.1
has
d
-dimensional support, in the sense that the linear span of its support is
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

So far we defined
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
on
C^{\infty}_{b}
, so in particular on Schwartz functions. But the definition of
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
on Schwartz distributions by duality is problematic, because for
\alpha\in(0,2)
the function
\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}
has a singularity in
0
. This motivates the next proposition.

Proposition 2.4

(Continuity of the operator
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2]
. Then for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{b}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}.

In particular,
\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}
can be uniquely extended to a continuous operator from
\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}
to
\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{p}
.

Proof.

For
j\geqslant 0
it follows from
[
BCD11
, Lemma 2.2]
the estimate
\|\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\beta-\alpha)}\|u\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}
. This uses that
\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}
is infinitely continuously differentiable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with
\lvert\partial^{\mu}\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}(z)\rvert\lesssim\lvert z\rvert^{\alpha-\lvert\mu\rvert}
for a multi-index
\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\lvert\mu\rvert:=\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d}\leqslant\alpha
and that
\Delta_{j}u
has a Fourier transform, which is supported in
2^{j}\cal{A}
, where
\cal{A}
is the annulus, where
p_{0}
is supported. For
j=-1
we use that
-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\varphi=A\varphi
for test functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}
and
A
defined as

\displaystyle A\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\{\lvert y\rvert\leqslant 1\}}(y)\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot y\big)\mu(dy)\qquad\text{for }\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}.

(2.5)

and therefore

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}(x+y)-\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}u(x)-\nabla\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}u(x)\cdot y\mathds{1}_{\{|y|\leqslant 1\}}\right)\mu(dy)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B(0,1)}\|D^{2}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}|y|^{2}\mu(dy)+\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mu(B(0,1)^{c})\lesssim 1

where
B(0,1)=\{|y|\leqslant 1\}
and
\tilde{p}_{-1}
is smooth and compactly supported in a ball and such that
\tilde{p}_{-1}p_{-1}=p_{-1}
.
Then we obtain with
-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\Delta_{-1}u=-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}\ast\Delta_{-1}u
and Young’s convolution inequality,

\displaystyle\lVert-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\Delta_{-1}u\rVert_{L^{p}}\leqslant\lVert-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}\rVert_{L^{1}}\lVert\Delta_{-1}u\rVert_{L^{p}}\leqslant\lVert-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{p}_{-1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\Delta_{-1}u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}.

∎

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, we also have

\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}(z)=|z|^{\alpha}\int_{S}\Big|\Big\langle\frac{z}{|z|},\xi\Big\rangle\Big|^{\alpha}\nu(d\xi)\geqslant|z|^{\alpha}\min_{|y|=1}\int_{S}|\langle y,\xi\rangle|^{\alpha}\nu(d\xi),

and by
2.3
the minimum on the right hand side is strictly positive. Otherwise, there exists some
y_{0}\neq 0
with
\int_{S}|\langle y_{0},\xi\rangle|^{\alpha}\nu(d\xi)=0
and this would mean that the support of
\nu
(and thus also its span) is contained in the orthogonal complement of
\operatorname{span}(y_{0})
. Therefore,
e^{-\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}}
decays faster than any polynomial at infinity and outside of
0
it even behaves like a Schwartz function.

Lemma 2.5

(Semigroup estimates)
.

Let
\nu
be a finite, symmetric measure on the sphere
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
2.3
. Let
P_{t}\varphi:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{-t\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}}\hat{\varphi})=\rho_{t}\ast\varphi
, where
t>0
,
\rho_{t}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}e^{-t\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}}\in L^{1}
, and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{b}
. Then we have for
\vartheta\geqslant 0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\in[1,\infty]

\displaystyle\lVert P_{t}\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta+\vartheta}_{p}}\lesssim(t^{-\vartheta/\alpha}\vee 1)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}},

(2.6)

and for
\vartheta\in[0,\alpha]

\displaystyle\lVert(P_{t}-\operatorname{Id})\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\vartheta}_{p}}\lesssim t^{\vartheta/\alpha}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}.

(2.7)

Furthermore, for
\beta\in(0,1)
,
p=\infty
,

\displaystyle\lVert(P_{t}-\operatorname{Id})\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{\beta/\alpha}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}}.

(2.8)

Therefore, if
\vartheta\geqslant 0
, then
P_{t}
has a unique extension to a bounded linear operator in
L(\mathcal{C}^{\beta},\mathcal{C}^{\beta+\vartheta})
and this extension satisfies the same bounds.

Proof.

In the case
\theta\in[0,\alpha)
, this follows from
[
GIP15
, Lemma A.5]
, see also
[
GIP15
, Lemma A.7]
, whose generalization to integrability

is immediate. For the case
\vartheta=\alpha
in (
2.7
), we estimate

\displaystyle\lVert(P_{t}-\operatorname{Id})\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{t}(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})P_{r}\varphi dr\bigg\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\lVert(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})P_{r}\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta-\alpha}_{p}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\lVert P_{r}\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}dr\lesssim t\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}

using
Proposition

2.4
and (
2.6
) for
\vartheta=0
. (
2.8
) follows from
[
GIP15
, Lemma A.8]
.
∎

Let

\displaystyle\lVert(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})P_{t}(u\varolessthan v)-u\varolessthan(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})P_{t}v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\sigma+\varsigma-\alpha+\theta}_{p}}\lesssim(t^{-\theta/\alpha}\vee 1)\lVert u\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\sigma}_{p}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\varsigma}}.

The mild formulation of the Kolmogorov equation is given by

\displaystyle u_{t}=P_{T-t}u_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}P_{r-t}(V_{r}\cdot\nabla u_{r}-f_{r})dr=:P_{T-t}u_{T}+J^{T}(V\cdot\nabla u-f)(t).

(2.10)

Due to the Schauder estimates, considering a singular terminal condition with

.
This motivates the definition of blow-up spaces below, from which we can build the solution space in the next section.

For

,

, and a Banach space

,

(2.11)

(2.12)

3
Schauder theory and commutator estimates for blow-up spaces

In this section, we define the solution space
\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\alpha+\beta}
and prove Schauder and commutator estimates. We conclude the section with interpolation estimates for the solution spaces.

Heuristically, the solution space shall combine maximal space regularity (i.e.
\alpha+\beta
) in a time-blow-up space with maximal time regularity (i.e. Lipschitz) in a space of low space regularity. By interpolation, the solution will then also admit all time and space regularities “in between”.

Let us thus define for
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\theta}:=\mathcal{M}_{T}^{\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}\cap C_{T}^{1-\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}\cap C_{T}^{\gamma,1}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}.

(3.1)

We moreover define for
\gamma=0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T}^{0,\theta}:=C_{T}^{1}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}\cap C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p},

(3.2)

where
C^{1}_{T}X
denotes the space of
1
-Hölder or Lipschitz functions with values in
X
.

For
\overline{T}\in(0,T)
, we define
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma,\theta}:=\mathcal{M}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}\cap C_{\overline{T},T}^{1-\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}\cap C_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma,1}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}
and similarly
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T}^{0,\theta}
.

The spaces
\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\theta}
are Banach spaces equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\theta}}

\displaystyle:=\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}_{T}^{\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}}+\lVert u\rVert_{C_{T}^{1-\gamma}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}}+\lVert u\rVert_{C_{T}^{\gamma,1}\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T)}(T-t)^{\gamma}\lVert u_{t}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{p}}+\sup_{0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T}\frac{\lVert u_{t}-u_{s}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}}}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{1-\gamma}}+\sup_{0\leqslant s<t<T}\frac{(T-t)^{\gamma}\lVert u_{t}-u_{s}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}_{p}}}{\lvert t-s\rvert}.$}

Notice, that
u\in\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\theta}
in particular implies that
t\mapsto\lVert u_{t}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\theta-\alpha}}
is
(1-\gamma)
-Hölder continuous at

.

Lemma 3.1

Corollary 3.2

(Schauder estimates)
.

Let
(P_{t})
and
\nu
be as in
Lemma

2.5
. Let
T>0
,
\overline{T}\in(0,T]
. For

,

,

\displaystyle\lVert P_{T-\cdot}w\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma,\beta+\vartheta}}\lesssim\overline{T}^{(\gamma\alpha-\vartheta)/\alpha}\lVert w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}

(3.4)

and for

,

\displaystyle\lVert J^{T}v\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma,\beta+\alpha}}\lesssim\overline{T}^{\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{p}}.

(3.5)

Proof.

For (
3.4
) we only prove the estimate in

and in

, the estimate in

follows from a direct application of

2.5
.

Therefore we write

\displaystyle\lVert P_{T-t}w-P_{T-s}w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta+\vartheta-\alpha}}\lesssim\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{t-s})w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta+\vartheta-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{1-\vartheta/\alpha}\lVert w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\overline{T}^{(\gamma\alpha-\vartheta)/\alpha}(t-s)^{1-\gamma}\lVert w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta}}

using

and

. This controls

\displaystyle\lesssim(T-t)^{-\vartheta/\alpha}\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{t-s})w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T-t)^{-\vartheta/\alpha}(t-s)\lVert w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\overline{T}^{(\gamma\alpha-\vartheta)/\alpha}(T-t)^{-\gamma}(t-s)\lVert w\rVert_{\mathscr{C}_{p}^{\beta}}.

To estimate the

-norm in (
3.5
), we use

To prove the bounds on the time regularity in (

using that

On the other hand, we can also estimate that term by

Moreover, by

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert P_{T-\cdot}u^{T}+J^{T}(f)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{T}^{0,\alpha+\beta}}+[\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T}^{\gamma,\alpha+\beta}}+\lVert u_{T}\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\alpha+\beta}_{p}}]\lVert V\rVert_{C_{T}\mathscr{C}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}.

(4.2)

This implies that indeed

and we constructed the solution on
[T-\overline{T},T]
.

Moreover, the choice of
\overline{T}
does not depend on the terminal condition

and therefore we can iterate the construction of the solution on subintervals

for

and

such that

. Here, we choose the terminal condition of the solution on
[T-k\overline{T},T-(k-1)\overline{T}]
equal to the initial value of the solution constructed in the previous iteration step. We then obtain the solution

on
[0,T]
by patching the solutions on the subintervals together. Indeed,

is the fixed point of

, due to the semigroup property

for

.

The continuity of the solution map follows from

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{T}^{0,\alpha+\beta}}\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T-k\overline{T}}^{0,\alpha+\beta}}

for

such that
T-(n+1)\overline{T}\leqslant 0
, together with (
4
) applied for each of the terms on the right-hand-side and the contraction property on each of the spaces
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{T},T-k\overline{T}}^{\gamma,\alpha+\beta}
.
For a terminal condition
u^{T}\in\mathscr{C}^{(1-\gamma)\alpha+\beta}_{p}
, the above arguments show that we obtain a solution in
\mathcal{L}_{T}^{\gamma,\alpha+\beta}
. Notice that the blow-up just occures for the solution on the last subinterval
[T-\overline{T},T]
. That is, the solutions on
[T-k\overline{T},T-(k-1)\overline{T}]
for

have a regular terminal condition in
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha+\beta}_{p}
.
∎

Remark 4.4

Remark 4.6

The intuition behind the paracontrolled ansatz is as follows. Assume for simplicitiy regular data

Remark 4.13

(Super-exponential dependency of the Lipschitz constant on
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
)
.

The Lipschitz constant of the solution map on
[0,T]
depends super-exponentially on the norms
\lVert\mathcal{V}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{\beta,\gamma^{\prime}}}
,
\lVert\mathcal{W}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{\beta,\gamma^{\prime}}}
. Indeed, to obtain the Lipschitz estimate of the solution map on
[0,T]
, we have to apply the estimate in (
4
) on every subinterval
[T-(k+1)\overline{T},T-k\overline{T}]
, where we have to choose
\overline{T}
small enough so that
\overline{T}^{\kappa}<C^{-1}
for
\kappa=\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}\wedge\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma^{\prime\prime}
and for the constant
C=C(\lVert\mathcal{V}\rVert,\lVert\mathcal{W}\rVert,\lVert u^{T}\rVert,\lVert v^{T}\rVert,\lVert f\rVert,\lVert g\rVert)
. This means that in (
4
) we have to iterate the estimate at least
T/C^{-\kappa}=TC^{\kappa}
times, and each time we multiply with the constant
\tilde{C}
in (
4
), leading roughly speaking to a factor
\tilde{C}^{TC^{\kappa}}
. By doing the analysis more carefully we can show that there is (super-)exponential dependence only on
\lVert\mathcal{V}\rVert,\lVert\mathcal{W}\rVert
and that the Lipschitz constant actually depends linearly on
\lVert u^{T}\rVert,\lVert v^{T}\rVert,\lVert f\rVert,\lVert g\rVert
. But the super-exponential dependence on
\lVert\mathcal{V}\rVert,\lVert\mathcal{W}\rVert
is inherent to the problem and we expect that it cannot be significantly improved. By similar arguments, we also see that the norm of the solution
u
to the Kolmogorov backward equation in
Theorem

4.7
depends super-exponentially on
\|\mathcal{V}\|
.

This will be relevant when we take
\mathcal{V}
random, cf. the Brox diffusion with Lévy noise in
[
KP22
]
. If we do not have super-exponential moments for
\lVert\mathcal{V}\rVert_{\mathcal{X}^{\beta,\gamma^{\prime}}}
, then we do not know if
u
has finite moments. And if
V
is Gaussian, then the second component of the lift
\mathcal{V}
is a second order polynomial of a Gaussian and therefore it does not have super-exponential moments.

But note that this only concerns Hölder norms of the Kolmogorov backward equation. If we are only interested in the
L^{p}
norm,
p\in[1,\infty]
, we can always use the trivial bound
\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|u^{T}\|_{L^{p}}+T\|f\|_{L^{p}}
, provided that the right-hand side is finite, which holds for smooth
V,f,u^{T}
by the stochastic representation (Feynman-Kac) of the Kolmogorov backward equation, and which extends by approximation to the general setting.

Next we consider solutions of the Kolmorov PDE for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathscr{V}}
(for fixed
\mathscr{V}
) on subintervals
[0,r]
of
[0,T]
for bounded sets of terminal conditions

and right-hand-sides

. In certain situations one is interested in a uniform bound on the norms of the solutions

on

with

\displaystyle u^{r,\sharp}

\displaystyle=P_{r-\cdot}y^{r,\sharp}+J^{r}(-f^{\sharp})+J^{r}(\nabla u^{r}\varodot V)+J^{r}(V\varolessthan\nabla u^{r})

Proof of
Lemma

.

The proof of the lemma uses ideas from the proof of
[
Per14
, Lemma 5.3.20]
. Let
\psi=p_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
and let
j\geqslant 0
(for
j=-1
,
\Delta_{j}(f\varolessthan g)=0
, so there is nothing to estimate).
Then we estimate (notation:
S_{j-1}u:=\sum_{l\leqslant j-1}\Delta_{l}u
)

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{j}[(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})(f\varolessthan g)-f\varolessthan(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})g]\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-\psi_{\nu}^{\alpha}p_{j})(x-y)(S_{j-1}f(y)-S_{j-1}f(x))\Delta_{j}g(y)dy\bigg\rvert^{p}dx\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lvert\eta\rvert=1}\lVert[z\mapsto z^{\eta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-\psi_{\nu}^{\alpha}p_{j})(z)]\ast\partial^{\eta}(S_{j-1}f)\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\Delta_{j}g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lvert\eta\rvert=1}\lVert z\mapsto z^{\eta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{j})(z)\rVert_{L^{1}}\lVert\partial^{\eta}S_{j-1}f\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\Delta_{j}g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

for a multi-index
\eta
and using that
S_{j-1}f(x)-S_{j-1}f(y)=\int_{0}^{1}DS_{j-1}f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y))(x-y)d\lambda
with
\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y=(1+\lambda)x-\lambda y-(x-y)
(and substituting
y\to x-y
,
x\to(1+\lambda)x-\lambda y
) and Young’s inequality for the last estimate. We have that, as
\sigma<1
,

\displaystyle\lVert\partial^{\eta}S_{j-1}f\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\Delta_{j}g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma-1+\varsigma)}\lVert\partial^{\eta}f\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\sigma-1}_{p}}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\varsigma}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma-1+\varsigma)}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\sigma}_{p}}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\varsigma}}.

Moreover, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert z\mapsto z^{\eta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(-\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{j})(z)\rVert_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=2^{j\alpha}\lVert z\mapsto z^{\eta}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}(2^{-j}\cdot)p_{0}(2^{-j}\cdot))(z)\rVert_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=2^{j\alpha}2^{-j}\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\partial^{\eta}[\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{0}](2^{-j}\cdot))\rVert_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(\alpha-1)}

using that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\partial^{\eta}[\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{0}](2^{-j}\cdot))\rVert_{L^{1}}=\lVert 2^{jd}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\partial^{\eta}[\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{0}])(2^{-j}\cdot)\rVert_{L^{1}}=\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\partial^{\eta}[\psi^{\alpha}_{\nu}p_{0}])\rVert_{L^{1}}<\infty.

Together we have

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{j}[(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})(f\varolessthan g)-f\varolessthan(-\mathfrak{L}^{\alpha}_{\nu})g]\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma-\alpha)}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\sigma}_{p}}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{\varsigma}},

which yields the claim.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

The proof of the lemma uses the ideas from the proof of
[
GIP15
, Lemma A.9]
.
Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{T-t}{2})
to be chosen later. Then we have that for
j\geqslant-1

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\int_{t}^{T}f_{t,r}dr=\int_{t}^{T}\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}dr=\int_{t+\delta}^{T}\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}dr+\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}dr.

The first summand we estimate as follows, using Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{t+\delta}^{T}\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}dr\bigg\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{t+\delta}^{T}\lVert\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}\rVert_{L^{p}}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant C2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma+\varepsilon\varsigma)}\int_{t+\delta}^{T}(T-r)^{-\gamma}(r-t)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}dr

\displaystyle=C2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma+\varepsilon\varsigma)}(T-t)^{-\gamma-\varepsilon}\int_{\delta/(T-t)}^{1}(1-r)^{-\gamma}r^{-(1+\varepsilon)}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant[2\max(\varepsilon^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1})C]2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma+\varepsilon\varsigma)}(T-t)^{-\gamma-\varepsilon}(\delta/(T-t))^{-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=[2\max(\varepsilon^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1})C]2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma)}(T-t)^{-\gamma}(2^{j\varsigma}\delta)^{-\varepsilon},

where we used that for
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, as
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma<1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}^{1}(1-r)^{-\gamma}r^{-(1+\varepsilon)}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\sigma}^{1/2}(1-r)^{-\gamma}r^{-(1+\varepsilon)}dr+\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-r)^{-\gamma}r^{-(1+\varepsilon)}dr

\displaystyle\leqslant[(\frac{1}{2})^{-\gamma}\varepsilon^{-1}+(\frac{1}{2})^{-\gamma}(1-\gamma)^{-1}]\sigma^{-\varepsilon}\leqslant 2\max(\varepsilon^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1})\sigma^{-\varepsilon}.

For the second summand, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\Delta_{j}f_{t,r}dr\bigg\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C2^{-j\sigma}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}(T-r)^{-\gamma}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{1-\gamma}2^{-j\sigma}[(T-t)^{1-\gamma}-(T-t-\delta)^{1-\gamma}]

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{1-\gamma}2^{-j\sigma}(T-t)^{-\gamma}[(T-t)-(T-t-\delta)\big(\frac{T-t}{T-t-\delta}\big)^{\gamma}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{1-\gamma}2^{-j\sigma}(T-t)^{-\gamma}\delta.

The goal is to estimate
\sup_{j\geqslant-1}2^{j(\sigma+\varsigma)}\big\lVert\Delta_{j}\int_{t}^{T}f_{t,r}dr\big\rVert_{L^{p}}
. For that purpose, we use for
j
such that
2^{-j\varsigma}\leqslant\frac{T-t}{2}
the above estimates for
\delta=2^{-j\varsigma}
. If
j
is such that
2^{-j\varsigma}>\frac{T-t}{2}
, then we trivially estimate

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\Delta_{j}\int_{t}^{T}f_{t,r}dr\bigg\rVert_{L^{p}}\leqslant C2^{-j\sigma}\int_{t}^{T}(T-r)^{-\gamma}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{1-\gamma}2^{-j\sigma}(T-t)^{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{1-\gamma}2^{-j(\sigma+\varsigma)}(T-t)^{-\gamma}

using
\gamma<1
. Together we thus obtain uniformly in
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geqslant-1}2^{j(\sigma+\varsigma)}\bigg\lVert\Delta_{j}\int_{t}^{T}f_{t,r}dr\bigg\rVert_{L^{p}}\leqslant[2\max(\varepsilon^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1})C](T-t)^{-\gamma},

which yields the claim.

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, applying the Newton method, we transform the complex continuous-time algebraic Riccati matrix equation into a Lyapunov equation. Then, we introduce an efficient general alternating-direction implicit (GADI) method to solve the Lyapunov equation. The inexact Newton-GADI method is presented to save computational amount effectively. Moreover, we analyze the convergence of the Newton-GADI method. The convergence rate of the Newton-GADI and Newton-ADI methods is compared by analyzing
their spectral radii. Furthermore, we give a way to select the quasi-optimal parameter.
Corresponding numerical tests are shown to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms.

Keywords

Complex algebraic Riccati equation; Newton method;
alternating-direction implicit method; convergence analysis.

2
Newton-GADI algorithms

It is well known that one classical approach to solving the CARE (
1
) is to tackle its nonlinearity
with a Newton-type method. In this section, we apply the Newton method to transform this equation into a Lyapunov equation. Then we use the GADI method to solve the Lyapunov equation. The whole framework is denoted by the Newton-GADI.
To save computational amount, the inexact Newton-GADI method is presented. Further, we give the convergence analysis of the Newton-GADI algorithm.

2.1
Iteration schemes

Now we introduce the usual scheme of the Newton method
for solving the CARE (
1
).

Define the mapping
\mathcal{R}:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(X)=A^{*}X+XA-XKX+Q,~X\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},

where
A,~K,~Q
are defined in (
1
). The first Fr
\acute{\mbox{e}}
chet derivative of
\mathcal{R}

at a matrix
X
is a linear map

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{X}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{X}{(E)}=(A-KX)^{*}E+E(A-KX).

The Newton method for the CARE (
1
) is

\displaystyle X_{k+1}=X_{k}-(\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{X_{k}})^{-1}\mathcal{R}(X_{k}),~k=0,1,\cdots,

(4)

given that the map
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{X_{k}}
is invertible.
The Newton iteration scheme (
4
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle(A-KX_{k})^{*}X_{k+1}+X_{k+1}(A-KX_{k})+X_{k}KX_{k}+Q=0.

(5)

The convergence property for the Newton scheme (
5
) is illustrated by the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
23
]

For the CARE (
1
), assume that
(A,K)
is stabilizable and
(A,Q)
is detectable,
starting with any Hermitian matrix
X_{0}
such that
A-KX_{0}
is stable,
the sequence of Hermitian matrices
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

determined by (
5
)
quadratic converges to the Hermitian
positive semi-definite solution
X_{*}
of (
1
).
In other words, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\|X_{k}-X_{*}\|\leq\delta\|X_{k-1}-X_{*}\|^{2},~~k=0,1,\cdots,

where
\|\cdot\|
is any given matrix norm.
Moreover, the iteration sequence
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
has a monotone convergence behavior, i.e.,

\displaystyle 0\leq X_{*}\leq\cdots\leq X_{k+1}\leq X_{k}\leq\cdots\leq X_{1}.

Denote
A_{k}=KX_{k}-A
and
F(X_{k})=X_{k}KX_{k}+Q
, where the mapping
F:\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
Actually, due to
A-KX_{k}
is stable, then
A_{k}
is stable,

i.e.,

\mbox{Re}(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))>0
.
The iteration scheme can be written as

\displaystyle A_{k}^{*}X_{k+1}+X_{k+1}A_{k}=F(X_{k}).

(6)

It is required to solve the Lyapunov equation (
6
)
in each Newton iteration step.
We recall an existence and uniqueness
theorem for this equation.

Theorem 2.2
.

The Lyapunov equation

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{*}Y+Y\hat{A}+Z=0

has a uniquely Hermitian positive semi-definite solution
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
if and only if
\hat{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is stable, for any Hermitian matrix
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.

We apply the GADI framework in
[
32
]
to solve the Lyapunov equation (
6
). The GADI scheme is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\alpha_{k}I+A_{k}^{*})X_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=X_{k+1,\ell}(\alpha_{k}I-A_{k})+F(X_{k}),\\
X_{k+1,\ell+1}(\alpha_{k}I+A_{k})=X_{k+1,\ell}[A_{k}-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}I]+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}X_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}},\end{cases}

(7)

where
\ell=0,1,\cdots
,
X_{k+1,0}=X_{k}
,
\alpha_{k}>0
and
\omega_{k}\in[0,2)
.

Combining the Newton scheme with the GADI scheme, we have the Newton-GADI algorithm, summarized in Algorithm
1
below.

Algorithm 1

(Newton-GADI Algorithm)

Given the matrices
A,~K,~Q
, the integer numbers
k_{\max}>1
and
\ell_{\max}>1
,
the outer and inter iteration tolerance
\varepsilon_{out}

and
\varepsilon_{inn}
, respectively.

Step 1. Compute
\beta=1+\|A\|_{\infty},~~B=A+\beta I

and the initial matrix
X_{0}=X^{-1}

by solving Lyapunov equation
B^{*}X+XB-2Q=0
.

Step 2. Set
X_{k+1,0}=X_{k}

and compute the matrices

\displaystyle A_{k}=KX_{k}-A,~~F(X_{k})=X_{k}KX_{k}+Q.

Step 3. Solve the GADI iteration scheme (
7
) to obtain
X_{k+1}=X_{k+1,\ell_{k}}
such that

\displaystyle\|A^{*}X_{k+1,\ell_{k}}+X_{k+1,\ell_{k}}A-X_{k+1,\ell_{k}}KX_{k+1,\ell_{k}}+Q\|<\varepsilon_{inn}~~\mbox{or}~~\ell_{k}>\ell_{\max}.

Step 4. Compute the normalized residual

\displaystyle\mbox{NRes}(X_{k+1})=\frac{\|A^{*}X_{k+1}+X_{k+1}A-X_{k+1}KX_{k+1}+Q\|_{2}}{\|A^{*}X_{k+1}\|_{2}+\|X_{k+1}A\|_{2}+\|X_{k+1}KX_{k+1}\|_{2}+\|Q\|_{2}}.

Step 5. If
\mbox{NRes}(X_{k+1})<\epsilon_{out}
or
k>k_{max}
,
then the approximate solution of the CARE (
1
) is
\tilde{X}=X_{k+1}
;
else set
k=k+1
and return to Step 2.

Remark 2.3
.

From Algorithm
1
, it can be seen that when
\omega_{k}=0
, the GADI method naturally reduces to
the ADI method in
[
34
]
. We adopt the method given in
[
33

to select the desired initial matrix
X_{0}
,
see Step 1 in Algorithm

For large-scale Lyapunov equations at each Newton step,
it is important to control the accuracy of the solution to gain efficiency,
and wish to keep the overall fast convergence property of the Newton method.
The inexact Newton method proposed in
[
30
]

has shown a rigorous guideline for the termination of the inner iteration while the fast local rate of convergence is retained.
The Lyapunov equation can be approximately solved such that

\displaystyle\|(A-KX_{k})^{*}X_{k+1}+X_{k+1}(A-KX_{k})+X_{k}KX_{k}+Q\|\leq\eta_{k}\|\mathcal{R}(X_{k})\|

\displaystyle=\eta_{k}\|A^{*}X_{k}+X_{k}A-X_{k}KX_{k}+Q\|.

Formally, the iteration scheme is determined by solving

\displaystyle(A-KX_{k})^{*}X_{k+1}+X_{k+1}(A-KX_{k})+X_{k}KX_{k}+Q=R_{k}.

(8)

The following convergence property for the inexact Newton method is referenced in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
30
]

For the CARE (
1
), assume that
(A,K)
is stabilizable and
(A,Q)
is detectable,
and
X_{*}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is the Hermitian positive semi-definite solution. If there exist
\tilde{\delta}>0
and
\tilde{\eta}>0

such that for an initial matrix
X_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
meeting

\|X_{0}-X_{*}\|\leq\tilde{\delta}
, then the iteration sequence
X_{k}
generated by (
8
)
converges to
X_{*}
if the residual
R_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|R_{k}\|\leq\eta_{k}\|A^{*}X_{k}+X_{k}A-X_{k}KX_{k}+Q\|.

The convergence rate is linear if
\eta_{k}\in(0,\tilde{\eta}]
.
It is super-linear if
\eta_{k}\rightarrow 0
and is quadratic if

\eta_{k}\leq K_{\eta}\|A^{*}X_{k}+X_{k}A-X_{k}KX_{k}+Q\|

As a consequence,
the inexact Newton-GADI method
is summarized in Algorithm

(Inexact Newton-GADI Algorithm)

2.2
Convergence analysis

In this subsection, we prove that the Newton-GADI scheme is convergent. The convergence analysis of the Inexact Newton-GADI scheme is similar and hence is omitted. We recall the definition and property of Kronecker product.

For
A_{1}=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{A}\times m_{A}}

and
B_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{B}\times m_{B}}
,
the Kronecker product is defined as

\displaystyle A_{1}\otimes B_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}B_{1}&a_{12}B_{1}&\cdots&a_{1m_{A}}B_{1}\\
a_{21}B_{1}&a_{22}B_{1}&\cdots&a_{2m_{A}}B_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{n_{A}1}B_{1}&a_{n_{A}2}B_{1}&\cdots&a_{n_{A}m_{A}}B_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n_{A}n_{B})\times(m_{A}m_{B})}.

The vectorization operator (abbreviated as vec):
\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{mn}
is

\displaystyle\mbox{vec}(X_{1})=(x^{T}_{1},x^{T}_{2},\cdots,x^{T}_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{mn},~~\mbox{with}~~X_{1}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}.

A brief review of some properties related to Kronecker products is required.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
A_{1},~B_{1},~C,~D,~X_{1}
with appropriate dimensions, we have

(i)
(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})(C\otimes D)=(A_{1}C)\otimes(B_{1}D)
;

(ii)
(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})^{-1}=A_{1}^{-1}\otimes B_{1}^{-1}
;

(iii)
\Lambda(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})=\{\lambda\mu:~\lambda\in\Lambda(A_{1})~\mbox{and}~\mu\in\Lambda(B_{1})\}
;

(iv)
(A_{1}\otimes B_{1})x=\mbox{vec}(B_{1}^{T}X_{1}A_{1})
,
\mbox{vec}(X_{1})=x
.

Thereby, we have the following convergence conclusion for the Newton-GADI method.

Theorem 2.6
.

For the CARE (
1
), assume that
(A,K)
is stabilizable and
(A,Q)
is detectable,
the parameters
\alpha_{k}>0
and
0\leq\omega_{k}<2
.
Then, for any
k=0,1,\cdots
,
the iteration sequence
\{X_{k+1,\ell}\}^{\infty}_{\ell=0}

defined by (
7
) converges to
X_{k+1}
.

Proof
.

Taking the operation “vec” from the GADI iteration scheme (
7
)
yields

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\mbox{vec}(X_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}})=(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)\mbox{vec}(X_{k+1,\ell})+\mbox{vec}(F(X_{k})),\\
(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)\mbox{vec}(X_{k+1,\ell+1})=[A^{T}_{k}\otimes I-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}I]\mbox{vec}(X_{k+1,\ell})+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}\cdot\mbox{vec}(X_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}}).\end{cases}

(9)

Denote
x=\mbox{vec}(X)
and

f(X_{k})=\mbox{vec}(F(X_{k}))
.
The iteration scheme (
9
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})x_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)x_{k+1,\ell}+f(X_{k}),\\
(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)x_{k+1,\ell+1}=[A^{T}_{k}\otimes I-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}I]x_{k+1,\ell}+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}x_{k+1,\ell+\frac{1}{2}}.\end{cases}

(10)

As
\alpha_{k}>0
and
A_{k}
is stable,
then
\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*}
and
\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I

are nonsingular. By (
10
), we get

\displaystyle x_{k+1,\ell+1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}\Big\{\Big[A^{T}_{k}\otimes I-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}I\Big]+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)\Big\}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~x_{k+1,\ell}+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}f(X_{k})

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}\Big[\alpha_{k}^{2}I+(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}C_{k}\Big]x_{k+1,\ell}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}f(X_{k})

where
C_{k}=I\otimes A_{k}^{*}+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I
.
Denote

\displaystyle T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}\Big[\alpha_{k}^{2}I+(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}C_{k}\Big],

\displaystyle G_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})=(2-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1},

thus

\displaystyle x_{k+1,\ell+1}=T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})x_{k+1,\ell}+G_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})f(X_{k}).

(11)

Next, we prove that the iteration matrix
\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))<1
for
0<\alpha_{k}

and
0\leq\omega_{k}<2
. Note that

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{k}C_{k}=-(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)+(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I),

then

\displaystyle T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}\Big[\alpha_{k}^{2}I+(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)-(1-\omega_{k})\alpha_{k}C_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}\Big[(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)+\omega_{k}\alpha_{k}C_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(2-\omega_{k})(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)+\omega_{k}I\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[(2-\omega_{k})T_{k}(\alpha_{k})+\omega_{k}I]

(12)

with

\displaystyle T_{k}(\alpha_{k})=(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I).

(13)

Using (
12
), it is evident that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))=\frac{1}{2}[(2-\omega_{k})\lambda_{i}(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))+\omega_{k}](i=1,2,\cdots,n),

and

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))\leq\frac{1}{2}[(2-\omega_{k})\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))+\omega_{k}].

Obviously, the matrix
T_{k}(\alpha_{k})
defined in (
13
) is similar to

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{k}(\alpha_{k})=(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}

through the matrix
\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I
.
Thus we can obtain

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\|(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\|_{2}\cdot\|(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\|_{2}\cdot\|(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|A^{L}_{k}\|_{2}\|A^{R}_{k}\|_{2},

(14)

where
A^{L}_{k}=(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})^{-1}(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})

and
A^{R}_{k}=(\alpha_{k}I-A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)(\alpha_{k}I+A^{T}_{k}\otimes I)^{-1}
.
Then, for
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n^{2}\times 1}
, we get

\displaystyle\|A^{L}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1}\frac{\|(\alpha_{k}I-I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}}{\|(\alpha_{k}I+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\max_{\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1}\frac{\|(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}-\alpha_{k}\bm{x}^{*}(I\otimes A_{k}+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}+\alpha_{k}\bm{x}^{*}(I\otimes A_{k}+I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\|\bm{x}\|_{2}=1}\frac{\|(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(I\otimes A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|(I\otimes A_{k}^{*})\bm{x}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(I\otimes A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(I\otimes A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(I\otimes A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}.

Analogously, we obtain

\displaystyle\|A^{R}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{T}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{T}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}.

Since
A_{k}
is stable and
\alpha_{k}>0
, by (
14
), it follows that
\|A^{L}_{k}\|_{2}<1
and
\|A^{R}_{k}\|_{2}<1
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))<1,~~\mbox{and}~~\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))\leq\frac{1}{2}[(2-\omega_{k})\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))+\omega_{k}]<1,

which implies that the iteration scheme (
11
) is convergent. The proof is completed.

2.3
Parameter selection

In this subsection, we first compare the convergence rate between the Newton-GADI and Newton-ADI methods.
That is, the relation between
\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))
and
\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))
defined in Section 2.2 is discussed. Then we present a practical method to select the quasi-optimal parameter.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that the eigenvalues of
A_{k}
have positive real parts.
For
j_{1},~j_{2}\in\{1,\cdots,n^{2}\}
, denote

\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))=|\zeta_{j_{1}}|=|a_{j_{1}}+i\,b_{j_{1}}|,~~\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))=|\eta_{j_{2}}|=|c_{j_{2}}+i\,d_{j_{2}}|.

(i) When
|\eta_{j_{2}}|^{2}\leq c_{j_{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))<\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))<1.

(ii) When
|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}>c_{j_{1}}
and
0<\omega_{k}<\frac{4(|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}-c_{j_{1}})}{(1-c_{j_{1}})^{2}+d_{j_{1}}^{2}}<2,

we have

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))<\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))<1.

Proof
.

For
\zeta_{j}=a_{j}+ib_{j}\in\Lambda(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))
,
\eta_{j}=c_{j}+id_{j}\in\Lambda(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))(j=1,2,\cdots,n)
, then

\displaystyle 2|\zeta_{j}|=|(2-\omega_{k})\eta_{j}+\omega_{k}|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle 4|\zeta_{j}|^{2}=|(2-\omega_{k})\eta_{j}+\omega_{k}|^{2}=[(2-\omega_{k})c_{j}+\omega_{k}]^{2}+[(2-\omega_{k})d_{j}]^{2}=(2-\omega_{k})^{2}|\eta_{j}|^{2}+\omega_{k}^{2}+2(2-\omega_{k})\omega_{k}c_{j},

i.e.,

\displaystyle 4|\zeta_{j}|^{2}-4|\eta_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{k}^{2}-4\omega_{k})|\eta_{j}|^{2}+\omega_{k}^{2}+2(2-\omega_{k})\omega_{k}c_{j}

\displaystyle=(1-2c_{j}+|\eta|^{2})\omega_{k}^{2}-4(|\eta_{j}|^{2}-c_{j})\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=[(1-c_{j})^{2}+d_{j}^{2}]\omega_{k}^{2}-4(|\eta_{j}|^{2}-c_{j})\omega_{k}.

Note that

\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))=|\zeta_{j_{1}}|,~~\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))=|\eta_{j_{2}}|.

(i) When
|\eta_{j_{2}}|^{2}\leq c_{j_{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))<\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))<1.

(ii) When
|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}>c_{j_{1}}
and
0<\omega_{k}<\frac{4(|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}-c_{j_{1}})}{(1-c_{j_{1}})^{2}+d_{j_{1}}^{2}}<2,
we have

\displaystyle|\zeta_{j_{1}}|<|\eta_{j_{1}}|,

which means that
\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))<\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))<1
.

Remark 2.8
.

By Theorem
2.7
, if
|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}>c_{j_{1}}
and
0<\omega_{k}<\frac{4(|\eta_{j_{1}}|^{2}-c_{j_{1}})}{(1-c_{j_{1}})^{2}+d_{j_{1}}^{2}}<2,
the Newton-GADI has a faster convergence rate than Newton-ADI proposed in
[
34
]
. This is verified by numerical examples in Section
3
.

Next, we provide a method for selecting theoretical quasi-parameters by minimizing
\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k},\omega_{k}))
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume that the eigenvalues of
A_{k}
have positive real parts, set

\displaystyle\nu_{n}=\max_{j}\sigma_{j}(A_{k}),~~\min_{j}Re(\lambda_{j}(A_{k}))=Re(\mu_{1})=Re(a_{\mu}+ib_{\mu})=a_{\mu}.

(15)

Then the quasi-optimal parameter
\alpha_{k}
can be obtained by

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{*}=\mbox{arg}\min_{\alpha_{k}}\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}-2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}=\nu_{n}.

(16)

Proof
.

Using the bound defined by (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\rho^{2}(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{L}\|^{2}_{2}\|A_{R}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{*}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}\cdot\frac{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{T}\|^{2}_{2}-2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\|I\otimes A_{k}^{T}\|^{2}_{2}+2\alpha_{k}\min_{i}Re(\lambda_{i}(A_{k}))+\alpha^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}-2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}\cdot\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}-2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho(T_{k}(\alpha_{k}))\leq\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}-2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}}{\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha^{2}_{k}},

where
\nu_{n}
and
a_{\mu}
are given in (
15
).
Denote

\displaystyle\Psi(\alpha_{k})=\frac{\nu_{n}^{2}-2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha_{k}^{2}}{\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha_{k}^{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Psi(\alpha_{k})}{\partial\alpha_{k}}=\frac{4a_{\mu}(\alpha_{k}^{2}-\nu_{n}^{2})}{(\nu_{n}^{2}+2\alpha_{k}a_{\mu}+\alpha_{k}^{2})^{2}}.

It is easy to show that
\Psi(\alpha_{k})
gets to its minimum
when
\alpha_{k}=\nu_{n}
, i.e.,

\alpha_{k}^{*}=\mbox{arg}\min\limits_{\alpha_{k}}\Psi(\alpha_{k})=\nu_{n},

where
\nu_{n}
depends on the matrix
A_{k}
.

Remark 2.10
.

In terms of Theorem
2.9
, we usually select
\omega_{k}=1
and
\alpha_{k}^{*}
given in (
16
)
as the quasi-optimal parameters. It can be seen that this parameter choice is reasonable using the residual analysis in Section
3
.

Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed the Newton-GADI algorithm to solve the complex CARE. Moreover, by inaccurately solving a Lyapunov equation in each Newton step,
the inexact Newton-GADI algorithm is presented.
Finally, we have offered numerical experiments to compare the effectiveness.
It can be seen that the Newton-GADI method has a faster inter iterative convergence rate
than the Newton-ADI method, while the inexact Newton-GADI method is more efficient.
Therefore, the Newton-GADI and inexact Newton-GADI methods could be valid and attractive algorithms
to solve the ill-conditioned Riccati equation.
In the future, we will further study parameter selection and solving the low-rank large-scale CARE.

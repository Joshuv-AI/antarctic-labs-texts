On the W W mass and New Higgs Bosons
Pavel Fileviez Pérez, Hiren H. Patel, Alexis D. Plascencia

Abstract
We discuss the prediction of the W W boson mass in a simple extension of the Standard Model ( Σ ​ SM \Sigma{\rm SM} ) with a real scalar triplet. A shift in the W W mass as reported by the CDF II collaboration can naturally be accommodated by the model without modifying the Standard Model value for the Z Z mass. We discuss the main implications and the properties of the new Higgs bosons. Namely, the partial decay widths of the new charged Higgs are predicted. Furthermore, the neutral Higgs has suppressed couplings to fermions and decays predominantly into a pair of W W gauge bosons.

The Standard Model (SM) of particle physics describes with high precision the properties of quarks and leptons and how they interact through the electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions.
Nevertheless, there exist motivations for physics beyond the SM such as the existence of massive neutrinos, the dark matter and the origin of the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the Universe.
Recently, the CDF II collaboration
Aaltonen et al. 2022

W

In this Letter we discuss the main properties of the physical scalars in the
\Sigma\text{SM}
and show how the new result reported by the CDF II collaboration allows us to find the allowed value for the triplet vacuum
expectation value in agreement with all experimental constraints. This theory predicts two extra physical scalars, a
CP
-even neutral Higgs, and a charged Higgs.
We show that in the limit when the lightest Higgs is SM-like, the heavy
CP
-even neutral Higgs is almost fermiophobic. We discuss the numerical predictions for the Higgs decays
and comment on the possible production channels at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC).

The scalar sector of the
\Sigma\text{SM}
is composed of the SM Higgs,

H\sim(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},1/2)

\Sigma\sim(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{3},0)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{scalar}}=(D_{\mu}H)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}H)+\Tr(D_\mu\Sigma)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\Sigma)-V(H,\Sigma),\,

(1)

where
H^{T}=(\phi^{+},\,\phi^{0})

\Sigma=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma^{0}&\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{+}\\
\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{-}&-\Sigma^{0}\end{array}\right),\,

(2)

with
\Sigma^{0}

\Sigma^{+}=(\Sigma^{-})^{*}

D_{\mu}\Sigma=\partial_{\mu}\Sigma+ig_{2}[{W}_{\mu},\Sigma].

Here
W_{\mu}
and
g_{2}
are the
\,{\rm SU}(2)_{L}
gauge bosons and coupling, respectively.

This model predicts a new
CP
-even Higgs,

Consequently, the dominant two-body decay for the new

-even Higgs is

H
becomes almost fermiophobic. On the other hand, the main decay channels of the charged Higgs are

H^{+}\to\tau^{+}{\nu}_{\tau}

,

As we have discussed in the previous section, the CDF II value for
M_{W}
fixes values for
x_{0}
and
\theta_{+}
. Therefore, the couplings to
H^{+}

One of the main production channels at hadron colliders for the neutral Higgs is
u\bar{d}\to W^{*}\to W^{+}H

\sqrt{s}=13

In this Letter, we showed that the simplest extension of the SM model that automatically explains the CDF II result for
M_{W}
is the
\Sigma\text{SM}
.
Consistency with CDF II result in this model fixes the branching ratios of the charged Higgs, and are shown in Fig.

We also noted that the heavy
CP
-even Higgs is fermiophobic-like, and the theory predicts that its dominant decay channel is
H\rightarrow WW
. We briefly discussed the main production channels at the LHC.

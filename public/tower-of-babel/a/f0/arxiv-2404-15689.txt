Parametric decay instability of circularly polarized Alfvén wave in magnetically dominated plasma
Wataru Ishizaki, Kunihito Ioka

Abstract
We investigate parametric decay instability (PDI) of circularly polarized Alfvén wave into daughter acoustic wave and backward Alfvén wave in magnetically-dominated plasma, in which the magnetization parameter σ \sigma (energy density ratio of background magnetic field to matter) exceeds unity. We analyze relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), focusing on wave frequencies sufficiently lower than the plasma and cyclotron frequencies. We derive analytical formulae for the dispersion relation and growth rate of the instability as a function of the magnetization σ \sigma , wave amplitude η \eta , and plasma temperature θ \theta . We find that PDI persists even in high magnetization σ \sigma , albeit with a decreased growth rate up to σ → ∞ \sigma\to\infty . Our formulae are useful for estimating the decay of Alfvén wave into acoustic wave and heat in high magnetization σ \sigma plasma, which is a ubiquitous phenomenon such as in pulsars, magnetars, and fast radio bursts.

26
), we obtain the following equations:

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{\|}}{\partial t}+\left(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}\right)\frac{\partial\beta_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\left(2\left(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}\right)+\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\right)\left(\delta\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\beta}_{\perp}\right)\right]+\frac{B_{0}}{4\pi}\left[\bm{\beta}_{\perp}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\delta\bm{B})+\delta\bm{\beta}\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{b}_{\perp}}{\partial z}\right],

(27)

\left(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}\right)\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\beta_{\|}}{\partial t}+\beta_{s}^{2}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{\delta\bm{B}\cdot\bm{b}_{\perp}}{4\pi}\right)+\frac{B_{0}}{4\pi}\left(\delta\bm{B}\cdot\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{\beta}_{\perp}}{\partial t}+\bm{b}_{\perp}\cdot\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\delta\bm{\beta})\right),

(28)

\mathcal{E}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{\beta}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-\frac{B_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{\partial\bm{b}_{\perp}}{\partial z}=-\left(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}\right)\beta_{\|}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\delta\bm{\beta})-\left(1+\beta_{s}^{2}\right)\frac{B_{0}\epsilon_{\|}}{4\pi\mathcal{E}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\delta\bm{B})-\delta\bm{\beta}\beta_{s}^{2}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{\|}}{\partial t}+\frac{B_{0}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\beta_{\|}\delta\bm{B}\right),

(29)

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{b}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-B_{0}\frac{\partial\bm{\beta}_{\perp}}{\partial z}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\beta_{\|}\delta\bm{B}\right),

(30)

where

\mathcal{E}\equiv\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}+B_{0}^{2}/4\pi.

(31)

Note that all the terms in the right-hand sides of these equations are
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\eta)

\varepsilon\ll\eta

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})

On the right-hand side of these equations, there are some terms that did not appear in the analysis of parametric decay instabilities based on the non-relativistic MHD equations

68

69

These terms stem from (1) the velocity squared (i.e.,
\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})
), (2) the internal energy density that can be comparable to the rest mass energy density, and (3) the displacement current, which were neglected for the non-relativistic regime.

II.5
dimensionless equations

To clarify the dominated terms in the equation, we normalize the equation and obtain a non-dimensionalized equation.
The quantity representing the amplitude of the parent wave is normalized as follows:

\delta\bm{u}\equiv\frac{\delta\beta}{\beta_{A}},\quad\delta\bm{e}=\frac{\delta\bm{B}}{B_{0}}.

(32)

Note that this non-dimensionalisation yields
|\delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}=|\delta\mbox{\boldmath$e$}|^{2}=\eta^{2}
.
Also, since
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})
is ignored here, the normalization scale of
\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}
is
\beta_{A}
rather than
\beta_{A,\eta}
.
The amplitude of the daughter Alfvén wave of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
is normalized as follows:

\bm{u}_{\perp}\equiv\frac{\beta_{\perp}}{\beta_{A}},\quad\bm{e}_{\perp}=\frac{\bm{b}_{\perp}}{B_{0}}.

(33)

For the daughter acoustic wave of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
:

u_{\|}\equiv\frac{\beta_{\|}}{\beta_{s}},\quad e_{\|}\equiv\frac{\epsilon_{\|}}{\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}}.

(34)

Here, we define two dimensionless parameters that characterize this system.
One is the magnetization parameter
\sigma
, defined as follows:

\sigma\equiv\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{4\pi\left(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}\right)}.

(35)

By using
\sigma
, the Alfvén speed can be written as
\beta_{A}^{2}=\sigma/(1+\sigma)
.
The other is the ratio of the sonic velocity to the Alfvén velocity:

\theta\equiv\frac{\beta_{s}}{\beta_{A}}.

(36)

Note that this corresponds to approximately the 1/2 power of a quantity called ”plasma beta” in the non-relativistic case
3
3

3

The plasma beta (
\beta_{\rm pl}

\beta_{\rm pl}\equiv p_{0}/(B_{0}^{2}/8\pi)
).
The value derived from this definition and the square of the ratio of the sound speed to the Alfvén velocity is nearly equal, in fact for a polytropic gas with a specific heat ratio
\Gamma
(i.e., satisfying
\Gamma

\theta^{2}=(2/\Gamma)\beta_{\rm pl}
.

.

Using equations (
32
)–(
36
), we obtain following normalized equations:

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial e_{\|}}{\partial t}+\beta_{s}\frac{\partial u_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\beta_{A}^{2}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\delta\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{\perp}\right)-\sigma\delta\bm{u}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{u}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-\beta_{A}\frac{\partial\bm{e}_{\perp}}{\partial z}\right),

(37)

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial u_{\|}}{\partial t}+\beta_{s}\frac{\partial e_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\theta^{-1}\beta_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\delta\bm{e}\cdot\bm{e}_{\perp}\right)+\sigma\theta^{-1}\delta\bm{e}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{u}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-\beta_{A}\frac{\partial\bm{e}_{\perp}}{\partial z}\right),

(38)

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{u}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-\beta_{A}\frac{\partial\bm{e}_{\perp}}{\partial z}=\theta\beta_{A}^{2}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(u_{\|}\delta\bm{e}\right)-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}\left[\beta_{s}u_{\|}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\delta\bm{u})+\beta_{A}e_{\|}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(\delta\bm{e})+\beta_{s}^{2}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(e_{\|}\delta\bm{u}\right)\right],

(39)

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\bm{e}_{\perp}}{\partial t}-\beta_{A}\frac{\partial\bm{u}_{\perp}}{\partial z}=-\theta\beta_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(u_{\|}\delta\bm{e}\right).

(40)

Here we used the following normalized equation of the order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
:

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\delta\bm{u}\right)=\beta_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\delta\bm{e}\right),

(41)

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\delta\bm{e}\right)=\beta_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\delta\bm{u}\right).

(42)

The second term on the right side of equation (

) is found to be a small quantity of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\eta^{2})
using equation (
39
).
Therefore, the equation (
37

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial e_{\|}}{\partial t}+\beta_{s}\frac{\partial u_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\delta\bm{u}\cdot\bm{u}_{\perp}\right).

(43)

Similarly for equation (
38
), we have

\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial u_{\|}}{\partial t}+\beta_{s}\frac{\partial e_{\|}}{\partial z}=-\theta^{-1}\beta_{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\delta\bm{e}\cdot\bm{e}_{\perp}\right).

(44)

III
Dispersion relation

As seen in the previous section, the
\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon\right)
perturbation quantities
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\perp}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\perp}
,
u_{\|}
,
e_{\|}
follow the linear differential equations (
39
), (
40
), (
43
), and (
44
).
In this section, we examine the stability of the wave solutions in these equations by finding the dispersion relation.

III.1
mode expansion

In order to find the dispersion relation, each physical quantity of the wave is divided into Fourier components.
Since the parent Alfvén wave is a circularly polarized wave with a given wavenumber
k_{0}
and frequency
\omega_{0}
, it can be written as

\delta\bm{e}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\delta e_{0}\exp\left(i\phi_{0}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}+\text{c.c.}\right),

(45)

\delta\bm{u}=-\delta\bm{e},

(46)

where
\phi_{0}=k_{0}z-\omega_{0}t
is the phase of the parent Alfvén wave,
\bm{e}_{\rm R}=\left(\bm{e}_{x}+i\bm{e}_{y}\right)/\sqrt{2}
is the unit polarization vector for right-handed polarization,
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{j}
(
j=x,y,z
) is the unit vector, and
c.c.
denotes the complex conjugate.
Note that
|\delta e_{0}|^{2}=\eta^{2}
.
Here, although equation (
45
) seems to represent only right-handed polarization waves, equation (
45
) simultaneously represents not only right-handed polarized waves with positive
\omega_{0}
and positive
k_{0}
but also negative
\omega_{0}
and negative
k_{0}
at the same time
[
98
]
.
Furthermore, in the MHD regime (i.e., where the wave frequency is much smaller than the cyclotron frequency and plasma frequency), right- and left-handed polarizations are symmetric, so that what happens with one polarization also occurs with the other polarization.
Therefore, it is sufficient to discuss wave stability for the parent wave expressed by the equation (
45
).

For the acoustic wave perturbation, we write the frequency as
\omega
and the wavenumber as
k
as follows:

e_{\|}=\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{k}\exp(i\phi)+\text{c.c.}\right),

(47)

u_{\|}=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{k}\exp(i\phi)+\text{c.c.}\right),

(48)

where
\phi=kz-\omega t
is the phase of the acoustic wave.
Since there are two daughter Alfvén waves produced by the interaction of the acoustic wave and the parent Alfvén wave, corresponding to Stokes and anti-Stokes waves, they are written as follows:

\bm{e}_{\perp}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(e_{+}\exp\left(i\phi_{+}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}+\text{c.c.}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(e_{-}\exp\left(i\phi_{-}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}+\text{c.c.}\right),

(49)

\bm{u}_{\perp}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(u_{+}\exp\left(i\phi_{+}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}+\text{c.c.}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(u_{-}\exp\left(i\phi_{-}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}+\text{c.c.}\right),

(50)

where
\phi_{\pm}=\phi_{0}\pm\phi=k_{\pm}z-\omega_{\pm}t
is the phase of the daughter Alfvén waves (
-
for the Stokes wave, and
+
for the anti-Stokes wave), and
k_{\pm}=k_{0}\pm k
and
\omega_{\pm}=\omega_{0}\pm\omega
are wavenumbers and frequencies of daughter Alfvén waves, respectively.

Substituting equations (
45
)–(
50
) into equation (
39
), the following equation for the Fourier component is obtained from the term proportional to
\exp(i\phi)
:

\omega e_{k}-c\beta_{s}ku_{k}=\beta_{A}^{2}\omega\left(u_{+}\delta e_{0}^{*}+u_{-}^{*}\delta e_{0}\right).

(51)

From equation (
40
), the following equation is obtained from the
\exp(i\phi)
component:

\omega u_{k}-c\beta_{s}ke_{k}=\theta^{-1}c\beta_{A}k\left(e_{+}\delta e_{0}^{*}+e_{-}^{*}\delta e_{0}\right).

(52)

Similarly from the
\exp(i\phi_{+})\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}
component of equation (
43
), we obtain the following:

\omega_{+}u_{+}+c\beta_{A}k_{+}e_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\delta e_{0}\left[\left(\theta\beta_{A}^{2}\omega_{+}-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}\theta c\beta_{A}k_{0}\right)u_{k}+\frac{1}{1+\sigma}\left(c\beta_{A}k_{0}+\theta^{2}\beta_{A}^{2}\omega_{+}\right)e_{k}\right],

(53)

and from the
\exp(-i\phi_{-})\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*}_{\rm R}
component:

\omega_{-}u_{-}+c\beta_{A}k_{-}e_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\delta e_{0}^{*}\left[\left(\theta\beta_{A}^{2}\omega_{-}-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}\theta c\beta_{A}k_{0}\right)u_{k}+\frac{1}{1+\sigma}\left(c\beta_{A}k_{0}+\theta^{2}\beta_{A}^{2}\omega_{-}\right)e_{k}\right].

(54)

Similarly, from the
\exp(i\phi_{+})\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\rm R}
component of equation (
44

\omega_{+}e_{+}+c\beta_{A}k_{+}u_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\theta c\beta_{A}k_{+}u_{k}\delta e_{0},

(55)

and from the
\exp(-i\phi_{-})\mbox{\boldmath$e$}^{*}_{\rm R}

\omega_{-}e_{-}^{*}+c\beta_{A}k_{-}u_{-}^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\theta c\beta_{A}k_{-}u_{k}\delta e_{0}^{*}.

(56)

Equations (

56

III.2
dispersion relation

Hereafter, we adopt the following unit system:

\omega_{0}=1,\quad k_{0}=1.

(57)

From the conditions for the existence of nontrivial solutions to
e_{k},u_{k},e_{+},u_{+},e_{-}^{*},u_{-}^{*}

By equating the determinant of the coefficient matrix of equations (

)–(

0

\displaystyle(\omega-k)(\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}k^{2})\left\{(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{2}}{(1+\sigma)^{3}}\left(S_{0}+S_{1}\sigma+S_{2}\sigma^{2}+S_{3}\sigma^{3}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{4}k\omega\sigma}{(1+\sigma)^{4}}\left(T_{0}+T_{1}\sigma+T_{2}\sigma^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{2}(\omega^{3}+k\omega^{2}-3\omega+k),

(59)

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2S_{0}-\omega^{3}+k\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}k^{2}\left[2k\omega^{2}+(2k^{2}-3)\omega-k\right],

(60)

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{0}-\omega^{3}+k\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}\left[\omega^{5}+2k\omega^{4}+(k^{2}-3)\omega^{3}-3k(k^{2}+1)\omega^{2}-k^{2}(3k^{2}-7)\omega+k^{3}\right],

(61)

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{2}k\omega(\omega-k)\left[(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right],

(62)

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k,

(63)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega+2k-\theta^{2}(k\omega^{2}+k^{2}\omega-k),

(64)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega+k-\theta^{2}(k\omega-1)(\omega+k).

(65)

Note that since we have only calculated correctly up to the lowest order of
\eta

(
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})

III.3
Non-relativistic limit

In order to check the validity of the obtained dispersion relation, we take the non-relativistic limit and compare it with the known results.
The non-relativistic limit here means the limit where the velocity scale of the system is much slower than the speed of light and the rest mass energy density is much larger than the internal energy density.
This can be achieved by taking the limit of Alfvén velocity
\beta^{2}_{A}\ll 1
, or since
\sigma=\beta_{A}^{2}/(1-\beta_{A}^{2})
, the limit of
\sigma\to 0
.
By taking the limit in equation (
58
), we obtain

(\omega-k)\left(\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}k^{2}\right)\left\{(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right\}=\eta^{2}k^{2}\left(\omega^{3}+k\omega^{2}-3\omega+k\right).

(66)

This is the same dispersion relation as previous results obtained for non-relativistic MHD equation system
[
68
,
69
]
.
Therefore, in this regime (
\sigma\ll 1
), based on this dispersion relation, the daughter Alfvén and the acoustic waves grow exponentially.

III.4
\sigma\to\infty
limit

As another extreme case, let us consider the case where
\sigma\to\infty
.
The dispersion relation (
58
) leaving only the highest order of
\sigma
is as follows:

(\omega-k)\left\{(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right\}\left(\omega^{2}-\eta^{2}\theta^{2}k\omega-\theta^{2}k^{2}\right)=0.

(67)

Since there are only real solutions of
\omega
to this equation for any
k
,
\theta
, and
\eta
, it can be seen that instability does not occur in the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
.

III.5
1/\sigma
expansion

As seen in the previous two sections, in the limit of small
\sigma
(i.e., non-relativistic limit), the PDI occurs, but in the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
, no instability occurs.
This fact implies that the parametric decay instability (PDI) is suppressed as
\sigma
increases.
In order to investigate this quantitatively, we expand the dispersion relation with
\sigma^{-1}
as a small quantity and investigate how the instability is suppressed up to the first order of
\sigma^{-1}
.
Hereafter, the term
\eta^{4}
in the dispersion relation is ignored as small, and
\theta\ll 1
is assumed for simplicity.
The dispersion relation (
58
) expanded to the first order of
\sigma^{-1}
is as follows:

(\omega-k)\left(\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}k^{2}\right)\left[(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right]=\eta^{2}\left[S_{3}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(S_{2}-3S_{3}\right)\right].

(68)

Since this equation is a
6
-th-order algebraic equation, it cannot be solved analytically in general.
Therefore, in order to derive the conditions for the existence of unstable solutions, we set
\eta
to be small and expand the dispersion relation around the following resonance frequency
\omega_{1}
and wavenumber
k_{1}
, which satisfies both the dispersion relation
\omega=\theta k
for forward acoustic waves and
\omega+k-2=0
for backward Alfvén waves (Stokes waves).
The resonance frequency
\omega_{1}
and wavenumber
k_{1}
can be written as

\omega_{1}=\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta},~~k_{1}=\frac{2}{1+\theta}.

(69)

We take the frequency
\omega
and the wavenumber
k
as follows:

\omega=\omega_{1}+\delta\omega,~~k=k_{1}+\delta k.

(70)

We assume that
\delta\omega
and
\delta k
are small quantities of magnitude about
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
.
This will be confirmed later to be correct in the case where the resonance condition is satisfied.
Expanding the left-hand side of equation (
68
) to the lowest order of
\eta
, we obtain

(\omega-k)\left(\omega^{2}-\theta^{2}k^{2}\right)\left\{(\omega+k)^{2}-4\right\}\sim-32\theta\delta\omega\left(\delta\omega+\delta k\right).

(71)

Note that here we used
\theta\ll 1
and keep only the lowest order of
\theta
, but assuming that
\theta
is greater than
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
.
Similarly, the following equation can be obtained for the right-hand side by expanding to the lowest order of
\eta
:

\eta^{2}\left[S_{3}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(S_{2}-3S_{3}\right)\right]\sim\frac{8\eta^{2}}{\sigma}.

(72)

Equating the above results, we thus obtain:

\delta\omega^{2}+\delta k\delta\omega+\frac{\eta^{2}}{4\sigma\theta}=0.

(73)

If equation (
73
) has a solution with an imaginary part, equation (
68
) has an unstable growing solution.
Regarding equation (
73
) as a quadratic equation of
\delta\omega
, the condition that its discriminant is negative gives the following condition that
\delta\omega
has an imaginary part, namely:

-\eta\sigma^{-1/2}\theta^{-1/2}<\delta k<\eta\sigma^{-1/2}\theta^{-1/2}.

(74)

The imaginary part
\Gamma\equiv{\rm Im}(\delta\omega)
takes its maximum value
\Gamma_{\rm max}
when the resonance condition is satisfied (i.e.
\delta k=0
) and is as follows:

\Gamma_{\rm max}=\frac{1}{2}\eta\sigma^{-1/2}\theta^{-1/2}.

(75)

Note that the validity of the assumption that
\delta\omega
and
\delta k
are
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
can be confirmed from equations (
74
) and (
75
).
As can be seen from equations (
74
) and (
75
), the instability ceases in the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
.

III.6
Decay rate for general

IV
Discussion

The obtained expression for the growth rate of the instability reveals that the maximum growth rate
\Gamma_{\rm max}
is a decreasing function of
\sigma
, and in the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
, the instability ceases.
Since
\sigma
is the ratio of the energy densities of the matter and electromagnetic fields, the limit of large
\sigma
corresponds to a situation close to the force-free limit.
Our findings are consistent with the fact that an Alfvén wave is stable to resonant three-wave interactions in the force-free limit.
While it is not evident whether the methods using force-free equations and the MHD equations employed in our study are mathematically equivalent, we can demonstrate that they are equivalent within the scope of this study.
By eliminating
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\perp}
from equations (
39
) and (
40
), and taking the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
, we obtain

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{b}_{\perp}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{b}_{\perp}}{\partial z^{2}}=0.

(83)

This is the equation satisfied by the magnetic field of the free electromagnetic wave.
Similarly, by eliminating
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{\perp}
from equations (
39
) and (
40
), and taking the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
, we obtain

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\perp}-\theta u_{\|}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$e$}\right)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\perp}-\theta u_{\|}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$e$}\right)=0.

(84)

The quantity
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\perp}-\theta u_{\|}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$e$}
that satisfies the wave equation in vacuum can be interpreted as the electric field
\mbox{\boldmath$E$}_{\perp,xy}
associated with the daughter Alfvén wave perpendicular to the background magnetic field
\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}
.
By multiplying both sides of equation (
84
) by
\beta_{A}\mbox{\boldmath$B$}_{0}
as an outer product from the left side, we obtain

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$E$}_{\perp,xy}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$E$}_{\perp,xy}}{\partial z^{2}}=0,

(85)

where

\mbox{\boldmath$E$}_{\perp,xy}=-\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\beta_{\perp}$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$B_{0}$}+\mbox{\boldmath$\beta_{\|}$}\times\delta\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\right).

(86)

This corresponds to the electric field in the
xy
-plane, except for the one associated with the parent Alfvén wave (see equation (
98
)).
In this way, equations (
39
) and (
40
) describing daughter Alfvén waves reduce to the wave equation of free electromagnetic waves in the limit
\sigma\to\infty
.
On the other hand, the wave equation for acoustic waves still has a non-zero source term in the limit of
\sigma\to\infty
:

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{\|}}{\partial t^{2}}-\beta_{s}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{\|}}{\partial z^{2}}=-w_{0}\left[\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\left(\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}_{\perp}\right)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{\perp}}{B_{0}^{2}}\right)\right],

(87)

\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{\|}}{\partial t^{2}}-\beta_{s}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{\|}}{\partial z^{2}}=-\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial z}\left(\frac{\delta\mbox{\boldmath$B$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$b$}_{\perp}}{B_{0}^{2}}-\beta_{s}^{2}\delta\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\beta$}_{\perp}\right).

(88)

Although the acoustic waves are generated by the interaction between parent and daughter Alfvén waves even for
\sigma\to\infty
, the PDI is considered to be suppressed because the acoustic wave is not possible to excite daughter Alfvén waves.

In this work, we have treated the amplitude
\eta
of the parent Alfvén wave as constant and calculated the growth rate of infinitesimal amplitude daughter waves.
Consequently, the decay rate of the parent wave cannot be determined from this analysis.
A formulation that considers the case
\epsilon\sim\eta
is necessary for a more precise derivation.
However, according to the general consideration of three-wave interactions, there is an implication that the decay rate of the parent wave differs only by a logarithmic factor from the growth rate of the daughter wave, namely the decay time scale of the parent wave can be written as

t_{\rm decay}\sim\Gamma_{\rm max}^{-1}\ln\left(\eta/\epsilon_{{\rm d},0}\right),

(89)

where
\epsilon_{{\rm d},0}
is the normalized initial amplitude of the daughter waves (e.g., for the Alfvén wave,
\epsilon_{{\rm d},0}=b_{\perp}/B_{0}
at the initial state)
[
67
]
.
Therefore, the growth rate obtained in this study is effective for estimating the decay rate of the parent wave.

In this study, we analyze the idealized setting of an infinitely extended uniform plane wave in a dissipationless system.
However, when applying these results to real systems, it is essential to evaluate the impacts of the following effects.
The first is the finite bandwidth effect of the parent wave.
Although stability analysis is conducted for monochromatic waves in this study, it is more natural to consider waves excited in general systems as wave packets with a finite bandwidth
\Delta\omega
.
In the context of the parametric instability, it is known that when the bandwidth of the parent wave is larger than the growth rate of daughter waves in the monochromatic case (
\Delta\omega\gg\Gamma_{\rm max}
), the effective growth rate of the daughter waves becomes
\Gamma_{\rm eff}\sim\Gamma_{\rm max}^{2}/\Delta\omega
, suppressed by
\Gamma_{\rm max}/\Delta\omega

[
104
]
.
On the other hand, if
\Delta\omega\ll\Gamma_{\rm max}
, the phase of the parent wave and the daughter waves can be considered synchronized during the time scale of daughter wave growth, allowing us to use the analysis of the monochromatic parent wave.
The second is the effect of dissipation in the plasma.
The dominant dissipation process in the plasma can vary depending on the system under consideration.
Here, we assume an appropriate timescale for the dissipation process as
\Gamma_{\rm diss}^{-1}
.
For instability to occur in the wave, the timescale of instability without considering dissipation should be shorter than the dissipation timescale (i.e.,
\Gamma_{\rm max}>\Gamma_{\rm diss}
)
[
104
]
.
In this case, the growth rate of instability can be estimated as
\Gamma_{\rm eff}\sim\Gamma_{\rm max}-\Gamma_{\rm diss}
.
When the effect of dissipation is weak, so that the dissipation proceeds over a much longer timescale compared to the growth timescale of the instability, it is justified to use the growth rate obtained from the analysis of the monochromatic parent wave.

In this study, we have only investigated the PDI within the linear stage (i.e.,
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)\ll 1
), so that further research is necessary to understand the development in the nonlinear stage.
In systems where
\sigma\gg 1
, meaning the electromagnetic energy density greatly exceeds the matter energy density, non-thermal high-energy phenomena such as non-thermal particle acceleration may occur due to the transfer of electromagnetic energy to low-density regions.
Additionally, for the generated acoustic waves, damping effects including the Landau damping are expected to occur, thus requiring an analysis incorporating kinetic effects to further quantitatively understand the PDI.
Magnetohydrodynamic simulation encounters difficulty in dealing with large
\sigma
and cannot analyze kinetic phenomena such as particle acceleration, making Particle-in-Cell (PIC) simulations more suitable for such studies.
While this study also applied linearization to
\eta
, further extensions are possible.
Equations (19)-(22) are formulated for general
\eta
, and we are currently conducting ongoing research to directly analyze these equations.
When
\eta
reaches unity, higher-order wave-wave interactions (e.g., 4-wave interaction) may become significant, although this phenomenon lies beyond the scope of this study.
Exploring higher-order wave-wave interactions through simulations, particularly PIC simulations, will provide valuable insights into understanding the stability of Alfvén waves.

V
Summary

We have investigated the parametric decay instability (PDI) of circularly polarized Alfvén waves in a plasma with relativistic magnetization, i.e.,
\sigma\gg 1
, based on the special relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations.
Our results indicate that even in plasmas with relativistic magnetization, the Alfvén wave is unstable to three-wave interactions where the parent wave generates two daughter waves - a forward propagating acoustic wave and a backward propagating Alfvén wave.
We have obtained the analytical expression of the maximum growth rate (see equation (
75
)) as follows:

\Gamma_{\rm max}/\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\eta\theta^{-1/2}\frac{\sqrt{1-\theta}}{1+\theta}\frac{1+\sigma\left(1+\theta^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\sigma\right)^{3/2}},

where
\eta=\delta B/B_{0}
is the amplitude of the parent Alfvén wave,
\sigma=B_{0}^{2}/(4\pi(\epsilon_{0}+p_{0}))
is the magnetization,
\theta=\beta_{\rm A}/\beta_{\rm s}
is the ratio of the sound speed to the Alfvén velocity, and
\omega_{0}
is the frequency of the parent Alfvén wave.
This is only valid when the ratio
\theta
of the sound speed to Alfvén velocity is greater than
\theta_{c}
, which is expressed by equation (
79
).
For
\theta\ll\theta_{c}
, the growth rate can be written as follows:

\Gamma_{\rm max}/\omega_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\eta^{2/3}\left(1+\sigma\right)^{-1/3}.

As can be seen from these equations, we have observed that the instability is suppressed with increasing magnetization
\sigma
.
Our results for the instability growth rates are consistent with the known non-relativistic limits.
Such a process may provide a clue to the energy conversion mechanism from electromagnetic field to plasma in environments around compact objects, where strongly magnetized plasmas are expected to exist, e.g., in the magnetospheres of neutron stars or black holes.

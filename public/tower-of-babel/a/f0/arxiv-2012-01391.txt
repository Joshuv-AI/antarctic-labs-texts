The 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral of type A n A_{n}
Richárd Rimányi ⋄ and Alexander Varchenko ⋆

Abstract
. We prove an 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral formula of type A n A_{n} , in which the 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral is an element of the finite field 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} with odd prime number p p of elements. The formula is motivated by analogy between multidimensional hypergeometric solutions of the KZ equations and polynomial solutions of the same equations reduced modulo p p . For the type A 1 A_{1} the formula was proved in a previous paper by the authors.

1.
Introduction

In 1944 Atle Selberg proved the following integral formula:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dots\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{\alpha-1}(1-x_{i})^{\beta-1}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=\ \prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(1+j\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)\,\Gamma(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+(k+j-2)\gamma)}\,,

see
[
Se
,
AAR
]
.
Hundreds of papers are devoted to the generalizations of the Selberg
integral formula and its applications, see for example
[
AAR
,
FW
]
and references
therein. There are
q
-analysis versions of the formula,
the generalizations associated with Lie algebras, elliptic versions, finite field versions,
see some references in
[
AAR
,
FW
,
As
,
Ha
,
Ka
,
Op
,
Ch
,
TV1
,
TV2
,
TV4
,
Wa1
,
Wa2
,
Sp
,
R
,
FSV
,
An
,
Ev
]
.
In the finite field versions,
one considers additive and multiplicative characters of a finite field, which
map the field to the field of
complex numbers, and forms an analog of equation (
1.1
), in which both sides are complex numbers.
The simplest of such formulas is the classical relation between Jacobi and Gauss
sums, see
[
AAR
,
An
,
Ev
]
.

In
[
RV
]

we suggested another version of the Selberg integral formula, in which the

{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral is an element of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with an odd prime number
p
of elements.

Our motivation in
[
RV
]

came from the theory of Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov
(KZ) equations, see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
. These are the
systems of linear differential equations, satisfied by
conformal blocks on the sphere in the WZW model of conformal field theory.
The KZ equations were solved in multidimensional hypergeometric integrals in
[
SV1
]
, see also
[
V1
,
V2
]
.
The following general principle was formulated in
[
MuV
]
: if an example of the KZ-type
equations has a one-dimensional space of solutions, then the
corresponding multidimensional hypergeometric integral can be evaluated explicitly.
As an illustration of that principle in
[
MuV
]
, an example of the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
differential KZ
equations with a
one-dimensional space of solutions
was considered, the corresponding multidimensional hypergeometric integral was reduced to the
Selberg integral and then evaluated by formula (
1.1
). See other illustrations in
[
FV
,
FSV
,
TV1
,
TV2
,
TV4
,
V3
,
RTVZ
]
.

Recently in
[
SV2
]
the KZ equations were considered modulo a prime number
p
and polynomial solutions of the
reduced equations were constructed, see also
[
SlV
,
V3
,
V4
,
V5
,
V6
,
V7
]
. The construction
is analogous to the construction
of the multidimensional hypergeometric solutions,
and the constructed polynomial solutions were called
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.

In
[
RV
]
we considered the reduction modulo
p
of the same example of the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

differential KZ equations,
that led in
[
MuV
]
to the Selberg integral. We evaluated the corrersponding
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solution
by analogy with the evaluation of the Selberg integral and
obtained the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral formula in
[
RV
, Theorem 4.1]
.

In
[
TV4
]
the Selberg integral formula of type
A_{2}
was proved,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{C_{c}^{k_{1},k_{2}}[0,1]}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}t_{i}^{\alpha_{1}-1}(1-t_{i})^{\beta_{1}-1}\,\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-s_{j})^{\beta_{2}-1}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}|s_{j}-t_{i}|^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaaa}\times\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<i^{\prime}\leqslant k_{1}}(t_{i}-t_{i^{\prime}})^{2\gamma}\prod_{1\leqslant j<j^{\prime}\leqslant k_{2}}(s_{j}-s_{j^{\prime}})^{2\gamma}\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{k_{1}}\,ds_{1}\dots ds_{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}-k_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\beta_{1}+(i-1)\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta_{1}+(i+k_{1}-2)\gamma)}

\displaystyle\times\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\ \frac{\Gamma(\beta_{2}+(i-1)\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\beta_{2}+(i+k_{2}-k_{1}-2)\gamma)}\,\ \frac{\Gamma(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+(i-2)\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+(i+k_{2}-3)\gamma)}

\displaystyle\times\ \prod_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(1+(i-k_{1}-1)\gamma)\,\Gamma(i\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\ \prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+(i-1)\gamma)\,\Gamma(i\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\,.

The starting point of this formula
was an example of the joint system of the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{3}}
trigonometric differential KZ equations and associated dynamical
difference equations, an example in which the space of solutions is one-dimensional.
The
A_{n}
-type Selberg integral formula for aribitrary

2.
Preliminary remarks

Let
p
be an odd prime number throughout the paper.

2.1.
Cancellation of factorials

Lemma 2.1
.

If
a,b
are nonnegative integers and
a+b=p-1
, then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle a!\,b!\,=\,(-1)^{a+1}\,.

Proof.

We have
a!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)\dots(p-a)
and
p-a=b+1
. Hence

a!\,b!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)!=(-1)^{a+1}
by Wilson’s Theorem.
∎

2.2.
Dyson’s formula

We shall use Dyson’s formula

(2.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{C.T.}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(1-x_{i}/x_{j})^{c}(1-x_{j}/x_{i})^{c}=\frac{(kc)!}{(c!)^{k}}\,,

where C.T. denotes the constant term. See the formula in
[
AAR
, Section 8.8]
.

2.3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Integrals

Let
M
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-module. Let
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be a polynomial
with coefficients in
M
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{d}\,c_{d}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{k}^{d_{k}}.

Let
l=(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{k}
. The coefficient

c_{l_{1}p-1,\dots,l_{k}p-1}
is called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral over the
p
-cycle
[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}

and is denoted by
\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
i=1,\dots,k-1
we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{i+1},l_{i},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1},x_{i},\dots,x_{k})dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaa}=\ \int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}\,.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
i=1,\dots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}=0\,.

∎

Let
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{>0}
and

(2.5)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}:=[(1)_{k_{1}};(k_{1})_{k_{2}};\dots;(k_{n-1})_{k_{n}}]_{p},

where
x_{y}
denotes the
y
-tuple
(x,\dots,x)
.
For example for
n=2
,
\boldsymbol{k}=(3,2)
, we have
[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}=[1,1,1;3,3]_{p}
.

2.4.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Beta integral

For nonnegative integers the classical beta integral formula says

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!}\,.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
V7
]
)
.

Let

a<p
,
b<p
,
p-1\leqslant a+b
.
Then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=\,-\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1-p)!}\,.

If
a+b<p-1
, then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=0\,.

3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral of type
A_{n}

3.1.
Admissible parameters

Let
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{>0}
and
k_{i}>k_{i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
. Set
k_{0}=k_{n+1}=0
.

Let
a,b_{1},\dots,b_{n},c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Denote
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})

and

(3.1)

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)=

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant n}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{r}-k_{r+1}}\frac{(r-s+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(i+s-r-1)c)!}{(r-s+1+a_{s}+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(i+s-r+k_{s}-k_{s-1}-2)c-\delta_{s,1}p)!}

\displaystyle\times\ (-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(a_{1}+(i-1)c)!\Big)\Big(\prod_{r=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\frac{(ic)!}{c!}\Big)\Big(\prod_{r=2}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{r}}(p+(i-k_{r-1}-1)c)!\Big),

where
a_{1}=a
,
a_{2}=\dots=a_{n}=0
;
\delta_{s,1}
is
1
if
s=1
and is zero otherwise.

We say that
a,b_{1},\dots,b_{n},c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
are
admissible
if

a+(k_{1}-1)c<p-1
and for any factorial
x!
in the right-hand side of (
3.1
) we have

0\leqslant x<p
. The set of all admissible
(a,b,c)

is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The set
\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is defined
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{n+2}

by the following system of inequalities:

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r-s+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(s-r)c,

\displaystyle r-s+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(k_{r}-k_{r+1}+s-r-1)c\leqslant p-1,

for
1\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant n
;

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0\leqslant r-s+1+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(s-r+k_{s}-k_{s-1}-1)c,

\displaystyle r-s+1+b_{s}+\dots+b_{r}+(s-r+k_{r}-k_{r+1}+k_{s}-k_{s-1}-2)c\leqslant p-1,

for
2\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant n
;

(3.4)

\displaystyle p\leqslant r+a+b_{1}+\dots+b_{r}+(k_{1}-r)c,

\displaystyle r+a+b_{1}+\dots+b_{r}+(k_{r}-k_{r+1}+k_{1}-r-1)c<2p,

for
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
;

(3.5)

\displaystyle a+(k_{1}-1)c<p-1,\quad b_{1}\geqslant p-1-(a+(k_{1}-1)c),\quad 0<k_{1}c<p\,.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. Then

(3.6)

\displaystyle b_{1}\geqslant p-1-(a+(k_{1}-1)c),\qquad b_{s}\geqslant(k_{s-1}-k_{s}+1)c-1,\quad s=2,\dots,n.

Proof.

The inequality

b_{s}\geqslant(k_{s-1}-k_{s}+1)c-1
for
s=2,\dots,n
follows from the first inequality in (
3.3
) for
r=s
.
The inequality
b_{1}\geqslant p-1-(a+(k_{1}-1)c)
follows from the first inequality in (
3.4
) for
r=1
.
∎

Example
.

Let
n=1
,
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1})
. Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle R_{(k_{1})}(a,b_{1},c)=\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{(ic)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(i-1)c)!\,(b_{1}+(i-1)c)!}{(1+a+b_{1}+(i+k_{1}-2)c-p)!}

and

\mathcal{A}_{(k_{1})}
consists of
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that

(3.8)

\displaystyle a+(k_{1}-1)c<p-1,\qquad\qquad b_{1}+(k_{1}-1)c\leqslant p-1,\qquad k_{1}c\,\leqslant\,p-1,

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b_{1}+(k_{1}-1)c,\qquad a+b_{1}+(2k_{1}-2)c<2p-1\ .

3.2.
Main theorem

Given
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{>0}
introduce

k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}
variables

(3.9)

\displaystyle t=(t^{(1)},\dots,t^{(n)}),\quad\operatorname{where}\quad t^{(i)}=(t^{(i)}_{1},\dots,t^{(i)}_{k_{i}}),\quad\,i=1,\dots,n.

Define the
master polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t;a,b,c)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}(t^{(i)}_{j})^{a_{i}}(1-t^{(i)}_{j})^{b_{i}}\prod_{1\leqslant j<j^{\prime}\leqslant k_{i}}(t^{(i)}_{j}-t^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}})^{2c}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i+1}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{k_{i}}(t^{(i+1)}_{j}-t^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}})^{p-c}\,.

Denote

(3.10)

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)=\int_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\,dt\,.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral
S_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)

is called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral of type
A_{n}
.

Here is our main result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{>0}
,
k_{i}>k_{i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
.
Then for any
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{A}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
we have the equality in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
:

(3.11)

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)\,=\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)\,.

For
n=1
this theorem is
[
RV
, Theorem 4.1]
. For
n=2
the theorem is deduced from the case
of
n=1
in Section
5
. More generally, for any
k
the theorem for
n=k
is deduced
from the theorem for
n=k-1
similarly, see Section
5.4
.

Remark
.

Theorem
3.3
can be extended to the case of
\boldsymbol{k}
such that

k_{i}\geqslant k_{i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
, but the structure of inequalities in Lemma
3.1
will depend on
the appearance of equalities
k_{i}=k_{i+1}
in
\boldsymbol{k}
, and the proof of Theorem
3.3
will split into different
sub-cases. To shorten the exposition we restrict ourselves with
\boldsymbol{k}
such that
k_{i}>k_{i+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Example
.

Here is the simplest
A_{2}
-type
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral formula with
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
a,b_{1},b_{2},c
are integers such that

\displaystyle 0\leqslant a<p,\quad 0<c\leqslant p,\quad 0\leqslant b_{2}-c+1<p,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant b_{1}+b_{2}-c+1<p,\quad p-1\leqslant a+b_{1}+b_{2}-c+1<2p-1.

Then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{[1;1]_{p}}t^{a}(1-t)^{b_{1}}(s-t)^{p-c}(1-s)^{b_{2}}dt\,ds=\ \frac{a!\,(b_{1}+b_{2}-c+1)!}{(a+b_{1}+b_{2}-c+2-p)!}\,\frac{(p-c)!\,(b_{2})!}{(b_{2}-c+1)!}\,.

Proof.

Change variables
s=t+(1-t)v
, then the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral becomes equal to

\displaystyle\int_{[1,1]_{p}}t^{a}(1-t)^{b_{1}+b_{2}-c+1}v^{p-c}(1-v)^{b_{2}}dt\,dv.

Applying the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-beta integral formula we obtain the theorem.
∎

The simplest
A_{3}
-type
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral formula
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=1

is given by the next theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
a,b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},c
be integers such that
all factorials in the right-hand side of formula (
3.13
) are factorials of nonnegaive integers less than
p
.
Then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{[1;1;1]_{p}}t^{a}(1-t)^{b_{1}}(s-t)^{p-c}(1-s)^{b_{2}}(u-s)^{p-c}(1-u)^{b_{2}}dt\,ds\,du

\displaystyle\phantom{aa}=\ -\ \frac{a!\,(b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}-2c+2)!}{(a+b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}-2c+3-p)!}\,\frac{(p-c)!\,(b_{2}+b_{3}-c+1)!}{(b_{2}+b_{3}-2c+2)!}\,\frac{(p-c)!\,(b_{3})!}{(b_{3}-c+1)!}\,.

Proof.

The proof is the same as of the previous theorem.
∎

The versions of identities (
3.12
), (
3.13
) over complex numbers see in

[
MuV
, Theorem 1]
.

3.3.
Relation between the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integrals of types
A_{n-1}
- and
A_{n}

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
n>1
and
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
,
\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1})
,
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
,

b^{\prime}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1})
.
Assume that formula (
3.11
) holds for the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral

S_{[\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}]_{p}}(a,b^{\prime},c)
of type
A_{n-1}
.
Also assume that
b_{n}=(k_{n-1}-k_{n}+1)c-1
.
Then formula (
3.11
) holds for
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral
S_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}(a,b,c)
of type
A_{n}
.

Proof.

Under the assumption
b_{n}=(k_{n-1}-k_{n}+1)c-1
all variables
(t^{(n)}_{j})

in
\Phi_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}(\boldsymbol{t};a,b,c)
are used to reach
the monomial
\prod_{j=1}^{k_{n}}(t^{(n)}_{j})^{k_{n-1}p-1}
in the calculation of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral

S_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}(a,b,c)
. The remaining free variables

(t^{(i)}_{j})
with
i<n
all belong to the factor

\Phi_{[\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}]_{p}}(t^{(1)},\dots,t^{(n-1)};a,b^{\prime},c)
of
\Phi_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}(\boldsymbol{t};a,b,c)

and are used to calculate the coefficient of

\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(t^{(1)}_{j})^{p-1}\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}(t^{(i)}_{j})^{k_{i-1}p-1}
.

More precisely, under the assumptions of the theorem we have

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)=(-1)^{b_{n}k_{n}+ck_{n}(k_{n}-1)/2}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}\,S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c),

where
(-1)^{b_{n}k_{n}+ck_{n}(k_{n}-1)/2}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}
is the coefficient of

\prod_{j=1}^{k_{n}}(t^{(n)}_{j})^{k_{n-1}p-1}
in the expansion of

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k_{n}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{k_{n-1}}(t^{(n)}_{j}-t^{(n-1)}_{j^{\prime}})^{p-c}\prod_{1\leqslant j<j^{\prime}\leqslant k_{n}}(t^{(n)}_{j}-t^{(n)}_{j^{\prime}})^{2c}\,,

see Dyson’s formula. We have
(-1)^{b_{n}k_{n}+ck_{n}(k_{n}-1)/2}=(-1)^{(k_{n-1}k_{n}-k_{n}(k_{n}+1)/2)c-k_{n}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(k_{n-1}k_{n}-k_{n}(k_{n}+1)/2)c-k_{n}}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}\,S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(k_{n-1}k_{n}-k_{n}(k_{n}+1)/2)c-k_{n}}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,,

where
S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)=R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)
holds by assumptions.
To prove the theorem we need to show that

\displaystyle R_{[\boldsymbol{k}]_{p}}(a,(b^{\prime},b_{n}),c)=(-1)^{(k_{n-1}k_{n}-k_{n}(k_{n}+1)/2)c-k_{n}}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,.

Indeed we have

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{k}}(a,(b^{\prime},b_{n}),c)=\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(ic)!}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}(p+(i-k_{n-1}-1)c)!

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leqslant s\leqslant n}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(n-s+b_{s}+\dots+b_{n}+(i+s-n-1)c)!}{(n-s+1+a_{s}+b_{s}+\dots+b_{n}+(i+s-n+k_{s}-k_{s-1}-2)c-\delta_{s,1}p)!}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leqslant s\leqslant n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(n-s+a_{s}+b_{s}+\dots+b_{n-1}+(i+s-n+k_{s}-k_{s-1}-3)c-\delta_{s,1}p)!}{(n-1-s+b_{s}+\dots+b_{n-1}+(i+s-r-1)c)!}

\displaystyle=\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(ic)!}{c!}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(b_{n}+(i-1)c)!\,(p+(i-k_{n-1}-1)c)!}{(1+b_{n}+(i+k_{n}-k_{n-1}-2)c)!}\,.

\displaystyle=\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{(ic)!}{c!}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k_{n}}\frac{((i+k_{n-1}-k_{n})c-1)!\,(p+(i-k_{n-1}-1)c)!}{((i-1)c))!}\,.

\displaystyle=\,R_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}(a,b^{\prime},c)\,(-1)^{(k_{n-1}k_{n}-k_{n}(k_{n}+1)/2)c-k_{n}}\frac{(k_{n}c)!}{(c!)^{k_{n}}}\,,

where in the last step we use the cancellation Lemma
2.1
.
The theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 4.2

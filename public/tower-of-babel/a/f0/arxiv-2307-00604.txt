Listing Small Minimal s , t s,t -separators in FPT-Delay
Batya Kenig

Abstract
Let G G be an undirected graph, and s , t s,t distinguished vertices of G G . A minimal s , t s,t -separator is an inclusion-wise minimal vertex-set whose removal places s s and t t in distinct connected components. We present an algorithm for listing the minimal s , t s,t -separators of a graph, whose cardinality is at most k k , with FPT-delay, where the parameter depends only on k k . This problem finds applications in various algorithms parameterized by treewidth, which include query evaluation in relational databases, probabilistic inference, and many more. We also present a simple algorithm that enumerates all of the (not necessarily minimal) s , t s,t -separators of a graph in ranked order by size.

Introduction

Let
G(V,E)
be a finite, undirected graph with nodes
V=\texttt{V}(G)
and edges
E=\texttt{E}(G)
.
For two distinguished vertices
s,t\in\texttt{V}(G)
, an
s,t
-vertex-separator is a subset
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
such that
s
and
t
reside in distinct connected components of the graph that results from
G
by removing vertices
S
and their adjacent edges; an
s,t
-edge-separator is a subset
A\subseteq\texttt{E}(G)
such that
s
and
t
reside in distinct connected components of the graph
G(V,E\setminus A)
.
Vertex and edge separators are widely studied in graph theory, with a long and deep history that is motivated by a wide range of applications in computer science
[
31
]
.
Finding optimal (e.g., minimum weight) edge and vertex separators are fundamental problems, that are solved in polynomial time using network flow techniques
[
21
,
15
,
14
]
. A natural extension is the task of their enumeration, and in particular, enumerating edge and vertex-separators in ranked order by their size or weight. In this work, we focus on
s,t
-vertex-separators
[
2
,
24
,
33
]
.
An
s,t
-separator
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
is a
minimal
s,t
-separator
if no strict subset of
S
is an
s,t
-separator. We say that
S
is a
minimum
s,t
-separator
if
|S^{\prime}|\geq|S|
for every
s,t
-separator
S^{\prime}
.

Enumerating minimal separators of bounded cardinality refines and extends two well-studied enumeration problems
[
25
]
: enumeration of all minimal separators, and enumeration of all minimum separators
[
22
]
.
Berry et al.
[
2
]
developed an efficient algorithm that lists the minimal separators of a graph with a delay of
O(|\texttt{V}(G)|^{3})
between consecutive outputs. The algorithm of Berry et al.
[
2
]
, as well as others
[
24
,
33
,
32
]
, does not list the minimal separators in any ranked order. Kanevsky
[
22
]
developed a complicated algorithm that enumerates all the minimum separators of a graph. In this work, we present an algorithm that given an undirected graph
G
with
n
vertices and
m
edges, and a bound
k
, lists all of the minimal
s,t
-separators whose size is at most
k
with delay
O(p(n,m)\cdot 4^{k})
where
p
is a polynomial in
n,m
. Recently, Korhonen presented an algorithm that lists the minimal
s,t
-separators whose size is at most
k
in incremental-polynomial-time
[
25
]
. We improve this to FPT-delay. We also present a simple algorithm that enumerates the
s,t
-separators in ranked order by size.

Motivation and Applications.
Fixed Parameter Tractable (FPT) algorithms
[
10
,
16
]
are those with running time
O(n^{c}f(k))
, for a constant
c
independent of both the size of the input
n
, and the parameter
k
, and
f
is a
computable function. The
treewidth
of a graph is an important graph complexity measure that
occurs as a parameter in many FPT algorithms
[
16
,
5
]
that operate over the
tree decomposition
of the graph.
A tree decomposition of a graph
G
is a tree
{\mathcal{T}}
such that each
node of
{\mathcal{T}}
is associated with a bag of vertices of
G
, every
edge in
\texttt{E}(G)
is contained in at least one bag, and every vertex
in
\texttt{V}(G)
spans a connected subtree of
{\mathcal{T}}
.
The
width
of a tree decomposition is defined as the size of the largest bag minus one. The
treewidth
of a graph
G
is the minimum width among all possible tree decompositions of
G
.

A tree decomposition is
proper
if it cannot be “improved” by removing or splitting a bag
[
8
]
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between the
minimal triangulations
and the proper tree decompositions of a graph
G

[
8
]
.
A graph
G
is
chordal
if every cycle of
G
with four or more vertices contains a chord.
A
triangulation
of
G
is a graph
G^{\prime}
that is obtained from
G
by adding edges so that
G^{\prime}
is chordal. The cardinality of the set of added edges is called the
fill-in
of the triangulation
G^{\prime}
. A triangulation is
minimal
if no triangulation can be obtained using a strict subset of the added edges. It is shown in
[
8
]
that every edge in a proper tree decomposition represents a minimal separator in
G
; the intersection of the two bags adjacent to the edge is a minimal separator of
G
. Therefore, a necessary condition for a (proper) tree decomposition to have low width, is that the minimal separators represented by its edges have bounded cardinality. Specifically, a proper tree decomposition whose width is
k
can only represent minimal separators (i.e., via its edges) whose size is at most
k

[
34
]
.

Indeed, while computing an optimal tree decomposition, with the smallest width, is NP-hard
[
1
]
, current state-of-the-art algorithms for finding an optimal tree-decomposition require the set of minimal separators with bounded cost
[
34
,
26
,
25
,
6
,
7
]
. A central (exponential time) algorithm for finding an optimal tree decomposition
is the one due to Bouchitté and Todinca
[
6
]
(BT algorithm). The BT algorithm has been shown to be highly efficient
[
11
,
26
]
, and applicable to a range of cost functions
[
4
,
18
]
.
The first phase of the BT algorithm, and its extensions, employs a subroutine for listing the set of minimal separators of the graph
[
6
]
. Consequently, an algorithm that efficiently lists the small minimal separators of a graph can lead to significant performance gains in finding
good
, or low-width, tree decompositions. Another parameter of interest is
treedepth

[
37
,
36
]
, important in a variety of combinatorial optimization problems. Its computation also requires all the minimal separators of the graph of bounded size. Motivated by these tasks, we study the problem of efficiently enumerating small minimal
s,t
-separators.

In any ranked enumeration algorithm, finding the top element is basically an
optimization problem
. In our case, there are well known algorithms for finding a
minimum-cardinality
s,t
-separator

[
21
,
15
,
9
]
. For
k>1
, finding the
k
-th ranking item amounts to computing the optimal minimal
s,t
-separator under the restriction that it is not among the first
k-1
items previously returned. Handling this constraint is the main challenge when designing ranked enumeration and top-
K
algorithms.

The technique of Lawler and Murty
[
28
]
provides a general framework for ranked enumeration corresponding to discrete optimization problems. The main idea is to reduce a ranked enumeration problem to an optimization problem with constraints
[
19
]
.
In the standard approach to applying the Lawler-Murty technique, the algorithm first finds the optimal solution
S
(e.g., minimum
s,t
-separator), then the subspace of solutions (excluding
S
) is partitioned using
inclusion
and
exclusion
constraints. The straightforward approach to applying the Lawler-Murty method to ranked enumeration of minimal
s,t
-separators is by solving the following optimization problem: find the smallest minimal
s,t
-separator that excludes a subset
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, and includes a subset
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
of vertices. Using this approach, we immediately hit an obstacle. In Appendix
A
, we show that deciding whether
there exists a minimal
s,t
-separator that includes a distinguished vertex
v\in\texttt{V}(G)
, is NP-complete by reduction from the
3
-in-a-path
problem
[
12
]
. On the other hand, we show that the task of finding the smallest
s,t
- separator that excludes a subset of vertices can be performed in polynomial time. This leads to an algorithm for enumerating the
s,t
-separators in ranked order by size.
Overall, we prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite, undirected graph, and
s,t\in\texttt{V}(G)
be non-adjacent. The set of minimal
s,t
-separators whose cardinality is at most
k
can be listed with delay
O(nk4^{k}T(n,m))
where
n=|\texttt{V}(G)|
,
m=|\texttt{E}(G)|
, and
T(n,m)
is the time to find a minimum
s,t
-separator of
G
.

The problem of finding a minimum
s,t
-separator in an undirected graph can be reduced, by standard techniques
[
14
]
, to finding a maximum flow from
s
to
t
.
Following a sequence of improvements to max-flow algorithms in the past few years
[
29
,
23
,
35
]
, the current best performing algorithm runs in almost linear time
m^{1+o(1)}

[
9
]
.
We also prove the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a finite, undirected graph, and
s,t\in\texttt{V}(G)
be non-adjacent. There is an enumeration algorithm that outputs the
s,t
-separators of
G
in ranked order by cardinality, whose delay is
O(n\cdot T(n,m))
. In particular, our algorithm lists the minimum
s,t
separators of
G
with delay
O(k\cdot T(n,m))
, where
k
is the cardinality of a minimum
s,t
-separator.

The algorithm of Theorem
2
follows the framework of Lawler and Murty, and is easily implementable (see Figure
1
).

Organization.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Following preliminaries in Section
2
, we prove some useful properties of minimal

-separators in Section

.
In Section

3
A required Characterization of Minimal
s,t
-separators

In this section, we prove three useful lemmas concerning minimal
s,t
-separators that will be instrumental for the enumeration algorithm.

Lemma 10
.

Let
v\in N_{G}(s)
, and let
G^{\prime}
denote the graph where
\texttt{V}(G^{\prime})=\texttt{V}(G)
and
\texttt{E}(G^{\prime})=\texttt{E}(G)\cup\mathord{\{(s,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y\in N_{G}(v)\}}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})=\mathord{\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v\notin S\}}
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
such that
v\notin S
. Since
v\in N_{G}(s)
, then
v\in C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
, and hence
N_{G}(v)\subseteq S\cup C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. Therefore,
S
is an
s,t
-separator in
G^{\prime}
as well. Since
\texttt{E}(G^{\prime})\supseteq\texttt{E}(G)
, then
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
. Now, let
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
. Since
N_{G^{\prime}}(v)\subseteq N_{G^{\prime}}(s)
, and
v\in N_{G^{\prime}}(s)
, then
v\in C_{s}(G^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
, and in particular,
v\notin T
. Since
\texttt{E}(G^{\prime})\supseteq\texttt{E}(G)
, then
T
separates
s
from
t
in
G
. Therefore, there is a subset
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Since
v\notin T
, then
v\notin T^{\prime}
. Hence,
T^{\prime}\in\mathord{\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v\notin S\}}
. By the previous,
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
. If
T^{\prime}\subset T
, we arrive at a contradiction that
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
. Therefore,
T^{\prime}=T
, and hence
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
as required.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
S,T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Then:

\displaystyle C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)\text{ if and only if }T\subseteq S\cup C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)

Proof.

If
T\subseteq S\cup C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
, then by definition
T\cap C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)=\emptyset
. Therefore,
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
remains connected in

. This means that
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
.

Now, suppose that
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
. By Lemma
3
, it holds that

. Since

, then

. Since

then by definition it holds that

. This, in turn, implies that
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)

G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
. In particular, we have that
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)\subseteq C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. By Lemma
3
, it holds that
T=N_{G}(C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T))
. Since
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)\subseteq C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
, then
T=N_{G}(C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T))\subseteq S\cup C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

Let

be the graph that results from

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S})=\mathord{\{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Q\subseteq S\cup C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\}}

Proof.

Let

where

remains connected in

. Therefore,
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Q)
. By Lemma
3
,
S=N_{G}(C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S))
. Since
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Q)
, then
S=N_{G}(C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S))\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Q)\cup Q
. In particular,
S\cap C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Q)=\emptyset
. Consequently,

C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Q)
from
s
in

s,t
-separator in

. Since

Let

S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S})
where

. By Lemma

.
∎

4.1
Proof of Correctness

We begin by establishing that a subset

is printed by the algorithm if and only if
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, and
|S|\leq k
. Then, we analyze the runtime.

Theorem 13
.

If
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
is printed by the algorithm, then
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
and
|S|\leq k
.

Proof.

We first note that every subset of vertices inserted into the queue (in lines
7
and
17
) has cardinality at most
k
, and separates
s
from
t
. Therefore, we only need to show that every subset of vertices printed by the algorithm belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
.

Suppose, by way of contradiction, that this is not the case, and let
T\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
be the first subset of vertices printed by the algorithm where
T\notin\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Then
T
must be inserted into the queue in line
17
. Consider the set
S
that was printed before
T
is inserted into the queue. By our assumption
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Therefore,
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(H_{S}^{v})
, where
v\in S
. By Lemma
12
,
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Since
v\in N_{H_{S}}(s)
, and
H_{S}^{v}
is the graph that results from
H_{S}
by adding all edges
\mathord{\{(s,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}y\in N_{H_{S}}(v)\}}
, by Lemma
10
, it holds that
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S}^{v})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
.
Since
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(H_{S}^{v})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(H_{S}^{v})
, we get that
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, which brings us to a contradiction.
∎

We now prove that every minimal separator
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, where
|T|\leq k
, is printed by the algorithm.

Lemma 14
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
such that
|T|\leq k
. There exists a
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
such that
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
.

Proof.

If
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
, then the claim is immediate. We prove the claim by induction on
|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)|
. If
|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)|=1
, then clearly
T\subseteq N_{G}(s)
. By Lemma
4
,
T
is the unique minimal
s,t
-separator that is close to
s
, and hence
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
, and the claim follows.
So, we assume the claim holds for all
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, where
|T|\leq k
, and
1\leq|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)|\leq\ell
. Let
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, where
|T|\leq k
, and
|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)|=\ell+1
. Since
T\notin\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
, then by definition, there exists a
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
such that
|T^{\prime}|\leq|T|\leq k
, and
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T^{\prime})\subset C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
. If
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
, then we are done.
Otherwise, observe that
|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T^{\prime})|<|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)|=\ell+1
. Therefore,
|C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T^{\prime})|\leq\ell
, and by the induction hypothesis there exists a
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
such that
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T^{\prime})
. Consequently, we have that
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T^{\prime})\subset C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
, and thus
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\subseteq C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)
, where
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t,k}^{*}(G)
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Corollary 15
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
such that
|T|\leq k
. There exists a

such that

By Lemma

, it holds that

.
∎

Theorem 16

Let

. Then

is printed by Algorithm

in Figure

.

Proof.

Suppose that

Theorem 17
.

The delay between the printing of consecutive minimal
s,t
-separators whose size is at most

is

, where

,

5
Enumeration Algorithms for
s,t
-Separators

In this section, we develop an enumeration algorithm that returns all (not necessarily minimal)
s,t
-separators of
G
in ranked order by cardinality, and an enumeration algorithm that returns only the minimum-cardinality
s,t
-separators of
G
(i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
).
We characterize vertices included in minimum-cardinality
s,t
-separators in Section
5.1
, and vertices excluded from any minimal
s,t
-separator (and in-particular, any minimum
s,t
-separator) in Section
5.2
.
The resulting simple algorithms are then presented in Section
5.3
.

5.1
Characterizations of Vertices Included In Minimum
s,t
-Separators

In this section, we show that there is a polynomial-time algorithm, that given a subset of vertices
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, returns a minimum
s,t
-separator that contains
I
, if one exists.

Lemma 18
.

Let
v\in\texttt{V}(G)
. There exists a minimum
s,t
-separator
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
that contains
v
if and only if
\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}v)=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{s,t}(G)=k
. Let
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
be such that
v\in S
. Consider the graph
G^{\prime}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}v
. Removing a single vertex can decrease the
s,t
-connectivity of a graph by at most
1
. Therefore,
\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1\leq\kappa_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
. On the other hand,
S\setminus\mathord{\{v\}}
is an
s,t
-separator in
G^{\prime}
, and hence
\kappa_{s,t}(G^{\prime})\leq|S|-1=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1
. Overall, we get that
\kappa_{s,t}(G^{\prime})=\kappa_{s,t}(G-v)=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1
.

Now, suppose that
\kappa_{s,t}(G)=k
and
\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}v)=k-1
. Take
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}v)
. By our assumption
|S|=k-1
. Since
S
separates
s
from
t
in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}v
, then clearly
S\cup\mathord{\{v\}}
separates
s
from
t
in
G
. Since
|S\cup\mathord{\{v\}}|=k=\kappa_{s,t}(G)
, then
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
where
v\in S
.
∎

Corollary 19
.

Let
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, and let
k_{I}=|I|
. There exists a minimum separator
S\in\mathcal{L}_{st}(G)
that contains
I
if and only if
\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}I)=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-k_{I}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{s,t}(G)=k
. We prove by induction on
k_{I}
, the size of
I
. The case where
I=\emptyset
is trivial, and the case where
|I|=1
is proved in Lemma
18
. So assume the claim holds for all sets
I
where
0\leq|I|\leq\ell
, and we prove for the case where
|I|=\ell+1
.

Let
x\in I
. By Lemma
18
, there exists a minimum separator
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
that contains
x
if and only if
\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}x)=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1
. Therefore, if
\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}x)>\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1
, there does not exist a minimum separator that includes
x
, and hence, there does not exist one that contains
I
.
Otherwise, let
H\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}x
, and hence
\kappa_{s,t}(H)=k-1
. Let
Y\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}I\setminus\mathord{\{x\}}
. Since
|Y|\leq\ell
, then we can apply the induction hypothesis. There is a minimum separator
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(H)
that contains
Y
if and only if
\kappa_{s,t}(H\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Y)=\kappa_{s,t}(H)-|Y|=\kappa_{s,t}(H)-\ell
.
Since
H=G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}x
, we get that

\displaystyle\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}I)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{s,t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}(Y\cup\mathord{\{x\}}))

\displaystyle=\kappa_{s,t}(H\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}Y)=\kappa_{s,t}(H)-|Y|=\kappa_{s,t}(H)-\ell

\displaystyle=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-1-\ell=\kappa_{s,t}(G)-(\ell+1)

∎

Proposition 20
.

Let
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
. We can determine whether there is a minimum
s,t
-separator
S\in\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G)
that contains
I
, and return one if exists, in time
O(n+T(n,m))
, where
T(n,m)
is the time to find a minimum
s,t
-separator.

5.2
Excluding Vertices from Minimal Separators

In this section, we characterize minimal
s,t
-separators that exclude a subset
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
of vertices.
We define some required notation.
Let
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})
the set of minimal
s,t
-separators that exclude
U
. Formally:

\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\mathord{\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)\setminus U\}}

(1)

For a single vertex
u\in\texttt{V}(G)
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{u})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\mathord{\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}u\notin S\}}
.
We denote by
\kappa_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})
the minimum size of any minimal
s,t
-separator that excludes
U
. Formally:

\kappa_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\min\mathord{\{|S|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\}}

(2)

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})
the subset of
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})
that have the smallest cardinality. Formally:

\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\mathord{\{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}|S|=\kappa_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})\}}

(3)

Let
u\in\texttt{V}(G)
; we denote by
\mathrm{Sat}(G,u)
the graph that results by adding edges between all vertices in
N_{G}[u]
. In other words,
\mathrm{Sat}(G,u)
is the graph where the set
N_{G}[u]
has been
saturated
, and forms a clique.
For a set of vertices
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, we denote by
\mathrm{Sat}(G,U)
the graph that results by adding edges between all vertices in
N_{G}[u]
for all
u\in U
. Formally,
\texttt{V}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))=\texttt{V}(G)
and

\texttt{E}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\texttt{E}(G)\cup\bigcup_{u\in U}\mathord{\{(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x,y\in N_{G}[u]\}}

(4)

We prove that
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
.
We proceed by a series of lemmas.

Lemma 21
.

Let
u\in\texttt{V}(G)
such that
N_{G}[u]
forms a clique. Then
u\notin S
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. By Lemma
3
,
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
contains two full components
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
and
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
containing
s
and
t
respectively, such that
S=N_{G}(C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S))=N_{G}(C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S))
. Therefore, if
u\in S
, then it has two neighbors
v_{1}\in C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
and
v_{2}\in C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
that are connected by an edge (because
N_{G}[u]
is a clique). But then, there is an
s,t
-path in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
that avoids
S
, which contradicts the fact that
S
is an
s,t
-separator.
∎

Lemma 22
.

For every
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{u})
, there exists a connected component
C_{u}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
such that
N_{G}[u]\subseteq C_{u}\cup S
.

Proof.

Let
C_{u}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
be the connected component that contains
u
. Such a component must exist because
u\notin S
. If
N_{G}(u)\not\subseteq C_{u}\cup S
, then there exists a vertex
v\in N_{G}(u)
that resides in a connected component
C_{v}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
distinct from
C_{u}
. But this is a contradiction because, by definition,
(u,v)\in\texttt{E}(G)
. Hence,
C_{v}=C_{u}
, and this proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
u\in\texttt{V}(G)
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{u})=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}}))
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{u})
. Let
C_{u}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
denote the connected component containing
u
in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
.
By Lemma
22
,
N_{G}[u]\subseteq C_{u}\cup S
. Therefore, no added edge in
\texttt{E}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}}))\setminus\texttt{E}(G)
connects vertices in distinct connected components in
\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
. Hence,
S
separates
s
and
t
also in
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
. Since the addition of edges cannot eliminate any path between
s
and
t
, we get that
S
is a minimal
s,t
-separator also in
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
.

Now, let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}}))
. Hence,
N_{G}[u]
is a clique in
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
. By Lemma
21
,
u\notin S
.
Since
G
is a subgraph of
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
, then if
S
separates
s
from
t
in
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
, it must separate
s
from
t
in
G
. Hence,
S
is an
s,t
-separator in
G
where
u\notin S
.
It is left to show that
S
is a
minimal

s,t
-separator in
G
. To that end, we show that the connected components
C_{s},C_{t}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})}(S)
, containing
s
and
t
respectively, are full connected components of
S
also in
G
. That is, we show that
S=N_{G}(C_{s})=N_{G}(C_{t})
. By Lemma
3
, this proves that
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{u})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
.

Denote by
D_{s},D_{t}\in\mathcal{C}_{G}(S)
the connected components containing
s
and
t
respectively in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
.
Since
G[D_{s}]
(
G[D_{t}]
) is connected,
D_{s}\cap S=\emptyset
(
D_{t}\cap S=\emptyset
), and
s\in D_{s}
(
t\in D_{t}
), then
D_{s}\subseteq C_{s}
(
D_{t}\subseteq C_{t}
). We now prove that
C_{s}\subseteq D_{s}
. We first consider the case where
u\notin C_{s}
. Hence, by definition of connected component of
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
, we have that
N_{G}[u]\cap C_{s}=\emptyset
. Since the only added edges are between vertices in
N_{G}(u)
, then
\texttt{E}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,u)[C_{s}])=\texttt{E}(G[C_{s}])
. Therefore
C_{s}
is a connected component containing
s
also in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
, thus
C_{s}\subseteq D_{s}
, and
C_{s}=D_{s}
. Since
N_{G}[u]\cap C_{s}=\emptyset
, then
N_{G}(C_{s})=N_{\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})}(C_{s})=S
as required.

We now consider the case where
u\in C_{s}
, and
suppose, by way of contradiction, that
C_{s}\not\subseteq D_{s}
. Let
v\in C_{s}\setminus D_{s}
. This means that there is a path from
s
to
v
in
\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})
that avoids
S
. Let
P
denote the shortest such path. Then
P
passes through a single edge
(y,w)\in\texttt{E}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,u))\setminus\texttt{E}(G)
. In other words, there is a path
P_{vy}
from
v
to
y
in
G
that avoids
S
, and a path
P_{sw}
from
s
to
w
in
G
that avoids
S
. In particular,
\texttt{V}(P_{sw})\subseteq D_{s}
.
By construction,
\mathord{\{y,w\}}\subseteq N_{G}(u)
.
Since
\texttt{V}(P_{ws})\cap S=\emptyset
,
w\in N_{G}(u)
, and
\mathord{\{u,w,y\}}\cap S=\emptyset
, this means that
\mathord{\{u,w,y\}}\subseteq D_{s}
.
But this means that the path
P_{vy}uP_{ws}
is contained in
G
, and avoids
S
. Consequently,
v\in D_{s}
, and we arrive at a contradiction.
Hence,
D_{s}=C_{s}
. Since
u\in C_{s}
, we get that
N_{G}(C_{s})=N_{\mathrm{Sat}(G,\mathord{\{u\}})}(C_{s})
, making
C_{s}
a full connected component of
S
also in
G
. This completes the proof.

∎

Theorem 24
.

Let
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
23
by a simple induction on
|U|
.
∎

Corollary 25
.

The following holds for every
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
.

\mathcal{L}_{st}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{L}_{st}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
.

Proof.

In Theorem
24
, we have shown that for any
U\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, it holds that
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
.
Let
0\leq k\leq n
be an integer, and
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})_{k}
and
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))_{k}
denote the sets of minimal
s,t
-separators in
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})
and
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
whose size is exactly
k
, respectively. Since

\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
, then
\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})_{k}=\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))_{k}

\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(G,\overline{U})=\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(\mathrm{Sat}(G,U))
(see (
3
)).
∎

APPENDIX

A
Minimal Separators and Chordless
s,t
-paths

In this section we show that given a set
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, it is NP-hard to decide whether there exists a minimal
s,t
-separator
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
such that
I\subset S
. We prove this by showing a reduction from the problem
3-in-a-path
that
asks whether there is an induced (or chordless) path containing three given terminals. Bienstock
[
3
]
has shown that deciding whether two terminals belong to an induced cycle is NP-hard. From this, it is easy to show that the
3-in-a-path
problem is NP-hard even for graphs whose degree is at most three
[
12
]
. In fact, even deciding whether there is such a path of length at most
k
was shown to be
W[1]
-complete with respect to the length parameter
k

[
20
]
. The related problem, called
three-in-a-tree
, for deciding whether there is an induced tree containing three terminals, is in PTIME
[
27
]
.

Theorem 26
.

Let
v\in\texttt{V}(G)
. There exists a minimal
s,t
-separator that includes
v
if and only if there exists a chordless
s,t
-path through
v
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
where
v\in S
, and let
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
,
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
denote the connected components of
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
that contain
s
and
t
respectively. By Lemma
3
, there exists a path from
s
to
v
where all the internal vertices belong to
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. Let
P_{sv}
denote the shortest such path. Likewise, let
P_{vt}
denote the shortest path from
v
to
t
where all internal vertices belong to
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. Clearly,
P_{sv}
and
P_{vt}
are both chordless paths. Since
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)\cap C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)=\emptyset
, then
\texttt{V}(P_{sv})\cap\texttt{V}(P_{vt})=\mathord{\{v\}}
. Since
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
, then there are no edges between vertices in
C_{s}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
and vertices in
C_{t}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. Consequently, there are no edges between vertices in
\texttt{V}(P_{sv})
and
\texttt{V}(P_{vt})
. Therefore, the path
P_{sv}P_{vt}
is a chordless
s,t
-path that passes through
v
. In other words, if
v\in S
, then there is an induced
s,t
-path through
v
.

Let
P=s,a_{1},\dots,a_{k},v,b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell},t
denote a simple, chordless
s,t
-path through
v
. If
v\in N_{G}(s)
(
v\in N_{G}(t)
), then
k=0
(
\ell=0
). Contract all edges on the sub-path
P_{a}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}(s,a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
such that
P_{a}
is reduced to an edge
(s,v)
. Likewise, contract all edges on the sub-path
P_{b}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}(b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell},t)
such that
P_{b}
is reduced to an edge
(v,t)
. Denote the resulting graph by
G^{\prime}
. Since
P
is chordless, then there are no edges between
(a_{i},b_{j})
for all
i\in[1,k]
and all
j\in[1,\ell]
. Therefore, following the contraction,
s
and
t
are not adjacent in the new graph
G^{\prime}
, and hence separable.

Let
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G^{\prime})
be a minimal
s,t
-separator in
G^{\prime}
. By construction,
v\in N_{G^{\prime}}(s)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(t)
, and hence
v\in S^{\prime}
. It is left to show that
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
. Let
C_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
and
C_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
denote the full connected components of
G^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S^{\prime}
containing
s
and
t
respectively.
Define
D_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}C_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})\cup\mathord{\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}}
and
D_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}C_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})\cup\mathord{\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}\}}
. By construction,
D_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)
and
D_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)
are disjoint, and
G[D_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)]
(
G[D_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)]
) are both connected components in
G
. Since
C_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
and
C_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
are full components of
S^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
, and
D_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)\supseteq C_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
and
D_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)\supseteq C_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G^{\prime})
, then
D_{s}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)
and
D_{t}(S^{\prime}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}G)
are full components of
S^{\prime}
in
G
. By Lemma
3
,
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{s,t}(G)
.
∎

Theorem
26
provides a characterization of when a vertex
v
is included in a minimal
s,t
-separator. By reduction from the
3-in-a-path
problem we conclude that deciding whether there is a minimal
s,t
-separator containing a subset
I\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
is an NP-complete problem.

Sharp bounds for rainbow matchings in hypergraphs
Cosmin Pohoata, Lisa Sauermann, Dmitrii Zakharov

Abstract
Suppose we are given matchings M 1 , … . , M N M_{1},....,M_{N} of size t t in some r r -uniform hypergraph, and let us think of each matching having a different color. How large does N N need to be (in terms of t t and r r ) such that we can always find a rainbow matching of size t t ? This problem was first introduced by Aharoni and Berger, and has since been studied by several different authors. For example, Alon discovered an intriguing connection with the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv problem from additive combinatorics, which implies certain lower bounds for N N . For any fixed uniformity r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , we answer this problem up to constant factors depending on r r , showing that the answer is on the order of t r t^{r} . Furthermore, for any fixed t t and large r r , we determine the answer up to lower order factors. We also prove analogous results in the setting where the underlying hypergraph is assumed to be r r -partite. Our results settle questions of Alon and of Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó.



1
Introduction

Motivated by classical questions about transversals in Latin Squares such as the famous Ryser–Brualdi–Stein Conjecture, there has been a lot of work on finding rainbow matchings in properly edge-colored graphs (see e.g.
[
10
,
15
]
and the references therein). In a properly edge-colored graph every color class is a matching, so these questions amount to finding a rainbow matching among a collection of matchings of different colors. Similar questions have also been studied in the setting of hypergraphs, which will be the focus of this paper.

A matching in an
r
-uniform hypergraph is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges (and the size of the matching is the number of edges it consists of). Given matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
in some
r
-uniform hypergraph, where we think of each matching as colored in a different color, a
rainbow matching
is a matching consisting of edges
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{\ell}\in M_{i_{\ell}}
with distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
(in other words, a matching consisting of edges with distinct colors).

The following problem goes back to Aharoni and Berger
[
1
]
.

Problem 1.1
.

Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
be matchings of size
t
in some
r
-uniform hypergraph. How large does
N
need to be (in terms of
t
and
r
), such that it is always possible to find a rainbow matching of size
t
? In other words, how large does
N
need to be such that it is always possible to find distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{t}\in M_{i_{t}}
?

We remark that there may be edges belonging to more than one of the matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
, in which case we can think of such edges having more than one color. To form a rainbow matching, one may choose which color to use these edges with.

Equivalently to Problem
1.1
, one may ask about the maximum possible number of matchings of size
t
in some
r
-uniform hypergraph without a rainbow matching of size
t
. Denoting this maximum possible number by
F(r,t)
, the answer to Problem
1.1
is precisely
N=F(r,t)+1
.

It is also natural to ask about Problem
1.1
with the additional restriction that the underlying
r
-uniform hypergraph is
r
-partite. This was in fact the original version of the problem proposed by Aharoni and Berger in

[
1
]
. Let
f(r,t)
be the maximum possible number of matchings of size
t
in some
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph without a rainbow matching of size
t
. We clearly have the inequality
f(r,t)\leq F(r,t)
.

In the case of uniformity
r=2
, i.e. in the case of graphs, Aharoni and Berger
[
1
]
proved that
f(2,t)=2t-2
(relying on previous ideas of Drisko
[
11
]
). For
F(2,t)
the best known bounds are
2t-2=f(2,t)\leq F(2,t)\leq 3t-4
, where the upper bound is due to Aharoni–Briggs–Kim–Kim
[
3
]
(building upon work of Aharoni–Berger–Chudnovsky–Howard–Seymour
[
2
]
showing a slightly weaker bound). In general, Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó
[
14
]
conjectured that
f(r,t)
is upper-bounded by a linear function of
t
for any fixed
r\geq 2
(or stated more formally, that for any fixed
r\geq 2
there is a constant
c(r)
such that
f(r,t)\leq c(r)\cdot t
holds for all
t
). Alon
[
6
]
had also already asked in 2011 whether this is true, based on an intriguing connection with the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv problem
[
12
]
from additive combinatorics. For the fractional version of Problem
1.1
(where
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
are fractional matchings and one is looking for a rainbow fractional matching), such a bound was recently proved by Aharoni, Holzman and Jiang
[
4
]
, using tools from topology.

Nevertheless, it turns out that the conjecture of Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó
[
14
]
is actually false: In this paper, we show that for any fixed uniformity
r\geq 3
, the functions
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
are in fact on the order of
t^{r}
(up to constant factors depending on
r
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For any fixed uniformity
r\geq 3
, there exist positive constants
c_{r}
and
C_{r}
such that

c_{r}t^{r}\leq f(r,t)\leq F(r,t)\leq C_{r}t^{r}

holds for all
t\geq 2
.

Note that for fixed uniformity
r\geq 3
, this theorem determines
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
up to constant factors. In other words, Theorem
1.2
essentially (up to constant factors) solves Problem
1.1
(as well as its
r
-partite analogue) in the setting of fixed uniformity
r\geq 3
.

Theorem
1.2
shows in particular that the behavior of
f(r,t)
is very different from the behavior of Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constants. Indeed, Alon–Dubiner
[
7
]
proved that the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constant
\mathfrak{s}(\mathbb{Z}_{t}^{r})
is linear in
t
for any fixed
r
(and the linearity factor was recently improved by the third author
[
21
]
). Alon
[
6
]
established that
\mathfrak{s}(\mathbb{Z}_{t}^{r-1})\leq f(r,t)
, which in part lead to the conjecture that the function
f(r,t)
as the combinatorial counterpart to the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv constant should also be linear in
t
for fixed uniformity
r
.

The best previous lower bounds for both
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
were linear in
t
. The best previous upper bound for both
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
was
(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
due to Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon
[
20
]
, stated in the following theorem. This upper bound is on the order of
t^{r+1}
for fixed
r
, so Theorem
1.2
improves both the known lower and upper bounds in this regime.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
20
]
)
.

For any
t\geq 2
and
r\geq 2
, we have

f(r,t)\leq F(r,t)\leq(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}.

This upper bound due to Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon
[
20
]
improved upon previous upper bounds of Alon
[
6
]
and Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó
[
14
]
. Their proof of
F(r,t)\leq(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
is of a linear algebraic nature, using exterior power algebras. For such an approach
(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
is an important barrier, because this bound is tight for the natural linear-algebraic abstraction of the problem (see the discussion in Section
4.3
).

Our proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
is purely combinatorial, and is motivated by arguments that first appeared in the context of the famous sunflower problem
[
8
,
19
]
and were later used in the resolution of the fractional Kahn–Kalai conjecture by Frankston–Kahn–Narayanan–Park
[
13
]
. For the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
we give an explicit construction.

It is also interesting to study the functions
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
in the opposite regime, where
t\geq 2
is fixed and
r
is large. For example, Alon
[
6
]
explicitly asked about studying the function
f(r,t)
in the case of
t=3
and large
r
, i.e. about understanding the growth behavior of
f(r,3)
as a function of
r
. Furthermore, for
t=2
, Problem
1.1
is closely related to Bollobás’ celebrated “Two Families Theorem”. So studying this problem for fixed
t\geq 3
, can be viewed as a natural extension of Bollobás’ “Two Families Theorem” to a “
t
Families Theorem”.

For fixed
t\geq 2
and large
r
, the upper bound
(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
for
F(r,t)
due to Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon is (up to constant factors depending on
t
) on the order of
(t^{t}/(t-1)^{t-1})^{r}/\sqrt{r}
, i.e. it is exponential in
r
with base
t^{t}/(t-1)^{t-1}
(which, for relatively large
t
, is roughly
et
). We prove that this upper bound is tight up to sub-exponential factors in
r
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any fixed
t\geq 2
, and any large
r
, we have

F(r,t)\geq\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{r-O(\sqrt{r})}.

Here, the implicit constant in the
O
-notation may depend on
t
.

For
f(r,t)
, i.e. in the setting of
r
-partite graphs, an easy adaptation of the proof of Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon gives the upper bound
f(r,t)\leq(t-1)\cdot t^{r}
. Again, we prove that for fixed
t\geq 2
and large
r
, this is tight up to sub-exponential factors in
r
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any fixed
t\geq 2
, and any
r\geq 2
, we have

t^{r-O(\sqrt{r})}\leq f(r,t)\leq(t-1)\cdot t^{r}.

Again, the implicit constant in the
O
-notation may depend on
t
.

Theorem
1.5
in particular answers a question of Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó
[
14
]
, asking whether
f(r,t)
can be upper-bounded by a function of the form
\alpha_{t}\beta^{r}
(where
\alpha_{t}
may depend on
t
, but
\beta
is an absolute constant). In fact, the best previous lower bounds for both
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
were of this form with
\beta=e\approx 2.71
(to be precise, the best previous lower bound, due to Alon
[
6
]
, was
(e-o(1))^{r-1}
where the
o(1)
-term converges to zero as
t
grows). However, Theorem
1.5
shows that one cannot get such an upper bound with an absolute constant
\beta
.

The lower bounds in Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
can be proved via a probabilistic argument with some ingredients from additive combinatorics. This argument is similar to the proofs of the lower bounds for the so-called multi-colored sum-free theorem in
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
. In fact, to prove Theorem
1.4
, one can use
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
as a black-box, but for the setting of Theorem
1.5
one needs to adapt the strategy and use different arguments to control linear dependencies that affect the relevant probabilistic bounds.

Note that Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
(together with the upper bound
F(r,t)\leq(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
due to Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon) determine the functions
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
for fixed
t\geq 2
up to lower-order terms (i.e. up to sub-exponential terms in
r
).

Organization.
We first prove Theorem
1.2
about fixed uniformity
r
, showing the upper bound in Section
2
and the lower lower bound in Section
3
. Afterwards, we prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
about fixed matching size
t
in Section
4
.

2
Upper bound for fixed uniformity

In this section, we prove the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
be integers. Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
be matchings of size
t
in some
r
-uniform hypergraph, with
N\geq(tr+t)^{r}
. Then there exists a rainbow matching of size
t
(in other words, there exist distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{t}\in M_{i_{t}}
).

This theorem shows that
F(r,t)\leq(tr+t)^{r}
for
r\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
, and therefore implies the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
with
C_{r}=(r+1)^{r}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be the vertex set of the underlying
r
-uniform hypergraph. Now, let
\mathcal{F}=M_{1}\cup\dots\cup M_{N}
be the family of all edges appearing in at least one of the matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
. Note that
\mathcal{F}
is a family of subsets of
V
, each of size
r
.

For a subset
S\subseteq V
and sub-family
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, let
\mathcal{G}(S)=\{X\in\mathcal{G}\mid S\subseteq X\}
denote the family of all edges in
\mathcal{G}
that contain
S
. Then
|\mathcal{G}(S)|
is the number of edges in
\mathcal{G}
containing
S
. Note that
\mathcal{G}(\emptyset)=\mathcal{G}
and in particular
|\mathcal{G}(\emptyset)|=|\mathcal{G}|
. Furthermore note that
|\mathcal{G}(S)|=0
whenever
|S|>r
and
|\mathcal{G}(S)|\leq 1
whenever
|S|=r
.

Let us now define a sequence
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\dots\supset\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
as follows. Define
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}
. Now for some positive integer
k
, assume that we have already defined
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. If
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|\leq(tr+t)^{r}
, we stop the process at this point (setting
\ell=k
). Otherwise, if
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>(tr+t)^{r}
, let us consider a maximal subset
S_{k}\subseteq V
with the property that

|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}},

(2.1)

and let us then define
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}=\mathcal{F}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
(i.e.
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
is obtained from
\mathcal{F}_{k}
by removing all edges containing
S_{k}
).

Let us remark that at every step of this process, there is indeed a subset
S_{k}\subseteq V
satisfying (
2.1
), since
S_{k}=\emptyset
always has this property (and so, among all subsets
S_{k}\subseteq V
satisfying (
2.1
) we can choose a maximal one since
V
is a finite set).

Also note that by (
2.1
) at every step
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
is non-empty and so
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}=\mathcal{F}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. In particular, since
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}
is a finite set, this means that the sequence
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\mathcal{F}_{1}\supset\dots\supset\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
needs to indeed terminate eventually (i.e. the above process must indeed stop at some point after some finite number of steps). By the definition of the process, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\leq(tr+t)^{r}
for the last family in the sequence (and we have
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>(tr+t)^{r}
for
k=1,\dots,\ell-1
).

Recall that whenever
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|\leq(tr+t)^{r}
, the process terminates and we do not need to choose a subset
S_{k}\subseteq V
(and we do not define another family
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
). We claim that whenever
S_{k}
is defined (i.e. whenever
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>(tr+t)^{r}
), we have
|S_{k}|\leq r-1
. Indeed, if
|S_{k}|>r
, then
|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})|=0
while the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is positive, so the inequality in (
2.1
) cannot be satisfied. If
|S_{k}|=r
, then
|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})|\leq 1
while the right-hand side of (
2.1
) is larger than
1
(since
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>(tr+t)^{r}
), so again (
2.1
) cannot be satisfied. This shows that indeed all the sets
S_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,\ell-1
occurring in this process satisfy
|S_{k}|\leq r-1
.

Recall that
|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\leq(tr+t)^{r}\leq N
. Let us imagine that every edge in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
is given one dollar (then the total amount of money is
|\mathcal{F}_{\ell}|\leq N
dollars). Every edge in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
is contained in at least one of the matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
. Now, imagine that every edge
e\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
gives its one dollar in equal shares to all the indices
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
which satisfy
e\in M_{i}
(if there are
z
such indices, then
e
gives each of them
1/z
dollars). In other words, if we imagine the matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
as colors, each edge gives its one dollar in equal shares to all of its colors.

Since the total amount of money is at most
N
dollars, there must be an index
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
receiving at most one dollar. Let us fix such an index
j
.

Recall that
M_{j}
is a matching of size
t
. Let
m=\left|M_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\right|
be the number of edges of
M_{j}
that are part of the family
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
(these are the edges contributing money to the index
j
in the process described above), then
0\leq m\leq t
. Let us denote the
t
edges in
M_{j}
by
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
in such an order that we have
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
and
e^{*}_{m+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}\not\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
.

Our goal is now to find a rainbow matching of size
t
by adapting the matching
M_{j}
in a suitable way. First, we use the following claim for the first
m
edges
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{m}
of
M_{j}
.

Claim 2.2
.

There exists
m
distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
e_{1}^{*}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{m}^{*}\in M_{i_{m}}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the bipartite graph with vertices labeled by
e_{1}^{*},\dots,e_{m}^{*}
on the left side and vertices labeled by
1,\dots,N
on the right side, where we draw an edge between vertex
e_{h}^{*}
on the left and vertex
i
on the right (for
1\leq h\leq m
and
1\leq i\leq N
) if and only if
e_{h}^{*}\in M_{i}
. Note that vertex
j
on the right side is adjacent to all vertices on the left side (since
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{m}\in M_{j}
by definition). The claim asserts that this bipartite graph has a matching of size
m
(i.e. a matching that covers all the vertices
e_{1}^{*},\dots,e_{m}^{*}
on the left side).

In order to obtain such a matching in this auxiliary bipartite graph, by Hall’s marriage theorem it suffices to check that for any
1\leq a\leq m
, and any subset of
a
vertices on the left side, the union of their neighborhoods on the right side has size at least
a
. So suppose there exist
a
vertices on the left side, such that the union of their neighborhoods on the right side has size at most
a-1
.

Let us consider the
a
dollars that these
a
vertices on the left side obtained (recall that
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{m}\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
, so every vertex on the left side of this bipartite graph obtained a dollar). Each of these vertices gives its dollar in equal shares to its neighbors
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
on the right side of this bipartite graph (since for every vertex
e_{h}^{*}
on the left side its neighbors on the right side are precisely those indices
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
e_{h}^{*}\in M_{i}
). By our assumption, at most
a-1
vertices are receiving some share of these
a
dollars, so one vertex
j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
on the left side must receive a share of more than one dollar among these
a
dollars. On the other hand, all vertices on the left side are adjacent to vertex
j
on the right side, so each of the
a
vertices we considered on the left side gives at least as much money to index
j
as to index
j^{\prime}
. Thus,
j
must also receive a share of more than one dollar among the
a
dollars we considered. However, by our choice of
j
, the total amount of money that
j
receives is at most one dollar, so this is a contradiction.
∎

Claim
2.2
states that we can assign distinct colors to the first
m
edges
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{m}
of
M_{j}
(i.e. to the edges of
m_{j}
that are contained in
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
). We will now use the following claim to modify the remaining edges
e^{*}_{m+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
in such a way that we obtain a rainbow matching.

Claim 2.3
.

For each
h\in\{m,\ldots,t\}
, there exist distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{h}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and edges
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{h}\in M_{i_{h}}
such that the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{h},e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
are pairwise disjoint (in other words, the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{h},e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
form a matching of size
t
).

Note that for
h=t
, this claim states precisely that there is a rainbow matching of size
t
among our original matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{t}
. Thus, showing this claim concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction on
h
. For
h=m
, the statement follows from Claim
2.2
. Indeed, we can take distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
as in Claim
2.2
and define
e_{a}=e^{*}_{a}
for
a=1,\dots,m
. Then we have
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{m}\in M_{i_{m}}
and the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m},e^{*}_{m+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
are pairwise disjoint (since they are precisely the edges
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
of the matching
M_{j}
).

So let us now assume that
m+1\leq h\leq t
, and that we already proved Claim
2.3
for
h-1
. This means that there exist distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{h-1}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and edges
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{h-1}\in M_{i_{h-1}}
such that the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1},e^{*}_{h},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
are pairwise disjoint.

Recall that
e^{*}_{h}\not\in\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
(since
h\geq m+1
). As
e^{*}_{h}\in M_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
, there must be an index
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
such that
e_{h}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
. Recalling that we defined
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}=\mathcal{F}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
, this means that
e_{h}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
, so
S_{k}\subseteq e_{h}^{*}
. In particular, this means that
S_{k}
is disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
.

Recall from (
2.1
) that

|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}.

On the other hand,
S_{k}
was chosen to be a maximal subset of
V
satisfying (
2.1
). Hence, for every vertex
v\in e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{h-1}\cup e^{*}_{h+1}\cup\dots\cup e^{*}_{t}
, we have

|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k}\cup\{v\})|<\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|+1}}.

Therefore the total number of edges in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that contain at least one vertex in the union
e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{h-1}\cup e^{*}_{h+1}\cup\dots\cup e^{*}_{t}
is at most

\sum_{v}|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k}\cup\{v\})|<tr\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|+1}}=\frac{r}{r+1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}

(where the sum on the left-hand side is over all
v\in e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{h-1}\cup e^{*}_{h+1}\cup\dots\cup e^{*}_{t}
, noting that
|e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{h-1}\cup e^{*}_{h+1}\cup\dots\cup e^{*}_{t}|=(t-1)r\leq tr
).
Thus, the family
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
contains at least

|\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})|-\frac{r}{r+1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}-\frac{r}{r+1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}=\frac{1}{r+1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{k}|}{(tr+t)^{|S_{k}|}}>\frac{1}{r+1}\cdot(tr+t)^{r-|S_{k}|}

edges that are disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
(in the last step, we used that
|\mathcal{F}_{k}|>(tr+t)^{r}
since
k<\ell
). We claim that these edges cannot all be contained in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
.

Indeed, if
S_{k}\neq\emptyset
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
contains at most one edge from each of the matchings
M_{i_{1}},\dots,M_{i_{h-1}}
(since the edges in each matching are pairwise disjoint) and therefore at most
h-1<t
edges in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
. However, the number of edges in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that are disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
is at least
(tr+t)^{r-|S_{k}|}/(r+1)\geq t
(as
|S_{k}|\leq r-1
). So there must indeed be at least one edge in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that is disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
and is not contained in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
.

In the other case, where
S_{k}=\emptyset
, the number of edges in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that are disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
is at least
(tr+t)^{r-|S_{k}|}/(r+1)\geq(r+1)t^{2}
(as
r-|S_{k}|=r\geq 2
). On the other hand, the number of edges in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
is at most
(h-1)t<t^{2}
, so again there must be at least one edge in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that is disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
and is not contained in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
.

In either case, let
e_{h}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
be such an edge in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})
that is disjoint from
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1}
and
e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
and not contained in
M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
. Then, as desired, the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1},e_{h},e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
are pairwise disjoint (recall that
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1},e^{*}_{h+1},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
must be pairwise disjoint since we started with pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{h-1},e^{*}_{h},\dots,e^{*}_{t}
). Since
e_{h}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(S_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{F}=M_{1}\cup\dots\cup M_{N}
and
e_{h}\not\in M_{i_{1}}\cup\dots\cup M_{i_{h-1}}
, there must be an index
i_{h}\in\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{i_{1},\dots,i_{h-1}\}
with
e_{h}\in M_{i_{h}}
. Now
i_{1},\dots,i_{h}
are distinct, and we have
e_{1}\in M_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{h}\in M_{i_{h}}
as desired. This finishes the induction step.
∎

We already saw that Claim
2.3
finishes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.∎

We remark that the condition in (

) is motivated by the concept of “spread” that first appeared in the context of the famous sunflower problem

).

In this section, we prove Theorems

4.1
Proof of Theorem

We can deduce Theorem

from the following proposition, which is a special case of a proposition due to Lovász and the second author
[

]
(to be precise, it corresponds to the case

in

[

in the case of

below, while the proof of the more general proposition in

takes up around 40 pages).

Proposition 4.1
.

For every fixed
t\geq 3
, there exists a constant
C_{t}\geq 0
such that the following holds. For every positive number
n
which is divisible by
t
, there exists a collection of
t
-tuples
(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{t,j})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\times\dots\times\{0,1\}^{n}
for
j=1,\dots,N
with

N\geq\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{n/t-C_{t}\sqrt{n}}

such that the following conditions hold:

•

For all
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we have

x_{1,j_{1}}+\dots+x_{t,j_{t}}=\mathbbm{1}^{n}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad j_{1}=j_{2}=\dots=j_{t}.

•

For all
i=1,\dots,t
and
j=1,\dots,N
, the vector
x_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
consists of precisely
n/t
ones.

Here,
\mathbbm{1}^{n}
denotes the all-ones vector. Let us remark that the lower bound for
N
in
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
is written as
N\geq\Gamma_{2,t}^{n-O(\sqrt{n})}
(where the implicit constant in the
O
-notation may depend on

), but by the definition of
\Gamma_{2,t}
from
[

, pp. 1]
we have

\Gamma_{2,t}=\min_{0<\gamma<1}\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma^{1/t}}=\frac{1+1/(t-1)}{(t-1)^{-1/t}}=\frac{t}{(t-1)^{(t-1)/t}}

and hence we indeed obtain

N\geq\Gamma_{2,t}^{n-O(\sqrt{n})}=\left(\frac{t}{(t-1)^{(t-1)/t}}\right)^{n-O(\sqrt{n})}=\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{n/t-O(\sqrt{n})}

as stated above.

Let us now deduce Theorem
1.4
from Proposition
4.1
. In fact, this deduction gives the following stronger statement, which immediately implies Theorem

.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every fixed

, there exists a constant

such that the following holds. For any

, there exist matchings

of size

in some

and such that for any pairwise disjoint edges

(for some indices

) we must have

.

Note that in order for

not to have a rainbow matching (which suffices for deducing Theorem

), it would already be sufficient to have “at least two of the indices

must agree” instead of “we must have

” at the end of the statement. In this sense, Theorem

is significantly stronger than Theorem

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

First, let us consider the case that

, let us consider a complete

-uniform hypergraph on

vertices. The edges of this hypergraph can be partitioned into

matchings of size
2
(one matching for each of the ways to split the vertex set into two subsets of size
r
). These matchings satisfy the conditions in Theorem
4.2
, since any two disjoint edges indeed belong to the same matching and since
N=\binom{2r}{r}/2\geq 2^{2r}/(4r+2)\geq 4^{r}/(4\sqrt{r})^{2}\geq 4^{r}/(2^{4\sqrt{r}})^{2}=(2^{2}/1^{1})^{r-4\sqrt{r}}
. Thus, the statement in Theorem
4.2
for
t=2
holds with
D_{2}=4
.

Let us now assume that
t\geq 3
, and define
D_{t}=C_{t}\cdot\sqrt{t}+1
for the constant
C_{t}\geq 0
in Proposition
4.1
. Let us apply Proposition
4.1
with
n=t(r-1)
. We obtain a a collection of
t
-tuples
(x_{1,j},x_{2,j},\dots,x_{t,j})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\times\dots\times\{0,1\}^{n}
for
j=1,\dots,N
with

N\geq\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{n/t-C_{t}\sqrt{n}}\geq\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{r-1-C_{t}\sqrt{tr}}\geq\left(\frac{t^{t}}{(t-1)^{t-1}}\right)^{r-D_{t}\sqrt{r}}

satisfying the conditions in Proposition
4.1
.

To construct the desired matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
, let us consider the complete
r
-uniform hypergraph on
tr
vertices labeled by
1,\dots,n
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}
(recall that
n=t(r-1)
). For
j=1,\dots,N
, let us define a matching
M_{j}
of size
t
as follows. By the second condition in Proposition
4.1
, for
i=1,\dots,t
the vector
x_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
consists of precisely
n/t=r-1
ones, so it corresponds to a subset
X_{i,j}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
of size
r-1
. Now, let the matching
M_{j}
consist of the
t
edges
\{a_{i}\}\cup X_{i,j}
for
i=1,\dots,t
.

Each of these edges has size
r
, so in order to check that
M_{j}
is indeed a matching, it suffices to check that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}(\{a_{i}\}\cup X_{i,j})=\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
. Indeed, by the first condition in Proposition
4.1
we have
x_{1,j}+\dots+x_{t,j}=\mathbbm{1}^{n}
and hence
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}X_{i,j}=\{1,\dots,n\}
. So we indeed obtain
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}(\{a_{i}\}\cup X_{i,j})=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}X_{i,j}=\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
and
M_{j}
is a matching for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

It remains to show that for any pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1}\in M_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{t}\in M_{j_{t}}
(for some indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
) we must have
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}
. Each edge contains precisely one element from
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
, and the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
must contain distinct elements from
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
since they are pairwise disjoint. Upon relabeling, we may assume that
e_{i}
contains
a_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. Then for
i=1,\dots,t
, we have
e_{i}=\{a_{i}\}\cup X_{i,j_{i}}
, where
X_{i,j_{i}}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
is the subset (of size
r-1
) corresponding to ones the vector
x_{i,j_{i}}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Since
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
are
t
pairwise disjoint subsets of size
r
of the vertex set of
\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
of size
tr
, we have
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}(\{a_{i}\}\cup X_{i,j_{i}})=e_{1}\cup\dots\cup e_{t}=\{1,\dots,n\}\cup\{a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\}
. Thus,
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}X_{i,j_{i}}=\{1,\dots,n\}
and therefore (as the sets
X_{i,j_{i}}\subseteq e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
are pairwise disjoint) we have
x_{1,j_{1}}+\dots+x_{t,j_{t}}=\mathbbm{1}^{n}
. But by the first condition in Proposition
4.1
this implies that
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}
, as desired.
∎

4.2
Proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.5

In this subsection, we prove the lower bound in Theorem
1.5
for
r
-partite hypergraphs. Again, we prove a significantly stronger statement (which immediately implies the lower bound in Theorem
1.5
):

Theorem 4.3
.

For every fixed
t\geq 2
, there exists a constant
D_{t}\geq 0
such that the following holds. For any
r\geq 2
, there exist matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
of size
t
in some
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph with
tr
vertices, where

N\geq t^{r-D_{t}\sqrt{r}}

and such that for any pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1}\in M_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{t}\in M_{j_{t}}
(for some indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
) we must have
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}
.

We prove this theorem with a very similar strategy as in the proof of Lovász and the second author
[
18
]
for what is stated as Proposition
4.1
above, which is in turn very similar to the proof of Kleinberg–Sawin–Speyer
[
16
]
in the case of
t=3
.

In the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we will use the following lemma. This lemma appears as
[
18
, Lemma 3.4]
and its proof relies on a modification of Behrend’s construction
[
9
]
due to Alon
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every integer
t\geq 3
and every prime
P\geq t
, there is a collection of
t
-tuples
(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})\in\mathbb{F}_{P}\times\dots\times\mathbb{F}_{P}
for
h=1,\dots,R
with
R\geq P\cdot\exp(-12\sqrt{\ln P\ln t})

such that for all
h_{1},\dots,h_{t}\in\{1,\dots,R\}
we have

y_{1,h_{1}}+\dots+y_{t,h_{t}}=0\text{ in }\mathbb{F}_{P}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad h_{1}=h_{2}=\dots=h_{t}.

(4.1)

Note that the conditions in this lemma in particular imply that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
the vectors
y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,R}
are distinct (indeed, if
y_{i,h}=y_{i,h^{\prime}}
for
h\neq h^{\prime}
, then we have
y_{1,h}+\dots+y_{i-1,h}+y_{i,h^{\prime}}+y_{i+1,h}+\dots+y_{t,h}=y_{1,h}+\dots+y_{t,h}=0
contradicting the condition).

In order to prove Theorem
4.3
, we will use a randomized procedure to sample certain matchings in a complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph with
t
vertices in each part. This procedure will use a collection of
t
-tuples
(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
as in Lemma
4.4
, for a suitably chosen prime
P
. To describe the procedure, we make the following definitions.

Setting 4.5
.

Let
t\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
be integers. Fix a prime
P
such that

2t^{t+1}\cdot(t-1)!^{(r-1)/(t-2)}\leq P\leq 4t^{t+1}\cdot(t-1)!^{(r-1)/(t-2)},

(4.2)

and a collection of
t
-tuples
(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})\in\mathbb{F}_{P}\times\dots\times\mathbb{F}_{P}
for
h=1,\dots,R
as in Lemma
4.4
. For
i=1,\dots,t
, define
Y_{i}=\{y_{i,h}\mid 1\leq h\leq R\}
.

Furthermore, let
Z\subseteq\{0,1\}^{tr}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
be the set of vectors
z\in\{0,1\}^{tr}
such that the restriction of
z
to each of the index subsets
\{1,\dots,t\},\{t+1,\dots,2t\},\dots,\{t(r-1)+1,\dots,tr\}
has exactly one
1
-entry. Consider the partition
Z=Z_{1}\cup\dots\cup Z_{t}
given by defining
Z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
to be the set of vectors
z\in Z
such that the
i
-th entry of
z
is
1
.

Now, let
f:\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}\to\mathbb{F}_{P}
a uniformly random
\mathbb{F}_{P}
-linear map with the condition that
f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr})=0
.

Let us say that a
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
is a
candidate
t
-tuple
if
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
f(z_{i})\in Y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. Let us sat that a candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
is
isolated
if there is no other candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
such that
z_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
.

We remark that for any
t\geq 3
, finding a prime
P
satisfying (
4.2
) is possible by Bertrand’s postulate. Note that the notions of a candidate
t
-tuple and an isolated candidate
t
-tuple depend on the outcome of the random function
f
(since the sets
Z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Z_{t}^{\prime}
depend on the outcome of
f
).

To motivate this setup, we start with a few simple observations. First, note that the vectors in
Z
are in correspondence to the edges of a complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph with
t
vertices in each part. Indeed, let us consider a complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
\{1,\dots,tr\}
, partitioned into the
r
parts
\{1,\dots,t\},\allowbreak\{t+1,\dots,2t\},\dots,\{t(r-1)+1,\dots,tr\}
, each of size
t
. The indicator vectors of the edges of this hypergraph are precisely the vectors in
Z
. To see this, note that the elements of
Z\subseteq\{0,1\}^{tr}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
are precisely those vectors with exactly
r
ones and
tr-r
zeroes, which have exactly one
1
-entry among the first
t
indices (i.e. among the index set
\{1,\dots,t\}
), exactly one
1
-entry among the next
t
indices (i.e. among the index set
\{t+1,\dots,2t\}
), and so on.

Now, a matching of size
t
in this hypergraph corresponds to a collection
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{t}\}\subseteq Z
of
t
vectors in
Z
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
. Note that here it does not matter whether the equation
z_{+}\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is considered over
\mathbb{F}_{P}
or over the reals. Indeed, each of the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{tr}
consists of precisely
r
ones, and so the
t
vectors sum to
\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
over
\mathbb{F}_{P}
(or over the reals) if and only if their supports are a partition of
\{1,\dots,tr\}
.

For any
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{t}\}\subseteq Z
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
(i.e. for any matching of size
t
in the hypergraph), each of the (pairwise disjoint) sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t}
must contain precisely one of the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
. Thus, upon relabeling the indices, each matching of size
t
in the hypergraph corresponds to a
t
-tuple of vectors
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
. To obtain the desired matchings in Theorem
4.3
, we will consider the matchings corresponding to isolated candidate
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
. It is therefore important to show that (at least for some outcome of the random map
f
) there are many isolated candidate
t
-tuples.

The following lemma gives a lower bound for the probability that a given
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is an isolated candidate
t
-tuple. Proving this lemma is the hardest part of the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Consider Setting
4.5
. Suppose that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
satisfies
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
. Then the probability that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is an isolated candidate
t
-tuple is at least
R/(2P^{t-1})
.

Before proving this lemma, let us first show how it can be used to prove Theorem
4.3
.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
assuming Lemma
4.6
.

Let us consider the vertex set
\{1,\dots,tr\}
, partitioned into the
r
parts
\{1,\dots,t\},\allowbreak\{t+1,\dots,2t\},\dots,\{t(r-1)+1,\dots,tr\}
, each of size
t
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the the complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph on the vertex set
\{1,\dots,tr\}
with this
r
-partition.

Let us first treat the case
t=2
. Then
\mathcal{H}
has
2^{r}
edges. These edges can be divided into
N=2^{r}/2
matchings of size
2
(each consisting of an edge and its complement). These matchings satisfy the conditions in Theorem
4.3
, since any two disjoint edges indeed belong to the same matching and since
N=2^{r}/2\geq 2^{r-\sqrt{r}}
. Thus, the statement in Theorem
4.3
for
t=2
holds with
D_{2}=1
.

So let us from now on assume that
t\geq 3
, and consider Setting
4.5
. Recall from the above discussion that the edges of the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
correspond precisely to the vectors in
Z
and that the matchings of size
t
in
\mathcal{H}
correspond precisely to the
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
.

Note that
|Z|=t^{r}
and
|Z_{1}|=|Z_{2}|=\dots=|Z_{t}|=t^{r-1}
. Furthermore, the number of
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is precisely
t!^{r-1}
(indeed, this is the total number of matchings
(e_{1},\dots,e_{t})
in the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
such that
i\in e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
, since for each part the
r
-partition of the vertex set of
\mathcal{H}
except the first part there are exactly
t!
possibilities how the
t
elements of the part are distributed between
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
).

By Lemma
4.6
, each of these
t!^{r-1}
different
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is an isolated candidate
t
-tuple with probability at least
R/(2P^{t-1})
. Thus, the expected number of isolated candidate
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
is at least

t!^{r-1}\cdot\frac{R}{2P^{t-1}}.

Note that we have
(t-1)!=2\dotsm(t-1)\leq t^{t-2}
and hence
P\leq 4t^{t+1}\cdot(t-1)!^{(r-1)/(t-2)}\leq t^{2t+1}\cdot t^{r-1}=t^{2t+r}
, so

R\geq P\cdot\exp\Big(-12\sqrt{\ln P\ln t}\Big)\geq P\cdot\exp\Big(-12\sqrt{2t+r}\cdot\ln t\Big)\geq\frac{P}{t^{24t+12\sqrt{r}}}.

Thus, choosing
D_{t}=t^{2}+26t+13
, the expected number of isolated candidate
t
-tuples in
Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
is at least

t!^{r-1}\cdot\frac{R}{2P^{t-1}}\geq t!^{r-1}\cdot\frac{1}{2P^{t-2}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{24t+12\sqrt{r}}}\geq\frac{t!^{r-1}}{(t-1)!^{r-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{(4t^{t+1})^{t-2}\cdot 2t^{24t+12\sqrt{r}}}\geq t^{r-1}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{t^{2}+26t+12\sqrt{r}}}\geq t^{r-D_{t}\sqrt{r}},

where for the second inequality we used the upper bound for
P
in (
4.2
). So let us fix an outcome of the random map
f
(see Setting
4.5
) such that there are at least
t^{r-D_{t}\sqrt{r}}
isolated candidate
t
-tuples.

Each isolated candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
satisfies
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
, and so it corresponds to a matching of size
t
in
\mathcal{H}
. Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
be the resulting matchings of size
t
in the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, where
N\geq t^{r-D_{t}\sqrt{r}}
is the number of isolated candidate
t
-tuples. It remains to show that the condition at the end of the statement of Theorem
4.3
holds. This is the content of the following claim.

Claim 4.7
.

For any pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1}\in M_{j_{1}},\dots,e_{t}\in M_{j_{t}}
(for some indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
), we must have
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}

Proof.

Since the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
are pairwise disjoint and each of them contains exactly one vertex from the first part
\{1,\dots,t\}
of the
r
-partition of
\mathcal{H}
, upon relabeling we may assume that for
i=1,\dots,t
we have
i\in e_{i}
. Let
z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime}\in Z\subseteq\{0,1\}^{tr}
be the vectors corresponding to
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
, then we have
z_{i}^{\prime}\in Z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. In other words, we have
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots,\times Z_{t}
. Since
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
form a matching, we furthermore have
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
.

We claim that
f(z_{i}^{\prime})\in Y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. Indeed, consider some
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
and recall that
z_{i}^{\prime}
corresponds to the edge
e_{i}\in M_{j_{i}}
. The matching
M_{j_{i}}
corresponds to some isolated candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
, and, since
e_{i}
is an edge of
M_{j_{i}}
, one of the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
must be equal to
z_{i}^{\prime}
. Because
z_{i}^{\prime}\in Z_{i}
(and
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t}
are pairwise disjoint), we must have
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
. So we can conclude that
f(z_{i}^{\prime})=f(z_{i})\in Y_{i}
(as
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple).

Now we established that
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots,\times Z_{t}
satisfies
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
f(z_{i}^{\prime})\in Y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. This means that
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
is a candidate
t
-tuple.

We claim that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
the isolated candidate
t
-tuple corresponding to the matching
M_{j_{i}}
must be precisely
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
. Indeed, consider some
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
and let
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
be the isolated candidate
t
-tuple corresponding to
M_{j_{i}}
. Recalling that
e_{i}
is one of the edges of
M_{j_{i}}
and that
e_{i}
corresponds to the vector
z_{i}^{\prime}
, we can see that
z_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}
. If we had
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\neq(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
, then the candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
would not be isolated (since
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
is a candidate
t
-tuple satisfying
z_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}
). Hence we must have
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})=(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
, and the isolated candidate
t
-tuple corresponding to the matching
M_{j_{i}}
is indeed
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
.

We proved that all of the matchings
M_{j_{1}},\dots,M_{j_{t}}
correspond to the same isolated candidate
t
-tuple, namely to
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
. This means that the matchings
M_{j_{1}},\dots,M_{j_{t}}
are all equal and we have
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}
.
∎

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

It remains to prove Lemma
4.6
, which is the aim of the remainder of this subsection. We adopt the definitions and conventions in Setting
4.5
throughout the rest of this subsection.

We prepare the proof of Lemma
4.6
with a series of claims.

Claim 4.8
.

Suppose
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
are vectors such that
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}
and
\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{P}
. Then for the random linear map
f:\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}\to\mathbb{F}_{P}
as above (with the condition that
f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr})=0
), the images
f(x_{1}),\dots,f(x_{\ell})
are independent uniformly random elements of
\mathbb{F}_{P}
.

Proof.

We can extend
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell},\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
to some basis of
\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
, and model the choice of the linear map
f:\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}\to\mathbb{F}_{P}
by choosing independent uniformly random images in
\mathbb{F}_{P}
for all basis vectors except
\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
(for
\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
, we have to choose
f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr})=0
). With this description for the map
f
, the claim is immediate.
∎

Our next claim gives a helpful characterization of candidate
t
-tuples in terms of the
t
-tuples
(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})\in\mathbb{F}_{P}\times\dots\times\mathbb{F}_{P}
from Lemma
4.4
.

Claim 4.9
.

A
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
satisfying
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is a candidate
t
-tuple if and only if there is some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
.

Proof.

Since we already assumed that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
and
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
, the
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple if and only if we have
f(z_{i})\in Y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
.

If
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
for some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
, then we indeed have
f(z_{i})=y_{i,h}\in Y_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,t
(recalling the definition of
Y_{i}
in Setting
4.5
).

For the reverse direction, assume that
f(z_{i})\in Y_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
. This means that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
, there is an index
h_{i}\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
f(z_{i})=y_{i,h_{i}}
. We now have
y_{1,h_{1}}+\dots+y_{t,h_{t}}=f(z_{1})+\dots+f(z_{t})=f(z_{1}+\dots+z_{t})=f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr})=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{P}
. By the condition in Lemma
4.4
this means that
h_{1}=\dots=h_{t}
. So there exists some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
f(z_{i})=y_{i,h}
for
h=1,\dots,t
, meaning that
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
.
∎

By combining the two previous claims, we can determine the probability that a given
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
is a candidate
t
-tuple.

Claim 4.10
.

Suppose that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
satisfies
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
. Then the probability that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple is precisely
R/P^{t-1}
.

Proof.

First, note that the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t-1},z_{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
, are linearly independent. Indeed, when restricting the index set to
\{1,\dots,t\}
, these vectors are the standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{P}^{t}
. Since
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
, this implies that the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t-1}
and
\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{P}
. Hence, by Claim
4.8
, the images
f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t-1})
are independent uniformly random elements of
\mathbb{F}_{P}
.

By Claim
4.9

(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple if and only if we have
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
for some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
.

We claim that for any
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
, having
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
is equivalent to having
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t-1}))=(y_{1,h},\dots,y_{t-1,h})
.
Indeed, if
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t-1}))=(y_{1,h},\dots,y_{t-1,h})
for some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
, then (using that
y_{1,h}+\dots+y_{t,h}=0
by the condition in Lemma
4.4
) we also have

f(z_{t})=f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr}-z_{1}-\dots-z_{t-1})=f(\mathbbm{1}^{tr})-f(z_{1})-\dots-f(z_{t-1})=0-y_{1,h}-\dots-y_{t-1,h}=y_{t,h},

and hence
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
.

Recalling that the
t
-tuples
(y_{1,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
for
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
are distinct (see the condition in Lemma
4.4
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\Pr[(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\text{ is a candidate $t$-tuple}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{R}\Pr[(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},\dots,y_{t,h})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{R}\Pr[(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t-1}))=(y_{1,h},\dots,y_{t-1,h})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{R}\Pr[f(z_{1})=y_{1,h}]\dotsm\Pr[f(z_{t-1})=y_{t-1,h}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{R}1/P^{t-1}=R/P^{t-1},

where in the third step we used that the images
f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t-1})
are probabilistically independent, and in the fourth step we used that these images are uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{P}
.
∎

In order to deduce Lemma
4.6
from Claim
4.10
we need to give an upper bound for the probability that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple but not isolated. This happens precisely when there is another candidate
t
-tuple
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
with
z_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
. The next claim bounds the probability that this happens for any given
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
.

Claim 4.11
.

Suppose that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
are
t
-tuples in
Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
satisfying
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
, such that
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
. Let
d=\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
. Then the probability that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
are both candidate
t
-tuples is at most
R/P^{d-1}
.

Here,
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
denotes the span of
z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime}
, interpreted as vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
(so,
d
is the dimension of this span). We remark that in the above claim the probability that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
are both candidate
t
-tuples is actually equal to
R/P^{d-1}
, but we will only need the upper bound for this probability as stated in the claim.

Proof of Claim
4.11
.

If
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
are candidate
t
-tuples, then by Claim
4.9
there are
h,h^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
(f(z_{1}),\dots,f(z_{t}))=(y_{1,h},y_{2,h},\dots,y_{t,h})
and
(f(z_{1}^{\prime}),\dots,f(z_{t}^{\prime}))=(y_{1,h^{\prime}},y_{2,h^{\prime}},\dots,y_{t,h^{\prime}})
. However, recall that for some

we have

and therefore
y_{i,h}=f(z_{i})=f(z_{i}^{\prime})=y_{i,h^{\prime}}
. Hence, since
y_{i,1},\dots,y_{i,R}
are distinct (see the comment below Lemma
4.4
), we must have
h=h^{\prime}
.

Thus, if
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
and
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
are candidate
t
-tuples, then there must be some
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
f(z_{i})=f(z_{i}^{\prime})=y_{i,h}
for all

. So it suffices to show that for every
h\in\{1,\dots,R\}
with probability at most
1/P^{d-1}
we have
f(z_{i})=f(z_{i}^{\prime})=y_{i,h}
for all
i=1,\dots,t
. So let us fix some

.

Since the span of the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}_{P}^{tr}
has dimension

, we can choose

such that these

are linearly independent. Then by Claim
4.8
the images of these

vectors under the map

are independent uniformly random elements of

. Thus, the probability of having the desired images

or

for all of these

. Hence the probability of having
f(z_{i})=f(z_{i}^{\prime})=y_{i,h}
for all

is at most
1/P^{d-1}
, as desired.
∎

Our next claim gives some bounds for the dimension

occurring in Claim

.

Claim 4.12
.

Consider
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t}),(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}

. If
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\neq(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
, then we have
t+1\leq d\leq 2t-1

Proof.

The upper bound
d=\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\leq 2t-1
follows from the fact that we have
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}=z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}
, so
z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime}
cannot be linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{P}
.

For the lower bound
d\geq t+1
, recall when restricting the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
to the first
t
coordinates (i.e. when restricting the index set to
\{1,\dots,t\}
), these vectors become the standard basis vectors (this follows from the definition of the sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{t}
and the fact that
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
). In particular, the vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
are linearly independent, and we have
\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})=t
.

Furthermore, since
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\neq(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
, there must be an index
i
such that
z_{i}^{\prime}\neq z_{i}
. Suppose we could express
z_{i}^{\prime}
as a linear combination of
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
. Looking at the first
t
coordinates, the vector
z_{i}^{\prime}\in Z_{i}
also restricts to the
i
-th standard basis vector, so this linear combination would need to be
z_{i}^{\prime}=0\cdot z_{1}+\dots+0\cdot z_{i-1}+1\cdot z_{i}+0\cdot z_{i+1}+\dots+0\cdot z_{t}
. But this means that
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction. Hence
z_{i}^{\prime}
cannot be expressed as a linear combination of
z_{1},\dots,z_{t}
, which implies that
d\geq\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{i}^{\prime})\geq\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})+1=t+1
.
∎

In order to bound the probability that a given
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
is a candidate
t
-tuple but not isolated, we need to sum up the probabilities in Claim
4.11
for all the relevant

-tuples
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
(i.e. all
t
-tuples
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
for some

). Recall that the relevant probabilities in the sum depend on
d=\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
. So, given
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})
, we need to bound the number of such
t
-tuples
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
for any given value of
d=\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})
. Such a bound is stated in the following claim, which is the last missing ingredient in order to prove Lemma
4.6
.

Claim 4.13
.

Consider a
t
-tuple
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}+\dots+z_{t}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and an integer
d
with
t+1\leq d\leq 2t-1
. Then the number of
t
-tuples
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
for some

, which satisfy

t^{t}\cdot(t-1)!^{(r-1)(d-t)/(t-2)}

For the proof of this claim, we first need a series of further claims as preparations. We will bound the number of
t
-tuples
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
in the claim by looking at all possibilities for the component structure of the auxiliary bipartite graph

in the following definition.

Definition 4.14
.

For
t
-tuples

with

) and
t

) as follows. For

let us draw an edge between vertex

on the left and vertex
j
on the right if the vectors
z_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{tr}
and
z_{j}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{tr}

-entry in a common position (i.e. if in some position both

and

Note for any

z_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{tr}
and

Claim 4.15
.

For any
t
-tuples
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t}),(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with

We remark that one can show that the number of connected components of

Proof of Claim
4.15
.

Let us consider the linear subspace of

consisting of all
(a_{1},\dots,a_{t},a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{t}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}_{P}^{2t}

j

such that both the vectors
z_{i}
and
z_{j}^{\prime}
have a
1
-entry in the

-th position. Since

have a zero in the
s
-th position. Hence in order for

in
\mathbb{F}_{P}
.

Thus, for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{t},a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{t}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}_{P}^{2t}

, the entries in

On the other hand, by the rank-nullity theorem, the dimension of the subspace of all

Claim 4.16
.

For every given

(|I_{1}|!\dotsm|I_{\ell}|!)^{r-1}
possibilities for choosing
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
as in the claim statement.
∎

In order to bound the expression

occurring in Claim

, we use the following simple observation.

Claim 4.17
.

For integers
1\leq a\leq b
with
b\geq 2
, we have
a!\leq b!^{(a-1)/(b-1)}
.

Proof.

For
a=1
, note that
1!=1=b!^{(1-1)/(b-1)}
, and for
a=b
note that
a!=a!^{(a-1)/(a-1)}
. For
2\leq a\leq b-1
, we have

a!^{b-1}=(2\dotsm a)^{b-1}=(2\dotsm a)^{a-1}\cdot(2\dotsm a)^{b-a}\leq(2\dotsm a)^{a-1}\cdot((a+1)\dotsm b)^{a-1}=b!^{a-1},

where the inequality holds because
(2\dotsm a)^{b-a}
is a product of
(a-1)(b-a)
factors in
\{2,\dots,a\}
while
((a+1)\dotsm b)^{a-1}
is a product of
(a-1)(b-a)
factors in
\{a+1,\dots,b\}
. Now, rearranging gives the desired inequality.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Claim

.

Proof of Claim
4.13
.

For any
t
-tuple
(z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}
with
z_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+z_{t}^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}^{tr}
and
z_{i}=z_{i}^{\prime}
for some

, such that
\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{F}_{P}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{t},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{t}^{\prime})=d
, let us consider the bipartite graph

as in Definition
4.14
. The component structure of this auxiliary bipartite graph is given by some partition

, and by Claim
4.15
we must have
\ell\geq 2t-d
. Furthermore, at least one of the sets

must have size

. Indeed, recalling that there is an index

such that

, one of the sets

must be equal to

. In particular, this means that each of the sets

has size at most

.

There are at most

possible partitions

of

into at least

non-empty subsets of size at most

. For each such partition

, by Claim

there are at most

possibilities for

with

such that the component structure of

is given by the partition

. Note that by Claim

(using that

|I_{1}|!\dotsm|I_{\ell}|!\leq(t-1)!^{(|I_{1}|-1)/(t-2)}\dotsm(t-1)!^{(|I_{\ell}|-1)/(t-2)}=(t-1)!^{(t-\ell)/(t-2)}\leq(t-1)!^{(d-t)/(t-2)},

where in the last step we used that

. Hence the total number of possibilities for

as in Claim

is at most

\sum_{\{1,\dots,t\}=I_{1}\cup\dots\cup I_{\ell}}(|I_{1}|!\dotsm|I_{\ell}|!)^{r-1}\leq t^{t}\cdot\left((t-1)!^{(d-t)/(t-2)}\right)^{r-1}=t^{t}\cdot(t-1)!^{(r-1)(d-t)/(t-2)},

where the sum is over all partitions

of

into at least

non-empty subsets of size at most

.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

Recall that
(z_{1},\dots,z_{t})\in Z_{1}\times\dots\times Z_{t}

. Since

4.3
Proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.5

As mentioned in the introduction, Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon
[
20
]
proved the upper bound

in the setting of Theorem

. Their argument is based on linear algebra, and leads to the following linear-algebraic statement.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
V
be a vector space over a field
\mathbb{F}
, let
t\geq 2
and let
\varphi:V^{t}=V\times\dots\times V\to\mathbb{F}
be a multi-linear map. Consider a collection of
t
-tuples
(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,t})\in V^{t}
for
j=1,\dots,N
, with
N>(t-1)\cdot\dim V
, such that
\varphi(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,t})\neq 0
for
j=1,\dots,N
. Then there exist distinct indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and elements
y_{1},\dots,y_{t}
with

for
i=1,\dots,t
such that
\varphi(y_{1},\dots,y_{t})\neq 0
.

This theorem implies the bound
F(t,r)\leq(t-1)\cdot{tr\choose r}
in Theorem
1.3
due to
[
20
]
by taking

to be the
r
-th exterior power of some
(rt)
-dimensional vector space and taking

(the details of the deduction can be found later in this subsection).

The question of how large one needs to take

in Theorem

can be viewed as the natural linear-algebraic abstraction of Problem

. Interestingly, while the bound

is not tight in general, the bound

in Theorem

is actually best-possible for any given

and any given dimension

. Indeed, one can take

(with standard basis vectors

) for any given

, take
\varphi:V^{t}\to\mathbb{F}
to be the multi-linear defined by
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})=\sum_{s=1}^{d}x_{1}^{(s)}\dotsm x_{t}^{(s)}
where
x_{i}=(x_{i}^{(1)},\dots,x_{i}^{(d)})
are the coordinates of

for

, and take collection of
t
-tuples
(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,t})\in V^{t}
for

consisting of
t-1
copies of
(e_{s},\dots,e_{s})
for

.

Munhá Correia, Sudakov, and Tomon
[
20
]
did not state Theorem
4.18
in this general form (they worked only in the setting where

is an exterior power and

), but their arguments for proving

easily generalize to this setting. They prove and use a result about the max-flattening rank of tensors of a certain diagonal-like shape (called “semi-diagonal”). For the reader’s convenience, we give an alternative self-contained proof of Theorem
4.18

Proof of Theorem
4.18
.

Let us prove the theorem by induction on
\dim V
. For
\dim V=0
the theorem is tautologically true (since
V=\{0\}
and so it is impossible to have
\varphi(x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,t})\neq 0
for any
j
).

Let us now assume that
\dim V\geq 1
. Let us consider all possible choices of indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
satisfying the following three conditions:

•

N
appears at least once among the indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}
.

•

Every element of
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
appears at most once among the indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}
.

•

There exist elements
y_{1},\dots,y_{t}
with
y_{i}\in\{x_{j_{i},1},\dots,x_{j_{i},t}\}
for
i=1,\dots,t
such that
\varphi(y_{1},\dots,y_{t})\neq 0
.

Note that it is indeed possible to choose indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with these three conditions, for example by taking
j_{1}=\dots=j_{t}=N
(then the third condition is satisfied because we can take
y_{i}=x_{N,i}
for
i=1,\dots,t
since
\varphi(x_{N,1},x_{N,2},\dots,x_{N,t})\neq 0
by the assumption in Theorem
4.18
).

Among all choices of indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
satisfying these conditions, let us fix a choice with the minimum possible number of repetitions of
N
. If there is only one repetition of
N
, then
j_{1},\dots,j_{t}
are distinct and so the conclusion of Theorem
4.18
is satisfied. So let us now assume that among

there are
\ell\geq 2
repetitions of
N
.

By relabeling the indices in
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
we may assume that besides the
\ell
repetitions of
N
the other

indices among

are
N-1,N-2,\dots,N-t+\ell
. Furthermore, choose an index

such that
j_{i}=N
.

Suppose we had
y_{i}\in\operatorname{span}(\bigcup_{j=1}^{N-t+\ell-1}\{x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,t}\})
. Then we could write

as a linear combination of the vectors

for

and

. Since

, this would mean that we must have
\varphi(y_{1},\dots,y_{i-1},x_{j,k},y_{i+1},\dots,y_{t})\neq 0
for some

and

. But then choosing

and

, the list of indices

obtained from

would also satisfy the three conditions above and would have only

.

Hence

. In particular, this span is not the entire space

and so we can find a subspace

with

such that

for all

. Note that

. Hence by the induction hypothesis (applied to

and the restriction of the multi-linear map

to

), there exist distinct indices

and elements

with

for

∎

are roughly equal and both reasonably large, the lower bounds in this subsection are actually better than the bounds obtained from Theorem
3.1
,
1.4
or
1.5
.

Theorem 4.19
.

For any
t\geq 2
, we have
F(r,t)\geq\binom{(tr-2)/2}{r-1}\cdot 2^{r-1}

for any even
r\geq 2
, and
F(r,t)\geq\binom{(tr-t-2)/2}{r-2}\cdot 2^{r-2}
for any odd
r\geq 3
.

For fixed matching size
t
and large uniformity
r
, this lower bound is (up to constant factors depending on
t
) on the order of
(t^{t}/(t-2)^{t-2})^{r/2}/\sqrt{r}
. In other words, the bound is exponential in
r
with exponential base
(t^{t}/(t-2)^{t-2})^{1/2}
note that this base is only slightly smaller than the base
t^{t}/(t-1)^{t-1}
in Theorem
1.4
(in fact, the quotient between these two bases converges to
1
as
t
grows).

In the
r
-partite setting, our simple lower bound has a similar behavior:

Theorem 4.20
.

For any
t\geq 2
, we have
f(r,t)\geq\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-1}
for any odd
r\geq 3
and
f(r,t)\geq\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-2}
for any even
r\geq 4
.

For fixed
t
and large
r
, this lower bound is (up to constant factors depending on
t
) equal to
\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r}
. Here, the exponential base
\sqrt{t(t-1)}
is only slightly smaller than the exponential base
t
in Theorem
1.5
.

We remark that our constructions showing Theorem
4.19
and Theorem
4.20
both use hypergraphs with
tr
vertices. In other words, both constructions actually use perfect matchings. This means that the lower bounds in Theorem
4.19
and Theorem
4.20
also apply to the variant of Problem where one requires
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
to be perfect matchings (and where one is looking for a rainbow perfect matching).

As mentioned above, our proofs of Theorem
4.19
and Theorem
4.20
rely on fairly simple explicit constructions. We start by proving Theorem
4.19
.

Proof of Theorem
4.19
.

First, assume that
r\geq 2
is even. Consider
tr
vertices labeled by
1,\dots,tr-2
,
a
and
a^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be the collection of all subsets
X\subseteq\{1,\dots,tr-2\}
of size
r-1
such that there do not exist two elements
x,x^{\prime}\in X
with
x+x^{\prime}=tr-1
. In other words,
\mathcal{X}
be the collection of subsets
X\subseteq\{1,\dots,tr-2\}
with
|X|=r-1
containing at most one element from each of the
(tr-2)/2
pairs
\{x,tr-1-x\}
for
1\leq x\leq(tr-2)/2
. We have
|\mathcal{X}|=\binom{(tr-2)/2}{r-1}\cdot 2^{r-1}
, since there are
\binom{(tr-2)/2}{r-1}
possibilities which
r-1
pairs
X
should contain an element of, and
2^{r-1}
possibilities to choose an element from each of these
r-1
pairs.

We will now define matchings
M_{X}
of size
t
for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
, and it is sufficient to show that these matchings do not have a rainbow matching of size
t
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{X}
, consider the two sets
X\cup\{a\}
and
\{tr-1-x\mid x\in X\}\cup\{a^{\prime}\}
, which both have size
r
and are disjoint (by the definition of
\mathcal{X}
). Let these two sets be the first two edges of
M_{X}
. The remaining vertices outside these two edges are a union of
r(t-2)/2
pairs of the form
\{x,tr-1-x\}
. Let us divide these
r(t-2)/2
pairs into
t-2
groups of size
r/2
(in an arbitrary way). For each group the union of the pairs in the group is a set of size
r
, and let this set be an edge of
M_{X}
. Then in total
M_{X}
has
2+(t-2)=t
edges as desired (and it is easy to see that the
t
edges of
M_{X}
are pairwise disjoint).

Let us now check that the matchings
M_{X}
for
X\in\mathcal{X}
do not have a rainbow matching of size
t
. Since there are only
tr
vertices in total, every matching of size
t
must be a perfect matching, covering all vertices. So if there was a rainbow matching of size
t
, one of its edges would need to contain
a
, so it would need to be of the form
X\cup\{a\}
for some
X\in\mathcal{X}
. Now, consider the
r-1
vertices of the form
tr-1-y
for
y\in X
(which are all outside of the edge
X\cup\{a\}
). In the rainbow matching, none of these vertices can be covered by an edge consisting of a union of pairs of the form
\{x,tr-1-x\}
(indeed,
tr-1-y
would need to be in the same pair as
y
, but
y
was already covered by the edge
X\cup\{a\}
). So each of these
r-1
vertices needs to be covered by an edge containing
a^{\prime}
. In other words, the rainbow matching must contain the edge
\{tr-1-y\mid y\in X\}\cup\{a^{\prime}\}
. However, both the edges
X\cup\{a\}
and
\{tr-1-y\mid y\in X\}\cup\{a^{\prime}\}
occur only in the matching
M_{X}
, so there cannot be a rainbow matching containing both of these edges.

This finishes the proof for even
r\geq 2
. If
r\geq 3
is odd, we can use the above construction for
r-1
(which is even) to obtain matchings
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
of size
t
in an
(r-1)
-uniform hypergraph on
t(r-1)
vertices without a rainbow matching of size
t
, where

N=\binom{(t(r-1)-2)/2}{(r-1)-1}\cdot 2^{(r-1)-1}=\binom{(tr-t-2)/2}{r-2}\cdot 2^{r-2}.

Let us now take
t
additional vertices, and for each matching
M_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, let us add one of these additional vertices to each edge of
M_{j}
(such that each of the
t
additional vertices is added to exactly one of the
t
edges of
M_{j}
). This way, we obtain
N
matchings of size
t
in an
r
-uniform hypergraph on
tr
vertices, and there is still no rainbow matching of size
t
.
∎

Finally, let us prove Theorem
4.20
, which relies on a similar construction.

Proof of Theorem
4.20
.

This time, let us first assume that
r\geq 3
is odd. Let us consider a vertex set of size
tr
, divided into
r
parts of size
t
. We will construct matchings in the complete
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
on this vertex set with this
r
-partition.

Inside each of the first
r-1
parts of the vertex set, let us label the vertices by
1,\dots,t
. In the last part of the vertex set, let us label the vertices by
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},\dots,b_{t-2}
. An edge in
\mathcal{H}
corresponds to an
r
-tuple in
\{1,\dots,t\}^{r-1}\times\{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},\dots,b_{t-2}\}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\{1,\dots,t\}^{r-1}
be the collection of all
(r-1)
-tuples
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})\in\{1,\dots,t\}^{r-1}
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2},x_{3}\neq x_{4},\dots,x_{r-2}\neq x_{r-1}
(i.e. the collection of all
(r-1)
-tuples
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})
where the first two entries are distinct, the next two entries are distinct, and so on). Note that
|\mathcal{X}|=(t(t-1))^{(r-1)/2}=\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-1}
.

Let us now define matchings
M_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})}
of size
t
in the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})\in\mathcal{X}
. It will then suffice to show that there is no rainbow matching.

For any
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})\in\mathcal{X}
, we begin to form the matching
M_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})}
by taking the two edges of
\mathcal{H}
corresponding to the two
r
-tuples
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1},a_{1})
and
(x_{2},x_{1},x_{4},x_{3},\dots,x_{r-1},x_{r-2},a_{2})
. Note that these two edges are disjoint (by the definition of
\mathcal{X}
). In each of the
r
vertex parts there are
t-2
remaining vertices not covered by these two edges. In the first and the second vertex part, the labels of the uncovered vertices agree (namely, these are the labels in the set
\{1,\dots,t\}\setminus\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
). Similarly, in the third and fourth vertex part, the labels of the uncovered vertices agree, and so on. Hence we can cover the remaining vertices by
t-2
pairwise disjoint edges corresponding to
r
-tuples of the form
(y_{1},\dots,y_{r-1},b_{i})\in\{1,\dots,t\}^{r-1}\times\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t-2}\}
with
y_{1}=y_{2},y_{3}=y_{4},\dots,y_{r-2}=y_{r-1}
. Let us add these
t-2
edges to
M_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})}
, then we indeed obtain a matching of size
t
.

Now suppose for contradiction that there is a rainbow matching of size
t
. Since there are only
tr
vertices in total, this rainbow matching would need to cover all vertices. Then vertex
a_{1}
in the last vertex part is covered by an edge corresponding to an
r
-tuple of the form
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1},a_{1})
for some
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})\in\mathcal{X}
. But now let us look at the vertex in the first vertex part with label
x_{2}
. This vertex cannot be covered by an edge containing one of the vertices
b_{1},\dots,b_{t-2}
, since any such edge must be of the form
(y_{1},\dots,y_{r-1},b_{i})
with
y_{1}=y_{2},y_{3}=y_{4},\dots,y_{r-2}=y_{r-1}
(and so it would also need to contain the vertex with label
x_{2}
in the second vertex part, which is already covered by the edge corresponding to
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1},a_{1})
). Hence the edge covering the vertex in the first part with label
x_{2}
must contain
a_{2}
. The same argument can be made for the vertex in the second part with label
x_{1}
, the vertex in the third part with label
x_{4}
, the vertex in the fourth part with label
x_{3}
, and so on (finishing with the vertex in the
(r-1)
-th part with label
x_{r-2}
). In the rainbow matching, each of these
r-1
vertices must be covered by an edge that also contains
a_{2}
. In other words, the rainbow matching must contain the edge corresponding to the
r
-tuple
(x_{2},x_{1},x_{4},x_{3},\dots,x_{r-1},x_{r-2},a_{2})
, and it also contains the edge corresponding to the
r
-tuple
(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1},a_{1})
. However, both of these edges only occur in the matching
M_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1})}
, so there cannot be a rainbow matching containing both of these edges.

This finishes the proof for odd
r\geq 3
. For even
r\geq 4
, we can take the above construction for
r-1
(which is odd), which gives
\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-2}
matchings of size
t
in some
(r-1)
-partite
(r-1)
-uniform hypergraph on
t(r-1)
vertices. Let us now add an additional vertex part with
t
vertices and for each of the
\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-2}
matchings, let us add one vertex from the new part to each edge (such that each of the
t
vertices in the new part is added to exactly one of the
t
edges of each matching). This way, we obtain
\sqrt{t(t-1)}^{r-2}
matchings of size
t
in an
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph on
tr
vertices, without a rainbow matching of size
t
.
∎

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we study the maximum possible number of matchings of size
t
(each colored in a different color) in a
r
-uniform hypergraph without a rainbow matching of size
t
. This maximum possible number is denoted by
F(r,t)
. Furthermore,
f(r,t)
is defined the answer to the analogous problem with the additional assumption that the underlying
r
-uniform hypergraph is
r
-partite. The problem of estimating the functions
F(r,t)
and
f(r,t)
has attracted the attention of many different researchers
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
14
,
20
]
. For fixed uniformity
r\geq 3
, we determine
F(r,t)
and
f(r,t)
up to constant factors (depending on
r
), showing that both are on the order of
t^{r}
. This in particular disproves a conjecture of Glebov–Sudakov–Szabó
[
14
]
. Furthermore, we determine
F(r,t)
and
f(r,t)
up to lower-order terms in the opposite regime, for fixed
t\geq 2
and large uniformity
r
.

It would be interesting to also understand the behavior of the functions
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
when both
r
and
t
are growing. In Section
4.4
, we give some additional lower bounds for
f(r,t)
and
F(r,t)
. These bounds are better than our lower bounds in our main theorems stated in the introduction if
r
and
t
are both large and of similar order (if, say,
r
and
t
are within constant factors of each other). In particular, if
r=t
, our lower bounds in Section
4.4
are matching the upper bound
F(r,t)\leq(t-1)\binom{tr}{r}
due to Munhá Correia–Sudakov–Tomon
[
20
]
and the upper bound
f(r,t)\leq(t-1)\cdot t^{r}
in Theorem
1.5
up to polynomial factors in
r=t
.

Finally, we remark that all lower bounds in this paper are based on (partly probabilistic) constructions where
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
are matchings of size
t
in an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
tr
vertices. In other words, all our lower bounds are based on constructions where
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
are
perfect
matchings. It is natural to also ask about Problem
1.1
(as well as its
r
-partite analogue) in the special setting where
M_{1},\dots,M_{N}
are perfect matchings, and one is looking for a rainbow perfect matching. Of course, all of the upper bounds for
F(r,t)
and
f(r,t)
in particular apply to this special case of perfect matchings. Given the constructions we use in this paper, our lower bounds also all apply to this variant of the problem.

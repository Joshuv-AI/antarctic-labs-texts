Abstract
. We study the orchard problem on cubic surfaces. We classify possibly reducible cubic surfaces X ⊆ ℙ 3 ​ ( ℂ ) X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{C}}) with smooth components on which there exist families of finite sets (of unbounded size) with quadratically many 3-rich lines which do not concentrate (in a natural sense) on any projective plane. Namely, we prove that such a family exists precisely when X X is a union of three planes sharing a common line. Along the way, we obtain a general result about nilpotency of groups admitting an algebraic action satisfying an Elekes-Szabó condition, and we prove the following purely algebrogeometric statement: if the composition of four Geiser involutions through sufficiently generic points a , b , c , d a,b,c,d on a smooth irreducible cubic surface has infinitely many fixed points, then a single plane contains a , b , c , d a,b,c,d and all but finitely many of the fixed points.

Introduction

1.1.
Summary of results

We prove the following result for the 3-dimensional orchard problem on a
complex cubic surface
S
, showing that if a large finite subset of
S
has
many collinear triples, this must be due to a coplanar subset.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
3.8
, symmetric case)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
and
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
if
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})
is a cubic surface with smooth irreducible components
which is not the union of three planes sharing a common line, and
A\subseteq S

is a finite subset with
|A|\geq N_{0}
, and
|C|\geq|A|^{2-\eta}
where

C:=\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in A^{3}:a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\text{ are distinct, collinear,
and not contained in a line contained in $S$}\}
,
then for some plane
\pi\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})

we have
|C\cap\pi^{3}|\geq|A|^{2-\epsilon}
.

1.2.
Background and motivation

The (planar)
orchard problem
asks for finite configurations of points
in the real plane such that many lines pass through exactly three of the
points. Call such lines
3-rich
. See
[
3
]
for details
of the long history of this problem, and the result that for any
n\geq 3
,
taking a subgroup of order
n
of an elliptic curve yields a configuration
with
\lfloor n(n-3)/6\rfloor+1
3-rich lines. In
[
16
, Theorem 1.3,
§9]
, Green and Tao show that this is optimal for large
n
,
and moreover that taking finite subgroups of irreducible cubics is the only
source of such optimal configurations.

In
[
12
]
, Elekes and Szabó consider a coarse asymptotic version of
the problem: for what configurations of
n
points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
are there
\sim n^{2}
lines passing through at least three of the points? Call such lines

triple lines
.
In this context, any (possibly reducible) planar cubic curve provides such
configurations, again by considering group structures induced by collinearity.
If one restricts to the case that the configuration is to be a subset of an
algebraic curve, the problem becomes an instance of that considered in

[
13
]
, where algebraic groups are seen to be the only possible way
to achieve the quadratic bound;
[
12
]
uses this to show that only
cubic curves yield quadratically many triple lines. A subsequent strengthening
by Raz, Sharir, and de Zeeuw
[
20
, Corollary 6.2]
) gives a bound

O_{d}(n^{2-\frac{1}{6}})
on the number of such lines for
n
points on an
irreducible curve of degree
d>3
.

The motivation behind this paper is the problem of extending
[
13
]

to higher dimension, without the restrictive general position assumption made
in that paper (nor the only slightly weaker “coarse general position”
assumption in
[
1
]
). As a natural example of this, we consider the

spatial orchard problem
, in which one now considers configurations of

n
points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and again considers the number of triple lines. See

[
11
]
and its references for what little literature there is on
these spatial problems.

We can again formulate a problem of Elekes-Szabó-type by taking an algebraic
surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and asking for configurations of
n
points on the surface
with
\sim n^{2}
triple lines. We have uninteresting solutions to this: if
S

is a cubic surface, then the intersection of
S
with a plane is a cubic
curve, which typically supports a configuration as above. Distributing our
points across a family of
m=m(n)
such planes, we can get
\geq\Omega(\frac{n^{2}}{m})
triple lines with
\leq O((\frac{n}{m})^{2})
of them on any
given plane.
To rule out these unoriginal solutions, we may phrase the question as follows
(where we also pass to the more algebraically natural generality of surfaces
in complex projective space rather than
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
):

Question 1.2
.

For which algebraic surfaces
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})
is there
\epsilon>0
and
c>0

such that there are arbitrarily large finite subsets
A\subseteq S

supporting
\geq c|A|^{2}
triple lines,
no
|A|^{2-\epsilon}
of which are coplanar?

We answer this question with additional conditions on
S
: we assume
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{C}})
is a possibly reducible cubic surface, i.e. the zero-set of a cubic
homogeneous polynomial with no repeated factors, and that each irreducible
component is smooth. We find that such an
S
satisfies the conditions of
Question
1.2
if and only if
S
is the union of three planes sharing a
common line. This is a consequence of Corollary
3.8
stated
above, which in fact has better and uniform (albeit inexplicit) bounds.

1.3.
Overview

In proving the results sketched above, our first move will be to pass to
pseudofinite sets (ultraproducts of finite sets). This pseudofinite setting
converts asymptotic questions about arbitrarily large finite sets into more
convenient questions about individual pseudofinite sets. We make the further
move of working with complete types in a suitable language, in the sense of
first-order logic. This small abstraction leads to cleaner statements and
proofs; in particular it makes “popularity arguments” an invisible part of
the formalism. Furthermore, it brings into play useful tools from model
theory, particularly independence calculus via Hrushovski’s coarse
pseudofinite dimension, which converts asymptotic exponents in finite
cardinalities into a real-valued dimension with good properties. This leads us
naturally to the notion of
weak general position
, which is a useful
formalisation of the idea of the smallest subvariety on which a configuration
concentrates, and is a key technical tool in our analysis.

Our main proofs on cubic surfaces split into two largely disjoint cases,
according to whether the surface is reducible or not. In the reducible case,
the surface is either the union of a smooth quadric surface
Q
and a plane

P
, or the union of three distinct planes. In the former case, for
z\in Q\setminus P
we obtain an automorphism
\gamma_{z}\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(Q)\cong{\operatorname{PO}}_{4}
of the quadric,
defined generically by
\gamma_{z}(x)=y
if
(x,y,z)
are distinct and
collinear. Considering this along with the assumption that we have
quadratically many collinear triples, we prove a general result
(Theorem
4.4
) showing that in such a situation we obtain an approximate
subgroup of the automorphism group, of a kind which contradicts results of
Breuillard-Green-Tao
[
4
]
unless its Zariski closure is a nilpotent
subgroup. Analysing the possibilities for such subgroups yields the result in
this case. The case of three planes is similar (and easier).

In the case of a smooth irreducible cubic surface, the corresponding
birational maps
\gamma_{z}
, known as
Geiser involutions
in this case,
do not generate an algebraic group, and so the techniques of the previous
paragraph do not apply. Instead, we argue as follows. If
A\subseteq S
supports
quadratically many 3-rich lines, then the “energy” relation

\displaystyle E:=\{

\displaystyle((x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2}))\in S^{2}\times S^{2}:

\displaystyle\exists z\in S.\;((x_{1},y_{1},z)\text{ and }(x_{2},y_{2},z)\text{ are distinct
and collinear})\}

has
|E\cap A^{4}|\sim|A|^{3}
, which would contradict known bounds of
Szemerédi-Trotter type if
E
omitted the bipartite complete graph
K_{2,s}

for sufficiently small
s
. In fact
E
does not have this property, but we
show (under some genericity assumptions) that any
K_{2,s}
in
E
is planar
after deleting a bounded finite subset. Adapting the proof of such
Szemerédi-Trotter type bounds (we use the version due to
Chernikov-Galvin-Starchenko,
[
7
,
9
]
), we deduce that the energy of
A

concentrates on planes. Using the bounds in the Tóth-Zahl result on
Szemerédi-Trotter in
\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, we reduce to the case that the energy
concentrates on a single plane, which yields our result. To show that each

K_{2,s}
is almost planar, we consider a curve
C
of fixed points of the
composition of four (sufficiently generic) Geiser involutions

(\gamma_{a_{i}})_{i<4}
, and use algebro-geometric techniques to show that
C

is planar, and coplanar with the
a_{i}
. The main part of this argument is a
careful analysis ruling out hypothetical exceptional cases where the
a_{i}
are
singular points on
C
; for this we use divisor class calculations and the
arithmetic genus.

Our methods may be seen as pointing a way towards a general analysis of
Elekes-Szabó problems in higher dimension. One formulation of this for
surfaces would be as follows. Suppose
R\subseteq S_{1}\times S_{2}\times S_{3}
is a
constructible ternary relation between algebraic surfaces, such that the
projection to any pair
S_{i}\times S_{j}
is dominant with generically finite
fibres. Suppose that for any
n>0
, we can find
N\geq n
and
A_{i,n}\subseteq S_{i}

with
|A_{i,n}|=N
such that
|R\cap\prod_{i}A_{i,n}|\geq N^{2-\frac{1}{n}}
.
Then what can we say about
R
or the
A_{i,n}
? In
[
13
,
1
]
it is shown that if
R
is not the image under algebraic
correspondences on the co-ordinates of the graph of a commutative group
operation, then for some
i
each
A_{i,n}
has
|A_{i,n}|^{O(1)}

intersection with a subcurve of
S_{i}
. But the possibilities once one allows
such non-negligible intersections with curves are not at all clear. The
present work analyses this in the particular case that
R
is the collinearity
relation on
S_{1}=S_{2}=S_{3}
a cubic surface, and we can consider what our
methods and results have to say about the general problem. On one hand we can
take it as a warning that a straightforward solution is unlikely, since even
in this special case the analysis is difficult and intricate. More positively,
we can expect that at least some of the ideas which arose in this case, in
particular the role of infinite complete bipartite subgraphs of the “energy”
relation, will translate to the general problem.

Proof.

2.2.2.
Collinearity on planar cubic curves

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 0 and let
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
be an absolutely
irreducible cubic planar curve over
k
.
Then collinearity induces an abelian group structure as follows; see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 2.1, Example 2.2]
or the references in the proof of
Fact
2.18
.

Fact 2.17
.

Let
C^{\prime}
be the set of non-singular points of
C(k)
. Suppose
C^{\prime}
is
non-empty, and let
u\in C^{\prime}
.
For
a,b,c\in C^{\prime}
, write
a\circ b=c
if the divisor of the intersection of
some projective line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
C(k)
is the sum of the divisors
corresponding to the points
a,b,c
.

Then
x+y:=u\circ(x\circ y)
defines an abelian group structure on
C^{\prime}
,
and
x\circ y=(u\circ u)-x-y
for any
x,y\in C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We give the calculation for the final statement:

(x\circ y)+y+x=u\circ((x\circ y)\circ y)+x=u\circ x+x=u\circ((u\circ x)\circ x)=u\circ u.

∎

Fact 2.18
.

Let
(C^{\prime};+)
be as in Fact
2.17
. Then for each
n
, the
n
-torsion
subgroup of
C^{\prime}
is finite.

Proof.

We may assume
k
is algebraically closed.
If
C
is smooth, this is a standard fact about elliptic curves; see e.g.
[
22
, Example 3.4, p.179]
.
Otherwise,
C^{\prime}
is isomorphic to either the multiplicative or the additive
group of the field; this is well-known, we refer to

[
22
, Exercise 1.7.3]
for the fact that
C
is projectively
equivalent to either
y^{2}=x^{3}+x^{2}
(if the singularity is nodal) or

y^{2}=x^{3}
(if the singularity is cuspoidal), and

[
23
, Theorem III.7]
which proves (even though the statement only
discusses the rational points) that the group is then isomorphic to the
multiplicative or additive group respectively.
Finiteness of the
n
-torsion is then clear (since
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
).
∎

Fact 2.19
.

If three points
a,b,c\in C(k)
are distinct and collinear, then each is
non-singular on
C
.

Proof.

Since
C
is cubic, it has at most one singular point (as in the proof of
Lemma
5.4
below), so say
b
and
c
are non-singular. But then
by Fact
2.17
,
a=b\circ c
is also in the group
C^{\prime}
of non-singular
points, so
a
is also non-singular.
∎

3.
Elekes-Szabó for collinearity on a cubic surface

We refer to one-dimensional projective subspaces of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
as
lines
,
and two-dimensional projective subspaces as
planes
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field. For the purposes of this paper, a

cubic surface

S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}(K)
is the zero-set
S=\{[x:y:z:w]\in\mathbb{P}^{3}(K):f(x,y,z,w)=0\}
of a homogeneous degree 3 polynomial
f\in K[X,Y,Z,W]
with no repeated prime factors; in other words,
S
is the set
of
K
-rational points of the reduced (but possibly reducible) projective
variety defined by
f
.
(The reducedness condition that there are no repeated prime factors is
relevant only in the case that
f
splits into linear factors, i.e. that
S

is a union of planes, where it requires that the three planes be distinct.)

The zero-sets of the factors
g_{i}
of
f
are the irreducible components of

S
. We will often require these to be smooth, meaning that for each
i
,
the formal derivative
g_{i}^{\prime}
has no common zero with
g_{i}
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}(K)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}({\mathbb{K}})
be a cubic surface over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
Assume that
{\mathcal{L}}
contains constants for parameters defining
S
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
R_{S}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be the constructible relation:

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is a collinear triple of distinct points on
S

not contained in a line contained in
S
.

Note that if
R_{S}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
, then
a_{i}\in{\operatorname{acl}}^{0}(a_{j},a_{k})
(for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
).

Definition 3.3
.

•

A
wgp Elekes-Szabó triangle
(or

) for

is
a triple

such that each
{\operatorname{tp}}(a_{i})
is wgp and

and

for

.

A wES-triangle
{\overline{a}}
is

if there is a constructible
0
plane
\pi\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
with

•

By Lemma

Lemma 4.7

Let

5.1.3.

Given a good point

the
Geiser involution

\gamma_{a}
through

\gamma_{a}\in\operatorname{Bir}(S)
defined generically on

be the third point of intersection (counting multiplicity) with

\mathbb{P}^{3}

By

m_{a}^{b}=d_{d,a}-2m_{a}^{d}

(i), we get

. Similarly,
\{ba,cd,a\}
are distinct points on

, hence

. Therefore,

and
m_{a}^{b}=m_{a}^{d}
as desired.

∎

5.2.2.
Nonsingularity of
a,b,c,d

Let
m=m_{x}^{y}=d_{0}/3
be as in Lemma
5.27
.
Our next aim, achieved in Lemma
5.32
below, is to show that

, i.e. that

are not singular on the corresponding curves.

For this, we make limited use of the notion of

points. For our purposes, it will suffice to make the following ad hoc restricted definition; see
[
17
, p.392, Definition]
for the more general definition.

Definition 5.28
.

If
C
is an irreducible curve on
S
, a
1-infinitely near

is a point in the intersection of the strict transform
\widetilde{C}\subseteq\widetilde{S}_{y}
of

under the blowup of

at a point

with the exceptional divisor of this blowup. The multiplicity on
C
of such a 1-infinitely near point is defined to be its multiplicity on
\widetilde{C}
.

Lemma 5.29
.

Let
C
be an irreducible curve on
S
with divisor class
\alpha\ell-\sum_{i=1}^{6}b_{i}e_{i}
, and let

be distinct points or 1-infinitely near points on

, and let

be the multiplicity of

on

.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}r_{i}(r_{i}-1)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{6}b_{i}(b_{i}-1).

Proof.

This follows from
[
17
, Example V.3.9.2]
,
using that the 1-infinitely near points are among the infinitely near points,
that the genus of the normalisation of
C
is non-negative (
[
17
, Remark IV.1.1.1]
),
and that the right hand side is precisely the arithmetic genus of

(
[
17
, Proposition V.4.8(g)]
).
∎

Theorem 5.30
.

On at least one of the four curves
\ell_{d,a},\ell_{a,b},\ell_{b,c},\ell_{c,d}
,
there are at least 5 distinct (possibly 1-infinitely near) points each of multiplicity at least
m
.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. Suppose there are no five distinct (1-infinitely near) points of multiplicity at least
m
on any one of the four curves
\ell_{d,a},\ell_{a,b},\ell_{b,c},\ell_{c,d}
.

By Lemmas
5.20
and
5.25
,

a,d,cd,ba
are distinct points on
\ell_{d,a}
, each with multiplicity
m
by Lemma
5.21
(ii).

Suppose
cb=b
. Now
\gamma_{b}^{*}(\ell_{b,c})=\ell_{a,b}
, and recall this means by definition that in the blowup
\widetilde{S}_{b}

\widetilde{\gamma}_{b}
maps the strict transform
\widetilde{\ell}_{b,c}
to the strict transform
\widetilde{\ell}_{a,b}

m
on
\ell_{a,b}

since these are points in the usual sense, so this contradicts our assumption. We conclude that

By symmetry,
cb\notin\{c,b\}
and
ad\notin\{a,d\}
.

Now
cba\in\{a,d,cd,ba\}
, since
\mu_{cba}(\ell_{d,a})\geq m

5.21
(ii).
But
cba\neq d
by Lemma
5.25
, and
cba\neq ba

.

Suppose
cba=a

\widetilde{\gamma}_{a}(cb)

(ii). This contradicts our assumption.

Therefore,
cba=cd
. By symmetry (using

and

), we have

and

and

is co-planar with
\{c,b,d\}
, since
(cb)a=cd
. Let

be the intersection of

with the plane spanned by

is an irreducible curve by Lemma

then each of them is non-singular on

. So since

We consider the additive structure on the set

of non-singular points of

So

∎

The planar case:

5.3.
A variant on Szemerédi-Trotter

We will use a version of the Szemerédi-Trotter phenomenon,
Proposition
5.35
below, which handles relations which are not

K_{d,s}
-free by bounding what is left once (parts of) all instances of

K_{d,s}
are deleted. Here, we explain how to obtain this result and the
pseudofinite form Corollary
5.37
in which we will apply it.

Definition 5.34
.

Given
d,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a binary relation

a
(d,s)
-transversal
of

such that
for every

with
|A|\geq d
and
|B|\geq s

for every

b\in B
such that
(a,b)\in F
.

In particular, the union of all instances of
K_{d,s}
in

is a

(d,s)
-transversal.

Proposition 5.35
.

Let
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
, and

be constructible sets over a
field
L
of characteristic 0.
Then there exists
t>1
such that
for any

,

such that

and any

F
is a
(d,s)
-transversal of

|E\setminus F|\leq Cs(|A_{1}|^{\frac{(t-1)d}{td-1}}|A_{2}|^{\frac{td-t}{td-1}}+|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|).

In the case that
E
is
K_{d,s}
-free, so
F=\emptyset
is a

(d,s)
-transversal, Proposition
5.35
is essentially

[
13
, Theorem 9]
(the bound proven there is slightly weaker, but
would still be sufficient for our purposes).
A stronger form of that theorem is proven in the general setting of distal
cell decompositions as
[
9
, Theorem 2.6]
, and we will explain how to
adapt the proof of the latter to show the following corresponding
generalisation of Proposition
5.35
. We refer to
[
9
]
for the
definition of a distal cell decomposition. A reader who would prefer to
restrict to the context of Proposition

Lemma 5.39
.

be an internal set, and for

-anticlique

.

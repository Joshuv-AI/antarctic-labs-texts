Higher-order EW corrections in Z ​ Z ZZ and Z ​ Z ​ j ZZj production at the LHC
Enrico Bothmann, Davide Napoletano, Marek Schönherr, Steffen Schumann, Simon Luca Villani

Abstract
We consider the production of a pair of Z Z bosons at the LHC and study the inclusion of EW corrections in theoretical predictions at fixed order and based on multijet-merged parton-shower simulations. To this end we present exact NLO EW results for p ​ p → e + ​ e − ​ μ + ​ μ − pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-} , and, for the first time, for p ​ p → e + ​ e − ​ μ + ​ μ − ​ j pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j , and compare them to the EW virtual and NLL Sudakov approximation. We then match the exact NLO EW result to the resummed Sudakov logarithms to achieve an improved NLO EW + {}+{} NLL EW sud exp \text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}} result. Further, we discuss the inclusion of the above EW corrections in MePs@Nlo event simulations in the framework of the Sherpa event generator. We present detailed phenomenological predictions for inclusive Z ​ Z ZZ and Z ​ Z ​ j ZZj production taking into account the dominant EW corrections through the EW virtual approximation, as well as through (exponentiated) EW Sudakov logarithms.

Introduction

With the successful completion of the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) Run 1 and Run 2

2

NLO EW and resummed EW Sudakov corrections

The
{\cal{O}}(\alpha)
EW corrections to a given tree-level process comprise
virtual one-loop contributions,
i.e.
the interference of
loop-diagrams with tree-level amplitudes, as well as a real-emission part.
Due to the non-vanishing mass of the
W
and
Z
bosons, as well as their
straight-forward experimental identifiability, these are typically discarded
as real-emission corrections. At NLO EW, the differential cross section in
the fiducial region of an observable that is non-trivial at Born-level
can be written as a correction,
\delta^{\text{EW}}
, to its LO expression
given by the Born matrix element
\mathrm{B}
in the Born phase space
\Phi
, as

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{NLO EW}}=&\;\mathrm{d}\Phi\;\mathrm{B}(\Phi)\;\left(1+\delta^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\delta^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)=\frac{\tilde{\mathrm{V}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)}{\mathrm{B}(\Phi)}+\mathrm{d}\Phi_{1}\,\frac{\mathrm{R}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi\!\cdot\!\Phi_{1})}{\mathrm{B}(\Phi)}\;.\end{split}

(2.1)

\tilde{\mathrm{V}}^{\text{EW}}
includes the virtual correction

\mathrm{V}^{\text{EW}}
as well as the collinear
mass-factorisation terms. The phase space associated with the real-emission
correction
\mathrm{R}^{\text{EW}}
refers to one additional particle compared to the Born process.

2.1

EW corrections in the Sudakov limit

In the high-energy limit,
i.e.
when all invariants of
a process are large compared to the EW scale, the so-called
EW Sudakov regime, the exact NLO EW corrections are dominated
by the exchange of virtual electroweak gauge bosons and the
running of the EW input parameters
[
41
]
.
Thus, using the above building blocks, the NLO EW corrections
can be approximated by
[
46
]

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{LO${}+{}$$\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}$ }}=&\;\mathrm{d}\Phi\;\mathrm{B}(\Phi)\;\left(1+\delta_{\text{virt}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\delta_{\text{virt}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)=\frac{\mathrm{V}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)+\mathrm{I}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)}{\mathrm{B}(\Phi)}\;.\end{split}

(2.2)

In this so-called EW virtual approximation,
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
, the exact NLO EW
virtual correction
\mathrm{V}^{\text{EW}}
is supplemented with
integrated approximate real-emission corrections
\mathrm{I}^{\text{EW}}
,
corresponding to the EW Catani–Seymour subtraction
\mathbf{I}
-operator

[
18
]
.
The such constructed correction is infrared finite and contains
both all EW next-to-leading logarithms (NLL) of the high-energy limit,
as well as important finite corrections throughout phase space and
EW scheme-dependent renormalisation terms that reduce the
scheme dependence at higher orders in
\alpha
.
On the other hand, as exact NLO EW one-loop matrix elements
need to be evaluated, this type of correction is computationally
rather expensive. In practice the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation is therefore
applicable for rather low-multiplicity processes.

Alternatively, without the need to know the
exact one-loop amplitude, an equally NLL
accurate universal Sudakov
factor can be constructed
[
41
,
42
]
:

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{LO${}+{}$$\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}$ }}=&\;\mathrm{d}\Phi\;\mathrm{B}(\Phi)\;\left(1+\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\quad\;\text{with}\quad\;\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)=\frac{\mathrm{V}_{\text{NLL}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)+\mathrm{I}_{\text{NLL}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)}{\mathrm{B}(\Phi)}=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{NLL}}(\Phi)\;.\end{split}

(2.3)

Therein,
\mathrm{V}_{\text{NLL}}^{\text{EW}}
is based mainly on the soft and
collinear limits of the dominating one-loop virtual electroweak
gauge-boson exchange diagrams.
Also included in
\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}

are the integrated real-photon emission corrections
\mathrm{I}_{\text{NLL}}^{\text{EW}}

to the same accuracy.
This results in residual corrections proportional to logarithms
of the large kinematic invariants that are universal. The above
defined approximate correction thus
directly translates into the relative correction
\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{NLL}}

to the LO cross section.
*
*

*

Please note, in variance with
K_{\text{NLL}}
given in
[
44
]
,

\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{NLL}}
used here is defined such that
1+\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{NLL}}=K_{\text{NLL}}
.

A general and automated implementation of such NLL EW Sudakov corrections
in the
Sherpa
framework has been presented in
[
44
]
.
In the course of the work presented in this paper, several improvements to
this implementation have been accomplished, related to the presence of
intermediate resonances and multiple energy scales in the pairwise invariants,
see App.
A
for details.

Further, EW corrections in the Sudakov limit can be resummed through
exponentiation
[
83
,
84
,
85
,
86
,
87
,
88
,
89
,
90
,
91
]
.
This provides a more accurate description at very high energies, when Sudakov
logarithms become large, as it takes account of these effects to all orders.
While the relative correction of the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation is generally
not suitable for exponentiation as it contains a number of non-universal
finite terms, the pure NLL correction of the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation can be
directly used as the basis of the resummation.
Thus, the resulting cross section including NLL accurate resummed EW Sudakov
corrections reads

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{LO${}+{}$$\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}}$ }}=&\;\mathrm{d}\Phi\;\mathrm{B}(\Phi)\;\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\;.\end{split}

(2.4)

All of the above approximations can be supplemented with a soft-photon
resummation in the YFS scheme
[
92
,
93
]
without
losing the formal NLL accuracy in the Sudakov regime,
see
Sec.
3.2
.
In consequence, the restoration of the differential description of
real-photon emissions will typically improve the agreement with
the exact NLO EW calculation.

2.2

Matched NLO EW
{}+{}
NLL
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}}
corrections

The resummed NLL EW Sudakov corrections can be matched
to the exact NLO EW result to achieve the optimal description
for inclusive observables
and
the high-energy tails of kinematic
distributions. We choose a matching scheme in which we replace
the
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha\right)}
coefficient in the expansion of the exponential
with the exact NLO EW expression,
i.e.

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\text{NLO EW${}+{}$NLL $\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}}$ }}=&\;\mathrm{d}\Phi\;\mathrm{B}(\Phi)\;\left[\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)-\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)+\delta^{\text{EW}}(\Phi)\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right]\;.\end{split}

(2.5)

In this way, at high energies when
\delta^{\text{EW}}\approx\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}
, we obtain the resummed result as expected.
On the other hand, when the Sudakov logarithms are small,

i.e.

\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}\right)\approx 1+\delta_{\text{sud}}^{\text{EW}}
, the fixed-order result is recovered.

It is worth stressing that this matching formula, when expanded
to
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha^{2}\right)}
, coincides with the approach used in

[
94
]
to estimate the approximate NNLO EW
corrections, and its third-order coefficient serving as an estimate for
its uncertainty.
Here we will use the complete matched result to obtain
an improved central value that incorporates the dominant terms
to all orders, and estimate the uncertainty by a change of
the EW renormalisation scheme. In terms of the uncertainty
estimation, this ansatz is more conservative, as scheme-compensating
terms are not included beyond NLO in the matched calculation.
However, such uncertainty estimate is expected to be more reliable
in the fixed-order regime.

QCD Multijet merging with approximate EW corrections

Multijet-merging calculations attempt to describe the high-energetic QCD radiation
accompanying a production process through exact matrix elements rather than solely
relying on the parton-shower approximation. However, as discussed in
Sec.
2
, the high-energy tails of physical distributions are also
rather susceptible to EW corrections and in particular Sudakov-suppression effects.
This motivates the development of calculational schemes to incorporate the leading EW
contributions in QCD merging approaches.

In this section we briefly review the merging formalism used in the
Sherpa
framework. To
this end we revisit in particular the definitions of the
MePs@Lo
and
MePs@Nlo
schemes. We then describe their respective combinations with approximate EW corrections,
i.e.
the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approaches. To account for higher-order QED corrections,
i.e.
the emission of soft photons from the final-state leptons, we employ the
Yennie–Frautschi–Suura (YFS) approach to QED resummation
[
92
]
. Its combination
with the approximate EW corrections will also be addressed in what follows.

3.1

QCD multijet merging at LO and NLO

QCD multijet merging aims for the consistent combination of processes with varying multiplicity
of associated jets that get evolved by a QCD parton-shower model. The generic multijet-merged cross
section can be written as

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma=&\;\sum\limits_{n=0}^{n_{\text{max}}-1}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{\text{excl}}+\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n_{\text{max}}}^{\text{incl}}\;.\end{split}

(3.1)

Therein, each exclusive
n
-jet process—relative to some core process which may already contain
jets not counted in
n
—can be evaluated either at LO or at NLO in QCD. The notion of exclusive
and inclusive cross sections is closely linked to the shower evolution of the underlying
multi-parton ensembles and is realised through a jet-resolution parameter,
i.e.
the merging scale
Q_{\text{cut}}
. By including higher-multiplicity matrix elements, multijet-merged
simulations account both for the bulk of the total production cross section
as well as
rare
configurations with (multiple) associated high-energetic QCD jets.

M

E

P

.

The
Sherpa
leading-order merging prescription,
i.e.
the
MePs@Lo
method
[
95
]
,
is based on tree-level matrix elements dressed by a truncated-shower evolution using the Catani–Seymour
dipole shower
[
96
]
.
The exclusive
n
-jet cross section is thereby derived from the
simple combination of the LO
n
-parton matrix element
\mathrm{B}_{n}
(including all symmetry, flux and PDF factors)
and the appropriate parton-shower functional
\mathcal{F}_{n}
restricted to emissions below the merging scale
Q_{\text{cut}}
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}^{\text{excl},\text{{MePs@Lo}}}=\;&\mathrm{d}\Phi_{n}\;\mathrm{B}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)\;\Theta_{n}(Q_{\text{cut}})\;\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(\mu_{Q}^{2};Q_{\text{cut}}\right)\;.\end{split}

(3.2)

Here,
\Theta_{n}(Q_{\text{cut}})
implements cuts and constraints on the fiducial phase space for
the final state of the core process, and further requires at least
n
resolved jets with
the merging parameter
Q_{\text{cut}}
playing the role of the jet-resolution scale. The
inverse of the parton shower must thereby be used
as a clustering and recombination algorithm
to produce a consistent result.
In general, the parton-shower functional
\mathcal{F}
takes the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(t\right)=&\;\Delta_{n}\left(t_{c},t\right)\,+\int_{t_{c}}^{t}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{1}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{K}_{n}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\prime}\right)\,\Delta_{n}\left(t^{\prime},t\right)\;\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\;,\end{split}

(3.3)

with
t
the shower-evolution variable and
t_{c}
its IR cutoff,
\mathrm{K}_{n}
the splitting kernel,
and
\Delta_{n}\left(t^{\prime},t\right)=\exp(-\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{1}\,\mathrm{K}_{n}(\Phi_{1}))

the corresponding Sudakov form factor.
As mentioned above,
in the context of the exclusive processes entering the multijet-merging,
the shower emissions are restricted to occur below
Q_{\text{cut}}
to prevent
filling the phase space governed by higher-multiplicity matrix elements twice.
This constraint is implemented as follows:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(t;Q_{\text{cut}}\right)=&\;\Delta_{n}\left(t_{c},t\right)\,+\int_{t_{c}}^{t}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{1}^{\prime}\,\mathrm{K}_{n}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\prime}\right)\,\Theta\left(Q_{\text{cut}}-Q_{n+1}(t^{\prime})\right)\,\Delta_{n}\left(t^{\prime},t\right)\,\mathcal{F}_{n+1}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\;,\end{split}

(3.4)

with
Q_{n+1}
being the smallest reconstructed emission scale of
the newly formed
(n+1)
-parton ensemble.
In consequence, its unitary is broken, giving a Sudakov
suppression that turns the inclusive Born expression into
the description of an exclusive
n
-jet cross section down to
a jet resolution of
Q_{\text{cut}}
.
Hence,
\mu_{Q}^{2}
in Eq. (
3.2
) defines
the parton-shower starting scale.
In contract,
the highest multiplicity
n=n_{\text{max}}
needs to
be treated inclusively as indicated in Eq. (
3.1
).
Accordingly,
Q_{\text{cut}}
is in this case event-wise replaced
with the lowest reconstructed emission scale
Q_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\geq Q_{\text{cut}}
, and
the parton shower
\mathcal{F}_{n_{\text{max}}}=\mathcal{F}_{n_{\text{max}}}\left(t;Q_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\right)
is allowed to fill the complete phase space
below it.

3.2

Incorporating EW corrections

The expressions for the QCD contributions to multijet-merged samples can
be combined with EW corrections,
\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}
. However, in order not to interfere
with the ordering of the QCD emissions in the matrix elements and parton
showers this requires well-defined approximations. We here consider the two
cases introduced in Sec.
2.1
,
the high-energy limit treated either in the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
or
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation,
supplemented by final-state
resolved
photon radiation off the charged leptons,
treated in the YFS resummation approach.
With such factorised technique we can account for EW corrections on top of QCD ones,
which would otherwise not be viable if we were to include the full set of NLO EW
contributions.

First we deal with the region dominated by virtual gauge-boson
exchange and/or the emission of a soft or collinear
unresolved

photon. In this scenario,
\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}
is local in the corresponding
n
-particle
phase space, affecting the weight of Born-,
\mathbb{S}
-, and
\mathbb{H}
-type events,
such that EW corrections are easily incorporated via

\begin{split}\mathrm{B}_{n}\to\mathrm{B}_{n}\,\left(1+\delta_{n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\end{split}

(3.9)

for LO QCD contributions, and

\begin{split}\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\,\left(1+\delta_{n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}_{n}\to\mathrm{H}_{n}\,\left(1+\delta_{n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\end{split}

(3.10)

for NLO QCD
\mathbb{S}
- and
\mathbb{H}
-type contributions.
‡
‡

‡

Of course,
the Born matrix element
\mathrm{B}_{n}
appearing within
\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}
does not
receive a second separate EW correction.

The case where LO matrix elements are merged on top of NLO
matrix elements deserves special attention.
The key object in its construction is the local
k
-factor

k_{n}
which in turn contains lower-multiplicity Born-,

\mathbb{S}
-, and
\mathbb{H}
contributions, see Eq. (

).
For both possibilities to construct the correction
factors
\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}
that we discuss in the following,
we also detail
how EW corrections are included in
k_{n}
and how this
impacts the achieved accuracy.

Please note, for loop-induced processes we set all
\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}
to
zero and only effect the YFS soft-photon resummation.

\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation.

A first way to define the
\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}
is by using the exact
NLO EW virtual corrections, supplemented with real
corrections approximated in the soft-collinear limit
integrated over the extra-emission phase space.
This corresponds to the EW virtual approximation
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
introduced in Sec.
2.1
, with

\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{V}_{n}^{\mathrm{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)+\mathrm{I}_{n}^{\mathrm{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)}{\mathrm{B}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)}\;,\qquad\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)=0\;,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=0\;.

(3.11)

As remarked in Sec.
2.1
,
the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
is in practice computationally expensive, and therefore
the approximation is
restricted to low-multiplicity processes.
These are typically the ones that QCD
one-loop matrix elements are computed for,
i.e.

n\leq n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}
.
For the same reason the EW corrections for
\mathbb{H}
-event topologies
are neglected.
The higher-multiplicity LO processes receive an approximate
correction through the local
k
-factor
k_{m}
with
m=n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}

defined in Eq. (
3.8
).
With the definitions of Eq. (
3.11
), this
k
-factor
now takes the form

\begin{split}k_{\text{virt},m}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{m},\Phi_{m+1}\right)=&\;\frac{\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{m}\left(\Phi_{m}\right)\left(1+\delta_{\text{virt},m,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{m})\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)}{\vphantom{\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{m}\left(\Phi_{m}\right)\left(1+\delta_{\text{virt},m,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{m})\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)}\mathrm{B}_{m}\left(\Phi_{m}\right)}\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{H}_{m}\left(\Phi_{m+1}\right)}{\mathrm{B}_{m+1}\left(\Phi_{m+1}\right)}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{H}_{m}\left(\Phi_{m+1}\right)}{\mathrm{B}_{m+1}\left(\Phi_{m+1}\right)}\;.\end{split}

(3.12)

This has the effect that in
(n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}+l)
-jet events the underlying

n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}
-jet topology receives the correct EW correction.
In particular, the such-constructed approximate EW correction is
insensitive to additional jet production just above
Q_{\text{cut}}
.
However, once one of the additional jets enters the EW Sudakov regime,
for energy scales much larger than
Q_{\text{cut}}
, the effected correction will be incomplete.

An alternative variant of
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
is given by

\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{V}_{n}^{\mathrm{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)+\mathrm{I}_{n}^{\mathrm{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)}{\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)}\;.

(3.13)

This differs from Eq. (
3.11
) by terms of relative
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}\alpha\right)}
.

§

It is important to note that this definition represents
the original formulation of the method
[

]
,
and allows the incorporation of additional subleading

\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}^{-1}\alpha\right)}

with respect to
\mathrm{B}_{n}
, due to its complete cancellation
of
\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}
in the
\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\,(1+\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}})

Unwanted higher-order terms are thus avoided.

While a superficial correspondence of Eq. (
3.11
)
to the multiplicative and Eq. (
3.13
) to the
additive scheme used to combine QCD and EW corrections in
fixed-order calculations is tantalising, it is important to note
that these schemes combine relative correction factors
on the level of observable histograms, whereas the present
formulation operates point-wise in phase space prior to adding
higher-order QCD corrections through the parton shower.
As a result, both formulations automatically induce

\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}\alpha\right)}
relative to the Born

106
]

\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation.

Another way to define the corrections
\delta_{n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}
,
\delta_{n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}
and
\delta_{n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}
is given by the EW Sudakov approximation,
see
Sec.
2.1
,

\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{NLL}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n+1}^{\text{NLL}}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)\,.

(3.14)

Therein, the approximate NLO correction
\delta^{\text{EW}}_{\text{sud}}
contains
all contributions up to NLL in the high-energy limit of the one-loop
EW corrections to the
n
-jet process.
The evaluation of the Sudakov corrections is only slightly more involved than
the underlying LO matrix-element computations and can therefore be applied to
all contributions of all multiplicities used in a given calculation,
i.e.
Born-,
\mathbb{S}
-
and
\mathbb{H}
-type events.
In particular, all higher-multiplicity LO
contributions receive their own multiplicity-dependent correction
factor
\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}
.
The correction factors in the local
k
-factor
k_{n}
of Eq. (
3.12
)
cancel, thus avoiding a double counting of the higher-order EW effects.
In consequence, and in contrast to the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approach, the additional
LO multiplicities at
n>n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}
receive the complete EW Sudakov factor
when all jets are produced in the EW Sudakov regime.

Exponentiated
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
corrections.

The all-orders resummation of the NLL EW Sudakov logarithms is achieved by
the replacement
1+\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}\to\exp{\left(\delta_{n}^{\text{EW}}\right)}
throughout.
Though, as discussed in Sec.
2.1
, this does
not generally hold for the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation,
where a naive exponentiation would also include non-universal
finite terms, thus introducing an error that depends on their relative size.
However, the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation is ideally suited for this task.
In particular, in the case of a LO QCD calculation
we modify Eq. (
3.9
) to

\begin{split}\mathrm{B}_{n}\to\mathrm{B}_{n}\,\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right),\end{split}

(3.15)

while in the NLO QCD case, in analogy to Sec.
2.2
, Eq. (
3.10
) becomes

\begin{split}\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\,\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}_{n}\to\mathrm{H}_{n}\,\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\;.\end{split}

(3.16)

Matched NLO
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
+ NLL Sudakov.

Due to the absence of a suitable
matching implementation that achieves full NLO EW accuracy for
inclusive observables in the present formalism, we match the
resummed
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
corrections to the NLO
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
ones.
Although there is no improvement in the formal accuracy of such a
matching—both the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximations have formal
NLL accuracy in the EW Sudakov regime—such a matched calculation
benefits from the combination of the better handling of EW
renormalisation-scheme dependence and phenomenologically important
finite
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha\right)}
terms included in the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
scheme
on the one hand side, and the improved all-orders structure of
the resummed
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
corrections on the other.
We thus set

\begin{split}\mathrm{B}_{n}\to\mathrm{B}_{n}\,\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right),\end{split}

(3.17)

while in the NLO QCD case, Eq. (
3.10
)
becomes

\begin{split}\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\,\left[\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)-\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}+\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}\right]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{H}_{n}\to\mathrm{H}_{n}\,\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)\;.\end{split}

(3.18)

As is evident, neither the structure of the resummation nor that of
the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation at
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha\right)}
have been upset.
With the above choice and setting

\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}=\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathrm{B}}^{\text{EW}}=\delta_{\text{sud},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}=\delta_{\text{sud},n-1,\mathbb{H}}^{\text{EW}}=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{NLL}}
,
the local
k
-factor gets
modified to

\begin{split}k_{\text{matched},n}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n},\Phi_{n+1}\right)=&\;\frac{\overline{\mathrm{B}}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)\left[\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{n})\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)-\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{n})+\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{n})\right]}{\mathrm{B}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)\exp\left(\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}(\Phi_{n})\vphantom{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}\right)}\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)}{\mathrm{B}_{n+1}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)}\right)\\
&{}+\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)}{\mathrm{B}_{n+1}\left(\Phi_{n+1}\right)}\;.\\[-14.22636pt]
\end{split}

(3.19)

As
\delta_{\text{virt},n,\mathbb{S}}^{\text{EW}}
and
\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}
have the same formal
accuracy,
k_{\text{matched},n}^{\text{EW}}
contains no EW corrections
to NLL accuracy.
However, beyond the formal accuracy one can show that the inclusive
behaviour of the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation is preserved with the
above definitions, see App.
B
.

With the formulae presented so far, an automatic implementation of the full
matched result is in principle a technical matter only, which, however, we
leave for future work.

Soft-photon resummation.

The inherent approximation of the above
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
constructions can be partially unfolded again by
adding the effects of final-state photon radiation.
In particular, we use the soft-photon resummation in the YFS
scheme
[
92
]
. The
Sherpa
implementation described in

[
93
]
is restricted to photon emission off
final-state leptons in order not to interfere with the strongly
ordered resummation of QCD radiation in the parton shower.
For both same-flavour lepton pairs,
i.e.

e^{+}e^{-}
and
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,
it constructs a pseudo-resonant
Z
-boson decay and then
corrects the apparent LO decay width
\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{0}

to the all-orders resummed decay rate

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\Gamma^{\text{YFS}}=\;&\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{0}\cdot e^{\alpha Y(\omega_{\text{cut}})}\cdot\sum\limits_{n_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{n_{\gamma}!}\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n_{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}\Phi_{k_{i}}\cdot\alpha\,\tilde{S}(k_{i})\,\Theta(k_{i}^{0}-\omega_{\text{cut}})\right]\cdot\mathcal{C}\,.\end{split}

(3.20)

Therein, the YFS form factor
Y(\omega_{\text{cut}})
resums
unresolved real and virtual soft-photon corrections.
Individual resolved photons with momenta
k_{i}
are distributed according
to the eikonal
\tilde{S}(k_{i})
in their phase space
\Phi_{k_{i}}
.
The parameter
\omega_{\text{cut}}
separates the explicitly-generated resolved
from the integrated-over unresolved real-photon emission phase-space
regions. As default value we use
\omega_{\text{cut}}=1\,\text{MeV}
.
The correction factor
\mathcal{C}
contains exact higher-order
corrections which we incorporate up to NLO in QED.
¶
¶

¶

Although NNLO QED + NLO EW corrections are available for

Z\to\ell\ell
decays
[
107
]
, their impact
is numerically too insignificant to be included in this study.

It is important to note that the constructions used here,
LO
{}+{}
EW
{}_{\text{virt}}+{}
YFS and LO
{}+{}
EW
{}_{\text{sud}}+{}
YFS, do not achieve full
NLO EW accuracy.
In particular, there is an overlap in the virtual corrections
and the unresolved integrated real-emission corrections
generated in the YFS resummation and the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximations.
This overlap, however, is non-logarithmic in the high-energy
limit where our approximation is valid, and therefore does
not compromise its accuracy.
On the contrary, the addition of a detailed all-orders
description of final-state radiation allows for an accurate
calculation of realistic fiducial cross sections.

\begin{split}&q\,\bar{q}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,g\,,\quad g\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\\

In contrast to the inclusive case, new channels emerge, where the external
gluon gets replaced by a photon. So besides the canonical EW loops inserted in the LO
channels, also QCD-loop corrections to channels with external photons, contributing
at tree-level only in subleading orders, appear.
See Fig.

for examples of one-loop amplitudes corresponding to the
two types of processes.
New channels also open for the real corrections:

\begin{split}&q\,\bar{q}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,g\,\gamma\,,\quad g\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,\gamma\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\,,\quad\\
&\gamma\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,g\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\,,\quad\gamma\,g\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,q\,\bar{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\quad\text{at}\quad\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}\alpha^{5}\right)}\;.\end{split}

The last process, involving four external quarks and no external
photon, deserves an explicit discussion.
This contribution is completely infrared finite and, thus,
separable from the other processes.
It consists of an interference of diagrams of
\mathcal{O}{\left(g_{s}^{2}e^{4}\right)}

and
\mathcal{O}{\left(e^{6}\right)}
, commonly referred to as QCD production
and EW production, respectively.
Due to the colour algebra involved they typically entail

s
-/

-channel or
t
-/

-channel interferences.
Two examples are given in Fig.

It is noteworthy that, due to their nature as interference terms,
they are of indeterminate sign, and are typically small for inclusive
observables.
However, as they are the only contributions at this order which
can contain two valence quarks as initial states,
e.g.

uu
,
ud
, or
dd
, they can still be quite sizeable in the TeV range.

Although technically only a NNLO correction, gluon-induced loop-squared
contributions have a numerically important phenomenological impact,
both for inclusive and one-jet observables.
Therefore, in addition to the standard quark-induced four-lepton production
processes, we consider the gluon-induced loop-squared contributions as
one component of our multijet-merging approach.
Here, immediately the question of systematically separating both production
modes arises, as they coexist at higher orders that are at least
approximated in any calculation matched to a parton shower.
To this end, keeping in mind the parton shower’s generating functional
of Eq. (
3.3
) and following
[
104
]
,
we include all diagrams where all four leptons couple through one (or more)
electroweak gauge boson to the closed quark loop, but not any other
quarks in the process.
This excludes in particular diagrams where one lepton pair is radiated
off an external quark.
This means, that while

g\,g\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\quad\text{at}\quad\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}^{2}\alpha^{4}\right)}

solely constitutes inclusive loop-induced four-lepton production,

g\,g\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,g\,,\quad g\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\hskip-5.0pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{(}}}\hskip 3.7pt\text{\raisebox{0.75pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{)}}}}\quad\text{and}\quad q\,\bar{q}\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\,g\quad\text{at}\quad\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}^{3}\alpha^{4}\right)}

contribute for the jet-associated final state. Fig.
5

contains example Feynman diagrams contributing to the loop-induced modes
of hadronic
e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
(a) and
e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j
(b) production.
This comprises triangle-, box- and pentagon-type contributions.
We include light quarks and top quarks
in the closed fermion loops and allow for both double- and
single-resonant as well as Higgs-boson mediated topologies.
It is important to note, that in the single-resonant diagrams
the
Z
-boson couples through its axial component in triangle-like
and its vector component in box-like topologies.

Electroweak corrections to these loop-induced processes
are of two-loop complexity, they have not been calculated yet,
and consequently we do not consider them in this paper.

Fixed-order results

In this section we validate the approximations introduced in Sec.
3.2

applied to

and
pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j

The

and

as discussed in Sec.

in order to account for real-emission kinematics and effects.
We further provide predictions based on matching the full NLO EW result
with NLL EW Sudakov resummation, as an improved description including
the all-orders resummed EW corrections in the region where they are large.

For these calculations,
the renormalisation and factorisation scales are set
in accordance with Refs.
[

i.e.

\mu_{\mathrm{R}}=\mu_{\mathrm{F}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(E_{\mathrm{T},ee}+E_{\mathrm{T},\mu\mu}\right)\,,

Here,
m_{\ell\ell}
and

denote the invariant mass
and the transverse momentum of the off-shell charged dressed lepton pair.

Further, all inclusive cross sections are given both in the
G_{\mu}
scheme
and in the

scheme, with the former being our default choice
for the final predictions presented in Sec.

focusing on the EW Sudakov region where our matched result

and renormalisation scheme dependence.

4.4

Multijet-merged results

In this section we use the approximations validated for both

ZZ
and
ZZj
production in Sec.
4.3
to incorporate
higher-order electoroweak effects in a particle-level
calculation, as introduced in Sec.
3
.
Before we present our final results, however, we are
first proceeding with a structural analysis and validation
of our calculation in order to understand the interplay of each
contribution of the multijet-merged computation and their impact
on the final result.

Structural analysis and validation

The
MePs@Nlo
predictions are obtained by merging NLO
QCD
matrix elements
for
pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j

and the QCD tree-level matrix elements for

pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}jj
and
pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}jjj
.
As a way to study the interplay between QCD and EW corrections, we also provide
results at
MePs@Lo
accuracy, which we obtain by merging the same parton multiplicities

n=0,1,2,3
, but in this case all are evaluated at LO only,
i.e.

\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha_{s}^{n}\alpha^{4}\right)}
.
The merging is performed using the algorithms outlined in
Sec.
3.1
, with the merging scale set to

Q_{\text{cut}}=30\;\,\text{GeV}\,.

(4.8)

The renormalisation, factorisation, and resummation scales are set according
to the CKKW scale-setting prescription.
The renormalisation scale is thereby defined through

\mu_{\mathrm{R}}=\mu_{\text{CKKW}}

[
134
]
, with

\begin{split}\alpha_{s}^{n}(\mu_{\text{CKKW}}^{2})=&\;\alpha_{s}(t_{1})\cdots\alpha_{s}(t_{n})\;,\end{split}

(4.9)

where the
t_{i}
are the reconstructed shower-emission scales of the
n
-jet
hard-process configuration. The scale of the inner core process,
\mu_{\text{core}}
, is set to

\mu_{\text{core}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\left(E_{\mathrm{T},ee}+E_{\mathrm{T},\mu\mu}\right)\;,

(4.10)

and is used to define the factorisation and resummation scales via
\mu_{\mathrm{F}}=\mu_{\mathrm{Q}}=\mu_{\text{core}}
.
Lastly, note that the simulation of soft and collinear
photon emissions off the final-state leptons in the YFS formalism is enabled
for all results in this section.

We use the QCD
MePs@Nlo
prediction as a reference to determine the impact of approximate
EW corrections.
We incorporate EW correction in the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation into the underlying
MePs@Nlo
calculation,
both in the additive and the multiplicative scheme, as defined in Eq. (
3.13
)
and Eq. (
3.11
), respectively. Furthermore, we consider the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation as
an alternative.

Finally, we give the QCD
MePs@Lo
prediction,
both as-is, serving as an additional reference for the inclusive fiducial cross section,
and including
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
corrections.

prediction by the global QCD
k
-factor
given by the ratio of the inclusive fiducial cross sections
of the
MePs@Nlo
and
MePs@Lo

Note that
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
is not readily available for
this

given that it requires NLO EW one-loop matrix elements for all multiplicities
(

up to

-factor mechanism as described
for

in Eq. (

by both the

and

.

)

and/or

for

,

,

and all transverse-momentum distributions,

i.e.
whenever the observable favours
contributions from events with many hard and/or widely separated jets,
which are mainly generated by the
2j
and
3j
matrix
elements. While the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
falls back on the use of a

-factor
for these two contributions, we do not find evidence
that the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
generally begin to deviate
from one another in the regions dominated by high-multiplicity matrix elements,
suggesting that the use of a lower multiplicity (here:
1j
)
to calculate approximate EW corrections via the

-factor
does not introduce a large error for the observables studied here,
as is proven in App.

.

Finally, for the
MePs@Lo

The

and the fact that the contribution of

for the

The finding that
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
gives identical corrections in both cases indeed serves
as an important cross-check of our implementation.

Final results

After the structural analysis of the multijet-merged calculation
with respect to the inclusion of EW corrections through the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation,
we are now ready to present our final results,
which are directly relevant for comparisons to data.
To this end, we adjust our predictions with respect to the previous section
as follows:

•

The
MePs@Nlo
calculation remains the reference result,
but we add a scale-variation band to estimate
its theoretical uncertainty related to the choice of the QCD
renormalisation and factorisation scales
\mu_{\mathrm{R}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{F}}
.
The band is defined as the envelope of the 7-point variations
[
135
]

\left\{(\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{R}},\;\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{R}},\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(\mu_{\mathrm{R}},\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(\mu_{\mathrm{R}},\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(\mu_{\mathrm{R}},2\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(2\mu_{\mathrm{R}},\mu_{\mathrm{F}}),\;(2\mu_{\mathrm{R}},2\mu_{\mathrm{F}})\right\}\,,

that are evaluated using on-the-fly reweighting
[
136
]
.
The
\alpha_{\text{S}}
and PDF input scales in the parton shower are varied
along with the
\mu_{\mathrm{R}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{F}}
values used in the hard process.
Comparing the QCD scale uncertainties with the size of the EW corrections
allows us to assess the phenomenological relevance of the latter.

•

As a second addition in the QCD sector, we provide a
MePs@Loop
2
prediction,
merging loop-induced matrix elements for
ZZ
and
ZZj
production at LO.
This is added to the
MePs@Nlo
calculation.

•

We drop the additive

Conclusions

To adequately address precision measurements of final states produced
in hadronic collisions, the inclusion of higher-order corrections
in corresponding theoretical predictions based on perturbation theory
is required. Besides the dominant QCD contributions, typically considered at NLO
or NNLO, also EW corrections should ideally be included, certainly when
attempting to describe phase-space regions involving high-energetic particles.
In this limit the exact NLO EW corrections factorise from the production process,
leading to logarithms of the involved kinematic invariants that can eventually
be resummed to all orders.

In this article we discussed the means to compute EW corrections at exact NLO accuracy,
as well as the high-energy virtual EW (
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
) and Sudakov (
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
) approximations,
in the
Sherpa
framework. We have presented a simple matching formula for the combination
of resummed NLL EW Sudakov corrections with the exact
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha\right)}
result. This
NLO EW
{}+{}
NLL
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}}
calculation enables us to account for the dominant EW corrections both in the bulk of the
inclusive cross section as well as for high-energetic kinematic configurations.
Properly modelling multiple QCD parton emissions off the initial and final state
of the process requires the inclusion of parton-shower simulations,
i.e.
the
matching and merging of QCD matrix elements with a QCD parton cascade. Such methods
for LO and NLO matrix elements are readily available in
Sherpa
, referred to as

MePs@Lo
and
MePs@Nlo
, respectively. However, the consistent inclusion of (approximate)
EW corrections in such shower-evolved computations is an area of active theoretical
development.
In our work we have presented the methods available in
Sherpa
for the largely automated
incorporation of EW corrections in
MePs@Lo
and
MePs@Nlo
calculations. This includes in
particular the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation for
\mathcal{O}{\left(\alpha\right)}
corrections, and the more recently implemented

\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
scheme for including (resummed) NLL EW contributions. Both approaches can
consistently be combined with QED soft-photon resummation for final-state leptons,
treated in the YFS formalism.

As an highly non-trivial application and benchmark for the various calculational schemes
we analysed
ZZ
and
ZZj
production under LHC conditions, thereby including off-shell
and non-resonant contributions to the
e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

and
e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j
channels, respectively.
For the first time we have presented results for detailed kinematical distributions for

pp\to e^{+}e^{-}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}j
at NLO EW. In a first analysis we studied the quality of the

\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
schemes in approximating the exact NLO EW results, with one-loop
amplitudes provided by O

PEN

L

OOPS

and
Recola
. For both the

ZZ
and the
ZZj
channel we found a correction of the inclusive fiducial cross section
from LO to NLO of about
-7\text{\,}\%
when using the
G_{\mu}
input-parameter and renormalisation scheme.
This effect is well reproduced by the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
and
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximations when combined
with the YFS soft-photon resummation. While for the
considered inclusive cross sections the exponentiation of the NLL Sudakov logarithms has
only a very mild effect, it significantly alters the production rate of high-energetic event
configurations,
e.g.
when applying a hard cut on the transverse momentum of one
of the
Z
bosons. Through matching to the exact NLO EW result these higher-order EW
effects can be incorporated in the combined prediction of NLO EW
{}+{}
NLL
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}^{\text{exp}}
accuracy.

Motivated by the good quality of the EW virtual and Sudakov approximation for the
diboson-production processes, we then considered their inclusion in
MePs@Nlo
predictions based on the NLO QCD matrix elements for the zero- and one-jet channel,
supplemented by the tree-level two- and three-jet processes. Both for the inclusive
production rates and for the considered differential distributions, we found good agreement
of the resulting
MePs@Nlo
+
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
and
MePs@Nlo
+
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
predictions. Only for
phase-space regions dominated by the higher-order tree-level contributions did we observe
systematic deviations. These can be traced back to the limitation of the
\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation to model the EW corrections for these tree-level contributions
through an effective
k
-factor corresponding to the highest-multiplicity available
at NLO, here the one-jet process. In the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation, however, higher-order
tree-level processes with kinematics in the high-energy regime receive the complete
NLL EW Sudakov factor. Having analysed in detail the anatomy of the multijet-merged
samples with EW corrections included, we presented our final phenomenological
predictions, thereby also including an uncertainty estimate corresponding to scale
variations and taking into account the QCD loop-induced contributions to
ZZ
and
ZZj

production. The loop-induced channels contribute at the order of
10\text{\,}\%

of the total production rate only. Accordingly, neglecting their EW corrections seems well
justified. However, we could clearly show that EW corrections for the direct
production mode—in particular for high-energetic event configurations—amount to
up to
-40\text{\,}\%
, and thereby largely exceed the QCD scale uncertainty of
the
MePs@Nlo
computation.

Accordingly, the inclusion of EW corrections in theoretical predictions is of paramount
importance for upcoming measurements of diboson production at the LHC in Run 3 and the
subsequent High-Luminosity phase.
This applies both to fixed-order calculations and to fully differential multijet-merged
Monte Carlo simulations to be used in precision Standard Model studies.
The inclusion is similarly important for analyses
in which these processes contribute as Standard Model background to New Physics
searches.also Consequently, the methods developed here, due to their automation in the
Sherpa
framework,
can and should be applied to any other process.
They will be publicly available with
Sherpa
-3.

Appendix A
EW Sudakov corrections outside the strict high-energy limit

Following
[
41
,
42
]
,
the strict condition of the high-energy limit
that all invariants

r_{kl}=(p_{k}+p_{l})^{2}

with external momenta
p_{k}
and
p_{l}

are large compared to the EW energy scale
has the consequence that a naive application
to processes with intermediate resonances,
such as
W
and
Z
bosons decaying into leptons,
will result in
\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{NLL}}=0
.
This is because the intermediate
resonance will constrain the invariant
mass of the decay products
k
and
l
to be close
to its on-shell mass.
In consequence, at least this
r_{kl}
is of the
order of the EW scale and not large compared to it.
To address this,
we follow the same strategy as is used in the YFS
resummation in processes with internal resonances

[
93
,
53
]
.

We have extended the original
Sherpa
implementation of
Sudakov corrections
presented in
[
44
]
in the following way.
Before calculating
\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{NLL}}
,
we combine any lepton pair
(k,l)
with an invariant
mass
m_{kl}
close to the mass
M_{V}
(
V=W,Z
) of a vector boson
of the same quantum numbers, based on the measure

\Delta_{kl}=\frac{|m_{kl}-M_{V}|}{\Gamma_{V}}<\Delta_{\text{thr}}\;,

(A.1)

with
\Gamma_{V}
the width of the vector boson.

\Delta_{\text{thr}}
is an arbitrary threshold parameter that we set to
10
.
If multiple possible combinations exist,
e.g.
in
e^{+}e^{+}e^{-}e^{-}

final states, the pair with the smallest
\Delta_{kl}
is combined first.
The resulting amplitude now has
m\leq n
external particles. Note that this also implies that the
phase space used to evaluate the EW Sudakov contribution changes as well, and it
now refers to
m\leq n
(or
m\leq n+1
for
\mathbb{H}
events) final-state particles,
with
m
depending on the number of combined lepton pairs.
We then set

\delta_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{EW}}\left(\Phi_{n}\right)=\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},m}^{\text{NLL}}\left(\Phi_{m}\right)
.
The clustered phase space
\Phi_{m}
is constructed by combining the four momenta
of all clustered lepton pairs (
k
,
l
), and assign it to new external vector bosons,

i.e.

p_{V,kl}=p_{k}+p_{l}
.
After all possible daughter leptons are clustered,
all momenta in
\Phi_{m}
are reshuffled such that all external particles are on
their mass shell, in particular
p^{2}_{V,kl}=m^{2}_{V}
. To this end, we use the same algorithm
as in Ref.
[
44
]
to ensure that all matrix elements
entering the calculation
of
\mathrm{K}_{\text{sud},n}^{\text{NLL}}
have on-shell external momenta after potential
\text{SU}(2)
rotations
of external states, see the discussion therein.

As a second provision to improve the
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
approximation
in phase-space regions where not all invariants are of the same large size,
we include terms proportional to

L(|r_{kl}|,s)=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}\log^{2}\left(\frac{r_{kl}}{s}\right)\;,

(A.2)

as they appear in the split of the double logarithmic (DL) terms
in
[
41
]
.
Here,
s
is the squared partonic centre-of-mass energy.
The terms in Eq. (
A.2
)
are neglected in
[
41
]

and in our original implementation
[
44
]
,
as the strict high-energy limit requires
M_{W}^{2}\ll r_{kl}\sim s
.
However, we find that in the
ZZ
and
ZZj
observables studied here
neglecting these terms consistently reduces the level of agreement
between
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
and NLO EW by up to
5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
,
due to the contribution of kinematic configurations
where at least for some invariants
a looser high-energy limit

M_{W}^{2}\ll r_{kl}\ll s

is approached.

Hence, we have included these contributions in all
\text{EW}_{\text{sud}}
results
and made this behaviour the new default of the implementation in
Sherpa
.
Note that this term is also retained in
[
45
]

as part of the so-called
\mathrm{SSC}^{{s\rightarrow r}_{kl}}
contribution.

Matched EW corrections in multijet-merged calculations

This appendix discusses how the interplay of the EW corrections
contained in the local
k
-factor and those explicitly effected
onto the higher-multiplicity LO matrix elements achieves both

\text{EW}_{\text{virt}}
approximation
of the highest multiplicity it is available for.

n=n_{\text{max}}^{\text{NLO}}

respective Sudakov logarithms.

First, we show that for the highest multiplicity for which

3.18
).

n
-parton cross section

n
-parton cross section

(n+1)
-parton cross section.

(n+1)
-parton
cross section and the inclusive
(n+2)
-parton cross section
follows trivially and does not change our conclusions.

and

with

On an application of higher energies to Sidon sets This work is supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant 19–11–00001.
Shkredov I.D

Abstract




Annotation.

We show that for any finite set
A
and an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
there is
k=k(\varepsilon)
such that the higher energy
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)
is at most
|A|^{k+\varepsilon}
unless
A
has a very specific structure.
As an application we obtain that any finite subset
A
of the real numbers or the prime field either contains an additive Sidon–type subset of size
|A|^{1/2+c}
or a multiplicative Sidon–type subset of size
|A|^{1/2+c}
.

1
Introduction

Sidon sets is a classical object of Combinatorial Number theory, which was introduced by S. Sidon in
[
24
]
.
A subset
S
of an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
is a
Sidon set
iff all its non–zero differences are distinct.
Being ”more random than random”  this interesting class of sets were extensively studied by various authors, see
[
1
]
–
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
–
[
19
]
and many others papers.
Detailed information about Sidon sets can be found in survey
[
17
]
, for example.

Let
\mathsf{Sid}(A)
be size of the maximal (by cardinality) Sidon subset of a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
. If we want to underline the group operation we write
\mathsf{Sid}^{+}(A)
or
\mathsf{Sid}^{\times}(A)
.
In
[
11
]
(also, see paper
[
21
]
) it was proved that for any
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
one has

\mathsf{Sid}(A)\geqslant c\sqrt{|A|}\,,

(1)

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Of course, this result is tight (just take
A
equals a segment of integers to see that
\mathsf{Sid}^{+}(A)\ll\sqrt{|A|}
).
Oleksiy Klurman and Cosmin Pohoata (see
[
10
]
) asked the following sum–product-type question (on the sum–product phenomenon see, e.g.,
[
25
]
): is it true that bound (
1
) can be improved either for
\mathsf{Sid}^{+}(A)
or for
\mathsf{Sid}^{\times}(A)
, where
A
is any finite subset of the real numbers?

We write
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(A)
for size of maximal subset of
A
having at most
k
representations of any non–zero element as a difference.
Thus
\mathsf{Sid}_{1}(A)=\mathsf{Sid}(A)
and bound (
1
) cannot be improved for the quantity
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(A)
(again take
A
equals a segment of integers).
Our main result is

Theorem 1

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}
be a set, where
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
or
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
(in the prime field case suppose, in addition, that
|A|<\sqrt{p}
, say).
Then there are some absolute constants
c>0
,
K\geqslant 1
such that

\max\{\mathsf{Sid}^{+}_{K}(A),\mathsf{Sid}_{K}^{\times}(A)\}\gg|A|^{1/2+c}\,.

(2)

On the other hand, for any integer
k\geqslant 1
there is
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}
with

\max\{\mathsf{Sid}^{+}_{k}(A),\mathsf{Sid}^{\times}_{k}(A)\}\ll k^{1/2}|A|^{2/3}\,.

(3)

Oliver Roche-Newton and Audie Warren obtained a bound similar to (
3
) and another estimate of the same form was obtained by Green–Peluse, see
[
10
]

(also, see
[
6
, page 57]
).
Our construction is different from these counterexamples and we give our own proof of (
3
) at the end of section
3
for completeness.

Actually, Theorem
3
is a consequence of a more general fact about so–called
higher energies

[
20
]
(all required definitions can be found in section
2
).
We think that Theorem
2
below is interesting in its own right and it can find further applications in Additive Combinatorics.

Theorem 2

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be parameters.
Then there is
k=k(\delta,\varepsilon)
such that either
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
or there is
H\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|H|\gg|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)}
,
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|

and there exists
Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|Z||H|\ll|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
with
|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
.

In other words, we can always take large
k
to make
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)
as small as possible unless the set
A
has a very rigid structure.
It is easy to see (or consult
[
22
, Theorem 22]
) that
Theorem
2
is, actually, a criterion.

Finally, at our last section we study
k
–Sidon sets (and its generalizations), that is, sets
A
having at most
k
representations of any non–zero element as a difference.
This class of sets were introduced by Erdős in
[
6
]
and it is strongly connected with the higher energies.
We show that such sets are even more natural than usual
B_{2}[g]
–sets, see the definition from
[
17
]
.
In particular, size of such sets can be
estimated
relatively easily (unlike to
B_{2}[g]
–sets), they have heritability properties, they have a natural reinterpretation in terms of its Cayley graph and so on.

We thank Oliver Roche–Newton who communicated the question of Oleksiy Klurman and Cosmin Pohoata to the author.
Also, we thank him for very useful comments, discussions and remarks.

2
Definitions

By
{\mathbf{G}}
we denote an abelian group.
Sometimes we underline the group operation writing
+
or
\times
in the considered quantities (as the energy, the representation function and so on, see below).
Let
\mathbb{F}
be the field
{\mathbb{R}}
or
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for a prime
p
.

We use the same capital letter to denote set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}
and its characteristic function
A:\mathbb{F}\to\{0,1\}
.
Given two sets
A,B\subset{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
We write
\dotplus
for a direct sum, i.e.,
|A\dotplus B|=|A||B|
.
For an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}

the Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
) holds stating

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|\,,

(4)

where
n,m
are any positive integers.
We use representation function notations like
r_{A+B}(x)
or
r_{A-B}(x)
and so on, which counts the number of ways
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
can be expressed as a sum
a+b
or
a-b
with
a\in A
,
b\in B
, respectively.
For example,
|A|=r_{A-A}(0)
.

For any two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
the additive energy of
A
and
B
is defined by

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\mathsf{E}^{+}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}+b_{1}=a_{2}+b_{2}\}|\,.

If
A=B
, then we simply write
\mathsf{E}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}(A,A)
.
For
k\geqslant 2
put

\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)=\sum_{x}r^{k}_{A-A}(x)=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k-1}}|A\cap(A+\alpha_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+\alpha_{k-1})|^{2}\,.

(5)

Clearly,
|A|^{k}\leqslant\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+1}
.
Also, we write
\hat{\mathsf{E}}_{k}(A)=\sum_{x}r^{k}_{A+A}(x)
.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
All logarithms are to base
2
.
If we have a set
A
, then we will write
a\lesssim b
or
b\gtrsim a
if
a=O(b\cdot\log^{c}|A|)
,
c>0
.
Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
By
K_{s,t}
denote the complete subgraph with two parts of sizes
s
and
t
.

3
Proof of the main result

We
start with
a
probability lemma, which was known before in the case
k=2
, see
[
1
]
, say.
Roughly speaking, ignoring the presence of the parameter
k
in
\mathsf{Sid}_{O(k)}
from formula (
6
) below,
Lemma
6
works better than inequality (
1
) if
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\omega}

for a certain

\omega<1/2
.

Lemma 3

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Then for any
k\geqslant 2
one has

\mathsf{Sid}_{3k-3}(A)\gg\left(\frac{|A|^{2k}}{\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)}\right)^{1/(2k-1)}\,,\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad\mathsf{Sid}_{2k-2}(A)\gg\left(\frac{|A|^{2k}}{\hat{\mathsf{E}}_{k}(A)}\right)^{1/(2k-1)}\,.

(6)

P r o o f.

Form a random set
A_{*}\subseteq A

piking
elements of
A_{*}
from
A
independently at random with probability
q
.
Then the expectation of the solutions to the equation

\mathsf{E}^{\prime}_{k}(A):=|\{x_{1}-x^{\prime}_{1}=\dots=x_{k}-x^{\prime}_{k}~:~x_{j},x^{\prime}_{j}\in A,\,~~x_{j},x^{\prime}_{j}\mbox{ are different}\}|

(7)

is

q^{2k}\mathsf{E}^{\prime}_{k}(A)\leqslant q^{2k}\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)
.
If
q^{2k}\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant q|A|/2
, then we can delete roughly a half of elements of
A_{*}
to
find a subset
B
of
A_{*}
,
|B|\gg|A_{*}|
, having no solutions to equation (
7
).
Let us take any
z\neq 0
and prove that
r_{B-B}(z)<3k-2:=g
.
Suppose that for some
x_{j},x^{\prime}_{j}\in B
the following holds

0\neq z=x_{1}-x^{\prime}_{1}=\dots=x_{g}-x^{\prime}_{g}\,.

(8)

Clearly, any

x_{j}
or
x^{\prime}_{j}
presents in (
8
) in at most two equations and hence a pair
(x_{j},x^{\prime}_{j})
presents in at most three equations.
Also, by the definition of the set
B
any
k
equations from (
8
) must have some equal variables.
It follows that
r_{B-B}(z)\leqslant 3\cdot(k-1)
as required.
Finally, taking
q=(|A|\mathsf{E}^{-1}_{k}(A)/2)^{1/(2k-1)}
, we obtain
our
result.
The second bound in (
6
) can be obtained similarly.
In this case the correspondent analogue of (
8
) is

x_{1}+x^{\prime}_{1}=\dots=x_{g}+x^{\prime}_{g}

(9)

and we see that a pair
(x_{j},x^{\prime}_{j})
presents in at most two equations from (
9
).
Again by the definition of the set
B
any
k
equations from (
9
) must have some equal variables.
It follows that
r_{B+B}(z)\leqslant 2k-2
as required.

\hfill\Box

We now obtain our driving result (Theorem
2
from the introduction is a particular case of Theorem
4
).
The proof is in the spirit of
[
22
, Theorem 21]
.

Theorem 4

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be parameters,
\varepsilon\leqslant\delta
.

1)~
Then there is
k=k(\delta,\varepsilon)=\exp(O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta)))
such that either
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
or there is
H\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|H|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)}
,
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|

and there exists
Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|Z||H|\ll|A|^{1+\varepsilon}

with

|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}\,.

2)~
Similarly, either there is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
and
P\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|P|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta}
such that for all
x\in A^{\prime}
one has
r_{A-P}(x)\gg|P||A|^{-\varepsilon}
or
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
with
k\ll 1/\varepsilon
.

P r o o f.

Let
\mathsf{E}_{l}=\mathsf{E}_{l}(A):=|A|^{l+\kappa_{l}}
, where
\kappa_{l}\in[0,1]
and
l\geqslant 2
be any integer.
We assume that
\kappa_{l}\geqslant\delta
.
By the pigeon–hole principle there is a number
\Delta>0
and a set
P\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
such that
P=\{x~:~\Delta<r_{A-A}(x)\leqslant 2\Delta\}

and
\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\lesssim\Delta^{l+1}|P|
.
In particular,

|P|\gtrsim\mathsf{E}_{l+1}|A|^{-(l+1)}=|A|^{\kappa_{l}}\geqslant|A|^{\delta}\,.

(10)

Let
M
be a parameter, which we will choose later in an appropriate way in each case
1)
and
2)
.
Suppose that

\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\geqslant\frac{|A|\mathsf{E}_{l}}{M}\,.

(11)

Then

\frac{|A|\Delta^{l}|P|}{M}\leqslant\frac{|A|\mathsf{E}_{l}}{M}\leqslant\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\lesssim\Delta^{l+1}|P|\,.

(12)

Hence

\frac{|A||P|}{M}\lesssim\sum_{x\in P}r_{A-A}(x)=\sum_{a\in A}|A\cap(P+a)|\,,

(13)

and the inequality
\Delta\gtrsim|A|/M
follows from (
12
).
Take
A^{\prime}=\{a\in A~:~|A\cap(P+a)|\gtrsim|P|/CM\}
with a sufficiently large constant
C
.
Then from (
13
), we get
|A^{\prime}|\gtrsim|A|/M
and by the definition of the set
A^{\prime}
, we have
r_{A-P}(x)\gtrsim|P|/M
for all
x\in A^{\prime}
.
To obtain
2)
we put
M=|A|^{\varepsilon/2}
and we are done under assumption (
11
).
Now suppose that
\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\leqslant|A|\mathsf{E}_{l}/M
.
Then apply the previous argument to
\mathsf{E}_{l}
instead of
\mathsf{E}_{l+1}
.
Again, if
\mathsf{E}_{l}\geqslant|A|\mathsf{E}_{l-1}/M
, then we are done otherwise
\mathsf{E}_{l}\leqslant|A|\mathsf{E}_{l-1}/M
and we repeat the argument.
Clearly, after at most
k\ll 1/\varepsilon
steps our algorithm finishes and we obtain
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
.

It remains to obtain
1)
.
Returning to (
13
) and using the Hölder inequality several times, we get

\frac{|A||P|^{2}}{M^{2}}\lesssim\mathsf{E}(A,P)=\sum_{x}r_{A-A}(x)r_{P-P}(x)\,,

and hence

\left(\frac{|A||P|^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)^{l+1}\lesssim\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\left(\sum_{x}r^{1+1/l}_{P-P}(x)\right)^{l}\lesssim|P|\Delta^{l+1}\cdot\mathsf{E}(P)|P|^{2l-2}\,.

In other words,

\mathsf{E}(P)\gtrsim\frac{|P|^{3}}{M^{2l+2}}:=\frac{|P|^{3}}{M_{*}}\,.

By the Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers Theorem (e.g., see,
[
25
]
), we find
H\subseteq P
,
|H|\gg|P|/M^{C}_{*}
,
|H+H|\ll M^{C}_{*}|H|
, where
C>0
is an absolute constant which may change from line to line.
As above

|A||H|/M\lesssim|H|\Delta\ll\sum_{x\in H}r_{A-A}(x)=\sum_{a\in A}|A\cap(H+a)|\,.

Again, we define
W\subseteq A
similarly to the set
A^{\prime}
and obtain in particular, that
|W|\gtrsim|A|/M
.
Also, let
Z\subseteq W
be a set such that the sets
\{H+z\}_{z\in Z}
form the maximal system of disjoint sets.
By maximality,
W\subseteq Z+H-H
and hence by the Plünnecke inequality (
4
), we get

|A|/M\lesssim|W|\leqslant|Z||H-H|\ll M^{C}_{*}|Z||H|

and
thus

|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|=\sum_{z\in Z}|A\cap(H+z)|\gtrsim|Z||H|/M\gtrsim|A|/M^{C}_{*}\,.

In particular,

M|A|\gtrsim|Z||H|\,.

We now put
M=|A|^{\frac{\varepsilon\kappa_{l}}{C_{*}l}}
, where
C_{*}>0
is a sufficiently large constant.
Hence we get
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|
and
|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
.
Also, in view of (
10
) one has

|H|\gg|P|/M^{C}_{*}\gtrsim|A|^{\delta}/M^{C_{*}}\gtrsim|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)}

as required.
Now suppose that inequality (
11
) fails, i.e.,
\mathsf{E}_{l+1}\leqslant|A|\mathsf{E}_{l}/M
.
It gives us
\kappa_{l+1}\leqslant\kappa_{l}(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{*}l})
and thus iterating, we see that our algorithm stops after at most
\exp(O(\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta)))
steps.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Remark 5

Notice that Lemma
6
, as well as the first part of Theorem
4
work for sums as for differences and thus the first part of Theorem
3
can be obtained
for the quantity
\mathsf{Sid}^{*}_{k}
defined via pluses but not differences.

A consequence of Theorem
4
is Corollary
6
below, which is a direct result having no the sum–product flavour.
Roughly speaking, it says that basic estimate (
1
) can be easily improved for a wide class of sets, if we
ignore the presence of the parameter
K
in formula (
14
).

Corollary 6

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
\delta\in(0,1/2)
be a parameter.
Then either for some
K=K(\delta)
,
c=c(\delta)

the following holds

\mathsf{Sid}_{K}(A)\gg|A|^{1/2+c}

(14)

or there is a set of shifts
T
,
|T|\gg|A|^{\delta/8}

such that for any
t\in T
one has
|A\cap(A+t)|\gg|A|^{1-\delta}
.

P r o o f.

We apply the first part of Theorem
4
with
\delta=\delta/2
and
\varepsilon=\delta/16
.
If the first alternative holds, then we are done in view of Lemma
6
because
\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
implies (
14
).
Otherwise there is
H\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|H|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)/2}
,
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|

and there exists
Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|Z||H|\ll|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
with
|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}\,.

For any
z\in Z
put
A_{z}=A\cap(H+z)\subseteq A
.
Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality several times, we have

\sum_{x}r_{A-A}(x)r_{H-H}(x)=\mathsf{E}(A,H)\geqslant\sum_{z\in Z}\mathsf{E}(A_{z},H)\gg\sum_{z\in Z}\frac{|A_{z}|^{2}|H|^{2}}{|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|}\gg

\gg(|H||Z|)^{-1}|A|^{2-3\varepsilon}|H|^{2}\gg|A|^{1-4\varepsilon}|H|^{2}\,.

(15)

Taking
T=\{t\in{\mathbf{G}}~:~|A\cap(A+t)|\gg|A|^{1-4\varepsilon}\}
, we obtain from (
15
) that

|T|\gg|H||A|^{-4\varepsilon}\gg|A|^{\delta/8}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to obtain bound (
2
) of Theorem
3
.
We give even two proofs using both statements of our driving Theorem
4
.

P r o o f.

Take any
\delta<1/2
, e.g.,
\delta=1/4
and let
\varepsilon\leqslant\delta/4
be a parameter, which we will choose later.
In view of Lemma
6
we see that
\mathsf{E}^{+}_{k}(A)\leqslant|A|^{k+\delta}
implies

\mathsf{Sid}^{+}_{3k-3}(A)\gg|A|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1-2\delta}{2(2k-1)}}=|A|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4(2k-1)}}

(16)

and we are done.
Here
k=k(\varepsilon)
.
Otherwise there is
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}
,
|H|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta(1-\varepsilon)}\geqslant|A|^{\delta/2}
,
|H+H|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H|

and there exists
Z\subseteq\mathbb{F}
,
|Z||H|\ll|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
with
|(H\dotplus Z)\cap A|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}\,.

Put
A_{*}=(H\dotplus Z)\cap A
,
|A_{*}|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
and we want to estimate
\mathsf{E}^{\times}_{l+1}(A_{*})
or
\hat{\mathsf{E}}_{l+1}^{\times}(A_{*})
for large
l
.
After that having a good upper bound for
\mathsf{E}^{\times}_{l+1}(A_{*})
or
\hat{\mathsf{E}}_{l+1}^{\times}(A_{*})
, we apply Lemma
6
again to find large multiplicative Sidon subset of
A_{*}
.

First of all, notice that
|A_{*}+H|\leqslant|H+H||Z|\ll|A|^{\varepsilon}|H||Z|\ll|A|^{1+2\varepsilon}
.
In other words, the set
A_{*}
almost does not grow after the addition with
H
.
Let
Q=A_{*}+H
,
|Q|\ll|A|^{1+2\varepsilon}
.
Secondly, fix any
\lambda\neq 0
.
The number of the solutions to the equation
a_{1}a_{2}=\lambda
, where
a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{*}
does not exceed

\sigma_{\lambda}:=|H|^{-2}|\{h_{1},h_{2}\in H,\,q_{1},q_{2}\in Q~:~(h_{1}-q_{1})(h_{2}-q_{2})=\lambda\}|\,.

The last equation can be interpreted as a question about the number of incidences between points and modular hyperbolas (see
[
23
]
) and for each non–zero
\lambda
the quantity
\sigma_{\lambda}
can be estimated as

\sigma_{\lambda}\ll|H|^{-2}\cdot|Q||H|^{2-\kappa}\ll|A|^{1+2\varepsilon}|H|^{-\kappa}\,,

see
[
14
]
in the case
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
and
[
23
, Theorem 22]
in the case of the prime field.
Here
\kappa=\kappa(\delta)>0
.
Recalling that
|H|\gg|A|^{\delta/2}
,
|A_{*}|\gg|A|^{1-\varepsilon}
and taking any
\varepsilon\leqslant\delta\kappa/100
, we obtain after some calculations that
\sigma_{\lambda}\ll|A_{*}|^{1-\delta\kappa/4}
.
Hence taking sufficiently large
l\gg(\delta\kappa)^{-1}
, we derive

\hat{\mathsf{E}}_{l+1}^{\times}(A_{*})=\sum_{\lambda}r^{l+1}_{A_{*}A_{*}}(\lambda)\ll|A_{*}|^{l+1}+(|A_{*}|^{1-\delta\kappa/2})^{l}|A_{*}|^{2}\ll|A_{*}|^{l+1}+|A|^{l+2-\delta\kappa l/2}\ll|A_{*}|^{l+1}\,.

Applying Lemma
6
and choosing
\varepsilon\ll l^{-1}
, we see that

\mathsf{Sid}^{\times}_{2l}(A)\geqslant\mathsf{Sid}^{\times}_{2l}(A_{*})\gg|A_{*}|^{\frac{l+1}{2l+1}}\gg|A|^{\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(l+1)}{2l+1}}=|A|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1-2\varepsilon(l+1)}{2(2l+1)}}\gg|A|^{\frac{1}{2}+c}\,,

where
c=c(\delta)>0
is an absolute constant.

Now let us give the second proof applying the last part of Theorem
4
.
The argument is almost the same but we estimate
\hat{\mathsf{E}}^{\times}_{l+1}(A^{\prime})
.
Again, the number of the solutions to the equation
a_{1}a_{2}=\lambda
, where
a_{1},a_{2}\in A^{\prime}
does not exceed

\sigma_{\lambda}:=(|P||A|^{-\varepsilon})^{-2}\cdot|\{a_{1},a_{2}\in A,\,p_{1},p_{2}\in P~:~(a_{1}-p_{1})(a_{2}-p_{2})=\lambda\}|\,.

By results from
[
14
]
,
[
23
]
one has

\sigma_{\lambda}\ll(|P||A|^{-\varepsilon})^{-2}\cdot|A||P|^{2-\kappa}\ll|A|^{1+2\varepsilon}|P|^{-\kappa}\,,

where
\kappa=\kappa(\delta)>0
.
Again
|P|\gtrsim|A|^{\delta}
and
we can use the arguments as above.
This
concludes
the proof.

\hfill\Box

To complete the proof of Theorem
3
we need a simple lemma on upper estimates of sizes of Sidon sets in sumsets.

Lemma 7

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
A=B+C
, and
k\geqslant 1
be an integer.
Then

\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(A)\leqslant\min\{|C|\sqrt{k|B|}+|B|,|B|\sqrt{k|C|}+|C|\}\,.

More generally, if
r_{B+C}(a)\geqslant\sigma
for any
a\in A
, then

\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(A)\leqslant\sigma^{-1}\min\{|C|\sqrt{k|B|}+|B|,|B|\sqrt{k|C|}+|C|\}\,.

P r o o f.

We give two proofs.
The first proof uses theory of graphs and it demonstrates transparently how
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}
–sets are naturally connected with Cayley graphs.

Let
\Lambda
be the maximal
subset of
A
such that
r_{\Lambda-\Lambda}(x)\leqslant k
for any
x\neq 0
.
Consider the graph
G=G(V,E)
, where
V
is the disjoint union of
B
and
C
, the edge
(b,c)\in E
iff
b+c\in\Lambda
.
Moreover, we assume that
|E|=|\Lambda|
ignoring elements of
\Lambda
, which have several representations as
b+c
.
Using the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we obtain

|\Lambda|^{2}\leqslant|B|\sum_{b\in B}\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}E(b,c)E(b,c^{\prime})=

=|B|\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C}\,\sum_{b\in B}E(b,c)E(b,c^{\prime})=|B||\Lambda|+|B|\sum_{c,c^{\prime}\in C,\,c\neq c^{\prime}}\,\sum_{b\in B}E(b,c)E(b,c^{\prime})\,.

If the last sum over
b
is at least
k+1
, then we find a
complete bipartite subgraph
K_{2,k+1}

in
G
and hence there are different elements
\lambda_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k+1},\lambda^{\prime}_{k+1}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda_{1}-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{\prime}_{k+1}
. The last fact contradicts the assumption that
r_{\Lambda-\Lambda}(x)\leqslant k
for any
x\neq 0
.
Hence

|\Lambda|^{2}\leqslant|B|\leqslant|B||\Lambda|+k|B||C|^{2}

as required.

To obtain the second part of our lemma we use a little bit different method.
Again, let
\Lambda
be the set as before.
Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

(\sigma|\Lambda|)^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{S}^{2}:=\left(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}r_{B+C}(x)\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{b\in B}r_{\Lambda-C}(b)\right)^{2}\leqslant|B|\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}|B\cap(x-C)\cap(y-C)|\leqslant

\leqslant|B|\mathcal{S}+|B|\sum_{x\neq y\in\Lambda}|(x-C)\cap(y-C)|\leqslant|B|\mathcal{S}+k|B||C|^{2}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we can easily obtain a non–trivial upper bound for size of maximal Sidon set in any difference set or sumset.

Corollary 8

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set and
D=A-A
,
S=A+A
.
Then for any positive integer
k
one has
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(D)\ll\sqrt{k}\min\{|A|^{3/2},\sqrt{|D|^{3}}|A|^{-1}\}
and

\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(S)\ll\sqrt{k}\min\{|A|^{3/2},|A|^{-1}\min\{|D|\sqrt{|S|}+|S|,|S|\sqrt{|D|}+|D|\}\}\,.

P r o o f.

The bound
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(D),\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(S)\ll\sqrt{k}|A|^{3/2}
follows immediately from the first part of Lemma
7
.
Further it is easy to see (or consult
[
20
]
) that for any
d\in D
one has
r_{D-D}(d)\geqslant|A|
.
Applying the second part of Lemma
7
with
A=B=C=D
and
\sigma=|A|
, we obtain
\mathsf{Sid}_{k}(D)\ll\sqrt{k|D|^{3}}|A|^{-1}
.
To prove the second part of our lemma notice that for any
s\in S
the following holds
r_{D+S}(s)\geqslant|A|
.
This
concludes
the proof.

\hfill\Box

In the case

we put

4
On
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
–sets

In the previous section we
have obtained some results about the family of sets
S
with

r_{S-S}(x)\leqslant g\,,\quad\quad\forall x\in{\mathbf{G}}\,,\quad x\neq 0\,.

(17)

This class of sets were introduced by Erdős in
[
6
, page 57]

(also, see
[
7
]
)
and we denote this family as
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
(Erdős used the symbol
B^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{2}
).
It was said in
[
6
]
that ”V.T. Sós and I considered
B^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{2}
sequences… We could not decide whether there is a
B^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{2}
sequence which is not the union of a finite number of Sidon sequences.” According to the author’s knowledge this paper of Erdős and Sós was not published.
The question
from
[
6
]
is
a nice problem of Erdős, which is open and if it has
a negative
answer, then the original question of
Klurman–Pohoata
would be closed thanks to our Theorem
3
.
Let us underline it one more time that it is possible to construct sets
S
with bounded
r_{S+S}(x)
which are not the union of a finite number of Sidon sequences (see
[
6
]
and
[
1
]
).
It seems like
condition (
17
) has another nature
and that is why
we devote this section studying some further properties of
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
–sets.
To see that this is a special family, notice that, for example, the condition
r_{S-S}(x)\ll 1
,
x\neq 0
has an interpretation in terms of the Cayley graph of
S
but
r_{S+S}(x)\ll 1

cannot be expressed in terms of any Cayley graph.

First of all, notice that if
S
is a random subset of
[N]
, which was obtained piking elements from
[N]
independently at random with probability
q\sim N^{-1/2}
, then
with probability
1-o(1)
one has
r_{S-S}(x)\ll\log N
see, e.g.,
[
5
, Lemma 4.3]
and a similar lower bound for the function
r_{S-S}(x)
takes place.
Thus for any fixed
g
sets belonging to
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]

are far
from random sets.

Secondly, as it was noted in the proof of Lemma
7
a set
S
belongs to the family
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
iff its Cayley graph
\mathsf{Cay}(S,{\mathbf{G}})
has no complete bipartite subgraphs
K_{2,g+1}
.
Recall that the vertex set of
\mathsf{Cay}(A,{\mathbf{G}})
is
{\mathbf{G}}
and
(x,y)
is an edge of
\mathsf{Cay}(A,{\mathbf{G}})
iff
x-y\in A
.
Another equivalent interpretation of (
17
) is

|S\cap(S+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x_{g})|\leqslant 1\quad\quad\mbox{for all distincts and non--zero }\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{g}\in{\mathbf{G}}\,.

(18)

This reinterpretation of
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
–sets says that
the considered
family is naturally connected with the higher energies
[
20
]
(also, see the second formula in definition (
5
)).
Also, if we put for an arbitrary
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}

\Delta_{g}(A):=\{(a,\dots,a)\in A^{g}~:~a\in A\}\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}^{g}\,,

then (
18
) means that the sum
S^{g}
and
\Delta_{g}(S)
is direct, in other words,
S^{g}
and
\Delta_{g}(S)
form a
co–Sidon pair
, see
[
5
]
.
Further formula (
18
) suggests us the following definition for
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
sets

|S\cap(S+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x_{g})|<k\quad\quad\mbox{for all distincts and non--zero }\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{g}\in{\mathbf{G}}\,.

(19)

Similarly,
S\in B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
iff
\mathsf{Cay}(S,{\mathbf{G}})
does not contain
K_{k,g+1}
or, in other words,
S
does not contain any sumsets
X+Y
, where
|X|=g+1
,
Y=k
.
Since the number of edges in
\mathsf{Cay}(S,{\mathbf{G}})
equals
|S||{\mathbf{G}}|
for any finite group
{\mathbf{G}}
, it follows that to estimate size of
S

it is enough to bound the number of edges in
K_{k,g+1}
–free graphs.
Such results are discussed in detail in survey
[
8
]
, for example.
Of course there is a direct approach to estimate cardinality of
S\in B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
.
Namely, if
S\in B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
belongs to a group
{\mathbf{G}}
of size
N
, then, obviously,

|S|^{2}=\sum_{x}r_{S-S}(x)\leqslant|S|+g(N-1)

and hence

|S|<\sqrt{gN}+1\,.

(20)

Similarly, if
S\in B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
and
S
belongs to a group
{\mathbf{G}}
of size
N
, then

|S|^{g+1}=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{g}}|S\cap(S+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x_{g})|<kN^{g}+\binom{g+1}{2}|S|^{g}

and thus
|S|<k^{\frac{1}{g+1}}N^{\frac{g}{g+1}}+\binom{g+1}{2}
.
Of course in the case when
S\subseteq[N]
there are no such good upper bounds for size of
S
even if
S
is a classical Sidon set.
Nevertheless, we easily obtain a generalization of Linstrom’s result
[
13
]
for
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
–sets in the segment (as well as for
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
–sets but it is not the main topic of our paper, for better bounds see
[
16
]
).

Proposition 9

Let
S\subseteq[N]
belongs to the family
B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
.
Then

|S|<\sqrt{gN}+(gN)^{1/4}+1\,.

(21)

Generally, if
S\in B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
, then

|S|<k^{\frac{1}{g+1}}N^{\frac{g}{g+1}}+\binom{g+1}{2}^{\frac{1}{g+1}}k^{\frac{g}{(g+1)^{2}}}N^{\frac{g^{2}}{(g+1)^{2}}}+1\,.

(22)

P r o o f.

We use the method from
[
9
]
.
Let
u=[N^{3/4}g^{-1/4}]
be a parameter and
I=[u]
.
Embed
S
into
\mathbb{Z}/(N+u)\mathbb{Z}
. It is easy to check that for any
x\in[-u,u]\setminus\{0\}
one has
r_{S-S}(x)\leqslant g
, where now
x
runs over
\mathbb{Z}/(N+u)\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to estimate the common energy
\mathsf{E}(S,I)
, we get

\frac{|S|^{2}u^{2}}{N+u}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(S,I)=\sum_{x}r_{S-S}(x)r_{I-I}(x)<|S|u+gu^{2}

or, in other words,

gu^{2}+u(gN+|S|-|S|^{2})+|S|N>0

and substituting
u=[N^{3/4}g^{-1/4}]<N^{3/4}g^{-1/4}
and
|S|=\sqrt{gN}+(gN)^{1/4}+C
, we find after some calculations that the condition
C\leqslant 1
is enough.
To obtain (
22
) we use a similar argument (with another parameter
u
, of course) to estimate an analogue of the higher common energy of
S
and
I
, namely,

\frac{(|S|u)^{g+1}}{(N+u)^{g}}\leqslant\sum_{x}r_{S-I}^{g+1}(x)=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{g}}|S\cap(S+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x_{g})||I\cap(I+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(I+x_{g})|<

(23)

<ku^{g+1}+\binom{g+1}{2}\sum_{x}r_{S-I}^{g}(x)\leqslant ku^{g+1}+\binom{g+1}{2}|S|^{g}u:=ku^{g+1}+C_{g}|S|^{g}u\,.

We have used in (
23
) that all variables
x_{j}
belong to
[-u,u]
.
Finally, in the case of
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
–sets
an appropriate
choice of the parameter
u
is
u=[C^{1/(g+1)}_{g}k^{-1/(g+1)^{2}}N^{1-g/(g+1)^{2}}]
and after some calculations we obtain (
22
) (we roughly estimate the third term in this formula by one and actually, we do not optimize the constant in the middle term of (
22
), taking it for the simplest way to check).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

As for lower bounds on size of maximal subsets of
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
, again one can consult
[
8
]
to find correspondent lower bound for the number of edges in graphs having no
K_{s,t}
.
Our graphs must be
Cayley graphs
and such constructions are known for
K_{2,2}
,
K_{3,3}
(Brown’s construction, see
[
2
]
) and for
K_{s,t}
,
t\geqslant s!+1
(so–called, norm–graphs), see
[
8
]
.
As for
K_{2,t}
,
t>2
one can obtain a result similar to
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
.
Namely, define for any
g
the quantity

\alpha_{g}=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\max\{|S|~:~S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\,,S\in B^{\circ}_{2}[g]\}}{\sqrt{N}}\,.

Theorem 10

We have

\alpha_{g}=\sqrt{g}+O(g^{3/10})\,.

P r o o f.

Actually, our argument almost coincides with the approach of the proof
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
, so we omit the details.

By the method of
[
4
, section 4]
it is enough to construct a set
A\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}
(here
p
is a prime number) such that
A\in B^{\circ}_{2}[g]
with
g=k^{2}+O(k^{3/2})
and
|A|=kp+O(k)
.
We put
A=\bigsqcup_{u\in U}A_{u}
, where
U
is an appropriate arithmetic progression,
U=t+[k]
and for any non–zero
u\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
we define

A_{u}=\{(x,x^{2}/u)~:~x\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}\,.

Clearly,
|A|=kp-k+1
.
Let
r_{u,v}(x)=r_{A_{u}-A_{v}}(x)
.
Our task is to estimate for any
x
the sum

r_{A-A}(x)\leqslant\sum_{u,v\in U}r_{u,v}(x)\,.

By
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
one has
r_{u,v}(x)+r_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}(x)=2
, provided
u+v=u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}
and
\left(\frac{uvu^{\prime}v^{\prime}}{p}\right)=-1
.
Using this lemma and acting exactly as on pages 2794–2795 of
[
4
]
, we find

r_{A-A}(x)\leqslant k^{2}+\sum_{|l|<k}\left|\sum_{i+j=k+1+l}\left(\frac{(t+i)(t+j)}{p}\right)\right|\,.

After that taking the summation over
t
(to find an appropriate
U
) and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Weil’s bound on sum of Legendre symbols, we obtain the required estimate (see the rest of the argument from
[
4
]
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Finally, let us mention that the construction of Linström

For different

x_{1}\dots,x_{s}
and any set

Taking the same set

In particular, in Brown’s construction

Proposition 11

Let
S\in B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
,
l\geqslant 2
and take any sets
X_{1},\dots,X_{l}
with
|X_{1}|+\dots+|X_{l}|\geqslant g+\binom{l}{2}+1

forms a co–Sidon pair.
Then

|S_{X_{1}}\cap(S_{X_{2}}+z_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S_{X_{l}}+z_{l-1})|<k\quad\quad\mbox{ for any different }\quad\quad z_{1},\dots,z_{l-1}\neq 0\,.

(24)

In particular, if
S\in B^{\circ}_{2}[2]
and
{\mathbf{G}}
has no elements of order two, then for any non–zero

the set
S_{w}=S\cap(S+w)
is a Sidon set.

P r o o f.

Let
X_{j}=\{x^{(j)}_{1},\dots,x^{(j)}_{s_{j}}\}
,
j\in[l]
.
For an arbitrary
z\in{\mathbf{G}}
, we have

\mathcal{S}:=S_{X_{1}}\cap(S_{X_{2}}+z_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S_{X_{l}}+z_{l-1})=

(S+x^{(1)}_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x^{(1)}_{s_{1}})\cap(S+x^{(2)}_{1}+z_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x^{(2)}_{1}+z_{1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x^{(l)}_{1}+z_{l-1})\cap\dots\cap(S+x^{(l)}_{1}+z_{l-1})\,.

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{l}s_{j}\geqslant g+\binom{l}{2}+1

.

but

In the case

We finish this section discussing the

of Lemma

.
In the next proposition we show that any set
A
contains rather large (in terms of its energy) subset with controllable size of the maximal
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]

Proposition 12

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Then there is
A_{*}\subseteq A
such that
\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A_{*})\gg_{g}\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)
and
the maximal
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
subset of
A_{*}
has size
O_{g}(k^{1/(g+1)}|A|^{2}\mathsf{E}^{-1/(g+1)}_{g+1}(A))
.

In particular, for any
n
one has
\mathsf{Sid}_{n}(A_{*})\ll n^{1/2}|A|^{2}\mathsf{E}^{-1/2}(A)
.

P r o o f.

We have

\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)=\sum_{y}r^{g+1}_{A-A}(y)=\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{x\in A}r^{g}_{A-A}(x-a)\,.

Put
A_{*}=\{a\in A~:~\sum_{x\in A}r^{g}_{A-A}(x-a)\geqslant\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)/(2|A|)\}
.
Then

\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)\leqslant 2\sum_{a\in A_{*}}\sum_{x\in A}r^{g}_{A-A}(x-a)=2\sum_{y}r^{g}_{A-A}(y)r_{A-A_{*}}(y)

and using the Hölder inequality several times, we obtain

\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)\leqslant 2^{g+1}\sum_{y}r^{g+1}_{A-A_{*}}(y)\leqslant 2^{g+1}(\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A_{*})\mathsf{E}^{2g+1}_{g+1}(A))^{1/(2g+2)}

(25)

or, in other words,
\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A_{*})\gg_{g}\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)
.
Now let
\Lambda
be the maximal
B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
subset of
A_{*}
.
Then by the definition of the set
A_{*}
, one has

2^{-1}|\Lambda|\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)|A|^{-1}\leqslant\sum_{y}r^{g}_{A-A}(y)r_{A-\Lambda}(y)\,.

(26)

Hence as in (
25
)

2^{-(g+1)}|\Lambda|^{g+1}\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)|A|^{-(g+1)}\leqslant\sum_{y}r^{g+1}_{A-\Lambda}(y)=

=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{g}}|A\cap(A+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+x_{g})||\Lambda\cap(\Lambda+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(\Lambda+x_{g})|\,.

Applying the fact that
\Lambda\in B^{\circ}_{k}[g]
, we get

2^{-(g+1)}|\Lambda|^{g+1}\mathsf{E}_{g+1}(A)|A|^{-(g+1)}\leqslant k|A|^{g+1}+\binom{g+1}{2}|A||\Lambda|^{g}\,.

Noting that the second term in the last formula is negligible, we obtain the required result.

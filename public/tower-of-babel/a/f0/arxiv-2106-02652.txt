Ladder resummation of spin 1/2 fermion many-body systems with arbitrary partial-wave content
J. M. Alarcón, J. A. Oller

Abstract
We resum the ladder diagrams for the calculation of the energy density ℰ \cal{E} of a spin 1/2 fermion many-body system in terms of arbitrary vacuum two-body scattering amplitudes. The partial-wave decomposition of the in-medium two-body scattering amplitudes is developed, and the expression for calculating ℰ \cal{E} in a partial-wave amplitude expansion is also given. The case of contact interactions is completely solved for any content in partial waves and it is shown to provide renormalized results, expressed directly in terms of scattering data parameters, within cutoff regularization in a wide class of schemes. The S S - and P P -wave interactions are considered up to including the first three-terms in the effective-range expansion, paying special attention to the parametric region around the unitary limit.

2.3.1

In the subsequent we consider that there is only one Fermi momentum, called

6

For symmetric nuclear matter, even though there are neutrons and protons, the Fermi momenta are also the same.
The general case with different Fermi momenta will be addressed in Ref.

Given two fermions of four-momenta

and

we define

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})~,

(2.15)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})~,

and the inverse relation is
k_{1}=a+p\,
,
k_{2}=a-p\,.

For on-shell fermions
k_{i}^{0}=E(\mathbf{k}_{i})=\mathbf{k}_{i}^{2}/2m
.

The expression for

is (in the following we use the notation

)

\displaystyle L_{m}(p,\mathbf{a})=-i\int\frac{d^{3}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{k}_{1}|)\int\frac{d^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i}{k_{2}^{0}-\frac{|\mathbf{k}_{2}|^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}(2\pi)^{4}\delta(k_{1}+k_{2}-2a)|\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}|

\displaystyle-i\int\frac{d^{3}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{k}_{2}|)\int\frac{d^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i}{k_{1}^{0}-\frac{|\mathbf{k}_{1}|^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}(2\pi)^{4}\delta(k_{1}+k_{2}-2a)|\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}\rangle\langle\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}|

(2.16)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|)+\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|)\right]\frac{|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}\rangle\langle\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|}{2a^{0}-\frac{|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{2m}-\frac{|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{2m}+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle=-m\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|)+\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|)\right]\frac{|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}\rangle\langle\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|}{\mathbf{k}^{2}-(2ma^{0}-\mathbf{a}^{2})-i\epsilon}~.

Since the total four-momentum is conserved it follows then that

is the same for any

\displaystyle 2ma^{0}-\mathbf{a}^{2}

\displaystyle L_{m}(p,\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=-m\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|)+\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|)\right]\frac{|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}\rangle\langle\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|}{\mathbf{k}^{2}-\mathbf{p}^{2}-i\epsilon}~.

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{1,2}=-1/2}^{1/2}|\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}|~.

\displaystyle L_{m}(p,\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle=im\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|)\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|)\delta(\mathbf{k}^{2}-\mathbf{p}^{2})\,|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}\rangle\langle\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|

\displaystyle=i\frac{mp}{8\pi^{2}}\int d\hat{\mathbf{k}}\,\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|)\theta({k_{F}}+|\mathbf{a}-p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|)\,|\mathbf{a}+p\hat{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{a}-p\hat{\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle\mathbf{a}+p\hat{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{a}-p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|~.

\displaystyle L_{d}(p,\mathbf{a})=i\frac{mp}{8\pi^{2}}\int d\hat{\mathbf{k}}\,\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|)\theta({k_{F}}+|\mathbf{a}-p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|)\,|p\hat{\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle p\hat{\mathbf{k}}|\otimes\mathbb{I}_{S}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{IS}~,

where the identity operators in the spaces of spin and isospin are explicitly indicated.

\displaystyle t_{m}(\mathbf{a})

By including explicitly all the involved arguments this IE becomes

\displaystyle t_{m}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a})_{BA}

\displaystyle=t_{V}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})_{BA}

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2}\sum_{C}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}t_{V}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k})_{BC}\frac{\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{k}|)+\theta({k_{F}}-|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{k}|)}{\mathbf{k}^{2}-\mathbf{p}^{2}-i\epsilon}t_{m}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{p},\mathbf{a})_{CA}~.

In order to show that
\cal{E}_{L}

and the vacuum potential

\displaystyle t_{m}(\mathbf{a})=\left[I-t_{V}L_{m}(p,\mathbf{a})\right]^{-1}t_{V}~,

(2.26)

The inversion of the latter gives

\displaystyle t_{m}(\mathbf{a})^{-1}

\displaystyle=t_{V}^{-1}-L_{m}(p,\mathbf{a})~.

(2.27)

It is also the case that the vacuum
T
-matrix

satisfies a Lippmann-Schwinger equation
in terms of the potential
V

. Therefore,

\displaystyle t_{V}^{-1}

\displaystyle=V^{-1}+G(p)~.

(2.28)

Here

5
Method
applied to solve the PWAs in the medium for contact interactions

We solve
t_{V}
for the case of contact interactions by adapting the method developed in
Sec. 4.1 of Ref.
[
61
]
, also reviewed in Ref.
[
62
]
, to the many-body environment.
Instead of reproducing it here we directly moved to solve the similar IE in Eq. (
4
) for
t_{m}(a\hat{\mathbf{z}})
in PWAs,
which reduces to
t_{V}
in the limit in which the Fermi momentum
{k_{F}}\to 0
. The different coupled partial-waves are called
channels and are fixed by the quantum numbers in the state vector
|J\mu\ell Sp\rangle
. To avoid confusion in the following the PWAs in the medium and in vacuum are denoted by
{\mathsf{t}_{m}}(k,p)
and
{\mathsf{t}_{V}}(k,p)
, respectively, and it should be understood that
\mathbf{a}=a\hat{\mathbf{z}}
.

Let us develop the method
that allows us to calculate
{\mathsf{t}_{m}}(k,p)
given a contact interacting potential. The potential coupling the channels
\alpha
and
\beta
is expressed in a polynomial expansion as

\displaystyle v_{\alpha\beta}(k,p)

\displaystyle=k^{\ell_{\alpha}}p^{\ell_{\beta}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}v_{\alpha\beta;ij}k^{2(i-1)}p^{2(j-1)}~.

(5.1)

In this equation the Greek subscripts denote the channels and run from 1 to
n
and the factor

k^{\ell_{\alpha}}p^{\ell_{\beta}}
keeps track of the right threshold behavior, which factorizes in a zero-range potential.
We next introduce a matrix notation and write the potential as

\displaystyle v_{\alpha\beta}(k,p)

\displaystyle=[k_{\alpha}]^{T}\cdot[v]\cdot[p_{\beta}]~,

(5.2)

\displaystyle[v]

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}[v_{11}]&[v_{12}]&\ldots&[v_{1n}]\\
[v_{21}]&[v_{22}]&\ldots&[v_{2n}]\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
[v_{n1}]&[v_{n2}]&\ldots&[v_{nn}]\\
\end{matrix}\right)~,

(5.3)

\displaystyle[k_{\alpha}]^{T}

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{N(\alpha-1)\text{~places}},k^{\ell_{\alpha}},k^{\ell_{\alpha}+2},\ldots,k^{\ell_{\alpha}+2(N-1)},0,\ldots,0)~.

(5.4)

Here each
[v_{\alpha\beta}]
is an
N\times N
of matrix elements
v_{\alpha\beta;ij}
,
i,j=1,\ldots,N
, and the

[k_{\alpha}]
are
Nn\times 1
column vectors.
The solution to the IE in Eq. (
4.13
) can also be written in a matrix notation as

\displaystyle{\mathsf{t}_{m}}(k,p)

\displaystyle=[k_{\alpha}]^{T}\cdot[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]\cdot[p_{\beta}]~,

(5.5)

with
[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]
a squared
Nn\times Nn
matrix, analogous to
[v]
in Eq. (
5.3
).
The fulfillment of Eq. (
4.13
) (also for the full-off-shell case) implies that
[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]
must satisfy the
algebraic
equation

\displaystyle[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]

\displaystyle=[v]-[v]\cdot[{\cal G}(p)]\cdot[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]~.

(5.6)

The
Nn\times Nn
matrix
[{\cal G}(p)]
can be inferred from Eq. (
4.13
) to be

\displaystyle[{\cal G}(p)_{\alpha\beta}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}dk}{k^{2}-p^{2}-i\epsilon}{\cal A}_{\alpha\beta}[k_{\alpha}][k_{\beta}]^{T}~.

(5.7)

The sought solution to Eq. (
5.6
) can then be expressed as

\displaystyle[\hat{t}_{m}(p)]

\displaystyle=\left(I+[v][{\cal G}(p)]\right)^{-1}[v]~,

(5.8)

The (free part of the) loop functions in Eq. (
5.7
) are evaluated by employing a cutoff regularization with a cutoff scale
\Lambda
, which at the end of the renormalization procedure is taken to infinity.
In general, power-like divergences of
\Lambda
appear through the regularized integrals
[
51
]

\displaystyle L_{n}=-\frac{m}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dkk^{n-1}=\theta_{n}\Lambda^{n}~,

(5.9)

where
\theta_{n}
is a number that depends on the regularization scheme chosen. E.g. for a sharp cutoff
\theta_{n}=-m/(2\pi^{2}n)
, and for the the case of dimensional regularization (DR)
\theta_{n}=0
.

The renormalization process consists of reproducing the ERE up to some order for vacuum scattering.
The ERE amounts to a polynomial expansion of the
n\times n
matrix

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{m}(p^{\ell}){\mathsf{t}_{V}}(p,p)^{-1}(p^{\ell})+i(p^{\ell})^{2}(p)=-(a)^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}(r)p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}(v^{(2i)})p^{2(i+1)}~,

(5.10)

where
(a)
,
(r)
and
(v^{(2i)})
are
n\times n
matrices corresponding to the scattering lengths, effectives ranges and higher-order shape parameters, respectively. Other diagonal matrices are
(p)
, that is
p
times the identity matrix, and
(p^{\ell})
given by
{\rm diag}(p^{\ell_{1}},\ldots,p^{\ell_{n}})
. Once the couplings
v_{\alpha\beta;ij}
, Eq. (
5.1
), are given as a function of the shape parameters of the ERE and the cutoff, we finally take the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
.
In this regard, we require that the resulting
T
matrix of PWAs be finite and independent of the regulator-dependent numbers
\theta_{n}
, cf. Eq. (
5.9
), for
\Lambda\to\infty
. Reaching this limit successfully calls for the inclusion of
enough numbers of counterterms because of the factor
[k_{\alpha}][k_{\beta}]^{T}
in the free part of
[{\cal G}(p)_{\alpha\beta}]
, which gives rise to increasing power-like divergences
with the orbital angular momenta, starting with
\Lambda^{\ell_{\alpha}+\ell_{\beta}+1}
.

Interestingly enough, in all the explicit calculations that we have performed making use of
this method
for cutoff regularization (
\theta_{n}\neq 0
),
it is found that the renormalized solution for the off-shell vacuum PWAs

\displaystyle{\mathsf{t}_{V}}(k,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{m}(k)^{\ell}\tau(p)(q)^{\ell}~,

\displaystyle\tau(p)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-(a)^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}(r)p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}(v^{(2i)}_{\ell})p^{2(i+1)}-i(p^{\ell})^{2}(p)~,

where
M

\tau(p)
is a purely on-shell (

the off-shellness is incorporated in

(k)^{\ell}
and
(q)^{\ell}

A clarifying remark is in order. When solving a Lippmann-Schwinger equation for on-shell scattering one has to provide also the solution for the half-off-shell

For such cases the result in Eq. (

Solving

Given an uncoupled ERE expansion

An interesting aspect to be studied is the influence of the effective range in the Fermi gases at around unitarity. Indeed, from simple scaling arguments
[
69

one expects that in the ERE for a PWA with orbital angular momentum
\ell

\ell+1

p\to\lambda p
in the ERE
and factorize
\lambda^{2\ell+1}
from the phase space term

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\ell+1}a_{\ell}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{r_{\ell}}{\lambda^{2\ell-1}}p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}\frac{v^{(2i)}_{\ell}}{\lambda^{2\ell-1-2i}}p^{2(i+1)}~.

(6.1)

Attending to the evolution of the different terms for
\lambda\to 0

(the low-momentum limit)
one obtains the expectation claimed.
12
12

12

From a physical point of view this scaling argument is just reflecting the fact that, taking into account the typical range of interactions (
R
), the effective range
r_{\ell}
depends on it as
R^{-\ell+1}
, while the shape parameters
v_{\ell}^{(2i)}
do so as
R^{-2\ell-1+2i}
(see Ref.
[
70
]
for explicit calculations in nucleon-nucleon scattering in which case

is settled by the inverse of the pion mass). Notice that the exponent in the corresponding power of
R
is the negative of the exponent of
\lambda
for the same term in Eq. (
6.1
). Therefore, from these scaling arguments, the first
\ell+1
terms in the ERE in the low-momentum limit,

, can give rise to large contributions, so that it is necessary to consider them.

This result can be taken to the scattering of fermions in the medium because of Eq. (
5.12
).
Therefore, it is pertinent to study the dependence of the energy density
\cal{E}
on the higher-order shape parameters in the ERE, even if the system is at unitarity. In this way, one can ascertain whether there is a perturbative dependence of this magnitude on the effective range and even on other shape parameters.

Regarding this point, we find that
P
-wave results can only be renormalized in cutoff regularization
once both the scattering length and effective range are reproduced,
as already obtained in Ref.
[
71
]
.

A paradigmatic example where these results are of interest is neutron matter, where the scattering length has a large magnitude,
a_{0}=-18.95
fm, and the effective range

fm is sizeable, both of them compared with the inverse of the pion mass around 1.4 fm.
In ultracold atoms there is also the possibility to tune the scattering length, and also the effective range through the dark-state optical control of Feshbach resonances
[
72
,
73
]
.

A non-perturbative fact that we take into account when applying our formalism is to exclude those
values of the scattering length and effective range which give rise to an unacceptable pole content of
\tau(p)
, Eq. (
5.11

For instance, a pair of resonant poles located in the complex-

6.1

Given a constant potential

the vacuum scattering amplitude then reads
{\mathsf{t}_{V}}=\left(1/c_{0}+\theta_{1}\Lambda-imp/4\pi\right)^{-1}
. The renormalization is achieved by setting

, so that

{\mathsf{t}_{V}}=-4\pi/m\left(1/a_{0}+ip\right)^{-1}
.
The expression for

is equally simple in this case,

\displaystyle\tau_{m}(p)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{a_{0}}-ip+{\cal G}_{m}(p)\right)^{-1}~.

(6.2)

In terms of it,
\cal{E}_{L}
in Eq. (

) reads

\displaystyle\cal{E}_{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4i}{m\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{k_{F}}a^{2}da\int_{0}^{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}-a^{2}}}pdp\log\left(1-\frac{4\pi/m}{-\frac{1}{a_{0}}-ip+{\cal G}_{m}(p)}L_{d}\right)

(6.3)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4i}{m\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{k_{F}}a^{2}da\int_{0}^{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}-a^{2}}}pdp\log\left(\frac{1-a_{0}(-ip+{\cal G}_{m}(p)-4\pi L_{d}/m)}{1-a_{0}(-ip+{\cal G}_{m}(p))}\right)~.

Explicit expressions for the functions
{\cal G}_{m}(p)
and
L_{d}(p,a\hat{\mathbf{z}})
are given in the Appendix C of Ref.
[
35
]
. The connection with the functions there defined is
{\cal G}_{m}(p)=4\pi L_{10,m}/m
and
L_{d}(p,a\hat{\mathbf{z}})=-L_{10,d}
. Regarding the one-loop functions
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
,
R
and

introduced by Kaiser in Ref.
[

]
we have the relations:
B_{1}=a_{0}{\cal G}_{m}(p)
,
B_{2}=-4\pi a_{0}L_{d}/m=-i2k_{F}a_{0}I
,
\Re B_{1}=a_{0}k_{F}R/\pi
, such that
a_{0}k_{F}(R+i\pi I)/\pi=a_{0}(-ip+{\cal G}_{m})
and
a_{0}k_{F}(R-i\pi I)/\pi=a_{0}(-ip+{\cal G}_{m}-4\pi L_{d}/m)
.
Then, Eq. (
6.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\cal{E}_{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4i}{m\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{k_{F}}a^{2}da\int_{0}^{\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}-a^{2}}}pdp\log\left(\frac{1-a_{0}k_{F}(R/\pi-iI)}{1-a_{0}k_{F}(R/\pi+iI)}\right)~,

(6.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{8k_{F}^{5}}{m\pi^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}ds\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\kappa d\kappa\arctan\left(\frac{I}{(a_{0}k_{F})^{-1}-R/\pi}\right)~,

The previous expression is the same as the one from Ref.
[

For completeness we reproduce the expressions for the functions

and

\displaystyle R(s,\kappa)

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1-(s+\kappa)^{2}}{2s}\log\frac{1+s+\kappa}{|1-s-\kappa|}+\frac{1-(s-\kappa)^{2}}{2s}\log\frac{1+s-\kappa}{1-s+\kappa}~,

(6.5)

\displaystyle I(s,\kappa)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa&\text{for }0<\kappa<1-s\\
\frac{1-s^{2}-\kappa^{2}}{2s}&\text{for }1-s<\kappa<\sqrt{1-s^{2}}~.\end{array}\right.

7
Conclusions

We have presented the derivation of the resummation of the ladder diagrams for the evaluation of the energy density
\cal{E}
of a spin 1/2 fermion many-body system in terms of arbitrary vacuum two-body interactions. All in-medium two-body intermediate states are accounted for. In standard many-body notation this means that we have resummed this iteration by taking into account both particle-particle and hole-hole intermediate states. In our derivations it has been essential to use the formulation of many-body quantum field theory from Ref.
[
1
]

because of the resulting framework in which the many-body theory is organized. This fact provides us with a rearrangement of the diagrams involved
in the calculation of
\bar{\cal{E}}
that allows the solution of the non-trivial combinatoric problem associated.

The resulting expression provides a real value for
\cal{E}
because of in-medium unitarity, as we prove. At the practical level, since it is usually the case that the two-body finite-range interactions are studied in partial waves, it is interesting to express
\cal{E}
in a partial-wave amplitude expansion.
This requires a special treatment in the many-body environment because of extra mixing among the partial-wave amplitudes
due to the breaking of rotational invariance within the relative degrees of freedom. The reason is because the scattering amplitudes also depend on the total momentum.
The necessary formalism for the partial-wave expansions has been worked out in detail.

The case of contact interactions has been fully solved, providing explicit renormalized results within a cutoff regularization in a wide class of schemes. First concrete examples have been considered involving
S
- and
P
-wave interactions and including up to the first three terms in the effective-range expansion, namely,
a_{\ell}
,
r_{\ell}
and
v_{\ell}^{(2)}
with
\ell=0
or 1. Special attention has been payed to the study of the parametric region around the unitary limit (in normal matter). It is shown that the effective range in
S
waves,
r_{0}
, plays there a perturbative role as expected. We also study the
S
-wave contributions to
{\cal{E}}
for neutron matter and the Tan density contact parameter
{\mathsf{C}}
. The case of a spin-independent
P
-wave interaction is also analyzed and
we show that the effective range
r_{1}
is needed together the scattering length
a_{1}
to obtained renormalized results.
We have given several plots for
{\cal{E}}/{\cal{E}_{\rm free}}
where we show its dependence with
-1/a_{1}k_{F}^{3}
for
r_{1}=0
, and then move to the limit
a_{1}\to\infty
and give it as a function of the dimensionless parameter
r_{1}/k_{F}
. We also plot
{\cal{E}}/{\cal{E}_{\rm free}}
as a function of
-1/a_{1}k_{F}^{3}
and
r_{1}/k_{F}
.
The next shape parameter
v_{1}^{(2)}
is also considered and we plot
{\cal{E}}/{\cal{E}_{\rm free}}
within the plane

r_{1}/k_{F}
and
v_{1}^{(2)}k_{F}
in the limit
a_{1}\to\infty
.
In all cases interesting non-trivial shapes for
{\cal{E}}/{\cal{E}_{\rm free}}
are found.

We have largely generalized the previous Kaiser’s pivotal analyses in Refs.
[
43
,
44
]
, regarding the resummation of the ladder series. Apart from what was mentioned previously, it is worthwhile to list here for concreteness the attainment of several other important achievements of this work, as explained along the paper:

i)

We have derived a general formula for the resummation of the ladder series expressed in terms of arbitrary fermion-fermion interactions in vacuum.

ii)

We have developed a partial-wave expansion for calculating the general formula referred in i).

iii)

We can consider any number of partial waves contributing, either separately or simultaneously.

iv)

For every partial wave we can include any number of terms in the effective-range expansion.

v)

We can apply our method with cutoff regularization in a generic scheme obtaining always scheme independent renormalized results.

vi)

We can also work out the case with dimensional regularization (the one used by Kaiser exclusively in Refs.
[
43
,
44
]
).
However, this method is generally not correct for non-perturbative calculations as discussed in the manuscript.

vii)

We have shown that the ansatz used by Kaiser
[
44
]
to resum the ladder series when including up to the effective range in
S
waves is correct.

As a concluding remark, we consider that further applications of this powerful approach should be pursued in many-body systems,
like nuclear matter or ultracold trapped atoms and ions.

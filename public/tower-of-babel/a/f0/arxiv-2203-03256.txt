Separating Many Localisation Cardinals on the Generalised Baire Space
Tristan van der Vlugt

Abstract
Given a cofinal cardinal function h ∈ κ κ h\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa for κ \kappa inaccessible, we consider the dominating h h -localisation number, that is, the least cardinality of a dominating set of h h -slaloms such that every κ \kappa -real is localised by a slalom in the dominating set. It was proved in [ BBTFM18 ] that the dominating localisation numbers can be consistently different for two functions h h (the identity function and the power function). We will construct a κ \kappa -sized family of functions h h and their corresponding localisation numbers, and use a ≤ κ {\leq}\kappa -supported product of a cofinality-preserving forcing to prove that any simultaneous assignment of these localisation numbers to cardinals above κ \kappa is consistent. This answers an open question from [ BBTFM18 ] .



In an effort to generalise the cardinal characteristics related to the null ideal from the context of the continuum
{}^{\omega}\omega
to the generalised Baire space
{}^{\kappa}\kappa
, the authors of
[
BBTFM18
]
considered localisation cardinals. These cardinals were first described in the context of
{}^{\omega}\omega
by Tomek Bartoszyński
[
Bar87
]
, and are defined using slaloms. Let
\kappa
be a regular strong limit cardinal (hence
\kappa
is inaccessible or equal to
\omega
) and let
h\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
. An
h
-slalom
is any function
\varphi:\kappa\to[\kappa]^{<\kappa}
such that
|\varphi(\alpha)|\leq|h(\alpha)|
for all
\alpha\in\kappa
.

For
f\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
, we say
f\in^{*}\varphi
, or
f
is
localised
by
\varphi
, if there exists some
\xi<\kappa
such that
f(\alpha)\in\varphi(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in[\xi,\kappa)
. We denote the set of all
h
-slaloms by
\mathrm{Loc}_{h}
.

Using these concepts, we can define the following two cardinal characteristics, sometimes called
localisation cardinals
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{\kappa}^{h}(\in^{*})

\displaystyle=\text{the least cardinality of a family }\mathcal{F}\subseteq{}^{\kappa}\kappa\text{ such that }\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Loc}_{h}\exists f\in\mathcal{F}(f\notin^{*}\varphi),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{h}(\in^{*})

\displaystyle=\text{the least cardinality of a family }\Phi\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}_{h}\text{ such that }\forall f\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa\exists\varphi\in\Phi(f\in^{*}\varphi).

In the case that
\kappa=\omega
these cardinals give a combinatorial definition of two of the cardinal invariants of the Lebesgue null ideal
\mathcal{N}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\text{the least cardinality of a family }\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{N}\text{ such that }\bigcup\mathcal{A}\notin\mathcal{N},

\displaystyle\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\text{the least cardinality of a family }\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{N}\text{ such that }\forall N\in\mathcal{N}\exists C\in\mathcal{C}(N\subseteq C)

Bartoszyński introduced slaloms in
[
Bar87
]
to give the following combinatorial definition to
\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{N})
and
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{N})
.

Fact 1

\mathrm{add}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{b}_{\omega}^{h}(\in^{*})
and
\mathrm{cof}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{d}_{\omega}^{h}(\in^{*})
.

The choice of
h\in{}^{\omega}\omega
is irrelevant here, as it does not influence the cardinality of
\mathfrak{b}_{\omega}^{h}(\in^{*})
and
\mathfrak{d}_{\omega}^{h}(\in^{*})
. This turns out to be different in the generalised case. Recently, it was proved in
[
BBTFM18
]
that
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{h}(\in^{*})
can consistently have different values for different
h\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
. In particular, it was shown that
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{pow}}(\in^{*})<\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{id}}(\in^{*})
is consistent, where
\mathsf{pow}:\alpha\mapsto 2^{\alpha}
and
\mathsf{id}:\alpha\mapsto\alpha
.

This consistency was proved using a generalisation of Sacks forcing on
{}^{\kappa}2
that has the generalised
\mathsf{pow}
-Sacks property, but not the generalised
\mathsf{id}
-Sacks property.

Definition 2

Let
h\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
. A forcing
\mathbb{P}
has the
generalised
h
-Sacks property
if for every
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{f}
and
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
p\Vdash\text{``}\,\dot{f}:\check{\kappa}\to\check{\kappa}\,\text{''}
there exists an
h
-slalom
\varphi
and
q\leq p
such that
q\Vdash\text{``}\,\dot{f}(\check{\alpha})\in\check{\varphi}(\check{\alpha})\,\text{''}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
. We will from now on simply say
h
-Sacks property and omit “generalised”.

Hence, if
\Phi\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}_{h}
is a family as in the definition of
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{h}(\in^{*})
in the ground model, then
\Phi
will still witness the size of
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{h}(\in^{*})
in the extension by a forcing with the
h
-Sacks property. Therefore, the generalised Sacks forcing is unable to increase the size of
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{pow}}(\in^{*})
, but it is shown to be possible to increase the size of
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{\mathsf{id}}(\in^{*})
by using an iteration or a product of the forcing.

In this text we will answer an open question from
[
BBTFM18
]
and prove that there exist
\kappa
many functions
h_{\xi}\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
and cardinals
\lambda_{\xi}>\kappa
such that it is simultaneously consistent that
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{h_{\xi}}(\in^{*})=\lambda_{\xi}
for all
\xi<\kappa
. The strategy will be the same, in that we consider a product of Sacks-like forcings that have the
h_{\xi}
-Sacks property, but not the
h_{\eta}
-Sacks property for different
\xi
and
\eta
. In the first section, we introduce the Sacks-like forcing
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
and prove that it preserves cardinals and cofinalities and use fusion to show that it satisfies certain Sacks properties. In the second section we consider products of such forcings, we show that properties such as the preservation of cardinals and cofinalities and the relevant Sacks properties are preserved under
{\leq}\kappa
-support products and we use this to prove the consistency of our result.

For the sake of brevity, from now on we will assume that
\kappa
denotes a strongly inaccessible cardinal. We will also fix the convention that

denote increasing cofinal cardinal functions (i.e.
\mathrm{ran}(h)
is cofinal in
\kappa
and

is a cardinal for each

). This convention extends to

,
F_{0}
, and other subscripts.

The Forcing

Let us establish some notation to discuss trees on
{}^{\kappa}\kappa
before we define our forcing notion.

Let
T\subseteq{}^{<\kappa}\kappa
be a tree, then we let
[T]
denote the set of branches of
T
. Usually
[T]\subseteq{}^{\kappa}\kappa
. We call
u\in T
a
non-splitting
node (of
T
) if there is a unique
\beta<\kappa
such that
u^{\frown}\beta\in T
and otherwise we call
u
an
\alpha
-splitting
node (of
T
), where
\alpha\leq\left|\left\{\beta<\kappa\mid u^{\frown}\beta\in T\right\}\right|
. We say
u
is a
splitting
node if it is a 2-splitting node. If
u
is an
\alpha
-splitting node, but not a
\xi
-splitting node for any
\xi
with
|\xi|>|\alpha|
, then we say that
u
is a
sharp

\alpha
-splitting node. We let
u\in\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)
iff
u
is splitting and
\mathrm{ot}(\left\{\beta<\mathrm{ot}(u)\mid u\restriction\beta\text{ is splitting}\right\})=\alpha
. If
u\in T
is splitting, let
\mathrm{suc}(u,T)=\left\{v\in T\mid\exists\beta<\kappa(v=u^{\frown}\beta)\right\}
.

Definition 3

The conditions of the forcing
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
are trees
T\subseteq{}^{<\kappa}\kappa
that satisfy the following properties:

(i)

for any
u\in T
there exists
v\in T
such that
u\subseteq v
and
v
is splitting,

(ii)

if
u\in\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)
, then
u
is an
h(\alpha)
-splitting node in
T
,

(iii)

if
\left\langle u_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\gamma\right\rangle
is an increasing sequence of splitting nodes, then
\bigcup_{\alpha<\gamma}u_{\alpha}
is splitting.

The order is defined as
T\leq_{{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}}S
(here
T
is stronger than
S
) iff
T\subseteq S
and for every
\alpha<\kappa
and
u\in T
a splitting node, if
\mathrm{suc}(u,T)\neq\mathrm{suc}(u,S)
, then
|\mathrm{suc}(u,T)|<|\mathrm{suc}(u,S)|
. If the forcing notion is clear from context, we will write
T\leq S
in place of
T\leq_{{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}}S

Note that conditions (i) and (iii) imply that
[T]\subseteq{}^{\kappa}\kappa
and that the set of splitting nodes is club in
T
. We naturally want
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
to preserve cardinalities. If we assume that
\mathbf{V}\vDash\text{``}\,2^{\kappa}=\kappa^{+}\,\text{''}
, then it is clear that
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
has the
{<}\kappa^{++}
-chain condition, since
|{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}|\leq|\mathcal{P}({}^{<\kappa}\kappa)|=2^{\kappa}=\kappa^{+}
, where the former equality is implied by
\kappa^{<\kappa}=\kappa
, which in turn follows from
\kappa
being inaccessible. It is therefore clear that cardinalities above
\kappa^{+}
are preserved under assumption of
\mathbf{V}\vDash\text{``}\,2^{\kappa}=\kappa^{+}\,\text{''}
.

To preserve cardinalities less than or equal to
\kappa
, we show that
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
is
{<}\kappa
-closed.

Lemma 4

{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
is
{<}\kappa
-closed. That is, for any
\lambda<\kappa
, if
\left\langle T_{\xi}\mid\xi<\lambda\right\rangle
is a descending chain of conditions, then there exists a condition
T
such that
T\leq T_{\xi}
for all
\xi<\lambda
.

The condition
T
that is below all
T_{\xi}
will be simply
T=\bigcap_{\xi}T_{\xi}
. Clearly
T
is a tree as well. We have to show that
T\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
, which we do by veryfing points (i), (ii) and (iii) from
3
. We will make use of the following claim.

Claim.
If
u\in T=\bigcap T_{\xi}
, then there is
\eta<\lambda
such that
\mathrm{suc}(u,T)=\mathrm{suc}(u,T_{\xi})
for all
\xi\in[\eta,\lambda)
.

Suppose that
u\in T
, and let
\lambda_{\xi}=|\mathrm{suc}(u,T_{\xi})|
, then the ordering on
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
dictates that
\left\langle\lambda_{\xi}\mid\xi<\lambda\right\rangle
is a descending sequence of cardinals, hence there is
\eta<\lambda
such that
\lambda_{\xi}=\lambda_{\eta}
for all
\xi\in[\eta,\lambda)
. But then
\mathrm{suc}(u,T_{\xi})=\mathrm{suc}(u,T_{\eta})
for all
\xi\in[\eta,\lambda)
by the ordering of
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
.
∎

(i) Let
u\in T
, and let
f\in[T]
be a branch for which
u\subseteq f
. If
\mathrm{ot}(f)<\kappa
, then
f\in T_{\xi}
for each
\xi
, thus by the claim there is some
\xi<\lambda
for which
\mathrm{suc}(f,T_{\xi})=\mathrm{suc}(f,T)=\varnothing
. Then clearly
T_{\xi}\notin{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
, which is a contradiction, hence
\mathrm{ot}(f)=\kappa
. Let
S_{\xi}=\left\{\alpha\in[\mathrm{ot}(u),\kappa)\mid f\restriction\alpha\text{ is splitting in }T_{\xi}\right\}
, then since
T_{\xi}\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
satisfies (i) and (iii), we see that
S_{\xi}
is a club set. But then
\bigcap_{\xi<\lambda}S_{\xi}
is club. Any
v\in\bigcap_{\xi<\lambda}S_{\xi}
is splitting in all
T_{\xi}
, thus by the claim it is splitting in
T
, and by definition of
S_{\xi}
it follows that
u\subseteq v
.

(ii) If
u\in\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)
, then by the claim there is
\xi<\lambda
such that
\mathrm{suc}(u,T_{\xi})=\mathrm{suc}(u,T)
. If
v\subseteq u
is splitting in
T
, then
v
is splitting in
T_{\xi}
as well, therefore
u\in\mathrm{Split}_{\beta}(T_{\xi})
for some
\beta\geq\alpha
. This shows that
u
is
h(\beta)
-splitting in
T_{\xi}
, hence it is
h(\beta)
-splitting in
T
and by

we see that
u
is also
h(\alpha)
-splitting in
T
.

(iii) Let
\left\langle u_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\gamma\right\rangle
be an increasing sequence of splitting nodes in
T
, then for every
\xi<\lambda
we also see that
\left\langle u_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\gamma\right\rangle
is a sequence of splitting nodes in
T_{\xi}
, and thus
u=\bigcup_{\alpha<\gamma}u_{\alpha}
is a splitting node in all
T_{\xi}
, hence by the claim
u
is splitting in
T
.
∎

Corollary 5

{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
preserves all cardinalities and cofinalities
\leq\kappa
.

What is left, is to show that
\kappa^{+}
is also preserved. This will be a consequence of showing that
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
has the
F
-Sacks property for some suitably large
F\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
: if
\dot{f}
is a
{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
-name and
T\Vdash\text{``}\,\dot{f}:\check{\kappa}\to\check{\kappa}^{+}\,\text{''}
for some

, then using (the proof of) the

Before we can prove that

indeed has the
F
-Sacks property for some suitably large

, we will need to show that

is closed under fusion. It will be helpful to establish some notation for subtrees. If

, let
T_{u}=\left\{v\in T\mid u\subseteq v\lor v\subseteq u\right\}
. It is clear that
T_{u}\leq T
.

Let a tree
T\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
be called

if every

is a sharp
h(\alpha)
-splitting node. It is clear that by pruning we may find a sharp
T^{*}
below any condition
T\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
such that
\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T^{*})\subseteq\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)
for every
\alpha<\kappa
, and we may assume that we can canonically do so, thus we will hereby fix the notation

to denote a sharp tree below condition

. We will write
({\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}})^{*}=\left\{T\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}\mid T\text{ is sharp}\right\}
, which embeds densely into

.

For
T,S\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
, we let
T\leq_{\alpha}S

and
\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)=\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(S)
. A

is a sequence
\left\langle T_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}\mid\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
such that

for all
\alpha\leq\beta<\kappa
.

Lemma 6

If
\left\langle T_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}\mid\alpha<\kappa\right\rangle
is a fusion sequence, then
T=\bigcap T_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
.

Clearly
T
is a tree on
{}^{<\kappa}\kappa
. We check conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) of
3
.

(i) Let
u\in T
and
\alpha=\mathrm{ot}(u)
, then for any

we see that
\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T_{\beta})=\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T_{\beta^{\prime}})
, thus pick
\beta>\alpha
arbitrarily. Since

, necessarily there exists some

such that

, and since

we see that

.

(ii) Let
s\in\mathrm{Split}_{\alpha}(T)
, then

is
h(\alpha)
-splitting in

. Let
\lambda_{s}=|\mathrm{suc}(s,T_{\alpha+1})|\geq h(\alpha)
and let

enumerate those

. For all

, therefore for each

we see that

for all

, thus

. Therefore

is
h(\alpha)
-splitting in

.

(iii) Let

for each

, then

is an

-splitting node in

. It follows that

\displaystyle B_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\left\{\beta_{\xi}^{v}\mid v\in V_{\xi}\right\}.

Corollary 8

{\mathbb{S}^{h}_{\kappa}}
preserves
\kappa^{+}
.

Before we prove the existence of

does not have the

\dot{f}

Lemma 16

Let
A
be a set of ordinals, let
\left\langle h_{\xi}\mid\xi\in A\right\rangle
be a sequence of functions, let
\overline{\mathbb{S}}=\prod_{\xi\in A}{\mathbb{S}^{h_{\xi}}_{\kappa}}

, and let

.

This is a standard argument.

Theorem 17

There exists a family of functions
\left\{g_{\eta}\mid\eta<\kappa\right\}\subseteq{}^{\kappa}\kappa
such that for any
\gamma<\kappa^{+}
and any increasing sequence
\left\langle\lambda_{\xi}\mid\xi<\gamma\right\rangle
of cardinals with
\lambda_{0}\geq 2^{\kappa}
and any
\sigma:\kappa\to\gamma
, there exists a forcing extension in which
\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{g_{\eta}}(\in^{*})=\lambda_{\sigma(\eta)}
for all
\eta<\kappa
.

We assume that
\mathbf{V}\vDash\text{``}\,2^{\kappa}=\kappa^{+}\,\text{''}
, or otherwise we first use a forcing to collapse
2^{\kappa}
to become
\kappa^{+}
. By a result of Solovay (theorem 8.10 in
[
Jec03
]
) there exists a family of
\kappa
many disjoint stationary subsets of
\kappa
, thus let
\left\{S_{\eta}\mid\eta<\kappa\right\}
be such a family. Let
\kappa\leq\gamma<\kappa^{+}
and
\sigma:\kappa\to\gamma
be given. We will assume without loss of generality that
\sigma
is bijective, and hence that
\sigma^{-1}:\gamma\to\kappa
is a well-defined bijection. Let
\left\langle\lambda_{\xi}\mid\xi<\gamma\right\rangle
be an increasing sequence of cardinals with
\lambda_{0}\geq\kappa^{+}
.

Fix some
F
such that
F(\alpha)^{|\alpha|}=F(\alpha)
. For each
\eta<\kappa
we define a function
g_{\eta}
as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\eta}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}F(\alpha)&\text{ if }\alpha\in S_{\eta}\\
2^{F(\alpha)}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

For each
\xi<\gamma
we define
H_{\xi}\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\xi}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}F(\alpha)&\text{ if }\alpha\in\bigcup_{\xi^{\prime}<\xi}S_{\sigma^{-1}(\xi^{\prime})}\\
2^{F(\alpha)}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Note that
g_{\eta}\geq H_{\sigma(\eta)+1}
: if
\alpha\in S_{\eta}
, then
\alpha\in S_{\sigma^{-1}(\sigma(\eta))}
, so
\alpha\in\bigcup_{\xi<\sigma(\eta)+1}S_{\sigma^{-1}(\xi)}
, and thus
H_{\sigma(\eta)+1}(\alpha)=F(\alpha)
. Therefore
\mathsf{ZFC}\vdash\text{``}\,\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{g_{\eta}}(\in^{*})\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{H_{\sigma(\eta)+1}}(\in^{*})\,\text{''}
.

For each
\xi<\gamma
let
A_{\xi}
be a set of ordinals with
|A_{\xi}|=\lambda_{\xi}
, such that
\left\langle A_{\xi}\mid\xi<\gamma\right\rangle
is a sequence of mutually disjoint sets, and let
A=\bigcup_{\xi<\gamma}A_{\xi}
. For each
\xi<\gamma
and
\beta\in A_{\xi}
, we define
h_{\beta}\in{}^{\kappa}\kappa
as
h_{\beta}=H_{\xi}
.

The forcing that we will consider is
\overline{\mathbb{S}}=\prod_{\beta\in A}{\mathbb{S}^{h_{\beta}}_{\kappa}}
with
{\leq}\kappa
-support. Let
G
be
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
-generic. We will fix some
\xi<\gamma
for the remainder, and let
B=\bigcup_{\xi^{\prime}\leq\xi}A_{\xi^{\prime}}
and
B^{c}=A\setminus B=\bigcup_{\xi<\xi^{\prime}<\gamma}A_{\xi^{\prime}}
. Since we assume
F(\alpha)^{|\alpha|}=F(\alpha)
, we see
\sup_{\beta\in B^{c}}h_{\beta}(\alpha)=\sup_{\xi<\xi^{\prime}<\gamma}H_{\xi^{\prime}}(\alpha)=H_{\xi+1}(\alpha)
and therefore
H_{\xi+1}:\alpha\mapsto(\sup_{\beta\in B^{c}}h_{\beta}(\alpha))^{|\alpha|}
.

By
16
we see that
(\mathrm{Loc}_{H_{\xi+1}})^{\mathbf{V}[G\restriction B]}
has cardinality
\kappa^{+}\cdot|B|=\kappa^{+}\cdot|\sup_{\xi^{\prime}\leq\xi}A_{\xi^{\prime}}|=\lambda_{\xi}
. Combining this with
13
, we see that there exists a family in
\mathbf{V}[G]
of size
\lambda_{\xi}
that forms a witness for
\mathbf{V}[G]\vDash\text{``}\,\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{H_{\xi+1}}(\in^{*})\leq\lambda_{\xi}\,\text{''}
.

Finally, for each
\eta<\kappa
we see that if
\alpha\in S_{\eta}
and
\beta\in A_{\sigma(\eta)}
, then
h_{\beta}(\alpha)=H_{\sigma(\eta)}(\alpha)=2^{F(\alpha)}

\mathbf{V}[G]\vDash\text{``}\,\lambda_{\sigma(\eta)}=|A_{\sigma(\eta)}|\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{g_{\eta}}(\in^{*})\,\text{''}

In conclusion,
\mathbf{V}[G]\vDash\text{``}\,\lambda_{\sigma(\eta)}\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{g_{\eta}}(\in^{*})\leq\mathfrak{d}_{\kappa}^{H_{\sigma(\eta)+1}}(\in^{*})\leq\lambda_{\sigma(\eta)}\,\text{''}
for each
\eta<\kappa

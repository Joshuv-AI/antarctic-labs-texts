Abstract
. Consider the matrix 𝖠 𝒢 \mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}} chosen uniformly at random from the finite set of all N N -dimensional matrices of zero main-diagonal and binary entries, having each row and column of 𝖠 𝒢 \mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}} sum to d d . That is, the adjacency matrix for the uniformly random d d -regular simple digraph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} . Fixing d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , it has long been conjectured that as N → ∞ N\to\infty the corresponding empirical eigenvalue distributions converge weakly, in probability, to an explicit non-random limit, given by the Brown measure of the free sum of d d Haar unitary operators. We reduce this conjecture to bounding the decay in N N of the probability that the minimal singular value of the shifted matrix 𝖠 ⁡ ( w ) = 𝖠 𝒢 − w ​ 𝖨 \mathsf{A}(w)=\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}-w\mathsf{I} is very small. While the latter remains a challenging task, the required bound is comparable to the recently established control on the singularity of 𝖠 𝒢 \mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}} . The reduction is achieved here by sharp estimates on the behavior at large N N , near the real line, of the Green’s function (aka resolvent) of the Hermitization of 𝖠 ⁡ ( w ) \mathsf{A}(w) , which is of independent interest.

Introduction

The method of moments and the Stieltjes transform approach
provide rather precise information on asymptotics of the
Empirical Spectral Distribution (in short
esd
),
for many
Hermitian
random matrix models.
In contrast, both methods fail for
non-Hermitian
matrix models,
and the only available general scheme for finding the limiting spectral
distribution in such cases is the one proposed by Girko (in
[
7
]
).
It is extremely challenging to rigorously justify this scheme, even
for the matrix model consisting of i.i.d. entries (of zero mean and finite variance).
Indeed, the
circular law
conjecture, for the i.i.d. case, was established
in full generality only in 2010, after rather long series of partial results
(see
[
18
]
and historical references in
[
6
]
). Beyond this
simple model, only a few results have been rigorously proved
in the non-Hermitian regime. Our focus here is on the long standing conjecture
about the limit, as
N\to\infty
, of the
esd
of the adjacency matrix

\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
in case
\mathcal{G}
is a uniformly random
d
-regular
directed graph
(aka digraph)
of
N
vertices.
Specifically, in this context it is conjectured (see
[
6
]
), that, fixing
d\geq 3
,
such
esd

converge weakly, in probability, to a measure
\mu_{d}
on the complex plane,
whose density with respect to Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
is

(1.1)

h_{d}(w):=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{d^{2}(d-1)}{(d^{2}-|w|^{2})^{2}}\mathbb{I}_{\{|w|\leq\sqrt{d}\}}\,.

From a graph-theoretic point of view, it is most natural to consider the

simple digraph
(
sd
) version of this problem, whereby we restrict
\mathcal{G}
to be a
simple digraph, or alternatively choose
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
uniformly from the ensemble of
all matrices of
\{0,1\}
-valued entries with zero main diagonal and having each of
its row and column sum to
d
. One may alternatively aim at proving this conjecture for
the closely related

configuration model
(
cm
), where

\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
is uniformly chosen from among all matrices of non-negative integer
entries with all rows and columns sum to
d
(that is,
\mathcal{G}
is still uniform,
directed and
d
-regular, but now allowing also for self and multiple edges).
The preceding conjecture is best appreciated in the context of yet another
version, the so called
permutation model
(
pm
), where
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
is
taken as the sum of
d
uniformly and independently chosen

N
-dimensional permutation matrices (that is, with
\mathcal{G}
a
d
-regular
multi-graph, now of a slightly non-uniform law).

Indeed, permutation matrices form a
subgroup of the group of orthogonal matrices, and it was shown in
[
3

that
as
N\to\infty
, the
esd
for the sum of
d
independently chosen,
Haar
distributed

N
-dimensional,

\mu_{d}

whose density is

of (
1.1
). This result of
[
3
]

brings us back to the rationale behind the conjecture
(which should hold for all three versions of uniform
d
-regular digraph
we have mentioned). Namely, the observation that
\mu_{d}
is the
Brown measure
of the

free sum
of
d\geq 2

Haar unitary operators
(see
[
10
, Example 5.5]
).
To understand the plausible route and challenges in affirming this conjecture, recall the
definition of Brown measure for a bounded operator (see
[
10
, Page 333]
),
where
\langle{\rm Log},\mu\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|x|d\mu(x)

for any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
(for which such integral is well defined).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a non-commutative
W^{*}
-probability space, i.e.
a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a normal faithful tracial
state
\tau
(see
[
1
, Defn. 5.2.26]
).
For
h
a positive element in
\mathcal{A}
, let
\mu_{h}
denote the unique
probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\tau(h^{n})=\int t^{n}d\mu_{h}(t)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
The Brown measure
\mu_{a}
associated with each bounded
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
is the Riesz measure corresponding to the

[-\infty,\infty)
-valued sub-harmonic function

w\mapsto\langle{\rm Log},\mu_{|a-w|}\rangle
on
\mathbb{C}
.
That is,
\mu_{a}
is the unique Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{C}
such that

(1.2)

d\mu_{a}(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{w}\langle{\rm Log},\mu_{|a-w|}\rangle\,dw,

where
\Delta_{w}
denotes the two-dimensional Laplacian operator
(with respect to
w\in\mathbb{C}
), and the identity (
1.2
)
holds in distribution sense (i.e. when integrated against
any test function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
).

Recall that as
N\to\infty
, independent Haar distributed
N
-dimensional orthogonal
matrices converge in
\star
-moments (see
[
17
]
for a definition), to the
collection
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
of
\star
-
free
Haar unitary operators
(see
[
1
, Thm. 5.4.10]
). The challenge, even in the context of
[
3
]
,
stems from the fact that the convergence of
\star
-moments (or even the stronger
convergence in distribution of traffics, see
[
15
]
), does not imply convergence
of the corresponding Brown measures
1
1

1

The Brown measure of a matrix
is its
ESD
(see
[
17
, Prop. 1]
)
(see
[
17
, §2.6]
).
Moreover, while
[
17
, Thm. 6]
shows that if the original matrices
are perturbed by adding small Gaussian (of

), then the Brown measures do converge,
removing the Gaussian, or merely identifying the variance needed, are often hard tasks (e.g.

[
9

provide an example of an ensemble where no
Gaussian matrix of polynomially vanishing variance can regularize the Brown measures, in this sense).

Thus, instead of relying on the preceding free probability intuition, we shall utilize Girko’s approach, which
we now summarize. First, one associates to any
N
-dimensional (non-Hermitian)
matrix
\mathsf{A}_{N}
and every
w\in\mathbb{C}
the
2N
-dimensional

Hermitian
matrix

(1.3)

\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(0,w):=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathsf{A}_{N}-w\mathsf{I}_{N}\\
\mathsf{A}_{N}^{*}-\overline{w}\mathsf{I}_{N}&0\end{bmatrix}\,.

Applying Green’s formula to the characteristic polynomial
of a matrix
\mathsf{A}_{N}
(whose
esd
we
denote hereafter by
L_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}
), results with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(w)dL_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(w)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi N}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta\psi(w)\log|\det(w\mathsf{I}_{N}-\mathsf{A}_{N})|dw,\qquad\forall\psi\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{C})\,.

The eigenvalues of
\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(0,w)
are merely

\pm 1
times the singular values of
w\mathsf{I}_{N}-\mathsf{A}_{N}
. Hence, denoting the
esd

of
\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(0,w)
by
\nu_{N}^{w}
, we have that

\frac{1}{N}\log|\det(w\mathsf{I}_{N}-\mathsf{A}_{N})|=\frac{1}{2N}\log|\det\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(0,w)|=\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle\,,

out of which we deduce Girko’s formula,

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(w)dL_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta\psi(w)\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle dw\,,\qquad\forall\psi\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{C})\,.

The preceding identity suggests the following recipe for proving
convergence of
L_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}
per given family of non-Hermitian random matrices
\{\mathsf{A}_{N}\}

(to which we referred already as Girko’s method).

Step 1
: Show that for (Lebesgue almost)
every
w\in\mathbb{C}
, as
N\rightarrow\infty
the measures

\nu_{N}^{w}
converge weakly, in probability, to
some measure
\nu^{w}
.

Step 2
: Justify that

\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle\to\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle

in probability, for almost every
w\in\mathbb{C}

(which is the main technical challenge of this approach).

Step 3
: A uniform integrability argument allows one to convert
the
w
-a.e. convergence of
\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle
to
the corresponding convergence for a suitable collection

\mathcal{S}\subseteq C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
of (smooth) test functions.
It then follows from (
1.4
) that
for each fixed
\psi\in\mathcal{S}
, we have the convergence in probability

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(w)dL_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(w)\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta\psi(w)\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle dw

(1.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi(w)h(w)dw\,,\quad\text{for}\quad h(w):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle\,,

provided
w\mapsto\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle
is smooth enough to justify the integration
by parts. For
\mathcal{S}
large enough, this implies the convergence in probability of the
esd
-s

L_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}
to a limit whose density
is
h(w)
.

For example,
[
3
]
follows this approach, inductively over
d\geq 2
, and

[

]

(who for Haar distributed orthogonal

, in probability, implies the same for

, whose limit is
the Brown measure of a certain
operator). Utilizing this, it suffices in our case to only consider
Step 2
. Indeed,
for any adjacency matrix
\mathsf{A}_{N}
of a
d
-regular digraph we have that

is a (Markov) probability transition matrix, hence of spectral norm one. Any
such
\mathsf{A}_{N}
must then have singular values bounded by
d
and a Hilbert-Schmidt norm

\|\mathsf{A}_{N}\|_{2}\leq d\sqrt{N}
. The same applies for any sum of

d
unitary matrices, and in particular to the sum
\mathsf{B}_{N}
of
d
independent
Haar distributed unitary matrices. Recall, from
[

(of density

as in (

)), and from

that for almost every

\frac{1}{N}\log|\det(w\mathsf{I}_{N}-\mathsf{B}_{N})|\to\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle\,.

It then follows from applying the

[

for ensembles

The following theorem, which is our main result, addresses the latter convergence.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{N,d}
denote the uniform distribution over all
d
-regular, simple digraphs on
N
vertices,
with
\mathsf{A}_{N}=A_{\mathcal{G}}
the adjacency matrix of such a digraph, and

the minimal singular

\mathsf{A}_{N}-w\mathsf{I}_{N}

Suppose for any fixed
\kappa>0
and a.e

,

(1.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N,d}(s_{1}\leq\exp[-N^{\kappa}])=0\,.

Then, Girko’s method
Step 2
holds for our
\nu_{N}^{w}

(i.e.
\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle\to\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle
, in probability),
and the
esd

\{L_{\mathsf{A}_{N}}\}
converge weakly, in probability
to
\mu_{d}
, whose density is
h_{d}(w)
of (
1.1
).

There is a long history regarding bounds on the smallest singular value of random matrix ensembles
which are of the type of (
1.6
) (see, e.g.
[
18
,
16
,
3
,
2
,
13
,
14
]
).
Of particular note is the paper
[

]
, which for

shows that

as

[

]
relies on

As we shortly show, Theorem

following local law for the Green’s function
\mathsf{G}_{N}(z,w):=[\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{A}_{N}}(0,w)-z\mathsf{I}_{2N}]^{-1}

of our Hermitized matrices.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m_{\star}(z,w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda-z)^{-1}d\nu^{w}(\lambda)
(which is well defined
for
\Im(z)>0
).
For any
0<\mathfrak{c}<1
and

, there exist

\mathbb{P}_{N,d}(\Omega^{\prime}_{N})\geq 1-O(N^{-\mathfrak{c}})

of

(1.7)

\sup_{\mathcal{G}\in\Omega_{N}^{\prime}}\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D},\Im(z)\geq N^{-\varepsilon}}\Big|\frac{1}{2N}{\rm trace}(\mathsf{G}_{N}(z,w))-m_{\star}(z,w)\Big|=o_{N}(1)\,,

for some non-random

as
N\to\infty
.

Many recent papers deal with establishing local laws for graph models. For example,
[
5

4
,
12

derive local laws via switching estimates that generate useful
self-consistent equations. The most relevant here are

[
4
,
12
]
, which introduce the strategy of preserving local neighborhoods and performing switches along the boundary of the local neighborhood. While we follow the same strategy,
a major novel challenge arises because having a digraph model, requires us to keep track of two
different types of edges. In particular, as we show in the sequel, one must track the specific orientation of the edges used in the path between each pair of vertices, which strongly impacts the value of
the entry of
\mathsf{G}_{N}
that corresponds to this pair. Proving Theorem
1.3
is

We proceed now with a standard, short derivation of Theorem

out of

, and for the remainder of this paper, present the proof

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fixing
d\geq 3
,
\mathfrak{c}>0
,
\kappa<\varepsilon(\mathfrak{c},d)
and

for which
(
1.6
) holds, we restrict
wlog
to the collections
\Omega_{N}\subset\Omega_{N}^{\prime}

of digraphs for which
the minimal singular value of

\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}-w\mathsf{I}_{N}
exceeds
\delta_{N}:=e^{-N^{\kappa}}
(and
\mathbb{P}_{N,d}(\Omega_{N})\to 1
).
Recall that for any
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and

,

\frac{1}{2N}{\rm trace}(\mathsf{G}_{N}(z,w))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda-z)^{-1}d\nu_{N}^{w}(\lambda)\,.

In particular, (
1.7
) implies the convergence as

of the Stieltjes transform of
\nu_{N}^{w}
to that of
\nu^{w}
, at any

, uniformly over

\Omega_{N}
. This of course implies that

\Omega_{N}
. Further, with

,
and recalling that the spectral norm of
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}(w)

we have for

\Omega_{N}
, that as

,

|\langle{\rm Log},\nu_{N}^{w}\rangle_{\eta}^{\infty}-\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle_{\eta}^{\infty}|\to 0\,.

By definition, on
\Omega_{N}
we have that
\nu_{N}^{w}(-(\delta_{N},\delta_{N}))=0
.
We also know that
\langle{\rm Log},\nu^{w}\rangle_{0}^{\eta}\to 0

,

Remark 1.4

1.1.
The mode and the Green’s function

We start with a more formal definition of our model.

Definition 1.5

(Digraph)
.

A digraph
\mathcal{G}
has vertices
V
and directed edges
E
, where each oriented
edges
e=(v,w)\in E
has an initial vertex
v
and a terminal vertex
w\neq v
(in particular,
the
(v,w)
and
(w,v)
are distinct edges). In the adjacency matrix
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}

of a digraph
\mathcal{G}
, the entry
a_{i,j}
indicates the number of edges that
are oriented from the vertex
i
to the vertex
j

(and such adjacency matrix is, in general, not Hermitian), where a simple digraph has
only
\{0,1\}
-valued entries.

Definition 1.6

(Uniformly random
d
-regular digraph)
.

A
d
-regular digraph is a graph such that for each vertex
v\in V
, exactly
d
edges of the graph
have
v
as their initial vertex and exactly
d
edges have
v
as their terminal vertex. We let

\mathcal{G}_{N,d}
denote the (finite) set of all
d
-regular, simple, digraphs on
N
vertices with

\mathbb{P}_{N,d}
the corresponding uniform distribution on
\mathcal{G}_{N,d}
(namely,
selecting each specific instance from
\mathcal{G}_{N,d}
with probability
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{N,d}|}
).

Hereafter we often use lighter notations, with the dimension
N
and the identity matrix
\mathsf{I}_{N}

implicit, as are sometimes also the arguments
w
and
z
. Choosing
\mathcal{G}

according to
\mathbb{P}_{N,d}
,
wlog
we also replace
\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
hereafter by
the normalized matrix

A:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\mathsf{A}_{\mathcal{G}}
and for
w\in\mathbb{C}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, let

(1.8)

G(z,w):=H(z,w)^{-1}\,,\qquad H(z,w):=\begin{bmatrix}-z&A-w\\
A^{*}-\overline{w}&-z\end{bmatrix}\,.

By definition,
G(z,w)=\sqrt{d-1}\mathsf{G}_{N}(\sqrt{d-1}z,\sqrt{d-1}w)
and
towards Theorem
1.3
our goal is to
control for large
N
, the normalized trace of
G(z,w)
, where of most interest is the
case of
\Im(z)\downarrow 0
.

To this end, recall the following expansion of
the Green’s function, at
|z|
large enough,

(1.9)

G(z,w)=-z^{-1}(I-z^{-1}H(0,w))^{-1}=-z^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{-k}H(0,w)^{k}.

Fixing
w
and using
H:=H(0,w)
, we further have for any
a,b\leq N
and
k\geq 0
,

[H^{2k}]_{a,b}=\sum_{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{2k-1}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}H_{v_{2j},v_{2j+1}+N}H_{v_{2j+1}+N,v_{2j+2}},

where the sum is over
v_{i}\leq N
, with
v_{0}=a
and
v_{2k}=b
. Similarly,

[H^{2k}]_{a+N,b+N}=\sum_{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{2k-1}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}H_{v_{2j}+N,v_{2j+1}}H_{v_{2j+1},v_{2j+2}+N},

again summing over
v_{i}\leq N
, with
v_{0}=a
and
v_{2k}=b
. Moreover,

[H^{2k+1}]_{a,b+N}=\sum_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{2k}}\Big[\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}H_{v_{2j},v_{2j+1}+N}H_{v_{2j+1}+N,v_{2j+2}}\Big]H_{v_{2k},b+N}

summing over
v_{i}\leq N
, with
v_{0}=a
, whereas
[H^{2k+1}]_{a+N,b}
is the complex conjugate of

[H^{2k+1}]_{a,b+N}
.

Now, in case

v_{2j}\neq v_{2j+1}
,
an entry
H_{v_{2j},v_{2j+1}+N}
represents a forward edge (agreeing with the orientation) from vertex
v_{2j}
to vertex
v_{2j+1}
while
H_{v_{2j+1}+N,v_{2j+2}}=H_{v_{2j+2},v_{2j+1}+N}
represents a traversal through a backward edge (opposing the orientation) from the vertex
v_{2j+1}
to
v_{2j+2}
. For this reason, we include in a local neighborhood of each vertex
v\in\mathcal{G}
also all
those vertices that are reachable through edges traversed in the reverse direction.
When we have in the expansion for
[H^{2k}]_{a,b}
some
v_{2j}=v_{2j+1}
, it follows that

v_{2j-1}
travels to
v_{2j}
via a backward edge, stays at
v_{2j}
with weight factor
-w

(as there are no self-edges in
\mathcal{G}
), and proceeds from
v_{2j+1}
to
v_{2j+2}

via a backward edge (once again). Similarly, in case we have in such expansion
some
v_{2j-1}=v_{2j}
, we enter
v_{2j-1}
via a forward edge, stay with weight factor

-\overline{w}
and leave
v_{2j}
to
v_{2j+1}
again via a forward edge. Thus, the inclusion
of such factors
-w
or
-\overline{w}
breaks the otherwise
regular pattern of alternating between traveling on forward edges and on backward edges.
More generally, we may travel a few forward edges in succession or a few backward edges
in succession (each consecutive pair of forward edges then contributes the factor

-\overline{w}
while each consecutive pair of backward edges contributes
-w
).
This
solidifies the assertion that a local neighborhood
should consider traversal via forward and backward edges
in any type of succession
,
measuring the neighborhood’s size by the distance in the unoriented graph.

1.2.
The
d
-regular directed tree and its Green’s function.

Our proof relies on relating entries

of the Green’s function for a
d
-regular digraph,
to the Green’s function of the local neighborhood of the graph. Typically, such local neighborhood
should look like some subset of the infinite directed tree which we describe next.

Definition 1.7

(Construction of the
d
-regular
K
-neighborhoods in directed tree)
.

First, fix a vertex
r
to be the root. From this root

, create a
2d
regular tree of
K

2d
children while any other vertex that is not a root or a child at the
K
level will have
2d-1
children. At this point, we can start orienting the edges. For convenience, assume that we have drawn this tree on the plane so that the root is on the very top and any vertex at distance
\ell
from the root will be below its parent at the
(\ell-1)
-th level. With this planar drawing, the notion of
j
leftmost children of a vertex would be a meaningful notion. With these notions, we can now describe the orientation of our edges.

Our process proceeds level by level; first, we orient the edges that are adjacent to the root in the unoriented graph. Afterwards, we proceed to orient the edges that are adjacent to a vertex of distance
1
from the root in the unoriented graph and so on. At the root
r
, we will orient the leftmost
d
edges away from the root and the rightmost
d
edges are oriented toward the root. Now assume that we have oriented the edges that are adjacent to a vertex of distances
1
to
\ell-1
from the root in the unoriented graph. We now proceed to orient the edges that are adjacent to a vertex at the
\ell
-th level in the unoriented graph that have not been assigned an orientation as of yet. Observe for every vertex
v
at the
\ell
-th level, there are still
2d-1
children edges that have not been assigned an orientation and these edges connect the vertex
v
to a vertex below it.

Depending on the orientation of the parent edge (i.e., the edge already assigned an orientation), there will be two ways to assign the orientation to the remaining children edges. If the parent is oriented away from the vertex in question, then orient the
d-1
leftmost children edges of the vertex away from the vertex and the remaining
d
edges towards the vertex. If the parent edge is oriented towards the vertex, then we orient the
d
leftmost children away from the vertex and the remaining
d-1
edges towards the vertex.

Definition 1.8

(
d
-regular infinite directed trees)
.

We have three natural notions of
d
-regular directed trees (while only one is truly
d
-regular,
we abuse the notation to make our descriptions more concise):

(a)

The
d
-regular infinite directed tree of Definition

is denoted
by
\mathcal{T}
. Ignoring orientation, it is a
2d
-regular infinite tree, and every

d
outedges and
d
inedges.

(b)

The tree
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is such that its unoriented version is a
(2d-1)

2d-1
edges while all other vertices have
2d
edges in the unoriented version. When oriented,

\mathcal{T}_{1}

d-1

(c)

The tree

is also such that its unoriented version is a

-regular directed tree

Our primary quantity of interest is the Green’s function

However, in order to utilize

\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}

removing the root

\mathcal{T}
yields

disjoint copies of

\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
d
disjoint copies of
\mathcal{T}_{2}

\mathcal{T}_{1}

\mathcal{T}_{1}

\mathcal{T}_{2}

\mathcal{T}_{2}

and

rows in the corresponding Hermitized

Specifically, our starting point in deriving Green’s function identities is the block decomposition
of a matrix and its inverse,

(1.10)

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Z&W\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\

resulting with by elementary algebra with the Schur complement formulas

X=(A-BD^{-1}C)^{-1},\qquad\quad Z=-D^{-1}CX,\qquad\quad W=D^{-1}+D^{-1}CXBD^{-1}\,.

Suppressing the common arguments

(1.11)

\displaystyle G|_{T\times T}

\displaystyle=(H|_{T\times T}-H|_{T\times T^{c}}\,G^{(T)}\,H|_{T^{c}\times T})^{-1},

(1.12)

\displaystyle G|_{T^{c}\times T}

\displaystyle=-G^{(T)}\,H|_{T^{c}\times T}\,G|_{T\times T},

(1.13)

\displaystyle G|_{T^{c}\times T^{c}}

\displaystyle=G^{(T)}+G^{(T)}\,H|_{T^{c}\times T}\,G|_{T\times T}\,H|_{T\times T^{c}}G^{(T)}\,.

Our analysis of

relies on the removal of both indices
r
and

associated with the root vertex
r
, to relate

\mathcal{T}
to
d
copies of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

\mathcal{T}_{2}

(we use the same notation

for the root indices of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
,
and it be clear from the context to which tree’s root we refer each time). Likewise,
removing the root of

d
copies of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

copies of
\mathcal{T}_{2}

index of its children (namely, the

\mathcal{T}_{2}
). Moreover, having no contribution from

to the entries indexed by

or

(1.14)

\begin{bmatrix}(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{r,r}&(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{r,r+\aleph}\\

\begin{bmatrix}(G_{\mathcal{T}_{2}})_{r,r}&(G_{\mathcal{T}_{2}})_{r,r+\aleph}\\

Since, as mentioned earlier,

(1.16)

\begin{bmatrix}(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r,r}&(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r,r+\aleph}\\

Further, the investigation of the path expansion of the Green’s function at large

1.3.
Extension of graphs and main result

A key ingredient in the asymptotic evaluation of the Green’s function for our digraphs, is the
following concept of graph extension (or more precisely, the extension of the
associated weighted adjacency matrix).

Definition 1.9

(Extensions of graphs)
.

Suppose

is a digraph of vertex set

, where each

has

and out-degree

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{\text{def}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})=

\displaystyle H-\sum_{v\in V}\Big[\frac{d-d_{o}(v)-\text{def}_{o}(v)}{d-1}\Delta_{1}e_{v,v}+\frac{d-d_{in}(v)-def_{in}(v)}{d-1}\Delta_{2}e_{v+\aleph,v+\aleph}\Big]\,,

where

and

denote, respectively, the

and

(1.21)

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{i}(\mathcal{G},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})=

\displaystyle H-\sum_{v\in V}\left[\frac{d-d_{o}(v)}{d-1}\Delta_{1}e_{v,v}+\frac{d-d_{in}(v)-\delta_{v,r}}{d-1}\Delta_{2}e_{v+\aleph,v+\aleph}\right].

(1.22)

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{o}(\mathcal{G},\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})=H-\sum_{v\in V}\left[\frac{d-d_{o}(v)-\delta_{v,r}}{d-1}\Delta_{1}e_{v,v}+\frac{d-d_{in}(v)}{d-1}\Delta_{2}e_{v+\aleph,v+\aleph}\right].

2.
The heuristics behind
the proof of Theorem
1.12

2.1.
Part 1: Heuristics of the switching argument

We proceed to provide the heuristics behind our derivation of the self-consistent equations
for the Green’s function of the random
d
-regular digraph. To this end, we first introduce
the following important control quantities

(2.1)

\displaystyle Q_{I}:=\frac{1}{Nd}\sum_{(x\to y)\in E}G_{y+N,y+N}^{(x,x+N)}

\displaystyle Q_{O}:=\frac{1}{Nd}\sum_{(y\to x)\in E}G_{y,y}^{(x,x+N)}

(and as before, we suppress the arguments
z,w
whenever their specific value is irrelevant).
Such quantities
deal with the effect at of removing an edge on the Green’s function of the digraph. Specifically,

G_{y+N,y+N}^{(x,x+N)}

measures the effect at
y
of
removing the incoming edge
x\to y
,
and
G_{y,y}^{(x,x+N)}
likewise measures the effect at
y
of removing the outgoing edge

y\to x
. Thus, our control quantities
Q_{I}
and
Q_{O}
measure the average effect on
the Green’s function due to the removal of a uniformly chosen incoming edge,
or outgoing edge, respectively.
As outlined next, our proof strategy consists of the following two steps:

(a)

Our first step relates the Green’s function of
\mathcal{G}
around a vertex
v
to the
Green’s function of the
\ell
neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(v)
of
v
for
\ell_{N}=O(\log\log N)
.
It yields a formula for the entries of
G
inside
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(v)
which
depends only on the edges connecting
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(v)
to
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}^{c}(v)
.

(b)

Performing a switching of the edges connecting
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(v)
to
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}^{c}(v)

averages out the effects of specific edges, leading to
Q_{I}
and
Q_{O}
. We then show that
the latter functions concentrate for large
N
around the non-random
m_{\infty}
.

2.1.1.
Reduction to the local

neighborhood

Ignoring for the time being the specific value of
\ell=\ell_{N}
, consider the relation
via the Schur complement formula between entries of the Green’s function of

within

and the matrix

restricted to this local neighborhood.
Specifically, denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{K}(v)
the collection of indices in

and

, with

of directed edges that start in
\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)
and terminate in
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{K}(v)
, while

denotes the directed edges that start in
\mathcal{N}^{c}_{K}(v)
and

\mathcal{N}_{K}(v)
. Considering
(

)

(2.2)

We now proceed with the following approximations of the two sums on the

In order to even discuss our proof, it is first necessary to devote some time to defining appropriate notation and the correct order parameters. The goal of this section is to introduce the notation that we will use later as well as give a broad overview of the strategy of the proof. The sections that will appear later will go over many of the detailed estimates.

We will start with definitions related to the graph.

Definition 2.1

(Ball of Radius

that need to be removed in order to make the neighborhood tree-like.

Our method also requires the introduction of many scales of order parameters.

Definition 2.2

(Order Parameters)
.

(1)

We define the value
\mathfrak{R}:=\frac{\mathfrak{c}}{4}\log_{d-1}N

(2)

We introduce the value

(3)

Finally, we have the value

We have the following order of scales on our constants:
\mathfrak{R}>r\gg l

Indeed with our order parameters, we can make the following definition of good

3.

|P_{v_{1}v_{2}}|\leq C\prod_{i=b_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}K_{e^{1}_{i},e^{1}_{i+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}-1}K_{e^{2}_{j},e^{2}_{j+1}}

.

The path back from

to

Thus, we would have that,

(3.6)

\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}}|P_{rv_{1}}||P_{v_{1}v_{2}}||P_{v_{2}r}|\leq\sum_{A_{1},v_{1},v_{2}}C^{3}K_{A_{1}r}^{2}K_{A_{1}v_{1}}^{2}K_{A_{1}v_{2}}^{2}.

In the general case, we would need to generate more ancestral vertices. One can define

Step 2: The summation along the tree

The purpose of the tree structure is to introduce the new summation variables

the fact that the products

Assume that the path from

\displaystyle|P_{v_{i},v_{i+1}}|

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}P_{\mathfrak{e}_{j}},

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{k}|P_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}|

\displaystyle\leq C^{4k}\prod_{\mathfrak{e}\in T}K^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}},

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}|P_{v_{i}v_{i+1}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}C^{4k}\prod_{\mathfrak{e}\in T}K^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}}

\mathcal{T}

Fix a tree structure
\mathcal{T}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\prod_{\mathfrak{e}\in T}K^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\prod_{\mathfrak{e}\in T^{\prime}}K^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}}\sum_{p\to l}K^{2}_{l}\leq(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))\sum_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\prod_{\mathfrak{e}\in T^{\prime}}K^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}}

The last line sums up

is an application of Lemma

Since, we know that the distance between any two vertices

Part 2: Deriving the correct coefficient structure of the first order term

We have more exact expressions for the coefficient of the first order terms.

(3.8)

\sum_{v\in\delta(G)}\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}(Q_{I}-m_{\infty})+\sum_{v\in\delta(G)}\frac{d-d_{\text{in}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v+\aleph}P_{v+\aleph,r}(Q_{O}-m_{\infty}).

Here,

We can imbed our original graph

into a tree

\text{diam}(G)
.
For each vertex

denote the vertices of

that are children of

denotes the vertices of
T_{\text{diam}}
that are children of

An alternative way to write the expression in (

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\delta(G)}\Big[\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{out}_{v}}P_{r,w}P_{w,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{out}_{v}}P_{r,w+\aleph}P_{w+\aleph,r}[Q_{O}-m_{\infty}]\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{v\in\delta(G)}\Big[\frac{d-d_{\text{in}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{in}_{v}}P_{r,w}P_{w,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{in}_{v}}P_{r,w+\aleph}P_{w+\aleph,r}[Q_{O}-m_{\infty}]\Big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{v\in\delta(T_{\text{diam}})}\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}(Q_{I}-m_{\infty})+\frac{d-d_{\text{in}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v+\aleph}P_{v+\aleph,r}(Q_{O}-m_{\infty}).

The main point is that the last line can be computed exactly via a standard recursion, while the terms in the first two lines should correspond to a smaller order error term with the correct singularity we have to consider. We now treat bounding the last two lines. Indeed, for
w\in S^{out}_{v}
, we can first try to write
P_{rw}P_{wr}
as
P_{rv}P_{vr}(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{vw}(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{wv}
. Here, we treat
v
as the root of a new tree of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and used the fact that the product
P_{rw}
splits as the product
P_{rv}
times this
(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{vw}
. The main benefit now is the fact that we have explicit ways to compute

.

Namely, the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}Y&X\\
X&Y\end{bmatrix}
satisfies the property that we can write

\Big[\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{out}_{v}}P_{r,w}P_{w,r}[Q_{I}-m_{\infty}]-\sum_{w\in S^{out}_{v}}P_{r,w+\aleph}P_{w+\aleph,r}[Q_{O}-m_{\infty}]\Big]

as,

(3.10)

\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}\Big(I-\begin{bmatrix}Y&X\\
X&Y\end{bmatrix}^{\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})-\text{dist}(v,r)}\Big)\begin{bmatrix}Q_{I}-m_{\infty}(z,w)\\
Q_{O}-m_{\infty}(z,w)\end{bmatrix}.

Now, we can diagonalize the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}Y&X\\
X&Y\end{bmatrix}

and show that we can rewrite (

) as,

(3.11)

\frac{d-d_{\text{out}}(v)}{d-1}P_{r,v}P_{v,r}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[1-(X+Y)^{k}]Q_{+}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[1-(Y-X)^{k}]Q_{-}\right].

In absolute value, this can be bounded by,

(3.12)

\frac{d-d_{\text{out}(v)}}{d-1}|P_{r,v}||P_{v,r}|(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))\left[|1-(X+Y)||Q_{+}|+|1-(Y-X)||Q_{-}|\right]

Finally, we see that,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{v}\frac{d-d_{\text{out}(v)}}{d-1}|P_{r,v}||P_{v,r}|(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))\left[|1-(X+Y)||Q_{+}|+|1-(Y-X)||Q_{-}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq O((1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})^{2})\left[|1-(X+Y)||Q_{+}|+|1-(Y-X)||Q_{-}|\right]

We can treat the second line of (
3.9
) in a similar way.

Combining these error estimates gives us the desired result (

Our next lemma gives us a perturbation analysis of
G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))
with respect to

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a graph with excess of at most
1
. Assume further that
\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})^{3}[|Q_{I}-m_{\infty}|+|Q_{O}-m_{\infty}|]\ll 1
. Then, we have,

(3.14)

\displaystyle|G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))-G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty}))|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{d-1}\right)^{\text{dist}(i,j)}(1+\text{dist}(i,j))[|Q_{I}-m_{\infty}|+|Q_{O}-m_{\infty}|]

Proof.

As a shorthand, we let
P_{ij}
denote the Green’s function
G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})
, while
G_{ij}
will denote the Green’s function of
G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))
. We can determine the value of
|G_{ij}-P_{ij}|
via the resolvent formula. Let
l_{\alpha}
denote the indices corresponding to vertices on the boundary of the graph
\mathcal{G}
. We have the following,

(3.15)

|G_{ij}-P_{ij}|=\sum_{k}\sum_{l^{1},\ldots,l^{k}}P_{i,l^{1}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}[Q_{j(l^{m})}-m_{\infty}]P_{{l^{m}},l^{m+1}},

where
j(l)=I
or
O

if
l^{m}
is of the form
v+\aleph
or
v
, respectively. We use the convention that, upon fixing
k
, the final

is the same as
r
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, our biggest issue is to determine the value of the
k

(3.16)

Ex_{k}(i,j):=\sum_{l^{1},\ldots,l^{k}}|P_{i,l^{1}}|\prod_{m=1}^{k}|P_{l^{m}.l^{m+1}}|.

Now, we cannot directly apply the analysis detailed in the aforementioned lemma, as the proof there assumed a purely tree-like structure.

What we have do instead is appeal to the fact that our graph has a tree-like covering and relate the values of
P_{l^{i},l^{j}}
to the Green’s functions of the covering. Now, the results of Lemma
A.6
show that, up to a constant factor,
|P_{l^{i},l^{i+1}}|
can be bounded by
2\max(|P_{\tilde{l}^{i},\tilde{l}^{i+1}_{1}}|,|P_{\tilde{l}^{i},\tilde{l}^{i+1}_{2}}|)
. Here,
\tilde{l}^{i}
is some chosen covering of
l^{i}
and
\tilde{l}^{i+1}_{1}
and
\tilde{l}^{i+1}_{2}
are the two closest chosen coverings of
l^{i+1}
to
\tilde{l}^{i}
.
Thus, when we consider the lift to the covering tree,

\sum_{l^{1},\ldots,l^{k}}|P_{i,l^{1}}|\prod_{m=1}^{k}|P_{l^{m},l^{m+1}}|,

can be upper bounded by the sum

2^{k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{dist}(\tilde{l}^{n},\tilde{l}^{n+1})\leq 3\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})\\
\tilde{l}^{k+1}\text{ covers }j\end{subarray}}|P_{\tilde{i},\tilde{l}^{1}}|\prod_{m=1}^{k}|P_{\tilde{l}^{m},\tilde{l}^{m+1}}|.

Here,
\tilde{i}
is a fixed lift of
i
.
The restriction that
\text{dist}(\tilde{l}^{n},\tilde{l}^{n+1})\leq 3\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})
comes from the fact that the closest and the second closest cover of any point differ by distance at most
|C|\leq 2\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})
. To perform the above summation, we first fix the final cover of the point
j
, and then compute the above sum in the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Namely, we decompose the product
|P_{l^{m},l^{m+1}}|
into its
K

K
factors associated with the vertices appearing on the path
p_{ij}
between the lift of
i
and
j
may not appear twice. To deal with these vertices, we remove the edges in the tree that correspond to vertices that are connected via this path. The remaining forest graph can be bounded via the same methods of the proof of Lemma
3.2
and will give the same bound.

We only need to reintroduce the
K
factors that we did not consider on the path between
i
and
j
. Observe that at least
dist(\tilde{i},\tilde{j})-3k
of the vertices have
K
factors associated with them, since each factorization from (
A.10
) can lose no more than
3
natural
K
factors.
Once can see that the
K
factors corresponding to this path between
i
and
j
will give a factor bounded by
\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\right)^{\text{dist}(\tilde{i},\tilde{j})}(\sqrt{d-1})^{3k}
. We see that the last
(\sqrt{d-1})^{3k}
can be absorbed by
|Q_{I}-m_{\infty}|^{k}
. This latter factor can allow us again to sum over the possible lifts of
\tilde{j}
, since the successive lifts of
\tilde{j}
increase by distance
|C|
from the lift
\tilde{i}
. This allows the term
\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\right)^{\text{dist}(\tilde{i},\tilde{j})}
to be summable over successive lifts.

Now, if we want to be careful, we can improve the first order coefficient from
(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))^{3}
to
1+\text{dist}(i,j)
. The method of proof is similar, one only needs to more careful in the discussion of branching. First, consider the path
p_{ij}
between the lifts of
i
and
j
. To choose
\tilde{l}_{1}
, one first picks a point
Q_{i}
on the path
p_{ij}
and then starts branching from there. There are
1+\text{dist}(\tilde{i},\tilde{j})
choices for the point
Q_{i}
. Now, if one takes into account that
\tilde{l}_{1}
is the lift of a boundary point, one can use the superior analysis of the first order coefficient from Lemma
3.2
to get rid of the factor of
(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))^{2}
and replace it with an order

coefficient. This gives us our final answer.
∎

Because of the particular dependence of pointwise estimates on Green’s functions from

Corollary 3.5
.

Consider the setting of the previous Lemma

Without loss of generality we may assume that

Let

G_{rx}

(3.18)

\sum_{x\in\delta(\mathcal{G})}\sum_{o_{x}}|G_{r,x+o_{x}}|^{2}\leq 2\sum_{x\in\delta(\mathcal{G})}\sum_{o_{x}}[|P_{r,x+o_{x}}|^{2}+|G_{r,x+o_{x}}-P_{r,x+o_{x}}|^{2}].

Recall

We have,

(3.19)

\displaystyle|G_{r,x+o_{x}}-P_{r,x+o_{x}}|^{2}

\sum_{x}\sum_{o_{x}}Ex_{k}(r,x+o_{x})^{2}\leq(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G}))^{k}\sum_{l^{1},\ldots,l^{k},l^{k+1}}|P_{il^{1}}|^{2}\prod_{m=1}^{k}|P_{l^{m},l^{m+1}}|^{2}

to understand this as

\lesssim(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})^{6k+3}

\ll 1

The stability result detailed above shows, as one would expect, that the approximation of

Lemma 3.6

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a graph containing two vertices

and

\mathcal{H}
be a subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
that contains the radius

(3.22)

\displaystyle|G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))-G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{H},Q_{I},Q_{O}))|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{d-1}\Big)^{r}

\displaystyle+(1+\text{diam}(\mathcal{G})^{2}[S_{g}^{1}|Q_{I}+Q_{O}-2m_{\infty}(z,w)|+S_{g}^{2}|Q_{I}-Q_{O}|]

\displaystyle+(\log N)^{2}[|Q_{I}-m_{\infty}|^{2}+|Q_{O}-m_{\infty}|^{2}],

provided we have that
(\log N)^{9}|Q_{I/O}-m_{\infty}|\ll 1

We can write the adjacency matrices of

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\

\mathcal{H}
.

By the Schur complement formula, we have,

(3.24)

G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))=[(A-BC^{-1}B^{*})^{-1}]_{ij}.

At this point, we can apply the resolvent estimates,

(3.25)

\displaystyle|G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))-G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{H},Q_{I},Q_{O}))|

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c,d}G_{ia}(Ext(\mathcal{G},Q_{I},Q_{O}))B_{ab}(C^{-1}_{bc}-\delta_{b,c}Q_{k})B^{*}_{cd}G_{dj}(Ext(\mathcal{H},Q_{I},Q_{O}))

Here,
Q_{k}=Q_{I}
if

is of the form
v+\aleph
for a vertex

in the complement of
\mathcal{H}
while

if

is of the form
v
for a vertex
v
in the complement of
\mathcal{H}
. Let us first consider the off-diagonal terms with

.

In order to get non-zero terms,
we see from the above that

\mathcal{H}
, and are thus distance greater than

from
a
and

and

\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathcal{H}
that are adjacent to vertices in
\mathcal{H}
. Our combinatorial assumption on the graph
\mathcal{G}
allows us to make the following assertion: there are only finitely many pairs of vertices
(b,c)
with

such that
C^{-1}_{bc}\neq 0

Just to give a summary for the reasoning behind this point, we remark that

5.1.

In the past section, we used

By the Ward Identity, we have that,

8.
Concentration Estimates

The main issue with our stability estimate in equation (

) is the presence of the term whose decay is
\left(\frac{1}{d-1}\right)^{\frac{|d_{j}-d_{i}|}{2}}
. As long as

and

are relatively small, this term will not decay as

and

While the triangle inequality will not show an improvement, the main point is that this new graph came from random switchings. Thus, we can try to apply a concentration argument to show that that the quantity that appears above has superior bounds. Loosely speaking, the improvement will improve the constant
\sum_{\alpha}|P_{i,l_{\alpha}}||P_{l_{\alpha},j}|

\sqrt{\sum_{\alpha}|P_{i,l_{\alpha}}P_{l_{\alpha},j}|^{2}}

(A.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\partial B_{k}(r),o_{l}}|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r+o_{r},l+o_{l}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r+o_{r},r}|^{2}\sum_{l\in\partial B_{k-1}(r),o_{l}}|(G_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})_{r+\aleph,l+o_{l}}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{d-1}|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r+o_{r},r+\aleph}|^{2}\sum_{l\in\partial B_{k-1}(r),o_{l}}|(G_{\mathcal{T}_{2}})_{r,l+o_{l}}|^{2}.

We conclude by observing that both terms are bounded, uniformly in

The following lemma allows us to generalize our bounds on the decay of Green’s function values with distance to the case of almost tree-like graphs with at most one cycle. The most important part is the relation of the Green’s function of the graph to the Green’s function of the simply connected cover, which is the infinite tree.

Lemma A.6
.

Consider a digraph
\mathcal{G}
with excess at most 1; let
i
and
j
be vertices in
\mathcal{G}
. Let
p_{1}
be the shortest path connecting
i
and
j
in
\mathcal{G}
and let
p_{2}
be the second shortest non-backtracking path connecting
i
and
j
in
\mathcal{G}
. Furthermore, let
sp_{1}
be a sequence of letters
f
or
r
indicating whether each edge in the path
p_{1}
were traversed in the forward or reverse direction, and let
sp_{2}
be the same object for the path
p_{2}
. Given these sequences
sp_{1}
and
sp_{2}
, consider the ‘pure’ infinite tree
\mathcal{T}
and vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
\mathcal{T}
that are reachable from the root
r
using a sequences of edges that match
sp_{1}
and
sp_{2}
, respectively. For example, if
sp_{1}
involved only forward edges, then
v_{1}
should be a vertex that is reachable from
r
using only forward edges.

We can derive the following estimate on the Green’s function extension,

(A.17)

\displaystyle|G(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty}))_{i+o_{i},j+o_{j}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r+o_{i},v_{1}+o_{j}}|+|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{r+o_{i},v_{2}+o_{j}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\frac{\max\{|m_{\infty}|,|m^{uod}_{\infty}|\}}{\sqrt{d-1}}\right]^{\text{dist}(i,j)}.

Here,
o_{i}
and
o_{j}
can be
0
or
\aleph
to distinguish the two indices that correspond to the same vertex.

Remark A.7
.

In the top line of the preceding bound, we
distinguish the specific edges that are used in the path connecting
i
and
j

in order to be able to derive sharp bounds when large neighborhoods are considered.

Proof.

The extension of
Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})
involves adding an appropriate infinite tree to every boundary vertex of the digraph
\mathcal{G}
that does not yet have full degree. Since we have assumed that the digraph
\mathcal{G}
has excess at most 1, this would imply that there is at most one cycle in the extension graph.

Now, we can always relate the Green’s function of a graph to its infinite cover as follows. First, we remark that the Green’s function is the unique solution to the following system of equations,

(A.18)

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}+zG_{ik}=wG_{\aleph+i,k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\sum_{(i\to j)\in E}G_{\aleph+j,k}

\displaystyle zG_{i,\aleph+k}=wG_{\aleph+i,\aleph+k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\sum_{(i\to j)\in E}G_{\aleph+j,\aleph+k}

\displaystyle zG_{\aleph+i,k}=\overline{w}\,G_{ik}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\sum_{(j\to i)\in E}G_{j,k}

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}+zG_{\aleph+i,\aleph+k}=\overline{w}\,G_{i,\aleph+k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\sum_{(j\to i)\in E}G_{\aleph+j,\aleph+k}.

We argue that if we have a new graph
\hat{H}
, with Green’s function
\hat{G}
, that is a lift of the graph
H
, with covering map
\pi
. Then, we would have the following relationship between the Green’s function
G
of the original graph and the Green’s function of the covering graph.

(A.19)

\displaystyle G_{ij}=\sum_{y:\pi(y)=j}G_{xy}

\displaystyle G_{\aleph+i,j}=\sum_{y:\pi(y)=j}G_{\aleph+x,y}

\displaystyle G_{i,j+\aleph}=\sum_{y:\pi(y)=j}G_{x,\aleph+y}

\displaystyle G_{i+\aleph,j+\aleph}=\sum_{y:\pi(y)=j}G_{x+\aleph,y+\aleph}.

Here,
x
is a vertex in
\hat{H}
that maps to
i
under the map
\pi
; the value on the right hand side does not depend on the choice of
x
due to translation invariance. It is implicitly implied that the above results hold as long as the quantities that appear on the right hand side are convergent. In order to check the above relation, we substitute the proposed equalities in equation (
A.19
) into the defining equations of equation (
A.18
) and check that equality holds. This mainly involves realizing that if we choose
\hat{i}
so that
\pi(\hat{i})=i
, then we can parameterize the vertices
j
such that
i\to j
is an edge by some lifted vertex
\hat{j}
such that
\pi(\hat{j})=j
and
\hat{i}\to\hat{j}
is a edge in
\hat{H}
. This confirms the first two equations in equation (
A.18
). We can do the same thing for edges
j\to i
as well, and this verifies the last two equations of (
A.18
).

We can use the above formula to compute the Green’s function of
Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})
by setting
H=Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})
. This graph has only one cycle due to our assumption on the excess; thus, the covering graph
\hat{H}
is an infinite tree. The preimage of any vertex

will correspond to the set of non-backtracking paths in
\hat{H}
.
\sum_{y:\pi(y)=j}
now merely correspond to a ‘sum’ over non-backtracking paths.

For points
i
and
j
that are of distance
k
in
H
, the non-backtracking paths all have lengths at least
k
and the different non-backtracking paths have length that differ by
|C|
where
|C|
is the length of the unique simple cycle in
H
. Recall that
p_{1}
was the shortest path between
i
and
j
while
p_{2}
was the second-shortest non-backtracking path between
i
and
j
. We have the following structural properties on the paths
p_{j}
. We can divide
p_{2}
as
Q_{i}\cup C\cup Q_{o}
where
C
is the cycle and further shortest backtracking paths
p_{j}
are
Q_{i}\cup C\cup\ldots\cup C\cup Q_{o}
where there are
j-1
copies of
C
. The path
p_{1}
must be either included completely in
Q_{o}
or equal to the union
Q_{i}\cup Q_{o}
.

We also have the following property, let
v
be a fixed lift of
i
in
\hat{H}
and
w_{1}
be the lift of
j
in
\hat{H}
that is closest to
v
. Then, if
\hat{p}_{1}
is the path between
v
and
w_{1}
, and
\hat{sp}_{1}
is the set of labels ( either

or

) of the edges of
\hat{p}_{1}

is the same sequence as

. In addition, let

is the lift of

is the path between

.

Thus, we see that

\displaystyle|G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{v,w_{n}}|\leq|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{v,w_{1}}|+|(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{v,w_{2}}|\frac{1}{1-\frac{|m_{\infty}|\vee|m_{\infty}^{uod}|}{\sqrt{d-1}}}.

Here,

is the

∎

\sum_{j\in R_{k}}|G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})|^{2}\lesssim 1.

A graph with excess

We can understand graphs of excess

Recall that we have,

(A.21)

G_{ij}(Ext(\mathcal{G},m_{\infty},m_{\infty})=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(G_{\mathcal{T}})_{vw_{l}},

A.2.
Comparison of Stieltjes transforms

Lemma A.9
.

We show the following equality between the Stieltjes transform proposed in equations (
1.18
) and those coming from free probability computations as in
[
3
]
:

m_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}(z,w)=\sqrt{d-1}m_{\star}(\sqrt{d-1}z,\sqrt{d-1}w).

Proof.

First, notice that if we remove the scaling of edges in the adjacency matrix by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}
, then the corresponding equations for
m_{\infty}
and
m_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}
would be,

(A.24)

\displaystyle m_{\infty}=\frac{z+dm_{\infty}}{|w|^{2}-\big(z+dm_{\infty}\big)(z+(d-1)m_{\infty})},

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}=\frac{z+dm_{\infty}}{|w|^{2}-\big(z+dm_{\infty}\big)^{2}}.

Consider
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, our infinite
d
-regular digraph tree; from this, pick a subgraph
S_{d-1}
in
\mathcal{T}_{d}
that is isomorphic to the infinite
d-1
regular tree
\mathcal{T}_{d-1}
. For every vertex
v
that is part of
S_{d-1}
, there will be one edge,
i_{v}
, into it and one edge,
o_{v}
, out of it that will be part of the original graph
\mathcal{T}_{d}
, but will not be part of the subgraph
S_{d-1}
. If we remove the edge
o_{v}
, the connected component remaining that is totally disjoint from
S_{d-1}
will be isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
(the
2d-1
-ary tree). If we remove
i_{v}
, the connected component remaining that is totally disjoint from
S_{d-1}
will be isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}_{2}
. For
|z|>(d-1)+|w|^{2}
, we compute the Green’s function of the Hermitized (unscaled) adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{T}_{d}
by counting paths and carefully tracking where such paths uses edges of
S_{d-1}
. (We will naturally consider
w
and
w^{*}
to correspond to a weighted self-loops at the appropriate vertex.)

Consider trying to compute
(G_{\mathcal{T}_{d}})_{rr}
at the root
r
. For its first step, it could either take an edge that is part of
S_{d-1}
or else it could take the outedge
o_{r}
. If the path takes the outedge
o_{r}
, the path will perform a walk in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
before returning to the root by traversing
o_{r}
in the reverse direction. The weighted contribution of paths that take this option would be
\frac{m_{\infty}(z)}{z}
, corresponding to the weighted contribution of walks that return to the root in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Before the walk takes an edge that is part of
S_{d-1}
, it could repeat this process indefinitely. Thus, the total weighted contribution of paths that could occur before the walk’s first step in
S_{d-1}
is,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{m_{\infty}}{z}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{m_{\infty}}{z}}.

If the path takes an edge that is part of
S_{d-1}
to get to the vertex
p
, we can repeat a similar argument. Either the next step after this is also a part of
S_{d-1}
or it could take the inedge
i_{p}
. (Note that this is still true if we took the weighted edge corresponding to the self loop.) We can count the contribution of all paths that use the inedge
i_{p}
as
\frac{1}{1-\frac{m_{\infty}}{z}}
. If we repeat this argument as we traverse edges of
S_{d-1}
we see that
m_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}
can be expressed as follows. Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}
denote the total weight of all paths in
S_{d-1}
that use
k
edges.

(A.25)

m_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{k}}{z^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{m_{\infty}(z)}{z}}\right)^{k+1}.

Thus,

(A.26)

m^{d}_{\mathcal{T}}=m^{d-1}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(z-m_{\infty}(z)\right).

If we return to the equations in (
A.24
), we find that,

(A.27)

\frac{1}{m^{d}_{\mathcal{T}}}=\frac{|w|^{2}}{z+dm_{\infty}}-(z+dm_{\infty})=\frac{|w|^{2}}{z+dm_{\infty}(z)}-(z+(d-1)m_{\infty})-m_{\infty}(z)=-m_{\infty}(z)+\frac{1}{m_{\infty}}.

Solving the quadratic gives,

(A.28)

m_{\infty}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4m_{d}^{2}}}{2m_{d}}.

Returning to equation (
A.26
), we see that,

(A.29)

m^{d}_{\mathcal{T}}(z)=m^{d-1}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(z-\frac{2m^{d}_{\mathcal{T}}}{1+\sqrt{1+4(m^{d}_{\mathcal{T}})^{2}}}\right).

This is the same recursion as in equation (4.6) of
[
3
]
. Notice that though we only confirmed the identity for
|z|\geq(d-1)+|w|^{2}
, the identity would hold for all values of
z
by analytic continutation. To fully confirm the equality of Green’s functions, it suffices to show the equality of Green’s functions when
d=1
.

When
d=1
, we have to solve the quadratic,

(A.30)

m_{\infty}^{2}+m_{\infty}\left[z+\frac{1}{z}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{z}\right]+1=0.

Setting the quantity
A=z+\frac{1}{z}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{z}
, we see that we have,

(A.31)

m_{\infty}=\frac{-A-\sqrt{A^{2}-4}}{2},

and, using (
A.27
), we see that,

(A.32)

\displaystyle m^{1}_{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{A^{2}-4}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(z+\frac{1}{z}-\frac{|w|^{2}}{z}\right)^{2}-4}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{\sqrt{\left(z^{2}+1-|w|^{2}\right)^{2}-4z^{2}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{\sqrt{\left(z^{2}+2z+1-|w|^{2})(z^{2}-2z+1-|w|^{2}\right)}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{\sqrt{\left((z+1)^{2}-|w|^{2})((z-1)^{2}-|w|^{2}\right)}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{\sqrt{(z+1-|w|)(z+1+|w|)(z-1-|w|)(z-1+|w|)}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{\sqrt{(z^{2}-(1-|w|)^{2})(z^{2}-(1+|w|)^{2})}}.

This Green’s function can be checked to correspond to the density of equation (4.1) in
[
3
]
. ∎

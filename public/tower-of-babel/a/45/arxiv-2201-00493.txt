Beauville-Voisin filtrations on zero cycles of moduli space of stable sheaves on K3 surfaces
Zhiyuan Li, Ruxuan Zhang

Abstract
. The Beauville-Voisin conjecture predicts the existence of a filtration on projective hyper-Kähler manifolds opposite to the conjecture Bloch-Beilinson filtration, called the Beauivlle-Voisin filtration. In [ Voi16 ] , Voisin has introduced a filtration on zero cycles of an arbitrary projective hyper-Kähler manifold. On moduli space of stable objects of a projective K3 surface, there are other candidates constructed by Shen-Yin-Zhao, Barros-Flapan-Marian-Silversmith in [ SYZ20 , BFMS19 ] and more recently by Vial in [ Via20 ] from different point of views. According to the work in [ Via20 ] , all of them are proved to be equivalent except Voisin’s filtration. In this paper, we show that Voisin’s filtration is the same as the other filtrations. As an application, we prove a conjecture in [ BFMS19 ] .

1.
Introduction

On a projective K3 surface
X
, Beauville and Voisin
[
BV04
]
showed that
X
carries a canonical zero-cycle class of degree 1,

[o_{X}]\in\CH_{0}(X),

where
o_{X}
can be taken any point lying on a rational curve in
X
. The intersections of divisor classes in
X
, as well as the second Chern class of
X
, lie in
\mathbb{Z}\cdot[o_{X}]
. In
[
O’G13
]
, O’Grady introduced a filtration
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}(X)
on
\CH_{0}(X)
,

\mathrm{S}_{i}(X)=\bigcup_{\deg([z])=i}\{[z]+\mathbb{Z}\cdot[o_{X}]\}

for all effective zero-cycles
z
of degree
i
, which gives another characterization of the Beauville-Voisin class
[o_{X}]
.
More generally, Beauville-Voisin’s conjecture predicts that a similar picture also holds for any projective hyper-Kähler manifold
M
and there exists a filtration (called Beauville-Voisin filtration) on the Chow ring viewed as an opposite to the conjectural Bloch-Beilinson filtration. For zero cycles, Voisin has constructed a filtration
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)\subseteq\CH_{0}(M)
in
[
Voi16
]
defined by

\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M):=\left<x\in M|~\dim O_{x}\geq d-i\right>

where
O_{x}
is the orbit of
x
under the rational equivalence.

In the case
M=M_{\sigma}(v)
is the moduli space of
\sigma
-stable objects in the derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
of a projective K3 surface
X
for some Bridgeland stability condition
\sigma
. Shen-Yin-Zhao discovered a filtration on the derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
, which gives another natural filtration
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)
,

\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M):=\left<E|~c_{2}(E)\in\mathrm{S}_{i}(X)\right>.

They showed that there is an inclusion

\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M),

between the two filtrations. A natural question is whether the two filtration coincides (cf.
[
SYZ20
, Remark 2.6]
).
This has been confirmed on Hilbert scheme of points on
X
via using the density of constant cycle subvarieties. In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to this question and the main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M=M_{\sigma}(v)
be the moduli space of
\sigma
-stable objects in
\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)
with a primitive Mukai vector
v
. Suppose
\sigma
is
v
-generic. Then we have

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M).

As a corollary, we obtain the density of constant cycle subvarieties on
M
(See Corollary
3.2
). Finally, let us compare Voisin’s filtration with other filtrations. In
[
BFMS19
]
, Flapan, Marian and Silversmith have constructed two filtrations
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm small}\CH_{0}(M)
and
\mathrm{S}^{\rm BFMS}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)
from the product point of view, while Vial has found a so called
co-radical
filtration
\mathrm{R}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)
from the co-algebra structure on birational motives. Combined with the results in
[
BFMS19
,
Via20
]
, our main theorem indicates that

Theorem 1.2
.

With the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
, we have

(1)

\mathrm{S}^{\mathrm{small}}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}^{\rm BFMS}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{R}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M).

As a consequence,
[
BFMS19
, Conjecture 4]
holds.

2.
Degenerate loci of vector bundles

In this section, we recap Voisin’s work in
[
Voi15
]
which establish a relation between the zero cycles on the moduli space of stable sheaves and the Hilbert scheme of points.
Let
(X,H)
be a polarized K3 surface and let
M=M_{H}(v)
be the moduli space of Gieseker
H
-stable sheaves on
X
with a primitive Mukai vector
v=(r,D,s)
. We may assume
H
is
v
-generic so that
M
is a smooth projective hyper-Kähler variety of dimension

v^{2}+2=D^{2}-2rs+2.

Given a locally free sheaf
E\in M
, let
d=\frac{1}{2}\Ext^{1}(E,E)
and
g=\dim\Gr(r-1,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,E))
. As in
[
Voi15
]
, if
E
is globally generated and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,E)=0
, we have an exact sequence for a general
(r-1)
-dimensional sections of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,E)

(2)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1}\xrightarrow{s}E\rightarrow I_{Z}\otimes D\rightarrow 0

where

is the degeneracy locus of
s
consisting of
d+g

Then the sequence defines a rational map

\phi_{E}:\mathrm{G}_{E}\dashrightarrow X^{[d+g]},

where
\mathrm{G}_{E}=\Gr(r-1,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,E))
, sending
s
to
I_{Z}\otimes D\subset X^{[d+g]}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume the condition
(\star)

E~\hbox{is globally generated and}~\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,E)=0~\hbox{for}~i=1,2.

holds.
The morphism
\phi_{E}
is generically injective.
Moreover, for any two stable sheaves
E,F\in M
satisfying
(\star)
as extensions of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1}
and
I_{Z}\otimes D
for some

, we have
E\cong F
.

Proof.

The generic injectivity of
\phi_{E}
is already proved in
[
Voi15
, Proposition 3.2]
. It follows from the computation of
\Hom(E,I_{Z}\otimes D)
. Indeed, apply the functor
\Hom(E,-)
to the exact sequence (
2
), one can obtain a long exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\Hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1})\rightarrow\Hom(E,E)\rightarrow\Hom(E,I_{Z}\otimes D)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1})\to\ldots

By the Serre duality, we have

\Hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1})=\Ext^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1})=0.

Since
E
is simple,
\Hom(E,E)\cong\mathbb{C}
. It follows that

\Hom(E,I_{Z}\otimes D)\cong\mathbb{C}.

It implies that
\phi_{E}
is injective on
s
whenever we have the sequence (
2
) for
s
. If we have another extension

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r-1}\rightarrow F\rightarrow I_{Z}\otimes D\rightarrow 0,

after applying
\Hom(E,-)
, one can find that

\Hom(E,F)\cong\Hom(E,I_{Z}\otimes D)\cong\mathbb{C}.

In particular, if
F
is a stable sheaf, then
E\cong F
.
∎

3.
Proof of Main theorem

Let
v=(r,D,s)
be a primitive Mukai vector with
r\geq 0
and assume that
H
is
v
-generic. We first consider the locally free sheaves in
M_{H}(v)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a locally free sheaf in
M=M_{H}(v)
. Then
c_{2}(E)\in\mathrm{S}_{i}(X)
if there exists a constant cycle subvariety
Y\subseteq M
such that
[E]\in Y
and
\dim Y\geq d-i
. In particular, if
M
consists of stable locally free sheaves, then
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)
.

Proof.

After tensoring line bundles
mH
for sufficiently large
m
without changing
M
, we may assume that the sheaves in
M
satisfy the condition
(\star)
by
[
Lan06
, Theorem 0.1, Section 10.1]
. There exists an
1\boxtimes\alpha
-twisted universal sheaf on
X\times M
. We take a Severi-Brauer variety

\nu:P\rightarrow M

for the Brauer class
\alpha
. Then there is a universal sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X\times P
in the sense that for
x\in P
, the stalk
\mathcal{E}_{x}
of
\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to
E_{\nu(x)}
, where
E_{\nu(x)}
is the sheaf corresponding to
\nu(x)\in M
.

With the same notations in the previous section, for any subvariety
Y\subseteq M
containing
E
, take
B=\nu^{-1}(Y)
, we can apply relative version of Voisin’s construction to obtain a rational map

\phi_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}:\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}\dashrightarrow X^{[d+g]}

By Proposition
2.1
, we have

(1)

the restriction of
\phi_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}
to
the fiber of
p:\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}\rightarrow\nu^{-1}(Y)
is generic injective and hence
\phi_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}
sends each fiber to a
g
-dimensional constant cycle subvariety in
X^{[d+g]}
.

(2)

for different
E^{\prime}\in Y
and the images
\phi(p^{-1}(E^{\prime}))
in
X^{[d+g]}
are disjoint if
E^{\prime}
is locally free.

Moreover, for any two points
s_{1},s_{2}\in\nu^{-1}(E^{\prime})
, their corresponding sheaves are the same and so are their images in
X^{[d+g]}
. Then we have

\mathrm{dim}~\phi(p^{-1}(Y))=\dim Y+g.

Now, suppose
Y
is a constant cycle subvariety on
M
of dimension
\geq d-i
. According to the main Theorem in
[
MZ20
]
, two sheaves
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}\in M
are rationally equivalent in
\CH_{0}(M)
if and only if
c_{2}(E^{\prime})
and
c_{2}(E^{\prime\prime})
are rationally equivalent in
\CH_{0}(X)
. For any point in
\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{-1}(Y)}
, its image
I_{Z}\otimes D
represent the same class in
\CH_{0}(X^{[d+g]})
as

c_{2}(I_{Z}\otimes D)=c_{2}(E)

in
\CH_{0}(X)
because the points in
Y
are constant in
\CH_{0}(M)
. This implies that the Zariski closure of the image
\phi(p^{-1}(Y))
is a constant cycle subvariety on
X^{[d+g]}
. By
[
Voi15
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have

c_{2}(I_{Z}\otimes D)\in\mathrm{S}_{i}(X)

and hence
E\in\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M)
. For the last statement, we have

\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)

from the definition of
\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M)
. The assertion then follows from the inclusion
\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first deal with the case
M=M_{H}(v)
with

. By tensoring the sheaf in

with

Let

be the slope function with respect to

. Under these conditions, Yoshioka has shown in

induced by a Fourier-Mukai transform such that for any stable sheaf

,

is a

-stable sheaf of rank

[

). The conditions also ensures that the

-stable sheaves in

[

and
[

).
In fact, there is an exact sequence

with

being

-stable and

being a
0
-dimensional sheaf whose support is of length

. Then
v(E^{\vee\vee})^{2}=v(E)^{2}-2rl\geq-2
. Therefore

if

.
By Theorem
3.1
, we have

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}(M_{\widehat{H}}(\widehat{v}))=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}(M_{\widehat{H}}(\widehat{v}))

Finally, according to
[

, Proposition 0.4]
,

induces an isomorphism between the two filtartions

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M_{H}(v))\cong\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M_{\widehat{H}}(\widehat{v})).

It follows that
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)
.

is a Bridgeland moduli space. According to

, every Bridgeland moduli space is isomorphic to a moduli space of Gieseker stable sheaves via a derived equivalence. Then again by

Corollary 3.3
.

The filtration
S_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)
is invariant under birational transform between hyper-Kähler varieties, i.e. if
f:M=M_{\sigma}(v)\dashrightarrow M^{\prime}
is a birational map, then the induced isomorphism

\CH_{0}(M^{\prime})\cong\CH_{0}(M)

preserves the filration

.

Proof.

The hyper-Kähler birational models of

is controlled by its birational ample cone.
According to
[
BM14
, Theorem 1.2]
, there is a continuous map

from a connected component of space of stability conditions on

in some chamber

, such that

and

for the chamber

containing
\sigma
. Moreover, up to an automorphism, the birational map

is actually induced by a derived (anti) autoequivalence.

Thus we can deduce the result by the
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}=\mathrm{S}^{\rm SYZ}_{\bullet}
and that

is independent of modular interpretations.

4.
Applications: Comparison with other filtrations

With the notations as before, on the moduli space
M=M_{H}(v)
of stable sheaves, there is a distinguished point
c_{M}=[E_{0}]\in M
such that
c_{2}(E_{0})=ko_{X}
with
k=\frac{D^{2}}{2}+r-s
. The existence of
c_{M}
is ensured by the surjectivity of the projection map from the incident variety
R
to
X^{[d]}
. Let us recall the filtrations defined in
[
BFMS19
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Delta
be the diagonal in the product
M\times M
and we set

(8)

\overline{\Delta}=\Delta-M\times c_{M}\in\CH_{2d}(M\times M)

Then one can define

(9)

\mathrm{S}^{\rm BFMS}_{i}(\CH_{0}(M)):=\{\alpha~|~\alpha\cdot\overline{\Delta}_{0,1}\ldots\cdot\overline{\Delta}_{0,i+1}=0\in\CH_{\ast}(M\times M^{i+1})\},

where
\overline{\Delta}_{0,j}
is the pull back of
\overline{\Delta}
from the
0
-th and
j
-th factors for
j=0,1,\cdots i+1
. It is useful to single out the subgroup

\mathrm{S}^{\mathrm{small}}_{i}(\CH_{0}(M))\subset\mathrm{S}^{\rm BFMS}_{i}(\CH_{0}(M))

generated by point classes
[F]\in\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm BFMS}(\CH_{0}(M))
with
F\in M
.

In
[
BFMS19
]
and
[
Via20
]
, it has been proved that

(10)

\mathrm{S}^{\mathrm{small}}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}^{\rm BFMS}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)=\mathrm{R}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M),

(cf.
[

, Proposition A.6, Theorem 8.4]
). Furthermore, for 0-cycles, the termination of this filtration implies that for all

Furthermore, they conjectured that a generalization of this equation for higher dimensional constant cycle varieties is still valid. As a consequence of Theorem

, we confirm their conjecture.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
BFMS19
, Conjecture 4]

Let

be a constant cycle subvariety. Then

, Conjecture holds if and only if

The assertion follows directly from (

)
(cf.
[

, Lemma 3]
and
[

, Theorem 8.4]
).
∎

Combining the above results, one can conclude Theorem

.

Remark 4.3
.

It was asked in
[
SYZ20
, Question 3.2]
whether one has the following equivalence

[E]\in\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm SYZ}\CH_{0}(M)~{\rm if~and~only~if}~c_{2}(E)\in\mathrm{S}_{i}(X).

An affirmative answer of this question can be implied by the following statement:

(11)

[V_{E}]\cdot\overline{\Delta}_{0,1}\cdot\overline{\Delta}_{0,2}\ldots\overline{\Delta}_{0,d}\neq 0

for a maximal constant cycle variety

containing

. This is due to a direct computation in
[
BFMS19
, Remark 6]
: if

, one must have dim

assuming (

). Then up to taking a derived equivalence preserving O’Grady’s filtration, one can assume that

is locally free and obtain

by applying Theorem
3.1
.

In
[
BFMS19
, Remark 6]
, it also claims that (
11
) holds if

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{small}}\CH_{0}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\CH_{0}(M).

which is always true by Theorem

. However, it seems to us that there exists a gap in the proof: it’s unclear why there exists a constant cycle variety
V_{E}
containing
E
with dim

for
E\in\mathrm{S}_{i}^{\rm small}\CH_{0}(M)\cap\mathrm{S}_{i}\CH_{0}(M)\setminus\mathrm{S}_{i-1}^{\rm small}\CH_{0}(M)
. Hence the question seems still open.

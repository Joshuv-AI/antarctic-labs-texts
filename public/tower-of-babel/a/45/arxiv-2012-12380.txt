Quantitative PAT with simplified P N P_{N} approximation
Hongkai Zhao, Yimin Zhong

Abstract
The photoacoustic tomography (PAT) is a hybrid modality that combines the optics and acoustics to obtain high resolution and high contrast imaging of heterogeneous media. In this work, our objective is to study the inverse problem in the quantitative step of PAT which aims to reconstruct the optical coefficients of the governing radiative transport equation from the ultrasound measurements. In our analysis, we take the simplified P N P_{N} approximation of the radiative transport equation as the physical model and then show the uniqueness and stability for this modified inverse problem. Numerical simulations based on synthetic data are presented to validate our analysis.



Key words.

photoacoustic tomography (PAT), radiative transport equation, simplified
P_{N}
method, diffusion approximation, numerical reconstruction

Introduction

The photoacoustic tomography (PAT)
[
3
,
6
,
9
,
21
,
37
,
34
,
29
,
31
]
is an emerging hybrid imaging modality that reconstructs high resolution images of optical properties of heterogeneous media. The PAT experiment uses a pulse of near-infra-red (NIR) laser into the medium of interest (e.g. fat, bone, tumor tissues). These photons propagate inside the medium by following the radiative transport process. During the propagation, a portion of the photons is absorbed by the medium and then converted into heat which causes a local thermoelastic expansion. Such expansion induces a transient pressure change and leads to the propagation of ultrasound. The ultrasound signals are measured around the boundary of the medium and we need to infer the optical properties from the acoustic measurements.

The photon transport process is usually modeled by radiative transport equation. Let
X=\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where
\Omega
is the physical domain and
\mathbb{S}^{2}
denotes the unit sphere in 3D, the photon density function
u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
satisfies the following

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})+\sigma_{t}(\mathbf{x})u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{x})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}p(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}^{\prime})u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{\prime})d\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }

\displaystyle X\,,

(1)

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}\,,

where
\Gamma_{-}=\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\mid-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbf{v}>0\}
is the incoming boundary set.
\sigma_{s},\sigma_{t}
are the scattering and total absorption coefficients respectively,
\sigma_{a}:=\sigma_{t}-\sigma_{s}
is the intrinsic absorption coefficient.
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
is the external illumination source. The scattering phase function
p(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
is usually chosen as the Henyey-Greenstein function

p(\cos\theta)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1-g^{2}}{(1+g^{2}-2g\cos\theta)^{3/2}}\,,

(2)

where
g\in(-1,1)
is the anisotropy parameter.

The energy absorbed by the medium is
\sigma_{a}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})d\mathbf{v}
, then the initial pressure field generated by the
photoacoustic effect
is:

H(\mathbf{x}):=\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{x})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})d\mathbf{v},

(3)

where
\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})
is the dimensionless Grüneisen coefficient which measures the efficiency of the photoacoustic effect.

Then the initial pressure field
H(\mathbf{x})
propagates the ultrasound wave, which satisfies the following equation
[
35
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c^{2}(\mathbf{x})}\frac{\partial^{2}p(\mathbf{x},t)}{\partial t^{2}}-\Delta_{\mathbf{x}}p(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty),

(4)

\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x},0)=H(\mathbf{x}),\quad\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(\mathbf{x},0)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Here
c(\mathbf{x})
is the wave speed of the underlying medium. The measured acoustic signals are
p(\mathbf{x},t)
on
\partial\Omega\times[0,T]
for sufficient large observation time
T
.

The usual reconstruction of PAT is a two-step process. The first step is to reconstruct the initial pressure field
H(\mathbf{x})
from the ultrasound measurements. This problem has been studied extensively by
[
1
,
2
,
35
,
17
,
18
]
and the references therein. Here we assume this step has been finished and recovered the initial pressure field
H(\mathbf{x})
and we focus on the second step to reconstruct the optical properties
(\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s},\Upsilon)
from the quantity
H(\mathbf{x})
. Under the diffusion approximation, this quantitative PAT (qPAT) problem has been well studied
[
10
,
28
,
4
,
5
]
. However, with the radiative transport equation (
1
), the multi-source inverse problem theory has not been well established except for
albedo
type data
[
23
,
4
]
, which requires infinitely many angularly resolved illumination sources
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
. The reconstruction of only absorption coefficient
\sigma_{a}
has been recently considered in
[
33
]
for nonlinear setting. It is still unclear about the uniqueness and stability of the reconstructions of
(\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s},\Upsilon)
with finite many source functions or angularly independent sources.

In this paper, we aim to study the qPAT problem with the simplified
P_{N}
(
N
being an odd integer) approximation to the equation (
1
) with angularly independent source functions, that is,
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})=f(\mathbf{x})
. The simplified
P_{N}
approximation is also referred as
SP_{N}
method, which is utilized to solve the radiative transport equation by forming a system of elliptic equations
[
25
,
20
]
. The
SP_{N}
approximation with relatively small
N\leq 7
has been applied to many optical imaging methods
[
38
,
8
,
19
]
and outperforms the traditional diffusion approximation (
P_{1}
method). Theoretically, the simplified
P_{N}
approximation is derived from the
P_{N}
formulation
[
15
]
and the
P_{N}
approximation converges to the exact solution of RTE as
N\to\infty
. Under appropriate conditions
SP_{N}
and
P_{N}
are equivalent, see
[
25
]
, however in general, they are different and not necessarily converging to the same limit. For the qPAT problem, the case with
N=3
has been considered in
[
12
]
, in our work, we extend the theory to arbitrary order
N
under a unified framework.

Under the
SP_{N}
approximation, the RTE’s solution is expanded with Legendre polynomials, which derives a weakly coupled diffusion equation system
[
20
]
. Formally, the 3D
SP_{N}
approximation takes the 1D
P_{N}
equations and replace the diffusion operators with 3D’s counterpart, which is

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n+1}{2n+1}\right)\nabla\frac{1}{\sigma_{n+1}}\nabla\left[\left(\frac{n+2}{2n+3}\right)\phi_{n+2}+\left(\frac{n+1}{2n+3}\right)\phi_{n}\right]

(5)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{n}{2n+1}\right)\nabla\frac{1}{\sigma_{n-1}}\nabla\left[\left(\frac{n}{2n-1}\right)\phi_{n}+\left(\frac{n-1}{2n-1}\right)\phi_{n-2}\right]+\sigma_{n}\phi_{n}=0

for
n=0,2,4,\dots,N-1
, where coefficients
\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{a}+\sigma_{s}(1-g^{n})
,
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{a}
. The system is closed by seting
\phi_{n}
as zeros for
n\geq N+1
and
n\leq-2
and only consists of even-indexed
\phi_{n}
.
Physically speaking,
\phi_{n}
represents the
n
-th Legendre moments of the solution
u(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
and
\phi_{0}
will be the angular average of the solution.
The corresponding mixed boundary conditions are derived from the 1D
P_{N}
equation’s boundary conditions by replacing
\frac{\rm{d}}{\rm{d}x}
with
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla
on the boundary
[
20
]
.

In the following context, we let

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}=(2n-1)\phi_{2n-2}+(2n)\phi_{2n},\quad n=1,2,\dots,(N+1)/2\,,

(6)

and the column vector
\Phi=[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots,\varphi_{(N+1)/2}]^{\text{T}}
, which satisfies

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\\
\varphi_{3}\\
\vdots\\
\varphi_{(N+1)/2}\end{pmatrix}=M\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{4}\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}\text{ where }M:=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&0&\dots&0\\
0&3&4&\dots&0\\
0&0&5&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&N\end{pmatrix}\,,

(7)

since the matrix
M
is upper triangular, its inverse is also upper triangular, let the row vector
\mathbf{s}_{k}=[s_{k,1},\dots,s_{k,(N+1)/2}]
represent the
k
-th row of the inverse matrix
M^{-1}
, according to Lemma
A.1
, its entries are

s_{k,l}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2k-1}(-1)^{l-k}\frac{((2l-2))!!}{(2l-1)!!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(2k-2)!!},\quad&l\geq k\,,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(8)

Then we derive the
SP_{N}
diffusion system

-\nabla\cdot\frac{A_{n}}{(4n-1)\sigma_{2n-1}}\nabla\varphi_{n}-\nabla\cdot\frac{B_{n}}{(4n-5)\sigma_{2n-3}}\nabla\varphi_{n-1}+\sigma_{2n-2}\mathbf{s}_{n}\cdot\Phi=0

(9)

for
n=1,2,\dots,(N+1)/2
, where the constants
A_{n},B_{n}
are defined by

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n-1}{(4n-3)},\quad B_{n}=\frac{2n-2}{(4n-3)}.

We remark that the following matrix differs from
M^{\text{T}}
by only a factor of diagonal matrix,

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&0&\dots&0\\
B_{2}&A_{2}&0&\dots&0\\
0&B_{3}&A_{3}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&B_{(N+1)/2}&A_{(N+1)/2}\end{pmatrix}=LM^{\text{T}}\;\text{ where }\;L:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&\frac{1}{5}&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&\frac{1}{9}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\frac{1}{2N-1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(10)

The corresponding mixed boundary conditions are

\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\mu_{2m-1,2n-2}\phi_{2n-2}+\frac{1}{(4m-1)\sigma_{2m-1}}\frac{\partial\varphi_{m}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=k_{2m-1}f,\quad m=1,2,\dots,(N+1)/2\,.

(11)

The constants
\mu_{2m-1,2n-2}
and
k_{2m-1}
are

\displaystyle\mu_{2m-1,2n-2}

\displaystyle=(4n-3)\int_{0}^{1}P_{2m-1}(x)P_{2n-2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+n-1}\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n-\frac{1}{2})}{\pi\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)(m+n-1)}\frac{(4n-3)}{2n-2m-1}\,,

\displaystyle k_{2m-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}P_{2m-1}(x)dx=(-1)^{m-1}\frac{(2m-1)!!}{(2m-1)(2m)(2m-2)!!}\,,

where
P_{l}
is the degree
l
Legendre polynomial with normalization condition
P_{l}(1)=1
and the symbol
!!
dnotes the double factorial. Since we have assumed
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})=f(\mathbf{x})
, these boundary condition coefficients are simply obtained through integration with Legendre polynomials on the half sphere (incoming directions) and independent of
f
.
For convenience, we also define the following matrices
\Sigma_{e}
and
R
for later uses,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{e}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{0}&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&\sigma_{2}&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&\sigma_{4}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&\sigma_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}

and

R=(R_{ij})_{i,j=1,2,\dots,(N+1)/2},\quad\text{with }R_{ij}=\mu_{2i-1,2j-2}.

In the quantitative photoacoustic tomography, we suppose the datum
H(\mathbf{x})=\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{x})\phi_{0}(\mathbf{x})
has been reconstructed from the measured acoustic signals. In the following sections, we will analyze the uniqueness and stability of reconstruction of the coefficients
\Upsilon,\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s}
from the internal data
H
. We also make the following general assumptions for the rest of paper.

\mathcal{A}
-i

The physical domain
\Omega
is simply connected with
C^{2,1}
boundary.

\mathcal{A}
-ii

The coefficients
(\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s},\Upsilon)
are non-negative and bounded. There exists constants
\underline{c}
and
\overline{c}
that

0\leq\underline{c}\leq\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s},\Upsilon\leq\overline{c}<\infty\,.

(12)

\mathcal{A}
-iii

The coefficient
\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega})
. There exists a constant
M_{0}
that

\|\sigma_{a}\|_{C^{1,1}(\Omega)},\|\sigma_{s}\|_{C^{1,1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{0}<\infty\,.

Moreover,
\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s}
are both known on
\partial\Omega
.

\mathcal{A}
-iv

The boundary source function
f\in H^{5/2}(\partial\Omega).

2
General properties

For the forward problem, we establish the wellposedness for the
SP_{N}
approximation. In order to show there exists a unique weak solution
\Phi\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
for (
9
) and (
11
), we only have to consider the corresponding variational form for the diffusion system. By rewriting (
9
) and (
11
) into the matrix form,

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(LM^{\text{T}}D\nabla\Phi)+\Sigma_{e}M^{-1}\Phi

\displaystyle={\mathbf{0}},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }

\displaystyle\Omega,

(13)

\displaystyle RM^{-1}\Phi+D\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}f,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }

\displaystyle\partial\Omega,

where
D
is a diagonal matrix with elements
D_{nn}=\frac{1}{(4n-1)\sigma_{2n-1}}
,
n=1,2,\dots,(N+1)/2
and
\mathbf{k}f
is a vector with
n
-th element as
k_{2n-1}f
.
Let
\Psi=[\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{(N+1)/2}]^{T}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
be a test function vector. Multiply the matrix form (
13
) with the vector
L^{-1}M^{-1}\Psi
and integrate over
\Omega
, then the weak form of the
SP_{N}
system is

\displaystyle B(\Phi,\Psi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(4n-1)\sigma_{2n-1}}\nabla\varphi_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi_{n}d\mathbf{x}

(14)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\langle M^{-\text{T}}L^{-1}\Sigma_{e}M^{-1}\Phi,\Psi\rangle d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\langle RM^{-1}\Phi,\Psi\rangle d\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(f,\Psi)\,,

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bilinear form,
\mathcal{L}
is a linear functional only involving boundary integrals that

\mathcal{L}(f,\Psi):=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}k_{2n-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}f\psi_{n}d\mathbf{s}\,.

(15)

We prove the following property of the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The bilinear form (
14
) is bounded and strictly coercive for any
SP_{N}
approximation.

Proof.

The boundedness is obvious since
L
,
M
are both invertible matrices. We only need to prove the coerciveness. In the following, we will show that the matrices
M\Sigma_{e}^{-1}LM^{\text{T}}
and
M^{T}R
are positive definite. For
M\Sigma_{e}^{-1}LM^{\text{T}}
, it is obvious since the diagonal matrix
\Sigma_{e}^{-1}L
has all positive entries. For the matrix
M^{\text{T}}R
, we compute its
(i,k)
-th entry by

\displaystyle(2i-1)R_{i,k}+(2i-2)R_{i-1,k}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2i-1)(4k-3)\int_{0}^{1}P_{2i-1}(x)P_{2k-2}(x)dx+(2i-2)(4k-3)\int_{0}^{1}P_{2i-3}(x)P_{2k-2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4i-3)(4k-3)\int_{0}^{1}xP_{2i-2}(x)P_{2k-2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}q_{i}(x)q_{k}(x)dx\quad\text{with }q_{i}(x)=(4i-3)\sqrt{x}P_{2i-2}(x)\,,

where
P_{k}
is the
k
-th Legendre polynomial and we have used the recurrence relation

(2i-1)P_{2i-1}(x)+(2i-2)P_{2i-3}(x)=(4i-3)xP_{2i-2}(x)\,.

(17)

Hence
M^{\text{T}}R
is semi-positive definite. On the other hand, if there is a vector
\mathbf{z}=[z_{1},\dots,z_{(N+1)/2}]^{\text{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)/2}
that

0=\mathbf{z}^{\text{T}}(M^{\text{T}}R)\mathbf{z}=\int_{0}^{1}x\left(\sum_{k=1}^{(N+1)/2}z_{k}(4k-3)P_{2k-2}(x)\right)^{2}dx\,,

(18)

then for any
x\in[0,1]
, the following polynomial must vanish,

\sum_{k=1}^{(N+1)/2}z_{k}(4k-3)P_{2k-2}(x)=0\,.

(19)

Hence the polynomial equals zero for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and use the fact
\{P_{k}(x)\}_{k=1}^{(N+1)/2}
forms an orthogonal basis on
[-1,1]
, then
\forall k,z_{k}=0
. Therefore
M^{\text{T}}R
is strictly positive definite, so is
RM^{-1}=M^{-\text{T}}(M^{\text{T}}R)M^{-1}
.
∎

The wellposedness immediately derives from the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique weak solution
\Phi\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
for arbitrary odd integer
N
. In fact, using the assumptions
\mathcal{A}
-i to
\mathcal{A}
-iv, the regularity theorem of elliptic systems
[
26
]
implies that the unique solution
\Phi\in[H^{3}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
, by the Sobolev embedding, the solution
\Phi\in[C^{1,1/2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
.

3
Reconstruction under
SP_{N}
approximation

Generally speaking, if
\sigma_{a}
is not negligible, the inverse problem is highly nonlinear and very challenging. Therefore in the following, we only consider the practical scenario that

\sigma_{a}\ll\sigma_{s}(1-g)
, which means we can simplify the coefficients
\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{a}+(1-g^{n})\sigma_{s}\simeq(1-g^{n})\sigma_{s}
for
n\geq 1
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{a}
. This simplification decouples the coefficients
\sigma_{a}
and
\sigma_{s}
. In particular, if
g=0
, there is no need to perform such simplification.

Reconstruction of
\sigma_{a}
only.

Suppose the coefficients
\Upsilon,\sigma_{s}
are known on
\Omega
, we consider the reconstruction of
\sigma_{a}
from a single measurement datum
H
. Using the assumption that
\sigma_{n}\simeq(1-g^{n})\sigma_{s}
for
n\geq 1
, the coefficients
\sigma_{n}
are all known for
n\geq 1
. Since
\sigma_{a}=\sigma_{0}
, then using
H(\mathbf{x})=\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{x})\phi_{0}(\mathbf{x})
and
\phi_{0}=\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi
, we derive that

\sigma_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{H(\mathbf{x})}{\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi}\,.

(20)

By isolating the term relevant to
\sigma_{0}
(
n=0
) in the bilinear form (
14
), we can reformulated it as

\displaystyle B(\Phi,\Psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(4n-1)\sigma_{2n-1}}\nabla\varphi_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi_{n}d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{H(\mathbf{x})}{\Upsilon(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Psi)d\mathbf{x}

(21)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}(4n-3)\sigma_{2n-2}(\mathbf{s}_{n}\cdot\Phi)(\mathbf{s}_{n}\cdot\Psi)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\langle RM^{-1}\Phi,\Psi\rangle d\mathbf{s}\,.

where the row vector
\mathbf{s}_{k}
denotes the
k
-th row of
M^{-1}
. We can establish the following uniqueness and stability result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given any
SP_{N}
approximation, under the assumptions
\mathcal{A}
-i to
\mathcal{A}
-iv and suppose
(\Upsilon,\sigma_{s})
are known,
\sigma_{a,1}
and
\sigma_{a,2}
are two admissible absorption coefficients,
H_{1},H_{2}
are the corresponding internal data, respectively. Then
H_{1}=H_{2}
implies
\sigma_{a,1}=\sigma_{a,2}
and the following stability estimate holds

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{a,1}-\sigma_{a,2})\frac{H_{1}}{\sigma_{a,1}\Upsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\|(H_{1}-H_{2})/\Upsilon\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(22)

where
C=C(N,\Omega)
is a positive constant depending on
N
and
\Omega
only.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
and
\widetilde{\Phi}
be the weak solutions to the
SP_{N}
system for the absorption coefficients
\sigma_{a,1}
and
\sigma_{a,2}
, respectively. Let
\delta\Phi:=\Phi-\widetilde{\Phi}=[\delta\varphi_{1},\dots,\delta\varphi_{(N+1)/2}]^{\text{T}}
, then from the bilinear form (
21
), we obtain the equation

\widetilde{B}(\delta\Phi,\Psi)=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Psi)d\mathbf{x}\,,

(23)

where the above modified bilinear form
\widetilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}(\delta\Phi,\Psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(4n-1)\sigma_{2n-1}}\nabla\delta\varphi_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi_{n}d\mathbf{x}

(24)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}(4n-3)\sigma_{2n-2}(\mathbf{s}_{n}\cdot\delta\Phi)(\mathbf{s}_{n}\cdot\Psi)d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\langle RM^{-1}\delta\Phi,\Psi\rangle d\mathbf{s}\,.

Since
RM^{-1}
is strictly positive definite, the coerciveness of
\widetilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is immediately deduced from the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality
[
39
]
that
\forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\|u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\leq C_{ps}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\,,

(25)

where
C_{ps}=C_{ps}(\Omega)
is a positive constant depending on
\Omega
only. Therefore there exists another constant
C_{1}(N,\Omega)
that

\displaystyle C_{1}(N,\Omega)\|\delta\Phi\|^{2}_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{B}(\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi)=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi)d\mathbf{x}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(H_{1}-H_{2})/\Upsilon\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(H_{1}-H_{2})/\Upsilon\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\|\delta\Phi\|_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}

by the Hölder inequality that

\|\mathbf{v}\cdot\Phi\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\ell^{q}}\|\Phi\|_{[W^{k,p}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}},\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,

where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{[W^{k,p}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}
is defined by

\|\mathbf{f}\|_{[W^{k,p}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\|f_{n}\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}^{p},\quad\mathbf{f}=[f_{1},\dots,f_{(N+1)/2}]^{\text{T}}\,.

The estimate (
26
) implies

\|\delta\Phi\|_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}(N,\Omega)}\left\|(H_{1}-H_{2})/\Upsilon\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\,.

(27)

Therefore the uniqueness is proved. For the stability estimate, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{a,1}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi)-\sigma_{a,2}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\widetilde{\Phi})

(28)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{a,1}-\sigma_{a,2})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi)+\sigma_{a,2}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi)\,.

Thus using (
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{a,1}-\sigma_{a,2})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma_{a,2}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\overline{c}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{C_{1}(N,\Omega)}\right)\left\|\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Our proof is completed by noticing (
20
).
∎

The reconstruction algorithm for
\sigma_{a}
is then naturally divided into two steps. First, we solve
\Phi
from the modified bilinear form (
21
), then recover
\sigma_{a}
by the relation (
20
) whenever
\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi\neq 0
. For the general
SP_{N}
system, we cannot guarantee the positivity of
\phi_{0}=\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi
for any positive source function
f(\mathbf{x})
.

Under appropriate conditions
[
25
]
, the
SP_{N}
approximation will be eventually converging to the radiative transfer model. However, intuitively, when the order of system
N
grows, the reconstruction of the coefficients will be less stable due to the coupling of the Legendre moments in the solution. In the following, we study the relation of reconstruction’s stability and the system order
N
. It can be shown that the reconstruction’s stability estimate’s constant in Theorem
3.1
grows at most proportional to
N^{11/8}(1+\log N)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the assumptions
\mathcal{A}
-i to
\mathcal{A}
-iv, suppose
(\Upsilon,\sigma_{s})
are known,
\sigma_{a,1}
,
\sigma_{a,2}
are two admissible absorption coefficients,
H_{1},H_{2}
are the corresponding internal data, respectively. Then
H_{1}=H_{2}
implies
\sigma_{a,1}=\sigma_{a,2}
and the following stability estimate holds

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{a,1}-\sigma_{a,2})\frac{H_{1}}{\sigma_{a,1}\Upsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq CN^{11/8}(1+\log N)\left\|(H_{1}-H_{2})/\Upsilon\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(30)

where
C=C(\Omega)
is a positive constant independent of
N
.

Proof.

Recall the estimate (
29
), we only have to give an estimate for
C_{1}(N,\Omega)
and
\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}
with respect to
N
. Using the equation (
24
), we can estimate the lower bound of the coerciveness for
\widetilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
by neglecting the second term,

\widetilde{B}(\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi)\geq\frac{1}{(2N+1)\sigma_{s}}\|\nabla\delta\Phi\|_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}^{2}+\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(RM^{-1})\|\delta\Phi\|_{[L^{2}(\partial\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}^{2}\,,

(31)

where
\lambda_{n}(K)
denotes the
n
-th singular value of
K
ordered from largest to smallest. Use the inequality introduced in
[
24
]
, we estimate the smallest singular value of
RM^{-1}
that

\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(RM^{-1})\geq\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(R)\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(M^{-1})=\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(R)/\lambda_{1}(M)\,.

(32)

Since the largest singular value
\lambda_{1}(M)=\|M\|_{op}
, let
\mathbf{v}=[v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{(N+1)/2}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+1)/2}
and take the convention that
v_{(N+3)/2}=0
, then use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\|M\|_{op}^{2}=\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|=1}\|M\mathbf{v}\|^{2}=\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|=1}\sum_{k=1}^{(N+1)/2}((2k-1)v_{k}+(2k)v_{k+1})^{2}\leq 4N^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}=4N^{2}\,.

(33)

Therefore we have
\lambda_{1}(M)\leq 2N
. In the next,
we only need to estimate the smallest singular value of
R
. According to the lower bound estimates introduced in
[
27
,
16
]
, the smallest singular value satisfies

\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(R)\geq\left(\frac{\frac{N-1}{2}}{\|R\|_{F}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{4}}|\det(R)|\,,

(34)

where
\|\cdot\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm, then use the results from Lemma
A.2
and Lemma
A.3
in Appendix, we have the estimate

\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(R)\geq\left(\frac{N-1}{N+1}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{4}}|\det(R)|=\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/8})\,.

(35)

Therefore
C_{1}(N,\Omega)\geq c\min(\frac{1}{(2N+1)\sigma_{s}},\lambda_{(N+1)/2}(RM^{-1}))=\mathcal{O}(N^{-11/8})
. To estimate the upper bound of
\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|^{2}
, we follow the Lemma
A.1
that the row vector
\mathbf{s}_{1}=[s_{1,1},\dots,s_{1,(N+1)/2}]
is given by

|s_{1,k}|=\frac{(2k-2)!!}{(2k-1)!!}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})}\,.

(36)

Use the Gautschi’s inequality
[
14
]
that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+\frac{1}{2}}}<\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{k-\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

(37)

we immediately find out

\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{1}{i-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{O}(1+\log N)\,.

(38)

From the result of Theorem
3.1
, the stability estimate now can be formulated as

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{a,1}-\sigma_{a,2})\mathbf{s}_{1}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mathcal{O}(N^{11/8}(1+\log N))\left\|\frac{H_{1}-H_{2}}{\Upsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,.

(39)

∎

Remark 3.4
.

As
N\to\infty
, the above result shows that the stability estimate’s constant will also grow to infinity, this seems to give a negative answer to the uniqueness for qPAT with the radiative transport equation. However, such estimate is only meant for the worst case, since the boundary source could be chosen arbitrarily. In practice, if the source function

is known from the measurement

. In addition, we also assume that

is known on the boundary

. When

]
for
{\sigma_{s}}^{-1}

For convenience, we also denote
\mathbf{P}
and

n
-th rows as
\mathbf{p}_{n}
and
\mathbf{q}_{n}
, respectively. In the following, we first prove a simple lemma to estimate the variation in solutions with respect to changes in the scattering coefficient.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the assumptions
\mathcal{A}
-i to
\mathcal{A}
-iv, suppose
\sigma_{s,1}
and
\sigma_{s,2}
are two admissible scattering coefficients that
\delta\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s,1}-\sigma_{s,2}
satisfies
\delta\sigma_{s}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Let
\Phi_{i}=[\varphi_{i,1},\dots,\varphi_{i,(N+1)/2}]^{T}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
the solution associated with scattering coefficient
\sigma_{s,i}
,
i=1,2
, then

\|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\leq C\frac{N^{23/8}}{3(1-|g|)}\|\Phi_{1}\|_{[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\|\sigma_{s,1}-\sigma_{s,2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,

(45)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
N
.

Proof.

Let
B_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)
the bilinear form in (
14
) for coefficient pair
(\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s,i})
,
i=1,2
, then for any test function
\Psi\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
, we have

B_{1}(\Phi_{1},\Psi)=B_{2}(\Phi_{2},\Psi)\,.

(46)

Denote
\delta\Phi=[\delta\varphi_{1},\dots,\delta\varphi_{(N+1)/2}]^{T}:=\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}
, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\displaystyle B_{2}(\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(4n-1)(1-g^{2n-1})}\left[\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,1}\sigma_{s,2}}\nabla\varphi_{1,n}\cdot\nabla\delta\varphi_{n}-\delta\sigma_{s}\mathbf{q}_{n}\cdot\Phi_{1}\delta\varphi_{n}\right]d\mathbf{x}

(47)

\displaystyle\leq\theta\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\left[\|\nabla\delta\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}\nabla\varphi_{1,n}}{\sigma_{s,1}\sigma_{s,2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{3\left\|\delta\varphi_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{4n-1}\left\|\delta\sigma_{s}\mathbf{q}_{n}\cdot\Phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\theta\kappa\|\delta\sigma_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\|\delta\varphi_{n}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\theta{\kappa\sqrt{\frac{(N+1)}{2}}}\|\delta\sigma_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\delta\Phi\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\,,

where the constants
\theta=(3(1-|g|))^{-1}
,
\kappa=\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{1,n}/(\sigma_{s,1}\sigma_{s,2})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{3}{4n-1}\left\|\mathbf{q}_{n}\cdot\Phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Since
\sigma_{s,1},\sigma_{s,2}
are bounded from below by positive constants, the first term in
\kappa
is bounded by
\|\Phi_{1}\|_{[W^{1,\infty}]^{(N+1)/2}}
. The second term needs to estimate
\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{3}{4n-1}\|\mathbf{q}_{n}\|_{\ell^{1}}
. From the definition of
\mathbf{q}_{n}
in (
44
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n-1}\|\mathbf{q}_{n}\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{(N+1)/2}(4k-3)\|\mathbf{u}_{k,n}\|_{\ell^{1}}

(48)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{n}(4k-3)\sum_{j=k}^{(N+1)/2}|s_{k,n}s_{k,j}|

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=2}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{(k+\frac{1}{2})(4k-3)}{(2k-1)^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\text{(Gaustchi's inequality) }

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{2}{\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}}\quad(\text{since }\frac{1}{2}(k+1)(4k-3)\leq(2k-1)^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(N).

This implies
\kappa\leq\mathsf{c}N\|\Phi_{1}\|_{[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}
for certain constant
\mathsf{c}>0
. On the other hand, from the Corollary
3.2
, there exists a constant
C_{1}
independent of
N
that

B_{2}(\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi)\geq C_{1}N^{-11/8}\|\delta\Phi\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}^{2}\,.

(49)

Combine the estimates (
49
) and (
47
), we obtain

\|\delta\Phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\mathsf{c}}{C_{1}}\frac{N^{23/8}}{3(1-|g|)}\|\Phi_{1}\|_{[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\|\delta\sigma_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\,.

(50)

∎

61
) can be further reduced to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}\right)^{2}\left[\left(\sigma_{s,1}+2\sigma_{s,2}\right)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})^{2}+\kappa_{N}\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})-\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,1}}\nabla\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})\right]d\mathbf{x}

(64)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}\left[\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,2}}\nabla\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\right)-\kappa_{N}\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon}-\sigma_{s,2}\left(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi\right)\right](\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})d\mathbf{x}\,.

Due to Lemma
A.4
,
\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq C_{2}N^{3/2+1/p}
for certain constant
C_{2}
independent of
N
, then combine with Lemma
3.6
, the second term on right-hand-side of (
64
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\Omega}\delta\sigma_{s}\left(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi\right)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})d\mathbf{x}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{2}}\left\|\delta\sigma_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\delta\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}N^{2}\left\|\delta\sigma_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\delta\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}N^{39/8}\|\Phi_{1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\delta\sigma_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,,

where the constant
C_{3}
is independent of
N
as well. Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
denote the following quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle:=\kappa_{N}\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})-\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,1}}\nabla\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})\,,

(65)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle:=C_{3}N^{39/8}\|\Phi_{1}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,,

then we obtain the following inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}\right)^{2}\left[(\sigma_{s,1}+2\sigma_{s,2})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})^{2}+\mathcal{X}-2\sigma_{s,2}^{2}\mathcal{Y}\right]d\mathbf{x}

(66)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,2}}\nabla\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\right)-\kappa_{N}\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon}\right)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})\right]^{2}d\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,2}}\nabla\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\right)-\kappa_{N}\frac{\delta H}{\Upsilon}\right)^{2}d\mathbf{x}\,,

where
\lambda>0
is an arbitrary number and the last inequality has used the AM-GM inequality. For the uniqueness, we let
\delta H=0
in above inequality, then it becomes

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,2}}\right)^{2}\left[(\sigma_{s,1}+2\sigma_{s,2}-\lambda)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})^{2}+\mathcal{X}-2\sigma_{s,2}^{2}\mathcal{Y}\right]d\mathbf{x}\leq 0\,.

(67)

Recall that
\underline{c}\leq\sigma_{s,2}\leq\overline{c}
, therefore if

(\sigma_{s,1}+2\underline{c}-\lambda)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})^{2}+\kappa_{N}\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})-\frac{1}{\sigma_{s,1}}\nabla\frac{H_{1}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})>2\overline{c}^{2}\mathcal{Y}\,,

(68)

we could conclude that
\delta\sigma_{s}=0
a.e.. The stability estimate is straightforward by noticing
\kappa_{N}=\mathcal{O}(N)
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

As
N\to\infty
, the requirement that
\mathcal{V}(x;\lambda)>0
could be difficult to fulfill since the growth of
\mathcal{Y}
is much faster than the other terms. The estimate could be greatly improved by giving a tighter bound to
\int_{\Omega}\delta\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi)(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{1})d\mathbf{x}
, for instance, estimate the Frechét derivative of
\frac{\delta\Phi}{\delta\sigma_{s}}
.

In the next, we focus on two important cases of simultaneous reconstructions:
(\Upsilon,\sigma_{a})
and
(\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s})
with multiple illumination sources. In
SP_{1}
, one can only reconstruct any two coefficients with the knowledge about the third one
[
5
,
28
]
and it is impossible to recover all of them unless additional information is provided. In
SP_{N}
, it is still unclear whether or not all of the coefficients can be recovered uniquely.

Reconstruction of
\sigma_{a}
and
\Upsilon
.

In this case, we consider the simultaneous reconstruction of both
\sigma_{a}
and
\Upsilon
with multiple sources
f_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq J
(
J\geq 2
). We denote
H_{j}
the corresponding measurement for
f_{j}
from qPAT. The simplest case
SP_{1}
is studied in
[
5
,
28
]
and linearized case of
SP_{3}
is discussed in
[
12
]
. The key observation is that the ratio of two measurements is independent of the coefficients, which only implicitly depends on
\sigma_{a}
.
We first consider the linearized setting, suppose the scattering coefficient
\sigma_{s}
and the background absorption and Grüneisen coefficients
\sigma_{a},\Upsilon
are known and the perturbations are
\delta\sigma_{a},\delta\Upsilon
. For each
1\leq j\leq J
, suppose
\Phi_{j}
is the background solution with source
f_{j}
, the perturbation in the solution is denoted by
\delta\Phi_{j}
, then by linearizing (
43
),
\delta\Phi_{j}=[\delta\varphi_{j,1},\dots,\delta\varphi_{(N+1)/2}]^{\text{T}}
satisfies the linearized
SP_{N}
system

-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\nabla\delta\varphi_{j,n}\right)+\sigma_{a}\mathbf{p}_{n}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}+\sigma_{s}\mathbf{q}_{n}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}=-\delta\sigma_{a}\mathbf{p}_{n}\cdot\Phi_{j},\quad n=1,\dots,(N+1)/2\,.

(69)

For any pair of indices
1\leq i<j\leq J
, we define the following quantity

\mathcal{H}_{ij}:=(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})\frac{\delta H_{j}}{\sigma_{a}\Upsilon}-(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\sigma_{a}\Upsilon}=(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})-(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i})\,,

(70)

which is known and only depends on
\delta\sigma_{a}
and independent of
\Upsilon
. Our reconstruction will be a natural two-step process, first solve
\delta\sigma_{a}
from the crossing quantity
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
(
1\leq i<j\leq J
), then solve
\delta\Phi_{i}
using the recovered
\delta\sigma_{a}
, finally if
\sigma_{a}>0
find
\delta\Upsilon
through

\delta\Upsilon=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{\delta H_{j}-\Upsilon\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}-\Upsilon\delta\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}}{\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}}\,.

(71)

By taking linearization over the bilinear form (
14
), for any test function
\Psi\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}
,

B(\delta\Phi_{j},\Psi)=-\int_{\Omega}\left[\delta\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\mathbf{s}_{1}\right]\cdot\Psi d\mathbf{x}\,.

(72)

Take
\Psi=\delta\Phi_{j}
and use the fact

B(\delta\Phi_{j},\delta\Phi_{j})=\tilde{B}(\delta\Phi_{j},\delta\Phi_{j})+\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})^{2}d\mathbf{x}\geq\tilde{B}(\delta\Phi_{j},\delta\Phi_{j}),

(73)

we can easily conclude the following estimates from the coerciveness of
\tilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
,

\displaystyle\|\delta\Phi_{j}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(N^{11/8}\|\delta\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)

(74)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(N^{11/8}(1+\log N)\|\delta\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\,,

On the other hand, multiply (
69
) with
s_{1,n}
and sum over
n
,

\delta\sigma_{a}\kappa_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})=-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})\right)+\sigma_{a}\kappa_{N}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}+\sigma_{s}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi_{j}\,,

(75)

therefore we have a straightforward estimate

\|\delta\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\max(\kappa_{N}\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\|_{\ell^{2}},\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{2}})}{\kappa_{N}}\|\delta\Phi_{j}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\right)\,,

(76)

where the constant
\kappa_{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}(4n-1)(1-g^{2n-1})s_{1,n}^{2}=\Theta(N)
and
\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
from Lemma
A.4
, where
\Theta
denotes the Laudau big Theta notation. These two estimates imply that

c^{-1}N^{-11/8}(1+\log N)^{-1}\|\delta\Phi_{j}\|_{[H_{1}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}\leq\|\delta\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq cN\|\delta\Phi_{j}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{(N+1)/2}}.

(77)

for some constant
c>1
independent of
N
.

Therefore if there exists two source distinct functions
f_{i},f_{j}
such that the linear mapping
\delta\sigma_{a}\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{ij}
is invertible and
\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}\neq 0
over
\Omega
, then one can recover both
\delta\sigma_{a}
and
\delta\Phi_{j}
from
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
uniquely. In general such problem is ill-posed due to the compactness of the mapping
\delta\sigma_{a}\mapsto\mathcal{H}_{ij}
, numerical reconstruction of
\delta\sigma_{a}
can be done through the following minimization formulation with regularization,

\delta\sigma_{a}^{\ast}=\argmin_{\delta\sigma_{a}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq J}\left[\Big\|\mathcal{H}_{ij}-\left[(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})-(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i})\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]+\alpha\Big\|\nabla\delta\sigma_{a}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,,

where
\alpha
is the regularization parameter.

Particularly, when the background absorption coefficient
\sigma_{a}=0
or negligible, then we approximately have
\delta H_{i}=\delta\sigma_{a}\Upsilon\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i}
, which does not contain the perturbation
\delta\Upsilon
, in this case, we can only reconstruct
\delta\sigma_{a}
, the stability estimate is similar to the Theorem
3.1
.

Without linearization, we take the ratio of two data sets
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
, then

\frac{H_{i}}{H_{j}}=\frac{\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i}}{\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}}\,,

(78)

which only depends on
\sigma_{a}
, therefore our reconstruction strategy is similar to the linearized case. First, try to solve the minimization problem:

\sigma_{a}^{\ast}=\argmin_{\sigma_{a}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq J}\|H_{i}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})-H_{j}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\|\nabla\sigma_{a}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,.

(79)

Then compute
\Upsilon^{\ast}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{1\leq i\leq J}\frac{H_{i}}{\sigma_{a}^{\ast}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i}}
with the reconstructed
\sigma_{a}^{\ast}
.

Reconstruction of
\sigma_{s}
and
\sigma_{a}
.

We consider the simultaneous reconstruction of both
\sigma_{s}
and
\sigma_{a}
from multiple sources
f_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq J
provided that
\Upsilon
is known. Similar to the previous case, we denote
H_{j}
the measurement for
f_{j}
from the qPAT experiments. Under the linearized setting, let
\sigma_{a}
and
\sigma_{s}
the background absorption and scattering coefficients, the corresponding perturbations are
\delta\sigma_{a}
and
\delta\sigma_{s}
. For each source
f_{j}
, let
\Phi_{j}
the background solution and the perturbation in the solution is
\delta\Phi_{j}
, the corresponding perturbation in the measurement is
\delta H_{j}
. Linearize the variational form (
21
), we obtain the following equation,

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}(\delta\Phi_{j},\Psi)=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta H_{j}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Psi)d\mathbf{x}+\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{(4n-1)(1-g^{2n-1})}\left[\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}^{2}}\nabla\varphi_{1,n}\cdot\nabla\psi_{n}-\mathbf{q}_{n}\cdot\Phi_{j}\psi_{n}\right]d\mathbf{x}\,,

where the bilinear form
\widetilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is from (
24
) and
\mathbf{q}_{n}
is defined in (
44
). Hence the perturbation
\delta\Phi_{j}
only linearly depends on
\delta\sigma_{s}
. On the other hand, since
\delta H_{j}/\Upsilon=\delta\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}+\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}
, the crossing quantity
\mathcal{H}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{a}\Upsilon^{2}}(H_{i}\delta H_{j}-H_{j}\delta H_{i})=(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})-(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i})
only linearly depends on
\delta\sigma_{s}
, therefore we first try to reconstruct
\delta\sigma_{s}
from
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
, then find
\delta\Phi_{j}
and recover
\delta\sigma_{a}
using

\delta\sigma_{a}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left[\left(\frac{\delta H_{j}}{\Upsilon}-\sigma_{a}\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j}\right)/(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})\right]\,.

(80)

Similar to the previous case, the uniqueness is immediate if the crossing term
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
as a linear functional of
\delta\sigma_{s}
is uniquely solvable and
\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j}\neq 0
over
\Omega
. However since
\delta\sigma_{s}\mapsto\delta\Phi_{j}
is a compact mapping, the inverse problem is ill-posed. Numerically, we consider the following
L^{2}
optimization formulation with regularization:

\delta\sigma_{s}^{\ast}=\argmin_{\delta\sigma_{s}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq J}\left[\Big\|\mathcal{H}_{ij}-\left[(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{j})-(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{j})(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i})\right]\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right]+\alpha\Big\|\nabla\delta\sigma_{s}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,.

Additionally, if we are provided
a priori
estimate on the perturbation
\delta\sigma_{a}
that
\|\frac{\delta\sigma_{a}H_{i}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}^{2}}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\ll 1
for certain
1\leq i\leq J
, then linearize the equation (
56
) for
\Phi_{i}
, we obtain

-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{-\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s}^{2}}\nabla\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i}\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\nabla\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i}\right)+\kappa_{N}\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\Upsilon}+\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\left(\delta\sigma_{s}\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{i}+\sigma_{s}\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi_{i}\right)=0\,,

(81)

where
\mathbf{Q}
is defined in (
44
) and
\kappa_{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{(N+1)/2}(4n-1)(1-g^{2n-1})s_{1,n}^{2}
. Following the similar approach in Theorem
3.7
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}\left[\kappa_{N}\frac{H_{i}}{\Upsilon}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{i})+3\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{i})^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\Phi_{i})\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{i})\right]d\mathbf{x}

(82)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s}}\left[\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{s}}\nabla\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i}\right)-\kappa_{N}\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\Upsilon}-\sigma_{s}\left(\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\delta\Phi_{i}\right)\right](\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\Phi_{i})d\mathbf{x}\,,

Replace
\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\delta\Phi_{i}=(\delta H_{i}-\delta\sigma_{a}H_{i}/\sigma_{a})/(\Upsilon\sigma_{a})
and
we immediately get the following estimate from the argument of Theorem
3.7
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{V}_{N}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{\delta\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s}}\right)^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{C}\left(N^{2}\left\|\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\right\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\frac{H_{i}\delta\sigma_{a}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}^{2}}\right\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

(83)

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{C}\left(N^{2}\left\|\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\Upsilon\sigma_{a}}\right\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\,,

where
\mathcal{V}_{N}(\mathbf{x})
is the same as in the Theorem
3.7
,
\mathsf{C}
is a constant independent of
N
.

4.4
Reconstruction of
\sigma_{s}
only

The reconstruction of
\sigma_{s}
is a nonlinear problem for
N\geq 3
. In order to provide a fair comparison of across the
SP_{N}
models, we will use the optimization based method to reconstruct the scattering coefficient:

\sigma_{s}=\argmin_{\sigma_{s}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|H(\mathbf{x})-H^{\ast}|^{2}+|\nabla(H(\mathbf{x})-H^{\ast})|^{2}\right)d\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{s}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\,,

(84)

where
H^{\ast}
is the measured datum and
\beta
is the regularization parameter. The optimization problem is solved by L-BFGS method, the gradient is computed from the adjoint state technique. The choice of regularization parameter
\beta
should depend on the noise level. One should notice that we use
H^{1}(\Omega)
norm instead of the traditional
L^{2}(\Omega)
minimization:

\sigma_{s}=\argmin_{\sigma_{s}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|H(\mathbf{x})-H^{\ast}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sigma_{s}|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\,.

(85)

This is due to the stability estimate in Theorem
3.7
, where actually the
H^{2}(\Omega)
norm is needed for the stability estimate. However, such regularity requirement implies that
H(\mathbf{x})
needs to be globally
C^{1}
from Sobolev embedding, which means our finite element space needs to equip with polynomials of five or higher degrees (e.g. Argyris element). Here we relax objective functional to
H^{1}(\Omega)
norm simply to avoid the extraordinary computational cost. To get a brief impression about the two optimization schemes, we take the
f_{1}
source function with
SP_{3}
model (both data generation and reconstruction) for an example, the datum
H
is not contaminated (noise level
\gamma=0
)
and regularization parameter
\beta=0
as well. The reconstructed scattering coefficients are shown in Fig
5
. One can tell from the images that the coefficient recovered from
L^{2}
optimization (
85
) still contains background artifacts. The reason behind is the relatively strong smoothing effect of the mapping
\sigma_{s}(\mathbf{x})\mapsto H(\mathbf{x})
, where the high frequency information in
\sigma_{s}
could not be fully recovered if we emphasize equally on
H(\mathbf{x})
’s frequency information.

Figure 5:
Left: The reconstructed scattering coefficeint using
L^{2}
minimization, the
L^{2}
relative error is
4.25\%
. Right: The reconstructed scattering coefficient using
H^{1}
minimization, the
L^{2}
relative error is
1.10\%
.

Therefore the data contamination should be treated carefully for the
H^{1}
minimization (
84
). This is because if we still apply the random noises by
H^{\ast}=H(1+\gamma\texttt{random})
at each mesh node, then
\|H-H^{\ast}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N}\gamma)
, where
N
is the total number of nodes assuming the mesh is uniform. Therefore instead of pointwise multiplicative noise, we aggressively contaminate the datum by perturbing its Fourier modes

\mathcal{F}{H^{\ast}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\mathcal{F}{H}(\boldsymbol{\xi})(1+\gamma\texttt{random})\,,

(86)

where we have used
\gamma=2\%
as the noise level parameter. Note that such noise will perturb the low frequency modes of
H
which might cause severe global artifacts in the reconstruction.

(\sigma_{a}

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s_{k,l}
be the
(k,l)
-th entry of
M^{-1}
, then

s_{k,l}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2k-1}(-1)^{l-k}\frac{((2l-2))!!}{(2l-1)!!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(2k-2)!!},\quad&l\geq k\,,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(90)

Proof.

Let
S
be the matrix with
(k,l)
-th entry as
s_{k,l}
, we then compute the
(k,j)
-th entry of
SM
by

a_{k,j}:=s_{k,j}(2j-1)+s_{k,j-1}(2j-2)
. If
k=j
,
s_{k,j}=\frac{1}{2j-1}
, therefore
a_{k,j}=\frac{1}{2j-1}(2j-1)=1
. If
j<k
, notice that now
s_{k,j}=0
, we must have
a_{k,j}=0
. If
j>k
, we compute
a_{k,j}
directly

\frac{1}{2k-1}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(2k-2)!!}\left((-1)^{j-k}\frac{((2j-2))!!}{(2j-1)!!}(2j-1)+(-1)^{j-k-1}\frac{((2j-4))!!}{(2j-3)!!}(2j-2)\right)=0\,.

(91)

Hence
S=M^{-1}
.

Lemma A.2
.

\det(R^{-1})\leq\mathcal{O}(N^{3/8})
, hence
\det(R)\geq\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/8})
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle R_{ij}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j-1}\frac{2\Gamma(i+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}{\pi\Gamma(i)\Gamma(j)}\frac{(4j-3)}{(2i+2j-2)(2j-2i-1)}\,,

(92)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i+j}\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}{\pi\Gamma(i)\Gamma(j)}\frac{2j-\frac{3}{2}}{(i-\frac{1}{4})^{2}-(j-\frac{3}{4})^{2}}\,,

which gives the factorization

R=UGV\,,

(93)

where diagonal matrices

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle={\rm diag}(u_{1},\dots,u_{(N+1)/2})\,,

(94)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle={\rm diag}(v_{1},\dots,v_{(N+1)/2})\,,

with
u_{i}=(-1)^{i}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(i)},v_{j}=(-1)^{j}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(j)}(j-\frac{3}{4})\,,

and
G
is the Cauchy-Toeplitz matrix,

G=\left(\frac{1}{(i-\frac{1}{4})^{2}-(j-\frac{3}{4})^{2}}\right)_{i,j=1,\dots,(N+1)/2}\,.

(95)

We denote
x_{i}=(i-\frac{1}{4})^{2}
and
y_{j}=(j-\frac{3}{4})^{2}
, then use the Theorem 2.1 in
[
36
]
,

G^{-1}=PG^{T}Q\,,

(96)

where
{P}=\text{diag}(p_{1},\dots,p_{(N+1)/2})
and
Q=\text{diag}(q_{1},\dots,q_{(N+1)/2})
satisfy

\displaystyle G[p_{1},\dots,p_{(N+1)/2}]^{T}

\displaystyle=[1,\dots,1]^{T},

(97)

\displaystyle G^{T}[q_{1},\dots,q_{(N+1)/2}]^{T}

\displaystyle=[1,\dots,1]^{T}\,.

Also
p_{i},q_{j}
are computed explicitly by Cramer’s law,

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}(x_{l}-y_{i})/\prod_{l=1,l\neq i}^{(N+1)/2}(y_{l}-y_{i})\,,

(98)

\displaystyle q_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}(x_{j}-y_{l})/\prod_{l=1,l\neq j}^{(N+1)/2}(x_{j}-x_{l})\,.

By replacing
x_{i},y_{j}
with their values, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}(l+i-1)(l-i+\frac{1}{2})/\prod_{l=1,l\neq i}^{(N+1)/2}(l-i)(l+i-3/2)

(99)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{l+i-1}{l+i-\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left(2i-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2l}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2-i}\left(1+\frac{1}{2l}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{N+1}{2})}{\Gamma(i+\frac{N}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(i-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(i)}\left(2i-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2l}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2-i}\left(1+\frac{1}{2l}\right)\right)\,,

\displaystyle q_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}(j+l-1)(j-l+\frac{1}{2})/\prod_{l=1,l\neq j}^{(N+1)/2}(j-l)(j+l-1/2)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{j+l-1}{j+l-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(2j-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{2l}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2-j}\left(1-\frac{1}{2l}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(j+\frac{N+1}{2})\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(j+\frac{N}{2}+1)\Gamma(j)}\left(2j-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{2l}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2-j}\left(1-\frac{1}{2l}\right)\right)\,.

Then we can easily deduce
\det(R^{-1})=\sqrt{\det(P)\det(Q)}/(\det(V)\det(U))
, since all matrices involved are diagonal, let
S_{i}=\frac{\pi}{2}\prod_{l=1}^{i-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{2l}\right)\prod_{l=1}^{(N+1)/2-i}\left(1-\frac{1}{2l}\right)
, then from the theory of Gamma functions, we know

\displaystyle S_{i}=\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2}-i+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(i)\Gamma(\frac{(N+1)}{2}-i+1)}\,.

(100)

Hence we can estimate
\det(R^{-1})
’s upper bound by estimating

\displaystyle\det(R^{-1})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{\sqrt{p_{i}q_{i}}}{u_{i}v_{i}}

(101)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}S_{i}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{N+1}{2})^{2}\Gamma(i)^{2}(i-\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(i+\frac{N}{2})\Gamma(i+\frac{N}{2}+1)\Gamma(i+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(i-\frac{1}{2})(i-\frac{3}{4})}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{\Gamma(i-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(i)}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{(N+1)}{2})^{2}(i-\frac{1}{2})(i-\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma(i+\frac{(N+1)}{2}-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(i+\frac{(N+1)}{2}+\frac{1}{2})(i-\frac{3}{4})}}\,.

Then by noticing the Chu’s Double Inequality
[
7
]
,

\sqrt{x-\frac{1}{4}}<\frac{\Gamma(x+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(x)}<\frac{x}{\sqrt{x+\frac{1}{4}}}\,,

(102)

the following estimates hold,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(i+\frac{N+1}{2})^{2}}{\Gamma(i+\frac{N}{2})\Gamma(i+\frac{N}{2}+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{i+\frac{(N+1)}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}{i+\frac{(N+1)}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}<1\,,

(103)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(i-\frac{1}{2})^{2}}{\Gamma(i)^{2}}\frac{(i-\frac{1}{2})(i-\frac{1}{4})}{i-\frac{3}{4}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{(i-\frac{1}{2})(i-\frac{1}{4})}{(i-\frac{3}{4})^{2}}}\,,

which implies

\displaystyle\det(R^{-1})

\displaystyle<\prod_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\sqrt{\frac{(4i-2)(4i-1)}{(4i-3)^{2}}}

(104)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\log\left(1+\frac{3}{4i-3}+\frac{2}{(4i-3)^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{(N+1)/2}\frac{3}{4i-3}+\frac{2}{(4i-3)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\exp\left(\frac{3}{8}\log\left(\frac{(N+1)}{2}\right)\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}(N^{3/8})\,.

∎

Lemma A.3
.

\|R\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{N+1}{2}
.

Proof.

We show that for all
m\geq 1
,

\sum_{j=1}^{m}|R_{j,m}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|R_{m,j}|^{2}\leq 1\,.

(105)

The above estimate is true for
m=1
since
R_{1,1}=\frac{1}{2}
, we only focus on the cases that
m\geq 2
.
Reformulate
R_{j,m}
by the Gamma function as

|R_{j,m}|=\frac{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(m-\frac{1}{2})}{\pi\Gamma(j)\Gamma(m)(j+m-1)}\frac{(4m-3)}{2m-2j-1}\,.

(106)

From the Chu’s Double Inequality
[
7
]

\sqrt{x-\frac{1}{4}}<\frac{\Gamma(x+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(x)}<\frac{x}{\sqrt{x+\frac{1}{4}}}\,,

(107)

the following estimates hold,

\frac{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(j)}<\frac{j}{\sqrt{j+\frac{1}{4}}}\,,\quad\frac{\Gamma(m-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(m)}<\frac{1}{(m-\frac{3}{4})^{1/2}}\,,

(108)

we can deduce the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}|R_{j,m}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\Gamma(j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(m-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(j)\Gamma(m)}\right)^{2}\frac{(4m-3)^{2}}{(j+m-1)^{2}}\frac{1}{(2m-2j-1)^{2}}

(109)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(4m-3)^{2}(j-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4(1+4j)})}{(m-\frac{3}{4})(j+m-1)^{2}(2m-2j-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(4j-1+\frac{1}{(1+4j)})(4m-3)}{(2j+2m-2)^{2}(2m-2j-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\frac{1}{(2m-2j-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2j+2m-2)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(4m-3)}{(1+4j)(2j+2m-2)^{2}(2m-2j-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left[\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-\frac{1}{4m}\right)+\frac{1}{5m(2m-3)^{2}}+\frac{1}{9m}\sum_{j=2}^{m}\frac{1}{(2m-2j-1)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left[\left(1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\right)+\left(\frac{11}{180}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{72}\right)\frac{1}{m}\right]\,.

The other part can be estimated in a similar way,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|R_{m,j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(j-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(j)}\right)^{2}\frac{(4j-3)^{2}}{(j+m-1)^{2}}\frac{1}{(2j-2m-1)^{2}}

(110)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{(4j-3)^{2}(m-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4(1+4m)})}{(j-\frac{3}{4})(j+m-1)^{2}(2j-2m-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{(4m-1+\frac{1}{1+4m})(4j-3)}{(2j+2m-2)^{2}(2j-2m-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\left[\frac{1}{(2m+1-2j)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2j+2m-2)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{4j-3}{(1+4m)(2j+2m-2)^{2}(2m+1-2j)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-1-\frac{1}{4m}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{4m^{2}(2m+1-2j)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-1-\frac{1}{4m}\right)+\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-1\right)\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\right]\,.

Hence we can estimate that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}|R_{j,m}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|R_{m,j}|^{2}\leq 1-\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{17}{90}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{72}\right)\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-1\right)\frac{1}{m^{2}}\,,

(111)

the above estimate is strictly less than
1
for
m\geq 2
, then the Frobenius norm’s square of
R
is estimated by

\|R\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{m=2}^{(N+1)/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|R_{j,m}|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|R_{m,j}|^{2}\right)\leq\frac{N}{2}\,.

(112)

∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{k}
the
k
-th row of the matrix
M^{-1}
in (
7
), and matrix
\mathbf{Q}
is defined in (
44
), then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{p}}=\mathcal{O}(N^{3/2+1/p})\,.

(113)

Proof.

Let
s_{k,j}
denote the
j
-th entry of vector
\mathbf{s}_{k}
. Combine the Lemma
A.1
and Gautschi’s inequality
[
14
]
, we have

|s_{k,j}|<\frac{1}{2k-1}\frac{\sqrt{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}}\,.

(114)

For the matrix
\mathbf{Q}
, we denote its
(n,j)
-th entry by
Q_{nj}
, which can be estimated by

\displaystyle|Q_{nj}|

\displaystyle\leq(4n-1)\sum_{k\geq 2}^{\min(j,n)}(4k-3)|s_{k,n}s_{k,j}|\leq(4n-1)\sum_{k\geq 2}^{\min(j,n)}\frac{(4k-3)}{(2k-1)^{2}}\frac{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}}}

(115)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(4n-1)}{\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{k\geq 2}^{\min(j,n)}\frac{(4k-3)(k+\frac{1}{2})}{(2k-1)^{2}}\,.

Since
(4k-3)(k+\frac{1}{2})\leq 2(2k-1)^{2}
for all
k
, we will have
|Q_{n,j}|\leq\frac{(8n-2)}{\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}}(\min(j,n)-1)
, therefore, by simple calculations,
\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 1}|s_{1,n}||Q_{n,j}|\leq\sup_{j\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(8n-2)(\min(j,n)-1)}{(n-\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{j-\frac{1}{2}}}

(116)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(N^{3/2})\,.

Then the
\ell^{p}
estimate is

\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\|\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\left(\frac{(N+1)}{2}\right)^{1/p}=\mathcal{O}(N^{3/2+1/p})\,.

∎

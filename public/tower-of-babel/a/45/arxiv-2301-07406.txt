Lower semicontinuity and relaxation for free discontinuity functionals with non-standard growth
Stefano Almi, Dario Reggiani, Francesco Solombrino

Abstract
. A lower semicontinuity result and a relaxation formula for free discontinuity functionals with non-standard growth in the bulk energy are provided. Our analysis is based on a non-trivial adaptation of the blow-up [ 8 ] and of the global method for relaxation [ 17 ] to the setting of generalized special function of bounded variation with Orlicz growth. Key tools developed in this paper are an integral representation result and a Poincaré inequality under non-standard growth.

1.
Introduction

Integral functionals with non-standard growth first appeared in the works of Zhikov
[
62
,
61
]
for modeling composite materials characterized by a strongly anisotropic behavior. The non-standard character of such functionals is typically expressed in terms of a point-dependent integrability of the deformation gradient, which may be captured, in a functional setting, in terms of variable exponents spaces
[
51
]
or, more in general, in Orlicz type of spaces (see, e.g.,
[
45
]
). As relevant examples of bulk energies undergoing non-standard growth we report here the so-called
variable exponent
and the
double-phase
case

(1.1)

|\xi|^{p(x)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\xi|^{p}+a(x)|\xi|^{q}\qquad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$,}

for suitable choices of the exponent function
p\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(1,+\infty)
, of the exponents
1<p<q<+\infty
, and of the weight function
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
. We refer to Section
4.3
for a list of relevant examples in the literature.

In a Sobolev/Orlicz setting, the study of integral functionals with non-standard growth has attracted an increasing attention in the last decades. Lower semicontinuity, relaxation, and integral representation have been tackled in a number of papers: we mention the works
[
1
,
18
,
42
,
43
]
, dealing with the role of convexity and quasiconvexity in the
gap problem
for functionals characterized by a
(p,q)
-growth, i.e., different grow rates from above and below. Further results on relaxation in the gap problem have been presented in
[
56
]
, where some explicit examples of concentration effects for bulk densities of double-phase type (
1.1
) are discussed, pointing out the importance of the Hölder regularity of
a(\cdot)
in the relaxation procedure. The variable exponent setting has been dealt with in
[
30
]
under the so called log-Hölder continuity assumption on the exponent function
p(\cdot)
. Further instances of non-standard growth in partial differential equations may be found in
[
15
,
16
,
22
,
31
,
38
,
55
,
58
]
, while applications ranging from optimal design to electro-rheological fluids and homogeneization appeared in
[
13
,
14
,
59
,
64
,
65
]
. Let us also mention the somehow related topic of regularity of minimizers of functionals with non-standard growth, which have been thoroughly discussed in
[
2
,
4
,
29
,
39
,
40
]
for the variable exponent, in, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
,
27
,
28
,
35
,
36
,
54
]
for the double phase, and in
[
49
]
in a unified and generalized framework.

When dealing with composite materials, it is rather natural to account for failure phenomena, such as fracture, which can not be captured by a mere bulk energy defined on Sobolev or Orlicz spaces. This leads us to the extension of the above non-standard growth functionals to a Free Discontinuity setting, where singularities may appear in the form of jump discontinuities. Besides Materials Science, applications of a variable exponent in the setting of functions of bounded variation already appeared in image reconstruction
[
23
]
(see also
[
46
,
47
]
), where an intermediate regime between Total Variation and the isotropic diffusion away from the edges was proposed.

The aim of this paper it to provide a
unified framework
for lower semicontinuity and relaxation of functionals of the form

(1.2)

\mathcal{G}(u):=\int_{\Omega}f(x,\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}g(x,[u](x),\nu_{u})\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,,

focusing on the role played by the non-standard growth condition of volume integrand
f(x,\xi)
. In formula (
1.2
),
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u\in GSBV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a function of
generalized special bounded variation
(see
[
10
, Section 4.5]
),
J_{u}
denotes the jump set of
u
,
\nu_{u}
stands for the approximate unit normal to
u
, and
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
represents the jump of
u
, that is, the difference between the traces
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
of
u
on
J_{u}
, defined according to the orientation of
\nu_{u}
. For the sole variable exponent case, lower semicontinuity results for
\mathcal{G}
have been obtained in
[
34
]
, while
\Gamma
-convergence and relaxation issues have been recently considered in
[
60
]
. The key assumptions in the mentioned papers are superlinearity of
p(\cdot)
(meaning
\min_{\Omega}p(\cdot)>1
), which allows for a separation of scales in the
\Gamma
-convergence and relaxation processes, and a log-Hölder continuity of the exponent, necessary to avoid the Lavrentiev’s phenomenon, as originally observed in
[
63
]
.

Our primary interest is in providing suitable conditions which either imply the lower semicontinuity of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in (
1.2
) or guarantee an explicit formula for its lower semicontinuous envelope, while assuming
BV
-ellipticity
[
10
, Chapter 5]
of
g
and non-standard growth for
f
. In this regard, we will suppose that there exists a superlinear
generalized
\Phi
-function
(see also Definitions
4.3
and
4.13
)
\psi\colon\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(1.3)

a\psi(x,|\xi|)\leq f(x,\xi)\leq b(1+\psi(x,|\xi|))\qquad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}$}

for some
0<a<b<+\infty
. The superlinearity of
\psi
(and thus of
f
) is expressed by the conditions
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
and
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
for some
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
(see also Definition
4.12
), meaning that for a.e.
x\in\Omega
the maps
t\mapsto\frac{\psi(x,t)}{t^{p}}
and
t\mapsto\frac{\psi(x,t)}{t^{q}}
are monotone increasing and monotone decreasing, respectively. The third basic hypothesis on
\psi
, common to all our results, is
(A0)
. Loosely speaking, such condition does not allow for a too degenerate behavior on small balls contained in
\Omega
of the functions

(1.4)

\psi^{+}_{B}(t):=\sup_{x\in B}\,\psi(x,t)\qquad\psi^{-}_{B}(t):=\inf_{x\in B}\,\psi(x,t)\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,+\infty)$ and $B\subseteq\Omega$}.

We remark that
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
,
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
, and
(A0)
are well-suited for a blow-up argument, which is at the core of our proofs strategy, and are rather standard in the theory of generalized Orlicz spaces
[
45
]
.

Let us discuss our results in more details. In Theorem
3.3
we prove the lower semicontinuity of the functional
\mathcal{G}
in the space
GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, the space of functions
u\in GSBV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
L^{\psi}
-integrable approximate gradient
\nabla u
. We refer to Definition
4.28
and Section
4.2
for more details on such space. Besides
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
,
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
, and
(A0)
, in Theorem
3.3
we ask for the quasiconvexity of
f
and for a mild continuity property of
\psi(x,t)
with respect to
x\in\Omega
(see (
3.6
) for a precise statement). We notice that the last condition allows for improvements in the existing theory of generalized Orlicz spaces. For instance, in the variable exponent case the log-Hölder continuity of
p(\cdot)
is not necessary for (
3.6
), as pointed out in Section
4.3
. The proof of Theorem
3.3
is based on the approximation strategy of
[
53
]
and on a localization technique which leads us to study the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{G}
around Lebesgue points of the limit energy, as first done in
[
8
]
in the
GSBV
-setting (see also
[
10
, Theorem 5.29]
). Our assumptions are designed in such a way that the maximal operator for
SBV^{p}
-functions, exploited in
[
8
]
for the construction of more regular approximating sequences, can be replaced by the maximal operator in Orlicz spaces, for which continuity estimates has been obtained in
[
50
]
(see also Theorem
4.35
).

In Theorem
3.4
we show a relaxation formula of the functional
\mathcal{G}
of the form

(1.5)

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}f(x,\nabla u)\,dx+\int_{J_{u}}\mathcal{R}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})d\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

which therefore maintains the original structure (
1.2
). In particular, in (
1.5
) the functions
\mathcal{Q}f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
and
\mathcal{R}g\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
denote the quasiconvex and the BV-elliptic envelope of
f
and
g
, respectively. When dealing with relaxation, we have to strengthen condition (
3.6
). Thus, in Theorem
3.4
we replace the latter with the stronger assumption
(adA1)
, which allows to estimate
\psi^{+}_{B}
with
\psi^{-}_{B}
on small balls
B\subseteq\Omega
with a fixed control rate. We remark that condition
(adA1)
is weaker than the more traditional
(A1)
, introduced in the framework of Orlicz spaces (see, e.g.,
[
45
]
). In the variable exponent case,
(adA1)
still requires log-Hölder continuity of
p(\cdot)
, while in the double-phase case it calls for a Hölder continuity of the weight
a(x)
, which may be however weaker than the one considered in regularity theory in Sobolev/Orlicz setting (see, for instance,
[
12
,
28
]
). Further examples are discussed in Section
4.3
.

The proof of Theorem
3.4
follows the well-established strategy of the global method of relaxation
[
17
,
19
]
, which need to be adapted to the non-standard growth framework (
1.4
). The crucial step towards the relaxation formula (
1.5
) is the integral representation of the lower semicontinuous envelope
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
in
GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(see Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
). This ensures that
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
can still be written as the sum of a bulk energy depending on the approximate gradient
\nabla u\in L^{\psi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
and of a surface term obtained by integrating over the jump set
J_{u}
of
u
a suitable function depending on
x
,
[u]
, and
\nu_{u}
. The fundamental tool to characterize the behavior of the blow-up energy on jump points and on approximate differentiability points of
u
is a new Poincaré inequality for special functions of bounded variation with
L^{\varphi}
-integrable approximate gradient, where
\varphi
denotes a (generalized)
\Phi
-function in the sense of Definition
4.3
. Such inequality, obtained in Theorems
5.1
and
5.9
, is of independent mathematical interest, as it can not be recovered by the classical approach of
[
37
]
or of
[
60
]
for the variable exponent, while it builds upon fine properties of rearrangments of
BV
-functions, adapting and combining the techniques of
[
5
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. The possible non-homogeneity of
\varphi
in space implies that a suitable truncation of
u
can not be controlled only in term of
\nabla u
, as some remainder term appears, that can be estimated along the blow-up procedure thanks to
(adA1)
. The crucial role played by the Poincaré inequality is evident in Lemmas
6.6
and
6.9
and enables us to recover the densities of the lower semicontinuous envelope
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
as blow-up limits of cell minimization formulas. In both lemmas, the Poincaré inequality is exploited to replace the optimal blow-up sequences with more regular functions, without excessively increasing the energy.

Outlook and open problems.

Our paper presents a general framework for lower semicontinuity and relaxation of free discontinuity functionals under non-standard growth of the bulk functional. The generalized
\Phi
-functions we consider cover most of the examples appeared in the literature, and in particular among them the variable exponent and the double phase (
1.1
). Because of the superlinear assumption
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
for
p>1
, however, we can not handle an
\ell\log\ell
-kind of behavior, which has been studied in
[
52
]
for a lower-semicontinuity problem in
SBV
without
x
-dependence on the integrand function in (
1.2
). Hence, an extension of our results in the above direction will be considered in a forthcoming research. Furthermore, regularity issues for minimizers of the functional
\mathcal{G}
may be investigated, in the spirit of
[
10
, Chapter 6]
, and could lead to stronger assumptions on the growth function
\psi
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
for the variable exponent). Also the investigation of lower semicontinuity and relaxation issues for free discontinuity functionals with bulk energies having mixed
(p,q)
-growth condition, relevant for the modeling of determinant constraints, is not fully covered by our theory and deserves further analysis.

and

the

\Omega
.
Given

\varepsilon>0
, for any set

A_{\varepsilon,x_{0}}:=x_{0}+\varepsilon(A-x_{0}).

The closure of a set

(2.2)

\varphi^{-}_{B}(t):=\essinf_{x\in B\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,t)\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \varphi^{-}_{B}(t):=\esssup_{x\in B\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,t).

.

3.
Main Results

The paper will be concerned with integral representation, lower semicontinuity, and relaxation of functionals defined on the space
GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of generalized special functions of bounded variation with
\psi
-growth on the gradient. Here
\psi
is a suitable (generalized) Orlicz function
\psi
. We will consider functionals

\mathcal{F}:GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)

satisfying the following assumptions:

(H1)

\mathcal{F}(u,\cdot)
is a Borel measure for any
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

(H2)

\mathcal{F}(\cdot,A)
is lower semicontinuous with respect to convergence in measure in
\Omega
for any
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
;

(H3)

\mathcal{F}(\cdot,A)
is local for every
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
, that is, if
u,v\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfy
u=v

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in
A
then
\mathcal{F}(u,A)=\mathcal{F}(v,A)
;

(H4)

there exist
0<a<b
such that for every
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
it holds

a\left(\int_{B}\psi(x,|\nabla u|)dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)\leq\mathcal{F}(u,B)\leq b\left(\int_{B}(1+\psi(x,|\nabla u|))dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right).

In (
H4
), as we said, we consider a generalized Orlicz function
\psi
. The assumptions we make on
\psi
will be detailed in Section
4
(see
(aInc)
,
(aDec)
and the so-called weight condition
(A0)
). These properties are standard in the theory of generalized Orlicz functions. We also assume that
\psi
satisfies

(adA1)

For every ball
B\subset\Omega
with
\textnormal{diam}(B)\leq 1
there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\psi_{B}^{+}(\beta t)\leq\psi_{B}^{-}(t)\ \ \ \mbox{for all $t\in\left[\sigma,(\psi_{B}^{-})^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\textnormal{diam}(B)}\right)\right]$}\,,

where
\sigma\geq 1
is the constant in the weight condition
(A0)
. We remark that this condition is a
weaker variant
of the standard assumption
(A1)
(see Definition
4.21
), called local continuity condition and usually considered for generalized Orlicz spaces.
All the main results of the paper will hold, if one requires that
(A0)
,
(adA1)
,
(aInc)
and
(aDec)
are satisfied on
\Omega
. They encompass a broad range of applications, with some relevant examples that will be discussed in Section
4.3
.

Our first result concerns the integral representation, and requires some notation to be fixed. For every
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and every
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
we define

(3.1)

\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{F}}(u,A):=\inf_{v\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\{\mathcal{F}(v,A)\colon v=u\ \mbox{in a neighborhood of $\partial A$}\}.

Moreover, given
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, we define the affine function
\ell_{x_{0},u_{0},\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

\ell_{x_{0},u_{0},\xi}:=u_{0}+\xi(x-x_{0}).

Given
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we also introduce
u_{x_{0},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

u_{x_{0},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\nu}=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{a}&if $(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu>0$,\\
\mathfrak{b}&if $(x-x_{0})\cdot\nu<0$.\end{cases}

Our main result concerning the integral representation is the following. Below,
\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
denotes the class of weak generalized
\Phi
-functions, whose definition is recalled in Definition
4.13
.

Theorem 3.1

(Integral representation in
GSBV^{\psi}
)
.

Let
\psi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfy
(A0)
,
(adA1)
,
(aInc)
and
(aDec)
on
\Omega
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}:GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies assumptions (
H1
)–(
H4
). Then, for all
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and all
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)

\mathcal{F}(u,A)=\int_{A}f(x,u(x),\nabla u(x))\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap A}g(x,u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle f(x_{0},u_{0},\xi):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{F}}(\ell_{x_{0},u_{0},\xi},B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{d}\varepsilon^{d}}\ \ \ \mbox{for $(x_{0},u_{0},\xi_{0})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$},

(3.3)

\displaystyle g(x_{0},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\nu):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{x_{0},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\nu},B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{d-1}\varepsilon^{d-1}}\ \ \ \mbox{for all $x_{0}\in\Omega$, $\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$}.

If translation invariance with respect to
u
is assumed on
\mathcal{F}
, namely

(H5)

\mathcal{F}(u+c,A)=\mathcal{F}(u,A)
for every
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, every
A\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
and every
c\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

we have the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\psi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
be as in Theorem

and suppose that
\mathcal{F}:GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies assumptions (

). Then, for all
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and all
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)

\mathcal{F}(u,A)=\int_{A}f(x,\nabla u(x))\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap A}g(x,[u](x),\nu_{u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
f
and

are as in (
3.2
) and (

Our next result concerns the lower semicontinuity in
GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of variational functionals
\mathcal{G}:L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
of the form

(3.4)

\mathcal{G}(u,A)=\int_{A}f(x,\nabla u(x))+\int_{J_{u}}g(x,[u](x),\nu_{u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

In (
3.4
) the function
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies the following assumptions:

(f1)

f
is a Carathéodory function;

(f2)

there exist two constants
a,b>0
such that

(3.5)

a\psi(x,|\xi|)\leq f(x,\xi)\leq b(1+\psi(x,|\xi|))

for every
x\in\Omega
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
.

On the other hand, the function
g\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies the following assumptions:

(g1)

g
is a Borel measurable function lower semicontinuous in
x
and continuous in the remaining variables;

(g2)

there exist
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
such that for every

,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

\alpha_{1}\leq g(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq\alpha_{2}.

For the result below, we can further weaken

. We will namely assume that

complies with the following property: for
\sigma\geq 1
being the constant in the weight condition
(A0)
, and for
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e.

there exists

such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{given $\theta>\sigma$, we can find $\varepsilon_{0}>0$}&\mbox{ such that for every $\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}$ and every $t\in[\sigma,\theta],$}\\
&\psi^{+}_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}(t)\leq C\psi^{-}_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}(t).\end{split}

As we will discuss in Section
4
, if

satisfies

,
(aDec)
and

on

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\psi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)

,

and

Consider a functional

The third main result is a relaxation result, which requires both the use of Theorems

and

,

Theorem 3.4
.

4.
Preliminaries

In this preliminary section we recall some basic facts about (generalized)
\Phi
-functions and Orlicz spaces, and on

functions. Finally, in Subsection

we give a list of non-standard growth functions

4.1.
(Generalized)
\Phi
-functions and Orlicz spaces

We begin by collecting some basic definitions and useful facts about
\Phi
-functions and generalized Orlicz spaces. For a complete treatment of the topic (and the proofs of the statements below which are left without proof) we refer to
[
45
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A function
g\colon(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is almost increasing (resp. almost decreasing) if there exists a constant
a\geq 1
such that
g(s)\leq ag(t)
(resp
ag(s)\geq g(t)
) for every
0<s<t
.

Increasing and decreasing functions are included in the above definition if
a=1
.

Definition 4.2
.

L
et
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
and
p,q>0
. We say that
f
satisfies

\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}

if
\frac{f(t)}{t^{p}}
is increasing;

\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}

if
\frac{f(t)}{t^{p}}
is almost increasing;

\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}

if
\frac{f(t)}{t^{q}}
is decreasing;

\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}

if
\frac{f(t)}{t^{q}}
is almost decreasing;

We say that
f
satisfies
(aInc)
,
(Inc)
,
(aDec)
or
(Dec)
if there exist
p>1
or
q<\infty
such that
f
satisfies
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
,
\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}
,
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
or
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
, respectively.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
be increasing with
\varphi(0)=0=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(t)
and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\varphi(t)=+\infty
. Such
\varphi
is called a
\Phi
-prefunction. We say that a
\Phi
-prefunction
\varphi
is a

-

weak
\Phi
-function if it satisfies
\textnormal{(aInc)}_{1}
on
(0,+\infty)
;

-

convex
\Phi
-function if it is left-continuous and convex;

-

strong
\Phi
-function if it is continuous in the topology of
[0,+\infty]
and convex.

The set of weak, convex and strong
\Phi
-functions are denoted by
\Phi_{w}
,
\Phi_{c}
and
\Phi_{s}
, respectively.

It follows by definition that
\Phi_{s}\subset\Phi_{c}\subset\Phi_{w}
. If a function
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
satisfies
(aDec)
then
\varphi\in\Phi_{s}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Two function
\varphi
and
\psi
are called equivalent,
\varphi\simeq\psi
, if there exists
L\geq 1
such that
\varphi(t/L)\leq\psi(t)\leq\varphi(Lt)
for all
t\geq 0
. The notation "
\approx
" is instead used with the following meaning:
\varphi\approx\psi
if and only if there exist two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}\varphi\leq\psi\leq c_{2}\varphi
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
be increasing with
\varphi\simeq\psi
. Then, the following facts hold:

(a)

if
\varphi
is a
\Phi
-prefunction, then
\psi
is a
\Phi
-prefunction;

(b)

if
\varphi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}
, then
\psi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}
;

(c)

if
\varphi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
, then
\psi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
satisfies
\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}
with
p\geq 1
, then there exists
\psi\in\Phi_{c}
equivalent to
\varphi
such that
\psi^{1/p}
is convex. In particular,
\psi
satisfies
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{p}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Every weak
\Phi
-function is equivalent to a strong
\Phi
-function. Moreover, if
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
is finite valued and satisfies
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{1}
, then we can find
\psi\in\Phi_{s}
such that

\psi(t)\leq\varphi(t)\leq\psi(2t)\ \ \ t\geq 0.

Now, we recall the concept of doubling functions and its equivalence with
(aDec)
.

Definition 4.8
.

We say that a function
\varphi\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
, or that it is doubling, if there exists a constant
K\geq 2
such that

\varphi(2t)\leq K\varphi(t)\ \ \ \mbox{for all $t\geq 0$}.

Lemma 4.9
.

The following statements hold.

(a)

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
, then
\Delta_{2}
is equivalent to
(aDec)
.

(b)

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
, then
\Delta_{2}
is equivalent to
(Dec)
.

Remark 4.10
.

By the previous Lemma it follows that if
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}
satisfies
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
, then it satisfies
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q_{2}}
for some possibly larger

. This and Lemma
4.5
(c) imply that if
\varphi
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
and
\psi\simeq\varphi
, then
\psi
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
. Then
\varphi\simeq\psi
if and only if
\varphi^{-1}\approx\psi^{-1}
. In particular, if
\psi(t/L)\leq\varphi(t)\leq\psi(Lt)
for some
L\geq 1
then,

\psi^{-1}(t)/L\leq\varphi^{-1}(t)\leq L\psi^{-1}(t).

We now come to the generalized setting, where explicit dependence on the space variable

is allowed.

Definition 4.12
.

L
et
(A,\Sigma,\mu)

\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}

Definition 4.13
.

Let
(A,\Sigma,\mu)

-finite measure space. A function

-

a strong generalized

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]

Properties of

\Phi
-function case.

Definition 4.15
.

Two function
\varphi,\psi\colon A\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]

, if there exists

\varphi(x,t/L)\leq\psi(x,t)\leq\varphi(x,Lt)\ \ \ \mbox{for $\mu$-a.e. $x\in A$ and for all $t\geq 0$.}

Let
\varphi,\psi\colon A\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty]
, be increasing with respect to the second variable, such that

-prefunction;

\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}

(c)

if
\varphi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
, then
\psi
satisfies
\textnormal{(aDec)}_{q}
.

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(A,\mu)

\textnormal{(aInc)}_{p}

We recall the concept of generalized Orlicz space and report a brief list of useful definitions and properties of such spaces.
Given
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
and

we define the

\rho_{\varphi}(f)

\rho_{\varphi}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|f(x)|)\,dx.

The set

L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega)\colon\rho_{\varphi}(\lambda f)<+\infty\ \ \ \mbox{for some $\lambda>0$}\},

is called a generalized Orlicz space.

Lemma 4.27
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Then

(a)

L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\rho_{\varphi}(\lambda f)=0\}
;

(b)

if
\varphi
additionally satisfies
(aDec)
we have

L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon\rho_{\varphi}(f)<+\infty\}.

Next we define the (quasi)-norm associated to generalized Orlicz spaces.

Definition 4.28
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. For
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we define

\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\rho_{\varphi}\left(\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

Most of the time we will abbreviate the notation writing
\|f\|_{\varphi}
. Now we present some properties linking the modular with the Orlicz norm.

Lemma 4.29
.

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
then
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
is a quasi-norm. If
\varphi\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
then
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
is a norm.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. If
\varphi\simeq\psi
, then
L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})=L^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and the two norms are equivalent.

Remark 4.31
.

Lemmas
4.24
,
4.25
and
4.30
entail, in particular, that it suffices to prove all our results under the stronger assumption that
\psi\in\Phi_{s}(\Omega)
satisfying
(A0)
,
(Inc)
,
(Dec)
and
(adA1)
(or (
3.6
), depending on the need) on
\Omega
. The extension to a
\psi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
is indeed immediate, upon noticing that, if we take equivalent Orlicz functions, then the generalized Orlicz space remains the same.

Lemma 4.32

(Unit ball property)
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. Given
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we have

\|f\|_{\varphi}<1\ \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \ \rho_{\varphi}(f)\leq 1\ \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \ \|f\|_{\varphi}\leq 1.

If in addition
\varphi
is left continuous then
\rho_{\varphi}(f)\leq 1\Leftrightarrow\|f\|_{\varphi}\leq 1
. Moreover, the following properties hold:

(a)

if
\|f\|_{\varphi}<1
, then
\rho_{\varphi}(f)\leq\|f\|_{\varphi}
;

(b)

if
\|f\|_{\varphi}>1
, then
\|f\|_{\varphi}\leq\rho_{\varphi}(f)
;

(c)

in any case,
\|f\|_{\varphi}\leq\rho_{\varphi}(f)+1
.

In generalized Orlicz spaces we also have a generalization of the concept of Lebesgue points.

Proposition 4.33
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfying
(A0)
and
(aDec)
. Then, for every
f\in L^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
\rho_{\varphi}(f)<+\infty
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}\varphi(x,|f(x)-f(x_{0})|)\,dx=0\ \ \ \mbox{for $\mathcal{L}^{d}$-a.e. $x_{0}\in\Omega$}.

Proof.

The proof can be carried out exactly as the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
44
]
with some minor changes, so we omit it.
∎

We present some properties of the maximal operator in Orlicz spaces (see
[
45
, Chapter 4]
or
[
50
]
). Since the proof of Proposition
7.3
only needs the estimate for
\Phi
-functions independent of
x
, we give the statement only for non generalized
\Phi
-functions.

Definition 4.34
.

Given
f\in L^{1}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we define the restricted maximal operator to
\Omega
as

Mf(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}|f(y)|\,dy.

Analogously, for a non-negative, finite Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
one can define

M\mu(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}.

Theorem 4.35
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
finite valued satisfy
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
. Then, the restricted maximal operator
M\colon L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\to L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is bounded.
In particular, we have that if
\|f\|_{\varphi}\neq 0
, setting
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{2\|f\|_{\varphi}}
, there exists
c=c(\varphi^{-1}(1),d,\gamma)
such that
\rho_{\varphi}(c\varepsilon Mf)\leq 1
.

Proof.

The proof builds upon the key estimate
[
45
, Theorem 4.3.3]
. In particular, let
c_{1}=c_{1}(d,\gamma)
be the constant such that
\|Mf\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|f\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}
. Then, the constant
c
is given by

c:=\frac{(\varphi^{-1}(1))^{3}}{16c_{1}}.

∎

Corollary 4.36
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
finite valued satisfy
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
. Assume in addition that it satisfies
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
with
1<q<\infty
. Then, there exists
C=C(\varphi^{-1}(1),d,\gamma,q)>0
such that for every
f\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})

(4.3)

\rho_{\varphi}(Mf)\leq C(\rho_{\varphi}(f)+1)^{q}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
4.7
, we can find
\psi\in\Phi_{s}
such that
\psi(t)\leq\varphi(t)\leq\psi(2t)
for every
t\geq 0
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.5
,
\psi
satisfies
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{\gamma}
and
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
. Let us prove (
4.3
) for
\psi
. If
f\equiv 0
then there is nothing to prove. Assume that
f
is not identically zero on
\Omega
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{\psi}
is a norm by Lemma
4.29
, we have that
\|f\|_{\psi}\neq 0
. Let
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{2\|f\|_{\psi}}
and
c=c(\psi^{-1}(1),d,\gamma)=c(\varphi^{-1}(1),d,\gamma)>0
be the constant from Theorem
4.35
. If
c\varepsilon\leq 1
by
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}
and Theorem
4.35
we have

1\geq\int_{\Omega}\psi(c\varepsilon Mf(x))\,dx\geq(c\varepsilon)^{q}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,Mf(x))\,dx.

Therefore, using also Lemma
4.32
(c) we get

\int_{\Omega}\psi(Mf(x))\,dx\leq c^{-q}(2\|f\|_{\psi})^{q}\leq 2^{q}c^{-q}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\psi(|f(x)|)\,dx\right)^{q},

If
c\varepsilon>1
, then it is enough to observe that since
\psi
is increasing

\int_{\Omega}\psi(Mf(x))\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\psi(c\varepsilon Mf(x))\,dx\leq 1\leq 1+\|f\|_{\psi}\leq 2+2\rho_{\psi}(f)\leq 2(1+\rho_{\psi}(f))^{q}.

Setting

we conclude (

) for

\rho_{\varphi}(Mf/2)\leq\rho_{\psi}(Mf)\leq C(\rho_{\psi}(f)+1)^{q}\leq C(\rho_{\varphi}(f)+1)^{q},

using again

as well with

∎

4.2.
(Generalized) Special functions of Bounded Variation

We now discuss the definition and some useful properties of
GSBV
and
SBV
function. For a complete treatment of the topic we refer to
[
10
]
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x\in A
such that

\limsup_{r\searrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(A\cap B(x,r))}{r^{d}}>0,

Given
u\in L^{0}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we say that
a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the approximate limit of
u
at
x
if

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega\cap B(x,r)\cap\left\{|a-v(x)|>\varepsilon\right\})}{r^{d}}=0\ \ \ \mbox{for every $\varepsilon>0$}.

In that case we write

\aplim_{y\to x}u(y)=a.

We say that
x\in A
is an approximate jump point of
u
, and we write
x\in J_{u}
, if there exists
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
a\neq b
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

\underset{\underset{(y-x)\cdot\nu>0}{y\to x}}{\textnormal{ap-}\lim}u(y)=a\ \ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \ \underset{\underset{(y-x)\cdot\nu<0}{y\to x}}{\textnormal{ap-}\lim}u(y)=b.

In particular, for every
x\in J_{u}
the triple
(a,b,\nu)
is uniquely determined up to a change of sign of
\nu
and a permutation of
a
and
b
. We indicate such triple by
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))
. The jump of
u
at
x\in J_{u}
is defined as
[u](x):=u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}
. The set
J_{u}
is countably rectifiable and has
\nu
as an approximate unit normal vector at
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-every point (see
[
57
]
).

The space
\textnormal{BV}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of functions of bounded variation is the set of
u\in L^{1}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
whose distributional gradient
Du
is a bounded Radon measure on
A
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
. Given
u\in\textnormal{BV}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we can write
Du=D^{a}u+D^{s}u
, where
D^{a}u
is absolutely continuous and
D^{s}u
is singular w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}^{d}
. The density
\nabla u\in L^{1}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
of
D^{a}u
w.r.t.
\mathcal{L}^{d}
coincides a.e. in
A
with the approximate gradient of
u
. That is, for a.e.
x\in A
it holds

\underset{y\to x}{\textnormal{ap-}\lim}\frac{u(y)-u(x)-\nabla u(x)\cdot(y-x)}{|x-y|}=0.

The space
SBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
of special functions of bounded variation is defined as
the set of all
u\in\textnormal{BV}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
|D^{s}u|(A\setminus J_{u})=0
. Moreover, we denote by
SBV_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
the space of functions belonging to
SBV(U,\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
U\Subset A
open. For
p\in[1,+\infty)
,
SBV^{p}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
stands for the set of functions
u\in SBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, with approximate gradient
\nabla u\in L^{p}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty
, that is

SBV^{p}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in SBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\}.

We say that
u\in GSBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
if
X(u)\in SBV_{\textnormal{loc}}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
for every
X\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Also for
u\in GSBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
the approximate gradient
\nabla u
exists
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in
A
and the jump set
J_{u}
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-rectifiable with approximate unit normal vector
\nu_{u}
. Finally, for
p\in[1,+\infty)
, we define as before

GSBV^{p}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\{u\in GSBV(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})\colon\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty\};

it is known that
GSBV^{p}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a vector space (see e.g.
[
33
, pg. 172]
).

Moreover, given a generalized Orlicz function
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
we denote with
SBV^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
the space of functions
u\in SBV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<+\infty
and
\nabla u\in L^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m\times d})
. The definition of
GSBV^{\varphi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is analogous.

The following three theorems can be found in
[
37
]
for the scalar case and in
[
21
]
for vector valued
SBV
functions.

Theorem 4.37

(
[
37
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
u\in SBV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that

\int_{K}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap K)<+\infty

for every compact
K\Subset\Omega
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap J_{u})\right)=0\ \ \ \mbox{for $\mathcal{H}^{d-1}$-a.e. $x_{0}\in\Omega\setminus J_{u}$.}

For the other two theorems we need first to fix some notations.
Given
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we set

a\wedge b:=(\min(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,\min(a_{m},b_{m}))\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ a\vee b:=(\max(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,\max(a_{m},b_{m})).

Let
B
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For every measurable function
u\colon B\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, given
0\leq s\leq\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)
, we define

u_{*}(s;B):=((u_{1})_{*}(s;B),\dots,(u_{m})_{*}(s;B)),

where, for every
i=1,\dots,m
,

(u_{i})_{*}(s;B):=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathcal{L}^{d}(\left\{u_{i}<t\right\})\geq s\right\}.

Moreover, we define
\displaystyle\textnormal{med}(u;B):=u_{*}\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)}{2};B\right)
.

Let
\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}
be the dimensional constant in the relative isoperimetric inequality in balls. For every
u\in GSBV(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B),

we define

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tau^{\prime}(u;B):=u_{*}\left(\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}};B\right),\\
&\tau^{\prime\prime}(u;B):=u_{*}\left(\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)-\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}};B\right)\end{split}

and the truncation operator in
B

(4.5)

T_{B}u(x):=(\tau^{\prime}(u;B)\vee u(x))\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}(u;B).

Notice that it holds

(4.6)

\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{T_{B}u\neq u\}\cap B)\leq 2\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}.

Theorem 4.38

(
[
37
, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2]
)
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a ball and
u\in SBV(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfy

(4.7)

\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B).

If
1\leq p<d
then,

(4.8)

\left(\int_{B}|T_{B}u-\textnormal{med}(u,B)|^{p^{*}}\,dx\right)^{1/p^{*}}\leq\frac{2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}p(d-1)}{d-p}\left(\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},

and

(4.9)

\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{T_{B}u\neq u\}\cap B)\leq 2\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}.

If
p\geq d
instead, for every
q\geq 1
we have

(4.10)

\|T_{B}u-\textnormal{med}(u,B)\|_{L^{q}(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq\frac{2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}q(d-1)}{d}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)^{\frac{1}{d}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})}.

Theorem 4.39

(
[
37
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

Let
u\in SBV_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
p>1
and let
x_{0}\in\Omega
. If

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\left(\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\cap J_{u})\right)=0,

then
x_{0}\neq J_{u}
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\textnormal{med}(u,B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))=\aplim_{x\to x_{0}}u(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Moreover, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for
\delta\in(3/4,1)
and
\varepsilon,\rho>0
with
\delta\varepsilon<\rho<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}(u,B_{\rho}(x_{0}))\leq\textnormal{med}(u,B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))\leq\tau^{\prime\prime}(u,B_{\rho}(x_{0})),

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}(u,B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))\leq\textnormal{med}(u,B_{\rho}(x_{0}))\leq\tau^{\prime\prime}(u,B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})).

Poincaré inequality in
SBV^{\varphi}

In this section we present a fundamental ingredient we need in the proofs of our main results and which is in our opinion of independent interest. Namely, a Poincaré inequality for functions belonging to
SBV^{\varphi}
with small jump set a là De Giorgi-Carriero-Leaci. We start with the case of
\Phi
-functions, while the extension to the generalized setting will be given in Subsection
5.3
.

Theorem 5.1

(Poincaré inequality in
SBV^{\varphi}
)
.

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}
be a finite valued Orlicz function satisfying
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{1}
and let
B_{r}(x)
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r
and centred in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Set
\phi:=\varphi^{\frac{d}{d-1}}t^{-\frac{1}{d-1}}
and let
u\in SBV^{\varphi}(B_{r}(x))
be such that

(5.1)

\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap B_{r}(x))\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{r}).

Then, there exists a dimensional constant
C=C(d)
such that the following inequality holds

(5.2)

\phi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\phi\left(\frac{|T_{B_{r}(x)}u(y)-\textnormal{med}(u,B_{r}(x))|}{r}\right)\,dy\right)\leq C\varphi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\varphi(C|\nabla u(y)|)\,dy\right),

where
T_{B_{r}(x)}u
is defined in (
4.5
).

Notice that since we have a truncation of a scalar
SBV
function, the result above applies also to real valued
GSBV
functions. Also observe that if
\varphi
has linear growth, then the inequality in (
5.2
) reduces to the standard homogeneous Poincaré inequality with
p=1
for
SBV
functions.

Remark 5.2
.

By translation invariance and rescaling, it is enough to verify inequality (
5.2
) only on
B_{1}
. Indeed, let
u\colon B_{r}(x)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, define
v\colon B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
as
v(y):=u(x+ry)/r
. We have
u_{*}(s;B_{r})=rv_{*}(s/r^{d};B_{1})
for every
s\in[0,\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{r})]
. Thus,

\tau^{\prime}(v,B_{1})=\frac{\tau^{\prime}(u,B_{r})}{r},\ \ \ \tau^{\prime\prime}(v,B_{1})=\frac{\tau^{\prime\prime}(u,B_{r})}{r},\ \ \ \textnormal{med}(v,B_{1})=\frac{\textnormal{med}(u,B_{r})}{r},

and
T_{B_{1}}v(y):=T_{B_{r}(x)}u(x+ry)/r
for every
y\in B_{1}
.

Then, if the Poincaré inequality (
5.2
) holds on
B_{1}
for
v
, we deduce

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\phi\left(\frac{|T_{B_{r}(x)}u(y)-\textnormal{med}(u,B_{r}(x))|}{r}\right)\,dy\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\phi\left(|T_{B_{1}}v(z)-\textnormal{med}(v,B_{1})|\right)\,dz\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varphi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{1}}\varphi(C|\nabla v(z)|)\,dz\right)

\displaystyle=C\varphi^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\varphi(C|\nabla u(y)|)\,dx\right).

The proof of Theorem
5.1
will be given in Subsection
5.2
, after recalling and proving some preliminary material.

5.1.
Rearrangements of
BV
functions

We are going to make use of the theory of rearrangements for
BV
functions. Since they are used only in this section, we report here their main definitions and properties. For a complete treatment of the topic see
[
26
]
. We say that two functions
u
and
v
are equi-measurable if
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u>t\})=\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{v>t\})
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

For simplicity, let us denote by
B
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)=\omega_{d}
. Let
u\colon B\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. We define the distribution function
\nu_{u}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\omega_{d}]
of
u
as

(5.3)

\nu_{u}(t):=\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{x\in B\colon u(x)>t\})\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

We have that
\nu_{u}
is right-continuous and non-increasing. By definition,
\nu_{u}(\esssup u)=0
. Moreover,
\nu_{u}(t-)=\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u\geq t\})
and
\nu_{u}(t-)-\nu_{u}(t)=\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u=t\})
. Thus,
\nu_{u}
is continuous in
t
if and only if
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u=t\})=0
. We can now define also the signed decreasing rearrangement of
u
as a function
u^{0}\colon[0,\omega_{d}]\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
given by

(5.4)

u^{0}(s):=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon\nu_{u}(t)>s\}\ \ \ s\in[0,\omega_{d}].

We have that
u^{0}
is right-continuous, non-increasing and it is equi-measurable with
u
. By definition,
u^{0}(0)=\esssup u
. Moreover, notice that

\{s\in[0,\omega_{d}]\colon u^{0}(s)>t\}=[0,\nu_{u}(t))\ \ \ \mbox{for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Therefore,
u^{0}
and
u
are equi-distributed, that is
\nu_{u}=\nu_{u^{0}}
. We also have

(5.5)

u^{0}(\nu_{u}(t))\leq t\ \ \mbox{for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ t\leq u^{0}(\nu_{u}(t)-)\ \ \mbox{for every $t\in(-\infty,\esssup u)$}

and

(5.6)

\nu_{u}(u^{0}(s))\leq s\ \ \mbox{for every $s\in[0,\omega_{d}]$}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ s\leq\nu_{u}(u^{0}(s)-)\ \ \mbox{for every $s\in[0,\omega_{d})$}.

To summarize,
u^{0}
is a monotone real valued function on
(0,\omega_{d})
and thus
u^{0}\in BV_{\textnormal{loc}}((0,\omega_{d}))
. Moreover,
u^{0}
is constant in the interval
(s_{1},s_{2})
if and only if there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
where
\nu_{u}
jumps and
(s_{1},s_{2})\subset(\nu_{u}(t),\nu_{u}(t-))
. Vice versa,
u^{0}
jumps at some point
s_{0}\in(0,\omega_{d})
and
(t_{1},t_{2})\subset(u^{0}(s_{0}),u^{0}(s_{0}-))
if and only if
\nu_{u}
is constant on
(t_{1},t_{2})
. Therefore, a countable family of left-open intervals
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}]
exists such that

(5.7)

(\essinf u,\esssup u)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}]\subseteq u^{0}((0,\omega_{d}))\subseteq[\essinf u,\esssup u]\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}].

Finally, if
u
is a non-negative function on
B
, we indicate the distribution function of
u
as

(5.8)

\mu_{u}(t):=\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{x\in B\colon u(x)>t\})\,,\ \ t\in[0,+\infty),

and the decreasing rearrangement as
u^{*}\colon[0,\omega_{d}]\to[0,+\infty]
is given by

(5.9)

u^{*}(s):=\sup\{t\in[0,+\infty)\colon\mu_{u}(t)>s\}\,,\ \ s\in[0,\omega_{d}].

We start by proving the following fact.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\psi\in\Phi_{s}
be a finite valued Orlicz function satisfying
\textnormal{(Inc)}_{1}
. Then, for every
u\in SBV(B)
such that (
5.1
) holds, we have that
(T_{B}u)^{0}
is absolutely continuous in
(0,\omega_{d})
and

(5.10)

\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}\psi\left(c(d)\min\{s,1-s\}^{\frac{d-1}{d}}\left(-\frac{d(T_{B}u)^{0}}{ds}(s)\right)\right)\,ds\leq\int_{B}\psi\left(2|\nabla u(x)|\right)\,dx,

where
c(d)
is a positive constant depending only on the dimension.

In order to prove Proposition
5.3
, we need the following two results, the first one is
[
26
, Equation (3.21)]
and the second is contained in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u\in BV(B)
and set
C_{u}\subseteq B
the set of points in
B
where
u
is approximately continuous. We have that

\partial^{M}\{u>t\}\cap C_{u}=\emptyset\ \ \mbox{for a.e. $t\in(\essinf u,\esssup u)\setminus u^{0}((0,\omega_{d}))$}.

Proof.

The proof can be carried out exactly as the proof of
[
26
, Equation (3.21)]
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(0,\omega_{d})
such that
f(x)\geq 0
and
g(x)\geq 0
for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in(0,\omega_{d})
and let
F\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a convex function with
F(0)=0
. Assume also that

\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}g^{*}(s)\,ds\ \ \ \mbox{for every $t\in[0,\omega_{d}]$,}\ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}F(g^{*}(s))\,ds<+\infty.

Then,

(5.11)

\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}F(f(x))\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}F(g(x))\,dx.

Proof.

The result is essentially proved in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
, which actually requires
F
to be also Lipschitz. However, an inspection of the proof reveals that we need
F
to be Lipschitz only on the set
\{f^{*}(s)\colon s\in[0,\omega_{d}]\}
. Since
F
is convex and thus locally Lipschitz, fixing
\eta>0
arbitrarily small, we can apply
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
to
f\wedge f^{*}(\eta)
and
g
getting

\int_{\eta}^{\omega_{d}}F(f^{*}(s))\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}F(g^{*}(s))\,ds.

Letting
\eta\to 0^{+}
, by monotone convergence theorem and using the fact that
F
is strictly increasing, we deduce

\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}(F(f))^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{\omega_{d}}(F(g))^{*}(s)\,ds.

Finally, using the equi-measurability of rearrangements we conclude (
5.11
).
∎

We are now in a position to prove Proposition
5.3
.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

In order to ease the notation we set
(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}))^{\frac{d}{d-1}}=:\Lambda
. We split the proof into three steps.

Step 1:

u^{0}
is continuous in
[\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda]
. We actually prove that
u^{0}
is continuous in

(5.12)

\left(\left(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}},\omega_{d}-\left(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\right).

Assume this is false. Then, we can find
s
belonging to the interval in (
5.12
) such that
u^{0}
jumps in
s
and thus
(u^{0}(s),u^{0}(s-))\neq\emptyset
. Hence, owning Lemma
5.4
and the fact that
u\in SBV(B)
, we can find
t\in(u^{0}(s),u^{0}(s-))
such that
\partial^{M}\{u>t\}\cap C_{u}=\emptyset
and the set
\{u>t\}
has finite perimeter. This implies
\partial^{*}\{u>t\}\subset J_{u}
. Moreover, recall that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{M}\{u>t\}\setminus\partial^{*}\{u>t\})=0
. By definition of
u^{0}
we have that
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u>t\})=s
. Thus, both
\{u>t\}
and
B\setminus\{u>t\}
have measure strictly greater than
(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}))^{\frac{d}{d-1}}
. Recalling the isoperimetric inequality this gives

\left(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}\{u>t\})\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}=\left(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}P(\{u>t\},B)\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}>\left(\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}.

This means that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})<\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}\{u>t\})
, a contradiction. Hence,
u^{0}
is continuous in the interval in (
5.12
) and thus in
[\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda]
. Since
u^{0}
admits a continuous representative in
[\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda]
, let us define

\tau^{\prime}:=u^{0}\left(\omega_{d}-\Lambda\right)\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \tau^{\prime\prime}:=u^{0}\left(\Lambda\right).

Notice that
\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}
by definition of
u^{0}
and
\Lambda
coincide with
\tau^{\prime}(u,B)
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}(u,B)
, respectively, defined in (
4.4
).

Step 2:

u^{0}
is absolutely continuous in
(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)
. We shall use some arguments from
[
24
, Theorem 6.5 and Lemma 6.6]
. Assume
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})>0
, otherwise the absolute continuity of
u^{0}
follows by
[
24
, Lemma 6.6]
. Let

h_{B}(s):=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}}\min\{s,\omega_{d}-s\}^{\frac{d-1}{d}}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
|\nabla u|\in L^{1}(B)
, by the standard theory of rearrangements we have
|\nabla u|^{*}\in L^{1}((0,\omega_{d}))
. Thus, we can find
\delta>0
such that

(5.13)

\int_{0}^{\delta}|\nabla u|^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in u^{0}((\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda))
with
\alpha\leq\beta
. We have that
\alpha,\beta\in(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})
by Step 1.
Set
u^{\beta}_{\alpha}\in SBV(B)
as
u^{\beta}_{\alpha}:=\alpha\vee u\wedge\beta
. Using the coarea formula we deduce

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\!P(\{u>t\},B)\,dt=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!P(\{u_{\alpha}^{\beta}>t\},B)\,dt=|Du^{\beta}_{\alpha}|(B)\leq\int_{B}|\nabla u^{\alpha}_{\beta}(x)|\,dx+(\beta-\alpha)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}).

On the other hand, by definition of
h_{B}
and the isoperimetric inequality in
B
we infer

(5.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}P(\{u>t\},B)dt\geq\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt.

Since
t\in(\alpha,\beta)\subset(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})

5.6
) we have that

\lim_{t\to u^{0}(\Lambda)^{-}}\nu_{u}(t)=\nu_{u}(u^{0}(\Lambda)-)\geq\Lambda\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{t\to u^{0}(\omega_{d}-\Lambda)^{+}}\nu_{u}(t)=\nu_{u}(u^{0}(\omega_{d}-\Lambda))\leq\omega_{d}-\Lambda.

This gives that
\nu_{u}(t)\in(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)

h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\geq h_{B}(\Lambda)=h_{B}\left(\left(2\gamma_{\textnormal{iso}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})\right)^{\frac{d}{d-1}}\right)=2\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}).

Combining (
5.14
)–(
5.16
) gives

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt\leq\int_{B}|\nabla u^{\beta}_{\alpha}(x)|\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt,

and finally,

(5.17)

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt\leq 2\int_{\{\alpha\leq u\leq\beta\}}|\nabla u(x)|\,dx.

Consider now a finite family of disjoint open intervals contained in
(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)
, namely
(a_{i},b_{i})
for

, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}(b_{i}-a_{i})\leq\delta
where
\delta>0
is as in (
5.13
).
Now consider inequality (
5.17
) with
\alpha:=u^{0}(b_{i})\leq u^{0}(a_{i})=:\beta
for every

. If we add all these inequalities we get

(5.18)

\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{N}(u^{0}(b_{i}),u^{0}(a_{i}))}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt\leq 2\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{N}\{u^{0}(b_{i})<u<u^{0}(a_{i})\}}|\nabla u(x)|\,dx,

where we have also used the fact that
\int_{\{u=t\}}|\nabla u|\,dx=0
for every

(see
[
10

).
Recalling (
5.6
), for every

it holds

(5.19)

\lim_{t\to u^{0}(a_{i})^{-}}\nu_{u}(t)=\nu_{u}(u^{0}(a_{i})-)\geq a_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \lim_{t\to u^{0}(b_{i})^{+}}\nu_{u}(t)=\nu_{u}(u^{0}(b_{i}))\leq b_{i}.

Hence, by definition of
\nu_{u}
we have that
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{u^{0}(b_{i})<u<u^{0}(a_{i})\})=\nu_{u}(u^{0}(b_{i}))-\nu_{u}(u^{0}(a_{i})-)\leq b_{i}-a_{i}
for every

.
For every non-negative measurable function
f
and every Borel set

\int_{E}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mathcal{L}^{d}(E)}f^{*}(t)\,dt.

Combining this result with inequality (

) gives

(5.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{N}(u^{0}(b_{i}),u^{0}(a_{i}))}h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\,dt&\leq 2\int_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\nu_{u}(u^{0}(b_{i}))-\nu_{u}(u^{0}(a_{i}))]}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(b_{i}-a_{i})}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt\\
&\leq 2\int_{0}^{\delta}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt\leq 2\varepsilon.\end{split}

Again by (

\nu_{u}(t)\in(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)
for every
t\in\cup_{i=1}^{N}(a_{i},b_{i})
. Therefore, arguing as before,

h_{B}(\nu_{u}(t))\geq 2\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})

for every

,

. Thus,

2\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u})\sum_{i=1}^{N}(u^{0}(a_{i})-u^{0}(b_{i}))\leq 2\varepsilon,

and this gives the absolute continuity of

on
(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)
since the family of sub-intervals and

where arbitrary.

By the previous steps we have that

is uniformly continuous and absolutely continuous in
[\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda]
and
(\Lambda,\omega_{d}-\Lambda)
, respectively. Hence, by definition of
\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}
, the function
\tau^{\prime}\vee u^{0}\wedge\tau^{\prime\prime}
is absolutely continuous in
(0,\omega_{d})
. Observe that by definition of

and

(see (

)), we have that

h_{B}
be as in Step 2, and let us set

. Recalling inequalities (

) and (
5.20

is constant in

\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
in
(0,\omega_{d})
, it holds

\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{N}(v^{0}(b_{i}),v^{0}(a_{i}))}h_{B}(\nu_{v}(t))\,dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(b_{i}-a_{i})}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt.

Moreover, since

is absolutely continuous on
(0,\omega_{d})
, by a change of variables we get

\int_{\cup_{i=1}^{N}(a_{i},b_{i})}-\frac{dv^{0}}{dr}(r)h_{B}(r)\,dr\leq 2\int_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(b_{i}-a_{i})}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt,

Observe that by approximation the inequality holds even if the union is countable.

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}((0,\omega_{d})),\ \mathcal{L}^{1}(A)=\ell\ }\int_{A}-\frac{dv^{0}}{dr}(r)h_{B}(r)\,dr\leq 2\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt.

In particular, taking

we deduce that

. Thus, since every Borel set

\sup_{E\in\mathcal{B}((0,\omega_{d})),\ \mathcal{L}^{1}(E)=\ell\ }\int_{E}-\frac{dv^{0}}{dr}(r)h_{B}(r)\,dr\leq 2\int_{0}^{\ell}|\nabla u|^{*}\,dt.

\int_{0}^{\ell}\left(-\frac{dv^{0}}{dr}(r)h_{B}(r)\right)^{*}\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}|2\nabla u|^{*}\,dt.

) holds for every

, we can apply Lemma

to the functions

where we have also used that
\psi

, we get (
5.10
) with

. This concludes the proof.

Proof of Theorem

We begin by recalling that since

The next lemma concerns the approximation of

Lemma 7.2

(Lusin approximation in

)
.

For every

∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\psi\in\Phi_{s}(B_{1})

(7.4)

\textnormal{(Inc)}_{\gamma}
and
\textnormal{(Dec)}_{q}

Assuming the liminf in (

Hence,

.

We first use the truncation argument in Lemma
7.1
applied to
u_{k}
with
\eta>0
small and
\theta>1
large enough, in order to find
\{v_{k}\}_{k}\subset SBV^{\psi_{k}}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{m})

From now on

\psi
. Taking into account (

) and (

\sup_{k\geq 1}\int_{B_{1}}\psi_{k}(x,|\nabla v_{k}(x)|)\,dx<+\infty

. Moreover, by (ii) of Lemma

v_{k}\to u
in measure on

.

(7.7)

\int_{B_{1}}{\psi}^{-}_{k}\left(M(w)(x)\right)\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}}{\psi}_{k}^{-}\left(|w(x)|\right)\,dx+1\right)^{q}.

Thus, by (
7.6

7.7
) we get that the sequence

is bounded in

. Hence, by Chacon biting lemma, we can find a sequence of sets

as

\{{\psi}^{-}_{k}(M(|\nabla v_{k}|))\chi_{B_{1}\setminus E_{h}}\}_{k}

Due to equiintegrability, we deduce that
\Lambda_{h}(s)\to 0
as
s\searrow 0
for every
h\geq 1
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Thanks to Lemma
7.2
applied to
v_{k}
, we find functions
u_{k}^{\lambda}:B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathcal{L}^{d}

(7.8)

\textnormal{Lip}(u_{k}^{\lambda})\leq\tau\lambda\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ u_{k}^{\lambda}=v_{k}\mbox{ in $B_{1}\setminus E_{k}^{\lambda}$},

where
\tau=\tau(d,m)

Using (

\lambda>0

(7.9)

\mathcal{L}^{d}(E_{k}^{\lambda}\setminus A)\leq\frac{\tau}{\lambda}2\mu\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{v_{k}})+\frac{1}{{\psi}_{k}^{-}(\lambda)}\int_{\{M(|\nabla v_{k}|)>\lambda\}\setminus A}{\psi}^{-}_{k}(M(|\nabla v_{k}|))\,dx.

Observe that for every

For every fixed

for a fixed

as

.

We are finally in a position to conclude. Since

as

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}}f_{k}(x,\nabla v_{k})\,dx\geq\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{1}\setminus E_{h})f(\nabla u).

We now fix
\zeta>\tau\lambda>\sigma

for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}f^{\zeta}_{k}(x,\nabla v_{k})\,dx&\geq\int_{B_{1}\setminus(E_{h}\cup E_{k}^{\lambda})}f_{k}^{\zeta}(x,\nabla v_{k})\,dx\\
&=\int_{B_{1}\setminus(E_{h}\cup E_{k}^{\lambda})}f_{k}^{\zeta}(x,\nabla u_{k}^{\lambda})\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{B_{1}\setminus E_{h}}f_{k}^{\zeta}(x,\nabla u_{k}^{\lambda})\,dx-\int_{E_{k}^{\lambda}\setminus E_{h}}2\mathfrak{C}b(1+{\psi}_{k}^{-}(\lambda))\,dx.\end{split}

Thus, for every

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Borel measurable function satisfying (
f1
)–(
f2
) with
a,b>0
and such that

\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}

\psi\in\Phi_{s}(\Omega)

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{A}f(x,\nabla u_{k})\,dx\geq\int_{A}f(x,\nabla u)\,dx

The proof is standard and we only sketch it. Possibly extracting a subsequence we can assume that
u_{k}
converges to

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. in

\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{\lambda(B_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}))}{\varepsilon^{d}}\geq\omega_{d}f(x_{0},\nabla u(x_{0})).

3.6
) holds and let

8.
Relaxation

Using the integral representation and the lower semicontinuity result for quasiconvex and BV-elliptic integrands, we can now prove the relaxation Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Using Theorem
3.3
and taking the infimum over all the sequences, for every function
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
we get

(8.1)

\int_{A}\mathcal{Q}f(x,\nabla u(x))\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap A}\mathcal{R}g(x,[u](x),\nu_{u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\leq\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u,A).

We now want to show that
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies (
H1
)–(
H5
). By definition of
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
we deduce immediately that it satisfies (
H2
), (
H3
) and (
H5
). The upper bound of (
H4
) is obvious since we have by definition
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u,A)\leq\mathcal{G}(u,A)
for every
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, the lower bound of (
H4
) for
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
can be deduced observing that by Ioffe’s Theorem the functional

u\mapsto\int_{A}\psi(x,|\nabla u(x)|)\,dx+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J_{u}\cap A)

is lower semicontinuous with respect to the convergence in measure on
A
. Finally, the proof of (
H1
) for
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
is standard and builds upon the fundamental estimate (see e.g.
[
17
]
proof of Theorem 10), we omit it.

Hence, by Corollary
3.2
there exist two Borel functions
\overline{f}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
and
\overline{g}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
such that, for every
u\in GSBV^{\psi}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(u,A)=\int_{A}\overline{f}(x,\nabla u(x))\,dx+\int_{J_{u}\cap A}\overline{g}(x,[u](x),\nu_{u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

We now proceed as in
[
17
, Proof of Theorem 4]
and show that

(8.2)

\overline{f}(x,\xi)\leq\mathcal{Q}f(x,\xi)\ \ \ \mbox{for $\mathcal{L}^{d}$ -a.e. $x\in\Omega$ and every $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$}.

Let
\eta>0
, since
f
is a Carathéodory function, by the Scorza-Dragoni theorem there exists a compact set
K\subset\Omega
with
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega\setminus K)\leq\eta
, such that the function

is continuous. Let
K^{1}
be the set of points with density one for

. Fix
(x,\xi)\in K^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that

(8.3)

\mathcal{Q}f(x,\xi)+\eta\geq\int_{B}f(x,\xi+\nabla\varphi(y))\,dy.

For any
\varepsilon>0
such that
B_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\Omega
, set
v_{\varepsilon}(y):=\varepsilon\varphi((y-x)/\varepsilon)
. Then,
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(B_{\varepsilon}(x),\mathbb{R}^{m})
and, by definition of
\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\overline{f}
(see (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)),

(8.4)

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x,\xi)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{G}}(\xi(\cdot-x)+v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}(x))}{\varepsilon^{d}}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}f(y,\xi+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\,dy.

Using the modulus of uniform continuity for
f
we get that for
\varepsilon
small enough

(8.5)

|f(x,\xi+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))-f(y,\xi+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))|\leq\eta

for every
y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Thus, by (
8.3
), (
8.4
), (
8.5
) and recalling properties
(A0)
and
(Dec)
of
\psi
together with (
f2
),

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap K}\!f(y,\xi+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\,dy+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus K}\!b(1+\psi(x,|\xi|+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}))\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap K}f(x,\xi+\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\,dy+\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}f(x,\xi+\nabla\varphi(y))\,dy+\eta\leq\mathcal{Q}f(x,\xi)+2\eta,

where we have used also that
x\in K^{1}
. Letting
\eta\to 0
, gives (
8.2
).

We now deal the surface part, showing that, for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
and every
(\zeta,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

(8.6)

\overline{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq\mathcal{R}g(x,\zeta,\nu).

Take
\eta>0
and fix
(x,\zeta,\nu)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Since
g
satisfies (
g1
)–(
g4
), we can find a function
w\in SBV^{\psi}(Q_{\nu},\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that
\nabla w=0
a.e. in
B
,
w=u_{x,\zeta,0,\nu}
in a neighborhood of
\partial B
and

(8.7)

\mathcal{R}g(x,\zeta,\nu)+\eta\geq\int_{B}g(x,[w](y),\nu_{w}(y))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

We recall that

u_{x,\zeta,0,\nu}(y)=\begin{cases}\zeta&if $(y-x)\cdot\nu>0$,\\
0&if $(y-x)\cdot\nu\leq 0$.\end{cases}

For any
\varepsilon>0
such that
B_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\Omega
, set
v_{\varepsilon}(y):=w((y-x)/\varepsilon)
. Then, by definition of
\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\overline{g}
(see (
3.1
) and (
3.3
)),

(8.8)

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{G}}(v_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}(x))}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap J_{v_{\varepsilon}}}g(y,[v_{\varepsilon}](y),\nu_{v_{\varepsilon}}(y))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

Using the modulus of uniform continuity of
g
we get that for
\varepsilon
small enough, for every
y\in B_{\varepsilon}(x)
it holds

(8.9)

|g(x,[v_{\varepsilon}](y),\nu_{v_{\varepsilon}}(y))-g(y,[v_{\varepsilon}](y),\nu_{v_{\varepsilon}}(y))|\leq\eta.

Combining (
8.7
), (
8.8
) and (
8.9
) we get

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap J_{v_{\varepsilon}}}g(y,[v_{\varepsilon}](y),\nu_{v_{\varepsilon}}(y))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap J_{v_{\varepsilon}}}g(x,[v_{\varepsilon}](y),\nu_{v_{\varepsilon}}(y))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)+\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{B\cap J_{w}}g(x,[w](y),\nu_{w}(y))\,d\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)+\eta\leq\mathcal{R}g(x,\zeta,\nu)+2\eta.

Letting
\eta\to 0
gives (
8.6
).
∎

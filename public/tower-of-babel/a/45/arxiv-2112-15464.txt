An explicit formula of powers of the 2 × 2 2\times 2 quantum matrices and its applications
Genki Shibukawa

Abstract
We present an explicit formula of the powers for the 2 × 2 2\times 2 quantum matrices, that is a natural quantum analogue of the powers of the usual 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrices. As applications, we give some non-commutative relations of the entries of the powers for the 2 × 2 2\times 2 quantum matrices, which is a simple proof of the results of Vokos-Zumino-Wess (1990).

Introduction

Let
A
be a
2\times 2
matrix over a fixed base field
k
and
a,b,c,d\in k
are entries of
A
:

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

For any positive number
n
, the following explicit formula of
A^{n}
holds:

\displaystyle A^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}af_{n-1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\,{A}f_{n-2}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)&bf_{n-1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)\\
cf_{n-1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)&df_{n-1}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\,{A}f_{n-2}\left(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\,{A}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(1.1)

Here
f_{n}(x)
is the polynomial of degree
n
defined by

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(x,y):=\sum_{l=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{l}\binom{n-l}{l}x^{n-2l}y^{l},\quad f_{-1}(x):=0,

(1.2)

which is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind:

U_{n}(x):=\sum_{l=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{l}\binom{n-l}{l}x^{n-2l}.

In fact this formula is well-known in linear algebra, and its proof is easy by induction and the recurrence relation for
f_{n}(x)
:

\displaystyle f_{n+1}(x)=xf_{n}(x)-yf_{n-1}(x).

(1.3)

In this note, we give a quantum analogue of the formula (
1.1
) and its applications.
First, we review some fundamental objects and facts on quantum matrix or groups
[

We call

\displaystyle ab=qba,\quad ac=qca,\quad ad-da=(q-q^{-1})bc,

\displaystyle bc=cb,\quad bd=qdb,\quad cd=qdc,

)

Here

A quantum analogue of the coordinate ring
\mathcal{A}_{q}(Mat(2))

The quantum adjoint matrix of any quantum matrix

\displaystyle\hat{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}&\hat{b}\\
\hat{c}&\hat{d}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}d&-q^{-1}b\\
-qc&a\end{pmatrix}.

(1.4)

By the definition of
\hat{A}
and the relations (

R
q

), the quantum adjoint matrix
\hat{A}
satisfies the relations
(R_{q^{-1}})

\displaystyle\hat{a}\hat{b}=q^{-1}\hat{b}\hat{a},\quad\hat{a}\hat{c}=q^{-1}\hat{c}\hat{a},\quad\hat{a}\hat{d}-\hat{d}\hat{a}=(q^{-1}-q)\hat{b}\hat{c},

\displaystyle\hat{b}\hat{c}=\hat{c}\hat{b},\quad\hat{b}\hat{d}=q^{-1}\hat{d}\hat{b},\quad\hat{c}\hat{d}=q^{-1}\hat{d}\hat{c}.

Hence, the relations (

\displaystyle bc=cb,\quad A\hat{A}=\hat{A}A=\begin{pmatrix}\delta&0\\

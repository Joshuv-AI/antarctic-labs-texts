The canonical trace of Cohen-Macaulay algebras of codimension 2
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. In the present paper, we investigate a conjecture of Jürgen Herzog. Let S S be a local regular ring with residue field K K or a positively graded K K -algebra, I ⊂ S I\subset S be a perfect ideal of grade two, and let R = S / I R=S/I with canonical module ω R \omega_{R} . Herzog conjectured that the canonical trace tr ⁡ ( ω R ) \operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R}) is obtained by specialization from the generic case of maximal minors. We prove this conjecture in several cases, and present a criterion that guarantees that the canonical trace specializes under some additional assumptions. As the final conclusion of all of our results, we classify the nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals of height two.

Introduction

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},K)
be either a local ring or a positively graded
K
-algebra. Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be the (graded) maximal ideal of
R
. Assume that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and admits a canonical module
\omega_{R}
. The
canonical trace
of
R
is then defined as

\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\ =\ \sum_{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega_{R},R)}\varphi(\omega_{R}).

It turns out that
R
is a Gorenstein ring if and only if
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=R
. Thus, the canonical trace measures, in a certain sense, how far is
R
from being Gorenstein.

The ring
R
is called
nearly Gorenstein
if
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})
contains
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Nearly Gorenstein rings were introduced Herzog, Hibi and Stamate in
[
19
]
, although they were implicitly considered in the work of Huneke and Vraciu
[
21
]
. Many people are interested to develop their theory, classify nearly Gorenstein rings, and compare the nearly Gorenstein property with other properties of
R
. See
[
8
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
.

Let
{\bf x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a regular sequence on
R
, and denote by “
\overline{\phantom{l.}}
” the reduction modulo
{\bf x}
. It is well-known that the canonical module specializes, that is
\overline{\omega_{R}}=\omega_{\overline{R}}
. One would guess that the canonical trace specializes as well:
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{\overline{R}})=\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\overline{R}
? It is easy to see that
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\overline{R}\subseteq\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{\overline{R}})
. However, in
[
12
, Example 3.3]
we discovered a counterexample to this expectation. Let
R=S/I
where
S=K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]
,
I=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{2},x_{3}y_{3},x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}y_{3})
and let
{\bf x}=x_{1}-y_{1}
. Then
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{\overline{R}})\neq\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\overline{R}
. This example is remarkable, because
I
is the edge ideal of a Cohen-Macaulay very well-covered graph, see
[
6
,
7
]
.

Now, let
S
be a Noetherian local ring or a positively graded
K
-algebra, and let
I\subset S
be an ideal, which we assume to be homogeneous if
S
is graded. We furthermore assume that
S
is regular. We will explain this assumption in a moment.

We set
R=S/I
, and as before we assume that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with canonical module
\omega_{R}
. The simplest Cohen-Macaulay algebras of this form are those such that
I
is a perfect ideal of grade two. Recall that

is

if
\textup{grade}\,R=\operatorname{pd}R
. These algebras are characterized by the celebrated

[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.4.17]
,
[
9
, Theorem 20.15]
)
With the notation introduced, assume that

has a free resolution

{\mathbb{F}}\ \ :\ \ 0\rightarrow S^{m-1}\xrightarrow{\varphi}S^{m}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0.

Then, there exists a
S

. If

is projective, then

, otherwise
\operatorname{pd}I=1

is perfect of grade two.

Conversely, if

is a non-zero divisor on

,
\operatorname{grade}I_{m-1}(\varphi)\geq 2

sends the

th basis element to

times the minor obtained from

by leaving out the

th row, then
{\mathbb{F}}
is the minimal free resolution of
I=aI_{m-1}(\varphi)
.

Fixing bases of

and

, we may represent

by a matrix

is called an

of

. Furthermore, we may compute

0\rightarrow C\xrightarrow{\alpha}R^{m-1}\xrightarrow{\psi}R^{m}\rightarrow I/I^{2}\rightarrow 0,

where

, and

Since, as we assumed before,

[

to obtain that

.

Let now
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a graded polynomial ring over

. Let

be the minimal number of generators of

of a generic matrix

is an Hilbert-Burch matrix of

, then

where
{\bf g}=x_{11}-f_{11},\ldots,x_{m,m-1}-f_{m,m-1}
is a regular sequence on

. By
[

, we have
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{K[X]/I_{m-1}(X)})=I_{m-2}(X)/I_{m-1}(X)

I_{m-2}(\psi)=I_{m-2}(A)R\ \subseteq\ \operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=I_{1}(\alpha).

These facts, and several experimental evidences, lead to the following conjecture which Jürgen Herzog posed to the author of this paper in private conversations.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(Herzog)
Let
S
be either a regular local ring with residue class field
K
or a regular positively graded
K
-algebra. Let
I
be a perfect ideal of grade two and let

be an Hilbert-Burch matrix of

\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=I_{\mu(I)-2}(A)R.

In this paper we address this conjecture, and we settle it in important cases. Along the way we characterize perfect monomial ideals of grade two which are generically Gorenstein. More generally, we show that Conjecture
1.2
holds if we assume in addition that
I
is generically Gorenstein. Furthermore, we prove that Conjecture
1.2
holds for any Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal
I
of
S=K[x,y]
, and again we classify the nearly Gorenstein algebras
S/I
in this class. Finally, we classify the nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals of height two of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Before breaking out the content of the paper, let us stress the relevance of the Hilbert-Burch Theorem
1.1
across the fifty-five years of Jürgen Herzog research. Herzog first paper ever
[
17
]
, titled
Generators and relations of abelian semigroups and semigroup rings
, contains among other fundamental and basic results on semigroup rings, an explicit description of the minimal free resolution of the semigroup ring of any numerical semigroup with three generators. As Herzog remarked many times in his lifetime, this is just a special case of the Hilbert-Burch theorem, which, however, he did not know at the time as a student.

The Hilbert-Burch theorem gained again Herzog interest in the last few months of his life, as a motivation for Conjecture
1.2
, but also in the study of the so-called
Fitting ideals

\textup{Fitt}_{j}(I)
of an ideal
I
. This led to a joint paper
[
10
]
with Eisenbud, Herzog, Moradi and the author of this paper. Such article, the third to last paper of Herzog, deals with the problem of characterizing those ideals
I
of a Noetherian ring such that
I=\textup{Fitt}_{j}(I)
for some
j
. In the case
j=1
, a certain converse of the Hilbert-Burch Theorem
1.1
was proved.

The outlines of the paper are as follows. In Section
2
we present a wide class of perfect ideals of grade two for which Conjecture
1.2
holds true. Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring, and let
I\subset S
be a perfect graded ideal of grade two. It is proved in Theorem

that, if in addition
I
is generically Gorenstein, then the canonical trace is the expected one, namely it is generated by the submaximal minors
I_{\mu(I)-2}(X)
, modulo

, of any Hilbert-Burch matrix

of
I
. This result was already proved in
[
12
, Corollary 3.5]
by a different method. Our new proof is based on Lemma

and some results of Avramov and Herzog
[
3
]
.

On the other hand, the inclusion
I_{\mu(I)-2}(X)R\subseteq\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})
holds true always as we show in Lemma
2.3
. Next, we characterize the perfect monomial ideals of height two which are generically Gorenstein. It turns out that these ideals are parameterized by finite simple graphs

with
t

{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
of length

such that a certain graph

, constructed from such data, is cochordal. This is the content of Theorem

\Rightarrow

[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
.

In Section
3
we consider, among monomial ideals, the simplest class of perfect monomial ideals of grade two, which are not necessarily generically Gorenstein. Namely, the Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideals in

. For this class of ideals, we obtain in Lemma

that Conjecture

holds for this class of ideals. Hence, in Proposition

As the final conclusion of all of our results, in Theorem

we classify the nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals of height two.

2.
Perfect generically Gorenstein ideals of height two

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a graded polynomial ring over a field
K
with
\deg x_{i}=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
S
is Cohen-Macaulay, by
[

for any ideal

\operatorname{grade}I=\textup{height}(I)
. Thus

is perfect if and only if

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Recall that
I\subset S
is called

if

is Gorenstein for any minimal prime
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)

is called
generically a complete intersection
if
S_{P}/I_{P}
is a complete intersection for any minimal prime
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Recall that given an ideal
J
of a Noetherian local (or graded) ring

we have

, and if equality holds

is called a

. If

is Cohen-Macaulay, then

is a complete intersection if and only if

is generated by a regular sequence.

The goal of this section is to prove

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded perfect ideal of height two with
\mu(I)=m

be an Hilbert-Burch matrix of

, and assume that

is generically Gorenstein. Then

\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=I_{m-2}(X)/I.

This result was already shown in
[
12
, Corollary 3.5]
with a different method. Here, we provide a different and somewhat easier argument.

We shall need the following

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a Cohen-Macaulay ideal of height two. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

I
is generically Gorenstein.

(b)

I
is generically a complete intersection.

Proof.

That (b) implies (a) is clear. Conversely, assume (a) and let
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
be a minimal associated prime. Then
I_{P}
is again a Cohen-Macaulay ideal of height two, and by assumption

is Gorenstein. Thus

and the Cohen-Macaulay type of

is one. By the Hilbert-Burch Theorem

is generated by two elements. Hence

is a complete intersection.

Next, we provide a structure theorem for perfect monomial ideals of height two which are generically Gorenstein. To state this result, we need some preparation.

, Proposition 5.1(a)-(b)]
implies that
a_{m}=b_{1}=0
. Conversely, if
a_{m}=b_{1}=0
, then
x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{m}}\in I
and
\dim S/I=0
. Thus
\operatorname{depth}S/I=0
as well, and
S/I
is Cohen-Macaulay.

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}
and
\mu(I)=m
. If
m=1
, then
R
is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=R=I_{-1}(X)/I
. Let
m>1
. Then
a_{m}=b_{1}=0
by Lemma
3.4
, and the assertion follows from Theorem
3.2
and
[
19
, Corollary 3.2]
.

∎

We have the following classification.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x,y]
be a monomial ideal such that
R=S/I
is Cohen-Macaulay. Then
R
is nearly Gorenstein if and only if
\mu(I)=1
,
\mu(I)=2
or
\mu(I)=3
and
I=(x^{a},x^{b}y^{c},y^{d})
for some integers
a>b\geq 0
,
0\leq c<d
satisfying the conditions:
(i)

a-b=1
or
b=1
,
(ii)

d-c=1
or
c=1
, and
(iii)

b+c\geq 1
.

Proof.

If
\mu(I)=1
or
\mu(I)=2
, then
R
is a complete intersection, and thus a (nearly) Gorenstein ring. If
\mu(I)>3
, then
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, where
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x,y)R
. Finally assume
\mu(I)=3
. Then, by Lemma
3.4
,
I=(x^{a_{1}},x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}},y^{b_{3}})
where
a_{1}>a_{2}\geq 0
,
0\leq b_{2}<b_{3}
and
a_{2}+b_{2}\geq 1
. By equation (
9
), an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
is

\left(\begin{matrix}\phantom{-}y^{b_{2}}&0\\
-x^{a_{1}-a_{2}}&\phantom{-}y^{b_{3}-b_{2}}\\
0&-x^{a_{2}}\end{matrix}\right).

Hence
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=(x^{a_{1}-a_{2}},x^{a_{2}},y^{b_{3}-b_{2}},y^{b_{2}})/I
, and the assertion follows.

∎

4.
Nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals of height two

We conclude the paper with the classification of the nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals of height two. For a monomial
u\in S
, we set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}:x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Up to a relabeling of the variables, the only nearly Gorenstein monomial ideals in
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of height two are:

(a)

(u,v)
for two monomials
u,v\in S
with
\operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v)=\emptyset
.

(b)

(x_{1}^{a},x_{1}^{b}x_{2}^{c},x_{2}^{d})
with
n=2
and
a>b\geq 0
,
0\leq c<d
satisfying the conditions:
(i)

a-b=1
or
b=1
,
(ii)

d-c=1
or
c=1
, and
(iii)

b+c\geq 1
.

(c)

(x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})
with
a\geq 1
,
b\geq 0
,
a+b\geq 2
and
n=3
.

(d)

(x_{1}x_{2}^{b},x_{2}^{b+1},x_{1}x_{3})
with
b\geq 1
and
n=3
.

(e)

(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{4})
and
n=4
.

Proof.

Let
I\subset S
be a nearly Gorenstein monomial ideal of height two and set
R=S/I
. By
[
19
, Proposition 2.3(a)]
,
I
is Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum, and so is generically Gorenstein. Theorem
2.1
implies that
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=I_{m-2}(X)R
where
m=\mu(I)
and
X
is an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
. Since each non-zero entry of
X
has positive degree,
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{m-2}
, with
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})R
. Thus, if
I
is nearly Gorenstein, then
m\leq 3
. We cannot have
m=1
, otherwise
I
has height one.

If
m=2
, then
I=(u,v)
. If
x_{i}\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v)
, then
\operatorname{height}(I)=1
against the assumption. Thus
\operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v)=\emptyset
and case (a) follows. Conversely, if (a) holds, then
I=(u,v)
is a complete intersection, and thus is (nearly) Gorenstein.

Assume now
m=3
, then
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}
and so we must have
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})={\mathfrak{m}}
. Since
m=3
, then
n\geq 2
. If
n=2
, then Proposition
3.5
implies that case (b) holds.

Hence, we may assume
n\geq 3
. Let
G(I)=\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}
. Then, we have the following matrix arising from the so-called Taylor resolution (see
[
18
, Section 7.1]
):

T=\left(\begin{matrix}\phantom{-}\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{1},u_{2})}{u_{1}}&\phantom{-}\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{1},u_{3})}{u_{1}}&0\\
-\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{1},u_{2})}{u_{2}}&0&\phantom{-}\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{2},u_{3})}{u_{2}}\\
0&-\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{1},u_{3})}{u_{3}}&-\frac{\operatorname{lcm}(u_{2},u_{3})}{u_{3}}\end{matrix}\right).

It follows from
[
18
, Lemma 9.2.4]
that an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
is an appropriate
3\times 2
submatrix of
T
. Let
X
be such an Hilbert-Burch matrix. Then
X
has only four non-zero entries. Since we must have
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})R
, but we also have
\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{R})=I_{1}(X)R
, we deduce that
n
is at most four.

Let
n=3
. Up to relabeling and exchange of rows, we have

X=\left(\begin{matrix}\phantom{-}x_{1}&0\\
-x_{2}&\phantom{-}w\\
0&-x_{3}\end{matrix}\right)\,\,\,\,\,\,\textup{or}\,\,\,\,\,\,X=\left(\begin{matrix}\phantom{-}x_{1}&0\\
-x_{2}&\phantom{-}x_{3}\\
0&-w\end{matrix}\right),

(15)

where
w
is a suitable monomial.

In the first case
I=I_{2}(X)=(x_{1}w,x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})
. Note that
x_{3}
cannot divide
w
, otherwise we would have
m<3
. Thus
w=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}
for some
a,b\geq 0
with
a+b\geq 1
. Case (c) follows. Conversely, if (c) holds and
I=(x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})
for some
a\geq 1
,
b\geq 0
and
n=3
, then
I
is generically Gorenstein, putting
w=x_{1}^{a-1}x_{2}^{b}
, the first matrix
X
in (
15
) is an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
and so
I
is nearly Gorenstein.

In the second case
I=I_{2}(X)=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}w,x_{2}w)
. Notice that
x_{1}
and
x_{3}
cannot divide
w
, otherwise
I
would have height one. Thus
w=x_{2}^{b}
with
b\geq 1
and case (d) follows. Conversely, if (d) holds and
I=(x_{1}x_{2}^{b},x_{2}^{b+1},x_{1}x_{3})
with
b\geq 1
and
n=3
, then
I
is generically Gorenstein, putting
w=x_{2}^{b}
the second matrix
X
given in (
15
) is an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
with
I_{1}(X)={\mathfrak{m}}
and
I
is nearly Gorenstein.

Finally, assume
n=4
. Then, up to relabeling and exchange of rows,

X=\left(\begin{matrix}\phantom{-}x_{1}&0\\
-x_{2}&\phantom{-}x_{3}\\
0&-x_{4}\end{matrix}\right).

(16)

Thus
I=I_{2}(X)=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{4})
and case (e) follows. Conversely, if (e) holds, then the
I=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{4})
is generically Gorenstein, the matrix
X
given in (
16
) is an Hilbert-Burch matrix of
I
and thus
I
is nearly Gorenstein.

∎

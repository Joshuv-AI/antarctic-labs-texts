Abstract
. We show that a parametrized ♢ \diamondsuit principle, corresponding to the uniformity of the meager ideal, implies that the minimum cardinality of an infinite maximal almost disjoint family of block subspaces of a countable vector space is ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} . Consequently, this cardinal invariant is ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} in the Miller model. This verifies a conjecture of the author from [ 14 ] .

1.
Introduction

The setting of this note is a countably infinite-dimensional vector space
E
over a countable field
F
, with a distinguished basis
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For example, we may take
E=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F
and
e_{n}
to be the
n
th unit coordinate vector. If
X
is a set or sequence of vectors in
E
, then
\langle X\rangle
will denote its linear span. We also fix a well-ordering of
E
that will be used implicitly.

We will focus on
block subspaces
of
E
, that is, those linear subspaces which have a basis
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, called a
block sequence
, such that for all
n
,

\max(\mathrm{supp}(x_{n}))<\min(\mathrm{supp}(x_{n+1})),

where the
support
of a nonzero vector
v
is the set
\mathrm{supp}(v)
of those
n
for which the coefficient of
e_{n}
in the basis expansion of
v
is nonzero. We will abbreviate
\max(\mathrm{supp}(v))<\min(\mathrm{supp}(w))
by writing
v<w
. It is easy to see that every infinite-dimensional subspace contains a block subspace.

Two subspaces
X
and
Y
of
E
are
almost disjoint
if their intersection
X\cap Y
is finite-dimensional. A collection
\mathcal{A}
of infinite-dimensional subspaces which are pairwise almost disjoint is called an
almost disjoint family of subspaces
, and it is
maximal almost disjoint
, or
mad
, if it is not properly contained in any other such family. Note that an almost disjoint family consisting of block subspaces is maximal if and only if it is maximal amongst almost disjoint families of block subspaces.

Our focus here is the following cardinal invariant:

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}=\min\{|\mathcal{A}|:\mathcal{A}\text{ is an infinite mad family of block subspaces}\}.

The basic properties of mad families of block subspaces and
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}
can be found in
[
14
]
. In particular,
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}
is uncountable (Proposition 2.5 in
[
14
]
).

When
|F|=2
, our setting corresponds exactly to the study of combinatorial subspaces in
\mathrm{FIN}
, the set of finite nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. The resulting cardinal invariant,
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{FIN}}

[
5
]
, who proved the following lower bound, which the author observed could be extended to
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary 2.11 in
[
14
]
)

\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}
.

Here,
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})
is the
uniformity of the meager ideal

\mathcal{M}
, that is, the smallest cardinality of a nonmeager subset of the reals, or equivalently, any perfect Polish space. Consequently, in any model of set theory in which
\mathrm{non}(\mathcal{M})

The result

We can now state and prove our main result.

Theorem 3.1

\diamondsuit(\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E^{2}),=^{\infty})

Lemma 3.2

Let

(V+\langle y\rangle)\cap A_{i}=V\cap A_{i}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad y\notin A_{i}.\qed

.

To define

. By Lemma

Remark
.

One might expect that we would define

Let

\diamondsuit(\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E^{2}),=^{\infty})

. For each

(g(\delta)_{n}^{0},g(\delta)_{n}^{1})

(A_{n})_{n<\omega}

\omega\leq\delta<\omega_{1}

A_{\xi}\cap A_{\delta}
is finite-dimensional for all
\xi<\delta
.

Consider the set
S=\{\delta<\omega_{1}:A_{\delta}\text{ is infinite}\}
. If
B
is such that
((A_{\xi})_{\xi<\delta},B)
is as described in the first paragraph above, and
g
guesses
F((A_{\xi})_{\xi<\delta},B)
, then
\delta\in S
. Since countable almost disjoint families of block subspaces fail to be maximal, it follows that
S
is stationary.

We claim that
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\delta}:\delta\in S\}
is a mad family of subspaces. Towards a contradiction, suppose that
B
is an infinite-dimensional subspace almost disjoint from each
A_{\delta}
, for
\delta\in S
. Then,
g
guesses
F((A_{\xi})_{\xi<\delta},B)
at some
\delta\geq\omega
, and by the preceding paragraph,
\delta\in S
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
g(\delta)(k)=F((A_{\xi})_{\xi<\delta},B)(k)=(x_{k}^{0},x_{k}^{1})
and suppose that
n
is the first value for which we “attempt” to set
w_{n}=g(\delta)_{k}^{1}
. In particular,
n\leq k
. By definition of
\mathrm{bb}^{\infty}(E^{2})
, we know that
w_{0},\ldots,w_{n-1}
have supports contained in
[0,\max(\mathrm{supp}(x_{k}^{0}))]
, and thus
x_{k}^{1}
has the property that for all
i<k
, and in particular, for
i<n
,

\langle w_{0},\ldots,w_{n-1},x_{k}^{1}\rangle\cap A_{e_{\delta}(i)}=\langle w_{0},\ldots,w_{n-1}\rangle\cap A_{e_{\delta}(i)},

and so we must have set
w_{n}=x_{k}^{1}
. Since this happens for all values of
k
for which
g(\delta)(k)=F((A_{\xi})_{\xi<\delta},B)(k)
, it follows that
A_{\delta}
has infinite-dimensional intersection with
B
, contrary to our supposition.
∎

Combining this with Corollary
2.6
, we have:

Corollary 3.3
.

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathrm{vec},F}=\aleph_{1}
in the Miller model. ∎

Separation of horocycle orbits on moduli space in genus 2
John Rached

Abstract
We prove a quantitative closing lemma for the horocycle flow induced by the SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) -action on the moduli space of Abelian differentials with a double-order zero on surfaces of genus 2. The proof proceeds via construction of a Margulis function measuring the discretized fractal dimension of separation of a horocycle orbit of a point from itself, in a direction transverse to the SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) -orbit. From this, we deduce that small transversal separation guarantees the existence of a nearby point with a pseudo-Anosov in its Veech group. This is reminiscent of the initial dimension phases in Bourgain-Gamburd for random walks on compact groups, Bourgain-Lindenstrauss-Furman-Mozes for quantitative equidistribution in tori, and quantitative equidistribution of horocycle flow for a product of SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) with itself due to Lindenstrauss-Mohammadi-Wang, and multiple other works.

Introduction

Dynamical closing lemmas have been investigated for several decades, in a broad variety of contexts. In general terms, "closing lemma" refers to perturbing an initial point in a dynamical system, whose orbit is recurrent to a compact set, to find a point of small distance from the initial point that lies on a periodic orbit. "Quantitative closing lemma" generally indicates a bound in terms of the time parameter on the period of the closed orbit achieved. Two results that have inspired substantial research activity are Pugh’s closing lemma for
C^{1}
-diffeomorphisms of compact smooth manifolds
[
35
]
, and Anosov’s closing lemma
[
25
]
for hyperbolic sets of a flow. An open line of research in the direction of the former is to achieve such a closing lemma for higher orders of smoothness than
C^{1}

[
1
]
. This was resolved for the class of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of closed surfaces by Asaoka and Irie
[
2
]
. A quantitative result in this spirit, for Reeb orbits on contact 3-manifolds, was recently shown by Hutchings
[
23
]
.

In Teichmüller dynamics, the diagonal flow induced by the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action on the Hodge bundle over moduli space projects to geodesics for the Teichmüller metric on moduli space. This action is uniformly hyperbolic (Anosov) on compact sets; Hamendstädt
[
20
]
and Eskin-Mirzakhani-Rafi
[
14
]
have independently proven closing lemmas for this flow. A version of this lemma for affine invariant submanifolds of strata in the Hodge bundle is used by Wright in
[
46
]
, to prove that such manifolds are defined over number fields. A similar version of this lemma is invoked in
[
15
]
to show that the Zariski closure of the monodromy of the Kontsevich-Zorich cocycle restricted to the tangent bundle of an affine invariant manifold is the full group of endomorphisms preserving the symplectic form on the manifold.

Our concern in this article is with the action of a different one-parameter subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
acting on a stratum of the Hodge bundle, namely, the action of the unipotent
U=\left\{u_{s}\ |\ s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

-action remains poorly understood, in contrast to the full
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action, where a complete classification of invariant and stationary measures was obtained by Eskin-Mirzkakhani
[
11
]

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit closures are known to be affine invariant submanifolds of a stratum of the Hodge bundle
[
12
]
, and are in fact algebraic varieties
[
16
]
. On the other hand,

-orbit closures can have fractional Hausdorff dimensions
[
8
]
and exhibit other features distinctive from the homogeneous theory
[
7
]

[

[

\mu_{t}
of the extremal quasi-conformal map
\psi_{t}:X\rightarrow X_{t}
is given by

\mu_{t}=\left(\frac{i-t}{i+t}\right)\cdot\frac{\bar{q}}{|q|}.

(2.1)

Now, assume

is the square of a holomorphic
1
-form
w
,
q=\omega^{2}
. Then, these complex geodesics have an interpretation in terms of the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
action on
\Omega_{1}\mathcal{T}_{g}\left(\sigma\right)
. In particular, any holomorphic
1
-form
\omega
yields, away from its zeroes, a flat metric Euclidean metric
|\omega|
and an atlas of charts
U_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
whose transition functions are translations. The
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action on
\Omega_{1}\mathcal{T}_{g}\left(\sigma\right)
is defined by composition of these charts with matrices
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
acting linearly on
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
A\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
can be interpreted as an affine map in these coordinate charts
A\cdot(X,\omega)=(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
. This yields a quasi-conformal map
f:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
with terminal holomorphic
1
-form
\omega^{\prime}
. In the case that

, the map

is conformal, so the action descends to a faithful action of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})(X,\omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{g}.

Furthermore, composition with the projection
\pi:\mathcal{T}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}
defines the complex geodesic

f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}.

One can check that if
t\in\mathbb{C}
parametrizes the Riemann surfaces, the complex dilatation of the extremal quasi-conformal map

is given by

\mu_{t}=\left(\frac{i-t}{i+t}\right)\cdot\frac{\bar{\omega}}{|\omega|}

(2.2)

which accords with Equation
2.1
. Furthermore,
f
factors through the quotient space

, where
\Gamma=\left\{B\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{H}):f(B\cdot t)=f(t)\ \forall t\right\}
. In the case that
\Gamma
is a lattice, we call the quotient map

a Teichmüller curve.

is proper and generically injective. The image

is not a normal subvariety, but we will not distinguish between the Teichmüller curve as a map

and the normalization of
f(V)
. One can interpret
\Gamma
above directly in terms of flat geometry. Let
\mathrm{Aff}^{+}(X,\omega)
be the set of orientation-preserving affine diffeomorphisms of
(X,\omega)
. For such an affine diffeomorphism
\phi:(X,\omega)\mapsto(X,\omega)
, the derivative
D\phi
is constant. Since these diffeomorphisms are assumed to be orientation preserving, and also preserve the area of
\omega
, the map

defines a map of
\mathrm{Aff}^{+}(X,\omega)
into
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. We will call the image of this map the

\mathrm{SL}(X,\omega)

[

]

2.3
Period Coordinates and Holonomy Vectors

For any
(X,\omega)\in\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}\left(\sigma\right)
, there exists a triangulation
T
of the underlying surface
X
by saddle connections. Denote the zeroes of
\omega
by

, where
|Z(\omega)|=k
. One can choose

directed edges

of

of

in its stratum, so that there exists an open analytic embedding:

to be the relative class

\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma).
For any other geodesic triangulation

, the map

is linear.
A consequence of the uniformization theorem is that every element
(X,\omega)\in\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}\left(\sigma\right)
can be presented in the form

(X,\omega)=(P,dz)\big/\sim

for a polygon
P\subset\mathbb{C}
, and the
1
-form
dz
on
\mathbb{C}
. The reason for this is that one can construct a geodesic triangulation of the flat surface
(X,|\omega|)
, with
Z(\omega)
among its vertices.

can then be presented as a quotient
\sim
of the edges of a collection of triangles. One can check that the periods
\int_{w_{i}}\omega
defining

correspond to the vectors
\left\{v_{i}\right\}\in\mathbb{C}
, the edges of the polygon
P
.

2.4
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action and Transversal Directions

For a pair
(X,\omega)
, one can define the area of
(X,\omega)

\mathrm{area}(X,\omega)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{X}\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}.

We denote by
\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)\subset\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
the unit-area locus. Assume
\left\{w_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{h}

\mathbb{Z}
-basis of

\mathcal{F}
for the action of
\mathrm{Mod}\left(S_{g}\right)

\Omega_{1}\mathcal{T}_{g}(\sigma)

\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
. In particular, for a pair

will have no non-trivial automorphisms. Since applying a rotation matrix to
(X,\omega)
doesn’t change the complex structure on

For any subset

\mathbb{R}\mathcal{N}=\left\{(X,t\omega)\ |\ (X,\omega)\in\mathcal{N},t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma).

Denote by

the complex flat vector bundle over

whose fiber over any

, and by

the complex flat vector bundle whose fiber over

Definition 2.1
.

We call a subset

The following observation is helpful for our purposes.

Proposition 2.2
.

The tangent bundle of
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
is determined in local period coordinates by a subspace

T_{\mathbb{C}}(\tilde{\mathcal{N}})=T_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{\mathcal{N}})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}

-action is on

-linear span of

Definition 2.3
.

The complex vector bundle

has a symplectic intersection form given by

\langle{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\rangle}:=\frac{i}{2}\int_{X}\omega_{1}\wedge\overline{\omega_{2}}

By the preceeding discussion, we have a decomposition of the tangent bundle

Definition 2.4

(AGY-metric,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
x=(X,\omega)\in\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
. Identify the tangent space at
(X,\omega)
with
H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
. For a tangent vector

, consider the norm

\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}:=\underset{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{sup}}\Big\lvert\frac{v(\mathbf{s})}{\mathrm{Hol}_{\omega}(\mathbf{s})}\Big\rvert

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the set of saddle connections on
(X,\omega)
, and
\mathrm{Hol}_{\omega}(\mathbf{s})\in\mathbb{C}
is the holonomy vector associated to
\mathbf{s}
for the form
\omega
. The set of holonomy vectors is always discrete. It is shown in
[
4
]
that this definition induces a complete Finsler metric on
\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
. Let
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
be a differentiable path and denote its AGY-length by

l(\kappa):=\int_{0}^{1}\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(t)}\ dt.

The distance function associated to this length will be denoted by
d_{AGY}
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
be a differentiable path. Then for any tangent vector
v\in H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
,

e^{-\mathrm{length}(\kappa)}\leq\frac{\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(0)}}{\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(1)}}\leq e^{\mathrm{length}(\kappa)}.

The following and its proof are essentially Proposition 5.3 in
[
5
]
; there, it is stated for vectors in the unstable space, but the proof goes through for any choice of tangent vectors. We briefly recapitulate the proof ingredients for the general case, as we will make extensive use of the AGY-norm.

Lemma 2.6

("Exponential map",
[
5
]
)
.

Let
x=(X,\omega)\in\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
and
v\in H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
, a tangent vector. Consider the path
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
starting from
x
satisfying
\kappa^{\prime}(t)=v
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Implicitly, we assume
\kappa(t)
is well-defined for all
t\in[0,1]
. Define
\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v):=\kappa(1)\in\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
. Let
B(0,r)
denote the ball of radius
r
centered at the origin of
H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
with respect to the norm
\lvert\lvert\cdot\rvert\rvert_{x}
. Then, there exists an absolute constant
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}=(C_{1}+1)^{2}C_{1}
such that the following hold:

1.

for the rescaling
\omega\mapsto s\omega
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
l(\kappa)=l(s\kappa)
. That is, the AGY-metric is invariant under real scalings.

2.

The exponential map
\mathrm{exp}_{x}:H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
is well-defined over
B(0,1/C_{1})
.

3.

For every
v\in B(0,1/C_{1})
,

d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v))\leq C_{1}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}.

4.

For every
v\in B(0,1/C_{1})
and every
w\in H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
,

1/C_{1}\leq\frac{\lvert\lvert w\rvert\rvert_{x}}{\lvert\lvert w\rvert\rvert_{\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v)}}\leq C_{1}.

5.

For
v\in B(0,1/C_{2})
,

d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v))\geq\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{\omega}/C_{1}.

Proof.

For 1., we have

\displaystyle l(s\kappa)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lvert\lvert s\kappa^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{s\kappa(t)}\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\underset{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{sup}}\Bigg\lvert\frac{s\kappa^{\prime}(t)(\mathbf{s})}{\mathrm{Hol}_{s\kappa(t)}(\mathbf{s})}\Bigg\rvert dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\underset{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{sup}}\Bigg\lvert\frac{s\kappa^{\prime}(t)(\mathbf{s})}{s\mathrm{Hol}_{\kappa(t)}(\mathbf{s})}\Bigg\rvert dt

\displaystyle=l(\kappa).

For 2., let
\kappa:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega_{1}\mathcal{M}_{g}(\sigma)
be a path with
\kappa(0)=(X,\omega)
and
\kappa^{\prime}(t)=v\ \forall t\in[0,1]
. By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(t)}=\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(r)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(r)}\ dr}.

(2.4)

Consider the length function
L(t):\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
defined by
L(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(r)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(r)}\ dr
. By Equation
2.4
, we have
L^{\prime}(t)\leq e^{L(t)}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}.
Integrating both sides of this inequality and rearranging, we have

\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(t)}\leq\frac{\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}}{1-t\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}},

which immediately implies that for any
C_{1}>1
,
\mathrm{exp}_{\omega}(v)
is well-defined on the ball
B(0,1/C_{1})
. Further, note that

d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v))\leq\int_{0}^{1}\lvert\lvert\kappa^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(t)}\leq\frac{\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}}{(1-\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x})},

which implies
d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v))\leq C_{1}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}
for
v\in B(0,1/C_{1})
, establishing 3. Since
\mathrm{exp}_{\omega}
is well-defined over
B(0,1/C_{1})
by 2., another application of Lemma
2.5
gives

e^{-L(1)}\leq\frac{\lvert\lvert w\rvert\rvert_{x}}{\lvert\lvert w\rvert\rvert_{\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v)}}\leq e^{L(1)}

for any
w\in H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
, proving 4., since
L(1)\leq\mathrm{log}(C_{1})
. Finally, we claim that for
C_{2}=(C_{1}+1)^{2}C_{1}
, we have

d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v))\geq\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}/C_{1}

so long as
v\in B(0,1/C_{2})
. To see this, consider a (nearly) length-minimzing path
\kappa
connecting
(X,\omega)
and
\mathrm{exp}_{\omega}(v)
. By part 3., we have

l(\kappa)\leq C_{1}\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}\leq\frac{1}{(C_{1}+1)^{2}}.

(2.5)

Consider a lift of
\kappa
,
\tilde{\kappa}
to the tangent space to the stratum identified with
H^{1}(X,Z(\omega),\mathbb{C})
. By part 4., we have
\lvert\lvert\tilde{\kappa}^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{x}\leq C_{1}\lvert\lvert\tilde{\kappa}^{\prime}(t)\rvert\rvert_{\kappa(t)}
. This inequality can be integrated from
0
to
t
to obtain

\lvert\lvert\tilde{\kappa}(t)\rvert\rvert_{x}\leq C_{1}l(\kappa)\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{(C_{1}+1)^{2}}<1/C_{1}.

(2.6)

We conclude that
\tilde{\kappa}(t)\in B(0,1/C_{1})\ \forall t\in[0,1]
and in particular
\tilde{\kappa}(1)
is well-defined and we have

\displaystyle\lvert\lvert\tilde{\kappa}(1)\rvert\rvert_{x}=\lvert\lvert v\rvert\rvert_{x}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}l(\kappa)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}d_{AGY}(x,\mathrm{exp}_{x}(v)),

proving 5.
∎

2.5
Unstable and stable foliations

In contrast to the homogeneous setting, the unstable and stable foliations for the geodesic flow are only locally defined. We review these definitions and their relationships to our objects of interest.

Definition 2.7

(Proposition 4.1
[
5
]
)
.

Recall that for any

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{\tau}\mathbf{h}_{s}u_{-\tau}&=\mathbf{p}_{s^{\prime}}(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}(\mathbf{p}_{s^{\prime}})^{-1}\\
&=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}+a_{3,s}\tau&a_{2,s}+(a_{4,s}-a_{1,s})\tau-a_{3,s}\tau^{2}\\
a_{3,s}&a_{4,s}-a_{3,s}\tau\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(4.18)

By Proposition
4.11
, and the fact that
\mathrm{max}\left\{e^{7t}|a_{3,s}|,|a_{1}-1|,|a_{4}-1|\right\}\gg\beta^{\alpha}
, we may conclude that for every
s\in J_{\beta}
, the set of
s^{\prime}\in J_{\beta}
so that

|a_{2,s}e^{-7t}+(a_{4}-a_{1})(s^{\prime}-s)-a_{3}e^{7t}(s^{\prime}-s)^{2}|\leq e^{-6t}

(4.19)

is of measure
\ll\beta^{-2\alpha}e^{-3t}
. If we denote by
J_{\beta,s}
the set of
s^{\prime}\in J_{\beta}
for which Equation
4.19
is valid, and we let
s^{\prime}\in J_{\beta}\setminus J_{\beta,s}
then for all such
s^{\prime}
we compute

\frac{||u_{\tau}\mathbf{h}_{s}u_{-\tau}||}{||\mathbf{h}_{s^{\prime}}||}\gg e^{-2t}

using Equation
4.18
and the fact that
||\mathbf{h}_{s^{\prime}}||\ll e^{2t}
. This is in contradiction to Equations
4.15
and
4.16
. We conclude that for
s\in J_{\beta}
, the set of
s^{\prime}\in J_{\beta}
for which
[\gamma_{s}]=[\gamma_{s^{\prime}}]
has measure
\ll\beta^{-2\alpha}e^{-3t}
. Therefore, the set of
s^{\prime}\in J_{\beta}
for which
(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}=(\mathbf{p}_{s^{\prime}})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s^{\prime}}\mathbf{p}_{s^{\prime}}
is of measure
\ll\beta^{-2\alpha}e^{-3t}
. Since
||(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}||\ll e^{9t}
, the set

\left\{(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s},\ s\in J_{\beta}\right\}

has cardinality
\gg e^{2.9t}
.

Existence of hyperbolic element

We would like to show the group
\mathbf{G}
generated by

\langle(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s},\ s\in I_{bad}\rangle
cannot be unipotent; proceeding by contradiction, assume that

(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}=u_{-s}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}&e^{-7t}a_{2,s}\\
e^{7t}a_{3,s}&a_{4,s}\end{bmatrix}u_{s}\in gUg^{-1}

(4.20)

for all
s\in I_{bad}
, where
U=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&r\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\,r\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
and
g\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Since
\mathbf{G}
is unipotent, we know

\big\lvert\left\{\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}:\ ||\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}||\leq e^{4t/3}\right\}\big\rvert\ll e^{8t/3}.

We have just shown
\big\lvert\left\{(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s},\ s\in J_{\beta}\right\}\big\rvert\gg e^{2.9t}
so we can therefore conclude

\big\lvert\left\{\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}:\ ||\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}||\geq e^{4t/3}\ \textrm{and}\ s\in J_{\beta}\right\}\big\rvert\gg e^{t/4}.

(4.21)

Let
\beta^{2\alpha}\chi\leq\Psi\leq e^{4t/3}
. A computation shows that if

||(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}(\mathbf{p}_{s})-I||=\Bigg\lvert\Bigg\lvert u_{-s}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}&e^{-7t}a_{2,s}\\
e^{7t}a_{3,s}&a_{4,s}\end{bmatrix}u_{s}-I\Bigg\rvert\Bigg\rvert\geq 20\Psi

then, for
t
large enough

|a_{3,s}|\geq\Psi e^{-7t}.

(4.22)

By Equation
4.20
, for each
s\in I_{bad}
, there is
r_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
so that

u_{-s}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}&e^{-7t}a_{2,s}\\
e^{7t}a_{3,s}&a_{4,s}\end{bmatrix}u_{s}=g\begin{bmatrix}1&r_{0}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}.

(4.23)

Let
s_{0}\in J^{\prime}\cap I_{bad}
. Setting
u_{s_{0}}g=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
, Equation
4.23
gives

\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s_{0}}&e^{-7t}a_{2,s_{0}}\\
e^{7t}a_{3,s_{0}}&a_{4,s_{0}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1-acr_{0}&a^{2}r_{0}\\
-c^{2}r_{0}&1+acr_{0}\end{bmatrix}

(4.24)

for some
r_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying Equation
4.22
with
\Psi=\beta^{2\alpha}\chi
, we have
|a_{3,s_{0}}|\geq e^{-7t}\beta^{2\alpha}\chi
, and, since
g
is fixed so its entries are bounded by absolute constants, we may compare matrix entries on the right and left hand sides to conclude
|a|\ll e^{-2.9t}
and
|c|\gg 1
. Now, let
s\in J_{\beta}
. By Equation
4.21
, we may assume
||(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}||\geq e^{4t/3}
. Set
s_{1}=s-s_{0}
,
a^{\prime}_{2,s}=e^{-7t}a_{2,s}
,
a^{\prime}_{3,s}=e^{7t}a_{3,s}
. By Equation
4.22
applied with
\Psi=e^{4t/3}
, we deduce
|a^{\prime}_{3,s}|\geq e^{4t/3}
. For the sake of once again comparing matrix entries, apply Equation
4.20
to obtain

\displaystyle u_{-s_{1}}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}&a^{\prime}_{2,s}\\
a^{\prime}_{3,s}&a_{4,s}\end{bmatrix}u_{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,s}-s_{1}a^{\prime}_{3,s}&a^{\prime}_{2,s}+(a_{4,s}-a_{1,s})s_{1}-a^{\prime}_{3,s}(s_{1})^{2}\\
a^{\prime}_{3,s}&a_{4,s}+s_{1}a^{\prime}_{3,s}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1-acr&a^{2}r\\
-c^{2}r&1+acr\end{bmatrix}

where
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Compare matrix entries using
|a^{\prime}_{3,s}|\geq e^{4t/3}
and, by
d(J,J^{\prime})\gg\beta^{\alpha}
, the inequality
\beta\leq\beta^{\alpha}\leq s_{1}\leq 1
. We conclude
|a|^{2}
and
|c|^{2}
are comparable in size. This contradicts
|a|\ll e^{-2.9t}
and
|c|\gg 1
for
t
large.

In light of the discussion in Subsection
2.4
, we may assume
(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s}
are not elliptic, and we have just demonstrated
\langle(\mathbf{p}_{s})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_{s}\mathbf{p}_{s},\ s\in I_{bad}\rangle
is not unipotent. It is, in particular not generated by a single parabolic element. It must therefore contain two transverse parabolic elements, and by the ping-pong lemma must contain a hyperbolic element. Considering traces, there must be an
\mathbf{h}_{s}
hyperbolic.

All in all, there exist
z,\bar{z}\in E_{t}\cdot a_{7t}u_{s}\cdot(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
,
\bar{z}=\mathbf{h}_{s}\cdot z
where
d_{\mathcal{T}}(z,\bar{z})\ll e^{-80t}
. By Theorem
4.5
,
d_{H}(z,\bar{z})\ll e^{-60t}
. By Theorem
4.7
, there exists a point
z^{\prime}
fixed by
\mathbf{h}_{s}
with
d_{\mathcal{T}}(z^{\prime},z)\ll e^{-60t}
. In particular, by Equation
4.9
, and the fact that
d_{\mathcal{T}}(z^{\prime},z)\ll e^{-60t}
,
\gamma_{s}
corresponding to
\mathbf{h}_{s}
must be non-trivial. The translation length
l(\mathbf{h}_{s})
satisfies
e^{-t/2}\ll\mathrm{log}(l(\mathbf{h}_{s}))\ll e^{20t}
. The diagonalizable element
\mathbf{h}_{s}
is in the Veech group of
z^{\prime}
and by Theorem
4.8
,
z^{\prime}
lies on a Teichmüller curve. Theorem
1.2
follows from this, taking
D\geq 6\kappa_{4}+100
.

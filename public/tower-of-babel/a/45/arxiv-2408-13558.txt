Combinatorial invariants for certain classes of non-abelian groups
Naveen K. Godara, Renu Joshi, Eshita Mazumdar

Abstract
This article focuses on the study of zero-sum invariants of finite non-abelian groups. We address two main problems: the first centers on the ordered Davenport constant and the second on Gao’s constant. We establish a connection between the ordered Davenport constant and the small Davenport constant for a finite non-abelian group of even order, which in turn gives a relation with the Noether number. Additionally, we confirm a conjecture of Gao and Li for a non-abelian group of order 2 ​ p α 2p^{\alpha} , where p p is a prime. Furthermore, we prove a conjecture that connects the ordered Davenport constant to the Loewy length for certain classes of finite 2 2 -groups.



Keywords:

Gao constant, Davenport constant, Product-one sequence, Loewy length.

By the definitions of

\mathsf{d}(G)\leq\big\lfloor\frac{|G|}{2}\big\rfloor

Moreover, in the realm of invariant theory, an important constant to consider is the Noether number, which is related to zero-sum invariants. For a finite-dimensional

\displaystyle M_{1}(G)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle G,

S^{\ast}=(a^{-1})^{[2^{\alpha}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(b^{-1})^{[2^{\beta}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(a[a,b])^{[2^{\gamma}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(b[a,b])^{[2^{\gamma}-1]}

over
G
of length
|S^{\ast}|=2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}+2^{\gamma+1}-4
. If possible, let us assume that
S^{\ast}
has a non-trivial product-one ordered subsequence. Then

(a^{-1})^{x}(b^{-1})^{y}(a[a,b])^{z}(b[a,b])^{w}=1

(5.1)

with
x\in[0,(2^{\alpha}-1)],\;y\in[0,(2^{\beta}-1)],\;z,w\in[0,(2^{\gamma}-1)],
with not all zero.
Equation
(\ref{LBEQ1})
results in
a^{-x+z}b^{-y+w}[a,b]^{z+w+yz}=1
. So, we have the following system of equations

\displaystyle-x+z

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\alpha})

\displaystyle-y+w

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\beta})

\displaystyle z+w+yz

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\gamma}).

If
\gamma=1
, then the trivial solution is the only solution to the above system of equations, providing a contradiction. Thus,
\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\geq 2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}+2^{\gamma+1}-3.

(2)

Let
G=G_{2}.
From Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
4.2
, we have
\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\leq 2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}-1
. To prove the reverse inequality, we consider the ordered sequence

over

of length
|S^{\ast}|=2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}-2
. Since
\langle a\rangle\cap\langle b\rangle=1
, the ordered sequence
S^{\ast}
has no non-trivial product-one ordered subsequence. Hence,
\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\geq 2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}-1.

(3)

Let
G=G_{3}.
Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
4.3
implies that
\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\leq 2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}+2^{\sigma+1}-3
.
For the reverse inequality, consider the ordered sequence

S^{\ast}=(a^{-1})^{[2^{\alpha}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(b^{-1})^{[2^{\beta}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(a[a,b])^{[2^{\sigma}-1]}\boldsymbol{\cdot}(b[a,b])^{[2^{\sigma}-1]}

over
G
of length
|S^{\ast}|=2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}+2^{\sigma+1}-4
. Suppose
S^{\ast}
has a non-trivial product-one ordered subsequence. In this case, we have

(a^{-1})^{x}(b^{-1})^{y}(a[a,b])^{z}(b[a,b])^{w}=1

(5.2)

for
x\in[0,(2^{\alpha}-1)],\;y\in[0,(2^{\beta}-1)],\;z,w\in[0,(2^{\sigma}-1)],
with not all zero. Equation
(\ref{LBEQ3})
leads to
a^{-x+z}b^{-y+w}[a,b]^{z+w+yz}=1.

Let us consider
\overline{G}:=\frac{G}{\langle a,c\rangle}

Thus, the above equation simplifies to
a^{-x+z}[a,b]^{z+w+yz}=1
. Using the relation
[a,b]=a^{2^{\alpha-\gamma}}c
, we obtain following system of equations

\displaystyle-x+z+2^{\alpha-\gamma}(z+w+yz)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\alpha})

\displaystyle-y+w

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\beta})

\displaystyle z+w+yz

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textrm{mod}\ 2^{\sigma}).

If
\sigma=1
, the only solution to the given system of equations is the trivial solution, leading to a contradiction. This yields
\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\geq 2^{\alpha}+2^{\beta}+2^{\sigma+1}-3.

(4)

Let
G=G_{4}.
Using Lemma
2.2
and Lemma

\mathsf{D}_{o}(G)\leq 2^{\gamma+2}-3.
For the reverse inequality, we consider the ordered sequence

Proof of Corollary

Proof of Theorem

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we concentrated on several zero-sum invariants and explored their interrelationships. In Theorem
1.3
, we identify a class of non-abelian groups of the form

, where

p

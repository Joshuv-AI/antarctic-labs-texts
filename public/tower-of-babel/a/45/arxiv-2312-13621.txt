Scattering results for the (1+4) dimensional massive Maxwell-Dirac system under Lorenz gauge condition Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q41, 35Q55, 35Q40. Thanks: Keywords and phrases. Maxwell-Dirac system, global existence, space-time resonance, scattering theory, Lorenz gauge condition.
Kiyeon Lee

Abstract
. This paper investigates the massive Maxwell-Dirac system under the Lorenz gauge condition in (4+1) dimensional Minkowski space. The focus is on establishing global existence and scattering results for small solutions on the weighted Sobolev class. The imposition of the Lorenz gauge condition transforms the Maxwell-Dirac system into a set of Dirac equations coupled with an electromagnetic potential derived from five quadratic wave equations. To achieve a comprehensive understanding of the global solution and its behavior, we employ various energy estimates based on the space-time resonance argument. This involves addressing diverse resonance functions arising from the free Dirac and wave propagators. Additionally, we identify the space-time resonant sets associated with the massive Maxwell-Dirac system.

The subject of this paper is the global existence of the (1+4) dimensional
massive

To handle the nonlinearity that possesses both of Klein-Gordon type phase and wave type phase, we utilize the space-time resonance argument. To this end, we investigate the time, space, and space-time resonant sets, combined with the Dirac and Maxwell parts. Then we proceed with a bootstrap argument, based on the energy estimates. See Sections

1.4.
Main idea

The proof of the global solution and scattering results is based on a bootstrap argument under the suitable a priori assumption with the weighted energy estimates. To this end, we use the space-time resonant argument, developed by Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah
[
18
,
20
,
19
]
.By employing Duhamel’s formula, the nonlinear terms of (
1.2
) give rise to several oscillatory integrals, with the stationary points of the phase function dictating their large-time behaviors.

Our analysis begins with addressing the space, time, and space-time resonance of
phases
originating from the Dirac spinor and wave operator within the oscillatory integrals, arising from Dirac and wave propagators. Specifically, through a standard reformulation with the Dirac projection operator and first-order wave equations, we obtain the system of the following nonlinearities
\mathbf{f}_{\Theta}
and
\mathbf{F}_{\mu,K}
for the Dirac and Maxwell parts, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\,\Theta}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{is\,p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(\xi)\widehat{F_{\mu,\theta_{2}}}(s,\eta)\alpha^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{1}}}(s,\xi-\eta)\,d\eta ds,

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mu,K}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=|\xi|^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{isq_{K}(\xi,\eta)}\left\langle\widehat{f_{{\kappa}_{2}}}(s,\eta),\alpha_{\mu}\widehat{f_{{\kappa}_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta ds,

\displaystyle p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\theta_{0}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle-\theta_{1}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|,

\displaystyle q_{K}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=-{\kappa}_{0}|\xi|-{\kappa}_{1}\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle+{\kappa}_{2}\left\langle\eta\right\rangle,

for 3-tuples
\Theta=({\theta_{0}},{\theta_{1}},{\theta_{2}})
and
K=({{\kappa}_{0}},{{\kappa}_{1}},{{\kappa}_{2}})
where
{\theta_{j}},{\kappa}_{j}\in\{+,-\}\,(j=0,1,2)
(see Section
2.1
for the rigorous derivation). The functions
p_{\Theta}
and
q_{K}
are commonly referred to as
phases
. These phases exhibit various sign relations, requiring consideration of different resonance cases. While the phases introduce space-time resonance, the nonlinearities (
MD
) lack sufficient null structures to eliminate these resonances. As observed in
[
8
,
24
,
16
]
, the interaction between projection operators and Dirac matrices
{\alpha}_{j}
induces sign-changed projection parts and Riesz transform parts:

\displaystyle{\alpha}^{j}\Pi_{\theta}(\xi)=\Pi_{-\theta}(\xi){\alpha}^{j}+\theta\frac{\xi_{j}}{|\xi|}.

Depending on the sign relation, cases that require null structures emerge, while the interaction generates terms without null structures, regardless of the sign relation.

To overcome this lack of null structure, we impose a homogeneous Sobolev norm in the a priori assumption of the Maxwell part. This assumption provides as a null structure in the estimates for the Maxwell part. However, it also leads to singularities in the estimates of the Dirac part. To handle these singularities, we exploit the almost optimal time decay effect of the Dirac spinor and the dimensional advantage.

As we have observed in numerous previous works, the relationship of (
MD
) to (MKG) becomes apparent. The emergence of the Dirac equation can be traced back to an endeavor to find the square root of the Klein-Gordon equation, to formulate an equation that is first-order in time. Consequently, squaring the Dirac component of the system yields an equation that bears a resemblance to the Klein-Gordon segment of (MKG). Nevertheless, as pointed out in
[
8
]
, this idea appears to have limited applicability, as squaring the Dirac equation results in the loss of much of its spinorial null structure. It is worth noting, however, that our proof in this paper does not depend on exploiting this null structure. Consequently, our approach can be extended to encompass the
massive
(MKG).

It remains still open to obtain the global existence and asymptotic behavior of (

L^{2}
–norm of nonlinearities are not integrable in time on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+3}

[
9
,
6
,
7
]
, then we anticipate that the global solution to (
MD
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+3}
exhibits a long range behavior which requires suitable phase modification.

\displaystyle=\psi_{0,\theta_{0}}+i\sum_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\{\pm\}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\theta_{0}is{\left\langle D\right\rangle}}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(D)\big(A_{\mu,\theta_{2}}\alpha^{\mu}\psi_{\theta_{1}}\big)(s)\,ds,

(2.9)

\displaystyle F_{\mu,{\kappa}_{0}}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{\mu,{\kappa}_{0}}-{\kappa}_{0}\frac{i}{2}\sum_{{\kappa}_{1},{\kappa}_{2}\in\{\pm\}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-{\kappa}_{0}is|D|}|D|^{-1}\left\langle\psi_{{\kappa}_{1}},\alpha_{\mu}\psi_{{\kappa}_{2}}\right\rangle(s)\,ds,

(2.10)

for
{\theta_{0}},{{\kappa}_{0}}\in\{+,-\}
. By taking Fourier transform, one gets the frequency representation as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\theta_{0}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\psi_{0,\theta_{0}}}+i\sum_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\{\pm\}}\mathbf{f}_{\,\Theta}(t,\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{F_{\mu,{\kappa}_{0}}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{a_{\mu,{\kappa}_{0}}}(\xi)-{\kappa}_{0}\frac{i}{2}\sum_{{\kappa}_{1},{\kappa}_{2}\in\{\pm\}}\mathbf{F}_{\,\mu,K}(t,\xi),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\,\Theta}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{is\,p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(\xi)\widehat{F_{\mu,\theta_{2}}}(s,\eta)\alpha^{\mu}\widehat{f_{\theta_{1}}}(s,\xi-\eta)\,d\eta ds,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\,\mu,K}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=|\xi|^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{isq_{K}(\xi,\eta)}\left\langle\widehat{f_{{\kappa}_{2}}}(s,\eta),\alpha_{\mu}\widehat{f_{{\kappa}_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right\rangle\,d\eta ds,

(2.12)

\displaystyle p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\theta_{0}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle-\theta_{1}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|,

(2.13)

\displaystyle q_{K}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=-{\kappa}_{0}|\xi|-{\kappa}_{1}\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle+{\kappa}_{2}\left\langle\eta\right\rangle,

(2.14)

for 3-tuples
\Theta=({\theta_{0}},{\theta_{1}},{\theta_{2}})
and
K=({{\kappa}_{0}},{{\kappa}_{1}},{{\kappa}_{2}})
. We call
p_{\Theta},q_{K}
the
phase
.

2.2.
Space-time resonance for Maxwell-Dirac system

We proceed our proof by a bootstrap argument based on space-time resonance argument

[
18
,
19
,
20
]
and
[
22
]
, independently, which is our frame work. In this section we investigate a time, space, and space-time resonances for
massive
Maxwell-Dirac system, respectively. For the time resonant set of wave and Klein-Gordon type systems, we refer to
[
26
,
27
]
. We also refer to
[
36
,
4
]
for the observation of Dirac operator. According to the definition in
[
18
]
, we define time, space, and space-time resonant sets as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{T}_{p_{\Theta}}&:=\left\{(\xi,\eta):p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},\\
\mathcal{S}_{p_{\Theta}}&:=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},\\
\mathcal{R}_{p_{\Theta}}&:=\mathcal{T}_{p_{\Theta}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{p_{\Theta}},\end{aligned}\hskip 56.9055pt\begin{aligned} \mathcal{T}_{q_{K}}&:=\left\{(\xi,\eta):q_{K}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},\\
\mathcal{S}_{q_{K}}&:=\left\{(\xi,\eta):\nabla_{\eta}q_{K}(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},\\
\mathcal{R}_{q_{K}}&:=\mathcal{T}_{q_{K}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{q_{K}}.\end{aligned}

It is difficult to examine precisely elements of these sets. In view of these resonant sets, it suffices to observe the lower bounds of
p_{\Theta},\nabla_{\eta}p_{\Theta}
and
q_{\Theta},\nabla_{\eta}q_{\Theta}
, respectively.

Let us observe that
p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)
never vanishes, when
\xi\neq 0
and
\eta\neq 0
, for any sign
\theta_{0},{\theta_{1}},{\theta_{2}}\in\{+,-\}
. In fact, if for some
\xi,\eta
,
p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)=0
, then

(\theta_{0}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|)^{2}=1+|\xi-\eta|^{2}.

This implies that

{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}|\eta|=|\xi\cdot\eta|\;\;\mbox{and hence}\;\;{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\leq|\xi|,

which is a contradiction. Hence we have

\displaystyle|p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)|

\displaystyle=\left|\theta_{0}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}-\theta_{1}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|\right|=\left|\frac{(\theta_{0}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\theta_{2}|\eta|)^{2}-\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle^{2}}{\theta_{0}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\theta_{1}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2\theta_{0}\theta_{2}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}|\eta|+2\xi\cdot\eta}{\theta_{0}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\theta_{1}\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\theta_{2}|\eta|}\right|.

If
\theta_{0}=\theta_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)|&\geq\frac{2|\eta|({\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}-|\xi|)}{{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+|\eta|}\gtrsim\frac{|\eta|}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\eta\right\rangle)}.\end{aligned}

(2.15)

If
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1},\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{2}
, then

\displaystyle|p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)|\geq\frac{2|\eta|({\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}-|\xi|)}{|{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}-\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle-|\eta||}\gtrsim({\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}-|\xi|)\gtrsim\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{-1}.

Also if
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1},\theta_{0}=\theta_{2}
, then trivially
|p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)|\geq\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle
. Then we have, for
{\theta_{0}}\neq{\theta_{1}}
,

\displaystyle|p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)|\gtrsim\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{-1}.

(2.16)

Therefore
\mathcal{T}_{p_{\Theta}}=\{\eta=0\}
when
{\theta_{0}}={\theta_{1}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{p_{\Theta}}=\emptyset
, otherwise.

Let us observe the space resonance of (
2.11
). For any sign cases, there is no space resonant as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\eta}p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)\right|=\left|{\theta_{1}}\frac{\xi-\eta}{\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle}-{\theta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{|\eta|}\right|\gtrsim 1-\frac{|\xi-\eta|}{\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle}\gtrsim\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle^{-2}.

(2.17)

Therefore we deduce that
\mathcal{S}_{p_{\Theta}}=\emptyset
, for any sign relations, which implies
\mathcal{R}_{p_{\Theta}}=\emptyset
.

By virtue of a similarity between
p_{\Theta}(\xi,\eta)
and
q_{K}(\xi,\eta)
, we readily see that

\displaystyle|q_{K}(\xi,\eta)|\gtrsim\begin{cases}\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\eta\right\rangle(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle+\left\langle\eta\right\rangle)}&\mbox{ for }{{\kappa}_{1}}={{\kappa}_{2}},\\
\left\langle\eta\right\rangle^{-1}&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.18)

and

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\eta}q_{K}(\xi,\eta)|\gtrsim\begin{cases}\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle^{3}}&\mbox{ for }{{\kappa}_{1}}={{\kappa}_{2}},\\
\left|\frac{\xi+\eta}{\left\langle\xi+\eta\right\rangle}+\frac{\eta}{\left\langle\eta\right\rangle}\right|&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.19)

Note that space resonance of
q_{K}
is different from that of
p_{\Theta}
. Therefore, we have
\mathcal{T}_{q_{K}}=\mathcal{S}_{q_{K}}=\mathcal{R}_{q_{K}}=\{\xi=0\}
when
{{\kappa}_{1}}={{\kappa}_{2}}
.
On the other hand, when
{{\kappa}_{1}}\neq{{\kappa}_{2}}
,

\mathcal{T}_{q_{K}}=\emptyset
,
\mathcal{S}_{q_{K}}=\{\xi=2\eta\}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{q_{K}}=\emptyset
.

2.3.
A priori assumption

As we discussed in introduction, we set a priori assumption in this section for the bootstrap argument.
For
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
to be chosen
later, we assume a priori smallness of solutions: for some
0<\beta<{\delta}
,
a given time
T>0
, a priori assumptions of Dirac part and Maxwell part are suggested by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\textbf{D}}}+\|A_{\mu}\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\textbf{M}}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1},\end{aligned}

(2.20)

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\textbf{D}}}&:=\|\psi_{+}\|_{\Sigma_{T,+}^{\textbf{D}}}+\|\psi_{-}\|_{\Sigma_{T,-}^{\textbf{D}}}\\
\|\psi_{\theta}\|_{\Sigma_{T,\theta}^{\textbf{D}}}&:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{H^{n}}+\left\|xf_{\theta}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-{\delta}}\left\|x^{2}f_{\theta}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right],\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|A_{\mu}\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\textbf{M}}}&:=\|A_{\mu,+}\|_{\Sigma_{T,+}^{\textbf{M}}}+\|A_{\mu,-}\|_{\Sigma_{T,-}^{\textbf{M}}}\\
\|A_{\mu,\kappa}\|_{\Sigma_{T,\kappa}^{\textbf{M}}}&:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{{\delta}}{2}}\|A_{\mu,\kappa}(t)\|_{{H}^{n}}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{{\delta}}{2}}\left\|xF_{\mu,\kappa}(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left\|x^{2}F_{\mu,\kappa}(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\right].\end{aligned}

.

As mentioned in Introduction, we do not pursue the optimality of regularity condition

\displaystyle 0<\beta<{\delta},\;\;\frac{56}{n-2}\leq\beta,\;\mbox{ and }\;\beta+{\delta}\leq\frac{1}{4},

(2.21)

According to these conditions,
(n,\beta,{\delta})=(500,\frac{4}{35},\frac{9}{70})
is an one of the parameter candidates. Moreover, we anticipate that it is possible to make the regularity condition

lower by imposing the regularity on the weighted Sobolev norm assumptions (
2.20
) or dividing frequencies elaborately in energy estimates. For the sake of the simplicity of the energy estimates, we do not treat that.

Remark 2.2
.

For the extension of our method, we refer to the asymptotic behavior results of the dispersive and wave system
[

,

,

]

2.4.
Time decay estimates

Proposition 2.3

(Time decay of amplitudes)
.

Let
\theta\in\{+,-\}
. Assume
that
\psi
satisfies a priori assumption (
2.20
),
for given
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
T
. Then there exists
C

satisfying that for
0\leq k\leq 20
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{W^{k,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-2+\beta+{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1},

(2.22)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
\beta,\delta
are in (
2.20
).

Remark 2.4
.

In view of the parameter condition (
2.21
), for arbitrary small
\beta,{\delta}>0
, we can choose the regularity
n
. Indeed, the global bound results (
2.22
) is almost optimal under the sufficiently high regularity condition.

Proof.

Let us first consider the (
2.22
). We write

\psi_{\theta}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi,

where

\displaystyle\phi_{\theta}(\xi):={-\theta}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle+\xi\cdot\frac{x}{t}.

(2.23)

If
t\lesssim 1
, then by Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle D\right\rangle^{k}\psi_{\theta}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}|\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)|d\xi\lesssim\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k-n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\theta}\|_{H^{n}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{\textbf{D}}}.

Hence it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\psi_{\theta}(t)\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\lesssim t^{-2+\beta+{\delta}}{\varepsilon}_{1}.\end{aligned}

for
t\gg 1
. To this end, we take a frequency decomposition of
\psi_{\theta}
such that

\psi_{\theta}(t,x)=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi.

Then we get

\displaystyle t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\left|\left\langle D\right\rangle^{k}\psi_{\theta}(t,x)\right|\leq\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x),

where

I_{N}(t,x)=t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\right|.

The proof is based on the stationary phase method. We first decompose the frequency support as

\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{N}(t,x)=\left(\sum_{N\leq t^{-1}}+\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}+\sum_{t^{-1}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}\right)I_{N}(t,x).

The high and low-frequency part can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{N\leq t^{-1}}+\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}\right)I_{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{N\leq t^{-1}}+\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}\right)t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{k}\|\rho_{N}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{N\leq t^{-1}}+\sum_{N\geq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}\right)t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}N^{2}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{k-n}\|\psi_{\theta}\|_{H^{n}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Let us now focus on the mid-frequency part. To prove this part, we use both the non-stationary and stationary
phase methods. From (
2.23
), we have

\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)=-\theta\frac{\xi}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}+\frac{x}{t}.

Then, when
|x|\geq t
, the phase
\phi_{\theta}
is non-stationary. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)|\gtrsim\left|\frac{|x|}{t}-\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\right|\gtrsim 1-\frac{|\xi|}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}\gtrsim\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{-2}.

(2.24)

On the other hand, the phase
\phi_{\theta}
could be stationary around

\xi_{0}
when
|x|<t
:

\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi_{0})=0\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\xi_{0}=-\theta\frac{x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}.

We now set
|\xi_{0}|\sim N_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
N\nsim N_{0}
, then the phase is non-stationary and simple calculation yields that

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}(\xi)\Big|\gtrsim\max\left(\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}},\frac{|\xi-\xi_{0}|}{\left\langle N_{0}\right\rangle^{3}}\right).

(2.25)

Let
\mathbf{p}_{\phi}=\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}}{|\nabla_{\xi}\phi_{\theta}|^{2}}
. Then we write the mid-frequency of

I_{N}(t,x)
by the integration by parts as follows:

t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{4}I_{N}^{j}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{N}^{1}(t,x)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}^{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\,d\xi,\\
I_{N}^{2}(t,x)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\cdot\Big[(\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\Big]\,d\xi,\\
I_{N}^{3}(t,x)&=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\,d\xi,\\
I_{N}^{4}(t,x)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot[(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}]\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\,d\xi.\end{aligned}

(2.26)

By (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we obtain the following bounds independent
of
N_{0}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3}N^{-1},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|+\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5}N^{-2},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot[(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}]\right|&\lesssim\left\langle N\right\rangle^{7}N^{-3}.\end{aligned}

(2.27)

Using (
2.27
) and Sobolev embedding, we see that

\displaystyle\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{6}N^{-2}\left(\left\langle N\right\rangle^{k}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\left\langle N\right\rangle^{k-1}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\left(\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\left\langle N\right\rangle^{k-2}\left\|\left(\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{6+k}N^{-2}\left(N^{2}\|x^{2}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}+N^{2}\left\|\widehat{xf_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{4}}+N^{2-4{\varepsilon}}\left\|\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{6+k}N^{-2}\left(N^{2}\|x^{2}f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}+N^{2-4{\varepsilon}}\left\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{2}f_{\theta}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{6+k}\left(1+N^{-4{\varepsilon}}\right){\varepsilon}_{1},

for sufficiently small
0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1
, which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{t^{-1}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}\Big|I_{N}^{1}(t,x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1},

since
\frac{52}{n-2}\leq\beta
. We also obtain, for the
0<{\varepsilon}\ll 1
,

\displaystyle|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|+|I_{N}^{3}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{8+k}N^{-3}\left\|\nabla_{\xi}\left(\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{8+k}N^{-3}\left(N^{3}\left\|\widehat{xf_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{4}}+N^{3-4{\varepsilon}}\left\|\widehat{f_{\theta}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\beta}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{8+k}N^{-4{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t^{-1}\leq N\leq t^{\frac{2}{n-2}}}|I_{N}^{2}(t,x)|+|I_{N}^{3}(t,x)|\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

(2.28)

Note that the estimate (
2.28
) requires that
\frac{56}{n-2}\leq\beta
. As for
I_{N}^{4}
, we can estimate the easier way by using the (
2.27
).

It remains to consider the stationary phase case,
N\sim N_{0}
. We
further decompose
|\xi-\xi_{0}|
into dyadic pieces
L
. Let
L_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}

satisfy that
\frac{L_{0}}{2}<t^{-1}\leq L_{0}
. Then we write

t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\right|\leq\sum_{L=L_{0}}^{2^{10}N}|J_{L}|,

where

\displaystyle J_{L}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{\leq L_{0}}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\qquad\mbox{when }L=L_{0},\\
&t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi\quad\qquad\mbox{when }L>L_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

The case
L=L_{0}
is the stationary one. The fact
L_{0}\sim t^{-1}
implies that

|J_{L_{0}}|\lesssim t^{2-\beta-{\delta}}L_{0}^{2}\|\rho_{N}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}.

The case
L>L_{0}
returns to non-stationary phase. By the integration by parts, we write
J_{L}(t,x)
as in (
2.26
):

J_{L}(t,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{4}J_{L}^{j}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{L}^{1}(t,x)&=-t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}^{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{2}(t,x)&=-t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\cdot\Big[(\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)\Big]d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{3}(t,x)&=-2t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)\right)d\xi,\\
J_{L}^{4}(t,x)&=-t^{-\beta-{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{it\phi_{\theta}(\xi)}\nabla_{\xi}\cdot[(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}]\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{k}\rho_{L}(\xi-\xi_{0})\rho_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f_{\theta}}(\xi)d\xi.\end{aligned}

Each
J_{L}^{j}
can be estimated similarly to
I_{N}^{j}
with the following phase
bounds:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim L^{-1}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|+\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi}\right|&\lesssim L^{-1}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{2}+L^{-2}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{3},\\
\left|\nabla_{\xi}\cdot[(\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{\phi})\mathbf{p}_{\phi}]\right|&\lesssim L^{-2}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{6}+L^{-3}\left\langle N\right\rangle^{5}.\end{aligned}

This finishes the proof of time decay of Dirac part (
2.22
).
∎

Proposition 2.5

(Time decay of amplitudes for Maxwell part)
.

Let
\mu=0,\cdots,4
and
\kappa\in\{+,-\}
. Assume
that
A_{\mu}
satisfies a priori assumption (
2.20
),
for given
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
T
. Then there exists
C

satisfying that for
0\leq s\leq 20
,

\displaystyle\|A_{\mu,\kappa}(t)\|_{W^{s,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\langle t\right\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1},

(2.29)

for any
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

By the same argument as the proof of time decay of spinor, we may assume that
t\gg 1
. We will show that

\displaystyle\|A_{\mu,{\kappa}}(t)\|_{W^{s,\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{1},

for
{\kappa}\in\{+,-\}
. We take a frequency decomposition for

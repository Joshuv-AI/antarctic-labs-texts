Pseudodifferential operators on time-frequency invariant Banach spaces and applications to Gabor Frames
Gianluca Garello, Alessandro Morando

Abstract
. Starting from the study of pseudodifferential operators with completely periodic symbols, we obtain results of continuity and invertibility of a class of Gabor operators on time-frequency invariant Banach spaces. As an applications we find sufficient conditions for the existence of Gabor frames on L 2 L^{2} , associated to a general lattice L ​ ℤ 2 ​ d L\mathbb{Z}^{2d} , where L L is an invertible square matrix.



Keywords.
Frame Theory, Gabor Frames, Pseudodifferential Operators .

1.
Introduction

Let us consider a continuous signal

f=f(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(where
t
stands for “time”). The continuous
Time-Frequency
(TF) representation of
f
is

(1)

f=\frac{1}{(g,\gamma)}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V_{g}f(x,\omega)M_{\omega}T_{x}\gamma\,dx\,d\omega\,,\quad g,\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\,(g,\gamma)\neq 0.

In (
1
),
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with norm
\|f\|^{2}_{2}:=(f,f)
and
V_{g}f(x,\omega):=(f,M_{\omega}T_{x}g)
is the
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT) with respect to the
analysis window

g=g(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

M_{\omega}T_{x}h(t):=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}h(t-x)
is the
time-frequency shift
of a function
h=h(t)
, with parameter
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, whereas
\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the so-called
reconstruction (synthesis) window
.

Formula (
1
) looks as a representation of the signal
f
as a continuous superposition of time-frequency shifts of the window
\gamma
with “coefficients”provided by the STFT of
f
with respect to the window
g
. This is a fundamental counterpart of the Fourier inversion formula (which is just a
frequency
representation of a signal
f
) where the involved elementary functions
M_{\omega}T_{x}\gamma
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Due expecially to numerical purposes, one of the main task of Time Frequency Analysis is replacing the
continuous
representation in (
1
) by an analogous
discrete
representation in terms of countably many shifts
M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}\gamma
, for
(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where the STFT
V_{g}f
is sampled at the
discrete lattice
made by points
(\alpha h,\beta k)
, with the
width parameters

\alpha,\beta>0
, that is

(2)

f=\sum\limits_{h,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(f,M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g)M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}\gamma\,.

Differently from the continuous case where
g
and
\gamma
do not need to satisfy any condition unless
(g,\gamma)\neq 0
, in order to get the discrete representation formula (
2
) the window functions
g
and
\gamma
cannot be chosen arbitrarily. First, one needs that the analysis window
g
and the width parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
are taken such that
\sum\limits_{h,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|(f,M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g)|^{2}
is equivalent to
\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
; under such assumption on
g
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, it is possible to find a proper reconstruction window
\gamma
(called a
dual window
of
g
), obeying the same conditions as
g
and making (
2
) to be satisfied, see
[
24
, Proposition 5.2.1]
. This naturally leads to the study of
Gabor frames
. For a nontrivial
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
and given positive numbers
\alpha,\beta
, the system

(3)

\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta):=\{M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g\,,\,\,(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

is said to be a
Gabor frame
provided that for some constants
A,B>0
:

(4)

A\|f\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum\limits_{h,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|(f,M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g)|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}_{2}\,,\quad\forall\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

A classical problem in Time Frequency Analysis is to find conditions on triples
(g,\alpha,\beta)
under which (
3
) is a Gabor frame. The available literature devoted to such a problem is wide; among the others, we recall that a necessary condition for a system (
3
) to be a Gabor frame is that
\alpha\beta\leq 1
, see
[
14
]
,
[
24
]
and, for a detailed survey on these results,
[
29
]
. Gröchenig - Stöckler
[
28
]
are able to show that (
3
) is a Gabor frame if and only if
\alpha\beta<1
, in the case when the window
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
totally positive function of finite type at least two
. We also quote Dai - Sun
[
13
]
, where the authors focus on the characteristic function
\chi_{[0,c]}
of a real interval and study the problem of finding necessary or sufficient conditions on the parameters
\alpha,\beta,c>0
such that
\mathcal{G}(\chi_{[0,c]},\alpha,\beta)
is a Gabor frame.

For every system
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
one can formally define the
Gabor operator
as

(5)

S_{g}f:=\sum\limits_{h,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(f,M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g)M_{\beta k}T_{\alpha h}g\,,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
is a Gabor frame if and only
S_{g}
is invertible as a linear bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see
[
30
]
,
[
5
]
.
In this case there exists
\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\alpha,\beta)
is still a Gabor frame, said
dual frame
of
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
. Moreover the discrete representation (
2
) holds true for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with unconditional convergence. More precisely one can take
\gamma=S^{-1}g
, see for instance
[
24
, Proposition 5.2.1]
.
The above considerations motivate the interest in studying the
L^{2}
boundedness and invertibiliy of the Gabor operator (
5
).

Let us notice that in
[
5
]
the Gabor operator (
5
) is expressed in terms of a pseudodifferential operator with Kohn-Nirenberg quantization, that is

(6)

S_{g}f(x)=\int e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}p(x,\omega)\widehat{f}(\omega)d\omega\,,

where the symbol
p(x,\omega)
reads as

(7)

p(x,\omega):=\sum_{h,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q(x+\alpha h,\omega+\beta k)\quad\mbox{with}\quad q(x,\omega):=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}g(x)\overline{\widehat{g}}(\omega).

The periodicity of the symbol in both
x
and
\omega
assures that
p(x,\omega)
is bounded, but prevents any decay behavior at infinity. Therefore applying to (
6
)
Calderón-Vaillancourt Theorem
of
L^{2}-
boundedness, in
[
5
]
the authors find sufficient conditions on
g
, and on
\alpha
,
\beta
such that
S_{g}
is continuous and invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Concerning the analysis of pseudodifferential operators with partially periodic symbols, we quote
[
43
]
, where pseudodifferential operators with symbols on compact Lie groups are studied; this includes symbols
p(x,\omega)
with periodic
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and discrete
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The converse case of
p(x,\omega)
discrete in
x
and periodic in
\omega
, which corresponds to the so called
pseudo-difference operators
are studied
[
6
]
and
[
39
]
. Fourier multipliers with periodic symbol
p(\omega)
were earlier analyzed in
[
15
]
,
[
34
]
.
L^{2}
boundedness and invertility of pseudodifferential operators with symbols
p(x,\omega)
periodic both in
x
and
\omega
are studied in our previous work
[
21
]
.

For
q(x,\omega)
such that the series in (
7
) is convergent, we say
p(x,\omega)
“periodization”of
q(x,\omega)
.

Let us observe that the lattice widths
\alpha,\beta
satisfying the condition in
[
5
]
are too small, also in one dimensional case, for having any computational utilities.

The aim of this paper is to give results of continuity and invertibility for pseudodifferential operators with
periodized
symbol and provide applications for finding conditions on the the window
g
and the lattice widths
\alpha,\beta
for a Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
to be a Gabor frame. The matter is studied in the framework of generalized
L
-periodic symbols, for a general invertible matrix
L\in GL(2d)
, that is
T_{L\kappa}p=p
, for all
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
.
In this framework a key role is played by the Poisson summation formula, which is a classical tool of the time-frequency analysis, see
[
24
, §1.4]
and the references given there.

The paper develops as follows. In Section
2
we introduce and discuss the technical tools needed later on. Particularly we define the periodic distributions with respect to a generic invertible matrix. Moreover we introduce the periodization of functions and focus on the generalized Poisson formula. In Section
3
we introduce the pseudodifferential operators with general
0\leq\tau\leq 1
quantization and completely periodic symbol, moreover we recall the related continuity and invertibility results obtained in
[
21
]
. Such results are then applied to the case of
periodized
symbols
q_{L}(x,\omega)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}q(z+L\kappa)
,
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. In Section
4
we apply the previous results for obtaining continuity and invertibility for a generalized Gabor operator

(8)

S^{L}_{g,\gamma}u:=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}(u,\pi_{-L\kappa}g)_{L^{2}}\,\pi_{-L\kappa}\gamma\,,

where
\pi_{-L\kappa}
is the time-frequency shift introduced in the next (
13
) and
L
an invertible matrix of order
2d
.

Continuity and invertibility, under suitable assumption on
g,\gamma
, are obtained in general “time-frequency invariant”Banach spaces, see (
61
). The sufficient conditions on
L
,
g
and
\gamma
, found in Section
4
for obtaining the continuity and invertibility of the related Gabor operator, are expressed in implicit terms. Thus in Section
5
we restrict our analysis to the case of diagonal matrix in order to get direct regularity condition on
g
,
\gamma
and on the
volume

|\textup{det}L|
of the matrix, assuring the invertibility of the Gabor operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Applying this result to the case
\gamma=g
, we get a sufficient condition for the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(L,g)
to be a Gabor frame and explicit estimates of the frame bounds
A
and
B
in (
4
), see Corollary
5.3
.

In Appendix
A
the details of the proof of Proposition
4.2
are given.

Notations and tools

In whole the paper we will use the following notations and tools:

•

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{0}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
;

•

x\cdot\omega=\langle x,\omega\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}\omega_{j}
,
x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

•

(f,g)=\int f(x)\bar{g}(x)\,dx
is the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

•

\mathcal{F}f(\omega)=\hat{f}(\omega)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\,dx
is the Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with the well known extension to
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

•

GL(d)
is the space of invertible matrices of size
d\times d
.

In the following we will use in many cases the matrix in
GL(2d)

(9)

\mathcal{J}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-I\\
I&0\end{array}\right),

where here and below
0
and
I
are respectively the
d\times d
zero and unit matrices. Through the matrix
\mathcal{J}
, we define the
symplectic
form, see
[
24
, §9.4]
,

(10)

[z_{1},z_{2}]:=\langle z_{1},\mathcal{J}z_{2}\rangle=x_{2}\cdot\omega_{1}-x_{1}\cdot\omega_{2}\quad,\quad z_{1}=(x_{1},\omega_{1}),\,z_{2}=(x_{2},\omega_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

2.1.
Time frequency shifts (tfs)

For
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
we define the operators:

(11)

\displaystyle T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)

\displaystyle\text{(translation)};

(12)

\displaystyle M_{\omega}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t)

\displaystyle\text{(modulation)},

(13)

\displaystyle\pi_{z}f=M_{\omega}T_{x}f

\displaystyle\text{(time-frequency shift)},

with suitable extension to distributions in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

For
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the next properties easily follow:

(14)

\displaystyle T_{x}M_{\omega}u=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}M_{\omega}T_{x}u,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(T_{x}u)=M_{-x}\mathcal{F}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(T_{x}u)=M_{x}\mathcal{F}^{-1}u,

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(M_{\omega}u)=T_{\omega}\mathcal{F}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(M_{\omega}u)=T_{-\omega}\mathcal{F}^{-1}u,

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\pi_{z}u)=e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\pi_{\mathcal{J}^{T}z}\mathcal{F}u;

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\pi_{z}u)=e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\pi_{\mathcal{J}z}\mathcal{F}^{-1}u.

2.2.
Short-time Fourier transform, Modulation spaces

Definition 2.1
.

For a fixed nontrivial function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the short-time Fourier transform (STFT in the sequel) of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to
g
is defined as

(18)

V_{g}f(z):=(f,\pi_{z}g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\overline{g(t-x)}dt\,,\quad z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,.

It is well-known that for
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
V_{g}f
is a uniformly continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
V_{g}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and

\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{2}}=\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\,,

see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 3.1.1, Corollary 3.2.2]
.

In the context of the definition above the
window function

g
is usually chosen to be a smooth cut-off function on a neighborhood of the origin. However, STFT can be usefully studied with much more irregular window functions
g
or arguments
f
; in principle,
V_{g}f
can be suitably extended to be a distribution in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for general
g,f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, provided the right-hand integral in (
18
) is intended in a formal “weak” sense, see again
[
24
]
for details. Moving along this direction, particular interest is devoted to the analysis of the STFT in the case when
g
or
f
are taken to belong to
weighted modulation spaces
.

The
polynomial weight function

v
is defined for some
s\geq 0
by

(19)

v(z)=v_{s}(z)=(1+|z|)^{s}\,,\quad\forall\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,.

A non negative measurable function
m=m(z)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is said to be a
polynomially moderate
(or
temperate
) weight function if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(20)

m(z_{1}+z_{2})\leq Cv(z_{1})m(z_{2})\quad\text{for all}\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

For other details about weight functions see
[
24
, §11.1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
p,q\in[1,+\infty]
, the
m-
weighted space
L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
consists of all (Lebesgue) measurable functions
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, such that the norm

\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{m}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(x,\omega)|^{p}m(x,\omega)^{p}dx\right)^{q/p}d\omega\right)^{1/q}

is finite (with the expected modification in the case when at least one among
p
or
q
equals
+\infty
).

Definition 2.3
.

For a fixed
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
and
p,q\in[1,+\infty]
, the
m-
weighted modulation space
M^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
V_{g}f\in L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, provided with the natural norm

(21)

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{m}}:=\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{p,q}_{m}}\,.

The definition of the space
M^{p,q}_{m}
is independent of the choice of the window
g
in the STFT, and
M^{p,q}_{m}
turns out to be a Banach space with respect to a norm (
21
) corresponding to any nonzero window
g
(norms (
21
) related to different windows are shown to be equivalent to each other). We definitely address the reader to
[
24
]
and the references therein for a thorough study of weighted modulation spaces and their properties. Following
[
24
]
, in the case of
p=q
we denote
M^{p}_{m}:=M^{p,p}_{m}
and in the
unweighted case
of
m(x,\omega)\equiv 1
we write
M^{p,q}
and
M^{p}
instead of
M^{p,q}
.

2.3.
Frames in Hilbert spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space, with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and norm
\|x\|^{2}=(x,x)
.
A sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is a
frame
if there exist two positive constants
A,B
such that.

(22)

A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|(x,x_{n})|^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and the operator formally defined by
S:x\to Sx=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(x,x_{n})x_{n}
, then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a frame;

ii)

there exist two positive constants
A,B
such that

(23)

A\|x\|^{2}\leq(Sx,x)\leq B\|x\|^{2};

iii)

the operator
S
is a bounded invertible operator in
\mathcal{H}
, with bounded inverse.

More precisely we have
A=\frac{1}{\|S^{-1}\|}
,
B=\|S\|
, where
\|S\|
stands for the operator norm.

For the proof see
[
30
]
,
[
5
]
.

2.4.
Periodic distributions

Consider now and in the whole paper
L=(a_{ij})\in GL(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that a distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
L-
periodic if

(24)

T_{L\kappa}u=u\quad,\quad\text{for any}\quad\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Setting
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}/L\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we identify the set of
L-
periodic distributions with the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
of linear continuous forms on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
. Notice that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For any
L-
periodic
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the distribution
v=u(L\cdot)
is
I_{n}-
periodic, where
I_{n}
is the unit matrix of size
n
; we say in short that
v
is
1-
periodic
. Thus, up to a linear change of variable, we may always reduce to consider the case of
1-
periodic distributions and set
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{T}^{n}_{I_{n}}
.

Any
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
admits the Fourier expansion

(25)

u=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\kappa,L}(u)e^{2\pi i\langle L^{-T}k,\cdot\rangle},

with the Fourier coefficients

(26)

c_{\kappa,L}(u):=c_{\kappa}(u(L\cdot))=\frac{1}{|\textup{det}L|}\langle u,e^{-2\pi i\langle L^{-T}\kappa,\cdot\rangle}\rangle_{\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L}}.

For short in the following we set
c_{\kappa}(u)=c_{\kappa,L}(u)
.

For details about the rigorous calculus of Fourier coefficients in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
see
[
32
, §7.2]
and the Appendix A in
[
21
]
.

Consider
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the set of measurable
L
-periodic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})}=\int_{L[0,1]^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx<\infty
, with obvious modification for the definition of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
.

Then for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
the following:

(27)

f(x)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\kappa}(f)e^{-2\pi iL^{-T}\kappa\cdot x}

holds with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

(28)

c_{\kappa}(f)=\frac{1}{|\textup{det}L|}\int_{L[0,1]^{n}}e^{2\pi iL^{-T}\kappa\cdot x}f(x)\,dx.

Remark 1
.

It can be useful to write the Fourier expansion of
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L})
in terms of the lattice
\Lambda=L\mathbb{Z}^{n}
:

(29)

u=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^{\bot}}\hat{u}(\mu)e^{2\pi i\langle\mu,\cdot\rangle},

with

(30)

\hat{u}(\mu):=\frac{1}{\textup{vol}(\Lambda)}\langle u,e^{-2\pi i\langle\mu,\cdot\rangle}\rangle_{\mathbb{T}^{n}_{L}}.

Here
\Lambda^{\perp}:=L^{-T}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\textup{vol}(\Lambda):=|\textup{det}L|=\text{meas}\,(L[0,1]^{n})
are respectively called dual lattice and volume of
\Lambda
.

2.5.
Periodization and Poisson formula

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
L\in GL(n)
, we call
L
-
periodization
of
f
the function formally defined by

(31)

F_{L}(x):=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(x+L\kappa)\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

Provided that the series in the right-hand side is convergent, at least for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F_{L}
is by construction
L-
periodic.

Later on, it will be useful to refer to the partial sums of the series in the right-hand side of (
31
); thus for any non negative integer
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we set

(32)

F_{L,h}(x):=\sum\limits_{|\kappa|\leq h}f(x+L\kappa)\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

where, hereafter, the
order

|\kappa|
of
\kappa=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is defined by
|\kappa|:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|k_{j}|
.

On the consistence of the definition of the
L-
periodization
F_{L}
, the following result can be proved.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L\in GL(n)
be given. Then the series in the right-hand side of (
31
) is absolutely convergent for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F_{L}\in L^{1}(L[0,1)^{n})
and

(33)

\int_{L[0,1]^{n}}F_{L}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx\,.

Moreover, for any real number
N>n
, we have
F_{L}(x)(1+|x|)^{-N}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(34)

Lemma 2.1

Let

be an

periodic function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

. Then

for any real number

Proof.

Without loss of generality let us consider a
1-
periodic function

, with
Q:=[0,1]^{n}
the unit cube of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Q_{\kappa}:=\kappa+Q
, for every
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. By the countable-additivity of Lebesgue’s integral we get, for any

,

(35)

\int|G(x)|(1+|x|)^{-N}dx=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{Q_{\kappa}}|G(x)|(1+|x|)^{-N}dx\,.

On the other hand for

(that is

for all

)

|x|=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2})^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2})^{n-1}}(|\kappa|-n)\,,

hence splitting the sum in the right-hand side of (

(37)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}&\int_{Q_{\kappa}}|G(x)|(1+|x|)^{-N}dx\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{|\kappa|\leq n}\int_{Q_{\kappa}}|G(x)|dx+({\sqrt{2}})^{n-1}\sum\limits_{|\kappa|>n}\frac{1}{(|\kappa|-n)^{N}}\int_{Q_{\kappa}}|G(x)|dx\,.\end{split}

The

and a change of variable under the integral give

\int_{Q_{\kappa}}|G(x)|\,dx=\int_{\kappa+Q}|G(x+\kappa)|\,dx=\|G\|_{L^{1}(Q)}\,,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,,

hence

(39)

\int|G(x)|(1+|x|)^{-N}dx\leq\left(\sum\limits_{|\kappa|\leq n}1+({\sqrt{2}})^{n-1}\sum\limits_{|\kappa|>n}\frac{1}{(|\kappa|-n)^{N}}\right)\|G\|_{L^{1}(Q)}\,.

Since

, the series in right-hand side above is convergent. Thus the proof ends by observing that, for some positive constant

depending only on

∎

Proof of Proposition

Remark 2
.

In order to stay in the classical setup of rapidly decreasing functions and tempered distributions, when dealing with modulation spaces, we reduce our previous study to the case when

, is a polynomial weight (

). However, weighted modulation spaces can be defined even for more general types of non polynomial weight functions, that are only sub-multiplicative, namely satisfying

This allows e.g. weight functions which exhibit an exponential growth at infinity.

One way to make such an extension is the one indicated by Gröchenig
[

, Chapter 11.4]

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

An alternative approach is the one resorting to the Björck’s theory of ultradistributions
[
2
]
, where the modulation spaces are recovered as subspaces of ultradistributions under suitable Gelfand-Shilov type growth conditions
[
22
]
. Along this second approach, Dimovski et al.
[
16
]
introduced a notion of translation-modulation shift invariant spaces, generalizing to the framework of ultradistributions the notion of time-frequency shift invariant spaces considered in the present paper, see the definition above. It is likely expected that our main results in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
could be extended to the case of non polynomial weight functions, by working in the more general setting of “tempered”ultradistristributions introduced in
[
16
]
, instead of standard tempered distributions in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

such that

(77)

\gamma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{g}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and let
q(x,\omega)
be the symbol defined by

(78)

q(x,\omega):=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\gamma\otimes\bar{\hat{g}})(x,\omega)=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\gamma(x)\bar{\hat{g}}(\omega)\,,\quad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,,

so that
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

For a given matrix
L\in GL(2d)
, we define the
L-
periodic symbol

(79)

q_{L}(x,\omega)=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}q(x+I_{1}L\kappa,\omega+I_{2}L\kappa)\,,\quad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,,

where
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are the
d\times 2d
matrices defined block-wise as

(80)

I_{1}:=(I\,\,0)\,,\qquad I_{2}:=(0\,\,I)\,,

and
I
and
0
are respectively the
d\times d
unit and zero matrices.
q_{L}(x,\omega)
is just the
L-
periodization of
q(x,\omega)
, see (
67
).

Following Boggiatto-Garello
[
5
]
, we consider the pseudodifferential operator
q_{L}(\cdot,D)
with Kohn-Nirenberg quantization (i.e.
\tau=0
) and symbol
q_{L}(x,\omega)
.
q_{L}(\cdot,D)
associates to any rapidly decreasing function
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the tempered distribution defined by the formal integral

(81)

q_{L}(x,D)u(x)=\int e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}q_{L}(x,\omega)\hat{u}(\omega)d\omega\,,

namely

(82)

\begin{split}\langle q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle:&=\iint e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}q_{L}(x,\omega)\varphi(x)\hat{u}(\omega)dxd\omega\\
&=\langle q_{L}(x,\omega),e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\,,\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.\end{split}

Substituting (
79
) in (
82
), since the series in the right-hand side of (
79
) is convergent in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we compute

(83)

\begin{split}\langle&q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle=\langle q_{L}(x,\omega),e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle q(x+I_{1}L\kappa,\omega+I_{2}L\kappa),e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle e^{-2\pi i(x+I_{1}L\kappa)\cdot(\omega+I_{2}L\kappa)}(\gamma\otimes\bar{\hat{g}})(x+I_{1}L\kappa,\omega+I_{2}L\kappa),e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle e^{-2\pi i(x\cdot I_{2}L\kappa+I_{1}L\kappa\cdot\omega+I_{1}L\kappa\cdot I_{2}L\kappa)}(\gamma\otimes\bar{\hat{g}})(x+I_{1}L\kappa,\omega+I_{2}L\kappa),(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\\
&=\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle e^{-2\pi iI_{1}L\kappa\cdot I_{2}L\kappa}(M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}\gamma)\otimes(M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}}),(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle(T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\gamma)\otimes(M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}}),(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})\rangle\\
&=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\gamma,\varphi\rangle\,\langle M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}},\hat{u}\rangle\,,\\
\end{split}

obtaining

(84)

\langle q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\langle T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\gamma,\varphi\rangle\,\langle M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}},\hat{u}\rangle\,,

where all the involved (numerical) series above are convergent and we made use of the fundamental identity
T_{x}M_{\omega}=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}M_{\omega}T_{x}
, see (
14
). Let us focus on the second test
\langle M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}},\hat{u}\rangle
. Using the identities (
15
), (
16
),
\bar{\hat{f}}=\hat{\tilde{\bar{f}}}
, where
\tilde{f}(t):=f(-t)
is the symmetric of
f=f(t)
(extended to distributions by duality, as customary) and Parseval’s formula, we get

(85)

\begin{split}\langle&M_{-I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}\bar{\hat{g}},\hat{u}\rangle=\langle\overline{M_{I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}{\hat{g}}},\hat{u}\rangle=(\hat{u},M_{I_{1}L\kappa}T_{-I_{2}L\kappa}{\hat{g}})_{L^{2}}\\
&=(\hat{u},\widehat{T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}g})_{L^{2}}=(u,T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}g)_{L^{2}}\,.\end{split}

Substituting (
85
) into (
84
) and recovering the notation
\pi_{-L\kappa}:=T_{-I_{1}L\kappa}M_{-I_{2}L\kappa}
, we have found

(86)

\langle q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}(u,\pi_{-L\kappa}g)_{L^{2}}\,\langle\pi_{-L\kappa}\gamma,\varphi\rangle\,,\quad\forall\,u\,,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

with convergent series in the right-hand side, that is (up to a change of sign of the summation index)

(87)

q_{L}(\cdot,D)u=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}(u,\pi_{L\kappa}g)_{L^{2}}\pi_{L\kappa}\gamma\,,\quad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

with series convergent in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We can then state the following

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider
g,\gamma
measurable functions such that
\gamma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\hat{g}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
L\in GL(2d)
. Then

(88)

S_{g,\gamma}^{L}u=q_{L}(\cdot,D)u\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the symbol
q_{L}(x,\omega)
is defined in (
79
).

Considering the Fourier transform of
q(x,\omega)
in (
78
), from Fubini’s Theorem and Fourier Inversion formula we easily get

(89)

\hat{q}(\eta,z)=V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}(\eta,z))\,\quad\forall\,(\eta,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,,

where
\mathcal{J}
is the matrix defined in (
9
).

Indeed, for arbitrary
\eta
,
z
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we compute

\begin{split}\hat{q}(\eta,z)&=\iint e^{-2\pi i(\eta\cdot x+z\cdot\omega)}q(x,\omega)dxd\omega=\iint e^{-2\pi i(\eta\cdot x+z\cdot\omega)}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\gamma(x)\bar{\hat{g}}(\omega)dxd\omega\\
&=\int e^{-2\pi i\eta\cdot x}\gamma(x)\left(\int e^{-2\pi i(x+z)\cdot\omega}\bar{\hat{g}}(\omega)d\omega\right)dx\\
&=\int e^{-2\pi i\eta\cdot x}\gamma(x)\left(\overline{\int e^{2\pi i(x+z)\cdot\omega}\hat{g}(\omega)d\omega}\right)dx\\
&=\int e^{-2\pi i\eta\cdot x}\gamma(x)\overline{g(x+z)}dx=\int\gamma(x)\overline{M_{\eta}T_{-z}g(x)}dx\\
&=(\gamma,M_{\eta}T_{-z}g)_{L^{2}}=V_{g}\gamma(-z,\eta)=V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}(\eta,z))\,\quad\forall\,(\eta,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\,.\end{split}

Notice in particular that setting
z=\eta=0
in (
89
) we get

(90)

\hat{q}(0,0)=(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}\,.

Now we apply Poisson’s formula (
55
) to
q(x,\omega)
to get

(91)

q_{L}(x,\omega)=\frac{1}{|detL|}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}\hat{q}(L^{-T}\kappa)e^{2\pi iL^{-T}\kappa\cdot(x,\omega)}\,,

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
of the series in the right-hand side, and use

(92)

\hat{q}(L^{-T}\kappa)=V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\,,\quad\forall\,\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}\,.

In order to make the subsequent computations, let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be real matrices of size
2d\times d
such that

(93)

L^{-1}=B=(B_{1}\,\,B_{2})\,,

so that for
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
and
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

(94)

L^{-T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B_{1}^{T}\\
B_{2}^{T}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad L^{-T}\kappa\cdot(x,\omega)=B_{1}^{T}\kappa\cdot x+B_{2}^{T}\kappa\cdot\omega\,.

For arbitrary
u,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we compute

(95)

\begin{split}\langle&q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle=\langle q_{L}(x,\omega),e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}(\varphi\otimes\hat{u})(x,\omega)\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{|detL|}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\iint e^{2\pi iL^{-Y}\kappa\cdot(x,\omega)}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\omega}\varphi(x)\hat{u}(\omega)dxd\omega\\
&=\frac{1}{|detL|}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\iint e^{2\pi i(B_{1}^{T}\kappa\cdot x+B_{2}^{T}\kappa\cdot\omega)}e^{2\pi x\cdot\omega}\varphi(x)\hat{u}(\omega)dxd\omega\,,\end{split}

where all (numerical) series occurring above are convergent, because of the convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
of the distribution series in the right-hand side of (
91
). By Fubini’s Theorem and Fourier’s Inversion formula, the double integrals under the summation above can be recast as

(96)

\begin{split}&\iint e^{2\pi i(B_{1}^{T}\kappa\cdot x+B_{2}^{T}\kappa\cdot\omega)}e^{2\pi x\cdot\omega}\varphi(x)\hat{u}(\omega)dxd\omega\\
&=\int e^{2\pi iB_{1}^{T}\kappa\cdot x}\left(\int e^{2\pi i(B_{2}^{T}\kappa+x)\cdot\omega}\widehat{u}(\omega)d\omega\right)\varphi(x)dx\\
&=\int e^{2\pi iB_{1}^{T}\kappa\cdot x}T_{-B_{2}^{T}\kappa}u(x)\varphi(x)dx=\int M_{B_{1}^{T}\kappa}T_{-B^{T}_{2}\kappa}u(x)\varphi(x)dx\\
&=\langle M_{B_{1}^{T}\kappa}T_{-B^{T}_{2}\kappa}u,\varphi\rangle=\langle\pi_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa}u,\varphi\rangle\,.\end{split}

where
\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa=(-B_{2}^{T}\kappa,B_{1}^{T}\kappa)
, in view of (
94
). By Substituting (
96
) into (
95
) we end up with

(97)

\langle q_{L}(\cdot,D)u,\varphi\rangle=\frac{1}{|detL|}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\langle\pi_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa}u,\varphi\rangle\,,

that is for every
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(98)

q_{L}(\cdot,D)u=\frac{1}{|detL|}\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\pi_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa}u\,,

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the series in the right-hand side.

Formula (
98
) is just Janssen’s representation of the Gabor frame operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}=q_{L}(\cdot,D)
, see
[
24
]
. Let us notice that both representations (
87
) and (
98
) of the Gabor frame operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
hold true under the regularity assumption (
77
) alone on the windows
\gamma,g
.

4.1.
Weighted Wiener spaces

Recall that
Q:=[0,1]^{n}
is the
unit cube
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
\chi_{r}=\chi_{r}(x)
denotes the characteristic function of
Q_{r}:=Q+r
, that is

\chi_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}1\,,\quad\mbox{if}\,\,x\in Q_{r}\,,\\
0\,,\quad\mbox{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Let
v=v(x)
be a polynomial weight function as in (
19
); the following inequality

(99)

v(x)\leq M_{v}v(r)\,,\quad\forall\,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,,\,\,\forall\,x\in Q_{r}\,,

holds true, where
M_{v}:=\max\limits_{z\in Q}v(z)
; indeed it is enough to observe that any
x\in Q_{r}
can be decomposed as
x=z+r
, for some
z\in Q
, hence sub-multiplicativity yields

v(x)=v(z+r)\leq v(z)v(r)\,;

and (
99
) follows at once from the definition of
M_{v}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The weighted Wiener space
W(L^{1}_{v})
is defined to be the class of all functions
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(100)

\|f\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}:=\sum\limits_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}v(r)\|\chi_{r}f\|_{L^{\infty}}

is finite.

It is well-known that
W(L^{1}_{v})
is a Banach space with respect to the norm (
100
), see e.g.
[
24
]
. Let
W_{0}(L^{1}_{v})
denote the (closed) subspace of
W(L^{1}_{v})
consisting of all its continuous elements.

Remark 4
.

In view of (
99
), it is clear that a norm equivalent to (
100
) could be obtained by replacing
v(r)
with the value of
v
at any point of the cube
Q_{r}
different of
r
, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

The following result deals with the sampling of a function
f\in W_{0}(L^{1}_{v})
on a discrete lattice
\Lambda:=L\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for a real matrix
L\in GL(n)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every real matrix
L\in GL(n)
a positive constant
C_{L,v}
, depending only on
L
and the weight function
v
, exists such that for all
f\in W_{0}(L^{1}_{v})
:

(101)

\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}v(L\kappa)|f(L\kappa)|\leq C_{L,v}\|f\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}\,.

Precisely

(102)

C_{L,v}:=M_{v}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}\right|\right]+1\right)\,,

where
(a^{ij})
is the cofactor matrix of
L
and
M_{v}
is the constant in (
99
).

The proof of Proposition
4.2
is detailed in Appendix
A
.

In the remaining part of this section
X
is a time-frequency invariant Banach space as defined in Definition
3.1
.

4.2.
Boundedness of Gabor operators

As an application of Proposition
3.1
to the symbol
q_{L}(x,\omega)
defined as in (
78
) we get the following

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\gamma=\gamma(t)
and
g=g(t)
measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the assumption (
77
) and let
L\in GL(2d)
be such that

(103)

\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}:=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}v\left(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa\right)|V_{g}\gamma(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)|<+\infty\,.

Then the Gabor operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
estends to a linear bounded operator in
\mathcal{L}(X)
and the operator norm of
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
enjoys the estimate

(104)

\|S^{L}_{g,\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\frac{C}{|{\rm det}L|}\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}\,,

being
C>0
the constant in (
61
).

As a consequence of Proposition
4.3
we have the following result

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume that
\gamma,g\in M^{1}_{v}
. Then for every
L\in GL(2d)
the Gabor operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
extends to a linear bounded operator in
\mathcal{L}(X)
; as such, the operator norm of
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
satisfies the following estimate

(105)

\|S^{L}_{g,\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\|g\|_{M^{1}_{v}}\|\gamma\|_{M^{1}_{v}}\,,

with
C>0
depending only on the weight
v
.

Proof.

From
[
24
, Proposition 12.1.4]
we know that
g,\gamma\in M^{1}_{v}
implies that
\gamma\in L^{1}_{u_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\hat{g}\in L^{1}_{u_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

(106)

u_{1}(x):=\inf\limits_{\omega}v(x,\omega)\qquad u_{2}(\omega):=\inf\limits_{x}v(x,\omega)\,.

Since
v
(hence
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
) are polynomial weights,
u_{1}\geq 1
and
u_{2}\geq 1
. It follows at once that
\gamma
and
\hat{g}
belong to the space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as required by Proposition (
4.3
).

[
24
, Proposition 12.1.11]
also gives that
V_{g}\gamma\in W(L^{1}_{v})
, with the estimate

(107)

\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}\leq C\|g\|_{M^{1}_{v}}\|\gamma\|_{M^{1}_{v}}\,,

with some positive constant
C
independent of
g
and
\gamma
. For the sequel, it is useful recalling that

(108)

\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}=\sum\limits_{(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}v(r,s)\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}\,,

where we set
Q_{(r,s)}:=(r,s)+Q
, being
Q:=[0,1]^{2d}
the unit cube, and
\chi_{(r,s)}
is the characteristic function of the cube
Q_{(r,s)}
.

Since
V_{g}\gamma
is also a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
(for it is the Fourier transform of
q_{L}(x,\omega)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
), applying Proposition
4.2
with the matrix
\mathcal{J}L^{-T}
instead of
L
, we get the convergence of the series in (
103
), as required in the statement of Proposition
4.3
; moreover for the sum
\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}
we get the estimate

(109)

\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}\leq C_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T},v}\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}\,,

where (
102
) (with
\mathcal{J}L^{-T}
replaced by
L
) provides an explicit (though involved) dependence on
L
and
v
of the contant
C_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T},v}
, which, instead, is independent of
g
and
\gamma
.

So we are in the position to apply Proposition
4.3
to conclude that
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
extends to a linear bounded operator in
\mathcal{L}(X)
. Estimate (
105
) then follows from gathering the preceding estimates (
104
), (
107
), (
109
).
∎

4.3.
Invertibility of Gabor operators

As in the preceding section, we take measurable functions
g=g(t)
and
\gamma=\gamma(t)
satisfying assumption (
77
). We seek conditions on
L
and the window functions
g
and
\gamma
ensuring the invertibility of the Gabor frame operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
as a linear bounded operator in
\mathcal{L}(X)
.

From stating Proposition
3.2
for the symbol
q_{L}(x,\omega)
defined by (
78
) through
g
and
\gamma
(recall in particular (
89
), (
90
)) we get the following

Proposition 4.4
.

Consider
g
and
\gamma
satisfying (
77
),
(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}\neq 0
and
L\in GL(2d)
such that

(110)

\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}v(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa\right)\right|<\frac{1}{C}\left|(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}\right|\,,

where
C
is the constant in (
61
). Then
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
is invertible as an element of
\mathcal{L}(X)
. The norm of the inverse operator satisfies

(111)

\|(S_{g,\gamma}^{L})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\dfrac{|\textup{det}L|}{\left(1+C\right)|(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}|-C\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}},

where
\sigma_{L,g,\gamma}
is defined in (
103
) and
C
is again the constant in (
61
).

Remark 5
.

Notice that condition (
103
), ensuring that
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
extends to an element of
\mathcal{L}(X)
, trivially follows from (
110
).

Corollary 4.2
.

Consider
L\in GL(2d)
and
g\in L^{1}\setminus\{0\}
, such that
\hat{g}\in L^{1}
, which satisfy

(112)

\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}v(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa)\left|V_{g}g\left(\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa\right)\right|<\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

then the Gabor system
\mathcal{G}_{L}:=\{\pi_{L\kappa}g\}_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}
is a frame in
L^{2}
, with possible frame bounds

(113)

\begin{array}[]{l}A=\frac{2\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sigma_{L,g}}{|\textup{det}L|};\\
\\
B=\frac{\sigma_{L,g}}{|\textup{det}L|}<\frac{2}{|\textup{det}L|}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{array}

where
\sigma_{L,g}:=\sigma_{L,g,g}
.

5.
The diagonal case

With the aim of making explicit the conditions on
g,\gamma
and
L
for the invertibility of the Gabor operator
S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
we restrict the analysis to the diagonal lattice
\Lambda=L\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
, that is we assume the matrix
L\in GL(2d)
to be a diagonal one

(114)

L=diag(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d})\,,\quad\alpha_{j}\,,\,\,\beta_{j}>0\,,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,j=1,\dots,d\,;

then one computes at once

(115)

L^{-T}=diag\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}},\dots.\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}},\frac{1}{\beta_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta_{d}}\right)

and for every
\kappa=(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}

\mathcal{J}L^{-T}\kappa=\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\,,

where for
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{d})
,
k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
,
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})
,
\beta:=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d})
, we set

\frac{h}{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}},\dots,\frac{h_{d}}{\alpha_{d}}\right)\,,\quad\frac{k}{\beta}:=\left(\frac{k_{1}}{\beta_{1}},\dots,\frac{k_{d}}{\beta_{d}}\right)

We consider now the case where
\gamma,g\in M^{1}_{v}
. Then from
[
24
, Proposition 12.1.11]
we know that
V_{g}\gamma\in W(L^{1}_{v})
and it is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. thus in view of Proposition
4.2
the left-hand side of (
110
) can be estimated by the
W(L^{1}_{v})-
norm of
V_{g}\gamma
. However, since the origin is excluded from the aforementioned summation, by revisiting the arguments used in the proof of Proposition
4.2
we show that actually we do not need the whole norm
\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}
in order to get an upper bound of the series in the left-hand side of (
110
): a sufficiently great number of terms of the series definining such a norm can be dropped out, provided that the
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}
s and
\beta_{j}^{\prime}
s are sufficiently small.

If we look at the proof of Proposition
4.2
the key point is to majorize each of the terms
v\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\right|
in (
110
) by
\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
, with a suitable pair
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
such that the lattice point
\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)
belongs to the cube
Q_{(r,s)}
.

The next technical lemma makes better explicit the matter.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
satisfies

(116)

|r_{j}|<\left[\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\right]\quad\mbox{and}\quad|s_{j}|<\left[\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}\right]\,,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,j=1,\dots,d\,,

then the cube
Q_{(r,s)}
does not contain any point
\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)
with
(h,k)\neq(0,0)
.

Proof.

Consider a pair
(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}
such that
\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\in Q_{(r,s)}
; this means that

(117)

r_{j}\leq-\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}<r_{j}+1\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{j}\leq\frac{h_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}<s_{j}+1\,,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,j=1,\dots,d\,.

From our assumption, at least one component of
h
or
k
must be nonzero. Assume that is
k_{1}\neq 0
and consider first the case where
k_{1}<0
. Let us set for simplicity
b_{1}:=\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}
and
\ell_{1}:=-k_{1}
(so that
\ell_{1}\geq 1
, as it must be an integer number). Then the inequality for
k_{1}
in (
117
) is restated as
r_{1}\leq b_{1}\ell_{1}<r_{1}+1
which yields
b_{1}<r_{1}+1
hence
[b_{1}]+1\leq r_{1}+1
. If otherwise
k_{1}>0
then inequality for
k_{1}
in (
117
) becomes equivalent to
-1-r_{1}<b_{1}k_{1}\leq-r_{1}
which yields
-r_{1}\geq b_{1}
hence
r_{1}\leq-[b_{1}]
. In summary,
k_{1}\neq 0
implies that
|r_{1}|\geq[b_{1}]
. Since the preceding argument applies whatever
k_{j}
or
h_{j}
is taken instead of
k_{1}
(where
r
must be replaced by
s
if the argument is given for some nonzero component of
h
), we conclude that if the cube
Q_{(r,s)}
contains any point
(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}
then there exists at least one index
j\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
such that

(118)

|r_{j}|\geq\left[\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\right]\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad|s_{j}|\geq\left[\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}\right].

∎

In view of the preceding lemma, it appears that some of the series terms involved in the definition of the
\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}
see (
108
), precisely those corresponding to cubes
Q_{(r,s)}
which “lower-left” vertex
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
satisfies

(119)

|r_{j}|<\left[\frac{1}{\alpha}_{j}\right]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|s_{j}|<\left[\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}\right]\,,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,j=1,\dots,d\,,

do not contribute to majorize any term
v\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\right|
of the series in (
110
). Let us denote by
Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}
the union of those cubes
Q_{(r,s)}
whose vertex
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}
satisfies (
119
). Then arguing as above and in view of (
99
) we have

(120)

\begin{split}\sum_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}&v\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\right|\\
&\leq C_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T},v}\left(\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}-\!\!\sum\limits_{(r,s)\in Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}}v(r,s)\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\,.\end{split}

By replacing
L
with
\mathcal{J}L^{-T}
in (
102
) we obtain

(121)

C_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T},v}=M_{v}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left([\alpha_{j}]+1\right)\left([\beta_{j}]+1\right)\,,

where
M_{v}
is the constant in (
99
).
It is clear that in order to make advantage from the estimate above, at least one of the
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
’s must be less or equal than 1; otherwise
\left[\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\right]=\left[\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}\right]=0
for all
j=1,\dots,d
and
Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}
should be empty. Actually, we notice that if we assume
0<\alpha_{j}\leq 1
and
0<\beta_{j}\leq 1
for all
j=1,\dots,d
then we can upper bound
C_{\mathcal{J}L^{-T},v}
in (
121
) by
4^{d}M_{v}
. Since
\sum\limits_{(r,s)\in Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}}v(r,s)\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
can be regarded as a “partial sum” of the series defining the norm
\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}
, if we take all
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}
s and
\beta_{j}^{\prime}
s sufficiently small, the number of terms of
\sum\limits_{(r,s)\in Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}}\!\!v(r,s)\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
becomes arbitrarily large and the corresponding “reminder”

\|V_{g}\gamma\|_{W(L^{1}_{v})}-\!\!\sum\limits_{(r,s)\in Q_{(\alpha,\beta)}}v(r,s)\|\chi_{(r,s)}V_{g}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}

arbitrarily small.

In particular, since the constant
C
in the right-hand side of (
110
) is also independent of
L
(and so of
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
’s), we can take the
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}
s and
\beta_{j}^{\prime}
s small enough so as to make the right-hand side of (
120
) smaller than
\frac{1}{C}|(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}|
, as required in (
110
), therefore the following result holds as a corollary of Proposition
4.4
.

From now on we write
S^{\alpha,\beta}_{g,\gamma}:=S^{L}_{g,\gamma}
, with
L=diag(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d})
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\gamma,g\in M^{1}_{v}
satisfy
(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}\neq 0
and
\theta:=\max\limits_{j=1,\dots,d}\{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\}
. Then there exists
0<\theta_{0}<1
depending only on the weight
v
and
(\gamma,g)_{L^{2}}
such that if
\theta\leq\theta_{0}
then the Gabor operator
S^{\alpha,\beta}_{g,\gamma}
is invertible as an element of
\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Unfortunately it seems that no explicit upper bound of
\theta
is available without imposing additional decay estimates on the growth at infinity of
\gamma
and
g
.

In order to find an explicit upper bound of
\theta
, defined as above, we introduce a suitable decay at infinity of window functions
\gamma
and
g
. We consider the scale of Banach spaces
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as in
[
5
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon
be a positive number. We define
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be the vector space of distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
(1+|\cdot|)^{d+\varepsilon}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, equipped with its natural norm

(122)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}:=\|(1+|\cdot|)^{d+\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\,.

Remark 6
.

It is straightforward to check that the norm (
122
) turns
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into a Banach space. Moreover one can check that
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is included in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with continuous embedding; indeed for all
f\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we get at once

\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\,,

with
C^{2}_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-2(d+\varepsilon)}dx
.
Then the Gabor transform is well defined by (
18
) as a standard Lebesgue integral.

It can be also checked that
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is included, with continuous imbedding, into the Wiener space
W(L^{1})
, see Definition
4.1
with
v\equiv 1
.

In view of the application of Proposition
4.4
, our main goal here is to find suitable decay conditions on
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
in order to provide an explicit bound of the series

(123)

\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\right|\,,

by the sizes
\alpha_{j}
’s,
\beta_{j}
’s of the lattice associated to the Gabor operator
S^{\alpha,\beta}_{g,\gamma}
.

5.1.
Some preparatory results

Here and in the following we set for shortness
T^{d}:=\{0,1\}^{d}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

(124)

x_{j}x^{\alpha}g\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\qquad\forall\,\alpha\in T^{d}\quad\mbox{and}\quad j=1,\dots,d\,.

Then for every integer
k=1,\dots,d

(125)

(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}(1+|x_{k}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+|x_{j}|)|g(x)|\leq 2^{d+1}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g,k}\,,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

where

(126)

\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g,k}:=\max\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left\{\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}},\|x_{k}x^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\right\}\,.

In particular

(127)

(1+|\cdot|)^{d+\varepsilon}g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,

and the following estimate holds

(128)

\|(1+|\cdot|)^{d+\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{1}}\leq d^{d}2^{2d+1}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\,,

with

(129)

\mathcal{H}_{g}^{\varepsilon}:=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq d}\mathcal{H}_{g,k}^{\varepsilon}\,.

Proof.

Let us notice that

(130)

\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}|x^{\alpha}|\,.

Assume, without loss of generality,
k=1
; after (
130
), we compute

(131)

(1+|x_{1}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq 1}(1+|x_{j}|)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}|x^{\alpha}|+2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|x^{\alpha}|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|x_{1}||x^{\alpha}|\,,

hence, by noticing that the number of multi-indices
\alpha\in T^{d}
with either
\alpha_{1}=0
or
\alpha_{1}=1
is
2^{d-1}
, we have

\begin{split}&(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}(1+|x_{1}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq 1}(1+|x_{j}|)|g(x)|=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|x^{\alpha}g(x)|\\
&+2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|x^{\alpha}g(x)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|x_{1}x^{\alpha}g(x)|\leq 2^{d+1}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g,1}\,.\end{split}

Notice that in (
125
) we use the weight
(1+|x_{k}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+|x_{j}|)
,
1\leq k\leq d
instead of the easier weight
\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)
, since this latter is not enough to get integrability of
(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}g
with respect to
x
variable.
The presence of the power two of one factor
(1+|x_{k}|)^{2}
in the former weight allows the
x
-integrability of
(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}g(x)
, for it is enough to provide a bit faster decay at infinity with respect to all of
x
.

For an arbitrary
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
1\leq k\leq d
be the integer number, dependent on
x
, such that

(132)

|x_{k}|=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq d}|x_{j}|\,.

Then

(133)

\begin{split}(1&+|x_{k}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+|x_{j}|)\\
&=(1+|x_{k}|)\underbrace{(1+|x_{k}|)^{1/d}\dots(1+|x_{k}|)^{1/d}}_{d\,\,{\rm times}}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+|x_{j}|)\\
&\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{1+1/d}\end{split}

and from (
125
) we obtain

(134)

(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|g(x)|\leq 2^{d+1}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-1-1/d}\,,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Then (
127
) follows as the function

x\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-1-1/d}

is integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

By integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
both sides of (
134
) and applying Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-1-1/d}dx=\left(2\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+y)^{-1-1/d}dy\right)^{d}=2^{d}d^{d}\,,

which proves (
128
).
∎

Let us now prove the following result

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\gamma,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy

(135)

\begin{split}&x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma,x^{\beta}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma,x_{j}x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\\
&x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}g,x^{\beta}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}g,x_{j}x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}g\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\end{split}

for all
\alpha,\beta\in T^{d}
and every integer
1\leq j\leq d
.
Then, for any
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and any integers
1\leq k\leq d
, there holds

(136)

(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon/2}(1+|\omega_{k}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq k}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d+1}\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}

where

(137)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}:=\max\limits_{\alpha,\beta\in T^{d}\,,\,\,1\leq j\leq d}\left\{\|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}},\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}},\|x_{j}x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\right\}\,;\\
&\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}:=\max\limits_{\alpha,\beta\in T^{d}\,,\,\,1\leq j\leq d}\left\{\|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}},\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}},\|x_{j}x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\right\}\end{split}

Proof.

Applying Lemma
5.2
and the trivial inequality

1+|x|\leq(1+|t|)(1+|t-x|)\,,

we get

(138)

\begin{split}(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|t|)^{d+\varepsilon}|\gamma(t)|(1+|t-x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|g(t-x)|dt\\
\leq\|\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|y|)^{d+\varepsilon}|g(y)|dy\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\|\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\end{split}

Now let us observe that for given
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
is nothing but the Fourier transform of
\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g}
computed at
\omega
, that is

V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)=\mathcal{F}\left(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g}\right)(\omega)\,,

see (
18
). Then for every
\alpha\in T^{d}
the identity

(139)

\omega^{\alpha}V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)=\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g})\right)(\omega)

holds true at least in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\omega})
and point-wise in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then by Leibniz’s formula applied to the product of the
L^{2}
-functions
\gamma
and
T_{x}\overline{g}
, whose derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}\gamma,\partial^{\alpha}T_{x}\overline{g}\equiv T_{x}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}g}
still belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we get

(140)

\begin{split}\omega^{\alpha}V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)&=\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\sum\limits_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\mathcal{F}\left(\gamma^{(\beta)}\cdot T_{x}\overline{g^{(\alpha-\beta)}}\right)(\omega)\\
&=\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\sum\limits_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}V_{g^{(\beta)}}\gamma^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x,\omega)\,.\end{split}

Here we used the shortcut
h^{(\nu)}:=\partial^{\nu}h
to write the partial derivatives of
\gamma
and
g
of order
|\nu|
for any
\nu\in T^{d}
.

By (
130
) and repeating the same arguments used to get estimate (
138
) with
g^{(\beta)}
and
\gamma^{(\alpha-\beta)}
instead of
g
and
\gamma

we have

(141)

\begin{split}&(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\sum\limits_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}|V_{g^{(\beta)}}\gamma^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x,\omega)|\\
&\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\sum\limits_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\|\gamma^{(\beta)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g^{(\alpha-\beta)}}\\
&\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}2^{|\alpha|}=2^{2d+1}d^{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\,.\end{split}

Eventually, we notice that the number of multi-indices of
T^{d}
which have exactly
k
components equal to one is
\binom{d}{k}
, hence

(142)

\sum\limits_{\alpha\in T^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{d}{k}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{k}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d}

and

(143)

(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\,.

In order to get the estimate (
136
), set
k=1
, without loss of generality, and apply (
131
) to
\omega
.
Then the estimate of
(1+|\omega_{1}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq 1}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|
is reduced to the evaluation of

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}|\omega^{\alpha}||V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|\omega^{\alpha}||V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\,,

which thanks again to (
130
) are already included within
\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|
estimated by (
143
), and

(144)

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|\omega^{\alpha}|\,|\omega_{1}||V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|.

Here the multiplier of
|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|
, under summation over multi-indices
\alpha\in T^{d}
such that
\alpha_{1}=1
, includes an additional factor
|\omega_{1}|
, namely
|\omega_{1}|
is involved in
|\omega^{\alpha}||\omega_{1}|
with the power
2
(as
\alpha_{1}=1
).

Arguing similarly as we did before to estimate
|\omega^{\alpha}V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|
for any
\alpha\in T^{d}
, the additional multiplication by
\omega_{1}
turns into a further differentiation of
\gamma(t)\cdot T_{x}\overline{g}(t)
with respect to
t_{1}
under Fourier transform, see (
139
), hence

\begin{split}\omega_{1}\omega^{\alpha}V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)&=\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{1}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g})\right)(\omega)\\
&=\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}\left\{\left[\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\partial_{1}\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g})\right)+\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{\partial_{1}g})\right)\right](\omega)\right\}\,.\end{split}

Now we argue on each of the two terms in the right-hand side above as done for
\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g})\right)(\omega)
, with either
\partial_{1}\gamma
and
g
or
\partial_{1}g
and
\gamma
replacing
\gamma
and
g
respectively. Note that in view of (
125
) both
\partial_{1}\gamma
and
\partial_{1}g
obey the same regularity constraints as
\gamma
and
g
in order have (
141
). In particular, the reason why
\gamma
, and not
\partial_{1}g
, plays the role of
g
in the estimate of
\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{\partial_{1}g})\right)(\omega)
by the same arguments leading to (
141
) is because, in view of (
135
) and Lemma
5.2
,
(1+|\cdot|)^{d+\varepsilon}\partial^{\alpha}\gamma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
\alpha\in T^{d}
; the same could not be true for
\partial_{1}g
without adding to (
135
) the further regularity
x_{k}x^{\beta}\partial_{j}\partial^{\alpha}g\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\alpha,\beta\in T^{d}
and integers
1\leq k,j\leq d
. We then get

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|\omega_{1}||\omega^{\alpha}||V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}\left\{|\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\partial_{1}\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{g})\right)(\omega)|+|\mathcal{F}\left(\partial^{\alpha}(\gamma\cdot T_{x}\overline{\partial_{1}g})\right)(\omega)|\right\}\\
&\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\beta\leq\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\left[\|\partial_{1}\gamma^{(\beta)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{g^{(\alpha-\beta)}}+\|\partial_{1}g^{(\beta)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma^{(\alpha-\beta)}}\right]\\
&\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}2^{|\alpha|+1}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}=2^{2d+1}d^{d}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\end{split}

and analogously to (
142
),

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|+1}=\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|-1}\!\!=\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d-1}\,.

Thus we obtain

(145)

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}|\omega_{1}||\omega^{\alpha}||V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d-1}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\,.

Gathering the preceding estimates we end up with

(146)

\begin{split}&(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}(1+|\omega_{1}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq 1}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\\
&\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\!\right)\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\end{split}

On the other hand

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}&+2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+\left(2+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}\\
&=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+\left(2+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)\frac{1}{\pi}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|-1}\end{split}

and, similar to (
142
),

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|-1}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d-1}\,,

hence

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=0\end{subarray}}&\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|}+\left(2+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)\frac{1}{\pi}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in T^{d}\\
\alpha_{1}=1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{|\alpha|-1}\\
&=\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d-1}\left(1+\left(2+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)\frac{1}{\pi}\right)\\
&=\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d-1}\left(1+\frac{2}{\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\right)=\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d+1}.\end{split}

Inserting the last into (
146
) eventually gives

\begin{split}(1+&|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}(1+|\omega_{1}|)^{2}\prod_{j\neq 1}(1+|\omega_{j}|)|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq 2^{2d+1}d^{d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d+1}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\,,\end{split}

that is (
136
) for
k=1
.
∎

Remark 7
.

As regards to the estimates (
136
), as well as (
143
), the presence of the weight
(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}
in the left-hand side yields a sufficiently fast decay, as
|x|
goes to infinity, to make the function
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
integrable with respect to
x
. The reason why the estimates (
143
) are not enough to our future purposes is that the weight
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)
in the left-hand side of (
143
) does not yield sufficiently fast decay, as
|\omega|
goes to infinity, to provide
\omega
-integrability of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
. Instead, as we already saw in the proof of preceding Lemma
5.2
the presence of one factor
(1+|\omega_{k}|)
with power two in the
\omega
-weight appearing in the left-hand side of (
136
) above allows recovering the
\omega
-integrability of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
, so as to make the latter to be an integrable function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\omega}
.

Repeating all the arguments used in the proof of Lemma
5.2
in order to pass from estimate (
125
) to estimate (
134
), we manage to derive from (
136
)

(147)

|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq C_{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}(1+|x|)^{-d-\varepsilon}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)^{-1-\frac{1}{d}}\,,

where

(148)

C_{d}:=2^{2d+1}d^{d}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\right)^{d+1}\,,

which provides the wished integrability of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\omega}
.

By the calculations above, we have shown that both functions
g
and
\gamma
, obeying the assumptions of Proposition
5.1
, actually belong to
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for the function
g
above (playing the role of window in the Gabor transform
V_{g}\gamma
) could be replaced by any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the role of
\gamma
and
g
in the Gabor transform could be interchanged through the formula
V_{\gamma}g(x,\omega)=e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot x}\overline{V_{g}\gamma(-x,-\omega)}
(recall that both
g
and
\gamma
belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, cf Remark
6
). Therefore, in principle the invertibility of the generalized Gabor operator
S^{\alpha,\beta}_{g,\gamma}
, correponding to any diagonal matrix
L=diag(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d})
with positive numbers
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
’s, could be reducted to the framework of Corollary
5.1
.

However, by making use of the decay estimate (
147
) of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
, we wish to provide an explicit, possibly computable, upper bound of the sizes
\alpha_{j}
,
\beta_{j}
’s of the lattice
\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, ensuring the invertibility of the related Gabor operator
S^{\alpha,\beta}_{g,\gamma}
. In view of Proposition
4.4
, this consists in providing an explicit upper bound for the sum of the series (
123
).

A drawback of estimate (
147
) is the different size and type of decay at infinity of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
, with respect to
x
and to
\omega
.The decay at infinity in
x
is of “homogeneous”type, being measured with respect to
|x|
, with size
d+\varepsilon
, whereas the decay in
\omega
is measured “separately”in each coordinate direction
\omega_{j}
, with size
1+1/d
.

The lack of symmetry of (
147
) could be somehow compensated if we assume some additional regularity to the functions
\gamma
and
g
. The starting point is noticing that whenever
\gamma,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, from Plancherel’s formula and the use of (
14
)-(
16
), we get (see
[
24
, Lemma 3.1.1]
)

\begin{split}V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)&=(\gamma,M_{\omega}T_{x}g)_{L^{2}}=(\hat{\gamma},\widehat{M_{\omega}T_{x}g})_{L^{2}}=(\hat{\gamma},T_{\omega}M_{-x}\hat{g})_{L^{2}}\\
&=e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot x}(\hat{\gamma},M_{-x}T_{\omega}\hat{g})_{L^{2}}=e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot x}V_{\hat{g}}\hat{\gamma}(\omega,-x)\,,\end{split}

thus in particular

(149)

|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|=|V_{\hat{g}}\hat{\gamma}(\omega,-x)|\,.

The above suggests that if we assume, in addition to (
135
) on
\gamma
and
g
, that the Fourier transforms of
\gamma
and
g
satisfy the same of (
135
), i.e.

(150)

\begin{split}&\omega^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{\gamma},\omega^{\beta}\partial_{\omega_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{\gamma},\omega_{j}\omega^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\\
&\omega^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{g},\omega^{\beta}\partial_{\omega_{j}}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{g},\omega_{j}\omega^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}\hat{g}\in L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\end{split}

then, in view of (
149
), the Gabor transform
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
will obey the same decay estimates as (
147
), where the role of
x
and
\omega
is exchanged.

More precisely, from Proposition
5.1
we can deduce the following result.

Corollary 5.2
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
\gamma,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy the assumptions (
135
) and (
150
). Then

(151)

\begin{split}|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq C_{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma,\hat{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g,\hat{g}}&(1+|x|)^{-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(1+|\omega|)^{-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\\
&\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}}(1+|\omega_{j}|)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}}\,,\end{split}

where
C_{d}
is the constant defined in (
148
),

(152)

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma,\hat{\gamma}}:=\left(\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\hat{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g,\hat{g}}:=\left(\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\hat{g}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,\end{split}

\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}
are the constants in (
137
) and
\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\hat{\gamma}}
,
\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\hat{g}}
the same constants with
\hat{\gamma}
,
\hat{g}
instead of
\gamma
,
g
.

Proof.

Starting from (
149
), the same arguments used above lead to the following counterpart of (
147
)

(153)

|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq C_{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g}(1+|\omega|)^{-d-\varepsilon}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-1-\frac{1}{d}}\,,

where
\mathcal{K}_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\varepsilon}
,
\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\hat{g}}
are defined as in (
137
) for
\hat{\gamma}
and
\hat{g}
and
C_{d}
is the constant in (
148
). Then a convex combination of (
147
) and (
153
) gives raise to the estimate in (
151
).
∎

5.2.
Existence of Gabor frames

Although the different decay of
V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)
shown in (
151
) does not prevent integrability in both
x
and
\omega
, considering a single decay with respect to the whole variable
(x,\omega)
will simplify our subsequent calculations. Therefore, we “reduce”the decay associated to the homogeneous weights
1+|x|
,
1+|\omega|
to the somewhat “worser”decay corresponding to the weights
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)
,
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|\omega_{j}|)
. This can be done by the trivial estimate

(154)

(1+|x|)^{d+\varepsilon}=\underbrace{(1+|x|)^{1+\varepsilon/d}\dots(1+|x|)^{1+\varepsilon/d}}_{d\,\,{\rm times}}\geq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{1+\varepsilon/d}\,.

Then from (
151
) we deduce at once

(155)

|V_{g}\gamma(x,\omega)|\leq C_{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma,\hat{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g,\hat{g}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1+|x_{j}|)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}(1+|\omega_{j}|)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\,,

Using (
155
) to estimate each of the summands of the series (
123
), we get

(156)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}&\left|V_{g}\gamma\left(-\frac{k}{\beta},\frac{h}{\alpha}\right)\right|\\
&\leq C_{d}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma,\hat{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}^{\varepsilon}_{g,\hat{g}}\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\left(1+\left|\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}.\end{split}

In order to go on with an estimate of right-hand side above, it is worth to rearrange the terms in the multi-dimensional sum over
(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}
in such a way to be reduced to estimate several one-dimensional elementary sums. Let us make a reordering of the summands in
(h,k)
according to the increasing number of nonzero components of
(h,k)
, that is

(157)

\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\left(1+\left|\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{2d}\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell)}}a_{(h,k)}\,.

In the right-hand side above
a_{(h,k)}
stands for the general term of the sum over
(h,k)
in the left-hand side, that is

a_{(h,k)}:=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\left(1+\left|\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\,,

and for any integer number
1\leq\ell\leq 2d
,
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell)}
denotes the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}
consisting of those multi-integers
(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2d}
which have exactly
\ell
components
h_{j}
’s and
k_{j}
’s different from zero. Therefore

\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{\ell=1}^{2d}\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell)}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell_{1})}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell_{2})}=\emptyset\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,\,\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}\,.

Let us first consider the most simple case of
\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}}a_{(h,k)}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}
is the set of multi-integers
(h,k)
with all components but one components equal to zero. All multi-integers
(h_{1},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{d-1},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{d})
, for every
h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, will belong to
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}
, and for the sum of the related terms we get

(158)

\sum\limits_{h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}a_{(h_{1},0,\dots,0,0,\dots,0)}=\sum\limits_{h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\,,

where the one-dimensional sum in the right-hand side is estimated by

(159)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}&\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}=2\sum\limits_{h_{1}=1}^{+\infty}\left(1+\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\\
&\leq 2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(1+\frac{y}{\alpha_{1}}\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}dy=\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\alpha_{1}\,.\end{split}

The whole set
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}
is obtained by letting only one among the indices
h_{j}
’s,
k_{j}
’s to be different from zero. Thus a similar one-dimensional summation as in (
158
) is involved in
\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}}a_{(h,k)}
, where
h_{1}
and
\alpha_{1}
will be respectively replaced by
h_{2}
,
\alpha_{2}
, …,
h_{d}
,
\alpha_{d}
,
k_{1}
,
\beta_{1}
, …,
k_{d}
,
\beta_{d}
, and will be estimated as in (
159
).

For any integer
j=1,\dots,d
one computes the same as above with
h_{j}
,
\alpha_{j}
or
k_{j}
,
\beta_{j}
replacing
h_{1}
,
\alpha_{1}
, and similarly as before

(160)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}&\left(1+\left|\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\leq\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\beta_{j}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,d\,.\end{split}

In summary, we get

(161)

\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(1)}}a_{(h,k)}\leq\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})\,.

Let us estimate now the sum

(162)

\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(2)}}a_{(h,k)}\,.

Here above
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(2)}
consists, by definition, of all multi-integers
(h,k)
where only two components are different from zero. To fix the ideas, let us consider the case where all components of
h
and
k
, but
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, are equal to zero. Then the sum of the corresponding
a_{(h,k)}
’s becomes

\begin{split}\Sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}:&=\sum\limits_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}a_{(h_{1},h_{2},{\small\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{d-2},\underbrace{0\dots,0}_{d}})}\\
&=\!\!\sum\limits_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\!\!\!\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\!\!\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\,.\end{split}

By interchanging the sum and the product, we rearrange
\Sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}
as follows

\Sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\sum\limits_{h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\!\!\sum\limits_{h_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}

and use again (
159
) for each of the sums over
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
above, to get

(163)

\Sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\,.

It is clear that a similar sum as
\Sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}
is involved within the sum (
162
), corresponding to any way we can select two (and only two) integer components amongst all
h_{j}
’s and
k_{j}
’s, to be different from zero. The corresponding sums, say
\Sigma_{h_{i},h_{j}}
,
\Sigma_{k_{i},k_{j}}
with
1\leq i<j\leq d
or
\Sigma_{h_{i},k_{j}}
with
1\leq i,j\leq d
, will be estimated similarly to (
163
), by

\Sigma_{h_{i},h_{j}}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\,,\quad\Sigma_{k_{i},k_{j}}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\,,\quad\Sigma_{h_{i},k_{j}}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}\,.

Eventually, summing over all different
\Sigma
’s as above we end up with

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(2)}}&a_{(h,k)}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d\,,\,\,j_{1}<j_{2}}\!\!\!\alpha_{j_{1}}\alpha_{j_{2}}\right.\\
&\left.+\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d}\alpha_{j_{1}}\beta_{j_{2}}+\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d\,,\,\,j_{1}<j_{2}}\!\!\!\beta_{j_{1}}\beta_{j_{2}}\right)\,.\end{split}

In a similar way one can go on to estimate each of the sums
\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell)}}a_{(h,k)}
as the integer
\ell
increases from
1
to
2d
. At the general step the resulting estimate will be

(164)

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(\ell)}}a_{(h,k)}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{\ell}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{\ell}\leq d}\!\!\!\alpha_{j_{1}}....\alpha_{j_{\ell}}+\dots+\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{\ell}\leq d}\!\!\!\beta_{j_{1}}....\beta_{j_{\ell}}\right)\,,\end{split}

where the intermediate dots in the right-hand side above are used to mean any other sum of “mixed”products of
\ell
numbers amongst both
\alpha_{j}
’s and
\beta_{i}
’s, occurring in all possible combinations. Let us notice that when the integer
\ell\leq 2d
is larger than
d
then only products of both
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{i}
factors may occur in the right-hand side above.

In particular, as regards to the set
\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(2d)}
of multi-integers
(h,k)
with all their
2d
scalar components different from zero, we will get

\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{(2d)}}a_{(h,k)}\leq\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{2d}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}\,.

According to (
157
), we sum up the estimates (
164
) collected above to get

(165)

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{(h,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}_{0}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\left|\frac{h_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\left(1+\left|\frac{k_{j}}{\beta_{j}}\right|\right)^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2d}}\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{2d}\left(\frac{4d}{1+\varepsilon}\right)^{\ell}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{\ell}\leq d}\!\!\!\alpha_{j_{1}}....\alpha_{j_{\ell}}+\dots+\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{\ell}\leq d}\!\!\!\beta_{j_{1}}....\beta_{j_{\ell}}\right)\,.\end{split}

Let us set
\theta:=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq d}\left\{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\right\}
. Then the products of
\ell
factors
\alpha_{j_{1}}\dots\alpha_{j_{\ell}}
,
\beta_{j_{1}}\dots\beta_{j_{\ell}}
and the mixed products involving both
\alpha_{j}
’s and
\beta_{j}
’s, in all possible combination (hidden behind the dots in the right-hand side above) are always majorized by
\theta^{\ell}

Proof of Proposition

We need some preparatory lemmata.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
[x]
the greatest integer number
\leq x
, so that the inequalities
[x]\leq x<[x]+1
trivially follow.

Lemma A.1
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a\leq b
, let
\#_{a,b}
be the number of integers belonging to the closed interval
[a,b]
. Then
\#_{a,b}\geq 1
if and only if
a\leq[b]
. Under the previous condition, we also have

(174)

\#_{a,b}\leq[b-a]+1\,.

Proof.

As for the first assertion, it is clear from the definition of
[b]
that
a\leq[b]
means that the real interval
[a,b]
contains at least the integer
[b]
; conversely, if
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer belonging to
[a,b]
, the inequalities
a\leq k\leq[b]
trivially follow from definition of
[b]
once again.

Assuming now that
a\leq[b]
, let us turn to the second assertion of the lemma. Inequality (
174
) becomes trivial when
a=b
(in which case the real interval reduces to the singleton
\{b\}
and
[b]\equiv b
is the only integer contained therein). Now for
a<b
let us suppose there are at least
[b-a]+2
subsequent integers that belong to
[a,b]
. This would imply that
[a,b]
should contain at least the
[b-a]+1
unitary sub-intervals bounded by the subsequent pairs of those integer numbers, giving rise to the contradiction
b-a\geq[b-a]+1
. Therefore (
174
) must be true.
∎

From (
100
), it appears rather clear that proving the estimate (
101
) should be strictly related to evaluate the maximum number of points of the lattice
\Lambda:=L\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that belong to the same cube
Q_{r}
with given
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The next result deals with the above question.

In the sequel, let
L\in GL(n)
be any real invertible matrix, which
(i,j)
-th entry will be denoted by
a_{i,j}
for every
i,j=1,\dots,n
. Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the
cofactor matrix
of
L
, which
(i,j)
-th entry will be denoted by
a^{i,j}
for every
i,j=1,\dots,n
. Then as it is well-known

L^{-1}=\frac{1}{detL}\mathcal{L}^{T}.

In agreement to the above, te
(i,j)
-th entry of
L^{-1}
is given by
\displaystyle\frac{a^{j,i}}{detL}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
.

Lemma A.2
.

For a real matrix
L\in GL(n)
and a given vector
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
\#_{L,r}
denote the number of elements of the discrete set

(175)

Z_{L,r}:=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,;\,\,\,L\kappa\in Q_{r}\}\,.

Then we have that

(176)

\#_{L,r}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}\right|\right]+1\right)

Proof.

It is first convenient to notice that whatever is
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the condition

L\kappa\in Q_{r}

is equivalent to

(177)

\kappa\in L^{-1}Q_{r}\,,

in view of the invertibility of
L
, namely
Z_{L,r}
is the set of all
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
belonging to the set
\mathcal{P}_{r}:=L^{-1}Q_{r}
. It is easy to observe that the set
\mathcal{P}_{r}
is parametrized by the following vector equation

(178)

x=L^{-1}(r+t)\,\quad\mbox{for}\,\,\,t:=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in Q=[0,1]^{n}\,,

and geometrically represents the convex
n-
parallelogram, which faces are (generally) transversal to the coordinate hyperplanes of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and which vertices are the
2^{n}
points attained from (
178
) as long as the vector parameter
t
spans the subset
\{0,1\}^{n}
of
Q
. It is then clear that
Z_{L,r}
is included into the parallelogram, say
\mathcal{R}_{r}
, which faces are parallel to the coordinate hyperplanes, generated from the Cartesian product of the
n
real intervals obtained from projecting all vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{r}
orthogonally on each of the coordinate hyperplanes. To go on, first notice that the
j-
th coordinate of all vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{r}
are the values computed from

(179)

x_{j}^{(t)}=\frac{1}{detL}\left(a^{1,j}(r_{1}+t_{1})+\dots+a^{n,j}(r_{n}+t_{n})\right)\,,

for all possible
t=(t_{1}\,\dots,t_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
. If we set

(180)

x_{j}^{\min}:=\min\limits_{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\{x_{j}^{(t)}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad x_{j}^{\max}:=\max\limits_{t\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\{x_{j}^{(t)}\}\,,

then the parallelogram
\mathcal{R}_{r}
, which we are looking for, is defined as

(181)

\mathcal{R}_{r}:=[x_{1}^{\min},x_{1}^{\max}]\times\dots\times[x_{n}^{\min},x_{n}^{\max}]\,;

of course
\mathcal{P}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{r}
, so the number of
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
contained in
\mathcal{R}_{r}
will be certainly greater than or equal to the number
\#_{L,r}
of members of
Z_{L,r}
.

Notice also that for any
j=1,\dots,n
the coefficients
\displaystyle\frac{a^{1,j}}{detL},\dots,\displaystyle\frac{a^{n,j}}{detL}
involved in (
179
) cannot be all zero, otherwise the matrix
L^{-1}
should have some zero row contradicting the invertibility of
L^{-1}
(that is the invertibility of
L
).

Let
n_{+}
,
n_{-}
and
n_{0}
denote respectively the number of positive, negative and zero elements of the
j-
th row
\left\{\displaystyle\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}\,,\,\,\,i=1,\dots,n\right\}
(so that
n=n_{+}+n_{-}+n_{0}
); assuming (without loss of generality) that
n_{\pm}
and
n_{0}
are all strictly positive and

\begin{split}&\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}>0\,,\quad i=1,\dots,n_{+}\,,\\
&\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}<0\,,\quad i=n_{+}+1,\dots,n_{+}+n_{-}\,,\\
&\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}=0\,,\quad i=n_{+}+n_{-}+1,\dots,n_{+}+n_{-}+n_{0}=n\,,\end{split}

then (
179
) reduces to

(182)

x_{j}^{(t)}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{+}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}(r_{i}+t_{i})+\sum\limits_{i=n_{+}+1}^{n_{+}+n_{-}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}(r_{i}+t_{j})\,,

and it is easy to derive

(183)

\begin{split}&x_{j}^{\min}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{+}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}r_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=n_{+}+1}^{n_{+}+n_{-}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}(r_{i}+1)\,,\\
&x_{j}^{\max}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{+}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}(r_{i}+1)+\sum\limits_{i=n_{+}+1}^{n_{+}+n_{-}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}r_{i}\,.\end{split}

In view of Lemma
A.1
, the number of integers belonging to the interval
[x_{j}^{\min},x_{j}^{\max}]
, say
\#_{j}
, satisfies

(184)

\#_{j}\leq[x_{j}^{\max}-x_{j}^{\min}]+1

and for the lenght
x_{j}^{\max}-x_{j}^{\min}
of the interval, we easily derive from (

(185)

x_{j}^{\max}-x_{j}^{\min}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{+}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}-\sum\limits_{i=n_{+}+1}^{n_{+}+n_{-}}\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}\right|\,.

Because of (

belonging to

(186)

\#_{\mathcal{R}_{r}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\frac{a^{i,j}}{detL}\right|\right]+1\right)

from which (
176
) follows at once.

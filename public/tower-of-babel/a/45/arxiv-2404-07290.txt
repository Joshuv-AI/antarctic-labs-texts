Planar RG Flows on Line Defects
Ivri Nagar, Amit Sever, and De-liang Zhong

Abstract
We study a class of renormalization group flows on line defects that can be described by a generalized free field with ordered planar contractions on the line. They are realized, for example, in large N N gauge theories with matter in the fundamental representation and arise generically in non-relativistic CFTs. We analyze the flow exactly and compute the change in the g g -function between the UV and IR fixed points. We relate the result to the change in the two-point function of the displacement operator and check the monotonicity of the defect entropy along the flow analytically. Finally, we give a general realization of this type of flow starting from the direct sum of the IR fixed point and a trivial line. This type of defect renormalization group flow parallels the well-studied case of double-trace flow.

Introduction

We study renormalization group (RG) flows that take place along a line defect in large
N
conformal field theories (CFTs) in
d\geq 2
dimensions. We focus on straight lines or circular defects. At a fixed point of these flows, the defect preserves an
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})\times SO(d-1)

d

S\ \rightarrow\ S+\lambda\,M^{1-\Delta_{\mathbb{O}}}\int\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathbb{O}}(x)\,.

2
RG Flow on the Straight Line

Our starting point is a straight conformal line defect
{\cal W}
that ends on a fundamental operator
{\cal O}
of dimension
\Delta
. We normalize the operator so that its two-point function on the straight defect of length
L
takes the standard form

{\cal D}_{0}^{\text{line}}(L)=\langle\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(L){\cal W}[L,0]_{ij}\mathcal{O}_{j}(0)\rangle\equiv\langle\!\langle\overline{\mathcal{O}}(L)\,\mathcal{O}(0)\rangle\!\rangle=\frac{1}{L^{2\Delta}}\,,

(2.1)

where
i
and
j
are color indices.

We assume that
\Delta\in[0,1/2)
and
turn on the fundamental anti-fundamental (
\bar{F}F
) deformation. Due to the planar structure of the large
N
factorization, the OPE of
{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}
with itself consists of
{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}
and its descendants only. Hence, no other operator can be generated along the flow. (This is in contrast to the double-trace deformation, where the OPE of two
{\mathbb{O}}_{\text{DT}}
operators includes the operator
{\mathbb{O}}_{\text{DT}}^{2}
which also becomes relevant for
\Delta<1/4
.)
As a result, the two-point function changes to

{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(L)\equiv\langle\!\langle\overline{\mathcal{O}}(L)\,{\cal P}\exp\Big[-\lambda M^{1-2\Delta}\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}(x)\Big]\mathcal{O}(0)\rangle\!\rangle={1\over L^{2\Delta}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left({-\lambda\over(ML)^{2\Delta-1}}\right)^{n}J_{n}\,,

(2.2)

where
{\cal P}
stands for path ordering, and
J_{n}
is the integrated
n
-point function of the operator
{\mathbb{O}}
in (
1.3
). It is given by

J_{n}=\int\limits_{0<\sigma_{1}<\ldots<\sigma_{n}<1}{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}\over[(1-\sigma_{n})(\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{n-1})\ldots(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})\sigma_{1}]^{2\Delta}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(1-2\Delta\right)^{n+1}}{\Gamma\left((n+1)(1-2\Delta)\right)}\,.

(2.3)

The series in (
2.2
) sums to

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(L)=\frac{\Gamma(1-2\Delta)}{L^{2\Delta}}E_{1-2\Delta\,,1-2\Delta}\left(-\lambda(ML)^{1-2\Delta}\Gamma(1-2\Delta)\right)\,,

(2.4)

where
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)
is the Mittag-Leffler (E) function; see
[
32
]
for a review of its relevant properties. This result can also be obtained by solving the Schwinger-Dyson equation

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(L)=\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\text{line}}(L)-\lambda M^{1-2\Delta}\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathcal{D}_{0}^{\text{line}}(L-s)\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(s)\,.

(2.5)

Because
\Delta<1/2
, all integrals in (
2.3
) are finite and there is no need to introduce a new regularization scale. Hence, no new terms are generated. Here, we are working with the bare operator, and therefore the corresponding
\beta
-function is obtained by simple dimensional analysis from (
1.1
),
\beta_{\lambda}=\beta_{\text{bare}}=-(1-2\Delta)\lambda
. It is evident that
\lambda=0
is an unstable fixed point, with no other fixed point at finite
\lambda
. To analyze the IR limit, we study the asymptotic behavior of the two-point function (
2.4
) at large
ML
. For
\lambda>0
we have

{\cal D}_{\lambda>0}^{\text{line}}(L)=\frac{(1-2\Delta)\sin(2\pi\Delta)}{\pi(\lambda M^{1-2\Delta})^{2}}\frac{1}{L^{2(1-\Delta)}}\big(1+O\left(1/(ML)\right)\big)\,.

(2.6)

It follows that
\lambda\to\infty
is a stable fixed point. The dimension of the fundamental operator
{\cal O}
flows from
\Delta_{\cal O}=\Delta
in the UV to
\Delta_{\cal O}=1-\Delta
in the IR.

Since the flow is super-renormalizable, the only dimensionful scale on which the wave function renormalization factor can depend is
\lambda M^{1-2\Delta}
. This factor is independent of the UV operator’s normalization choice.
It follows that the ratio

\lim_{ML\to\infty}\left(\lambda(ML)^{1-2\Delta}\right)^{2}{{\cal D}_{\lambda>0}^{\text{line}}(L)\over{\cal D}_{0}^{\text{line}}(L)}={1\over\pi}(1-2\Delta)\sin(2\pi\Delta)\,

(2.7)

is physical. In section
5
we will relate this quantity to the change in the two-point function of the displacement operator.

For
\lambda<0
the two-point function grows exponentially in the IR,

{\cal D}_{\lambda<0}^{\text{line}}(L)=\frac{M^{2\Delta}|\lambda|^{\frac{2\Delta}{1-2\Delta}}(\Gamma(1-2\Delta))^{\frac{1}{1-2\Delta}}}{1-2\Delta}\,e^{[-\lambda\Gamma(1-2\Delta)]^{\frac{1}{1-2\Delta}}ML}\,+\frac{1}{L^{2\Delta}}O\left(1/(ML)\right)\,.

(2.8)

This deformation of the line therefore destabilizes the IR. It reflects an instability that can affect the theory far from the defect, and we cannot determine its fate on the basis of our assumptions. The same behavior is expected to hold for the double-trace deformation.

In several examples of double-trace deformations that have been studied in
[
33
,
34
]
it was found that a small correction to the factorization (which would be of order
1/N
in our setup) is responsible for restoring conformal invariance in the IR. Assuming that this is the case, in section
4
, we will evaluate the corresponding change in the defect entropy.

\Delta=1/2

The case where
\Delta=1/2
requires special care because
\mathbb{O}_{\bar{F}F}
is classically marginal and the loop integrals in (
2.3
) have logarithmic divergences. We choose to work with a point-splitting regularization,
\sigma_{i+1}-\sigma_{i}>\epsilon/L
. Working to second order in perturbation theory, we find

{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(L)=\frac{1}{L}\left(1-2\lambda\log\frac{L}{\epsilon}+\lambda^{2}(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}+3\log^{2}\frac{L}{\epsilon})+O(\lambda^{3})\right)\,.

(2.9)

To analyze the flow, we plug
{\cal D}
into the corresponding Callan-Symanzik equation

(\epsilon\,\partial_{\epsilon}-\beta_{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}+2\gamma_{\cal O}){\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{line}}(L)=0\,,

(2.10)

where
\gamma_{\cal O}
is the anomalous dimension matrix. From (
2.10
) we find that

\gamma_{\cal O}=-\lambda+O(\lambda^{3})\,,\qquad\beta_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}+O(\lambda^{3})\,.

(2.11)

We see that
\partial_{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}=\lambda+O(\lambda^{2})
, and so for
\lambda<0
the
\bar{F}F
deformation is marginally relevant while for
\lambda>0
it is marginally irrelevant.

Summary

By comparing the analysis at
\Delta<1/2
and
\Delta=1/2
, we see that, as
\Delta\to 1/2
from below, the two fixed points collide. As
\Delta
crosses
1/2
, they interchange with each other. To make this more manifest, we impose the renormalization condition that the two point function of the renormalized boundary operators at
ML=1
is finite as
\Delta\to 1/2
. Using (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we find that this renormalization condition is satisfied by the renormalized operators and coupling

\lambda{\cal O}=f{\cal O}^{\text{ren}}\,,\quad\lambda{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}=f{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}^{\text{ren}}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad f(\lambda)={\lambda\over 1+\lambda/(1-2\Delta)}\,.

(2.12)

In terms of these, the two-point function evaluated at
ML=1
takes the form

\langle\!\langle\overline{\mathcal{O}}^{\text{ren}}(L)\,{\cal P}\,\text{exp}\Big[-fL^{2\Delta-1}\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}^{\text{ren}}(x)\Big]\mathcal{O}^{\text{ren}}(0)\rangle\!\rangle\\
=\frac{1}{L^{2\Delta}}\left[1+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{f}{1-2\Delta}\right)^{m}\times O(1-2\Delta)\right]\,.

(2.13)

The corresponding
\beta
-function reads

\beta_{f}=f(f-(1-2\Delta))\,.

(2.14)

The two fixed points are
f=0
and
f=1-2\Delta
. The latter (former) is the stable one for
\Delta<1/2
(
\Delta>1/2
). As
\Delta\to 1/2
the two fixed points collide.

3
The Change in the
g
-function

Next, we turn to the computation of the change in the
g
-function between the UV and IR fixed points, (
1.6
). At any point along the flow, the change in the expectation value of the defect takes the form

\delta\langle W\rangle\equiv\langle W_{\lambda}\rangle-\langle W_{0}\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-\lambda M^{1-2\Delta})^{n}I_{n}\,,

(3.1)

where
I_{n}
is the integrated correlator of
n
insertions of
{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}
on the circle. It is given by a factorized product of two-point functions between neighboring ordered fundamental and anti-fundamental operators. Together, these form a planar connected contraction of
{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}
, as seen in figure
2
. This integrated correlator is given by

I_{n}=R^{n}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{0<\theta_{1}<\ldots<\theta_{n}<2\pi}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})\,,

(3.2)

where
\theta_{i}
are angles on the circle, with
\theta_{n+1}\equiv\theta_{1}+2\pi
. Here,
D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)
is the two-point function of the operator
{\cal O}
on an arc of the circle with opening angle
\theta
and radius
R
at
\lambda=0
,

{\cal D}_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)\equiv\langle\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{i}(\theta){\cal W}_{\text{arc}}[\theta,0]_{ij}\mathcal{O}_{j}(0)\rangle=\Big[4R^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)\Big]^{-\Delta}\,.

(3.3)

It can be obtained by a conformal transformation from the two-point function on a straight line (
2.1
).

Using the representation of
\delta g
as the IR value of the sum in (
3.1
)
we arrive at (see figure
3
)

\partial_{\Delta}\delta g=\lim_{\tilde{\epsilon}\to 0}\left(1-R\partial_{R}\right)\,2\pi R^{2}\!\int\limits_{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon}}\mathrm{d}\theta\left[\lambda M^{1-2\Delta}\,{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{arc}}(2\pi-\theta)_{\text{IR}}\right]\partial_{\Delta}\left[\lambda M^{1-2\Delta}\,{\cal D}_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)\right]\,,

(3.4)

where
\tilde{\epsilon}
is a point-splitting regulator on the propagator, and the
\left(1-R\partial_{R}\right)
factor removes the local divergence at
\theta\to 0,2\pi
.
Instead, one can simply subtract a local counter term. After doing so,
\partial_{\Delta}\delta g
is independent of
R
. Here,
{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)_{\text{IR}}
is the arc two-point function at the IR fixed point. To evaluate it, we note that the wave function renormalization factor in (
2.6
) is a local property of the operator. For
\Delta<1/2
, there are no UV divergences on a line segment or an arc, so it is also scheme-independent. Therefore, its value at the IR fixed point is the same on the straight line and on the arc. We conclude that

{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)_{\text{IR}}=\frac{(1-2\Delta)\sin(2\pi\Delta)}{\pi(\lambda M^{1-2\Delta})^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\Big[4R^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)\Big]^{1-\Delta}}\,.

(3.5)

By plugging (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) into (
3.4
), we arrive at

\partial_{\Delta}\delta g=2\pi(1-2\Delta)\sin(2\pi\Delta)\,.

(3.6)

g
-function, we note that at
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}
the deformation is marginally irrelevant, and therefore
\left.\delta g\right|_{\Delta=\frac{1}{2}}=0
.
Since
\delta g
is continuous in
\Delta
at this point, we conclude that

\delta g=-\frac{\sin(2\pi\Delta)}{\pi}-(1-2\Delta)\cos(2\pi\Delta)\,.

(3.7)

The functions
\delta g

and
\partial_{\Delta}\delta g
are plotted in figure

. We note that
\partial_{\Delta}\delta g
is positive for

and therefore
\delta g
increases monotonically in

. Since
\left.\delta g\right|_{\Delta=\frac{1}{2}}=0
, it follows that
g_{\text{UV}}>g_{\text{IR}}
for all

4
Interpretation of
g_{\text{UV}}-g_{\text{IR}}

We have found that
\delta g
ranges from
\delta g=0
for a marginally irrelevant deformation,
2\Delta=1
, to
\delta g=-1
for
2\Delta=0
. In this subsection, we explain the physics that underlies this result. To this end,
we first construct the unstable line as the endpoint of a DRG flow from the direct sum of the stable line and the trivial (empty) line. We then use this realization of the unstable line to describe the DRG flow to the stable line that was considered in the previous section. The construction is general and mimics a perturbative procedure as laid out in
[
31
]
.

Let
{\cal L}_{0}
be the one-dimensional Lagrangian of the stable line. For example, in the case where the defect is a Wilson line,
{\cal L}_{0}
is proportional to the gauge connection in the direction of the line (which is an
N\times N
matrix in color space). Using
{\cal L}_{0}
, the Lagrangian of the direct sum of the stable line and a trivial line can be written as

{\cal L}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}{\cal L}_{0}(x)&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(4.1)

It is evident that in the direct sum of lines, there is one more degree of freedom. Consequently, its
g
-function differs by one unit,

g_{\text{stable}\oplus 1}=\langle{\rm tr~}{\cal P}\,e^{-\oint\mathrm{d}x\,{\cal L}(x)}\rangle=\langle{\rm tr~}{\cal P}\,e^{-\oint\mathrm{d}x\,{\cal L}_{0}(x)}\rangle+1=g_{\text{stable}}+1\,.

(4.2)

To construct the unstable line, we deform the line Lagrangian as
{\cal L}\to{\cal L}+\delta{\cal L}
, with

\delta{\cal L}=\alpha\,M^{\Delta}\begin{pmatrix}0&{\cal O}\\
\overline{{\cal O}}&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\beta_{c.t.}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(4.3)

where
M
is an RG scale, and
\beta_{c.t.}

is a local counter term, that we will fine tune and determine below so that the flow ends at the unstable fixed point.
This deformation mixes between the stable and trivial lines. When the line is expanded in powers of
\delta{\cal L}
, it decomposes into empty and filled segments, with
\delta{\cal L}
transitioning between them. At small
\alpha
the deformation is relevant because
{\cal O}
has dimension
1-\Delta<1
. Due to the large
N
factorization property of the correlators of
\mathbb{O}
on the stable line, no new operator can be generated along the DRG flow.
4
4

4

As we will see, the operator
\overline{{\cal O}}\times{\cal O}
remains irrelevant along this flow.
Assuming that the flow ends at a new conformal line, we can represent the conformal line in the IR as in (
4.3
) with some specific
\alpha=\alpha_{*}
.

To find the new fixed point, we consider the expectation value of two mesonic lines. One is defined before the deformation,

{\cal M}(L)\equiv\langle\overline{{\cal O}}(L)\mathcal{P}e^{-\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,{\cal L}_{0}(x)}{\cal O}(0)\rangle=\langle\overline{{\cal O}}(L)\Big[\mathcal{P}e^{-\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\,{\cal L}(x)}\Big]_{11}{\cal O}(0)\rangle={1\over L^{2(1-\Delta)}}\,,

(4.4)

where we have chosen a canonical normalization for
{\cal O}
. The other mesonic line is defined after the line is deformed,

\widetilde{\cal M}(L)\equiv\langle\Big[\mathcal{P}e^{-\int\limits_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left({\cal L}(x)+\delta{\cal L}(x)\right)}\Big]_{22}\rangle\,.

(4.5)

From the structure of
\delta{\cal L}
in (
4.3
) it follows that
\widetilde{M}(L)
satisfies the differential equation

\partial_{L}\widetilde{\cal M}(L)=\alpha^{2}M^{2\Delta}\int\limits_{0}^{L-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}x\,{\cal M}(L-x)\,\widetilde{\cal M}(x)-\beta_{c.t.}\widetilde{\cal M}(L-\epsilon)\,,

(4.6)

where
\epsilon
is a point-splitting regulator. Note that here we assume that a cutoff is only needed near
x=L
, and not near
x=0
. That is, point-splitting is only necessary for
{\cal M}
, but not
\widetilde{{\cal M}}
. Consequently, we shall only look for a solution that is consistent with this assumption.
We begin by treating
0<\Delta<1/2
, excluding the points
0
and
1/2
. For the divergence near
x=L
to cancel, we see that

\beta_{c.t.}={\alpha^{2}\over 1-2\Delta}{M^{2\Delta}\over\epsilon^{1-2\Delta}}\,.

(4.7)

At the UV fixed point, the boundary operator
(0,1)^{\intercal}
has the lowest dimension, equal to zero. Due to the gap, it cannot mix with
\left({\cal O},0\right)^{\intercal}
at small
\alpha
. In fact, from (
4.6
) it follows that at the IR fixed point,
\left({\cal O},0\right)^{\intercal}_{\text{IR}}
is the
SL(2)
descendant of
\left(0,1\right)^{\intercal}_{\text{IR}}
. In particular, at the IR fixed point
(0,1)^{\intercal}
is an
SL(2)
primary of some dimension
\Delta_{\text{IR}}
,

\lim_{(Mx)\to\infty}\widetilde{{\cal M}}(x)\propto\frac{1}{|x|^{2\Delta_{\text{IR}}}}\left(1+O(1/(Mx)\right)\,.

(4.8)

For this behavior to be consistent with our assumption that the integral in (
4.6
) is finite near
x=0

\Delta_{\text{IR}}<1/2
.

We plug the IR limit (
4.8

4.6
), where the normalization of
\widetilde{M}
drops out because (
4.6
) is homogeneous. Taking then the limit
\epsilon/L\to 0

0={\Gamma(1-2\Delta_{\text{IR}})\Gamma(2\Delta-1)\over\Gamma(2\Delta-2\Delta_{\text{IR}})}(ML)^{2\Delta}-\left[{\Delta_{\text{IR}}\over\Delta}+{2\Delta_{\text{IR}}\over 1-2\Delta}\right](M\epsilon)^{2\Delta}+{2\Delta_{\text{IR}}\over\alpha^{2}_{*}}+\text{[subleading]}\,.

(4.9)

The local coefficient
\alpha_{*}
may depend on the RG and regularization scales, but it cannot depend on
L
. Terms with different powers of
L
must vanish independently.
From the cancellation of the
L
independent terms we conclude that

\alpha^{2}_{*}M^{2\Delta}=2\Delta(1-2\Delta)\epsilon^{-2\Delta}\,.

(4.10)

From the term of order
(ML)^{2\Delta}
, upon restriction to
\Delta_{\text{IR}}<1/2
, we can solve for
\Delta_{\text{IR}}
,

\Delta_{\text{IR}}=\Delta\,.

(4.11)

Hence, the flow that was triggered by (
4.3
) has landed at the unstable fixed point of a single line operator.

We can now use (
4.3
) to represent the flow back to the stable line that we have considered in the previous sections. The
\bar{F}F
deformation takes the form

{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}=(0,1)^{\intercal}\times(0,1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(4.12)

The unstable line (
4.3
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
), can be thought of as a condensation of empty segments mixed within the stable line. The relevant
\bar{F}F
deformation with positive coefficient leads to an exponential suppression of the empty segments. At the end of the flow that it triggers, the empty segments disappear, and we remain with the stable line only. The direct sum in (
4.1
), (
4.3
) can equivalently be realized using a worldline fermion, see appendix
A
for details. In this realization, the
\bar{F}F
deformation (
4.12

In summary, we have constructed the unstable line as the fixed point of a DRG from the stable line plus a trivial line. The stable line is then obtained as the fixed point of a second DRG flow that is triggered by (
4.12
). Therefore, the
g

g_{\text{stable}}<g_{\text{unstable}}<g_{\text{stable}}+1\,,

(4.13)

in agreement with (
3.7
).

The limit
\Delta\to 0

4.3

We find that for
\Delta=0

\alpha_{*}^{2}(\Delta=0)=0

\delta{\cal L}
is marginally irrelevant and
\Delta_{\text{IR}}=0=\Delta
.
We conclude that in this limit
g_{\text{unstable}}\to g_{\text{stable}}+1
, in agreement with (
3.7

The change in the expectation value of the defect

is of order

\delta s=(1-R\partial_{R})\log\frac{\langle W_{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle W_{0}\rangle}=(1-R\partial_{R}){\delta\langle W\rangle\over\langle W_{0}\rangle}\left(1+O(1/N)\right)\,.

The expansion of
\delta\langle W\rangle

is given in (
3.1

To evaluate

in (
3.2

\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{1}+2\pi
, and let
\theta_{2},\ldots,\theta_{n+1}

\tilde{I}_{n}(a)\equiv R^{n+1}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\hskip 16.38895pt\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\ldots<\theta_{n+1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}\theta_{n+1}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})\,e^{\mathrm{i}a(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})}\,.

(6.2)

Here, we have dressed

by

I_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathrm{d}a\over 2\pi R}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}a\cdot 2\pi}\tilde{I}_{n}(a)\,,

(6.3)

where the combinatorial factor of

6.1
Divergences and Regularization

The loop integrals in (
3.2
) have potential divergences from the integration regions where some of the insertion points collide. Consider first the region where only a subset of
m<n
consecutive points collide.
In this region, the integral behaves in the same way as the line integral,
J_{n}
in (
2.3
), behaves in the region where
m
insertion points collide.
Because
J_{n}
is finite, this region does not cause a divergence. To see this more explicitly, we denote by
\rho
the common relative distance between these insertion points. Near
\rho=0
, the integral behaves as
\int\rho^{m-2}\mathrm{d}\rho/\rho^{2(m-1)\Delta}
. Here, the numerator comes from the measure after factoring out the center of mass of these
m
points. The denominator comes from the
m-1
contractions between the
m
insertion points. This integral is finite for
\Delta<1/2
. Therefore, the only potential divergence comes from the integration region where all
n
points collide. In this region, all
m=n
contractions are singular (instead of only
m-1
for
m<n
). Consequently, the integral behaves as
\int\rho^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\rho/\rho^{2n\Delta}\propto\epsilon^{n(1-2\Delta)}/\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is a UV cutoff on
\rho
. We conclude the following:

•

For any
\Delta<1/2
, only finitely many
I_{n}
’s are divergent.

•

The divergences are linear in
R
and can be removed by a local counter term. They are also removed by the factor
(1-R\partial_{R})
in the defect entropy (
1.5
).

•

To regulate these divergences, it suffices to regulate one of the
n
insertions.

In the following, we choose to regulate the
I_{n}
integrals by point-splitting the insertion at
\theta_{1}
as

{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}(\theta_{1})=\overline{{\cal O}}(\theta_{1})\times{\cal O}(\theta_{1})\quad\rightarrow\quad\overline{{\cal O}}(\theta_{1})\times{\cal O}(\theta_{1}+\tilde{\epsilon})\,,

(6.4)

where
\tilde{\epsilon}=\epsilon/R
is the regulator. This is achieved by modifying (
6.3
) to

I_{n}^{\text{reg}}\equiv\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathrm{d}a\over 2\pi R}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}a\cdot(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})}\tilde{I}_{n}(a)\,.

(6.5)

6.2
Evaluation of
\delta s

The integrals in
\tilde{I}_{n}
, (
6.2
),
are ordered convolutions. They are therefore diagonalized by a Laplace transform

{\cal L}(s)\equiv R^{2\Delta}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\theta\,D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)e^{-s\theta}=\frac{\pi\Gamma(1-2\Delta)\,\text{csch}(\pi s)}{\Gamma\left(1-\Delta-\mathrm{i}s\right)\Gamma\left(1-\Delta+\mathrm{i}s\right)}\,.

(6.6)

The inverse transform reads

D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}\,R^{2\Delta}}\int\limits_{\cal C}\!\mathrm{d}s\,{\cal L}(s)e^{s\theta}\,,

(6.7)

where the path
{\cal C}
is parallel to the imaginary axis at some positive real value, and is oriented towards the positive imaginary
s
axis.
Here, for convenience, we have factored out the dependence on
R
so that
{\cal L}(s)
is dimensionless.

Using the convolution relation

\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\theta\,D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\beta-\theta)D_{0}^{\text{arc}}(\theta-\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}R^{4\Delta}}\int\limits_{\cal C}\mathrm{d}s\,{\cal L}^{2}(s)\,e^{s(\beta-\alpha)}\,,

(6.8)

the regulated integral (
6.5
) takes the form

I_{n}^{\text{reg}}=-{\mathrm{i}\over n}\,R^{(1-2\Delta)n}\int\limits_{\cal C}\mathrm{d}s\,{\cal L}^{n}(s)\,e^{(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})s}\,.

(6.9)

In this fashion, the regulated partition function resums to

\delta\langle W\rangle^{\text{reg}}=\mathrm{i}\int\limits_{\cal C}\mathrm{d}s\,e^{(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})s}\log\left[1+\lambda(MR)^{1-2\Delta}{\cal L}(s)\right]+O(1/N)\,.

(6.10)

The renormalized operator is obtained from (
6.10
) by subtracting the local divergent contributions. For
\Delta<1/4
the only divergent contribution is the term linear in
\lambda
, which is due to the self-contractions of the two operators in
{\mathbb{O}}_{\bar{F}F}
, (
1.3
). After subtracting it, one can take
\tilde{\epsilon}\to 0
. Other values of
\Delta
can be obtained by analytically continuing the result in
\Delta
or, equivalently, by subtracting additional divergent terms. These local divergent terms drop out from the change in the defect entropy (
6.1
).

6.3
IR Limit

We can now check that the change in the

-function (
3.7
) is reproduced from (

\Delta<\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda>0

\int\limits_{\cal C}\mathrm{d}s\,e^{(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})s}\propto\delta(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})=0\,,

(6.11)

\delta\langle W\rangle^{\text{reg}}_{\text{IR}}=-\mathrm{i}\int\limits_{\cal C}\mathrm{d}s\,e^{(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})s}\log\left[\sinh(\pi s)\Gamma\big(1-\Delta-\mathrm{i}s\big){\Gamma\big(1-\Delta+\mathrm{i}s\big)}\right]\,.

(6.12)

To evaluate this integral, we take a derivative with respect to
\Delta

\partial_{\Delta}\delta\langle W\rangle^{\text{reg}}_{\text{IR}}=\mathrm{i}\int\limits_{{\cal C}}\mathrm{d}s\,e^{(2\pi-\tilde{\epsilon})s}\left[\psi\big(1-\Delta-\mathrm{i}s\big)+\psi\big(1-\Delta+\mathrm{i}s\big)\right]\,,

We thus find

\partial_{\Delta}\left(g_{\text{IR}}-g_{\text{UV}}\right)=2\pi(1-2\Delta)\sin(2\pi\Delta)\,,

in agreement with (

For the purpose of checking this relation, we take

\Big[R^{4\Delta}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{arc}}(2\pi-\theta)\,{\cal D}_{\lambda}^{\text{arc}}\,(\theta)(1-\cos\theta)\Big]_{n-2}=\int\limits_{{\cal C}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}e^{2\pi s}f_{n}(s)\,,

of the function

Appendix A
Worldline Fermion Realization

In section
4
we constructed the unstable fixed point using a two-dimensional linear space on the line. One way of realizing such a space is by thinking of it as the Hilbert space of a worldline fermion. In this appendix, we elaborate on this realization.

Consider two worldline fermions
\chi_{i}(\tau)
,
i=1,2
, where
\tau
is some parametrization of the line. The worldline Lagrangian reads
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}\chi_{i}\partial_{\tau}\chi^{i}
. The corresponding equation of motion states that
\chi^{i}
is constant. Upon quantization, the canonical anti-commutation relations are
\{\chi^{i}(\tau),\chi^{j}(\tau)\}=2\delta^{ij}
. The irreducible representation of these relations are constant
2\times 2
matrices, which we choose to be the Pauli matrices
\chi^{i}(\tau)=\sigma^{i}
.

Using this worldline representation, the direct sum of the stable line and a trivial line (
4.1
) corresponds to the worldline Lagrangian
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{\chi}+\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\,{\cal L}_{0}(x(\tau))
, where
x(\tau)
parametrizes the line defect and
\chi^{\pm}=(\chi^{1}\pm\mathrm{i}\,\chi^{2})/2
. Similarly, the deformation of the line (
4.3
) takes the form
\delta{\cal L}=\alpha M^{\Delta}\left(\chi^{+}\mathcal{O}+\chi^{-}\overline{{\cal O}}\right)+\beta_{\text{c.t.}}\chi^{-}\chi^{+}
. Finally, the DRG flow from the unstable to the stable fixed point is triggered by the operator
\chi^{-}\chi^{+}
, which is a mass term on the worldline.

In summary, the action of the worldline fermion is quadratic. Upon integrating out
\chi^{i}
we end up with the
2\times 2
matrix structure of section
4
.

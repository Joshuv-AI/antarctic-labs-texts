Hearts of twin cotorsion pairs revisited: Integral and Abelian Hearts
Yu Liu and Panyue Zhou

Abstract
. Hearts of twin cotorsion pairs are shown to be quasi-abelian. But they are not always integral. In this article, we provide a sufficient and necessary condition for the hearts of twin cotorsion pairs being integral (resp. abelian).

1.
Introduction

The notion of a cotorsion pair was first introduced by Salce
[
Sa
]
. Since then, it has been extensively studied in representation theory, particularly in tilting theory and Cohen-Macaulay modules. On triangulated categories, cotorsion pair unifies the notion of
t
-structures in the sense of
[
BBD
]
, co-
t
-structures in the sense of Pauksztello
[
P
]
, and the notion of cluster tilting subcategories in the sense of Keller-Reiten
[
KR
]
(see also
[
BMRRT
]
).

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
[1]
. A
cotorsion pair
on
\mathcal{C}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
such that
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])=0
and any object
C\in\mathcal{C}
admits a triangle
V\to U\to C\to V[1]
with
U\in\mathcal{U}
,
V\in\mathcal{V}
. In fact,
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a cotorsion pair if and only if
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is a torsion pair. Nakaoka
[
N1
]
defined the hearts of cotorsion pairs on triangulated categories and showed that they are abelian.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite. If
T
is a cluster tilting object in
\mathcal{C}
, then
(\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT[1],\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT[1])
is a cotorsion pair. By the result of Koenig and Zhu
[
KZ
, Theorem 3.3]
, the ideal quotient
\mathcal{C}/\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(T[1])
is abelian. In fact, it is the heart of
(\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(T[1]),\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(T[1]))
and is equivalent to
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(T)^{\operatorname{op}\nolimits}
. However, when
T
is just rigid, generally speaking,
\mathcal{C}/\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(T[1])
is no longer abelian. When
\mathcal{C}
has a Serre functor, Buan and Marsh
[
BM
]
considered the quotient
\mathcal{C}/\mathscr{X}_{T}
instead, where
\mathscr{X}_{T}=\{C\in\mathcal{C}~|~\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(T,-)=0\}
. Note that if
T
is cluster tilting, we have
\mathscr{X}_{T}=\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(T[1])
. Unfortunately, even in this case,

\mathcal{C}/\mathscr{X}_{T}
is not abelian in general. But they showed that
\mathcal{C}/\mathscr{X}_{T}
still admits a good structure. This quotient category is integral, which means that the localization of this category with respect to the epic-monic morphisms is abelian (it is equivalent to
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\operatorname{End}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(T)^{\operatorname{op}\nolimits}
).

Integral category is a subclass of preabelian category which was first studied systematically by Rump
[
R
]
. One of Rump’s key observations was that the class of epic-monic morphisms in an integral category can be treated by using calculus of fractions (defined by Gabriel and Zisman
[
GM
]
). As a result, the canonical localization of the category at this class is an abelian category. Now we recall the definition of integral category (for the convenience of the readers, we also recall the definitions of semi-abelian and quasi-abelian category).

Definition 1.1
.

An additive category is called preabelian if any morphism admits a kernel and a cokernel. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a preabelian category and the following diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{a}}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{b}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{c}}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{d}}
\textstyle{D}

is a pull-back diagram.

(1)

\mathcal{A}
is called left semi-abelian if
\mathbf{a}
is an epimorphism whenever
\mathbf{d}
is a cokernel;

(2)

\mathcal{A}
is called left integral if
\mathbf{a}
is an epimorphism whenever
\mathbf{d}
is an epimorphism;

(3)

\mathcal{A}
is called left quasi-abelian if
\mathbf{a}
is a cokernel whenever
\mathbf{d}
is a cokernel;

Dually, we can define right integral (resp. right semi-abelian, right quasi-abelian) category.
\mathcal{A}
is called integral (resp. semi-abelian, quasi-abelian) if it is both left and right integral (resp. semi-abelian, quasi-abelian).

We have the following relation:

\{\mbox{Abelian Cat.}\}\subset\{\mbox{Integral Cat.}\}\subset\{\mbox{Semi-Abelian Cat.}\}\subset\{\mbox{Preabelian Cat.}\}.

Under the condition Buan and Marsh considered, we can find a pair of cotorsion pairs
((\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT[1],\mathscr{X}_{T})
,
(\mathscr{X}_{T},\mathcal{V}))
. It is a special case of the concept
twin cotorsion pair
defined in
[
N2
]
. The ideal quotient
\mathcal{C}/\mathscr{X}_{T}
is the heart of the twin cotorsion pair (also defined in
[
N2
]
)
((\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptT[1],\mathscr{X}_{T}),(\mathscr{X}_{T},\mathscr{Y}))
. A pair of cotorsion pairs
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
is called a twin cotorsion pair if
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
. A cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
can be realized as a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
. Heart of twin cotorsion pair is a generalization of heart of cotorsion pair, but it is not always abelian (it is even not integral in general). A result showed in
[
N2
]
implies that if we have a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{V}))
, then its heart is integral (the twin cotorsion pair hidden in
[
BM
]
admits this condition). It is reasonable to consider further when the heart of a twin cotorsion pair becomes integral, since we can get an abelian category through localization. In this article, we will give an “ if and only if ” condition for the hearts being integral (resp. abelian), under a more general setting.

Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
introduced the concept of extriangulated categories, which combines the characteristics of both triangulated and exact categories. On extriangulated categories, various concepts can be defined, such as (twin) cotorsion pairs
[
NP
]
, their hearts
[
LN
]
, all of which have similarities to their definitions on triangulated and exact categories.
Now we recall the definition of the heart of a twin cotorsion pair on an extriangulated category
from
[
LN
]
. Let
(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category.

Definition 1.2
.

For a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
on
\mathcal{B}
, let

(i)

\mathcal{B}^{+}=\{X\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mbox{there exists an}~\text{ }\mathbb{E}\text{-triangle }V\to W\to X\dashrightarrow\text{, }V\in\mathcal{V}\text{ and }W\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T}\}
,

(ii)

\mathcal{B}^{-}=\{Y\in\mathcal{B}\text{ }|\text{ }\mbox{there exists an}~\text{ }\mathbb{E}\text{-triangle }Y\to W^{\prime}\to S\dashrightarrow\text{, }S\in\mathcal{S}\text{ and }W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T}\}
,

(iii)

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{B}^{+}\cap\mathcal{B}^{-}
.

We call the ideal quotient
\mathcal{H}/(\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T})
the heart of
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
. For a single cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
, the heart of twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}),(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}))
is called the heart of the
(\mathcal{U}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
.

For convenience, denote
\mathcal{H}/(\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T})
by
\underline{\mathcal{H}}
. Hassoun and Shah
[
HS
]
provided
a sufficient condition for the heart being integral, which unifies the results on triangulated categories
[
N2
, Theorem 6.3]
and exact categories
[
L1
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
HS
, Theorem 3.7]

For a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
on
\mathcal{B}
,

(1)
if
\mathcal{B}
has enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
,
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\ast\mathcal{T}
, then
\underline{\mathcal{H}}
is integral;

(2)
if
\mathcal{B}
has enough injectives
\mathcal{I}
,
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\ast\mathcal{V}
, then
\underline{\mathcal{H}}
is integral.

In this article, we first provide an “ if and only if ” condition for the hearts being integral.

Theorem 1.4
.

(see Definition
2.10
and Theorem
3.1
for details)

For a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
on
\mathcal{B}
,
\underline{\mathcal{H}}
is integral if and only if
\mathcal{B}^{-}\cap(\mathcal{T}*{{}_{\rm epi.}}\mathcal{U})\subseteq\mathcal{U}
.

Some “ if and only if ” conditions for the hearts being abelian are discussed for the twin cotorsion pairs induced by
n
-cluster (
n>2
) tilting subcategories (see
[

]
and
[
HZ
]
); for general case in
[

]
. Our second main result provides another sufficient and necessary condition for the heart of a twin cotorsion pair being abelian.

Theorem 1.5
.

(see Definition

and Theorem
4.6
for details)

For a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}))
on
\mathcal{B}
, denote by
\underline{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
the heart of
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T})
and

\underline{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
the heart of
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
. Then

\underline{\mathcal{H}}
is abelian if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

{\rm(1)}~\underline{\mathcal{H}}=\underline{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\cap\underline{\mathcal{H}_{2}};\quad{\rm(2)}~{{}_{\rm epi.}}\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{W};\quad{\rm(3)}~\mathcal{T}_{\rm mono.}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{W}.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some elementary concepts and properties of cotorsion pairs on extriangulated categories.
In Section 3, we provide a sufficient and necessary condition for the heart of a twin cotorsion pair
being integral. In Section 4, we provide a sufficient and necessary condition for the heart of a twin cotorsion pair being abelian.

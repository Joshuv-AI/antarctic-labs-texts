On Lie superalgebroids of Poisson type
Dennise García-Beltrán, Óscar Guajardo

Abstract
The purpose of this work is to study Lie superalgebroid structures on the space of superdifferential 1 1 -forms over a special kind of supermanifolds —which we call cotangent supermanifolds — whose superfunctions are the differential forms on its underlying manifold. These superalgbroids are constructed from graded Poisson structures defined on the latter superalgebra.

Primary: 53D17. Secondary: 17B60, 17B66.

Keywords:
Superalgebroids, Poisson supergeometry, Gerstenhaber
superalgebras.

Introduction

Poisson manifolds and Lie algebroids are two well-studied structures in differential
geometry, both for their applications in other areas of mathematics, as well as for their
usefulness in describing physical phenomena; it is also well-known the interplay between
these two structures (see for instance
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
, and
references therein).

On the other hand, given a Lie algebroid there is associated to it another algebraic
structure, a
Gerstenhaber algebra
, whose prototype is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra of
sections of multivectors on a manifold
M
, endowed with both the wedge product and the
Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\operatorname{SN}}
; this bracket is the only
extension to this space that restricts to the usual Lie bracket of vector fields, endows the
former with the structure of a Lie algebra (actually, of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra), and
which is also compatible with the wedge product, in the sense that maps of the form

[P,\cdot]_{\operatorname{SN}}
are derivations of the wedge product, for any section
P
of

\Lambda TM
. These relations have also been well-studied—cf. e.g.
[
7
]
,

[
13
]
. Gerstenhaber algebras have also been used in theoretical physics, specifically in
the study of BRST quantisation (see for example
[
6
]
). In a related direction,
graded Poisson structures are also of interest, not only as a generalisation of (ungraded)
Poisson structures to graded and supermanifolds, but also as algebraic structures relevant
for the aforementioned studies—cf.
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
, for instance.

In this paper we consider Poisson structures on the graded-commutative algebra
of differential forms
\mathcal{A}=\Omega(M)
of a smooth manifold
M
. We
endow the pair
(M,\mathcal{A})
with the structure of a smooth
supermanifold and consider superalgebroids defined on the space

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
of graded (or super-) differential
1
-forms on this supermanifold. More
specifically, we consider superalgebroid structures defined using the given
Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{A}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows: in Section
1
we define
the concepts relevant to this work; to wit: supermanifolds, superbundles and
superalgebroids. In Section
2
we introduce graded Poisson
structures on a superalgebra
\mathcal{A}
and study some of their properties;
in particular we construct Poisson-type superalgebroids on the space

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, which is defined as a certain subspace of

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
; we also characterise these superalgebroids. In
Section
3
we prove the equivalence between a Lie superalgebroid structure on

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
and a Gerstenhaber superalgebra (suitably defined in this section)
on graded forms
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
; this result is well-known in the classical case (see,
for instance,
[
9
]
), but it seems to be lacking in the supergeometric setting, which
is the reason why we write down a proof. This last section also establishes the equivalence
between
differential
Gerstenhaber superalgebras and certain Poisson superstructures.

1
Preliminaries

In this work, the category of (real) supermanifolds will be understood in the sense of

[
5
]
; to wit, a supermanifold of dimension
(m|n)
is a pair

\left({M}\middle|\mathcal{R}{M}\right)

where
M
is a smooth manifold of dimension
m
and
\mathcal{R}M
is a superalgebra bundle
over
M
such that each fibre
\mathcal{R}_{p}M
(for any
p
in
M
) is isomorphic to the
exterior algebra
\Lambda{S}^{*}
for some (real) vector space
S
of dimension
n
; the

smooth superfunctions
are the sections of the bundle
\mathcal{R}M
, i.e., they are
the elements of the supercommutative unital algebra
\Gamma\left({\mathcal{R}M}\right)
.

By a
supervector bundle
over
\left({M}\middle|\mathcal{R}{M}\right)
we mean simply a vector
bundle
E

over
M
such that

1.

E=E_{0}\oplus{E_{1}}
, and

2.

each fibre
E_{p}
carries the structure of a (left)
\mathcal{R}_{p}M
-supermodule.

We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for a comprehensive study of
supervector bundles. Do note that the objects studied there are quite more
general than what we have called a supervector bundle; for our purposes, we
need only consider the supermodule structure on the space of sections of

E
—and not a supermanifold structure on its total space—, so our
definition is general enough, but the reader should bear in mind the scope of
the usual definition of a supervector bundle. The space of sections
over

M
of
E
is then a

\Gamma\left({\mathcal{R}M}\right)
-supermodule. We will consider a special kind of these:

1.1 Definition
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a supercommutative algebra. A
standard (left) supermodule

is a bilateral supermodule
N
such that
an=(-1)^{\lfloor{a}\rceil\lfloor{n}\rceil}na
for any
homogeneous
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
n\in{N}
.

There is of course an analogous definition of a right standard supermodule.
Throughout this work, the symbol
\lfloor{\cdot}\rceil
denotes the parity of a
homogeneous element of a superalgebra. It will be tacitly assumed that all
supervector bundles arise from standard supermodules. In particular we adopt
the convention that the module of supervector fields is a left standard module
(over the superalgebra
\mathcal{A}=\Gamma\left({\mathcal{R}M}\right)
), whereas the space of
superforms
\Omega\left({M}\middle|\mathcal{R}{M}\right)
is a bigraded superalgebra (with bigrade over

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
) and a right standard module; we will refer to the

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading as the
parity
of a bihomogeneous superform, and to its

\mathbb{Z}
-grading as the
degree
.

We are interested mostly on the supermanifolds whose smooth superfunctions are
the differential forms on ordinary manifolds, that is where

\mathcal{R}M=\Lambda{T}^{*}M
.

1.2 Definition
.

The supermanifold
\left({M}\middle|{\Lambda{T^{*}}M}\right)
is the
cotangent
supermanifold
on
M
. We will denote it by
\left({M}\middle|{TM}\right)
(cf.

[
5
, Section 1.4]
for this notation and its meaning).

In what follows, in order to save space, we shall denote a supermanifold by

\mathcal{M}=\left({M}\middle|\mathcal{R}{M}\right)
, its tangent superbundle by
T\mathcal{M}
, and a supervector
bundle simply by
E
.

1.3 Definition
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a supermanifold and
E
a supervector bundle over
\mathcal{M}
. A

superalgebroid structure

on
E
is an even bundle map
\rho:\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(T\mathcal{M})\simeq\operatorname{Der}(\Gamma(\mathcal{R}M))
(the
anchor
) and a
bilinear operation
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\rightarrow\Gamma(E)
(the
bracket
) such that

1.

\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
gives
\Gamma(E)
the structure of a Lie superalgebra,
and

2.

\llbracket X,fY\rrbracket=\rho(X)(f)Y+(-1)^{\lfloor{X}\rceil\lfloor{f}\rceil}f\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket
for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(E)
,
f\in\Gamma\left({\mathcal{R}M}\right)

(graded
Leibniz identity).

1.4 Examples
.

1.

The supertangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
is a Lie superalgebroid
with
\rho=\operatorname{Id}
and whose brackets is the usual supercommutator of supervector
fields.

2.

If
L\colon{T}\mathcal{M}\to{T^{*}}\mathcal{M}
is a bundle
isomorphism, then
T^{*}\mathcal{M}
becomes a Lie superalgebroid by
defining
\rho={L}^{\operatorname{-}\!1}
and
\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=L([{L}^{\operatorname{-}\!1}(\alpha),{L}^{\operatorname{-}\!1}(\beta)])
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the same bracket of the previous example.

2
Poisson-type superalgebroids

We now turn our attention to special sorts of
\mathbb{R}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
.

2.1 Definition
.

A
graded Poisson algebra
of degree
k

(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded associative and graded-commutative algebra

(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet},\cdot)
together with
an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear operation, the
Poisson bracket
,

\{\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that

1.

\{\mathcal{A}^{a},\mathcal{A}^{b}\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{a+b+k}
(the bracket
has degree
k
);

2.

\{f,g\}=-(-1)^{(\left|{f}\right|+k)(\left|{g}\right|+k)}\{g,f\}
(graded skew-symmetry);

3.

\{f,\{g,h\}\}=\{\{f,g\},h\}+(-1)^{(\left|{f}\right|+k)(\left|{g}\right|+k)}\{g,\{f,h\}\}
(graded Jacobi identity), and

4.

\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+(-1)^{(\left|{f}\right|+k)(\left|{g}\right|)}g\{f,h\}

(graded Leibniz identity),

for all
f,g,h\in\mathcal{A}
. Here
\left|{\cdot}\right|
denotes the degree of an element of

\mathcal{A}
.

Note that a
graded-commutative algebra furnishes a supercommutative algebra by defining the parity of a

\mathbb{Z}
-homogeneous element as the parity of its degree; in this case, parity and
degree are
indistinguishable.

For any graded algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the space
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is
defined as
the kernel of the multiplication map

\mathbf{m}\colon\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
; let us define

\begin{split}\mathbf{d}\colon\mathcal{A}&\to\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\\
a&\mapsto a\otimes{1}-1\otimes{a}\end{split}

(2.1)

and observe that
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is generated by the image of this
map; also, note that
\mathbf{d}
is a derivation with values in the latter module,
which is the dual module to the one of derivations
\operatorname{Der}\mathcal{A}
with the
pairing

\langle D,\mathbf{d}{a}\rangle=D(a)

(2.2)

(all this
is in accordance with
[
4
, p.3]
).

2.2 Remark
.

The module
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is technically a bimodule with
left and right multiplications

a\otimes(\alpha\otimes\beta)=a\alpha\otimes\beta\quad\text{and}\quad(\alpha\otimes\beta)\otimes{a}=\alpha\otimes\beta{a}

respectively. Since the definition of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
renders it
as a submodule of the one above, we can consider it to be a bilateral
module on its own,
but a right module
with respect to the pairing
(
2.2
). However, since our definition of a superalgebroid
implicitly assumes such an object to be a left module, we shall treat

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
as a left module whenever necessary, considering
it as a submodule of
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
; this is done in order
to avoid clumsy computations below.

Let us note,
however, that since we shall
be working with
\mathcal{A}=\Gamma\left({\mathcal{R}M}\right)
, the space

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is in
fact the space of
1
-superforms on
\mathcal{M}
and the map
\mathbf{d}
is
simply the
exterior superderivative, which is even and has degree
1
; its definition
agrees with the above.

2.3 Examples
.

1.

Any Poisson algebra in the classical sense is a graded Poisson algebra whose
bracket has degree
0
.

2.

If
(V,g)
is a pseudoeuclidean vector space then defining on
the
exterior algebra

\Lambda V
the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
by the relations

(a)

\{1,\cdot\}=0
;

(b)

\{x,y\}=g(x,y)
for
x
and
y
in
V
, and

(c)

\{x,y\wedge z\}=\{x,y\}z-\{x,z\}y
for all
x,y
and
z
in
V
,

we obtain a graded Poisson algebra of degree
-2
.

3.

If
(M,(\cdot,\cdot))
is a Poisson manifold, then by defining on

\mathcal{A}=\Omega(M)

the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
by the relations

(a)

\{a,b\}=0
for
a,b\in\Omega^{0}(M)
;

(b)

\{{d}a,b\}=\{a,{d}b\}=(a,b)
for
a,b\in\Omega^{0}(M)
;

(c)

\{{d}a,{d}b\}={d}(a,b)
, and

(d)

\{\alpha,\beta\wedge\gamma\}=\{\alpha,\beta\}\wedge\gamma+(-1)^{(\left|{\alpha}\right|-1)\left|{\beta}\right|}\beta\wedge\{\alpha,\gamma\}
for all

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
in
\Omega^{1}(M)
,

we obtain a graded Poisson algebra on
\mathcal{A}
of degree
-1
. This
bracket was completely characterised in
[
1
]
.

If
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is graded Poisson algebra of degree
k
, we can define an

\mathcal{A}
-module morphism
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{A})
by

\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})=\{f,\cdot\}
for
f\in\mathcal{A}
(where
\mathbf{d}
is
defined by (
2.1
)), and extending by

\mathcal{A}
-linearity.
This map furnishes a derivation of degree
k
on
\mathcal{A}
.

We can also define a bracket

\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\times\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
by

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{(\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil+k)(\lfloor{\beta}\rceil+k)}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))

(2.3)

where
\lrcorner
denotes interior product and which satisfies

1.

\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=-(-1)^{(\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil+k)(\lfloor{\beta}\rceil+k)}\llbracket\beta,\alpha\rrbracket

2.

\llbracket\alpha,\llbracket\beta,\gamma\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket,\gamma\rrbracket+(-1)^{(\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil+k)(\lfloor{\beta}\rceil+k)}\llbracket\beta,\llbracket\alpha,\gamma\rrbracket\rrbracket

3.

\llbracket\alpha,f\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)(f)\beta+(-1)^{(\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil+k)\lfloor{f}\rceil}f\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
, which
can
be verified with direct computations.

2.4 Definition
.

When
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a graded Poisson algebra of even degree, then

(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is called an
Poisson
superalgebra
.

Note that if
k
is even then
\lfloor{\lfloor{a}\rceil+k}\rceil=\lfloor{a}\rceil
for all

\alpha
in
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, so it suffices to consider the case

k=0
.

2.5 Theorem
.

If
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Poisson superalgebra, then
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie superalgebroid with anchor map

\rho

defined by
\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})=\{f,\cdot\}
for
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
bracket defined
by

\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))

(2.4)

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

All the required properties can be checked directly from the definition
of the bracket
(
2.4
).
To verify the Jacobi identity, it is easier to consider a system
\left\{{\mathbf{d}{f_{i}}}\right\}_{i\in I}
of
generators of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, and to express the elements
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
as
\alpha=\sum_{i}{g_{i}}(\mathbf{d}{f_{i}})
for
g_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
.
∎

2.6 Example
.

If
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold, we can define

\displaystyle\{\phi,\cdot\}

\displaystyle=0

(2.5)

\displaystyle\{\mu,\nu\}

\displaystyle=g(\mu^{\sharp},\nu^{\sharp})

(2.6)

\displaystyle\{\mu,\nu\wedge\xi\}

\displaystyle=g(\mu^{\sharp},\nu^{\sharp})\xi-g(\mu^{\sharp},\xi^{\sharp})\nu

(2.7)

for
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
,
\mu,\nu,\xi\in\Omega^{1}(M)
; here,
\alpha^{\sharp}
is the vector field associated to a
1
-form
\alpha
via the
musical isomorphism
induced by
g
.
It is easy to see that
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
defines a structure of
graded Poisson algebra of degree
-2
on
\mathcal{A}=\Omega(M)
(in fact,

2
of
2.3
is a particular case of this example, when
M=\{*\}
and
(M,g)

is a pseudoriemannian manifold). Then, by Theorem
2.5
,
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie superalgebroid with anchor map

\rho
defined by
\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})=\{f,\cdot\}
for
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
bracket defined by (
2.4
) for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.

More explicitly, if
\alpha=f_{1}\mathbf{d}{h_{1}}
and
\beta=f_{2}\mathbf{d}{h_{2}}
with

f_{1},f_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
, then

\begin{split}\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket&=f_{1}\{h_{1},f_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{h_{2}}-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}f_{2}\{h_{2},f_{1}\}\mathbf{d}{h_{1}}\\
&-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil+\lfloor{h_{2}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil}f_{2}f_{1}\mathbf{d}\{h_{2},h_{1}\}\end{split}

(2.8)

Theorem
2.5
tells us that given a Poisson superalgebra we have a Lie
superalgebroid naturally associated to it. We are interested now in the
reciprocal question: When
does a Lie superalgebroid
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)

determine a Poisson superalgebra on
\mathcal{A}
?

In order to answer this question, let us consider a Lie superalgebroid

(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
; for any

f,h\in\mathcal{A}
we can define

\{f,h\}=(\mathbf{d}{h})(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}))=(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}))(h),

(2.9)

which is clearly
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear and
satisfies the Leibniz identity

\begin{split}\{f,hh^{\prime}\}&=(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}))(hh^{\prime})=\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})(h)h^{\prime}+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{h}\rceil}h\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})(h^{\prime})\\
&=\{f,h\}h^{\prime}+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{h}\rceil}h\{f,h^{\prime}\},\end{split}

for all
f,h,h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
homogeneous.

Thus, in order to get a Poisson superalgebra on
\mathcal{A}
we only need to take care of
the skew-supersymmetry and the graded Jacobi identity.

2.7 Definition
.

The anchor map
\rho
of a Lie superalgebroid
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is said to be
skew-supersymmetric
if

\alpha(\rho(\beta))=-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\beta(\rho(\alpha))

(2.10)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.

Observe that if the anchor map is skew-supersymmetric, then the new operation
defined by (
2.9
) is also skew-supersymmetric, so henceforth we
will assume
\rho
to satisfy (
2.10
). Let us now turn our
attention to
the graded Jacobi identity.

2.8 Theorem
.

Let
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
be a Lie superalgebroid, and
define
Q\in\Lambda^{2}(\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{A}))
by
Q(\mathbf{d}{f},\mathbf{d}{g})=\mathbf{d}{g}(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}))
for
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

the operation defined by
\{f,g\}=\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})(g)

satisfies the Jacobi identity,

2.

[Q,Q]_{\operatorname{SN}}=0
,

3.

[\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}),\rho(\mathbf{d}{g})]=\rho(\mathbf{d}\{f,g\})
, where

\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is defined in
1
.

Here,
[\cdot,\cdot]_{SN}
denotes the generalisation of the
Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket to elements of the bigraded algebra

\Lambda\operatorname{Der}\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

For the equivalence of
1
and
2
see
[
3
, Equation
(3.8)]
).

Suppose that the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
satisfy the graded Jacobi identity, that is,

\{f,\{g,h\}\}=\{\{f,g\},h\}+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,\{f,h\}\}

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
. With this,

\begin{split}[\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}),\rho(\mathbf{d}{g})](h)&=\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})(\rho(\mathbf{d}{g})(h))-(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g})(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})(h))\\
&=\{f,\{g,h\}\}-(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,\{f,h\}\}\\
&=\{\{f,g\},h\}\\
&=\rho(\mathbf{d}\{f,g\})(h).\end{split}

Now suppose that
3
is true, then

\begin{split}\{\{f,g\},h\}&=\rho(\mathbf{d}\{f,g\})(h)=[\rho(\mathbf{d}{f}),\rho(\mathbf{d}{g})](h)\\
&=\{f,\{g,h\}\}-(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,\{f,h\}\}\end{split}

(2.11)

∎

This result motivates the following:

2.9 Definition
.

A Lie superalgebroid
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is of

Poisson
type
if:

1.

the anchor map
\rho
is skew-supersymmetric, in the sense of
(
2.10
); and

2.

both the bracket and anchor map satisfy any of the conditions of
Theorem
2.8
.

In other words, a Lie superalgebroid
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is of Poisson type if it determines a Poisson superalgebra on
\mathcal{A}
.

Another important issue for us is to determine the form of the bracket of a Lie
superalgebroid of
Poisson type. We know
[
10
, Example 2.4.5]
that the classical example of Poisson
supermanifolds
leads to brackets of the type

\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\pi^{\sharp}(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))

where
\pi
is a Poisson structure on a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
and

\pi^{\sharp}\colon{T\mathcal{M}}\to{T^{*}\mathcal{M}}
is the musical morphism
associated to it. The following result characterises the class of such
algebroids.

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
be a Lie
superalgebroid with skew-supersymmetric anchor
\rho
, and
\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g\rrbracket=\mathbf{d}\{f,g\}
;
then the bracket of the Lie superalgebroid is of the form

\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha))).

(2.12)

Proof.

Since every element of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is an
\mathcal{A}
-linear
combination of elements of the
form
\mathbf{d}{f_{i}}
for some
f_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
, it is enough to prove the
statement for

\alpha=f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}
and
\beta=f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}
:

\begin{split}\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket&=\llbracket f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\rrbracket\\
&=\rho(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})(f_{2})\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}f_{2}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})(f_{1})\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil}f_{2}f_{1}[\mathbf{d}{g_{2}},\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}]\end{split}

\begin{split}&=f_{1}\{g_{1},f_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}f_{2}\{g_{2},f_{1}\}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil}f_{2}f_{1}\mathbf{d}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\\
&=f_{1}\{g_{1},f_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{1+\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}f_{1}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{f_{2}}\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}f_{2}\{g_{2},f_{1}\}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\end{split}

\begin{split}&\quad+(-1)^{1+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil}f_{2}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{f_{1}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil}f_{2}f_{1}\mathbf{d}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}f_{1}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{f_{2}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil}f_{2}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\mathbf{d}{f_{1}}\\
&=\rho(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})\lrcorner\mathbf{d}(f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}}\rceil}\rho(f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\lrcorner\mathbf{d}(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})\\
&\quad+\mathbf{d}(f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}(\rho(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})))\\
&=\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))\qed\end{split}

An equivalent statement for the hypothesis of Proposition

2.10
is:

2.11 Proposition
.

If
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie
superalgebroid with skew-supersymmetric anchor
\rho
, then the
following assertions are equivalent:

1.

\llbracket\mathbf{d}{f},\mathbf{d}{g}\rrbracket=\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{d}{g}(\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})))=\mathbf{d}\{f,g\}

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A},

2.

\mathbf{d}\alpha=0=\mathbf{d}\beta
implies
\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=0

for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

First, let us assume that
1
holds. By Proposition
2.10
we know
that
the bracket is of the form

\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha))),

and hence condition
2
holds,

\begin{split}\mathbf{d}\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket&=\mathbf{d}(\rho(\alpha)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\beta-(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\rho(\beta)\lrcorner\mathbf{d}\alpha+\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha))))\\
&=\mathbf{d}^{2}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))=0,\end{split}

whenever
\mathbf{d}\alpha=0=\mathbf{d}\beta
.

Now let us assume that condition
2
holds. Define

C(\alpha,\beta)=\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket-\mathbf{d}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))

for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
. Notice that
C
is skew-supersymmetric and

\mathbf{d}{C}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbf{d}(\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket)-\mathbf{d}^{2}(\beta(\rho(\alpha)))=0,

for any closed
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Note that

\begin{split}C(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})&=\llbracket f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\rrbracket-\mathbf{d}(dg_{2}(\rho(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})))\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})(f_{1})\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}f_{1}[\mathbf{d}{g_{2}},\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}]\\
&\quad-\mathbf{d}(f_{1}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})(g_{2}))\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})(f_{1})\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}f_{1}[\mathbf{d}{g_{2}},\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}]\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})(g_{2})\mathbf{d}{f_{1}}\\
&\quad-f_{1}\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})(g_{2}))\\
&=f_{1}C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})-(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\lrcorner\mathbf{d}(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})\end{split}

If
f_{1}=g_{1}
, then

\begin{split}C(g_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})&=g_{1}C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\\
&\quad-(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\lrcorner\mathbf{d}(g_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})\\
&=g_{1}C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\end{split}

Now, applying the operator
\mathbf{d}
and taking into account that
g_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}
is
closed, we get

\begin{split}0&=\mathbf{d}{C}(g_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\\
&=\mathbf{d}(g_{1}C(dg_{1},dg_{2}))\\
&=\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\wedge C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})+g_{1}\mathbf{d}{C}(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})\\
&=\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\wedge C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}).\end{split}

(2.13)

Interchanging the roles of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, we have

0=\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\wedge C(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}},\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})=-(-1)^{\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\wedge C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}).

(2.14)

Relations (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) imply that
\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}
,
\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}
and

C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})

are
linearly dependent for arbitrary
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.
In particular, if
\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}
and
\mathbf{d}g_{2}
are linearly independent, then

C(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}})=0
, and
hence

\llbracket\mathbf{d}{g_{1}},\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\rrbracket=\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}(\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}})))=\mathbf{d}\{g_{1},g_{2}\}

which proves the Proposition.
∎

As an immediate consequence of all the work made above, we obtain:

2.12 Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the cotangent supermanifold on the smooth manifold
M
,
and suppose
(\Omega(M),\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Poisson superalgebra. Under
these conditions, the cotangent superbundle

T^{*}\mathcal{M}
inherits a Lie superalgebroid structure whose anchor map
is given by
\rho(\mathbf{d}{f})=\{f,\cdot\}
and whose bracket is given by
(
2.12
).

3
Further structures on superforms

Recall we defined on (
2.1
) the map

\mathbf{d}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\ker{\mathbf{m}}
that associates to any
a
a
generator of the latter space, which we have denoted as

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
; now we set
\Omega^{0}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}
and
define

\Omega^{r}(\mathcal{A}):=\left(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\right)^{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega(\mathcal{A}):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{A});

so defined, the space
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
is a bigraded-commutative algebra;
if
\mathcal{A}=\Omega(M)

then
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
is naturally isomorphic to the superalgebra of graded
forms on the supermanifold
\left({M}\middle|{TM}\right)
—see, for instance,

[
11
, Section 2]
—.

Let now
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Poisson superalgebra. We define a
bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
for elements of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
by the
following relations:

\begin{split}[f,g]&=0\\
[f,\mathbf{d}{g}]&=[\mathbf{d}{f},g]=\{f,g\}\\
[\mathbf{d}{f},\mathbf{d}{g}]&=\mathbf{d}\{f,g\}\end{split}

(3.1)

for all
f,g,h\in\mathcal{A}
, and we extend to all of
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
by

[\alpha,\beta\wedge\gamma]=[\alpha,\beta]\wedge\gamma+(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\beta\wedge[\alpha,\gamma]

to obtain the following structure

3.1 Definition
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},\wedge)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded superalgebra; if there is a
bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}

such that

1.

[\alpha,\beta]=-(-1)^{(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1)+\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}[\beta,\alpha]

2.

[\alpha,[\beta,\gamma]]=[[\alpha,\beta],\gamma]+(-1)^{(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1)+\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}[\beta,[\alpha,\gamma]]

3.

[\alpha,\beta\wedge\gamma]=[\alpha,\beta]\wedge\gamma+(-1)^{(|\alpha|-1)|\beta|+\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{\beta}\rceil}\beta\wedge[\alpha,\gamma]

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{B}
, then
(\mathcal{B},\wedge,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a
Gerstenhaber superalgebra
.

The following is a generalisation of a well-known result (see
[
13
]
and
references therein):

3.2 Theorem
.

\Omega(\mathcal{A})
is a Gerstenhaber superalgebra if and only if

\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is a Lie superalgebroid.

Proof.

Suppose that there is Lie superbracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
of degree
-1

that makes

\Omega(\mathcal{A})
into a Gerstenhaber superalgebra. It is clear that

(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket)
is a Lie superalgebra with

\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket=[\alpha,\beta]
for
\alpha
and

\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.
Now, for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
and
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
, it
follows
from the properties of Gerstenhaber superalgebra that

[\alpha,fg]=[\alpha,f]g+(-1)^{\lfloor{\alpha}\rceil\lfloor{f}\rceil}f[\alpha,g].

Hence,
[\alpha,\cdot]
is a derivation on
\mathcal{A}
. If we define

\rho:\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}(\mathcal{A})
by

\rho(\alpha)=[\alpha,\cdot]
, then

\llbracket\alpha,f\beta\rrbracket=\rho(\alpha)(f)\beta+f\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket

for
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
.
This shows that
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is
indeed a Lie
superalgebroid.

Conversely, given a Lie superalgebroid
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
,we define

\begin{split}[f,g]&=0\\
[\alpha,f]&=\rho(\alpha)(f)\\
[\alpha,\beta]&=\llbracket\alpha,\beta\rrbracket\end{split}

for
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
; for
decomposable forms
\mathbf{A}={\alpha}_{1}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}{\alpha}_{k}
and

\mathbf{B}={\beta}_{1}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}{\beta}_{l}
we define

[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]=\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{k,l}(-1)^{a_{i}+b_{j}+k-1}\mathbf{A}_{i}\wedge[\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}]\wedge\mathbf{B}_{j},

where we have defined:

\mathbf{A}_{i}:={\alpha}_{1}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}\widehat{{\alpha}_{i}}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}{{\alpha}_{k}}
,

\mathbf{B}_{j}:={\beta}_{1}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}\widehat{{\beta}_{j}}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}{{\beta}_{l}}
,

a_{i}:=\lfloor{\alpha_{i}}\rceil\sum_{p=i+1}^{k}\lfloor{\alpha_{p}}\rceil+i
and

b_{j}:=\lfloor{\beta_{j}}\rceil\sum_{q=1}^{j-1}\lfloor{\beta_{q}}\rceil+j
; the notation

{\alpha}_{1}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}\widehat{{\alpha}_{i}}{\wedge}\cdots{\wedge}{{\alpha}_{k}}
means the
i
th factor is omitted. A long
computation shows that
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
with
[\cdot,\cdot]
is indeed
a Gerstenhaber superalgebra.
∎

3.3 Definition
.

Let
[\cdot,\cdot]
be a bracket on
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
such that

(\Omega(\mathcal{A}),\wedge,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Gerstenhaber
superalgebra. If the exterior superderivative is a derivation of
degree 1 for
[\cdot,\cdot]
, that is if

\mathbf{d}[\alpha,\beta]=[\mathbf{d}\alpha,\beta]+(-1)^{|\alpha|-1}[\alpha,\mathbf{d}\beta]

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega(\mathcal{A})
, we call it a
differential Gerstenhaber
superalgebra
.

3.4 Theorem
.

A Gerstenhaber superbracket on
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
is differential if and
only if it is the bracket associated to a Poisson superstructure on

\mathcal{A}
in the sense
of (
3.1
).

Proof.

Let
[\cdot,\cdot]
be a differential Gerstenhaber superbracket on

\Omega(\mathcal{A})
. We
define the map

\{\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
by

(f,g)\mapsto\{f,g\}:=[f,\mathbf{d}g]
which is a Poisson superbracket; indeed:

1.

\{f,g\}=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,f\}
because

\begin{split}0=&\mathbf{d}[f,g]=[\mathbf{d}{f},g]+(-1)^{(|f|-1)}[f,\mathbf{d}{g}]\\
&=-(-1)^{(|\mathbf{d}{f}|-1)(|g|-1)+\lfloor{\mathbf{d}{f}}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}[g,\mathbf{d}{f}]-[f,\mathbf{d}{g}]\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,f\}-\{f,g\};\end{split}

2.

the graded Jacobi identity holds:

\begin{split}\{f,\{g,h\}\}&=[f,\mathbf{d}[g,\mathbf{d}{h}]]\\
&=[f,[\mathbf{d}{g},\mathbf{d}{h}]+(-1)^{(|g|-1)}[g,\mathbf{d}^{2}h]]\\
&=[[f,\mathbf{d}{g}],\mathbf{d}{h}]+(-1)^{(|f|-1)(|\mathbf{d}{g}|-1)+\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{\mathbf{d}{g}}\rceil}[\mathbf{d}{g},[f,\mathbf{d}{h}]]\\
&=\{\{f,g\},h\}+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\{g,\{f,h\}\}\\
\end{split}

3.

the graded Leibniz identity holds:

\begin{split}\{f,g\wedge h\}&=[f,\mathbf{d}(g\wedge h)]=[f,\mathbf{d}g\wedge h+g\wedge dh]\\
&=[f,\mathbf{d}g\wedge h]+[f,g\wedge\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[f,\mathbf{d}g]\wedge h+(-1)^{(|f|-1)|\mathbf{d}g|+\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{\mathbf{d}g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}{g}\wedge[f,h]\\
&\quad+[f,g]\wedge\mathbf{d}{h}+(-1)^{(|f|-1)|g|+\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g\wedge[f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=\{f,g\}\wedge h+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g\wedge\{f,h\}\end{split}

So,
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is a Poisson superbracket on
\Omega(M)
; moreover

\{\cdot,\cdot\}

induces
[\cdot,\cdot]
by the relations (
3.1
).

Now, let
[\cdot,\cdot]:\Omega(\mathcal{A})\times\Omega(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\Omega(\mathcal{A})
be the bracket associated to a Poisson superalgebra

(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
. It is enough show that it is differential for

\alpha=\mathbf{d}f

and

\beta=g\mathbf{d}h
for some
f,g,h\in\mathcal{A}
:

\begin{split}\mathbf{d}[\alpha,\beta]&=\mathbf{d}[\mathbf{d}f,g\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=\mathbf{d}([\mathbf{d}f,g]h+(-1)^{(\left|{\mathbf{d}f}\right|-1)(\left|{g}\right|-1)+\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h])\\
&=\mathbf{d}[\mathbf{d}f,g]\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}g\wedge[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=\mathbf{d}\{f,g\}\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{(\left|{\mathbf{d}{f}}\right|-1)(|g|-1)+\lfloor{df}\rceil\lfloor{\mathbf{d}g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}{g}\wedge[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g]\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{(|\mathbf{d}f|-1)(|g|-1)+\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{\mathbf{d}g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}g\wedge[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g\wedge\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}^{2}f,g\mathbf{d}h]+(-1)^{|\mathbf{d}f|-1}[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}(g\mathbf{d}h)]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}\alpha,\beta]+(-1)^{|\alpha|-1}[\alpha,\mathbf{d}\beta]\qed\end{split}

Let
(\Omega(\mathcal{A}),\wedge,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathbf{d})
be a differential
Gerstenhaber
superalgebra; from the Theorem above,

(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie superalgebroid.
Moreover,
\rho
is skew-supersymmetric since

\begin{split}f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}(\rho(f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}))&=\langle f_{2}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}),f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}\rangle\\
&=(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil}f_{2}[\mathbf{d}g_{2},\mathbf{d}g_{1}]f_{1}\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil}f_{2}[\mathbf{d}g_{1},\mathbf{d}g_{2}]f_{1}\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil+\lfloor{g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{g_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil\lfloor{[\mathbf{d}g_{1},\mathbf{d}g_{2}]}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}}\rceil+\lfloor{f_{1}\lfloor{[\mathbf{d}g_{1},\mathbf{d}g_{2}]}\rceil}\rceil}f_{1}[\mathbf{d}g_{1},\mathbf{d}g_{2}]f_{2}\\
&=-(-1)^{\lfloor{f_{1}\mathbf{d}g_{1}}\rceil\lfloor{f_{2}\mathbf{d}g_{2}}\rceil}\langle f_{1}\rho(\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}),f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}\rangle\\
&=f_{2}\mathbf{d}{g_{2}}(\rho(f_{1}\mathbf{d}{g_{1}}))\end{split}

for all
f_{1},f_{2},g_{2},g_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the pairing of
supervectorfields and
1
-superforms. Also,
due to the fact that the bracket is differential
we have

\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}f)(g))=\mathbf{d}([\mathbf{d}f,g])=[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g].

That is, the hypotheses of Proposition
2.10
are satisfied, therefore
the bracket of the Lie superalgebroid is of the form (
2.4
) and
therefore the
superalgebroid is of Poisson type. Now, we can prove the following

3.5 Proposition
.

If
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie algebroid
with

1.

\rho
skew-supersymmetric

2.

\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g\rrbracket=\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}f)(g))
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A}

then
(\Omega(\mathcal{A}),\wedge,[\cdot,\cdot],\mathbf{d})
is a differential
Gerstenhaber superalgebra.

Proof.

We only need to prove that the Gerstenhaber superalgebra induced by the superalgebroid is
differential. For this, first note that

\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g\rrbracket=\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}f)(g))=\mathbf{d}[\mathbf{d}f,g]

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
and remember that it is enough show that the bracket
induced by
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
in
\Omega(\mathcal{A})
it is differential for

\alpha=\mathbf{d}f
and
\beta=g\mathbf{d}h
for some
f,g,h\in\mathcal{A}
:

\begin{split}\mathbf{d}[\mathbf{d}f,g\mathbf{d}h]&=\mathbf{d}\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,g\mathbf{d}h\rrbracket\\
&=\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}f)(g)\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h\rrbracket)\\
&=\mathbf{d}(\rho(\mathbf{d}f)(g))\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}g\wedge\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h\rrbracket+(-1)^{\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g\mathbf{d}\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h\rrbracket\\
&=\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g\rrbracket\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{\lfloor{f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}g\wedge\llbracket\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h\rrbracket+(-1)^{\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}g\mathbf{d}^{2}[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}g]\wedge\mathbf{d}h+(-1)^{\lfloor{\mathbf{d}f}\rceil\lfloor{g}\rceil}\mathbf{d}g\wedge[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}h]\\
&=[\mathbf{d}f,\mathbf{d}(g\mathbf{d}h)].\end{split}

This establishes the proposition.
∎

Observe that if
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Poisson superalgebra,
we know from the
previous
section that
(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a
Lie superalgebroid of Poisson type with bracket defined by
(
2.4
)
. All of the above implies that
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
coincides with the restriction on
1
-superforms of the bracket defined
by
(
3.1
)
. We have now proved:

3.6 Theorem
.

(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Poisson superalgebra if and only if

(\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}),\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket,\rho)
is a Lie superalgebroid of Poisson
type with bracket defined by
(
2.12
)
.

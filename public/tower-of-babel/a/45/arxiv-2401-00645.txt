Density bounds for unit ball packings relative to their outer parallel domains
Károly Bezdek, Zsolt Lángi

Abstract
. We prove that the highest density of non-overlapping translates of a given centrally symmetric convex domain relative to its outer parallel domain of given outer radius is attained by a lattice packing in the Euclidean plane. This generalizes some earlier (classical) results. Sharp upper bounds are proved for the analogue problem on congruent circular disks in the spherical (resp., hyperbolic) plane and on congruent balls in Euclidean 3 3 -space.

Introduction

Packings and coverings, in particular, those with congruent balls, have a long history in mathematics, with many applications in sciences. Motivated by recent developments in natural sciences (see for example,
[
9

15

To prove the theorems, we use the description of the properties of Voronoi cells of point systems in normed planes in e.g.
[

\mathbf{M}
is strictly convex and it has a

-class, simple curve. For the proofs of our theorems, we need Lemma

Lemma 2.1
.

For any mutually distinct points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}
,
B(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and
B(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})
intersect if and only if
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}
are not collinear, and in this case they have a unique intersection point.

Proof.

The proof is based on the following reformulation of the statement: if
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}
are collinear, then there is no homothetic copy of
\mathbf{M}
containing all of them in its boundary, and if they are not collinear, there is a unique such copy. To prove this statement, it is enough to observe that for any distinct points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
, there is a
1
-parameter family of homothetic copies of
\mathbf{M}
containing them in their boundaries.
∎

First, we show how Theorem
1.9
yields Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Consider a packing
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{M}:\mathbf{c}\in X\}
of translates of
\mathbf{M}
such that the density of
X+\mathbf{M}
with respect to its outer parallel domain
X+(1+\lambda)\mathbf{M}
of outer radius
\lambda
is at least
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(\lambda)-\varepsilon
. Note that adding translates of
\mathbf{M}
to
\mathcal{P}
may decrease its density, and thus, we cannot assume that it is a saturated packing. Nevertheless, we show that there is some
r>0
and a modified packing
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
with density at least
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(\lambda)-\varepsilon
such that for any
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{M}}
,
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\mathbb{M}}\leq 2r
for some
\mathbf{c}\in X
.

Indeed, for any
r>2
, let
X_{r}=X\cap(r-1)\mathbf{M}
. Clearly, we may choose some sufficiently large value
r^{\prime}
such that the density of
X_{r^{\prime}}+\mathbf{M}
with respect to its outer parallel domain of outer radius
\lambda
is at least
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(\lambda)-\varepsilon
. Choose some countable set
Y\subset{\mathbb{M}}
such that
\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{M}}\geq 2r^{\prime}
for any distinct
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in Y
, and
Y+2r^{\prime}\mathbf{M}=\mathbb{M}
. We define
X^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\mathbf{y}\in Y}(\mathbf{y}+X_{r^{\prime}})
. Then
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{M}:\mathbf{c}\in X^{\prime}\}
is a packing of translates of
\mathbf{M}
. Furthermore, the density of this packing is at least
\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(\lambda)-\varepsilon
, and for any
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{M}}
, we have
\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}\|_{\mathbb{M}}\leq 2r^{\prime}
for some
\mathbf{c}\in X^{\prime}
.

In the remaining part, we show that if
\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{M}:\mathbf{c}\in X\}
is a packing of translates of
\mathbf{M}
such that for some
r>0
,
X+r\mathbf{M}={\mathbb{M}}
, then

(2.1)

\frac{\area(\mathbf{M})}{A_{6}(\mathbf{M},\lambda)}\geq\delta_{\mathbb{M}}(\lambda,\mathcal{P}).

Here we observe that by Theorem
1.9
, there is an
\mathbf{o}
-symmetric B-
6
-gon circumscribed about
\mathbf{M}
with area equal to
A_{6}(\mathbf{M},\lambda)
. The sides of this B-
6
-gon belong to bisectors of the form
B(\mathbf{o},\pm\mathbf{x})
,
B(\mathbf{o},\pm 2\mathbf{y})
and
B(\mathbf{o},\pm(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}))
for some linearly independent vectors
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{M}
, implying that the packing
\mathcal{P}_{lattice}=\{k\mathbf{x}+m\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{M}:k,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a lattice packing of
\mathbf{M}
with density
\frac{\area(\mathbf{M})}{A_{6}(\mathbf{M},\lambda)}
, and thus, (
2.1
) implies Theorem
1.5
.

Let us decompose
\mathbb{M}
into the Voronoi cells of the elements of
\mathcal{P}
, where for any
\mathbf{c}_{i}\in X
,
\mathbf{C}_{i}
denotes the cell of
\mathbf{c}_{i}
in this decomposition. Furthermore, by our assumptions and Lemma
2.1
,
\mathbf{C}_{i}
is a starlike topological disk, and its boundary is a closed chain of arcs such that each arc is a connected part of a bisector
B(\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{c}_{j})
for some
\mathbf{c}_{j}\in X
. These arcs are called the
sides
of
\mathbf{C}_{i}
, and the unique intersection point of two consecutive sides is a
vertex
of
\mathbf{C}_{i}
. This also shows that the decomposition is edge-to-edge. Let
n_{i}
denote the number of sides of
\mathbf{C}_{i}
, and
A_{i}
denote the area of the region
\mathbf{C}_{i}\cap\left(\mathbf{c}_{i}+(1+\lambda)\mathbf{M}\right)
.

For any
R>0
, let
\mathcal{P}_{R}
denote the family of Voronoi cells contained in
R\mathbf{B}^{2}
, and let
N_{R}
denote the cardinality of
\mathcal{P}_{R}
.
Since for any cell
\mathbf{C}_{i}
,
\mathbf{c}_{i}+\mathbf{M}\subseteq\mathbf{C}_{i}\subseteq\mathbf{c}_{i}+r\mathbf{M}
, it is easily deduced that the limit
n:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{C}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}n_{i}}{N_{R}}
exists. Furthermore, as the edge graph of the Voronoi decomposition, formed by the vertices and sides of the cells, is planar, by Euler’s theorem we obtain that
n\leq 6
.
Then, by Theorem
1.9
,

(2.2)

\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{C}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}A_{i}}{N_{R}}\geq\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{C}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{R}}A_{n_{i}}(\mathbf{M},\lambda)}{N_{R}}\geq A_{6}(\mathbf{M},\lambda),

which readily yields the inequality in (
2.1
).

.

\mathbf{P}

\mathcal{S}

\widehat{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}}

from

.
For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, let
\mathbf{p}_{x}

\bd(\mathbf{M})

\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}})
denote the (closed) region bounded by the counterclockwise oriented arc in

touching

\mathbf{p}_{y}

\mathbf{o}\notin R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}})

A(\widehat{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}})

(1+\lambda)\mathbf{M}\cap R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}})

[

]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function with
f(\widehat{pp})=0
for all
p\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. For any integer
n\geq 3
, let

m_{n}=\inf\left\{\sum_{S\in X}f(S):X\subset\mathcal{S}\hbox{ is a tiling of }\mathbb{S}^{1}\hbox{ with }|X|=n\right\}.

If for any
\widehat{x_{2}x_{3}}\subset\widehat{x_{1}x_{4}}
, we have

f(\widehat{x_{1}x_{3}})+f(\widehat{x_{2}x_{4}})\leq f(\widehat{x_{1}x_{4}})+f(\widehat{x_{2}x_{3}}),

then the sequence
\{m_{n}\}
is convex.
Furthermore, if in addition, there is some positive integer
k
that divides
n
, and
f
has
k
-fold rotational symmetry, and there is an
n
-element tiling
X
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
m_{n}=\sum_{S\in X}f(S)
, then there is an
n
-element tiling
X^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
k
-fold rotational symmetry such that
m_{n}=\sum_{S\in X^{\prime}}f(S)
.

Let
I[X]:\mathbb{E}^{2}\to\{0,1\}
denote the indicator function of the set
X\cap(1+\lambda)\mathbf{M}
for any
X\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{2}
. Consider arcs
\widehat{x_{2}x_{3}}\subset\widehat{x_{1}x_{4}}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then it is easy to check that

I[R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{4}})](\mathbf{q})+I[R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}_{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}})](\mathbf{q})-I[R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{3}})](\mathbf{q})-I[R(\widehat{\mathbf{x}_{2}\mathbf{x}_{4}})](\mathbf{q})\geq 0

. This shows that the conditions in Lemma
2.2
are met for the function

, implying Theorem
1.9
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

In the proof, for any points
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{P}
, where on the sphere we assume that they are not antipodal, we denote by
[\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}]
the shortest geodesic arc connecting
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
. For any triangle
\mathbf{T}
with
\mathbf{q}
as a vertex, we set

\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}):=\frac{\area(\mathbf{T}\cap\mathbf{B})}{\area(\mathbf{T}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\lambda})},\hbox{ and }\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}):=\frac{\area(\mathbf{T}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\lambda})}{\area(\mathbf{T})}.

Before proving the theorem, we first prove two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L
be a line through
\mathbf{q}
, and let
R_{p}
be a half line starting at a point
\mathbf{p}
of
L\setminus\inter(\mathbf{B})
such that
R_{p}
is perpendicular to
L
. For any
s>0
(on the sphere for any
0<s<\frac{\pi}{2})
, let
\mathbf{p}(s)
denote the point of
R_{p}
whose distance from
\mathbf{p}
is
s
. Furthermore, for any
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
(on the sphere for any
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\frac{\pi}{2})
, let
\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2})
denote the triangle with vertices
\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}(s_{1})
and
\mathbf{p}(s_{2})
. Then
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))
and
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))
are non-increasing functions of
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<s_{2}^{\prime}
be given (on the sphere assume also that
s_{2}^{\prime}<\frac{\pi}{2}
). Without loss of generality, assume that the ratio of the angles of
\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2})
and
\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}^{\prime})
at
\mathbf{q}
is rational. Then we can dissect
\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}^{\prime})
into triangles
\mathbf{T}_{1},\mathbf{T}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{T}_{k}
, all having
\mathbf{q}
as a vertex such that they have equal angles at
\mathbf{q}
, denoted by
\gamma
, any two consecutive members share a side, and
\mathbf{p}(s_{2})
is a vertex of some triangles
\mathbf{T}_{i}
and
\mathbf{T}_{i+1}
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}
be the rotation around
\mathbf{q}
with angle
\gamma
that moves
\mathbf{p}(s_{1})
closer to
\mathbf{p}(s_{2})
. Then, for any value of
i
,
\varphi(T_{i})\subseteq T_{i+1}
,
\varphi(\mathbf{T}_{i}\cap\mathbf{B})=\mathbf{T}_{i+1}\cap\mathbf{B}
and
\varphi(\mathbf{T}_{i}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\lambda})=\mathbf{T}_{i+1}\cap\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}
.
This proves that
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))\geq\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}^{\prime}))
, implying that
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))
is a non-increasing function of
s_{2}
. To prove that it is a non-increasing function of
s_{1}
, we may apply a similar argument.

To verify the monotonicity property of
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))
, note that there is a unique value
\bar{s}
such that
\mathbf{p}(s)\in\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}
if and only if
s\leq\bar{s}
. Furthermore, if
0<s_{1}<s_{2}\leq\bar{s}
, then
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))=1
. Thus, to prove that
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))
is a non-increasing function of
s_{2}
, it is sufficient to consider values
\bar{s}\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<s_{2}^{\prime}
. But in this case, repeating the argument in the previous paragraph, we obtain

\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}))\geq\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}(s_{1},s_{2}^{\prime}))
, showing that the function is non-increasing in the second variable. To prove the monotonicity in the first variable, we can repeat the same argument.
∎

3.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
be a point at distance
R(r)
from
\mathbf{q}
. For
i=1,2
, let
\mathbf{T}_{i}=\conv\{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}_{i}\}
be a triangle with right angle at
\mathbf{p}_{i}
, let
r_{i}
denote the distance of
\mathbf{p}_{i}
and
\mathbf{q}
. Assume that
r\leq r_{1}<r_{2}<R(r)
. Then

\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{1})\geq\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{2}),\hbox{ and }\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{1})\geq\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{2}).

Proof.

It is easy to show that
r_{1}<r_{2}
implies that the angle of
\mathbf{T}_{1}
at
\mathbf{q}
is larger than that of
\mathbf{T}_{2}
, and thus we may assume that
[\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}_{2}]
and
[\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}_{1}]
cross. Let the intersection point of these segments be
\mathbf{x}
(see Figure
4
). Let
\mathbf{T}_{a}=\conv\{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{x}\}
,
\mathbf{T}_{b}=\conv\{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\}
and
\mathbf{T}_{c}=\conv\{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q}^{\prime},\mathbf{p}_{2}\}
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{a})\geq\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{b})\geq\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{c}),

implying that
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{1})\geq\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{2})
. By the same lemma we similarly obtain that
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{a})\geq\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{b})
. Furthermore, an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
yields
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T}_{b})\geq\frac{\area(\mathbf{B}_{\lambda}\cap\mathbf{T}_{c})}{\area(\mathbf{T}_{c})}
, which readily implies the required inequality for
\hat{\rho}
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.10
.
Let us dissect the Voronoi cell
\mathbf{V}
of
\mathbf{q}
into triangles by connecting
\mathbf{q}
to all vertices of
\mathbf{V}
. Furthermore, if the orthogonal projection of
\mathbf{q}
onto any side of
\mathbf{V}
lies inside the side, we dissect the corresponding triangle obtained in the previous step into two right triangles by connecting
\mathbf{q}
to this ortogonal projection. Note that any triangle obtained in this way is either obtuse, or a right triangle.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a triangle obtained in the previous paragraph. Let
r_{T}
denote the distance of
\mathbf{q}
from the sideline of
\mathbf{T}
opposite to it, and let
d_{T}
denote the distance of
\mathbf{q}
from the farthest vertex of
\mathbf{T}
. Note that
r_{T}\geq r
and
d_{T}\geq R(r)
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we have that fixing the value of
r_{T}
,
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T})
and
\hat{\rho}(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T})
are maximized if
\mathbf{T}
is a right triangle, and
d_{T}=R(r)
. Under these conditions, by Lemma
3.2
,
\rho(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T})
is maximal if

. But in this case

, implying Theorem

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.12

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the distance of every face plane of
\mathbf{V}
from
\mathbf{o}
is at most
1+\lambda
.
Let the face-planes of
\mathbf{V}
be
H_{i}
with
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. For any
H_{i}
, let
\mathbf{C}_{i}
denote the spherical segment of
(1+\lambda)\mathbf{B}^{3}
truncated by
H_{i}
from this ball.
To minimize the density, we need to maximize
\vol_{3}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{C}_{i}\right)
. Let the height of
\mathbf{C}_{i}
, defined as
(1+\lambda)
minus the distance of
H_{i}
from
\mathbf{o}
, be denoted by
h_{i}
.
Note that

\vol_{3}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{C}_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\vol_{3}(\mathbf{C}_{i}).

A well-known formula shows that
\vol_{3}(\mathbf{C}_{i})=\pi\left((1+\lambda)h_{i}^{2}-\frac{h_{i}^{3}}{3}\right)
. We set

F(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{m}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi\left((1+\lambda)h_{i}^{2}-\frac{h_{i}^{3}}{3}\right).

Consider the function
f(h):=\pi\left((1+\lambda)h^{2}-\frac{h^{3}}{3}\right)
. Then
f^{\prime}(h)=\pi h(2(1+\lambda)-h)>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(h)=\pi(2(1+\lambda)-2h)>0
for all
0<h<1+\lambda
. It follows that
f
is a
strictly increasing and convex function of
h
on the interval
[0,\lambda]
.
Hence, if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\leq 12\lambda
, then

(4.1)

F(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(h_{i})\leq 12\pi\left((1+\lambda)\lambda^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2}\right).

Note that the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is
\vol_{3}((1+\lambda)\mathbf{B}^{3}\setminus\mathbf{D})
, implying Theorem
1.12
in this case.

In the remaining part we show that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\leq 12\lambda
. Suppose the contrary, and note that this implies
m>12
. We recall the following lemma of Hales from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{0}:=1.26
, and
\mathbf{x}_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,m
be a set of points in
\mathbb{E}^{3}
satisfying
2\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|\leq 2\alpha_{0}
and
2\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j}\|
for any
i\neq j
.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}L\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|\right)\leq 12,

where
L(t):=\frac{\alpha_{0}-t}{\alpha_{0}-1}
for
0\leq t\leq\alpha_{0}
.

Applying Lemma
4.1
for the centers of the balls generating the faces of
\mathbf{V}
, we have

12\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}L(1+\lambda-h_{i})=\frac{\alpha_{0}-1-\lambda}{\alpha_{0}-1}m+\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}.

Combining it with the indirect assumption, we obtain

(4.2)

12\lambda<\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\leq 12(\alpha_{0}-1)-m(\alpha_{0}-1-\lambda).

From this, a rearrangement of the terms in the leftmost and rightmost expressions yields

(m-12)(\alpha_{0}-1-\lambda)\leq 0,

which contradicts the fact that both factors on the left-hand side are positive.

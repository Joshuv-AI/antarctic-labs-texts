Symplectic forms on trisected 4-manifolds
Peter Lambert-Cole

Abstract
. Previously work of the author with Meier and Starkston showed that every closed symplectic manifold ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) with a rational symplectic form admits a trisection compatible with the symplectic topology. In this paper, we describe the converse direction and give explicit criteria on a trisection of a closed, smooth 4-manifold X X that allows one to construct a symplectic structure on X X . Combined, these give a new characterization of 4-manifolds that admit symplectic structures. This construction motivates several problems on taut foliations, the Thurston norm and contact geometry in 3-dimensions by connecting them to questions about the existence, classification and uniqueness of symplectic structures on 4-manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth, closed, oriented 4-manifold. It is of great interest in low-dimensional topology to determine whether
X
admits a symplectic structure and if so, to classify all such symplectic structures. For example, work of Donaldson and Gompf showed that
X
admits a symplectic structure if and only if it admits a compatible Lefschetz pencil. This essentially reduces the problem to
algebra and mapping class groups of surfaces.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is a
trisection
of
X
, which is a particular decomposition into three 4-dimensional 1-handlebodies.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a closed, smooth,oriented 4-manifold. A
trisection
of
X
is a decomposition
X=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup Z_{3}
such that

(1)

each sector
Z_{\lambda}
is a 4-dimensional 1-handlebody,

(2)

each double intersection
H_{\lambda}=Z_{\lambda}\cap Z_{\lambda+1}
is a 3-dimensional 1-handlebody of genus
g
, and

(3)

the triple intersection
\Sigma=Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\cap Z_{3}
is a closed, orientable surface of genus
g
.

The trisection
\mathcal{T}
and the 4-manifold
X
itself can be encoded (up to diffeomorphism) by a
trisection diagram

(\Sigma;\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta},\mathbf{\gamma})
, which consists of a closed, oriented surface and three cut systems of curves. This data determines how to build a trisected 4-manfiold from the ‘inside-out’. The cut systems determine how to attach each double intersection
H_{\lambda}
to the central surface to produce the
spine

H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}
. Capping off by attaching the 4-dimensional sectors
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}
is unique up to diffeomorphism by the well-known result of Laudenbach and Poenaru
[
LP72
]
.

The following is a natural question to ask.

Question 1.2
.

Is there a criterion on a trisection that ensures
X
admits a symplectic structure?

However, answering this question has proven surprising difficult. The first problem is to find the correct notion of compatibility between a symplectic structure and a trisected 4-manifold. For example, the central surface is nullhomologous, therefore it cannot be symplectic, and in general has negative Euler characteristic, therefore it cannot be Lagrangian. The notions of
Weinstein trisections
of symplectic 4-manifolds and
Stein trisections
of complex manifolds were introduced in
[
LCM22
]
. Some recent progress has been made in constructing Stein trisections
[
LCM22
,
LC21
,
Zup22
]
. Moreover, by using Auroux’s work on representing symplectic 4-manifolds as branched coverings of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

[
Aur00
]
, it was shown that every symplectic 4-manifold admits a Weinstein trisection
[
LCMS21
]
. This construction is evidence that the latter structure is the conceptually correct one to look for.

A second difficulty is that the compatibility between trisections and symplectic topology does not appear to fit into existing conceptual frameworks. For example, the boundary of a relative trisection inherits an open book decomposition
[
GK16
, Theorem 20]
. This suggests that for a compact, symplectic manifold with contact boundary, the open book induced by the relative trisection should support the contact structure. However, consider the complement of a complex curve
\mathcal{C}_{d}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. The contact structure on the boundary of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus\nu(\mathcal{C}_{d})
is Stein-fillable, hence tight.
Naively applying the method of Miller-Kim
[
KM20
]
to the curves in bridge position given in
[
LCM22
]
can yield an induced open book that is not right-veering. In particular, this open book supports a contact structure that is
not
tight.

In this paper, we describe how to construct a symplectic structure from geometric data on the spine of a trisected 4-manifold.

1.1.
Representing cohomology via cocycles

The construction of the closed 2-form
\omega
on the trisected 4-manifold in Theorem
1.4
inherently determines the class
[\omega]\in H^{2}_{DR}(X)
. Before stating the theorem, we review how to interpret cohomology classes on trisected 4-manifolds.

Feller-Klug-Schirmer-Zemke
[
FKSZ18
]
and Florens-Moussard
[
FM22
]
gave presentations of the singular homology groups of a 4-manifold in terms of a trisection diagram. This was reinterpreted in
[
LC22
]
as the Cech cohomology groups of certain presheaves with respect to an open cover determined by the trisection.

In particular, let
\mathcal{H}^{i}_{DR}
denote the presheaf on
X
that assigns to each open set
U
the group
H^{i}_{DR}(U)
. By abuse of notation, let
\mathcal{T}=\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\}
denote the open cover of
X
by three sets obtained by slightly thickening the three sectors of the trisection. In particular,
U_{\lambda}
is an open neighborhood of
X_{\lambda}
;
U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\lambda+1}
is an open neighborhood of the handlebody
H_{\lambda}
, and
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\cap U_{3}
is an open neighborhood of the central surface
\Sigma
.

By analyzing the Cech-to-derived-functor spectral sequence, applied to the open cover
\mathcal{T}
and presheaf
\mathcal{H}^{i}_{DR}
, one can show there is an isomorphism (
[
LC22
]
)

H^{2}_{DR}(X)\cong\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{H}^{1}_{DR})

In words, this states that a class
[\omega]\in H^{2}_{DR}(X)
can be represented by a 1-cocycle of closed 1-forms. Specifically, there is a triple
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
, where
\beta_{\lambda}
is a closed 1-form on
H_{\lambda}
, and

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=0

when restricted to the central surface
\Sigma
.

1.2.
Main theorem

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a closed, oriented 4-manifold and let
\mathcal{T}
be a trisection of
X
with central surface
\Sigma
and 3-dimensional sectors
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}
. A
symplectic 1-cocycle
for
(X,\mathcal{T})
is a triple
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})
of closed, nonvanishing 1-forms on the three handlebodies such that the restrictions
\{\beta_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda|_{\Sigma}}\}
satisfy the following conditions

(1)

the cocycle condition:

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=0

(2)

the pairwise positivity condition

\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}\geq 0

(3)

they all vanish at the same
2g-2
points
b_{1},\dots,b_{2g-2}
in
\Sigma
and there exists a smooth function
f:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and neighborhoods
\{b_{i}\in U_{i}\}
such that
f|_{U_{i}}=\pm 1
and
\sum_{i}f(b_{i})=0
and

\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}=\beta_{\lambda}+d(sf)

on a collar neighborhood of
\partial H_{\lambda}=\Sigma
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a closed, oriented 4-manifold and let
\mathcal{T}
be a trisection of
X
with central surface
\Sigma
. Let
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})
be a symplectic 1-cocycle for
(X,\mathcal{T})
. Then

(1)

there is a symplectic form
\omega
on
\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
representing the class

(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})\in\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{H}^{1}_{DR})\cong H^{2}_{DR}(X)\cong H^{2}_{DR}(\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}))

In addition, there exists inward-pointing Liouville vector fields for
\omega
along each of the three boundary components
\widehat{Y}_{1},\widehat{Y}_{2},\widehat{Y}_{3}
.

(2)

If each of the induced contact structures
(\widehat{Y}_{\lambda},\widehat{\xi}_{\lambda})
are tight, then there exists a global symplectic form
\omega
on
X
representing the class

(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})\in\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{H}^{1}_{DR})\cong H^{2}_{DR}(X)

that is well-defined up to diffeomorphsim.

(3)

an
n
-parameter family of symplectic 1-cocycles extends to an
n
-parameter family of symplectic forms
\omega
. In particular, if the class
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})\in\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{H}^{1}_{DR})\cong H^{2}_{DR}(X)
remains constant in the family, then the constructed symplectic forms are isotopic.

1.3.
How can the construction fail?

Many smooth 4-manifolds are known to not admit symplectic structures. Where might the obstructions lie when considered in light of Theorem
1.4
?

(1)

No triple
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
of
closed, nonvanishing
1-forms exist
. This may be for cohomological reasons, as
[\beta_{\lambda}]
is not in the image of the restriction map
H^{1}_{DR}(H_{\lambda})\rightarrow H^{1}_{DR}(\Sigma)
. The 1-cocycle
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
encodes a class
[\omega]
in
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
and
[\beta_{1}]\wedge[\beta_{2}]
is positive in
H^{2}_{DR}(\Sigma)
if and only if
[\omega]\wedge[\omega]
is positive in
H^{4}_{DR}(X)
. In particular, if
b_{2}^{+}(X)=0
, then the required cocycle cannot exists. Secondly, if there is no cohomological obstruction, any triple
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
of closed 1-forms may be forced to vanish in some points.

(2)

The induced contact structure
\widehat{\xi}_{\lambda}
is not tight
. This may happen at the level of homotopy. The contact structure is a 2-plane field, which are classified up to homotopy by their Euler class in
H^{2}(Y)
and a 3-dimensional obstruction. In addition, the contact structure is homotopic to the field of
J
-complex tangencies along
Y
, where
J
is an
\omega
-tame almost-complex structure. This extends to an almost-complex structure on the 4-dimensional sector if and only if
\xi
has vanishing Euler class and is homotopic to the unique tight contact structure. Consequently, if
X
does not admit an almost-complex structure, then at least one of the constructed contact structures must be overtwisted. Secondly, in the unique correct homotopy class of 2-plane fields, there exists one tight and one overtwisted contact structure. Therefore, the construction may simply yield the overtwisted one.

The 4-manifold
3\mathbb{CP}^{2}
has
b_{2}^{+}>0
and admits an almost-complex structure, but is known not to admit a symplectic structure. Moreover, it admits a genus 3 trisection. It would be interesting to understand how no possible choice of data on this trisection can satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.4
. Consequently, this motivates the task of classifying the grafted contact structures that can be constructed on the genus 3 Heegaard splitting of
S^{3}
.

1.4.
Symplectic structures and trisection diagrams

A trisected 4-manifold can be encoded by a
trisection diagram
, which is a tuple
(\Sigma;\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta},\mathbf{\gamma})
consisting of the central surface
\Sigma
and three cut systems of curves
\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta},\mathbf{\gamma}
that determine how the handlebodies
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}
are glued in. There has been significant interest in finding a corresponding diagrammatic theory of symplectic trisections. One approach is a so-called
multisection with divides
, introduced by Islambouli and Starkston
[
IS-Divides
]
.

The restrictions
\{\beta_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}|_{\Sigma}\}
determine a different picture on the central surface, namely three transverse singular foliations
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3}
. Moreover, if we suppose that
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
are
integral
1-forms, then they define maps

\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}:\Sigma\longrightarrow S^{1}

with
2g-2
nondegenerate critical points. The preimage of a regular value is a multicurve and for a fixed
\beta_{\lambda}
, the union of these multicurves determine a pants decomposition of
\Sigma
– i.e. a maximal system of
3g-3
curves on
\Sigma
.

The foliations
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
are not transverse to the boundary
\partial H_{\lambda}=\Sigma
, but they do satisfy a condition analogous to being taut, namely the existence of a closed transversal intersecting every leaf. The leaves of a taut foliation are known to minimize the Thurston norm
[
Thu86
]
. If
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
is integral, then compact leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
also minimize the generalized Thurston norm (specifically, Scharlemann’s generalization
[
Sch89
]
).

This begs the question of whether the extensions
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
are necessary pieces of data or are uniquely determined by data defined in terms of the central surface
\Sigma
.

Question 1.5
.

Given the data of

(1)

an intrinsically harmonic, integral 1-form
\beta
on
\Sigma
with nondegenerate zero set
B
,

(2)

a Lagrangian subspace
L\subset H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
containing
[\beta]
, and

(3)

a class
c_{1}\in H_{1}(\Sigma,B;\mathbb{Z})

Is there (a unique) handlebody
H
with
\partial H=\Sigma
such that

(1)

L=\text{image}(H^{1}(H)\rightarrow H^{1}(\Sigma))
, and

(2)

for each regular value
\theta
for
\beta:\Sigma\rightarrow S^{1}
, the Thurston norm of the unique element
[F]\in H_{2}(H,\beta^{-1}(\theta))
is given by the pairing
\langle c_{1},\beta^{-1}(\theta)\rangle
?

1.5.
Grafted contact structures

In the course of the proof of Theorem
1.4
, three contact structures are constructed in Theorem
1.4
. In order to cap off the 4-dimensional sectors with strong symplectic fillings, it is necessary that these contact structures be tight.

Intuitively, these contact structures are obtained from ‘taut’ foliations by a so-called
grafting
process. This is a very natural operation that has nonetheless been hardly studied. The inspiration arises from complex analysis and geometry. Let
X
be a complex surface. A subset
P\subset X
is an
analytic polyhedron
if there exists an open set
U
containing the closure of
P
and holomorphic functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
on
U
such that

P=\{z\in U:|f_{1}(z)|,\dots,|f_{n}(z)|\leq 1\}

For a generic choice of
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
, the boundary of
P
is a smooth manifold with corners.

The top-dimensional faces of
\partial P
are Levi-flat, which means they are foliated by complex codimension 1 holomorphic submanifolds. The function

g=\text{max}(|f_{1}|,\dots,|f_{n}|)

is a continuous, plurisubharmonic function on
U
and
P
is the sublevel set
g^{-1}([0,1])
If
X
is a Stein surface, then
g
can be approximated by a smooth, strictly plurisubharmonic function.
\widetilde{g}
. The sublevel set
\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{g}^{-1}([0,1])
is a compact Stein domain and the field of complex tangencies along
\partial\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{g}^{-1}(1)
is a contact structure.

Given the importance of tightness in Theorem
1.4
, it would be very interesting to produce criteria that ensures the resulting grafted contact structure is tight.

Problem 1.6
.

Give topological criteria so that the contact structures obtained in Theorem
1.4
are tight.

Establishing overtwistedness criteria may prove easier, as there may be an analogous criteria to
non-right-veering monodromy
for an open book decomposition.

1.6.
Symplectic cone

Let
X
be a closed, oriented 4-manifold. The
symplectic cone
of
X
is the subset of
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
that can be represented by a symplectic form. It is a cone since any nonzero multiple of a symplectic form is also a symplectic form. Given a trisection
\mathcal{T}
, we define the
symplectic cone of
\mathcal{T}
to be the subset of
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
obtained by constructing symplectic forms via Theorem
1.4
from symplectic 1-cocycles.

Question 1.7
.

Is the symplectic cone of
(X,\mathcal{T})
always equal to the symplectic cone of
X
?

We can obtain partial information about the symplectic cone of a trisection. Suppose that
(X,\omega)
is constructed via Theorem
1.4
from a symplectic 1-cocycle
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
and each
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
is a rational closed 1-form. Then by integrating, we can identify
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
with a map

\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}:H_{\lambda}\rightarrow S^{1}

Consequently, all regular leaves are compact. Let
S_{\lambda}\subset H_{2}(H_{\lambda},\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(H_{\lambda};\mathbb{Z})
denote the positive cone generated by these compact leaves. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{T},\widetilde{\beta})
be the set of triples
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})\in S_{1}\oplus S_{2}\oplus S_{3}
that satisfy the cocycle condition

F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}=0

when restricted to
\Sigma
. The set
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{T},\widetilde{\beta})
generalizes the cone of curves on a complex surface.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
be a rational symplectic 1-cocycle defining on a global symplectic structure on
(X,\mathcal{T})
. Then for every
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{T},\widetilde{\beta})
, there is a symplectic 1-cocycle

\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+PD(F_{\lambda})\}

that defines a global symplectic form on
X
. In particular,
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+PD(F_{\lambda})\}
lies in the symplectic cone of
(X,\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

Let
F_{\lambda}
be a finite, real-linear combination of surfaces in
S_{\lambda}
. Then we can construct Poincare duals to the compact leaves in such a way that each
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+PD(F_{\lambda})
is a closed, nonvanishing 1-form on
H_{\lambda}
. Since the triples
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})
and
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})
satisfy the cocycle condition, so do their sum. Since
F_{\lambda}
is a positive linear combination of leaves of
\text{ker}(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
, the inequality
\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}\geq 0
implies that we can assume

\beta_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\partial F_{\lambda+1}),PD(\partial F_{\lambda})\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1},PD(\partial F_{\lambda})\wedge PD(\partial F_{\lambda+1})\geq 0

Consequently,

\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+tPD(F_{\lambda})\}

for
t\in[0,1]
is a family of symplectic 1-cocycles that determine a 1-parameter homotopy of global symplectic structures on
(X,\mathcal{T})
.
∎

1.7.
Classification of symplectic structures

There are several natural equivalence relations on symplectic structures on a 4-manifold
X
:

(1)

the pair
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are
isotopic
if they are connected by a path of cohomologous symplectic forms,

(2)

the pair
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are
homotopic
if they are connected by a path of symplectic forms,

(3)

the pair
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are
diffeomorphic
if there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi:X\rightarrow X
such that
\phi^{*}(\omega_{2})=\omega_{1}
, and

(4)

the pair
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are
deformation-equivalent
if there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi:X\rightarrow X
such that
\omega_{1}
and
\phi^{*}(\omega_{2})
are homotopic.

We refer the reader to the excellent survey of Salamon on the classification and uniqueness of symplectic forms on 4-manifolds
[
Sal13
]
, which has also been incorporated into the most recent edition of
[
MS17
]
.

Question 1.9
.

Suppose that
[\omega_{1}]=[\omega_{2}]
. Are they diffeomorphic?

The construction here gives one potential approach to this question: The symplectic structure obtained (a) encodes the cohomology class of
[\omega]
, (b) is well-defined by the data up to diffeomorphism, and (c) extends to 1-parameter families of the data. Hence studying the moduli of data may be profitable in classifying symplectic structures. Moreover, this construction means that different symplectic forms can be directly compared.

Theorem 1.10

(
[
LCMS21
]
)
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be rational symplectic forms on
X
. Then there exists a single smooth trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
such that
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
can both be constructed via Theorem
1.4
on
\mathcal{T}
.

Question 1.11
.

Given a pair of cohomologous symplectic 1-cocycles
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
and
\{\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}
on
(X,\mathcal{T})
, are they connected by a 1-parameter family of symplectic 1-cocycles?

The condition that
\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}\geq 0
, with
2g-2
nondegenerate zeros, implies that we can choose a conformal class of metrics
\{\lambda^{2}g\}
on
\Sigma
such that
\beta_{\lambda},\beta_{\lambda+1}
are harmonic with respect to the Riemannian metrics in this conformal class and furthermore
\ast_{\lambda^{2}g}\beta_{\lambda}=\beta_{\lambda+1}
. Equivalently, we can encode this data in terms of a pair
(j,\zeta)
where
j
is a complex structure on
\Sigma
and
\zeta
is a holomorphic 1-form, and recover
\beta_{\lambda},\beta_{\lambda+1}
as the real and imaginary parts of
\zeta
.

In particular, consider the space
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}
of holomorphic 1-forms on Riemann surfaces of genus
g
. A point
(C,\zeta)
in
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}
is one necessary piece of data in Theorem
1.4
. Isoperiodic deformations of
(C,\zeta)
, which are deformations that preserve
[\zeta]\in H^{1}_{DR}(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
, then lead to a family of symplectic forms in the same DeRham cohomology class in
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
. The connectivity and dynamics of leaves of the isoperiodic foliation on
\Omega\mathcal{M}_{g}
was determined by Calsamiglia, Deroin and Francaviglia
[
CDF15
]
.

2.
Inspiration

We describe the inspiration for Theorem
1.4
by working out the geometry of
(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS})
with respect to the standard trisection and then describing how the geometry pulls back to an arbitrary projective surface via a holomorphic branched covering.

2.1.
The Fubini-Study form on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

The construction for a general trisection is analogous to the construction of the Fubini-Study form on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

In particular, we have that

\omega_{FS}=-\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\log(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2})

The Kaḧler potential
\phi=\log(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2})
is well-defined on homogeneous coordinates, up to adding a constant, hence its exterior derivatives are well-defined. In the chart
z_{\lambda-1}=1
, we can equivalently describe the form by

\omega_{FS}=dd^{\mathbb{C}}\log(1+|z_{\lambda}|^{2}+|z_{\lambda+1}|^{2})

where
d^{\mathbb{C}}f:=df(J-)
for a function
f
and
J:T\mathbb{CP}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}
the almost-complex structure on the tangent bundle. In particular, the form

\alpha_{\lambda}=d^{\mathbb{C}}\log(1+|z_{\lambda}|^{2}+|z_{\lambda+1}|^{2})

is a primitive for
\omega_{FS}
in this coordinate chart. In polar coordinates
z_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\phi(r_{\lambda},r_{\lambda+1})\left(2r_{\lambda}dr_{\lambda}(J-)+2r_{\lambda+1}dr_{\lambda+1}(J-)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi(r_{\lambda},r_{\lambda+1})\left(2r_{\lambda}^{2}d\theta_{\lambda}+2r_{\lambda+1}^{2}d\theta_{\lambda+1}\right)

where

\phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}

Now, consider the change of coordinates from
\{z_{\lambda-1}=1\}
to
\{z_{\lambda+1}=1\}
:

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\theta_{\lambda}-\theta_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle s_{\lambda-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r_{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda-1}

\displaystyle=-\theta_{\lambda+1}

Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda-1}

\displaystyle=\phi(s_{\lambda-1},s_{\lambda})(2s^{2}_{\lambda-1}d\psi_{\lambda-1}+2s^{2}_{\lambda}d\psi_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=r^{2}_{\lambda+1}\phi(r_{\lambda},r_{\lambda+1})\left(-2\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\lambda+1}}d\theta_{\lambda+1}+2\frac{r^{2}_{\lambda}}{r^{2}_{\lambda+1}}(d\theta_{\lambda}-d\theta_{\lambda+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi(r_{\lambda},r_{\lambda+1})\left(-2d\theta_{\lambda+1}+2r^{2}_{\lambda}(d\theta_{\lambda}-d\theta_{\lambda+1})\right)

So that

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}-\alpha_{\lambda-1}

\displaystyle=\phi\left(2r^{2}_{\lambda+1}d\theta_{\lambda+1}+2d\theta_{\lambda+1}+2r^{2}_{\lambda}d\theta_{\lambda+1}\right)=2r^{2}_{\lambda}d\theta_{\lambda}

Therefore, along
H_{\lambda}=Z_{\lambda}\cap Z_{\lambda-1}=\{r_{\lambda}=1\}
, we obtain the closed 1-form
\beta_{\lambda}=2d\theta_{\lambda}
. In summary, by tracing through the
\check{\text{C}}
ech-DeRham isomorphism, we can replace a closed 2-form with a 1-cocycle of closed 1-forms:

\omega_{FS}\mapsto(2d\theta_{1},2d\theta_{2},2(-d\theta_{1}-d\theta_{2}))

Now consider the restriction to the central torus. The pair
\{d\theta_{1},d\theta_{2}\}
define a framing of
T^{*}T^{2}
. Moreover, they are Hodge dual for some flat metric
C
on
T^{2}
that makes this an orthonomal framing. The three 1-forms induce three kernel foliations
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{3}
that are linear with respect to the coordinates
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
. The condition
d\theta_{\lambda}\wedge d\theta_{\lambda+1}>0
implies that these foliations are pairwise transverse.

2.2.
Branched covering

Now consider a projective surface
X
with a holomorphic branched covering

p:X\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}

that is generic with respect to the standard trisection
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Such a branched covering always exists by choosing an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{N}
and projecting onto a generic projective plane. We can then pull back the standard trisection via
p
to get a decomposition

X=\widetilde{Z}_{1}\cup\widetilde{Z}_{2}\cup\widetilde{Z}_{3}

into three analytic polyhedra. In general, the smooth decomposition is not a trisection, as the sectors may have 2-handles. However, it accurately illustrates the geometric picture in the holomorphic setting.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset X
be the ramification locus of
\pi
. Assume that
p(\mathcal{K})
is immersed and self-transverse and in general position with respect to the stratification induced by the trisection. In particular, it intersects the central
T^{2}
in
2b
points and each
H_{\lambda}=S^{1}\times\mathbb{D}^{2}
along arcs. Positivity of intersection implies that these arcs are transverse to the foliation by holomorphic disks.

Now, consider the sector
Z_{\lambda}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. It lies in the open chart
U_{\lambda}=\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus L_{\lambda}
, where
L_{\lambda}=\{z_{\lambda-1}=0\}
denotes the projective line at infinity with respect to this chart. In addition, it is the analytic polyhedron defined by the holomorphic coordinate functions
z_{\lambda},z_{\lambda+1}
in the chart
U_{\lambda}
.

The preimage
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}=p^{-1}(U_{\lambda})
is the complement of a hyperplane divisor in
X
, therefore it is an open Stein surface in
X
. The preimage
\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}=p^{-1}(Z_{\lambda})
is an analytic polyhedron

\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}=\{w\in\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}:\text{max}\left(|z_{\lambda}\circ p(w)|,|z_{\lambda+1}\circ p(w)|\right)\leq 1\}

in
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}
, where
f_{1}=z_{\lambda}\circ p
and
f_{2}=z_{\lambda+1}\circ p
are holomorphic functions on the Stein surface
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}
.

The boundary of
\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}
is piecewise-smooth, with a corner at
p^{-1}(T^{2})
. Over the codimension-0 faces
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda+1}
, the boundary is Levi-flat and foliated by compact holomorphic curves. In particular,

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\theta}\left\{w\in\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}:z_{\lambda}\circ p(w)=e^{2\pi i\theta}\right\}\cap\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\theta}\left\{w\in\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}:z_{\lambda+1}\circ p(w)=e^{2\pi i\theta}\right\}\cap\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}

If the branch locus
p(\mathcal{K})
intersects
H_{\lambda}
transversely, then these curves are nonsingular.

Next, the branch locus
p(\mathcal{K})
intersects
T^{2}
transversely. Since
T^{2}
is nullhomologous, the signed intersection of
p(\mathcal{K})
with
T^{2}
vanishes and there exist
b
positive intersections and
b
negative intersections for some
b
. If
p
has degree
n
as a finite holomorphic map, then
p^{-1}(T^{2})
is a simple
n
-fold branched covering of
T^{2}
over the
2b
points. In general, it may be disconnected. In
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, the torus
T^{2}
is Lagrangian with respect to the Fubini-Study Kähler form. The pullback
p^{*}(\omega_{FS})
also vanishes along
\Sigma=p^{-1}(T^{2})
, although this form is not symplectic. Its square vanishes precisely along the ramification locus
\mathcal{K}
. To modify it to a symplectic form, it is necessary to add an exact 2-form
d\phi
as in
[
Aur00
, Proposition 10]
. The restriction of this 2-form to
\Sigma
will have nonzero support in a neighborhood of the ramification locus
B=\Sigma\cap\mathcal{K}
. Consequently, this surface is no longer Lagrangian.

Finally, a flat metric
g
on
T^{2}
pulls back to a singular flat metric on
\Sigma
. The closed 1-forms
(d\theta_{2},-d\theta_{1},d\theta_{1}-d\theta_{2})
pull back to harmonic 1-forms with respect to the conformal structure determined by the singular flat metric. Moreover, the form
p^{*}(\beta_{\lambda})
extends across
H_{\lambda}
as the 1-form defining the Levi-flat foliation by holomorphic curves. The fact that

p^{*}(\beta_{\lambda})\wedge p^{*}(\beta_{\lambda+1})=p^{*}(\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1})\geq 0

implies that these foliations meet transversely as well. Thinking 4-dimensionally, this transversality condition is equivalent to the statement that
\Sigma
is a convex corner of the analytic polyhedron
\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}
.

The singular points
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{K}
are the preimages of the branch points on
T^{2}
. We can describe this branching in local coordinates by identifying
T^{2}
with
i\mathbb{R}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
and consider the complex surface

\{z^{2}=w_{1}\mp iw_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}

for a positive (resp. negative) intersection point. The surface
\Sigma
is the intersection of
X
with the subset
\{\text{Re}(w_{1})=\text{Re}(w_{2})=0\}
. The tangent plane at
0\subset\Sigma
is an isolated complex point of
\Sigma
, meaning that
T_{0}\Sigma
is a complex line with respect to the almost-complex structure on
X
(and not simply a copy of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Complex lines come with an orientation, therefore the singular point is oriented and this agrees with the orientation of
p(\mathcal{K})\cap T^{2}
downstairs. But since this orientation is induced by the complex structure, it must extend in a compatible way with the foliation on
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
by holomorphic curves.

Finally, the boundary of
\widetilde{Z}_{\lambda}
can be smoothed to obtain a Stein-fillable contact structure. The defining function
\phi_{\lambda}=\text{max}(|f_{1}|,|f_{2}|)
is a continuous, plurisubharmonic function on
\widetilde{U}_{\lambda}
. It can be
C^{0}
-approximated by a smooth, strictly plurisubharmonic function
\widetilde{\phi}_{\lambda}
. The level set
\widetilde{Y}_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}_{\lambda}(1)
naturally admits a Stein-fillable contact structure.

3.
Handlebody setup

In order to construct the required symplectic structure over each handlebody
H_{\lambda}
, we need to construct an exact 2-form
d\mu_{\lambda}
that orients the leaves of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}=\text{ker}(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
.

3.1.
Morse form foliations

We restrict attention to foliations that are defined as the kernel of a smooth, closed 1-form
\beta
. In particular, a closed 1-form is
Morse
if near each singular point, there exists local coordinates and a function
f
that is quadratic in these coordinates such that
\beta=df
. Moreover, the closed 1-form
\beta
is
intrinsically harmonic
if it is harmonic for some Riemannian metric.

We can apply various results on the topology of Morse form foliations using the following doubling argument. Recall that
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}=\beta_{\lambda}+d(sf)
near the boundary. We can take the double
\#_{g}S^{1}\times S^{2}
of
H_{\lambda}
and extend
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
as a closed 1-form
D(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
on the total space by assuming

D(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})=\beta_{\lambda}+d(s^{2}f)

on a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
. This 1-form has Morse singularities at the singular points
B\subset\Sigma
that are of index 1 or 2, according to the sign of
f
. In addition, the induced foliation is topologically conjugate to
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
.

There are significant restrictions on the topology of a foliation defined by a closed, Morse 1-form. For example, the foliation has trivial holonomy. The leaves of a closed Morse 1-form are either
compact
or
noncompact
. A finite number of
singular
leaves intersect the vanishing locus of the 1-form, while all remaining leaves are
nonsingular
. Given a closed Morse 1-form on a compact
n
-manifold
M
, there is a decomposition
M=M_{c}\cup M_{\int}
into two compact
n
-manifolds such that the interior
M_{c}
is the union of all compact leaves, the interior of
M_{\infty}
is the union of all noncompact leaves, and
M_{c}\cap M_{\infty}=\partial M_{c}\cap\partial M_{\infty}
is a subvariety that is the union of some compactified components of noncompact, singular leaves
[
FKL98
, Proposition 1]
.

A
closed transversal
to a foliation is closed, smooth curve that is everywhere positively-transverse to the foliation.

3.2.
Tautness of the foliation

Recall that a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is
taut
if for every point
p\in M
, there is a closed transversal to
\mathcal{F}
through
p
. In this subsection, we will prove that the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
on
H_{\lambda}
satisfy the following stronger version of tautness.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each point
p\in H_{\lambda}
, there exists

(1)

an embedded, closed transversal
\gamma_{p}
to
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
through
p
,

(2)

an embedded annulus
A_{p}\cong S^{1}\times[0,1]
consisting of a family of embedded, closed transversals to
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
such that the unoriented boundary is
\partial A_{p}=\gamma_{p}\cup\gamma
, where
\gamma
is an embedded curve in
\partial H_{\lambda}=\Sigma
.

The proposition follows easily from the following two results.

Lemma 3.2
.

Through every point
q\in\Sigma\setminus B
, there exists an embedded curve
\gamma\subset\Sigma
that is a closed transversal to the kernel foliation of
\beta_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\beta_{\lambda}
is a harmonic 1-form on
\Sigma
and therefore Calabi’s condition ensures there is an immersed closed transversal through each nonsingular point
[
Cal69
]
. By a perturbation, we can assume that the curve is self-transverse and resolve the self-intersections preserving the fact that is a (possibly disconnected) embedded closed transversal. If this closed transversal is disconnected, choose the component through
p
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Every leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
intersects the boundary
\partial H_{\lambda}=\Sigma
.

This follows immediately by combining Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
. We defer these proofs until after the following corollary and the proof of the main proposition.

Corollary 3.4
.

The doubled 1-form
D(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
on
D(H_{\lambda})
is intrinsically harmonic.

Proof.

Since each leaf intersects
\Sigma
and
\beta_{\lambda}
is intrinsically harmonic on
\Sigma
, then
D(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
satisfies Calabi’s condition.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The boundary foliation admits an embedded closed transversal that intersects every leaf (Lemma
3.2
). Since each leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is connected to the boundary (Proposition
3.3
), we can find
\gamma_{p}
by a leaf-preserving isotopy. The trace of this isotopy is the required annulus

.
∎

We now address the case of compact leaves.

Lemma 3.5
.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
on
H_{\lambda}
has no
T^{2}
leaves.

Proof.

Every torus in a 1-handlebody is compressible and moreover bounds a solid torus
Q
. Then
[\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}]=[0]\in H^{1}_{DR}(Q,\partial Q)
and so there is a function
\phi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(\partial Q)=0
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}=d\phi
on

and therefore
(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})_{x}=d\phi_{x}=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Next, we can use the Euler class of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
to show that every closed, compact leaf must be an (excluded) torus. Using the doubling argument, we get isomorphisms

\displaystyle H_{1}(H,B)

\displaystyle\cong H_{1}(Y,B)

\displaystyle H_{2}(H,\Sigma\setminus B)

\displaystyle\cong H_{2}(Y\setminus B)

We can therefore use the intersection pairing

H_{1}(Y,B)\times H_{2}(Y\setminus B)\rightarrow H_{0}(Y\setminus B)\cong H_{0}(Y)\cong\mathbb{Z}

to define an intersection pairing

\langle,\rangle:H_{1}(H_{\lambda},B)\times H_{2}(H_{\lambda},\Sigma\setminus B)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

Choose a vector field
v_{\lambda}
directing the kernel of
\beta_{\lambda}
on
\Sigma
and extend it to a section of
\text{ker}(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
over
H_{\lambda}
. Generically, this section will vanish along a smooth 1-complex representing a well-defined class Euler class
e(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})\in H_{1}(H_{\lambda},B)
of the foliation.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
L\in H_{\lambda}
be a compact leaf
L
of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}

is given by

\langle e(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}),[L]\rangle

Proof.

A section

of
\text{ker}(\beta_{\lambda})
, restricted to

, is a vector field on

and the Euler characteristic of

, which is therefore given by the signed count of the zeros of

with

.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Every compact leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
intersects
\Sigma
.

Proof.

If
L
is a compact, closed, co-oriented hence oriented leaf, then by Lemma

its Euler characteristic vanishes since

. But this implies it is a torus, which is excluded by Lemma

.
∎

We now finish the proof of Proposition
3.3
by excluding noncomapct leaves.

Lemma 3.8
.

Every noncompact leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
intersects
\Sigma
.

Proof.

The boundary of the closure of a noncompact leaf

contains a compactified component

of a singular leaf (by
[

). The set of accumulation points of

is open in

, hence

intersects
\Sigma
and in particular it intersects
\Sigma
along a compactified component

of a singular, noncompact leaf of the kernel foliation of

on

Choose some point

in

∎

3.3.
Thurston norm

Just as compact leaves of a taut foliation are known to minimize the Thurston norm
[
Thu86
]
, the nonsingular compact leaves of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
minimize the (generalized) Thurston norm defined by Scharlemann
[
Sch89
]
.

To prove this, we first establish an embedding result that reduces the problem to the original case.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
M
be a compact 3-manifold with boundary and let
f:M\rightarrow S^{1}
be a smooth map such that

(1)

f
has no critical points in
M
, and

(2)

f|_{\partial M}
has only nondegenerate critical points of index 1.

.
Then there exists a fibered 3-manifold
\pi:Y\rightarrow S^{1}
and an embedding
i:M\hookrightarrow Y
such that
\pi\circ i=f
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
0
is a regular value of
f|_{\partial M}
. Let
F_{0}=f^{-1}(0)
be the compact, regular level set. As
\theta
varies in
[0,2\pi]
, the topology of the regular level set
f^{-1}(\theta)
changes at the critical points of
f|_{\partial M}
by either adding or removing a 1-handle. Fix a Morse-Smale metric
g
on
\Sigma
. If
p\in\text{Crit}(f|_{\partial M})
corresponds to adding a 1-handle to the regular level set, then we can use the unstable manifold of
p
to isotope the attaching
S^{0}
for this down to
\partial F_{0}
. On the other hand, if
p
is a critical point corresponding to removing a 1-handle, then we can use the stable manifold of
p
to isotope the attaching
S^{0}
for the cancelling 1-handle up to
F_{2\pi}
.

We therefore have two surfaces

\widehat{F}_{0}=F_{0}\cup\{\text{1-handles}\}\qquad\widehat{F}_{2\pi}=F_{2\pi}\cup\{\text{1-handles}\}

Let
M^{0}
be the compact 3-manifold obtained by cutting
M
along
f^{-1}(0)
. Then
M^{0}
embeds into
\widehat{F}_{0}\times[0,2\pi]
in a fiber-preserving manner. Similarly, it also embeds into
\widehat{F}_{2\pi}\times[0,2\pi]
.

Now choose a closed surface
F
and embeddings
\widehat{F}_{0},\widehat{F}_{2\pi}\hookrightarrow F
. To obtain
Y
, we choose any homeomorphism
\phi:F\rightarrow F
such that
\phi(F_{2\pi})=F_{0}
and take the mapping torus

Y=F\times[0,2\pi]/(x,2\pi)\sim(\phi(x),0)

The embedding
M_{0}\subset\widehat{F}_{0}\times[0,2\pi]\subset F\times[0,2\pi]
then descends to an embedding of
M
into
Y
.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
is integral. Then each nonsingular leaf
L
minimizes the Thurston norm for the class
[L]\in H_{2}(H_{\lambda},\partial L)
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
is integral, there exists a map
f:H_{\lambda}\rightarrow S^{1}
such that
f^{*}(d\theta)=\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}
. By Proposition
3.9
, we obtain an embedding
H_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow Y
into a fibered 3-manifold that extends
f
.

Let
L=f^{-1}(\theta_{0})
be a nonsingular, compact leaf. The
L
embeds in the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\theta_{0})
. Let
F_{\theta_{0}}
denote the complement of
L
in the fiber. Let
L^{\prime}
be any embedded surface representing
[L]\in H_{2}(H_{\lambda},\partial L)
. Then
L^{\prime}\cup F_{\theta_{0}}
is a closed surface representing the class of the fiber in
H_{2}(Y)
. Since
\pi^{-1}(\theta_{0})
minimizes the Thurston norm in

, we consequently have the inequality

-\chi(L\cup F_{\theta_{0}})\leq-\chi(L^{\prime}\cup F_{\theta_{0}})

which implies that
-\chi(L)\leq-\chi(L^{\prime})
.
∎

\mathcal{S}:=\bigcup_{\lambda=1,2,3}\gamma_{\lambda}

. This is where the functions
\{\phi^{\prime}_{p}\}
are supported. Here, the primitive is

\alpha_{\lambda}=(t+2)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+(1-s)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi_{p}(s)PD(\gamma_{p})

and so

d\alpha_{\lambda}=dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}-ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p}ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

Therefore

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\left(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p}PD(\gamma_{p})\right)

\displaystyle=dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(t+2)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{t+2}{C}\sum_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\left(-\phi_{p}-\phi^{\prime}_{p}(1-s)\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\left(\phi_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})\wedge PD(\gamma_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(t-1)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{t-1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\left(-\phi_{p}-\phi^{\prime}_{p}(1-s)\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C^{2}}\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\left(\phi_{p}\phi^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})\wedge PD(\gamma_{p^{\prime}})

Fix some volume form
dvol_{\Sigma}
. Then

dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}=h_{\lambda}dt\wedge ds\wedge dvol_{\Sigma}

for some nonnegative function
h_{\lambda}
that vanishes precisely at
B
. Note that
PD(\gamma_{p})
is supported outside of the cylinder
R\leq R_{0}
. On the complement of this cylinder, there is a lower bound on
h_{\lambda}
independent of
C
. Therefore, the positive 4-form
dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}
dominates the remaining terms for
C
sufficiently small on the entire complement of
R\leq R_{0}
. Moreover, it is the only nonzero term for
R\leq R_{0}
, where it is nonnegative for all
R
and strictly positive for
R>0
.

Finally, since
\psi^{\prime}_{p}
is not supported in
R\leq R_{0}
, we can leave this computation to Case 2 below.

Case 2:
s\in[2\epsilon,3\epsilon]
. Here, the primitive is

\alpha_{\lambda}=(t+2)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\Psi(s)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

with exterior derivative

d\alpha_{\lambda}=dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\Phi^{\prime}ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

Therefore we have

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}(t+2)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}(t-1)dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

which satisfy the required inequalities since
\Phi^{\prime}<0
.

For
R\leq R_{0}
, since
df=0
for
R\leq R_{0}
, we have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\lambda}+fds

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\lambda+1}+f(dt-ds)

so therefore

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(t+2)(\beta_{\lambda}+fds)+\Phi(\beta_{\lambda+1}+f(dt-ds))

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=dt\wedge\beta_{\lambda}-\Phi^{\prime}ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}+2fdt\wedge ds

As a result

d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{B}=2f^{2}dt\wedge ds\wedge dx\wedge dy>0

and

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}+\delta\mu_{B})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}dt\wedge ds\wedge((t+2)\beta_{\lambda}+\delta\mu_{B})\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}((t+2)R+\delta Q)dt\wedge ds\wedge dx\wedge dy\geq 0

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\beta_{\lambda}+\delta\mu_{B})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}dt\wedge ds\wedge((t-1)\beta_{\lambda}+\delta\mu_{B})\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}((t-1)R+\delta Q)dt\wedge ds\wedge dx\wedge dy\leq 0

where
\mu_{B}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}=Qdx\wedge dy
.

Finally

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{B}

\displaystyle=\left((t+2-\Phi)f^{2}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge dx\wedge dy>0

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\beta_{\lambda})\wedge d\mu_{B}

\displaystyle=\left((t-1-\Phi)f^{2}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge dx\wedge dy<0

This suffices to ensure the required inequalities for
R\leq R_{0}
in this case.

Case 3:
s\in[3\epsilon,4\epsilon]
. This is where
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
go to 0 and contains the support of
\psi^{\prime}_{q}
(but not
\phi^{\prime}_{p}
). Here the primitive is

\alpha_{\lambda}=(t+2)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\Phi(s)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi_{p}PD(\gamma_{p})-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi_{q}PD(\gamma_{q})

and

d\alpha_{\lambda}=dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\Phi^{\prime}ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi^{\prime}_{q}ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

since each
\phi_{p}
is constant in this region.

Therefore

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(-\Phi^{\prime})dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi^{\prime}_{q}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

The first term is positive on the intersection of the supports of
\Phi^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}
. Similarly the second term is positive on the intersection of the supports of
\psi^{\prime}_{q}
and
PD(\gamma_{q})
, since
\phi^{\prime}_{q}\geq 0
and
\gamma_{q}
is positively transverse to the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
. In particular, this is strictly positive when
s\in[3\epsilon,4\epsilon]
and
R\geq R_{0}
.

Next,

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-(t+2)\Phi^{\prime}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{t+2}{C}\sum_{q}(-\psi^{\prime}_{q})dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}(\Phi^{\prime}\psi_{q}-\Phi\psi^{\prime}_{q})dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}(-\Phi^{\prime}\psi_{p})dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C^{2}}\sum_{p,q}\phi_{p}\psi^{\prime}_{q}dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C^{2}}\sum_{q,q^{\prime}}\psi_{q^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}_{q}dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{q^{\prime}})\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

By assumption, the supports of
PD(\gamma_{p}),PD(\gamma_{q})
are contained in the support of
\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})

\displaystyle=g_{p}dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle=g_{q}dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

for some functions
g_{p},g_{q}
on
\Sigma\times[-5\epsilon,0]
and

\displaystyle dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{p})\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle=h_{p,q}dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

\displaystyle dt\wedge ds\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})\wedge PD(\gamma_{q^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=h_{q,q^{\prime}}dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q^{\prime}})

for some functions
h_{p,q}
and
h_{q,q^{\prime}}
on
\Sigma\times[0,6\epsilon]
. Consequently, we can group the first, second and fourth terms as

\left(-\Phi^{\prime}(t+2)+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}g_{q}(\Phi^{\prime}\psi_{q}-\Phi\psi^{\prime}_{q})+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}g_{p}(-\Phi^{\prime}\psi_{p})\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

Using the relation
\Phi^{\prime}\leq\Phi\leq 0
and
\psi^{\prime}_{q}\leq 0
, this is bounded below by

-\Phi^{\prime}\left((t+2)-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}|g_{q}|(|\psi_{q}|+|\psi^{\prime}_{q}|)+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}g_{p}\psi_{p}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

which is positive for
C
sufficiently large and
s\leq 4\epsilon
. Furthermore, we can group the third, fifth and sixth terms as

-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi^{\prime}_{q}\left(t+2-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi_{p}h_{p,q}-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\psi_{q^{\prime}}h_{q,q^{\prime}}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

which is positive for
C
sufficiently large.

Next, since

\displaystyle dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{\prime}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda+1}-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi^{\prime}_{q}dt\wedge ds\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

we have that

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

is bounded above by

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}\left((t-1)+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}|g_{q}|(|\psi_{q}|+|\psi^{\prime}_{q}|)+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}g_{p}\psi_{p}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q}\psi^{\prime}_{q}\left(t-1-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{p}\phi_{p}h_{p,q}-\frac{1}{C}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\psi_{q^{\prime}}h_{q,q^{\prime}}\right)dt\wedge ds\wedge\beta_{\lambda}\wedge PD(\gamma_{q})

which for
C
sufficiently large is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive when
s\in[3\epsilon,4\epsilon]
and
R\geq R_{0}
.

As in Case 1 above, since
\psi_{q}
is not supported in
R\leq R_{0}
and
\psi_{p}
is not supported when
R\leq R_{0}
and
s\in[3\epsilon,4\epsilon]
, this computation reduces to Case 2 above.

Case 4:
s\geq 4\epsilon
. This is where
\Phi
vanishes. Here, the primitive is

\alpha_{\lambda}=(t+2)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{C}\mu_{H}

and

d\alpha_{\lambda}=dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{C}d\mu_{H}

So

d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{C}dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{H}

which by construction is positive.
In addition, we have that

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\left(dt\wedge\left((t+2)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{C}\mu_{H}\right)\wedge d\mu_{H}\right)

which for
C
sufficiently large is positive multiple of a perturbation of

(t+2)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{H}>0

Similarly, the form

dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{C}\left(dt\wedge\left((t-1)\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{C}\mu_{H}\right)\wedge d\mu_{H}\right)

is a positive multiple of a perturbation of

(t-1)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{H}<0

for
C
sufficiently large.

For
R\leq R_{0}
, we can check that

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{B}

\displaystyle=dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge dx\wedge dy

\displaystyle dt\wedge\alpha_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{B}

\displaystyle=(t+2)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge dx\wedge dy+\frac{1}{C}dt\wedge\mu_{H}\wedge dx\wedge dy

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}+\mu_{B})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\left((t+2)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{H}+dt\wedge\mu_{B}\wedge d\mu_{H}+\frac{1}{C}dt\wedge\mu_{H}\wedge d\mu_{H}\right)

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})\wedge d\mu_{B}

\displaystyle=(t-1)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge dx\wedge dy+\frac{1}{C}dt\wedge\mu_{H}\wedge dx\wedge dy

\displaystyle dt\wedge(\alpha_{\lambda}-3\beta_{\lambda}+\mu_{B})\wedge d\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\left((t-1)dt\wedge\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\wedge d\mu_{H}+dt\wedge\mu_{B}\wedge d\mu_{H}+\frac{1}{C}dt\wedge\mu_{H}\wedge d\mu_{H}\right)

The first, second and fourth terms are positive for
C
sufficiently large. Moreover, the second dominates the third and the fourth dominates the fifth for
C
sufficiently large.
∎

5.
4-dimensional sectors

5.1.
Contact structures on the 3-manifold pieces

At this point, we have constructed a symplectic form on
\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
. We now want to construct an inward-pointing Liouville vector field
\rho_{\lambda}
along each boundary component
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
. It is equivalent to constructing a primitive
\alpha_{\lambda}
for
\omega
in a neighborhood of
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
that restricts to a contact form. Then this 1-form
\alpha_{\lambda}
is
\omega
-dual to the required Liouville vector field.

We now define the following 1-form over
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\lambda}&\text{over $\nu(H_{\lambda})$}\\
\mu_{\Sigma,\lambda}+\delta\mu_{B}&\text{over $\nu(\Sigma)$}\\
\alpha_{\lambda-1}-3\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda-1}&\text{over $\nu(H_{\lambda-1})$}\end{cases}

and 2-form

\omega:=\begin{cases}d\alpha_{\lambda}&\text{over $\nu(H_{\lambda})$}\\
\omega_{0}+d\mu_{B}&\text{over $\nu(\Sigma)$}\end{cases}

Combining Corollary
4.5
and Proposition
4.6
yields

Proposition 5.1
.

The 2-form
\omega
is well-defined and symplectic on
\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
. Moreover, the form
\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda}
is well-defined, is a primitive for
\omega
, and is a contact form along
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
.

5.2.
Capping off with symplectic fillings

The last remaining piece is to fill in the three concave ends of
(\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}),\omega)
with Weinstein 1-handlebodies to obtain a closed symplectic 4-manifold.

We fix a standard model
(W_{k},\omega_{k},\rho_{k},\phi_{k})
of a 4-dimensional Weinstein 1-handlebody, where

(1)

W_{k}
has a handle decomposition consisting of one 0-handle and
k
1-handles for
k\geq 0
,

(2)

\omega_{k}
is an exact symplectic form,

(3)

\phi_{k}
is a proper, exhausting Morse function with exactly one index-0 critical point and exactly
k
index-1 critical points, and

(4)

\rho_{k}
is a complete Liouville vector field that is gradient-like for the function
\phi_{k}
.

By translation, we can assume that all critical values of
\phi_{k}
are negative, so that the level set
Y_{k}=\phi^{-1}_{k}(0)
is homeomorphic to either
S^{3}
(if
k=0
) or
\#_{k}S^{1}\times S^{2}
(if
k\geq 1
). Let
\alpha_{k}=\omega_{k}(\rho_{k},-)
be the induced contact form and
\xi_{k}
the corresponding contact structure. The symplectization
(\mathbb{R}\times Y,d(e^{t}\alpha_{k}))
embeds symplectically into
(W_{k},\omega_{k})
by flowing along the Liouville vector field
\rho_{k}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If the contact structure
(\widehat{Y}_{\lambda},\widehat{\xi}_{\lambda})
induced by the contact form of Proposition
5.1
is tight, then it can be filled in by a symplectic 1-handlebody.

Proof.

There is a unique tight contact structure on
S^{3}
and on
\#_{k}S^{1}\times S^{2}
for each
k\geq 1
, which is contactomorphic to the standard model
(Y_{k},\xi_{k})
. Choose an identification
(\widehat{Y}_{\lambda},\widehat{\xi}_{\lambda})
with
(Y_{k},\xi_{k})
for the appropriate
k\geq 0
. There exists some positive function
f:\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda}=f\alpha_{k}

Consequently, we can symplectically identify a neighborhood of
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}\subset(X,\omega)
with a neighborhood of the graph of
\log f
in the symplectization
(\mathbb{R}\times Y_{k},d(e^{t}\alpha_{k}))
. We can then smoothly identify the 4-dimensional sector
\widehat{Z}_{\lambda}
of the trisection with the sublevel set of the graph of
\log f
to obtain a symplectic structure extending the one we constructed on the spine.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

An
n
-parameter family of contact forms
\alpha_{\lambda}
determines a
n
-parameter family of symplectic fillings.

Proof.

An
n
-parameter family of contact forms
\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda,t}
corresponds to a family of positive functions of the form

\widehat{\alpha}_{\lambda,t}=f_{t}\alpha_{k}

This determines a family of graphs in
W_{k}
and therefore a family of diffeomorphisms of
\widehat{Z}_{\lambda}
into
W_{k}
. Pulling back, we obtain a family of symplectic forms on
\widehat{Z}_{\lambda}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The theorem of Laudenbach and Poenaru implies that each 4-dimensional sector
Z_{\lambda}
can be glued in uniquely up to diffeomorphism. Similarly, the symplectic structure we construct on
Z_{\lambda}
is essentially unique as there is a unique tight contact structure on
Y_{k}
, which has a unique Weinstein filling up to deformation
[
CE12
, Theorem 16.9(c)]
.

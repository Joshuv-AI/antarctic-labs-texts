Characterizing nonclassical correlations of tensorizing states in a bilocal scenario
S. Bhuvaneswari, R. Muthuganesan, R. Radha

Abstract
In the present paper, we attempt to address the question of ”can tensorizing states ( ρ ⊗ ρ ​ or ​ ρ ⊗ ρ ′ ) (\rho\otimes\rho\penalty\ \text{or}\penalty\ \rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}) ” have quantum advantages?”. To answer this question, we exploit the notion of measurement-induced nonlocality (MIN) and advocate a fidelity based nonbilocal measure to capture the nonlocal effects of tensorizing states due to locally invariant von Neumann projective measurements. We show that the properties of the fidelity based nonbilocal measure are retrieved from that of MIN. Analytically, we evaluate the nonbilocal measure for any arbitrary pure state. The upper bounds of the nonbilocal measure based on fidelity are also obtained in terms of eigenvalues of correlation matrix. As an illustration, we have computed the nonbilocality for some popular input states.

2
Measurement-induced nonlocality

Measurement-induced nonlocality (MIN) is manifested in the nonlocal effects due to locally invariant eigenprojective measurements and is a faithful measure of bipartite quantum systems. It is originally defined as the maximal Hilbert-Schmidt distance between pre and post measurement states and is defined as
Luo2011

N(\rho)=\penalty\ ^{\text{max}}_{\Pi^{a}}\|\rho-\Pi^{a}(\rho)\|^{2},

(1)

where the maximization is taken over the locally invariant projective measurements on subsystem
a
,
\Pi^{a}(\rho)=\sum_{k}(\Pi^{a}_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}^{b})\rho(\Pi^{a}_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}^{b})
with
\Pi^{a}=\{\Pi^{a}_{k}\}=\{|k\rangle\langle k|\}
being the projective measurements on the subsystem
a
which does not change the marginal state
\rho^{a}
locally i.e.,
\Pi^{a}(\rho^{a})=\rho^{a}
. Here
\|\mathcal{O}\|=\sqrt{\text{Tr}\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}}
is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of operator
\mathcal{O}
. The MIN is in some sense dual to the geometric version of quantum discord (GD) of the given state
\rho
and is formulated as

;
Luo2010pra

D(\rho)=\penalty\ ^{\text{min}}_{\Pi^{a}}\|\rho-\Pi^{a}(\rho)\|^{2}.

(2)

If
\rho^{a}
is nondegenerate, the optimization is not required and the above measures are equal. The Hilbert-Schmidt distance (HS) is an important quantity and its operational meaning is the informational distance between quantum states
Lee2003
. Recently, it is shown that the HS norm is easy to compute and measurable experimentally using many-particle interference techniques
Travnicek2019
. Further, the Hilbert-Schmidt distance has been widely used in quantum mechanics, particularly in quantifying quantum resources, such as quantum entanglement
Pandya2000
, quantum discord
Dakic
;
Luo2010pra
, measurement-induced nonlocality
Luo2011
;
Indrajith2021

. The relations between the Hilbert-Schmidt distance and the trace distance have been studied

. Nevertheless, Hilbert-Schmidt distance is not a bonafide measure of quantum correlation which is shown by considering a simple map
\Gamma^{\sigma}:X\rightarrow X\otimes\sigma
, i.e., the map adding a noisy ancillary state to the party
b

. Under such an operation, we have

\displaystyle\|X\|\rightarrow\|\Gamma^{\sigma}X\|=\|X\|\sqrt{\text{Tr}\sigma^{2}}.

(3)

Due to the addition of local ancilla
\rho^{c}
, the MIN of resultant state is

\displaystyle N(\rho^{a:bc})=N(\rho^{ab})\text{Tr}(\rho^{c})^{2}

implying that MIN differs arbitrarily due to local ancilla

is mixed. Defining the MIN in terms of any one of the contractive distance measures seems to be a natural way of rectifying the local ancilla problem. One such form of MIN based on the fidelity is given by

N_{F}(\rho)=1-\penalty\ ^{\text{min}}_{\Pi^{a}}\penalty\ F(\rho,\Pi^{a}(\rho)).

(4)

Here, the minimum is taken over the locally invariant projective measurements on subsystem

F(\rho,\sigma)=\text{Tr}\sqrt{\rho^{1/2}\sigma\rho^{1/2}}

The above definition of fidelity involves the square root of density operator. Hence, the computation of fidelity in higher dimensions is quite intractable. To reduce the computational complexity, we follow another version of fidelity

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{\text{Tr}(\rho\sigma)^{2}}{\text{Tr}(\rho^{2})\text{Tr}(\sigma^{2})}.

(5)

This measure also possesses all the properties of fidelity introduced by Josza
Jozsa1994
and is useful in defining MIN

N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho)=1-\penalty\ ^{\text{min}}_{\Pi^{a}}\mathcal{F}(\rho,\Pi^{a}(\rho)).

(6)

It is worth mentioning at this juncture that the fidelity-based MIN fixes the local ancilla problem of Hilbert-Schmidt MIN and satisfies all the necessary axioms of a bonafide measure of quantum correlations.

Nonbilocality Measure

In this section, we introduce the fidelity-based nonbilocal measure. Let us consider a quantum state shared by four parties in the separable composite finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{a}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{b}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{c}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})=1-\penalty\ ^{\text{min}}_{\Pi^{bc}}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd},\Pi^{bc}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})),

(7)

where the optimization is taken over the locally invariant eigenprojective measurements
\Pi^{bc}=\{\Pi^{bc}_{k}\}
which does not alter the marginal state
\rho^{bc}=\text{Tr}_{ad}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})
locally .
d_{\mathcal{F}}(\cdot,\cdot)

\Pi^{bc}(\sqrt{\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}})=\sum_{k,l}(\mathds{1}^{a}\otimes\Pi^{bc}_{kl}\otimes\mathds{1}^{d})\sqrt{\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}}(\mathds{1}^{a}\otimes\Pi^{bc}_{kl}\otimes\mathds{1}^{d})

unit matrix acting on

\rho^{b}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{b}\rangle\langle i_{b}|

\rho^{c}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}|j_{c}\rangle\langle j_{c}|

\rho_{bc}

\Pi^{bc}=\{\Pi^{b}\otimes\Pi^{c}\}

Using the orthogonality of projectors and cyclic property of trace of matrices, we can show that
\text{Tr}(\Pi^{bc}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}))^{2}=\text{Tr}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}\Pi^{bc}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}))

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})=1-\penalty\ ^{\text{min}}_{\Pi^{bc}}\frac{\text{Tr}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}\Pi^{bc}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}))}{\text{Tr}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})^{2}}.

(8)

The above measure quantifies the quantum correlation of any bilocal state from the perspectives of eigenprojective measurements. It is worth mentioning to MIN is a useful resource for bipartite quantum communication. In view of this, the nonbilocal measure is also helpful for multipartite communication protocol. In general, the bilocal states are useful in typical entanglement swapping and the fidelity based nonbilocal measure involves in swpping of quantum resources. The nonbilocal measure given by Eq. (

(i)

\rho_{cd}=\rho^{c}\otimes\rho^{d}

and
\rho_{cd}=\sum_{j}q_{j}|j_{c}\rangle\langle j_{c}|\otimes\rho^{d}_{j}

(ii)

N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})
is locally unitary invariant in the sense that

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}((U_{ab}\otimes U_{cd})\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}(U_{ab}\otimes U_{cd})^{\dagger})=N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd}),

(9)

where
U_{ab}=U_{a}\otimes U_{b}
and
U_{cd}=U_{c}\otimes U_{d}
are the local unitary operators.

(iii)

If
N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})
is positive, then, it implies that at least any one of the input states is entangled.

(iv)

If
\rho^{b}
and
\rho^{c}

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})=1-\penalty\ \mathcal{F}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd},\Pi^{bc}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})).

(v)

For any pure input state, the fidelity-based nonbilocal measure is given by

(vi)

For any arbitrary pure state, the fidelity-based MIN and nonbilocal measures are related as

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ba}\otimes\rho_{ab})\geq N_{\mathcal{F}}^{\text{MIN}}(\rho).

(vii)

Although
N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab})=N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{cd})=0

N_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{ab}\otimes\rho_{cd})>0

6
Conclusions

In this article, we have introduced a measure of nonbilocal correlations of two bipartite input states using fidelity based measurement-induced nonlocality. We notice that a closer connection exists between the nonlocal and nonbilocal correlation measures. For any arbitrary pure input state, we have evaluated the nonbilocal correlation analytically. The upper bounds of the fidelity-based nonbilocal measure are also obtained for mixed input states. As an illustration, the nonbilocality is computed for some well-known examples.

1.
Introduction

Definition 1.1

(
[
Zan82
]
)
.

Let
K
a number field and
l
a positive integer. Define

I_{K}^{l}:=\underset{\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{p})=l}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\;\text{prime of $K$}}}\mathfrak{p},

the ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{K}
formed by the product of all primes of
K
with norm
l
. When
l
is not a norm of prime ideal, then
I_{K}^{l}=\mathscr{O}_{K}
.
A number field is called a Pólya field when all the ideals
I_{K}^{l}
are principal.

Definition 1.2

(
[
CC97
]
, II.4)
.

The Pólya group of a number field is a subgroup of the class group of
K
generated by the classes of the ideals of
I_{K}^{l}
. This subgroup is noted
Po(K)
.

In our study, we will only consider number fields Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
. This hypothesis gives us an other definition of Pólya group in term of ambiguous ideals.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
a finite Galois extension and
G
its Galois group. Then, the group of ambiguous ideals
\mathscr{I}(K)^{G}
of
K
, i.e. of ideals invariant under the action of
G
, is generated by the primes of the form

\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K}}:=\underset{\mathfrak{p}\;\text{prime of K}}{\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|p}}\mathfrak{p}=(I_{K}^{p^{f_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}),

where
p
is a positive prime number and
f_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
the inertia degree of each prime
\mathfrak{p}|p
.

Consequently, the Pólya group of
K
is generated by the classes of the ambiguous ideals. For simplicity, for every prime number
p
, we will use the notation
\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K}}
instead of
I_{K}^{p^{f_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})}}
.

We give now some results about the behavior of the Pólya groups in linearly disjoint Galois extensions to obtain the main proposition of this section (see for instance
[
Ler11
]
or
[
Cha18
]
).

Notation
.

Let
L/K
a finite extension of number fields. Consider the injective morphism:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{K}^{L}:\mathscr{I}(K)&\to&\mathscr{I}(L)\\
I&\mapsto&I\mathscr{O}_{L}.\end{array}

It induces a morphism:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\varepsilon_{K}^{L}:Cl(K)&\to&Cl(L)\\
\overline{I}&\mapsto&\overline{I\mathscr{O}_{L}}.\end{array}

If
L
and
K
are Galois extensions over
\mathbb{Q}
, the Pólya group of
K
and
L
behave well with respect to this morphism.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
K\subseteq L
are two Galois extensions over
\mathbb{Q}
, then

\varepsilon_{K}^{L}(Po(K))\subseteq Po(L).

For the next two part, we will need some results, directly deduced from Abhyankar’s Lemma. In
[
Sti08
]
3.9.1, the Theorem is proved for function fields over finite fields but the proof remains valid for number fields.

Lemma 1.4

(Abhyankar)
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
two number fields,
L=K_{1}K_{2}
and
K
a subfield of
K_{1}\cap K_{2}
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
a prime of
L
such that
\mathfrak{q}\cap K=\mathfrak{p}
. Let
p\mathbb{Z}=\mathfrak{q}\cap\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{1}=\mathfrak{q}\cap K_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}=\mathfrak{q}\cap K_{2}
. If
p
does not divide one of the ramification indices
e(\mathfrak{p}_{1}/\mathfrak{p})
or
e(\mathfrak{p}_{2}/\mathfrak{p})
, then
e(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{p})=\text{lcm}(e(\mathfrak{p}_{1}/\mathfrak{p}),e(\mathfrak{p}_{2}/\mathfrak{p}))
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
a Galois extension, then
|Po(K)^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}|=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
p
a prime number and
e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
the ramification index of each prime
\mathfrak{P}
of
K
lying over
p
. As
e_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})|[K:\mathbb{Q}]
we obtain that
\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K}}^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
is principal.
∎

For the convenience of the reader we give a detailed proof of the next two results.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
two finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, and
L=K_{1}K_{2}
. If for each prime number p, at least one extension

Ki/\mathbb{Q}
(
i=1,2
) is tamely ramified with respect to
p
, then:

Po(L)=\varepsilon_{K_{1}}^{L}(Po(K_{1}))\cdot\varepsilon_{K_{2}}^{L}(Po(K_{2})).

Proof.

(See also
[
Cha18
]
Lemma 3.2).
Let
p
a prime number that ramifies in
L
. Let
m=e_{p}(L/\mathbb{Q})
,
e_{1}=e_{p}(K_{1}/\mathbb{Q})
and
e_{2}=e_{p}(K_{2}/\mathbb{Q})
, the ramification indices of primes lying over
p
in the different extensions. By Abhyankar’s Lemma,
m=\text{lcm}(e_{1},e_{2}).
Then we have:

\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{1}}}\mathscr{O}_{L}=\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}^{\frac{m}{e_{1}}}\;\text{and}\;\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{2}}}\mathscr{O}_{L}=\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}^{\frac{m}{e_{2}}}.

But
\frac{m}{e_{1}}
and
\frac{m}{e_{2}}
are coprime, thus there exist
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
u\frac{m}{e_{1}}+v\frac{m}{e_{2}}=1
. Therefore

\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}=\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}^{u\frac{m}{e_{1}}}\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}^{v\frac{m}{e_{2}}}=(\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{1}}}\mathscr{O}_{L})^{u}(\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{2}}}\mathscr{O}_{L})^{v}.

We then deduce

\langle\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{1}}}\mathscr{O}_{L},\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{K_{2}}}\mathscr{O}_{L}\rangle=\langle\sqrt{p\mathscr{O}_{L}}\rangle\;.

∎

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
two finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
such that the degrees
[K_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
and
[K_{2}:\mathbb{Q}]
are coprime, and let
L=K_{1}K_{2}
. Then:

Po(L)\simeq Po(K_{1})\oplus Po(K_{2}).

Proof.

(See also
[
Cha18
]
Theorem 3.7).
The map
\varepsilon_{K_{i}}^{L}:(Po(K_{i}))\to Cl(L)
is injective for
i=1,2
. Indeed, if
I\in\ker(\varepsilon_{K_{1}}^{L})
, then
N_{L/K_{1}}(I\mathscr{O}_{L})=I^{[L:K_{1}]}=I^{[K_{2}:\mathbb{Q}]}
is principal. By Lemma
1.5
and the fact that the degrees are coprime,
I
is already principal. So we have
\varepsilon_{K_{i}}^{L}(Po(K_{i}))\simeq Po(K_{i}).

We already have the sum decomposition according to the previous Corollary. It remains to show that the intersection
\varepsilon_{K_{1}}^{L}(Po(K_{1}))\cap\varepsilon_{K_{2}}^{L}(Po(K_{2}))
is trivial in
Po(L)
.

By Lemma
1.5
if
p
divides
|Po(K_{i})|
then it divides
[K_{i}:K]
. Consequently, as the degrees
[K_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
and
[K_{2}:\mathbb{Q}]
are coprime, we conclude that the sum is direct:

Po(L)\simeq Po(K_{1})\oplus Po(K_{2}).

∎

2.
On Pólya fields

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
l
a prime number and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. There exist infinitely many cyclic extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
l^{n}
sutch that
Po(K)=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
p
a prime number verifying
p\equiv 1\mod l^{n}
. Let
K
the subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
of degree
l^{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. By
[
Was97
]
, Theorem 10.4,
|Cl(K)|\not\equiv 0\mod l
, and so, by Lemma
1.5
,
Po(K)=\{0\}
. We conclude with Dirichelt’s Theorem on arithmetic progressions.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
an abelian group. There exist infinitely many Pólya fields, abelian over
\mathbb{Q}
, tamely ramified, such that
Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})\simeq G.

Proof.

As
G
is abelian, it is the direct product of its
p
-Sylow subgroups:
G=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{r}
. We start to show that each
p_{i}
-Sylow subgroup
H_{i}
of
G
is the Galois group of a certain Pólya field. Let
H_{i}=\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{e_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{e_{n}}\mathbb{Z}
, by Proposition
2.1
, for all
1\leq j\leq n
, there exist infinitely many cyclic Pólya fields of degree
p_{i}^{e_{j}}
. Consequently, by Corollary
1.6
, there exist infinitely many Pólya fields, tamely ramified with Galois group
H_{i}
. Let
K_{i}
such a field for
1\leq i\leq r
. When
i\neq j
,
[K_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]
and
[K_{j}:\mathbb{Q}]
are coprime and so, by Corollary
1.6
applied to
K_{1},\dots,K_{r}
, we have

Po(L)=\varepsilon_{K_{1}}^{L}(Po(K_{1}))\cdot\dots\cdot\varepsilon_{K_{r}}^{L}(Po(K_{r}))=\{1\}

where
L
is the compositum
L=K_{1}\dots K_{r}
.

∎

3.
Yagahi’s Theorem

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Yah78
]
)
.

Let
l
a prime number and
G
an abelian group of exponent
l^{m}
,
m\geq 0
. Then for all
n\geq m
,
n\geq 1
, there exist infinitely many cyclic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
l^{n}
whose
l
-class groups are isomorphic to
G
.

In fact, in his paper, Yahagi shows refined result, especially concerning Pólya groups. We give here a sketch of the proof for the sake of keeping this paper self-contained.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
l
a prime number and
G
an abelian group of exponent
l^{m}
,
m\geq 0
. Then for all
n\geq m
,
n\geq 1
, there exist infinitely many cyclic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
l^{n}
whose Pólya groups are isomorphic to
G
.

Proof.

For a cyclic extension
F/k
of a number field
k
, and
\tau
a generator of its Galois group, Yahagi defines the set
{S_{F}^{(\tau)}}_{s}=\{c\in S_{F}\;
;
c
contains an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
F
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{\tau}=\mathfrak{a}\}
(
[
Yah78
]
p.276) where
S_{F}
is the
l
-class group of
F
. When
k=\mathbb{Q}
this set corresponds with the
l
-part of the Pólya group of
F
, for it is generated by classes of ambiguous ideals. As we work with
l
-extensions,
Po(F)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{l}=Po(F).

By class field theory, if
S_{F}={S_{F}^{(\tau)}}_{s}
, then
{S_{F}^{(\tau)}}_{s}\simeq G(K/F)
where
K
is the maximal abelian
l
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in the genus field of
F/\mathbb{Q}
.

Yahagi starts to build an abelian extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and a subfield
F
, cyclic over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
K
is exactly the maximal abelian
l
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in the genus field of
F/\mathbb{Q}
and
G(K/F)\simeq G
(
[
Yah78
]
p. 280-281).

Then, considering the map
\varphi:S_{F}^{(\tau)}\to S_{F}/S_{F}^{1-\tau}
so that the diagram below is commutative:

\textstyle{S_{F}^{(\tau)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{S_{F}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S_{F}/S_{F}^{1-\tau}}

Yahagi shows that the image of
{S_{F}^{(\tau)}}_{s}
by
\varphi
is isomorphic to
S_{F}/S_{F}^{1-\tau}
, which implies that
\varphi
is bijective and so
S_{F}={S_{F}^{(\tau)}}_{s}
.
∎

We can now deduce our main result from Yagahi’s Theorem.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
a finite abelian group. Then there exist infinitely many abelian extensions
E/\mathbb{Q}
so that
Po(E)\simeq G.

Remark 3.4

(
[
Ler11
]
, Ex.1)
.

For the case
G=\{0\}
, it suffices to consider cyclotomic fields.

Proof.

Since
G
is abelian, it is isomorphic to a direct product of its
p
-Sylow subgroups:

G\simeq H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{r}.
For all
1\leq i\leq r
, Yagahi’s Theorem gives us infinitely many abelian extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
whose Pólya groups are isomorphic to
H_{i}
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
K_{i}
such an extension, with Pólya group isomorphic to
H_{i}
. Then for all
i\neq j
,
([K_{i}:\mathbb{Q}],[K_{j}:\mathbb{Q}])=1
and so, by Corollary
1.7
,

Po(K_{1}\dots K_{r})\simeq Po(K_{1})\times\dots\times Po(K_{r})\simeq G.

∎

This study leads us to define the following mathematical object: for a number field
K
, Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, we set
\tilde{Cl(K)}:=Cl(K)/Po(K)
.
Then, for such a
K
, it is interesting to find whether this relative class group is trivial or not. Here
comes a natural conjecture:

Conjecture 3.5
.

There exist infinitely many number fields
K
, Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, such that
\tilde{Cl(K)}=\{0\}.

Remark 3.6
.

Chabert proved in
[
Cha18a
]
that there exist infinitely many non Galois number fields
K
such that
Cl(K)=Po(K)
.

However, in the case of imaginary quadratic fields, the problem seems easier to solve. Indeed just as we can prove that there exist a finite number of imaginary quadratic fields with class number one, so we show that there exist a finite number of imaginary quadratic fields with trivial relative class group.

Theorem 3.7
.

There exist a finite number of imaginary quadratic fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\tilde{Cl(K)}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
K
an imaginary quadratic field,
h_{K}
its class number and
d_{K}
its discriminant. First, by
Brauer-Siegel’s Theorem
[
Lan70
]
, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
sucht that
h_{K}>C_{\varepsilon}d_{K}^{1/2-\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, by Dirichlet’s Theorem (
[
Was97
]
p.38) about class number for imaginary quadratic fields:

\frac{2\pi h_{K}}{w\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=L(1,\chi),

where
w
stands for the number of roots of unity in
K
. From this equality we conclude that
h_{K}<<\sqrt{|d_{K}|}\log(|d_{K}|)
.
Considering these two results, we show that

\frac{\log h_{K}}{\log|d_{K}|}\underset{|d_{K}|\to+\infty}{\to}\frac{1}{2}.

So, in term of growth,
h_{K}\sim\sqrt{|d_{K}|}.

For the Pólya group of an imaginary quadratic field
K
, Hilbert
[
Hil95
]
proved that

|Po(K)|=2^{s_{K}-1},

where
s_{K}
denotes the number of ramified prime numbers in the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
. If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, with
d
squarefree, then the discriminant
d_{K}
is
d
or
4d
depending on
d\equiv 1\mod 4
or
d\equiv 2,3\mod 4
. If
2
is ramified,
|d_{K}|=2^{m}p_{1}\dots p_{s_{K}-1}
, where the
p_{i}
’s are distinct odd primes, and
m\leq 3.
So,
|d_{K}|\geq 2^{3}P_{s_{K}-1}
where
P_{s_{K}-1}
is the product of the first
s_{K}-1
odd primes. On the one hand, when
s_{K}
is not bounded,
\sqrt{P_{s_{K}-1}}\geq 2^{s_{k}-1}
and so
\underset{d_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{|Po(K)|}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=0
.
On the other hand, when
s_{K}
is bounded, having
|d_{K}|\to+\infty
means that at least one
p_{i}
is not bounded, and so
\underset{|d_{K}|\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{|Po(K)|}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=0
. Consequently, the Pólya group and the class group will only correspond for bounded discriminants with bounded
s_{K}
.
By Hermite’s Theorem
[
Neu99
]
, there are only finitely many number fields of bounded discriminant
d_{K}
, hence there is a finite number of imaginary quadratic fields with discriminant
d_{K}
.

∎

We can generalize this result to the case of abelian CM fields.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
K
runs through an infinite sequence of abelian CM fields. Let

,

the conductor and the class number of

\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{\log(h_{K}^{-})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}=1,

In order to prove this Theorem we need to discuss about the discriminant

of an abelian CM field

. For every abelian number field

the conductor of

Theorem 3.9

(
[
Vic17
]
4.1)
.

Let
K
an abelian number field of conductor
m_{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, where the
p_{i}
’s are prime number and
\alpha_{i}
’s and
l
are positive integers. Let
d_{K}
denotes the discriminant of

. Then,

|d_{K}|=(\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-\lambda_{i}})^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}

where

\lambda_{i}=\frac{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-(p_{i},2)}-1+(p_{i}-1)/u_{i}}{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-(p_{i},2)}(p_{i}-1)}

and

u_{i}=\frac{[K\cdot\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}})]}{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}}.

Corollary 3.10
.

With the notations above,
\lambda_{i}
is bounded by 2 for
i=1,\dots,l
.

Proof.

We have:

\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{(p_{i}-1)}-\frac{1}{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-(p_{i},2)}(p_{i}-1)}+\frac{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-(p_{i},2)}[K\cdot\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}})]}.

If
p_{i}
is odd, then
\lambda_{i}\leq\frac{1}{(p_{i}-1)}+\frac{1}{[K\cdot\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}})]}\leq 2
.

If
p_{i}=2
, then
\lambda_{i}=1-\frac{1}{2^{\alpha_{i}-2}}+\frac{2}{[K\cdot\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/2^{\alpha_{i}}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/2^{\alpha_{i}}})]}
. As
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
,
K\not\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/2^{\alpha_{i}}})
and
[K\cdot\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}):\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_{K}/p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}})]\geq 2
. So

\lambda_{i}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2^{\alpha_{i}-2}}+1\leq 2.

∎

K
. Let
h_{K}^{-}=h_{K}/h_{K}^{+}
, then

(1)

\frac{\log(h_{K}R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|)}}=\frac{\log(h_{K}^{+})+\log(h_{K}^{-})+\log(R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}.

For CM fields, the quotient of
R_{K}
by
R_{K}^{+}
is well known (see for instance
[
Was97
]
Proposition 4.16):

\frac{R_{K}}{R_{K}^{+}}=\frac{1}{Q}2^{\frac{1}{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}]-1},

with
Q=1
or 2,
and so the equation (1) becomes:

\frac{\log(h_{K}R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|)}}=\frac{\log(h_{K}^{+})+\log(h_{K}^{-})+\log(R_{K}^{+})+(\frac{1}{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}]-1)\log(2)-\log(Q)}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}.

By Lemma
3.11

\frac{(\frac{1}{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}]-1)\log(2)-\log(Q)}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0,

and by Lemma
3.12
,

\frac{\log(h_{K}^{+})+\log(R_{K}^{+})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0.

Finally we have

\frac{\log(h_{K}^{-})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|)}}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}1.

So, in term of growth,
h_{K}^{-}\sim\sqrt{|d_{K}|}
.

∎

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
K
runs through an infinite sequence of abelian fields. Then:

\frac{|Po(K)|}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0.

Proof.

We know that
|Po(K)|
divides
[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{l}
, where
l
is such that
m_{K}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, and we want to show that

\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{l}}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0.

By Theorem
3.9

\sqrt{|d_{K}|}=(\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{i}^{\frac{\alpha_{i}-\lambda_{i}}{2}})^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
, so if the
p_{i}
’s are bounded, then at least one
\alpha_{i}
tends to
+\infty
when
m_{K}
tends to
+\infty
and

\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{l}}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=0.

If the
p_{i}
’s are not bounded, then
(\prod_{i=1}^{l}p_{i}^{\frac{\alpha_{i}-\lambda_{i}}{2}})^{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}>>[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{l}
and

\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{[K:\mathbb{Q}]^{l}}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=0.

Finally,

\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{|Po(K)|}{\sqrt{|d_{K}|}}=0.

∎

Corollary 3.15
.

There exist a finite number of abelian CM fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that

\tilde{Cl(K)}=\{0\}.

Concerning real quadratic fields, the problem is different since we do not control the behavior of
R_{K}
. The regulator may have the same growth asymptotically as
\sqrt{|d_{K}|}
.

Conjecture 3.16
.

There exists an infinite sequences of real quadratic fields such that

\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\lim}\frac{\log(R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}=1.

In fact, we can construct two infinite sequence of real abelian quadratic fields such that

\frac{\log(R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0.

For this we will give some preliminary results.

Theorem 3.17

(
[
Est31
]
or
[
Hea12
]
)
.

There exist infinitely many integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n^{2}+1
is square-free.

Lemma 3.18
.

There exist infinitely many integers
n
such that
\varepsilon:=n+\sqrt{n^{2}+1}
is the fundamental unit of the real quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+1})
, where
n^{2}+1
is square-free.

Proof.

Theorem
3.17
gives us infinitely many
n
such that
n^{2}+1
is square-free. Let
n
such an integer.
If
n^{2}+1\equiv 2\mod 4
, then
\mathbb{Z}[\varepsilon]
is the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+1})
and Louboutin shows that the fundamental unit of
\mathbb{Z}[\varepsilon]
is
\varepsilon

(
[
Lou08
]
, Thm 1).

If
n^{2}+1\equiv 1\mod 4
, let
\eta
be the fundamental unit of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{n^{2}+1})
. Let
d_{\eta}
(resp.
d_{\varepsilon}
) the discriminant of the order
\mathbb{Z}[\eta]
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}[\varepsilon])
. By definition,
d_{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}
where
\varepsilon^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
\varepsilon
. Here
\varepsilon^{\prime}=n-\sqrt{n^{2}+1}
and
d_{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2}=4(n^{2}+1)
.

If
\varepsilon=\eta^{m}
with
m\geq 2
, since
\eta>1
:

(\eta^{2}-\eta^{-2})^{2}\leq(\eta^{m}-\eta^{-m})^{2}\leq(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-1})^{2}=d_{\varepsilon}\leq 4d_{\eta}\leq 4(\eta+\eta^{-1})^{2}.

Then,
0\leq(\eta-\eta^{-1})^{2}\leq 4
i.e.
\eta-\eta^{-1}\leq 2
, and so
\eta\leq 1+\sqrt{2}
.

Hence we have:

\eta^{m}+\eta^{-m}=\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-1}\leq 2(\eta+\eta^{-1}),

and

\varepsilon=n+\sqrt{n^{2}+1}<2\eta+2.

So

\eta>\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+1}}{2}-1\;\text{and}\;\eta\leq 1+\sqrt{2}.

When
n
is big enough, we obtain a contradiction and finally
\varepsilon=\eta
.
∎

Proposition 3.19
.

There exist infinitely many integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
4n^{2}-1
is square-free.

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
p
a prime number. Define

S_{N}=\{n\leq N\;|\;4n^{2}-1\;\text{is square-free}\}
and
S_{N,p}=\{n\leq N\;|\;4n^{2}\equiv 1\mod p^{2}\}
. Then,

|S_{N}|\geq N-\sum_{3\leq p\leq N}S_{N,p}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle|S_{N,p}|

\displaystyle=\underset{4n^{2}\equiv 1\mod p^{2}}{\sum_{n\leq N}}1

\displaystyle\leq(1+\left[\frac{N}{p^{2}}\right])\underset{4x^{2}\equiv 1\mod p^{2}}{\sum_{x\mod p^{2}}}1\leq 2+\frac{2N}{p^{2}}.

Consequently,

|S_{N}|\geq N-2\sum_{p\leq N}1-2N\sum_{3\leq p\leq N}\frac{1}{p^{2}}.

By prime number Theorem we obtain,

|S_{N}|\leq N(1-\sum_{p\geq 3}\frac{1}{p^{2}})+o(N),

and

\sum_{p\geq 3}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\leq\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1-\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{5}{12}.

Finally,

|S_{N}|\geq\frac{N}{6}+o(N)

which prove the proposition.
∎

Lemma 3.20
.

There exist infinitely many integers
n
such that
\varepsilon:=2n+\sqrt{4n^{2}-1}
is the fundamental unit of the real quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{4n^{2}-1})
, where
4n^{2}-1
is square-free.

Proof.

It is a direct application of Louboutin’s Theorem (
[
Lou08
]
, Thm 1), because the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{4n^{2}-1})
is precisely
\mathbb{Z}[2n+\sqrt{4n^{2}-1}]=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{4n^{2}-1}]
.
∎

Proposition 3.21
.

There exist an infinite sequence of real quadratic fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that

\frac{\log(R_{K})}{\log(\sqrt{|d_{K}|})}\underset{m_{K}\to+\infty}{\to}0.

Proof.

We present two such infinite sequences.
By Lemma
3.18
(respectively Lemma
3.20
) and the fact that
R_{K}
is the logarithm of the fundamental unit, there exist infinitely many integers

, the result holds.
∎

Phase shift, ellipticity, angle, and topological number in skyrmion lattices
Kotaro Shimizu 1, Shun Okumura 2, Yasuyuki Kato 1, and Yukitoshi Motome 1

Abstract
We theoretically study skyrmion lattices realized in a Kondo lattice model on a triangular lattice, focusing on the phase, ellipticity, and angle of the constituent multiple- Q Q waves. Analyzing the numerical data obtained in the previous study [Ozawa R, Hayami S and Motome Y 2017 Phys. Rev. Lett. 118 147205], we extract these parameters for the two types of skyrmion lattices with the skyrmion number of 1 1 and 2 2 . We show that the topological transition between the two skyrmion lattices driven by an external magnetic field is accompanied by significant modulations of all three parameters.



1
Introduction

The magnetic skyrmion lattice (SkL) is a two-dimensional topological spin texture
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
It is described by a superposition of multiple spin density waves, and hence, called a multiple-
Q
spin state.
In such a state, the topological property can be changed by modulating the constituent waves.
For instance, the symmetry of the spin textures and the skyrmion number
N_{\rm sk}
, which characterizes the topology of the SkL, are changed by phase shift in the constituent waves
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
Since there are many other parameters in the superpositions, further studies are desired to establish the way of controlling the topological property of multiple-
Q
states.

In this report, we clarify the evolution of the superposed waves in the SkLs discovered in the previous study
[
9
]
.
Analyzing the numerical data of the real-space spin configurations, we extract the phases of the constituent waves,
and the ellipticity and the angle of the propagating planes for two types of SkLs with
|N_{\rm sk}|=1
and
2
.
We show that all these three parameters change abruptly at the topological phase transition from
|N_{\rm sk}|=2
to
1
while increasing the magnetic field, indicating that the constituent waves are modulated through the topological change.

Model and method

We analyze the spin configurations numerically obtained in the ground state of the Kondo lattice model on a triangular lattice
[
9
]
.
The Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{{\bf r},{\bf r}^{\prime},\sigma}t_{{\bf r}{\bf r}^{\prime}}\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{{\bf r}\sigma}\hat{c}_{{\bf r}^{\prime}\sigma}-J\sum_{{\bf r},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}{\bf S}_{{\bf r}}\cdot\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{{\bf r}\sigma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{c}_{{\bf r}\sigma^{\prime}}-h\sum_{{\bf r}}S_{{\bf r}}^{z},

(1)

where the operator
\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}_{{\bf r}\sigma}

(\hat{c}_{{\bf r}\sigma})
creates (annihilates) an electron with spin index

\sigma=\pm

{\bf r}

the localized spins

See

In the previous study, two types of SkLs were found in the model (
1

N_{\rm sk}=1

Since both of them are regarded as superpositions of three spin density waves (i.e.,

\eta=1,2,3

\frac{2\pi}{3}

\displaystyle{\bf S}^{3Q}_{{\bf r}}(\{\varphi_{\eta}\},m,\theta,\epsilon,\Gamma;\hat{\bf n},\xi)\propto R\left(\hat{\bf n},\xi\right)\left[\sum_{\eta=1}^{3}~\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left({\bf e}_{\eta}^{1}(\theta,\Gamma)\cos\mathcal{Q}_{\eta}+\epsilon{\bf e}_{\eta}^{2}(\theta,\Gamma)\sin\mathcal{Q}_{\eta}\right)+m\hat{\bf z}\right],

(2)

where

R(\hat{\bf n},\xi)
denotes the three-dimensional rotation matrix about the unit vector
\hat{\bf n}
with the angle
\xi

{\bf e}_{\eta}^{1}(\theta,\Gamma)=\left[R\left(\hat{\bf z},(-1)^{\Gamma}\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)\right]^{\eta-1}(0,(-1)^{\Gamma}\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\mathsf{T}}
,

{\bf e}_{\eta}^{2}(\theta,\Gamma)=\left[R\left(\hat{\bf z},(-1)^{\Gamma}\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)\right]^{\eta-1}(0,-(-1)^{\Gamma}\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\mathsf{T}}
,

\mathcal{Q}_{\eta}={\bf q}_{\eta}\cdot{\bf r}+\varphi_{\eta}
,
\Gamma

\hat{\bf z}=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf{T}}

In (

\varphi_{\eta}

\theta

We note that the model in (

the localized spins, which corresponds to the change of

This operation reverses the sign of

For the spin configurations obtained numerically,

\displaystyle U(\{\varphi_{\eta}\},m,\theta,\epsilon,\Gamma;\hat{\bf n},\xi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf r}}\left(1-{\bf S}^{\rm GS}_{{\bf r}}\cdot{\bf S}^{3Q}_{{\bf r}}(\{\varphi_{\eta}\},m,\theta,\epsilon,\Gamma;\hat{\bf n},\xi)\right),

4
Concluding remarks

To summarize, we analyzed the real-space spin configurations of two types of triple-
Q
SkLs previously found
in the Kondo lattice model, and clarified the phase, the ellipticity, and the angle of the superposed waves.
We elucidated that these parameters abruptly change at the topological phase transition from
|N_{\rm sk}|=2
to
1

while increasing the magnetic field.
We note that our results are qualitatively consistent with those obtained by the recent variational study in
[
8
]
, where the relative phase between the constituent sinusoidal and cosinusoidal waves, which partly corresponds to

\tilde{\varphi}
,
\epsilon
, and
\theta
in our analysis, is shifted at the topological phase transitions induced by the magnetic anisotropy and the magnetic field.
Our results indicate that the parameters to characterize the constituent waves underlie the change of
the topological properties in the triple-
Q
SkLs.

Our finding provides a first step toward the systematic investigation on other topological phase transitions in terms of
the detailed parameters in the constituent waves, not only for the SkLs but also for
other topological spin textures, such as
the three-dimensional hedgehog lattices
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
It is desired to establish the mechanism to control the constituent waves, e.g., by the external magnetic field and pressure, in the future studies.
It is also intriguing to study the relations between the nature of the constituent waves and the electronic, transport,
and dynamical properties in the topological multiple-
Q
states.

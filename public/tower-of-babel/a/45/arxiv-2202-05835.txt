Final State Observability in Banach spaces with applications to Subordination and Semigroups induced by Lévy processes
Dennis Gallaun, Jan Meichsner, Christian Seifert

Abstract
This paper generalizes the abstract method of proving an observability estimate by combining an uncertainty principle and a dissipation estimate. In these estimates we allow for a large class of growth/decay rates satisfying an integrability condition. In contrast to previous results, we use an iterative argument which enables us to give an asymptotically sharp estimate for the observation constant and which is explicit in the model parameters. We give two types of applications where the extension of the growth/decay rates naturally appear. By exploiting subordination techniques we show how the dissipation estimate of a semigroup transfers to subordinated semigroups. Furthermore, we apply our results to semigroups related to Lévy processes. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020). 47D06, 35Q93, 47N70, 93D20, 93B05, 93B07. Keywords. final state observability estimate, Banach space, C 0 C_{0} -semigroups, null-controllability, fractional powers

Introduction

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces,
A
a densely defined closed linear operator in
X
such that
A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
,
C\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, and
T>0
. We consider the observation system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)\quad(t\in(0,T]),\qquad x(0)=x_{0}\in X,

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=Cx(t)\quad(t\in[0,T]).

Note that for
x_{0}\in X
the state function
x\colon[0,T]\to X
is given by the mild solution
x(t)=S(t)x_{0}
for
t\in[0,T]
, and the observation function
y\colon[0,T]\to Y
is given by
y(t)=Cx(t)=CS(t)x_{0}
for
t\in[0,T]
.
For given
r\in[1,\infty]
we are interested in a
final state observability estimate
for the system, i.e., the existence of an observability constant
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that for all
x_{0}\in X
we have

\left\lVert S(T)x_{0}\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\begin{cases}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert CS(t)x_{0}\right\rVert_{Y}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/r}&r\in[1,\infty),\\
\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert CS(t)x_{0}\right\rVert_{Y}&r=\infty.\end{cases}

(OBS)

Put differently, the final state
x(T)
can be estimated by knowing only the observations
y(t)
for
t\in[0,T]
. Observe that the formulation of (
OBS
) just requires a semigroup with suitable measurability properties, namely measurability of
t\mapsto\left\lVert CS(t)x_{0}\right\rVert_{Y}
.

A by now well-known method (see
[
Mil10
,
TT11
,
BPS18
,
NTTV20
,
GST20
]
) to obtain a final state observability estimate is given by establishing a so-called
uncertainty principle
, sometimes also called
spectral inequality
,

\forall\lambda>0,\,x\in X:\quad\left\lVert P_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{0}\mathrm{e}^{d_{1}\lambda^{\gamma_{1}}}\left\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{Y}

(UP)

and a related
dissipation estimate

\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S(t)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq d_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-d_{3}\lambda^{\gamma_{2}}t^{\gamma_{3}}}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X},

(DISS)

where
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
is a family of bounded linear operators on
X
and
d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}>0
with
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
.
Then
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
can be explicitly determined of the form

C_{\mathrm{obs}}=\frac{C_{1}}{T^{1/r}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{2}}{T^{\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}}{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}}}+C_{3}T\right)

with explicitly known constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\geq 0
, see
[
NTTV20
,
GST20
]
.

In mathematical systems theory, also control systems are studied. There, given another Banach space
U
and a bounded linear operator
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,X)
, one considers the control system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t)+Bu(t)\quad(t\in(0,T]),\qquad x(0)=x_{0}\in X,

and asks whether, for given
r\in[1,\infty]
, for every
x_{0}\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a control function
u\in L_{r}([0;T];U)
such that we have
\|x(T)\|<\varepsilon
. Then the control system is called
approximate null-controllable
. If, furthermore, there exists
C_{\mathrm{unc}}\geq 0
such that we can choose the control function
u
to satisfy
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L_{r}((0;T);U)}\leq C_{\mathrm{unc}}\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert_{X}
for all
x_{0}\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, then the approximate null-controllability is called
cost-uniform
. Note that for reflexive Banach spaces this is equivalent to the standard concept of
null-controllability
.
By means of duality
[
Dou66
,
DR77
,
Car88
,
Vie05
]
, cost-uniform approximate null-controllability is equivalent to a final state observability estimate for the corresponding dual system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=A^{\prime}x(t)\quad(t\in(0,T]),\qquad x(0)=x_{0}\in X^{\prime},

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=B^{\prime}x(t)\quad(t\in[0,T]),

and
C_{\mathrm{unc}}
agrees with the corresponding observability constant.
Therefore, in order to prove null-controllability properties, we only need to establish a final state observability estimate of the form (
OBS
) for the dual semigroup
(S(t)^{\prime})_{t\geq 0}
of the
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by
A
.

In this paper we focus on the above-mentioned well-established method to prove an observability estimate (
OBS
) given an uncertainty principle (
UP
) and a dissipation estimate (
DISS
), i.e.,

\eqref{eq:UP}+\eqref{eq:DISS}\implies\eqref{eq:OBS}.

We will work with general growth rates for
(
UP
)
and decay rates for (
DISS
), i.e., with increasing functions
f,g,h\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

\forall x\in X,\,\lambda>0:\quad\left\lVert P_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{X}\leq\mathrm{e}^{f(\lambda)}\left\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\right\rVert_{Y}

(UPgen)

and

\forall x\in X,\,\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T]:\quad\left\lVert(I-P_{\lambda})S(t)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(t)}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X}.

(DISSgen)

We show that under suitable assumptions on the functions
f,g
and
h
the method can still be applied to obtain a final state observability estimate, i.e.,

\eqref{eq:UPg}+\eqref{eq:DISSg}\implies\eqref{eq:OBS}.

Such a form of generalization has already been considered for the Hilbert space setting in
[
DM12
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the abstract point of view using semigroups on Banach spaces was not addressed so far. Moreover, compared to
[
DM12
]
, we provide an (asymptotically sharp) estimate for
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
which is explicit in the model parameters. Furthermore, we also allow for more general functions
f,g
and
h
as in
[
DM12
]
. In this sense, we extend the so far known results.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we state and prove the abstract theorem stating the implication

\eqref{eq:UPg}+\eqref{eq:DISSg}\implies\eqref{eq:OBS}.

Moreover, we relate the assumptions on
f,g
and
h
to the ones in the existing literature and give illustrative examples.
Then in Section
3
we exploit subordination techniques to show that (
UPgen
) and (
DISSgen
) do not just imply (
OBS
) for one observation system, but also for many related ones. It turns out that only the function
g
is influenced by subordination, namely if
\varphi
is the corresponding Bernstein function and
\varphi\circ g
satisfies the assumptions on
g
instead, then the subordinated semigroup also satisfies a final state observability estimate. This fits nicely to the intuition since the subordinated semigroup is generated by
-\varphi(-A)
, where
A
is the generator of the original semigroup. In Section
4
we will apply our results to semigroups induced by vaguely continuous convolution semigroups of sub-probability measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
, i.e., of semigroups related to Lévy processes. The function
g
is then related to the corresponding Lévy measure, or put differently, the symbol of the semigroup of measures.
These two types of applications naturally give rise to the extension of the growth/decay rates to general functions.

2
Sufficient Criteria for Final State Observability

The following theorem provides sufficient conditions for a final state observability estimate in terms of a generalized uncertainty principle and dissipation estimate. Moreover, the theorem gives an upper bound on the observability constant.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
C\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
a semigroup on
X
,
M\geq 1
and
\omega\in\mathds{R}
such that
\lVert S(t)\rVert\leq M\mathrm{e}^{\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
, and assume that for all
x\in X
the mapping
t\mapsto\lVert CS(t)x\rVert_{Y}
is measurable. Consider measurable functions
f,g,h\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
f
and
h
are strictly monotonically increasing and bijective, and
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/g(f^{-1}(\lambda))
is monotonically decreasing. Let
T>0
and assume that there exists
\lambda_{T}\geq 0
with the property

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{T}}^{\infty}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda}{g(f^{-1}(\lambda))}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{\ln(2)}{4}\min\{1,T\}.

(1)

Furthermore, let
C_{1}\geq 0
,
C_{2}\geq 1
and assume that

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,x\in X:\quad\lVert P_{\lambda}x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{f(\lambda)}\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\rVert_{Y},

(2)

and

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(t)x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(t)+\omega t}\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

(3)

Then we have

\forall x\in X:\quad\left\lVert S(T)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\text{with}\quad C_{\mathrm{obs}}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{6\lambda_{T}+\omega_{+}T},

(4)

where
\omega_{+}=\max\{\omega,0\}
and

C_{3}=8\mathrm{e}^{3}MC_{1}\left(M\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}\right)^{\frac{6}{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}}.

The integrability condition (
1
) in Theorem
2.1
describes the class of functions
f,g,h
for which our strategy works. Moreover, the quantity
\lambda_{T}
describes the
T
-dependence of
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
. In Example
2.5
appropriate values of
\lambda_{T}
for concrete scenarios are computed. Note that in Theorem
2.1
we do not assume the semigroup
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
to be strongly continuous, but only that the mapping
t\mapsto\lVert CS(t)x\rVert_{Y}
is measurable. This makes the theorem also applicable for dual semigroups on non-reflexive Banach spaces.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is given at the end of this section. Before, we mention direct consequences of Theorem
2.1
in Remark
2.2
. And in Remark
2.3
as well as Example
2.4
we compare our result to
[
DM12
]
and
[
GST20
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

We can use Hölder’s inequality to obtain from (
4
) an observability estimate w.r.t. to the
L_{r}
-norm for
r\in[1,\infty]
.
Indeed, applying Theorem
2.1
for the rescaled semigroup
\widetilde{S}(t):=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}S(t)
(
t\geq 0
) yields for
x\in X
the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert S(T)x\right\rVert_{X}=\mathrm{e}^{\omega T}\|\widetilde{S}(T)x\|_{X}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\omega T}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\tau}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Note that, since
\bigl(\widetilde{S}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
is bounded,
C_{\mathrm{obs}}
does not depend on
\omega
.
Using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S(T)x\|_{X}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}^{r}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\quad\text{with }C=C_{\mathrm{obs}}\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\omega r^{\prime}T}-1}{\omega r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}&\text{for }\omega>0\\
T^{1/r^{\prime}}&\text{for }\omega=0\\
\left(\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{\omega r^{\prime}T}}{-\omega r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}&\text{for }\omega<0,\end{cases}

and
r^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
with
1/r+1/r^{\prime}=1
.

(b)

Similar as in
[
DM12
]
, instead of (
3
) we can use the dissipation estimate

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S_{t}x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(t)+\omega_{+}T+m\lambda}\lVert x\rVert_{X}

for some
m\geq 0
. In this case we get the final state observability estimate (
4
) with
\lambda_{T}\geq 0
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{T}}^{\infty}h^{-1}\left((4+m)\frac{\lambda}{g(f^{-1}(\lambda))}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{\ln(2)}{4}\min\{1,T\}.

Although in Theorem
2.1
we consider
m=0
, the case
m>0
can easily be implemented in our proof.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(q)}\int_{\varphi^{-1}(R)}^{\infty}\frac{f(g^{-1}(\lambda))}{\lambda^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{1}{\ln(q)}\int_{g^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(R))}^{\infty}\frac{f(\mu)g^{\prime}(\mu)}{g(\mu)^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(q)}\int_{f(g^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(R)))}^{\infty}\frac{\xi g^{\prime}(f^{-1}(\xi))}{g(f^{-1}(\xi))^{2}f^{\prime}(f^{-1}(\xi))}\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\frac{1}{\ln(q)}\int_{f(g^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(R)))}^{\infty}\frac{1}{g(f^{-1}(\xi))}\;\mathrm{d}\xi,

where we used that

, whereas

.
Finally, let us emphasize that in
[

]
no estimate on the constant in (

Example 2.4
.

Let us consider the important case of polynomial functions

(11)

for all
\lambda>0
,
t>0
, and
x=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\in L_{2}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
are satisfied with
C\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\Omega),L_{2}(E))
being the restriction operator, and
f(\lambda)=c\sqrt{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
and
h(t)=t
for
t>0
. Theorem
2.1
not only gives an observability estimate for the semigroup associated to
-g(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathrm{Dir}}})
, but also an estimate on the observation constant (suitable values of
\lambda_{T}
are given in the table of Example
2.5
). Note that we improve the result given in
[
DM12
, Theorem 7]
, since, e.g., the function
g(\lambda)=\lambda\bigl(\ln(1+\ln(1+\lambda))\bigr)^{s}
for
s\in(1,2]
does not satisfy the integrability condition in
[
DM12
]
. Finally, let us emphasize that, using duality results, the observability inequality given in Theorem
2.1
implies null-controllability of the anomalous diffusion equation with control set
E
.

We now complete this section by giving the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Instead of the telescopic series method used in
[
DM12
]
, the proof of Theorem
2.1
is based on an iteration argument due to
[
TT11
,
NTTV20
,
GST20
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

W.l.o.g., we may assume that
f(\lambda)=\lambda
for all
\lambda>0
. Indeed, for a general function
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
, satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, the claim then follows by replacing
g
by
g\circ f^{-1}
and considering the rescaled operators
\widetilde{P}_{\lambda}:=P_{f(\lambda)}
,
\lambda>0
, in (
2
) and (
3
).
Further, w.l.o.g. we may assume that the semigroup
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}

is bounded, i.e.,
\omega=0
. Indeed, the rescaled semigroup
\widetilde{S}(t):=\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}S(t)
(
t\geq 0
) is bounded and applying the theorem for bounded semigroups yields

\forall x\in X:\quad\|\widetilde{S}(T)x\|_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert C\widetilde{S}(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau,

and therefore

\|S(T)x\|_{X}=\mathrm{e}^{\omega T}\|\widetilde{S}(T)x\|_{X}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\omega T}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\tau}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{+}T}C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, it suffices to prove the theorem for bounded semigroups.
The proof is divided in two steps.

Step 1. Approximate observability estimate.
Let
\lambda>0
,
t\in(0,T]
, and
x\in X
. By (
2
) and (
3
), for
\tau\in(0,t]
we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert P_{\lambda}S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert CP_{\lambda}S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+\left(C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(\tau)}\lVert x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{X}+\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(\tau)+\lambda}\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

(12)

Since
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
is a bounded semigroup, we get

\lVert S(t)x\rVert_{X}=\lVert S(t-\tau)S(\tau)x\lVert_{X}\leq M\lVert S(\tau)x\rVert_{X}.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. Integrating (
12
) with respect to
\tau\in[\delta t,t]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\delta)t}{M}\lVert S(t)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\int_{\delta t}^{t}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau+\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}\int_{\delta t}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(\tau)+\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\,\lVert x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\int_{\delta t}^{t}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau+\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}(1-\delta)t\,\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(\delta t)+\lambda}\,\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

By multiplying both sides by
M/(1-\delta)t
we finally arrive at

\left\lVert S(t)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}(t,\lambda)\int_{\delta t}^{t}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\mathrm{d}\tau+\alpha(t,\lambda)\|x\|_{X}

(13)

with

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{obs}}(t,\lambda)=\frac{M}{(1-\delta)t}C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha(t,\lambda)=M\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)h(\delta t)+\lambda}.

(14)

Step 2. Iteration argument.
Set
K:=2^{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}}M\left(C_{1}\lVert C\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+1\right)C_{2}
and let
\lambda_{T}\geq 0
satisfy (
1
). We define

\lambda_{0}:=\frac{2\ln(K)}{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}+2\lambda_{T}

and for
k\in\mathds{N}
we set
\lambda_{k}:=\lambda_{0}2^{k}
. Furthermore, for
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, we define

\tau_{k}:=\frac{T}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}+2\max\{1,T\}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{k}}{g(\lambda_{k})}\right)>0

and recursively
T_{k+1}:=T_{k}-\tau_{k}
, where
T_{0}:=T
. Next, we show that
\tau_{k},T_{k}\in(0,T]
for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
. For this, note that since
C_{2}\geq 1
by assumption, we have
K\geq 2^{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}}
and hence
\ln(K)\geq\frac{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}{2}
. This shows that
\lambda_{0}\geq 1
and hence

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{k}}\leq 1.

(15)

Since
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/g(\lambda)
is monotonically decreasing and
h^{-1}
is monotonically increasing, for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{k}}{g(\lambda_{k})}\right)\leq\int_{k-1}^{k}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{0}2^{s}}{g(\lambda_{0}2^{s})}\right)\;\mathrm{d}s,

and therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tau_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}+2\max\{1,T\}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\;\int_{k-1}^{k}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{0}2^{s}}{g(\lambda_{0}2^{s})}\right)\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{2}+2\max\{1,T\}\int_{-1}^{\infty}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{0}2^{s}}{g(\lambda_{0}2^{s})}\right)\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2}+\frac{2\max\{1,T\}}{\ln(2)}\int_{\lambda_{0}/2}^{\infty}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda}{g(\lambda)}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Since
\lambda_{0}/2\geq\lambda_{T}
, by (
1
) we observe

\displaystyle\frac{2\max\{1,T\}}{\ln(2)}\int_{\lambda_{0}/2}^{\infty}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda}{g(\lambda)}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{2\max\{1,T\}}{\ln(2)}\frac{\ln(2)\min\{1,T\}}{4}=\frac{T}{2}.

Hence, we have
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tau_{k}\leq T
, which gives
T_{k}=T-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\tau_{k}\in(0,T]
.

Let
x\in X
. For
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, we apply the approximate observability estimate (
13
) to
t=\tau_{k}
,
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
,
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
and
S(T_{k+1})x
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert S(T_{k})x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\mathrm{d}\tau+\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\|S(T_{k+1})x\|_{X}

(16)

with

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})=\frac{2MC_{1}}{\tau_{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})=K\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda_{k})h(\tau_{k}/2)+\lambda_{k}}.

Note that for technical reasons the constant
K
in
\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})
is by the factor
2^{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2}}\geq 1
larger compared to (
14
), and we performed integration on
(0,T]
instead of
(\tau_{k}/2,\tau_{k}]
.
The inequalities (
16
) can be iterated. Starting from
k=0
, after
N+1
steps we obtain

\displaystyle\|S(T)x\|_{X}\leq\left(C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha(\tau_{l},\lambda_{l})\right)\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\mathrm{d}\tau

(17)

\displaystyle+\|S(T_{N+1})x\|_{X}\prod_{k=0}^{N}\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k}).

(18)

Let us first consider the term (
18
). For
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, by definition of
\tau_{k}
and since
h
is monotonically increasing, we obtain

h(\tau_{k}/2)\geq h\left(\max\{1,T\}h^{-1}\left(4\frac{\lambda_{k}}{g(\lambda_{k})}\right)\right)\geq 4\frac{\lambda_{k}}{g(\lambda_{k})},

and therefore

\displaystyle\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\leq K\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda_{k})\frac{4\lambda_{k}}{g(\lambda_{k})}+\lambda_{k}}\leq K\mathrm{e}^{-3\lambda_{k}}.

Using that

\sum_{k=0}^{N}\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{N}2^{k}=\lambda_{0}\left(2^{N+1}-1\right)=\lambda_{N+1}-\lambda_{0},

we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{N}\alpha(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\leq K^{N+1}\mathrm{e}^{-3\sum_{k=0}^{N}\lambda_{k}}=K^{N+1}\mathrm{e}^{-3\lambda_{N+1}+3\lambda_{0}}\to 0\quad(N\to\infty),

since
\lambda_{N+1}\geq 2^{N+1}
for all
N\in\mathds{N}_{0}
. Since
\bigl(S(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
is bounded, the summand (
18
) converges to zero as
N\to\infty
.

It remains to estimate the right-hand side in (
17
). First, note that for
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
we have, by the definition of
\tau_{k}
, that
\tau_{k}\geq\frac{T}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}
and hence

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})=\frac{2MC_{1}}{\tau_{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{k}}\leq\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda_{k}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha(\tau_{l},\lambda_{l})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda_{k}}K^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-3\lambda_{k}+3\lambda_{0}}=\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{3\lambda_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}K^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}.

Since
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}2^{k}
for
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, the series converges for
N\to\infty
. Next, we show that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}K^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}\leq 1.

For this, we estimate

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}K^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}\leq\sup_{x\geq 1}K^{x}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}2^{x}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}}=\left(\frac{2\ln(K)}{\lambda_{0}\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}\right)^{\frac{\ln(K)}{\ln(2)}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}},

where the last identity follows from elementary calculus. Since
\lambda_{0}\geq\frac{2\ln(K)}{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}
and by (
15
) we have

\left(\frac{2\ln(K)}{\lambda_{0}\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}\right)^{\frac{\ln(K)}{\ln(2)}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}}\leq 1.

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{k},\lambda_{k})\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\alpha(\tau_{l},\lambda_{l})\leq\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{3\lambda_{0}}.

By this estimate and
C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau_{0},\lambda_{0})\leq\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda_{0}}\leq\frac{4MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{3\lambda_{0}}
, (
17
) yields

\left\lVert S(T)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\text{with}\quad C_{\mathrm{obs}}\leq\frac{8MC_{1}}{T}\mathrm{e}^{3\lambda_{0}}.

By definition of
\lambda_{0}
, we conclude

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{obs}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8MC_{1}}{T}\exp\left(\frac{12\ln(K)}{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}+6\lambda_{T}\right)\leq\frac{8MC_{1}}{T}K^{\frac{6}{\mathrm{e}\ln(2)}}\mathrm{e}^{6\lambda_{T}}.

The claim now follows by inserting the definition of
K
.
∎

3
Final State Observability for Subordinated Semigroups

We now apply Theorem
2.1
to so-called subordinated semigroups introduced by Bochner in 1949
[
Boch49
]
. We will only deal with strongly continuous semigroups in the presentation; however, note that subordination can also be performed in more general settings (see e.g.
[
KMS21
]
), in particular for dual semigroups of strongly continuous semigroups.

Definition 3.1

(Bernstein function)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
.
Then
\varphi
is called
Bernstein function
provided
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
, and
(-1)^{k-1}\varphi^{(k)}\geq 0
for all
k\in\mathds{N}
.

Bernstein functions can be characterised as follows.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
SSV12
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

\varphi
is a Bernstein function.

(b)

There exist unique constants
a,b\geq 0
and a unique positive Radon measure
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
satisfying
\int_{(0,\infty)}1\wedge t\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty
such that

\varphi(\lambda)=a+b\lambda+\int\limits_{(0,\infty)}\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda t}\bigr)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)\quad(\lambda>0).

(19)

The representation of a Bernstein function
\varphi
in (
19
) is called
Lévy–Khinchin representation
and the triplet
(a,b,\mu)
is called
Lévy triplet
of
\varphi
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a family of Radon measures on
[0,\infty)
and
\mu
a Radon measure on
[0,\infty)
.
Then
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called

(a)

a family of
sub-probability measures
if
\forall t\in[0,\infty):\quad\mu_{t}\big([0,\infty)\big)\leq 1
,

(b)

a
convolution semigroup
if
\mu_{0}=\delta_{0}
and
\forall s,t\in[0,\infty):\quad\mu_{t}\ast\mu_{s}=\mu_{t+s}
,

(c)

vaguely continuous at
s\in[0,\infty)
with limit
\mu
if

\forall f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}[0,\infty):\quad\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow s}\int\limits_{[0,\infty)}f(\lambda)\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}\lambda)=\int\limits_{[0,\infty)}f(\lambda)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\lambda).

(20)

Note that if
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of sub-probability measures which is vaguely continuous at
0
with limit
\delta_{0}
, then
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is also weakly continuous, i.e., (
20
) actually even holds for all
f\in C_{\mathrm{b}}[0,\infty)
.

The Laplace transform relates Bernstein functions to vaguely continuous convolution semigroups of sub-probability measures on
[0,\infty)
.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
SSV12
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a convolution semigroup of sub-probability measures on
[0,\infty)

which is vaguely continuous at
0
with limit
\delta_{0}
.
Then there exists a unique Bernstein function
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
t\geq 0
the Laplace transform of
\mu_{t}
is given by

\mathcal{L}(\mu_{t})=\mathrm{e}^{-t\varphi}.

Conversely, given any Bernstein function
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, there exists a unique vaguely continuous convolution semigroup
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of sub-probability measures on
[0,\infty)
such that the above equation holds.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
, and
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a Bernstein function. Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the vaguely continuous convolution semigroup of sub-probability measures on
[0,\infty)
associated with
\varphi
. For
t\geq 0
we define
S^{\varphi}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
by

S^{\varphi}(t)x:=\int_{[0,\infty)}S(s)x\;\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)\quad(x\in X).

Then
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is called
subordinated semigroup
to
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
w.r.t.
\varphi
.

A first question to ask is whether this defines again a
C_{0}
-semigroup.
The answer is affirmative.

Proposition 3.6

(see, e.g.,
[
SSV12
, Proposition 13.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
,
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a Bernstein function, and
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
the subordinated semigroup to
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
w.r.t.
\varphi
.
Then
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is again a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.

In view of Proposition
3.6
, the generator of
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is given by
-\varphi(-A)
, where
A
is the generator of
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
; cf.
[
Phi52
, Theorem 4.3]
.

The next lemma will be an important ingredient in order to apply
Theorem

2.1
to subordinated semigroups.
It describes how dissipation of the original semigroup transfers to subordinated semigroups.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
, and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.
Let further
T>0
,
g\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be measurable and
C_{2}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(t)x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)t}\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

Let finally
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Bernstein function and
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
the subordinated semigroup to
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
w.r.t.
\varphi
.
Then

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S^{\varphi}(t)x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(g(\lambda))t}\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the vaguely continuous convolution semigroup associated with
\varphi
.
Let
\lambda>0
,
t\in(0,T]
, and
x\in X
.
Then

\displaystyle\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S^{\varphi}(t)x\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle=\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})\int_{0}^{\infty}S(s)x\;\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)\rVert_{X}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(s)x\rVert_{X}\;\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)s}\;\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X}.

Note that the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathds{K}
has the generator
-g(\lambda)
. Thus, the corresponding subordinated semigroup has the generator
-\varphi(g(\lambda))
by Proposition
3.6
.
Hence,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)s}\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}s)=\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(g(\lambda))t}
and thus

\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S^{\varphi}(t)x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\varphi(g(\lambda))t}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{X}.\hfill\qed

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
C\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
(P_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
, and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
. Let
T>0
,
g\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
measurable and
C_{1}\geq 0
,
C_{2}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,x\in X:\quad\lVert P_{\lambda}x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\lambda}\lVert CP_{\lambda}x\rVert_{Y}

and

\displaystyle\forall\lambda>0,\,t\in(0,T],\,x\in X:\quad\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S(t)x\rVert_{X}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{e}^{-g(\lambda)t}\lVert x\rVert_{X}.

Let
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Bernstein function,
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
the subordinated semigroup to
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
w.r.t.
\varphi
, and assume that
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/(\varphi\circ g(\lambda))
is monotonically decreasing and that
1/(\varphi\circ g)
integrable at
\infty
.
Then there exists
C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
such that

\forall x\in X:\quad\left\lVert S^{\varphi}(T)x\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert CS^{\varphi}(\tau)x\rVert_{Y}\;\mathrm{d}\tau.

(21)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
the dissipation estimate for
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is satisfied. Thus, the statement follows from

2.1
.
∎

Example 3.9
.

As a standard example, we consider the heat semigroup on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
where
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Let
A:=\Delta
the Laplacian in
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
with domain
W_{p}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})
and
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
the
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by
A
.
Let
g(\lambda):=\lambda^{2}
for
\lambda>0
,
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, and
\varphi(\lambda):=\lambda^{s}
for
\lambda>0
which defines a Bernstein function.
Then
\lambda\mapsto\lambda/\varphi(g(\lambda))
is monotonically decreasing and
1/(\varphi\circ g)
is integrable at
\infty
.
Let
E\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be a so-called thick set, i.e.,
E
is measurable and

\left\lvert E\cap\left(\bigtimes_{i=1}^{n}(0,L_{i})+x\right)\right\rvert\geq\rho\prod_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\quad(x\in\mathds{R}^{n}),

for some
\rho\in(0,1]
and
L\in(0,\infty)^{n}
. Here,
|\cdot|
denotes Lebesgue measure in
\mathds{R}^{n}
. Moreover, let
C\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n}),L_{p}(E))
be the restriction operator and for
\lambda>0
let
P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n}))
be induced by a smooth cut-off function
\mathds{1}_{B(0,\lambda/2)}\leq\chi_{\lambda}\leq\mathds{1}_{B(0,\lambda)}
in Fourier space; cf.
[
BGST21
]
.
Then the uncertainty principle is satisfied by a Logvinenko–Sereda type theorem (see
Proposition

4.11
below or
[
EV18
,
WWZZ19
,
BGST21
]
). Moreover, exploiting the heat kernel, we deduce a dissipation estimate with
g
and
h(t)=t
for all
t>0
(see
[
BGST21
]
).
Hence,
Theorem

3.8
is applicable and we obtain final state observability for
(S^{\varphi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, which is generated by the fractional Laplacian
-\varphi(-A)=-(-A)^{s}=-(-\Delta)^{s}
.
Note that our method does not work for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, as
1/(\varphi\circ g)
is not integrable at
\infty
anymore. In
[
Koe20
]
, it was shown that the
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by
-(-\Delta)^{1/2}
on
L_{2}(\mathds{R})
does not satisfy a final state observability estimate.

Remark 3.10
.

In order to apply
Theorem

3.8
a rather explicit knowledge about the Bernstein function
\varphi
is needed.
This can be difficult if only the Lévy triplet
(a,b,\mu)
of
\varphi
is given. We only consider the case
a=0
.

If
b\neq 0
, nothing has to be done since this means
\varphi(\lambda)\geq b\lambda
for all
\lambda>0
.

If
b=0
, however, a closer examination of
\mu
becomes necessary.
It is easy to see that for all
\lambda>0
we have

\frac{1}{2}\lambda\int\limits_{(0,\frac{1}{\lambda})}t\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)\leq\varphi(\lambda)\leq\lambda\int\limits_{(0,\frac{1}{\lambda})}t\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)+2\mu\Bigl(\bigl[\frac{1}{\lambda},\infty\bigr)\Bigr),

since
\frac{1}{2}x\leq 1-\textrm{e}^{-x}\leq x
for
x\in(0,1)
(note that
\lambda t\in(0,1)
for
t\in(0,\frac{1}{\lambda})
).
Thus, growth properties of
\varphi
can be studied by looking at
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\int_{(0,\frac{1}{\lambda})}t\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
instead.
As a (standard) example,
let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be defined by the Lévy triplet
(0,0,\mu)
with
\mu(B):=\int_{B}t^{-1-s}\,\mathrm{d}t
for
B\subseteq(0,\infty)
measurable, i.e.,

\varphi(\lambda)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\textrm{e}^{-\lambda t}\bigr)t^{-1-s}\mathrm{d}t\quad(\lambda>0).

(22)

Since

\varphi(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda\int\limits_{(0,\frac{1}{\lambda})}t\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\frac{\lambda^{s}}{2(1-s)}\quad(\lambda>0),

we can apply
Theorem

3.8
for the Laplacian on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
as in
Example

3.9
, and for
\varphi
as in (
22
) with
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

4
Final State Observability for semigroups of sub-probability measures

In this section we apply our results to semigroups generated by families
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of sub-probability measures in
\mathds{R}^{n}
. The following definition is analogous to
Definition

3.3
for Radon measures on
[0,\infty)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a family of Radon measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
and
\mu
a Radon measure on
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Then
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called

(a)

a family of
sub-probability measures
if
\forall t\in[0,\infty):\quad\mu_{t}(\mathds{R}^{n})\leq 1
,

(b)

a
convolution semigroup
if
\mu_{0}=\delta_{0}
and
\forall s,t\in[0,\infty):\quad\mu_{t}\ast\mu_{s}=\mu_{t+s}
,

(c)

vaguely continuous at
s\in[0,\infty)
with limit
\mu
if

\forall f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathds{R}^{n}):\quad\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow s}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Due to the following theorem, the Fourier transform of vaguely continuous semigroups of sub-probability measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
corresponds to continuous positive-definite functions.
Recall that a function
f\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
is called
positive-definite
if for all
k\in\mathds{N}
and all
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
the matrix
\bigl(f(x_{i}-x_{l})\bigr)_{1\leq i,l\leq k}\in\mathds{C}^{k\times k}
is positive semi-definite in the spectral sense, i.e., all its eigenvalues are non-negative.

Theorem 4.2

(Bochner,
[
Jac01
, Theorem 3.5.7]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a positive and finite Radon measure on
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Then its
Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\mu\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\ni\xi\mapsto(\mathcal{F}\mu)(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\textrm{e}^{-i\left(x|\xi\right)}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

is a continuous positive-definite function.
Conversely, every continuous positive-definite function is a Fourier transform of a positive and finite Radon measure.

Recall that a function
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
is called
negative-definite
if
\psi(0)\geq 0
and for every
t\geq 0
the function
\xi\mapsto\textrm{e}^{-t\psi(\xi)}
is positive-definite.

Corollary 4.3

(
[
Jac01
, Theorem 3.6.16]
)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a vaguely continuous semigroup of sub-probability measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists a unique continuous negative-definite function
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
such that

\mathcal{F}\mu_{t}=\textrm{e}^{-t\psi}\quad(t\geq 0).

Conversely, for every continuous negative-definite function
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
there exists a vaguely continuous semigroup of sub-probability measures
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
such that the above equation holds.

In view of
Corollary

4.3
the negative-definite function
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
corresponding to a vaguely continuous semigroup of sub-probability measures
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
is called its
symbol
.

As in
Section

3
there is a
Lévy–Khinchin formula
.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
Jac01
, Theorem 3.7.8]
)
.

Let
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

\psi
is negative-definite.

(b)

There exist unique
c\geq 0
,
d\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
Q\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
positive semi-definite and a unique measure
\mu
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
such that

\psi(\xi)=c+i\left(d|\xi\right)+\left(\xi|Q\xi\right)+\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\Bigl(1-\textrm{e}^{-i\left(x|\xi\right)}-\frac{i\left(x|\xi\right)}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\quad(\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n}).

(23)

The measure
\mu
in (
23
) is called
Lévy measure
associated with
\psi
.

We will be interested in continuous negative-definite functions
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
such that
\operatorname{Re}\psi
tends to
+\infty
at
\infty
.
Note that the growth properties of
\operatorname{Re}\psi
are entirely determined by the corresponding
Q
and
\mu
.
In any case
\operatorname{Re}\psi
will grow if
Q
is positive definite, i.e., all its eigenvalues are strictly positive because then there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)\geq\alpha\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert^{2}
for all
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Hence, we need to concentrate on the integral term in
Equation

23
in case
Q
is not positive definite.
The following lemma addresses the case
Q=0
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathds{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mu(\{0\})=0
and
\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
.
For
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
define
B_{\xi}:=B(0,\tfrac{1}{\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert})
and functions
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
and
\phi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathds{R}
by

\psi(\xi):=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\Bigl(1-\textrm{e}^{-i\left(x|\xi\right)}-\frac{i\left(x|\xi\right)}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\phi(\xi):=\int\limits_{B_{\xi}}\left(\xi|x\right)^{2}\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Then

\frac{11}{24}\phi(\xi)\leq\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)\leq 2\left(\phi(\xi)+\mu\bigl(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\xi}\bigr)\right)\quad(\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

In particular,

\lim\limits_{\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert\to\infty}\phi(\xi)=\infty
implies
\lim\limits_{\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert\to\infty}\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Note that

\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\Bigl(1-\cos\bigl(\left(x|\xi\right)\bigr)\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Therefore

\int\limits_{B_{\xi}}\Bigl(1-\cos\bigl(\left(x|\xi\right)\bigr)\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\leq\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)\leq\int\limits_{B_{\xi}}\Bigl(1-\cos\bigl(\left(x|\xi\right)\bigr)\Bigr)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)+2\mu\bigl(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\xi}\bigr).

(24)

For
x\in B_{\xi}
we have that
\bigl(\tfrac{\left(x|\xi\right)^{2k}}{(2k)!}\bigr)_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
is a monotonically decreasing sequence of non-negative numbers with limit
0
.
Hence,

\frac{11}{24}\left(x|\xi\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(x|\xi\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{24}\left(x|\xi\right)^{4}\leq 1-\cos\bigl(\left(x|\xi\right)\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(x|\xi\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(x|\xi\right)^{2}.

Thus, we obtain the assertion.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

(a)

If
\mu(\mathds{R}^{n})<\infty
then
\operatorname{Re}\psi
is bounded.

(b)

If
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
is a continuous negative-definite function with Lévy–Khinchin representation (
23
), then
Lemma

4.5
can be easily generalised such that

\lim\limits_{\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert\to\infty,\xi\in N(Q)}\phi(\xi)=\infty

implies
\operatorname{Re}\psi(\xi)\to\infty
as
\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert\to\infty
.
Here
N(Q)
denotes the null space of the matrix
Q
and
\phi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathds{R}
is as in
Lemma

4.5
.

Let
m\in L_{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{n})
and
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Then
m
is called
Fourier multiplier on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
if
T_{m}\colon\mathcal{S}(\mathds{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathds{R}^{n})^{\prime}
given by

T_{m}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}m\mathcal{F}f

extends to an operator
T_{m}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n}))
which then is translation invariant.
Moreover, every Fourier multiplier on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
is also a Fourier multiplier on
L_{q}(\mathds{R}^{n})
for
q\geq p
(
[
Gra08
, p. 143]
).
In general, it is rather hard to characterise the set of Fourier multipliers on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.
However, for
p=2
, Plancherel’s theorem yields that the set of Fourier multipliers is precisely given by
L_{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{n})
, while, for
p=1
, the set of Fourier multipliers is the algebra of Fourier transforms of bounded Radon measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
More precisely, let
\mu
be a bounded Radon measure on
\mathds{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{F}\mu\in C_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathds{R}^{n})
and the operator
S_{\mu}\colon\mathcal{S}(\mathds{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathds{R}^{n})
given by
S_{\mu}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}\mu\cdot\mathcal{F}f)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\mu\ast f=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{n}}f(\cdot-y)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y)
has a continuous extension
S_{p,\mu}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n}))
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
, cf.
[
Gra08
, Thm. 2.5.8, Thm. 2.5.10]
.
Thus, given a vaguely continuous convolution semigroup of sub-probability measures
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathds{R}^{n}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we obtain a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{p}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
of contractions given by
S_{p}(t):=S_{p,\mu_{t}}
for
t\geq 0
(see, e.g.
[
Jac01
, Ex. 4.6.29]
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\lambda>0
and
n\in\mathds{N}
. Then
\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)^{n}}
is a Fourier multiplier on
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

(i)
For
n=1
the argument is well known.
Namely, by the Mikhlin multiplier theorem (
[
Gra08
, Thm. 5.2.7 (a)]
) the function
h:=-i\sgn
is a Fourier multiplier (called the Hilbert transform), and therefore so is

\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}=1-\frac{i}{2}h(\cdot-\lambda)-\frac{i}{2}h(\cdot+\lambda).

(ii)
We shall prove the statement for the case
n=2
for convenience.
The proof for general
n
is exactly the same.
It is enough to consider functions
f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{B_{i}}
, where the
A_{i}
’s as well as the
B_{i}
’s are mesurable with finite measure and pairwise disjoint since their linear span is dense in
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{2})
.
In the following, for
i=1,2
we shall write
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for the Fourier transform with respect to the first and second coordinate, respectively. Let
C:=\left\lVert T_{\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathds{R}))}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)\times(-\lambda,\lambda)}\mathcal{F}f\right\rVert_{L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\underbrace{\biggl(\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\Bigl|\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}\mathcal{F}_{2}\sum\limits_{i}c_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}})(x_{1})\mathds{1}_{B_{i}}\Bigr|^{p}(x_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\biggr)}_{\leq C^{p}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\Bigl|\sum\limits_{i}c_{i}(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}})(x_{1})\mathds{1}_{B_{i}}\Bigr|^{p}(x_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{p}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\sum\limits_{i}\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert^{p}\left\lvert(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}})(x_{1})\right\rvert^{p}\mathds{1}_{B_{i}}(x_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2p}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\int\limits_{\mathds{R}}\sum\limits_{i}\left\lvert c_{i}\right\rvert^{p}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(x_{1})\mathds{1}_{B_{i}}(x_{2})\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{2p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{2})}.\qed

Remark 4.8
.

Note that
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{2})=L_{p}(\mathds{R})\otimes L_{p}(\mathds{R})

.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a vaguely continuous convolution semigroup of sub-probability measures on
\mathds{R}^{n}
with related symbol
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{C}
and

4.5
.
Let
\bigl(S_{2}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}

Then for all
\lambda>0
,

and

\left\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S_{2}(t)f\right\rVert_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\textrm{e}^{-tg(\lambda)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})}.

\left\lVert(\operatorname{Id}-P_{\lambda})S_{2}(t)f\right\rVert_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})}=\left\lVert(1-\mathds{1}_{(-\lambda,\lambda)^{n}})\textrm{e}^{-t\operatorname{Re}\psi}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq\textrm{e}^{-tg(\lambda)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{n})},

where for the last estimate we used

4.5
.
∎

.

Let

,

,
\bigl(S_{p}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}

such that

holds for all

Proposition 4.11

.

C_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq 0
with

\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n}):\quad\left\lVert S_{p}(T)f\right\rVert_{L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\mathrm{obs}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert S_{p}(t)f\right\rVert_{L_{p}(E)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We finish the paper by having a look at examples and non-examples.

Example 4.13
.

We have already seen that in the abscence of a suitable matrix
Q
the growing properties of a negative-definite function
\psi
is solely determined by how the corresponding Lévy measure
\mu
in the Lévy–Khinchin formula behaves near
0
.
For this reason let us consider in the example symbols of the form

\psi(\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\Bigl(1-\textrm{e}^{-i\left(x|\xi\right)}-\frac{i\left(x|\xi\right)}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\Bigr)\rho(\left\lVert x\right\rVert)\,\mathrm{d}x

with measurable
\rho\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathds{R}
, i.e.
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure with a radially symmetric density
\rho
(and
c=0
,
d=0
, and
Q=0
in the Lévy–Khinchin formula).

(a)

If we choose
\rho:=\mathds{1}_{(0,1)}
then a calculation shows that
g(\lambda)=D\cdot\lambda^{-n}
(
\lambda>0
) for some suitable constant
D>0
.
Hence,
Theorem

4.12
is clearly not applicable.

(b)

By
(a)
we sense that we should try to get a singularity for
\rho
at
0
.
For this purpose choose now
\rho\colon r\mapsto r^{-n-2+\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
.
In this case we find
g(\lambda)=D\cdot\lambda^{2-\varepsilon}
(
\lambda>0
) for some
D>0
and
g
is increasing as long as
\varepsilon<2
.
Note that
g
grows slower than
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{2}
(however the growth can get arbitrarily close to it) in accordance with general results on Lévy processes.
Moreover,
Theorem

4.12
is applicable if
\varepsilon<1
.

(c)

A special case of (a scaled version of)
(b)
is given by
\varepsilon=2-2\alpha
for
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Thus, we recover the statement of
Example

3.9
since

\psi(\xi)=\int\limits_{\mathds{R}^{n}}\Bigl(1-\textrm{e}^{-i\left(x|\xi\right)}-\frac{i\left(x|\xi\right)}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}}\Bigr)\rho(\left\lVert x\right\rVert)\,\mathrm{d}x=\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert^{2\alpha}

for

\rho(r)=\frac{4^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\alpha)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(-\alpha)}r^{-n-2\alpha},

as a longer calculation shows.
We see that using
Lemma

4.5
instead of the explicit knowledge of the symbol
\psi
yields the same result.

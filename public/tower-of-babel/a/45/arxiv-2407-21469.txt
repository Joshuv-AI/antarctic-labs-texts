Hesselink strata in small characteristic and Lusztig–Xue pieces Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification 21020 . Primary 14L30, 17B45. Secondary 14L15, 20G41. Key words and phrases : simple algebraic groups, nilpotent orbits, Hesselink strata
Alexander Premet

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 {\Bbbk} of characteristic p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 and 𝔤 = Lie ( G ) {\mathfrak{g}}=\mathop{Lie}(G) . In this paper we show that the nilpotent pieces LX ⁡ ( Δ ) {\rm LX}(\Delta) introduced by Lusztig form a partition of the nilpotent cone of 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} and hence coincide with the Hesselink strata ℋ ⁡ ( Δ ) {\mathcal{H}}(\Delta) where Δ \Delta runs through the set of all weighted Dynkin diagrams of G G . Thanks to earlier results obtained by Lusztig, Xue and Voggesberger this boils down to describing the pieces LX ⁡ ( Δ ) {\rm LX}(\Delta) for groups of type E 7 {\rm E_{7}} in characteristic 2 2 and for groups of type E 8 {\rm E_{8}} in characteristic 2 2 and 3 3 . Our arguments are computer-free, but rely very heavily on the results of Liebeck–Seitz obtained in [ [ LS ] ].

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group of rank
\ell
over an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
and
T
a maximal torus of
G
. Let
\Sigma
be the root system of
G
with respect to
T
and
\Pi
a basis of simple roots of
\Sigma
.
Write
X(T)
(resp.
X_{*}(T)
) for the lattice of rational characters
(resp. cocharacters) of
T

and
X_{*}^{+}(T)
for the intersection of
X_{*}(T)
with the dual Weyl chamber of
X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
associated with
\Pi
.
Each rational cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(G)
gives rise to a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading

{\mathfrak{g}}=\textstyle{\bigoplus}_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,i),\ \ \ \ {\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,i)\,=\,\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\,|\,\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,\lambda(t))\,x=t^{i}x\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ t\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}\}

of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathop{Lie}(G)
. For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we put
{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,\geq d):=\bigoplus_{i\geq d}\,{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,i)
and
{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,<d):=\bigoplus_{i<d}\,{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,i)
, and denote by

P(\lambda)=L(\lambda)R_{u}(\lambda)
the parabolic subgroup of
G
associated with
\lambda
.
Here
L(\lambda)=Z_{G}(\lambda)
is a Levi subgroup of
G
.
Recall that
\mathop{Lie}(P(\lambda))={\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,\geq 0)
and
\mathop{Lie}(L(\lambda))={\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,0)
.

We let
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
denote the nilpotent cone of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the variety of all
(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)
-unstable vectors of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and write
\mathfrak{D}_{G}
for the set of all

Dynkin labels

attached to the nilpotent orbits of a complex Lie algebra with root system
\Sigma
.
As explained in [
[
CP
]
], the Hesselink strata of
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
are parameterised by the set of cocharacters
\tau_{\Delta}\in X_{*}^{+}(T)
with
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
and form a partition of
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, so that

{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})\,=\,\textstyle{\bigsqcup}_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}}\,{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta).

The cocharacter
\tau_{\Delta}
can be read off the weighted Dynkin diagram
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})
associated with the complex nilpotent orbit with label
\Delta

as follows:
if
x
is a root vector of
{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to a simple root
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
then

(\mathrm{Ad}\,\tau_{\Delta}(t)))(x)=t^{a_{i}}x
for all
t\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
. The Hesselink stratum attached to
\tau_{\Delta}
has the form

{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)\,=\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,\big({\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss}+{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},\geq 3)\big)

where
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss}\neq\varnothing
is the set of all
(\mathrm{Ad}\,L(\tau_{\Delta}))
-semistable vectors of
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)
(see [
[
CP
]
] for more detail).

Given
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
we write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
for the set of all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)

such that
G_{x}\subset P(\tau_{\Delta})
where
G_{x}=Z_{G}(x)
is the stabiliser of
x
in
G
. As explained in [
[
CP
]
, Remark 7.3] each set
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
contains
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss}
, a nonempty Zariski open subset of
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)
. The set

{\rm LX}(\Delta)\,:=\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\big({\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}+{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},\geq 3)\big)

containing
{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
will be referred to as
the
Lusztig-Xue piece
of
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
associated with
\Delta
.
The pieces
{\rm LX}(\Delta)
and their analogues for
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
and for the unipotent variety of
G
were introduced by Lusztig.
Viability of these pieces has to do with the fact that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
is defined in a more transparent fashion than its elusive subset
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss}
.

In [
[
L2
]
, Appendix A], Lusztig and Xue proved that the pieces
{\rm LX}(\Delta)
form a partition of
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})

in the case where
G
is a classical group.
Very recently, the same property was established by Voggesberger for groups of type
{\rm G}_{2}
,
{\rm F}_{4}
and
{\rm E}_{6}
(see our discussion in
2.1
for more detail). These results imply that
\rm{LX}(\Delta)={\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
provided that
G
is not of type
{\rm E_{7}}
or
{\rm E_{8}}
.

The partition property of the coadjoint analogues of
{\rm LX}(\Delta)
was established by Lusztig [
[
L3
]
] and Xue [
[
Xue1
]
] in all cases where
G
is a simple algebraic group and
p={\rm char}({\Bbbk})
equals the ratio of the squared lengths of long and short roots in
\Sigma
. In all other cases
there is a
G
-equivariant bijection between
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\mathcal{N}}({{\mathfrak{g}}}^{*})
which enables one to identify the nilpotent coadjoint orbits and pieces of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with those of
{\mathfrak{g}}
; see [
[
PS
]
, Section 5.6].

It was conjectured in [
[
Vog
]
] that the equality

\rm{LX}(\Delta)={\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)

should also hold for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
in the case where
G
is a group of type
{\rm E_{7}}
or
{\rm E_{8}}
.
Our goal in this paper is to prove this conjecture.

The orbits
{\mathcal{O}}(e)=(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,e
with
e\in{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})

will be denoted by their Dynkin labels
\Delta
or their
variants
(\Delta)_{p}
. The latter are attached to a small number of new nilpotent orbits which appear when
(\Sigma,p)\in\{({\rm G_{2}},3),({\rm F_{4}},2),({\rm E_{7}},2),({\rm E_{8}},2),({\rm E_{8}},3)\}
; see [
[
LS
]
] for detail.
Combining our results obtained in Section 2 with the results of Lusztig, Xue and Voggesberger mentioned above we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
of characteristic
p\geq 0
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)={\rm LX}(\Delta)
for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
and hence

{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})\,=\,\textstyle{\bigsqcup}_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}}\,{\rm LX}(\Delta).

Since proving Theorem
1.1
reduces quickly to the case where
\Sigma
is an irreducible root system, we may assume without loss of generality that our algebraic group
G
is simple and simply connected.

We use Steinberg’s notation
x_{\alpha}(t)
for elements of the unipotent root subgroups
U_{\alpha}
of
G
; see [
[
Stein
]
, §3]. Simple root vectors

e_{\alpha_{i}}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
are denoted by
e_{i}
, and we always use Bourbaki’s numbering [
[
Bou
]
] of simple roots. We assume that root vectors
e_{\gamma}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\gamma}
come from a Chevalley basis of an admissible lattice
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex Lie algebra with root system
\Sigma
.
Since we mostly work over fields of characteristic
2
, the signs of structure constants do not really affect our computations.

The Weyl group of
\Sigma
is denoted by
W
and we write
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
for the canonical pairing between
X(T)
and
X_{*}(T)
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. We fix a
W
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-valued inner product
(\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,)
on
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}=X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
which enables one to identify
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
with the dual vector space
X(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}=X(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

When we need to specify a particular root vector, we sometimes follow the conventions of [
[
LS
]
]. For example, a root vector
e_{\gamma}
with
\gamma=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{4}+2\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6}
is denoted by
e_{234^{2}5^{2}6}
.
When confusion is unlikely, we prefer standard conventions for specifying root vectors.

We occasionally use the
(\mathrm{Ad}\,\,G)
-invariant
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued bilinear form
\kappa
on a minimal admissible lattice
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
introduced in [
[
CP
]
, 7.2]. Its reduction modulo

will be denoted by the same symbol.
When describing certain cocharacters
\tau\in X_{*}(T)
we often specify their effect
on the root vectors
e_{i}
where
1\leq i\leq\ell
. More precisely, if
(\mathrm{Ad}\,\tau(t))\,e_{i}=t^{r_{i}}\,e_{i}
for all

, then we write
\tau=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{\ell})
. This will cause no confusion since
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of

.

If
{\rm char}({\Bbbk})=p>0
then

carries a canonical restricted Lie algebra structure
{\mathfrak{g}}\ni x\mapsto x^{[p]}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
equivariant under the adjoint action of

.
It is well known that the nilpotent cone
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
coincides with the set of all

such that
x^{[p]^{N}}=0
fore
N\gg 0
.
A restricted Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of

is called

-
nilpotent
(resp.

) if
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
(resp. if the
[p]
-mapping

is one-to-one on
\mathfrak{a}
). Given

we write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
for the centraliser of

in the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we often use the fact that
\mathop{Lie}(G_{x})\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
.
If
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,r)
for some
\lambda\in X_{*}(G)
and

then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\lambda,i)
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\lambda,i)={\mathfrak{g}}_{e}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda,i)
.
We say that
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
is

if
x^{[p]}=x

2.
Hesselink strata and Lusztig–Xue pieces

2.1.

Let
\tau=\tau_{\Delta}\in X_{*}(T)
be the cocharacter associated with
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
and write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
for the set of all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)

such that
G_{x}\subset P(\tau)
.
Since the parabolic subgroup
P(\tau)
is optimal for all
x\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau,2)_{ss}
in the sense of the Kempf–Rousseau theory, we have the inclusion

{\mathcal{V}}(\tau,2)_{ss}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
. From [
[
CP
]
, Theorem 6.1(iii)] it follows that
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau,2)_{ss}
is a nonempty Zariski open subset of
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
.
The set

{\rm LX}(\Delta)\,:=\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\big({\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}+{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,\geq 3)\big)

is called the
Lusztig-Xue piece
of
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
associated with
\Delta
.
By the above,

{\rm LX}(\Delta)
contains
{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
for every
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
.

The pieces
{\rm LX}(\Delta)
were first introduced by Lusztig [
[
L2
]
] in the Lie algebra case. In [
[
L3
]
], the definition was extended to cover the coadjoint nullcone
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
and the unipotent variety
\mathfrak{U}(G)
of
G
.
In [
[
L2
]
, Appendix A], Lusztig and Xue proved that the following decomposition

(1)

{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})\,=\,\textstyle{\bigsqcup}_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}}\,\mathrm{LX}(\Delta)

holds for all groups of type
\rm A
,
\rm B
,
\rm C
,
\rm D
, the key point being that the union is disjoint. Very recently, the same property was established by L. Voggesberger for groups of type
{\rm G}_{2}
,
{\rm F}_{4}
and
{\rm E}_{6}

with the help of
Magma
; see [
[
Vog
]
, Theorem 1.1].

Note that if (
1
) holds for
G
then
\rm{LX}(\Delta)={\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
; see [
[
CP
]
, Remark 7.3.1] for detail. It was conjectured in [
[
Vog
]
, Conjecture 1.2] that (
1
) should also hold for the
{\Bbbk}
-groups of type
{\rm E_{7}}
and
{\rm E_{8}}
. A similar expectation (covering
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}})
,
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
and
\mathfrak{U}(G)
) was expressed by Lusztig in [
[
L3
]
, 2.3].

In this paper we aim to confirm Voggesberger’s conjecture. Lusztig’s expectation related to partitioning the unipotent variety of
G
will be discussed in
2.12
.
For completeness, we also provide a new proof for groups of type
{\rm G_{2}}
,
{\rm F_{4}}
and
{\rm E_{6}}
.

Our task will become much simpler if we establish the following:

(2)

\mbox{\it If $e\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)$ and $G_{e}\subset P(\tau_{\Delta})$ then $e\in{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)$}.

Proving statement (
2
) will occupy the main body of the paper.
From now on we assume that the group
G
is simple, simply connected, and has type
{\rm E}_{6}
,
{\rm E}_{7}
,
{\rm E}_{8}
,
{\rm F}_{4}
or
{\rm G}_{2}
. Let
\kappa
denote the normalised Killing form introduced in [
[
CP
]
, 7.3]. If all roots of
\Sigma
have the same length then the radical of
\kappa
coincides with the central toral subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
; see [
[
CP
]
, Lemma 7.3].
We start with a reduction lemma which will also explain why (
1
) always holds in good characteristic.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
G
is a group of type
\rm E
and let
\tau=\tau_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
.
If
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
is such that
G_{e}\subset P(\tau)
and

{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}=\mathop{Lie}(G_{e})
, then
e\in{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

As
G
is simply connected, the centre of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is spanned by a toral element
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)
which is nonzero if and only if
(\Sigma,p)
is one of
({\rm E_{6}},3)
of
({\rm E_{7}},2)
. For
i\geq 0
, we let
[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)]^{\perp}
denote the set of all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,-i-2)
such that
\kappa(x,[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)])=0
. Since
\kappa
is
(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)
-invariant
we have that

[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)]^{\perp}=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(-i-2)\,|\,\,[e,x]\in{\rm Rad}\,\kappa\}.

As

{\rm Rad}\,\kappa={\Bbbk}z\subset{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0),
we get

[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)]^{\perp}={\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\tau,-i-2)
for
i\geq 1
and
[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]^{\perp}=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,-2)\,|\,\,[e,x]\in{\Bbbk}z\}
.

By our assumption,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}=\mathop{Lie}(G_{e})\subset\mathop{Lie}(P(\tau))=\bigoplus_{\char 16\relax\geq 0}\,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i).
As
{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\tau,i)
it follows that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\tau,i)=0
for
i\leq-1
, forcing
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i+2)=[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)]
for all
i\geq 1
. Also,
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)=[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]
when
z=0
. If
z\neq 0
we can only say at this point that the subspace
[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]
has codimension
\leq 1
in
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
.

Since
{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}=\mathop{Lie}(G_{e})
we have that

\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim{\mathfrak{g}}-\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}=\textstyle{\sum}_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)-\textstyle{\sum}_{i\geq 0}\,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}_{e}(\tau,i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textstyle{\sum}_{i<0}\,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)+\textstyle{\sum}_{i\geq 0}\,\dim\,[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim\,[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]+\textstyle{\sum}_{i\not\in\{0,1,2\}}\,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i).

Since it follows from [
[
HSp
]
, Theorem 2] that
\dim\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,e

and
\textstyle{\sum}_{i\not\in\{0,1\}}\,\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,i)
are even numbers, it must be that

\dim\,[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]\equiv\dim{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)\mod 2
. In view of our earlier remarks this yields
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)=[e,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)]
.

Let
L(\tau)=Z_{G}(\tau)
, a Levi subgroup of
P(\tau)
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0)
. Since
[{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,0),e]
is contained in the tangent space
T_{e}\big((\mathrm{Ad}\,L(\tau))\,e\big)
, the
(\mathrm{Ad}\,L(\tau))
-orbit of
e
is Zariski open in

{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
and hence intersects with
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau,2)_{ss}\neq\varnothing
. This yields
e\in{\mathcal{H}}(\Delta)
. ∎

2.2.

Given a connected reductive
{\Bbbk}
-group
H
and a nilpotent element
e\in\mathop{Lie}(H)
(i.e. an unstable vector of the
(\mathrm{Ad}\,H)
-module
\mathop{Lie}(H)
), we write
\hat{\Lambda}_{H}(e)
for the set of all
cocharacters in
X_{*}(H)
optimal for
e
in the sense of the Kempf–Rousseau theory. By [
[
Hes78
]
, Theorem 7.2], this set of cocharacters does not depend on the choice of an
H
-invariant

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
-valued norm mapping on
X_{*}(H)
.

Let
\tau=\tau_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
, and let
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
be such that
G_{e}\subset P(\tau)
. As
e
is a
G
-unstable vector of
{\mathfrak{g}}
it affords an optimal cocharacter
\tau^{\prime}\in X_{*}(G)

with the property that
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau^{\prime},\geq 2)
; see [
[
CP
]
] for detail. Since
\mathrm{Ad}\,\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})
preserves the line
{\Bbbk}e
and the cocharacter
\tau^{\prime}
is optimal for any nonzero scalar multiple of
e
, it follows from the main results of the Kempf-Rousseau theory that
\mathrm{Ad}\,\tau({\Bbbk})
normalises the optimal parabolic subgroup
P(\tau^{\prime})
of
e
; see [
[
P03
]
, Theorem 2.1(iv)], for example.
Since
the latter is self-normalising and contains
G_{e}
we have the inclusion
\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})G_{e}\subset P(\tau^{\prime})
.
Hence
N_{e}:=N_{G}({\Bbbk}e)=\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})G_{e}
is a subgroup of
P(\tau^{\prime})
.
It follows from [
[
Bor
]
, 11.14(2)] (applied to tori) that for any maximal torus
D
of
G_{e}
there is a maximal torus
\tilde{D}
of
N_{e}
such that
D\subseteq\tilde{D}\cap G_{e}
. Since

\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})
is contained in a maximal torus of
N_{e}
and
all maximal tori of
N_{e}
are conjugate, this shows that
G_{e}
contains a maximal torus which commutes with
\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})
; we call it
T_{0}
.

As
N_{e}\subset P(\tau^{\prime})
, there is a maximal torus
T^{\prime}
of
P(\tau^{\prime})
which contains the maximal torus
\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})T_{0}
of
N_{e}
. Since
L:=Z_{G}(T_{0})
is a Levi subgroup of
G
, there exists
g\in G
such that

gLg^{-1}
is a standard Levi subgroup
L
of
G
.
Replacing
e
and
\tau
by
(\mathrm{Ad}\,g)\,e
and
g\tau g^{-1}
we may assume without loss that
L
is a standard Levi subgroup of
G
.
By the main results of the Kempf–Rousseau theory (and the description of Hesselink strata in [
[
CP
]
]), there exists a unique cocharacter
\tau^{\prime\prime}\in\hat{\Lambda}_{G}(e)\cap X_{*}(T^{\prime})
conjugate to
\tau^{\prime}
under
P(\tau^{\prime})
and
such that
e=\sum_{i\geq 2}\,e_{i}
where
e_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau^{\prime\prime},i)
and
e_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(\tau,2)_{ss}
; see [
[
P03
]
, Theorem 2.1(iii)].

By construction,
T^{\prime}
is a maximal torus of
L
containing
\tau({\Bbbk}^{\times})
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}({\Bbbk}^{\times})
. Furthermore,
e\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathrm{Ad}\,T_{0}}=\,\mathop{Lie}(L)
. Let
L^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}L
, the derived subgroup of
L
, and
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}=\mathop{Lie}(L^{\prime})
. It is immediate from Jacobson’s formula for
[p]
-th powers in the restricted Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{l},[p])
that

.

2.3.

Let
d=\dim T_{0}
and
r=\ell-d
where
\ell={\rm rk}\,G
. In this subsection we begin to investigate the case where
d>0
, i.e. the case where
e
is not distinguished in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
The group
L^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}L
is semisimple and
T^{\prime}\cap L^{\prime}
contains a maximal torus of
L^{\prime}
; we call it
T_{1}
. The subtorus
T_{0}\cdot T_{1}
of
T^{\prime}
being self-centralising, it must be that

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose

is such that

and

. If the group

Proof.

As

is a maximal torus of

it must be that

. As
\nu^{-1}(t)\cdot\tau(t)\in T^{\prime}\cap G_{e}
for all

\nu-\tau\in X_{*}(T_{0})
. As

maps isomorphically onto a maximal torus of the semisimple group

. It follows that

We identify
T_{0}
with its image in
S_{e}
. As

\nu({\Bbbk}^{\times})\subset N_{e}
normalises

, it acts on

by rational automorphisms.

It is straightforward to see that the connected algebraic group

is reductive and
\nu({\Bbbk}^{\times})T_{0}
is a maximal torus of
\tilde{S}_{e}

. On the other hand, our assumption on
\nu

\tilde{S}_{e}

\tilde{S}_{e}
as well.

Since

\mathbb{Q}
-independent

2.12.

We would like to finish this paper by a brief discussion of the unipotent analogues of the Lusztig–Xue pieces,
{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)
, introduced by Lusztig in
[
[
L2
]
, 2.3].

Let
\mathfrak{U}(G)
denote the unipotent variety of
G
, the set of all
(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)
-unstable elements of
G
. The Hesselink stratification

\mathfrak{U}(G)\,=\,\textstyle{\bigsqcup}_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}}\,{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(\Delta)

is described in [
[
CP
]
] as follows. Let
\tau=\tau_{\Delta}
and write
P(\tau)=Z_{G}(\tau)\,U(\tau)
where
U(\tau)=R_{u}(P(\tau))
. Given
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
we denote by
U_{\geq k}(\tau)
the connected normal subgroup of
U(\tau)
generated by all

x_{\gamma}(t)
with
t\in{\Bbbk}
and all
\gamma\in\Sigma
such that

\langle\gamma,\tau\rangle\geq k
. It is well known that the factor-group
U_{\geq 2}(\tau)/U_{\geq 3}(\tau)
is endowed with a natural
vector space structure over
{\Bbbk}
and
\mathrm{Ad}\,Z_{G}(\tau)
acts
{\Bbbk}
-linearly on
U_{\geq 2}(\tau)/U_{\geq 3}(\tau)
. Furthermore,
U_{\geq 2}(\tau)/U_{\geq 3}(\tau)\cong{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
as
(\mathrm{Ad}\,Z_{G}(\tau))
-modules, and there is a module isomorphism
\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}\colon\,U_{\geq 2}(\tau)/U_{\geq 3}(\tau)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
sending a coset
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\beta_{i}}(t_{i})U_{3}(\tau)
with
\langle\beta_{i},\tau\rangle=2
to
\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}e_{\beta_{i}}
, where
e_{\beta_{i}}
are root vectors
independent of the choice of
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in{\Bbbk}^{r}
; see [
[
L2
]
, 2.2], [
[
LS
]
, Lemma 18.1] or [
[
CP
]
, 3.6]. Composing
\bar{\pi}_{\Delta}
with the canonical homomorphism
U_{\geq 2}(\tau)\to U_{\geq 2}(\tau)/U_{\geq 3}(\tau)
we obtain a natural surjection

\pi_{\Delta}\colon\,U_{\geq 2}(\tau)\twoheadrightarrow\,{\mathfrak{g}}(\tau,2)
.

It follows from [
[
CP
]
, Theorems 3.6 and 5.2] that

{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(\Delta)\,=\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,\big(\pi_{\Delta}^{-1}({\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss})\big)

for every
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
. In [
[
L2
]
, 2.3], the unipotent pieces
{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)

are defined in a similar fashion except that
{\mathcal{V}}(\tau_{\Delta},2)_{ss}
is replaced by an
a priory
larger set
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!}
. More precisely,

{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)\,=\,(\mathrm{Ad}\,G)\,\big(\pi_{\Delta}^{-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\Delta,!})\big).

In [
[
L2
]
, Theorem 2.4], Lusztig proved that

(5)

\mathfrak{U}(G)\,=\,\textstyle{\bigsqcup}_{\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}}\,{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)

when
G
is a simple algebraic group of type
{\rm A}
,
\rm B
,
\rm C
or
\rm D
, and he expected that (
5
) would continue to hold for all connected reductive groups.
Our next result shows that this expectation was correct.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field. Then

{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)={\mathcal{H}}_{u}(\Delta)
for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
and
(
5
) holds for
\mathfrak{U}(G)
.

Proof.

Theorem
1.1
in conjunction with
the preceding discussion shows that
{\rm LX}_{u}(\Delta)={\mathcal{H}}_{u}(\Delta)
for all
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
. Since the Hesselink strata
{\mathcal{H}}_{u}(\Delta)
with
\Delta\in\mathfrak{D}_{G}
form a partition of
\mathfrak{U}(G)
by [
[
CP
]
, Theorem 5.2], we deduce that (
5
) holds for
\mathfrak{U}(G)
.
∎

Double and Lagrangian extensions for quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras Thanks: SB was supported by the grant NYUAD-065.
Sofiane Bouarroudj, Yoshiaki Maeda

Abstract
. A Lie superalgebra is called quasi-Frobenius if it admits a closed anti-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form. We study the notion of double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra when the form is either orthosymplectic or periplectic. We show that every quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra that satisfies certain conditions can be obtained as a double extension of a smaller quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. We classify all 4-dimensional quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras, and show that such Lie superalgebras must be solvable. We study the notion of T ∗ T^{*} -extensions (or Lagrangian extensions) of Lie superalgebras, and show that they are classified by a certain cohomology space we introduce. Several examples are provided to illustrate our construction. Keywords. Quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, orthosymplectic and periplectic forms; double extension; T ∗ T^{*} -extension



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras

Following,
[
E
,
O
]
a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
quasi-Frobenius
Lie algebra if it admits a closed anti-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear from
\omega
. It is called a
Frobenius
Lie algebra if the bilinear
\omega
is exact. Namely, there exists
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
such that

(1)

\omega(x,y)=f([x,y])\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

In this case, the form
\omega
in Eq. (
1
) is sometimes referred to as a Kirillov form.

According to
[
O
]
, this terminology is due to Seligman. Those Lie algebras have been introduced to answer a question raised by Jacobson: If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is finite-dimensional, what conditions one can put on
{\mathfrak{g}}
in order the enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
admits an exact simple module? The answer to this question was settled in
[
O
]
.

On the other hand, sometimes in the literature, quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras were referred to as
symplectic
Lie algebras, see
[
MR2
,
BC
,
BaBe
]
. Perhaps this terminology stems from the fact that these Lie algebras are those of certain symplectic Lie groups over the field of the reals.

The superization of these notions is immediate, see §
2.3
. We have preferred to retain the term ‘quasi-Frobenius’ in the super setting, in order not to confuse them with the orthosymplectic
\mathfrak{osp}
and the periplectic Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{pe}
.

1.2.
Double extensions of Quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras

The notion of
symplectic double extension
of Lie algebras was introduced by Medina and Revoy
[
MR2
]
. Roughly speaking, one adds a symplectic plane to a quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra to obtain another quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra. This notion is the
anti-symmetric
analogue of the double extension of Lie algebras with symmetric non-degenerate bilinear forms introduced by Medina and Revoy in
[
MR1
]
(such Lie algebras were also called
quadratic
). The notion of symplectic double extension was introduced to classify nilpotent symplectic groups by their Lie algebras. Recall that a group admits an invariant symplectic structure if there exists a left-invariant closed 2-form whose maximal rank is equal to the dimension of the group. Medina and Revoy proved that the group of affine transformations admits an invariant and exact symplectic structure. Further, they proved that every nilpotent quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras can be obtained by a sequence of symplectic double extensions from the trivial Lie algebra. As a by-product, all
4
-dimensional quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras have been classified.

Regrettably, a coboundary condition needed to carry out symplectic double extensions was missed in
[
MR2
]
; this was rectified in
[
MD
]
. The converse, namely every quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be obtained as a symplectic double extension of a quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra, is also true under certain conditions. This double extension cannot be obtained when the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is zero – even in the case of solvable Lie algebras. Nevertheless, in the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is an abelian para-Kähler Lie algebra which is automatically solvable, Benayadi and Bajo in
[
BaBe
]
have generalized the notion of double extensions even if the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is trivial. Benayadi and Bajo also showed that such an algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be obtained by a sequence of generalized double extensions from the
affine
Lie algebra. In fact, the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable but not necessarily abelian para-Kähler has not yet been solved.

Double extensions of Quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

In this paper, we study the notion of

double extensions of Lie superalgebras, superizing the results of Medina, Revoy and Dardié

MR2

]

. This notion is the anti-symmetric analogue of the double extension of Lie superalgebras equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form introduced and studied in
[

,
ABBQ
,
BaBe
,
BeB

B
]
; see
[
BeBou
]
for the peculiar case where the ground field is of characteristic

. Our approach is similar to that of Benayadi and Bajo
[
BaBe
]
where the center might be trivial. The converse, namely every quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be obtained as a symplectic double extension of a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, is also true under certain conditions, see Theorems
4.1.4.  Theorem
,
4.2.4.  Theorem
,
5.1.2.  Theorem
,
5.2.2.  Theorem

We classify all 4-dimensional

quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras. Our result is based on the classification of 4-dimensional real Lie superalgebras carried out by Blackhouse in
[
Ba

. Our list is given in Tables

. Surprisingly enough, it turns out that some of the 4-dimensional Lie superalgebras can only admit closed non-degenerate

bilinear forms. This means that the purely even/odd part of the bilinear form turns into a degenerate form. As the classification of 4-dimensional real Lie superalgebras was carried out in
[

, we observe that every 4-dimensional real Lie superalgebra admitting a closed non-degenerate anti-symmetric form must be solvable, superizing the result of

We also study the filiform Lie superalgebras

1.4.
T^{*}
-extensions and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions (also known as Lagrangian extensions)

As far as we know, the notion of
T^{*}
-extension
of Lie algebras was introduced by Bordemann
[
Bor
]
.
Given a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
equipped with an
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
-valued 2-form
\alpha
. A
T^{*}
-extension of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
constructed by means of the form
\alpha
that satisfies a certain cohomological requirement. The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
naturally admits a non-degenerate bilinear form. However, in contrast to the double extension, the method
of
T^{*}
-extension applies not only to Lie algebras, but to arbitrary nonassociative algebras and is a one-step rather than a multi-step extension, see
[
Bor
]
.

Bordemann showed that every finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
with an invariant symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form “is” a suitable
T^{*}
-extension. If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a real finite-dimensional Lie algebra belonging to a Lie group
G
, then the
T^{*}
-extension of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the Lie algebra of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G
of
G
, considered as a Lie group. This differential geometric fact justifies the terminology and the notation.

The notion of
T^{*}
-extension was also studied by Baues and Cortés in
[
BC
]
in the context of Lie algebras admitting a flat connection, and called
Lagrangian extensions
since the Lie sub-algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is a Lagrangian ideal in
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. Baues and Cortés developed the notion of
Lagrangian cohomology
and showed that this cohomology captures all equivalence classes of
T^{*}
-extensions.

In this paper, we
superize
the notion of
T^{*}
-extensions (or Lagrangian extensions), generali-zing several results of
[
BC
]
. Following the same ideas of
[
BC
]
, the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
is equipped with a
flat connection
and a
2-form
that satisfies a certain cohomological requirement, see §
6.1
. It turns out that our construction remains valid over any field of characteristic
p\not=2
. In the super setting, we have observed that there are two ways to perform this extension: we consider either
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
or
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
, where
\Pi
is the change of parity functor, see §
2.2
. We call them
T^{*}
-extension and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
, respectively.

Conversely, we prove that every
strongly polarized
Lie superalgebra – see §
6
for the definition – is either a
T^{*}
-extension or
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of a flat Lie superalgebra, see Theorem
6.1.4.  Theorem
.

We study equivalence classes of Lagrangian extensions, and show that isomorphic
T^{*}
-extensions (or
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions) give rise to the same associated quotient flat Lie superalgebra.

We show that isomorphism classes of Lagrangian extensions of a flat Lie superalgebra are classified by a suitable two-dimensional cohomology, called the
Lagrangian extension cohomology
, superizing the construction of
[
BC
]
. We then show that the 2-cocycles give the same Lagrangian extensions if and and only if the 2-cocycles are cohomologous. We further establish a map between Lagrangian cohomology and ordinary cohomology.

Several examples of four-dimensional real Lie superalgebras that can be obtained as
T^{*}
-extensions or
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions are given in §
7
.

(2)

B_{ij}=(-1)^{p(B)p(v_{i})}{\mathcal{B}}(v_{i},v_{j})\text{~~for the basis vectors $v_{i}\in V$}

Consider the

u({\mathcal{B}})(w,v)=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}{\mathcal{B}}(v,w)\text{~~for any $v\in V$ and $w\in W$.}

In terms of the Gram matrix
B
of
{\mathcal{B}}
: the form

is

if and only if

(4)

u(B)=B,\;\text{ where $u(B)=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R^{t}&(-1)^{p(B)}T^{t}\\
(-1)^{p(B)}S^{t}&-U^{t}\end{pmatrix}$}$ for $B=\text{\small$\begin{pmatrix}R&S\\

Similarly,

of

{\mathcal{B}}
means that

.

The phrase “the bilinear form

is

” can be often encountered in the literature,

is a reflection of the braiding isomorphism

Denote by

the functor that assigns to

defined as the other copy of

with the opposite parity of its homogeneous components. Namely,

(\Pi(V))_{\bar{0}}=V_{\bar{1}},\hskip 10.00002pt(\Pi(V))_{\bar{1}}=V_{\bar{0}}.

The space
\Pi(V)
consists of the linear combinations of elements that we denote by
\Pi(f)
for every homogeneous

. For every superspace

, denote the canonical odd homomorphism induced by the functor

We will identify

Observe that the passage from

turns every symmetric

\text{${\mathcal{B}}^{\Pi}(\Pi(x),\Pi(y)):=(-1)^{p({\mathcal{B}})+p(x)+p(x)p(y)}{\mathcal{B}}(x,y)$ for any $x,y\in V$}.

Following the custom in differential geometry, we will denote any anti-symmetric bilinear form by

2.2.
Lie superalgebras (Basics)

A Lie superalgebra over a field of characteristic
p
, where
p\not=2,3
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
endowed with a bilinear operation
[-,-]
that satisfies anti-commutativity and the Jacobi identities amended by the Sign Rule. In characteristic
3
, one has to add one more condition

(6)

[f,[f,f]]=0\;\text{ for all $f\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$}.

Superalgebras for which the bracket
[-,-]
do not satisfy the condition (
6
) are called
pre-Lie superalgebras
, see
[
BBH
]
.

A derivation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a linear map on
{\mathfrak{g}}
that satisfies:

{\mathscr{D}}([f,g])=[{\mathscr{D}}(f),g]+(-1)^{p({\mathscr{D}})p(f)}[f,{\mathscr{D}}(g)])\text{ for all $f,g\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

Denote by
{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{g}})
the space of all derivations on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
\omega\in\mathop{\mathrm{Bil}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})
be an arbitrary non-degenerate bilinear form. To each derivation
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{g}})
we can assign a unique linear map
{\mathscr{D}}^{*}
, called the
adjoint
of
{\mathscr{D}}
, that satisfies the following condition:

\omega({\mathscr{D}}(f),g)=(-1)^{p(f)p({\mathscr{D}})}\omega(f,{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(g)).

From the very definition of
{\mathscr{D}}^{*}
, it is easy to see that
{\mathscr{D}}^{*}
is also a derivation.

2.3.
Quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

An even (resp. odd) non-degenerate bilinear form is called
orthosymplectic
(resp.
periplectic
).

Let
\omega\in\text{Bil}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}})
be an anti-symmetric bilinear form. The form
\omega
is called
closed
if it satisfies the following condition (for all
f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}
):

(7)

(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega(f,[g,h])+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega(h,[f,g])+(-1)^{p(g)p(f)}\omega(g,[h,f])=0.

This is to say,
\omega
is a non-degenerate 2-cocycle on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
{\mathbb{K}}
, see
[
F
]
. Sometimes, it is more convenient to write the cocycle condition (
7
) in the form

\omega(f,[g,h])-(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}\omega(g,[f,h])-\omega([f,g],h)=0.

A Lie superalgebra is called
quasi-Frobenius
if it is equipped with a closed anti-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\omega
. Such a Lie superalgebra will be denoted by
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
.

A quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is called
Frobenius
if the form
\omega
is exact. Namely, there exists
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
such that

\omega(x,y)=f([x,y])\text{ for all $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.

A quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is called
orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius
(resp.
periplectic quasi-Frobenius
) if the form
\omega
is even (resp. odd).

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra and let
S\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be a subspace. The orthogonal of
S
in
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is

S^{\perp}=\{v\in{\mathfrak{g}}\;|\;\omega(v,w)=0\text{ for all }w\in S\}.

The subspace
S
is called
non-degenerate
if
S\cap S^{\perp}=\{0\}
. It is called

isotropic
if
{S\subset S^{\perp}}
. A maximal isotropic subspace is called
Lagrangian
if it satisfies
S=S^{\perp}
.

Lemma

(The center of

Let

Let

(-1)^{p(x)p(g)}\omega(x,[f,g])=-(1)^{p(g)p(f)}\omega(g,[x,f])-(-1)^{p(f)p(x)}\omega(f,[g,x])=0+0=0.\qed

An

\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(\nabla_{u}v,a)=-(-1)^{p(\tilde{v})p(\tilde{u})}\kappa(\tilde{v},[\tilde{u},a]),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all $u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}$ and for all $a\in I$}.

Proof.

For Part (i), the representation
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{I}
is well-defined because
I
is centralized by
I^{\perp}
. Let us only show that the map (
9
) is indeed a representation. First, we compute

\displaystyle\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(u)\circ\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(v)(\Pi(\xi))=\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(u)(-(-1)^{p(v)(p(\xi)+1)}\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{v}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(p(u)+p(v))(p(\xi)+1)+p(u)p(v)}\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{v}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}.

Now,

\displaystyle\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(u)\circ\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(v)(\Pi(\xi))-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(u)\circ\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}(v)(\Pi(\xi))-\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}([u,v])(\Pi(\xi))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(p(u)+p(v))(p(\xi)+1)}\left((-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{v}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}-\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{v}}+\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{[\tilde{u},\tilde{v}]}\right)=0.

For Parts (ii) and (iii), let us first compute the kernel of
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
. It consists of all elements
u\in{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
\omega(\tilde{u},\cdot)=0
. This is equivalent to
\tilde{u}\in I^{\perp}
. Therefore,
u=0
. Now, since
\mathop{\mathrm{codim}}\nolimits(I^{\perp})=\dim(I)
, it follows that
\dim({\mathfrak{h}})=\dim(I^{*})
. Therefore,
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
is an isomorphism. The same argument can be applied to the periplectic form
\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Let us check the 1-cocycle condition:

\displaystyle u\cdot\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(v,\cdot)-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}v\cdot\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\cdot)-\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}([u,v],\cdot)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{p(u)(p(\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(v,\cdot))+1)}\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(v,\cdot)\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}+(-1)^{p(v)(p(\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\cdot))+1)+p(u)p(v)}\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(v,\cdot)\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}

\displaystyle-\Pi\circ\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}([\tilde{u},\tilde{v}],\cdot)=-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\Pi\circ(\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}(\tilde{v},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{u}}(\cdot))+\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}(\tilde{u},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{v}}(\cdot))-\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}([\tilde{u},\tilde{v}],\cdot))=0,

since
\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
is periplectic on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Part (iv) follows from the fact that
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}
are isomorphisms together with the fact that
\omega
and
\kappa
are closed forms.
∎

and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\not=\{0\}
.

Lemma

(Superdimension constraints)
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a finite-dimensional superspace such that
V_{\bar{1}}\not=\{0\}
, equipped with an anti-symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\omega
.

(
i
)
If
p(\omega)={\bar{0}}
, then
\dim(V_{\bar{0}})
is even.

(
ii
)
If
p(\omega)={\bar{1}}
, then
\dim(V_{\bar{0}})=\dim(V_{\bar{1}})
.

Proof.

(i). The result follows from the fact that
\omega|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}}
is non-degenerate, and from the well-known result in the non-super setting stating that such forms exist only on even dimensional spaces.

(ii). Let
0\not=x\in V_{\bar{1}}
. Since
\omega
is non-degenerate and odd, there exists
y\in V_{\bar{0}}
such that
\omega(x,y)=1
. Consider now the vector superspace
S=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{x,y\}
. We claim that
S^{\perp}\cap S=\{0\}
. Indeed, let
z\in S^{\perp}\cap S
and let us write
z=\lambda_{1}x+\lambda_{2}y
for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
. It follows that

0=\omega(x,z)=\omega(x,\lambda_{1}x+\lambda_{2}y)=\omega(x,\lambda_{2}y)=\lambda_{2}.

Similarly,
\lambda_{1}=0
. Let us now consider an arbitrary
w\in V
. We can write

u=(u+\omega(u,x)y-\omega(u,y)x)-(\omega(u,x)y-\omega(u,y))x\in S^{\perp}+S.

It follows that
V=S\oplus S^{\perp}
, and therefore
\mathop{\mathrm{sdim}}\nolimits(V)=\mathop{\mathrm{sdim}}\nolimits(S^{\perp})+(1|1)
. The form
\omega|_{S^{\perp}}
is also non-degenerate and the same argument can be applied to
S^{\perp}
. The result follows because
V
is finite-dimensional.
∎

3.6.
Filiform Lie superalgebras

Following
[
V
]
, a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called filiform if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent whose nil index is maximal, that is equal to
\dim({\mathfrak{g}})-1
. The simplest filiform Lie algebra,
L^{n}
, is defined by the only non-zero
bracket products:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[X_{1},X_{i}]&=&X_{i+1};\hskip 9.24994pt2\leq i\leq n-1.\end{array}

All other filiform Lie algebras can be obtained from it by deformations, see
[
V
]
.

The simplest filiform Lie superalgebra,
L^{n,m}
, is defined by the only non-zero
bracket products:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[X_{1},X_{i}]&=&X_{i+1};\hskip 9.24994pt2\leq i\leq n-1\\[5.69054pt]
[X_{1},Y_{j}]&=&Y_{j+1};\hskip 9.24994pt1\leq j\leq m-1\end{array}

where
p(X_{i})={\bar{0}}
, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, and
p(Y_{j})={\bar{1}}
, for
j=1,\ldots,m
. This filiform Lie superalgebra is in complete analogy with filiform Lie algebras, since all the other filiform Lie superalgebras can be obtained from it by deformations, see
[
BGKN
]
.

Proposition

(Orthosymplectic and periplectic forms on
L^{n,m}
)
.

(
i
)
For
n=m
, the Lie superalgebra
L^{n,m}
admits a closed anti-symmetric
periplectic
from given by
(
where
\lambda\mu\not=0
)

\omega=\lambda X_{1}^{*}\wedge Y_{n}^{*}+\mu\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}X_{i}^{*}\wedge Y_{n+1-i}^{*}.

(
ii
)
For
n
even and
m
odd, the Lie superalgebra
L^{n,m}
admits a closed anti-symmetric
ortho-symplectic
form given by
(
\lambda\mu\nu\not=0
)

\omega=\lambda X_{1}^{*}\wedge X_{n}^{*}+\mu\sum_{i=2}^{\frac{n}{2}}(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}-i}X_{i}^{*}\wedge X_{n+1-i}^{*}+(-1)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\nu\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}(-1)^{i}Y_{i}^{*}\wedge Y_{m+1-i}^{*}+\frac{\nu}{2}\,Y_{\frac{m+1}{2}}^{*}\wedge Y_{\frac{m+1}{2}}^{*}.

(
iii
)
For
n
even, the Lie superalgebra
L^{n,n-2}
admits a closed anti-symmetric non-degenerate
non-homogeneous
form given by
(
where
\lambda\mu\not=0
)

\omega=\lambda X_{1}^{*}\wedge X_{n}^{*}-\mu\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}X_{i}^{*}\wedge Y_{n-i}^{*}.

Proof.

We will only prove Part (i). The proof of Parts (ii) and (iii) are almost identical. Let
a=a_{\bar{0}}+a_{\bar{1}},b=b_{\bar{0}}+b_{\bar{1}},c=c_{\bar{0}}+c_{\bar{1}}\in L^{n,m}
, and let us express them as (where
\lambda_{i},\tilde{\lambda}_{i},\mu_{i},\tilde{\mu}_{i},\gamma_{i},\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
):

a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}X_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}Y_{i},\hskip 9.24994ptb=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}X_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\mu}_{i}Y_{i},\hskip 9.24994ptc=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}X_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\gamma}_{i}Y_{i},

A direct computation shows that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[a,b]&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(\lambda_{1}\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}\mu_{1})X_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{1}\tilde{\mu}_{i}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\mu_{1})Y_{i+1}.\end{array}

Obviously, the expressions
[b,c]
and
[c,a]
can be obtained from that of
[a,b]
by changing the roles of
a
and
b
. On the other hand, using the fact that
[L^{n,m}_{\bar{1}},L^{n,m}_{\bar{1}}]=\{0\}
and
\omega
is odd, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega(a,[b,c])&=&\omega(a_{\bar{0}},[b_{\bar{0}},c_{\bar{1}}])+\omega(a_{\bar{0}},[b_{\bar{1}},c_{\bar{0}}])+\omega(a_{\bar{1}},[b_{\bar{0}},c_{\bar{0}}])\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\displaystyle\lambda\lambda_{1}(\mu_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{n-1}-\tilde{\mu}_{n-1}\gamma_{1})+\mu\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-i}\lambda_{i}(\mu_{1}\tilde{\gamma}_{n-i}-\tilde{\mu}_{n-i}\gamma_{1})\\[5.69054pt]
&&\displaystyle-\mu\sum_{i=3}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\tilde{\lambda}_{n+1-i}(\mu_{1}\gamma_{i-1}-\mu_{i-1}\gamma_{1}).\end{array}

Now, it is a matter of direct computation to show that
(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega(a,[b,c])+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)=0
.

The matrix associated with the form
\omega
retains the shape

\mathrm{antidiag}(\lambda,(-1)^{n}\mu,(-1)^{n-1}\mu,\ldots,\mu,\ldots,-(-1)^{n-1}\mu,-(-1)^{n}\mu,-\lambda).

This matrix is invertible if and only if
\lambda\mu\not=0.

∎

4.
Orthosymplectic double extensions

4.1.
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{0}}
-extensions

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}_{\bar{0}}({\mathfrak{a}})
be a derivation such that the following 2-cocycle

(10)

(a,b)\mapsto\Omega(a,b):=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+2{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(a)+\lambda({\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{D}}^{*})(a),b)

is in
B^{2}({\mathfrak{g}};{\mathbb{K}})
(i.e., a 2-coboundary); namely, there exists
\chi\in C^{1}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathbb{K}})
such that
\Omega(a,b)=\chi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Since
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, there exists
Z_{\Omega}\in{\mathfrak{a}}
such that

(11)

\Omega(a,b)=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all $a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}.

Theorem

(
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{0}}
-extension – the case where
\omega
is orthosymplectic)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}_{\bar{0}}({\mathfrak{a}})
be a derivation such that condition
(
10
)
is satisfied. Then, there exists a Lie superalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*}
, where
\mathscr{K}:=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{x\}
for
x
even, defined as follows
(
for any
a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}
)
:

\begin{array}[]{l}[x,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\lambda x,\;[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a)+{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(a),b)x,\;[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathscr{D}}(a)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},a)x,\end{array}

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
Z_{\Omega}\in{\mathfrak{a}}
is as in Eq. (
11
). There exists a closed anti-symmetric orthosymplectic form
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}|_{{\mathfrak{a}}\times{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}},\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathfrak{a}},\mathscr{K}):=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathfrak{a}},\mathscr{K}^{*}):=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x):=1,\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,x):=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x^{*}):=0.

The orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
will be called the
double extension
by means of a 1-dimensional space of the orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
. In the particular case where
x
is central in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, namely
\lambda=0
, then the double extension is called
classical
(see
[
BaBe
]
).

Proof.

For simplicity and clear presentation, let us introduce the following notations

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\phi(a,b)&:=&\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}\\[5.69054pt]
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,g,h)&:=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}[f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.\end{array}

To check the Jacobi identity, we proceed as follows.

The identity
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,f,g)=0
is certainly satisfied since
x
is central in
{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathscr{K}}
and
x^{*}\not\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

The identity
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},f,g)=0
is also satisfied for the following reasons. If either
f
or
g
is
x
, then we are done. Now if
f=x^{*}
(or the other way round,
g=x^{*}
), then

J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x^{*},g)=[x^{*},[x^{*},g]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x^{*},[x^{*},g]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0.

Let us assume now that
f,g\in{\mathfrak{a}}.
We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},f,g)&=&[x^{*},\phi(a,b)x+[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}[g,{\mathscr{D}}(f)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},f)x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&&-[f,{\mathscr{D}}(g)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},g),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&-\phi(a,b)\lambda x+{\mathscr{D}}([f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})x\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}\left([g,{\mathscr{D}}(f)]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))x\right)-[f,{\mathscr{D}}(g)]_{\mathfrak{a}}-\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))x=0\end{array}

since
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{a}})
and the thanks to Lemma
4.1.2.  Lemma
.

Lemma

(Technical)
.

For all
f,g\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})+(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))-\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))-\lambda\phi(a,b)=0.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))+\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))+\lambda\phi(a,b)

\displaystyle=\phi({\mathscr{D}}(f),g)+\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))+\lambda\phi(a,b)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(f)),g)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),{\mathscr{D}}(g))+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),{\mathscr{D}}(g))+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,{\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(b)))

\displaystyle+\lambda(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}+2{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*}+\lambda{\mathscr{D}}+\lambda{\mathscr{D}}^{*})(f),g)=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}}).\qed

From now on we assume that
f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
. We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,g,h)&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}[f,\phi(g,h)x+[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}([f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})x)+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&0,\end{array}

because the JI holds on
{\mathfrak{a}}
and thanks to Lemma
4.1.3.  Lemma
.

Lemma

(Technical)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}
be in
{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Then,

(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\phi(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)=0\text{ for any $f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is orthosymplectic and
{\mathscr{D}}
is a derivation. Indeed,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\phi(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\ \circlearrowleft(f,g,h)&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,{\mathscr{D}}([g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})))\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(h),[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,{\mathscr{D}}([f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})))\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(-1)^{p(g)p(f)}(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(g),[h,f]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(g,{\mathscr{D}}([h,f]_{\mathfrak{a}}))).\end{array}

We will collect terms involving
{\mathscr{D}}(f)
and shows that they vanish. The same applies to terms involving
{\mathscr{D}}(g)
and
{\mathscr{D}}(h)
. Indeed,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,[{\mathscr{D}}(f),g]_{\mathfrak{a}})+(-1)^{p(g)p(f)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(g,[h,{\mathscr{D}}(f)]_{\mathfrak{a}})=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

since
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is closed. ∎

Let us now show that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is closed on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We should check that

(12)

(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $f,g$ and $h$ $\in{\mathfrak{g}}$.}

If
f=x^{*}
and
g=x
, but
h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,[x^{*},x]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[h,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,-\lambda x)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,-{\mathscr{D}}(h)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},h)x)=0.\end{array}

If
f=g=x^{*}
, but
h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x^{*},h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,[x^{*},x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[h,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x^{*},h]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x^{*},h]_{\mathfrak{g}})=0\end{array}

If
f=x^{*}
, but
g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,[x^{*},g]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,[h,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(g,h)x)+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,{\mathscr{D}}(g)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z_{\Omega},g)x)-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,{\mathscr{D}}(h)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(Z_{\Omega},h)x)

\displaystyle=\phi(g,h)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(g),h)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(g,{\mathscr{D}}(h))=0.

If
f=x
but
g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, Eq. (
12
) is trivially satisfied because of the very definition of
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

If
f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, then we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(f,g)x)+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)=0,\end{array}

since
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is closed on
{\mathfrak{a}}
.∎

Theorem

(Converse of Theorem
4.1.1.  Theorem
)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Suppose there exists an even non-zero
x\in([{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})^{\perp}
such that
\mathscr{K}:=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{x\}
is an ideal. Then,
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
is obtained as an
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{0}}
-extension by a 1-dimensional space from an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Moreover, if
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\bar{0}}({\mathfrak{g}})\not=0
, then we can choose
x\in{\mathfrak{z}}_{\bar{0}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, so the double extension is classical.

Proof.

The space
\mathscr{K}^{\perp}
is an ideal in
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Indeed, let us show first that
(\mathscr{K}^{\perp})_{\bar{1}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
. This is true because
x
is orthogonal to any odd element since
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is even. Let
f\in\mathscr{K}^{\perp}
and
g\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, then
[f,g]_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathscr{K}^{\perp}
because
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[f,g]_{\mathfrak{g}})=0
since
x\in[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\perp}
.

Since
\mathscr{K}
is 1-dimensional and
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,x)=0
, it follows that
\mathscr{K}\subset\mathscr{K}^{\perp}
and
\dim(\mathscr{K}^{\perp})=\dim({\mathfrak{g}})-1
. Therefore, there exists
x^{*}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
(since
(\mathscr{K}^{\perp})_{\bar{1}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
) such that

{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathscr{K}^{\perp}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ where $\mathscr{K}^{*}:=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{x^{*}\}$}.

We can normalize
x^{*}
so that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x)=1
.

Let us define
{\mathfrak{a}}:=(\mathscr{K}+\mathscr{K}^{*})^{\perp}
. We then have a decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*}
.

Let us define an orthosymplectic form on
{\mathfrak{a}}
by setting:

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}={\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}|_{{\mathfrak{a}}\times{\mathfrak{a}}}.

The form
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate on
{\mathfrak{a}}
. Indeed, suppose there exists an
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
such that

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,f)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for any $f\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}.

But
a
is also orthogonal to
x
as well as to
x^{*}
. It follows that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,f)=0
for any
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence,
a=0
, since
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is nondegenerate on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let us now show that the vector space
{\mathfrak{a}}
can be endowed with a Lie superalgebra structure, and there exists an orthosymplectic structure on
{\mathfrak{a}}
for which
{\mathfrak{g}}
is its double extension. Let
a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}
. The bracket
[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}
belongs to
\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
because
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathscr{K}^{\perp}=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
and the latter is an ideal. It follows that

[x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\lambda(a)x,\hskip 9.24994pt[x,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\lambda\,x,\hskip 9.24994pt[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\psi(a,b)+\phi(a,b)x,\hskip 9.24994pt[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathscr{D}}(a)+\chi(a)x.

where
\psi(a,b),{\mathscr{D}}(a)\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, and
\lambda,\lambda(a),\phi(a,b),\chi(a)\in\mathbb{K}
.
Let us show that

\phi(a,b)=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)).

Indeed, since

\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}})=-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(b,[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[b,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

it follows that

(13)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},\phi(a,b)x+\psi(a,b))&=&-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(b,\chi(a)x+{\mathscr{D}}(a))\\[5.69054pt]
&&+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,\chi(b)x+{\mathscr{D}}(b)).\end{array}

Since
\psi(a,b)
is orthogonal to
x^{*}
, and
a
is orthogonal to
x
, and
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x)=1
, it follows that the LHS of Eq. (
13
) gives
\phi(a,b)
while the RHS gives

-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(b,{\mathscr{D}}(a))+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)).

Let us show that
\lambda(a)=0
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
. Indeed, since

\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}})=-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[x^{*},x]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[a,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

The LHS reads

\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},\lambda(a)x)=\lambda(a),

while the RHS reads

-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[x^{*},x]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,[a,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})=-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,\lambda x)+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,{\mathscr{D}}(a)+\chi(a)x)=0+0=0.

The map
\psi
is a bilinear on
{\mathfrak{a}}
because
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
is bilinear, so for convenience let us re-denote
\psi
by
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}}
. We still have to show that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}}
satisfies the Jacobi identity.

For any
a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, the Jacobi identity on
{\mathfrak{g}}
reads

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[x^{*},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}&=&-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}[b,[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[a,[b,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}},b]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[a,[x^{*},b]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.\end{array}

The LHS reads

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[x^{*},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}&=&[x^{*},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(a,b)x]_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathscr{D}}([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\chi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})x-\phi(a,b)\lambda x.\end{array}

The RHS reads

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}},b]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[a,[x^{*},b]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}&=&[{\mathscr{D}}(a)+\chi(a)x,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)+\chi(b)x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&[{\mathscr{D}}(a),b]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&[{\mathscr{D}}(a),b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)x+[a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))x.\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

By comparaison, it follows that
{\mathscr{D}}([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[{\mathscr{D}}(a),b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+[a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)]_{\mathfrak{a}}
; this would imply that
{\mathscr{D}}
is a derivation once we show that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a Lie bracket on
{\mathfrak{a}}
. Moreover,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\chi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})&=&\phi({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+\phi(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))+\lambda\phi(a,b)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(a)),b)+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),{\mathscr{D}}(b))+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),{\mathscr{D}}(b))+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(b)))\\[5.69054pt]
&&-\lambda\phi(a,b)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+2{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(a)+\lambda({\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{D}}^{*})(a),b).\end{array}

Therefore, the map

(a,b)\mapsto\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+2{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)+{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(a)+\lambda({\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{D}}^{*})(a),b)

is a 2-coboundary (again, this will be justified when we show that
({\mathfrak{a}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Lie superalgebra).

For any
a,b
and
c
in
{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}[a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}[a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(b,c)x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}[a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{a}}+(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\phi(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})x+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\end{array}

This implies that
[.,.]_{\mathfrak{a}}
satisfies the Jacobi identity and

(14)

(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\phi(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})+(-1)^{p(c)p(b)}\phi(c,[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+(-1)^{p(b)p(a)}\phi(b,[c,a]_{\mathfrak{a}})=0.

Before checking Eq. (
14
) we will prove first that
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}:={\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}|_{{\mathfrak{a}}\times{\mathfrak{a}}}
is closed. Indeed, for any
a,b,c\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\phi(a,b)x)+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,[b,c]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(a,b,c)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&0,\end{array}

since
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is closed. Now, Let us rewrite Eq. (
14
); explicitly:

\begin{array}[]{l}(-1)^{p(a)p(c)}\left(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),[b,c]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,[{\mathscr{D}}(b),c]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,[b,{\mathscr{D}}(c)]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)\\[5.69054pt]
+(-1)^{p(c)p(b)}\left(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(c),[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(c,[{\mathscr{D}}(a),b]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(c,[a,{\mathscr{D}}(b)]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)\\[5.69054pt]
+(-1)^{p(b)p(a)}\left(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(b),[c,a]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(b,[{\mathscr{D}}(c),a]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(b,[c,{\mathscr{D}}(a)]_{\mathfrak{a}})\right)=0.\end{array}

We see that all these terms will cancel because
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is closed. The proof now is complete.
∎

4.2.
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{1}}
-extensions

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}_{\bar{1}}({\mathfrak{a}})
be a derivation such that

(15)

(a,b)\mapsto\Omega(a,b):=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}(a)-{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(a),b)

is in
B^{2}({\mathfrak{g}};{\mathbb{K}})
(i.e., a 2-coboundary). Let us write
\Omega=\delta(\chi)
for some
\chi\in C^{1}({\mathfrak{a}};{\mathbb{K}})
; namely,
\Omega(a,b)=\chi([a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Since
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate, there exists
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}
such that
\Omega(a,b)=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a_{0},[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}})
.

Theorem

(
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{1}}
-extension – the case where
\omega
is orthosymplectic)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
be an ortho-symplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}_{\bar{1}}({\mathfrak{a}})
be a derivation such that condition (
15
) is satisfied together with

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathscr{D}}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{a_{0}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ and }\hskip 9.24994pt{\mathscr{D}}(a_{0})=0.\end{array}

Then, there exists a Lie superalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*}
, where
\mathscr{K}:=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{x\}
for
x
odd, defined as follows:
(
for any
a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}[\mathscr{K},{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,&[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=[a,b]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\left(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+(-1)^{p(a)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))\right)x,\\[5.69054pt]
[x^{*},x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=2a_{0},&[x^{*},a]_{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathscr{D}}(a)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,a_{0})x.\end{array}

There exists a closed anti-symmetric orthosymplectic form
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}|_{{\mathfrak{a}}\times{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}},\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathfrak{a}},\mathscr{K}):=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathfrak{a}},\mathscr{K}^{*}):=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x):=1,\hskip 9.24994pt\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,x):=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x^{*}):=0.

Proof.

As we did in proving Theorem
4.1.1.  Theorem
, let us introduce the following notations:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\phi(a,b)&:=&\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(a),b)+(-1)^{p(a)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,{\mathscr{D}}(b))\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}\\[5.69054pt]
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,g,h)&:=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}[f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{g}}$}.\end{array}

To check the Jacobi identity, we proceed as follows.

If
h=x
, the identity
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,f,g)=0
is certainly satisfied since
x
is central in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

If
h=x^{*}
, the identity
J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},f,g)=0
is also satisfied for the following reasons. If either
f
or
g
is
x
, then we are done since
x
is central in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Now if
f=x^{*}
(or the other way round,
g=x^{*}
), then

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},x^{*},g)&=&(-1)^{p(g)}[x^{*},[x^{*},g]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{p(g)}[g,[x^{*},x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x^{*},[g,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(g)}[x^{*},{\mathscr{D}}(g)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,a_{0})x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{p(g)}[g,2a_{0}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(-1)^{p(g)}[x^{*},{\mathscr{D}}(g)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,a_{0})x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(g)}{\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(g))-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(g),a_{0})x+(-1)^{p(g)}([g,2a_{0}]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(g,2a_{0})x)\\[5.69054pt]
&&+(-1)^{p(g)}\left({\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(g))-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(g),a_{0})x\right)=0\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

since
{\mathscr{D}}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{a_{0}}
and
{\mathscr{D}}(a_{0})=0
.

Let us assume now that
f,g\in{\mathfrak{a}}.
We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},f,g)&=&(-1)^{p(g)}[x^{*},\phi(f,g)x+[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}[g,{\mathscr{D}}(f)-\omega(f,a_{0})x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&&-(-1)^{p(f)+p(g)}[f,{\mathscr{D}}(g)-\omega(g,a_{0}),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(g)}({\mathscr{D}}([f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}})-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}([f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}},a_{0})x+(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}\left([g,{\mathscr{D}}(f)]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))x\right)\\[5.69054pt]
&&-(-1)^{p(f)+p(g)}([f,{\mathscr{D}}(g)]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))x)=0\end{array}

since
{\mathscr{D}}\in{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{a}})
and thanks to Lemma
4.2.2.  Lemma
.

Lemma

(Technical)
.

For all
f,g\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}([f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}},a_{0})-(-1)^{(p(f)+1)p(g)}\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))+(-1)^{p(f)}\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))=0.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(p(f)+1)p(g)}\phi(g,{\mathscr{D}}(f))+(-1)^{p(f)}\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))=\phi({\mathscr{D}}(f),g)+(-1)^{p(f)}\phi(f,{\mathscr{D}}(g))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(f)),g)+(-1)^{p(f)+1}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),{\mathscr{D}}(g))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p(f)}(\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(f),{\mathscr{D}}(g))+(-1)^{p(f)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,{\mathscr{D}}({\mathscr{D}}(g))))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(({\mathscr{D}}\circ{\mathscr{D}}-{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\circ{\mathscr{D}}^{*})(f),g)=\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a_{0},[f,g]_{\mathfrak{a}}).\qed

From now on we assume that
f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
. We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}J_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,g,h)&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}[f,\phi(g,h)x+[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}([f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})x)+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h),\\[5.69054pt]
&=&0,\end{array}

because the JI holds on
{\mathfrak{a}}
and thanks to Lemma
4.2.3.  Lemma
.

Lemma

(Technical)
.

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},\omega_{\mathfrak{a}})
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Let
{\mathscr{D}}
be in
{\mathfrak{der}}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Then

(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\phi(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)=0\text{ for any $f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}.

Proof.

Similar to that of Lemma
4.1.3.  Lemma
taking into consideration that
{\mathscr{D}}
is an odd derivation.
∎

Let us now show that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is closed. We should check that

(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for any $f,g$ and $h$ $\in{\mathfrak{g}}$.}

This is true if
f=x
because
x
is central and by the very definition of
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
f=x^{*}
, but
g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,[x^{*},g]_{\mathfrak{g}})+(-1)^{p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,[h,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[g,h]_{\mathfrak{a}}+\phi(g,h)x)+(-1)^{p(h)p(g)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(h,{\mathscr{D}}(g)-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,a_{0})x)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(g)+p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(g,{\mathscr{D}}(h)-\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,a_{0})x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(h)}\phi(g,h)-(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}({\mathscr{D}}(g),h)-(-1)^{p(g)+p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(g,{\mathscr{D}}(h))=0.

If
f=g=x^{*}
, but
h\in{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(-1)^{p(f)p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(f,[g,h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\circlearrowleft(f,g,h)&=&(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[x^{*},h]_{\mathfrak{g}})+(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,[x^{*},x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\[5.69054pt]
&&-\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x^{*},[h,x^{*}]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\[5.69054pt]
&=&-2(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,a_{0})+(-1)^{p(h)}\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(h,2a_{0})=0\end{array}

If
f,g,h\in{\mathfrak{a}}

e\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
(since
(\mathscr{K}^{\perp})_{\bar{0}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
) such that

{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathscr{K}^{\perp}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ where $\mathscr{K}^{*}:=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\{e\}$}.

We can normalize
e
so that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(e,x)=1
. Besides,
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,x)=0
since
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{K}\cap\mathscr{K}^{\perp}
.

Let us define
{\mathfrak{a}}:=(\mathscr{K}+\mathscr{K}^{*})^{\perp}
. We then have a decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\mathscr{K}^{*}
.

Let us define a periplectic form on
{\mathfrak{a}}
by setting:

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}={\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}}|_{{\mathfrak{a}}\times{\mathfrak{a}}}.

The form
\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate on
{\mathfrak{a}}
. Indeed, suppose there exists an
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}
such that

\omega_{\mathfrak{a}}(a,f)=0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for any $f\in{\mathfrak{a}}$}.

But
a
is also orthogonal to
x
as well as to
e
. It follows that
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(a,f)=0
for any
f\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence,
a=0
, since
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is nondegenerate on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let us now show that vector space
{\mathfrak{a}}
can be endowed with a Lie superalgebra structure, and there exists a periplectic structure on
{\mathfrak{a}}
for which
{\mathfrak{g}}
is its double extension. Let
a,b\in{\mathfrak{a}}
. The bracket
[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}
belongs to
\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
because
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathscr{K}^{\perp}=\mathscr{K}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
and the latter is an ideal. It follows that

[a,b]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\psi(a,b)+\phi(a,b)x,\hskip 9.24994pt[e,a]_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathscr{D}}(a)+\chi(a)x,\hskip 9.24994pt[e,e]_{\mathfrak{g}}=2a_{0}+\nu x.

where
\psi(a,b),{\mathscr{D}}(a)\in{\mathfrak{a}},a_{0}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}
, and
\phi(a,b),\chi(a)\in\mathbb{K}
. The proof follows as in Theorem
4.2.1.  Theorem
.∎

6.
The
T^{*}
-extensions and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions (Lagrangian extensions)

Following
[
BC
]
, a
polarization
for a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a choice of a Lagrangian subalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}
of
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
. This terminology was motivated by the fact that Lagrangian foliations in a manifold with a symplectic form is a polarization of the manifold, see
[
BC
]
. A
strong polarization
of a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a pair
({\mathfrak{a}},N)
consisting of a Lagrangian
ideal

{\mathfrak{a}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
and a complementary Lagrangian
subspace

N\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
. The quadruple
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)
is referred to as a
strongly polarized
quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra.

An isomorphism of strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime})
is an even isomorphism of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
wich maps the strong polarization
({\mathfrak{a}},N)
to the strong polarization
({\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime})
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a flat torsion-free connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We call
(\mathfrak{h},\nabla)
a flat Lie superalgebra.

We will introduce the notion of
T^{*}
- and
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of Lie superalgebras. To our best knowledge, the notion of
T^{*}
-extension for Lie algebras was introduced by Bordemann in
[
Bor
]
. This notion was also studied in
[
BC
]
in the context of quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras and called a
Lagrangian extension
.

6.1.
The construction of
T^{*}
- and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions (Lagrangian extensions)

Recall that since the connection
\nabla
is flat, the map

{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})\hskip 9.24994ptu\mapsto\nabla_{u},

defines a representation. We define a map
\rho:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
, the dual representation, defined as follows:

(22)

\rho:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})\hskip 9.24994ptu\mapsto\rho(u),\text{ where }\rho(u)\cdot\xi=-(-1)^{p(u)p(\xi)}\xi\circ\nabla_{u}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all $u\in{\mathfrak{h}}$ and $\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}$}.

Similarly, we have a representation

(23)

\chi:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))\hskip 9.24994ptu\mapsto\chi(u):=(-1)^{p(u)}\,\Pi\circ\rho(u)\circ\Pi.

Lemma

(Dual representation)
.

The maps (
22
) and (
23
) are indeed representations.

Proof.

First, let us compute

\rho(u)\circ\rho(v)(\xi)=\rho(u)(-(-1)^{p(\xi)p(v)}\xi\circ\nabla_{v})=(-1)^{p(\xi)p(v)+p(u)(p(v)+p(\xi))}\xi\circ\nabla_{v}\circ\nabla_{u}

Now, using the fact that the connection is flat we get (for all
u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
):

\displaystyle\rho(u)\circ\rho(v)(\xi)-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\rho(v)\circ\rho(u)(\xi)-\rho([u,v])(\xi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(\xi)p(v)+p(u)(p(v)+p(\xi))}\xi\circ\nabla_{v}\circ\nabla_{u}-(-1)^{p(\xi)p(u)+p(v)p(\xi)}\xi\circ\nabla_{u}\circ\nabla_{v}-(-1)^{p(\xi)(p(u)+p(v))}\xi\circ\nabla_{[u,v]}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(\xi)(p(u)+p(v))}\xi\circ\left(\nabla_{u}\circ\nabla_{v}-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\nabla_{v}\circ\nabla_{u}-[u,v]\right)=0.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\chi(u)\circ\chi(v)(\Pi(\xi))-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\chi(v)\circ\chi(u)(\Pi(\xi))-\chi([u,v])(\Pi(\xi))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(u)+p(v)}\Pi\circ(\rho(u)\circ\rho(v)(\xi)-(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\rho(v)\circ\rho(u)(\xi)-\rho([u,v])(\xi))=0.\qed

By means of a 2-cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
(resp.
\beta\in Z^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))
we will construct a Lie superalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
), called
T^{*}
-extension (resp.
\Pi T^{*}
-extension). Recall that the space
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and
\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
are
{\mathfrak{h}}
-modules by means of the representations (
22
) and (
23
).

On the space
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
:
The brackets are defined as follows: (for all
u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
)

\begin{array}[]{l}[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\alpha(u,v),\hskip 9.24994pt[u,\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\rho(u)\cdot\xi.\end{array}

We define an
even
form
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
as follows: (for all
u+\xi,v+\zeta\in{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
)

(24)

\omega(u+\xi,v+\zeta)=\xi(v)-(-1)^{p(\zeta)p(u)}\zeta(u).

This construction will be referred to as the
T^{*}
-extension
, and denoted by
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\alpha,\nabla,\omega)
.

On the space
{\mathfrak{k}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
:
The brackets are defined as follows: (for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{a}}
)

\begin{array}[]{l}[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\beta(u,v),\hskip 9.24994pt[u,\Pi(\xi)]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\chi(u)\cdot\Pi(\xi).\end{array}

We define an
odd
form
\kappa
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
as follows: (for all
u+\Pi(\xi),v+\Pi(\zeta)\in{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
)

(25)

\kappa(u+\Pi(\xi),v+\Pi(\zeta))=\xi(v)-(-1)^{(p(\zeta)+1)p(u)}\zeta(u).

This construction will be referred to as the
\Pi T^{*}
-extension
, and denoted by
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),\beta,\nabla,\kappa)
.

Proposition

(Conditions on
\alpha
and
\beta
)
.

(
i
)
The form
\omega
is closed on
{\mathfrak{g}}
if and only if

(26)

(-1)^{p(u)p(w)}\alpha(u,v)(w)+\circlearrowleft(u,v,w)=0\text{ for all $u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{h}}$}.

(
ii
)
The form
\kappa
is closed on
{\mathfrak{g}}
if and only if

(27)

(-1)^{p(u)p(w)}\Pi\circ\beta(u,v)(w)+\circlearrowleft(u,v,w)=0\text{ for all $u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{h}}$}.

Proof.

Let us prove Part (i). It is enough to check the cocycle condition of the form
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
pertaining to the triple
(u,v+\zeta,w)
. First, we compute

\begin{array}[]{l}\omega([u,v+\zeta]_{\mathfrak{g}},w)=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\alpha(u,v)+\rho(u)\circ\zeta,w)=\alpha(u,v)(w)+\rho(u)\circ\zeta(w)\\[5.69054pt]
=\alpha(u,v)(w)-(-1)^{p(u)p(\zeta)}\zeta\circ\nabla_{u}(w).\end{array}

Similarly,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\omega([w,u]_{\mathfrak{g}},v+\zeta)&=&\alpha(w,u)(v)-(-1)^{p(\zeta)(p(w)+p(u))}\zeta([w,u]_{\mathfrak{h}})\\[5.69054pt]
\omega([v+\zeta,w]_{\mathfrak{g}},u)&=&\alpha(v,w)(u)+\zeta\circ\nabla_{w}(u).\end{array}

Because
\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}
is bilinear, we may assume that
p(v)=p(\zeta)
.

In order to check the condition

(-1)^{p(u)p(w)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}([u,v+\zeta]_{\mathfrak{g}},w)+\circlearrowleft(u,v+\zeta,w)=0,

we see that the part in
{\mathfrak{h}}
is equivalent to Eq. (
26
). The part in
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
contains the terms involving
\zeta
. Let us collect them. Using the fact that the connection is flat, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}(-1)^{p(u)p(w)}\left(-(-1)^{p(u)p(\zeta)}\zeta\circ\nabla_{u}(w)\right)+(-1)^{p(w)p(v)}\left(-(-1)^{p(\zeta)(p(w)+p(u))}\zeta([w,u]_{\mathfrak{h}})\right)\\[5.69054pt]
+(-1)^{p(v)p(u)}\left(\zeta\circ\nabla_{w}(u)\right)=(-1)^{p(u)p(v)}\zeta\circ\left(\nabla_{w}(u)-(-1)^{p(u)p(w)}\nabla_{u}(w)-[w,u]_{\mathfrak{h}}\right)=0.\end{array}

The proof of Part (ii) is similar.
∎

\displaystyle(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus i_{\omega})^{*}\omega_{{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}}(x,y)=\omega_{{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}}(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{N})+i_{\omega}(x_{a}),\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{N})+i_{\omega}(y_{a}))=

\displaystyle i_{\omega}(x_{a})(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{N}))-(-1)^{Pty}i_{\omega}(y_{a})(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{N}))=\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{a},\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{N}))-(-1)^{Pty}\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{a},\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{N}))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{a},y_{N})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{N},y_{a})=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{a},y_{a}+y_{N})+\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{N},y_{N}+y_{a})=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,y).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle[\Phi(x),\Phi(y)]=[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),i_{\omega}(y,\cdot)]=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}i_{\omega}(y,\cdot)\circ\nabla_{\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}(y,\nabla_{\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}([y,\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)],(\cdot))=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}([y,x],(\cdot))=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,y],(\cdot)).

Let us consider now
x\in N
and
y\in N

.

The case where the form is periplectic.

\displaystyle=i_{\kappa}(x_{a})(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{N}))-(-1)^{Pty}i_{\kappa}(y_{a})(\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{N}))=\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{a},\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{N}))-(-1)^{Pty}\kappa_{\mathfrak{h}}(y_{a},\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x_{N}))

Lemma

(

We will denote the form by

\displaystyle 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{(if the form $\omega$ is orthosymplectic)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\hskip 9.24994pt\text{(if the form $\kappa$ is periplectic).}

.

Now suppose
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
is an isomorphism of
{\mathfrak{h}}
(see, diagram (
39
)). Choose a complementary Lagrangian subspace
N
for
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})
. Then,
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime}=\Phi(N))
is an isomorphism of strongly polarized orthosymplectic (resp. periplectic) Lie superalgebras. By Lemma
6.2.2.  Lemma
, we have
\alpha_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime}}=(\Phi_{\mathfrak{h}})_{*}\alpha_{{\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N}
(resp.
\beta_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime}}=(\Phi_{\mathfrak{h}})_{*}\beta_{{\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N}
), since by assumption
\Phi|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{h}}
. This shows that isomorphic extensions over
{\mathfrak{h}}
have the same cohomology class.

It remains to show that two extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
with the same class are isomorphic. By Theorem
6.1.3.  Theorem
, it is enough to show that any two strongly polarized orthosymplectic (resp. periplectic) Lie superalgebras give rise to isomorphic extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
if the corresponding classes
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
if
p(\omega)={\bar{0}}
(resp.
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
if
p(\omega)={\bar{1}}
) satisfy
[\alpha]=[\alpha^{\prime}]\in H^{2}_{{\bar{0}},L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
(resp.
[\beta]=[\beta^{\prime}]\in H^{2}_{{\bar{0}},L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))
). That is
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\partial_{\rho}\sigma
(resp.
\beta^{\prime}=\beta-\partial_{\chi}\mu
). The map
(u,\xi)\mapsto(u,\xi+\sigma(u))
(resp.
(u,\xi)\mapsto(u,\xi+\mu(u))
) is an ismorphism.
∎

Remark

(A link between ordinary cohomology and Lagrangian cohomology)
.

The kernel of the map
H^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\rho}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
(resp.
H^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))\rightarrow H^{2}_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))
) is given by

\kappa_{L}=\frac{B^{2}_{\rho}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\cap Z^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})}{B^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})},\left(\text{resp. }\kappa_{L}=\frac{B^{2}_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))\cap Z^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))}{B^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))}\right),

where

B^{2}_{\rho}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})=\{\partial^{1}_{\rho}\lambda\mid\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\}\text{ (resp. }B^{2}_{\rho}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))=\{\partial^{1}_{\rho}\lambda\mid\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))\})

is the set of ordinary two-coboundaries with
\rho
-coefficients (resp.
\chi
-coefficient) and

B^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})=\{\partial^{1}_{\rho}\lambda\mid\lambda\in C^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}^{*})\}\text{ (resp. }B^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))=\{\partial^{1}_{\rho}\lambda\mid\lambda\in C^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{g}},\Pi({\mathfrak{g}}^{*}))\})

is the set of two-coboundaries for Lagrangian extension cohomology.

Proposition

(Filiforms as double and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions)
.

(
i
)
The filiform Lie superalgebra
L^{n,n}
can be obtained as a
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of the filiform Lie algebra
L^{n}
where the connection
\nabla
on
L^{n}
is given by

\nabla_{X_{1}}(X_{i})=X_{i+1}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for $1\leq i\leq n-1$ \hskip 9.44456ptand \hskip 9.44456pt$\nabla_{X_{j}}(X_{i})=0$ \hskip 9.44456ptfor all $j>1$}.

The odd part
\Pi({(L^{n}})^{*})
is given by

Y_{i}=(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n-i+1}\Pi X_{j}^{*}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for $i=1,\ldots,n$.}

(
ii
)
For
n
even and
m
odd, the filiform Lie superalgebra
L^{n,m}
can be obtained as a
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{0}}
-extension of the abelian Lie superalgebra
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-2,1}
, followed by successive
{\mathscr{D}}_{\bar{1}}
-extensions.

Proof.

For Part (i), let us first check that the connection is torsion-free. Indeed,

\nabla_{X_{1}}X_{i}-\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{1}-[X_{1},X_{i}]=X_{i+1}-X_{i+1}=0.

Now, the connection is flat since
\nabla_{X_{i}}\circ\nabla_{X_{j}}=0
and
\nabla_{[X_{i},X_{j}]}(X_{k})=0
for all
i,j,k=1,\ldots,n
.
Let us now check the Lie brackets. Indeed, for all

[X_{1},Y_{i}]=[X_{1},(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n-i+1}\Pi X_{j}^{*}]=-(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n-i+1}\Pi X_{j}^{*}\circ\nabla_{X_{1}}=(-1)^{i+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-i}\Pi X_{j}^{*}=Y_{i+1}.

[X_{1},Y_{n}]=[X_{1},(-1)^{n}\Pi(X_{1}^{*})]=-(-1)^{n}\Pi(X_{1}^{*})\circ\nabla_{X_{1}}=0.

Let us prove Part (ii). First, let us observe that

is a

-extension of

a_{0}=0,\hskip 9.24994pt{\mathscr{D}}(X_{1})=-(-1)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}Y_{2},\hskip 9.24994pt{\mathscr{D}}(a)=0\text{ for all $a\not=X_{1}$},\hskip 9.24994ptx^{*}=(-1)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}Y_{1},\hskip 9.24994ptx=Y_{m}.

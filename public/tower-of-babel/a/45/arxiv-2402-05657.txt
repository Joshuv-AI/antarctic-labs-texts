q q -Parikh Matrices and q q -deformed binomial coefficients of words
Antoine Renard, Michel Rigo, Markus A. Whiteland

Abstract
We have introduced a q q -deformation, i.e., a polynomial in q q with natural coefficients, of the binomial coefficient of two finite words u u and v v counting the number of occurrences of v v as a subword of u u . In this paper, we examine the q q -deformation of Parikh matrices as introduced by Eğecioğlu in 2004. Many classical results concerning Parikh matrices generalize to this new framework: Our first important observation is that the elements of such a matrix are in fact q q -deformations of binomial coefficients of words. We also study their inverses and as an application, we obtain new identities about q q -binomials. For a finite word z z and for the sequence ( p n ) n ≥ 0 (p_{n})_{n\geq 0} of prefixes of an infinite word, we show that the polynomial sequence ( p n z ) q \binom{p_{n}}{z}_{\!\!q} converges to a formal series. We present links with additive number theory and k k -regular sequences. In the case of a periodic word u ω u^{\omega} , we generalize a result of Salomaa: the sequence ( u n z ) q \binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q} satisfies a linear recurrence relation with polynomial coefficients. Related to the theory of integer partition, we describe the growth and the zero set of the coefficients of the series associated with u ω u^{\omega} . Finally, we show that the minors of a q q -Parikh matrix are polynomials with natural coefficients and consider a generalization of Cauchy’s inequality. We also compare q q -Parikh matrices associated with an arbitrary word with those associated with a canonical word 12 ⋯ k 12\cdots k made of pairwise distinct symbols.

Introduction

In a recent paper
[

q
-deformations of binomial coefficients of words.

{definition}

The

-binomial coefficient

(1)

As usual when dealing with

The present paper is thus the occasion to shed some new light on Eğecioğlu’s work

1.3
Our contributions

In
Section

2
, similarly to Şerbănuţă we extend Eğecioğlu’s definition to matrices induced by a word

.
We first use basic linear algebra techniques to obtain direct analogues of classical results on Parikh matrices:
we discuss the significance of the entries of these matrices and their inverse with respect to
q
-binomial coefficients of words. Up to a multiplication by a convenient power of

, the elements in the upper part of such a matrix are of the form
\binom{u}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j}}_{\!\!q}
for

.

For results about inverses, we work under the assumption that the fixed word

has no factor
aa
made of two identical letters. With
Section

2.1
we see why the inverse has a more intricate form when

does not fulfill this assumption. We also get that the inverse of a
q
-Parikh matrix associated with

is directly related (up to some reverse operation and sign alternation) to the
q
-Parikh matrix associated with the reversal
\widetilde{z}
.

As an application of our first results on

-Parikh matrices and their inverses, we obtain new relations on those

-binomials. For instance, we obtain identities such as

\binom{u}{a}_{\!\!q}\binom{u}{cb}_{\!\!q}+q^{2}\binom{u}{abc}_{\!\!q}=\binom{u}{c}_{\!\!q}\binom{u}{ab}_{\!\!q}+q^{2}\binom{u}{cba}_{\!\!q},

where

is a word and

are letters such that
a\neq b
and

.
Section

2.3
generalizes this relation for more than three letters with the restriction that adjacent letters are distinct.

Section

3
takes a different perspective, this time introducing new objects of an algebraic nature. It is quite evident that with any left-infinite word
\mathbf{x}
, if

is the prefix of length

of

converges to a formal power series

in
\mathbb{N}[[q]]
. If the infinite word
\mathbf{x}
is

-automatic, then

is shown to be

-regular. In particular, our considerations make it possible to express certain sequences (
A133009
from OEIS) encountered in additive number theory, respectively, on the number of representations of an integer

as the sum of two
odious
or
evil
numbers
[

,
6
,
8
,
11
]
, in the framework of
q
-binomial coefficients.

Let

be finite words. Salomaa has shown that the integer sequence

satisfies a linear recurrence relation with constant integer coefficients
[
25
]
. For this specific case, it is therefore natural to ask what more can be said about the sequence of polynomials
n\mapsto\binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q}
. As a generalization of Salomaa’s result, we show that it satisfies a linear recurrence relation over

\N[q]
.

Finally for a periodic infinite word
\mathbf{u}=\cdots uuu
, we have a precise description of the growth order of the coefficients of the series
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}(q)
. It will be observed by classical arguments that these coefficients also satisfy a linear recurrence relation with integer coefficients. We show that

vanishes periodically and those conditions are prescribed by a well understood arithmetic relation. If it does not vanish then it is in

. Interestingly, this study also makes connection with the extensively studied problems of partition of integers into distinct parts
[
15
]
.

In the last section, with

4.2
we express a

-Parikh matrix associated with an arbitrary word

as a

-Parikh matrix (of the same dimension) associated with a canonical word of the form

. To that end, we introduce a morphism

encoding the positions of a letter occurring in

. In particular, such a word

satisfies the assumption of

which permits us to also provide an expression for the inverse.
Then we consider minors of

-Parikh matrices. Interestingly, they are polynomials with non-negative integer coefficients. This is an alternative explanation of the fact that the inverse of a

-deformed Parikh matrix has entries in
\pm\mathbb{N}[q]
. Finally, we discuss a

All these results tend to show that our definition of a

-deformation of Parikh matrices is the right one, since we generalize results from several different papers

,
18
,
24
,
25
]

More generally, we have the following result.

{proposition}

and

2.3
, since

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u).\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)^{-1}]_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)]_{1,i}\cdot[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)^{-1}]_{i,n+1}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)^{-1}]_{1,n+1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)]_{1,i}\cdot[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)^{-1}]_{i,n+1}+[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)]_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}q^{n(|u|-1)}[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(\widetilde{u})]_{1,n+1}\left(\tfrac{1}{q}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=2}^{n}q^{\mathsf{s}(i-2)}\binom{u}{z_{1}\cdots z_{i-1}}_{\!\!q}\cdot(-1)^{i+n+1}q^{(n+1-i)(|u|-1)}\cdot[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(\widetilde{u})]_{i,n+1}\left(\tfrac{1}{q}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+q^{\mathsf{s}(n)}\binom{u}{z_{1}\cdots z_{n}}_{\!\!q}.

\displaystyle q^{(n+1-i)(|u|-1)}\cdot[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(\widetilde{u})]_{i,n+1}\left(\tfrac{1}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{(n+1-i)(|u|-1)}\cdot\left(q^{\mathsf{s}(n-i)}\binom{\widetilde{u}}{z_{i}\cdots z_{n}}_{\!\!q}\right)\left(\tfrac{1}{q}\right)

2.2

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u).\mathcal{P}_{z^{\prime}}(u)^{-1}]_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}q^{\mathsf{s}(n-1)}\binom{u}{z_{n}\,z_{n-1}\cdots z_{1}}_{\!\!q}

3
Convergence to a formal power series

We first observe that with any left-infinite word
\mathbf{x}=\cdots x_{2}x_{1}x_{0}
, if
p_{n}=x_{n-1}\cdots x_{0}
is the prefix of length
n
of this word, then the polynomial sequence
n\mapsto\binom{p_{n}}{z}_{\!\!q}
converges to a formal power series
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{x},z}
. If the infinite word is moreover
k
-automatic, then by classical arguments the limit series is shown to be
k
-regular.

To make a connection with what is known in the classical setting, Salomaa has shown that the integer sequence
n\mapsto\binom{u^{n}}{z}
satisfies a linear recurrence relation
[
25
]
. It is therefore natural to ask what more can be said about the sequence of polynomials
n\mapsto\binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q}
. As a generalization of Salomaa’s result, we show that it satisfies a linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients. Finally for a periodic infinite word
\mathbf{u}=\cdots uuu
, we have a precise description of the growth order of the coefficients of the series
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}
.

3.1
Convergence and automaticity

Due to
Section

1
and relation
Eq.

1
where the focus is on the rightmost letter, it is more convenient to consider a left-infinite word
\cdots x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{0}=\mathbf{x}
. We say that
x_{t-1}\cdots x_{0}
is the
prefix
of length
t
of
\mathbf{x}
.

{proposition}

Let
\cdots x_{n}x_{n-1}\cdots x_{0}=\mathbf{x}
be a left-infinite word and
z
be a finite word. For all
r\geq 0
, there exists
N
such that the coefficient of
q^{r}
in the
q
-binomials
\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}
is the same for all
n\geq N
. Otherwise stated, the sequence
\left([q^{r}]\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}\right)_{n\geq 0}
is eventually constant.

Proof.

If
z
occurs as a subword of some prefix of length
N
of
\mathbf{x}
, then it will occur within all longer prefixes of
\mathbf{x}
. Let
n\geq N-1
. Take a specific occurrence of
z=z_{1}\cdots z_{\ell}
such that

x_{n}\cdots x_{0}=z_{1}y_{1}z_{2}y_{2}\cdots z_{\ell}y_{\ell},

where
y_{1}
, …,
y_{\ell}\in A^{*}
. By
Theorem

2.2
it provides
\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}
with some monomial

where

Roughly speaking, the further to the left occurs the first letter of
z
(i.e., the larger

is such that
x_{n}=z_{1}
), the larger the corresponding exponent is.

Instead of focusing on a specific occurrence of
z
, let us focus on a specific exponent of the
q
-binomial. From the above discussion, for each given
r
and for large enough

, the coefficient of
q^{r}
in
\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}
is completely determined by a suffix of
\mathbf{x}
whose length is bounded by
r+|z|
. Hence, for all
n\geq r+|z|-1
, the polynomials
\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}
have the same coefficient for
q^{r}
.
∎

This result legitimates the next definition.

{definition}

Let
\mathbf{x}
be a left-infinite word and
z
be a finite word. For all
r\geq 0
, we let
c_{r}
denote the coefficient of
q^{r}
in the
q
-binomials
\binom{x_{n}\cdots x_{0}}{z}_{\!\!q}
for all

. We let
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{x},z}
denote the formal series defined by
\sum_{r\geq 0}c_{r}\,q^{r}
.

As an example, consider the (left) Thue–Morse sequence
\cdots 110010110=\mathbf{t}
and the word
z=00
. We obtain the (right-infinite) sequence of coefficients whose first terms are

00101101211211412313324323525505635534844655764765957847\cdots

For instance, this means that, for large enough

, in every

-binomial
\binom{t_{n}\cdots t_{0}}{00}_{\!\!q}
the monomial of least degree with a non-zero coefficient is

.
This is (up to a shift) the sequence

from OEIS
counting the number

of pairs
(x,y)
of integers such as their base-
2
expansions contain an odd number of ones (the Thue–Morse word is the characteristic sequence of the set of integers with this property),

and
x+y=n
. For instance,

because there is only one pair
(1,2)
such that

. This sequence was introduced in
[
11
]
and the connection with automatic sequences was studied in
[
2
]
.

We assume the reader familiar with
k

[

27
]
. For instance, the Thue–Morse sequence is

\cdots x_{2}x_{1}x_{0}=\mathbf{x}

3.2
A fine analysis of the periodic case

Let

be finite non-empty words. The left-infinite periodic word
\mathbf{u}=\cdots uuu
is

-automatic for all

\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}
is
k
-regular for all

. A generalization of Cobham’s theorem implies that the sequence of coefficients of
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}
satisfies a linear recurrence relation
[
3
]
.

Our aim in this section is to get a precise description of the polynomial
\binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q}
not reducing ourselves to the limit case given by
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}
. From this, we obtain a generalization of Salomaa’s result with

: The sequence of
q
-binomials

satisfies a linear recurrence relation over
\mathbb{Z}[q]
. In particular, our developments are here independent of the general theory of automatic and regular sequences.

{definition}

Given two words
u,z
where
u
is over the alphabet
A
of
z
and a non-negative integer
k
, let us define the square matrix Pow
{}_{z}(k)
of dimension
|z|+1
as follows:

[\Pow_{z}(k)]_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }i>j\\
1&\text{if }i=j\\
q^{(j-i)k}&\text{if }i<j,\\
\end{cases}\quad\text{ i.e., }\quad\Pow_{z}(k)={\small\begin{pmatrix}1&q^{k}&q^{2k}&\cdots&q^{|z|k}\\
0&1&q^{k}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&q^{2k}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&1&q^{k}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}}.

Finally, we let
H_{z,k}(u)
denote the Hadamard product
\mathcal{P}_{z}(u)\odot\Pow_{z}(k)
.

The following result allows us to express the Parikh matrix of
u^{n}
induced by
z
using the matrices
H_{z,k}(u)
we just defined.

{lemma}

Let
k\in\N
, and let us write
u=u_{\ell}\cdots u_{0}
with
u_{i}\in A
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
. We have

H_{z,k}(u)=\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell},k+\ell}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell-1},k+\ell-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{u_{1},k+1}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{u_{0},k}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|u|\geq 1
. If
|u|=1
, then
u=a\in A
and, using the definition of the Parikh matrix
\mathcal{P}_{z}(a)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{z,k}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{z}(a)\odot\Pow_{z}(k)=\mathcal{M}_{a,0}\odot\Pow_{z}(k)=\mathcal{M}_{a,k}.

Let us now assume that the result is true for all words
u
of length
\ell\geq 1
, and show that it still holds for a word
w=w_{\ell}\cdots w_{0}
of length
\ell+1
. If
|w|=\ell+1
, then there exist
u\in A^{*}
and
a\in A
such that
|u|=\ell
and
w=au=au_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}
. By induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{w_{\ell},k+\ell}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{w_{\ell-1},k+\ell-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{w_{0},k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell-1},k+\ell-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{u_{0},k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u).

We thus have to show that
\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u)=H_{z,k}(w)
. By definition,

[\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}]_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }i=j;\\
q^{k+\ell},&\text{if }i+1=j\text{ and }z_{i}=a;\\
0,&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

and

[H_{z,k}(u)]_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i>j;\\
1,&\text{if }i=j;\\
q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\binom{u}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q},&\text{if }i<j.\end{cases}

It is clear that
[\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u)]_{i,j}=\delta_{i,j}
for every pair
(i,j)
such that
1\leq j\leq i\leq|z|+1
. Now assume that
i<j
, and let us consider two cases.
If
z_{i}\neq a
, then

\displaystyle[\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u)]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\binom{u}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}

\displaystyle=q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\binom{au}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}

\displaystyle=[H_{z,k}(w)]_{i,j},

and if
z_{i}=a
, then

\displaystyle[\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u)]_{i,j}

\displaystyle=q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\binom{u}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}+q^{k+\ell}q^{(j-i-1)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-2)}\binom{u}{z_{i+1}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}

\displaystyle=q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\left(\binom{u}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}+q^{\ell-(j-i-1)}\binom{u}{z_{i+1}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{(j-i)k+\mathsf{s}(j-i-1)}\binom{w}{z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1}}_{\!\!q}

\displaystyle=[H_{z,k}(w)]_{i,j},

where the second to last equality comes from
Theorem

2.2
, since
|u|=\ell
and
|z_{i+1}\cdots z_{j-1}|=j-i-1
. In any case, we find that
[\mathcal{M}_{a,k+\ell}\cdot H_{z,k}(u)]_{i,j}=[H_{z,k}(w)]_{i,j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq|z|+1
, which completes the proof.
∎

{corollary}

For any positive integer

and

, we have

\mathcal{P}_{z}(u^{n})=H_{z,(n-1)|u|}(u)\cdot H_{z,(n-2)|u|}(u)\cdots H_{z,|u|}(u)\cdot H_{z,0}(u).

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(u^{n})
and
Section

3.2
. Let
u=u_{\ell}\cdots u_{0}
. Indeed, by definition we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{z}(u^{n})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell},n|u|-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{u_{0},(n-1)|u|})\cdots(\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell},2|u|-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{u_{0},|u|})(\mathcal{M}_{u_{\ell},|u|-1}\cdots\mathcal{M}_{u_{0},0})

\displaystyle=H_{z,(n-1)|u|}(u)\cdots H_{z,|u|}(u)\cdot H_{z,0}(u).

∎

3.1
.

The

\frac{1}{q^{\mathsf{s}(|z|-1)}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}R_{k}(q)\,\frac{1-q^{c_{k}n|u|}}{1-q^{c_{k}|u|}}

\frac{1}{q^{\mathsf{s}(|z|-1)}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}R_{k}(q)\,\frac{1}{1-q^{c_{k}|u|}}.

Proof.

For all

\overrightarrow{p_{n}}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{n,\ell}\\

\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}q^{\mathsf{s}(\ell-1)}\binom{u^{n}}{z_{1}\cdots z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\\
q^{\mathsf{s}(\ell-2)}\binom{u^{n}}{z_{2}\cdots z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{u^{n}}{z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\\

In particular,
\overrightarrow{p_{1}}
is the last column of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(u)
and, for convenience, we set

to be the column vector made of zeroes with only a

Section

\overrightarrow{p_{n+1}}=H_{z,n|u|}\cdot\overrightarrow{p_{n}}.

(6)

Since

from bottom to top. First, we have

for all

and we deduce from the above recurrence that

p_{n+1,1}=\binom{u}{z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{i|u|}=\binom{u}{z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\frac{1-q^{(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{|u|}}.

Let us produce one extra element, again from
Eq.

6

\displaystyle p_{n+1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{n,2}+\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}}_{\!\!q}q^{n|u|}\,p_{n,1}+q\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}q^{2n|u|}\,p_{n,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{n,2}+\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}}_{\!\!q}\binom{u}{z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\frac{1}{1-q^{|u|}}\left(q^{n|u|}-q^{2n|u|}\right)+\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}q^{2n|u|+1}.

We deduce that

p_{n+1,2}=\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}}_{\!\!q}\binom{u}{z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\left(\frac{1-q^{(n+1)|u|}}{(1-q^{|u|})^{2}}-\frac{1-q^{2(n+1)|u|}}{(1-q^{|u|})(1-q^{2|u|})}\right)+q\binom{u}{z_{\ell-1}z_{\ell}}_{\!\!q}\frac{1-q^{2(n+1)|u|}}{(1-q^{2|u|})}.

To get a better grasp of the statement, let us rewrite the polynomial as

p_{n+1,2}=R_{2,1}(q)\,\frac{1-q^{(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{|u|}}+R_{2,2}(q)\,\frac{1-q^{2(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{2|u|}}

\sum_{k=1}^{j}q^{\mathsf{s}(k-1)}\binom{u}{z_{\ell-j+1}\cdots z_{\ell-j+k}}_{\!\!q}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j-k}}\frac{R_{j-k,i}(q)}{{1-q^{c_{j-k,i}|u|}}}\,\left(\frac{1-q^{k(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{k|u|}}-\frac{1-q^{(c_{j-k,i}+k)(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{(c_{j-k,i}+k)|u|}}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle p_{n+1,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j}\overbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j-k}}q^{\mathsf{s}(k-1)}\binom{u}{z_{\ell-j+1}\cdots z_{\ell-j+k}}_{\!\!q}\frac{R_{j-k,i}(q)}{{1-q^{c_{j-k,i}|u|}}}}\,\frac{1-q^{k(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{k|u|}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j-k}}\underbrace{-q^{\mathsf{s}(k-1)}\binom{u}{z_{\ell-j+1}\cdots z_{\ell-j+k}}_{\!\!q}\frac{R_{j-k,i}(q)}{{1-q^{c_{j-k,i}|u|}}}}\,\frac{1-q^{(c_{j-k,i}+k)(n+1)|u|}}{1-q^{(c_{j-k,i}+k)|u|}}

where the braced factors are

having, by induction hypothesis, the right form.

To conclude with the proof,
\binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q}
is equal up to a multiplicative factor

[

{lemma}

Let
R_{j}(q)
be given rational functions and
c_{j}
be pairwise distinct non-negative integers. The sequence
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}R_{j}(q)\,q^{c_{j}n}\right)_{n\geq 0}

satisfies the following linear recurrence relation of order
s
with polynomial coefficients

p_{n+s}=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\underbrace{(-1)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq s}q^{c_{i_{1}}+\cdots+c_{i_{k}}}\right)}_{:=\mathfrak{D}_{k}}p_{n+s-k}

Proof.

The linear recurrent sequences
\left(q^{c_{j}n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
and
\left(R_{j}(q)q^{c_{j}n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
have both a characteristic polynomial of the form
X-q^{c_{j}}
. It is a well-known result that the characteristic polynomial of the sum of linear recurrent sequences (with distinct roots) is the product of the corresponding characteristic polynomials. Hence, the characteristic polynomial of the sequence of interest is

\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(X-q^{c_{j}n}\right)=X^{s}+\sum_{k=1}^{s}\mathfrak{D}_{k}X^{s-k}.

∎

{corollary}

Let
R_{j}(q)
be given rational functions and
c_{j}
be pairwise distinct non-negative integers. The sequence
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}R_{j}(q)\,\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{c_{j}i}\right)_{n\geq 0}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}R_{j}(q)\,\frac{1-q^{c_{j}(n+1)}}{1-q^{c_{j}}}\right)_{n\geq 0}

satisfies the following linear recurrence relation of order
s+1
with polynomial coefficients

p_{n+s+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{s+1}(\mathfrak{D}_{k}-\mathfrak{D}_{k-1})\,p_{n+k}

setting
\mathfrak{D}_{0}=-1
and
\mathfrak{D}_{s+1}=0
.

Proof.

This follows from the classical result that if
f(t)
is the rational function equal to the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{n}\,t^{n}
where
u_{n}
satisfies a linear recurrence relation, then
\frac{1}{1-t}.f(t)
encodes the series of the partial sums and one can get a linear relation from the denominator of the series.
∎

Letting
q=1
we recover Salomaa’s result
[
25
, Thm. 3]
as a special case of our results.

{corollary}

The sequence of
q
-binomials
(\binom{u^{n}}{z}_{\!\!q})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies a linear recurrence relation with polynomial coefficients. In particular, the sequence of binomials
(\binom{u^{n}}{z})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies a linear recurrence relation with constant coefficients.

Growth of the coefficients of the series in the periodic case

{lemma}

Let
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathbf{u},z}(q)=\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{i}\,q^{i}

\mathcal{O}(i^{|z|-1})

Proceeding as in the proof of

, we can express

where
P_{j}

,
\sum_{i=1}^{|z|}\alpha_{j_{i}}\leq|z|
. Indeed, in the expression

to get

One concludes by considering the partial fraction decomposition and recalling that the

.

3.2
.

Let

4.1
Properties of minors and other relations

A

[

{proposition}

Any minor of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(u)
is a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients.

Proof.

Proceed by induction on the length of

Assume that

in
\mathcal{P}_{z}(dw)

is equal to the sum of the

th row of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(w)
and

times the

st row of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(w)

\mathcal{P}_{z}(dw)

with coefficients

are polynomials with non-negative integer coefficients.
∎

{corollary}

Let

\displaystyle=(r-1)(j-i+1)+2(r-1)\sum_{m=i+1}^{j}(j-m+1)

\displaystyle=(r-1)(j-i+1)+2(r-1)\sum_{k=1}^{j-i}k

\displaystyle=(r-1)(j-i+1+(j-i)(j-i+1))=(r-1)(j+1-i)^{2}.

Thus the proof is complete.
∎

SNR Maximization in Beyond Diagonal RIS-assisted Single and Multiple Antenna Links
Ignacio Santamaria, Mohammad Soleymani, Eduard Jorswieck, Jesús Gutiérrez

Abstract
Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) architectures not limited to diagonal phase shift matrices have recently been considered to increase their flexibility in shaping the wireless channel. One of these beyond-diagonal RIS or BD-RIS architectures leads to a unitary and symmetric RIS matrix. In this letter, we consider the problem of maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in single and multiple antenna links assisted by a BD-RIS. The Max-SNR problem admits a closed-form solution based on the Takagi factorization of a certain complex and symmetric matrix. This allows us to solve the max-SNR problem for SISO, SIMO, and MISO channels.

II
Max-SNR BD-RIS: The SISO case

Let us begin by reminding the reader of some basic results.
We write the full SVD of the outer product channel matrix as
{\bf h}_{R}{\bf h}_{T}^{H}={\bf U}\bm{\Lambda}{\bf V}^{H}
, where
\bm{\Lambda}=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},0,\ldots,0)
. Then, it is known that the rank-1 unitary matrix
{\bf u}{\bf v}^{H}
, or the full-rank unitary matrix
{\bf U}{\bf V}^{H}
, maximize the output power achieving
P_{max}=\lambda_{1}^{2}
. Clearly, the difficulty of
{\cal P}_{1}
stems from the symmetry constraint
\bm{\Theta}=\bm{\Theta}^{T}
. Its solution is based on Takagi’s decomposition of a certain symmetric matrix, so we first review that result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\bf A}={\bf A}^{T}
be an
n\times n
complex symmetric matrix. Then, there exist an
n\times n
unitary matrix
{\bf Q}
and an
n\times n
positive semidefinite diagonal matrix
\bm{\Sigma}=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n})
such that
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
.

This factorization of a complex symmetric matrix is called the Autonne-Takagi factorization, or the Takagi factorization in short, originally proposed by Autonne
[
14
]
and Takagi
[
12
]
(see also
[
13
, Chapter 4, Corollary 4.4.4]
). The columns of
{\bf Q}
are called the Takagi vectors of
{\bf A}
and the diagonal elements of
\bm{\Sigma}
are its Takagi values. Notice that the Takagi values of
{\bf A}
coincide with the singular values of
{\bf A}
. We have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
{\bf h}_{R}{\bf h}_{T}^{H}=\lambda_{1}{\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H}
be the rank-one outer product matrix between
{\bf h}_{R}=\|{\bf h}_{R}\|{\bf u}_{R}
and
{\bf h}_{T}=\|{\bf h}_{T}\|{\bf u}_{T}
, where
\lambda_{1}=\|{\bf h}_{R}\|\|{\bf h}_{T}\|
. Form the rank-2 symmetric complex matrix
{\bf A}={\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H}+({\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H})^{T}
, and compute its Takagi’s factorization as
{\bf A}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
, where
{\bf Q}
is unitary and
\bm{\Sigma}=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},0,\ldots,0)
are the Takagi values (singular values) of
{\bf A}
. Then, the solution of
{\cal P}_{1}
is

\bm{\Theta}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}.

(2)

Furthermore, the maximum signal power achieved by this solution is
P_{max}=\lambda_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\bm{\Theta}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}
is unitary and symmetric is trivially checked. Let us define the vectors
{\bf g}_{R}={\bf Q}^{H}{\bf u}_{R}
and
{\bf g}_{T}={\bf Q}^{T}{\bf u}_{T}
. Since
{\bf Q}
is unitary
\|{{\bf g}}_{R}\|=1
and
\|{{\bf g}}_{T}\|=1
. With these definitions the equivalent channel is
{\bf h}_{R}^{H}\bm{\Theta}{\bf h}_{T}={\bf h}_{R}^{H}{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}{\bf h}_{T}=\lambda_{1}{{\bf g}}_{R}^{H}{{\bf g}}_{T}
. We want to prove that
{\bf g}_{R}^{H}{\bf g}_{T}=1
, which in turn implies that
{\bf g}_{T}={\bf g}_{R}
and that the received signal power is
P_{max}=\lambda_{1}^{2}
. To prove that
{{\bf g}}_{R}^{H}{\bf g}_{T}=1
or, equivalently, that

{{\bf g}}_{T}^{H}{\bf g}_{R}=1
, notice that

{\bf Q}^{H}{\bf A}{\bf Q}^{*}={\bf g}_{R}{\bf g}_{T}^{H}+({\bf g}_{R}{\bf g}_{T}^{H})^{T}=\bm{\Sigma},

(3)

where
\operatorname{tr}(\bm{\Sigma})=\operatorname{tr}({\bf A})=2
. Taking traces in (
3
) and applying the circular property of the trace
[
15
, pp. 360]
and
\operatorname{tr}({\bf g}_{R}{\bf g}_{T}^{H})=\operatorname{tr}(({\bf g}_{R}{\bf g}_{T}^{H})^{T})
, we finally get
2\operatorname{tr}({\bf g}_{R}{\bf g}_{T}^{H})=2\operatorname{tr}({\bf g}_{T}^{H}{\bf g}_{R})=2{\bf g}_{T}^{H}{\bf g}_{R}=2
and, therefore,
{\bf g}_{R}^{H}{\bf g}_{T}=1
thus proving the result

.

Although we have considered for simplicity of exposition the case in which the direct channel is blocked, the solution can be extended to the case in which the equivalent channel is
h_{eq}=h+{\bf h}_{R}^{H}\bm{\Theta}{\bf h}_{T}
. In this case, after computing Takagi’s factorization of
{\bf A}={\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H}+({\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H})^{T}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}

\bm{\Theta}=e^{j\angle h}{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}

\angle h

|h_{eq}|=|h|+|{\bf h}_{R}^{H}{\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}{\bf h}_{T}|=|h|+\lambda_{1}
.

Remark 1
.

The Max-SNR BD-RIS solution is not unique for the SISO case unless the number of BD-RIS elements is
M=2
. To show this, note that
{\bf A}={\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H}+({\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T}^{H})^{T}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T}
is a rank-2 matrix. Now, partition the unitary matrix
{\bf Q}
into signal and noise subspaces as
{\bf Q}=[{\bf Q}_{signal}|{\bf Q}_{noise}]

{\bf Q}_{signal}
contains the first

{\bf Q}

{\bf Q}_{noise}
contains the remaining

{\bf Q}_{noise}^{\prime}={\bf Q}_{noise}{\bf T}

{\bf T}
is an
(M-2)\times(M-2)

{\bf Q}^{\prime}=[{\bf Q}_{signal}|{\bf Q}_{noise}^{\prime}]

\bm{\Theta}^{\prime}={\bf Q}^{\prime}{\bf Q}^{\prime T}
that provides the maximum output power

. When
M=2
there is no noise subspace so the solution is unique up to a complex scaling of the form

.

In
{\cal{P}}_{1}
, it is considered that all ports of the RIS elements are connected to each other, therefore defining a fully-connected BD-RIS architecture, which may complicate the required circuitry. A good trade-off between complexity and performance is provided by the group-connected BD-RIS, in which the
\bm{\Theta}
matrix is block-diagonal with unitary and symmetric blocks
[
7
]
,
[

M

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{2}):\,\max_{\bm{\Theta}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\Theta}_{G}}\,\,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{g=1}^{G}{\bf h}_{R,g}^{H}\bm{\Theta}_{g}{\bf h}_{T,g}\right|^{2}

(4)

s.t.

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}_{g}^{H}\bm{\Theta}_{g}={\bf I}_{G},\,\forall g

\displaystyle\bm{\Theta}_{g}=\bm{\Theta}_{g}^{T},\,\forall g.

Performing Takagi’s decomposition of the

{\bf u}_{R,g}{\bf u}_{T,g}^{H}+({\bf u}_{R,g}{\bf u}_{T,g}^{H})^{T}={\bf Q}_{g}\bm{\Sigma}_{g}{\bf Q}_{g}^{T}

\bm{\Theta}_{g}={\bf Q}_{g}{\bf Q}_{g}^{T}

1

{\bf h}_{R,g}^{H}{\bf Q}_{g}{\bf Q}_{g}^{T}{\bf h}_{T,g}

Extensions

III-A

MISO and SIMO channels

An optimal closed-form solution for the Max-SNR BD-RIS optimization problem can also be obtained when either the transmitter or the receiver is equipped with multiple antennas. Let us take the multiple-input single-output (MISO) case as an example. The single-input multiple-output (SIMO) case is solved analogously. In the MISO case,
{\bf H}_{T}
is an
M\times N_{T}

{\bf h}_{R}
is an
M\times 1

\displaystyle({\cal P}_{3}):\,\max_{\bm{\Theta}}\,\,

\displaystyle\|{\bf h}_{R}^{H}\bm{\Theta}{\bf H}_{T}\|^{2}

(5)

s.t.

\displaystyle\,\,\eqref{eq:unitaryconst}\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,\eqref{eq:symmetryconst}.

Notice that
{\bf h}_{eq}={\bf h}_{R}^{H}\bm{\Theta}{\bf H}_{T}=[h_{eq}(1),\ldots,h_{eq}(N_{T})]
is now the equivalent

Corollary 1
.

The optimal symmetric and unitary BD-RIS matrix that maximizes

\bm{\Theta}={\bf Q}{\bf Q}^{T}

{\bf Q}
is a unitary matrix obtained from Takagi’s factorization of

{\bf A}={\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T,1}^{H}+({\bf u}_{R}{\bf u}_{T,1}^{H})^{T}={\bf Q}\bm{\Sigma}{\bf Q}^{T},

(6)

where
{\bf u}_{R}=\frac{{\bf h}_{R}}{\|{\bf h}_{R}\|}
and
{\bf u}_{T,1}
is the largest left singular vector of
{\bf H}_{T}={\bf U}_{T}\bm{\Lambda}_{T}{\bf V}_{T}^{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{T}}\lambda_{T,i}{\bf u}_{T,i}{\bf v}_{T,i}^{H}

The proof follows from the fact that, as pointed out in

19
]

is achieved when

is maximized. Then we simply have to apply the result of Proposition
1
to a SISO system with channels
{\bf u}_{R}
and
{\bf u}_{T,1}

Remark 4
.

Once the optimal BD-RIS has been obtained, the equivalent channel
{\bf h}_{eq}={\bf h}_{R}^{H}\bm{\Theta}{\bf H}_{T}
is known and the transmitter can apply, for example, the maximum ratio transmission (MRT) beamformer
{\bf w}_{T}={\bf h}_{eq}^{H}/\|{\bf h}_{eq}\|

\|{\bf h}_{eq}\|^{2}
attains its maximum value
P_{max}=\lambda_{T,1}^{2}\|{\bf h}_{R}\|^{2}
, thus solving the Max-SNR joint Tx-beamforming BD-RIS optimization problem.

V
Conclusion

The Takagi factorization of a certain complex symmetric matrix allows us to solve the problem of maximizing the SNR in SISO and multi-antenna links assisted by a beyond-diagonal RIS. Some open problems along this line are extensions to the multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) case, as well as to muti-antenna scenarios (SIMO, MISO, and MIMO) in which the direct channels are not blocked. It would also be interesting to consider the optimization of other metrics in networks assisted by a BD-RIS, such as the capacity in point-to-point MIMO links, the weighted sum rate in the MAC, or the interference leakage in interference channels
[
27
]
.

Extension and lifting of G G -bundles for stacks
Torsten Wedhorn

Abstract




Abstract.
We study extension properties for morphisms of stacks of bundles for group algebraic spaces. Applications are a short proof of the classification of bundles on the projective line for affine smooth group schemes whose identity component is reductive and the existence of splittings of filtered fiber functors for gerbes locally given by affine smooth group schemes defined over affine schemes.

14D23 (Primary), 14L15, 14L30, 14N05 (Secondary)

Introduction

In this paper we will study extension properties of
G
-bundles for morphisms of (not necessarily algebraic) stacks
{\mathcal{X}}
, where
G
is a group scheme (or even group algebraic space). We denote the groupoid of
G
-bundles on
{\mathcal{X}}
by
\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{X}})
and the set of isomorphism classes in
\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{X}})
by
H^{1}({\mathcal{X}},G)
. The classifying stack of
G
is denoted by
B(G)
. Before giving the main results let us start with an illustrative application.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
k
be a field and let
G
be an affine smooth group scheme over
k
such that its unit component is reductive. Then there is a natural bijection

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},G)\cong H^{1}(B(\mathbb{G}_{m,k}),G),

where
B(\mathbb{G}_{m,k})
denotes the classifying stack of the multiplicative group over
k
.

The right hand side can be identified (Section
3.1
) with isomorphism classes of pairs
(P,\mu)
, where
P
is a
G
-bundle over
k
and where
\mu\colon\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\to\underline{\Aut}_{G}(P)
is a cocharacter of the strong inner form of
G
attached to
P
. The classification of
G
-bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
has a long history. For reductive groups
G
, Grothendieck
[
Gro57
]
gave a classification over an algebraically closed field which was generalized by Harder
[
Har68
, 3.4]
to a classification of Zariski locally trivial
G
-bundles over any field for a split reductive group
G
. Biswas and Nagaraj classified in
[
BN09
]
Zariski locally trivial
G
-bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
for any field and any reductive group over
k
. Anschütz proved Theorem
1
in
[
Ans18
]
for all fields
k
and all reductive groups
G
expressing the right hand side in a “Tannakian way” (Remark
3.1
).

The main interest of Theorem
1
does not lie in its generalization to not necessarily connected groups (although it does have the amusing side effect of giving a new proof of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k})\cong\pi_{1}(k)
, see Remark
3.6
), but in its method of proof. In
[
BN09
]
and
[
Ans18
]
the proof was obtained by using the Harder-Narasimhan stratification and the classification of vector bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. Here we bypass such arguments completely and proceed as follows. Consider the diagram of algebraic stacks

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}=[\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\backslash(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}\setminus\{0\})]\mathrel{\smash{\overset{j}{\longrightarrow}}}[\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}]\mathrel{\smash{\overset{i}{\longleftarrow}}}[\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\backslash\{0\}]=B(\mathbb{G}_{m,k}).

Via pullback we obtain maps

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},G)\mathrel{\smash{\overset{j^{*}}{\longleftarrow}}}H^{1}([\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}],G)\mathrel{\smash{\overset{i^{*}}{\longrightarrow}}}H^{1}(B(\mathbb{G}_{m,k}),G)

and as an immediate applications of our main results we obtain that
j^{*}
and
i^{*}
are bijections.

The main focus of the paper is the following question. For a morphism
f\colon{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
of stacks (not necessarily algebraic) we study properties of the pullback functor
f^{*}\colon\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{X}})\to\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{Y}})
and aim to give criteria when

(I)

the functor
f^{*}
is fully faithful or even an equivalence of categories, or

(II)

f
is a closed immersion and
f^{*}
induces a bijection on isomorphism classes.

To be more concrete, let us introduce some notation. Let
S
be an algebraic space and let
G
be a group algebraic space over
S
. In the introduction, all stacks are defined over
S
, have a diagonal representable by algebraic spaces and admit an fpqc-covering by an algebraic space. Regarding Problem (I) we prove the following general result.

Theorem 2

(Corollary
1.7
, Theorem
1.9
)
.

Let
f\colon{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a representable qcqs morphism of stacks. Suppose that
{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}}=f_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
(a condition that can be checked fpqc locally on
{\mathcal{X}}
).

(1)

If
G\to S
is affine and flat, then
f^{*}\colon\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{X}})\to\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{U}})
is fully faithful.

(2)

Suppose that there exists an embedding
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow H
of flat affine group algebraic spaces such that the fppf-quotient
G\backslash H
is representable by an algebraic space that is affine over
S
. Then a
G
-bundle
P
on
{\mathcal{U}}
is in the essential image of
f^{*}
if and only if the
H
-bundle
\iota_{*}(P)
is fpqc locally on
{\mathcal{U}}
in the essential image of
f^{*}\colon\Bun_{H}({\mathcal{X}})\to\Bun_{H}({\mathcal{U}})
.

Such questions have also been considered in many other papers in various special situations (e.g.,
[
CTS79
, 6.13]
,
[
AHLH22
, Section 3.5]
just to name two). In this generality, the result seems to be new.

In Section
1.2
, applications of Theorem

are given where

is an open immersion (Proposition

),
f
is proper, flat, of finite presentation with geometrically connected and geometrically reduced fibers (Proposition

), or if

is a good moduli space (Proposition

a general linear group and use known results for vector bundles. For instance, if

is the open embedding

is the embedding

and by the theorem of Auslander–Buchsbaum, every vector bundle on

We conclude the paper with two appendices. In the first appendix we define
G
-bundles for (not necessarily finite type) group algebraic spaces
G
on (not necessarily algebraic) stacks and study variants thereof. We work in this generality because of two reasons. First, some of the more interesting results in the main text hold in this generality. Second, we would like to lay the ground for following articles in which we will also have to deal with
G
-bundles in this generality. Since our group algebraic spaces are not assumed to be a finite type, we work systematically with the fpqc topology, which unfortunately is not that well documented in the literature (e.g., the Stacks Project usually works with the fppf topology).

In this appendix we also study other variants of
G
-bundles: cohomological
G
-bundles, geometric
G
-bundles, and Tannakian
G
-bundles and give criteria when these notions are equivalent. In particular we use results of Schäppi
[
Sch12
,
Sch20
]
to show the result already mentioned above:

Proposition 4

(Proposition
A.30
)
.

Let
S
be a qcqs scheme and let
G
be an
S
-affine flat group scheme over
S
such that
B(G)
has the resolution property. Then for any prestack
{\mathcal{X}}
the groupoids of
G
-bundles on
{\mathcal{X}}
and of Tannakian

-bundles over
{\mathcal{X}}
are equivalent.

This seems to be a new result. It shows that one can apply the Tannakian formalism to a rather large class of group algebraic spaces (Example
A.28
).

The first appendix ends by studying the sequence in low degrees of non-abelian cohomology over prestacks and by describing
G
-bundles on the classifying stack of a group algebraic space.

The second appendix proves the following purely topological result, where
\ClOpen(X)
denotes the set of open and closed subsets of a topological space
X
.

Proposition 5

(Proposition
B.3
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be topological spaces such that the open and quasi-compact subsets form a basis of the topology and that any intersection of a finite family of open quasi-compact subsets is again quasi-compact (e.g., the underlying topological spaces of qcqs algebraic stacks). Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map. Then

induces a bijection
\ClOpen(Y)\overset{\sim}{\to}\ClOpen(X)
if and only if
\pi_{0}(f)\colon\pi_{0}(X)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
is a homeomorphism.

This result allows to give a new characterization of henselian pairs of algebraic stacks, see Proposition

.

(2)

Appendix B
Connected components and clopen subsets

For a topological space

we denote by
\pi_{0}(X)
its space of connected components, i.e., the underlying set is the set of connected components of

and
\pi_{0}(X)
is endowed with the quotient topology of

is a continuous map of topological spaces, sending a connected component

to the unique connected component of

that contains

yields a continuous map
\pi_{0}(f)\colon\pi_{0}(X)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
. We obtain a functor
\pi_{0}
from the category of topological spaces to the category of totally disconnected spaces. This functor is left adjoint to the inclusion functor.

From now we make the following assumption on
X
.

(T)

X
is a topological space such that the open and quasi-compact subsets form a basis of the topology of
X
and that any intersection of a finite family of open quasi-compact subsets is again quasi-compact.

In particular
X

X
is a spectral topological space, e.g., the underlying topological space of a qcqs algebraic stack
[
Sta18
, 0DQP]
.

Let
X
satisfy (T). We call a subset
Z
of
X

pro-clopen
if it is the intersection of subsets of
X
that are both open and closed. By
[
Sta18
, 08ZN,04PL,0900]
, the following assertions hold.

Lemma B.1
.

(1)

Every connected component of
X
is the intersection of the open and closed subsets containing it.

(2)

A subset is pro-clopen if and only if it is closed in
X
and a union of connected components of
X
.

(3)

The space
\pi_{0}(X)
is pro-finite and in particular compact Hausdorff.

From these results one easily deduces the following lemma.

Lemma B.2
.

Let

satisfy (T). Let

be the canonical quotient map. Then sending

\displaystyle\{\text{$Y\subseteq\pi_{0}(X)$ closed}\}

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\to}\{\text{$Z\subseteq X$ pro-clopen}\},

\displaystyle\{\text{$Y=\{C\}\subseteq\pi_{0}(X)$ singleton}\}

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\to}\{\text{$\emptyset\neq Z\subseteq X$ minimal pro-clopen}\},

\displaystyle\{\text{$Y\subseteq\pi_{0}(X)$ open and closed}\}

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\to}\{\text{$Z\subseteq X$ open and closed}\},

The inverse map is given by
Z\mapsto p(Z)
.

A subset

of

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map of topological spaces satisfying condition (T). Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

Sending
V\subseteq Y
to
f^{-1}(V)\subseteq X
induces a bijection
\ClOpen(Y)\overset{\sim}{\to}\ClOpen(X)
.

(ii)

The map
\pi_{0}(f)\colon\pi_{0}(X)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
is bijective.

(iii)

The map
\pi_{0}(f)\colon\pi_{0}(X)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
is a homeomorphism.

These conditions are for instance satisfied if
f
has connected fibers (in particular,
f
is surjective) and endows
Y
with the quotient topology of
X

[
GW23
, 24.52]
.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{0}(X)
and
\pi_{0}(Y)
are compact Hausdorff,
(ii)
and
(iii)
are equivalent.

By the third bijection of Lemma
B.2
, Assertion
(i)
is equivalent to the fact that
\pi_{0}(f)^{-1}(-)
induces a bijection between the open and closed subsets of
\pi_{0}(Y)
and of
\pi_{0}(X)
. This is clearly the case if
\pi_{0}(f)
is a homeomorphism. This shows that
(iii)
implies
(i)
and that the converse follows from the following lemma.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map of pro-finite spaces such that
f^{-1}(-)
induces a bijection between the open and closed subsets of
Y
and of
X
. Then
f
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
f
is bijective. Let us show that
f
is injective. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
with
f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})=:y
. As each singleton in a pro-finite space is the intersection of its open and closed neighborhoods, it suffices to show that every open and closed neighborhood
A
of
x_{1}
is also an open and closed neighborhood of
x_{2}
. By hypothesis, there exists an open and closed neighborhood
B\subseteq Y
of
y
with
f^{-1}(B)=A
. Hence
A
contains also
x_{2}
.

It remains to show that
f
is surjective. Let
y\in Y
and write
\{y\}=\bigcap_{y\in B}B
as the intersection of the open and closed neighborhoods
B
of
y
. The
B
’s form a cofiltered system and
f^{-1}(B)
is non-empty by hypothesis since
B
is non-empty. Hence
f^{-1}(y)=\bigcap_{y\in B}f^{-1}(B)\neq\emptyset
because
X
is quasi-compact.
∎

Abstract

Introduction

Stephen Lichtenbaum, in a series of papers

[

,
28
,

]

was further studied by Geisser

[

,

,

. Morin defined

The aim of this paper is to remove the assumption that

Notation and conventions

Arithmetic schemes.

In this work, an
arithmetic scheme
is a scheme
X
that is separated
and of finite type over
\Spec\mathbb{Z}
.

Abelian groups.

Let

We denote by

(resp.

(resp. maximal torsion subgroup), and by

A/A_{\text{\it tor}}

We say that

Complexes.

All our constructions take place in the derived category of abelian groups

{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
. For our purposes, we introduce the following terminology. Recall first
that a complex of abelian groups

is
perfect

bounded (i.e.

Definition 1.1

A complex of abelian groups

2
-torsion in arbitrarily high degree.
That is,

A complex of abelian groups

A complex of abelian groups

This terminology is ad hoc and was invented for this text, since such complexes

Étale cohomology.

For an arithmetic scheme

Motivic cohomology
H^{i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))
.

Our construction is based on motivic cohomology defined in terms of complexes
of sheaves
\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)
on
X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
. We follow the notation of

[
16
]
.

Briefly, for
i\geq 0
we consider the algebraic simplex

\Delta^{i}=\Spec\mathbb{Z}[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i}]/(\sum_{i}t_{i}-1).

We fix a negative weight
n\leq 0
. Let
z_{n}(X,i)
be the free abelian group
generated by the closed integral subschemes
Z\subset X\times\Delta^{i}
of
dimension
n+i
that intersect the faces properly. Then
z_{n}(X,\bullet)
is
a (homological) complex of abelian groups whose differentials are given by the
alternating sum of intersections with the faces. We consider the (cohomological)
complex of étale sheaves

\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=z_{n}(\text{\textvisiblespace},-\bullet)[2n].

The boundedness from below of
\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)
is not known in general; it is a
variant of the Beilinson–Soulé vanishing conjecture. To work
unconditionally with the derived functors, we use
K
-injective resolutions

[
39
,
37
]
(resp.
K
-flat resolutions for the derived
tensor products).

To avoid any confusion, we use cohomological numbering for all complexes
in this paper, so we set

H^{i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=H^{i}(R\Gamma(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))).

(
[
16
]
uses homological numbering.)

If
X
is
proper, regular and of pure dimension
d
, then for
n\leq 0

there exists an isomorphism

(1)

H^{i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))\cong H^{2d+i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(d-n)),

where the right-hand side is the “usual” motivic cohomology defined for
positive weights; see the original Bloch’s paper
[
5
]
for the case
of varieties, and also
[
11
,
13
]
for the
definitions and properties over
\Spec\mathbb{Z}
.

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n)),R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n)))\\
\to\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n)),R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n)))

induced by the composition with

R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\to R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
, is mono, and
therefore

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n)),R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n)))\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\\
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n)),R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n)))\otimes\mathbb{Q}

is also mono. However,
u_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
by
Theorem
II
, and this implies that
i_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
.
∎

We are now ready to define the Weil-étale complexes.

Definition 7.5
.

We let

R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
be an object in the derived category

{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
which is a mapping fiber of
i_{\infty}^{*}
:

R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\to R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\xrightarrow{i_{\infty}^{*}}R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))\to R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))[1]

The
Weil-étale cohomology with compact support
is given by

H_{\text{\it W,c}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=H^{i}(R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))).

Remark 7.6
.

Note that this defines
R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
up to a non-unique isomorphism
in
{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
, and the groups
H_{\text{\it W,c}}^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
are also defined
up to a non-unique isomorphism. In a continuation of this paper we will make
use of the determinant
\det\nolimits_{\mathbb{Z}}R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
in the
sense of
[
26
]
, which will be defined up to a canonical
isomorphism.

However, we recall from Proposition
5.6
that

R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
is defined up to a unique isomorphism in the
derived category
{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
. If we could define

i_{\infty}^{*}\colon R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\to R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))
as an explicit, genuine morphism of complexes (not just as a morphism
in the derived category
{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
), this would give us a canonical and functorial
definition for
R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
.

Case of varieties over finite fields

For varieties over finite fields, our Weil-étale cohomology has a simple
description, and it is
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
-dual to the arithmetic homology studied by
Geisser in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 7.7
.

If
X
is a variety over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then assuming

\mathbf{L}^{c}(X,n)
, there is an isomorphism of complexes

(13)

R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\cong R\!\Hom(R\Gamma(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Z}[-1]),

and an isomorphism of finite groups

\displaystyle H^{i}_{W,c}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom(H^{2-i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom(H_{i-1}^{c}(X_{\text{\it ar}},\mathbb{Z}(n)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
H_{\bullet}^{c}(X_{\text{\it ar}},\mathbb{Z}(n))
are the arithmetic homology groups
defined in
[
15
, §3]
.

Proof..

Under our assumptions,
X(\mathbb{C})=\emptyset
, and therefore

R\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))=0
, so that

R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\cong R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
. Finally, by
Proposition
5.4
, we have an isomorphism

R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\cong R\!\Hom(R\Gamma(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Z}[-1])
. We recall from
Proposition
4.2
that the
groups
H^{i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))
are finite under our assumption.

To relate this to Geisser’s arithmetic homology, according to

[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, there is a long exact
sequence

\cdots\to H_{i-1}^{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\to H_{i}^{c}(X_{\text{\it ar}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\to CH_{n}(X,i-2n)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to H_{i-2}^{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\to\cdots

Here the homological notation means that

\displaystyle H_{i}^{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))

\displaystyle=H^{-i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),

\displaystyle CH_{n}(X,i-2n)_{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle=H_{i}^{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Q}(n))=0,

and therefore

H_{i}^{c}(X_{\text{\it ar}},\mathbb{Z}(n))\cong H^{1-i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)).

Now (
13
) gives

E_{2}^{p,q}=\Ext_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(H^{1-q}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Z})\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{W,c}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n)),

and again, by finiteness of
H^{1-q}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n))
, this spectral
sequence is concentrated in
p=1
, where the interesting terms are

\Ext_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}(H^{1-q}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Z})\cong\Hom(H^{1-q}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

so that

H^{1+i}_{W,c}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\cong\Hom(H^{1-i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\cong\Hom(H_{i}^{c}(X_{\text{\it ar}},\mathbb{Z}(n)),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).\qed

Perfectness of the complex

Our next aim is to verify that
R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
is a perfect
complex. From now on we tacitly assume Conjecture
\mathbf{L}^{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},n)
.

Lemma 7.8
.

The groups
H^{i}_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
are finitely generated for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof..

In the long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{i-1}_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))\to H^{i}_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\to H^{i}_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))\\
\xrightarrow{H^{i}(i_{\infty}^{*})}H^{i}_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))\to\cdots

the groups
H^{i}_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))
and
H^{i}_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
are
finitely generated by
Proposition
3.2
, and
Proposition
5.5
, respectively.
This implies the finite generation of
H^{i}_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
.
∎

Lemma 7.9
.

One has
H^{i}_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))=0
for
i<0
.

Proof..

The definitions of
R\Gamma_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
and
R\Gamma_{\text{\it W,c}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))

yield exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i-1}_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}(n))}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\text{\it fg}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(H^{1-i}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}^{c}(n)),\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i+1}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}(n))}

If

.

One has

for

Appendix A
Some homological algebra

This appendix contains some basic results about the derived category of abelian
groups
{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}
which are used throughout the text. The following lemmas are
isolated from the proofs in
[
10
]
, with some modifications to
treat the
2
-torsion.

First, recall that every complex of abelian groups
A^{\bullet}
(not necessarily
bounded) is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology:

A^{\bullet}\cong\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{i}(A^{\bullet})[-i]\cong\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{i}(A^{\bullet})[-i]\\
=\Bigl(\cdots\to H^{i-1}(A^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{0}H^{i}(A^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{0}H^{i+1}(A^{\bullet})\to\cdots\Bigr).

Here
\coprod
and
\prod
denote the coproduct and product in the category of
complexes, which coincide in this case. This gives us a useful expression for
morphisms in the derived category: since

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(A,B[i])\cong\Ext_{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(A,B)
, and
\Ext_{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}(A,B)=0
for

i>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{i}(A^{\bullet})[-i],\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{j}(B^{\bullet})[-j])

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{j}(B^{\bullet})[i-j])

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\Hom(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i}(B^{\bullet}))\oplus\Ext(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet}))\right)

(18)

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i}(B^{\bullet}))\oplus\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Ext(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet})).

Lemma A.1
.

1)

If
C^{\bullet}
and
C^{\prime\bullet}
are almost perfect in the sense of
Definition
1.1
, then the group

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(C^{\bullet},C^{\prime\bullet})
has no nontrivial
divisible subgroups.

2)

If
A^{\bullet}
is a complex such that
H^{i}(A^{\bullet})
are
finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces and
C^{\bullet}
is a complex such
that

are finitely generated abelian groups, then the group

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(A^{\bullet},C^{\bullet})

Proof..

In 1), we consider the decomposition (

) for

\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(C^{\bullet},C^{\prime\bullet})
, and observe that under
our assumptions, both groups

, where

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose that
A^{\bullet}
and
B^{\bullet}
are almost of cofinite type in the
sense of Definition
1.1
. Then a morphism

f\colon A^{\bullet}\to B^{\bullet}
is torsion (i.e. a torsion element in the
group
\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet})
, i.e.
f\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
) if
and only if the morphisms
H^{i}(f)\colon H^{i}(A^{\bullet})\to H^{i}(B^{\bullet})

are torsion; that is, they are trivial on the maximal divisible subgroups:

(H^{i}(f)_{\text{\it div}}\colon H^{i}(A^{\bullet})_{\text{\it div}}\to H^{i}(B^{\bullet})_{\text{\it div}})=0.

Proof..

Under our assumptions, the groups
H^{i}(A^{\bullet})
and

H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet})
appearing in (
18
) are of
cofinite type. We calculate that in this case,

\Ext(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet}))
is finite.

For
i\gg 0
, the groups
H^{i}(A^{\bullet})
and
H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet})
are
finite
2
-torsion, and therefore

\Ext(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet}))
is finite
2
-torsion as
well. It follows that the whole product

\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Ext(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i-1}(B^{\bullet}))
is of
the form

, where
G
is finite and
T
is (possibly infinite)

2
-torsion. We have therefore
(G\oplus T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
.

Similarly, the group

\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i}(B^{\bullet}))
consists of some
part of the form
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r}\oplus G
, where
G
is finite,
and some possibly infinite
2
-torsion part, which is killed by tensoring
with
\mathbb{Q}
. It follows from (
18
) that there is an
isomorphism

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet})\otimes\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\cong\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom(H^{i}(A^{\bullet}),H^{i}(B^{\bullet}))\otimes\mathbb{Q},

\displaystyle f\otimes\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\mapsto(H^{i}(f)\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.\qed

Cohomology with compact support

For any arithmetic scheme
f\colon X\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}
there exists a

Nagata compactification

[
7
,

]

(see also
[
2
, Exposé XVII]
)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec\mathbb{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where
j
is an open immersion and
g
is a proper morphism.

Definition B.1
.

Let
X
be an arithmetic scheme and let
\mathcal{F}
be an abelian torsion
sheaf on
X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
. Then one defines the

cohomology with compact support
of
\mathcal{F}
via the complex

R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R\Gamma(\mathfrak{X}_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},j_{!}\mathcal{F}).

For torsion sheaves, this does not depend on the choice of

j\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{X}
, but here we would like to fix this
choice in order to compare cohomology with compact support on
X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
with
the singular cohomology with compact support on
X(\mathbb{C})
.

Comparison with the analytic cohomology

Definition B.2
.

Given a Nagata compactification
j\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{X}
,
we consider the corresponding open immersion

j(\mathbb{C})\colon X(\mathbb{C})\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{C})
,
and for a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X(\mathbb{C})
we define

\Gamma_{c}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Gamma(\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{C}),j(\mathbb{C})_{!}\mathcal{F}).

Similarly, for a
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariant sheaf on
X(\mathbb{C})
we define

\Gamma_{c}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Gamma(G_{\mathbb{R}},\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{C}),j(\mathbb{C})_{!}\mathcal{F}).

The canonical reference for the comparison between étale and singular
cohomology is
[
2
, Exposé XI, §4]
, so we borrow some definitions
and notations from there. Let
X
be an arithmetic scheme.

1.

The base change from
\Spec\mathbb{Z}
to
\Spec\mathbb{C}
gives us a morphism of
sites

\gamma\colon X_{\mathbb{C},\text{\it\'{e}t}}\to X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}.

2.

Let
X_{\text{\it cl}}
be the site of étale maps

f\colon U\to X(\mathbb{C})
. A covering family in
X_{\text{\it cl}}
is a family of
maps
\{U_{i}\to U\}
such that
U
is the union of images of
U_{i}
.

(We recall that in the analytic topology,
f\colon U\to X(\mathbb{C})
is
étale

if it is a
local on the source homeomorphism
: for each
u\in U
there
exists an open neighborhood
u\ni V
such that

\left.f\right|_{V}\colon V\to f(V)
is a homeomorphism.)

Since the inclusion of an open subset
U\subset X(\mathbb{C})
is an étale map,
we have a fully faithful functor
X(\mathbb{C})\subset X_{\text{\it cl}}
, and the
topology on
X(\mathbb{C})
is induced by the topology on
X_{\text{\it cl}}
. This
gives us a morphism of sites
\delta\colon X_{\text{\it cl}}\to X(\mathbb{C})
, which
by the comparison lemma
[
2
, Exposé III, Théorème 4.1]

induces an equivalence of the corresponding categories of sheaves

\delta_{*}\colon\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{\it cl}})\to\mathbf{Sh}(X(\mathbb{C})).

3.

A morphism of schemes

over

is
étale if and only if the map

is
étale
[

, Exposé XII, Proposition 3.1]
, and therefore the
functor
X^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightsquigarrow X^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
gives us a morphism of sites

\epsilon\colon X_{\text{\it cl}}\to X_{\mathbb{C},\text{\it\'{e}t}}.

Definition B.3
.

We define the functor

\alpha^{*}\colon\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}})\to\mathbf{Sh}(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}))

via the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\mathbb{C},\text{\it\'{e}t}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{\it cl}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Sh}(X(\mathbb{C}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{*}}

As we start from a scheme over

Lemma B.4
.

\alpha^{*}

Proof..

\alpha^{*}
is the composition of the inverse image functors

and

(which are left adjoint) and an equivalence

Proposition B.5
.

Given a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
, there exists a natural morphism

\Gamma(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\to\Gamma(G_{\mathbb{R}},X(\mathbb{C}),\alpha^{*}\mathcal{F}),

Proof..

If

is a Nagata compactification, we

j(\mathbb{C})\colon X(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{C})

-equivariant sheaves: if

is a

-equivariant sheaf on

is a
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariant sheaf on

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Sh}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}})}

Modified étale cohomology

Here we briefly review the

modified étale cohomology with compact support

R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},-)
. It was introduced by Th. Zink in

[
22
, Appendix 2]
for the case of number rings

X=\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
, and it is also discussed in

[
33
, §II.2]
. The general definition for
X\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}

is treated in
[
10
, §6.7]
and

[
18
, §2]
.

Thanks to the Leray spectral sequence

R\Gamma(\mathfrak{X}_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},-)\cong R\Gamma(\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},-)\circ Rg_{*}
,
we have

R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R\Gamma(\mathfrak{X}_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},j_{!}\mathcal{F})\cong R\Gamma((\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},Rf_{!}\mathcal{F}),\quad\text{where }Rf_{!}\mathcal{F}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=Rg_{*}j_{!}\mathcal{F}.

First we recall that for a finite group
G
and a
G
-module
A
the
corresponding group cohomology
H^{i}(G,A)
(resp. Tate cohomology

\widehat{H}^{i}(G,A)
) can be defined in terms of resolutions
P_{\bullet}

(resp. complete resolutions
\widehat{P}_{\bullet}
) of
\mathbb{Z}
by free

\mathbb{Z}G
-modules (see e.g.
[
6
, Chapter VI]
). More
generally, if
A^{\bullet}
is a bounded (cohomological) complex of

G
-modules, we obtain a
double complex
of abelian groups

\Hom^{\bullet\bullet}(P_{\bullet},A^{\bullet})
(resp.
\Hom^{\bullet\bullet}(\widehat{P}_{\bullet},A^{\bullet})
), and it makes sense to define the
corresponding
group hypercohomology

(resp.
Tate hypercohomology
) via the complexes

R\Gamma(G,A^{\bullet})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Tot^{\oplus}(\Hom^{\bullet\bullet}(P_{\bullet},A^{\bullet})),\quad R\widehat{\Gamma}(G,A^{\bullet})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Tot^{\oplus}(\Hom^{\bullet\bullet}(\widehat{P}_{\bullet},A^{\bullet})).

Now if
\mathcal{F}
is an abelian sheaf on
(\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
, then the
corresponding
modified cohomology with compact support
is characterized
by the distinguished triangle

R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}((\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\to R\Gamma((\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\to R\widehat{\Gamma}(G_{\mathbb{R}},v^{*}\mathcal{F})\to R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}((\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})[1]

Here
v\colon\Spec\mathbb{R}\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}
is the canonical morphism, and

v^{*}\mathcal{F}
is the corresponding sheaf on
(\Spec\mathbb{R})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
, which can be
viewed as a
G_{\mathbb{R}}
-module by
[
2
, Exposé VII, 2.3]
, and

R\widehat{\Gamma}(G_{\mathbb{R}},v^{*}\mathcal{F})
denotes the corresponding Tate
cohomology.

In general, given an arithmetic scheme
X\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}
and a torsion abelian
sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}}
, we choose a Nagata compactification as above and
set

R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}((\Spec\mathbb{Z})_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},Rf_{!}\mathcal{F}).

We have a natural morphism

R\widehat{\Gamma}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\to R\Gamma_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F}),

which is an isomorphism if
X(\mathbb{R})=\emptyset
. In general, Tate cohomology

\widehat{H}^{i}(G_{\mathbb{R}},-)
is annihilated by multiplication by
2=\#G_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and therefore

\widehat{H}^{i}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})\to H^{i}_{c}(X_{\text{\it\'{e}t}},\mathcal{F})
has

2
-torsion kernel and cokernel.

For canonicity and functoriality, I refer to
[
18
, §2]
.

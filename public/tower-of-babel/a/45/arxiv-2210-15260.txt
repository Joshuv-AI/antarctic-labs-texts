An algebraic treatment of the Pastro polynomials on the real line
Vutha Vichhea Chea, Luc Vinet, Meri Zaimi, Alexei Zhedanov

Abstract
The properties of the Pastro polynomials on the real line are studied with the help of a triplet of q q -difference operators. The q q -difference equation and recurrence relation these polynomials obey are shown to arise as generalized eigenvalue problems involving the triplet of operators, with the Pastro polynomials as solutions. Moreover, a discrete biorthogonality relation on the real line for the Pastro polynomials is obtained and is then understood using adjoint operators. The algebra realized by the triplet of q q -difference operators is investigated.



1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to study the bispectrality and biorthogonality of the Pastro polynomials on the real line using a set of
q
-difference operators with bidiagonal actions.

The Pastro polynomials were introduced in
[
13
]
as an infinite family of biorthogonal polynomials on the complex unit circle with respect to the
q
-beta distribution (see also
[
1
]
). They are expressed in terms of a two-parameter
q
-hypergeometric series of type
{}_{2}\phi_{1}
. Their biorthogonality in the case where
q
is a root of unity has been studied in
[
26
]
. The Pastro polynomials belong to the class of Laurent biorthogonal polynomials, which can be characterized by a three-term recurrence relation of the form
[
8
,
9
,
18
,
26
]

P_{n+1}(z)+\mu_{n}^{(1)}P_{n}(z)=z(P_{n}(z)+\mu_{n}^{(2)}P_{n-1}(z)).

(1.1)

In addition to this recurrence relation, they also satisfy a
q
-difference equation that can be recovered from that of the little
q
-Jacobi polynomials through a reparametrization which depends on the degree
n

[
11
]
. Therefore, the Pastro polynomials are said to be bispectral, in a generalized sense.

Past developments have shown that biorthogonal functions with bispectral properties can be understood algebraically in terms of operators in a way analogous to the classical orthogonal polynomials (OPs) of the (
q
-)Askey scheme. Indeed, all classical OPs satisfy both a recurrence relation and a (
q
-)difference or differential equation that can be viewed as two eigenvalue problems for two operators
X
and
Y
which act in a tridiagonal fashion either on the degree or the variable
[
11
]
. The OPs then arise as solutions to these eigenvalue problems. The pair of bispectral operators
X,Y
realizes (a specialization of) the Askey–Wilson algebra
[
25
]
and is known as a Leonard pair in the finite dimensional case
[
14
]
. The OPs appear as overlap coefficients in the representation theory of their associated algebra. In the case of biorthogonal functions, one deals instead with a triplet of operators
X,Y,Z
and the bispectrality is understood in terms of two generalized eigenvalue problems (GEVPs) involving the pairs
X,Y
and
X,Z

[
27
]
. This algebraic approach has been used for studying several families of bispectral biorthogonal functions: the Hahn and
q
-Hahn rational functions
[
16
,
21
,
4
]
, the Askey polynomials on the unit circle
[
22
]
and the
R_{I}
polynomials of Hahn type
[
17
]
. The goal of this paper is to adopt a similar approach for understanding the properties of the Pastro polynomials. We will be interested here to the case where the domain is restricted to the real line instead of being the complex plane. This will lead to a different biorthogonality relation for the Pastro polynomials in comparison to previous works.

The rest of the paper continues as follows. Three
q
-difference operators, that act bidiagonally on the monomial basis, are introduced in Section
2
. A generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP) generated by a pair of these operators is considered in Section
3
. Its polynomial solutions will be shown to be given by the Pastro polynomials. The bispectral properties of the Pastro polynomials are obtained in Section
4
using the actions of the three
q
-difference operators. Then a discrete biorthogonality relation on the real line for these polynomials is obtained in Section
5
via two methods. The first method makes use of Baxter’s recurrence system
[
3
]
while the second method involves a pair of operators and their adjoints. Finally, the algebra realized by the triplet of
q
-difference operators is investigated in Section
6
. A connection with the
q
-Hahn algebra is made explicit. Section
7
consists of concluding remarks.

2
A triplet of
q
-difference operators

We begin with the introduction of three
q
-difference operators, crucial for studying the properties of the Pastro polynomials. Throughout this paper,
q
is assumed generic and real.

Let
a,b
be two real parameters. We define the following operators acting on the space
{\mathcal{M}}
of real functions
f(x)
depending on a real variable
x
:

\displaystyle X^{(a,b)}=(x-q)T^{-}+(q-bx){\mathcal{I}},

(2.1)

\displaystyle Y^{(a,b)}=(x-a^{-1}q)T^{+}+(a^{-1}q-b^{-1}x){\mathcal{I}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle Z^{(a,b)}=(1-qx^{-1})T^{-}+(qx^{-1}-b){\mathcal{I}}.

(2.3)

The notations
T^{\pm}
and
{\mathcal{I}}
are used to denote the
q
-shift operators and the identity operator respectively. They are defined as

T^{\pm}f(x)=f(q^{\pm 1}x),\quad{\mathcal{I}}f(x)=f(x).

(2.4)

For convenience, we will omit the dependencies of the triplet of operators on the parameters
a,b
and write
X,Y,Z
instead.
From the explicit expressions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), it is obvious that

Z=x^{-1}X.

(2.5)

In what follows, we will consider more specifically the subspace of functions
f(x)\in{\mathcal{M}}
which are polynomials. A basis of interest for this subspace is the monomial basis

\varphi_{n}(x)=x^{n},

(2.6)

where
n
is any non-negative integer. All operators
X
,
Y
and
Z
act bidiagonally on this basis:

\displaystyle X\varphi_{n}(x)=(q^{-n}-b)\varphi_{n+1}(x)+q(1-q^{-n})\varphi_{n}(x),

(2.7)

\displaystyle Y\varphi_{n}(x)=(q^{n}-b^{-1})\varphi_{n+1}(x)+a^{-1}q(1-q^{n})\varphi_{n}(x),

(2.8)

\displaystyle Z\varphi_{n}(x)=(q^{-n}-b)\varphi_{n}(x)+q(1-q^{-n})\varphi_{n-1}(x).

(2.9)

It is observed that the operators
X,Y,Z
raise the degree of any polynomial by at most one, and hence belong to the class of generalized Heun operators
[
2
,
20
]
.

3
Pastro polynomials as solutions of a generalized eigenvalue problem

In this section, we demonstrate how the Pastro polynomials arise as solutions of a GEVP built from the pair of operators
X
and
Y
. We use the standard notations for the
q
-hypergeometric function
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
and the
q
-Pochhammer symbol
(z;q)_{n}
, see
[
6
,
11
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The normalized polynomial solutions
P_{n}(x)
of the GEVP

YP_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}XP_{n}(x)

(3.1)

are given by the Pastro polynomials

P_{n}(x)=P_{n}(x;a,b;q)=\frac{(a^{-1}bq^{1-n};q)_{n}(q;q)_{n}}{(q^{-n};q)_{n}(b;q)_{n}}\ _{2}\phi_{1}\left({q^{-n},b\atop a^{-1}bq^{1-n}};q,x\right)

(3.2)

with eigenvalues

\lambda_{n}=-b^{-1}q^{n},

(3.3)

for
n=0,1,2,\dots

Proof.

Any polynomial solution to equation (
3.1
) can be expanded in the basis of the monomials
\varphi_{k}(x)
. Therefore, one can take

P_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}C_{n,k}\ \varphi_{k}(x),

(3.4)

where
n
is a non-negative integer and
C_{n,k}
are some coefficients. The expansion (
3.4
) and the bidiagonal actions (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) of
X
and
Y
can be used to develop each side of equation (
3.1
) in terms of the basis functions
\varphi_{k}(x)
. Equalizing the coefficients of the terms proportional to
\varphi_{n+1}(x)
, one finds that the associated eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
must satisfy equation (
3.3
). The other terms lead to the following two-term recurrence relation for the coefficients
C_{n,k}
, for
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
:

(1-q^{k-n})(1-bq^{k})C_{n,k}=(1-a^{-1}bq^{k+1-n})(1-q^{k+1})C_{n,k+1}.

(3.5)

Note that the explicit expression (
3.3
) of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
has been used to obtain (
3.5
). The solution to this recurrence relation is found to be

C_{n,k}=\frac{(q^{-n};q)_{k}(b;q)_{k}}{(a^{-1}bq^{1-n};q)_{k}(q;q)_{k}}C_{n,0}.

(3.6)

The solution (
3.2
) then follows by substituting (
3.6
) into the expansion (
3.4
), and by choosing the normalization constant
C_{n,0}
so as to provide monic condition

P_{n}(x)=x^{n}+O(x^{n-1}).

(3.7)

∎

Using the explicit expression (
3.2
) and the actions (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), it can be shown that the triplet of operators
X,Y,Z
have the effect of producing a
q
-shift on the paramater
b
when acting on the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x;a,b;q)
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle XP_{n}(x;a,b;q)=q^{-n}(1-bq^{n})xP_{n}(x;a,bq;q),

(3.8)

\displaystyle YP_{n}(x;a,b;q)=-b^{-1}(1-bq^{n})xP_{n}(x;a,bq;q),

(3.9)

\displaystyle ZP_{n}(x;a,b;q)=q^{-n}(1-bq^{n})P_{n}(x;a,bq;q).

(3.10)

Remark 3.2
.

The actions (
3.8
)–(
3.10
) can be written in terms of the transformation

{\mathcal{S}}:b\mapsto bq

(3.11)

by replacing the polynomials
P_{n}(x;a,bq;q)
which appear on the RHSs by
{\mathcal{S}}P_{n}(x;a,b;q)
.

4
Bispectrality

Let us now show how the bispectrality of the Pastro polynomials is expressed through two GEVPs: a
q
-difference equation in the variable
x
with eigenvalue depending on the degree
n
and a recurrence relation in the degree
n
with eigenvalue depending on the variable
x
.

4.1
q
-Difference equation

Consider again the GEVP (
3.1
):
YP_{n}=\lambda_{n}XP_{n}
. We have shown in Section
3
that the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x)
arise as solutions to this GEVP with eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}=-b^{-1}q^{n}
. Observe that this is consistent with the actions (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) of the operators
X
and
Y
on
P_{n}(x)
. Using the defining expressions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) of the operators
X
and
Y
in the GEVP (
3.1
), one gets

(x-a^{-1}q)P_{n}(qx)+(a^{-1}q-b^{-1}x)P_{n}(x)=-b^{-1}q^{n}\left((x-q)P_{n}(q^{-1}x)+(q-bx)P_{n}(x)\right),

(4.1)

which is a second order
q
-difference equation for the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x)
.

4.2
Recurrence relation

We already know that the operators
X
,
Y
and
Z
all act bidiagonally in the monomial basis
\varphi_{n}(x)
. Consider now the action of these operators on the basis of the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x)
. From the contiguity relations of the
{}_{2}\phi_{1}
series in particular, one finds that the actions (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) can be re-expressed as follows:

\displaystyle XP_{n}(x)=q^{-n}(1-bq^{n})P_{n+1}(x)+q(1-a^{-1}bq^{-n})P_{n}(x),

(4.2)

\displaystyle ZP_{n}(x)=q^{-n}(1-bq^{n})P_{n}(x)+\frac{bq(1-q^{-n})(1-aq^{n-1})}{a(1-bq^{n-1})}P_{n-1}(x).

(4.3)

Note that the action of
Y
on the basis
P_{n}(x)
is deduced from (
4.2
) using the GEVP (
3.1
).
Applying both sides of equality (
2.5
) on the polynomials
P_{n}(x)
, we get

ZP_{n}(x)=x^{-1}XP_{n}(x).

(4.4)

Then, using the actions (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) of the operators
X
and
Z
, equation (
4.4
) can be written as

P_{n+1}(x)+\mu_{n}^{(1)}P_{n}(x)=x\left(P_{n}(x)+\mu_{n}^{(2)}P_{n-1}(x)\right),

(4.5)

where the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{(1)}
and
\mu_{n}^{(2)}
are given by

\mu_{n}^{(1)}=-\frac{q(b-aq^{n})}{a(1-bq^{n})},\quad\mu_{n}^{(2)}=-\frac{bq(1-q^{n})(1-aq^{n-1})}{a(1-bq^{n})(1-bq^{n-1})}.

(4.6)

Equation (
4.5
) is a three-term recurrence relation for the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x)
. Note the presence of the term proportional to
P_{n-1}(x)
with a factor
x
which does not appear for classical OPs but which is expected for Laurent biorthogonal polynomials
[
8
,
9
,
26
]
.

We thus see that both the
q
-difference equation (
4.1
) and the recurrence relation (
4.5
) of the Pastro polynomials can be understood through two GEVPs involving the pairs of operators
X,Y
and
X,Z
; this is how the triplet
X,Y,Z
accounts for the bispectrality of the Pastro polynomials. Note that in order to view (
4.4
) as a GEVP, one should define
X
and
Z
as operators acting on the degree
n
using (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

5
Biorthogonality

This section is devoted to the proof of the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and take the parameter
a
to be

a=q^{1-N}.

(5.1)

Define moreover the following
q
-grid on the real line:

x_{s}=q^{s+1},\quad s=0,1,\dots,N-1.

(5.2)

Then, the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x;a,b;q)
satisfy the biorthogonality relation

\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w_{s}P_{n}(x_{s})R_{m}(x_{s})=h_{n}\delta_{mn},\quad m,n=0,1,\dots,N-1,

(5.3)

where the weight function
w_{s}
and normalization constant
h_{n}
are given by

\displaystyle w_{s}=\frac{1}{(b;q)_{N-1}}\frac{(q^{1-N};q)_{s}}{(q;q)_{s}}(bq^{N-1})^{s},

(5.4)

\displaystyle h_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n}}{(ab^{-1}q;q)_{n}(b;q)_{n}},

(5.5)

and where the normalized biorthogonal partners
R_{n}(x)
are given by the Laurent polynomials

R_{n}(x)=R_{n}(x;a,b;q)=\frac{(q^{-n};q)_{n}(bq^{-1};q)_{n}}{(a^{-1}bq^{-n};q)_{n}(q;q)_{n}}\ _{2}\phi_{1}\left({q^{-n},ab^{-1}q\atop b^{-1}q^{2-n}};q,\frac{q^{2}}{ax}\right).

(5.6)

In Subsection
5.1
, the discrete biorthogonality relation for the Pastro polynomials on the real line as described in Proposition
5.1
will be obtained in a systematic manner by making use of a recurrence system proposed by G. Baxter. Then, with hindsight from the known result, the case
m\neq n
of this biorthogonality relation will be recovered in Subsection
5.2
using the pair of
q
-difference operators

and the properties of their adjoints.

5.1
Derivation from recurrence system

As mentioned in the introduction, the Pastro polynomials are an example of polynomials satisfying a biorthogonality relation on the unit circle. In consequence, they should obey the following recurrence system proposed by G. Baxter
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle P_{n+1}(x)=xP_{n}(x)-\alpha_{n}x^{n}Q_{n}(x^{-1}),

(5.7)

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}(x)=xQ_{n}(x)-\beta_{n}x^{n}P_{n}(x^{-1}),

(5.8)

\displaystyle P_{0}(x)=Q_{0}(x)=1,

(5.9)

where
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
are the recurrence coefficients. This system involves two sets of polynomials, namely the Pastro polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and some polynomials
Q_{n}(x)
. We can define the constant
h_{n}
as follows

h_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}),\quad h_{0}=1.

(5.10)

For
N
any positive integer, consider the conditions

\displaystyle h_{n}\neq 0,\quad h_{N}=0,\quad\text{for }n=0,1,\dots,N-1,

(5.11)

\displaystyle P_{N}(x_{s})=0,\quad P_{N}^{\prime}(x_{s})\neq 0,\quad\text{for }s=0,1,\dots,N-1,

(5.12)

where
x_{s}
are all the zeros of the polynomial
P_{N}(x)
. It has been shown in
[
28
]
that if conditions (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) are satisfied in addition of equations (
5.7
)–(
5.9
), then the following biorthogonality relation holds for

\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w_{s}P_{n}(x_{s})Q_{m}(x_{s}^{-1})=h_{n}\delta_{mn},

(5.13)

where the normalization constant
h_{n}
is given in (

) and the weight function
w_{s}
is

w_{s}=\frac{h_{N-1}}{P_{N}^{\prime}(x_{s})Q_{N-1}(x_{s}^{-1})}.

Our goal for the remainder of this subsection is to find explicit expressions for the objects which appear in (
5.13
), hence leading to the biorthogonality relation of Proposition
5.1
.

We begin by finding the recurrence coefficients
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
of Baxter’s system (
5.7
)–(
5.9
). The first relation (
5.7
) can be solved for

Q_{n}(x^{-1})=\frac{xP_{n}(x)-P_{n+1}(x)}{\alpha_{n}x^{n}}.

Substituting (

) into the second relation (

), one gets the following three-term recurrence relation

P_{n+1}(x)-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n-1}}P_{n}(x)=x\left(P_{n}(x)+\left(\alpha_{n}\beta_{n-1}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\alpha_{n-1}}\right)P_{n-1}(x)\right).

(5.16)

Comparing (
5.16
) with the recurrence relation (
4.5
) of the Pastro polynomials, one deduces

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=-\alpha_{n-1}\mu_{n}^{(1)},

(5.17)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=\frac{\mu_{n+1}^{(2)}-\mu_{n+1}^{(1)}}{\alpha_{n+1}},

where the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{(1)}

\mu_{n}^{(2)}

For the coefficient

The explicit expressions of the normalization constant

) and (

)

It is observed from (

5.2
Derivation from operators

We will now recover the biorthogonal partners

of the Pastro polynomials that are given in (

) using the

-difference operators
X
and
Y
. We will restrict for the remainder of the present subsection the parameter
a
to be as in (

), with

to take values only on the

-grid

Let us equip the space of real Laurent polynomials

(f(x_{s}),g(x_{s}))_{(b)}=\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w_{s}^{(b)}f(x_{s})g(x_{s}),

P_{n}(x_{s})
. We shall now obtain the explicit form of

by making use of the GEVP (
3.1
) and the properties of the adjoint operators

and
Y^{*}
contained in the adjoint GEVP (

). Before we begin, note that under the constraint (
5.1
) and the restriction of

-grid (

), the operators

and

become

\displaystyle X=q(q^{s}-1)T^{-}+q(1-bq^{s}){\mathcal{I}},

(5.28)

\displaystyle Y=(q^{s+1}-q^{N})T^{+}+(q^{N}-b^{-1}q^{s+1}){\mathcal{I}},

(5.29)

with
T^{\pm}f(x_{s})=f(x_{s\pm 1})

, using the convention
f(x_{-1})=f(x_{N})=0
. Also note that equation (

XP_{n}(x_{s})=(q^{-n}-b)q^{s+1}{\mathcal{S}}P_{n}(x_{s}),

where we recall that the transformation
{\mathcal{S}}
is defined in (

Let us start by finding the expressions of the adjoint shift operators
(T^{\pm})^{*}

\displaystyle(T^{+})^{*}=\frac{w_{s-1}}{w_{s}}T^{-}=\frac{q(1-q^{s})}{b(q^{N}-q^{s})}T^{-},

(5.31)

\displaystyle(T^{-})^{*}=\frac{w_{s+1}}{w_{s}}T^{+}=\frac{b(q^{N}-q^{s+1})}{q(1-q^{s+1})}T^{+}.

(5.32)

Using the restricted forms (

5.32

\displaystyle X^{*}=b(q^{s+1}-q^{N})T^{+}+q(1-bq^{s}){\mathcal{I}},

(5.33)

\displaystyle Y^{*}=b^{-1}q(q^{s}-1)T^{-}+(q^{N}-b^{-1}q^{s+1}){\mathcal{I}}.

(5.34)

Consider now the transformation

\displaystyle\tau:

\displaystyle b\mapsto b^{-1}q^{1-N},

(5.35)

\displaystyle s\mapsto N-s-1,

\displaystyle T^{\pm}\mapsto T^{\mp}.

One can observe that the action of
\tau
on
b
and
s
is equivalent to the action of
{\mathcal{S}}\circ{\mathcal{T}}
, see equations (

) and (
5.22
), and that
\tau^{2}
is the identity transformation. Note that
\tau
has been chosen so that for any
q
-difference operator
W
and any function

, the following relation holds

\tau(Wf(x_{s}))=\tau(W)\tau(f(x_{s})).

(5.36)

By applying the transformation
\tau
to the operators
X
and

\tau(X)

\tau(Y)

\displaystyle X^{*}=-bq^{s}\tau(X),

(5.37)

\displaystyle Y^{*}=-b^{-1}q^{s+1-N}\tau(Y).

(5.38)

On one hand, relations (
5.37
) and (

\tau(Y)P_{n}^{*}(x_{s})=-bq^{n+N-1}\tau(X)P_{n}^{*}(x_{s}).

(5.39)

On the other hand, one can apply the transformation

\tau(Y)\tau(P_{n}(x_{s}))=\tau(\lambda_{n})\tau(X)\tau(P_{n}(x_{s})).

Using again the expression of the eigenvalues

\tau(\lambda_{n})=-bq^{n+N-1},

(5.41)

\tau(X)

P_{n}^{*}(x_{s})=\nu_{n}\tau(P_{n}(x_{s})),

6
Pastro algebra

In the case of the classical orthogonal polynomials of the (
q
-)Askey scheme, it is known that the associated pair of bispectral operators satisfy an algebra of Askey–Wilson type
[
25
]
. In this section, we identify the algebra generated by the triplet of bispectral operators of the Pastro polynomials; we will call this the Pastro algebra. Connections with the Askey–Wilson algebra and its specialization the
q
-Hahn algebra will be made.

By direct computation using definitions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
), it is found that the operators
X,Y,Z
obey the following relations

\displaystyle qXY-YX=q(q-1)\left(a^{-1}X+Y\right),

(6.1)

\displaystyle qYZ-ZY=(q-1)\left(-b^{-1}q^{-1}(1+q)X-bY+a^{-1}qZ+a^{-1}b^{-1}(1-b)(a-bq)\right),

(6.2)

\displaystyle qZX-XZ=(q-1)\left(-bX+qZ\right).

(6.3)

Under the affine transformations

\displaystyle X\mapsto\frac{a}{bq(q-1)}X-\frac{a}{b(q-1)},

(6.4)

\displaystyle Y\mapsto bq^{-1}Y-a^{-1}b,

(6.5)

\displaystyle Z\mapsto-a^{-1}Z-a^{-1}b,

(6.6)

the relations (
6.1
)–(
6.3
) become

\displaystyle qXY-YX=1,

(6.7)

\displaystyle qYZ-ZY=\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2},

(6.8)

\displaystyle qZX-XZ=1,

(6.9)

where

\alpha_{1}=a^{-2}bq^{-1}(q-1)^{2}(q+1),\quad\alpha_{2}=a^{-2}q^{-1}(q-1)(ab+aq+bq).

(6.10)

The Pastro algebra can then be observed to be a special case of the Askey–Wilson algebra
[
25
]
. We comment on this below.

The usual Casimir element of the Askey–Wilson algebra (see
[
19
]
for instance) takes the following form in the specialization (
6.7
)–(
6.9
):

Q=(q^{-2}-1)XYZ+q^{-1}\alpha_{1}X^{2}+q^{-1}(q^{-1}+1)(\alpha_{2}X+q^{-1}Y+Z).

(6.11)

In other words, the element
Q
given above is central in the Pastro algebra, as can be verified directly using relations (
6.7
)–(
6.9
).

6.1
The Askey–Wilson algebra and biorthogonal functions

Let us recall the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
presentation of the Askey–Wilson algebra
[
23
,
10
,
5
]
:

\displaystyle qXY-YX=\beta_{1}Z+\beta_{2},

(6.12)

\displaystyle qYZ-ZY=\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2},

(6.13)

\displaystyle qZX-XZ=\delta_{1}Y+\delta_{2},

(6.14)

These relations can be standardized by affine transformations in non-singular parametric situations. Manifestly the algebra associated to the Pastro polynomials and defined by (
6.7
)–(
6.9
) is a special case of the Askey–Wilson algebra with
\beta_{1}=0,\beta_{2}=1
and
\delta_{1}=0
,
\delta_{2}=1
. A first significant observation is that the Askey–Wilson algebra (and its degenerations) does not only provide algebraic interpretations of the orthogonal polynomials of the Askey scheme but also, as is the case here, of biorthogonal polynomials.

As we shall indicate in the next subsection, the situation where either
\beta_{1}
or
\delta_{1}
is equal to
0
(but not both) corresponds to the algebra describing the bispectrality of the
q
-Hahn polynomials. It is hence tempting to ask to what orthogonal polynomials does correspond the algebra defined by (
6.7
)–(
6.9
) where both
\beta_{1}
and
\delta_{1}
are equal to
0
. It is interesting to realize that this is an ill-founded question and that there are no finite families of orthogonal polynomials whose bispectrality is encoded in this algebra. Indeed, discussing orthogonal polynomials in this algebraic framework, one looks at representations in bases where one of the generator is diagonal. (Recall that we have considered here in contradistinction generalized eigenbases.) On the one hand, the relations (
6.7
) and (
6.9
) are each recognized as defining a
q
-oscillator algebra. It is known
[
12
,
15
]
, that in any finite-dimensional irreducible representation of this algebra, the generators have a spectrum of exponential type
q^{\pm{k}}
and such must hence be the case for
X,Y,Z
in view of (
6.7
) and (
6.9
). On the other hand, given (
6.8
), when
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
, the representation theory of the Askey–Wilson algebra
[
25
]
shows that in finite dimensions
Z
must have a spectrum of the form
c_{1}q^{k}+c_{2}q^{-k}
where the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are such that
c_{1}c_{2}\neq 0
. This is in contradiction with the preceding assertion. Note however that this conundrum does not arise when
\alpha_{1}=0
or in the case of the
q
-Hahn algebra to be discussed below where
\beta_{1}
or
\delta_{1}
is
\neq 0
and there is only one
q
-oscillator relation.

6.2
Connection with the
q
-Hahn algebra

One distinctive feature of the Pastro algebra is that it contains the
q
-Hahn algebra. The
q
-Hahn algebra is associated to the
q
-Hahn polynomials and is defined by three generators
A
,
B
and
C
subject to the relations

\displaystyle qAB-BA=C+\gamma_{1},

(6.15)

\displaystyle qBC-CB=\gamma_{2},

(6.16)

\displaystyle qCA-AC=\gamma_{3}B+\gamma_{4},

(6.17)

where
\gamma_{i}
for
i=1,2,3,4
are arbitrary real constants. In the realization of the
q
-Hahn algebra by bispectral operators, these coefficients
\gamma_{i}
take explicit values involving the parameters of the
q
-Hahn polynomials. In what follows, it will be useful to view the coefficients
\gamma_{i}
as central elements instead.

Let us now describe the embedding of the
q
-Hahn algebra into the Pastro algebra by introducing the linear pencil

L=X+\mu Y,

(6.18)

where
\mu
is an arbitrary real parameter. We define an operator
M
in terms of the operators
L
and
Z
as follows:

M=qLZ-ZL-\mu\alpha_{2}-1.

(6.19)

It can be verified that the elements
L,M,Z
together with the central element
Q
given in (
6.11
) generate an algebra with the following relations:

\displaystyle qLZ-ZL=M+\mu\alpha_{2}+1,

(6.20)

\displaystyle qZM-MZ=\mu\alpha_{1},

(6.21)

\displaystyle qML-LM=\mu q^{-1}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)Z-\mu q^{2}(q-1)Q+\mu^{2}\alpha_{1}.

(6.22)

Comparing (
6.15
)–(
6.17
) with (
6.20
)–(
6.22
), it is then seen that the
q
-Hahn algebra is embedded in the Pastro algebra under the mappings

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\mapsto\mu\alpha_{2}+1,\quad\!\gamma_{2}\mapsto\mu\alpha_{1},\quad\!\gamma_{3}\mapsto\mu q^{-1}(q+1)^{2}(q-1),\quad\!\gamma_{4}\mapsto-\mu q^{2}(q-1)Q+\mu^{2}\alpha_{1},

(6.23)

\displaystyle A\mapsto L,\quad B\mapsto Z,\quad C\mapsto M.

(6.24)

7
Conclusion

In summary, we have introduced a triplet of
q
-difference operators that plays a role similar to the pair of bispectral operators of the classical OPs, but for biorthogonal polynomials instead. We have then shown through two GEVPs how these operators provide a description of the bispectral property of the Pastro polynomials. We have also shown how to obtain a discrete biorthogonality relation of the Pastro polynomials on the real line using two methods; the first is by exploiting a recurrence system proposed by G. Baxter and the second is purely with the use of a pair of operators and the properties of their adjoints. The underlying algebra of the Pastro polynomials, called Pastro algebra, was then obtained and observed to be a special case of the Askey–Wilson algebra. A connection with the
q
-Hahn algebra has been established by showing its embedding into the Pastro algebra.

One natural direction for future research would be to consider the Pastro algebra from a more abstract point of view and study its representation theory, in a similar manner as in
[
21
,
22
]
. It is expected that the Pastro biorthogonal polynomials will appear as overlap coefficients between bases associated to eigenvalue or generalized eigenvalue problems. Another direction would be to pursue the program of providing an algebraic treatment of other families of biorthogonal functions such as those given in
[
7
,
24
]
. The next step could be to examine the
{}_{4}\phi_{3}
level. Finally, relating both directions, the presence of the Askey–Wilson algebra in this larger picture of bispectral biorthogonal functions would merit more investigations. We hope to study these aspects in the future.

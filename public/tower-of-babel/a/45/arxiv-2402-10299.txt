Stability for the 3D Riemannian Penrose inequality
Conghan Dong

Abstract
. We show that Schwarzschild manifold is stable for the 3 3 -dimensional Riemannian Penrose inequality in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology modulo negligible spikes and boundary area perturbations.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete, smooth, asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and ADM mass
m(g)
whose outermost minimal boundary has total surface area
A
. Then the (Riemannian) Penrose inequality states that
m(g)\geq\sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}}
, with equality if and only if
(M^{3},g)
is isometric to the Schwarzschild
3
-manifold of mass
m(g)
. This theorem was proved by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
HI01
]
in the case that the boundary is connected, and by Bray
[
Bra01
]
in the general case. See also
[
BL09
,
KWY17
,
AMMO22
]
etc. for additional extensions and generalizations. As a corollary, Penrose inequality implies the Positive Mass theorem that
m(g)\geq 0
, which was firstly proved by Schoen-Yau
[
SY79
]
. See also other generalizations
[
Wit81
,
SY81
]
etc.

A natural question for the Penrose inequality to then ask is whether the almost equality would imply that the manifold is close to the Schwarzschild manifold in some topology. This is called the stability problem for the Penrose inequality. Recently, there has been a growing interest in studying similar stability problems. Notably, the author and Antoine Song proved a stability result for the Positive Mass theorem, see
[
DS23
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we prove that, up to boundary area perturbations, the Schwarzschild manifold is stable for the Penrose inequality in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology modulo negligible spikes (c.f. Definition
2.3
). More precisely, we have the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A_{0}\geq 0
be a fixed constant and
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i})
be a sequence of asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds, each of which has nonnegative scalar curvature and a compact connected outermost minimal boundary with area
A_{0}
. Suppose that the ADM mass
m(g_{i})
converges to
\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}
, then for all
i
, there is a smooth submanifold
N_{i}\subset M_{i}
such that
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial N_{i})\to A_{0}
, a spike domain
Z_{i}\subset N_{i}
with smooth boundary such that
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial Z_{i})\to 0
, and for
\check{N}_{i}:=N_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
,

(\check{N}_{i},\hat{d}_{g_{i},\check{N}_{i}},p_{i})\to(M_{sc}^{3},g_{sc},x_{o})

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology, and

\displaystyle(\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i},\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}})\to(\partial M_{sc}^{3},\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}^{3}})

in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology,where
(M_{sc}^{3},g_{sc},x_{o})
is the standard Schwarzschild
3
-manifold with boundary area
\mathrm{Area}(\partial M^{3}_{sc})=A_{0}
, mass
m(g_{sc})=\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}
and
x_{o}\in\partial M_{sc}^{3}
,
\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}=\partial N_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
, and
\hat{d}_{g_{i},\cdot}
are the length metrics on the corresponding spaces induced by
g_{i}
respectively, and
p_{i}
is any base point on
\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}
(see Figure
1
for a simplified geometric picture).

We also have a similar stability result for the mass-capacity inequality. Recall that for an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)
with outermost minimal boundary
\Sigma
and an end
\infty_{1}
, the capacity of
\Sigma
in
(M^{3},g)
is defined by

\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g):=\inf\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}:\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\text{ on }\Sigma,\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}\varphi(x)=1\}.

Then, as a corollary of the Positive Mass theorem, it was shown by
[
Bra01
, Theorem 9]
that
m(g)\geq 2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)
, and equality holds if and only if
(M^{3},g)
is the Schwarzschild manifold. For more details, please refer to Section
3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m_{0}>0
be a fixed constant and
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i})
be a sequence of asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds, each of which has nonnegative scalar curvature and a compact connected outermost minimal boundary. Suppose that
m(g_{i})-2\mathcal{C}(\partial M_{i},g_{i})\to 0
and
m(g_{i})\to m_{0}
, then for all
i
, there is a smooth submanifold
N_{i}\subset M_{i}
such that
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial N_{i})\to 16\pi m_{0}^{2}
, a spike domain
Z_{i}\subset N_{i}
with smooth boundary such that
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial Z_{i})\to 0
, and for
\check{N}_{i}:=N_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
,

(\check{N}_{i},\hat{d}_{g_{i},\check{N}_{i}},p_{i})\to(M_{sc}^{3},g_{sc},x_{o})

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology, and

\displaystyle(\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i},\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}})\to(\partial M_{sc}^{3},\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}^{3}})

in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology, where
(M_{sc}^{3},g_{sc},x_{o})
is the standard Schwarzschild
3
-manifold with boundary area
\mathrm{Area}(\partial M^{3}_{sc})=16\pi m_{0}^{2}
, mass
m(g_{sc})=m_{0}

and
x_{o}\in\partial M_{sc}^{3}
,
\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}=\partial N_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
, and
\hat{d}_{g_{i},\cdot}
are the length metrics on the corresponding spaces induced by
g_{i}
respectively, and
p_{i}
is any base point on
\partial^{*}\check{N}_{i}
(see Figure
1
for a simplified geometric picture).

Figure 1.
This simplified picture visually illustrates the conclusions in the above theorems. The original manifold
M
is represented in black and blue. The removed spikes
Z
are shown in blue, with a very small boundary area.
\check{N}
refers to the region below the red minus blue parts, and
\partial^{*}\check{N}
corresponds to the red part, resembling an almost standard sphere with some minor handles attached and a few small disks or annuluses removed.

Our stability results can be taken as generalizations of the main theorem in
[
DS23
]
. In particular, the case when
A_{0}=0
in Theorem
1.1
was proved in
[
DS23
]
. Our proof to the main theorems will be built on the tools developed in
[
DS23
]
as well as some new techniques.

We give a remark about the topology used in these main theorems. It would be an interesting question to prove similar results for other topology. See for example
[
LS14
,
LNN20
,
KKL21
,
Don22
]
etc. and the references therein for related results.
A key step in our proof is that we can develop
W^{1,2}
-convergence along the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence modulo negligible spikes (c.f. Section
6
). This type of analysis has been studied extensively in cases where the Ricci curvature has a uniform lower bound, see among others
[
CC96
,
Gig15
]
etc. So our analysis can be viewed as a generalization from Ricci curvature to scalar curvature, which may have its own interests.

We also give a remark on the boundary area perturbations in the conclusions. Notice that this does not mean that the new boundary
\partial^{*}\check{N}
is inside a very small neighborhood of the original boundary
\partial M
. In
[
MS15
]
, for some non-standard metric on
2
-sphere
(S^{2},g)
, Mantoulidis-Schoen constructed a sequence of asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i})
with nonnegative scalar curvature such that
\partial M_{i}^{3}
is isometric to
(S^{2},g)
and minimal, and ADM mass
m(g_{i})\to\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Area}_{g}(S^{2})}{16\pi}}
. Also, outside a neighborhood of
\partial M_{i}
, which could be an arbitrarily long neck,
g_{i}
is isometric to a Schwarzschild metric with mass
m(g_{i})
. That is, we need to cut out the long necks in these examples such that the remaining part has almost the same boundary area and is stable for the Penrose inequality. Such phenomena coincide with our stability results. In particular, the submanifold
\check{N}_{i}
in our stability results corresponds to the region where
g_{i}
is the Schwarzschild metric constructed in Mantoulidis-Schoen’s examples.

Outline of proof

We will mainly focus on the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Up to a boundary area perturbation, Theorem
1.1
will be a corollary of Theorem
1.2
by using an argument in
[
Bra01
, Section 7]
.

Assume the conditions stated in Theorem
1.2
, i.e.,
(M^{3},g)
is an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and a connected outermost minimal boundary
\Sigma
such that
m(g)-2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)\ll 1
. By employing a doubling technique and allowing for a perturbation as in
[
Bra01
]
, we can assume that
\bar{M}=M\cup_{\Sigma}M
is a smooth asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and two ends
\infty_{1},\infty_{2}
.
Then the infimum in the definition of capacity is achieved by the Green’s function
f
defined on
\bar{M}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}f=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}f(x)=1,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty_{2}}f(x)=0.

From symmetry of
\bar{M}
,
f

equals
\frac{1}{2}
on
\Sigma
. We now consider the conformal metric
h=f^{4}g
on
\bar{M}
. Then
\infty_{2}
can be compactified such that
\bar{M}^{*}:=\bar{M}\cup\{\infty_{2}\}
is a smooth manifold and
(\bar{M}^{*},h)
is an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature. Also it can be shown that the ADM mass
m(h)=m(g)-2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)
as in
[
Bra01
]
.

Based on our assumptions,
m(h)\ll 1
, the case discussed in
[
DS23
]
. So modulo negligible spikes,
(\bar{M}^{*},h)
is close to the Euclidean
3
-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology. For the original metric
g=f^{-4}h
, we will show that
f
is uniformly close to the conformal factor in the Schwarzschild metric in the following two steps.

We first prove a new integral inequality involving scalar curvature and the hessian of
f
(c.f. Proposition
4.1
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{8\pi(m(g)^{2}-(2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g))^{2})}{m(g)^{2}}\geq\\
&\ \ \int_{M}\left(\frac{|\nabla^{2}f-(1-f)^{-1}f^{-1}(2f-1)|\nabla f|^{2}(g-3\nu\otimes\nu)|^{2}}{|\nabla f|}+R_{g}|\nabla f|\right)\mathrm{dvol}_{g},\end{split}

where
\nu=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
and the integration is taken over regular set of
f
.
This formula is very similar to the mass inequality proved by
[
BKKS22
, Theorem 1.2]
.
One can follow the proof of
[
BKKS22
, Theorem 1.2]
and employ the technique of
integration over level sets of
f
to control the integral in above inequality.
This method was initially explored by
[
Ste22
]
,
and there have been many other applications and generalizations in recent studies, including
[
BHK+23
,
AMO21
,
AMMO22
]
and others.
But by this argument, we can only get an integral inequality with a coarse upper bound.
To obtain the final effective estimate, we study a weighted volume, and use the nonnegative scalar curvature conditions together with ODE comparison to prove a weighted volume comparison (c.f. Lemma
4.3
), which finally implies the desired estimate. As a corollary, we also get a mass-area-capacity inequality (c.f. Proposition
4.5
).

Then by using the integral inequality of
f
, together with the techniques used in
[
DS23
]
, we are able to find a region
\mathcal{E}\subset\bar{M}^{*}
with a small area boundary. In this region
\mathcal{E}
, the behavior of
|\nabla f|
closely resembles the conformal factor in the Schwarzschild metric, and particularly
|\nabla f|
is uniformly bounded. By Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, along the convergence of
(\mathcal{E},h)
, we can take a limit of such
f
and get a limit function
f_{\infty}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, it is not immediately clear whether
f_{\infty}
is precisely the conformal factor in the Schwarzschild metric.
To address this, we note that a favorable property of the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence modulo negligible spikes, is that
f
also converges to
f_{\infty}
in the
W^{1,2}
-sense (c.f. Lemma
6.1
). Therefore, the elliptic equations satisfied by
f
are preserved in this convergence, and
f_{\infty}
also satisfies an elliptic equation on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The fact that the area of the boundary
\partial\mathcal{E}
converges to
0
has been used essentially here. The elliptic equation satisfied by
f_{\infty}
together with some growth conditions would imply the rigidity of
f_{\infty}
(c.f. Section
6
).

Finally, using metric geometry tools and similar arguments in
[
DS23
, Section 4]
, we can utilize these properties of
f
and
h
to prove the main theorems.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

We will use
C,C^{\prime}
to denote a universal positive constant (which may be different from line to line);
\Psi(t),\Psi(t|a,b,\cdots)
denote small constants depending on
a,b,\cdots
and satisfying

\lim_{t\to 0}\Psi(t)=0,\ \lim_{t\to 0}\Psi(t|a,b\cdots)=0,

for each fixed
a,b,\cdots
.

We denote the Euclidean metric by
g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}
or
\delta
, and the induced geometric quantities with subindex
\mathrm{Eucl}
or
\delta
.

For a general Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and any
\pi\in M
, the geodesic ball with center
p
and radius
r
is denoted by
B_{g}(p,r)
or
B(p,r)
if the underlying metric is clear. Given a Riemannian metric, for a surface
\Sigma
and a domain
\Omega
,
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)
is the area of
\Sigma
,
\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)
is the volume of
\Omega
with respect to the metric.

Finally we introduce some notations about length metric that will be used later.
Given a subset
U
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, let
(U,\hat{d}_{g,U})
be the induced length metric on
U
of the metric
g
, that is, for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in U
,

\hat{d}_{g,U}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\inf\{L_{g}(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a rectifiable curve connecting }x_{1},x_{2}\text{ and }\gamma\subset U\},

where
L_{g}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}|_{g}
is the length of
\gamma
with respect to metric
g
. For any
D>0
and
p\in U
, we use
\hat{B}_{g,U}(p,D)
to denote the geodesic ball inside
(U,\hat{d}_{g,U})
, that is

\hat{B}_{g,U}(p,D):=\{x\in U:\hat{d}_{g,U}(p,x)\leq D\}.

2.2.
Geometry of Schwarzschild metric

For a positve number
m>0
, the Schwarzschild
3
-manifold
(M^{3}_{sc},g_{sc})
(with mass
m
) is given by the following warped product metric on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,\infty)
:

(1)

\displaystyle g_{sc}=ds^{2}+u_{m}(s)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},\ \ s\in[0,\infty),

where
g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
is the spherical metric with
\mathrm{Area}(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})=4\pi
, and
u_{m}
is a positive increasing function satisfying

(2)

\displaystyle u_{m}(0)=2m,\ u_{m}^{\prime}(0)=0,\ u_{m}^{\prime}(s)=\left(1-\frac{2m}{u_{m}(s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ u_{m}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{m}{u_{m}(s)^{2}}.

Then the scalar curvature of
g_{sc}
is identically zero, and the boundary
\Sigma_{sc}:=\partial M^{3}_{sc}
is the only minimal surface inside
M^{3}_{sc}
.

Under Cartesian coordinate,
M^{3}_{sc}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(\frac{m}{2})
, where
B(\frac{m}{2})
is the Euclidean ball with radius
\frac{m}{2}
around the center, and we have

(3)

\displaystyle g_{sc,ij}(x)=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{4}\delta_{ij},\ \forall|x|\geq\frac{m}{2}.

This metric can also be extended to give a Schwarzschild metric
g_{sc}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.

Define
\rho_{m}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,\Sigma_{sc})
. Then

\rho_{m}(x)=\int_{\frac{m}{2}}^{|x|}\left(1+\frac{m}{2t}\right)^{2}dt=|x|-\frac{m^{2}}{4|x|}+m\log\frac{2|x|}{m},

which implies that

(4)

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(x)-m\log\frac{2\rho_{m}(x)}{m}\leq|x|\leq\rho_{m}(x).

Using these two representations (
1
) and (
3
) of
g_{sc}
to compute the area of geodesic spheres, we have

u_{m}(\rho_{m}(x))^{2}\cdot 4\pi=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{4}\cdot 4\pi|x|^{2},

i.e.

(5)

\displaystyle u_{m}(\rho_{m}(x))=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{2}\cdot|x|.

In particular, together with (
4
),

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{u_{m}(r)}{r}=1.

Now we introduce another harmonic function
f_{m}
and rewrite above identities using
f_{m}
instead of
|x|
. Define

\displaystyle f_{m}(x):=\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|}\right)^{-1}.

Standard computations imply that

\Delta_{g_{sc}}f_{m}=0,\ \ f_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\text{ on }\Sigma_{sc},\ \ \lim_{|x|\to\infty}f_{m}(x)=1.

Then

\displaystyle|x|=\frac{m}{2}\cdot\frac{f_{m}(x)}{1-f_{m}(x)},

and

(7)

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(x)=\rho_{m}(f_{m}(x))=\frac{m}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-f_{m}(x)}-\frac{1}{f_{m}(x)}\right)+m\log\frac{f_{m}(x)}{1-f_{m}(x)}.

Moreover,

(8)

\displaystyle u_{m}(\rho_{m}(x))=\frac{m}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{f_{m}(x)(1-f_{m}(x))}.

2.3.
Asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds

A smooth orientable connected complete Riemannian
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)
is called asymptotically flat if there exists a compact subset
K\subset M
such that
M\setminus K=\sqcup_{k=1}^{N}M_{\mathrm{end}}^{k}
consists of finite pairwise disjoint ends, and for each
1\leq k\leq N
, there exist
C>0,\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, and a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi_{k}:M_{\mathrm{end}}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(1)
such that under this identification,

|\partial^{l}(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\sigma-|l|},

for all multi-indices
|l|=0,1,2
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(1)
.
Furthermore, we always assume the scalar curvature
R_{g}
is integrable over
(M^{3},g)
. The ADM mass from general relativity of each end
M_{\mathrm{end}}^{k}
,
1\leq k\leq N
, is then well-defined (see
[
ADM61
,
Bar86
]
) and given by

m_{k}(g):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S_{r}}\sum_{i}(g_{ij,i}-g_{ii,j})\nu^{j}dA

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal to the coordinate sphere
S_{r}
of radius
|x|=r
in the given end, and
dA
is its area element.

Definition 2.1
.

A surface
\Sigma\subset(M^{3},g)
is called a horizon if it is a minimal surface. It is called an outermost horizon if it is a horizon and it is not enclosed by another minimal surface in
(M^{3},g)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold. By Lemma 4.1 in
[
HI01
]
, we know that inside
M^{3}
, there is a trapped compact region
T
whose topological boundary consists of smooth embedded minimal
2
-spheres. An “exterior region”
M^{3}_{ext}
is defined as the metric completion of any connected component of
M\setminus T
containing one end. Then
M^{3}_{ext}
is connected, asymptotically flat, has a compact minimal boundary
\partial M^{3}_{ext}
(
\partial M^{3}_{ext}
may be empty), and contains no other compact minimal surfaces, that is,
M^{3}_{ext}
is an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with outermost horizon boundary.

We will be able to perturb an asymptotically flat metric to a metric with nicer behavior at infinity in each end because of the following definition and proposition.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
(M^{3},g)
is harmonically flat at infinity if
(M^{3}\setminus K,g)
is isometric to a finite disjoint union of regions with zero scalar curvature which are conformal to
(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B,\delta)
for some compact set
K
in
M^{3}
and some ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
centered around the origin.

By definition, if
(M^{3},g)
is harmonically flat, then on each end,
g_{ij}(x)=V(x)\delta_{ij}
for some bounded positive
\delta
-harmonic function
V(x)
, which satisfies that
\Delta_{\delta}V(x)=0
and (c.f.
[
Bra01
, Equation (10)]
)

(9)

\displaystyle V(x)=a+\frac{b}{|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right).

In this case, its ADM mass on this end is given by
2ab
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
SY81
]
)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete, asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with
R_{g}\geq 0
and ADM mass
m_{k}(g)
in the
k
-th end. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a metric
\hat{g}
such that
e^{-\epsilon}g\leq\hat{g}\leq e^{\epsilon}g
,
R_{\hat{g}}\geq 0
,
(M^{3},\hat{g})
is harmonically flat at infinity, and
|m_{k}(\hat{g})-m_{k}(g)|\leq\epsilon
, where
m_{k}(\hat{g})
is the ADM mass of
\hat{g}
in the
k
-th end.

2.4.
pm-GH convergence modulo negligible spikes

In this subsection, we recall some definitions for the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology.

Assume
(X,d_{X},x),(Y,d_{Y},y)
are two pointed metric spaces.
The pointed Gromov-Hausdorff (or pGH-) distance is defined in the following way. A pointed map
f:(X,d_{X},x)\to(Y,d_{Y},y)
is called an
\varepsilon
-pointed Gromov-Hausdorff approximation (or
\varepsilon
-pGH approximation) if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

f(x)=y
;

(2)

B(y,\frac{1}{\varepsilon})\subset B_{\varepsilon}(f(B(x,\frac{1}{\varepsilon})))
;

(3)

|d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))|<\varepsilon
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x,\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
.

The pGH-distance is defined by

\displaystyle d_{pGH}((X,d_{X},x),

\displaystyle(Y,d_{Y},y)):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\varepsilon>0:\exists\ \varepsilon\text{-pGH approximation }f:(X,d_{X},x)\to(Y,d_{Y},y)\}.

We say that a sequence of pointed metric spaces
(X_{i},d_{i},p_{i})
converges to a pointed metric space
(X,d,p)
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology, if the following holds

d_{pGH}((X_{i},d_{i},p_{i}),(X,d,p))\to 0.

If
(X_{i},d_{i})
are length metric spaces, i.e. for any two points
x,y\in X_{i}
,

d_{i}(x,y)=\inf\{L_{d_{i}}(\gamma):\gamma\text{ is a rectifiable curve connecting }x,y\},

where
L_{d_{i}}(\gamma)
is the length of
\gamma
induced by the metric
d_{i}
, then equivalently,

d_{pGH}((X_{i},d_{i},p_{i}),(X,d,p))\to 0

if and only if for all
D>0
,

d_{pGH}((B(p_{i},D),d_{i}),(B(p,D),d))\to 0,

where
B(p_{i},D)
are the geodesic balls of metric
d_{i}
.

A pointed metric measure space is a structure
(X,d_{X},\mu,x)
where
(X,d_{X})
is a complete separable metric space,
\mu
a Radon measure on
X
and
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
.

We say that a sequence of pointed metric measure length spaces
(X_{i},d_{i},\mu_{i},p_{i})
converges to a pointed metric measure length space
(X,d,\mu,p)
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff (or pm-GH) topology, if for any
\varepsilon>0,D>0
, there exists
N(\varepsilon,D)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that for all
i\geq N(\varepsilon,D)
, there exists a Borel
\varepsilon
-pGH approximation

f_{i}^{D,\varepsilon}:(B(p_{i},D),d_{i},p_{i})\to(B(p,D+\varepsilon),d,p)

satisfying

(f_{i}^{D,\varepsilon})_{\sharp}(\mu_{i}|_{B(p_{i},D)})\text{ weakly converges to }\mu|_{B(p,D)}\text{ as }i\to\infty,\text{ for }a.e.D>0.

In the case when
X_{i}
is an
n
-dimensional manifold, without extra explanations, we will always consider
(X_{i},d_{i},p_{i})
as a pointed metric measure space equipped with the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{d_{i}}
induced by
d_{i}
.

Finally, we introduce a notation about the topology that we used in this paper. For simplicity, we only consider manifolds, and one can easily generalize it to general metric measured spaces.

Definition 2.3
.

For a sequence of pointed Riemannian
n
-manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})
and
(M,g,p)
, we say that
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})
converges to
(M,g,p)
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology modulo negligible spikes, if there exist open subsets
Z_{i}\subset M_{i}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial Z_{i})\to 0
,
p_{i}\in M_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
and

(M_{i}\setminus Z_{i},\hat{d}_{g_{i}},p_{i})\to(M,d_{g},p)

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology for the induced length metric.

3.
Capacity of horizon and Green’s function

In this section, we introduce some properties of capacity and Green’s function, which are known in the literature and will be used later in this paper. Most of this section follows from
[
Bra01
]
.

Let’s firstly introduce the capacity of a surface in the special case when it is the horizon of an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold which is harmonically flat at infinity.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a complete, asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)
with a connected outermost horizon boundary
\Sigma
, nonnegative scalar curvature and one harmonically flat end
\infty_{1}
, the capacity of
\Sigma
in
(M^{3},g)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g):=\inf\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{M^{3}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}:\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\ \varphi=\frac{1}{2}\text{ on }\Sigma,\ \lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}\varphi(x)=1\}.

From standard theory (c.f.
[
Bar86
]
), the infimum in the definition of
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)
is achieved by the Green’s function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M^{3})
which satisfies

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{g}\varphi&=0,\\
\varphi&=\frac{1}{2}\text{ on }\Sigma,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}\varphi(x)&=1.\end{split}

By maximum principle,
\varphi(x)\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
for any
x\in M^{3}
.
Define the level sets of
\varphi
to be

\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}:=\{x\in M^{3}:\varphi(x)=t\}.

Then by Sard’s theorem,
\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}
is a smooth surface for almost all
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. By the co-area formula,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\int_{\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}}|\nabla\varphi|.

For any regular value
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, integrating
\Delta\varphi=0
over
\{\frac{1}{2}\leq\varphi\leq t\}
, and using Stokes’ theorem, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|=\int_{\Sigma^{\varphi}_{t}}|\nabla\varphi|.

So

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|.

Since
(M^{3},g)
is harmonically flat at infinity, we have the following expansion at infinity of the Green’s function (c.f.
[
Bar86
]
):

(13)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=1-\frac{\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)}{|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\text{ as }x\to\infty_{1}.

Now we introduce another definition which is closely related to the capacity of a horizon surface.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a complete, asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(\bar{M}^{3},\bar{g})
with multiple harmonically flat ends and one chosen end
\infty_{1}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\bar{g}):=\inf\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\bar{M}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{\bar{g}}:\phi\in\mathrm{Lip}(\bar{M}),\ \lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}\phi(x)=1,\ \lim_{x\to\{\infty_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}}\phi(x)=0\}.

Similarly the infimum in the definition of
\mathcal{C}(\bar{g})
is achieved by the Green’s function
\phi
which satisfies

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{\bar{g}}\phi&=0,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}\phi(x)&=1,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{k}}\phi(x)&=0\text{ for all }k\geq 2.\end{split}

For a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)
with a connected outermost horizon boundary
\Sigma
, nonnegative scalar curvature and one end
\infty_{1}
, we can take another copy of
(M^{3},g)
and glue them together along the boundary
\Sigma
to get a new metric space
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
. In general,
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
is only a Lipschitz manifold with two asymptotically flat ends
\{\infty_{1},\infty_{2}\}
. From the proof of
[
Bra01
, Theorem 9]
, for any
\delta>0
small enough, we can smooth out
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
and construct a smooth complete
3
-manifold
(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
with nonnegative scalar curvature and two asymptotically flat ends which, in the limit as
\delta\to 0
, approaches
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
uniformly. For reader’s convenience, we recall the details of
[
Bra01
]
in the following.

Let
(M_{1}^{3},g),(M_{2}^{3},g)
be the two copies of
(M^{3},g)
. A first step is to construct a smooth manifold (c.f.
[
Bra01
, Equation (92)]
)

\displaystyle(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\bar{g}_{\delta}):=(M_{1}^{3},g)\sqcup(\Sigma\times(0,2\delta),G)\sqcup(M_{2}^{3},g),

where
\Sigma\times\{0\}
and
\Sigma\times\{2\delta\}
are identified with
\Sigma\subset(M^{3},g)
,
G
is a warped product metric and symmetric about
t=\delta
, and
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}\subset(\Sigma\times(0,2\delta),G)
is totally geodesic. In general, the scalar curvature of
G
only satisfies
R_{G}\geq R_{0}
for some constant
R_{0}\leq 0
independent of
\delta
, and may not be nonnegative.

Then a second step is to take a conformal deformation of
\bar{g}_{\delta}
to get a new metric with nonnegative scalar curvature. Define a smooth function
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
, which equals
R_{0}
in
\Sigma\times[0,2\delta]
, equals
0
for
x
more than a distance
\delta
from
\Sigma\times[0,2\delta]
, takes values in
[R_{0},0]
everywhere and symmetric about
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}
. In particular,
R_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}(x)\geq\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(x)
for any
x\in\tilde{M}_{\delta}
. Define
u_{\delta}(x)
such that (c.f.
[
Bra01
, Equation (101)]
)

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(-8\Delta_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}+\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(x))u_{\delta}(x)&=0,\\
\lim_{x\to\{\infty_{1},\infty_{2}\}}u_{\delta}(x)&=1.\end{split}

Then
u_{\delta}
is a smooth function and satisfies that (c.f.
[
Bra01
, Equation (102)]
)

\displaystyle 1\leq u_{\delta}(x)\leq 1+\epsilon(\delta)

where
\epsilon
goes to
0
as
\delta\to 0
. Define

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\delta}:=u_{\delta}^{4}\cdot\bar{g}_{\delta}.

The scalar curvature of
\tilde{g}_{\delta}
satisfies

\displaystyle R_{\tilde{g}_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=u_{\delta}^{-5}\left(R_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}u_{\delta}-8\Delta_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}u_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=u_{\delta}^{-4}\left(R_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}-\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0.

By definition,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}m(\tilde{g}_{\delta})=m(\bar{g})
and
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{C}(\tilde{g}_{\delta})=\mathcal{C}(\bar{g})
.

To see the relation between
\mathcal{C}(\bar{g})
and
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)
, we define the reflection map

\Phi:M_{1}^{3}\cup_{\Sigma}M_{2}^{3}\to M_{1}^{3}\cup_{\Sigma}M_{2}^{3}

such that for any
x\in M_{1}^{3}
,
\Phi(x)\in M_{2}^{3}
is the same point under the identification
M_{1}^{3}=M_{2}^{3}=M^{3}
,
\Phi^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
and
\Phi|_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Id}
. If
\phi
satisfies (
14
), then
1-\phi\circ\Phi
also satisfies (
14
) and by the uniqueness we have
\phi(x)=1-\phi\circ\Phi(x)
, which implies that
\phi|_{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}
. So
\phi|_{M_{1}^{3}}
also satisfies (
10
), which implies that

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\bar{g})=2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g).

Similarly, we can define the reflection map
\Phi_{\delta}:\tilde{M}_{\delta}\to\tilde{M}_{\delta}
and from the equation (
15
) and the fact that
\bar{g}_{\delta}=\bar{g}_{\delta}\circ\Phi_{\delta},\mathcal{R}_{\delta}=\mathcal{R}_{\delta}\circ\Phi_{\delta}
, we know
u_{\delta}
is also symmetric about
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}
and particularly
\left<\nabla u_{\delta},\vec{n}\right>_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}=0
on
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}
, where
\vec{n}
is the normal vector of
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}\subset(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\bar{g}_{\delta})
. Thus,
\tilde{g}_{\delta}
is symmetric about
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}
and the mean curvature of
\Sigma\times\{\delta\}\subset(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
is

\displaystyle H_{(\Sigma\times\{\delta\},\tilde{g}_{\delta})}=u_{\delta}^{-2}H_{(\Sigma\times\{\delta\},\bar{g}_{\delta})}-2\left<\nabla u_{\delta}^{-2},\vec{n}\right>_{\bar{g}_{\delta}}=0.

Let
(M_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
be one half of
(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
with minimal boundary
\Sigma_{\delta}:=\Sigma\times\{\delta\}
and one asymptotically flat end. Then
(M_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta},\Sigma_{\delta})
converges to
(M^{3},g,\Sigma)
uniformly as
\delta\to 0
.

Without loss of generality, by applying Proposition
2.1
, we can assume that
(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
is also harmonically flat at infinity.

In summary, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given a complete asymptotically flat one-ended
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)
with a connected outermost horizon boundary
\Sigma
and nonnegative scalar curvature, there is a sequence of smooth complete
3
-manifolds
(\tilde{M}^{3}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
, which have nonnegative scalar curvature and two harmonically flat ends, and are symmetric about a minimal surface
\Sigma_{\delta}\subset(\tilde{M}^{3}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
, such that
(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})\to(\bar{M},\bar{g})
and
(M_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})\to(M,g)
uniformly as
\delta\to 0
, where
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
is the doubling of
(M,g)
along the boundary
\Sigma
, and
(M_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
is one half of
(\tilde{M}_{\delta},\tilde{g}_{\delta})
with minimal boundary
\Sigma_{\delta}
.

To conclude this section, we give a remark about the relations between the mass, capacity and boundary area of the outermost horizon. Let’s briefly recall Bray’s proof of the Penrose inequality in
[
Bra01
]
. Given a complete smooth
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g_{0})
with a harmonically flat end, nonnegative scalar curvature, an outermost minimizing horizon
\Sigma_{0}
of total area
A_{0}
and total mass
m_{0}
. Then for all
t\geq 0
, we can construct a continuous family of conformal metrics
g_{t}
on
M^{3}
which are asymptotically flat with nonnegative scalar curvature and total mass
m(t)
. Let
\Sigma(t)
be the outermost minimal enclosure of
\Sigma_{0}
in
(M^{3},g_{t})
. Then
\Sigma(t)
is a smooth outermost horizon in
(M^{3},g_{t})
with area
A(t)
being a constant function about
t
. It was shown that
m(t)
is decreasing. And as
t\to\infty
,
(M^{3},g_{t})
approaches a Schwarzschild manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\},g_{sc})
with total mass
\lim_{t\to\infty}m(t)=\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}.
In particular,
m_{0}\geq\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}
, which proves the Penrose inequality.

If we assume that
m_{0}-\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}\leq\varepsilon\ll 1
, then the total variation of
m(t)
is bounded by
\varepsilon
. From
[
Bra01
, Section 7]
, we know that for a.e.
t
,
m^{\prime}(t)=2(2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma(t),g_{t})-m(t))
(notice that our definition of capacity differs by a scale). Then we can choose a small perturbation, say
t_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\sqrt{\varepsilon})
so that
m(t_{\varepsilon})-2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma(t_{\varepsilon}),g_{t_{\varepsilon}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1
. And as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
g_{t_{\varepsilon}}
approaches uniformly to
g_{0}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{t_{\varepsilon}}}(\Sigma_{t_{\varepsilon}})=\mathrm{Area}_{g_{0}}(\Sigma_{0})
and
|m(g_{t_{\varepsilon}})-m(g_{0})|\leq\Psi(\varepsilon)
.

4.
Integral estimate and weighted volume comparison

In this and the following section, we assume that
(\tilde{M}^{3},g)
is a complete, asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with two harmonically flat ends
\{\infty_{1},\infty_{2}\}
and nonnegative scalar curvature obtained as in Proposition
3.1
. In particular, the topology of
\tilde{M}^{3}
is
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, and there is a minimal surface
\Sigma\subset(\tilde{M}^{3},g)
such that
g
is symmetric about
\Sigma
. Let
(M^{3},g)
be the half of
(\tilde{M}^{3},g)
which contains the end
\infty_{1}
and has minimal boundary
\Sigma
. So
\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to a
2
-sphere,
M^{3}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(1)
, and
(\tilde{M}^{3},g)=(M^{3},g)\cup_{\Sigma}(M^{3,\prime},g)
, where we use
M^{\prime}
to denote a copy of
M
containing the other end
\infty_{2}
.

Let
f(x)
be the solution to (
14
) on
(\tilde{M}^{3},g)
, that is

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{g}f&=0,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}f(x)&=1,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{2}}f(x)&=0.\end{split}

Then
f
is a smooth function satisfying
0<f<1
and the following expansion at infinity (c.f.
[
Bar86
,
Bra01
]
)

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(x)&=1-\frac{c_{1}}{|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\text{ as }x\to\infty_{1},\\
f(x)&=\frac{c_{2}}{|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\text{ as }x\to\infty_{2},\end{split}

where
c_{k}
are positive constants for
k=1,2
. Moreover, for some
\tau\in(0,1)
,

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{j}f(x)&=\frac{c_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\frac{x^{j}}{|x|}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2+\tau}}\right),\\
\partial_{j}\partial_{k}f(x)&=\frac{c_{1}\delta_{jk}}{|x|^{3}}-\frac{3c_{1}}{|x|^{3}}\cdot\frac{x^{j}x^{k}}{|x|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{3+\tau}}\right).\end{split}

By the symmetry of
\tilde{g}
about
\Sigma
, we know that on
(M^{3},g)
,
f
satisfies (
10
), that is

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{g}f&=0,\\
f&=\frac{1}{2}\text{ on }\Sigma,\\
\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}f(x)&=1.\end{split}

So by (
12
) and (
13
),

(21)

\displaystyle c_{1}=\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|.

Similarly, on
(M^{\prime},g)
,
1-f
also satisfies (
10
), so

(22)

\displaystyle c_{2}=\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)=c_{1}.

Since
f
is a proper smooth map, by Sard’s theorem, the regular values of
f
is an open dense subset of
[\frac{1}{2},1)
. For any regular value
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we define

\displaystyle M_{t}:=\{\frac{1}{2}\leq f\leq t\},\ \ \Sigma_{t}:=\{f=t\}.

Notice that by maximum principle, a regular level set
\Sigma_{t}
is connected and separates
\Sigma
from
\infty_{1}
. In particular,
\Sigma_{t}
is a
2
-sphere.

Our main goal in this section is to prove the following quantitative integral estimate. In the proof, we will use the technique of integration over level sets of
f
,
as well as a comparison lemma for weighted volumes (c.f. Lemma
4.3
).

Proposition 4.1
.

We have the following integration inequality for the Green’s function
f
on
(M^{3},g)
:

\displaystyle 8\pi\cdot

\displaystyle\frac{m(g)^{2}-(2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g))^{2}}{m(g)^{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\ \ \int_{M^{3}}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{2}f-f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)|\nabla f|^{2}(g-3\nu\otimes\nu)\right|^{2}}{|\nabla f|}+R_{g}|\nabla f|\right)\mathrm{dvol}_{g},

where
\nu=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
, and the integral is taken over the regular set of
f
.

Proof.

We first smooth
|\nabla f|
by defining for any
\epsilon>0
,

\phi_{\epsilon}:=\sqrt{|\nabla f|^{2}+\epsilon}.

If
\Sigma_{t}
is a regular level set of
f
, then the Gauss-Codazzi equation implies that

\displaystyle R_{g}-2\mathrm{Ric}\left(\nu,\nu\right)=R_{\Sigma_{t}}+|II|^{2}-H^{2},

where
\nu=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
and
II=\frac{\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma_{t}}f}{|\nabla f|},H=\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma_{t}}II
are the second fundamental form and mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
in
(M^{3},g)
respectively.
So

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(R_{g}-R_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla f|^{2}&=2\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}-2|\nabla|\nabla f||^{2}\\
&\ \ \ -(\Delta f)^{2}+2\Delta f\cdot\nabla^{2}f(\nu,\nu)\\
&=2\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)+|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}-2|\nabla|\nabla f||^{2},\end{split}

where we used the equation
\Delta f=0
. Together with the Bochner formula

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}=2|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+2\left<\nabla\Delta f,\nabla f\right>+2\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f),

we have

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+2|\nabla|\nabla f||^{2}+(R_{g}-R_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla f|^{2}.

So at any point on a regular level set
\Sigma_{t}
, we can compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\phi_{\epsilon}&=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-3}||\nabla|\nabla f|^{2}|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+(R_{g}-R_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla f|^{2}\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{3}}|\nabla|\nabla f||^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+(R_{g}-R_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla f|^{2}\right).\end{split}

Notice that the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
is

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma_{t}}=\frac{\Delta f-\nabla^{2}f(\nu,\nu)}{|\nabla f|}=-\frac{\nabla^{2}f(\nu,\nu)}{|\nabla f|}.

Taking integration on
M_{t}
and using integration by parts and co-area formula, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nu\right>&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}R_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{|\nabla f|}{\phi_{\epsilon}}ds,\end{split}

where we have used the fact that
\int_{\Sigma}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nu\right>=-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{H_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}}{\phi_{\epsilon}}=0
since
H_{\Sigma}=0
.

Define the tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:=\nabla^{2}f-f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)|\nabla f|^{2}(g-3\nu\otimes\nu).

Then

(25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}&=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}|\mathcal{T}|^{2}-3\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-2}(1-f)^{-2}(2f-1)^{2}|\nabla f|^{4}\\
&\ \ -3\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f).\end{split}

Notice that

\displaystyle\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}

\displaystyle f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\left<\nabla|\nabla f|^{2},\nabla f\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{div}\left(\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-2}(1-f)^{-2}(2f-1)^{2}|\nabla f|^{4}-\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-2}(1-f)^{-2}(2f-1)^{2}|\nabla f|^{4}-\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{4},

so

(26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}&f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)\\
&=\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)\\
&\ \ -\frac{\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)\\
&\ \ -\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-2}(1-f)^{-2}(2f-1)^{2}|\nabla f|^{4}-2\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{4}.\end{split}

Substituting (
26
) into (
25
), we have

(27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}&=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}|\mathcal{T}|^{2}-3\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla f\right)\\
&\ \ +6\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{4}\\
&\ \ +\frac{3\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f).\end{split}

So (
24
) is equivalent to

(28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nu\right>&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}R_{\Sigma_{s}}ds\\
&\ \ -3\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)|\nabla f|^{3}+6\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{4}\\
&\ \ +\int_{M_{t}}\frac{3\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)\\
&\ \ +\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}R_{\Sigma_{s}}\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}+\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|}ds.\end{split}

For any regular value
t
of
f
,
define

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}(t):=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|.

Notice that by co-area formula, integration by parts and
\Delta f=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nu\right>ds

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{t}}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nabla f\right>

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|-\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|.

So
h_{\epsilon}(t)
can be defined for any
t\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and it is a Lipschitz function with

h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left<\nabla\phi_{\epsilon},\nu\right>\text{ for a.e. }t\in(\frac{1}{2},1).

Together with Gauss-Bonnet formula, we can rewrite (
28
) as

(29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ -4\pi(t-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{3(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|+6\int_{M_{t}}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|^{2}\\
&\ \ +3\epsilon\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)-6\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{2}\\
&\ \ +3\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-3}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)\\
&\ \ +\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{R_{\Sigma_{s}}}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}+\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|}ds\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ -4\pi(t-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{3(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}h_{\epsilon}(t)+6\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\frac{h_{\epsilon}(s)}{s(1-s)}ds\\
&\ \ -6\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{2}\\
&\ \ +3\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-3}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)\\
&\ \ +\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{R_{\Sigma_{s}}}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}+\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|}ds.\end{split}

Using (
19
),
|\nabla f|=\frac{c_{1}}{|x|^{2}}+O(\frac{1}{|x|^{3}})
,
|\nabla^{2}f|=O(\frac{1}{|x|^{3}})
, and (
11
), we notice that

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{M_{t}}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla f|ds

\displaystyle=16\pi\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\frac{1}{1-s}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(g)\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot\log\frac{1}{1-t},

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-3}f^{-1}(1-f)^{-1}(2f-1)|\nabla^{2}f(\nabla f,\nabla f)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{M_{t}}(1-f)^{-1}|\nabla^{2}f|

\displaystyle\leq C(g)\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{1-t},

and by (
23
),
|R_{\Sigma_{t}}|=O((1-t)^{2})
, so

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{|R_{\Sigma_{s}}|}{\phi_{\epsilon}^{2}+\phi_{\epsilon}|\nabla f|}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(g)\sqrt{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{1-t}.

Thus for a.e.
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ -4\pi(t-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{3(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}h_{\epsilon}(t)+6\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\frac{h_{\epsilon}(s)}{s(1-s)}ds-\frac{C(g)\sqrt{\epsilon}}{1-t}.\end{split}

For any regular value
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
of
f
, as in the proof of
[
BKKS22
, Theorem 1.2]
, we can divide the integrals into two disjoint parts such that one is the integral over preimage of an open set containing the critical values of
f
, then letting
\epsilon\to 0
and using Sard’s theorem, we can have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left<\nabla|\nabla f|,\nu\right>

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{t}}\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-4\pi(t-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{3(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla f|^{2}+6\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla f|^{2}ds.

Choose a sequence of regular values
t_{i}\to 1
. Notice that by (
19
),

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{i}\to 1}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{i}}}\left<\nabla|\nabla f|,\nu\right>=\lim_{t_{i}\to 1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}f(\nu,\nu)|}{|\nabla f|}=\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\frac{2c_{1}}{|x|^{3}}}{\frac{c_{1}}{|x|^{2}}}=0,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t_{i}\to 1}\frac{3(2t_{i}-1)}{t_{i}(1-t_{i})}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{i}}}|\nabla f|^{2}=12\pi c_{1}\lim_{t_{i}\to 1}\frac{|\nabla f|}{1-t_{i}}=0.

So we have the following global integral inequality:

Lemma 4.2
.

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\pi\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)+6\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}.\end{split}

It remains to estimate
2\pi-6\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}
.

For any
t\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t):=\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{t}s(1-s)\left(1-\frac{h_{\epsilon}(s)}{4\pi s^{2}(1-s)^{2}}\right)ds.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}\in W^{2,\infty}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=t(1-t)-\frac{h_{\epsilon}(t)}{4\pi t(1-t)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=1-2t-\frac{h_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{4\pi t(1-t)}-\frac{(2t-1)h_{\epsilon}(t)}{4\pi t^{2}(1-t)^{2}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(\frac{1}{2})=0,\ \ \mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|\sqrt{|\nabla f|^{2}+\epsilon}=:a_{\epsilon}.

So (
30
) implies that

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{(1-t)^{2}}&\geq\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\frac{2(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{6}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}\\
&\ \ \ +\frac{1}{8\pi t(1-t)}\int_{M_{t}}\phi_{\epsilon}^{-1}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\frac{2(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{6}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon},\end{split}

where we have used that
R_{g}\geq 0
in the last inequality.

Consider the solution
\mathcal{U}
of the equation

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\frac{2(2t-1)}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{6}{t(1-t)}\mathcal{U}(t)=\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{(1-t)^{2}},

satisfying initial conditions

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\frac{1}{2})=\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(\frac{1}{2}),\ \mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})=\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}).

A general solution to (
33
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(t)=-\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{18}\left(6t^{3}\log\frac{1-t}{t}+6t^{2}+3t-1\right)+\alpha_{1}\left(3t^{2}-3t+1\right)+\alpha_{2}(1-t)^{3}.

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{18}\left(18t^{2}\log\frac{1-t}{t}-\frac{6t^{3}}{1-t}-6t^{2}+12t+3\right)+3\alpha_{1}(2t-1)-3\alpha_{2}(1-t)^{2},

we know

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{9}+\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{1}+\frac{1}{8}\alpha_{2}=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{3}-\frac{3}{4}\alpha_{2}=a_{\epsilon},

i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{2C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{3}+\frac{2a_{\epsilon}}{3},\ \ \alpha_{2}=-\frac{4C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{9}-\frac{4a_{\epsilon}}{3}.

Then from ODE comparison, for any
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t)\leq\mathcal{U}(t),

i.e.

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t)&\leq\left(\frac{2C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{3}+\frac{2a_{\epsilon}}{3}\right)\left(3t^{2}-3t+1\right)-\left(\frac{4C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{9}+\frac{4a_{\epsilon}}{3}\right)(1-t)^{3}\\
&\ \ \ -\frac{C^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{18}\left(6t^{3}\log\frac{1-t}{t}+6t^{2}+3t-1\right).\end{split}

For any fixed
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, since by definition,

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}-\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{M_{t}}\frac{1}{f(1-f)}|\nabla f|^{2}\sqrt{|\nabla f|^{2}+\epsilon},

which is a strictly increasing function as
\epsilon\to 0+
. Since (
31
) shows that
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t)
is always bounded, by monotone convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}-\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{M_{t}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}.

Together with (
34
), we have proved the following weighted volume comparison.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
t\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}t^{2}-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}-\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{M_{t}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}\leq\frac{2a_{0}}{3}(3t^{2}-3t+1)-\frac{4a_{0}}{3}(1-t)^{3},

where
a_{0}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}
.

Taking
t\to 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}\leq\frac{2a_{0}}{3},

which implies that

\displaystyle 2\pi-6\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla f|^{3}}{f(1-f)}\leq 16\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\right).

Together with (
31
), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

(36)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\left(|\mathcal{T}|^{2}+R_{g}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\leq 32\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\right).

By the Hölder inequality and the Penrose inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq 16\pi m(g)^{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}.

Since
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|
, we have

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\geq\frac{\pi\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)^{2}}{m(g)^{2}},

i.e.

32\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)\leq 8\pi\left(1-\left(\frac{2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)}{m(g)}\right)^{2}\right).

This completes the proof.

∎

Notice that from (

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{4}.

Applying Proposition

5.
Harmonic coordinate for conformal metric

Let
f
be the harmonic function defined by (
17
) on
(\tilde{M}^{3},g)
. We consider the conformal metric
h:=f^{4}g
. Then on the end
\infty_{2}
, since
g
is harmonically flat, we have
h_{ij}(x)=f^{4}(x)V^{4}(x)\delta_{ij}
, where
V(x)
is a positive bounded
\delta
-harmonic function. So on the end
\infty_{2}
,
h
is conformal to a punctured ball with the conformal factor
(fV)^{4}(x)
, where
(fV)(x)
is a bounded
\delta
-harmonic function in the punctured ball. Hence, by the removable singularity theorem,
fV
can be extended to the whole ball, which together with the expansion (
18
) and (
9
) implies that
h
can be extended smoothly over the one point compactification
\tilde{M}^{*}:=\tilde{M}\cup\{\infty_{2}\}
.
Moreover, by standard computations,
(\tilde{M}^{*},h)
also has nonnegative scalar curvature and has a single harmonically flat end
\infty_{1}
with ADM mass
m(h)=m(g)-2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)\geq 0
(c.f.
[
Bra01
, Equation (84)]
).

In the following, we assume
0\leq m(h)\ll 1
,
|m(g)-m_{0}|\ll 1
for a fixed
m_{0}>0
and follow the arguments in
[
DS23
]
to construct a harmonic coordinate map, which is an almost isometry into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

For
g
-harmonic function
f
, since
|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}=f^{4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}
and
\mathrm{dvol}_{g}=f^{-6}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}
, (
47
) is equivalent to

(49)

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{M}^{*}}\frac{|\nabla\left((1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\right)|_{h}^{2}}{(1-f)^{-8}|\nabla f|_{h}^{3}}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}\leq C\cdot m(h).

Notice that we also have

(50)

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{M}^{*}}f^{-2}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}=4\pi\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g).

Let
\{x^{j}\}_{j=1}^{3}
denote the asymptotically flat coordinate system of the end
\infty_{1}
. We firstly solve the harmonic coordinate functions
u^{j}
, for each
j\in\{1,2,3\}
, such that

(51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{h}u^{j}&=0,\\
|u^{j}(x)-x^{j}|&=o(|x|^{1-\sigma})\text{ as }x\to\infty_{1},\end{split}

where
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
is the order of the asymptotic flatness.
Denote by
\mathbf{u}
the resulting harmonic map

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}:=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3}):(\tilde{M}^{*},h)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For any fixed small
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, by
[
DS23
]
, we know that there exists a connected region
\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset(\tilde{M}^{*},h)
containing
\infty_{1}
, with smooth boundary, such that

\mathrm{Area}(\partial\mathcal{E}_{1})\leq m(h)^{2-\epsilon},

and
\mathbf{u}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{Y}_{1}:=\mathbf{u}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a diffeomorphism with the Jacobian satisfying

|\mathrm{Jac}\,\mathbf{u}-\mathrm{Id}|\leq\Psi(m(h)),

and under the identification by
\mathbf{u}
, the metric tensor satisfies

\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}(h_{jk}-\delta_{jk})^{2}\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}.

Now we modify
\mathcal{E}_{1}
to get a subset such that
f
is locally uniformly Lipschitz. For this purpose, we denote by
a_{g}:=\frac{m(g)}{2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma,g)}\geq 1
, and define
P:\tilde{M}^{*}\to[0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle P(x):=\left(\frac{m(g)^{2}}{4}(1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}-a_{g}^{2}\right)^{2}.

By (
48
),
\lim_{x\to\infty_{1}}P(x)=\lim_{x\to\infty_{2}}P(x)=0
. Notice that
|a_{g}-1|\ll 1
.

If
x
is a regular point of
f
such that
P(x)\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}
and
f(x)\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}
, then

(52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\nabla P|_{h}(x)&\leq 2\sqrt{6}m(h)^{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{m(g)^{2}}{4}|\nabla\left((1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\right)|_{h}\\
&=2\sqrt{6}m(h)^{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{m(g)^{2}}{4}\frac{|\nabla\left((1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\right)|_{h}}{(1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\cdot(1-f)^{-3}|\nabla f|_{h}^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(1-f)^{-1}\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{|\nabla\left((1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\right)|_{h}}{(1-f)^{-4}f|\nabla f|_{h}^{\frac{3}{2}}},\end{split}

which together with (
49
) implies that

(53)

\displaystyle\int_{\{P\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}\}\cap\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\}}|\nabla P|_{h}^{2}\leq Cm(h).

By the co-area formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{m(h)^{2\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Area}_{h}(\{P=s\}\cap\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\{P\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}\}\cap\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\}}|\nabla P|_{h}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Cm(h)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\mathrm{Vol}_{h}(\{P\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}\}\cap\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{h}(\{P\leq m(h)^{2\epsilon}\}\cap\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\})

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{f\leq 1-m(h)^{\epsilon}\}}(1-f)^{-4}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Cm(h)^{-4\epsilon}\int_{\tilde{M}^{*}}f^{-2}|\nabla f|_{h}^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Cm(h)^{-4\epsilon},

where we used (
50
) in the last inequality, for a generic

.

Denote by
M_{sc}(\epsilon):=\{f_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon\}
, and
x_{o}(\epsilon)\in\{f_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2}+3\epsilon\}
a base point.

Proposition 7.1
.

(60)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)},q_{i})\to(M_{sc}(\epsilon),d_{g_{sc}},x_{o}(\epsilon))

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology, and

(61)

\displaystyle(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)})\to(\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)})

in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology for the induced length metrics.

Proof.

We firstly show (
61
). By the construction of
\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
,
we know
\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
is inside
\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon)
-neighborhood of
\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)\cap\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)
, so

(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)})\to(\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}})

in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology. It is sufficient to show that for any
x,y\in\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)=\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)}(x,y).

Let
\gamma\subset\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)
be a geodesic between
x,y\in\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
for
\hat{d}_{g_{sc}}
. Since
\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)\cap\partial\mathcal{E}_{i}
consists of smooth curves with total length converging to
0
, we can always perturb
\gamma
to get
\tilde{\gamma}\subset\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)\cap\mathcal{E}_{i}
such that
|\mathrm{Length}_{g_{sc}}(\gamma)-\mathrm{Length}_{g_{sc}}(\tilde{\gamma})|\to 0
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}|_{g_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}))\int_{0}^{1}|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}|_{g_{sc}},

where we used
f_{i}\to f_{\infty}
uniformly.
Taking
i\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)}(x,y).

Similarly, it’s easy to check

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y).

This completes the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence in (
61
).

Notice that for any
x,y\in\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)
, there is a uniform constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x,y)\leq\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq Cd_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x,y),

which implies that, for any
x,y\in\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
, for all large enough
i
,

(62)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\hat{d}_{h_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}}(x,y)\leq\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq C\hat{d}_{h_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}}(x,y)+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon).

Now we prove (
60
). It is enough to show that for any fixed
D>0
and any
x,y\in\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)\cap B(q_{i},D)
,

|\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)-d_{g_{sc}}(x,y)|\to 0\text{ as }i\to\infty.

It’s easy to check that
d_{g_{sc}}(x,y)\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)
. In the following, we will check the other inequality.

For any fixed
\delta_{0}>0
. We firstly assume that
d_{g_{sc}}(x,y)\geq\delta_{0}
and
d_{g_{sc}}(x,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\geq\delta_{0}
,
d_{g_{sc}}(y,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\geq\delta_{0}
. If
\gamma
is a
g_{sc}
-geodesic between
x,y
, then
d_{g_{sc}}(\gamma,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\geq\delta_{0}
. For any
0<\delta\ll\delta_{0}
, we can use piecewise line segments
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N(\delta,D)}
to approximate
\gamma
such that
\Sigma_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{Length}_{g_{sc}}(\gamma_{j})\leq\mathrm{Length}_{g_{sc}}(\gamma)+\Psi(\delta),
and
\mathrm{Length}_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(\gamma_{j})\geq\delta
. For each
\gamma_{j}
with
\gamma_{j}(0)=x_{j},\gamma_{j}(1)=y_{j}
, from the proof of
[
DS23
, Lemma 4.5]
, we can find peturbed points
x_{j}^{\prime},y_{j}^{\prime}
such that the straight line segment
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
between
x_{j}^{\prime},y_{j}^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
and
d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x_{j},x_{j}^{\prime})+d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(y_{j},y_{j}^{\prime})\leq\Psi(\varepsilon_{i})
. Then

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x_{j}^{\prime},y_{j}^{\prime})+C\sum_{j}(d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x_{j},x_{j}^{\prime})+d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(y_{j},y_{j}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\int|\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime}|_{g_{i}}+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\delta,D)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|D))\sum_{j}\mathrm{Length}_{g_{sc}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{j})+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\delta,D),

where we have used that
f_{i}\to f_{\infty}
uniformly. Taking
i\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq d_{g_{sc}}(x,y)+\Psi(\delta).

Taking
\delta\to 0
gives

(63)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq d_{g_{sc}}(x,y).

If
d_{g_{sc}}(x,y)\leq\delta_{0}
and
d_{g_{sc}}(x,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\leq\delta_{0}
, then
d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\leq\delta_{0}
and
d_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(x,y)\leq\delta_{0}
. We can take an almost
\hat{d}_{h_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}}
-geodesic
\gamma\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}
between
x,y
, so
\mathrm{Length}_{h_{i}}(\gamma)\leq 2\delta_{0}
. If
\gamma\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
, we have
\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq C\delta_{0}
; otherwise, let
x^{\prime}
be the first intersection point of
\gamma
and
\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)
and
y^{\prime}
be the last intersection point. By (
62
), we have

\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\leq\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\leq C\hat{d}_{h_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon)\leq C\delta_{0}+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon).

So

\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,y)\leq\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x,x^{\prime})+\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}(y^{\prime},y)\leq C\delta_{0}+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon).

This shows that the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance

\displaystyle d_{pGH}((\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)},q_{i}),(\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)\cap\{x:d_{g_{sc}}(x,\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))\geq\delta_{0}\},\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}

\displaystyle,q_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon).

Together with (
63
), we have that

\displaystyle d_{pGH}((\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)},q_{i}),(M_{sc}(\epsilon),d_{g_{sc}},x_{o}(\epsilon)))\leq C\delta_{0}+\Psi(\varepsilon_{i}|\epsilon).

First taking
i\to\infty
and then taking
\delta_{0}\to 0
gives the conclusion on the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence in (
60
).

Since the Hausdorff measure induced by
\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}
and
\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon)}
are the same as the volume element
\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{i}}
and the area element
\mathrm{dA}_{g_{i}}
respectively, together with isoperimetric inequality, it’s standard to check that these measures also converge weakly (c.f.
[
DS23
, Page 22]
). In particular,
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon))\to\mathrm{Area}_{g_{sc}}(\partial M_{sc}(\epsilon))
.

∎

Choosing a sequence
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
, and using a diagonal argument, we can take a subsequence such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon_{i}),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon_{i})},q_{i})\to(M_{sc},d_{g_{sc}},x_{o})

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology, and

\displaystyle(\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon_{i}),\hat{d}_{g_{i},\partial^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon_{i})})\to(\partial M_{sc},\hat{d}_{g_{sc},\partial M_{sc}})

in the measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology.

Finally, we can take
Z_{i}=M_{i}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
N_{i}\subset M_{i}
to be a smooth submanifold such that
\mathbf{u}_{i}(N_{i}\setminus Z_{i})=\mathcal{E}_{i}(\epsilon_{i})
. This completes the proof.

∎

Then we prove the stability for the Penrose inequality as a corollary of the stability for the mass-capacity inequality.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
A_{0}\geq 0
be a fixed constant and
(M_{i}^{3},g_{i})
be a sequence of asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature, whose boundaries are compact connected outermost minimal surfaces with area
A_{0}
. Suppose that the ADM mass
m(g_{i})
converges to
\sqrt{\frac{A_{0}}{16\pi}}
.

From the remark in the end of Section
3
, we can find a smooth subset
M_{i}^{\prime}\subset M_{i}
and a metric
g_{i}^{\prime}
such that
(M^{\prime}_{i},g_{i}^{\prime})
is also an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and a connected outermost horizon
\Sigma_{i}^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{i}^{\prime})=A_{0}
, the uniform distance between
g_{i}^{\prime}
and
g_{i}
on
M^{\prime}_{i}
converges to
0
, and
m(g_{i}^{\prime})-2\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{i}^{\prime},g_{i}^{\prime})\to 0
.

Then we can apply the stability for mass-capacity inequality to
(M^{\prime}_{i},g_{i}^{\prime})
. So the conclusion that up to boundary area perturbations, the Schwarzschild
3
-manifold is stable for the Penrose inequality follows.

∎

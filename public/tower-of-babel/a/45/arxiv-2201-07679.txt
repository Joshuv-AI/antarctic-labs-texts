1
Introduction

We are interested in the regularity properties of solutions to problems of the form

(1.1)

\min\Set{\int_{\Omega}f\left(x,Dv(x)\right)dx\,:\,v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi}(\Omega)},

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set,
n>2
,
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory map, such that
\xi\mapsto f(x,\xi)
is of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for a. e.
x\in\Omega
,
\psi:\Omega\mapsto[-\infty,+\infty)
belonging to the Sobolev class
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
is the
obstacle
, and

\mathcal{K}_{\psi}(\Omega)=\Set{v\in u_{0}+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}):v\geq\psi\text{ a.e. in }\Omega}

is the class of the admissible functions, with
u_{0}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
a fixed boundary datum.

Let us observe that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
is a
solution to the obstacle problem (
1.1
) in

\mathcal{K}_{\psi}(\Omega)
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi}(\Omega)
and
u
is a solution to the variational
inequality

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}\left<A(x,Du(x)),D(\varphi(x)-u(x))\right>dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi}(\Omega),

where the operator
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined
as follows

A_{i}(x,\xi)=D_{\xi_{i}}f(x,\xi)\qquad\forall i=1,...,n.

We assume that
A
is a
p
-harmonic type operator, that is it satisfies the following
p
-ellipticity and
p
-growth conditions with respect to
the
\xi
-variable. There exist positive constants
\nu,L,\ell

and an exponent
1<p\leq 2
and a parameter
0\leq\mu\leq 1
such that

(1.3)

\left|A(x,\xi)\right|\leq\ell\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(1.4)

\left<A(x,\xi)-A(x,\eta),\xi-\eta\right>\geq\nu|\xi-\eta|^{2}\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

(1.5)

\left|A(x,\xi)-A(x,\eta)\right|\leq L|\xi-\eta|\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for almost every
x\in\Omega.

First, we show that an higher differentiability property of integer order of the gradient of the obstacle tranfers to the solution of problem (
1.1
), provided the partial map
x\mapsto D_{\xi}f(x,\xi)
belongs to a suitable Sobolev class, with no loss in the order of differentiation.

More precisely we assume that the map
x\mapsto A\left(x,\xi\right)
belongs to
W^{1,p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
or, equivalently, that there exists a non-negative function
g\in L^{p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
such that

(1.6)

\left|A\left(x,\xi\right)-A\left(y,\xi\right)\right|\leq\left(g(x)+g(y)\right)\left|x-y\right|\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

for a. e.
x,y\in\Omega
and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

(1.7)

\left|D_{x}A\left(x,\xi\right)\right|\leq g(x)\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(see
[
34
]
).

Note that, since
f
, as a function of the
\xi
variable, is of class
C^{2}
, then the operator
A
is of class
C^{1}
with respect to
\xi
, and (
1.5
) implies

(1.8)

\left|D_{\xi}A(x,\xi)\right|\leq c\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Set{0}
and for a.e.
x\in\Omega.

Higher differentiability results for solutions to homogeneous obstacle problems of the type (
1.1
) under a Sobolev assumption on the partial map
x\mapsto A(x,\xi)
have been obtained when the energy density satisfies standard
p
-growth conditions, both in case

(
[

]
) and in case
1<p<2
(
[

]
). The case of non-standard growth conditions has been faced, for example, in
[

-growth and in case of variable exponents in

However, taking into account the result obtained for unconstrained problem in

.

Let

\psi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\mbox{ and }V_{p}\left(D\psi\right)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\implies V_{p}\left(Du\right)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right).

B_{8R}\Subset\Omega
, the following estimate holds

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\left|DV_{p}\left(Du(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx

(1.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\left(\left\Arrowvert\psi\right\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{8R}\right)}^{2}+\left\Arrowvert u\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p^{*}}\left(B_{8R}\right)}^{2}\right)^{\sigma_{1}}}{R^{2}}

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{B_{4R}}\left|DV_{p}\left(D\psi(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{4R}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|D\psi(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right]^{\sigma_{2}},

2.1
Difference quotients

A key instrument in studying regularity properties of solutions to problems of Calculus of Variations and PDEs is the so called
difference quotients method
.

In this section, we recall the definition and some basic results.

Definition 2.5
.

Given
h\in\mathbb{R}
, for every function

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for any
s=1,...,n
the finite difference operator in the direction
x_{s}
is
defined by

\tau_{s,h}F(x)=F(x+he_{s})-F(x),

where
e_{s}
is the unit vector in the direction
x_{s}
.

In the following, in order to simplify the notations, we will omit the vector
e_{s}
unless it is necessary, denoting

\tau_{h}F(x)=F(x+h)-F(x),

where
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We now describe some properties of the operator
\tau_{h}
whose proofs can be found, for example, in
[
31
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
F
and
G
be two functions such that
F,G\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, with
p\geq 1
, and let us consider the set

\Omega_{|h|}:=\Set{x\in\Omega:d\left(x,\partial\Omega\right)>\left}{h}.

Then

(a)

\tau_{h}F\in W^{1,p}\left(\Omega_{|h|}\right)
and

D_{i}(\tau_{h}F)=\tau_{h}(D_{i}F).

(b)
If at least one of the functions
F
or
G
has support contained
in
\Omega_{|h|}
then

\int_{\Omega}F(x)\tau_{h}G(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}G(x)\tau_{-h}F(x)dx.

(c)
We have

\tau_{h}(FG)(x)=F(x+h)\tau_{h}G(x)+G(x)\tau_{h}F(x).

The next result about finite difference operator is a kind
of integral version of Lagrange Theorem.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
0<\rho<R
,
|h|<\frac{R-\rho}{2}
,
1<p<+\infty
,
and
F,DF\in L^{p}(B_{R})
then

\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{h}F(x)|^{p}\ dx\leq c(n,p)|h|^{p}\int_{B_{R}}|DF(x)|^{p}\ dx.

Moreover

\int_{B_{\rho}}|F(x+h)|^{p}\ dx\leq\int_{B_{R}}|F(x)|^{p}\ dx.

The following result is proved in
[
31
]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
F\in L^{p}\left(B_{R}\right)
with
1<p<+\infty
. Suppose that there exist
\rho\in(0,R)
and
M>0
such that

\sum_{s=1}^{n}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{s,h}F(x)|^{p}dx\leq M^{p}|h|^{p}

for
\left|h\right|<\frac{R-\rho}{2}
. Then
F\in W^{1,p}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover

\left\Arrowvert DF\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\leq M,

\left\Arrowvert F\right\Arrowvert_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(B\rho)}\leq c\left(M+\left\Arrowvert F\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\right),

with
c=c(n,N,p,\rho,R)
. Moreover

\frac{\tau_{s,h}F}{\left|h\right|}\to D_{s}F\qquad\mbox{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right),\mbox{ as }h\to 0,

for each
s=1,...,n.

2.2
An auxiliary function

Here we define an auxiliary function of the gradient variable that will be useful in the following.

The function

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

(2.15)

One can easily check that, for a

In what follows, the following result can be useful.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,

\int_{B_{r}}\left|D^{2}v(x)\right|^{p}dx\leq c\cdot\left[1+\int_{B_{R}}\left|DV_{p}\left(Dv(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx+c\int_{B_{R}}\left|Dv(x)\right|^{p}dx\right]

Proof.

We will prove the existence of the second-order weak derivatives of

Let us consider a ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega

.

For

0<\frac{R}{2}<r<\rho_{1}:=r+|h|<R-|h|=:\rho_{2}<R
, and by (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\left|\tau_{s,h}Dv(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\tau_{s,h}Dv(x)|^{p}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x+he_{s}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p(p-2)}{4}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x+he_{s}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p(2-p)}{4}}dx.

By Hölder’s Inequality with exponents

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\left|\tau_{s,h}Dv(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left|\tau_{s,h}V_{p}\left(Dv(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x+he_{s}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}},

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{B_{4r}}\left|DV_{p}\left(D\psi(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{4r}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|D\psi(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right]^{\sigma_{2}},

for a constant
c=c(n,p,\nu,L,\ell)
, for any ball
B_{8r}\Subset B_{\tilde{R}}.

Moreover, since and
g\in L^{p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
, we have

(2.193)

g_{\varepsilon}\to g\qquad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{p+2}\left(B_{\tilde{R}}\right),\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0

and, up to a subsequence, almost everywhere in
B_{\tilde{R}}
.

Since by (
2.181
),
\left|A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du\right)\right|\leq\ell\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
and since
A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du\right)
converges
almost everywhere to
A\left(x,Du\right)
, by the dominated convergence Theorem we have

(2.194)

A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du\right)\to A\left(x,Du\right)\qquad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(B_{\tilde{R}}\right),\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Using the ellipticity condition (
2.182
), we have

(2.195)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{2}dx

(2.197)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

(2.201)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

(2.207)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx

Using
u
and
u_{\varepsilon}
as test functions in
(
2.185
) and (
1.2
)
respectively, we have

(2.208)

\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx\leq 0

and

(2.209)

-\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle dx\leq 0,

therefore, thanks to (
2.208
) and (
2.209
), (
2.195
) implies

(2.210)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{2}dx

(2.212)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A\left(x,Du(x)\right),\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\rangle\,dx

(2.216)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Now let us observe that, by (
2.208
), using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\left(p,\frac{p}{p-1}\right)
and recalling (
2.181
), we get

(2.217)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\langle A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du(x)\rangle dx

(2.221)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(2.225)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Moreover, using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\left(\frac{2}{p},\frac{2}{2-p}\right)
and thanks to the ellipticity condition (
2.182
), we have

(2.226)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p\left(p-2\right)}{4}}\cdot\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p\left(2-p\right)}{4}}dx

(2.230)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(2.234)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.

We can notice that, since the ellipticity condition implies

\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\geq 0,

and so

\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\geq\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>,

for a.e.
x\in B_{\tilde{R}}
.

Hence, if we denote

E_{1}:=\Set{x\in B_{\tilde{R}}:\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right><0},

and

E_{2}:=\Set{x\in B_{\tilde{R}}:\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\geq 0},

we have

\left|\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\right|\leq\left|\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\right|

for a.e.
x\in E_{1}
, and

\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\leq\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>+\left|\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\right|

for a.e.
x\in E_{2}
.
Therefore (
2.226
) implies

(2.239)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(c\int_{E_{1}}\left|\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\right|dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{E_{2}}\left(\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>+\left|\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,0\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>\right|\right)dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(c\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(2.245)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{E_{1}}\mu^{p-1}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{E_{2}}\left(\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>+\mu^{p-1}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|\right)dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}},

where, in the last line, we used (
2.181
).

Using Young’s inequality with exponents
\left(\frac{2}{p},\frac{2}{2-p}\right)
, by (
2.239
) we deduce

(2.248)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\mu^{p-1}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|dx+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>dx

\displaystyle+\sigma\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

(2.252)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left<A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right),Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right>dx

\displaystyle+2\sigma\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx+c_{\sigma}\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|,

where we also used Young’s inequality with exponents
\left(p,\frac{p}{p-1}\right)
and the fact that
\mu\in[0,1]
.

Now, joining (
2.217
) with (
2.248
), we get

(2.255)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\sigma}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+2\sigma\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx+c_{\sigma}\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|

(2.259)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}dx+3\sigma\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|,

where we used Young’s inequality with exponents
\left(p,\frac{p}{p-1}\right)
and the fact that
\mu\in[0,1]
again.

Choosing
\sigma<\frac{1}{3}
, (
2.255
) implies

(2.260)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}dx+c\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|.

Let us observe that, using Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\left(\frac{2}{p},\frac{2}{2-p}\right)
recalling (
2.260
), we have

(2.263)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p(p-2)}{4}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p(2-p)}{4}}dx

(2.267)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(2.271)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}+\left|Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(2.277)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left[\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(2.283)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}},

where we also used (
2.210
).

By Young’s inequality with exponents
\left(2,2\right)
, (
2.263
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du-Du_{\varepsilon}\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|\right)^{2-p},

for any
\sigma>0
, and if we choose
\sigma<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|\left(Du_{\varepsilon}-Du\right)(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|A\left(x,Du(x)\right)-A_{\varepsilon}\left(x,Du(x)\right)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}dx\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\left|B_{\tilde{R}}\right|\right)^{2-p}.

Hence, by (
2.194
), we deduce

Du_{\varepsilon}\to Du\qquad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(B_{\tilde{R}}\right),

which implies

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\qquad\mbox{ strongly in }L^{p^{*}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(B_{\tilde{R}}\right)

and, up to a subsequence

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\qquad\mbox{ almost everywhere in }B_{\tilde{R}},

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Moreover, by the continuity of the map
\xi\mapsto DV_{p}\left(\xi\right)
, we also get

DV_{p}\left(Du_{\varepsilon}\right)\to DV_{p}\left(Du\right)\qquad\mbox{ a.e. in }B_{\tilde{R}},\mbox{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Therefore, recalling (
2.193
), if we pass to the limit in (
2.186
), by Fatou’s Lemma and a covering argument, we conclude, proving (
1.9
).
∎

1.1
, Lemma
2.11
and Remark
2.13
, we get the following result.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
be a solution to the obstacle problem (
1.1
) under assumptions (
1.3
)–(
1.5
) and let us assume that there exists a function
g\in L^{p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
such that (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) hold, for
1<p<2
.

Then the following implication holds:

\psi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\mbox{ and }V_{p}\left(D\psi\right)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\implies u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\mbox{ and }Du\in L^{p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right).

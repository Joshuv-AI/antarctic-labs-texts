Temperate distributions with locally finite support and spectrum on Euclidean spaces
Sergii Yu.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

We prove that supports of a wide class of temperate distributions with uniformly discrete support and spectrum on Euclidean spaces are finite unions of translations of full-rank lattices.
This result is a generalization of the corresponding theorem for Fourier quasicrystals, and its proof uses the technique of almost periodic distributions.

Denote by

The Fourier transform of a temperate distribution

Almost periodic functions and distributions

We recall here definitions and properties of almost periodic functions and distributions that will be used in what follows. A more complete exposition of these issues is available in

Definition 1
.

A continuous function
g
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is almost periodic if for any
\varepsilon>0
the set of its
\varepsilon
-almost periods

\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|g(t+\tau)-g(t)|<\varepsilon\}

is a relatively dense set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

An equivalent definition follows:

Definition 2
.

A continuous function
g
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is almost periodic if for any sequence
\{t_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is a subsequence
\{t^{\prime}_{n}\}
such that
the sequence of functions
g(t+t^{\prime}_{n})
converge uniformly in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Using an appropriate definition, one can prove various properties of almost periodic functions.

•

almost periodic functions are bounded and uniformly continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

•

the class of almost periodic functions is closed with respect to taking absolute values and linear combinations of a finite family of functions,

•

a limit of a uniformly convergent sequence of almost periodic functions is also almost periodic,

•

any finite family of almost periodic functions has a relatively dense set of common
\varepsilon
-almost periods,

•

for any almost periodic function
g(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the function
h(t)=g(tx)
is almost periodic in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
for any fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; in particular,

g(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
is almost periodic in each variable
t_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},\ j=1,\dots,d
, if the other variables are held fixed.

Also, we will use the following definition

Definition 3
.

A distribution
g
is almost periodic if the function
(g(y),\varphi(t-y))

is almost periodic in

for each
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support.
A measure
\mu
is almost periodic if it is an almost periodic distribution.

Clearly, every linear combination of almost periodic distributions is almost periodic, and each almost periodic distribution has a relatively dense support.

Note that the usual definition of almost periodicity for measures (instead of

.

Let

6.
Representation of distributions with discrete support and spectrum

First we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
g_{0}(y),g_{1}(y),\dots,g_{N}(y)
be temperate distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that for every
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi(y)
with compact support the functions

(g_{n}(y),\varphi(t-y)),\quad t=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d}),\quad n=0,\dots,N,

are almost periodic in
t_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for every fixed
(t_{2},\dots,t_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
. If the distribution

F=\sum_{n=0}^{N}y_{1}^{n}g_{n}(y),\quad y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d}),

has a uniformly discrete support
T
, then the set
\cup_{n}\supp g_{n}
is uniformly discrete and
\eta(\cup_{n}\supp g_{n})\geq\eta(T)
.

Proof
. Note that for every
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi(y)
with compact support we have

(17)

(F(y),\varphi(t-y))=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(g_{n}(y),y_{1}^{n}\varphi(t-y))=\sum_{m=0}^{N}t_{1}^{m}\sum_{n=m}^{N}\Phi_{n,m}(t),

where

\Phi_{n,m}(t)=\binom{n}{m}(g_{n}(y),(y_{1}-t_{1})^{n-m}\varphi(t-y)),\quad n\geq m.

We will prove that for every
n,m\leq N
and every
y\in\supp g_{n},\,\tilde{y}\in\supp g_{m}
we get
|y-\tilde{y}|\geq\eta(T)
.

Consider the case
n=m=N
. Assume the converse. Then there are points
y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in\supp g_{N}
such that
0<|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|<\eta(T)
.
Pick

\alpha<(1/2)\min\{|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|,\,(\eta(T)-|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|)\}

and
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi
with
\supp\varphi\subset B(0,\alpha)
such that

\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime})=(g_{N}(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}-y))\neq 0,\qquad\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime\prime})=(g_{N}(y),\varphi(y^{\prime\prime}-y))\neq 0.

Take
\varepsilon>0
such that
|\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime})|>2\varepsilon
,
|\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime\prime})|>2\varepsilon
. If
\tau
is a common
\varepsilon
-almost period of the functions

\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime}+se_{1}),\quad\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime\prime}+se_{1}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad e_{1}=(1,0,0\dots 0),\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

then
|\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1})|>\varepsilon
,
|\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1})|>\varepsilon
. We have

(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-N}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))=\Phi_{N,N}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1})+\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{m-N}\sum_{n=m}^{N-1}\Phi_{n,m}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}).

Note that all functions
\Phi_{n,m}(y^{\prime}+se_{1}),\,0\leq n,m\leq N,
are almost periodic in
s
, hence they are uniformly bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore if
\tau
is large enough then

(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-N}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))\neq 0.

Hence there exists a point
t^{\prime}\in B(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap T
. For the same reason, there exists a point
t^{\prime\prime}\in B(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap T
with the same
\tau
. We have

(18)

|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|\leq|t^{\prime}-y^{\prime}-\tau e_{1}|+|t^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}-\tau e_{1}|+|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|<2\alpha+|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|<\eta(T).

But this inequality is impossible.

Now suppose that our assumption is proved for all
n,\,m
such that
n+m>M
with
0\leq M<2N
; then we prove it for
p,q\geq 0
such that
p+q=M
.

Assume the converse. There are points
y^{\prime}\in\supp g_{p},\ y^{\prime\prime}\in\supp g_{q}
such that
0<|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|<\eta(T)
. Pick

\alpha<(1/2)\min\{|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|,\,(\eta(T)-|y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime}|)\}

and
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi
with
\supp\varphi\subset B(0,\alpha)
such that

\Phi_{p,p}(y^{\prime})=(g_{p}(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}-y))\neq 0,\qquad\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime})=(g_{q}(y),\varphi(y^{\prime\prime}-y))\neq 0.

Take
\varepsilon>0
such that
|\Phi_{p,p}(y^{\prime})|>2\varepsilon
and
|\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime})|>2\varepsilon
. Obviously, for a common
\varepsilon
-almost period
\tau
of the functions

\Phi_{p,p}(y^{\prime}+se_{1})
and
\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime}+se_{1})

we get

|\Phi_{p,p}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1})|>\varepsilon,\qquad|\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1})|>\varepsilon.

If there exists
n>p
such that at least one of the numbers
\Phi_{n,j}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}),\,j=0,\dots,n
does not vanish,
then there is a point
t^{\prime}\in B(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap\supp g_{n}
. Since
\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1})\neq 0
, we see that there is a point
t^{\prime\prime}\in B(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap\supp g_{q}
. By (
18
),
|t^{\prime}-t^{\prime\prime}|<\eta(T)
, hence
this inequality contradicts to our assumption
\eta(\supp g_{n}\cup\supp g_{q})\geq\eta(T)
for
n+q>M
. In the same way, we obtain a contradiction in the case
when at least one of the numbers
\Phi_{n,j}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1})
for
n>q,\,j=0,\dots,n
, does not vanish.

Therefore, for
t=y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}
equality (
17

(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-p}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))=\Phi_{p,p}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1})+\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{m-p}\sum_{n=m}^{p-1}\Phi_{n,m}(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}),

and for
t=y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1}
implies

(y^{\prime\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-q}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))=\Phi_{q,q}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1})+\sum_{m=0}^{q-1}(y^{\prime\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{m-q}\sum_{n=m}^{q-1}\Phi_{n,m}(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1}).

If
\tau
is large enough, then we get

(y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-p}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))\neq 0,\qquad(y^{\prime\prime}_{1}+\tau)^{-q}(F(y),\varphi(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1}-y))\neq 0,

hence there exists
t^{\prime}\in B(y^{\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap T
and
t^{\prime\prime}\in B(y^{\prime\prime}+\tau e_{1},\alpha)\cap T
with the same

. By (
18
), we obtain a contradiction. Thus our assumption is valid for all
n,\,m\leq N
.

be a
C^{\infty}
-function with compact support. Since the inverse Fourier transform of the function
\psi(t-x)
is
e^{2\pi i\langle t,y\rangle}\hat{\psi}(y)
, we get

(23)

(\psi\star\mu_{k})(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi(t-x)\mu_{k}(dx)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{2\pi i\langle y,t\rangle}\hat{\psi}(y)\hat{\mu}_{k}(dy)=\sum_{\gamma\in\supp\hat{\mu}_{k}}\hat{\mu}_{k}(\gamma)\hat{\psi}(\gamma)e^{2\pi i\langle\gamma,t\rangle}.

Clearly,
\hat{\psi}\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\hat{\psi}(\gamma)=o(|\gamma|^{-d-1})
as
\gamma\to\infty
. Also, the measure
\hat{\mu}_{k}
has uniformly bounded masses and uniformly discrete support, therefore the series in the right-hand side is absolutely converges and
(\psi\star\mu_{k})(t)\in W
. In particular, the measure
\mu_{k}
is almost periodic.

Furthermore, the function

F(t)=\sum_{\|k\|\leq K}(\psi\star\mu_{k})(t)\overline{(\psi\star\mu_{k})(t)}

belongs to
W
as well. Now suppose that
\psi
has support in the ball
B(0,\eta(\Lambda)/2)
and
\psi(0)=1
. Taking into account that

(24)

(\psi\star\mu_{k})(\lambda)=p_{k}(\lambda)\qquad\mbox{for}\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,

and using (
10
), we get

F(\lambda)=\sum_{\|k\|\leq K}|p_{k}(\lambda)|^{2}\geq\alpha>0\qquad\forall\,\lambda\in\Lambda.

Using Proposition
3
with
h(z)=1/z
, we construct the function

g(t)=\sum_{n}c_{n}e^{2\pi i\langle t,\tau_{n}\rangle}\in W

with the property
g(\lambda)=1/F(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Consider the dual pair
(\mathfrak{R},{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{discr})
. Set

\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{\|k\|\leq K}\mathfrak{r}_{k}(\mathfrak{t})\star\overline{\mathfrak{r}_{k}(-\mathfrak{t})},\quad\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{R}.

We get

\hat{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda)=F(\lambda)\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{\mathfrak{a}}(x)=0\quad\forall x\not\in\Lambda.

Since
\sum_{n}|c_{n}|<\infty
, we see that the series
\sum_{n}c_{n}\mathfrak{a}(y+\tau_{n})
converges with respect to the norm to a measure
\mathfrak{b}(y)
such that

\hat{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=\sum_{n}c_{n}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\tau_{n}\rangle}\hat{\mathfrak{a}}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x\in\Lambda,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
6
,
\Lambda
belongs to the coset ring of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{discr}
. By Theorem
7
,

(25)

\Lambda=\cup_{n=1}^{N}(A_{n}\setminus\cup_{s=1}^{S_{n}}B^{s}_{n}),

where
A_{n},\,B^{s}_{n}
are cosets of discrete lattices.

Let
A,\,A^{\prime}
be two arbitrary cosets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
L,\,L^{\prime}
be the corresponding lattices. Clearly,
A\cap A^{\prime}
is a coset of
L\cap L^{\prime}
. If
r(A\cap A^{\prime})=d
, then the factor-groups
L/(L\cap L^{\prime})
and
L^{\prime}/(L\cap L^{\prime})
are finite.
Therefore,
A\setminus A^{\prime}=A\setminus(A\cap A^{\prime})
is a finite union of disjoint cosets of
L\cap L^{\prime}
, and the same is valid for

A\cup A^{\prime}=(A\setminus(A\cap A^{\prime}))\cup(A^{\prime}\setminus(A\cap A^{\prime}))\cup(A\cap A^{\prime}).

We repeat this transformation for each pair of cosets with dimension
d
of their intersection. After a finite number of steps, we get a representation similar to (
25
) such that
r(B^{s}_{n})<d

and
r(A_{n}\cap A_{n^{\prime}})<d
for
n\neq n^{\prime}
.

Put
\delta_{A}=\sum_{x\in A}\delta_{A}
. Using the equalities
\delta_{A\setminus A^{\prime}}=\delta_{A}-\delta_{A\cap A^{\prime}}
and
\delta_{A\cup A^{\prime}}=\delta_{A}+\delta_{A^{\prime}}-\delta_{A\cap A^{\prime}}
, we get

\delta_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{D_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{J_{1}}\delta_{E_{j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{J_{2}}\delta_{F_{j}},

where
D_{j},\,E_{j},\,F_{j}
are cosets such that
r(D_{j})=d
and
r(E_{j})<d,\,r(F_{j})<d
.

Every coset
D_{j}
is a translation of full-rank lattice, hence
\delta_{D_{j}}
is
d
-periodic and
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{D_{j}}
is almost periodic.
Above we showed that all measures
\mu_{k}
are almost periodic. By Proposition
2
ii), the measures
|\mu_{k}|
are almost periodic, hence the measure
\sum_{k}|\mu_{k}|

with support
\Lambda
is almost periodic as well.
Using (
10
) and Proposition
2
iii), we get that the measure
\delta_{\Lambda}
is almost periodic. Therefore the left-hand side of the equality

(26)

\sum_{j=1}^{J_{1}}\delta_{E_{j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{J_{2}}\delta_{F_{j}}=\delta_{\Lambda}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{D_{j}}

is an almost periodic measure as well. But its support is contained in a finite union of hyperplanes and isn’t relatively dense. Therefore the measure in the left-hand side of (
26
) is identically zero, and
\delta_{\Lambda}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{D_{j}}
.
Since the measure
\delta_{\Lambda}
has the unit masses at every point of
\Lambda
, we see that the cosets
D_{j}
are pairwise disjoint and for some full-rank lattices
L_{j}
and
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(27)

\Lambda=\cup_{j=1}^{J}D_{j}=\cup_{j=1}^{J}(\lambda_{j}+L_{j}).

Recall that
\psi\star\mu_{k}\in W
. It follows from (
24
) and (
27
) that

(28)

\mu_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(\psi\star\mu_{k})\delta_{L_{j}+\lambda_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{s}a_{s,k}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\gamma_{s,k}\rangle}\delta_{L_{j}+\lambda_{j}}

for some
\gamma_{s,k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
a_{s,k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\sum_{s}|a_{s,k}|<\infty
.
For every
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is
\beta
inside the parallelepiped generated by corresponding
L_{j}^{*}
such that
\gamma-\beta\in L_{j}^{*}
. Therefore,

e^{2\pi i\langle x,\gamma_{s,k}\rangle}=e^{2\pi i\langle x,\beta_{s,k,j}\rangle}
for
x\in L_{j}
, and the set of all points
\beta_{s,k,j}
is bounded.
Therefore (
28
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{s}\sum_{x\in L_{j}}b_{s}(k,j)e^{2\pi i\langle x,\beta_{s,k,j}\rangle}\delta_{x+\lambda_{j}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad b_{s}(k,j)=a_{s,k}e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda_{j},\gamma_{s,k}-\beta_{s,k,j}\rangle}.

By (
14
), we obtain the statement of Theorem
9
.

Here we actually proved the following statement:

Proposition 4
.

Let
\{\nu_{n}\}
be a finite set of measures from
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that for each
n

a)
\supp\nu_{n}
is uniformly discrete,

b)
\supp\hat{\nu}_{n}
is locally finite of bounded density,

c)
\hat{\nu}_{n}
is a measure with uniformly bounded masses.

Set
\mathfrak{L}=\cup_{n}\supp\nu_{n}
. If

\inf_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{L}}\max_{n}|\nu_{n}(\lambda)|>0,

then
\mathfrak{L}
is a finite union of translates of several full-rank lattices.

Note that the boundedness of masses
\nu_{n}(\lambda)
follows from (
23
) with
\nu_{n}
instead of
\mu_{k}
and
\psi
such that
\supp\psi\subset B(0,\eta(\supp\nu_{n}))
and
\psi(0)=1
.

Remark 4
. It is not hard to check that conditions b) and c) can be replaced by

b’)
\hat{\nu}_{n}
is a purely point measure,

c’)
|\hat{\nu}_{n}|(B(0,r))=O(r^{d})
as
r\to\infty
.

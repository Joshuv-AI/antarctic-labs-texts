1.
Introduction

In the late 1980s, Witten used methods from theoretical physics to construct an elliptic genus valued in integral modular forms
[
Wit88a
]
. His arguments suggested a much deeper connection between elliptic cohomology and quantum field theory. Pursuing these ideas, Segal formulated a precise definition of 2-dimensional quantum field theory as a projective representation of a bordism category
[
Seg04
]
. During the same period, Hopkins and collaborators grew the subject of elliptic cohomology, culminating in the construction of topological modular forms (TMF) and a string orientation lifting Witten’s genus to a map of ring spectra
[
Hop02
,
Lur09b
]
.
Through a variety of insights and efforts spanning several decades, a rich picture is emerging that connects TMF with the geometry of field theories. In Stolz and Teichner’s framework, the basic conjecture can be summarized as follows

]

1.1.
Why the conjecture is interesting

The central role of topological K-theory in 20th century mathematics comes from the way it intermingles with many disciplines. In geometry, vector bundles determine K-theory classes. In analysis, the space of Fredholm operators represents K-theory. In algebra, representations of compact Lie groups determine (equivariant) K-theory classes. Many important results rely on these interconnections, with the Atiyah–Singer index theorem being the crowning example that interfaces analysis, geometry, and topology.
Within algebraic topology, TMF is considerably deeper than K-theory, e.g.,
\pi_{*}{\rm TMF}
detects many more classes in the stable homotopy groups of spheres.
However, the absence of a geometric description has largely confined the reach of TMF to the realm of homotopy theory. Resolving Conjecture
1.1
would change this landscape.

Complementing this motivation from algebraic topology, a current theme in theoretical physics is to understand various spaces of quantum field theories, including the landscape problem in string theory
[
Sus07
,
Dou10
]
and topological phases of matter
[
Kit09a
,
FM13
]
. Conjecture
1.1
asserts that one such space of quantum field theories has a very interesting topology. The quantum theories in question have the minimal supersymmetry in dimension 2; this imposes relatively meager constraints on observables, making the standard computations difficult. Physical reasoning assuming the validity of Conjecture
1.1
has led to a slew of surprising predictions, many of which connect back with mathematics, e.g., see
[
GJF23
,
GPPV21
,
Tac21
,
TY23b
,
TY23a
,
TYY23
,
LP23
]
. To mention two specific examples, the abundant torsion in TMF indicates new deformation invariants of quantum field theories that refine elliptic genera
[
GJFW21
]
, and the
576

[
DH11
]
. All told, there is an expectation that resolving Conjecture
1.1
will provide important mathematical tools for studying spaces of quantum field theories in general, with deep insight into the structure of 2-dimensional field theories and string theory in particular
[
Wit88a
]
.

There are also anticipated applications of (
1.1
) outside of homotopy theory and theoretical physics. We highlight three areas.

In differential geometry, Stolz conjectured that the Witten genus obstructs the existence of a positive Ricci curvature metric
[
Sto96
]

In algebra, lattice vertex operator algebras can encode sporadic groups, most famously through Borcherd’s proof of monstrous moonshine

In representation theory, Grojnowski described elliptic generalizations of standard examples built from equivariant cohomology and equivariant K-theory
[

In these constructions—in contrast to

1.2.
Frameworks for supersymmetric quantum field theory

A reoccurring theme in mathematical quantum field theory is the interplay between the analysis of operator algebras and the geometry of spacetime. For example, Jones’s contributions—as further amplified by Witten—used quantum field theory to forge a deep connection between the geometry of knots and the analysis of von Neumann algebras
[
Jon87
,
Wit89

Ati90
]

[

]

(9)

\displaystyle(\hbox{{\sf Bord}}_{d},\coprod)\to({\sf Vect},\otimes).

For example, in dimension 2 the source can be chosen to capture moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces (as in Segal’s original work on conformal field theory), and the target can be chosen to encode the analysis of nuclear Fréchet spaces under the projective tensor product.

In addition to (

1.4.
Disclaimer and overview

The chapter has two primary goals: (i) provide motivation and intuition for Conjecture
1.1
, and (ii) describe the central challenges that remain in its verification. Both of these goals require an understanding of examples from theoretical physics, e.g., the sigma models and free fermions. Attempting to fit these (partially-constructed) examples within a mathematical framework for quantum field theory unfortunately introduces many technicalities that tend to obscure the larger picture. For this reason, each section below starts with somewhat ad hoc definitions of quantum mechanical systems and quantum field theories that explicitly capture analytical data found in the physics literature. For supersymmetric quantum mechanics in particular, the relevant data of a theory is totally unambiguous and the examples are well-understood.
After connecting these ad hoc definitions with objects in algebraic topology, we contextualize the analytic data in the paradigm (
10
) as part of a representation of a bordism category. This bordism definition points towards new structures in 2-dimensional quantum field theory that are difficult to glean from naive generalizations of supersymmetric quantum mechanics alone. To emphasize the ongoing development of this mathematical framework, we label a statement with sketched proof as a
Hypothesis
; the references given indicate that a closely related statement has been proved, though with technical caveats we do not address here. We expect that any reasonable definition ought to make the stated Hypothesis true, and so this presentation is also intended to provide specific signposts to verify that proposed (fully-extended) definitions are on the right track.

We provide a brief overview of the chapter. Section
2
introduces quantum mechanics first from the analytic perspective before translating the basic structure into the bordism framework. Section
3
repeats this for supersymmetric quantum mechanics, and sketches a version of the K-theory variant of Conjecture
1.1
. Section
4
starts with the analytic perspective on quantum field theory as a generalization of quantum mechanics, before translating these structure into the bordism framework. This framework leads to a variety of insights into the ultimate content of Conjecture
1.1
.

Throughout,
{\mathcal{H}}
will denote a (fixed) infinite-dimensional separable, complex Hilbert space. All tensor products of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded objects are the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded tensor product whose symmetry isomorphism encodes the Koszul sign.

2.
The homotopy type of quantum mechanics

Let
M

Classical mechanics on

4.

4.1.

Remark 4.14
.

Corollary
4.13
raises a red flag: the edge homomorphism

(110)

\displaystyle\pi_{*}{\rm TMF}\to{\rm MF}^{\mathbb{Z}}

from the coefficients of

. Therefore, if Conjecture

is to hold, not all integral modular forms should be realized as partition functions of supersymmetric field theories. In particular, Conjecture

4.3.
Repackaging as representations of a bordism category

One can repackage the analytic data in Definitions
4.6
and
4.9
as part of a representation of the bordism categories
{\sf EBord}_{2}
and
{\sf EBord}_{2|1}
, respectively. We briefly review these bordism categories, referring to
[
ST11
]
for details.

The objects of
{\sf EBord}_{2}
are finite disjoint unions of metrized circles
S^{{\sf R}}_{r}
and
S^{{\sf NS}}_{r}
with spin structure, where
r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
records the radius,
{\sf R}
stands for the
Ramond
(alias: periodic, odd, or nonbounding) spin structure and
{\sf NS}
stands for
Neveu–Schwarz
(alias: antiperiodic, even, or bounding) spin structure. The morphisms of
{\sf EBord}_{2}
are flat 2-manifolds with spin structure and geodesic boundary, as pictured in Figure
7
. In addition to the cylinders and tori in Figure
5
, changing source and target data of a cylinder provides the “macaronis” in Figure
7
. From the discussion at the beginning of §
4.1
, the moduli space of Euclidean macaronis for a fixed source and target is
{\mathbb{H}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
records the radius, and
\tau=\frac{x+it}{2\pi r}\in{\mathbb{H}}
gives the height
t
of a cylinder and
x
is the difference in boundary parameterizations. Composition corresponds to addition in
{\mathbb{H}}
, e.g.,
{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau}\circ{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau^{\prime}}\simeq{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau+\tau^{\prime}}.

There are 4 spin structures on the torus
{\mathbb{C}}/2\pi r{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus 2\pi\tau{\mathbb{Z}}
. The action by the metaplectic group
{\rm MP}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
permutes the three even spin structures, while the odd spin structure is preserved by this action. We emphasize that the flat metric prohibits closed surfaces of higher genus. Flat metrics together with geodesic boundaries prohibits bordisms between circles of different sizes or different numbers of circles, e.g., pairs of pants are not morphisms in
{\sf EBord}_{2}
.

Adding supersymmetry, the objects of
{\sf EBord}_{2|1}
are
1|1
-dimensional super circles; the moduli of such are again parameterized by a spin structure and radius
[
BE23c
, §4.5]
, so we use the same notation,
S^{{\sf R}}_{r},S^{{\sf NS}}_{r}\in{\sf EBord}_{2|1}
. Bordisms between these are
2|1
-dimensional Euclidean manifolds with geodesic boundary. Analogous to the
1|1
-dimensional case, this introduces odd directions in the moduli space of morphisms in
{\sf EBord}_{2|1}
. For example, super Euclidean cylinders in the Ramond sector are parameterized by
{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\times{\mathbb{H}}^{2|1}
, with composition

(111)

\displaystyle{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}\circ{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}}\simeq{\rm C}^{\sf R}_{r,\tau+\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}+\bar{\tau}^{\prime}+\theta\theta^{\prime},\theta+\theta^{\prime}},\qquad r\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},\ (\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2|1}.

This gives the geometric counterpart to the supersemigroup representation (
98
).

To set up the degree
n
super Euclidean field theories relevant to Conjecture
1.1
, we first must describe the degree twist. Let

denote the Clifford algebra of the vector space

for bilinear pairing

k

-k

\int_{S^{1}}\langle\phi(t),\psi(t)\rangle dt

{\mathbb{C}}

\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}

[

4.4.
Additional insights from the bordism framework

In contrast to Hypothesis
3.20
, the map
{\sf QFT}_{2|1}^{n}\to{\sf sqft}^{n}
in Hypothesis
4.18
is no longer an epimorphism of stacks: the bordism definition contains strictly more information than Definition
4.9
, e.g., values
E(S^{{\sf NS}}_{r})
on the Neveu–Schwarz sector.
We highlight a few additional enhancements below.

Example 4.19

(Real structures)
.

In the bordism framework of
[
ST11
]
, both the objects and morphisms in
{\sf EBord}_{2|1}(\mathfrak{X})
form stacks, meaning the isometry group of a bordism is encoded in the structure. This leads to an additional
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-automorphism on objects that is not present in the 1-dimensional case, boiling down to the fact that
-{{\rm id}}\in{\rm O}_{2}
is an orientation preserving isometry of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(in contrast to
-1\in{\rm O}(1)
that reverses the orientation on
{\mathbb{R}}
). Physically, this automorphism is a parity-time reversal. For reflection positive theories, this symmetry determines an isomorphism between the space of states and its complex conjugate, i.e., is the data of a real structure
[
BE23c
, §8.4]
, thereby refining the
{\rm K}(\!(q)\!)
-valued cocycle map in
4.18
to a
{\rm KO}(\!(q)\!)
-valued map. This argument can also be understood in physical language, see
[
Wit87
]
and
[
GPPV21
, 3.2.2]
.

Example 4.20

(Torsion invariants of field theories)
.

Following Remark
3.25
, the supermoduli spaces encoded by
{\sf EBord}_{2|1}(\mathfrak{X})
can be quite subtle. In particular, the moduli of super Euclidean tori with a map to
\mathfrak{X}
is surprisingly rich. For
\mathfrak{X}=X
a smooth manifold, in
[
BE23a
]
it was shown that functions on this moduli space determine a cocycle representative of a class in
{\rm H}(M;{\rm MF})
, i.e., ordinary cohomology with values in modular forms. Subtle features of the moduli space of super tori encode holomorphic anomaly cancelation data in the cocycle representative, recovering the information anticipated by physical arguments from
[
GJFW21
,
GJF23
]
. Together with Witten’s partial construction of the supersymmetric sigma model (see Example
4.3
), this anomaly cancelation data can be used to provide a quantum field theory interpretation of Bunke and Naumann’s torsion invariant of
4k-1
-dimensional string manifolds
[
BN14
]
. These ideas are contextualized in the framework of
[
ST11
]
in
[
BE23c
]
, providing the first examples of
{\rm TMF}
-torsion captured by 2-dimensional supersymmetric field theories.

Example 4.21

(Symmetries of the degree twist and the elliptic Thom class)
.

In analogy to Example
3.15
, the degree twist from Definition
4.15
can be constructed as the quantization of a theory of
n
chiral free fermions
[
ST04
, §2.6]
, defined by the fields and action functional

\psi\in\Gamma(\Sigma;{\mathbb{S}}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad S(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\langle\psi,\bar{\partial}\psi\rangle

where
\Sigma
is a spin Riemann surface with spinor bundle
{\mathbb{S}}\to\Sigma
and chiral Dirac operator
\bar{\partial}
. This theory has classical (gauge) symmetries given by maps
\Sigma\to{\rm O}(n)
, acting on
\psi
by rotating
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In the quantum theory, this leads to a projective action of the loop group
L{\rm O}(n)
on the Hilbert space of states that intertwines with the action of the Clifford algebra
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}(L{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This loop group representation is precisely the fermionic Fock space construction for the level one representation of
\widehat{L{\rm Spin}(n)}
, the central extension of the loop group
L{\rm Spin}(n)
classified by
\frac{p_{1}}{2}\in{\rm H}^{4}(B{\rm Spin}(n);{\mathbb{Z}})

[
PS86
, §12]
. This chiral conformal field theory determines a Euclidean field theory with
\mathcal{N}=(0,1)
supersymmetry. Under Conjecture
1.1
, it is expected to recover the universal elliptic Thom class, see
[
ST04
, Theorem 1.0.3]
and
[
DH11
]
.

4.5.
The final frontier: Extending down

S_{\ell,\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}
S_{\ell^{\prime},\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm glue}}}{{\implies}}
C_{\ell+\ell^{\prime},\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm glue}}}{{\implies}}
S_{\ell+\ell^{\prime},\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}

Figure 8.

Horizontal composition of 2-morphisms in the fully-extended
2|1
-dimensional Euclidean bordism category. The strips
S_{\ell,\tau,\bar{\tau},\theta}
are indexed by a length of the incoming and outgoing string as well as a parameter in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2|1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2|1}
measuring an angle and a height of the strip. We view these as open strings that propagate in the upward (time) direction, and have a gluing operation in the horizontal (space) direction. Gluing the boundaries of a strip together recovers the previously considered category of cylinders.

A fully-extended
2|1
-dimensional Euclidean bordism 2-category encodes the cutting and gluing of
2|1
-dimensional super Euclidean manifolds with corners: the theory of closed strings encoded by the operations in Figure
7
is refined to a theory of open strings pictured in Figure
8
. The expectation is that extending down will refine the
{\rm KO}(\!(q)\!)
and
{\rm MF}^{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued invariants described in Hypothesis
4.18
to invariants of field theories valued in
{\rm TMF}
, i.e., the cocycle map in (
1.1
).

Some rough features of such fully-extended field theories are known e.g.,
[
Moo03
, 3.5]
and
[
Seg01
, §4]
. However, a lot of mystery remains: extending down is not uniquely defined, and no candidate definition has been spelled out in detail. As usual, the work can be divided into two interrelated pieces: (i) the geometry of the bordism 2-category and (ii) the analysis of the target 3-category. The language of
n
-fold Segal spaces provides a promising framework in which to study fully-extended bordism categories in general (e.g., see
[
JFS17
,
GP20
]
), and fully-extended
2|1
-dimensional Euclidean bordisms in particular. One candidate for the target is the symmetric monoidal 3-category of conformal nets
[
DH11
,
BDH15
,
BDH17
,
BDH19
,
BDH18
]
, though there are some anticipated technical modifications as this 3-category does not deloop the 2-category
{\sf Alg}
for 1-extended field theories from Definition
1.5
and
[
ST11
]
.

One way to guide the choices in extending down is to get a better handle on the examples. Perhaps the most fundamental example is the degree twist (
4.15
) and its expected connection with the elliptic Thom class, as sketched in Example
4.21
. This generalizes known structures in K-theory, see Example
3.15
. Indeed, a fully-extended degree
n
field theory implementing a universal twisted Thom isomorphism of the form

{\sf QFT}_{2|1}^{k}({\rm pt}{/\!\!/^{\!\nabla}}\!{\rm O}(n))\to{\sf QFT}_{2|1}^{n+k+\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}{/\!\!/^{\!\nabla}}\!{\rm O}(n))_{c},\qquad E\mapsto E\boxtimes{\rm Th}_{n}

would automatically construct a string oriented multiplicative cohomology theory from 2-dimensional supersymmetric field theories. Following Lurie’s suggestion from
[
Lur09b
, §5.5]
, higher equivariant structures in field theories could then be used to construct a comparison with
{\rm TMF}
as in (
1.1
). Evidence for higher equivariance comes from the expectation that symmetries of the free fermion field theory are the string 2-group
[
ST04
,
DH
,
SP11
,
Wal13
]
.

We offer one further speculation. Let
\chi{\sf QFT}_{2}^{n}\subset{\sf QFT}_{2}^{n}
denote the subcategory of degree
n
theories whose image under (
118
) is chiral in the sense of Example
4.2
. As in Corollary
4.13
, it is expected that such chiral conformal field theories determine supersymmetric ones, giving an inclusion

(124)

\displaystyle\chi{\sf QFT}_{2}^{\bullet}\xhookrightarrow{\rm group\ completion?}{\sf QFT}_{2|1}^{\bullet}.

Analogizing with (
3.11
), one might hope that (
124
) implements a group completion of chiral field theories. Indeed, in dimension 1 the topological field theories are precisely the ones invariant under the renormalization group flow, and in dimension 2 these are the chiral conformal theories. Together with Conjecture
1.1
, (
124
) would realize
{\rm TMF}
as a group completion of chiral conformal field theories. At present it is unclear how to parse this statement, but it is likely that the rigid objects in the image of (
124
) play an important role in Conjecture
1.1
just as vector bundles are the basic geometric objects in K-theory.

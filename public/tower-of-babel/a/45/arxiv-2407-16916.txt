Phantom hairy black holes and wormholes in Einstein-bumblebee gravity
Chikun Ding 1,2, Changqing Liu 1, Yuehua Xiao 1, Jun Chen 1

Abstract
Abstract In this paper we study Einstein-bumblebee gravity theory minimally coupled with external matter—a phantom/non-phantom(conventional) scalar field, and derive a series of hairy solutions—bumblebee-phantom(BP) and BP-dS/AdS black hole solutions, regular Ellis-bumblebee-phantom (EBP) and BP-AdS wormholes, etc. We first find that the Lorentz violation (LV) effect can change the so called black hole no-hair theorem and these scalar fields can give a hair to a black hole. If LV coupling constant ℓ > − 1 \ell>-1 , the phantom field is admissible and the conventional scalar field is forbidden; if ℓ < − 1 \ell<-1 , the phantom field is forbidden and the conventional scalar field is admissible. By defining the Killing potential ω a ​ b \omega^{ab} , we study the Smarr formula and the first law for the BP black hole, find that the appearance of LV can improve the structure of these phantom hairy black holes—the conventional Smarr formula and the first law of black hole thermodynamics still hold; but for no LV case, i.e., the regular phantom black hole reported in [Phys. Rev. Lett. 96 , 251101], the first law cannot be constructed at all. When the bumblebee potential is linear, we find that the phantom potential and the Lagrange-multiplier λ \lambda behave as a cosmological constant Λ \Lambda .

Summary

In this paper, we have studied Einstein-bumblebee gravity theory minimally coupling to an external matter—conventional non-phantom scalar or exotic phantom scalar field. In Einstein-bumblebee grvity theory, an constant vector

Appendix B
An examples for Ref.
ovgun

In Ref.
ovgun
, Övgün
et al
studied the traversable wormholes in bumblebee gravity with the external isotropic matter
(-\tilde{\rho},\tilde{p},\tilde{p},\tilde{p})
, where
\tilde{\rho}
is the energy density of the external matter, and
\tilde{p}
is the pressure of the matter. They used the metric ansatz,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{W(r)}{r}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(91)

and found that

\displaystyle W(r)=\frac{1}{1+\ell}\left[\ell r+r_{0}\big(\frac{r_{0}}{r}\big)^{-\frac{5\ell+3}{3\ell+1}}\right],

(92)

where
r_{0}
is a constant and stands for throat radius. This metric can be rewritten as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\frac{(1+\ell)dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},\qquad f(r)=\left[1-\big(\frac{r_{0}}{r}\big)^{-\frac{2\ell+2}{3\ell+1}}\right].

(93)

When the coupling constant
\ell=-2
, the shape function
f(r)
and the energy density are

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\big(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\big)^{\frac{2}{5}},\qquad\tilde{\rho}=\frac{7}{5\kappa r_{0}^{2}}(r_{0}r^{2})^{-\frac{4}{5}}>0.

(94)

So this matter is conventional positive energy matter.

Appendix C
Komar integral for phantom regular black hole in Ref.
bronnikov

In Ref.
bronnikov
, Bronnikov and Fabris reported a regular phantom black hole which was reconsidered by Ding
et al
in Ref.
ding2013
. Its metric is,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=f(\rho)dt^{2}-\frac{1}{f(\rho)}d\rho^{2}-(\rho^{2}+p^{2})d\Omega^{2},

(95)

where the function
f(\rho)
is,

\displaystyle f(\rho)=1-\frac{3M}{p}\Big[\big(1+\frac{\rho^{2}}{p^{2}}\big)\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{\rho}{p}\big)-\frac{\rho}{p}\Big].

(96)

The black hole horizon
\rho_{h}
is the biggest root of the equation that,

\displaystyle\frac{3M}{p}\Big[\big(1+\frac{\rho_{h}^{2}}{p^{2}}\big)\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{\rho_{h}}{p}\big)-\frac{\rho_{h}}{p}\Big]=1.

(97)

Its Hawking temperature
T
is,

\displaystyle T=\frac{3M-\rho_{h}}{2\pi(p^{2}+\rho_{h}^{2})}.

(98)

The non-vanishing components of the tensor
\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}
and
R_{t}^{t}
are given by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\rho}\xi^{t}=-\nabla^{t}\xi^{\rho}=\frac{f^{\prime}(\rho)}{2},\quad R_{t}^{t}=\frac{3M}{pr^{2}}\Big[\big(1+\frac{3\rho^{2}}{p^{2}}\big)\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\frac{\rho}{p}\big)-\frac{3\rho}{p}\Big].

(99)

The function
g(\rho)
is

\displaystyle g(\rho)=\frac{3}{(3p^{2}+\rho^{2})}(1-f).

(100)

One uses the 2-surface element
d\Sigma_{\rho t}=-(\rho^{2}+p^{2})d\Omega/2
, the left and right hand sides of this integral are,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\partial\tilde{S}_{\infty}}\left[\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}+g(\rho)\omega^{ab}\right]d\Sigma_{ab}=\big(M-2M\big),

(101)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\partial\tilde{S}_{h}}\left[\nabla^{b}\xi^{a}+g(\rho)\omega^{ab}\right]d\Sigma_{ab}=\big(2TS-\rho_{h}\big).

(102)

So the integral (
66
) can give,

\displaystyle M=2TS+\big(2M-\rho_{h}\big),

(103)

which is very strange to us. And we find that the first law cannot be constructed.

Consistency around a cube property of Hirota’s discrete KdV equation and the lattice sine-Gordon equation
Nobutaka Nakazono

Abstract
. It has been unknown whether Hirota’s discrete Korteweg-de Vries equation and the lattice sine-Gordon equation have the consistency around a cube (CAC) property. In this paper, we show that they have the CAC property. Moreover, we also show that they can be extended to systems on the 3-dimensional integer lattice.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the following two integrable partial difference equations (P
\Delta
Es).
One is Hirota’s discrete Korteweg-de Vries (dKdV) equation
[
7
,
23
,
12
]
:

u_{l+1,m+1}-u_{l,m}=\dfrac{\beta_{m+1}-\alpha_{l}}{u_{l,m+1}}-\dfrac{\beta_{m}-\alpha_{l+1}}{u_{l+1,m}},

(1.1)

where
(l,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a lattice parameter,
u_{l,m}\in\mathbb{C}
is its function and
\alpha_{l},\beta_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
are parameters that depend only on
l
and
m
, respectively,
and the other is the lattice sine-Gordon (lsG) equation
[
13
]
:

\dfrac{x_{l+1,m+1}}{x_{l,m}}=\left(\dfrac{p_{l+1}-q_{m}x_{l+1,m}}{q_{m}-p_{l+1}x_{l+1,m}}\right)\left(\dfrac{q_{m+1}-p_{l}x_{l,m+1}}{p_{l}-q_{m+1}x_{l,m+1}}\right),

(1.2)

where
(l,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a lattice parameter,
x_{l,m}\in\mathbb{C}
is its function and
p_{l},q_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
are parameters that depend only on
l
and
m
, respectively.

The P
\Delta
E (
1.1
) is an integrable non-autonomous discrete version of the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation
[
14
]
:

w_{t}+6ww_{x}+w_{xxx}=0,

(1.3)

where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
w=w(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
,
which is known as a mathematical model of waves on shallow water surfaces.
On the other hand, the P
\Delta
E (
1.2
) is an integrable non-autonomous discrete version of the sine-Gordon equation:

\phi_{tt}-\phi_{xx}+\sin{\phi}=0,

(1.4)

where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\phi=\phi(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
,
which is known as a motion equation of a row of pendulums hanging from a rod and being coupled by torsion springs.

The consistency around a cube (CAC) property
[
19
,
18
,
22
,
21
,
20
]
is known as an integrability of quad-equations.
(For the CAC property and the definition of quad-equation, see Appendix
A.1
.)
As classifications of quad-equations by using the CAC property, the lists of equations by Adler-Bobenko-Suris (ABS)
[
1
,
2
]
, Boll
[
4
,
5
]
and Hietarinta
[
8
]
are known.
These classifications have been done under the following additional conditions:

[
1
]
:

All face-equations on the same cube are similar equations differing only by the parameter values and are invariant under the group
D_{4}
of the square symmetries.
Moreover, the CAC cube also has the tetrahedron property.

[
2
,
4
,
5
]
:

The face-equations on the same cube are allowed to be different, and no assumption is made about symmetry.
However, the tetrahedron property is still required.

[
8
]
:

No assumptions are made for symmetry or the tetrahedron property, but all face-equations are assumed to be homogeneous quadratic.
The face-equations on the same cube allow different equations on the three orthogonal planes but are the same equations on parallel planes.

Note here that a CAC-cube means a cube with the six quad-equations on its faces (face-equations) which has the CAC property.
However, the P
\Delta
Es (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are not included in these lists, and the structures of their consistency have not been reported.

Recently, it has been shown that the P
\Delta
Es (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) have the consistency around a broken cube (CABC) property
[
11
,
15
]

(For the CABC property, see Appendix

By considering the Bäcklund transformation (BT)

\displaystyle u_{l,m}-v_{l+1,m+1}-\dfrac{\beta_{m}-\alpha_{l+1}}{u_{l+1,m}}+\dfrac{\gamma-\alpha_{l}}{v_{l,m+1}}=0,

(1.5a)

\displaystyle(u_{l,m}-v_{l,m})\left(\dfrac{\beta_{m}-\alpha_{l}}{u_{l,m}}+v_{l,m+1}\right)-\beta_{m}+\gamma=0,

(1.5b)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\beta_{m}-\alpha_{l}}{u_{l,m}}-\dfrac{\gamma-\alpha_{l}}{v_{l,m}}-u_{l+1,m}+v_{l+1,m}=0,

(1.5c)

from the P
\Delta
E (

\begin{split}&\beta_{m}\left(v_{l,m}\,v_{l+1,m}-v_{l,m+1}\,v_{l+1,m+1}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\bigl(v_{l,m}-v_{l+1,m+1}\bigr)\bigl(v_{l+1,m}-v_{l,m+1}\bigr)\bigl(\gamma-v_{l,m}\,v_{l+1,m}\bigr)\bigl(\gamma-v_{l,m+1}\,v_{l+1,m+1}\bigr)\\

Similarly, by considering the following BT from the P
\Delta

1.2

\displaystyle\dfrac{v_{l,m+1}v_{l+1,m+1}}{p_{l}p_{l+1}}-\dfrac{x_{l,m}(p_{l+1}-q_{m}x_{l+1,m})}{p_{l+1}x_{l+1,m}-q_{m}}=0,

It is natural to consider that the CAC property can be shown using the CABC property because both are meant to show integrability and are the properties around a cube.
This is the motivation for this study.

4.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, using the CABC properties of the dKdV equation (
1.1
) and the lsG equation (
1.2
), we have shown that they have the CAC property.
Using the CABC and CAC properties, we also obtained their new Lax pairs.
Moreover, we have constructed the integrable system (
3.1
) on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
by stacking the cubes associated with the CABC property in the third direction and have clarified the structure of its
\tau
-function.

In addition to the dKdV and lsG equations, there exist other integrable P
\Delta
Es for which we still do not know whether they have the CAC property.
We have shown that it is useful to consider the CABC property to clarify the CAC property of P
\Delta
Es.
Many studies have been done to find P
\Delta
Es which have the CAC property (see §
1
).
However, the dKdV and lsG equations have the CAC property but are not included in these previous studies.
For the reasons above, classifying P
\Delta
Es with the CABC property may be important for clarifying the CAC property of known integrable P
\Delta
Es.
Moreover, it may lead to finding new integrable P
\Delta
Es.
This should be a subject of future research.

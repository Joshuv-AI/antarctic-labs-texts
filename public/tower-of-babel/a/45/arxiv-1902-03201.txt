On dimension of product of groups
Alexander Dranishnikov

Abstract
. We prove the product formula cd ⁡ ( Γ × Γ ) = 2 ​ cd ⁡ Γ \operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma for cohomological dimension of geometrically finite groups.



1.
Introduction

We recall that the cohomological dimension of a group
\Gamma
is defined as

\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\max\{n\mid H^{n}(\Gamma,M)\neq 0\}

where the maximum is taken over all

{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-modules
M
. If there is no such maximum we write
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\infty
.
By the Eilenberg-Ganea theorem
[
Br
]
the cohomological dimension
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
coincides with the geometric dimension of
\Gamma
whenever
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma\neq 2
. The geometric dimension of
\Gamma

is the minimal dimension of CW-complexes representing the Eilenberg-MacLane space
K(\Gamma,1)
.

Bestvina and Mess made a connection between the cohomological dimension of a hyperbolic torsion free group and topological dimension of its boundary
[
BM
]
,
[
Be
]
:

\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\dim\partial\Gamma+1.

In the 30s Pontryagin constructed compact subsets
X_{p}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{5}
for all prime
p
, now called Pontryagin surfaces, such that
\dim(X_{p}\times X_{q})=3
whenever
p\neq q

and
\dim(X_{p}\times X_{p})=4
for all
p
.
Then in the late 40s Boltyanskii constructed a 2-dimensional compact subset
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{5}
with
\dim(B\times B)=3
. In
[
Dr1
]
the right-angled Coxeter groups with boundaries
Pontryagin surfaces
X_{p}
were constructed for all

p
. This brought an example of a family of hyperbolic groups
\Gamma_{p}
with
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma_{p}=3
for all
p
and
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma_{p}\times\Gamma_{q})=5
for
p\neq q

[
Dr2
]
.
Also in
[
Dr1
]
it was proven that Boltyanskii compactum cannot be a boundary of a Coxeter group. This result left some hope for
the logarithmic law

\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma

for all geometrically finite groups. The above equality is the main result of this paper.
We recall that a group
\Gamma
is called geometrically finite if
there is a finite Eilenberg-MacLane complex
K(\Gamma,1)
.
Generaly, for groups which are not geometrically finite the above equality does not hold: For example, for the group of rationals
\operatorname{cd}{\mathbb{Q}}=2
and
\operatorname{cd}({\mathbb{Q}}\times{\mathbb{Q}})=3
.

The author is thankful to Alex Margolis for valuable remarks.

2.
Dimension of the square of a group

2.1.
Cohomological dimension of group with respect to a ring

Let
R
be a commutative ring with unit and let
\Gamma
be a group. By
R\Gamma
we denote the group ring,
Recall that
R\Gamma
is the ring of all functions
f:\Gamma\to R
with finite support and the convolution as the product.
The cohomological dimension of a group
\Gamma
with respect to the ring

R
is defined as follows
[
Br
]
,
[
Bi
]
,

\operatorname{cd}_{R}\Gamma=\max_{M}\{n\mid H^{n}(\Gamma,M)\neq 0\}

where the maximum is taken over all
R\Gamma
-modules. In the case
R={\mathbb{Z}}
we use the notation

\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma

and call the number
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
the
cohmological dimension
of
\Gamma
.

For a
\Gamma
-module
M
we use notations
H^{*}(\Gamma,M)
for cohomology groups of a discrete group
\Gamma
and
H^{*}(X;M)
for
cohomology groups of a topological space
X
with the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)=\Gamma
. Note that a
\Gamma
-module defines a locally trivial sheaf
\mathcal{M}
on
X
and

H^{*}(X;M)
can be treated as cohomology with coefficients in the sheaf
[
Bre
]
,
H^{*}(X;M)=H^{*}(X;\mathcal{M})
. Note that
H^{*}(\Gamma,M)=H^{*}(B\Gamma;M)
.

For abelian groups
A
and
B
we are using the notation
A\ast B=Tor(A,B)
. A group
\Gamma
is a group of finite type if there is a classifying CW complex
B\Gamma
with finite skeletons
B\Gamma^{(n)}
for all
n
.

We recall the Cohomology Universal Coefficient Formula for twisted coefficients

UCF:

For a group
\Gamma
of finite type, a
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module
M
, and an abelian group
R
there the Universal Coefficient Formula

[
Bre
]
:

H^{n}(\Gamma,M\otimes R)=H^{n}(\Gamma,M)\otimes R\ \bigoplus\ H^{n+1}(\Gamma,M)\ast R.

Note that
R\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\otimes R
for any commutative ring
R
. Then the UCF turns into

H^{n}(\Gamma,R\Gamma)=H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)\otimes R\ \bigoplus\ H^{n+1}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)\ast R.

We recall the following facts about cohomological dimension of groups
[
Bi
]
,
[
Br
]
:

(1)
\operatorname{cd}_{R}\Gamma
equals the minimal length of projective resolution of
R
as a trivial
R\Gamma
-module;

(2) For geometrically finite groups
\operatorname{cd}_{R}\Gamma=\max\{n\mid H^{n}(\Gamma,R\Gamma)\neq 0\}
.

2.1 Proposition
.

For any group
\Gamma
of finite type and any ring
R
,
\operatorname{cd}_{R}\Gamma\leq\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma<\infty
.
By the UCF if
H^{n}(\Gamma,R\Gamma)\neq 0
, then either
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)\neq 0
or
H^{n+1}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)\neq 0
.
In either case
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma)\geq n
.
∎

The main techical result of the paper is the following theorem which will be proven at the end of the section.

2.2 Theorem
.

For any geometrically finite group
\Gamma
there is a field
{\mathbb{k}}
such that
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma
.

The Kunneth Formula:

[
Bre
]

For groups
\Gamma
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
of finite type, a
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module
M
, and a
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma^{\prime}
module
M^{\prime}
with
M\ast M^{\prime}=0
as abelian groups,
there is the equality

H^{n}(\Gamma\times\Gamma^{\prime},M\otimes M^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{k}H^{k}(\Gamma,M)\otimes H^{n-k}(\Gamma^{\prime},M^{\prime})\oplus\bigoplus_{k}H^{k+1}(\Gamma,M)\ast H^{n-k}(\Gamma^{\prime},M^{\prime}).

Usually the Kunneth Formula as well as the Universal Coefficient Formula are stated as short exact sequences. This happens because the splitting of those short exact sequences is not natural. Since the naturality is not used in this paper we state both as formulas.

There is a similar formula for
R\Gamma
and
R\Gamma^{\prime}
modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
for any principal ideal domain
R

in which
\otimes
and
\ast
are taken over
R
. When
R=k
is a field, the Kunneth Formula turns into the following

H^{n}(\Gamma\times\Gamma^{\prime},M\otimes_{k}M^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}H^{k}(\Gamma,M)\otimes_{k}H^{n-k}(\Gamma^{\prime},M^{\prime}).

2.3 Proposition
.

For any geometrically finite group
\Gamma
and any field
{\mathbb{k}}
,

\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma=n
. Then the vector space
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma)\neq 0
is nonzero. By the Kunneth Formula over a field,

H^{2n}(\Gamma\times\Gamma,{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma)=H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma)\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma)\neq 0.

∎

Here is our main result.

2.4 Theorem
.

For any geometrically finite group
\Gamma
,

\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma.

Proof.

Let a field
{\mathbb{k}}
be as in Theorem
2.2
. Then by Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
2.1

2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=2\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{k}}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)\leq\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma).

The result follows in view of an obvious inequality
2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma\geq\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
.
∎

2.5 Corollary
.

For any geometrically finite group
\Gamma
,

\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})=n\operatorname{cd}\Gamma.

Proof.

In view of the obvious inequality
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})\leq n\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
it suffices to show the inequality
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})\geq n\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
.

By induction on
k
we obtain the equality
\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{2^{k}})=2^{k}\operatorname{cd}\Gamma
. For
n<2^{k}
the inequality

2^{k}\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{2^{k}})\leq\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})+\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{2^{k}-n})\leq\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})+(2^{k}-n)\operatorname{cd}\Gamma

implies that
n\operatorname{cd}\Gamma\leq\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma^{n})
.
∎

2.6 Corollary
.

Boltyaskii’s compactum cannot be a
Z
-boundary of a geometrically finite group.

Proof.

Suppose that Boltyanskii’s compactum
B
is a
Z
-boundary of a geometrically finite group
\Gamma
. Then the join product
B\ast B
is a
Z
-boundary of
\Gamma\times\Gamma
.
In view of Bestvina’s formula

\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}\partial G+1=\operatorname{cd}G.

for a group
G
with a
Z
-boundary
\partial G

[
Be
]
we obtain
a contradiction:

5=\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}(B\ast B)+1=\operatorname{cd}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=2(\dim_{\mathbb{Z}}B+1)=6.

∎

We use the notations
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
for the cyclic group of order
p
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{\infty}}=\lim_{\rightarrow}\{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}\}
for the quasi-cyclic group, and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
for the localization of
{\mathbb{Z}}
at
p
.

2.7 Proposition
.

For any geometrically finite group
\Gamma
there is a prime number
p
such that
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=n
. Let
T(A)
denote the torsion subgroup of
A=H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)
.
If
A/T(A)\neq 0
, then
A/T(A)\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\neq 0
and, hence,
A\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\neq 0
for all
p
.
By the UCF,
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)\neq 0
. Hence
\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma\geq n
.
Proposition
2.1
implies that
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma
.

Let
T(A)=A
. Every torsion abelian group
A
contains either
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{\infty}}
for some
p
as a direct summand
[
Fu
]
.
Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\neq 0
as well as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{\infty}}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\neq 0
, we obtain
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma)\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\neq 0
. Hence
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)\neq 0
and therefore,
\operatorname{cd}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma
.
∎

.

Suppose that

We apply induction on

. Since

.
Then

. This implies that

.

Assume that the statement of Proposition holds for

. Let
J\subset\oplus^{m+1}R\Gamma
and

where
M=(\oplus^{m+1}R\Gamma)/J
.
Let
\pi:\oplus^{m}R\Gamma\oplus R\Gamma\to\oplus^{m}R\Gamma

We consider the following commutative diagram

3.
Cohomological dimension over fields

We recall the excision theorem for cohomology with coefficients in a sheaf
[
Bre
]
, Proposition 12.3.

3.1 Theorem
.

If
A\subset X
is a closed subset of compact space, then for any sheaf
\mathcal{M}
on
X
there is a natural isomorphism

H^{p}(X,A;\mathcal{M})\cong H^{p}_{c}(X\setminus A;\mathcal{M}).

The excision theorem and the long exact sequence of pair imply the following

3.2 Proposition
.

For a finite complex
X
and any local coefficient system,

H^{m}(X;\mathcal{M})=H^{m}(X^{(m+1)};\mathcal{M})

for all
m
.

3.3 Theorem
.

For geometrically finite groups
\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
for all but finitely many prime numbers
p
.

Proof.

Let
B\Gamma
be a finite complex of
\dim B\Gamma=n
. Let
m>d=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
. Then
H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)=0
and
in view of Proposition
3.2
,
H^{m}(B\Gamma^{(m+1)};{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)=0
.
Let
\{C_{i},\partial_{i}\}
denote the cellular chain complex for
E\Gamma
.
The cohomology groups of
B\Gamma^{(m+1)}
are defined by means of the truncated chain complex

0\to C_{m+1}\to C_{m}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to C_{0}.

The condition
H^{m}(B\Gamma^{(m+1)},{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)=0
means that
the homomorphism

\delta:\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma}(C_{m-1},{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma}(C_{m}/\partial(C_{m+1}),{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)

defined by the boundary homomorphism
\partial:C_{m}\to C_{m-1}
is onto.
Let
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma}(C_{m-1},{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma)
be a generating set of the free
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-module.
For all
(m-1)
-cells in
B\Gamma
,
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\ell}
, we fix their lifts
\bar{\kappa}_{1},\dots,\bar{\kappa}_{\ell}
in
E\Gamma
.
Let

\alpha_{j}(\bar{\kappa}_{i})=\sum\lambda^{i,j}_{k}g^{i,j}_{k}\ \ \text{where}\ \ \lambda^{i,j}_{k}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ g^{i,j}_{k}\in\Gamma.

Clearly, there is a finite set of primes
\mathcal{P}_{m}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{s_{m}}\}
such that

\lambda^{i,j}_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{p_{1}\cdots p_{s}}]
. Then the homomorphism

\delta_{p}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}(C_{m-1},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}((C_{m}/\partial C_{m+1},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)

is surjective for all
p\notin\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Therefore,
H^{m}(B\Gamma^{(m+1)};{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)=0

for all
p\notin\mathcal{P}_{m}
. By Proposition
3.2
,
H^{m}(B\Gamma;{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}\Gamma)=0

for all
p\notin\mathcal{P}_{m}
.
Hence,

\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma\leq d
for all
p\notin\bigcup_{d<m\leq n}\mathcal{P}_{m}
.

The inequality
\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Gamma\leq\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}\Gamma
completes the proof.
∎

3.4 Proposition
.

For all groups for every prime
p
and any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}}\Gamma=\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Gamma.

Proof.

We apply induction on
k
. The short exact sequence
0\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k+1}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\to 0
produces the short exact sequence of
\Gamma
-modules

0\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k+1}}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\Gamma\to 0.

Let
\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k+1}}}\Gamma=n
. Then the coefficient long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k}}\Gamma)\to H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{k+1}}\Gamma)\to H^{n}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\Gamma)\to\dots

and the induction assumption imply that
\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Gamma\geq n
. The same sequence at dimension
n+1
together with the induction assumption implies that

\operatorname{cd}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}\Gamma<n+1
.
∎

Finitely generated torsion modules over
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma

An algebraic reason for the main result of the paper is the fact that for a geometrically finite group

{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module does not contain
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{\infty}}

4.1 Proposition
.

Let

be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-module for a geometrically finite group
\Gamma
where the underlying abelian group

is a torsion group. Then for any prime

the subgroup of
p
-torsions

M[p]\subset M

Proof.

Let

{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma

∎

4.2.
Application to topological complexity

Motivated by Topological Robotics, Michael Farber introduced the notion of topological complexity
TC(X)
of the configuration space
X

[
F
]
as the minimal number
k
such that

X\times X
can be covered by
k+1
open sets
U_{0},\dots,U_{k}
for each of which there is a continuous map
\phi_{i}:U_{i}\to C([0,1],X)
satisfying the condition

\phi_{i}(x,y)(0)=x
and
\phi_{i}(x,y)(1)=y
for all
(x,y)\in U_{i}
. Since
TC(X)
is a homotopy invariant, the topological complexity can be defined for groups
\Gamma
as
TC(B\Gamma)
.
Computation of topological complexity of groups presents a great challenge. The main theorem of this paper allows to complete the computation of topological complexity of hyperbolic groups
[
Dr3
]

TC(\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma

originated by Farber and Mescher
[
FM
]
.
Also in the case of geometrically finite groups the main theorem simplifies the formula for topological complexity of the free product of groups
[
DS
]
to the following

TC(\Gamma\ast\Gamma)=2\operatorname{cd}\Gamma.

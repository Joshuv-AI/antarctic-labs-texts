On ruin probabilities with investments in a risky asset with a switching regime price
Yuri Kabanov, Serguei Pergamenshchikov

Abstract
We investigate the asymptotic of ruin probabilities when the company invests its reserve in a risky asset with a switching regime price. We assume that the asset price is a conditional geometric Brownian motion with parameters modulated by a Markov process with a finite number of states. Using the technique of the implicit renewal theory we obtain the rate of convergence to zero of the ruin probabilities as the initial capital tends to infinity.

Introduction

Models, where an insurance company
invests its reserve (or a part of it) in a risky asset, constitutes an important class currently under an extensive study. Considering a single risky asset is justified by the common practice of investing in a market portfolio
or in an index (a fund which simulates an index like the DAX or the S&P500) which is an economically reasonable strategy. Since the insurance contacts usually are of a long duration and the return may depend, e.g., on the business cycles of economy, models with regime switching are now more and more popular. The main question is the rate of decay of the ruin probability as the initial reserve tends to infinity.

In this note
we extend the recent result of Ellanskaya and Kabanov,
EK
, established
for a model with characteristics of the asset price depending on a telegraph process, i.e. on a Markov process with two states,
0
and
1
. Here we study the case where the characteristics depend on the ergodic Markov process

\theta
with a finite
number of states. When
\theta_{t}=k
, the asset price evolves as a geometric Brownian motion with drift
a_{k}
and volatility

\sigma_{k}
. It is well known, see, e.g.
FrKP
;
KP
;
KPukh
, that in the case of a single regime, i.e. when the price process is the classic gBm with drift
a
and volatility
\sigma
,
the ruin probability decreases to zero as the initial capital
u
tends to infinity with the rate
\beta:=2a/\sigma-1
provided that
\beta>0
. In
EK
is was shown that in the model with two regimes,
0
and
1
, the ruin
probabilities decrease with a rate
\beta
where
\beta
is a number between the values
\beta_{k}:=2a_{k}/\sigma_{k}-1
,
k=0,1
, assumed to be strictly positive. This
\beta
is the root of an algebraic equation of third order and does not depend on the initial value of
\theta
.

In the present paper we extend this result to the case where
the number of states of the hidden Markov process
\theta
is
K\geq 2
. It happens that, provided all
\beta_{k}>0
,
k=0,\dots,K-1
, the rate of convergence
to zero of the ruin probabilities, depending, in general, on the initial state
i
, is a root of the cumulant generating function of the value of log price process at the first return time of
\theta
to the state
i
.
It is worth to note that the switching by telegraph signal is rather specific: the latter returns to the initial state after the second jump while for a general Markov process the return may happen after arbitrary number of jumps.

Though the main idea is again based on the implicit renewal theory, it happens that the analysis of the considered model is much more complicated and the calculation of the rate parameter, depending, in
general, on the initial state, is not so straightforward.
We hope that the result of this paper elucidate challenging problems of estimation of ruin probabilities for other stochastic volatility models.

The model

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},{\bf F}=({\cal F}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}},{\bf P})

,
a Poisson random measure

on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}
with
the mean
\tilde{\pi}(dt,dx)=\Pi(dx)dt
, and a piecewise constant right-continuous Markov process
\theta=(\theta_{t})
. For the latter we assume that it takes values in the finite set
\{0,1,...,K-1\}

K\times K

transition intensity matrix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{jk})
with communicating states,
and the initial value
\theta_{0}=i
(so that
\theta=\theta^{i})

The
\sigma

,
\pi

Recall that
\lambda_{jj}=-\sum_{k\neq j}\lambda_{jk}

\lambda_{i}:=-\lambda_{ii}>0

Let

be the successive jumps
of the Poisson process

be the successive jumps of
\theta

\tau_{0}=0

Recall that the lengths of the intervals between the consecutive jumps of

The reserve

of an insurance company evolves not only due to the business activity

Cramér–Lundberg model, but also due to the stochastic interest rate. We assume that the reserve is fully invested in a risky asset whose price

is a conditional geometric Brownian motion given the Markov process
\theta
. That is,

is given by a so-called hidden Markov model with

dS_{t}=S_{t}(a_{\theta_{t}}dt+\sigma_{\theta_{t}}dW_{t}),\qquad S_{0}=1,

where

,
\sigma_{k}>0
,

. In this case,

X_{t}=u+\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}dR_{s}+dP_{t}

(1)

where
dR_{t}=a_{\theta_{t}}dt+\sigma_{\theta_{t}}dW_{t}=dS_{t}/S_{t}
, that is

is the relative price process,

P_{t}=ct+\int_{0}^{t}\int x\pi(dt,dx)=ct+x*\pi_{t}.

(2)

So, the reserve evolution is described by the process
(X^{u},\theta)=(X^{u,i},\theta^{i})

where

is the initial capital and
i
is the initial regime, i.e. the initial value of
\theta
.

We assume that

is not an increasing process: otherwise the probability of ruin is zero.

We also assume that

\Pi({\mathbb{R}})<\infty
, that is

\Pi(dx)=\alpha_{1}F_{1}(dx)+\alpha_{2}F_{2}(dx)

is a probability distribution on

compound Poisson processes with intensities

of jumps downwards and upwards and whose absolute values have the distributions

and

The solution of the linear equation (

X_{t}^{u}={\cal E}_{t}(R)(u-Y_{t})=e^{V_{t}}(u-Y_{t})

(3)

where

Y_{t}:=-\int_{[0,t]}{\cal E}^{-1}_{s}(R)dP_{s}=-\int_{[0,t]}e^{-V_{s}}dP_{s}=-e^{-V}\cdot P_{t},

(4)

the stochastic exponential
{\cal E}_{t}(R)
is equal to
{S_{t}}
, and
the log price process

admits the stochastic differential

dV_{s}=\sigma_{\theta_{s}}dW_{s}+(a_{\theta_{s}}-(1/2)\sigma_{\theta_{s}}^{2})ds,\quad V_{0}=0.

Of course,

,

,

, and

depend on

Let
\tau^{u,i}:=\inf\{t>0:\ X^{u,i}_{t}\leq 0\}
be the instant of ruin corresponding to the initial capital
u
and the initial regime
i

Then

\Psi_{i}(u):={\bf P}[\tau^{u,i}<\infty]

It is clear that

.

Recall that the constant parameter values

,
\sigma=0
, correspond to the
Cramér–Lundberg model for which the process

. In the actuarial literature the compound Poisson process

P_{t}=ct-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{t}}\xi_{k}

(5)

where either
\xi_{k}\geq 0
,

(i.e.
F_{2}=0
— jumps only downwards — the case of non-life insurance) or
\xi_{k}\leq 0
,

(i.e.

— jumps only upwards — the case of life insurance or annuity payments). Models with both kinds of jumps
are also considered in the literature, see, e.g.,

For the classical models with a positive average trend and

having a “non-heavy” tail, the Lundberg inequality asserts that the ruin probability decreases
exponentially as the initial capital

increases to infinity.
For the exponentially distributed claims the ruin
probability admits an explicit expression, see

For the models with investment in a risky asset the situation is completely
different. For example, for the model with exponentially distributed jumps
and price following a geometric Brownian motion with the drift coefficient

a
and the volatility
\sigma>0
in the case where

Cu^{1-2a/\sigma^{2}}
. If

,
KP
,
PZ
,
KPukh
.

To formulate our result for the model where the volatility and drift are modulated by
a finite-state Markov process
we assume
throughout this note that

7

\beta_{j}:=2a_{j}/\sigma_{j}^{2}-1>0,\quad j=0,...,K-1,

(in other words,

Let

Proposition 1

The function
{\Upsilon}_{i}
is strictly convex, continuous, and there is unique
\gamma_{i}>0
such that
{\Upsilon}_{i}(\gamma_{i})=1
.

Note that one can characterize

also as the strictly positive roots of the cumulant generating functions

, strictly convex and continuous.

Postponing the proof of Proposition
1

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that
\Pi(|x|^{\gamma_{i}}):=\int|x|^{\gamma_{i}}\Pi(dx)<\infty
. Then

0<\liminf_{u\to\infty}u^{\gamma_{i}}\Psi_{i}(u)\leq\limsup_{u\to\infty}u^{\gamma_{i}}\Psi_{i}(u)<\infty.

Important remark.
In the case where
\theta
is a telegraph signal, i.e.
a two-state Markov process, the values
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
coincide (see

). In the considered general case,

may depend on the initial value
i

To alleviate formulae, we fix the initial value
i=0
and omit the index

when this does not lead to ambiguity.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is based on the implicit renewal theory.
To apply it, we verify that the random variables
Q=Q_{1}:=-e^{-V}\cdot P_{{\upsilon}_{1}}

belong to
L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
, the process
Y
has at infinity a finite limit

and show that
Y_{\infty}
is a random variable unbounded from above with the same law as
Q+MY_{\infty}
where

M=M_{1}
and
{\bf E}[M_{1}^{\gamma}]=1
. Also
{\bf E}[M_{1}^{\gamma+{\varepsilon}}]<\infty
for some

{\varepsilon}>0
. Clearly, the law of the random variable

is not arithmetic. We establish the bounds
\bar{G}(u)\leq\Psi_{i}(u)\leq C\bar{G}(u)
where
\bar{G}_{i}(u)={\bf P}[Y_{\infty}>u]
and a constant

, Lemma
6
.

With these facts Theorem
2.1
follows from Theorem

Go91
, combined with a statement on strict positivity of

due to Guivarc’h and Le Page,

Theorem 2.2

Let
Y_{\infty}
has the same law as
Q+MY_{\infty}
where
M>0
.
Suppose that
(M,Q)
is such that the law of

is non-arithmetic and, for some
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}[M^{\gamma}]=1,\ \ \ {\bf E}[M^{\gamma}\,(\ln\,M)^{+}]<\infty,\ \ \ {\bf E}[|Q|^{\gamma}]<\infty.

(7)

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\,u^{\gamma}\,{\bf P}[Y_{\infty}>u]=C_{+}<\infty,

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to\infty}\,u^{\gamma}\,{\bf P}[Y_{\infty}<-u)]=C_{-}<\infty,

If the random variable

is unbounded from above, then

Integrability of

The following identity is obvious:

Y_{{\upsilon}_{n}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}e^{-(V_{{\upsilon}_{j}}-V_{{\upsilon}_{j-1}})}\int_{]{\upsilon}_{k-1},{\upsilon}_{k}]}e^{-(V_{s}-V_{{\upsilon}_{k-1}})}dP_{s}.

(12)

The random variables

Lemma 2

Let

Then

\displaystyle C(q,\lambda,\beta,\sigma):={\bf E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-(\sigma W_{s}+(1/2)\sigma^{2}\beta s)}ds\right)^{q}\right]<\infty.

Proof.
Put
W^{(\sigma\beta/2)}_{s}:=W_{s}+(1/2)\sigma\beta s

\rho q<r(\lambda,\beta,\sigma)

{\bf E}\left[\int_{0}^{{\upsilon}_{1}}e^{\delta(V_{\upsilon_{1}}-V_{s}})ds\right]<\infty,\quad\quad{\bf E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{{\upsilon}_{1}}e^{(V_{\upsilon_{1}}-V_{s})}ds\right)^{\delta}\right]<\infty.

(19)

Proof.
The arguments are very similar to that of Lemma
3
and we only sketch them. The only new feature is that we need to consider processes of the form
(V_{T}-V_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
rather than
(V_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
.
The crucial observation is that the process
(W_{T}-W_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
in the reversed time
s^{\prime}:=T-s
is a Wiener process.

First, observe that

{\bf E}\left[\int_{0}^{{\upsilon}_{1}}e^{\delta(V_{\upsilon_{1}}-V_{s})}ds\right]={\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}e^{\delta(V_{\upsilon_{1}}-V_{\tau_{k}})}\int_{\tau_{k-1}}^{\tau_{k}}e^{\delta(V_{\tau_{k}}-V_{s})}ds\right]

Given a trajectory of
\theta
, the exponential and the integral in each summand are conditionally independent
and their conditional expectations admit explicit expressions. For the integral it is

1/\lambda_{\vartheta_{k-1}}\tilde{f}_{\vartheta_{k-1}}(\delta)
where
\tilde{f}_{j}
in (
18
).
Note that for
\delta\in]0,|\beta^{*}|[
the conditional
expectation of the integral is dominated by
1/\lambda_{*}
implying that

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\int_{0}^{{\upsilon}_{1}}e^{\delta(V_{\upsilon_{1}}-V_{s})}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{*}}{\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}e^{\delta(V_{\tau_{\varpi}}-V_{\tau_{k}})}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{*}}{\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}\prod_{n=k}^{\varpi-1}e^{\delta(V_{\tau_{n+1}}-V_{\tau_{n}})}\right]=\frac{1}{\lambda_{*}}{\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}\prod_{n=k}^{\varpi-1}\tilde{f}_{\theta_{n}}(\delta)\right].

Due to the choice of
\beta
, we have that
\tilde{f}^{*}:=\max_{j}\tilde{f}_{j}(\delta)<1
and, therefore,

{\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}\prod_{n=k}^{\varpi-1}\tilde{f}_{\theta_{n}}(\delta)\right]\leq{\bf E}\left[\sum^{\varpi}_{k=1}(\tilde{f}^{*})^{\varpi-k}\right]\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\tilde{f}^{*})^{k}<\infty.

The first property in (
19
) is proven.

Let
\tau
be an exponential random variable with parameter
\lambda>0
. For any
\delta\in]0,\widetilde{r}[
, where

\widetilde{r}:=\sqrt{{2\lambda}/{\sigma^{2}}+{\beta^{2}}/{4}}+|\beta|/{2},

we have, according to (1.1.1) in Ch. 2 of the reference book
BS
, that

\tilde{C}(\delta,\lambda,\beta,\sigma):={\bf E}\left[e^{\delta\sigma\sup_{s\leq\tau}W^{(\sigma\beta/2)}_{s}}\right]=\frac{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}-\delta}<\infty.

We get (as in Corollary
1
) that for all
k\geq 1

{\bf E}\left[\left(\int_{\tau_{k-1}}^{\tau_{k}}e^{V_{\tau_{k}}-V_{s}}ds\right)^{\delta}\right]\leq\tilde{C}^{*}(\delta),

with some constant
\tilde{C}^{*}(\delta)<\infty
, and we complete the proof of the second property in (
19
) as in Lemma
3
.
\Box

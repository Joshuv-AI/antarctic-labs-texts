Dagger groups and p p -adic distribution algebras
Aranya Lahiri, Claus Sorensen, Matthias Strauch

Abstract
. Let ( G , ω ) (G,\omega) be a p p -saturated group and K / ℚ p K/\mathbb{Q}_{p} a finite extension. In this paper we introduce the space of K K -valued overconvergent functions 𝒞 † ​ ( G , K ) \mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K) . In the process we promote the rigid analytic group attached to ( G , ω ) (G,\omega) in [ LS23 ] to a dagger group. A main result of this article is that under certain assumptions (satisfied for example when G G is a uniform pro- p p group) the distribution algebra D † ​ ( G , K ) D^{\dagger}(G,K) , i.e. the strong dual of 𝒞 † ​ ( G , K ) \mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K) , is a Fréchet-Stein algebra in the sense of [ ST03 ] . In the last section we introduce overconvergent representations and show that there is an anti-equivalence of categories between overconvergent G G -representations of compact type and continuous D † ​ ( G , K ) D^{\dagger}(G,K) -modules on nuclear Fréchet spaces. This is analogous to the anti-equivalence between locally analytic representations and modules over the locally analytic distribution algebra as proved in [ ST02 ] .



1.
Introduction

The theory of locally analytic representations plays a central role in the
p
-adic Langlands program. For now, let
G
be any locally analytic group (over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for simplicity).
In
[
ST02
, Cor. 3.3]
Schneider and Teitelbaum establish a duality between locally analytic
G
-representations on
K
-vector spaces of compact type and certain
modules over the locally analytic distribution algebra
D(G,K)
. (Here
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a fixed finite extension which serves as our coefficient field throughout this paper.)
In the sequel
[
ST03
]
they introduce the notion of a Fréchet-Stein algebra, and one of their main results is that
D(G,K)
has this property when
G
is compact.
This gives rise to an abelian category of coadmissible
D(G,K)
-modules, and by duality an abelian category of
admissible
locally analytic representations. Moreover, in
[
ST03
, Sect. 8]
they develop a dimension theory for modules over a Fréchet-Stein algebra. The Fréchet-Stein structure on
D(G,K)
has many other applications. We mention
the irreducibility criterion for locally analytic principal series representations in
[
OS10
]
, the proof of which makes critical use of the Fréchet-Stein structure.

In this paper we will narrow our focus and only consider
p
-valuable groups. Instead of
D(G,K)
we will introduce and study a distribution algebra
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
which is dual to the space of overconvergent functions on
G
. One of our goals (Theorem
1.2
below) is to show
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is Fréchet-Stein when
G
is uniform.

To state our results precisely, let
(G,\omega)
be a
p
-valued group. Thus
G
is a pro-
p
-group endowed with a function
\omega:G\backslash\{1\}\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
satisfying the axioms (a)–(d) in
[
Sch11
, p. 169]
. Moreover, the topology of
G
is defined by
\omega
. Note that
G
is necessarily torsion-free since
\omega(g^{p})=\omega(g)+1
for all
g\in G
.

Without loss of generality we may and will assume
\omega
is
\mathbb{Q}
-valued, see
[
Sch11
, Cor. 33.3]
. Furthermore, we will exclusively consider
saturated
groups
(G,\omega)
. This means
\omega(g)>\frac{p}{p-1}\Rightarrow g\in G^{p}
. Also,
(G,\omega)
is assumed to be of finite rank
d
, and we may therefore choose an ordered basis
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
by
[
Sch11
, Prop. 26.6]
. This puts a global chart on
G
via the homeomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})

\displaystyle\longmapsto g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}.

Recall that
\omega(g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}})=\min_{i=1,\ldots,d}\{\omega(g_{i})+v(x_{i})\}
by virtue of
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
being an ordered basis. (Here
v
is the standard valuation on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
v(p)=1
.) Via
\psi
we view functions on
G
as functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
, and vice versa.

The construction in
[
LS23
]
associates a rigid analytic group
\mathbb{G}^{\text{rig}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with any saturated group
(G,\omega)
as above. Its underlying space is
the polydisc
\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle)
, and there is a natural isomorphism of abstract groups
\mathbb{G}^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}G
. In this paper
we first refine this construction and promote
\mathbb{G}^{\text{rig}}
to a
dagger
group
\mathbb{G}
(by which we mean a group object in the category of dagger spaces, cf.
[
GK00
]
).

In the process we introduce the space
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
of overconvergent functions
f:G\longrightarrow K
taking values in a finite extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Via
\psi
such
f
correspond to functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
which extend to rigid functions on slightly larger polydiscs. See Definition
5.5
below for a more precise formulation. The space

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
is endowed with a natural locally convex topology known as the fringe (or direct limit) topology.

The central object in this article is the continuous dual
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
equipped with the strong topology. This becomes an algebra under convolution, essentially due to the existence of the dagger group
\mathbb{G}
. In this paper we establish some foundational structural properties of these
\dagger
-distribution algebras.

Our first general result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any saturated
p
-valued group
(G,\omega)
as above,
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is a nuclear Fréchet algebra. (That is, it can be expressed as an inverse limit of Banach
K
-algebras with compact transition maps.)

The Banach algebras in this Theorem are rigid distribution algebras
D^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{G}_{N},K)
(see
[
Em17
, Cor. 5..1.8]
for their general definition) of a sequence of affinoid rigid analytic groups
(\mathbb{G}_{N})_{N\geq 1}
which we call the
strict neighborhood groups
of the dagger group
\mathbb{G}
. The underlying space of
\mathbb{G}_{N}
is the
polydisc
\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})
where
\tau_{N,i}=\frac{1}{N+1}(\omega(g_{i})-\frac{1}{p-1})
. One of the key technical aspects of our work is to endow these polydiscs with a group structure compatible with the one on
\mathbb{G}
.

Unfortunately, rigid distribution algebras such as
D^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{G}_{N},K)
are not Noetherian in general. This was observed by Berthelot, and the argument is exposed in

[
Clo18
, App. A.2]
for example. As a result, it is not at all clear whether
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is Fréchet-
Stein
– in the sense of
[
ST03
, Sect. 3]
. Although we believe this to be true in general, we can currently only prove it for certain equi-
p
-valued groups (the main example being uniform groups). This is our second main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
(G,\omega)
is equi-
p
-valued, with
\omega(g_{1})=\cdots=\omega(g_{d})=\omega_{0}
. Assume
2\omega_{0}>\frac{p}{p-1}
. Then there is an isomorphism of topological

K
-algebras,

(1.3)

D^{\dagger}(G,K)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\varprojlim}_{s<\theta^{1/\omega_{0}}}D_{s}(G,K),

where
\theta=p^{-1/(p-1)}
denotes the radius of convergence of the
p
-adic exponential function, and
D_{s}(G,K)
are the algebras from
[
ST03
, p. 162]
.
In particular
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is a Fréchet-Stein algebra.

The inequality
2\omega_{0}>\frac{p}{p-1}
is exactly the condition (HYP) in
[
ST03
, p. 163]
. As we explain in Example
8.8
this is satisfied for a uniform pro-
p
-group

G
endowed with the canonical
p
-valuation
\omega^{\text{can}}
which gives the lower
p
-series. In this example
\omega_{0}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p>2$}\\
2&\text{if $p=2$}\end{cases}
.

We emphasize that, in the situation of Theorem
1.2
, the Fréchet-Stein structure on
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
follows immediately by appealing to
[
ST03
, Thm. 4.5, Thm. 4.9]
.
Our contribution is the isomorphism (
1.3
).

In Section
9
we introduce the notion of an overconvergent
G
-representation (of compact type) and establish a duality à la
[
ST02
]
with continuous

D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-modules on nuclear Fréchet spaces.

As a future project we intend to view the base change map in
[
Clo17
, Thm. 4.1]
through the lens of
\dagger
-distribution algebras. Classically, unramified base change is given by a morphism
b:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
between spherical Hecke algebras (for
\text{GL}_{n}
say, to fix ideas). Clozel has defined an analogous base change map
b^{\text{rig}}:D^{\text{rig}}(I_{r},K)\longrightarrow D^{\text{rig}}(I,K)
between the rigid distribution algebras for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups
I
and
I_{r}
in
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{r}})
respectively – assuming
p
is large enough. (Our notation differs from Clozel’s – he denotes our
b^{\text{rig}}
by
b:\mathcal{D}_{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
.) A natural question is whether there is a
\dagger
-version

b^{\dagger}:D^{\dagger}(I_{r},K)\longrightarrow D^{\dagger}(I,K)
. As noted above, we expect the algebra
D^{\dagger}(I,K)
to be Fréchet-Stein (and similarly for
I_{r}
) but we do not currently have a proof of this.

2.
Notation

We let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. If
\mathcal{K}
is a field, a power series in
\mathcal{K}[\![X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]\!]
will be written as
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
.
Here
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
is a tuple of non-negative integers and
X^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. We adopt the notation

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.

When
\mathcal{K}
is a non-archimedean local field,
T_{n}
denotes the Tate algebra of all power series
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}

satisfying the condition
c_{\alpha}\longrightarrow 0
as
|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty
. The Gauss norm of such a power series is

\|{\sum}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\|={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}|c_{\alpha}|,

and
T_{n}
thus becomes a Banach algebra over
\mathcal{K}
.

3.
The category of dagger spaces

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a non-archimedean local field with absolute value
|\cdot|
. It extends uniquely to an absolute value on the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, and we let

\Gamma=|\overline{\mathcal{K}}^{\times}|
denote the valuation subgroup of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. (Eventually we will take
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, in which case
\Gamma=p^{\mathbb{Q}}
.) In this section we review the category of dagger spaces over
\mathcal{K}
introduced in
[
GK00
, Df. 2.12]
.

3.1.
Dagger algebras

We start with the Washnitzer algebra
W_{n}=\mathcal{K}\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\rangle^{\dagger}
in the variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. This is the subalgebra of the Tate algebra
T_{n}
consisting of all power series
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}[\![X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]\!]
such that
|c_{\alpha}|r^{|\alpha|}\longrightarrow 0
as
|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty
, for some
r>1
. Since
\Gamma
is dense in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we may and will assume
r\in\Gamma
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
we let

T_{n,r}=\bigg\{{\sum}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}[\![X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]\!]:{\lim}_{|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty}|c_{\alpha}|r^{|\alpha|}=0\bigg\}.

By
[
BGR84
, Sect. 6.1.5, Thm. 4]
this is a
\mathcal{K}
-affinoid algebra with norm

\|{\sum}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\|_{r}={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}|c_{\alpha}|r^{|\alpha|}.

There are two natural ways to put a topology on
W_{n}
. There is the topology induced by the Gauss norm on the ambient
T_{n}
, but to us the following finer topology will be more important in the sequel.
Obviously
W_{n}=\bigcup_{r\in\Gamma_{>1}}T_{n,r}
, and we endow
W_{n}
with the finest locally convex topology for which all the inclusions

T_{n,r}\hookrightarrow W_{n}
are continuous. See
[
Sch02
, p. 22]
for more details on locally convex final topologies. In
[
GK00
, Sect. 4.2]
the latter is referred to as the direct limit topology on
W_{n}
. In
[
Ked06
, Df. 2.3.7]
it is called the fringe topology.

A dagger algebra is a
\mathcal{K}
-algebra
A
which is isomorphic to
W_{n}/I
for some ideal
I\subset W_{n}
, for some
n
. The quotient semi-norm on
W_{n}/I
turns out to be a norm
[
GK00
, Prop. 1.6]
. However, we prefer to endow
A
with the direct limit topology as in
[
GK00
, Sect. 4.2]
:
A\simeq\varinjlim_{r\rightarrow 1}T_{n,r}/IT_{n,r}
, where we take the limit over sufficiently small
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
(smaller than an
r_{0}>1
for which
T_{n,r_{0}}\supset I
).

Definition 3.2
.

For
A
as above, we let
\hat{A}
denote its completion with respect to the natural equivalence class of norms on
A\simeq W_{n}/I
. (In
[
GK00
]
the completion is denoted by
A^{\prime}
. To avoid confusion with duals later on, we adopt the notation
\hat{A}
.)

The completion
\hat{A}
is an affinoid
\mathcal{K}
-algebra, with an associated rigid space
\text{Sp}(\hat{A})
.

3.2.
Dagger spaces

\text{Sp}(A)
is the set of maximal ideals of a dagger algebra
A
. By
[
GK00
, Thm. 1.7]
there is a bijection
\text{Sp}(\hat{A})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\text{Sp}(A)
given by pulling back along the completion

A\rightarrow\hat{A}
. Via this bijection we transfer the usual
G
-topology on
\text{Sp}(\hat{A})
to a
G
-topology on
\text{Sp}(A)
. As in rigid geometry, the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\text{Sp}(A)}
is determined by its values on affinoid subdomains
U\subset\text{Sp}(A)
. We will not need the exact definitions in our paper, and we refer the reader to
[
GK00
, Sect. 2]
for all details. We emphasize that
\mathcal{O}_{\text{Sp}(A)}(U)
is again a dagger algebra for such
U
.

A dagger space over
\mathcal{K}
is then a locally
G
-ringed space
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
which admits an admissible covering
X=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
such that all
(U_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}})
are affinoid
\mathcal{K}
-dagger spaces (that is, isomorphic to
\text{Sp}(A_{i})
for some dagger algebras
A_{i}
). Morphisms between dagger spaces are morphisms of locally
G
-ringed spaces. This gives a
category
\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
which admits fiber products by
[
GK00
, 1.16, 2.13]
. Moreover, by
[
GK00
, Thm. 2.19]
there is a faithful functor
\widehat{(\cdot)}:\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}\longrightarrow\text{Rig}_{\mathcal{K}}
to the category of rigid spaces over
\mathcal{K}
which takes an affinoid dagger space
\text{Sp}(A)
to the rigid space
\text{Sp}(\hat{A})
.

3.3.
Distributions

Let
A
be a dagger algebra over
\mathcal{K}
, expressed as a locally convex inductive limit of
\mathcal{K}
-affinoid algebras
A_{r}=T_{n,r}/IT_{n,r}
, indexed by small enough
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
as above. If
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
, the transition map
A_{r_{2}}\rightarrow A_{r_{1}}
is compact by
[
Em17
, Prop. 2.1.16]
for example.
(Note that
\text{Sp}(A_{r})
is a closed subvariety of a rigid polydisc.) See also
[
Sch02
, p. 98]
. In other words,
A
is a locally convex space of compact type, cf.
[
Em17
, Df. 1.1.16, part (ii)]
. By passing to quotients of the
A_{r}
, one can write
A
as an inductive limit of affinoid algebras with compact and
injective
transition maps, as explained in
[
Em17
, p. 15]
for instance.

Definition 3.3
.

The space of distributions on
\text{Sp}(A)
is the strong dual
A_{b}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}(A,\mathcal{K})_{b}
. (We stress that
A
carries the direct limit topology here.)

Thus
A_{b}^{\prime}
is a nuclear Fréchet space. If the transition maps
A_{r_{2}}\rightarrow A_{r_{1}}
are injective (this happens when
A=W_{n}
for example)
[
Sch02
, Prop. 16.10]

gives a topological isomorphism

A_{b}^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\varprojlim(A_{r})_{b}^{\prime}.

Moreover, each
(A_{r})_{b}^{\prime}
is just the dual
\mathcal{L}(A_{r},\mathcal{K})
with the operator norm topology, cf.
[
Sch02
, Rem. 6.7]
.

4.
Dagger groups

4.1.
Group objects

\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
has a terminal object
\text{Sp}(\mathcal{K})
, and finite products such as
X\times_{\text{Sp}(\mathcal{K})}Y
exist. It therefore makes sense to talk about group objects in
\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A dagger group over
\mathcal{K}
is a group object in the category
\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Thus a dagger group
G
comes with three morphisms in
\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
,

G\times_{\text{Sp}(\mathcal{K})}G\overset{m}{\longrightarrow}G,\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\text{Sp}(\mathcal{K})\overset{e}{\longrightarrow}G,\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptG\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}G,

satisfying the usual conditions (
m
is associative,
e
is a two-sided unit, and
i
is a two-sided inverse). Equivalently
X\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}}(X,G)
gives a contravariant functor from
\text{Dag}_{\mathcal{K}}
to the category of groups.

All dagger groups considered later in this paper will be affinoid. Thus
G=\text{Sp}(A)
for a dagger algebra
A
equipped with morphisms of
\mathcal{K}
-algebras

(4.2)

A\overset{m^{*}}{\longrightarrow}A\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}A,\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptA\overset{e^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{K},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptA\overset{i^{*}}{\longrightarrow}A,

such that the standard diagrams commute. See
[
Spr09
, p. 22]
for instance. We recall the definition of the tensor product
\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}
from
[
GK00
, 1.16]
: If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are dagger algebras, first write them as quotients of Washnitzer algebras
W_{n_{i}}\twoheadrightarrow A_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
A_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}A_{2}
is defined to be the image of
W_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
under the natural map
T_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\rightarrow\hat{A}_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K}}\hat{A}_{2}
. For example,
W_{n_{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}W_{n_{2}}=W_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
.

Remark 4.3
.

As a locally convex space,
A_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}A_{2}
is the same as the tensor product
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K},\pi}A_{2}
considered in
[
Sch02
, p. 107]
and

[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.32]
for example. (The latter is the Hausdorff completion of
A_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}A_{2}
with the projective tensor product topology, cf.
[
Sch02
, Ch. 17, part B]
. Under our assumptions
A_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K},\pi}A_{2}
coincides with the inductive tensor product
A_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{K},\iota}A_{2}
, cf.
[
Sch02
, Ch. 17, part A]
, and we may drop the subscripts
\pi
and
\iota
.) To see this, let
A_{i}=W_{n_{i}}
for simplicity. Then, by
[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.32]
,

W_{n_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K},\pi}W_{n_{2}}=\varinjlim_{r_{1},r_{2}}T_{n_{1},r_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K}}T_{n_{2},r_{2}}=\varinjlim_{r}T_{n_{1},r}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K}}T_{n_{2},r}=\varinjlim_{r}T_{n_{1}+n_{2},r}=W_{n_{1}+n_{2}}.

Remark 4.4
.

We note that the morphisms in (
4.2
) are automatically continuous for the norm topology by
[
GK00
, Prop. 1.6]
. See also
[
Ked06
, Df. 2.2.2]
. In fact, any
\mathcal{K}
-algebra morphism between dagger algebras is automatically continuous – not just for the norm topology, but also with respect to the direct limit topology by
[
GK00
, Lem. 1.8]
and
[
Ked06
, Cor. 2.3.3, Df. 2.3.7]
.

4.2.
Distribution algebras

We consider the duals of the maps in (
4.2
). First we observe that by Remark
4.3
, combined with
[
Sch02
, Prop. 20.13]
and
[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.32]
, we have

(A\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}^{\dagger}A)_{b}^{\prime}=(A\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K},\pi}A)_{b}^{\prime}=A_{b}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K},\pi}A_{b}^{\prime}.

Thus the dual of
m^{*}
gives a jointly continuous and associative
\mathcal{K}
-bilinear multiplication map on the space of distributions

\ast:A_{b}^{\prime}\times A_{b}^{\prime}\longrightarrow A_{b}^{\prime}.

(Associativity follows from dualizing the first diagram in
[
Spr09
, p. 22]
.) Furthermore, the dual of
e^{*}
gives a two-sided unit

\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow A_{b}^{\prime}
for
\ast
(by dualizing the third diagram in
[
Spr09
, p. 22]
).

We conclude that any dagger group of the form
G=\text{Sp}(A)
gives rise to a distribution algebra, whose underlying space
A_{b}^{\prime}
is nuclear Fréchet. One of the themes of this article is
to show
A_{b}^{\prime}
is a nuclear Fréchet
algebra
, see
[
Em17
, Df. 1.2.12]
, for
G
arising naturally from certain
p
-valued groups. Essentially what this means is we want to promote
\text{Sp}(A_{r})
to a rigid analytic group for a cofinal set of
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
. In general, for a rank
d
saturated group, we are forced to replace
r
by a sequence of polyradii
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
for this to work. See
6.2
below.

5.
Examples of dagger groups

5.1.
A refinement of
[
Sch11
, Prop. 29.2]

Let
(G,\omega)
be a
p
-valued group with ordered basis
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
. For any (finite) sequence of elements
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
from
G
, and any tuple
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{s}
, we form the product
h_{1}^{y_{1}}\cdots h_{s}^{y_{s}}
and express it as
g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}
for a uniquely determined tuple
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
.

[
Sch11
, Prop. 29.2]
tells us that
x_{i}=F_{i}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
for certain power series
F_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{s}\rangle
.

A close inspection of the proof immediately gives the

5.3.
Overconvergent functions on a saturated group

We keep our saturated
p
-valued group
(G,\omega)
. For the most part we will suppress the
p
-valuation
\omega
from the notation. Fix a finite field extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}

and consider the space
\mathcal{C}(G,K)
of all continuous functions

. In this section we will define the subspace
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
of
overconvergent
functions.
This is parallel to the definition of the space of rigid functions
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(G,K)
in
[
LS23
, Df. 4.5]
.

Pick an ordered basis
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
for
(G,\omega)
. Then each power series

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}\in K\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}

gives a function
f(g)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
where
g=g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}
. The resulting map
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}\mapsto f
is injective by
[
Sch11
, Cor. 5.8]
, and we let
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
be the image. Mimicking the discussion leading up to
[
LS23
, Df. 4.5]
one easily checks that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
is independent of the choice of basis.

This justifies the following definition.

Definition 5.5
.

A function
f:G\rightarrow K
is
overconvergent
if
f(g)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
for all
g=g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}
, where the coefficients
c_{\alpha}\in K
satisfy
|c_{\alpha}|r^{|\alpha|}\longrightarrow 0
as
|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty
for some
r>1
. (This notion is independent of the choice of basis

(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
.)

The space
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
of overconvergent functions carries a topology by identifying it with
K\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}
(with the direct limit topology). Again, this topology is
independent of the choice of basis since any algebra morphism between dagger algebras is automatically continuous (
[
GK00
, Lem. 1.8]
).

Remark 5.6
.

For a fixed
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
the subalgebra
T_{d,r}\subset K\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}
corresponds to a subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
which
does

depend on the choice of basis in general. The same with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
.

5.4.
The
\dagger
-distribution algebra of a saturated group

Observe that
G\times G
has a natural
p
-valuation given by
\omega_{2}(g,h)=\min\{\omega(g),\omega(h)\}
. An ordered basis for
(G\times G,\omega_{2})
is given by

((g_{1},e),\ldots,(g_{d},e),(e,g_{1}),\ldots,(e,g_{d}))

where
e\in G
is the identity. (See
[
Sch11
, p. 210]
.) In particular
(G\times G,\omega_{2})
is saturated since
(G,\omega)
is saturated. To see this use
[
Sch11
, Prop. 26.11]
.
Therefore the space
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G\times G,K)
is defined, and there is a natural isomorphism of dagger algebras

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\otimes_{K}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G\times G,K)

\displaystyle f_{1}\otimes f_{2}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\big[(g,h)\mapsto f_{1}(g)f_{2}(h)\big].

This is immediate after choosing bases as above.

The dagger group structure on
\text{Sp}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K))
can now be described, without resorting to bases, by the three morphisms

•

m^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\otimes_{K}^{\dagger}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
such that
(\Psi\circ m^{*})(f)(g,h)=f(gh)
;

•

e^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\longrightarrow K
such that
e^{*}(f)=f(e)
;

•

i^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
such that
i^{*}(f)(g)=f(g^{-1})
.

As in Section
4.2
this gives an algebra structure on the strong dual.

Definition 5.7
.

The
\dagger
-distribution
K
-algebra of a saturated group
(G,\omega)
is the strong dual

D^{\dagger}(G,K)=\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)_{b}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K),K)_{b}.

Multiplication in
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is the convolution product

\displaystyle\ast:D^{\dagger}(G,K)\times D^{\dagger}(G,K)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D^{\dagger}(G,K)

\displaystyle(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\big[f\mapsto(\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2})(m^{*}(f))\big],

where
(\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2})(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})=\delta_{1}(f_{1})\delta_{2}(f_{2})
. As noted in
4.2
,
\ast
is jointly continuous. The two-sided unit element for
\ast
is the Dirac delta
distribution
e^{*}=\delta_{e}\in D^{\dagger}(G,K)
.

One of the goals of this article is to establish finer structural properties of these algebras.

6.1.
Proposition

In this section we improve Proposition

Proposition 6.1

\displaystyle v\bigg(\frac{c_{\underline{i},\beta}}{\beta_{1}!\cdots\beta_{s}!}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\bigg(\omega(g_{i})-\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\beta_{j}\bigg(\omega(h_{j})-\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)-\frac{1}{p-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{s_{p}(\beta_{j})}{p-1}

(g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}})(g_{1}^{y_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{y_{d}})=g_{1}^{F_{1}(x,y)}\cdots g_{d}^{F_{d}(x,y)}

for all tuples
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. In this situation the sequence of elements
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}
in Proposition
5.1
is

g_{1},\ldots,g_{d},g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}
. Moreover,
\tau_{N,i}=\frac{1}{N+1}\big(\omega(g_{i})-\frac{1}{p-1}\big)
and

\rho_{N,1}=\rho_{N,d+1}=\tau_{N,1},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\rho_{N,2}=\rho_{N,d+2}=\tau_{N,2},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\ldots\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt,\rho_{N,d}=\rho_{N,2d}=\tau_{N,d}.

Theorem
6.5
therefore gives a map

\displaystyle m:\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})\times_{\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(F_{1}(x,y),\ldots,F_{d}(x,y)).

We are building a group object in
\text{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. The unit
e:\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})
is given by the origin.

The inversion map is the result of another application of Theorem
6.5
. This time we write

(6.9)

(g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}})^{-1}=(g_{d}^{-1})^{x_{d}}\cdots(g_{1}^{-1})^{x_{1}}=g_{1}^{I_{1}(x)}\cdots g_{d}^{I_{d}(x)}

with
I_{1},\ldots,I_{d}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}
. We switch coordinates and introduce variables
Y_{1}=X_{d},\ldots,Y_{d}=X_{1}
. Correspondingly, we let

J_{i}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d})=I_{i}(Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{1})
. In this notation (
6.9
) becomes

(g_{d}^{-1})^{y_{1}}\cdots(g_{1}^{-1})^{y_{d}}=g_{1}^{J_{1}(y)}\cdots g_{d}^{J_{d}(y)}.

Now the elements
h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}
from Proposition
5.1
are
g_{d}^{-1},\ldots,g_{1}^{-1}
. Since
\omega
is invariant under inversion, see
[
Sch11
, p. 169]
, the associated sequence
(\rho_{N,j})
in the current setup therefore amounts to

\frac{1}{N+1}\big(\omega(g_{d}^{-1})-\frac{1}{p-1}\big)=\tau_{N,d},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\ldots\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt,\frac{1}{N+1}\big(\omega(g_{1}^{-1})-\frac{1}{p-1}\big)=\tau_{N,1}.

By Theorem
6.5
we know that

|y_{1}|\leq p^{\tau_{N,d}},\ldots,|y_{d}|\leq p^{\tau_{N,1}}\Longrightarrow\forall i:|J_{i}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})|\leq p^{\tau_{N,i}}.

Switching back to the original coordinates, and expressing this implication in terms of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
, gives a morphism between polydiscs

\displaystyle i:\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto(I_{1}(x),\ldots,I_{d}(x)).

Since
(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})
is a formal group law, the above morphisms
(m,e,i)
endow
\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})
with the structure of a rigid analytic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
∎

Definition 6.10

We refer to
\mathbb{G}_{N}:=\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})
as the
N^{\text{th}}
strict neighborhood group of
\mathbb{G}
.

7.
Fréchet algebras

According to
[
Em17
, Df. 1.2.12]
a nuclear Fréchet algebra over
\mathcal{K}
is a topological
\mathcal{K}
-algebra
S
for which there exists a projective system of
\mathcal{K}
-Banach algebras

S_{1}\leftarrow S_{2}\leftarrow\cdots
, with compact transition maps, and an isomorphism of topological
\mathcal{K}
-algebras
S\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\varprojlim_{n}S_{n}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(G,\omega)
be a saturated group where
\omega
is
\mathbb{Q}
-valued. Then
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is a nuclear Fréchet algebra over
K
.

In preparation for the proof we introduce some notation.

Definition 7.2
.

B_{N}=\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}\in K[\![Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}]\!]:\lim_{|\alpha|\rightarrow\infty}|c_{\alpha}|p^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{N,i}\alpha_{i}}=0\}
.

By
[
BGR84
, Thm. 6.1.5/4, p. 225]
this is an affinoid algebra over
K
with norm

\|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha}\|_{N}={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|c_{\alpha}|p^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{N,i}\alpha_{i}}.

The rigid analytic group structure on
\mathbb{G}_{N}\times_{\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\text{Sp}(K)=\text{Sp}(B_{N})
is given by the three morphisms

B_{N}\overset{m^{*}}{\longrightarrow}B_{N}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}B_{N},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptB_{N}\overset{e^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Q}_{p},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptB_{N}\overset{i^{*}}{\longrightarrow}B_{N},

which are restrictions of their counterparts on
K\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}
from the proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Proof.

(Theorem
7.1
.)
First note that
B_{N}\subset T_{d,p^{\mu}}
for
\mu=\min_{i}\tau_{N,i}
, and
T_{d,r}\subset B_{N}
for
N>\!\!>_{r}0
. Both inclusions are continuous (indeed contractions).
Therefore
\varinjlim_{N}B_{N}=W_{d}=K\langle Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rangle^{\dagger}
as locally convex vector spaces, where we equip
W_{d}=\varinjlim_{r}T_{d,r}
with the usual direct limit topology. The transition maps
B_{1}\hookrightarrow B_{2}\hookrightarrow\cdots
are (injective and) compact by
[
Em17
, Prop. 2.1.16]
. By
[
Sch02
, Prop. 16.10]
we get a topological isomorphism after passing to strong duals,

(7.3)

(W_{d})_{b}^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\varprojlim_{N}(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}.

The dual transition maps
(B_{1})_{b}^{\prime}\leftarrow(B_{2})_{b}^{\prime}\leftarrow\cdots
remain compact by
[
Sch02
, Lem. 16.4]
, and each
(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}
is a Banach algebra by Lemma
7.4
below. What is left is to observe (
7.3
) is an isomorphism of algebras. This follows because the identification
\varinjlim_{N}B_{N}=W_{d}
preserves comultiplication:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{N}\widehat{\otimes}_{K}B_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{d}\otimes_{K}^{\dagger}W_{d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{*}}

commutes for all
N\geq 1
.
∎

The next result, used above, is presumably well-known. We include a proof, for lack of a reference.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
B
be an affinoid
\mathcal{K}
-algebra, and suppose
\text{Sp}(B)
has a rigid analytic group structure. Then the distribution algebra
B_{b}^{\prime}
(which is
\mathcal{L}(B,\mathcal{K})
with the operator norm topology by
[
Sch02
, Rem. 6.7]
) is a
\mathcal{K}
-Banach algebra: There is a constant
c>0
such that
\|\delta_{1}\ast\delta_{2}\|\leq c\|\delta_{1}\|\cdot\|\delta_{2}\|
for all
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in B_{b}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
B\overset{m^{*}}{\longrightarrow}B\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{K}}B
be the morphism defining multiplication on
\text{Sp}(B)
. Then, by definition of the convolution
\ast
we have

(\delta_{1}\ast\delta_{2})(f)=(\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2})(m^{*}(f))

for
f\in B
, where
(\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2})(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})=\delta_{1}(f_{1})\delta_{2}(f_{2})
. Expanding
\varphi\in B\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}B
as
\varphi=\sum_{i}f_{1,i}\otimes f_{2,i}
we find that

|(\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2})(\varphi)|\leq\max_{i}\{|\delta_{1}(f_{1,i})|\cdot|\delta_{2}(f_{2,i})|\}\leq\|\delta_{1}\|\cdot\|\delta_{2}\|\cdot\max_{i}\{\|f_{1,i}\|\cdot\|f_{2,i}\|\}

Taking the infimum over all expansions
\varphi=\sum_{i}f_{1,i}\otimes f_{2,i}
this shows
\|\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2}\|\leq\|\delta_{1}\|\cdot\|\delta_{2}\|
. (See the definition of the tensor product norm on
B\otimes_{\mathcal{K}}B
in
[
BGR84
, Sect. 2.1.7]
or
[
Sch02
, Sect. 17, Part B]
.) Consequently,

|(\delta_{1}\ast\delta_{2})(f)|\leq\|\delta_{1}\times\delta_{2}\|\cdot\|m^{*}\|\cdot\|f\|\leq\|m^{*}\|\cdot\|\delta_{1}\|\cdot\|\delta_{2}\|\cdot\|f\|.

In other words, we may take
c=\|m^{*}\|
.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

If the affinoid algebra
B
in Lemma
7.4
is a Tate algebra, then the operator norm on
B_{b}^{\prime}
is submultiplicative (

). Indeed, every
\mathcal{K}
-algebra morphism between Tate algebras is a contraction by
[

, Thm. 5.1.3/4, p. 194]
, and there are canonical isometric isomorphisms

by
[
BGR84
, Cor. 6.1.1/8, p. 224]
. Hence the comultiplication
m^{*}
from the proof of
7.4
automatically has operator norm at most one.

8.1.
An exercise in
p
-adic analysis

For the reader’s convenience, we include a proof of the following basic result from
p
-adic analysis. This is based on the proofs of
[
KCon
, Thm. 4.4]
and
[
S84
, Thm. 54.4]
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
we let
\binom{x}{\alpha}=\binom{x_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\binom{x_{d}}{\alpha_{d}}
. Also, we let
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{d}!
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d},K)
be a continuous function with Mahler expansion
f(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}m_{\alpha}\binom{x}{\alpha}
, with coefficients
m_{\alpha}\in K
.
Let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}\in p^{\mathbb{Q}}
be numbers
>1
. Then
f
extends (uniquely) to a rigid analytic function on the polydisc

\mathbb{B}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{A}_{\text{rig}}^{d},\forall i:|x_{i}|\leq r_{i}\}

if and only if
\lim_{|\alpha|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{|\alpha|}=0
.
(Here we adopt the notation
r^{|\alpha|}=r_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots r_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
.)

Furthermore, if
f(x)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\beta}x^{\beta}
is the Taylor expansion of such an
f
, then

\|f\|_{r}:={\sup}_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|c_{\beta}|r^{|\beta|}={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{|\alpha|}.

Proof.

We will give the proof for
d=1
, and ask the reader to make the necessary modifications in the higher-dimensional case. Thus
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)
and
r\in p^{\mathbb{Q}}
is
>1
.
Moreover,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

First assume
\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\alpha}\rightarrow 0
as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
. Expand the falling powers
x^{\underline{\alpha}}:=x(x-1)\cdots(x-\alpha+1)
as

\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations of monomials
x^{\underline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}a_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\beta}
, where
a_{\alpha,\alpha}=1
. Then for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we have

f(x)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{m_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}x^{\underline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\frac{m_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}a_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\beta}.

The double sum is absolutely convergent for
|x|\leq r
since its terms go to zero as
\alpha+\beta\rightarrow\infty
:

|\frac{m_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}a_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\beta}|\leq\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\beta}\leq\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\alpha}.

We may therefore interchange the order of summation, and hence
f(x)=\sum_{\beta}\big(\sum_{\alpha\geq\beta}\frac{m_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}a_{\alpha,\beta}\big)x^{\beta}
provided
|x|\leq r
. This extends
f
to a rigid analytic function on
\mathbb{B}(r)
.

On the other hand, now suppose
f(x)=\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}x^{\beta}
for
|x|\leq r
and certain
c_{\beta}\in K
. We now express the monomials
x^{\beta}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations of falling powers,
x^{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\leq\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}x^{\underline{\alpha}}
. (The coefficients
s_{\beta,\alpha}
are known as Stirling numbers of the second kind.)
With this notation we have

f(x)=\sum_{\beta}\sum_{\alpha\leq\beta}c_{\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}x^{\underline{\alpha}}=\sum_{\beta}\sum_{\alpha\leq\beta}c_{\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}\alpha!\binom{x}{\alpha}.

Again, the double sum is absolutely convergent, at least for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, since
|c_{\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}\alpha!\binom{x}{\alpha}|\leq|c_{\beta}|
for such
x
and
certainly
c_{\beta}\rightarrow 0
for
\beta\rightarrow\infty
. (Even
|c_{\beta}|r^{\beta}
is a null sequence.). Swapping the order of summation we find that
f
has Mahler coefficients

m_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\geq\alpha}c_{\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}\alpha!.

To finish the proof of the only if part, we simply check that

\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\alpha}\leq\max_{\beta\geq\alpha}\{|c_{\beta}|r^{\alpha}\}\leq\max_{\beta\geq\alpha}\{|c_{\beta}|r^{\beta}\}.

We are now done since
|c_{\beta}|r^{\beta}
is a null sequence by hypothesis. We conclude
\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\alpha}
is a null sequence.

The last claim about
\|f\|_{r}
follows straightforwardly from the formulas
m_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\geq\alpha}c_{\beta}s_{\beta,\alpha}\alpha!
and
c_{\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\geq\beta}\frac{m_{\alpha}}{\alpha!}a_{\alpha,\beta}
derived above. For example, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we have

|c_{\beta}|r^{|\beta|}\leq\max_{\alpha\geq\beta}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{|\beta|}\leq\max_{\alpha\geq\beta}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{|\alpha|}\leq{\max}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{|\alpha|}.

Taking the supremum over
\beta
shows
\|f\|_{r}
is
\leq
the right-hand side. A similar argument for
m_{\alpha}
gives the other inequality.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

Let
\theta:=p^{-1/(p-1)}
be the radius of convergence of the
p
-adic exponential function. Note that
\theta\in p^{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\frac{1}{p}\leq\theta<1
. Keep the setup of Lemma
8.1
, but
assume
d=1
for simplicity. Then:

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}|m_{\alpha}|s^{\alpha}=0
for some
s>\theta^{-1}

\Longrightarrow

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|m_{\alpha}|}{|\alpha!|}r^{\alpha}=0
for some
r>1

\Longrightarrow

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}|m_{\alpha}|s^{\alpha}=0
for
s=\theta^{-1}
.

This is easily checked, by using the asymptotic formula
v(\alpha!)=\frac{\alpha}{p-1}+O(\log_{p}\alpha)
as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
. Recall that, by Amice’s theorem,
f
is
locally
analytic on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
if and only if
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}|m_{\alpha}|s^{\alpha}=0
for some
s>1
.

8.2.
Distributions on polydiscs

We keep the setup from Lemma
8.1
and
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{B}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}),K)
denotes the space of rigid functions on the polydisc of polyradius

(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
. Here we will give a description of the strong dual
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{B}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}),K)_{b}^{\prime}
(the space of continuous linear forms with the operator norm topology).

First, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we introduce the linear form
{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}(f)=m_{\alpha}
. This is clearly continuous, and it has operator norm

\|{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}\|\leq|\alpha!|r^{-|\alpha|}
. All the distributions on
\mathbb{B}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
are suitable linear combinations of
\{{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Every
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{B}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}),K)^{\prime}
has a unique expansion as a weakly convergent infinite series
\delta=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}
where the coefficients
d_{\alpha}\in K
satisfy the following boundedness condition:

\|\delta\|={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}<\infty.

Proof.

This follows straightforwardly from the duality between null sequences and bounded sequences. See the example in
[
Sch02
, p. 12]
for example, which gives an isometry

c_{0}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
. With Lemma
8.1
in hand, a simple reparametrization of the coefficients gives the result.
∎

We will mainly be interested in the polyradii
(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. In this case

(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{B}(p^{\tau_{N,1}},\ldots,p^{\tau_{N,d}}),K)_{b}^{\prime}

is the distribution
algebra
appearing in the description of
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
as a Fréchet algebra.

8.3.
Contact with the Schneider-Teitelbaum algebras

We return to our saturated group
(G,\omega)
with a choice of ordered basis
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
. In particular this gives a homeomorphism

\displaystyle\psi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}G

\displaystyle x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})

\displaystyle\longmapsto g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}.

Via
\psi
we view functions on
G
as functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
, and vice versa. More formally, we have an isomorphism of
K
-Banach spaces

\displaystyle\psi^{*}:\mathcal{C}(G,K)

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d},K)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\longmapsto f\circ\psi.

Similarly for the space
\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)
of locally analytic functions.

Following
[
ST03
, Sect. 4]
we embed
K[\![G]\!]:=K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
in the locally analytic distribution algebra
D(G,K)
by sending
g\mapsto\delta_{g}
. We introduce the elements
b_{i}=g_{i}-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, and for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we let
{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}=b_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. Elements of
K[\![G]\!]
have the form

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}
with bounded coefficients
d_{\alpha}\in K
. Similarly, a distribution
\lambda\in D(G,K)
has a unique expansion

\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{b}}^{\alpha},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\|\lambda\|_{s}^{\prime}={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|d_{\alpha}|s^{|\alpha|}<\infty,\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\forall s\in(0,1).

The sum converges in
D(G,K)
essentially by (the proof of)
[
ST02
, Lem. 2.5]
. Note that
{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}(f)=m_{\alpha}
is the
\alpha^{\text{th}}
Mahler coefficient of
f
. We recall from
[
ST03
, Prop. 4.2]
that the norm

\|\lambda\|_{s}={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\tau\alpha:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega(g_{i})\alpha_{i},

is submultiplicative for
s\in[\frac{1}{p},1)
. Note that the collection of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{s}:0<s<1\}
define the same topology as
\{\|\cdot\|_{s}^{\prime}:0<s<1\}
. Indeed

\|\lambda\|_{s^{\min\omega(g_{i})}}^{\prime}\leq\|\lambda\|_{s}\leq\|\lambda\|_{s^{\max\omega(g_{i})}}^{\prime}
.

In
[
ST03
]
the
K
-Banach algebra
D_{s}(G,K)
is defined as the completion of

with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{s}
, where
s\in[\frac{1}{p},1)
is now fixed.
The elements of
D_{s}(G,K)

\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{b}}^{\alpha},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}\longrightarrow 0\text{ as }|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty.

The Fréchet-Stein structures in

arise from the isomorphism
D(G,K)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\varprojlim_{s\in[\frac{1}{p},1)}D_{s}(G,K)
.

Lemma 8.4
.

Any distribution
\lambda\in D^{\dagger}(G,K)
has a unique expansion as a weakly convergent sum
\lambda=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}

d_{\alpha}\in K

\|\lambda\|_{s}^{\dagger}:={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|s^{|\alpha|}<\infty,\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\forall s\in(0,1).

This follows from Lemma
8.3
with
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{d}=\frac{1}{s}

∎

Recall that
\omega(g)>\frac{1}{p-1}
for all

\theta<1
. Therefore
\frac{1}{p}<\theta^{1/\min\omega(g_{i})}
. The next result at least gives a continuous algebra homomorphism

D^{\dagger}(G,K)\longrightarrow{\varprojlim}_{s\in[\frac{1}{p},\theta^{1/\min\omega(g_{i})})}D_{s}(G,K).

Under favorable circumstances we will show this is an isomorphism, and then deduce from
[
ST03
]
that
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
is Fréchet-Stein.

Lemma 8.5
.

We have a continuous inclusion of algebras
D^{\dagger}(G,K)\hookrightarrow D_{s}(G,K)
for
\frac{1}{p}\leq s<\theta^{1/\min\omega(g_{i})}
.

Proof.

Let
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}
be a weakly convergent sum representing an element of
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
. Then for all tuples
\alpha
we have the estimate

\displaystyle|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}\frac{1}{|\alpha!|}

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|s^{\min\omega(g_{i})|\alpha|}p^{|\alpha|/(p-1)}

\displaystyle=|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|(s^{\min\omega(g_{i})}\theta^{-1})^{|\alpha|}

which shows that
|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}\longrightarrow 0
as
|\alpha|\longrightarrow\infty
provided
s^{\min\omega(g_{i})}\theta^{-1}<1
.
∎

8.4.
Comparison with the Fréchet algebra structure

We compare the inverse system
(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}
to the system
D_{s}(G,K)
from
[
ST03
]
. Recall that
D^{\dagger}(G,K)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\varprojlim_{N}(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}
by
(the proof of) Theorem
7.1
.

Lemma 8.6
.

The following holds.

(1)

Let
\frac{1}{p}\leq s<\theta^{1/\min\omega(g_{i})}
. Then for all
N>\!\!>0
there is a continuous linear map

\displaystyle(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D_{s}(G,K)

\displaystyle{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}.

(2)

Let
N\geq 1
. Then for all
s\geq\frac{1}{p}
sufficiently close to
\theta^{1/\max\omega(g_{i})}
there is a continuous linear map

\displaystyle D_{s}(G,K)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}.

Proof.

The proof of part (1) is very similar to that of Lemma
8.5
. Fix an
s
in the given range. We must show
|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}\rightarrow 0
as
|\alpha|\rightarrow 0
, provided
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}d_{\alpha}{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}
lies in
(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}
where
N
is large enough. So, assume
A:={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}<\infty
where
r_{i}=p^{\tau_{N,i}}
. Then

\displaystyle|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}

\displaystyle=|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}r^{|\alpha|}s^{\tau\alpha}\frac{1}{|\alpha!|}

\displaystyle\leq A\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}(r_{j}s^{\min\omega(g_{i})}\theta^{-1})^{\alpha_{j}}.

By assumption
s^{-\min\omega(g_{i})}\theta>1
. Since
p^{\tau_{N,j}}\rightarrow 1^{+}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, we can arrange that
r_{j}s^{\min\omega(g_{i})}\theta^{-1}<1
for all
j
by choosing
N
sufficiently large. This shows
|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}
goes to zero – plus the fact that the resulting map is continuous (in fact a contraction).

For the proof of part (2) we now start with an
N\geq 1
and seek conditions on
s
which guarantee that
|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}
is bounded when

{\lim}_{|\alpha|\rightarrow\infty}|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}=0
. Here we keep the polyradius
r_{i}=p^{\tau_{N,i}}
.

\displaystyle|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle=|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}s^{-\tau\alpha}r^{-|\alpha|}|\alpha!|

\displaystyle\leq B\cdot(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{d})^{C}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}(s^{-\max\omega(g_{i})}r_{j}^{-1}\theta)^{\alpha_{j}}

where
B:={\sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}|d_{\alpha}|s^{\tau\alpha}
, and
C>0
is a large enough constant arising from the asymptotics
v(n!)=\frac{n}{p-1}+O(\log_{p}n)
.
Now the
r_{j}>1
are fixed, and if we choose
s
close enough to
\theta^{1/\max\omega(g_{i})}
we can arrange that

s^{-\max\omega(g_{i})}r_{j}^{-1}\theta<1
for all
j
. This implies that
|\alpha!d_{\alpha}|r^{-|\alpha|}
indeed stays bounded, and moreover that the map taking

{\bf{b}}^{\alpha}\mapsto{\bf{m}}_{\alpha}
is continuous (but not necessarily a contraction).
∎

Altogether, this gives the commutative diagram of continuous linear maps:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{s<\theta^{1/\max\omega(g_{i})}}D_{s}(G,K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{N}(B_{N})_{b}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{s<\theta^{1/\min\omega(g_{i})}}D_{s}(G,K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\dagger}(G,K)}
inclusion
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

If
(G,\omega)
is equi-
p
-valued, which means
\omega(g_{i})=\omega_{0}
is constant for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, then we deduce an isomorphism of topological algebras

(8.7)

D^{\dagger}(G,K)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\varprojlim}_{s<\theta^{1/\omega_{0}}}D_{s}(G,K).

The condition (HYP) in
[
ST03
, p. 163]
here amounts to the inequality
2\omega_{0}>\frac{p}{p-1}
(and saturation). If this is satisfied
[
ST03
, Thm. 4.5, Thm. 4.9]
imply the following properties for any two
\frac{1}{p}<s^{\prime}\leq s<1
in
p^{\mathbb{Q}}
.

•

D_{s}(G,K)
is a (left and right)
Noetherian
integral domain;

•

The transition map
D_{s}(G,K)\longrightarrow D_{s^{\prime}}(G,K)
is
flat
.

In particular, having established the isomorphism (
8.7
) the main results of
[
ST03
]
immediately give the Fréchet-Stein structure on
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
– under the assumption that
(G,\omega)
is equi-
p
-valued and
2\omega_{0}>\frac{p}{p-1}
.

Example 8.8
.

Suppose
G
is a
uniform
pro-
p
-group. By
[
DdSMS
, Thm. 4.5]
this means
G
is topologically finitely generated, torsion-free, and powerful – which means

G/\overline{G^{p\varepsilon}}
is abelian, where
\varepsilon:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p>2$}\\
2&\text{if $p=2$}\end{cases}
.
Let
P_{i}(G)
be the lower
p
-series, as defined in
[
DdSMS
, Df. 1.15]
. One can easily check that

\omega^{\text{can}}(g)=\sup\{i\geq 1:g\in P_{i}(G)\}+\begin{cases}0&\text{if $p>2$}\\
1&\text{if $p=2$}\end{cases}

defines a
p
-valuation on
G
, and
(G,\omega^{\text{can}})
is a saturated group which admits a basis
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})
with

\omega^{\text{can}}(g_{i})=\varepsilon
for all
i
. For instance, these claims are checked in
[
HKN11
, p. 249]
. In particular
(G,\omega^{\text{can}})

satisfies the condition (HYP) from
[
ST03
]
since
2\varepsilon>\frac{p}{p-1}
for all primes
p\geq 2
.

Remark 8.9
.

In
[
ST03
, Sect. 5]
the authors proceed to remove the hypothesis (HYP) and show that the locally analytic distribution algebra
D(G,K)
is Fréchet-Stein
for
any
compact
p
-adic Lie group
G
. See
[
ST03
, Thm. 5.1]
. To do so, they select an open normal subgroup
U\vartriangleleft G
which is uniform, along with
coset representatives
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}
. The Dirac distributions
\delta_{y_{1}},\ldots,\delta_{y_{\ell}}
then form a basis for
D(G,K)
as a
D(U,K)
-module, and this is used to define
a Fréchet-Stein on
D(G,K)
in terms of that on
D(U,K)
.

This approach does not work in the overconvergent setting. The coset decomposition of the space of locally analytic functions
\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(y_{j}U,K)
does not have an analogue for
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
. The overconvergence of a function
f:G\longrightarrow K
cannot be detected locally on the cosets of
U
.

9.
Overconvergent representations

9.1.
The space of
V
-valued overconvergent functions

Let
V
be a locally convex
K
-vector space of compact type. In this section we will define a space
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)
of locally analytic functions
f:G\longrightarrow V
which is canonically topologically isomorphic to the completed tensor product
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V
. (Since
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
is also of compact type, the inductive and projective tensor product topologies coincide. We adopt the convention in
[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.32]
and omit the corresponding subscripts
\iota
and
\pi
.)

Lemma 9.1
.

Suppose
V
is a locally convex
K
-vector space of compact type. Then the following holds.

(1)

The inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)
is continuous;

(2)

The induced map
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V
is a continuous injection.

Proof.

After choosing a basis for
(G,\omega)
we have to check the continuity of the inclusion
T_{d,r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)
for all
r\in\Gamma_{>1}
. The latter factors
as
T_{d,r}\hookrightarrow T_{d}
composed with
T_{d}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)
. Both of these maps are continuous. (In the terminology of
[
ST02
, p. 447]
take a

V
-index consisting of a single global chart
G\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
.)

In part (2) continuity is clear. The fact that the map is an injection follows from
[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.26]
, which applies since compact type spaces are bornological and complete (
[
ST02
, Thm. 1.1]
).
∎

The dual of the inclusion in part (1) is a continuous algebra homomorphism
D(G,K)\longrightarrow D^{\dagger}(G,K)
which is
injective
. (Indeed, if
\lambda\in D(G,K)

vanishes on all polynomial functions, such as
x\mapsto\binom{x}{\alpha}
, then clearly

\lambda=0
.)

By
[
Em17
, Prop. 2.1.28]
there is a topological isomorphism

(9.2)

\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V.

We define
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)
to be the subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)
which corresponds to
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V
under (
9.2
).

Definition 9.3
.

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)
is the space of functions
f:G\longrightarrow V
arising from
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V
, where the latter is viewed as a subset of

\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)
via the inverse of (
9.2
) and the injection in part (2) of Lemma
9.1
. We endow
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)
with a topology by transferring the topology from
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V
.

We thus have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the horizontal maps are topological isomorphisms, and the vertical maps are continuous injections.

9.2.
The notion of an overconvergent representation

Now we assume
V
is a locally analytic
G
-representation of compact type. Let
\rho:V\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)
be the orbit map
v\mapsto\rho_{v}
, where
\rho_{v}(g)=gv
. This is a
continuous
injection by
[
Em17
, Thm. 3.6.12]
(and the paragraph right below its proof). Obviously
\rho
is
G
-equivariant if we let
G
act by
right translations on
\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)
.

Definition 9.4
.

We say
V
is overconvergent if there is a continuous map
V\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

commutes. (The dashed arrow is necessarily unique if it exists.)

When
V
is overconvergent we will equip
V_{b}^{\prime}
with a
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-module structure. We start by reviewing how
V_{b}^{\prime}
becomes a
D(G,K)
-module. Composing the isomorphism (
9.2
) with the orbit map yields

c:V\overset{\rho}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V.

Passing to strong duals gives a continuous map

\displaystyle D(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V)_{b}^{\prime}\overset{c^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}V_{b}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lambda\otimes v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\lambda\ast v^{\prime})(v)=\lambda\big(g\mapsto\langle gv,v^{\prime}\rangle\big).

(The isomorphism is discussed in part (ii) of
[
Em17
, Prop. 1.1.32]
.) As a special case, temporarily taking
V=C^{\text{an}}(G,K)
gives a jointly continuous multiplication map

\ast
on
D(G,K)
which is the
opposite
of the usual convolution product. For instance
\delta_{x}\ast\delta_{y}=\delta_{yx}
, as is easily checked. Since
D(G,K)
is isomorphic to its opposite algebra via
g\mapsto g^{-1}
this subtle difference will not play a role.

A standard and straightforward calculation shows that
V_{b}^{\prime}
thus becomes a left module over
D(G,K)
, and
the action
D(G,K)\times V_{b}^{\prime}\longrightarrow V_{b}^{\prime}
is jointly continuous (by definition of the projective tensor product topology).

When
V
is an overconvergent representation we have a commutative diagram of continuous maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}V.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The dual diagram implies the
D(G,K)
-action on
V_{b}^{\prime}
factors through
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
. This argument is reversible. The dual of a
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-module is an overconvergent
representation. As in
[
ST02
, Cor. 3.4]
the strong duality functor
V\mapsto V_{b}^{\prime}
restricts to an anti-equivalence of categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{overconvergent $G$-representations of compact type}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{continuous $D^{\dagger}(G,K)$-modules on nuclear Fr\'{e}chet spaces}\}.}

9.3.
Examples of overconvergent representations

Our first example is
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
on which
G
acts by right translations. (By a small modification of the proof of
[
LS23
, Lem. 4.6]
this is a
topological

G
-action in the sense of
[
Em17
, Sect. 0.3.11]
, which means each
g\in G
acts via a topological automorphism of
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
.)

We have already noted that
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
is of compact type (since
W_{d}
with the fringe topology is of compact type). Moreover, the orbit map may be viewed as a map into

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G\times G,K)
as follows:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}:\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)\underset{\Psi}{\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G\times G,K)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\longmapsto\big[(x,y)\mapsto f(yx)\big].

(See Remark
4.3
for the relation between
\otimes_{K}^{\dagger}
and
\widehat{\otimes}_{K}
. The isomorphism
\Psi
was defined in Section
5.4
.) This map
\widetilde{\rho}
is clearly a homomorphism of dagger algebras, and it is therefore automatically continuous with respect to the fringe topology by
[
GK00
, Lem. 1.8]
.
This shows that indeed
\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
is an overconvergent representation.

More generally, let
W\subset\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,K)
be a closed
G
-invariant subspace (with the induced topology). Note that
W
is of compact type by part (i) of
[
ST02
, Prop. 1.2]
. Then
W
is an overconvergent representation by the following result.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
V
be an overconvergent representation. Then any closed
G
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
is an overconvergent representation (relative to the induced topology).

Proof.

We construct the dual of the orbit map
W\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(G,W)
. Passing to strong duals gives a
strict
surjection
p:V_{b}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow W_{b}^{\prime}
by part (i) of

[
ST02
, Prop. 1.2]
, whose kernel
\{v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}:v^{\prime}|_{W}=0\}
is a
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-submodule (since
W
is
G
-invariant). This equips
W^{\prime}
with an abstract

D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-module structure for which
p
is
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
-linear. What remains is verifying the action map
D^{\dagger}(G,K)\times W_{b}^{\prime}\longrightarrow W_{b}^{\prime}
is jointly continuous. As
D^{\dagger}(G,K)
and
W_{b}^{\prime}
are both Fréchet spaces, it suffices to show
separate
continuity by
[
Sch02
, Prop. 17.6]
.

First, for a fixed
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
the map
D^{\dagger}(G,K)\rightarrow W_{b}^{\prime}
which takes
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\ast w^{\prime}
is clearly continuous (write
w^{\prime}=p(v^{\prime})
and compose
p
with

\lambda\mapsto\lambda\ast v^{\prime}
, which is continuous since
V
is assumed overconvergent).

On the other hand, for a fixed
\lambda\in D^{\dagger}(G,K)
the map
a_{W}:W_{b}^{\prime}\rightarrow W_{b}^{\prime}
sending
w^{\prime}\mapsto\lambda\ast w^{\prime}
is continuous by the following argument:
For an open subset
U\subset W_{b}^{\prime}
, in order to show
a_{W}^{-1}(U)
is open it suffices to show
p^{-1}(a_{W}^{-1}(U))
is open – since
p
is strict. This is now obvious from the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{b}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{b}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{b}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{b}^{\prime}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

This essentially finishes the proof: By the completeness of
W_{b}^{\prime}
, the action map extends uniquely to a continuous map
D^{\dagger}(G,K)\widehat{\otimes}_{K}W_{b}^{\prime}\longrightarrow W_{b}^{\prime}
, the dual of which is the desired continuous orbit map.
∎

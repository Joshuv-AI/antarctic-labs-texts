On a refined local converse theorem for SO ⁡ ( 4 ) {\mathrm{SO}}(4) Thanks: The second named author was partially supported by NSFC grant 12371010.
Pan Yan, Qing Zhang

Abstract
. Recently, Hazeltine-Liu, and independently Haan-Kim-Kwon, proved a local converse theorem for SO 2 ​ n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{2n}(F) over a p p -adic field F F , which says that, up to an outer automorphism of SO 2 ​ n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{2n}(F) , an irreducible generic representation of SO 2 ​ n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{2n}(F) is uniquely determined by its twisted gamma factors by generic representations of GL k ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{k}(F) for k = 1 , … , n k=1,\dots,n . It is desirable to remove the “up to an outer automorphism” part in the above theorem using more twisted gamma factors, but this seems a hard problem. In this paper, we provide a solution to this problem for the group SO 4 ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{4}(F) , namely, we show that a generic supercuspidal representation π \pi of SO 4 ​ ( F ) \mathrm{SO}_{4}(F) is uniquely determined by its GL 1 \mathrm{GL}_{1} , GL 2 \mathrm{GL}_{2} twisted local gamma factors and a twisted exterior square local gamma factor of π \pi .

Introduction

Let
F
be a local field. Let

denote the split classical groups
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1},{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}
or quasi-split classical groups
{\mathrm{U}}_{2n},{\mathrm{U}}_{2n+1}
over
F
. Here in the quasi-split case, the unitary groups are defined with respect to a fixed quadratic extension
E/F
. Given an irreducible generic representation
\pi
of
G_{n}(F)
and an irreducible generic representation
\tau
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
(in the split case) or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(E)
(in the unitary group case), one can associate a local gamma factor
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
using either Langlands-Shahidi method, or appropriate Rankin-Selberg integrals; see
[
19
]
for a nice survey on the definition of these gamma factors using Rankin-Selberg method (and see
[
4
,
22
,
9
]
in the unitary group case). Here
\psi
is a fixed additive character of
F
. The local converse theorems for these groups, see
[
17
,
8
,
16
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
18
,
23
]
for various cases, assert that the representation
\pi
is uniquely determined by the family
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
as
\tau
runs over all irreducible generic representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(F)
(or
{\mathrm{GL}}_{m}(E)
) for all
m
with
1\leq m\leq n
. See
[
20
]
for more references. However, a similar converse theorem for
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}
is false in general because of the existence of an outer automorphism, which is defined by conjugation of an element
c\in{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}(F)-{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
(for an explicit matrix form of

, see Section
3.4
). In fact, the twisted gamma factors of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
cannot distinguish
\pi
and
c\cdot\pi

[
HL:p-adic
,
HL:finite
,
15
]
. Here
c\cdot\pi
is the representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
defined by
(c\cdot\pi)(g)=\pi(cgc^{-1})
. Thus if one only uses the twisted gamma factors of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
, the best possible result one could expect is that these gamma factors can determine
\pi
up to the conjugation by
c
. This is actually the main result of
[
HL:p-adic
]
and
[
15
]
. A similar phenomenon is also known over finite fields, see
[
HL:finite
]
. This phenomenon is consistent with the work of Arthur on the local Langlands correspondence
[
1
]
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible generic representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
. Since
\pi
and
c\cdot\pi
are generic with respect to the same Whittaker datum, they cannot be in the same
L
-packet unless
\pi\cong c\cdot\pi
by the uniqueness of generic element (with respect to a fixed Whittaker datum) in an
L
-packet as proved in
[
2
]
. It is thus desirable to find additional invariants to distinguish
\pi
and
c\cdot\pi
if
\pi\ncong c\cdot\pi.

In this paper, for the small group
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)
, we construct an additional twisted local gamma factor
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta,\psi)
, where
\wedge^{2}_{+}
is a constituent of
\wedge^{2}
of the dual group
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
(see explanation below), and
\eta
is a character of
F^{\times}
. We show that, the twisted gamma factors of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}
for
k=1,2
, plus the gamma factors
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta,\psi)
can determine the representation
\pi
uniquely. Namely, we show the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be two irreducible
\psi
-generic supercuspidal representations of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)
with the same central character. If
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
and
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime},\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta,\psi)
for any irreducible generic representation
\tau
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
with
k=1,2
and any quasi-character
\eta
of
F^{\times}
, then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}.

Let us now explain the representation
\wedge^{2}_{+}
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
. In fact, such a representation can be defined for
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
where
n
is any positive integer, as explained below. Let
W={\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
and let
\{e_{i},1\leq i\leq 2n\}
be the standard basis of
W
. Consider the bilinear form
Q
on
W
defined by

Q(e_{i},e_{j})=\begin{cases}1,&i+j=2n+1,\\
0,&i+j\neq 2n+1.\end{cases}

In other words, the bilinear form
Q
is given by
Q(v_{1},v_{2})=v_{1}J_{2n}v_{2}^{t}
, where
v_{i}\in W
is viewed as a row vector, and
J_{2n}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&&1\\
&\iddots&\\
1&&\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}
. Using the bilinear form
Q
, we can realize the groups
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
as

{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})=\left\{{g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}}):Q(gw_{1},gw_{2})=Q(w_{1},w_{2})\text{ for all }w_{1},w_{2}\in W}\right\},

and

{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})=\left\{{g\in{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}}):\det(g)=1}\right\}.

We fix an isomorphism
\wedge^{2n}(W)\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by

e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{2n}\mapsto 1.

Then the bilinear form
Q
defines an isomorphism

\varphi_{1}:\wedge^{n}(W)\to\wedge^{n}(W^{*})

and the wedge product
\wedge^{n}(W)\times\wedge^{n}(W)\to\wedge^{2n}(W)\cong{\mathbb{C}}
determines an isomorphism

\varphi_{2}:\wedge^{n}(W^{*})\to\wedge^{n}(W).

Denote
\rho=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
. Then we can check that
\rho^{2}={\mathrm{id}}
. Let
W_{j}\subset\wedge^{n}(W)
be the
j
-eigenspace of
\rho
for
j\in\left\{{\pm 1}\right\}
.
Each
W_{j}
is invariant under the natural action of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Denote the corresponding action of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
on
W_{j}
by
\wedge^{n}_{+}
(resp.
\wedge^{n}_{-}
) if
j=1
(reps.
j=-1
). Then we have

\wedge^{n}=\wedge^{n}_{+}\oplus\wedge^{n}_{-}.

In fact, by
[
11
]
*Theorem 19.2, both
\wedge^{n}_{+}
and
\wedge^{n}_{-}
are irreducible. Actually, one can also check that
\wedge^{n}_{-}=c\cdot\wedge^{n}_{+}
, where
c
denotes the outer automorphism of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Note that, the definition of
\wedge^{n}_{+}
depends on the choice of the isomorphism
\wedge^{2n}(W)\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus there is no canonical choice of
\wedge_{+}^{n}.

We assume that
F
is a
p
-adic field and
W_{F}^{\prime}
is the Weil-Deligne group of
F
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
. Arthur
[
1
]
defined the local Langlands parameter
\phi_{\pi}:W_{F}^{\prime}\to{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
of
\pi
up to the outer conjugation
c\in{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})-{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\sigma
be an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
for some positive integer
k
then we write
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{n}_{\pm}\times\sigma,\psi):=\gamma(s,(\wedge^{n}_{\pm}\circ\phi_{\pi})\otimes\phi_{\sigma},\psi)
by abuse of notation, where
\psi
is a fixed nontrivial additive character of
F
, and
\phi_{\sigma}:W_{F}^{\prime}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}({\mathbb{C}})
is the local Langlands parameter of
\sigma
. Since there is no canonical choice of
\phi_{\pi}
because of the existence of the outer automorphism
c
, the above notation of
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{n}_{\pm}\times\sigma,\psi)
is vague. Thus it is desirable to give pure representation theoretic definition of these local gamma factors
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{n}_{+}\times\sigma,\psi)
.

In this article, for the small group
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}
case, given an automorphic cuspidal generic representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{A}})
where
{\mathbb{A}}
is the ring of adeles of a global field
F
, and a character
\eta
of
F^{\times}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
, we construct a global integral which is Eulerian and at an unramified place, it represents the local
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{v},\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta_{v})
. The local gamma factors
\gamma(s,\pi_{v},\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta_{v},\psi_{v})
are then constructed using the local functional equations of those local integrals. It turns out that the new local gamma factor can determine the values of certain Whittaker functions of
\pi
on the Bruhat cells, at which only the sum of Whittaker functions of
\pi
and
c\cdot\pi
can be determined using the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}
-twisted gamma factors, as shown in
[
HL:p-adic
,
15
]
. Theorem
1.1
follows from this consideration and the results of
[
HL:p-adic
,
15
]
easily.

Although we can only handle the small rank case, we expect that our result can shed some light on the general problem: find enough gamma factors for generic representations of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
so that they can distinguish a representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
and its
c
-conjugate
c\cdot\pi
if
\pi\ncong c\cdot\pi
. After Theorem
1.1
, one might expect the twisted gamma factor
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{n}_{+}\times\sigma,\psi)
will be enough for the purpose, where
\pi
is an irreducible generic representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
and
\sigma
is an irreducible generic representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
. In other words, if
\pi
is an irreducible generic representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}(F)
such that
\pi\ncong c\cdot\pi
, then one should expect that there is an irreducible generic representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(F)
for some
k\leq n
such that
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{n}_{+}\times\sigma,\psi)\neq\gamma(s,c\cdot\pi,\wedge^{n}_{+}\times\sigma,\psi).
But one referee told us that they could find a counterexample even for
n=3
. Thus one needs other gamma factors to distinguish
\pi
and
c\cdot\pi
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the local zeta integral and compute it at unramified places, and use it to define the gamma factor
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\eta,\psi)
. We also construct a global integral and show that it is Eulerian and its local piece is the local zeta integral we study in this paper. In Section
3
we review the theory of partial Bessel functions as well as a local converse theorem of Hazeltine and Liu. Finally, in Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Weil representations of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}

We recall the theory of the Weil representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, the metaplectic double cover of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
. Recall that the product on
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
is given by

(g_{1},\zeta_{1})(g_{2},\zeta_{2})=(g_{1}g_{2},\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\mathbf{c}(g_{1},g_{2})),

where
\mathbf{c}:{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)\rightarrow\left\{{\pm 1}\right\}
is defined by

\mathbf{c}(g_{1},g_{2})=({\bf{x}}(g_{1}),{\bf{x}}(g_{2}))_{F}(-{\bf{x}}(g_{1}){\bf{x}}(g_{2}),{\bf{x}}(g_{1}g_{2}))_{F},

where

{\bf{x}}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c,&c\neq 0,\\
d,&c=0.\end{array}\right.

For a subgroup
A
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, we denote by
\widetilde{A}
the preimage of
A
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, which is a subgroup of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
. For an element
g\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, we sometimes also let
g
denote the element
(g,1)
inside
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
.

A representation
\pi
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
is called genuine if
\pi(\zeta g)=\zeta\pi(g)
for all
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
and
\zeta\in\mu_{2}
. Let
\omega_{\psi}
be the Weil representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
realized on
{\mathcal{S}}(F)
, the space of Bruhat-Schwartz functions on
F
. For any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(F)
, the action is given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{\psi}(w_{2})f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\psi)\hat{f}(x),

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{\psi}({\bf{n}}(b))f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\psi(bx^{2})f(x),b\in F,

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{\psi}(t_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(a))f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=|a|^{1/2}{\mu_{\psi}(a)}f(ax),a\in F^{\times},

and

\omega_{\psi}(\zeta)f(x)=\zeta f(x),\zeta\in\mu_{2}.

Several notations need to be explained. Here
\hat{f}(x)=\int_{F}f(y)\psi(2xy)dy
is the Fourier transform of

and the Haar measure

(\hat{f})^{\hat{~}}(x)=f(-x)
. The function
\mu_{\psi}
is defined as
\mu_{\psi}(a)=\frac{\gamma(\psi)}{\gamma(\psi_{a})}
, where the constant
\gamma(\psi)
is the Weil index associated to the character
\psi
. For

, the character
\psi_{a}
is defined by
\psi_{a}(x)=\psi(ax)
.

The product in
\widetilde{T}_{2}
is given by the Hilbert symbol, i.e.,

(t_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(a),1)(t_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(b),1)=(t_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(ab),(a,b)_{F}).

The function
\mu_{\psi}
satisfies

and thus defines a genuine character of
\widetilde{T}_{2}
.
All of the above facts in this subsection can be found in
[

Let

The choice of

defined above is to make the integrals for

and
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
easier; see
[

,
HL:finite
,
HL:p-adic
]
. In fact, if we fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi_{0}
of

a priori and take
\psi(x)=\psi_{0}(\frac{1}{4}x)
, the character
\psi_{U}
is the same as those defined in
[
19
,
HL:finite
,
HL:p-adic
]
. Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be a
\psi_{U}
-generic representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)
. For
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{U})
,
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F)
and

, we consider the local zeta integral

Formally this is well-defined because for any

Lemma 2.1
.

The local zeta integral

is absolutely convergent for

and defines a meromorphic function of

. Moreover, we can choose

such that

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the asymptotic behavior of

) (in the proof of Proposition

∎

2.3.
The local functional equation

Recall that
N=\left\{{{\bf{x}}_{\beta}(y):y\in F}\right\}
. Denote
\psi_{N}=\psi_{U}|_{N}
. For
A\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
, we have

{\bf{m}}(A){\bf{x}}_{\beta}(y){\bf{m}}(A)^{-1}={\bf{x}}_{\beta}(\det(A)y).

Thus the stabilizer of
\psi_{N}
in
M\cong{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
is
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
. From this relation, it is easy to check the following

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
n\in N
and
h\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
, we have

W({\bf{m}}(p(h))n)=\psi_{N}(n)W({\bf{m}}(p(h))).

We now prove the following results.

Proposition 2.3
.

Except for a finite number of
q^{-s}
, there is at most one trilinear form
T
on
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{U})\times\omega_{\psi^{-1}}\times I(s,\eta,\psi^{-1})
such that

T(\pi(n)W,\phi,f_{s})=\psi_{N}(n)T(W,\phi,f_{s}),\forall n\in N

and

T(\pi({\bf{m}}(p(h)))W,\omega_{\psi^{-1}}(h)\phi,r(h)f_{s})=T(W,\phi,f_{s}),\forall h\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F).

Here
r(h)
denotes the right translation by
h
.

Proof.

A trilinear form
T
as in the proposition defines an element in

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}}(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}\otimes\omega_{\psi^{-1}}\otimes I(s,\eta,\psi^{-1}),{\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{P}_{2}}(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}\otimes\omega_{\psi^{-1}},\widetilde{\eta}_{s}^{-1}),

where
\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}
is the Jacquet module of
\pi
with respect to
(N,\psi_{N})
and it is viewed as a representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
by the projection
p
, and
\widetilde{\eta}_{s}=\mu_{\psi^{-1}}\eta_{s-1/2}
.

We consider the representation
\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
. We have the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\oplus_{\alpha\in F^{\times}/F^{\times,2}}{\mathrm{ind}}_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}}((\pi_{N,\psi_{N}})_{N_{2},\psi_{\alpha}})\rightarrow\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}\rightarrow(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}})_{N_{2}}\rightarrow 0.

Here
{\mathrm{ind}}
denotes non-normalized compact induction. Recall that
\psi_{\alpha}
denotes the character of
F
defined by
\psi_{\alpha}(x)=\psi(\alpha x)
. This exact sequence follows from a simple application of the general theory of
[
5
]
; see
[
24
]
*(1.1) for a proof of a similar situation. Since the Jacquet functor preserve admissibility and sends a finitely generated representation to a finitely generated representation,
\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}
has finite length as a representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
and
(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}})_{N_{2}}
is finite dimensional. By the uniqueness of Whittaker model, we have
\dim(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}})_{N_{2},\psi_{\alpha}}\leq 1
and by our assumption, we have

\dim(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}})_{N_{2},\psi}=\dim\pi_{U,\psi_{U}}=1.

Thus after excluding a finite number of
q^{-s}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{P}_{2}}(\pi_{N,\psi_{N}}\otimes\omega_{\psi^{-1}},\tilde{\eta}_{s})

\displaystyle=\oplus_{\alpha\in F^{\times}/F^{\times,2}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\widetilde{P}_{2}}({\mathrm{ind}}_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}}(\psi_{\alpha})\otimes\omega_{\psi^{-1}},\widetilde{\eta}_{s})

\displaystyle=\oplus_{\alpha\in F^{\times}/F^{\times,2}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{2}}({\mathrm{ind}}_{N_{2}}^{P_{2}}(\psi_{\alpha}),\omega_{\psi}\otimes\widetilde{\eta}_{s}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\oplus_{\alpha\in F^{\times}/F^{\times,2}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N_{2}}(\psi_{\alpha},\omega_{\psi}\otimes\widetilde{\eta}_{s}^{-1}).

The result follows from the fact that
(\omega_{\psi}\otimes\widetilde{\eta}_{s}^{-1})_{N_{2},\psi_{\alpha}}=0
if
\alpha\neq 1
and
\dim(\omega_{\psi}\otimes\widetilde{\eta}_{s}^{-1})_{N_{2},\psi_{\alpha}}=1
, which could be checked easily from the Weil representation formulas.
∎

Let
M_{s}:I(s,\eta,\psi^{-1})\to I(1-s,\eta^{-1},\psi^{-1})
be the standard intertwining operator defined by

M_{s}(f_{s})(g)=\int_{N_{2}}f_{s}(w_{2}ng)dn,

where recall that
w_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
. Consider the character
\chi
of
F^{\times}
defined by
\chi(a)=(a,-1)_{F}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

There is a meromorphic function
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta,\psi)
such that

\Psi(W,\phi,M_{s}(f_{s}))=\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta)\Psi(W,\phi,f_{s}),

for all
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi),\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F)
and
f_{s}\in I(s,\eta,\psi)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.2
, we can check that both trilinear forms
(W,\phi,f_{s})\mapsto\Psi(W,\phi,f_{s})
and
(W,\phi,f_{s})\mapsto\Psi(W,\phi,M_{s}(f_{s}))
satisfy the conditions in Proposition
2.3
. Thus by the uniqueness of such trilinear forms, these two trilinear forms are proportional. Denote this proportion by
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta,\psi)
temporarily. By Lemma
2.1
,
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta,\psi)
is a meromorphic function of
s
.
∎

The notation
\gamma(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta,\psi)
will be justified by the unramified calculation in the next subsection.

2.4.
Unramified calculation

Let
\pi
be an unramified representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)
with Satake parameter
{\mathrm{diag}}(a,b,b^{-1},a^{-1})\in{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
W^{0}
be the unramified Whittaker function for
\pi
normalized so that
W^{0}(I_{4})=1
.
Let
a_{k}=t(\varpi^{k},\varpi^{-k})
. By the Casselman-Shalika formula
[
6
]
*Theorem 5.4, we have

W^{0}(a_{k})=\frac{q^{-k}}{(a-b)(ab-1)}(a^{k+2}b^{-k+1}-a^{-k+1}b^{k+2}-a^{k+1}b^{-k}+a^{-k}b^{k+1})

if
k\geq 0
, and
W^{0}(a_{k})=0
if
k<0
. For
h\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)=N_{2}T_{2}K_{2}
, with
K_{2}={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
, we can write
h=n{\mathrm{diag}}(a,a^{-1})k
. The Haar measure on the quotient
N_{2}\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)
is
dh=|a|^{-2}dkda.
We also assume that
\psi
is unramified. Thus we have
\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(u)=1
for
u\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
. Let
\phi^{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}(F)
be the characteristic function of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
and let
f_{s}^{0}\in I(s,\eta,\psi^{-1})
be the function such that
f(k)=1
for
k\in K_{2}
.

We have

f_{s}^{0}({\mathrm{diag}}(\varpi^{k},\varpi^{-k}))=\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(\varpi^{k})\eta(\varpi)^{k}|\varpi^{k}|^{s+1/2},

and

(\omega_{\psi^{-1}}({\mathrm{diag}}(\varpi^{k},\varpi^{-k}))\phi^{0})(1)=|\varpi^{k}|^{1/2}\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(\varpi^{k}).

Notice that

\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(\varpi^{k})\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(\varpi^{k})=\mu_{\psi^{-1}}(\varpi^{2k})(\varpi^{k},\varpi^{k})=(\varpi^{k},\varpi^{k})=(\varpi,-1)^{k}=\chi(\varpi)^{k}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi(W^{0},\phi^{0},f_{s}^{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W^{0}(a_{k})\eta(\varpi)^{k}q^{-ks}\chi(\varpi)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a-b)(ab-1)}\sum_{k}(a^{k+2}b^{-k+1}-a^{-k+1}b^{k+2}-a^{k+1}b^{-k}+a^{-k}b^{k+1})t^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+t}{(1-ab^{-1}t)(1-a^{-1}bt)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t^{2}}{(1-t)(1-ab^{-1}t)(1-a^{-1}bt)},

where

Hence we get

\Psi(W^{0},\phi^{0},f_{s}^{0})=\frac{L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta)}{L(2s,\eta^{2})}.

We summarize the above calculation in the following

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
\pi
is an unramified representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)
with Satake parameter
{\mathrm{diag}}(a,b,b^{-1},a^{-1})
,
W^{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
is the unramified Whittaker function such that
W^{0}(I_{4})=1
,
\phi^{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}(F)
is the characteristic function of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
and
f_{s}^{0}\in I(s,\eta,\psi^{-1})
is the function such that
f_{s}^{0}(k)=1
for
k\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
. Then we have

\Psi(W^{0},\phi^{0},f_{s}^{0})=\frac{L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}_{+}\times\chi\eta)}{L(2s,\eta^{2})}.

In this subsection, we present a global integral so that it is Eulerian and its local piece at a finite local place is the the local zeta integral defined in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2

We have

where

in the theta series.

\varphi
be a cusp form on

\displaystyle Z(s,\varphi,\theta(\phi),f_{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}(F)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}})}\int_{N(F)\backslash N({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi(n{\bf{m}}(g))\psi^{\prime}(n)dn\theta(\phi)(g)f_{s}(g)dg.

\theta(\phi)
, and note that the contribution from
\theta_{0}(\phi)
is

\int_{N_{2}({\mathbb{A}})T_{2}(F)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}})}\int_{N_{2}(F)\backslash N_{2}({\mathbb{A}})}\int_{N(F)\backslash N({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi(n{\bf{m}}(u){\bf{m}}(g))\psi^{\prime}(n)\omega_{\psi}(g)\phi(0)f_{s}(g)dndudg,

\displaystyle Z(s,\varphi,\theta(\phi),f_{s})

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{N_{2}(F)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}})}\int_{N(F)\backslash N({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi(n{\bf{m}}(g))\psi^{\prime}(n)dn(\omega_{\psi}(g)\phi)(1)f_{s}(g)dg

Thus the global integral is Eulerian and its local piece is exactly the local integral defined in §

We recall the theory of Howe vectors as developed in

4.2.
The group
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}

First, we introduce a closely related group, the orthogonal similitude group
\mathrm{GO}_{4}(F)
, which is realized as
\mathrm{GO}_{4}(F)=\{g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(F):g^{t}J_{4}g=\lambda(g)J_{4},\,\lambda(g)\in F^{\times}\}
.
Since
\det(g)^{2}=\lambda(g)^{4}
, it has two connected components depending on whether
\det(g)/\lambda(g)^{2}
is
1
or
-1
. We denote the identity component by
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
, i.e.,
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)=\{g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(F):g^{t}J_{4}g=\lambda(g)J_{4},\,\lambda(g)\in F^{\times},\det(g)=\lambda(g)^{2}\}
.
Note that
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}(F)=\{g\in{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F):\lambda(g)=1\}
. Note that
c\in{\mathrm{G}}{\mathrm{O}}_{4}(F)-{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
and thus the conjugation by
c
is an outer automorphism on
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
.

The two simple roots also define embeddings
\iota_{\alpha},\iota_{\beta}:{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)\to{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
which can be described explicitly as

\iota_{\alpha}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0&0\\
c&d&0&0\\
0&0&a&-b\\
0&0&-c&d\end{pmatrix},\,\,\iota_{\beta}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b&0\\
0&a&0&-b\\
c&0&d&0\\
0&-c&0&d\end{pmatrix}.

Notice that
\lambda(\iota_{\alpha}(g))=\det(g)
and
\lambda(\iota_{\beta}(g))=\det(g)
for
g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. The embeddings
\iota_{\alpha}
and
\iota_{\beta}
define a homomorphism

{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)\to{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)

(g,h)\mapsto\iota_{\alpha}(g)\iota_{\beta}(h),

which gives an isomorphism

{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)\cong({\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F))/\Delta(F^{\times}),

where
\Delta(F^{\times})=\{(aI_{2},a^{-1}I_{2}):a\in F^{\times}\}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
. Thus an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
is of the form
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
with
\omega_{\pi_{1}}=\omega_{\pi_{2}}
, where
\pi_{i}
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
and
\omega_{\pi_{i}}
is the central character of
\pi_{i}
.

Note that the outer automorphism
c
on
{\mathrm{GSO}}_{4}(F)
satisfies

c\iota_{\alpha}(g)c=\iota_{\beta}(g),\forall g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F).

Thus for an irreducible representation

of

with irreducible representations

of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
with

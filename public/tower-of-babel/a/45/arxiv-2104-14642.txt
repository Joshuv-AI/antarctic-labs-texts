Abstract
. We determine the integral Chow and cohomology rings of the moduli stack ℬ r , d \mathcal{B}_{r,d} of rank r r , degree d d vector bundles on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} bundles. We first show that the rational Chow ring A ℚ ∗ ​ ( ℬ r , d ) A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}) is a free ℚ \mathbb{Q} -algebra on 2 ​ r + 1 2r+1 generators. The isomorphism class of this ring happens to be independent of d d . Then, we prove that the integral Chow ring A ∗ ​ ( ℬ r , d ) A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}) is torsion-free and provide multiplicative generators for A ∗ ​ ( ℬ r , d ) A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}) as a subring of A ℚ ∗ ​ ( ℬ r , d ) A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}) . From this description, we see that A ∗ ​ ( ℬ r , d ) A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}) is not finitely generated as a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -algebra. Finally, the cohomology ring of ℬ r , d \mathcal{B}_{r,d} is isomorphic to its Chow ring.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we fully describe the intersection theory of the moduli stack
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundles. Precisely, an object of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
over a scheme
T
is the data of a rank
2
vector bundle
W
on
T
and a rank
r
, relative degree
d
vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}W
.
To describe generators of the Chow or cohomology ring,
let
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
be the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle and let
w_{1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{W})
and
w_{2}=c_{2}(\mathcal{W})
be Chern classes of the universal rank
2
bundle
\mathcal{W}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the universal rank
r
bundle on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}
. If
z=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1))
, the Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}
are uniquely expressible as
c_{i}(\mathcal{E})=\pi^{*}(a_{i})+\pi^{*}(a_{i}^{\prime})z
for
a_{i}\in A^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
and
a_{i}^{\prime}\in A^{i-1}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
. We show that the rational Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
is freely generated by these classes. Then, we show that the integral Chow ring is torsion-free, and describe it as a subring of the rational Chow ring. This also determines the cohomology ring of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
, as it agrees with the Chow ring.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})=\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]
. The integral Chow ring
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})\subset A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is the subring generated by
w_{1},w_{2}
and the Chern classes of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(i)
for
i=0,1,2,\ldots
. Moreover, the cycle class map
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})\to H^{2*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is an isomorphism.

In the special case when the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle is trivial (
W
is trivial), the integral Chow ring has a somewhat simpler description. This describes the Chow ring of the moduli space
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger}
of vector bundles on a fixed
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. (Precisely, an object of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger}
over a scheme
T
is a rank
r
, relative degree
d
vector bundle
E
on the trivial
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times T
.) The map
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger}\to\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
is the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
bundle associated to
\mathcal{W}
on
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
. By
[
9
, Theorem 2]
of Vistoli, the pullback
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})\to A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger})
is surjective with kernel generated by
w_{1},w_{2}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

We have
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger})=\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]
.
Integrally,
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger})\subset A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d}^{\dagger})
is the subring generated by

a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
and the coefficients
f_{i}
of the power series

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}t^{i}=\exp\left(\int\frac{d\cdot(a_{1}+a_{2}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r-1})-(a_{2}^{\prime}+a_{3}^{\prime}t+\ldots+a_{r}^{\prime}t^{r-2})}{1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r}}dt\right).

Moreover, the cycle class map

is an isomorphism.

We point out several interesting features of our results:

(1)

Although
A^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is obviously finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Q}
algebra,
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is
not
finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}
algebra (see Corollary
5.5
).

(2)

The rational Chow ring
A^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is independent of
d
.
This may lead one to wonder if the isomorphism class of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
could be independent of
d
. However, considering integral Chow rings, one can show that
\mathcal{B}_{2,1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2,0}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}_{2,1}^{\dagger}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2,0}^{\dagger}
) are
not
isomorphic (see Corollary
5.6
).

(3)

To show
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,d})
is torsion-free, we stratify by splitting loci, which in turn are modeled by spaces admitting affine stratifications. This stratification is also what allows us to see that the Chow and cohomology rings of
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
agree (see Lemma
5.3
).

(4)

Using the theory of higher Chow groups, we show that push forward maps for including strata are all injective on Chow. This relies on a vanishing result for higher Chow groups of a point with torsion coefficients. Although this vanishing result only holds for an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, we deduce from it a rank equality which allows us to establish our theorem in
all
characteristics.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.
Preliminaries on equivariant Chow and higher Chow

Most of the stacks we encounter in this paper are quotients of open subsets
X
of affine space by linear algebraic groups
G
. The Chow ring of a quotient stack
[X/G]
is defined as the
G
-equivariant Chow ring of
X
, which in turn is defined in
[
5
]
using models based on Borel’s mixing construction. More precisely, given a representation
V
of
G
and an open subset
U\subset V
on which
G
acts freely, if
\codim U^{c}>i
, we define
A^{i}([X/G])=A^{i}(X\times_{G}U)
where
X\times_{G}U
is the quotient of
X\times U
by the diagonal
G
action.
This is well-defined because
A^{*}(V)\cong A^{*}(X)
whenever
V
is a vector bundle over
X
(“homotopy”) and
A^{i}(X-Z)=A^{i}(X)
whenever
\codim Z>i
(“excision”).

Example 2.1
.

Let
V_{N}=\mathrm{Mat}_{r\times N}(k)=k^{\oplus rN}
with
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
acting on
V_{N}
by left multiplication.
The group
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
acts freely on the open subset
U_{N}\subset V_{N}
of full rank matrices. This determines a model
pt\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}U_{N}=U_{N}/\mathrm{GL}_{r}=G(r,N)
. Since
\codim U_{N}^{c}=N-r+1
, we have
A^{i}(\mathrm{BGL}_{r})=A^{i}(G(r,N))
for
i<N-r+1
. It is a classical result that
A^{*}(G(r,N))
is generated by the Chern classes
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}
of the tautological rank
r
bundle with no relations in degrees less than
N-r+1
. Taking larger and larger
N
, it follows that
A^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{r})=\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}]
.

Variants of the above construction allow one to approximate all quotient stacks in this paper with concrete models which are fiber bundles over Grassmannians.

The higher Chow groups of these quotient stacks will also be an important tool.
In
[
2
]
, Bloch defines the higher Chow groups of a quasi-projective variety
X
as the homology of a complex
z^{*}(X,-)
of free abelian groups, i.e.
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,n)=H_{n}(z^{*}(X,-))
.
Higher Chow groups with coefficients in a ring
R=\mathbb{Z}/m
or
\mathbb{Z}
are defined similarly by
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,n,R)=H_{n}(z^{*}(X,-)\otimes R)
.
Some properties of higher Chow groups are the following.

(1)

Weight zero: we have
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,0,R)=A^{*}(X)\otimes R
.

(2)

Functoriality: there are proper push forwards and and flat pull backs.

(3)

Localization long exact sequence: If
Y\subset X
is a closed subscheme of pure codimension
d
, then there is a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*-d}(Y,1,R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,1,R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X-Y,1,R)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*-d}(Y,0,R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,0,R)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X-Y,0,R).

(4)

Homotopy:
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{m},n,R)\cong\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,n,R)
. By (
2
) and (
3
) it follows that if
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is any affine bundle, then
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,n,R)\cong\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\widetilde{X},n,R)
.

Edidin and Graham
[
5
]
extend the notion of higher Chow groups to quotients
[X/G]
by defining them to be higher Chow groups of suitable models:
\mathrm{CH}^{*}([X/G],n,R):=\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X\times_{G}U,n,R)
where
U
is an open subset of a representation of
G
whose complement has sufficiently high codimension and
G
acts freely on
U
. This is well-defined by the homotopy property, and Edidin-Graham obtain a localization long exact sequence for the corresponding quotients in (
3
) when
Y
is
G
-equivariant.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the higher Chow groups of a point with torsion coefficients are known:

(2.1)

\mathrm{CH}^{i}(pt,n,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/\ell&\text{if $n=2i$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This follows from
[
8
, Corollary 4.3]
, which relates higher Chow groups to certain étale cohomology groups, though this special case was likely known earlier.
Using the long exact sequence and the homotopy property, it follows that over such a field,
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,1;\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=0
for any
X
admitting an affine stratification.
In particular, since
\mathrm{BGL}_{r}
is modeled by Grassmannians
G(r,N)
(see Example
2.1
), we have

(2.2)

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{s}},1;\mathbb{Z}/\ell)=0

over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

3.
Construction of the moduli stack

Given some rank
r\geq 0
and degree
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the moduli stack
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
of vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundles by

\mathcal{B}_{r,d}(T)=\left\{(W,E):\parbox{220.00034pt}{$W$ a rank $2$ vector bundle on $T$ \\
$E$ a rank $r$, relative degree $d$ vector bundle on $\mathbb{P}W$}\right\}.

An arrow
(W,E)\to(W^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is the data of an isomorphism
\phi:W\to W^{\prime}
— which induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{P}\phi:\mathbb{P}W\to\mathbb{P}W^{\prime}
— and an isomorphism
\psi:E\to(\mathbb{P}\phi)^{*}E^{\prime}
.
Given a vector bundle
E
on a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle
\mathbb{P}W\rightarrow T
, we write
E(m):=E\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}W}(1)^{\otimes m}
.
There are equivalences
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}\cong\mathcal{B}_{r,d+mr}
sending
(W,E)\mapsto(W,E(m))
. Thus, it would suffice to study
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
for
0\leq\ell<r
. Throughout,
d=\ell+mr
will be an arbitrary degree in the residue class of
\ell
modulo
r
.

The stack
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
is a union of open substacks

\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}_{m}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\mathcal{U}_{0}\subset\mathcal{U}_{1}\subset\mathcal{U}_{2}\subset\cdots.

where

\mathcal{U}_{m}:=\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}(T)

\displaystyle=\{(W,E)\in\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}(T):\text{$E(m)$ is globally generated on each fiber over $T$}\}

\displaystyle=\{(W,E)\in\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}(T):\text{$R^{1}\pi_{*}E(m-1)=0$ for $\pi:\mathbb{P}W\rightarrow T$ projection}\}.

The stack
\mathcal{U}_{0,r,\ell}
is equal to the stack of globally generated rank
r
, degree
\ell
vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundles, which we shall denote
\mathcal{V}_{r,\ell}
. Via the twist by
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}W}(m)
, there are isomorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}\cong\mathcal{V}_{r,\ell+mr}
for each
m
.

3.1.
Splitting loci

Every vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
splits as a direct sum of line bundles, say
E=\mathscr{O}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{O}(e_{r})
for
e_{1}\leq\cdots\leq e_{r}
. We call
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
the
splitting type
of
E
, and abbreviate the corresponding sum of line bundles by

\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}):=\mathscr{O}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{O}(e_{r}).

The moduli space
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
admits a stratification by the
splitting loci
of the universal vector bundle.
These are the locally closed substacks
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
which parametrize families of vector bundles with splitting type
\vec{e}
on each fiber of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle.

Suppose that
\mathscr{O}(\vec{e})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathscr{O}(d_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}
with
d_{1}<\ldots<d_{s}
(so the
d_{i}
are the
distinct
degrees in
\vec{e}
and the
r_{i}
their multiplicities).
Let us identify
\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))
with block upper triangular matrices whose entries in the
i,j
block are homogeneous polynomials on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d_{j}-d_{i}
.
The block diagonal matrices correspond to the subgroup
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{GL}_{r_{i}}\hookrightarrow\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))
.
The group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
acts on the off-diagonal blocks via change of coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The data of a vector bundle
E
on a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle
\mathbb{P}W\rightarrow T
whose restriction to each fiber has splitting type
\vec{e}

is the same as a principal bundle for the semi-direct product
H_{\vec{e}}:=\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))\ltimes\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.
In other words,
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
is equivalent to the classifying stack
BH_{\vec{e}}
. From this, we see that

(3.1)

\codim\Sigma_{\vec{e}}=h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},End(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))=\sum_{i,j}\max\{0,e_{i}-e_{j}-1\}=:u(\vec{e}).

The complement of
\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}\subset\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}

is the union of splitting loci
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
with
e_{1}<-m
. In particular, using (
3.1
), one sees that this union of splitting loci has codimension
\ell+mr+1
(see also
[
7
, Section 5]
). In particular, as
m
increases, the codimension of the complement of
\mathcal{U}_{m}
goes to infinity.
The key to understanding the intersection theory of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
is therefore to understand each
\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}
, which is equivalent to the stack of globally generated vector bundles
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
for
d=\ell+mr
.

3.2.
Globally generated vector bundles

The stack
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
of rank
r
, degree
d
globally generated vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
was constructed by Bolognesi–Vistoli in
[
3
]
. We briefly motivate and review their construction.
If
E
is a globally generated vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then there is a surjection
H^{0}(E)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to E
. Since
h^{0}(E)=r+d
, the kernel of this map is rank
d
, degree
-d
. Furthermore, the kernel has no global sections, so it must be
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus d}
. That is, given a globally generated
E
, it sits naturally in a sequence

(3.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(r+d)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

Let
M_{r,d}:=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus d},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(r+d)})
be the space of
d\times(r+d)
matrices of linear forms on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The sequence (
3.2
) determines an element
\psi\in M_{r,d}
which is well-defined up to the choice of framings of the source and target. Moreover,
\psi
lies in the open subvariety
\Omega_{r,d}\subset M_{r,d}
of matrices of linear forms having full rank
d
at each point on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Let
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
act on
M_{r,d}
by left multiplication and
\mathrm{GL}_{r+d}
act by right multiplication. In addition, let
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
act on
M_{r,d}
by change of coordinates on the entries, which live in the two-dimensional vector space
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1))
. These three actions commute, so we obtain an action of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{d+r}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
on
M_{r,d}
. The locus
\Omega_{r,d}\subset M_{r,d}
is preserved by this action and thus inherits an action of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{d+r}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Bolognesi-Vistoli
[
3
]
, Theorem 4.4)
.

There is an isomorphism of fibered categories

\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\cong[\Omega_{r,d}/\mathrm{GL}_{d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r+d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}].

In other words, Theorem
3.1
says
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
is an open substack of a vector bundle over
\mathrm{BGL}_{d}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r+d}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2}
.
In particular, by the homotopy and excision properties, we have a surjection

(3.3)

A^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{d}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r+d}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2})\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d}).

Let
\mathcal{W}
denote the universal rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
(pulled back from the
\mathrm{BGL}_{2}
factor), and let
\mathcal{E}^{gg}:=\mathcal{E}_{r,d}^{gg}
be the universal globally generated rank
r
, degree
d
vector bundle on
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
.
Let
T_{d}
and
T_{r+d}
denote the universal vector bundles on
\mathrm{BGL}_{d}
and
\mathrm{BGL}_{r+d}
. We wish to identify their pullbacks to
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
in terms of
\mathcal{E}^{gg}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma:\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\rightarrow\mathrm{BGL}_{d}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r+d}
be the natural map.
We have

(3.4)

\gamma^{*}T_{d}=(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}(-1))\otimes\det\mathcal{W}^{\vee}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma^{*}T_{r+d}=\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}.

In particular,
A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})
is generated by the Chern classes of the three vector bundles
\mathcal{W}
,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}(-1)
and
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}
.

Proof.

By the construction of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
as a quotient of
\Omega_{r,d}\subset M_{r,d}
, the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
is equipped with an exact sequence of vector bundles

(3.5)

0\rightarrow(\pi^{*}\gamma^{*}T_{d})(-1)\rightarrow\pi^{*}\gamma^{*}T_{r+d}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{gg}\rightarrow 0.

Pushing forward (
3.5
) by
\pi
induces an isomorphism

\gamma^{*}T_{r+d}\cong\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\gamma^{*}T_{r+d}\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}.

On the other hand, tensoring (
3.5
) with
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(-1)
and pushing forward by
\pi
induces an isomorphism

(3.6)

\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}^{gg}(-1)\xrightarrow{\sim}R^{1}\pi_{*}((\pi^{*}\gamma^{*}T_{d})(-2))\cong\gamma^{*}T_{d}\otimes R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(-2)

Noting that the relative dualizing sheaf of
\pi
is
\omega_{\pi}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(-2)\otimes\det\mathcal{W}^{\vee}
, Serre duality provides an isomorphism of the righthand term in (
3.6
) with
\pi^{*}\gamma^{*}T_{d}\otimes\det\mathcal{W}
. Having identified the tautological vector bundles, (
3.3
) now establishes the claim about generators of
A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})
.
∎

Remark
.

provides a quick proof of the existence half of

: Pulling back the classes of closures of the universal splitting loci
\overline{\Sigma}_{\vec{e}}
on
\mathcal{B}_{r,d}
, it follows that
when the splitting loci of a vector bundle

on a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle

\pi_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}W}(1)
and the bundles
\pi_{*}E(i)
for suitable

.
This observation does not, however, give an indication of how to find these formulas, as done in

Now, let
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}_{r,\ell}
denote the universal rank

The Chow ring

4.
The rational Chow ring

The rational Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
can be described with fewer generators than the integral generators of Lemma
3.3
.
Let
w_{1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{W})
,
w_{2}=c_{2}(\mathcal{W})
and
z=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1))
.
The Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}
can be written as
c_{i}(\mathcal{E})=\pi^{*}(a_{i})+\pi^{*}(a_{i}^{\prime})z
for unique
a_{i}\in A^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
and
a_{i}^{\prime}\in A^{i-1}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
. (Note that
a_{1}^{\prime}=\ell
.) By Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, the Chern classes of the vector bundle
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)
on
\mathcal{U}_{m}
are expressible in terms of the
a_{i}
and
a_{i}^{\prime}
for any
m
, so these classes are generators for the rational Chow ring of each
\mathcal{U}_{m}
and therefore of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
. The main result of this section will be that there are no relations among these generators on
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
. We first consider relations on an open
\mathcal{U}_{m}\cong\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The ring
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})
is a quotient of
\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]
with all relations in degrees
d+1
and higher.

Proof.

Set
G:=\mathrm{GL}_{d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{r+d}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.
We let
T_{d},T_{r+d}
and
\mathcal{W}
denote the corresponding tautological bundles on
B:=BG
. Let
t_{i}=c_{i}(T_{d})
and
u_{i}=c_{1}(T_{r+d})
and
w_{i}=c_{i}(\mathcal{W})
be their Chern classes, which freely generate
A^{*}(B)
.
Next, define
M:=[M_{r,d}/G]
, which is the total space of the vector bundle
\mathcal{H}om(T_{d},T_{r+d})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\vee}
over
B
. Theorem
3.1
says that
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
is an open substack of
M
. Let us write
X:=[\Omega_{r,d}^{c}/G]
for its closed complement. Here,
\Omega_{r,d}^{c}\subset M_{r,d}
is the space of matrices of linear forms that drop rank along some point on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
By excision, we have a right exact sequence

(4.1)

A^{*-\mathrm{codim}X}(X)\rightarrow A^{*}(M)\rightarrow A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})\rightarrow 0.

We shall see soon that
X
is irreducible of codimension
r
, which immediately implies there are no relations among generators of
A^{*}(M)
restricted to
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
in degrees less than
r
. In what follows, we describe relations among the restrictions of these Chern classes in degrees
r
up to
d
.

First we construct a space
\widetilde{X}
whose total space maps properly to
M
with image
X
.
Let
\mathbb{P}(T_{d})
be the projectivization of the tautological rank
d
bundle and let
\sigma:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d})\rightarrow B
be the map to the base. On
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d})
we have a surjection of vector bundles

\sigma^{*}M=\sigma^{*}(\mathcal{H}om(T_{d},T_{r+d})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{d})}(1)\otimes\sigma^{*}T_{r+d}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1),

corresponding to evaluation of the map along a one-dimensional subspace of the fiber of
T_{d}
. Let
\widetilde{X}\subset\sigma^{*}M
denote the total space of the kernel vector bundle.
Informally,

\widetilde{X}=\{(p,\Lambda,\psi):p\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W},\Lambda\subset(T_{d})_{\pi(p)},\psi\in M_{\pi(p)},\Lambda\subset\ker\psi(p)\subset(T_{d})_{\pi(p)}\}.

We have a commutative diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

where
\rho,\rho^{\prime},
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}
are all vector bundle maps.
By construction,
\sigma^{\prime}(\iota(\widetilde{X}))=X
and
\sigma^{\prime}\circ\iota
is projective, as desired. The map
\widetilde{X}\to X
is also generically
1
-to-
1
. This establishes that

\dim X=\dim\widetilde{X}=\dim M+\dim(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d}))-\rank T_{k+r}=\dim M-r.

Since
\sigma^{\prime}\circ\iota
is projective, the pushforward of rational Chow groups
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\widetilde{X})\to A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(X)
is surjective. (Take the preimage of any cycle; if the fibers are generically finite, the pushforward is some multiple of the original cycle. Otherwise, slice with enough copies of the hyperplane class to get a cycle mapping with generically finite fibers to the same image.) It follows that the image of
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*-r}(X)\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(M)
via pushforward of cycles is the same as the image of
(\sigma^{\prime}\circ\iota)_{*}:A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*-r}(\widetilde{X})\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(M)
.

The pullback maps
(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{*}
,
(\rho^{\prime})^{*}
and
\rho^{*}
all induce isomorphisms on Chow rings.
Let
(\rho^{\prime})^{*}\beta
be the fundamental class of
\widetilde{X}
in the Chow ring of
\sigma^{*}M
. In fact,

\beta:=c_{r+d}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{d})}(1)\otimes\sigma^{*}T_{r+d}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1)).

We can write every class in
A^{*-r}(\widetilde{X})
as
(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{*}\alpha
for a unique class
\alpha\in A^{*-r}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d}))
.
Then the effect of
(\sigma^{\prime}\circ\iota)_{*}
is

(4.2)

\sigma^{\prime}_{*}\iota_{*}(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{*}\alpha=\sigma^{\prime}_{*}\iota_{*}\iota^{*}(\rho^{\prime})^{*}\alpha=\sigma^{\prime}_{*}([\widetilde{X}]\cdot(\rho^{\prime})^{*}\alpha)=\sigma^{\prime}_{*}(\rho^{\prime})^{*}(\beta\cdot\alpha)=\rho^{*}\sigma_{*}(\beta\cdot\alpha).

Now, let
z=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1))
and
\zeta=c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{d})}(1))
.
By the projective bundle theorem,

(4.3)

A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d}))=A^{*}(B)[z,\zeta]/(z^{2}+w_{1}z+w_{2},\zeta^{d}+\zeta^{d-1}t_{1}+\ldots+\zeta t_{d-1}+t_{d}).

Thus, each class
\alpha\in A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\times_{B}\mathbb{P}(T_{d}))
is uniquely expressible as

(4.4)

\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(\sigma^{*}\gamma_{i})\zeta^{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\sigma^{*}\gamma_{i}^{\prime})z\zeta^{i-1},

where
\gamma_{i}\in A^{*}(B)
with
\deg\gamma_{i}=\deg\gamma_{i}^{\prime}=\deg\alpha-i
.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.4
), the image of
(\sigma^{\prime}\circ\iota)_{*}:A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*-r}(\widetilde{X})\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(X)\cong A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(B)
is the ideal generated by
the classes

f_{i,j}:=\sigma_{*}(\beta\cdot z^{i}\zeta^{j})\qquad\text{for }0\leq i\leq 1,\ 0\leq j\leq d-1.

As
\rho^{*}
is an isomorphism on Chow, we omit it above and in what follows for ease of notation.
By the excision sequence (
4.1
), we have

A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})=\frac{A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(B)}{\langle f_{i,j}:0\leq i\leq 1,0\leq j\leq d-1\rangle}\cong\frac{\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},t_{1},\ldots,t_{d},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+d}]}{\langle f_{i,j}:0\leq i\leq 1,0\leq j\leq d-1\rangle}.

Since
\sigma
has relative dimension
d
, the codimension of
f_{i,j}
is
(r+d)+i+j-d=r+i+j
.
Recall that there are no relations among the generators of
A^{*}(M)\cong A^{*}(B)
, so
f_{i,j}
is a unique polynomial of codimension
i+j+r
in the
t
’s,
u
’s and
w
’s.
We are interested in particular in the coefficients of
t_{i+j+r}
and
u_{i+j+r}
in this expression for
f_{i,j}
.
By the splitting principle and (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=c_{r+d}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{d})}(1)\otimes\sigma^{*}T_{r+d}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1))=\sum_{i=0}^{r+d}(\zeta+z)^{r+d-i}\sigma^{*}u_{i}

\displaystyle=(\zeta^{r+d}+(r+d)z\zeta^{r+d-1})+(\zeta^{r+d-1}+(r+d-1)z\zeta^{r+d-2})\sigma^{*}u_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\ldots+(\zeta^{d}+dz\zeta^{d-1})\sigma^{*}u_{r}+\ldots+\sigma^{*}u_{r+d}+\langle\sigma^{*}w_{1},\sigma^{*}w_{2}\rangle.

The push forward of any term involving
\sigma^{*}w_{1}
or
\sigma^{*}w_{2}
cannot contribute to the coefficient of
t_{i+j+r}
or
u_{i+j+r}
. Since
z^{2}\in\langle\sigma^{*}w_{1},\sigma^{*}w_{2}\rangle
, after we multiply
z^{i}\zeta^{j}
with
\beta
, we only care about the resulting terms where the power of
z
is
1
(if the power of
z
is zero, then the push forward vanishes).

To compute the push forward of such terms, iterated use of (
4.3
) tell us (or c.f.
[
6
, Corollary 2.6]
)

\sigma_{*}(z\zeta^{d-1+i})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $i<0$}\\
1&\text{if $i=0$}\\
\sum_{m_{1}\cdot 1+\ldots+m_{d}\cdot d=i}(-1)^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{d}}\cdot\frac{(m_{1}+\ldots+m_{d})!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{d}!}\cdot t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{m_{d}}&\text{if $i\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

The coefficient in front of a monomial for
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})
above is the number of ordered partitions of
i
so that
j
appears with multiplicity
m_{j}
. The terms we are interested in will come from that monomial being
1
or
t_{i}
. In particular, we compute

\displaystyle f_{1,j-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{*}(\beta z\zeta^{j-1})=-t_{j+r}+u_{j+r}+\ldots

\displaystyle f_{0,j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{*}(\beta\zeta^{j})=-(r+d)t_{j+r}+(d-j)u_{j+r}+\ldots.

Hence, in
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})
,
the classes
t_{n}
for
r\leq n\leq d
and
u_{m}
for
r+1\leq m\leq d
are expressible as polynomials in
w_{1},w_{2},t_{1},\ldots,t_{r-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
. Moreover, after eliminating these higher degree generators, the
f_{i,j}
produce no additional relations in degrees less than or equal to
d
among the restrictions to
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
of
w_{1},w_{2},t_{1},\ldots,t_{r-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
. Hence, the map

(4.5)

\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{r-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}]\to A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d})

is an isomorphism in degrees
*\leq d
.
For dimension reasons, there can be no relations among the generators
w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}
in degrees less than
d
because
they are a list of the same number of generators in the same degrees as (
4.5
).
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

We have
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})=\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

Recall that the codimension of the complement of
\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell}
is
\ell+mr+1
. Thus, choosing
m
such that
\ell+mr+1>i
, we see

\displaystyle\dim A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})

\displaystyle=\dim A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}(\mathcal{U}_{m,r,\ell})=\dim A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{i}(\mathcal{V}_{r,mr+\ell})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]_{i}.

We already know
\mathbb{Q}[w_{1},w_{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{r}^{\prime}]\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
, so we conclude it has no kernel for dimension reasons.
∎

5.
The integral Chow ring

Our plan is to describe the integral Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
by building it up as a union of the splitting loci
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
using excision.
The reader may be interested to compare the ideas and use of higher Chow groups here with work of Bae and Schmidtt in their study of the moduli stack of pointed genus
0
curves
[
1
]
.

The Chow rings of our strata
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
are particularly nice.
Fix some
\vec{e}
and write
\mathscr{O}(\vec{e})=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathscr{O}(d_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}
as in Section
3.1
.
There is an inclusion of groups
\prod\mathrm{GL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}\hookrightarrow H_{\vec{e}}
, which induces a map on classifying stacks

(5.1)

\prod\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2}\rightarrow BH_{\vec{e}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The map (
5.1
) factors as a sequence of affine bundles.
In particular
A^{*}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})=A^{*}(\prod\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2})
, which is a free
\mathbb{Z}
algebra. Furthermore, over
\mathbb{C}
, the first higher Chow groups satisfy

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}},1,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{CH}^{*}\left(\prod\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2},1,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)=0

for all primes
p
.

Proof.

We induct on
s
. The
s=1
case is immediate. Let
G^{\prime}=\Aut(\oplus_{i=1}^{s-1}\mathscr{O}(d_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}})
and
H=G^{\prime}\times GL_{r_{s}}\subset\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))
. There is a quotient map
\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))\rightarrow H
defined by forgetting blocks not in
H
, which expresses
\Aut(\mathscr{O}(\vec{e}))
as a semidirect product
N\rtimes H
where
N\cong H^{0}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d_{s}-d_{1}))^{\oplus(r_{1}r_{s})}\oplus\cdots\oplus H^{0}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d_{s}-d_{s-1}))^{\oplus(r_{s-1}r_{s})}
is affine. Moreover,

H_{\vec{e}}=(N\rtimes H)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2}=N\rtimes((G^{\prime}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2})\times\mathrm{GL}_{r_{s}}).

Now let
H_{\vec{e}}
act on
N
where elements of
N
act by left multiplication and elements of
(G^{\prime}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2})\times\mathrm{GL}_{r_{s}}
act by conjugation.
This action is affine linear, so the quotient, which is
B((G^{\prime}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2})\times\mathrm{GL}_{r_{s}})
, is an affine bundle over
BH_{\vec{e}}
.
By the homotopy property, the Chow ring and higher Chow groups of
BH_{\vec{e}}
agree with that of
B(G^{\prime}\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{2})\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{s}}
, which, by induction, are isomorphic to those of
(\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{s-1}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2})\times\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{s}}
. The vanishing of the first higher Chow group over
\mathbb{C}
is equation (
2.2
).
∎

Using the above lemma, we find that inclusion of cycle classes from strata is injective and deduce that the Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
is torsion-free.

Lemma 5.2
.

The Chow group
A^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
is a finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module for all
i
.

Proof.

Suppose
U
is a finite union of strata and
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
is a disjoint stratum which is closed in
X=U\cup\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
.
By Lemma
5.1
, we know
A^{i}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})
is a finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module for all
i
. By induction, we may also assume
A^{i}(U)
is a finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module.
It suffices to show that
A^{i}(X)
is also a finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Let us first deduce the result over
\mathbb{C}
.
We have a localization long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}},1,\mathbb{Z}/p)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,1,\mathbb{Z}/p)\rightarrow\mathrm{CH}^{*}(U,1,\mathbb{Z}/p)

\displaystyle\rightarrow A^{*-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow A^{*}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow A^{*}(U)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have
\mathrm{CH}^{*-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}},1,\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
, so if

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(U,1,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})=0
then we have
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(X,1,\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
too. It follows by induction that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(U,1,\mathbb{Z}/p)=0
for any finite union of strata.
Hence, we have an exact sequence

(5.2)

0\rightarrow A^{*-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow A^{*}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow A^{*}(U)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0

for each prime
p
.
Because the
\mathbb{Z}
-modules involved are finitely-generated, the exactness of (
5.2
) for all
p
implies that

(5.3)

0\rightarrow A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\rightarrow A^{i}(X)\rightarrow A^{i}(U)\rightarrow 0

is exact.
Since
A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})
and
A^{i}(U)
are finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, so is
A^{i}(X)
. This concludes the proof over
\mathbb{C}
.

The exactness of (
5.3
) over
\mathbb{C}
also tells us that

(5.4)

\rank A^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})=\sum_{\vec{e}}\rank A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}}).

We claim that (
5.4
) in fact holds over any ground field. Indeed, Theorem
4.1
holds over any field, so the
left-hand side is independent of the ground field. Similarly, using Lemma
5.1
, we have
A^{*}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})=A^{*}(\prod\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2})
and the latter is independent of the ground field, so the right-hand side of (
5.4
) is independent of the ground field.

Now, working over any ground field, we claim that the map
A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\to A^{i}(X)
for attaching each stratum is injective. If not, then the image of
A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})\to A^{i}(X)
would have rank strictly less than
\rank A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})
. Then,
\rank A^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
would be less than the sum
\sum_{\vec{e}}\rank A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})
, violating (
5.4
). Hence, we must have an exact sequence as in (
5.3
) for each
\vec{e}
. Arguing as before, we know
A^{i-u(\vec{e})}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})
and
A^{i}(U)
are finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, so because (
5.3
) is exact,
A^{i}(X)
is also a finitely-generated free
\mathbb{Z}
module.
∎

An analogous argument in cohomology can be used to show that Chow and cohomology rings of
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
agree.

Lemma 5.3
.

The cycle class map
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})\to H^{2*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})

Proof.

As before, suppose

is a finite union of strata and
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}
is a disjoint stratum which is closed in

.
Because

is the reduced cohomology of the Thom space of the normal bundle of

. By the Thom isomorphism, the cohomology of this pair is then

.
By Lemma

, we have
H^{*}(\Sigma_{\vec{e}})=H^{*}(\prod\mathrm{BGL}_{r_{i}}\times\mathrm{BGL}_{2})
, which vanishes in odd degrees.
By induction, we may assume that the odd cohomology of

vanishes. Thus, the long exact sequence for the pair

gives an exact sequence

.
The cycle class map sends the short exact sequence (

) to (

). Because

, we see that

is also an isomorphism.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary
4.2
determines
A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
. Lemma

shows

is a subring of

identifies it as
the subring generated by the Chern classes of the sheaves
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)
. Lemma
5.3
shows that the cycle class map is an isomorphism.
∎

To make this subring more explicit, we provide formulas for the Chern classes of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)
in terms of the rational generators (working modulo
\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle
). Recall that
a_{1}^{\prime}=\ell
is the relative degree of
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

(5.6)

F(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}t^{i}=\exp\left(\int\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}(a_{1}+a_{2}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r-1})-(a_{2}^{\prime}+a_{3}^{\prime}t+\ldots+a_{r}^{\prime}t^{r-2})}{1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r}}dt\right).

Then

(5.7)

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{i}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m))t^{i}=F(t)(1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r})^{m+1}\quad\mod\langle w_{1},w_{2},t^{mr+\ell+1}\rangle.

Proof.

We will use Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch to compute the Chern characters and make use of formal manipulations that turn power sums (Chern characters) into elementary symmetric functions (Chern classes). It is convenient to package this information in generating functions.

Given some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
, let
\sigma_{j}:=\sigma_{j}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,a_{r})=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}}\alpha_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{j}}
denote the
j
th elementary symmetric function in the
\alpha_{i}
. For each
j\geq 0
, there is a polynomial
p_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
such that
p_{j}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})=\alpha_{1}^{j}+\ldots+\alpha_{r}^{j}
. These polynomials satisfy

(5.8)

\log(1+x_{1}t+\ldots+x_{r}t^{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}p_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})t^{j}.

For any vector bundle
E
, the
j
th Chern character is related to the Chern classes by

\mathrm{ch}_{j}(E)=\frac{1}{j!}p_{j}(c_{1}(E),\ldots,c_{r}(E)).

Each Chern character of the universal
\mathcal{E}
on
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
is expressible as
\mathrm{ch}_{j}(\mathcal{E})=\pi^{*}(c_{j})+\pi^{*}(c_{j}^{\prime})z
for some
c_{j}\in A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{j}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
and
c_{j}^{\prime}\in A_{\mathbb{Q}}^{j-1}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
.
In what follows, we work modulo the ideal
\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle
, so that
z^{2}=0
. We have

(5.9)

\displaystyle c_{j}=\frac{1}{j!}p_{j}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{\prime}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}).

We write
c=c_{0}+c_{1}+c_{2}+\ldots
and
c^{\prime}=c_{1}^{\prime}+c_{2}^{\prime}+\ldots
, so
\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}(m))=\pi^{*}(c)+\pi^{*}(c^{\prime})z
.

The relative tangent bundle of
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}
has Todd class

\mathrm{td}(T_{\pi})=1+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(T_{\pi})=1+z.

Moreover,
\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}(m))=\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})\mathrm{ch}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(m))=\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E})(1+mz)
.
On
\mathcal{U}_{m}
, we have
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)=0
so Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch tells us

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m))

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}(\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}(m))\mathrm{td}(T_{\pi}))

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}((\pi^{*}(c)+\pi^{*}(c^{\prime})z)(1+(m+1)z))=c^{\prime}+(m+1)c.

To recover the Chern classes, we evaluate

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j-1)!(-1)^{j+1}\mathrm{ch}_{j}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m))t^{j}\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(j-1)!(-1)^{j+1}(c_{j+1}^{\prime}+(m+1)c_{j})t^{j}\right)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j(j+1)}\frac{\partial p_{j+1}}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})t^{j}\right)(1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r})^{m+1}.

To evaluate the infinite sums inside (
5.10
), we consider

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j(j+1)}\frac{\partial p_{j+1}}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})t^{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j+1}\frac{\partial p_{j+1}}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})t^{j-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})t^{j-2}.

Note that
\partial p_{j}/\partial x_{i}=0
if
j<i
. Therefore taking the partial derivative of (
5.8
) with respect to
x_{i}
, we see that the above is equal to

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{-1}{t^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\log(1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r})-\delta_{i1}t\right)

(5.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\begin{cases}(a_{1}+a_{2}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r-1})/(1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r})&\text{if $i=1$}\\
-t^{i-2}/(1+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{r}t^{r})&\text{if $i\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

Substituting the formal integral of the terms in (
5.11
) into (
5.10
) and exponentiating (
5.10
) then gives the formula (
5.7
).
∎

The Chern classes of

Corollary 5.5
.

The integral Chow rings
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
and
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}^{\dagger})
are not finitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}
algebras.

Proof.

Since
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell})
surjects onto
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}^{\dagger})
, it suffices to prove the claim for
\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}^{\dagger}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}^{\dagger})
were finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra. Then there would exist a finite set of primes
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
such that for all
\eta\in A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{r,\ell}^{\dagger})
, there exist

such that

. Let
q
be a prime not in
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
. Let us consider the coefficient

5.6
). Performing the formal integral, we see that

Corollary 5.6
.

(1) The integral Chow rings

and

are not isomorphic. Hence,
\mathcal{B}_{2,0}^{\dagger}
is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{2,1}^{\dagger}
.

(2) The integral Chow rings
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{2,1})
and
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{2,0})

\mathcal{B}_{2,0}
is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{2,1}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
\sum f_{i}t^{i}=F(t)
as in (
5.6
).
Then
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}_{2,\ell}^{\dagger})\subset\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime}]
is the subring generated by the classes
f_{i}
together with
a_{1},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime}
. We must show that we obtain non-isomorphic rings (not just different subrings) when
\ell=a_{1}^{\prime}=0
versus when
\ell=a_{1}^{\prime}=1

Setting
\ell=a_{1}^{\prime}=0
, we see

is generated by

\displaystyle f_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime 2}),\quad a_{1}^{2},\quad a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime},\quad a_{2}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle(\ell=0).

On the other hand, setting
\ell=a_{1}^{\prime}=1

is generated by

\displaystyle f_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(-a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime 2}+a_{2}),\quad a_{1}^{2},\quad a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime},\quad a_{2}\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle(\ell=1).

Now already, we can see that we have produced different subrings of
\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime}]
. To see that our rings are not isomorphic though, we look at classes in codimension
4
.

Let us consider the group

1.2

In particular,

\displaystyle f_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24}(-2a_{1}^{3}a_{2}^{\prime}-a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{\prime 2}+2a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime 3}+a_{2}^{\prime 4}+2a_{1}^{2}a_{2}+6a_{1}a_{2}a_{2}^{\prime}-2a_{2}a_{2}^{\prime 2}-3a_{2}^{2})\in A^{4}(\mathcal{B}_{2,1}^{\dagger})

1

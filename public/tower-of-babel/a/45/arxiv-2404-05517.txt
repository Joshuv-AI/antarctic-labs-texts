The L p L^{p} estimate for the gain term of the Boltzmann collision operator and its application
Ling-Bing He, Jin-Cheng Jiang, Hung-Wen Kuo, Meng-Hao Liang

Abstract
. We prove the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev type L p L^{p} estimates for the gain term of the Boltzmann collision operator including Maxwellian molecule, hard potential and hard sphere models. Combining with the results of Alonso et al. [ 2 ] for the soft potential and Maxwellian molecule models, we provide an unified form of L p L^{p} estimates for all cutoff models which are sharp in the sense of scaling. The most striking feature of our new estimates for the hard potential and hard sphere models is that they do not increase the moment, the same as Maxwellian molecule and soft potential models. Based on these novelties, we prove the global existence and scattering of the non-negative unique mild solution for the Cauchy problem of the Boltzmann equation when the positive initial data is small in the weighted L x , v 3 L^{3}_{x,v} space.



1.
Introduction and Main results

We consider the Boltzmann equation,

{\partial_{t}f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=Q(f,f),

in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

where the collision operator
Q(f,f)
is given by

Q(f,f)(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}(f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}-ff_{*})B(v-v_{*},\omega)\;d\omega dv_{*},

and
d\omega
is the solid element in the direction of unit vector
\omega
in the upper hemisphere
S^{2}_{+}
.
We use the abbreviations
f^{\prime}=f(x,v^{\prime},t)\;,\;f^{\prime}_{*}=f(x,v^{\prime}_{*},t)\;,\;f_{*}=f(x,v_{*},t)
,
and the relation between the pre-collisional velocities of particles and
after collision is given by

v^{\prime}=v-[\omega\cdot(v-v_{*})]\omega\;,\;v^{\prime}_{*}=v_{*}+[\omega\cdot(v-v_{*})]\omega\;,\;\omega\in S^{2}_{+}.

In this paper, we consider the cutoff models, including Maxwellian molecule, hard potential
and hard sphere models.
Namely, the collision kernel
B
being the product of kinetic part and angular part,

(1.1)

B(v-v_{*},\omega)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;b(\cos\theta)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;\cos\theta,

where

0\leq\gamma\leq 1\;,\;\cos\theta=\langle\omega,(v-v_{*})/|v-v_{*}|\rangle\;,0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2.

The angular function
b(\cos\theta)=\cos\theta
satisfying the Grad’s cutoff assumption

(1.2)

\int_{S^{2}_{+}}b(\cos\theta)d\omega<\infty

is given in the explicit form for the simplicity of representation and the result of this work still holds if
it is replaced by smooth function with same decay when
\theta
towards
0
and
\pi/2
.
The cases
-3<\gamma<0,\;\gamma=0,\;0<\gamma<1
and
\gamma=1
are called the sot potential,
Maxwell molecules, hard potential and hard sphere model respectively. When the cutoff condition (
1.2
)
is satisfied, the collision operator
Q
can be split into the gain term
Q^{+}
and the loss term
Q^{-}
.
It is useful to introduce the bilinear gain and loss terms

(1.3)

\begin{split}Q^{+}(f,g)(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{S^{2}_{+}}f(v^{\prime})g(v_{*}^{\prime})B(v-v_{*},\omega)d\omega dv_{*},\\
Q^{-}(f,g)(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{S^{2}_{+}}f(v)g(v_{*})B(v-v_{*},\omega)d\omega dv_{*}.\end{split}

In order to study the Boltzmann equation, it is essential to understand the properties of the collision operator
and which is clearly non-trivial due to the complex structure of the operator.
In particular, the estimate for the gain term is the core part of the task.
Among all, the regularization property of the gain term found first by
Lions
[
31
]
was used to prove the existence of renormalized solutions.
In
[
31
]
, Lions assumed that both the relative velocity and angular function are compact
supported and the support of the later is away from
0
and
\pi/2
. It was extended to the full
collision kernel by several authors
[
25
,
26
,
32
,
33
,
39
]
in different forms for various purposes.
For example the sharp regularizing estimates, for
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
,
given in
[
26
]
take the form:

(1.4)

\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}},\;\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{{H}^{\gamma}}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}},

where
\dot{H}^{\gamma}
and
H^{\gamma}
are the homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev space
respectively,
1/{\mathfrak{p}}+1/{\mathfrak{q}}=3/2,\;1\leq\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\leq 2
and
\langle v\rangle=(1+|v|^{2})^{1/2}

is the Japanese bracket (see also the notations in the end of this section).

Secondly, it is also useful to regard the gain term as a convolution-like operator. The Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev
type estimates was first given by Gustsfsson
[
18
]
, then by several authors
[
15
,
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
The recent result of Alonso et al.
[
2
]
for the soft potential and Maxwellain models,
i.e.
-3<\gamma\leq 0
, reads
1<\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}<\infty
and

(1.5)

\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}},\;\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=1+\frac{\gamma}{3}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}.

This estimate is employed by the first and second authors in the recent work
[
21
,
22
]
where they solve
the small initial data Cauchy problem for the 3 dimensional Boltzmann equation for the cases

-1\leq\gamma<0
. Because the estimate (
1.5
) does not increase the moment and
thus gives the desirable nonlinear estimate,

(1.6)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}Q^{+}(f_{+},f_{+})\|_{L^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{+}\|^{2}_{L^{p}},

for suitable
(\tilde{p}^{\prime},p)
which fits the requirement of the Strichatz estimate (see also
Section
6
for the details).
The above estimate allows us to close the iteration by the fixed point argument.

On the other hand, for the hard potential and hard sphere models
i.e.,
0<\gamma\leq 1
, the estimate of Alonso et al.
[
2
]
reads

(1.7)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell+\gamma}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell+\gamma}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}},

where

(1.8)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}},\;\ell\in\mathbb{R},\;1\leq\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}\leq\infty.

There is an increase of moment of order
\gamma
in the estimate (
1.7
) comparing to the
estimate (
1.5
) which makes the approach in
[
21
,
22
]
being not practicable.

However the first estimate of (
1.4
) and Sobolev embedding implies that for

1\leq\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\leq 2
,

\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}},\;{\rm if}\;\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=\frac{3}{2}=1+\frac{\gamma}{3}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}.

This means (
1.5
) holds for hard potential and hard sphere models at least for
some of triplets
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r})
. It is natural to ask if it is necessary to increase the moment when we consider
the
L^{p}
estimate for the gain term with hard potential and hard sphere models.

The first part of this work provides an unexpected observation to the gain term for the hard potential and
hard sphere models for this question. We prove the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev type
L^{p}
estimates
for the gain term of the Boltzmann collision operator including Maxwellian molecule, hard potential and
hard sphere models.
Combining with the results of Alonso et al.
[
2
]
for the soft potential and Maxwellian molecule
models, we provide an unified form of
L^{p}
estimates for all cutoff models which
are sharp in the sense of scaling. The most striking feature of our new estimates for the hard potential
and hard sphere models is that they do not increase the moment, the same as Maxwellian molecule
and soft potential models.
Therefore the damping effect inside the collision operator for the hard potential
and hard sphere models comes from the loss term only,
at least in the suitable
L^{p}
space. This might be a distinguishing feature of hard potential
and hard sphere models comparing to the Maxwellian molecule
and soft potential models.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell_{0}\geq 0
,
1<\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}<\infty
,
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and

(1.9)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}+\frac{\gamma}{3},\;\;\frac{\gamma}{6}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}\leq 1-\frac{5\gamma}{6},

and both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0
.
Consider

B(v-v_{*},\omega)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;\cos\theta.

Then the bilinear operator
Q^{+}(f,g)
satisfies

(1.10)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\;\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

If
\ell_{1}>3/m
and
1<\mathfrak{p}_{m},\mathfrak{q}_{m},m,\mathfrak{r}_{m}<\infty
satisfy

(1.11)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}<1\;,\;\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}<1,

both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0
, and

(1.12)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}+\frac{\gamma}{3},\;\;\frac{\gamma}{6}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}\leq 1-\frac{5\gamma}{6},

then we have

(1.13)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\;\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Our second result is the application of the above result which concerns the global well-posedness of the
Cauchy problem for the cutoff Boltzmann equation with small initial data:

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\partial_{t}f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=Q(f,f),\\
&f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v).\end{aligned}\right.

The first result in this aspect is due to Illner and Shinbrot
[
24
]
who showed the global existence of solutions for
the Cauchy problem (
1.14
) for several cutoff models when the initial data has exponential decay
in spatial variable and has suitable weight in velocity variable. The Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration method
in
[
24
]
comes from the earlier work
[
28
]
. Small initial data Cauchy problem
for different cut-off models was extensively studied during that decade using the same iteration or fixed
point argument, see
[
8
,
16
]
and reference therein for more details. We remark that the assumption
that the initial data has exponential decay in spatial variable or in velocity variable is necessary to get these
results.

The Strichartz estimates for the kinetic equation in the note of Castella and Perthame
[
13
]
provide
a possible tool to relax the harsh assumption, exponential decay, posed on the initial data.
Assumed the kinetic part of the collision kernel is
L^{p}
integrable for some
p
depending on
dimension, Arsénio
[
4
]
was able to use Strichartz estimates to prove the existence of
global weak (non-unique) solution
when the initial data is small in
L^{D}_{x,v}
where
D
is the dimension. For the authentic
cutoff kernel with
-1\leq\gamma<0
, dimension 3 and initial data is small in weighted
L^{3}
space, the global well-posed
problem is solved by the first and second authors
[
21
,
22
]
by combining Strichartz
estimates
[
13
,
38
]
and Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration
[
24
,
28
]
. As we mentioned before the
most important ingredient of all is the estimate (
1.6
).
Note that the result for the two dimensional case with
\gamma=0
is obtained by Chen, Denlinger and
Pavlović
[
9
]
using a different approach.
With Theorem
1.1
in hand, we are able
to deal with the small initial data Cauchy problem of the Boltzmann equation for the Maxwellian molecule,
hard potential and hard sphere models following the approach of
[
22
]
.

To state the result, let us introduce the mixed Lebesgue norm

\|f(t,x,v)\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}L^{p}_{v}}

where the notation
L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}L^{p}_{v}
stands for the space
L^{q}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})))
. It is understood that we use

L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R})=L^{q}_{t}([0,\infty))
for the well-posedness problem which can
be done by imposing support restriction to the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates.
We use
L^{a}_{x,v}
to denote
L_{x}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{3};L_{v}^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Please note that
the notation
L^{p}_{v}
is used to indicate the variable of integration is
v
which appears only
when we use mixed norm. This should not be confused with weighted
L^{p}
norm used in many
papers.

We also define the scattering of the solution with respect to kinetic transport operator as the following.
We say that a global solution
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{a}_{x,v})
scatters in
L^{a}_{x,v}
as

t\rightarrow\infty
if there exits
f_{\infty}\in L^{a}_{x,v}
such that

(1.15)

\|f(t)-U(t)f_{\infty}\|_{L^{a}_{x,v}}\rightarrow 0

where
U(t)f(x,v)=f(x-vt,v)
is the solution map of the kinetic transport equation

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=0.

See also
[
6
]
and reference therein for the discussion about the scattering of the solution and
its relation with famous H-theorem. With these terminologies, we state the second result
as the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and

B(v-v_{*},\omega)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;\cos\theta.

Let
\ell>2\gamma+\frac{10}{9}
and
\varepsilon
satisfy

\max{\big\{}0,\frac{5\gamma}{12}-\frac{7}{18}\}<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{9}-\frac{\gamma}{12}.

There exists a small number
\eta>0
such that if the initial data

f_{0}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{\ell}_{\eta}=\{f_{0}|f_{0}\geq 0,\;\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{0}\|_{L^{3}_{x,v}}<\eta,\;\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{0}\|_{L^{15/8}_{x,v}}<\infty\}\subset L^{3}_{x,v},

then the Cauchy problem (
1.14
) admits a unique and non-negative mild solution

\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\in C([0,\infty),L^{3}_{x,v})\cap L^{\mathbbm{q}}([0,\infty],L^{\mathbbm{r}}_{x}L^{\mathbbm{p}}_{v}),

where the triple

(1.16)

(\frac{1}{\mathbbm{q}},\frac{1}{\mathbbm{r}},\frac{1}{\mathbbm{p}})=(\frac{1}{3}-3\varepsilon,\;\frac{2}{9}+\varepsilon,\;\frac{4}{9}-\varepsilon).

The solution map
\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{0}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{\ell}_{\eta}\rightarrow\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\in L^{\mathbbm{q}}_{t}L^{\mathbbm{r}}_{x}L^{\mathbbm{p}}_{v}

is Lipschitz continuous and the solution
\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f
scatters with respect to the kinetic
transport operator in
L^{3}_{x,v}
.

Notations

We set Japanese bracket
\langle v\rangle=(1+|v|^{2})^{1/2},\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
(a\cdot b)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}b_{i}

the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)g(x)dx
the inner product in

L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{3}}).

The differential operator

D^{s},s\in\mathbb{R}
is expressed through the Fourier transform:

D^{s}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

where the Fourier transform

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx.

The fractional homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces are denoted by

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}=\||\xi|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}\;,\;\|f\|_{{H}^{\alpha}}=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}

We use the multi-indices notation
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}
.
By
\partial_{x}
or by
\nabla_{x}
, we will denote the gradient.
A function
p(x,\xi)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

(1.17)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}p(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|}

for any multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
is called a symbol of order
m
. The class of such function is denoted by
S^{m}_{1,0}
.
We will see that the symbols
p(x,\xi)
in this paper always enjoy the better decaying condition, i.e.,

(1.18)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}p(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}-|\alpha|}\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}-|\beta|}

for any multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
and which is called a
SG
symbol of order
(m_{1},m_{2})
, used by
Cordes
[
11
]
and Coriasco
[
12
]
.
We use
SG^{m_{1},m_{2}}
to denote the set of such symbols. For each
p(x,\xi)\in S_{1,0}^{l}
, the associate operators

P(x,D)h(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}p(x,\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi

is called a pseudodifferential operator of order
l
. The standard notation
S^{-\infty}_{1,0}=\cap_{\;l}S^{l}_{1,0},l\in\mathbb{Z}

is also used. If
p(x,\xi)\in S^{-\infty}_{1,0}
, it is called a symbol of the smooth operator.
The operator

\mathscr{T}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\psi(x,\xi)}p(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

with symbol
p(x,\xi)\in S^{l}_{1,0}
and the phase function
\psi(x,\xi)
satisfies
non-degeneracy condition is called a Fourier integral operator of order
l
.
We say a phase function
\psi(x,\xi)

satisfies the non-degeneracy condition if there is a constant
c>0
such that

(1.19)

|\det{\Big[}\partial_{x}\partial_{\xi}\psi(x,\xi){\Big]}|=|\det{\Big[}\partial^{2}\psi(x,\xi)/\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{\xi_{j}}{\Big]}|\geq c>0

for all
(x,\xi)\in{\rm supp}\;p(x,\xi)
.

2.
L^{p}
estimate for the gain term

To study the gain term of the Boltzmann collision operator, we recall the Radon transform
introduced by Lions
[
31
]
:

(2.1)

\mathbb{T}h(x)=|x|^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}b(\cos\theta)h(x-(x\cdot\omega)\omega)d\omega,\;0\leq\gamma\leq 1

where

\cos\theta={(x\cdot\omega)}/{|x|},x\neq 0
,
x=|x|(0,0,1)
and

\omega=(\cos\varphi\sin\theta,\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
,
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
.
In our case, we have

b(\cos\theta)=\cos\theta.

Lions
[
31
]
assumed that
|x|^{\gamma}
and
\theta
both vary inside compact sets
and the support of
\theta
is away from
0
and
\pi/2
. Such a restriction has been removed in the second author’s
work
[
25
,
26
]
in the proof of the
L^{2}
based regularizing estimates.

By the inverse Fourier transform, we may write

(2.2)

\begin{split}\mathbb{T}h(x)&=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i{x\cdot\xi}}{A}(x,\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi\end{split}

where

{A}(x,\xi)=|x|^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}e^{-i(x\cdot\omega)(\xi\cdot\omega)}b(\cos\theta)d\omega.

To explain the structure of (
2.2
), we review some fact about the Fourier integral
operators (FIOs).
The Fourier integral operators which we consider have the form:

(2.3)

\mathscr{T}h(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\psi(x,\xi)}p(x,\xi)\;\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi

where
p(x,\xi)
is a SG-symbol of order
(\alpha,\beta)
defined in (
1.18
)
and the phase function
\psi
satisfies the non-degeneracy condition, i.e., (
1.19
)
for all
(x,\xi)\in{\rm supp}\;p(x,\xi)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and the pair of orders
(m_{1},m_{2})
satisfy

(2.4)

m_{1}\leq-2\;{\Big|}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}{\Big|},\;\;m_{2}\leq-2\;{\Big|}\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}{\Big|},\;\;1<p<\infty.

When the symbol
p(x,\xi)
has the compact support as a function of
x
, thus the first inequality
of (
2.4
) is automatically satisfied, it is called a local FIO.
The local FIO whose symbol satisfies (
2.4
) enjoys

\|\mathscr{T}h\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{p}}

is proved by Seeger, Sogge and Stein
[
34
]
, see also Stein’s book
[
35
]
.
An FIO whose symbol is not compact supported in
x
variable is called a
global FIO. The fact that the SG-symbol global FIO whose symbol satisfies (
2.4
)
enjoys the same estimate as the local one is proved by Coriasco and Ruzhansky
[
14
]
,
see also the references therein for more details.

It has been showed in
[
26
]
that the behavior of the operator
\mathbb{T}
defined
by (
2.1
) depends on its position in the phase space
(x,\xi)

as well as the angle spanned by
x
and
\xi
. By splitting
the phase space into cones, we can write
\mathbb{T}
as a sum of global FIOs
and degenerate operators where each FIO is defined on one cone whose size is related to the angle spanned by

x
and
\xi
.
The SG-symbols of FIOs all have (we should call it a symbol later on) the order

(2.5)

m_{1}=\gamma-1,\;m_{2}=-1,

while the phase functions has no uniform non-degeneracy condition, i.e.,
the constant
c
in (
1.19
) may close to
0
arbitrary and which
is reason for splitting the phase space into cones. The degenerate operators need different
analysis with the same geometric setup. With such geometric consideration, the
L^{2}
-type
estimate was build in
[
26
]
as good as a single global FIO.

It is natural to expect that

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
1<\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}<\infty
,
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and

(2.9)

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}+\frac{\gamma}{3},\;\;\frac{\gamma}{6}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}\leq 1-\frac{5\gamma}{6},

and both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0
.
Consider

B(v-v_{*},\omega)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;\cos\theta.

Then the bilinear operator
Q^{+}(f,g)
is a bounded operator from
L^{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{\mathfrak{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
via the estimate

(2.10)

\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Proof.

By the duality, it suffices to show that

{\big|}\langle Q^{+}(f,g),h\rangle{\big|}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}}\|h\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}}

holds for any
h\in L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}
where
\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
{\mathfrak{r}}
.
Using change of variables and Hölder inequality

\begin{split}&{\big|}\langle Q^{+}(f,g),h\rangle{\big|}\\
&={\big|}\iiint f(v)g(v_{*})B(v-v_{*},\omega)h(v^{\prime})d\omega dv_{*}dv{\big|}\\
&\leq\|f(v)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}}\|Q^{+t}_{g}(h)(v)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}Q^{+,t}_{g}(h)(v)&=\iint g(v_{*})B(v-v_{*},\omega)h(v^{\prime})d\omega dv_{*}\\
&=\int g(v_{*})(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v)dv_{*}.\end{split}

where we used the the notations as Lemma
2.1
in the last line.
Thus it is reduced to proving that

(2.11)

\|Q^{+,t}_{g}(h)(v)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}}\|h\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}}.

where

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\;,\;\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}}\overset{\rm def}{=}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}+\frac{\gamma}{3}.

Define
H(v,v_{*})=(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v)
. By

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}=1,

and Hölder inequality, we have

\begin{split}&{\big|}Q^{+t}_{g}(h)(v){\big|}\leq\int{\big|}H(v,v_{*}){\big|}{\big|}g(v_{*}){\big|}dv_{*}\\
&=\int{\Big(}{\big|}H(v,v_{*}){\big|}^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}{\big|}g(v_{*}){\big|}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\Big)}^{1/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\big|}H(v,v_{*}){\big|}^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\big|}g(v_{*}){\big|}^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}dv_{*}\\
&\leq\|(|H(v,v_{*})|^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}|g(v_{*})|^{\mathfrak{p}})^{1/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}(v_{*})}\times\\
&{\hskip 56.9055pt}\||H(v,v_{*})|^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\frac{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}}(v_{*})}\||g(v_{*})|^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\frac{\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p}}}(v_{*})}.\end{split}

Here

(2.12)

\||g(v_{*})|^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\frac{\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p}}}(v_{*})}=(\|g(v_{*})\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}(v_{*})})^{\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}},

(2.13)

\||H(v,v_{*})|^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime})/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\frac{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}}(v_{*})}=(\|(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ T\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v)\|_{L^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}(v_{*})})^{\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}},

and

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\|(|H(v,v_{*})|^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}|g(v_{*})|^{\mathfrak{p}})^{1/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}(v_{*})}\\
&=(\int{\big|}g(v_{*}){\big|}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\big|}(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v){\big|}^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}dv_{*})^{\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Applying (
2.8
) of Lemma
2.1
by plugging

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}-\frac{\gamma}{3}.

We see the inequality in (
2.9
) comes from (
2.6
) and that if

\mathfrak{r}
satisfies (
2.9
) then

(2.15)

~\eqref{E:H-v-v_*-1}\leq C(\|h\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}})^{\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}}

where
C
is independent of
v
.
Combine (
2.12
), (
2.15
) and
(
2.14
), we have

(2.16)

\begin{split}&\|Q^{+,t}_{g}(h)(v)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}(v)}^{\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}}\\
&\leq C(\|g(v_{*})\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}(v_{*})})^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p})}(\|h\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}})^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime})}\times\\
&{\hskip 56.9055pt}\int\int{\big|}g(v_{*}){\big|}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\big|}(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ T\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v){\big|}^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}dv_{*}dv\\
&\leq C(\|g(v_{*})\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}(v_{*})})^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{p})}(\|h\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}})^{(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime})}\times\\
&{\hskip 56.9055pt}\int|g(v_{*})|^{\mathfrak{p}}\sup\limits_{v_{*}}\|(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ T\circ\tau_{v_{*}})h(v)\|_{L^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}_{v}}^{\widetilde{\mathfrak{r}}^{\prime}}dv_{*}.\\
\end{split}

Then we conclude (
2.11

) of
Lemma
2.1
to the last line of (

satisfies (

The proof of (

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell_{0}\geq 0
,
1<\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{r}<\infty
,
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
and

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}+\frac{\gamma}{3},\;\frac{\gamma}{6}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}}\leq 1-\frac{5\gamma}{6},

and both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0

Consider

B(v-v_{*},\omega)=|v-v_{*}|^{\gamma}\;\cos\theta.

Then the bilinear operator
Q^{+}(f,g)
satisfies

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

If
\ell_{1}>3/m
and

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}<1\;,\;\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}<1,

\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}+\frac{1}{m}=1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}+\frac{\gamma}{3},\;\frac{\gamma}{6}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}\leq 1-\frac{5\gamma}{6},

and both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0
, then we have

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(\mathfrak{p}_{m},\ell)\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{1}}g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Proof.

Recall
\langle v\rangle=(1+|v|^{2})^{1/2}
, we consider the quantity

(2.17)

\langle\langle v\rangle^{\ell}Q^{+}(f,g),\langle v\rangle^{-\ell}\psi\rangle,\;\ell\geq 0.

From the conservation of energy, either
\langle v^{\prime}\rangle\leq 2\langle v\rangle
or
\langle v^{\prime}\rangle\leq 2\langle v_{*}\rangle
has to be true. Hence
for any
\ell_{0}\geq 0
we have either

(2.18)

\langle v\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}\leq C\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}\;{\rm or}\;\langle v_{*}\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}\leq C\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}.

Recall
H(v,v_{*}):=(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ T\circ\tau_{v_{*}})\psi(v)
and let

\psi_{-\ell_{0}}(v)=\langle v\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}\psi(v)

From (
2.18
), we know that one of the following estimates is true, i.e.,

(2.19)

\begin{split}&{\big|}H(v,v_{*}){\big|}\leq\int_{S^{2}}{\big|}\psi(v^{\prime}){\big|}B(v-v_{*},\omega)d\omega\\

&=C\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}}){\big|}\psi_{-\ell_{0}}{\big|}(v),\end{split}

or

(2.20)

\begin{split}&{\big|}H(v,v_{*}){\big|}\leq\int_{S^{2}}{\big|}\psi(v^{\prime}){\big|}B(v-v_{*},\omega)d\omega\\
&\leq C\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\ell_{0}}\int_{S^{2}}{\big|}\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{-\ell_{0}}\psi(v^{\prime}){\big|}B(v-v_{*},\omega)d\omega\\
&=C\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\ell_{0}}(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}}){\big|}\psi_{-\ell_{0}}{\big|}(v).\end{split}

Denote
f_{\ell_{0}}(v)=\langle v\rangle^{\ell_{0}}f(v)
and
g_{\ell_{0}}(v_{*})=\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\ell_{0}}g(v_{*})
.
Combining (
2.19

2.20

\begin{split}&{\Big|}\int Q^{+}(f,g)(v)\;\psi(v)dv{\Big|}\\
&\leq C\iint{\Big\{}{\big|}f_{\ell_{0}}(v)g(v_{*}){\big|}+{\big|}f(v)g_{\ell_{0}}(v_{*}){\big|}{\Big\}}(\tau_{-v_{*}}\circ\mathbb{T}\circ\tau_{v_{*}}){\big|}\psi_{-\ell_{0}}{\big|}(v)dv_{*}dv.\end{split}

Following the proofs of the Theorem
2.2
, we have

(2.21)

\begin{split}&{\Big|}\int Q^{+}(f,g)(v)\;\psi(v)dv{\Big|}\\
&\leq C{\big(}\|f_{\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\big)}\|\psi_{-\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq C\|f_{\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\psi_{-\ell_{0}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

thus we conclude (
1.10
) by duality and (
2.17
) . The relation (
1.9
)
follows from (
2.9
).

The proof of estimate (
1.10
) is an easy consequence of above argument which can be done
by revising (

1/a_{1}=1/\mathfrak{p}_{m}+1/m<1
and

\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}=1+\frac{1}{\tau_{m}}+\frac{\gamma}{3}\;\;\;{\rm or}\;\;\;\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}=1+\frac{1}{\tau_{m}}+\frac{\gamma}{3}

1.9

2.21

\begin{split}&{\Big|}\int Q^{+}(f,g)(v)\;\psi(v)dv{\Big|}\\
&\leq C{\big(}\|f_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g\|_{L^{a_{2}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|f\|_{L^{a_{1}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\big)}\|\psi_{-\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq C{\big(}\|f_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{m}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\|f\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{-\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{m}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\big)}\|\psi_{-\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq C\|f_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{p}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|g_{\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{q}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\psi_{-\ell_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}_{m}}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

where we used the condition

3.
Decomposition of
\mathbb{T}

In this section, we reduce the boundedness of
\mathbb{T}:L^{\widetilde{p}}\rightarrow L^{p}

to
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of two operators
T_{A}
and
T_{B}
which will be
proved in Section 4 and Section 5 respectively.

Recall that

(3.1)

\mathbb{T}h(x)=|x|^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}b(\cos\theta)h(x-(x\cdot\omega)\omega)d\omega,\;0\leq\gamma\leq 1

where

\cos\theta={(x\cdot\omega)}/{|x|},x\neq 0
,
x=|x|(0,0,1)
and

\omega=(\cos\varphi\sin\theta,\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
,
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
.
The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the operator defined by (
3.1
). Suppose
p
and
\widetilde{p}
satisfy

\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq 1-\frac{\gamma}{2},\;\frac{1}{\widetilde{p}}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\gamma}{3},

and both
\leq
are replaced by
<
when
\gamma=0
,
then we have

(3.2)

\|\mathbb{T}h\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}}.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we first reduce it to a
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
estimate.
Recall that, by inverse Fourier transform, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{T}h(x)&=|x|^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}b(\cos\theta)h(x-(x\cdot\omega)\omega)d\omega\\
&=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i{x\cdot\xi}}{A}(x,\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi\end{split}

where

(3.3)

{A}(x,\xi)=|x|^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}e^{-i(x\cdot\omega)(\xi\cdot\omega)}b(\cos\theta)d\omega.

We define

(3.4)

a(x,\xi)=A(x,\xi)|\xi|^{\gamma}=(|x||\xi|)^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}e^{-i(x\cdot\omega)(\xi\cdot\omega)}b(\cos\theta)d\omega,

and define

(3.5)

Th(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i{x\cdot\xi}}{a}(x,\xi)\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi.

Then we can rewrite

(3.6)

\mathbb{T}h(x)=T\circ(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}h(x)

where
(-\Delta)^{-s/2}:=\mathcal{I}_{s}
with
0<{\rm Re}\;s<\infty
is the Riesz potential operator
of order

. We recall the Sobolev embedding theorem concerning
\mathcal{I}_{s}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem 1.2.3 of
[
17
]
)
.

Let
s
be a real number, with
0<s<n
, and let
1<p<q<\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{s}{d}.

Then there exist constants
C(d,s,p),C(s,d)<\infty
such that for all
f
belong to Schwarz space

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\|\mathcal{I}_{s}(f)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C(d,s,p)\|f\|_{L^{p}},

and

\|\mathcal{I}_{s}(f)\|_{L^{\frac{d}{d-s},\infty}}\leq C(d,s,p)\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

Consequently
\mathcal{I}_{s}
has a unique extension on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all

p
with
1\leq p<\infty
such that the preceding estimates are valid.

By Theorem
3.2
and (
3.6
), the Theorem
3.1
is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be the operator defined by (
3.5
) and (
3.4
). Suppose

satisfies

(3.7)

\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq 1-\frac{\gamma}{2},

\leq
are replaced by

(3.8)

\|Th\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{p}}.

Proof.

Recall that

Th(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i{x\cdot\xi}}\;{a}(x,\xi)\;\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi,

where

{a}(x,\xi)=(|x||\xi|)^{\gamma}\int_{\omega\in S^{2}_{+}}e^{-i(x\cdot\omega)(\xi\cdot\omega)}b(\cos\theta)d\omega.

As in
[
26
]
, we can give the function

a concrete description by decomposing
the phase space
(x,\xi)
.

First of all, we need a dyadic decomposition in the interval
(0,\pi)
which is constructed below.
Let
\zeta(\theta)\in C^{\infty}
be supported in the interval
(\pi/8,\pi/2)
and satisfy
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta(2^{-z}\theta)=1

for all
\theta>0
. Let
\zeta_{n}(\theta)=\zeta(2^{n}\theta)

n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\widetilde{\zeta}(\theta)=1-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\zeta_{n}(\theta)
. Define
\zeta_{0}(\theta)
equals
\widetilde{\zeta}(\theta)
, if

and
\zeta_{0}(\theta)=\zeta_{0}(\pi-\theta)
,
if
\pi/2<\theta<\pi
. This extension of
\zeta_{0}
from
0<\theta\leq\pi/2

to
\pi/2<\theta<\pi
by reflection keeps
\zeta_{0}
a smooth function since
\widetilde{\zeta}
equals
1
near
\pi/2
.
We also define
\zeta_{-n}(\theta)=\zeta_{n}(\pi-\theta)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}

1=\zeta_{0}(\theta)+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\zeta_{n}(\theta)+\zeta_{-n}(\theta))
in the interval
\theta\in(0,\pi)

Abuse the notations, we define

(3.9)

\zeta_{0}(x,\xi)=\zeta_{0}(\arccos(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{|x||\xi|}))\;,\;\zeta_{\pm n}(x,\xi)=\zeta_{\pm n}(\arccos(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{|x||\xi|}))\;,\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus the supports of
\zeta_{0}(x,\xi),\zeta_{n}(x,\xi),\zeta_{-n}(x,\xi)
lie respectively
in the cones

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\Lambda_{0}={\Big\{}(x,\xi)|\;\frac{\pi}{8}<\arccos(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{|x||\xi|})<{\pi}-\frac{\pi}{8}{\Big\}},\\
&\Lambda_{n}={\Big\{}(x,\xi)|\;\frac{\pi}{2^{n+3}}<\arccos(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{|x||\xi|})<\frac{\pi}{2^{n+1}}{\Big\}}\\
&\Lambda_{-n}={\Big\{}(x,\xi)|\;\pi(1-\frac{1}{2^{n+1}})<\arccos(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{|x||\xi|})<\pi(1-\frac{1}{2^{n+3}}){\Big\}}.\end{split}

Define

(3.11)

a_{z}(x,\xi)=\zeta_{z}(x,\xi)a(x,\xi),\;z\in\mathbb{Z}

and write
a(x,\xi)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{z}(x,\xi)
.
Then we have
T=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{z}
where

(3.12)

T_{z}h(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i{x\cdot\xi}}a_{z}(x,\xi)\;\widehat{h}(\xi)d\xi,\;z\in\mathbb{Z}.

Each

is in fact the sum of two FIOs and a degenerate operator which means that we need a

.

First we introduce a dyadic partition of unity on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
We let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\;0\leq\rho\leq 1
be supported in
the open interval
(4,16)\subset\mathbb{R}

\begin{split}\int|K_{z\pm,j}^{\nu}(x,y)|dx&\leq c_{z\pm}2^{j}\int(1+|2^{j}(x-y)_{1}|+|2^{j/2}(x-y)^{\prime}|)^{-2N}dx\\
&\leq c_{z\pm}2^{-j}\end{split}

if we choose

. Here we abuse the notations

, since for the same reason mentioned early
for (

satisfy the relation

(4.60)

c_{(z+1)\pm}\leq 2\cdot c_{z\pm}\;\;{\rm if\;}z\geq 0\;{\rm and}\;\;c_{(z-1)\pm}\leq 2\cdot c_{z\pm}\;\;{\rm if\;}z<0.

Recalling supp
p_{z\pm}(x,\xi)\subset\Lambda_{z}
,
{\rm card}_{z}\leq c2^{j}
and (

),
we conclude that

(4.61)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{z\pm,j}(x,y)|dx\leq C_{z\pm},\;{\rm all\;}y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

From (
4.37
) and (
4.60
), we have

(4.62)

C_{(z+1)\pm}\leq(\frac{1}{2})\cdot C_{z\pm}\;{\rm if}\;z\geq 0\;{\rm and}\;C_{(z-1)\pm}\leq(\frac{1}{2})\cdot C_{z\pm}\;{\rm if}\;z\leq 0.

Another two estimates about
K_{z\pm,j}
which are the following.

(4.63)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{z\pm,j}(x,y)-K_{z\pm,j}(x,y^{\prime})|dx\leq C_{z\pm}\cdot|y-y^{\prime}|\cdot 2^{j},\;{\rm all\;}y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(4.64)

\int_{\prescript{c}{}{D}_{z\pm}^{*}}|K_{z\pm,j}(x,y)|dx\leq\frac{C_{z\pm}\cdot 2^{-j}}{\delta},\;{\rm if\;}y\in B,{\rm and}\;2^{-j}\leq\delta,

is independent of
j,y,y^{\prime}
and
\delta

)
They are proved by the similar argument, we omit the details as one can find them in
[

]
.
We abuse the notation
C_{z\pm}
in above two estimates, as the relation (

) holds for the same reason.

Now we are ready to estimate the series of the integrals in (

). Recall (
4.55
), we write

(4.65)

\rho_{1}\cdot T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}\;u(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\big(}T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}{\big)}_{j}=\sum_{2^{j}\leq\delta^{-1}}+\sum_{2^{j}>\delta^{-1}}.

\sum_{2^{j}>\delta^{-1}}\int_{\prescript{c}{}{D}_{z\pm}^{*}}|{\big(}T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}{\big)}_{j}u(x)|dx\leq C_{z\pm}{\Big(}\int|u(y)|dy{\Big)}\cdot{\Big(}\sum_{2^{j}\leq\delta^{-1}}2^{-j}{\Big)}\cdot\delta^{-1}\leq C_{z\pm}

where

form a convergent series by (

).

For the first sum of (

), using property of atom
\int u(y)dy=0
, we rewrite

{\big(}T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}{\big)}_{j}u(x)=\int K_{z\pm,j}(x,y)u(y)dy=\int_{D}[K_{z\pm,j}(x,y)-K_{z\pm,j}(x,\overline{y})]u(y)dy

\int|{\big(}T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}{\big)}_{j}u(x)|dx\leq C_{z\pm}\cdot 2^{j}\|u\|_{L^{1}}\cdot\delta

and

\sum_{2^{j}\leq\delta^{-1}}\int|{\big(}T^{(-1)}_{z\pm,I}{\big)}_{j}u(x)|dx\leq C_{z\pm}\sum_{2^{j}\leq\delta^{-1}}2^{j}\cdot\delta\leq C_{z\pm}.

where

by combing all the results.

The goal of this section is proving the following.

Theorem 5.1

Let

and

Suppose
p
satisfies

then we have

(5.1)

\|T_{B}\;h\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{p}}.

Proof.

We should first show that
T_{0,II}
is
L^{p}
bounded. Then the
L^{p}
boundedness of all

T_{z,II}
hold by the same reasons. To show those

we need to observe how the bounds varies with

As we point out before that on the region II of phase space
(x,\xi)
, the stationary phase
formula does not apply for the calculation of

. On the other hand, the

5.2

large oscillation. This means that in the estimate
of the kernel of the operator

, we must avoid using integration by parts argument since
the derivative of

is not controllable.

Recalling (
3.29
), we have

(5.2)

a(x,\xi)=C\cdot\sin\theta_{0}\cos\theta_{0}\cdot(|x||\xi|)^{\gamma-1},\;{\rm if}\;(x,\xi)\in\;{\rm region\;II},

where
C
is independent of
x,\xi
and
\theta_{0}=\arccos(x\cdot\xi/|x||\xi|)
is the angle spanned
by
x
and
\xi
.

We expand
\widehat{h}(\xi)
and rewrite

(5.3)

T_{z,II}h(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K_{z}(x,y)h(y)dy

where

K_{z}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{z,II}(x,\xi)\;e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\;a(x,\xi)d\xi.

We use generalized Schur’s lemma (see Lemma
7.2
) to show the

boundedness of
the
T_{0}
operator by replacing
K_{0}(x,y)
in (
5.3
) with
|K_{0}(x,y)|
. Thus we need to
find two positive measurable functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
such that the followings hold:

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\int|K_{0}(x,y)|^{\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}h_{1}(y)^{p^{\prime}}dy\leq C_{p^{\prime}}h_{2}(x)^{p^{\prime}},\\
&\int|K_{0}(x,y)|^{\delta_{2}p}h_{2}(x)^{p}dx\leq C_{p}h_{1}(y)^{p}\end{split}

where
1<p,\;p^{\prime}<\infty
,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
,
0<\delta_{1},\delta_{2}<1
and
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}=1
.
Then we will show that the bounds of
T_{z,II}
can be obtained by a scaling argument and
verify that the bounds of

form a convergent series of

.

Next we estimate
|K_{0}(x,y)|
according to (

) and the sizes of

and

.

Case 1.
If

, using (

(5.5)

\begin{split}|K_{0}(x,y)|&\leq c^{\prime}_{0}\;|x|^{\gamma-1}\int_{\{(x,\xi)\in\Lambda_{0},\;|x||\xi|<512\}}|\xi|^{\gamma-1}d\xi\\
&=c^{\prime\prime}_{0}\;|x|^{-1+\gamma}\int_{0}^{512/|x|}r^{-1+\gamma}\cdot r^{2}dr\leq C_{0}\;(512)^{2+\gamma}|x|^{-3}.\end{split}

Before we go to the next case, let us discuss how the coefficients vary with
z
.
The coefficient
c^{\prime}_{0}
represents the contribution from
\cos\theta_{0}\sin\theta_{0}
, inside the definition of
a(x,\xi)
,
over
{\rm supp}\;\psi_{0,II}
. We note that
c^{\prime\prime}_{0}
inherits
c^{\prime}_{0}
and furthermore represents
the use of spherically coordinate on

{\rm supp}\;\psi_{0,II}
, i.e.,
\Lambda_{0}
. Suppose the similar calculation for

produces

From definition of

c^{\prime}_{z}=c^{\prime}_{|z|},\;c^{\prime}_{|z|+1}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot c^{\prime}_{|z|}\;{\rm and}\;c^{\prime\prime}_{|z|+1}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\cdot c^{\prime\prime}_{|z|}.

Thus

(5.6)

Case 2.
If

, let

\begin{split}|K_{0}(x,y)|&\leq c^{\prime}_{0}\;{\Big|}\int_{\{(x,\xi)\in\Lambda_{0},\;|x||\xi|<512\}}e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}(|x||\xi|)^{\gamma-1}d\xi{\Big|}\\

&\leq C_{0}\;(512)^{2+\gamma}|y|^{-3}{\big(}\frac{|x|}{|y|}{\big)}^{3}.\end{split}

Again (
5.6
) is satisfied as before if we follow the same
method to estimate

.

In order to prove (

We take

(5.8)

\begin{split}&\alpha=\frac{28}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\;,\;\beta=\frac{28}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{p}\;,\;\ell_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\;,\;\ell_{2}=1+\frac{p}{2p^{\prime}}\\
&\delta_{1}=\frac{28}{30}\cdot\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{30}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\;,\;\delta_{2}=\frac{29}{30}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{30}\cdot\frac{1}{p},\end{split}

\begin{split}&-\alpha p^{\prime}+3>0\;,\;-6\delta_{1}p^{\prime}-\alpha p^{\prime}+3<0\;,\;-3\delta_{1}p^{\prime}+\ell_{1}(-\alpha p^{\prime}+3)=-\beta p^{\prime}\\
&-\beta p+3>0\;,\;-3\delta_{2}p-\beta p+3<0\;,\;-3\delta_{2}p+\ell_{2}(-\beta p+3)=-\alpha p.\end{split}

With these in hand and taking

).

Let

\int_{|y|>|x|^{\ell_{1}}}|K_{0}(x,y)|^{\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}h_{1}(y)^{p^{\prime}}dy\leq C_{0}(512)^{2+\gamma}|x|^{-3\delta_{1}p^{\prime}+\ell_{1}(-\alpha p^{\prime}+3)}.

Use the spherically coordinate with

If

(5.12)

\begin{split}\int_{|y|>|x|^{\ell_{1}}}|K_{0}(x,y)|^{\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}h_{1}(y)^{p^{\prime}}dy&\leq C_{0}(512)^{2+\gamma}|x|^{3\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}\cdot(r^{-6\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}\cdot r^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+3}){\Big|}_{r=|x|^{\ell_{1}}}\\

\int_{|y|\leq|x|^{\ell_{1}}}|K_{0}(x,y)|^{\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}h_{1}(y)^{p^{\prime}}dy\leq C_{0}(512)^{2+\gamma}|x|^{-3\delta_{1}p^{\prime}+\ell_{1}(-\alpha p^{\prime}+3)},

Proposition 6.8

Assume the kernel B defined in (

Proof.

Recall the definition of the loss term (

Next we recall that the Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration ensures that if there exist measurable functions

6.4

\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{\infty}\in L^{3}_{x,v}
such that

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f(t)-U(t)(\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{\infty})\|_{L^{3}_{x,v}}\rightarrow 0\;\;{\rm as}\;t\rightarrow\infty.

The above statement is equivalent to prove that

(6.53)

\|U(-t)(\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f)(t)-\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f_{\infty}\|_{L^{3}_{x,v}}\rightarrow 0\;\;{\rm as}\;t\rightarrow\infty,

since
U(t)
preserves the
L^{3}_{x,v}
norm.

By the Duhamel formula, we have

(6.54)

U(-t)f(t)=f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)Q(f,f)(s)ds.

Hence the scattering of
f(t)
is confirmed if we have the convergence of the integral

\langle v\rangle^{\ell}\int_{0}^{\infty}U(-t)Q(f,f)(t)dt

in
L^{3}_{x,v}
. In this case
f_{\infty}
is given by

(6.55)

f_{\infty}=f_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}U(-t)Q(f,f)(t)dt.

We rewrite (
6.54
) as

U(-t)f(t)+\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)Q^{-}(f,f)(s)ds=f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)Q^{+}(f,f)(s)ds.

Since each term of above equation is non-negative, thus we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)Q^{-}(f,f)(s)ds&\leq f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)Q^{+}(f,f)(s)ds\\
&\leq f_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}U(-s)Q^{+}(f,f)(s)ds.\end{split}

By monotone convergence theorem, it holds that

(6.56)

\int_{0}^{\infty}U(-s)Q^{-}(f,f)(s)ds\leq f_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}U(-s)Q^{+}(f,f)(s)ds.

Then we are reduced to prove the right hand side of (
6.56
) is bounded
in
L^{3}_{x,v}
.

Let
U^{*}(t)
be the adjoint operator of
U(t)
, it is clearly that
U^{*}(t)=U(-t)
.
Let
(\tilde{\mathbbm{q}},\tilde{\mathbbm{r}},\tilde{\mathbbm{p}})
be the KT-admissible triplet chosen in (
6.18
)
and recall that
1/\tilde{\mathbbm{p}}^{\prime}+1/\tilde{\mathbbm{r}}^{\prime}=2/3
.
By duality, the homogeneous Strichartz estimate

\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}U(t)g\|_{L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{q}}}_{t}L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{r}}}_{x}L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{p}}}_{v}}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}g\|_{L^{{3}/{2}}_{x,v}}

implies

\begin{split}&\bigg\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle v\rangle^{\ell}U^{*}(t)Q^{+}(f,f)dt\bigg\|_{L^{3}_{x,v}}\\
&\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{q}}^{\prime}}_{t}L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{r}}^{\prime}}_{x}L^{\tilde{\mathbbm{p}}^{\prime}}_{v}}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f\|^{2}_{L^{\mathbbm{q}}_{t}L^{\mathbbm{r}}_{x}L^{\mathbbm{p}}_{v}}\end{split}

as before. Thus we conclude that
\langle v\rangle^{\ell}f
scatters.

7.
Some tools

One of the most powerful tools in estimating the oscillatory integral

I_{\Lambda}(u,f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{i\Lambda f(y)}u(y)dy,

for large
\Lambda
is the
following lemma of stationary phase asymptotics. There are several
versions used widely, here we only record one of these which is
from Theorem 7.7.5 of Hörmander
[
23
]
. We use the notation
D_{j}=-i\partial_{j}
.

Theorem 7.1

(Stationary phase asymptotics)
.

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a compact set,
X
an open neighborhood of
K
and

k
a positive number.
If
u\in C^{2k}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),f\in C^{3k+1}(X)
and

{\rm Im}\;f\geq 0
in
X,{\rm Im}\;f(y_{0})=0,f^{\prime}(y_{0})=0,{\rm det}\;f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})\neq 0,f^{\prime}\neq 0
in
K\backslash\{{y_{0}}\}
then

(7.1)

\displaystyle|I

\displaystyle-e^{i\Lambda f(y_{0})}({\rm det}(\Lambda f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})/2\pi i))^{-1/2}\sum_{j<k}\Lambda^{-j}L_{j}u|

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2k}\sup|D^{\alpha}u|\;,\;\Lambda>0

Here
C
is bounded when
f
stays in a bounded set in
C^{3k+1}(X)
and

|y-y_{0}|/|f^{\prime}(y)|
has a uniform bound.
With

g_{y_{0}}(y)=f(y)-f(y_{0})-\langle f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})(y-y_{0}),y-y_{0}\rangle/2

which
vanish of third order at
x_{0}
we have

(7.2)

L_{j}u=\sum_{\nu-\mu=j}\;\;\sum_{2\nu\geq 3\mu}i^{-j}2^{-\nu}\langle f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})^{-1}D,D\rangle^{\nu}(g_{y_{0}}^{\mu}u)(y_{0})/\mu!\nu!

which is a differential operator of order
2j
acting on
u
at
y_{0}
. The coefficients are rational
homogeneous functions of degree
-j
in
f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0}),\cdots,f^{2j+2}(y_{0})
with denominator

({\rm det}\;f^{\prime\prime}(y_{0}))^{3j}
. In every term the total number of derivatives of
u
and
f^{\prime\prime}
is at most
2j
.

We need the following Schur’s Lemma which can be found in
[
40
]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two positive measures on the space
X
. Let

K(x,y)
be a nonnegative measurable functions on
X\times X
.
Let
T
be the integral operator with kernel
K
, as

T(f)(x)=\int_{Y}K(x,y)f(y)d\nu(y)

Suppose
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Let
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}

be two real numbers such that
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}=1
. If there exist positive functions

h_{1}
and
h_{2}
with positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{1}
such that

\int_{X}K(x,y)^{\delta_{1}p^{\prime}}h_{1}(y)^{p^{\prime}}d\mu(y)\leq C_{1}h_{2}(x)^{p^{\prime}}

for almost all
x\in X
, and

\int_{X}K(x,y)^{\delta_{2}q}h_{2}(x)^{q}d\mu(y)\leq C_{2}h_{1}(y)^{q}

Then
T
maps
L^{p}(Y)
to
L^{q}(X)
with norm at most
C_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}C_{2}^{1/q}
.

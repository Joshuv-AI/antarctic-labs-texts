Path-integral Bosonization of d = 2 d=2 𝒫𝒯 {\cal P}{\cal T} symmetric models
C.M. Naón, F.A. Schaposnik

Abstract
We discuss bosonization of non-Hermitian 𝒫𝒯 {\cal P}{\cal T} invariant fermion models in d = 2 d=2 space-time dimensions within the path-integral approach in which the generating functionals associated to the fermion and boson models can be related. We first discuss the 𝒫𝒯 {\cal P}{\cal T} symmetric Thirring-sine-Gordon connection and then extend the treatment to bosonize the Gross-Neveu model.



The possibility that non-Hermitian (NH) quantum Hamiltonians could have real energy eigenvalues was a subject of study since the 1940 decade. Seminal results were obtained more recently by Bender and collaborators, starting from the work
[
1
]

{\cal P}
) and time reversal (
{\cal T}
) transformations leading to real energy eigenvalues and conserved probabilities (see
[
2

and
[
3
]
for a more complete list of references). Among the many applications to quantum field theories, one should mention the case of NH quantum electrodynamics
[
4
]
-
[
5

, dual quantum field theories
[
6
]
, and many other models.

More recently it was observed that
{\cal P}{\cal T}
-symmetry in topological insulators guarantees unitarity of states in the bulk but lost at the boundary thus violating the bulk-boundary correspondence of topological phases; in connection with this, the role of chiral symmetry was also investigated (see
[
7
]
and references therein).
There were also studies of
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetric models in condensed matter physics concerning quantum critical points
[

[

[

-

In view of the recent developments mentioned above, it seems relevant to study boson and fermion quantum field theories within the path-integral framework which, as recently observed, is particularly useful to study
{\cal P}{\cal T}

[

It is the purpose of this work to analyze bosonization of
d=2

{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetry in the absence of hermiticity. In the path-integral approach this implies to show the connection between generating functionals of the boson and fermion models and this will be achieved for different

The plan of this work is the following: we first discuss, inspired in ref.

The
{\cal P}{\cal T}
sine-Gordon model

The generating functional for the
{\cal P}{\cal T}
extension of the original Hermitian sine-Gordon model proposed in ref.
[
6
]
reads

Z_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}={\cal N}\int D\phi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}xL_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}\right)

(1)

with
L_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
given by

L_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta^{2}}\left(\cos\beta\phi+i\epsilon\sin\beta\phi\right)

(2)

Note that we have not included the external source coupled to the scalar field since it does not play any role in the bosonization process.

The addition of the sine term in (
2
) breaks the original sine-Gordon model hermiticity but its imaginary coefficient guarantees that
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetry holds.

The interaction term in
L_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
can be written as

\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta^{2}}\left(\cos\beta\phi+i\epsilon\sin\beta\phi\right)=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}(1+i\epsilon)\exp(i\beta\phi)+(1-i\epsilon)\exp(-i\beta\phi)\right)

(3)

In order to perform the path-integral defining

Z_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
by means of a power expansion in
\alpha_{0}
we shall then have to consider vacuum expectations of the form

\left\langle\prod_{i}\exp\left(i\beta_{i}\phi(x_{i})\right)\right\rangle_{0},

(4)

where
\langle~\rangle_{0}
indicates v.e.v.’s for the case of free boson fields
\phi
, and

\beta_{i}=\pm\beta

(5)

The result of such v.e.v.’s involves scalar propagators
which have serious infrared divergences. Indeed, the
d=2
scalar propagator for a free scalar of mass
\mu
takes, at short distances the form

\Delta_{\mu}(x;\mu)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(c\mu|x|)

(6)

where
c=(1/2)\exp\gamma
with
\gamma
the Euler constant. One has also to take care of ultraviolet divergencies introducing a cutoff
\Lambda
. Using the Wick product and adopting an appropriate normal ordering (which in the path-integral consists in the addition of appropriate counterterms to kill tadpoles) one can easily see that the v.e.v. in (
4
) takes the form

\left\langle\prod_{i}\exp\left(i\beta_{i}\phi(x_{i})\right)\right\rangle_{0}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\Delta_{\rho}(x_{i}-x_{j};\mu)\right)

(7)

where the regularized propagator
\Delta_{\rho}(x;\mu)
takes the form

\Delta_{\rho}(x;\mu)=\Delta(\mu|x|)-\Delta_{\rho}(\rho|x|)

(8)

Here
\rho
is a mass that takes care of divergencies arising when
x\to 0
. Indeed,

2\pi\lim_{\mu|x|\to 0}\Delta_{\rho}(|x|)=-\log(\mu c)+\log(\rho c)=-\log(\mu/\rho)

(9)

Now, one has also to take care of ultraviolet divergencies and this is achieved by introducing a mass
\Lambda
which will act as a cutoff by introducing a propagator
\tilde{\Delta}_{\rho}(x;\mu)
in the form

\tilde{\Delta}_{\rho}(x;\mu)=\Delta_{\rho}(x;\mu)-\Delta_{\rho}(x;\Lambda)

(10)

so that finally eq.(
7
) takes the form

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i}\exp\left(i\beta_{i}\phi(x_{i})\right)\right\rangle_{\!\!0}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{\rho}(x_{i}-x_{j};\mu)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{\rho}(x_{i}-x_{j};\mu)\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\tilde{\Delta}_{\rho}(x_{i}-x_{j};\mu)\right)

where we have separated the
x_{j}=x_{i}
and the
x_{j}\neq x_{i}
factors. Using eq.(
9
) the former one takes the form

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{i}\to x_{j}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\frac{\beta_{i}^{2}}{8\pi}\tilde{\Delta}(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu}{\rho}\right)^{\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}/(8\pi)}\left(\frac{\rho}{\Lambda}\right)^{-\sum_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}/(8\pi)}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu}{\Lambda}\right)^{\left({(\sum_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}/(4\pi)}\right)}

If we now include the
i\neq j
terms one can prove that the complete
\mu
-dependent factor becomes

\langle\exp i{\footnotesize\sum_{j}}\beta_{j}\phi(x_{j})\rangle_{0}=\left(\frac{\mu}{\rho}\right)^{\left({(\sum_{i}\beta_{i})^{2}/(4\pi)}+\sum_{i\neq j}(\beta_{i}\beta_{j}/4\pi)\tilde{\Delta}(x_{i}-x_{j})\right)}

(13)

Since bosons
\phi
are massless one has to take the
\mu\to 0
limit, this implying that the v.e.v. vanishes, except if

\sum_{i}\beta_{i}=0

(14)

Hence, only the
i\neq j
factor remains. Now, taking
\Lambda|x_{i}-x_{j}|\gg 1
and
\mu|x_{i}-x_{j}|\ll 1

one has
\Delta(x_{i}-x_{j})
as given in eq.(
6
).

We can now use result (
13
) taking into account eqs.(
4
)-(
5
) and perform a perturbative expansion in
\alpha_{0}

[
16
]
. As a consequence of result (
14
) only the terms which are even in
\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta^{2}}
contribute to the series, so that the properly normalized generating functional (
1
) takes the form

\displaystyle Z_{sG}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!^{2}}(\alpha/\beta^{2})^{2k}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{k}

(15)

\displaystyle\times\int\frac{\prod_{i>j}^{k}(c^{2}\rho^{2}|x_{i}-x_{j}||y_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{k}(c\rho|x_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}d^{2}x^{i}d^{2}y^{i}

where we have defined the renormalized constant

\alpha=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\Lambda}\right)^{\beta^{2}/4\pi}

(16)

The
{\cal P}{\cal T}
Thirring model

Let us start from the generating functional
Z_{Th}
for the
{\cal P}{\cal T}
invariant Thirring model introduced in ref.
[
6
]
,

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\int D\bar{\psi}D\psi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}xL_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}\right)\;,

(17)

with the Thirring
{\cal P}{\cal T}
invariant Lagrangian given by

L_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\bar{\psi}(i\not\!\partial-m_{1}-m_{2}\gamma_{5})\psi+\frac{g^{2}}{2}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)\;,

(18)

where we are working in Minkowski space (
{\rm diag}\,g_{\mu\nu}={(1,-1)}
) with gamma matrices taken as

\gamma_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma_{5}=\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}\;,\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu}

(19)

Parity reflection operator
{\cal P}
and time reversal
{\cal T}
acting on fermions satisfy

\displaystyle{\cal P}\psi(x,t){\cal P}=\gamma^{0}\psi(-x,t)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal P}\bar{\psi}(x,t){\cal P}=\bar{\psi}(-x,t)\gamma^{0}

(20)

\displaystyle{\cal T}\psi(x,t){\cal P}=\gamma^{0}\psi(x,-t)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal T}\bar{\psi}(x,t){\cal T}=\bar{\psi}(x,-t)\gamma^{0}

(21)

Note that the
m_{2}
term is non-Hermitian because it changes sign under Hermitian conjugation. Now, being odd both under parity and time reversal transformations it is
{\cal P}{\cal T}
-symmetric
since
L_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}={\cal P}{\cal T}L_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}{\cal P}{\cal T}
.

Following
[
16
]
, we now proceed to linearize the quartic fermion interaction by introducing an auxiliary vector field through the identity

\exp\left(i\frac{g^{2}}{2}\int d^{2}x(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)\!\!=\!\!\int\!\!DA_{\mu}\exp\left(\!i\int d^{2}x\left(-\frac{1}{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}-g\bar{\psi}\not\!\!A\psi\right)\right)

(22)

so that the generating functional (
17
) can be written as

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}\!=\int\!D\bar{\psi}D\psi DA_{\mu}\!\exp\!\left(\!i\!\int\!d^{2}x\bar{\psi}(i\not\!\partial-m_{1}-m_{2}\gamma_{5})\psi-\frac{1}{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}-g\bar{\psi}\not\!\!A\psi\right)\!.

(23)

Now, in
d=2
dimensions, the vector field
A_{\mu}
can be written in the form

A_{\mu}=\frac{1}{g}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\nu}\phi+\frac{1}{g}\partial_{\mu}\eta.

(24)

This formula implies that while
\eta
is a scalar field,
\phi
is a pseudoscalar in view of the Levi-Civita symbol character, as it was originally established within the original (Hermitian) Thirring-sine Gordon bosonization connection
[
15
]
. Such behavior under
{\cal P}
is a general feature in any dimensions and follows from the bosonization formula which relates the fermion current to a (pseudo) Kalb-Ramond bosonic dual
[
17
]
.

Let us now consider the following change in the path-integral variables
\bar{\psi},\psi\rightarrow\bar{\chi},\chi
and
A_{\mu}\rightarrow\phi,\eta
so that fermions change according to

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\exp(i\gamma_{5}\phi(x)+i\eta(x))\chi(x)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(x)=\bar{\chi}(x)\exp(i\gamma_{5}\phi(x)-i\eta(x))

(25)

Now, at the generating functional level we have to take into account the Fujikawa Jacobian
[
18
]

J[\phi]
which takes the form
[
16
]

J[\phi]=\exp\left({-i}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}x(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}\right)

(26)

The
\eta
field completely decouples from fermions so that it will play no role in the Thirring model bosonization
[
16
]
. We shall then ignore it in what follows.
Concerning the
A_{\mu}\to(\phi,\eta)
change of variables, it just gives a trivial Jacobian
J_{A}\propto(1/g^{2})\det\nabla^{2}
which can be absorbed in the generating functional normalization so that

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
reads

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\int D\bar{\psi}D\psi D\phi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}xL_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}\right)\;,

(27)

with
L_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}[\bar{\chi},\chi,\phi]

given by

L_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\left(\frac{1}{2g^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}+\bar{\chi}i\not\!\partial\chi-\bar{\chi}\exp(i\gamma_{5}\phi)(m_{1}+m_{2}\gamma_{5})\exp(i\gamma_{5}\phi)\chi

(28)

In order
to prove the equivalence between the
{\cal P}{\cal T}
invariant Thirring and sine-Gordon models we shall make a perturbative expansion of the former in the “mass” term
\bar{\chi}M(x)\chi
, where
M(x)
is the matrix

M(x)\equiv(m_{1}+\gamma_{5}m_{2})\exp(2\gamma_{5}\phi)

(29)

At this point, in analogy with the procedure followed for the sine-Gordon model leading to expression (
4
) , choosing an adequate normalization we write
Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
as the following vacuum to vacuum expectation value

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\left\langle\exp\left(-i\int d^{2}x\bar{\chi}M(x)\chi\right)\right\rangle_{0},

(30)

where the v.e.v. above must be computed in a theory of free fermions and free bosons, with propagators

G_{F}(x)=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\gamma^{\mu}x_{\mu}}{x^{2}},

(31)

and

\Delta_{\mu}(x;\mu)=-\lambda^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(c\mu|x|),

(32)

respectively, with

\lambda^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{1-g^{2}/\pi}.

(33)

In order to get insight into the structure of the perturbative expansion, we shall display terms up to order
M^{2}
explicitly. To this end we express the fermionic bispinors as

\chi=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\chi_{1}\\
\chi_{2}\end{array}\right)

(34)

and
\bar{\chi}=\chi^{\dagger}\gamma_{0}
. The first order term reads

\bar{\chi}M\chi=(m_{1}+m_{2})\exp(2i\phi)\bar{\chi}_{1}\chi_{1}++(m_{1}-m_{2})\exp(-2i\phi)\bar{\chi}_{2}\chi_{2}.

(35)

Concerning second order one has, after some work,

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}M\chi(x)\bar{\chi}M\chi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})\left(\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}A_{+}(x)A_{-}(y)\exp(2i(\phi(x)-\phi(y))\right.

(36)

\displaystyle+\left.\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})}A_{-}(x)A_{+}(y)\exp(-2i(\phi(x)-\phi(y))\right),

where we have defined

A_{\pm}\equiv\bar{\chi}\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\gamma_{5})\chi=\bar{\chi}_{j}\chi_{j},

(37)

with
j=1(2)
for upper (lower) sign of
A_{\pm}
(repeated indices do not imply sum in this formula).

Then, each term in this expansion can be written as the product of a fermionic and a bosonic factor. In particular one can easily see that the expectation value of the first order term (
35
) vanishes. Indeed, in this case, the v.e.v.’s of both fermionic and bosonic factors are zero when an adequate normal ordering that removes Wick contractions of fields at the same point, is adopted. Concerning the evaluation of the bosonic piece, it is completely similar to the one performed for the sine-Gordon model, which led to equation (
14
), the only difference being that in the present case one has
\beta_{i}=\pm 2
.

In this way one can calculate all orders in
M(x)
powers. One finds that the above arguments leading to the vanishing of the first order term can be generalized to all odd orders. Concerning the even ones, all of them contribute to (
30
), and can be readily computed using (
31
) and (
32
). By carefully analyzing the combinatorics of each term, and writing
m_{1}=m
and
m_{2}=\epsilon m
we finally obtain

Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!^{2}}(m^{2}(1-\epsilon^{2}))^{k}\int\frac{\prod_{i>j}^{k}(c^{2}\rho^{2}|x_{i}-x_{j}||y_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}\prod_{i=1}^{k}}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{k}(c\rho|x_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}}d^{2}x_{i}d^{2}y_{i}

(38)

Comparing (
15
) and (
38
) one can see that both generating functionals are identical provided the following identification hold,

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{1-g^{2}/\pi}

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta\phi)=m\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\bar{\psi}\psi

(40)

For
\epsilon=0
the generating function
Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
just coincides with the generating functional for the Hermitian Thirring model with fermion mass
m
. Hence as originally found in
[
6
]
one should impose
\epsilon^{2}<1
in order to have a consistent
{\cal P}{\cal T}
Thirring massive model with a real fermion mass

m(1-\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}
.

The
{\cal P}{\cal T}
Gross-Neveu model and its bosonization

We start from the path-integral defining the generating functional
Z_{GN}
for the Hermitian
d=2
Gross-Neveu fermion model

Z_{GN}=\int D\bar{\psi}D\psi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}xL_{GN}[\bar{\psi},\psi]\right)\;,

(41)

with
\psi
a massless fermion and the Lagrangian
L_{GN}
given by

L_{GN}[\bar{\psi},\psi]=\bar{\psi}i\!\!\not\!\partial\psi+\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime 2}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}

(42)

Introducing an auxiliary scalar field
\sigma(x)
,
Z_{GN}
can be written in the form

Z_{GN}\!=\!\int D\sigma\exp\left(\!-i\int d^{2}x\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\int D\bar{\psi}D\psi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}x\left(\bar{\psi}i\!\!\not\!\partial\psi-g^{\prime}\bar{\psi}\psi\sigma\right)\right)

(43)

or

Z_{GN}=\int D\sigma\exp\left(\!-i\int d^{2}x\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\det(i\!\!\not\!\partial-g^{\prime}\sigma)

(44)

with the fermion determinant given by

\det(i\!\!\not\!\partial-g^{\prime}\sigma)=\left\langle\exp\left(-ig^{\prime}\int d^{2}x\sigma\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle\right)\right\rangle_{0}

(45)

where
\langle~\rangle_{0}
indicates the v.e.v. for the case of free fermions.
Inspired
in the mass expansion that we performed in the case of the Thirring model we shall now proceed, starting from the r.h.s. of eq.(
45
) to expand the exponential in powers of
g^{\prime}
taking into account that no odd
g^{\prime}
powers will contribute after an appropriate tadpole treatment. Moreover, a careful comparison with the Hermitian Thirring model allows to verify that the combinatorial factors of each term coincides with the Thirring case. Then, it becomes apparent that the above fermionic determinant can be expressed as the same expansion in the Hermitian Thirring coupling constant limit
g\to 0
and
m\to m(x)\to g^{\prime}\sigma(x)
. Thus, one gets

\det(i\!\!\not\!\partial-g^{\prime}\!\sigma)=\!\!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\!\!\frac{1}{k!^{2}}{g^{\prime}}^{2k}\!\!\int\!\!\frac{\prod_{i>j}^{k}(c^{2}\rho^{2}|x_{i}-x_{j}||y_{i}-y_{j}|)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{k}(c\rho|x_{i}-y_{j}|)^{2}}\sigma(x_{i})\sigma(y_{i})d^{2}x_{i}d^{2}y_{i}

(46)

We shall now show that the same expansion in eq.(
46
) can be obtained for a sine-Gordon like bosonic model provided the cosine coefficient is
x
-dependent. Indeed, consider the following vacuum functional

\displaystyle Z_{sG_{x}}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\int\!\!D\phi\exp\left(i\int d^{2}x\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\right)\exp\left(i\frac{\alpha_{0}(x)}{\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta\phi)\right)

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\exp\left(i\int d^{2}x\frac{\alpha_{0}(x)}{\beta^{2}}\cos(\beta\phi)\right)\right\rangle_{0}

where now
\langle~\rangle_{0}
is the v.e.v. for free bosons.

To see this connection we make an expansion in powers of

\alpha_{0}(x)
in the r.h.s. of (
47
). Again, odd powers vanish so that, for the v.e.v. (
47
) we get an expression similar to the one corresponding to the usual, Hermitian sine-Gordon model, but with the coefficient
\alpha
promoted to be a function of
x
:

\displaystyle Z_{sG_{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!^{2}}(1/\beta^{2})^{2k}\times

(48)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\prod_{i>j}^{k}(c^{2}\rho^{2}|x_{i}-x_{j}||y_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{k}(c\rho|x_{i}-y_{j}|)^{\beta^{2}/2\pi}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\alpha(x_{i})\alpha(y_{i})d^{2}x_{i}d^{2}y_{i}

where we have defined the renormalized function

\alpha(x)=\frac{\alpha_{0}(x)}{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\Lambda}\right)^{\beta^{2}/4\pi}.

(49)

Comparing this result with that in (
46
) for the fermion model, we see that both expansions coincide provided the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle g\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(x)}{\rho}.

(50)

We have then that the GN fermion model can be bosonized becoming the sG
x
boson model since their generating functionals have been identified

Z_{GS_{x}}[\bar{\psi},\psi,\sigma]=Z_{GN}[\phi]

(51)

Hence, together with identities (
50
) the bosonization rule for the interaction terms reads

g\sigma(x)\bar{\psi}\psi=\frac{\alpha(x)}{4\pi}\cos(2\sqrt{\pi}\ \phi)

(52)

Up to now we have considered the path-integral bosonization of the usual, Hermitian Gross-Neveu model, which was not previously discussed in the literature. Concerning the connection for the case of
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetric models, from what we learnt for the case of
{\cal P}{\cal T}
sine-Gordon-Thirring equivalence, it is evident that for the case of the sine-Gordon model with an
\alpha(x)
dependent coefficient, in order to define a
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetric model
one should extend (
47
) as

Z_{sG_{x}}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}=\!\!\int D\phi\exp\left(\!i\int d^{2}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi+\frac{\alpha(x)}{4\pi}(\cos(\beta\phi(x))+i\epsilon\sin(\beta\phi(x))\right)\right)

(53)

with
\beta=2\sqrt{\pi}
. Then, it is easy to show that the bosonic partition function

Z_{sG_{x}}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}

is equivalent to a fermionic
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetric Gross-Neveu model that reads

Z_{GN}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}\!\!=\!\!\int\!\!D\sigma\exp\left(\!\!-i\int\!\!d^{2}x\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\!\right)\!\!\int\!\!D\bar{\psi}D\psi\exp\left(\!\!i\int\!\!\!d^{2}x\!\!\left(\bar{\psi}i\!\!\not\!\partial\psi-g^{\prime}\bar{\psi}\psi\sigma\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}\,\right)\!\right)\!.

(54)

This result completes our discussion on the equivalence between non Hermitian fermionic and bosonic 2D models with
{\cal P}{\cal T}
symmetry, through path-integral methods.

In summary, we have presented a path-integal approach to bosonization of
{\cal P}{\cal T}
models complementary to the one originally discussed in
[
6
]
. Concerning the sine-Gordon-Thirring connection we have explicitly shown the identity between the resulting generating functionals
Z_{Sg}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
and
Z_{Th}^{{\cal P}{\cal T}}
, the path-integral analog of the well-honored Coleman perturbation series results for the Hermitian models
[
14
]
. Using the same approach we have also discussed bosonization in the case of the Gross-Neveu model finding the corresponding generating functionals of the dual boson and fermion

Differentiating the State Evaluation Map from Matrices to Functions on Projective Space
Ghaliah Alhamzi & Edwin Beggs

Abstract
We show that the pure state evaluation map from M n ​ ( ℂ ) M_{n}(\mathbb{C}) to C ⁡ ( ℂ ​ ℙ n − 1 ) C(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) (a completely positive map of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras) extends to a cochain map from the universal calculus on M n ​ ( ℂ ) M_{n}(\mathbb{C}) to the holomorphic ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} calculus on ℂ ​ ℙ n − 1 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} . The method uses connections on Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -bimodules. This implies the existence of various functors, including one from M n ​ ( ℂ ) M_{n}(\mathbb{C}) modules to holomorphic bundles on ℂ ​ ℙ n − 1 \mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1} .

Apart from the concrete description of the state space, another reason why we are interested in the calculi on matrix algebras and the link with representations and states is Connes’ noncommutative derivation of the standard model

The fact that from a relatively simple noncommutative beginning involving matrices Connes constructs the standard model indicates that there probably something very interesting in the geometry of the initial noncommutative space. Most gauge theories in physics are described in terms of calculi and so we are naturally led to questions about calculi on matrices and how they relate to states.

2.3
Line bundles and calculus in
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

On
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
we have homogenous coordinates
v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
for

. We take
\underline{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
to lie on the sphere

in

on

by

\displaystyle z\triangleright(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=(zv_{1},\dots,zv_{n})\ .

We define

as

quotiented by this circle action, identifying points

for all

. We use notation

for the equivalence classes. We consider subsets of continuous functions on

, defining for integer

\displaystyle C_{m}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1})=\{f\in C(S^{2n-1}):f(z\triangleright\underline{v})=z^{m}f(\underline{v})\quad\text{for all}\ z\in U_{1},\underline{v}\in S^{2n-1}\}

is the usual smooth functions on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
There is an alternative view given by grading monomials in

and
\bar{v}_{i}
by
||v_{i}||=1
and
||\bar{v}_{i}||=-1
. Then a monomial of grade

is in

.
A grade zero monomial such as

is invariant for the circle action and so gives a function on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

An element of the tautological bundle
\tau
at

is given by

for

and the inner product on

is given by

\langle\overline{\alpha\,\underline{v}},\beta\underline{v}\rangle=\bar{\alpha}\beta\in\mathbb{C}\ ,

(3)

noting the use of the conjugate bundle to give bilinearity and be consistent with the earlier Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule inner product. A section of the tautological bundle is a function

so that

is a multiple of
\underline{v}
. We have a

correspondence between continuous sections of
\tau
and
C_{-1}(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1})

is a section and if

is a section then

is in

.

Recalling that
\sum_{i}v_{i}\bar{v}_{i}=1
and applying

gives
\sum_{i}\big(\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\bar{v}_{i}+v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{v}_{i}\big)=0
and as we require a complex calculus on

we get both
\sum_{i}\,\mathrm{d}v_{i}\,\bar{v}_{i}=0
and
\sum_{i}v_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{v}_{i}=0

again gives

Connection formulae for the radial Toda equations I
Martin A. Guest, Alexander R. Its, Maksim Kosmakov, Kenta Miyahara, Ryosuke Odoi

Abstract
. This paper is the first in a forthcoming series of works where the authors study the global asymptotic behavior of the radial solutions of the 2D periodic Toda equation of type A n A_{n} . The principal issue is the connection formulae between the asymptotic parameters describing the behavior of the general solution at zero and infinity. To reach this goal we are using a fusion of the PDE analysis and the Riemann-Hilbert nonlinear steepest descent method of Deift and Zhou which is applicable to 2D Toda in view of its Lax integrability. A principal technical challenge is the extension of the nonlinear steepest descent analysis to Riemann-Hilbert problems of matrix rank greater than 2 2 . In this paper, we meet this challenge for the case n = 2 n=2 (the rank 3 3 case) and it already captures the principal features of the general n n case.

This paper is the first in a forthcoming series of works where the authors study the global asymptotic behavior of the solutions of the 2D periodic Toda equation of type

(1.0.7)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}X,\quad\sigma>0,

(1.0.8)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln(24\sqrt{3})+\frac{3\pi}{4}+\alpha-\arg\Gamma\left(\frac{2i}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma^{2}\right)

(1.0.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X&=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln\Bigg[\dfrac{1}{8\cos\frac{\pi(1-\gamma)}{3}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi(1-\gamma)}{3}}\Bigg(q^{\mathbb{R}}+\frac{1}{q^{\mathbb{R}}}\Bigg)+\dfrac{1}{4\sin^{2}\frac{\pi(1-\gamma)}{3}}\Bigg]\end{aligned}

\displaystyle q^{\mathbb{R}}=\frac{2(\gamma-1)^{2}}{e^{-2\rho}}3^{2(\gamma-1)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2\gamma-2}{3}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2-2\gamma}{3}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{3}\right)}.

1.0.4

2 Direct monodromy problem

2.1  The Lax pair

The radial Toda equation (
1.0.4
) admits a Lax pair representation which follows from the more general Lax pair found first by A. Mikhailov for general
A_{n}
2D periodic Toda equation (
1.0.1
) as a self-similar reduction of the latter
[
Mik81
]
. We shall now reproduce this reduction for the case
n=2
following notations of
[
GIL15a
]
. Consider

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Psi_{t}=\left(w_{t}+\frac{1}{\lambda}W\right)\Psi\\
\Psi_{\bar{t}}=\left(-w_{\bar{t}}-\lambda W^{T}\right)\Psi\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle w=diag(w_{0},0,-w_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad W=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-w_{0}}&0\\
0&0&e^{-w_{0}}\\
e^{2w_{0}}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then, the compatibility condition,
\Psi_{t\bar{t}}=\Psi_{\bar{t}t}
is equivalent to

(2.1.2)

\displaystyle 2w_{t\bar{t}}=-[W^{T},W].

For radial solutions of (
2.1.1
), i.e. when
w=w(t,\bar{t})
depends only on the real variable
x=|t|
, we additionally impose the Euler-type homogeneity condition

\displaystyle\lambda\Psi_{\lambda}+t\Psi_{t}-\bar{t}\Psi_{\bar{t}}=0.

Writing
\zeta=\frac{\lambda}{t}
, equation (
2.1.1
) becomes

(2.1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Psi_{\zeta}=\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}W-\frac{x}{\zeta}w_{x}-x^{2}W^{T}\right)\Psi\\
\Psi_{x}=\left(-w_{x}-2x\zeta W^{T}\right)\Psi,\end{cases}

and the compatibility condition (
2.1.2
) of system (
2.1.3
) corresponds to
the radial Toda equation (
1.0.4
):

\displaystyle(w_{0})_{xx}+\frac{1}{x}(w_{0})_{x}=2e^{-2w_{0}}-2e^{4w_{0}}.

Remark
.

After the change of variables

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x=\frac{3}{4}s^{\frac{2}{3}}\\
\tilde{w}=s^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-2w_{0}},\end{cases}

one can show that (
1.0.4
) becomes

(2.1.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{ss}=\frac{\tilde{w}_{s}^{2}}{\tilde{w}}-\frac{\tilde{w}_{s}}{s}-\frac{\tilde{w}^{2}}{s}+\frac{1}{\tilde{w}},

which is a special case of the
P_{III}
equation.

Proposition 2.1
.

The real solution
w_{0}(x)
of (
1.0.4
) is smooth on the positive real line except for
0
and
\infty
.

Proof.

From the Painlevé property, a solution of the equation (
2.1.4
) could have only a pole on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, i.e.,
\tilde{w}(s)
is of the form,

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(s)=\frac{c}{(s-s_{0})^{n}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{(s-s_{0})^{n-1}}\right)

where
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
. This implies that
w_{0}(x)
might have a singularity only of the form,

(2.1.5)

\displaystyle w_{0}(x)=a\ln(x-x_{0})+b+\mathcal{O}\left(x-x_{0}\right)

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, but one can show that the ansatz (
2.1.5
) is not compatible with equation (
1.0.4
) for real
a
,
x_{0}
, and
b
.
∎

2.2  The monodromy data

Following the general theory of ODEs with singular points (see for instance Chapter 1 of
[
FIKN06
]
), we will obtain the monodromy data for the first equation of system (
2.1.3
).

2.2.1  Formal solutions

We start with the formal solutions of the equation

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\zeta}=\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}W-\frac{x}{\zeta}w_{x}-x^{2}W^{T}\right)\Psi.

This equation has two irregular singularities at
\zeta=0
and at
\zeta=1
.

The leading term of (
2.2.1
) at
\zeta=0
is
\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}W
. The
coefficient matrix
W
is diagonalizable:

(2.2.2)

\displaystyle W=P_{0}d_{3}P_{0}^{-1}

where

(2.2.3)

\displaystyle P_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-w_{0}}&e^{-w_{0}}&e^{-w_{0}}\\
1&\omega&\omega^{2}\\
e^{w_{0}}&\omega^{2}e^{w_{0}}&\omega e^{w_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\;d_{3}=diag(1,\omega,\omega^{2}),\quad\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}.

Moreover, matrix
P_{0}
has the following decomposition

(2.2.4)

\displaystyle P_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-w_{0}}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&e^{w_{0}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&\omega&\omega^{2}\\
1&\omega^{2}&\omega\end{pmatrix}=e^{-w}\Omega,

where we defined

\displaystyle e^{-w}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-w_{0}}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&e^{w_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
1&\omega&\omega^{2}\\
1&\omega^{2}&\omega\end{pmatrix}.

By Proposition 1.1 of
[
FIKN06
]
, one can obtain a unique formal solution of (
2.2.1
) at
\zeta=0
of the form

(2.2.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{f}^{(0)}(\zeta)=P_{0}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta))e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}d_{3}}.

Similarly, we can obtain the formal solution at
\zeta=\infty
. After the transformation
\zeta\mapsto\frac{1}{\zeta}
, equation (
2.2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\Psi_{\zeta}=\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}W^{T}+\frac{x}{\zeta}w_{x}+W\right)\Psi.

The leading term is
x^{2}W^{T}
, and
W^{T}
can be diagonalized as follows:

(2.2.6)

\displaystyle W^{T}=P_{\infty}d_{3}P_{\infty}^{-1},

\displaystyle P_{\infty}=P_{0}^{T-1}=e^{w}\Omega^{-1}.

Again, by Proposition 1.1 of
[
FIKN06
]
, one can obtain a unique formal solution of (
2.2.1
) at
\zeta=\infty
of the form

(2.2.7)

\displaystyle\Psi_{f}^{(\infty)}(\zeta)=P_{\infty}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}.

Remark
.

Observe that
\omega^{2}+\omega+1=0
, thus

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}=3C,

where

\displaystyle C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
C^{2}=I
, we have

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle\Omega^{-1}=\frac{1}{3}C\Omega=\frac{1}{3}\Omega C=\frac{1}{3}\bar{\Omega}.

2.2.2  Stokes sectors

Next, we describe the
Stokes rays

l_{n}^{(i,j)}
and
Stokes sectors

\Omega_{k}
that correspond to equation (
2.2.1
).

We start with
\zeta=0
.
Recall that Stokes sectors are defined so that the fundamental solution is uniquely determined there by the asymptotic solution
\Psi_{f}^{(0)}
.
In other words, suppose we have two fundamental solutions
\Psi^{(0)}
and
\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}
with the same asymptotics at
\zeta=0
, then their ratio
\Psi^{(0)}(\zeta)^{-1}\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}(\zeta)
is a constant matrix. To obtain the uniqueness of the solution for
\zeta\in\Omega_{k}
we need to have

(2.2.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}\Psi^{(0)}(\zeta)^{-1}\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}(\zeta)=I,\quad\zeta\in\Omega_{k}.

Since
\Psi^{(0)}
and
\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}
have the same asymptotics at
\zeta=0

\displaystyle\Psi^{(0)}(\zeta)^{-1}\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}(\zeta)=\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}e^{-\frac{1}{\zeta}d_{3}}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta))e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}d_{3}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}\begin{pmatrix}1+*&*e^{-\frac{1}{\zeta}(1-\omega)}&*e^{-\frac{1}{\zeta}(1-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}(1-\omega)}&1+*&*e^{-\frac{1}{\zeta}(\omega-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}(1-\omega^{2})}&*e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}(\omega-\omega^{2})}&1+*\end{pmatrix}

Hence in order to be able to conclude that
\Psi^{(0)}(\zeta)^{-1}\tilde{\Psi}^{(0)}(\zeta)=I
, we need the sector
\Omega_{k}
to contain at least one ray

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}{(\omega^{j-1}-\omega^{i-1})\zeta^{-1}}=0,

for each pair
(i,j)
,
i<j
. They are

\displaystyle l^{(1,2)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\;\big|\;\arg\zeta=-\frac{2\pi}{3}-n\pi\right\},

\displaystyle l^{(1,3)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\;\big|\;\arg\zeta=-\frac{\pi}{3}-n\pi\right\},

\displaystyle l^{(2,3)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\;\big|\;\arg\zeta=-n\pi\right\}.

By the definition, Stokes sectors are sectors that contain exactly one Stokes ray for each superscript
(i,j)
with
i<j
. Thus, one can take the following Stokes sectors:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0)}_{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;-\frac{2\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0)}_{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;-\frac{5\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<-\frac{\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0)}_{3}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;-\frac{8\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<-\frac{4\pi}{3}\right\}.

Thus, we have only two distinct Stokes sectors at
\zeta=0

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;-\frac{2\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{(\infty)}_{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;\frac{\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{5\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{(\infty)}_{3}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\>\big|\;\frac{4\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{8\pi}{3}\right\}.

Moreover, we have

(2.2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Omega^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta)&=\Omega^{(\infty)}_{n}(\zeta^{-1})\;\text{ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.}\end{aligned}

Thus, we take the following Stokes sectors at
\zeta=\infty
.

Strictly speaking, sectors
\Omega_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
should be considered as lying on the universal covering
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
.

2.2.3  Stokes matrices

Next, we define the
canonical solutions
. According to the general theory (see e.g. Theorem 1.4 in
[
FIKN06
]
), in each sector
\Omega_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
,
n=1,2,3
there exist unique solutions
\Psi^{(\infty,0)}_{n}

of (
2.2.1
) satisfying the asymptotic condition,

\displaystyle\Psi^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta)\sim\Psi^{(0)}_{f}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\to 0,\quad\zeta\in\Omega^{(0)}_{n},

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n}(\zeta)\sim\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\to\infty,\quad\zeta\in\Omega^{(\infty)}_{n}.

It also should be noticed that

(2.2.12)

\Psi_{3}^{(\infty)}(\zeta)=\Psi_{1}^{(\infty)}(\zeta e^{-2\pi i})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Psi_{3}^{(0)}(\zeta)=\Psi_{1}^{(\infty)}(\zeta e^{2\pi i}).

Remark
.

Another fundamental fact following from the general theory is that every solution of (
2.2.1
), and, in particular, all our canonical solutions
\Psi^{(\infty,0)}_{n}
admit analytical continuation on the whole universal covering
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
Relations (
2.2.12
) should be then understood as equations on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
where we accept a somewhat “colloquial” convention to denote the point on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
and its projection on
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
by the same letter
\zeta
. We also note that, in the case of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, we can write that

\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}\equiv\left\{\tilde{\zeta}=\Bigl(\zeta,\,\arg(\zeta)\Bigr)\,\Big|\,\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\right\}

so that the canonical “altering sheets” involution
\beta
on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
can be indicted in short writing as

\beta:\zeta\mapsto\zeta e^{2\pi i}.

With this convention, the rigorous version of relations (
2.2.12
) reads,

\Psi_{3}^{(\infty)}=(\beta^{*})^{-1}[\Psi_{1}^{(\infty}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Psi_{3}^{(0)}=\beta^{*}[\Psi_{1}^{(0)}].

In what follows we shall however use a “colloquial” form (
2.2.12
) of this relation, but when talking about
\Psi^{(\infty,0)}_{n}(\zeta)
will always recognize that
\arg\zeta
matters.

The Stokes matrices are defined by

(2.2.13)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{(0)}=[\Psi^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta)]^{-1}\Psi^{(0)}_{n+1}(\zeta),

(2.2.14)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{(\infty)}=[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n}(\zeta)]^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty}_{n+1}(\zeta),

where
n=1,2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Stokes matrices have the following structures,

\displaystyle S^{(\infty)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&0\\
0&1&0\\
*&*&1\end{pmatrix},\;S^{(\infty)}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&*\\
*&1&*\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&*\\
*&1&*\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\;S^{(0)}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&0\\
0&1&0\\
*&*&1\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We only give proof for
S_{1}^{(\infty)}
. The same approach can be applied to determine the structure of other Stokes matrices.

By the definition of the Stokes matrices (
2.2.13
) we have
\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n}S^{(\infty)}_{n}\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{n+1}=I
. For
\zeta\in\Omega_{n}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{n+1}^{(\infty)}
, it can be further written as

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}S^{(\infty)}_{n}\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{f}=I

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}P_{\infty}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}S^{(\infty)}_{n}e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))^{-1}P_{\infty}^{-1}=I

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}S^{(\infty)}_{n}e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}=I,

or

(2.2.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\begin{pmatrix}*&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega)}&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega)}&*&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega^{2})}&*e^{x^{2}\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})}&*\end{pmatrix}=I,

for
\zeta\in\Omega_{n}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{n+1}^{(\infty)}
.

When
n=1
,
\zeta
belongs to
\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{2}^{(\infty)}
, which means

\displaystyle\zeta\in\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\;\bigg|\;\frac{\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\}.

And it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}<\arg\zeta(1-\omega)<\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}<\arg\zeta(1-\omega^{2})<\frac{5\pi}{6}

\displaystyle\frac{5\pi}{6}<\arg\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})<\frac{7\pi}{6}.

Thus equality (
2.2.15
) holds if
S^{(\infty)}_{1}
have the following structure

\displaystyle S^{(\infty)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&0\\
0&1&0\\
*&*&1\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Lemma
2.2
describes the structure of
S_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
. In the next subsections, we will discuss some symmetries of these matrices that will help us to parameterize each
S_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
.

2.2.4  Anti-symmetry and inversion symmetry relations

In this section, we consider the anti-symmetry relations of the formal and canonical solutions and the Stokes matrices at
\zeta=\infty
, and the inversion symmetry relations of those at
\zeta=0
.

2.2.4.1 Anti-symmetry relation at
\zeta=\infty

Let
A(\zeta)
be the coefficient matrix of (
2.2.1
):

\displaystyle A(\zeta)=-\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}W-\frac{x}{\zeta}w_{x}-x^{2}W^{T}.

Then, one can observe the following symmetry:

(2.2.16)

\displaystyle\Delta A^{T}(-\zeta)\Delta=A(\zeta),

where

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&1&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, (
2.2.16
) implies the following lemma:

Lemma 2.3
.

\Psi(\zeta)
is a solution of (
2.2.1
), then so is
\tilde{\Psi}(\zeta):=\Delta\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)
.

Proof.

Since
\Psi(\zeta)
is a solution of (
2.2.1
), it satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d\Psi(\zeta)}{d\zeta}=A(\zeta)\Psi(\zeta).

Taking a transposition and changing
\zeta\mapsto-\zeta
, we have

(2.2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{d\Psi^{T}(-\zeta)}{d\zeta}=\Psi^{T}(-\zeta)A^{T}(-\zeta).

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{\Psi}(\zeta)}{d\zeta}

\displaystyle=\Delta\left(-\frac{d\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)}{d\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)\frac{d\Psi^{T}(-\zeta)}{d\zeta}\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)

\displaystyle=\Delta\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)\Psi^{T}(-\zeta)A^{T}(-\zeta)\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)\;\text{by \eqref{for proving prop 3.1}}

\displaystyle=\Delta A^{T}(-\zeta)\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)

\displaystyle=\Delta A^{T}(-\zeta)\Delta\Delta\Psi^{T-1}(-\zeta)

\displaystyle=A(\zeta)\tilde{\Psi}(\zeta)\;\text{by \eqref{antisymmetry with A at infty} and definition of $\tilde{\Psi}$.}

∎

One can observe that

\displaystyle\Delta P_{\infty}^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}=P_{\infty}.

Together with Lemma
2.3
this leads to the anti-symmetry relation for the formal solution:

(2.2.18)

\displaystyle\Delta[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(-\zeta)]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\zeta).

Indeed,

\displaystyle\Delta[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(-\zeta)]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}

\displaystyle=\Delta[P_{\infty}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}

\displaystyle=\Delta P_{\infty}^{T-1}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}\frac{d_{3}}{3}

\displaystyle=\Delta P_{\infty}^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}

\displaystyle=P_{\infty}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\zeta).

Moreover, considering the Stokes sectors of each canonical solution (see Figure
2
), we have that (
2.2.18
) implies the anti-symmetry of canonical solutions at
\zeta=\infty
:

(2.2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}(e^{i\pi}\zeta)]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}&=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1}(\zeta)\\
\Delta[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{3}(e^{i\pi}\zeta)]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}&=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}(\zeta)\end{aligned}

In terms of Stokes matrices, this can be interpreted as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

One has

(2.2.20)

\displaystyle S_{2}^{(\infty)}=d_{3}^{-1}[S_{1}^{(\infty)}]^{T-1}d_{3}.

Proof.

\displaystyle S_{1}^{(\infty)}=[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1}]^{-1}[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}]=3d_{3}^{-1}[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}]^{T}\Delta\Delta[\Psi^{(\infty)}_{3}]^{T-1}\frac{d_{3}}{3}\;\text{ by \eqref{antisymmetry for canonical solution at infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\;\;\;\;=d_{3}^{-1}(\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{3}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2})^{T}d_{3}=d_{3}^{-1}[S_{2}^{(\infty)}]^{T-1}d_{3}.

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow S_{2}^{(\infty)}=d_{3}^{-1}[S_{1}^{(\infty)}]^{T-1}d_{3}.

∎

2.2.4.2 Inversion symmetry relation at
\zeta=0

The matrix
A(\zeta)
admits another symmetry, namely

(2.2.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}\zeta^{2}}\Delta A\left(-\frac{1}{x^{2}\zeta}\right)\Delta=A(\zeta).

For this, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\Psi(\zeta)
is a solution of (
2.2.1
), then so is

\tilde{\Psi}(\zeta):=\Delta\Psi\left(-\frac{1}{x^{2}\zeta}\right).

On the level of Stokes matrices, it gives the following result.

Lemma 2.6
.

One has

(2.2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S^{(0)}_{n}&=d_{3}S^{(\infty)}_{n+1}d_{3}^{-1}\\
S_{2}^{(0)}&=d_{3}[S_{1}^{(0)}]^{T-1}d_{3}^{-1}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

From Lemma
2.5
one can obtain that the corresponding formulas for the formal solutions are

(2.2.23)

\displaystyle\Delta\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}\left(-\frac{1}{x^{2}\zeta}\right)3d_{3}^{-1}=\Psi^{(0)}_{f}(\zeta),

and for the canonical solutions:

(2.2.24)

\displaystyle\Delta\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n+1}\left(e^{i\pi}\frac{1}{x^{2}\zeta}\right)3d_{3}^{-1}=\Psi^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta),

where
n=1,2
. In terms of
\Psi^{(0)}_{k}
it can be read as

(2.2.25)

\displaystyle\Psi^{(0)}_{2}(\zeta)=\Delta\left[\Psi^{(0)}_{1}(e^{i\pi}\zeta)\right]^{T-1}3d_{3}^{-1}.

which gives the corresponding formulas for the Stokes matrices.
∎

2.2.5  Cyclic symmetry relations

The matrix
A(\zeta)
has what is called
Cyclic symmetry
, namely

(2.2.26)

\displaystyle\omega d_{3}^{-1}A(\omega\zeta)d_{3}=A(\zeta).

Using this symmetry, we obtain the next lemma:

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\Psi(\zeta)
is a solution of (
2.2.1
), then so is
\tilde{\Psi}(\zeta):=d_{3}^{-1}\Psi(\omega\zeta)
.

2.2.5.1 Cyclic symmetry relations of the formal solutions

Let us introduce

\displaystyle\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then Lemma
2.7
together with equalities

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}P_{\infty}\Pi=P_{\infty},\quad\Pi^{-1}e^{x^{2}\omega\zeta d_{3}}\Pi=e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}},

gives the following formula for the formal solution:

(2.2.27)

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\omega\zeta)\Pi=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\zeta).

Similarly, for
\zeta\to 0
, we have:

(2.2.28)

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(0)}_{f}(\omega\zeta)\Pi^{-1}=\Psi^{(0)}_{f}(\zeta),

which follows from the equalities

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}P_{0}\Pi^{-1}=P_{0},\;\Pi e^{\frac{1}{\omega\zeta}d_{3}}\Pi=e^{\frac{1}{\zeta}d_{3}}.

2.2.5.2 Cyclic symmetry relations of the fundamental solutions

In order to formulate the cyclic symmetry of the fundamental solutions we need a collection of Stokes sectors compatible with this symmetry.

First, we consider the case
\zeta=\infty
. For
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
we introduce additional sectors

(2.2.29)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1+n}^{(\infty)}

\displaystyle:=e^{i\pi n}\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}.

In these sectors we consider fundamental solutions
\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1+n}(\zeta)
of (
2.2.1
) determined by

(2.2.30)

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1+n}(\zeta)\sim\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\rightarrow\infty,\quad\zeta\in\Omega_{1+n}^{(\infty)}.

Then one can show that the cyclic symmetry relations of the formal solutions (
2.2.27
) translates to:

(2.2.31)

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n+\frac{2}{3}}(\omega\zeta)\Pi=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{n}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\in\Omega_{n}^{(\infty)},

for
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
.

Similarly, near
\zeta=0
, for
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
we introduce additional sectors

(2.2.32)

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}^{(0)}(\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\Omega_{n}^{(\infty)}(\zeta^{-1}),

which is consistent with the relations (
2.2.11
) we had before.
In these sectors we consider fundamental solutions
\Psi^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta)
of (
2.2.1
) determined by

(2.2.33)

\displaystyle\Psi^{(0}_{n}(\zeta)\sim\Psi^{(0)}_{f}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0,\quad\zeta\in\Omega_{n}^{(0)},

where
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}.
Then the cyclic symmetry relations (
2.2.28
) translates to

(2.2.34)

\displaystyle d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(0)}_{n-\frac{2}{3}}(\omega\zeta)\Pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{(0)}_{n}(\zeta),\quad\zeta\in\Omega_{n}^{(0)},

for
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2.5.3 Cyclic symmetry relations of Jump matrices

It is not straightforward to formulate the cyclic symmetries for the Stokes matrices. We will obtain them in several steps; first, we define jump matrices for
\pi/3
rotated fundamental solutions.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
, consider constant matrices

(2.2.35)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{(\infty)}:=\Psi_{n}^{(\infty)-1}\Psi_{n+\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}.

Then, these jump matrices have the following structure,

\displaystyle Q^{(\infty)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\;Q^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{1}{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&*&1\end{pmatrix},\;Q^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{2}{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
*&0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle Q^{(\infty)}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
*&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\;Q^{(\infty)}_{2\frac{1}{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&*\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\;Q^{(\infty)}_{2\frac{2}{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&*\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

We will prove the structure for
Q_{1}^{(\infty)}
only. The same approach can be applied to determine the structure of other jump matrices.

For
Q^{(\infty)}_{1}
, equation (
2.2.35
) can be written as

\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1}Q^{(\infty)}_{1}\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{1\frac{1}{3}}=I
.
Using the definition of the canonical solutions, it implies

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{f}Q^{(\infty)}_{1}\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{f}=I,\qquad\zeta\in\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}P_{\infty}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}Q^{(\infty)}_{1}e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}(I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}))^{-1}P_{\infty}^{-1}=I

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}e^{-x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}Q^{(\infty)}_{1}e^{x^{2}\zeta d_{3}}=I,

or

(2.2.36)

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\begin{pmatrix}*&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega)}&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega)}&*&*e^{-x^{2}\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})}\\
*e^{x^{2}\zeta(1-\omega^{2})}&*e^{x^{2}\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})}&*\end{pmatrix}=I,

for
\zeta\in\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}
. By the definition of
\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}
and
\Omega_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}
, we have that

-\frac{\pi}{3}<\arg\zeta<\frac{2\pi}{3},

and

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle<\arg\zeta(1-\omega)<\frac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle<\arg\zeta(1-\omega^{2})<\frac{5\pi}{6},

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle<\arg\zeta(\omega-\omega^{2})<\frac{7\pi}{6}.

Thus equality (
2.2.36
) holds if
Q^{(\infty)}_{1}
has the following structure

\displaystyle Q^{(\infty)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&*&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

∎

In a similar way, for
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
we consider constant matrices

(2.2.37)

\displaystyle Q^{(0)}_{n}:=\Psi^{(0)-1}_{n}\Psi^{(0)}_{n+\frac{1}{3}}.

Using the same reasoning as for (
2.2.20
) and (
2.2.22
), one can obtain the following symmetry relations for
Q^{(\infty,0)}_{n}
,

(2.2.38)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q_{n+1}^{(\infty)}&=d_{3}^{-1}[Q_{n}^{(\infty)}]^{T-1}d_{3},\\
Q_{n+1}^{(\infty)}&=d_{3}^{-1}Q_{n}^{(0)}d_{3},\\
Q_{n+1}^{(0)}&=d_{3}[Q_{n}^{(0)}]^{T-1}d_{3}^{-1},\end{aligned}

where
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
. Observe that from (
2.2.38
) it follows that
Q_{n}^{(0)}
has the same structure as
Q_{n+1}^{(\infty)}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For
n\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following cyclic symmetry relations of jump matrices
Q^{(\infty,0)}_{n}
:

(2.2.39)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{(\infty)}=\Pi^{-1}Q_{n+\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}\Pi,

(2.2.40)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{(0)}=\Pi^{-1}Q_{n+\frac{2}{3}}^{(0)}\Pi.

Proof.

From (
2.2.31
) we have

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{(\infty)}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{1}(\zeta)\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{1}{3}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=(d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{2}{3}}(\omega\zeta)\Pi)^{-1}d_{3}^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}(\omega\zeta)\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi^{-1}\Psi^{(\infty)-1}_{1\frac{2}{3}}(\omega\zeta)\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}(\omega\zeta)\Pi

\displaystyle=\Pi^{-1}Q_{1\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}\Pi

when
\zeta\in\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}
. Other cases can be proved in a similar way.
∎

Finally, the Stokes matrices
S_{1}^{(\infty,0)}
and
S_{2}^{(\infty,0)}
can be expressed in terms of
Q_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

For
n=1,2
, one has

(2.2.41)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{(\infty)}=Q_{n}^{(\infty)}Q_{n+\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}Q_{n+\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)},

(2.2.42)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{(0)}=Q_{n}^{(0)}Q_{n+\frac{1}{3}}^{(0)}Q_{n+\frac{2}{3}}^{(0)}.

Proof.

For
\zeta\in\Omega_{1}^{(\infty)}\cap\Omega_{2}^{(\infty)}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi^{(\infty)}_{2}

\displaystyle=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{2}{3}}Q_{1\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1\frac{1}{3}}Q_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}Q_{1\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}=\Psi^{(\infty)}_{1}Q_{1}^{(\infty)}Q_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}Q_{1\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}.

By the definition of the Stokes matrix
S_{1}^{(\infty)}
, we have that
S_{1}^{(\infty)}=Q_{1}^{(\infty)}Q_{1\frac{1}{3}}^{(\infty)}Q_{1\frac{2}{3}}^{(\infty)}
. Other cases can be proved in a similar way.
∎

2.2.6.1 Parametrization of
S^{(\infty)}_{n}

Let us parameterize

\displaystyle Q^{(\infty)}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then, using the anti-symmetry and inversion symmetry relations (

) and the cyclic symmetry of Jump matrices (
2.2.39
), (
2.2.40
), we have

3 Inverse monodromy problem and Asymptotics near
x=\infty

3.1  Riemann-Hilbert problem

Recall that
\Psi_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
were originally defined on the Stokes sector
\Omega_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
and then were extended to on the universal covering
\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
as was explained in the subsection
2.2.3
. In this section instead we consider
\Psi_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
as holomorphic functions on the projected regions
\Omega_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. In other words, these functions are sectionally holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{*}
whose jumps are given by the Stokes matrices
S_{n}^{(0,\infty)}
and the connection matrices
E_{n}
. And they are parametrized by certain real parameters called the monodromy data
\mathcal{M}
.

In the next subsection, we reconstruct the coefficient
A(\zeta)
in the equation (
2.2.1
) (and so the solution
w_{0}(x)
of (
1.0.4
)) from the given monodromy data.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\{s^{\mathbb{R}},\,y^{\mathbb{R}}\}\mapsto w_{0}(x,\,\mathcal{M}).

This inverse monodromy problem forms a
Riemann-Hilbert Problem
because
\Psi_{n}^{(\infty,0)}
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega_{n}^{(\infty,0)}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
whose jumps are given by either Stokes matrices or connection matrices and whose asymptotics can be given as formal solutions.

A Riemann-Hilbert problem (RHP), roughly speaking, is the problem of finding a sectionally analytic function in the complex plane having a prescribed jump across a curve. More precisely, suppose
\Gamma
is an oriented contour in the complex plane
\zeta
. The contour
\Gamma
might have self-intersections, and then it might have several connected components
\Omega_{k}
. The orientation defines, in the usual way the
+
and
-
sides of
\Gamma
. Suppose, in addition, that we are given a map
G
from
\Gamma
into a set of
N\times N
invertible matrices. A
Riemann-Hilbert problem
defined by the pair
(\Gamma,G)
, consists of finding piecewise analytic
N\times N
matrix-valued function
Y(\zeta)
with the following properties:

•

The function
Y|_{\Omega_{k}}
is analytic in
\Omega_{k}
and continuous in
\overline{\Omega}_{k}
.

•

The pointwise limit
Y_{-}(\zeta)
of
Y
from the minus side of
\Gamma
and the pointwise limit
Y_{+}(\zeta)
from the plus side of
\Gamma
, which exist and are continuous by the virtue of the previous condition, are related for
\zeta\in\Gamma
, by

Y_{+}(\zeta)=Y_{-}(\zeta)G(\zeta).

•

Y(\zeta)=I+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-1}),\;\text{as }\;\zeta\rightarrow\infty.

Later, in subsection
3.4.2
, using the RHP, we will obtain the asymptotics of the solution
w_{0}(x)
of the radial Toda equation when
x\rightarrow\infty
.

Finally, consider the connection matrices defined on a unit circle. Since

4 Asymptotics near
x=0
and Connection formulae

4.1  Asymptotic data of local solutions near
x=0

It is known
[
Nil09
]
that the radial Toda equation,

\displaystyle(w_{0})_{xx}+\frac{1}{x}(w_{0})_{x}=2e^{-2w_{0}}-2e^{4w_{0}},

admits a solution with asymptotic behavior near
x=0
of the form

(4.1.1)

\displaystyle w_{0}(x)=\gamma\ln x+\rho+o(1)

where
-\frac{1}{2}<\gamma<1
. The two real parameters
\{\gamma,\rho\}
in (
4.1.1
) are called the
asymptotic data
.

It was pointed out in
[
GIL20
]
that the method of loop group Iwasawa factorization produces a local solution of the 2D periodic Toda equations near
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
from any matrix of the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&0&p_{0}\\
p_{1}&0&0\\
0&p_{2}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

where each function
p_{i}=p_{i}(z)
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
z_{0}
. To obtain solutions near zero, we need to take
p_{i}(z)=c_{i}z^{k_{i}}
for certain real
k_{i}
and
c_{i}
, and this is exactly the same
holomorphic data
as for the tt*-Toda case. All solutions with asymptotics (
4.1.1
) can be obtained this way.

As we obtained the asymptotic formula for the solution near
x=\infty
as in Theorem
3.6
, we are now interested in finding connection formulae between two kinds of parameters: monodromy data
\{s^{\mathbb{R}},y^{\mathbb{R}}\}
and asymptotic data
\{\gamma,\rho\}
. And the key step is an introduction of a third kind of parameter, Iwasawa data
\{c_{i},k_{i}\}
, which connects the previous two data. This discussion is parallel to the one in
[
GIL20
]
.

4.1.1  Iwasawa Factorization

Consider the (possibly multi-valued) holomorphic matrix function
L(z,\lambda)
that satisfies the following ODE,

(4.1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{dL}{dz}=\frac{1}{\lambda}L\eta,\quad L\big|_{z=0}=I,

where

(4.1.3)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\eta=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&p_{0}\\
p_{1}&0&0\\
0&p_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle p_{i}=c_{i}z^{k_{i}},\;c_{i}>0,\;k_{i}>-1.

Consider the
twisted loop group

\Lambda SL_{3}^{t}\mathbb{C}
:

\displaystyle\Lambda SL_{3}^{t}\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle=\left\{M=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda^{-j}M_{j}\,\Bigg|\,\det M=1,\,\Delta M^{T-1}(-\lambda)\Delta=M(\lambda)\right\}

where

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&1&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

From (
4.1.3
) and the general theory of ODEs, it follows that
L\in\Lambda SL_{3}^{t}\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
L(z,\lambda)
be the element of
\Lambda SL_{3}^{t}\mathbb{C}
defined in (
4.1.2
). Then, there is a unique Iwasawa factorization of
L(z,\lambda)
:

\displaystyle L(z,\lambda)=L_{\mathbb{R}}(z,\overline{z},\lambda)L_{+}(z,\overline{z},\lambda)

where

•

\left[L_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\left(1/\overline{\lambda}\right)\right]^{-1}=L_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda)
,

•

L_{+}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}L_{j}(z,\overline{z})\lambda^{j}
, with
L_{0}=diag(b_{0},1,b_{0}^{-1}),\;b_{0}>0
, where
b_{0}
is smooth in a small neighbourhood of
0
.

•

The maps
z\mapsto L_{\mathbb{R}}(z),\;z\mapsto L_{+}(z)
are (possibly multi-valued) maps from a small neighborhood of
0

into
\Lambda SL_{3}^{t}\mathbb{C}
,

•

L_{\mathbb{R}}\big|_{z=0}=L_{+}\big|_{z=0}=I
.

Proof.

This is a special case of Theorem 8.1.1 of
[
PS86
]
. See also
[
McI94
]
.
∎

Remark
.

For convenience, we write only those arguments of
L(z,\bar{z},\lambda)
that are necessary.

Moreover, we have that

(4.1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}\frac{dL_{\mathbb{R}}}{dz}=a+\frac{1}{\lambda}A^{T}\\
L_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}\frac{dL_{\mathbb{R}}}{d\overline{z}}=-\overline{a}-\lambda\overline{A}\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle a=diag\left(\frac{d}{dz}\ln b_{0},0,-\frac{d}{dz}\ln b_{0}\right),

\displaystyle A^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&A_{0}\\
A_{1}&0&0\\
0&A_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle A_{0}=p_{0}b_{0}^{2},\;A_{1}=p_{1}b_{0}^{-1}.

Introduce

\displaystyle G(z,\overline{z}):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{|h_{0}|}{h_{0}}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&\frac{h_{0}}{|h_{0}|}\end{pmatrix},\quad h_{0}=p_{1}^{1/3}p_{0}^{-1/3}.

One can verify that

(4.1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dz}(L_{\mathbb{R}}G)=L_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(w_{z}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\nu W^{T}\right)\\
\frac{d}{d\overline{z}}(L_{\mathbb{R}}G)=L_{\mathbb{R}}G\left(-w_{\overline{z}}-\lambda\overline{\nu}W\right)\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle w=diag(w_{0},0,-w_{0}),\quad w_{0}=\ln\frac{b_{0}}{|h_{0}|},\quad\nu=p_{0}^{1/3}p_{1}^{2/3}=(c_{0}c_{1}^{2}z^{k_{0}+2k_{2}})^{\frac{1}{3}},

\displaystyle\quad W=\begin{pmatrix}0&e^{-w_{0}}&0\\
0&0&e^{-w_{0}}\\
e^{2w_{0}}&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Next, we introduce a new variable
t
by

\displaystyle t=(c_{0}c_{1}^{2})^{1/3}z^{N/3}\frac{3}{N},\quad N=k_{1}+2k_{2}+3.

Note that
\frac{dt}{dz}=\nu
. Then, define

(4.1.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}(t,\overline{t},\lambda):=(L_{\mathbb{R}}G)^{T}(z(t,\bar{t}),\bar{z}(t,\bar{t}),\lambda).

After this transformation, equation (
4.1.5
) becomes

(4.1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d\tilde{\Psi}}{dt}=\left(w_{t}+\frac{1}{\lambda}W\right)\tilde{\Psi}\\
\frac{d\tilde{\Psi}}{d\overline{t}}=\left(-w_{\overline{t}}-\lambda W^{T}\right)\tilde{\Psi},\end{cases}

which is exactly the Lax pair (
2.1.1
) for the (periodic, anti-symmetric) 2D Toda equation. This change of variables gives

\displaystyle w_{0}(t)=-\frac{k_{1}-k_{0}}{N}\ln|t|-\frac{k_{1}-k_{0}}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{3}(c_{0}c_{1})^{-1/3}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\ln\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\right)+o(1).

Comparing it with (
4.1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{k_{0}-k_{1}}{N},\quad\rho=\ln\left[\left(\frac{N}{3}\right)^{\gamma}c_{0}^{\frac{-\gamma+1}{3}}c_{1}^{\frac{-2\gamma-1}{3}}\right].

Let

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda):=(g(\lambda)L)^{T},\quad\hat{\Psi}(\lambda):=(g(\lambda)L_{\mathbb{R}}G)^{T}

where

\displaystyle g(\lambda)=\lambda^{m},\quad m=diag(-\gamma,0,\gamma),\quad\gamma=\frac{k_{0}-k_{1}}{N}.

Assuming that
\Phi(t,\bar{t},\lambda)
and
\hat{\Psi}(t,\bar{t},\lambda)
have radial symmetry, i.e., depend only on
x=|t|
and
\lambda
, we have

(4.1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d\Phi}{d\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left[-\frac{3}{N}\frac{z}{\lambda^{2}}\eta^{T}+\frac{1}{\lambda}m\right]\Phi,

(4.1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\Psi}}{d\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left[-\frac{t}{\lambda^{2}}W-\frac{1}{\lambda}xw_{x}-\overline{t}W^{T}\right]\hat{\Psi}.

Finally, consider
\lambda=\zeta t
, and define

\displaystyle\mathring{\Phi}(\zeta):=t^{-m}\Phi(\lambda)\big|_{\lambda=\zeta t},\quad\Psi(\zeta):=(g(\lambda)L_{\mathbb{R}}G)^{T}\big|_{\lambda=\zeta t}.

Then from (
4.1.7
) and (
4.1.9
) we have that

(4.1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{d\Psi}{d\zeta}=\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}W-\frac{x}{\zeta}w_{x}-x^{2}W^{T}\right)\Psi\\
\frac{d\Psi}{dx}=\left(-w_{x}-2x\zeta W^{T}\right)\Psi,\end{cases}

and from (
4.1.8
), using similar arguments as in
[
GIL20
]
, one can obtain

(4.1.11)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathring{\Phi}}{d\zeta}=\left[-\frac{1}{\zeta^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\kappa&0\\
0&0&\kappa\\
\kappa^{-2}&0&0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{\zeta}m\right]\mathring{\Phi},

where
\kappa=\left(\frac{3}{N}\right)^{\gamma}c_{1}^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{3}}c_{0}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{3}}=e^{-\rho}
.

Summarizing, we obtained the Lax pair (
4.1.10
) and the subsidiary ODE (
4.1.11
) from Iwasawa factorization and the connection between Iwasawa data
\{c_{i},k_{i}\}
and asymptotic data
\{\gamma,\rho\}
:

(4.1.12)

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{k_{0}-k_{1}}{N},\quad\kappa=\left(\frac{3}{N}\right)^{\gamma}c_{1}^{\frac{2\gamma+1}{3}}c_{0}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{3}}=e^{-\rho}.

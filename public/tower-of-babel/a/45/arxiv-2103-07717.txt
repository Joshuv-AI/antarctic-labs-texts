Statistical inference for ARTFIMA time series with stable innovations
Jinu Kabala, Farzad Sabzikar

Abstract
. Autoregressive tempered fractionally integrated moving average with stable innovations modifies the power-law kernel of the fractionally integrated time series model by adding an exponential tempering factor. The tempered time series is a stationary model that can exhibits semi-long-range dependence. This paper develops the basic theory of the tempered time series model, including dependence structure and parameter estimation.



1.
Introduction

An autoregressive fractionally integrated moving average (ARFIMA) with stable innovations introduced in
[
9
]
. The ARFIMA model with stable innovations can be constructed using the fractional difference (or integral) operator of
\alpha
-stable innovations. The ARFIMA model is useful in practice, especially for the dataset that shows long-range dependence,
see, for example,
[
5
]
and the reference therein. Since the ARFIMA model with stable innovations does not have a finite second moment, then the covariance function cannot describe the dependence structures. Instead, one can use another distance measure called co-difference, see
[
22
]
. Using this tool, Kokoszka and Taqqu investigated the asymptotic behavior of the difference of ARFIMA model with stable innovations for
\alpha\in(0,2)
, where
\alpha
is the index of stability of a stable distribution. Later on, the parameter estimation for ARFIMA model with stable innovations studied by

[
8
]
and
[
4
]
for stable ARTFIMA models with positive and negative memory parameter
d
, respectively.

This paper develops a model extension based on the tempered fractional difference operator introduced in
[
16
]
. The resulting stationary time series model, called autoregressive tempered fractionally integrated moving average (ARTFIMA) with stable innovations, is obtained by replacing the fractional integral operator with a tempered fractional integral operator has additional tempering parameter
\lambda>0
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
develops the stable ARTFIMA model, proves causality and invertibility. Unlike the ARFIMA model, the stable ARTFIMA model is stationary and invertible for any
d\in\mathbb{R}-{Z}_{-}
due to the presence of an exponential tempering function in its moving average representation. Section
3
computes the dependence structure of stable ARTFIMA, which can exhibit the heavy-tailed analog of semi-long range dependence. Section
4
presents the parameter estimation of stable ARTFIMA model, including the estimators’ consistency and asymptotic distributions. Sections
5
and
6
contain the simulation of stable ARTFIMA model and an application of this model to Solar-Flare dataset respectively. All the proofs of the paper results are collected in the appendix
7
.

2.
The ARTFIMA model with stable innovations

This section defines the ARTFIMA model with stable innovations and studies its essential properties, including causality and invertibility. First, we provide some basic definitions and assumptions.

The tempered fractionally integrated operator is defined by:

(2.1)

\Delta^{-d,\lambda}f(x)=(I-e^{-\lambda}B)^{-d}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)f(x-j),

where
d
is a positive non-integer value, the tempering parameter
\lambda>0
,
BX(t)=X(t-1)
is the shift operator, and

(2.2)

\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j):=\frac{\Gamma(j+d)}{\Gamma(d)\Gamma(j+1)}e^{-\lambda j}

with the gamma function
\Gamma(d)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{d-1}dx
. Applying the well-known property
\Gamma(d+1)=d\Gamma(d)
, we can extend (
2.1
) to non-integer values of
d<0
. In this case,
\Delta^{-d,\lambda}
is called tempered fractional difference operator with the positive exponent
-d
. If
\lambda=0
, then equation (
2.1
) reduces to the usual fractionally integrated operator. See
[
7
,
14
]
for more details.

A
S\alpha S
random variable
Z_{\alpha}=\{Z_{\alpha}(t)\}
has characteristic function

(2.3)

\mathbb{E}[e^{i\theta Z_{\alpha}(t)}]=e^{-\sigma^{\alpha}|\theta|^{\alpha}|t|},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R},0<\alpha\leq 2.

The parameter
\sigma
is called the scale parameter of
Z
. If
\alpha=2
,
Z
is Gaussian with
variance
2\sigma^{2}
. If
0<\alpha<2
, then
\mathbb{E}[|Z|^{p}]=\infty
for
p\geq\alpha
, and for
0<p<\alpha

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}[|Z|^{p}]=\mathbb{E}[|\sigma^{-1}Z|^{p}]\sigma^{p}=c(p,a)\sigma^{p},

where the constant
c(p,\alpha)
does not depend on the scale parameter
\sigma
.

Next, we provide two main assumptions that we will use for the rest of the paper.

Assumption 1:
In this paper, we assume
\{Z(t)\}
is a sequence of i.i.d
S\alpha S
random variables with the scale parameter
\sigma=1
.

Assumption 2:
Let
\Phi_{p}(z)
and
\Theta_{q}(z)
be polynomial with real coefficients defined by

\Phi_{p}(z)=1-\phi_{1}z-\phi_{2}z^{2}-\ldots-\phi_{p}z^{p}
, and
\Theta_{q}(z)=1+\theta_{1}z+\theta_{2}z^{2}+\ldots+\theta_{q}z^{q}
respectively. In this paper, we always assume the polynomials
\Phi_{p}
and
\Theta_{q}
have no common roots and the polynomial
\Phi_{p}
has no roots in the closed unit disk
\{z:|z|\leq 1\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The stochastic process
\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is said to be stable
autoregressive tempered fractional integrated moving average
, denoted by ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
, model if
\{X(t)\}_{t\in Z}
satisfies the tempered fractional difference equations

\Phi(B)X(t)=\Theta(B)\Delta^{-d,\lambda}Z(t),

where
\{Z(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
are i.i.d
S\alpha S
for
0<\alpha\leq 2
,
\Phi_{p},\Theta_{q}
are polynomials in Assumption 2,
d\in\mathbb{R}-\{-1,-2,\cdots\}
, and
\lambda>0
.

The next proposition shows the ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
with stable innovations is causal and invertible.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\{X(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
time series that satisfies Definition
2.1
. Then,

(a)

X(t)
has the moving average representation

(2.5)

X(t)=X_{p,d,\lambda,q}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{-d,\lambda}(j)Z(t-j),

where

a_{-d,\lambda}(j)=\sum_{s=0}^{j}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(s)b(j-s)

with
\Theta_{q}(z)\Phi_{p}(z)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b(j)z^{j}
for
|z|\leq 1
. Moreover, the series in (
2.5
) converges a.s. and in
L^{\nu}
for any
\nu<\alpha
.

(b)

X(t)
is invertible. That is

(2.6)

Z(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{d,\lambda}(j)X(t-j),

where

c_{d,\lambda}(j)=\sum_{s=0}^{j}\omega_{d,\lambda}(j)c(j-s)

with
\frac{\Phi_{p}(z)}{\Theta_{q}(z)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c(j)z^{j}
for
|z|\leq 1
. Moreover, the series in (
2.6
) converges a.s. and in
L^{\nu}
for any
\nu<\alpha
.

Remark 2.3
.

(a)

There is another version of an ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
that was introduced in
[
20
]
in the following sense: A discrete time stochastic process
X^{I\!I}_{p,d,\lambda,q}
is called ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
process with stable innovations
\{Z(t)\}
if

(2.7)

X^{I\!I}_{p,d,\lambda,q}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j}a_{-d}(j)Z(t-k),\qquad t\in\mathbb{Z},

where

a_{-d}(j)=\sum_{s=0}^{j}\omega_{-d}(s)b(j-s),\qquad k\geq 0,

where
\omega_{-d}(j)=\omega_{-d,0}(j)=\frac{\Gamma(k+d)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(d)}
.
The stochastic process
\{X^{I\!I}_{p,d,\lambda,q}\}
should be considered as ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
of the second kind.

(b)

We note that
\{X_{p,d,\lambda,q}\}
in (
2.5
) and
\{X^{I\!I}_{p,d,\lambda,q}\}
in (
2.7
) are two different stochastic processes since
a_{-d,\lambda}(j)\neq e^{-\lambda j}a_{-d}(j)
in general. However, these two processes are the same if
\Phi_{p}(z)=\Theta_{q}(z)=1
which means
X_{0,d,\lambda,0}=X^{I\!I}_{0,d,\lambda,0}
. In this paper, we focus on the
X_{p,d,\lambda,q}
.

3.
Dependence structure of ARTFIMA model with stable innovations

In this section, we investigate the dependence structure of the ARTFIMA model with stable innovations. The covariance function stops working in the presence of stable innovations since the finite second moment does not exist. Instead, we will use other distance measure which is called co-difference to describe the dependence structure. Let
X(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c(j)Z(n-j)
be a casual moving average with

S\alpha S
innovations
\{Z(t)\}
for
\alpha\in(0,2)
. A co-difference
\tau_{n}
of
X_{0}
and
X(n)
is defined by

\tau_{n}:=\tau(X(0),X(n))=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\big[|c(j)|^{\alpha}+|c(j+n)|^{\alpha}-|c(j)-c(j+n)|^{\alpha}\big].

Note that if
\alpha=2
, then
\tau(X(0),X(n))={\rm cov}(X(0),X(n))
. We refer the reader to
[
22
]
for more properties of the co-difference.

The next two theorems provide the asymptotic behavior of the stable ARTFIMA model’s co-difference when
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
respectively. Without loss of generality, we may consider stable ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
in the next two Theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X_{d,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)Z_{t-j}
be an ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
with
S\alpha S
innovations
\{Z(t)\}
. Suppose
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
d\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{N}_{-}
, and
\lambda>0
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tau_{d,\lambda}(n)}{e^{-\lambda\alpha n}n^{\alpha(d-1)}}=(\Gamma(d))^{-\alpha}(1-e^{-\lambda\alpha})^{-1}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X_{d,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{-d,\lambda}(j)Z_{t-j}
be an ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
with
S\alpha S
innovations
\{Z(t)\}
. Suppose
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
d\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{N}_{-}
, and
\lambda>0
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tau_{d,\lambda}(n)}{e^{-\lambda n}n^{d-1}}=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(d)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j}\omega_{-d,\lambda}^{\alpha-1}(j).

A stationary casual moving average representation
X(n)
with finite second moments innovations, i.e.
\mathbb{E}(Z^{2})<\infty
is called to have long memory if
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma(n)=\infty
where
\gamma(n)={\rm Cov}(X(0),X(n))
. In the absence of finite second moment innovation, i.e.
\mathbb{E}(Z^{2})=\infty
,
X(n)
is called to have long memory if

(3.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\tau(n)|=\infty

where
\tau(n)
is the co-difference defined by (
7.10
).

Corollary 3.3
.

The stable ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
does not have long memory in the sense of (
3.1
).

Remark 3.4
.

According to Corollary
3.3
, the stable ARTFIMA model is not long-range dependent. But, it does exhibit
semi-long range dependence
under the assumptions of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
. That is, for a sufficiently small value of
\lambda
, the sum in (
3.1
) is large because it tends to infinity as
\lambda
approaches to zero. Figure
1
illustrates the co-difference of the ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)

model. The differencing parameter
d
behaves similarly to that of ARFIMA
model. A higher value of
d
gives a stronger co-difference, so the co-difference falls off more
slowly with lag. A higher value of the tempering parameter
\lambda
makes the co-difference fall off
more rapidly.

Remark 3.5
.

The explicit form and asymptotic behavior of the covariance function for stationary ARTFIMA
X^{I\!I}_{0,d,\lambda,0}
when the innovations have the finite second moment obtained in
[
20
]
. In this case,

\rm Cov(X^{I\!I}_{0,d,\lambda,0}(0)X^{I\!I}_{0,d,\lambda,0}(k))\sim Ck^{d-1}e^{-\lambda k}
as
k\to\infty
. By Letting
\Phi_{p}=\Theta_{q}=0
and the innovations
Z(t)
have the finite second moment in ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
, Theorem
3.2
implies that

\tau_{d,\lambda}(n)\sim Ce^{-\lambda n}n^{d-1}
which is similar to the results in
[
20
]
. In other words, when
1<\alpha\leq 2
, the power law and exponential function involved in the asymptotic behavior of the co-difference and covariance function does not depend on
\alpha
.

model. The left panel shows the co-difference for

\lambda
. The right panel shows the codifference for

4.
Parameter Estimation

In this section, we prove the consistency and asymptotic distribution of a variant Whittle estimator for ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)

(p,q)
model was studied by Mikosch et. al
[
17
]

[
8

for ARFIMA
(p,d,q)
when

and
\alpha\in(1,2)

[
4

investigated the parameter estimation for ARFIMA
(p,d,q)
when

and
\alpha\in(2/3,2]

and it plays an important role to show the consistency of the parameters. Following the Whittle’s method in
[

8

4

where the

\begin{split}E:&=\Big\{{\bf\beta}:\phi_{p},\theta_{q}\neq 0,\Phi_{p}(z),\Theta_{q}(z)\neq 0\ {\rm for}\ |z|\leq 1,\\
&\ d\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{Z}_{-},\lambda>0\Big\}.\end{split}

We should note that

and
g_{p,d,0,q}
reduce to the transfer function in
[
17

and

5.
Simulation Results

In this section, we simulate ARTFIMA model with stable innovations. In order to simulate stable ARTFIMA time series, we use the Durbin-Levinson algorithm
[
10
]
in which the innovations are replaced by the stable simulations
[
6
]
.
We consider ARTFIMA
(0,0.3,0.045,0)
of length 10000 when
\alpha\in\{2.0,1.3,0.7\}
. The time series plot and the corresponding cumulative variance plot that illustrates infinite variance
[
1
]
are shown in Figures
2
,
3
and
4
. The Gaussian time series when
\alpha=2
lacks the jumps in the cumulative variance plot which is evident when

Proof of Theorem
3.1
:

Let

(7.10)

\begin{split}\tau_{d,\lambda}(n):&=\tau(X_{d,\lambda}(0),X_{d,\lambda}(n))\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}+|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\big]\\
&:=I_{1}+I_{2},\end{split}

where
I_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}
and
I_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}
. Since
\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)\sim\frac{1}{\Gamma(d)}e^{-\lambda j}j^{d-1}
as
j\to\infty
, we may work with the asymptotic form of
\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)
.
For any
j>0
,

(7.11)

\begin{split}e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}&=Ce^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}|e^{-\lambda\alpha(n+j)}(n+j)^{d-1}|\\
&=e^{-\lambda\alpha j}\Big(\frac{j+n}{n}\Big)^{\alpha(d-1)}\to e^{-\alpha\lambda j}\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.\end{split}

We note that

(7.12)

\begin{split}\sup_{n>1}\Big(\Big|e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(n+j)\Big|\Big)&=C\sup_{n>1}\Big(\Big|e^{-\lambda\alpha j}\Big(\frac{j+n}{n}\Big)^{\alpha(d-1)}\Big|\Big)\\
&\leq\begin{cases}e^{-\lambda\alpha j},&\alpha(d-1)\leq 0,\\
e^{-\lambda\alpha j}(1+j)^{\alpha(d-1)},&\alpha(d-1)>0,\end{cases}\end{split}

which belongs to
L^{1}(0,\infty)
. Now, using (
7.11
), (
7.12
), the dominated convergence theorem implies that

(7.13)

\begin{split}e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}I_{1}&=Ce^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+n)^{\alpha(d-1)}e^{-\lambda\alpha(j+n)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\to\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\alpha j}=\frac{1}{1-e^{-\lambda\alpha}},\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.\end{split}

Next, we show
e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}I_{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For each
j>0
,

(7.14)

\begin{split}&e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\Big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\Big]\\
&=\Big|\Big(\frac{j}{n}\Big)^{\alpha(d-1)}e^{-\lambda\alpha(j-n/\alpha)}\Big|-\Big|\Big(\frac{j}{n}\Big)^{(d-1)}e^{-\lambda(j-n)}-\Big(\frac{n+j}{n}\Big)^{(d-1)}e^{-\lambda j}\Big|^{\alpha}\\
&=:|a_{n}+b_{n}|^{\alpha}-|b_{n}|^{\alpha},\end{split}

where
a_{n}=-\Big(\frac{n+j}{n}\Big)^{(d-1)}e^{-\lambda j}
and
b_{n}=\Big(\frac{j}{n}\Big)^{(d-1)}e^{-\lambda(j-n)}
. It is obvious that

a_{n}\to-e^{-\lambda j}
and
b_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then using
|a_{n}+b_{n}|^{\alpha}-|b_{n}|^{\alpha}\to 0
as
n\to\infty

since
0<\alpha\leq 1
and hence

(7.15)

e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\Big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\Big]\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Now, using the fact that
\Big||a|^{\alpha}-|b|^{\alpha}\Big|\leq|a-b|^{\alpha}
, for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we have

(7.16)

\Big||j^{d-1}e^{-\lambda j}|^{\alpha}-|j^{d-1}e^{-\lambda j}-(n+j)^{d-1}e^{-\lambda(n+j)}|^{\alpha}\Big|\leq|(n+j)^{d-1}e^{-\lambda(n+j)}|^{\alpha}

and hence

(7.17)

\begin{split}&\sup_{n>1}\Big|e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\Big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\Big]\Big|\leq\sup_{n>1}\Big|e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)^{\alpha}\Big|\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\begin{cases}e^{-\lambda\alpha j},&\alpha(d-1)\leq 0,\\
e^{-\lambda\alpha j}(1+j)^{\alpha(d-1)},&\alpha(d-1)>0,\end{cases}\end{split}

From (
7.14
)-(
7.17
), the dominated convergence theorem implies that

(7.18)

e^{\lambda\alpha n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}I_{2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

Finally, (
7.13
) and (
7.18
) together yield

(7.19)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tau_{d,\lambda}(n)}{e^{-\lambda\alpha n}n^{\alpha(d-1)}}=\Gamma(d)^{-\alpha}(1-e^{-\lambda\alpha})^{-1}

for
0<\alpha<1
and
d\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{N}_{-}
. The proof is completed now.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
:

Recall from the proof of Theorem
3.1
that

I_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}
and
I_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}

\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)

3.1

j>0

(7.20)

since

which belongs to
L^{1}(0,\infty)
. Now, using (
7.20
), (
7.21

(7.22)

\begin{split}e^{\lambda n}n^{(d-1)}I_{1}&=Ce^{\lambda n}n^{(d-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+n)^{\alpha(d-1)}e^{-\lambda\alpha(j+n)}\\

e^{\lambda n}n^{-(d-1)}I_{2}\to\alpha e^{-\lambda\alpha j}j^{(d-1)(\alpha-1)}
as
n\to\infty
. For each

\begin{split}&e^{\lambda n}n^{-(d-1)}\Big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\Big]\\

(7.25)

1<\alpha\leq 2

(7.26)

\begin{split}&\sup_{n>1}\Big|e^{\lambda n}n^{-(d-1)}\Big[|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)|^{\alpha}-|\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j)-\omega_{-d,\lambda}(j+n)|^{\alpha}\Big]\Big|\leq\sup_{n>1}b_{n}^{\alpha}+\alpha\sup_{n\geq 1}a_{n}b_{n}^{\alpha-1}\\

which belongs to
L^{1}
. From (
7.23
)-(
7.26
), the dominated convergence theorem implies that

(7.27)

e^{\lambda n}n^{-\alpha(d-1)}I_{2}\to\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\alpha j}j^{(d-1)(\alpha-1)}\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

Finally, (
7.22
) and (
7.27
) together yield

(7.28)

1<\alpha<2
and
d\in\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{N}_{-}

Proof of Lemma
4.5
:

Let
|z|<1
and
\beta\in E
. Recall from the proof of Proposition
2.2
,

\begin{split}A_{\beta}(z)&=A_{p,d,\lambda,q}(z)=(1-e^{-\lambda}z)^{-d}\ {\Theta_{q}(z)}{\Phi_{p}(z)}^{-1}\\
&=\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\omega_{-d,\lambda}(i)z^{i}\Big)\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}b(s)z^{s}\Big)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{-d,\lambda}(j)z^{j},\end{split}

where
a_{p,-d,\lambda,q}(j)=a_{-d,\lambda}(j)
is given by (
7.1
). We also defined,

\begin{split}A_{\beta}(z)&=C_{p,d,\lambda,q}(z)=(1-e^{-\lambda}z)^{d}\ \frac{\Phi_{p}(z)}{\Theta_{q}(z)}\\
&=\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\omega_{d,\lambda}(i)z^{i}\Big)\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}c(s)z^{s}\Big)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{d,\lambda}(j)z^{j},\end{split}

where
c_{p,d,\lambda,q}(j)=c_{d,\lambda}(j)
is given by (
7.8
). Let
\xi(n)
be a sequence of Gaussian random variables with zero mean and unit variance, and
X_{\beta_{1}}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{-d_{1},\lambda_{1}}(j)\xi(n-j)
be a Gaussian ARTFIMA
(p_{1},d_{1},\lambda_{1},q_{1})
. By mimicking a similar argument in
[
3
, Section 13.2]
, it can be shown that

{\rm Var}\big(X_{\beta_{1}}(n+1)-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\theta(j)}X_{\beta_{1}}(n-j)\big)
will be minimized if and only if
\theta(j)=-c_{p,d_{1},\lambda_{1},q}(j)
and the smallest value of the variance is one. Now, for
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
, we have
C_{\beta_{1}}(z)\neq C_{\beta_{2}}(z)
, and hence
{\rm Var}\big(X_{\beta_{1}}(n+1)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{c_{p,d_{1},\lambda_{1},q}(j)}X_{\beta_{1}}(n-j)\big)>1
. Therefore

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{g_{p_{1},d_{1},\lambda_{1},q_{1}}(\omega,\beta_{1})}{g_{p_{2},d_{2},\lambda_{2},q_{2}}(\omega,\beta_{2})}&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big|\frac{A_{d_{1},\lambda_{1}}(e^{-i\omega})}{A_{d_{2},\lambda_{2}}(e^{-i\omega})}\Big|^{2}\ d\omega\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big|C_{d_{2},\lambda_{2}}(e^{-i\omega})A_{d_{1},\lambda_{1}}(e^{-i\omega})\Big|^{2}\ d\omega\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big|\sum_{s=0}^{j}c_{d_{2},\lambda}(s)a_{-d_{1},\lambda_{1}}(j-s)\Big|^{2}\\
&={\rm Var}\big(X_{\beta_{1}}(n+1)-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\theta(j)}X_{\beta_{1}}(n-j)\big)>1\end{split}

and this completes the proof.

\Box

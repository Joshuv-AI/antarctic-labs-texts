Two Matrix Weighted Inequalities for Commutators with Fractional Integral Operators
Roy Cardenas, Joshua Isralowitz

Abstract
. In this paper we prove two matrix weighted norm inequalities for the commutator of a fractional integral operator and multiplication by a matrix symbol. More precisely, we extend the recent results of the second author, Pott, and Treil on two matrix weighted norm inequalities for commutators of Calderon-Zygmund operators and multiplication by a matrix symbol to the fractional integral operator setting. In particular, we completely extend the fractional Bloom theory of Holmes, Rahm, and Spencer to the two matrix weighted setting with a matrix symbol.

1.
Introduction

Let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
L^{p}(w)
be the standard weighted Lebesgue space with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Furthermore, let A
p,q
for
p,q>1
be the Muckenhoupt class of weights
w
satisfying

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}w(x)\,dx\right)\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty

where
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}
is the unweighted average over
Q
(which will also occasionally be denoted by
m_{Q}
). When
p=q
we write
A_{p}:=A_{p,p}
as usual.

Given a weight
\nu
, we say
b\in\text{BMO}_{\nu}
if

\|b\|_{\text{BMO}_{\nu}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}|b(x)-m_{Q}b|\,dx<\infty

(where
\nu(Q)=\int_{Q}\nu
) so that clearly

\text{BMO}=\text{BMO}_{\nu}
when
\nu\equiv 1
. Further, given a linear operator
T,
define the commutator
[M_{b},T]=M_{b}T-TM_{b}
with
M_{b}
being multiplication by
b
. In the papers
[
HLW16
,
HLW17
]
the authors extended earlier work of S. Bloom
[
Blo85
]
and proved that if
u,v\in\text{A}_{p}
and
T
is any Calderón-Zygmund operator (CZO) then

\|[M_{b},T]\|_{L^{p}(u)\rightarrow L^{p}(v)}\lesssim\|b\|_{\text{BMO}_{\nu}}

(1.1)

where
\nu=(uv^{-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}
and it was proved in
[
HLW17
]
that if
R_{s}
is the
s^{\text{th}}
Riesz transform then

\|b\|_{\text{BMO}_{\nu}}\lesssim\max_{1\leq s\leq d}\|[M_{b},R_{s}]\|_{L^{p}(u)\rightarrow L^{p}(v)}.

(1.2)

Furthermore, let
I_{\alpha}
be the fractional integral operator defined by the formula

I_{\alpha}{f}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{{f}(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{d-\alpha}}\dee\!y,\text{ for }0<\alpha<d.

It was proved in
[
HRS16
]
that if
0<\alpha<d
and
\alpha/d+1/q=1/p
, if
u,v\in A_{p,q}
, and if
\nu=u^{\frac{1}{q}}v^{-\frac{1}{q}}
then

\|[M_{b},I_{\alpha}]\|_{L^{p}(u^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{p}(v)}\approx\|b\|_{\text{BMO}(\nu)}

(1.3)

On the other hand, matrix weighted extensions and generalizations of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) that surprisingly hold for two arbitrary matrix weights (and provided new results even in the scalar
p=2
setting of a single scalar weight) were proved in
[
IPT
]
, and it is the purpose of this paper to extend the results of
[
IPT
]
to the fractional setting, providing matrix weighted extensions of (
1.3
) that hold for two arbitrary matrix weights. Note that for the rest of this paper we will assume that
0<\alpha<d
and
\alpha/d+1/q=1/p
.

In particular, for any linear operator
T
acting on scalar valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can canonically extend
T
to act on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
valued functions
\vec{f}
by the formula
T\vec{f}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(T\left\langle\vec{f},\vec{e}_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\right)\vec{e}_{j}
where
\{\vec{e}_{j}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(and note that this is easily seen to be independent of the orthonormal basis chosen.) Let
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}
be an
n\times n
matrix weight (a positive definite a.e.
\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}
valued function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and let
L^{p}(W)
be the space of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
valued functions
\vec{f}
such that

\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{p}(W)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Furthermore, for
p,q>1
we will say that a matrix weight
W
is a matrix A
p,q
weight (see
[
IM19
]
) if it satisfies

\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\,dx<\infty

(1.4)

and when
p=q
we say
W
is a matrix A
p
weight (see
[
Rou03
]
).

Now for scalar weights
u
and
v
, notice that by multiple uses of the A
q
property and Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle m_{Q}\nu\approx(m_{Q}u)^{\frac{1}{q}}(m_{Q}v^{-\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\approx(m_{Q}u)^{\frac{1}{q}}(m_{Q}v)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\approx(m_{Q}u^{\frac{1}{q}})(m_{Q}v^{\frac{1}{q}})^{-1}.

Thus,
b\in\text{BMO}_{\nu}
when
u
and
v
are A
q
weights if and only if

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}(m_{Q}v^{\frac{1}{q}})(m_{Q}u^{\frac{1}{q}})^{-1}|b(x)-m_{Q}b|\,dx<\infty,

which is a condition that easily extends to the matrix weighted setting, noting that
A_{p,q}\subset A_{q}
when
q>p
, since then
q^{\prime}<p^{\prime}
and so Hölder’s inequality gives us

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\|^{q^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,dx\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\,dx.

Namely, if
U,V
are
n\times n
matrix A
p,q
weights, then we define
{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}
to be the space of
n\times n
locally integrable matrix functions
B
where

\|B\|_{{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\|(m_{Q}V^{\frac{1}{q}})(B(x)-m_{Q}B)(m_{Q}U^{\frac{1}{q}})^{-1}\|\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty

so that
\|b\|_{{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}\approx\|b\|_{\text{BMO}{\nu}}
if
U,V
are scalar weights and
b
is a scalar function. Note that the
{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}
condition is much more naturally defined in terms of reducing matrices, which will be discussed in Section
3
.

We will need a definition before we state our first result. We say that a linear operator
R
acting on scalar functions is a fractional lower bound operator if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
n\times n
matrix weight
W
we have

\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|T\|_{L^{p}(W^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(W)}

(1.5)

with the bound independent of
W
(but not necessarily independent of
n
), and
\|T\|_{L^{p}(W^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(W)}<\infty
if
W
is a matrix A
p,q
weight.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T
be any linear operator acting on scalar valued functions where its canonical
\mathbb{C}^{n}
valued extension is bounded from
L^{p}(W^{\frac{p}{q}})
to
L^{q}(W)
for all
n\times n
matrix A
p,q
weights
W
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with bound depending on
T,n,d,p
, and
\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}
(which is known to be true for fractional integral operators, see
[
IM19
, Theorem 1.4]
.) If
U,V
are
m\times m
matrix A
p
weights and
B
is an
m\times m
locally integrable matrix function for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\|[M_{B},T]\|_{L^{p}(U^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(V)}\lesssim\|B\|_{{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}

(1.6)

with bounds depending on
T,m,d,p,\left\|U\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}
and
\left\|V\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}
.

Furthermore, for any fractional lower bound operator
T
we have the lower bound estimate

\|B\|_{{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}\lesssim\|[M_{B},T]\|_{L^{p}(U^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(V)}

(1.7)

Like in
[
IPT
]
, we will use matrix weighted arguments inspired by
[
GPTV04
]
in the next section to prove Theorem
1.1
in terms of a weighted BMO quantity
\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}
that is equivalent to
\|B\|_{{{\text{BMO}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}
when
U
and
V
are matrix A
p,q
weights (see Theorem
3.1
) but is much more natural for more arbitrary matrix weights
U
and
V
. More precisely, define

\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}^{q}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
Q\text{ is a cube}\end{subarray}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left\|V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)(B(x)-B(y))U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\,dx.

(1.8)

We will then give relatively short proofs of the following two results in Section
2
.

Lemma 1.2

Let
T
be any linear operator defined on scalar valued functions where its canonical
\mathbb{C}^{n}
valued extension
T
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies

\|T\|_{L^{p}(W^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(W)}\leq\phi(\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}})

for some positive increasing function
\phi
(possibly depending on
T,d,n,p,q
.) If

are

weights and

is a locally integrable

Lemma 1.3

If
T
is any fractional lower bound operator then for any
m\times m
matrix A
p,q
weights
U,V
and an
m\times m
matrix symbol
B
we have

\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}\lesssim\|[M_{B},T]\|_{L^{p}(U^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(V)}

where the bound depends possibly on
n,p,d
and
T
but is independent of
U
and
V
.

As in
[
IPT
]
, we will prove that the fractional integral operator is a fractional lower bound operator in Section

by utilizing the Schur multiplier/Wiener algebra ideas from
[
LT13
]
, and thus recover (
1.7
). These arguments will in fact prove the following (see
[
IPT
]
for an analogous result with respect to the Riesz transforms). Here, for ease of notation, we set
U^{\prime}=U^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}
and
V^{\prime}=V^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
U
and
V
be any (not necessarily A
p
) matrix weights. If
B
is any locally integrable
m\times m
matrix valued function then

\max\left\{\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}},\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{U^{\prime},V^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}}\right\}\lesssim\|[M_{B},I_{\alpha}]\|_{L^{p}(U^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(V)}.

(1.9)

Note that the two quantities
\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}
and
\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{U^{\prime},V^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}}
are equivalent when
U,V\in\text{A}_{p,q}
(which will be proved in Section
4
) and in general should be thought of as “dual” matrix weighted BMO quantities. Finally, we will show that an Orlicz “bumped” version of these conditions are sufficient for the general two matrix weighted boundedness of fractional integral operators. In particular, we will prove the following result in Section
5
(see
[
IPT
]
for an analogous result for Calderon-Zygmund operators)

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
U
and
V
be any
m\times m
matrix weights, and suppose that
C
and
D
are Young
functions with
\bar{D}\in B_{p,q}
and
\bar{C}\in B_{q^{\prime}}
.

Then

\|[M_{B},I_{\alpha}]\|_{L^{p}(U^{\frac{p}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q}(V)}\lesssim\min\{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\}

where

\begin{split}\kappa_{1}&=\sup_{Q}\|\|V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)(B(x)-B(y))U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\|_{C_{x},Q}\|_{D_{y},Q}\\
\kappa_{2}&=\sup_{Q}\|\|V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)(B(x)-B(y))U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\|_{D_{y},Q}\|_{C_{x},Q}\end{split}

We refer the reader to Section
5.2
in
[
CUIM18
]
for the standard Orlicz space related definitions used in the statement of Proposition
1.5
.

It is important to emphasize that Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
1.5
are new, even in the scalar setting of a single weight.

2.
Intermediate fractional upper and lower bounds

As stated in the introduction, we will give short proofs of Lemma
1.2
and Lemma
1.3
in this section, beginning with Lemma
1.2
.

2.1.
Proof of Lemma
1.2

Define the
2\times 2
block matrix-valued function
\Phi=\Phi_{U,V,B}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow M_{2m}(\mathbb{C})
by

\Phi(x):=\begin{pmatrix}V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)&0\\
0&U^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&B(x)\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)&V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)B(x)\\
0&U^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\end{pmatrix},

so that for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\Phi^{-1}(y)=\begin{pmatrix}V^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)&-B(y)U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\\
0&U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\end{pmatrix}.

Thus, we have

\Phi T\Phi^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{\frac{1}{q}}TV^{-\frac{1}{q}}&V^{\frac{1}{q}}[M_{B},T]U^{-\frac{1}{q}}\\
0&U^{\frac{1}{q}}TU^{-\frac{1}{q}}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
W:=\left(\Phi^{*}\Phi\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}
is a matrix weight and, by polar decomposition, there exists a unitary a.e. matrix function
\mathscr{U}
such that
\Phi(x)=\mathscr{U}(x)W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)
. This gives us that

\displaystyle\left|\left|T\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(W^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(W)}\phantom{A}=\left|\left|W^{\frac{1}{q}}TW^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}

\displaystyle\phantom{A}=\left|\left|\Phi T\Phi^{-1}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\phantom{A}\approx\max\left\{\left|\left|V^{\frac{1}{q}}TV^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}},\left|\left|V^{\frac{1}{q}}[M_{B},T]U^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}},\left|\left|U^{\frac{1}{q}}TU^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{A}\geq\left|\left|V^{\frac{1}{q}}[M_{B},T]U^{-\frac{1}{q}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\phantom{A}=\left|\left|[M_{B},T]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}

Using the assumption in Lemma
1.2
that
\left|\left|T\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(W^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(W)}\lesssim\phi(\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}})
we get that

\left|\left|[M_{B},T]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}\lesssim\phi(\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}).

(2.1)

Unravelling the
A_{p,q}
condition for
W
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}=\sup_{Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left|\left|W^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\right|\right|^{p^{\prime}}\dee\!y\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\dee\!x

\displaystyle\phantom{A}=\sup_{Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left|\left|\Phi(x)\Phi^{-1}(y)\right|\right|^{p^{\prime}}\dee\!y\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\dee\!x

\displaystyle\phantom{A}\leq 3^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left|\left|U\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\left|\left|V\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\sup_{Q}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}\left|\left|V^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)\left(B(x)-B(y)\right)U^{-\frac{1}{q}}(y)\right|\right|^{p^{\prime}}\dee\!y\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\dee\!x\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{A}=3^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left|\left|U\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\left|\left|V\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\left|\left|B\right|\right|^{q}_{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}^{p,q}_{V,U}}\right)

and thus

\left|\left|[M_{B},I_{\alpha}]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}\lesssim\phi\left(3^{\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left|\left|U\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\left|\left|V\right|\right|_{A_{p,q}}+\left|\left|B\right|\right|^{q}_{\widetilde{BMO}^{p,q}_{V,U}}\right)\right).

Re-scaling with
B
replaced by
B\left|\left|B\right|\right|^{-1}_{\widetilde{BMO}^{p,q}_{V,U}}
now completes the proof.

2.2.
Proof of Lemma
1.3

We now prove Lemma
1.3
. Let
W
and
\Phi
be defined as in the previous subsection, so that

\displaystyle\left(\left\|U\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}+\left\|V\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}+\|B\|_{{{\widetilde{\text{BMO}}}_{V,U}^{p,q}}}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\approx\left\|W\right\|_{\text{A}_{p,q}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\left|\left|T\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(W^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(W)}

\displaystyle\phantom{W}\lesssim\left|\left|\left[M_{B},T\right]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}+\left|\left|T\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(U)}+\left|\left|T\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(V^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}.

Clearly we may assume that
\left|\left|\left[M_{B},T\right]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}<\infty
and so by assumption all quantities above are finite. Re-scalling
B\mapsto rB
for

, dividing by

, and taking

, we obtain

\left|\left|B\right|\right|_{\widetilde{BMO}^{p,q}_{V,U}}\lesssim\left|\left|\left[M_{B},I_{\alpha}\right]\right|\right|_{L^{p}\left(U^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)\longrightarrow L^{q}(V)}.

which is the desired lower bound.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We now prove Theorem
1.1
(assuming that
I_{\alpha}

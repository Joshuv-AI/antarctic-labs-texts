Abstract
. We classify connected graphs G G whose binomial edge ideal is Gorenstein. The proof uses methods in prime characteristic.

Introduction

Our main goal is to present homological properties of binomial edge ideals. These ideals are a generalization of determinantal ideals and ideals generated by adjacent 2-minors in a
2\times n
generic matrix. The binomial edge ideals were introduced by Herzog, Hibi, Hreindóttir, Kahle, and Rauh
[
HHH+10
]
and by Ohtani
[
Oht11
]
independently and about the same time.

Let
G
a simple graph (i.e.
G
has no loops and multiple edges) on the vertex set
V(G)=[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
with edge set
E(G)
. Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be the polynomial ring on
2n
variables over a field
K
. The
binomial edge ideal

\mathcal{J}_{G}
of
G
is

\mathcal{J}_{G}:=(f_{ij}\,|\,\{i,j\}\in E(G)\,i<i),

where
f_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
for
i<j
.
The properties of binomial edge ideals have been studied vastly by many researchers, for instance:

•

Cohen-Macaulayness
[

,
BMS18
,
EHH11
,
HHH+10
,
KSM15
,
MR18
,
RR14
,
Rin13
,
Rin19
,
Zaf12
]
,

•

Betti numbers and regularity
[
Bas16
,
CDI16
,
dAH18
,
EZ15
,
KSM14
,
KSM16
,
MM13
,

,
SMK18
,

]
,

•

Gröbner bases
[
BBS17
,
CR11
,
HHH+10
,
Oht11
]
.

Herzog et al. characterized the graphs whose binomial edge ideal has quadratic Gröbner base.
For a graph
G
, the generators
f_{ij}
of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
form a quadratic Gröbner basis if and only if for all edges
\{i,j\}
and
\{k,l\}
with
i<j
and
k<l
one has
\{j,l\}\in E(G)
if
i=k
, and
\{i,k\}\in E(G)
if
j=l

[
HHH+10
, Theorem 1.1]
.
A graph
G
that satisfies the aforementioned condition is called
closed with respect to the given labelling of the vertices
. We say that a graph
G
is
closed
if there exists a labeling of its vertices such that
G
is closed with respect to that labeling.

Ene, Herzog and Hibi proved that if
G
is a closed graph, then
S/\mathcal{J}_{G}
is Gorenstein if and only if
G
is a path
[
EHH11
, Corollary 3.4]
. This motivated us to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a connected graph such that
S/\mathcal{J}_{G}
is Gorenstein. Then,
G
is a path.

This is achieved through the applications of methods in prime characteristic, in particular,
F
-pure thresholds
[
TW04
]
. Along the way we compute the
F
-pure threshold of binomial edge ideals associated to closed graphs and show that the
F
-pure threshold of the binomial edge ideal coincide with the
F
-pure threshold of the initial ideal of the binomial edge ideal for closed graphs. This follows the line of research which establish that the binomial edge ideal and its initial ideal have similar properties for closed graphs
[
dAH18
,
EHH11
]
.

1.
Background

In this section we recall some notions and known facts regarding binomial edge ideals. In this paper, all graphs are simple.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
[n]
, and let
i
and
j
be two vertices of
G
with
i<j
.
A path
i=i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}=j
from
i
to
j

is called
admissible
, if

(i)

i_{k}\neq i_{\ell}
for
k\neq\ell
;

(ii)

for each
k=1,\ldots,r-1
one has either
i_{k}<i
or
i_{k}>j
;

(iii)

for any proper subset
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\}
of
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r-1}\}
, the sequence
i,j_{1},\ldots,j_{s},j
is not a path.

Given an admissible path

\pi:i=i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}=j

from
i
to
j
, where
i<j
, we associate the monomial

u_{\pi}=\left(\prod_{i_{k}>j}x_{i_{k}}\right)\left(\prod_{i_{\ell}<i}y_{i_{\ell}}\right).

We now recall a characterization of the Gröbner basis for binomial edge ideals.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
HHH+10
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
[n]
, and let
<
be the lexicographic order on
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
induced by
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{1}>y_{2}>\cdots>y_{n}
. Then the set of binomials

\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{i<j}\,\{\,u_{\pi}f_{ij}\,:\;\text{$\pi$ is an admissible path from $i$ to $j$}\,\}

is a reduced Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Since
\mathcal{J}_{G}
has a square-free Gröbner basis, we conclude that
\mathcal{J}_{G}
is a radical ideal
[
HHH+10
, Corollary 2.2]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Every monomial
u_{\pi}x_{i}y_{j}
such that
\pi
is an admissible path from
i
to
j
is the initial term of an element of
\mathcal{G}
. From the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is a reduced Gröbner basis, we conclude that the set
\operatorname{In}(\mathcal{G})
is the minimal generating set of the ideal
\operatorname{In}(\mathcal{J}_{G})
.

The binomial edge ideal of a path
P_{n}
with
n
vertices is a complete intersection having
n-1
generators of degree 2 and
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{P_{n}})=n-1

[
EZ15
]
. The following results state that regularity
n-1
implies that the graph is a path.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
MM13
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on the set
[n]
of vertices. Then

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{G})\leq n-1

Theorem 1.5

(
[
KSM16
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
which is not a path. Then
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{G})\leq n-2

We now recall the Plücker relation for binomials. This plays an important role while we study
the
F
-pure threshold for
S/\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Proposition 1.6

(Plücker relation)
.

Let
i<j<k<l
be positive integers. Then,

f_{ij}f_{kl}-f_{ik}f_{jl}+f_{il}f_{jk}=0.

2.
F-pure thresholds of graded rings

In this section we introduce the basic definitions for methods in prime characteristic. We also list some properties for the
F
-pure threshold of standard graded rings. We consider the
F
-pure threshold with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, the maximal graded ideal. This invariant was introduced by Takagi and Watanabe
[
TW04
]
. The
F
-pure threshold is related to the log-canonical threshold
[
BFS13
,
TW04
]
, and roughly speaking measures the asymptotic splitting order of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring of prime characteristic
p
. We say that
R
is
F
-finite
if it is finitely generated
R
-module via the action induced by the Frobenius endomorphism

\displaystyle F:R

\displaystyle\rightarrow R

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\mapsto r^{p}.

For
e\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F^{e}:R\rightarrow R
the
e
-th iteration of the Frobenius endomorphism on
R
. If
R
is reduced,
R^{1/p^{e}}
denotes the ring of
p^{e}
-th roots of
R
. We often identify
F^{e}
with the inclusion
R\subseteq R^{1/p^{e}}
. In this case,
R
is
F
-finite if and only if
R^{1/p}
is a finitely generated
R
-module. For a standard graded
K
-algebra
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
,
R
is
F
-finite if and only if
K
is
F
-finite, that is, if and only if
[K:K^{p}]<\infty
. A ring
R
is called
F
-pure
if
F
is a pure homomorphism of
R
-modules, that is
F\otimes 1:R\otimes M\rightarrow R\otimes M
is injective for all
R
-modules
M
. We say that
R
is called
F
-split
if
F
is a split monomorphism.
If
R
is an
F
-finite ring,
R
is
F
-pure if and only
R
is
F
-split
[
HR76
, Corollary 5.3]
.
Let
J\subseteq R
an ideal we write
J^{[p]}:=(x^{p}|\,x\in J)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Fedder’s Criterion for graded rings
[
Fed83
, Theorem 1.12]
)
.

Let
K
be a field of prime characteristic
p
, and
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over
K
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the irrelevant maximal ideal of
S
, and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be a homogeneous ideal of
S
. Then
S/I
is
F
-pure if
I^{[p]}:I\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
TW04
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be a standard graded
K
-algebra which is
F
-finite and
F
-pure, and let
I\subseteq R
be a homogeneous ideal. For a real number
\lambda\geq 0
, we say that
(R,I^{\lambda})
is
F
-pure
if for every
e\gg 0
, there exists an element
f\in I^{\lfloor(p^{e}-1)\lambda\rfloor}
such that the inclusion of
R
-modules
f^{1/p^{e}}R\subseteq R^{1/p^{e}}
splits.
The
F
-pure threshold of
I
is defined by

\operatorname{fpt}(I):=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}|(R,I^{\lambda})\text{ is }F\text{ pure}\}.

If
I=\mathfrak{m}
, we denote the
F
-pure threshold by
\operatorname{fpt}(R)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be a standard graded
K
-algebra which is
F
-finite and
F
-pure, and
J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be and ideal. We define

I_{e}(R):=\{r\in R|\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})\in\mathfrak{m}\text{ for every }\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(R^{1/p^{e}},R)\}

and

b_{J}(p^{e}):=\max\{r|J^{r}\nsubseteq I_{e}(R)\}.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
DSNnB18
, Proposition 3.10]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
be a standard graded
K
-algebra which is
F
-finite and
F
-pure. Let
J\subseteq R
be a homogeneous ideal. Then

\operatorname{fpt}(J)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{b_{J}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
DSNnB18
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over an
F
-finite field
K
. Let
\mathfrak{n}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
denote the maximal homogeneus ideal. Let
I\subseteq S
be an homogeneous ideal such that
R:=S/I
is an
F
-pure ring, and let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}R
. Then,

\min\left\{s\in\mathbb{N}\middle|\left[\frac{I^{[p^{e}]}:I+\mathfrak{n}^{[p^{e}]}}{\mathfrak{n}^{[p^{e}]}}\right]_{s}\neq 0\right\}=n(p^{e}-1)-b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e}).

Theorem 2.7

(
[
DSNnB18
, Theorem 7.3]
)
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over an
F
-finite field
K
of prime characteristic
p
. Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal such that
R=S/I
is
F
-pure and Gorenstein. Then,

\operatorname{reg}_{S}(R)=\dim(R)-\operatorname{fpt}(R).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
the ring of polynomials in
n+m
variables over a field
K
, and let
J=\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\subseteq R
be an ideal such that
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal in the variables
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\mathfrak{b}
is an ideal in the variables
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
. Then,

\operatorname{fpt}(S/J)=\operatorname{fpt}(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\mathfrak{a})+\operatorname{fpt}(K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/\mathfrak{b}).

Proof.

We note that

S/J\cong K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\mathfrak{a}\otimes K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/\mathfrak{b}

by properties of tensor product. We now prove that
b_{J}(p^{e})=b_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})+b_{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e})
. Set
\gamma=b_{J}(p^{e})
. By the definition of
b_{J}(p^{e})
, we have that
J^{\gamma}\nsubseteq I_{e}(S)
. This means that there exists a generator
r
of
J^{\gamma}
and also a morphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(S^{1/p^{e}},S)
such that
\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})\notin\mathfrak{m}
. This generator can be written as
r=a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{s}}b_{j_{1}},\ldots,b_{j_{t}}
for some generators
\{a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{s}}\}
of
\mathfrak{a}
and some generators
\{b_{j_{1}},\ldots,b_{j_{t}}\}
of
\mathfrak{b}
with
\gamma=s+j
. This element correspond by

(2.0.1)

S\cong K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\otimes K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]

to a element
\alpha\otimes\beta
in the tensor product with
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{s}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{b}^{t}
. We have the next composition of morphisms:

\displaystyle K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{1/p^{e}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle S^{1/p^{e}}

\displaystyle\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow

\displaystyle K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

\displaystyle\alpha^{1/p^{e}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle r^{1/p^{e}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})},

where the leftmost morphism send
x^{1/p^{e}}\mapsto x^{1/p^{e}}\otimes\beta^{1/p^{e}}
and the rightmost is the natural projection. We have an element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{s}
and a morphism

\varphi*\in\operatorname{Hom}(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{1/p^{e}},K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])

(the composition of the morphisms above). Then,
\varphi*
sends
\alpha^{1/p^{e}}
to
\overline{\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})}
. We note that
\overline{\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})}
is not in the maximal ideal of
K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, because
\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})\notin\mathfrak{m}
. Now we note that

b_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})=\max(r|\mathfrak{a}^{r}\nsubseteq I_{e}(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]))\geq s.

By symmetric argument, we obtain that

b_{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e})=\max(r|\mathfrak{b}^{r}\nsubseteq I_{e}(K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]))\geq t

proving that
b_{J}(p^{e})\leq b_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})+b_{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e})
. For the reverse inequality let
s=b_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})
,
t=b_{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e})
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{s}
,
\beta\in\mathfrak{b}^{t}
,

\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{1/p^{e}},K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])

and

\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}(K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]^{1/p^{e}},K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}])

such that
\varphi(\alpha^{1/p^{e}})
and
\psi(\beta^{1/p^{e}})
are not in their respective maximal ideals. Then, by equation
2.0.1
,
\alpha\otimes\beta
corresponds to an element
\alpha\beta\in J^{t+s}
. Since

(\varphi\otimes\psi)(\alpha^{1/p^{e}}\otimes\beta^{1/p^{e}})=\varphi(\alpha^{1/p^{e}})\psi(\beta^{1/p^{e}})\notin\mathfrak{m}.

Then
b_{J}(p^{e})\geq t+s
.
∎

Theorem 2.9

(
[
DSNnB18
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},K)
a standard graded
K
-algebra wich is
F
-finite and

-pure, and let

a compatible ideal. Then,

\operatorname{fpt}(R)\leq\operatorname{fpt}(R/J).

In particular,

\operatorname{fpt}(R)\leq\operatorname{fpt}(R/\mathfrak{p})

.

The next result shows us how to compute the
F
-pure threshold of a squarefree monomial ideal.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
I
be a square-free monomial ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then
\operatorname{fpt}(S/I)
is equal to the number of variables that do not appear in its minimal set of generators.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{t}}\}
be the set of variables that appear in the minimal generating set of
I
. Then,

x_{i_{1}}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{p^{e}-1}\in(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.

Set

d:=\deg{x_{i_{1}}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{p^{e}-1}}.

For every monomial
m
of degree less than
d
, we have that
m\notin(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
. By Lemma
2.6

b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})=(n-t)(p^{e}-1)
. Dividing both sides by

and taking the limit as

∎

3.
F-pure thresholds of binomial edge ideals

Through this section
K
is a
F-finite
field of characteristic
p
.
For a sequence
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}
of natural numbers, we set

f_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}}:=f_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{p-1}\cdots f_{v_{n-1}v_{n}}^{p-1}

f_{ji}:=-f_{ij}

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\{a,b\}\in E(G)
. Then,

f_{v_{1},\ldots,c,a,b,d,\ldots,v_{s}}\equiv f_{v_{1},\ldots,c,b,a,d,\ldots,v_{s}}\,\mod\mathcal{J}_{G}.

Proof.

By the Plücker relations, we have that

\displaystyle f_{ca}^{p-1}f_{ab}^{p-1}f_{bd}^{p-1}

\displaystyle=f_{ab}^{p-1}(f_{cb}f_{ad}-f_{cd}f_{ab})^{p-1}

\displaystyle=f_{ab}^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{i}(-1)^{i}f_{cb}^{p-i-1}f_{ad}^{p-i-1}f_{cd}^{i}f_{ab}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p-1}{i}f_{cb}^{p-i-1}f_{ad}^{p-i-1}f_{cd}^{i}f_{ab}^{p+i-1}.

By assumption
f_{ab}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
, so all the terms of the sum with
i>0
are contained in
\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}
. This gives

\displaystyle f_{v_{1},\ldots,c,a,b,d,\ldots,v_{s}}

\displaystyle\equiv f_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{p-1}\cdots f_{ca}^{p-1}f_{ab}^{p-1}f_{bd}^{p-1}\cdots f_{v_{n-1}v_{n}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\equiv f_{v_{1}v_{2}}^{p-1}\cdots f_{cb}^{p-1}f_{ad}^{p-1}f_{ab}^{p-1}\cdots f_{v_{n-1}v_{n}}^{p-1}\,

\displaystyle\equiv f_{v_{1},\ldots,c,b,a,d,\ldots,v_{s}}

modulo
\mathcal{J}_{G}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple connected closed graph which is not the complete graph and let
S:=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
and
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal homogeneous ideal. Then,
S/\mathcal{J}_{G}
is

-pure, and

\min\left\{s\in\mathbb{N}\middle|\left[\frac{\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}:\mathcal{J}_{G}+\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}\right]_{s}\neq 0\right\}\leq 2(n-1)(p-1).

Proof.

By the Fedder’s criterion (Lemma
2.2
), it suffices to show that

f_{1,2,\ldots,n}\in(\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}:\mathcal{J}_{G})\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}.

First we prove that
f_{1,\ldots,n}\notin\mathfrak{m}
. Let
<
the lexicographic on
S
induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{1}>\cdots>y_{n}
. Then

\operatorname{In}(f_{1,\ldots,n})=x_{1}^{p-1}\cdots x_{n-1}^{p-1}y_{2}^{p-1}\cdots y_{n}^{p-1}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}.

Next we show that
f_{1,\ldots,n}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}:\mathcal{J}_{G}
. It is enough to show that
f_{1,\ldots,n}f_{ij}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}
for all
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. We assume that
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. If
j=i+1
, then
f_{1,\ldots,n}f_{ij}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}
. We assume that

. If
j\neq n
, then
\{k,j\}\in E(G)
for all
k\in\{i+1,\ldots,j-1\}

[
Mat18
, Propsition 1.3]
. Hence, by using repeatedly Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

f_{1,2,\ldots,i,i+1,\ldots,j-1,j,j+1,\ldots,n}\equiv f_{1,2,\ldots,i,j,i+1,\ldots,j-1,j+1,\ldots,n}\,\mod\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}.

Since
f_{i,j}^{p-1}
is a factor of the last expression, we have that
f_{1,\ldots,n}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}:\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

If
j=n
, then
i\neq 1
. For if
\{1,n\}\in E(G)
, then
G
is complete
[
Mat18
, Proposition 1.3]
.
By iterating Proposition
3.1
,

f_{1,2,\ldots,i,i+1,\ldots,j-1,j,j+1,\ldots,n}\equiv f_{1,2,\ldots,i-1,i+1,\ldots,i,n}\,\mod\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}.

Then
f_{in}^{p-1}
is a factor of the last expression, and
f_{1,\ldots,n}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p]}:\mathcal{J}_{G}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a closed graph, let
S:=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal homogeneous ideal. Then,
\operatorname{fpt}(S/\mathcal{J}_{G})=2
.

Proof.

If
G
is complete, then
S/\mathcal{J}_{G}
is determinantal, and so,
\operatorname{fpt}(S/\mathcal{J}_{G})=2

[
STV17
, Proposition 4.3]
.
First we prove by induction on
e
that if
f^{p-1}\in(I^{[p]}:I)\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}
, then
f^{p^{e}-1}\in(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
.

The step base follows from our assumptions.

For
f^{p^{e}-1}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
we have that

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}:f^{p^{e}-1})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e+1}]}:f^{p^{e+1}-p})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow((\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e+1}]}:f^{p^{e+1}-p}):f^{p-1})\subseteq(\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}:f^{p-1})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e+1}]}:f^{p^{e+1}-1})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

This means that
f^{p^{e+1}-1}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e+1}]}
.

If
f^{p^{e}-1}\in I^{[p^{e}]}:I
, Then,

\displaystyle f^{p-1}I\subseteq I^{[p]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(f^{p-1}I)^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e}}I^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e}}(f^{p^{e}-1}I)\subseteq f^{p^{e+1}-p^{e}}(I^{[p^{e}]})\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow f^{p^{e+1}-1}I\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]},

thus
f^{p^{e+1}-1}\in I^{[p^{e+1}]}:I
. This means that
(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n-1}y_{2}\cdots y_{n})^{p^{e}-1}\in(\mathcal{J}_{G}^{[p^{e}]}:\mathcal{J}_{G})\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
Using Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

\displaystyle 2n(p^{e}-1)-b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})

\displaystyle\leq 2(n-1)(p^{e}-1)

\displaystyle-b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})

\displaystyle\leq-2(p^{e}-1)

\displaystyle\frac{b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(p^{e}-1)}{p^{e}}

\displaystyle\operatorname{fpt}(S/\mathcal{J}_{G})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{b_{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{2(p^{e}-1)}{p^{e}}=2.

Since
\mathcal{J}_{K_{n}}
is a minimal prime over
\mathcal{J}_{G}
, the reverse inequality is a consequence of
2.9
and the fact that
\operatorname{fpt}{\mathcal{J}_{k_{n}}}=2
.
∎

Proposition 3.4

In this section we prove our main result. We start with a preparation theorem regarding
F

Notation 4.1

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field with maximal homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{m}

Let
I

<

\operatorname{in}(I)
is square-free.
There exists a vector

such that

[

.
Let

be a polynomial ring,

, and

.
We set

the homogenization of

Remark 4.2

Under Notation

, it is well known that

(1)

(2)

(3)

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field,
K
, of prime characteristic.
Let
I
be an ideal and
<
a monomial order such that
\operatorname{in}_{<}(I)

S/I

-injective.

Proof.

We use Notation
4.1
and the facts in Remark

in this proof.
We have that
R/tR

F
-pure. Since
F
-pure rings are
F
-full
[

, Lemma 2.5]
and
F

R/tR
satisfies these properties. Since

is a nonzero divisor, we have that
R
is also
F
-full and
F

MQ18
, Theorem 1.1]
. Then,
R\otimes_{A}L=S/I\otimes_{K}L
are both
F
-injective and
F
-full, because these properties are preserved under localization.
We note that
S/I
is a direct summand of
S/I\otimes_{K}L
.
We note that
\mathfrak{m}
expands the maximal homogeneous ideal in
S/I\otimes_{K}L
. Then,
we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S/I)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{S/I}}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}

We are now ready to show our main result in prime characteristic.

Theorem 4.4

Theorem 4.5

Flatness-based control revisited: The HEOL setting
\firstname Cédric \lastname Join \CDRorcid 0000-0001-6304-1262, \firstname Emmanuel \lastname Delaleau \CDRorcid 0000-0002-0257-568X, \firstname Michel \lastname Fliess \CDRorcid 0000-0003-3426-3637

Abstract
. We present the algebraic foundations of the HEOL setting, which combines flatness-based control and intelligent controllers, two advances in automatic control that have been proven in practice, including in industry. The result provides a solution to many pending questions on feedback loops concerning flatness-based control and model-free control (MFC). Elementary module theory, ordinary differential fields and the generalization of Kähler differentials to differential fields provide an intrinsic definition of the tangent linear system. The algebraic manipulations associated with the operational calculus lead to homeostat and intelligent controllers. They are illustrated via some computer simulations. Keywords. Flatness-based control, model-free control, intelligent controllers, differential algebra, module theory.

{altabstract}
On présente les fondations algébriques de la méthode HEOL qui combine commande par platitude et bouclage intelligent, c’est-à-dire deux avancées de l’automatique ayant fait leur preuve en pratique, y compris industrielle. On résoud ainsi plusieurs questions pendantes sur les bouclages à propos de la platitude et de la commande sans modèle. Théorie élémentaire des modules, corps différentiels ordinaires, et la généralisation à ces corps des différentielles de Kähler permettent une définition intrinsèque du système linéaire tangent. Les manipulations algébriques associées au calcul opérationnel conduisent à l’homéostat et aux correcteurs intelligents, illustrés par simulations numériques.

Mots-clefs.

2.
Algebraic Preliminaries

2.1.
Linear systems

An (
ordinary
)
differential ring

R
is a commutative ring equipped with a single
derivation

\frac{d}{dt}=\dot{}
such that,
\forall a\in R
,
\frac{da}{dt}=\dot{a}\in R
, and,
\forall a,b\in R
,
\frac{d}{dt}(a+b)=\dot{a}+\dot{b}
,
\frac{d}{dt}(ab)=\dot{a}b+a\dot{b}
. A
constant
is an element
c\in R
such that
\dot{c}=0
. An (
ordinary
)
differential field
is an (ordinary) differential ring which is a field.

Let
k
be a differential field. Write
k[\frac{d}{dt}]
the ring of linear differential operators
\sum_{\rm finite}a_{\ell}\frac{d^{\ell}}{dt^{\ell}}
,
a_{\ell}\in k
. This ring is obviously commutative if, and only if,

is a field of constants. In the general noncommutative case, any finitely generated left
k[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the following property (see,
e.g.
,
[
14
]
), which is classic in the commutative case,

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{F}\bigoplus\mathcal{T}

(3)

where
\mathcal{F}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}
) is a
free
(resp.
torsion
) finitely generated left
k[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module. Note that for a finitely generated module
\mathcal{M}
the following two properties are equivalent

—

\mathcal{M}
is torsion,

—

\mathcal{M}
is finite-dimensional as a
k
-vector space.

Notation.
Write
{\rm span}_{k[\frac{d}{dt}]}(S)
the submodule of
\mathcal{M}
spanned by
S\subset\mathcal{M}
.
A
linear system
over the differential ground field
k
is a finitely generated left
k[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module
\Lambda
.
A
linear control system
over the ground field
k
is a finitely generated left
k[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module
\Lambda
where
there is a finite set
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}\subset\Lambda
of

such that the quotient module
\Lambda/{\rm span}_{k[\frac{d}{dt}]}(U)
is torsion.
The control variables are said to be
independent
if, and only if,
{\rm span}_{k[\frac{d}{dt}]}(U)
is free of rank
m
.
System
\Lambda
is said to be

[
18
]
if, and only if,
\Lambda
is a free module. Contrary
to the usual approaches, this definition does not depend on any distinction between system
variables and any state space description. It has been proved nevertheless
[
18
]
that for a standard state-variable representation this module-theoretic definition is equivalent to the classic Kalman’s approach.
The set of

Y=\{y_{1},...,y_{p}\}\subset\Lambda
is a finite subset of the system
\Lambda
. The

\Lambda
, with

18
]
if, and only if,

2.2.
Nonlinear systems

2.2.1.
Differential field extension

Differential fields are assumed to be of characteristic
0
. A
differential field extension

L/K
is given by two differential fields

and

, such that
K\subset L
, and the restriction to
K

coincides with the derivation of
K

is assumed to be finitely generated. Write
K\langle S\rangle
the differential subfield of

K
by

, there exists a polynomial

\pi[\xi,\dot{\xi},\dots,\xi^{(\nu)}]=0

\xi
is said to be
differentially transcendental over
K
, or
differentially
K
-transcendental
, if, and only if, it is not differentially
K
-algebraic. The extension

is said to be
differentially algebraic
(resp.

) if, and only if, any (resp. at least one) element in

is (resp. is not) differentially
K
-algebraic. A
set
\left\{\,\xi_{i}\,|\,i\in I\,\right\}
is said to be
differentially

-algebraically independent

\left\{x_{i}^{(\nu)}|i\in I,\nu=0,1,\dots\right\}
is algebraically

-independent. Such an independent set, which is maximal with respect to inclusion, is called a
differential transcendence basis
of the
L/K
. Two such bases have the same cardinality,
i.e.
, the same number of elements, which is called the
differential transcendence degree
of
L/K
and is denoted diff tr d°
L/K
. The differential field extension
L/K
is said to be
purely differentially transcendental
if, and only if, it is generated by a differential transcendence basis. The two following properties are equivalent

—

diff tr d°
L/K

=0
;

—

the familiar,
i.e.
, non-differential, transcendence degree of

is finite.

2.2.2.
Nonlinear systems and differential flatness

A
system
is a finitely generated differential field extension
\mathcal{D}/k
. In a

—

there is a finite set

of

—

The control variables are said to be

if, and only if,

is a purely differential transcendental extension where

is a differential transcendence basis. Introduce a finite set of

The system

(differentially)

Note that any flat system is obviously observable with respect to any flat outputs.

Consider the linear system

2.2.3.
Kähler differentials

Kähler differentials
were introduced in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry to mimic some features of differential calculus (see,
e.g.
,
[
17
,
61
]
). They have been extended to differential algebra
[
40
]
. Consider again a finitely generated differential field extension

K

. Introduce the (Kähler) differential
{\rm d}_{L/K}:L\rightarrow\Omega_{L/K}
where
\Omega_{L/K}
is a finitely generated left
L[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module, such that
8
8

8

\forall a\in L
,
{\rm d}_{L/K}a\in\Omega_{L/K}
should intuitively be viewed as a “small” variation of

.

—

\forall a\in L
,
{\rm d}_{L/K}\dot{a}=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm d}_{L/K}a
;

—

\forall a,b\in L

{\rm d}_{L/K}(a+b)={\rm d}_{L/K}a+{\rm d}_{L/K}b

{\rm d}_{L/K}(ab)=a{\rm d}_{L/K}b+b{\rm d}_{L/K}a
;

—

\forall c\in K
,
{\rm d}_{L/K}c=0
.

The following properties justify the introduction of Kähler differentials.

—

A set
\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}\}
is a differential transcendence basis of

if, and only if,
\{\rm d_{L/K}\eta_{1},\dots,{\rm d}_{L/K}\eta_{m}\}
is a maximal set of

. Thus the differential transcendence degree of

\Omega_{L/K}
.

—

is differentially algebraic if, and only if,
\Omega_{L/K}
is torsion. A set
\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{\nu}\}
is a transcendence basis of
L/K
if, and only if,
\{{\rm d}_{L/K}\chi_{1},\dots,{\rm d}_{L/K}\chi_{\nu}\}
is a basis of the
L
-vector space
\Omega_{L/K}
.

—

L/K
is an algebraic extension if, and only if,
\Omega_{L/K}=\{0\}
.

The
tangent linear system
, or
variational linear system
associated to the system

is the left
\mathcal{D}[\frac{d}{dt}]
-module
\Omega_{\mathcal{D}/k}
of Kähler differentials. If
\mathcal{D}/k
is flat,
\Omega_{\mathcal{D}/k}
is obviously free : the tangent system is controllable.

3.1.1.

Consider a control system

\sum_{0\leqslant\iota\leqslant n}\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial y^{(\iota)}}{\rm d}_{k\langle u,y\rangle/k}y^{(\iota)}+\frac{\partial E}{\partial u}{\rm d}_{k\langle u,y\rangle/k}u=0

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\iota\neq\nu}\frac{\frac{\partial E}{\partial y^{(\iota)}}}{\frac{\partial E}{\partial y^{(\nu)}}}{\rm d}_{k\langle u,y\rangle/k}y^{(\iota)}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{\partial E}{\partial u}}{\frac{\partial E}{\partial y^{(\nu)}}}

The

,

\frac{d^{\nu}}{dt^{\nu}}\Delta y=F+\alpha\Delta u

There

\Delta y=y-y^{\star}
,
\Delta u=u-u^{\star}
, where

is a reference trajectory for the flat system

(corresponding) nominal control

—

, where

—

5.
Conclusion

The HEOL setting,
i.e.
, the introduction of homeostats deduced from the tangent linear system, suggests elementary solutions to questions, which are crucial from a practical viewpoint and have been around for many years, like feedback control of flat systems and implementation issues in model-free control. There are of course other issues which might benefit from our approach. It has been observed (see,
e.g.
,

]

]
). Convincing concrete illustrations should be available soon.

Let’s conclude with some more general considerations. In applied sciences, too, an appropriate formalism might be of paramount importance in order to trivialize technical investigations that seemed before beyond the reach. This is the aim of the present paper in control engineering.

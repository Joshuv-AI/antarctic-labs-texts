1-cocycles of the Witt algebra with coefficients in tensor product of modules Thanks: ∗ * : Corresponding author.
Shoulan Gao, Dong Liu, Yufeng Pei ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, we classify 1-cocycles of the Witt algebra with coefficients in the tensor product of two arbitrary tensor density modules. In a special case, we recover a theorem originally established by Ng and Taft in [ 24 ] . Furthermore, by these 1-cocycles, we determine Lie bialgebra structures over certain infinite-dimensional Lie algebras containing the Witt algebra.

1.
Introduction

The Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}
is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra that emerges as the algebra of derivations acting on the Laurent polynomial ring in a single variable. The Virasoro algebra, which is the universal central extension of the Witt algebra, holds significant importance in the realms of both mathematics and theoretical physics. The cohomology of the Witt algebra and the Virasoro algebra has been an active area of research owing to its deep relations with representation theory
[
10
,
16
,
21
,
20
]
. In particular, the algebraic cohomology of the Witt algebra, when considering coefficients related to the tensor density modules
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
, has been the subject of study in various works
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
25
,
29
]
. In
[
10
]
, Feigin and Fuchs achieved a highly non-trivial classification of length two extensions for the Witt algebra using the first algebraic cohomology, with
coefficients in the module of the space of homomorphisms between the two arbitrary tensor density modules. Recently, in
[
4
]
, Dykes computed all possible 1-cocycles for the Witt algebra by employing a compatible action of the commutative algebra of Laurent polynomials in one variable.

Motivated by Witten’s study of left-invariant symplectic structures on the Virasoro group (
[
31
]
), Ng and Taft established that all Lie bialgebra structures on the Witt algebra and the Virasoro algebra are triangular coboundary through computation of 1-cocycles with coefficients in the tensor product of the adjoint modules in
[
24
]
(see also
[
17
]
).
The Lie bialgebra structures on various infinite-dimensional Lie algebras related to the Witt algebra have been investigated by computing the first algebraic cohomology
[
8
,
15
,
19
,
18
,
27
]
. As mentioned in
[
19
]
, a key and complicated step of characterizing these Lie bialgebras is dealing with 1-cocycles of the Witt algebra with coefficients in the tensor product of two tensor density modules. To the best knowledge of the authors, there is no general formula for these 1-cocycles in the literature.

This paper aims to classify 1-cocycles of the Witt algebra with coefficients in the tensor product of two arbitrary tensor density modules. Our main theorem can be stated as follows:

Main Theorem
(Theorem
5.7
): For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})=\begin{cases}5,&(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0);\\
2,&(\alpha,\beta)=(0,1)\text{ or }(1,0);\\
1,&(\alpha,\beta)=(0,2),(2,0),(1,1),\text{ or }\alpha+\beta=1,\beta\neq 0,1;\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Establishing the proof for this theorem is challenging and involves numerous calculations. Nonetheless, the fundamental concept underlying the proof remains clear. First, suitable normalizations allow selecting well-defined representatives for each cohomology class, reducing the computation of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})
to that of normalized 1-cocycles. Through several technical lemmas, we then demonstrate a normalized 1-cocycle must assume a specialized form, enabling an upper bound on
\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})
. Finally, exhibiting linearly independent normalized 1-cocycles produces the result.

The interest of this classification for these 1-cocycles lies in the applications to the Lie bialgebras associated with some infinite dimensional Lie algebras.
When
(\alpha,\beta)=(-1,-1)
, we recover a theorem first proven by Ng and Taft in
[
24
]
. Furthermore, as consequence of the Main Theorem, we establish Lie bialgebra structures over various infinite-dimensional Lie algebras that contain the Witt algebra, such as the Oveshenko-Roger algebra
[
25
]
, the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
1
]
, the BMS algebra
[
2
,
32
]
, and the Schrödinger-Virasoro algebra
[
26
]
.

It is worth noting that, as stated in
[
10
]
, an isomorphism exists:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha},\mathcal{F}_{\beta}))\cong\mathrm{H}_{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}^{\prime}
denotes the graded dual modules of
\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
.
Our main theorem may be considered as a dual counterpart to the 1-cycles presented in
[
10
]
. However, there is currently no more efficient method for computing 1-cocycles via 1-cycles.

2

Section

2.
Preliminaries and notations

In this section, we review some preliminaries and notations on 1-cocycles of the Witt algebra and obtain several useful lemmas.

Definition 2.1
.

The Witt algebra
\mathcal{W}
is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra spanned by
\{L_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
with the bracket

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\quad\forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the tensor density module
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}v_{n}
for
\mathcal{W}
is given by

L_{m}\cdot v_{n}=-(\alpha m+n)v_{m+n},\quad\forall m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

For
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
is a
\mathcal{W}
-module with

L_{m}\cdot(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=-(i+\alpha m)v_{m+i}\otimes v_{j}-(j+\beta m)v_{i}\otimes v_{m+j},\quad\forall m,i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

In particular,

L_{0}\cdot(v_{i}\otimes v_{j})=-(i+j)v_{i}\otimes v_{j},\quad\forall i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

It follows that
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
is equipped with a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading:

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}=\bigoplus\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})_{k},\quad\text{where}\quad(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}v_{i}\otimes v_{k-i}.

Definition 2.3

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra and
M
an
L
-module. Denote by
\mbox{\rm Der}(L,M)
the set of 1-cocycles (derivations)
d:L\rightarrow M
with coefficients in
M
, which are linear maps satisfying the condition
d([x,y])=x\cdot d(y)-y\cdot d(x)
for
x,y\in L
. The set
\mbox{\rm Inn}(L,M)
consists of the 1-cocycles
d_{v}
for some
v\in M
, where
d_{v}
is called 1-coboundary (inner derivation) with coefficients in
M
defined by
d_{v}:x\mapsto x\cdot v
for
x\in L
. A 1-cocycle with coefficients in
M
is called non-trivial if it is not a 1-coboundary with coefficients in
M
. We denote by
{\rm H}^{1}(L,M)=\mbox{\rm Der}(L,M)/\mbox{\rm Inn}(L,M)
the first algebraic cohomology of
L
with coefficients in
M
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is a well-known fact
[
12
]
that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between elements in
{\rm H}^{1}(L,M)
and equivalence classes of extensions of
L
-modules by
M
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be an abelian group,
L=\bigoplus_{g\in G}L_{g}
a
G
-graded Lie algebra over
{\mathbb{C}}
. An
L
-module
V
is said to be
G
-graded if
V=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_{g}
and
L_{g}\cdot V_{h}\subset V_{g+h}
for all
g,h\in G
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
9
]
)
.

Assume that
L
is finitely generated as a
G
-graded Lie algebra. Let
V
be a G-graded
L
-module. Then

\operatorname{Der}(L,V)=\underset{g\in G}{\bigoplus}\operatorname{Der}(L,V)_{g}.

Lemma 2.8
.

\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})=\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})_{0}+\mbox{\rm Inn}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})

Proof.

Assuming

and
D_{k}\in\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})_{k}

D_{k}(L_{0})=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{s}a(i_{r})v_{i_{r}}\otimes v_{k-i_{r}}
, where

D_{k}[L_{n},L_{0}]=L_{n}\cdot D_{k}(L_{0})-L_{0}\cdot D_{k}(L_{n})

L_{n}\cdot D_{k}(L_{0})=-kD_{k}(L_{n})

for any integers

D_{k}(L_{n})=-\frac{1}{k}L_{n}\cdot D_{k}(L_{0})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{r=1}^{s}a(i_{r})\Big((i_{r}+\alpha n)v_{n+i_{r}}\otimes v_{k-i_{r}}+(k-i_{r}+\beta n)v_{i}{{}_{r}i}\otimes v_{n+k-i_{r}}\Big).

This implies that

D_{k}(L_{n})=L_{n}\cdot\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}{\frac{a(i_{r})}{k}}v_{i_{r}}\otimes v_{k-i_{r}}\right),

which shows that

is a 1-coboundary.

Lemma 2.9

For any

D(L_{0})=a\delta_{\alpha,0}\delta_{\beta,0}v_{0}\otimes v_{0}

Let
D\in\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})_{0}

Then

L_{n}\cdot D(L_{0})=-\sum\limits_{i=p}^{q}a(i)\Big((i+\alpha n)v_{n+i}\otimes v_{-i}+(-i+\beta n)v_{i}\otimes v_{n-i}\Big),\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Taking

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=p}^{q}a(i)\Big((i+\alpha)v_{1+i}\otimes v_{-i}+(-i+\beta)v_{i}\otimes v_{1-i}\Big)=\sum\limits_{i=p}^{q}a(i)\Big((i-\alpha)v_{i-1}\otimes v_{-i}+(-i-\beta)v_{i}\otimes v_{-i-1}\Big)=0.

Then

a(p)(-p+\beta)=0,\quad a(p)(p-\alpha)=0,\quad a(q)(q+\beta)=0,\quad a(q)(q+\alpha)=0;

(2.1)

a(i)(-i+\beta)+a(i-1)(i-1+\alpha)=0,\quad\quad\,p+1\leq i\leq q;

(2.2)

a(i+1)(i+1-\alpha)-a(i)(i+\beta)=0,\quad\quad\,p\leq i\leq q-1.

(2.3)

It is clear that

a(p)(\alpha-\beta)=a(q)(\alpha-\beta)=0.

Replacing
i

in (
2.3

a(i)(i-\alpha)-a(i-1)(i-1+\beta)=0,\qquad\,p+1\leq i\leq q.

(2.4)

Using (
2.2
) and (
2.4

[a(i)-a(i-1)](\alpha-\beta)=0
for
p+1\leq i\leq q.

Case 1:
\alpha\neq\beta

Clearly,
a(i)=0
for all

D(L_{0})=0
.

Case 2:
\alpha=\beta\notin{\mathbb{Z}}

For any integers
p,q

a(p)=a(q)=0
. Therefore, using (
2.2

2.3

a(i)=0
for all

D(L_{0})=0

Case 3:
\alpha=\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}

Without loss of generality, let us assume that both

are non-zero. Then from (

\beta=\alpha=p=-q

If
\alpha=\beta\neq 0

D(L_{0})=\sum_{i=-q}^{q}a(i)v_{i}\otimes v_{-i}.

Now applying the operator
{L_{n}}

-\sum_{i=-q}^{q}\Big(a(i)(i+\alpha n)v_{n+i}\otimes v_{-i}-a(i)(-i+\beta n)v_{i}\otimes v_{n-i}\Big)=0,\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Thus, for every positive integer value of

we obtain two equations:

and

Since both

and

, which leads to a contradiction.

Therefore, we conclude that

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, we define a linear operator

{\rm d}_{1-\beta,\beta}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{F}_{1-\beta}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
as follows:

{\rm d}_{1-\beta,\beta}(L_{n})=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(i-n\beta)v_{i}\otimes v_{-i+n},&n\geq 1;\\
\qquad\,0,&n=0;\\
-\sum\limits_{i=n}^{-1}(i-n\beta)v_{i}\otimes v_{-i+n},&n\leq-1.\end{cases}

Then
{\rm d}_{1-\beta,\beta}
is a non-trivial 1-cocycle of
\mathcal{W}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{F}_{1-\beta}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta}

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
{\rm d}_{1-\beta,\beta}\in\mbox{\rm Der}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{F}_{1-\beta}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\beta})
. Assume

In

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W},(\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\otimes\mathcal{W}(\alpha))^{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{W}(\alpha),\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\otimes\mathcal{W}(\alpha))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha},\mathcal{W}(\alpha)\otimes\mathcal{W}(\alpha))^{\mathcal{W}(\alpha)}.

It is easy to see

∎

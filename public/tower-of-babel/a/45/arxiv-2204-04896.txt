Exponential and logarithm of multivector in low dimensional ( n = p + q < 3 n=p+q<3 ) Clifford algebras
Adolfas Dargys, Artūras Acus

Abstract
Closed form expressions for a multivector exponential and logarithm are presented in real Clifford geometric algebras 𝐶𝑙 p , q \mathit{Cl}_{p,q} when n = p + q = 1 n=p+q=1 (complex and hyperbolic numbers) and n = 2 n=2 (Hamilton, split and conectorine quaternions). Starting from 𝐶𝑙 0 , 1 \mathit{Cl}_{0,1} and 𝐶𝑙 1 , 0 \mathit{Cl}_{1,0} algebras wherein square of a basis vector is either − 1 -1 or + 1 +1 , we have generalized exponential and logarithm formulas to 2D quaternionic algebras, 𝐶𝑙 0 , 2 \mathit{Cl}_{0,2} , 𝐶𝑙 1 , 1 \mathit{Cl}_{1,1} , and 𝐶𝑙 2 , 0 \mathit{Cl}_{2,0} . The sectors in the multivector coefficient space where 2D logarithm exists are found. They are related with a square root of the multivector.

Properties of exponential and logarithm in
GA

Let
\mathbf{e}_{i}
be the basis vector and

\mathbf{e}_{ij}\equiv\mathbf{e}_{i}\mathbf{e}_{j}=-\mathbf{e}_{ji}
be the bivector. The latter is
the geometric product of two orthogonal basis vectors. Complex and
hyperbolic numbers (aka Clifford numbers) in GA
[
18
]

are represented by the following MVs

\begin{split}\mathit{Cl}_{0,1},\quad&\mathsf{A}=a_{0}+a_{1}I,\quad\text{where\ }I\equiv\mathbf{e}_{1}\ \text{and\ }\mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=-1,\\
\mathit{Cl}_{1,0},\quad&\mathsf{A}=a_{0}+a_{1}I,\quad\text{where\ }I\equiv\mathbf{e}_{1}\ \text{and\ }\mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=+1,\\
\end{split}

(2.1)

where
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are the real coefficients,
a_{0},a_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.

a_{0}
is called the scalar part of MV and
a_{1}I
the
pseudoscalar. In 1D GAs the basis vectors coincide with an
elementary pseudoscalar
I
. The squares,
\mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}\equiv\mathrm{i}^{2}=-1
in
\mathit{Cl}_{0,1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=1
in
\mathit{Cl}_{1,0}
, suggest that
we have to do with complex and hyperbolic numbers, respectively.

In 2-dimensional (2D) algebras there are two basis vectors

\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
, and a bivector
\mathbf{e}_{12}\equiv I
(oriented
plane). The general Clifford number
\mathsf{A}
is

\begin{split}&\mathit{Cl}_{0,2}:\ \mathsf{A}=a_{0}+a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{12}I,\ \text{where\ }\ \mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{2}^{2}=-1,\ I^{2}=-1,\\
&\mathit{Cl}_{1,1}:\ \mathsf{A}=a_{0}+a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{12}I,\ \text{where\ }\ \mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=-\mathbf{e}_{2}^{2}=-1,\ I^{2}=1,\\
&\mathit{Cl}_{2,0}:\ \mathsf{A}=a_{0}+a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+a_{12}I,\ \text{where\ }\ \mathbf{e}_{1}^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{2}^{2}=+1,\ I^{2}=-1.\end{split}

(2.2)

The sum
\mathbf{a}=a_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}
represents a general vector in 2D
bivector plane. The basis vectors satisfy
\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf{e}_{2}=0

(orthogonality) and
\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbin{\wedge}\mathbf{e}_{2}=\mathbf{e}_{1}\mathbf{e}_{2}\equiv\mathbf{e}_{12}

(oriented unit plane), where the dot and wedge denote the inner
and outer products.
\mathbf{e}_{12}
plays the role of an elementary
pseudoscalar
I
. The sign of
I^{2}
depends on algebra,
Eq. (
2.2
). The algebras
\mathit{Cl}_{1,1}
and
\mathit{Cl}_{2,0}
are
isomorphic under the following exchange of GA basis elements:

\mathbf{e}_{1}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{e}_{1}
,
\mathbf{e}_{2}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{e}_{12}
and

\mathbf{e}_{12}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{e}_{2}
.

The main involutions, namely the reversion, inversion and
Clifford conjugation denoted, respectively, by tilde, circumflex
and their combination are defined by the following changes in
component signs of MV
\mathsf{A}=a_{0}+\mathbf{a}+a_{12}I
,

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}=a_{0}+\mathbf{a}-a_{12}I,\qquad\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}=a_{0}-\mathbf{a}+a_{12}I,\qquad\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}}=a_{0}-\mathbf{a}-a_{12}I.\end{split}

(2.3)

For complex and hyperbolic numbers there is only a single
involution,
\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}=a_{0}+a_{1}\wideparen{I}=a_{0}-a_{1}I
, that usually is denoted by asterisk in physics and
engineering and overline in mathematics.

The exponential of MV is another MV that belongs to the same
geometric algebra
\mathit{Cl}_{p,q}
. If
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{B}
are MVs, the
following properties hold:

\begin{split}&\text{exp}(\mathsf{A}+\mathsf{B})=\text{exp}(\mathsf{A})\text{exp}(\mathsf{B})\quad\text{iff \ }\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A},\\
&\widetilde{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}},\quad\wideparen{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}},\quad\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}}},\\
\end{split}

(2.4)

where
\mathrm{e}
is the base of the natural logarithm. In 1D algebras
the first property is always satisfied since the commutation of
scalar and vector is satisfied.

The GA exponential can be represented as a power series in a form
similar to scalar exponential
[
19
]
. In numerical
form, i.e., when coefficients at basis elements
\mathbf{e}_{1}
,
\mathbf{e}_{1}

and
\mathbf{e}_{12}
are real numbers, the exponential can be summed up
approximately
[
14
]
. To minimize the number of
multiplications it is convenient to rewrite the exponential in a
nested form (aka Horner’s rule),

\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}=1+\frac{\mathsf{A}}{1}(1+\frac{\mathsf{A}}{2}(1+\frac{\mathsf{A}}{3}(1+\frac{\mathsf{A}}{4}(1+\dots)))),

(2.5)

which requires fewer MV products. If numerical coefficients
in
\mathsf{A}
are small enough the exponential
\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}
can be
approximated by truncated series (
2.5
) to high
precision. For examples, refer to paper
[
14
]
.

The following properties hold for MV logarithm:

\begin{split}&\text{log}(\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B})=\text{log}(\mathsf{A})+\text{log}(\mathsf{B})\quad\text{iff\ }\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A},\\
&\mathrm{e}^{-\text{log}(\mathsf{A})}=\mathsf{A}^{-1},\\
&\widetilde{\log(\mathsf{A})}=\log(\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}),\quad\wideparen{\log(\mathsf{A})}=\log(\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}),\quad\widetilde{\wideparen{\log(\mathsf{A})}}=\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}}}.\\
\end{split}

(2.6)

When the logarithm of MV exists, it may be approximated by series

\begin{split}\log\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{B}(1+\mathsf{B}(-\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathsf{B}(\tfrac{1}{3}+\mathsf{B}(-\tfrac{1}{4}+\mathsf{B}(\tfrac{1}{5}+\dotsm))))),\quad 0<\lvert\mathsf{B}\rvert<1.\end{split}

(2.7)

Here
|\mathsf{B}|
is the determinant
norm
[
14
,
20
]
. If logarithm exists,
a finite series (
2.7
) can be summed up in a
numerical form approximately. However, as we shall see later there
may be sector(s) in the MV coefficient domain where the logarithm
does not exist at all.

In
\mathit{Cl}_{0,1}
algebra the norm
\lvert\mathsf{B}\rvert
, which is equal
to the square root of MV determinant

\text{Det}(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{B}\wideparen{\mathsf{B}}=b_{0}^{2}+b_{1}^{2}>0
, is
called the magnitude or absolute value of the MV (or magnitude of
the complex number in this case). In hyperbolic number theory
similar role is played by product

\mathsf{B}\wideparen{\mathsf{B}}=b_{0}^{2}-b_{1}^{2}
, which may be positive,
negative or zero. In this case the magnitude called a determinant
semi-norm (or pseudonorm)

\lVert\mathsf{B}\rVert=\sqrt{\abs(\mathsf{B}\wideparen{\mathsf{B}})}=\sqrt{\abs(b_{0}^{2}-b_{1}^{2})}\geq 0
is introduced. Note that now the equality
sign appears, therefore, the semi-norm
\lVert\mathsf{B}\rVert
may be
zero even if
\mathsf{B}\neq 0
. The equality sign in case of the norm

\lvert\mathsf{B}\rvert
would require the MV to nullify.

\mathit{Cl}_{0,2}

In this algebra according to Table

the norm (or

\lvert\mathsf{B}\rvert=(b_{0}^{2}+\lvert B^{\prime}\rvert^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\log\lvert\mathsf{B}\rvert+\Bigl(\arctan\bigl(b_{0},\lvert B^{\prime}\rvert\bigr)+2\pi k\Bigr)\frac{B^{\prime}}{\lvert B^{\prime}\rvert},\quad\lvert B^{\prime}\rvert\neq 0,\\

7
Conclusions and discussion

The paper presents exponential and logarithm functions of
multivector argument for
\mathit{Cl}_{0,1}
,
\mathit{Cl}_{1,0}
(1-dimensional
commutative) and
\mathit{Cl}_{0,2}
,
\mathit{Cl}_{1,1}
,
\mathit{Cl}_{2,0}
(2-dimensional
non-commutative) Clifford geometric algebras (GAs). The well-known
approach to Hamilton quaternion identified by three imaginaries

\{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j},\mathrm{k}\}
was generalized and adapted, specifically, the
imaginaries have been replaced by 2D unit multivectors the
squares of which are equal to
\pm 1
and which have been
constructed from Clifford basis vectors
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{12}\}
.

The 2D basis-free exponentials in this approach assume a form of
either Euler’s or de Moivre’s rules. The principal logarithm was
determined as an inverse of respective exponential.
2\pi

multiplicity in the logarithm was included by adding a free MV

\mathsf{F}
that satisfies the condition
\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{F}}=1
. The
obtained formulas for exponential and logarithm can be applied
to quaternions too, since
\mathit{Cl}_{0,2}
,
\mathit{Cl}_{1,1}
and
\mathit{Cl}_{2,0}

algebras are isomorphic to, correspondingly, the Hamilton
quaternion, coquaternion, and conectorine
[
13
]
,

Since in GA the
n
-th root of MV is

(\mathsf{A})^{1/n}=\exp\big(\tfrac{1}{n}\log(\mathsf{A})\big)
the obtained
exponential and logarithm formulas may be applied to extract the

n
-th root from general MV. However, this exp-log formula allows
to calculate no more then two (plus/minus) square roots. Workable
formulas for square roots are presented. In particular, using the
defining equation we have found explicit formulas for square
roots in 1D and 2D Clifford algebras and the sectors of their
existence in MV coefficient space. In the space of MV
coefficients the sectors where the roots do not exist the
logarithm does not exist as well. Also, multiple square roots (up
to four) have been found in
\mathit{Cl}_{1,0}
and
\mathit{Cl}_{1,1}
algebras.

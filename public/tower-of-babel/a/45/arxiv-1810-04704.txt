Equivalence of generics
Iian B. Smythe

Abstract
. Given a countable transitive model of set theory and a partial order contained in it, there is a natural countable Borel equivalence relation on generic filters over the model; two are equivalent if they yield the same generic extension. We examine the complexity of this equivalence relation for various partial orders, with particular focus on Cohen and random forcing. We prove, amongst other results, that the former is an increasing union of countably many hyperfinite Borel equivalence relations, while the latter is neither amenable nor treeable.

1.
Introduction

Given a countable transitive model
M
of
\mathsf{ZFC}
and a partial order
\mathbb{P}
in
M
, we can construct
M
-generic filters
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
and their corresponding generic extensions
M[G]
using the method of forcing. We say that two such
M
-generic filters
G
and
H
are
equivalent
, written
G\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}H
, if they produce the same generic extension, that is:

G\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}H\quad\text{if and only if}\quad M[G]=M[H].

It is this equivalence relation that we aim to study.

The countability of
M
and the definability of the forcing relation imply that
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is a
countable Borel equivalence relation
(Lemma
2.6
), that is, each equivalence class is countable and
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is a Borel set of pairs in some appropriately defined space of
M
-generic filters for
\mathbb{P}
. The general theory of countable Borel equivalence relations affords us a broad set of tools for analyzing the relative complexity of each
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
; see the surveys
[
12
]
and
[
29
]
. In turn, each
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
provides a natural, well-motivated example.

To briefly review the general theory, for Borel equivalence relations
E
and
F
on Polish, or standard Borel, spaces
X
and
Y
, a
Borel reduction
of
E
to
F
is a Borel measurable function
f:X\to Y
satisfying

x\,E\,y\quad\text{if and only if}\quad f(x)Ef(y)

for all
x,y\in X
. If such an
f
exists, we say that
E
is
Borel reducible
to
F
, written
E\leq_{B}F
. The relation
\leq_{B}
gives a measure of complexity amongst Borel equivalence relations. If
E\leq_{B}F
and
F\leq_{B}E
, then we say that
E
and
F
are
Borel bireducible
; they have the same level of complexity. If
f
only satisfies the forward implication in the displayed line above, we say that
f
is a
Borel homomorphism
of
E
to
F
. A bijective Borel reduction from
E
to
F
is called a
Borel isomorphism
, in which case we say that
E
and
F
are
Borel isomorphic
and write
E\cong_{B}F
.

The simplest Borel equivalence relations, called
smooth
, are those Borel reducible to the equality relation
\Delta(\mathbb{R})
on the reals. A benchmark example of a non-smooth countable Borel equivalence relation is eventual equality of binary strings, denoted by
E_{0}
:

x\,E_{0}\,y\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\exists m\forall n\geq m(x(n)=y(n)),

for
x,y\in 2^{\omega}
.

Amongst the countable Borel equivalence relations, those Borel reducible to
E_{0}
are exactly those which are
hyperfinite
, that is, equal to an increasing union of countably many Borel equivalence relations, each with finite classes. These also coincide with orbit equivalence relations of Borel actions of
\mathbb{Z}
(see Theorem 5.1 in
[
4
]
).

Every hyperfinite equivalence relation is
(Fréchet) amenable
, see
[
12
]
for the (somewhat technical) definition. In fact, every orbit equivalence relation induced by a countable amenable group is amenable (Proposition 2.13 in
[
12
]
), while it remains open whether the converse holds, and whether amenability and hyperfiniteness coincide for equivalence relations.

More generally, every countable Borel equivalence relation can be realized as the orbit equivalence relation of a Borel action of some countable group (Theorem 1 in
[
5
]
). Consequently, much of the theory consists of analyzing the dynamics of group actions. Of particular importance are the
Bernoulli shift actions
: Given a countably infinite group
\Gamma
,
\Gamma
acts on the space
2^{\Gamma}
by:

(\gamma\cdot x)(\delta)=x(\gamma^{-1}\delta)

for
x\in 2^{\Gamma}
,
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
. The corresponding orbit equivalence is denoted by
E(\Gamma,2)
. The
free part
of this action,

(2)^{\Gamma}=\{x\in 2^{\Gamma}:\forall\gamma\in\Gamma(\gamma\neq 1\Rightarrow\gamma\cdot x\neq x)\},

is a
\Gamma
-invariant Borel set on which the action is free, and is easily seen to be conull with respect to the usual product measure on
2^{\Gamma}
. Denote by
F(\Gamma,2)
the restriction of
E(\Gamma,2)
to
(2)^{\Gamma}
.

When
\Gamma=F_{2}
, the free group on
2
generators,
E(F_{2},2)
and
F(F_{2},2)
are not amenable, and thus not hyperfinite (cf. Proposition 1.7 in
[
12
]
and
[
24
]
).
E(F_{2},2)
is
universal
, every countable Borel equivalence relation is Borel reducible to it (Proposition 1.8 in
[
4
]
).
F(F_{2},2)
is
treeable
, meaning there is a Borel acyclic graph on the underlying space whose connected components are exactly the equivalence classes of
F(F_{2},2)
. Every hyperfinite Borel equivalence relation is treeable, while no universal one is (see
[
12
]
).

One precursor to the present work is the recent paper
[
2
]
on the classification of countable models of
\mathsf{ZFC}
up to isomorphism. While much of
[
2
]
is concerned with ill-founded models, the proof of Theorem 3.2 therein, that
E_{0}
Borel reduces to the isomorphism relation for countable well-founded models, makes essential use of the the fact that
\equiv^{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
, for
\mathbb{C}
Cohen forcing, is not smooth. This observation was a starting point for our work here.

This paper is arranged as follows: Section
2
consists of general results which apply to an arbitrary partial order
\mathbb{P}
in
M
. We describe spaces of
M
-generic objects for
\mathbb{P}
, prove Borelness of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
on these spaces, verify that the Borel complexity of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is independent of different presentations of
\mathbb{P}
, and discuss automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}
and their relationship to
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
. We show that, for many of the partial orders one encounters in forcing,
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is not smooth (Theorem
2.12
).

Section
3
is devoted to Cohen forcing
\mathbb{C}
. We prove that
\equiv^{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
is an increasing union of countably many hyperfinite equivalence relations and is thus amenable (Theorem
3.1
).

In Section
4
, we consider random forcing
\mathbb{B}
. For groups
\Gamma\in M
, we establish a connection between
F(\Gamma,2)
and
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
(Theorem
4.3
), and use this to show that
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
is not amenable (Theorem
4.4
), not treeable (Theorem
4.7
), and in particular, not hyperfinite. We also produce partial results concerning whether
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
can be induced by a free action (Theorem
4.9
) and whether it is universal (Theorem
4.11
).

Section
5
concludes the paper with a series of further questions which we hope will motivate continued study of the equivalence relations
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
.

When we assert that “
\mathbb{P}

M
”, or just “
\mathbb{P}\in M

\mathbb{P}
is a set partially ordered by
\leq
, and both
\mathbb{P}

\leq
are elements of
M
.
\mathbb{P}

\mathbf{V}

M\models\text{``$\mathbb{A}$ is a complete Boolean algebra''}

2.1.
Spaces of generics

Recall that a filter
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is
M
-generic
if it intersects each dense subset of
\mathbb{P}
which is contained in
M
. Since
M
is countable, such filters always exist (Lemma VII.2.3 in
[
16
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

For a partial order
\mathbb{P}
in
M
, the
space of
M
-generics for
\mathbb{P}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
, is

\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}=\{G\subseteq\mathbb{P}:G\text{ is an $M$-generic filter}\},

We identify
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
as a subspace of
2^{\mathbb{P}}
with the product topology.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
2^{\mathbb{P}}
, and thus a Polish space.

Proof.

Note that for any
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the sets
\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:p\in F\}
and
\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:p\notin F\}
are clopen. Enumerate the dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}
in
M
as
\{D_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Then,

\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}=\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:F\text{ is a filter}\}\cap\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:F\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset\}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:F\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset\}=\bigcup_{p\in D_{n}}\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:p\in D\},

and these sets are open, so
\bigcap_{n\in\omega}\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:F\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset\}
is
G_{\delta}
.

F\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is a filter if and only if
F\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:\forall p,q\in\mathbb{P}((p\in F\land p\leq q)\rightarrow q\in F)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{F:\forall p,q((p,q\in F)\rightarrow\exists r(r\in F\land r\leq p,q))\}.

Observe that

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\bigcap_{p\leq q\in\mathbb{P}}\left(\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:p\notin F\}\cup\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:q\in F\}\right),

which is
G_{\delta}
, while

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\bigcap_{p,q\in\mathbb{P}}\left(\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:p\notin F\}\cup\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:q\notin F\}\cup\bigcup_{r\leq p,q}\{F\subseteq\mathbb{P}:r\in F\}\right)

which is also
G_{\delta}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is
G_{\delta}
.
∎

The following will simplify arguments involving the topology of
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The topology on
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
has a basis consisting of clopen sets of the form

N_{p}=\{G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}:p\in G\}

for
p\in\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
has the subspace topology it inherits from
2^{\mathbb{P}}
, the sets
N_{p}
above are clopen. Suppose we are given a non-empty basic open set

U=\{G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}:p_{0},\ldots,p_{n}\in G\text{ and }q_{0},\ldots,q_{m}\notin G\},

for
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n},q_{0},\ldots,q_{m}\in\mathbb{P}
. Since
M
-generic filters for
\mathbb{P}
are, in particular, maximal, we may find conditions
q_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,q_{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}
so that

U^{\prime}=\{G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}:p_{0},\ldots,p_{n},q_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,q_{m}^{\prime}\in G\}

is non-empty and contained in
U
. Taking
p
to be a common lower bound of
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n},q_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,q_{m}^{\prime}
, which exists since
U^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, we have
\emptyset\neq N_{p}\subseteq U^{\prime}
.∎

Recall that a partial order
\mathbb{P}
is
atomless
if for any
p\in\mathbb{P}
, there are
q,r\leq p
with
q\perp r
(i.e., they have no common lower bound). In all cases of interest,
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
will be uncountable, a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
has no isolated points if and only if
\mathbb{P}
is atomless.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is atomless. Take
G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
. By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to consider a basic open set
N_{p}
containing
G
, for
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Since
\mathbb{P}
is atomless, there are
q,r\leq p
with
q\perp r
. Take
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
to be
M
-generic filters containing
q
and
r
, respectively. Then
G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}\in N_{p}
, and at least one of them must be unequal to
G
, showing that
G
is not isolated.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
has no isolated points. Take
p\in\mathbb{P}
, and let
G
be an
M
-generic filter containing
p
. Since
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
has no isolated points, there is a filter
G^{\prime}\in N_{p}
distinct from
G
, say with
p^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}\setminus G
. As
G
is maximal, there is a
q\in G
, which we may assume is
\leq p
, with
q\perp p^{\prime}
. But
p\in G^{\prime}
, so there is also a
r\leq p,p^{\prime}
, and thus
q\perp r
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

For
\mathbb{P}
a partial order in
M
, define
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
on
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
by

G\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}H\quad\text{if and only if}\quad M[G]=M[H].

By the minimality of generic extensions (Lemma VII.2.9 in
[
16
]
),
G\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}H
if and only if
G\in M[H]
and
H\in M[G]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is a countable Borel equivalence relation.

Proof.

For
G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
, the
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
-class of
G
is a subset of
M[G]
, which is countable as there are only countably many
\mathbb{P}
-names in
M
. To see that
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is Borel, note that
G\in M[H]
if and only if there is a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\tau\in M
such that for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
,

p\in G\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\exists q\in H(q\mathrel{\Vdash}\check{p}\in\tau).

Since the forcing relation is arithmetic in a code for the model
M
, and both
M
and
\mathbb{P}
are countable, this is a Borel condition on
G
and
H
.∎

The next lemma verifies that the Borel complexity of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is invariant for forcing-equivalent presentations of
\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
are partial orders in
M
.

(a)

If
i:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is a dense embedding in
M
, then
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}\;\cong_{B}\;\equiv^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
.

(b)

If
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
have isomorphic Boolean completions in
M
, then
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}\;\cong_{B}\;\equiv^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
.

Proof.

(a) Define
\widehat{i}:\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}\to\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
by
\widehat{i}(H)=i^{-1}(H)
. By standard results (Theorem VII.7.11 in
[
16
]
),
\widehat{i}
is a well-defined bijection with inverse

\widehat{i}^{-1}(G)=\{q\in\mathbb{Q}:\exists p\in G(i(p)\leq q)\},

and satisfying
M[H]=M[\widehat{i}(H)]
for all
H\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
. Thus

M[H_{0}]=M[H_{1}]\quad\text{if and only if}\quad M[\widehat{i}(H_{0})]=M[\widehat{i}(H_{1})]

for all
H_{0},H_{1}\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
, showing that
\widehat{i}
is a reduction. To see that
\widehat{i}
is Borel, given
N_{p}\subseteq\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
, for
p\in\mathbb{P}
, a basic clopen as in Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\widehat{i}^{-1}(N_{p})

\displaystyle=\{H\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}:p\in\widehat{i}(H)\}

\displaystyle=\{H\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}:i(p)\in H\},

which is clopen in
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}
. Thus,
\widehat{i}
is continuous, and in particular, Borel.

(b) Follows immediately from (a) by composing Borel isomorphisms.
∎

Every Borel set in
M
is
coded
by a real
\alpha
in
M
; we denote the interpretation of this code in a model
N\supseteq M
by
B_{\alpha}^{N}
, a Borel set in
N
, omitting the superscript when
N=\mathbf{V}
. We will often just refer to a Borel set
B
coded in
M
, without reference to the code itself. Likewise for Borel functions coded in
M
, identified with their Borel graphs. The basic properties of Borel codes can be found in
[
13
]
or
[
25
]
.

Many partial orders whose generic extensions are generated by adjoining a single real can be presented as
idealized forcings

[
32
]
, that is, as the set
P_{I}
of all Borel subsets of
2^{\omega}
not in
I
, ordered by inclusion, where
I
is a non-trivial
\sigma
-ideal of Borel sets.

Given an
M
-generic filter
G
for an idealized forcing
P_{I}
in
M
, there is a unique (Proposition 2.1.2 in
[
32
]
) real
x_{G}\in 2^{\omega}
in
M[G]
such that

\{x_{G}\}=\bigcap\{B^{M[G]}:B^{M}\in G\}=\bigcap\{B:B^{M}\in G\},

called an
M
-generic real
for
P_{I}
. Since
G
is computed from
x_{G}
in any model
N\supseteq M
containing it as

G=\{B^{M}\in P_{I}:x_{G}\in B^{N}\},

we have that
M[G]=M[x_{G}]
.

Given an idealized forcing
P_{I}
in
M
, let
\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}
be the set of all
M
-generic reals for
P_{I}
. Abusing notation, we define
\equiv^{P_{I}}_{M}
in the obvious way on
\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}
is a Borel subset of
2^{\omega}
, and thus a standard Borel space.

Proof.

x
is an
M
-generic real for
P_{I}
if and only if

x\in\bigcap_{{\mathcal{D}\in M}\atop\text{$\mathcal{D}$ dense in $P_{I}$}}\bigcup\{B:B^{M}\in\mathcal{D}\}.

Since
M
is a countable transitive model, the set on the right is Borel.
∎

Exactly as in Lemma
2.6
, we have:

Lemma 2.9
.

\equiv^{P_{I}}_{M}
is a countable Borel equivalence relation.∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that in
M
,
\mathbb{P}
is a partial order,
I
a
\sigma
-ideal of Borel subsets of
2^{\omega}
, and
i:P_{I}\to\mathbb{P}
a dense embedding. Then,
\equiv^{P_{I}}_{M}\;\cong_{B}\;\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.7
, the map
\widehat{i}:\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}\to\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}
given by
\widehat{i}(H)=x_{i^{-1}(H)}
, the
M
-generic real corresponding to
i^{-1}(H)
, is a bijection which maps
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
-equivalent filters to
\equiv^{P_{I}}_{M}
-equivalent reals, and vice-versa. To see that
\widehat{i}
is Borel, given
N_{p}\subseteq\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
with
p\in\mathbb{P}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{i}(N_{p})=\{x\in\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}:x\in i(p)^{\mathbf{V}}\}=\mathcal{G}^{P_{I}}_{M}\cap i(p)^{\mathbf{V}},

which is Borel by Lemma
2.8
. Thus,
\widehat{i}^{-1}
is Borel, and so
\widehat{i}
is as well.
∎

In sum, when analyzing the complexity of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
we will be able to use various equivalent presentations of
\mathbb{P}
.

2.2.
Automorphisms and homogeneity

Given a partial order
\mathbb{P}
in
M
, let
\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})
be the automorphism group of
\mathbb{P}
in
M
. By absoluteness,

\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P})\cap M.

There is a natural action
\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})\curvearrowright\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
given by

(e,G)\mapsto e^{\prime\prime}G=\{e(p):p\in G\},

for
e\in\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})
and
G\in\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
. This action is well-defined and
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
-invariant, in the sense that
M[e^{\prime\prime}G]=M[G]
(Corollary VII.7.6 in
[
16
]
). Treating
\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})
as a discrete group, this action is continuous.

A partial order
\mathbb{P}
is
weakly homogeneous
(in
M
) if for all
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
, there is an automorphism
e
of
\mathbb{P}
(in
M
) such that
e(p)
is compatible with
q
.

A continuous action of a group
\Gamma
(or equivalence relation
E
, respectively) on a Polish space
X
is
generically ergodic
if every
\Gamma
-invariant (
E
-invariant, respectively) Borel set is either meager or comeager. Equivalently, the action of
\Gamma
is generically ergodic if and only if for all non-empty open
U,V\subseteq X
, there is a
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
(cf. Proposition 6.1.9 in
[
6
]
)

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a partial order in
M
. The action of
\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})
on
\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is generically ergodic if and only if
\mathbb{P}
is weakly homogeneous in
M
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is weakly homogeneous. Let
U,V\subseteq\mathrm{Gen}^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
be non-empty open sets. By Lemma
2.3
, we may assume that
U=N_{p}
and
V=N_{q}
, for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
. By weak homogeneity, there is an
e\in\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{P})
such that
e(p)
is compatible with
q
, say with common lower bound
r
. Let
G
be an
M
-generic filter for
\mathbb{P}
which contains
e^{-1}(r)
. Since
e^{-1}(r)\leq p
,
G\in N_{p}
. Likewise, since
r\in e^{\prime\prime}G
and
r\leq q
,
e^{\prime\prime}G\in N_{q}
, proving
e(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that the action is generically ergodic. Pick

5.
Further questions

Much of this paper has focused on Cohen and random forcing. This is due to the intimate connections these examples have with Baire category and measure, respectively. A natural next step is to consider those partial orders mentioned in Example
2.15
:

Question 5.1
.

What can we say about the complexity of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
when
\mathbb{P}
is, e.g., Sacks, Miller, Mathias, or Laver forcing, or any of the other classical forcing notions? In particular, is
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
hyperfinite in any of these cases?

The increasing unions problem for hyperfinite equivalence relations
[
4
]
asks whether an increasing union of countably many hyperfinite Borel equivalence relations is hyperfinite. By Theorem
3.1
, a positive resolution to this problem would imply that
\equiv^{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
is hyperfinite, when
\mathbb{C}
is Cohen forcing. This provides an interesting special case of this long-standing open problem:

Question 5.2
.

Is
\equiv^{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
hyperfinite?

Given a positive answer to Question
5.2
, the Glimm-Effros dichotomy for countable Borel equivalence relations (Theorem 1.5 in
[
11
]
) would allow us to improve Corollary
4.5
to
\equiv^{\mathbb{C}}_{M}\;<_{B}\;\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
.

In Theorem
4.9
, we left open the question of whether
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
, for
\mathbb{B}
random forcing, is
essentially free
, that is, reducible to the orbit equivalence relation of a free Borel action of some countable group. Note that it is easy to check that the associated action of
\mathrm{Aut}^{M}(\mathbb{B})
on the random reals is not free.

Question 5.3
.

Is
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
essentially free?

Since there is no countable collection of groups such that an equivalence relation is essentially free if and only if it is reducible to an orbit equivalence relation coming from a free action of a group in that collection (cf. Corollary 3.10 in
[
28
]
), Theorem
4.9
cannot be used directly to resolve Question
5.3
.

Can we obtain the result in Theorem
4.11
without the extra hypotheses of Marks’s uniformity conjecture and the
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1}_{2}
-correctness of
M
?

Question 5.4
.

Is
\equiv^{\mathbb{B}}_{M}
non-universal (for any
M
)?

More generally, to what extent does the model
M
affect the complexity of
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
? In asking this question, we have to avoid certain trivialities; for instance, by collapsing
|\mathbb{P}|^{M}
to
\aleph_{0}
, we can go from a model
M
for which
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M}
is smooth (Example
2.17
), to a model
M^{\prime}
in which
\mathbb{P}
is equivalent to Cohen forcing and thus
\equiv^{\mathbb{P}}_{M^{\prime}}
is not smooth. So, we focus on the case of idealized forcings which are
\mathsf{ZFC}
-correct, in the sense of
[
32
]
, and thus have a natural interpretation in any model.

Question 5.5
.

Is there a
\mathsf{ZFC}
-correct
\sigma
-ideal
I
on
2^{\omega}
and countable transitive models
M
and
N
such that
\equiv^{(P_{I})^{M}}_{M}
and
\equiv^{(P_{I})^{N}}_{N}

Periodic Trading Activities in Financial Markets: Mean-field Liquidation Game with Major-Minor Players Thanks: We thank Guanxing Fu for helpful and constructive comments and discussion.
Yufan Chen, Lan Wu, Renyuan Xu, and Ruixun Zhang

Abstract




Abstract

Motivated by recent empirical findings on the periodic phenomenon of aggregated market volumes in equity markets, we aim to understand the causes and consequences of periodic trading activities through a game-theoretic perspective, examining market interactions among different types of participants. Specifically, we introduce a new mean-field liquidation game involving major and minor traders, where the major trader evaluates her strategy against a periodic targeting strategy while a continuum of minor players trade against her. We establish the existence and uniqueness of an open-loop Nash equilibrium. In addition, we prove an
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
approximation rate of the mean-field solution to the Nash equilibrium in a major-minor game with
N
minor players. In equilibrium, minor traders exhibit front-running behaviors in both the periodic and trend components of their strategies, reducing the major trader’s profit. Such strategic interactions diminish the strength of periodicity in both overall trading volume and asset prices. Our model rationalizes observed periodic trading activities in the market and offers new insights into market dynamics.

Keywords
: Periodicity in financial market; Algorithmic trading; Optimal liquidation; Mean-field game.

Introduction

Motivation.

It has long been documented that trading activities and trading volumes contain persistent high-frequency periodicities beyond the well-known intraday U shapes.
For instance,
Heston, Korajczyk, and Sadka (2010)
observed that stock returns and trading volumes in half-hour intervals show periodicities at daily frequencies;
Hasbrouck and Saar (2013)
found that trading messages arrive at the market with large peaks in several milliseconds shortly after the one-second boundary;
Wu, Zhang, and Dai (2022)
documented strong and universal second- and minute-level periodicities in a large panel of US and Chinese stocks;
Hansen, Kim, and Kimbrough (2024)
found intraday periodic patterns in volatility and volume in the cryptocurrency market.

These periodic patterns yield important implications for the underlying asset and market participants.
They contain information on the timing and magnitude of price changes
(
Karpoff, 1987
;
Wang, 1994
)
, lead to abnormal excess returns for stocks dominated by high-frequency fluctuations in volume
(
Chinco and Ye, 2017
)
, are associated with price efficiency and price informativeness in the market
(
Wu, Zhang, and Dai, 2022
)
, and provide better predictions for intraday volume and execution costs
(
Brownlees, Cipollini, and Gallo, 2011
;
Wu, Zhang, and Dai, 2022
)
.

Despite the persistence and importance of these periodicities, it remains unclear what drives them, and how the interactions between market participants affect these patterns. One potential driving factor is the
periodic targeting strategy
of institutional traders. When liquidating positions, institutional traders have incentives not only to reduce price impacts but also to minimize deviations from pre-specified targeting strategies
(
Cartea and Jaimungal, 2015
;
Cheng, Guo, and Wang, 2024
)
that track, for example, implementation shortfall orders
(
Almgren and Chriss, 2001
)
, volume-weighted average price (VWAP), or time-weighted average price (TWAP)
(
Guéant, Pu, and Royer, 2015
)
.
Such targeting strategies are naturally implemented with discrete-time periodicities in practice.

Our work and contributions.

To understand the causes and consequences of periodic trading activities through a game-theoretic perspective, we introduce a new mean-field liquidation game involving a major and a continuum of minor traders. The major trader evaluates and improves her strategy against a periodic and deterministic targeting strategy while a continuum of minor players trades against her and tries to make profits. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time a mean-field game (MFG) has been used to explain the periodic phenomena in financial markets that have been extensively documented in the empirical studies discussed above.

To explain and understand periodicity across the trader population, this liquidation game introduces three key modeling innovations that distinguish it from the existing literature, presenting unique mathematical challenges.
Firstly, the game stands out as an extended MFG, where traders’ interactions are shaped by the permanent impacts of their strategies on the fundamental price. Secondly, all traders, both major and minor, are influenced by a common noise that drives the benchmark price process, reflecting the impact of market uncertainties faced by all participants. Lastly, traders face terminal state constraints due to liquidation requirements, which are consistent with financial practices.

Theoretically, we adopt a probabilistic approach to establish the existence and uniqueness of an open-loop Nash equilibrium. Similar approaches have been used to study the Nash equilibrium of extended MFGs
without
a major player
(
Fu et al., 2021
)
and MFGs with a major player, where players interact with each other through the states
but not the controls

(
Carmona and Delarue, 2018
, Chapter 7.1)
. In addition, we prove an
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
approximation rate of the mean-field solution to the Nash equilibrium in a major-minor game with
N
minor players.

We show both theoretically and numerically that, in equilibrium, minor traders exhibit front-running behaviors in both the periodic and trend components of their strategies, which reduces the major trader’s profit, an implication consistent with the findings of
Brunnermeier and Pedersen
’s (
2005
) model of predator trading.
The major trader reduces the strength of her periodic behavior in response to the strategic actions of the minor trader.
Overall, such strategic interactions diminish the strength of periodicity in both the overall trading volume and asset prices, thereby serving as a stabilizing mechanism for the aggregate market. To conclude, our model rationalizes observed periodic trading activities in the market and offers new insights into market dynamics.

Related literature.

Our work is closely related to two lines of literature.

Mean-field games (MFGs).

MFG was first introduced in the seminal works of
(
Caines, Huang, and Malhamé, 2006
;
Lasry and Lions, 2007
)
, which studied a stochastic game with a continuum of homogeneous agents interacting through the distribution of the population state. Since these foundational studies, extensive research has explored various extensions of MFG. While a comprehensive survey is well beyond the scope of this paper, we summarize several key directions that closely align with our formulation.

Extended MFGs focus on game interactions through action distributions instead of state distributions. In this framework,
Gomes and Voskanyan (2016)
proved an existence result and established uniqueness conditions under deterministic dynamics;
Graber (2016)
studied the linear-quadratic formulation. See also
Gomes, Patrizi, and Voskanyan (2014)
for the results on infinite-horizon stationary setting and
Bonnans, Hadikhanloo, and Pfeiffer (2021)
for potential games.

MFGs with common noise consider scenarios where the dynamics of individual agents is influenced by a common process such as Brownian motion.
Ahuja (2016)
and
Carmona, Delarue, and Lacker (2016)
provided the existence and uniqueness of solutions for MFGs with common noise.
Bensoussan, Yam, and Zhang (2015)
and
Cardaliaguet et al. (2019)
studied the well-posedness of the corresponding master equations. In addition,
Djete (2023)
provided the existence and approximated Nash equilibrium for extended MFGs with common noise.

To address heterogeneity among agents, the framework of Major-Minor MFG, which involves a major player and a continuum of minor players, has gained popularity in both theoretical exploration and practical applications. Under the Nash equilibrium criterion,
Huang (2010)
first introduced the concept of MFG with a major player in a linear-quadratic setting (with linear dynamics).
Nourian and Caines (2013)
and
Carmona and Zhu (2016)
further extended this framework to nonlinear stochastic state dynamics using a probabilistic approach. Recently,
Lasry and Lions (2018)
and
Cardaliaguet, Cirant, and Porretta (2020)
studied a general setting using the master equation approach. In addition to the Nash equilibrium criterion, the Stackelberg game criterion has also been investigated; see
Bensoussan, Chau, and Yam (2016)
;
Huang, Si, and Wu (2021)
;
Guo, Hu, and Zhang (2022)
;
Aurell et al. (2022)
;
Dayanikli and Lauriere (2023)
.

We remark that none of the existing theoretical developments mentioned above can be directly applied to establish the existence and uniqueness of our proposed Major-Minor MFG, due to the unique mathematical formulation that arises from our context of periodic trading.

Liquidation games.
The research on optimal liquidation for a single trader traces back to at least
Bertsimas and Lo (1998)
and
Almgren and Chriss (2001)
, which sought to identify dynamic trading strategies that minimize the expected trading cost over a fixed period. For a review of this extensive literature, we direct interested readers to
Cartea (2015)
and references therein.

A natural extension is to study optimal execution games with a finite number of players to understand the strategic interactions and competitions among traders. For example,
Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2005)
and
Carlin, Lobo, and Viswanathan (2007)
analyzed a two-player game in which one player has a liquidation target, while the other exploits this information to engage in predatory trading.
Cont, Micheli, and Neuman (2023)
studied a stochastic Stackelberg liquidation game between a low-frequency and a high-frequency trader, in which equilibrium strategies exhibit daily periodic patterns when the high-frequency trader adopts a periodic inventory penalty. Other studies on liquidation games, such as
Schoeneborn and Schied (2009)
,
Moallemi, Park, and Van Roy (2012)
,
Schied and Zhang (2018)
, and
Voß (2022)
, however, do not focus on the periodic phenomena.

Stochastic games with more than two players are usually difficult to solve, which limits the insights they generate. In contrast, the mean-field approximation provides an ingenious and tractable aggregation approach to approximate the otherwise challenging
N
-player stochastic games. In the mean-field regime,
Carmona and Lacker (2015)
developed a probabilistic formulation to study price impact models, where players interact through the impact of their strategies on the payoff functions of the other players. This model has been further discussed in
Cardaliaguet and Lehalle (2018)
and
Casgrain and Jaimungal (2020)
.
Fu et al. (2021)
considered an MFG of optimal portfolio liquidation under asymmetric information with hard terminal conditions. Extensions include the incorporation of the market drop-out time
(
Fu, Hager, and Horst, 2024b
)
and the market entering time
(
Fu, Hager, and Horst, 2024a
)
.

When both major and minor market participants are considered,
Huang, Jaimungal, and Nourian (2019)
studied the Nash equilibrium of an optimal execution game using a dynamic programming approach.
Although our formulation is largely inspired by
Huang, Jaimungal, and Nourian (2019)
, their dynamic programming approach cannot handle the terminal state constraint in our setting. In addition,
Huang, Jaimungal, and Nourian (2019)
does not consider targeting strategies for the major player. Finally, the Stackelberg MFG was analyzed in
Féron, Tankov, and Tinsi (2020)
,
Fu and Horst (2020)
and
Evangelista and Thamsten (2021)
for liquidation games.

). If the optimal strategy is unique (which can be proved; see Remark
2
), we can define the mapping

\mathcal{M}(\mu;v^{\mathrm{Mj}})=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]\right)_{t\in[0,T]}.

Conditioned on a fixed strategy from the major trader
v^{\mathrm{Mj}}=(v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj}}
, we say that
(\mu^{*},v^{\mathrm{Mn},*})\in L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is the corresponding Conditional Mean Field Equilibrium (CMFE), if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(a)

Consistency:

\mu^{*}
satisfies
\mathcal{M}(\mu^{*};v^{\mathrm{Mj}})=\mu^{*}
(i.e.,
\mu^{*}
is a fixed-point of
\mathcal{M}(\cdot;v^{\mathrm{Mj}})
), and

(b)

Optimality:

v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}
is the optimal strategy of the control problem (
2.12
) under
\mu=\mu^{*}
.

We note that the CMFE of
Problem

2
,
(\mu^{*},v^{\mathrm{Mn},*})
, satisfies the following consistency condition:

\mu^{*}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right],\,\mathbb{P}-\mathrm{a.s.},\quad t\in[0,T].

(2.13)

Moreover,
Problem

2
is an extended MFG
(
Gomes and Voskanyan, 2016
;
Graber, 2016
)
, where players interact with each other through their controls.

If
Problem

2
has a unique CMFE solution
(\mu^{*},v^{\mathrm{Mn},*})
for any given
v^{\mathrm{Mj}}\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj}}
(which can be proved; see Remark
2
), the following well-defined response function of the representative minor trader

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{Mn}}:\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj}}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mn}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{Mj}}

\displaystyle\mapsto v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}.

With the response functions of the major trader (
2.11
) and the representative minor trader (
2.14
), we define the open-loop Nash equilibrium for the above Major-Minor MFG as follows.

Definition 1

(Open-loop Nash Equilibrium for the Major-Minor MFG)
.

A couple of admissible strategies
(v^{\mathrm{Mj},*},v^{\mathrm{Mn},*})\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj}}\times\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mn}}
is an open-loop Nash equilibrium for the Major-Minor MFG if

v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}=\Gamma^{\mathrm{Mj}}(v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}),\quad v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}=\Gamma^{\mathrm{Mn}}(v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}).

Remark 2

(Well-definedness of the open-loop Nash equilibrium)
.

The well-definedness of the open-loop Nash equilibrium in
Definition

1
relies on two key assumptions: the uniqueness of the solutions to the stochastic control problems (
2.10
) and (
2.12
), and the uniqueness of the CMFE solution
(\mu^{*},v^{\mathrm{Mn},*})
in
Problem

2
. The first uniqueness condition is easy to establish because both (
2.10
) and (
2.12
) are of linear-quadratic forms. In addition, the second uniqueness condition is proved in
Theorem

2
.

Remark 3

(Nash vs. Stackelberg Equilibria)
.

Note that both Major-Minor Nash equilibrium and Stackelberg equilibrium are used to study the game between a major player and a continuum of minor players. See
Féron, Tankov, and Tinsi (2020)
;
Fu and Horst (2020)
;
Evangelista and Thamsten (2021)
for references on Stackerberg games. Mathematically, the difference is that in the Stackelberg game,
\bar{v}^{\mathrm{Mn}}
in
Problem

1
is replaced by
\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{\mathrm{Mn}}(v^{\mathrm{Mj}})\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]
. Intuitively, in the Stackelberg equilibrium, the major trader commits to a strategy first and then the minor traders make their decisions based on this strategy. However, in practice, public commitment to a strategy is infeasible because minor traders can arbitrage the committed strategy, leading major traders to naturally avoid such information-revealing disadvantages. Consequently, for the financial application we focus on, it is more appropriate to consider the Nash equilibrium defined in Definition
1
, where all traders determine their strategies simultaneously.

2.2
Periodic Strategy

Throughout the paper, we focus on scenarios where the major trader evaluates her strategy against a periodic targeting strategy. In this section, we introduce the mathematical definitions of periodic strategies, starting with differentiable periodic strategies characterized by their trading rates and extending this concept to generic periodic strategies with piecewise differentiable inventory processes.

Definition 2

(Differentiable Periodic Strategy)
.

A differentiable trading strategy with inventory process
Q=(Q_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and trading rate
v=(v_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is called a differentiable periodic strategy with
n
periods and initial inventory
q_{0}
, if
Q_{0}=q_{0}
and
v_{t}
is a periodic function with
n
periods in
[0,T]
. That is,

v_{t}=v_{t+T/n},\quad t\in\left[0,(n-1)T/n\right].

The differentiable TWAP strategy is an example of a differentiable periodic strategy with a constant trading rate.

Example 1

(Differentiable TWAP Strategy)
.

The inventory process of a differentiable TWAP strategy with initial inventory
q_{0}
is defined as

Q_{t}^{\text{\emph{D-TWAP}},q_{0}}:=q_{0}\cdot\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right),

and its trading rate is

v_{t}^{\text{\emph{D-TWAP}},q_{0}}:=-\frac{q_{0}}{T}.

Among the differentiable periodic strategies, the cosine trading strategy will be frequently used in later analysis.

Example 2

(Cosine Trading Strategy)
.

The inventory process of a cosine trading strategy is defined as

Q^{\cos,n,q_{0},b}_{t}:=q_{0}\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)+b\sin\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}t\right),\quad t\in[0,T].

(2.15)

The corresponding trading rate is

v^{\cos,n,q_{0},b}_{t}=-\frac{q_{0}}{T}+b\frac{2\pi n}{T}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{T}t\right).

(2.16)

For a differentiable periodic strategy
Q
with
n
periods and an initial inventory
q_{0}
, one can show that
Q_{kT/n}=q_{0}(1-k/n)
for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
. Then, for
\tilde{Q}_{t}:=Q_{t}-Q_{t}^{\text{D-TWAP},q_{0}}
, we have

\tilde{Q}_{t+T/n}-\tilde{Q}_{t}=\int_{t}^{t+T/n}\left(v_{t}+\frac{q_{0}}{T}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T/n}\left(v_{t}+\frac{q_{0}}{T}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=(Q_{0}-Q_{T/n})+\frac{q_{0}}{n}=0

for any
t\in\left[0,(n-1)T/n\right]
. Therefore,
\tilde{Q}_{t}
is a periodic function with
n
periods in
[0,T]
satisfying
\tilde{Q}_{kT/n}=0
for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
. In other words, any differentiable periodic strategy following Definition
2
can be written as a combination of a differentiable TWAP strategy and a periodic round-trip strategy.

Based on this observation, we can define a more general periodic strategy, relying solely on the inventory process, which may not have a trading rate as assumed in
Definition

2
.

Definition 3

(Periodic Strategy)
.

A trading strategy is called a periodic strategy with
n
periods and initial inventory
q_{0}
, if the inventory process
Q=(Q_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
has a decomposition:

Q_{t}=Q_{t}^{\text{\emph{D-TWAP}},q_{0}}+\tilde{Q}_{t},

(2.17)

where
Q_{t}^{\text{\emph{D-TWAP}},q_{0}}
is the inventory process of a differentiable TWAP strategy with initial inventory
q_{0}
, and
\tilde{Q}_{t}
is a periodic piecewise differentiable function with
n
periods in
[0,T]
satisfying

\tilde{Q}_{k\cdot T/n}=0,\quad k=0,1,\ldots,n.

(2.18)

With
Definition

3
, we can further consider a TWAP strategy that trades only at given discrete timestamps
t=kT/n
for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
.

Example 3

(TWAP Strategy)
.

A trading strategy is called a TWAP strategy with
n
periods and an initial inventory
q_{0}
if its inventory process is

Q^{\mathrm{TWAP},q_{0},n}_{t}=\begin{cases}\frac{n-k}{n}q_{0},&t=\frac{k}{n}T,\,k=0,1,\ldots,n,\\
\frac{2n-2k+1}{2n}q_{0},&\frac{k-1}{n}T<t<\frac{k}{n}T,\,k=1,2,\ldots,n.\\
\end{cases}

(2.19)

To see that (
2.19
) satisfies Definition
3
,

Q^{\mathrm{TWAP},q_{0},n}_{t}-Q_{t}^{\text{\emph{D-TWAP}},q_{0}}=\begin{cases}0,&t=\frac{k}{n}T,\,k=0,1,\ldots,n,\\
-\frac{q_{0}}{2n}+\frac{q_{0}}{T}\left(t-\frac{k-1}{n}T\right),&\frac{k-1}{n}T<t<\frac{k}{n}T,\,k=1,\ldots,n\\
\end{cases}

is a periodic piecewise constant function with
n
periods in
[0,T]
and satisfies (
2.18
).

3
The Open-Loop Nash Equilibrium

In this section, we adopt a probabilistic approach to characterize the Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG introduced in Section
2.1
. Our method is inspired by
Fu et al. (2021)
and
Carmona and Delarue (2018)
. In particular,
Fu et al. (2021)
study the Nash equilibrium of extended MFGs
without
a major player, and Chapter 7.1 of
Carmona and Delarue (2018)
studies MFGs with a major player, where players interact with each other through the states
but not the controls
. We integrate these existing methods to solve our Major-Minor MFG, with a major player and interactions through controls.

3.1
Stochastic Maximum Principle

For the major trader, the Hamiltonian of
Problem

1
is defined

H^{\mathrm{Mj}}(t,q^{\mathrm{Mj}},p^{\mathrm{Mj}},v^{\mathrm{Mj}};\mu,R)=p^{\mathrm{Mj}}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}+\lambda\left(q^{\mathrm{Mj}}-R_{t}\right)\mu_{t}-a_{0}\left(v^{\mathrm{Mj}}\right)^{2}-\phi\left(q^{\mathrm{Mj}}-R_{t}\right)^{2},

where
H^{\mathrm{Mj}}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{PD}([0,T])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
According to the stochastic maximum principle
(
Yong and Zhou, 1999
)
, the solution to the optimization problem can be characterized as a forward-backward stochastic differential equation (FBSDE):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi_{0}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t}\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

Here the process
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}
is the adjoint process to the controlled state process
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}
, and
Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\in L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
is the martingale representation term of
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}
with respect to
W^{0}
. The liquidation constraint
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0
results in a
singularity
of the value function at the liquidation time. As a result, the terminal condition for
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}

cannot be determined a priori
. In particular, the first equation of (
3.1
) holds on
[0,T]
, and the second equation holds on
[0,T)
. A standard approach
(
Yong and Zhou, 1999
)
yields the following candidate optimal control:

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{2a_{0}}.

(3.2)

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
),
Problem

1
reduces to the following FBSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{2a_{0}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi_{0}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t}\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

Similarly, the Hamiltonian of
Problem

2
for the representative minor trader is defined as

H^{\mathrm{Mn}}(t,q^{\mathrm{Mn}},v^{\mathrm{Mn}},p^{\mathrm{Mn}};v^{\mathrm{Mj}},\mu)=p^{\mathrm{Mn}}v^{\mathrm{Mn}}+q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\left(\lambda_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\lambda\mu_{t}\right)-a\left(v^{\mathrm{Mn}}\right)^{2}-\phi\left(q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\right)^{2},

where
H^{\mathrm{Mn}}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The stochastic maximum principle suggests that the solution to the optimization problem can be characterized in terms of the FBSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=(\lambda_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\lambda\mu_{t}-2\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}&=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

where
\tilde{W}=(W^{0},W)
, and
Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\in L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
is the martingale representation of
P^{\mathrm{Mn}}
with respect to
\tilde{W}
. Similar to (
3.1
), the first equation of (
3.4
) holds on
[0,T]
, and the second equation holds on
[0,T)
. A standard approach
(
Yong and Zhou, 1999
)
yields the candidate optimal control

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}.

(3.5)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) with the consistency condition (
2.13
), the analysis of
Problem

2
reduces to the following McKean-Vlasov FBSDE (MV-FBSDE):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\lambda_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}&=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

Finally, combining (
3.3
) and (
3.6
), the open-loop Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG is characterized by the following MV-FBSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{2a_{0}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-dP^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi_{0}(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\lambda_{0}\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{2a_{0}}+\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0,\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

3.2
General Existence and Uniqueness of Solutions

Now we are ready to proceed with the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to the MV-FBSDE system (
3.7
). To establish these results, we assume that the following conditions hold.

Assumption 1
.

(i)

The initial inventory of the representative minor trader,
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}
has bounded second moment, namely,
\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}|^{2}\right]<+\infty
.

(ii)

The deterministic target strategy of the major trader,
R=\left(R_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
, satisfies
\int_{0}^{T}\left(R_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<+\infty
.

(iii)

There exist constants
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}>0
such that

\theta_{2}>\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2},\quad 1/(\theta_{1}^{-1}+\theta_{3}^{-1})>\frac{\lambda}{2},\quad\theta_{1}<\frac{8\phi_{0}a}{\lambda},\quad\frac{\lambda_{0}}{a_{0}}\theta_{2}+\frac{\lambda}{a}\theta_{3}<8\phi.

(3.8)

Assumption
1
(i) is standard for the MFG to be well-defined
(
Bensoussan, Frehse, and Yam, 2013
;
Carmona and Delarue, 2018
)
. Assumption
1
(ii) guarantees the objective function of the major player is well-defined
(
Cartea, 2015
;
Cheng, Guo, and Wang, 2024
)
. Assumption
1
-(iii) ensures that the permanent impacts of the major and minor traders (
\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda
) will not dominate the temporary impacts (
a_{0}
and
a
) nor the inventory penalties (
\phi_{0}
and
\phi
). This is consistent with empirical findings observed in the financial market
(
Almgren and Chriss, 2001
;
Cartea and Jaimungal, 2016
)
. On the other hand, given that the major and minor traders interact with each other only through their permanent impacts on the price dynamics (
2.1
), Assumption
1
-(iii) is consistent with the weak interaction condition often assumed in the game theory literature (e.g.,
(
Horst, 2005
)
).

We are now ready to state the first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
1
, there exists a unique solution
(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}},Q^{\mathrm{Mn}},P^{\mathrm{Mj}},P^{\mathrm{Mn}},Z^{\mathrm{Mj}},Z^{\mathrm{Mn}})
to the FBSDE (
3.7
) in the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}

\displaystyle\times D_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times D_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})

(3.9)

\displaystyle\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)\times L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right).

The proof of Theorem
1
is deferred to Section
6.2
.

Next, we show that the solution of (
3.7
) indeed characterizes the Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG.

Theorem 2
.

Given the unique solution
\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mj},*},Q^{\mathrm{Mn},*},P^{\mathrm{Mj},*},P^{\mathrm{Mn},*},Z^{\mathrm{Mj},*},Z^{\mathrm{Mn},*}\right)
of the FBSDE (
3.7
), the unique Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG (defined in
Problems

1
and
2
) is

v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}}{2a_{0}},\quad v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}}{2a},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.10)

Under this unique Nash equilibrium, the optimal cost functionals of the major trader and the representative minor trader are

\displaystyle J^{\mathrm{Mj}}\left(v^{\mathrm{Mj},*};v^{\mathrm{Mn},*},R,q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[a_{0}\int_{0}^{T}v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}R_{t}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}-R_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right],

and

\displaystyle J^{\mathrm{Mj}}\left(v^{\mathrm{Mj},*};v^{\mathrm{Mn},*},R,q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[a\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}Q^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}\left(\lambda_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}+\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\middle|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right],

respectively.

The proof of Theorem
2
is deferred to Section
6.3
.

3.3
(N+1)
-player Game Approximation

Finally, we state the approximation property of the Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG to a finite-player game.

Throughout this section, we consider the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P})
as in Section
2.1
. This space further supports
N
independent
(m-1)
-dimensional standard Brownian motions
W^{1},\ldots,W^{N}
, which are independent of
W^{0}
. In addition,
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P})
carries
N
independent and identically distributed random variables
\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{Q}^{N}_{0}
that share the same distribution
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}
, and are independent of
W^{0},W^{1},\dots,W^{N}
. We define
\tilde{\mathbb{F}}^{i}:=(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{i}_{t},0\leqslant t\leqslant T)
with
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{i}_{t}=\sigma(\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{0},W^{0}_{s},W^{i}_{s},0\leqslant s\leqslant t)
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.

Consider a liquidation game between a major trader and
N
minor traders, which is a finite-player counterpart of the MFG in Section
2.1
. The major trader has an initial inventory
q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and the
i
-th minor trader has an initial inventory
q^{i}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,2,\dots,N
. The inventory process of the major trader
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}=(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and the
i
-th minor trader
Q^{i}=(Q^{i}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
follow, respectively,

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}

\displaystyle=v^{0}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\text{ with boundary conditions }Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0,

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=v^{i}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\text{ with boundary conditions }Q^{i}_{0}=q^{i}_{0},\,Q^{i}_{T}=0,

where
v^{\mathrm{Mj}}=(v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
v^{i}=(v^{i}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
are the trading rates. Moreover, the cost functionals of the major and the
i
-th minor trader are, respectively,

\displaystyle J^{N,\mathrm{Mj}}\left(v^{\mathrm{Mj}};\bm{v}^{(N)},R,q_{0}^{0}\right)

(3.11)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[-\lambda\int_{0}^{T}(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t})\bar{v}^{(N)}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+a_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\left(v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\phi_{0}\int_{0}^{T}(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right],

and

\displaystyle J^{N,i}\left(v^{i};v^{\mathrm{Mj}},\bm{v}^{(N),-i},q^{i}_{0}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[-\int_{0}^{T}Q^{i}_{t}\left(\lambda_{0}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\lambda\bar{v}^{(N)}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+a\int_{0}^{T}\left(v^{i}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\phi\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{i}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\middle|\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{0}=q^{i}_{0}\right].

Here
\bm{v}^{(N)}:=(v^{1},\ldots,v^{N})
,
\bm{v}^{(N),-i}:=(v^{1},\dots,v^{i-1},v^{i+1},\dots,v^{N})
for
i=1,2,\dots,N
, and
\bar{v}^{(N)}_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v^{i}_{t}/N

The Yamada-Watanabe result for MV-FBSDE
(
Carmona and Delarue, 2018
, Theorem 1.33)
yields that, there exist two measurable functions
\psi^{\mathrm{Mj}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{C}([0,T])\to\mathcal{C}([0,T-])
and
\psi^{\mathrm{Mn}}:\mathbb{R}\times(\mathcal{C}([0,T]))^{2}\to\mathcal{C}([0,T-])
such that the equilibrium trading rates can be expressed as

v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}=\psi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},W^{0}),\quad v^{\mathrm{Mn},*}=\psi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},W^{0},W).

(3.13)

From the integrability results in
Theorem

1
, we know that, there exist a constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\psi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},W^{0})\right|^{2}\right]\leqslant\kappa,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\psi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},W^{0},W)\right|^{2}\right]\leqslant\kappa,

(3.14)

and a deterministic function
K:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\psi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},W^{0},W)\right|^{2}\middle|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right]\leqslant K(q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}).

(3.15)

With the constant
\kappa
in (
3.14
) and the function
K
in (
3.15
), we consider the following admissible sets for the major trader and the
i
-th minor trader:

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj},\kappa}:=\left\{v\in L_{\mathbb{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\ \middle|\ \int_{0}^{T}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]\leqslant\kappa\right\}

(3.16)

\mathcal{A}^{i}:=\left\{v\in L_{\tilde{\mathbb{F}}^{i}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\ \middle|\ \int_{0}^{T}v^{i}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{0},\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|v^{i}_{t}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\middle|\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{0}=q^{i}_{0}\right]\leqslant K\left(q^{i}_{0}\right)\right\}.

(3.17)

We are now ready to state the approximate Nash equilibrium result for the Major-Minor MFG in
Section

2.1

v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}:=\psi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},W^{0}),\quad v^{i,*}:=\psi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{0},W^{0},W^{i}),\,i=1,\dots,N.

(3.18)

form an
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})
–Nash equilibrium for the finite-player game (
3.11
)-(
3.12

3.16
) and (
3.17

Theorem 3

(Approximate Nash Equilibrium)
.

Given
v^{\mathrm{Mj},*}

in (

\bm{v}^{(N),*}:=(v^{1,*},\ldots,v^{N,*})

\bm{v}^{(N),-i,*}:=(v^{1,*},\dots,v^{i-1,*},v^{i+1,*},\dots,v^{N,*})

3.4
Special Cases

In this section, we discuss two special cases of the Major-Minor MFG.

3.4.1
Special Case I: No Interactions

We consider a special case where the major trader does not affect the trading of minor traders, and vice versa. In this case, traders only consider their own price impacts, similar to the concept of “orderly liquidation value” introduced in
Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2005)
. This scenario serves as a benchmark to highlight the effects of interactions between traders.

Setting
\lambda=0
in
Problem

1
suggests that the trading activities of minor traders do not affect the strategy of the major trader. In this case, the stochastic control problem for the major trader reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{v^{\mathrm{Mj}}\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[a_{0}\int_{0}^{T}(v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\phi_{0}\int_{0}^{T}(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle dQ^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}=v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\quad Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0.

This formulation was studied in
Cheng, Guo, and Wang (2024)
, and the optimal strategy of the major trader can be characterized explicitly as

\displaystyle Q^{\mathrm{Mj},*,\lambda=0}_{t}

\displaystyle=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\cdot\frac{\sinh(\theta_{0}(T-t))}{\sinh(\theta_{0}T)}

(3.22)

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta_{0}}{\sinh(\theta_{0}T)}\int_{0}^{T}R_{s}\sinh(\theta_{0}\min(s,t))\sinh(\theta_{0}(T-\max(s,t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where
\theta_{0}=\sqrt{\phi_{0}/a_{0}}
.

Similarly, we set
\lambda_{0}=0
in
Problem

2
, which implies that the trading activities from the major trader do not affect the strategy of the minor traders. In this case, minor traders face an MFG that was studied in
Fu et al. (2021)
. In this case, the equilibrium strategy of the representative minor trader follows

Q^{\mathrm{Mn},*,\lambda_{0}=0}_{t}=\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{4a}t\right)\frac{\sinh(\gamma(T-t))}{\sinh(\gamma T)}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}]+\frac{\sinh(\theta(T-t))}{\sinh(\theta T)}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}]),

(3.23)

where
\theta=\sqrt{\phi/a}
and
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{\phi}{a}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16a^{2}}}
.

Comparing the results in Sections
3.2
and
3.4.1
, we see that the targeting strategy
R
directly affects the trading of the major trader, and further influences minor traders through the interactions (if exist) between the traders. More specifically, in the scenario without interactions, the targeting strategy
R
influences
only
the major trader’s strategy (
3.22
) and does not affect the strategy of the representative minor trader (
3.23
). As a comparison, the equilibrium strategies of both the major and minor traders in Theorem
2
are affected by the targeting strategy.

3.4.2
Special Case II: Identical Inventory of Minor Traders

Here we consider a special case where all minor traders have the same constant initial inventory, namely,
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\equiv q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}
for some constant
q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}
almost surely. In this case, the strategies of the major trader and the representative minor trader are both deterministic, and are characterized by the first-order ODE system of
(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}},Q^{\mathrm{Mn}},P^{\mathrm{Mj}},P^{\mathrm{Mn}})
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-dv^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2a_{0}}v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}-\frac{\phi_{0}}{a_{0}}(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}-R_{t})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a}v^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{2a}v^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}-\frac{\phi}{a}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0,\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.24)

In addition, (
3.24
) is equivalent to the following second-order ODE system

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{0}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-\phi_{0}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=-\phi_{0}R_{t}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t},\\
a\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.25)

with boundary conditions
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}
,
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0
,
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}
, and
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0
.

This special case serves as a primary example in the discussion of the periodicity phenomena of Major-Minor MFG, as detailed in Section
4
.

4
Periodic Phenomena in the Liquidation Game

In this section, we analyze the periodicity in the Major-Minor MFG from a theoretical perspective, focusing on the scenario that the targeting strategy of the major trader is a periodic strategy (see Definition
3
).
Section
4.1
provides a periodic-trend decomposition of the Nash equilibrium, and Section

4.1
Periodic-Trend Decomposition of the Nash Equilibrium

To analyze the periodicity in the Nash equilibrium of the Major-Minor MFG, we first prove a general result to decompose the strategies of the major and the representative minor traders into periodic components and trend components.

\displaystyle Q^{\mathrm{Mj},*}_{t}

\displaystyle=Q^{\mathrm{Mj},\mathrm{per}}_{t}+Q^{\mathrm{Mj},\mathrm{tre}}_{t}=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\cdot\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)+\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mj},\mathrm{per}}_{t}+Q^{\mathrm{Mj},\mathrm{tre}}_{t},

\displaystyle Q^{\mathrm{Mn},*}_{t}

\displaystyle=Q^{\mathrm{Mn},\mathrm{per}}_{t}+Q^{\mathrm{Mn},\mathrm{tre}}_{t},

w_{0}=q^{\mathrm{Mn},\mathrm{per}}_{0}
,

Solve the following linear equations on

\displaystyle a_{0}\frac{z_{j+1}-2z_{j}+z_{j-1}}{h^{2}}-\phi_{0}z_{j}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{w_{j+1}-w_{j}}{h}=-\phi_{0}\tilde{R}_{jh},\hskip 9.24994ptj=1,2,\ldots,n^{\mathrm{mesh}}-1,

In the second step, we numerically decompose the Nash equilibrium into its periodic and trend components according to

4

5.2.4
Summary

We summarize the key observations from our numerical experiments.
From the investors’ perspective, in equilibrium, the major trader follows the targeting strategy closely, while minor traders mimic the periodic patterns of the major trader in order to profit from it. Minor traders front-run the major trader in both the periodic and trend components of her strategy. In reaction to minor traders’ behavior, the major trader reduces the strength of her periodic activities. These strategic interactions decrease (increase) the major (representative minor) trader’s expected profit, which is consistent with the implications from
Brunnermeier and Pedersen
’s (
2005
) model of predator trading. However, the aggregate costs of both parties are reduced thanks to the decreased expected profit of the targeting strategy in equilibrium.

From a market perspective, the Major-Minor MFG increases the aggregate trading volume, accelerates the aggregate trading speed, and lowers the price in the market. Furthermore, the interactions between both parties decrease the strength of periodicity in both the aggregate trading rate and the price.

In general, the strategic interactions among the major and minor traders not only reduce individual costs but also enhance market stability by mitigating periodic fluctuations in both the trading rates and the price.

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi_{0}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+2\phi_{0}R_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-\frac{1}{2a_{0}}A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+2\phi_{0}R_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-\frac{1}{2a}A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=-A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\quad P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}=-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0,\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.2)

Hence, it suffices to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the FBSDE (
6.2
). To show this, we prove the following general result.

Theorem 5
.

Given
g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\in L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
, for any constant
\mathfrak{p}\in[0,1]
, the FBSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}}{2a_{0}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\frac{P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{2a}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\mathfrak{p}\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi_{0}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\mathfrak{p}\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\mathfrak{p}\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-2\phi Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-\frac{1}{2a_{0}}A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{t},\\
-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]-\frac{1}{2a}A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\\
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=-A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\quad P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}=-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{T}=0,\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0},\,Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T}=0\end{aligned}\right.

(6.3)

has a unique solution
(Q^{\mathrm{Mj}},Q^{\mathrm{Mn}},P^{\mathrm{Mj}},P^{\mathrm{Mn}},B^{\mathrm{Mj}},B^{\mathrm{Mn}},Z^{\mathrm{Mj}},Z^{\mathrm{Mn}})
in the space of

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}\times D_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times D_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}\times\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}

(6.4)

\displaystyle\times L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)\times L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}\left([0,T-]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}\right),

where
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
is a constant.

Given the condition
\int_{0}^{T}(R_{t})^{2}dt<+\infty
in Assumption
1
, we can obtain the existence and uniqueness of the FBSDE system (
6.2
) when taking
\mathfrak{p}=1
,
g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}=2\phi R_{t}
, and
g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}=0
in Theorem
5
, which leads to the result in Theorem
1
.

We first provide some inequalities on
A^{0}_{t}
and
A_{t}
to control the norms of the solution.

Lemma 1
.

For
A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}
and
A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}
defined in (
6.1
), we have the following estimates

0<\frac{2a_{0}}{T-t}\leqslant A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\leqslant\frac{2a_{0}}{T-t}+\frac{2}{3}\phi_{0}(T-t),\quad 0<\frac{2a}{T-t}\leqslant A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\leqslant\frac{2a}{T-t}+\frac{2}{3}\phi(T-t).

(6.5)

Moreover, for
t\in[0,T]
, define that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(t,s)

\displaystyle:=e^{-\frac{1}{2a_{0}}\int_{t}^{s}A^{0}_{r}dr}=\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\phi_{0}/a_{0}}(T-s))}{\sinh(\sqrt{\phi_{0}/a_{0}}(T-t))},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(t,s)

\displaystyle:=e^{-\frac{1}{2a}\int_{t}^{s}A_{r}dr}=\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\phi/a}(T-s))}{\sinh(\sqrt{\phi/a}(T-t))}.

Then we have

\Phi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(t,s)\leqslant\frac{T-s}{T-t}\leqslant 1,\quad\Phi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(t,s)\leqslant\frac{T-s}{T-t}\leqslant 1.

(6.7)

Proof.

(
6.5
) holds due to the fact that
\tanh(x)\leqslant x\leqslant(1+x^{2}/3)\tanh(x)
for all
x>0
, and (
6.7
) results from the fact that
\sinh(x)/x
is monotonically increasing in
x>0
.
∎

We now proceed to prove Theorem
5
. We first show that Theorem
5
holds when
\mathfrak{p}=0
.

Lemma 2
.

When
\mathfrak{p}=0
, Theorem
5
holds and the solution of (
6.3
) can be expressed as

\left\{\begin{aligned} B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(t,s)g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right],\\
B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(t,s)g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right],\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(0,t)+\frac{1}{2a_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mj}}(s,t)B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\\
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(0,t)+\frac{1}{2a}\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{\mathrm{Mn}}(s,t)B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\\
P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=-A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\quad P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}=-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.8)

and
Z^{0}_{t}
and
Z_{t}
are given by the martingale representation theorem. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|B^{\mathrm{Mj}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{s}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right],\quad\|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right],

(6.9)

and

\displaystyle\|Q^{\mathrm{Mj}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\leqslant Cq^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{0}+C\|B^{\mathrm{Mj}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2},\quad\|Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|^{2}\right]+C\|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}.

(6.10)

Proof.

When
\mathfrak{p}=0
, (
6.3
) becomes a standard FBSDE, and we can easily get the explicit form (
6.8
) of the solution by, for example, Proposition 4.1.2 in
Zhang (2017)
. It remains to establish the integration properties of the solution, and we only need to consider
(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})
without loss of generality. First, for
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
, we can obtain by Hölder’s inequality that

\frac{\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\leqslant\frac{1}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right]\leqslant\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right]\right)^{1-\gamma}<\infty,

and the Doob’s maximal inequality and Jensen’s inequality imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\frac{B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right]\right)^{2(1-\gamma)}\right]

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{2(1-\gamma)}{1-2\gamma}\right)^{2(1-\gamma)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2(1-\gamma)}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{2(1-\gamma)}{1-2\gamma}\right)^{2(1-\gamma)}T^{1-2\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]<+\infty.

Hence,
B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}
and (
6.9
) holds.

Furthermore, by the inequality (
6.7
), we have

\displaystyle\left|Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|\frac{T-t}{T}+\frac{1}{2a}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{T-t}{T-s}\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{s}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant(T-t)\left[\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|+\frac{1}{2a}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\frac{\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(T-s)^{\gamma-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant(T-t)\left[\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|+\frac{1}{2a\gamma}T^{\gamma}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\frac{\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right)\right],

and then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\frac{Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{T-t}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\left(|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}|+\frac{1}{2a\gamma}T^{\gamma}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\frac{\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right)\right)^{2}\right]

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2a^{2}\gamma^{2}}T^{2\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\frac{\left|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right|^{2}\right]+C\|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}<+\infty,

So
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}
and (
6.10
) holds.

Finally, we establish the integratability property of
P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}
. First, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the upper bound of
A_{t}
in (
6.5
), we have

\displaystyle\left|P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=(-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})^{2}\leqslant 2\left(A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}+2\left(B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2[A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}(T-t)]^{2}\left|\frac{Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{T-t}\right|^{2}+2(T-t)^{2\gamma}\left|\frac{B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left[2a+\frac{2}{3}\phi(T-t)^{2}\right]^{2}\left|\frac{Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{T-t}\right|^{2}+2(T-t)^{2\gamma}\left|\frac{B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right|^{2}.

So, for any
\tau\in[0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\left|P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left[2a+\frac{2}{3}\phi T^{2}\right]^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\frac{Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{T-t}\right|^{2}\right]+2T^{2\gamma}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\frac{B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}}{(T-t)^{\gamma}}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C\|Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}<+\infty.

In addition, we consider the integration by part of
Q_{t}P_{t}
and obtain that

\displaystyle Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}-Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}

\displaystyle=2\phi\int_{0}^{T-\epsilon}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T-\epsilon}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(6.13)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2a}\int_{0}^{T-\epsilon}\left(P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T-\epsilon}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}Z^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t}.

Because
A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}>0
,
B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}
and
Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}
, we can show that, when
\epsilon\to 0
,

Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}=Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}(-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}+B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon})\leqslant Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}B^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{T-\epsilon}\to 0.

Thus, taking expectations on both sides of (
6.13
) and letting
\epsilon\to 0
yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left|P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant-\mathbb{E}\left[Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right]-2\phi\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{0}\right)^{2}\right]+C\|Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}^{2}+C\|B^{\mathrm{Mn}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]<+\infty.

Hence,
P^{\mathrm{Mn}}\in D_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mn}}}^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Lemma 3

If Theorem

holds for some
\mathfrak{p}\in[0,1)
, then there exists a sufficiently small

such that Theorem

holds for
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{o}

Proof.

For given

and

, we consider the following system of equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=\frac{1}{2a_{0}}\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\
d\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}&=\frac{1}{2a}\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\\

&\qquad\qquad\left.+\mathfrak{o}\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\mathfrak{o}\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]+g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\tilde{Z}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}d\tilde{W}_{t},\\
\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}&=-A^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\quad\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}=-A^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}+\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\\

(6.14)

Note that

g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}(P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}):=\mathfrak{o}\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]+g^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\quad g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}(P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})=\mathfrak{o}\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2a_{0}}P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}+\mathfrak{o}\frac{\lambda}{2a}\mathbb{E}\left[P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}\middle|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t}\right]+g^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}

are both in
L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)

holds for some
\mathfrak{p}\in[0,1)
, as assumed in the statement of Lemma
3
, we can show that the FBSDE (

) has a unique solution

(\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{Z}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{Z}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})\in\mathcal{S}.

This defines a map
\varphi:(P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})\mapsto(\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{Q}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{P}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{B}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t},\tilde{Z}^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},\tilde{Z}^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})

To establish the contraction property of

(P^{\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{Mn}}_{t}),(P^{\prime\mathrm{Mj}}_{t},P^{\prime\mathrm{Mn}}_{t})\in L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)\times L_{\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{Mj}}}^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right),

7
Conclusion

To explore the causes and consequences of periodic trading activities, this paper develops a novel mean-field liquidation game involving a major trader and a continuum of minor traders. The major trader trades against a periodic and deterministic targeting strategy, while the minor traders maximize profit subject to inventory risk.

Theoretically, our study significantly extends the existing results of MFGs to a novel setting with both major and minor players, interactions through actions, and terminal state constraints. We adopt a probabilistic approach to establish the existence and uniqueness of an open-loop Nash equilibrium, and demonstrate an
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})
approximation rate for the mean-field solution to a major-minor game with
N
minor players, building on approaches from extended MFGs
(
Fu and Horst, 2020
)
and MFGs with a major player
(
Carmona and Delarue, 2018
)
.

Furthermore, we contribute to the understanding of driving factors behind periodic trading activities by modeling the interactions between market participants with different trading purposes. We show that minor traders exhibit front-running behaviors in both the periodic and trend components of their strategies, reducing the major trader’s profit and the intensity of her periodic trading behavior. These strategic interactions also reduce periodicity in both the aggregate trading volume and the asset price in the market, thereby serving as a stabilizing force.

Introduction

For a pair of self-adjoint operators
H
and
V
acting on a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}

such that
V
belongs to the trace class ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
, Krein proved that
there is a unique function
\xi_{H,V}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
called the
spectral shift function,
such that the following trace formula holds:

{\rm Tr}\left(f(H+V)-f(H)\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f^{\prime}(t)\,\xi(t)\,dt,

for every function
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
whose derivative
f^{\prime}
admits the representation

f^{\prime}(\lambda)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-\lambda t}d\mu(t)

for some finite (complex) Borel measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}

(see
[
17
]
) and Tr is the standard trace on trace class operators.
Krein’s formula originated from research in physics
[
18
]
. It has been applied in perturbation theory
[
31
]

and in noncommutative geometry in the study of spectral
flow
[
1
,
2
]
. Dykema and Skripka
[
11
]
recently extended Krein’s formula to perturbation by a weak trace class operator, where the formula now involves a trace on the weak trace class operators. We recall all traces on the weak trace class are singular, that is they vanish on trace class operators and particularly on those of finite rank. As noted in
[
11
]
the use of traces on the weak ideal introduces new perturbation formulas
that calculate the difference up to trace class perturbation and also introduces spectral measures that are not absolutely continuous. Singular traces are important in classical and noncommutative geometry as well as in applications to physics (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
20
]
and references cited therein),
and perturbation formulas for singular traces on the weak trace class ideal open new ground for applications. In particular,
a very recent publication
[
25
]
employs singular traces of a Taylor expansion (as in Theorem
1.4
below) for a concrete function
f(t)=t^{p}
, which are used as a technical tool for studying Fréchet differentiability of the
L_{p}
-norm of Haagerup
L_{p}
spaces.

Krein’s trace formula was extended to Hilbert-Schmidt operators by Koplienko
[
16
]
.
He showed that if a perturbation
V
is from
{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
and if
f
is any rational function with non-real poles which
is bounded at infinity, then the difference

f(H+V)-f(H)-\frac{d}{dt}\left(f(H+tV)\right)\Bigr|_{t=0}

belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and there exists a
function
\eta=\eta_{H,V}\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\mathrm{Tr}\left(f(H+V)-f(H)-\frac{d}{dt}\left(f(H+tV)\right)\Bigr|_{t=0}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime\prime}(t)\,\eta(t)\,dt.

(1)

Recently Potapov, Skripka and Sukochev
[
24
]
have
extended the trace formula further to the case of the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
Schatten-von Neumann ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
.
More precisely, they proved that for
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
there exists a
function
\eta_{n}=\eta_{n,H,V}
in
L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(f(H)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{j!}\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}f(H+tV)\bigg|_{t=0}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{(n)}(t)\eta_{n}(t)\,dt.

(2)

The main aim of the present paper is to extend (
2
)
to the weak Schatten-von Neumann ideals
\mathcal{L}_{n,\infty}
, which are the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
order convexification of the weak trace class ideal

\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
as the Schatten-von Neumann ideals
\mathcal{L}_{n}
are the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
order convexification of the
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.
The weak trace class ideal possesses a infinite lattice of traces which are well studied
[
5
,
20
,
27
]
.
The objective to extend (
2
) naturally therefore involves traces on the weak trace class ideal
(which are all singular traces, that is traces vanishing on finite rank operators) instead of the classical trace.

The first step in this direction was done recently by Dykema and Skripka (see
[
11
]
). They proved
the analogues of Krein’s and Koplienko’s results for a specific class of Lorentz ideals
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}

and Dixmier traces, which are singular traces of a special type (see e.g.
[
20
]
).
We state their results for the special case when the ideal
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}
coincides with the classical Dixmier-Macaev ideal

\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
of all compact operators
A\in B({\mathcal{H}})
such that

\|A\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\infty}}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\log(1+n)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu(k,A)<\infty,

where
\{\mu(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence of singular values of a compact operator
A\in B({\mathcal{H}})
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}_{1,\infty}
denote the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
root (or
n
-convexification) of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\infty}
, in other words

{\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}_{1,\infty}
consists of all operators
A
such that
A^{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\infty}
(see e.g.
[
22
,
4
,
20
]
for details).

depending only on

and

(3)

for every

2. If

and

are self-adjoint operators and if

belongs to the Lorentz ideal

then for every bounded trace
\tau
on

on
{\mathbb{R}}

depending only on

and
\tau

(4)

(in the setting of self-adjoint operators) as follows:

.

2
Preliminaries

Let
C^{n}
denote the space of all
n
times
continuously differentiable complex-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the usual norm and let
C_{b}^{n}
denote the subclass of
C^{n}
of bounded functions.
Also by
C({\mathbb{R}})
we denote the space of continuous real-valued functions equipped with the standard norm.

Let
B({\mathcal{H}})
be the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a separable Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}
equipped with the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.9
.

A trace on an ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
of
B({\mathcal{H}})
is a linear functional
\tau:{\mathcal{I}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such
that

\tau(AB)=\tau(BA)

for all
A\in{\mathcal{I}}
and
B\in B({\mathcal{H}})
. A trace
\tau
is called singular if it vanishes on finite rank operators.

Note, that we do not require a trace to be positive.

By
{\rm Tr}
we denote the standard (normal) trace on
B({\mathcal{H}})
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}:={\mathcal{L}}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
denote the Schatten-von Neumann ideal, that is the set of all
A\in B({\mathcal{H}})
such that
{\rm Tr}(|A|^{p})<\infty
,
0<p<\infty
.
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
23
]
and references cited therein
for basic definitions and facts).
Whereas there are no non-trivial bounded traces on
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
,
0<p<1
,
the stock of traces on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
is plentiful.
In particular, it contains Dixmier traces
[
9
]
(see also
[
6
, Section IV.2.
\beta
]
), which are bounded traces of a special form.

We now define the derivatives appearing in the perturbation formulae.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\mathcal{SA}
be the subspace of all self-adjoint operators from
B({\mathcal{H}})
and
A,B\in\mathcal{SA}
.
Consider the function
t\mapsto g(A+tB)
and define its derivative as follows

\frac{d}{dt}\left[g\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{g(A+tB)-g(A)}{t}

provided the limit exists.
By
\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[g\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}
,
k\geq 2
we understand the derivative
of the function

t\mapsto\frac{d^{k-1}}{dt^{k-1}}\left[g\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
g:\mathcal{SA}\to\mathcal{SA}
and
A\in\mathcal{SA}
.
The function
g
is called
k
times Fréchet differentiable at
A

if there exist bounded linear operators

F_{A}^{(j)}:\underbrace{\mathcal{SA}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{SA}}_{j-\text{times}}\to\mathcal{SA},\quad 1\leq j\leq k

such that

\lim_{B\to 0}\frac{\|g(A+B)-g(A)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}F_{A}^{(j)}(B,\dots,B)\|_{\infty}}{\|B\|_{\infty}}=0.

The multilinear operator
F_{A}^{(k)}
is called the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
Fréchet derivative of
g
at
A
.

The following lemma describes the relationship between the derivatives introduced in Definitions
2.10
and
2.11
.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Schwartz function and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then:

(i) for every
A,B\in\mathcal{SA}
the derivative
\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}
exists;

(ii) the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
Fréchet derivative of
the function
A\mapsto f(A)
from
\mathcal{SA}
to
\mathcal{SA}
exists;

(iii) for every
A,B\in\mathcal{SA}
we have

F_{A}^{(k)}(\underbrace{B,\dots,B}_{k-\text{times}})=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 5.7]
for every Schwartz function
f
the function

A\mapsto f(A)
has Fréchet derivatives of any order, which proves (ii).

It is shown in
[
21
, Chapter VIII, §6 (3)]
that the derivative

\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0}
exists and

F_{A}^{(k)}(\underbrace{B,\dots,B}_{k-\text{times}})=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(A+tB\right)\right]|_{t=0},

which proves the first and the third assertions.
∎

The following theorem is proved in
[
24
]
(see Theorem 1.1).

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
H,V
be self-adjoint operators such that
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
.
For every Schwartz function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
the operator

f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}

belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and
there is a unique function
\eta_{n}\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
depending only on
n,H,V
such that

{\rm Tr}\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f^{(n)}(t)\,\eta_{n}(t)\,dt.

Theorem
1.4
extends Theorem
2.13
to the case of weak
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
-spaces.
It is one of the main results of this paper.
The main technical ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.4
is the following estimate.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
H,V\in B({\mathcal{H}})
be self-adjoint operators
such that
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}
.
For every Schwartz function
f
we have that:

1. The operator

\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}

belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
;

2. There is a constant
c_{n}
depending only on
n
and
H
such that for every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
the following estimate

\left|\tau\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)\right|\leq\,c_{n}\,\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}\,\left\|V\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}^{n}

(9)

holds.

Before we proceed with the proof of Theorem
2.14
we show that
the estimate (
9
) easily proves Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{n+1}
, it follows from Theorem
2.13
that for every Schwartz function
f
, we have

f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1}.

Since every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
vanishes on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
, it follows that

\tau\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\,\tau\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right),

for every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
.

Hence, the inequality (
9
) yields

\left|\tau\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)\right|\leq\,c_{n}\,\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}\,\left\|V\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}^{n}

Therefore, for every Schwartz functions
f
the functional

f^{(n)}\mapsto\tau\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)

is bounded in the norm of
C_{b}^{n}
.
Let us consider the subspace
E\subset C({\mathbb{R}})
consisting of all bounded functions
h
such that
h=f^{(n)}
for some Schwartz function
f
.
Observe that such a function
f
is necessarily unique, and therefore the mapping

h\mapsto\varphi(h):=\tau\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right),\quad h\in E

is a correctly defined linear functional on
E
satisfying

|\varphi(h)|\leq c^{\prime}_{n}\|h\|_{C({\mathbb{R}})},\quad h\in E.

Hence, applying the Hahn-Banach theorem one can extend the functional
\varphi
to a bounded functional on the space
C({\mathbb{R}})
.

By
[
26
, Theorem IV.14, p.108]
every linear functional on the space
C({\mathbb{R}})
of continuous functions is a linear combination of positive linear functionals.
On the other hand, by the Riesz representation theorem
[
13
, 436K]
for every positive linear functional
g
on
C({\mathbb{R}})

there exists a unique totally finite Radon measure
\eta
on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

g(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\ d\eta,\ \forall u\in C({\mathbb{R}}).

Combining these two results, for every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}

we deduce the existence of a unique totally finite Radon signed measure
m_{n}
on
{\mathbb{R}}

such that

\tau\left(f\left(H+V\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f^{(n)}(t)\,dm_{n}(t).

Moreover, it follows from (
9
) and the latter formula that the total variation of
m_{n}
is bounded as follows:
\|m_{n}\|\leq\|V\|^{n}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}
.
∎

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.14
.
To prove it we need the concept of multiple operator integrals (MOI) and the method developed in
[
24
]
(see proof of Theorem 5.3 there).
Two obstacles arise here. First is the construction of MOI on the quasi-Banach spaces
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
,
p\geq 1
.
Note that in
[
1
]
MOI have been constructed on the Banach spaces
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
,
p\geq 1
(using the Bochner integral) and one can interpolate them to
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
,
p\geq 1
.
Here the second problem comes into play.
The crucial technical feature of the method from
[
24
]
is that at some stage one needs to swap a trace and an integral.
Which is not a problem if we are in the setting of
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and the standard normal trace.
(The proof of this fact, which can be found in
[
1

To overcome these obstacles in Section

we construct a (Riemann) integral on the quasi-Banach spaces
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
,
p<1

as a limit of integral sums of a specific form (see (
15
) below)

{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
,
p\geq 1
.
In this construction we follow the ideas of Turpin and Waelbroeck
[

further developed by Kalton in
[
15
]
.
Using these new integrals we introduce the notion of MOI in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
,
p\geq 1

and prove that they are well-defined for a sufficiently large class of functions.
Also this construction of the integral allows us to swap a trace (not necessarily normal) and an integral for sufficiently large class of integrands (see Theorem
3.25
below).

Next, in sections
4
and
5
we employ multiple operator integrals to prove Theorems
1.4
and
1.2
respectively.
In Section

H
.
The last section is an appendix, where we have gathered the results concerning the polylinear interpolation which are used in this paper.

The following Riesz-Fischer type theorem is used in Theorem
3.18
below.
Although this result is folklore, we give a short proof for the convenience of a reader.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
0<p<1
.
If
A_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
are such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}<\infty
,
then
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
and

\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}.

Proof.

Since the quasi-norm of
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
satisfies the following inequality (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 4.9]
)

\|A+B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\|A\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}+\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p},\ A,B\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p},

(10)

it follows that

\|\sum_{k=m}^{n}A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\sum_{k=m}^{n}\|A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\to 0,\ \text{as}\ m,n\to\infty.

Hence,
\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
with respect to the metric
d_{p}
defined as follows

d_{p}(A,B)=\|A-B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}.

(11)

Since
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
is complete with respect to the metric
d_{p}
(see e.g.
[
28
]
), it follows that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}
converges in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

Next, using the well-known Fatou property of
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
, we obtain

\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\|A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|A_{k}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}.

∎

3
Multiple operator integrals

We fix an integer
n\geq 1
and denote by
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
the positive cone of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{C}}_{n}
be the class of functions
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}}

admitting the representation

\phi(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}\prod_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}(\lambda_{j},s)\,d\nu(s),

(12)

for some
bounded
Borel functions
a_{j}\left(\cdot,s\right):{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}}
and measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
such that
|\nu|
is finite and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}\prod_{j=0}^{n}\left\|a_{j}(\cdot,s)\right\|_{\infty}\,d|\nu|(s)<\infty.

(13)

Following
[
1
, Definition 4.1]
for every function
\phi\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{n}
we define
the corresponding operator integral.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
1\leq p_{j}\leq\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
and
p
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{j}}
.
For every
\phi\in{\mathfrak{C}}_{n}
we define the operator

T_{\phi}:{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1}}\times\dots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{n}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}

as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}):=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}a_{0}(H,s)\,x_{1}\,a_{1}(H,s)\,x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}a_{n}(H,s)\,d\nu(s),

(14)

where the integral above is the Bochner integral on
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

For the rest of the section we prove auxiliary results required to extend the notion of
multiple operator integrals to the case of
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
spaces.

First we define the integration in an arbitrary quasi-Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
.
However, we will only use it in the case when
X
either
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
or
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

For every
k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
we denote
\textbf{k}:=(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
,
\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}}:=(\frac{k_{0}}{2^{m}},\frac{k_{1}}{2^{m}},\dots,\frac{k_{n}}{2^{m}})

and
\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}}:=(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{k_{0}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}},\frac{2\lfloor\frac{k_{1}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}},\dots,\frac{2\lfloor\frac{k_{n}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}}).

Also, by writing
\textbf{k}\geq 0
we understand, that all
k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0
.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a quasi-Banach space.
Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let

u:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}\to X
.
The integral sums
S_{m}
,
m\geq 1
are defined by the following formula

S_{m}:=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}}),

(15)

where the sum is taken over all
0\leq\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
.

If the series in (
15
) and the sequence
\{S_{m}\}\subset X
itself are convergent in the quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, then we set

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds:=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}.

Throughout the paper all integrals are understood in the sense of the preceding definition, unless explicitly specified.

Note that, in particular, the latter definition introduces a notion of an integral of
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
-valued functions,
p\geq 1
.
However, for
p\geq 1
the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
is Banach. Therefore, in these settings, it makes sense also to speak of the Bochner integral of an
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
-valued function.
So, for
p\geq 1
there are two notions of an integral of an
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
-valued function: the Bochner integral and the integral in the sense of Definition
3.17
.
Theorem
3.20
below shows that if
p\geq 1
and a function
u:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
(satisfying some additional conditions) is integrable in the sense of Bochner, then

u
is also integrable in the sense of Definition
3.17
and the Bochner integral coincides with that introduced in Definition
3.17
.

We start with a result proving that the integral (in the sense of Definition
3.17
) exists for a wide class of functions
u:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p>\frac{n+1}{n+2}
.
Let

u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
be such that
u=wv
, where

w:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
is a bounded Lipschitz function and

v:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is such that

|v(s)|,|\triangledown v(s)|\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{(1+\sum_{i=0}^{n}|s_{i}|)^{\alpha}}

(16)

for every
s=(s_{0},\dots,s_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and every
\alpha>0
,
where
\triangledown
denotes the gradient of a function and
C_{\alpha}
is a constant depending on
\alpha
only.

We have

1. For every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
the series

S_{m}:=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})

is convergent in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
and
\|S_{m}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\cdot 2^{m(n+1)/p}
;

2. The sequence
\{S_{m}\}
is convergent in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
and, so, the integral
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds
is defined
and
\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}
;

3. The following estimate holds:

\|S_{m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}2^{m(\frac{n+1}{p}-(n+2))},\ \forall m\geq 1,

where
\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}:=\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\|w(s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}<\infty
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem for the case
\frac{n+1}{n+2}<p<1
.
For
p\geq 1
the proof is similar (and easier), with the only difference that instead of Theorem
2.15

we use the triangle inequality for
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
-norm.

1. For every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we first prove that the series

\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\|u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

is convergent.

Using the first inequality (
16
) for
v
with
\alpha=\frac{n+2}{p}
and the fact that the function
w
is bounded, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\|u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}^{p}\cdot|v|^{p}(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i})^{n+2}}.

For the latter sum we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1+2^{-m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}k_{i})^{n+2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\frac{l}{2^{m}})^{n+2}}\sum_{k_{0}+\dots+k_{n}=l}1\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{l^{n}}{(1+\frac{l}{2^{m}})^{n+2}}

(17)

\displaystyle=2^{mn}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{l}{2^{m}}\right)^{n}}{(1+\frac{l}{2^{m}})^{n+2}}\leq 2^{mn}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\lfloor\frac{l}{2^{m}}\rfloor+1\right)^{n}}{(1+\lfloor\frac{l}{2^{m}}\rfloor)^{n+2}}

\displaystyle=2^{m(n+1)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+1)^{n}}{(1+j)^{n+2}}=2^{m(n+1)}\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

Hence, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
Theorem
2.15
yields

\|S_{m}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2^{mp(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\|u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\leq 2^{m(1-p)(n+1)}\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}<\infty,

that is series
S_{m}
is convergent in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

2. For every
m\geq 2
consider

S_{m-1}=\frac{1}{2^{(m-1)(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m-1}}).

By the first part of the proof the latter series is absolutely convergent in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
, so one can change the order of summation.
We have

S_{m-1}=\frac{1}{2^{(m-1)(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}u(\frac{2\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}u(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}}),

since in the latter sum every element is repeated
2^{n+1}
-times.

Hence, for
m\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle S_{m}-S_{m-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left[u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-u(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right]

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left[w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left(w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\left(v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right).

For the first sum, since
w
is Lipschitz and
v
satisfies (
16
) (with
\alpha=\frac{n+2}{p}
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|\left(w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}\cdot|v|^{p}(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{Lip}^{p}\ 2^{-mp}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\frac{C_{\alpha}}{(1+2^{-m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}|k_{i}|)^{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\ \|w\|_{Lip}^{p}\ 2^{m(n+1-p)},

due to (
17
).

For the second sum, since
w
is bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
\triangledown v
satisfies (
16
) (with
\alpha=\frac{n+2}{p}
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\left(v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

(20)

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}^{p}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left|v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}^{p}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\frac{C_{\alpha}2^{-mp}}{(1+2^{-m}\sum_{i=0}^{n}|k_{i}|)^{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}^{p}\ 2^{m(n+1-p)},

due to (
17
).

Combining (
18
) with (
19
) and (
20
) and using Theorem
2.15
we obtain

\displaystyle\|S_{m}-S_{m-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{mp(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|\left(w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

(21)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{mp(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\left(v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\ 2^{m(n+1-p)}}{2^{mp(n+1)}}(\|w\|_{Lip}^{p}+\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}^{p})

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}^{p}\ 2^{m(n+1-(n+2)p)}.

Since
p>\frac{n+1}{n+2}
, it follows that
n+1-(n+2)p<0
. Therefore,

\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\|S_{m}-S_{m-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}<\infty.

Hence,
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
with respect to the metric
d_{p}
defined by the formula (
11
).
Since
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
is complete with respect to the metric
d_{p}
, it follows that
the limit of the sequence
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
exists and, so, the integral
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds
is well-defined in the sense of Definition
3.17
.

3. The inequality (
21
) yields

\displaystyle\|S_{m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\|S_{k}-S_{k-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}^{p}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}2^{k(n+1-(n+2)p)}.

Hence,

\|S_{m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}2^{m(\frac{n+1}{p}-(n+2))}.

Note that for
p\geq 1
we can prove a stronger estimate.
Indeed, using (
16
) with
\alpha=n+2
we obtain the following versions of (
19
) and (
20
):

\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|\left(w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C_{\alpha}\ \|w\|_{Lip}\ 2^{mn}

(22)

and

\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\left(v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-v(\frac{2\lfloor\frac{\textbf{k}}{2}\rfloor}{2^{m}})\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C_{\alpha}\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\ 2^{mn}.

(23)

Hence, combining (
18
) with (
22
), (
23
)
and using the triangle inequality for the norm (instead of the inequality (

) for the quasi-norm) we obtain

\|S_{m}-S_{m-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}^{p}\ 2^{-m}

and

\|S_{m}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}u(s)\ ds\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C\max\{\|w\|_{Lip},\|w\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}_{p})}\}2^{-m}.

∎

Now we show that our Definition
3.17
coincides with the definition of the Bochner integral in
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
,
p\geq 1
.
We need the following result established in
[
30
]
.

Recall that a measure
\nu
on a topological space
\Omega
is called tight if for every
\varepsilon>0

there is a compact set
K\subset\Omega
such that
|\nu|(\Omega\setminus K)<\varepsilon
.

Theorem 3.19

(
[
30
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a tight Borel measure on a metric space
\Omega
, let
X
be
a Banach space. If
w:(\Omega,\nu)\to X
is Bochner integrable,
then for every sequence of partitions

P^{(m)}=\{\Omega_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j\geq 0}

of
\Omega
satisfying
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{j\geq 0}{\rm diam}\ \Omega_{j}^{(m)}=0

there exists a sequence of sample point sets

Z^{(m)}=\{z_{j}^{(m)}\in\Omega_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j\geq 0}

such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w(z_{j}^{(m)})\nu(\Omega_{j}^{(m)})-\int_{\Omega}w(s)\ d\nu(s)\right\|_{X}=0,

where the latter integral is the Bochner integral in
X
.

The following result shows that our new definition of integral extends the notion of Bochner integral to
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p\geq 1
.
Let

u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
be such that
u=wv
, where

w:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
is a bounded Lipschitz function and

v:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies (
16
).
Then
\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}
(
S_{m}
is defined as in (
15
)) is equal to the Bochner integral
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}w(s)\ d\nu(s)
, where
d\nu(s)=v(s)ds
.

Proof.

In this proof all integrals are understood in the sense of Bochner.

Set

P^{(m)}=\{[\frac{k_{0}}{2^{m}},\frac{k_{0}+1}{2^{m}}]\times\dots\times[\frac{k_{n}}{2^{m}},\frac{k_{n}+1}{2^{m}}],\quad\forall\ \textbf{k}\geq 0\}.

Note that the diameter of every set from this partition is
\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{2^{m}}}\to 0
,
m\to\infty
.
Also note, that since
v
satisfies (
16
) the measure
\nu
is a finite tight Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

and the function
w
is Bochner integrable with respect to the measure
\nu
(since
w
is bounded Lipschitz and
\nu
is finite).

Hence, by Theorem
3.19
for every
\textbf{k}\geq 0
there exists
z^{(m)}(\textbf{k})\in[\frac{k_{0}}{2^{m}},\frac{k_{0}+1}{2^{m}}]\times\dots\times[\frac{k_{n}}{2^{m}},\frac{k_{n}+1}{2^{m}}],
such that

\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}w(s)\ d\nu(s)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\to 0,m\to\infty.

Similarly to (
22
) and (
23
) (using (
16
) with
\alpha=n+2
) we obtain

\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C2^{mn}.

(24)

Here
C
is a constant depending on
w
and
n
.

Next, since
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
,
p\geq 1
is a Banach space, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}w(s)\ d\nu(s)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}w(s)\ d\nu(s)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}[w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}}))-w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))]\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}.

For the second term above, using (
24
) and the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}[w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}}))-w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))]\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}\left\|w(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})v(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}}))-w(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))v(z^{(m)}(\textbf{k}))\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq C2^{-m}.

Consequently,

\left\|\frac{1}{2^{m(n+1)}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\geq 0}u(\frac{\textbf{k}}{2^{m}})-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}}w(s)\ d\nu(s)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\to 0,m\to\infty.

In other words, the Bochner integral

∎

Lemma 6.34

Proof.

We prove the formula by induction. For

Assume that

f^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n-1} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_{n-1} = m+n-1}}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i})^{-m_{i}}.

We have

\displaystyle f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n})-f^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n})}{\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1}}\left(\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n-1} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_{n-1} = m+n-1}}(z-\lambda_{0})^{-m_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i+1})^{-m_{i}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\left.\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n-1} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_{n-1} = m+n-1}}(z-\lambda_{1})^{-m_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i+1})^{-m_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n-1} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_{n-1} = m+n-1}}\left((z-\lambda_{0})^{-m_{0}}-(z-\lambda_{1})^{-m_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i+1})^{-m_{i}}.

Note that,

\frac{(z-\lambda_{0})^{-m_{0}}-(z-\lambda_{1})^{-m_{0}}}{\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{1}}=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1\le k,l \le m_0}{k+l =m_0+1}}(z-\lambda_{0})^{-k}(z-\lambda_{1})^{-l}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n-1} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_{n-1} = m+n-1}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1\le k,l \le m_0}{k+l =m_0+1}}(z-\lambda_{0})^{-k}(z-\lambda_{1})^{-l}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i+1})^{-m_{i}}.

Rename the variables as follows:
m_{0}:=k,m_{1}:=l,m_{i}:=m_{i-1}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Finally, we obtain

f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n})=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}\prod_{i=0}^{n}(z-\lambda_{i})^{-m_{i}}.

∎

If
f
is as in the previous lemma, then for every
0\leq p_{i}\leq\infty
and
x_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{i},\infty}
,
1\leq i\leq n
we have

T_{f^{[n]}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(z-\lambda_{0})^{-m_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}(z-\lambda_{i})^{-m_{i}},

where
T_{f^{[n]}}:{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1},\infty}\times\dots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{n},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
is the operator integral in the sense of Definition
3.21
.

The following theorem is a cornerstone estimate in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 6.35
.

Let
f
be a rational function with non-real poles which
is bounded at infinity. If
H,V
are self-adjoint operators
such that
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}
, then

1. The operator
\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
;

2. For every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
we have

\left|\tau\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)\right|\leq c_{n}\,\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}\,\left\|V\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}^{n}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the first assertion for the function
g(\lambda)=(z-\lambda)^{-m}
.

Due to
[
16
, formula 2.4]
we have

\frac{1}{n!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[g\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(zI-H)^{-m_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V(zI-H)^{-m_{i}}.

Note that, every term in the latter sum is a product of bounded operators
(zI-H)^{-m_{i}}
and
n
operators
V
.
Since
V\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}
, it follows that the every term in this sum belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
,
that is
\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[g\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
.
The first assertion has been proved.

Next, for every bounded trace
\tau
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
we have

\displaystyle\tau\left(\frac{1}{n!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[g\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(zI-H)^{-m_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V(zI-H)^{-m_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(zI-H)^{-m_{0}-m_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}V(zI-H)^{-m_{i}}\cdot V\right),

where the latter equality is due to the following property of traces:

\tau(AB)=\tau(BA)
for all
A\in{\mathcal{L}}_{1,\infty}
and
B\in B({\mathcal{H}})
.

Note that

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(zI-H)^{-m_{0}-m_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}V(zI-H)^{-m_{i}}

equals to
T_{\psi}\bigl(\underbrace{V,\ldots,V}_{\text{$n-1$-times}}\bigr)
, where

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \le m_0,\dots, m_{n} \le m}{m_0+\cdots+m_n = m+n}}(z-\lambda_{0})^{-m_{0}-m_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{i})^{-m_{i}}

\displaystyle=g^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{0}),

due to Lemma
6.34
.

Therefore,

\tau\left(\frac{1}{n!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[g\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)=\tau\left(T_{\psi}\bigl(\underbrace{V,\ldots,V}_{\text{$n-1$-times}}\bigr)\cdot V\right)

(43)

for every function
g\in\left\{\lambda\mapsto(z-\lambda)^{-m}\ :\ m\in{\mathbb{N}},z\notin{\mathbb{R}}\right\}.

As was mentioned at the beginning of this section the function
f
is a linear combination of such functions
g
.
Therefore, due to the linearity of both sides of (
43
), we obtain

\tau\left(\frac{1}{n!}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)=\tau\left(T_{\phi}\bigl(\underbrace{V,\ldots,V}_{\text{$n-1$-times}}\bigr)\cdot V\right),

(44)

where
\psi(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})=f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{0}).

Due to
[
7
, Chapter IV, §7(a)]
we have

f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=f^{[n]}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n},\lambda_{0}).

So,

\phi(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})=f^{[n]}(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}).

By the second part of Lemma
4.30
we have

\phi(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})=-i\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}s_{0}f^{(n)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{j}s_{j}\right)d\sigma_{n-1}.

Hence, the function
\phi
satisfies the conditions of
[
24
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Next, by
[
24
, Theorem 5.3]
for every
1<p_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
such that
0<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{p_{n-1}}<1

the following estimate holds:

\left\|T_{\phi}\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{n-1}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}.

Next, Theorem
7.37
below yields that the operator
T_{\phi}

acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1},\infty}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{n-1},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}
and

\left\|T_{\phi}\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{1},\infty}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{p_{n-1},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{p,\infty}}\leq c^{\prime}_{p}\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}},

for every
1<p_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
such that
0<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{p_{n-1}}<1
.

In the particular case when
p_{j}=n
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
we obtain

\left\|T_{\phi}\bigl(\underbrace{V,\ldots,V}_{\text{$(n-1)$-times}}\bigr)\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}}\leq c_{n}\,\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}\,\left\|V\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}^{n-1},\ \ 0\leq t\leq 1.

(45)

Combining (
45
) with (
44
), yields

\left|\tau\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left[f\left(H+tV\right)\right]\biggr|_{t=0}\right)\right|\leq\,c_{n}\,\left\|f^{(n)}\right\|_{L_{\infty}}\,\left\|V\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{n,\infty}}^{n}.

∎

Now using Theorem
6.35
we can prove Theorem
1.5
. The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.4
and therefore omitted.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
one needs a verbatim repetition of all the construction described in this section for the Dixmier-Macaev ideal
{\mathcal{M}}_{1,\infty}
.
We omit futher details.

7
Appendix (Polylinear interpolation)

In this section we prove the result concerning the polylinear interpolation that we used in the preceding sections.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
. Define the Lorentz ideal
{\mathcal{L}}_{pq}
by setting (see e.g
[
19
]
and
[
10
]
)

{\mathcal{L}}_{p,q}:=\left\{A\in B({\mathcal{H}})\text{ is compact}:\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\mu^{q}(k,A)<\infty\right\}.

Note that
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,p}={\mathcal{L}}_{p}
.

We use the following
noncommutative version of the general Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem for
{\mathcal{L}}_{p,q}
below.
It is a combination of
[
3
, Theorem 5.3.2]
and
[
8
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Theorem 7.36
.

Let
0<\alpha^{\prime}\neq\alpha^{\prime\prime}<\infty
,
0<\beta^{\prime}\neq\beta^{\prime\prime}<\infty
,
0<\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}\leq\infty
and
0<\theta<1
.
Suppose that a linear operator
W
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}
and from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}}.

If
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{\theta}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}
and
\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\theta}{\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\beta^{\prime\prime}}
,
then
W
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\gamma}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta,\gamma}
for every
0<\gamma\leq\infty

and

\|W\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\gamma}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta,\gamma}}\leq\|W\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}}^{\theta}\|W\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime}}}^{1-\theta}.

The following theorem is the main result of the present section.

Theorem 7.37
.

If a multilinear operator
R
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}

for every
0<\alpha_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
and

\left\|R\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}}\leq\,c_{\alpha},

then
the operator
R
acts from

{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n},\infty}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

for every
0<\alpha_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
and

\left\|R\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\,c^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

Proof.

1. Fix operators
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}
,
j=2,3,\ldots,n
and consider the operator

W_{1}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}
given by the formula

W_{1}(x_{1}):=R(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}).

Let us fix
0<\theta<1
and find
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha_{1}<\alpha^{\prime\prime}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\alpha^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}.

Note that,
\frac{\theta}{\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\beta^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

By the assumption on operator
R
we have

W_{1}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime}}\ \text{and}\ W_{1}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}.

Hence, by Theorem
7.36
(with
\gamma^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}
,
\delta^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}
,
\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\alpha^{\prime\prime}
,
\delta^{\prime\prime}=\beta^{\prime\prime}
and
\gamma=\infty
)

W_{1}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

and

\|W_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\|W_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime}}}^{\theta}\|W_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}}^{1-\theta}.

Therefore, by the definition of operator
W_{1}
we conclude that
the operator
R
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

for every
0<\alpha_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
and

\left\|R(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\,C\|x_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}},

for every
x_{1}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}
and
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}
,
j=2,3,\ldots,n
.

2. Fix the operators
x_{1}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}

x_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}
,
j=3,4,\ldots,n
and consider the operator

W_{2}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}
given by the formula

W_{2}(x_{2}):=R(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}).

Similarly, we fix
0<\theta<1
and find
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha_{2}<\alpha^{\prime\prime}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\alpha^{\prime\prime}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}.

Note that,
\frac{\theta}{\beta^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\beta^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

It was proved in the first part, that
R
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

for every
0<\alpha_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
.
Hence,

W_{2}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime},\infty}\ \text{and}\ W_{2}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\infty}.

Therefore, by Theorem
7.36
(with
\gamma^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}
,
\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\alpha^{\prime\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime\prime}=\gamma=\infty
)

W_{2}:{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

and

\|W_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\|W_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime},\infty}}^{\theta}\|W_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

In other words,

R
acts from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2},\infty}\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{3}}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}

for every
0<\alpha_{j}<\infty
,
1\leq j\leq n
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}
and

\left\|R(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\,C\|x_{1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}}\|x_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2},\infty}}\prod_{j=3}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}},

for every
x_{1}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}
,
x_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2},\infty}
and
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j}}
,
j=3,\ldots,n
.

Repeating this procedure
(n-2)
more times we end up having

\left\|R(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\,c^{\prime}_{\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j},\infty}},

for every
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{j},\infty}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, that is

\left\|R\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{1},\infty}\times\ldots\times{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{n},\infty}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,\infty}}\leq\,c^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

∎

Improved approximations of resolvents in homogenization of fourth-order operators with periodic coefficients
S. E. Pastukhova

Abstract




In the whole space
R^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, we study homogenization of a divergence form elliptic fourth-order operator
A_{\varepsilon}
with measurable

\varepsilon
-periodic coefficients, where
\varepsilon
is a small parameter. For the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
, acting as an operator from
L^{2}
to
H^{2}
, we find an approximation with remainder term of order
O(\varepsilon^{2})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Relying on this result,
we construct the resolvent approximation with remainder of order
O(\varepsilon^{3})

in the operator
L^{2}
-norm.
We employ
two-scale expansions that involve smoothing.

1
Introduction

In the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, we consider a fourth-order differential operator (DO)

A_{\varepsilon}=\sum\limits_{i,j,s,t}D_{ij}(a_{ijst}(x/\varepsilon)D_{st}),\quad D_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),

(1.1)

with summation over all indices
i,j,s,t
from 1 to d. Here, the 1-periodic symmetric measurable real-valued fourth-order tensor

a(x){=}\{a_{ijst}(x)\}

is subject to conditions of positive definiteness and boundedness which ensure that the operator is elliptic.

The well-known result in
[
1
]
, where
G
-convergence and homogenization of families of DO much more general than (
1.1
) were studied, means, in particular, that the resolvent of the operator
A_{\varepsilon}

is close to the resolvent of some limit, or homogenized, operator
\hat{A}

in the sense of strong operator convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}f\to(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
The limit operator

\hat{A}
is of the same ellipticity class, as
A_{\varepsilon}
, but is essentially simpler:

\hat{A}=\sum\limits_{i,j,s,t}D_{ij}(\hat{a}_{ijst}D_{st}),

(1.2)

where the tensor
\hat{a}=\{\hat{a}_{ijst}\}
is constant and may be calculated in terms of solutions of auxiliary
periodic problems.

The stronger operator convergence of
A_{\varepsilon}
to
\hat{A}
, namely, the uniform resolvent convergence was proved in
[
2
]
,

[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and, moreover, the convergence rate estimate with respect to the parameter
\varepsilon
was deduced:

\|(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}-(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon

(1.3)

with the constant
C
depending only on the spatial dimension and the ellipticity constant of the tensor
a(x)
.
In
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, even the stronger convergence in the operator
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-norm was proved:

\|(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}-(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}-\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon

(1.4)

with the constant
C
of the same type as in (
1.3
). Here, we have the approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in the energy operator norm and this is the sum of the
zeroth term
(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}

and the correcting term
\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
.
The operator
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}

is determined by solutions of auxiliary
periodic problems on the periodicity cell which are introduced to define the limit (or homogenized) tensor
\hat{a}
.
Note that

\|\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}C,\quad\|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}C,

(1.5)

where the constants
C
are of the same type, as in (
1.4
).
Thus, due to (
1.5
)
2
, the estimate (
1.3
) in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-operator norm follows from
(
1.4
) if the weaker operator norm is taken and the term
\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
is omitted being nonessential with respect to the weaker norm.
By a more subtle use of an

H^{2}
-estimate of type (
1.4
), it was showed in
[
5
]
that the order
of accuracy in the
L^{2}
-estimate (
1.3
) can be improved
up to
\varepsilon^{2}
:

\|(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}{-}(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}

(1.6)

where the constant
C
depends only on the spatial dimension and the ellipticity constant of the tensor
a(x)
.
Assuming no symmetry conditions on the tensor
a(x)
, the approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-operator norm becomes more complicated (see
[
6
]
), namely,

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}=(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}_{1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(1.7)

where the operator
\mathcal{K}_{1}
is independent of
\varepsilon
in contrast with its counterpart in (
1.4
).

In
[
7
]
, the approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in the
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}){\to}H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-operator norm was obtained with
remainder term of order
O(\varepsilon^{2})
. This approximation should be constructed as a sum

(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{3}\mathcal{K}_{3}(\varepsilon)

(1.8)

of the zero term of a new type (in comparison with (
1.4
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
)) and the two correctors.
Here, the operator

\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}=\hat{A}+\varepsilon B
is a singular perturbation of
\hat{A}
, where
B
is a fifth-order DO.
The operators
\mathcal{K}_{2}(\varepsilon)
,
\mathcal{K}_{3}(\varepsilon)
and
B
are determined with the help of solutions to auxiliary problems on the cell of periodicity (see §3), the number of which increases compared to the case of order
\varepsilon
approximation in the same operator norm.
The approximation (
1.8
) is specified in (
2.11
)–(
2.14
). We note here only that

\|\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{2}(\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}C,\quad\|\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{K}_{3}(\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}C,

(1.9)

where the constant
C
depends only on the spatial dimension and the ellipticity constant of the tensor
a(x)
.

Possessing
the approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in the
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}){\to}H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-operator norm with remainder term of order
\varepsilon^{2}
, we construct the approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in the
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}){\to}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-operator norm
of order
\varepsilon^{3}
, and this is is the main goal of the paper. To this end, we use the approach proposed in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
to obtain the improved
L^{2}
-approximations of the resolvent of order
\varepsilon^{2}
basing on its approximation of order
\varepsilon
in the energy operator norm. The exact formulation of the main result is given in Theorem
5.2
.

2
. The statement of the problem

2.1.

We study the fourth-order equation in

\mathbb{R}^{d}

with parameter
\varepsilon{\in}(0,1)
:

\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{\varepsilon}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad A_{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),&\\
A_{\varepsilon}{=}D^{*}\,a_{\varepsilon}(x)D,\quad a_{\varepsilon}(x){=}a(x/\varepsilon).&\\
\end{array}

(2.1)

Here

a(y){=}\{a_{ijst}(y)\}
is a measurable real-valued fourth-order tensor, periodic
with periodicity cell
Y{=}[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
and satisfying the symmetry and ellipticity conditions

\displaystyle{a_{ijst}=a_{stij},\quad a_{ijst}=a_{jist},}\atop\displaystyle{\exists\lambda>0:\quad\xi\cdot\xi\leq a(\cdot)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\lambda^{-1}\xi\cdot\xi}

(2.2)

for any symmetric matrix
\xi{=}\{\xi_{ij}\}
, where

\xi\cdot\eta
denotes the inner product of the matrices матриц
\xi{=}\{\xi_{ij}\}
and

\eta{=}\{\eta_{ij}\}
, i.e.

\xi\cdot\eta{=}\xi_{ij}\eta_{ij}
.
Throughout the paper, summation from
1

to
d
over repeated indices
is assumed (unless otherwise specified). The constant
\lambda
is called the ellipticity constant of the tensor
a(x)
.

By the symmetry property in (
2.2
), the action of the tensor
a

on a symmetric matrix
\xi

is the symmetric matrix
\eta{=}a\xi
,
and for arbitrary matrix
\xi
we have

a\xi{=}a\xi^{s}
,
where
\xi^{s}{=}1/2(\xi{+}\xi^{T})
is the symmetric part of
\xi
, or
a(\xi{+}\xi^{T})=2a\xi
.

We need to define DO in (
2.1
). Let

D\varphi=\nabla^{2}\varphi=\left\{D_{ij}\right\}_{i,j=1}^{d},\quad D_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

so that the operator
D
sends scalar functions into matrix functions. Then the adjoint operator

D^{*}{=}\nabla^{*}\nabla^{*}{=}{\rm div}\,{\rm div}
acts formally on a matrix function

\eta{=}\{\eta_{ij}\}

by

D^{*}\eta=D_{ij}\eta_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\eta_{ij},

sending a matrix into a scalar function.
The equality

D^{*}\eta{=}f,

where
\eta{\in}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d\times d}
and
f{\in}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
is understood in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
So, the expanded form of the operator
A_{\varepsilon}

is given by

A_{\varepsilon}=D_{ij}\left(a_{ijst}(x/\varepsilon)D_{st}\right),\quad D_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},

which is the same as in (
1.1
), by summation convention;
a solution of equation
(
2.1
) is understood in the sense of the integral identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(a_{\varepsilon}(x)Du^{\varepsilon}\cdot D\varphi+u^{\varepsilon}\varphi\right)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\varphi dx\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the test functions can be taken from

H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by the closure property.
By the Riesz Representation Theorem for a linear functional on a Hilbert space, the solution to
(
2.1
) exists and is unique, and satisfies an estimate

\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq c(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

It worth noting that we can define a norm on the space

H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, equivalent to the standard one, by the relation

\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|\varphi|^{2}+|D\varphi|^{2})\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

2.2.

We introduce the equation

\begin{array}[]{cc}u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\hat{A}u+u=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),&\\
\hat{A}{=}D^{*}\,\hat{a}\,D,&\\
\end{array}

(2.3)

with the constant fourth-order tensor
\hat{a}
defined in
(
3.6
). The expanded form of
\hat{A}

\hat{A}=D_{ij}\hat{a}_{ijst}D_{st},

which is the same as in (
1.2

2.3
) are constant and satisfy the ellipticity condition of the type (
2.2
), it is easy to obtain
the following elliptic estimate for the solution of this problem:

\|u\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad C=const(d,\lambda).

(2.4)

According to

[
1
]
, the solutions to (
2.1

2.3

are conected with the convergence

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2.5)

which allows to call (
2.3
) the limit, or homogenized, equation for (
2.1

u^{\varepsilon}=(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}f
and
u=(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}f

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}

(moreover, with convergence rate estimates that may contain appropriate correctors), formulated in (

1.6

(
1.4

also allows us to consider

Approximating the resolvent

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}

We introduce the new version of the homogenized equation with the operator (

(2.8)

Since the coefficients of the equation are constant, its solution can be obtained with the help of

\left(1+\Lambda(\xi)-i\varepsilon\Lambda_{0}(\xi)\right)F[\hat{u}^{\varepsilon}]=F[f],\quad i=\sqrt{-1},

3
. Cell problems

3.1.
We introduce the following problem on the periodicity cell
Y
:

N^{ij}\in\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y),\quad D^{*}_{y}[a(y)(D_{y}N^{ij}(y)+e^{ij})]=0,\,\,i,j=1,\ldots,d.

(3.1)

Here the tensor
a(y)
is the same as in Section 2.1; the entries of the matrix

e^{ij}=\{e^{ij}_{sh}\}_{s,h}
are
e^{ij}_{sh}=\delta^{i}_{s}\delta^{j}_{h}

\delta^{i}_{s}
is the Kronecker delta;

\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y){=}\{\varphi{\in}{H}^{2}_{per}(Y):\langle\varphi\rangle{=}0\}

\langle\varphi\rangle=\int_{Y}\varphi\,dy.

The equality

D^{*}_{y}b=0

(3.2)

b\in L_{per}^{2}(Y)^{d\times d}
means that the integral identity

\langle b\cdot D\varphi\rangle=0\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{per}(Y),

(3.3)

holds, where the test functions can be taken from the space
\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y)
by closure; matrices satisfying (
3.2

In the theory of homogenization it is well known that (
3.2
) can be understood also in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is, as an alternative to (
3.3
) we can consider the integral identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot D\varphi\,dx=0\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By (

3.1
) satisfies the identity

\langle aDN^{ij}\cdot D\varphi\rangle=-\langle ae^{ij}\cdot D\varphi\rangle\quad\forall\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y).

(3.4)

The existence of a unique solution
N^{ij}
to equation (

It is appropriate to note here that the norm in

,
introduced by

is equivalent to

\langle|v|^{2}\rangle\leq C_{P}\langle|\nabla v|^{2}\rangle\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(Y),\,\langle v\rangle=0,

\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y)
and its derivatives
D_{j}\varphi
,

.

From the integral identity (

\|N^{ij}\|^{2}_{\tilde{H}^{2}_{per}(Y)}\leq C,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,d,

with the constant

\lambda

The fourth-order tensor
\hat{a}

\hat{a}e^{ij}=\langle a(\cdot)(D_{y}N^{ij}(\cdot)+e^{ij})\rangle,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,d.

]

Lemma 3.1

For brevity, we denote the norm and the inner product in

4.1. Steklov‘s smoothing operator.

We recall the definition of Steklov‘s smoothing operator

S^{\varepsilon}\varphi(x)=\int_{Y}\varphi(x-\varepsilon\omega)\,d\omega,\quad Y=[-1/2,1/2)^{d},

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi\|,

(4.2)

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\|\leq(\sqrt{d}/2)\varepsilon\|\nabla\varphi\|\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(4.3)

We mention also the evident property
S^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi)=\nabla(S^{\varepsilon}\varphi)
, which is used systematically further. The following properties are pivotal for our method (see their proof in

[
11
]
–
[
13
]

Lemma 4.1

If
\varphi{\in}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

,
and

b_{\varepsilon}S^{\varepsilon}\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\|b_{\varepsilon}S^{\varepsilon}\varphi\|\leq\langle b^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\|\varphi\|.

Lemma 4.2

If
b{\in}L^{2}_{\rm per}(\Box)

\langle b\rangle{=}0
,
b_{\varepsilon}(x){=}b(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
,

,

(4.5)

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad C=const(d),

which

(4.7)

The

Lemma 4.3

7
. Some remarks

Remark 1.
Our proof of estimates (
2.15
) and (
2.18
) does not rely in an essential way on the selfadjointness of the operator
A_{\varepsilon}
. Similar estimates hold true if the symmetry condition from the assumption (
2.2
) is omitted.
The advantage of the symmetry condition is that our formulas become less cumbersome, and a lower number of objects is requested for their derivation.
For instance, passing from (
5.3
) to (
5.4
), we rewrite the sum of five terms as the sum of only four terms. Moreover, the symmetry property of the tensor
a(y)
is inherited by other objects of our consideration, e.g.,

g^{ij}
,
G^{ij,st}
,
g^{ijk}
,
G^{ijk,st}
(see §3); as a consequence, the formulas, where these
objects are involved, may also become simpler. In particular, the equality (
3.17
) (employed not once in the representation of the type (
3.15
)) has a more cumbersome version in the case of a nonsymmetric matrix
B
.

Remark 2.
Assume that the symmetry condition is not required in (
2.2
). Then the
H^{2}
-approximation of
\varepsilon^{2}
order for the resolvent of
A_{\varepsilon}
constructed in a similar fashion as in (
2.11
)–(
2.14
) provides at the same time the

L^{2}
-approximation of
\varepsilon^{2}
order and, thus,

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}=(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

if the correctors are dropped and the main term is simplified by properties of smoothing. The latter approximation has a right to be mentioned along with other ones, e.g., that of (
1.7
).

Remark 3.
The approximations (
1.8
) and (
2.17
) of the resolvent
A_{\varepsilon}
involve
the resolvent of the homogenized fifth-order operator

\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}
, although the order of
A_{\varepsilon}
is four. This phenomenon is known; in various homogenization problems, there appeared formerly not once homogenized operators, which had the order larger than that of the given operator.
For instance,
in
[
18
]
, where a non-stationary diffusion equation in a periodic medium is considered
and approximations for its solution
u(x,t)
are sought in

L^{2}
-norm in sections
t{=}const{>}0
with accuracy
O(t^{-\frac{m}{2}})

(
m{\geq}2
is a natural parameter) as
t{\to}+\infty
,
the order of the homogenized operator is dictated by the order of approximation and increases without limit simultaneously with the parameter
m
.

Remark 4.
The operator
\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}
appears as a singular perturbation of the operator
\hat{A}
commonly employed in homogenization.
Singularly perturbed operators
are known and widely used in the elasticity theory of thin plates and shells
(see, e.g.,
[
19
]
). But the
operator
\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}
, which is involved in
(
2.11
) or (
2.17
), does not refer to any type of the singular perturbation described in

[
19
]
.

Remark 5.

In the proof of Theorem
5.2

∘

Consider one of them:

Remark 6.
Some words about the method we use to prove operator estimates. We apply
the approach proposed firstly by Zhikov in
[
20
]
,
but in the version from
[

]

two close to each other ways proposed in

[

[
20

[
5
]

[
12
]
or
[
14
]
–
[
16
]
).

Another method to prove operator estimates in homogenization is based on the Floquet–Bloch transformation and spectral arguments. It is used for high order operators, e.g., in
[
2
]
,
[
21
]
, and
[
8
]
.
The result recently announced in
[
8

is related to a fourth-order elliptic self-adjoint matrix differential operator

with
\varepsilon

In
[
8
]

Commuting Line Defects At q N = 1 q^{N}=1
Davide Gaiotto, Gregory W. Moore, Andrew Neitzke, and Fei Yan

Abstract
We explain the physical origin of a curious property of algebras 𝒜 𝔮 {\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}} which encode the rotation-equivariant fusion ring of half-BPS line defects in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 {\cal N}=2 supersymmetric quantum field theories. These algebras are a quantization of the algebras of holomorphic functions on the three-dimensional Coulomb branch of the SQFTs, with deformation parameter log ⁡ 𝔮 \log{\mathfrak{q}} . They are known to acquire a large center, canonically isomorphic to the undeformed algebra, whenever 𝔮 {\mathfrak{q}} is a root of unity. We give a physical explanation of this fact. We also generalize the construction to characterize the action of this center in the 𝒜 𝔮 {\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}} -modules associated to three-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 {\cal N}=2 boundary conditions. Finally, we use dualities to relate this construction to a construction in the Kapustin-Witten twist of four-dimensional 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 gauge theory. These considerations give simple physical explanations of certain properties of quantized skein algebras and cluster varieties, and quantum groups, when the deformation parameter is a root of unity.

The centers of algebras of line defects at roots of unity

2.1
Preliminaries on line defects

The main actors in our construction are half-BPS line defects in a four-dimensional
{\mathcal{N}}=2
supersymmetric quantum field theory we denote as
T
. We refer to
Gaiotto:2010be
for a detailed discussion of the properties of such line defects and a physical derivation of the algebras
{\cal A}_{\mathfrak{q}}[T]
associated to these line defects.
2
2

2

Here
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a square root of
q
, whose role will appear momentarily.

Many of the details of the physical definition of half-BPS line defects are actually unnecessary for this physical derivation. In particular, a physical defect may belong to a large deformation class of defects, which would be identified at the level of the algebra.

A more modern perspective would employ a holomorphic-topological (HT) twist of the physical theory, i.e. a twist which makes two directions of space-time topological and combines the remaining two in an holomorphic direction. See e.g.
Aganagic:2017tvx
;
Costello:2020ndc
for a three-dimensional analogue. Supersymmetric line defects map under the twist to topological line defects wrapping one of the topological directions (e.g. “time”) and can be fused along the transverse topological direction.

Working equivariantly for the group
\Spin(2)
of rotations of the holomorphic direction, such topological defects form a (derived)
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant monoidal category. The “collection of line defects” could be tentatively formalized as the K-theory of such a category. The construction of
2018arXiv180108111C
should be understood as an example of such a construction.

In the following we will write formulae assuming the existence of some countable collection of
linear generators
{\mathfrak{L}}_{i}
for the K-theory, so that
the fusion of (classes of) line defects will be a direct sum of (classes of) line defects with coefficients which are K-theory classes of “Chan-Paton” vector spaces, which may carry quantum numbers for the symmetries of the problem:

{\mathfrak{L}}_{i}\,{\mathfrak{L}}_{j}=\bigoplus_{k}[V^{k}_{ij}]\otimes{\mathfrak{L}}_{k}\,.

(4)

The language of K-theory is particularly useful in the context of
S^{1}
compactifications of the theory. Line defects wrapping
S^{1}
appear as local operators in the three-dimensional effective field theory, which only depend on the K-theory class of the original line. Relations such as (
4
) capture the product of these 3d local operators,
while making manifest integrality properties inherited from the underlying 4d setup: the 3d structure constants in this basis will be expressed as characters of the
[V^{k}_{ij}]
. We will come back to this momentarily in
subsection 2.2
.

We will not employ the language of twisted SQFT in the rest of this paper, but a translation should be straightforward (and would likely streamline our arguments). Constructions involving six-dimensional
(2,0)
SCFTs should be analogously recast in terms of their HT twist.

The Melvin space
M_{q}
is the simplest non-trivial three-dimensional “HT” geometry, i.e. a manifold equipped with a transverse holomorphic foliation
Aganagic:2017tvx
. A four-dimensional HT
theory can thus be defined on
\mathbb{R}\times M_{q}
. This is the context where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
will arise.

In the physical theory, it is natural to consider line operators which are equivariant under the
\Spin(3)
group of rotations. However, both the HT twist and the operation of fusion are only compatible with equivariance under the
\Spin(2)
subgroup fixing the topological fusion direction. It would be interesting to find criteria to pin down K-theory classes which include
\Spin(3)
-equivariant defects. We suspect they would generate the collection in an appropriate sense; this conjecture could have interesting mathematical consequences, but we will not study it in this paper.

2.2
Line defects and local operators

The four-dimensional SQFT
T
can be compactified on a circle with Ramond spin structure while preserving all supercharges. The resulting effective three-dimensional
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SQFT has a Coulomb branch of vacua.
Upon choosing a parameter
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, we can identify this Coulomb branch as
a complex symplectic manifold
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}[T]
, which parameterizes the expectation values of half-BPS line defects wrapping the internal circle.
3
3

3

The
3d Coulomb branch is a hyperkähler manifold and is thus equipped with a whole
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
family of complex structures. In two of these complex structures (
\zeta=0
and
\zeta=\infty
),
the 3d Coulomb branch is a torus fibration over the 4d Coulomb branch, and cannot be parameterized by BPS local operators. In all of the other complex structures (
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
), BPS line defects give holomorphic functions on the 3d Coulomb branch. The choice of
\zeta
corresponds to the choice of which 1/2-BPS subalgebra of the 3d supersymmetry algebra is preserved by the line defects.

Throughout this paper we will hold
\zeta
fixed — say, to
\zeta=1
— and not consider the extra structure coming from varying this choice (with one brief exception, in

subsection 2.7
.)
As a complex symplectic manifold,
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}[T]
has a weak dependence on the radius
R
of the circle: a change of radius can be absorbed in a rescaling of the dimensionful parameters of
T
(if any). Thus we will generally hold
R=1
, except in a few places where we consider the limit of small
R
.

We denote as
{\cal A}_{-}[T]
the Poisson algebra generated by the half-BPS line defects wrapping the internal circle. This commutative algebra has integral structure constants (“fusion coefficients”) which capture coarse information about the
fusion of line defects. If we denote as
L_{i}
the local operator obtained by circle-wrapping
{\mathfrak{L}}_{i}
, the algebra takes the form

L_{i}L_{j}=\sum_{k}n^{k}_{ij}L_{k}\,.

(5)

By taking expectation values we can identify
{\mathcal{A}}_{-}[T]
with the algebra of global holomorphic functions on
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}[T]
, and under
this identification the product in (
5
) is the usual product of functions.

The circle compactification can be twisted by holonomies for every “flavour” symmetry of the theory, i.e. every global symmetry which commutes with the supercharges.
This gives commutative deformations of
{\mathcal{A}}_{-}[T]
, as the fusion coefficients in the algebra are replaced by characters of fusion spaces, which are Laurent polynomials in appropriate fugacities
\mu
for the flavour symmetries:

L_{i}L_{j}=\sum_{k,f}n^{k}_{ij}[f]\mu^{f}L_{k}

(6)

This is the algebra of functions on “mass deformations” of the 3d Coulomb branch, with
\mu
being combinations of masses and flavour holonomies. Whenever we refer to
{\mathcal{A}}_{-}[T]
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}[T]
etc. below we
will include the possibility of such deformations.

All 4d
{\mathcal{N}}=2
theories which we will discuss are equipped with an
SU(2)_{R}
R-symmetry. The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
diagonal combination of the centers

and

\Spin(3)
rotation group and of

commutes with the supercharges and is always available to twist the circle compactification. The result is another 3d Coulomb branch

and Poisson algebra of functions

4
4

4

Typically,

are isomorphic, though not canonically so. We will see these isomorphisms explicitly in some of the examples later, e.g. in

In order to “quantize” the Poisson algebras

for a certain combination of

2.3
The Melvin compactification

Fix

2.4
Line defects in the Melvin background

Now we consider placing supersymmetric line defects in
{{\mathbb{R}}}\times M_{q}
.
Each line defect will be placed at a point
t\in{{\mathbb{R}}}
and extended along a circle in
M_{q}
. In order for the line defect to be supersymmetric, this circle must be a closed Euclidean geodesic in
M_{q}
, i.e. locally a straight line.
6
6

6

If we used the language of the holomorphic-topological twist, we would say instead that the circle must be a closed leaf of the foliation of
M_{q}
which we discussed above. In this example, it happens that the closed geodesics are the same as the closed leaves, so we can use either language.

When
q\neq 1
is generic, the only closed Euclidean geodesic in
M_{q}
is the one located at
z=0
, i.e. the image of
\psi:S^{1}\to M_{q}
discussed in the previous section. Thus the space of possible insertion locations for the line defects is
{{\mathbb{R}}}_{t}
. The (K-theory classes of) line defects can be fused along the
{{\mathbb{R}}}_{t}
direction to give an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}[T]
, whose structure coefficients are Laurent polynomials in
{\mathfrak{q}}
with integer coefficients:

L_{i}L_{j}=\sum_{k,s}n^{k}_{ij}[s]{\mathfrak{q}}^{s}L_{k}\,.

(9)

The parameter
{\mathfrak{q}}
plays the role of the fugacity for the combination of spin and R-symmetry rotations.
The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}[T]
is typically not commutative, as wrapped lines cannot exchange their position in
{{\mathbb{R}}}_{t}
without colliding with each other.

In the
q\to 1
limit, we recover the commutative algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}[T]
.
From the geometric perspective, this commutativity arises because we now have closed geodesics at any value
z=z_{0}
, and thus wrapped line defects can be moved away from
z=0
to exchange their position in
{{\mathbb{R}}}_{t}
without colliding with each other.

It is also interesting to consider the behavior
near

q=1
.
One geometric way to realize this is as follows. We rescale the metric in
M_{q}
by a factor of

, so that the geodesic at

now has length
R
. Next we set

,
and take

while holding

theory on
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{3}
, in

;

;

The wrapped line defects reduce to local operators of the

(see e.g.

2.5
Line defects in the IR and Seiberg-Witten theory

At generic points
u
of the 4d Coulomb branch, the theory
T
admits a low-energy effective description in the form of an abelian gauge theory. Any BPS line defect
{\mathfrak{L}}
also admits an effective description in terms of abelian line defects
\mathfrak{X}_{\gamma}
with gauge and flavour charges
\gamma
.
The qualifications we made in
subsection 2.1
apply here: rather than the full
physical line defects, at best we can discuss K-theory classes in some category of topological line defects in a twist of the theory.
8
8

8

It would be rather interesting to actually discuss the “RG flow functor” mapping the category of UV line defects into a category of IR line defects
2018arXiv180108111C
, perhaps in the same sense as in the web description of categories of 2d TFTs
Gaiotto:2015aoa
.

With this understood we write the UV-IR map as

{\mathfrak{L}}\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad\bigoplus_{\gamma}\left[V_{{\mathfrak{L}},\gamma}\right]\otimes\mathfrak{X}_{\gamma}

(19)

where
V_{{\mathfrak{L}},\gamma}
are (perhaps virtual) Chan-Paton vector spaces.

The framed BPS degeneracies introduced in
Gaiotto:2010be
agree with
the characters of these Chan-Paton vector spaces,

\overline{\underline{\Omega}}(\mathfrak{L},\gamma,{\mathfrak{q}})={\rm Tr}_{V_{{\mathfrak{L}},\gamma}}(-1)^{2J_{3}}(-{\mathfrak{q}})^{2I_{3}+2J_{3}}\,.

(20)

These framed BPS degeneracies jump at walls of framed marginal stability in the Coulomb branch.
Accordingly, the IR line defects
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\gamma}
also jump, by cluster-like transformations controlled by the BPS spectrum of the theory.

As we did in
subsection 2.4
, we can
wrap the IR line defects
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\gamma}
around the
core circle
\psi_{0}
, thus obtaining generators
X_{\gamma}:=L_{{\mathfrak{X}}_{\gamma}}
of a noncommutative algebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
of local operators on the line.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
can be described explicitly: it is the quantum torus
Gaiotto:2010be

X_{\gamma}X_{\gamma^{\prime}}={\mathfrak{q}}^{\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}X_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}\,.

(21)

The fusion of line defects commutes with the flow from UV to IR; thus this flow gives a
homomorphism
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

All of our arguments about the multiwrapping map apply just as well in the IR theory as in the UV theory.
Moreover, since the IR theory is an abelian gauge theory, and any IR line defect
is a Wilson line in some duality frame,
we can determine the multiwrapping map
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\pm 1}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
completely
using the Adams operations: indeed (
17
) just becomes

X_{\gamma,N}=X_{N\gamma}\,.

(22)

Now we can make a consistency check: we see directly from the quantum torus relation (
21
) that
X_{N\gamma}
is central and

X_{N\gamma}X_{N\gamma^{\prime}}={\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}X_{N(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})}\,,

(23)

so the multiwrapped line defects indeed obey the relations of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\pm 1}
,
with the sign
\pm 1={\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}
which we derived from the holonomy of the R-symmetry connection in
subsection 2.4
.

As we have already remarked,
the local geometry around the multiwrapped defects is precisely the same as for the singly-wrapped
defects. Moreover, the UV-IR map can be computed using only the local geometry around the defect and the constant vevs of Coulomb branch scalars of the 4d theory. It follows that the UV-IR map commutes with the multiwrapping map.
Thus, given a wrapped line defect
L
(arising as
L=L_{\mathfrak{L}}
for some
{\mathfrak{L}}
), we may compute its multiwrapped image
L_{N}

by first flowing to the IR and then applying the simple rule (

To summarize:
if in some chamber
L
has the IR expansion

L^{\mathrm{IR}}=\sum_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}[L,{\mathfrak{q}}]X_{\gamma}\,,

(24)

then the image of

under the multiwrapping map
defined in
subsection 2.4

has IR expansion

L_{N}^{\mathrm{IR}}=\sum_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}[L,{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}]X_{N\gamma}\,.

(25)

This is the main result of this section: it gives a simple way of computing
L^{\mathrm{IR}}_{N}
in terms of
L^{\mathrm{IR}}
.

We should observe that for gauge theories, the IR image of a non-abelian Wilson line is typically
not
a direct sum of abelian Wilson lines. The compatibility between our IR and UV predictions for multi-wrapped Wilson lines thus appears to impose strong constraints on the possible framed BPS spectra of non-abelian Wilson lines. We will explore this phenomenon in examples.

Setting aside the physical discussion for a moment,
suppose we regard the right side of (
25

. Then it is easy to see that the composite map

L[{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}]\mapsto L^{\mathrm{IR}}[{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}]\mapsto L^{\mathrm{IR}}_{N}

homomorphism
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{IR}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

with central image.
What is not obvious is that

is actually the IR image of an element

Nevertheless, this follows from our physical construction of

in terms

It also follows from this construction that

to match the classical wall-crossing transformation of

\prod_{s=1}^{\langle\gamma^{\prime},\gamma\rangle}(1-(-{\mathfrak{q}})^{m}q^{-s}X_{\gamma^{\prime}})

Gaiotto:2010be

9
9

9

Gaiotto:2010be

If we make the replacement

We conclude that different choices of IR chamber give equivalent definitions of

The simplest possibility is that in some chamber we have

A more interesting example occurs in

In these theories, many line defects can be expanded as

10

10

Thus we recover a fact proven by different means in

This discussion generalizes

We find it remarkable that the multiwrapping map

encodes properties of line defects in a manner which is independent of the duality frame or IR chamber. It would be tempting
to try to classify line defects according to how multiwrapping acts on them. For instance, we could call a line defect “pure” if it has

(We have seen above that this is the case for any line defect which has a single framed BPS state in some chamber, but we stress that the property of being pure is a UV property, independent of any particular chamber.) More generally we could consider, for example, the class of line defects which have

2.6
Line defects on the boundary

We can also equip the 4d
{\mathcal{N}}=2
theory
T
with a half-BPS boundary condition
B
. Such a boundary condition supports a collection of BPS line defects,
which can fuse with each other along the boundary, and also fuse with bulk line defects brought to the boundary.
We now repeat all of our earlier constructions in the presence of the boundary.

First, we can consider
T
on
[0,\infty)_{t}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}\times S^{1}
, with the boundary condition
B
at
\{t=0\}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}\times S^{1}
.
Then wrapping boundary line defects on
S^{1}
gives operators which are free to move in the boundary
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
, and thus form a commutative algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
.
The algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
is also acted on by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}
, since the wrapped line defects in the bulk can fuse with the wrapped line defects on the boundary.

Let us discuss the structure of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}

as a module over
{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}
.
Given a point
x\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}

with corresponding maximal ideal
I_{x}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}
, we can consider the quotient algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}/I_{x}{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
;
letting
x
vary this gives a sheaf of
quotient algebras over
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
.
What can we say about this sheaf?
Physically, the boundary condition
B
descends to a boundary condition for the 3d theory, preserving 2d
(2,2)
supersymmetry.
11
11

11

There is some freedom in the choice of which
{\mathcal{N}}=(2,2)
subalgebra is preserved; we choose it in such a way that the bulk line defects with parameter
\zeta
preserve
2
of the supersymmetries.

Such a boundary condition should be supported on some complex Lagrangian submanifold

{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[B]
of the
3d Coulomb branch
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
, consisting of the 3d vacua compatible with the boundary. We thus expect the sheaf representing
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
to be supported on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[B]\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
.

We can now “quantize”
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}
, by placing
T
on
[0,\infty)_{t}\times M_{q}
,
with the half-BPS boundary condition
B
at
\{t=0\}\times M_{q}
.
Boundary line defects wrapping the core circle at
z=0
in
M_{q}

form an
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

With respect to bases given by boundary line defects,
the entries of the matrices giving the action of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
are again integral Laurent polynomials in
{\mathfrak{q}}
.

For

When

can see that these multiwrapped boundary defects make up a commutative algebra, identified with
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pm}

\pm 1={\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}
.
We illustrate single-wrapped and multiwrapped boundary lines in

.

2.7
An orbifold defect in 3d

In this section we briefly discuss another point of view on our construction.
Assume that
q
is a primitive
N^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity. We can dimensionally reduce
along the fibration

\pi_{2}:M_{q}\to\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}

(32)

defined by
\pi_{2}:[(z,x)]\mapsto[z]
.
12
12

12

This is a circle bundle over an orbifold, or more properly, a circle bundle over a quotient groupoid (i.e. a quotient stack).

The reduced theory is thus a 3d theory on a supersymmetric orbifold
{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
.
We can think of this as the 3d theory in the presence of a codimension-2 supersymmetric defect,
supported at the orbifold line. Passing to an appropriate topological twist of the 3d theory, the
orbifold should be invisible and we should be able to treat this as a standard line defect.

We can then move to the 3d Coulomb branch, where the 3d theory has an effective description as an IR-free non-linear sigma-model with target
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
. In this setup a supersymmetric codimension-2 defect should admit an effective description in terms of some auxiliary finite-dimensional quantum-mechanical system coupled to the sigma model, i.e. a hyperholomorphic sheaf

V_{\mathfrak{q}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
which is a finite-rank vector bundle at least over a Zariski open subset of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]

Thompson:2000pw

Considering UV line defects at
z=0
as local operators on the orbifold defect would give a map from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
to the space of global sections of
{\mathrm{End}}(V_{\mathfrak{q}})

{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
with functions on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}[T]
.

We will now assume that this map is actually an isomorphism. This is a non-trivial assumption: it means that operators which are different in the UV will act differently on the IR data, and every possible operator in the IR description is the image of some UV operator. We will not try to justify this assumption. It is
analogous to the observation that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\pm}
is exactly the algebra of holomorphic functions on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\pm}

13

13

is not affine. Generic complex masses, though, should deform the geometry and remove the problem.

At any rate, under this assumption, we could glean information about

14

One new statement we

3.4
Coulomb branch algebras and quantum groups

In this section we discuss several classes of quantized Coulomb branch algebras which are related to quantum groups, and the corresponding root-of-unity phenomena.

3.4.1
Quantum groups and class
{\mathcal{S}}

First we consider theories of class
{\mathcal{S}}

Klemm:1996bj
;
Witten:1997sc
;
Gaiotto:2009we
;
Gaiotto:2009hg
. Recall that these theories are obtained by twisted compactification of 6d
(2,0)
theories, labelled by an ADE Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, on a Riemann surface
C
possibly decorated with punctures and/or twist lines. The 3d Coulomb branch of a class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory is a
character variety
, i.e. a moduli space of flat
{\mathfrak{g}}
-connections on
C
.
21
21

21

There are some important topological subtleties here, relating the choice of global form of the gauge group in the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory to choices of global form of the gauge group in the space of flat connections. We will ignore these subtleties here.

The relation to quantum groups arises when we
consider class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories in the case of a surface
C
with a rank 1 irregular puncture (and any number of other punctures, regular or irregular).
By a “rank
1
irregular puncture” we mean a puncture where the flat
{\mathfrak{g}}
-connection has a second-order pole
with semisimple residue.
The Stokes data at such a puncture
consists of matrices
S^{\pm}
in opposite Borel subgroups of
G
, together with a formal monodromy matrix

W
in the Cartan subgroup
H
. The total monodromy around the puncture is
S^{+}WS^{-}
.

To be precise, the theories we need
to consider are not exactly the usual class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories.
In a class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory with a rank

1
irregular puncture,
there is a global
H
symmetry;
the formal monodromy
W
is fixed in terms of mass parameters for that symmetry, and the matrix elements of
S^{\pm}
are not
well-defined functions on the 3d Coulomb branch: rather, they are well defined
only up to the conjugation action of
H
.
We modify the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory by gauging this global
H
symmetry. This enlarges the 3d Coulomb branch: it makes
W
into a function on the Coulomb branch rather
than a fixed mass parameter, and removes the identification under conjugation by
H
. The matrix elements of
S^{\pm}
, along with
W
, are well defined functions on the enlarged 3d Coulomb branch; thus they give elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
.

The matrices
S^{\pm}
and
W
associated with a rank
1
irregular puncture have another interpretation: they parameterize the “dual Poisson-Lie group”
G^{*}
, as originally observed in
boalchpoissonlie
.
22
22

22

More precisely, there is an issue of finite coverings here, which we are suppressing in the main text. Given
opposite Borel subgroups
B_{\pm}
of
G
with
\pi:B_{\pm}\to H
the standard
projection, one can
define
G^{*}
as
\{(g_{-},g_{+})\in B_{-}\times B_{+}\mid\pi(g_{-})\pi(g_{+})=1
}.
The Stokes data
S^{\pm}
,
W
are related to
g_{\pm}

by
g_{+}=S^{+}W^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
g_{-}=(W^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{-})^{-1}
,
so
G^{*}
is actually a
2^{\mathrm{rank}\ G}
-fold cover of the space of Stokes data, with the fiber given by the square roots of
W\in H
.

Thus we have a map
\mu:{\mathcal{O}}(G^{*})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
.
Moreover, this map is actually a Poisson map, and it is known that it lifts to a map between the standard quantizations on the two sides: on the one hand
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
quantizes to
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
as we have been discussing, on the other hand
{\mathcal{O}}(G^{*})
can be quantized to give the quantum group
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and there is a canonical algebra map
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

Gaiotto:2014lma
;
alex2019quantum
;
schradershapiro
;
shenqg
.

If we take
{\mathfrak{q}}
to be a primitive root of unity with

{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}=1
,
then our general setup predicts that
there must be a canonical multiwrapping map
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, whose image is central.
Anticipating a result from the next section, this multiwrapping map is in an appropriate sense local on
C
. As a consequence, we can focus just on the
neighborhood of the irregular singularity.
Thus we predict that the composite map

{\mathcal{O}}(G^{*})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
should factor through a map
{\mathcal{O}}(G^{*})\to U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
, in a manner independent of the geometry of the rest of
C
.
When
{\mathfrak{q}}^{N^{2}}=-1
we have a similar statement,
replacing
G^{*}
by its
{\mathfrak{q}}=-1
analog which
we call
G^{*}_{-}
.

In other words, our setup predicts that when
q
is a primitive root of unity, the center of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})

should contain a copy of
{\mathcal{O}}(G^{*})
or
{\mathcal{O}}(G_{-}^{*})
.
This is indeed a well known fact in the theory of quantum groups,
as described e.g. in
MR1124981
;
BECK199429
.

Finally we remark that the map
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is compatible with the coproduct and R-matrix of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
, relating them to the amalgamation of character varieties
in the sense of
MR2263192
;
alex2019quantum
;
shenqg
.
The amalgamation procedure can be given a physical interpretation, as governing the behaviour of the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories in a limit where the gauge coupling associated to the irregular singularity becomes very large. It should be possible to use this interpretation to define 3d
{\cal N}=2
interfaces which implement the amalgamation/coproduct, and thus physically
derive the observation that when
q
is a root of unity the coproduct
localizes on its classical limit, i.e. the Poisson map
G^{*}\times G^{*}\to G^{*}
which makes

into a Poisson-Lie group.

This map can be given a physical interpretation via dualities starting from Witten’s geometric engineering construction

Nevertheless, the 4d theory must admit a supersymmetric coupling to the 6d worldvolume theory.
Upon compactification on
M_{q}
, we expect the 6d theory to reduce to 4d holomorphic-topological Chern-Simons theory with coupling
(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{-1}\log q

\mathbb{C}^{*}

23

23

The 4d theory really reduces to a 2d system defined on an half-plane, but the 2d path integral effectively produces an “analytically continued” 1d system at the boundary

. This conjecture is a trigonometric version of the constructions in
Ishtiaque:2018str
;
Costello:2021zcl

for the 4d CS theory to the algebra of local operators of the quantum-mechanical system, i.e.

The role of

in this discussion strongly suggests that the root-of-unity behavior we have discussed should also apply to

3.4.3
Combining these constructions

In the last two subsections we discussed two different
ways of relating quantum groups to quantized Coulomb branches: either using class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories or linear quivers.
There are some examples which can be obtained in both ways. In these cases the expected map
U_{q}(L{\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
associated to the brane realization factors through the well-known map
U_{q}(L{\mathfrak{g}})\to U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
to give the map

{\mathcal{S}}
construction.

The simplest example is a linear quiver with
gauge groups
U(1)\times\cdots\times U(n-1)
and
n

{\mathcal{S}}
realization with
{\mathfrak{g}}=\fsl(N)
, associated to a sphere
with a rank

following a general recipe given in

Witten:1997sc
;
Gaiotto:2009hg

In this case the character variety is parameterized by

and

belongs to a fixed conjugacy class, determined by the
mass parameter for the flavour symmetry associated

Thus the Coulomb branch is the intersection of

a regular orbit of

This Coulomb branch is quantized to a
central quotient of
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Again, it should be possible to push our analysis to derive statements about the coproduct on
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
when

is a root of unity.

4
Class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories and the Kapustin-Witten twist

There is an alternative point of view on the
root-of-unity phenomena in class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories, which emerges from a connection to the
Kapustin-Witten twist of
N=4
super Yang-Mills.

4.1
The large center from Kapustin-Witten twist

Fix a surface
C
and Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. As in the previous section, we will ignore topological subtleties associated to the global form of the group. The
{\mathfrak{g}}
-character variety of
C
coincides with the phase space of 3d Chern-Simons theory compactified on
C
. The natural classical observables on this phase space are (networks of) Wilson lines, or “skeins,”
in
M=C\times\mathbb{R}
. When
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a complex Lie algebra their algebra reproduces the algebra of holomorphic functions on the character variety.

The Chern-Simons path integral provides a natural quantization of this classical phase space. The standard path-integration contour is only well-defined if the Chern-Simons level
\kappa
is appropriately quantized. More generally, the path integral can be done in an “analytically continued” manner for any level

\kappa\in{\mathbb{C}}

Witten:2010cx
;
Witten:2010zr
. This theory provides a canonical quantization of the algebra of observables, i.e. a quantization of the algebra of holomorphic functions on the character variety; this is the skein algebra
\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathfrak{q}}[{\mathfrak{g}},C]
with parameter
{\mathfrak{q}}=\exp\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{\kappa}
.

The analytically continued Chern-Simons path integral on a 3-manifold
M
can be given a more robust definition, as the path integral for the Kapustin-Witten twist
Kapustin:2006pk
of 4d
{\mathcal{N}}=4
super Yang-Mills with parameter
\Psi=\kappa
,
on
M\times[0,\infty)_{t}
, with Neumann boundary conditions at
t=0
.
Thus the Chern-Simons theory is “relative” and cannot be assigned a canonical Hilbert space.

From this point of view, the skein algebra is realized by skeins of boundary Wilson line defects,
and the root-of-unity phenomena arise from a special property of the
Kapustin-Witten twist, as follows. For a generic value of
\Psi
,
the twisted theory does not admit any line defects. When
\Psi=p/q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, the situation is
different: a ’t Hooft-Wilson line labeled by electric and magnetic weights
(\lambda,\lambda^{\vee})

survives into the twisted theory exactly if
q\lambda=p\lambda^{\vee}

Kapustin:2006pk
.
Thus for
\Psi\in{\mathbb{Q}}
(equivalently, for
{\mathfrak{q}}
a root of unity)
the twisted theory has a family of bulk line defects labeled by weights; they are
pure Wilson lines in the special case
\Psi=\infty
, and pure ’t Hooft lines
if
\Psi=0
.
When bulk lines do exist, we can make skeins out of them, and then bring them
to the Neumann boundary; the resulting boundary skeins are central in the skein
algebra
\Sk_{\mathfrak{q}}[{\mathfrak{g}},C]
, since
they are free to move in the fourth dimension. Thus we obtain a large center
in
\Sk_{\mathfrak{q}}[{\mathfrak{g}},C]
, isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the character variety.

4.2
A duality

We have now given two different physical points of view on the appearance of the
large center in
\Sk_{\mathfrak{q}}[{\mathfrak{g}},C]
: one in
section 2
using an
{\mathcal{N}}=2
theory
of class
{\mathcal{S}}
, the other in
subsection 4.1
using
{\mathcal{N}}=4
super Yang-Mills.

The two pictures are related by a chain
of dualities.
Indeed, we simply need to consider the 6d
(2,0)
theory of type
{\mathfrak{g}}
on
M_{q}\times C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
, with a twist along
C
.
We can view this setup in two ways:

•

Reducing first on the
C
factor gives a class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory
on
M_{q}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
. This is the setup
we used in
section 2
.

•

If we replace the
z
-plane in
M_{q}
by a cigar and reduce along its circle direction, the 6d theory
reduces to 5d super Yang-Mills theory on a half-space. The boundary corresponds to the locus

z=0
in
M_{q}
, and carries a principal Nahm pole boundary condition from the 5d perspective. Reducing along the
x
-circle in
M_{q}

corresponds to a further circle reduction of the 5d super Yang-Mills, with a twist by the instanton charge; the resulting system is 4d
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SYM on
[0,\infty)\times C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
,
with coupling
\Psi=\frac{1}{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\log{\mathfrak{q}}
and a principal Nahm pole boundary condition. Finally, S-duality maps this to coupling
\Psi^{-1}=\frac{1}{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\log{\mathfrak{q}}
and a Neumann boundary condition. This is the setup we discussed in

subsection 4.1
.

This sketchy derivation can be made more precise by twisting. The 6d
(2,0)
theory admits a holomorphic-topological twist with four topological directions.
We can repeat the above reduction using the twisted theory
on
M_{q}\times C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
, where the holomorphic direction is the
z
-plane in
M_{q}
.
The twisted theory then reduces to
the holomorphic-topological twist of the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory on
M_{q}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
, and to the
Kapustin-Witten twist of
{\mathcal{N}}=4
super Yang-Mills on
[0,\infty)\times C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Now let us revisit the skeins of Chern-Simons theory
in
C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
. From the 6-dimensional perspective, we can realize
these skeins as 2-dimensional defects: they are the product of a skein in
C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
the geodesic
\psi_{0}
in
M_{q}
.
(Note that these defects continue to exist in the 6-dimensional holomorphic-topological twist,
since they are extended only in the 4 topological directions.)
In the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory, these defects
reduce to supersymmetric line defects in
M_{q}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}

wrapping
\psi_{0}
in
M_{q}
.
In
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SYM, they become boundary Wilson line skeins lying on the Neumann boundary condition.

When
q
is a root of unity, we can also consider “multiwrapping” these 2-dimensional defects,
by putting them on the product of a skein in
C\times{{\mathbb{R}}}
and one of the
geodesics
\psi_{z_{0}}
in
M_{q}
.
These multiwrapped defects reduce to the multiwrapped line defects in the class
{\mathcal{S}}
theory, and to the bulk skeins in the
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SYM theory. Thus the two ways we obtained the large center
in
\Sk_{\mathfrak{q}}[{\mathfrak{g}},C]
are
indeed related by our 6-dimensional setup.

We conclude with a few remarks:

1.

The 4d SYM perspective allows one further manipulation. We can find
an element of the
SL(2,{{\mathbb{Z}}})
duality group (generalized
S
-duality) which maps
\Psi=m/N
to
\Psi=\infty
, where the bulk lines are Wilson lines. Correspondingly, this duality maps the Nahm boundary condition to a more general “
(N,m)
-fivebrane” boundary condition.

We can attempt to describe this
(N,m)
-fivebrane boundary condition as the result of coupling the 4d gauge theory to a 3d SCFT
{\cal T}_{N,m}[{\mathfrak{g}}]
.
To do so, we consider
the 4d theory on an interval, where at one end we place the

(N,m)
boundary condition, and at the other end we place a
Dirichlet boundary condition; the resulting 3d theory should be

{\cal T}_{N,m}[{\mathfrak{g}}]
.
The Dirichlet boundary condition fixes the value of the bulk skeins, so effectively
the algebra of skeins in
{\cal T}_{N,m}[{\mathfrak{g}}]
is a quotient of the full skein algebra,
where the central elements are fixed to specific values determined by a point of the character
variety.
In this way one should be able to identify the theories
{\cal T}_{N,m}[{\mathfrak{g}}]
with the ones
considered in
Creutzig:2021ext
.

2.

A direct consequence of the 4d SYM
description is that the construction of
L_{N}
from
L

must be a local operation on the skein representing
L
: a bulk line labelled by a representation
R
must correspond to a boundary line in some (possibly virtual) representation
R_{N}
.
25
25

25

In principle this construction could also depend on the numerator
m
in
\Psi=m/N
, through the choices of intertwiners at junctions.
We have already seen examples of this, for skeins consisting of a single loop. Similarly, the intertwiners at the junctions of bulk lines must map universally to intertwiners of boundary lines, not depending on the rest of the skein.

In other words, the construction of
L_{N}
should follow from a categorical statement about the braided category of lines in analytically continued Chern-Simons theory and the center which appears at roots of unity.

3.

Class
{\mathcal{S}}
theories come equipped with a large collection of “class
R
” boundary conditions, arising from the compactification of the
(2,0)
6d theories on 3d manifolds

with cylindrical asymptotic regions

In the 3d Coulomb branch perspective, such a boundary
condition is supported on the Lagrangian submanifold of flat connections on

which extend to

D
; upon quantization they give rise to skein modules. For some background on the notion of skein module see e.g.

;

.

Following the general picture in
subsection 2.6
, we predict some
additional structure when

is a root of unity.
First, the skein module associated to a class

will be supported on its classical image in the character variety.
Second, this skein module will admit a large submodule
which has a natural commutative algebra structure.
Concretely, this algebra structure arises from the fact
that the skeins in the image of the multiwrapping map can be freely isotoped across one another,
so there is a well defined operation of juxtaposing them.

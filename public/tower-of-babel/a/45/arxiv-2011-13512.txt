Finite convergence of locally proper circumcentered methods
Hui Ouyang

Abstract
In view of the great performance of circumcentered isometry methods for solving the best approximation problem, in this work we further investigate the locally proper circumcenter mapping and circumcentered method. Various examples of locally proper circumcenter mapping are presented and studied. Inspired by some results on circumcentered-reflection method by Behling, Bello-Cruz, and Santos in their recent papers [ 11 ] , [ 12 ] , and [ 1 ] , we provide sufficient conditions for one-step convergence of circumcentered isometry methods for finding the best approximation point onto the intersection of fixed point sets of related isometries. In addition, we elaborate the performance of circumcentered reflection methods induced by reflectors associated with hyperplanes and halfspaces for finding the best approximation point onto (or a point in) the intersection of hyperplanes and halfspaces.

Throughout this paper, we assume that

Denote by

Let

Assume that

Particularly, if all

For every

In
[
9

[
6

Results on circumcentered methods accelerating the well-known Douglas-Rachford method and method of alternating projections can be found in
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[

Note that circumcenter mappings induced by sets of operators that are not isometric are generally not globally proper, which implies that the related circumcentered methods may not be well-defined on the whole space. But clearly if a circumcenter mapping is proper over a nonempty set, say

\mathcal{D}

In this work,
our goal is to study locally proper circumcenter mappings and to explore the finite convergence of circumcentered methods (not necessarily proper over

) for solving the best approximation and feasibility problems

Lemma 2.5

Recall that

Circumcenters

According to

Definition 3.1

[

.

[

, Theorem 8.4]

Suppose that

and denote the cardinality of

by

.
Then exactly one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

.

(ii)

(iii)

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{p,x,y\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\lVert p-x\rVert=\lVert p-y\rVert
.

(ii)

\langle p-x,y-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}
.

(iii)

\langle p-\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{x+y}{2}-x\rangle=0
.

(iv)

\langle p-\frac{x+y}{2},y-x\rangle=0
.

(v)

\langle x-p,y-p\rangle=\lVert p-x\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
, we know that
Item

(i)

\Leftrightarrow

Item

(ii)
.
Because

, we have that

\displaystyle\langle p-x,y-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2\Big\langle p-x,\frac{x+y}{2}-x\Big\rangle=2{\Big\lVert}\frac{x+y}{2}-x{\Big\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Big\langle p-\frac{x+y}{2}+\frac{x+y}{2}-x,\frac{x+y}{2}-x\Big\rangle={\Big\lVert}\frac{x+y}{2}-x{\Big\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Big\langle p-\frac{x+y}{2},\frac{x+y}{2}-x\Big\rangle=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Big\langle p-\frac{x+y}{2},y-x\Big\rangle=0,

which shows the equivalence of
Item

(ii)
,
Item

(iii)
and
Item

(iv)
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\langle p-x,y-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\langle p-x,y-p+p-x\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\langle x-p,y-p\rangle=\lVert p-x\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2},

which necessitates that
Item

(ii)

\Leftrightarrow

Item

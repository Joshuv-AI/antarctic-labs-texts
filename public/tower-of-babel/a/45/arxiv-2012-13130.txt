1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a reduced projective curve over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. Let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
on
C
. Suppose
V
is a linear subspace of
H^{0}(E)
of dimension
k>r
such that
V
generates
E
. Such a pair
(E,V)
is called a generated pair on
C
. The kernel bundle
M_{E,V}
on
C
of the generated pair
(E,V)
is defined by the following exact sequence :

(1.1)

0\rightarrow M_{E,V}\rightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0.

Also, the generated pair
(E,V)
gives a morphism

\varphi_{E,V}:C\rightarrow Gr(r,V):=G,

with
E\cong\varphi_{E,V}^{*}(Q)
, where
Q
is the quotient bundle on
G
defined by the exact sequence

(1.2)

0\rightarrow S\rightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{G}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0,

and
S
is the universal bundle on
G
. Therefore,
M_{E,V}
can be observed as the pull back of the universal bundle
S
on
G
via the morphism
\varphi_{E,V}
. In particular, for the case of a line bundle
L
, we have
\varphi^{*}_{L,V}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}(1))\cong M_{L,V}
, where
\mathbb{P}
and
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
are respectively the projective space
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
and its twisted cotangent bundle. When
V=H^{0}(E)
, we denote the kernel bundle by
M_{E}
instead of
M_{E,V}
.

The question of (semi)stability of
M_{E,V}
is a long standing problem. One of the reasons for studying this question lies in its application to the syzygy problems
[
12
,
15
]
. It also has a deep connection with the higher rank Brill-Noether Theory
[
3
]
.

Over a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
, it was proved by Ein and Lazarsfeld that
M_{L}
is (semi)stable whenever
L
is a line bundle on
C
with
\deg L(\geqslant)>2g

[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
. This result was generalised for the case of a (semi)stable vector bundle by Butler. More precisely, he proved that
M_{E}
is (semi)stable if
\deg E(\geqslant)>2gr
, where
E
is a rank
r
(semi)stable vector bundle on a smooth curve
C
of genus
g\geqslant 2

[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
. Much later, the bound on the degree was improved for the case of a line bundle by Camere
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
. Moreover, the bound obtained by her is the best known bound so far.

The (semi)stability of
M_{E,V}
has been studied in connection with theta-divisor
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
16
]
as well. But still not much is known when
V\neq H^{0}(E)
.

In
[
10
]
, Butler made a conjecture on the semistability of
M_{E,V}
for a semistable vector bundle
E
on
C
. One version of the conjecture is as follows:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
C
be a general smooth curve of genus
g\geqslant 3
. Then, for a general choice of a generated pair
(E,V)
on
C
, where
E
is a semistable vector bundle, the kernel bundle
M_{E,V}
is semistable.

A lot of work has been done to address this conjecture (see for example,
[
1
,
4
,
7
]
). Nevertheless, it is yet to reach complete solution. In 2015, Bhosle, Brambila-Paz and Newstead proved the conjecture for a line bundle
L
by using the wall crossing formulae for coherent systems of a curve
[
5
]
.
Though there has been a considerable amount of literature on the (semi)stability of the kernel bundle over a smooth projective curve, not much is known for the case of a singular curve.

In 2019, Brivio and Favale
[
8
]
studied the (semi)stability of
M_{E,V}
on a reducible curve with a node. Surprisingly, they were able to prove results which go in the opposite direction with respect to what is known in the smooth curve case. More precisely, suppose
C
is a reducible nodal curve with two smooth components
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
intersecting at a node
p
,
(E,V)
is a generated pair on
C
and
E_{j}
is the restriction of
E
to
C_{j}
for
j=1,2
. Then Brivio and Favale proved that whenever
E_{j}
is semistable on
C_{j}
and
V\cap H^{0}(E_{j}(-p))\neq 0
for each
j
, then the kernel bundle
M_{E,V}
is strongly unstable (
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
). Further, they also provided conditions under which
M_{E,V}
becomes
w
-semistable for a suitable choice of polarization
w
(
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Motivated by their results, in this article, we consider the same question for the case of a
chain-like curve

C
having
n
smooth components
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
and
n-1
nodes
p_{1},\dots,p_{n-1}
, where
n\geq 2
(see section
2
for the precise definition of a chain-like curve). We first prove a more general result about the
w
-semistability of any subbundle of a trivial bundle over
C
. More precisely, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem
3.8
.

Let
C
be a chain-like curve. Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is a nonzero finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a subbundle of the trivial bundle
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}
on
C
such that
\text{rk}(\mathcal{M})=m
,
\chi(\mathcal{M})<0
and the quotient
\mathcal{E}:=\frac{\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}}{\mathcal{M}}
is locally free. Suppose
\mathcal{M}_{|_{C_{j}}}
is semistable for each
j
. Then there exists a polarization
w
such that
\mathcal{M}
is
w
-semistable. Further, if
\mathcal{M}_{|_{C_{j}}}
is stable for some
j
, then
\mathcal{M}
is
w
-stable.

Since the kernel bundle
M_{E,V}
associated to a generated pair
(E,V)
is a subbundle of
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}
, as a corollary to the above theorem, we obtain the following result:

Corollary
3.9
.

Let
C
be a chain-like curve and
(E,V)
be a generated pair on
C
. If for each
j
,
{M_{E,V}}_{|_{C_{j}}}
is semistable, then there exists a polarization
w
such that
M_{E,V}
is
w
-semistable.

Here, we would like to emphasize that
if the number of components in
C
is greater than
2
, the inequalities involving the polarization and Euler characteristics (the inequalities (
2.6
) to be precise) will be slightly more complicated than the case when
n=2
. So the proof for existence of such a polarization
w
which makes
M_{E,V}

w
-semistable, is a little more involved (see Lemma
3.5
).

We then provide some sufficient conditions under which
M_{E,V}
and
M_{E,V}\otimes L
become strongly unstable, where
L
is any line bundle on
C
. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem
4.1
.

Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on a chain-like curve
C
. Then
M_{E,V}
is strongly unstable, if any of the following conditions hold:

(a)

V\cap H^{0}(E_{1}(-p_{1}))\neq 0
(or
V\cap H^{0}(E_{n}(-p_{n-1}))\neq 0
),
E_{1}
(resp.
E_{n}
) is semistable and
(k-r)<d_{1}
(resp.
(k-r)<d_{n}
).

(b)

there exists
j\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
such that
V\cap H^{0}(E_{j}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j}))\neq 0
,
E_{j}
is semistable and
(k-r)<\frac{d_{j}}{2}
,

where
d_{j}:=\deg(E_{j})
.

Theorem
4.6
.

Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on a chain-like curve
C
with
\frac{d}{k-r}>(n-1)
. Suppose for each
j
,
\rho_{j}
denotes the natural map
H^{0}(E)\rightarrow H^{0}(E_{j})
and
\text{Ker}({\rho_{j}}_{|_{V}})\neq 0
. Then
M_{E,V}\otimes L
is strongly unstable, for any line bundle
L
on
C
. In particular,
M_{E,V}
is strongly unstable.

As a corollary, we show that when
n=2
,
E_{j}
is semistable for
j=1,2
and
L=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, the last theorem stated above is nothing but
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer. Let
C
be a projective reducible nodal curve over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers having
n
smooth irreducible components
C_{i}
of genus
g_{i}\geq 2
and
n-1
nodes
p_{i}
such that
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
whenever
|i-j|>1
and
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}=\{p_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. We call such a curve a
chain-like
curve.

On such a curve
C
,
we have the following exact sequence:

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{T}
is supported only at the nodal point(s).
Also, it is not hard show that each
\mathcal{T}_{p_{i}}
is a one dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
and that
h^{0}(\mathcal{T})=(n-1)
.
From the exact sequence
2.1
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}})-\chi(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}-(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}

So if we let
p_{a}(C)=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})
be the arithmetic genus of
C
, then
p_{a}(C)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}
.

Now suppose
E
is a vector bundle of rank
r
on
C
. Tensoring the exact sequence (
2.1
) with
E
, denoting
E_{|_{C_{j}}}
by
E_{j}
and the cokernel by
\mathcal{T}_{E}
, we obtain

(2.3)

0\rightarrow E\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{E}\rightarrow 0.

We also have

\chi(E)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi(E_{j})-r(n-1).

Further, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have the following short exact sequences :

(2.4)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C_{j}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}\rightarrow 0,

where

(\mathcal{I}_{C_{j}})_{q}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}_{C,q}~\text{if}~q\in C\setminus C_{j},\\
0~~\text{if}~~q\in C_{j}\setminus(C_{j}\cap\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n-1}\}),\\
t\mathcal{O}_{C,q}~~\text{if}~~q\in C_{j}\cap\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n-1}\}\end{cases}

and
t\in\mathfrak{m}_{C,q}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{C,q}
such that
C_{j}\cap\text{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{C,q})
is defined by
t=0
.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
and
E_{j}
be
E_{|_{C_{j}}}
. From now on, by
E_{1}(-p_{0}-p_{1})
and
E_{n}(-p_{n-1}-p_{n})
we mean
E_{1}(-p_{1})
and
E_{n}(-p_{n-1})
respectively. There are no such points as
p_{0}
and
p_{n}
on
C
. But this convention helps in stating some of the subsequent results in a compact way. We also make an explicit mention of the sheaves
E_{1}(-p_{1})
and
E_{n}(-p_{n-1})
if required.

With this notation, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we also have the following inclusions:

(2.5)

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}(-p_{i-1}-p_{i})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{C_{j}}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F
be a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules. We call
F
a pure sheaf of dimension one if for every proper
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-submodule
G\subset F
and
G\neq 0
, the dimension of support of
G
is equal to one.

Vector bundles on
C
are examples of pure sheaves of dimension one. Suppose
F
is a pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
. Let
F_{j}=\frac{F_{|_{C_{j}}}}{\text{Torsion}(F_{|_{C_{j}}})}
for each
j
, where
\text{Torsion}(F_{|_{C_{j}}})
is the torsion subsheaf of
F_{|_{C_{j}}}
. Then
F_{j}
, if non-zero, is torsion-free and hence locally free on
C_{j}
. Let
r_{j}
denote the rank of
F_{j}
. Also let
d_{j}
denote the degree of
F_{j}
for each
j
. Then we call the n-tuples
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
and
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
respectively the
multirank
and
multidegree
of
F
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
be a n-tuple of rational numbers such that
0<w_{j}<1
for each
j
and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}=1
. We call such a n-tuple a polarization on
C
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
F
is a pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
of multirank
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
. Then the slope of
F
with respect to a polarization
w
, denoted by
\mu_{w}(F)
, is defined by
\mu_{w}(F)=\frac{\chi(F)}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}r_{j}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle defined on
C
. Then
E
is said to be
w
-semistable (resp.
w
-stable) if for any proper subsheaf
F\subset E
one has
\mu_{w}(F)\leq\mu_{w}(E)
(resp.
\mu_{w}(F)<\mu_{w}(E)
). If
E
is not
w
-semistable for any polarization
w
on
C
, we call it a strongly unstable bundle.

The following result from
[
17
, Theorem-1, Steps-1,2]
will be useful in Theorems
3.8
and
4.1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
C
is a chain-like curve and
E
is a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
. Let
E_{i}
denote the restriction of
E
to the component
C_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
w
denote a polarization on
C
. Then

(i)

if
E
is
w
-semistable,
\chi
is the Euler characteristic of
E
and
\chi_{i}
the Euler characteristic of
E_{i}
for each
i
, then

(2.6)

(\sum_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+r(i-1)\leq\chi_{i}\leq(\sum_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+ri,

where
i
varies from
1
to
n-1
;

(ii)

if
E_{i}
is a semistable vector bundle for
i=1,\dots,n
and the Euler characteristics of
E
and
E_{i}
satisfy the inequalities (
2.6
), then
E
is
w
-semistable. Further, if one of the
E_{i}
is stable, then
E
is
w
-stable.

3.
The w-semistable kernel bundles

Let
C
be a chain-like curve defined in the previous section. Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on
C
and
E_{j}
denote the restriction of
E
to
C_{j}
. Then we have a natural morphism

\displaystyle\rho_{j}:H^{0}(E)\rightarrow H^{0}(E_{j})

defined by
\rho_{j}(s)=s|_{E_{j}}
for all
s\in H^{0}(E)
. We denote
\rho_{j}(V)
by
V_{j}
. The pair
(E_{j},V_{j})
is said to be
of type

(r,d_{j},k_{j})
, if
{\rm rk}(E_{j})=r
,
\deg(E_{j})=d_{j}
and
\dim(V_{j})=k_{j}
. Let
d=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}
and
\dim(V)=k
. We say that the pair
(E,V)
is
of type

(r,d,k)
.

.

(
d
) Here again we prove the result for

∎

Lemma 3.5

Let

be a chain-like curve. Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is a nonzero finite dimensional vector space over

and
\mathcal{M}
is a subbundle of the trivial bundle
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}
on
C
such that
\text{rk}(\mathcal{M})=m
,
\chi(\mathcal{M})<0
and the quotient
\mathcal{E}:=\frac{\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}}{\mathcal{M}}
is locally free. Then there exists a polarization
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
such that

(\sum_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+m(i-1)\leq\chi_{i}\leq(\sum_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+mi,

where
\chi:=\chi(\mathcal{M})
,
\chi_{j}:=\chi(\mathcal{M}_{|_{C_{j}}})
and

varies from
1
to

Proof.

The hypothesis that the cokernel

is locally free implies (by arguments similar to Lemma
3.1
(a)) that
\mathcal{M}_{|_{C_{j}}}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}
for each

. So

for each

. Also since

, we can conclude that for each

,

, and for
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
0<\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-m(i-1)}{\chi}<1
and
0<\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}<1
.

Now, we will first prove the existence of

such that

w_{1}\chi\leq\chi_{1}\leq w_{1}\chi+m,

and then by induction, prove the existence of the remaining

. So first we want

such that

(3.6)

\begin{cases}\chi_{1}\leq w_{1}\chi+m\\
\chi_{1}\geq w_{1}\chi.\end{cases}

The inequalities (
3.6
) are equivalent to

(3.7)

\chi_{1}-m\leq w_{1}\chi\leq\chi_{1}.

Multiplying the inequalities (
3.7
) by (

) and using the hypothesis that

, we get

(3.8)

\frac{\chi_{1}-m}{\chi}\geq w_{1}\geq\frac{\chi_{1}}{\chi}.

We know that the numbers
\frac{\chi_{1}-m}{\chi}
and
\frac{\chi_{1}}{\chi}
are between
0
and
1
. So it is always possible to choose

satisfying inequalities (
3.6
).

Now assume
i\geq 1
to be a positive integer strictly less than
n-1
. Suppose that
w_{i}
’s are chosen from
(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
,

\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}\geq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j}\geq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-m(i-1)}{\chi}

or equivalently

(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+m(i-1)\leq\chi_{i}\leq(\sum_{j=1}^{i}w_{j})\chi-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}\chi_{j}+mi.

We want to prove that there exists
w_{i+1}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
and

(3.9)

(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j})\chi-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}+mi\leq\chi_{i+1}\leq(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j})\chi-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}+m(i+1).

Again proceeding in the same way as we did for
w_{1}
, the inequality (
3.9
) is equivalent to

(3.10)

\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-m(i+1)}{\chi}\geq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j}\geq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}.

Since
\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-m(i+1)}{\chi}
and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j}\geq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}
are numbers between
0
and
1

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}w_{j}
is in

. Choose any

satisfying (

); it is enough to prove that

3.10
) are equivalent to

\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-m(i+1)}{\chi}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j}\geq w_{i+1}\geq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j}.

Since
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}w_{j}\leq\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}
, we have

\displaystyle w_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i+1}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}-\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i}\chi_{j}-mi}{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{i+1}}{\chi}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

Once we have chosen

4.
strongly unstable kernel bundles

In the previous section, we have seen that if the restriction of
M_{E,V}
to each component is semistable, there exists a polarization
w
such that
M_{E,V}
is
w
-semistable. In this section, we provide some sufficient conditions under which
M_{E,V}
becomes strongly unstable. By Corollary
3.9
, for
M_{E,V}
to be strongly unstable, there should exist at least one
j
such that
M_{E,V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}
is not semistable. Lemma
3.2
(d) says that this can be achieved if
V\cap H^{0}(E_{j}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j}))\neq 0
. So to obtain sufficient conditions for
M_{E,V}
to be strongly unstable, it is reasonable to assume the hypothesis that
V\cap H^{0}(E_{j}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j}))\neq 0
, or, more generally that
\text{Ker}(\rho_{j_{|_{V}}})\neq 0
(for at least one
j
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on a chain-like curve
C
. Then
M_{E,V}
is strongly unstable, if any of the following conditions hold:

(a)

(or

),

(resp.
E_{n}

(resp.
(k-r)<d_{n}
).

(b)

there exists a

such that

is semistable and

.

Proof.

Suppose
M_{E,V}
is
w
-semistable with respect to a polarization
w
.

(a) Given
(k-r)<d_{1}
. By Theorem
2.1
(i), the polarization
w
satisfies the inequalities (
2.6
). In particular, by using the facts that
\chi_{1}=-(k-r)((g_{1}-1)+d_{1})
,

and the inequality (

), we have

w_{1}\geq\frac{(k-r)(g_{1}-1)+d_{1}}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}.

On the other hand, since
\text{Ker}(\rho_{1_{|_{V}}})\neq 0

3.2

is a non-trivial subsheaf of
M_{E,V}
. So the fact that
M_{E,V}
is
w
-semistable will imply

(4.2)

\mu_{w}({\rm Ker}(\rho_{1_{|_{V}}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}(-p_{1}))\leq\mu_{w}(M_{E,V}).

Denoting
\text{dim}(\text{Ker})(\rho_{1_{|_{V}}})
by

, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{w}({\rm Ker}(\rho_{1_{|_{V}}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}(-p_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}(-p_{1}))}{w_{1}t_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-g_{1}}{w_{1}}.

Also

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{w}(M_{E,V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k-r)(1-p_{a}(C))-d}{(k-r)}.

Using equations (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) in the inequality (

From the inequalities (

(b) Given

\displaystyle w_{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(k-r)(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}g_{i}-1)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}d_{i}}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}w_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(k-r)(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}g_{i}-1)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}d_{i}}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}-\frac{(k-r)(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}g_{i})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}d_{i}}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k-r)(g_{j}-1)+d_{j}}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}.

Since
\text{Ker}(\rho_{j_{|_{V}}})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j})\subseteq M_{E,V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j})\subseteq M_{E,V}
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle w_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(k-r)(g_{j}+1)}{(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1)+d}.

Comparing both the above inequalities involving
w_{j}
, we get

2(k-r)-d_{j}\geq 0
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

(1)

Suppose
M_{E,V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}
is semistable for some
j
. Then by the proof of Lemma
3.2
(d),
V\cap H^{0}(E_{j}(-p_{j-1}-p_{j}))=0
. But this will not imply
\text{Ker}(\rho_{j_{|_{V}}})=0
if
n>2
.

(2)

Suppose
M_{E,V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}
is semistable for some
j
and not a trivial bundle on
C_{j}
. Also suppose
E_{j}
is semistable for such a
j
. Then we claim that
\text{Ker}(\rho_{j_{|_{V}}})=0
. For, if
\text{Ker}(\rho_{j_{|_{V}}})\neq 0
, from the commutative diagram in Lemma
3.2
(b), the semistability of

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on a chain-like curve
C
with
E_{j}
semistable for all
j
. Suppose there exists
l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
d_{l}>0
. Then
k<d+r
.

Proof.

To prove this, we consider several cases.

(1)

Suppose that
\mu(E_{j})>2g_{j}-2
for each
j
. Then
h^{1}(E_{j})=0
, since each
E_{j}
is semistable. By Lemma
3.1
(b), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}k\leqslant h^{0}(E)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h^{0}(E_{j})-(n-1)r\\
&=d+r(n-p_{a}(C))-(n-1)r\\
&<d+r,\end{split}

as
p_{a}(C)\geq 2
.

(2)

Suppose that
\mu(E_{j})\leqslant 2g_{j}-2
for each
j
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}k\leqslant h^{0}(E)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h^{0}(E_{j})-(n-1)r\\
&\leq\frac{d}{2}+nr-(n-1)r~~(\text{by Theorem \ref{theorem directly from the paper}}),\\
&<d+r,\end{split}

as
d_{j}\geq 0
for each
j
,
d_{l}>0
for some
l
and
d=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}
.

(3)

Suppose
A
is a non-empty proper subset of
\{j\in\mathbb{N}|1\leqslant j\leqslant n\}
with
\mu(E_{j})\leqslant 2g_{j}-2
. Then by Theorem
4.2
and Riemann-Roch Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}k\leqslant h^{0}(E)&=\sum_{j\in A}h^{0}(E_{j})+\sum_{t\notin A}h^{0}(E_{t})-(n-1)r\\
&\leqslant\sum_{j\in A}(d_{j}/2+r)+\sum_{t\notin A}(d_{t}+r(1-g_{t}))-(n-1)r\\
&=\sum_{j\in A}d_{j}/2+\sum_{t\notin A}d_{t}-\sum_{t\notin A}rg_{t}+r\\
&<\sum_{j\in A}d_{j}+\sum_{t\notin A}d_{t}+r=d+r,\end{split}

as
g_{t}\geqslant 2
.

In case (3), as
A
is an arbitrary non-empty proper subset of
\{j\in\mathbb{N}|1\leqslant j\leqslant n\}
such that
\mu(E_{j})\leqslant 2g_{j}-2
, it covers all the remaining cases.
Therefore,
k<d+r
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Suppose there exists
l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
V\cap H^{0}(E_{l}(-p_{l-1}-p_{l}))\neq 0
and
E_{l}
is semistable. Then by Lemma
3.2
(c),
d_{l}>0
.

Suppose
E
is a rank
r
vector bundle and
L
is a line bundle on
C
. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi(E\otimes L)&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chi(E_{j}\otimes L)-r(n-1).\\
\end{split}

By projection formula,
\chi(E_{j}\otimes L)=\chi(E_{j}\otimes L_{j})=\chi(E_{j})+r\text{deg}(L_{j})
, where
L_{j}:=L_{|_{C_{j}}}
. So

(4.7)

\chi(E\otimes L)=\chi(E)+r\text{deg}(L),

where by
\deg(L)
we mean
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\deg(L_{j})
. In particular, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\chi(M_{E,V}\otimes L)=(k-r)(1+\deg(L)-p_{a}(C))-d.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(E,V)
be a generated pair on a chain-like curve
C
with
\frac{d}{k-r}>(n-1)
. Suppose that
\text{Ker}({\rho_{j}}_{|_{V}})\neq 0
for each
j
. Then
M_{E,V}\otimes L
is strongly unstable, for any line bundle
L
on
C
. In particular,
M_{E,V}
is strongly unstable.

Proof.

Suppose that
M_{E,V}\otimes L
is
w
-semistable with respect to some polarisation
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
. Since
\text{Ker}({\rho_{j}}_{|_{V}})\neq 0
for each
j
, and
M_{E,V}\otimes L
is
w
-semistable, proceeding as in Theorem
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle(k-r)(g_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w_{1}[(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1-\text{deg}(L))+d]+(k-r)\text{deg}(L_{1})

\displaystyle(k-r)(g_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w_{n}[(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1-\text{deg}(L))+d]+(k-r)\text{deg}(L_{n})

\displaystyle(k-r)(g_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle w_{j}[(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1-\text{deg}(L))+d]+(k-r)(\text{deg}(L_{j})-1)

\displaystyle\text{for}~j\in\{2,\dots n-1\}.

Adding the above inequalities, we get

\displaystyle(k-r)p_{a}(C)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle[(k-r)(p_{a}(C)-1-\text{deg}(L)+\text{deg}(L)-(n-2)]+d,

which implies
0\geq-(n-1)(k-r)+d
. This contradicts the fact that
\frac{d}{(k-r)}>(n-1)
.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

On any nodal curve which is reduced and reducible, if
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle which is semistable with respect to a polarization
w
then it is not true in general that
\mathcal{F}\otimes L
is
w
-semistable, for any line bundle
L
on
C
. It is also not true in general that if
\mathcal{F}
is strongly unstable, then
\mathcal{F}\otimes L
is strongly unstable. For example, let
C
be a two component curve with a single node and let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
. By Lemma
3.5
and Theorem
3.8
, there exists a polarization
w
such that
\mathcal{O}_{C}
is
w
-semistable. Now suppose
L
is a line bundle on
C
which is obtained by gluing line bundles
L_{1}
on
C_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
C_{2}
such that
\text{deg}(L_{1})=0
and
\text{deg}(L_{2})=(g_{1}+g_{2})
, where
g_{1}\geq 2
is the genus of
C_{1}
and
g_{2}\geq 2
is the genus of
C_{2}
. Then
\chi(L)=1~\text{and}~\chi_{1}:=\chi(L_{1})<0
. We claim that
L
is strongly unstable. For, if
L
is
w
-semistable, then
\chi_{i}
’s and
w_{i}
’s have to satisfy the inequalities (
2.6
). In particular,
w_{1}
should be such that
\frac{\chi_{1}-1}{\chi}\leq w_{1}\leq\frac{\chi_{1}}{\chi}
. But
\frac{\chi_{1}}{\chi}=\chi_{1}<0
. This implies

, a contradiction. So even though
\mathcal{O}_{C}
is
w
-semistable with respect to a polarization
w
,
\mathcal{O}_{C}\otimes L=L
is strongly unstable. Similarly, if we tensor this particular

with its dual

, then we have
L
as strongly unstable but

as

-semistable.

Therefore, Theorem
4.6
becomes significant as it is saying that under suitable conditions, not only

, but also
M_{E,V}\otimes L
is strongly unstable for any line bundle
L
on a chain-like curve

.

Suppose

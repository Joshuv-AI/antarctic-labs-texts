Spectral Moment Formulae for G ​ L ​ ( 3 ) × G ​ L ​ ( 2 ) GL(3)\times GL(2) L L -functions II: The Eisenstein Case
Chung-Hang Kwan

Abstract
. This is a sequel to Kwan [ Kw23 ] . We continue our investigations on the spectral moments of L L -functions of G ​ L ​ ( 3 ) × G ​ L ​ ( 2 ) GL(3)\times GL(2) -type from the perspective of period integrals. By manipulating an equality between two different periods for the group G ​ L ​ ( 3 ) GL(3) , we establish an exact identity of Motohashi type connecting the shifted cubic moment of G ​ L ​ ( 2 ) GL(2) L L -functions to the shifted fourth moment of G ​ L ​ ( 1 ) GL(1) L L -functions. Furthermore, we are able to provide an intrinsic, automorphic explanation for the sources and symmetries of the full sets of main terms for both moments. They are shown to be in agreement with the CFKRS Moment Conjectures. This work presents a strategy for accomplishing the aforementioned tasks, distinct from the previous ones in the literature.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In the last two decades, the cubic moment of automorphic
L
-functions for the group
GL(2)
has found plenty of striking applications in number theory. These include the landmark result of Conrey-Iwaniec
[
CI00
]
and the vast recent extensions by Petrow-Young
[
PY20
,
PY23
]
culminating in the full Weyl-strength subconvexity for all primitive Dirichlet
L
-functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2},\chi\right)\ \ll_{\epsilon}\ q^{\frac{1}{6}+\epsilon}

(1.1)

for primitive characters
\chi\ (\bmod\ q)
. Readers should consult
[
Y17
,
PY19
,
BK17
,
AW23
,
Iv01
,
Iv02
]
for applications in other directions — just to name a few.

An unexpected structural feature the cubic moment of
GL(2)

L
-functions is that it is dual to a moment of a very different shape and is of significant interests in classical analytic number theory. This type of phenomena was first discovered by Motohashi
[
Mo93
,
Mo97
]
in which an exact identity of the shape

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ \left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{4}\ dt\ \ =\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f:\text{ level $1$ }\\
\text{cusp forms}\end{subarray}}\ L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{3}\ \ +\ \ (***)

(1.2)

was established, where the weight functions for the moments are suppressed and
(***)
represents certain intricate polar and continuous contributions.

To study the cubic moment, one must perform a
spectral inversion
to the identity (
1.2
). This was only obtained relatively recently by Petrow
[
Pe15
]
in the level aspect and Frolenkov
[
Fr20
]
in the weight aspect. While
[
Pe15
]
was built upon the foundation of
[
CI00
]
which starts with approximate functional equations followed by relative trace formulae of Petersson/Kuznetsov type, deducing a Motohashi-type identity turned out to require a decent amount of refinements and they are too elaborate to be described here. The relevant analytic machinery was developed in various ways and aspects by a number of authors, e.g.,
[
Bl12b
,
Li11
,
Y17
,
KPY19
]
.

1.2.
Moment Conjectures

A fundamental goal in any moment problem is to understand the
sources
and the
structures
for the full sets of main terms. Throughout these years, this quest has gained a good amount of interests in the literature and the outcomes have proven to be fruitful. Most notably, there are two sets of heuristics leading to very precise conjectures for the asymptotics of moments. Conrey et. al.
[
CFKRS05
]
developed their heuristics (or the ‘
Recipes
’) based on the notion of
shifted moments
and the use of approximate functional equations — their conjectures are nonetheless intrinsic. They were able to draw surprising connections to
Random Matrix Theory
which reveal many unexpected symmetries and combinatorics behind moments. On the other hand, Diaconu et. al.
[
DGH03
]
developed the framework of
Multiple Dirichlet Series
for moments of the
\zeta
-function and the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions. Their predictions for the asymptotic formulae are deduced from the (conjectural) analytic continuation, polar structures and groups of functional equations of certain carefully constructed multiple Dirichlet series.

Regarding the two moments to be examined in this article, i.e., the ones in (
1.2
), it was a delicate task to extract the full structures behind the main terms (which were suppressed as part of ‘
(***)
’ in (
1.2
)). For the fourth moment of the
\zeta
-function, Heath-Brown
[
HB79
]
and Motohashi
[
Mo93
]
proved the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\ \left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{4}\ dt\ \ =\ \ T\cdot P_{2}(\log T)\ \ +\ \ O(T^{1-\delta}).

(1.3)

for
\delta<1/8
and
\delta<1/3
respectively, where
P_{2}
is a polynomial of degree
4
. The shapes of
P_{2}
obtained in these works (see also Conrey
[
C96
]
) were unfortunately very complicated. It would be daunting to compute all of the coefficients of
P_{2}
using their formulae.

Motohashi
[
Mo93
]
considered a smooth and shifted variant of (
1.3
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ \eta(t)\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+\alpha_{1}\right)\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+\alpha_{2}\right)\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-it+\beta_{1}\right)\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-it+\beta_{2}\right)\ dt,

(1.4)

initially for
\re\alpha_{1},\ \re\alpha_{2},\ \re\beta_{1},\ \re\beta_{2}\gg 1
, where
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}[T,2T]
. This variant plays a pivotal role in the intricate analytic continuation argument of
[
Mo93
]
. Conrey et. al.
[
CFKRS05
]
takes one step further by realizing (
1.4
) actually leads to an elegant intrinsic form of the main terms which is far from obvious from the work
[
Mo93
]
. There are six main terms in the formulation of
[
CFKRS05
]
from which the polynomial
P_{2}
can be computed in full simply by letting the shifts
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\to 0
, see Section 5.1 of
[
CFKRS05
]
.

The ‘recipe’ of
[
CFKRS05
]
works equally well for the shifted cubic moment of
GL(2)

L
-functions. Because of the differences in the families and their underlying arithemetic, there will be eight different main terms this time. The authors of
[
CI00
]
were unable to obtain an asymptotic formula but had nevertheless expressed interests in accomplishing this task (pp. 1177 therein). We shall review these conjectures in Section
3
and examine the technical difficulties in identifying these terms in the previous approaches towards the end of Section
1.4
.

1.3.
Beyond the Petersson/ Kuznetsov paradigm: Recent Progress

The source of the
‘reciprocity’
in (
1.2
) is not entirely apparent from the original work of Motohashi
[
Mo93
]
. In particular, there is no resaon to expect the off-diagonal or the error terms should be nicely given by any moment of
L
-functions. Having a conceptual understanding of this phenomenon, without intermediate objects like Kloosterman sums, shifted convolution sums or the Kuznetsov formulae, is hence of interest to this field.

This task was perhaps first picked up by Bruggeman-Motohashi
[
BM05
]
. To construct the two distinct-looking moments in (
1.2
), their approach utilizes a conjunction of spectral projections, Hecke operators and a two-fold limiting process to an automorphic kernel. The choice of the automorphic kernel in
[
BM05
]
is by no means straight-forward. Indeed, delicate smoothing and regularization are essential, and one must handle various issues regarding convergence, regularity, and exchanges of limits carefully. In turn, these ingredients allow
[
BM05
]
deeper understanding of the integral transform through the theories of Whittaker-Kirillov models and Bessel functions for the group
PGL(2)
(see
[
CPS90
]
).

Michel-Venkatesh
[
MV06
]
(Section 4),
[
MV10
]
(Section 4.5), and Reznikov
[
R08
]
(pp. 453) took an extra step and asked for an even more intrinsic account than the one offered by
[
BM05
]
. They envisioned a proof of Motohashi’s formula in terms of a structural framework known as the
strong Gelfand formation
using a
‘regularized’
geodesic period for the product of two
GL(2)

Eisenstein series
formally looks like

\displaystyle\text{Reg}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\ E(iy,s_{1})E(iy,s_{2})y^{s_{3}-1/2}\ d^{\times}y.

(1.5)

However, one must overcome numerous technical obstructions if one wishes to implement their strategy. Indeed, the sources of the many main terms or degenerate terms are rather unclear from (
1.5
), as well as a number of integrals experience serious divergence issues due to non-compactness of domains and non-square-integrability of Eisenstein series. Moreover, it has become evident that one requires a more sophisticated regularization techniques than that developed in
[
MV10
]
. See also Blomer et. al.
[
BHKM20
]
for discussions on other intricacies related to this approach. Only very recently Nelson
[
Ne20+
]
managed to make the period integral strategy of Michel-Venkatesh and Reznikov completely rigorous with very impressive techniques.

Alternatively, Motohashi’s formula can be interpreted under another framework due to Wu
[
Wu22
]
. Wu’s work is a generalization of
[
BM05
]
based on two different (spectral and geometric) decompositions of a co-invariant tempered distribution on the Schwartz space of
2
-by-
2
matrices. The two sides can then be recasted in terms of Godement-Jacquet’s
L
-functions which in turn lead to the moments occured in (
1.2
). Further explications and analytic subtlties are addressed in the follow-up work Balkanova-Frolenkov-Wu
[
BFW21+
]
using many heavy inputs from the theory of special functions. The authors of
[
BFW21+
]
also discussed the obstacles in obtaining an exact spectral inversion of Motohashi’s result.

Overall speaking, the presence of these significant challenges in the aforementioned approaches renders (
1.2
) a particularly intriguing instance of
‘Spectral Reciprocity’
and serves as a good testing ground for our period-based approach (see below). Readers are invited to consult the references of this subsection for more detailed technical discussions.

3.1.

We now describe their set-up in the particular case of our interest, i.e., the

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\ H(\mu_{j})\ \frac{\Lambda(s-\alpha_{1},\phi_{j})\Lambda(s-\alpha_{2},\phi_{j})\Lambda(s-\alpha_{3},\phi_{j})}{\langle\phi_{j},\phi_{j}\rangle}

\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{3}\right),\hskip 15.0pt\left(-\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{3}\right),\hskip 15.0pt\left(-\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right).

\displaystyle\zeta(1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\zeta(1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3})\zeta(1-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3}),

\displaystyle\zeta(1-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\zeta(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3})\zeta(1+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3}),

\displaystyle\zeta(1-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\zeta(1-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3})\zeta(1-\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}).

3.2.

To state the Moment Conjecture for the Riemann

]

.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ \eta\left(t\right)\

BPS Correlators for AdS 3 / CFT 2 \text{AdS}_{3}/\text{CFT}_{2}
Matthias R. Gaberdiel and, Beat Nairz

Abstract
The BPS correlators of the symmetric product orbifold Sym N ​ ( 𝕋 4 ) \text{Sym}_{N}(\mathbb{T}^{4}) are reproduced from the dual worldsheet theory describing strings on AdS 3 × S 3 × 𝕋 4 \text{AdS}_{3}\times{\rm S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4} with minimal ( k = 1 k=1 ) NS-NS flux. More specifically, we show that the worldsheet duals of the symmetric orbifold BPS states can be identified with their lift to the covering surface, thereby making the matching of the correlators essentially manifest. We also argue that the argument can be generalised to arbitrary descendants, using suitable DDF operators on the worldsheet.

Introduction

The
\text{AdS}_{3}/\text{CFT}_{2}
correspondence is a useful playground to study aspects of the AdS/CFT correspondence explicitly. In particular, string theory on
\text{AdS}_{3}\times{\rm S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
with minimal
k=1
units of pure NS-NS flux is exactly dual to the free symmetric product orbifold
\text{Sym}_{N}(\mathbb{T}^{4})
in the large
N
limit
Eberhardt:2018ouy
;
Eberhardt:2019ywk
;
Dei:2020zui
, and this has allowed for detailed precision tests. For example, the correlators of the symmetric orbifold can be calculated in terms of holomorphic covering maps
Lunin:2000yv
;
Lunin:2001pw
, and this is reproduced from the worldsheet perspective where the worldsheet correlators localise the integral over the worldsheet moduli to those configurations for which a holomorphic covering map exists
Eberhardt:2019ywk
;
Dei:2020zui
, see also
Eberhardt:2020akk
;
Knighton:2020kuh
for the analysis at higher worldsheet genus. Thus, the string worldsheet indeed plays the role of the covering surface in the orbifold theory, as originally suggested in
Lunin:2000yv
;
Lunin:2001pw
;
Pakman:2009zz
;
Pakman:2009ab
.

So far, this worldsheet analysis has only been performed for the vertex operators that are dual to the twisted sector ground states of the symmetric orbifold theory,
1
1

1

In addition, some simple correlators of the
{\cal N}=4
superconformal fields were analysed in
Gaberdiel:2021njm
.
and it would be important to generalise it to arbitrary single particle states of the symmetric orbifold theory. Here, we start to tackle this problem by looking at another special class of orbifold correlators: the correlators of BPS states

Dei:2020zui

Lunin:2001pw

\phi

;
Naderi

The Symmetric Orbifold

In this section we briefly review the symmetric product orbifold following
Lunin:2000yv
;
Lunin:2001pw
. In particular, we explain their method of calculating path-integral correlators of twisted sector fields using branched covering maps. We also identify the BPS states of the symmetric orbifold theory. In the following we shall not attempt to give a comprehensive review, but rather introduce what will be needed below; more details about symmetric orbifolds can e.g. be found in
Lunin:2000yv
;
Lunin:2001pw
;
Pakman:2009zz

Roumpedakis:2018tdb

2.1
The Setup

Given a seed theory with target manifold
M
, the symmetric product orbifold theory is defined as

\mathrm{Sym}_{N}(M)=M^{N}/S_{N}\ .

(2.1)

As the action of
S_{N}
is not free, this target space is not a smooth manifold. The identification of the different copies via
S_{N}
results in ‘twisted’ sectors for which the fields satisfy the twisted boundary conditions,

v^{(k)}(e^{2\pi i}x+x_{0})=v^{(\varrho(k))}(x_{0})\ ,\quad\varrho\in S_{N}\ ,

(2.2)

where
e^{2\pi i}x
is a short-hand for going around a small circle centered at
x=0
, and
v^{(k)}
denotes the field
v
in the
k
’th copy of the product theory.
2
2

2

Due to the identification of the copies, these boundary conditions are associated to equivalence classes of permutations (rather than individual permutations).
We may think of these twisted boundary conditions as being the consequence of a ‘twist field’ at
x_{0}
. The analogue of the ‘single-trace’ operators of SYM are the twist fields that are associated to permutations
\varrho
consisting of a single cycle, and we shall only consider them here (since they corresponds to the single string states of the perturbative worldsheet theory). We shall denote the
w
-cycle twist operator of smallest conformal dimension by
\sigma_{w}
.

In a path-integral formulation of the theory, the boundary conditions on the fields can be elegantly implemented using a branched covering map. A branched covering map is a holomorphic map

\Gamma:\ \Sigma\longrightarrow S^{2}\ ,

(2.3)

where
\Sigma
is another Riemann surface. If twist operators of cycle length
w_{i}
are inserted at
x_{i}
, then
\Gamma
should have ramification index
w_{i}
at one point
z_{i}
in the pre-image of
x_{i}
, i.e. it should be of the form

\Gamma(z)=x_{i}+a_{i}\,(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\bigl((z-z_{i})^{w_{i}+1}\bigr)\ ,\quad z\to z_{i}\ .

(2.4)

Such a map then lifts the
v^{(k)}
satisfying the twisted boundary conditions to a single-valued field
\mathcal{V}:\,\Sigma\longrightarrow M
. More precisely, around each point
x
that is not a twist insertion and for each
k
, there is a neighbourhood of
x
on which
v^{(k)}
is single valued. Then, there exists a
z_{k}
with
\Gamma(z_{k})=x
and a neighbourhood of
z_{k}
such that
\Gamma
is a bi-holomorphism between the two neighbourhoods, where

v^{(k)}(\Gamma(z))\ \longleftrightarrow\ (\Gamma^{\prime}(z))^{-h}\,\mathcal{V}(z)\ ,

(2.5)

with
h
the conformal dimension of
v
. Here we have written “
\leftrightarrow
” instead of “
=
” because the operators technically act on different Hilbert spaces; the arrow is meant to indicate that the application of an operator on the left in the twisted sector corresponds to the application of the one on the right in the Hilbert space of the theory on the covering surface. The situation is sketched in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
A sketch of the branched covering approach in orbifold correlator calculations. The fields in the twisted sector are lifted by
\Gamma
to a single field on a covering surface
\Sigma
. Note that the genus of the covering surface can be higher than that of the original surface.

The
w
-cycle twisted sector is generated from the ground state
\sigma_{w}(0)
by the action of fractional modes that can be defined as follows. Let us consider the superpositions

\mathbf{v}^{j}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{w}\omega^{j\,(k-1)}\,v^{(k)}(x)\ ,\quad j=0,\dots,w-1\ ,

(2.6)

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/w}
is a primitive
w
-th root of unity. These fields satisfy the boundary conditions

\mathbf{v}^{j}(e^{2\pi i}\,x)=\omega^{-j}\,\mathbf{v}^{j}(x)\ ,

(2.7)

and thus have a mode expansion

\mathbf{v}^{j}(x)=\sum_{r\in w\mathbb{Z}+j-h\,w}v_{r/w}\,x^{-r/w-h}\ ,

(2.8)

since all
v^{(k)}
have conformal dimension
h
, and hence so does
\mathbf{v}^{j}
.
As
j
ranges from
0
to
w-1
, the fractional modes
v_{r/w}
have a spacing
r\in\mathbb{Z}-h\,w
.

2.2
Orbifold Correlators

If
\ket{\phi}
is a state in the
w
-twisted sector, the vertex operator associated to
v_{-r/w}\,\ket{\phi}

equals

V(v_{-r/w}\,\ket{\phi},x_{0})=\underset{C(x_{0})}{\oint}\frac{dx}{2\pi i}\,\,\mathbf{v}^{j}(x)\,(x-x_{0})^{-r/w+h-1}\,V(\ket{\phi},x_{0})

(2.9)

in the orbifold, where
C(x_{0})
denotes a small contour around
x_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}^{j}
is the field containing the mode
v_{-r/w}
in its expansion, i.e.
r\in w\mathbb{Z}+j-h\,w
.
If
\Gamma(z)
is a covering map corresponding to a correlator with the insertion of
V(v_{-r/w}\,\ket{\phi},x_{0})
, we can change coordinates using the identification (
2.5
) to lift the vertex operator to the covering surface
\Sigma
. Under this transformation, the mode
v_{-r/w}
corresponds to an insertion of

\underset{C(z_{0})}{\oint}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\,\mathcal{V}(z)\,(\Gamma(z)-x_{0})^{-r/w+h-1}\,(\partial\Gamma)^{1-h}

(2.10)

on the covering surface.
For example, for
z_{0}=x_{0}=0
and
\Gamma(z)=a\,z^{w}
, we simply obtain

v_{-r/w}\ \longleftrightarrow\ w^{1-h}\,a^{-r/w}\,\mathcal{V}_{-r}\ .

(2.11)

The factor of
w
is inconsequential, while the factor of
a
is important, as it contains information about the other insertions inside the correlator.
3
3

3

Note that the covering map depends on all the fields present in the correlator.
We should stress that the mode on the covering surface
\Sigma
, eq. (
2.10
), in general also depends on subleading terms in an expansion of
\Gamma
around
z_{0}
. As we shall see in Section
2.3
below, this problem is absent for BPS states, and as a consequence the lifting of BPS states to the covering surface is particularly simple.

2.3
The BPS States

The symmetric orbifold theory
\text{Sym}_{N}(\mathbb{T}^{4})
has an
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal symmetry, and hence the BPS condition is

h=j\ ,

(2.12)

where
h
is the dimension and
j
the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
spin of a state. In this section we shall describe the different BPS states of the symmetric orbifold, as well as their lift to the covering surface; our exposition will follow closely
Lunin:2001pw
.

2.3.1
The case when
w
is odd

Let us first consider the case of odd
w
. Then the twisted vacuum has conformal dimension

h_{w}=\frac{w^{2}-1}{4w}\ ,

(2.13)

and
\mathfrak{su}(2)
spin
j=0
. The lift of the twisted sector ground state
\sigma_{w}(x_{0})
to the covering surface is the vacuum
\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}
, up to normalisation.

Let us denote the
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
currents of the seed theory by
J^{k,a}_{x}(x)
, where the subscript
x
distinguishes these currents from their lift to the covering surface
J^{a}_{z}(z)
. One can form the fractional modes as before, see eq. (
2.8
),

J^{a}_{x,m/w}:=\underset{C(0)}{\oint}\frac{dx}{2\pi i}\,\sum_{k=1}^{w}J^{k,a}_{x}(x)\,e^{2\pi\,i\,m(k-1)/w}\,x^{m/w}\ ,

(2.14)

as the integrand is single valued, and these modes satisfy an
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{w}
algebra

\displaystyle[J^{3}_{x,m/w},J^{3}_{x,n/w}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,w\,\frac{m}{w}\,\delta_{m+n,0}\ ,

(2.15a)

\displaystyle[J^{3}_{x,m/w},J^{\pm}_{x,n/w}]

\displaystyle=\pm J^{\pm}_{x,(m+n)/w}\ ,

(2.15b)

\displaystyle[J^{+}_{x,m/w},J^{-}_{x,n/w}]

\displaystyle=w\,\frac{m}{w}\,\delta_{m+n,0}-2\,J^{3}_{x,(m+n)/w}\ .

(2.15c)

The commutator with the dilation operator is

[L_{0},J^{a}_{x,-m/w}]=\frac{m}{w}\,J^{a}_{x,-m/w}\ .

(2.16)

Hence the fractional mode
J^{+}_{x,-m/w}
raises the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
charge by
1
, while only increasing the dimension by
m/w
; thus as long as
m/w<1
, the application of
J^{+}_{x,-m/w}
brings a state ‘closer to the BPS-bound’. We should mention that while the modes of
J^{a}_{x}
form an
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{w}
algebra, their lifts
J^{a}_{z}
give rise to an
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
algebra on the covering surface — this is how it should be since
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
is the symmetry of the seed theory.

Using the fractional modes, two chiral BPS states can be found in the
w
-twisted sector, namely
4
4

4

Here, ‘chiral’ means that
h=j=m
, where
m
is the
J^{3}_{x,0}
eigenvalue. The chiral BPS states are therefore
\mathfrak{su}(2)
highest weight states.

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}_{w}

\displaystyle=J^{+}_{x,-w/w}\cdots J^{+}_{x,-3/w}J^{+}_{x,-1/w}\,\sigma_{w}(0)\ ,

(2.17a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{-}_{w}

\displaystyle=J^{+}_{x,-(w-2)/w}\cdots J^{+}_{x,-3/w}J^{+}_{x,-1/w}\,\sigma_{w}(0)\ ,

(2.17b)

with
h=j=(w\pm 1)/2
, respectively.

For the calculation in
Lunin:2001pw
, as well as for the comparison with the worldsheet theory, we need the lift of these states to the covering surface. As explained in eq. (
2.10
) above, a state
V(J^{a}_{x,-n/w}\,\ket{\phi},x_{0})
in the orbifold corresponds to a state

\underset{C(z_{0})}{\oint}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\,J^{a}_{z}(z)\,\bigl(\Gamma(z)-x_{0}\bigr)^{-n/w}\,V(\ket{\Phi},z_{0})

(2.18)

on the covering surface, where
V(\ket{\Phi},z_{0})
is the lift of
V(\ket{\phi},x_{0})
. As a warm-up, consider first the state
J^{+}_{x,-1/w}\,\sigma_{w}(0)
inserted at
x_{0}
. Near
z=z_{0}
the covering map has the expansion

\Gamma(z)=x_{0}+a\,(z-z_{0})^{w}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}b_{m}\,(z-z_{0})^{w+m}\ ,

(2.19)

and thus

(\Gamma(z)-x_{0})^{-1/w}=\frac{a^{-\frac{1}{w}}}{z-z_{0}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}\,(z-z_{0})^{m}\ .

(2.20)

The state on the covering surface is hence

\underset{C(z_{0})}{\oint}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\,(a^{-\frac{1}{w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-1}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}\,J^{+}_{z,m})\,V(\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}},z_{0})=a^{-\frac{1}{w}}\,V(J^{+}_{z,-1},z_{0})=a^{-\frac{1}{w}}\,J^{+}_{z}(z_{0})\ ,

(2.21)

as the
J^{+}_{z,m}
modes do not contribute because they annihilate the vacuum. Similarly, if we consider the state
J^{+}_{x,-3/w}J^{+}_{x,-1/w}\,\sigma_{w}(0)
, the function
(\Gamma(z)-x_{0})^{-3/w}
will have a third order pole. Then the contour integral picks out
a^{-3/w}\,J^{+}_{z,-3}
, but in general there are also lower order contributions. For the BPS states, however, the subleading terms vanish because they are null. In particular,
J^{+}_{z,-1}\,J^{+}_{z,-1}\,\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}=0
is null at level
k=1
, as is its
L_{-1}
descendant, which is proportional to
J^{+}_{z,-2}\,J^{+}_{z,-1}\,\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}
.
5
5

5

Alternatively, this follows directly from the free field realisation in terms of free fermions.
Thus the lift of the state
J^{+}_{x,-3/w}J^{+}_{x,-1/w}\,\sigma_{w}(0)
is simply

a^{-\frac{4}{w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-3}\,J^{+}_{z,-1}\,\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}\ ,

(2.22)

inserted at
z_{0}
. We can iterate this argument for the chiral BPS states, and in each step only the leading pole contributes; thus we have the simple dictionary

J^{+}_{x,-m/w}\ \longleftrightarrow\ a^{-\frac{m}{w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-m}\ ,

(2.23)

where
a
is the leading coefficient of
\Gamma
from eq. (
2.19
).

Disregarding the normalisation of the vacuum for now, the BPS states in the
w
-cycle twisted sector (
2.17
) thus correspond to

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{(w+1)^{2}}{4w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-w}\cdots J^{+}_{z,-3}J^{+}_{z,-1}\,

\displaystyle\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}

(2.24a)

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{(w-1)^{2}}{4w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-(w-2)}\cdots J^{+}_{z,-3}J^{+}_{z,-1}\,

\displaystyle\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}\

(2.24b)

on the covering surface. We stress again that this simple lift, which only depends on the leading coefficient
a
of
\Gamma
, is due to the special form of the BPS states; for general states also the subleading terms of
\Gamma
in eq. (
2.20
) will play a role.

Due to the
J^{3}_{x,0}
conservation, correlators of chiral BPS states are zero. We will therefore also need to consider
\mathfrak{su}(2)
descendants of these states, i.e. states of the form

\big(J^{-}_{x,0}\big)^{k}\,\sigma^{\pm}_{w}(0)\ .

(2.25)

Using (
2.18
), one sees that on the covering surface, the application of
J^{-}_{x,0}
corresponds to the application of
J^{-}_{z,0}
. Thus the corresponding states on the covering surface are
\mathfrak{su}(2)
descendants of the states in eq. (
2.24
). In particular, their
a
-dependence is the same.

2.3.2
The case when
w
is even

For even
w
the situation is very similar. The main difference is that the twisted sector ground states now form a doublet (
j=\frac{1}{2}
) under
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, and their conformal dimension equals

h_{w}=\frac{w}{4}\ .

(2.26)

Due to the parity of
w
, the states on the covering surface will be in the Ramond sector, see
Lunin:2001pw

\sigma_{w}
the
m=-\frac{1}{2}

w
-cycle twisted sector are of the form

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}_{w}

\displaystyle=J^{+}_{x,-w/w}\cdots J^{+}_{x,-2/w}J^{+}_{x,0}\,\sigma_{w}(0)\ ,

(2.27a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{-}_{w}

\displaystyle=J^{+}_{x,-(w-2)/w}\cdots J^{+}_{x,-2/w}J^{+}_{x,0}\,\sigma_{w}(0)\ .

(2.27b)

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{w(w+2)}{4w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-w}\cdots J^{+}_{z,-2}J^{+}_{z,0}\,

(2.28a)

\displaystyle a^{-\frac{w(w-2)}{4w}}\,J^{+}_{z,-(w-2)}\cdots J^{+}_{z,-2}J^{+}_{z,0}\,

3
The Worldsheet Theory

In this section we shall review the worldsheet description of strings on
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}\times{\rm S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
with
k=1
units of NS-NS flux; this is the worldsheet theory that is exactly dual to the symmetric product orbifold of
\mathbb{T}^{4}
. In the hybrid formalism of
Berkovits:1999im
the background can be described by a WZW model based on the symmetry algebra
\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
. We shall first discuss the free field realisation
Dei:2020zui
;
Eberhardt:2019ywk
of the theory and its highest weight representations. Then we shall explain how the other representations of the worldsheet theory are obtained from the Ramond sector highest weight representation by spectral flow. Finally, we shall define the relevant worldsheet correlators and explain that they localise to those points in the moduli space where branched coverings exist
Dei:2020zui
, making the duality with the orbifold manifest. Again, our aim is not to be comprehensive — more details can be found, in particular, in
Dei:2020zui
;
Eberhardt:2019ywk
— but rather to fix our notation and introduce what will be needed below.

3.1
The Free Field Algebra

The worldsheet
\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
algebra can be realised using free fields, specifically two complex symplectic bosons denoted by
\xi^{\pm}
,
\eta^{\pm}
, and two complex fermions,
\psi^{\pm}
,
\chi^{\pm}
with (anti)-commutation relations
6
6

6

All of these fields have conformal weight
h=\frac{1}{2}
.

[\xi^{\alpha}_{r},\eta^{\beta}_{s}]=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\delta_{r+s,0}\,,\quad\{\psi^{\alpha}_{r},\chi^{\beta}_{s}\}=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\delta_{r+s,0}\ ,

(3.29)

where
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}
and
\epsilon^{+-}=1
. These fields generate a
\mathfrak{u}(1,1|2)_{1}
algebra, see
Eberhardt:2018ouy
for more details.

For the purpose of calculating the BPS correlators, the bosonic
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
subalgebra, spanned by the
K^{a}_{m}
modes, is of some importance. In terms of the free fermions, they are given as

\displaystyle K^{3}_{m}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\chi^{-}\psi^{+}+\chi^{+}\psi^{-})_{m}\ ,

(3.30a)

\displaystyle K^{\pm}_{m}

\displaystyle=\pm\,(\chi^{\pm}\psi^{\pm})_{m}\ ,

(3.30b)

with commutation relations

\displaystyle[K^{3}_{m},K^{\pm}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\pm K^{\pm}_{m+n}\ ,

(3.31a)

\displaystyle[K^{3}_{m},K^{3}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\delta_{m+n,0}\ ,

(3.31b)

\displaystyle[K^{+}_{m},K^{-}_{n}]

\displaystyle=m\,\delta_{m+n,0}+2\,K^{3}_{m+n}\ .

(3.31c)

3.2
The Highest Weight Representations

The
\mathfrak{u}(1,1|2)_{1}
algebra has only two highest weight representations: the NS vacuum representation, in which all free fields are half-integer moded, and that is generated from a single state
\ket{0}
, satisfying

\xi^{\pm}_{r}\,\ket{0}=\eta^{\pm}_{r}\,\ket{0}=\psi^{\pm}_{r}\,\ket{0}=\chi^{\pm}_{r}\,\ket{0}=0\ ,\qquad r>0\ ,

(3.32)

by the action of the negative modes. The other representation is the Ramond sector representation, in which all modes are integer moded. On the ground states the zero modes of the symplectic bosons may be taken to act as
Dei:2020zui

\displaystyle\xi_{0}^{+}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=\ket{m_{1},m_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}}\ ,

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{+}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\,m_{1}\,\ket{m_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2},m_{2}}\ ,

(3.33a)

\displaystyle\xi_{0}^{-}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=-\ket{m_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2},m_{2}}\ ,

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{-}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=-2\,m_{2}\,\ket{m_{1},m_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}}\ .

(3.33b)

Then we have

J_{0}^{3}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}=(m_{1}+m_{2})\,\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\ ,

(3.34)

while the Casimir of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
is given by
C^{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})}=-j(j-1)
with

j=m_{1}-m_{2}
.

Including the fermions and choosing
\chi_{0}^{+}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}=\psi_{0}^{+}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}=0
, one finds for each
\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}
a
\mathfrak{su}(2)
doublet spanned by

\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\ ,\quad\chi_{0}^{-}\psi_{0}^{-}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\ ,

(3.35)

and two singlets, spanned by

\chi_{0}^{-}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\ ,\quad\psi_{0}^{-}\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\ ,

(3.36)

respectively. The
Z_{0}=0
condition, reducing
\mathfrak{u}(1,1|2)_{1}
to
\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
, see
Dei:2020zui
, then forces
j=1/2
for the doublet and
j=0
(or
j=1
) for the singlets. Explicitly, the doublets are described by

\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\otimes\ket{\uparrow}_{\mathrm{R}}\ ,\quad\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}\otimes\ket{\downarrow}_{\mathrm{R}}\ ,

(3.37)

with
m_{1}-m_{2}=1/2
. These doublets will play an important role below.

3.3
Spectral Flow

An essential ingredient of the worldsheet theory is that it does not only contain highest weight representations, but also representations that are obtained from them by spectral flow
Maldacena:2000hw
, see also
Henningson:1991jc
. These spectrally flowed representations are in general not highest weight. In terms of the free fields there are two kinds of spectral flow,

\displaystyle\sigma^{(+)}(\eta^{+}_{r})

\displaystyle=\eta^{+}_{r-1/2}\ ,

\displaystyle\sigma^{(-)}(\xi^{+}_{r})

\displaystyle=\xi^{+}_{r-1/2}\ ,

(3.38a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(+)}(\xi^{-}_{r})

\displaystyle=\xi^{-}_{r+1/2}\ ,

\displaystyle\sigma^{(-)}(\eta^{-}_{r})

\displaystyle=\eta^{-}_{r+1/2}\ ,

(3.38b)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(+)}(\chi^{+}_{r})

\displaystyle=\chi^{+}_{r+1/2}\ ,

\displaystyle\sigma^{(-)}(\psi^{+}_{r})

\displaystyle=\psi^{+}_{r+1/2}\ ,

(3.38c)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(+)}(\psi^{-}_{r})

\displaystyle\sigma^{(-)}(\chi^{-}_{r})

\displaystyle=\chi^{-}_{r-1/2}\ ,

\displaystyle=J^{3}_{m}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,w\,\delta_{m,0}\ ,

\displaystyle\sigma^{w}(J^{\pm}_{m})

(3.39b)

\displaystyle\sigma^{w}(K^{3}_{m})

\displaystyle=K^{3}_{m}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,w\,\delta_{m,0}\ ,

\displaystyle\sigma^{w}(K^{\pm}_{m})

\displaystyle=K^{\pm}_{m\pm w}\ ,

(3.39d)

Spectral flow defines an automorphism of

(3.41)

3.6
Bosonic Correlators and the Incidence Relation

The
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
subalgebra of

(3.48)

In

;

only the vertex operators corresponding to the twisted sector ground states were considered, and they were denoted by

It was shown in

\left\langle\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\right\rangle\ ,

is characterised by its degree and the property that

\Gamma(z)=x_{i}+a_{i}^{\Gamma}\,(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\bigl((z-z_{i})^{w_{i}+1}\bigr)\,,\quad z\to z_{i}\ ,

for all

and

, there are only finitely many choices of

for which such a map exists — in particular, this implies that the worldsheet correlator localises. This localisation property could be derived from the identity (which in turn was deduced from the OPEs of
\xi^{\pm}
with the

\left\langle\Bigl(\xi^{-}(z)+\Gamma(z)\,\xi^{+}(z)\Bigr)\,\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\right\rangle=0\ ,

(3.52)

provided that a branched covering exists. If no branched covering exists, the correlator is zero. As a consequence, the correlators take the form

\left\langle\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\right\rangle\ =\sum_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma}(z_{i},u_{\alpha},j_{i})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}\,(a_{i}^{\Gamma})^{-h_{i}}\,\prod_{i=4}^{n}\delta(x_{i}-\Gamma(z_{i}))\ ,

(3.53)

where

is the
\mathrm{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
spin,

is the worldsheet conformal dimension, and
a_{i}^{\Gamma}
denotes the coefficients in (
3.51
).

While the incidence relation (
3.52
) allows one to deduce this localisation property, it does not quite fix the complete

dependence of (
3.53

appears is much more difficult, and only the case of three- and four-point correlators has so far been worked out in detail

;

a_{i}
dependence of the correlator was found to equal

a_{i}^{-h_{i}+(w_{i}-1)/4}\ .

(3.54)

Furthermore, there are additional factors depending on the residues of the poles of
\Gamma
that guarantee that the

dependence of the correlator is correct.
8
8

8

For example, for a
3
-point function this is fixed by the conformal symmetry.
We will ignore these pole coefficients and only consider the structure in the

Eberhardt:2020akk

3.7
The Worldsheet BPS States

We close this section by identifying the worldsheet states that are dual to the BPS states of Section

In the worldsheet description, the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
algebra generated by

[\Phi^{+}]^{\sigma^{w}}=\left[\ket{1/2,0}\otimes\ket{\uparrow}\right]^{\sigma^{w}}\,,\qquad[\Phi^{-}]^{\sigma^{w}}=\left[\ket{0,-1/2}\otimes\ket{\downarrow}\right]^{\sigma^{w}}\ ,

(3.55)

where we have used the notation from eq. (
3.37

Obviously, the same is true for any

For the following it will be convenient to undo the spectral flow for the fermions. Let us first concentrate on the case where

(3.57)

and similarly

\psi^{+}_{-s}\,[\Phi^{-}]^{\sigma^{w}}=\chi^{+}_{-s}\,[\Phi^{-}]^{\sigma^{w}}=0\ ,\quad s\in\{1/2,\dots,(w-2)/2\}\ .

(3.58)

This characterises the fermionic part of the state completely, and we can rewrite eq. (

\displaystyle[\Phi^{+}]^{\sigma^{w}}

\displaystyle=[\ket{1/2,0}]^{\sigma^{w}}\otimes\bigl(K^{+}_{-w}\cdots K^{+}_{-1}\,\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}\bigr)\ ,

\displaystyle[\Phi^{-}]^{\sigma^{w}}

\displaystyle=[\ket{0,-1/2}]^{\sigma^{w}}\otimes\bigl(K^{+}_{-(w-2)}\cdots K^{+}_{-1}\,\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}\bigr)\ ,

(3.59b)

where the spectral flow now only acts on the

Comparing this to the orbifold state on the covering space, see eq. (

\displaystyle[\Phi^{-}]^{\sigma^{w}}

\displaystyle=[\ket{0,-1/2}]^{\sigma^{w}}\otimes\bigl(K^{+}_{-(w-2)}\cdots K^{+}_{-2}\,K^{+}_{0}\,\ket{\downarrow}_{\mathrm{R}}\bigr)\ .

(3.60b)

Again, this has the same

We should stress that in either case the

This provides a concrete realisation of the idea that the worldsheet states can be identified with the lift of the orbifold states — this must in a sense be true if the worldsheet plays indeed the role of the covering surface, as originally suggested in

4
The BPS Correlators

In this section we shall explain that the correlators of the BPS states in the symmetric orbifold theory agree with those calculated from a worldsheet perspective. The key observation is that the lift of a BPS state of the symmetric orbifold to the covering surface agrees, as regards its
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
descendant structure, with the corresponding worldsheet dual state, compare eqs. (
2.24
) and (
3.59
), or eqs. (
2.28
) and (
3.60
). As we shall explain, this is sufficient to deduce that their correlators must agree up to an overall factor.
9
9

9

This factor is the contribution which is not fixed by the incidence relation (
3.52
), and is thus dependent on the cycle length of the twist operators and their insertion points.
We shall also exemplify this finding by an explicit calculation, see Appendix
A
.

4.1
The symmetric orbifold calculation

Let us first review the calculation of the BPS correlators for the symmetric orbifold. For a given choice of
(x_{i},w_{i})
, there exist only finitely many branched covering maps, i.e. holomorphic maps that behave as

\Gamma(z)=x_{i}+a_{i}\,(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}\bigl((z-z_{i})^{w_{i}+1}\bigr)\ ,\quad z\to z_{i}\

(4.61)

near
z=z_{i}
, and are only ramified at these points, i.e.
\partial\Gamma(z)=0
only at
z=z_{i}
. As we explained in Section
2.3
, the lift of a BPS state to the covering surface only depends on the leading behaviour of a branched covering
\Gamma
near the ramification points, i.e. on the coefficient
a_{i}
in (
4.61
).
The BPS correlator on the covering surface
\Sigma
then involves
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
descendants of the highest weight states in eqs. (
2.24
) and (
2.28
). Keeping track of the various factors of
a_{i}
, and remembering that there is a contribution

a_{i}^{-(w^{2}_{i}-1)/(4w_{i})+\frac{w_{i}-1}{4}}

(4.62)

due to the conformal anomaly
Lunin:2000yv
, the total exponent of
a_{i}
becomes, for
w_{i}
odd,

-\frac{(w_{i}\pm 1)^{2}}{4w_{i}}-\frac{w^{2}_{i}-1}{4w_{i}}+\frac{w_{i}-1}{4}=-\frac{w_{i}\pm 1}{2}+\frac{w_{i}-1}{4}\ ,

(4.63)

where the sign depends on which of the two BPS states in the
w
-cycle twisted sector is being considered, see eq. (
2.24
). Finally, we need to sum over all the possible branched covering maps. Note that there can be contributions from disconnected covering surfaces if the twist fields permute independent copies
Dei:2019iym
. In this case, the correlator factors into the connected correlators, and it is thus sufficient to study the connected contributions.

For even
w_{i}
, there is an additional contribution due to the insertion of
\ket{\downarrow}_{\mathrm{R}}
. The Ramond vacua are created from the Neveu-Schwarz vacuum by the spin operators
\mathcal{S}^{\updownarrow}
, which have conformal dimension
1/4=c/24
. Therefore, an insertion of
\mathcal{S}^{\downarrow}
comes with a factor of
a_{i}^{-1/(4\,w_{i})}
on the covering surface, and the analogue of eq. (
4.63
) for
w_{i}
even is

-\frac{w_{i}(w_{i}\pm 2)}{4w_{i}}-\frac{1}{4\,w_{i}}-\frac{w^{2}_{i}-1}{4w_{i}}+\frac{w_{i}-1}{4}=-\frac{w_{i}\pm 1}{2}+\frac{w_{i}-1}{4}\ .

(4.64)

This then also agrees with eq. (
4.63
).

4.2
The worldsheet calculation

We can now compare these results to the worldsheet correlator. In the hybrid formalism, once the correct supercharges are applied, see Section
3.5
, we need to integrate over the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
of punctured Riemann surfaces. As the dual BPS states (
3.59
) have a symplectic boson part of the form studied in
Dei:2020zui
, the incidence relation (
3.52
) shows that the worldsheet correlator localises to those points in moduli space where a branched covering map
\Gamma
exists. This holds for all genera of the worldsheet
Eberhardt:2020akk
;
Knighton:2020kuh
. Furthermore, from (
3.53
), it follows that a factor of

a_{i}^{-(w_{i}\pm 1)/2}

(4.65)

appears in the worldsheet correlator. We are thus left with the same
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
correlator, evaluated over the same covering surface,
10
10

10

As mentioned in the previous section, in the orbifold there can be disconnected covering surfaces. Our worldsheet calculation only gives the contribution of the connected surfaces, but it is clear that the disconnected contributions are correctly reproduced if we go to a second quantised formulation.
and hence we manifestly reproduce the symmetric orbifold calculation, up to the symplectic boson prefactor not fixed by the incidence relation. If we assume that the ground state correlators agree with the orbifold result, as has been shown for three- and four-point correlators
Dei:2021xgh
;
Dei:2021yom
;
Dei:2022pkr
, then the identical
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
structure implies that the BPS correlators also agree.

There is one small additional subtlety that needs to be addressed: the worldsheet correlator also contains additional
insertions of the
W
and
\mathcal{Q}
fields, see Section
3.5
, and one may be worried that they modify the result. However, with respect to the
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
algebra, both fields behave as the vacuum: for
W
this is the case by construction since it behaves as the vacuum with respect to
\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
, and for the
\mathcal{Q}
field, this is a consequence of the
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}
term that defines a
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
singlet. The
W
and
\mathcal{Q}
fields thus can only contract with each other and they just give rise to an overall prefactor. Note that these arguments hold for arbitrary genus, since they only use localisation, the behaviour of the leading order terms of the covering map, and the
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
structure of the dual BPS states. Therefore, the
full
correlators (i.e. including the sum over all covering maps) match between the two sides.

We can also test these ideas more explicitly. In
Lunin:2001pw
, many three-point functions of BPS operators in the symmetric product orbifold were calculated (for genus zero), and a general result was conjectured. As we explain in Appendix
A
, we have managed to reproduce ratios of their conjectured result (that are independent of the overall normalisation ambiguity) from a worldsheet calculation.

4.3
Formal Generalisation

The above argument hinges on the fact that the worldsheet state corresponding to a given BPS state
\phi
in the symmetric orbifold theory can be identified with the lift of
\phi
to the covering surface. This then implies that the worldsheet correlator is essentially the same as the correlator calculated on the covering surface. Given the generality of the argument, one may suspect that something similar should hold in general, not just for the BPS states.

The situation is, however, a little bit more complicated. The covering map depends, in general, on all the fields in the correlator, and hence so does the lift to the covering surface. For the BPS states the lift to the covering surface only depends on the
a_{i}
parameters, see the discussion in Section
2.3
, and this factor appeared, from the worldsheet perspective, from the correlators of the symplectic boson states, see in particular eq. (
3.53
). For a general state, however, also the subleading terms in eq. (
2.20
) will contribute to the lift to the covering surface, and it is then not obvious how they will be reproduced from the worldsheet perspective.

In the following we shall argue that this difficulty is resolved once we consider the correct physical states of the worldsheet theory. More specifically, the worldsheet dual of an arbitrary descendant state of the symmetric orbifold can be described in terms of the so-called DDF operators on the worldsheet. These operators depend on a field
\gamma
, which inside any correlator behaves as the covering map; as a consequence the DDF operators reproduce precisely the lifting of
\phi
to the covering surface.

4.3.1
The Wakimoto Representation

The DDF operators
DelGiudice:1971yjh
;
Giveon:1998ns
;
Eberhardt:2019qcl
are most easily constructed using the Wakimoto representation
Wakimoto:1986gf
. For
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
the Wakimoto representation takes the form

\displaystyle J^{+}

\displaystyle=\beta\ ,

(4.66a)

\displaystyle J^{3}

\displaystyle=(\beta\gamma)-\partial\Phi\ ,

(4.66b)

\displaystyle J^{-}

\displaystyle=(\beta\gamma\gamma)-2\,\partial\gamma-2\,(\partial\Phi\,\gamma)\ .

(4.66c)

Here,
\Phi
is a timelike free boson with background charge
1
, and
\beta
and
\gamma
are fields of dimension
1
and
0
, respectively, with OPE

\beta(z)\,\gamma(w)\sim-\frac{1}{z-w}\ .

(4.67)

The field
\gamma
acts on the spectrally flowed ground states as

\gamma(z)\,[\ket{m_{1},m_{2}}]^{\sigma^{w}}=z^{w}\,[\ket{m_{1}-1/2,m_{2}-1/2}]^{\sigma^{w}}+\mathcal{O}(z^{w+1})\ ,\quad z\to 0\ .

(4.68)

Thus,
\gamma^{1/w}
is a well-defined field in the
w
-twisted sector
Eberhardt:2019qcl
. It furthermore satisfies

e^{x\,J_{0}^{+}}\,\gamma(z)\,e^{-x\,J_{0}^{+}}=\gamma(z)-x\ ,

(4.69)

as follows directly from the above free field realisation, see eq. (
4.66
). Using the incidence relation of eq. (
3.52
) together with the
m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}
dependence of the correlator of eq. (
3.53
), it follows that

\frac{\Big\langle\gamma(z)\,\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\Big\rangle}{\Big\langle\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\Big\rangle}=x_{i}+a_{i}\,(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}}+\mathcal{O}((z-z_{i})^{w_{i}+1})\ ,\,\,z\to z_{i}\ ,

(4.70)

i.e., inside a correlator,
\gamma
agrees with the covering map to leading order. This suggests the identity

\Big\langle\gamma(z)\,\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\Big\rangle=\Gamma(z)\,\Big\langle\prod_{\alpha=1}^{n-2}W(u_{\alpha})\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{m_{1}^{i},m_{2}^{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\Big\rangle\ ,

(4.71)

which was in fact confirmed experimentally also to subleading order
Eberhardt:2019ywk
. We should mention that while (
4.71
) appears very natural, it predicts that the correlator on the left has poles in
z
at positions where there are no operator insertions — this simply follows from the fact that the covering map
\Gamma(z)
has such poles. Thus the correlator must have additional field insertions that give rise to these poles, but are invisible from the perspective of
\mathfrak{psu}(1,1|2)_{1}
. While it was shown in
(
Eberhardt:2019ywk
, Section 6.2)
that such operators do indeed exist, the question of why these operators are present and inserted at the correct positions has not yet been understood satisfactorily. This issue may also be related to the question of whether screening operators are required, see
Giribet:2000fy
;
Hosomichi:2000bm
.

4.3.2
The DDF Operators

Using the
\gamma
field, we can now construct the DDF operators via

\mathcal{K}^{a}_{r}=\underset{C(0)}{\oint}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\,K^{a}(z)\,\gamma^{r}(z)\ ,

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have shown that the BPS correlators of the symmetric orbifold are reproduced correctly from the string worldsheet perspective, i.e. from string theory on
\text{AdS}_{3}\times{\rm S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
with
k=1
units of NS-NS flux. The key step in our argument was to observe that the worldsheet states that are dual to the BPS states of the symmetric orbifold can essentially be identified with the lift of the BPS states under the covering map; then the matching of the correlators is manifest, and we have also checked it explicitly for some simple 3-point functions. The proposed relation between spacetime and worldsheet states obviously fits very nicely with the general idea that the worldsheet plays the role of the covering surface in this duality.

Given the relative simplicity of this identification it suggests that the result should be true more generally, and we have shown that, at least formally, the argument also works for any
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
descendant. In that case, the dual worldsheet state can be described by applying the corresponding DDF operators of
DelGiudice:1971yjh
;
Giveon:1998ns
;
Eberhardt:2019qcl
to the twisted sector ground state. We only spelled this out for the
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
descendants, but one should expect this argument to work even more generally, and it would be interesting to work this out. The key technical problem will be to really make sense of the Wakimoto field
\gamma(z)
which seems to behave like the covering map in all correlators.

Appendix A
Calculation of Dual BPS Three-Point Functions

In this appendix we calculate the fermionic part of three-point functions of BPS states on the worldsheet explicitly. As we shall see, combining this with the symplectic boson part and considering ratios of correlators (that do not depend on the undetermined overall cofficient) we can reproduce the results of
Lunin:2001pw
for many cases; we also conjecture a general form of the correlator which reproduces the ratios of their conjectured general result.

Recall that the symplectic boson part of the three-point correlator is described by (
3.53
). For three punctures, a unique branched covering exists for any choice of
w_{i}
and
x_{i}
provided that the
w_{i}
satisfy

w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\in 2\,\mathbb{Z}+1\ ,\quad w_{i}+w_{i+1}\geq w_{i+2}+1\ .

(A.80)

The coefficients
a_{i}
for the corresponding covering map were found in
Lunin:2000yv
, and are explicitly given by

a_{i}=\frac{d!\,(d-w_{i+1})!\,(d-w_{i+2})!}{w_{i}!\,(w_{i}-1)!\,(d-w_{i})!}\ ,

(A.81)

where
d=\frac{1}{2}\,(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}-1)
is the degree of the branched covering.

The fermionic part of the correlator is a pure
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
correlator after the
W
and
\mathcal{Q}
fields have been contracted with one another. Moreover, by charge conservation, the dependence on the choice of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
descendants is described by the 3j symbols, i.e. by the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients of the three
\mathfrak{su}(2)
representations fusing into the singlet,

\ket{0,0}=\sum_{m_{1},\,m_{2},\,m_{3}}\begin{pmatrix}j_{1}&j_{2}&j_{3}\\
m_{1}&m_{2}&m_{3}\end{pmatrix}\,\ket{j_{1},\,m_{1}}\otimes\ket{j_{2},\,m_{2}}\otimes\ket{j_{3},\,m_{3}}\ ,

(A.82)

where
\ket{j,m}
is the state with Casimir
j(j+1)
and
J^{3}_{0}
eigenvalue
m
. For the following it is convenient to take the fermionic part of the three dual BPS states (
3.59
) as
\ket{j,-j}
,
\ket{k,k}
, and
\ket{l,j-k}
. Written in terms of the free fermions, the highest weight states are

\ket{k,k}=\psi^{+}_{-(2k-1)/2}\chi^{+}_{-(2k-1)/2}\cdots\psi^{+}_{-1/2}\chi^{+}_{-1/2}\ket{0}_{\mathrm{NS}}\ .

(A.83)

To find the fermionic part, we then need to calculate the proportionality constant
c^{\mathrm{ferm}}_{jkl}
in

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle V(\ket{j,\,-j},z_{1})\,V(\ket{k,\,k},z_{2})\,V(\ket{l,\,j-k},z_{3})\right\rangle=\\[3.60004pt]

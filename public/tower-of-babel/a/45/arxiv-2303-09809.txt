On tropical Hodge theory for tropical varieties
Ryota Mikami

Abstract
. To prove log-concavity of the characteristic polynomials of matroids, Adiprasito-Huh-Katz proved the Kähler package (the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations) for their Chow rings. Amini-Piquerez generalized it to tropical cohomology of smooth projective tropical varieties. Their proofs were combinatorial. In this paper, we establish tropical Hodge theory except for regularity of solutions of Laplacian, and the Kähler package follows from tropical Hodge theory in the same way as compact Kähler manifolds.

1.
Introduction

Log-concavity
(i.e.,
w_{k-1}(M)w_{k+1}(M)\leq w_{k}(M)^{2}
) of the absolute values
w_{k}(M)
of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{M}(\lambda)
of a general matroid
M
of rank
r+1

was conjectured by Welsh
[
Wel76
]
in 1976.
Adiprasito-Huh-Katz
[
AHK18
]
proved it by showing
the Kähler package
for its Chow rings
A^{*}(M)
,
i.e., for
p\leq\frac{r}{2}
and an ample element
l\in A^{1}(M)\otimes\mathbb{R}
,

\cdot

(the hard Lefschetz theorem
[
AHK18
, Theorem 1.4 (1)]
)

A^{p}(M)\otimes\mathbb{R}\to A^{r-p}(M)\otimes\mathbb{R},\quad a\mapsto l^{r-2p}\cdot a

is an isomorphism,
and

\cdot

(the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations
[
AHK18
, Theorem 1.4 (2)]
) a symmetric bilinear map

A^{p}(M)\otimes\mathbb{R}\times A^{p}(M)\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad(a,b)\mapsto(-1)^{p}\deg(l^{r-2p}\cdot a\cdot b)

is positive definite on the kernel of
l^{r-2p+1}
.

(For more information on log-concavity, e.g., the case of realizable matroids, see
[
AHK18
, Section 1]
.)
Their proof was based on
tropical geometry
.

Amini-Piquerez
[
AP20
]
generalized the Kähler package
to
tropical singular cohomology

H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{sing}}\nolimits}^{p,q}(Y)
of smooth (i.e., locally matroidal) projective
tropical variety

Y
.
(For a natural compactification
\overline{\Sigma_{M}}
of the Bergman fan
\Sigma_{M}
of a matroid
M
,
we have

H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{sing}}\nolimits}^{p,p}(\overline{\Sigma_{M}})\cong A^{p}(M)

and
H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{sing}}\nolimits}^{p,q}(\overline{\Sigma_{M}})=0

(p\neq q)
.)

Tropical singular cohomology
H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{sing}}\nolimits}^{p,q}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{R}

is isomorphic to
tropical Dolbeault cohomology

H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{Dol}}\nolimits}^{p,q}(Y)
(
[
JSS19
, Theorem 1]
), which is based on
tropical analogs of differential
(p,q)
-forms on complex manifolds, called
superforms
.
(Superforms were
first introduced by Lagerberg (
[
Lag12
]
) for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and generalized by Chambert-Loir and Ducros (
[
CLD12
]
) to tropical varieties and non-archimedean analytic spaces.
Many mathematicians have studied them, e.g.,
[
BGGJK21
]
,
[
Gub16
]
,
[
GJR21-1
]
,
[
GJR21-2
]
,
[
GK17
]
,
[
Jel16A
]
,
[
Jel16T
]
,
[
JSS19
]
,
[
Mih21
]
.)

Although the Kähler package for singular (or Dolbeault) cohomology groups of compact Kähler manifolds
follows from
Hodge theory

(in particular, isomorphisms of Dolbeault cohomology groups and the spaces of harmonic forms),
the known proofs (
[
AHK18
]
,
[
BHMPW22
]
,
[
AP20
]
) of the above Kähler packages did not use differential geometry of tropical varieties.

In this paper, we study a tropical analog of Hodge theory.
The goal is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a tropically compact projective tropical variety
which is

\mathbb{Q}
-smooth in codimension
1
(
3.5
).
We assume that

6.5
(regularity of solutions of tropical Laplacian) holds.

Then the Kähler package holds for a new tropical Dolbeault cohomology
H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{Dol}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{pqs}}\nolimits,\mathbf{t}^{\prime\prime}_{\min}}^{p,q}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 1.2
.

When
\Lambda
is smooth (in particular,
\mathbb{Q}
-smooth in codimension
1
),
our new tropical Dolbeault cohomology
H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{Dol}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{pqs}}\nolimits,\mathbf{t}^{\prime\prime}_{\min}}^{p,q}(\Lambda)
is isomorphic to the usual one
H_{\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits,\mathop{\mathrm{Dol}}\nolimits}^{p,q}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 1.3
.

Classically, regularity of solutions of Laplacian
on compact Riemann manifolds (without boundaries)
are known (see
[
GH78
, p.380]
or
[
Wel08
, Theorem 4.9]
).
That on compact Riemann manifolds with smooth boundaries are
also known (
[
Sch95
, Theorem 2.2.6]
),
see also
6.6
.

However, there is a tropically compact projective tropical variety
which is
\mathbb{Q}
-smooth in codimension
1

and does not satisfy
6.5
(
[
BH17
, Section 5]
). See

Our tropical Hodge theory imitates those of compact Kähler manifolds and of compact Riemann manifolds with smooth boundaries.
We shall introduce

generalizations of Largerberg’s

-Kähler forms to tropical varieties,

\cdot

boundary conditions

\cdot

For technical reason,

3.1.
Tropical varieties

In this subsection,
we recall the compactification
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
of

and tropical varieties in it.

We put

\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma}):=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
as a set.
We define a topology on
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
as follows.
We extend the canonical topology on
\mathbb{R}
to that on
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
so that
(a,\infty]
for

are a basis of neighborhoods of
\infty
.
We also extend the addition on
\mathbb{R}
to that on
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
by

for
a\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We consider the set of semigroup homomorphisms
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(M\cap\sigma^{\vee},\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\})
as a topological subspace of
(\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\})^{M\cap\sigma^{\vee}}
.
We define a topology on

\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\sigma}):=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Sigma\\
\tau\subset\sigma\end{subarray}}N_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}

by the canonical bijection

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(M\cap\sigma^{\vee},\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\})\cong\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Sigma\\
\tau\subset\sigma\end{subarray}}N_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}.

Then we define a topology on
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
by glueing the topological spaces
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\sigma})
together.

A toric morphism
\varphi\colon T_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\to T_{\Sigma}

induces a natural map

\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma^{\prime}})\to\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma}),

which is also denoted by

.

We shall define polyhedral complexes in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A subset of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
is called a
rational polyhedron

if it is the intersection of sets of the form

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\mid\langle x,a\rangle\leq b\}\ (a\in\mathbb{Z}^{r},b\in\mathbb{R}),

here

is the usual inner product of

.

In this paper, we call rational polyhedron simply polyhedron.

Definition 3.2
.

For a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
and a polyhedron
C\subset N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}
,
we call its closure
P:=\overline{C}
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
a
polyhedron
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
.
We put
\dim(P):=\dim(C)
.

Let
P\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
be a polyhedron.
We put
\sigma_{P}\in\Sigma
the unique cone such that
P\cap N_{\sigma_{P},\mathbb{R}}\subset P
is dense.
A subset
Q
of
P
in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
is called a

of

if it is
the closure of the intersection

in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})

for some

and some cone
\tau\in\Sigma
,
where

is the closure of

\{x\in P\cap N_{\sigma_{P},\mathbb{R}}\mid x(a)\leq y(a)\text{ for any }y\in P\cap N_{\sigma_{P},\mathbb{R}}\}

in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
.
A finite collection
\Lambda
of polyhedra in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
is called a

if it satisfies the following two conditions.

\cdot

For all
P\in\Lambda
, each face of
P
is also in
\Lambda
.

\cdot

For all

, the intersection

is a face of

and

.

Let

be a polyhedral complex of pure dimension

.
We put
\Lambda_{\max}\subset\Lambda
be the subset of maximal dimensional (i.e.,

-dimensional) polyhedra.
We put

be the subset of

-dimensional polyhedra.

Definition 3.3
.

A (projective)
tropcial variety

(\Lambda,(m_{P})_{P\in\Lambda_{\max}})
of dimension

in
\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})

consists of

of pure dimension

and

where

is the primitive vector spanning the image of

.
(Here

is the tangent space considered as a subspace of

By abuse of notation,

Definition 3.4
.

In this paper,

(

,

,

.

We say that a tropically compact tropical variety

3.2.
Quasi-smooth superforms

In this subsection, we shall introduce (piece-wise) quasi-smooth superforms.

First, we shall define quasi-smooth superforms on tropical toric varieties.
We put

-\log\lvert\cdot\rvert\colon T_{\Sigma}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})

the coordinate-wise map,
i.e., for any cone

Definition 3.6
.

Let
U\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Trop}}\nolimits(T_{\Sigma})
be an open subset.
A
quasi-smooth
(p,q)
-superform
on
U

(-\log\lvert\cdot\rvert)^{-1}(U)

is
a smooth differential
(p,q)
-form
\omega
on
a complex manifold
(-\log\lvert\cdot\rvert)^{-1}(U)
of the form

\displaystyle\omega=\sum^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{p})\\
J=(j_{1},\dots,j_{q})\end{subarray}}\alpha_{I,J}(-\log\lvert z\rvert)d(-\frac{i}{2}\log z_{I})\wedge d(-\frac{1}{2}\log\overline{z_{J}}),

for some
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions

\alpha_{I,J}(-\log\lvert z\rvert):=\alpha_{I,J}(-\log\lvert z_{1}\rvert,\dots,-\log\lvert z_{n}\rvert),

where
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in M
is a basis considered as a holomorphic coordinate of

Proof.

[

is the case of

.
We briefly recall their strategy.

is bounded,

a small neighborhood

of

\Arrowvert\omega\Arrowvert_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Then, by Lipnitz rule,

When

Therefore, in general, for given large

they take

(in particular,

not only on a neighborhood of

is large”

(for details see [loc.cit.]).

for each

. (Such a function is

“where some
\lvert f_{i}\rvert
is large”.)

[

, Theorem 9.9.1]

and

,

, Theorem 9.9.1]
(i.e.,
\omega_{\lambda}
for

Then we have

\cdot

\cdot

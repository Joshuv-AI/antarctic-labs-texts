Fluctuations of Transverse Increments in Two-dimensional First Passage Percolation
Ujan Gangopadhyay

Abstract
. We consider a model of first passage percolation (FPP) where the nearest-neighbor edges of the standard two-dimensional Euclidean lattice are equipped with random variables. These variables are i.i.d. nonnegative, continuous, and have a finite moment generating function in a neighborhood of 0 0 . We derive consequences about transverse increments of passage times, assuming the model satisfies certain properties. Approximately, the assumed properties are the following: We assume that the standard deviation of the passage time on scale r r is of some order σ ⁡ ( r ) \sigma(r) , and { σ ⁡ ( r ) , r > 0 } \left\{\sigma(r),r>0\right\} grows approximately as a power of r r . Also, the tails of the passage time distributions for distance r r satisfy an exponential bound on a scale σ ⁡ ( r ) \sigma(r) uniformly over r r . In addition, the boundary of the limit shape in a neighborhood of some fixed direction θ \theta has a uniform quadratic curvature. By transverse increment we mean the difference of passage times from the origin to a pair of points which are located as follows: they are approximately in the same direction, say θ \theta , from the origin; the direction of one of them from the other is the direction of the tangent of the boundary of the limit shape at the point on the limit shape in the direction θ \theta . The main consequence derived is the following. If σ ⁡ ( r ) \sigma(r) varies as r χ r^{\chi} for some χ > 0 \chi>0 , and ξ \xi is such that χ = 2 ​ ξ − 1 \chi=2\xi-1 , then the fluctuation of the transverse increment of passage time between a pair of points situated at distance r r from each other is of the order of r χ / ξ r^{\chi/\xi} .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the transverse increments of
passage times in the classical model of first passage
percolation (FPP) on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, which was introduced in
[
17
]
.

1.1.
A brief description of the model

Let
\mathtt{E}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
be the set of nearest-neighbor edges in

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. On
\mathtt{E}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
we consider a collection of random
variables
\mathbb{T}:=\left\{\tau_{e}:e\in\mathtt{E}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\right\}
, which
are called
edge-weights.
We assume certain properties
of the edge-weights. We categorize these assumptions as being
basic or technical. The basic we use throughout the paper,
and the technical we use more selectively.

The basic assumptions:

We assume that
the edge-weights are i.i.d., nonnegative, and continuous. In
addition, there exists
C>0
such that
\Exp\exp\left(C\tau_{e}\right)<\infty
.

Using the edge-weights, we define the
passage time
of
a self-avoiding lattice path
\gamma
, denoted by

T(\gamma)
, as the sum of the edge-weights of all the edges
on the path
\gamma
, i.e.,

T(\gamma):=\sum_{\gamma\mbox{ contains }e}\tau_{e}.

In the above definition, we adopt the convention that a path
is a continuous curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Next, we define the passage
time between two points
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as

T(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\inf\left\{T(\gamma)\mid\gamma\;\mbox{is a path joining $\boldsymbol{u}$ and $\boldsymbol{v}$}\right\}.

It follows from the above definition that
T
is a random
pseudo-metric on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. It was shown in

[
31
]
, under only the i.i.d. and
nonnegative assumptions on the edge-weights, the infimum in
the definition of
T
is
attained for some paths, i.e., the infimum is a minimum.
Since we have assumed that the edge-weights are also
continuous, it follows that there is only one such minimizing
path almost surely. We call this path
the geodesic

between
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
, and denote it by
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})
.
Since the edge-weights have finite expectation, the passage
times
T(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})
, for all
\boldsymbol{u}
,
\boldsymbol{v}
, also have finite
expectation. Therefore,

h(\boldsymbol{u}):=\Exp T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{u})

is well-defined. From the triangle inequality of
T
it
follows that
h
is subadditive, i.e., for any

\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we have

h(\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{v})\leq h(\boldsymbol{u})+h(\boldsymbol{v}).

The subadditive ergodic theorem in
[
23
]
implies
that for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the following limits exist almost
surely and in
L^{1}
:

g(\boldsymbol{u}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{T(\boldsymbol{0},n\boldsymbol{u})}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n\boldsymbol{u})}{n}=\inf_{n>0}\frac{h(n\boldsymbol{u})}{n}.

The domain of
g
can be extended to
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
by taking limit
along appropriate subsequences in the above definition. By
extending the domain in this way,
g
becomes a norm on

\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. Therefore, the domain of
g
can be further extended
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The unit ball in the norm
g
is

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:g(\boldsymbol{x})\leq 1\right\},

This is called the
limit shape
. The
wet region

at time
t
is defined as

\mathcal{B}(t):=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})\leq t\right\}.

The shape theorem in
[
11
]
implies, under
conditions milder than our basic assumptions,
\mathcal{B}(t)

approaches
\mathcal{B}
in an appropriate sense as
t\to\infty
. In
addition,
\mathcal{B}
is compact, convex, has a nonempty interior,
and has all the symmetries of the lattice.

Notation 1.1
.

Here, we define passage times between points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
\lfloor\boldsymbol{x}\rfloor
be the
down-left corner of the unit square containing
\boldsymbol{x}
in

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let

T(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=T(\lfloor\boldsymbol{x}\rfloor,\lfloor\boldsymbol{y}\rfloor).

Similarly, by
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
we mean the geodesic

\Gamma(\lfloor\boldsymbol{x}\rfloor,\lfloor\boldsymbol{y}\rfloor)
.
Furthermore, for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let

h(\boldsymbol{x}):=h(\lfloor\boldsymbol{x}\rfloor).

Remark 1.1
.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
C,C_{0},C_{1},C_{2},\dots
constants that depend only on the distribution of the edge-weights. We restart numbering of
C_{i}
s in each proof. Often we break the proof of a theorem in propositions and claims. In these situations, we do not restart numbering the constants in the proof of the propositions and claims. Also, when we use a result which has been proved before, we use “tilde-version” of the variables and the parameters.

Remark 1.2
.

Extending definition of
T
from
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
yields a minor technical issue. Although
g(\boldsymbol{x})\leq h(\boldsymbol{x})
for all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, this may not be true for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Instead, we have, for some constant
C_{1}>0
and for all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

g(\boldsymbol{x})-C_{1}\leq h(\boldsymbol{x}).

Similarly, we have, for some constant
C_{2}>0
and for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

h(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{y})-C_{2}\leq h(\boldsymbol{x})+h(\boldsymbol{y}).

1.2.
Heuristics of the main results

It is common in the literature, for instance, in the works

[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,

[
25
]
,
[
12
]
,

[
13
]
, to assume specific unproved
properties of the limit shape. Often properties such as
differentiability, and curvature, either locally or globally,
which eliminates the possibility of facets or corners. These
properties are believed to be valid under our assumptions,
but there is no proof yet. We also make similar assumptions.

Suppose the boundary of the limit shape is differentiable at
a direction
\theta
, and
\theta^{t}
is the corresponding
tangential direction. By
transverse increments
we mean
differences of the form
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})-T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{y})
where

\boldsymbol{x}
has direction
\theta
,
\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}
has direction
{\theta^{t}}
.
Heuristically we can say what the order of the fluctuations
of transverse increments should be. For this, we need the
scaling exponents
\chi
and
\xi
.

It is believed that for FPP on Euclidean lattices of any
dimension, that there exists an exponent
\chi
, called the

‘fluctuation exponent,’
such that

T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{v})-h(\boldsymbol{v})
is of the order of
\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\rvert^{\chi}

(
\lvert\cdot\rvert
is the Euclidean norm.) Also, it is believed
that there exists an exponent
\xi
, called the

‘wandering exponent,’
such that the geodesic

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{v})
wanders
\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\rvert^{\xi}
distance on
average from the line joining
\boldsymbol{0}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
. The two
exponents are related by the equation
\chi=2\xi-1
which was
proved in
[
10
]
assuming
\chi
and

\xi
exist in a certain sense. In dimension
d=2
it is
believed that
\chi=1/3
and
\xi=2/3
. In
d=3
it is
believed that
\chi
is approximately
1/4
, and in higher
dimensions there does not seem to be a consensus even among
physicists about values of
\chi
and
\xi
, see for example

[
26
]
,
[
24
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
5
]
. In the exactly solvable models of two-dimensional last passage percolation, it has been proved that
\chi=1/3
and
\xi=2/3
, see
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
7
]
.

If one assumes the existence of these exponents in some
appropriate sense, then fluctuations of the transverse
increment
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})-T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{y})
should be of the
order of
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert^{\chi/\xi}
. The heuristic of this
is the following, see Figure
1
. We expect that the
geodesics
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})
and
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{y})

stay disjoint after starting from
\boldsymbol{x}
and
\boldsymbol{y}

respectively for a distance of the order of

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert^{1/\xi}
. Then these two branches should
contribute approximately independently

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert^{\chi/\xi}
to the fluctuation. The right
scale of the coalescence time as above has been proved in

[
8
]
for the exactly solvable model of
two-dimensional last passage percolation.

Figure 1.
Illustration for the heuristic of the exponent

\chi/\xi
: directions of
\boldsymbol{x}
and
\boldsymbol{y}
are
approximately
{\theta_{0}}
; direction of
\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}
is
{\theta_{0}^{t}}
;
the two geodesics
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})
and

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{y})
are expected to coalesce
approximately at distance
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert^{1/\xi}
in

-{\theta_{0}}
direction when traced starting from
\boldsymbol{x}
and

\boldsymbol{y}
respectively.

One reason for studying the fluctuations of transverse
increments is the following. In
d=2
, it is believed that
the transverse increments behave like increments of Brownian
motion, that is, the increments are approximately
uncorrelated. If this is true, then the exponent for
fluctuation of transverse increments should be
1/2
so that

\chi/\xi=1/2
. This with
\chi=2\xi-1
would imply

\chi=1/3
and
\xi=2/3
.

Figure 2.
Illustration for the heuristic of the exponent

2\chi/(1-\xi)
: directions of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
are
approximately
{\theta_{0}}
; direction of
\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b}
is
{\theta_{0}^{t}}
;
distances of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
from origin are
approximately
n
; distance between
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
is

2Jn^{\xi}
; the two geodesics
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})
are expected to branch apart at
approximately distance
m
from the origin where

m^{\xi}/m=Jn^{\xi}/n
.

As an application of the upper bound on fluctuations of
transverse increments we get an upper bound on long-range
correlations. By long-range correlation we mean the
correlation between
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})

where
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
are points approximately in the same
direction from the origin and distance between
\boldsymbol{a}
and

\boldsymbol{b}
is large compared to typical wanderings of the
geodesics
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})
.
Heuristically we can say the following about this
correlation, see Figure
2
. Assuming

\chi
and
\xi
exist, typical wandering of the geodesics

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})
is of the
order of
n^{\xi}
where
n
is the distance of the points

\boldsymbol{a}
,
\boldsymbol{b}
from the origin. Suppose the distance between

\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
is
Jn^{\xi}
for some large
J
. Then

\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})
are expected
to branch apart at a distance
m
from the origin such that
the distance between the rays joining
\boldsymbol{0}
to
\boldsymbol{a}
and

\boldsymbol{0}
to
\boldsymbol{b}
at distance
m
from the origin is of the
order of the typical wandering of the geodesics of at
distance
m
from origin. So we have approximately

m^{\xi}/m=Jn^{\xi}/n
. Hence
m=nJ^{-(1-\xi)^{-1}}
. Then the
covariance between
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})
and
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{b})
is
expected to be of the order of
m^{2\chi}
. So the
correlation should be of the order of
J^{-2\chi/(1-\xi)}
.

1.3.
Advanced assumptions

Along with our basic assumptions we make the following
assumptions. Similar assumptions have been used in

[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
. We assume
that there exists
\sigma:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
the following hold.

Assumption 1.1
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert\geq C_{1}
, and all
t>0
, we have

\Prob\left(\,|T(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})-h(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})|\geq t\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert)\,\right)\leq C_{2}e^{-C_{3}t}.

(A1)

Assumption 1.2
.

There exist constants
\mathfrak{p}>0
,
\mathfrak{q}>0
,

\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\leq\beta
,
such that for all
x>y>0
we have

\mathfrak{p}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\frac{\sigma(x)}{\sigma(y)}\leq\mathfrak{q}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{\beta}.

(A2)

Assumption 1.3
.

There exist positive constants
\epsilon
,
C
,
such that for
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert\geq C
, we have

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\,T(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\leq h(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})-\epsilon\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert)\,\right)\geq\epsilon,

(A3)

\displaystyle\Prob\left(\,T(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\geq h(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})+\epsilon\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert)\,\right)\geq\epsilon.

Remark 1.3
.

By Remark 1.1 of
[
3
]
, we assume without
loss of generality that
\sigma
is monotonically
increasing and continuous.

Remark 1.4
.

Under Assumption
1.1
,
Assumption
1.3
is equivalent to saying
that there exist positive constants
C_{1}
,

C_{2}
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

with
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert\geq C_{1}
, we have

\Var(T(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))\geq C_{2}\sigma^{2}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert).

Remark 1.5
.

The assumption
\beta<1
is natural because, from results
in
[
21
]
, the passage times are known to
satisfy exponential concentration with scaling exponent

1/2
, which shows that
\chi
must be
\leq 1/2
under
any reasonable definition.

Remark 1.6
.

The assumption
\alpha>0
is natural because, for certain
definition of
\chi
and
\xi
it was shown in

[
30
]
that
\chi\geq(1-(d-1)\xi)/2
in

d
-dimensions, which in two dimensions coupled with

\chi\geq 2\xi-1
and
\chi\leq 1/2
yields
\chi\geq 1/8
.

For any direction
\theta

\theta

{\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}}

.

1.4.
Main results

Notation 1.3
.

Given two distinct directions
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}

define the projections
\pi^{1}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
and

\pi^{2}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}

\boldsymbol{v}

\boldsymbol{v}=\pi^{1}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta_{1}}+\pi^{2}_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta_{2}}.

Notation 1.4
.

For
n>0

\Delta(n):=\left(n\sigma(n)\right)^{1/2}.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\theta_{0}}
be a direction of both type I and II. For

n>0
,
L>0
, define

\mathcal{I}(n,L):=\left\{\,\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,\pi^{1}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{0}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{x})=n,\,0\leq\pi^{2}_{\theta_{0},\theta_{0}^{t}}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq L\,\right\},

and

\mathcal{D}(n,L):=\max\left\{\,|T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})-T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{y})|\,:\,\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{I}(n,L)\,\right\}.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1]
. Then, under the
Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
,
there exist positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
L_{0}
,

n_{0}
,

, such that for

,

,

The following theorem is our lower bound on the fluctuations
of transverse increments. In this theorem, we show that the
standard deviation of the transverse increment between a pair

is at least of the order of

\sigma\left(\Delta^{-1}(L)\right)
with a correction factor smaller
than any power of

.

Let
{\theta_{0}}
be a direction of both type I and II. Fix

,
1.2
, and

,

,

,

,

2.
Wandering of geodesics

In this section we establish some upper bounds on the wandering of geodesics.

Notation 2.1
.

For any set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
let

\Diam(A):=\sup\left\{\,\lvert\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}\rvert:\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in A\,\right\}.

The following result provides a preliminary bound on the
wandering of geodesics. The proof of this result follows from
Proposition 5.8 of
[
20
]
under our basic
assumptions. The result has been shown to hold under milder
assumptions in Theorem 6.2 of

[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist positive constants , ,
, such that the following holds. If

\lvert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rvert\geq\Cr{bw1}
for some
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
then

\Prob\left(\,\Diam(\Gamma(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}))\geq\Cr{bw2}\lvert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rvert\,\right)\leq e^{-\Cr{bw3}\lvert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rvert}.

Utilizing the curvature of the limit shape we get a more
refined bound on the wandering of the geodesics. The
curvature of the limit shape is used in the following manner.
Consider two points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The shortest path between
them in the
g
-norm is, of course, the line joining them.
When the geodesic between them wander transversely too far
from the line joining them, the extra distance covered by the
geodesic in the
g
-norm can be thought of as a cost for
excessive wandering. A lower bound of this cost yields an
upper bound on the wandering of the geodesics. A lower bound
of the curvature of the limit shape provides a lower bound on
the cost associated with the
g
-norm. This is stated in the
following result, which is essentially same as Lemma 2.3 of

[
3
]
. We state it without proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\theta_{0}}
be a direction of type I. Then there exist positive constants
C
and
\delta
such that for
n>0
,
k
,
l
,
d
, satisfying
|l|/n\leq\delta

g(\boldsymbol{u})+g(\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{u})-g(\boldsymbol{a})\geq C\min\left\{|d|,\frac{d^{2}}{n}\right\},

where
\boldsymbol{a}:=n{\boldsymbol{e}_{{\theta_{0}}}}+l{\boldsymbol{e}_{{\theta_{0}^{t}}}}
and
\boldsymbol{u}:=k{\boldsymbol{e}_{{\theta_{0}}}}+(l\frac{k}{n}+d){\boldsymbol{e}_{{\theta_{0}^{t}}}}
.

Geodesics cannot wander too much because the cost associated
with the
g
-norm becomes difficult to be compensated by the
fluctuations of passage times. Thus, bounds on the
fluctuations
T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})-g(\boldsymbol{x})
, combined with
Lemma
2.2
yields upper bounds on geodesic
wanderings. By Assumption
1.1
we know

T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x})-h(\boldsymbol{x})
satisfies exponential concentration
in the scale
\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert)
uniformly over
\boldsymbol{x}
. So we
need a bound on the differences

differences are known as nonrandom fluctuations in the
literature. A general method of bounding the nonrandom
fluctuations was developed in
[
1

2

There it was shown, using exponential concentration of

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{1/2}

[

is at most of
the order of
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{1/2}\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert

Alexander’s method can be used with no significant alteration
to yield a bound of the order of

\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert)\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert
, which we state below.
Improvements to the logarithmic bound has been made in

[
29
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
4
]

for some related
models, which we briefly discuss in

approximation property’ from
[

]
.

4.
Refined upper bound on nonrandom fluctuations

Theorem
3.2
of the previous section provides a
preliminary upper bound of the fluctuations of the transverse
increments. To prove the refined bound of
Theorem
1.1
, we need to reduce the
correction factor in Proposition
2.3
from

\log
to fixed but arbitrary small power of
\log
. Related
results are known in the literature. In
[
29
]

it has been shown that
(\log x)^{1/2}
is a valid correction
factor in FPP on Cayley Graphs on integer lattices. In

[
14
]
it have shown that any iterate of
\log
is
a valid correction factor in a spherically symmetric model of
FPP. In
[
4
]
a bound with only a constant correction factor has been shown in a spherically symmetric model. In our case, to reduce the correction factor, we use a modified
version of the procedure of
[
2
]
. We now introduce
the concept of convex hull approximation property from

[
2
]
.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{0}
be the set of directions where the boundary of
\mathcal{B}
is differentiable. Consider
\boldsymbol{x}
with direction in
\mathcal{S}_{0}
. Let
H_{\boldsymbol{x}}
be the tangent to
\partial g(\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{B}
at
\boldsymbol{x}
. Let
H_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{0}
be the line through
\boldsymbol{0}
parallel to
H_{\boldsymbol{x}}
. Let
g_{\boldsymbol{x}}
be the unique linear functional on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})=0
for all
y\in H_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{0}
, and
g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x})=g(\boldsymbol{x})
. Recall
\Phi
from Notation
2.2
. Define for
\phi\in\Phi
,
\nu\geq 0
,
C>0
,
K>0
,

Q_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\nu,\phi,C,K):=\left\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\lvert\boldsymbol{y}\rvert\leq K\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert,g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})\leq g(\boldsymbol{x}),h(\boldsymbol{y})\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})+C\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\nu}\phi\left(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert\right)\right\}.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
h
satisfies the
convex hull
approximation property
(CHAP) with exponent

and correction factor

,

,

,

for some

\alpha\in[1,a]
, for all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
with

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert\geq M
and direction of
\boldsymbol{x}
in
\mathcal{S}
,
where

denotes the convex hull. When we want
to specify the specific constants, we say

satisfies

\text{CHAP}(\nu,\phi,M,C,K,a)
in sector

\mathcal{S}
.

The procedure of
[
2
]
in our terminology as follows.
The objective of
[
2
]
was to prove GAP with exponent

\alpha
and correction factor
\phi_{1}
. To achieve this,
first, it is shown that CHAP with exponent
\alpha
and
correction factor
\phi_{1}
holds. This is done in an
iterative way. GAP with exponent

and correction factor

\phi_{1}
holds trivially because

is sublinear. Then the
exponent of GAP is reduced from

to
\alpha
iteratively
using CHAP with exponent

\gamma_{qi}:=q\alpha_{qi}-\lfloor q\alpha_{qi}\rfloor\in[0,1).

Therefore,

\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}+\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L}+\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}.

Direction of
\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L}
:

Consider
i\in L(q)
. Then

\lvert\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\rvert\geq\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{1-\nu}.

(4.34)

Using Lemma
4.6
,

\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{x}/q}(\alpha,\widehat{\phi},C_{c},K)
,

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert\geq M
, assuming
M
is large enough, and
(
A2
), we get

\pi^{2}_{\theta,\theta^{t}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\leq\Delta(\boldsymbol{x}/q)(\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert)^{1/2}\leq\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{(1+\beta)/2}(\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert)^{1/2}.

Therefore, using (
4.34
), (
A2
), and

1-\nu=1-(1-\beta)/4>(1+\beta)/2
, we get

\pi^{1}_{\theta,\theta^{t}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\geq\lvert\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\rvert-\pi^{2}_{\theta,\theta^{t}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\geq\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{1-\nu},

and, further, using
1-\nu-(1+\beta)/2=(1-\beta)/4
we get

\frac{\pi^{2}_{\theta,\theta^{t}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})}{\pi^{1}_{\theta,\theta^{t}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})}\leq\frac{\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{(1+\beta)/2}(\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert)^{1/2}}{\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{1-\nu}}\leq\frac{(\log\log M)^{1/2}}{M^{(1-\beta)/4}}.

Since
\theta\in\mathcal{S}_{m+1}
, assuming
M
is large enough,
we get direction of
\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}
is in
\mathcal{S}_{m}
. This implies
\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L}
has direction in
\mathcal{S}_{m}
.

Bounding
h(\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast})
:

Using
subadditivity of

h
, (
4.33
), (
4.32
), and (
A2
),
we get

h(\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lfloor q\alpha_{qi}\rfloor h(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lfloor q\alpha_{qi}\rfloor\left[g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})+C_{c}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert)\right]\\
\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast})+c_{1}\mathfrak{p}^{-1}q^{1-\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert).

(4.35)

Bounding
h(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})
when
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert
is
large:

Suppose
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert\geq M_{g}
. Using
q\geq c_{2}=3K>3
,

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert\geq M\geq 1
, and (
4.33
) we get

\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert=\lvert\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\rvert\leq\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\lvert\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\rvert\leq 3\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert^{1-\nu}\leq 3\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert\leq\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert.

Using this and

\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert\leq\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha\nu}(\alpha\nu)^{-1}

we get

\frac{\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert)\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert}{\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert)\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\left(\frac{\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert}{\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}{\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\frac{c_{2}^{\alpha}q^{\alpha\nu}\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}{q^{\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha\nu}\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{2}^{\alpha}(\alpha\nu)^{-1}q^{-\alpha(1-\nu)}.

Since
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert\geq M_{g}
, using (
4.30
) we get

\displaystyle h(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})

\displaystyle\leq g(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})+C_{g}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})+C_{g}\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert)\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})+\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\left[g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})-g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\right]+C_{g}\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert)\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})+\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\left[g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})-g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\right]+C_{g}\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{2}^{\alpha}(\alpha\nu)^{-1}q^{-\alpha(1-\nu)}\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert)\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})+\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\left[g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})-g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\right]+C_{g}\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{2}^{\alpha}(\alpha\nu)^{-1}q^{1-\alpha}\sigma(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert)\log\log\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert.

(4.36)

Bounding
h(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})
when
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert
is
small:

Suppose
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert\leq M_{g}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{y}_{qi}\in Q_{\boldsymbol{x}/q}(\alpha,\phi_{2},C_{c},K)

So, letting

, and using definition of

, and (
A2
), we have

g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})=\sum_{i\not\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\geq-\sum_{i\in I(q)}|g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})|\geq-c_{0}q^{-\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}/q\rvert)\geq-\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{0}q^{-\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert).

Therefore, using
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert\leq M_{g}
and

h(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S})\leq r\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf S}\rvert

(4.37)

Bounding

Using this and applying

, which holds by the induction hypothesis for

\displaystyle h(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L})

\displaystyle\leq g(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L})+C\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L}\rvert^{\alpha}\psi_{m}\left(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L}\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})+C\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{2}^{\alpha}q^{-\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha}\psi_{m}\left(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\leq g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\sf L})+\sum_{i\in L(q)}\gamma_{qi}\left[g(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})-g_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{qi})\right]+C\mathfrak{p}^{-1}c_{2}^{\alpha}q^{-\alpha}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{\alpha}\psi_{m}\left(\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert\right)

Bounding

Suppose

. Then by similar calculations that

(4.39)

Using

Feasibility of

We need to verify that

,

, choosing

Case I:
Suppose
\boldsymbol{x}
is such

Case II:

\Exp\left(\left(T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})-T^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{a})\right)^{2}\mathbb{1}(\mathcal{E}_{3})\right)\leq\Exp\max_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{I}}\left(T(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{z})-T^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{z})\right)^{2}.

(7.10)

Combining this with (

proves Proposition

.

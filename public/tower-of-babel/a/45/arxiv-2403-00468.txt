Probabilistic Central Bell Polynomials Thanks: ∗ Corresponding authors
Rongrong Xu, Yuankui Ma, Taekyun Kim*, Dae San Kim, Salah Boulaaras

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In this paper, we study the probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated with random variable Y Y , as probabilistic extension of the central Bell polynomials. In addition, we investigate the probabilistic central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with Y Y and the probabilistic central Fubini polynomials associated with Y Y . The aim of this paper is to derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers.

1.
Introduction

Assume that
Y
is a random variable satisfying the moment condition (see (
16
)).
The aim of this paper is to study, as probabilistic extensions of central Bell polynomials, the probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated with
Y
, along with the probabilistic central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
and the probabilistic central Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
. We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers. In addition, we consider the case that
Y
is the Poisson random variable with parameters
\alpha>0
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the central factorials, the central factorial numbers of the second and the central Bell polynomials. We remind the reader of the Stirling numbers of the second kind, the Bell polynomials, the partial Bell polynomials and the complete Bell polynomials. Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
,
E[e^{tY}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}E[Y^{n}],\quad(|t|<r)
, exists for some
r>0
. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\,\,(k\geq 1)
,   with
S_{0}=0
. Then we recall the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
{n\brace k}_{Y}
and the probabilistic Bell polynomials. Section 2 is the main results of this paper. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1},\,\,S_{k},\,\,(k=0,1,\dots)
be as in the above. We define the probabilistic central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
T^{Y}(n,k)
. We derive an explicit expression for
T^{Y}(n,k)
in Theorem 2.1. We define the probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated
Y
,
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
. The generating function of
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
is found in Theorem 2.2. Explicit expressions for
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
are deduced in Theorems 2.3 and 2.4. An additional explicit expression for
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
is derived in Theorem 2.15 when
Y
is the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha
. We define the probabilistic central Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
,
F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
. We obtain explicit expressions for
F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
in Theorems 2.5 and 2.6. As to
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
, several facts are found, namely expressions in terms of partial Bell polynomials in Theorems 2.7 and 2.10, a recurrence relation in Theorem 2.8, convolution formula in Theorem 2.9 and higher-order derivatives in Theorem 2.13. We derive some identities involving
T^{Y}(n,k)
and the partial Bell polynomials in Theorem 2.11 and 2.12. In Theorem 2.14, we find an expression of
T^{Y}(2n,2k)
in terms of the partial Bell polynomial. In the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the central factorials
x^{[n]}
are defined by

(1)

\displaystyle x^{[0]}=1,\quad x^{[n]}

\displaystyle=x\bigg(x+\frac{n}{2}-1\bigg)\bigg(x+\frac{n}{2}-2\bigg)\cdots\bigg(x+\frac{n}{2}-n+1\bigg)

\displaystyle=x\binom{x+\frac{n}{2}-1}{n-1}(n-1)!,\quad(n\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,10-12,17,18]).

From (
1
), we note that

(2)

\bigg(\frac{t}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{t^{2}}{4}+1}\bigg)^{2x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{[n]}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10-12,17,18]).

The central factorial numbers of the second kind are defined by

(3)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)x^{[k]},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]).

From (
2
) and (
3
), we have

(4)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,11]).

The central Bell polynomials are defined by

(5)

B_{n}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

When
x=1
,
B_{n}^{(c)}=B_{n}^{(c)}(1)
are called the central Bell numbers.

From (
5
), we note that

(6)

e^{x(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12,17,18]).

From (
6
), we have

(7)

B_{n}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{l+j}{l}(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{l}{2}-\frac{j}{2}\bigg)^{n}\frac{x^{l+j}}{(l+j)!},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

For
n\geq 0
, the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined as

(8)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-24]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

The Bell polynomials are defined by

(9)

\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,15,16,19]).

When
x=1
,
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1)
are called the Bell numbers.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, the partial Bell polynomials are defined by

(10)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,13,17]).

Thus, by (
10
), we get

(11)

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-k+1}=k\\
l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)l_{n-k+1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{n-k+1}!}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\bigg)^{1!}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\bigg)^{l_{2}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\bigg)^{l_{n-k+1}}.

Thus, by (
10
), we get

(12)

B_{n,k}(1,1,\dots,1)={n\brace k},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

The complete Bell polynomials are given by

(13)

\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,13,17]).

From (
10
) and (
13
), we note that

(14)

B_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1}),\quad(n\geq 0).

By (
9
) and (
14
), we get

(15)

B_{n}(x,x,\dots,x)=\phi_{n}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
,

(16)

E\big[e^{tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r),\ \ \textrm{exists for some $r>0$}.

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}),\ \mathrm{with}\quad S_{0}=0.

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(17)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e^{tY}]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,13]).

In view of (
9
), the probabilistic Bell polynomials are defined by

(18)

\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13]).

From (
17
), we have

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[S_{j}^{n}\big],\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

When
Y=1
,
{n\brace k}_{Y}={n\brace k},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0)
.

By (
18
), we get

(19)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{tY}]-1)}.

When
Y=1
,
\phi^{Y}(x)=\phi_{n}(x),\quad(n\geq 0)
.

2.
Probabilistic Central Bell polynomials

Let
Y
be a random variable and let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
with

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\geq 1).

In view of (
4
), we define the
probabilistic central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
by

(20)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}]\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

When
Y=1
, we have
T^{Y}(n,k)=T(n,k),\ (n\geq k\geq 0)
. From (
20
), we have

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]\Big)^{k-j}(-1)^{j}\Big(E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}E\Big[e^{-\frac{t}{2}(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{j})}\Big]E\Big[e^{\frac{t}{2}(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k-j})}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}E\Big[e^{-\frac{t}{2}S_{j}}\Big]E\Big[e^{\frac{t}{2}S_{k-j}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}(-1)^{n-l-j}E\big[S_{k-j}^{l}\big]E\big[S_{j}^{n-l}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

2^{n}T^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{n-l-j}E\big[S_{k-j}^{l}\big]E\big[S_{j}^{n-l}\big].

In view of (
5
), we define the
probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated with
Y
by

(22)

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T^{Y}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

When
Y=1
,
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=B_{n}^{(c)}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
. In particular, for
x=1
,
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}=B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(1)
are called the probabilistic central Bell numbers.

From (
22
), we note that

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}T^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}.

Therefore, by (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

The generating function of the probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated with
Y
is given by

(24)

e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
24
), we have

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}])}e^{-x(E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}E\big[e^{\frac{t}{2}S_{l}}\big]\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}x^{j}E\big[e^{-\frac{t}{2}S_{j}}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[S_{l}^{m}]\frac{1}{m!}\bigg(\frac{t}{2}\bigg)^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}x^{j}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}E\big[S_{j}^{p}\big]\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!}\bigg(\frac{t}{2}\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l+j}}{l!j!}(-1)^{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{n-m}E\big[S_{l}^{m}\big]E\big[S_{j}^{n-m}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{l+j}}{l!j!}(-1)^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{n-m}E\big[S_{l}^{m}\big]E\big[S_{j}^{n-m}\big].

When
Y=1
, we get the following result in (
7
):

B_{n}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{l+j}{l}(-1)^{j}\frac{x^{l+j}}{(l+j)!}\bigg(\frac{l}{2}-\frac{j}{2}\bigg)^{n}.

Now, we observe that

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}x^{l}\frac{1}{l!}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}x^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}x^{l}\sum_{i=l}^{\infty}{i\brace l}_{Y}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}x^{j}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}{k\brace j}_{Y}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{i}x^{l}{i\brace l}_{Y}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}x^{j}{k\brace j}_{Y}(-1)^{k}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}x^{j+l}{n-k\brace l}_{Y}\binom{n}{k}{k\brace j}_{Y}(-1)^{j+k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{j+k}\binom{n}{k}{n-k\brace l}_{Y}{k\brace j}_{Y}x^{j+l}.

Now, we define the
probabilistic central Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
by

(27)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}.

In particular,
F_{n}^{(c,Y)}=F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(1),\ (n\geq 0)
, are called the probabilistic central Fubini numbers. By (
20
) and (
27
), we get

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{n}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}k!T_{n}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!T_{n}^{Y}(n,k)x^{k}.

From (
27
), we note that

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{k-j}(-1)^{j}\Big(E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{(k-j)!}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{k-j}(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-1\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{i=k-j}^{n-j}{i\brace k-j}_{Y}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}{n-i\brace j}_{Y}(-1)^{n-i}\binom{n}{i}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=k-j}^{n-j}x^{k}k!{i\brace k-j}_{Y}{n-i\brace j}_{Y}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i-j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=k-j}^{n-j}(-1)^{n-i-j}k!{i\brace k-j}_{Y}{n-i\brace j}_{Y}\binom{n}{i}x^{k}.

By (
24
), we get

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(x\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-(-1)^{j}}{2^{j}}E[Y^{j}]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(xE[Y],0,\frac{x}{2^{2}}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\frac{x(1-(-1)^{n-k+1})}{2^{n-k+1}}E[Y^{n-k+1}]\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}\Big(xE[Y],0,\frac{x}{2^{2}}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\frac{x(1-(-1)^{n-k+1})}{2^{n-k+1}}E[Y^{n-k+1}]\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}\Big(xE[Y],0,\frac{x}{2^{2}}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\frac{x(1-(-1)^{n-k+1})}{2^{n-k+1}}E[Y^{n-k+1}]\Big).

From (
24
), we note that

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n+1}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=x\bigg(E\bigg[\frac{Y}{2}e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\bigg]+E\bigg[\frac{Y}{2}e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\bigg]\bigg)e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2l}E\big[Y^{2l+1}\big]\frac{t^{2l}}{(2l)!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x\sum_{l=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\binom{n}{2l}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2l}E\big[Y^{2l+1}\big]B_{n-2l}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n+1}^{(c,Y)}(x)=x\sum_{l=0}^{[\frac{n}{2}]}\binom{n}{2l}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2l}E\big[Y^{2l+1}\big]B_{n-2l}^{(c,Y)}(x).

We observe that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x+y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{(x+y)(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}e^{y(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(c,Y)}(y)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}^{(c,Y)}(x)B_{n-k}^{(c,Y)}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, we obtain the following convolution formula.

Theorem 2.9

(Convolution formula)
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}^{(c,Y)}(x)B_{n-k}^{(c,Y)}(y).

By using (
24
), we have

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\Big(e^{(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}-1+1\Big)^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{x}{k}k!\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{x}{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(B_{1}^{(c,Y)},B_{2}^{(c,Y)},\dots,B_{n-k+1}^{(c,Y)}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}k!B_{n,k}\Big(B_{1}^{(c,Y)},B_{2}^{(c,Y)},\dots,B_{n-k+1}^{(c,Y)}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}k!B_{n,k}\Big(B_{1}^{(c,Y)},B_{2}^{(c,Y)},\dots,B_{n-k+1}^{(c,Y)}\Big).

It is easy to show that

(34)

te^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}=t\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j-1}^{(c,Y)}(x)j\frac{t^{j}}{j!}.

Thus, by (
34
), we get

(35)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jB_{j-1}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}=t^{k}\Big(e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=t^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{j}x^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=t^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{j}x^{j}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^{j}x^{j}T^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n+k}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}k!\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}T^{Y}(n-k,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
35
), we note that

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}T^{Y}(n-k,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jB_{j-1}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(B_{0}^{(c,Y)}(x),2B_{1}^{(c,Y)}(x),3B_{2}^{(c,Y)}(x),\dots,(n-k+1)B_{n-k}^{(c,Y)}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}k^{j}x^{j}T^{Y}(n-k,j)=B_{n,k}\Big(B_{0}^{(c,Y)}(x),2B_{1}^{(c,Y)}(x),3B_{2}^{(c,Y)}(x),\dots,(n-k+1)B_{n-k}^{(c,Y)}(x)\Big).

By making use of (
24
), we note that

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(B_{1}^{(c,Y)}(x),B_{2}^{(c,Y)}(x),\dots,B_{n-k+1}^{(c,Y)}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}{j\brace k}x^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}]\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}{j\brace k}x^{j}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=k}^{n}{j\brace k}T^{Y}(n,j)x^{j}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.12
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

B_{n,k}\Big(B_{1}^{(c,Y)}(x),B_{2}^{(c,Y)}(x),\dots,B_{n-k+1}^{(c,Y)}(x)\Big)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}{j\brace k}T^{Y}(n,j)x^{j}.

From (
24
), we note that

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}k!\Big(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}]\Big)^{k}e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T^{Y}(l,k)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}k!\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{j}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}(x)T^{Y}(n-j,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
38
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

For
k\geq 1
, we have

\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=k!\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{j}B_{j}^{(c,Y)}(x)T^{Y}(n-j,k).

From (
20
), we get

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]-E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}E[Y^{j}]\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{j}\big(1-(-1)^{j}\big)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\bigg(E[Y],0,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n-k+1}E\big[Y^{n-k+1}\big]\big(1-(-1)^{n-k+1}\big)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
39
), we get

(40)

\displaystyle T^{Y}(n,k)

\displaystyle=B_{n,k}\bigg(E[Y],0,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n-k+1}E\big[Y^{n-k+1}\big]\big(1-(-1)^{n-k+1}\big)\bigg),

where
n\geq k\geq 0
.

From (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.14
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle T^{Y}(2n,2k)

\displaystyle=B_{2n,2k}\bigg(E[Y],0,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}E[Y^{3}],0,\dots,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2n-2k}E\big[Y^{2n-2k+1}\big]\bigg).

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then we have

(41)

E\big[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}e^{-\alpha}e^{\frac{n}{2}t}=e^{\alpha(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-1)},

and

E\big[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}e^{-\alpha}e^{-\frac{n}{2}t}=e^{\alpha(e^{-\frac{t}{2}}-1)}.

Thus, by (
24
) and (
41
), we get

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{\frac{Y}{2}t}]-E[e^{-\frac{Y}{2}t}])}

\displaystyle=e^{x(e^{\alpha(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-1)}-e^{\alpha(e^{-\frac{t}{2}}-1)})}

\displaystyle=e^{x(e^{\alpha(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-1)}-1)}e^{-x(e^{\alpha(e^{-\frac{t}{2}}-1)}-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}(x)\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-1\big)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(-x)\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\big(e^{-\frac{t}{2}}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}(x)\alpha^{j}\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}{i\brace j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(-x)\alpha^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}(-1)^{l}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\phi_{j}(x)\alpha^{j}{i\brace j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\phi_{k}(-x)\alpha^{k}{l\brace k}(-1)^{l}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{n-l}\phi_{j}(x)\alpha^{j+k}\binom{n-l}{j}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{n}\phi_{k}(-x){l\brace k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n}{l}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
42
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{n-l}(-1)^{l}\binom{n}{l}\binom{n-l}{j}{l\brace k}\alpha^{j+k}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{k}(-x).

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. Then we have

(43)

E\big[Y^{n}\big]=\sum_{i=0}^{1}i^{n}p(i)=0^{n}p(0)+1^{n}p(1)=p,\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By Theorem 2.14 and (
43
), we get

\displaystyle T^{Y}(2n,2k)

\displaystyle=B_{2n,2k}\bigg(p,0,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}p,0,\dots,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2n-2k}p\bigg)

\displaystyle=p^{2k}T(2n,2k).

3.
Conclusion

Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In this paper, we studied by using generating functions probabilistic extensions of several special polynomials and numbers, namely the probabilistic central factorial numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, the probabilistic central Bell polynomials associated with
Y
and the probabilistic central Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
.

In more detail, we obtained an explicit expression for
T^{Y}(n,k)
in Theorem 2.1 and a representation in terms of
B_{2n,2k}
for
T^{Y}(2n,2k)
in Theorem 2.14. A generating function of
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)

was derived in Theorem 2.2. We deduced explicit expressions for
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
in Theorems 2.3 and 2.4, and in Theorem 2.15 for the special case when
Y
is the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. We found explicit expressions for
F_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
in Theorems 2.5 and 2.6. We deduced several facts about
B_{n}^{(c,Y)}(x)
, namely representions in terms of
B_{n,k}
in Theorems 2.7 and 2.10, a recursive formula in Theorem 2.8, a convolution formula in Theorem 2.9 and higher-order derivatives in Theorem 2.13. We obtained identities involving
T^{Y}(n,k)
and
B_{n,k}
in Theorems 2.11 and 2.12.

It is one of our future projects to continue to study probabilistic versions of many special polynomials and numbers and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

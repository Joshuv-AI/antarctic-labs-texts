Light-quark mass dependence of the nucleon axial charge and pion-nucleon scattering phenomenology
Fernando Alvarado, Luis Alvarez-Ruso

Abstract
The light-quark mass dependence of the nucleon axial isovector charge ( g A g_{A} ) has been studied up to next-to-next-to-leading order, 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 4 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{4}) , in relativistic chiral perturbation theory using extended-on-mass-shell renormalization, without and with explicit Δ ⁡ ( 1232 ) \Delta(1232) degrees of freedom. We show that in the Δ \Delta -less case, at this order, the flat trend of g A ​ ( M π ) g_{A}(M_{\pi}) exhibited by state-of-the-art lattice QCD (LQCD) results cannot be reproduced using low energy constants (LECs) extracted from pion-nucleon elastic and inelastic scattering. A satisfactory description of these LQCD data is only achieved in the theory with Δ \Delta . From this fit we report g A ​ ( M π ⁡ ( phys ) ) = 1.260 ± 0.012 g_{A}(M_{\pi\rm(phys)})=1.260\pm 0.012 , close to the experimental result, and d 16 = − 0.88 ± 0.88 d_{16}=-0.88\pm 0.88 GeV -2 , in agreement with its empirical value. The large uncertainties are of theoretical origin, reflecting the difference between 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 3 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{3}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 4 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{4}) that still persists at large M π M_{\pi} in presence of the Δ \Delta .

II
The nucleon axial charge in relativistic BChPT

In the isospin limit, the matrix element of the axial isovector current

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{a}(x)=\bar{q}(x)\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\frac{\tau^{a}}{2}q(x)\,,

(1)

with
q=(u,d)^{T}
the quark-field doublet, taken between on-shell nucleon states of equal four-momenta
p
(
p^{2}=m_{N}^{2}
), can be written as

\langle N(p)|A_{\mu}^{a}(0)|N(p)\rangle=\bar{u}(p)g_{A}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\frac{\tau^{a}}{\displaystyle 2}u(p)\,.

(2)

The isovector character of the current is manifest given the presence of the Pauli isospin matrices
\tau^{a}
.

We calculate the axial charge
g_{A}
employing relativistic BChPT with pions, nucleons and
\Delta
as degrees of freedom. We use the standard power counting
Weinberg 1991
, extended to diagrams with
\Delta
baryons following small scale expansion (SSE) of Refs.
Hemmert et al. 1997
;
Hemmert et al. 1998
. In this power counting, the mass difference
\delta=m_{N}-m_{\Delta}
is
\mathcal{O}(p)
.

In order to absorb the ultraviolet divergencies generated by loops, we perform dimensional renormalization in the

at the scale
\mu=\mathring{m}

\mathring{m}

Yao et al. 2016
;
Yao et al. 2017

\delta

\mathring{g}_{A}

\Lambda^{n-1}\,c_{n}\sim 1
, encoded in Gaussian priors

\chi^{2}_{\rm prior}=\sum_{\text{free LECs}}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{n-1}\,c_{n}}{5}\right)^{2}\,.

(13)

Here,

\mathcal{O}(p^{n})
; the breakdown scale is set to
\Lambda=1
GeV
\sim\,4\pi F_{\pi}

Manohar and Georgi 1984

Scherer and Schindler 2012

\mathring{g}_{A}

The large relative contribution of
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
terms discussed in the previous section and illustrated by Fig.
3
is a clear indication that the theoretical error associated with truncation of the perturbative expansion should be taken into account. We follow the method proposed in Ref.
Epelbaum et al. 2015
. Let
X
be an observable with a chiral perturbative expansion

X=X^{(0)}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Delta X^{(m)}\,;

(14)

\Delta X^{(m)}=X^{(m)}-X^{(m-1)}
encompasses all the monomials that start at order
m
. If
X
is calculated up to order
n
,
X\approx X^{(n)}
, assuming that the truncation error is dominated by order
n+1
, its contribution
\Delta X^{(n+1)}
can be estimated in a conservative way as
Epelbaum et al. 2015
;
Siemens et al. 2016

|\Delta X^{(n+1)}|=\max\left\{Q^{n+1}|X^{(0)}|,Q^{n}|\Delta X^{(1)}|,...,Q|\Delta X^{(n)}|\right\}\,,

(15)

where
Q
denotes the expansion variable.

For
g_{A}
,
Q=M_{\pi}/\Lambda
. Recalling that in this case
\mathcal{O}(p^{5})=\mathcal{O}(M_{\pi}^{4})
, for our
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
calculation, Eq. (
15
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta g_{A\chi}^{(5)}=

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\frac{M_{\pi}}{\Lambda}\right)^{4}\left|\mathring{g}_{A}\right|,\left(\frac{M_{\pi}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2}\left|\Delta g_{A}^{(3)}\right|,\frac{M_{\pi}}{\Lambda}|\Delta g_{A}^{(4)}|\right\}\sim Q^{4}=\mathcal{O}(p^{5})\,.

(16)

Connecting to Eq. (
9
),
\Delta g_{A}^{(3)}=4d_{16}M_{\pi}^{2}+g_{A\rm{(loop)}}^{(3)}
and
\Delta g_{A}^{(4)}=g_{A\rm{(loop)}}^{(4)}
. Based on the results in Sec.
III
it is easy to foresee that at larger
M_{\pi}
,
\Delta g_{A\chi}^{(5)}
will be determined by the last term in Eq. (
16
). In our
\mathcal{O}(p^{3})
fits we do not assume any prior knowledge about
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
and, therefore, the truncation error is given by

\displaystyle\Delta g_{A\chi}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\frac{M_{\pi}}{\Lambda}\right)^{3}\left|\mathring{g}_{A}\right|,\frac{M_{\pi}}{\Lambda}\left|\Delta g_{A}^{(3)}\right|\right\}\sim Q^{3}=\mathcal{O}(p^{4})\,.

(17)

In our
\chi^{2}
, Eq. (
11
), this theoretical error is added in quadrature to the one of LQCD points:

(\Delta g^{i}_{A})^{2}=(\Delta g^{i}_{A\rm{LQCD}})^{2}+(\Delta g_{A\chi}(M_{\pi}^{i}))^{2}\,.

(18)

This recipe assigns larger uncertainties to points at high
M_{\pi}
, where the convergence of the chiral expansion is poorer, therefore reducing their impact on the fit. We should also mention that, as discussed in Refs.
Epelbaum et al. 2015
;
Siemens et al. 2016
;
Furnstahl et al. 2015
, although this theoretical error estimate does not have a clear statistical interpretation, results are similar to those obtained in a preferable but beyond the scope of the present study Bayesian approach
Wesolowski et al. 2016
.

Fits are carried out iteratively until convergence is achieved. The first minimization is performed without theoretical errors, which are subsequently evaluated using the LECs determined in the previous step. The lattice discretization parameters
x_{j}
are fixed in the first iteration because evolving them results in overfitting. With the described procedure, LQCD and truncation errors are not independent and it is not obvious how to combine them in the error for a given observable. Consequently, following Ref.
Siemens et al. 2016
, we plot them separately in the error bands for
g_{A}(M_{\pi})
. Furthermore, as a quantitative measure of the agreement of our best-fit curve with the LQCD data, we also provide the
\chi^{2}_{0}
value, defined as

\chi^{2}_{0}=\sum_{i}\frac{\left(g_{A}(M^{i}_{\pi},a_{i})-g^{i}_{A}\right)^{2}}{(\Delta g^{i}_{A\rm{LQCD}})^{2}}\,.

(19)

Finally, we have tested the convergence range of our calculation by varying the maximum
M_{\pi}
of the lattice data included in the fits in the range of
M_{\pi\rm cut}\in[200,402]
MeV. A plateau in the
\chi^{2}

V
Conclusions and outlook

We have investigated the pion mass dependence of the nucleon axial coupling up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})\equiv\mathcal{O}(M_{\pi}^{3})
(NNLO) in relativistic BChPT with EOMS renormalization. We have shown that, at this order and without including
\Delta(1232)
explicitly, the pion mass dependence of
g_{A}
obtained using LECs extracted from phenomenological analyses of pion-nucleon elastic and inelastic (
\pi N\rightarrow\pi\pi N
) scattering cannot describe the rather flat behavior predicted by state-of-the-art LQCD simulations. The disagreement is manifest from pion masses right above the physical point.
This feature, earlier observed in non-relativistic heavy-baryon ChPT
Bernard and Meissner 2006
;
Procura et al. 2007
, is therefore also present in the relativistic theory. The fact that
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
terms become large from relatively low
M_{\pi}\gtrsim 200
MeV implies that
\mathcal{O}(p^{3})
analyses of
g_{A}(M_{\pi})
likely underestimate theoretical uncertainties.

In line with the findings of Ref.
Procura et al. 2007
within heavy-baryon ChPT, we can satisfactorily describe the LQCD data for
g_{A}(M_{\pi})
at
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
only after the
\Delta
is incorporated as an explicit degree of freedom. However, although in a much smaller degree than in the
\not{\Delta}
case, a fast increase in the relative size of
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
terms with
M_{\pi}
is observed and reflected by the estimate of theoretical uncertainties. This fact jeopardizes the precision of the ChPT description of
g_{A}(M_{\pi})
at
M_{\pi}\gtrsim 300
MeV and negatively influences the extraction of LECs based on LQCD data at such
M_{\pi}
. Together with the sizable correlations, which can be reduced by considering both the
M_{\pi}
and
q^{2}
dependence of the axial form factor in the fits, this feature implies that heavier resonances and/or
\mathcal{O}(p^{5})
terms are still required to reach a good convergence and reduce theoretical errors. The impact of setting the baryon masses in the loops to the values obtained by the LQCD simulations may be also worth exploring in view of the results of Ref.
Lutz et al. 2020
although this would correspond to the resummation of baryon selfenergy insertions of higher order. For this purpose it would be relevant to have more information about the
\Delta
pole position for the different lattice ensembles.

From our
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
analysis of recent LQCD data we have obtained
g_{A}(M_{\pi\rm(phys)})=1.260\pm 0.012
, close to the experimental value, and
d_{16}=-0.88\pm 0.88
GeV
-2
, in agreement with
\pi N
phenomenology. As a consequence of the previously discussed issues, errors are still large, particularly for
d_{16}
that is naturally extracted from the light-quark mass dependence of the nucleon axial coupling. New LQCD results at
M_{\pi}\lesssim 250
MeV will improve the precision of the extracted LECs. Besides, our ongoing effort to extend the analysis to the whole axial form factor (at low
q^{2})
may shed more light on
d_{16}
, as well as other LECs such as
d_{22}
.

Appendix A
LEC conversion

The covariant renormalization prescription adopted in Refs.
Siemens et al. 2016
;
Siemens et al. 2017
differs from EOMS. In EOMS only power-counting breaking terms are absorbed in the LECs. To obtain an equivalence between covariant and heavy-baryon results, the prescription of Ref.
Siemens et al. 2016
also subtracts infrared-regular terms at the order of the calculation, as well as pieces proportional to
\log(\mpi^2/m_N^2)
. After setting
\bar{\lambda}=0
and
\mu=m_{N}
in Eq. (21) of Ref.
Siemens et al. 2016
one is left with a dimensionally regularised LEC,

INERTIAL MODIFIED S-ITERATION PROCESS FOR SPLIT MONOTONE INCLUSION AND FIXED POINT PROBLEM IN REAL HILBERT SPACE

Abstract.

In this article, we present a modified S-iteration process that we combine with inertial extrapolation to find a common solution to the split monotone inclusion problem and the fixed point problem in real Hilbert space. Our goal is to establish a strong convergence theorem for approximating a common solution. Under some mild conditions, the problem can be solved. We also provide a numerical example to show that our algorithm’s acceleration works well.

Keywords.

Split monotone inclusion problem; Inertial extrapolation; Modified S-iteration; Nonexpansive mappings; Fixed point problem

Let

(ii)

\alpha

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}x-\mathcal{S}y,x-y\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}~~~~~\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H};

(iii)

\beta

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}x-\mathcal{S}y,x-y\rangle\geq\beta\|\mathcal{S}x-\mathcal{S}y\|^{2}~~~~~\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H};

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}x-\mathcal{S}y,x-y\rangle\geq\|\mathcal{S}x-\mathcal{S}y\|^{2}~~~~~\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H};

2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{n}-x^{*}\|,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n}-x^{*}\|,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n}-x^{*}\|\leq c,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(1-\alpha_{n})(\mathcal{S}y_{n}-x^{*})+\alpha_{n}(\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-x^{*})\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n+1}-x^{*}\|=c.

(3.16)

Applying lemma
2.1
in Eq
(\ref{3.17})
and
(\ref{3.18})
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-w_{n}\|=0

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(y_{n})-\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})\|=0

(3.18)

However, we know that
y_{n}-w_{n}=\beta_{n}(\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-w_{n})
and
w_{n}-x_{n}=\theta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})

which yeild

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n}-w_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-w_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-w_{n}\|=0.

(3.19)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|w_{n}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{n}\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|=0.

(3.20)

Note that by (D3) and
\theta_{n}\rightarrow 0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-w_{n}\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{n}\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|=0.

(3.21)

It follows that, by (
3.18
), (
3.19
), (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and the nonexpansiveness of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(x_{n})-\mathcal{S}(w_{n})\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-w_{n}\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-x_{n}\|=0.

(3.22)

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n})-\mathfrak{J}(y_{n})\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-\mathcal{S}(w_{n})\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(y_{n})-\mathcal{S}(w_{n})\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(w_{n})-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-w_{n}\|+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|w_{n}-y_{n}\|=0.

(3.23)

Therefore,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|=0=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|
as desired.
∎

.

\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}

\Sigma=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S})\bigcap\Omega\neq\phi

\{x_{n}\}
in algorithm
3.1
converges weakly to a common fixed point of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

x^{*}\in\Sigma
. By Theorem (
3.1
)(
1
),
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-x^{*}\|

\{x_{n}\}

\{x_{n_{k}}\}
and

\{x_{n_{j}}\}

\{x_{n}\}
with the two weak limits
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, respectively. By theorem
3.1
(2),
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n_{k}}-\mathcal{S}(x_{n_{k}})\|=0
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n_{k}}-\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n_{k}})\|=0
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{S}(x_{n_{j}})\|=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n_{j}})\|=0
. By Lemma (
2.5
),
\mathcal{S}(q_{i})=q_{i},\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(q_{i})=q_{i}
for

Theorem 3.3
.

let
\mathcal{H}
is a uniformly convex Hilbert space, Suppose that
\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are two nonexapansive mappings with
\Sigma=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S})\bigcap\Omega\neq\phi
and satisfy the
condition (B)
. Then the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in (
3.1
) converges strongly to a common fixed point of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
y\in\Sigma
. Now by
(\ref{3.8})
, we get

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n+1}-y\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|w_{n}-y\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{-\theta_{n}\|x_{n-1}-y\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}+\theta_{n}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{-\theta_{n}\|x_{n-1}-y\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}+\theta_{n}[\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{-\theta_{n}\|x_{n-1}-y\|^{2}\}

\displaystyle+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}]\{\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}\}

(3.24)

Denote
\Psi_{n}:=\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}
.Then (
3.24
) becomes

\displaystyle\Psi_{n+1}\leq\Psi_{n}+\theta_{n}(\Psi_{n}-\Psi_{n-1})+\delta_{n},

(3.25)

where
\delta_{n}=\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}
. Observe that by (D1),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{n}(1+\theta_{n})(2K)^{2}<\infty.

(3.26)

By Lemma
2.3
(2), there exist
\Psi^{*}\in[0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{n}=\Psi^{*}
.

That is,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|^{2}\}
exists. Therefore,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}
exists. Since
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathfrak{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
satisfy
condition B
, by Theorem
3.1
(
1
) it implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f\big(inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}\big)=0

and, thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}=0

So, we can find a subsequence
\{x_{n_{j}}\}
of
\{x_{n}\}
and a sequence
\{x^{*}_{j}\}\subset\Sigma
satisfying
\|x_{n_{j}}-x^{*}_{j}\|<\frac{1}{2^{j}}
. Next we will show that
\{x^{*}_{j}\}
is a cauchy sequence. Let
\epsilon>0
. Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}=0
, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\inf_{y\in\Sigma}<\frac{\epsilon}{6}
for all
n\geq N
. For all
m,n\geq N
, we have

\displaystyle\|x_{m}-x_{n}\|\leq\|x_{m}-y\|+\|x_{n}-y\|

for all
y\in\Sigma
.Thus,

\displaystyle\|x_{m}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{m}-y\|+\|x_{n}-y\|\}=\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{m}-y\|\}+\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}<\frac{\epsilon}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{6}=\frac{\epsilon}{3}

for all
\rm m,n\geq N
. Also, there is
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{1}{2^{j_{0}}}<\frac{\epsilon}{3}
. Choose
\rm M=max\{N,j_{0}\}
. Then, for all
j>k\geq M
, we have

\displaystyle\|x^{*}_{j}-x^{*}_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{*}_{j}-x_{n_{j}}\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-x_{n_{k}}\|+\|x_{n_{k}}-x^{*}_{k}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{6}+\frac{\epsilon}{6}=\frac{\epsilon}{3}=\epsilon.

Therefore,
\{x^{*}_{j}\}
is a cauchy sequence and so there exist
q\in\mathcal{H}
such that
x^{*}_{j}
converges to q. Since
\Sigma
is closed,

. As a result , we see that
x_{n_{j}}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-q\|

3.1
(1), the conclusion follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
is a uniformly convex Hilbert space, Suppose that
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are two nonexpansive mapping with
\Sigma=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S})\cap\Omega\neq\phi
and
\mathcal{S}
is semicompact. Then the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in (
3.1
)converges strongly to a common fixed point of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

From theorem
3.1
,
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded and
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-\mathcal{S}(x_{n})\|=0=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}\|
. By the semicompactness of
\mathcal{S}
, there exists
q\in\mathcal{H}
and a subsequence
\{x_{n_{j}}\}

of
\{x_{n}\}
such that
x_{n_{j}}\rightarrow q
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\|q-\mathcal{S}(q)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|q-x_{n_{j}}\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{S}(x_{n_{j}})\|+\|\mathcal{S}(x_{n_{j}})-\mathcal{S}(q)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|q-x_{n_{j}}\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{S}(x_{n_{j}})\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{S}(q)\|

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0~~~as~~~j\rightarrow\infty.

(3.27)

Also,

\displaystyle\|q-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(q)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|q-x_{n_{j}}\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n_{j}})\|+\|\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n_{j}})-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(q)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|q-x_{n_{j}}\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n_{j}})\|+\|x_{n_{j}}-\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}(q)\|

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0~~~as~~~j\rightarrow\infty.

(3.28)

Thus
q\in\Sigma
. As in the proof of the theorem
3.3
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}
exists. We observe that
\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n_{j}}-y\|\}\leq\|x_{n_{j}}-q\|\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty,
hence
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{y\in\Sigma}\{\|x_{n}-y\|\}=0
. It follows, as in the proof theorem
3.3
, that
\{x_{n}\}
converges strongly a common fixed point
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Conclusion:

In this paper, we find a common solution of split monotone inclusion problem and fixed point problem by modified S-iteration process combining with inertial step. Weak and strong convergence theorems are established under standard assumptions imposed on cost operators and mappings. Finally, several preliminary, numerical experiments have also been performed to illustrate the convergence of the proposed algorithms.

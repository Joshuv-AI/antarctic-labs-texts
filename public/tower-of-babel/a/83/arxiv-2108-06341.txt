Interpolation between noncommutative martingale Hardy and BMO spaces: the case 𝟎 < 𝐩 < 𝟏 \mathbf{0<p<1}
Narcisse Randrianantoanina

Abstract
. Let ℳ \mathcal{M} be a semifinite von Nemann algebra equipped with an increasing filtration ( ℳ n ) n ≥ 1 (\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1} of (semifinite) von Neumann subalgebras of ℳ \mathcal{M} . For 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty , let 𝗁 p c ​ ( ℳ ) \mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}) denote the noncommutative column conditioned martingale Hardy space and 𝖻𝗆𝗈 c ​ ( ℳ ) \mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}) denote the column “little” martingale BMO space associated with the filtration ( ℳ n ) n ≥ 1 (\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1} . We prove the following real interpolation identity: if 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty and 0 < θ < 1 0<\theta<1 , then for 1 / r = ( 1 − θ ) / p 1/r=(1-\theta)/p , ( 𝗁 p c ​ ( ℳ ) , 𝖻𝗆𝗈 c ​ ( ℳ ) ) θ , r = 𝗁 r c ​ ( ℳ ) , \big(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,r}=\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M}), with equivalent quasi norms. For the case of complex interpolation, we obtain that if 0 < p < q < ∞ 0<p<q<\infty and 0 < θ < 1 0<\theta<1 , then for 1 / r = ( 1 − θ ) / p + θ / q 1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q , [ 𝗁 p c ​ ( ℳ ) , 𝗁 q c ​ ( ℳ ) ] θ = 𝗁 r c ​ ( ℳ ) \big[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big]_{\theta}=\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M}) with equivalent quasi norms. These extend previously known results from p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 to the full range 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty . Other related spaces such as spaces of adapted sequences and Junge’s noncommutative conditioned L p L_{p} -spaces are also shown to form interpolation scale for the full range 0 < p < ∞ 0<p<\infty when either the real method or the complex method is used. Our method of proof is based on a new algebraic atomic decomposition for Orlicz space version of Junge’s noncommutative conditioned L p L_{p} -spaces. We apply these results to derive various inequalities for martingales in noncommutative symmetric quasi-Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction

Hardy space theory takes on many forms and appears in many aspects of mathematics such as harmonic analysis, PDE’s, functional analysis, probability theory, and many others. Interpolation spaces between Hardy spaces in various contexts have a long history. We refer to the articles
[
13
,
23
,
35
]
for some background on
interpolations between classical Hardy spaces from harmonic analysis and
[
18
,
47
]
for interpolations between Hardy spaces from martingale theory. On the other hand, the theory of noncommutative martingales has seen rapid development in many directions. Indeed, since the establishment of the noncommutative Burkholder-Gundy inequalities in
[
37
]
, many classical inequalities are now understood for this context. We refer to the book
[
36
, Chap. 14]
for a summary of some of the main inequalities from noncommutative martingale theory.
Further references relevant to our purpose are
[
1
,
7
,
24
,
26
,
32
,
44
]
.
The main focus of the present article is on Hardy spaces arising from noncommutative martingale theory. More specifically, column/row Hardy spaces defined from column/row conditioned square functions initiated by Junge and Xu in
[
26
]
in connection with the noncommutative Burkholder/Rosenthal inequalities. These spaces are generally referred to as conditioned Hardy spaces and its column (resp. row) version is usually denoted by
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
(resp.
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{r}
). We would like to emphasize that this particular class of martingale Hardy spaces is instrumental in classical theory. We refer to the monograph
[
47
]
for more in depth treatment of the classical setting. Likewise, the Hardy spaces
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
and
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{r}
are proven to be fundamental objects in various aspects of the new progress made in the noncommutative martingale theory during the last several years. For instance, the formulation of the noncommutative Burkholder inequality for the case
1<p<2
was mainly due to a reformulation of the case
2\leq p<\infty
as equivalence of norms involving the spaces
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
and
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{r}
. The asymmetric Doob maximal inequalities in
[
17
]
were derived from properties of
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
and
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{r}
where
1\leq p<2
.
The general theme of the present article is on interpolation spaces for the quasi-Banach space couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c},\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c})
when
0<p<q<\infty
.

To motivate our consideration, let us review some interpolation results from classical martingale theory. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence of
\sigma
-subfields of
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the condition
\mathcal{F}=\sigma(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{n})
.
For
0<p\leq\infty
, denote by
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\Omega)
(resp.
\mathsf{h}_{p}(\Omega)
) the martingale Hardy space defined by square functions (resp. conditioned square functions) and
{\mathcal{BMO}}(\Omega)
(resp.
\mathsf{bmo}(\Omega)
) the martingale BMO (resp. martingale little BMO) space associated with the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. We refer to
[
14
,
47
]
for precise definitions and properties of these spaces along with discussions on their importance for the classical theory. It is well established in the literature that the spaces
{\mathcal{BMO}}(\Omega)
and
\mathsf{bmo}(\Omega)
play important role in interpolation
theory as they may be used as natural substitutes for
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Our motivation comes from the following three classical results that involved martingale BMO spaces as one of the endpoints of interpolations:

(1.1)

\big[\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Omega),{\mathcal{BMO}}(\Omega)\big]_{\theta}=\mathcal{H}_{r}(\Omega),\quad 0<\theta<1\ \text{and}\ \frac{1}{r}=1-\theta;

(1.2)

\big[\mathcal{H}_{p}(\Omega),{\mathcal{BMO}}(\Omega)\big]_{\theta}\subsetneq\mathcal{H}_{r}(\Omega),\quad 0<p<1,\ 0<\theta<1,\ \text{and}\ \frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{p}<1;

(1.3)

\big(\mathsf{h}_{p}(\Omega),\mathsf{bmo}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,r}=\mathsf{h}_{r}(\Omega),\quad 0<p<\infty,\ 0<\theta<1,\ \text{and}\ \frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{p},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
(resp.
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,r}
) denotes the complex (resp. real) interpolation method. The identity (
1.1
) and the inclusion (
1.2
) were obtained by Janson and Jones in
[
18
]
while (
1.3
) was established in its present form by Weisz in
[
46
]
.
It is a natural question to consider if the three assertions stated above still hold for the noncommutative setting. The first result in this direction is due to Musat in
[
32
]
who proved a noncommutative analogue of (
1.1
) where as introduced in
[
37
]
, the noncommutative Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is the sum of the column version and the row version and the BMO space is defined as the intersection of column BMO and the row BMO. Later, Bekjan
et al.
established in
[
1
]
that the noncommutative analogue of (
1.3
) holds for the Banach space range. That is, (
1.3
) remains valid for the interpolation couple
(\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c},\mathsf{bmo}^{c})
. To the best of our knowledge,
[
1
]
and
[
32
]
are the only articles available in the literature that contain substantial advances in the study of interpolation spaces of noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces to date.
The present paper extends the interpolation results from
[
1
]
to the case
0<p<1
thus providing a full noncommutative generalization of (
1.3
) for column/row spaces. We refer to Theorem
3.5
below for detailed formulation. We also obtained interpolation spaces between spaces of adapted sequences which may be viewed as the right substitute for (
1.1
) when
0<p<1
. This is stated in Theorem
3.13
. We should point out that the result on the spaces of adapted sequences appears to be new even for the classical setting. Moreover, the noncommutative analogue of (
1.2
) can be easily deduced from the result on adapted sequences.

Our method of proof is very different from
[
1
,
32
]
. In fact, the techniques used in both
[
1
]
and
[
32
]
appear to work only for Banach couples. Our main objective is to compare
K
-functionals for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c},\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c})
where
0<p<q<\infty
to those associated to the well-known couple
(L_{p},L_{q})
associated with appropriate amplified von Neumann algebras. In the classical setting, this type of reduction is usually achieved through some strategic use of stopped martingales. We note that a the time of this writing there is no direct analogue of stopping times available for the noncommutative setting but the so-called Cuculescu projections (
[
8
]
) are often used as a substitute for stopping times. However,
Cuculescu projections do not appear to be efficient enough to provide the desired truncations. This makes our approach very
different from the classical setting as found in
[
18
,
46
]
. Our method is based on the so-called algebraic atomic decompositions. The notion of algebraic atoms were introduced by Perrin in
[
33
]
for Junge’s noncommutative conditioned
L_{p}
spaces
and the Hardy spaces
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
for
1\leq p<2
and found to be instrumental in the study of Doob’s maximal inequality for martingale in noncommutative Hardy spaces (
[
17
]
). Algebraic atomic decompositions for the Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}
with
0<p<1
was recently studied in
[
7
]
by constructive approach.
Another recent development in this direction is that the noncommutative Orlicz-Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
admits algebraic atomic decomposition for convex function
\Phi
satisfying some natural conditions. Using insights from the constructive approach used in
[
7
]
and
[
44
]
, we established that noncommutative conditioned Orlicz-Hardy spaces
admit version of algebraic atomic decompositions when the Orlicz function is
p
-convex and
q
-concave for
0<p<q<2
(see Theorem
2.8
for details). These more general atomic decompositions turn out to be one of the decisive tools we used for our results on
K
-functionals for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c},\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c})
when the distance between
p
and
q
is sufficiently small. The results on
K
-functionals coupled with the well-known Wolff’s interpolation theorem provide the full noncommutative generalization of (
1.3
). The case of spaces of adapted sequences is handle with the same techniques. That is, estimating the
K
-functionals through some version of algebraic atomic decompositions.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we review the basics of
noncommutative symmetric quasi-Banach spaces following the formulation of
[
28
,
50
]
and give full detailed accounts of the construction of noncommutative martingale conditioned Hardy spaces associated with symmetric quasi-Banach spaces. We also formulate and prove the algebraic decomposition for noncommutative conditioned spaces associated with Orlicz function spaces and for spaces of adapted sequences.

Section 3 contains formulations and proofs of our principal results: noncommutative generalization of (
1.2
), noncommutative generalization of (
1.3
), and an extension of (
1.1
) to the full range when spaces of adapted sequences are used.

In Section 4, we explore some further applications of our results and methods from Section 2 and Section 3 to various inequalities involving noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces in the context of general symmetric spaces of measurable operators.

2.
Definitions and preliminary results

Throughout, we adopt the notation
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
to indicate that there is a constant
C_{\alpha}
depending only on the parameter
\alpha
such that the inequality
A\leq C_{\alpha}B
is satisfied. Similarly,
A\approx_{\alpha}B
is used if both
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
and
B\lesssim_{\alpha}A
hold.

2.1.
Noncommutative symmetric spaces

Throughout,
\mathcal{M}
denotes a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
denote the associated topological
*
-algebra of
\tau
-measurable operators in the sense of
[
12
]
. For
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we recall that its
generalized singular number

\mu(x)

is the real-valued function defined by

\mu_{t}(x)=\inf\big\{s>0:\tau\big(\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)\big)\leq t\big\},\quad t>0,

where
\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)
is the spectral projection of
|x|
associated with the interval
(s,\infty)
.
We observe that
if
\mathcal{M}
is the abelian von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
with the trace given by integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
becomes the space of measurable complex functions on
(0,\infty)
which are bounded except on a set of finite measure and for
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
\mu(f)
is precisely the usual decreasing rearrangement of the function
|f|
. We refer to
[
38
]
for more information on noncommutative integration.

We denote by
L_{0}
, the space of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\infty)
.
Recall that a quasi-Banach function space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\infty)
is called
symmetric
if for any
g\in E
and any
f\in L_{0}
with
\mu(f)\leq\mu(g)
, we have
f\in E
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq\|g\|_{E}
. Throughout, all function spaces are assumed to be defined on the interval
(0,\infty)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space. We define the corresponding noncommutative space by setting:

E(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\Big\{x\in\widetilde{M}\ :\ \mu(x)\in E\Big\}.

Equipped with the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}:=\|\mu(x)\|_{E}
, the linear space
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
becomes a complex quasi-Banach space (
[
28
,
50
]
) and is usually referred to as the
noncommutative symmetric space
associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
corresponding to
E
.
We remark that if
0<p<\infty
and
E=L_{p}
, then
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is exactly the usual noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. In the sequel,
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
will be abbreviated to
E(\mathcal{M})
.

Other classes of examples that are relevant for our purpose are the class of Orlicz spaces and the class of Lorentz spaces. We review these two classes for convenience.

A function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called an
Orlicz function
whenever it is strictly increasing, continuous,
\Phi(0)=0
, and
\lim_{u\to\infty}\Phi(u)=\infty
.
The
Orlicz space

L_{\Phi}
is the collection of all
f\in L_{0}
for which
there exists a constant
c
such that
I_{\Phi}(|f|/c)<\infty
where the modular functional
I_{\Phi}(\cdot)
is defined by:

I_{\Phi}(|g|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(|g(t)|)\ dt,\quad g\in L_{0}.

The space
L_{\Phi}
is equipped with the Luxemburg quasi-norm:

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}=\inf\big\{c>0:I_{\Phi}(|f|/c)\leq 1\big\}.

If
\Phi
is convex then
L_{\Phi}
is a symmetric Banach function space. However, we do not restrict ourselves to just the case of normed spaces.
We refer to
[
45
]
for Orlicz spaces associated with non necessarily convex functions.

We recall that for
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\Phi
is called
p
-convex
(resp.,
q
-concave
) if the function
t\mapsto\Phi(t^{1/p})
(resp.,
t\mapsto\Phi(t^{1/q})
) is convex (resp., concave).
Below, we only consider Orlicz spaces associated with Orlicz functions that are
p
-convex and
q
-concave for some
0<p\leq q<2
.

For the next relevant example, assume that
0<p,q\leq\infty
. The
Lorentz space

L_{p,q}
is the space of all
f\in L_{0}
for which
\|f\|_{p,q}<\infty
where

\big\|f\big\|_{p,q}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{t}^{q}(f)\ d(t^{q/p})}\right)^{1/q},&0<q<\infty;\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}\mu_{t}(f)},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

If
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
, then
L_{p,q}
is a symmetric Banach function space. If
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq q\leq\infty
, then
L_{p,q}
can be equivalently renormed to become a symmetric Banach function (
[
2
, Theorem 4.6]
). In general,
L_{p,q}
is only a symmetric quasi-Banach function space.

Both noncommutative Orlicz spaces and noncommutative Lorentz spaces will be heavily involved throughout the paper.

2.2.
Martingale Hardy spaces and conditioned spaces

In the sequel, we always denote by
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
an
increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
{\mathcal{M}}

whose union is w*-dense in

\mathcal{M}
. For every
n\geq 1
, we assume that there is a trace preserving conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{n}

from
{\mathcal{M}}
onto
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
is called
a
noncommutative martingale
with respect to
({\mathcal{M}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
if
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{n+1})=x_{n}
for every
n\geq 1
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space and
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a martingale. If for every
n\geq 1
,
x_{n}\in E(\mathcal{M}_{n})
, then we say that
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an
E(\mathcal{M})
-martingale. In this case, we set

\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|x_{n}\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

If
\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}<\infty
, then
x
will be called
a bounded
E(\mathcal{M})
-martingale.

For a martingale
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we set
dx_{n}=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
with the usual convention that
x_{0}=0
. The sequence
dx=(dx_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is called the
martingale difference sequence
of
x
. A martingale
x
is called a
finite martingale
if there exists
N
such that
dx_{n}=0
for all
n\geq N
.

Let us now review some basic definitions related to martingale Hardy spaces associated to noncommutative symmetric spaces.

Following
[
37
]
, we define the
column square functions
of a given
martingale
x=(x_{k})
by setting:

S_{c,n}(x)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad S_{c}(x)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

The conditioned versions were introduced in
[
26
]
. For a given
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
-martingale
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, we set

s_{c,n}(x)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad s_{c}(x)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

The operator
s_{c}(x)
is called the
column conditioned square function
of
x
.
For convenience, we will use the notation

\mathcal{S}_{c,n}(a)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}|a_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|a_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

and

\sigma_{c,n}(b)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|b_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad\sigma_{c}(b)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|b_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for sequences
a=(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
and
b=(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
that are not necessarily martingale difference sequences.
It is worth pointing out that the infinite sums of positive operators stated above may not always make sense as operators but we only consider below special cases where they do converge in the sense of the measure topology.

We will now describe various noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces associated with symmetric quasi-Banach function spaces.

Assume that
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{E}
the collection of all finite martingales in
E(\mathcal{M})
.
For
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\mathcal{F}_{E}
, we set:

\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}}=\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\,.

Then
(\mathcal{F}_{E},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}})
is a quasi-normed space.
If we denote by
(e_{i,j})_{i,j\geq 1}
the family of unit matrices in
B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
, then
the correspondence
x\mapsto\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}\otimes e_{k,1}
maps
\mathcal{F}_{E}
isometrically into
a (not necessarily closed) linear subspace of
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
.
We define the column Hardy space

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
to be the completion of
(\mathcal{F}_{E},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}})
. It then follows that

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
embeds isometrically into a closed subspace of the quasi-Banach space
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
.

In the sequel, we will also make use of the more general space
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
which is defined as the set of all sequences
a=(a_{k})
in
E(\mathcal{M})
for which
\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)\in E(\mathcal{M})
. In this case, we set

\big\|a\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Under the above quasi-norm, one can easily see that
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is a quasi-Banach space.
The closed subspace of
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
consisting of adapted sequences will be denoted by
E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. That is,

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\Big\{(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}):\forall n\geq 1,a_{n}\in E(\mathcal{M}_{n})\Big\}.

Note that for
1<p<\infty
, it follows from the noncommutative Stein inequality that
L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is a complemented subspace of
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. One should not expect such complementation if one merely assume that
E
is quasi-Banach symmetric function space.

Next, we will discuss conditioned versions of the spaces defined earlier.
Consider the linear space
\mathcal{FS}
consisting of all
x\in\mathcal{M}
such that there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\tau(e)<\infty
, and
x=exe
. We should note that if
\mathcal{M}
is finite, then
\mathcal{FS}=\mathcal{M}
.
For every
n\geq 1
and
0<p\leq\infty
, we define the space
L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})
to be the completion of
\mathcal{FS}
with respect to the quasi-norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})}=\big\|\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)\big\|_{p/2}^{1/2}.

We would like to emphasize here that if
x=exe\in\mathcal{FS}
is as described above, then
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)=e\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)e
is a well-defined operator in
\mathcal{M}
and since
\tau(e)<\infty
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)\in L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})
.

According to
[
24
]
, for every
0<p\leq\infty
, there exists an isometric right
\mathcal{M}_{n}
-module map
u_{n,p}:L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n};\ell_{2}^{c})
such that

(2.1)

u_{n,p}(x)^{*}u_{n,q}(y)=\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}y)\otimes e_{1,1},

whenever
x\in L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{n})
,
y\in L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{n})
, and
1/p+1/q\leq 1
.
An important fact about these maps is that they are independent of
p
as the index
p
in the presentation of
[
24
]
was only needed to accommodate the non-tracial case. Below, we will simply use
u_{n}
for
u_{n,p}
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the range of
u_{n}
is complemented in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\ell_{2}^{c})
. In fact, as proved in
[
24
, Proposition 2.8(iii)]
,
there exists a contractive projection
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
from
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n};\ell_{2}^{c})
onto the range of
u_{n}
such that for every
\xi\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n};\ell_{2}^{c})
,

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\xi)^{*}\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\xi)\leq\xi^{*}\xi.

This fact will be used in the sequel.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the collection of all finite sequences
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{FS}
. For
0<p<\infty
, we defined the
conditioned space

L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
to be the completion of the linear space
\mathfrak{F}
with respect to the quasi-norm:

(2.2)

\big\|a\big\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|\sigma_{c}(a)\big\|_{p}

(here, we take
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{1}
).
According to
[
24
]
,
L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
can be isometrically embedded into an
L_{p}
-space associated to a semifinite von Neumann algebra by means of the following map:

U:L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))

defined by setting:

U((a_{n})_{n\geq 1})=\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n-1}(a_{n})\otimes e_{n,1},\quad(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}.

The range of
U
may be viewed as a double indexed sequences
(x_{n,k})
such that
x_{n,k}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for all
k\geq 1
. As an operator affiliated with
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
, this may be expressed as

\sum_{n,k}x_{n,k}\otimes e_{k,1}\otimes e_{n,1}
.
It is immediate from (
2.1
)
that if
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
, then

(2.3)

U((a_{n}))^{*}U((b_{n}))=\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(a_{n}^{*}b_{n})\big)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}.

In particular, if
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
then
\|(a_{n})\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|U((a_{n}))\|_{p}
and
hence
U
is indeed an isometry.

Now, we generalize the notion of conditioned spaces to the setting of symmetric spaces of operators. This is done in steps.

\bullet
Assume first that
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space satisfying
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}\subseteq E\subseteq L_{p}+L_{\infty}
for some
0<p<\infty
and
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
. This is the case for instance when
E
is a separable fully symmetric quasi-Banach function space. For a given
sequence
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
, we set:

\big\|(a_{n})\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|\sigma_{c}(a)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\big\|U((a_{n}))\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

This is well-defined and induces a quasi-norm on the linear space
\mathfrak{F}
. We define the quasi-Banach space
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
to be the completion of the quasi normed space
(\mathfrak{F},\|\cdot\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})})
. The space
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
will be called
the column conditioned space associated with
E
.
It is clear that

U
extends to an isometry from
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
into
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
which we will still denote by
U
.

Below, we use the notation
E^{0,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
for the closure of the linear space

\mathfrak{F}^{0}=\{a=(a_{n})\in\mathfrak{F}:a_{1}=0\}
in
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. One can easily see that for
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
, we have

\max\Big\{\big\|\mathcal{E}_{1}(|a_{1}|^{2})^{1/2}\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{1})},\big\|(a_{n})_{n\geq 2}\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\Big\}\leq\big\|(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

This shows that we have the direct sum

(2.4)

E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=E^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{1})\oplus E^{0,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

\bullet
Assume now that
E\subseteq L_{p}+L_{q}
for some
0<p,q<\infty
that is not necessarily separable. We set

E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\Big\{x\in(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}):U(x)\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))\Big\}

equipped with the quasi-norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|U(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

It is clear that
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
as defined is a linear quasi-normed space. We claim that it is complete. Indeed, if
(x_{\nu})_{\nu\geq 1}
is a Cauchy
sequence in
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, then it converges to some
x\in(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Since
(U(x_{\nu}))_{\nu\geq 1}
is also a Cauchy sequence in the quasi-Banach space
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
and
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))\subseteq(L_{p}+L_{q})(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
, it follows that
x\in E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
(x_{\nu})_{\nu\geq 1}
converges to
x
in
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. We should note here that if
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
then the above definition coincides with the one described in the previous bullet. We also define

E^{0,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=(L_{p}+L_{q})^{0,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

The direct sum stated in (
2.4
) still applies.

Remark 2.2
.

At the time of this writing, we do not know of any suitable definition for conditioned space associated with
L_{p}+L_{\infty}
when
0<p<2
. It is also unclear if our definition of
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
for non separable space
E\subset L_{p}+L_{q}
when
0<p<2
is independent of the isometry
U
.

We now recall the construction of column conditioned martingale Hardy spaces.
As in the conditioned spaces, we describe the noncommutative conditioned Hardy spaces in steps. Let
\mathfrak{F}(M)
be the collection of all finite martingale
(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}
for which
x_{N}\in\mathcal{FS}
. We can easily see that for every
0<p\leq\infty
,
\mathfrak{F}(M)\subseteq\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. As in the case of conditioned spaces,
\mathfrak{F}(M)
is dense in
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
when
0<p<\infty
.

\bullet
First, assume that
E\subseteq L_{2}+L_{\infty}
. In this case, column conditioned square functions are well-defined for bounded martingales in
E(\mathcal{M})
. We define

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
to be the collection of all bounded martingale
x
in
E(\mathcal{M})
for which
s_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M})
. We equip
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with the norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

One can easily verify that
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
is complete.

\bullet
Next, we consider quasi-Banach space
E
such that
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
for some
0<p<\infty
. This is the case if
E
is separable.
Let
x\in\mathfrak{F}(M)
. As noted above,
s_{c}(x)\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathcal{M}
. In particular,
s_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M})
. We equip
\mathfrak{F}(M)
with the quasi-norm

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\big\|(dx_{n})\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The column conditioned Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is the completion of
(\mathfrak{F}(M),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
. Clearly, the map
x\mapsto(dx_{n})
(from
\mathfrak{F}(M)
into

\mathfrak{F}
) extends to an isometry from
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
into
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
which we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{c}
. In particular,
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is isometrically isomorphic to a subspace of
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
via the isometry
U\mathcal{D}_{c}
. We should note here that if
L_{2}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
and
E\subseteq L_{2}+L_{\infty}
, then the two definitions provide the same space.

\bullet
Assume now that
E\subset L_{q}+L_{q}
for
0<p,q<\infty
. As in the case of conditioned spaces, we set

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\Big\{x\in\mathsf{h}_{L_{p}+L_{q}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}):U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))\Big\}

equipped with the quasi-norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

Since the operator
U
is independent of the index, one can easily see that the space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is independent of
p
and
q
. Moreover, one can verify as in the case of conditioned spaces that the quasi-normed space
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
is complete. Furthermore, if
E
is such that
L_{2}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
then
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
coincides with the one defined through completion considered in the second bullet.

The following fact will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<p<q<\infty
and assume that
E\subseteq L_{p}+L_{q}
.
The Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is
1
-complemented in
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. More precisely,
there is an onto map
\Pi:E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with

\|\Pi\|=1
and
\Pi{\mathcal{D}}_{c}
is the identity map in
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
and
b\in\mathcal{FS}
. Using the Kadison-Schwarz inequality for conditional expectations, we have

(2.5)

\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(|\mathcal{E}_{n}(b)-\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(b)|^{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(|b|^{2}).

We define
\Pi:\mathfrak{F}\to\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
by setting:

a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{n}(a_{n})-\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(a_{n}).

It follows from (
2.5
) that
s_{c}(\Pi(a))\leq\sigma_{c}(a)
for every
a\in\mathfrak{F}
.
If
\mathfrak{F}
is dense in
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, then
\Pi
extends to a bounded linear map from
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
onto
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with
\|\Pi:E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\|\leq 1
. Clearly, if
x\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we have
\Pi{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(x)=x
. This verifies the lemma for the case where
E
is separable.

For the general case,
we observe that from the fact that
\mathfrak{F}
is dense in
(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, the inequality on conditioned square functions above can be restated as:

(2.6)

\big|U{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(\Pi(y))\big|\leq\big|U(y)\big|,\quad y\in(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

If
y\in E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, then by definition
y\in(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
U(y)\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
.
By the separable case,
\Pi(y)\in\mathsf{h}_{L_{p}+L_{q}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, it follows from
(
2.6
) that
U{\mathcal{D}}(\Pi(y))\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. This means,
\Pi(y)\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with
\|\Pi(y)\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}\leq\|y\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
.
∎

We refer to
[
6
,
11
,
19
,
22
,
26
,
42
,
43
]
for more information on noncommutative Hardy spaces associated with symmetric spaces of measurable operators. In the sequel, noncommutative column Hardy spaces associated with Orlicz space
L_{\Phi}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
while those associated with the Lorentz space
L_{p,q}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{h}_{p,q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
.

We conclude this subsection with a description of the dual space of the Hardy scpace
\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. A martingale
x
belongs to the
column little bmo space
denoted by
\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
if

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{bmo}^{c}}=\max\Big\{\big\|x_{1}\big\|_{\infty};\sup_{m}\sup_{1\leq n\leq m}\big\|\mathcal{E}_{n}(|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2})\big\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\Big\}.

The Banach space
(\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{bmo}^{c}})
was introduced in
[
34
]
for finite case where it was shown that it coincides with the dual of the Hardy space

\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. The proof given there can be easily generalized to the semifinite case. We record this for further use:

(2.7)

(\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))^{*}=\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with equivalent norms.

2.3.
Atomic decompositions for martingale Orlicz-Hardy spaces

In this subsection, we will analyze conditioned spaces and conditioned Hardy spaces associated with Orlicz spaces. More specifically, we will describe a type of atomic decomposition in the context of Orlicz spaces. Results from this subsection play key role in the next section. Toward this end, we will start from setting up some notations.

Throughout this subsection, we always assume that
0<p\leq q<2
and
\Phi
is an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave. First, we fix a positive Borel measure
\mu
on the interval
[0,\infty)
so that:

(2.8)

\Phi(t)\approx_{p,q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{(ts)^{p},(ts)^{q}\}\ d\mu(s).

The existence of such integral representation was proved for convex functions (see the proof of
[
21
, Lemma 6.2]
). The argument given in
[
21
]
can be readily adjusted to include the more general case of
p
-convex functions when
0<p<1
.

Next, we fix an Orlicz function
\Theta
so that
L_{\Phi}
admits the factorization:

(2.9)

L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}

where the product
L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
is the collection of all function
f
that admit factorization
f=gh
with
g\in L_{2}
and
h\in L_{\Theta}
. The quasi-norm
on
L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
is given by:

\big\|f\big\|_{L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}}:=\inf\left\{\big\|g\big\|_{L_{2}}\big\|h\big\|_{L_{\Theta}}:g\in L_{2},h\in L_{\Theta},f=gh\right\}.

We note that the Orlicz function
\Theta
can be taken to be the inverse of the function
t\mapsto t^{-1/2}\Phi^{-1}(t)
for
t>0
. We refer to
[
31
]
for more details.

We will also make use of the following function:

(2.10)

\Psi(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{2-p}s^{-p}+t^{2-q}s^{-q})^{-1}\ d\mu(s)

where
\mu
is the positive Borel measure from the representation of
\Phi
in
(
2.8
). The reason for the consideration of
\Psi
is summarized in the next lemma. The first three items are straightforward generalizations of
[
44
, Proposition 3.3]
while the last item can be deduced as in
[
44
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

\Psi(t)\approx_{p,q}t^{-2}\Phi(t)
;

(ii)

\Theta\big(\Psi(t)^{-1/2}\big)\approx_{p,q}\Phi(t)
;

(iii)

t\mapsto\Psi(t^{1/2})
is operator monotone decreasing;

(iv)

for any increasing sequence of positive operators
a_{n}\uparrow a
, we have:

\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\big((a_{n+1}^{2}-a_{n}^{2})\Psi(a_{n+1})\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\tau\big(\Phi(a)\big).

We now introduce a concept of atoms for conditioned space constructed from the Orlicz function space
L_{\Phi}
.

Definition 2.5
.

A sequence
x\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is called
an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom
if it admits a factorization
x=\alpha\ .\ \beta
where

(i)

\alpha=\sum_{j<n}\alpha_{n,j}\otimes e_{n,j}
is a strictly lower triangular matrix in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
with

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}=\big(\sum_{j<n}\|\alpha_{n,j}\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq 1;

(ii)

\beta\in L_{\Theta}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
with
\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq 1
.

The above definition was motivated by the case of
L_{r}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
for
1\leq r<2
introduced for the first time in
[
33
]
and explored further in
[
17
]
. We also refer to
[
7
]
where the same concept was considered for the case of conditioned Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
for the range
0<r\leq 1
. More recently, the case of Orlicz-conditioned Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{\varphi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
associated with convex Orlicz function
\varphi
was formulated in
[
44
]
in the context of
\varphi
-moments.

We should note that since strictly lower triangular matrices were used in the definition of algebraic atoms, it follows that if
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom then
x_{1}=0
.

The next lemma can be deduced as in
the first part of
[
33
, Theorem 3.6.10]
using the factorization
L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
. We include the argument for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

Every algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom belongs to
L_{\Phi}^{0,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. More precisely,
if
x
is an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom then

\displaystyle{\|x\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq 1}
.

Proof.

Let
x=\alpha\ .\ \beta
be an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom. As observed earlier,
x_{1}=0
and for
n\geq 2
,

x_{n}=\sum_{j<n}\alpha_{n,j}\beta_{j}
.
Since
\beta
is adapted, it follows that

\mathcal{E}_{n-1}|x_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{m,j<n}\beta_{m}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,m}^{*}\alpha_{n,j})\beta_{j}.

From the property of the module map
u_{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n-1}|x_{n}|^{2}\otimes e_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,j<n}(\beta_{m}^{*}\otimes e_{1,1}).u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,m})^{*}u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j}).(\beta_{j}\otimes e_{1,1})

\displaystyle=|\sum_{j<n}u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j}).(\beta_{j}\otimes e_{1,1})|^{2}.

This implies that

\sigma_{c}^{2}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}=\sum_{n\geq 2}|\sum_{j<n}u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j}).(\beta_{j}\otimes e_{1,1})|^{2}.

We write further that

\sigma_{c}^{2}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}=|\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{j<n}[u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j}).(\beta_{j}\otimes e_{1,1})]\otimes e_{n,1}|^{2}.

This allows us to deduce that

\sigma_{c}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}=\big|\big(u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j})\big)_{j<n}\ .\ \sum_{k\geq 1}\beta_{k}\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{k,1}\big|

where the strictly lower triangular matrix
\widehat{\alpha}=(\big(u_{n-1}(\alpha_{n,j}))_{j<n}
takes its values in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
. We may view

\widehat{\alpha}
as an operator affiliated with
\mathfrak{M}=\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
. If
\mu(\cdot)
denote the generalized singular number relative to
\mathfrak{M}
equipped with its natural trace, then

\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}=\|\mu(\sigma_{c}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1})\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}.

It follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
that

\displaystyle\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu(\widehat{\alpha})\big\|_{2}.\big\|\mu\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\beta_{k}\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{k,1}\big)\big\|_{L_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\big\|\widehat{\alpha}\big\|_{2}.\big\|\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{j<n}\|\alpha_{n,j}\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}.\big\|\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}|\beta_{k}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L_{\Theta}}.

This proves that
\|x\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq 1
.
∎

Using the above notion of atoms, we may naturally consider the following concept of atomic decompositions:

Definition 2.7
.

A sequence
x\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is said to admit an
algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atomic decomposition
if

x=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a^{(k)},

where for each
k
,
a^{(k)}
is either an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom or
a^{(k)}
belongs to the unit ball of the conditioned space
L_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{1})
and
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<\infty
for
0<p<1
and
\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
for
1\leq p<2
. Since
\Phi
is
p
-convex and algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atoms belong to the unit ball of
L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, it follows that if
x
admits an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atomic decomposition then it belongs to
L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

Following
[
33
]
, the corresponding
algebraic atomic column conditioned space

L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is defined to be the completion of the space of all
x
that admit algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atomic decompositions in the space
L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. If
x
admits an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atomic decomposition, we set:

\|x\|_{L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\inf\Big(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\quad\text{for}\ 0<p\leq 1

and

\|x\|_{L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\inf\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\quad\text{for}\ 1<p<2,

where the infimum is taken over all decompositions of
x
as described above. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for a more in depth discussion on the need to separate the two cases.

The following result generalizes the atomic decomposition of conditioned
L_{p}
-spaces from
[
33
]
in two directions: it is valid for Orlicz spaces and also cover the quasi-Banach space range. This will play a crucial role in the next section. The approach of
[
33
]
was by duality which is not applicable to the present situation since we are dealing with not necessarily convex functions. Our constructive proof given below combined ideas from
[
7
]
and the case of Hardy spaces associated with convex Orlicz functions considered in
[
44
]
. This may be of independent interest.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\Phi
be
p
-convex and
q
-concave for
0<p\leq q<2
. Then the two spaces

L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
coincide (with constant of isomorphism depending only on
p
and
q
).

More precisely, every
x\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
admits a factorization
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
where
\alpha
is a strictly lower triangular matrix in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
and
\beta\in L_{\Theta}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})
satisfying:

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Proof.

First, we recall from earlier discussion that
L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\subseteq L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Thus, we only need to verify one inclusion.
This will be deduced from the second part of the theorem.

Let
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
. The construction below is an adaptation of the argument used in
[
44
]
. By definition,
x_{1}=0
and there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
with
\tau(e)<\infty
such that for
2\leq n\leq N
,
x_{n}=ex_{n}e
.

First, we note that for
j\geq 2
,
we have
\sigma_{c,j}(x)\in e\mathcal{M}_{j-1}e
. By approximation, we may assume that each of the
\sigma_{c,j}(x)
’s is invertible with bounded inverse in
e\mathcal{M}e
. Below, we simply write
\sigma_{j}
for
\sigma_{c,j}(x)
and the function
\Psi
is as defined in (
2.10
). Let
\lambda>0
to be determined later. For
n\geq 2
, we write

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})^{-1}

\displaystyle=x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big[\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{2})^{-1}+\sum_{3\leq m\leq n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{m})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{m-1})^{-1}\big]

\displaystyle=x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{2})^{-1}+\sum_{2\leq j<n}x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j+1})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j})^{-1}\big).

We define the strictly lower triangular matrix
\alpha
by setting:

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\alpha_{n,1}&:=x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{2})^{-1/2};\\
\alpha_{n,j}&:=\displaystyle{x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\left(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j+1})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j})^{-1}\right)^{1/2},\quad\text{for $2\leq j<n$}.}\end{cases}

We should point out here that since the function
t\mapsto\Psi(t^{1/2})
is operator monotone decreasing and
\lambda^{2}\sigma_{j}^{2}\leq\lambda^{2}\sigma_{j+1}^{2}
, we have
\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j+1})\leq\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j})
. Taking inverses,

\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j+1})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j})^{-1}\geq 0
. Thus, taking
1/2
-power in the expression above is justified.

The column sequence is defined by setting:

(2.12)

\begin{cases}\beta_{1,1}&:=\displaystyle{\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{2})^{-1/2};}\\
\beta_{m,1}&:=\displaystyle{\left(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{m+1})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{m})^{-1}\right)^{1/2},\quad\text{for $m\geq 2$}}.\end{cases}

Then
\alpha=(\alpha_{n,j})_{j<n}
is a strictly lower triangular matrix and
\beta=(\beta_{m,1})_{m\geq 1}
is an adapted sequence. Moreover, for every
n\geq 2
, it clearly follows from the definition that

(\alpha.\beta)_{n,1}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{n,j}.\beta_{j,1}=x_{n}.

That is, we have the factorization
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
.
We claim that the product
\alpha\,.\,\beta
satisfies the desired norm estimate. We begin with the
L_{2}
-norm of
\alpha
.

\displaystyle\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{1\leq j<n}\big\|\alpha_{n,j}\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\tau\big(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})|x_{n}|^{2}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big[\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{2})^{-1}+\sum_{2\leq j<n}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j+1})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{j})^{-1}\big]\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\tau\big(|x_{n}|^{2}\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big).

Since
(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n}))
is a predictable sequence, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\tau\big(\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(|x_{n}|^{2})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\tau\big((\sigma_{n}^{2}-\sigma_{n-1}^{2})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-2}\sum_{n\geq 2}\tau\big(((\lambda\sigma_{n})^{2}-(\lambda\sigma_{n-1})^{2})\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{n})\big).

We may deduce from Lemma
2.4
(iv) that there is a constant
C_{p,q}
so that

(2.13)

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{p,q}\lambda^{-2}\tau\big(\Phi(\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))\big).

On the other hand, Lemma
2.4
(ii) implies that there exists a constant
C_{p,q}^{\prime}
so that

(2.14)

\tau\Big[\Theta\Big(\big(\sum_{m\geq 1}|\beta_{m,1}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big)\Big]=\tau\Big[\Theta\Big(\Psi(\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))^{-1/2}\Big)\Big]\leq C_{p,q}^{\prime}\tau\big(\Phi(\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))\big).

Let
K_{p,q}=\max\{C_{p,q},C_{p,q}^{\prime}\}+1
.
Since
t\mapsto t^{-p}\Phi(t)
is non-decreasing, one can easily verify that

\Phi(t)\leq K_{p,q}^{-1}\Phi(K_{p,q}^{1/p}t)
. Using this fact, we get from (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) that

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lambda^{-2}\tau\big(\Phi(K_{p,q}^{1/p}\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))\big)

and

\tau\Big[\Theta\Big(\big(\sum_{m\geq 1}|\beta_{m,1}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big)\Big]\leq\tau\big(\Phi(K_{p,q}^{1/p}\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))\big).

Choose
\lambda
so that
\tau\big(\Phi(K_{p,q}^{1/p}\lambda\sigma_{c}(x))\big)\leq 1
. This can be achieved with
\lambda^{-1}=K_{p,q}^{1/p}\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}
.
With the above choice of
\lambda
, we clearly have

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}\leq K_{p,q}^{1/p}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
and
\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq 1
.
This proves the desired estimate and therefore the second part of the theorem.

To conclude the proof, we apply the case of
\mathfrak{F}^{0}
with the direct sum (
2.4
). We see that every
y\in\mathfrak{F}
admits an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atomic decomposition with
\|y\|_{L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\|y\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
. Since
\mathfrak{F}
is dense in
L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, it follows that

L_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Using
\lambda=1
in the proof above, we also obtain a moment version of the preceding theorem: given a sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
, the column matrix
\overline{x}=\sum_{n\geq 1}x_{n}\otimes e_{n,1}
admits a factorization
\overline{x}=\alpha\ .\ \beta
with
\alpha
is a strictly lower triangular in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
and
\beta
is an adapted column matrix satisfying

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}+\tau\big[\Theta\big((\sum_{n\geq 1}|\beta_{n,1}|^{2})^{1/2}\big)\big]\lesssim_{p,q}\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sigma_{c}(x)\big)\big].

We consider now the version of algebraic atomic decomposition for martingale Hardy spaces. The next consideration generalizes notions from
[
7
]
and
[
44
]
for non necessarily convex Orlicz functions.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
0<p<q<2
and
\Phi
be an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave.
An operator
x\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
is called an
algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atom
, whenever it can be written in the form
x=\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}b_{n}
, with
y_{n}
and
b_{n}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(y_{n})=0
and
b_{n}\in L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for all
n\geq 1
;

(ii)

\displaystyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}\big\|y_{n}\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1}
and
\displaystyle{\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|b_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1}
.

Following
[
7
,
44
]
, this concept of atoms naturally leads to the consideration of the corresponding atomic decomposition for conditioned Orlicz-Hardy spaces:
we say that an operator
x\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
admits an algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atomic decomposition if

x=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where for each
k
,
a_{k}
is an algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atom or an element of the unit ball of
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M}_{1})
, and
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<\infty
for
0<p\leq 1
and

\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
for
1<p<2
. The corresponding
algebraic atomic column martingale Hardy space

\mathsf{h}_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is defined to be the space of all
x
which admit a algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atomic decomposition and is equipped with

\|x\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{c}}=\inf\Big(\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\quad\text{for $0<p\leq 1$}

and

\|x\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{c}}=\inf\,\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|\quad\text{for $1<p<2$},

where the infimum are taken over all decompositions of
x
as described above.

We note that the above concepts were introduced in
[
44
]
for the case where
\Phi
is a convex function. Our main focus here is the case where
\Phi
is
p
-convex with
0<p<1
.

The next result is an extension of
[
7
, Theorem 3.10]
to the case of Orlicz function spaces. It follows immediately from Theorem
2.8
and the complementation result stated in Lemma
2.3
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
0<p<q<2
and
\Phi
is an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave. Then

\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{\Phi,\mathrm{aa}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

More precisely, if
x\in\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, then
x
admits a unique decomposition

x=x_{1}+y
where
x_{1}\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M}_{1})
and
y
is a scalar multiple of an algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atom.

Indeed, if
\Pi:L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the norm one projection described in Lemma
2.3
and
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
is a algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,{\rm cond}}
-atom with
\alpha
being a strictly lower triangular matrix in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
and
\beta
is an adapted column matrix in
L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, then we have

\displaystyle\Pi(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\sum_{n>k}d_{n}(\alpha_{n,k})\beta_{k,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\big(\sum_{n>k}d_{n}(\alpha_{n,k})\big)\beta_{k,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}a_{k}\beta_{k,1}.

Clearly, we have for every
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{E}_{k}(a_{k})=0
. Moreover,
\sum_{k\geq 1}\|a_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\alpha\|_{2}^{2}
. This shows in particular that
\Pi(x)
is an algebraic
\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}
-atom. The assertions in the corollary follow from combining this fact with Theorem
2.8
and direct sum. ∎

We conclude this section with a companion of Theorem
2.8
for spaces of adapted sequences. It may be viewed as the algebraic atomic decompositions for spaces of adapted sequences. This will be used in the next section.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\Phi
be a
p
-convex and
q
-concave Orlicz function for
0<p<q<2
. If
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence in
L_{\Phi}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

then there exists a sequence
\beta=(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{\Theta}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and a lower triangular matrix
\alpha
with the following properties:

(i)

\alpha\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
;

(ii)

for every
1\leq j\leq n
,
\alpha_{n,j}\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
;

(iii)

x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
;

(iv)

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
.

Conversely, any sequence
x
admitting a factorization as above belongs to
L_{\Phi}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
with

\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

First, we note that since square functions are well-defined for elements of
L_{\Phi}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, reduction to finite sequences or sequences of finite supports is not necessary.

Sketch of the proof.

Let
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in L_{\Phi}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. The construction is an adaptation of the proof of Theorem
2.8
but using square functions in place of conditioned square functions.

For each
n\geq 1
,
\mathcal{S}_{c,n}(x)\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
. That is,
(\mathcal{S}_{c,n}(x))_{n\geq 1}
is an adapted sequence. We simply write
\varsigma_{n}
for
\mathcal{S}_{c,n}(x)
. As before, we may assume that the
\varsigma_{n}
’s are invertible with bounded inverse.
Similarly,
(\Psi(\varsigma_{n}))_{n\geq 1}
is an adapted sequence with the
\Psi(\varsigma_{n})
’s being invertible.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.8
, fix
\lambda>0
and set

\begin{cases}\alpha_{n,1}&:=x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{n})\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{1})^{-1/2};\\
\alpha_{n,j}&:=\displaystyle{x_{n}\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{n})\left(\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{j})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{j-1})^{-1}\right)^{1/2},\quad\text{for $2\leq j\leq n$};}\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\beta_{1,1}&:=\displaystyle{\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{1})^{-1/2};}\\
\beta_{m,1}&:=\displaystyle{\left(\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{m})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda\varsigma_{m-1})^{-1}\right)^{1/2},\quad\text{for $m\geq 2$}}.\end{cases}

A slight difference here is that unlike in the proof of Theorem
2.8
, we do not make the indexing shift. The factorization
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
is straightforward. Clearly,
(\beta_{m,1})_{m\geq 1}
is an adapted sequence. Moreover, as
x_{n}\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
, it follows that
\alpha_{n,j}
is affiliated with
\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
(\alpha_{n,j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
is a lower triangular matrix.

We may choose
\lambda
exactly as in the proof of Theorem
2.8
. That is,

\lambda^{-1}=K_{p,q}^{1/p}\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}
. With this choice,
the verification of the fact that
\big\|\alpha\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
follows the same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
2.8
and is left to the reader.

On the other hand, if
x
admits a factorization
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
, then from the fact that
L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
, we may conclude as in the proof of Lemma
2.6
that

\|x\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq\|\alpha\|_{2}\,.\,\|\beta\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

Using factorizations of operator-valued triangular matrices (or more generally, elements of Hardy spaces associated with semifinite version of subdiagonal algebras in the sense of Arveson), the existence of a factorization
x=\alpha\,.\,\beta
, where
\alpha
is a lower triangular and
\beta
is a column matrix, is clear. We refer to
[
37
]
for this fact. The main point of Proposition
2.12
is that when
x
is an adapted sequence, we can choose the sequence
\beta
to be adapted and the matrix
\alpha
to satisfy
the extra property stated in item (ii). These additional facts are very important in the next section.

3.
Interpolation of conditioned spaces

This section is devoted to interpolation spaces between noncommutative column/row conditioned Hardy spaces and related spaces.
Our main references for interpolation theory are the books
[
2
,
3
,
30
]
.

Since we will be concerned with
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
when
0<p<1
, we will consider the more general framework of quasi-Banach spaces. We begin with some basic definitions.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces in the sense that both
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
embed continuously into some topological vector space
\mathcal{Z}
. This allows us to define the spaces
A_{0}\cap A_{1}
and
A_{0}+A_{1}
. These are quasi-Banach spaces when equipped with quasi-norms:

\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}\cap A_{1}}=\max\Big\{\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}},\big\|x\big\|_{A_{1}}\Big\}

and

\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}+A_{1}}=\inf\Big\{\big\|x_{0}\big\|_{A_{0}}+\big\|x_{1}\big\|_{A_{1}}:\,x=x_{0}+x_{1},\,x_{0}\in A_{0},\,x_{1}\in A_{1}\Big\},

respectively.

Definition 3.1
.

A quasi-Banach space
A
is called an
interpolation space
for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
if
A_{0}\cap A_{1}\subseteq A\subseteq A_{0}+A_{1}
and whenever a bounded linear operator
T:A_{0}+A_{1}\to A_{0}+A_{1}
is such that
T(A_{0})\subseteq A_{0}
and
T(A_{1})\subseteq A_{1}
, we have
T(A)\subseteq A

and

\big\|T:A\to A\big\|\leq c\max\left\{\big\|T:A_{0}\to A_{0}\big\|,\big\|T:A_{1}\to A_{1}\big\|\right\}

for some constant
c
.

If
A
is an interpolation space for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
, we write
A\in{\rm Int}(A_{0},A_{1})
. Below, we are mostly interested in two well-known specific interpolation methods generally referred to as real method and complex method.

We begin with a short discussion of the
real interpolation
method.
A fundamental notion for the construction of real interpolation spaces is the
K
-functional
which we now describe. For
x\in A_{0}+A_{1}
, we define the
K
-functional by setting for
t>0
,

K(x,t)=K\big(x,t;A_{0},A_{1}\big)=\inf\Big\{\big\|x_{0}\big\|_{A_{0}}+t\big\|x_{1}\big\|_{A_{1}}:\,x=x_{0}+x_{1},\,x_{0}\in A_{0},\,x_{1}\in A_{1}\Big\}.

Note that each
t>0
,
x\mapsto K(x,t)
gives an equivalent quasi-norm on
A_{0}+A_{1}
. There is also the dual notion of
J
-functionals which is defined for
y\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}
and
t>0
,

J(y,t)=J\big(y,t;A_{0},A_{1}\big)=\max\Big\{\big\|y\big\|_{A_{0}},t\big\|y\big\|_{A_{1}}\Big\}.

If
0<\theta<1
and
0<\gamma<\infty
, we recall the real interpolation space
A_{\theta,\gamma}=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}
by
x\in A_{\theta,\gamma}
if and only if

\big\|x\big\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{-\theta}K\big(x,t;A_{0},A_{1}\big)\big)^{\gamma}\ \frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/\gamma}<\infty.

If
\gamma=\infty
, we define
x\in A_{\theta,\infty}
if and only if

\big\|x\big\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}t^{-\theta}K(x,t;A_{0},A_{1})<\infty.

For
0<\theta<1
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,\gamma}
is a quasi-norm and
(A_{\theta,\gamma},\|\cdot\|_{\theta,\gamma})
is a quasi-Banach space. Moreover,
the space
A_{\theta,\gamma}
is an interpolation space for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
in the sense of Definition
3.1
. There is also an equivalent description of
A_{\theta,\gamma}
using the
J
-functionals for which we refer to
[
3
,
30
]
for the exact formulation.

It is worth noting that the real interpolation method is well understood for the couple
(L_{p_{0}},L_{p_{1}})
for both the classical case and the noncommutative case. We record here that
Lorentz spaces can be realized as real interpolation spaces for the couple
(L_{p_{0}},L_{p_{1}})
. More precisely,
if
\mathcal{N}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra,
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
0<q\leq\infty
then, up to equivalent quasi-norms (independent of
\mathcal{N}
),

\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{N}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{N})\big)_{\theta,q}=L_{p,q}(\mathcal{N})

where
1/p=(1-\theta)p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
. In particular, we have

\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{N}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{N})\big)_{\theta,p}=L_{p}(\mathcal{N})

with equivalent quasi-norms. These facts can be found in
[
37
]
.

Wolff’s interpolation theorem will be used repeatedly throughout the next subsection. We record it here for convenience.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
48
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
B_{i}
(
i=1,2,3,4
) be quasi-Banach spaces such that
B_{1}\cap B_{4}
is dense in
B_{j}
(
j=2,3
) and satisfy:

B_{2}=(B_{1},B_{3})_{\phi,r}\ \ \text{and}\ \ B_{3}=(B_{2},B_{4})_{\theta,q}

for
0<\phi,\theta<1
and
0<r,q\leq\infty
. Then

B_{2}=(B_{1},B_{4})_{\xi,r}\ \ \text{and}\ \ B_{3}=(B_{1},B_{4})_{\zeta,q}

where
\displaystyle{\xi=\frac{\phi\theta}{1-\phi+\phi\theta}}
and
\displaystyle{\zeta=\frac{\theta}{1-\phi+\phi\theta}}
.

In order to make the presentation below more concise, we introduce the following terminology.

Definition 3.3
.

A family of quasi-Banach spaces
(A_{p,\gamma})_{p,\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
is said to form a
real interpolation scale
on an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
if for every
p,q\in I
,
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/r=(1-\theta)p+\theta/q
,

A_{r,\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta,\gamma}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

The next result may be viewed as a version of Wolff’s interpolation theorem at the level of family of real interpolation scale.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that a family of quasi-Banach spaces
(A_{p,\gamma})_{p,\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
forms a
real interpolation scale
on two different intervals
I
and
J
. If
|J\cap I|>1
, then

(A_{p,\gamma})_{p,\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
forms a real interpolation scale on
I\cup J
.

Proof.

We may assume that
I
and
J
are closed intervals. As
|I\cap J|>1
, we may assume that
\sup I>\inf J
and
I\cap J=[w_{1},w_{2}]
where
w_{1}=\inf J
and
w_{2}=\sup I
. Fix
p\in I\setminus J
,
q\in J\setminus I
, and
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma\leq\infty
. We divide the proof into three cases.

\diamond
Case 1. Assume that
r_{1}\in(w_{1},w_{2})
.

Since both
p
and
w_{2}
belong to
I
, by assumption, for any given
0<\gamma_{3}\leq\infty
,

A_{r_{1},\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{w_{2},\gamma_{3}})_{\theta_{1},\gamma}

where
1/{r_{1}}=(1-\theta_{1})/p+\theta_{1}/{w_{2}}
.

On the other hand, as
r_{1}
and
q
belong to
J
and
r_{1}<w_{2}<q
, the assumption also gives that

A_{w_{2},\gamma_{3}}=(A_{r_{1},\gamma},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\psi_{1},\gamma_{3}}

where
1/{w_{2}}=(1-\psi_{1})/r_{1}+\psi_{1}/q
.
Applying Wolff’s interpolation theorem, with
B_{1}=A_{p,\gamma_{1}}
,
B_{2}=A_{r_{1},\gamma}
,

B_{3}=A_{w_{2},\gamma_{3}}
, and
B_{4}=A_{q,\gamma_{2}}
, we deduce that

A_{r_{1},\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta,\gamma}

where
\displaystyle{\theta=\frac{\theta_{1}\psi_{1}}{1-\theta_{1}+\theta_{1}\psi_{1}}}
. One can readily verify that
1/r_{1}=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
.

\diamond
Case 2. Assume that
r_{2}\in(p,w_{1}]
.

Fix
w_{3}\in(w_{1},w_{2})
. Since
p<r_{2}<w_{3}
and
p,w_{3}\in I
, by assumption,
we have for every
0<\gamma_{3}\leq\infty
,

A_{r_{2},\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{w_{3},\gamma_{3}})_{\theta_{2},\gamma}

where
1/{r_{2}}=(1-\theta_{2})/p+\theta_{2}/{w_{3}}
. Using the previous case with
r_{2}
in place of
p
and
w_{3}
in place of
r_{1}
, we have

A_{w_{3},\gamma_{3}}=(A_{r_{2},\gamma},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\psi_{2},\gamma_{3}}

with
1/w_{3}=(1-\psi_{2})/{r_{2}}+\psi_{2}/q
.
Using Wolff’s interpolation theorem with
B_{1}=A_{p,\gamma_{1}}
,
B_{2}=A_{r_{2},\gamma}
,

B_{3}=A_{w_{3},\gamma_{3}}
, and
B_{4}=A_{q,\gamma_{2}}
, we obtain that

A_{r_{2},\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta,\gamma}

where
\displaystyle{\theta=\frac{\theta_{2}\psi_{2}}{1-\theta_{2}+\theta_{2}\psi_{2}}}
. As before, one can verify that
1/{r_{2}}=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
.

\diamond
Case 3. Assume
r_{3}\in[w_{2},q)
.

As in the previous case, let
w_{3}\in(w_{1},w_{2})
. Since
w_{3},r_{3},q\in I
, we have

A_{r_{3},\gamma}=(A_{w_{3},\gamma_{3}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta_{3},\gamma}

where
1/{r_{3}}=(1-\theta_{3})/{w_{3}}+\theta_{3}/q
.
Next, we apply Case 1 with
w_{3}
in place of
r_{1}
in order to get that

A_{w_{3},\gamma_{3}}=(A_{p,\gamma_{1}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\psi_{3},\gamma_{3}}

with
1/{w_{3}}=(1-\theta_{3})/p+\theta_{3}/q
. The desired statement can be deduced as in Case 2.

Combining the three cases above, we may state that if
p\in I\setminus J
,
q\in J\setminus I
,
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
, then

A_{r,\gamma}=(A_{p,\gamma_{2}},A_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta,\gamma},

which is equivalent to the statement that the family
(A_{p,\gamma})_{p,\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
forms a real interpolation scale on the interval
I\cup J
.
∎

We now state the primary result of the paper. It is an extension of
[
1
, Theorem 4.8]
to the full range
0<p<\infty
and a noncommutative generalization of (
1.3
) (see also
[
46
]
).

Theorem 3.5
.

If
0<\theta<1
,
0<p<\infty
, and
0<\lambda,\gamma\leq\infty
, then for
1/r=(1-\theta)/p
,

\big(\mathsf{h}_{p,\lambda}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,\gamma}=\mathsf{h}_{r,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with equivalent quasi-norms.

Before we proceed, we should point out that for
0<p<\infty
and
0<\lambda\leq\infty
, the pair
(\mathsf{h}_{p,\lambda}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
forms a compatible couple. Indeed, as described in the preliminary
section,
\mathsf{h}_{p,\lambda}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
embeds isometrically into
L_{p,\lambda}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
which in turn is continuously embeded into the topological vector space
L_{0}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. Now we verify that
\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
also embeds continuously into
L_{0}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. This is immediate if
\mathcal{M}
is finite as

\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq L_{0}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
.
When
\mathcal{M}
is infinite, this can be achieved as follows: since
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is semifinite, choose a family of mutually disjoint finite projections
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
so that

\sum_{j\in J}e_{j}={\bf 1}
for the strong operator topology.
Let
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and for each
j\in J
, set
xe_{j}=(x_{n}e_{j})_{n\geq 1}
. It is clear that
xe_{j}\in\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
s_{c}^{2}(xe_{j})=e_{j}s_{c}^{2}(x)e_{j}
. Since
\tau(e_{j})<\infty
,
xe_{j}\in\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. Therefore
U\mathcal{D}_{c}(xe_{j})
is well-defined in
L_{0}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. Moreover, the module property of
U
implies that

U\mathcal{D}_{c}(xe_{j})=U\mathcal{D}_{c}(xe_{j}).(e_{j}\otimes Id)
where
Id
is the identity of

B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
. This implies that
\sum_{j\in J}U\mathcal{D}_{c}(xe_{j})
converges in measure. This shows in particular that the map
x\mapsto\sum_{j\in J}U\mathcal{D}_{c}(xe_{j})
provides a continuous embedding of
\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
into
L_{0}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
.

Similarly, if
0<r,s\leq\infty
, then
\big(L_{r}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{s}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)
is a compatible couple as both spaces embed continuously into

(L_{r}+L_{s})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

We need some preparation for the proof.
We consider the Orlicz structure of the symmetric function space
L_{r}+tL_{s}
for any given
0<r<s<\infty
and
t>0
. To describe this structure, we consider the following Orlicz function:

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}(u)=\min\{u^{r},t^{s}u^{s}\},\quad u\geq 0.

One can verify that
\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}(\cdot)
is
r
-convex and
s
-concave. According to
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have for every
f\in L_{r}+L_{s}
and
t>0
,

(3.1)

2^{-1-1/r}\big\|f\big\|_{\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}}\leq K\big(f,t;L_{r},L_{s}\big)\leq 2\big\|f\big\|_{\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}}.

In particular, the Orlicz space
L_{\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}}
coincides with the space
L_{r}+tL_{s}
with isomorphism constant depending only on the index
r
and therefore independent of
t
.

Let
0<p,q,r,s<2
and assume that
p<q
,
1/p=1/2+1/r
, and
1/q=1/2+1/s
.
One can easily check that

L_{p}+tL_{q}=L_{2}\odot(L_{r}+tL_{s}).

It follows by identification that the following factorization holds for the corresponding Orlicz spaces:

L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}},

with constants depending only on the indices
p
and
q
.

We will verify that the atomic decomposition results from Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.12
apply to this specific factorization. Ideally, we would like to have that the function
u\mapsto u^{-1/2}(\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)})^{-1}(u)
is equivalent to
(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)})^{-1}
but in order to avoid working with inverse functions, we will proceed directly to proving a corresponding result to Lemma
2.4
. It also highlights the fact that for this particular case, integral representations of the Orlicz functions involved are not needed.
To this end, set for
u>0
,

\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u):=(u^{2-p}+t^{-q}u^{2-q})^{-1}.

We have the following properties:

Lemma 3.6
.

(i)

\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)\approx_{p,q}u^{-2}\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)
;

(ii)

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}\big((\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)
;

(iii)

u\mapsto\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)
is operator monotone decreasing;

(iv)

for any increasing sequence of positive operators
a_{n}\uparrow a
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau\big((a_{n+1}^{2}-a_{n}^{2})\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(a_{n+1})\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\tau\big(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(a)\big).

Proof.

For the first item
(i)
, we write
u^{-2}\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)=\min\{u^{-2+p},t^{q}u^{-2+q}\}=\big(\max\{u^{2-p},t^{-q}u^{2-p}\}\big)^{-1}
and note that

2^{-1}(u^{2-p}+t^{-q}u^{2-p})\leq\max\{u^{2-p},t^{-q}u^{2-p}\}\leq u^{2-p}+t^{-q}u^{2-p}.

It follows from taking inverses that

\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)\leq u^{-2}\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)\leq 2\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u).

Next, item
(iii)
can be seen as follows: if
a
and
b
are positive operators with
a\leq b
then since
0<1-(p/2)<1
and
0<1-(q/2)<1
, we have

a^{1-(p/2)}+t^{q}a^{1-(q/2)}\leq b^{1-(p/2)}+t^{q}b^{1-(q/2)}.

Taking inverse operators, we see that

\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(b^{1/2})\leq\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(a^{1/2}).

This shows that
u\mapsto\psi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u^{1/2})
is operator monotone decreasing.

With the equivalence
(i)
on hand, the proof of
(iv)
is identical to the proof of
[
44
, Lemma 3.4]
so we leave it to the reader. It remains to verify
(ii)
. From the equivalence
(i)
, it suffices to prove that

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}\big(u(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u).

Since
u(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}=\max\{u^{1-{p/2}},t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}}\}
, we have
from the definition of
\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}(\cdot)
that

(3.2)

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}\big(u(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}\big)=\min\Big\{\max\{(u^{1-{p/2}})^{r},(t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}})^{r}\},\max\{t^{s}(u^{1-{p/2}})^{s},t^{s}(t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}})^{s}\}\Big\}.

Note that
(u^{1-{p/2}})^{r}=u^{p}
and
t^{s}(t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}})^{s}=t^{q}u^{q}
. If
u^{1-(p/2)}\geq t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}}
then
t^{q}u^{q}\geq u^{p}
. In particular,
\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)=u^{p}
. Then (
3.2
) implies that

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}\big(u(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}\big)=\min\big\{u^{p},t^{s}(u^{1-(p/2)})^{s}\big\}\leq\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u).

Similarly, if
u^{1-(p/2)}\leq t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}}
then
t^{q}u^{q}\leq u^{p}
and therefore,
\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u)=t^{q}u^{q}
. We can deduce from (
3.2
) that

\Phi_{t}^{(r,s)}\big(u(\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u))^{-1/2}\big)=\min\Big\{(t^{-q/2}u^{1-{q/2}})^{r},t^{q}u^{q}\Big\}\leq\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}(u).

This completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition constitutes the decisive step toward our proof of Theorem
3.5
. We formulate it here for the more general conditioned spaces.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\nu
be a positive integer with
\nu\geq 2
. Assume that
2/(\nu+1)<p\leq 2/\nu
and
p<q<2/(\nu-1)
.
If
x\in\mathfrak{F}
, then for every
t>0
,

K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\approx_{p,q}K\big(\sigma_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M})\big).

Proof.

\bullet

We observe first that one inequality in the equivalence follows easily from the isometric embeddings described in the preliminary section. Indeed, let
x\in\mathfrak{F}
and set
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
. Recall that for
0<r\leq\infty
, the map
U:L_{r}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{r}(\mathcal{N})
is an isometry satisfying the identity

|U(x)|=\sigma_{c}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}.

It follows that for every
t>0
,

\displaystyle K\big(\sigma_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M})\big)

\displaystyle=K\big(|U(x)|,t;L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N})\big)

\displaystyle=K\big(U(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N})\big)

\displaystyle\leq K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

which verifies one inequality with constant
1
.

\bullet

The reverse inequality is more involved.
We consider the following two finite sequences of indices: set
p_{0}=p
and
q_{0}=q
and for
1\leq m\leq\nu-1
,

1/p_{m-1}=1/2+1/p_{m}\ \text{and}\ 1/q_{m-1}=1/2+1/q_{m}.

From earlier discussions, the following factorization holds for the respective Orlicz spaces:

L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{m-1},q_{m-1})}}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{m},q_{m})}}.

We note that by Lemma
3.6
, atomic decompositions stated in Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.12
apply to each of these factorizations.

The main idea in the argument below is to repeatedly apply the above factorization until one gets indices that are strictly larger than
1
. At that point, splittings into adapted sequences are possible via the noncommutative Stein inequality. The restriction imposed on the values of
p
and
q
is needed in the argument since at every step (except the last one) we need both indices
p_{m}
and
q_{m}
to remain in the open interval
(0\,,\,2)
so that algebraic atomic decompositions from the previous section can be applied.

We now present the details of the proof. Assume first that
x\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
(the general case will be dealt later).

Fix
t>0
. We apply Theorem
2.8
to the Orlicz space

L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}}
.
We have the following factorization:

(3.3)

x=\alpha^{(1)}.\,\beta^{(1)}

where
\alpha^{(1)}
is a strictly lower triangular matrix with entries in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and
\beta^{(1)}
is an adapted column matrix in
L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{1},q_{1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
satisfying:

(3.4)

\big\|\alpha^{(1)}\big\|_{2}\ .\ \big\|\beta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{1},q_{1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p,q)}}(\mathcal{M})}.

Next, we inductively construct finite sequences
\{\alpha^{(m)}:2\leq m\leq\nu-1\}
and

\{\beta^{(m)}:1\leq m\leq\nu-1\}
where the
\alpha^{(m)}
’s are lower triangular matrices taking values in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying
\alpha^{(m)}_{n,j}\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for
1\leq j\leq n
, and
\beta^{(m)}
’s are adapted column matrices. Both sequences satisfy for
2\leq m\leq\nu-1
:

(3.5)

\beta^{(m-1)}=\alpha^{(m)}\,.\,\beta^{(m)}

and

(3.6)

\big\|\alpha^{(m)}\big\|_{2}\ .\ \big\|\beta^{(m)}\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{m},q_{m})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\beta^{(m-1)}\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{m-1},q_{m-1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

This is done by applying Proposition
2.12
to
\beta^{(m-1)}\in L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{m-1},q_{m-1})}}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. The constant depends on
p_{m-1}
and
q_{m-1}
but since they depend on
p
and
q
respectively, we may state that for each step, the constant depends on
p
and
q
.

Clearly, the above construction induces a factorization:

x=\alpha^{(1)}\dots\alpha^{(\nu-1)}\,.\,\beta^{(\nu-1)}.

Now, we consider the adapted sequence
\beta^{(\nu-1)}\in L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{\nu-1},q_{\nu-1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. By identification, we have
\beta^{(\nu-1)}\in(L_{p_{\nu-1}}+tL_{q_{\nu-1}})(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+tL_{q_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

It follows from the definition of sum of two Banach spaces that
\beta^{(\nu-1)}
admits a decomposition
\beta^{(\nu-1)}=\xi^{(1)}+\xi^{(2)}
with
\xi^{(1)}\in L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
\xi^{(2)}\in L_{q_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
satisfying the norm estimate:

(3.7)

\big\|\xi^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\big\|\xi^{(2)}\big\|_{L_{q_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq 2\big\|\beta^{(\nu-1)}\big\|_{(L_{p_{\nu-1}}+tL_{q_{\nu-1}})(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The important fact here is that
1<p_{\nu-1}<q_{\nu-1}<\infty
.
By applying the noncommutative Stein inequality (
[
37
]
), we may replace
\xi^{(1)}
and
\xi^{(2)}
by adapted sequences
\zeta^{(1)}=\{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\xi_{n}^{(1)})\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\zeta^{(2)}=\{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\xi_{n}^{(2)})\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying:

(3.8)

\big\|\zeta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\big\|\zeta^{(2)}\big\|_{L_{q_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|\beta^{(\nu-1)}\big\|_{(L_{p_{\nu-1}}+tL_{q_{\nu-1}})(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The constant
C_{p,q}
can be taken to be equal to
2\max\{\gamma_{p_{\nu-1}},\gamma_{q_{\nu-1}}\}
where
\gamma_{r}
is the constant from the noncommutative Stein inequality for
1<r<\infty
. This justifies that
C_{p,q}
depends only on
p
and
q
since
p_{\nu-1}
and
q_{\nu-1}
depend on
p
and
q
respectively.

Next, we consider two sequences of operators by setting:

x^{(1)}=\alpha^{(1)}\dots\alpha^{(\nu-1)}.\ \zeta^{(1)}\quad\text{and}\quad x^{(2)}=\alpha^{(1)}\dots\alpha^{(\nu-1)}.\ \zeta^{(2)}.

Since
\beta^{(\nu-1)}=\zeta^{(1)}+\zeta^{(2)}
, we clearly have
x=x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}
. We claim that

x^{(1)}\in L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
x^{(2)}\in L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Indeed, let
y^{(1)}=\alpha^{(2)}\dots\alpha^{(\nu-1)}.\ \zeta^{(1)}
and
y^{(2)}=\alpha^{(2)}\dots\alpha^{(\nu-1)}.\ \zeta^{(2)}
. The fact that
\zeta^{(1)}
is adapted and the property that
\alpha_{n,j}^{(\nu-1)}\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for
1\leq j\leq n
implies that

\widehat{\beta}^{(\nu-2)}:=\alpha^{(\nu-1)}\,.\,\zeta^{(1)}
is an adapted sequence. Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality, we have
\widehat{\beta}^{(\nu-2)}\in L_{p_{\nu-2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
with

\big\|\widehat{\beta}^{(\nu-2)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-2}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq\big\|\alpha^{(\nu-1)}\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\zeta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

One can work backward and set
\widehat{\beta}^{(m-1)}=\alpha^{(m)}\,.\,\widehat{\beta}^{(m)}
for
2\leq m\leq\nu-2

to see that
y^{(1)}
is an adapted column sequence. Moreover, a repeated use of Hölder’s inequality yields:

\big\|y^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq\Big(\prod_{m=2}^{\nu-1}\big\|\alpha^{(m)}\big\|_{2}\Big)\big\|\zeta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}<\infty.

Since
\alpha^{(1)}
is strictly lower triangular and
y^{(1)}
is adapted, it follows that
x^{(1)}=\alpha^{(1)}\,.\,y^{(1)}\in L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Similar argument can be applied to deduce that
x^{(2)}\in L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

We now verify that the decomposition
x=x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}
provides the desired inequality between the two
K
-functionals. Indeed,
we have the following norm estimates:

\displaystyle K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big\|x^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\big\|x^{(2)}\big\|_{L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\nu-1}\big\|\alpha^{(m)}\big\|_{2}\Big).\big(\big\|\zeta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{p_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\big\|\zeta^{(2)}\big\|_{L_{q_{\nu-1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\nu-1}\big\|\alpha^{(m)}\big\|_{2}\Big).\,\big\|\beta^{(\nu-1)}\big\|_{(L_{p_{\nu-1}}+tL_{q_{\nu-1}})(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\Big(\prod_{m=1}^{\nu-1}\big\|\alpha^{(m)}\big\|_{2}\Big).\,\big\|\beta^{(\nu-1)}\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{\nu-1},q_{\nu-1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Applying (
3.6
) successively
(\nu-2)
-times, we get

K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\alpha^{(1)}\big\|_{2}\,.\,\big\|\beta^{(1)}\big\|_{L_{\Phi_{t}^{(p_{1},q_{1})}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

By (
3.4
) and norm equivalence, we arrive at

K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{(L_{p}+tL_{q})(\mathcal{M})}.

This proves the case where
x\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
.

We now consider an arbitrary
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
. Let
z=(0,x_{2},x_{3},\dots)\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}
. By the direct sum (
2.4
) and the previous case,
we have:

\displaystyle K\big(x,t;

\displaystyle L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}K\big(x_{1},t;L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}),L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1})\big)+K\big(z,t;L_{p}^{,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}K\big(x_{1},t;L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}),L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1})\big)+K\big(\sigma_{c}(z),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M})\big).

Since
u_{1}(L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}))
and
u_{1}(L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}))
are one complemented in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
L_{q}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c})
respectively, we have

\displaystyle K\big(x_{1},t;L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}),L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=K\big(u_{1}(x_{1}),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

\displaystyle=K\big(|u_{1}(x_{1})|,t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}_{1};\ell_{2}^{c})\big).

As
|u_{1}(x_{1})|=\mathcal{E}_{1}(|x_{1}|^{2})^{1/2}\otimes e_{1,1}
, it follows that

K\big(x_{1},t;L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1}),L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{1})\big)=K\big(\mathcal{E}_{1}(|x_{1}|^{2})^{1/2},t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{1}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}_{1})\big).

As
\mathcal{E}_{1}(|x_{1}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq\sigma_{c}(x)
and
\sigma_{c}(z)\leq\sigma_{c}(x)
, we may conclude that

K\big(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\lesssim_{p,q}K\big(\sigma_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M})\big).

The proof is complete.
∎

3.3.
The complex method

We now turn our attention to the case of complex interpolation method which we now briefly review.

Let
S
(respectively,
\overline{S}
) denote the open strip
\{z:0<\operatorname{Re}z<1\}
(respectively, the closed strip
\{z:0\leq\operatorname{Re}z\leq 1\}
) in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Let
A(S)
be the
collection of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions that are analytic on
S
and continuous and bounded on
\overline{S}
. For a compatible couple of complex quasi-Banach spaces
(A_{0},A_{1})
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})
the family of functions of the form
f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(z)x_{k}
with
f_{k}\in A(S)
and
x_{k}\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}
.
We equip
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})
with the quasi-norm:

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})}=\max\Big\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big\|f(it)\big\|_{A_{0}},\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big\|f(1+it)\big\|_{A_{1}}\Big\}.

Then
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})
becomes a quasi-Banach space. For
0<\theta<1
, the complex interpolation norm on
A_{0}\cap A_{1}
is defined by:

\big\|x\big\|_{[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}}=\inf\Big\{\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})}:f(\theta)=x,\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(A_{0},A_{1})\Big\}.

The
complex interpolation space
(of exponent
\theta
)
[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}
is defined as the completion of the quasi-normed space
(A_{0}\cap A_{1},\|\cdot\|_{[A_{0},A_{1}]_{\theta}})
.

As in the real method, complex interpolations of the couple
(L_{p},L_{q})
(for
0<p<q\leq\infty
) are well-known. Indeed, if
\mathcal{N}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra and
0<\theta<1
, then

\big[L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N})\big]_{\theta}=L_{r}(\mathcal{N})

isometrically for
1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
. This fact comes from
[
49
, Theorem 4.1]
(see also
[
38
]
).

The next result is the version of Theorem
3.5
for the complex method.

Theorem 3.16
.

If
0<p,q<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{p}+\frac{\theta}{q}}
, then

\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\big[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big]_{\theta}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

For the proof, we will use the next result which provides a connection between complex interpolation method and real interpolation method that is valid for quasi-Banach spaces. We should note that for Banach spaces, the inequality in the next theorem is actually an equivalence but for quasi-Banach spaces only one inequality is valid in its full generality.

Theorem 3.17

(
[
9
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces. Let

0<\theta_{j}<1
,
0<\theta<1
, and
0<\gamma_{j}\leq\infty
. Denote
E_{j}=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta_{j},\gamma_{j}}
for
j=0,1
. If
1/\gamma=(1-\theta)/\gamma_{0}+\theta/\gamma_{1}
and
\lambda=(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1}
then for every
a\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}\subset E_{0}\cap E_{1}
,

\big\|a\big\|_{[E_{0},E_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq C\big\|a\big\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\lambda,\gamma}}.

Proof of Theorem
3.16
.

Assume that
0<p<q<\infty
and fix
0<p_{0}<p
. Then according to Theorem
3.5
,
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathsf{h}_{p_{0}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))_{\theta_{0},p}
and
h_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathsf{h}_{p_{0}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))_{\theta_{1},q}
where
1/p=(1-\theta_{0})/p_{0}
and
1/q=(1-\theta_{1})/p_{0}
. We may state from Theorem
3.17
that if
1/\gamma=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
and

\lambda=(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1}
then for every
a\in\mathsf{h}_{p_{0}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
,

\big\|a\big\|_{[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})]_{\theta}}\leq C\big\|a\big\|_{(\mathsf{h}_{p_{0}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))_{\lambda,\gamma}}.

One can easily verify that
(1-\lambda)/{p_{0}}=1/\gamma=1/r
and therefore we may deduce from Theorem
3.5
that

(3.12)

\big\|a\big\|_{[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})]_{\theta}}\leq C^{\prime}\big\|a\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}}.

On the other hand, let
U:L_{s}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{s}(\mathcal{N})
(where
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
) be the family of isometric embeddings as described in the preliminary section which are valid for all
0<s\leq\infty
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{c}
the extension of the map
x\mapsto(dx_{n})_{n\geq 1}

from
\mathsf{h}_{s}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
into
L_{s}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Then for every
0<s\leq\infty
,
U\mathcal{D}_{c}
is an isometric embedding of
\mathsf{h}_{s}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
into
L_{s}(\mathcal{N})
. Let
b\in\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. Interpolating the operator
U\mathcal{D}_{c}
, we have

\big\|U\mathcal{D}_{c}(b)\big\|_{[L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N})]_{\theta}}\leq\big\|b\big\|_{[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})]_{\theta}}.

Since
\big[(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N})\big]_{\theta}=L_{r}(\mathcal{N})
isometrically, it follows that

\big\|U\mathcal{D}_{c}(b)\big\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{N})}\leq\big\|b\big\|_{[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})]_{\theta}}.

From the fact that
U\mathcal{D}_{c}
is an isometry on
\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we deduce that

(3.13)

\big\|b\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{r}^{c}}\leq\big\|b\big\|_{[\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})]_{\theta}}.

From combining (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain the desired equivalence.
∎

When
0<p<1
, we do not know if the corresponding statement to Theorem
3.16
remains valid if the interpolation couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is used. Since reiteration theorem is not available for complex interpolations of quasi-Banach spaces, in general, this consideration is independent of Theorem
3.16
.
We leave this as an open problem.

The same method of proofs can be applied to conditioned spaces and spaces of adapted sequences to deduce the following interpolation results from Proposition
3.12
and Theorem
3.13
respectively.

Proposition 3.18
.

If
0<\theta<1
and
0<p,q<\infty
then for
1/{r}=(1-\theta)/{p}+{\theta}/q
, the following hold:

\big[L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big]_{\theta}=L_{r}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

and

\big[L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big]_{\theta}=L_{r}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

with equivalent quasi-norms.

As in the case of real interpolation, we may view the second assertion in the proposition as an extension of Musat’s result to the quasi-Banach space range. Moreover,
using
[
48
, Lemma 1]
(which is valid for quasi-Banach spaces), one can adapt the argument used in the proof of Corollary
3.15
to show that the noncommutative analogue of (
1.2
) holds:

Corollary 3.19
.

Assume that
0<p<1
and
0<\theta<\infty
are such that
1/r=(1-\theta)/p<1
.
Then

\big[\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),{\mathcal{BMO}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big]_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

Our method of proof only applies under the assumption that
r>1
. We should point out that the reverse inclusion does not hold even in the classical setting. An example exhibited in
[
18
, p. 66]

shows that there exists a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and an increasing filtration of
\sigma
-fields
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{F}=\sigma(\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{n})
and such that if
0<p<1<r<\infty
, then for
1/r=(1-\theta)/p
,
\big[\mathcal{H}_{p}(\Omega),{\mathcal{BMO}}(\Omega)\big]_{\theta}\neq\mathcal{H}_{r}(\Omega)
.

All results stated in this section have row counterparts. However, at the time of this writing, it is unclear if for
0<p,q<1
, the interpolation results for column/row conditioned Hardy spaces have counterparts to the couple of diagonal Hardy spaces
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{d}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{d}(\mathcal{M}))
.

4.
Applications to martingale inequalities

In this section, we present various martingale inequalities in the general framework of noncommutative symmetric spaces that can be derived from methods we develop in the previous two sections.

We will need the following generalization of real interpolation:

Definition 4.1
.

An interpolation space
E
for a couple of quasi-Banach spaces
(E_{0},E_{1})
is said to be
given by a
K
-method
if there exists a quasi-Banach function space
\mathcal{F}
such that
x\in E
if and only if
t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\in\mathcal{F}

and there exists constant
C_{E}>0
such that

C_{E}^{-1}\big\|t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\big\|x\big\|_{E}\leq C_{E}\big\|t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

In this case, we write
E=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\mathcal{F};K}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0<p<q\leq\infty
. Every interpolation space
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
is given by a
K
-method.

For the Banach space range, this fact is known as a result of Brudnyi and Krugliak (see
[
27
, Theorem 6.3]
). An argument for the quasi-Banach space range is given in Dirksen’s thesis (
[
10
]
). Alternatively, the quasi-Banach space range can be deduced from the Banach space case as follows:
assume that
0<p<1
and
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
.
Let
E^{(1/p)}
be the
1/p
-convexification of
E
. That is,

E^{(1/p)}=\Big\{h\in L_{0}:|h|^{1/p}\in E\Big\}

equipped with the norm
\|h\|_{E^{(1/p)}}=\|\,|h|^{1/p}\,\|_{E}^{p}
.
According to
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
,
E^{(1/p)}\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q/p})
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Banach function space so that
E^{(1/p)}=(L_{1},L_{q/p})_{\mathcal{F};K}
. We make the observation from Homlsted’s formula (
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
) that if
a
is a positive function in
L_{p}+L_{q}
then:

K\big(a,t;L_{p},L_{q}\big)\approx_{p,q}\Big[K\big(a^{p},t^{p};L_{1},L_{q/p}\big)\Big]^{1/p}.

Let
x\in E
. We have

\begin{split}\big\|x\big\|_{E}^{p}&=\big\|\,|x|^{p}\,\big\|_{E^{(1/p)}}\\
&\approx_{E}\big\|t\mapsto K(|x|^{p},t;L_{1},L_{q/p})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}\\
&\approx_{E}\big\|t\mapsto\big[K(|x|,t^{1/p};L_{p},L_{q})\big]^{p}\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}\\
&\approx_{E}\big\|t\mapsto K(|x|,t^{1/p};L_{p},L_{q})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}^{(p)}}^{p}.\end{split}

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\{f\in L_{0}:t\mapsto f(t^{1/p})\in\mathcal{F}^{(p)}\}
with the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{Z}}=\|t\mapsto f(t^{1/p})\|_{\mathcal{F}^{(p)}}
. We see now that
E=(L_{p},L_{q})_{\mathcal{Z};K}
. ∎

Below, we will also use the
J
-method version of the interpolation associated with function space which we now briefly describe: for
x\in E_{0}+E_{1}
, we recall that by a representation of
x
with respect to the couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
, we mean a measurable function
u:(0,\infty)\to E_{0}\cap E_{1}
satisfying

x=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(t)\ \frac{dt}{t}

where the convergence is taken in
E_{0}+E_{1}
. Recall that for
y\in E_{0}\cap E_{1}
and
t>0
,

J(y,t;E_{0},E_{1})=\max\{\|y\|_{E_{0}};t\|y\|_{E_{1}}\}.

For a given function space
\mathcal{F}
, we define the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})_{\mathcal{F};J}}:=\inf\big\{\|t\mapsto J(u(t),t;E_{0},E_{1})\|_{\mathcal{F}}\big\}

where the infimum is taken over all representation
u(\cdot)
of
x
with respect to the couple
(E_{0},E_{1})
. The interpolation space
(E_{0},E_{1})_{\mathcal{F};J}
is defined as the collection of all
x\in E_{0}+E_{1}
for which
\|x\|_{(E_{0},E_{1})_{\mathcal{F};J}}<\infty
.
We refer to
[
30
]
for more on this interpolation method.

From the fact that every interpolation space of the couple
(L_{p},L_{q})
is given by a
K
-method, the following can be easily deduced from our results on
K
-functionals from the previous section:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\nu
be a positive integer with
\nu\geq 2
. Assume that
2/(\nu+1)<p\leq 2/\nu
and
p<q\leq 2/(\nu-1)
. If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
with
E=(L_{p},L_{q})_{\mathcal{F};K}
for a quasi Banach function space
\mathcal{F}

then the following hold:

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\mathcal{F};K},

E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\big(L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)_{\mathcal{F};K},

and

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\big(L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)_{\mathcal{F};K}.

The first assertion in the preceding observation motivates the following more general question: assume that
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
are symmetric quasi-Banach function spaces and
E\in{\rm Int}(E_{0},E_{1})
, does it follow that
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\in{\rm Int}\big(\mathsf{h}_{E_{0}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{E_{1}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)
?

Note that if for
j\in\{0,1\}
,
E_{j}\in{\rm Int}(L_{p_{j}},L_{q_{j}})
for some
1<p_{j},q_{j}<\infty
, then one can deduce from Junge’s representation and interpolation that
\mathsf{h}_{E_{j}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
embeds complementably into
E_{j}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. Thus, in this special case, the answer to the above question is clearly positive. Even for the particular case where
E_{0}=L_{p}
and
E_{1}=L_{q}
, we do not know if the assumptions on
p
and
q
in Proposition
4.3
can be removed when
0<p<q\leq 1
. This of course is closely related to asking whether the statement about
K
-functionals in Proposition
3.7
is valid for any
0<p<q\leq 1
.

Next, we make the observation that a well-known result for convex functions extends to the general setting of
p
-convex functions for
0<p<1
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
0<p<q<\infty
, and
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
. There exists a constant
c_{E}
such that the following holds: if
f
,
g
are functions such that
\|g\|_{L_{\Phi}}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\Phi}}
for every function
\Phi
that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave, and if
f\in E
, then
g\in E
with

\|g\|_{E}\leq c_{E}\|f\|_{E}.

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
as in the statement of the proposition.
Using the Orlicz space description of
L_{p}+tL_{q}
in the previous section, we have from the assumption that

K(g,t;L_{p},L_{q})\lesssim_{p,q}K(f,t;L_{p},L_{q}),\quad t>0.

According to Proposition
4.2
, the interpolation space
E
is given by a
K
-method. Fix a function space
\mathcal{F}
so that for every
h\in E
,

\|h\|_{E}\approx_{E}\|t\mapsto K(h,t;L_{p},L_{q})\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

We may now deduce from the inequality on
K
-functionals that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{E}

\displaystyle\approx_{E}\|t\mapsto K(g,t;L_{p},L_{q})\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{E}\|t\mapsto K(f,t;L_{p},L_{q})\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\approx_{E}\|f\|_{E}.

This verifies the desired conclusion.
∎

Our first result in this section is a comparison between conditioned column space and column space for a class of symmetric spaces.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
0<p<q<2
and
F\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
. There exists a constant
C_{F}
such that for any
x\in F^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, the following holds:

\big\|x\big\|_{F(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq C_{F}\big\|x\big\|_{F^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Similarly, if
\Phi
is an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave for
0<p<q<2
then there exists a constant
C_{p,q}
so that for any sequence
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
with
\sigma_{c}(x)\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
,

\tau\big[\Phi\big(\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)\big)\big]\leq C_{p,q}\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sigma_{c}(x)\big)\big]

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave.
According to Theorem
2.8
, if
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the column vector
\overline{x}=\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\otimes e_{k,1}
admits a factorization
\overline{x}=\alpha\,.\,\beta
where
\alpha
is a strictly lower triangular matrix taking values in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and
\beta\in L_{\Theta}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
where
\Theta
is the Orlicz function satisfying
L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
(as described in Theorem
2.8
) and

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))}\ .\ \big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

From the factorization of
L_{\Phi}
, it follows that

\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))}\leq\big\|\alpha\big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))}\ .\ \big\|\beta\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Combining the two inequalities leads to

\big\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|\sigma_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}.

By density, we may restate this as there is a map
J:L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
with
J(x)=x
for
x\in\mathfrak{F}
. For simplicity, we write
J(y)=y
for arbitrary
y\in L_{\Phi}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

Now let
\xi
be an element of
F^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Then, by definition,
U(\xi)\in F(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
and from boundedness of
J
implies

\big\|\mu(\xi)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|\mu(U(\xi))\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}

where the generalized singular value on the left hand side is taken with respect to
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
and the one the right hand side is taken with respect to
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
. It follows from Proposition
4.4
that there exists a constant
c_{F}
such that

\big\|\mu(\xi)\big\|_{F}\leq C_{F}\big\|\mu(U(\xi))\big\|_{F}.

This is equivalent to

\big\|\xi\big\|_{F(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq C_{F}\big\|\xi\big\|_{F^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

which is the desired conclusion.

For the
\Phi
-moment version, it suffices to repeat the above argument but using Remark
2.9
in place of Theorem
2.8
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.5
, we have the following extension of the reverse dual Doob inequality proved in
[
26
, Theorem 7.1]
for noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces (
0<p<1
) to the general case of noncommutative symmetric quasi-Banach spaces.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space with
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
for
0<p<q<1
. There exists a constant
C_{E}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(a_{k})
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the following holds:

\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{E}\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(a_{k})\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Similarly, if
\Phi
is an Orlicz space that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave for
0<p<q<1
, then there exists a constant
C_{p,q}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(a_{k})
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the following holds:

\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\big)\big]\leq C_{p,q}\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(a_{k})\big)\big]

Proof.

Let
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
with
0<p<q<1
.
Let
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive operators in
\mathfrak{F}
. If
E^{(2)}
is the
2
-convexification of
E
, then
E^{(2)}\in{\rm Int}(L_{2p},L_{2q})
. The conclusion follows immediately from the first inequality in Theorem
4.5
using
F=E^{(2)}
and
(x_{k})=(a_{k}^{1/2})
. Similar argument applies to the
\Phi
-moment case using
the second inequality in Theorem
4.5
to the Orlicz function
t\mapsto\Phi(t^{2})
.
∎

The next result extends
[
20
, Theorem 4.11]
to the case of noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces associated with symmetric function spaces and moment inequalities.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
0<p<q<2
.
If
F\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
then there exists a constant
C_{F}
such that for every
x\in\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, the following two inequalities hold:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{F}\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}

and

\big\|x\big\|_{F(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{F}\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}.

Similarly, if
\Phi
is
p
-convex and
q
-concave for
0<p<q<2
then there exists a constant
C_{p,q}
so that for every
x\in\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we have

\max\Big\{\tau\big[\Phi\big(S_{c}(x)\big)\big];\tau\big[\Phi\big(|x|\big)\big]\Big\}\leq C_{p,q}\tau\big[\Phi\big(s_{c}(x)\big)\big].

Proof.

\bullet
For the first inequality, let
y
be a martingale in
\mathfrak{F}(M)
. Repeating the argument in the proof of Theorem
4.5
with the martingale difference sequence
(dy_{k})
, we have for every
p
-convex and
q
-concave Orlicz function
\Phi
:

\big\|y\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|y\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}.

By density, the above inequality shows that there exists a bounded linear map
I:\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with
I(y)=y
for every
y\in\mathfrak{F}(M)
. For simplicity, for a given
x\in\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we will denote
I(x)
by
x
. That is, for
x\in\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
we may state:

\big\|\mu(S_{c}(x))\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|\mu(|U{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(x)|)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}}

where the generalized singular numbers are computed in the appropriate von Neumann algebras.
By Proposition
4.4
, there exists a constant
C_{F}
so that
whenever
x\in\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we have
\mu(|U{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(x)|)\in F
and

\big\|\mu(S_{c}(x))\big\|_{F}\leq C_{F}\big\|\mu(|U{\mathcal{D}}_{c}(x)|)\big\|_{F}.

This is equivalent to the first inequality:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{F}\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})}.

\bullet
In view of the argument above, it suffices to verify the second inequality for the case of Orlicz function spaces. For this special case, our proof below is modeled after the argument used in
[
7
, Corollary 3.14]
for the case of
L_{p}
-spaces. Let
\Phi
be a
p
-convex and
q
-concave Orlicz function and
x
be a martingale in
\mathfrak{F}(M)
.
By approximation, we assume that for every
n\geq 1
,
s_{c,n}(x)
is invertible with bounded inverse. As above, we denote
s_{c,n}(x)
by
s_{n}
and we take
s_{0}=0
.

Let
\lambda>0
. We write
x=\sum_{l\geq 1}a_{l}b_{l}
where for every
l\geq 1
, we set:

a_{l}=\sum_{n\geq l}dx_{n}\Psi(\lambda s_{n})(\Psi(\lambda s_{l})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda s_{l-1})^{-1})^{1/2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ b_{l}=(\Psi(\lambda s_{l})^{-1}-\Psi(\lambda s_{l-1})^{-1})^{1/2}.

Using the factorization
L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
, we may deduce that:

\displaystyle\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{l\geq 1}a_{l}b_{l}\Big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}a_{l}a_{l}^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{2}.\Big\|\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}b_{l}^{*}b_{l}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}\big\|a_{l}\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\big\|\Psi(\lambda s)^{-1/2}\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M})}.

Following the same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
2.8
, there exists a constant
C_{p,q}
and a corresponding choice of
\lambda
so that

\Big(\sum_{l\geq 1}\big\|a_{l}\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}

and

\big\|\Psi(\lambda s)^{-1/2}\big\|_{L_{\Theta}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1.

This yields that the inequality in the statement is verified for Orlicz function spaces. More precisely,

(4.1)

\big\|x\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{p,q}\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})}.

The case of moment inequalities follows directly from the moment part of Theorem
4.5
and from using
\lambda=1
in the proof above.
∎

We now proceed with further application of Proposition
2.12
. Below, we show that noncommutative Davis decompositions can be easily deduced from factorizations of adapted sequences. We refer to
[
25
]
,
[
34
]
, and
[
43
]
for various forms of noncommutative Davis decompositions. We refer to
[
44
]
for formal definition of the noncommutative vector-valued space
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{1}^{c})
used below.

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume that
0<p<q<2
and
\Phi
is an Orlicz function that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave. Given an adapted sequence
\xi=(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, there exist two adapted sequences
y=(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
z=(z_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that:

(i)

\xi=y+z
;

(ii)

y\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{1}^{c})
with
\big\|y\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{1}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\xi\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
;

(iii)

z=\lambda a
where
a
is an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom and

\lambda\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\xi\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
.

In particular,

\big\|y\big\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{1}^{c})}+\big\|z\big\|_{L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\xi\|_{L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
be an Orlicz function such that
L_{\Phi}=L_{2}\odot L_{\Theta}
. Consider the factorization
\xi=\alpha\,.\,\beta
where
\alpha
is a lower triangular matrix in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
and
\beta\in L_{\Theta}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

according to Proposition
2.12
. Define the strictly lower triangular matrix
\alpha^{-}
by setting
\alpha^{-}_{n,j}=\alpha_{n,j}
for
1\leq j<n
and the diagonal matrix
d=\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n,n}\otimes e_{n,n}
. Clearly,

\alpha=\alpha^{-}+d
. Set:

y=d\,.\,\beta\quad\text{and}\quad z=\alpha^{-}\,.\,\beta.

Then
\xi=y+z
and from Proposition
2.12
(ii),
y
and
z
are adapted. Since
\alpha^{-}
is strictly lower triangular and
\beta
is adapted,
z
is a scalar multiple of an algebraic
L_{\Phi}^{c,\rm cond}
-atom. The verification of the norm estimates are straightforward.
∎

We now turn our attention to Davis type inequalities involving other classes of symmetric spaces of measurable operators using interpolation results from the previous section. The next result deals with the case of Lorentz spaces.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\xi=(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an adapted sequence that belongs to
L_{2/3}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Then there exist two adapted sequences
y=(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
z=(z_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{2/3}(\mathcal{M})\cap L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
such that:

(i)

\xi=y+z
;

(ii)

for every
2/3<p<2
and
0<q\leq\infty
, the following holds:

\big\|y\big\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}+\big\|z\big\|_{L_{p,q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\big\|\xi\big\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Proof.

The key ingredients for the proof are the decomposition from
[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
and the two interpolations results from Proposition
3.12
and Theorem
3.13
.

Fix

\xi\in L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.
Choose a discrete representation of
\xi
with respect to the couple
(L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))
,

\xi=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\xi^{(\nu)}

with
\xi^{(\nu)}\in L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
for every
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, the series is convergent in
L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, and
so that

J\big(\xi^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\leq 4K\big(\xi,2^{\nu},L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big).

We refer to
[
3
, Lemma 3.3.2]
for the existence of a representation satisfying the properties discribed above.

For each
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, we apply
[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
to the adapted sequence
\xi^{(\nu)}
: there exists two adapted sequences
y^{(\nu)}
and
z^{(\nu)}
such that
\xi^{(\nu)}=y^{(\nu)}+z^{(\nu)}
,

J\big(y^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{2/3}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}))\big)\lesssim J\big(\xi^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big),

and

J\big(z^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{2/3}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\lesssim J\big(\xi^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{2/3}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big).

It follows that for
0<\theta<1
and
0<q\leq\infty
, we have:

(4.2)

\big\|(J(y^{(\nu)},2^{\nu}))_{\nu}\big\|_{\lambda^{\theta,q}}\lesssim_{\theta,q}\big\|(K(\xi,2^{\nu}))_{\nu}\big\|_{\lambda^{\theta,q}}

and

(4.3)

\big\|(J(z^{(\nu)},2^{\nu}))_{\nu}\big\|_{\lambda^{\theta,q}}\lesssim_{\theta,q}\big\|(K(\xi,2^{\nu}))_{\nu}\big\|_{\lambda^{\theta,q}}

where for a given sequence
(a_{\nu})_{\nu}
of scalars, we use the quasi-norm

\big\|(a_{\nu})_{\nu}\big\|_{\lambda^{\theta,q}}:=\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(2^{-\nu\theta}|a_{\nu}|\big)^{q}\Big)^{1/q}.

Let
y=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}y^{(\nu)}
and
z=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{(\nu)}
. It is clear
y
and
z
are adapted sequences and
\xi=y+z
.
For any given
2/3<p<2
and
0<q\leq\infty
, choose
\theta=3/2-1/p
.
It clearly follows from (
4.2
) and Theorem
3.13
that:

\big\|y\big\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}\lesssim_{p,q}\|\xi\|_{L_{p,q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Similarly, we may deduce from (
4.3
) and Proposition
3.12
that

\big\|z\big\|_{L_{p,q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{p,q}\|\xi\|_{L_{p,q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}}.

The desired inequality follows from combining the last two inequalities.
∎

We isolate the following important example. Motivated by the noncommutative Khintchine inequality for weak-
L_{1}
spaces (
[
4
]
), we state below a weak-
L_{1}
-version of the Davis-decomposition for adapted sequences. This appears to be new even for the classical setting.

Example 4.10
.

There exists a constant
C>0
so that for every adapted sequence
\xi\in L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, there exist two adapted sequences
y
and
z
such that:

(i)

\xi=x+z
;

(ii)

\big\|y\big\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}+\big\|z\big\|_{L_{1,\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq C\big\|\xi\big\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
.

It is worth pointing out that the preceding example allows us to deduce the noncommutative weak-type
(1,1)
version of the Burkholder/Rosenthal
(
[
41
, Theorem 3.1]
) from the simpler weak-type inequality involving square functions given in
[
40
, Theorem 2.1]
.

The idea used in the proof of Proposition
4.9
can be extended for the case of general symmetric spaces for the Banach space range. More precisely, we have the following result:

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
E
be a Banach function space.
If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for
1<q<2
, then

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})+E^{\rm cond,ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

where
E^{\rm cond,ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
denotes is the subspace of
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
consisting of adapted sequences.

Sketch of the proof.

It is easy to see that under the assumption,
E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})\subseteq E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. On the other hand, we have from Theorem
4.5
that

E^{\rm cond,ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\subseteq E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Thus, we only need to verify one inequality.

The proof rests upon few facts. The interpolation space
E
is given by a
K
-method, the simultaneous nature of Proposition
4.9
above, and Proposition
4.3
.

Since
E
is given by a
K
-method, we may fix a Banach function space
\mathcal{F}
such that for any given semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
,

C_{E}^{-1}\big\|a\big\|_{\mathcal{F};K}\leq\big\|a\big\|_{E(\mathcal{N})}\leq\big\|a\big\|_{\mathcal{F};K}\ ,\quad a\in E(\mathcal{N}).

Under the assumption
1<q<2
and Proposition
4.3
, we may also state that for every sequence
\xi\in E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
,

\big\|\xi\big\|_{\big(L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)_{\mathcal{F};K}}\approx_{E}\big\|\xi\big\|_{E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Similarly, for every
\zeta\in E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
,

\big\|\zeta\big\|_{\big(L_{1}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)_{\mathcal{F};K}}\approx_{E}\big\|\zeta\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

We now outline the argument. Fix
\xi\in L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)
. Repeating the argument in the proof of Proposition
4.9
(taking into account the fact that the decomposition in Proposition
4.9
works simultaneously), we obtain a decomposition
\xi=y+z
where
y=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}y^{(\nu)}
and
z=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{(\nu)}

are representations with respect to the couple
(L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}))
and
(L_{1}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))
respectively and further satisfy that for every
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
,

J\big(y^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty});L_{q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})\big)\lesssim_{q}K\big(\xi,2^{\nu},L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)

and

J\big(z^{(\nu)},2^{\nu},L_{1}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\lesssim_{q}K\big(\xi,2^{\nu},L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big).

Consider the following functions defined on the semi-axis
(0,\infty)
:

f(t)=J(y^{(\nu)},2^{\nu})\quad\text{for}\ t\in[2^{\nu},2^{\nu+1}),

g(t)=J(z^{(\nu)},2^{\nu})\quad\text{for}\ t\in[2^{\nu},2^{\nu+1}),

and

h(t)=K(\xi,2^{\nu})\quad\text{for}\ t\in[2^{\nu},2^{\nu+1}).

It follows that for every
t>0
,

\max\big\{f(t);g(t)\big\}\lesssim_{q}h(t).

Taking the norms on the function space
\mathcal{F}
, we have

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}+\big\|g\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}\lesssim_{q}\big\|h\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

From the definitions of the three functions, we further get that:

(4.4)

\|y\|_{(L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})_{\mathcal{F};J}}+\|z\|_{(L_{1}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))_{\mathcal{F};J}}\lesssim_{q}\|\xi\|_{(L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))_{\mathcal{F};K}}.

From the equivalence of the
J
-methods and
K
-methods relative the function space
\mathcal{F}
(see for instance,
[
30
, Theorem 2.9]
) and the equivalence of norms stated at the beginning of the proof, we may conclude that:

\|y\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}+\|z\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\lesssim_{E}\|\xi\|_{E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

which is the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The argument in the proof of Theorem
4.11
can be carried out for the larger class
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
with
2/3<p<q<2
to get that every
\xi\in L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
admits a decomposition
into two adapted sequences
y
and
z
satisfying:

\|y\|_{(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})_{\mathcal{F};J}}+\|z\|_{(L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c});L_{q}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))_{\mathcal{F};J}}\lesssim_{E}\|\xi\|_{E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

However, we do not know if the equivalence of the
J
-method and the
K
-method relative to the function space
\mathcal{F}
is valid for the case of quasi-Banach couples.

As an immediate application of Proposition
4.11
, we deduce the next result which partially answers a problem from
[
43
, Remark 3.11]
.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
E
be a Banach function space.
If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for
1<q<2
, then

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

We only need to verify the inclusion
\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})
. That is to show the existence of a constant
\alpha_{E}
such that for every
x\in\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, the following holds:

\inf\left\{\big\|x^{d}\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}}+\big\|x^{c}\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}\right\}\leq\alpha_{E}\big\|x\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}}

where the infimum is taken over all
x^{d}\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})
and
x^{c}\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
such that
x=x^{d}+x^{c}
.

Let
\xi=(dx_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.
It is enough to take
martingales
x^{c}
and
x^{d}
with for every
n\geq 1
,

dx_{n}^{c}=z_{n}-\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(z_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad dx_{n}^{d}=y_{n}-\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(y_{n}),

where
y
and
z
are the adapted sequences from Theorem
4.11
. Clearly,
x=x^{d}+x^{c}
.
Since the map
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\mapsto(\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(a_{n}))_{n\geq 1}
is a contraction in
L_{p}(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})
for every
1\leq p<\infty
, it follows by interpolation that it is also bounded in
E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})
. In particular,

\|x^{d}\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}}\lesssim_{E}\|y\|_{E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})}.

On the other hand, since for every
n\geq 1
,
\mathcal{E}_{n-1}|dx_{n}^{c}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{n-1}|z_{n}|^{2}
, we immediately get that

\|x^{c}\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}\leq\|z\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Combining these two inequalities, we arrive at

\|x^{d}\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}}+\|x^{c}\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}\lesssim_{E}\|\xi\|_{E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We recall that the conclusion of Corollary
4.13
also applies to interpolation space
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{2})
for
1<p<2
(see
[
43
, Theorem 3.9]
).
We suspect that the preceding corollary is valid for any Banach function space in
{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
but our method is restricted to
1<q<2
(see also
[
42
, Problem 4.2]
for a related question).

As examples of spaces that are not covered by previously known results, we consider the general Lorentz space
\Lambda_{1,w}
where
w
is a positive decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
with
\int_{0}^{\infty}w(t)\ dt=\infty
. The Lorentz space
\Lambda_{1,w}
is the linear space consisting of all
f\in L_{0}
such that

\big\|f\big\|_{\Lambda_{1,w}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{t}(f)w(t)\ dt<\infty.

The space
(\Lambda_{1,w},\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda_{1,w}})
is a fully symmetric Banach function space. According to
[
29
]
,
\Lambda_{1,w}
is
r
-concave if and only if
(for
1/r+1/r^{\prime}=1
),

\Big(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}w(s)^{r^{\prime}}\ ds\Big)^{1/r^{\prime}}\lesssim w(t),\quad t>0.

With the preceding criterion, one can isolate the family of weights
w
for which
\Lambda_{1,w}\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for some
1<q<2
and therefore the Davis decomposition applies to martingales from the corresponding Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda_{1,w}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
.

Bijecting hidden symmetries for skew staircase shapes Thanks: August 10, 2026
Zachary Hamaker, Alejandro H. Morales, Igor Pak, Luis Serrano, Nathan Williams

Abstract
. We present a bijection between the set SYT ⁡ ( λ / μ ) {{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu) of standard Young tableaux of staircase minus rectangle shape λ = δ k \lambda=\delta_{k} , μ = ( b a ) \mu=(b^{a}) , and the set ShSYT ′ ​ ( η ) {{\rm ShSYT}}^{\prime}(\eta) of marked shifted standard Young tableaux of a certain shifted shape η = η ⁡ ( k , a , b ) \eta=\eta(k,a,b) . Numerically, this result is due to DeWitt (2012). Combined with other known bijections this gives a bijective proof of the product formula for | SYT ⁡ ( λ / μ ) | |{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)| . This resolves an open problem by Morales, Pak and Panova (2019), and allows an efficient random sampling from SYT ⁡ ( λ / μ ) {{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu) . Other applications include a bijection for semistandard Young tableaux, and a bijective proof of Stembridge’s symmetry of LR–coefficients of the staircase shape. We also extend these results to set-valued standard Young tableaux in the combinatorics of K K -theory , leading to new proofs of results by Lewis and Marberg (2019) and Abney-McPeek, An and Ng (2020).



1.
Introduction

The phrase ‘hidden symmetries’ in the title refers to coincidences
between the numbers of seemingly different (yet similar) sets of combinatorial objects.
When such coincidences are discovered, they tend to be
fascinating because they reflect underlying algebraic
symmetries — even when the combinatorial objects themselves appear to possess
no such symmetries.

It is always a relief to find a simple combinatorial explanation
of hidden symmetries. A direct bijection is
the most natural approach, even if sometimes such a bijection is both hard to find
and to prove (cf.
\lx@sectionsign
5.5
). Such a bijection restores order
to a small corner of an otherwise disordered universe,
suggesting we are on the right path in our understanding.
It is also an opportunity to learn more about our combinatorial objects.

2.5.
Reduced words for fully commutative permutations

The symmetric group is generated by the simple transpositions
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
with relations

s_{i}s_{j}=s_{j}s_{i}\ \ \text{for}\ |i-j|>1,\ \ \ s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ s_{i}^{2}=1.

The first relation is called a
commutation relation
, while the second is called a
braid relation
.
For
w\in W
, we say that
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})
is a
reduced word
of
w
if
w=s_{a_{1}}\dots s_{a_{p}}
and
p=\ell(w)
is the number of inversions in
w
.
Let
\mathcal{R}(w)
denote the set of reduced words of
w
.
In this setting, the
Matsumoto–Tits theorem
(see e.g.
[
Bump
, Thm 25.2]
),
says that
\mathcal{R}(w)
is connected by commutation relations and braid relations.
A permutation
w
is called
fully commutative
if
\mathcal{R}(w)
is connected only by commutation relations.
We summarize a key result about fully commutative permutations from
[
BJS
]
.

Theorem 2.11

(
[
BJS
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
)
.

For each fully commutative permutation
w
, there is a skew shape
\sigma(w)=\lambda/\mu
and a bijection
\Phi:{{\rm SYT}}(\sigma(w))\to\mathcal{R}(w)
, so that

\mathrm{Des}\bigl(\Phi(T)\bigr)\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\mathrm{Des}(T)^{r}.

Our description of
\Phi
follows
[
Ste2
]
.
Put a partial order
(I(w),<)
on the
inversion set

I(w)\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt\bigl\{(i,j)~:~i<j\,,\ w^{-1}(i)>w^{-1}(j)\bigr\}.

We say that
(i,j)\gtrdot(k,l)\in I(w)
if
k=i
and
l=\min\{p>j:(j,p)\not\in I(w)\}
, or
j=l
and
i=\max\{p<k:(p,k)\not\in I(w)\}
.
The skew shape

is now defined to be the poset

.
Each cell of

corresponds to an inversion of

\prod^{i-1}_{j=1}\hskip 1.70709ptL(j)\hskip 1.70709pts_{a_{i}}\,=\,\prod^{i}_{j=1}\hskip 1.70709ptL(j)\hskip 1.70709pt.

5.6.

Random generation (sampling) of combinatorial objects from the (exactly) uniform
distribution is a classical problem in both Combinatorics, see e.g.
[
NW
]
,
and Theoretical Computer Science, see e.g.
[
JVV
]
. The approach of using
determinantal formulas for uniform random generation
of planar structures was introduced by Wilson
[
Wil
]
.

For Young tableaux of staircase minus rectangle shape, our approach is
also greatly superior to the MCMC approach for the
nearly uniform

generation of linear extensions of all posets. Indeed, the best known
general bound is
O(n^{3}\log n)
time for
n
-element posets,
due to Bubley and Dyer
[
BD
]
. In our case, we have
n=\Theta(k^{2})
, giving only a
O(k^{6}\log k)
time, which is
much weaker compared with the
O(k^{3}\log k)
time in
Theorem

1.3
.
It would be interesting to see if a nearly linear MCMC algorithm can be obtain
for nearly uniform sampling from
{{\rm SYT}}(\lambda/\mu)
in our case, or
(even better) for general skew shapes.

Finally, we should mention a detailed complexity analysis of the

NPS algorithm
given in
[
ScS
]
; similar results likely
hold for Fischer’s algorithm
[
Fis
]
. Note that a rough
O(k^{3})
suffices for our purposes as the cost of the algorithm is (marginally)
dominated by the Worley–Sagan insertion.

In particular, no determinantal formula is known for the

in

\lx@sectionsign

. It would be
interesting to show that the number of such tableaux is

-complete.

5.9.

Theorem

1.7
has a geometric explanation in terms
of certain
spherical orbit closures
on the type A flag varieties (see
[
HMP3
, Thm 4.58]
).
Here, the skew Schur functions are geometric representatives
for
Schubert varieties
indexed by fully commutative permutations,
while the Schur
P
–functions represent certain
involution Schubert varieties
.
The use of
\Red(w^{\delta_{n}})
in our proof reflects the fact, shown
in
[
HMP2
, Prop. 3.30]
, that the varieties in question have the same cohomology
representatives.

From this perspective, the desired bijection follows from applying both
\Phi^{-1}

and
involution Coxeter–Knuth insertion

[
HMP3
, Thm 5.17]
to
\Red(w^{\delta_{n}/\mu})
.
In this setting, the involution Coxeter–Knuth insertion restricts to
the Worley–Sagan insertion, recovering
\varphi
.
We use the parallel theory for fixed-point-free involution Schubert varieties in our
proof of
Theorem

4.9
since their
K
-theory is easier to understand.

The corresponding involutions are

\mathcal{I}
-Grassmannian in the sense of
[
HMP3
]
.
By analogy with the map
\Phi
, it is an interesting open question to
give a direct bijection between reduced involution words for

\mathcal{I}
-Grassmannian involutions and marked shifted tableaux
of the appropriate shape. Here, direct can mean either without using
an insertion algorithm or while using
O\bigl(|\lambda|\hskip 0.85355pt\log|\lambda|\bigr)
operations.

5.10.

Our proof of
Theorem

4.9
in fact proves a stronger statement.
If
y
is a FPF-vexillary fixed-point-free involution in the sense of
[
HMP4
]
, we show its symplectic Grothendieck polynomial is a single
GP_{\lambda}
.
The analogous statement for vexillary permutations appears in
[
RTY
, Lem. 5.4]
.
As a consequence, the insertion tableau
P_{SH}
associated to
y
must be a unique rectification target in the sense of
[
BS
]
.
Identifying unique rectification targets is an interesting and challenging question that has received some attention
[
G+
]

5.11.

The limit curves of random standard Young tableaux are of interest in

Most recently, their existence has been shown for a large class of skew
shapes
[

[
AHRV
]
and the shifted staircase
[

[

We implemented our bijection for

. Our code is available
online

[
H++
]
.
It would be interesting to see if there are exact formulas for limit shapes
in this case.

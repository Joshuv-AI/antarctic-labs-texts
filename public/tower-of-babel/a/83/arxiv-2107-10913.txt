Vector Portal Pseudo-Goldstone Dark Matter


Abstract
We present a model of pseudo-Goldstone dark matter that interacts through a light vector mediator based on a spontaneously broken su (2) dark sector. The dark matter mass is induced by the explicit breaking of the dark su (2) symmetry. A residual global u (1) symmetry prevents dark matter decay. The behavior of this model is studied under the assumption that the observed dark matter relic abundance is due to thermal freeze-out. We consider self-interaction targets for small scale structure anomalies and the possibility of interacting with the Standard model through the vector mediator.

2
Symmetry Structure

The scalar sector of our theory consists of an
su
(2)
doublet
H^{i}
and a triplet
\Phi=\phi_{a}T^{a}
where
T^{a}=\sigma^{a}/2
are the generators of
su
(2)
and
\sigma^{a}
are the Pauli matrices. In the limit of no interactions, the full symmetry group of the dark sector is

\displaystyle\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}\times\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}\times\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}=\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}\times\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}\times\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}

(2.1)

with the field transformations for the dark sector given by

\displaystyle\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}:\Phi\to U_{\Phi}\Phi U_{\Phi}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}:H\to U_{H}H

\displaystyle\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}:H\to e^{i\theta_{H}}H

(2.2)

where
U_{\Phi,H}=\exp{\left(i\alpha_{\Phi,H}^{a}T^{a}\right)}
is a
2\times 2
special unitary matrix. By analogy to chiral symmetry breaking, we have expressed the full symmetry group in terms of its so called vector and axial subgroups. The vector subgroup corresponds to the transformations where
U_{\Phi}=U_{H}
while the axial subgroup corresponds to the transformations where
U_{\Phi}=U_{H}^{\dagger}
. We gauge the vector subgroup
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
, explicitly breaking
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
. The global
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
symmetry corresponds to an accidental “Higgs number” symmetry and behaves similar to Standard Model hypercharge.

2.1
Lagrangian and Symmetry Breaking Potential

The most general, renormalizable Lagrangian which respects
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}\times\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}F^{a\mu\nu}+|DH|^{2}+\tr|\mathcal{D}\Phi|^{2}-V(H,\Phi)

(2.3)

where
D
and
\mathcal{D}
are the covariant derivatives for
su
(2)
in the fundamental and adjoint representations respectively. The potential
V(H,\Phi)
is responsible for spontaneously breaking
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}\times\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}\to\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}
as well as explicitly breaking
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
. The most general renormalizable potential invariant under
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}\times\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
can be written in the form

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\phantom{+}\frac{\lambda}{4!}\left(2\tr\Phi^{2}-f_{0}^{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{4!}\left(2|H|^{2}-v_{0}^{2}\right)^{2}+\mu H^{\dagger}\Phi H+\lambda^{\prime\prime}|H|^{2}\tr\Phi^{2}\,.

(2.4)

The first term spontaneously breaks
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}\to\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{\Phi}
, the second term spontaneously breaks
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}\to\varnothing
, and the trilinear term explicitly breaks
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
. The quartic term proportional to
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
introduces mixing between the doublet and the triplet. Additional quartic terms can all be reduced to the term proportional to
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. We neglect terms involving the pseudo-conjugate field
\widetilde{H}^{i}\equiv\epsilon^{ij}H^{\dagger}_{\phantom{{\dagger}}j}
as they violate the
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
symmetry which we assume is respected by the Lagrangian. In the following sections we study the symmetry breaking pattern induced by the vacuum expectation values of the scalar fields
H
and
\Phi
.

2.2
Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking

We assume the vacuum expectation values (vevs)

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\rangle=fT^{3}

\displaystyle\langle H\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v\end{pmatrix}

(2.5)

which break
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}\to\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{\Phi}
and
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}\to\varnothing
respectively. All three generators of
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}
are broken by
\langle H\rangle
while the
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}
generator parallel to
\langle\Phi\rangle
remains unbroken. The resulting spectrum of states consists of two massive radial modes and five massless Goldstone bosons: two corresponding to the broken generators of
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}
and three to the broken generators of
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}
.

While the
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
symmetry is spontaneously broken by (
2.5
), the linear combination

\displaystyle T_{H^{\prime}}=T^{3}+\frac{1}{2}T_{H}\,,

(2.6)

where
T_{H}
is the generator of
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H}
, remains unbroken. In this representation
T_{H}=\mathbb{1}_{2\times 2}
. We denote the symmetry generated by
T_{H^{\prime}}
as
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}
. While only the vector subgroup,
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{V}
, spontaneously broken by
\langle H\rangle
is gauged, the associated dark photon’s interactions respect
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}
as well. We therefore identify the global
charge
of a dark sector state with respect to this symmetry. This residual symmetry ensures the stability of the lightest charged state.

2.3
Explicit Symmetry Breaking

By explicitly breaking the global symmetry group, we can remove all of the massless degrees of freedom from the theory. Our dark sector is constructed to contain two sources of explicit global symmetry breaking:

1.

Gauge bosons from the explicit gauging of the subgroup
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
.

2.

Trilinear mixing between the fundamental scalar
H
and adjoint scalar
\Phi
.

Gauging
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
explicitly breaks
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
since the covariant derivatives are constructed to only respect the symmetry of the gauged subgroup. As a result three massless Goldstone modes are eaten by vector bosons, becoming their longitudinal components. The remaining two degrees of freedom become pseudo-Goldstone bosons (
pgb
) associated with
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
. In the absence of explicit symmetry breaking terms in the potential the
pgb
s are massless at tree level. By introducing a trilinear mixing between
H
and
\Phi
the
pgb
s pick up a finite mass proportional to the root of the order of parameter responsible for the explicit breaking
\mu
. We can see such a term should be allowed by considering a product of irreducible representations of
su
(2)
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{2}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{2}}\otimes\mathbf{3}=\mathbf{5}\oplus\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{3}\oplus\mathbf{1}\,,

(2.7)

noting that the singlet corresponds to the operator
H^{\dagger}\Phi H
. From (
2.2
) we can see this operator is clearly invariant under transformations where
U_{H}=U_{\Phi}
, corresponding to an
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
singlet. Since
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{A}
is not a proper subgroup of
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}\times\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}
, operators of this form which break
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{\Phi}\times\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{H}
may only be singlets under
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
or break the symmetry completely. By removing the massless degrees of freedom we have ensured the lightest stable particle in our dark sector is indeed massive, and thus a viable dark matter candidate.

2.4
Particle Spectrum

Our theory yields a rich spectrum of states. After symmetry breaking, the remaining degrees of freedom consist of three massive gauge bosons, the two scalar radial modes, and two massive
pgb
. We take the limit where

\displaystyle\langle\tr\Phi^{2}\rangle=\frac{f^{2}}{2}\qquad\gg\qquad\langle|H|^{2}\rangle=\frac{v^{2}}{2}

(2.8)

setting the mass scale of the gauge boson associated with the broken
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{V}
subgroup much lower than the gauge bosons corresponding to the other two broken generators. Requiring the mediator to be light and remaining gauge bosons to be heavier than the
pgb

1.

Dark Matter
: Pseudo-Goldstone bosons
\pi^{\pm}
with mass
\sim\sqrt{\mu f}
.

2.

Mediators
: Dark photon
A
and light radial mode
H_{1}
with masses
\sim gv
, and
\lambda^{\prime}v
respectively.

3.

Heavy modes
:
W^{\pm}
gauge bosons and radial mode
H_{2}
with masses
\sim gf
and
\lambda f
respectively.

Due to
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}

W^{\pm}

\pi^{\pm}

\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}

[
33

\displaystyle H=e^{i\frac{\varphi_{H}\cdot T}{v/2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\

\displaystyle\varphi_{\Phi}\cdot T=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varphi_{\Phi}^{+}T^{+}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varphi_{\Phi}^{-}T^{-}

\displaystyle\varphi_{H}\cdot T=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varphi_{H}^{+}T^{+}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varphi_{H}^{-}T^{-}+\varphi_{H}^{0}T^{3}

(3.2)

with
T^{\pm}=T^{1}\pm iT^{2}
. The radial modes

and

3.1
Gauge Boson Masses

The covariant derivatives for the scalar fields are

\displaystyle D_{\mu}H=\partial_{\mu}H-igT^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}H

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\Phi-igA_{\mu}^{a}\left[T^{a},\Phi\right]

(3.3)

where
g
and
A^{a}_{\mu}
are the
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
coupling and gauge field respectively. Evaluating the kinetic terms in the Lagrangian at the vevs gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset g^{2}\left(f^{2}+\frac{v^{2}}{4}\right)W^{+}_{\mu}W^{-}_{\mu}+\frac{g^{2}}{8}v^{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}

(3.4)

where we have defined the mass eigenstates

\displaystyle W^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(A^{1}\mp iA^{2}\right)

\displaystyle A=A^{3}

(3.5)

such that they are labeled according to their
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{H^{\prime}}
charges. The gauge boson masses follow directly from (
3.4
) to be

\displaystyle m_{W}^{2}=g^{2}\left(f^{2}+\frac{v^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle m_{A}^{2}=\frac{g^{2}v^{2}}{4}\,.

(3.6)

Even before we diagonalize the potential, the massive pseudo-Goldstone eigenstates can be identified from the kinetic terms of the Lagrangian:

\displaystyle|DH|^{2}+\text{Tr}\,|\mathcal{D}\Phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\supset-g\left(\frac{v}{2}\partial\varphi_{H}^{+}+f\partial\varphi_{\Phi}^{+}\right)W^{-}+\text{h.c.}-g\frac{v}{2}\partial\varphi_{H}^{0}A\ .

(3.7)

A linear combination of
\varphi_{H}^{\pm}
and
\varphi_{\Phi}^{\pm}
is eaten by
W^{\pm}
since both
\langle\Phi\rangle
and
\langle H\rangle
break
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}_{V}
while
\varphi_{H}^{0}
is only eaten by
A
since the
\text{{{u}}{\footnotesize(1)}}_{V}
subgroup is only broken by
\langle H\rangle
. We define the eaten Goldstone
\varphi_{V}
and the orthogonal state
\varphi_{A}
as

\displaystyle\varphi_{V}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{f\varphi_{\Phi}^{\pm}+(v/2)\varphi_{H}^{\pm}}{\sqrt{f^{2}+(v/2)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\varphi_{A}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{f\varphi_{H}^{\pm}-(v/2)\varphi_{\Phi}^{\pm}}{\sqrt{f^{2}+(v/2)^{2}}}\ .

(3.8)

\varphi_{A}
is the would-be Goldstone of the spontaneously broken axial symmetry .The Goldstone mode
\varphi_{V}^{\pm}
is eaten by
W^{\pm}
contributing to it’s longitudinal polarization. When one ignores the trilinear term in (
2.4
), the
\varphi_{A}^{\pm}
is massless at tree level. However, due to the broken axial symmetry, loops of the gauge bosons induce a radiative mass.

3.2
vevs and Scalar Boson Masses

Minimizing the potential (
2.4
), we find the vevs to be

\displaystyle f^{2}

\displaystyle=f_{0}^{2}+\frac{3v^{2}}{\lambda}\left(\frac{\mu}{2f}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}\right)

\displaystyle v^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{0}^{2}+\frac{3f^{2}}{\lambda^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mu}{f}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}\right)\ .

(3.9)

The radial modes
h
and
\phi
mix in this vacuum. In the basis
\begin{pmatrix}h,&\phi\end{pmatrix}
, their mass matrix is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\lambda^{\prime}v^{2}}{3}&\lambda^{\prime\prime}vf-\dfrac{\mu v}{2}\\
\lambda^{\prime\prime}vf-\dfrac{\mu v}{2}&\dfrac{\lambda f^{2}}{3}+\dfrac{\mu v^{2}}{4f}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.10)

The eigenvalues of (

\displaystyle m_{1,2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{2}\mp\left|\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}_{H}^{2}\right)_{12}}{\sin 2\alpha}\right|

(3.11)

where

is the angle that parameterizes the orthogonal transformation
\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}
that diagonalizes (
3.10
). We define the transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}\\
H_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{+}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\
-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}

(3.12)

where the mixing angle
\alpha
is related to the model parameters by

\displaystyle\tan 2\alpha=\frac{\mu v-2\lambda^{\prime\prime}vf}{\lambda f^{2}/3+\mu v^{2}/4f-\lambda^{\prime}v^{2}/3}\ .

(3.13)

We can expand (
3.11
) in the limit
v\ll f
yielding the approximate eigenvalues

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}v^{2}}{3}-\frac{3v^{2}}{4\lambda f^{2}}\left(\mu-2f\lambda^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{v^{4}}{f^{4}}\right)

(3.14)

\displaystyle m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda f^{2}}{3}+\frac{\mu v^{2}}{4f}+\frac{3v^{2}}{4\lambda f^{2}}\left(\mu-2f\lambda^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{v^{4}}{f^{4}}\right)\ .

(3.15)

In this limit the masses of the radial modes form a hierarchy such that

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}\sim\lambda^{\prime}v^{2}\ll m_{2}^{2}\sim\lambda f^{2}\ .

(3.16)

The trilinear term in (
2.4
) causes the Goldstone modes to mix as well. As we did for the radial modes, we expand (

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{G}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{+}\dfrac{\mu v^{2}}{4f}&-\dfrac{\mu v}{2}\\
-\dfrac{\mu v}{2}&\phantom{+}\mu f\end{pmatrix}\,.

(3.17)

It is easy to see that
\Det\mathcal{M}_{G}^{2}=0
, implying the existence of a zero eigenvalue corresponding to the Goldstone eaten by
W^{\pm}
. The other eigenvalue of (
3.17
) is simply
\tr\mathcal{M}_{G}^{2}
. As we did for the radial modes, we diagonalize
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{2}
with the orthogonal transformation:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}G^{\pm}\\
\pi^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{\Phi}^{\pm}\\
\varphi_{H}^{\pm}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{+}\cos\beta&\sin\beta\\
-\sin\beta&\cos\beta\end{pmatrix}

(3.18)

where
\pi^{\pm}
is a massive
pgb
and

is the massless Goldstone eaten by

. The masses of these states are

\displaystyle m_{G}^{2}=0

\displaystyle m_{\pi}^{2}=\mu f\left(1+\frac{v^{2}}{4f^{2}}\right)\,.

(3.19)

By requiring

\displaystyle\tan\beta=\frac{v}{2f}\,.

The mass eigenstates defined by (

8
Conclusion

We present a model of pseudo-Goldstone boson dark matter and dark photon mediator. The dark matter mass is finite due to the explicit breaking of the axial subgroup of an
\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}\times\text{{{su}}{\footnotesize(2)}}
symmetry. Spontaneous symmetry breaking sets the scale of the dark matter and mediator masses, realizing a rich spectrum of states. A residual global
u
(1)
stabilizes the psuedo-Goldstone states which are assumed to be lightest in the spectrum charged under this symmetry.
We find that the
pgb
states may saturate the observed dark matter relic abundance in the standard thermal freeze-out scenario. For certain benchmark models some small-scale structure anomalies may be resolved by dark matter self-interactions, however the requirement that the dark matter be a thermal relic makes fitting the self-interaction cross section to observations on dwarf galaxy and cluster scales simultaneously difficult. We leave a precise fit of the self-interaction cross section to observed small-scale structure anomalies for future work.
We present direct detection bounds on our
pgbdm
which is assumed to primarily interact with the visible sector through a vector portal. In general a Higgs portal may be considered. However, such interactions introduce further mixing between the radial modes and thus are beyond the scope of this study.

The model presented in this work offers several avenues for further study. One may consider inelastic scattering off of nucleons, in which a
\pi^{\pm}
up-scatters off of a nucleon producing a
W^{\pm}
. This model may be understood as the phase of the model of spin-1 self-interacting dark matter we present in Ref.
[
33
]
, where
m_{W}>m_{\pi}
. Thus, a natural extension of these scenarios is to consider multi-component dark matter in which the observed dark matter abundance consists of a combination of
\pi^{\pm}
and
W^{\pm}
. Such models describe inelastic dark matter which may admit novel phenomenology.
Another exciting possibility is to restore the Higgs portal interaction with the visible sector.

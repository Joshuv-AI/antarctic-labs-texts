Solvability of a class of systems of quadratic integral equations
Yuming Chen, Vitali Vougalter

Abstract
. The article is devoted to the existence of solutions of a certain system of quadratic integral equations in H 1 ​ ( ℝ , ℝ N ) H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N}) . We show the existence of a perturbed solution by using a fixed point technique in the Sobolev space on the real line.

1.
Introduction

The present work deals with the existence of
solutions of the following system of integral equations,

(1.1)

u_{m}(x)=u_{0,m}(x)+[T_{m}u_{m}(x)]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u(y))dy,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

with
1\leq m\leq N
. The vector function involved in problem (
1.1
) is
given by

(1.2)

u(x)=(u_{1}(x),u_{2}(x),...,u_{N}(x))^{T}.

Similarly,

(1.3)

u_{0}(x)=(u_{0,1}(x),u_{0,2}(x),...,u_{0,N}(x))^{T}.

The exact conditions on
u_{0}(x),\ g_{m}(u)
, the linear operators

T_{m}
and the kernels
K_{m}(x)
will be stated below. For each equation
of system (
1.1
) the second term in its
right side is a product of
T_{m}u_{m}(x)
and the integral operator
acting on the function
g_{m}(u)
, for which the sublinear growth will be
demonstrated in the proof of Theorem 1.3. further down. Thus, the system of
integral equations of this kind is called
quadratic
.

The theory of
integral equations has many useful applications in describing numerous
events and phenomena of the real world. It is caused by the fact that such
theory
is frequently applicable in various branches of mathematics, in mathematical
physics, economics, biology as well as in dealing with some real world problems.
Quadratic integral equations arise in the theories of the radiative
transfer and neutron transport, in the kinetic theory of gases, in the design
of the bandlimited signals for the binary communication using the simple
memoryless correlation detection when signals are disturbed by
additive white Gaussian noises (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
11
]

and references therein).
Article
[
1
]
is devoted to the
solvability of a nonlinear quadratic integral equation in the Banach space
of real functions being defined and continuous on a bounded and closed
interval using the fixed point result. Works
[
2
]
and
[
4
]

deal with the existence of solutions for
quadratic integral equations on unbounded intervals. The existence of
solutions for quadratic integral inclusions was discussed in
[
3
]
.
In
[
10
]
the authors study the nondecreasing solutions of a
quadratic integral equation of Urysohn-Stieltjes type.
The solvability of
quadratic integral equations in Orlicz spaces was considered in

[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
.
The integro-differential equations, which may involve either Fredholm or
non-Fredholm operators, arise in mathematical biology when
studying systems with nonlocal consumption of resources and
intra-specific competition (see
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,

[
16
]
and references therein).
The contraction argument was used
in
[
14
]
to evaluate the perturbation to the standing solitary wave
of the Nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation when either the external
potential or the nonlinear term was perturbed. The analogous ideas were used
to demonstrate the persistence of pulses for some reaction-diffusion type
equations (see
[
6
]
).

Suppose that the assumption below is satisfied.

Assumption 1.1.

For
1\leq m\leq N
, the kernels

K_{m}(x):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
do not vanish identically on the real
line, so that
K_{m}(x)\in W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The functions

u_{0,m}(x):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\ u_{0,m}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})

and
u_{0,m}(x)
are nontrivial for some
1\leq m\leq N
.
We also suppose that the linear operators

T_{m}:H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\to H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
are bounded, so that their
norms
0<\|T_{m}\|<\infty
.

Here we use the Sobolev space

(1.4)

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}):=\big\{\phi(x):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}}\ |\ \phi(x)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\ \frac{d\phi}{dx}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\big\}.

It is equipped with the norm

(1.5)

\|\phi\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}:=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+\Big\|\frac{d\phi}{dx}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

For a vector function (
1.2
) we use the norm

(1.6)

\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{N}\Big\|\frac{du_{m}}{dx}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},

with

(1.7)

\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}:=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}.

Another norm relevant to our argument is given by

(1.8)

\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}:=\|K_{m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\Big\|\frac{dK_{m}}{dx}\Big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

We define

(1.9)

Q:=\sqrt{\sum_{m=1}^{N}\|T_{m}\|^{2}\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}},

such that
0<Q<\infty
.

Let the functions
V_{m}(x):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be nontrivial and

V_{m}(x)\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
. Hence,
V_{m}(x)
and its derivative

\displaystyle{\frac{dV_{m}}{dx}}
are bounded on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
it can be trivially checked that the multiplication operators

(1.10)

T_{m}\phi(x):=V_{m}(x)\phi(x),\quad\phi(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})

satisfy Assumption 1.1.

By virtue of the Sobolev inequality in one dimension (see e.g. Sect 8.5 of

[
13
]
), we have

(1.11)

\|\phi(x)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|\phi(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Let us recall the algebra property for our Sobolev space.

For any
\phi_{1}(x),\ \phi_{2}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and

(1.12)

\|\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq c_{a}\|\phi_{1}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\phi_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},

where
c_{a}>0
is a constant.

By means of the Young’s inequality (see e.g.
Section 4.2 of
[
13
]
), we obtain the upper bound on the norm of the
convolution as

(1.13)

\|\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Evidently, inequality (
1.13
) implies the estimate

(1.14)

\Big\|\frac{d}{dx}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{1}(x-y)\phi_{2}(y)dy\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\Big\|\frac{d\phi_{1}}{dx}\Big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Let us look for solutions of the nonlinear system of equations
(
1.1
) of the form

(1.15)

u(x)=u_{0}(x)+u_{p}(x),

with
u_{0}(x)
given by (
1.3
) and

(1.16)

u_{p}(x):=(u_{p,1}(x),u_{p,2}(x),...,u_{p,N}(x))^{T}.

Obviously, we derive the perturbative system

u_{p,m}(x)=

(1.17)

[T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+u_{p,m}(x))]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+u_{p}(y))dy,

with
1\leq m\leq N
.

Let us use a closed ball in the Sobolev space

(1.18)

B_{\rho}:=\{u(x)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ |\ \|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq\rho\},\quad 0<\rho\leq 1.

We seek solutions of the system of equations (
1.17
) as fixed points of
the auxiliary nonlinear problem

(1.19)

u_{m}(x)=[T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{m}(x))]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v(y))dy,

where
1\leq m\leq N
in the ball (
1.18
).

Let us define the closed ball in the space of
N
dimensions as

(1.20)

I:=\Big\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\ |\ |z|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)\Big\}

along with the closed ball in the space of
C^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})

vector-functions, namely

D_{M}:=

(1.21)

\{g(z):=(g_{1}(z),g_{2}(z),...,g_{N}(z))\in C^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ |\ \|g\|_{C^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq M\},

with
M>0
.
In such context the norm

(1.22)

\|g\|_{C^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}:=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\|g_{m}\|_{C^{1}(I)},

(1.23)

\|g_{m}\|_{C^{1}(I)}:=\|g_{m}\|_{C(I)}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big\|\frac{\partial g_{m}}{\partial z_{n}}\Big\|_{C(I)},

where
\|\phi\|_{C(I)}:=\hbox{max}_{z\in I}|\phi(z)|
for
\phi\in C(I)
.

Assumption 1.2.

Let
1\leq m\leq N
. Assume that

g_{m}(z):{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, so that

g_{m}(0)=0
. It is also assumed that
g(z)|_{I}\in D_{M}
and
it does not vanish identically in the ball
I
.

Let
\tau_{g}
be the operator defined by the right side of system (
1.19
),
so that
u=\tau_{g}v
.
Our first main statement is as follows.

Theorem 1.3.

Let Assumptions 1.1 and 1.2 hold and

(1.24)

c_{a}M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)^{2}Q\leq\frac{\rho}{2}.

Then the map
\tau_{g}:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
associated with the system of
equations (
1.19
) is a strict contraction.
The unique fixed point
u_{p}(x)
of this map
\tau_{g}
is the only solution of
problem (
1.17
) in
B_{\rho}
.

Clearly, the resulting solution of system (
1.1
) given by (
1.15
) will
not vanish identically on the real line since all
g_{m}(0)=0
, the operators

T_{m}
are linear and the functions
u_{0,m}(x)
are nontrivial for some

1\leq m\leq N
as assumed.

For the technical purpose we introduce

(1.25)

\sigma:=2c_{a}QM(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)>0.

Our second major proposition is about the continuity of the cumulative solution
of problem (
1.1
) given by formula (
1.15
) with respect to the nonlinear
vector-function
g
.

Theorem 1.4.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem 1.3 are valid.
Let
u_{p,j}(x)
be the unique fixed point of the map

\tau_{g_{j}}:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
for
g_{j},\ j=1,2
and the resulting solution
of problem (
1.1
) with
g(z)=g_{j}(z)
be given by

(1.26)

u_{j}(x)=u_{0}(x)+u_{p,j}(x).

Then

(1.27)

\|u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq

\frac{\sigma}{2M(1-\sigma)}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)\|g_{1}(z)-g_{2}(z)\|_{C^{1}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

We proceed to the proof of our first main result.

2.
The existence of the perturbed solution

Proof of Theorem 1.3.
We choose an arbitrary
v(x)\in B_{\rho}
.
By virtue of (
1.19
) along with (
1.12
), we derive the estimate from
above for
1\leq m\leq N
as

(2.1)

\|u_{m}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

c_{a}\|T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{m}(x))\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v(y))dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Let us consider the right side of (
2.1
). Evidently,

(2.2)

\|T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{m}(x))\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|T_{m}\|(\|u_{0}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

By means of bound (
1.13
), we have

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v(y))dy\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.3)

\|K_{m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Clearly, inequality (
1.14
) gives us

\Big\|\frac{d}{dx}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v(y))dy\Big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.4)

\Big\|\frac{dK_{m}}{dx}\Big\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) easily imply that

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v(y))dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.5)

\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Note that, for
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.6)

g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla g_{m}(t(u_{0}(x)+v(x))).(u_{0}(x)+v(x))dt,

where the dot stands for the scalar product of two vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

Let us recall inequality (
1.11
). Hence, for
v(x)\in B_{\rho}
we have

(2.7)

|u_{0}+v|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

Thus,

|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))|\leq

(2.8)

\hbox{sup}_{z\in I}|\nabla g_{m}(z)|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)+v(x)|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\leq M|u_{0}(x)+v(x)|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}},

with the ball
I
introduced in (
1.20
), which yields

(2.9)

\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

Let us combine the estimates above to derive, for
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.10)

\|u_{m}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq c_{a}M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)^{2}\|T_{m}\|\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})},

such that

(2.11)

\|u(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq c_{a}M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)^{2}Q.

By means of (
1.24
), we obtain that

\|u(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq\rho
.
Hence, the vector-function
u(x)
, which is uniquely determined by (
1.19
)
is contained in
B_{\rho}
as well.
This means that the system of equations (
1.19
) defines a map

\tau_{g}:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
under the stated assumptions.

Our goal is to demonstrate that under the given conditions such map is a strict
contraction. Let us choose
arbitrary
v_{1,2}(x)\in B_{\rho}
. By virtue of the argument above,

u_{1,2}:=\tau_{g}v_{1,2}\in B_{\rho}
. According to (
1.19
), for

1\leq m\leq N

u_{1,m}(x)=

(2.12)

[T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{1,m}(x))]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))dy,

u_{2,m}(x)=

(2.13)

[T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{2,m}(x))]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{2}(y))dy.

By means of (
2.12
) and (
2.13
),

(2.14)

u_{1,m}(x)-u_{2,m}(x)=

[T_{m}v_{1,m}(x)-T_{m}v_{2,m}(x)]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))dy+

[T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{2,m}(x))]\times

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)[g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))-g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{2}(y))]dy.

From (
2.14
) using (
1.12
) we easily deduce that

(2.15)

\|u_{1,m}(x)-u_{2,m}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq c_{a}\|T_{m}v_{1,m}(x)-T_{m}v_{2,m}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\times

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+

c_{a}\|T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{2,m}(x))\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\times

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)[g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))-g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{2}(y))]dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

We derive the estimate from above on the right side of (
2.15
).
Clearly,

(2.16)

\|T_{m}v_{1,m}(x)-T_{m}v_{2,m}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|T_{m}\|\|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Note that by the same reasoning as above

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.17)

\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

Hence, the first term in the right side of formula (
2.15
) can
be bounded from above by

(2.18)

c_{a}\|T_{m}\|\|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}M(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

Thus, it remains to consider the second term in the right side of
(
2.15
). Obviously, for
1\leq m\leq N
,

(2.19)

\|T_{m}(u_{0,m}(x)+v_{2,m}(x))\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|T_{m}\|(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1).

Using (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), we easily derive

\Big\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_{m}(x-y)[g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{1}(y))-g_{m}(u_{0}(y)+v_{2}(y))]dy\Big\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.20)

\|K_{m}\|_{W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))-g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{2}(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Let us express, for
1\leq m\leq N
,

g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))-g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{2}(x))=

(2.21)

\int_{0}^{1}\nabla g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+tv_{1}(x)+(1-t)v_{2}(x)).[v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)]dt.

Formula (
2.21
) implies that

(2.22)

|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))-g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{2}(x))|\leq M|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}},

so that for the norm

\|g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x))-g_{m}(u_{0}(x)+v_{2}(x))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq

(2.23)

M\|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Hence, the second term in the right side of bound (
2.15
) can
be estimated from above by expression (
2.18
) as well.

We arrive at

\|u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq

(2.24)

2c_{a}QM(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+1)\|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Formula (
2.24
) and definition (
1.25
) give us

(2.25)

\|\tau_{g}v_{1}(x)-\tau_{g}v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq\sigma\|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

It can be trivially checked via (
1.24
) that the constant in the right
side of inequality above

(2.26)

\sigma<1.

Therefore, the map
\tau_{g}:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
defined
by system (
1.19
) is a strict contraction under the stated assumptions.
Its unique fixed point
u_{p}(x)
is the only solution of the system of
equations (
1.17
) in the ball
B_{\rho}
.
The resulting
u(x)
given by (
1.15
) solves problem (

).

Let us recall bound (

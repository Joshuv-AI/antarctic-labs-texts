Axisymmetric steady Navier-Stokes flows under suction
Mitsuo Higaki

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove the existence of solutions for the axisymmetric steady Navier-Stokes system around an infinite cylinder under external forces. The solutions are constructed to be decaying at the horizontal infinity, despite an analogue of the Stokes paradox for the linearized system, and having neither periodicity nor decay in the vertical direction. The proof is based on perturbation of the nonlinear system around a suction flow. The class of functions in this paper, which is a subspace of the space of Fourier transformed vector finite Radon measures, is inspired by Giga-Saal (2013) treating rotating boundary layers.

Introduction

We consider the steady Navier-Stokes system in
\Omega

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\nabla p=-u\cdot\nabla u+f&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\operatorname{div}u=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u=b&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\\
u(x,z)\to 0&\mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty.\end{array}\right.

(NS)

Here
\Omega=D\times\mathbb{R}
and
D=\{x=(x^{1},x^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}~|~|x|>1\}
denotes a two-dimensional exterior disk. Thus
\Omega
represents the domain exterior to an infinite straight cylinder. The unknowns are the three-dimensional velocity field
u=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{z})
and the pressure field
p
. The given data are the external force
f
and the boundary condition
b
on
\partial\Omega
. We have used standard notation for derivatives:

\partial_{j}=\partial/\partial x^{j}
,

\partial_{z}=\partial/\partial z
,

\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial^{2}_{j}+\partial_{z}^{2}
,

\nabla=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{z})
,

u\cdot\nabla u=(\sum_{j=1}^{2}u^{j}\partial_{j}+u^{z}\partial_{z})u

and

\operatorname{div}u=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}u^{j}+\partial_{z}u^{z}
.

In this paper, we discuss the existence of solutions for the system (
NS
). A notable property of (
NS
) is that, for given data of a specific form, it is reduced to the Navier-Stokes system in the two-dimensional exterior domain
D
. Indeed, if the data
f,b
are independent of the variable
z
and
f^{z}=b^{z}=0
, the system (
NS
) is equivalent with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{\rm 2d}w+\nabla_{\rm 2d}s=-w\cdot\nabla_{\rm 2d}w+(f_{1},f_{2})&\mbox{in}\ D\\
\operatorname{div}_{\rm 2d}w=0&\mbox{in}\ D\\
w=(b_{1},b_{2})&\mbox{on}\ \partial D\\
w(x)\to 0&\mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty,\end{array}\right.

(NS
2d
)

of which the unknowns are
w=(w_{1},w_{2})
and
s
. The symbols with the subscript “2d” refer to the two-dimensional operators on
D
corresponding to the three-dimensional ones on
\Omega
. It is well known that, for general data
f,b,D
, the existence and uniqueness of solutions for (
NS2d
) is widely open. There are difficulties due to lack of embedding in two-dimensional unbounded domains and to the famous Stokes paradox
[
6
,
37
,
15
,
52
,
16
]
caused by logarithmic spatial growth of the fundamental solution of the linearized system. We refer to a recent survey
[
35
]
for an overview of the literature and challenging questions left open.

These aspects are deeply related to controlling the behavior of weak solutions far away from the boundaries, and hence, the zero condition at spatial infinity in (
NS2d
) requires careful handling. There are two methods that guarantee the existence of solutions. The one is to impose symmetry on given data; see
[
15
,
51
,
60
,
16
,
50
,
61
,
62
]

for the existence of solutions and
[
48
,
62
]
for the uniqueness. The other is to perturb the system. Taking a
(-1)
–homogeneous smooth solution of the Navier-Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, we perturb (
NS2d
) around it for a suitable boundary condition
(b_{1},b_{2})
to obtain a new nonlinear problem. As a prototypical example, denoting by
(r,\theta)
the polar coordinates on
D

\displaystyle\begin{split}&x_{1}=r\cos\theta,\quad x_{2}=r\sin\theta,\quad r=|x|\geq 1,\quad\theta\in[0,2\pi),\end{split}

(1.1)

we may take the following exact solution, which is also one of the Hamel solutions
[
24
]
:

{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,\gamma}(r,\theta)=\frac{\alpha}{r}(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)-\frac{\gamma}{r}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\quad\gamma,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

This is a linear combination of a rotating flow and a flow carrying flux
-2\pi\gamma
; see
[
57
,
23
]
for the characterization of the Hamel solutions as solutions to the Navier-Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. The idea of the perturbation method is to use scalar transport by
{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,\gamma}
, whose effect is expected to localize fluid flow and to improve spatial decay of vorticity. Once such an improved decay is obtained, we associate with it the velocity field by using the streamfunction. The known results along this approach are summarized as follows.

•

The case with
\gamma=0
is considered in Hillairet-Wittwer
[
30
]
. The transport by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,0}\cdot\nabla_{\rm 2d}\omega=\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}\omega\end{split}

(1.2)

improves spatial decay of solutions to the linearized problem. Moreover, when
|\alpha|
is sufficiently large, more precisely, when
|\alpha|>\sqrt{48}
, the existence of solutions is proved in the form of
w={\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,0}+o(r^{-1})
as
r\to\infty
. These solutions are driven by Dirichlet boundary data on which no symmetry is imposed. This highlights the difference between
[
30
]
and the works above under symmetry
[
15
,
51
,
60
,
16
,
50
,
61
,
62
]
. We refer to
[
46
]
for a related result in the two-dimensional whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

•

The case with
\gamma\neq 0
is considered in
[
26
]
. The transport by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,\gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\rm 2d}\omega=\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}\omega-\frac{\gamma}{r}\partial_{r}\omega\end{split}

(1.3)

improves the spatial decay as in
[
30
]
. The existence of solutions is also obtained if
\gamma
is sufficiently large, more precisely, if
\gamma>2
in the class
w={\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,\gamma}+o(r^{-1})
as

. Unlike
[
30
]
, however, the existence is verified for all

. Therefore, the existence essentially relies on the presence of a pure suction flow
{\mathcal{V}}_{0,\gamma}
. This result is extended in
[
28
]
to the system (
NS
) under the assumption that all data are independent of the variable
z
, which makes the situation similar to that in
[
26
]
.

The aim of this paper is to provide an existence theorem of axisymmetric solutions for (
NS
) by developing the above perturbation method. Recall that a vector field
u
on
\Omega

u=u^{r}(r,\theta,z){\bf e}_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,\theta,z){\bf e}_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,\theta,z){\bf e}_{z},

where
(r,\theta,z)
is the cylindrical coordinates on
\Omega=D\times\mathbb{R}
induced by (
1.1
) and
{\bf e}_{r},{\bf e}_{\theta},{\bf e}_{z}
are respectively the unit vectors in the radial, toroidal, and vertical directions:

{\bf e}_{r}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0),\quad{\bf e}_{\theta}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0),\quad{\bf e}_{z}=(0,0,1),

is said to be axisymmetric if
u^{r},u^{\theta},u^{z}
are independent of
\theta
. The corresponding unsteady system of (
NS
) has been widely studied in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In the absence of swirl, namely when
u^{\theta}=0
, the global well-posedness is established by Ladyženskaja
[
40
]
and Ukhovksii-Yudovich
[
56
]
. The asymptotic behavior of solutions in large time is obtained by Gallay-Šverák
[
17
]
. The global well-posedness in the presence of swirl is an open question and there are extensive works on the regularity of solutions; see for instance
[
8
,
7
,
33
,
41
]
and
[
43
,
42
]
for recent results on the global solutions. The steady system (
NS
) has been studied in various domains in connection to Liouville-type theorems; see for instance
[
8
,
33
,
41
,
34
,
53
,
5
,
55
,
38
,
39
]
. Among others, Kozono-Terasawa-Wakasugi
[
39
]
studies Liouville-type theorems in
\Omega=D\times\mathbb{R}
assuming periodicity in the vertical direction.

In contrast, the existence of solutions for (
NS
) is less investigated; see
[
58
,
49
]
and the references therein for the systems in which the zero condition at horizontal infinity is replaced by the boundary conditions with growth in
(r,z)
at spatial infinity. There is an inherent difficulty analogous to the Stokes paradox
[
6
,
37
,
15
,
52
,
16
]
. Indeed, for given
f,b
independent of
z
and a solution
u
of (
NS
), the component
u^{z}
must be subject to

-\Delta_{{\rm 2d}}u^{z}=-\Delta u^{z}=-(u\cdot\nabla u)^{z}+f^{z}.

Thus the two-dimensional Laplacian
\Delta_{{\rm 2d}}
appears, whose fundamental solution has logarithmic growth in space. This cannot be avoided also in an axisymmetric setting. Therefore, even if the given data
f,b
have specific forms, the solvability of (
NS
) is a subtle issue.

To overcome this difficulty, we adapt the method in
[
30
,
26
]
. However, since the interaction in fluid is more complicated in three-dimensional cases, it is completely nontrivial whether the spatial decay of solutions can be improved by the perturbation. Also, in axisymmetric settings, such improvement cannot be expected from rotating flows around the
z
-axis, as suggested by (
1.2
). We thus take a pure suction flow towards the wall
\partial\Omega

\displaystyle\begin{split}V(r,\theta,z)=V(r,\theta)=-\frac{\gamma}{r}{\bf e}_{r},\quad\gamma>0\end{split}

(1.4)

and perturb around
V
the Navier-Stokes system (
NS
) with the boundary condition

b=(-\gamma x,0)=-\gamma{\bf e}_{r}.

Assuming the form of solutions
u=v+V
and using the formula

v\cdot\nabla V+V\cdot\nabla v=-V\times\operatorname{rot}v+\nabla\Big(\frac{|v+V|^{2}-|v|^{2}-|V|^{2}}{2}\Big),

where
\times
denotes the cross product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we derive the nonlinear problem to be solved

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta v-V\times\operatorname{rot}v+\nabla q=-v\cdot\nabla v+f&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\operatorname{div}v=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
v=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\\
v(x,z)\to 0&\mbox{as}\ |x|\to\infty.\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

By the formulas in Subsection
2.1
, we write (
1.5
) in the cylindrical coordinates as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Big(\Delta-\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)v^{r}+\partial_{r}q=-v\cdot\nabla v^{r}+\dfrac{(v^{\theta})^{2}}{r}+f^{r}&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
-\Big(\Delta+\dfrac{\gamma}{r}\partial_{r}-\dfrac{1-\gamma}{r^{2}}\Big)v^{\theta}=-v\cdot\nabla v^{\theta}-\dfrac{v^{r}v^{\theta}}{r}+f^{\theta}&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
-\Delta v^{z}+\dfrac{\gamma}{r}(\partial_{z}v^{r}-\partial_{r}v^{z})+\partial_{z}q=-v\cdot\nabla v^{z}+f^{z}&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(rv^{r})+\partial_{z}v^{z}=0&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
(v^{r},v^{\theta},v^{z})=(0,0,0)&\mbox{on}\ \{1\}\times\mathbb{R}\\[5.0pt]
(v^{r},v^{\theta},v^{z})\to(0,0,0)&\mbox{as}\ r\to\infty,\end{array}\right.

(NP)

where
\Delta=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}
and
v\cdot\nabla=v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z}
.

Notice that the problem (
NP
) is imposed in the vertically unbounded domain
\Omega\simeq(1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, whereas the two-dimensional one (
NS2d
) is in

. Given the development of the results
[

,
26
]
, it is meaningful to study (

) for external forces

that are neither periodic nor decaying in
\mathbb{R}
. The function space to which

belongs is desirable to include periodic functions in the vertical direction. Thus, with reference to Giga-Saal
[
21
]
treating rotating boundary layers, we work in a space of Fourier transformed finite vector Radon measures
\operatorname{FM}
. In such a space, a function
f(r,z)
on
(1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
periodic in

can be viewed as the Fourier transformation of a sum of Dirac measures, with

-variable coefficients, on
\mathbb{R}
supported at integer points. However, the proofs require some technical advancements, since the results
[
30
,
26
]
make use of mode-by-mode computation by the Fourier series expansion on
\mathbb{T}
. It is emphasized that lack of spatial decay along boundaries in fluid flow is a natural concept in the analysis of boundary layers; see
[
1
,
32
,
3
,
19
,
29
]
for fluids over rough boundaries and
[

,
20
,

,

,

,

,

To state the main result, we introduce function spaces. More details are summarized in Subsection

. For
\rho\geq 0
, we define

(1,\infty)
with weight

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{\infty}_{\rho}=\{f\in L^{\infty}(1,\infty)~|~\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}<\infty\},\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}:=\esssup_{r\in(1,\infty)}\,r^{\rho}|f(r)|.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})=\bigg\{f=g{\rm d}\Lambda+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}\delta_{m}~\bigg|~g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho}),\mkern 9.0mu\{a_{m}\}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{Z},L^{\infty}_{\rho})\bigg\}\end{split}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f\|_{\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})}:=\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|a_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho}}.\end{split}

. Denoting by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})=\{\hat{f}~|~f\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})\}\end{split}

It is a closed subspace of the space of

-valued bounded continuous functions
\operatorname{BUC}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})^{3}=\{f=f^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+f^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta}+f^{z}{\bf e}_{z}~|~f^{r},f^{\theta},f^{z}\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})\}.\end{split}

Notice that any element of

.

The main result in this paper is the existence of solutions of (

Theorem 1.1

Let
\gamma>2
and
2<\rho<3
satisfy
\rho\leq\gamma
. If
f\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})^{3}

and
\|f\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})}
is sufficiently small depending on
\gamma,\rho
, then there is a unique weak solution
v\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho-1})^{3}
of (

). Consequently, there is an axisymmetric weak solution

of (

) with
b=-\gamma{\bf e}_{r}
unique in a suitable class satisfying

\displaystyle u(r,\theta,z)=-\frac{\gamma}{r}{\bf e}_{r}+O(r^{-\rho+1})\quad\mbox{as}\ \,r\to\infty.

Remark 1.2

(i)

To the best of our knowledge, the existence theorem in Theorem
1.1
is new even for vertically periodic data
f
. If such periodicity is assumed, the proofs in this paper are simplified considerably, since the Fourier series expansion is applicable.

(ii)

It is unclear whether the class of external forces
f
in Theorem
1.1
can be extended to the entire
\operatorname{FM}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})^{3}
. This is related to the Radon-Nikodým property
[

, Chapter
\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{I}
, Definition 3]
of the space
L^{\infty}_{\rho+1}
. This point will be discussed in Remark
2.2
.

(iii)

In the presence of a rotating flow, namely, changing
V
in (
1.4
) into

\displaystyle\begin{split}V(r,\theta,z)=V(r,\theta)=\frac{\alpha}{r}{\bf e}_{\theta}-\frac{\gamma}{r}{\bf e}_{r},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\mkern 9.0mu\gamma>0,\end{split}

(1.10)

one can ask whether an analogy of Theorem
1.1
holds. Nevertheless, we need smallness on
|\alpha|
in this case to prove the existence of solutions by the method in this paper. This restriction is due to the interaction between the swirl

and the rotating flow. Hence there is a clear difference between the existence theorem for (

) and that for the two-dimensional system in
[
26
]
in which no smallness on

is needed. The proof of the existence of solutions when

will be outlined in Appendix

The proof of Theorem

is based on a representation formula for the linearized problem of (

) in Section

It is worth noting that the difficulties in this part have similarity to those in estimating at large time the semigroup for the Stokes system in two-dimensional exterior domains

]
or for the perturbed system around Hamel solutions

2.1
Operators

This subsection collects the operators in the cylindrical coordinates on
\Omega
. Let
(r,\theta,z)
be the coordinates defined in the introduction and
{\bf e}_{r},{\bf e}_{\theta},{\bf e}_{z}
be the unit vectors.

Let
v
be an axisymmetric vector field on
\Omega
, namely,

v(r,\theta,z)=v^{r}(r,z){\bf e}_{r}+v^{\theta}(r,z){\bf e}_{\theta}+v^{z}(r,z){\bf e}_{z}.

Then, by the well-known formulas for
u=u^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+u^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta}+u^{z}{\bf e}_{z}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{div}u&=\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(ru^{r})+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}u^{\theta}+\partial_{z}u^{z},\\
\operatorname{rot}u&=\Big(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}u^{z}-\partial_{z}u^{\theta}\Big){\bf e}_{r}+(\partial_{z}u^{r}-\partial_{r}u^{z}){\bf e}_{\theta}+\dfrac{1}{r}\Big(\partial_{r}(ru^{\theta})-\partial_{\theta}u^{r}\Big){\bf e}_{z},\end{split}

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{div}v&=\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(rv^{r})+\partial_{z}v^{z},\\
\operatorname{rot}v&=-\partial_{z}v^{\theta}{\bf e}_{r}+(\partial_{z}v^{r}-\partial_{r}v^{z}){\bf e}_{\theta}+\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(rv^{\theta}){\bf e}_{z}.\end{split}

(2.1)

The vector Laplacian on
v
is represented as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\{\Big(\Delta-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)v^{r}\Big\}{\bf e}_{r}+\Big\{\Big(\Delta-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)v^{\theta}\Big\}{\bf e}_{\theta}+(\Delta v^{z}){\bf e}_{z},\end{split}

(2.2)

where
\Delta
denotes the differential operator on axisymmetric scalar fields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}.\end{split}

(2.3)

Moreover, by the formula for
W=W^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+W^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta}

and

\omega=\omega^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+\omega^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta}+\omega^{z}{\bf e}_{z}

\displaystyle\begin{split}W\times\omega&=(W^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+W^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta})\times(\omega^{r}{\bf e}_{r}+\omega^{\theta}{\bf e}_{\theta}+\omega^{z}{\bf e}_{z})\\
&=W^{\theta}\omega^{z}{\bf e}_{r}-W^{r}\omega^{z}{\bf e}_{\theta}+(W^{r}\omega^{\theta}-W^{\theta}\omega^{r}){\bf e}_{z},\end{split}

(2.4)

we have, for
V
in (
1.4
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}V\times\operatorname{rot}v&=-V^{r}(\operatorname{rot}v)^{z}{\bf e}_{\theta}+V^{r}(\operatorname{rot}v)^{\theta}{\bf e}_{z}\\
&=\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(rv^{\theta}){\bf e}_{\theta}-\frac{\gamma}{r}(\partial_{z}v^{r}-\partial_{r}v^{z}){\bf e}_{z}.\end{split}

(2.5)

2.2
Function spaces

This subsection collects the definitions and properties of vector-valued function spaces used in this paper. The reader is referred to

Let
E
be a Banach space. We denote by

the

\Lambda
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(E)}

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
denote the space of rapidly decreasing functions. We extend the Fourier transformation on

\hat{f}(\zeta)=\mathcal{F}f(\zeta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0muz\zeta}f(z){\rm d}z

by duality to the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R},E)
, the set of bounded linear operators from

to

. This extension is again denoted by

or

for

to the space

or

.

Let

denote the Borel

-algebra over

and
\mathcal{A}
a
\sigma
-algebra over

. A mapping

. The space

is a Banach space under the total variation norm

Let

and

be the space in (

) in the introduction. It follows by definition that

is a closed subspace of

. We define a multiplication on
\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
as follows. For given

written as

we define the convolution

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)}&=\bigg(g^{(1)}\ast g^{(2)}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(a^{(1)}_{m}\tau_{m}g^{(2)}+a^{(2)}_{m}\tau_{m}g^{(1)})\bigg){\rm d}\Lambda\\
&\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{m=k+l}a^{(1)}_{k}a^{(2)}_{l}\bigg)\delta_{m},\end{split}

(2.6)

where

is the translation

.
Then it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}\leq\|f^{(1)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}\|f^{(2)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}\end{split}

(2.7)

by using the Young inequality. Thus the convolution (

\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
. Moreover, if additionally

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}})}\leq\|f^{(1)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{1}})}\|f^{(2)}\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{2}})}.\end{split}

(2.8)

By embedding

\hat{f}(\zeta)=\mathcal{F}f(\zeta)=\widehat{g}(r,\zeta)+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}(r)e^{\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mum\zeta},\quad f\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho}).

in (

and is continuously embedded in
\operatorname{BUC}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
. By definition, the value of this norm does not change if the Fourier transformation is replaced by its inverse:

\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\cdot\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}=\|\mathcal{F}\cdot\|_{\operatorname{X}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}.

Also, the multiplication is well-defined. Indeed, for
\widehat{f^{(1)}},\widehat{f^{(2)}}\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
, from

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{f^{(1)}}\widehat{f^{(2)}}=\widehat{f^{(1)}\ast f^{(2)}}\end{split}

and (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\widehat{f^{(1)}}\widehat{f^{(2)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}\leq\|\widehat{f^{(1)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}\|\widehat{f^{(2)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}.\end{split}

Moreover, if additionally
\widehat{f^{(1)}}\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho_{1}})
and
\widehat{f^{(2)}}\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho_{2}})
, by (

), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\widehat{f^{(1)}}\widehat{f^{(2)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}})}\leq\|\widehat{f^{(1)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{1}})}\|\widehat{f^{(2)}}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho_{2}})}\end{split}

Lemma 2.1

Let
\rho,\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\geq 0
. Then the following hold.

(1)

Moreover,

3
Axisymmetric Biot-Savart law

In this section, we define the axisymmetric Biot-Savart law. Its applications given in Subsection
3.2
are essential when studying the linearized problem of (
NP
) in the introduction.

For a given scalar field
\omega=\omega(r,z)
, we consider the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Big(\partial_{r}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}\Big)\widetilde{\psi}=r\omega&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
\widetilde{\psi}=0&\mbox{on}\ \{1\}\times\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

The solution
\widetilde{\psi}
is called the axisymmetric streamfunction or the Stokes streamfunction. In this paper, it is more convenient to use the functions
\psi=\psi(r,z)
defined by

\psi(r,z)=\frac{\widetilde{\psi}(r,z)}{r}.

It is easy to see that
\psi
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Big(\Delta-\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)\psi=\omega&\mbox{in}\ (1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\[10.0pt]
\psi=0&\mbox{on}\ \{1\}\times\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Using this solution
\psi
, we formally define the vector field
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}[\omega]
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{r}(r,z){\bf e}_{r}+\mathcal{V}^{z}(r,z){\bf e}_{z},\\
&\mathcal{V}^{r}(r,z):=-\partial_{z}\psi(r,z),\qquad\mathcal{V}^{z}(r,z):=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\psi(r,z)).\end{split}

(3.2)

The formula
\mathcal{V}
in (
3.2
) is called the axisymmetric Biot-Savart law. It is clear that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\operatorname{div}\mathcal{V}=0,\qquad\operatorname{rot}\mathcal{V}=\omega{\bf e}_{\theta},\qquad\mathcal{V}^{r}|_{\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}}=0.\end{split}

(3.3)

Now we let
\rho\geq 0
and
\omega\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
and will solve (
3.1
) using the Fourier transformation. The transform
\hat{\psi}
solves the boundary value problem

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\hat{\psi}}{{\rm d}r^{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\psi}}{{\rm d}r}+\Big(\zeta^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)\hat{\psi}=\hat{\omega},\quad r>1,\qquad\hat{\psi}(1)=0.

(3.4)

The linearly independent solutions of the homogeneous equation are

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K_{1}(|\zeta|r)\mkern 9.0mu\text{and}\mkern 9.0muI_{1}(|\zeta|r)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[5.0pt]
r^{-1}\mkern 9.0mu\text{and}\mkern 9.0mur&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0,\end{array}\right.

where
K_{\nu}(z),I_{\nu}(z)
are the modified Bessel functions in Appendix
A
, with the Wronskian

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[5.0pt]
2r^{-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0.\end{array}\right.

Thus, if the Fourier transform of
\omega
is given by

\hat{\omega}=h(r,\zeta){\rm d}\Lambda+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{m}(r)\delta_{m},

the solution
\hat{\psi}
of (
3.4
) belonging to
\bigcup_{\rho\geq 0}\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
has the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}(r)=\int_{1}^{\infty}(\hat{\omega}\lfloor\sigma_{1})(r,s){\rm d}s+\int_{1}^{r}(\hat{\omega}\lfloor\sigma_{2})(r,s){\rm d}s+\int_{r}^{\infty}(\hat{\omega}\lfloor\sigma_{3})(r,s){\rm d}s.\end{split}

(3.5)

Here
\hat{\omega}\lfloor\sigma_{i}
denotes the Borel measure

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\omega}\lfloor\sigma_{i}(r,s,\mathcal{O})&=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sigma_{i}(r,s,\zeta)h(s,\zeta){\rm d}\Lambda\\
&\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\cap\mathcal{O}}\sigma_{i}(r,s,m)b_{m}(s),\quad\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\end{split}

(3.6)

and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
are the functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{1}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\dfrac{I_{1}(|\zeta|)}{K_{1}(|\zeta|)}K_{1}(|\zeta|r)sK_{1}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}r^{-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0\end{array}\right.\\
\sigma_{2}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}K_{1}(|\zeta|r)sI_{1}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}r^{-1}s^{2}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0\end{array}\right.\\
\sigma_{3}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{1}(|\zeta|r)sK_{1}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}r&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.7)

A representation of
\psi
is obtained by the inverse Fourier transform of
\hat{\psi}
in (
3.5
)–(
3.7
).

The solution
\hat{\psi}
in (
3.5
)–(
3.7
) of the problem (
3.4
) is unique in

; see for instance
[
31
, Chapter
\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{I}
]
for the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the linear ordinary differential equations with smooth coefficients in the space of distributions.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\Phi}[g](r,\zeta)&=\int_{1}^{r}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)M[g](s,\zeta){\rm d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{r}^{\infty}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)N[g](s,\zeta){\rm d}s\end{split}

(4.13)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}M[g](s,\zeta)&=\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\zeta s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)g^{r}(s,\zeta)+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}(s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s))g^{z}(s,\zeta),\\
N[g](s,\zeta)&=\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\zeta s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)g^{r}(s,\zeta)+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}(s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s))g^{z}(s,\zeta).\end{split}

By the relation (
A.5
) in Appendix
A
, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}(s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s))&=|\zeta|s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s),\\
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}(s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s))&=-|\zeta|s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s).\end{split}

Thus
M[g]
and
N[g]
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}M[g](s,\zeta)&=\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\zeta s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)g^{r}(s,\zeta)+|\zeta|s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s)g^{z}(s,\zeta),\\
N[g](s,\zeta)&=\mathrm{i}\mkern 1.0mu\zeta s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)g^{r}(s,\zeta)-|\zeta|s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s)g^{z}(s,\zeta).\end{split}

(4.14)

The function
c[g](\zeta)
is chosen so that
d[h](\zeta)
in Lemma
3.1
with
h=\hat{\omega}
is zero, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}sK_{1}(|\zeta|s)\Big(c[g](\zeta)s^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)+\hat{\Phi}[g](s,\zeta)\Big){\rm d}s=0,\quad\zeta\neq 0,\end{split}

which leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}c[g](\zeta)=-\frac{1}{F(\zeta)}\int_{1}^{\infty}sK_{1}(|\zeta|s)\hat{\Phi}[g](s,\zeta){\rm d}s,\quad\zeta\neq 0.\end{split}

(4.15)

Here
F(\zeta)
is the nonnegative function

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(\zeta):=\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{1}(|\zeta|s)K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s){\rm d}s,\quad\zeta\neq 0,\end{split}

(4.16)

of which quantitative estimates are given in Lemma
A.2
.

In the following proof, Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
will be used without any reference. Let us estimate the right-hand side of (
4.12
). First we consider the second term
\hat{\Phi}[g]
since the first term depends on it. Using (
4.14
), we start from

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)M[g](s,\zeta)|\\
&\lesssim|\zeta|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\Big(I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)+I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s)\Big)|g(s,\zeta)|,\\
&|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)N[g](s,\zeta)|\\
&\lesssim|\zeta|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\Big(K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|s)+K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(|\zeta|s)\Big)|g(s,\zeta)|.\end{split}

When
0<|\zeta|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)M[g](s,\zeta)|\\
&\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\gamma-1}s^{\gamma+1}(|\zeta|s+1)|g(s,\zeta)|&\mbox{if}\ s\leq r\leq|\zeta|^{-1}\\[5.0pt]

r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-|\zeta|(r-s)}|g(s,\zeta)|&\mbox{if}\ |\zeta|^{-1}\leq s\leq r\end{array}\right.\\
&\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\gamma-1}s^{\gamma-\rho}\|g(\zeta)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rho+1}}&\mbox{if}\ s\leq r\leq|\zeta|^{-1}\\[5.0pt]

(4.17)

and

(4.18)

When

\displaystyle\begin{split}|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)M[g](s,\zeta)|&\lesssim r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-|\zeta|(r-s)}|g(s,\zeta)|\\

(4.19)

and

(4.20)

Then we see from (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{1}^{r}|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)M[g](s,\zeta)|{\rm d}s+\int_{r}^{\infty}|r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}(|\zeta|r)N[g](s,\zeta)|{\rm d}s\\

(4.21)

and from (

Now the estimates (

Thanks to (

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\hat{v}^{\theta}}{{\rm d}r^{2}}-\frac{1+\gamma}{r}\frac{{\rm d}\hat{v}^{\theta}}{{\rm d}r}+\Big(\zeta^{2}+\dfrac{1-\gamma}{r^{2}}\Big)\hat{v}^{\theta}=\hat{f}^{\theta},\quad r>1

(4.42)

with

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{\theta}(1)=0,\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}\hat{v}^{\theta}(r)=0.

(4.43)

The linearly independent solutions of the homogeneous equation are

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|r)\mkern 9.0mu\text{and}\mkern 9.0mur^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|r)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[5.0pt]
r^{-\gamma+1}\mkern 9.0mu\text{and}\mkern 9.0mur^{-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0\end{array}\right.

with the Wronskian

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[5.0pt]
(\gamma-2)r^{-\gamma-1}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0.\end{array}\right.

Thus, if the Fourier transform of
f^{\theta}
is given by

\hat{f}^{\theta}=g^{\theta}(r,\zeta){\rm d}\Lambda+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{\theta}_{m}(r)\delta_{m},

the solution
\hat{v}^{\theta}
of (
4.42
) belonging to
\bigcup_{\rho\geq 0}\operatorname{X}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})
has the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{v}^{\theta}(r)=\int_{1}^{\infty}(\hat{f}^{\theta}\lfloor\sigma_{7})(r,s){\rm d}s+\int_{1}^{r}(\hat{f}^{\theta}\lfloor\sigma_{8})(r,s){\rm d}s+\int_{r}^{\infty}(\hat{f}^{\theta}\lfloor\sigma_{9})(r,s){\rm d}s.\end{split}

(4.44)

Here
\hat{f}^{\theta}\lfloor\sigma_{i}
denotes the Borel measure

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{f}^{\theta}\lfloor\sigma_{i}(r,s,\mathcal{O})&=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\sigma_{i}(r,s,\zeta)g^{\theta}(s,\zeta){\rm d}\Lambda\\
&\quad+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\cap\mathcal{O}}\sigma_{i}(r,s,m)a^{\theta}_{m}(s)\delta_{m},\quad\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\end{split}

(4.45)

and
\sigma_{7},\sigma_{8},\sigma_{9}
are the functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{7}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\dfrac{I_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|)}{K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|)}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|r)s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\gamma-2}r^{-\gamma+1}s^{2}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0\end{array}\right.\\
\sigma_{8}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|r)s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}I_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\gamma-2}r^{-\gamma+1}s^{\gamma}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0\end{array}\right.\\
\sigma_{9}(r,s,\zeta)&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}I_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|r)s^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}K_{|\frac{\gamma}{2}-1|}(|\zeta|s)&\mbox{if}\ \zeta\neq 0\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\gamma-2}r^{-1}s^{2}&\mbox{if}\ \zeta=0.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(4.46)

A representation of
\hat{v}^{\theta}
is obtained by the inverse Fourier transform of (

)–(

Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. However, since the proof is standard using the Banach fixed-point theorem, we only present the outline for simplicity.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Define the Banach space

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}=\Big\{w\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho-1})^{3}~\Big|~w|_{\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}}=0,\mkern 9.0mu(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})w\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho})^{2\times 3}\Big\}

with the norm
\|w\|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}}:=\|w\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho-1})}+\|(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})w\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}
. By Proposition
4.1
, for any given
f\in\operatorname{FX}(\mathbb{R},L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})^{3}
and
w\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}
, there is a unique weak solution
v_{w}
to (
LP
) with the components of
-w\cdot\nabla w+f
as an external force. Moreover,
v_{w}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v_{w}\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho-1+j})}&\lesssim\|-w\cdot\nabla w\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})}+\|f\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})}\\
&\lesssim\|w\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho-1})}\|(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})w\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho})}+\|f\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})}.\end{split}

Here Lemma
2.1
and a trivial inequality
\rho+1\leq 2\rho-1
are used. Thus the mapping
T:{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}\ni w\mapsto v_{w}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}
is well-defined. It is easy to check that, if
\|f\|_{\operatorname{FX}(L^{\infty}_{\rho+1})}
and
\delta
are sufficiently small depending on
\gamma,\rho
, then
T
is a contraction on the closed ball
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{\rho}
centered at the origin with radius
\delta
. Hence the existence of weak solutions of (
NP
) unique in
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
follows from the Banach fixed-point theorem. The assertions for the Navier-Stokes system (
NS
) are obvious. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Modified Bessel function

The modified Bessel function of the first kind
I_{\nu}(z)
of order
\nu
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{\nu}(z)=\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{2k},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0],\end{split}

(A.1)

where
\Gamma(z)
denotes the Gamma function, the second kind
K_{\nu}(z)
of order
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
is by

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(z)-I_{\nu}(z)}{\sin(\nu\pi)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0],

(A.2)

and
K_{n}(z)
of order
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is by the limit of
K_{\nu}(z)
in (
A.2
) as
\nu\to n
. It is well known that
I_{\nu}(z)
grows exponentially and
K_{\nu}(z)
decays exponentially as
z\to\infty
in
\{\Re z>0\}
; see
[
2
, Section 4.12]
. Moreover,
K_{\nu}(z)
and
I_{\nu}(z)
are linearly independent solutions of

-\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\omega}{{\rm d}z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z}\frac{{\rm d}\omega}{{\rm d}z}+\Big(1+\frac{\nu^{2}}{z^{2}}\Big)\omega=0

and the Wronskian is

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{\nu}(z)&I_{\nu}(z)\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{{\rm d}K_{\nu}}{{\rm d}z}(z)}&\displaystyle{\frac{{\rm d}I_{\nu}}{{\rm d}z}(z)}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{z}.

It is known that
I_{\nu}(z)
and
K_{\nu}(z)
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}I_{\nu}}{{\rm d}z}(z)&=\frac{\nu}{z}I_{\nu}(z)+I_{\nu+1}(z)=-\frac{\nu}{z}I_{\nu}(z)+I_{\nu-1}(z),\\
\frac{{\rm d}K_{\nu}}{{\rm d}z}(z)&=\frac{\nu}{z}K_{\nu}(z)-K_{\nu+1}(z)=-\frac{\nu}{z}K_{\nu}(z)-K_{\nu-1}(z).\end{split}

(A.5)

The following lemma collects the properties of

and

with

and

. Each of them easily follows from the formulas that can be found in

Lemma A.1

Let

Estimate of the function in (

)

The function
F(\zeta)
in (

and

Lemma A.2

Let
\gamma>0
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(\zeta)\gtrsim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\zeta|^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}-2}&\mbox{if}\ 0<|\zeta|\leq 1\\[5.0pt]
|\zeta|^{-2}e^{-2|\zeta|}&\mbox{if}\ |\zeta|\geq 1.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

that, when

Kink correlations, domain size distribution, and the emptiness formation probability after the Kibble-Zurek quench in the quantum Ising chain
Jacek Dziarmaga, Marek M. Rams

Abstract
Linear quench of the transverse field drives the quantum Ising chain across a quantum critical point from the paramagnetic to the ferromagnetic phase. We focus on normal and anomalous quadratic correlators between fermionic kink creation and annihilation operators. They depend not only on the Kibble-Zurek (KZ) correlation length but also on a dephasing length scale, which differs from the KZ length by a logarithmic correction. Additional slowing down of the ramp in the ferromagnetic phase further increases the dephasing length and suppresses the anomalous correlator. The quadratic correlators enter Pfaffians that yield experimentally relevant kink correlation functions, the probability distribution of ferromagnetic domain sizes, and, closely related, emptiness formation probability. The latter takes the form of a Pfaffian of a block Toeplitz matrix that allows for some analytic asymptotes. Finally, we obtain further insight into the structure of the state at the end of the ramp by interpreting it as a paired state of fermionic kinks characterized by its pair wave function. All those quantities are sensitive to quantum coherence between eigenstates with different numbers of kinks, thus making them a convenient probe of the quantumness of a quantum simulator platform.

In this article, we aim at providing further experimentally relevant characteristics of the post-quench state in the transverse field Ising chain. These include distribution of the domain sizes, the emptiness formation probability, and higher kink-kink correlators.

The most simple picture of QKZM assumes an adiabatic-impulse-adiabatic approximation, where the evolution at a distance from the critical point is adiabatic, and the state of the system freezes out in the neighborhood of the critical point due to the closing of the energy gap. While such an approximation has deficits, it predicts the correct KZM scaling laws. A more detailed causality-based picture, emphasizing the role of the sonic horizon and the speed of the relevant sound, is discussed in Ref.
Sadhukhan et al. 2020
.

In QKZM scenario a system, initially prepared in its ground state, is smoothly ramped across a continuous quantum critical point. A generic ramp can be linearized near the critical point as

\epsilon(t)=\frac{t-t_{c}}{\tau_{Q}}.

(1)

Here
\epsilon
is a dimensionless parameter in the Hamiltonian, whose magnitude measures the distance from the critical point,
\tau_{Q}
is the quench time, and
t_{c}
is the instance of a time when the critical point is crossed. Initially, the evolution is adiabatic and the system follows its instantaneous ground state. The adiabaticity fails at
t_{c}-\hat{t}
when the reaction rate of the system, proportional to its energy gap
\Delta\propto|\epsilon|^{z\nu}
, equals instantaneous transition rate, proportional to
|\dot{\epsilon}/\epsilon|=1/|t-t_{c}|
. Here
z
and
\nu
are, respectively, the dynamical and the correlation length critical exponent. The equality implies

\hat{t}\propto\tau_{Q}^{z\nu/(1+z\nu)},

(2)

and the corresponding
\hat{\epsilon}=\hat{t}/\tau_{Q}\propto\tau_{Q}^{-1/(1+z\nu)}
. In the cartoon impulse approximation, the ground state at
-\hat{\epsilon}
, with a corresponding correlation length,

\hat{\xi}\propto\hat{\epsilon}^{-\nu}\propto\tau_{Q}^{\nu/(1+z\nu)},

(3)

is expected to survive until
t_{c}+\hat{t}
, when the adiabatic evolution can restart [in a more complete sonic horizon argument, the correlation length gets increased by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(5)
]. In this way,
\hat{\xi}
becomes imprinted on the initial state for the final adiabatic stage of the evolution after
+\hat{t}
. The adiabatic-impulse-adiabatic approximation predicts the correct scaling of the characteristic length scale
\hat{\xi}

\tau_{Q}
, see Eq. (
3

\hat{t}\propto\hat{\xi}^{z}

In the integrable quantum Ising chain, the excitations are well defined as Bogoliubov quasiparticles

is a quantum superposition over many eigenstates with amplitudes that have frozen magnitudes (determined by

), which, however, accumulate dynamical

While the average density of kinks

—and even their number distribution

II
Quantum Ising chain

The transverse field quantum Ising chain is

H\penalty\ =\penalty\ -\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(g\sigma^{x}_{n}+\sigma^{z}_{n}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}\right),

(4)

where we assume periodic boundary conditions,

, and even

In the thermodynamic limit,

(7)

define subspaces with even/odd numbers of fermions as the parity, related to

\displaystyle H^{\pm}\penalty\ =\penalty\ \sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[g\left(c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-c_{n}^{\dagger}c_{n+1}+c_{n}c_{n+1}\right]+{\rm h.c.}\penalty\

It brings the Hamiltonian to an additive form,

(11)

where

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(g-\cos k)\left(c_{k}^{\dagger}c_{k}-c_{-k}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\right)+

(12)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 2\sin k\left(c^{\dagger}_{k}c^{\dagger}_{-k}+c_{-k}c_{k}\right).

III
Fermionic kink quasiparticles

We introduce a

kink operator that annihilates a kink on a bond connecting sites
n
and

:

\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\left(\prod_{l\leq n}\sigma^{x}_{l}\right)\frac{\sigma^{z}_{n}-\sigma^{z}_{n+1}}{2i}.

(23)

Here the
\sigma^{x}
-string flips all the spins to the left of the bond. Before the flip, the

-factor checks if there is a kink on the bond and at the same time changes the parity.
A phase factor is included for later convenience. Overall, the operator annihilates the kink, if there is one, or gives zero otherwise. For our periodic boundary conditions this operator seemingly has an unwanted effect on the bond connecting sites

1

and its Fourier transform is

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\pi/4}}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n}\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-ik(n+\frac{1}{2})}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{k}\sin\frac{k}{2}+c_{-k}^{\dagger}\cos\frac{k}{2}.

This formula can be understood as a Bogoliubov transformation.

In the ferromagnetic Ising chain at
g=0

II

\varepsilon=-2

\displaystyle\ket{\rm GS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\frac{k}{2}\ket{0_{k}}+\cos\frac{k}{2}c_{-k}^{\dagger}c_{k}^{\dagger}\ket{0_{k}},

(26)

\displaystyle\ket{\rm ES}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\frac{k}{2}\ket{0_{k}}-\sin\frac{k}{2}c_{-k}^{\dagger}c_{k}^{\dagger}\ket{0_{k}}.

(27)

The ground state is a no-kink vacuum,

IV
Linear quench

We ramp the system across the quantum critical point as

g(t\leq 0)\penalty\ =\penalty\ -\frac{t}{\tau_{Q}}\penalty\ .

(29)

with the characteristic quench time
\tau_{Q}
. The ramp crosses the critical point at
t_{c}=-\tau_{Q}
. For the most universal features of the QKZM, it is enough to assume that the ramp can be linearized near the critical point with a slope equal
-1/\tau_{Q}
. Here, we will proceed with an analytic solution of the linear ramp. The system is initially in its ground state at the large initial value of
g\gg 1
but, as
g
is ramped down to zero, it gets excited from its instantaneous ground state and the final state, at
t=0
, has a finite number (density) of kinks.

The states in Eq. (
13
) evolve according to the time-dependent Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations:

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{k}\\
v_{k}\end{array}\right]=2\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g-\cos k&\sin k\\
\sin k&-g+\cos k\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{k}\\
v_{k}\end{array}\right].

These equations can be mapped to the Landau-Zener problem and the final state at the end of the ramp reads
Dziarmaga 2005
;
Cincio et al. 2007

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{k}(0)\\
v_{k}(0)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\sin\frac{k}{2}\\
\cos\frac{k}{2}\end{array}\right]\sqrt{1-p_{k}}+\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\frac{k}{2}\\
-\sin\frac{k}{2}\end{array}\right]\sqrt{p_{k}}e^{i\varphi_{k}},

(37)

up to a global phase.
Here, the Landau-Zener excitation probability

p_{k}\simeq e^{-2\pi\tau_{Q}k^{2}},

(38)

and a dynamical phase
Cincio et al. 2007
;
Nowak and Dziarmaga 2021

\varphi_{k}\simeq\frac{\pi}{4}+2\tau_{Q}+k^{2}\tau_{Q}\ln\tau_{Q},

(39)

where both formulas are accurate when
\tau_{Q}\gg 1
. In other words, the final state is

\ket{\psi_{k}(0)}=\left(\sqrt{1-p_{k}}+\sqrt{p_{k}}e^{-i\varphi_{k}}\gamma^{\dagger}_{-k}\gamma^{\dagger}_{k}\right)\ket{\rm GS},

(40)

where
k>0
.

An average number of kinks in this state is

{\cal N}\penalty\ =\penalty\ \sum_{k}\penalty\ p_{k}.

For large
N
, the sum can be replaced by an integral and the density of kinks becomes

Dziarmaga 2005
:

\rho=\frac{\cal N}{N}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\penalty\ p_{k}\simeq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\tau_{Q}}}.

(41)

The density scales like
\hat{\xi}^{-1}\propto\tau_{Q}^{-1/2}
, in agreement with the QKZM. It is natural to make the definition of
\hat{\xi}
precise as

\hat{\xi}\equiv 2\pi\sqrt{2\tau_{Q}}=\frac{1}{\rho},

(42)

so that an inverse of it is equal to the final density of kinks.

Quadratic fermionic Kink correlators

The Gaussian state Eq. (

\displaystyle N_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\gamma^{\dagger}_{n+R+\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\penalty\ p_{k}\cos kR

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}^{-1}e^{-\pi(R/\hat{\xi})^{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{n+R+\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\penalty\ \sqrt{(1-p_{k})p_{k}}e^{-i\varphi_{k}}\mathrm{sgn}(k)\sin kR

\displaystyle c\frac{R}{\sqrt{\hat{\xi}l^{3}}}e^{-\frac{3\pi}{2}(R/l)^{2}}e^{-i\phi_{R}}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\pi+2\tau_{Q}-\frac{3}{2}{\rm arg}\left(1-\frac{3i\ln\tau_{Q}}{4\pi}\right)+

\displaystyle-\frac{9}{8}(R/l)^{2}\ln\tau_{Q},

By their very definitions,

are probing quantum coherence between two classical spin configurations with opposite spin polarization of

However, before we proceed it should be stressed that

are expectation values of operators with support on a finite subsystem. The rest of the system can play the role of its environment. Therefore, even though the whole chain remains in a pure state, its subsystem can undergo decoherence. For instance, if the subsystem is two bonds

, where
\ket{\tilde{0}}
is the state with no kinks in the subsystem. In this basis
\Delta_{R}
is coherence between
\ket{\tilde{0}}
and
\ket{\tilde{2}}
. This coherence vanishes when phase
\varphi_{k}
in Eq. (
44
) depends on
k
strongly enough to suppress the integral. As we will see, the
k
-dependence increases with time due to non-trivial quasiparticle dispersion (i.e., their mobility). We will call this process dephasing to distinguish it from the decoherence of the whole chain that is absent for the unitary evolution. The dephasing can obscure the coherence of the whole chain when probed through
\Delta_{R}
with the finite support, but, as we will see, a typical linear quench does not give the dephasing enough time to become significant. The ramp would have to be deliberately slowed down after crossing the quantum critical point for the dephasing to take effect.

VI
Kink correlation functions

Back in the spin representation, the quadratic fermionic correlators are expectation values of highly non-local string operators and, therefore, are hard to measure. However, they can be probed indirectly through
M
-kink correlators:

C_{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{M}}=\left\langle K_{m_{1}}K_{m_{2}}\dots K_{m_{M}}\right\rangle.

(47)

Here,

K_{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\gamma^{\dagger}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}

(48)

is a projector on a state with a kink on the bond between sites
n
and
n+1
, and we will use half-integer numbers to index them. It is worth observing here that this projector can be also expressed as
K_{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\overline{\gamma}^{\dagger}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\overline{\gamma}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\overline{\gamma}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}
are hard-core bosonic kink annihilation operators,
\overline{\gamma}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}=-i\gamma_{n+\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}=\left(\prod_{l\leq n}\sigma^{x}_{l}\right)\frac{1-\sigma^{z}_{n}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}}{2}=\left(\prod_{l\leq n}\sigma^{x}_{l}\right)K_{n+\frac{1}{2}}
.

With the help of the Wick theorem we obtain:

\displaystyle C_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho,

(49)

\displaystyle C_{m+R,m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}+\left|\Delta_{R}\right|^{2}-N^{2}_{R}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\left[1+c^{2}\left(\hat{\xi}/l\right)\left(R/l\right)^{2}e^{-3\pi(R/l)^{2}}-e^{-2\pi(R/\hat{\xi})^{2}}\right].

A connected part of the
2
-kink correlator is

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}^{2}C_{m+R,m}^{(c)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}^{2}\left(C_{m+R,m}-C_{m+R}C_{m}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{2}\left(\hat{\xi}/l\right)\left(R/l\right)^{2}e^{-3\pi(R/l)^{2}}-e^{-2\pi(R/\hat{\xi})^{2}}.

It was scaled here by a factor
\hat{\xi}^{2}=\rho^{-2}
to be properly normalized with respect to the average kink density.
The scaled connected correlator is shown in Fig.
1
.
This form of the
2
-kink correlator is already known from Ref.
Nowak and Dziarmaga 2021
, but here it was derived without some unnecessary approximations. When dephasing length
l
is not too large, the correlator has a peak that served as one of hallmarks of coherent quantum annealing by a D-Wave machine
King et al. 2022
, see Fig. 3 in that work.
With extra dephasing, which would make dephasing length
l
long enough, the correlator would simplify to

\hat{\xi}^{2}C_{m+R,m}^{(c)}=-e^{-2\pi(R/\hat{\xi})^{2}},

(52)

see the dashed plot in Fig.
1
.

In the same manner, a 3-kink correlator is

\displaystyle C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}=

\displaystyle\rho^{3}+\left(C_{m_{1},m_{2}}+C_{m_{2},m_{3}}+C_{m_{3},m_{1}}\right)\rho+2{\cal N}_{12}{\cal N}_{23}{\cal N}_{31}+

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\left[{\cal N}_{12}{\cal D}_{23}{\cal D}^{*}_{31}+{\cal N}_{23}{\cal D}_{31}{\cal D}^{*}_{12}+{\cal N}_{31}{\cal D}_{12}{\cal D}^{*}_{23}\right].

(53)

Here
{\cal N}_{ij}=N_{m_{i}-m_{j}}
and
{\cal D}_{ij}=\Delta_{m_{i}-m_{j}}
are matrix elements of
M\times M
matrices. In general, the
M
-kink correlator is a Pfaffian,

C_{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{M}}=\left(-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}M(M-1)}{\rm Pf}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\cal D}^{\dagger}&{\cal N}\\
-{\cal N}^{T}&{\cal D}\end{array}\right),

(54)

that can be efficiently calculated numerically. Matrix
{\cal D}
becomes negligible for sufficient dephasing, and the correlator simplifies to

C_{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{M}}={\rm Det}\penalty\ {\cal N}.

(55)

For instance, a connected
3
-kink correlator becomes

C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}^{(c)}=2{\cal N}_{12}{\cal N}_{23}{\cal N}_{31}
in that limit.

VII
Domain size distribution

By definition, the probability that a ferromagnetic domain has size
L
is:

P_{L}=\frac{1}{\left\langle K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle}\left\langle K_{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-K_{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\dots\left(1-K_{L-\frac{1}{2}}\right)K_{L+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle.

(56)

This is a conditional probability that, provided that there is a kink on bond
\frac{1}{2}
, there are no kinks on
L-1
consecutive bonds
\frac{3}{2},\dots,L-\frac{1}{2}
and a kink at bond
L+\frac{1}{2}
. Given that
\left\langle K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle=\rho
and
1-K_{m}=\gamma_{m}\gamma^{\dagger}_{m}
, we can write the distribution as a Pfaffian:

P_{L}=\rho^{-1}\penalty\ {\rm Pf}\penalty\ {\cal M}=\rho^{-1}\sqrt{{\rm Det}\penalty\ {\cal M}},

(57)

where
{\cal M}
is a skew-symmetric matrix of dimensions
2(L+1)\times 2(L+1)
. Its elements above the diagonal, for
i<j
, are
{\cal M}_{ij}=\langle\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}\rangle
, where operators
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{2(L+1)}
represent

\gamma^{\dagger}_{\frac{1}{2}},\gamma_{\frac{1}{2}},\gamma_{\frac{3}{2}},\gamma_{\frac{3}{2}}^{\dagger},\dots,\gamma_{L-\frac{1}{2}},\gamma^{\dagger}_{L-\frac{1}{2}},\gamma_{L+\frac{1}{2}}^{\dagger},\gamma_{L+\frac{1}{2}},

respectively.

Figure
2
(a) shows the domain size distribution for several values of the quench time, with both the domain size and the probability rescaled by the KZ length. They do not quite collapse because the anomalous correlators in Eq. (
44
), that contribute to the matrix
{\cal M}
, depend not only on
\hat{\xi}
but also on the dephasing length in Eq. (
46
), which for a linear ramp differs from
\hat{\xi}
by a logarithmic correction. However, when a smooth ramp slows in the ferromagnetic phase for long enough to suppress the anomalous correlators, the anomalous correlators can be neglected and the domain size distribution depends on
\hat{\xi}
only. The dephased distribution is shown in Fig.
2
(a) as a dashed line.

VIII
Emptiness formation probability

The domain size distribution is intimately related to an emptiness formation probability (EFP):

E_{L}=\left\langle\left(1-K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\dots\left(1-K_{L-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right\rangle.

(58)

It is a probability that there are no kinks on
L
consecutive bonds. Just like the domain size distribution, it is experimentally accessible and, therefore, also interesting in its own right. Given that
1-K_{m}=\gamma_{m}\gamma^{\dagger}_{m}
, the Wick theorem allows one to rewrite EFP as a Pfaffian of a skew-symmetric block Toeplitz matrix:

E_{L}=(-1)^{L(L-1)/2}\penalty\ {\rm Pf}\penalty\ {\cal T}=\sqrt{{\rm Det}\penalty\ {\cal T}}.

(59)

Here

{\cal T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{\cal D}&1-\tilde{\cal N}\\
\tilde{\cal N}-1&\tilde{\cal D}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right).

(60)

The blocks are
\tilde{\cal D}_{mm^{\prime}}=\Delta_{m-m^{\prime}}
and
\tilde{\cal N}_{mm^{\prime}}=N_{m-m^{\prime}}
.
The EFP has been extensively studied in the Ising/XY chains at equilibrium
Shiroishi et al. 2001
;
Franchini and Abanov 2005
;
Stéphan 2014
;
Ares and Viti 2020
, in which case the block Toeplitz matrics simplifies to a simple Toeplitz matrix (after rotation to the Majorana fermionic operators) due to
\tilde{\cal D}
being real. The latter is not the case in the state after the linear quench that we consider here and, in general, one has to deal with a full block Toeplitz matrix. However,
with sufficient dephasing, when the anomalous correlators can be neglected, the probability simplifies to

E_{L}={\rm Det}\penalty\ \left(1-\tilde{\cal N}\right).

(61)

Figure
2
(b) shows
E_{L}
in the function of scaled distance,
L/\hat{\xi}
.

Asymptotic results

In this section, we derive asymptotes of
E_{L}
and
P_{L}
valid for either large or small
L
. We begin with the dephased case when the anomalous correlators are negligible.

IX.1
Dephased limit; asymptotes for large
L

The Toeplitz matrix in Eq. (
61
) has a Fourier transform:

\delta_{mn}-N_{m-n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\left(1-p_{k}\right)e^{ik(m-n)}

(66)

for
m,n=1,\dots,L
. The generating function has zero at
k=0
, and we factorize it in line with the Fisher-Hartwig conjecture
Fisher and Hartwig 1969
;
Widom 1973
;
Basor and Morrison 1994
;
Ehrhardt and Silbermann 1997
;
Deift et al. 2013
,

1-p_{k}=1-e^{-(\hat{\xi}k)^{2}/4\pi}\equiv(2-2\cos k)^{1}\penalty\ \tau(k).

(67)

Then the asymptote for large
L
reads

E_{L}\approx\alpha_{D}\frac{L}{\hat{\xi}}e^{-\beta L/\hat{\xi}},

(68)

where

\beta=-\hat{\xi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\penalty\ \log\tau(k)\simeq 2.6124.

(69)

The prefactor in Eq. (
68
) can, in principle, be worked out with the conjecture, but here we treat it as a fitting parameter obtaining
\alpha_{D}=2.00
. With Eq. (
64
) we get, in the leading order in
L/\hat{\xi}
,

\hat{\xi}P_{L}\approx\alpha_{D}\beta^{2}\frac{L}{\hat{\xi}}e^{-\beta L/\hat{\xi}}.

(70)

This asymptotes are shown in Fig.
2
with black dotted lines.

IX.2
Dephased limit; asymptotes for small
L

For small
L
, the Toeplitz matrix in Eq. (
60
) can be rearranged as a sum of a large and a small matrix:

\displaystyle\delta_{mn}-N_{m-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{mn}-\frac{1}{\hat{\xi}}\right)+\frac{1}{\hat{\xi}}\left(1-e^{-\pi(m-n)^{2}/\hat{\xi}^{2}}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle A_{mn}+B_{mn}.

Matrix
A_{mn}
has
L-1
eigenvalues equal to
1
and one smaller eigenvalue
1-L/\hat{\xi}
with eigenvector
\ket{1}=(1,\dots,1)/\sqrt{L}
. Consequently,

E_{L}={\rm Det}\penalty\ (A+B)=\left(1-\frac{L}{\hat{\xi}}\right)\penalty\ {\rm Det}\left(1+A^{-1}B\right).

(72)

For
L\ll\hat{\xi}
, matrix
A^{-1}B
is small, and we can proceed as

\displaystyle\log{\rm Det}\left(1+A^{-1}B\right)=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\penalty\ \log\left(1+A^{-1}B\right)\approx{\rm Tr}\penalty\ A^{-1}B=

\displaystyle\bra{1}B\ket{1}\frac{L/\hat{\xi}}{1-L/\hat{\xi}}\approx\frac{L}{\hat{\xi}}\bra{1}B\ket{1}.

(73)

Here, we used
{\rm Tr}\penalty\ B=0
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\bra{1}B\ket{1}\approx\frac{1}{\hat{\xi}L}\sum_{m,n=1}^{L}\frac{\pi(m-n)^{2}}{\hat{\xi}^{2}}\approx\frac{\pi}{6}\frac{L^{3}}{\hat{\xi}^{3}}.

(74)

Finally, we have

E_{L}\approx\left(1-L/\hat{\xi}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\frac{L^{4}}{\hat{\xi}^{4}}\right),

(75)

and with Eq. (
64
), in the leading order in
L/\hat{\xi}
,

\hat{\xi}P_{L}\approx 2\pi\left(\frac{L}{\hat{\xi}}\right)^{2}.

(76)

This asymptotes are shown in Fig.

with black dotted lines.

IX.3
Dephased limit; interpolating formula

The asymptotes derived in the last two subsections capture the behavior of the tails of
P_{L}
. However, as can be seen in Fig.
2
(a), they diverge from the exact value near the peak of

. Finding a rigorous expression in this range poses a daunting challenge. Indeed, even including a subleading term in Eq. (

Kozlowski 2008

X
Kink pair wave function

The Gaussian state is fully characterized by its quadratic correlators, and, as we have seen, they allow efficient computation of a number of experimentally relevant quantities. However, the nature of the fermionic paired state can also be encapsulated in its pair wave function. Although not as computationally potent as the correlators, it still can provide some interpretations. Along these lines, the state in Eq. (
40
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(0)}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{k>0}{\cal Z}_{k}\gamma_{-k}^{\dagger}\gamma_{k}^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\rm GS}

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n}Z_{n}\gamma_{m+n}^{\dagger}\gamma_{m}^{\dagger}\right)\ket{\rm GS},

with half-integer
m
in the last line.
Here
\ket{\rm GS}
is the ferromagnetic ground state (
26
) without any kinks and

{\cal Z}_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{p_{k}}{1-p_{k}}}e^{-i\varphi_{k}}

(96)

is a pair wave function. Its position representation is

Z_{n}=-\frac{4}{N}\sum_{k>0}{\cal Z}_{k}\sin kn.

(97)

For large
N
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}dk\penalty\ {\cal Z}_{k}\sin kn.

(98)

For small
k
,
{\cal Z}_{k}\simeq e^{-i\varphi_{0}}/\sqrt{2\pi\tau_{Q}k^{2}}
, therefore, for large
|n|
the asymptotes are

Z_{n}\approx-2\sqrt{\pi}\hat{\xi}^{-1}\penalty\ e^{-i\varphi_{0}}\penalty\ {\rm sgn}(n).

(99)

This is not a bound kink-antikink pair. The kinks are free to move all along the spin chain except for repulsion when they approach one another, see Fig.
4
.

Thanks to the KZ freeze-out at
\hat{\epsilon}
above the critical transverse field, this deconfinement is inherited from the paramagnetic phase. This can be illustrated by a simple example of a sudden quench with
\tau_{Q}\to 0
.
In this limit, final
(u_{k},v_{k})=(1,0)
at
g=0
is the same as the initial ground state for
g\to\infty
. Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
) then imply
{\cal Z}_{k}=\cot{k/2}
and
Z_{n}=-2\penalty\ {\rm sgn}(n)
, that indeed has finite asymptotes for large
|n|
.

A more relevant example is a sudden quench from
g=1+\epsilon
just above the phase transition to
g=0
, see Fig.
5
. The correlation length in the initial ground state is
\xi\propto 1/\epsilon
. The quenched pair wave functions for different
\epsilon
collapse when scaled with
\xi
. For large
|n|\gg\xi
, they tend to a finite asymptote proportional to density of kinks in the quenched state,
Z_{n}\propto-\xi^{-1}{\rm sgn}(n)
. Those finite asymptotes demonstrate the deconfinement of kinks quenched from the paramagnetic to the ferromagnetic phase. They are free to move apart all along the chain and destroy the ferromagnetic long-range order.

For comparison, ferromagnetic-to-ferromagnetic quenches from
\epsilon<0
to
g=0
result in pair functions that decay quickly to zero when
|n|\gg\xi
, see Fig.
5
. They describe bound kink-antikink pairs that do not affect the long-range order.

Finally, in Fig.
5
, there appear to develop a peak for
\epsilon\to 0^{\pm}
. However, its unscaled magnitude is independent of
\epsilon
, and its maximum is located at
n=1
(corresponding to individual flipped spins). The peak originates from ultraviolet excitations with large
k
, which are absent in the state after the linear ramp, reflected by the excitation spectrum in Eq. (
38
). This reflects that the freeze-out picture of QKZM is a sudden quench from
+\hat{\epsilon}
to
-\hat{\epsilon}
instead of all the way down to
g=0
.

XI
Conclusion

By eliminating some unnecessary approximations
Nowak and Dziarmaga 2021
, the fermionic kink annihilation operators facilitate calculations of the higher-order kink correlators, the domain size distribution, and the emptiness formation probability. All those quantities are experimentally accessible and sensitive to quantum coherence between eigenstates with different numbers of kinks. The coherences prove to be quite robust to the quasiparticle dephasing in the ferromagnetic phase. Indeed, full dephasing would require deliberately slowing the quench in the ferromagnetic phase for a time equal to several quench times. This robustness makes these observables a convenient test of quantumness for quantum simulators. In fact, the basic kink-kink correlator in Eq. (
51
) has already been used as a probe of quantum coherence in Ref.
King et al. 2022
, where the quantum Ising chain was realized in the coherent D-Wave quantum annealer. The same Kibble-Zurek quantum quench was also realized in Ref.
Keesling et al. 2019
in a chain of Rydberg atoms quantum simulating the Ising chain. In both experiments the state after the quench is measured in the
\sigma^{z}
basis. From the outcomes of these measurements, one can infer any correlators obtained in this article. They, in turn, provide insights into the quantum coherence of the experimental set-up.

Appendix B
Dephasing after a non-linear ramp

The dynamical phase in Eq. (
39

|k|
less than
\hat{\xi}^{-1}\propto\tau_{Q}^{-1/2}

in Eq. (

21

k

\varepsilon_{k}\approx 2|1-g|+\frac{g}{|1-g|}k^{2}.

(103)

In the main text we consider a linear ramp

with the time running from
t=-\infty
to
t=0
. When the linear ramp is a good approximation between
t_{c}-\hat{t}
and
t_{c}+\hat{t}

p_{k}

t_{c}+\hat{t}
and does not depend on the actual shape of the subsequent ramp as long as it is smooth. It is not the case for the dynamical phase.

Let us then consider a more general non-linear smooth ramp
\tilde{g}(t)

t_{c}\pm\hat{t}

\tilde{g}(t)\approx g(t)

\displaystyle\delta\varphi_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\tau_{Q}}^{t_{0}}dt^{\prime}\left[2|1-\tilde{g}(t^{\prime})|+\frac{\tilde{g}(t^{\prime})}{|1-\tilde{g}(t^{\prime})|}k^{2}\right]

(104)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\tau_{Q}}^{0}dt^{\prime}\left[2|1-g(t^{\prime})|+\frac{g(t^{\prime})}{|1-g(t^{\prime})|}k^{2}\right].

Given that both the linear and the non-linear ramps depend on time

\delta\varphi_{k}=A\penalty\ \tau_{Q}+B\penalty\ \tau_{Q}k^{2}.

B

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\pi+(2+A)\tau_{Q}-\frac{3}{2}{\rm arg}\left(1-\frac{3i\left(\ln\tau_{Q}+B\right)}{4\pi}\right)+

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}\penalty\ \sqrt{1+\left[\frac{3\left(\ln\tau_{Q}+B\right)}{4\pi}\right]^{2}}.

Stability analysis of warm quintessential dark energy model
Suratna Das, Saddam Hussain, Debottam Nandi, Rudnei O. Ramos, Renato Silva

Abstract
A dynamical system analysis is performed for a model of dissipative quintessential inflation realizing warm inflation at early primordial times and dissipative interations in the dark sector at late times. The construction makes use of a generalized exponential potential realizing both phases of accelerated expansion. A focus is given on the behavior of the dynamical system at late times and the analysis is exemplified by both analytical and numerical results. The results obtained demonstrate the viability of the model as a quintessential inflation model in which stable solutions can be obtained.

Model

Late time dynamics: A qualitative analysis

The quintessential scalar field predominantly dissipates to matter

terms of

displays only the trivial critical point

(0,0,z,0)
, while the other possible critical points,
including any accelerating solutions, remain hidden.
This seems to be a drawback of the choice of variables made, but that
can be overcame as follows. To work around the above mentioned difficulty,
we first redefine

Next, we can make a nontrivial transformation of the variables

and

\xi

and

(51)

such that

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{2u}{(1+u)v},\quad z=\frac{2u(1-u)}{(1+u)^{2}v}.

(52)

The

and

(53)

(x,y,u,v)

VI
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied a phenomenological model for
quintessential inflation that is motivated from warm inflation. At
early times, the quintessential scalar inflaton field decays into
radiation during warm inflation, while at late times it is allowed to
decay into matter, thus realizing a model of dissipative interaction
in the dark sector at late times. The construction also makes use of a
generalized exponential potential able to realize both phases of
accelerated expansion, at early- and late-times. The full dynamical
system was analyzed, with a focus on the behavior of the dynamical
system at late times. The analysis was exemplified by both analytical
and numerical results and for different illustrative values of
parameters. The analysis performed here extents and generalizes the
results originally obtained in Ref.
Lima:2019yyv
, where a
version of this model was first proposed. The results obtained
demonstrate the viability of the model as a quintessential inflation
model and in which stable solutions can be obtained. In addition, we
have also analyzed the stability of the slow-roll solutions at both
early- and late-times, which allowed us to put some constraints in the
model parameters.

Appendix A
Slow-roll analysis of the dynamical system

In this section, we shall consider the stability of the slow-roll
approximated dynamical system of the Warm Quintessential Dark Energy
Model following
Moss:2008yb
. From the set of equations
(
1
), (
2
) and (
3
), we see that
there are three dynamical quantities
\phi
,
\rho_{r}
and
\rho_{m}
. We
express the radiation energy density
\rho_{r}

\rho_{r}=(3/4)sT

equations become

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}+3H(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})\dot{\phi}+V,_{\phi}=0,

\displaystyle T\dot{s}+3HTs=3HQ_{r}\dot{\phi}^{2},

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{m}+3H\rho_{m}=3HQ_{m}\dot{\phi}^{2}.

(60)

Under the slow-roll conditions, this set of background equations then
reduce to

\displaystyle 3H(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})\dot{\phi}+V,_{\phi}=0,

\displaystyle Ts=Q_{r}\dot{\phi}^{2},

\displaystyle\rho_{m}=Q_{m}\dot{\phi}^{2}.

(61)

The leading order slow-roll parameters in this model

Moss:2008yb

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(\frac{V,_{\phi}}{V}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi G}\frac{V,_{\phi\phi}}{V},

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi G}\frac{V,_{\phi}}{\phi V},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi G}\frac{V,_{\phi}\Gamma_{r},_{\phi}}{V\Gamma_{r}}=p\kappa,

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi G}\frac{V,_{\phi}\Gamma_{m},_{\phi}}{V\Gamma_{m}}=q\kappa,

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{TV,_{\phi T}}{V,_{\phi}}.

(62)

Here, we have defined an extra slow-roll parameter
\gamma
in
connection with the dissipation to the matter energy density, which is
in general not present in standard WI models.

We find it convenient to change the independent variable from cosmic

\phi

\frac{d}{dt}=u\frac{d}{d\phi}

. Then, the set of equations given in
Eq. (
60
) can be written as

\displaystyle u^{\prime}=-3H-\Gamma_{r}-\Gamma_{m}-V,_{\phi}u^{-1}\equiv f(u,s,w),

\displaystyle s^{\prime}=-3Hsu^{-1}+T^{-1}\Gamma_{r}u\equiv g(u,s,w),

\displaystyle w^{\prime}=-3Hwu^{-1}+\Gamma_{u}u\equiv h(u,s,w),

(63)

where prime denotes derivative w.r.t.
\phi
. Therefore, the
background set of equations can be compactly written as

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=F(x),

(64)

where

\displaystyle x\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
s\\
w\end{array}\right).

We take a background
\bar{x}
, which satisfies the slow-roll
equations, Eq. (
61
). Then, the linearized
perturbations satisfy the equations

\displaystyle\delta x^{\prime}=M(\bar{x})\delta x-\bar{x},

(69)

where the
M
matrix is defined as

\displaystyle M=\left.\frac{\partial(f,g,h)}{\partial(u,s,w)}\right|_{u=\bar{u},s=\bar{s},w=\bar{w}}.

(70)

We find the matrix elements as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}=\frac{H}{u}\left[-3(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv\mathcal{A},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}=\frac{H}{s}\left[-cQ_{r}-\frac{Q_{r}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\delta(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})\frac{}{}\right]\equiv\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial w}=\frac{H}{w}\left[-\frac{3k}{4}Q_{m}-\frac{Q_{m}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial u}=\frac{Hs}{u^{2}}\left[6-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{C}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial s}=\frac{H}{u}\left[c-4-\frac{Q_{r}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{D}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial w}=\frac{Hs}{uw}\left[-\frac{Q_{m}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{F}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial u}=\frac{Hw}{u^{2}}\left[6-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{G}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}=\frac{Hw}{su}\left[-\frac{Q_{r}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{H}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial w}=\frac{H}{u}\left[-3+\frac{3k}{4}-\frac{Q_{m}\epsilon}{(1+Q_{r}+Q_{m})^{2}}\right]\equiv{\mathcal{I}}.

(71)

The matrix
M
can then be read as

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\mathcal{A}}&&\mathcal{B}&&{\mathcal{E}}\\
{\mathcal{C}}&&{\mathcal{D}}&&{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{G}}&&{\mathcal{H}}&&{\mathcal{I}}\end{array}\right).

The sufficient condition for stability of this slow-roll approximated
system is that the
M
matrix varies slowly, which is justified by
having all the three eigenvalues of the diagonalized matrix to be
negative. If all the three eigenvalues of the diagonalized matrix are negative, then
both
\det(M)
and

{\rm tr}(M)
should be negative as well. We find, at leading order (ignoring slow-roll
parameters),

\displaystyle{\rm det}(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{9}{4}\left((c-4)(4-k)+(c-4)(4+k)Q_{m}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(c+4)(k-4)Q_{r}\right),

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-7+c+\frac{3k}{4}-3(1+Q_{m}+Q_{r}).

(76)

Thus, to have
\det(M)
negative, we find the conditions
-4<c<4
and

-4<k<4
. These conditions also make
{\rm tr}(M)
negative.
We can see it explicitly that these conditions yield three negative eigenvalues of the matrix
M
in three different physical situations:

1.

Strong dissipative inflationary regime (
Q_{r}\gg 1
and
Q_{m}\ll 1
)
: During slow-roll, with these limits, we find three eigenvalues of the matrix
M
as
\lambda_{1}=(3/4)(-4+k)
,
\lambda_{2}=2c-3Q_{r}
and
\lambda_{3}=-4-c
. We note that the three eigenvalues can be simultaneously negative only if
-4<c<4
and
-4<k<4
.

2.

Weak dissipative inflationary regime (
Q_{r}\ll 1
and
Q_{m}\ll 1
)
: In this case, we find the three eigenvalues as
\lambda_{1}=-3
,
\lambda_{2}=-4+c
and
\lambda_{3}=(3/4)(-4+k)
. Here also, we note that the conditions to get all the three eigenvalues negative are
-4<c<4
and

-4<k<4
.

3.

Quintessence driven Dark Energy dominated regime (
Q_{r}\ll 1
and
Q_{m}\gg 1
)
: Here the three eigenvalues turn out to be
\lambda_{1}=-4+c
,
\lambda_{2}=(3/2)(k-2Q_{m})
and
\lambda_{3}=-(3/4)(4+k)
. Like in the previous two cases, in this case too, the conditions to get all the three eigenvalues negative are
-4<c<4
and

-4<k<4
.

Therefore, we
see that for the system to be stabilized, the form of the dissipative
coefficients
\Upsilon_{r}
and
\Upsilon_{m}
must involve the powers
c
and
k

satisfying the conditions
-4<c<4
and

-4<k<4
.

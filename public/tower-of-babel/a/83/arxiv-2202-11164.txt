A weak Galerkin method and its two-grid algorithm for the quasi-linear elliptic problems of non-monotone type
Peng Zhu, Shenglan Xie

Abstract
In this article, a weak Galerkin (WG) method is firstly presented and analyzed for the quasi-linear elliptic problem of non-monotone type. By using Brouwer’s fixed point technique, the existence of WG solution and error estimates in both the energy norm and the L 2 L^{2} norm are derived. Then an efficient two-grid WG method is introduced to improve the computational efficiency. The convergence error of the two-grid WG method is analyzed in the energy norm. Numerical experiments are presented to verify our theoretical findings.

weak Galerkin, finite element method, quasi-linear elliptic problem,

Introduction

Consider the quasi-linear elliptic problem of the form

(1a)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(a(x,u){\nabla u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g,\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,

\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a twice continuously differentiable function in
\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}
and all the derivatives of

\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}
. We also assume that there exist positive constants
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
such that

0<\alpha_{0}\leq a(x,u)\leq\alpha_{1}.

For sake of notational simplicity, we denote
a(x,u)
by
a(u)
in the rest of this paper.

[

,

]
for the numerical solution of second order elliptic problems. Since then, there has been considerable interest in WG methods for the numerical solution of a wide range of partial differential equations. We refer the reader to

,

]
, and the references therein for details. However, there are few papers that are concerned to the nonlinear elliptic problems. To the best of our knowledge, only references
[

,

,

]
conducted their investigations in this direction.

For the second order elliptic quasi-linear PDEs, the existence of solutions of the WG methods was shown in
[
22
]
by a Schauder’s fixed point argument. However, the uniqueness and the error estimations of the numerical approximations are restricted only to the linear PDEs, and have not been addressed for the nonlinear ones. Recently in
[

]
, the authors gave the well-posedness and error estimate in the energy norm for the monotone quasi-linear PDEs. Most recently,
[

investigated the stabilizer-free WG methods

Direct application of the techniques presented in
[

37
]
to establish the a priori error estimates of the WG solutions for non-monotone type quasi-linear elliptic problem is not possible because the associated differential operators may not satisfy monotonicity property. Non-monotone type quasi-linear elliptic problems have been studied by conforming finite element methods

The first aim of this paper is to make an effort for conducting a priori error analysis of the WG method for solving non-monotone quasi-linear elliptic problems. Following the ideas in
[
12
]
, we employ Brouwer’s fixed point theorem to prove the existence of the discrete solution, and in turn derived the a priori error estimates in a mesh-dependent energy norm and in the

As it is well known, the two-grid method is an efficient algorithm for solving nonlinear partial differential equations
[
15
,
14
]
. Two-grid finite volume element method
[
2
]
and two-grid discontinuous Galerkin method
[
3
]
were further investigated for non-monotone quasi-linear elliptic problems. Another aim of this paper is to propose an efficient WG method by adopting the two-grid idea for the quasi-linear elliptic problem. Convergence of the two-grid WG method is rigorously analyzed.
As far as we know, the present work is the first attempt to apply and analyze the two-grid technique in the setting of the WG method.

The outline of this paper is as follows. Section 2 introduces the WG method for the problem (
1a
)-(
1b
). In Section 3, we derive optimal order error estimates of the WG method in both the energy norm and the
L^{2}
norm. The two-grid algorithm of the WG method is proposed in Section 4, which is then followed by derivation of its convergence analysis in the energy norm. Section 5 carries out numerical experiments to verify our theoretical findings. A summary is given in Section 6.

2
Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods

First of all, let us introduce the concept of weak function.
Let
K
be any polygonal domain with boundary
\partial K
. A weak function on
K
refers to a function
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}
such that
v_{0}\in L^{2}(K)
and
v_{b}\in H^{1/2}(\partial K)
, where
v_{0}
means the value of
v
in
K
, and
v_{b}
represents the boundary value of
v
. Note that
v_{b}
may not necessarily be related to the trace of
v_{0}
on
\partial K
. Let
W(K)
be the set of weak functions on
K
, i.e.,

W(K):=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}\in L^{2}(K),v_{b}\in H^{1/2}(\partial K)\}.

It is worth to point out that a function
v\in H^{1}(K)
can be viewed as a weak function
\{v_{0},v_{b}\}
of
W(K)
with
v_{0}=v|_{K}
and
v_{b}=v|_{\partial K}
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a shape regular polygonal partition of the domain
\Omega

[
31
]
.
Denote by
{\cal E}_{h}
the set of all edges in
{\cal T}_{h}
, and let
{\cal E}_{h}^{0}={\cal E}_{h}\backslash\partial\Omega
be
the set of all interior edges. For each element
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we denote by
h_{K}
the diameter of
K
, i.e.,
h_{K}=\mathrm{diam}(K)
. Similarly, let
h_{e}
be the length of an edge
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
.

On this partition of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we introduce the following broken Sobolev space

\displaystyle H^{m}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in H^{1}(K),\;\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

for any integer
m\geq 0
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{m}(D)
denote the set of polynomials defined on
D
with degree no more than
m
, where
D
may be an element
K
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
or an edge
e
of
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
. In what follows, we often consider the broken polynomial spaces

\mathbb{P}_{m}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}(K),\,\,\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

and

\mathbb{P}_{m}({\mathcal{E}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{h}):v|_{e}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}(e),\,\,\forall e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\}.

Then a WG finite element space
V_{h}
associated with
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
for
k\geq 1
is defined by

(2)

V_{h}=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:\ v_{0}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}),v_{b}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}({\mathcal{E}}_{h})\}.

Denote by
V_{h}^{0}
a subspace of
V_{h}
with vanishing traces,

(3)

\displaystyle V_{h}^{0}=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h},\ v_{b}|_{e}=0,\ e\subset\partial K\cap\partial\Omega\}.

For the sake of simplicity, we introduce the following notations: for any
v,w\in H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,

\displaystyle(v,w)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(v,w)_{K},\quad\mbox{where }(v,w)_{K}=\int_{K}vw\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

and

\displaystyle\langle v,w\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle v,w\rangle_{\partial K},\quad\mbox{where }(v,w)_{\partial K}=\int_{\partial K}vw\mathrm{d}s.

Definition 1

(Weak Gradient)
.

For any function
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}
, its weak gradient
\nabla_{w}v
,
is piecewisely defined as the unique polynomial
(\nabla_{w}v)|_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)]^{2}
such that

(4)

(\nabla_{w}v,\bm{\phi})_{K}=-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot\bm{\phi})_{K}+\langle v_{b},\bm{\phi}\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{\partial K},\quad\forall\bm{\phi}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)]^{2},

for any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Now, we are ready to present our weak Galerkin finite element method for the problem (
1a
)-(
1b
).

Algorithm 1

(The WG method)
.

The weak Galerkin finite element scheme for solving problem (
1a
)-(
1b
) is defined as follows: find
u_{h}=\{u_{0},u_{b}\}\in V_{h}
such that
u_{b}=\mathcal{Q}_{b}g
on
\partial\Omega
and the following equation

(5)

A_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},v_{h})=(f,\;v_{0}),\quad\forall\ v_{h}=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0},

where

(6)

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{h};v_{h},w_{h})=(a(u_{0})\nabla_{w}v_{h},\nabla_{w}w_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+s_{h}(v_{h},w_{h}),

with the stabilization term
s_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

s_{h}(v_{h},w_{h}):=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\langle v_{0}-v_{b},w_{0}-w_{b}\rangle_{\partial K},\quad\forall v_{h},w_{h}\in V_{h},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{b}
is an
L^{2}
projection defined by (
14
).

3
Error analysis

In this section, we shall derive the a priori error estimates of the WG method (
5
) for solving the quasi-linear elliptic problem (
1a
)-(
1b
). To this end, we firstly introduce notations and some useful lemmas in Sect. 3.1 and then derive an error equation for the WG method (

In what follows, the trace inequality and inverse inequality are frequently used in our analysis, which state as

Lemma 4

For any weak function

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{h}v)|_{K}:=\{\mathcal{Q}_{0}(v|_{K}),\mathcal{Q}_{b}(v|_{\partial K})\}.

For a given

\displaystyle D_{h}(\phi;v_{h},v_{h})+\beta\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle D_{h}(\phi;v_{h},v_{h})+\beta\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{0}\|\nabla_{w}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}^{2}+s_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})+\beta\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}^{2}

The proof is completed.

Error equation

Now for the exact solution

e_{h}=\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-u_{h}:=\{e_{0},e_{b}\},

with

e_{0}=\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u_{0},\quad e_{b}=\mathcal{Q}_{b}u-u_{b}.

The aim of this subsection is to obtain an error equation for
e_{h}
by the use of bilinear form
D_{h}(u;\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 8

(Error equation)
.

Let
u
and
u_{h}
be the solutions of the problem (
1a
)-(
1b
) and the WG scheme (
5
), respectively.
For any
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
, we have

(22)

\displaystyle D_{h}(u;\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-u_{h},v)=E_{h}(u,v)+R_{h}(u;u_{h},v),

where

(23)

\displaystyle E_{h}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\ell(u,v)+s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v),

\displaystyle R_{h}(u;u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=((u-u_{0})\widetilde{a}_{u}(u_{0})(\nabla u-\nabla_{w}u_{h}),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

(24)

\displaystyle\qquad+((u-u_{0})^{2}\widetilde{a}_{uu}(u_{0}){\nabla u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

with
\ell(u,v):=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{3}\ell_{i}(u,v)
, and

(25a)

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=(a(u)(\Pi_{h}(\nabla u)-\nabla u),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

(25b)

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\langle(a(u)\nabla u-\Pi_{h}(a(u)\nabla u))\cdot\bm{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

(25c)

\displaystyle\ell_{3}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=((\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u)a_{u}(u)\nabla{u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we denote
a(u)\nabla u
by
\sigma
.
For any
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h}^{0}
, testing (
1a
) by
v_{0}
and using the fact that

\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\langle\sigma\cdot\bm{n},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}=0

and integration by parts, we arrive at

(26)

\displaystyle(f,v_{0})=-(\nabla\cdot\sigma,v_{0})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=(\sigma,\nabla v_{0})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-\langle\sigma\cdot\bm{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Next we investigate the term
(\sigma,\nabla v_{0})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}
in the above equation.
Using the definition of
\Pi_{h}
, integration by parts and the definition of weak gradient, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma,\nabla v_{0})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Pi_{h}\sigma,\nabla v_{0})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot\Pi_{h}\sigma)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle v_{0},\Pi_{h}\sigma\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v,\Pi_{h}\sigma)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle v_{0}-v_{b},\Pi_{h}\sigma\cdot\bm{n}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sigma,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle\Pi_{h}\sigma\cdot\bm{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

which together with (
26
) yields

(27)

\displaystyle(\sigma,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=(f,v_{0})+\langle(\sigma-\Pi_{h}\sigma)\cdot\bm{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Recalling Lemma
5
and adding
s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v)
on both sides of (
27
), we arrive at

\displaystyle(a(u)\nabla_{w}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v)=(f,v_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\ell_{i}(u,v)+s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v).

Subtracting the WG scheme (
5
) from the above equation yields

\displaystyle A_{h}(u;\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-u_{h},v)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\ell_{i}(u,v)+s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v)+((a(u_{0})-a(u))\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

then adding the term
((\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u_{0})a_{u}(u)\nabla{u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}
on both sides gives

(28)

\displaystyle D_{h}(u;\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-u_{h},v)=E_{h}(u,v)+\mathfrak{T},

with
E_{h}(u,v)
is defined by (
23
), and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}

\displaystyle=((a(u_{0})-a(u))\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+((u-u_{0})a_{u}(u)\nabla{u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=((a(u_{0})-a(u))(\nabla_{w}u_{h}-\nabla u),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

(29)

\displaystyle\quad+((a(u_{0})-a(u)-(u_{0}-u)a_{u}(u))\nabla{u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Using the Taylor expansions (
17
) and (
18
), we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}=((u-u_{0})\widetilde{a}_{u}(u_{0},u)(\nabla u-\nabla_{w}u_{h}),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+((u-u_{0})^{2}\widetilde{a}_{uu}(u_{0},u){\nabla u},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

which together with (
28
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Assume
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)\cap W_{\infty}^{1}(\Omega)
. There exists a constant
C
such that the following estimates hold true:

(30)

\displaystyle|\ell_{1}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

(31)

\displaystyle|\ell_{2}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}s_{h}^{1/2}(v,v),

(32)

\displaystyle|\ell_{3}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

(33)

\displaystyle|s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}s_{h}^{1/2}(v,v).

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, Hölder’s inequality and the approximation property of
\Pi_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle|\ell_{1}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|(\Pi_{h}(\nabla u)-\nabla u,a(u)\nabla_{w}v)_{K}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}M_{a}\|\Pi_{h}(\nabla u)-\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|\nabla_{w}{v}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

(34)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}.

For the estimate (
31
), from Hölder’s inequality, (
16
) and the trace inequality (
9
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\ell_{2}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|a(u)\nabla u-\Pi_{h}(a(u)\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}\|a(u)\nabla u-\Pi_{h}(a(u)\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}s_{h}^{1/2}(v,v).

By Hölder’s inequality and (

\displaystyle|\ell_{3}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}M_{a}\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{k+1}|u|_{H^{k+1}(K)}|u|_{W_{\infty}^{1}(K)}\cdot\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now we consider the estimate (
33
). By the trace inequality (
9
) and (
15
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u\|_{L^{2}(\partial K)}\leq Ch_{K}^{k+1/2}|u|_{H^{k+1}(K)}.

Then, it follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{b}
that

\displaystyle|s_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}|\langle\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-\mathcal{Q}_{b}u,v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}|\langle\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u,v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial K}|

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u-u\|_{L^{2}({\partial K})}^{2})^{1/2}(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|_{L^{2}({\partial K})}^{2})^{1/2}

3.3

Motivated by the form of the error equation (

Definition 10

(The fixed point mapping

)
.

For a given

, let

be a map

\displaystyle D_{h}(u;\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-\psi,v_{h})=E_{h}(u,v_{h})+R_{h}(u;\xi,v_{h}),

Remark 3.1
.

Equivalently, we can restate (

) as follows: find
\psi\in V_{h}
such that

(36)

\displaystyle D_{h}(u;\psi,v_{h})=\Psi_{\xi}(v_{h}),

for any
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, with

is defined by

Obviously,
\Psi_{\xi}(v)
is a continuous linear functional of

. By Riesz representation theorem, there exists a function
\widehat{f}
such that

. The solution

of (

) as the proof in
[

]
. Then the map

Now consider the ball

\mathbb{B}_{h}(\mathcal{Q}_{h}u)=\{\omega\in V_{h}:{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\mathcal{Q}_{h}u-\omega{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}\},

), we get

(81)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{w}(u_{h}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\leq Ch^{-1}\|\nabla_{w}(u_{h}-\mathcal{Q}_{h}u)\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\leq C|u|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

In view of the approximation property (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}(\nabla u)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{h}(\nabla u)-\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C|u|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+|u|_{W_{\infty}^{1}(\Omega)},

which combining with (
80
) and (
81
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 23
.

For a given
\phi\in H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, there holds

\displaystyle A_{h}(\phi;v_{h},v_{h})\geq\min\{\alpha_{0},1\}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},\quad\forall\;v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

Proof.

The proof is trivial, we omit it here.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove convergence estimates in the
H^{1}
-like norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
for the two-grid algorithm of the WG method under the assumption
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)\cap W_{\infty}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 24
.

Assume that
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)\cap W_{\infty}^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}
and
u^{h}\in V_{h}
be the solutions obtained by Algorithm
1
and
2
, respectively. For
\tau\ll 1
, we have

(82)

\displaystyle\|u-u^{h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq C(u)(h^{k}+\tau^{k+1}),

where
C(u)
is a positive constant, which is independent of
h
but dependent on
|u|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}
and
|u|_{W_{\infty}^{1}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

From (
5
) and the Step 2 of Algorithm
2
, we know that

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{\tau};u^{h},v)=(f,v_{0})=A_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},v),\quad\forall\;v\in V_{h}^{0},

and thus using the definition of
A_{h}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{\tau};u^{h}-u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=A_{h}(u_{\tau};u^{h},v)-A_{h}(u_{\tau};u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=A_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},v)-A_{h}(u_{\tau};u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=((a(u_{h,0})-a(u_{\tau,0}))\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

(83)

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2},

with

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=((a(u_{h,0})-a(u))\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=((a(u)-a(u_{\tau,0}))\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

By the Taylor expansion (
17
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|S_{1}|

\displaystyle=|(\widetilde{a}_{u}(u_{h,0},u)(u_{h,0}-u)\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle\leq M_{a}\|u_{h,0}-u\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|\nabla_{w}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

then using Lemma
22
and Theorem
21
yields

(84)

\displaystyle|S_{1}|\leq C(u)h^{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.

In a similar way, we obtain

\displaystyle|S_{2}|\leq C(u)\tau^{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

which together with (
84
) and (
4
) leads to

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{\tau};u^{h}-u_{h},v)\leq C(u)\tau^{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.

Taking
v=u^{h}-u_{h}
in the above equation and using Lemma
23
and
2
yields

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-u^{h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq C(u)\tau^{k+1},

which is combining with Theorem
21
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Theorem
24
suggests that optimal order of convergence in the two-grid WG method with
k=1
can be achieved by employing
\tau=O(h^{1/2})
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, a WG method is proposed and analyzed for
the quasi-linear elliptic problem of non-monotone type. With the use of
Brouwer’s
fixed point theorem, the existence of WG solution and error estimates in both the energy norm and the
L^{2}
norm are derived. Moreover, a two-grid
WG method is introduced and its corresponding error estimates is analyzed
in the energy norm. Those theoretical results
are verified by numerical results.

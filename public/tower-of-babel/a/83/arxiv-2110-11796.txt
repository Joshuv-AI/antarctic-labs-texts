Connes spectral distance and nonlocality of generalized noncommutative phase spaces
Bing-Sheng Lin, Tai-Hua Heng

Abstract
We study the Connes spectral distance of quantum states and analyse the nonlocality of the 4 ​ D 4D generalized noncommutative phase space. By virtue of the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, we obtain the Dirac operator and construct a spectral triple corresponding to the noncommutative phase space. Based on the ball condition, we obtain some constraint relations about the optimal elements, and then calculate the Connes spectral distance between two Fock states. Due to the noncommutativity, the spectral distances between Fock states in generalized noncommutative phase space are shorter than those in normal phase space. This shortening of distances implies some kind of nonlocality caused by the noncommutativity. We also find that these spectral distances in 4 ​ D 4D generalized noncommutative phase space are additive and satisfy the normal Pythagoras theorem. When the noncommutative parameters equal zero, the results return to those in normal quantum phase space.

\displaystyle e_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{m+1,i}|m+1,i\rangle\langle m+1,i|

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(m+1)}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|m+1,i\rangle\langle m+1,i|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(m+1)}}|m+1\rangle_{\!1\,1\!}\langle m+1|\otimes\mathds{I}_{2}.

So the Connes distance between the states
|m+1,n\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+1,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+1,n}e_{o})-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e_{o})|

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle m+1,n|e_{o}|m+1,n\rangle-\langle m,n|e_{o}|m,n\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(m+1)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}.

Similarly, the Connes spectral distance between Fock states
|m,n+1\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

d(\omega_{m,n+1},\omega_{m,n})=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(n+1)}}=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}.

(57)

Next, let us consider the Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n\rangle
,
k>1
, and the corresponding Connes spectral distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|=\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m+k-1,n}+c_{m+k-1,n}-c_{m+k-2,n}

\displaystyle\qquad+...+c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+k,n}+E_{m+k-1,n}+...+E_{m+1,n}|.

From (
3
), we have

\displaystyle(1+t^{2})(G_{m+2,n}+H_{m+1,n+1})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\qquad(1+t^{2})(G_{m+1,n}+H_{m+1,n})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

\displaystyle(G_{m+2,n}+H_{m+1,n})+t^{2}(G_{m+1,n}+H_{m+1,n+1})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

\displaystyle(G_{m+1,n}+H_{m+1,n+1})+t^{2}(G_{m+2,n}+H_{m+1,n})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}.

(59)

So in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|=|E_{m+k,n}+E_{m+k-1,n}+...+E_{m+1,n}|
,
one must let
|c_{m+2,n}-c_{m,n}|=|E_{m+2,n}+E_{m+1,n}|
as large as possible.
There should be
H_{m+1,n}=H_{m+1,n+1}=0
, and

\displaystyle G_{m+2,n}+t^{2}G_{m+1,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\qquad G_{m+1,n}+t^{2}G_{m+2,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

\displaystyle(1+t^{2})G_{m+2,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\qquad(1+t^{2})G_{m+1,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}.

(60)

Therefore we have

G_{m+1,n}=G_{m+2,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}(1+t^{2})}.

(61)

Similarly, in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|
, there must be

H_{m+i,n}=H_{m+i,n+1}=0,\qquad G_{m+i,n}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}(1+t^{2})},\qquad i=1,2,...,k\,,

(62)

so

c_{m+i,n}-c_{m+i-1,n}=E_{m+i,n}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}.

(63)

For example, one can choose

c_{m+p,i}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sum_{j=1}^{m+p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}=\zeta_{0;m+p}\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}},

(64)

where
i=0,1,2...
,
p=0,1,...,k
, and the function
\zeta_{p;q}
is

\zeta_{p;q}\equiv\sum_{i=p+1}^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}=\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},p+1\right)-\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},q+1\right),

(65)

where
\zeta(s,q)
is the Hurwitz zeta function

\zeta(s,q)\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(q+i)^{s}}.

(66)

Obviously, there is
\zeta_{i;j}=-\zeta_{j;i}
, and therefore
\zeta_{i;i}=0
.
We also have
\zeta_{i;j}-\zeta_{i;k}=\zeta_{k;j}
.
It is easy to verify that these coefficients
c_{i,j}
(
64
) satisfy the relations (
62
).

Suppose
e_{o}
to be the optimal element with the coefficients
c_{i,j}
(
64
) which can attain the supremum in (
58
). There is

\displaystyle e_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{m+p,i}|m+p,i\rangle\langle m+p,i|

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sum_{p=0}^{k}\zeta_{0;m+p}|m+p\rangle\langle m+p|\otimes\mathds{I}_{2},

and the Connes spectral distance between the states
|m+k,n\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n}e_{o})-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e_{o})|

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle m+k,n|e_{o}|m+k,n\rangle-\langle m,n|e_{o}|m,n\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta_{m;m+k}\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}.

Since these distances do not depend on
n
, for any integers
n\neq n^{\prime}
, we always have
d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m+k,n^{\prime}},\omega_{m,n^{\prime}})
.
Obviously, there is
d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m,n},\omega_{m+k,n})
.

It is easy to see that, for
m,n,k,l\geqslant 0
, these distances satisfy

d(\omega_{m+k+l,n},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})+d(\omega_{m+k+l,n},\omega_{m+k,n}).

(69)

This means that these distances are additive.

Similarly, the Connes spectral distance between Fock states
|m,n+k\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

d(\omega_{m,n+k},\omega_{m,n})=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+i}}=\zeta_{n;n+k}\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}.

(70)

We also have
d(\omega_{m,n+k},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m^{\prime},n+k},\omega_{m^{\prime},n})
for
m\neq m^{\prime}
.

Compare with the result in Refs.
[
27
,
32
,
34
]
, one can find that the Connes spectral distances between Fock states in generalized noncommutative phase space have one more factor
\sqrt{1-\theta^{2}\!/\hbar^{2}}=\sqrt{1-\mu\nu\!/\hbar^{2}}
than those in normal quantum phase space. So in general, the Connes spectral distances in generalized noncommutative phase space are shorter than those in normal quantum phase space. This is intuitive and reasonable, because the noncommutativity of the space will lead to some kind of nonlocality. The partial nonlocality can equivalently make the distances shorter.

Obviously, when
\theta\to\hbar
, these Connes spectral distances will become zero. This will cause some singularities.

Furthermore, using the above method, one can find that for any integers
m,n,k,l\geqslant 0
, the Connes spectral distance between the Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|k,l\rangle
is (see Appendix
B
for more details)

\displaystyle d(\omega_{k,l},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;l}^{2}}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\hbar^{2}-\theta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;l}^{2}}\,.

It is easy to verify that, these spectral distances between Fock states in NCPS also satisfy the Pythagoras theorem,

d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})=\sqrt{d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})^{2}+d(\omega_{m,n+l},\omega_{m,n})^{2}}.

(72)

This also coincides with the result in Ref.
[
30
]
.

5
Discussions and conclusions

In this paper, we study the Connes spectral distance of Fock states in
4D
generalized noncommutative phase space. By virtue of the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, we obtain the Dirac operator and a spectral triple corresponding to the generalized noncommutative phase space. Based on the ball condition, we obtain some constraint relations about the optimal elements. Using these constraint relations, we construct the corresponding optimal elements and derive the Connes spectral distance between two Fock states.

We find that the spectral distances in noncommutative phase space are shorter than those in normal phase space. This is intuitive and reasonable, because the noncommutativity of the space will lead to some kind of nonlocality.
This nonlocality can make the distances shorter equivalently.
We also find that these spectral distances in
4D
generalized noncommutative phase space are additive and satisfy the normal Pythagoras theorem. When the noncommutative parameter
\theta=\sqrt{\mu\nu}
equals zero, the results return to those in normal quantum phase space. But if
\theta\to\hbar
, these Connes spectral distances will become zero. This will cause some singularities. Usually, we assume
\theta\ll\hbar
.

Here we only study the Connes spectral distances between Fock states. Obviously, these distances depend on the specific quantum states being considered.
One can also study the spectral distances between other kinds of pure states and mixed states.
In a noncommutative space, a pure state is the analog of a traditional point in a normal commutative space, and the spectral distance between pure states corresponds to the geodesic distance between points. Different kinds of quantum states can form different kinds of abstract spaces, and these spaces have different mathematical structures.
So studies of the Connes spectral distances between quantum states can help us to study the mathematical structures of the spaces and also physical properties of the quantum systems.

Our results are significant for the study of the Connes spectral distances of physical systems in noncommutative spaces.
Our methods can be used to study other physical systems in other kinds of noncommutative spaces.
Using our method, one can also study the Connes spectral distance between quantum states in higher-dimensional noncommutative spaces.
But usually the calculations and results will be much more complicated.

Appendix B
Calculations of Connes distance between Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n+l\rangle

First, let us consider the distance between the states
|m+1,n+1\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
,

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+1,n+1},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+1,n+1}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|

(120)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+1,n+1}-c_{m,n}|=\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|.

From (
3
), there is

\displaystyle(1+t^{2})(G_{m+1,n}+H_{m,n+1})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\qquad(1+t^{2})(G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m+1,n+1})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

\displaystyle G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\qquad G_{m+1,n}+H_{m+1,n+1}\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}.

(121)

In order to attain the supremum of
|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|
, one must let
G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}
as large as possible.
But if

G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}>\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})\beta^{2}},

(122)

then compare with (
B

G_{m+1,n}<G_{m+1,n+1},\qquad H_{m+1,n+1}<H_{m,n+1}.

(123)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
E_{m+1,n+1},F_{m,n+1}\geqslant 0
. Therefore there are

E_{m+1,n}<E_{m+1,n+1},\qquad F_{m+1,n+1}<F_{m,n+1}.

(124)

But

E_{m+1,n}+F_{m+1,n+1}=c_{m+1,n+1}-c_{m,n}=E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1},

(125)

this is in contradiction with the relations (
124
). This means that the inequality (
122
) is not true.

So there is

G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}\leqslant\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})\beta^{2}},

(126)

In order to attain the supremum of
|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|
, there must be

G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}=\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})\beta^{2}},

(127)

and

G_{m+1,n}=G_{m+1,n+1},\qquad H_{m+1,n+1}=H_{m,n+1},

(128)

or

E_{m+1,n}=E_{m+1,n+1},\qquad F_{m+1,n+1}=F_{m,n+1}.

(129)

|m+1,n+1\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+1,n+1},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|

(130)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}E_{m+1,n+1}\sqrt{m+1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}F_{m,n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m,n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})\beta^{2}}}.

In the above inequality, we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})^{2}\leqslant(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}),

(131)

where the equality holds if
x_{1}y_{2}=x_{2}y_{1}
.

So one can set

(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}=(n+1)F_{m,n+1},

(132)

and there should be

d(\omega_{m+1,n+1},\omega_{m,n})\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}.

|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n+l\rangle

k,l>1

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n+l}\,e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}\,e)|

(134)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|=\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m+k,n}+c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{l}(c_{m+k,n+i}-c_{m+k,n+i-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(c_{m+j,n}-c_{m+j-1,n})\right|

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{l}F_{m+k,n+i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{m+j,n}\right|.

From (
3

|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|

\displaystyle(1+t^{2})(G_{m+2,n+1}+H_{m+1,n+2})=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},\quad(1+t^{2})(G_{m+1,n+1}+H_{m+1,n+1})=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

G_{m+2,n+1}=G_{m+1,n+1},\qquad H_{m+1,n+1}=H_{m+1,n+2}.

\displaystyle G_{m+i,n+j}=G_{m+1,n},\qquad 1\leqslant i\leqslant k,0\leqslant j\leqslant l,

\displaystyle H_{m+i,n+j}=H_{m,n+1},\qquad 0\leqslant i\leqslant k,1\leqslant j\leqslant l,

E_{m+i,n+j}=\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{m+i}}E_{m+1,n},\qquad F_{m+i,n+j}=\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{n+j}}F_{m,n+1}.

(138)

(1+t^{2})(G_{m+1,n}+H_{m,n+1})=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}},

(139)

or

(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}=\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})\beta^{2}}.

(140)

Therefore the Connes spectral distance between the Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n+l\rangle
is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{m+i,n}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}F_{m+k,n+j}\right|

(141)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}+\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+j}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\zeta_{m;m+k}+\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\zeta_{n;n+l}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}.

Here we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (
131
) in the above inequality, and the equality holds if

\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\zeta_{n;n+l}=\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\zeta_{m;m+k}.

(142)

From (
140
) and (
142
), one can derive

E_{m+1,n}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}\frac{\zeta_{m;m+k}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}=c_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}},

(143)

F_{m,n+1}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}\frac{\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}=d_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}},

(144)

where

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{\zeta_{m;m+k}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}},\qquad d_{0}=\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}.

(145)

So in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|
, for example, one can choose

\displaystyle c_{p,q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}+d_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}=c_{0}\zeta_{0;p}+d_{0}\zeta_{0;q}

(146)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\frac{\zeta_{0;p}\zeta_{m;m+k}+\zeta_{0;q}\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}},

where
p=m,m+1,...,m+k
and
q=n,n+1,...,n+l
.
Obviously, there is

E_{i+1,j}=c_{i+1,j}-c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i+1}}c_{0},\qquad F_{i,j+1}=c_{i,j+1}-c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}}d_{0}.

(147)

The corresponding optimal element
e_{o}
is

\displaystyle e_{o}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=m}^{m+k}\sum_{q=n}^{n+l}c_{p,q}|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|

(148)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sum_{p=m}^{m+k}\sum_{q=n}^{n+l}\frac{\zeta_{0;p}\zeta_{m;m+k}+\zeta_{0;q}\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|,

and the spectral distance between the Fock states
|m+k,n+l\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n+l}\,e_{o})-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}\,e_{o})|

(149)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle m+k,n+l|e_{o}|m+k,n+l\rangle-\langle m,n|e_{o}|m,n\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\zeta_{m;m+k}+d_{0}\zeta_{n;n+l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}.

It is easy to verify that, these results (
148
) and (
149
) are also true for
k\leqslant 0
and/or
l\leqslant 0
.

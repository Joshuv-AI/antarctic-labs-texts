The sum relation as a primitive concept of mereology
Rafał Gruszczyński, Dazhu Li

Abstract
. Mereology in its formal guise is usually couched in a language whose signature contains only one primitive binary predicate symbol representing the part of relation, either the proper or improper one. In this paper, we put forward an approach to mereology that uses mereological sum as its primitive notion, and we demonstrate that it is definitionally equivalent to the standard parthood-based theory of mereological structures. MSC: 03A05 Keywords: mereology, mereological sum, mereological axioms, definitional equivalence of mereological theories, theory of relations

Introduction

‘Mereology’ is a catch-all term encompassing the whole menagerie of theories of parts and whole, both formal and informal. As formal theories, various systems of mereology usually share a common language whose signature contains only one primitive binary predicate symbol representing the
part of
relation. Various axioms are applied to it, leading to a variety of theories that represent different philosophical perspectives.

is not the only choice among the primitives of mereology.

3.
Sum structures

In this part, we present another approach to mereology, with the mereological sum as the primitive, which has not been considered so far in the literature. Given a class of objects
M
, our starting point is a binary relation
\mathord{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}\subseteq M\times\mathcal{P}(M)
whose intended interpretation is as follows:
x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X
iff
x
is a mereological sum of the collection
X
. For the relation, we assume the following postulates:

(S1)

\displaystyle(\forall X\in\mathcal{P}_{\!+}(M))(\exists{x\in X})\,x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\,,

(S2)

\displaystyle x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\wedge y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\rightarrow x=y\,,

(S3)

\displaystyle x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\wedge y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Y\wedge x\in Y\rightarrow y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\cup Y\,,

(S4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X&\wedge x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Y\wedge y\in Y\rightarrow\\
&(\exists z\in X)(\exists Z,U\in\mathcal{P}(M))\,(z\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Z\wedge y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}U\wedge Z\cap U\neq\emptyset)\,.\end{split}

Before showing more axioms for the relation
\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}
, let us first explain the ideas of the principles above. Axiom (

) states that every non-empty collection has a sum. Principle (

) indicates that any collection

) shows that whenever

X

is a sum of the augmented collection

. Finally, (

) says that if two collections

of objects have the same sum, then for each object in one collection, there is another object in the other collection such that the two objects are sums of collections with some common objects.

The meaning of (

(
\mathrm{df}\ingr_{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}

\displaystyle x\ingr_{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}y

\displaystyle{}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{{df}}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}(\exists X\in\mathcal{P}(M))\,(y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\wedge x\in X)\,,

(
\mathrm{df}\,{\overl_{\mathord{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}}}
)

\displaystyle x\overl_{\mathord{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}}y

\displaystyle{}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{{df}}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}(\exists X,Y\in\mathcal{P}(M))\,(x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\wedge y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Y\wedge X\cap Y\neq\emptyset)\,,

\mathrm{df}\,\mathord{\mathrel{\bot}_{\mathord{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}}}

\displaystyle x\mathrel{\bot}_{\mathord{\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}}}y

\displaystyle{}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{{df}}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}(\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{P}(M))\,(x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}X\wedge y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Y\rightarrow X\cap Y=\emptyset)\,.

6.
Summary and further work

We have presented and analyzed well-justified axioms for a system of mereology in which the
sum
relation replaces the
part-of
relation as a basic concept. The system composed of (
S1
)–(
S5
) works well in the sense that within it we capture the binary
parthood
relation, and we prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between mereological structures (in the sense of
Pietruszczak 2018
) and the sum structures as defined here.

There are good things and there is one we consider <<bad>> about our system. The good things are axioms (
S1
)–(
S4
) that are natural, simple and has an immediate interpretation. (
S5
), on the other hand, formulated in the two primitives of the theory,
\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}
and
\in
, is complex, convoluted and contrasts with the simplicity of the remaining four postulates:

\begin{split}(\forall y\in M)\,\bigl[(\exists Z\in\mathcal{P}(M))(x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Z&{}\wedge y\in Z)\rightarrow(\exists u\in X)(\exists Y,U\in\mathcal{P}(M))(y\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}Y\wedge u\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}U\wedge Y\cap Z\not=\emptyset)\bigr]\rightarrow\\
&x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}\bigcup_{u\in X}\{y\in M\mid(\exists U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(M))\,(x\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}U_{1}\wedge u\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}U_{2}\wedge y\in U_{1}\wedge y\in U_{2})\}\,.\end{split}

We believe that it can be replaced by simpler conditions, which we hope to discover in future investigations into the sum structures. We also believe that the sigma operation from Section
3.2
may be employed to obtain a completely new abstract perspective on theories of parts and wholes.

The theory from this paper falls within the scope of second-order systems. There are also other formal tools that are suitable for studying different relations, among which modal logic is an important tradition. As suggested by
Blackburn et al. 2001
, the tool usually has lower computational complexity although it is essentially a second-order theory, while many theories of mereology are undecidable (see e.g.,
Tsai 2013
). Based on a
relational semantics
,
Li and Wang 2022
defined a series of mereological theories for
\ingr
, but it remains to develop a desired modal system that corresponds to our axioms for
\mathrel{\mathsf{S}}
, for which the
neighborhood semantics

(
Pacuit 2017
)
would be useful. The development of this kind of approach is going to be our next step.

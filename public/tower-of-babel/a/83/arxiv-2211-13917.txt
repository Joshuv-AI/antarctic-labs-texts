The Russian Option with A Random Time Horizon
Zhuoshu Wu, Libo Li

Abstract
This paper is intended to provide a unique valuation formula for the Russian option with a random time horizon; in particular, such option restricts its holder to make their stopping decision before the last exit time of the price of the underlying asset at its running maximum. By the theory of enlargement of filtrations associated with random times, this pricing problem can be transformed into an equivalent optimal stopping problem with a semi-continuous, time-dependent gain function whose partial derivative is singular at certain points. Despite these unpleasant features of the gain function, with our choice of the parameters, we establish the monotonicity of the free boundary and the regularity of the value function, which in turn lead us to the desired free-boundary problem. After this, the nonlinear integral equations that characterise the free boundary and the value function are derived. We also examine the solutions to these equations in details.

When a contingent claim is valued, it is conventional to assume that there is no risk that the counter-party will default, that is, the counter party will always be able to make the promised payment. . Furthermore, observe that

\displaystyle V(t,y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau\in[0,T-t]}\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G\left(t+\tau,Y_{t+\tau}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau\in[0,T-t]}\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G\left(t+\tau,Y_{t+\tau}\right)I\{\tau<T-t\}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)}G\left(T,Y_{T}\right)I\{\tau=T-t\}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\bigg\{\sup_{\tau\in[0,T-t)}\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G\left(t+\tau,Y_{t+\tau}\right)\right),\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda(T-t)}G\left(T,Y_{T}\right)\right)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\max\bigg\{\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\bar{\tau}_{D}}G\left(t+\bar{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\bar{\tau}_{D}}\right)\right),\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda(T-t)}G\left(T,Y_{T}\right)\right)\bigg\},

\displaystyle V(t,y)=\begin{cases}&\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\bar{\tau}_{D}}G\left(t+\bar{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\bar{\tau}_{D}}\right)\right),\,\,\bar{\tau}_{D}<T-t,\\
&\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda(T-t)}G\left(T,Y_{T}\right)\right),\qquad\,\,\,\bar{\tau}_{D}>T-t,\end{cases}

which implies that

, and the desired claim follows.

3.3
The Continuation and Stopping Sets

We first need to make some preparations in order to prove (

) and (
3.41
).

Since the gain function
G(t,y)
is a continuous function whose derivative in
y=L
is not continuous, we apply the local time-space formula on
e^{-\lambda s}G(t+s,Y_{t+s})
and obtain

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda s}G(t+s,Y_{t+s})

\displaystyle=G(t,y)+\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}H(t+u,Y_{t+u})I\{Y_{t+u}>L\}du

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}G_{y}(t+u,Y_{t+u})I\{Y_{t+u}=1\}dR_{t+u}+M_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}\left(G_{y}(t+u,L+)-G_{y}(t+u,L-)\right)dl_{u}^{L}(Y),

(3.48)

where we set

\displaystyle H(t,y)

\displaystyle=\left(-\lambda G+\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}-ry\frac{\partial G}{\partial y}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}y^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial y^{2}}\right)(t,y),

(3.49)

and
M_{s}=-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma Y_{t+s}G_{y}(t+s,Y_{t+s})I\{Y_{t+s}\neq L\}d\widetilde{W}_{s}
is a martingale.

To simplify (
3.49
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(t,y)=(y-L)\frac{\partial Z(t,y)}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial y}(t,y)=Z(t,y)+(y-L)\frac{\partial Z(t,y)}{\partial y},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial y^{2}}(t,y)=2\frac{\partial Z(t,y)}{\partial y}+(y-L)\frac{\partial^{2}Z(t,y)}{\partial y^{2}},

for all
y>L
.

By combining the above computation and using the fact that
Z(t,y)
satisfying (
3.29
), we therefore have (
3.49
) rewritten as follows

\displaystyle H(t,y)=\left(-\lambda(y-L)Z-ryZ+\sigma^{2}Ly\frac{\partial Z}{\partial y}\right)(t,y).

Then, by taking the expectation under
\widetilde{P}_{t,y}
and applying the optional sampling theorem to get rid of the martingale part, we find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda s}G(t+s,Y_{t+s})\right)

\displaystyle=G(t,y)+\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}H(t+u,Y_{t+u})I\{Y_{t+u}>L\}du

\displaystyle+\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}G_{y}(t+u,1+)I\{Y_{t+u}\neq L\}dR_{t+u}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\lambda u}\left(G_{y}(t+u,L+)-G_{y}(t+u,L-)\right)dl_{u}^{L}(Y).

(3.50)

We first investigate the map
t\mapsto H(t,y)
by a direct differentiation in

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(t,y)

\displaystyle=-(ry+\lambda y-\lambda L)\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}+\sigma^{2}Ly\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial y\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{8\pi}(T-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{-2(ry+\lambda y-\lambda L)\log{y}}{\sigma}+2\sigma^{2}L-\frac{2(\log{y})^{2}L}{T-t}+(2r-\sigma^{2})L\log{y}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{y^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{2}}L}{\sqrt{8\pi}(T-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(-\frac{2r\log{L}}{\sigma}+2\sigma^{2}-\frac{2(\log{L})^{2}}{T}+(2r-\sigma^{2})\log{L}\right),

(3.51)

from which one sees that there exists a wide range of parameters for

Lemma 3.11

\displaystyle G_{y}(t,y)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for $y\leq L$},\\

and for
L>1

\displaystyle G_{y}(t,1+)=0,

(3.52)

\displaystyle G_{y}(t,L+)=Z(t,L+),

(3.53)

\displaystyle G_{y}(t,L-)=0.

(3.54)

Let

\displaystyle V(t_{1},y)-V(t_{2},y)\geq\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G(t_{1}+\tau,Y_{t_{1}+\tau}^{y})\right)-\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G(t_{2}+\tau,Y_{t_{2}+\tau}^{y})\right)

Proposition 3.15
.

The optimal stopping boundary
b

and

Proof.

We first show that (i)

is decreasing on

exists.

\mathcal{D}
is closed so that its limit point

, it then follows, together with (

), that

.
However, the fact that
b
is decreasing suggests that

and therefore,

is right-continuous as claimed.

We then show that (ii)
b
is left-continuous. Assume that, for contradiction, there exists
t\in(0,T)
such that

. Then, fix a point

.

By (
3.43
) and (

\displaystyle V(s,y_{*})-G(s,y_{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\int_{x}^{b(s)}\left(V_{yy}-G_{yy}\right)(s,z)dzdx,

for each

where

and
t-\delta>0
. Knowing that the value function satisfies

\displaystyle V_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{Y}V=\lambda V,\qquad\text{in $\mathcal{C}$},

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}y^{2}}{2}\left(V_{yy}-G_{yy}\right)(t,y)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}y^{2}}{2}\left(V_{yy}-G_{yy}\right)(t,y)\geq\left(ry(V_{y}-G_{y})-H\right)(t,y),

in

.

Using (
3.56
) and (
3.57
), we find that

\displaystyle V(s,y_{*})-G(s,y_{*})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\int_{x}^{b(s)}\left(\frac{2r}{\sigma^{2}z}\left(V_{z}-G_{z}\right)-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}z^{2}}H\right)(s,z)dzdx,

and since

is strictly negative in
\{(s,y)\in[t-\delta,t]\times[y_{*},b(t-\delta)]\}
(as

), we set

\displaystyle m=\inf\bigg\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}y^{2}}H(s,y):(s,y)\in[t-\delta,t]\times[y_{*},b(t-\delta)]\bigg\}>0,

\displaystyle V(s,y_{*})-G(s,y_{*})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2r}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\int_{x}^{b(s)}\frac{1}{z}\left(V_{z}-G_{z}\right)(s,z)dzdx+m\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}(b(s)-x)dx.

Via an integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\int_{x}^{b(s)}\frac{1}{z}d\left(V-G\right)(s,z)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\left(V-G\right)(s,z)\big|_{x}^{b(s)}+\int_{x}^{b(s)}\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(V-G\right)(s,z)dz\right)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}-\frac{1}{x}\left(V-G\right)(s,x)dx+\int_{y_{*}}^{b(s)}\int_{x}^{b(s)}\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(V-G\right)(s,z)dzdx,

where the second equality follows from (
3.43
).

From Lemma
3.7
and the fact that the gain function is continuous on
(1,\infty)\times[0,T]
, we know that

\displaystyle V(t,y)=V(t-,y),

\displaystyle G(t,y)=G(t-,y),

and from (
3.47
), it follows that

\displaystyle V(t,y)=G(t,y)\,\,\text{for all $y\in[y_{*},b(t-))$}.

Then, we observe that

\displaystyle\big|V-G\big|(s,y)

\displaystyle\leq V(s,y)\leq\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq{T-s}}\widetilde{E}_{s,y}\left(Y_{s+\tau}^{y}\right)\leq ye^{\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)T}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{2\sigma\sqrt{T}X}\right)<\infty,

\displaystyle V(t,y_{*})-G(t,y_{*})

\mathcal{D}
, indicating that such point cannot exist. Therefore, by combining statements (i) and (ii), we have established the continuity of

on
[0,T]
.

. Assume that,
b(T-)>L

Lemma 3.16
.

(Smooth-fit Condition)
The value function
V(t,y)
is differentiable at the optimal stopping boundary
b

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}V(t,y)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(t,y),

whenever
V(t,y)=G(t,y)
for
y=b(t)
.

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T)
be given and fixed and set

. Knowing that
b(t)>L
, let
\epsilon>0
so that
y-\epsilon>L
. Since

\displaystyle V(t,y)=G(t,y),

\displaystyle V(t,y-\epsilon)>G(t,y-\epsilon),

we have

\displaystyle\frac{V(t,y)-V(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\leq\frac{G(t,y)-G(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon},

(3.58)

and taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{V(t,y)-V(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{G(t,y)-G(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(t,y).

To prove the reverse inequality, let
\tau_{\epsilon}
be the optimal stopping time for
V(t,y-\epsilon)

\displaystyle\frac{V(t,y)-V(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(G\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)-G\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-L\right)^{+}-\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)^{+}\right)Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\qquad+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)-Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)\right)\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\qquad+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)-Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)\right)\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}I\{y-\epsilon\geq S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\}\frac{\epsilon}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\qquad+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\left(Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)-Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)\right)\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)^{+}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}I\{y-\epsilon\geq S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\}\frac{1}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}Z\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\qquad+\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{\epsilon}}\frac{Z_{y}\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}I\{y-\epsilon\geq S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\}\right),

where the second inequality holds via the facts that
Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\geq Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}
(
\geq L
because of the definition of the optimal stopping time) and that

\displaystyle\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-L\right)^{+}-\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}-L\right)^{+}

\displaystyle=I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right)+\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-L\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\geq I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}\left(Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\right),

and the third inequality follows from

\displaystyle Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}-Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{(y-S_{\tau_{\epsilon}})^{+}+S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}-(y-\epsilon-S_{\tau_{\epsilon}})^{+}-S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{I\{y-\epsilon\geq S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\}\epsilon+\left(y-S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\right)^{+}}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}}\geq\frac{I\{y-\epsilon\geq S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\}\epsilon}{X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}},

and by mean value theory for
x\in[y-\epsilon,y]
, the last equality follows. Note that
I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\geq L\}=1
implies
I\{Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\geq L\}=1
so that
Z_{y}\left(t+\tau_{\epsilon},Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\right)
is bounded by
C_{1}
.

Finally, let
\epsilon\to 0
so that
\tau_{\epsilon}\to 0
a.s (see Lemma
A.1
) and the dominated convergence theorem once again yields
S_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\to 1
,
X_{\tau_{\epsilon}}\to 1
,
Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{x}\to y
,
Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y-\epsilon}\to y
,
Y_{t+\tau_{\epsilon}}^{y}\to y
. Then, recalling that
y=b(t)\geq L>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{V(t,y)-V(t,y-\epsilon)}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq Z(t,y)+(y-L)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Z(t,y)=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(t,y),

(3.59)

which, joining with (
3.58
), proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

The normal reflection condition holds, that is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}V(t,1+)=0.

Proof.

We begin by noticing that from (
3.32
) and the construction of the process
Y

\displaystyle V(t,y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq{T-t}}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}G\left(t+\tau,\frac{(y-S_{\tau})^{+}+S_{\tau}}{X_{\tau}}\right)\right),

(3.60)

and that, in fact, there exists
y_{*}\in(1,L]
such that the map
y\mapsto G\left(t,y\right)
is increasing on
[1,y_{*}]
in the sense that
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(t,y)=\left(Z+(y-L)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Z\right)(t,y)>0
.

It then follows, from the view of (
3.60
), that
y\mapsto V(t,y)
is increasing on
[1,y_{*}]
, meaning that
V_{y}(t,1+)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T)
, and moreover, since the value function
V
is
C^{1,2}
on the continuation set, we have
t\mapsto V_{y}(t,1+)
is continuous on
[0,T)
.

Assume that, for contradiction, there exists
t\in[0,T)
so that
V_{y}(t,1+)>0
. Let
\tau_{D}
be the optimal stopping time for
V(t,1+)
and let
\hat{\tau}_{D}=\tau_{D}\wedge s
for
s\in[0,T-t]
. Then, we apply Itô’s formula and the optional sampling theorem to obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}V\left(t+\hat{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\right)\right)=V(t,1)+\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(\int_{0}^{\hat{\tau}_{D}}e^{-\lambda u}V_{y}(t+u,Y_{t+u})dR_{t+u}\right).

Now in order to complete the proof, we need to further establish the martingale property of

\displaystyle\bigg\{e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}V\left(t+\hat{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}^{1}\right),\mathcal{F}_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\bigg\}_{0\leq\hat{\tau}_{D}\leq T-t}.

(3.61)

Let us check the martingale property claimed in (
3.61
) for all
u\leq\hat{\tau}_{D}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}V\left(t+\hat{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}^{1}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}\widetilde{E}_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}^{1}}\left(e^{-\lambda(\tau_{D}-\hat{\tau}_{D})}G\left(t+\tau_{D},Y_{t+\tau_{D}}\right)\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda(\tau_{D}-\hat{\tau}_{D})}G\left(t+\tau_{D},Y_{t+\tau_{D}}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{E}\left(\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{D}}G\left(t+\tau_{D},Y_{t+\tau_{D}}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{D}}G\left(t+\tau_{D},Y_{t+\tau_{D}}\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{-ru}\widetilde{E}_{t+u,Y_{t+u}^{1}}\left(e^{-r(\tau_{D}-u)}G\left(t+\tau_{D},Y_{t+\tau_{D}}\right)\right)=e^{-ru}V\left(t+u,Y_{t+u}^{1}\right),

where the first equality follows from the definitions of
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
and the value function
V
, the second and the fifth equalities are immediate from the strong Markov property of the process
Y
whereas the fourth equality holds by smoothing lemma as
\mathcal{F}_{t+u}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}
, and thereby, proving the martingale property.

With the aid of the above observation, we then obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(e^{-\lambda\hat{\tau}_{D}}V\left(t+\hat{\tau}_{D},Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\right)\right)=V(t,1),

which implies that

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(\int_{0}^{\hat{\tau}_{D}}e^{-\lambda u}V_{y}(t+u,Y_{t+u})dR_{t+u}\right)=0.

In particular,
V_{y}(t+u,Y_{t+u})dR_{t+u}=V_{y}(t+u,1+)dR_{t+u}
, which, together with the assumption that
V_{y}(t+u,1+)>0
for all
u\in[0,\hat{\tau}_{D}]
, tells us that

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(\int_{0}^{\hat{\tau}_{D}}dR_{t+u}\right)=0.

(3.62)

By the solution of (
2.7
), equation (
3.62
) and the optional sampling theorem, we see that

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\left(Y_{t+\hat{\tau}_{D}}\right)-1+r\widetilde{E}_{t,1}\int_{0}^{\hat{\tau}_{D}}Y_{t+u}du=0.

(3.63)

As
Y_{t+u}>1
for all
u\in[0,T-t]
, (
3.63
) implies that
\widetilde{P}_{t,1}(\hat{\tau}_{D}=0)=1
, which is not possible in the sense that the optimal stopping boundary
b>1
and
\hat{\tau}_{D}>0
, proving the desired assertion.
∎

With a bit more effort, we can verify the following result to apply the local time-space formula freely later.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let the function
F(t,y)=e^{-\lambda t}V(t,y)
. Then

\displaystyle\text{$F(t,y)$ is $C^{1,2}$ on $\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{\bar{D}}$},

(3.64)

\displaystyle\text{$F_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{Y}F$ is locally bounded $\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{\bar{D}}$},

(3.65)

\displaystyle\text{$t\mapsto F_{y}(t,b(t)\pm)$ is continuous},

(3.66)

\displaystyle\text{$F_{yy}=F_{1}+F_{2}$ on $\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{\bar{D}}$},

\displaystyle\text{where $F_{1}$ is non-negative and $F_{2}$ is continuous on ${\mathcal{C}}$ and $\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}$},

(3.67)

where
\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}=\mathcal{D}\setminus\{(t,b(t))\}
.

Proof.

First of all, (
3.64
) is immediate from the fact that

\displaystyle F(t,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{-\lambda t}V(t,y),&\text{for $(t,y)\in\mathcal{C}$},\\
e^{-\lambda t}G(t,y),&\text{for $(t,y)\in\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}$},\end{cases}

and that
V(t,y)
is
C^{1,2}
in
\mathcal{C}
, so is
G(t,y)
in
\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}
.

From
[
11
, Page 409]
, we know that to verify (
3.65
) is to show that
F_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{Y}F
is locally bounded on
\mathcal{K}\cap(\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{{\bar{D}}})
for each compact set
\mathcal{K}
in
[0,T)\times[1,\infty)
. In
\mathcal{C}
, we have
F_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{Y}F=0
in the sense that
V
satisfies
(
3.42
)
. As in
\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}
,
F_{t}+\mathbb{L}_{Y}F=e^{-\lambda t}H(t,y)
, which is continuous on the compact set
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}
and hence, the range of
H
is bounded as claimed.

Next, (
3.66
) follows via (
3.44
) and Lemma
3.15
.

Moving down the list, (
3.67
) follows from

\displaystyle F_{yy}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2e^{-\lambda t}}{\sigma^{2}y^{2}}\left(\lambda V-V_{t}+ryV_{y}\right)(t,y),\,\,\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\text{for $(t,y)\in\mathcal{C}$},\\
\frac{2e^{-\lambda t}}{\sigma^{2}y^{2}}\left(\lambda G-G_{t}+ryG_{y}+H\right)(t,y),\,\,\,\,\text{for $(t,y)\in\mathcal{{\bar{D}}}$},\end{cases}

where the first two terms on both regions are nonnegative and since the continuous function
V
is
C^{1,2}
in
\mathcal{C}
because of the
C^{1,2}
-property of
V
, the partial derivatives
V_{t}
,
V_{y}
and
V_{yy}
exist and are continuous, whereas as
y=b(t)
,

V_{y}(t,b(t))=G_{y}(t,b(t))=Z(t,b(t))+(b(t)-L)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}Z(t,b(t)),

whose continuity is immediate, so are the latter two terms in
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

We follow, essentially,

\displaystyle\tau_{b}=\inf\{0\leq s\leq T-t:Y_{t+s}\geq b(t+s)\}.

By replacing
s
in (
3.71
) and (
3.75
) with
\tau_{b}
and taking the expectation under
\widetilde{P}_{t,y}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{b}}G\left(t+\tau_{b},Y_{t+\tau_{b}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=V(t,y),

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{b}}G\left(t+\tau_{b},Y_{t+\tau_{b}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=G(t,y)+\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{b}}e^{-\lambda u}H\left(t+u,Y_{t+u}\right)I\{Y_{t+u}\geq c(t+u)\}du\right).

Knowing that
V(t,y)>G(t,y)
as
y<b(t)
, we find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{b}}e^{-\lambda u}H\left(t+u,Y_{t+u}\right)I\{Y_{t+u}\geq c(t+u)\}du\right)>0,

but
H(t,y)\leq 0
in
[0,T)\times[1,\infty)
suggests otherwise, we have reach a contradiction and thereby establishing the uniqueness of
b
.

The theorem is completely proved.
∎

5. Useful Results for the Proof of Smooth-fit Condition

Lemma A.1

(For Chapter 4)
.

As
\epsilon\to 0
,
\tau_{\epsilon}\to 0
almost surely.

Proof.

By construction of
\tau_{\epsilon}
and the definition of
Y
,

\displaystyle\tau_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\inf\{0\leq s\leq T-t:Y_{t+s}^{y-\epsilon}\geq b(t+s)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{0\leq s\leq T-t:\frac{(y-\epsilon)\vee S_{s}}{X_{s}}\geq b(t+s)\bigg\}.

Note that
\bigg\{\frac{y-\epsilon}{X_{s}}\geq b(t+s)\bigg\}\subseteq\bigg\{\frac{(y-\epsilon)\vee S_{s}}{X_{s}}\geq b(t+s)\bigg\}
so that

\displaystyle\tau_{\epsilon}\leq\hat{\tau}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{0\leq s\leq T-t:\frac{y-\epsilon}{X_{s}}\geq b(t+s)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{0\leq s\leq T-t:(y-\epsilon)e^{-\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)s-\sigma\widetilde{W}_{s}}\geq b(t+s)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{0\leq s\leq T-t:-\widetilde{W}_{s}\geq\frac{\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{y-\epsilon}}+\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)s}{\sigma}\bigg\}.

Let
\beta=r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
and
g(s)=\frac{\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{y-\epsilon}}+\beta s}{\sigma}
for notational convenience.
As
\epsilon\to 0
, (recalling that
y=b(t)
),

\displaystyle g(s)\to\frac{\log{\frac{b(t+s)}{b(t)}}+\beta s}{\sigma}\leq\frac{\beta s}{\sigma},

where the inequality follows from the map

being decreasing, and since

is a lower function for standard Brownian motion

under measure

, so is

, implying that

6. Property of the Value Function

Lemma A.2
.

The map
t\mapsto V(t,y)
is decreasing.

Proof.

By the time-homogeneous property of process

, for

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{t,y}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}\left(Y_{t+\tau}-L\right)^{+}Z\left(t+\tau,Y_{t+\tau}\right)\right)=\widetilde{E}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau}\left(Y_{\tau}^{y}-L\right)^{+}Z\left(t+\tau,Y_{\tau}^{y}\right)\right),

8. The Uniform Integrability of
Y

To apply the dominated convergence theorem, we observe that

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{y\vee\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq t}X_{u}}{X_{t}}\leq\frac{y\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq T}X_{u}}{X_{t}}=ye^{-(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})t-\sigma\widetilde{W}_{t}}\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq T}X_{u}

\displaystyle\leq ye^{\sigma\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq T}(-\widetilde{W}_{u})}e^{\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)T+\sigma\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq T}(\widetilde{W}_{u})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}ye^{\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)T}\left(e^{2\sigma\max\limits_{0\leq u\leq T}(-\widetilde{W}_{u})}+e^{2\sigma\max\limits_{0\leq u\leq T}\widetilde{W}_{u}}\right){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}}\,ye^{\left(r+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)T}e^{2\sigma\sqrt{T}X},

(A.81)

where the first inequality holds as
y\geq 1
,
\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq T}X_{u}\geq\max\limits_{0\leq{u}\leq t}X_{u}\geq 1
and the last inequality follows from Young’s inequality and the random variable
X
has the probability density function given as follows

\displaystyle f_{X}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}},\qquad x>0.

We further estimate the following

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}\left(e^{2\sigma\sqrt{T}X}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\sigma\sqrt{T}x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(x^{2}-4\sigma\sqrt{T}x+4\sigma^{2}T\right)+2\sigma^{2}T}dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{2\sigma^{2}T}\int_{-2\sigma\sqrt{T}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}dy\qquad\text{ (Change of variable $y=x-2\sigma\sqrt{T}$)}

\displaystyle=e^{2\sigma^{2}T}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{2\sigma^{2}T}\int_{-2\sigma\sqrt{T}}^{0}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}dy<\infty.

We therefore, in a somewhat lengthy way, have proven that
Y_{t}^{y}
is dominated by a positive integrable random variable.

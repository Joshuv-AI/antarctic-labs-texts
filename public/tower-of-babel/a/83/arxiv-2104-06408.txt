Ill-posedness for the Euler equations in Besov spaces
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In the paper, we consider the Cauchy problem to the Euler equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
. We construct an initial data
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
showing that the corresponding solution map of the Euler equations starting from
u_{0}
is
discontinuous at
t=0
in the metric of
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
, which implies the ill-posedness for this equation in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
. We generalize the periodic result of Cheskidov and Shvydkoy
[
4
]
(Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 138 (2010), 1059–1067).

Keywords:
Euler equations, Ill-posedness, Besov spaces

MSC (2010):
76D03; 35Q30

1
Introduction

In this article, we consider the Euler equations governing the motion of an incompressible
fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the vector field
u(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
stands for the velocity of the fluid, the quantity
P(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes
the scalar pressure, and
\mathrm{div\,}u=0
means that the fluid is incompressible. The mathematical study of the Euler equations of ideal hydrodynamics has a long and distinguished history. We do not detail the literature since it is huge and refer the readers to see
the monographs of Bahouri–Chemin–Danchin
[
3
]
and Majda–Bertozzi
[
13
]
.

We say that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is Hadamard (locally) well-posed in a Banach space
X
if for any data
u_{0}\in X
there exists (at least for a short time)
T>0
and a unique solution in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
which depends continuously on the data. In particular, we say that the solution map is continuous if for any
u_{0}\in X
, there exists a neighborhood
B\subset X
of
u_{0}
such that
for every
u\in B
the map
u\mapsto U
from
B
to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];X)
is continuous, where
U

denotes the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
. Our goal of this paper is to consider the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and generalize the periodic result of Cheskidov and Shvydkoy
[
4
]
.
Our main result of this paper reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

Let
d\geq 2
. Assume that

\sigma>1+\frac{d}{p}\quad\text{with}\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

There exits
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Remark 1.1

Theorem
1.1
demonstrates the ill-posedness of the Euler equations in
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
. More precisely, there exists
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
such that the corresponding solution to the Euler equations that starts from
u_{0}
does not converge back to
u_{0}
in the metric of
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
as time goes to zero.

Remark 1.2

The method we used in proving Theorem
1.1
is completely different from that
[
4
]
.

Remark 1.3

Due to the incompressible condition
\eqref{e}_{2}
, the pressure can be eliminated from (
1.1
). In fact, applying the Leray operator
\mathcal{P}
to
\eqref{e}_{1}
, then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-\mathcal{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)=:\mathbf{P}(u).

Thus, we transform (
1.1
) equivalently into the nonlocal form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\mathbf{P}(u),\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.2)

Concerning the well-posedness of the Euler equations we refer to see
[
3
,
5
,
13
]
. Next, we recall some results about the properties of the data-to-solution map. The first results of this type was proved by Kato
[
9
]
who showed that the
solution operator for the (inviscid) Burgers equation is not Hölder continuous in the

H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
-norm
(s>3/2)
for any Hölder exponent. Kato–Ponce
[
11
]
obtained the continuity results for the solution map of the Euler equations in
W^{s}_{p}
(see also
[
10
]
).
Pak–Park
[
17
]
established existence and uniqueness
of solutions of the Euler equations in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
and showed that the solution map is in fact Lipschitz
continuous when viewed as a map between
B^{0}_{\infty,1}
and
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty);B^{0}_{\infty,1})
. Later, Cheskidov–Shvydkoy
[
4
]
proved that the solution of the Euler equations cannot be continuous as a function
of the time variable at
t=0
in the spaces
B^{s}_{r,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
where
s>0
if
2<r\leq\infty
and
s>d(2/r-1)

if
1\leq r\leq 2
. Furthermore, Bourgain–Li
[
1
,
2
]
proved the strong local ill-posedness of the Euler equations in borderline Besov spaces
B^{d/p+1}_{p,r}
with
(p,r)\in[1,\infty)\times(1,\infty]
when
d=2,3
. Subsequently, Misiołek–Yoneda
[
14
]
studied the borderline cases and showed that the 2D Euler equations are not locally well-posed in the sense of Hardamard in the
C^{1}
space and in the Besov space
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
.
Recently, Misiołek–Yoneda
[
15
]
showed that the solution map for the Euler equations is not even continuous in the space of Hölder continuous functions and thus not locally Hadamard well-posed in
B^{1+\sigma}_{\infty,\infty}
with any
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Concerning the non-uniform continuity of the data-to-solution map, we refer the readers to see
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
12
]
.

2
Littlewood-Paley Decomposition and Besov Spaces

We will use the following notations throughout this paper. The notation
A\lesssim B
(resp.,
A\gtrsim B
) means that there exists a harmless positive constant
c
such that
A\leq cB
(resp.,
A\geq cB
). For
X
a Banach space and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I;X)
the set of continuous functions on
I
with values in
X
.
Let us recall that for all
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}u
, also denoted by
\widehat{u}
, is defined by

\mathcal{F}u(\xi)=\widehat{u}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}u(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any}\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Due to the Holdge decomposition, we know that any vector field
f=(f_{1},...,f_{d})
with components in
S_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
may be decomposed into one potential part
\mathcal{Q}f
and one divergence-free part
\mathcal{P}f
, where the projectors
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{I}+(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla{\rm{div}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{Q}=-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla{\rm{div}}.

(2.3)

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces and their some useful properties.

Proposition 2.1
(Littlewood-Paley decomposition, See
[
3
]
)

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}.

There exist two radial functions
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
both taking values in
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\chi^{2}(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\leq 1\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, we can deduce that
\varphi\equiv 1
for
\frac{4}{3}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}
. This basic fact will be used in the sequel.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the inhomogeneous dyadic blocks
{\Delta}_{j}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle 0,

if
j\leq-2
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

if
j=-1
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\big),

if
j\geq 0
.

In the inhomogeneous case, the following Littlewood-Paley decomposition makes sense

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{for any}\;u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We turn to the definition of the Besov Spaces and norms which will come into play in our paper.

Definition 2.1
(Besov Space
[
3
]
)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all tempered distribution
f
such that

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{sjr}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/r},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

if
r=\infty
.

Remark 2.1

It should be emphasized that the following facts will be often used implicity:

•

B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow B^{t}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{for}\;s>t\quad\text{or}\quad s=t,1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty.

•

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s>\frac{d}{p}+1
,
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach algebra.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
only for the case
d=2
since the case
d\geq 3
requires very little changes (for more details see
[
12
]
).

3.1
Construction of Initial Data

Firstly, we need to introduce smooth, radial cut-off functions to localize the frequency region.
Let
\widehat{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be an even, real-valued and non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=

\displaystyle 1,

if
|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{16}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=

\displaystyle 0,

if
|\xi|\geq\frac{1}{4}
.

We set the initial data
u_{0}(x)
as follows:

\displaystyle u_{0}(x):=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=0}f_{kj}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle f_{m}(x):=2^{-m(\sigma+1)}\begin{pmatrix}-\partial_{2}\\
\partial_{1}\end{pmatrix}g_{m}\quad\text{with}\quad g_{m}(x):=\phi(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{m}x_{1}\Big)\phi(x_{2}),\quad m\geq 0.

3.2
A Key Lemma

We establish the following crucial Lemmas which involves the properties of the initial data.

Lemma 3.1

Define the initial data
u_{0}(x)
by (
3.4
) above. Then there exist some sufficiently large
k
and
n
such that

(1)

\mathord{{\rm div}}u_{0}=0
;

(2)

\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\leq C
;

(3)

\|\Delta_{kn}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{p}}\geq c2^{kn}2^{-kn\sigma}
,

where
C
and
c
are uniform constants.

Proof.
A direct calculation yields
(1)
.

Easy computations give that

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{m}(\xi)=2^{-{m}(\sigma+1)-1}\begin{pmatrix}-i\xi_{2}\\
i\xi_{1}\end{pmatrix}\Big[\widehat{\phi}\Big(\xi_{1}-\frac{17}{12}2^{m}\Big)+\widehat{\phi}\Big(\xi_{1}+\frac{17}{12}2^{m}\Big)\Big]\widehat{\phi}(\xi_{2}),

which implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{f}_{m}

\displaystyle\subset\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \frac{17}{12}2^{m}-\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{17}{12}2^{m}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\subset\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \frac{33}{24}2^{m}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{35}{24}2^{m}\Big\}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 4.

Notice that

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)\equiv 1\quad\text{in}\;\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \frac{4}{3}2^{j}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}2^{j}\Big\},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(\Delta_{j}(f_{m})\big)=\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\widehat{f}_{m},

then we deduce for
m\geq 4

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(f_{m})=\begin{cases}f_{m},&if\;\;j=m,\\
0,&if\;\;j\neq m.\end{cases}

(3.5)

We should mention that (
3.5
) also holds if
\emph{cos}
were replaced by
\emph{sin}
in the definition of
g_{m}
.

Thus, we deduce from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\ell\geq-1}2^{\ell\sigma}\|\Delta_{\ell}u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C.

This gives
(2)
.

To prove
(3)
, we write

\displaystyle u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{kj}+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{j-1}_{i=0}(f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}+f_{ki}\cdot\nabla f_{kj}).

(3.6)

On the one hand, we have for
\ell=1,2

\displaystyle\big(f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{kj}\big)_{\ell}

\displaystyle=2^{-2kj(\sigma+1)}\Big[\Phi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})+\Phi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kj+1}x_{1}\Big)+\Phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2})\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kj+1}x_{1}\Big)\Big],

where

\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi_{1}},\;\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi_{2}},\;\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{\Phi_{3}}\subset\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ |\xi|\leq 2\big\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{kn}\left((f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{kj})_{\ell}\right)\equiv 0\quad\text{for}\quad j\geq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\Delta_{kn}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{kj}\right)\equiv 0.

(3.7)

On the other hand, notice that

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \mathcal{F}\left(f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}\right)

\displaystyle\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \frac{17}{12}2^{kj}-\frac{17}{12}2^{ki}-\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{17}{12}2^{kj}+\frac{17}{12}2^{ki}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \frac{33}{24}2^{kj}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{35}{24}2^{kj}\right\},

and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{kn}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{j-1}_{i=0}(f_{kj}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}+f_{ki}\cdot\nabla f_{kj})\right)=\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}(f_{kn}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}+f_{ki}\cdot\nabla f_{kn}).

(3.8)

Thus, we deduce from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\Delta_{kn}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}f_{kn}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}+\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}f_{ki}\cdot\nabla f_{kn}+f_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{kn}

\displaystyle=:\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}+\mathbf{I}_{3}.

The first two terms can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{1}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\|f_{kn}\cdot\nabla f_{ki}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{kn}\|_{L^{p}}\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\|\nabla f_{ki}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-kn\sigma}\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}2^{-ki(\sigma-1)}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-kn\sigma}

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{2}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\|f_{ki}\cdot\nabla f_{kn}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f_{kn}\|_{L^{p}}\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}\|f_{ki}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{kn}2^{-kn\sigma}\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}2^{-k\sigma i}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{kn}2^{-kn\sigma}2^{-k\sigma}.

(3.10)

For the last term
f_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{kn}
, we compute the two components:

\displaystyle\big(f_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{kn})_{1}

\displaystyle=-2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}\big[(f_{0})_{1}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}g_{kn}+(f_{0})_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}g_{kn}\big]

\displaystyle=2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}(\partial_{2}g_{0}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}g_{kn}-\partial_{1}g_{0}\partial_{2}^{2}g_{kn})

\displaystyle=2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}(\partial_{2}g_{0}h_{2}-\partial_{1}g_{0}h_{1})=:\mathbf{I}_{3,1}

and

\displaystyle\big(f_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{kn})_{2}

\displaystyle=2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}\big[(f_{0})_{1}\partial^{2}_{1}g_{kn}+(f_{0})_{2}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}g_{kn}\big]

\displaystyle=2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}(\partial_{1}g_{0}h_{2}-\partial_{2}g_{0}h_{3})+\Big(\frac{17}{12}\Big)^{2}2^{-kn(\sigma-1)}h_{4}

\displaystyle=:\mathbf{I}_{3,2}+\mathbf{I}_{3,3},

where

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle:=\phi(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{2}),

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle:=\phi^{\prime}(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi^{\prime}(x_{2})-\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}\phi(x_{1})\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi^{\prime}(x_{2}),

\displaystyle h_{3}

\displaystyle:=\phi^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi(x_{2})-\frac{17}{6}2^{kn}\phi^{\prime}(x_{1})\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi(x_{2}),

\displaystyle h_{4}

\displaystyle:=\phi^{2}(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}x_{1}\Big)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{kn}x_{1}\Big)\phi(x_{2})\phi^{\prime}(x_{2}).

By simple computations, one has

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{3,1}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\mathbf{I}_{3,2}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-kn(\sigma+1)}\|\partial_{1}g_{0},\partial_{2}g_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-kn\sigma}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{3,3}\|_{L^{p}}=\Big(\frac{17}{12}\Big)^{2}2^{-kn(\sigma-1)}\|h_{4}\|_{L^{p}}\geq c2^{-kn(\sigma-1)},

where we have used the simple fact for sufficiently large
n

\|h_{4}\|_{L^{p}}=\Big\|\phi^{2}(x_{1})\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}x_{1}\Big)\cos\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x_{1}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\Big\|\phi(x_{2})\phi^{\prime}(x_{2})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\geq c_{0}.

This gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}_{3}\|_{L^{p}}\geq c_{0}2^{-kn(\sigma-1)}-C2^{-kn\sigma}.

(3.11)

Combining (
3.9
)–(
3.11
) yields

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{kn}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{p}}\geq c_{0}\cdot 2^{kn}2^{-kn\sigma}-C2^{kn}2^{-kn\sigma}2^{-k\sigma}-C2^{-kn\sigma}.

We take sufficiently large
k
and
n
and obtain the desired
(3)
. Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

3.3
Error Estimates

Proposition 3.1

Let
u_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
. Assume that
u\in L^{\infty}_{T}B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
be the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}\leq Ct^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}},

here and in what follows we denote

\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0}):=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{P}(u_{0}).

Proof.
For simplicity, we denote
u(t):=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
here and in what follows. Due to the fact
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}\hookrightarrow\rm Lip
, we know that there exists a positive time
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}})
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\leq C.

(3.12)

Moreover, for
\gamma\geq\sigma-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}}.

(3.13)

By the Mean Value Theorem, we obtain from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{p,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u\otimes u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}\cap\dot{B}^{1}_{p,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u\otimes u\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}^{2},

(3.14)

where we have used the fact that
B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}
is a Banach algebra.

By the Mean Value Theorem and (
1.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u-\mathbf{P}(u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)-\mathbf{P}(u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u\otimes u-u_{0}\otimes u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u\otimes u-u_{0}\otimes u_{0}\|_{{B}^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\|u_{0},u(\tau)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2}\|u_{0}\|^{3}_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}},

where we have used (
3.3
) in the last step.

Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Now we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Using Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{kn\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{kn}\big(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{{kn\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{kn}\big(t\mathbf{P}(u_{0})+\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{kn\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{kn}\big(\mathcal{P}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}-2^{{kn\sigma}}\big\|\Delta_{kn}\big(\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{kn\sigma}}\|\Delta_{kn}\big(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}\big)\|_{L^{p}}-t2^{{kn\sigma}}\|\Delta_{kn}\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle~~~~-2^{{2kn}}2^{{kn(\sigma-2)}}\big\|\Delta_{kn}\big(\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{kn\sigma}}\|\Delta_{kn}\big(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}\big)\|_{L^{p}}-Ct\|\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle~~~~-C2^{2{kn}}\|\mathbf{w}(t,u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma-2}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq ct2^{{kn}}-Ct-C2^{2{kn}}t^{2},

where we have used the fact:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}=\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla(\partial_{i}u^{j}_{0}\partial_{j}u_{0}^{i})\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{i}u^{j}_{0}\partial_{j}u_{0}^{i}\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}.

Thus, picking
t2^{kn}\approx\varepsilon
with small
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\infty}}\geq c\varepsilon-C\varepsilon^{2}\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

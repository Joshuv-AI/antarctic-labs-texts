Join operation and 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} -finite map-germs
Rodrigues Hernandes, M. E, Ruas, M. A. S

Abstract
In this work we define some map-germs, called elementary joins, for the purpose of producing new 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} -finite map-germs from them. In particular, we describe a general form of an 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} -finite monomial map from ( ℂ n , 0 ) (\mathbb{C}^{n},0) to ( ℂ p , 0 ) (\mathbb{C}^{p},0) for p ≥ 2 ​ n p\geq 2n of any corank in terms of elementary join maps. Our main tools are the delta invariant and some invariants of curves.



Keywords:
join operation, parameterized curves, analytic invariants.

Introduction

The class of map-germs from
(\mathbb{K}^{n},0)
to
(\mathbb{K}^{2n},0)
, where
\mathbb{K}
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, has been investigated by many authors using different approaches. For instance, Gaffney gives in
[
5
]
a formula for the number of double points at the origin of
a stable perturbation of a map-germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2n},0)

in terms of the zero dimensional Segre number of the double point
ideal of
f
and the number of Whitney umbrella points of the
composition of
f
with a generic projection from
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
to

\mathbb{C}^{2n-1}
. Klotz, Pop and Rieger classify
{\mathcal{A}}
-simple germs
(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)
(see
[
10
]
) to show that all of them have a
real deformation with the maximal number of real double-points. Symplectic invariants of curves and surfaces in this class of maps were also studied in
[
9
]
by Ishikawa and Janeczko. Parameterized singular surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
appear in
[
2
]
, where Birbrair, Mendes and Nuño-Ballesteros study relations between topological and metric properties, and also in
[
1
]
where Benedini, Ruas and Sinha study their second order geometry. In
[
15
]
, the authors classify
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined monomial surfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

Our main purpose is to present an “operation”, called join operation, among a finite number of map-germs in order to produce classes of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined (or
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite) map-germs from
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
to
(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq 2n
. In particular, we provide families of these maps of any corank. For two map-germs
F
and
G
, this operation consists in joining them by means of a germ
H
, denoted
(F*G)_{H}
, defined as
(F*G)_{H}(x,y):=(F(x),G(y),H(x,y))
which preserve in some sense the properties of
F
and
G
. Different type of operations to obtain new
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite germs from
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\mathbb{C}^{p}
from germs in lower dimensions were defined in
[
3
]
, see also
[
13
]
.

We define basic map-germs, called elementary joins, which are obtained by joining the identity and a parameterized curve or a finite number of parameterized curves. These maps are
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite (see Propositions
3.2
and
3.5
). In Theorem
4.2
and Corollaries
4.3
and
4.6
we obtain normal forms of some classes of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial map-germs which are completely characterized in terms of the elementary joins. The result in Corollary
4.3
generalizes for map-germs from
\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
the classification given in Theorem 2.4 in
[
15
]
for surfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. We also give the general form of an
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial map-germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
for
p\geq 2n
of any corank. More precisely, in Theorem
4.4
, we prove that if
f
is a corank
q
monomial map-germ, then
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to the join of the identity in
\mathbb{C}^{n-q}
with
q
parameterized curves. As a corollary we obtain infinitely many families of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite map-germs, not necessarily monomial. Two fundamental tools to obtain the previous results were the invariants: the semigroup associated to a parameterized curve and the delta invariant
\delta_{f}:=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})}
, defined in
[
15
]
.

The monomial normal forms of Theorem
4.4
can be seen as
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite reflection maps, as defined in
[
14
]
. In addition, their images
f(\mathbb{C}^{n})
are non normal toric varieties in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
with isolated singularity at the origin, as they are the loci of a binomial ideal (see
[
4
]
). Conditions for the equisingularity of families of isolated non-normal singularities parameterized by this class of map-germs were investigated in
[
7
]
.

In Section 5, we focus our study on some
{\mathcal{A}}
-invariants of map-germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
for
p\geq 2n
. The delta invariant is also useful for verifying whether a map is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined, since
\delta_{f}
is easier to be calculated than the
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}
-codimension of
f
(
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(f)
). If
p=2n
, we also have the number
d(f)
of double points appearing in a stable perturbation of
f
. The results of this section provide estimates of
\delta_{f}
and consequently for
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)
and some formulas for
d(f)
. In Subsection 5.1, we consider
f

{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to
(g_{k},h)
, where
g_{k}=\pi_{k}\circ f
in which
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
is a canonical projection and
h:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p-k},0)
. Such decomposition can be interesting in the study of analytic invariants, if one knows some properties of
g_{k}
and

. In Theorem
5.5
, we prove that
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(f)\leq{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g_{k})+(p-k)\delta_{f}
, with a formula when
g_{k}
is a stable map. If the projection
g_{n}
of
f
is a fold map, we prove in Proposition
5.6
that

and

{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(f)
.

2
Basic notations of singularities

Analytical invariants play a central role in this work. The delta invariant of a map-germ and some invariants of the theory of curves such as the semigroup of values and its set of gaps are fundamental for the characterization of some classes of map-germs.

Let
{\cal O}(n,p)
be the set of smooth map-germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to\mathbb{C}^{p}
, which is a free
{\cal O}_{n}
-module of rank
p
, where
{\cal O}_{n}
is
the local ring of function germs in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
at the origin, with
maximal ideal
{\cal M}_{n}
. Denoting by
{\mathcal{R}}=Diff(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and
{\mathcal{L}}=Diff(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
the groups of germs of diffeomorphisms at the origin, we consider the set
{\cal M}_{n}{\cal O}(n,p)=\{f\in{\cal O}(n,p);f(0)=0\}
under the action of the group
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{L}}
. Two map-germs
f,g\in{\cal M}_{n}{\cal O}(n,p)
are called
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent, denoted
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}g
, if there exist
(\rho,\psi)\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
g=\psi\circ f\circ\rho^{-1}
.

The set of vector fields along
f
is defined by
\theta(f)=\{\sigma:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to T\mathbb{C}^{p};\ \pi\circ\sigma=f\}
such that
\pi:T\mathbb{C}^{p}\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
is the natural projection. It is a free
{\cal O}_{n}
-module of rank
p
. The set of germs of vector fields in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
at the origin is a free
{\cal O}_{n}
-module of rank
n
, denoted by
\theta(n)
. Similarly,
\theta(p)
denotes the
{\cal O}_{p}
-module of rank
p
of vector fields in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
at the origin. We define
tf:\theta(n)\to\theta(f)
by
tf(\epsilon)(x)=df_{x}(\epsilon(x))
, where
df
is the differential of
f
and
wf:\theta{(p)}\to\theta(f)
such that
wf(\eta)=f^{*}(\eta):=\eta\circ f
. The
extended tangent space

T{{\mathcal{A}}}_{e}(f)
to the
{\mathcal{A}}
-orbit
{\mathcal{A}}(f)
at the point
f
is the complex vector space
T{{\mathcal{A}}}_{e}(f)=tf(\theta(n))+wf(\theta{(p)})
. The
extended tangent space

T{\mathcal{L}}_{e}(f)
to the
{\mathcal{L}}
-orbit of
f
is defined as
T{\mathcal{L}}_{e}(f)=wf(\theta{(p)})
.

If
{\mathcal{G}}
is one of the groups of Mather
{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{L}}
or
{\mathcal{R}}
, a smooth map-germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
is called
{\mathcal{G}}
-finitely determined (or
{\mathcal{G}}
-finite) if there exists
k<\infty
such that for all smooth map-germs
g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
j^{k}f(0)=j^{k}g(0)
we have that
f
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-equivalent to
g
, where
j^{k}f(0)
denotes the
k
-jet of
f
at the origin.

Finitely determined map-germs in
{\cal M}_{n}{\cal O}(n,p)
are particularly interesting for the class
p\geq 2n
. In this class of map-germs, the Mather-Gaffney geometric criterion of finite determinacy states that
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if there exists a neighborhood
U
at the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
0
is an isolated singularity of
f
and
f|_{U\setminus\{0\}}
is one-to-one. Moreover, Gaffney proves that
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if
f
is
{\cal L}
-finite (see
[
16
, Theorem 2.5]
). This result can be algebraically translated in terms of the
{\cal G}_{e}
-codimension of
f
, defined as
{\cal G}_{e}cod(f)=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta(f)}{T{\cal G}_{e}(f)}
, where
{\cal G}={\mathcal{A}}
or
{\cal L}
. More precisely, by the Infinitesimal criterion of finite determinacy (
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
),
f
is
{\mathcal{G}}
-finite if and only if
{\cal G}_{e}
-codimension of
f
is finite. Therefore, if
p\geq 2n
then
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)
is finite if and only if
{\cal L}_{e}cod(f)
is finite.

In
[
15
]
the authors consider the so-called delta invariant of
f
defined by
\delta_{f}:=dim_{\rm{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})}
. Notice that

\frac{\theta(f)}{wf(\theta{(p)})}\ \simeq\ \frac{\oplus_{i=1}^{p}{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}\left(\oplus_{i=1}^{p}{\cal O}_{p}\right)}\ \simeq\ \bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})},

then it follows that
{\cal L}_{e}cod(f)=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ \frac{\theta(f)}{wf(\theta{(p)})}=p\cdot\delta_{f}
. Thus, for
p\geq 2n
we obtain that
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if
\delta_{f}
is finite. In other words, the finite determinacy of
f
is described in terms of the delta invariant, which is easier to be calculated than
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)
. By a previous relation
\delta_{f}
is an
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariant. More strongly, it is an
{\mathcal{A}}
-invariant. In fact, Gaffney in his thesis (see
[
16
]
, Theorem 2.7(iv)) proves that if
f,g\in{\cal O}(n,p)
are
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite and
n<p
, then
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}g
if and only if the algebras
f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
and
g^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
are isomorphic.

In what follows we introduce some tools of the theory of curves. For more details see
[
8
]
.

Let
\phi_{k}:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)
be a parameterized curve in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
in which we assume (up to
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalence) be given in the form

\phi_{k}(y)=(y^{m_{1}},y^{m_{2}}+\sum_{i>m_{2}}a_{i2}y^{i},\ldots,y^{m_{k}}+\sum_{i>m_{k}}a_{ik}y^{i})

(1)

with
1<m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\ldots\leq m_{k}
. We suppose that the map
\phi_{k}
is primitive, that is, we cannot reparameterize it by a power of a new variable, or equivalently the greatest common divisor of the exponents of all components of
\phi_{k}
is equal to
1
. The integer
m_{1}
is called the
multiplicity
of the curve.

Numerical Semigroups and Apéry sequence

A subset
\mathbf{S}\neq\{0\}
is called a semigroup of
\mathbb{N}
if
\mathbf{S}
contains the element
0
and it is closed under addition. Any semigroup
\mathbf{S}
of
\mathbb{N}
is finitely generated, that is, there exist
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathbf{S}
such that
\mathbf{S}:=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\rangle=\{\lambda_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{r}v_{r};\ \lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N},i=1,\ldots,r\}.

If
r\geq 2
and
gcd(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=1
, then
\mathbf{S}
has a conductor, which means that there exists an element
c\in\mathbf{S}
such that
c-1\notin\mathbf{S}
and
c+\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbf{S}
. Consequently
\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbf{S}
is finite and its elements are called
gaps
of
\mathbf{S}
.

In order to describe the gaps of
\mathbf{S}
we introduce the notion of the
Apéry sequence
of
\mathbf{S}
with respect to an element
q\in\mathbf{S}\setminus\{0\}
, defined inductively by:
a_{0}=0
and for
j=1,\ldots,q-1
,

a_{j}=min\left(\mathbf{S}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{j-1}(a_{i}+q\mathbb{N})\right).

(2)

For more details about these concepts see
[
8
]
.

Semigroup and gaps associated to a curve

Let
({\cal C},0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)
be an analytic irreducible curve, parameterized by
\phi_{k}
as in (
1
), with
{\cal O}:=\mathbb{C}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\}/I
the local ring of
({\cal C},0)
, where
I
is the defining ideal of the curve. The value semigroup
\Gamma
associated to
\phi_{k}
(or to the curve) is the set

\Gamma=\{ord_{y}(\phi_{k}^{*}(h));\ h\in{\cal O}\setminus\{0\}\}.

If
\Gamma=\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\rangle
, since
\phi_{k}
is a primitive parameterization, then
gcd(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})=1
which implies that
\Gamma
has a conductor
c
. If we denote by
\overline{{\cal O}}
the integral closure of
{\cal O}
in its field of fractions, then
\overline{{\cal O}}\simeq\mathbb{C}\{y\}
. The conductor ideal of
{\cal O}
in
\overline{{\cal O}}
is defined by
({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})=\{h\in{\cal O};\ h\overline{{\cal O}}\subseteq{\cal O}\}
which is an ideal of
{\cal O}
satisfying that
({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})=\langle y^{c}\rangle
. Moreover,
({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})
is also an ideal of
\overline{{\cal O}}
. In fact, the conductor ideal is the largest ideal of
\overline{{\cal O}}
that is contained in
{\cal O}
.

We consider the Apéry set
B=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}-1}\}
of
\Gamma
with respect to the multiplicity
m_{1}
of
\phi_{k}
. Both the semigroup and the set of gaps
L
of
\Gamma
can be determined in terms of the Apéry set
B
. In fact, any element
s\in\Gamma
can be written as
s=a+\beta m_{1}
for some
a\in B
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and

L=\left\{a_{j}-\beta_{j}m_{1};\ j=1,\ldots,m_{1}-1,\ 1\leq\beta_{j}\leq\left[\frac{a_{j}}{m_{1}}\right]\right\},

(3)

where
[\cdot]
denotes the integral part. The delta invariant of
\phi_{k}
is given by

\delta_{\phi_{k}}=dim_{\rm{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{{\cal O}_{1}}{\phi_{k}^{*}({\cal O}_{k})}=dim_{\rm{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{\mathbb{C}\{y\}}{\phi_{k}^{*}({\cal O})}.

Thus, the previous dimension coincides with the number of elements in
\mathbb{N}
that do not belong to the semigroup

\Gamma
, that is, with the number of gaps of
\Gamma
. Thus,
\phi_{k}
is a primitive parameterized curve if and only if
\delta_{\phi_{k}}
is finite if and only if
\phi_{k}
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite. For a parameterized plane curve
\phi_{2}
, the conductor
c
of the semigroup is the Milnor number
\mu
of the defining equation of the curve. Moreover, in this case

c=\mu=2\cdot\delta_{\phi_{2}}.

Example 2.1
.

The semigroup associated to the parameterized curve
(y^{4},y^{5}+y^{6})
is the set
\Gamma=\langle 4,5\rangle=\{0,4,5,8,9,10,12,13,14,\ldots\}
, the conductor of
\Gamma
is
c=12
and its set of gaps is
L=\{1,2,3,6,7,11\}
.

3
Elementary join maps

In this section we define an “operation” between two map-germs
F
and
G
, called the join of
F
and
G
, in order to produce a new map-germ that carries, in a certain sense, the
F
and
G
structures.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p_{1}},0)
and
G:(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p_{2}},0)
with
p_{1}\geq n
and
p_{2}\geq m
. The join of
F
and
G
(with respect to
H
), denoted by
(F*G)_{H}
, is a map from
(\mathbb{C}^{n+m},0)
to
(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
such that

(F*G)_{H}(x,y)=(F(x),G(y),H(x,y)),

for some map-germ
H:(\mathbb{C}^{n+m},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p_{3}},0)
in which
p=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}
.

Naturally we can extend the above definition for a finite number of maps
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
with respect to
H
of the form:
(F_{1}*\ldots*F_{r})_{H}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}):=(F_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,F_{r}(x_{r}),H(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}))
.

The
{\mathcal{A}}
-class of
(F*G)_{H}
is well defined in the sense that if
F\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}\widetilde{F}
and
G\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}\widetilde{G}
, then
(F*G)_{H}\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(\widetilde{F}*\widetilde{G})_{\widetilde{H}}
, for some map-germ
\widetilde{H}
that is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to
H
.

Defining some special
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite map-germs that are given as a join of the identity and a parameterized curve or a join of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite parameterized curves, we produce new families of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite map-germs
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq 2n
.

In the next two results, we provide two families of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite maps of corank
1
and
n
, respectively, that allow us to characterize any
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial map of corank
q
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\phi_{k}
be the germ of a parameterized curve in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
as in (
1
) and
I
the identity in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
with
n,k\geq 2
. Then the join
(I*\phi_{k})_{H}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2n+k-2},0)
, with respect to
H(\underline{x},y)=(x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y)
, that is,

(I*\phi_{k})_{H}(\underline{x},y):=(\underline{x},\ \phi_{k}(y),\ x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y)

is an
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite map-germ of corank
1
, where
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Proof:
The proof that
f:=(I*\phi_{k})_{H}
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite follows a similar idea of
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
. We can verify that
f
is one-to-one, since
\phi_{k}
is a primitive parameterization and in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
the map
df_{x}
has maximal rank. Thus
f
is stable outside the origin, and therefore,
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite by Mather-Gaffney geometric criterion. However we give an algebraic proof in order to estimate the delta invariant of
f
.

Let
\Gamma
be the semigroup of
\phi_{k}
with conductor
c
and
L=\mathbb{N}\setminus\Gamma
the set of gaps of
\Gamma
. We will prove that
\delta_{f}
is finite since the following elements belong to
f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
, where
p:=2n+k-2
:

(a)

x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{r_{n-1}}y^{s}\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
, for all
r_{i}\geq 0
and
s=0
or
s\geq c\
;

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
:

(b)

x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{i}^{r_{i}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{r_{n-1}}y^{s}\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
, with
s\in\Gamma
,
s<c\
,
r_{i}\geq a\lambda_{i}
for some
a
depending on
s
and for all
r_{j}\geq 0
with
i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
;

(c)

x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{i}^{r_{i}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{r_{n-1}}y^{l}\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
, with
l\in L
,
r_{i}\geq(m_{1}-1)\lambda_{i}
and for all
r_{j}\geq 0
with
i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.

In fact, it is clear that
x_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{r_{n-1}}\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
for all
r_{i}\geq 0
. On the other hand, by definition of the conductor of a semigroup, any integer
s\geq c
satisfies
s\in\Gamma
(see Section
2
). We recall that the conductor ideal satisfies
({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})=\langle y^{c}\rangle
. Since
({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})
is an ideal of
{\cal O}
(and of
\overline{{\cal O}}
) we have for any
s\geq c
that
y^{s}=y^{s-c}y^{c}\in({\cal O}:\overline{{\cal O}})\subset{\cal O}
. Moreover
{\cal O}\simeq\mathbb{C}\{\phi_{k}(y)\}
, thus there exists
h\in\mathbb{C}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\}
such that
y^{s}=h(\phi_{k})\in\phi_{k}^{*}({\cal O})\subset f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
.

For item
(b)
, if
s\in\Gamma
with
s<c
, we only guarantee that there exists
h\in\mathbb{C}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\}
such that
h(\phi_{k})=y^{s}+h.o.t.\in\phi_{k}^{*}({\cal O})
. But as we remarked before (
3
), we can write
s=a+\beta m_{1}
, for some
a
in the Apéry set
B
of
\Gamma
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Thus for each

4
General form of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial maps of corank
q

Our purpose in this section is to give a general form for any map-germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq 2n
, satisfying the properties that
f
is monomial and
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined. Moreover we obtain normal forms of some classes of map-germs.

In the next lemma we prove that the class of parameterized curves appears naturally in the characterization of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial maps in
{\cal O}(n,p)
for
p\geq 2n
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq 2n
is a singular
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial map-germ, then there exists at least one
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite parameterized monomial curve
\phi_{k}:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)
with
2\leq k<p
such that
g
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to
f(\underline{x},y)=\left(f_{1}(\underline{x},y),\ldots,f_{p-(n+k-1)}(\underline{x},y),x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y,\phi_{k}(y)\right)
, where
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
.

Proof:
Denote by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
the variables of
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{p})
. By the hypothesis that
\delta_{g}
is finite, we have that
{\cal M}_{n}^{\beta}\subset g^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
x_{1}^{\gamma_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}\in g^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
for all
\gamma_{i}\geq\beta
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Since
g
is monomial, it follows that for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
there exists a component of
g
given by
x_{i}^{r_{i}}
for some
r_{i}\geq 1
. Moreover, being
g
singular, then at least one variable, namely
x_{n}
, is such that
r_{n}\geq 2
, or else
g
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to an immersion. Let us rename
x_{n}
and
r_{n}
respectively by
y
and
m_{1}
.

In this way, in order to obtain all powers of the variable
y
of the form
y^{b}\in g^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
for all
b\geq\beta
with
g
monomial, it is necessary to have other components of
g
given as powers of
y
so that there is an
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite parameterized curve in
y
. Then there exists
k
with
1<k<p
such that
g
can be given by
g(\underline{x},y)=(g_{1}(\underline{x},y),\ldots,g_{p-k}(\underline{x},y),y^{m_{1}},\ldots,y^{m_{k}})
with
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
and
g_{i}(\underline{x},0)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,p-k
and
gcd(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})=1
. If we suppose
1<m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}
, by the same argument that
g
is monomial and
\delta_{g}
is finite, the elements
x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y
appear as components of
g
, for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. Otherwise we could not obtain these elements in terms of other monomial components of
g
, since the multiplicity
m_{1}>1
. Then,
g
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to
f(\underline{x},y)=(f_{1}(\underline{x},y),\ldots,f_{p-(n+k-1)}(\underline{x},y),x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y,\phi_{k}(y))
.

In Theorem
4.2
and Corollaries
4.3
and
4.6
we characterize some classes of
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite monomial map-germs in terms of elementary joins.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\phi_{k}(y)=(y^{m_{1}},\ldots,y^{m_{k}})
be a parameterized monomial curve in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, with
1<m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}
,
gcd(m_{1},\ldots,m_{k})=1
and
n,k\geq 2
. Then, a singular monomial map-germ

satisfying

is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to

(I*\phi_{k})_{H}(\underline{x},y)=(\underline{x},y^{m_{1}},\ldots,y^{m_{k}},x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y),

where
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
,

is the identity in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Proof:
Notice that the sufficient condition follows from Proposition
3.2
.

By hypothesis

with
f_{i}(\underline{0},y)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,2n-2
. From the proof of Lemma

, there exist

of the form

with

for

. We claim that
r_{i}=1
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. In fact, if we assume by contradiction that some
r_{i}
is greater than
1
, say
r_{1}
, we have an
{\mathcal{A}}
-finite parameterized curve in the variable
x_{1}
as component of
f
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that such curve is given by
(x_{1}^{n_{1}},x_{1}^{n_{2}})
with
r_{1}:=n_{1}
,
1<n_{1}<n_{2}
and
gcd(n_{1},n_{2})=1
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
, the elements:
x_{1}^{n_{1}},x_{1}^{n_{2}},x_{2}^{s_{2}}x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{s_{n-1}}x_{1},y^{s_{1}}x_{1},y^{m_{1}},\ldots,y^{m_{k}},x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}y,\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\lambda_{n-1}}y
are components of

, for some

,

. But this is not possible, since the target dimension is

. Then
r_{1}=\ldots=r_{n-1}=1
and we conclude that

is a corank one map. Therefore,

is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to a join of the identity
I
in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
\phi_{k}
, with respect to the map
H:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n-1},0)
,

.

It is interesting to observe that if

is a singular monomial and

-finite map-germ, then

cannot have as component a parameterized curve
\phi_{j}:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{j},0)
for

As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
and Theorem

, we extend for maps in

the classification of parameterized monomial surfaces in

obtained in

{\mathcal{A}}
-finite if and only if

is
{\mathcal{A}}
-equivalent to

(i)

-th root of unity, with

d=gcd(r,s)
if and only if
\eta
is a
r
-th and
s
-th root of
unity, that is, the polynomials
z^{r}-y^{r}
and
z^{s}-y^{s}
have
d

common roots. In this way by hypothesis
(z-y)P_{r-1}(z,y)=(z-y)f_{1}
and

(z-y)P_{s-1}(z,y)=(z-y)f_{2},
where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
don’t have
common roots. Therefore
P_{r-1}
and
P_{s-1}
have no common
components which implies that the codimension of the ideal generated by them is finite.

Since

d(f)=\frac{1}{2}dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y,z}}{\langle\underline{x},\varphi_{2n-1},\varphi_{2n}\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\cal O}_{y,z}}{\langle P_{m_{1}-1}(z,y),P_{m_{2}-1}(z,y)\rangle}

in which
gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
, we have that the polynomials
P_{m_{i}-1},i=1,2
have no common
factor, then
d(f)
is finite and by Bézout’s Theorem we have the required formula, remembering that the delta invariant of
\phi_{2}
is
\delta_{\phi_{2}}=\frac{(m_{1}-1)(m_{2}-1)}{2}
.

For the item
2
, the formula for
d(f)
can be easily obtained by (
9
).

5.1
{\mathcal{A}}
-invariants under projections

For the study of invariants of a map
f
under some projection of it, we only assume
f\in{\cal M}_{n}{\cal O}(n,p)
in which
p\geq n
and
0
is an isolated singularity of
f
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
be fixed and
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
be a canonical projection, and
g_{k}=\pi_{k}\circ f
such that

f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(g_{k},h),
where

h:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p-k},0)
.
Notice that
\theta(f)\simeq\theta(g_{k})\oplus\theta(h)
. It is well known that

\omega\in T{\cal A}_{e}(f)
if and only if there exist

\epsilon\in\theta(n)
and
\eta\in\theta(p)
such that
\omega=df(\epsilon)+\eta(f)
.

We consider the subspaces

T_{k}=\left\{\omega_{k}\in\theta(g_{k});\ \omega_{k}=dg_{k}(\epsilon)+(\pi_{k}\circ\eta)(f),\ \epsilon\in\theta(n),\eta\in\theta(p)\right\}\ \mbox{and}

T=\{\omega_{h}\in\theta(h);\ (0,\omega_{h})\in T{\cal A}_{e}(f)\}

and their codimensions are respectively defined as

cod\ T_{k}=\dim_{\rm{\mathbb{C}}}\displaystyle\frac{\theta(g_{k})}{T_{k}}
and
cod\ T=\dim_{\rm{\mathbb{C}}}\displaystyle\frac{\theta(h)}{T}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq n
, such that
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(g_{k},h)
as above, for some
k=1,\dots,p-1
. If
cod\ T_{k}
and
cod\ T
are finite, then
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined and

{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=cod\ T_{k}+cod\ T.

Proof:
The proof is similar to the Proposition 2.1
in
[
11
]
for divergents diagrams. If
\omega\in\theta(f)
, we denote by
\overline{\omega}
the class of this element in the quotient

\theta(f)/T{\cal A}_{e}(f)
, similarly (with an abuse of notation) for
\theta(g_{k})
and
\theta(h)
. The
result follows considering the following exact sequence:

0\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{\theta(h)}{T}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \frac{\theta(f)}{T{\cal A}_{e}(f)}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \frac{\theta(g_{k})}{T_{k}}\ \longrightarrow\ 0,

where if
\overline{\omega_{h}}\in\theta(h)/T
then
i^{*}(\overline{\omega_{h}})=\overline{(0,\omega_{h})}
and given
\omega=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{p})\in\theta(f)
the projection

\pi^{*}(\overline{\omega})=\overline{(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k})}
.

In a natural way, we can extend the above result for multigerms.

For the germ
h:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p-k},0)
we define the map
\overline{w}h:\theta(p)\to\theta(h)
by
\overline{w}h(\eta)=(\eta_{k+1}\circ f,\dots,\eta_{p}\circ f)
, where
\eta:=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{p})\in\theta(p)
. Given
\omega_{h}\in T
we have
(0,\omega_{h})\in T{\cal A}_{e}(f)
, which implies that there exist

\epsilon\in\theta(n)
and
\eta\in\theta(p)
such that
dg_{k}(\epsilon)+(\pi_{k}\circ\eta)(f)=0
and
dh(\epsilon)+\overline{w}h(\eta)=\omega_{h}
. Let

\rho:\frac{\theta(h)}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}\longrightarrow\frac{\theta(h)}{T}

(10)

such that
\rho
of the class of
\omega_{h}\in\theta(h)
in
\theta(h)/\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))
is the class of this element in
\theta(h)/T
.

In the next result we present a relation among
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)
and invariants of some projection
g_{k}
of
f
, that can be interesting if you have some information about
g_{k}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Consider
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
p\geq 2n
and
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(g_{k},h)
. If
g_{k}
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-finitely determined and
\delta_{f}
is finite, then

(i)

{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)\leq{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g_{k})+(p-k)\delta_{f}.

(ii)

If
g_{k}
is stable, then
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=cod\ T=(p-k)\delta_{f}-dim_{\mathbb{C}}Ker(\rho)
.

Proof:
(i)
Notice that
\theta(h)/T
is isomorphic to a submodule of
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-k}{\cal O}_{n}/f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
and
therefore
cod\ T\leq(p-k)\delta_{f}<\infty
. On the other hand, the extended tangent space to
g_{k}
is

T{\cal A}_{e}(g_{k})=\{\omega_{k}\in\theta(g_{k});\omega_{k}=dg_{k}(\epsilon)+\eta(g_{k}),\epsilon\in\theta(n),\eta\in\theta{(k)}\},

which implies
cod\ T_{k}\leq{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g_{k})
. By Proposition
5.4
,
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)\leq{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g_{k})+(p-k)\delta_{f}.

(ii)
If
g_{k}
is
stable, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g_{k})=0
and consequently
cod\ T_{k}=0
. Thus
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=cod\ T
.
If
\rho
is as defined in (
10
), it follows by exact sequence

0\ \longrightarrow\ Ker(\rho)\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{\theta(h)}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \frac{\theta(h)}{T}\ \longrightarrow\ 0

that
cod\ T=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\theta(h)}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}-dim_{\mathbb{C}}Ker(\rho)
. But

\displaystyle\frac{\theta(h)}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}

is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-k}\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})}.

It is well known that the invariants
\delta_{f}
and
d(f)
coincide for parameterized plane curves, but this does not hold in general. In
[
15
, Proposition 2.9]
we proved that parameterized surfaces
f(x,y)=(x,x^{\lambda}y,y^{m_{1}},y^{m_{2}})
satisfy
d(f)\leq\delta_{f}
with equality if and only if
m_{1}=2
.

We prove in the next proposition that these two invariants coincide for any
f\in{\cal O}(n,2n)
which has a stable projection equivalent to a fold. Moreover we obtain an estimate for
Ker(\rho)
in Theorem
5.5
and consequently for the
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}
-codimension. For what follows, we denote the ring
{\cal O}_{n}
by
{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
be an

{\mathcal{A}}
-finite map-germ for
p\geq 2n
. We suppose
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(g_{n},h)
such that

g_{n}(\underline{x},y)=(\underline{x},y^{2})
. Thus

1.

if
p=2n
, then
d(f)=\delta_{f}
;

2.

if
p\geq 2n
, then
(p-2n+1)\delta_{f}\leq{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)\leq(p-n)\delta_{f}-1
.

Proof:
We can consider change of coordinates such that
f\underset{{\mathcal{A}}}{\sim}(\underline{x},y^{2},yu_{n+1}(\underline{x},y^{2}),\ldots,yu_{p}(\underline{x},y^{2}))

for some
u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{p}\in\mathbb{C}\{\underline{x},y\}
, as in

[
12
]
.

If
p=2n
, then by (
8
) we have that

\varphi_{n}(\underline{x},y,z)=z+y
and

\varphi_{j}(\underline{x},y,z)=\frac{zu_{j}(\underline{x},z^{2})-yu_{j}(\underline{x},y^{2})}{z-y}

for all
j=n+1,\dots,2n
. Identifying
z=-y
we have

\varphi_{j}(\underline{x},y,z)=u_{j}(\underline{x},y^{2})
. However

{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}\simeq{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}
thus

\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}=\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle\ y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}.

Hence,
d(f)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}dim_{\mathbb{C}}\displaystyle\frac{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}}{\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}}=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\displaystyle\frac{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}{\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}.

On the other hand, we can identify
f^{*}({\cal O}_{2n})
with

{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}
which implies

\delta_{f}=\displaystyle dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle\ y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}{\langle u_{n+1},\ldots,u_{2n}\rangle{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}}=d(f).

In order to prove the item
2
, notice that it follows from Theorem
5.5
that
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=cod\ T
, since

g_{n}
is a stable map. But

\omega=(\omega_{n+1},\dots,\omega_{p})\in T
if and only if

(0,\omega)\in T{\cal A}_{e}(f)
, that is, there exist
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n})\in\theta(n)
and
\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{p})\in\theta(p)
such that
\epsilon_{i}=-\eta_{i}(f)
for

i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
2y\epsilon_{n}=-\eta_{n}(f)
which implies that for each
j
with
n+1\leq j\leq p
,

\omega_{j}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(y\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\eta_{i}(f)\right)+\left(u_{j}(\underline{x},y^{2})+2y^{2}\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial y}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\right)\epsilon_{n}(\underline{x},y)+\eta_{j}(f)

satisfies
\gamma_{j}:=u_{j}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\epsilon_{n}({\underline{x}},y)-y\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial y}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\eta_{n}(f)+\eta_{j}(f)\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
. In fact,

y\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial y}({\underline{x}},y^{2})
is even in

y
and by isomorphism
{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y}\simeq{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}\oplus y{\cal O}_{\underline{x},y^{2}}
we can write

\epsilon_{n}(\underline{x},y)=\epsilon_{n_{1}}(\underline{x},y^{2})+y\epsilon_{n_{2}}(\underline{x},y^{2})
. Thus

u_{j}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\epsilon_{n}({\underline{x}},y)=u_{j}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\epsilon_{n_{1}}(\underline{x},y^{2})+yu_{j}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\epsilon_{n_{2}}(\underline{x},y^{2})\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})

because

u_{j},\epsilon_{n_{1}},\epsilon_{n_{2}}
are even in
y
and

yu_{j}({\underline{x}},y^{2})\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
. It follows from (
10
) and the proof of Theorem
5.5
that
\omega\in\frac{T}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}
if and only if
\omega\in M
, where

M:=\frac{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}\}+\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}

is a

f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
-module finitely generated by

v_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}y\frac{\partial u_{n+1}}{\partial x_{i}}\\
\vdots\\
y\frac{\partial u_{p}}{\partial x_{i}}\end{array}\right),

with
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. By abuse of notation we denote
v_{i}
its class in

\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))
.

Notice that
v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}
are not all identically zero in

M
. In fact, since
p\geq 2n
and
f
is
{\mathcal{A}}
(and therefore
{\cal L}
) finitely determined, for each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
there exists

k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}y\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
for
all
\gamma_{i}\geq k_{i}
. This implies that writing

u_{n+i}(\underline{x},y^{2})=\phi_{n+i}(\underline{x})+y^{2}R_{n+i}(\underline{x},y)
there are at least
n-1
maps
\phi_{j}
’s not identically
zero, that we can suppose they are
\phi_{n+1},\ldots,\phi_{2n-1}
. For each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
there exists
l_{i}
such that

\phi_{l_{i}}(\underline{x})=a_{l_{i}}x_{i}^{l_{i}}+\ldots
with

ord_{x_{i}}\phi_{l_{i}}(\underline{x})\leq ord_{x_{i}}\phi_{j}(\underline{x})
for all
j
with
l_{i},j\in\{n+1,\ldots,2n-1\}
. Thus

ord_{x_{i}}\frac{y\partial u_{l_{i}}(x,y^{2})}{\partial x_{i}}<l_{i}

and, therefore, the

l_{i}
-coordinate of
v_{i}
not belongs to
f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
, by minimality
of the order of
\phi_{l_{i}}
with respect to
x_{i}
. Consequently

v_{i}\not\in\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))
and we conclude that

dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ Ker(\rho)\geq 1
.

If
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
are non zero in
M
, this means that each
non-zero coordinate of
v_{i}
is an element of

\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})}\simeq\mathbb{C}\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\delta_{f}}\}
. Since
f
is
{\cal L}
-finite, for each
i=1,\ldots,n-1
there exist (finite)

m_{i1},\ldots,m_{ir_{i}}\in f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
such that
m_{ij}v_{i}\not\in\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))
, with
1\leq j\leq r_{i}
.
But, in each non zero component of
v_{i},m_{i1}v_{i},\ldots,m_{ir_{i}}v_{i}
we have at most
\delta_{f}
linearly independent
elements in
\frac{{\cal O}_{n}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})}
. According to these
arguments we have

dim_{\mathbb{C}}M\leq(n-1)\delta_{f}
. Therefore
1\leq dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ Ker(\rho)=dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ \frac{T}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(p))}\leq(n-1)\delta_{f}
. The
result follows from Theorem
5.5
.

Corollary 5.7
.

If
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
(as defined in the above proposition) are

f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
-linearly independent, then
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)\leq(p-n)\delta_{f}-n+1
.

Proof:
If
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\in M
are
f^{*}({\cal O}_{p})
-linearly
independent we have
dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ Ker(\rho)\geq n-1
.

Example 5.8
.

In this example we present
{\mathcal{A}}
-simple map-germs from the
classification obtained in
[
10
]
. Notice that the estimates for the
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}
-codimension in Proposition
5.6
are sharp. In fact, the germs
f(x,y)=(x,xy,y^{2},y^{2k+1})
and
g(x,y)=(x,y^{2},y^{3},x^{k}y)
satisfy
\delta_{f}=k=\delta_{g}
. Thus
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=k=\delta_{f}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{e}cod(g)=2k-1=2\delta_{g}-1
.

Now, the map
f(x_{1},x_{2},y)=(x_{1},x_{2},y^{2},x_{1}x_{2}y,(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})y,y^{3})
illustrates the computation of
Ker(\rho)
. It is possible to prove that
\frac{{\cal O}_{3}}{f^{*}({\cal O}_{6})}\simeq\mathbb{C}\{y,x_{1}y,x_{2}y,x_{1}^{2}y\}
, thus

\delta_{f}=4
. In this case,

Ker(\rho)\simeq\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{C}\{v_{1},v_{2},x_{1}v_{1},x_{1}v_{2}\}+\overline{\omega}h(\theta(6))}{\overline{\omega}h(\theta(6))}

is a
f^{*}({\cal O}_{6})
-module
generated by
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
, where

v_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}y\\
-2x_{2}y\\
0\end{array}\right)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ v_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{2}y\\
2x_{1}y\\
0\end{array}\right).

By Theorem
5.5
, we conclude that
{\cal A}_{e}cod(f)=3\delta_{f}-dim_{\mathbb{C}}Ker(\rho)=12-4=8<3\delta_{f}-n+1.

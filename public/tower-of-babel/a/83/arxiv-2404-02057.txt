Uniformity in nonreduced rings via Noetherian operators
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Jack Jeffries

Abstract
. We prove a differential version of the Artin-Rees lemma with the use of Noetherian differential operators. As a consequence, we obtain several uniformity results for nonreduced rings.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13N05, 13N99, 13A15

1.
Introduction

A curious historical tradition is that analytic and algebraic techniques have often competed to prove equivalent results.
This paper lies at the crossroads of the following two topics where both algebraic and analytic techniques have played essential roles:

(a)

Primary ideals and Noetherian operators:

It is known that one can use Macaulay’s inverse systems to describe zero-dimensional primary ideals in terms of differential operators (see
[
13
,
14
,
21
]
).
However, analysts first described arbitrary primary ideals via differential operators in polynomial rings over the complex numbers.
Indeed, a main step in the Fundamental Principle of Ehrenpreis and Palamodov
[
9
,
23
]
is the characterization of primary ideals by differential operators.
Following Palamodov’s notation, a set of differential operators describing a primary ideal is called a set of
Noetherian (differential) operators
.
Subsequent important algebraic developments were made by Brumfiel
[
3
]
and Oberst
[
22
]
.
More recently, this theme has seen a rebirth in the areas of commutative algebra and computational algebra (see
[
8
,
7
,
6
,
4
,
11
,
5
]
).

(b)

Integral closure and the Briançon-Skoda theorem:
The Briançon-Skoda theorem
[
2
]
, proved initially in the complex analytic case, states that given an ideal
I
, there is a constant
c
such that
\overline{I^{n+c}}\subset I^{n}
for any
n\geq 0
, where
\overline{I^{n+c}}
denotes the integral closure of
I^{n+c}
.
The first algebraic proof of this result was given by Lipman and Sathaye for regular rings
[
19
]
(also, see
[
16
,
24
]
).
Then, Huneke went even further in his uniform bounds paper
[
18
]
, where he showed that for large classes of reduced rings one could choose a uniform constant
c
that is valid for all ideals
I
. The reducedness hypothesis is necessary since the nilradical is contained in the integral closure of any ideal.

Our point of departure is a paper of Sznajdman
[
25
]
that combines
(a)
and
(b)
in a spectacular way: in a complex analytic setting, it is shown that one can use Noetherian operators to provide a version of the Briançon-Skoda theorm for nonreduced analytic spaces.
Following historical tradition, we extend Sznajdman’s result to nonrestrictive algebraic settings.

Our main result is a differential version of the Artin-Rees lemma with Noetherian operators.
As a consequence, we obtain a metatheorem stating that
“uniform containment type results hold for many classes of rings when they hold for the corresponding reduced ring.”

Let
R
be an algebra essentially of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
and assume that
R
has no embedded associated primes.
Let
{R_{\rm red}}=R/\sqrt{0}
be the reduced ring corresponding to
R
.
For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
such that
\text{Ass}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\text{Ass}(R)
, one can find a finite set of differential operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
such that

\mathfrak{a}\;=\;\{f\in R\mid\delta_{i}(f)=0\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m\};

here we say that
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}
is a
set of Noetherian operators describing the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
(see
Corollary 2.7
); when
\mathfrak{a}=(0)
, we call such a set a
set of Noetherian operators for
R
.
Notice that a set of Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
for
R
yields a
\mathbb{k}
-linear inclusion
R\hookrightarrow\left({R_{\rm red}}\right)^{m}
.
Given an ideal
I\subset{R_{\rm red}}
and a finite set of differential operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
, we set the following notation

I:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\{f\in R\mid\delta_{i}(f)\in I\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m\}\;=\;\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{i}^{-1}(I).

We now present our main result.

Theorem A

(Differential Artin-Rees)
.

Let
R
be an algebra essentially of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
and assume that
R
has no embedded associated primes.
Let
{R_{\rm red}}=R/\sqrt{0}
.
Then, there exists a set of Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
for
R
and an integer
c\geq 0
such that

I^{n+c}:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\;\subseteq\;J^{n}

for any ideal
J
in
R
with image
I
in
{R_{\rm red}}
and any
n\geq 0
.

Complementary to
Theorem A
, we also have a quite basic reverse inclusion (see
Remark 2.10
).
The main corollary is the algebraic analogue of the result of Sznajdman
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Corollary B

(Briançon-Skoda theorem for nonreduced rings)
.

Under the assumptions and notation of
Theorem A
, there exists a set of Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
for
R
and an integer
c\geq 0
such that

\overline{I^{n+c}}:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\;\subseteq\;J^{n}

for any ideal
J
in
R
with image
I
in
{R_{\rm red}}
and any
n\geq 0
.

Our second application is to establish an analogue of a celebrated containment statement of symbolic powers for not necessarily regular rings in the case
{R_{\rm red}}
is regular (see
[
17
,
10
,
20
]
).

Corollary C

(Containment of symbolic powers for nonregular rings)
.

Keep the assumptions and notation of
Theorem A
.
Moreover, assume that
{R_{\rm red}}
is a regular ring of finite dimension
d
.
Then, there exists a set of Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
for
R
and an integer
c\geq 0
such that

I^{(nd+c)}:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\;\subseteq\;J^{n}

for any ideal
J
in
R
with image
I
in
{R_{\rm red}}
and any
n\geq 0
.

2.
Proofs of our results

In this section, we provide the proofs for all our results.
We start with the following basic lemma showing the existence of certain special filtrations we need.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring.
Then, there is a filtration of
R
by ideals

0=\mathfrak{a}_{0}\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{1}\,\subsetneqq\,\cdots\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{k}=R

satisfying the following:

(i)

\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}
is isomorphic to a submodule of
R/\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.

(ii)

\text{Ass}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1})\subseteq\text{Ass}(R)
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

(iii)

\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}=\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.
By
[
12
, Lemma 3.5.3]
, there exists a filtration
{0=\mathfrak{a}_{0}\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{1}\,\subsetneqq\,\cdots\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}=\mathfrak{p}_{0}}
such that each
\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}
is isomorphic to an ideal of
R/\mathfrak{p}
for some associated prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
considered as a submodule of
R
; setting
\mathfrak{a}_{k}=R
then yields a filtration evidently satisfying conditions
(i)
and
(iii)
.
Then condition
(ii)
holds because
R/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}
has a filtration with successive quotients isomorphic to submodules of
R/\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.
∎

We now recall the notion of
differential operators
.
A general reference in this topic is
[
15
, §16]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R
be a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
M,N
be
R
-modules.
The
n
-th order
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators
{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(M,N)\subseteq{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{k}}(M,N)
from
M
to
N
are defined inductively by:

(i)

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{0}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\text{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)
.

(ii)

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big\{\delta\in{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{k}}(M,N)\mid[\delta,f]\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n-1}(M,N)\text{ for all }f\in R\big\}
.

Here we use the bracket notation
[\delta,f](m)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\delta(fm)-f\delta(m)
for all
\delta\in{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{k}}(M,N)
,
f\in R
, and
m\in M
.
The
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators from
M
to
N
are given by

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(M,N).

For completeness, we include a lemma that describes the behavior of differential operators under localization.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R
be an algebra essentially of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
,
W\subseteq R
be a multiplicatively closed subset, and
M,N
be
R
-modules, with
M
finitely generated.
Then

(i)

W^{-1}R\otimes_{R}{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(M,N)\cong{\rm Diff}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}(W^{-1}M,W^{-1}N)
, and

(ii)

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(M,W^{-1}N)\cong{\rm Diff}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}(W^{-1}M,W^{-1}N)
.

Proof.

Let
P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
denote the module of principal parts of order
n
of
R
over
\mathbb{k}
, cf.,
[
15
, §16.3]
.
By
[
15
, Theorem 16.4.14]
(cf.,
[
1
, Proposition 2.16]
), for each
n
, we have
W^{-1}P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}}\cong P^{n}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}
, so

W^{-1}(M\otimes_{R}P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}})\cong W^{-1}M\otimes_{W^{-1}R}P^{n}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}.

Then, by the universal property of localization, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(M,W^{-1}N)

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}},W^{-1}N)

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{W^{-1}R}(W^{-1}M\otimes_{W^{-1}R}P^{n}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}},W^{-1}N)\cong{\rm Diff}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(W^{-1}M,W^{-1}N),

and since
P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
is finitely generated, we also have

\displaystyle W^{-1}R\otimes_{R}

\displaystyle{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(M,N)\cong W^{-1}{\text{Hom}}_{R}(M\otimes_{R}P^{n}_{R/\mathbb{k}},N)

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{W^{-1}R}(W^{-1}M\otimes_{W^{-1}R}P^{n}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}},W^{-1}N)\cong{\rm Diff}_{W^{-1}R/\mathbb{k}}^{n}(W^{-1}M,W^{-1}N).\qed

We also use the following lemma, which follows along similar lines to
[
3
, Proposition 1.3]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra,
M
,
N
be
R
-modules,
J
be an ideal of
R
, and
\delta
be a differential operator from
M
to

of order at most

.
Then for any integer

\delta(J^{n+t}M)\subseteq J^{t}N
.

The following theorem allows us to describe primary ideals with differential operators.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
R
be an algebra essentially of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
,
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(R)
a prime ideal and
Q
be a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary ideal.
Then, there exists a finite set of differential operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,R/\mathfrak{p})
such that

Q\;=\;\{f\in R\mid\delta_{i}(f)=0\;\text{ for all \;$0\leq i\leq m$}\}.

A set of operators satisfying the conclusion of
Theorem 2.5
is called a set of
Noetherian operators describing
Q
.
We shall use the following setup for the rest of this section.

Setup 2.6
.

Let
R
be an algebra essentially of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
and assume that
R
has no embedded associated primes.
Let
{R_{\rm red}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R/\sqrt{0}
be the reduced ring corresponding to
R
.
Let
\pi:R\rightarrow{R_{\rm red}}
be the natural projection.

A standard application of
Theorem 2.5
yields the possibility of describing certain (not necessarily primary) ideals in terms of differential operators. For arbitrary ideals, we can use the notion of
differential primary decomposition
from
[
7
]
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Assume
Setup 2.6
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
be an ideal such that
\text{Ass}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\text{Ass}(R)
.
There exists a set of Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
describing the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
; that is,

\mathfrak{a}=\{f\in R\mid\delta_{i}(f)=0\;\text{ for all }\;1\leq i\leq m\}.

Proof.

Consider the multiplicatively closed set
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)}\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W^{-1}R
.
By
Lemma 2.3
, we have the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle W^{-1}R\otimes_{R}{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})

\displaystyle\;\cong\;{\rm Diff}_{S/\mathbb{k}}(S,{\text{Quot}}({R_{\rm red}}))

\displaystyle\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)}{\rm Diff}_{S/\mathbb{k}}(R,R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)}{\rm Diff}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{k}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Therefore, for any associated prime
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)
and any differential operator
\delta^{\prime}\in{\rm Diff}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{k}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})
, we can write
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{w}\cdot\delta
for some
\delta\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
and
w\in W
.

Write an irredundant primary decomposition
\mathfrak{a}=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{\ell}
where
Q_{i}
is a
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary ideal for some
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.
From
Theorem 2.5
, we obtain a set of Noetherian operators

\delta_{i,1}^{\prime},\ldots,\delta_{i,m_{i}}^{\prime}\;\in\;{\rm Diff}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}/\mathbb{k}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}},R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}})

describing the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
-primary ideal
Q_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
.
We can write
\delta_{i,j}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{w_{i,j}}\cdot\delta_{i,j}
with
\delta_{i,j}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
and
w_{i,j}\in W
.
Since
w_{i,j}
is a nonzerodivisor on
{R_{\rm red}}
, we have

f\in Q_{i}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\delta^{\prime}_{i,j}(f)=0\ \text{for all $j$}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\delta_{i,j}(f)=w_{i,j}\delta^{\prime}_{i,j}(r)=0\ \text{for all $j$};

that is,
\delta_{i,1},\ldots,\delta_{i,m_{i}}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
is a set of Noetherian operators describing the ideal
Q_{i}
.
Finally, the union of all the
\delta_{i,j}
’s gives a set of Noetherian operators describing the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
.
∎

The following technical proposition is our fundamental tool for obtaining the differential operators used in
Theorem A
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume
Setup 2.6
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subsetneqq\mathfrak{b}\subset R
be two ideal ideals such that
\text{Ass}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\text{Ass}(R)
.
Let
\psi:\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\hookrightarrow R/\mathfrak{p}
be an injective
R
-linear map for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(R)
.
Then, there is a differential operator
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
satisfying the following:

(i)

\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{a})=0
.

(ii)

Denoting by
\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,R/\mathfrak{p})
the induced differential operator, we obtain a nonzero element
{d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}\in{\text{Quot}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
such that

{\overline{\delta}}_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(f)\;=\;\psi\left(\overline{f}\right)\cdot{d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad f\in\mathfrak{b}.

Proof.

From
Corollary 2.7
, we can choose Noetherian operators
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
that describe the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
. In particular, for any
f\in R
, we have that
f\in\mathfrak{a}
if and only if
\delta_{i}(f)=0
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Since
\mathfrak{b}\supsetneqq\mathfrak{a}
, there exists a differential operator
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
of minimal order such that
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{b})\neq 0
and
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{a})=0
.
For all
f\in R
and
g\in\mathfrak{b}
, we have the equality

\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(fg)\;=\;\pi(f)\cdot\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(g)+\left[\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}},f\right](g).

As
[\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}},f]
has strictly lower order than
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}
and
[\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}},f](\mathfrak{a})=0
, the minimality of
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}
forces the vanishing
[\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}},f](g)=0
.
Thus we get
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(fg)=\pi(f)\cdot\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(g)
for all
f\in R
and
g\in\mathfrak{b}
.
This means that the restriction

\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{b}\rightarrow{R_{\rm red}}\quad\text{ is a nonzero $R$-linear map with }\quad\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{a})=0.

Since
\mathfrak{p}\cdot\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
, we can make the identification
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow(0:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\mathfrak{p})
.
Let
\tau:{R_{\rm red}}\rightarrow R/\mathfrak{p}
be the quotient map.
From the fact that
(0:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\mathfrak{p})\cap\mathfrak{p}{R_{\rm red}}=0
, we obtain a nonzero
R
-linear map

\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}=\tau\circ\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow R/\mathfrak{p}.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{Im}(\psi)\subset R/\mathfrak{p}
and notice that we have the isomorphisms

{\text{Hom}}_{R/\mathfrak{a}}(\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{p})\;\cong\;{\text{Hom}}_{R/\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{c},R/\mathfrak{p})\;\cong\;\left(R/\mathfrak{p}:_{{\text{Quot}}(R/\mathfrak{p})}\mathfrak{c}\right).

Therefore, there exists a nonzero element
{d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}\in{\text{Quot}}(R/\mathfrak{p})
such that
\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(f)=\psi\left(\overline{f}\right)\cdot{d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}
for all
f\in\mathfrak{b}
.
This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Another important part of our proof is the lemma below, where we utilize Huneke’s uniform Artin-Rees lemma from
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume the notation and assumptions of
Proposition 2.8
.
There is an integer
c_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}
only depending on
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
such that, for any ideal
J
in
R
with image
I
in
{R_{\rm red}}
and any
n\geq 0
, if
f\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(f)\in I^{n+c_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}
, then
f\in\mathfrak{b}\cap J^{n}+\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Let
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(J+\mathfrak{p})/\mathfrak{p}\subset R/\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
f\in\mathfrak{b}
with
\delta_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(f)\in I^{n}
.
This implies
\overline{\delta}_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}(f)=\psi(\overline{f}){d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}\in K^{n}
.
Write
{d_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}}=s/t
with
s,t\in R/\mathfrak{p}
and notice that
\psi(\overline{f})s\in K^{n}
. From the uniform Artin-Rees lemma
[
18
, Theorem 4.12]
, we obtain a constant
c_{1}
such that

\psi(\overline{f})s\;\in\;K^{n}\cap(s)\;\subset\;sK^{n-c_{1}}.

Since
s
is a nonzero element in the domain
R/\mathfrak{p}
, it follows that
\psi(\overline{f})\in K^{n-c_{1}}
.
Consider the ideal
\mathfrak{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{Im}(\psi)\subset R/\mathfrak{p}
.
Again, by the uniform Artin-Rees lemma, there is a constant
c_{2}
such that

\psi(\overline{f})\;\in\;K^{n-c_{1}}\cap\mathfrak{c}\;\subset\;\mathfrak{c}K^{n-c_{1}-c_{2}}.

Via
\psi:\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\mathfrak{c}\subset R/\mathfrak{p}
, we obtain the induced isomorphism
J^{n-c_{1}-c_{2}}\cdot\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}K^{n-c_{1}-c_{2}}\cdot\mathfrak{c}
.
It then follows
f\in\mathfrak{b}\cap J^{n-c_{1}-c_{2}}+\mathfrak{a}
.
So the claim of the lemma holds with
c_{\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c_{1}+c_{2}
.
∎

After the previous developments, we are ready for the proof of our main result.

Proof of
Theorem A
.

Choose a filtration
0=\mathfrak{a}_{0}\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{1}\,\subsetneqq\,\cdots\,\subsetneqq\,\mathfrak{a}_{k}=R
from
Lemma 2.1
.
Let

\delta_{i}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\delta_{\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}}\;\in\;{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})

be the differential operator obtained by applying
Proposition 2.8
to the ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}\subsetneqq\mathfrak{a}_{i}
.
Let
{c_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c_{\mathfrak{a}_{i}/\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}}}
be the constant given by
Lemma 2.9
.
Notice that we may assume
\delta_{k}=\tau:R\rightarrow R/\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}
is the natural projection.
Let
e_{1}
be the maximum of the orders of the operators
\delta_{i}
and
e_{2}
be the maximum of the constants
c_{i}
.
Set
e=e_{1}+e_{2}
and
c=ke
.

Let
f\in R
be an element such that
\delta_{i}(f)\in I^{n+c}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
Since
\tau(f)=\delta_{k}(f)\in I^{n+c}
, we can write

f\;=\;f_{k-1}+f_{k}

where
f_{k}\in J^{n+c}=\mathfrak{a}_{k}\cap J^{n+ke}
and
f_{k-1}\in\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}
.
Suppose by induction that we have obtained the equation

f\;=\;f_{i}+f_{i+1}+\cdots+f_{k}

with
f_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}
and
f_{j}\in\mathfrak{a}_{j}\cap J^{n+je}
for all
j>i
.
By applying the operator
\delta_{i}
, we get

\delta_{i}(f_{i})\;=\;\delta_{i}(f)-\delta_{i}(f_{i+1})-\cdots-\delta_{i}(f_{k}),

and since by
Lemma 2.4
,
\delta_{i}(f)\in I^{n+ke}
and
\delta_{i}(f_{j})\in I^{n+je-e_{1}}
for all
j>i
, it follows that
\delta_{i}(f_{i})\in I^{n+(i+1)e-e_{1}}
.
Therefore
Lemma 2.9
yields
f_{i}=f_{i-1}+f_{i}^{\prime}
with
f_{i-1}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i-1}
and
f_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cap J^{n+ie}
.
Consequently, by proceeding inductively we can write

f\;=\;f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots+f_{k}\quad\text{ with }\quad f_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cap J^{n+ie}.

In particular, we get the desired inclusion
f\in J^{n}
.
If
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k}
do not give a set of Noetherian operators describing the zero ideal
(0)
, we can further adjoin differential operators from
Corollary 2.7
until we get a set of Noetherian operators.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

In the setting of
Theorem A
, let
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m}\in{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{k}}(R,{R_{\rm red}})
be any set of Noetherian operators for
R
.
If
e
is the maximum of the orders of the operators
\delta_{i}
, then by
Lemma 2.4
we have

J^{n+e}\;\subseteq\;I^{n}:_{{R_{\rm red}}}\{\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{m}\}.

Finally, we have the proofs of our main corollaries.

Proof of
Corollary B
.

Fix a constant
c_{1}
satisfying the conclusion of
Theorem A
.
By the uniform Briançon-Skoda theorem
[
18
, Theorem 4.13]
applied to
{R_{\rm red}}
, there is a constant
c_{2}
such that
\overline{I^{n+c_{2}}}\subseteq I^{n}
independent of
I
. Take
{c=c_{1}+c_{2}}
. Then we have

\overline{I^{n+c}}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ =\ \overline{I^{n+c_{1}+c_{2}}}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ \subseteq\ {I^{n+c_{1}}}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ \subseteq\ J^{n}.\qed

Proof of
Corollary C
.

Again, fix a constant
c_{1}
satisfying the conclusion of
Theorem A
.
By
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
applied to
{R_{\rm red}}
, we get the containment
I^{(nd)}\subset I^{n}
for any ideal
I\subset R
.
Take
{c=c_{1}d}
.
Then we have

I^{(nd+c)}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ =\ I^{((n+c_{1})d)}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ \subseteq\ {I^{n+c_{1}}}:_{R_{\rm red}}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}\}\ \subseteq\ J^{n}.\qed

Halpern-Type Accelerated and Splitting Algorithms For Monotone Inclusions
Quoc Tran-Dinh ∗, Yang Luo

Abstract
In this paper, we develop a new type of accelerated algorithms to solve different classes of maximally monotone equations as well as inclusions. Instead of using Nesterov’s accelerating approach, our methods rely on a so-called Halpern fixed-point iteration in halpern1967fixed , and has recently exploited by a number of researchers, including diakonikolas2020halpern ; yoon2021accelerated . Firstly, we derive a new variant of the extra-anchored gradient scheme in yoon2021accelerated based on Popov’s past extra-gradient method popov1980modification to solve a maximally monotone equation G ⁡ ( x ) = 0 G(x)=0 . We show that our method achieves the same 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right) convergence rate (up to a constant factor) as in the extra-anchored gradient algorithm on the operator norm ‖ G ⁡ ( x k ) ‖ \|G(x_{k})\| , but requires only one evaluation of G G at each iteration, where k k is the iteration counter. Next, we develop two splitting algorithms to approximate a zero point of the sum of two maximally monotone operators. The first algorithm relies on the extra-gradient method combining with a splitting technique, while the second one is its Popov’s variant which can reduce the per-iteration complexity. Both algorithms appear to be new and can be viewed as accelerated variants of the Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting method. They both achieve 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right) rates on the norm ‖ G γ ​ ( x k ) ‖ \|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\| of the forward-backward residual operator G γ ​ ( ⋅ ) G_{\gamma}(\cdot) associated with the problem. We also propose a new accelerated Douglas-Rachford splitting scheme for solving this problem which also attains 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right) convergence rate on ‖ G γ ​ ( x k ) ‖ \|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\| under only maximally monotone assumptions using both varying and constant stepsizes. Finally, we specify our first algorithm to solve convex-concave minimax problems and apply our accelerated DR scheme to derive a new accelerated variant of the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM).

Introduction

This paper aims at developing a new class of accelerated algorithms to approximate a solution of the following maximally monotone inclusion:
Finding

The inclusion (

Related work.

Theory and methods for solving (

It is well-known that (

This problem plays a key role in convex optimization, game theory, and robust optimization.
It has recently attracted a great attention due to key applications in machine learning and robust optimization such as adversarial generative networks (GANs), distributionally robust optimization, and robust learning

madry2018towards

Many solution methods have been developed to solve convex-concave minimax problems based on the framework of monotone inclusions such as prox-methods, Arrow-Hurwicz, extra-gradient, and primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) schemes, and their variants, see, e.g.,

In recent years, due to the revolution of accelerated first-order methods and their applications in large-scale optimization and machine learning problems, convergence rates, computational complexity, and theoretical performance theory have been extensively developed for optimization algorithms and their extensions.
One of such research topics is theoretical convergence rates, especially sublinear convergence rates for first-order-type algorithms.
In the context of variational inequality and convex-concave minimax problems,
Nemirovskii2004

Nesterov2007a

Both papers show an
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
convergence rate on the gap function associated with the problem under standard assumptions, where

is the iteration counter.
Note that existing methods prior to
Nemirovskii2004
;
Nesterov2007a
often focus on asymptotic convergence or linear rates, while their sublinear rates are largely elusive.
The sublinear rates are usually

davis2016convergence

Unlike convex optimization where the objective residual can be used to characterize convergence rate, it is often challenging to design an appropriate criterion or “metric” to characterize convergence rates of approximate solutions in monotone inclusions.
One natural metric is the residual norm
\|x-T(x)\|
of a fixed-point operator

associated with (
1

While the distance
\|x_{k}-x^{\star}\|
between the iterate
x_{k}
to a solution
x^{\star}
is a very common metric, it ramains challenging to establish a sublinear rate on this criterion.

Recently, some remarkable progress has been made to develop accelerated algorithms for variational inequalities and monotone inclusions, including
adly2021first
;
attouch2020convergence
;
chen2017accelerated
;
he2016accelerated
;
juditsky2011solving

kim2021accelerated

mainge2021fast

These methods essentially rely on Nesterov’s accelerated ideas

An alternative approach to design accelerated methods has been recently opened up, which relies on a classical result in
halpern1967fixed
.
This technique is now referred to as the
Halpern-type fixed-point iteration
in this paper.
In
lieder2021convergence
, the author provides an elementary analysis to achieve optimal convergence rate of a Halpern fixed-point method, while
diakonikolas2020halpern
further investigates this type of scheme for solving monotone equations and VIPs by using Lyapunov function analysis.
The authors in
yoon2021accelerated
extend this scheme to the extra-gradient method and develop an elegant analysis framework to accelerate their algorithms, which we will exploit in this paper.
A recent extension to co-monotone operators is considered in
lee2021fast

Our goal.

In this paper, we will focus on two settings of (
1

The first one is when

is single-valued and
L

G=A+B

In the second setting, problem (

{0\in A(x)+B(x).}\vskip-1.07639pt

(2)

Due to its special structure, splitting methods that rely on operations of

Our main goal in this paper is to develop a new class of accelerated algorithms to approximate a solution of (

Our approach relies on the ideas of Halpern-type fixed-point iteration scheme in

Our contribution.

To this end, our main contribution can be summarized as follows:

(a)

Firstly, we develop a new accelerated algorithm to solve a maximally monotone equation
G(x)=0
by combining the Halpern-type fixed-point iteration

and Popov’s past extra-gradient method

Our scheme achieves

(b)

Secondly, we proposed a novel accelerated splitting extra-gradient method to solve (

and

We establish

To reduce the per-iteration complexity of this method, we derive a new variant using Popov’s past-gradient scheme.
This new variant still achieves the same convergence rate (up to a constant factor) under the same assumptions.
Both algorithms can be viewed as accelerated variants of the Douglas-Rachford splitting method.

(c)

Thirdly, we develop a new accelerated Douglas-Rachford splitting method to solve (
2

and
B

This algorithm essentially has the same per-iteration complexity as the standard DR splitting method.
Our algorithm achieves

Our scheme and its convergence guarantee are different from recent works

(d)

Finally, we specify our first algorithm to solve convex-concave minimax problems in both nonlinear and linear cases.
We also apply our accelerated DR scheme to derive a new variant of ADMM, where our theoretical convergence rate guarantee is still valid.

Let us highlight the following points of our contribution.
Firstly, our methods rely on a Halpern-type scheme

Background and Preliminary Results

We recall some basic concepts from convex analysis and theory of monotone operators using in this paper.
These concepts and properties can be found, e.g., in
Bauschke2011
;
Facchinei2003
;
Konnov2001
.

Maximal monotonicity and cocoerciveness.

We work with finite dimensional spaces
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For a set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
,
\mathrm{dom}(G)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:G(x)\not=\emptyset\right\}
denotes its domain,
\mathrm{graph}(G)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in G(x)\right\}
denotes its graph, where
2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
The inverse of
G
is defined as
G^{-1}(y):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in G(x)\}
.

For a set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we say that
G
is monotone if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
,
u\in G(x)
, and
v\in G(y)
.

G
is said to be
\mu_{G}
-strongly monotone (or sometimes called coercive) if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\mu_{G}\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
,
u\in G(x)
, and
v\in G(y)
, where
\mu_{G}>0
is called a strong monotonicity parameter.
If
\mu_{G}<0
, then we say that
G
is weakly monotone.
If
G
is single-valued, then these conditions reduce to
\langle G(x)-G(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle G(x)-G(y),x-y\rangle\geq\mu_{G}\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, respectively.
We say that
G
is maximally monotone if
\mathrm{graph}(G)
is not properly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator.
Note that
G
is maximally monotone, then
\alpha G
is also maximally monotone for any
\alpha>0
, and if
G
and
H
are maximally monotone, and
\mathrm{dom}(F)\cap\mathrm{int}\left(\mathrm{dom}(H)\right)\not=\emptyset
, then
G+H
is maximally monotone.

A single-valued operator
G
is said to be
L
-Lipschitz continuous if
\|G(x)-G(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, where
L\geq 0
is a Lipschitz constant.
If
L=1
, then we say that
G
is nonexpansive, while if
L\in[0,1)
, then we say that
G
is
L
-contractive, and
L
is its contraction factor.
We say that
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-cocoercive if
\langle G(x)-G(y),x-y\rangle\geq\frac{1}{L}\|G(x)-G(y)\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
.
If
L=1
, then we say that
G
is firmly nonexpansive.
Note that if
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-cocoercive, then it is also monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, but the reverse statement is not true in general.
If
L<0
, then we say that
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-monotone
bauschke2020generalized
.

Resolvent operator.

The operator
J_{G}(x):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:x\in y+G(y)\right\}
is called the resolvent of
G
, often denoted by
J_{G}(x)=(\mathbb{I}+G)^{-1}(x)
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity mapping.
Clearly, evaluating
J_{G}
requires solving a strongly monotone inclusion
0\in y-x+G(y)
.
If
G
is monotone, then
J_{G}
is singled-valued, and if
G
is maximally monotone then
J_{G}
is singled-valued and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{G})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
If
G
is monotone, then
J_{G}
is firmly nonexpansive
(
Bauschke2011
, Proposition 23.10)
.

Given a proper, closed, and convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
\mathrm{dom}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:f(x)<+\infty\}
denotes its domain, and
\partial{f}(x):=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle w,y-x\rangle,\ \forall y\in\mathrm{dom}(f)\}
denotes its subdifferential.
The subdiffrential
\partial{f}
is maximally monotone.
The resolvent
J_{\partial{f}}(x)
of
\partial{f}
becomes the proximal operator of
f
, denoted by
\mathrm{prox}_{f}(x)
, and defined as
\mathrm{prox}_{f}(x):=\mathrm{arg}\min_{y}\left\{f(y)+\tfrac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}\right\}
.
If
f=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, the indicator of a convex set
\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathrm{prox}_{f}
reduces to the projection
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}(\cdot)
onto
\mathcal{X}
.
Since
\partial{f}
is maximally monotone,
\mathrm{prox}_{f}
and
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathcal{X}}
have the same properties as the resolvent of a maximally monotone operator.

Halpern fixed-point iteration scheme.

In
halpern1967fixed
, B. Halpern proposed the following iterative scheme to approximate a fixed-point
x^{\star}=T(x^{\star})
of a nonexpansive mapping
T
:

{x_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})T(x_{k}),}\vskip-1.07639pt

(3)

where
x_{0}=0
is chosen and
|1-\beta_{k}|<1
.
Asymptotic convergence results were proved in
halpern1967fixed
;
wittmann1992approximation
under some suitable choices of
\beta_{k}
(i.e.
\beta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
,
\sum_{k}\beta_{k}=\infty
, and
\sum_{k}|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|<\infty
).
In a recent work
lieder2021convergence
, Lieder first proved an
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
sublinear convergence rate on the residual norm
\|x_{k}-T(x_{k})\|
for (
3
) by using
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
and any initial point
x_{0}
.
Diakonikolas further extended this idea to maximally monotone equations and VIPs in
diakonikolas2020halpern
, where a Lyapunov function of the form
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}(x):=\|x-T(x)\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\langle x-T(x),x-x_{0}\rangle
was used.
When solving a monotone equation
G(x)=0
, one interesting observation is that (
3
) uses a
\frac{2}{L}
stepsize instead of the usual
\frac{1}{L}
stepsize widely used in standard forward/gradient methods, where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
G
.

3
Accelerated Popov Method For Monotone Equations

Motivation.

Let us first consider a special case of (
1
) where
G
is single-valued and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
In this case, (
1
) becomes a maximally monotone equation of the form:

{G(x^{\star})=0.}\vskip-1.07639pt

(4)

Though special, it still covers the smooth case of convex-concave minimax problems.
In
yoon2021accelerated
, Yoon and Ryu applied the Halpern-type scheme developed in
halpern1967fixed
to extra-gradient and obtain an accelerated algorithm.
Unlike the original paper
halpern1967fixed
and a direct proof technique in
lieder2021convergence
, the analysis in
yoon2021accelerated
is different and relies on a Lyapunov function proposed in
diakonikolas2020halpern
.
We highlight that the method in
diakonikolas2020halpern
relies on a standard forward (or gradient) scheme used in variational inequality, and its convergence guarantee requires the cocoerciveness of
G
.
To go beyond this cocoerciveness, the classical extra-gradient scheme from
korpelevich1976extragradient
has been used in
yoon2021accelerated
.
However, extra-gradient-based methods require two evaluations of
G
at each iteration.
This observation motivates us to develop a new variant of the extra-anchored gradient method
yoon2021accelerated
by exploiting
popov1980modification
proposed by Popov.

Note that when solving (
4
), the extra-gradient method is equivalent to the forward-backward-forward (or Tseng’s modified forward-backward) scheme in
tseng2000modified
.
However, another method proposed in
popov1980modification
, which we call Popov’s method, also achieves a similar convergence guarantee using the same assumptions as in extra-gradient method but only requires one evaluation of
G
at each iteration.
Although the theoretical performance of algorithms depends on the constant factor in the final complexity bounds, the use of two evaluations at each iteration leads to some motivation to remove it.
Numerous attempts have been made to improve or remove this extra evaluation in the context of VIPs, see, e.g.,
censor2011subgradient
;
malitsky2015projected
;
malitsky2019golden
;
malitsky2014extragradient
;
popov1980modification
;
solodov1999new
;
tseng2000modified
.
We also believe that this improvement is significant in stochastic settings.
Finally, we highlight that Popov’s method is also equivalent to the
forward-reflected gradient
method in
malitsky2015projected
and the
optimistic gradient
method used in online learning
hsieh2019convergence
when solving (
4
).

3.1
The Derivation of Anchored Popov’s Scheme

Inspired by
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
yoon2021accelerated
, in this section, we develop an accelerated variant of Popov’s method in
popov1980modification
to approximate a solution of (
4
).
Our scheme can be described as follows:

{\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k}&:=&\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\eta_{k}G(y_{k-1}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\eta_{k}G(y_{k}),\end{array}\right.}\vskip-1.07639pt

(5)

where
\beta_{k}\in[0,1)
and
\eta_{k}>0
are given parameters, and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is fixed.
This scheme is similar to the extra-anchored gradient method in
yoon2021accelerated
, except for
G(x_{k})
is replaced by
G(y_{k-1})
to save one evaluation of
G
at each iteration
k
.

By switching the first and second lines, this scheme can be written equivalently to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\eta_{k}G(y_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})x_{k+1}-\eta_{k+1}G(y_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(6)

Clearly, if
\beta_{k}=0
, then (
6
) reduces to the original Popov’s scheme in
popov1980modification
.

Alternatively, from the second line of (
5
), we also have
x_{k}=\beta_{k-1}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k-1})x_{k-1}-\eta_{k-1}G(y_{k-1})
.
Hence,
G(y_{k-1})=\frac{1}{\eta_{k-1}}\left[\beta_{k-1}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k-1})x_{k-1}-x_{k}\right]
.
Substituting this expression into the first line of (
5
), we get the following update:

y_{k}=\left(\beta_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k-1}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)x_{0}+\left(1-\beta_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)x_{k}-\frac{(1-\beta_{k-1})\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}x_{k-1}.

Therefore, the scheme (
5
) can also be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k}&:=&\left(\beta_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k-1}\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)x_{0}+\left(1-\beta_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}\right)x_{k}-\frac{(1-\beta_{k-1})\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k-1}}x_{k-1},\\
x_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\eta_{k}G(y_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(7)

Clearly, if
\beta_{k}=0
and
\eta_{k}=\eta>0
, then this scheme reduces to
x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\eta G(2x_{k}-x_{k-1})
, the reflected gradient method proposed in
malitsky2015projected
.
Hence, our scheme (
7
) can be considered as an
accelerated variant
of the
reflected gradient method
.
Furthermore, without the accelerated step, as discussed in
hsieh2019convergence
, Popov’s method can be referred to as a past-extra-gradient method, and it is equivalent to a so-called
optimistic gradient
method used in online learning
hsieh2019convergence
.

3.2
One-Iteration Analysis: Key Estimate

Let us first define the following potential (or Lyapunov) function:

\mathcal{V}_{k}:=a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{0}\rangle+c_{k}L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},

(8)

where
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
and
c_{k}
are three given positive parameters.
This function is slightly different from the one in
diakonikolas2020halpern
due to the last term.
Then, we have the following result.

Lemma 1

Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
be generated by (
5
), where
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
,
b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
,
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
,

0<\eta_{k+1}<\frac{\left(1-\beta_{k}^{2}-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\beta_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}},\quad M\eta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}<1,\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}{M\beta_{k}\eta_{k}},

(9)

with
M:=2L^{2}(1+\theta)
for any
\theta>0
.
Then,
\mathcal{V}_{k}
defined by (
8
) satistifes

\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}\geq L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}+L^{2}(c_{k}-a_{k})\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(10)

Proof

First, from (
5
), we can easily show that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}-x_{k}&=&\beta_{k}(x_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G(y_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}-x_{k}&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}(x_{0}-x_{k+1})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}G(y_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}-y_{k}&=&-\eta_{k}(G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})).\end{array}\right.

(11)

Since
G
is monotone, we have
\langle G(x_{k+1})-G(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\geq 0
.
This inequality leads to

\langle G(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\geq\langle G(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle.

Using the first two lines of (
11
) into this inequality, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\langle G(x_{k+1}),x_{0}-x_{k+1}\rangle&\geq&\beta_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x_{0}-x_{k}\rangle-\eta_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
and noting that
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&b_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{0}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle G(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&=&b_{k+1}\eta_{k}\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G(x_{k+1})-G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle.\end{array}

(12)

Now, using the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
from (
8
), we have

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{0}\rangle\\
&&-{~}b_{k+1}\langle G(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{0}\rangle+c_{k}L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}-c_{k+1}L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:Popov_scheme_01_proof1}}{\geq}&a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G(x_{k+1})-G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\eta_{k}\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle+c_{k}L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}-c_{k+1}L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(13)

Next, we upper bound
\|G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}&=&\|G(y_{k})-G(x_{k})+G(x_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&2\|G(x_{k})-G(y_{k})\|^{2}+2\|G(x_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&2\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-4\langle G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle+2\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}+2L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
G
and the last line of (
11
), we have
\|G(x_{k+1})-G(y_{k})\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}
.
Adding
\theta L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}
to this inequality, for some
\theta>0
, and then combining the result and the last estimate, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|G(x_{k+1})-G(y_{k})\|^{2}+\theta L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}&=&\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}-2\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&&+{~}\theta L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\eta_{k}^{2}L^{2}(1+\theta)\|G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&M\eta_{k}^{2}\Big[\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-2\langle G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle+\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}\Big]\\
&&+{~}ML^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},\end{array}

where
M:=2L^{2}(1+\theta)
.
Rearranging this inequality, we obtain

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{ll}\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}&+{~}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}-M\eta_{k}^{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-2\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&-{~}2M\eta_{k}^{2}\langle G(x_{k+1})-G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle+\theta L^{2}\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&-{~}L^{2}M\eta_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq 0.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(14)

Multiplying (
14
) by
\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}
and adding the result to (
11
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-a_{k+1}\right)\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{a_{k}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\left(\frac{a_{k}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{2}\right)\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left(\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}-2a_{k}\right)\langle G(x_{k+1})-G(x_{k}),G(y_{k})\rangle+L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}L^{2}(c_{k}-a_{k})\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Now, let us choose
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
and define the following three quantities:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclcl}S^{11}_{k}&:=&\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-a_{k+1}&=&\frac{b_{k}}{2M\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k+1}}{2(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}},\\
S^{22}_{k}&:=&\frac{a_{k}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}&=&\frac{b_{k}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})}{2M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}},\\
S^{12}_{k}&:=&\frac{a_{k}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{2}&=&\frac{(1-\beta_{k}-M\eta_{k}^{2})b_{k}}{2M(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}.\end{array}\right.

Then, the above inequality can be written as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&S_{k}^{11}\|G(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+S_{k}^{22}\|G(y_{k})\|^{2}-2S_{k}^{12}\langle G(x_{k+1}),G(y_{k})\rangle\\
&&+{~}L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}+L^{2}(c_{k}-a_{k})\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Clearly, if we impose three conditions
S_{k}^{11}\geq 0
,
S_{k}^{22}\geq 0
, and
\sqrt{S_{k}^{11}S_{k}^{22}}\geq S_{k}^{12}
, then

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\|\sqrt{S_{k}^{11}}G(x_{k+1})-\sqrt{S_{k}^{22}}G(y_{k})\|^{2}+L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}L^{2}(c_{k}-a_{k})\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}+L^{2}(c_{k}-a_{k})\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},\end{array}

which proves (
10
).

Finally, we can easily show that the condition
\sqrt{S_{k}^{11}S_{k}^{22}}\geq S_{k}^{12}
holds if

\frac{(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})}{M\eta_{k}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{M\eta_{k}}-\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k+1}}{(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}}\right)\geq\left(\frac{1-\beta_{k}-M\eta_{k}^{2}}{M(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}\right)^{2}.

This condition is equivalent to
0<\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2})}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})}\cdot\eta_{k}
provided that
M\eta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}<1
.
Clearly, this one is exactly the first condition of (
9
).
The condition
S_{k}^{11}\geq 0
is equivalent to
\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}{M\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}
, which is the third condition of (
9
).
The condition
S_{k}^{22}\geq 0
is equivalent to
M\eta_{k}^{2}\leq 1
.
Combining this and
M\eta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\leq 1
, we get the second condition of (
9
).

\square

3.3
Parameter Updates

From the first condition of (
9
), let us update the parameters
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
of (
5
) as follows:

\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{\left(1-\beta_{k}^{2}-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\beta_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}},

(15)

where
M:=2L^{2}(1+\theta)
and
\eta_{0}
is chosen such that
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
.

From (
15
), we can show that
\{\eta_{k}\}
is non-increasing and has positive limit as stated in the following lemma.
This lemma is proved in
yoon2021accelerated
, but we provide a slightly different proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2

Given a constant
M>0
, let
\{\beta_{k}\}
and
\{\eta_{k}\}
be respectively updated by

\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta_{k+1}\big(1-\beta_{k}^{2}-M\eta_{k}^{2}\big)}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})}\cdot\eta_{k},

(16)

where
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
.
Then,
\{\eta_{k}\}
is nonincreasing, i.e.,
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
.

If, additionally,
\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
, then
\eta_{*}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}
exists and
\eta_{*}>\underline{\eta}:=\frac{\eta_{0}(1-2M\eta_{0}^{2})}{1-M\eta_{0}^{2}}>0
.

Proof

Substituting
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
into (
16
), we have

\eta_{k+1}=\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}\left(1-\frac{1}{S_{k}(k+2)^{2}}\right)\cdot\eta_{k},\quad\text{where}\quad S_{k}=1-M\eta_{k}^{2}.

Let us show that
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
by induction.

•

For
k=0
, we have
\eta_{1}=\frac{4}{3}\big(1-\frac{1}{4S_{0}}\big)\eta_{0}=\frac{4}{3}\big(1-\frac{1}{4(1-M\eta_{0}^{2})}\big)\eta_{0}
.
Clearly, for
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
, we have
\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
.

•

Assume that
\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{k-1}\leq\eta_{0}<1
for all
k\geq 1
.
Now, we show that
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}
.
Using (
16
) and
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
, we have

\frac{\eta_{k+1}}{\eta_{k}}=\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}\left(1-\frac{1}{S_{k}(k+2)^{2}}\right)<\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}\left(1-\frac{1}{(k+2)^{2}}\right)=1,

where in the first inequality, we use
0<S_{k}=1-M\eta_{k}^{2}<1
.

Hence, by induction, we conclude that
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Since
S_{k}=1-M_{0}\eta_{k}^{2}\geq S_{0}:=1-M_{0}\eta_{0}^{2}
due to the nonincrease of
\eta_{k}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\eta_{k+1}&\geq&\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}\left(1-\frac{1}{S_{0}(k+2)^{2}}\right)\cdot\eta_{k}=\left(1-\frac{(1-S_{0})}{S_{0}(k+1)(k+3)}\right)\cdot\eta_{k}\\
&\geq&\eta_{k}-\frac{(1-S_{0})\eta_{0}}{S_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}.\end{array}

By induction, we have
\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{0}-\frac{(1-S_{0})\eta_{0}}{S_{0}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{(i+1)(i+2)}=\eta_{0}-\frac{(1-S_{0})\eta_{0}}{S_{0}}\left(1-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\geq\frac{(2S_{0}-1)\eta_{0}}{S_{0}}=\frac{\eta_{0}(1-2M\eta_{0}^{2})}{1-M\eta_{0}^{2}}:=\underline{\eta}>0
provided that
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
.
Hence, we conclude that
0<\underline{\eta}\leq\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}\leq\cdots\leq\eta_{0}
for all
k\geq 0
.
The limit
\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}=\eta_{*}\geq\underline{\eta}>0
exists.

\square

In fact, if we scale
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\sqrt{M}\eta_{k}
, then the update (
16
) can be written in terms of
\hat{\eta}_{k}
as

\hat{\eta}_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta_{k+1}\big(1-\beta_{k}^{2}-\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\big)}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(1-\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2})}\cdot\hat{\eta}_{k},

provided that
0<\hat{\eta}_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.
If we choose
\hat{\eta}_{0}:=0.65<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, then we obtain
\hat{\eta}_{*}\geq 0.4370579>\hat{\underline{\eta}}=0.1745
.
Note that since our lower bound
\underline{\eta}
in Lemma
2
is loosely estimated, it happens that even when
\underline{\eta}\leq 0
, the limit
\eta_{*}
may still be positive.
Therefore, the condition
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
can be relaxed in implementation.

3.4
Convergence Rate Guarantees

Finally, we can prove the following main theorem on the convergence of our scheme (
5
).

Theorem 3.1

Assume that
G
in (
4
) is maximally monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{x_{k}\}
be generated by (
5
) to solve (
4
) as a special instance of (
1
) using with
y_{-1}:=x_{0}
.
Let
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
be updated by (
15
) with
0<\eta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2L\sqrt{3}}
and
M:=4L^{2}
.
Then, we have
\eta_{*}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}>\underline{\eta}:=\frac{\eta_{0}(1-2M\eta_{0}^{2})}{1-M\eta_{0}^{2}}>0
.
Moreover, the following bound holds:

\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+2L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}\left[\eta_{0}\|G(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{*}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\right].

(17)

Consequently, if we define
C_{*}:=\frac{4(\eta_{0}\eta_{*}L^{2}+1)}{\eta_{*}^{2}}
, then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{C_{*}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)(k+2)},\\
\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{C_{*}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{2L^{2}(k+1)(k+2)},\\
\|G(y_{k})-G(y_{k-1})\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{C_{*}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{2L^{2}\eta_{*}^{2}(k+2)(k+3)}.\end{array}\right.

(18)

Hence, the convergence rate of both
\{\|G(x_{k})\|\}
and
\{\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|\}
is
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)
.

Proof

First, let us verify the conditions in (
9
) of Lemma
1
.
Since
\eta_{k}
is updated by (
15
), by Lemma
2
,
\{\eta_{k}\}
is nonincreasing and
\eta_{*}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}>0
.
The first condition of (
9
) holds.
Since
\eta_{k}\leq\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
, the second condition
M\eta_{k}^{2}\leq 1-\beta_{k}^{2}
of (
9
) holds.
By
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}
, the last condition holds if
\eta_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}{M\beta_{k}}=\frac{(k+1)}{M(k+3)}
, which is equivalent to
\eta_{k}\leq\sqrt{\frac{k+1}{M(k+3)}}
.
Clearly, this holds if we choose
\eta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3M}}
.
In summary, all three conditions in (
9
) of Lemma
1
hold if we choose
0<\eta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3M}}
.

Next, let us choose
c_{k}:=a_{k}
.
Then, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}Q_{k}&:=&L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-c_{k+1}\right)=L^{2}\left(\frac{\theta a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-a_{k+1}\right)=\frac{L^{2}b_{k}}{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{M\beta_{k}\eta_{k}}-\frac{\eta_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}\right).\end{array}

Assume that
Q_{k}\geq 0
, which is equivalent to
\eta_{k}\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\theta\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}{M\beta_{k}}=\frac{\theta(k+1)}{M(k+3)}
.
Since
\eta_{k+1}\leq\eta_{k}
, the last condition holds if
\eta_{k}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\theta(k+1)}{M(k+3)}}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Clearly, if we choose
\eta_{0}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{3M}}
, then
Q_{k}\geq 0
.
Combining two conditions of
\eta_{0}
, we have

\eta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3M}}=\frac{1}{L\sqrt{6(1+\theta)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{0}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{3M}}=\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{L\sqrt{6(1+\theta)}}.

Let us choose

Then, these three conditions hold if

as desired.

Now, let
H_{k}:=a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{0}\rangle
.
Then, similar to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}H_{k}&=&a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G(x_{k})-G(x^{\star}),x_{k}-x^{\star}\rangle+b_{k}\langle G(x_{k}),x^{\star}-x_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&a_{k}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&=&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(19)

By the choice of
\eta_{0}

2
holds.
Hence, we have
\eta_{*}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}\geq\underline{\eta}>0
.
Moreover, since

b_{k}=b_{0}(k+1)
for any

.
Consequently,
a_{k}=c_{k}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)\eta_{k}}{2}\geq\frac{b_{0}\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
.

Finally, from (

\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq H_{k}+\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
, leading to

\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+a_{k}L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{V}_{k}+\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.

Using
y_{-1}=x_{0}
, the last estimate, (
10
), and
a_{k}=c_{k}\geq\frac{b_{0}\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
, by induction, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{b_{0}\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}{4}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{0}\eta_{*}L^{2}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G(x_{k})\|^{2}+a_{k}L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{k}+\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{0}+\frac{b_{0}^{2}(k+1)^{2}}{b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)\eta_{k}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&b_{0}\eta_{0}\|G(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2},\end{array}

which implies (

).
The estimates in (

) and
\|G(x_{0})\|^{2}=\|G(x_{0})-G(x^{\star})\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
.

\square

4
Halpern-Type Accelerated Methods for Monotone Inclusions

In this section, we develop two different splitting schemes using Halpern-type fixed-point iterations to solve (
2
) when
B
is additionally single-valued and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.

4.1
The forward-backward residual mapping

To characterize exact and approximate solutions of (
2
) as well as convergence rates of our algorithms, we define the following forward-backward residual (FBR) mapping of (
2
):

G_{\gamma}(x):=\gamma^{-1}\left(x-J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))\right),\qquad\text{where}\quad\gamma>0.

(20)

Note that
x^{\star}
is a solution of (
2
), i.e.,
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B)
if and only if
G_{\gamma}(x^{\star})=0
.
Using this mapping, we can transform (
2
) into an equation
G_{\gamma}(x^{\star})=0
.
Unfortunately, under our assumptions,
G_{\gamma}
is
not monotone
.
In convex optimization, we call
G
a prox-gradient mapping or simply a gradient mapping
Nesterov2004
.
We instead use FBR mapping to name
G_{\gamma}
.

To develop algorithms for solving (
2
), we need the following properties of
G_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 3

Let
A
and
B
in (
2
) be maximally monotone, and
B
be single-valued.
Let
G_{\gamma}(\cdot)
be defined by (
20
).
Then, the following statements hold.

\mathrm{(a)}

G_{\gamma}(\cdot)
satisfies

\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle+\gamma\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),B(x)-B(y)\rangle\geq\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}.

(21)

\mathrm{(b)}

If, in addition,
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then
G_{\gamma}(\cdot)
is
\frac{(1+\gamma L)}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Proof

(a) Let
M_{\gamma A}(u):=\frac{1}{\gamma}(u-J_{\gamma A}(u))
be the Yosida approximation of
A
.
By
(
Bauschke2011
, Corollary 23.11)
,
M_{\gamma A}(\cdot)
is
\gamma
-cocoercive.
Since
G_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x-J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x)))
, we have
J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))=x-\gamma G_{\gamma}(x)
.
Hence,
M_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x-\gamma B(x)-J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x)))=G_{\gamma}(x)-B(x)
.
Using the
\gamma
-cocoerciveness of
M_{\gamma}
, we have

\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)-(B(x)-B(y)),x-y-\gamma(B(x)-B(y))\rangle\geq\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)-(B(x)-B(y))\|^{2}.

This inequality leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle-\gamma\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),B(x)-B(y)\rangle\\
&&-{~}\langle B(x)-B(y),x-y\rangle+\gamma\|B(x)-B(y)\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}+\gamma\|B(x)-B(y)\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),B(x)-B(y)\rangle,\end{array}

which is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle+\gamma\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),B(x)-B(y)\rangle&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\langle B(x)-B(y),x-y\rangle.\end{array}

Using the monotonicity of
B
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle+\gamma\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),B(x)-B(y)\rangle&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2},\end{array}

which proves (
21
).

(b) Using the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
B
, this can be further estimated as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}-\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|\|B(x)-B(y)\|\\
&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}-\gamma L\|x-y\|\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|\\
&=&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|\left[\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|-L\|x-y\|\right].\end{array}

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we can also show that
(1+\gamma L)\|x-y\|\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|\geq\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x)-G_{\gamma}(y)\|^{2}
, leading to the
\frac{(1+\gamma L)}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz continuity of
G_{\gamma}
.

\square

4.2
The splitting extra-anchored gradient method

The derivation of our scheme.

We first develop a new scheme to solve (
2
) by exploiting the idea of the extra-anchored gradient (EAG) method in
yoon2021accelerated
.
We adopt the name in
yoon2021accelerated
and call it a splitting extra-anchored gradient method since it uses the operators
A
and
B
separately.
However, our method is fundamentally different from EAG as we will see below.

More precisely, our scheme consists of the following two main steps:
Starting from
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, it sets
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
and at each iteration
k\geq 0
, it updates

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k})-\gamma B(y_{k})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k})-\gamma B(x_{k+1})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(x_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(22)

where
G_{\gamma}
is defined by (
20
).
This is indeed an implicit scheme since
y_{k}
and
x_{k+1}
are involved in both the left- and the right-hand sides of (
22
).

Implementable version.

To obtain an equivalent and implementable version (
22
), we can rewrite the second step of (
22
) as follows:

x_{k+1}+\gamma B(x_{k+1})=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-(x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})))-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k}),

If we introduce
u_{k}:=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})
, then we get
x_{k}=J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})
, and the above step becomes
u_{k+1}=u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k})
, where
J_{\gamma B}(u)=(\mathbb{I}+\gamma B)^{-1}(u)
is the resolvent of
\gamma B
.

Similarly, the first step of (
22
) can be written as
y_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k})=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-(x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})))-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k})
.
Let us introduce
v_{k}:=y_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k})
. Then, we have
y_{k}=J_{\gamma B}(v_{k})
and
v_{k}=u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k})
.
Hence, (
22
) can eventually be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}v_{k}&:=&u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),\\
y_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(v_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&:=&u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(23)

Unlike (
22
), which is rather conceptual, (
23
) is implementable.

Douglas-Rachford interpretation.

To remove the evaluation of
B
, using the identity
\gamma B(J_{\gamma B}(w))=w-J_{\gamma B}(w)
, we have
\gamma B(y_{k})=\gamma B(J_{\gamma B}(v_{k}))=v_{k}-J_{\gamma B}(v_{k})=v_{k}-y_{k}
.
Hence,
G_{\gamma}(y_{k})=\frac{1}{\gamma}(y_{k}-J_{\gamma A}(2y_{k}-v_{k}))
.
Similarly,
G_{\gamma}(x_{k})=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x_{k}-J_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}))
.
We can rewrite the above scheme (
23
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{v}_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}),\\
v_{k}&:=&u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma}(x_{k}-\hat{v}_{k}),\\
y_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(v_{k}),\\
\hat{u}_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma A}(2y_{k}-v_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&:=&u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma}(y_{k}-\hat{u}_{k+1}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(24)

This scheme does not require to evaluate
B
as in (
23
), which can be viewed as an accelerated variant of the Douglas-Rachford splitting method (see Section
5.1
).
However, it uses the resolvent of
A
and
B
twice at each iteration.

One-iteration analysis – Key estimate.

To analyze (
22
), let us consider the following potential function:

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}:=a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})-u_{0}\rangle.

(25)

This potential function is still different from the ones in
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
yoon2021accelerated
.
The following lemma provides a key estimate to analyze convergence of (
22
).

Lemma 4

Assume that
A
and
B
in (
2
) are maximally monotone, and
B
is single-valued and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k},u_{k},v_{k})\}
be generated by (
22
), and
G_{\gamma}
be defined by (
20
).
Let
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
,
\beta_{k}
,
\gamma
, and
\eta_{k}
be positive and chosen such that
b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
,
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
,

0<\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\eta_{k}\beta_{k+1}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2})}{(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})},\quad M\eta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}<1,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}{M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}.

(26)

where
M:=\frac{(1+\gamma L)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}
.
Then,
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
defined by (
25
) satisfies
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}
for all

.

Proof

First, from the third line of (
22
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{k+1}-u_{k}&=&\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}-u_{k}&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}(u_{0}-u_{k+1})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}G_{\gamma}(y_{k}).\end{array}

(27)

Since
x_{k+1}=J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1})
, we have
x_{k+1}+\gamma B(x_{k+1})=u_{k+1}
.
Using this into (
21
), we have

\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k+1}-u_{k}\rangle\geq\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(28)

Substituting (
27
) into (
28
) and rearranging the result, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),u_{0}-u_{k+1}\rangle&\geq&\beta_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{0}-u_{k}\rangle+\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta_{k}}{(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle-\eta_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle.\end{array}

(29)

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
, rearranging the result and using
\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}=b_{k+1}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle&-{~}b_{k+1}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-u_{0}\rangle\geq\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&+{~}\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle.\end{array}

(30)

Next, using
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
from (
25
) and (
30
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:proof10_est4}}{\geq}&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle\\
&=&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\eta_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle.\end{array}

(31)

Let
M:=\frac{(1+\gamma L)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}
.
From (
22
), we have
u_{k+1}-v_{k}=-\eta_{k}(G_{\gamma}(y_{k})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k}))
.
Using this expression, the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{\gamma}
from Lemma

(b), and the nonexpansiveness of
J_{\gamma B}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|G(x_{k+1})-G(y_{k})\|^{2}&\leq&M\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}=M\|J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1})-J_{\gamma B}(v_{k})\|^{2}\leq M\|u_{k+1}-v_{k}\|^{2}\\

Expanding this inequality to get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}&+{~}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\|G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\|^{2}-M\eta_{k}^{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-2\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle\\

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}
and adding the result to (

), we obtain

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-a_{k+1}\right)\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+a_{k}\left(\frac{1}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-1\right)\|G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\left(a_{k}\left(\frac{1}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-1\right)-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{2}\right)\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left(\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}-2a_{k}\right)\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-21.52771pt

(32)

Let us choose
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
and define

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lclcl}\tilde{S}_{k}^{11}&:=&\frac{a_{k}}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-a_{k+1}&=&\frac{b_{k}}{2M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k+1}}{2(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}},\\

\tilde{S}_{k}^{12}&:=&a_{k}\left(\frac{1}{M\eta_{k}^{2}}-1\right)-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{2}&=&\frac{b_{k}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})}{2M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2(1-\beta_{k})}.\end{array}\right.

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\tilde{S}_{k}^{11}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\tilde{S}_{k}^{22}\|G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\|^{2}-2\tilde{S}_{k}^{12}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\rangle\\

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\|\big(\tilde{S}_{k}^{11}\big)^{1/2}G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-\big(\tilde{S}_{k}^{22}\big)^{1/2}G_{\gamma}(y_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\geq 0.\end{array}

Convergence guarantees.

For
M:=\frac{(1+\gamma L)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}
, let us update

and

\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{\eta_{k}\beta_{k+1}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2})}{(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}(1-M\eta_{k}^{2})},

where

The following theorem states the convergence of (

Theorem 4.1

4.3
The splitting past extra-anchored gradient method

The derivation of our scheme.

As we have observed, each iteration of (
24
) requires two evaluations of
J_{\gamma A}
and two evaluations of
J_{\gamma B}
.
To reduce the computation at each iteration, we propose a modification of (
22
) relying on Popov’s past extra-gradient method as in Section
3
.
We call this method a splitting past extra-anchored gradient scheme.

More precisely, our new scheme starts from
y_{-1}:=x_{0}
and
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and consists of the following two main steps:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k-1})-\gamma B(y_{k})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(y_{k-1}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k})-\gamma B(y_{k})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(y_{k-1}),\end{array}\right.

(39)

where
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{k}>0
are given parameters which will be determined later.

Implementable version.

While the second line of (
39
) is a forward (or explicit) step, the first line remains implicit.
We can write the first line of (
39
) as
(\mathbb{I}+\gamma B)(y_{k})=x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k-1})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(y_{k-1})
.
Using the resolvent
J_{\gamma B}
of
B
, we can rewrite (
39
) into the following implementable version:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}v_{k}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k-1})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(y_{k-1}),\\
y_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(v_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&x_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-x_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(y_{k})-\gamma B(y_{k})+\gamma(1-\beta_{k})B(y_{k-1}).\end{array}\right.

(40)

Unlike our previous scheme (
22
), this scheme only requires one evaluation
G_{\gamma}(y_{k})
, consisting of one
J_{\gamma A}
and one
B(y_{k})
, and one resolvent operation
J_{\gamma B}(v_{k})
.
Moreover, (
40
) is implementable compared to (
39
).

DR splitting interpretation.

Similar to (
24
), we can eliminate the computation of
B(y_{k})
in (
40
), to obtain the following equivalent scheme:

\hskip-0.08612pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(v_{k}),\\
z_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(2y_{k}-v_{k}),\\
\Delta_{k}&:=&y_{k}-z_{k},\\
v_{k+1}&:=&v_{k}+\beta_{k+1}(u_{0}-v_{k})-\frac{1}{\gamma}\left[\eta_{k+1}\Delta_{k}+(1-\beta_{k+1})\eta_{k}\left(\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k-1}\right)\right],\\
x_{k}&:=&y_{k-1}+\frac{v_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}-v_{k-1}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\Delta_{k-1}-\frac{\beta_{k}u_{0}}{1-\beta_{k}}.\end{array}\right.\hskip-17.22217pt

(41)

Clearly, we can also view this scheme as an accelerated variant of the DR splitting method (see Section
5.1
below).
It essentially has the same per-iteration complexity as the standard DR splitting method.
Note that the DR scheme is convergent under only the maximal monotonicity of
A
and
B
, while (
41
) requires
B
to be Lipschitz continuous.
However, again, (
41
) is an accelerated method.

One-iteration analysis.

To analyze convergence of (
39
), we consider the following potential function:

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}:=a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k-1})-u_{0}\rangle+c_{k}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},

(42)

where
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
, and
c_{k}
are three positive parameters which will be determined later.

Lemma 5

Let
A
and
B
in (
2
) be maximally monotone, and
B
be single-valued and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let

Proof

Rearranging the last inequality and using Young’s inequality

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}+\gamma(B(y_{k})-B(y_{k-1}))\rangle&\geq&\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}+\gamma(B(y_{k})-B(y_{k-1}))\rangle\\

\square

Lemma 6

Let
N:=\frac{(1+\gamma L)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}
and
M:=4N
.
Let
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
,
c_{k}
,
\beta_{k}
, and
\eta_{k}
be updated by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\beta_{k}&:=&\frac{1}{k+2},\quad b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}=b_{0}(k+2),\quad a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}},\\
\eta_{k+1}&:=&\frac{\eta_{k}\beta_{k+1}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2}\right)}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)},\qquad\quad c_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\left(\gamma L^{2}+N\eta_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

(51)

where

,

,

, and

Moreover,

such that

\begin{array}[]{l}0<\eta_{0}\leq\min\left\{\bar{\eta}_{0},\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3N}}\right\},\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{\eta}_{0}:=\frac{1}{2(4\gamma L^{2}+\sqrt{16\gamma^{2}L^{4}+3N})},\end{array}

(52)

then
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
defined by (

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
for all

.

Proof

The third condition (
43
) is equivalent to

0<\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\eta_{k}\beta_{k+1}\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}-\beta_{k}^{2}\right)}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})\left(1-M\eta_{k}^{2}\right)},

(53)

provided that
M\eta_{k}^{2}\leq 1-\beta_{k}^{2}
.
Since we update
\eta_{k}
as in (

), (

) is obviously satisfied.
As proved in Lemma
2
,
\{\eta_{k}\}
is nonincreasing and its limit exists.
If

\eta_{*}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}\geq\underline{\eta}>0
.
If we choose
\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}

obviously holds and therefore

, which satisfies the first condition of (

The second condition (

This condition holds if

Overall, three conditions of (

Now, let us choose

Then,
Q_{k}:=c_{k}-\frac{(\gamma L^{2}+N\eta_{k})b_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}=0
.
To guarantee

Since

.

Finally, under the choice of

\square

Convergence guarantee.

Now, we establish the convergence of the scheme (
39
).

Theorem 4.2

Let
A
and
B
of (
2
) be maximally monotone, and
B
be single-valued and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k},v_{k})\}
be generated by (
39
) to approximate a zero point of (
2
) using
y_{-1}:=x_{0}
and
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
, and the update rules (
51
) of parameters in Lemma
6
.
Then, we have the following statements:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{4C_{*}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)},\\
\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{4C_{*}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\left[2(\gamma L^{2}+N\eta_{*})(k+2)-\gamma\right](k+1)},\end{array}\right.

(54)

where
N:=\frac{(1+\gamma L)^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}
and
C_{*}:=\frac{N(\eta_{0}\eta_{*}\gamma^{2}+1)}{\gamma^{2}\eta_{*}}
.

Proof

First, since
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
due to Lemma
5
, by induction, we have
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
.
Hence
a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+c_{k}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k-1})-u_{0}\rangle\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
.
As proved previously, we can easily show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k-1})-u_{0}\rangle&\geq&-\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x^{\star}+\eta B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{4}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Using this estimate and
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
in (
42
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}&=&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),x_{k}+\gamma B(y_{k-1})-u_{0}\rangle+c_{k}\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\left(c_{k}-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{4}\right)\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|x^{\star}+\eta B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\frac{\eta_{*}b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}{4}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{0}}{4}(k+1)\left[2(\gamma L^{2}+N\eta_{k})(k+2)-\gamma\right]\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{b_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|x^{\star}+\eta B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Here, we have used the update rules of
\beta_{k}
,
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
, and
c_{k}
, and
\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{*}>0
.
Combining the last inequality and
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
, and noting that
\eta_{k}\geq\eta_{*}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\textrm{RHS}&:=&\frac{\eta_{*}b_{0}}{4}(k+1)(k+2)\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{0}}{4}(k+1)\left[2(\gamma L^{2}+N\eta_{*})(k+2)-\gamma\right]\|x_{k}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}+\frac{b_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Since
y_{-1}=x_{0}
and
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
, we have
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}=a_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}+b_{0}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{0}),x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})-u_{0}\rangle=b_{0}\eta_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}\leq b_{0}\eta_{0}N^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Moreover,
\|x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\leq(1+\gamma L)^{2}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Substituting these estimates into the last inequality, we get (
54

\square

5
Halpern-Type Accelerated Douglas-Rachford Splitting Method

Overview and motivation.

The Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting scheme is perhaps one of the most common algorithms to solve (
2
).
This method is proposed in
douglas1956numerical
and intensively studied in several works, including
bauschke2017douglas
;
Davis2014
;
eckstein1992douglas
;
Lions1979
;
svaiter2011weak
, just to name a few.
Under only maximal monotonicity of
A
and
B
, the iterate sequence generated by a DR splitting scheme converges to a solution of (
2
), see, e.g.,
Lions1979
;
svaiter2011weak
.
The first accelerated DR splitting method is perhaps due to
patrinos2014douglas
, which minimizes the sum of a convex and a quadratic function.
However, it is unclear how to extend this method to general settings such as monotone inclusions.

Recently, Kim developed an accelerated proximal point method for maximally monotone inclusions of the form (
1
) in
kim2021accelerated
using a performance estimation problem approach
drori2014performance
.
This scheme covers the DR splitting method as a special case since it can be written in the form of proximal-point method
eckstein1992douglas
;
Facchinei2003
.
However, as shown in
(
kim2021accelerated
, Corollary 6.2)
, the
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
-convergence rate is on an intermediate squared residual
\|u_{k}-\mathcal{T}^{\textrm{DR}}_{\gamma}(u_{k})\|
, but not on the shadow sequence
\{x_{k}\}
with
x_{k}=J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})
, where
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}
is the fixed-point DR operator defined by (
56
) below.
This motivates us to develop an accelerated variant of the DR splitting scheme using Halpern-type iteration and with a direct analysis where the convergence rate is on
\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|
of the shadow last-iterate sequence
\{x_{k}\}
.
Note that the convergence of the shadow sequence
\{x_{k}\}
has been studied in the literature, but only in asymptotic sense
Bauschke2011
.

Unlike two schemes in Section
4
, we develop an accelerated variant of the Douglas-Rachford splitting method by utilizing the Halpern-type idea in
halpern1967fixed
and the Lyapunov analysis in
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
yoon2021accelerated
without requiring the Lipschitz continuity of
B
.

5.1
The derivation of accelerated DR splitting method

Equivalent expressions of DR splitting scheme.

The common form of the standard Douglas-Rachford splitting scheme
douglas1956numerical
;
Lions1979
for solving (
2
) is written as

{\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\gamma B}(u_{k}),\\
\hat{v}_{k}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&:=&u_{k}+\hat{v}_{k}-x_{k}.\end{array}\right.}\vskip-1.07639pt

(55)

First, we can rewrite (
55
) as follows by combining three lines in one:

{u_{k+1}:=\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}(u_{k})\equiv u_{k}+J_{\gamma A}\left(2J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})-u_{k}\right)-J_{\gamma B}(u_{k}).}\vskip-1.07639pt

(56)

Using the identity
\gamma B(J_{\gamma B}(u))=u-J_{\gamma B}(u)
and assume that
B
is single-valued, we can further rewrite (
56
) as

x_{k+1}:=J_{\gamma B}\left(J_{\gamma A}(x_{k}-\gamma B(x_{k}))+\gamma B(x_{k})\right).

(57)

The DR splitting scheme (
56
) only requires one resolvent
J_{\gamma A}
and one resolvent
J_{\gamma B}
without any evaluation of
B
as in (
57
).
However, the convergence of (
56
) is often on
\{u_{k}\}
, an intermediate sequence, compared to
\{x_{k}\}
as in (
57
), the shadow sequence that converges to
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.
Note that
\{u_{k}\}
converges to
u^{\star}
such that
x^{\star}=J_{\gamma B}(u^{\star})
is a solution of (
2
).

Now, let us consider the fixed-point mapping of the iterate scheme (
57
) as

\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}(x):=J_{\gamma B}\left(J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))+\gamma B(x)\right).

If we recall the forward-backward mapping
G_{\gamma}(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x-J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))
of (
2
) from (
20
), then we have
J_{\gamma A}(x-\gamma B(x))=x-\gamma G_{\gamma}(x)
, and therefore
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}(x)=J_{\gamma B}(x-\gamma(G_{\gamma}(x)-B(x)))
.
Clearly, the update
x_{k+1}=\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}(x_{k})
in (
57
) becomes
x_{k+1}+\gamma B(x_{k+1})=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})-\gamma G_{\gamma}(x_{k})
.
Let us introduce
u_{k}:=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})
.
Then,
x_{k}=J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})
.
Moreover, (
57
) becomes
u_{k+1}=u_{k}-\gamma G_{\gamma}(x_{k})
.
Using this expression, we can write (
57
) equivalently to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{k+1}&:=&u_{k}-\gamma G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(58)

This scheme uses both forward and backward operators of
B
.

We highlight that, as shown in
eckstein1992douglas
,
M_{\gamma}=(\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma})^{-1}-\mathbb{I}
is maximally monotone and
J_{M_{\gamma}}=\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}
, which is firmly nonexpansive.
Hence, one can directly apply the Halpern fixed-point scheme to
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}
as
u_{k+1}=\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}(u_{k})
.
However, the convergence rate guarantee will be on
\|u_{k}-\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{DR}}_{\gamma}(u_{k})\|
as in
kim2021accelerated
instead of
\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|
.

The new accelerated DR splitting scheme.

Now, inspired by
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
halpern1967fixed
;
kim2021accelerated
, we aim at developing an accelerated DR splitting scheme by modifying (
58
) instead of (
56
) as follows:
Starting from
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we set
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
and at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}u_{k+1}&:=&u_{k}+\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(59)

To avoid the evaluation of
B(x_{k})
, we note that since
\gamma B(J_{\gamma B}(u_{k}))=u_{k}-J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})
and
x_{k}=J_{\gamma B}(u_{k})
, we have
\gamma B(x_{k})=u_{k}-x_{k}
.
Hence, we can write
G_{\gamma}(x_{k})=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x_{k}-J_{\gamma A}(x_{k}-\gamma B(x_{k})))=\frac{1}{\gamma}(x_{k}-J_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}))
.
Consequently, we can rewrite (
59
) equivalently to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k}),\\
v_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})u_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma}(v_{k}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(60)

Clearly, (
60
) has essentially the same per-iteration complexity as in the standard Douglas-Rachford splitting method (
55
) with one
J_{\gamma B}
and one
J_{\gamma A}
per iteration.
In addition, it does not requires
B
to be single-valued as in (
59
).
Note that only the third line of (
60
) is different from the standard DR splitting scheme (
55
).
If
\beta_{k}=0
and
\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma}=1
, then (
60
) coincides with (
55
).
This new scheme is also different from (
41
) above at the last line.

5.2
Convergence analysis

We first prove a key estimate to analyze the convergence of (
59
) in the following lemma.

Lemma 7

Let
\{(x_{k},u_{k})\}
be generated by (
59
) and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
be a Lyapunov function defined as

\mathcal{L}_{k}:=a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle.

(61)

Let the parameters
\gamma>0
,
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
,
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
are chosen such that
2\gamma(1-\beta_{k}^{2})>\eta_{k}
and

\hskip-4.30554pt\beta_{k}\in(0,1),\ \ b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}},\ \ a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}},\ \text{and}\ 0<\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}\left[2\gamma(1-\beta_{k}^{2})-\eta_{k}\right]\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(2\gamma-\eta_{k})}.\hskip-4.30554pt

(62)

Then, we have
\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof

First, from the first step of (
59
), we have

u_{k+1}-u_{k}=\beta_{k}(u_{0}-u_{k})-\eta_{k}G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),\quad\text{and}\quad u_{k+1}-u_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}(u_{0}-u_{k+1})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}G_{\gamma}(x_{k}).

Since
u_{k+1}=x_{k+1}+\gamma B(x_{k+1})
and
u_{k}=x_{k}+\gamma B(x_{k})
, using (
21
), we have
\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k+1}-u_{k}\rangle\geq\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}
.
Hence, combining this inequality and the last expressions, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\beta_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle-\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\gamma\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\rangle-\eta_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
and using
b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})-G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\rangle-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
\end{array}

Now, using this inequality, from (
61
), we can lower bound
\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&&-{~}b_{k+1}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{2b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1}),G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\rangle-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&=&\left(a_{k}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right)\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}-a_{k+1}\right)\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}2\left(\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\right)\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),G_{\gamma}(x_{k+1})\rangle.\end{array}

Hence, if we impose the following conditions
a_{k+1}<\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
and

\left(a_{k}+\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right)\left(\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}-a_{k+1}\right)\geq\left(\frac{b_{k}\gamma}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\right)^{2}.

(63)

then the last inequality guarantees that
\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.

Finally, let us choose
a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
.
Then
a_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k+1}}{2\beta_{k+1}}=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k+1}}{2(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}}
.
The condition (
63
) becomes

4(1-\beta_{k})^{2}\left(\gamma-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\right)\left(\gamma-\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k+1}}{2(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}}\right)\geq(2\gamma(1-\beta_{k})-\eta_{k})^{2}.

This condition is equivalent to

0<\eta_{k+1}\leq\frac{\beta_{k+1}\left[2\gamma(1-\beta_{k}^{2})-\eta_{k}\right]\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(2\gamma-\eta_{k})},

which is exactly the last condition of (
62
).
Condition
a_{k+1}<\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}
automatically holds.

\square

Next, we need the following technical lemma to prove the convergence of our scheme (
59
).
The proof of this lemma is very similar to the one in Lemma
2
and therefore, we omit.

Lemma 8

Given
\hat{\eta}_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{3}{4}\right)
, let
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
and
\{\hat{\eta}_{k}\}
be updated by

\hat{\eta}_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta_{k+1}(1-\hat{\eta}_{k}-\beta_{k}^{2})\hat{\eta}_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(1-\hat{\eta}_{k})}=\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}\left(1-\frac{1}{(1-\hat{\eta}_{k})(k+2)^{2}}\right)\hat{\eta}_{k}.

(64)

Then,
\{\hat{\eta}_{k}\}
is nonincreasing and
\hat{\eta}_{*}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{\eta}_{k}
exists.
If, in addition,
\hat{\eta}_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, then
\hat{\eta}_{*}>\underline{\hat{\eta}}:=\frac{1-2\hat{\eta}_{0}}{1-\hat{\eta}_{0}}>0
.

In particular, if we choose
\hat{\eta}_{0}:=0.5
, then we can compute
\hat{\eta}_{*}\approx 0.276314355842637>0.2763
.
From the last condition of (
62
), let us update
\eta_{k}
as

\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta_{k+1}\left[2\gamma(1-\beta_{k}^{2})-\eta_{k}\right]\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(2\gamma-\eta_{k})}.

(65)

where
\eta_{0}
satisfies
0<\eta_{0}<\gamma
.
Then, using
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
and defining
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{2\gamma}
, we can easily check that
\{\hat{\eta}_{k}\}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
8
.
Hence,
\{\eta_{k}\}
is nonincreasing and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}=\eta_{*}>\underline{\eta_{0}}:=\frac{4\gamma(\gamma-\eta_{0})}{2\gamma-\eta_{0}}>0
.
Note that this sequence of
\{\eta_{k}\}
is slightly different from the one in Lemma
2
.
In particular, if we choose
\eta_{0}:=\gamma
, then
\eta_{*}>0.5526\gamma>\frac{\gamma}{2}
.

Now, we can prove the convergence of our scheme (
59
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
(Varying stepsize
\eta_{k}
)

Assume that
A
and
B
are maximally monotone and
B
is single-valued and
G_{\gamma}
is defined by (
20
) for some
\gamma>0
.
Let
\{(x_{k},u_{k})\}
be generated by (
59
)
(
or equivalently, (
60
)
)
to solve (
2
) starting from
u_{0}
and using the following rules:

\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2},\qquad\eta_{0}\in(0,\gamma),\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{\beta_{k+1}\left[2\gamma(1-\beta_{k}^{2})-\eta_{k}\right]\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})(2\gamma-\eta_{k})}.

Then, the following bound holds:

\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}\left(\eta_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{*}}\|x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\right),

(66)

where
\eta_{*}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}>\frac{2\gamma(\gamma-\eta_{0})}{2\gamma-\eta_{0}}>0
.
If, additionally,
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then

\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{4(1+\gamma L)^{2}(\eta_{0}\eta_{*}+\gamma^{2})}{\eta_{*}^{2}\gamma^{2}(k+1)(k+2)}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.

(67)

Proof

Let
u^{\star}:=x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})
.
Then, using (
21
), we can derive that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle&=&b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k})-G_{\gamma}(x^{\star}),x_{k}-x^{\star}+\gamma(B(x_{k})-B(x^{\star}))\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u^{\star}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:key_est1_of_G}}{\geq}&\gamma b_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&-\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Using this estimate and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
in (
61
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}&=&a_{k}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle G_{\gamma}(x_{k}),u_{k}-u_{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|u^{\star}-x_{0}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\frac{\eta_{*}b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}{4}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Now, since
\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}
from Lemma
7
, by induction, we have

\frac{\eta_{*}b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}{4}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}=a_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}=b_{0}\eta_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}.

Rearranging this inequality, we obtain the first inequality of (
66
).
Finally, noting that
u_{0}:=x_{0}+\gamma B(x_{0})
and
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, we have
\|x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|\leq\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|+\gamma\|B(x^{\star})-B(x_{0})\|\leq(1+\gamma L)\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|
.
In addition, by Lemma
3
(b),
G_{\gamma}
is
\frac{1+\gamma L}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz continuous, and
G_{\gamma}(x^{\star})=0
.
Hence, we have
\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|=\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})-G_{\gamma}(x^{\star})\|\leq\frac{(1+\gamma L)}{\gamma}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|
.
Consequently, we can bound

C_{*}:=\frac{4\eta_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{4}{\eta_{*}^{2}}\|x^{\star}+\gamma B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4(1+\gamma L)^{2}(\eta_{0}\eta_{*}+\gamma^{2})}{\eta_{*}^{2}\gamma^{2}}\|x_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.

Using this upper bound into (
66
), we obtain (
67
).

\square

Our next result is then the stepsize
\eta_{k}
is fixed at
\eta_{k}:=\eta>0
.

Theorem 5.2
(Constant stepsize)

Assume that
A
and
B
are maximally monotone and
B
is single-valued and
G_{\gamma}
is defined by (
20
).
Let
\{(x_{k},u_{k})\}
be generated by (
60
) to solve (
2
) starting from
u_{0}
and using a fixed constant stepsize
\gamma:=\eta>0
and
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
.
Then

\|G_{\eta}(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{k(k+1)}\left[\|G_{\eta}(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{2}{\eta^{2}}\|x^{\star}+\eta B(x^{\star})-u_{0}\|^{2}\right],\quad\forall k\geq 1.

(68)

If, additionally,
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then

\|G_{\eta}(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{4(1+\eta L)(2\eta+1)}{2\eta k(k+1)}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2},\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Proof

Similar to the proof of Lemma
7
, if we choose
\eta_{k}=\gamma=\eta>0
,
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, and
b_{k}:=k+1
, then we can lower bound
\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}&\geq&a_{k}\|G_{\eta}(x_{k})\|^{2}-k(k+2)\eta\left\langle G_{\eta}(x_{k}),G_{\eta}(x_{k+1})\right\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left[(k+1)(k+2)\eta-a_{k+1}\right]\|G_{\eta}(x_{k+1})\|^{2}.\end{array}

Now, if we impose
a_{k}\geq 0
and

0<a_{k+1}\leq(k+1)(k+2)\eta-\frac{k^{2}(k+2)^{2}\eta^{2}}{4a_{k}},

(69)

then the last inequality guarantees that
\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.

Let us update
a_{k}
as
a_{k+1}:=(k+1)(k+2)\eta-\frac{k^{2}(k+2)^{2}\eta^{2}}{4a_{k}}
, where
a_{0}:=\frac{\eta}{2}
.
Then, it satisfies (
69
).
By induction, one can easily show that
a_{k}\geq a_{0}k(k+1)
for all
k\geq 0
.
Hence, the condition
a_{k}\geq 0
is automatically holds for
k\geq 0
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}&\geq&\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|u^{\star}-x_{0}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\eta k(k+1)}{4}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta}\|u^{\star}-x_{0}\|^{2}\\
\end{array}

Since
\mathcal{L}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}
, by induction, we have

\frac{\eta k(k+1)}{4}\|G_{\eta}(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}=a_{0}\|G_{\eta}(x_{0})\|^{2}=\frac{\eta}{2}\|G_{\eta}(x_{0})\|^{2}.

Rearranging this inequality, we obtain (
68
).
The remaining proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

\square

Remark 1
(Multivalued

Note that if

In this case, our bound (

\inf_{v_{k}\in B(x_{k})}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\dfrac{4}{\eta_{*}(k+1)(k+2)}\left(\eta_{0}\|G_{\gamma}(x_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta_{*}}\|u^{\star}-u_{0}\|^{2}\right),

Compared to the accelerated DR scheme in

compared to

.
We hope that the approach in this section can also be utilized to provide a direct proof for the DR scheme in
kim2021accelerated
.

6
Applications to Minimax Problems and Constrained Convex Optimization

In this section, we specify our methods developed in the previous sections to solve two special cases of (
1
).
The first one is a class of convex-concave minimax problems, and the second application is an accelerated ADMM variant for solving constrained convex optimization problems derived from our accelerated DR splitting scheme.

6.1
Convex-Concave Minimax Problems

Let us consider the following convex-concave minimax problem:

\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\mathcal{H}(z,w):=f(z)+\mathcal{L}(z,w)-g(w)\Big\},

(71)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
are two proper, closed, and convex functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively, and
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,w)
is convex w.r.t.
z
for any given
w
, and
\mathcal{L}(z,\cdot)
is concave w.r.t.
w
for any given
z
.
Moreover, we assume that
\mathcal{L}
is differentiable on both
z
and
w
, and its partial gradients are Lipschitz continuous, i.e. for all
(z,w),(\hat{z},\hat{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\|(\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(z,w),\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(z,w))-(\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z},\hat{w}),\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z},\hat{w}))\|\leq L\|(z,w)-(\hat{z},\hat{w})\|,

(72)

In this case, we say that
\mathcal{L}
is
L
-smooth.

Case 1: The smooth and nononlinear case.

We first consider the first case when both
f(z)=0
and
g(w)=0
so that
\mathcal{H}(z,w)=\mathcal{L}(z,w)
.
Hence,
x^{\star}:=(z^{\star},w^{\star})
is a saddle-point of (
71
), i.e.
\mathcal{L}(z^{\star},w)\leq\mathcal{L}(z^{\star},w^{\star})\leq\mathcal{L}(z,w^{\star})
for all
(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if and only if
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(z^{\star},w^{\star})=0
.

Let us define the following mappings:

\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(z,w):=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(z,w)\\
\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(z,w)\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad G(x):=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(z,w)\\
-\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(z,w)\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}\ \ x:=(z,w).

Then, it is well-known that
G
is maximally monotone, and due to the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}
,
G
is also
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Moreover, it is obvious that
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(z,w)\|=\|G(x)\|
.

Now, let us apply our scheme (
6
) to solve (
71
) when
f(x)=0
and
g(y)=0
, leading to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\eta_{k}G(\hat{x}_{k}),\\
\hat{x}_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})x_{k+1}-\eta_{k+1}G(\hat{x}_{k}).\end{array}\right.

This scheme can be written explicitly as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\eta_{k}\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z}_{k},\hat{w}_{k}),\\
w_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})w_{k}+\eta_{k}\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z}_{k},\hat{w}_{k}),\\
\hat{z}_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})z_{k+1}-\eta_{k+1}\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z}_{k},\hat{w}_{k}),\\
\hat{w}_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})w_{k+1}+\eta_{k+1}\nabla_{w}\mathcal{L}(\hat{z}_{k},\hat{w}_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(73)

Under the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla{\mathcal{L}}
, our convergence rate guarantee
\|\nabla{\mathcal{L}}(z_{k},w_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{*}(\|z_{0}-z^{\star}\|^{2}+\|w_{0}-w^{\star}\|^{2})}{(k+1)(k+2)}
in Theorem
3.1
remains valid, where
x
encodes both
z
and
w
.

Case 2: The nonsmooth and linear case.

Now, we consider the general case, where
f
and
g
are proper, closed, and convex, but
\mathcal{L}
is bilinear, i.e.
\mathcal{L}(z,w)=\langle Kz,w\rangle
.
By defining

G(x):=\begin{bmatrix}\partial{f}(z)+K^{\top}w\\
\partial{g}(w)-Kz\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial{f}&K^{\top}\\
-K&\partial{g}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z\\
w\end{pmatrix},

where
x:=(z,w)
, we can write the optimality condition of (
71
) into (
1
).
To apply (
6
) to this problem, we consider the following operator:

G_{H}(x):=x-\left(H+G\right)^{-1}(Hx)=x-J_{H^{-1}G}(x),\quad\text{where}\quad H:=\begin{bmatrix}\tau^{-1}\mathbb{I}&-K^{\top}\\
-K&\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{I}\end{bmatrix}.

Here,
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
are chosen such that
H
is positive definite (i.e.
\tau\sigma\|K\|^{2}<1
).
Then
x^{\star}=(z^{\star},y^{\star})
is a solution of (
71
) iff

0\in G(x^{\star})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad G_{H}(x^{\star})=0.

One can easily show that
G_{H}
is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous with
L=1
w.r.t. a weighted inner product
\langle u,v\rangle_{H}:=\langle Hu,v\rangle
and a weighted norm
\|u\|_{H}:=\langle Hu,u\rangle^{1/2}
.

Let us apply (
6
) to solve the equation
G_{H}(x^{\star})=0
, leading to the following scheme:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{s}_{k}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\tau f}(\hat{z}_{k}-\tau K^{\top}\hat{w}_{k}),\\
\hat{r}_{k}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\sigma g}(\hat{w}_{k}+\sigma(2\hat{s}_{k}-K\hat{z}_{k})),\\
z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\eta_{k}\left(\hat{z}_{k}-\hat{s}_{k}\right),\\
w_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})w_{k}+\eta_{k}\left(\hat{w}_{k}-\hat{r}_{k}\right),\\
\hat{z}_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})z_{k+1}-\eta_{k+1}\left(\hat{z}_{k}-\hat{s}_{k}\right),\\
\hat{w}_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k+1}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k+1})w_{k+1}+\eta_{k+1}\left(\hat{w}_{k}-\hat{r}_{k}\right).\end{array}\right.

(74)

Here, we have used the fact that
(H+G)^{-1}(\xi,\zeta)=\left(\mathrm{prox}_{\tau f}(\tau\xi),\ \mathrm{prox}_{\sigma g}(\sigma\zeta+2\sigma\mathrm{prox}_{\tau f}(\tau\xi))\right)
, see, e.g.,
(
mainge2021fast
, Proposition 5.2)
.

By utilizing the convergence of (
6
) we still obtain
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
convergence rate of
\|G_{H}(x_{k})\|_{H}
.
The scheme (
74
) essentially has the same per-iteration complexity as in the standard primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) method
Chambolle2011
;
Esser2010
.
It is also different from the variant in
mainge2021fast
.

It should be noted that since
J_{H^{-1}G}(x)=(\mathbb{I}+H^{-1}G)^{-1}(x)
is firmly nonexpansive, one can directly apply a Halpern-type method in
diakonikolas2020halpern
;
lieder2021convergence
to
J_{H^{-1}G}
, and get a different accelerated scheme with the same convergence rate (up to a constant factor) and essentially the same per-iteration complexity as (
74
).
However, it remains unclear which scheme will have better theoretical bound as well as practical performance.

6.2
Application to Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers

The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is one of the most popular methods in both convex and nonconvex optimization
Boyd2011
.
The goal of this subsection is to derive an accelerated ADMM from our accelerated DR splitting scheme (
59
).
For this purpose, let us consider the following linear constrained convex optimization problem:

\min_{z,w}\Big\{F(z,w):=f(z)+g(w)\quad\textrm{s.t.}\quad Pz+Qw=r\Big\},

(75)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{p_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
are two proper, closed, and convex functions,
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p_{1}}
,
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p_{2}}
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given.

The dual problem of (
75
) can be written as

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{D(x):=f^{*}(P^{\top}x)+g^{*}(Q^{\top}x)-r^{\top}x\Big\},

(76)

where
f^{*}
and
g^{*}
are Fenchel conjugates of
f
and
g
, respectively.
The optimality condition of this problem is

0\in P\partial{f}^{*}(P^{\top}x^{\star})+Q\partial{g}^{*}(Q^{\top}x^{\star})-r,

(77)

which is necessary and sufficient for
x^{\star}
to be an optimal solution of (
76
) under suitable qualification conditions.
Let us define
A(x):=P\partial{f}^{*}(P^{\top}(x))-r
and
B(x):=Q\partial{g}^{*}(Q^{\top}x)
.
Then, both
A
and
B
are maximally monotone, and (
77
) can be written in the form of (
2
).

Let us apply our accelerated DR splitting scheme (
60
) to solve (
77
).
Our first step is to switch the first and the last lines of (
60
) to obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}v_{k}&:=&J_{\gamma A}(2x_{k}-u_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})u_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma}(v_{k}-x_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\gamma B}(u_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

Now, we apply this scheme to (
77
).
After some standard transformations (see, e.g.,
lorenz2018non
for more details), we obtain the following accelerated variant of ADMM:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min_{z}\Big\{f(z)-\langle x_{k},Pz\rangle+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|Pz+Qw_{k}-r\|^{2}\Big\},\\
\tilde{x}_{k}&:=&\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-(\eta_{k}-\gamma)(Pz_{k+1}-r)+[(1-\beta_{k})\gamma-\eta_{k}]Qw_{k},\\
w_{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min_{w}\Big\{g(w)-\langle\tilde{x}_{k},Qw\rangle+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|Pz_{k+1}+Qw-r\|^{2}\Big\},\\
x_{k+1}&:=&\tilde{x}_{k}-\gamma(Pz_{k+1}+Qw_{k+1}-r).\end{array}\right.

(78)

Alternatively, we can also choose constant stepsize
\eta_{k}
as
\eta_{k}=\eta=\gamma>0
in (
78
) according to Theorem
5.2
to get the following variant:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min_{z}\Big\{f(z)-\langle x_{k},Pz\rangle+\tfrac{\eta}{2}\|Pz+Qw_{k}-r\|^{2}\Big\},\\
\tilde{x}_{k}&:=&\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})x_{k}-\beta_{k}\eta Qw_{k},\\
w_{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min_{w}\Big\{g(w)-\langle\tilde{x}_{k},Qw\rangle+\tfrac{\eta}{2}\|Pz_{k+1}+Qw-r\|^{2}\Big\},\\
x_{k+1}&:=&\tilde{x}_{k}-\eta(Pz_{k+1}+Qw_{k+1}-r).\end{array}\right.

(79)

This variant of ADMM is only different from the standard ADMM method at the update of
\tilde{x}_{k}
.
Clearly, if
\beta_{k}=0
, then
\tilde{x}_{k}=x_{k}
, and (
79
) is thus coincided with the standard ADMM.

The scheme (
79
) is also similar to the one in
kim2021accelerated
except for the update of
\tilde{x}_{k}
.
It is also simpler than (
78
).
If we apply Theorem
5.1
to obtain a convergence guarantee of (
78
), then only the rate on the dual forward-backward residual norm
\|\nabla{G}_{\gamma}(x_{k})\|
is obtained.
It is interesting to note that our penalty parameter
\gamma
remains fixed as in standard ADMM.
The parameters
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
are updated explicitly without any heuristic strategies as in
He2000
;
lorenz2018non
.

7
Conclusions

We have developed four different algorithms to solve monotone inclusions in two different settings: monotone equations and structured monotone inclusions.
The first algorithm is a variant of the extra-anchored gradient method in
yoon2021accelerated
, but relies on a past extra-gradient scheme from
popov1980modification
with only one operator evaluation per iteration.
Our algorithm still achieves the same convergence rate as in
yoon2021accelerated
up to a constant fator.
The second and third algorithms aim at solving the structured monotone inclusion (
2
), when
B
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous.
These algorithms can be viewed as variants of the DR splitting method, but in an accelerated manner.
While the second algorithm requires two evaluations of the resolvent of
A
and
B
at each iteration, the third one only needs one evaluation each per iteration.
The last algorithm is a direct accelerated variant of the DR splitting method, which achieves
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
rates on the forward-backward residual operator norm of the shadow sequence
\{x_{k}\}
for (
2
) under only the maximal monotonicity of
A
and
B
. This algorithm works with both varying and constant stepsize, and the analysis is rather simple compared to the third algorithm.
Our analysis framework is inspired by the technique in
yoon2021accelerated
and the Lyapunov function in
diakonikolas2020halpern
.
However, our algorithmic design as well as our Lyapunov functions are new.
Finally, we have also shown how to derive new algorithms for solving convex-concave minimiax problems and a new accelerated ADMM variant with varying and constant stepsizes.

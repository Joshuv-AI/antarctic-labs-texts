The corank of a flow over the category of linearly compact vector spaces.
Ilaria Castellano

Abstract
For a topological flow ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) - i.e., V V is a linearly compact vector space and ϕ \phi a continuous endomorphism of V V - we gain a deep understanding of the relationship between ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) and the Bernoulli shift: a topological flow ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) is essentially a product of one-dimensional left Bernoulli shifts as many as ent ∗ ​ ( V , ϕ ) \mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi) counts. This novel comprehension brings us to introduce a notion of corank for topological flows designed for coinciding with the value of the topological entropy of ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) . As an application, we provide an alternative proof of the so-called Bridge Theorem for locally linearly compact vector spaces connecting the topological entropy to the algebraic entropy by means of Lefschetz duality.



Key words and phrases: linearly compact vector space, algebraic entropy, topological entropy, algebraic dynamical system, dual goldie dimension, corank, Bernoulli shift.

Introduction

Adler, Konheim and McAndrew
[
1
]
introduced the notion of topological entropy
\mathrm{h}_{\textup{top}}
for continuous self-maps of compact spaces, and they sketched a definition of the algebraic entropy
\mathrm{h}_{\textup{alg}}
for endomorphisms of abelian groups. Later on these entropy functions have been pushed forward by many different authors, e.g.,
[
3
,
18
,
23
,
21
]
(see also
[
11
]
for a complete survey).

Here we investigate the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
and the algebraic entropy
\mathrm{ent}
introduced in
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
for continuous endomorphisms of locally linearly compact vector spaces (see §
2
for all definitions and properties). In particular, the ultimate aim of this paper is to characterise the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
for linearly compact vector spaces in terms of a corank function; and this aim is achieved with Theorem C in Section 5.

Let
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
denote the category of linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-spaces and their continuous endomorphisms. A
topological flow
is a pair
(V,\phi)
consisting of an object
V
of
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
and an endomorphism
\phi
of
V
. Recall that the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
for linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-spaces is actually an invariant

\mathrm{ent}^{*}\colon\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},

of the category
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
of topological flows. Therefore, the aim is to design a
corank function

\mathrm{cork}\colon\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

for topological flows able to satisfy the following equality:

\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi),\quad\forall\ (V,\phi)\in\mathrm{ob}(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})).

The initial motivation is the well-know characterisation of the algebraic entropy of algebraic flows (i.e., pairs of type
(W,\psi)
consisting of a discrete
\mathbb{K}
-space
W
and an endomorphism
\psi
of
W
) in terms of the torsion-free rank of modules over PID; a detailed proof of this characterisation is given in
[
16
]
. Since
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
and
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect}
are known to be dual each other by Lefschetz duality (see §2.1, 2.2), it is worth looking for the topological counterpart of the characterisation proved in
[
16
]
.

After a few pages of preliminaries, we essentially start with Section
3
that is dedicated to topological flows of non-zero topological entropy. There we give an accurate description of the role played by left Bernoulli shifts in order to get
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)>0
. The main idea is to look at those closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspaces
S
of
V
such that
\phi
induces a Bernoulli-like action on the factor
V/S
. Then
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)
is not zero only when
S
turns out to be a
\phi
-cotrajectory
C(\phi,U)=U\cap\phi^{-1}U\cap\ldots\cap\phi^{-n+1}U\cap\ldots
of a fairly large linearly compact open subspace
U
of
V
; see Corollary
3.3
. Such a cotrajectory has to be both
cocyclic
(i.e.,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(V/U)=1
) and
non-stationary
(i.e.,
C(\phi,U)
is not open in
V
). But one can say even more:

Theorem A
.

For a topological flow
(V,\phi)
the following are equivalent:

a)

the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=k\in\mathbb{N}
,

b)

there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
coindependent non-stationary cocyclic
\phi
-cotrajectories
\{C(\phi,U_{i})\mid i=1,\ldots,k\}
such that

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(C,\phi\restriction_{C})=0,\quad\text{where}\quad C=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}C(\phi,U_{i}).

Moreover,
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\infty
if, and only if, there exists an infinite (countable) coindependent family of non-stationary cocyclic
\phi
-cotrajectories.

In other words, a topological flow
(V,\phi)
is “essentially” a product of one-dimensional left Bernoulli shifts as many as
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)
counts. This insight of the relationship between Bernoulli shifts and non-zero entropy topological flows will lead us towards all the remaining results of the paper.

Here “essentially” means that
(V,\phi)
is considered up to topological subflows of topological entropy
=0
. In Section
4
, we detail how the vanishing of the topological (resp. algebraic) entropy defines a torsion theory over the category of topological (resp. algebraic) flows. It is important to highlight that we cannot provide here a module-theoretic property able to capture the vanishing of the topological entropy even if such module-theoretic property does exist in the algebraic case (namely, the torsion-free rank). For this reason the construction of the torsion theory over the category of topological flows cannot be made (at the moment) independent on the topological entropy itself.

However, treating the notion of torsion axiomatically has the advantage to let us prove easily the existence of Pinsker-like constructions in our setup (see
[
2
,
10
]
for this kind of constructions in other contexts).
Namely, any topological flow
(V,\phi)
admits the largest factor

(P_{top}(V,\phi),\overline{\phi}^{t})

of zero topological entropy, called
Pinsker factor
, and any algebraic flow
(W,\psi)
admits the
Pinsker subflow

(P_{alg}(W,\psi),\psi\restriction_{a})
that is the largest subflow of algebraic entropy
=0
. Since the torsion theories over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
and
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect}
turns out to be dual each other, one deduces the following relation between Pinsker factor and Pinsker subflow:

Theorem B
.

Let
(V,\phi)
be a topological flow. Then

(P_{top}(V,\phi),\overline{\phi}^{t})\cong(V/P_{alg}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})^{\perp},\overline{\phi}^{a}),

where
\overline{\phi}^{t}\colon P_{top}(V,\phi)\to P_{top}(V,\phi)
and
\overline{\phi}^{a}\colon V/P_{alg}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})^{\perp}\to V/P_{alg}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})^{\perp}
are both induced by
\phi
. In particular,

D_{+}(V,\phi)\cong_{top}P_{alg}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{D_{+}(V,\phi)}\cong F_{+}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi}).

The
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces
D_{+}(V,\phi)
and
F_{+}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})
denote respectively the
domain of completely positive topological entropy of
(V,\phi)
and the
factor of completely positive algebraic entropy of
(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})
that will be defined in Section
4
.

Notice that the analogue
[
9
, Thm. A]
of Theorem B in the context of Pontryagin duality is dissimilarly deduced by the Bridge Theorem connecting the topological entropy and the algebraic entropy of abelian groups by means of Pontryagin duality. Notice that, since a version of the Bridge Theorem for locally linearly compact spaces is also available in
[
6
]
, one can choose to use it in order to shorten some proofs in this paper.
However we prefer to avoid any possible application of the Bridge Theorem here in order to first make the proofs less mysterious and then provide an alternative proof of the Bridge Theorem itself as an application of Theorem C at the end of the last section; this important result finds some analogues in many other contexts (see, e.g.,
[
23
,
20
,
9
,
12
,
22
]
).

We conclude the paper with the section devoted to the definition of the corank function over
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
characterising
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
. Such a function has the purpose to measure the torsion of a topological flow
(V,\phi)
, i.e., its domain of completely positive topological entropy
D_{+}(V,\phi)
.
Subsequently, the corank of a topological flow
(V,\phi)
is defined to be a dimension of
(D_{+}(V,\phi),\phi\restriction_{D_{+}(V,\phi)})
regarded as a lattice:
the corank of
(V,\phi)
is the dual Goldie dimension of the modular lattice formed by all closed
\phi
-invariant subspaces of
D_{+}(V,\phi)
.

The idea to use the dual Goldie dimension to measure
D_{+}(V,\phi)
comes from the observation that the rank of a torsion-free module over a PID coincides with its Goldie dimension. Therefore, the authors of
[
16
]
actually prove that the algebraic entropy of an algebraic flow coincides with the Goldie dimension of its factor of completely positive algebraic entropy. By the way, it is surprising how the dual Goldie dimension plays a central role in this context, since it usually does not appear in the setting of topological modules.

ery locally linearly compact space
V
together with a continuous endomorphism
\phi
corresponds univocally to a topological
\mathbb{K}[t]
-module
{}_{\phi}V
, where
\mathbb{K}[t]
denotes the (discrete) polynomial ring in the variable
t
. For an algebraic flow
(V,\phi)
, the
\mathbb{K}[t]
-module
{}_{\phi}V
is actually discrete and we recover the well-known equivalence of categories
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})\cong{}_{\mathbb{K}[t]}\mathrm{Mod}.

Finally, as a consequence of Theorem A, one proves

Theorem C
.

For every topological flow
(V,\phi)
, one has

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Linearly compact vector spaces

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a discrete field. A Hausdorff topological
\mathbb{K}
-space
V
is said to be
linearly topologized
if it admits a neighborhood basis at
0
consisting of
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces of
V
. A linearly topologized
\mathbb{K}
-space
V
is
linearly compact
if any collection of closed linear varieties of
V
with the finite intersection property has non-empty intersection. E.g., discrete vector spaces are linearly compact as well as compact linearly topologized spaces.

Here we collect a few properties that we frequently use further on with no previous acknowledgement.
Let
V,V^{\prime}
and
W
be linearly topoligized
\mathbb{K}
-spaces such that
W\leq V
, then

(LC1.)

if
W
is linearly compact, then
W
is closed in
V
;

(LC2.)

if
V
is linearly compact and
W
is closed, then
W
is linearly compact;

(LC3.)

linear compactness is preserved by continuous homomorphisms;

(LC4.)

if
V
is discrete, then
V
is linearly compact if and only if
V
has finite dimension;

(LC5.)

if
W
is closed, then
V
is linearly compact if and only if
W
and
V/W
are linearly compact;

(LC6.)

an inverse limit of linearly compact vector spaces is linearly compact;

(LC7.)

every linearly compact vector space is complete;

(LC8.)

for
V
linearly compact, any continuous homomorphism
f\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is open onto its image;

(LC9.)

the isomorphism theorems hold in
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
;

(LC10.)

every linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space is a Tychonoff product of one-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-spaces.

A topological
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
V
is
locally linearly compact
(briefly, l.l.c. ) if it admits a neighbourhood basis at
0
consisting of linearly compact open
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces. Let
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
denote the category of l.l.c.
\mathbb{K}
-spaces and their continuous homomorphisms.
Clearly,
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
and
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect}
(i.e., the category of discrete
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces) are full subcategories of
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
.

2.2
Lefschetz Duality

For an l.l.c.​ space
V
, the Lefschetz dual
\widehat{V}
is defined to be
\mathrm{CHom}(V,\mathbb{K})
endowed with the (linearly compact)-open topology. Namely, the topology on
\widehat{V}
has the family

\{A^{\perp}\mid A\leq V,\ A\ \text{linearly compact}\}

(2.1)

as neighbourhood basis at
0
, where
A^{\perp}=\{\chi\in\mathrm{CHom(V,\mathbb{K})}:\chi(A)=0\}
. Given a closed
\mathbb{K}
- subspace
U
of
V
, one has

\widehat{V/U}\cong_{top}U^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{U}\cong_{top}\widehat{V}/U^{\perp}.

(2.2)

Lefschetz dual of discrete
\mathbb{K}
-spaces is linearly compact, and vice-versa.
More generally, the dual functor

\widehat{-}:\ _{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}\to_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}

is such that
\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\widehat{-}}}\colon_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}\,\to\,_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
is naturally ismorphic to
\mathrm{id}\colon_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}\,\to\,_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
.

2.3
Topological entropy and algebraic entropy over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}

Let
V
be an l.l.c.
\mathbb{K}
-space and
\mathcal{B}(V)
denote the set of all linearly compact open
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces of
V
. Inspired by the notion of topological entropy for totally disconnected locally compact groups and their endomorphisms (see
[
13
,
15
]
), the
topological entropy
for l.l.c.
\mathbb{K}
-spaces is defined to be

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}(V)}H^{*}(\phi,U),\quad(V,\phi)\in\textup{ob}(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC})),

(2.3)

where
H^{*}(\phi,U)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\dim(U/(U\cap\phi^{-1}U\cap\ldots\cap\phi^{-n+1}U))
.

Dually, the
algebraic entropy
of
(V,\phi)\in\textup{ob}(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}))
is given by

\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi)=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{B}(V)}H(\phi,U),

(2.4)

where
H(\phi,U)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\dim((U+\phi U+\cdots+\phi^{n-1}U)/U)
.

Both
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
and
\mathrm{ent}
are invariants of the category
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC})
satisfying:

P1.

Invariance under conjugation
: for every topological isomorphism
\alpha\colon V\to V^{\prime}
one has

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V^{\prime},\alpha\phi\alpha^{-1})=\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ent}(V^{\prime},\alpha\phi\alpha^{-1})=\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi);

P2.

Monotonicity
: for any closed linear subspace
W
of
V
such that
\phi(W)\leq W
one has

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)\geq\max\{\mathrm{ent}^{*}(W,\phi\restriction_{W}),\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/W,\overline{\phi})\}\ \text{and}

\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi)\geq\max\{\mathrm{ent}(W,\phi\restriction_{W}),\mathrm{ent}(V/W,\overline{\phi})\},

where
\overline{\phi}\colon V/W\to V/W
is induced by
\phi
;

P3.

Logarithmic law
: if
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi^{k})=k\cdot\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi^{k})=k\cdot\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi);

P4.

Continuity on inverse limits
: let
\{W_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be a directed system (under inverse inclusion) of closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspaces of
V
. If
V=\varprojlim V/W_{i}
, then
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\phi)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\overline{\phi}_{W_{i}})
, where any
\overline{\phi}_{W_{i}}\colon V/W_{i}\to V/W_{i}
is the continuous endomorphism induced by
\phi
;

P5.

Continuity on direct limits
: let
V
be an l.l.c.​ vector space and
\phi\colon V\to V
a continuous endomorphism. Assume that
V
is the direct limit of a family
\{V_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspaces of
V
, and let
\phi_{i}=\phi\restriction_{V_{i}}
for all
i\in I
. Then
\mathrm{ent}(\phi)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathrm{ent}(\phi_{i});

P6.

Vanishing property
:
\mathrm{ent}^{*}\restriction_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ent}\restriction_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})}=0;

P7.

Addition Theorem
: for every closed linear subspace
W
of
V
such that
\phi(W)\leq W
one has

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{ent}^{*}(W,\phi\restriction_{W})+\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/W,\overline{\phi}),

\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{ent}(W,\phi\restriction_{W})+\mathrm{ent}(V/W,\overline{\phi}),

where
\overline{\phi}\colon V/W\to V/W
is induced by
\phi
.

Moreover, since it has been pointed out in
[
5
]
that
\mathrm{ent}\restriction_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})}
coincides with the dimension entropy
\mathrm{ent}_{dim}
studied in
[
16
]
, throughout this paper all the well-known results concerning
\mathrm{ent}_{dim}
will be automatically considered to be properties of the algebraic entropy
\mathrm{ent}\restriction_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The main ingredient in both definitions is the fact that
U\cap\phi(U)
has finite codimension in
\phi(U)
(or, equivalently,
U\cap\phi^{-1}(U)
has finite codimension in
U
). This is a phenomenon - giving
the name
\phi
-inert
to
U
- inspired by a series of papers of U. Dardano and coauthors (see the survey

[
7
]
).

Example 2.2

(Bernoulli shifts)
.

For the purposes of this paper, we consider here only one type of Bernoulli shift, which will be referred to as
1-dimensional Bernoulli shift
.

Let
V_{c}
be the linearly compact product
\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{K}
. The
left 1-dimensional Bernoulli shift
is

{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta\colon V_{c}\to V_{c},\quad(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

Therefore,
(V_{c},{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta)
is a topological flow and
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V_{c},{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta)=1
.

Dually, let
V_{d}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{K}
be equipped with the discrete topology. Thus one defines the
right (1-dimensional) Bernoulli shift
as

\beta_{\mathbb{K}}\colon V_{d}\to V_{d},\quad(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\mapsto(0,x_{0},x_{1},\dots).

In particular,

\widehat{(V_{c},{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta)}=(\widehat{V_{c}},\widehat{{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta})\cong(V_{d},\beta_{\mathbb{K}}),

and
(V_{d},\beta_{\mathbb{K}})
has algebraic entropy
=1
.

3
Counting left one-dimensional Bernoulli shifts

3.1
Coindependent families of linearly compact spaces

Let
M
be a module over an associative ring with unit. A finite set
\{N_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of proper submodules of
M
is said to be
coindependent
if

N_{i}+\bigcap_{j\neq i}N_{j}=M\quad\forall i\in I.

More generally, an arbitrary set of proper submodules of
M
is coindependent if every finite subset is.
When
I=\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to check coindependence of
\{N_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
for each
n
.

Given a coindependent finite set
\{N_{0},\dots,N_{k}\}
of proper submodules of
M
, one has a canonical isomorphism of modules

\xi_{k}\colon\frac{M}{N_{0}\cap\ldots\cap N_{k}}\longrightarrow\prod_{i=0}^{k}\frac{M}{N_{i}},

(3.1)

where
m+N_{0}\cap\ldots\cap N_{k}\mapsto(m+N_{0},\ldots,m+N_{k}),
for all
m\in M.
When
M
is a linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space and each
N_{i}
is closed in
M
, then (
3.1
) is a topological isomorphism because of the open mapping theorem (cf. LC8. in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{ss:lc}
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(V,\phi)
be a topological flow and
\{W_{0},\dots,W_{n}\}
a coindependent set of closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspaces of
V
. For
W=W_{0}\cap\ldots\cap W_{n}
one has

n+1\leq\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/W,\overline{\phi})\leq\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)

whenever
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\overline{\phi}^{i})>0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, where each
\overline{\phi}^{i}\colon V/W_{i}\to V/W_{i}
is induced by
\phi
.

Proof.

By (
3.1
), the following diagram

\textstyle{\frac{V}{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\phi}}
\scriptstyle{\xi_{n}}
\textstyle{\frac{V}{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\xi_{n}}
\textstyle{\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{V}{W_{i}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\prod\overline{\phi}^{i}}
\textstyle{\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{V}{W_{i}}}

commute, where
\overline{\phi}\colon V/W\to V/W
and
\overline{\phi}^{i}\colon V/W_{i}\to V/W_{i}
(for all
i
) are induced by
\phi
. Since
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
takes values in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, the Addition Theorem together with the invariance under conjugation yields

n+1\leq\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/W,\overline{\phi})\leq\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi).

∎

3.2
Non-stationary cocyclic
\phi
-cotrajectory

Let
(V,\phi)
be a topological flow. For
U
linearly compact open
\mathbb{K}
-subspace in
V
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
set

C_{n}(\phi,U)=U\cap\phi^{-1}U\cap\ldots\cap\phi^{-n+1}U\in\mathcal{B}(V).

(3.2)

Then one defines the
\phi
-cotrajectory of
U
to be

C(\phi,U)=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}C_{n}(\phi,U).

(3.3)

A
\phi
-cotrajectory
C(\phi,U)
is said to be
cocyclic
if
U
has codimension 1 in
V
, and
C(\phi,U)
is said to be
non-stationary
if
C(\phi,U)
is not open in
V
, i.e., the descending chain of linearly compact open spaces

U\geq\cdots\geq C_{n}(\phi,U)\geq C_{n+1}(\phi,U)\geq\cdots,

is not stationary (see
[
6
, Proposition 3.6]
). Notice that a
\phi
-cotrajectory is stationary whenever there exists some
n
such that
C_{n}(\phi,U)=C_{n+1}(\phi,U)
by
[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(V,\phi)
be a topological flow and
C(\phi,U)
a non-stationary cocyclic
\phi
-cotrajectory. Then

1.

the closed subspace
\phi^{-n}U
has codimension 1 in
V
for every
n\in N
;

2.

\mathfrak{F}=\{\phi^{-n}U\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a coindependent family in
V
;

3.

the topological entropy of
\phi
with respect to
U
is equal to 1, i.e.,
H^{*}(\phi,U)=1
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon V\to V/U
be the canonical projection. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the rank of the linear map

\pi\circ\phi^{n}\colon V\to V/U
is less or equal to
\dim(V/U)
. Hence
\dim(V/\phi^{-n}U)\leq 1
since
C(\phi,U)
is cocyclic. Moreover, for
C(\phi,U)
is non-stationary,
C_{n}(\phi,U)\neq C_{n+1}(\phi,U)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
\phi^{-n}U\neq V
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(1)
holds.

In order to prove
(2)
, by
[
17
, Lemma 1.1]
, it suffices to show that
\phi^{-n}U+C_{n}(\phi,U)=V
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. This follows easily by

Theorem A

Let

By Corollary

Now, since

\phi

4
Torsion theories and Pinsker-like constructions

4.1
Torsion theory for abelian categories

Following
[
8
]
, a torsion theory for a subcomplete abelian category
\mathcal{C}
consists of a couple
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of classes of objects of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

(I)

\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}=\{0\}
.

(II)

If
T\to A\to 0
is exact with
T\in\mathcal{T}
, then
A\in\mathcal{T}
.

(III)

If
0\to A\to F
is exact with
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
A\in\mathcal{F}
.

(IV)

For each object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
there is an exact sequence

0\to T\to X\to F\to 0,

with
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Thus
\mathcal{T}
is a
torsion class
and
\mathcal{F}
is a
torsion-free class
of
\mathcal{C}
. If both classes
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{F}
satisfy (IV) and contain with any object all its isomorphic images, then axioms (I) through (III) can be replaced by the
ortogonality axiom

(V)

\mathrm{Hom}(T,F)=0,\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T},F\in\mathcal{F}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{F}
are complete with respect to the orthogonality axiom - i.e., if an object
T
of
\mathcal{C}
is such that
\mathrm{Hom}(T,F)=0
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
T\in\mathcal{T}
; and the other way round - then
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a torsion theory (cf.
[
8
, Thm. 2.1]
). For further applications we state here the following result.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Let
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be a torsion theory of
\mathcal{C}
, and let
M\in ob(\mathcal{C})
. Then there is a unique largest subobject
M_{t}
of
M
which is a member of
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover,
M/M_{t}\in\mathcal{F}
. The object
M_{t}
can be calculated by either of the equalities

1.

M_{t}=\bigcup\{T\subseteq M\mid T\in\mathcal{T}\}
, or

2.

M_{t}=\bigcap\{S\subseteq M\mid M/S\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Hence any torsion theory over
\mathcal{C}
is uniquely determined by its torsion class:

\mathcal{F}=\{M\in ob(\mathcal{C})\mid M_{t}=0\}.

4.2
Pinsker factor of a topological flow

Since the category of topological flows can be regarded as a functor category over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
,
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
is a complete abelian category since
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
is.
Let

\mathcal{F}_{top}=\{(F,\varphi)\in ob(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}))\mid\mathrm{ent}^{*}(F,\varphi)=0\}.

(4.1)

For all the properties stated in
\lx@sectionsign
2.3
,
\mathcal{F}_{top}
is closed under taking kernels, infinite products and extensions. Thus
[
8

applies and
\mathcal{F}_{top}
is a well-defined torsion-free class on
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})

\mathcal{T}_{top}
can be obtained by the orthogonality axiom. Namely,

(T,\tau)\in\mathcal{T}_{top}
if, and only if,

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})}((T,\tau),(F,\varphi))=0,\ \forall(F,\varphi)\in\mathcal{F}_{top}.

(4.2)

A topological flow
(V,\phi)
is said to have

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/Z,\overline{\phi})\neq 0
for every closed

-invariant proper linear subspace

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)>>0
whenever

does not admit non-trivial factors of zero topological entropy.

Proposition 4.2
.

\mathcal{T}_{top}=\{(V,\phi)\in ob(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}))\mid\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)>>0\}\cup\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
(T,\tau)\in T_{top}
and suppose it admits a non-trivial factor

of zero topological entropy. Thus

and

Conversely, let

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})}((V,\phi),(F,\varphi))\neq 0.

Thus
(V,\phi)
admits a non-trivial factor

isomorphic to a subflow of

4.4

Here we relate Pinsker factors and Pinsker subflows by Lefschetz duality. Firstly observe that
(\mathcal{T}_{top},\mathcal{F}_{top})
goes over into the torsion theory

of the opposite category

\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})^{op}

\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})

\mathcal{T}^{*}_{alg}=\{(V,\phi)\in ob(\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect}))\mid\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})=0\}.

Since
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})=0
if, and only if,
\mathrm{ent}(V,\phi)=0
by Corollary
3.6
, one has
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{alg}=\mathcal{T}_{alg}
. In other words, the torsion theories
(\mathcal{T}_{top},\mathcal{F}_{top})
and

are dual one each other. Therefore one deduces by (

) the following analogue of

Theorem A

Let

D_{+}(V,\phi)\cong_{top}P_{alg}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{D_{+}(V,\phi)}\cong F_{+}(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi}).

Moreover,

, the right 1-dimensional Bernoulli shift has completely positive algebraic entropy: every non-trivial
\beta_{\mathbb{K}}
-invariant
\mathbb{K}
-subspace of
V_{d}
is isomorphic to
V_{d}
since such a subspace has countable but not finite dimension being
\beta_{\mathbb{K}}
-invariant.

Consequently, the left 1-dimensional Bernoulli shift has completely positive topological entropy. In particular, a left 1-dimensional Bernoulli shift cannot admit any proper subflow of positive topological entropy by Proposition
4.3
(2).

5
The corank of a topological flow

5.1
(Dual) Goldie dimension

Here we recall both the definitions of Goldie dimension and of dual Goldie dimension placing greater emphasis on dual Goldie dimension. These notions were firstly introduced in module theory, but modular lattices seem to be the proper setting for their definition.

Following
[
14
]
, let
(L,\vee,\wedge)
denote a modular lattice - i.e.,
x\vee(a\wedge b)=(x\vee a)\wedge b
whenever
x\leq b
- with a smallest element 0 and a greatest element 1. We start from a bit of terminology. A finite subset
\{a_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of
L\setminus\{0\}
is said to be
coindependent
if

a_{i}\vee(\bigwedge_{j\neq i}a_{j})=1\quad\forall i\in I.

(5.1)

Every element
x
of
L\setminus\{0\}
(considered as a singleton
\{x\}
) is coindependent. An arbitrary subset
A
of
L\setminus\{0\}
is coindependent if all its finite subsets are coindependent.
In a lattice
L
with
1
an element
a\in L
is called
superfluos
if
a\vee b\neq 1
for all
b\neq 1
. A lattice
L\neq\{0\}
is said to be
couniform
if every element
a\neq 1
is superfluous. An element
a\neq 1
of
L
is called
couniform
if the sublattice
[a,1]=\{x\in L\mid a\leq x\}
is couniform. Consequently one has the following well-known result.

Proposition 5.1

(see
[
14
]
)
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a modular lattice
L
with
0
and
1
.

1.

L
does not contain infinite coindependent subsets.

2.

L
contains a finite coindependent set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
with
a_{i}
couniform for every
i
and
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{n}
superfluous in
L
.

3.

The cardinality of a coindependent subset of
L
is
\leq m
for an
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Moreover, if these conditions hold and
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
is a finite coindependent subset of
L
with
a_{i}
couniform for every
i
and
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{n}
superfluous in
L
, then any other coindependent subset of
L
has cardinality
\leq n
.

Thus if the modular lattice
L
contains infinite coindependent subsets, then
L
is said to have
infinite dual Goldie dimension
; otherwise the (finite)
dual Goldie dimension
of
L
is defined to be the maximal cardinality
\mathrm{codim}\,{L}
of a finite coindependent subset of
L\setminus\{0\}
. Since the dual lattice
(L,\wedge,\vee)
of a modular lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge)
is also a modular lattice, one may define the
Goldie dimension
of
(L,\vee,\wedge)
to be the dual Goldie dimension of the dual lattice
(L,\wedge,\vee)
.

Fact 5.2

(
[
14
, Propositions 2.37 and 2.42]
)
.

For a modular lattice
L
,

1.

\mathrm{codim}\,{L}=0
if, and only if,
L
has exactly one element;

2.

\mathrm{codim}\,{L}=1
if, and only if,
L
is couniform.

Moreover, the dual version of
(1)
and
(2)
holds for the Goldie dimension.

Let
M
be a module over an associative ring with unit. Now we apply the (dual) Goldie dimension of modular lattices just introduced to the lattice
(L(M),+,\cap)
of all submodules of
M
. Thus the
dual Goldie dimension

\mathrm{codim}\,{M}
of
M
is defined to be the dual Goldie dimension of
L(M)
. In particular,
\mathrm{codim}\,{M}
turns out to be the maximal cardinality of a finite coindependent family (see §
3.1
) of proper submodules of
M
( or
\infty
). Analogously, the
Goldie dimension

\dim(M)
of
M
is the Goldie dimension of
({L}(M),+,\cap)
and it coincides with the maximal
n
(or
\infty
) such that there exists a direct sum
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\subseteq M
of non-zero submodules of
M
.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
14
, §2.7]
)
.

Let
E(M)
be the injective hull of
M
. Then the following hold:

(a)

\dim(M)=\dim(E(M))
;

(b)

\dim(M)=n<\infty
if, and only if,
E(M)
is the direct sum of
n
indecomposable modules.

Remark 5.4
.

Since the category
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})
is equivalent to
{}_{\mathbb{K}[t]}\mathrm{Mod}
, Proposition
5.3
can be applied directly to algebraic flows.

The notion of projective cover is dual to that of injective hull. Although each abstract module has an injective hull, projective covers seldom exist, therefore the latter result cannot be dualised to the dual Goldie dimension of abstract modules. Nevertheless such a statement holds over the dual category when it does exists.

5.2
Modular lattice of a topological flow

Let
(V,\phi)
be a flow over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
. We can associate to
(V,\phi)
the lattice of all closed
\phi
-invariant
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces of
V
, which will be denoted by

(\Lambda(V,\phi),+,\cap)
.
By properties LC1.–LC3., the lattice
(\Lambda(V,\phi),+,\cap)
is modular. Therefore we are allowed to compute the dual Goldie dimension of
(\Lambda(V,\phi),+,\cap)
, i.e., the maximal cardinality of a set of coindependent closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspaces of
V
. We denote it by
\mathrm{codim}\,{(}V,\phi)
.

Remark 5.5
.

Notice that, by Lefschetz duality, the dual lattice of
\Lambda(V,\phi)
is equivalent to the lattice consisting of all
\widehat{\phi}
-invariant
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces of
\widehat{V}
, say
\Lambda(\widehat{V},\widehat{\phi})
.

Proposition 5.6
.

For every discrete field
\mathbb{K}
the following hold:

1.

\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
is an abelian category with enough injectives and projectives;

2.

\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
admits projective covers.

Moreover, for every topological flow
(V,\phi)
with projective cover
(P,\rho)
one has

3.

\mathrm{codim}\,{(}V,\phi)=\mathrm{codim}\,{(}P,\rho)
;

4.

\mathrm{codim}\,{(}V,\phi)=n<\infty
iff
(P,\rho)
is the product of exactly
n
indecomposable topological flows.

Proof.

It follows by the fact that
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})
is the Lefschetz dual of
\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect})
- which is nothing but
{}_{\mathbb{K}[t]}\mathrm{Mod}
- and Proposition
5.3
.
∎

5.3
The corank function

For a topological flow
(V,\phi)
, one may consider the domain of completely positive topological entropy
(D_{+}(V,\phi),\phi\restriction_{+})
- i.e., the torsion subflow of
(V,\phi)
- and give the following definition.

Definition 5.7
.

The corank
\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)
of a topological flow
(V,\phi)
is defined to be the dual Goldie dimension of
(D_{+}(V,\phi),\phi\restriction_{+}).
Thus one obtains a corank function

\mathrm{cork}\colon\mathrm{Flow}(_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC})\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

Indeed, the corank is meant to measure the
\mathbb{K}
-subspace where the map
\phi
is actually creating disorder. By Fact
5.2
, one easily deduces the following properties:

(C1)

\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=0
if, and only if,
D_{+}(V,\phi)=0
.

(C2)

\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=1
if, and only if,
\Lambda(D_{+}(V,\phi),\phi\restriction_{+})
is couniform, i.e., every proper closed
\phi\restriction_{+}
-invariant linear subspace of
D_{+}(V,\phi)
is superfluous in
(\Lambda(D_{+}(V,\phi),\phi\restriction_{+}),+,\cap)
.

Remark 5.8
.

By analogy with the topological case, one can define the
rank
of an algebraic flow, say
\mathrm{rk}(W,\psi)
, by using the notion of Goldie dimension: this rank does value the size of the factor of completely positive algebraic entropy, i.e.,
\mathrm{rk}(W,\psi)=\dim(W,\psi):=\dim(\Lambda(F_{+}(W,\psi),\overline{\psi}^{+}))
, where
\dim
denotes the Goldie dimension. Since
F_{+}(W,\psi)
is the torsion-free factor of the algebraic flow with respect to the torsion theory
(\mathcal{T}_{alg},\mathcal{F}_{alg})
, the notion of rank so introduced simply coincides with the torsion-free rank of the algebraic flow
(W,\psi)
regarded as a module over
\mathbb{K}[t]
(see Remark
4.6
) and so it does not yield any new information.

Finally, we are ready to prove that the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}
is essentially a dimension of the topological flow.

Theorem C
.

For every topological flow
(V,\phi)
, one has

\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi).

Proof of Theorem C.

By Corollary
4.4
,
V
can be replaced by its domain of completely positive topological entropy without loss of generality.
So let assume
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)>>0
.

When
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=0
there is nothing to prove.
Thus firstly suppose
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. By Theorem B and Proposition
3.1
, one obtains
k\leq\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)\leq k
, i.e.,
k=\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)
.

Now assume
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=\infty
. By Theorem B,
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)\leq\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)
i.e.,
\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=\infty
.
Vice-versa, let
\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=\infty
. Suppose ad absurdum that
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)=k
is finite. Then there exist
k
coindependent non-stationary cocyclic
\phi
-cotrajectories
\{C_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,k\}
by Theorem B. By Proposition
3.4
,
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V/C_{i},\overline{\phi}^{i})=1
for all
i=1,\dots,k
, where
\overline{\phi}^{i}\colon V/C_{i}\to V/C_{i}
is induced by
\phi
. Since the topological entropy and the corank coincide in the finite case, one has
\mathrm{cork}(V/C_{i},\overline{\phi}^{i})=1
for all
i=1,\dots,k
by Proposition
3.4
. Therefore the lattice
\Lambda(V/C_{i},\overline{\phi}^{i})
is couniform for every
i=1,\dots,k
(see property (C2) above), i.e.,
C_{i}
is a couniform element of the lattice
\Lambda(V,\phi)
. Finally, we claim that
C=C_{1}\cap\ldots\cap C_{k}
is superfluos in the lattice
\Lambda(V,\phi)
, i.e.,
C+W\neq V
for every proper closed
\phi
-invariant linear subspace of
V
. Thus Proposition
5.1
(2) yields the contradiction:
\mathrm{cork}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{codim}\,{(}V,\phi)<\infty
.

Let us prove the claim. Let
W
be a closed
\phi
-invariant
\mathbb{K}
-subspace of
V
such that
C+W=V
. Then
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k},W\}
is a coindependent set of closed
\phi
-invariant
\mathbb{K}
-subspaces of
V
of cardinality
>\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)
. By Proposition
3.1
and
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi)>>0
, one has
W=C_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and so
W=C+W=V
.∎

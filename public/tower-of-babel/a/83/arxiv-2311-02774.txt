A stronger connection between the asymptotic rank conjecture and the set cover conjecture
Kevin Pratt

Abstract
We give a short proof that Strassen’s asymptotic rank conjecture implies that for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 there exists a ( 3 / 2 2 / 3 + ε ) n (3/2^{2/3}+\varepsilon)^{n} -time algorithm for set cover on a universe of size n n with sets of bounded size. This strengthens and simplifies a recent result of Björklund and Kaski that Strassen’s asymptotic rank conjecture implies that the set cover conjecture is false. From another perspective, we show that the set cover conjecture implies that a particular family of tensors T n ∈ ℂ N ⊗ ℂ N ⊗ ℂ N T_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{N} has asymptotic rank greater than N 1.08 N^{1.08} . Furthermore, if one could improve a known upper bound of 1 2 ​ 8 n \frac{1}{2}8^{n} on the tensor rank of T n T_{n} to 2 9 ⋅ n ​ 8 n \frac{2}{9\cdot n}8^{n} for any n n , then the set cover conjecture is false.

Given

1.1
Background on asymptotic tensor rank

We refer the reader to
[
Blä13
]
and Chapters 14 and 15 of
[

]

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}

{\mathop{\textup{}}{poly}}(n)

has

if for some choice of bases of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}

such that

for all
(i,j,k)

The following tensors are key.

Definition 1.4
.

Let

T_{k}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,T,U\in\binom{[3k]}{k}\\

Definition 1.5
.

Let
T
be a tensor. We say that
T
has rank one if
T=\sum a_{i}X_{i}\sum b_{i}Y_{i}\sum c_{i}Z_{i}
for some
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. The tensor rank of
T
, denoted
\mathbf{R}(T)
, is the minimum number

can be expressed as an
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear combination of
r

To orient the reader, it is not difficult to show that every tensor has rank at most
O(n^{2})
, and that “most” tensors meet this bound (see
[

, Theorem 20.9]
for precise bounds). It is also not difficult to see that if

\mathbf{R}(T)\geq n
.

Definition 1.6
.

The Kronecker product of
T=\sum_{i,j,k\in[n]}a_{ijk}X_{i}Y_{j}Z_{k}
and
T^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j,k\in[n]}b_{ijk}X_{i}Y_{j}Z_{k}
is the trilinear form
T\otimes T^{\prime}:{\mathbb{F}}^{n^{2}}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n^{2}}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n^{2}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
given by

T\otimes T^{\prime}:=\sum_{i,i^{\prime},j,j^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}\in[n]}a_{ijk}b_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}X_{i,i^{\prime}}Y_{j,j^{\prime}}Z_{k,k^{\prime}}.

We use the shorthand
T^{\otimes r}
to denote the
r

T
with itself.

Note that
\mathbf{R}(T\otimes T^{\prime})\leq\mathbf{R}(T)\mathbf{R}(T^{\prime})
. As a result, the following is well-defined.

Definition 1.7
.

{\utilde{\mathbf{R}}}(T):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathbf{R}(T^{\otimes r})^{1/r}
is the
asymptotic rank
of
T
.

The significance of asymptotic rank is that it that it characterizes the asymptotic complexity of computing high Kronecker powers of
T
. More formally, if
T
is concise then
{\utilde{\mathbf{R}}}(T)\leq x
implies that for every
\varepsilon>0
, one can compute
T^{\otimes r}
using
O((x+\varepsilon)^{r})
arithmetic operations.

This is not true of (non-asymptotic) tensor rank, which only characterizes multiplicative complexity.
This can be understood as a generalization of the relevance of tensor rank to fast matrix multiplication, and follows from a recursive algorithm analogous to Strassen’s algorithm
[
Str69
]
and all subsequent improvements thereon. Furthermore, this is optimal, as the computation of a trilinear form

\Omega(\mathbf{R}(T))

[

“Rank” can be replaced by “border rank” and all of our results still hold, because asymptotic rank and asymptotic border rank are equivalent

Conjecture 1.8

(Asymptotic Rank Conjecture
[
Str94
]
)
.

For every tensor
T:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}

{\utilde{\mathbf{R}}}(T)=n

That is, this posits that tensors of potentially high rank “simplify” as much as is possible under taking Kronecker powers. This conjecture is probably difficult, as a positive answer would imply that
\omega=2
and a negative answer would necessarily give

high-rank tensors

4

Amusingly, a disproof of the asymptotic rank conjecture could conceivably not tell us what the polynomial-time Turing machine producing the implied family of high-rank tensors is, but only that it exists.
, a longstanding challenge in algebraic complexity. To illustrate how poorly understood the behavior of tensor rank under powering is, as far as we are aware it is consistent with the current state of knowledge that for all

-dimensional tensors,

— in other words, we do not even know if tensors simplify as much as is possible once they are squared! More embarrassingly yet, we are not aware of

-dimensional tensors whose image contains tensors of superlinear (i.e.

Proof of
Theorem

1.9
.

Given an instance of
1.3
on a universe of size
3n_{0}
and with families
{\mathcal{F}}_{10},{\mathcal{F}}_{20},{\mathcal{F}}_{30}
, let
n:=k\lceil n_{0}/k\rceil
be the smallest multiple of

, and set
r:=n/k
. Pick any equipartition
S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}\sqcup S_{3}=[3n]\setminus[3n_{0}]
. We will consider the instance of
1.3
on
[3n]
with families
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:=\{X\cup S_{i}:X\in{\mathcal{F}}_{i0}\}
. This yields an instance of
1.3
which has a solution if and only if the original one did, which is constructible in time
{\mathop{\textup{}}{poly}}(n)
(remembering
k
is a constant), and on a negligibly larger universe (since
n<n_{0}+k
).

Let
X=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{3rk}:|(x_{1+3km},x_{2+3km},\ldots,x_{3k+3km})|=k
for
m=0,\ldots,r-1\}
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Hamming weight. That is, in each
x\in X
the
r
consecutive blocks of length
3k
contain
k
ones each. Note that

T_{k}^{\otimes r}=\sum_{(a,b,c)\in X^{3}:a\vee b\vee c=1^{3rk}}X_{a}Y_{b}Z_{c}.

The algorithm works as follows. First choose a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3n}
uniformly at random. Writing
\sigma({\mathcal{F}}):=\{\sigma(Y):Y\in{\mathcal{F}}\}
, note that
\sigma({\mathcal{F}}_{1}),\sigma({\mathcal{F}}_{2}),\sigma({\mathcal{F}}_{3})
contains a tripartition if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2},{\mathcal{F}}_{3}
did. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\prime}:=\sigma({\mathcal{F}}_{i})\cap X
. If
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}
contained no tripartition, certainly neither will
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\prime}\}
. If
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}
did contain a tripartition,
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\prime}\}
also will with probability at least

p:=\frac{(\binom{3k}{k}\binom{2k}{k})^{r}}{\binom{3n}{n}\binom{2n}{n}}

because a tripartition is sent to a uniformly random tripartition by
\sigma
, and
X
contains
(\binom{3k}{k}\binom{2k}{k})^{r}
(ordered) tripartitions.

Note that if we set all variables
X_{a},Y_{b},Z_{c}
where
a\notin{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{\prime},b\notin{\mathcal{F}}_{2}^{\prime},c\notin{\mathcal{F}}_{3}^{\prime}
to zero, the resulting restriction of
T_{k}^{\otimes r}
is identically zero if and only if
\{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{i}\}
contained a tripartition. So let
Y
be a subset of the prime field of
{\mathbb{F}}
with
|Y|=4
(taking an extension if
{\mathop{\textup{}}{char}}({\mathbb{F}})\in\{2,3\}
). Set
X_{a}=0
if
a\notin{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{\prime}
and let
X_{a}
be a uniformly random element of
Y
otherwise, and similarly for the
Y
and
Z
variables. By what we just said this is always zero on “no” instances, and by the Schwartz–Zippel lemma this is nonzero with probability at least
3/4
on ‘‘yes” instances.

Note that the use of randomization is unnecessary if
{\mathop{\textup{}}{char}}({\mathbb{F}})=0
.
By assumption, this evaluation can be done using
O(({\utilde{\mathbf{R}}}(T_{k})+\varepsilon/2)^{r})
field operations. Because asymptotic rank is invariant under field extension

, we may assume that these field operations only involve elements in

Repeat this test

{\mathcal{F}}

{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}:=\cup_{X\in{\mathcal{F}}}2^{X}
. This is done in time
|{\mathcal{F}}|\cdot{\mathop{\textup{}}{poly}}(n)

{\mathcal{F}}
contains at most
t

if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
contains at most
t
sets

[n]
. Moreover, notice that for any partition
X_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup X_{m}=[n]

such that

, and similarly with the sets indexed by

and
C
.

Next, for each
m\leq t
, compute all unions of all pairwise disjoint collections of
m

{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
, having size at most
n/3+s
. This can be done with dynamic programming in time
\binom{n}{n/3}\cdot{\mathop{\textup{}}{poly}}(n)
. Next remove from the resulting set family all sets of size less than
\lfloor n/3\rfloor-s
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{t}
be the resulting set families.

By the first paragraph, it now suffices to check for every
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
with
t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}\leq t

. For every
S\subset[n]

, and for every partition

, for

This is all done naïvely in time

{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{1}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{2}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{3}}^{\prime}

[n]

Local mirror symmetry via SYZ
Benjamin Gammage

Abstract
. In this note, we explain how mirror symmetry for basic local models in the Gross-Siebert program can be understood through the non-toric blowup construction described by Gross-Hacking-Keel. This is part of a program to understand the symplectic geometry of affine cluster varieties through their SYZ fibrations.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
53D37, 14J33

1.
Introduction

In the Gross-Siebert program, a pair of dual spaces is described by the combinatorial data of an integral affine manifold with singularities, which is supposed to be understood as the base of a Lagrangian torus fibration with singularities. Locally near a smooth point of the base,
the total space looks like the cotangent bundle of a torus, projecting to its cotangent fibers.

Near singular points in the base, the behavior of a torus fibration can become more complicated as torus fibers acquire singularities. However, in each dimension
N
, there is a finite list of such singularities
\mathfrak{s}_{n,m}
(with
n+m=N
) which are known to serve as building blocks for a wide class of integral affine manifolds. Above the singularity
\mathfrak{s}_{n,m},
the symplectic manifold is described by the local model

X_{n,m}:=\{z_{0}\cdots z_{n}=1+u_{1}+\cdots u_{m}\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{C},u_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\},

equipped with singular Lagrangian torus fibration
X_{n,m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
by

(1.1)

(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\mapsto(|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{n}|^{2},\log|u_{1}|,\ldots,\log|u_{m}|).

In the case
n=1,
this SYZ fibration on the conic bundle
X_{1,m}
was studied extensively in
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we will show (in §
4
) that the spaces
X_{n,m}
may be described using a generalization of the non-toric blowup construction of
[
21
,
22
]
.
The mirror to that construction was described in detail in
[
15
]
(following earlier works
[
36
,
29
,
31
]
); by generalizing that mirror construction to the case of
X_{n,m},

we can deduce a mirror symmetry equivalence between these spaces:

Theorem 1.2
.

There is an equivalence of categories
\textup{Fuk}(X_{m,n})\cong\textup{Coh}(X_{n,m}).

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.2
as formulated has long been expected; a proof of this theorem from a more traditional Fukaya-categorical perspective, providing a more detailed correspondence between homological mirror symmetry and SYZ geometry, will appear in
[
3
]
. Many special cases of Theorem
1.2
appear in the literature, including the case
m=-1
(the
(n-1)
-dimensional pants, studied in
[
25
]
and from the perspective of Lagrangian skeleta in
[
27
]
), and the case
n=1
(the substrate of the basic “generalized cluster transformation,” studied in
[
29
,
31
]
) – indeed, in some sense the content of this paper consists in combining those two calculations. (Other special cases of this theorem can be found in
[
6
,
32
]
.)

But the broader novelty in this work is in relating the Lagrangian skeleton of
\textup{Fuk}(X_{m,n})
to the geometry of its SYZ fibration (
1.1
). This is part of a larger program, to be discussed in §
4
, to understand Lagrangian skeleta and mirror symmetry for affine cluster varieties via their SYZ fibrations.

On the A-side, the main input to this theorem is a calculation of the Lagrangian skeleton
\mathbb{L}_{m,n}
of the Weinstein manifold
X_{m,n}.

Let

be the fan of cones in

spanned by subsets of the first

identifying each cone

Introduce the conic Lagrangian

\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}}:=\bigcup_{\sigma\subset[n]}\sigma^{\perp}\times\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}/\left(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+m}=\mathrm{T}^{*}T^{n+m},

where in the first factor we write

for the image of the subspace orthogonal to

This Lagrangian, studied in
[

,
11
,
13
]
, following earlier work
[

, is known
[

,

]
to be the skeleton of the Liouville-sectorial mirror to the toric variety
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}

[

,

,

]
, and its boundary

\partial\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}}=\bigcup_{\emptyset\neq\sigma\subset[n]}\sigma^{\perp}\times\partial^{\infty}\sigma\subset\mathrm{S}^{*}T^{n+m}

is mirror to the toric boundary
\partial\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}

[

,

]

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
16
]
, we avoided the translation

appearing in the definition of the quotient torus

in (

In this paper we prefer the more symmetrical convention for this polynomial.

Observe that

We will be interested in a second Lagrangian which involves the degeneration of the

be the “Lagrangian

studied in
[

as the skeleton of a mirror to a general linear hypersurface in

and in
[

as part of higher-dimensional cluster theory. Its Legendrian boundary

Our main result on the symplectic geometry of symplectic manifold

is a calculation of its Lagrangian skeleton

Theorem 1.6
.

The Lagrangian skeleton
\mathbb{L}_{m,n}
of

has a cover

\mathbb{L}_{m,n}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\cup_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{L}_{2},

where
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cong\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}},

\mathcal{L}_{2}\cong(\partial\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}})\times\mathbb{L}^{m}_{\operatorname{deg}},
and their intersection is
\mathcal{L}\cong(\partial\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}})\times\mathbb{R}\times T^{m}.

The proof of Theorem

.
Further discussion on the meaning of these results, and their relations to cluster theory and a generalization of

We conclude the introduction with an example recalling how our construction recovers the geometry of the “cluster local model”

Example 1.7
.

Let

so that
\mathfrak{s}_{1,1}
is the focus-focus singularity, whose local model

was first studied from the perspective of mirror symmetry in
[

admits a Lagrangian skeleton

which is the union of a torus

and a disk whose boundary is glued along a primitive homology class of

3.
Calculating the skeleton

Let
\varphi:X_{m,n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Morse-Bott Kähler potential for the Stein manifold
X_{m,n},
so that its Stein Kähler form is
\omega=dd^{c}\varphi,
which has primitive
\lambda=d^{c}\varphi.

Morse theory gives a handle presentation of the manifold
X_{m,n},
where only critical points of index up to
m+n
occur, and the union of stable manifolds for the critical points is a singular Lagrangian subset
\mathbb{L}\subset X_{m,n},
the
skeleton
of
X_{m,n}.
These Lagrangian skeleta are of great interest thanks to the fact that the Fukaya category
\textup{Fuk}(X)
of a Stein manifold
X
may be computed locally over its Lagrangian skeleton
\mathbb{L}

[
20
,
19
,
18
,
30
]
.

Let
\varphi_{1}:\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
and
\varphi_{2}:(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the respective functions

\varphi_{1}(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{i}|^{2}\right)^{2},\qquad\varphi_{2}(u):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\log|u_{i}|+\ell)^{2},

where we have fixed some
\ell\ll 0.

We will write

\varphi(z,u):=\varphi_{1}(z)+\varphi_{2}(u)

for the sum of these functions.

The function
\varphi
is natural from the perspective of the SYZ fibration
\pi:X_{m,n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
defined by

(3.1)

\pi(z,u):=(|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{m}|^{2},\log|u_{1}|,\ldots,\log|u_{m}|),

so that
\varphi
can be written as
\varphi(z,u)=d(\pi(z,u),(\vec{0},\vec{\ell})),
where
d
is the distance function on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}.
In what follows, we will write
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
for the natural coordinates on this SYZ base
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}.

Note that the general fiber of this fibration is an
(n+m)
-torus, and it degenerates where
\xi
is in the image of the map

(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}:\{1+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}=0\mid u_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\vec{u}\mapsto(\log|u_{1}|,\ldots,\log|u_{n}|)

and at least one of the
\eta_{i}
is zero.

As a symplectic hypersurface,
X_{m,n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
inherits a Stein Liouville structure from the restriction of the ambient structure on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
. For compatibility with the SYZ fibration, we would like equip
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
and
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
with the Stein structures defined by the respective potential functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}.

However, for our purposes, it will be useful to deform the Liouville forms on both
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
and
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}.
We begin with
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}.

The obvious problem with using the function
\varphi_{1}
to define the Stein structure on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
is that it is not quite a Stein potential: the form
dd^{c}\varphi_{1}
is not positive but only semipositive, vanishing when at least two of the coordinates
z_{i}
are 0. We will fix this by adding a perturbing term to
\varphi_{1}.

For
0\leq i\neq j\leq m,
let
r_{ij}:=|z_{i}|^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}
, and let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth cut-off function, such that for some fixed
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{0}>0,
the function
\chi
satisfies

\chi(t)=\begin{cases}1\text{ where }t<\alpha_{0},\\
0\text{ where }t>\alpha_{1}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.3
.

Choose
0<\epsilon\ll\alpha_{1},
and let
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}:\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function

\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}(z):=\varphi_{1}(z)+\epsilon\sum_{i\neq j}\chi(r_{ij})r_{ij}.

Then the function
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}(z)
is a Kähler potential on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}.

Proof.

First consider the regions where
\chi(r_{ij})
is constant: i.e., for each
i\neq j,
either
r_{ij}>\alpha_{1}
or
r_{ij}<\alpha_{0}.
In each case positivity of
dd^{c}\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}
is clear. In the former case, the second term vanishes, but the first term already defines a positive
(1,1)
-form. In the latter case, each term is semi-positive, and the second term is positive in those directions in which the first term isn’t, so the total sum is positive.

Thus it remains to consider the case where
\alpha_{0}<r_{ij}<\alpha_{1}.
In this region, the perturbation term in
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}
may fail to define a semi-positive
(1,1)
-form, since the function
\chi(r_{ij})
is not plurisubharmonic. However, by compactness of the interval
[\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}],
we can choose
\chi
such that its
C^{2}
norm on
[\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}]
is bounded by a constant
C
(depending on
\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0}
). Since the same is true of the function
r_{ij}
in the region
\alpha_{0}<r_{ij}<\alpha_{1},
we conclude that there exists an overall constant bounding

\left|dd^{c}\left(\chi(r_{ij})r_{ij}\right)\right|
in the region
\alpha_{0}<r_{ij}<\alpha_{1}.
On the other hand, in the region where
r_{ij}>\alpha_{0},

|dd^{c}\varphi_{1}|
is bounded below. Putting these together, we conclude that for
\epsilon
sufficiently small, the total function
\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}
will continue to define a positive
(1,1)
-form in this region.
∎

From now on, we write
\widetilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for the perturbed function

(3.4)

\widetilde{\varphi}(z,u):=\widetilde{\varphi}_{1}(z)+\varphi_{2}(u).

Lemma
3.3
ensures that this perturbation of

is the potential for a Stein structure on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}.

Both the original function

and the deformation

have a Morse-Bott manifold of minima given by the
(n+m)
-torus

\mathbb{T}:=\{\eta_{i}=0,\xi_{j}=\ell\},

and the remaining critical points of

also project under

to the

To simplify the determination of these higher-index critical points, we will imitate

1

in applying a deformation to the Liouville structure on

Within

The amoeba

Observe that the complement

For

Proposition 3.6

(

Remark 3.13

The point of Definition

Proposition 3.14
.

The family of Liouville structures interpolating between
X_{m,n}
and
\widetilde{X}_{m,n}
is a Liouville homotopy.

Proof.

For
0\leq s\leq 1,
write
X_{m,n}(s)
for the (symplectically isomorphic) family of submanifolds of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
interpolating between
X_{m,n}
and
\widetilde{X}_{m,n}
by the flow of the vector field

Observe that the Liouville form
\lambda_{s}
is the pullback to

of the Liouville form

on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n},
and therefore the Liouville vector field on
X_{m,n}(s)
is gradient-like (under the metric inherited from the Kähler metric on the ambient
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
) for the function
\widetilde{\varphi}
.

It will therefore be sufficient to show that for
N\gg 0,
the function
\widetilde{\varphi}|_{X_{s}}
has no critical points on
\widetilde{\varphi}^{-1}((N,\infty)).
This is obvious except where the
z_{i}
coordinates are 0, so we may reduce to the problem of establishing the corresponding fact for
|\operatorname{Log}-\ell|^{2}|_{P^{n-1}_{s}},
where
P^{n-1}_{s}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
is the partially tailored
(n-1)
-dimensional pants defined by the equation (
3.5
).

In other words,
in terms of the compactification
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n},

we must show that there exists a neighborhood
\overline{U}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
of
\partial\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

such that the gradient of
|\operatorname{Log}-\ell|^{2}
on
P^{n-1}_{s}
does not vanish within

for any
s.
Moreover, as this calculation, which is taking place at infinity, is insensitive to

we may as well take

for simplicity.

Let

be a point in the compactification of

inside
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
By symmetry, we may assume that

. This point is contained in
k
boundary divisors, for some

; again by symmetry, we may write

where
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}=0,
and

and we may take

to be the largest of the

.
Since at least one

must satisfy

we know in particular that

. Note also that in a neighborhood of

, the final monomial in

is a dominant term, so we have

identically 0 near

where
\psi_{n}
is the cut-off function used to define

We will now show that for small
\epsilon
, the gradient of

on

does not vanish for any

within an

– i.e., such that

for all

.
Observe that our assumptions ensure that for

near

, the germ of curve
(u_{1}+tu_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n-1},u_{n}+tu_{1}(1-s)\psi_{1})
at

lies in

and thus defines a tangent vector

; in real coordinates

we may write this tangent vector as

for some real constants

Since
|u_{n}|>\frac{1}{n}-\epsilon
and

and

we see that
by choosing

We would like a lower bound for

Since

we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(d|\operatorname{Log}|^{2}\right)(V)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\log(r_{1})}{r_{i}}+C\frac{\log(r_{n})}{r_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\frac{\log(r_{1})}{r_{i}}\right|-C\left|\frac{\log(r_{n})}{r_{n}}\right|.

Since

the first term is at least

which is large and grows monotonically as

decreases, whereas

is bounded away from 0, as desired.
∎

4.
SYZ geometry and cluster theory

In this section, we connect the results of this paper to the non-toric blowup construction of Gross-Hacking-Keel, and explain how our results fit into a program to understand mirror symmetry for a general affine cluster variety.

In
[
21
,
22
]
, it was proposed that a cluster variety
X
should be studied via its relation to a
toric model
: a toric variety
\overline{X}
containing a codimension-2 subvariety
H
inside its toric boundary divisor
D
such that
X
embeds in the blowup
\operatorname{Bl}_{H}\overline{X}
as the complement of the proper transform
\widetilde{D}
of the toric boundary divisor
D.

The requirement that the result
X
of this construction is a cluster variety imposes strong constraints on
H,
which must be defined by a character of the dense torus in its component of
D
.

In the case at hand, we relax this constraint, allowing
H
to be defined by a general linear polynomial. We also allow contributions from the places where
H
meets the singularities of the boundary divisor
D
. With these modifications to the above construction, we can produce our spaces
X_{n,m}.

Let
\overline{X}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m},
with coordinates
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}.

Write
D:=\{z_{1}\ldots z_{n}=0\}\subset\overline{X}
for the toric boundary divisor, and consider the non-toric subvariety
H=\{z_{1}\ldots z_{n}=0,\sum u_{i}=-1\}\subset D
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Bl}_{H}\overline{X}\setminus\widetilde{D}
be the result of blowing up
H
and then deleting the proper transform of the boundary divisor
D
. Then
X\cong X_{n,m}
.

Proof.

Let
f=z_{1}\cdots z_{n}
and
g=1+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{m}.
The center
H
is defined by the equations
f=g=0,
so that blowing up
H
involves introducing
\frac{f}{g},\frac{g}{f}
into the ring of functions of
\overline{X}.
Deleting the proper transform of
D
means it is sufficient to introduce the function
\frac{g}{f}
into the coordinate ring. If we denote this new variable by
z_{0},
then the coordinate ring of
X
is
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},u_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,u_{m}^{\pm}][z_{0}]/(z_{0}f=g)=\mathbb{C}[X_{n,m}].

∎

The equation
z_{0}\cdot z_{1}\cdots z_{n}=1+u_{1}+\cdots u_{m}
should be understood as a generalization of the usual “cluster transformation equation”
z_{0}z_{1}=1+u.
Note that in this simpler case, the variable
z_{1}
is obtained from
z_{0}
by a cluster mutation, whereas in the more general situation considered in this paper, there are
n+1

n

On the symplectic side, this corresponds to the fact that the singular locus
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
divides
\mathcal{H}
into
n

3
, we distinguished one of those chambers (the “all-negative” orthant), but we could equally have begun with a torus fiber in one of the other chambers instead. The difference between the torus fibers in the various chambers is a generalization of the familiar “Clifford/Chekanov” distinction in cluster symplectic geometry.

On the B-side, as we have seen, this new feature is due to the fact that the center
H
of the GHK blowup intersects the singular locus of the divisor
D.
Keeping track of contributions from these intersections is a crucial step in generalizing the results of
[
15
]
, which disregarded those contributions.

The results of this paper therefore suggest a strategy for studying mirror symmetry for an affine cluster variety
X
via its SYZ fibration. The SYZ base should have a presentation by gluing together local pieces modeled by the singularities
\mathfrak{s}_{n,m}
(or products of these). Each singularity has a monodromy-invariant space
\mathcal{H},
and if there is a point in the intersection of all of these spaces, it should play the rôle of the torus
\mathbb{T}_{\emptyset}
in the skeleton
\mathbb{L}
of
X,
with the remainder of the skeleton obtained by attaching and collapsing subtori in a manner prescribed by the geometry of the base, locally agreeing wih the construction in this paper (as illustrated in Figure
1
). This presentation should match a presentation of the mirror space
X^{\vee}
in terms of the non-toric blowup construction of Proposition
4.1

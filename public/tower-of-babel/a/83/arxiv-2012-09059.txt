A Galilei Invariant Version of Yong’s Model
Heinrich Freistühler

Abstract




In his pioneering paper
[
31
]
, Yong has described the dynamics of a compressible fluid
with Maxwell delayed viscosity as a symmetric hyperbolic system of balance laws, and shown
that the solutions of this system tend to solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations.

The original main purpose of the present note was to propose a Galilei invariant version of
Yong’s model, as a system of balance laws (while also communicating some simple observations on
its shock waves). This has changed in the course of time.

The model.

For a barotropic fluid,
e=e(v),v=1/\rho,
and
p=p(v)=-e^{\prime}(v)
, with
-p^{\prime}(v)=e^{\prime\prime}(v)>0

consider the equations of motion

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho_{t}+(\rho u^{j})_{,j}&=0\\
(\rho u^{i})_{t}+(\rho u^{j}u^{i}+p\delta^{ij})_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\sigma^{ij}+\tau\delta^{ij})_{,j}&=0\\
(\rho\sigma^{kl})_{t}+(\rho u^{j}\sigma^{kl})_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}c^{klj}_{i}u^{i}_{,j}&=-\frac{\sigma^{kl}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}}\\
(\rho\tau)_{t}+(\rho u^{j}\tau)_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}u^{j}_{,j}&=-\frac{\tau}{\nu\epsilon^{2}}\end{aligned}

(1)

with

c^{klj}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{k}_{i}\delta^{lj}+\delta^{l}_{i}\delta^{kj})-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{r}_{i}\delta^{j}_{r}\delta^{kl},

and define the total energy and its flux as

\displaystyle E=\rho(e+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}u^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{kl}\sigma^{kl}+\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}),\quad F^{j}=((E+p)\delta^{ij}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\sigma^{ij}+\tau\delta^{ij}))u_{i}.

(2)

Proof.

(i) Noting that

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma^{kl}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}},

\displaystyle\rho(\tau_{t}+u^{j}\tau)_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}u^{j}_{,j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau}{\nu\epsilon^{2}},

and

\sigma_{kl}c_{i}^{klj}=\sigma_{kl}(\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{k}_{i}\delta^{lj}+\delta^{l}_{i}\delta^{kj})-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{r}_{i}\delta^{j}_{r}\delta^{kl})=\sigma_{i}^{j},

one finds

\displaystyle E_{t}+F^{j}_{,j}

\displaystyle=\rho(e+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}u^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{kl}\sigma^{kl}+\tau^{2})_{t}+\rho u^{j}(e+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}u^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{kl}\sigma^{kl}+\tau^{2})_{,j}+(pu^{j})_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((\sigma^{ij}+\tau\delta^{ij})u_{i})_{,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{\rho}(\rho_{t}+u^{j}\rho_{,j})+\rho u_{i}(u^{i}_{t}+u^{j}u^{i}_{,j})+\rho\sigma_{kl}(\sigma^{kl}_{t}+u^{j}\sigma^{kl}_{,j})+\rho\tau(\tau_{t}+u^{j}\tau_{,j})+(pu^{j})_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((\sigma^{ij}+\tau\delta^{ij})u_{i})_{,j}

\displaystyle=\!-pu^{j}_{,j}\!\!-u_{i}(\delta^{ij}p_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\sigma^{ij}\!+\!\tau\delta^{ij})_{,j})-\sigma_{kl}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}c^{klj}_{i}u^{i}_{,j}\!+\!\frac{\sigma^{kl}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}})-\tau(\frac{1}{\epsilon}u^{j}_{,j}\!+\!\frac{\tau}{\nu\epsilon^{2}})+(pu^{j})_{,j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}((\sigma^{ij}\!+\!\tau\delta^{ij})u_{i})_{,j}

\displaystyle=-u_{i}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sigma^{ij}_{,j})-\sigma_{kl}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}c^{klj}_{i}u^{i}_{,j})+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\sigma^{ij}u_{i})_{,j}-\sigma_{kl}\frac{\sigma^{kl}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}}-u_{i}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\tau\delta^{ij})_{,j})-\tau(\frac{1}{\epsilon}u^{j}_{,j}+\frac{\tau}{\nu\epsilon^{2}})+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(\tau\delta^{ij}u_{i})_{,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}(-u_{i}\sigma^{ij}_{,j}-\sigma^{j}_{i}u^{i}_{,j}+(\sigma^{ij}u_{i})_{,j})-\sigma_{kl}\frac{\sigma^{kl}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(-u_{i}(\tau\delta^{ij})_{,j}-\tau u^{j}_{,j}+(\tau\delta^{ij}u_{i})_{,j})-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\nu\epsilon^{2}}

This proves (

Shock waves.
For general fluids with convex internal energy,
e^{\prime\prime}(v)>0
or

D^{2}e(v,\varsigma)>0
as considered
already so far, both the barotropic and the nonbarotropic Navier-Stokes equations support
shock waves in the sense of heteroclinic traveling wave solutions

\displaystyle(\rho(t,x),u(t,x))=(R(x\cdot n-ct),U(x\cdot n-ct))

(15)

or

\displaystyle(\rho(t,x),u(t,x),\theta(t,x))=(R(x\cdot n-ct),U(x\cdot n-ct),\Theta(x\cdot n-ct)),

(16)

respectively
[
9
,
28
,
2
]
, where
n\in S^{d-1}
is the direction and
c
the speed
of propagation and
(R,U)
, or
(R,U,\Theta)
respectively, is the “profile” of the shock,
a heteroclinic solution of the associated ODE, with limits

\displaystyle(R,U)(\pm\infty)=(\rho^{\pm},u^{\pm})\!\text{ or }\!(R,U,\Theta)(\pm\infty)=(\rho^{\pm},u^{\pm},\theta^{\pm}),\ \!\text{ respectively,}

(17)

that, as the respective pairs of downstream and upstream states, fulfil the Rankine-Hugoniot jump
conditions.
The time-asymptotic stability of these viscous shock waves against planar (“longitudinal“,
“onedimensional”) perturbations has early on been studied in

[
11
,
15
,
16
,
29
]
and then, Zumbrun et al. have shown
[
33
,
22
,
21
,
20
]

that the nonlinear stability
reduces completely to the so-called Evans function condition, that is, the requirement that
the Evans function
D=D^{v}
, a certain analytic function defined on an open superset of the closed
right half
\mathbb{H}=\{\lambda:\text{Re}(\lambda)\geq 0\}
of
\mathbb{C}
(cf., e. g.,
[
7
]
)
has precisely one zero on
\mathbb{H}
, namely at
0
,
and this zero is simple.

Zumbrun and co-workers have also studied shock profiles in systems of balance
laws (“relaxed conservation laws“)
[
23
,
25
,
19
,
18
]
, again with the result that the nonlinear
stability reduces to the Evans function condition, now applied to
D=D^{r}
, the relaxation
counterpart of the viscous Evans function
D^{v}
.

Now, (
1
) and (
8
) are relaxed systems of conservation laws and as such obvious
candidates for the application of Zumbrun’s stability theory. We observe, first in one space dimension:

Theorem 3
.

Consider any viscous shock wave solution
(R,U)
or
(R,U,\Theta)

of (
4

13

(i) For sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, also the respective relaxed system, i.e., (

(

(R_{\epsilon},U_{\epsilon},\Sigma_{\epsilon},T_{\epsilon})

with

(R_{\epsilon},U_{\epsilon})(\pm\infty)=(R,U)(\pm\infty)\quad\text{or}\quad(R_{\epsilon},U_{\epsilon},\Theta_{\epsilon})(\pm\infty)=(R,U,\Theta)(\pm\infty),\ \ \text{respectively.}

and

\Sigma_{\epsilon}(\pm\infty)=T_{\epsilon}(\pm\infty)=0;

these relaxation profiles are regular perturbations of the Navier-Stokes profiles,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}(R_{\epsilon},U_{\epsilon},\Sigma_{\epsilon}+T_{\epsilon})=(R,U,-{\tilde{\mu}}U^{\prime})\text{ or }\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}(R_{\epsilon},U_{\epsilon},\Theta_{\epsilon},\Sigma_{\epsilon}+T_{\epsilon})=(R,U,\Theta,-{\tilde{\mu}}U^{\prime}),\text{respectively.}

(ii) There exist an
\bar{\epsilon}>0
and an open superset
\Lambda

\mathbb{H}

\mathbb{C}

Evans function
D^{v}
of the Navier-Stokes shock profile

(18)

Proof.

We discuss only the barotropic case (as the nonbarotropic case can be treated in complete analogy).
Rescaling (and noticing that in 1D, setting
\tilde{\mu}=(2/3)\mu+\nu
,
\tau
is redundant)
we see that system (
1

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}&=0\\
(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2}+p(1/\rho))+\sigma)_{x}&=0\\
\epsilon((\rho\sigma)_{t}+(\rho u\sigma)_{x})+u_{x}&=-\frac{\sigma}{\tilde{\mu}}.\end{aligned}

(19)

(i) Choosing, due to Galilean invariance w. l. o. g.,
n=1
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (RU)^{\prime}&=0\\
(RU^{2}+p(1/R)+\Sigma)^{\prime}&=0\\
\epsilon(RU\Sigma)^{\prime}+U^{\prime}&=-\frac{\Sigma}{\tilde{\mu}}.\end{aligned}

\epsilon\to 0

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (RU)^{\prime}&=0\\
(RU^{2}+p(1/R)+\Sigma)^{\prime}&=0\\
U^{\prime}&=-\frac{\Sigma}{\tilde{\mu}},\end{aligned}

In both ODEs,

vanishes at the rest points; in the latter one,

identically

its orbit is a

Models like the above belong to the realm of rational extended thermodynamics

Exactly that model was indeed proposed almost 40 years ago by Ruggeri

Definition 1
.

(i) One calls
Y=(Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{n})

Godunov variables

and a vector
(X^{\alpha}(Y))_{\alpha=0,....,3}
a

4-potential
for a symmetric system of conservation laws

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}F^{\alpha b}(Y)=I^{\alpha b}(Y),\quad b=0,\ldots,n,

(24)

if the spatiotemporal fluxes
F^{\alpha b}
derive from
X^{\alpha}
as

\displaystyle F^{\alpha b}(Y)=\frac{\partial X^{\alpha}(Y)}{\partial Y_{b}},\quad\alpha=0,...,3,\ b=0,...,n.

(25)

(ii) In that case, system (
24
) is called
symmetric-hyperbolic
if

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}X^{0}(Y)}{\partial Y_{b}\partial Y_{c}}\right)_{b,c=0,\ldots,n}\quad\text{is definite.}

(26)

(iii) In the situation of (i), a scalar function
X(Y)
is called a
protopotential
for
system (
24
) if

X^{\alpha}(Y)=\frac{\partial X(Y)}{\partial Y_{\alpha}}.

(iv) If a system as in (i) is Lorentz invariant, it is called
causal
if

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}X^{\alpha}(Y)}{\partial Y_{b}\partial Y_{c}}T_{\alpha}\right)_{b,c=0,\ldots,n}\quad\text{is definite for all }T_{\alpha}\text{ with }T_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}<0.

(27)

For the Euler equations of classical inviscid fluid dynamics, the Godunov variables

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}\equiv\psi-\frac{u^{2}}{2\theta},\ \tilde{u}^{i}\equiv\frac{u^{i}}{\theta},\ \tilde{\theta}\equiv\frac{1}{\theta}

(28)

(where
\psi=g/\theta
with

the chemical potential)
and the corresponding 4-potential

\displaystyle X_{E}^{0}=\frac{p}{\theta},\quad X_{E}^{i}=\frac{p}{\theta}u^{i}

(29)

were proposed by Godunov
[
10
]
(while the production term is
I_{E}^{\alpha b}=0

in that case).
In
[
26
]
, Ruggeri augmented the list (

) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi},\ \tilde{u}^{i},\ \tilde{\theta},\ \tilde{\sigma}^{ij}\equiv\frac{\sigma^{ij}}{\theta},\ \tilde{\tau}\equiv\frac{\tau}{\theta},\ \tilde{q}^{i}\equiv\frac{q^{i}}{\theta^{2}},

(30)

and showed two things:
(a) With Godunov variables (
30
), the 4-potential

\displaystyle X^{0}=\frac{p}{\theta},\quad X^{i}=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\left(p\delta^{ij}-(\sigma^{ij}+\tau\delta^{ij)}\right)u_{j}+q^{i}\right),

(31)

and the production term

\displaystyle(I^{b})_{b=0,...,13}=(0_{1},0_{3},0_{1},-\frac{1}{\theta\eta_{Q}}q,\frac{1}{\theta\eta_{S}}\sigma,\frac{1}{\theta\eta_{B}}\tau),

(32)

system (
24
), (

equations. In (

) and (
31

an equation of state

\displaystyle p=\hat{p}(\theta,\psi).

(33)

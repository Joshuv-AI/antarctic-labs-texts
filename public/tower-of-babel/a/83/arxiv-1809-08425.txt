Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics and Donaldson’s functional for vector bundles
Yoshinori Hashimoto, Julien Keller

Abstract




Abstract.

For a holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a polarised Kähler manifold, we establish a direct link between the slope stability of
\mathcal{E}
and the asymptotic behaviour of Donaldson’s functional, by defining the Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics. In particular, we provide an explicit estimate which proves that Donaldson’s functional is coercive on the set of Fubini–Study metrics if
\mathcal{E}
is slope stable, and give a new proof of Hermitian–Einstein metrics implying slope stability.

Keywords.
holomorphic vector bundle, Quot scheme, Fubini–Study metrics, stability, Hermitian–Einstein metrics, Donaldson functional

1. Introduction

Overview

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a polarised smooth complex projective variety
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
. A fundamental result connecting differential and algebraic geometry states that
\mathcal{E}
admits Hermitian–Einstein metrics if and only if it is slope polystable (this is also called the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem or the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence in the literature).

One direction of this correspondence was proved by Kobayashi
[
Kob82
]
and Lübke
[
Lüb83
]
, and the other, arguably harder, direction was proved by Donaldson
[
Don83
,
Don85
,
Don87
]
; it was also extended to compact Kähler manifolds by Uhlenbeck and Yau
[
UY86
,
UY89
]
. Fundamental as they are, their argument connecting the differential geometry of Hermitian–Einstein metrics and the algebraic geometry of slope stability is rather technical, as we shall briefly review later.

The aim of this paper is to provide the first step towards establishing a more direct link between these two concepts, from the
variational
point of view of a functional defined by Donaldson
[
Don85
]
, whose critical point, if it exists, is the Hermitian–Einstein metric. More precisely, we study the asymptotic behaviour of Donaldson’s functional
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
on the set of Fubini–Study metrics
\mathcal{H}_{k}
, which is dense in the space of all Hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{E}
, and prove that it is controlled by the algebro-geometric slope of certain filtrations of
\mathcal{E}
by subsheaves (see Definition
5.3
). While the precise statement will be given in Theorems
7.2
and
7.3
in Section
7
, one of our main conclusions can be summarised in the following form.

Theorem
.

Suppose that we take
k\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\mathcal{E}(k):=\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(k)
is globally generated. Let
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a family of Fubini–Study metrics on
\mathcal{E}
, emanating from a reference metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
, defined by a 1-parameter subgroup
\sigma:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
in the category of algebraic groups. Then there exists an integrally graded filtration
\{\mathcal{E}_{\leq q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathcal{E}
by saturated subsheaves such that

(A)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})}{t}=2\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{\leq q})\left(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}_{\leq q})\right),

where
\mu(\mathcal{E})
(resp.
\mu(\mathcal{E}_{\leq q})
) is the slope of
\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}_{\leq q}
).

The actual results that we prove in Theorems
7.2
and
7.3
are stronger in various ways; in particular, we can relax the condition on the 1-parameter subgroup
\sigma
in the above, so that the collection of families of Fubini–Study metrics
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
as above exhausts a dense subset of the space of Hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{E}
; see (
2.9
).

In particular, we prove that the slope stability of
\mathcal{E}
implies that Donaldson’s functional is coercive on the set of Fubini-Study metrics (Corollary
7.5
). Moreover, we do so by making practically no use of the PDE theory.

An application of the above results provided in this paper is a new proof of the theorem of Kobayashi
[
Kob82
]
and Lübke
[
Lüb83
]
, which states that the existence of Hermitian–Einstein metrics implies the slope stability of
\mathcal{E}
(Section
8
). The converse, probably more interesting, direction of the correspondence is treated in the sequel to this paper
[
HK19
]
. More specifically, in
[
HK19
]
we provide an alternative proof of the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem, which asserts the existence of Hermitian–Einstein metrics on slope stable vector bundles, by assuming that a “uniform” version of the inequality in Theorem
7.2
holds; the novelty of this alternative proof is that it relies much less on analysis and PDE theory by using instead the methods established in this paper (see below and
[
HK19
]
for more details).

The key new definitions in achieving the results of the current paper are the
renormalised Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics
(Section
3
) and the
non-Archimedean Donaldson functional
(Section
5
), which relate a certain limit of Fubini–Study metrics to the flat limit in the Quot-scheme, thereby providing an algebro-geometric description of it. We believe that they are interesting in their own right and have a range of potential applications,
e.g.
the metric study of Gieseker stability notion for Higgs bundles initiated in
[
GFKR16
]
.

Comparison to other works

In order to clarify the novelty of our method, we compare our work to the well-established results concerning the correspondence between Hermitian–Einstein metrics and slope stability.

The crux of the theorem of Kobayashi
[
Kob82
]
and Lübke
[
Lüb83
]
is an application of the Gauss–Codazzi formula, together with a sheaf-theoretic argument which is rather technical (see also
[
Kob87
, Section 5.8]
). While our alternative proof (in Section
8
) is perhaps no simpler in terms of technicality, we believe that it is more
geometric
, as the proof reduces to studying the asymptotic behaviour of a convex energy functional.

The other direction of the correspondence, established by Donaldson
[
Don83
,
Don85
,
Don87
]
and Uhlenbeck–Yau
[
UY86
,
UY89
]
, rests on a sophisticated application of the nonlinear PDE theory. Both of their works can be described as a “continuous deformation” from an initial metric to the Hermitian–Einstein metric. In both cases, it aims to obtain the Hermitian–Einstein metric at the limit of such continuous deformations, where the deformation process and the limiting behaviour are analysed by means of the nonlinear PDE theory. When the bundle
\mathcal{E}
is slope unstable, both methods prove the existence of a destabilising subsheaf. Donaldson uses the Yang–Mills flow and argues by applying the Mehta–Ramanathan theorem and the induction on the dimension of the variety (see also the recent complementary results on the Yang–Mills flow
[
DW04
,
Jac15
,
Jac16
,
Sib15
,
SW15
]
and references therein). The continuity method of Uhlenbeck–Yau relies on a certain compactness result for treating the limit, but it is well known that the argument is highly technical (see also
[
Pop05
]
).

Our work, on the other hand, relies much less on analysis. Indeed, in this paper and the sequel
[
HK19
]
, the analysis that we use is mostly elementary, except for that the asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel (the so-called Tian-Yau-Zelditch expansion), or its well-known consequence that the set of Fubini–Study metrics is dense in the space of Hermitian metrics (
2.9
), will play a crucially important role in
[
HK19
]
; it also implies that our results in this paper cover a dense subset of the space of Hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{E}
. It seems interesting to point out that we establish a more direct relationship between slope stability and Donaldson’s functional by
not
relying much on analysis, especially the PDE techniques.

While our work has an obvious relationship to many classical results on holomorphic vector bundles, it is important to note that it is also inspired by the recent advances on the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture, relating the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metrics to
K
-stability. More precisely, our work can be regarded as a vector-bundle analogue of the well-known results due to Paul
[
Pau12
]
, Paul–Tian
[
PT04
,
PT09
]
and Phong–Ross–Sturm
[
PRS08
]
on the asymptotic behaviour of the Mabuchi energy on the set of Fubini–Study metrics, leading to the work of Boucksom–Hisamoto–Jonsson
[
BHJ17
,
BHJ19
]
. Recall that Boucksom–Hisamoto–Jonsson
[
BHJ17
,
BHJ19
]
identified the asymptotic slope of various energy functionals as a “non-Archimedean” functional that is defined in terms of algebro-geometric intersection formulae (see also
e.g.

[
Bou18
]
). Their method was applied to the problem of Kähler–Einstein metrics by Berman–Boucksom–Jonsson
[
BBJ21
]
. While their influence on our work should be clear, it is perhaps worth mentioning that we can draw parallels between many results and concepts in cscK metrics (varieties) and Hermitian–Einstein metrics (vector bundles), which is explained in more details in the sequel
[
HK21
]
.

Likewise, our proof of Hermitian–Einstein metrics implying slope stability is similar to many established results in cscK metrics, such as Berman–Darvas–Lu
[
BDL20
]
, Dervan–Ross
[
DR17
]
, and Sjöström Dyrefelt
[
S-D18
]
; the existence of canonical metrics implying stability is proved by evaluating the asymptotic behaviour of appropriate energy functionals (Mabuchi energy for cscK metrics, Donaldson’s functional for Hermitian–Einstein metrics). Indeed, if the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is unstable and destabilised by a subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we prove that we have a 1-parameter subgroup
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
(see Definition
6.1
) along which the Donaldson functional is unbounded below, by showing that the right hand side of the equation (
A
) for
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}
is a multiple of
\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{F})
and hence is negative (see Proposition
6.2
and Section
8
).

Finally, we note that a specific filtration of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
, called the Harder–Narasimhan filtration, played an important role in the study of Hermitian–Einstein metrics since the seminal work of Atiyah–Bott
[
AB83
]
. A novel point of our work could be that we consider
arbitrary
filtrations associated to the Quot-schemes, which can be compared to
[
Don05
, Section 2]
.

Organisation of the paper

In Section
2
, we introduce some background materials on Quot-schemes, Fubini–Study metrics, and the Donaldson functional. In Section
3
, we define the renormalised Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics, which provides us with a link between differential and algebraic geometry. This machinery is applied in Section
4
, in which we evaluate the asymptotic behaviour of the Donaldson functional; in particular, we prove that it can be controlled by the algebro-geometric data. In Section
5
, we define the non-Archimedean Donaldson functional in terms of filtrations of
\mathcal{E}
by subsheaves. In Section
6
, we look at a particular class of filtrations, which we call the two-step filtrations, defined by a saturated subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}
. In Section
7
, we summarise the main results of the paper (Theorems
7.2
,
7.3
, and Corollary
7.5
). In Section
8
, we apply them to provide an alternative proof of the theorem of Kobayashi and Lübke.

Notation

We shall consistently write
(X,L)
for a polarised smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
of complex dimension
n
, where we further assume that
L
is very ample. We work with a fixed Kähler metric
\omega\in c_{1}(L)
on
X
defined by a Hermitian metric
h_{L}
on
L
. We often write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(k)
for
L^{\otimes k}
.

We write
\mathcal{O}_{X}
for the sheaf of rings of holomorphic functions on
X
,
C^{\infty}_{X}
for the one of
\mathbb{C}
-valued
C^{\infty}
-functions on
X
.

Throughout in this paper, coherent sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules will be denoted by calligraphic letters (
e.g.

\mathcal{E}
). Block letters (
e.g.

E
) will be reserved for complex
C^{\infty}
-vector bundles. This leads to an unavoidable clash of notation for a holomorphic vector bundle, but we use the following convention: both
\mathcal{E}
and
E
will be used to denote a holomorphic vector bundle, but
E
will be treated as a
C^{\infty}
-object whereas
\mathcal{E}
is meant to be a sheaf. A
C^{\infty}
-vector bundles is clearly a locally free sheaf of
C^{\infty}_{X}
-modules, but when we say “locally free”, it is always understood that it is locally free as a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

The symbol
\vee
is used for the dual; for example, for a vector space
V
, we will write
V^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
and for a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, we write
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Also, for a
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle
F
, we write
F^{\vee}:=\mathcal{H}om_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(F,C^{\infty}_{X})
. The symbol
*
is reserved for the metric dual, with respect to some metric which will be specified in the text.

Given a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules,
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})=H^{0}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the set of global sections of
\mathcal{F}
; for example, we shall often write
H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))
for
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes L^{\otimes k})
for any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
. We write
\Gamma_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(X,F)=\Gamma_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(F)
for the one for the
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle
F
. Following the standard notation, we also write
\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F}):=\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{F}
and
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F}):=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F}))
. When
\mathcal{F}
is locally free, by regarding it as a
C^{\infty}
-complex vector bundle, we write
\mathrm{End}_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(F)
for the set of
C^{\infty}
-sections of the vector bundle
F^{\vee}\otimes F
.

2.1. Slope stability

Let
(X,L)
be a polarised smooth projective variety of complex dimension
n
, and
\mathcal{E}
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
of rank
r
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
. Its
slope

\mu(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{L}(\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{Q}
is defined as

\mu(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{\deg(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})},

where
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{N}
is the rank of
\mathcal{F}
(assumed to be non-zero) where it is locally free (
cf.

[
HL10
, p.11]
), and
\deg(\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{Z}
is defined as
\intop\nolimits_{X}c_{1}(\det\mathcal{F})c_{1}(L)^{n-1}/(n-1)!
(where
\det\mathcal{F}
is a line bundle defined in terms of a locally free resolution of
\mathcal{F}
, see
[
KM76
]
,
[
Kob87
, V.6]
for details).

In general we should define the rank and degree in terms of the coefficients of the Hilbert polynomial
[
HL10
, Definitions 1.2.2 and 1.2.11]
and hence they are a priori rational numbers. For us, however, they are defined as integers and the above definition suffices since
X
is smooth.

The following stability notion was first introduced by Mumford for Riemann surfaces, and was generalised to higher dimensional varieties by Takemoto by choosing a polarisation
L
.

Definition 2.2

(Slope stability)
.

A holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is said to be
slope stable
(or Mumford–Takemoto stable) if for any coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
with
0<\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})<\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})
we have
\mu(\mathcal{E})>\mu(\mathcal{F})
.
\mathcal{E}
is said to be
slope semistable
if the same condition holds with non-strict inequality, and
slope polystable
if it is a direct sum of slope stable bundles with the same slope.

Definition 2.3
.

A holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is said to be

(1)

reducible
if it can be written as a direct sum of two or more nontrivial holomorphic subbundles as
\mathcal{E}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j}\mathcal{E}_{j}
;

(2)

irreducible
if it is not reducible;

(3)

simple
if
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{C}
.

It follows immediately that simple vector bundles are irreducible. The following fact is well known.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
cf.

[
Kob87
, Corollary V.7.14]
)

If
\mathcal{E}
is slope stable, then it is simple.

Definition 2.5
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is called
reflexive
if it is isomorphic to its
reflexive hull

\mathcal{F}^{\vee\vee}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
is called
saturated
if
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free. Any subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
has a minimal saturated subsheaf containing
\mathcal{F}
, called the
saturation
of
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{E}
, defined as the kernel of the surjection
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}\to(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})/T(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})
, where
T(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})
is the maximal torsion subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}

[
HL10
, Section 1.1]
.

It is well known that saturated subsheaves of a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
are reflexive (
cf.

[
Kob87
, Proposition 5.5.22]
). An important fact is the following.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
cf.

[
Kob87
, Corollaries 5.5.11, 5.5.15, and 5.5.20]
)

Every coherent sheaf is locally free outside of a Zariski closed subset of codimension at least 1. Moreover, torsion-free
(
resp. reflexive
)
sheaves are locally free outside of a Zariski closed subset of codimension at least
2

(
resp.
3)
.

We have the following lemma, which follows from the well-known fact that, in order to test slope stability of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
, it is sufficient to consider its saturated subsheaves
[
Kob87
, Proposition 5.7.6]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

\mathcal{E}
is slope stable if and only if
\mu(\mathcal{E})>\mu(\mathcal{F})
for any saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
with
0<\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})<\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})
.

2.2. Filtrations and Quot-scheme limit

We recall some basic definitions and results that concern Quot-schemes. We refer to the reference book of Huybrechts and Lehn
[
HL10
]
for more details.

Definition 2.9
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
k
-regular
if
H^{i}(\mathcal{F}(k-i))=0
for all
i>0
. The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
\mathcal{F}
is the integer defined by

\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{F}):=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\mathcal{F}\text{ is $k$-regular.}\}.

By Serre’s vanishing theorem, any coherent sheaf is
k
-regular for
k
large enough and thus its regularity is well defined. Recall
[
HL10
, Lemma 1.7.2]
that if
\mathcal{F}
is
k
-regular, then
\mathcal{F}
is
k^{\prime}
-regular for all
k^{\prime}\geq k
and
\mathcal{F}(k)
is globally generated. Moreover, the natural homomorphism

H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(l))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k+l))

is surjective for
l\geq 0
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{F}
is
k
-regular,
\mathcal{F}
can be written as a quotient

(2.1)

\rho:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}

where the vector space
V:=H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))
has dimension
P_{\mathcal{F}}(k)
, the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{F}
.

We now consider the holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. We take
k\geq\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{E})
so that
\mathcal{E}
is given as a quotient
\rho:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\to\mathcal{E}
, where

V=H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k)),\quad\dim V=P_{\mathcal{E}}(k)=h^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k)).

In other words,
\rho:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\to\mathcal{E}
defines a point in the Quot-scheme
\mathrm{Quot}(\mathcal{Q},P_{\mathcal{E}})
where we have set
\mathcal{Q}:=V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)
. The reader is referred to
[
HL10
, Chapter 2]
for more details on Quot-schemes.

Suppose that we are given a 1-parameter subgroup (1-PS for short)
\sigma_{T}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\ni T\mapsto\sigma_{T}\in\SL(V)
. This defines a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-orbit in
\mathrm{Quot}(\mathcal{Q},P_{\mathcal{E}})
defined by
\{\rho\circ\sigma_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{C}^{*}}
. We can give an explicit description of the limit of this orbit as
T\to 0
, following
[
HL10
, Section 4.4]
.

Recall that giving a 1-PS
\sigma_{T}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(V)
is equivalent to giving a weight space decomposition
V=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
of
V
, where
\sigma_{T}
acts by
\sigma_{T}\cdot v=T^{i}v
for
v\in V_{i}
. Note that
V_{i}\neq 0
for only finitely many
i
’s. This defines a filtration

V_{\leq i}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j\leq i}V_{j}

which in turn induces a subsheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}
of
\mathcal{E}
by

\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}:=\rho(V_{\leq i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)),

defining a filtration
\{\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathcal{E}
by subsheaves. Then
\rho
induces surjections
\rho_{i}:V_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\to\mathcal{E}_{i}
(with
\mathcal{E}_{i}:=\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}/\mathcal{E}_{\leq i-1}
), and summing up these graded pieces we get a point in
\mathrm{Quot}(\mathcal{Q},P_{\mathcal{E}})
represented by the sheaf

(2.2)

\hat{\rho}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{i}:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\longrightarrow\hat{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}_{i}.

The importance of this sheaf is that it is, up to equivalence, the limit of the 1-PS
\sigma_{T}
in
\mathrm{Quot}(\mathcal{Q},P_{\mathcal{E}})
; recall that two quotient sheaves
\rho_{i}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{E}_{i},\ i=1,2
are equivalent if and only if there exists an isomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
of sheaves such that
\rho_{2}=\psi\circ\rho_{1}
. Writing
[\rho]
for the equivalence class of
\rho
, the above result can be stated as follows.

Lemma 2.10
.

(
cf.

[
HL10
, Lemma 4.4.3]
)

[\hat{\rho}]\in\mathrm{Quot}(\mathcal{Q},P_{\mathcal{E}})
is the limit of
[\rho:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\to\mathcal{E}]
as
T\to 0
under the 1-parameter subgroup defined by
\sigma_{T}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(V)
.

We shall review an outline of the proof of this result, following
[
HL10
]
, for the reader’s convenience.

Sketch of the proof.

We construct a family of sheaves

\theta:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\otimes\mathbb{C}[T]\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{E}}

over
\mathbb{C}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[T])
, such that we have
[\theta_{\alpha}]=[\rho]\circ\sigma_{T}
for the fibre
\theta_{\alpha}
over
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and look at its behaviour under the limit
\alpha\to 0
;
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
corresponds to the maximal ideal
(T-\alpha)\in\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[T])
, and the limit
\alpha\to 0
corresponds to
T\to 0
. Throughout in the proof, the tensor product is meant to be over
\mathbb{C}
.

Given the decomposition
V=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
, we define a map

\gamma:V\otimes\mathbb{C}[T]=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[T]\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{\leq i}\otimes T^{i}\subset V\otimes\mathbb{C}[T,T^{-1}]

by
\gamma(v_{j}\otimes p):=v_{j}\otimes(T^{j}\cdot p)
for
v_{j}\otimes p\in V_{j}\otimes\mathbb{C}[T]
. Note that
\gamma
is an isomorphism of vector spaces which coincides with the action of
\sigma_{T}
. In other words, the action of
\sigma_{T}
on
V
defines an isomorphism

\gamma:V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\otimes\mathbb{C}[T]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{\leq i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\otimes T^{i}=:\tilde{\mathcal{V}},

where we used the same symbol
\gamma
by abuse of notation.

Note also that
\rho
induces the following surjection

(2.3)

\tilde{\rho}:\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}\otimes T^{i},

defining a family
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
of sheaves over
\mathbb{C}
. Thus the desired family
\theta
can be given by
\tilde{\rho}\circ\gamma
; restricting to the central fibre at
T=0
, we have

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}/T\cdot\tilde{\mathcal{E}}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}_{i},

while the noncentral fibre is equivalent to
\rho\circ\sigma_{T}
since
\gamma
coincides with the action of
\sigma_{T}
.
∎

We shall later develop a “differential-geometric” version of this construction, in terms of Fubini–Study metrics. For this purpose, we shall rephrase the above lemma as follows. Suppose that we write

\hat{\rho}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\rho_{i},

with

\rho_{i}:V_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}/\mathcal{E}_{\leq i-1}

for the limit in the Quot-scheme by
\sigma_{T}
as in (
2.2
). By abuse of notation we write
\rho(V_{i})
(resp.
\rho_{i}(V_{i})
) for
\rho(V_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k))
(resp.
\rho_{i}(V_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k))
) in what follows. Noting
\rho(\sigma_{T}\cdot V_{i})=T^{i}\rho(V_{i})
and recalling (
2.3
), we find that
\rho(\sigma_{T}\cdot V_{i})
must be contained in the sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}
. Using the notation above, we may thus write

\rho(\sigma_{T}\cdot V_{i})=T^{i}\rho_{i}(V_{i})+T^{i}(\text{elements of $\mathcal{E}_{\leq i-1}$}).

Suppose that we write
\rho_{j\to i}(V_{j})
for the elements of
V_{j}
that are mapped to
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. With this notation, we collect the above for all
i
’s by using
V=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{i}
, to get

(2.4)

\rho(\sigma_{T}\cdot V)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{i}\left(\rho_{i\to i}(V_{i})+\sumop\displaylimits_{j>i}T^{j-i}\rho_{j\to i}(V_{j})\right).

Thus, we may write the limit of
\rho\circ\sigma_{T}
as
T\to 0
as

(2.5)

\rho(\sigma_{T}\cdot V)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{i}\left(\rho_{i}(V_{i})+o(T)\right),

where
o(T)
stands for the terms that go to zero as
T\to 0
, and
T^{i}
is the weight of
\sigma_{T}
on
\mathcal{E}_{i}
.

Remark 2.11
.

It is important to note that
\mathcal{E}_{i}
above is a priori just a coherent sheaf and in general
not
locally free; it may even have torsion. However, it is still possible to define a Zariski open subset of
X
over which all
\mathcal{E}_{\leq i}
and
\mathcal{E}_{i}
are locally free (torsion sheaves are treated as zero), recalling that
X
is smooth (by Lemma
2.7
or
[
HL10
, p.11]
).

Remark 2.12
.

The Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of
\mathcal{E}
does not play a significant role in what follows, contrary to the important role it plays in the construction of the Quot-scheme as above. In the rest of this paper
k
only needs to be large enough so that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated, since all we need is the quotient map (
2.1
), but there will be little harm in consistently assuming
k\geq\mathrm{reg}(\mathcal{E})
.

2.3. Fubini–Study metrics on vector bundles

Suppose that we fix
k
such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated, and hence there exists a holomorphic map
\Phi:X\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
to the Grassmannian such that the pullback under
\Phi
of the universal bundle is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}(k)
. More precisely, writing
\mathcal{U}
for the tautological bundle over the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(r,H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
of
r
-planes (rather than quotients), we have
\Phi^{*}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{E}(k)
.

Recall that a positive definite Hermitian form on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
defines a Hermitian metric on (the complex vector bundle)
U^{\vee}
on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
, called the Fubini–Study metric on the Grassmannian (see
e.g.

[
MM07
, Section 5.1.1]
). By pulling them back by
\Phi
, we have Hermitian metrics on
E
that are also called Fubini–Study metrics. We recall the details of this construction. Noting that the sections of
\mathcal{U}^{\vee}
can be naturally identified with
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
, Hermitian forms on
U^{\vee}
are the elements of
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}\otimes\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}
, where the bar stands for the complex conjugate.

We fix once for all a reference Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
on
E
. With the fixed Hermitian metric
h_{L}
on
L
, we have a Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
on
E(k)
, we define
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
to be an
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
-local orthonormal frame of
E
. We also fix a reference Hermitian form
I_{\mathbb{S}}
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
to be the
L^{2}
-Hermitian form defined by
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
and
\omega^{n}/n!
(see also Remark
2.14
); the notation here is meant to imply that the basis
\mathbb{S}:=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N_{k}}\}
,
N_{k}:=\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
, for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
is a fixed
I_{\mathbb{S}}
-orthonormal basis. We may identify
I_{\mathbb{S}}
with
\mathbb{S}
up to unitary equivalence, since fixing a positive Hermitian form is equivalent to fixing its (unitary equivalence class of) orthonormal basis.

We can also define the metric duals of the above. Fixing
\mathbb{S}
as above, we have a basis
\{s^{*}_{1},\dots,s^{*}_{N_{k}}\}
for
\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}
that is
I_{\mathbb{S}}
-metric dual of
\mathbb{S}
, and a local frame
\{e^{*}_{1}(x),\dots,e^{*}_{r}(x)\}
for
\overline{E(k)^{\vee}}
that is
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
-metric dual to the one
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
for
E(k)
. We also write
s_{i}(x)
(resp.
s^{*}_{i}(x)
) for the evaluation of
s_{i}
(resp.
s^{*}_{i}
) at
x\in X
with respect to the trivialisation given by
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
.

We first define
C^{\infty}
-maps

Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}:E(k)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\otimes C^{\infty}_{X},

and

Q^{*}_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}:\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}\otimes\overline{C^{\infty}_{X}}\longrightarrow\overline{E(k)^{\vee}},

with respect to
\mathbb{S}
and
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
as fixed above and hence defined globally over
X
. The bar, again, denotes the complex conjugate. A well-known result is the following, whose proof can be found in
[
Wan02
, Remark 3.5]
or
[
MM07
, Theorem 5.1.16]
.

Lemma 2.13
.

(
cf.

[
Wan02
, Remark 3.5]
or
[
MM07
, Theorem 5.1.16]
)

Suppose that we fix a Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
on
E
and an
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
-local orthonormal frame of
E(k)
which we write as
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
, as above. Let
\mathbb{S}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N_{k}}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
with respect to the
L^{2}
-Hermitian form defined by
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
and
\omega^{n}/n!
, and write
I_{\mathbb{S}}
for the
L^{2}
-Hermitian form itself. Then there exists a local
N_{k}\times r
matrix
Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}(x)
which smoothly depends on
x\in X
such that

s_{i}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{r}Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}(x)_{i\alpha}e_{\alpha}(x),\ \ \ \sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N_{k}}Q^{*}_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}(x)_{\alpha i}s^{*}_{i}(x)=e^{*}_{\alpha},

where the metric dual
*
is defined with respect to
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
and
I_{\mathbb{S}}
.

The Hermitian metric on
E(k)
defined by

h_{\mathbb{S}_{k},\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}(x):=Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}^{*}(x)\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}(x)

agrees with the pullback of the Fubini–Study metric on
U^{\vee}
with respect to
I_{\mathbb{S}}
over the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(r,H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
by the map
\Phi:X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(r,H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
.

Remark 2.14
.

In the above, the
L^{2}
-Hermitian form
I_{\mathbb{S}}
(defined by
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
and
\omega
) in fact involves a scaling by
\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
; see
e.g.

[
MM07
, Section 5.2.3]
.

Suppose that we now change the basis
\mathbb{S}
to
\sigma\cdot\mathbb{S}
with
\sigma\in\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k)))
. This defines another Hermitian metric on the universal bundle
U^{\vee}
over the Grassmannian, which we can pull back by
\Phi
to get a Hermitian metric on
E(k)
, exactly as before. Then, noting that
(\sigma^{-1})^{*}\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ\sigma^{-1}
has
\sigma\cdot\mathbb{S}
as its orthonormal basis, we may write the Hermitian metric
h_{\sigma\cdot\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
as

h_{\sigma\cdot\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}=Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}^{*}\circ((\sigma^{-1})^{*}\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ\sigma^{-1})\circ Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}},

which is also well known in the literature (see
e.g.

[
PS03
, Section 2]
). The appearance of the inverse in
\sigma^{-1}
can also be seen from the fact that
I_{\mathbb{S}}
is an element of
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}\otimes\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}
and hence
\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k)))
acts by the dual action.

In what follows, we shall adopt the following convention to simplify the notation. The Hermitian metrics
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
and
h_{L}
will be fixed as above, which defines an
L^{2}
-hermitian inner product
I_{\mathbb{S}}
(with the associated orthonormal basis
\mathbb{S}
). Moreover, choosing a local orthonormal frame
e_{L}(x)
of
L
with respect to
h_{L}
, we identify
\{e_{1}(x),\dots,e_{r}(x)\}
with a local orthonormal frame of
E
(rather than
E(k)
), which is associated to the Hermitian metric
h_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}
on
E
in Lemma
2.13
. Recalling
Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}:E(k)\to H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\otimes C^{\infty}_{X}
, we thus get a
C^{\infty}
-map

(2.6)

Q:E\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\otimes C^{\infty}_{X}(-k).

Similarly,
Q_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}^{*}:\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}\otimes\overline{C^{\infty}_{X}}\to\overline{E(k)^{\vee}}
yields a
C^{\infty}
-map

(2.7)

Q^{*}:\overline{H^{0}(E(k))^{\vee}}\otimes\overline{C^{\infty}_{X}(k)}\longrightarrow\overline{E^{\vee}},

by noting
\overline{E(k)^{\vee}}\cong\overline{E^{\vee}}\otimes\overline{C^{\infty}_{X}(-k)}
. With these conventions, Lemma
2.13
says
h_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}=Q^{*}\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ Q
. In what follows we shall simply write
Q^{*}Q
for
h_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}
and omit
I_{\mathbb{S}}
. Also, in order not to be bothered by the inverse in
\sigma^{-1}
, we shall consistently write

h_{\sigma}:=Q^{*}\sigma^{*}\sigma Q,

with
\sigma\in\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
, to denote
h_{\sigma^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}
.

We summarise the above argument to define the Fubini–Study metric on the bundle
E
.

Definition 2.15
.

Given a reference metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
on
E
and an
L^{2}
-Hermitian form
I_{\mathbb{S}}
for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
with respect to
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\otimes h_{L}^{\otimes k}
as above, there exist
C^{\infty}
-maps
Q
and
Q^{*}
defined by (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), such that the Hermitian metric

h:=Q^{*}Q\in\Gamma_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(E^{\vee}\otimes\overline{E^{\vee}})

agrees with the pullback of the Fubini–Study metric on the universal bundle over the Grassmannian defined by the Hermitian form
I_{\mathbb{S}}
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
.
h
is called the (reference)
Fubini–Study metric
on
E
defined by
I_{\mathbb{S}}
.

Moreover, given
\sigma\in\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
, the Hermitian metric

h_{\sigma}:=Q^{*}\sigma^{*}\sigma Q\in\Gamma_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(E^{\vee}\otimes\overline{E^{\vee}})

agrees with the one defined by the Hermitian form
I_{\sigma^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{S}}
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
.
h_{\sigma}
is called the
Fubini–Study metric
on
E
defined by the positive Hermitian form
\sigma^{*}\sigma=\sigma^{*}\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ\sigma
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
.

When it is necessary to make the exponent
k
more explicit, we also write
h_{k}
for
h
, for example. The construction above gives us a way of associating a Hermitian metric
FS(H)
on
E
to a positive definite Hermitian form
H
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
; we simply choose
\sigma\in GL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
so that
H=\sigma^{*}\circ I_{\mathbb{S}}\circ\sigma=\sigma^{*}\sigma
, and define
FS(H)=h_{\sigma}
as above.

Recall that there is an alternative definition (
[
Wan02
]
, see also
[
MM07
, Theorem 5.1.16]
) of
FS(H)
by means of the equation

(2.8)

\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N_{k}}s_{i}\otimes s_{i}^{*_{FS(H)}}=\mathrm{Id}_{E}

where
\{s_{i}\}
is an
H
-orthonormal basis for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
and
s_{i}^{*_{FS(H)}}
is the
FS(H)
-metric dual of
s_{i}
. Defining

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}:=\{\text{smooth Hermitian metrics on }E\},

we thus have a map
FS
that maps a positive definite Hermitian form
H
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}
to an element in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
. Writing
N_{k}=\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
and fixing an isomorphism
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\cong\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
, recall that the homogeneous space
GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})
can be identified with the set
\mathcal{B}_{k}
of all positive definitive Hermitian forms on the vector space
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
, by means of the isomorphism
GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})\ni\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{*}\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
.

These classical definitions will be important for us later, and are summarised as follows.

Definition 2.16
.

The
Bergman space

\mathcal{B}_{k}
at level
k
is defined to be the set of all positive definite Hermitian forms on the vector space
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
, which can be identified with the homogeneous space
\mathcal{B}_{k}=GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})
. The
Fubini–Study map

FS:\mathcal{B}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}

is defined by the equation (
2.8
). The image of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
by the Fubini–Study map is a subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
denoted by

\mathcal{H}_{k}:=FS(\mathcal{B}_{k}).

We thus have a natural inclusion
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
for each
k
, but it turns out that the union of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
’s is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
,
i.e.

(2.9)

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\overline{\cup_{k>>0}\mathcal{H}_{k}}.

More precisely, we have that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a sequence
\{h_{k,p}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of Fubini–Study metrics
h_{k,p}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
such that
h_{k,p}\to h
as
k\to+\infty
in the
C^{p}
-norm. The result (
2.9
) follows from the so-called Tian–Yau–Zelditch expansion for the Bergman kernel, which is essentially a theorem in analysis. Several proofs have been written especially when
E
has rank one. For higher ranks, we refer to
[
Cat99
,
Wan05
]
; see also the book
[
MM07
]
and references therein. An elementary proof can be found in
[
BBS08
]
.

Finally, recall that the homogeneous space
\mathcal{B}_{k}=GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})
has a distinguished Riemannian metric called the bi-invariant metric, with respect to which geodesics can be written as
t\mapsto e^{\zeta t}
for some
\zeta\in\mathfrak{gl}(N_{k},\mathbb{C})
. Recalling the isomorphism
GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})\ni\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{*}\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
, we thus have a 1-PS in
\mathcal{B}_{k}
defined for each
\zeta\in\mathfrak{gl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
by
\exp(\zeta^{*}t+\zeta t)
(note that we may choose the isomorphism
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\cong\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
so that the standard basis for
\mathbb{C}^{N_{k}}
is
I_{\mathbb{S}}
-orthonormal, and hence
\zeta^{*}
is the
I_{\mathbb{S}}
-Hermitian conjugate of
\zeta
). This defines an important 1-PS of Fubini–Study metrics as follows.

Definition 2.17
.

The
Bergman 1-parameter subgroup
(or Bergman 1-PS)
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{k}
emanating from
h_{\sigma_{0}}=Q^{*}Q
is a path of Fubini–Study metrics defined by

h_{\sigma_{t}}:=Q^{*}\sigma_{t}^{*}\sigma_{t}Q,

where
\sigma_{t}:=e^{\zeta t}\in\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
, in the notation of Definition
2.15
. When
\zeta
has rational eigenvalues, we call
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
a
rational
Bergman 1-PS. We say that a Bergman 1-PS
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{k}
is
generated by

\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
or
associated to
the 1-PS
\sigma:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
(in the category of complex Lie groups), where
\sigma_{t}=e^{\zeta t}
.

The Bergman 1-PS’s can be regarded as an analogue of geodesics in
\mathcal{B}_{k}=GL(N_{k},\mathbb{C})/U(N_{k})
, and play an important role later.

Remark 2.18
.

Throughout in what follows, we shall only consider
\zeta
’s that have rational eigenvalues, and hence any Bergman 1-PS will be meant to be rational (see
e.g.
Sections
3
and
5
).

We also recall that, in fact,
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
can be regarded as an “infinite dimensional homogeneous space”, with a well-defined notion of geodesics, as explained in
[
Kob87
, Section 6.2]
.

Definition 2.19
.

A path
\{h_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is called a
geodesic
in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
if it satisfies

(2.10)

\partial_{s}(h_{s}^{-1}\partial_{s}h_{s})=0,

as an equation in
\mathrm{End}_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(E)
; an overall constant scaling
h_{s}:=e^{bs}h_{0}
for some
b\in\mathbb{R}
will be called a
trivial
geodesic.

An elementary yet crucially important fact is that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is
geodesically complete
; for any
h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
there exists a geodesic path
\{h_{s}\}_{0\leq s\leq 1}
connecting them; we can write it explicitly as
h_{s}=\exp(s\log h_{1}h^{-1}_{0})h_{0}
.

Remark 2.20
.

Although the Bergman 1-PS
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
does not satisfy the geodesic equation (
2.10
), we can show
[
HK21
, Corollary 4.5]
that it is in fact a
subgeodesic
in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
,
i.e.

\partial_{t}(h_{\sigma_{t}}^{-1}\partial_{t}h_{\sigma_{t}})
is positive semidefinite as an element in
\mathrm{End}_{C^{\infty}_{X}}(E)
. While we do not use this result in this paper, it seems interesting to point out an analogy to the case of varieties, in which test configurations define subgeodesics (see
e.g.

[
BHJ19
, Section 3]
).

2.4.

We give here an interpretation of

Given the above definitions, the following is immediate.

Writing

\scriptstyle{a}

\textstyle{\SL(V)\times V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}

\textstyle{V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We now state the relationship between

Lemma 2.22

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}(k)

Proof.

Since both

∎

Remark 2.23
.

f_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}

and

2.5. Donaldson’s functional and the Hermitian–Einstein equation

As before,
(X,L)
stands for a polarised smooth complex projective variety and
\mathcal{E}
a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
. We write
\mathrm{Vol}_{L}:=\intop\nolimits_{X}c_{1}(L)^{n}/n!
. The following functional plays a central role in our paper.

Definition 2.24
.

Given two Hermitian metrics
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
on
E
,
the Donaldson functional

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}:\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\times\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0}):={\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0})-\mu(E){\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0}),

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0}):=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(h_{t}^{-1}\partial_{t}h_{t}\cdot F_{t}\right)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}dt

and

{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0}):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\intop\nolimits_{X}\log\det(h_{0}^{-1}h_{1})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

In the above,
\{h_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is a smooth path of Hermitian metrics between
h_{0}
to
h_{1}
and
F_{t}
denotes (
\sqrt{-1}/2\pi
) times the Chern curvature of
h_{t}
, with respect to the fixed holomorphic structure of
E
(such that the sheaf of germs of holomorphic sections of
E
is identified with
\mathcal{E}
).

Remark 2.25
.

Throughout in what follows, we shall fix the second argument of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}
as a reference metric. Thus
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{0})
is regarded as a function of
h
with a fixed reference
h_{0}
.

We recall some well-known properties of this functional as established in
[
Don85
]
; the reader is also referred to
[
Kob87
, Section 6.3]
for more details. First of all it is well-defined,
i.e.
does not depend on the path
\{h_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
chosen to connect
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
(
cf.

[
Kob87
, Lemma 6.3.6]
). This easily implies the following
cocycle property

(2.15)

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{2},h_{0})=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{2},h_{1})+\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{1},h_{0}),

for any
h_{0},h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
.

Remark 2.26
.

Recalling
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(e^{c}h,h)=0
for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}

[
Kob87
, Lemma 6.3.23]
, (
2.15
) implies

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(e^{c}h,h_{1})={\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(e^{c}h,h)+{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{1})={\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{1})

for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
i.e.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(-,h_{1})
is invariant under an overall constant scaling.

Second, the Euler–Lagrange equation for
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(-,h_{\mathrm{ref}})
is the
Hermitian–Einstein equation
. More precisely, a critical point of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(-,h_{\mathrm{ref}})
is the following object.

Definition 2.27
.

A Hermitian metric
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is called a
Hermitian–Einstein metric
if it satisfies

\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}=\frac{\mu(E)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\mathrm{Id}_{E},

where
\Lambda_{\omega}
is the contraction with respect to the Kähler metric
\omega
on the manifold (
i.e.
the dual Lefschetz operator).

Critical points of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
being Hermitian–Einstein metrics is a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.28
.

(
cf.

[
Don85
, Section 1.2]
)

Fixing a reference metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}_{1}(h_{t},h_{\mathrm{ref}})

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(h_{t}^{-1}\partial_{t}h_{t}\cdot F_{t}\right)\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}_{2}(h_{t},h_{\mathrm{ref}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}(h^{-1}_{t}\partial_{t}h_{t})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},

for a path
\{h_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
of smooth metrics with
h_{0}=h_{\mathrm{ref}}
, and hence

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{t},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(h_{t}^{-1}\partial_{t}h_{t}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{t}-\frac{\mu(E)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

The final important point is that
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
is convex along geodesics in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, as defined in (
2.10
). Recalling the important fact that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
is geodesically complete, these properties together imply that a critical point of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
is necessarily the global minimum. We summarise the above in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.29
.

(
cf.

[
Kob87
, Section 6.3]
)

The functional
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
is convex along geodesics in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, and its critical point
(
if it exists
\,)
attains the global minimum. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
is strictly convex along nontrivial geodesics if
E
is irreducible
(
in particular if
E
is simple
\,)
and in this case the critical point
(
if it exists
\,)
is unique up to an overall constant scaling.

3. Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics

3.1. Filtrations and metric duals

A central role in this paper is played by 1-parameter subgroups in
\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
and the weight decomposition of
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
associated to them. This is of course related to the Bergman 1-PS’s in Definition
2.17
, and for the compatibility with the differential-geometric situation therein we need to assume that 1-parameter subgroups that we consider are the complexifications of the fixed unitary action; more precisely, we fix a Hermitian form
I_{\mathbb{S}}
for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
which fixes
Q^{*}
as in Section
2.3
, or alternatively the unitary equivalence class of its orthonormal basis
\mathbb{S}
, and decree that the unitary group
U(N_{k})
acting on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}
is always with respect to
I_{\mathbb{S}}
.

It is well known that the set of all 1-parameter subgroups in a torus (in the category of algebraic groups) forms a lattice, and we can take its tensor product over
\mathbb{Q}
. We cannot naively repeat this operation for
\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
as it is nonabelian, but the following is still well defined and plays a very important role in the rest of the paper.

Definition 3.1
.

We write
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
(or simply
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
) for the set of 1-parameter subgroups
\sigma:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
in the category of complex Lie groups with the following property: for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
there exists an integer
m\geq 1
such that
\sigma^{m}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
is a 1-parameter subgroup in the category of algebraic groups and that
\sigma^{m}
is the complexification of a unitary 1-parameter subgroup
U(1)\to U(N_{k})
where the unitarity is defined with respect to
I_{\mathbb{S}}
on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
.

In a more down-to-earth terminology,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be defined as matrices as follows. An element
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
corresponds one-to-one with a Hermitian element
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
with rational eigenvalues by
\sigma_{t}:=e^{\zeta t}
; it is always understood that
\zeta
is Hermitian with respect to
I_{\mathbb{S}}
. We may change
\mathbb{S}
(without affecting
Q^{*}Q
) to a unitarily equivalent basis
\tau\cdot\mathbb{S}
(
\tau\in U(N_{k})
), say, so that it is compatible with the weight space decomposition

(3.1)

H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}V^{\vee}_{w_{i}}

defined by
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
, where its generator
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
acts on
V^{\vee}_{w_{i}}
with weight
w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
i=1,\dots,\nu
. Note that
\nu
depends on
\zeta
. Henceforth we shall assume the ordering

(3.2)

w_{1}>\cdots>w_{\nu}.

Remark 3.2
.

We shall assume without loss of generality that the operator norm (
i.e.
the modulus of the maximum eigenvalue) of
\zeta
is at most 1; this can be achieved by an overall constant multiple of
\zeta
, which just results in a different scaling of the parameter
t
.

Note also that a Bergman 1-PS
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is rational in the sense of Definition
2.17
if and only if
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We shall introduce an auxiliary variable

T:=e^{-t},

so that the limit
t\to+\infty
corresponds to
T\to 0
, which makes it easier for us to compare the limit
t\to+\infty
to the description of the Quot-scheme limit as a central fibre, as presented in Lemma
2.10
. In this description, for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
we may write

(3.3)

\sigma_{t}=T^{-\zeta},

for some
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
and the weight space decomposition (
3.1
) defines a filtration

(3.4)

V^{\vee}_{\leq-w_{i}}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{i}V^{\vee}_{w_{j}},

where we note that the minus sign appears because (
3.3
) gives the ordering of the weights

(3.5)

-w_{1}<\dots<-w_{\nu}

with respect to
T
.

We now consider the filtration that
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
induces on
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
. It turns out that the natural dual action is not the one relevant for our purpose. Recall that Lemma
2.22
implies that we have the following diagram

(3.6)

\textstyle{\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}\otimes\overline{C^{\infty}_{X}(k)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Q^{*}}
\scriptstyle{*}
\textstyle{\overline{E^{\vee}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{*,\ \textup{gauge}}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{\mathcal{E}}

where the left vertical arrow stands for the metric duality isomorphism given by
I_{\mathbb{S}}
and
h_{L}
, and the right vertical arrow for the one given by
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
and the
C^{\infty}
-gauge transformation (written
f_{\mathbb{S},\mathrm{ref}}
in Lemma
2.22
). The fact that the right vertical arrow involves taking the dual of the bundle
\mathcal{E}
will have nontrivial consequences concerning the sign (
cf.
Remark
4.5
).

We decree that the
I_{\mathbb{S}}
-metric dual gives a weight-preserving isomorphism between
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
and
\overline{H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee}}
, and hence defines a weight decomposition

H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}V_{w_{i}}

in which
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
acts with weight
w_{i}
on
V_{w_{i}}
. We thus get a filtration

V_{\leq-w_{i}}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{i}V_{w_{j}},

as in (
3.4
), which defines a filtration

(3.7)

0\neq\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}=\mathcal{E}

of
\mathcal{E}
by subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is defined by the quotient map

\rho:V_{\leq-w_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-k)\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}.

Recall, as in Lemma
2.10
, that the Quot-scheme limit associated to the above filtration is given by

\hat{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}\mathcal{E}_{-w_{i}},

where
\mathcal{E}_{-w_{i}}=\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}/\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}
for
i=1,\dots,\nu
(in which we assume
\mathcal{E}_{-w_{0}}=0
).

The components of
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
with nontrivial rank are the objects that we need later.

Definition 3.3
.

We define a subset
\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
of
\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, with ordering
\hat{1}<\dots<\hat{\nu}
, so that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{-w_{\alpha}})>0
if and only if
\alpha\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
.

The above definition, together with the invariance of the rank under the flat limit in Quot-schemes, implies

\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=\hat{1}}^{\hat{\nu}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{-w_{\alpha}})=\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}).

Remark 3.4
.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{1}}
is a non-zero torsion-free subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}
, we find
\hat{1}=1
in the above notation. In particular, note that
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
for all
\alpha\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
.

We shall mainly work over an open dense subset of
X
where each
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is a holomorphic subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
. We make the following definition, by recalling the standard definition of the singular locus.

Definition 3.5
.

The
singular locus

\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is the Zariski closed set in
X
where
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is not locally free. We define the
regular locus
to be the complement of

\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}})\cup\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}})

in
X
, and denote it by
X^{\mathrm{reg}}(\zeta)
or
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

Thus each
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is a holomorphic subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
over
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
, by recalling that a subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is a holomorphic subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
if and only if both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
are locally free. Observe that
X\setminus X^{\mathrm{reg}}
has codimension at least 1 in
X
by Lemma
2.7
.

We define a
C^{\infty}
complex vector bundle
\widehat{E}
over
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
as

\widehat{E}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha=\hat{1}}^{\hat{\nu}}E_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}/E_{\leq-w_{\alpha-1}},

where
E_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
is the
C^{\infty}
complex vector bundle over
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
defined by
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
, and the quotient is the one of the
C^{\infty}
complex vector bundles over
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
. Note that we have an isomorphism
\widehat{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}E
as
C^{\infty}
-vector bundles over
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
. Combining this with another
C^{\infty}
-isomorphism
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{E^{\vee}}
, afforded by the
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
-metric dual, we get

(3.8)

\overline{E^{\vee}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha=\hat{1}}^{\hat{\nu}}E_{-w_{\alpha}}

where we wrote
E_{-w_{\alpha}}=E_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}/E_{\leq-w_{\alpha-1}}
.

It turns out that what we need later is the modification of the filtration
0\neq\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}=\mathcal{E}
on a Zariski closed subset of
X
, given by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a filtration

(3.9)

0\neq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}=\mathcal{E}

of
\mathcal{E}
by subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
, such that

(1)

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}
is saturated in
\mathcal{E}
and agrees with
\mathcal{E}_{\leq{-w_{i}}}
on a Zariski open subset of
X
for all
i=1,\dots,\nu
,

(2)

setting
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}}:=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}
,
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})>0
if and only if
i\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
.

Proof.

We define (
3.9
) inductively as follows.
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}}}
is defined as the saturation of
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}}}
in
\mathcal{E}
; note that this implies
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}}}=\mathcal{E}
, since
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}}}
has full rank. We then replace
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}+1}},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}
by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\hat{\nu}}}=\mathcal{E}
. Continuing inductively, for
\alpha\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\widehat{\nu-1}\}
we define
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
to be the saturation of
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
in
\mathcal{E}
. Defining
\beta
to be the smallest element of
\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
that is larger than
\alpha\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\widehat{\nu-1}\}
, we replace
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\alpha+1}},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{\beta-1}}
by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\alpha}}
. The end product that we get is a filtration
0\neq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}=\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{E}
by saturated subsheaves, in which strict inclusion holds if and only if
i\in\{\hat{1},\dots,\hat{\nu}\}
(recall also
\hat{1}=1
as in Remark
3.4
).

Since the above construction does not change the rank of each
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,\nu
, and hence does not change
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
where it is locally free, we see that
\mathcal{E}_{\leq-w_{i}}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}
agree on the (Zariski open) locus where they are locally free.
∎

3.2. Renormalised Quot-scheme limit of Fubini–Study metrics

Our aim in this section is to find an expansion for
h_{\sigma_{t}}:=Q^{*}\sigma^{*}_{t}\sigma_{t}Q
as
t\to+\infty
. Although this limit is divergent, we can find an appropriate rescaling (in terms of the constant gauge transformation) to get a 1-PS of Hermitian metrics
\{\hat{h}_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
which is convergent. The limit
\hat{h}
, which we call the renormalised Quot-scheme limit of
h_{\sigma_{t}}
, is well defined only on the Zariski open subset
X^{\mathrm{reg}}
of
X

(Chern–Weil formula
[

,

]
)
.

Let

In the above,

Thus, we get

\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}(F(h)|_{\mathcal{S}})\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\deg(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})+\intop\nolimits_{X}|\mathrm{II}^{\prime}(h)|^{2}\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},

where the second fundamental form that appears on the right hand side is defined with respect to
h
and the saturation
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\{\hat{h}_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a renormalised Bergman 1-PS associated to
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
. Then there exists a constant

(
and

Remark 4.14
.

The sign convention concerning the metric duality isomorphism, as mentioned in Remark

is meant to be a Hermitian metric on

Proof.

We first consider the following general situation. Suppose that we have a geodesic segment

(

and

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{0})\geq\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(\gamma(s),h_{0}),

where we recall

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(\gamma(s),h_{0})=\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(v\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F(h_{0})-\frac{\mu(E)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Now define

\bar{v}:=\frac{1}{r\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}(v)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{Id}_{E},

so that

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(\gamma(s),h_{0})=\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left((v-\bar{v})\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F(h_{0})-\frac{\mu(E)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by using Cauchy–Schwarz, we have

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(\gamma(s),h_{0})\geq-\|v-\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Lambda_{\omega}F(h_{0})-\frac{\mu(E)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{L}}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\|_{L^{2}}

we get

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{0})\geq-\underline{C}(h_{0})\|v-\bar{v}\|_{L^{2}},

by recalling (
4.8
).

Let us consider
\{\hat{h}_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, a renormalised Bergman 1-PS associated to
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
emanating from
h_{k}
. We apply the above argument to

and

, in which we replace

by

) holds for the geodesic segment

and
\hat{h}_{\sigma_{t}}

which is only well defined on

[

,
\hat{h}_{\sigma_{t}}^{-1}

Thus (

To be more precise, the curvature

should be defined with respect to

composed with the metric duality isomorphism (as in Remark

We prove that

We choose an orthonormal frame with respect to which

and
\lambda_{\alpha,2}(x)

at

. Since

(4.11)

\lambda_{\alpha,1}(x)+\lambda_{\alpha,2}(x)=1

. Moreover, since

\lambda_{\alpha,1}(x)+e^{-c_{\alpha}t}\lambda_{\alpha,2}(x)\leq\mathrm{tr}(h_{k})

and all

.

We evaluate

\intop\nolimits_{X^{\mathrm{reg}}}\mathrm{tr}(v_{t}^{2})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{r}\intop\nolimits_{X^{\mathrm{reg}}}\left(\log(\lambda_{\alpha,1}(x)+e^{-c_{\alpha}t}\lambda_{\alpha,2}(x))\right)^{2}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

We fix

We thus evaluate the integral of
\left(\log(\lambda_{\alpha,1}(x)+e^{-c_{\alpha}t}\lambda_{\alpha,2}(x))\right)^{2}

5. The non-Archimedean Donaldson functional

Recalling Definition
3.1

there exists

such that

is a 1-parameter subgroup in the category of algebraic groups, which in particular has integral weights. This allows us to clear up the denominators in the materials in Section

Recall also that the 1-PS’s

in Definition

that appeared above is exactly the following.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
w_{1},\dots,w_{\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}
be the weights of
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
corresponding to an element in

Given the integrally graded filtration (

of

by saturated subsheaves as in Lemma
3.6
. With this convention understood, we prove the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Recalling
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}
as in Lemma

-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}w_{i}\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})-\mu(\mathcal{E})\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})\right)=\frac{1}{j(\zeta,k)}\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})).

Proof.

Note

\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})=\mathrm{rk}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}\right)=\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}})

and

\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})=\deg\left(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}\right)=\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i}})-\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{i-1}}).

Thus, defining
\bar{w}_{i}:=j(\zeta,k)w_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}w_{i}\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})-\mu(\mathcal{E})\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{-w_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{j(\zeta,k)}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\nu}\bar{w}_{i}\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-\bar{w}_{i}})-\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-\bar{w}_{i-1}})-\mu(\mathcal{E})(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-\bar{w}_{i}})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-\bar{w}_{i-1}}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j(\zeta,k)}\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}q\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})-\mu(\mathcal{E})(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1}))\right),

by recalling the ordering
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{\nu}
as in (
3.2
).

Since
q\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})=(q-1)\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})+\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})
, we get

\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}q\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})\right)=-\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})=-\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q}),

and similarly for the rank

\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}q\left(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})\right))=-\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q}).

Although the above infinite sums are divergent, we substitute them in

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}q\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1})-\mu(\mathcal{E})(\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q-1}))\right)

\displaystyle=-\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})-\mu(\mathcal{E})\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\right)

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})),

to get a finite sum; since the filtration
0\neq\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq-w_{\nu}}=\mathcal{E}
is finite, there are only finitely many nonzero terms in the above sum.
∎

We now define the non-Archimedean Donaldson functional, following the terminology of
[
BHJ19
, Definition 3.4]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Suppose that we take
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
which gives rise to the filtration
\{\mathcal{E}_{\leq q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\mathcal{E}
as in (
3.9
), which we may assume is graded by integers (as in (
5.1
)). Taking the saturation
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\leq q}
in
\mathcal{E}
, the
non-Archimedean Donaldson functional

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}:\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Q}
is defined as

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma):=\frac{2}{j(\sigma)}\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\left(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\right),

where
j(\sigma)\in\mathbb{N}
is the smallest positive integer such that
\sigma^{j(\sigma)}
is a 1-PS in the category of algebraic groups.

While the above formulation is conceptually straightforward, a formula in terms of the generator
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
of
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is also useful in a sequel
[
HK19
]
to this paper. By using the notation in Definition
5.1
, we write

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\zeta,k)=\frac{2}{j(\zeta,k)}\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\left(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\right),

for the generator
\zeta
of each element in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
.

The name “non-Archimedean” originally comes from the analogy with a theory on Berkovich spaces, as explained in
[
BHJ17
, Section 6.8]
. Recall also that a filtration on a vector space
V
corresponds one-to-one with a non-Archimedean norm on
V

[
BHJ17
, Section 1.1]
; see also
[
Bou18
, Section 1]
.

6. Two-step filtrations associated to saturated subsheaves

We now consider the special case when the filtration is given in two steps. A particularly important case is the one associated to saturated subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
, which defines a natural two-step filtration for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))=:V
.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose that we are given a saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
, and choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large such that
\mathcal{F}(k)
and
\mathcal{E}(k)
are both globally generated. Then it defines
0\neq H^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))=V
a two-step filtration (whose weights will be specified later in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
)). We write
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
for the element defining this filtration.

Note that the filtration of
\mathcal{E}
generated by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
as in (
3.9
) is exactly
0\neq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
.

We now specify the weights of
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
defined in Definition
6.1
. Recall that the weights
w_{1},w_{2}
of
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
are determined by
\mathrm{tr}(\zeta_{\mathcal{F}})=0
; more precisely (
cf.

[
GFKR16
, Section 4]
), we get

(6.1)

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))}{h^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))}\ \ \text{and}

(6.2)

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{h^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))-h^{0}(\mathcal{F}(k))}{h^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))},

where the factor
1/h^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))
is inserted so as to make the operator norm of
\zeta
to be less than 1 (
cf.
Remark
3.2
). Note that these weights are rational numbers, and hence that the 1-PS generated by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
defines an element in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We can restate some of the results in Section
4
for the two-step filtrations as follows.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
defines a two-step filtration associated to a saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
for large enough
k
as in Definition
6.1
. For the rational Bergman 1-PS
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
emanating from
h_{k}
, there exist a constant
c_{6}(h_{k},k)>0
such that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{k})\geq\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{F}))\cdot 2t-c_{6}(h_{k},k)

for all
t\geq 0
, and a constant
c_{7}(h_{k},\zeta_{\mathcal{F}},k)>0
such that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{k})\leq\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{F}))\cdot 2t+c_{7}(h_{k},\zeta_{\mathcal{F}},k)

holds for all sufficiently large
t>0
.

Proof.

We simply write down Propositions
4.1
for the specific filtration defined by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
. Direct computation immediately yields the claimed results.
∎

Finally, we end this section by proving the following rather technical result which we need later. Recall that the distance
\mathrm{dist}(h_{1},h_{2})
between two Hermitian metrics
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
can be defined by using the geodesic between them, as in Section
2.3
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Given a Bergman 1-PS
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
emanating from
h_{k}
, generated by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(E(k)))
associated to a saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
, we have
\mathrm{dist}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{k})\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
.

Proof.

We can explicitly write down the geodesic
\{\gamma_{s}(t)\}_{0\leq s\leq 1}
connecting
h_{k}
and
h_{\sigma_{t}}
as
e^{sv(\sigma_{t})}h_{k}
where
v(\sigma_{t}):=\log(h_{\sigma_{t}}h^{-1}_{k})
. We compute its Riemannian length as

|\partial_{s}\gamma_{s}(t)|=\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}(v(\sigma_{t})\cdot v(\sigma_{t}))^{1/2}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!},

where we recall that
\gamma_{s}(t)
being a geodesic is equivalent to
\partial_{s}\gamma_{s}(t)
being independent of
s
. We show that this length segment tends to
+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
.

We take an
h_{k}
-orthonormal frame. We now recall Proposition
3.9
, which states

v(\sigma_{t})=\log e^{wt}\left(\hat{h}+o(t)\right)e^{wt},

where
w:=\mathrm{diag}(w_{1},w_{2})
(as a block-diagonal matrix), where
w_{1},w_{2}
are as defined in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
). We also wrote
\hat{h}:=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{2}Q^{*}_{\alpha\to\alpha}Q_{\alpha\to\alpha}>0
for the renormalised Quot-scheme limit.
Observe that we have

\log e^{wt}\left(\hat{h}/2\right)e^{wt}<v(\sigma_{t})<\log e^{wt}\left(2\hat{h}\right)e^{wt},

for all large enough
t
, and hence

2wt+\log\left(\hat{h}/2\right)<v(\sigma_{t})<2wt+\log\left(2\hat{h}\right),

noting that
e^{wt}
commutes with the renormalised Quot-scheme limit
\hat{h}
. Thus each eigenvalue of
v(\sigma_{t})
can be estimated as
2w_{\alpha}t(1+o(t))
,
\alpha=1,2
.
Hence we get

\mathrm{tr}(v(\sigma_{t})v(\sigma_{t}))>4t^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{2}w_{\alpha}^{2}(1+o(t))>2t^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{2}w_{\alpha}^{2},

which tends to
+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
at each point
x\in X^{\mathrm{reg}}
.

Thus we can compute the geodesic length between
h_{k}
and
h_{\sigma_{t}}
, as we did in the proof of Lemma
4.13
, as

\mathrm{dist}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{k})=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}ds\intop\nolimits_{X}\mathrm{tr}(v(\sigma_{t})\cdot v(\sigma_{t}))^{1/2}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}>\text{const}\cdot t^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{2}w_{\alpha}^{2},

which tends to
+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
.
∎

7. Summary of the main results

We recall the following terminology (
cf.

[
BHJ19
, Definition 5.1]
).

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a function

\mathcal{N}:\SL(N,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}

that is continuous with respect to the topology on
\SL(N,\mathbb{C})
defined by a submultiplicative matrix norm
||\cdot||
(
e.g.
the operator norm).

(1)

\mathcal{N}
is said to be
coercive
if there exist two constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a>0
such that

\mathcal{N}(g)\geq a\log\|g\|-b

holds for any
g\in\SL(N,\mathbb{C})
;

(2)

\mathcal{N}
is said to have
log norm singularities
if it can be written as

\mathcal{N}(g)=a\log\|g\|+O(1)

for some constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
, where
O(1)
stands for the terms that remain bounded over
\SL(N,\mathbb{C})
.

We shall also say that
\mathcal{N}
is coercive (resp. has log norm singularities)
along any rational Bergman 1-PS
if in the above notation it satisfies
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{t})\geq a\log\|\sigma_{t}\|-b
(resp.
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{t})=a\log\|\sigma_{t}\|+O(1)
) for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Observe that by means of Bergman 1-PS’s, we can naturally define
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
as a map from
\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
to
\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(-,h_{k}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\SL(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
\sigma&\longmapsto&\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma},h_{k})\\
\end{array}\right.

with the fixed reference metric
h_{k}
, while we are mostly interested in the case when the domain of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(-,h_{k})
is restricted to a Bergman 1-PS
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Important functionals concerning the constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics have log norm singularities, as proved by Paul
[
Pau12
]
; see also
[
BHJ19
, Section 5]
. Note also that the property of having log norm singularities is independent of the choice of a matrix norm, up to changing the constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
.

A particularly important role is played by coercive functions; they are in particular bounded from below. If
\mathcal{N}
is coercive then it is proper,
i.e.
the preimage of any compact set is compact, which is equivalent in this setting to saying that
\mathcal{N}(g)
tends to
+\infty
as
\|g\|
tends to
+\infty
.

We now summarise what we have established so far. Suppose that we take
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
to be large enough for
\mathcal{E}(k)
to be globally generated, and also that we have a sequence
\{h_{k}\}_{k\geq k_{0}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
,
h_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, converging to the reference metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
in
C^{p}
for
p\geq 2
, afforded by (
2.9
). Write
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
for the Bergman 1-PS emanating from
h_{k}
associated to the 1-PS
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. In what follows we assume
k\geq k_{0}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

There exists a constant
c_{k}(h_{\mathrm{ref}})>0
that depends only on
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\geq\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)t-c_{k}(h_{\mathrm{ref}})

holds for all
t\geq 0
and all
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
, where

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)=\frac{2}{j(\sigma)}\sumop\displaylimits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\left(\mu(\mathcal{E})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\leq q})\right)

is as defined in Definition
5.3
.

Proof.

This follows from what we have established so far. By the cocycle property (
2.15
) of the Donaldson functional, we have

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{k})+\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{k},h_{\mathrm{ref}}).

By the convergence
h_{k}\to h_{\mathrm{ref}}
in
C^{p}
, we may assume
|\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{k},h_{\mathrm{ref}})|<1
for all
k
. The result now follows from Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
5.2
, by replacing
c_{1}(h_{k})+\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{k},h_{\mathrm{ref}})
by some constant
c_{k}(h_{\mathrm{ref}})>0
.
∎

A similar argument also yields the following result.

Theorem 7.3
.

There exists a constant
c_{k}(\zeta,h_{\mathrm{ref}})>0
depending on
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\zeta\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
, such that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})\leq\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)t+c_{k}(\zeta,h_{\mathrm{ref}})

holds for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
and all large enough
t>0
, with
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)
as defined in Definition
5.3
.

Note the following important observation for
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}
(where a filtration is called trivial if it is
0\subsetneq\mathcal{E}
), which follows immediately by considering the two-step filtrations (and 1-PS’s associated to them) as in Definition
6.1
.

Proposition 7.4
.

The non-Archimedean Donaldson functional
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)
is positive
(
resp. nonnegative
\,)
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
whose associated filtration by saturated subsheaves (
3.9
) is nontrivial and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated, if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is slope stable
(
resp. semistable
\,)
.

We finally summarise particularly important consequences of our main results as follows.

Corollary 7.5
.

With notation as in Theorems
7.2
and
7.3
and Definition
7.1
, we have the following results.

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}
is slope stable
(
resp. semistable
\,)
, then the Donaldson functional is coercive
(
resp. bounded from below
\,)
along any rational Bergman 1-PS in
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated.

(2)

The Donaldson functional has log norm singularities along any rational Bergman 1-PS in
\mathcal{H}_{k}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated, i.e. for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
we have

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)t+O(1),

where
O(1)
stands for the terms that remain bounded as
t\to+\infty
. In particular, the Donaldson functional is not bounded from below if
\mathcal{E}
is slope unstable.

(3)

We have

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{ref}})}{t}=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma)

for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{E}(k)
is globally generated.

8. From Hermitian–Einstein metrics to slope stability

In this section we provide a more geometric proof of a theorem of Kobayashi
[
Kob82
]
and Lübke
[
Lüb83
]
, as a simple application of Theorem
7.3
and the geodesic convexity of the Donaldson functional. We start by considering an irreducible holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
endowed with a Hermitian–Einstein metric.

Suppose that there exists a critical point for the Donaldson functional, which is necessarily the global minimum, say
h_{\mathrm{min}}
, over
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
by strict geodesic convexity (
cf.
Proposition
2.29
). Now we take
h_{\mathrm{min}}
as the reference metric
h_{\mathrm{ref}}
, and consider the Donaldson functional

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h):=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h,h_{\mathrm{min}})

for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{k}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
.

Using the results of Section
6
, consider
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(H^{0}(\mathcal{E}(k))^{\vee})
associated to a saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
, where we take
k\in\mathbb{N}
to be large enough so that
\mathcal{E}(k)
and
\mathcal{F}(k)
are both globally generated, and write
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{Q}}(k)
for the 1-PS generated by
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
. If
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})<0
, then from Theorem
7.3
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}})\to-\infty

as
t\to+\infty
, for the path
\{h_{\sigma_{t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. In particular, for large enough
t
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}})<\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\mathrm{min}})
, contradicting the definition of the minimum
h_{\mathrm{min}}
. Thus we get
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})\geq 0
for any saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
.

We now prove
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})>0
. Assume for contradiction
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})=0
. Using the geodesic completeness of
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, we consider the (nontrivial) geodesic between
h_{\sigma_{t}}
and
h_{\mathrm{min}}
(it may be not contained in
\mathcal{H}_{k}
, but this does not matter), and the geodesic distance
\mathrm{dist}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{min}})
between them. Then, from Lemma
6.3
, we have
\mathrm{dist}(h_{\sigma_{t}},h_{\mathrm{min}})\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
; recall that the sequence
\{h_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
,
h_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, converges to the reference metric
h_{\mathrm{min}}
in
C^{p}
for
p\geq 2
, as in Section
7
. Thus, using the strict geodesic convexity of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}
we obtain

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Don}}(h_{\sigma_{t}})\to+\infty,

as
t\to+\infty
.
But Theorem
7.3
implies that this contradicts the assumption
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})=0
. Consequently, we get
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NA}}(\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})>0
, which implies (by recalling Proposition
6.2
or
7.4
)

\mu(\mathcal{E})>\mu(\mathcal{F}),

and hence the slope stability of
\mathcal{E}
by Lemma
2.8
.

Finally, the case when
\mathcal{E}
is reducible can be treated similarly. By applying the previous argument to each irreducible component of
\mathcal{E}
, we find that
\mathcal{E}
is a direct sum of slope stable bundles. The Hermitian–Einstein equation implies that the slopes of these components are equal, and hence
\mathcal{E}
is slope polystable.

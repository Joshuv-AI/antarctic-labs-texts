An upper bound on the denominator of Eisenstein classes in Bianchi manifolds
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
A general conjecture of Harder relates the denominator of the Eisenstein cohomology of certain locally symmetric spaces to special values of L L -functions. In this paper we consider the locally symmetric space Y Γ = SL 2 ⁡ ( 𝒪 ) \ ℍ 3 Y_{\Gamma}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathscr{O})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{3} associated to SL 2 ⁡ ( K ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(K) where K K is an imaginary quadratic field. Berger proves a lower bound on the denominator of the Eisenstein cohomology in certain cases. In this paper, we show how results of Ito and Sczech can be used to prove an upper bound on the denominator in terms of a special value of L L -function. When the class number of K K is one, we combine this result with Berger’s result to obtain the exact denominator.

A few words on the proof of the theorem.

Let
\chi
be an unramified Hecke character of type
(-2,0)
. For every ideal class
\mathfrak{a}
corresponding to a cusp of
Y_{\Gamma}
, we have a form
\omega_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
on the corresponding boundary component. They are integral forms spanning the cohomology of the boundary
H^{1}(\partial X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})

E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
that span the
h

H_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})
. These forms are not integral, they define classes in
H_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};F)
but not in
\widetilde{H}_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};\mathscr{O}_{F})
.

On the other hand, we have another basis
\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
of the Eisenstein cohomology. These forms appear in the work of Ito
[
Ito87
]
. A more general construction of these Eisenstein classes
was done by Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia in
[
BCG20
,
BCG23
]
using the Mathai-Quillen formalism.

For a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
K
we define the Sczech cocycle
\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\begin{cases}I\left(\frac{a+d}{c}\right)G_{2}(\mathfrak{a})-D(a,c,\mathfrak{a})&\textrm{if}\quad c\neq 0,\\[8.5359pt]
I\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)G_{2}(\mathfrak{a})&\textrm{if}\quad c=0,\end{cases}

where
G_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
is an Eisenstein series and
D(a,c,\mathfrak{a})
a Dedekind sum; see Section
2
for the definitions. The form
\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
is related to the Sczech cocycle as follows

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathfrak{a}}(\gamma)=\chi(\mathfrak{a})\int_{u_{0}}^{\gamma u_{0}}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}},

where
u_{0}
is any point on
\mathbb{H}_{3}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. This formula is proved by using the idea of
[
BCG23
]
to move the path of integration
[u_{0},\gamma u_{0}]
to infinity. More precisely, choose a cusp

of
Y_{\Gamma}
and let
[r,\gamma^{-1}r]
be the modular symbol joining the two cusps
r

\gamma^{-1}r

[u_{0},\gamma u_{0}]
and
[r,\gamma^{-1}r]
; see Figure
4

gives the Dedekind sum, whereas the term

is a contribution from the cusps. Note that this formula already appears in the work of Ito
[
Ito87

[
Wes88

After a suitable normalization, the Sczech cocycle takes values in
\mathscr{O}_{H}

Remark 2.1

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma_{2}u_{0}}^{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}u_{0}}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}+\int_{u_{0}}^{\gamma_{2}u_{0}}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}

Lemma 3.8
.

For a fractional ideal

we have
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{b})=-\sqrt{D}\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{b})

Proof.

then

\mathcal{D}(L)=\omega_{1}\overline{\omega_{2}}-\overline{\omega_{1}}\omega_{2}=\begin{vmatrix}\omega_{1}&\omega_{2}\\

\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{b})=\begin{vmatrix}a+b\tau&c+d\tau\\[5.69046pt]

c&d\end{vmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}1&1\\
\tau&\overline{\tau}\end{vmatrix}=-\sqrt{D}(ad-bc).

, and
N=M^{-1}
. By (
3.26
) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}_{r}(\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}})=M^{\ast}\operatorname{res}_{\infty}(N^{\ast}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}).

(3.124)

where
\operatorname{res}_{\infty}(\omega)=\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\iota_{v}^{\ast}\omega.
After pulling back
N^{\ast}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}(u)
by the map
\iota_{v}(z)=z+jv
we get

\displaystyle\iota_{v}^{\ast}N^{\ast}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}(u)=\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{z}(u)dz+\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{\bar{z}}(u)d\bar{z}.

(3.125)

The limit of
\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{z}(u)
as
v
goes to
\infty
is the constant term in the Fourier expansion. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{z}(u)

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}_{M}^{-1}).

(3.126)

Similarly

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{\bar{z}}(u)

\displaystyle=-\chi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{a}_{M})^{2}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}_{M}).

(3.127)

Since for any fractional
\mathfrak{c}
ideal we have
\mathfrak{c}=\operatorname{N}(\mathfrak{c})\bar{\mathfrak{c}}^{-1}
, by the homogeneity of
G_{2}
we get

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{E}^{(N)}_{\bar{z}}(u)

\displaystyle=-\chi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{M}^{-1}).

(3.128)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}_{\infty}N^{\ast}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}=\chi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}\left(G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}_{M})dz-G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{M}^{-1})d\bar{z}\right).

(3.129)

After pulling back by
M
, and using that
[\mathfrak{a}_{M}]=[\mathfrak{a}_{c}]
we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}_{r}\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}=\chi(\mathfrak{a}_{c}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})\left(G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}_{c})\omega_{\chi,r}-G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{c}^{-1})\bar{\omega}_{\chi,r}\right).

(3.130)

Note that the right hand side of (
3.130
) does not depend on the ideal representatives
\mathfrak{a}_{c}
.

Let
\operatorname{res}_{r}(E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}})=\alpha(\mathfrak{a})\omega_{\chi,r}+\beta(\mathfrak{a})\bar{\omega}_{\chi,r}
be the restriction of
E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
to the boundary component
r
. Since the restriction map is linear, it follows from Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}_{r}(\widehat{E}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}{w(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})\operatorname{res}_{r}(E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{i}})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}{w(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})\alpha(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\right)\omega_{\chi,r}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}{w(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})\beta(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\right)\bar{\omega}_{\chi,r}.

(3.131)

Comparing with (
3.130
) we see that

\displaystyle\alpha(\mathfrak{a}_{i})=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if}\quad[\mathfrak{a}_{i}]=[\mathfrak{a}_{c}]\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

(3.132)

and

\displaystyle\beta(\mathfrak{a}_{i})=\begin{cases}-1&\textrm{if}\quad[\mathfrak{a}_{i}]=[\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{c}]\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.133)

Thus
\alpha(\mathfrak{a})=\delta_{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}_{c}}
and
\beta(\mathfrak{a})=-\delta_{\mathfrak{a},\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{c}}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We will not discuss this further in the rest of the paper, but let us mention that the fact that the Eisenstein classes have an integral restriction to the boundary is an important property to find congruences. See
[
Ber09
]
for more on the relation between the denominator ideal, congruences and the Selmer group.

Proposition 3.10
.

The forms
\{E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}\}_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\textrm{Cl}(K)}
span the Eisenstein cohomology
H^{1}_{\operatorname{Eis}}(Y_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let
V
be the subspace of the Eisenstein cohomology spanned by the forms
E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
. We know that the Eisenstein cohomology is
h
-dimensional, so
\dim(V)\leq h
. A class in
H^{1}(\partial X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})
is represented by a collection of forms
\alpha_{r}\omega_{\chi,r}+\beta_{r}\bar{\omega}_{\chi,r}
with
\alpha_{r},\beta_{r}
two complex numbers such that
\alpha_{\gamma r}=\alpha_{r}
and
\beta_{\gamma r}=\beta_{r}
. By Proposition
3.3
, the image of the restriction is the
-1
eigenspace by the involution
\iota
. Hence if the class lies in
\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{res})
, then we have in addition
\beta_{r}=-\alpha_{\bar{r}}
, by (
3.57
). It follows that the space
\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{res})
is spanned by the forms
\omega_{\chi,r}-\bar{\omega}_{\chi,\bar{r}}
. By the previous proposition, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}(E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{c}})=\omega_{\chi,r}-\bar{\omega}_{\chi,\bar{r}},

(3.134)

Hence the restriction map
\operatorname{res}\colon V\longrightarrow\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{res})
is surjective and
\dim(V)=h
.
∎

4
Denominators of the Eisenstein cohomology

We have seen that
H_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})
is an
h
-dimensional complex vector space spanned by the forms
E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
. The
{\mathscr{O}_{F}}
-lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}
of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{Eis}}(X_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}\coloneqq\operatorname{Eis}(\widetilde{H}^{1}(\partial X_{\Gamma};\mathscr{O}_{F}))=\bigoplus_{[\mathfrak{a}]\in\textrm{Cl}(K)}E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}{\mathscr{O}_{F}}

(4.1)

gives us an integral structure on
H^{1}_{\operatorname{Eis}}(X_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})
,
i.e

\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{F}}\mathbb{C}\simeq H^{1}_{\operatorname{Eis}}(X_{\Gamma},\mathbb{C})
. Another
{\mathscr{O}_{F}}
-lattice is given by the integral Eisenstein classes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}\coloneqq\widetilde{H}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};{\mathscr{O}_{F}})\cap H_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C})\subset H_{\operatorname{Eis}}^{1}(X_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C}).

(4.2)

We define the denominator of the Eisenstein cohomology to be the
\mathscr{O}_{F}
-ideal

\displaystyle\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}})\coloneqq\left\{\left.\lambda\in\mathscr{O}_{F}\right|\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}\subset\mathcal{L}_{0}\right\}.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.1
.

The submodule
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}
does not depend on the choice of the Hecke character
\chi
.

Proof.

Let us temporarily denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\chi,\operatorname{Eis}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\chi},\operatorname{Eis}}
the lattice that we obtain for two different Hecke characters
\widetilde{\chi}
and
\chi
of infinity type
(-2,0)
. Then
\varphi=\chi/\widetilde{\chi}
is an
F
-valued character on the class group of
K
. Since
E_{\widetilde{\chi},\mathfrak{a}}=\varphi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\chi,\operatorname{Eis}}=M\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\chi},\operatorname{Eis}}
, where

M=\operatorname{diag}(\varphi(\mathfrak{a}_{1})^{-1},\dots,\varphi(\mathfrak{a}_{h})^{-1}).

Since
\varphi(\mathfrak{a})^{h}=1
, the value
\varphi(\mathfrak{a})
is a unit in
\mathscr{O}_{F}
and
M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{h}(\mathscr{O}_{F})
. Furthermore, the determinant of
M
is

\det(M)=\prod_{\mathfrak{a}\in\textrm{Cl}(K)}\varphi(\mathfrak{a})^{-1}.

The substitution
\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
shows that
\det(M)=\det(M)^{-1}
. Hence
\det(M)=\pm 1
and
M
is in
\operatorname{GL}_{h}(\mathscr{O}_{F})
.
∎

4.1

The upper bound

We will need the following result on Dedekind determinants, of which a proof can be found in
[
Lan90
, Chapter. 3, Theorem. 6.1]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
any complex valued function on a finite abelian group
G
. Then

\det(f(a^{-1}b))_{a,b\in G}=\prod_{\varphi\in\widehat{G}}\left(\sum_{a\in G}\varphi(a)f(a^{-1})\right).

Theorem 4.3
.

The form

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{D}}L^{\operatorname{int}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0)E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}}

is integral in
\mathscr{O}_{F}
. In particular, the denominator has the upper bound

L^{\operatorname{int}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0){\mathscr{O}_{F}}\subset\operatorname{Den}(\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Eis}}).

Proof.

Let
M_{\chi}\in\operatorname{GL}_{h}(F)
be the matrix such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{E}^{\operatorname{alg}}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{E}^{\operatorname{alg}}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{h}}\end{pmatrix}=M_{\chi}\begin{pmatrix}E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{h}}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.4)

By Proposition
3.6
, we have

M_{\chi}=\left[\Omega^{-2}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}^{-1})}{w(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\mathfrak{b})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\mathfrak{b})\right]_{[\mathfrak{a}],[\mathfrak{b}]\in\textrm{Cl}(K)}.

Applying the previous lemma to the function

f(\mathfrak{a})=\Omega^{-2}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}{w(\mathfrak{a})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}),

we then have

\displaystyle\det(M_{\chi})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\varphi\in\widehat{\textrm{Cl}(K)}}\left(\Omega^{-2}\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in\textrm{Cl}(K)}\varphi(\mathfrak{a})\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a})}{w(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}G_{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\varphi\in\widehat{\textrm{Cl}(K)}}\Omega^{-2}L(\varphi\chi,0)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\varphi\in\widehat{\textrm{Cl}(K)}}L^{\operatorname{alg}}(\varphi\chi,0)

\displaystyle=L^{\operatorname{alg}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0).

(4.5)

We have already seen in the proof of Proposition
2.4
that for any fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{D}f(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})

\displaystyle=4\sqrt{D}\frac{\chi(\mathfrak{a})}{w(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})}\lambda(\mathfrak{a})^{2}G_{2}(L_{\mathfrak{a}})\in{\mathscr{O}_{F}}

(4.6)

is integral. Hence the matrix
\widetilde{M}_{\chi}\coloneqq 4\sqrt{D}M_{\chi}
is in
\operatorname{GL}_{h}(F)\cap\operatorname{Mat}_{h}({\mathscr{O}_{F}})
. Furthermore, the adjoint matrix
N_{\chi}\coloneqq\det(\widetilde{M}_{\chi})\widetilde{M}^{-1}_{\chi}
also has coefficients in
{\mathscr{O}_{F}}
and

\displaystyle N_{\chi}M_{\chi}

\displaystyle=4^{-1}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{\chi}\widetilde{M}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=4^{-1}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}\det(\widetilde{M}_{\chi})

\displaystyle=4^{h-1}D^{\frac{h-1}{2}}\det(M_{\chi})

\displaystyle=4^{h-1}D^{\frac{h-1}{2}}L^{\operatorname{alg}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0)

\displaystyle=4^{-1}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}L^{\operatorname{int}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0).

(4.7)

Applying
2N_{\chi}
to both sides of (
4.4
) we find that

\displaystyle 2N_{\chi}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{E}^{\operatorname{alg}}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{E}^{\operatorname{alg}}_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{h}}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{D}}L^{\operatorname{int}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0)\begin{pmatrix}E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{h}}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.8)

Since the left hand side is integral, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{D}}L^{\operatorname{int}}(\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{H/K},0)E_{\chi,\mathfrak{a}_{i}}

(4.9)

is integral for any
i
.
∎

Relation to the work of Berger

In his work
[

]

[

We recall the setting of
[
Ber09

\phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a pair of Hecke characters on the idèles
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
, where
\phi_{1,\infty}(z)=z
and
\phi_{2,\infty}(z)=z^{-1}
. Hence, the character
\chi=\phi_{1}/\phi_{2}
has infinity type

. Suppose that
\chi
is unramified.
Let
G\coloneqq\operatorname{GL}_{2/K}
, let

\mathfrak{M}_{i}
be the conductor of

and set

\displaystyle K_{f}=\prod_{v\mid\mathfrak{M}_{1}}U^{1}(\mathfrak{M}_{1,v})\prod_{v\mid\mathfrak{M}_{1}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathscr{O}_{v})

(4.10)

where

\mathfrak{M}_{1,v}
is the ideal
\mathfrak{M}_{1}\mathscr{O}_{v}
. We define the adelic space

\displaystyle S_{K_{f}}\coloneqq G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/K_{\infty}K_{f}.

(4.11)

Since the class number of
K
is one, we have a decomposition

\displaystyle G(\mathbb{A}_{f})=G(\mathbb{Q})K_{f}.

(4.12)

This yields the isomorphism

\displaystyle S_{K_{f}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow Y_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle G(\mathbb{Q})(g_{\infty},g_{f})K_{\infty}K_{f}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\Gamma g^{-1}g_{\infty}j,

(4.13)

where we write
g_{f}=gk_{f}
according to the decomposition (
4.12
), and
\Gamma=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K_{f}
.

Remark 4.1
.

The fact that

that is congruent to

and

are the same, up to a potential factor

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{k}
be the Lie algebra of
K_{\infty}
. We have an orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
with respect to the Killing form. We consider the following elements in

:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad S=\frac{1}{2}\left(X\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}1-Y\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}i\right).

(4.14)

Lemma 4.4
.

After identifying
\mathfrak{p}
with the tangent space of
\mathbb{H}_{3}
at
j

X\simeq 2{\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=j}},\qquad Y\simeq 2{\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=j}},\qquad S\simeq 2{\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=j}}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=j}}-i{\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=j}}.

Proof.

Let us prove the statement for

. Let

be a function on
\mathbb{H}_{3}
with coordinates

. The action of
X
on

at

is given by

\displaystyle(Xf)(j)\coloneqq{\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{d}{dt}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{t=0}}f(\Phi_{X}(t))

(4.15)

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}(t)\coloneqq\exp(tX)j=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh(t)&\sinh(t)\\
\sinh(t)&\cosh(t)\end{pmatrix}j.

(4.16)

In the coordinates

, the map is explicitely given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}(t)=\frac{1}{\cosh(t)^{2}+\sinh(t)^{2}}(\sinh(2t),0,1)\in\mathbb{H}_{3}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}

(4.17)

and its derivative is

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{X}(0)=(2,0,0).

(4.18)

Hence

\displaystyle(Xf)(j)=\Phi^{\prime}_{X}(0)d_{u=j}f=2\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(j).

(4.19)

The computation for
Y
is similar.

∎

Spectral computation with third-order tensors using the t-product
A. El Hachimi, K. Jbilou, A. Ratnani, L. Reichel

Abstract
The tensor t-product, introduced by Kilmer and Martin [ 26 ] , is a powerful tool for the analysis of and computation with third-order tensors. This paper introduces eigentubes and eigenslices of third-order tensors under the t-product. The eigentubes and eigenslices are analogues of eigenvalues and eigenvectors for matrices. Properties of eigentubes and eigenslices are investigated and numerical methods for their computation are described. The methods include the tensor power method, tensor subspace iteration, and the tensor QR algorithm. Computed examples illustrate the performance of these methods.



keywords

tensor, t-product, eigentube, eigenslice, tensor power method, tensor subspace iteration,
tensor QR algorithm.

The t-product for third order tensors

This section reviews definitions and results by Kilmer et al.

[
25
,
26
]
and uses notation from there, as well as from Kolda and
Bader
[
3
]
. Consider the third-order tensor

\mathscr{A}=[\mathscr{A}_{i,j,k}]\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times p\times n}
. A
slice
of

\mathscr{A}
is a 2D section obtained by fixing any one of the indices. Using MATLAB-type
notation,
\mathscr{A}_{i,:,:}
,
\mathscr{A}_{:,j,:}
, and
\mathscr{A}_{:,:,k}
stand
for the
i
th horizontal,
j
th lateral, and
k
th frontal slices of
\mathscr{A}
,
respectively. We also denote the
j
th lateral slice by
\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{j}
. It is a
tensor and is referred to as a
tensor column
. Sometimes it is convenient to
identify
\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{j}
with a matrix. The
k
th frontal slice,

\mathscr{A}_{:,:,k}
is also denoted by
\mathscr{A}^{(k)}
and is a matrix. A

fiber
of a third order tensor
\mathscr{A}
is a 1D section obtained by fixing any
two of the indices. Thus,
\mathscr{A}_{:,j,k}
,
\mathscr{A}_{i,:,k}
, and

\mathscr{A}_{i,j,:}
denote mode-1, mode-2, and mode-3 fibers (tubes), respectively. We
will use the notation
\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times p\times n}
for the space of
third-order tensors,
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times 1\times n}
stands for the space of lateral
slices, and
\mathbb{K}_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{1\times 1\times n}
denotes the space of tubes. Many of the tensors
considered will be
square
, i.e.,
\ell=p
.

The inner product of two third-order tensors

\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
is given by

\langle\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\rangle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{\ell,p,n}\mathscr{A}_{i,j,k}\bar{\mathscr{B}}_{i,j,k},

where the bar denotes complex conjugation, and the Frobenius norm of
\mathscr{A}
is
defined as

\left\|\mathscr{A}\right\|_{F}=\langle\mathscr{A},\mathscr{A}\rangle^{1/2}.

We note that a third-order tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
can be represented
as

\mathscr{A}=\left[\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{1},\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{p}\right].

The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) is helpful for the efficient evaluation of the
t-product of two tensors. The DFT of a vector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

\widehat{v}=F_{n}v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

(1)

where
F_{n}
denotes the DFT matrix defined as

F_{n}=\left[\left(e^{\frac{2i\pi}{n}}\right)^{(k-1)(j-1)}\right]_{k=1:n,j=1:n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}.

While the matrix
F_{n}
is not unitary, the scaled matrix
F_{n}/\sqrt{n}
is. The
computation of the vector
\widehat{v}
by straightforward evaluation of the matrix-vector
product in the right-hand side of (
1
) requires
O(n^{2})
arithmetic floating point
operations (flops). It is well known that this flop count is reduced to
O(n\log(n))
when
using the fast Fourier transform (FFT). This can be done in MATLAB with the command

\widehat{v}={\tt fft}(v)
.

Let
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
denote the tensor obtained by applying
the DFT along the third dimension of the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times p\times n}
.
Thus, we evaluate the DFT of each tube of
\mathscr{A}
; this can be done in MATLAB with
the command

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}={\tt fft}\left(\mathscr{A},[\,],3\right).

To transform
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}
back to
\mathscr{A}
, we use the MATLAB command

\mathscr{A}={\tt ifft}\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}},[\,],3\right).

For
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
, we have

\left\|\mathscr{A}\right\|_{F}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\widehat{\mathscr{A}}\|_{F},\quad\langle\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{n}\langle\widehat{\mathscr{A}},\widehat{\mathscr{B}}\rangle.

The following definitions are required below. For
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
, we
define the associated block diagonal matrix

{\tt bdiag}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}^{(1)}&&&&\\
&\mathscr{A}^{(2)}&&&\\
&&\mathscr{A}^{(3)}&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&\mathscr{A}^{(n)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell n\times pn}.

Also introduce the block circulant matrix

{\tt bcirc}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}^{(1)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n-1)}&\dots&\mathscr{A}^{(2)}\\
\mathscr{A}^{(2)}&\mathscr{A}^{(1)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n)}&\dots&\mathscr{A}^{(3)}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathscr{A}^{(n-1)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n-2)}&\dots&\mathscr{A}^{(1)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n)}\\
\mathscr{A}^{(n)}&\mathscr{A}^{(n-1)}&\dots&\mathscr{A}^{(2)}&\mathscr{A}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell n\times pn}.

The operators
{\tt unfold}
and
{\tt fold}
are defined as

{\tt unfold}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}^{(1)}\\
\mathscr{A}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathscr{A}^{(n)}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ell n\times p}\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\tt fold}({\tt unfold}\left(\mathscr{A}\right))=\mathscr{A}.

Thus,
{\tt unfold}
transforms the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times\ell}_{n}
into a
matrix and
{\tt fold}
is the inverse operator.

A block circulant matrix can be block diagonalized by using the DFT. Kilmer et al.

[
25
]
use this property to establish that for

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
, we have

\left(F_{n}\otimes I_{\ell}\right){\tt bcirc}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\left(F_{n}^{H}\otimes I_{p}\right)={\tt bdiag}(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}),

where
\otimes
stands for the Kronecker product. The following definition is due to
Kilmer et al.
[
25
]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times q}_{n}
and
\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{q\times p}_{n}
. Then the
t-product of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
is defined as

\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}={\tt fold}\left({\tt bcirc}\left(\mathscr{A}\right){\tt unfold}\left(\mathscr{B}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}.

(2)

The evaluation of the t-product by using the above definition is quite costly unless the
tensors
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are very sparse. Kilmer et al.

[
25
]
therefore propose to evaluate the t-product (
2
) with the
aid of the FFT. The following result makes this possible.

Proposition 1
.

(
[
25
]
)
Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times q}_{n}
and
\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{q\times p}_{n}
. Then the
tensor
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}
can be determined from

{\tt bdiag}(\widehat{\mathscr{C}})={\tt bdiag}(\widehat{\mathscr{A}})\,{\tt bdiag}(\widehat{\mathscr{B}}).

We briefly consider the special case of evaluating the t-product of real tensors

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times q\times n}
and
\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times p\times n}
. It
is well known that for
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the vector
\widehat{v}
satisfies

\widehat{v}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }{\tt conj}\left(\widehat{v}_{j}\right)=\widehat{v}_{n-i+1}\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ \penalty\ }i=1,\ldots,\left[\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right],

(3)

where
{\tt conj}
denotes the complex conjugation operator and
\left[(n+1)/2\right]

stands for the integer part of
(n+1)/2
. A vector that satisfies (
3
) is said to
be conjugate-even. Moreover, if the
n
-vector
\tilde{v}
is real and conjugate-even,
then
v=F_{n}^{H}\tilde{v}
is a real conjugate-even vector, where the superscript
H

denotes transposition and complex conjugation; see, e.g.,
[
24
, Chapter 15]
for many
properties of the DFT, as well as
[
33
]
.

Let the operator
{\tt vec}
denote the vectorization of a tube. We say that the tube

\bm{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is real conjugate-even, if the vector
a={\tt vec}(\bm{a})
is real
conjugate-even. By exploiting the symmetry of the DFT, it follows that for real
third-order tensors
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times p\times n}
, we have

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times p}\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}=\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(n-i+2)},\;i=2,3,\ldots,\left[\dfrac{n-1}{2}\right].

We recall the main properties of the t-product that will be used in the sequel. Proofs can
be found in
[
25
]
.

Proposition 2
.

(i)

The identity tensor under the t-product,
\mathscr{I}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
, has
the first frontal slice equal to the identity matrix and the remaining frontal slices are
zero matrices.

(ii)

The canonical lateral slice under the t-product,
\vec{\mathscr{E}}_{j}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell}_{n}
,
where
1\leq j\leq\ell
, has the
(j,1,1)
th entry equal to one, and all other entries
zero.

(iii)

The unit tube,
\bm{e}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
, has the entry
(1,1,1)
equal to one, and all other
entries zero.

(iv)

The conjugate transpose,
\mathscr{A}^{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times\ell}_{n}
, of the tensor

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
is obtained by first transposing and conjugating each
one of the frontal slices of
\mathscr{A}
, and then reversing the order of the conjugated
transposed frontal slices 2 through
n
. The tensor conjugate transpose has similar
properties as the matrix transpose. For instance, if
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are
tensors such that
\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{H}*\mathscr{A}^{H}
are defined,
then
(\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B})^{H}=\mathscr{B}^{H}*\mathscr{A}^{H}
.

(v)

A tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is said to be normal if

\mathscr{A}^{H}*\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{A}^{H}
.

(vi)

A third order tensor is said to be f-diagonal if its frontal slices in the Fourier
domain are diagonal matrices.

(vii)

A third order tensor is said to be f-upper-triangular if its frontal slices in the Fourier
domain are upper triangular matrices.

(viii)

The tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
is said to be f-Hermitian if

\mathscr{A}^{H}=\mathscr{A}
. In order for
\mathscr{A}
to be f-Hermitian each frontal
slice of
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}
has to be a Hermitian matrix.

(ix)

A tensor
\mathscr{Q}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
is said to be f-orthogonal (or f-unitary)
if
\mathscr{Q}^{H}*\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{Q}*\mathscr{Q}^{H}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell}
.

(x)

A tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
is invertible if there is a tensor

\mathscr{A}^{-1}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
such that

\mathscr{A}^{-1}*\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{A}^{-1}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell}
. This
definition implies that a third order tensor is invertible under the t-product if and only
if each frontal slice in the Fourier domain is invertible.

(xi)

Let
p
be a positive integer and
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
. Then

\mathscr{A}^{p}=\mathscr{A}*\ldots*\mathscr{A}
. We define
\mathscr{A}^{0}=\mathscr{I}_{\ell}
.

(xii)

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
. Then
\mathscr{A}\not\equiv\textbf{0}
means that

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\neq 0
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

In the following we will require the t-product of two tubes. This operation has been
defined in
[
25
]
. The definition suggests how the quotient of two tubes
can be defined.

Definition 3
.

Let
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
. Then

\bm{a}*\bm{b}={\tt ifft}(\widehat{\bm{a}}\circ\widehat{\bm{b}},[],3),

where
\circ
denotes the entry-wise product of the vectors and, as usual,

\widehat{\bm{a}}
and
\widehat{\bm{b}}
stand for the Fourier transform of the tubes

\bm{a}
and
\bm{b}
, respectively. Note that the tube product commutes.

Let, in addition, all entries of
\widehat{\bm{b}}
be nonvanishing. Then we
can define

\frac{\bm{a}}{\bm{b}}={\tt ifft}(\widehat{\bm{a}}\div\widehat{\bm{b}},[],3),

where
\div
denotes entry-wise division.

We also will need to evaluate the product of a tensor and a tube, as well as the
quotient of a tensor and a tube.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
and let
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
. With the usual notation,
we define the product
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}*\bm{b}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
as

\mathscr{C}={\tt ifft}(\widehat{\mathscr{C}},[],3),\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ where\penalty\ \penalty\ }\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{(i)}=\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\widehat{\bm{b}}^{(i)},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

We note that

\mathscr{A}*\bm{b}=\bm{b}*\mathscr{A}.

(4)

If, in addition, all entries of
\widehat{\bm{b}}
are nonvanishing, then we define the
quotient
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}/\bm{b}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
by

\mathscr{C}={\tt ifft}(\widehat{\mathscr{C}},[],3)
, where

\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{(i)}=\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}/\widehat{\bm{b}}^{(i)},\quad i=1,2,\dots,n.

We recall that the lateral slices
\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{j}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell}_{n}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,p
, of
the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
are third-order tensors. Therefore, it
follows from (
4
) that for any tube
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
,

\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{j}*\bm{b}=\bm{b}*\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{j}.

(5)

We will use this property below.

It is convenient to introduce the following space.

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=\{\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{1},\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{p}\}\subset\mathbb{K}^{\ell}_{n}
be the set of lateral slices of the tensor

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
. Then the set of the t-linear combinations of
these slices is a lateral slice space given by

\displaystyle\tspan\left\{\mathbb{G}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tspan\left\{\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{1},\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{2},\ldots,\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\vec{\mathscr{C}}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell}_{n}:\penalty\ \vec{\mathscr{C}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\vec{\mathscr{A}}_{i}*\bm{b}_{i},\mbox{\penalty\ \penalty\ where\penalty\ \penalty\ }\bm{b}_{i}\in\mathbb{K}^{n},\penalty\ i=1,2,\ldots,p\right\}.

3
Tensor factorizations

Many matrix factorizations can be extended to third-order tensors under the t-product. We
will review two available factorizations and introduce a new one, that will be used in the
sequel.

Theorem 6
.

(t-SVD) (
[
25
]
)
Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
. Then, using the t-product,
\mathscr{A}

can be factored into three third-order tensors,

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{U}*\mathscr{S}*\mathscr{V}^{H},

(6)

where
\mathscr{U}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
and
\mathscr{V}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
are
f-orthogonal tensors and
\mathscr{S}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
is an f-diagonal tensor. We
refer to the factorization (
6
) as the
singular tube decomposition
.

The tubes
\bm{s}_{i}=\mathscr{S}_{i,i,:}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,\min\{\ell,p\}
of the tensor

\mathscr{S}
are referred to as singular tubes, whereas

\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{j}=\mathscr{U}_{:,j,:}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,\ell
and

\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}=\mathscr{V}_{:,k,:}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,p
are called left and right
singular (lateral) slices, respectively. The norm of the singular tubes
\bm{s}_{i}
, given
by
\sigma_{i}=\left\|\bm{s}_{i}\right\|_{F}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,\min\{\ell,p\}
, are
referred to as the singular values of the tensor
\mathscr{A}
; they are enumerated in
decreasing order. The singular triplets

\{\bm{s}_{i},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i},\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\min\{\ell,p\}}
satisfy

\mathscr{A}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}=\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}*\bm{s}_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathscr{A}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}=\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}*\bm{s}_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ 1=1,2,\ldots,\min\{\ell,p\}.

We note that equation (
6
) can be written as

\mathscr{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{\ell,p\}}\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}*\bm{s}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}^{H}=\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{\ell,p\}}\bm{s}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}^{H},

where the last equality follows from (
5
).

Kilmer et al.
[
25
]
also describe the t-QR factorization of a tensor

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
,

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{Q}*\mathscr{R},

where
\mathscr{Q}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times\ell}_{n}
is an f-orthogonal tensor and

\mathscr{R}\in\mathbb{K}^{\ell\times p}_{n}
is an f-upper-triangular tensor.

Theorem 7
.

Let the third-order tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
be invertible. Then LU
factorization with partial pivoting can be applied in the Fourier domain, resulting in
the t-LU decomposition

\mathscr{P}*\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{L}*\mathscr{U},

(7)

where
\mathscr{P}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is the Fourier transform of permutation matrices,

\mathscr{L}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is an f-unit-lower triangular tensor, and

\mathscr{U}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is an f-upper-triangular tensor.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}={\tt fft}(\mathscr{A},[\,],3)
. Then, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we
apply LU factorization with partial pivoting to the frontal slices

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}
of
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}
. This gives the decompositions

\widehat{P}^{(i)}\widehat{A}^{(i)}=\widehat{L}^{(i)}\widehat{U}^{(i)},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
\widehat{P}^{(i)}
is a permutation matrix,
\widehat{L}^{(i)}
is a unit lower
triangular matrix, and
\widehat{U}^{(i)}
is an upper triangular matrix. These
decompositions exist because the tensor
\mathscr{A}
is nonsingular. The matrices

\widehat{P}^{(i)}
,
\widehat{L}^{(i)}
, and
\widehat{U}^{(i)}
are frontal slices of
the tensors
\widehat{P}
,
\widehat{L}
, and
\widehat{U}
. The tensors in the
decomposition (
7
) are determined by

\mathscr{P}={\tt ifft}\left(\widehat{\mathscr{P}},[\,],3\right),\quad\mathscr{L}={\tt ifft}\left(\widehat{\mathscr{L}},[\,],3\right),\quad\mathscr{U}={\tt ifft}\left(\widehat{\mathscr{U}},[\,],3\right).

∎

The following factorization generalizes the Hessenberg reduction for matrices. It will be
used below in the computation of tensor eigentubes and eigenslices.

Theorem 8
.

The tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is unitarily similar to an f-upper-Hessenberg
tensor, i.e.,

\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{W}^{H}*\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{W},

where
\mathscr{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is an f-upper-Hessenberg tensor (all
frontal slices in the Fourier domain are upper Hessenberg matrices) and

\mathscr{W}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
is an f-unitary tensor.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}={\tt fft}(\mathscr{A},[\,],3)
. Then, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, the
frontal slice
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}
of
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}
is unitarily
similar to a Hessenberg matrix
H_{i}
. We have

H_{i}=W_{i}^{H}\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}W_{i},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n,

where the
H_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
are upper Hessenberg matrices and the

W_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
are unitary matrices.

Define the tensors
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
as follows:

\mathscr{H}^{\prime(i)}=H_{i}{\rm\penalty\ \penalty\ and\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathscr{W}^{\prime(i)}=W_{i},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

Then

\mathscr{H}^{\prime(i)}=\left(\mathscr{W}^{\prime(i)}\right)^{H}\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\mathscr{W}^{\prime(i)},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

Consequently, defining the tensors

\mathscr{H}={\tt ifft}\left(\mathscr{H}^{\prime},[\,],3\right)
and

\mathscr{W}={\tt ifft}\left(\mathscr{W}^{\prime},[\,],3\right)
, we conclude that

\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{W}^{H}*\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{W},

where
\mathscr{H}
is an f-Hessenberg tensor since each frontal slice is an upper
Hessenberg matrix in the Fourier domain; obviously the tensor
\mathscr{W}
is unitary,
since its frontal slices in the Fourier domain are unitary.
∎

Following Kilmer et al.

The value of this bilinear form is a tube and the quantity

The next lemma gives some properties of this bilinear form.

Lemma 9

(

Tensor eigenpairs based on the t-product

This section introduces tensor eigenpairs for third-order tensors defined with the aid of
the t-product.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
and let

\gamma_{1,i},\gamma_{2,i},\ldots,\gamma_{p,i}
be the eigenvalues of

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, ordered such as

|\gamma_{j,i}|\geq|\gamma_{j+1,i}|,\quad j=1,2,\ldots,p-1.

The tubes
\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{p}
defined by

\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{(i)}=\gamma_{j,i},\;i=1,2,\ldots,n,\;j=1,2,\ldots,p,

are
eigentubes
of
\mathscr{A}
. For a given eigentube
\bm{\lambda}
of

\mathscr{A}
, a nonvanishing lateral slice
\vec{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathbb{K}^{p}_{n}
such that

\mathscr{A}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}=\bm{\lambda}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}

(8)

is said to be an
eigenslice
or
right eigenslice
of
\mathscr{A}
associated
with
\bm{\lambda}
. Moreover, the set
\{\bm{\lambda},\vec{\mathscr{U}}\}
is referred to
as an eigenpair of
\mathscr{A}
.

Note that equations (
8
) and (
5
), imply that

\mathscr{A}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}=\vec{\mathscr{U}}*\bm{\lambda}.

We remark that when
n=1
, the third-order tensor reduces to a square matrix, and the
eigentubes and eigenslices become eigenvalues and eigenvectors, respectively.

A tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}^{p\times p}
has
p
eigentubes. Let

\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{p}
be the eigentubes of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then

\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{1}\right\|_{F}\geq\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{2}\right\|_{F}\geq\ldots\geq\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{p}\right\|_{F}.

The set of all eigentubes of
\mathscr{A}
is referred to as the
spectrum
of

\mathscr{A}
and is denoted by
\bm{\Lambda}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)
. The

spectral radius
of
\mathscr{A}
is defined as

\rho\left(\mathscr{A}\right)=\max_{\bm{\lambda}\in\bm{\Lambda}\left(\mathscr{A}\right)}\left\|\bm{\lambda}\right\|_{F}.

The eigenvalues of a matrix are the zeros of the characteristic polynomial for the matrix.
We will derive an analogous result for third-order tensors. To this end, we require the
definition of the determinant of a square third-order tensor.

Definition 11
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
. The determinant of
\mathscr{A}
under the
t-product is the tube

\bm{\gamma}=\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\in\mathbb{K}_{n},

which is defined by the inverse Fourier transformation of the tube
\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}

with
\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)}=\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right)
,

i=1,2,\ldots,n
, where
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}={\tt fft}(\mathscr{A},[\,],3)
.

Thus, the determinant of a third-order tensor under the t-product is a function

\begin{split}\tdet:\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}&\to\mathbb{K}_{n}\\
\mathscr{A}&\mapsto\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right),\end{split}

such that
\widehat{\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)}^{(i)}=\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.
This determinant has the following properties.

Proposition 12
.

Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
and let

\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
. Then

(i)

\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}\right)=\tdet\left(\mathscr{B}*\mathscr{A}\right)
,

(ii)

\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}^{H}\right)=\left(\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\right)^{H}
,

(iii)

\tdet\left(\bm{\alpha}*\mathscr{A}\right)=\bm{\alpha}^{p}*\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}
. Then

\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{(i)}=\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{(i)}
for

i=1,2,\dots,n
. Consider
\bm{\gamma}=\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)}=\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{C}}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right).

We conclude that
\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}*\mathscr{B}\right)=\tdet\left(\mathscr{B}*\mathscr{A}\right)
.

(ii)

Let
\bm{\gamma}=\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}^{H}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)}=\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}^{H}}^{(i)}\right)=\det\left(\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right)^{H}\right)={\tt conj}\left(\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right)\right).

This shows that
\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}^{H}\right)=\left(\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)\right)^{H}
.

(iii)

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
. Then

\det\left(\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)}\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right)=\left(\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)}\right)^{p}\det\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}\right).

By the definition of the power of a third-order tensor, we obtain

\tdet\left(\bm{\alpha}*\mathscr{A}\right)=\bm{\alpha}^{p}*\tdet\left(\mathscr{A}\right)
.

∎

Definition 15

Proposition 16

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}

Proposition 18

A tensor

.

p

Proposition 21
.

Let
\vec{\mathscr{U}}

Theorem 22
.

Let the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}

\bm{\lambda}

\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}

m_{i}=\dim\left(\Null\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(i)}-\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}^{(i)}I\right)\right),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

Since
\bm{\lambda}
has geometric multiplicity
m
, we have
m=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}m_{i}
,
and the result follows.
∎

Definition 27
.

Assume that the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
has
\ell
distinct eigentubes

\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{\ell}
. The index of
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
is
the smallest integer such that

\tNull\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\mathscr{I}_{p}\right)^{\ell_{i}}=\tNull\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\mathscr{I}_{p}\right)^{k},\;\;\forall k\geq\ell_{i}.

Let
\ell_{i}
be the index of the eigentube
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
of
\mathscr{A}
. Then

\ell_{i}
is the maximum index of the eigenvalues
\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}^{(j)}
of the
matrix
\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(j)}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Note that if
\bm{\lambda}_{i}

is an f-semi-simple eigentube of
\mathscr{A}
, then
\ell_{i}=1
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\vec{\mathscr{X}}\in\mathbb{K}^{p}_{n}
. Then
\vec{\mathscr{X}}
can be represented as

\vec{\mathscr{X}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i},

where
\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}\in\tNull\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\mathscr{I}_{p}\right)^{\ell_{i}}
and
\ell_{i}
is the index of

\bm{\lambda}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{\mathscr{X}}\in\mathbb{K}^{p}_{n}
and let
\widehat{\vec{\mathscr{X}}}
denote the
associated tensor in the Fourier domain. The frontal slices

\widehat{\vec{\mathscr{X}}}^{(k)}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, of
\widehat{\vec{\mathscr{X}}}

live in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. There is a unique

x_{j,k}\in\Null\left(\widehat{\mathscr{A}}^{(k)}-\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{(k)}I\right)^{\ell_{i}}

such that

\widehat{\vec{\mathscr{X}}}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}x_{j,k}.

The sequences
\{x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{\ell,1}\},\;\ldots,\;\{x_{1,n},\ldots,x_{\ell,n}\}

determine the sequence of lateral slices

\{\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{1},\ldots,\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{\ell}\}
, such that

\vec{\mathscr{X}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{j},

with
\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{j}\in\tNull\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\mathscr{I}_{p}\right)^{\ell_{i}}
.
∎

5
Methods for eigenpair computation of third-order tensors

This section describes several methods for computing one or several eigenpairs of
third-order tensors.

5.1
Single lateral slice iteration

The power method is one of the oldest and simplest techniques for computing the
eigenvalue of largest magnitude and an associated eigenvector of a square matrix; see,
e.g.,
[
22
]
. It is also known as von Mises iteration
[
35
]
.
Presently, this method finds applications to page-rank computations; see

[
5
,
10
]
. We describe a t-power method for computing the eigenslice of
largest magnitude of a third-order tensor, and discuss some of its properties. These
properties are well known in matrix computations. Kilmer et al.
[
25
]

present an algorithm that is slightly different from the t-power method described below,
however, without defining eigentubes and eigenslices.

5.2
The t-power method

This method determines a sequence of lateral slices
\mathscr{A}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}
,

k=1,2,\ldots\penalty\
, suitably scaled, where
\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}\in\mathbb{K}^{p}_{n}
. Under suitable
conditions on
\mathscr{A}
and
\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}
(see below), the sequence generated
converges to an eigenslice associated with the eigentube of largest norm. Here and below
“norm” refers to the Frobenius norm. The main advantage of the t-power method, when
compared with other methods for computing eigenpairs, is the fairly small amount of
computer memory required. Algorithm
3
describes the computations with the
t-power method.

Theorem 29

Let

Theorem 30
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
have

distinct eigentubes

\bm{\lambda}_{1},\bm{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{p}

\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{1}\right\|_{F}>\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{2}\right\|_{F}\geq\ldots\geq\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{p}\right\|_{F}

\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}

\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}

\bm{\lambda}_{1}

Proof.

By Proposition
28
, we have

\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\bm{c}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i},

where
\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}\in\tNull\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\mathscr{I}_{p}\right)

Since
\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}
has a component in the invariant subspace associated with

\bm{\lambda}_{1}

\bm{c}_{1}\neq 0
.

We are interested in the space spanned by
\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
. This space is independent
of the scaling factors
\bm{\alpha}_{k}\neq\bm{0}
in Algorithm
3
. Therefore we may
assume in this proof may ignore the
\bm{\alpha}_{k}
for all
k
. We then have for

k=1,2,\ldots\penalty\
that

\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}=\mathscr{A}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\bm{c}_{i}*\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}=\bm{c}_{1}*\bm{\lambda}_{1}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{p}\left(\dfrac{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\right)^{k}*\bm{c}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}.

Therefore

\begin{split}\left\|\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}-\bm{c}_{1}*\bm{\lambda}_{1}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{1}\right\|_{F}=\left\|\sum_{i=2}^{p}\left(\dfrac{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\right)^{k}*\bm{c}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}\right\|_{F}&\leq\sum_{i=2}^{p}\left\|\left(\dfrac{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\right)^{k}*\bm{c}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{i}\right\|_{F}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=2}^{p}\left\|\dfrac{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\right\|_{F}^{k}\left\|\bm{c}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{i}\right\|_{F}.\end{split}

Since
\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{i}\right\|_{F}<\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{1}\right\|_{F}
for
all

k
increases. It follows
that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\vec{\mathscr{V}}_{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\bm{c}_{1}*\bm{\lambda}_{1}^{k}*\vec{\mathscr{X}}_{1}.

This shows the theorem.
∎

We can see from the proof of Theorem
30
, that the rate of convergence is larger,
the smaller the quotients

\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{i}\right\|_{F}/\left\|\bm{\lambda}_{1}\right\|_{F}
are for

.

The rate of convergence of the t-power method may be improved by applying the method to
the shifted tensor
\mathscr{A}+\bm{\sigma}*\mathscr{I}_{p}
for a suitable shift

\bm{\sigma}
, instead of applying the method to
\mathscr{A}
. This follows from the
proof of Theorem
30
. We omit the details.

In its simplest form this method can be applied to compute an eigenslice associated with
the eigentube of smallest norm. Then the method is obtained by applying the t-power method
to the tensor

contains a

\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}^{-1}

5.4
Deflation

Deflation is a well known technique in matrix computation. It makes it possible to use the
power method to compute more eigenpairs of a matrix than the pair associated with the
eigenvalue of largest magnitude. This section describes how deflation can be used together
with the t-power method.

Let the tensor
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
have a unique eigentube

\bm{\lambda}_{1}
of largest norm, and let
\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}
be an associated
eigenslice. Assume that the eigenpair
\{\bm{\lambda}_{1},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}\}
has been
computed, e.g., by the t-tensor power method. The idea behind deflation is to apply
Algorithm
3
to the tensor

\mathscr{A}_{1}=\mathscr{A}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H},

(9)

where
\vec{\mathscr{V}}\in\mathbb{K}^{p}_{n}
is an arbitrary lateral slice such that

\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}=\bm{e}
, and
\bm{\sigma}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
is an
appropriate shift. The tensor
\mathscr{A}_{1}
has the same eigentubes as
\mathscr{A}

except for
\bm{\lambda}_{1}
, which is transformed to
\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}
, as is
shown in the next theorem.

Theorem 31
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{p\times p}_{n}
have the eigenpairs

\{\bm{\lambda}_{i},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
, and let the tube

\bm{\sigma}\in\mathbb{K}_{n}
be such that
\bm{\lambda}_{i}\neq\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}
,

i=1,2,\ldots,\ell
. Then the tensor
\mathscr{A}_{1}
, defined by (
9
), has
the eigenpairs

\{(\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}),(\bm{\lambda}_{i},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}+\bm{\gamma}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1})_{i=2}^{\ell}\},

with

\bm{\gamma}_{i}=\dfrac{\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}}{\bm{\sigma}-(\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\lambda}_{i})},\quad i=2,3,\ldots,\ell.

Proof.

First note that
\bm{\lambda}_{i}\neq\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}
means that

\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}^{(j)}\neq\widehat{\bm{\lambda}}_{1}^{(j)}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}^{(j)},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

Let
\bm{\sigma}
be such that
\bm{\lambda}_{i}\neq\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}
. We will show
that
\{\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma},\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}\}
is an eigenpair of

\mathscr{A}_{1}
and that the deflation procedure preserves the eigentubes

\bm{\lambda}_{2},\bm{\lambda}_{3},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{\ell}
. We have

\begin{split}\mathscr{A}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}=\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}\right)*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}&=\mathscr{A}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}\\
&=\bm{\lambda}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}\\
&=\left(\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}\right)*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}.\end{split}

On the other hand, let
\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}
be the left eigenslice associated with

\bm{\lambda}_{j}
for
j=2,3,\ldots,\ell
. Then we have

\mathscr{A}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}=\bm{\lambda_{j}}*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j},

and since
\{\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}\}_{j=2}^{\ell}
are f-orthogonal to
\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}
, we get

\begin{split}\mathscr{A}_{1}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}=\left(\mathscr{A}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}\right)^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}&=\left(\mathscr{A}^{H}-\bm{\sigma}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}^{H}\right)*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j}\\
&=\bm{\lambda}_{j}*\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{j},\quad j=2,3,\ldots,\ell.\end{split}

Thus, deflation preserves the left eigenslices

\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{2},\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{3},\ldots,\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{\ell}
and the
eigentubes
\bm{\lambda}_{2},\bm{\lambda}_{3},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{\ell}
.

Since
\bm{\lambda}_{2},\bm{\lambda}_{3},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{\ell}
are eigentubes of

\mathscr{A}_{1}
, we will now construct eigenslices associated with these eigentubes. Let
for
i=2,3,\ldots,\ell
,

\check{\vec{\mathscr{U}}}_{i}=\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}-\bm{\gamma}_{i}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}.

Since

\mathscr{A}_{1}*\check{\vec{\mathscr{U}}}_{i}=\bm{\lambda}_{i}*\check{\vec{\mathscr{U}}}_{i}
, it follows that
\bm{\gamma}_{i}
is given by

\bm{\gamma}_{i}=\dfrac{\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{V}}^{H}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{i}}{\bm{\sigma}-\left(\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}\right)},\quad i=2,3,\ldots,\ell,

and
\bm{\gamma}_{1}=0
such that
\bm{\sigma}\neq\bm{\lambda}_{1}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}
.
∎

Deflation techniques for matrices have been discussed in several works, see, e.g., Saad

[
34
, Chapter 4.2]
, and the choice of the analogue of the slice

\vec{\mathscr{V}}
has received some attention. Analyses for the matrix case suggest that
we let
\vec{\mathscr{V}}=\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{1}
, where
\vec{\mathscr{W}}_{1}
is the left
eigenslice of
\mathscr{A}
, or
\vec{\mathscr{V}}=\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}
. The latter choice
preserves the t-Schur lateral slices.

Proposition 32
.

Let
\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}
be an eigenslice of
\mathscr{A}
of norm
1
associated with

\bm{\lambda}_{1}
and let

\mathscr{A}_{1}=\mathscr{A}-\bm{\sigma}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}*\vec{\mathscr{U}}_{1}^{H}

\mathscr{A}_{1}
are

and

\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}=\bm{\lambda}_{j}
,

\widetilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}

Let

5.6

references is provided in, e.g.,

The default QR algorithm used for matrix computation is fairly complicated: it uses

\mathscr{A}^{k}=\left(\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{Q}_{2}*\ldots*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\right)*\left(\mathscr{R}_{k}*\mathscr{R}_{k-1}*\ldots*\mathscr{R}_{1}\right),

are

Proof.

Let
k\geq 1
. Then

\begin{split}\mathscr{A}_{k+1}=\mathscr{R}_{k}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}&=\mathscr{Q}_{k}^{H}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}*\mathscr{R}_{k}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}=\mathscr{Q}_{k}^{H}*\mathscr{A}_{k}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\\
&=\mathscr{Q}_{k}^{H}*\mathscr{R}_{k-1}*\mathscr{Q}_{k-1}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\\
&=\mathscr{Q}_{k}^{H}*\mathscr{Q}_{k-1}^{H}*\mathscr{A}_{k-2}*\mathscr{Q}_{k-1}*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\\
&=\left(\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{Q}_{2}*\ldots*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\right)^{H}\mathscr{A}\left(\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{Q}_{2}*\ldots*\mathscr{Q}_{k}\right),\end{split}

which gives (

\begin{split}\mathscr{A}^{k}&=\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{R}_{1}*\ldots*\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{R}_{1}\\
&=\mathscr{Q}_{1}*\mathscr{Q}_{2}*\mathscr{R}_{2}*\ldots*\mathscr{Q}_{2}*\mathscr{R}_{2}*\mathscr{R}_{1}\\

which shows (

(12)

we obtain

\mathscr{A}_{k+1}=\breve{\mathscr{Q}}_{k}^{H}*\mathscr{A}_{k}*\breve{\mathscr{Q}}_{k}.

7
Conclusion and extensions

This work describes generalizations of eigenvalues and eigenvectors, referred to as
eigentubes and eigenslices, respectively, for third-order tensors using the t-product.
Some properties of eigentubes and eigenslices are shown, and several methods for
computing eigentubes and eigenslices are presented including the t-power method, the
t-inverse iteration method, and t-subspace iteration, as well as the t-QR algorithm. We
also introduce deflation methods. Numerical tests illustrate the performance of these
methods.

BACKWARD RAUZY-VEECH ALGORITHM AND HORIZONTAL SADDLE CONNECTIONS MSC classification: 37E05, 37E35
Przemysław Berk

Abstract
. We study the combinatorial and dynamical properties of translations surfaces with horizontal saddle connections from the point of view of backward Rauzy-Veech induction. Namely, we prove that although the horizontal saddle connections do not rule out existence of the infinite orbit under backward Rauzy-Veech algorithm, they disallow the ∞ \infty - completeness of such orbit. Furthermore, we prove that if an orbit under backward Rauzy-Veech algorithm is infinite, then the minimality of the horizontal translation flow is equivalent to the eventual appearance of all horizontal saddle connections as sides of the polygonal represenation of a surface.



The main goal of this note is to study the relations between horizontal saddle connections and the combinatorics of the inverse Rauzy-Veech algorithm for translation surfaces as well as dynamics of the horizontal translation flows. In
[
2
]
(Proposition 4.3) Marmi, Ulcigrai and Yoccoz prove that if a translation surface does not have horizontal saddle connections, then its backward Rauzy-Veech induction orbit is indefinitely well-defined and
\infty
-complete, that is every symbol is a backward winner infinitely many times. In the same article the authors pose a question, whether these two conditions are equivalent. We answer affirmatively to this question in Theorem
11
. The proof utilizes only combinatorics and geometry of translation surfaces.

However, before proving Theorem
11
, we prove Proposition
7
which states that, typically, possessing horizontal saddle connections does not rule out that the backward orbit with respect to the inverse Rauzy-Veech algorithm is well defined. Moreover, in Theorem
12
we prove that appearance of horizontal connections as sides of polygonal representations of translations surfaces is closely tied to the minimality of the horizontal translation flow. More precisely, we show that the horizontal translation flow is minimal if and only if all (if any) horizontal saddle connections appear as sides of a polygonal representation of a surface after applying a finite number of backward Rauzy-Veech induction steps.

Interval exchange transformations and translations surfaces

We recall first basic notions and properties related to IETs and translation surfaces. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alphabet of
\#\mathcal{A}\geq 2
elements.
For more information and basic properties, including the ergodic properties of interval exchange transformations, translation surfaces and Rauzy-Veech algorithm we refer the reader e.g. to
[

and

Let

\begin{split}S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}:=&\{\pi=(\pi_{0},\pi_{1}):\mathcal{A}\to\{1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{A}\}\times\{1,\ldots,\#\mathcal{A}\};\\
&\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{0}^{-1}\{1,\ldots,k\}=\{1,\ldots,k\}\Rightarrow\ k=\#\mathcal{A}\}\end{split}

be the set of irreducible permutations, where
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
are bijections. Let us also denote by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
the set of all
d
-dimensional positive real vectors and for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
let
|\lambda|:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}
.

An
interval exchange transformation on
[0,|\lambda|)
(IET)

T=(\pi,\lambda)\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
is a bijective piecewise translation, where the intervals

I_{\alpha}:=\left[\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{A};\pi_{0}(\beta)<\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta},\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{A};\pi_{0}(\beta)\leq\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta}\right)\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathcal{A}

are rearranged inside
[0,|\lambda|)
with respect to the permutation
\pi
. More precisely, for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

T(x)=x+\delta_{\alpha}\quad\text{if}\quad x\in I_{\alpha},

where

\delta_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{A};\ \pi_{1}(\beta)<\pi_{1}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\alpha}-\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{A};\ \pi_{0}(\beta)<\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\alpha}.

Note that
T
preserves Lebesgue measure.

We denote by
\Omega^{\pi}=[\omega_{\alpha\beta}]_{\alpha,\beta}
the associated
translation matrix
, with coefficients given by

\omega_{\alpha\beta}:=\begin{cases}+1&\text{ if }\pi_{0}(\alpha)<\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{ and }\pi_{1}(\alpha)>\pi_{1}(\beta);\\
-1&\text{ if }\pi_{0}(\alpha)>\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{ and }\pi_{1}(\alpha)<\pi_{1}(\beta);\\
\ \ 0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then, if
\delta:=[\delta_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
, we get

\delta=\Omega_{\pi}\cdot\lambda.

On the space
S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
we consider an operator
R
called
Rauzy-Veech induction
, defined as
R(\pi,\lambda)=(\pi^{1},\lambda^{1})
, where
(\pi^{1},\lambda^{1})
is the first return map of
(\pi,\lambda)
to the interval
[0,|\lambda|-\min\{\lambda_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)},\lambda_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}\})
.
If
\lambda_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)}>\lambda_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}
we say that
R
is of ”top” type and we say that it is of ”bottom” type if
\lambda_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)}<\lambda_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}
. We denote the symbol corresponding to the longer interval as
w
(the winner) and to the shorter one as
l
(the loser).

The map
R(\pi,\lambda)
is properly defined as an interval exchange transformation of
d
intervals if and only if
\lambda_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)}\neq\lambda_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}
. Keane
[
1
]
gave an equivalent condition on
(\pi,\lambda)
, for the iterations of Rauzy-Veech induction to be defined indefinitely. More precisely, we say that IET
T
satisfies
Keane’s condition
if for every two discontinuities
a
and
b
of
T
equality
T^{n}(a)=b
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
implies
n=1
,
a=T^{-1}(0)
and
b=0
. In particular, if the vector
\lambda
is rationally independent, that is for every choice of
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z},\ \alpha\in\mathcal{A}
we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}=0\ \Rightarrow\ c_{\alpha}=0\text{ for every }\alpha\in\mathcal{A},

then
(\pi,\lambda)
satisfies Keane’s condition. When it is well defined, we denote
R^{n}(\pi,\lambda)=(\pi^{n},\lambda^{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that the orbit of
(\pi,\lambda)
via Rauzy-Veech induction is
\infty
-complete
if every symbol in
\mathcal{A}
appears infinitely many times in the sequence of winners
\{w^{n}\}
.

Note that
\lambda^{1}=A^{1}(\pi,\lambda)\lambda
, where a matrix
A^{1}(\pi,\lambda)
is defined in the following way

A^{1}_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\alpha=\beta;\\
-1&\text{ if }\alpha=w\text{ and }\beta=l;\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Inductively, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

A^{n}(\pi,\lambda)=A^{1}(\pi^{n-1},\lambda^{n-1})A^{n-1}(\pi,\lambda).

Then
\lambda^{n}=A^{n}(\pi,\lambda)\lambda
. We will refer to
A^{n}(\pi,\lambda)
as
Rauzy-Veech matrices
. Note that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the matrix
(A^{n}(\pi,\lambda))^{-1}
is non-negative.

For every
\pi\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}
let

\begin{split}\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}=\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\pi})&=\Big\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{A}};\ \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A};\pi_{0}(\alpha)\leq k}\tau_{\alpha}>0\ \text{ and }\\
&\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A};\pi_{1}(\alpha)\leq k}\tau_{\alpha}<0\ \text{ for every }\ k\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\Big\}.\end{split}

Then every
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}

On a surface
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
we consider a
translation flow
, that is the flow which moves every non-singular point with unit speed in a fixed direction. In this note we mostly use the horizontal rightward direction and refer to such flows simply as
“horizontal flows”

\{T_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. If the orbit of some point hits a singularity, then we call such an orbit a
separatrix
of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. If the singularity is hit in negative time, then we say that the separatrix is
rightward
and if it is hit in positive time, then we say that the separatrix is
leftward
. A
saddle connection
is a separatrix which is both rightward and leftward.

The following fact concerning minimality, or rather its corollary (Cor.
2
), would be of later use.

Proposition 1

(see Theorem 3.13 in
[
4
]
)
.

Every translation surface
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
admits a decomposition into finitely many maximal subsets
D_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, invariant under the action of horizontal flow, such that the restriction of the horizontal flow to
D_{j}
for every
j=1,\ldots,k
is either periodic or minimal.

Corollary 2
.

The horizontal flow on
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is minimal if and only if there exists one half-orbit which is dense.

We extend the definition of Rauzy-Veech induction into the space
S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}
. Namely
\mathbf{R}(\pi,\lambda,\tau):=(\pi^{1},\lambda^{1},\tau^{1})
, where

(\pi^{1},\lambda^{1})={R}(\pi,\lambda)\ \text{ and }\ \tau^{1}=A^{1}(\pi,\lambda)\tau.

Thus,
\mathbf{R}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is well defined if and only if
R(\pi,\lambda)
is well defined.
We define the
type
of
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
as that of
(\pi,\lambda)
.

Note that
R
is not a invertible map. Indeed, every
(\pi,\lambda)\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}
has exactly two preimages. The map
\mathbf{R}
on the other hand is invertible whenever
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
satisfies
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}\neq 0
. We can thus consider the
backward Rauzy-Veech induction

\mathbf{R^{-1}}
. We say that
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of the
backward “top” type
if
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}<0
and is of the
backward “bottom” type
if
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}>0
.

Moreover, if
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of backward ”top” type, we say that
\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)
is a
backward winner
and
\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)
is a
backward loser
. Analogously if
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of backward ”bottom” type, we say that
\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)
is a
backward winner
and
\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)
is a
backward loser
. Although the following result is well known, we present its short proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3
.

We have that

(1)

\begin{split}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)&\ \text{is of the ``backward top" (``backward bottom") type}\\
&\Leftrightarrow\mathbf{R^{-1}}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\ \text{is of ``top" (``bottom") type.}\end{split}

Moreover

(2)

\begin{split}\alpha&\text{ is a backward winner of }\mathbf{R}^{-1}\text{ for }(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\\
&\Leftrightarrow\alpha\text{ is a winner of }\mathbf{R}\text{ for }\mathbf{R}^{-1}(\pi,\lambda,\tau).\end{split}

Proof.

Assume that
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of backward top type and
\alpha
is a backward winner that is
\alpha=\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)
(the backward bottom type case is done analogously). Denote also
\beta=(\pi_{1})^{-1}(\pi_{1}(\alpha)+1)
. Then
\pi_{0}^{-1}(\alpha)=\pi_{0}(\alpha)=\#\mathcal{A}
and
\pi_{1}(\beta)=\mathcal{A}
. Moreover

\lambda^{-1}_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}+\lambda_{\beta}>\lambda_{\beta}=\lambda^{-1}_{\beta}.

Thus
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of (forward) top type and
\alpha
is the winner.
∎

.

Note that
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
is a backward winner iff the segment corresponding to
\alpha
is the first segment hit by the rightward separatrix starting from the point
(0,0)
in the polygonal representation of
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)

\mathbf{R}^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)

We define a Rauzy-Veech matrix associated to
\mathbf{R}^{-1}
at point
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
by

A^{-1}(\pi,\lambda,\tau):=\left(A^{1}\left(\mathbf{R}^{-1}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\right)\right)^{-1},

and analogously as in the forward case for every

A^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau):=\left(A^{n}\left(\mathbf{R}^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\right)\right)^{-1},

whenever
\mathbf{R}^{-n}

\mathbf{R}^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)=(\pi^{-n},\lambda^{-n},\tau^{-n})
then we have

(3)

\lambda^{-n}=A^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\tau^{-n}=A^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\tau.

If
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau^{-n}_{\alpha}=0
for some

then the backward Rauzy-Veech induction stops, i.e.
\mathbf{R}^{-n-1}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is not well defined. If on the other hand
\mathbf{R}^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is well defined for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each symbol is a backward winner infinitely many times then we say that
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
has
\infty

.

Note that since
(A^{n}(\pi^{-n},\lambda^{-n}))^{-1}
is non-negative, we have

(4)

A^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\quad\text{is non-negative for every}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular

(5)

\min_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}A_{\alpha\beta}^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\quad\text{is non-decreasing as}\ n\to\infty.

In particular

(6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\lambda^{-n}|=\infty.

The following result is stated as a Remark 4.2 in
[
2
]
, however, due to its importance in this article, we present its short proof.

Lemma 5
.

The backward Rauzy Veech induction is defined indefinitely on
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
if and only if the horizontal rightwards separatrix starting from point
(0,0)
in
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is infinite, i.e. it is not a horizontal saddle connection.

Proof.

If
(\pi^{-n^{\prime}},\lambda^{-n^{\prime}},\tau^{-n^{\prime}})
is not properly defined for some
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
then
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}^{-n^{\prime}+1}=0
and the horizontal interval with endpoints at
(0,0)
and
(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}^{-n^{\prime}+1},0)
is a horizontal saddle connection.

On the other hand if there is a horizontal saddle connection starting at
(0,0)
of length
\ell>0
and
(\pi^{-n},\lambda^{-n},\tau^{-n})
is well defined for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then by (
6
) there exists
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}^{n^{\prime}}>\ell
. This implies however that the whole saddle connection is in the interior of
(\pi^{-n^{\prime}},\lambda^{-n^{\prime}},\tau^{-n^{\prime}})
seen as a polygon, in particular this applies to the right-hand side endpoint of the saddle connection. This is however a contradiction since the singularities of
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
can be only the vertices and they do not belong to the horizontal line.
∎

(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}

2.
The existence of the backward orbit

Note that the backward Rauzy-Veech induction algorithm stops the orbit of
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\in\{\pi\}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}
when
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}=0
. This implies that there is a horizontal connection between 0 and
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}+i\tau_{\alpha}
and that the latter is also an element of
\Sigma
(see Lemma
5
). The following result states that the existence of horizontal saddle connections does not imply that the Rauzy-Veech induction orbit stops.

Proposition 6
.

There exist translation surfaces with horizontal saddle connections, whose orbit under the action of backward Rauzy-Veech induction is defined indefinitely.

To prove the above result we will now see that, in general, it is enough to pick
\tau_{\beta}
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{A}
appropriately for the iterations of backward Rauzy-Veech induction on
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
to be defined infinitely many times.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\beta\in\mathcal{A}
. Assume that
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
is such that for every choice of integer numbers
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z},\ \alpha\in\mathcal{A}
we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}\tau_{\alpha}=0\ \Rightarrow\ c_{\beta}=0.

Then the backward Rauzy-Veech induction iterates are defined indefinitely.

Proof.

Note first that by assumptions taking
c_{\alpha}=1
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}\neq 0.

Thus
(\pi^{-1},\lambda^{-1},\tau^{-1})
is properly defined.

We proceed by induction. Assume that
(\pi^{-n},\lambda^{-n},\tau^{-n})
for
n\geq 1
is properly defined and let
B=A^{-n}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
be a Rauzy-Veech matrix of the
n
backward steps of induction. Then in particular
B
is a non-negative matrix and
B_{\beta\beta}\geq 1
. Indeed, the coefficients of the Rauzy-Veech matrix are non-decreasing (see (
5
)) and
A^{-1}(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
has ones on the diagonal.

We claim that
(\pi^{-n-1},\lambda^{-n-1},\tau^{-n-1})
is properly defined. Assume otherwise, that is

(7)

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}^{-n}=0.

Since
\tau^{-n}=B\tau
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}\tau_{\alpha}=0,

where
c_{\alpha}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}}B_{\gamma\alpha}
. In particular, since
B_{\beta\beta}\geq 1
and
B
is a non-negative matrix, we have
c_{\beta}>0
. This, together with (
7
), yields a contradiction with the assumption of the lemma.

∎

3.
Horizontal connections prevent
\infty
-completeness

We saw in Corollary
6
that a horizontal saddle connection does not necessarily prevent a proper definition of an infinite backward Rauzy-Veech orbit. We shall see that it does prevent
\infty
-completeness.
As the reader will see, it follows from the proof of Theorem
11
that horizontal saddle connections “freeze” some coordinates in the sense that they stop winning after finite number of steps of backward Rauzy-Veech algorithm. Before proving Theorem
11
, we present an easy condition to prevent a symbol from winning.

Lemma 8
.

Let
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)\in S_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Lambda^{\mathcal{A}}\times\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}
. If
\tau_{\beta}=0
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{A}
then
\beta
cannot be a backward winner.

Figure 4.
A horizontal segment cannot cross transversally
x
-axis, thus the corresponding symbol cannot be a backward winner.

Proof.

By accelerating backward Rauzy-Veech algorithm if necessary we can assume that
\beta
is a winner at step
1
of backward Rauzy-Veech induction and that
(\pi,\lambda,\tau)
is of backward top type, that is
\beta=\pi_{0}^{-1}(\#\mathcal{A})
(the case when it is of backward bottom type is symmetric). Then
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\tau_{\alpha}=0
. However, since
\tau_{\beta}=0
, we get

Classification of Stationary distributions for the stochastic vertex models
Yier Lin

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the stationary distributions for the stochastic vertex models. Our main focus is the stochastic six vertex (S6V) model. We show that the extremal stationary distributions of the S6V model are given by product Bernoulli measures. Moreover, for the S6V model under a moving frame of speed 1 1 , we show that the extremal stationary distributions are given by product Bernoulli measures and blocking measures. Finally , we generalize our results to the stochastic higher spin six vertex model. Our proof relies on the coupling of the S6V models introduced in [ Agg20 ] , the analysis of current and the method of fusion.



1.
Introduction

Stationary distribution is an important object for Markov processes and particularly for interacting particle systems.
A stationary distribution of a Markov process is a probability distribution that remains unchanged in the Markov process as time progresses.
When the state space is countable and the Markov process is irreducible, it is known that the existence of a stationary distribution is equivalent to the property of positive recurrence. Moreover, the stationary distribution is unique. However, in many examples of interacting particle systems, the state space is uncountable and there are an infinite number of stationary distributions. This paper intends to study and classify the set of stationary distributions for a family of interacting particle systems – the stochastic six vertex (S6V) model and its higher spin generalizations.

The S6V model is a classical model in two dimension statistical physics. The model was introduced by

1.4.
The proof idea

The proof for Theorem
1.3
and
1.5
rely on two tools –
coupling
and
current analysis
. The coupling method allows us to measure the difference between two stationary distributions. For example, via computing the generator of coupled ASEP,
[
Lig76
, Theorem 1.4]
classified all the stationary distributions of ASEP.
By
[
Agg20
, Section 2]
, there exists a coupling of two S6V models obtained via looking at the two-class S6V model. Unlike ASEP, the semigroup of the (coupled) S6V model is complicated. Instead of
carrying out
a direct computation as
[
Lig76
]
, we study the
discrepancies

of the coupled S6V model
.

The discrepancies refer to the locations where one of the S6V models in the coupled process has a particle and the other does not. There are two types of discrepancies, depending on whether we have a (particle, hole) or (hole, particle) in the coupled S6V model. The discrepancies play the role
of
second class particles in the two-class S6V model, so they
cannot
be created from nowhere and their behavior can be studied.
An important observation will be that
a pair of discrepancies with different types will annihilate each other if they end up at the same location.

The current refers to the number of particles that move across a location at a given time. For the S6V model
and its multi-class generalization
, a nice property that will be used several times in our proof is that the current is upper bounded. In particular, it is either zero or one.
Note that this is a special property for the S6V model since for general interacting particle systems, one might allow multiple particles to move across a location at a given time. The reason behind is that the current of the S6V model at time
t
and location
x
is indicated by the number of horizontal edge of
(x,t)
–
(x+1,t)
in the tiling interpretation of the S6V model, which is either zero or one.

The starting point of our proof is Proposition
3.1
, which shows that for any stationary distribution of the coupled S6V model, we
cannot

have both types of discrepancies. This would imply that all extremal translation invariant stationary distributions are product Bernoulli measures. Proposition
3.1
was proved in
[
Agg20
, Corollary 3.9]
, we provide a self-contained proof which is similar to
[
Agg20
]
.
The idea is via counting the number of discrepancies in an interval. On one hand, since the current is either zero or one, there can be at most two discrepancies entering the interval during each time of update for the coupled S6V model. On the other hand, if with positive probability there are discrepancies of different types, then by taking a long enough interval and translation invariance, the expected number of discrepancies that are annihilated each time can be very large. Hence, we can find an interval such that the expectation fo the number of discrepancies inside is
strictly
decreasing. This contradicts the stationarity, which implies that the expectation of the number of discrepancies in any interval must be the same as time evolves.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we need to show that any extremal stationary distribution must be translation invariant. We adopt the idea of
[
BLM02
]
and couple the S6V model with its horizontal shift. By tracking the discrepancies in the coupled process, we can show that the number of discrepancies inside
[-n,n{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}]}
at time
\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor
is
o(n)
as
n\to\infty
, see Proposition
4.8
.
Using this we can show that any extremal stationary distribution is either larger or equal or smaller than its horizontal shift
(there is a partial order on the space of probability measures, see Definition
3.3
)
. We rule out the first and last situation by noting that the expectation of the current of the S6V model at location
x
is a monotone function of the particle configuration on the left of
x
. Hence, if the stationary distribution is larger or smaller than its horizontal shift, the expectations of the currents at neighboring positions are not equal. This shows that all stationary distributions of the S6V model have to be translation invariant.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we first construct a stationary blocking measure by observing a local property of pseudo-stationarity, see Lemma
6.1
. We prove
that
the blocking measure
\mu_{*}
defined in (
1.3
) is stationary for the shifted S6V model by iterating this local property. This
will be
done in Section
6
.
In Section
7
, we apply coupling and current analysis to show that the extremal stationary distributions of the shifted S6V model are given by
\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}
and the projection of
\mu_{*}
onto the irreducible subspace
A_{n}
.

In the end, we generalize our results to the SHS6V model. By the method of fusion
[
KR87
,
CP16
]
, it turns out that we can unfuse the SHS6V model into the unfused SHS6V model defined in Definition
8.1
, see Proposition
8.2
. The unfused SHS6V model is an inhomogeneous version of the S6V model whose
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
parameters have period
I
in space and
J
in time. Using a similar argument used for proving Theorem
1.3
, we obtain all the extremal stationary distributions for the unfused SHS6V model. By the method of fusion, we then obtain all the extremal stationary distributions for the SHS6V model, see Theorem
1.10
. For the shifted SHS6V model with
I=J
, one can also obtain the stationary blocking measure
\mu_{*}^{\text{HS}}
by using the method of fusion and this shows Theorem
1.11
.

1.5.
Related literature

The stationary distribution
s
of various interacting particle systems
have
been studied intensively over the past 50 years. For the symmetric exclusion process, the stationary distributions are well-understood by the work of
[
Lig73
,
Lig74
]
. The question is more challenging in the asymmetric case.
For ASEP, the
set
of stationary distribution
s
is completely understood in the work of
[
Lig76
]
, in particular there exists a stationary blocking measure which is non-translation invariant.
[
BM02
,
BLM02
]
studied the non-nearest jump particle system and provided sufficient conditions for the existence/non-existence of the blocking measures.
[
BL05
]
studied the existence of the blocking measure for exclusion processes in higher dimensions. Recently,
[
ABBS21
]
studied a multi-lane generalization of the exclusion process, which interpolates the exclusion process in one and two dimensions. For the multi-class interacting particle systems, the stationary distributions of the multi-class totally asymmetric exclusion process and multi-class ASEP were studied in
[
Ang06
,
FM07
,
FM09
,
CdGW15
,
Mar20
,
CMW22
]
. Other results concerning the stationary distributions of different interacting particle systems include
[
And82
,
DEHP93
,
FMN19
,
FRS20
]
.

The previous results are for continuous time interacting particle systems, while the stochastic vertex models are discrete time particle systems which have intricate dynamics.
[
Agg18a
]
showed that the product Bernoulli measures are stationary for the S6V model. In addition,
[
Agg20
]
showed that these are the only extremal translation invariant stationary distribution. The product
q
-negative binomial distribution was shown to be stationary for the SHS6V model in

,

,

]
.
Our result provides a more complete characterization of the stationary distributions for the S6V and SHS6V model and their shifted versions.

2.1.
The S6V model with infinitely many particles

Let us explain how to extend the definition of the S6V model in Definition

to that with an infinite number of particles. Without loss of generality, we assume that

We adopt the method in

[

It suffices to explain how we define the update of the S6V model in Definition

from time

is given by the vector

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{p}_{t}=(\dots<p_{t}(-1)<p_{t}(0)<p_{t}(1)<\cdots).}

We recall the i.i.d. random variables

and know that

for every

and

. By Borel-Cantelli lemma, with probability one we can find a random sequence of integers

for every

. According to Definition

, this means that the

-th particle will jump one step to its right no matter what happens to its left. As a consequence, we can update the particles with labels that belong to
[d_{i},d_{i+1}-1]
independently, conditioned on the
d_{i}
-th particle would jump and
(d_{i+1}-1)
-th particle cannot move across

. This reduces the question of understanding the update of the S6V model on the full line to understanding the update of the S6V model on finite length intervals
[p_{t}(d_{i}),p_{t}(d_{i+1})],i\in\mathbb{Z}
, which has been defined in Definition
1.1
.

Agg20
, Section 2.4]
.

Definition 2.3

(Higher rank coupling)
.

We define the coupled S6V model
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, which is a discrete time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}.
It suffices to state the update rule from
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})
to
(\eta_{t+1},\xi_{t+1})
for every fixed
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We define two auxiliary particle configurations
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
as

(2.1)

\widetilde{\eta}_{t}(x):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\eta_{t}(x)=\xi_{t}(x)=1,\\
2,&\text{ if }\eta_{t}(x)=1,\xi_{t}(x)=0,\\
0&\text{ else},\end{cases}\qquad\widetilde{\xi}_{t}(x):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\xi_{t}(x)=\eta_{t}(x)=1,\\
2,&\text{ if }\xi_{t}(x)=1,\eta_{t}(x)=0,\\
0&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

In other words,
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
has a first-class particle at
x
iff this location is occupied in both
\eta_{t}
and
\xi_{t}
.
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
has a second-class particle
at
x
iff
x
is occupied in
\eta_{t}
but not in
\xi_{t}
. The interpretation for
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
is similar.

We update the two-class S6V models from
(\widetilde{\eta}_{t},\widetilde{\xi}_{t})
to
(\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1},\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1})
.
The update for both
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}\to\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}\to\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}
follows Definition
2.1
and uses the
same
family of random variables
\{\chi^{i}_{t}(x),j^{i}_{t}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z},i=1,2}
.

Then we redefine
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)=2
, we set
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)=1
. We keep the value of
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x),\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)
unchanged for all other
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We finally set

(2.2)

\eta_{t+1}(x):=\begin{cases}1,&\qquad\text{ if }\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=1\text{ or }2,\\
0&\qquad\text{ if }\widetilde{\eta}_{t=1}(x)=0.\end{cases}\qquad\xi_{t+1}(x):=\begin{cases}1,&\qquad\text{ if }\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)=1\text{ or }2,\\
0&\qquad\text{ if }\widetilde{\xi}_{t=1}(x)=0.\end{cases}

The above procedure defines the update from
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})
to
(\eta_{t+1},\xi_{t+1})
.

Remark 2.4
.

In Definition
2.3
, the redefinition of
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}
is related to procedure that if two second-class particles respectively in
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
arrive at the same location at time

, then they both become the first-class particles. The redefinition of
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}
will not change the value of
\eta_{t+1}
and
\xi_{t+1}
in (
2.2
). However, it is important since it ensures that
(\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1},\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1})
and
(\eta_{t+1},\xi_{t+1})
satisfy the relation in (
2.1
).

Remark 2.5
.

By Lemma
2.2
, we know that the marginals of the coupled S6V model are indeed the S6V models.

As observed in
[
Agg20
, Section 2.4]
, the coupling in Definition

is

.

Lemma 2.6

(Attractivity)
.

Under higher rank coupling, if
\eta_{t}\geq\xi_{t}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, then we have
\eta_{T}\geq\xi_{T}
for any
T\geq t
. If
\eta_{t}>\xi_{t}
, then we have
\eta_{T}>\xi_{T}
for any
T\geq t
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\eta_{t}\geq\xi_{t}
implies
\eta_{t+1}\geq\xi_{t+1}
, and
\eta_{t}>\xi_{t}
implies
\eta_{t+1}>\xi_{t+1}
.

Let us first show the former part. By Definition
2.3
, we know that
\eta_{t}\geq\xi_{t}
implies
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}=\xi_{t}
. This yields that
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
does not have a second-class particle, i.e.
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. As a consequence,
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, which implies that
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}=\xi_{t+1}
. In addition, we know that the sets of first-class particles of
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
are the same, i.e.

\{x:\widetilde{\eta}_{t}(x)=1\}=\{x:\widetilde{\xi}_{t}(x)=1\}
. Since the first-class particles in
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
have the same dynamics, we know that
\{x:\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=1\}=\{x:\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)=1\}
. Combining this with
\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}=\xi_{t+1}
, we know that
\{x:\xi_{t+1}(x)=1\}=\{x:\widetilde{\xi}_{t+1}(x)=1\}=\{x:\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=1\}\subseteq\{x:\eta_{t+1}(x)=1\}
. This implies that
\eta_{t+1}\geq\xi_{t+1}
.

We proceed to show the latter part. Since
\eta_{t}>\xi_{t}
, we know that
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
has at least a second-class particle, while
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
does not have a second class particle. As a consequence,
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}
has at least a second-class particle, i.e. the set
\{x:\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(x)=2\}
is non-empty. This implies the existence of
y\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\widetilde{\eta}_{t+1}(y)=2
. By Definition
2.3
, this yields that
\eta_{t+1}(y)=1
and
\xi_{t+1}(y)=0
. By the previous paragraph,
we already know that
\eta_{t}>\xi_{t}
implies that
\eta_{t+1}\geq\xi_{t+1}
. The existence of aforementioned
y
concludes that
\eta_{t+1}>\xi_{t+1}
.

∎

Recall that we say that there is a discrepancy at

When an
\eta

Lemma 4.3
.

Recall that we denote
\mu_{\rho}
to be the product Bernoulli measure with density
\rho
.
We consider the coupled S6V model
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})
that starts from the initial distribution
\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}=\mu_{\rho}\circ(\mathsf{Id},\tau^{-1})
.
Then
\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(t)=0
uniformly for
\rho\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{\rho}
is ergodic for the shift operator
\tau
, hence
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}
is ergodic under
\widetilde{\tau}
. Recall the higher rank coupling defined in Definition
2.3
, for all time

can be expressed as a function of
(\eta_{0},\xi_{0})
and

. Since
\{\chi_{s}^{i}(x)\}
and
\{j^{i}_{s}(x)\}
are two groups of i.i.d. random variables, the probability distribution of
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})
is ergodic for the operator
\widetilde{\tau}
.
By
Birkhoff
ergodic theorem
[
Dur19
, Theorem 6.2.1]
, for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

(4.2)

\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta_{t}(i)\neq\xi_{t}(i)\}},\qquad\mathbb{P}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}}\text{ a.e.}.

Since the location of discrepancies
\{i:\eta_{t}(i)\neq\xi_{t}(i)\}
travel as second-class particles in the two-class S6V model
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}

\eta
-type and one
\xi
-type) entering
[-n,n]

\sum_{i=-n}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta_{t+1}(i)\neq\xi_{t+1}(i)\}}\leq\sum_{i=-n}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}n}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta_{t}(i)\neq\xi_{t}(i)\}}+2.

Using this together with (
4.2
), we know that
\phi^{\rho}(t)
is non-increasing in

.

We proceed to conclude the lemma. Let
\lambda_{\rho}
be a limit point of the Cesàro average
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{\mu_{\rho}}}\widetilde{S}_{i}
as
n\to\infty
.
This means that we can find an increasing sequence of positive integers

\lambda_{\rho}\widetilde{S}_{1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}\widetilde{S}_{i}

\lambda_{\rho}\widetilde{S}_{1}-\lambda_{\rho}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}}(\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}\widetilde{S}_{n_{k}}-\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}})=0.

This shows that
\lambda_{\rho}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
. In addition, the probability distribution
\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}
is translation invariant under
\widetilde{\tau}
and so is the coupled S6V model. This implies that
\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}\widetilde{S}_{i}
is translation invariant under
\widetilde{\tau}
and so is
\lambda_{\rho}

\lambda_{\rho}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}

By Proposition

\lambda_{\rho}\big(\{(\eta,\xi):\eta=\xi\}\big)=1
.
As a consequence,
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(i)
converges to
0
as

. Since
\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(i)
is
non-increasing
in

\lim_{i\to\infty}\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(i)=0

\phi^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{\rho}}}(i)
is continuous in
\rho
for every fixed

∎

Lemma 4.4

Fix

L

Lemma 4.5
.

For the two-class S6V model and
k\in[-M_{2},N_{2}]
, we have for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(p_{t+1}^{2}(k)-p_{t}^{2}(k)\geq r\Big)\leq\max(b_{1},b_{2})^{r-1}

Proof.

Let us assume at time
t
, there are
m
first-class particles lying in the interval
(p_{t}^{2}(k),p_{t}^{2}(k)+r)
.
If
p^{2}_{t+1}(k)\geq p_{t}^{2}(k)+r
holds, then these first-class particles must stay during the update from time
t
to
t+1
. The probability is upper bounded by
b_{1}^{m}
. Moreover, according to Definition
2.1
, we require that
j_{t}^{2}(p_{t}^{2}(k))\geq r-m
. Since the previous events are independent, we have

\mathbb{P}\big(p_{t+1}^{2}(k)-p_{t}^{2}(k)\geq r\big)\leq b_{1}^{m}\mathbb{P}\big(j_{t}^{2}(p_{t}^{2}(k))\geq r-m\big)\leq\max(b_{1},b_{2})^{r-1}.\qed

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
. Denote
\mathsf{N}_{y}(n)
to be the number of discrepancies in the coupled S6V model that
start
from
(-\infty,y]

at time
0
and reach the interval
[-n,n]

at some time between

[\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor,n]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Fix
M>1+\frac{1}{1-\max(b_{1},b_{2})}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{I}
.
We have
\mathbb{E}^{\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}}[\mathsf{N}_{-Mn}(n)]=o(n)

Proof.

For the coupled S6V model
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, during its update from time
t
to
t+1
, there can be at most two discrepancies (one
\eta
-type and one
\xi
-type) moving across

from left to right. Therefore, we have
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathsf{N}}_{-Mn}(n)\leq 2{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(n-\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor)}\leq 2n
. It suffices to show that there exists

such that

(4.7)

\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}}\big({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathsf{N}}_{-Mn}(n)\geq 1\big)\leq Ce^{-\frac{n}{C}}.

To show (
4.7
), let
Z_{t}
be the location of the rightmost
\eta
-type
discrepancy in
(\eta_{t},\xi_{t})

that originally lies in
(-\infty,-Mn]

at time 0

, We have

\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}}(Z_{t}-Z_{t-1}\geq r)\leq\max(b_{1},b_{2})^{r-1}.

.
We use
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]
to denote the expectation of the current of the S6V model with initial data
\eta
, where
\eta(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists\,i,p_{0}(i)=x\}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix arbitrary
\eta\in\mathcal{S}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]=\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]+(1-\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}])(1-b_{1}),\qquad\text{ if }\eta(y)=1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]=b_{2}\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}],\hskip 121.00024pt\text{ if }\eta(y)=0.

Proof.

Given a particle configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{S}
and consider the update of it from time
0
to time
1
. During the update, assume that a particle has moved across
y-1
and arrived at
y
. If the location
y
is already occupied by another particle, then the former particle stops and the latter particle jumps to its right with probability
1
. If instead
y
is not occupied, then with probability
b_{2}
, the former particle continues to move across
y
. Hence, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\eta}(K_{y}=1)=b_{2}^{1-\eta(y)}\qquad\quad\text{ if }K_{y-1}=1.

Under a similar reasoning, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\eta}(K_{y}=1)=1-b_{1}^{\eta(y)}\quad\,\quad\ \text{if }K_{y-1}=0.

Combine (
5.2
)-(
5.3
), we conclude the lemma.
∎

The following proposition establishes the monotonicity of the
expectation of
the current.

Lemma 5.2

(Monotonicity)
.

Fix arbitrary
\eta,\xi\in\mathcal{S}
. If
\eta(z)\geq\xi(z)
for all
z\leq y
, then
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{y}]
. If additionally there exist
x\leq y
such that
\eta(x)>\xi(x)
, then
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]>\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{y}]
.

Proof.

We prove the first part of the lemma.
We couple the update of S6V models from
\eta_{0}=\eta
to
\eta_{1}
and from
\xi_{0}=\xi
to
\xi_{1}
using the higher rank coupling stated in Definition
2.3
.

Since
\eta_{0}(z)\geq\xi_{0}(z)
for all
z\leq y
,
\widetilde{\xi}_{0}(z)
is equal to either
0
or
1
for all
z\leq y
. In other words, on
(-\infty,y]
,
\widetilde{\xi}_{0}
only contains first-class particles.

\widetilde{\eta}_{0}
contains first-class particles at the same positions and might have a few more second-class particles. By Definition
2.3
, the first-class particles in
\widetilde{\eta}_{0}
and
\widetilde{\xi}_{0}

will
travel together.
Let
p^{\prime}_{t}
record the location of particles in
\xi_{t}
so that
\xi_{t}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists i,p^{\prime}_{t}(i)=x\}}
.
By the previous argument,
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p^{\prime}_{0}(k)\leq y<p^{\prime}_{1}(k)\}}=1
implies that
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p_{0}(k)\leq y<p_{1}(k)\}}=1
. Therefore, when
\eta(z)\geq\xi(z)
for all
z\leq y
, under higher rank coupling, we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p_{0}(k)\leq y<p_{1}(k)\}}\geq\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p^{\prime}_{0}(k)\leq y<p^{\prime}_{1}(k)\}}.}

Taking the expectation of both sides implies that
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{y}]
.

We proceed to prove the second part of the lemma.
If additionally there exists some
x
satisfying
x\leq y
and
\eta(x)>\xi(x)
,
we can define

m:=\max\{z:\eta_{0}(z)>\xi_{0}(z),z\leq y\}
. By definition, we have
\eta(m)=1
,
\xi(m)=0
and
\eta(z)=\xi(z)
for all
z\in[m+1,y]\cap\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
5.1
, we have

(5.4)

\begin{split}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m}]=\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m-1}]+(1-\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m-1}])(1-b_{1})},\\
&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m}]=\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m-1}]b_{2}.}\end{split}

Using the first part of the lemma, we have
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m-1}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m-1}]
. Using this together with (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m-1}]+(1-\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m-1}])(1-b_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m-1}]+(1-\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m-1}])(1-b_{1})

\displaystyle>\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m-1}]b_{2}=\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m}].

This implies that
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m}]>\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m}]
. Since
\eta_{0}(z)=\xi_{0}(z)
for all
z\in[m+1,y]
, this implies that
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{z}]
and
\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{z}]
follow the same recursion for
z\in[m+1,y]
in Lemma
5.1
. Since the recursions in Lemma
5.1
preserve the monotonicity, by
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{m}]>\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{m}]
,
we conclude that
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]>\mathbb{E}^{\xi}[K_{y}]
.
∎

Since the vertex of the S6V model is conservative in lines, we readily have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p_{0}(k)\leq x-1<p_{1}(k)\}}}+\sum_{k=x}^{y}\eta_{0}(k)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},p_{0}(k)\leq y<p_{1}(k)\}}}+\sum_{k=x}^{y}\eta_{1}(k).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
A.2
, we know that
\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
. To conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to show that for all
\nu\in\mathcal{I}_{e}
, we have
\nu\in\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}
. Since
\nu\in\mathcal{I}_{e}
, by Corollary
4.2
, we know that either
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
,

\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
or
\nu=\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
. We claim that only the last case is possible.

To prove this claim, we argue by contradiction. Let
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[K_{y}]=\int\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]\nu(d\eta)
.
If
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
, by Lemma
5.2
, there exists
y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(5.5)

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[K_{y{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}1}]<\mathbb{E}^{\nu\circ\tau^{-1}}[K_{y-1}]=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[K_{y}].

In addition, since the vertex of the S6V model is conservative in lines, we have

(5.6)

\eta_{0}(y)+K_{y-1}=\eta_{1}(y)+K_{y}.

Using (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we have
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\eta(y)]>\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\eta_{1}(y)]
, which contradicts the assumption that
\nu\in\mathcal{I}
. We can also rule out
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
using a similar argument. Consequently, we conclude
\nu=\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
, which implies
\nu\in\mathcal{P}
. Hence, we know that
\nu\in\mathcal{I}_{e}
implies
\nu\in\mathcal{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}\subseteq(\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{P})_{e}
. By Corollary
3.4
, we have
\nu\in\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}
. This concludes the theorem.
∎

In this section, we look at the shifted S6V model
(\eta_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}=(\tau_{t}(\eta_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
. Recall that we use

We have proved that

Recall the definition of the blocking measure

Remark 6.2
.

This lemma should be compared with Lemma

Proof of Lemma

The proof is straightforward. We can compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(v^{\prime}=1\big)

Note that for arbitrary
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the map
\tau_{t}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
is bijective and
\tau_{t}=\tau_{-t}^{-1}
. For arbitrary
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathcal{S})
, we define
\mu\circ\tau_{t}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathcal{S})
such that
(\mu\circ\tau_{t})(A)=\mu(\tau_{t}A)
for any measurable set
A
in
\mathcal{S}
. Note that the definition of
\mu\circ\tau_{t}
is consistent the definition of
\mu\circ\tau^{-1}_{-t}
in the second paragraph of Section
3
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that we have
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\eta}\sim(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})=\otimes_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{Ber}(\frac{q^{-k+t}}{1+q^{-k+t}})
, then for arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}K_{y}}\sim\text{Ber}\big(\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]:=\int\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]\,(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})(d\eta)=\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}
.
Using Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y}]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta(y)=1\}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]+\big(1-\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]\big)(1-b_{1})\Big)+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta(y)=0\}}\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]b_{2}.

Integrating both sides above (as a function
\eta
) with respect to the probability measure
\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}
. Note that
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]
only depends on
\{\eta(z)\}_{z\leq y-1}
, this implies that when
\eta\sim\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}
,
\mathbb{E}^{\eta}[K_{y-1}]
is independent with
\eta(y)
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]

\displaystyle=(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})(\eta(y)=1)\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]+\big(1-\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]\big)\big(1-b_{1}\big)\Big)+(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})(\eta(y)=0)\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]b_{2}

We have
(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})(\eta(y)=1)=\mu_{*}(\eta(y-t)=1)=\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}
and
(\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t})(\eta(y)=0)=\frac{1}{1+q^{-y+t}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]+\big(1-\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]\big)\big(1-b_{1}\big)\Big)+\frac{1}{1+q^{-y+t}}\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]b_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{-y+1+t}+1)b_{2}}{1+q^{-y+t}}\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]+\frac{(1-qb_{2})q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}.

This implies that

(6.2)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]-\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}=\frac{(q^{-y+1+t}+1)b_{2}}{1+q^{-y+t}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y-1}]-\frac{q^{-y+1+t}}{1+q^{-y+1+t}}\Big).

Iterating (
6.2
) yields that for any
x<y
, we have

(6.3)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]-\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}=\prod_{i=x+1}^{y}\frac{(q^{-i+1+t}+1)b_{2}}{1+q^{-i+t}}\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{x}]-\frac{q^{-x+t}}{1+q^{-x+t}}\Big).

Recall that
q=\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}
, it is straightforward
verify that

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(q^{-i+1+t}+1)b_{2}}{1+q^{-i+t}}=\max(b_{1},b_{2})<1.

Fix arbitrary
y
and let
x\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}\infty
in (
6.3
), the product
therein
tends to zero. Hence,
\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}}[K_{y}]=\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}
for arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we conclude the lemma.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

We have

\mu_{*}\in\mathfrak{I}.
As a consequence, for arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu_{(n)}\in\mathfrak{I}
.

Proof.

We need to show that
\tau_{t}(\eta_{t})\sim\mu_{*}
implies
\tau_{t+1}(\eta_{t+1})\sim\mu_{*}
. It suffices to show that if
\eta_{t}\sim\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}
, then we have
\eta_{t+1}\sim\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t+1}
. Fix arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{Z}
. Given
\eta_{t}\sim\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t}
,
using Lemma
6.3

with
\eta=\eta_{t}
, we know that
K_{y-1}\sim\text{Ber}(\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}q^{-y+1+t}}}{1+q^{-y+1+t}})
. Note that for all
y\in\mathbb{Z}
,
K_{y}
is given by (
5.1
) with
\eta=\eta_{t}
.

Since
K_{y-1}
only depends on
\eta_{t}(z),z\leq y-1
, we have that
K_{y-1}
and
\eta_{t}(y)
are independent.
Set
h=K_{y-1}
,
v=\eta_{t}(y)
,
h^{\prime}=K_{y}
,
v^{\prime}=\eta_{t+1}(y)
and
\rho=\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}}
,
\zeta=\frac{q^{-y+1+t}}{1+q^{-y+1+t}}
, we apply Lemma
6.1
and get
K_{y}\sim\text{Ber}(\frac{q^{-y+t}}{1+q^{-y+t}})
,
\eta_{t+1}(y)\sim\text{Ber}(\frac{q^{-y+1+t}}{1+q^{-y+1+t}})
. In addition,
K_{y}
and
\eta_{t+1}(y)
are independent. We keep applying Lemam 6.1 to location
y+1,y+2,\dots
, and we conclude that the random variables

are independent with
\eta_{t+1}(z)\sim\text{Ber}(\frac{q^{-z+1+t}}{1+q^{-z+1+t}})
. Since

\eta_{t+1}\sim\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{Ber}(\frac{q^{-k+1+t}}{1+q^{-k+1+t}})=\mu_{*}\circ\tau_{t+1}.

\mu_{*}\in\mathfrak{I}
.

We proceed to show that
\mu_{(n)}\in\mathfrak{I}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
q>1
. Recall that
\mu_{*}
is supported on the set
A

1.4
) and recall the sets
A_{n}
from (

). Note that

, moreover, it is straightforward to check that

.
∎

7.
Uniqueness of blocking measures and Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we show that there is no extremal stationary distribution for the shifted S6V model other than
\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}
and
\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and conclude Theorem
1.5
. Note that we can couple two shifted S6V models in a similar way as Definition
2.3
by looking at the coupled S6V model under a moving frame. We use
(\widetilde{\mathsf{S}}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}})_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}}
to denote the semigroup of the coupled shifted S6V model.
As stated in Theorem
1.5
, we assume
q=\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\neq 1
throughout the section.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
, then we have

(7.1)

\lim_{n\to-\infty}\nu\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=1,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=0.

If
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
, then we have

\lim_{n\to-\infty}\nu\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=0,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=1.

Proof.

It suffices to explain the proof when
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
. We have

\displaystyle 1\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\eta(m)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{\nu\circ\tau^{-1}}\Big[\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\eta(m)\Big]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\eta(m)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\eta(m+1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu\big(\eta(-n)=1\big)-\nu\big(\eta(n+1)=1\big)\leq 1.

The inequality on the left hand side is due to
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}.
As a consequence, we know that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu\big(\eta(-n)=1\big)-\nu\big(\eta(n+1)=1\big)=1.

Since the probability measure takes value between
0
and
1
, we conclude (
7.1
). The proof for the situation
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
is similar.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{I}_{e}
. We have either
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
,
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
or
\nu=\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
.

Proof.

The proof of lemma is rather similar to what we have done for proving Corollary
4.2
and Proposition
4.1
. The argument that works for the S6V model also applies similarly for the shifted S6V model. Hence the proof is omitted.
∎

Let
\mathcal{P}^{c}
be the set of non-translation invariant distributions, which complements
\mathcal{P}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Fix
q\neq 1
and
\nu\in\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c}
. If
q>1
, we have
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
.
If
q<1
, we have
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.2
, we know that for
\nu\in\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c}
, we have either
\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
or
\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
.
we only prove
that
q>1\Rightarrow\nu>\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
. The proof for
q<1\Rightarrow\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
is similar. We argue by contradiction.

If
q>1
and

\nu<\nu\circ\tau^{-1}
, by Lemma
7.1
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=1.

This shows that the density of particles tends to
1
as we
move to the right
, thus
\lim_{y\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(K_{y}=1\big)=1
. Since
\nu
is stationary for
\tau_{t}\circ\eta_{t}
, we have for any
x<y
,

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{k=x}^{y}\eta_{0}(k)\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{k=x+1}^{y+1}\eta_{1}(k)\Big].

This implies that

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{k=x}^{y}\eta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}1}}(k)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Big[\sum_{k=x}^{y}\eta_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0}}(k)\Big]={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\eta_{1}(x)-\eta_{1}(y+1)]}.

Using Lemma
5.3
, we have

(7.2)

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[K_{x-1}-K_{y}\big]=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\eta_{1}(x)-\eta_{1}(y+1)\big].

Since we have shown that
\lim_{y\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(\eta_{1}(y+1)=1\big)=\lim_{y\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(K_{y}=1\big)=1
, by letting
y\to\infty
in (
7.2
), we have
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\eta_{1}(x)\big]=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[K_{x-1}\big]
, which is equivalent to

(7.3)

\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(\eta_{1}(x)=1\big)=\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(K_{x-1}=1\big).

Since
\eta_{0}(x),K_{x-1},\eta_{1}(x),K_{x}
are the number of lines on the bottom, left, top and right of the vertex at

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(\eta_{1}(x)=1\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(K_{x-1}=1,\eta_{0}(x)=1\big)+b_{1}\mathbb{P}^{\nu}\big(K_{x-1}=0,\eta_{0}(x)=1\big)+(1-b_{2})\mathbb{P}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}\big(K_{x-1}=1,\eta_{0}(x)=0\big).

By (

Lemma 7.4
.

For two different probability measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c}

Proof.

The proof follows the spirit of the proof of Proposition

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lim_{n\to-\infty}\nu_{i}\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=1,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{i}\big(\eta(n)=1\big)=0.}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta}:=\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2}

Recall that the semigroup of the coupled shifted S6V model

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}^{\theta}\Big[\#\{x\in[-Mn,Mn]\cap\mathbb{Z}:\eta^{\prime}_{0}(x)\neq\xi^{\prime}_{0}(x)\}\Big]=0.}

We proceed to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For arbitrary fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
, let
\delta_{n}

be
a delta probability measure which is supported on the particle configuration
\eta
, where
\eta(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq n\}}
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\delta_{\infty}
denotes the particle configuration where there is a particle everywhere and
\delta_{-\infty}
denotes the particle configuration where there is no particle.

Let us show an ordering for the blocking measures. Recall that
\mu_{(n)}
is the stationary distribution for the shifted S6V model
(\eta^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}=(\tau_{t}(\eta_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
on the
countable state space
A_{n}
. Since
\eta^{\prime}_{t}
is irreducible on
A_{n}
,
\eta^{\prime}_{t}
with initial distribution
\mu_{(n)}
is positive recurrent. This implies that if we start the S6V model with initial distribution
\delta_{n}
and let time

goes to infinity, the distribution of
\eta_{t}
converges to
\mu_{(n)}
. In other words, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\delta_{n}\mathsf{S}_{t}=\mu_{(n)}
, recall that
(\mathsf{S}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is the semigroup of the shifted S6V model. Since
\delta_{n}>\delta_{n-1}
, using Lemma
2.6
, we have
\mu_{(n)}>\mu_{(n-1)}
. Moreover,
one can verify that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{(n)}=\delta_{\infty},\qquad\lim_{n\to-\infty}\mu_{(n)}=\delta_{-\infty}.

Using the above discussion and Lemma
7.4
, if
\nu\in\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c}

and
\nu\notin\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mu_{(k-1)}<\nu<\mu_{(k)}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}\Big[\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\eta(i)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{(k-1)}}}\Big[\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\eta(i)\Big]\geq 1,

\displaystyle\liminf_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}N}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu_{(k)}}}\Big[\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\eta(i)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu}}\Big[\sum_{i=-N}^{N}\eta(i)\Big]\geq 1.

Combine the two inequalities above and replace
N
with

, we obtain that

(7.5)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(k)}}}\Big[\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\eta(i)\Big]-\mathbb{E}^{\mu_{(k{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-1})}}\Big[\sum_{i=-n}^{n}\eta(i)\Big]\geq 2.

Since
\delta_{k}\circ\tau^{-1}=\delta_{k-1}
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\delta_{k}\mathsf{S}_{t}=\mu_{(k)}
, we have

(7.6)

\mu_{(k)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\circ\tau^{-1}}=\mu_{(k-1)}.

Therefore, we have

This yields a contradiction with (
7.5
). Hence, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mu_{(n)}=\nu
. This implies that
\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c}\subseteq\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, by Lemma
7.5
, we have
\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}\subseteq(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathcal{P})_{e}=\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}
. Therefore, we get

\mathfrak{I}_{e}=(\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P})\cup(\mathfrak{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{P}^{c})\subseteq\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}\cup\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
A.2
and Proposition
6.4
, we know that
\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}\cup\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\mathfrak{I}
. It is straightforward to see that every element of
\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}\cup\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

cannot
be written as a linear combination of the others. Hence, we conclude that
\mathfrak{I}_{e}=\{\mu_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in[0,1]}\cup\{\mu_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

(v,h,v^{\prime},h)=(\eta_{0}(y+1),K_{y},\eta_{1}(y+1),K_{y+1})
, this
yields
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}K_{y+1}}\sim\text{Ber}(\zeta)
and
\eta_{1}(y+1)\sim\text{Ber}(\rho)
. Moreover, the random variables
K_{y+1}
and
\eta_{1}(y+1)
are independent. Keep applying Lemma
A.1
for the vertex at
(i+1,0)
with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(v,h,v^{\prime},h^{\prime})=(\eta_{0}(i+1),K_{i},\eta_{1}(i+1),K_{i+1})}
, where
i=y+1,y+2,\dots
, we obtain that
\{(\eta_{1}(z)\}_{z>y}
follows the product Bernoulli distribution
with density
\rho
. Since
y\in\mathbb{Z}
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\eta_{1}\sim\mu_{\rho}
.
∎

Remark A.3
.

8.5
), a similar argument as above would also imply that the probability measures

defined in (
8.4

(\overline{\eta}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

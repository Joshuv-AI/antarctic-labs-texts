Arithmeticity for integral cohomological dimension of configuration spaces of manifolds
by

Abstract
Consider the configuration spaces of manifolds. We give a precise formula for the integral cohomological dimension (the degree of top non-trivial integral cohomology group) of unordered configuration spaces of manifolds with non-trivial co-dimension one cohomology group, and show that the the sequence of cohomological dimensions is arithmetic. This arithmeticity is not present in the classical example of Arnold. Moreover, we show that the top integral cohomology group is infinite. Furthermore, We give a lower bound for the rank of top integral cohomology group. We also predict that the top integral cohomology group of configuration spaces of manifolds with non-trivial co-dimension one cohomology group is eventually finite. To the best of our knowledge, there is no rigorous bound for the cohomological dimension of ordered configuration spaces. As an application of main results, we give a sharp lower bound for the cohomological dimension of ordered configuration spaces of manifolds. The first step of the proof of main results is to define a reduced Chevalley–Eilenberg complex.



Key Words
: Configuration spaces, cohomological dimension, arithmeticity, Chevalley–Eilenberg complex

Introduction

For any manifold
M
, let

F_{k}(M):=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in M^{k}|x_{i}\neq x_{j}\,for\,i\neq j\}

be the configuration space of
k
distinct ordered points in

with induced topology. The symmetric group
S_{k}
acts on

by permuting the coordinates. The quotient

C_{k}(M):=F_{k}(M)/S_{k}

is the unordered configuration space with quotient topology. The geometry and topology of configuration spaces have attracted a important attention, over the years. Configuration spaces is a vibrant area of research of geometry and topology with important applications in several areas including algebraic topology, geometric topology, algebraic geometry, knot theory, geometric group theory, differential topology or homotopy theory. These spaces are cornerstones objects in topology, source of several crucial topological data. For example, when the base manifold if the surface, the fundamental groups are the so-called braid groups which are fundamental in geometric group theory. If

is a manifold of dimension

and

are open manifolds of dimension

When

is an open surface, then

is a stein manifold. The manifold

is orientable if and only if

is orientable and even dimensional.

The central theme in the theory of configuration spaces is the (co)homology of these spaces. The cohomology of these spaces was first studied by Arnold (see

[

[

Using Morgan’s theory they describe a differential graded algebra model for the rational homotopy of

Furthermore, Fulton-MacPherson’s model was simplified by Kriz

An important question about the configuration space of manifolds is cohomological dimension. The aim of this work is to start the rigorous investigation of top non-trivial integral cohomology of these spaces. We describe how the cohomological dimension of these spaces is affected by increasing the number of points. All manifolds are assumed to be finite type (Betti numbers are finite numbers). Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a compact connected orientable even dimensional manifold . Assume

and

Then, for

Moreover,

Let

we have

Moreover,

is infinite.

Arnold proved the repetition property of

The behavior of the sequence of cohomological dimensions of configuration spaces of manifold

Question.
If

is an orientable even dimensional manifold with condition

Theorem 3
.

Let
M
be a compact orientable even dimensional manifold. Assume

\partial M\cup U\neq\phi

Then the group

\qquad\binom{\frac{k}{2}+n-1}{n-1}\qquad\text{if $k$ is even },

Let
M
be a closed orientable even dimensional manifold. Assume

Then the group

is infinite. More precisely, the rank of

\binom{\frac{k-1}{2}+n-1}{n-1}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if $k$ is odd}.

Remark 2

From the classical example of Arnold, we see that the integral cohomology group

We formulate a finiteness conjecture for the top integral cohomology of configuration spaces of manifolds with condition

Conjecture 1

(Finiteness)
Let

Bödigheimer–Cohen–Taylor

where the total degree

is given by the grading of

and

. We called
\omega
is a weight grading. The differential

Now, we will define the dual complex of

Reduced Chevalley–Eilenberg complex

In this section, we define an acyclic subcomplex of

Theorem 7
.

Let

be a closed orientable manifold of dimension

The subspace

\Omega_{k-2}^{*,*}(M).(v_{d}^{2},w_{2d-1})<\Omega_{k}^{*,*}(M)

is acyclic for

Proof.

Let

is closed and orientable. An element in

has a unique expansion

The operator

gives a homotopy

∎

We denote the reduced complex

by

Corollary 1

If

and

is closed orientable, then we have an isomorphism

H^{*}({}^{r}\Omega_{k}^{*,*}(M),D)\cong H^{*}(C_{k}(M)).

Remark 3

The particular case

Remark 4

If

is vanish.

M\cup U=\phi,
then from Theorem
8
, we have

Chdim(C_{k}(M))\leq(d-1)k-r+1.

Hence, from the previous computations, we have

Chdim(C_{k}(M))=(d-1)k+1.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\square

5
Application to ordered configuration spaces

As an application of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, we immediately obtain a sharp lower bound for the cohomological dimension of ordered configuration spaces.

Corollary 2
.

Let
M
be a compact connected orientable even dimensional manifold. Assume
\partial M\cup U\neq\phi
and
rank(H^{d-1}(M-U;\mathbb{Z}))\neq 0.
Then, for
k\geq 1,
we have

Chdim(F_{k}(M-U))\geq(d-1)k.

Corollary 3
.

Let
M
be a closed connected orientable even dimensional manifold. Assume
rank(H^{d-1}(M;\mathbb{Z}))\neq 0,1.
Then, for
k\geq 3,
we have

Chdim(F_{k}(M))\geq(d-1)k+1.

Proof of corollaries
2
and
3
.

The action of symmetric group
S_{k}
on
F_{k}(M)
induces an action on the cohomology group
H^{i}(F_{k}(M)),
and the (transfer) map for the finite cover
F_{k}(M)\rightarrow C_{k}(M)
gives an isomorphism

H^{i}(C_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})\approx H^{i}(F_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})^{S_{k}}

between the
S_{k}-
invariant part of the cohomology of the cover and the cohomology of the quotient. The cohomology group
H^{i}(C_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})
is obtained by the trivial representation of
H^{i}(F_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})
(for more details see
[
6
]
). It means that if
H^{i}(C_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})\neq 0
then
H^{i}(F_{k}(M);\mathbb{Q})
is also not zero. Hence, from Theorems
1
and
2
, we get the required lower bounds.

\hfill\square

Remark 5
.

For a closed orientable surface
S
different form sphere, we have

Chdim(F_{k}(S))=k+1.

For more details see Theorem 2.1 of
[
13
]
. In this sense the lower bound in corollary
3
is sharp. It seems that the lower bound in corollary
2
and corollary
3
are the precise formulas for the cohomological dimensions of ordered configuration spaces.

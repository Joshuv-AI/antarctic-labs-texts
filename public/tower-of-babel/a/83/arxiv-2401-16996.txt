Kudla Millson lift of toric cycles and restriction of Hilbert modular forms
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
Let V V be quadratic space of even dimension and of signature ( p , q ) (p,q) with p ≥ q > 0 p\geq q>0 . We show that the Kudla-Millson lift of toric cycles - attached to algebraic tori - is a cusp form that is the diagonal restriction of a Hilbert modular form of parallel weight one. We deduce a formula relating the dimension of the span of such diagonal restrictions and the dimension of the span of toric and special cycles.



1. Introduction

1.1. Intersection numbers of geodesics on modular curves

Let
Y_{0}(p)=\Gamma_{0}(p)\backslash{H}
be the open modular curve for some prime
p
. Let
\gamma_{\infty}
be the image in
Y_{0}(p)
of the geodesic in
{H}
from
0
to
\infty
. It defines a relative cycle

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(Y_{0}(p),\partial Y_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z}).

(1.1)

On the other hand, one can attach compact geodesics to a real quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
. Every ideal class
I
in the narrow class group
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{D}
defines a closed and oriented geodesic
\gamma_{I}
in the modular curve
Y_{0}(p)
. After taking linear combinations and twisting by an odd character
\psi\colon\mathcal{C}^{+}_{D}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
we get a cycle

\displaystyle\gamma_{\psi}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}^{+}_{D}}\psi(I)\gamma_{I}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(Y_{0}(p)).

(1.2)

There is a natural action of the Hecke operators on these geodesics by acting on the endpoints in
{H}
, which gives an element
T_{n}\gamma_{\psi}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(Y_{0}(p))
. Moreover, we have a pairing in homology

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle\colon H_{1}(Y_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z})\times H_{1}(Y_{0}(p),\partial Y_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

(1.3)

If two geodesics
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in
Y_{0}(p)
intersect transversely and in a compact set then
\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\rangle=\sum_{z\in\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}}\pm 1
is the the topological intersection number, where
\pm 1
depends on the local orientation at the intersection point. Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk prove the following in
[
DPV21
, Theorem. A]
.

Theorem

(Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk)
.

If
p
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
, the modular form

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma_{\infty}\otimes\gamma_{\psi}}(\tau)=L_{p}(\psi,0)-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\gamma_{\infty},T_{n}\gamma_{\psi}\rangle q^{n}

(1.4)

of weight
2
and level
\Gamma_{0}(p)
is the diagonal restriction of a
p
-stabilized Hilbert-Eisenstein series
E^{(p)}(\psi,\tau,\tau)
for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))
.

In
[
Bra23
]
, we showed how to recover (and generalize) the theorem of Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk by using the Kudla-Millson lift. The idea was to consider the embedding
F^{\times}\subset\operatorname{SO}(d,d)
of a totally real field
F
of degree
d
, which gives a non-compact cycle on the locally symmetric space of
\operatorname{SO}(d,d)
. The (regularized) integral of the Kudla-Millson theta lift over this cycles gives a generating series of intersection numbers generalizing the right handside of (
1.4
). On the other hand, the relation to a Hilbert-Eisenstein series follows from a Siegel-Weil formula and the seesaw

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}(d,d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}).}

In the case where
F
is real quadratic field, this yields to the theorem of Darmon-Pozzi-Vonk above.

Remark 1.1
.

Another closely related seesaw appears in
[
BCG20
]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),}

where the right hand side is the diagonal restriction of (the same) Hilbert-Eisenstein series, and the left hand side is the evaluation of an Eisenstein class on the same cycle above. The two seesaws are related by embedding
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Q})
in
\operatorname{SO}(d,d)
, and the Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia lift is closely related to the Kudla-Millson lift via the Mathai-Quillen form. We hope to explain this in more detail in future work.

1.2. Main result

In this paper, we replace the torus
F^{\times}\subset\operatorname{SO}(d,d)
by a more general anisotropic maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus, of maximal
\mathbb{R}
-rank in an orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}(p,q)
of signature
(p,q)
with
p\geq q>0
. We restrict ourselves to anisotropic tori, to avoid having to deal with the regularization of the integral as in
[
Bra23
]
. However, one can combine the result presented here with the result of
loc. cit.
to consider any maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus of maximal
\mathbb{R}
-rank, not necessarily anisotropic.

Remark 1.2
.

The study of cycles attached to algebraic tori in orthogonal groups is also motivated by the recent work of Darmon-Gehrmann-Linowski. In
[
DGL23
]
, the authors define rigid meromorphic cocycles in arbitrary signature, generalizing the rigid meromorphic cocycles in signature
(2,2)
studied in
[
DPV21
]
. They conjecture that evaluated on toric cycles, these cocycles should be algebraic. The seesaws presented here could be relevant.

Let
(V,Q)
be a non-degenerate rational quadratic space of dimension
2d
and signature
(p,q)
, where we suppose that
p\geq q>0
. Let
\operatorname{SO}_{V}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2d}
be the orthogonal group of
V
and let
\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\simeq\operatorname{SO}(p,q)^{+}
be the connected component containing the identity. Let
K_{\infty}
be a maximal compact subgroup
K_{\infty}\simeq\operatorname{SO}(p)\times\operatorname{SO}(q)\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
and
\mathcal{D}^{+}\simeq\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{R})^{+}/K_{\infty}
its associated symmetric space, which is a
pq
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.

Let
L\subset V
be an even integral lattice of level
N
. Let
K_{f}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}})
be an open compact subgroup stabilizing the finite Schwartz function
\varphi=\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{L}}\in\mathcal{S}(V_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}})
, where
\widehat{L}=L\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\subset V_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
are the finite adèles. We set
K=K_{\infty}K_{f}
and consider the adelic space

\displaystyle Y\coloneqq\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{A})/K,

(1.5)

which is a finite union of locally symmetric space
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{D}^{+}
for some congruence subgroup of
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
.

In
[
KM86
,
KM87
]
, Kudla and Millson construct an element

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi}\in H^{q}(Y)\otimes M_{d}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

(1.6)

that realizes a lift from the homology to the space of modular forms of weight
d=\frac{p+q}{2}
and level
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. As for the modular curve, there is a homological pairing

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle\colon H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\times H_{pq-q}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

(1.7)

If the homology classes are represented by two smooth immersed submanifolds that intersect transversely and in a compact set, then and the intersection number
\langle C_{1},C_{2}\rangle
is the signed intersection number, as for geodesics. The main feature of the Kudla-Millson lift is that for a cycle
C
in
H_{q}(Y)
it has the Fourier expansion

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,C)\coloneqq\int_{C}\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau)=c^{0}_{\varphi}(C)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle C,C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle q^{n},

(1.8)

where the cycles
C_{n}(\varphi)\in\mathcal{Z}_{pq-q}(Y,\partial Y,\mathbb{Z})
are
special cycles
coming from embeddings
\operatorname{SO}(p-1,q)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(p,q)
.

In this paper, we will evaluate the Kudla-Millson form on cycles attached to algebraic tori. Let
\operatorname{T}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}
be an anisotropic algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-torus, maximal and of maximal
\mathbb{R}
-rank. Since we assumed that
p\geq q
, the orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
is of real rank
q
. Hence, the (maximal) real rank of
\operatorname{T}
is
q
. Let

\chi=(\chi_{\infty},\chi_{f})\colon\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{A})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

be a character of finite order. The archimedean part
\chi_{\infty}
is a character on
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{R})\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{q}\times(S^{1})^{\frac{p-q}{2}}
. To avoid trivial cancellations of the integral, we need to make the assumption that the character is odd,
i.e.
it is the sign function at every real place, and trivial at every complex place. Let
K_{T}=K_{T,\infty}K_{T,f}\subset\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{A})
be such that
K_{T,\infty}=K_{\infty}\cap\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{R})
is maximal compact, and that
K_{T,f}\subset K_{f}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{T}
be the finite group of double cosets

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}\backslash\operatorname{T}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}})/K_{T,f}.

(1.9)

We define

\displaystyle Y_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{A})/K_{T}=\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{C}_{T}}\Lambda\backslash\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{q}

(1.10)

where
\Lambda\coloneqq\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}\cap K_{T,f}
. The embedding of
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{A})
in
\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{A})
induces an immersion
Y_{T}\longrightarrow Y
, and the image of the connected component
\Lambda\backslash\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{q}
associated to
I\in\mathcal{C}_{T}
defines a cycle
C_{T,I}\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}(Y)
.
Let us now suppose that
\chi_{f}
is trivial on
K_{T,f}
. We can see the finite part
\chi_{f}
as a function on
\chi_{f}\colon\mathcal{C}_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and define the cycle

\displaystyle C_{\chi}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}_{T}}\chi_{f}(I)C_{T,I}\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}(Y).

(1.11)

By the work of Kudla-Millson, it is known that if
q
is odd, then
\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,C)
is a cusp form for any cycle
C
; see
[
KM90
, Theorem. 2]
. We show that when we restrict to the cycles
C_{\chi}
, then the lift is always a cusp form.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be an even dimensional quadratic space of signature
(p,q)
with
p\geq q>0
and let
C_{\chi}
be the cycle attached to an anisotropic maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus of maximal real rank
q
. Then
\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,C_{\chi})\in S_{d}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is a cusp form of weight
d
and level
N
.

In the case of toric cycles
C_{\chi}
attached to isotropic
\mathbb{Q}
-tori, as considered in
[
Bra23
]
, the lift is not a cusp form in general. In fact, for suitable lattice in
V
the constant term of
\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,C_{\chi})
is a partial Hecke
L
-function of
\chi
.

1.2.1.

The crucial fact that we want to exploit in this paper, is that any even dimensional quadratic space can be obtained by restriction of scalars of an étale algebra with involution. More precisely, let
V
be a quadratic space over
\mathbb{Q}
as before. Let
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
a maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus in
\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})
. Then, there exists an étale algebra
E
of dimension
2d
with involution
\epsilon
and a
d
-dimensional subalgebra
F
fixed by
\epsilon
, such that
(V,Q)\simeq\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(E,Q_{\alpha})
where

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}(x,y)=\alpha(x\epsilon(y)+\epsilon(x)y)

(1.12)

for some
\alpha\in F^{\times}
. Moreover, we have
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq E^{1}
where
E^{1}
are the elements
x
in
E
of norm
x\epsilon(x)=1
.
The quadratic extension
E/F
is a product of quadratic extension
E_{i}/F_{i}
where
F_{i}
is a field. If
E_{i}=F_{i}\times F_{i}
is split, the involution permutes the two factors and
E_{i}^{1}\simeq F_{i}^{\times}
. This is the case considered in
[
Bra23
]
. On the other hand, when
E_{i}/F_{i}
is a field extension, the involution
\epsilon
is the Galois involution
\textrm{Gal}(E_{i}/F_{i})
. This is the case we want to consider in this paper. The assumption that
\operatorname{T}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic implies that none of the factors
E_{i}
is split. On the other hand, the assumption that the real rank of
\operatorname{T}
is maximal implies that the fields
F_{i}
are totally real.

Since the torus
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
is the restriction of scalars of
E^{1}
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\chi
be a character on an
\mathbb{Q}
-anisotropic torus
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq E^{1}
of maximal
\mathbb{R}
-rank. Suppose for simplicity that
E
is a quadratic field extension of the totally real field
F
of degree
d
. The generating series

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,C_{\chi})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle C_{\chi},C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle q^{n}=\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau,\cdots,\tau,\chi)\in S_{d}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

(1.13)

is the diagonal restriction of a Hilbert modular form
\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{d},\chi)
of parallel weight one for a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
.

If
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq E^{1}
is an anisotropic torus attached to a product of field extensions
E_{i}/F_{i}
, the seesaw argument is still valid. The right-hand side becomes a sum of products of diagonally restricted Hilbert modular forms for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F_{i})
. See Theorem
3.5
for the statement.

The theorem can be summarized by the following seesaw

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}).}

Remark 1.3
.

1.

The condition
p\geq q
on the signature of
V_{\mathbb{R}}
and on the maximality of the
\mathbb{R}
-rank of the torus are necessary to ensure that the cycle
C_{\chi}
has dimension
q
and can be paired with the Kudla-Millson form of degree
q
.

2.

This construction does not generalize to the dual pair
\operatorname{SO}_{V}\times\operatorname{Sp}_{r}(\mathbb{Q})
for
r>1
, since the tori do not give cycles of appropriate dimension. As we will see, the dimension of the cycles
C_{\chi}
satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}C_{\chi}\leq q
with equality exactly when
\operatorname{T}
is maximally
\mathbb{R}
-split. On the other hand, when
r>1
the Kudla-Millson forms are not of degree
q
, but of degree
rq
. Hence, the only way the degree of the forms can match the dimension of the cycles is when
r=1
.

1.3. Spans of diagonal restrictions and toric cycles

Let us now add the conditions that
V
is of dimension
p+q>4
, where
q
is odd, and that the lattice
L
is of level
1
(unimodular). Let
S
be the set of all characters
\chi
that are as above, odd and of finite order. Let

\displaystyle S_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{\left.\Theta_{\varphi}(C_{\chi})\right|\ \chi\in S\right\}\subset S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

(1.14)

be the subspace of cusp forms spanned by the diagonal restrictions
\Theta_{\varphi}(C_{\chi})
. Several authors (including
[
Li17
]
,
[
BL22
]
,
[
KP19
]
and
[
Yan05
]
) have considered spans of diagonal restrictions of Hilbert modular forms, mainly in the case of Hilbert-Eisenstein series.

On the other hand, is also natural to ask what part of the homology is spanned by cycles associated to the torus
\operatorname{T}
. Let us define the subspace

\displaystyle H_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{\left.C_{\chi}\ \right|\chi\in S\right\}\subset H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})

(1.15)

spanned by the toric cycles
C_{\chi}
. Let us also define

\displaystyle H_{\operatorname{cycle}}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{C_{n}(\varphi)\right\}\subset H_{pq-q}(Y,\mathbb{C})

(1.16)

to be the span of the special cycles. The orthogonal complement is the set

\displaystyle H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}\coloneqq\left\{\left.C\in H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\right|\langle C,C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle=0\ \textrm{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\right\}\subset H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C}).

(1.17)

With the previous conditions on the signature of
V
, the adjoint of the Kudla-Millson lift is injective by a result of Bruinier-Funke
[
BF10
]
. In section
5
we deduce the following.

Corollary 1.2.1
.

Suppose that
V
is of dimension
p+q>4
, where
p\geq q>0
and
q
odd. Then

\displaystyle\dim\left(S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\right)-\dim(S_{T})=\dim\left(H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\right)-\dim\left(\operatorname{span}\{H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp},H_{T}\}\right).

(1.18)

In particular, we would have
S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))=S_{T}
if and only if
H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})=\operatorname{span}\{H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp},H_{T}\}
.

1.4. Examples

Let us consider some examples in signature
(2,2)
. Let
V=\operatorname{Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
be the quadratic space with the quadratic form
\det
. For a suitable lattice
L
, the locally symmetric space attached to
\operatorname{SO}(2,2)
is
Y_{0}(p)\times Y_{0}(p)
.

Let us first consider the étale algebra
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})\times\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with involution being the Galois involution in both factors. After twisting by suitable characters, the toric cycle attached to
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq E^{1}
is
\gamma_{\psi}\times\gamma_{\psi^{\prime}}
, where
\gamma_{\psi}
and
\gamma_{\psi^{\prime}}
are both attached to the same real quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
, where
p
is split. This setting was considered by Darmon-Harris-Rotger-Venkatesh in
[
Dar+22
]
, where they show that the generating series

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma_{\psi}\otimes\gamma_{\psi^{\prime}}}(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\gamma_{\psi},T_{n}\gamma_{\psi^{\prime}}\rangle q^{n}

(1.19)

is the diagonal restriction of a ’Hilbert modular form’ for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. In fact, they prove a more precise result as they express the generating series as the trace from level
pD^{2}
to
p
of a product of two weight one modular forms. The corresponding seesaw is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}(2,2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{1}\times\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),}

where
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{1}
are the elements of norm
1
in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
.

Similarly, we can consider a biquadratic field
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})
with the involution that sends
\sqrt{D_{i}}
to
-\sqrt{D_{i}}
. The corresponding seesaw

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SO}(2,2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}D_{2}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),}

where
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})^{1}
are the elements of norm
1
. The image of the torus
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})^{1}
is a product of geodesics
\gamma_{1}\times\gamma_{2}
where the geodesics are attached to
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}})
and

2. Étale algebras with involutions and algebraic tori

In this section, we review the relations between algebraic tori in orthogonal groups and étale algebras with involutions, as explained in
[
BCKM03
]
,
[
BF14
]
and
[
DGL23
]
.

2.0.1.

Let
E
be a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of even dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(E)=2d
. It is said to be
étale over
\mathbb{Q}
if
E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{2d}
. Equivalently, the étale algebra
E
is a product of finitely many number fields
R
. Let
\epsilon
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear involution on
E
and let
F\coloneqq E^{\epsilon}
be the subalgebra fixed by
\epsilon
. We have the following three types

i)

the involution
\epsilon
preserves the factor
R
and
{\left.\kern-1.2ptR\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\epsilon}}\neq\mathrm{id}
,

ii)

the involution
\epsilon
does not preserve the factor
R
. In that case there is another factor
R^{\prime}
such that
R^{\prime}=\epsilon(R)\simeq R
,

iii)

the involution
\epsilon
preserves the factor
R
and
{\left.\kern-1.2ptR\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\epsilon}}=\mathrm{id}
.

Hence we can write
E
as a sum
E=E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{r}
of
\epsilon
-invariant subalgebras, where
E_{i}=R
is a field in case
i)
and
iii)
, and
E_{i}=R\times R^{\prime}
is a product of fields in case
ii)
. The fixed algebra
F
is then the sum
F=F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{r}
where
F_{i}=E_{i}^{\epsilon}
is a number field.
From now on suppose furthermore that
F
has degree
\frac{1}{2}[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]
over
\mathbb{Q}
. In that case we only have case
i)
or
ii)
and we deduce the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

There is an element
\delta\in F^{\times}
such that
E
is isomorphic to
F[\theta]/(\theta^{2}-\delta)
and the involution
\epsilon
sends
\theta
to
-\theta
.

Proof.

For the case
i)
the field extension
E_{i}/F_{i}
is of degree
2
. Hence we have
E_{i}\simeq F_{i}[\theta_{i}]/(\theta_{i}^{2}-\delta_{i})
for some
\delta_{i}
in
F_{i}^{\times}\setminus(F_{i}^{\times})^{2}
. In case
ii)
note we can identify
E_{i}\simeq F_{i}\times F_{i}
. Then we can take
\delta_{i}=1
so that we get an isomorphism

\displaystyle F_{i}[\theta_{i}]/(\theta_{i}^{2}-1)\longrightarrow E_{i},\qquad\theta_{i}\longmapsto(-1,1).

(2.1)

∎

2.1. Étale algebras as quadratic spaces

For
\alpha
in
F^{\times}
we define a
\epsilon
-hermitian form on
E
by

\displaystyle E\times E\longrightarrow E,\qquad(x,y)\longmapsto\alpha x\epsilon(y).

(2.2)

It is preserved by the elements of norm
1

\displaystyle E^{1}\coloneqq\left\{\left.x\in E^{\times}\,\right|\,x\epsilon(x)=1\right\}.

(2.3)

Suppose that
F
is a field and
E/F
is an étale algebra. As mentionned in the introduction, the case where
E=F\times F
is the split algebra will be excluded, hence we will restrict ourselves to the case where
E=F(\theta)
is a quadratic field extension of
F
. In order to work with orthogonal groups instead of unitary groups, we view
E
as an
F
-vector space and let
Q_{\alpha}
be the quadratic form obtained by composing the hermitian form with the trace

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}\colon E\times E\longrightarrow F,\qquad(x,y)\longmapsto\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}x\epsilon(y)=\alpha(x\epsilon(y)+\epsilon(x)y).

(2.4)

Let

be the orthogonal group of this quadratic space. We view it as an algebraic group over

whose

\displaystyle\operatorname{SO}_{E}(F)\coloneqq\left\{\left.g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)\right|Q_{\alpha}(gx,gy)=Q_{\alpha}(x,y)\right\}.

(2.5)

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\delta\in F^{\times}
be such that
E=F(\theta)
with
\theta^{2}=\delta
. The map

\displaystyle E^{1}\longrightarrow\operatorname{SO}_{E}(F),\quad x+y\theta\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}x&y\delta\\
y&x\end{pmatrix}

(2.6)

is a group isomorphism. Furthermore, the restriction of scalars
\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{SO}_{E}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-torus of rank
d
.

Proof.

The parameter
\alpha
is irrelevant here so let us assume
\alpha=1
. With respect to the basis
F

\{1,\theta\}
of
E
, the quadratic form
Q_{\alpha}
has Gram matrix
\operatorname{diag}(1,-\delta)
. It is clear that
E^{1}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{E}(F)
. On the other hand, for a matrix

c&d\end{pmatrix}
the condition
g^{T}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-\delta\end{pmatrix}g=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-\delta\end{pmatrix}
implies

,

and

By multiplying the last equation by

on both sides and using that

we find that

. Using the last equation again, we find that

. If

. If

one can easily check that

, hence

.

Over

, we have an isomorphism of quadratic space

\displaystyle E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle x+\theta y

\displaystyle\longmapsto(x+\theta y,x-\theta y).

(2.7)

On the other hand, the orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{E}((F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{2})
of

is isomorphic to
(F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{\times}
, where the isomorphism is given by the map

\displaystyle(F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{\times}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{SO}((F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{2})

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.8)

Hence
(\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\operatorname{SO}_{E})(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq(F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})^{\times}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}

d
.
∎

.

(n_{1}+2r_{\alpha}+2n_{3},n_{1}+2s_{\alpha}+2n_{3})

Proof.

2.

2.2. Étale algebras of maximal tori

Let
(V,Q)
be a non-degenerate quadratic
\mathbb{Q}
-space of dimension
2d
with orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
. An algebraic
\mathbb{Q}
-group
\operatorname{T}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-torus of rank
a
if
\operatorname{T}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{a}
. If
K
is a field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, we say that
\operatorname{T}
is
K
-split if
\operatorname{T}(K)\simeq K^{a}
. A torus
\operatorname{T}
in the orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
is maximal if its rank is equal to
\dim(V)
. The dimension of maximal
K
-split torus
\operatorname{T}^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{T}
is called the
K
-rank of
\operatorname{T}
. The orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
is of
\mathbb{R}
-rank
q
(since we assume that
p\geq q
). Hence, if
\operatorname{T}
is of maximal
\mathbb{R}
-rank, then the
\mathbb{R}
-rank of
\operatorname{T}
is
q
.

2.2.1.

Consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\operatorname{End}(V)
of
\mathbb{Q}
-linear endomorphism of
V
. We view
\operatorname{SO}_{V}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2d}
, so that we view
\operatorname{End}(V)\subset\operatorname{Mat}_{2d}(\mathbb{Q})
. It is equipped with a natural involution
\epsilon_{Q}
defined by

\displaystyle Q(xv,w)=Q(v,\epsilon_{Q}(x)w)

for
x\in E
. Explicitely we have
\epsilon_{Q}(x)=A^{-1}_{Q}x^{T}A_{Q}
where
A_{Q}
denotes the Gram matrix of
Q
and
x^{T}
is the transpose of
x
. Let
E_{T}\subset\operatorname{End}(V)
be the subalgebra consisting of all the
\mathbb{Q}
-endomorphisms
x\in\operatorname{End}(V)
such that
xt=tx
for any
t\in{\operatorname{T}}(\mathbb{Q})
. We will denote it simply by
E
since there is no risk of confusion, and let
F
be the subalgebra fixed by
\epsilon_{Q}
. We view
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\subset\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})\subset\operatorname{End}(V)
. The following proposition is proved in
[
BCKM03
, Proposition. 3.3]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\operatorname{T}
be a maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus in
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
. The algebra
(E,\epsilon_{Q})
is an étale algebra with involution. We have
2\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}F=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}E
.

Proof.

Over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, the quadratic space
V
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{2d}
with quadratic form
Q(v,v)=v_{1}v_{d+1}+v_{2}v_{d+2}+\cdots+v_{d}v_{2d}

i.e.
with Gram matrix
\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{1}_{d}\\
\mathbf{1}_{d}&0\end{pmatrix}
. With respect to this quadratic form, the torus can be diagonalized in
\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
to

\displaystyle\operatorname{T}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\left\{\left.\operatorname{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{d}^{-1})\right|t_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right\}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}

(2.19)

Hence, the centralizer
E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{2d}
consists of diagonal matrices in
\operatorname{End}(V_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Thus,
E
is an étale algebra. We have
\epsilon_{Q}(g)=g^{-1}
for any
g\in\operatorname{SO}_{V}(\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, we have
\epsilon_{Q}(t)=t^{-1}
for any
t\in\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
. Hence, the involution
\epsilon_{Q}
preserves
E
since for any
x\in E
we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{Q}(x)t=\epsilon_{Q}(xt^{-1})=\epsilon_{Q}(t^{-1}x)=t\epsilon_{Q}(x).

(2.20)

Moreover, the involution permutes
a_{i}
with
b_{i}
in
\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},b_{1},\dots,b_{d})\in E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle F\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\left\{\left.\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d},a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\right|a_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right\}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}

(2.21)

and
2\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}F=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}E=2d
.
∎

2.2.2.

For
\alpha\in F^{\times}
we have the quadratic form

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}\colon E\times E\longrightarrow F,\qquad(x,y)\longmapsto Q_{\alpha}(x,y)=\alpha\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}(x\epsilon_{Q}(y))

(2.22)

that we already defined in (
2.9
). The restriction of scalars
\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(E,Q_{\alpha})
is the quadratic
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space where the quadratic form is
\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\circ Q_{\alpha}
. Let us now prove that
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq E^{1}
, where
E=E_{T}
is the étale algebra with involution defined above.

Proposition 2.5
.

We have
\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(E,Q_{\alpha})\simeq(V,Q)
for some
\alpha
in
F^{\times}
. Moreover, the torus
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}_{E}(F)
.

Proof.

The algebra
E
acts faithfully on
V
. Since they have the same dimension,
V
is an
E
-module of rank
1
. Let
v_{0}
in
V
be a module generator, then we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}

E\simeq V
given by
e\mapsto ev_{0}
. We want to check that this is an isomorphism of quadratic spaces.

The quadratic form
Q
induces an isomorphism of
E
with its dual

\displaystyle f_{Q}\colon E

\displaystyle\longrightarrow E^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E,\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{Q}(x)[y]=Q(xv_{0},yv_{0}).

(2.23)

The map is
E
-linear in the sense that for every
e\in E
we have
f_{Q}(ex)=\epsilon_{Q}(e)f_{Q}(x)
.
On the other hand, the trace form also induces an
E
-linear isomorphism of
E
with its dual

\displaystyle f_{\operatorname{Tr}}\colon E

\displaystyle\longrightarrow E^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(E,\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{\operatorname{Tr}}(x)[y]=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(x\epsilon_{Q}(y)).

(2.24)

It is also linear in the sense that
f_{\operatorname{Tr}}(ex)=\epsilon_{Q}(e)f_{\operatorname{Tr}}(x)
.
Hence
f_{\operatorname{Tr}}^{-1}\circ f_{Q}
is an
E
-linear automorphism of
E
, satisfying
(f_{\operatorname{Tr}}^{-1}\circ f_{Q})(xe)=x(f_{\operatorname{Tr}}^{-1}\circ f_{Q})(e)
. It follows that
f_{\operatorname{Tr}}^{-1}\circ f_{Q}(x)=\alpha x
for some nonzero
\alpha
in
E
, so that
f_{Q}(x)=f_{\operatorname{Tr}}(\alpha x)
. Hence, for any
x,y
in
E

\displaystyle Q(xv_{0},yv_{0})=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha x\epsilon_{Q}(y)).

(2.25)

By the symmetry of
Q
, after setting
y=1
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=Q(xv_{0},v_{0})-Q(v_{0},xv_{0})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha x)-\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha\epsilon_{Q}(x))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha x)-\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\epsilon_{Q}(\alpha)x)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}((\alpha-\epsilon_{Q}(\alpha))x)

(2.26)

for any
x
in
E
. Since the trace form is non-degenerate, it follows that
\alpha
is in
F^{\times}
.

Finally, let us prove that
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\operatorname{SO}_{E}(F)
. We have shown that

. On the one hand, for
t\in\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
we have

. On the other hand, by definition of the involution
\epsilon_{Q}
we have

. Hence
\epsilon_{Q}(t)=t^{-1}
and

. This shows the inclusion
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\subset E^{1}
, and the equality follows from the fact that
\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})
is a maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-torus contained in the (also maximal) torus

, see Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\operatorname{T}
has maximal

-rank, then

and

Proof.

From Proposition

3. Kudla-Millson theta correspondence

3.1. Weil representation

Let
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{Q})
be a quadratic space of dimension
2m
over a totally real field
\mathcal{k}
of dimension
k
. Let
\operatorname{SO}_{\mathcal{V}}
be the orthogonal group of
\mathcal{V}
. We will consider the following two cases:

-

the field is
\mathcal{k}=\mathbb{Q}
of degree
k=1
and the quadratic space
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{Q})
is an arbitrary quadratic space
(V,Q)
like in the introduction, of dimension
2m=2d
. The group
\operatorname{SO}_{\mathcal{V}}
is the orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{V}
.

-

the field
\mathcal{k}=F
is an arbitrary totally real field of degree
k=d
. The quadratic space is
\mathcal{V}=E
, where
E
is an étale algebra viewed as quadratic space of dimension
2m=2
over
F
, and equipped with the quadratic form
Q_{\alpha}
. The orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{\mathcal{V}}=\operatorname{SO}_{E}\simeq E^{1}
is a torus.

3.1.1.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}\simeq\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{k}^{2}
be the
4m
-dimensional symplectic space over
\mathcal{k}
with the symplectic form

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\left(\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathcal{Q}(v_{1},w_{2})-\mathcal{Q}(w_{1},v_{2}).

(3.1)

Its symplectic group is
\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{W})\simeq\operatorname{Sp}_{4m}(\mathcal{k})
. At a place
w
of
\mathcal{k}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{w}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}\otimes k_{w}
be the completion. There is a local projective unitary representation
\omega
on the space
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{V}_{w})
of Schwartz-Bruhat functions, called the Weil representation. It is a projective representation in the sense
\omega(g_{1}g_{2})=c(g_{1},g_{2})\omega(g_{1})\omega(g_{2})
for some complex cocycle
c(g_{1},g_{2})
satisfying
|c(g_{1},g_{2})|=1
. After passing to the adèles, we get a unitary representation

\displaystyle\omega\colon\operatorname{Sp}_{4m}(\mathcal{k})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{A}})),

(3.2)

which is again only projective. However, for certain subgroups of
\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{A}})
the cocycle is trivial and we obtain a true (
i.e.
non-projective) representation. Let us consider some special cases and give some concrete formulas for the Weil representation.

3.1.2.

Consider the subgroup
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{k})\subset\operatorname{Sp}_{4m}(\mathcal{k})
, embedded as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}a&&&b&&\\
&\ddots&&&\ddots&\\
&&a&&&b\\

\gamma_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
is the geodesic attached to the identity in the narrow class group
\mathcal{C}_{D_{i}}^{+}
. In general, the cycle
C_{\chi}
should be a linear combinations of product of geodesics
\gamma_{I}\times\gamma^{\prime}_{I}
attached to a class in the class group of the torus
E^{1}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})^{1}
, and weighted by
\chi
. However, this class group is not the product of the narrow class groups
\mathcal{C}_{D_{i}}^{+}
.

2.

One could also consider the case where

(3,1)
and the space

5. Spans of diagonal restrictions and toric cycles

Let
(V,Q)
be a rational quadratic space of even dimension and signature
(p,q)
with
p\geq q>0
. Let
\varphi=\mathbf{1}_{\widehat{L}}
be the characteristic function of a lattice
\widehat{L}\subset V_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}}
, such that
L=\widehat{L}\cap V
is even and of level
1
(unimodular).
By Poincaré duality, the pairing

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle_{Y}\colon H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\times H^{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

(5.1)

is non-degenerate. For a subspace
V\subset H^{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})
let
V^{\perp}\subset H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})
denote the orthogonal complement with respect to the pairing. If we suppose that
q
is odd, then the Kudla-Millson lift is in fact a lift

\displaystyle\Theta=\Theta_{\varphi}\colon H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

(5.2)

into the space of cusp forms of weight
d=\frac{p+q}{2}
; see
[
KM90
, Theorem. 2]
.
On the other hand, the Kudla-Millson lift has an adjoint

\displaystyle\overline{\Theta}\colon S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\longrightarrow H^{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})

(5.3)

defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\Theta}(f)=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{H}}\overline{\Theta_{\varphi}(\tau)}f(\tau)\frac{dxdy}{y^{2-d}}.

(5.4)

It satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\Theta(C),f\rangle_{\operatorname{pet}}=\langle C,\overline{\Theta}(f)\rangle_{Y}

(5.5)

where the pairing on the left

\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle_{\operatorname{pet}}\colon S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\times S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

(5.6)

is the Peterson inner product.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
V
be a quadratic space of dimension
p+q>4
, where
p\geq q>0
and
q
is odd. Then the lift
\overline{\Theta}
is injective and the lift
\Theta
is surjective.

Proof.

We have
p+q>4=\max(4,3+r)
where
r
is the Witt index. Moreover, we assumed that
L
is an even unimodular lattice. Hence, the injectivity of
\overline{\Theta}
is the content of Corollary 1.2 of
[
BF10
]
. Suppose that
f\in S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
is a nonzero cusp form that is not contained in the image of
\Theta
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
f
is orthogonal to
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
with respect to the Peterson inner product, otherwise we replace
f
by
f-\textrm{proj}(f)
where
\textrm{proj}(f)
is the orthogonal projection of
f
onto
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
with respect to the Peterson inner product. Hence, for every
C\in H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})
we have

\displaystyle 0=\langle\Theta(C),f\rangle_{\operatorname{pet}}=\langle C,\overline{\Theta}(f)\rangle_{Y}.

(5.7)

Since the pairing is non-degenerate, this implies that
\overline{\Theta}(f)=0
. By the injectivity of
\overline{\Theta}
it follows that
f=0
, and that
\Theta
is surjective. Note that we have
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)=\ker(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}
and
\ker(\Theta)=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}
.
∎

Let
S
be the set of characters
\chi\colon\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\operatorname{T}(\mathbb{A})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with the same assumptions as in the rest of the paper. We define twe following two subspaces. First let

\displaystyle H_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{\left.C_{\chi}\ \right|\chi\in S\right\}\subset H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})

(5.8)

be the homology spanned by the cycles
C_{\chi}
for
\chi\in S
. For every cycle
C_{\chi}
, the lift
\Theta(C_{\chi})
is the diagonal restriction of a product of Hilbert modular forms for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
. Let

\displaystyle S_{T}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\left\{\left.\Theta(C_{\chi})\ \right|\chi\in S\right\}\subset S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

(5.9)

its span.
Let
H_{\operatorname{cycle}}\subset H_{pq-q}(Y,\partial Y,\mathbb{C})
be the homology spanned by the special cycles
C_{n}(\varphi)
.
Let
H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}\subset H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})
be the span of the cycles
C
that satisfy
\langle C,C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle=0
for every
C_{n}(\varphi)
.

Corollary 5.1.1
.

Let
V
be a quadratic space of dimension
p+q>4
, where
p\geq q>0
and
q
is odd. We have the following equality

\displaystyle\dim\left(S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\right)-\dim(S_{T})=\dim\left(H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\right)-\dim\left(\operatorname{span}\{H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp},H_{T}\}\right).

(5.10)

Proof.

Since
\Theta
is surjective we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Theta\colon H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})/\ker(\Theta)\longrightarrow S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

(5.11)

Since
S_{T}
is the image of
H_{T}
, the isomorphism restricts to an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Theta\colon H_{T}/\ker(\Theta)\cap H_{T}\longrightarrow S_{T}.

(5.12)

We deduce from (
5.11
) that

\displaystyle\dim\left(S_{d}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\right)=\dim\left(H_{q}(Y,\mathbb{C})\right)-\dim(\ker(\Theta))

(5.13)

and from (
5.12
) that

\displaystyle\dim\left(S_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\left(H_{T}\right)-\dim(\ker(\Theta)\cap H_{T})

\displaystyle=\dim\left(\operatorname{span}\left\{\ker(\Theta),H_{T}\right\}\right)-\dim(\ker(\Theta)).

(5.14)

The result follows by taking the difference of (
5
) and (
5.13
) and using that
\ker(\Theta)=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}=H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}
.
The last equality is due to Kudla and Millson
[
KM88
, Theorem. 4.2]
. We recall the proof. First, if
C\in H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}
, then
C\in\ker(\Theta)
since the Fourier coefficients of
\Theta(C)
are
\langle C,C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle=0
. Hence, we have
H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}\subset\ker(\Theta)
.
On the other hand, consider the
n
-th Poincaré series of weight
d
defined

\displaystyle P_{n}(\tau)=c\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\infty}}\frac{e^{2i\pi n\gamma\tau}}{j(\gamma,\tau)^{d}}.

(5.15)

The series converges when
p+q>4
and is a cusp form. The constant
c
is chosen such that
\langle f,P_{n}\rangle_{\operatorname{pet}}=a_{n}(f)
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficients of
f
. Now suppose that
C
is in
\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}
. In particular, for every
n>0
we have

\displaystyle 0=\langle C,\overline{\Theta}(P_{n})\rangle=\langle\Theta(C),P_{n}\rangle_{\operatorname{pet}}=\langle C,C_{n}(\varphi)\rangle.

(5.16)

Hence, we have
\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}\subset H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}
and the equality
\ker(\Theta)=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}=H_{\operatorname{cycle}}^{\perp}
follows from
\ker(\Theta)=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{\Theta})^{\perp}
.
∎

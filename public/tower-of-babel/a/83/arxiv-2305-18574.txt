On the number of primitive and quasi-primitive irreducible characters in finite groups
RIAD YOUMBAI, GANG CHEN

Abstract
. In this short note, it is proved that both the number of primitive characters and the number of quasi-primitive characters in a finite group G G is divisible by | G : G ′ | |G:G^{\prime}| , where G ′ G^{\prime} is the derived subgroup of G G .



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
20D45

1.
Introduction

Throughout this short note,
G
will be a finite group,
G^{\prime}
the derived subgroup of
G
, and
{\rm Irr}(G)
the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
. All other notations are taken from
[
1
]
.

Recall that an irreducible character
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
is
primitive
if
\chi
can not be of the form
\theta^{G}
for any character
\theta
of a proper subgroup of
G
. And
\chi
is called
quasi-primitive
if
\chi_{N}
is a multiple of an irreducible character for every normal subgroup
N
of
G
. The set of primitive irreducible characters and quasi-primitive irreducible characters of
G
are respectively denoted by
{\rm Irr}_{\rm pri}(G)
and
{\rm Irr}_{\rm qua}(G)
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. If
G^{\prime}\subseteq H
, then
{\rm Irr}(G/H)
acts on the set
{\rm Irr}(G)
; see
[
3
]
for more details about this action and its applications. Furthermore, one can easily see that this action induces actions of
{\rm Irr}(G/H)
on
{\rm Irr}_{\rm pri}(G)
and
{\rm Irr}_{\rm qua}(G)
. We denote the orbit and the stabilizer of an irreducible character
\chi
of
G
by
{\rm Orb}_{H}(\chi)
and
{\rm Stab}_{H}(\chi)
, respectively. For an irreducible character
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, any element
x\in G
satisfying
\chi(x)\neq 0
is called a
vanishing-off element
of
\chi
and the subgroup
{\bf V}(\chi)
generated by all vanishing-off elements is called the
vanishing-off subgroup
of
\chi
.

In this short note, we prove that the restriction of a quasi-primitive irreducible character to the derived subgroup is irreducible. Then we prove that both the number of primitive characters and the number of quasi-primitive characters in a finite group
G
is divisible by
|G:G^{\prime}|
, where
G^{\prime}
is the derived subgroup of
G
.

2.
Main results and Proofs

We start by giving the following lemma, which is
[
1
, Problem 6.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
H
a subgroup containing
G^{\prime}
. Let
\chi
be an irreducible character of
G
and write
Orb_{H}(\chi)=\{\lambda_{1}\chi,\lambda_{2}\chi,...,\lambda_{k}\chi\}
for some
\lambda_{i}\in{\rm Irr}(G/H)
. Let
\xi
be an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{H}
, then

e(\lambda_{1}\chi+\lambda_{2}\chi+...+\lambda_{k}\chi)=\xi^{G}

for some positive integer
e
. In particular,
\lambda_{1}\chi+\lambda_{2}\chi+...+\lambda_{k}\chi
vanishes on
G-H
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G^{\prime}\subseteq H\subseteq G
and
\xi\in{\rm Irr}(H)
, then the set of irreducible constituents of
\xi^{G}
forms an orbit under the action of
{\rm Irr}(G/H)
on
{\rm Irr}(G)
.

The following is
[
2
, Lemma 7.1 (c)]
. We give a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
and
G^{\prime}\subseteq H
be a subgroup of
G
. Then

H{\bf V}(\chi)=G\text{ if and only if }\chi_{H}\text{ is irreducible.}

Furthermore, if one of the equivalent condition happens, then
{\rm Stab}_{H}(\chi)=\{1_{G}\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
H{\bf V}(\chi)=G
and consider the action of
{\rm Irr}(G/H)
on
{\rm Irr}(G)
. We claim that

{\rm Stab}_{H}(\chi)=\{1_{G}\}.

To see this, suppose
\lambda\chi=\chi
for some
\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(G/H)
. Let
g\in G=H{\bf V}(\chi)
, one can write
g=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
for some
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}
, where
x_{i}
or
x_{i}^{-1}
is a non-vanshing element of
\chi
or belongs to
H
. It follows that
\lambda(x_{i})=1
for all
i
and so
\lambda(g)=1
. Thus we have
\lambda=1_{G}
as claimed.

Let
\xi
be an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{H}
and
e=[\chi_{H},\xi]
. Since the orbit of
\chi
contains
|{\rm Irr}(G/H):\{1_{G}\}|=|{\rm Irr}(G/H)|=|G:H|
elements, we have

|G:H|\xi(1)=\xi^{G}(1)=e|G:H|\chi(1)

where the second equality follows by Lemma 2.1. Since
\chi(1)\geq\xi(1)
, we have
e=1
and
\chi(1)=\xi(1)
. Thus,
\chi_{H}
is irreducible.

Conversely, assume that
\chi_{H}
is irreducible. Towards a contradiction, suppose
that
H{\bf V}(\chi)\neq G
. Denote
H{\bf V}(\chi)
by
K
. Then
\chi
is zero in
G-K
and one can see that
\lambda\chi=\chi
for any
\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(G/K)
.

By Lemma 2.1 we have
a\chi=\xi^{G}
for some positive integer
a
and some irreducible character
\xi
of
K
. Since
\chi_{H}
is irreducible, so is
\chi_{K}
. Write
\chi_{K}=\xi
. It follows that
a=1
and

\chi(1)=\xi^{G}(1)=|G:K|\xi(1)=|G:K|\chi(1),

which is a contradiction as we are assuming that
K
is proper in
G
. The last statement follows by the first paragraph of the proof.
∎

The next Lemma is Corollary 11.22 of
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a normal subgroup of
G
with
G/H
cyclic and let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(H)
be invariant in
G
. Then
\varphi
is extendible to
G
.

The following is the first main result of this short note.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\chi
be a quasi-primitive irreducible character of
G
. Then
\chi_{G^{{}^{\prime}}}
is irreductible.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be as in the assumption. We claim that
{\bf V}(\chi)G^{\prime}=G
.

If this is false, one can choose
{\bf V}(\chi)G^{\prime}\subseteq K\subseteq G
with
|G:K|
a prime.
Since
\chi(g)=0
for all
g\in G-K
,
Orb_{K}(\chi)=\{\chi\}
and so by Lemma 2.1, we have
a\chi=\xi^{G}
for some positive integer
a
and an irreducible character
\xi
of
K
.

Since
\chi
is quasi-primitive,
\xi
is invariant in
G
and thus is extandible by Lemma 2.6. It follows that
a=1
and

\chi(1)=\xi^{G}(1)=|G:K|\xi(1)=|G:K|\chi(1).

which is a contradiction as we are assuming that
K
is proper in
G
. Thus,
{\bf V}(\chi)G^{\prime}=G
and the result follows by Theorem 2.3.
∎

Since every primitive irreducible character is quasi-primitive by
[
1
, Corollary 6.12]
, the following corollary follows by Theorem
2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive irreducible character of a finite group
G
. Then
\chi_{G^{{}^{\prime}}}
is irreducible.

Corollary 2.7
.

Metabelian groups are M-groups.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be a nonlinear irreducible character of the metabelian group
G
. Since
G^{\prime}
is abelian, the restriction of
\chi
to
G^{\prime}
is reducible, and so by Corollary
2.6
,
\chi
is not primitive. Let
H<G
be minimal such that there exists
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(H)
with
\eta^{G}=\chi
. By transitivity of the induction,
\eta
is primitive. Since subgroups of metabelian groups are metabelian, we conclude that
\eta
is linear.

∎

The following is the second main result of this short note.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
{\rm gcd}(|{\rm Irr}_{\rm pri}(G)|,|{\rm Irr}_{\rm qua}(G)|)
is divisible by
|G:G^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.3
and
2.5
and Corollary
2.6
, one can see that the action of
{\rm Irr}(G/G^{\prime})
on
{\rm Irr}_{\rm pri}(G)
is semiregular and so is on
{\rm Irr}_{\rm qua}(G)
. Now the result follows by fundamental counting principle.

∎

3.
Final Remarks

It is known in non solvable groups that quasi-primitive characters are not necessarily primitive. Similarly, the converse of
2.5
is not true. For instance an irreducible character of
S_{4}
of degree 3 is not quasi-primitive and yet its restriction to
S_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}=A_{4}
is irreducible. Let
G
be a finite group and denote the set
\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G),\text{ }{\bf V}(\chi)G^{\prime}=G\}
by
{\rm Irr}_{\rm G^{{}^{\prime}}}(G)
. By Theorems
2.3
and
2.5
, we have

{\rm Irr}_{\rm pri}(G)\subseteq{\rm Irr}_{\rm qua}(G)\subseteq{\rm Irr}_{\rm G^{{}^{\prime}}}(G).

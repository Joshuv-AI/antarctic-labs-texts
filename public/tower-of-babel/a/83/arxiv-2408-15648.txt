Counting rational maps on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} with prescribed local conditions
Khoa D. Nguyen, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. We explore distribution questions for rational maps on the projective line ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} over ℚ \mathbb{Q} within the framework of arithmetic dynamics, drawing analogies to elliptic curves. Specifically, we investigate counting problems for rational maps ϕ \phi of fixed degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 with prescribed reduction properties. One of our main results establishes that with respect to the weak box densities in an earlier work of Poonen, a positive proportion of rational maps consist of those having globally minimal resultant. Additionally, for degree 2 rational maps, we perform explicit computations demonstrating that over 32.7 % 32.7\% possess a squarefree, and hence minimal, resultant.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and motivation

An important class of algebraic dynamical systems arises from rational functions on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with coefficients in the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
. These systems are studied in the burgeoning field of arithmetic dynamics, which seeks to understand the arithmetic properties of the iterates of such maps. Rational functions
\phi
in this context are viewed as global objects, and their arithmetic dynamics share many analogous properties to the diophantine geometry of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
A point
\alpha
is called
preperiodic
if its forward orbit is finite, and a
wandering point
otherwise. These are analogous to torsion points and points of infinite order on elliptic curves.

For an elliptic curve defined over a number field, one can define the local minimal discriminant at each non-archimedean place and define the (global) minimal discriminant as the product of the local minimal discriminants
[
Sil09
, Chapters VII–VIII]
. Similarly, for a rational map defined over a number field, one can define the local minimal resultant at each non-archimedean place and define the (global) minimal resultant as their product, see
[
Sil07
, Chapter 4.11]
. After homogenization, a rational map
\phi\in\mathbb{Q}(z)
is given by a pair of homogeneous
polynomials
F(X,Y),G(X,Y)\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]
without common factors in
\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]
. We say that
\phi
is globally minimal or has globally minimal resultant if the resultant of
F
and
G
is equal to the minimal resultant of
\phi
. Over a complete algebraically closed non-archimedean valued field, the notion of (local) minimal resultants give rise to the so called minimal resultant locus which has interesting analytic and moduli-theoretic characterizations
[
Rum15
,
Rum17
]
. Over
\mathbb{Q}
, globally minimal maps play an important role in a conjecture of Silverman on the uniformity of number of integral points in orbits. This is, in turn, analogous to a conjecture of Lang for integral points on elliptic curves after work of Dem’janenko, see
[
BM12
]
and the reference therein for more details.

In recent years, there have been various results concerning statistical aspects of the arithmetic dynamics on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, for example
[
HH19
,
Hin21a
,
Hin21b
,
DHHJ24
]
. However, the nature of these results involves a stochastic process in which one successively composes a random rational map from a finite set endowed with a probability measure. On the other hand, our work continues the classical analogy program between arithmetic dynamics on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and elliptic curves.
In arithmetic statistics of elliptic curves, considerable interest lies in questions regarding the distribution of sets
\mathcal{S}
with certain properties. For instance, the rank distribution conjecture of Katz and Sarnak predicts that exactly
1/2
(resp.
1/2
) of the elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
have rank
0
(resp.
1
). Cremona and Sadek
[
CS23
]
consider the density of global Weierstrass models of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with prescribed reduction types at the primes. We prove analogues of these results for models for dynamical systems, arising from rational maps of prescribed degree
d\geq 2
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

1.2.
Main results

Heights of polynomials are standard in diophantine geometry and we use them to formulate counting problems related to arithmetic dynamics. Fix
d\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
denote the
set of univariate rational maps of degree
d
with rational coefficients.
We count
\phi(z)\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
with certain prescribed reduction types. By homogenization, we write
\phi(X/Y)=\frac{F(X,Y)}{G(X,Y)}
where
F
and
G
are coprime homogeneous polynomials given by:

F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}\quad\text{and}\quad G(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}B_{j}X^{j}Y^{d-j},

with
A_{i},B_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
. To the map
\phi
, one associates the point

P(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[A_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B_{d}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q}).

The height of
\phi
, denoted
H(\phi)
, is defined to be the absolute multiplicative Weil height
of the point
P(\phi)

[
BG06
, Chapter 1]
.
For any
x>0
, let
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)=\{\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ H(\phi)\leq x\}
. It is seen that

\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)=\frac{(2x)^{2d+2}}{2\zeta(2d+2)}+O\left(x^{2d+1}\log x\right),

cf. Proposition
2.6
. Given a set of rational maps
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
, we define its height density to be

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\{\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)\mid\phi\in\mathcal{S}\}}{\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)}\right),

provided that the limit exists. We say that with respect to the height density more than
c\%
or rational maps satisfy
\mathcal{S}
if

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\{\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)\mid\phi\in\mathcal{S}\}}{\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)}\right)>c.

Inspired by Poonen’s work
[
Poo03
]
, there is a more general density to consider. First, it is possible to regard
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
as subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
, see Notation
2.2
. Then one may consider the
box density
in

[
Poo03
, p. 355]
. There are weaker versions of this called the
weak box densities
, cf. Theorem 3.2 of
loc. cit
. More specifically, for
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
and for each
natural number
n\in[1,2d+2]
, we have the notion of the weak box density

\mu_{n}(\mathcal{S})
, see Definition
2.11
. The following analogue of the result of Cremona-Sadek

[
CS23
]
arises: with respect to the box density, does a positive proportion of rational maps have globally minimal resultant? This question, along with related topics, will be explored in a future work.

In this paper, we obtain results for the less general densities, namely the height density and the weak box densities. Our results for the height density hold for
odd
values of
d
while results for the weak box densities hold for every
d\geq 2
:

Theorem A

(Remark
4.4
and Theorem
4.20
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
be an odd integer and let
\Sigma
be a finite set of prime numbers. Let
\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
be the set of rational maps
\phi(z)\in\mathbb{Q}(z)
of degree
d
with
2d
-power free resultant and good reduction at all primes
p\in\Sigma
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

each rational map
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
has globally minimal resultant;

(2)

\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
has positive height density.

Consequently, with respect to the height density, a positive proportion of rational maps have good reduction at all primes
p\in\Sigma
and globally minimal resultant.

Theorem B

(Remark
4.4
and Theorem
4.21
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
n\in[1,2d+2]
be integers and let
\Sigma
be a finite set of prime numbers. Let
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
be the set of rational maps
\phi(z)\in\mathbb{Q}(z)
of degree
d
with
d
-power free resultant and good reduction at all primes
p\in\Sigma
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

each rational map
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
has minimal resultant;

(2)

\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
has positive
\mu_{n}
density.

Consequently, with respect to the weak box density
\mu_{n}
, a positive proportion of rational maps have good reduction at all primes
p\in\Sigma
and globally minimal resultant.

In the special case when
d=2
, our results are quite explicit.

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

With respect to the weak box densities, more than
32.7\%
of rational maps of degree
2
over
\mathbb{Q}
have squarefree, hence globally minimal, resultant.

1.3.
Methodology

We briefly discuss the techniques used in the proof of Theorems
A
,
B
and
C
. A classical result of Schanuel
[
Sch79
]
gives the asymptotic count for the number of rational points in projective space with height
\leq x
. For two generic homogeneous polynomials
F(X,Y)
and
G(X,Y)
of degree
d
,
their resultant is a homogeneous polynomial in the coefficients of
F
and
G
.
Hence
F
and
G
have a common factor if and only if the resultant polynomial vanishes. A result by Schwartz and Zippel (cf. Lemma
2.5
) provides an upper bound on the number of zeros of this polynomial with height
\leq x
. Combining this result with Schanuel’s result implies that

\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)\sim\frac{(2x)^{2d+2}}{2\zeta(2d+2)},

as detailed in Proposition
2.6
. Following Poonen’s work
[
Poo03
]
, we can also study the “box version” of the above results.

In Section
3
, we study properties of subsets
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
defined by the so called local conditions. We define local densities at each prime
p
. Proposition
3.4
employs a sieve-theoretic argument to show that, if a certain admissibility condition is met, the density of
\mathcal{S}
is the product of local densities at all primes
p
. These results are applied in Section
4
to prove Theorems
A
and
B
. Although these local conditions are insufficient to describe the entire collection of
\phi
with minimal resultant, they provide positive lower bounds on the density. This relies on explicit local density calculations, with a main innovation involving counting the number of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-points on the integral scheme defined by the resultant polynomial and its partial derivatives. Some similar arguments have been employed in
[
Poo03
]
.
In Section
5
, an explicit primary decomposition for this integral scheme is used to obtain an explicit lower bound on the weak box densities when
d=2
. Our computations are aided by
Macaulay2
.

2.
The height density, box density, and weak box densities

Let
d\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
be the set of univariate rational maps of degree
d
with rational coefficients. This section establishes key concepts and notation for this article regarding the distribution of certain subsets of
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
.
We may regard
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
as a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
which, in turn, might be regarded as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
.

We
define the height
H(\phi)
of
\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
as the absolute multiplicative Weil height of the corresponding point in
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
.
Then we define the height density of a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
.
Using classical results of Schanuel
[
Sch79
]
for the distribution of rational points on projective spaces and the Schwart-Zipple lemma
[
Sch80
,
Zip79
]
, we derive an asymptotic formula for

\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ H(\phi)\leq x\}.

We also recall the notion of box density and weak box densities for subsets of

\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}

from Poonen’s work
[
Poo03
]
. We have the analogues of
Schanuel’s result and an asymptotic formula for counting
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
in a box; however the statement of these analogues is delayed until the next section since the proof relies on certain sieving results. Finally we define
the box density and weak box densities for a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
.

2.1.
The height density

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
. In homogeneous coordinates,
\phi
is given by
\phi(X/Y)=\frac{F(X,Y)}{G(X,Y)}
, where
F
and
G
are coprime homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
:

F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}\quad\text{and}\quad G(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}B_{j}X^{j}Y^{d-j},

with
A_{i},B_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 2.1
.

To the map
\phi
, we associate the point

P=P(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[A_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B_{d}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q}).

The
height

H(\phi)
of the pair
\phi
is defined as follows

(2.1)

H(\phi)=H(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{v}\max\{|A_{i}|_{v},|B_{j}|_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ 0\leq i,j\leq d\},

where
v
ranges over all places of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Thus we count models for dynamical systems, and not isomorphism classes. Nonetheless, this seems to be natural way to order rational maps, the definition is analogous to counting Weierstrass equations for elliptic curves according to their height (cf.
[
CS23
]
). For a detailed treatment of heights, we refer to
[
BG06
]
.

Notation 2.2
.

From now on, we shall identify
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
with the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
consisting of points
Q=(C_{1},\dots,C_{2d+2})
for which

(1)

C_{i}>0
, where
i
is the minimal index for which
C_{i}\neq 0
. In other words, the first non-zero coordinate is positive.

(2)

and
\gcd(C_{1},\dots,C_{2d+2})=1
.

We shall also denote the coordinates of
Q
by
(A_{0},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})
, i.e.,
A_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C_{i+1}
and
B_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C_{d+j+2}
.
Note that if
Q
satisfies (2), then exactly one of the points
Q
or
-Q
satisfies (1).

Note that if
(A_{0},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
corresponds to
P(\phi)
, then

H(\phi)=\max\{|A_{i}|,|B_{j}|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq i,j\leq d\},

see
[
BG06
, Chapter 1]
.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
x>0
. For a subset
S
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
, let

S(x)=\{(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})\in S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ \max|C_{i}|\leq x\}.

For a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
, let

\mathcal{S}(x)=\{P\in\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ H(P)\leq x\}.

However we typically use the notation
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)
instead
of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})(x)
.

We have the following well-known results by Schanuel, Schwartz, and Zippel:

Proposition 2.4

(Schanuel)
.

(2.2)

\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)=\frac{1}{2\zeta(2d+2)}(2x)^{2d+2}+O\left(x^{2d+1}\log x\right).

Proof.

This is from
[
Sch79
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Schwartz-Zippel)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
R
an integral domain, and
f\in R[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
a non-zero polynomial. There exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
f
such that the following holds. Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
be non-empty finite subsets of
R
, then the set

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\}

has cardinality at most

C\cdot|I_{1}|\cdots|I_{n}|\left(\frac{1}{|I_{1}|}+\cdots+\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\right).

Proof.

This was proven independently by Schwartz
[
Sch80
]
and Zippel
[
Zip79
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

We have

\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)=\frac{1}{2\zeta(2d+2)}(2x)^{2d+2}+O\left(x^{2d+1}\log x\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
d
.
Consequently,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)}=1.

Proof.

Let
f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{d},y_{0},\ldots,y_{d})
be the resultant of the two generic polynomials
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}x_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}y_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}
.
A point
(A_{0},\dots,B_{d})
in
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
(see Notation
2.2
) is not in
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
if and only if
the polynomials
F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}
and
G(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}B_{j}X^{j}Y^{d-j}
have a non-trivial common factor, hence

f(A_{0},\ldots,B_{d})=0
. From Lemma
2.5
, we have

\#(\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)\setminus\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x))=O(x^{2d+1})

where the implied constant depends only on
f
, hence only on
d
. Then we apply
Proposition
2.4
to finish the proof.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
, the
height density
of
\mathcal{S}
is

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(x)}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)},

provided the limit exists. The lower and upper height densities are

\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(x)}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)}\text{ and }\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(\mathcal{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(x)}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)}.

Let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
, the affine height density of
S
is

\mathfrak{d}^{*}(S)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(x)}{(2x)^{2d+2}}

provided the limit exists. The lower and upper affine height densities
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}^{*}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{d}}^{*}

are defined using
\liminf
and
\limsup
respectively.

Remark 2.8
.

What we call the affine height density is simply called the natural density in the literature. The reason for our somewhat unusual terminology and notation will become clear in the next section when we have various subsets of projective spaces and their affine liftings. When
\mathcal{S}
is a set of rational maps of degree
d
, it appears more natural to replace
\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},x)

by
\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(x)
in the definition of
\mathfrak{d}(S)
,
\underline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
, and
\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(S)
. But this is equivalent to the given definition thanks to Proposition
2.6
.

2.2.
The box density and weak box densities

Given a tuple of positive real numbers
\vec{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{2d+2})
, let

\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{2d+2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ |x_{i}|\leq r_{i}\text{ for all }i\in[1,2d+2]\}.

.

Let
x>0
. For a subset
S

\mathcal{S}
of

is naturally a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
, we reserve the simpler terminologies and notations for the projective setting. As in Remark
2.8
, when
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
, it is natural to define the various densities of
\mathcal{S}
using
\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(\vec{r})
instead of

\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},\vec{r})
. This is equivalent to the given definition thanks to the box version of Proposition
2.4

and Proposition
2.6
established in the next section.
For
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
(respectively

S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
), if the box density
\mu(\mathcal{S})

(respectively
\mu^{*}(S)
) exists, then the height density

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{S})
(respectively
\mathfrak{d}^{*}(S)
) and the
weak box density
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{S})
(respectively
\mu_{n}^{*}(S)
) exist
and have the same value.

3.
Projective sets with prescribed local conditions and their affine liftings

For a positive integer
m
and a commutative unital ring
R
, the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{m}(R)
consists of the equivalence classes of

\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{m})\in R^{m+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ \text{$x_{i}$ is a unit for some $i$}\}

under the relation
(x_{0},\dots,x_{m})\sim(y_{0},\dots,y_{m})
iff

(x_{0},\dots,x_{m})=c(y_{0},\dots,y_{m})
for some unit
c
. For
Q=(x_{0},\dots,x_{m})
in the above set, the equivalence class of
Q
in

\mathbb{P}^{m}(R)
is denoted
[Q]
.

Throughout this section, fix an integer
d\geq 2
. We discuss subsets of

\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
defined by local conditions, their so called affine liftings, and some results concerning the various densities in the previous section.
We adopt the following heuristic to help the reader keep track of the notation. When using capital letters to denote a set of points, those in calligraphic font usually denote subsets of projective spaces, those in normal font usually denote subsets of affine spaces, and the bar notation denotes sets of points defined over the ring of integers modulo a prime power.

3.1.
Local conditions and affine liftings

Given a point

Q=(A_{0},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})\in\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})

as in Notation
2.2
, and a natural number
N\geq 2
, we let
Q_{N}
be the reduction of
Q
modulo
N
. The reduction is an element in
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
p
be a prime. A subset
\mathcal{S}_{p}
of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
is defined by a local condition at
p
if it consists of all points
P
that reduce to a prescribed set of residue classes modulo a fixed power of
p
. In greater detail, there is an integer
n_{p}\geq 1
and a set
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\right)
such that

\mathcal{S}_{p}=\{P\in\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})\mid P_{p^{n_{p}}}\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\}.

We define

(3.1)

\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}\left(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}}\mathbb{Z}\right)}=\frac{p^{n_{p}-1}(p-1)\#\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}}{p^{(2d+2)n_{p}}\left(1-p^{-(2d+2)}\right)}.

A set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
is defined by local conditions if it is an intersection of the form
\mathcal{S}=\bigcap_{p}\mathcal{S}_{p}
, where
p
ranges over all primes, and
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is defined by a local condition at
p
. At each prime
p
, we set
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})\setminus\mathcal{S}_{p}
; note that
\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime})
is also defined by a local condition at
p
(since it is the preimage of
the complement of
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
) and
\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime})=1-\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is defined by a local condition at
p
. The affine lifting of
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
is the set:

\begin{split}\bar{S}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\{Q=(\bar{A}_{0},\dots,\bar{A}_{d},\bar{B}_{0},\dots,\bar{B}_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}})^{2d+2}\\
&\mid p\text{ does not divide some entry of }Q\text{ and }[Q]\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\}.\end{split}

The affine lifting
S_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is the preimage of
\bar{S}_{p}
with respect to the reduction map
\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}}\mathbb{Z})^{2d+2}
. We define

(3.2)

\pi(S_{p})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\bar{S}_{p}}{p^{(2d+2)n_{p}}},

then we have

(3.3)

\mu^{*}(S_{p})=\lim_{r_{1},\dots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S_{p}(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}=\pi(S_{p}).

From
\#\bar{S}_{p}=\#(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}}\mathbb{Z})^{*}\cdot\#\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}=p^{n_{p}-1}(p-1)\#\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
, we have

(3.4)

\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})=\frac{\#\bar{S}_{p}}{p^{(2d+2)n_{p}}\left(1-p^{-(2d+2)}\right)}=\frac{\pi(S_{p})}{1-p^{-(2d+2)}}.

Finally, if
\mathcal{S}=\bigcap_{p}\mathcal{S}_{p}
is defined by local conditions then the
affine lifting
S
of
\mathcal{S}
is the set

S=\bigcap_{p}S_{p}
where each
S_{p}
is the affine lifting of

\mathcal{S}_{p}
. It is immediate from the definition that
S
consists precisely the points
Q=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})
such that

\gcd(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})=1
and
[Q]\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
for every
p
.
In other words, from Notation
2.2
,
S
consists
of exactly pairs of points
Q
and
-Q
such that one of them belongs to
\mathcal{S}
. Hence for every
\vec{r}
, we have:

(3.5)

\#\mathcal{S}(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{2}\#S(\vec{r}).

We conclude this subsection with the box version of Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.6
. The idea used in the proof will be extended in the next subsection.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a real number
Z
, let
T_{>Z}
be the set of
(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
such that
\gcd(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})
is divisible by a prime
p>Z
. We have

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#T_{Z}(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}=0.

Proof.

When
Z
is fixed and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\#T_{Z}(\vec{r})=O\left(\sum_{p>Z}\frac{r_{1}\cdots r_{2d+2}}{p^{2d+2}}\right)=O\left(\frac{r_{1}\cdots r_{2d+2}}{Z^{2d+1}}\right)

where the implied constants depend only on
d
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\zeta(2d+2)}.

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{R}^{(d)}(\vec{r})}{\#\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q},\vec{r})}

\displaystyle=1.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.6
, the second identity follows from the first and Lemma
2.5
. Therefore it remains to show the first identity.

For each prime
p
, put
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}=\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})

so that
\mathcal{S}_{p}=\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
. We have
the affine lifting

S_{p}=\{(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ p\nmid C_{i}\ \text{for some $i$}\}.

Now
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{p}\mathcal{S}_{p}=\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
and its affine lifting is
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{p}S_{p}
.

Let
Z>0
and put
S_{\leq Z}=\bigcap_{p\leq Z}S_{p}
. Since
there are only finitely many congruence conditions in the definition
of
S_{\leq Z}
and since
S\subseteq S_{\leq Z}
, we have

\limsup_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}\leq\lim_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S_{\leq Z}(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}=\prod_{p\leq Z}\left(1-p^{-(2d+2)}\right).

Let
Z\rightarrow\infty
, we have:

(3.6)

\limsup_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}\leq\frac{1}{\zeta(2d+2)}.

Recall the set
T_{>Z}
in Lemma
3.1
. From the definitions of the involving sets, we have:

S_{\leq Z}\setminus(S_{\leq Z}\cap T_{>Z})=S.

Therefore
\#S(\vec{r})=\#S_{\leq Z}(\vec{r})-\#(S_{\leq Z}\cap T_{>Z})(\vec{r})
and hence

\liminf_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}=\prod_{p\leq Z}\left(1-p^{-(2d+2)}\right)-\limsup_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#(S_{\leq Z}\cap T_{>Z})(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}.

Let
Z\rightarrow\infty
and apply Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.7)

\liminf_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2d+2)}.

Combining (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
), we finish the proof of the first identity.
∎

3.2.
Some admissibility conditions

Throughout this subsection, for each prime
p
let
\mathcal{S}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})
be
defined by a local condition, let
\mathcal{S}=\bigcap_{p}\mathcal{S}_{p}
, let
S_{p}

be the affine lifting of
\mathcal{S}
, and let
S=\bigcap_{p}S_{p}
be the affine lifting of
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})\setminus\mathcal{S}_{p}
and
S_{p}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}\setminus S_{p}
.
Warning: while
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}
is also defined by a local condition
at
p
(since it is the preimage of
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{p}}\mathbb{Z})\setminus\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
), the
set
S_{p}^{\prime}
is not the affine lifting of
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}
.
In fact, the affine lifting of
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}
is the set of points in
S_{p}^{\prime}
having at least one entry that is not divisible by
p
.
For
Z>0
, let

\mathcal{S}_{>Z}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{p>Z}\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\prime}
and

S_{>Z}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{p>Z}S_{p}^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let the symbol
\delta
be either
\mathfrak{d}
,
\mu
, or
\mu_{n}
where
n
is an integer in
[1,2d+2]
. Note that
\delta^{*}
denotes the corresponding affine version,
\underline{\delta}
denotes the corresponding lower density, etc.
We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
\delta
-admissible if

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{S}_{>Z}^{\prime})=0.

We say that
S
is
\delta^{*}
-admissible if

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\overline{\delta}^{*}(S_{>Z}^{\prime})=0.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let the symbol
\delta
be either
\mathfrak{d}
,
\mu
, or
\mu_{n}
where
n
is an integer in
[1,2d+2]
. Suppose that either
\mathcal{S}
is
\delta
-admissible or
S
is
\delta^{*}
-admissible. Then we have

(3.8)

\delta(\mathcal{S})=\prod_{p}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})\ \text{and}\ \delta^{*}(S)=\prod_{p}\pi(S_{p}).

Remark 3.5
.

We interpret
\prod_{p}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})
as the limit of the sequence of partial products
\prod_{p\leq k}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})
. This limit exists (with value in
[0,1]
) since
0\leq\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p})\leq 1
for every
p
. The same holds for

\prod_{p}\pi(S_{p})
. Our admissibility condition is analogous to the admissibility condition for Weierstrass models considered by Cremona and Sadek in
[
CS23
, Definition 4]
.

Proof.

Let
S_{\leq Z}=\bigcap_{p\leq Z}S_{p}
. Since there are only finitely many congruence conditions in the definition of
S_{\leq Z}
and since
S\subseteq S_{\leq Z}
, we have:

\overline{\delta}^{*}(S)\leq\overline{\delta}^{*}(S_{\leq Z})=\prod_{p\leq Z}\pi(S_{p}).

Let
Z\rightarrow\infty
, we have:

\overline{\delta}^{*}(S)\leq\prod_{p}\pi(S_{p}).

From

S_{\leq Z}\subseteq S\cup\left(\bigcup_{p>Z}S_{p}^{\prime}\right)=S\cup S_{>Z}^{\prime},

we have

S_{\leq Z}\setminus(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})=S

and hence

\#S(\vec{r})=\#S_{\leq Z}(\vec{r})-\#(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})(\vec{r}).

Therefore

(3.9)

\underline{\delta}^{*}(S)=\prod_{p\leq Z}\pi(S_{p})-\overline{\delta}^{*}(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime}).

First, consider the case when
S
is
\delta^{*}
-admissible. From

\delta^{*}(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})\leq\delta^{*}(S_{>Z}^{\prime})

and the
\delta^{*}
-admissibility condition, we have

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\delta}^{*}(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})=0.

Now we consider the case that
\mathcal{S}
is
\delta
-admissible. The set

S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime}
consists of two types of points:

(1)

The first consists of
Q=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})
such that

\gcd(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})=1
. These points come in pairs (namely
Q
and
-Q
) and, according to Notation
2.2
and the definitions of the involving sets, exactly one point in each pair belongs to

\mathcal{S}_{>Z}^{\prime}
.

(2)

The second consists of
Q=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})
such that

p\mid\gcd(C_{1},\ldots,C_{2d+2})
for some prime
p>Z
. We now recall the set

T_{>Z}
in Lemma
3.1
.

This gives

\#(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})(\vec{r})\leq 2\#\mathcal{S}_{>Z}^{\prime}(\vec{r})+\#T_{>Z}(\vec{r}).

Lemma
3.1
and the
\delta
-admissibility of
\mathcal{S}
imply

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\delta}^{*}(S_{\leq Z}\cap S_{>Z}^{\prime})=0.

Therefore in either case, we may let
Z\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.9
) to have that

\underline{\delta}^{*}(S)=\prod_{p}\pi(S_{p}).

Combining this with the earlier upper bound for
\overline{\delta}^{*}(S)
, we have

\delta^{*}(S)=\prod_{p}\pi(S_{p}).

Finally, combining this with (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\delta(\mathcal{S})=\prod_{p}\pi(\mathcal{S}_{p}).

∎

4.
Minimal resultants

In this section, fix an integer
d\geq 2
and our goal is to prove that a positive portion of

\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
has globally minimal resultants. In fact, we consider a certain subset of
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
that includes globally minimal rational maps that have good reduction at a given finite set of primes and proves that this subset has positive height density (for odd
d
) as well as positive weak box density (for any
d
). In section
5
, we obtain an explicit lower bound for the weak box density in the case
d=2
.

4.1.
The minimal resultant

For a rational map
\phi(z)\in\mathbb{Q}(z)
of degree
d
, we express
\phi(X/Y)=\frac{F(X,Y)}{G(X,Y)}
with

F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}\text{ and }G(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}B_{j}X^{j}Y^{d-j},

where
(A_{0},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2d+2}
according to Notation
2.2
. We recall that this means
\gcd(A_{0},\ldots,B_{d})=1

and the first non-zero coordinate is positive. Then we define
\operatorname{Res}(\phi)
to be
\operatorname{Res}(F,G)
.

Let
p
be a prime number and set
\widetilde{F}(X,Y)
and
\widetilde{G}(X,Y)
to denote the mod-
p
reductions of
F(X,Y)
and
G(X,Y)
respectively, and set
\tilde{\phi}(X/Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\widetilde{F}(X,Y)}{\widetilde{G}(X,Y)}
. As is well known
[
Sil07
, Theorem 2.15]
, the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\operatorname{deg}(\widetilde{\phi})=\operatorname{deg}(\phi)
.

(2)

The equations
\widetilde{F}(X,Y)=\widetilde{G}(X,Y)=0
have no common solutions in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
.

(3)

The resultant
\operatorname{Res}(\phi)
is not divisible by
p
.

We say that
\phi
has good reduction at
p
if one of the above conditions holds.

Let
\mathrm{ord}_{p}
denote the usual
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
. The following definition
is taken from
[
Sil07
, Chapter 4]
.

Definition 4.1
.

•

Given
A\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, set
\phi^{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A^{-1}\circ\phi\circ A
. We set
\epsilon_{p}(\phi)
to be the minimum value of
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\operatorname{Res}(\phi^{A}))
, where
A
ranges over
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
.

•

The
minimal resultant
is defined to be the following product

\mathfrak{R}_{\phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{p}p^{\epsilon_{p}(\phi)}.

•

The rational map
\phi
is called
minimal at
p
if

\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\operatorname{Res}(\phi)\right)=p^{\epsilon_{p}(\phi)}
.

•

We say that
\phi
is
globally minimal
or
\phi
has globally minimal resultant if it is minimal at every prime, equivalently
\mathfrak{R}_{\phi}=|\operatorname{Res}(\phi)|
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

Suppose that for all primes
p
,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\operatorname{Res}(\phi)\right)\leq d-1
, then
\phi
is minimal.

(2)

Suppose that
d
is odd and for all primes
p
,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\left(\operatorname{Res}(\phi)\right)\leq 2d-1
, then
\phi
is minimal.

Proof.

The result is a special case of
[
BM12
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

We now introduce the relevant subsets of
\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
defined by local conditions. Let
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{(k)}
consist of
\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
such that
p^{k}\nmid\operatorname{Res}(\phi)
so that
\mathcal{G}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{N}_{p}^{(1)}
is the set of rational maps with good reduction at
p
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite (possibly empty) set of prime numbers. With respect to notation above, set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\bigcap_{p\in\Sigma}\mathcal{G}_{p}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{p\notin\Sigma}\mathcal{N}_{p}^{(d)}\right);\\
&\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\bigcap_{p\in\Sigma}\mathcal{G}_{p}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{p\notin\Sigma}\mathcal{N}_{p}^{(2d)}\right).\end{split}

In other words,
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
(resp.
\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
) consists of rational maps
\phi\in\mathcal{R}^{(d)}
with
d
-power free (resp.
2d
-power free) resultant and good reduction at all primes
p\in\Sigma
.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of primes. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that all
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
are globally minimal. Moreover, if
d
is odd, then all
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
are globally minimal.

4.2.
Equations over finite fields

In this subsection, we shall prove bounds for
\pi(\mathcal{G}_{p})
. For this purpose we recall the following well-known results about the number of solutions to equations over finite fields. Throughout,
q
will be a power of a prime
p
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
shall denote the field with
q
elements.

Theorem 4.5

(Warning’s theorem)
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial with degree
D<n
. Then the number of solutions
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
to
h(\alpha)=0
is divisible by
p
.

Proof.

See
[
LN97
, Theorem 6.5]
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
h
be as in Theorem
4.5
and let
N
be the number of solutions
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
to
h=0
. Assume that
N\geq 1
. Then, we have that
N\geq q^{n-D}
.

Proof.

See
[
LN97
, Theorem 6.11]
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial with degree
D
. Then the number of solutions to
h=0
is at most
Dq^{n-1}
.

Proof.

See
[
LN97
, Theorem 6.13]
.
∎

We will apply the above theorems to the generic resultant of two polynomials of degree
d
.

Definition 4.8
.

Consider two generic homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
:

F(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}x_{i}X^{i}Y^{d-i}\ \text{and}\ G(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}y_{j}X^{j}Y^{d-j}.

Let
\mathfrak{r}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{d},y_{0},\ldots,y_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\ldots,y_{d}]
be the resultant of
F
and
G
. This polynomial is homogeneous of degree
2d
and has degree
d
in each of the variables
x_{i}
’s and
y_{j}
’s.

The set
\mathcal{G}_{p}
is defined by a local condition at
p
since it is the preimage
of

\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{P\in\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ \mathfrak{r}(P)\neq 0\}

under the reduction map
\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2d+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. The affine lifting of
\bar{\mathcal{G}}_{p}
is

\bar{G}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{Q\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2d+2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ \mathfrak{r}(Q)\neq 0\}.

The preimage
G_{p}
of
\bar{G}_{p}
under the reduction map

Lemma 4.9

Notation 4.12
.

For the rest of this section, let
\mathfrak{s}
denote one of the partial derivatives of
\mathfrak{r}
(when necessary, which partial derivative will be specified precisely in later context). This is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2d-1
and has degree
d-1
in the variable for which we differentiate.

Proposition 4.13
.

\#\{P\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2d+2}\mid\mathfrak{r}(P)=\mathfrak{s}(P)=0\}\leq\gamma_{1}p^{2d}
.

Proof.

Consider the algebra
\mathfrak{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}[x_{0},\dots,x_{d},y_{0},\dots,y_{d}]/(\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
and let
\bar{x}_{i}
and
\bar{y}_{j}
denote the image of

x_{i}
and
y_{j}
in
\mathfrak{B}
. Since
\mathfrak{r}
is irreducible and does not divide

\mathfrak{s}
, the dimension of
\mathfrak{B}
is
D\leq 2d
. By the Noether normalization lemma,
there exist
X_{1},\ldots,X_{D}\in\mathfrak{B}
that are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
such that

\mathfrak{B}
is integral over
\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{D}]
. Thus there is a natural number
N
such that the
\bar{x}_{i}
’s and
\bar{y}_{j}
’s are all integral over
\mathbb{Z}[1/N,X_{1},\dots,X_{D}]
. Suppose
p\nmid N
, we find that any tuple
(X_{1},\dots,X_{D})\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{D}
gives rise to at most
O(1)
many

P\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(A_{0},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2d+2}

with
\mathfrak{r}(P)=\mathfrak{s}(P)=0
. If we also require
\gamma_{1}\geq N^{2}
then the inequality in the statement of the proposition holds trivially for
p\mid N
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

In the above proof, suppose
\bar{x}_{i}
(resp.
\bar{y}_{j}
) satisfies an integral dependence relation of degree
a_{i}
(respectively
b_{j}
) over

\mathbb{Z}[1/N,X_{1},\ldots,X_{D}]

then we may take

.

The following Hensel’s lifting lemma is well-known, we include a simple proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial and
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Assume that
h(\alpha)=0\bmod p
and
\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}(\alpha)\neq 0\bmod p
for some
i\in[1,n]
. Then for every
k\geq 1
, the element
\alpha
lifts to
p^{(n-1)(k-1)}
many values
\beta\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
for which
h(\beta)=0\bmod p^{k}
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
k
; the case
k=1
is immediate. Suppose the statement holds for
k
. Let
\beta\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
be one of the
p^{(n-1)(k-1)}
many liftings of
\alpha

with
h(\beta)=0\bmod p^{k}
. Let
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a preimage of
\beta
under the reduction map
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. We have the Taylor series expansion:

h(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=h(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})(x_{i}-b_{i})+\text{higher order terms}.

Liftings of
\beta
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k+1}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
are the images of integer tuples
of the form

(b_{1}+t_{1}p^{k},\ldots,b_{n}+t_{n}p^{k})
with
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
. We have

h(b_{1}+t_{1}p^{k},\ldots,b_{n}+t_{n}p^{k})=0\bmod p^{k+1}
if and only if

\frac{h(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})}{p^{k}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})t_{i}=0\bmod p.

Since
\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\neq 0\bmod p
for some
i
, there are
exactly
p^{n-1}
many solutions
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
. Hence, overall there are

p^{(n-1)k}
many desired liftings of
\alpha
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k+1}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.
∎

.

Let

many elements of

The set
W_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\bigcap_{p\in\Sigma}G_{p}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{p\notin\Sigma}N_{p}^{(2d)}\right)

Proposition 4.17

The set

\mathfrak{d}^{*}
-admissible (cf. Definition

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma})=\prod_{p\in\Sigma}\pi(\mathcal{G}_{p})\prod_{p\notin\Sigma}\pi(\mathcal{N}_{p}^{(2d)}).

The second assertion follows from the first and Proposition
3.4
. For

,

is the set of integer tuples
(A_{0},\ldots,A_{d},B_{0},\ldots,B_{d})\in[-x,x]^{2d+2}

for some prime

.
We need to show that

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#W_{>Z}^{\prime}(x)}{(2x)^{2d+2}}\right)=0.

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}=\frac{\partial\mathfrak{r}}{\partial x_{0}}
. We partition

into disjoint sets

and

It follows from
[
Poo03
, Lemma 5.1]

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#S_{>Z}(x)}{(2x)^{2d+2}}\right)=0.

Now we aim to show that

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#T_{>Z}(x)}{(2x)^{2d+2}}\right)=0.

Let
Q=(A_{1},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d+1}\cap[-x,x]^{2d+1}
and consider the
1
-variable polynomial
h_{Q}(u)
of degree at most

defined by

and note that

h_{Q}^{\prime}(u)=\mathfrak{s}(u,A_{1},\dots,A_{d},B_{0},\dots,B_{d-1},B_{d}).

We count the number of choices of
A_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-x,x]
such that
p^{2d}|h_{Q}(A_{0})
and
p\nmid h_{Q}^{\prime}(A_{0})
for some
p>Z
. We may assume that
h_{Q}(u)
and
h_{Q}^{\prime}(u)
are non-zero polynomials; otherwise there does not exist any such

. Once
Q
and
p
are fixed, the number of residue classes of

for which
p|h_{Q}(A_{0})
is
\leq\deg(h_{Q})\leq d
. On the other hand, since

4.15
that each residue class

lifts to a unique residue class

\leq d

in

has degree

it follows that

|\mathfrak{r}(A_{0},\dots,B_{d})|\leq\gamma_{3}x^{2d}.

is
<(2x+2)^{2d+1}<2\cdot(2x)^{2d+1}

This completes the proof of the result.

,
we need to count the number of
C_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-r_{n},r_{n}]
such that
p^{d}\mid h_{Q}(C_{n})
,
h_{Q}(C_{n})\neq 0
, and
p\nmid h_{Q}^{\prime}(C_{n})
. We may assume that
h_{Q}(x)
and
h_{Q}^{\prime}(x)
are non-zero polynomials; otherwise there does not exist such a
C_{n}
.

Once
Q
is fixed, the number of residue classes of
C_{n}\mod{p}
for which
p\mid h_{Q}(x)
is
\leq d
. On the other hand, since
p\nmid h_{Q}^{\prime}(C_{n})
, it follows from Lemma
4.15
that each residue class
C_{n}\mod{p}
lifts to a unique residue class
C_{n}\mod{p^{d}}
such that
p^{d}\mid h_{Q}(C_{n})
. Thus, there are
\leq d
residue classes
C_{n}\mod{p^{d}}
such that
p^{d}\mid h_{Q}(C_{n})
and
p\nmid h_{Q}^{\prime}(C_{n})
. Thus, the number of values of
C_{n}
in
\mathbb{Z}\cap[-r_{n},r_{n}]
is at most
\displaystyle d\left(\frac{2r_{n}}{p^{d}}+1\right)
.

There exists a positive constant
\Gamma
depending only on
d
and the
r_{i}
’s with
1\leq i\leq 2d+2
and
i\neq n
such that

|h_{Q}(x)|\leq\Gamma r_{n}^{d}

for every
x\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]
.
On the other hand, since
p^{d}\mid h_{Q}(C_{n})

and
h_{Q}(C_{n})\neq 0
, we have

p^{d}\leq|h_{Q}(C_{n})|\leq\Gamma r_{n}^{d}.

Thus, we find that
Z<p\leq\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n}
. The total number of choices for
Q
is less than
2\cdot\prod_{i\neq n}(2r_{i})
when the
r_{i}
’s are sufficiently large. We have shown that

\begin{split}\#B_{>Z}(\vec{r})\leq&\sum_{Z<p\leq\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n}}\left(2\cdot\prod_{i\neq n}(2r_{i})\left(\frac{2dr_{n}}{p^{d}}+d\right)\right)\\
\leq&2d\prod_{i=1}^{2d+2}(2r_{i})\sum_{Z<p}\frac{1}{p^{d}}+2d\prod_{i=1}^{2d+2}(2r_{i})\sum_{Z<p\leq\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n}}\frac{1}{2r_{n}}\\
\leq&2d\prod_{i=1}^{2d+2}(2r_{i})\sum_{Z<p}\frac{1}{p^{d}}+2d\prod_{i=1}^{2d+2}(2r_{i})\frac{\Gamma^{1/d}}{\log(\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n})},\\
\end{split}

where we use the fact that the number of primes
Z<p\leq\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n}
is less than

2\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n}/\log(\Gamma^{1/d}r_{n})
when
r_{n}
is large.

We recall that
\Gamma
is independent of
r_{n}
. The above estimate implies

\limsup_{r_{1},\dots,\hat{r}_{n},\dots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{r_{n}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#B_{>Z}(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}\leq 2d\sum_{Z<p}\frac{1}{p^{d}}.

Therefore

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\left(\limsup_{r_{1},\dots,\hat{r}_{n},\dots,r_{2d+2}\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{r_{n}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#B_{>Z}(\vec{r})}{\#\operatorname{Box}(\vec{r})}\right)=0

and we finish the proof.
∎

5.
Rational maps of degree
2

In this section, we illustrate that some direct computations can be done when the degree is small (for instance when the degree is
2
) in order to provide an explicit lower bound for the weak box densities of globally minimal rational maps.

For a homogeneous polynomial
R(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}]
, a prime
p
, positive integers
1\leq i\leq n
, and a point
P\in\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
, the statement that

R(P)=0\bmod p^{i}
is well-defined. This follows from the homogeneity of
R
and the fact that any two liftings of
P
in
\mathbb{A}^{m+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
differ (multiplicatively) by a unit of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. We have:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
m
be a positive integer,
R(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}]
a homogeneous polynomial of degree at least
2
. For each prime
p
, let

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{p}

\displaystyle=\{P\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ R(P)\neq 0\}\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\{P\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ R(P)=0\ \text{and}\ \frac{\partial R}{\partial X_{i}}(P)\neq 0\ \text{for some $0\leq i\leq m$}\right\}.

Then the set
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{P\in\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ R(P)\neq 0\bmod p^{2}\}

has

|\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{p}|=\frac{p^{m+1}|\bar{A}_{p}|+(p^{m+1}-p^{m})|\bar{B}_{p}|}{p(p-1)}.

Proof.

Each point of
\bar{A}_{p}
lifts to
p^{m+1}
many points in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{m+1}
.
By Lemma
4.15
, each point of
\bar{B}_{p}
lifts to exactly
p^{m+1}-p^{m}
many
points
P^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{m+1}
such that
R(P^{\prime})\neq 0\bmod p^{2}
.

Since
R
is homogeneous of degree at least
2
, the set of all the above liftings is invariant under multiplication by elements of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and every lifting has an entry that is in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Hence these liftings yield exactly
(p^{m+1}|\bar{A}_{p}|+(p^{m+1}-p^{m})|\bar{B}_{p}|)/(p(p-1))
many points in
\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
. Finally, we note that if
P\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m+1}
such that
R(P)=0
and
\displaystyle\frac{\partial R}{\partial X_{i}}(P)=0
for every
i
then using Taylor expansion
as in the proof of Lemma
4.15
we have
R(P^{\prime})=0\bmod p^{2}

for every lifting
P^{\prime}
of
P
in
\mathbb{A}^{m+1}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Noncollinear DFT+ U and Hubbard parameters with fully-relativistic ultrasoft pseudopotentials
Luca Binci, Nicola Marzari

Abstract
The magnetic, noncollinear parametrization of Dudarev’s DFT+ U U method is generalized to fully-relativistic ultrasoft pseudopotentials. We present the definition of the DFT+ U U total energy functional, and the calculation of forces and stresses in the case of orthogonalized atomic orbitals defining the localised Hubbard manifold, where additional contributions arising from the derivative of the inverse square root of the overlap matrix appear. We further extend the perturbative calculation of the Hubbard U U parameters within density-functional perturbation theory to the noncollinear relativistic case, by exploiting an existing and recently developed theoretical approach that takes advantage of the time-reversal operator to solve a second Sternheimer equation. We validate and apply the new scheme by studying the electronic structure and the thermodynamics of the binary compounds EuX (where X = O, S, Se, Te is a chalcogen atom), as representative simple crystals, and of the pyrochlore Cd 2 Os 2 O 7 , representative of a more structurally complex oxide.



I
introduction

Simulating electronic properties of materials containing transition-metal or rare-earth atoms has always been a considerable challenge for first-principles methods based on density-functional theory (DFT). The reasons are twofold: strongly localized electrons can display multi-determinant character/strong correlations, that are not captured by approximate DFT, and they give rise to large self-interaction errors
Perdew and Zunger 1981
. One of the most widely adopted scheme to improve the electronic description of systems hosting localized electrons is DFT+
U

Anisimov et al. 1991
;
Anisimov et al. 1997
;
Himmetoglu et al. 2014
. The approach consists in augmenting the DFT total energy functional with a corrective term aiming to improve the description of
d
- or
f
-electrons:
E_{\mathrm{DFT}+U}=E_{\mathrm{DFT}}+E_{U}
. Rather than addressing electronic correlations, DFT+
U
corrects the large self-interaction errors that are prevalent in systems that also display strong correlations
Kulik et al. 2006
. In fact, the main effect of the
E_{U}
correction enforces an integer-like electronic occupation in the target localized manifold, or in more modern terminology, a piece-wise linearity of the total energy with respect to the occupation of such a manifold
Anisimov et al. 1993
;
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
. It has been shown to be quite successful to describe the magnetic and insulating properties of oxides
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
;
Agapito et al. 2015
and molecules
Kulik et al. 2006
;
Kulik and Marzari 2008
containing
3d
transition-metals. Elements belonging to the same block of the periodic table but in the 5th and 6th rows have frontier
4d
and
5d
orbitals, which are spatially more extended and therefore less localised and magnetic than the
3d
ones; for this reason the +
U
correction is less frequently adopted to these ions. On the other hand, increasing the atomic number also increases the strenght of the relativistic effects, i.e. the spin-orbit coupling (SOC), which scales as
Z^{4}
for hydrogenic atoms (
Z
being the atomic number), and as
Z^{2}
(due to the shielding effect) in the external shells of many-electron atoms
Pyykko 1988
. As a consequence, in less explored but very interesting cases, it is the combined effect of electronic localization and SOC that determines the nature of the ground state. Relevant examples are the strontium iridate Sr
2
IrO
4

Kim et al. 2008
, pyrochlore iridates
Wan et al. 2011a
, americium monochalcogenides
Zhang et al. 2012
, and the osmium-based double perovskite Ba
2
NaOsO
6

Erickson et al. 2007
.

In this paper we introduce a generalization of the DFT+
U
method to deal with noncollinear magnetic structures and SOC – already described in
Dudarev et al. 2019
;
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2017
– and extend it to a fully-relativistic (FR) ultrasoft pseudopotentials (US-PP) formalism
Vanderbilt 1990
;
Corso and Conte 2005
. In doing this, we exploit Smogunov’s implementation of Liechtenstein’s DFT+
U
functional with FR US-PP
Giannozzi et al. 2017
;
Liechtenstein et al. 1995
(which is currently restricted to total energy calculations only, without structural optimization or determination of Hubbard parameters), and then adapt it to Dudarev’s parametrization of DFT+
U
(which consists in the neglect of high-order multipolar terms in the Coulomb interaction tensor within Liechtenstein’s DFT+
U

Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
). In the same PP framework, we extend the former implementation by deriving the expressions of forces and stresses, also when orthogonalized atomic orbitals are used as Hubbard projectors, thus requiring the inclusion of contributions coming from the derivatives of the inverse square root of the overlap matrix between different site-centered atomic-like orbitals. Finally, exploiting density-functional perturbation theory (DFPT)
Baroni et al. 2001
, we present a scheme to calculate the Hubbard
U
parameters from first-principles in the case of broken time-reversal symmetry (induced by the noncollinear magnetism) and including SOC in full at the self-consistent level. In doing so, we leverage the recent approach introduced in Ref.
Urru and Dal Corso 2019
for the calculation of phonon frequencies in the presence of SOC and noncollinear magnetism – that exploits the solution of two independent Sternheimer equations: a standard one and a time-reversed one, in order to obtain the first-order wavefunctions entering in the linear-response problem. In this way, the entire formulation is internally consistent and completely free from any adjustable parameters.

We then apply the theory we developed to the study of the series of binary europium monochalcogenides EuX (X = O, S, Se, Te) and of the more complex oxide osmium pyroclore Cd
2
Os
2
O
7
. For the EuX compounds, we determine both electronic structure and thermodynamic properties, such as lattice parameter and bulk modulus, and quantify the effect of different parametrizations of the exchange-correlation functional on the final results. We also study the pressure dependence of the enthalpy, and evaluate the critical pressure above which a structural phase transition occurs, where the crystal changes from the
Fm\bar{3}m
to the
Pm\bar{3}m
space group. For Cd
2
Os
2
O
7
, we study the electronic structure and energetics of different noncollinear magnetic orderings, and analyze how the magnetic patterns imposed, change the crystal structure following variable-cell structural relaxations.

The paper is organized as follows: in Sec.
II.1
we introduce the DFT+
U
functional in the FR US-PP scheme, and generalize the calculation of the forces and stress tensor to this framework, extending also to the case of orthogonalized atomic wavefunctions defining the localized Hubbard manifold. In Sec.
II.2
we discuss the theory concerning the perturbative calculation of the Hubbard
U
parameters, generalized to the noncollinear case in absence of time-reversal symmetry. Finally, in Sec.
III
we validate the new approach by applying it to the study of EuX oxide and chalcogenides and Cd
2
Os
2
O
7
.

II
theoretical formulation

II.1
Total energy functional, forces and stresses

Within DFT+
U
, the total energy DFT functional is modified by adding the so-called Hubbard corrective term. Dudarev’s formulation of DFT+
U

Dudarev et al. 1998
provides a prescription for the Hubbard augmentation part that is rotationally invariant with respect to the localized manifold of interest. Here, we adopt the parametrization of the noncollinear Dudarev’s functional as employed in
Dudarev et al. 2019
;
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2017
, and generalize it to the FR US-PP’s scheme. In principle, when atomic states from a FR calculation are used to build the Hubbard occupation matrix
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
, this latter should depend on the quantization-axis projection of the total angular momentum
\bm{J}=\bm{L}+\bm{S}
. However, to avoid to work in the basis of
\bm{J}
and recast the formulation in a similar way as is done in electronic-structure codes for US-PP’s
Vanderbilt 1990
, we use the
j
-averaged radial parts of the atomic wavefunctions. The assumption underlying this procedure is that magnetism (mostly driven by the electronic exchange) is dominant compared to the splitting induced by SOC. In this way, the total energy functional acquires the spin-resolved form
E=E_{\mathrm{DFT}}+E_{U}
, where

E_{U}=\sum_{I}\frac{U^{I}}{2}\sum_{m,\sigma}\bigg(n^{I\sigma\sigma}_{mm}-\sum_{m^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}n^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}}\,n^{I\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}_{m^{\prime}m}\bigg)

(1)

where
I
labels the atomic site within the unit cell, and
m
and
\sigma
are the atomic magnetic quantum number and spin index, respectively. The occupation matrix
n_{mm^{\prime}}^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
in the FR US-PP scheme reads
Timrov et al. 2021

\displaystyle n_{mm^{\prime}}^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}\tilde{\theta}_{i}\,\big\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{2}}\big|S^{\sigma_{2}\sigma^{\prime}}\big|{\phi}^{I\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\big\rangle\big\langle{\phi}^{I\sigma}_{m}\big|S^{\sigma\sigma_{1}}\big|\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{1}}\big\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle n^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{m}n_{mm}^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\big(n^{I}\delta^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\bm{\sigma}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{m}^{I}\big)

(3)

where
\psi
and
\phi
are, respectively, the (pseudo) Kohn-Sham (KS) and localised Hubbard states,
n^{I}=\sum_{\sigma}n^{I\sigma\sigma}
is the occupation and
\bm{m}^{I}=\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}n^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\bm{\sigma}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
is the magnetization of the Hubbard manifold, and the index
i
collectively represents the band and the quasimomentum indices. The 2
\times
2 ultrasoft (nonlocal)
S
matrix
\langle\bm{r}_{1}|S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\bm{r}_{2}\rangle=S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})
is given by

\begin{split}S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2})=\;\,&\delta(\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2})\,\delta^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\\
&+\sum_{I,\nu,\nu^{\prime}}Q^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\,\beta^{I}_{\nu}(\bm{r}_{1})\,\beta^{I}_{\nu^{\prime}}(\bm{r}_{2})^{\ast},\end{split}

(4)

where
\nu
is an index referring to the atomic quantum numbers of the
\beta
projector functions pertaining to the US-PP, and

Q^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}=\sum_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\sigma_{1}}f^{\sigma\sigma_{1}}_{\nu\nu_{1}}\,Q^{I}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}\,f_{\nu_{2}\nu^{\prime}}^{\sigma_{1}\sigma^{\prime}}

(5)

are the FR augmentation charges. These lead to having orthonormal constraints
\sum_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\langle\psi^{\sigma}_{i}|S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\psi^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
; see Ref.
Corso and Conte 2005
for an in-depth discussion. The KS pseudo-wavefunctions
\psi_{i}^{\sigma}(\bm{r})=\langle\bm{r},\sigma|\Psi_{i}\rangle
are defined as the
\sigma
-spin component of the KS spinor
|\Psi_{i}\rangle=\sum_{\sigma}|\psi_{i\sigma},\sigma\rangle
. The atomic pseudo-states
\phi^{I\sigma}_{m}=\phi^{I}_{m}
are actually spin-independent (because of the
j
-averaging procedure), but for practical purposes we still define the spinor
|\Phi^{I}_{m}\rangle=\sum_{\sigma}|\phi_{m}^{I},\sigma\rangle
, for which
\phi_{m}^{I\sigma}(\bm{r})=\langle\bm{r},\sigma|\Phi^{I}_{m}\rangle
(it will have nonzero spin-components as in the collinear case, and equal spatial parts). If these states are orthogonalized according to Löwdin’s decomposition, an overlap matrix
O^{I\sigma I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}}=\langle\phi^{I\sigma}_{m}|S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\rangle
needs to be introduced and the Hubbard atomic-like states become

\phi^{I\sigma}_{m}(\bm{r})\rightarrow\varphi^{I\sigma}_{m}(\bm{r})=\sum_{I^{\prime},m^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\big(O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big)_{m^{\prime}m}^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}I\sigma}\,\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{r}).

(6)

The former set of equations completely defines our total energy functional.

For the calculation of forces and stresses, the Hellmann-Feynamn theorem applies (provided the Hubbard
U
is considered independent of displacements and strain); as a consequence, the variation of
E_{U}
with respect to a parameter
\mu
(
\mu=\bm{u}_{I}
, the atomic displacement, for forces, and
\mu=\bm{\varepsilon}
, the strain, for the stress tensor) involves only the variations of the ultrasoft matrix and the
\phi^{I\sigma}_{m}(\bm{r})
functions:

\frac{\partial E_{U}}{\partial\mu}=\sum_{I}\frac{U^{I}}{2}\sum_{\{m\},\{\sigma\}}\Big(\delta_{mm^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}-2n^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}}\Big)\,\frac{\partial n^{I\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}_{m^{\prime}m}}{\partial\mu}

in which the terms to be differentiated, according to Eq. (
2
), are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma}|S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|\phi^{I\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\rangle\rightarrow\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma}|\bigg[\frac{\partial S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{\partial\mu}|\phi^{I\sigma^{\prime}}_{m}\rangle+S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\bigg|\frac{\partial\phi^{I\sigma^{\prime}}_{m}}{\partial\mu}\bigg\rangle\bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{\partial\mu}=\sum_{I,\nu\nu^{\prime}}Q^{I\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\bigg(\,\bigg|\frac{\partial\beta^{I}_{\nu}}{\partial\mu}\bigg\rangle\big\langle\beta^{I}_{\nu^{\prime}}\big|+\big|\beta^{I}_{\nu}\big\rangle\bigg\langle\frac{\partial\beta^{I}_{\nu^{\prime}}}{\partial\mu}\bigg|\,\bigg);

in the case of orthogonalized atomic orbitals, the derivative of Eq. (
6
) involves also the variation of the overlap matrix

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\varphi^{I\sigma}_{m}(\bm{r})}{\partial\mu}=&\sum_{I^{\prime},m^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{\partial O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\partial\mu}\bigg)_{m^{\prime}m}^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}I\sigma}\,\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{r})\\
&+\big(O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big)_{m^{\prime}m}^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}I\sigma}\,\frac{\partial\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}(\bm{r})}{\partial\mu}\bigg].\end{split}

The case of nonorthogonal atomic states is recovered by setting
O^{I\sigma I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}}=\delta^{II^{\prime}}\delta^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}
; notably, in this latter case, when evaluating forces, the second term of the former equation reduces to
|\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\rangle=|\partial_{\bm{u}_{J}}\phi^{I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta^{I^{\prime}J}|\partial_{\bm{u}_{J}}\phi^{J\sigma^{\prime}}_{m^{\prime}}\rangle
, and still contains the sum of inter-orbital terms within the same atom due to saturation of the
m^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
indexes with the overlap matrix. For the detailed expressions concerning how to evaluate in practice the derivatives of the atomic orbitals in reciprocal space, we refer to Ref.
Cococcioni 2010
, and for the variation of the
\beta^{I}_{\nu}
projectors we refer to Ref.
Dal Corso 2001
. In particular, the calculation of the derivative of the inverse square root of
O^{I\sigma I^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}}
is carried out using the formal solution of the Lyapunov equation

\begin{split}\frac{\partial O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\partial\mu}=-&\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\exp\big(-tO^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big)\\
&\times O^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\partial O}{\partial\mu}\bigg)\,O^{-1}\,\exp\big(-tO^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big)\end{split}

(7)

where the integral can be performed analytically element by element of
\partial_{\mu}O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

Timrov et al. 2020
. The usefulness of Eq. (
7
) – although computationally expensive for systems with many atoms (large supercells) – is that it is exact and shows that for the calculation of
\partial_{\mu}O^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
only the eigenvalues of
O
and of its derivative are needed.

II.2
First-principles determination of Hubbard parameters using perturbation theory

Following the linear-response approach for the calculation of the Hubbard parameters introduced by Cococcioni and de Gironcoli
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
, the Hubbard
U
can be defined as

U^{I}=\big(\chi_{0}^{-1}-\chi^{-1}\big)_{II},

(8)

where
\chi\;(\chi_{0})
are the interacting (noninteracting) response matrices to a localised perturbation indirectly changing – through a Legendre transform – the localised orbital occupation. In the original scheme of Ref.
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
, in order to have a localized perturbing potential, a finite-difference approach based on supercells was used. More recently, Timrov et al.
Timrov et al. 2018
;
Timrov et al. 2021
developed a more efficient scheme based on DFPT, according to which the supercell periodicity is substituted by a decomposition of the potential into monochromatic perturbations, with wave-vectors belonging to a
\bm{q}
-point grid of size commensurate to an equivalent supercell. Although in Refs.
Timrov et al. 2018
;
Timrov et al. 2021
time-reversal symmetry was not assumed – because of the presence of a spin-polarized exchange-correlation potential – the collinearity of the problem still allowed to recast the perturbative expansion as in the time-reversal-invariant case. However, it has been shown
Cao et al. 2018
;
Gorni et al. 2018
;
Urru and Dal Corso 2019
that when noncollinear magnetism is present, it is necessary to solve at least two separate Sternheimer linear systems. This approach was originally developed for the calculation of spin-fluctuation spectra in a norm-conserving pseudopotential (NC-PP) framework: in Ref.
Cao et al. 2018
two Sternheimer equations were solved, one at
+\bm{q}
and the other at
-\bm{q}
, while in
Gorni et al. 2018
the time-reversal operator
\mathcal{T}
was used in the second Sternheimer equation. In Ref.
Urru and Dal Corso 2019
the scheme employing
\mathcal{T}
was further generalized to FR US-PPs, and used for the calculation of phonon dispersions in elemental magnetic metals.

The original extension of DFPT to US-PPs was developed by Dal Corso for the calculation of vibrational spectra
Dal Corso 2001
, who showed how the change of orthonormalization constraint – provided by the presence of the
S
matrix – changes the induced charge density; then, by the same author, it was generalised to the FR nonmagnetic case
Dal Corso 2007
. In this work we follow the strategy of Ref.
Urru and Dal Corso 2019
, where the former DFPT was extended to the magnetic time-reversal symmetry broken case, and apply it to an external perturbation changing the orbital occupation. Indeed, to evaluate
ab initio
the Hubbard
U
parameter in the linear response method of Ref.
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
, one uses the constrained DFT technique
Dederichs et al. 1984
;
Hybertsen et al. 1989
: from the total energy
E
, a new energy functional is introduced
\tilde{E}\{q\}=\min_{\rho,q}\big\{E[\rho]+\sum_{I}\alpha_{I}(n^{I}-q^{I})\big\}
, where the orbital occupations
n^{I}
are controlled by the variables
q^{I}
. The additional term
\sum_{I}\alpha_{I}(n^{I}-q^{I})
acts precisely as a perturbation changing the orbital occupation of the localised manifold, thanks to which variations of
\tilde{E}\{q\}
with respect to
q^{I}
can be acquired. The Hubbard
U
parameter is then defined as the curvature of
\tilde{E}\{q\}
with respect to the variables
q^{I}
. In practice a Legendre transform is performed, which shifts the
q^{I}
-dependence to the
\alpha^{I}
-dependence:
\mathcal{E}=\tilde{E}\{q\}+\sum_{I}\alpha_{I}q^{I}
. The curvature of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\alpha_{I}
yields the
\chi
and
\chi_{0}
matrices, which are linked to the Hubbard
U
by Eq. (
8
)
Cococcioni and de Gironcoli 2005
;
Timrov et al. 2018
.

Introducing the projector
P^{I,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}=\sum_{m}S^{\sigma_{1}\sigma}|\phi^{I\sigma}_{m}\rangle\langle\phi^{I\sigma^{\prime}}_{m}|S^{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma_{2}}
– in terms of which the external perturbation becomes
\sum_{I}\alpha_{I}\sum_{i,\{\sigma\}}\tilde{\theta}_{i}\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{1}}|P^{I,\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}|\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{2}}\rangle
(the notation
\sum_{\{\sigma\}}
stands for a sum over all the
\sigma
indices appearing in the following expression) – the first derivative of the total energy
\mathcal{E}=E+\sum_{I}\alpha_{I}\sum_{m\sigma}n^{I\sigma\sigma}_{mm}
with respect to the external parameter
\alpha_{I}
, because of Hellmann-Feynman theorem, is
\partial_{I}\mathcal{E}=n^{I}=\sum_{i,\{\sigma\}}\tilde{\theta}_{i}\,\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{1}}|P^{I,\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}|\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{2}}\rangle
(we use the notation
\partial_{I}\equiv\partial/\partial\alpha_{I}
). The second derivative gives the response matrix
\partial_{I}\partial_{I^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}=\chi_{II^{\prime}}
, where

\begin{split}\chi_{II^{\prime}}&=\sum_{m,\sigma}\frac{\partial n_{mm}^{I\sigma\sigma}}{\partial\alpha_{I^{\prime}}}=\sum_{i,\{\sigma\}}\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{\partial\tilde{\theta}_{i}}{\partial\alpha_{I^{\prime}}}\bigg]\big\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{1}}\big|P^{I,\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\big|\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{2}}\big\rangle\\
&+\bigg(\tilde{\theta}_{i}\,\big\langle\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{1}}\big|P^{I,\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\bigg|\frac{\partial\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{2}}}{\partial\alpha_{I^{\prime}}}\bigg\rangle+\mathrm{c.c.}\bigg)\bigg\}.\end{split}

(9)

The first term accounts for the change of electronic occupation and for the intraband term, and it is present in metals for perturbations with the same periodicity of the underlying lattice
Baroni et al. 2001
. This latter presents no relevant difference compared to the nonmagnetic case
Dal Corso 2007

\chi_{II^{\prime}}|_{\psi}

Dal Corso 2001
;
Dal Corso 2007

\chi_{II^{\prime}}|_{\psi}
as

\begin{split}\chi_{II^{\prime}}|_{\psi}&=\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\sum_{i,j}\Big[\tilde{\theta}_{i}-\tilde{\theta}_{j}\Big]\tilde{\theta}_{ji}\bigg(\big\langle\psi_{j}^{\sigma_{3}}\big|S^{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}\bigg|\frac{\partial\psi_{i}^{\sigma_{4}}}{\partial\alpha_{I^{\prime}}}\bigg\rangle\\

&+\big\langle(\mathcal{T}\psi_{j})^{\sigma_{3}}\big|S^{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}\bigg|\bigg(\mathcal{T}\frac{\partial\psi_{i}}{\partial\alpha_{I^{\prime}}}\bigg)^{\sigma_{4}}\bigg\rangle\\

As discussed in Ref.

\displaystyle P^{I}=\sum_{\bm{q}}^{N_{\bm{q}}}\,\frac{e^{-\iota\bm{R}_{l}\cdot\bm{q}}}{N_{\bm{q}}}\,P^{\eta}(\bm{q})

(16)

\displaystyle P^{\eta,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{N_{\bm{k}}}\sum_{m}S^{\sigma_{1}\sigma}\big|\phi^{\eta\sigma}_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{q}}\big\rangle\big\langle\phi^{\eta\sigma^{\prime}}_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}}\big|S^{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma_{2}}

\begin{split}\phi_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\eta\sigma}(\bm{r})&=\sum_{l}\frac{e^{\iota\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{l}}}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{k}}}}\,\phi^{l\eta\sigma}_{m}(\bm{r})=\frac{e^{\iota\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r}}}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{k}}}}\,v_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{\eta\sigma}(\bm{r})\end{split}

Substituting the expansions Eq. (

\chi_{l\eta,l^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}}|_{\psi}=\sum_{\bm{q}}^{N_{\bm{q}}}\frac{e^{\iota\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{R}_{l}-\bm{R}_{l^{\prime}})}}{N_{\bm{q}}}\,\chi_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\bm{q})|_{\psi}

As routinely done within DFPT

\psi_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{k}}}}\,e^{\iota\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}u_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma}(\bm{r})

;
Timrov et al. 2021
), we rewrite everything in terms of the periodic parts

\begin{split}&\chi_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\bm{q})|_{\psi}=\frac{1}{N_{\bm{k}}}\sum_{n\bm{k}}\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\bigg[\big\langle u_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma_{1}}\big|P^{\eta,\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{q})\big|\Delta_{\bm{q}}u_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma_{2}}\big\rangle\\
+&\big\langle(\mathcal{T}u_{n(-\bm{k})})^{\sigma_{1}}\big|\big(\mathcal{T}P^{\eta}(\bm{q})\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}\big)^{\sigma\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\big|(\mathcal{T}\Delta_{(-\bm{q})}u_{n(-\bm{k})})^{\sigma_{2}}\big\rangle\bigg]\end{split}

where now the sum over

\Delta_{\bm{q}}u_{n\bm{k}}^{\sigma}

and a time-reversed one

\begin{split}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\Big(H^{[-\bm{B}]}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}&-\epsilon_{n(-\bm{k})}\,S_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Big)\big|(\mathcal{T}\Delta_{(-\bm{q})}u_{n(-\bm{k})})^{\sigma^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\\

IV

We have presented a comprehensive

i
) noncollinear magnetism and/or strong spin-orbit coupling requiring a fully-relativistic treatment, and (
ii

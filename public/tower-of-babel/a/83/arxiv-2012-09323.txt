Abstract
. Based on previous results on the classification of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras over dihedral groups and the characterization of simple modules of Drinfeld doubles, we compute the irreducible characters of the Drinfeld doubles of bosonizations of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras over the dihedral groups 𝔻 4 ​ t {\mathbb{D}}_{4t} with t ≥ 3 t\geq 3 . To this end, we develop new techniques that can be applied to Nichols algebras over any Hopf algebra. Namely, we explain how to construct recursively irreducible representations when the Nichols algebra is generated by a decomposable module, and show that the highest-weight of minimum degree in a Verma module determines its socle. We also prove that tensoring a simple module by a rigid simple module gives a semisimple module.



1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to study the representations
of certain families of Hopf algebras
\mathcal{D}(V,\mathbb{D}_{m})
, which are given by
Drinfeld doubles of bosonizations of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras
{\mathfrak{B}}(V)
over

of order

belongs to an infinite family of reducible

The small quantum groups or Frobenius-Lusztig kernels

As it is well-known,

can be described as a quotient
of the Drinfeld double of the quantum group

associated with a standard Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Consequently,

can also be described as a quotient of the Drinfeld double of the small quantum
group
u_{q}({\mathfrak{b}})
. The latter is a pointed Hopf algebra over the abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{n}
with
n=\operatorname{rk}{\mathfrak{g}}
.
As such, it is isomorphic to the smash product or
bosonization

u_{q}({\mathfrak{b}})\simeq u_{q}({\mathfrak{n}})\#{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{n}

and

is a Nichols algebra of diagonal type
[

[

]
,
[

. In fact,

]

all complex finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras over abelian groups are variations of

In these notes, we consider objects analogous to the small quantum groups

but containing
a non-abelian torus. These pointed Hopf algebras are given by
bosonizations of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras over the infinite family of dihedral
groups
\mathbb{D}_{m}
classified in
[
FG
]
. These Nichols algebras actually turn out to be exterior algebras over
semisimple objects in the braided category
{}^{\mathbb{D}_{m}}_{\mathbb{D}_{m}}\mathcal{YD}
. See Section
4
for more details.

More generally, one may consider the Drinfeld double
\mathcal{D}(V,H):=\mathcal{D}({\mathfrak{B}}(V)\#H)
of the
bosonization of a finite-dimensional Nichols algebra

over a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra

These kind of generalized small quantum groups admits a triangular decomposition

[

In case

In conclusion, with the classification of the irreducible representations at hand,

Our main contribution is the solution to this problem for small quantum groups at the dihedral groups

On the way, we develop new techniques and provide results that can be applied to Nichols algebras over any Hopf algebra.

3.1.1.

A left Yetter-Drinfeld module over

\lambda(h\cdot v)=h_{(1)}v_{(-1)}\mathcal{S}(h_{(3)}){\otimes}h_{\left(2\right)}\cdot v_{(0)}\qquad\text{ for all }h\in H,v\in V.

The finite-dimensional left Yetter-Drinfeld modules over

form a braided rigid tensor category denoted by

The braiding is given by

Let

For instance, its comultiplication is determined by setting

The

In case

A fixed linear generator

We refer to

3.2.1.
The character of the simple modules

For any
N\in{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
, the proper standard module
\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(N)
in the category
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{M}
inherits the grading
afforded by
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(N)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{-n_{top}}\big(\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(N)\big)_{k}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\big(\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(N)\big)_{k}={\mathfrak{B}}^{-k}(V){\otimes}N.

It follows from
[
GG
, Proposition 3.5]
that the head of
\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(N)
in
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{M}
is
a graded quotient. Moreover,
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}
commutes with the grading-forgetful functors.
In particular, for any
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
we have a decomposition

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{n}

making it an object in
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{G}
.

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{D}(H)
is concentrated in degree
0
, we can consider each
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
inside
{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{G}

as an object concentrated in degree
0
. Thus,
\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
is in bijection with
\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{G}
via
(\lambda,n)\leftrightarrow\lambda[n]
.

Assume now
H
is semisimple; hence
\mathcal{D}(H)
also is.
Then for each
n
,
\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{n}\simeq\oplus_{\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}}\,\mu^{\oplus t_{\mu,n}}
as
\mathcal{D}(H)
-modules.
We call
the character of
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\lambda)
the graded
\mathcal{D}(H)
-module

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}\big(\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\big):=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq 0\\
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\mu[n]^{\oplus t_{\mu,n}}.

This gives us good information about the simple modules as it is a complete invariant,
since
\operatorname{Res}\big(\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\big)=\lambda\oplus\mbox{weights in degree\,<0}
.

3.2.2.
The action on the Verma modules

By the paragraphs above, one immediately realizes that to describe a simple module
\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)

one has to deal with the submodules of the Verma module
\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
.
For explicit computations, it is convenient to keep in mind the following key facts.

(a)

The action
of
{\mathfrak{B}}(V)
and
\mathcal{D}(H)
on
\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
is given by

\displaystyle z\cdot(v{\otimes}\mathsf{m})=(zv){\otimes}\mathsf{m}\quad\mbox{and}\quad h\cdot(v{\otimes}\mathsf{m})=(h_{\left(1\right)}\cdot v){\otimes}(h_{\left(2\right)}\cdot\mathsf{m})

,

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\simeq{\mathfrak{B}}(V){\otimes}\lambda=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{n_{top}}{\mathfrak{B}}^{n}(V){\otimes}\lambda

is an isomorphism and a decomposition as
\mathcal{D}(H)
-modules, respectively.

(b)

Let

\displaystyle V\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{k}=\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{k-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{V}\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{k}\subseteq\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)_{k+1}.

(c)

To compute the action of

we use the commutation rule (

).

(d)

The action of
\overline{V}
on a
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
-module

is a morphism of
\mathcal{D}(H)
-modules:

\displaystyle h(\alpha\mathsf{m})=(h_{\left(1\right)}\cdot\alpha)(h_{\left(2\right)}\mathsf{m})

for all

3.2.3.
The simple
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
-modules as socles of the Verma modules

We introduce now the functor

\textstyle{{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}}
\textstyle{{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{C},}
\textstyle{\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(N)=\operatorname{soc}(\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(N)),}

i.e.

\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(N)
is the maximal semisimple submodule of

\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(N)
. As in the case of the head, it follows from
[

that the socle of a
standard module is a graded submodule even if

concentrated in degree 0. Also,
\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}

commutes with the grading-forgetful functors and the shift-of-grading functors.

The socles of the Verma modules give us another classification of the simple modules over
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
.
The next result for

[

]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(a)

\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
is a simple lowest-weight module with lowest-weight is
\lambda_{V}\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{C}

(b)

Any

{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
for a unique weight
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

From §
3.2.2

(a)
, we see that the socle of
M_{H}^{V}(\lambda)
in
{}_{\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(V,H)}\mathcal{C}
is
{\mathfrak{B}}^{n_{top}}(V){\otimes}\lambda
and this is simple and isomorphic to
\operatorname{Inf}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}^{\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(V,H)}(\lambda_{V}\lambda)

(a)

For
(b)
, we first consider the category
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{M}
in which the number of non-isomorphic simple modules is
\#\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
by Theorem

. In
(a)
we have found the same number of non-isomorphic simple modules as tensoring by
\lambda_{V}
gives a bijection on
\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
. Therefore any simple module in
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{S}_{H}^{V}(\lambda)
for a unique
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
.

Let now
S\in{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{G}
be a simple object and
F
the grading-forgetful functor. Then
FS\simeq\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
for a unique
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{M}
by the above paragraph and hence
S\simeq\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)[n]\simeq\mathsf{S}^{V}_{H}(\lambda[n])
for some

(b)
for
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{G}

∎

Naturally, the socle of a Verma module is isomorphic to a simple highest-weight module.
We can determine its highest-weight as follows. The next result is very useful to compute the simple modules.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}{}_{\mathcal{D}(H)}\mathcal{C}
be a weight and

\displaystyle n=\min\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\mbox{ there is a highest-weight $\mu$ in }\big(\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\big)_{k}\right\}.

Then
\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
has a unique highest-weight
\mu
in degree
n
and
\mathsf{S}_{H}^{V}(\lambda)\simeq\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mu)[n]

Proof.

As we mentioned, the socle of
\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(\lambda)
is a graded submodule, then

\mathsf{S}_{H}^{V}(\lambda)\simeq\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mu)[k]
for some homogeneous highest-weight

\mu\subset\big(\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\big)_{k}
. Let
\nu\subset\big(\mathsf{M}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)\big)_{j}

be another homogeneous highest-weight.
The submodule generated by
\nu
contains the socle. In particular,
\mu\subset\mathcal{D}(V,H)\nu
.
Also, by the triangular decomposition,
\mathcal{D}(V,H)\nu={\mathfrak{B}}(V)\nu=\nu+\sum_{i>0}{\mathfrak{B}}^{i}(V)\nu
.
Then §
3.2.2

(b)

implies that either

or
j=k
and
\nu=\mu
, and the corollary follows.
∎

3.2.5.
Tensoring by rigids

We observe that any semisimple object in

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathsf{N}=\oplus_{i\leq t}\mathsf{N}_{i}
be an object in
{}_{\mathcal{D}(V,H)}\mathcal{G}
with

.
If
\mathsf{N}_{t}
is a semisimple
\mathcal{D}(H)
-module and generates
\mathsf{N}

as
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
-module, then

\operatorname{top}(\mathsf{N})\simeq\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\mathsf{N}_{t}).

Proof.

We have that
\overline{V}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{t}=0
by the grading assumption on
\mathsf{N}
.
Then there is an epimorphism
p:\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}
.
Let
\mathsf{R}
be the Jacobson radical of
\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})
, that is
\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})/\mathsf{R}\simeq\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})
.
Then

induces a projection
\overline{p}:\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}/p(\mathsf{R})
and hence
\mathsf{N}/p(\mathsf{R})
is semisimple.
Also, the homogeneous component of
\mathsf{R}
of degree

is zero.
Then
\overline{p}_{|\mathsf{N}_{t}}
is injective and therefore
\overline{p}
so is.
This implies
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{top}(\mathsf{N})
.
On the other hand,
\operatorname{top}(\mathsf{N})
is a semisimple quotient of
\mathsf{M}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})
.
Then
\operatorname{top}(\mathsf{N})
is a direct summand of
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\mathsf{N}_{t})
. Thus we obtain the desired isomorphism.

<0
which is not possible by the paragraph above.

In conclusion, the
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
-submodule generated by
\lambda{\otimes}\mu
is semisimple and isomorphic to
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\lambda{\otimes}\mu)
.
We prove next that
\lambda{\otimes}\mu
actually generates the whole module
\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda){\otimes}\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\mu)
and hence

\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda){\otimes}\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\mu)\simeq\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\lambda{\otimes}\mu)
as we wanted. For that, we fix bases
\{m_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and

\{n_{k}\}_{k\in K}
of the weights
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively.
Since
\mu
is rigid, we have that
x\cdot n_{k}=0
for any homogeneous element
x\in{\mathfrak{B}}(V)

of degree
\geq 1
. Also, because
{\mathfrak{B}}(V)\#H
is a graded coalgebra, for such an element

one may write its comultiplication by

\Delta(x)=x{\otimes}1+\sum_{t}y_{t}{\otimes}z_{t}
, where the elements

are homogeneous of degree
\geq 1
for all

Hence, for all

and
k\in K

\displaystyle x\cdot(m_{j}{\otimes}n_{k})=(x\cdot m_{j}){\otimes}n_{k}+\sum_{t}(y_{t}\cdot m_{j}){\otimes}(z_{t}\cdot n_{k})=(x\cdot m_{j}){\otimes}n_{k}.

Since
\mathsf{L}^{V}_{H}(\lambda)
is generated as a
\mathcal{D}(V,H)
-module by the action of
{\mathfrak{B}}(V)
on

\{m_{j}\}_{j\in J}

Lastly, if
\lambda

\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\lambda{\otimes}\mu)
is concentrated in degree

This implies that
\mathsf{L}_{H}^{V}(\nu)=\nu
for every weight
\nu

\lambda{\otimes}\mu

\nu
is rigid.
∎

4.
The dihedral groups framework

From now on, we fix a natural number
m\geq 12

4
and an

m

\omega
. We also set
n=\frac{m}{2}
.

The dihedral group
of order
2m
is presented by generators and relations by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}_{m}=\langle x,y\mid x^{2},\ y^{m},\ xyxy\rangle.

It has
n+3
conjugacy classes:

\mathcal{O}_{e}=\{e\}
with
e
the identity,
\mathcal{O}_{y^{n}}=\{y^{n}\}
,

\mathcal{O}_{x}=\{xy^{j}:\ j\text{ even}\}
,
\mathcal{O}_{xy}=\{xy^{j}:\ j\text{ odd}\}

and
\mathcal{O}_{y^{i}}=\{y^{i},y^{-i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

The algebra of functions
\Bbbk^{{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}
is the dual Hopf algebra of
\Bbbk{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
.
We denote by
\{\delta_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}
the dual basis of the basis of
\Bbbk{\mathbb{D}}_{m}

given by the group-like elements,
i.e.

\delta_{t}(s)=\delta_{t,s}
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{D}_{m}
.
The comultiplication and the counit of these elements are

\Delta(\delta_{t})=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{D}_{m}}\delta_{s}{\otimes}\delta_{s^{-1}t}
and
\varepsilon(\delta_{t})=\delta_{t,e}

for all
t\in{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
, respectively.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
the Drinfeld double of
\Bbbk{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
. Since
\Bbbk^{{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}
is a commutative algebra,

\Bbbk^{{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}=\big(\Bbbk^{{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}\big)^{\operatorname{op}}
and consequently
\Bbbk^{{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}
and
\Bbbk{\mathbb{D}}_{m}

are Hopf subalgebras of
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
. Thus, the algebra structure of
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
is completely determined by
the equality

\displaystyle\delta_{tst^{-1}}\,t=t\,\delta_{s}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for all }s,t\in{\mathbb{D}}_{m}.

In this case, the

R
-matrix reads
R=\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{D}}_{m}}\delta_{t}{\otimes}t\in\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}{\otimes}\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}
.

4.1.

\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}

It is well-known that the simple modules over the Drinfeld double of a group algebra are classified by
the conjugacy classes of the group and irreducible representations of their centralizers, c.f.

and references therein.
Namely, for

4.2.
Some tensor products of weights

The category of
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-modules is semisimple. As such, any tensor product of two weights
can be written as a direct sum of weights. In order to perform our study on simple modules
over doubles of bosonizations of Nichols algebras, which is carried out in
§
5

dealing with Verma modules,
we need to know the direct summands of the following products of simple
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-modules.

In the following two lemmata, we decompose the tensor product of the
simple modules

with

as in §
4.1.4
, §
4.1.5

with some other families of simple modules.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M_{i,k}
be a simple
\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}
-module with

1\leq i\leq n
,
0\leq k\leq m-1
as in Notation
4.1
.
Then

M_{i,k}{\otimes}M_{r,s,t}\simeq M_{r+i,s+1+\delta_{i,n}t,t+k}\oplus M_{r+i,s+\delta_{i,n}t,t+k},

as
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-modules; here we write
r+i
,
s+1+\delta_{i,n}t
and
t+k
for their classes in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
Moreover, the simple submodules inside the tensor product are given by

N_{\pm}=\Bbbk\{y^{a}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}:\ 0\leq a\leq n-1\}
, where
\mathsf{n}_{\pm}=\omega^{rk}\boldsymbol{|-\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|0\rangle}\pm\boldsymbol{|+\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|i\rangle}
, with
\boldsymbol{|n\rangle}=\boldsymbol{|0\rangle}
if
i=n
, and

\displaystyle N_{+}\simeq M_{r+i,s+1+\delta_{i,n}t,t+k}\,\mbox{ and }\,N_{-}\simeq M_{r+i,s+\delta_{i,n}t,t+k}.

Proof.

We prove first the case
i<n
and
r=0
,
i.e.

M_{0,s,t}
is as in §
4.1.4
.
Let us show that the subspaces
N_{\pm}
are simple
\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}
-modules.
The elements
\mathsf{n}_{\pm}
are eigenvectors of
y^{n}
with eingenvalue
\operatorname{sgn}^{t+k}(y^{n})
because

\displaystyle y^{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{|\pm\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|0\rangle})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\operatorname{sgn}^{t}(y^{n})\,\boldsymbol{|\pm\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|0\rangle}

and
\omega^{n}=\omega^{-n}=-1
. Also, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle x\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}=\mp\operatorname{sgn}^{s}(x)y^{i}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}.

(13)

This implies that
N_{\pm}=\Bbbk\{y^{a}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}:\ 0\leq a\leq n-1\}
are
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-submodules of
M_{i,k}{\otimes}M_{r,s,t}
.
Moreover, the elements
\mathsf{n}_{\pm}
are homogeneous of the same degree for

\displaystyle\deg(\boldsymbol{|-\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|0\rangle})

\displaystyle=\deg\boldsymbol{|-\rangle}\deg\boldsymbol{|0\rangle}=y^{-i}x=xy^{i}\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\deg(\boldsymbol{|+\rangle}{\otimes}\boldsymbol{|i\rangle})

\displaystyle=\deg\boldsymbol{|+\rangle}\deg\boldsymbol{|i\rangle}=y^{i}xy^{2i}=xy^{i}.

Hence
\deg y^{a}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}=y^{a}xy^{i}y^{-a}=xy^{i-2a}
for
all
0\leq a\leq n-1
. This implies that in fact

are
\mathcal{D}\mathbb{D}_{m}
-modules with
\dim N_{\pm}=n
.
Moreover, a direct check shows that they are isomorphic
to the simple
\mathcal{D}{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-modules displayed in §
4.1.4
and §
4.1.5
,
depending on the parity of
i
. One way to distinguish these modules is by looking at the
eigenvalues of the action of
y^{n}
and
x

on the homogeneous component of
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-degree
x
if
i=2z
is even, or
the action of
y^{n}
and
xy

on the homogeneous component of
{\mathbb{D}}_{m}
-degree
xy
if

i=2z+1
is odd.
In the case of

, these homogeneous components are spanned by

, respectively.

If

is even, then

x\cdot\big(y^{z}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}\big)=\mp\operatorname{sgn}^{s}(x)\big(y^{z}\cdot\mathsf{n}_{\pm}\big)
by (
13
). Hence
M_{0,s,t+k}\simeq N_{-}
and
M_{0,s+1,t+k}\simeq N_{+}
. If

The strategy to prove the case

Abstract
. We revisit Schmidt’s theorem connecting the Schmidt rank of a tensor with the codimension of a certain variety and adapt the proof to the case of arbitrary characteristic. We also find a sharper result of this kind for homogeneous polynomials, assuming the characteristic does not divide the degree. We then use this to relate the Schmidt rank of a homogeneous polynomial (resp., a collection of homogeneous polynomials of the same degree) with the codimension of the singular locus of the corresponding hypersurface (resp., intersection of hypersurfaces). This gives an effective version of Ananyan-Hochster’s theorem [ 2 , Theorem A] .



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a field (of any characteristic) and

P:V_{1}\times V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}

a polylinear form, where
V_{i}
are finite dimensional vector spaces over
{\mathbf{k}}
. Equivalently, we view
P
as a tensor in
V_{1}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d}^{*}
.

Definition 1.1
.

(i) We say that
P\neq 0
has
Schmidt rank

1
if there exists a partition
[1,d]=I\sqcup J
into two nonempty
parts and polylinear forms
P_{I}(v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{r}})
,
P_{J}(v_{j_{1}},\ldots,v_{j_{s}})
, where

v_{a}\in V_{a}
,
I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{r}\}
,
J=\{j_{1}<\ldots<j_{r}\}
, such that

P=P_{I}\cdot P_{J}
. In general the
Schmidt rank
of
P
, denoted as
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)
,
is the smallest number
r
such that
P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}

with
P_{i}
of Schmidt rank
1
.
For a collection of tensors
\overline{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{s})
we define the
Schmidt rank

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\overline{P})
as
the minimum of Schmidt ranks of nontrivial linear combinations of
(P_{i})
.

(ii) Given a collection of nonempty subsets
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subset[1,d]
and a collection
(P_{I_{1}},\ldots,P_{I_{r}})
,
where
P_{I_{i}}
is a polylinear form on
\prod_{a\in I_{i}}V_{a}
,
we denote by
(P_{I_{1}},\ldots,P_{I_{r}})\subset V_{1}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d}^{*}
and call this the
tensor ideal generated by

P_{I_{1}},\ldots,P_{I_{r}}
, the subspace of polylinear forms of the form

P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{I_{i}}\cdot Q_{J_{i}},

for some polylinear forms
Q_{J_{i}}
on
\prod_{b\in J_{i}}V_{b}
, where
J_{i}=[1,d]\setminus I_{i}
.

The Schmidt rank of a tensor, along with a set of related notions, such as
slice rank
,
G
-rank
,
analytic rank
,
as well as the version of Schmidt rank for homogeneous polynomials also known as
strength
(see below),
has been a subject of study in many
recent works (see
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
and references therein). One of the goals of this paper is to establish a precise relation
(in the case of an algebraically closed base field
{\mathbf{k}}
) between this notion and the codimension
of the singular locus of the corresponding hypersurface, thus giving an effective version of Ananyan-Hochster’s theorem
[
2
, Theorem A]
.

Let us define the subvariety
Z_{P}=Z_{P}^{V_{1}}\subset V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
as the set of
(v_{2},\ldots,v_{d})
such that

P(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{d})=0
for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
.
Following Schmidt, let us set

g(P):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}}Z_{P}.

In
[
10
]
(where the authors consider the case
d=3
), this number is called
geometric rank
of
P
. Using
[
10
, Thm. 3.2]
, one can see that it does not depend on the ordering of
the variables
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
.

It is easy to see that one has

g(P)\leq{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)

(1.1)

(see Lemma
2.1
(i) below, or
[
10
, Thm. 1]
).

Similarly, for a collection
\overline{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{s})
, we define
Z_{\overline{P}}\subset V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}

by the condition on
(v_{2},\ldots,v_{d})
that the corresponding map

V_{1}\to{\mathbf{k}}^{s}:v_{1}\mapsto(P_{i}(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{d}))_{1\leq i\leq s}

has rank
<s
, and we set

g(\overline{P}):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}}Z_{\overline{P}}
.

The proof of the following theorem follows closely the proof of a similar result in the case where
{\mathbf{k}}=\C
and
P
is symmetric,
given in
[
11
]
. We modified the proof so that it would work in arbitrary characteristic and also streamlined some parts of the original argument.
The fact that the original proof can be adapted to arbitrary characteristic was also pointed out in
[
11
, Sec. 4]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(i) Let
g^{\prime}(P)
denote the codimension in
V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
of the Zariski closure of the set of
{\mathbf{k}}
-points in
Z_{P}

(so
g(P)\leq g^{\prime}(P)
and
g(P)=g^{\prime}(P)
if
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed).
Then one has

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)\leq C_{d}\cdot g^{\prime}(P),

where
C_{d}=\max(2+\theta_{d-2},2^{d-2}-1)
, and

\theta_{n}
is the number of ordered collections of disjoint nonempty
subsets
I_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{p}\subsetneq[1,n]
(with
p\geq 1
).
In particular, we have
C_{3}=2
,
C_{4}=4
and
C_{5}=14
.

(ii) Assume
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed. Then
for a collection
\overline{P}=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{s})
, one has

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\overline{P})\leq C_{d}\cdot(g(\overline{P})+s-1).

In the appendix we prove another version of theorem
1.2
with better bounds for
d\geq 6.

Even though Schmidt applied the above result to symmetric tensors
P
corresponding to homogeneous polynomials,
we observe that in the symmetric case it is natural to modify the relevant variety
Z_{P}
, and that this leads to
much better estimates on the rank.

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
on a finite-dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space
V
.
The
Schmidt rank
of
f
, denoted as
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)
, is the minimal number
r
such that
f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}h_{i}
, where
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
are homogeneous polynomials of positive degrees.
Note that if
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)=r
then in terminology of
[
2
]
,
f
has
strength

r-1
.
For a collection
\overline{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
, the Schmidt rank

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\overline{f})
is defined as the minimum of Schmidt ranks of nontrivial linear combinations of
f_{i}
.

Let
H_{f}(x)(\cdot,\cdot)

denote the Hessian form of
f
given by the second derivatives of
f
. It is a symmetric bilinear form on
V

depending polynomially on a point
x\in V
. The symmetric analog of the variety
Z_{P}
is the subvariety

Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{f}\subset V\times V
given as

Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{f}:=\{(v,x)\in V\times V\ |\ v\in\ker H_{f}(x)\}.

Let us set

g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V\times V}(Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{f}).

The symmetric analog of (
1.1
) is the inequality

g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)\leq 4{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)

(1.2)

(see Lemma
2.1
(ii)).

Similarly for a collection
\overline{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
, we define
the subvariety
Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{\overline{f}}\subset V\times V
as the set of
(v,x)
such that the map

V\to{\mathbf{k}}^{s}:v^{\prime}\mapsto(H_{f_{i}}(x)(v^{\prime},v))_{1\leq i\leq s}

has rank
<s
. We denote by
g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(\overline{f})
the codimension of
Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{\overline{f}}
in
V\times V
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(i) Assume that
d\geq 3
and that the characteristic of
{\mathbf{k}}
does not divide
(d-1)d
.
Let
g^{\prime}_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)
denote the codimension in
V\times V
of the Zariski closure of the set of
{\mathbf{k}}
-points in
Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{f}
.
Then one has

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)\leq 2^{d-3}\cdot g^{\prime}_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f).

(ii) With the same assumptions as in (i), assume also that
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed.
Then

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\overline{f})\leq 2^{d-3}\cdot(g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(\overline{f})+s-1).

For
{\mathbf{k}}
algebraically closed, we prove another version of theorem
1.3
in the appendix with better bounds for
d\geq 6.
The invariant
g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)
can be viewed as an invariant measuring singularities of the polar map

x\mapsto(\partial_{i}f(x))_{1\leq i\leq\dim V}
of
f
(see Sec.
3.3
).
We also prove that
g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)
is related to the codimension of the singular locus of the hypersurface
f=0
.
Namely, let us set

c(f):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V}{\operatorname{Sing}}(f=0).

Assuming that
\mathrm{char}({\mathbf{k}})
does not divide
2(d-1)
, we prove that

\begin{array}[]{c}c(f)\leq g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)\leq(d+1)\cdot c(f),\ \text{ for }d\ \text{ even},\\
c(f)\leq g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)\leq d\cdot c(f),\ \text{ for }d\ \text{ odd}\end{array}

(see Proposition
3.1
).

More generally, for a collection
\overline{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
, let us set

c(\overline{f}):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V}{\operatorname{Sing}}(V(\overline{f})),

where
V(\overline{f})\subset V
is the subscheme defined by the ideal
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
.
We also consider the related invariant

c^{\prime}(\overline{f}):={\operatorname{codim}}_{V}S(\overline{f}),

where
S(\overline{f})\subset V
is the locus where the Jacobi matrix of
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s})
has rank
<s
.
It is easy to see that

c^{\prime}(\overline{f})\leq c(\overline{f})\leq c^{\prime}(\overline{f})+s.

Here is our main result concerning the relation between the Schmidt rank and the codimension of the singular locus.
It can be viewed as a more precise version of the corresponding result in
[
9
]
in the case of
an algebraically closed field of sufficiently large (or zero) characteristic, as well as an effective version of a result of Ananyan and Hochster (see
[
2
, Theorem A(a)]
),
playing a central role in their proof of Stillman’s conjecture.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
\mathrm{char}({\mathbf{k}})
does not divide
d
. Let
c_{\mathbf{k}}(f)
be the codimension in
V
of the Zariski closure of the
{\mathbf{k}}
-points of
{\operatorname{Sing}}(f=0).

(i) We have

\frac{c(f)}{2}\leq{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)\leq(d-1)\cdot c_{\mathbf{k}}(f).

(ii) Assume
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed. Then for a collection
\overline{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}),
we have

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\overline{f})\leq(d-1)\cdot(c^{\prime}(\overline{f})+s-1).

Combining Theorem
1.4
(i) with
[
2
, Theorem A(c)]
we get the following result.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed and
\mathrm{char}({\mathbf{k}})
does not divide
d!
.
For
i=2,\ldots,d
, let
W_{i}\subset{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{i}
be a subspace of forms of degree
i
. Set
W=\bigoplus_{i}W_{i}
,
w=\dim W
.
Assume that for some
m\geq 1
, one has

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(W_{i})\geq(i-1)(m+2)+3(w-1),\text{ for }i=3,\ldots,d,

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(W_{2})-1\geq\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil+3(w-1).

Then every sequence of linearly independent homogeneous forms in
W
is regular and the corresponding complete intersection subscheme in
V
satisfies Serre
condition
R_{m}
.

Note that without any assumptions on the characteristic on
{\mathbf{k}}
we are able to estimate in terms of
c(f)

the rank of
H_{f}(x)(u,v)
viewed as a polynomial in
(u,v,x)\in V\times V\times V

(see Remark
3.3
).

For a homogeneous polynomial
f(x)
of degree
d
on
V
and a vector
v\in V
, we denote by

\partial_{v}f(x)
, the derivative of
f
in the direction
v
. Our next result concerns
\partial_{v}f
for generic
v
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq 3
.
Assume that
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed of characteristic not dividing
(d-1)d
.

(i) For generic
v\in V
, one has
{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(\partial_{v}f)\geq 2^{2-d}\cdot{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)
.

(ii) For
s\leq 2^{2-d}\cdot{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)+\frac{1}{2}
(resp.,
s\leq 2^{2-d}\cdot{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)-\frac{1}{2}
), and for generic
v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\in V
, the derivatives
(\partial_{v_{1}}f,\ldots,\partial_{v_{s}}f)
define a (resp., normal)
complete intersection of codimension
s
in
V
.

In the appendix we prove another version of theorem
1.6
with better bounds for
d\geq 6.
In Section
3.4
we will also discuss the relation of the invariant
g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)
with
the polar map of
f
and with the Gauss map of the corresponding projective hypersurface.

2.
Schmidt rank of polylinear forms

2.1.
Elementary observations

First, let us prove (
1.1
) and its symmetric version (
1.2
).
We denote by
{\mathbf{k}}[V]
the space of polynomial functions on a vector space
V
and by
{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{d}\subset{\mathbf{k}}[V]
the subspace
of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i) For
P\in V_{1}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d}^{*}
one has
g(P)\leq{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)
.

(ii) For
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{d}
one has
g_{{\operatorname{sym}}}(f)\leq 4{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(f)
.

Proof.

(i) If
r={\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)
then there exists a decomposition

P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}P_{I_{i}}\cdot Q_{J_{i}}

as in Definition
1.1
. Swapping some
I_{i}
with
J_{i}
if necessary, we can assume that

1\in I_{i}
for all
i
. Then the intersection of
r
hypersurfaces
Q_{J_{i}}=0
in
V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}

is contained in
Z_{P}
and has codimension
\leq r
.

(ii) If we have a decomposition
f=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}h_{i}
then over the subvariety
Y=V(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r},h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})\subset V
the symmetric form
H_{f}(x)
has rank

\leq 2r
: the subspace cut out by
dg_{1}|_{x},\ldots,dg_{r}|_{x},dh_{1}|_{x},\ldots,dh_{r}|_{x}
is contained in its kernel. Since
{\operatorname{codim}}_{V}Y\leq 2r
, the preimage of
Y
in
Z^{{\operatorname{sym}}}_{f}

has codimension
\leq 4r
in
V\times V
.
∎

For a subset of indices
I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\}\subset[1,d]
, let us set

V_{I}:=V_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{i_{s}}.

We have the following simple observation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V^{\prime}_{1}\subset V_{1}
be a subspace of codimension
c
,
and let
(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r})
be a basis of the orthogonal to
V^{\prime}_{1}
in
V^{*}_{1}
.
Suppose we have tensors

P_{I_{s}}\in V_{1}^{*}\otimes V_{I_{s}}^{*},\ \ Q_{J_{t}}\in V_{J_{t}}^{*}

for some subsets
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r},J_{1},\ldots,J_{p}\subset[2,\ldots,d]
, such that

P|_{V^{\prime}_{1}\times V_{2}\times\ldots V_{d}}
belongs to
the tensor ideal

(P_{I_{s}}|_{V^{\prime}_{1}\otimes V_{I_{s}}},Q_{J_{t}}\ |\ s=1,\ldots,r;t=1,\ldots,p).

Then
P
belongs to the tensor ideal

((\ell_{i}\ |\ i=1,\ldots,c),(P_{I_{s}},Q_{J_{t}}\ |\ s=1,\ldots,r;t=1,\ldots,p)).

In particular,

{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P)\leq{\operatorname{rk}}^{S}(P|_{V^{\prime}_{1}\times V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}})+c.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that the tensor ideal
(\ell_{i}\ |\ i=1,\ldots,c)
is exactly the kernel of the
restriction map

(V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d})^{*}\to(V^{\prime}_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d})^{*}.

∎

2.2.
Determinantal construction

Let
f:{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
be a morphism of vector bundles on a scheme
X
.
For every
r\geq 0
, we have a natural morphism

\kappa_{r}:{\bigwedge}^{r}{\mathcal{V}}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes{\bigwedge}^{r+1}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{1}:(\phi_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\phi_{r})\otimes\alpha\mapsto\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{1}}\ldots\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{r}}\alpha,

where for a section
\psi
of
{\mathcal{V}}^{\vee}
we denote by
\iota_{\psi}:\bigwedge^{i}{\mathcal{V}}\to\bigwedge^{i-1}{\mathcal{V}}

the corresponding contraction operator.

Lemma 2.3
.

(i) Assume that
{\bigwedge}^{r+1}f=0
. Then the image of
\kappa_{r}
is contained in
\ker(f)
.

(ii) Assume in addition that
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
are trivial vector bundles and that for some point
x\in X
,
the rank of
f(x):{\mathcal{V}}_{1}|_{x}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{2}|_{x}
is equal to
r
. Let
n={\operatorname{rk}}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
.
Then there exist
n-r
global sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-r}
of
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
such that
f(s_{i})=0
for all
i
, and

s_{1}(x),\ldots,s_{n-r}(x)
is a basis of
\ker f(x)
.

Proof.

(i) This is equivalent to the statement that
\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{r+1}}\kappa_{r}(\alpha)=0
for any local section
\phi_{r+1}

of
{\mathcal{V}}_{2}^{\vee}
. But
\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{1}}\ldots\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{r}}\iota_{f^{\vee}\phi_{r+1}}=0
since

{\bigwedge}^{r+1}f^{\vee}=0
.

(ii) Since
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
are trivial, we can choose splittings
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}={\mathcal{K}}\oplus{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
,

{\mathcal{V}}_{2}={\mathcal{C}}\oplus{\mathcal{W}}_{2}
into trivial subbundles,
such that
{\mathcal{K}}|_{x}=\ker f(x)
,
{\mathcal{W}}_{2}|_{x}=\operatorname{im}f(x)
, and
f(x):{\mathcal{W}}_{1}|_{x}\to{\mathcal{W}}_{2}|_{x}

is an isomorphism. Let us consider the composed map

s:{\bigwedge}^{r}{\mathcal{W}}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes({\bigwedge}^{r}{\mathcal{W}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{K}})\to{\bigwedge}^{r}{\mathcal{V}}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes{\bigwedge}^{r+1}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{r}}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}.

Then
f\circ s=0
and the image of
s(x)
is exactly
\ker f(x)
.
Choosing a trivialization of the target of
s
, we can write
s
as a collection of global sections of
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
, which has the
required properties.
∎

2.3.
Higher derivatives

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space, and let
{\mathbf{k}}[V]
denote the ring of polynomial functions on
V
.

For each
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]
, each
n\geq 1
and
v_{0}\in V
,
we define the homogeneous form of degree
n
on
V
,
f^{(n)}_{v_{0}}(v)
as the

n
th graded component of
f(v+v_{0})\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]
(viewed as a function of
v
, for fixed
v_{0}
)
with respect to the degree grading on
{\mathbf{k}}[V]
, so that we have
(finite) Taylor’s decomposition

f(v+v_{0})=\sum_{n\geq 0}f^{(n)}_{v_{0}}(v).

We refer to
f^{(n)}_{v_{0}}
as the
n
th derivative of
f
at
v_{0}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X\subset V
be an irreducible closed subvariety of codimension
c
,
v_{0}\in X
a smooth
{\mathbf{k}}
-point.
Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{c}
be a set of elements in the ideal
I_{X}
of
X
, with linearly independent differentials at
v_{0}
.
Then for any
f\in I_{X}
and any
n\geq 1
, the form
f^{(n)}_{v_{0}}\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]
belongs to the ideal in
{\mathbf{k}}[V]
generated by

((g_{i})^{(j)}_{v_{0}})_{i=1,\ldots,c;1\leq j\leq n}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
v_{0}=0
. Set
A={\mathbf{k}}[V]
, and let
\hat{A}
denote the completion with respect
to the ideal of the origin (the ring of formal power series). Then the key point is that

I_{X}\cdot\hat{A}
is generated by
g_{1},\ldots,g_{c}
. Indeed, this follows from the fact that
the local homomorphism of local regular
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras
A_{\mathfrak{m}}/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{c})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X,v_{0}}
(where
\mathfrak{m}

is the maximal ideal of
v_{0}
in
A
) induces an
isomorphism on tangent spaces, so it induces an isomorphism of completions. Note that higher derivatives make sense
for elements of
\hat{A}
(as components in
A_{n}={\mathbf{k}}[V]_{n}
), so the assertion follows once we express
any element of
I_{X}
in the form
\sum_{i}g_{i}h_{i}
for some
h_{i}\in\hat{A}
.
∎

We also need to work with certain polylinear forms of mixed derivatives.
Assume that we have a decomposition
V=V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{n}
.
Then we have the induced direct sum decomposition

{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{m}=\bigoplus_{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}=m}{\mathbf{k}}[V_{1}]_{m_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\mathbf{k}}[V_{n}]_{m_{n}}.

Now for
f\in{\mathbf{k}}[V]_{m}
with
m\leq n
, and a subset of indices
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\leq n
,
we denote by
f^{(V_{i_{1}},\ldots,V_{i_{m}})}
the component of
f
in
{\mathbf{k}}[V_{i_{1}}]_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\mathbf{k}}[V_{i_{m}}]_{1}
.
In particular, when we apply this to the
m
th derivative of
f
at
v_{0}
, we get a polylinear form

f^{(V_{i_{1}},\ldots,V_{i_{m}})}_{v_{0}}:=(f^{(m)}_{v_{0}})^{(V_{i_{1}},\ldots,V_{i_{m}})}\in V_{i_{1}}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{i_{m}}^{*},

(2.1)

which we call the
(V_{i_{1}},\ldots,V_{i_{m}})
-mixed derivative of
f
at
v_{0}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

In the situation of Lemma
2.4
, assume in addition that
V=V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{n}

Domination of nonlinear semigroups generated by regular, local Dirichlet forms
Ralph Chill, Burkhard Claus

Abstract
In this article we study perturbations of local, nonlinear Dirichlet forms on arbitrary topological measure spaces. We show that the semigroup of a local Dirichlet form ℰ \mathcal{E} dominates the semigroup generated by another functional ℱ \mathcal{F} if, and only if, ℱ \mathcal{F} is a specific zero order perturbation of ℰ \mathcal{E} . This helps to classify the perturbations that lie between Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions.



1
Introduction

In
[
AW03
]
Arendt and Warma showed the following result: Let
\big(e^{t\Delta_{N}}\big)_{t\geq 0}
and
\big(e^{t\Delta_{D}}\big)_{t\geq 0}
be the heat semigroups generated by the Laplacian with Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions, respectively, on some regular domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
S
be another linear
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by a (bilinear, symmetric) Dirichlet form. If
S
is dominated by
\big(e^{t\Delta_{N}}\big)_{t\geq 0}
and in turn dominates
\big(e^{t\Delta_{D}}\big)_{t\geq 0}
in the sense that

\displaystyle|S_{t}f|\leq e^{t\Delta_{N}}|f|

(1)

\displaystyle|e^{t\Delta_{D}}f|\leq S_{t}|f|

for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and every
t\geq 0
, then there is a measure
\mu
on
\partial\Omega
such that
S
is generated by the closure of the form

\displaystyle a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}uv\;\mathrm{d}\mu

for
u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Omega\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
. The representation of the form
a
shows that
S
is a semigroup generated by the Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions. The converse statement, namely that the heat semigroup generated by the Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions corresponding to any admissible measure
\mu
is always sandwiched in between the semigroups generated by the Laplacian with Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions in the sense of (
1
), is easy to see. Hence, this result fully characterises the sandwiched semigroups. To be precise, let us mention that he original statement in
[
AW03
]
actually assumed that the form generating
S
is local, but Akhlil
[
Akh18
]
shows that locality follows automatically from the positivity of
S
which in turn follows from the fact that
S
dominates
\big(e^{t\Delta_{D}}\big)_{t\geq 0}
. In
[
CW12
]
an analogous theorem characterizing sandwiched semigroups was shown for the
p
-Laplace operator and its associated semigroups. The main aim of this paper is to show a similar result for a large class of nonlinear Dirichlet forms as defined in
[
CG03
,
Cla23
]
. Both the Laplace and the
p
-Laplace operator are examples of such nonlinear Dirichlet forms.

In
[
Cla23
]
, the second author introduced Dirichlet spaces and capacities associated to nonlinear Dirichlet forms
\mathcal{E}
on
{L^{2}(X,m)}
, where
(X,m)
is some topological Hausdorff measure space. We give a short recollection of these results in the next section. In the following part we define the abstract boundary of
X
associated with
\mathcal{E}
. This enables us to investigate boundary conditions even in abstract settings where no topological boundary of
X
is available. We then prove a nonlinear version of the Riesz-Markov theorem, which states that any local monotone functional on the Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
is given by some integral. In the last part we use this representation theorem to show
6.2
, the main result of this article, which shows, that, under additional assumptions, a semigroup
S
is dominated by the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
S
is generated by some specific integral perturbation of
\mathcal{E}
. This yields a corollary in the spirit of
[
AW03
,
CW12
]
.

2
Preliminaries

We cite some definitions and results from
[
Cla23
]
. In the following,
(X,m)
denotes a Hausdorff topological measure space such that
\supp(m)=X
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a convex and lower semicontinuous functional with dense effective domain. We call
\mathcal{E}
a
Dirichlet form
if

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u\wedge v)+\mathcal{E}(u\vee v)\leq\mathcal{E}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)

(2)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\bigg(v+\frac{1}{2}\big((u-v+\alpha)_{+}-(u-v-\alpha)_{-}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathcal{E}\bigg(u-\frac{1}{2}\big((u-v+\alpha)_{+}-(u-v-\alpha)_{-}\big)\bigg)\leq\mathcal{E}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)

(3)

for every
u,v\in{L^{2}(X,m)},\alpha>0
.

We call
\mathcal{E}

symmetric
if
\mathcal{E}(0)=0
and
\mathcal{E}(-u)=\mathcal{E}(u)
for all
u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
.

Recall that every convex, lower semicontinuous functional
\mathcal{E}
generates a semigroup of contractions on
{L^{2}(X,m)}
, see
[
Bre73
, Théorèmes III.3.1, III.3.2]
(more precisely, the negative subgradient of
\mathcal{E}
generates the semigroup), and
\mathcal{E}
is a Dirichlet form if, and only if, the associated semigroup is order preserving and
L^{\infty}
-contractive. In fact,
S
is order preserving if and only if property (
2
) holds
[
Bar96
, Corollaire 2.2]
,
[
CG03
, Theorem 3.8]
, and it is
L^{\infty}
-contractive if and only if property (
3
) holds
[
CG03
, Corollary 3.9]
.

Furthermore, Barthélemy
[
Bar96
]
showed the following result:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be two Dirichlet forms and
T,S
the associated semigroups. Then
S
is dominated by
T
in the sense that

\displaystyle|S_{t}f|\leq T_{t}|f|

for every
f\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
and
t\geq 0
, if, and only if,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}((|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))+\mathcal{E}(|u|\vee v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)

for every
u,v\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
,
v\geq 0
.

As a corollary this yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(|u|)\leq\mathcal{E}(u)

if
\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric Dirichlet form.

Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(u)=\|u\|_{L^{2}(X,m)}^{2}+\mathcal{E}(u)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}=\{u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}\;|\;\exists\lambda>0:\mathcal{E}_{1}(\lambda u)<\infty\}

together with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathfrak{D}}=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0:\mathcal{E}_{1}\Big(\frac{u}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 1\Big\}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a symmetric Dirichlet form. Then
(\mathfrak{D},\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{D}})
is a Banach space and a lattice under the pointwise order such that

\displaystyle\|f\wedge g\|_{\mathfrak{D}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}+\|g\|_{\mathfrak{D}}

and

\displaystyle\|-c\vee f\wedge c\|_{\mathfrak{D}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}

for all
f,g\in\mathfrak{D}
and
c\geq 0
.
If, in addition,
\dom\mathcal{E}=\mathfrak{D}
, then the lattice operations
\wedge,\vee
are continuous and
-c\vee u\wedge c
converges to
u
for
c\rightarrow\infty
and to
0
for
c\rightarrow 0
.

In the following
\mathfrak{D}_{b}=\mathfrak{D}\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)
denotes the space of bounded functions in
\mathfrak{D}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A\subseteq X
. We define the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A}=\{u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}\;|\;u\geq 1\text{ on }U,A\subseteq U,\;U\text{ open}\},

and the
norm-capacity
by

\normcap(A)=\inf\{\|u\|_{\mathfrak{D}}\;|\;u\in\mathcal{L}_{A}\}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A,B\subseteq X
. Then
\normcap(A\cup B)\leq\normcap(A)+\normcap(B)
, and if
A\subseteq B
, then
\normcap(A)\leq\normcap(B)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A_{n},A\subseteq X
such that
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}=A
. Then
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\normcap(A_{n})\geq\normcap(A)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K_{n}\subseteq X
be compact subsets such that
K_{n}\downarrow K
. Then

\displaystyle\normcap(K)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\normcap(K_{n}).

Definition 2.8
.

We call a set
A\subseteq X

polar
, if
\normcap(A)=0
and some property depending on
x\in X
holds
quasi everywhere
(q.e.), if there is a polar set
A
such that the property holds everywhere on
X\setminus A
.
We say
f:X\rightarrow Y
for some topological space

is
quasicontinuous
, if for every
\epsilon>0
there is an open set

such that
\normcap(O)\leq\epsilon
and

is continuous.

Additionally we call

, if there is a representative which is quasicontinuous. Whenever this is the case, we denote this representative again by
f
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
A\subseteq X
be measurable and polar. Then
m(A)=0
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
U\subseteq X
be an open subset and

quasicontinuous. Then

\displaystyle f\geq 0\text{ a.e. on }U\Longleftrightarrow f\geq 0\text{ q.e. on }U.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be two quasicontinuous representatives of some

. Then
f_{1}=f_{2}
quasi everywhere on
X
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
f\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap C(X)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Then
f
is quasicontinuous on
X
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of quasicontinuous functions in
\mathfrak{D}
and

with

in
\mathfrak{D}
. Then

is quasicontinuous and there exists a subsequence which converges pointwise quasi everywhere.

All proofs can be found in
[
Cla23
]
.

3
The abstract boundary

A major point of interest in the study of partial differential equations are boundary conditions. In the next chapters we want to study these conditions in more detail. The most important question in this context is: What is the boundary of our topological space
X
? In general this is not well defined. In this section we construct a compact Hausdorff space
\hat{X}
with a measure
\hat{m}
on it such that
{L^{2}(X,m)}
is isometric to a subspace of
L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})
, such that
\mathcal{E}
can be seen as a Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})
, and such that the Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
associated with
\mathcal{E}
is isometric to
\mathfrak{D}
. Then we define the boundary of
X
as
\partial X:=\hat{X}\setminus X
.

Again we fix a symmetric Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call a closed unital subalgebra
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq C_{b}(X)
a
core
of
\mathcal{E}
if
\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is dense in
\mathfrak{D}
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is a lattice. We call
\hat{\mathcal{A}}

regular
if

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\langle\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}},\{1\}\rangle\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm}=\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm},\{1\}\rangle=\hat{\mathcal{A}}

and we call
\mathcal{E}

regular
, if it possesses a regular core.

We call a subalgebra
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq C_{b}(X)
a
precore
if
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is a lattice and
\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is dense in
\mathfrak{D}
. We call
\hat{\mathcal{A}}

regular
if the closure of
\langle\hat{\mathcal{A}},1\rangle
is regular. All following definitions also work for precores by using the associated core.

The following is a real version of the well known Gelfand-Naimark theorem.

Theorem 3.2

(Arens 1947)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
be a real commutative Banach algebra with unit e such that

\|e\|=1
. Then there is a compact Hausdorff space
\hat{X}
such that
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is isometrically isomorphic to the algebra
C(\hat{X})
, if and only if

\displaystyle\|a\|^{2}\leq\|a^{2}+b^{2}\|\text{ for all }a,b\in\hat{\mathcal{A}},

(4)

and in this case
\hat{X}
is the compact Hausdorff space of all characters on
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
and the isomorphism is given by the Gelfand transform.

See
[
Are47
]
for a proof.

We observe, that every closed unital subalgebra
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq C_{b}(X)
fulfills the property (
4
) from
3.2
, since, for
a,b\in C_{b}(X)
,
(b(x))^{2}\geq 0
for every
x\in X
and hence,

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\infty}^{2}=\|a^{2}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in X}|a^{2}(x)|\leq\sup_{x\in X}|a^{2}(x)+b^{2}(x)|=\|a^{2}+b^{2}\|_{\infty}.

From now on we assume that
\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric Dirichlet form which possesses a core
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. We write
\hat{X}
for the compact Hausdorff space given by
3.2
such that
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=C(\hat{X})
. Additionally, let

\displaystyle\omega:X

\displaystyle\rightarrow\hat{X},

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\delta_{x},

be the canonical embedding and denote by

\displaystyle\Delta:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow C(\hat{X}),

\displaystyle f\mapsto\hat{f},

the Gelfand transform. The map
\omega
is neither injective nor surjective in general, and it is injective if and only if
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
separates the points of
X
. In addition,
\omega(X)
is dense in
\hat{X}

[
GRS64
]
and

\displaystyle\omega^{*}:C(\hat{X})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto f\circ\omega

is the inverse of the Gelfand transform. We define the push forward measure
\hat{m}
on
\hat{X}
by

\displaystyle\hat{m}(A)=\omega^{*}m(A)=m(\omega^{-1}(A))\text{ for }A\in\mathcal{B}(\hat{X}).

Then
\hat{m}
is a Borel measure.
Let
f\in L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})
. By definition of the push forward measure,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\omega^{*}f)^{2}dm=\int_{X}(f\circ\omega)^{2}dm=\int_{\hat{X}}f^{2}d\hat{m}.

Hence, the following lemma holds:

Lemma 3.3
.

The map
\omega^{*}:L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})\rightarrow{L^{2}(X,m)}
is an isometric injection, which preserves the pointwise order.

Furthermore let us define the functional
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
on
L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})
by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{E}}(f)=\omega^{*}\mathcal{E}(f)=\mathcal{E}(f\circ\omega)\text{ for }f\in L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m}).

This is a Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\hat{X},\hat{m})
. We denote the associated Dirichlet space by
(\hat{\mathfrak{D}},{\|\cdot\|}_{\hat{\mathfrak{D}}})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
f\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}

\displaystyle{\|f\|}_{\hat{\mathfrak{D}}}=\|\omega^{*}f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}=\|f\circ\omega\|_{\mathfrak{D}}

and hence
\omega^{*}:\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}
is an isometric injection.

Indeed, even the following is true.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular, symmetric Dirichlet form with core
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. Then the map
\omega^{*}:\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}
is an isometric isomorphism.

Proof.

We have to show surjectivity. We already know, that
\omega^{*}
maps
\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
to
\mathfrak{D}
and
C(\hat{X})
bijectively to
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. Hence, it maps
\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
bijectively to
\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}
and the inverse is given by
\Delta
. Since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is a core,
{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
is dense in
\mathfrak{D}
. Thus
\Delta
is an isometric bijection defined on a dense subset of
\mathfrak{D}
. Let us extend
\Delta
to
\mathfrak{D}
. For an arbitrary
f\in\mathfrak{D}
, there is a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}}
converging to
f
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\omega^{*}(\Delta(f))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\omega^{*}(\Delta(f_{n}))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}=f.

This implies that
\omega^{*}
has a right inverse and thereby
\omega^{*}
is surjective and its inverse is given by
\Delta
.
∎

If the algebra
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
separates the points of
X
, then
\hat{X}
is a compactification of
X
. Clearly, this compactification depends on the algebra
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, and in order to keep the compactification as small as possible it is desirable to keep the algebra as small as possible. We call
\partial X:=\hat{X}\setminus X
the
boundary
of
X
. By
3.5
, every function in the Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
can be uniquely ”extended” to a function in
\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
, that is, to a function which is defined on the compactification
\hat{X}
. Also, the capacity associated with the form
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
is a capacity on subsets of
\hat{X}
which extends the capacity on
X
. Every function
f\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
is quasi-continuous on
\hat{X}
, and in particular it is quasi-continuous on the boundary
\partial X
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
X
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(X)}^{2}&\text{ if }f\in H^{1}(X),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\mathfrak{D}=H^{1}(X)
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=C(\bar{X})
, where
\bar{X}
is the usual closure of
X
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is a regular core of
\mathcal{E}
(here, we need some regularity of the usual boundary; the assumed
C^{1}
-regularity of the boundary is sufficient). Since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
separates points, the map
\omega
is injective, and since
\bar{X}
is already compact, we have
\hat{X}=\bar{X}
and the boundary
\partial X
is the usual boundary in the Euclidean space.

4
Separation of points

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular Dirichlet form,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
a regular core and
A,B\subseteq\hat{X}
two disjoint closed subsets. Then there exists a function
\hat{f}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
such that

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }B,

or

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }B.

Proof.

Let
A,B\subseteq\hat{X}
be two disjoint closed subsets.
Since
\langle\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathcal{A}},\{1\}\rangle
is dense in
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, we know that
\langle\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X}),\{1\}\rangle
is dense in
C(\hat{X})
. On the other hand,
\hat{X}
is a compact Hausdorff space and thus normal. By Urysohn’s Lemma there is a function
\hat{h}\in C(\hat{X})
such that
0\leq\hat{h}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\hat{h}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{h}=0\text{ on }B.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then
\hat{g}=(1+2\epsilon)\hat{h}-\epsilon
obeys

\displaystyle\hat{g}\geq 1+\epsilon\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{g}\leq-\epsilon\;\;\;\text{ on }B,

and
-\epsilon\leq\hat{g}\leq 1+\epsilon

By the uniform density mentioned above, we can find a function
\hat{f}_{1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\hat{f}_{1}-\lambda<1+\frac{3\epsilon}{2}\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle-\frac{3\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\hat{f}_{1}-\lambda<-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\;\;\;\;\;\text{ on }B,

or in other words

\displaystyle 1+\lambda+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\hat{f}_{1}<1+\lambda+\frac{3\epsilon}{2}\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle\lambda-\frac{3\epsilon}{2}

\displaystyle<\hat{f}_{1}<\lambda-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\quad\quad\text{ on }B.

We have to inspect three cases. First, if
\lambda>0
, we set

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\vee\bigg(\hat{f}_{1}-\big((-\lambda)\vee\hat{f}_{1}\wedge\lambda\big)\bigg)\wedge 1.

By
2.3
,
\hat{f}\in\mathfrak{D}\cap C(K)
and additionally it satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }B.

Second, if
0\geq\lambda\geq-1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, we take

\displaystyle\hat{f}=\left(\frac{1}{\min_{x\in A}\hat{f}_{1}(x)}\big(0\vee\hat{f}_{1}\big)\right)\wedge 1,

where the minimum exists and is greater than
0
since
A
is compact and
\hat{f}_{1}
continuous. Then

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }A\;\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }B.

In the third cases
\lambda<-1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, we can use

\displaystyle\hat{f}=\left(-\frac{2}{\epsilon}(\hat{f}_{1}-\hat{f}_{1}\vee\lambda)\right)\wedge 1,

which fulfills

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }A\;\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }B.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular Dirichlet form and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
its regular core.
Then there is at most one point
x_{0}\in\hat{X}
, such that
\hat{f}(x_{0})=0
for all
\hat{f}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
.

Proof.

Let us assume there are two distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\hat{X}
with this property. Since
\hat{X}
is Hausdorff,
\{x_{1}\}
and
\{x_{2}\}
are closed sets, and they are clearly disjoint. Hence, the result follows from the previous theorem.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Let
X
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(X)}^{2}&\text{ if }f\in H^{1}_{0}(X),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\mathfrak{D}=H^{1}_{0}(X)
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\{f\in C(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\;|\;f=\text{constant on }\partial X\}
is a regular core of
\mathcal{E}
. Since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
separates points, the map
\omega
is injective and we can interprete
X
as a subset of
\hat{X}
. The only character of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, which is not in
\omega(X)
, is the point evaluation at the boundary. Therefore, we can write
\hat{X}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu/\partial X
as a quotient of topological spaces.
Thus,
\hat{X}
is the one-point compactification of
X
. The point
x_{0}
from
4.2
corresponds to
\partial X
. Hence, in a certain way we can interpret

as the point, where we enforce Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Inspired by this example, we call the unique point
x_{0}
from
4.2
, if it exists, the
Dirichlet boundary point
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular Dirichlet form and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
a regular core,
u\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}
and
A,B\subseteq\hat{X}
two disjoint closed sets. Then there exists a function
\hat{f}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(\hat{X})
such that

\displaystyle\hat{f}\geq u\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }B.

Proof.

By
4.1
, there is a function
\hat{g}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}\cap C(K)
satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{g}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{g}=0\text{ on }B,

or

\displaystyle\hat{g}=0\text{ on }A\text{ and }

\displaystyle\hat{g}=1\text{ on }B.

In the first case one takes
\hat{f}=\|u\|_{\infty}\hat{g}
and in the second case
\hat{f}=u-u\wedge\|u\|_{\infty}\hat{g}
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular Dirichlet form,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
a regular core and
A,B\subseteq\hat{X}
two disjoint closed sets. If the Dirichlet boundary point
x_{0}
either exists and
x_{0}\notin A
or if
x_{0}
does not exist, then there exists a function
\hat{f}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{f}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{f}=0\text{ on }B.

Proof.

At first let us assume that
x_{0}
exists. Then we can find
\hat{g},\hat{h}\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap C(\hat{X})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\displaystyle\hat{g}=1-\lambda\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{g}=0-\lambda\text{ on }B,

and

\displaystyle\hat{h}=1\text{ on }A\cup B\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{h}=0\text{ on }\{x_{0}\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\hat{g}+\lambda\hat{h}=1\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{g}+\lambda\hat{h}=0\text{ on }B.

The same argument as in the proof of
4.1
implies the claim.

Now, let us assume that
x_{0}
does not exist. Again we find a function
\hat{g}\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap C(\hat{X})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{g}=1+\lambda\text{ on }A\text{ and }\;

\displaystyle\hat{g}=0+\lambda\text{ on }B.

If
\lambda\neq-1
the claim follows from
4.1
. Let us assume
\lambda=-1
.
Since
x_{0}
does not exist, there are, for every point
x\in A
, a continuous function
f_{x}\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap C(\hat{X})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm}
and an open neighbourhood
U_{x}
of
x
such that
f_{x}\geq 1
on
U_{x}
. Since
A
is compact, we can find finitely many
x_{1}\dots x_{n}\in A
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{x_{i}}\supset A.

This yields, that
\hat{h}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{x_{i}}\vee 0)\right)\wedge 1\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathfrak{D}\cap C(\hat{X})\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\inftynorm}
satisfies
0\leq\hat{h}\leq 1
and
\hat{h}=1
on
A
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{g}+\hat{h}=1\text{ on }A\;

\displaystyle\hat{g}+\hat{h}\leq 0\text{ on }B.

Again, an argument like in the proof of
4.1
implies the claim.
∎

5
A nonlinear Riesz-Markov representation theorem on the Dirichlet space

From now on, we fix a regular, symmetric Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
with core
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. We additionally assume that, for every
u\in\mathfrak{D}
,

\displaystyle u\wedge\cdot:\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D},v\mapsto u\wedge v\text{ is continuous},

(A1)

\displaystyle-c\vee u\wedge c\rightarrow u\text{ for }c\rightarrow\infty,

(A2)

\displaystyle-c\vee u\wedge c\rightarrow 0\text{ for }c\rightarrow 0,

(A3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}\textup{ is separable.}

(A4)

Note that the first three assumptions are, for example, satisfied if
D(\mathcal{E})=\mathfrak{D}
.
Again
\hat{X}
denotes the compact space associated with
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
. By the isomorphism between
\mathfrak{D}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
, we interpret
f\in\mathfrak{D}
as a quasicontinuous function on
\hat{X}
. In addition
\hat{X}
is by definition a compact Hausdorff space, and therefore normal. By capacity we mean the capacity on
\hat{X}
. All pointwise statements are meant quasi everywhere, if not specified otherwise.

Definition 5.1
.

We call a functional
\phi:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

local
, if for every
u,v\in D(\phi)
,

\displaystyle|u|\wedge|v|=0\Rightarrow\phi(u+v)=\phi(u)+\phi(v),

and
monotone
, if for every
u,v\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
the inequality
u\leq v
implies

\displaystyle\phi(u)\leq\phi(v).

The set

\displaystyle\supp(\phi)=\hat{X}\setminus\{

\displaystyle x\in\hat{X}\;|\;\textup{ there is a neighborhood }U\textup{ of }x

\displaystyle\textup{ such that for all }u\in D(\phi):\supp(u)\subseteq U\Rightarrow\phi(u)=0\}

is called the
support
of
\phi
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a regular Dirichlet form such that its Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
satisfies the assumptions (A1) to (A4).
Let
\psi:\mathfrak{D}^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be such that
\psi(0)=0
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The function
\psi
is lower semicontinuous, local, monotone and for every
u,v\in\mathfrak{D}

\psi(u\wedge v)+\psi(u\vee v)\leq\psi(u)+\psi(v).

(ii)

There exists a finite, regular Borel measure
\mu
on
\hat{X}
with
supp(\mu)\subseteq supp(\psi)
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to the capacity on
\hat{X}
, that is, every polar set has
\mu
-measure
0
, and a Borel function
B:\hat{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfying

\displaystyle B(.,s)\text{ is measurable }

\displaystyle\text{ for every }s\in\mathbb{R}

(5)

\displaystyle B(x,0)=0

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X}

(6)

\displaystyle B(x,.)\text{ is lower semicontinuous }

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X}

(7)

\displaystyle B(x,.)\text{ is monotone }

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X}

(8)

such that for all
u\in D(\psi)

\displaystyle\psi(u)=\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Proof of
5.2

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

We first show that
\psi
is lower semicontinuous. For this, let
u\in\mathfrak{D}^{+}
, and let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{D}^{+}
converging to
u
in
\mathfrak{D}
. If
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\psi(u_{n})=\infty
, then the inequality
\psi(u)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\psi(u_{n})
is clearly true. So we assume that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\psi(u_{n})
is finite. We can choose a subsequence of
(u_{n})_{n}
, denoted for simplicity again by
(u_{n})_{n}
, such that the limit inferior actually is a limit. By
2.13
, there is a subsequence, denoted for simplicity again by
(u_{n})_{n}
, converging quasi everywhere to
u
, that is, there is a polar set
A\subseteq\hat{X}
such that
u_{n}
converges pointwise everywhere on
\hat{X}\setminus A
. Since
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\normcap
,
\mu(A)=0
. The lower semicontinuity of
B
and Fatou’s lemma yield

\displaystyle\psi(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}\setminus A}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{X}\setminus A}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}B(x,u_{n}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\hat{X}\setminus A}B(x,u_{n}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u_{n}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(u_{n}).

Thus,
\psi(u)
is lower semicontinuous. The functional is local, since the integral is local, and it is monotone, since the integral is monotone and
B
is monotone in the second component. In addition,

\displaystyle\psi(u\wedge v)+\psi(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x)\wedge v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x)\vee v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u\leq v\}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\{u>v\}}B(x,v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\{u\leq v\}}B(x,v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\{u>v\}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\psi(u)+\psi(v)

for every
u,v\in\mathfrak{D}
.
∎

We prove the converse implication via several lemmas. We define a measure for every bounded
u\in D(\psi)
and use the Radon-Nikodym theorem to build the desired integral representation.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be a bounded function and
K\subseteq\hat{X}
be a compact subset. We define

\displaystyle R_{u}(K)=\{f\in\mathfrak{D}\;|\;f\geq u\text{ on an open neighborhood }U\text{ of }K\}.

We define the set function
\mu_{u}:\{K\subseteq\hat{X}\;|\;K\text{ is compact}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K)=\inf_{f\in R_{u}(K)}\psi(f).

We observe, that the infimum is always finite, since
u\in R_{u}(K)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded and
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\hat{X}
be disjoint, compact subsets. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})=\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

Proof.

Since the sets
K_{1},K_{2}
are compact and have empty intersection and
\hat{X}
is normal, there are open sets
O_{1},O_{2}
, with disjoint closure, separating
K_{1},K_{2}
and open sets
U_{1},U_{2}
such that
K_{i}\subseteq U_{i}\subseteq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subseteq O_{1}
. To construct such a
U_{i}
, we take a continuous function
a\in C(\hat{X})
that is
0
on
K_{i}
and
1
on
O_{i}^{c}
and define

\displaystyle U_{i}=a^{-1}\left(\left(-\infty,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

This function
a
exists by Urysohn’s Lemma.

By
4.4
, there are
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}
such that

f_{i}\geq u
on
U_{i}
and
f_{i}=0
on
O_{i}^{c}
. Hence
f_{1}\wedge f_{2}=0
and
f_{i}\in R_{u}(K_{i})
. Now let
f\in R_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})
. Note that
g_{i}=f\wedge f_{i}\in R(K_{i})
and

\displaystyle g_{1}+g_{2}=g_{1}\vee g_{2}\leq f.

By monotonicity and locality,

\displaystyle\psi(f)

\displaystyle\geq\psi(g_{1}+g_{2})

\displaystyle=\psi(g_{1})+\psi(g_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

Since
f
was arbitrary,
\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\geq\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2})
. In order to prove the converse inequality, we choose functions
h_{i}\in R(K_{i})
. Then

\displaystyle\psi(h_{1})+\psi(h_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\psi(h_{1}\wedge h_{2})+\psi(h_{1}\vee h_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\psi(h_{1}\vee h_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2}).

Again, since the
h_{i}
are arbitrary we conclude

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\leq\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

This inequality together with the reversed inequality, which we showed at the beginning of the proof, implies

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})=\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded and
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\hat{X}
be compact subsets such that
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1})\leq\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

Proof.

This follows immediately from the inclusion
R_{u}(K_{2})\subseteq R_{u}(K_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded and
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\hat{X}
be compact subsets. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})+\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cap K_{2})\leq\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}).

Proof.

Let
h_{i}\in R_{u}(K_{i})
. Then

\displaystyle\psi(h_{1})+\psi(h_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\psi(h_{1}\wedge h_{2})+\psi(h_{1}\vee h_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cap K_{2})+\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2}).

Taking the infimum over all
h_{i}\in R(K_{i})
implies the claim
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded and let
\mathcal{M}
be a downward directed family of compact subsets of
\hat{X}
, that is, for every
U,V\in\mathcal{M}
there is a set
W\in\mathcal{M}
with
W\subseteq U,V
. Then, for
K=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{M}}A
,

\displaystyle\inf_{A\in\mathcal{M}}\mu_{u}(A)=\mu_{u}(K).

Proof.

Since, for every
A\in\mathcal{M}
,
K\subseteq A
and, by
5.5
,
\mu_{u}
is monotone,
\inf_{A\in\mathcal{M}}\mu_{u}(A)\geq\mu_{u}(K)
. For the converse inequality, let us choose a function
f\in R_{u}(K)
and an associated open neighborhood
U
of
K
such that
u\leq f
on
U
. Then,

\displaystyle\emptyset=K\cap U^{c}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{M}}A\cap U^{c}.

Note that every
A\in\mathcal{M}
and
U^{c}
are closed sets. Since
\hat{X}
is a compact Hausdorff space,
\hat{X}
has the finite intersection property. That is, if
\mathcal{C}
is a collection of closed sets and
\bigcap\mathcal{C}=\emptyset
, then there are finitely many
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}=\emptyset
. Hence, there are finitely many
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\displaystyle\emptyset=K\cap U^{c}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{M}}A\cap U^{c}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\cap U^{c}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is downward directed and there are only finitely many
A_{i}
, there exists a set
A\in\mathcal{M}
such that
A\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\emptyset=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\cap U^{c}\supseteq A\cap U^{c}.

Therefore
A\subseteq U
. Since
A
is compact,
U
is an open neighborhood of
A
and
f\in R_{u}(A)
. This yields

\displaystyle\inf_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\mu_{u}(A^{\prime})\leq\mu_{u}(A)\leq\psi(f).

Since
f
was arbitrary,

\displaystyle\inf_{A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\mu_{u}(A^{\prime})\leq\mu_{u}(K),

which implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded. The set function
\mu_{u}
can be uniquely extended to a finite regular Borel measure on
\hat{X}
such that
\mu_{u}(\hat{X})=\psi(u)
and
\supp(\mu_{u})\subseteq\supp(\psi)
.

Proof.

By
5.4
,
5.5
and
5.6
,
\mu_{u}
is a monotone, additive function defined on the compact subsets of
\hat{X}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\leq\mu_{u}(K_{1})+\mu_{u}(K_{2}),

for every compact sets
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\hat{X}
. In addition, by
5.7
,
\mu_{u}
is downward
\tau
continuous
[
Kö97
, p. 12]
. Hence, by
[
Kö97
, Theorem 9.1 and 9.6]
,
\mu_{u}
can be uniquely extended to a regular Radon measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra. We denote this extension again by
\mu_{u}
.
Since
\hat{X}
is compact and
u\in R_{u}(\hat{X})
, we have
\mu_{u}(\hat{X})\leq\psi(u)
. On the other hand,
f\in R_{u}(\hat{X})
implies
f\geq u
. The monotonicity of
\psi
yields
\mu_{u}(\hat{X})\geq\psi(u)
. Hence,
\mu_{u}(\hat{X})=\psi(u)
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
u,v\in D(\psi)
be bounded functions such that
u\leq v
. Then
\mu_{u}\leq\mu_{v}
.

Proof.

Since the measures are regular, it is enough to show
\mu_{u}(K)\leq\mu_{v}(K)
for every compact
K\subseteq\hat{X}
. Let
K
be such a compact set,
f_{u}\in R_{u}(K)
and
f_{v}\in R_{v}(K)
. Then
f_{u}\wedge f_{v}\in R_{u}(K)
, and hence

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(K)\leq\psi(f_{u}\wedge f_{v})\leq\psi(f_{v}).

This implies
\mu_{u}(K)\leq\mu_{v}(K)
.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
D(\psi)
converging to
u\in D(\psi)
in
\mathfrak{D}
such that
u_{n}\leq u
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
u
is bounded. Then, for every measurable set
G
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{u_{n}}(G)=\mu_{u}(G).

Proof.

Let G be a measurable set. Since
u_{n}\leq u
, we have
\mu_{u_{n}}(G)\leq\mu_{u}(G)
by
5.9
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\mu_{u}(G)-\mu_{u_{n}}(G)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\mu_{u}(G)-\mu_{u_{n}}(G)+\mu_{u}(G^{c})-\mu_{u_{n}}(G^{c})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\mu_{u}(\hat{X})-\mu_{u_{n}}(\hat{X})\big)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(\psi(u)-\psi(u_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\psi(u)-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\psi(u_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Hence,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{u_{n}}(G)=\mu_{u}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded, and let
A\subseteq\hat{X}
be a polar subset. Then
\mu_{u}(A)=0
.

Proof.

Since the measure
\mu_{u}
is regular we only have to show the claim for compact sets. Let
K\subseteq\hat{X}
be a polar, compact set. By definition, there exists a sequence of open sets
(O_{n})_{n}
such that
\normcap(O_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
and
K\subseteq O_{n}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
O_{n}\subseteq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muO_{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subseteq O_{n-1}
. Since if for some arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
the inclusion
O_{n}\subseteq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muO_{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subseteq O_{n-1}
does not hold, there is a continuous function
f\in C(\hat{X})
such that
f=1
on
K
and
f=0
on
O_{n-1}^{c}
. Then

\displaystyle O_{n}\cap f^{-1}\left(\left(-\infty,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)

satisfies our assumptions. Note that such a continuous function exists, since
\hat{X}
is normal.

Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we find a function
w_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}
such that
w_{n}\geq 1
on
O_{n}
and
\|w_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{D}}\rightarrow 0
.

By
4.4
, there is a sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
in
\mathfrak{D}^{+}
such that
z_{n}\geq u
on
O_{n+1}
and
\supp(z_{n})\subseteq O_{n}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We can find
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\|z_{k}\wedge\|u\|_{\infty}w_{m}\|_{\mathfrak{D}}\leq\frac{1}{k}

for every
m\geq m_{k}
. Let us choose an increasing sequence
(k_{n})_{n}
such that
{k_{n}}\geq m_{k_{n-1}}
and define
U_{n}=O_{k_{n}}
. Then
(f_{n})_{n}
, defined by

f_{n}=u\wedge z_{k_{n}}\wedge\|u\|_{\infty}w_{m_{k_{n}}},

is a sequence converging to
0
in
\mathfrak{D}
and, in addition, satisfies
f_{n}=u
on
U_{n+1}
and
\supp(f_{n})\subseteq U_{n}
.

This implies
(u-f_{n})\wedge f_{n+1}=0
and
f_{n}\in R_{u}(K)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We compute

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(\hat{X})

\displaystyle=\psi(u)

\displaystyle\geq\psi(u-f_{n}+f_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\psi(u-f_{n})+\psi(f_{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{u-f_{n}}(\hat{X})+\mu_{u}(K).

We observe that
u-f_{n}\rightarrow u
and
u-f_{n}\leq u
. Hence, the preceding lemma yields

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(\hat{X})\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{u-f_{n}}(\hat{X})+\mu_{u}(K)=\mu_{u}(\hat{X})+\mu_{u}(K),

or, in other words,
\mu_{u}(K)=0
.
∎

Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two representatives of an element
f\in\mathfrak{D}
. By
Corollary

2.11
,
f_{1}=f_{2}
quasi everywhere. Hence, there is a polar set
A
such that
\{f_{1}=0\}\cup A=\{f_{2}=0\}\cup A
. Thus, for an arbitrary bounded
u\in D(\psi)

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(\{f_{1}=0\})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{u}(\{f_{1}=0\}\cup A)

\displaystyle=\mu_{u}(\{f_{2}=0\}\cup A)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{u}(\{f_{2}=0\})+\mu_{u}(A)

\displaystyle=\mu_{u}(\{f_{2}=0\}),

since
\mu_{u}(A)=0
by
5.11
. The converse inequality
\mu_{u}(\{f_{1}=0\})\geq\mu_{u}(\{f_{2}=0\})
follows by symmetry. Therefore
\mu_{u}(\{f_{1}=0\})=\mu_{u}(\{f_{2}=0\})
and
\mu_{u}(\{f=0\})
is well defined, since it is independent of the choice of the representative.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
u\in D(\psi)
be bounded. Then

Since

and

\displaystyle g_{n}=(u-\frac{1}{k})^{+}\wedge\|(u-\frac{1}{k})^{+}\|_{\infty}f^{k}_{n}.

Since

\displaystyle=\|u-(u-\frac{1}{k})^{+}+(u-\frac{1}{k})^{+}+u_{k}\|_{\mathfrak{D}}

.

. Therefore we can find a function

Inner regularity implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Let

\displaystyle\geq\psi(h\wedge f)

6
Domination of local sub-Markovian semigroups

Definition 6.1
.

We call a function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty]

bi-monotone
if

f
is monotone decreasing on
(-\infty,0)
and monotone increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a quasilinear, symmetric, local, regular Dirichlet form with core
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
, fulfilling the assumptions (A1) to (A4) and, for every
u,v\in D(\mathcal{E})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u\wedge v)+\mathcal{E}(u\vee v)=\mathcal{E}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v).

Let
T
be the Markovian semigroup generated by
\mathcal{E}
and
S
a semigroup generated by a convex, lower semicontinuous functional
\mathcal{F}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is local,
S
is order preserving and
S
is dominated by
T
in the sense that

\displaystyle|S_{t}(u)|\leq T_{t}(|u|)\text{ for every }t\geq 0\text{ and every }u\in{L^{2}(X,m)},

if and only if there exists a finite regular Borel measure
\mu
on
\hat{X}
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to
\normcap
on
\hat{X}
, and a Borel function
B:\hat{X}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfying

\displaystyle B(.,s)\text{ is measurable }

\displaystyle\text{ for every }s\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle B(x,0)=0

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X},

\displaystyle B(x,.)\text{ is lower semicontinuous}

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X},

\displaystyle B(x,.)\text{ is bi-monotone }

\displaystyle\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\hat{X},

such that for all
u\in D(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\mathcal{E}(u)+\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Remark 6.3
.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is quasilinear,
2.3
implies (A1), (A2) and (A3).

Proof.

First, let us assume that
\mathcal{F}
is local,
S
order preserving and
S
is dominated by
T
. By
2.2
, domination of
S
by
T
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}((|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))+\mathcal{E}(|u|\vee v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)\quad(u,\,v\in{L^{2}(X,m)}).

Let
f\in D(\mathcal{F})
. Choosing
v=0
and
u=f^{+}
in the previous inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f^{+})\leq\mathcal{F}(f^{+}).

Choosing
v=0
and
u=f^{-}
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f^{-})\leq\mathcal{F}(f^{-}).

Hence,
f^{+},f^{-}\in\mathfrak{D}
and therefore
f\in\mathfrak{D}
, the Dirichlet space associated with
\mathcal{E}
. Thus

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathfrak{D}.

Hence, the functional
\psi:\mathfrak{D}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
given by

\displaystyle\psi(u)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{E}(u)&\text{ if }u\in D(\mathcal{F}),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

is well defined. As a difference of local functionals, it is local. Let
u,v\in D(\mathcal{F})
. Then

\displaystyle\psi(u\wedge v)+\psi(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(u\wedge v)-\mathcal{E}(u\wedge v)+\mathcal{F}(u\vee v)-\mathcal{E}(u\vee v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(u\wedge v)+\mathcal{F}(u\vee v)-\mathcal{E}(u)-\mathcal{E}(v)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{E}(u)-\mathcal{E}(v)

\displaystyle=\psi(u)+\psi(v).

For either
u
or
v
not in
D(\mathcal{F})
the previous inequality is trivial. Hence,

\displaystyle\psi(u\wedge v)+\psi(u\vee v)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(u)+\psi(v)

for every
u,v\in\mathfrak{D}
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is quasilinear and therefore continuous on
\mathfrak{D}
,
\psi
is lower semicontinuous. By
2.2
, the domination of the semigroups implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}((|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))+\mathcal{E}(|u|\vee v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)

for every
u,v\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
such that
v\geq 0
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{E}(v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)-\mathcal{E}(u)

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{D}
, such that
0\leq v\leq u
. On the other hand, let
f,g\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
such that
f\leq g\leq 0
. Setting
u=f
and
v=-g
in the inequality from
2.2
leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(-(-g))+\mathcal{E}(-f)\leq\mathcal{F}(f)+\mathcal{E}(-g).

Since
\mathcal{E}
is symmetric, that is
\mathcal{E}(h)=\mathcal{E}(-h)
for every
h\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)-\mathcal{E}(g)\leq\mathcal{F}(f)-\mathcal{E}(f).

Hence
\psi
is monotone increasing on the cone of positive functions and monotone decreasing on the cone of negative functions.

Therefore, the functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:\mathfrak{D}^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(u)=\psi(u)\text{ and }\psi_{2}(u)=\psi(-u)\quad u\in\mathfrak{D},

satisfy all the assumptions of
5.2
. Hence, by
5.2
and
Remark

5.16
, there is a measure
\mu
on
\hat{X}
and functions
B_{1},B_{2}:\hat{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfying the conditions (
5
)-(
8
) from
5.2
, such that the integral representation

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(u)=\int_{\hat{X}}B_{i}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu,\;i=1,2

holds for all positive
u\in\mathfrak{D}^{+}
. We define
B:\hat{X}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle B(x,s)=\begin{cases}B_{1}(x,s)&\text{ if }s\geq 0\\
B_{2}(x,-s)&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Then

is measurable in the first argument and bi-monotone and lower semicontinuous in the second argument and, in addition,

for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\hat{X}
. For a fixed

, this yields

\displaystyle\psi(u)

\displaystyle=\psi(u^{+}-u^{-})=\psi(u^{+})+\psi(-u^{-})=\psi_{1}(u^{+})+\psi_{2}(u^{-})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}}B_{1}(x,u^{+}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\hat{X}}B_{2}(x,u^{-}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\mathcal{E}(u)+\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu,

which is the claim.

Now let us assume we have a measure
\mu
and a function
B
as in the theorem. It is easy to check, that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{E}(f)+\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,f(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

is local and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u\wedge v)+\mathcal{F}(u\vee v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{F}(v)

for every

in
\mathfrak{D}
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}

S
is order preserving. Now, by
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}((|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))+\mathcal{E}(|u|\vee v)\leq\mathcal{E}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,(|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|u|\leq v\}}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\{|u|>v\}}B(x,v\textup{sgn}(u))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|u|\leq v\}}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\{|u|>v\}}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{X}}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Both inequalities together imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}((|u|\wedge v)\textup{sgn}(u))+\mathcal{E}(|u|\vee v)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\mathcal{E}(v).

Hence, by

,

is dominated by

.
∎

2.2
, the two sided domination we assumed yields

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{X}}B_{\mathcal{G}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\int_{\hat{X}}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

Note that we now need both domination assumptions, in contrast to the previous theorem, since
\mathcal{F}
is not necessarily symmetric and
-S_{t}(-\cdot)
is the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{F}(-\cdot)
, see
[
CW19
]
for a similar discussion.

Now let us assume there is a set
A
as in the statement and
U=A^{c}
. Thus,
\mathcal{E}(f)=\mathcal{G}(f)=\mathcal{F}(f)
for every
f\in\mathfrak{D}_{A}
. Let us assume, that there is a function
u\in D(\mathcal{G})
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)>\mathcal{E}(u)+\int_{A}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu,

that is

\displaystyle\int_{U}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu>0.

Since
\mu
is regular, the net
(1_{K})_{K\subseteq U\atop K\text{ compact}}
converges pointwise
\mu
-almost everywhere to
1_{U}
. Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem, there is a compact set
K\subseteq U
such that

\displaystyle\int_{K}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu>0.

By the separation theorems, there is a function
v
such that
v=0
on A and
v=u
on
K
. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{K}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu=0,

since
v\in\mathfrak{D}_{A}
. But

\displaystyle 0=\int_{K}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,v(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{K}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

This is a contradiction. Thus, no such
u
exists and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{E}(f)+\int_{A}B_{\mathcal{F}}(x,f(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu

for every
f\in\mathfrak{D}
.
∎

7
Applications

Let us illustrate
6.2
and
Corollary

6.4
with the help of some examples. At first we consider the
p(x)
-Laplacian.

Example 7.1
.

Let
X
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{E}^{p(x)}:{L^{2}(X,m)}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
the energy of the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Neumann boundary conditions given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{p(x)}_{N}(f)=\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla f(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\;\mathrm{d}x

for
f\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
. We assume
p:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function, such that
1<\inf p\leq\sup p<\infty
and, in addition, that
1/p
is log-Hölder continuous. Then
[
DHHR11
, Theorem 9.1.7]
states that
C^{\infty}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is densely embedded into
\mathfrak{D}
. Hence,
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=C(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is a regular core of
\mathcal{E}^{p(x)}_{N}
. Therefore,
\hat{X}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. The assumptions (A1)-(A3) are fulfilled, since
\mathcal{E}^{p(x)}_{N}
is quasilinear and
[
DHHR11
, Theorem 8.1.6]
implies that
\mathfrak{D}
is separable, which is the assumption (A4). Let
T_{N}
be the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{E}^{p(x)}_{N}
and
S
another order preserving semigroup generated by a convex, local, lower semicontinuous functional
\mathcal{F}
such that for every
u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
,

\displaystyle|S(t)(u)|\leq T_{N}(t)(|u|).

Then
6.2
implies, that there is a positive measure
\mu
on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and a function
B:\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfying the properties stated in
6.2
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu.

For example the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{D}^{p(x)}(u)=\begin{cases}\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\;\mathrm{d}x&\text{ if }u\in\mathfrak{D}_{0}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muC^{\infty}_{C}(X)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

generates such a semigroup. In fact we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{D}^{p(x)}(u)=\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}B(x,u(x))\;\mathrm{d}\mu,

where
\mu
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and

\displaystyle B(x,s)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }x\in X\\
0&\text{ if }x\in\partial X,s=0\\
\infty&\text{ else. }\end{cases}

Let us denote by
T_{D}
the semigroup generated by the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Corollary

6.4
implies, that if a local and order preserving semigroup
S
generated by a convex and lower semicontinuous functional
\mathcal{F}
is sandwiched between
T_{N}
and
T_{D}
, that is

\displaystyle|T_{D}(t)(u)|\leq S(t)(|u|),

\displaystyle|T_{D}(t)(u)|\leq-S(t)(-|u|),

\displaystyle|S(t)(u)|\leq T_{N}(t)(|u|),

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{X}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial X}B_{S}(x,u(x))d\nu,

where
\nu
is a measure on
\partial X
and
B_{S}
fulfills the properties stated in
6.2
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
is the energy of a realization of the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions. In a certain way, this implies that all
local
semigroups sandwiched between the semigroup generated by the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Neumann boundary conditions and the semigroup generated by the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions are generated by the
p(x)
-Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions. This is exactly the statement proved in
[
AW03
]
for the Laplace operator and in
[
CW12
]
for the
p
-Laplace operator. In the recent article
[
Akh18
]
(see also
[
ACD24
]
) it was shown that at least for the case of the Laplace operator, the assumption that the sandwiched semigroup is local can be dropped; the locality is automatic and follows from positivity. It is not clear whether the assumption of locality can also be dropped in the nonlinear situation.

In general it is not possible to define a meaningful version with Dirichlet boundary conditions for an arbitrary Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
. Both functionals defined below are examples of this.

Example 7.2
.

Let
X
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}u^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

Obviously
\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric Dirichlet form, which satisfies all requirements of
6.2
and generates the semigroup

\displaystyle T_{t}(u)=e^{-t}u.

In addition
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=C(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is a core of
\mathfrak{D}
. But since
\normcap
is just the Lebesgue measure,
\partial X
is polar.
6.2
then implies, that every order preserving semigroup
S
generated by a local functional
\mathcal{F}
, which is pointwise exponentially decaying, that is

\displaystyle|S_{t}(u)|\leq e^{-t}|u|

for every
t\geq 0
and
u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
, is generated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}u^{2}dm+\int_{X}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu,

where
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and
B
some function satisfying the properties stated in
6.2
.

Even further, let us assume that, for an order preserving semigroup
R
which is generated by a local, lower semicontinuous, convex functional
\mathcal{G}
,

\displaystyle|R_{t}(u)|\leq|u|

holds for every
t\geq 0
and
u\in{L^{2}(X,m)}
. Since the semigroup
T_{t}=id
is generated by the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}=0
,
6.2
implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u)=\int_{X}B(x,u)\;\mathrm{d}\mu,

where, as before,
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and
B
is as in
6.2
. Hence, semigroups generated by local functionals, which depend on derivatives, can not be pointwise decreasing.

The examples we examined all lived on subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but this is not necessary. In fact the theorems hold for manifolds and arbitrary metric measure spaces. The main problem is to identify the space
\hat{X}
.

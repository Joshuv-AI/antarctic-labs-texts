1.
Introduction

In this work we are interested in the initial value problem for the k-generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation (k-gKdV)

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u+\partial_{x}(u^{k+1})=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

which was originally proposed to model the unidirectional propagation of nonlinear dispersive water waves (
[
20
]
), but has also been considered in connection with other physical systems, such as elastic rods (
[
26
]
), gravity waves (
[
33
]
) and plasma physics (
[
2
]
).

Next, we briefly review well-posedness results
(i.e. existence, uniqueness and stability of solutions) obtained for the k-gKdV equation with initial data in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, with emphasis on the minimal regularity
s\in\mathbb{R}
possible.

When
k=1
, (
1
) is simply referred as the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV).
Z. Guo
[
14
]
and N. Kishimoto
[
19
]
showed independently that the KdV is locally and globally (in time) well-possed for initial data in
H^{-3/4}(\mathbb{R})
.

For
k=2
, (
1
) is known as the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation (mKdV).
Its local well-posedness, provided that
s\geq 1/4
, was shown by C. Kenig, G. Ponce and L. Vega
[
18
]
and extended globally by J. Colliander, M. Keel, G. Staffilani, H. Takaoka and T. Tao
[
9
]
(see also
[
14
,
19
]
for the endpoint
s=1/4
).

If
k=3
,
the best results for
s\geq-1/6
are due to
A. Grünrock
[
13
]
and T. Tao
[
32
]
.

Finally, for
k\geq 4
and
s>(k-4)/(2k)
the local well-posedness was established in
[
18
]
. However, even for
k=4
, blow up in finite time has been shown by Y. Martel and F. Merle
[
21
]
.

Building upon the seminal work of T. Kato
[
15
]
, the solvability of various dispersive nonlinear equations has been explored within the framework of weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
, aiming to achieve enhanced control over the decay at infinity of solutions (as evidenced in studies such as
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
25
]

and their associated references).
Notably, for equations like the modified Korteweg-de Vries (mKdV) and the generalized Korteweg-de Vries (k-gKdV) with
k\geq 4
, optimal results within this context were established by J. Nahas
[
24
]
. Moreover, A. Muñoz-García
[
23
]
established the local well-posedness of the Korteweg-de Vries equation for
s>3/4
in weighted Sobolev spaces. In the same spirit, our objective is to extend these investigations to encompass the quartic generalized Korteweg-de Vries equation (in the case of
k=3
), as elaborated upon in the subsequent sections.

As is standard, we are going to apply the contraction mapping principle to the integral equation version of the IVP (
1
) with
k=3
, i.e.

(2)

u=e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}\partial_{x}(u^{4})(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

where
e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}
is the Airy semigroup introduced in Subsection
2.3
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

u_{0}\in H^{1/12+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)

and

r\in(0,R)
, for some

0<\varepsilon<1/4

and certain
0<R<1/24+\varepsilon/2
.
Then, there exist
T>0
and a unique
solution
u
of the integral equation (
2
) such that

u(\cdot,t)\in H^{1/12+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx),\quad t\in(0,T].

In light of the Sobolev optimal results mentioned earlier for
k=3
, it is natural to expect an improvement of Theorem
1.1
on the Sobolev regularity
s
, at least for some
0<s<1/12
. Similar observation applies in the situation
k=1
, if one compares
[
14
,
19
]
with
[
23
]
.
We believe that a treatment of the factor
|x|^{r}u

(see Section
4.8
below) in the realm of Bourgain-type spaces might be advantageous, as has been demonstrated in the low regularity unweighted cases
(
[
4
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
19
,
32
]
among others).
We are currently investigating this approach.

2.
Definitions and Preliminaries

In this article we write
A\lesssim B
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
. Moreover,
A\sim B
represents
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

2.1.
Function spaces

For a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, consider its Fourier transform

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}

and its inverse Fourier transform by

f^{\vee}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}f(\xi)\,d\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

In this work we use the inhomogeneous Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
, defined via the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\Big)^{1/2},

which satisfy the inclusion
H^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\leq s^{\prime}
, that is,

(3)

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}.

In order to measure the regularity of functions defined in the space–time domain
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
we introduce the mixed–norm Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}
or
L^{q}_{T}L^{p}_{x}
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, given respectively by the norms

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}:=\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(x,t)|^{q}\,dt\Big)^{p/q}\,dx\Big\}^{1/p}

and

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{p}_{x}}:=\Big\{\int^{T}_{0}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{q/p}\,dt\Big\}^{1/q}

with the standard modifications involving the essential supremum when
p
or
q
are equal to infinity.

2.2.
Fractional derivatives

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the fractional derivative
D_{x}^{\alpha}
as the Fourier multiplier given by

(D_{x}^{\alpha}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=|\xi|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

Analogously, we introduce the operator
(1+D_{x}^{2})^{\alpha}
via

\big((1+D_{x}^{2})^{\alpha}f\big)^{\wedge}(\xi):=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

Hence, the Plancherel identity allows us to write

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\sim\|(1+D_{x}^{2})^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|D_{x}^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Also, if we invoke the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
determined by

(\mathcal{H}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=-i\sgn(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

we can relate
D_{x}
with the standard derivative
\partial_{x}
as
D_{x}=\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}

or
\partial_{x}=\mathcal{H}D_{x}
.

A highly useful property in Section
4
will be the following fractional Leibniz rule-type inequality (see
[
18
, Theorem A.8]
)

(4)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}(fg)-fD_{x}^{\alpha}g-gD_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{T}}\|D_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{T}},

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,\alpha]
verify
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}

and the exponents
p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2}\in(1,\infty)

are given by

(5)

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}.

Moreover, for
\alpha_{1}=0
the value
q_{1}=\infty
is allowed.

We also recall the chain rule for fractional derivatives (
[
18
, Theorem A.6]
)

(6)

\|D_{x}^{\alpha}F(f)\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}(f)\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{T}}\|D_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{T}}

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{2}\in(1,\infty)

and
q_{1}\in(1,\infty]

satisfying (
5
).

Sometimes it is more convenient to use this other definition of fractional derivative,

\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{x}f(x):=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+2\alpha}}\,dy\Big)^{1/2},\quad\alpha\in(0,1),

which is related to
D_{x}^{\alpha}
in the following sense (see for example
[
25
, eq. (1.23)]
)

(7)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|D^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L^{p}}\sim\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L^{p}},

and also satisfies (
[
25
, eq. (2.2)]
)

(8)

\|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{x}(fg)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{x}g\|_{L^{p}}+\|g\mathscr{D}^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L^{p}}.

2.3.
Airy semigroup

The solution of the Airy equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}\right.

can be written as
u(x,t)=e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}(x)
, where
e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}
denotes the Fourier multiplier given by

(e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0})^{\wedge}(\xi):=e^{it\xi^{3}}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi).

The Plancherel theorem easily provides

(9)

\|e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\sim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Next, we recall additional boundedness properties of the family of operators
\{e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}\}_{t>0}
, which will be very helpful in proving our main result.
In
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
it was established that

(10)

\|\partial_{x}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

In addition, for any

\theta\in[0,1]
,

\alpha\in[0,1/2]
,

p=2/(1-\theta)

and

q=6/(\theta\alpha+\theta)

it was shown in
[
16
, Lemma 2.4]
that

(11)

\|D^{\theta\alpha/2}_{x}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}_{T}L_{x}^{p}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

and

\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}D^{\theta\alpha}_{x}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{p}_{x}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}}_{x}},

provided

1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1=1/q+1/q^{\prime}
.

We continue with some other crucial estimates involving integrals of the semigroup. In

[
18
, Theorem 3.5(ii)]
it was proved that

(12)

\Big\|\partial_{x}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{T}}

and

(13)

\Big\|\partial_{x}^{2}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{T}},

and
in
[
12
, eq. (2.13)]

(14)

\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{T}L^{p_{1}}_{x}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{2}}_{T}L^{p^{\prime}_{2}}_{x}},

provided

1/p_{2}+1/p^{\prime}_{2}=1=1/q_{2}+1/q^{\prime}_{2}

and

\frac{1}{q_{j}}=\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{3p_{j}},\quad 2\leq p_{j}\leq\infty,\quad j=1,2.

Next, we deduce other related estimates that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
0<\varepsilon<1/4
, one has that

(15)

\|e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{120/(26+51\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{30/(2-3\varepsilon)}}\lesssim\|D^{1/12+\varepsilon}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

and

(16)

\|e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{60/(13-12\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{15/(1-4\varepsilon)}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1/12+\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

It follows from
Stein’s theorem of analytic interpolation
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1, p. 313]
) to the family of operators

T^{(1)}_{z}:=D_{x}^{-\frac{2z}{3}-\frac{1}{12}}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}

for (
15
) and

T^{(2)}_{z}:=(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{z}{8}-\frac{1}{24}}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}

for (
16
),
where
z\in\mathbb{C}
, with
0\leq\textrm{Re}\,z\leq 1
.
More precisely, for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, observe from

[
18
, Lemma 3.26]
that

\|T^{(1)}_{i\gamma}u_{0}\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}=\|D^{-1/12}_{x}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}D_{x}^{-2i\gamma/3}u_{0}\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{-2i\gamma/3}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, we obtain from
[
18
, eq. (3.39.a)]
that

\|T^{(1)}_{1+i\gamma}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}=\|D^{-3/4}_{x}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}D_{x}^{-2i\gamma/3}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly,

[
18
, Lemma 3.26]
implies that

\|T^{(2)}_{i\gamma}u_{0}\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}=\|(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{1}{24}}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{i\gamma}{8}}u_{0}\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

and

[
29
, Theorem A.]
gives

\|T^{(2)}_{1+i\gamma}u_{0}\|_{L^{6}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}=\|(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{1}{6}}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{i\gamma}{8}}u_{0}\|_{L^{6}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we have for any
0<\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<1
and
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
, satisfying

\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{\frac{60}{13}}+\frac{\theta_{1}}{2},\quad\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1-\theta_{1}}{15}+\frac{\theta_{1}}{\infty}

and

\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{\frac{60}{13}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{6},\quad\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1-\theta_{2}}{15}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{\infty},

that

\|T^{(1)}_{\theta_{1}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

and

\|T^{(2)}_{\theta_{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, (
15
) and (
16
) are derived by choosing
\theta_{1}=3\varepsilon/2
and
\theta_{2}=4\varepsilon
, respectively.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
. Then, there hold that

\displaystyle\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{120/(26+51\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{30/(2-3\varepsilon)}}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|D^{1/12+\varepsilon}_{x}f\|_{L_{x}^{10/(7-3\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{30/(23-12\varepsilon)}}

and

\displaystyle\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{60/(13-12\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{15/(1-4\varepsilon)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim T^{\delta}\big(\|f\|_{L_{x}^{10/(7-8\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{30/(23-32\varepsilon)}}+\|D_{x}^{1/12+\varepsilon}f\|_{L_{x}^{10/(7-8\varepsilon)}L_{T}^{30/(23-32\varepsilon)}}\big)

for some

.

We define now

T^{(1)}_{z}f(x,t):=D_{x}^{-\frac{2z}{3}-\frac{1}{12}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}

We can proceed similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.1
using
(see
[
18
, eq. (3.55) and (3.51)]
)

\|T^{(1)}_{i\gamma}f\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}=\Big\|D^{-1/12}_{x}\int^{t}_{0}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}D_{x}^{-2i\gamma/3}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}\lesssim T^{1/6}\|f\|_{L^{10/7}_{x}L^{30/23}_{T}},

\|T^{(1)}_{1+i\gamma}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}=\Big\|D^{-3/4}_{x}\int^{t}_{0}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}D_{x}^{-2i\gamma/3}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}\lesssim T^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{2}_{T}}

)

\displaystyle\|T^{(2)}_{i\gamma}f\|_{L^{60/13}_{x}L^{15}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{1/6}\|f\|_{L^{10/7}_{x}L^{30/23}_{T}},

\displaystyle=\Big\|(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{1}{6}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial^{3}_{x}}(1+D^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{i\gamma}{8}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{6}_{x}L^{\infty}_{T}}

2.4.
Fonseca-Linares-Ponce pointwise formula

In this section, we present a pointwise formula derived in
[
10
]
that enables the commutation of fractional powers
|x|^{r}
and the Airy semigroup
e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}
with the necessary adjustments. Specifically, for
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
, where
0<s<2
and
0<r\leq s/2
, the following identity holds:

(17)

|x|^{r}e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}u_{0}(x)=e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}(|x|^{r}u_{0})(x)+e^{-t\partial^{3}_{x}}\{\Psi_{t,r}(\widehat{u}_{0})(\xi)\}^{\vee}(x)

for all
t>0
and almost any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the
L^{2}
–norm of the last term above can be controlled as follows:

(18)

\|\{\Psi_{t,r}(\widehat{u}_{0})(\xi)\}^{\vee}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{2r}_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\big).

It is important to note that in
[
10
]
, they specifically considered the case of
s=2\alpha
and
r=\alpha
for
0<\alpha<1
. However, a careful analysis of their proof reveals that this result can be extended to the slightly more general situation described here.

3.
Main estimates

In this section, we gather several interpolation formulas that will prove useful in the subsequent discussions.

3.1.
Interpolation formulas

We commence by revisiting a well-known estimate, the proof of which we include here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

p,p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},q,q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in[1,\infty]
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,1]
such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{p_{2}}+\frac{1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{p_{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{q_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{q_{2}}+\frac{1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{q_{3}},

that is
(1/p,1/q)
is a point in the convex hull generated by
(1/p_{1},1/q_{1})
,
(1/p_{2},1/q_{2})
and
(1/p_{3},1/q_{3})
. Then,

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{T}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{1}}_{T}}^{\theta_{1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{T}}^{\theta_{2}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p_{3}}_{x}L^{q_{3}}_{T}}^{1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}.

Proof.

Since

1=\frac{1}{\frac{q_{1}}{q\theta_{1}}}+\frac{1}{\frac{q_{2}}{q\theta_{2}}}+\frac{1}{\frac{q_{3}}{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}},

the Hölder inequality allows for the following representation:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q}_{T}}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|f|^{q\theta_{1}}|f|^{q\theta_{2}}|f|^{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(|f|^{q_{1}})^{\frac{q\theta_{1}}{q_{1}}}(|f|^{q_{2}})^{\frac{q\theta_{2}}{q_{2}}}(|f|^{q_{3}})^{\frac{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{q_{3}}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\big\|(|f|^{q_{1}})^{\frac{q\theta_{1}}{q_{1}}}\big\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{q_{1}}{q\theta_{1}}}}\,\big\|(|f|^{q_{2}})^{\frac{q\theta_{2}}{q_{2}}}\big\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{q_{2}}{q\theta_{2}}}}\,\big\|(|f|^{q_{3}})^{\frac{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{q_{3}}}\big\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{q_{3}}{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L_{T}^{q_{1}}}^{q\theta_{1}}\,\|f\|_{L_{T}^{q_{2}}}^{q\theta_{2}}\,\|f\|_{L_{T}^{q_{2}}}^{q(1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}.

Hence,

(19)

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{T}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{T}}^{\theta_{1}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{T}}^{\theta_{2}}\,\|f\|_{L^{q_{3}}_{T}}^{1-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}.

Similarly, utilizing the relation

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0

Firstly, recall from

the interpolation formula for fractional derivatives

\|D_{x}^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{T}^{q}}\lesssim\|D_{x}^{s_{1}}f\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{T}^{q_{1}}}^{\theta}\|D_{x}^{s_{2}}f\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{T}^{q_{2}}}^{1-\theta}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Recall from

\||x|^{\gamma}f\|_{L_{x}^{\tau}}\lesssim|f|^{a}_{W_{x}^{\theta\alpha,2/(1-\theta)}}\,\big\|{|x|^{r}f}\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{1-a},

\displaystyle\||x|^{\gamma}f\|_{L_{T}^{6/(\theta\alpha+\theta)}L_{x}^{\tau}}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

Define

It is known that (see for example

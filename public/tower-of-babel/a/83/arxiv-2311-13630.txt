Functional Matching and Renormalization Group Equations at Two-Loop Order
Javier Fuentes-Martín, Ajdin Palavrić, Anders Eller Thomsen

Abstract
We present a systematic method for determining the two-loop effective Lagrangian resulting from integrating out a set of heavy particles in an ultraviolet scalar theory. We prove that the matching coefficients are entirely determined from the (double-)hard region of the loop integrals and present a master formula for matching, applicable to both diagrammatic and functional approaches. We further employ functional methods to determine compact expressions for the effective Lagrangian that do not rely on any previous knowledge of its structure or symmetries. The same methods are also applicable to the computation of renormalization group equations. We demonstrate the application of the functional approach by computing the two-loop matching coefficients and renormalization group equations in a scalar toy model.



1   Introduction

The use of Effective Field Theories (EFTs) in beyond the Standard Model (BSM) searches is ubiquitous. Given the current experimental bounds, it seems increasingly likely that there is a large gap between the electroweak scale and the next energy threshold. In this event, EFTs are the ideal tool to capture the possible low-energy effects of new physics (NP), whatever it might be. With them, we can look for subtle NP effects, opening the door to the exploration of energy scales orders of magnitude beyond what can be reached on-shell at the LHC.

2   Functional Methods

Through path-integral manipulations in a UV theory, one can determine the EFT Lagrangian describing the dynamics of the light particles at low energies compared to the heavy-particles masses. We discuss here how this can be done at the two-loop level.

2.1  The vacuum functional and quantum effective action

The vacuum functional,
\mathcal{W}[J]
, is the generating functional of all connected Green’s functions of a theory and, therefore, contains all physical information. If we take the fields (including their conjugates) to be collectively denoted by
\eta_{a}(x)
and the action by
S[\eta]
, the vacuum functional is defined by the path integral:

\displaystyle e^{i\hbar^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\mathcal{W}[J]}=\int\hskip-3.50006pt\mathcal{D}\eta\,e^{i\hbar^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\left(S[\eta]+J_{I}\eta_{I}\right)}\,,

(1)

where the subindices with capital Latin letters
I,J,\ldots
correspond to DeWitt notation, where the spacetime dependence is included as part of the label, e.g.
I=(x,a)
. Thus, the contraction of repeated indices denotes not only an implicit summation in the field labels but also an integration over spacetime.
1
1

1

In the case at hand,
J_{I}\eta_{I}=\int_{x}\hskip-3.50006pt\,J_{a}(x)\eta_{a}(x)
with
\int_{x}\hskip-3.50006pt\;\equiv\int\hskip-3.50006pt\;\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x
.

The path integral can be evaluated perturbatively, using a saddle-point approximation around the classical background-field configuration
\overline{\eta}
, which is the solution to the tree-level equations of motion (EOMs), that is

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta S^{(0)}}{\delta\eta_{I}}[\overline{\eta}]+J_{I}=0\,,

(2)

where the superindex denotes loop order, with
(0)
indicating tree level. We parameterize the expansion of the action around the background-field configuration as

\displaystyle S[\eta+\overline{\eta}]

\displaystyle\!=\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{\ell}\Big[\overline{S}^{(\ell)}\!\!+\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{(\ell)}_{I}\!\eta_{I}\!+\overline{\mathcal{B}}^{(\ell)}_{IJ}\dfrac{\eta_{I}\eta_{J}}{2}\!+\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{(\ell)}_{IJK}\dfrac{\eta_{I}\eta_{J}\eta_{K}}{3!}

\displaystyle\quad+\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{(\ell)}_{IJKL}\dfrac{\eta_{I}\eta_{J}\eta_{K}\eta_{L}}{4!}+\mathcal{O}(\eta^{5})\Big]\,,

(3)

with
\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{(0)}_{I}=-J_{I}
, and the bar being shorthand for exclusive dependence on
\overline{\eta}
, e.g.
\overline{S}\equiv S[\overline{\eta}]
.
It is convention to denote the inverse dressed propagator of the quantum field (also known as the fluctuation operator) by
\mathcal{Q}_{IJ}\equiv\mathcal{B}^{(0)}_{IJ}
. For renormalizable theories, all higher-loop vertices
\mathcal{A}_{I}^{(\ell)},\mathcal{B}_{IJ}^{(\ell)},\ldots
for
\ell\geq 1
stem from the counterterms of the renormalized action; however, this could be different if our UV theory is itself an EFT.

Using the defining relation of the vacuum functional given by (
1
) together with the expansion of the action around the background field, the vacuum functional can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}[J]

\displaystyle=\overline{S}^{(0)}\!+J_{I}\overline{\eta}_{I}+\hbar\,\overline{S}^{(1)}\!+\frac{i\hbar}{2}\big(\log\overline{\mathcal{Q}}\big)_{II}\!+\hbar^{2}\,\overline{S}^{(2)}+\frac{i\hbar^{2}}{2}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{IJ}\overline{\mathcal{B}}^{(1)}_{JI}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{12}\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJK}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{IL}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{JM}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{KN}\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{LMN}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{8}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{IJ}\,\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{(0)}_{IJKL}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{KL}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\left(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{K}+\frac{i}{2}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{IJ}\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJK}\right)\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{KL}\left(\overline{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{L}+\frac{i}{2}\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{LMN}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{MN}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{3})\,.

(4)

The expansion of the vacuum functional reproduces the usual loop expansion (formally in
\hbar
). Therefore, the terms of
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})
in (
2.1
) correspond to the two-loop topologies, while the terms of higher order are dropped.

It is convenient to relate this functional to the quantum effective action,
\Gamma
, as we will ultimately relate
\Gamma
to the EFT action. The quantum effective action is the generating functional of all one-particle-irreducible (1PI) Green’s functions of the theory and is defined by the Legendre transform of

\displaystyle\Gamma[\hat{\eta}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}[J]-J_{I}\hat{\eta}_{I}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{I}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\delta\mathcal{W}}{\delta J_{I}}\,.

(5)

The background field
\hat{\eta}
and the sources then satisfy the quantum EOM

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta\eta_{I}}[\hat{\eta}]+J_{I}=0\,.

(6)

Plugging this back into definition (
5
) along with the saddle-point approximation of
\mathcal{W}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma[\hat{\eta}]

\displaystyle=\widehat{S}^{(0)}+\hbar\,\widehat{S}^{(1)}+\dfrac{i\hbar}{2}\big(\!\log\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}\big)_{II}+\hbar^{2}\widehat{S}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{i\hbar^{2}}{2}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{JI}^{(1)}-\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{8}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\,\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{(0)}_{IJKL}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{KL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{12}\,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJK}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{JM}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{KN}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{LMN}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{3})\,,

(7)

where, analogously to the bar notation, the hat is used as shorthand for exclusive dependence on
\hat{\eta}
, e.g.
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}\equiv\mathcal{Q}_{IJ}[\hat{\eta}]
. This is an extension of the well-known expression for the effective action at one-loop order, where all one-loop contributions are contained in a functional (super)trace. Interestingly, the terms in the expression above can be understood as vacuum graphs with
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}
acting as a quantum-field propagator, dressed with arbitrary insertions of the background fields
\hat{\eta}
, and
\mathcal{B}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{C}^{(0)}
and
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}
corresponding to two-, three-, and four-point quantum field interactions (again in the presence of background fields). This is represented in Figure

In general, evaluating the effective action (

at different spacetime points. Nonetheless, when all loop momenta are restricted to a hard region,

2.2  Master formula for EFT matching

Let us consider a weakly-coupled UV theory,
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}}}[\eta]
, where
\eta_{I}=(\Phi_{\alpha},\,\phi_{i})
denotes the collection of all fields, heavy and light, respectively. We seek to determine an EFT action
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[\phi]
that reproduces the physics of the full theory at energies much below the masses of the heavy fields, which we assume to lie around a generic scale
\Lambda
.

In off-shell matching computations, we begin with an even stronger requirement for the EFT matching condition: the EFT should reproduce all low-energy Green’s functions of the full theory. This stronger requirement lets us consider the generating functional of the theories rather than the
S
-matrix.
Thus, our aim is to determine
S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[\phi]
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[J_{\phi}]=\mathcal{W}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}\big[J_{\Phi}=0,\,J_{\phi}\big]\,.

(8)

That is, we enforce equality of all connected Green’s functions involving the light fields. Each side of (
8
) is to be understood in terms of power series in
1/\Lambda
.

To make matters simpler, we proceed with a Legendre transformation of the light-field sources in order to frame the matching condition in terms of the quantum effective actions:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]\,,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}}{\delta\Phi_{\alpha}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]=0\,.

(9)

The heavy fields
\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}]
are solutions to the quantum EOMs in the presence of the light background fields. In diagrammatic terms, condition (
9
) equates all one-light-particle-irreducible (1LPI) Green’s functions of the UV theory with the 1PI Green’s functions of the EFT.
It was demonstrated in
Fuentes-Martin:2016uol
;
Zhang:2016pja
that, at one-loop order, there is a cancellation between the loop contributions in the EFT and the soft-region of the loops in the UV theory. This enables a very direct computation of
S^{(1)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[\phi]
in terms of the hard region of the UV quantum effective action.

We are now ready to generalize these considerations and present a master formula for perturbative EFT matching at multi-loop order: the off-shell EFT action is determined by

\displaystyle S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]\Big|_{\mathrm{hard}}\,,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}\big|_{\mathrm{hard}}}{\delta\Phi_{\alpha}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]

\displaystyle=0\,.

(10)

Here the ‘hard’ part is taken to include all contributions without
any
soft loops. It includes tree-level contributions as well as loops where
all
loop momenta are hard. In many ways, this is an intuitive leap: the hard, local part of the UV theory is identified with the EFT action, while the long-distance physics is captured by loops in the EFT. Nevertheless, we have never seen an explicit formulation of this notion, much less a matching formula applicable to practical computations. A constructive proof of the matching formula (
10
) at two-loop order is provided in Appendix
A
, where we show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between (partially) soft loops in the UV and loops in the EFT. We postpone the discussion on a possible extension of this proof to higher-loop orders to a more comprehensive follow-up paper. The end result is that all loop integrals needed to perform the EFT matching (i.e. those in the hard limit) reduce to vacuum integrals, for which expressions are known up to three loops
Martin:2016bgz
.

The matching formula (
10
) is a
d
-dimensional off-shell relation. A complication associated to this kind of matching is that it does not produce the EFT Lagrangian directly in a four-dimensional on-shell basis. Rather, one has to apply field redefinitions to reduce the output to an on-shell basis. Likewise, the matching result may also produce EFT operators that are not present in a four-dimensional basis. As a result, one has to separate out evanescent operators and, preferably, convert the EFT action to an evanescence-free scheme
Dugan:1990df
;
Buras:1989xd
;
Herrlich:1994kh
;
Fuentes-Martin:2022vvu
;
Aebischer:2022tvz
;
Aebischer:2022aze
;
Aebischer:2022rxf
. A related consideration to be aware of beyond one-loop order is that lower-order matching coefficients may contain
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
contributions. These cannot be ignored, as their insertion in an EFT loop can lead to finite contributions. Therefore, one has to carefully remove the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
terms and absorb them into finite coefficients at higher-loop order, similarly to what is done for evanescent contributions
Fuentes-Martin:2022vvu
.

2.3  Functional approach for RG evolution

The MS (or
\overline{\text{\small MS}}
) counterterms of a theory,
S[\eta]
, can also be determined from the effective action from the observation that it must be free of UV divergences. If we use dimensional regularization to regularize the loop integrals, finiteness of the renormalized generating functional translates to the condition

\displaystyle K_{\epsilon}\,\Gamma[\hat{\eta}]=0\,,

(11)

where
K_{\epsilon}
is an operator that extracts all
1/\epsilon
poles of UV origin. This equation establishes a relation between the MS counterterms of the theory, identified with the UV poles of
\widehat{S}^{(\ell)}
, and the other terms in the effective action. Denoting the MS counterterms by
\delta\widehat{S}^{(\ell)}_{\rm MS}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta\widehat{S}^{(\ell)}_{\rm MS}=K_{\epsilon}\,\widehat{S}^{(\ell)}\,,

(12)

which, together with the expression of
\Gamma
and condition (
11
), establishes a direct relation to determine
\delta\widehat{S}^{(\ell)}_{\rm MS}
functionally. Other renormalization schemes of the subtraction family can also be obtained by appropriately adapting the definitions of the counterterms in the expression above.

Restricting to the two-loop expression of the effective action in (
2.1
), we obtain the following MS counterterms up to two-loop order:

\displaystyle\delta\widehat{S}^{(1)}_{\rm MS}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{i}{2}K_{\epsilon}\big(\!\log\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}\big)_{II}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\widehat{S}^{(2)}_{\rm MS}

\displaystyle=K_{\epsilon}\Big[-\frac{i}{2}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{IJ}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{(1)}_{JI}+\dfrac{1}{8}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{(0)}_{IJKL}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{KL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{1}{12}\,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJK}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{JM}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{KN}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{LMN}\Big]\,.

(13)

A convenient prescription for extracting the UV poles in theories with massless states consists in introducing a common mass,
\Lambda
, in all propagators of the loop integrals. This mass acts as a hard scale (assumed to be much larger than any other scales in the loop integrals) and serves as an infrared regulator.
The overall UV divergence of a loop integral is identified with the part where all loop momenta are large. Hence, it is easy to show that the UV divergences can be extracted from the hard-region (defined by all loop momenta being of order
\Lambda
) of the loop integrals
Chetyrkin:1997fm
. This effectively establishes a power-counting on
\Lambda
, around which
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}
can be expanded. As in the matching case, the resulting loop integrals are just vacuum integrals (in this case with a single mass
\Lambda
) for which results are known up to three loops
Martin:2016bgz
. The only drawback of this method for UV-pole extraction is that one also needs to consider spurious counterterms with positive powers of
\Lambda
. These spurious counterterms can break the symmetries of the original Lagrangian and are needed for the cancellation of subdivergences. After the counterterms have been determined, the RG equations can be readily obtained via standard techniques.

2.4  Functional evaluation of the effective action

The functional evaluation of the effective action requires manipulating the inverse dressed propagator,
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
, which can be generically parametrized by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}\equiv\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{ab}(x,y)=Q_{ab}(x,\,P_{x})\,\delta(x-y)\,,

(14)

with
P^{\mu}_{x}=i\partial^{\mu}_{x}
denoting the momentum operator. Since the action is local, it is always possible to factor out a delta function. This is also the case when dealing with more complicated functional forms involving
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
, such as
\ln\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
or
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}
. It is well-known that the one-loop contribution to the quantum effective action, c.f. (
2.1
), is given by the functional (super)trace

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\widehat{S}^{(1)}+\frac{i}{2}(\ln\widehat{\mathcal{Q}})_{II}

\displaystyle=\widehat{S}^{(1)}+\frac{i}{2}\int_{x,k}\hskip-3.50006pt\;\,[\ln Q(x,P_{x}-k)]_{aa}\,,

(15)

with
\int_{x,k}\hskip-3.50006pt\equiv\int\hskip-3.50006pt\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}x\int\hskip-3.50006pt\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{d}k/(2\pi)^{d}
. This expression is a non-local function of
Q
and is difficult to evaluate in general. However, as we are only interested in the hard region, where the loop momentum is taken to be of the order of the heavy scales, we can perform an operator-product expansion. For scalar theories, the inverse dressed propagator takes the generic form

\displaystyle Q_{ab}(x,\,P_{x})=(P_{x}^{2}-M_{a}^{2})\,\delta_{ab}-U_{ab}(x,P_{x})\,,

(16)

with
M_{a}
being a possible (hard) mass, and
U_{ab}
a generic interaction term which, as we make explicit in its argument, may involve derivatives. Denoting

\displaystyle\Delta^{\!\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{ab}(P_{x},k)

\displaystyle\equiv(k^{2}-M_{a}^{2}+P_{x}^{2}-2k\cdot P_{x})\,\delta_{ab}\,,

\displaystyle X_{ab}(x,P_{x},k)

\displaystyle\equiv U_{ab}(x,P_{x}-k)\,,

(17)

the operator-product expansion of the logarithm reads

\displaystyle\int_{x,k}\hskip-3.50006pt\ln Q(x,P_{x}-k)

\displaystyle=\int_{x,k}\hskip-3.50006pt\ln\Delta^{\!\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}-\int_{x,k}\hskip-3.50006pt\;\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\,(\Delta X)^{n}\,,

(18)

where
(\ln\Delta^{\!\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1})_{ab}=\delta_{ab}\ln(k^{2}-M_{a}^{2})
contributes to an unphysical constant that is subtracted when normalizing the path integral and the expansion for
\Delta
takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta_{ab}(P_{x},k)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ab}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-P_{x}^{2}+2k\cdot P_{x})^{n}}{(k^{2}-M_{a}^{2})^{n+1}}\,.

(19)

Likewise, the dressed propagator, necessary for evaluating the two-loop contributions, admits the expansion

\displaystyle Q^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}(x,P_{x}-k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\Delta X)^{n}\Delta\,.

(20)

The hard-region evaluation guarantees that subsequent terms in all these series are further suppressed, so only a finite number of terms need to be retained to a given order in the EFT expansion. A manifestly local result is obtained only in the hard-region limit.

Locality of the action likewise ensures that delta functions can be factored out of the remaining functional objects. We write the quantum-field interactions as series in the momenta operator:
3
3

3

For compactness, we employ a power-like notation with underlined superscripts for the Lorentz indices, that is, we denote
B_{ab}^{(1)\,\underline{m}}P_{x}^{\underline{m}}\equiv B_{ab}^{(1)\,\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{m}}P_{x}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots P_{x}^{\mu_{m}}
.

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{(1)}_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{(1)}_{ab}(x,y)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B^{(1)\,\underline{m}}_{ab}(x)P_{x}^{\underline{m}}\,\delta(x-y)\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJK}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{abc}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}C^{\underline{m},\underline{n}}_{abc}(z)P_{x}^{\underline{m}}P_{y}^{\underline{n}}\,\delta(x-z)\delta(y-z)\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{(0)}_{IJKL}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{D}}^{(0)}_{abcd}(x,y,z,w)=\sum_{m,n,r=0}^{\infty}D^{\underline{m},\underline{n},\underline{r}}_{abcd}(w)

\displaystyle\quad\times P_{x}^{\underline{m}}P_{y}^{\underline{n}}P_{z}^{\underline{r}}\,\delta(x-w)\delta(y-w)\delta(z-w)\,.

(21)

In most practical applications, only a small number of terms from this momentum operator expansions are present. In fact, for renormalizable theories, only the terms with at most two momenta in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
, one in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, and none in
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
are nonzero.

Having made these definitions, we can now evaluate the two-loop contributions to the effective action in (

). We parameterize them as

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}^{(2)}=\widehat{S}^{(2)}+\dfrac{i}{2}G_{\mathrm{ct}}-\dfrac{1}{8}G_{\mathrm{f8}}+\dfrac{1}{12}G_{\mathrm{ss}}\,,

(22)

where the loop contributions

are identified with the counterterm, figure-8, and sunset topologies, respectively (as depicted in Figure

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}

4   Conclusions and outlook

The discovery of physics beyond the SM is proving more challenging than initially anticipated. Given the precision increase associated with upcoming experimental searches and the absence of clear indications of the possible shape of NP, the use of novel and more precise EFT approaches becomes more important than ever.

In this letter, we have presented the initial steps toward functional EFT matching and RG evolution at two-loop order, so far restricted to the case of scalar theories. We have explicitly demonstrated that the
hard part
of the UV effective action is all that is needed for such calculations. To our knowledge, this is the first time that an explicit proof of this statement has been presented. While this result is applicable for both diagrammatic and functional approaches, it becomes particularly useful for the latter since it enables a power counting around which to perform an operator-product expansion of the functional results. Building on this, we have provided closed-form expressions for the evaluation of the two-loop effective action in the hard limit. In this way, and analogously to the one-loop functional result, our calculation of the two-loop EFT Lagrangian does not require the determination of the target EFT basis and essentially amounts to simple algebraic manipulations, making it particularly suitable for automation. We have also presented a toy-model example that illustrates the main rationale behind the application of our functional approach and, as a byproduct, we have verified recent literature results concerning the determination of two-loop RG equations in the geometric approach
Jenkins:2023bls
.

The extension of these methods to the more general case, including theories with fermions and/or gauge bosons, remains non-trivial and will be discussed in a forthcoming publication. In particular, we observe that the standard techniques to make the functional evaluations manifestly covariant
Gaillard:1985uh
;
Chan:1986jq
are no longer applicable at two-loop order, and new strategies are required. Likewise, the generalization of these results to higher-loop orders will also be explored in the future.

Appendix A
Matching formula at two-loop order

In this appendix, we use the functional expansion of the effective action to demonstrate the explicit cancellation between soft-region loops in the UV and loops in the EFT and prove the master formula for EFT matching (
10
) up to two-loop order.

Our starting point is the off-shell matching condition (
9
). On the UV side, we are dealing with the generating functional

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\phi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle\equiv\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]\,,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}}{\delta\Phi_{\alpha}}\big[\widehat{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}\big]=0\,,

(43)

which generates all 1LPI Green’s functions. We need an explicit expression for
\Gamma^{\phi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}
order-by-order in the loop expansion, and, so, we have to explicitly isolate each loop contribution in the heavy-field EOMs. To this end, let
\tilde{\eta}_{I}=\big(\overline{\Phi}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}],\,\hat{\phi}_{i}\big)
solve the tree-level heavy field EOMs in the presence of
\hat{\phi}
, that is

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(0)}}{\delta\Phi_{\alpha}}[\tilde{\eta}]=0\,.

(44)

Up to one-loop order, the quantum-EOM solution reads

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}]=\overline{\Phi}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}]-\hbar\,\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{\beta}+\dfrac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{\,(0)}_{\beta IJ}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{JI}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})\,,

(45)

where we employ the same compact notation as in the main text, i.e.
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}=\mathcal{Q}_{IJ}[\tilde{\eta}]
and likewise for other functions exclusively dependent of
\widetilde{\eta}_{I}
. Explicitly, the UV generating functional is then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{\phi}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(0)}+\hbar\,\widetilde{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(1)}+\dfrac{i\hbar}{2}\big(\log\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}\big)_{II}+\hbar^{2}\,\widetilde{S}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(2)}+\dfrac{i\hbar^{2}}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{JI}^{(1)}+\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{12}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{IJK}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{IL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{JM}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{KN}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{LMN}

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{8}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{IJKL}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{KL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}-\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\bigg(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{\alpha}+\dfrac{i}{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{IJ}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{IJ\alpha}\bigg)\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\bigg(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{(1)}_{\beta}+\dfrac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}_{\beta KL}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{KL}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{3})\,.

(46)

This expression is the same we would have gotten directly from (
2.1
) by setting
J_{\Phi}=0
and performing the Legendre transform on the light fields only. We will also need the expression

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\overline{\Phi}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}]}{\delta\hat{\phi}_{i}}=-\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{i\beta}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta\alpha}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\,,

(47)

for our proof,
which is obtained by taking a functional derivative of the light classical fields in (
44
). Finally, it is useful to define the upper-triangular transformation
V_{IJ}
that diagonalizes the inverse dressed propagator
\mathcal{Q}
. It takes the form

\displaystyle V_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{\alpha\beta}&-\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha\gamma}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma j}\\
0&\delta_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(48)

such that

\displaystyle\mathds{Q}_{IJ}

\displaystyle\equiv V^{\intercal}_{IK}\mathcal{Q}_{KL}V_{LJ}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&\mathcal{Q}_{ij}-\mathcal{Q}_{i\alpha}\mathcal{Q}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{Q}_{\beta j}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(49)

In the next sections, we proceed to establish the matching formula order by order in the loop expansion.

A.1  Tree level

It is important to discriminate between non-local objects such as
\mathcal{Q}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}
and their EFT operator-product expansions, as they have vastly different behaviors when performing the loop integrals. We denote the finite-order expansion of the heavy-field EOM solution by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}=\overline{\Phi}[\hat{\phi}]+\mathcal{O}(1/\Lambda^{N+1})\,,

(50)

which is sufficient to determine the
N
-order matching. In what follows, the superscript ‘s’ in any other quantities will be used to denote the dependence on
\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}[\hat{\phi}]
, e.g.
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}=\mathcal{Q}[\eta^{\mathrm{s}}]=\mathcal{Q}\big[\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}[\hat{\phi}],\hat{\phi}\big]
.

The tree-level matching criteria follows directly from the matching condition (
9
), understood as a series in
\Lambda
.
At leading order, we simply find

\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}[\hat{\phi}]=S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(0)}[\eta^{\mathrm{s}}]=S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}\,.

(51)

This is a familiar result: the tree-level EFT is obtained by substituting in the UV theory the classic EOMs of all heavy fields expanded in the heavy masses.

A.2  Expansion by regions

Figure 2:
Region decomposition of the sunset graph, with red and violet lines denoting soft and hard momenta, respectively.

Loop integrals in dimensional regularization can, under certain conditions, be written as a sum over different momentum regions
Beneke:1997zp
;
Jantzen:2011nz
. This decomposition is known as expansion by regions, and in each region the propagators are series-expanded according to the magnitude of the loop momenta in that region. Working as we are, with a generic heavy scale set by
\Lambda
, we discriminate only between whether (Euclidean) loop momenta are soft (
\ll\Lambda
) or hard (
\gtrsim\Lambda
). Given a topology
G
, we can decompose it in regions as

\displaystyle G=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\subseteq G\\
\mathrm{loops}\end{subarray}}R_{\mathrm{hard}}^{(\gamma)}(G)\,.

(52)

The sum runs over all (sub)loops
\gamma
of
G
and the operator
R_{\mathrm{hard}}^{(\gamma)}
sets the corresponding loop momenta hard, while the remaining ones are taken to be soft. For instance,
\gamma
runs over 5 subloops in the case of the sunset topology: no loops, 3 distinct one-loop graphs, and the full two-loop graph (see Figure
2
).
We have verified that this region decomposition is valid up to two-loop order, following the criteria in
Jantzen:2011nz
.
To keep track of the regions in DeWitt notation, we introduce colored indices to distinguish soft and hard momentum modes:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}I},\,{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha},\,{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}:\quad

\displaystyle p_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}I},\,p_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha},\,p_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}\ll\Lambda\,,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}I},\,{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}\alpha},\,{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}i}:\quad

\displaystyle p_{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}I},\,p_{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}\alpha},\,p_{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}i}\gtrsim\Lambda\,.

(53)

All dressed propagators are understood as being expanded according to the color of their indices, cf. (
2.4
).

In the EFT, there are no propagators with heavy masses, so all hard-momenta loops are scaleless and vanish in dimensional regularization. As a result,
all indices in the EFT are soft type
. The series expansion in
\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}[\hat{\phi}]
and in the soft regions are identical, so in general

\displaystyle\dfrac{\delta\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}]}{\delta\hat{\phi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}}}=\left.\dfrac{\delta\overline{\Phi}^{(0)}_{\alpha}[\hat{\phi}]}{\delta\hat{\phi}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}}}\right|_{\overline{\Phi}^{(0)}=\Phi^{\mathrm{s}}}\,,

(54)

which, however, is not the case in the hard region (or for generic indices). Having defined this new notation, we turn to the one-loop case.

A.3  One-loop level

We use expansion by regions to decompose the one-loop UV effective action as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{\phi(1)}[\hat{\phi}]=S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(1)\mathrm{s}}+\dfrac{i}{2}\big(\!\log\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}\big)_{\!{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}II}}+\dfrac{i}{2}\big(\!\log\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}\big)_{\!{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ii}}\,.

(55)

This follows from using the identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\log\mathcal{Q}=\log\mathrm{Det}\,\mathcal{Q}\,,

(56)

and block-diagonalizing the inverse dressed propagator with
V^{\mathrm{s}}
satisfying
\mathrm{Det}\,V=1
. The equality is then due to
(\log\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{s}})_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\alpha}}
being a scaleless loop, as can be seen from
\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}
, indicating that heavy masses are present in all propagators.

Next, we determine the inverse dressed propagator in the EFT, as we need it for the EFT loop contributions. Using (
54
) and (
47
), one can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ij}}[\hat{\phi}]=\dfrac{\delta^{2}S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(0)}[\eta^{\mathrm{s}}]}{\delta\hat{\phi}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}\delta\hat{\phi}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}j}}=\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ij}\,.

(57)

We can now determine the one-loop EFT action by inserting (
57
) and (
55
) into matching condition (
9
):

\displaystyle S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}^{(1)}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{\phi(1)}[\hat{\phi}]-\dfrac{i}{2}\big(\!\log\mathcal{Q}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}}\big)_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ii}}

\displaystyle=S_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{(1)\mathrm{s}}+\dfrac{i}{2}\big(\!\log\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{s}}\big)_{\!{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}II}}\,.

(58)

This reproduces the well-known one-loop matching formula
Fuentes-Martin:2016uol
;
Zhang:2016pja
, and we can finally proceed to generalize it at the two-loop order.

A.4  Two-loop level

It will be useful to work in the basis where the inverse dressed propagator is block-diagonal also at two-loop order. To this end, we introduce the notation

\displaystyle\mathds{A}^{(\ell)}_{I}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}_{J}V_{JI}\,,

\displaystyle\mathds{B}^{(\ell)}_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}_{KL}V_{KI}V_{LJ}\,,

\displaystyle\mathds{C}^{(\ell)}_{IJK}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{LMN}V_{LI}V_{MJ}V_{NK}\,,

\displaystyle\mathds{D}^{(\ell)}_{IJKL}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}^{(\ell)}_{MNOP}V_{MI}V_{NJ}V_{OK}V_{PL}\,.

(59)

Applying the expansion by regions (
52
) to the 1LPI UV effective action (
A
) yields

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{UV}}^{\mathrm{\phi(2)}}[\hat{\phi}]=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{i}{2}\bigg(\mathds{A}^{(1)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha}}+\dfrac{i}{2}\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}IJ}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}JI}{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha}}\bigg)\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\beta}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\beta}{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}KL}}\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}LK}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}+\dfrac{1}{8}\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}IJ}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}JI\alpha}}\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\beta}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\beta}{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}KL}}\mathds{Q}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}LK}}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}\,.

Building on the tree-level and one-loop EFT matching results, we can now construct the genuine loop contributions to the two-loop EFT effective action. EFT vertices are determined by the application of consecutive derivatives to the dressed propagator (

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(0){\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ijk}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ijk}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(0){\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ijk\ell}[\hat{\phi}]

\displaystyle=\mathds{D}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ijk\ell}-\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ij\alpha}\mathds{Q}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\beta}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\beta k\ell}-\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ik\alpha}\mathds{Q}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\beta}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\beta j\ell}

\displaystyle\quad-\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i\ell\alpha}\mathds{Q}^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\alpha\beta}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}\beta jk}\,.

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{(1){\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{EFT}}}_{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ij}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{i}{2}\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}IJ}}\mathds{D}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}JI}{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}ij}}-\dfrac{i}{2}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}i}{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}KL}}\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}KM}}\mathds{Q}^{\mathrm{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1\,s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}LN}}\mathds{C}^{(0)\mathrm{s}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.415,0.2069,0.6151}MN}{\color[rgb]{0.68,0.2565,0.3098}j}}\,,

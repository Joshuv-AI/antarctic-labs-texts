Harmonic Maass forms associated with CM newforms
Stephan Ehlen, Yingkun Li and Markus Schwagenscheidt

Abstract
. In this paper, we use a regularized theta lifting to construct harmonic Maass forms corresponding to binary theta functions of weight k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 under the ξ \xi -operator. As a result, we show that their holomorphic parts have algebraic Fourier coefficients, with compatible Galois action. As an application, we prove rationality properties of coefficients of harmonic Maaass forms corresponding to CM newforms, answering a question of Bruinier, Ono and Rhoades.



1.
Introduction

Harmonic Maass forms, which are non-holomorphic generalizations of classical holomorphic modular forms, have been introduced by Bruinier and Funke
[
BF04
]
in connection with theta liftings, but also played a crucial role in Zwegers’ investigation of Ramanujan’s mock theta functions
[
Zwe02
]
. Since then, there has been a lot of interest in harmonic Maass forms, in particular concerning the Fourier coefficients of their holomorphic parts. For example, it turns out that several natural generating series, e.g. of traces of CM values or cycle integrals of modular functions, appear as holomorphic parts of harmonic Maass forms
[
BF06
,
DIT11
]
. Moreover, it seems that the transcendency of these holomorphic part coefficients is a deep question. For instance, in some cases their transcendency controls the non-vanishing of central values of the first derivative of
L
-functions of certain newforms
[
BO10
]
.

For a given harmonic Maass form, there are only two cases when all its holomorphic part Fourier coefficients are known to be algebraic, namely when its shadow is a linear combination of unary or higher weight binary theta series.
The first case contains the modular completions of the generating series of Hurwitz class numbers
[
HZ76
]
and
the mock theta functions of Ramanujan studied by Zwegers
[
Zwe02
]
.
The second case includes newforms with complex multiplication
[
BOR08
]
, which we describe now.

Let
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})\subset{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
be an imaginary quadratic field.
Given an algebraic Hecke character
\varrho
of weight
k\geq 2

and modulus
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{E}
, one can associate
a newform
\vartheta_{\varrho}\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N,\chi)
of weight
k
, level
N=D\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{m})
with some Nebentypus
\chi
(see (
2.12
)).
In
[
BOR08
]
, Bruinier, Ono and Rhoades studied the algebraicity properties of a harmonic Maass form
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}\in H_{2-k}(N,\overline{\chi})
such that
\xi_{2-k}(\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho})=\vartheta_{\varrho}/\|\vartheta_{\varrho}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}
.
Though such a harmonic Maass form is not unique, they chose a so-called
good
one, whose holomorphic part
\tilde{\vartheta}^{+}_{\varrho}
satisfies the following additional conditions.

(1)

\tilde{\vartheta}^{+}_{\varrho}
is holomorphic at all cusps not equivalent to
\infty
.

(2)

The principal part of
\tilde{\vartheta}^{+}_{\varrho}
at
\infty
has coefficients in
F_{\varrho}
.

Here
F_{\varrho}
is the field of definition of
\vartheta_{\varrho}
.
It is not hard to see that such good forms exist, and are unique up to addition of weakly holomorphic forms in
M^{!}_{2-k}(N,\overline{\chi})
which have coefficients in
F_{\varrho}
and are holomorphic away from the cusp
\infty
.
Using indirect methods such as twisting and Hecke operators, Bruinier, Ono and Rhoades proved that all holomorphic part Fourier coefficients of good harmonic Maass forms for
\vartheta_{\varrho}

are in
F_{\varrho}(\zeta_{ND})
, where
\zeta_{ND}
denotes a primitive
(ND)
-th root of unity.
In addition, they raised two questions (see Remarks 3(v) and 3(vi) loc. cit.)

3(v)

Find an explicit construction of good
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}
.

3(vi)

Is there a good
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}
such that the Fourier coefficients of its holomorphic part are contained in
F_{\varrho}
?

In this paper, we answer the two questions above simultaneously.
In particular, we will extend the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
from CM newforms to their
\xi
-preimages in a compatible way.
In terms of the Galois actions on Laurent series and Hecke characters given in Section
2
, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every Hecke character
\varrho
of weight
k\geq 2
, there exists
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}\in H_{2-k}(N,\overline{\chi})
good for
\vartheta_{\varrho}
such that
its holomorphic part
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}^{+}
satisfies

(1.1)

(\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}^{+})^{\sigma}=\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho^{\sigma}}^{+}

for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
Here
\varrho^{\sigma}
is the Hecke character defined in (
2.11
).

The proof of this theorem follows from the explicit construction of harmonic Maass forms using regularized Petersson inner products, see Theorem
2.2
below. This method depends on Serre duality
[
Bor99
]
and has been applied in various contexts to produce real-analytic modular forms
[
Ehl17
,
ES18
,
Via19
,
LS22
]
.
For CM forms of weight
k\geq 2
, such inner products can be evaluated in terms of CM values of Borcherds-Shimura lifts, which are meromorphic modular forms of weight
2k-2
.
Up to suitable periods, these values are algebraic numbers, see Proposition
3.1
.
Then we need to suitably normalize the periods and understand the Galois action on these algebraic numbers, see Proposition
3.2
.
Putting these together, we arrive at Theorem
3.5
, which gives us vector-valued harmonic Maass forms having algebraic holomorphic part Fourier coefficients with compatible Galois actions.
To obtain Theorem
1.1
for scalar-valued newforms, we take suitable linear combinations of components of vector-valued harmonic Maass forms constructed in Theorem
3.5
. Here the crucial fact is that these newforms are themselves linear combinations of vector-valued binary theta series, see Proposition
2.6
.

The following corollary of Theorem
1.1
affirms question 3(vi) above.

Corollary 1.2
.

For a CM newform
\vartheta_{\varrho}\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N,\chi)
defined over
F_{\varrho}
, and any
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}\in H_{2-k}(N,\overline{\chi})
good for
\vartheta_{\varrho}
, all coefficients of its holomorphic part
\tilde{\vartheta}_{\varrho}^{+}
are contained in
F_{\varrho}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
Can14
]
, Candelori gave an algebraic geometric interpretation of
integral weight harmonic Maass forms. With this approach, he was able to prove that harmonic Maass forms associated with CM modular forms have algebraic holomorphic part Fourier coefficients.
From private communication, he informed us that the same approach could give another proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the case of weight
k=1
, the harmonic Maass forms with CM newforms as
\xi_{1}
-images are studied in
[
DL15
,
Ehl17
,
Via19
,
CL20
]
. The holomorphic part Fourier coefficients are shown to be
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
-linear combinations of logarithms of algebraic numbers.

It is generally believed that the Fourier coefficients of a generic harmonic Maass form are transcendental (see Remark 8 in
[
BO10
]
), and expected that the two known cases above are the only harmonic Maass forms with algebraic Fourier coefficients.
In the unary case, the second and third authors showed that the Fourier coefficients are rational with explicitly bounded denominators
[
LS22
]
. In the binary case, however, it seems that the algebraic coefficients have unbounded denominators. We formulate these observations into the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
\tilde{f}
be a harmonic Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
for a congruence subgroup such that
\xi(\tilde{f})\neq 0
. If the Fourier coefficients of its holomorphic part are all algebraic, then
\xi(\tilde{f})
is either a linear combination of unary theta series with
k\in\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\}
or of binary theta series with
2-k\geq 2
.
In those cases, the coefficients have bounded denominators if and only if
k
is half-integral.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains preliminary information, and a relation between CM newforms and vector-valued binary theta series in Proposition
2.6
.
In Section
3.1
, we prove the algebraicity of regularized Petersson inner product between weakly holomorphic modular forms and binary theta series.
Then in Section
3.2
, we compare the Petersson norm of binary theta series associated to different quadratic spaces in order to normalize the period in Proposition
3.2
.
Finally, we prove the main result in Section
3.3
, and give a numerical example in the last section.

2.1.
Modular forms for the Weil representation

Let
V
be a rational quadratic space of signature
(b^{+},b^{-})
with quadratic form
Q\colon V\to\mathbb{Q}
and corresponding bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Let
L\subset V
be an even lattice of level
N
and let
L^{\prime}
be the dual lattice of
L
. We also let
L^{-}
(resp.
V^{-}
) denote the lattice
L
(resp. vector space
V
) with quadratic form
-Q
. The discriminant group
L^{\prime}/L
is finite and isomorphic to
\hat{L}^{\prime}/\hat{L}
, where
\hat{L}^{\prime}:=L^{\prime}\otimes\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
\mu\in L^{\prime}/L
we consider the characteristic functions

(2.1)

\phi_{\mu}:=\mathrm{char}(\hat{L}+\mu),\quad\mu\in L^{\prime}/L,

which form a basis for the space

\mathcal{S}_{L}:=\bigoplus_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathbb{C}\phi_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{S}(V(\hat{\mathbb{Q}}))

of Schwartz functions on
V(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})
which are supported on
\hat{L}^{\prime}
and which are constant on cosets of
\hat{L}:=L\otimes\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
. We let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the bilinear pairing on
\mathcal{S}_{L}
defined by
\langle\phi_{\mu},\phi_{\nu}\rangle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}
.

Let
\rho_{L}
be the Weil representation of the two-fold metaplectic cover
{\mathrm{Mp}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{S}_{L}
(see
[
Bor98
, Section 4]
). It is self-adjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
If
L
has level
N
, then its matrix coefficients with respect to the standard basis
\{\phi_{\mu}:\mu\in L^{\prime}/L\}
are defined over
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})/\mathbb{Q})
.
Theorem 4.3 in
[
McG03
]
tells us that for any
\gamma\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

(2.2)

\rho_{L}(\gamma)^{\sigma_{a}}=\rho_{L}(\gamma^{a}),~\gamma^{a}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&a^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}),

with
\sigma_{a}\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})/\mathbb{Q})
the element associated with
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
by the Artin map.
Note that
\Gamma(N)
is in the kernel of
\rho_{L}
, and
\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{a}
is an automorphism of
\Gamma(N)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Therefore for any
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-invariant function
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{L}
valued in
\mathbb{Q}
, the function

(2.3)

\mathrm{tr}^{N}_{1}\phi:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\rho_{L}(\gamma)(\phi)\in\mathcal{S}_{L}

also has value in
\mathbb{Q}
.

For
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, let
H_{k,L}
be the space of harmonic (weak) Maass forms of weight
k
valued in
\mathcal{S}_{L}
for the representation
\rho_{L}
(see
[
BF04
]
) for which there is a Laurent polynomial

P_{f}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Q}_{\leq 0}\\
m\gg-\infty\end{subarray}}c_{f}^{+}(m,\mu)q^{m}\phi_{\mu},

called the principal part of
f
, such that
f(\tau)-P_{f}(\tau)=O(e^{-\varepsilon})
as
v=\mathrm{Im}(\tau)\to\infty
, for some
\varepsilon>0
. It contains
S_{k,L}\subset M_{k,L}\subset M_{k,L}^{!}
, the subspaces of cusp forms, holomorphic, and weakly holomorphic modular forms. The space
H_{k,L}
can only be non-trivial if

\kappa:=k-\frac{b^{-}-b^{+}}{2}

is an integer. In this case, the components of
f(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}f_{\mu}(\tau)\phi_{\mu}\in H_{k,L}
satisfy the symmetry

(2.4)

f_{-\mu}(\tau)=(-1)^{\kappa}f_{\mu}(\tau).

For
k\neq 1
every harmonic Maass form
f\in H_{k,L}
has a splitting
f=f^{+}+f^{-}
into a holomorphic and a non-holomorphic part with Fourier expansions of the shape

\displaystyle f^{+}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Q}\\
m\gg-\infty\end{subarray}}c_{f}^{+}(m,\mu)q^{m}\phi_{\mu},

\displaystyle f^{-}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Q}\\
m<0\end{subarray}}c_{f}^{-}(m,\mu)\Gamma(1-k,4\pi|m|v)q^{m}\phi_{\mu},

with coefficients
c_{f}^{\pm}(m,\mu)\in\mathbb{C}
and the incomplete Gamma function
\Gamma(s,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{s-1}dt
. The
\xi
-operator

\xi_{k}f=2iv^{k}\overline{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\tau}}f}

defines a surjective map
\xi_{k}\colon H_{k,L}\to S_{2-k,L^{-}}
.

We now fix a convenient basis for
M_{k,L}^{!}
whose elements all have rational coefficients (see the appendix of
[
BEY21
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 2
. Furthermore, let
d=\dim(M_{k,L})
.

(1)

There is a basis
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\}
of
M_{k,L}
with only rational Fourier coefficients, and indices
(n_{1},\beta_{1}),\dots,(n_{d},\beta_{d})
with
n_{j}+Q(\beta_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

c_{g_{j}}(n_{l},\beta_{l})=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{j,l}.

(2)

For each
m\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and
\mu\in L^{\prime}/L
with
m+Q(\mu)\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a unique weakly holomorphic modular form
f_{m,\mu}\in M_{k,L}^{!}
with only rational Fourier coefficients, having a Fourier expansion of the form

f_{m,\mu}=\frac{1}{2}q^{-m}(\phi_{\mu}+(-1)^{\kappa}\phi_{-\mu})+O(1)

and with
c_{f_{m,\mu}}(n_{j},\pm\beta_{j})=0
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Q}_{\leq 0}
and
\mu\in L^{\prime}/L
we put
f_{m,\mu}=(\pm 1)^{\kappa}g_{j}
if
(m,\mu)=(n_{j},\pm\beta_{j})
, and
f_{m,\mu}=0
otherwise. Then the non-zero forms
f_{m,\mu}
with
m\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\mu\in(L^{\prime}/L)/\{\pm 1\}
such that

form a basis of
M_{k,L}^{!}
.

Using the above basis for
M_{k,L}^{!}
we can explicitly construct
\xi
-preimages of cusp forms whose coefficients are given by regularized Petersson inner products

(2.5)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\left\langle f(\tau),\overline{g(\tau)}\right\rangle v^{k}\frac{dudv}{v^{2}},

where
\mathcal{F}_{T}=\{\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}:|\tau|\geq 1,|u|\leq 1/2,v\leq T\}
is a truncated fundamental domain for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
, and
f\in M_{k,L}^{!},g\in S_{k,L}
.
Theorem 2.14 in
[
ES18
]
gives us the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 2
and let
g\in S_{k,L}
be a cusp form of weight
k
for
\rho_{L}
. Then the generating series

\tilde{g}^{+}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Q}}\langle f_{m,\mu},g\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}\,q^{m}\phi_{\mu}

is the holomorphic part of a harmonic Maass form
\tilde{g}\in H_{2-k,L^{-}}
with
\xi_{2-k}(\tilde{g})=g
. Here
f_{m,\mu}\in M_{k,L}^{!}
are the modular forms constructed in Lemma
2.1
.

The above result also holds in the scalar-valued case. For the rest of this section, let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For any Laurent series
f
in
q^{1/N}
,
denote

\mathrm{prin}(f)
its principal part, which is a polynomial in
q^{-1/N}
.
For a harmonic Maass form
\tilde{f}\in H_{2-k}(\Gamma(N))
and a cusp form
g\in S_{k}(\Gamma(N))
, we denote

\mathrm{prin}(\tilde{f}\cdot g):=\mathrm{prin}(\tilde{f}^{+}\cdot g).

An application of Stokes’ theorem gives us

(2.6)

\begin{split}[{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma(N)]^{-1}\langle\xi_{2-k}(\tilde{f}),g\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}&=\sum_{c\in\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})}\mathrm{CT}(\mathrm{prin}(\tilde{f}\mid_{2-k}\gamma_{c})\cdot(g\mid_{k}\gamma_{c}))\\
&=\mathrm{CT}\left(\left(\mathrm{tr}^{N}_{1}(\tilde{f}\cdot g)\right)^{+}\right).\end{split}

Here
\gamma_{c}\in\Gamma(N)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is any element satisfying
\gamma(c)\cdot\infty=c
, and
the superscript

indicates the holomorphic part.
In particular, the sum on the right is always 0 when
\tilde{f}
is weakly holomorphic. Conversely, given any collection of principal parts
\{P_{c}:c\in\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\}
such that

\sum_{c\in\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})}\mathrm{CT}(P_{c}\cdot(g\mid_{k}\gamma_{c}))=0

for all
g\in S_{k}(\Gamma(N))
, then there exists
f\in M^{!}_{2-k}(\Gamma(N))
with
\mathrm{prin}(f\mid_{2-k}\gamma_{c})=P_{c}
.

2.2.
CM cusp forms

Let
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})\subset{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
be an imaginary quadratic field of fundamental discriminant
-D<0
. Let
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the fractional ideals of
E
coprime to a modulus
\mathfrak{m}
and

(2.7)

P_{\mathfrak{m},\mu}=\{\alpha\mathcal{O}_{E}\in I_{\mathfrak{m}}:\alpha\equiv\mu\bmod{\mathfrak{m}}\},\quad\mu\in(\mathcal{O}_{E}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}.

An algebraic Hecke character
\varrho
of weight
k\geq 1
is a multiplicative function on
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
satisfying

(2.8)

\varrho(\alpha\mathcal{O}_{E})=\alpha^{k-1},\quad\alpha\mathcal{O}_{E}\in P_{\mathfrak{m},1}\subset I_{\mathfrak{m}}.

We say that it has modulus
\mathfrak{m}
if
\mathfrak{m}
is the largest ideal such that (
2.8
) is satisfied.

The image of
\varrho
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)^{\times}
for a number field
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)\subset\mathbb{C}
, which contains
E
.
Note that
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)/\mathbb{Q}
is not necessarily Galois.
For any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
, the character

(2.9)

\varrho^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{a}):=(\varrho(\mathfrak{a}))^{\sigma}

is an algebraic Hecke character with the same modulus and infinity type as
\varrho
.
On the other hand for complex conjugation
\mathrm{c}\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, the character

(2.10)

\varrho^{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{a}):=\overline{\varrho(\overline{\mathfrak{a}})}

is an algebraic Hecke character modulo
\overline{\mathfrak{m}}
with the same infinity type as
\varrho
.
So for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
,
we set

(2.11)

\varrho^{\sigma}(\mathfrak{a}):=(\varrho(\sigma(\mathfrak{a}))^{\sigma},

which is an algebraic Hecke character of modulus
\sigma(\mathfrak{m})
and weight
k
.
This agrees with (
2.9
) when
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
since
\sigma(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a}
in that case.

The generating series

(2.12)

\vartheta_{\varrho}(\tau):=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}}\varrho(\mathfrak{a})q^{\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{a})}

is a CM newform in
S_{k}(N,\chi_{E}\eta)
with
N:=DM,M:=\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{m})
and

(2.13)

\chi_{E}(d):=\left(\frac{-D}{d}\right),\quad\eta(\alpha):=\frac{\varrho(\alpha\mathcal{O})}{\alpha^{k-1}},\quad\alpha\in E^{\times}.

Here
\chi_{E}\eta
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
of parity
(-1)^{k}
. For any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, we have

(2.14)

(\vartheta_{\varrho})^{\sigma}=\vartheta_{\varrho^{\sigma}}.

Let
F_{\varrho}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
denote the field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
\vartheta_{\varrho}
.
It is well-known
[
Rib77
, Proposition 3.2]
that
F_{\varrho}
is Kroneckerian, i.e. either totally real or a CM field, and contains the image of
\eta
.
We record here a result about the field
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
, which gives another proof of this fact.

Lemma 2.3
.

In the notations above, the extension
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)/F_{\varrho}
is Galois with Galois group an elementary 2-group.
Furthermore,
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
is Kroneckerian.

Proof.

By the Chebotarev Density Theorem,
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
is generated by
\varrho(\mathfrak{p})
over primes
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{d}
in
E
. Denote
c_{n}
the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
\vartheta_{\varrho}
.
If
p
is inert, then
\varrho(p\mathcal{O}_{E})=c_{p^{2}}\in F_{\varrho}
.
If
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
is split, then
\varrho(\mathfrak{p})+\varrho(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})=c_{p}
and
\varrho(\mathfrak{p})\varrho(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})=c_{p}^{2}-c_{p^{2}}
are in
F_{\varrho}
, which means
F_{\varrho}(\varrho(\mathfrak{p}))/F_{\varrho}
has degree at most 2.
Suppose
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)=F_{\varrho}(\varrho(\mathfrak{p}_{1}),\dots,\varrho(\mathfrak{p}_{r}))
, then
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)/F_{\varrho}
is the composite of the quadratic extensions
F_{\varrho}(\varrho(\mathfrak{p}_{i}))/F_{\varrho}
, hence Galois with elementary 2-group as its Galois group.

To prove the second claim, let
\mathfrak{p}
be an unramified prime in
E
.
We will show that

(2.15)

|\varrho(\mathfrak{p})^{\sigma}|^{2}=\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{p})^{k-1}

for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. Then Theorem 2 in
[
BL78
]
(see also section 4(a) loc. cit.) implies that
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho(\mathfrak{p}))
is Kroneckerian. Therefore as the composite of Kroneckerian fields,
\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
is also Kroneckerian. This gives another proof that
F_{\varrho}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\varrho)
is Kroneckerian.

To prove (
2.15
), suppose

3.
Main Results

3.1.
Algebraicity of regularized inner products.

In this section, we will prove a result about algebraicity and Galois action on ratios of certain regularized Petersson inner products involving binary theta series.
This result plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
since these inner products will appear as the Fourier coefficients of the holomorphic part of the harmonic Maass forms in Theorem
3.5
below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varrho
be a Hecke character of weight
k
for an imaginary quadratic field
E
.
For a lattice
P
in a quadratic space
U
as in (
2.19
), let
\Theta_{\varrho,P}\in S_{k,P}
be the vector-valued theta series in (
2.28
).
For any

defined over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
, the ratio

{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Furthermore,

(3.1)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\langle f(\tau),\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\|\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}\right)^{\sigma}&=\frac{\langle f^{\sigma}(\tau),\Theta_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}(\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\|\Theta_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}\end{split}

for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})

Proof.

Consider the unary lattice

with dual lattice
\frac{1}{12}\mathbb{Z}
.
From Lemma 2.1 in
[

]
, we have

Now for any

, we have

\displaystyle\langle f(\tau),\Theta_{P}(\tau,h)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\langle f_{\eta},\Theta_{P}(\tau,h)\otimes\overline{\theta_{N}(\tau)}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}=\frac{y_{0}^{1-k}}{4}\Phi_{L,k-1}(f_{\eta},z_{0},h)

where

is a lattice of signature
(1,2)
,

z_{0}=z_{0}^{+}\in\mathbb{D}_{L}
is the negative 2-plane associated with
U^{-}\otimes\mathbb{R}\subset L\otimes\mathbb{R}
and

Theorem
2.9
tells us that the function

is a meromorphic modular form of weight
2k-2
on the Shimura variety
X_{K(L)}
associated with

with

an open compact stabilizing

and acting trivially on
\hat{L}^{\prime}/\hat{L}
.
Furthermore, it has the same field of definition as that of

, hence also of

We can now write

(3.2)

\begin{split}\frac{\langle f(\tau),\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\|\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}&=\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z_{0},1),\\
\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z,h)&:=\frac{\sum_{h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\overline{\varrho(\overline{h^{\prime}}/h^{\prime})}\Phi_{L,k-1}(f_{\eta},z,hh^{\prime})}{\sum_{h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\overline{\varrho(\overline{h^{\prime}}/h^{\prime})}\Phi_{L,k-1}((\Theta_{\varrho,P})_{\eta},z,hh^{\prime})}\end{split}

with
\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z,h)
a meromorphic modular function on
X_{K(L)}
with poles away from
Z(U)
.
Furthermore,

\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z,1)=\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z,h)

for all
[z,h]\in X_{K(L)}
.
If
f
is defined over
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
, so is
f_{\eta}
and
\Psi_{\varrho,f}
. Then the special value
\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z_{0},h)
is in
{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
For
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}({\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
with image
\sigma_{h}\in{\mathrm{Gal}(E^{\mathrm{ab}}/E)}
for

, we can apply Remark

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\langle f(\tau),\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\|\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}\right)^{\sigma}&=\Psi_{\varrho,f}(z_{0},1)^{\sigma}=\Psi_{\varrho^{\sigma},f^{\sigma}}(z_{0},h^{-1})=\Psi_{\varrho^{\sigma},f^{\sigma}}(z_{0},1)\\
&=\frac{\langle f^{\sigma}(\tau),\Theta_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}(\tau)\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\|\Theta_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}.\end{split}

This holds similarly when

\mathrm{c}\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, hence (

∎

3.2.
Comparing Petersson norms

Now, we will compute
\|\theta_{U_{a},k}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}
for

and compare them with

varying.
The same calculations in the case of weight one newforms with CM by a real quadratic field are contained in

First, we identify

and

by

(3.3)

\begin{split}(1,1)&\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\quad(1,-1)\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right),\\
(\sqrt{D},\sqrt{D})&\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2D\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\quad(\sqrt{D},-\sqrt{D})\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
-2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).\end{split}

Notice we have the totally isotropic splitting

V_{a}=V_{a}^{+}+V_{a}^{-},\quad V_{a}^{\pm}:=\{(x,\pm x):x\in E\}\subset U_{a}\oplus U_{-a}.

Also, the linear map

&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)x

to
V_{a}
.
Let
L_{1}\subset V_{1}
be the unimodular lattice
M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and denote
L_{a}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)L_{1}\subset V_{a}
the unimodular lattice in
V_{a}
.

Let
z_{0}:=\mathbb{R}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)+\mathbb{R}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-D&\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{D}_{V_{a}}

So we can write

with
\phi^{(k,k)}=\phi\phi^{(k,k)}_{\infty}
for
\phi=\phi_{1}\otimes\overline{\phi_{2}}\in\mathcal{S}(\hat{U}_{a};\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\hat{U}_{-a};\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{S}(\hat{V}_{a};\mathbb{C})
and

.
By the Siegel-Weil formula, we have

2\int_{[{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}]}\theta_{V_{a}}(g,z,\phi^{(k,k)})dg=E^{H_{V_{a}}}([z,1],\hat{\phi}^{k})

, where
\hat{\phi}^{k}=\hat{\phi}\hat{\phi}_{\infty}^{k}\in\mathcal{S}((W\otimes V^{+}_{a})(\mathbb{A}))
is the Fourier transform of
\phi^{(k,k)}
, and
E^{H_{V_{a}}}
is the Eisenstein series induced from the parabolic
P^{+}
stabilizing

. Note that
E^{H_{V_{a}}}
a modular form of weight
(2k,0)
that is nearly holomorphic in
z_{1}
and holomorphic in
z_{2}
on the orthogonal Shimura variety

associated with
V_{a}
.
Note that the canonical isomorphism

induces a canonical isomorphism

for any

Also,

is isomorphic to disjoint unions of products of modular curves.
We now have the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every even integral lattice
P\subset U_{a}
and Hecke character
\varrho
of weight
k\geq 2
, there exists
\alpha_{\varrho,P}\in{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
\Omega_{\varrho,P}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

(3.6)

\alpha_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}=\alpha_{\varrho,P}^{\sigma},\qquad\Omega_{\varrho,P}=\frac{\|\Theta_{\varrho,P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\alpha_{\varrho,P}}=\frac{\|\Theta_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\alpha^{\sigma}_{\varrho,P}}=\Omega_{\varrho^{\sigma},P},

for every

with

Remark 3.3
.

When

is replaced by a newform, the corresponding result follows from Deligne’s conjecture for symmetric square

-function proved by Sturm
[

,
Stu89
]
. However, this does not directly imply Prop.

since it is not clear whether the ratio of

Proof.

For convenience, denote

(3.8)

and
\omega_{\varrho}:=\omega_{\varrho,\mathcal{O}_{E}}

.

Now for any

, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\langle\theta_{\varrho}(\tau,\phi),\theta_{\varrho}(\tau,\phi^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\omega_{\varrho,P}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}\langle\theta_{U_{a},k}(\tau,\omega_{a}(h_{1})\phi),\theta_{U_{a},k}(\tau,\omega_{a}(h_{2})\phi^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}\sum_{\mu\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{E}}\langle\theta_{U_{1},k}(\tau,\omega_{1}(h_{1})\phi_{\mu}),\theta_{U_{1},k}(\tau,\omega_{1}(h_{2})\phi_{\mu})\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}E^{H_{V_{a}}}([z_{0},(h_{1},h_{2})],\widehat{\phi\otimes\phi^{\prime}}^{k})}{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}E^{H_{V_{1}}}([z_{0},(h_{1},h_{2})],\hat{\phi}^{k})}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{\varrho}(z_{0},1;\widehat{\phi\otimes\phi^{\prime}}^{k}),

where

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{\varrho}(z,h;\varphi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}E^{H_{V_{a}}}([z,h(h_{1},h_{2})],\varphi^{k})}{\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathrm{Cl}(K_{\mathrm{f}})}\varrho(\overline{h_{1}}/h_{1})\overline{\varrho(\overline{h_{2}}/h_{2})}E^{H_{V_{1}}}([z,h(h_{1},h_{2})],\hat{\phi}_{1}^{k})}

So

is a ratio of nearly holomorphic modular forms on

, and reciprocity law still applies
[
Shi75
]
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
This gives us

(3.10)

\left(\frac{\omega_{\varrho,P}}{\omega_{\varrho}}\right)^{\sigma}=\frac{\omega_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}}{\omega_{\varrho^{\sigma}}}.

for all
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})
, since

is

-valued when

In particular,

By a similar argument, we can show that

(3.11)

\left(\frac{\omega_{\varrho^{\sigma},P}}{\omega_{\varrho,P}}\right)^{\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{\omega_{\varrho^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},P}}{\omega_{\varrho^{\sigma^{\prime}},P}}

So

Setting

Substituting (

∎

In this section we illustrate our construction of
\xi
-preimages of CM newforms in a numerical example. We consider the unique normalized newform

of weight

of weight

\xi
-preimage for

\xi

Since

and

[

using a different method. At the end of this section we will compare our construction to the harmonic Maass form from

As

, which is given in (

By Proposition
2.2
, the generating series

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}_{P,4}^{+}(z)=\sum_{\mu\in P^{\prime}/P}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Q}}\frac{\langle f_{m,\mu},\Theta_{P,4}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}{\langle\Theta_{P,4},\Theta_{P,4}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}}q^{m}\phi_{\mu}

is the holomorphic part of a harmonic Maass form
\tilde{\Theta}_{P,4}\in H_{-2,P^{-}}
with
\xi_{-2}\tilde{\Theta}_{P,4}=\Theta_{P,4}/\|\Theta_{P,4}\|_{\mathrm{Pet}}^{2}
. Here the forms

are the generating set of

from Lemma

of holomorphic modular forms is one-dimensional, and spanned by

. In particular, the forms

are given by

\displaystyle f_{m}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}q^{-m}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{-1})+O(q^{4/3}),\qquad(m\geq-1/3,\ m\equiv 2/3\pmod{1}).

Explicitly, for

f_{-1/3}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\eta(\tau)^{8}(\phi_{1}+\phi_{-1})

and for

f_{n}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}P_{n+1/3}(j(\tau))\cdot\eta(\tau)^{8}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{-1})

with certain polynomials

, the first few of which are given by

\displaystyle P_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=1,\quad P_{1}(X)=X-736,\quad P_{2}(X)=X^{2}-1480X+153860.

4.1.
Petersson inner products as special values of theta lifts

Now we evaluate the inner product
\langle f_{m,\mu},\Theta_{P,4}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}
appearing in (

, that is, to compute
\langle f_{m},\Theta_{P,4}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}
for
m\geq-1/3
with

. In the proof of Proposition

we evaluated these inner products by multiplying

with
\eta^{-1}(\tau)\phi_{\eta}
and thereby viewing
\langle f_{m},\Theta_{P,4}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}
as a special value of the Borcherds-Shimura theta lift of

on the signature
(1,2)
lattice
\mathbb{Z}[6]\oplus P^{-}
, where

is anisotropic, so we cannot directly work with the Fourier expansion of the Borcherds-Shimura lift. Hence it will be more convenient to choose a different function than

(1,2)

[

]
and Sections 2.7 and 4.1 in
[

Consider the (scalar-valued) weakly holomorphic modular form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{10}}{\eta(2\tau)^{5}\eta(4\tau)^{6}}+20\frac{\eta(\tau)^{2}\eta(4\tau)^{2}}{\eta(2\tau)^{5}}

\displaystyle\quad=q^{-1}+10-64q^{3}+108q^{4}-513q^{7}+808q^{8}-2752q^{11}+4016q^{12}+\dots

[

]
).

Following Section 7.1 in
[

]
we consider the (isotropic) lattice

c&-b\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

. Its discriminant group is isomorphic to

acts on it by conjugation. The vector

We consider the positive and negative definite sublattices

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=L\cap U^{\perp}=2\lambda_{0}\mathbb{Z},\qquad\mathcal{N}=L\cap U=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}b&(a-b)/3\\

This is the desired embedding of

A short computation shows that

y_{U}^{-2}\left(\lambda,\begin{pmatrix}-\overline{z}_{U}&\overline{z}_{U}^{2}\\
-1&\overline{z}_{U}\end{pmatrix}\right)=12(a\zeta+b)

4.2.
Evaluation of theta lifts

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-6i)^{-3}\Phi_{L,3}(f_{2/3}\otimes\mathcal{F},z)=-32q-182q^{2}-288q^{3}+983876q^{4}-3659968q^{5}+\dots

Unfortunately, this Fourier expansion is only valid for

into this expansion. Hence, we want to write the lift as a rational function in weakly holomorphic modular forms. The Borcherds-Shimura lift is a meromorphic modular form of weight

\displaystyle\frac{\eta(3z)^{2}}{\eta(z)^{3}\eta(9z)^{3}}-3\frac{\eta(z)^{3}\eta(9z)^{3}}{\eta(3z)^{10}}-18\frac{\eta(9z)^{6}}{\eta(3z)^{10}}

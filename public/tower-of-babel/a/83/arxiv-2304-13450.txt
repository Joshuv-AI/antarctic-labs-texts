A solution to a question by A.Wigderson and Y.Wigderson
Yiyu Tang

Abstract




1
Introduction

In this note, we give a solution to a little conjecture proposed by A. Wigderson and Y. Wigderson when they consider a family of "Heisenberg-like" uncertainty principle in their paper
[
3
]

Question
(
[
3
]
, Conjecture 4.13).
For
1<q\leq\infty
and
q\neq 2
, define
F_{q}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by

\displaystyle F_{q}(f):=\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}}

Is
F_{q}
surjective? i.e. is the image of
F_{q}
all of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
? Here,
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is the Schwartz space on real line, and
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform defined on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
by
\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}x\xi}\mathrm{d}x
.
Finally, notation
||f||_{q}
is the
L^{q}
norm of
f
:
||f||_{q}=\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\big)^{q}
.

The classical Heisenberg uncertainty principle says
||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}\leq 4\pi(\int|xf(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x)^{1/2}(\int|\xi\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi)^{1/2}
when
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. If, for example, the image of
F_{q}
is bounded below by
c>0
, then it tells us
||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}\leq\frac{1}{c}||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}
, this could be considered as a variant of classical uncertainty principle.

We answer this question affirmatively when
2<q<\infty
(the case
q=\infty
has been proved in
[
3
]
), and negatively when
1<q<2
. The author got this result in May 2021, and presented it in a conference
ComPlane: the next generation
in June 17-18, 2021, see
[
4
]
. The same result was also obtained in 19th July, 2021 by three people, Linzhe Huang, Zhengwei Liu, Jinsong Wu, see
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
2<q<\infty
, then the image of
F_{q}
is all
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. If
1<q<2
, then the image of
F_{q}
is bounded below by
1
, i.e.
F_{q}(f)\geq 1
for any
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
.

Also, we can consider the general pair
(q,p)
instead of
(q,2)
. This is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assuming
1<q<p<\infty
, and consider the mapping

F_{q,p}(f)=\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||_{p}||\hat{f}||_{p}},\ f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}.

(i) If
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
, then image of
F_{q,p}
is an infinite subinterval of
[1,\infty)
.

(ii) If
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<1
, then the image of
F_{q,p}
is
(0,\infty)
.

Remark 1.3
.

During the proof of the theorem, one can easily find that the bound
1
is far from better. In fact, we use the Hausdorff-Young inequality several times in the proof, which says that
||\hat{f}||_{p^{\prime}}\leq||f||_{p}
when
p\in[1,2]
. However, the sharp inequality should be
||\hat{f}||_{p^{\prime}}\leq\sqrt{(p^{1/p})/(p^{\prime})^{1/p^{\prime}}}||f||_{p}
. This is a famous theorem due to W. Beckner. Maybe find the infimum of image of
F_{q}
is more difficult than determine whether
F_{q}
is surjective.

2
Several facts

We recall the topology on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, for
\alpha,\ \beta\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(f)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big|x^{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\beta}}{\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}}f(x)\big|
. Let
\{\rho_{j}\}_{j}
be an enumeration of
\{\rho_{\alpha,\beta}\}_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}}
, the following is a metric on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
:

d(f,g)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\frac{\rho_{j}(f-g)}{1+\rho_{j}(f-g)}.

Under this metric, space
(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}),d)
is complete, so
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is a Frechet space. A sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is said to converges to an
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, if
d(f_{n},f)
goes to
0
when
n
tends to infinity.

The following facts are basic properties of
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
and Fourier transform on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. All of them can be found in any textbook of Fourier analysis.

Fact 2.1
.

Firstly, the Fourier transform
f\mapsto\hat{f}
is a homeomorphism on
\mathscr{S}
to itself.
Secondly, suppose that functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
f
belong to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, if
f_{n}
converges to
f
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f_{n}
converges to
f
in
L^{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Therefore, the function
f\mapsto||f||_{p}
is continuous on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

For a proof, see
[
1
]
, page 106, Proposition 2.2.6 and page 113, Corollary 2.2.15.
∎

Combining these facts, the mapping
f\mapsto||\hat{f}||_{p}
is continuous as a composition of two continuous mappings, so the mapping
f\mapsto F_{q}(f)
is continuous from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. As a metric space,
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
is connected, since
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is a Frechet space with
\dim\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})=\infty
. Recall that between two metric spaces, continuous image of a connected set is also connected, and connected sets in
\mathbb{R}
are intervals, we conclude that

Proposition 2.2
.

The image of
F_{q}
is an interval on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Now we only need to study the endpoint of
Im(F_{q})
. But firstly, let’s see what could we get by using some simple functions to calculate
F_{q}
. We use the same construction in
[
3
]
.

For
a>1
, define functions
f_{a}
by

f_{a}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(a^{2}-1)x^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}ax^{2}}.

Clearly, functions
f_{a}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, and one can calculate the Fourier transform of
f_{a}
:

\hat{f}_{a}(\xi)=\frac{1}{a+\mathrm{i}}\exp\bigg(\frac{-\pi\xi^{2}(a^{2}-1)}{(a^{2}+1)^{2}}\bigg)\exp\bigg(-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi^{2}\frac{a}{(a^{2}+1)^{2}}\bigg).

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x=1
, we get, for any
1\leq q<\infty
,

\big|\big|f_{a}\big|\big|_{L^{q}}=\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q(a^{2}-1)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},\ \big|\big|\hat{f}_{a}\big|\big|_{L^{q}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}}\bigg(\frac{a^{2}+1}{\sqrt{q(a^{2}-1)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore, one calculates
F_{q}
precisely:

F_{q}(f_{a})=\frac{||f_{a}||_{q}||\hat{f}_{a}||_{q}}{||f_{a}||^{2}}=\sqrt{2}\ \bigg(\frac{1}{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\frac{a^{2}+1}{a^{2}-1}\bigg)^{1/q-1/2}=\sqrt{2}\ \bigg(\frac{1}{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\bigg)^{1/q-1/2},\ a^{2}=t>1.

We notice that the mapping
t\mapsto\frac{t+1}{t-1}
is decreasing when
t>1
, so if
1/q-1/2>0
, the image of the mapping
t\mapsto(\frac{t+1}{t-1})^{1/q-1/2},\ t>1

is
(1,\infty)
. Similarly, if
1/q-1/2<0
, then image of the above mapping is
(0,1)
. In conclusion, by testing functions
f_{a}
on
F_{q}
, we have:

Proposition 2.3
.

(i) If
1<q<2
, then at least
\big(\sqrt{2}(\frac{1}{q})^{\frac{1}{q}},\infty\big)\subset Im(F_{q})
.

(ii) If
2<q<\infty
, then at least
\big(0,\sqrt{2}(\frac{1}{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\big)\subset Im(F_{q})
.

3
The case
2<q<\infty

In this section, we always assume that
2<q<\infty
, and we will prove that
Im(F_{q})=(0,\infty)
. By Proposition
2.3
, we only need to construct a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, so that
F_{q}(f_{n})\rightarrow\infty
as
n
goes to infinity. We use the same construction in
[
3
]
.

For each
c>0
, define the function

g_{c}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}}}+\sqrt{c}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi c^{2}x^{2}}.

Clearly, functions
g_{c}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
\hat{g_{c}}=g_{c}
for all
c>0
, so
F_{q}(g_{c})=\big(\frac{||g_{c}||_{q}}{||g_{c}||_{2}}\big)^{2}
. A direct calculation shows that

||g_{c}||^{2}_{2}=\sqrt{2}+\frac{2c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}},\ c>0.

(1)

Now we estimate
||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}
, by definition

\displaystyle||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}=\bigg\{\int\bigg(\frac{1}{c}\ \mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}}}+c\ \mathrm{e}^{-2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}+2\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(c^{2}+1/c^{2})x^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}.

Notice that
\big(\sum_{i}a_{i}\big)^{q/2}\geq\sum_{i}a_{i}^{q/2}
when
q/2>1
, therefore

\displaystyle||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}\geq

\displaystyle\bigg\{\int\bigg(\frac{1}{c}\bigg)^{q/2}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi q\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}}}+c^{q/2}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi qc^{2}x^{2}}+2^{q/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-\pi q}{2}(c^{2}+1/c^{2})x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{1}{c}\bigg)^{q/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q/c^{2}}}+c^{q/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{qc^{2}}}+2^{q/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q(c^{2}+1/c^{2})/2}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}.

In conclusion, for all
c>0
:

||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}\geq\bigg\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{1-\frac{q}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{\frac{q}{2}-1}+\frac{2^{(q+1)/2}}{\sqrt{q}}\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\bigg)^{2/q}=\bigg(\frac{1}{q}\bigg)^{1/q}c^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.

(3)

Combining (
1
) and (
3
), we get

F_{q}(g_{c})\geq\bigg(\frac{1}{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\frac{c^{1-\frac{2}{q}}}{\sqrt{2}+\frac{2c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}}.

Finally, let
c\rightarrow\infty
, and we have
F_{q}(g_{c})\rightarrow\infty
.

4
The case
1<q<2

In this section, we always assume that
1<q<2
. In fact, the case
q<2
is easier. Let
q^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
q
. Hölder inequality implies

||f||_{2}\leq||f||^{1/2}_{q}||f||^{1/2}_{q^{\prime}}.

(4)

Finally, recall the Hausdorff-Young inequality, when
1\leq q\leq 2
, we have

||\hat{f}||_{q\prime}\leq||f||_{q},\ f\in\mathscr{S}.

Therefore, we choose
g=\hat{f}\in\mathscr{S}
and we have
||\hat{g}||_{q^{\prime}}\leq||g||_{q}
, i.e.

||f||^{1/2}_{q^{\prime}}\leq||\hat{f}||^{1/2}_{q}.

(5)

Combining these inequalities, we get

||f||_{2}\leq||f||^{1/2}_{q}||\hat{f}||^{1/2}_{q},

which is to say
\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||^{2}_{2}}\geq 1
for any
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
.

5
General
p,q

In this section, we discuss a general case

F_{q,p}(f)=\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||_{p}||\hat{f}||_{p}},\ 1<q<p<\infty.

Firstly, let’s see what could we get by using special functions. We still use the functions
f_{a}
in Section 2. Recall

\displaystyle f_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(a^{2}-1)x^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}ax^{2}},\ a>1.

A direct calculation shows that for each
1<q<\infty
,

\displaystyle||f_{a}||_{q}=\bigg(\frac{1}{q(a^{2}-1)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2q}},\ ||\hat{f}_{a}||_{q}=(a^{2}+1)^{-1/2}\bigg(\frac{a^{2}+1}{\sqrt{q(a^{2}-1)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Replacing
q
by
p
, we have

F_{q,p}(f_{a})=\frac{(1/q)^{1/q}}{(1/p)^{1/p}}\bigg(\frac{a^{2}+1}{a^{2}-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{(1/q)^{1/q}}{(1/p)^{1/p}}\bigg(\frac{t+1}{t-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}},\ t=a^{2}>1.

So at least
\bigg(\frac{(1/q)^{1/q}}{(1/p)^{1/p}},\infty\bigg)\subset Im(F_{q,p})
.

5.1
Theorem
1.2
, case (i)

Now we prove (i) of Theorem
1.2
:
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
. Note that
q<2
always holds in this case. Dividing it into two sub-cases:
q<p<2
and
q<2\leq p
. Two sub-cases are a little bit different, although results are the same.

5.1.1
When
q<p<2

Writing
p
as a convex combination of
q
and
2
:

p=\lambda q+(1-\lambda)2,\ 0<\lambda<1.

Then
\int|f|^{p}=\int|f|^{\lambda q}|f|^{(1-\lambda)2}
. Notice that
\frac{1}{\lambda}\in(1,\infty)
, by Hölder’s inequality (
\lambda+(1-\lambda)=1
), we have

\displaystyle||f||_{p}\leq||f||^{\frac{1/p-1/2}{1/q-1/2}}_{q}||f||^{\frac{1/q-1/p}{1/q-1/2}}_{2}.

(6)

Inequality (
6
) also holds for
\hat{f}
,

\displaystyle||\hat{f}||_{p}\leq||\hat{f}||^{\frac{1/p-1/2}{1/q-1/2}}_{q}||\hat{f}||^{\frac{1/q-1/p}{1/q-1/2}}_{2}.

(7)

Combining the above two inequalities and definition of
F_{q,p}
, we have

F_{q,p}(f)\geq\Bigg(\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1/q-1/p}{1/q-1/2}}=\big(F_{q}(f)\big)^{\frac{1/q-1/p}{1/q-1/2}}.

Finally, notice that
\frac{1/q-1/p}{1/q-1/2}>0
, so if
1<q<p<2
, then we come back to the case
1<q<2,\ p=2
in Section
4
, which asserts that image of
F_{q}
is bounded below by
1
. Therefore, the image of
F_{q,p}
is bounded below by
1
.

5.1.2
When
q<2\leq p

Since
1<p^{\prime}\leq 2
, by Hausdorff-Young inequality
||\hat{f}||_{p}\leq||f||_{p^{\prime}}.

This shows that

\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||f||_{p}||\hat{f}||_{p}}\geq\frac{||f||_{q}||\hat{f}||_{q}}{||\hat{f}||_{p^{\prime}}||f||_{p^{\prime}}}=F_{q,p^{\prime}}(f).

Now we come back to the case in subsection 5.1.1, because
1<q\leq p^{\prime}<2
.

5.2
Theorem
1.2
, case (ii)

Now we discuss the case
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<1
. Also, remember that
q<p
, therefore
p>2
. Here we use the same function
g_{c}
in section 3. Recall that for
c>0
,

g_{c}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi\frac{x^{2}}{c^{2}}}+\sqrt{c}\ \mathrm{e}^{-\pi c^{2}x^{2}},\ g_{c}=\hat{g_{c}}.

The inequality (
2
) shows that (
p>2
)

||g_{c}||^{2}_{p}\geq\bigg\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}c^{1-\frac{p}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}c^{\frac{p}{2}-1}+\frac{2^{(p+1)/2}}{\sqrt{p}}\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{p}}\thicksim\bigg(\frac{c^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{\sqrt{p}}\bigg)^{2/p},\ \text{as\ }c\rightarrow\infty.

Similarly, for
q>1
, we have
(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq\max\{1,3^{q/2-1}\}\bigg(a_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}}+a_{2}^{\frac{q}{2}}+a_{3}^{\frac{q}{2}}\bigg)
, so we just reverse the inequality (
2
) and get

\displaystyle||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}\leq 4,\ \text{when\ }q=2.

\displaystyle||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}\leq

\displaystyle\bigg\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{1-\frac{q}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{\frac{q}{2}-1}+\frac{2^{(q+1)/2}}{\sqrt{q}}\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}\thicksim\bigg(\frac{c^{1-\frac{q}{2}}}{\sqrt{q}}\bigg)^{2/q},\ \text{as\ }c\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{when\ }q<2.

\displaystyle||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}\leq

\displaystyle 3^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{1-\frac{q}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}c^{\frac{q}{2}-1}+\frac{2^{(q+1)/2}}{\sqrt{q}}\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^{4}+1}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{q}}\thicksim 3^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\bigg(\frac{c^{\frac{q}{2}-1}}{\sqrt{q}}\bigg)^{2/q},\ \text{as\ }c\rightarrow\infty\ \text{when\ }q>2.

Consequently, as
c
goes to infinity, we have
||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}/||g_{c}||^{2}_{p}\leq C_{p,q}\ c^{2(1/q+1/p-1)}
when
q\leq 2
and
||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}/||g_{c}||^{2}_{p}\leq C_{p,q}\ c^{2(1/p-1/q)}
when
q>2
, where
C_{p,q}
depends only on
p
and
q
. Finally, just note that
1/q+1/p-1<0
and
1/p-1/q<0
, so
\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}||g_{c}||^{2}_{q}/||g_{c}||^{2}_{p}=0
.

Deformations of a Gorenstein toric pair
Matej Filip

Abstract
. We consider deformations of a pair ( X , ∂ X ) (X,\partial X) , where X X is an affine toric Gorenstein variety and ∂ X \partial X is its boundary. We compute the tangent and obstruction space for the corresponding deformation functor and for an admissible lattice degree m m we construct the miniversal deformation of ( X , ∂ X ) (X,\partial X) in degrees − k ​ m -km , for all k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} together. This in particular generalizes Altmann’s construction of the miniversal deformation of an isolated Gorenstein toric singularity to an arbitrary non-isolated Gorenstein toric singularity. We see that it is more natural to work with deformations of a pair ( X , ∂ X ) (X,\partial X) rather than only with deformations of X X , since many computations are in fact easier.



1.
Introduction

Mirror symmetry suggests that there is a relationship between Fano manifolds and certain Laurent polynomials, cf.
[
7
]
.
More precisely, if a Laurent polynomial
f
is mirror to a Fano manifold
Y
, it is expected that a Fano manifold
Y
admits a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degeneration to a singular toric variety, whose fan is the spanning fan of the Newton polytope
\Delta(f)
.
Thus studying deformations of toric varieties is important for classifying Fano varieties, which has become an extensive research area in recent years.

Altmann
[
1
]
constructed the miniversal deformation space of an isolated affine Gorenstein toric singularity and in
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
4
]
his work was generalized to some other cases that will be described more in detail below.
The deformations of the boundary
\partial X
of a toric variety (or more generally the deformations of toroidal crossing spaces, which are locally isomorphic to boundary divisors of toric varieties) are studied using log geometry and appear also in the Gross-Siebert program, cf.
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
.

Mirror symmetry also suggests to work with deformations of
(X,\partial X)
, which means deformations of a closed embedding
\partial X\hookrightarrow X
, instead of only working with deformations of
X
or
\partial X
, see e.g.
[
8
]
, where we analyse the case when
X
is a three-dimensional affine Gorenstein toric variety (having possibly non-isolated singularities).
In this case we state a conjecture that there is a canonical bijective correspondence between the set of smoothing components of
X
(which is the same as the set of smoothing components of
(X,\partial X)
) and the set of certain Laurent polynomials.

It is well known that Gorenstein toric Fano varieties are in correspondence with reflexive polytopes. By comparison theorem of Kleppe
[
13
]
, the deformations of Gorenstein toric Fano varieties can be obtained by (degree
0
) deformations of their affine cones, which are affine Gorenstein toric varieties. Some of the deformations that we construct in this paper also induce deformations of the corresponding Gorenstein toric Fano varieties as we will see below.

From now on let
X
be an affine Gorenstein toric variety, given by a rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset\tilde{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\tilde{N}
is a finite dimensional lattice. There exists
R^{*}\in\tilde{M}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\tilde{N},\mathbb{Z})
such that the integral generators of
\sigma
lie on an affine hyperplane
(R^{*}=1)
and thus
(R^{*}=1)\cap\sigma
is a lattice polytope that we denote by
P
.
The torus action on
X
induces a lattice grading on the tangent space
T^{1}_{X}
(resp.
T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}
) and the obstruction space
T^{2}_{X}
(resp.
T^{2}_{(X,\partial X)}
) of the deformation functor of
X
(resp.
(X,\partial X)
).
If
X
has an isolated singularity, then
T^{1}_{X}
is finite dimensional and lies in the single lattice degree
-R^{*}
.
For an arbitrary (having non-isolated singularities)
X
the
tangent spaces
T^{1}_{X}
and
T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}
are infinite-dimensional. For definition and basic properties of the miniversal deformation space of
(X,\partial X)
in the three-dimensional case see e.g.
[
8
, Remark 2.1]
. In this paper we focus on special finite-dimensional parts of the tangent space
T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}
and thus avoid problems that come with infinite dimension. More precisely, let
m\in\tilde{M}
be such that it achieves value
1
on some face
G
of
P
and it has value strictly less than
1
for any lattice point on
P
lying outside
G
, cf. (
10
).
For any such
m
we are going to construct a deformation of
(X,\partial X)
which is maximal with prescribed tangent space
\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)\subset T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}
, i.e. we cannot extend it to a deformation of
(X,\partial X)
with a larger base space by keeping the tangent space fixed. We say that this deformation is miniversal in degrees
-km
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, see Definition
5.1
and Remark
5.2
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we thus get a homogenous deformation in degree
-km
. We can also present those homogenous deformations in functorial lagnuage and the differential graded Lie algebra that controls it is the Harrison differential graded Lie algebra restricted to the desired degrees, see
[
10
]
.

In the following we are going to emphasize the main differences between this paper and the papers
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
which also construct some miniversal deformation spaces. Since
X
is Gorenstein, the results of loc. cit. can only be applied to the case when
P
has edges of lattice length
1
. More precisely, they all consider homogenous deformations in a primitive lattice degree
-m\in\tilde{M}
with an assumption that
m\in\sigma^{\vee}
and some other assumptions on the edges of
\sigma\cap(m=1)
, which imply that the results in the Gorenstein case work only for
m=R^{*}
and
P
having all edges of lattice length
1
, which is equivalent to
X
being smooth in codimension
2
. The assumption
m\in\sigma^{\vee}
in loc. cit. is very crucial, since the homogenous deformation in degree
-m
is constructed by proving flatness of some semigroups and this cannot be obtained if
m\not\in\sigma^{\vee}
.
In the Fano classification problems mentioned above it is crucial to understand deformations of
X
with arbitrary lattice polytope
P
.

In this paper we drop both of the assumptions made in the papers loc. cit. which means we condsider also degrees
m\not\in\sigma^{\vee}
and
P
arbitrary. Note that when considering deformations of the affine cones in order to deform the corresponding Fano Gorenstein toric variety, we need to have
m\not\in\sigma^{\vee}
(and of degree
0
) since otherwise we only get deformations in the affine case. In Remark
5.7
we describe the cases where our deformations deform also the corresponding Fano Gorenstein toric variety, which is not possible with the results obtained in loc. cit.

We prove flatness directly by lifting relations among equations of
X
and not by bypassing the problem to flatness of semigroups. This in particular enables us to work also with homogenous deformations in degrees
-m
with
m\not\in\sigma^{\vee}
. We still need to generalize some properties of the semigroups appearing in loc. cit., which is done in Section
2
and the proof that our constructed family is flat is done in Proposition
3.7
. Since
P
has edges of arbitrary lattice length we see that it is more natural to consider all homogenous deformations in degrees
-km
together for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that if
P
has edges of lattice length
1
, then
T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)=T^{1}_{X}(-km)=0
for all
k\geq 2
and thus we only get a homogenous deformation in degree
-m
.

Moreover, considering the deformations of
(X,\partial X)
instead of only
X
in fact simplifies the construction since the tangent space of the naturally constructed deformation family has dimension equal to

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)=1+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{X}(-km).

In fact also in Atmann’s isolated case (with
m=R^{*}
) the deformation family, constructed from the above mentioned semigroups, has its tangent space of dimension
1+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T^{1}_{X}=1+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T^{1}_{X}(-R^{*})
and thus non-trivial computations needed to be done to get the tangent space of the right dimension and then prove the bijectivity of the Kodaira-Spencer map and the same problem occurs also in loc. cit. In this paper we see that even if we are only interested in deformations of
X
it is better to consider deformations of
(X,\partial X)
and then apply the obvious forgetful functor.

Finally, to prove miniversality, we prove that the Kodaira-Spencer map of our constructed deformation family is bijective in Section
4
and that the obstruction map is surjective in Section
5
. This is done in a completely different way than in the previously mentioned papers. We conclude the paper by discussing how the admissible Minkowski decompositions of
P\cap(m=1)
are connected to irreducible components of the constructed deformation of
(X,\partial X)
and previously mentioned conjecture about smoothing components in
[
8
]
.

The miniversal deformation

In the following we are going to show that the deformation diagram (

We will see that the map

The following definition is inspired by
[

, see also Remark

.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a deformation

of
X
is
miniversal (resp. versal) in degrees
-km
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\tilde{M}
if

(1)

The Kodaira-Spencer map
T_{0}\mathcal{S}\to\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{X}(-km)\subset T^{1}_{X}
is bijective (resp. surjective).

(2)

The obstruction map of
f
is surjective.

We say that a deformation diagram (
9
) is
miniversal in degrees
-km
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
if

(1)

The Kodaira-Spencer map
T_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}\to\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)\subset T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}
is bijective.

(2)

The obstruction map of the map
f_{2}
is surjective.

For the definition of the obstruction map and its main applications (in versality problems) we refer to
[
12
, Section 10]
(see also
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
).

Remark 5.2
.

If a deformation
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}
of
X
, with the tangent space of
\mathcal{S}
isomorphic to
\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{X}(-km)
, is miniversal in degrees
-km
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we cannot extend the map
f
to a flat map with a larger base space (by keeping the tangent space fixed). Similarly, if a deformation diagram (
9
), with the tangent space of
\widetilde{S}
isomorphic to
\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)
, is miniversal in degrees
-km
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we cannot extend it to a deformation diagram with a larger base space (by keeping the tangent space fixed).
Moreover, we only require that the obstruction map of
f_{2}
is surjective, because if we can extend the map
f_{2}
to a larger base space, then we can also extend the whole deformation diagram of
(X,\partial X)
to a larger base space by Remark
4.3
. In particular, the miniversal deformation base space (if exists) of
(X,\partial X)
has its tangent space equal to
T^{1}_{X}
and by restricting it to
\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}T^{1}_{(X,\partial X)}(-km)
we obtain the miniversal deformation in degrees
-km
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that there might be some issues with algebraic definition of the miniversal deformation of
(X,\partial X)
if
\dim X>3
, cf.
[
8
, Remark 2.1]
.

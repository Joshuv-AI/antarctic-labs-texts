Ontology-Mediated Querying on Databases of Bounded Cliquewidth
Carsten Lutz, Leif Sabellek, Lukas Schulze

Abstract
We study the evaluation of ontology-mediated queries (OMQs) on databases of bounded cliquewidth from the viewpoint of parameterized complexity theory. As the ontology language, we consider the description logics 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 \mathcal{ALC} and 𝒜 ​ ℒ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℐ \mathcal{ALCI} as well as the guarded two-variable fragment GF 2 of first-order logic. Queries are atomic queries (AQs), conjunctive queries (CQs), and unions of CQs. All studied OMQ problems are fixed-parameter linear (FPL) when the parameter is the size of the OMQ plus the cliquewidth. Our main contribution is a detailed analysis of the dependence of the running time on the parameter, exhibiting several interesting effects.

Introduction

Ontology-mediated querying is an established subfield of knowledge
representation. The general aim is to enrich a database with an
ontology to add domain knowledge and to extend the vocabulary
available for query formulation

[
\citeauthoryearBienvenu et al.2014
,
\citeauthoryearBienvenu and Ortiz2015
,
\citeauthoryearCalvanese et al.2009
]
. Expressive
description logics (DLs) such as
\mathcal{ALC}
and
\mathcal{ALCI}
are a popular choice
for the ontology language as they underpin the widely used OWL DL
profile of the OWL 2 ontology language. However, ontology-mediated
querying with these languages is
coNP
-complete in data
complexity even for simple atomic queries (AQs) of the form
A(x)

and thus scales poorly to larger amounts of data. One reaction to this
problem is to resort to approximate query answers, as done for example
in
[
\citeauthoryearZhou et al.2015
,
\citeauthoryearHaga et al.2021
]
. A
potential alternative is to only admit databases from a class that is
sufficiently restricted so that non-approximate answers can be
computed in
PTime
, or to decompose the input database into component
databases from such a class.

The aim of this paper is to study ontology-mediated querying on
classes of databases of bounded cliquewidth. This is relevant because
such classes are maximal known ones on which ontology-mediated
querying with DLs such as
\mathcal{ALC}
and
\mathcal{ALCI}
is in
PTime
in data
complexity, even when unions of conjunctive queries (UCQs) are
admitted as queries. This follows from a folklore translation of
ontology-mediated querying into MSO
1
and the known result that
MSO
1
has
PTime
data complexity on structures of bounded
cliquewidth
[
\citeauthoryearCourcelle, Makowsky, and
Rotics2000
]
. In fact, it even
has linear time data complexity (when a
k
-expression for
constructing the database is provided). We recall that bounded
treewidth implies bounded cliquewidth since every database of
treewidth
k
has cliquewidth at most
3\cdot 2^{k-1}

[
\citeauthoryearCorneil and
Rotics2005
]
. The converse is false because
databases of bounded treewidth are sparse in the sense of
graph theory, whereas databases of bounded cliquewidth may be dense.

We do not claim that databases encountered in practical applications
typically have small cliquewidth. In fact, an empirical study of
real-world databases has found that their treewidth tends to be high

[
\citeauthoryearManiu, Senellart, and
Jog2019
]
, and one may expect similar results
for cliquewidth. The same study, however, also points out the
opportunities that lie in the decomposition of a database into a part
of high treewidth and parts of low treewidth, and this has in fact
been used successfully to implement efficient querying

[
\citeauthoryearWei2010
,
\citeauthoryearAkiba, Sommer, and
Kawarabayashi2012
,
\citeauthoryearManiu, Cheng, and
Senellart2017
]
. While
we are not aware that similar approaches based on cliquewidth have yet
been studied, it seems entirely reasonable to pursue them. The work
presented here may provide a foundation for such an
endeavour.

In this paper, we focus on the framework of parameterized complexity
theory. Linear time data complexity with a uniform algorithm trivially
implies
fixed-parameter linearity (FPL)
when the
parameter is the size of the ontology-mediated query (OMQ). Recall
that FPL is defined like the more familiar
fixed-parameter
tractability (FPT)
except that the running time may depend only
linearly on the size of the database, that is, it must be

f(|Q|)\cdot|\mathcal{D}|
where
f
is a computable function,
Q
the OMQ,
and
\mathcal{D}
the database. Our main goal is to determine the optimal
running time of FPL algorithms for ontology-mediated querying on
databases of bounded
cliquewidth and to provide algorithms that achieve this running time.

As the ontology language, we consider the DLs
\mathcal{ALC}
and
\mathcal{ALCI}
and the
more expressive guarded two-variable fragment GF
2
of first-order
logic. As queries, we admit AQs, conjunctive queries
(CQs), and UCQs. An
ontology-mediated query
(OMQ)
combines an ontology and a query, and an
OMQ language

is determined by the choice of an ontology language and a query
language. For instance,
(\mathcal{ALC},\text{AQ})
is the OMQ language that
combines
\mathcal{ALC}
ontologies with AQs. We find that the running time
may be single or double exponential in
|Q|
, depending on the choice
of the OMQ language. There are several surprising effects, which we
highlight in the following. An overview is provided in Figure
1
.

We show that in
(\mathcal{ALCI},\text{AQ})
, evaluating an OMQ
Q
on a
database
\mathcal{D}
is possible in time
2^{O(|Q|\cdot k)}\cdot|\mathcal{D}|

where
k
is the cliquewidth of
\mathcal{D}
.

\citeauthoryearLutz2008

|q|

|\mathcal{O}|

Finally we observe that, in
({\cal ALCF},\text{AQ})

{\cal ALCF}

Description Logics.
Let
{\sf N_{C}}
,
{\sf N_{R}}
, and

be countably

, and

. A

, where

(r^{-})^{-}=r
.

\mathcal{ALCI}

\mathcal{ALC}

We use

For

with

C
and

\mathcal{L}
-concepts.
A

of the form

,

, and

. We use

.
The

The semantics is given in terms of

\mathcal{I}=(\Delta^{\mathcal{I}},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}})
, we refer to

[
\citeauthoryearBaader et al.2017
]

An interpretation

, and a fact

, we

to denote the restriction of
\mathcal{I}
to domain
S
.
An interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is a

\mathcal{D}
is

\mathcal{I}

.

We also consider the
guarded two-variable fragment (GF
2
)
of

, only two fixed variables

and

2

2

. We use

\mathcal{I}

An

Ontology-Mediated Querying.

An
ontology-mediated query (OMQ)
is a pair

Q=(\mathcal{O},q)
with
\mathcal{O}
an ontology and
q
a query such as a UCQ. While OMQs are often defined to
include
an additional third component, the data signature, the problem of

and so we omit it. We write
Q(\bar{x})
to indicate that the answer variables of

are
\bar{x}
.
A tuple
\bar{a}\in{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D})^{|\bar{x}|}
is an

to

on a database
\mathcal{D}
, written
\mathcal{D}\models Q(\bar{a})
, if

\mathcal{I}\models q(\bar{a})
for all models
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{D}
. When
more convenient, we might alternatively write

\mathcal{D},\mathcal{O}\models q(\bar{a})
. We write

to denote

on
\mathcal{D}
. We use
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{Q})
to denote the

that
contains all OMQs
Q
in which
\mathcal{O}
is formulated in the DL
\mathcal{L}
and
q

in the query language
\mathcal{Q}
, such as in
(\mathcal{ALCI},\text{UCQ})
and
(\text{GF}_{2},\text{AQ})
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{Q})
be an OMQ language. We write

in

(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{Q})
to denote the problem to decide, given an OMQ

\mathcal{D}

\bar{c}\in{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D})^{|\bar{x}|}

\bar{c}\in Q(\mathcal{D})
. A

\kappa

assigns to every input

a parameter

\kappa(Q,\mathcal{D},\bar{c})\in\mathbb{N}
. We will use
\kappa=|Q|
in

\kappa=|Q|+k
in upper bounds where
k
is the
cliquewidth of
\mathcal{D}
as defined below. OMQ evaluation is

fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) for

if it can be
decided by an algorithm that runs in time
f(\kappa(Q,\mathcal{D},\bar{c}))\cdot{\mathtt{poly}}(n)

where
f
is a computable function, here and throughout the paper,

{\mathtt{poly}}
denotes an unspecified
polynomial, and

Fixed-parameter linearity (FPL) for

defined analogously, with running time

f(\kappa(Q,\mathcal{D},\bar{c}))\cdot O(n)
.

Treewidth and Cliquewidth.
Treewidth describes how
similar a graph (in our case: a database) is to a tree, and
cliquewidth does the same for cliques

[
\citeauthoryearCourcelle and
Engelfriet2012
]
. Due to space restrictions and since this article focuses on
cliquewidth, we provide the definition of treewidth only in the
appendix.
Databases of cliquewidth
k
can be constructed by a
k
-expression as
defined below, where
k
refers to the number of
labels
in the
expression. For convenience, we use a reservoir of fresh concept names

L_{1},L_{2},\dots
as labels that may occur in databases, but not
in ontologies and queries.
We say that
a constant
c\in{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D})
is
labeled

i
if
L_{i}(c)\in\mathcal{D}
.
An
expression

s
defines a
database
\mathcal{D}_{s}
built from four operations, as follows:

\boldsymbol{s=\iota(\mathcal{D})}
is the nullary operator for

constant introduction
where
i\geq 1
and
\mathcal{D}
is a database with
|{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D})|=1
that
contains
exactly one fact of the form
L_{i}(c)
, and
\mathcal{D}_{s}=\mathcal{D}
.

\boldsymbol{s=s_{1}\oplus s_{2}}
is the binary operator for

disjoint union
where
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are expressions such that

{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D}_{s_{1}})\cap{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D}_{s_{2}})=\emptyset

and
\mathcal{D}_{s}=\mathcal{D}_{s_{1}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{s_{2}}
.

\boldsymbol{s=\alpha_{i,j}^{r}(s^{\prime})}
is the unary
operator for
role insertion
where
r
is a role name,

i,j\geq 1
are distinct, and
s^{\prime}
is an expression. It links all constants
labeled
i
to all constants labeled
j
using role
r
, that
is,

\mathcal{D}_{s}=\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\cup\{r(a,b)\mid L_{i}(a)\in\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\text{ and }L_{j}(b)\in\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\}.

\boldsymbol{s=\rho_{i\rightarrow j}(s^{\prime})}
is the unary
operator for
relabeling
where
i,j\geq 1
are distinct and

is an expression. It changes all
i
-labels to

, that is,

\mathcal{D}_{s}=\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\setminus\{L_{i}(c)\mid c\in{\mathtt{adom}}(\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}})\}\cup\{L_{j}(c)\mid L_{i}(c)\in\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}\}.

An expression
s
is a
k
-expression
if all labels in

are from

\{L_{1},\dots,L_{k}\}
. A database
\mathcal{D}
has

if there is a
k
-expression
s
with
\mathcal{D}_{s}=\mathcal{D}
, but no

k^{\prime}
-expression
s^{\prime}
such that
k^{\prime}<k
and
\mathcal{D}_{s^{\prime}}=\mathcal{D}
, up to
facts of the form
L_{i}(c)
.

Example 1
.

Consider the class of databases about schools that use the concept names

{\mathtt{Pupil}}
,
{\mathtt{Teacher}}
and
{\mathtt{School}}
as well as role names

{\mathtt{teaches}}
,
{\mathtt{worksAt}}
and
{\mathtt{isClassmateOf}}
, and where
each teacher works at exactly one school and can teach any number of
pupils.
The set of all pupils is partitioned into groups of pupils that
are classmates of one another, and each teacher teaches exactly one
such group. A basic database from that class is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\

\displaystyle\{{\mathtt{Pupil}}(a_{1}),{\mathtt{Pupil}}(a_{2}),{\mathtt{Teacher}}(b),{\mathtt{School}}(c),

\displaystyle\ \ {\mathtt{worksAt}}(b,c),{\mathtt{teaches}}(b,a_{1}),{\mathtt{teaches}}(b,a_{2}),

\displaystyle\ \ {\mathtt{isClassmateOf}}(a_{1},a_{2}),{\mathtt{isClassmateOf}}(a_{2},a_{1})\}.

This database has cliquewidth
3
, and in fact so does any database
from the described class, independently of the number of pupils,
teachers, and schools. When constructing
k
-expressions, it
suffices to use up to two labels for pupils, one for teachers, and
one for schools. With proper relabeling, no more than three labels
are needed at the same time.

4
Lower Bounds

We prove that for
(\text{GF}_{2},\text{AQ})
and
(\mathcal{ALC},\text{CQ})
, the double exponential running time in
|\mathcal{O}|
cannot be
improved unless the exponential time hypothesis (ETH) is false. The
ETH states that if
\delta
is the infimum of real numbers such that
3SAT can be solved in time
O(2^{\delta n})
, where
n
is the number
of variables in the input formula, then
\delta>0
.
The following are the two main results of this section.

Theorem 4
.

If the ETH is true, then there is no algorithm with running time

2^{2^{o(|Q|)}}\cdot{\mathtt{poly}}(|\mathcal{D}|)
for OMQ
evaluation in

1.

(\text{GF}_{2},\text{AQ})
on databases of cliquewidth 2;

2.

(\mathcal{ALC},\text{CQ})
on databases of cliquewidth 3.

Note that OMQ evaluation in
(\text{GF}_{2},\text{AQ})
and

(\mathcal{ALC},\text{CQ})
are only
ExpTime
-complete in combined
complexity on unrestricted databases. The former is folklore and the
latter is proved in
[
\citeauthoryearLutz2008
]
. Thus,
Theorem
4
implies that, in these two OMQ languages,
one has to pay for the running time to be polynomial in
|\mathcal{D}|
on
databases of bounded cliquewidth by an exponential increase in the
running time in
|Q|
.

We now prove Theorem
4
.
A major contribution of the original paper bringing forward the ETH is
the sparsification lemma, which implies the
following useful consequence of the ETH
[
\citeauthoryearImpagliazzo, Paturi, and
Zane2001
]
:

(
*
)

If the ETH is true, then 3SAT cannot be solved in time
2^{o(m)}
, with
m
the number of clauses of the input formula.

Both results in Theorem
4
are proved by
contradiction against (
*

OMQ evaluation problems with running time

can be used to design an algorithm
for 3SAT with running time

, contradicting (

this, it suffices to give a polynomial time reduction from 3SAT to
OMQ evaluation that produces an ontology of size

where

is the number of clauses in the input formula. For both points of

, we first use a known reduction from 3SAT
to

(Theorem

reduce

to OMQ evaluation. In

We prove two results that concern OMQ evaluation on databases of

6
Conclusion

We leave open the interesting question whether OMQ evaluation in

(\text{GF}_{2},\text{(U)CQ})
on databases of bounded cliquewidth is
in
PTime
in data complexity. While all
other OMQ languages studied in this paper are easily translated into
MSO

, this is not the case for

(\text{GF}_{2},\text{(U)CQ})
. In fact, it is straightforward to
express the problem
Monochromatic Triangle
4
4

4

Can
the edges of a given graph be colored with two colors without generating a
monochromatic triangle?

in
(\text{GF}_{2},\text{CQ})
and to our knowledge it is open whether
this problem can be expressed in MSO

.

It would also be interesting to consider natural
extensions of
\mathcal{ALCI}
. We believe that adding role inclusions has no
impact on the obtained results. It is less clear what
happens for transitive roles, number restrictions, and nominals. In
the appendix, we make the following observation for the extension

of

with (globally) functional roles.

Theorem 8
.

OMQ evaluation in
(\mathcal{ALCF},\text{CQ})

treewidth 2 is
coNP

name assumption is not made.

The proof relies on an observation from

[
\citeauthoryearFigueira2016

. We conjecture that when the unique

It would also be very interesting to pursue the idea of
decomposing databases into a (hopefully small) part of high
cliquewidth and parts of low cliquewidth, with the aim of achieving

Appendix A
Additional Preliminaries

O
-Notation.
The use of the
O
-notation is somewhat subtle
when multiple inputs/variables and exponential functions are involved.
Regarding the multiple inputs, we follow the standard convention that
is mentioned in
[
\citeauthoryearCormen et al.2022
]
and actually rather well explained on the
Wikipedia page on “Big
O
Notation”. Assume that we have three
variables
n,m,k
. When writing something like

f(n,m,k)\in n\cdot 2^{m\cdot 2^{O(k)}}
what we mean is

\log\log f(n,m,k)\in O(\log\log n\cdot\log m\cdot k).

As a
convention, we only use the
O
-notation for the highest exponent,
corresponding to the intuition that multiplicative factors are more
‘effective’ there (given that none of our input sizes/variables can be
zero).

Treewidth is a widely used notion that
measures the degree of tree-likeness of a graph.
A

We also speak about the treewidth of CQs and of interpretations. Each

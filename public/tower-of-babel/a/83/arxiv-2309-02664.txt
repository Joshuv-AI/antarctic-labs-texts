Introduction

Guerrero et al.
[
7
]
propose a novel approach to building stationary first-order integer-valued autoregressive (INAR (
1
)) processes. based on the concept of thinning. The standard approach requires that the thinning operator be defined first and INAR (
1
) models with either a specified marginal (the forward approach) or a specified innovation (the backward approach) are developed. In contrast, the approach in
[
7
]
is to start out by specifying both the marginal distribution of the process and that of its innovation sequence, and then proceed to identify the thinning operator by solving a functional equation. In this article we discuss the connection between the thinning operators the authors derived for their novel geometric and negative binomial INAR (
1
) models and the thinning operator introduced in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. More specifically, we show that the existence of the two models has been established in
[
1
]
using the forward approach that only requires the marginal distribution of the process be negative binomial. It is shown in particular that several distributional properties in
[
7
]
follow essentially from results established in
[
1
]
under a different parameterization. In the process we strenghthen some of the authors’ results obtained for the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process by simplifying some of their assumptions and proofs and we proceed to extend their results to the novel negative binomial INAR (
1
) process. We also discuss in depth estimation procedures of key parameters of the novel negative binomial INAR (
1
) process by capitalizing on results established by several authors for branching processes with immigration.

The paper is organized as follows. We recall some basic facts in Section 2. Section 3 is devoted to the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process introduced in
[
7
]
. We simplify a few results, along with their proofs, and remove some unneeded assumptions for a couple of key results. The authors’ novel negative binomial INAR (
1
) process is studied in detail in Section 4. It is shown that its distributional properties follow from results established in
[
1
]
. Estimation procedures of its key parameters are the object of Section 5.

2
Some basic facts

We start out by recalling some basic facts. Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}
be a possibly multivariate parameter and
X
a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable. The thinning operator
\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X
is defined by the equation

\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X=\sum_{i=1}^{X}G_{i},

(2)

where
(G_{i},i\geq 1)
is a sequence of iid
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables independent of
X
. The probability generating function (pgf)
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X}(s)
of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X
satisfies the equation

\Psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X}(s)=\Psi_{X}(\Psi_{G}(s)),

(3)

where
\Psi_{X}(s)
is the pgf of
X
and
\Psi_{G}(s)
is the marginal pgf of the sequence
\{G_{i}\}
.

The operation
\star
is to be thought of as a scalar multiplication that preserves the discrete nature of the factor
X
. The moment properties of
\boldsymbol{\theta}\ast X
are deduced from those of
X
from standard random summation techniques (see
[
7
]
and the accompanying supplementary material).

A sequence
(X_{t},t\geq 0)
of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables is an INAR (
1
) process if for any
t\geq 0
,

X_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\theta}\star X_{t}+\varepsilon_{t+1},

(4)

where
(\varepsilon_{t},t\geq 0)
is an i.i.d. sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables, referred to as the innovation sequence of
\{X_{t}\}
. The sequences
\{\varepsilon_{t}\}
and
\{G_{i}\}
(of (
2
)) are assumed independent.

It is clear that the INAR (
1
) process
\{X_{t}\}
of (
4
) is a branching process with stationary immigration, and thus is a Markov chain (cf.
[
3
]
). As such, necessary and sufficient conditions for the stationarity of
\{X_{t}\}
are well known. They are based essentially on the existence of a stationary distribution
(\pi_{k},k\geq 0)
for
\{X_{t}\}
. In the forward approach, discrete self-decomposability of the marginal distribution insures the existence of
\{\pi_{k}\}
(see Proposition 2.5 and Lemma 4.1 in
[
1
]
). In the backward approach, an integrability condition or, equivalently, a log-moment condition on
\varepsilon_{t}
insures the existence of
\{\pi_{k}\}
(see Proposition 2.1 in
[
1
]
and references therein). Either one of the two conditions can be used for the novel approach in
[
7
]
. Stationarity of
\{X_{t}\}
is achieved by making
\{\pi_{k}\}
the initial of the process at
t=0
. This will be assumed in the remainder of the paper without further reference.

The marginal pgf’s
\Psi_{X}(s)
and
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
of the stationary INAR (
1
) process (
4
) and its innovation sequence must satisfy the functional equation

\Psi_{X}(s)=\Psi_{X}[\Psi_{G}(s)]\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s),

(5)

where
\Psi_{G}(s)
is the common pgf of the
G
variables in (
2
). In the standard approach,
\Psi_{G}(s)
is always known. If, in addtion, either
\Psi_{X}(s)
or
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
, but not both, is specified, the marginal pgf of the innovation process (forward approach), respectively, the marginal pgf of the INAR (
1
) process (backward approach), arises as the solution of the functional equation (
5
). In
[
7
]
both
\Psi_{X}(s)
and
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
are specified, and
\Psi_{G}(s)
is identified, again via (
5
), as

\Psi_{G}(s)=\Psi_{X}^{-1}\Bigl(\frac{\Psi_{X}(s)}{\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)}\Bigr).

(6)

In the remainder of this article, we will designate by
\star
the thinning operator characterized by (
6
). We will use a different symbol for other thinning operators.

As shown in
[
7
]
, the binomial thinning operator with
\Psi_{G}(s)=1-\alpha+\alpha s
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
is the solution to (
6
) for the stationary INAR (
1
) model with a prescribed Poisson(
\lambda
) marginal and a Poisson(
\lambda\overline{\alpha}
) innovation.
We provide another important example. Let
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1)
, and
\lambda>0
. A stationary INAR (
1
) process
\{X_{t}\}
whose marginal and innovation distributions have pgf

\Psi_{X}(s)=\exp\Bigl\{-\lambda\Bigl(\frac{1-s}{1-\theta s}\Bigr)^{\gamma}\Bigr\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\exp\Bigl\{-\lambda(1-\alpha^{\gamma})\Bigl(\frac{1-s}{1-\theta s}\Bigr)^{\gamma}\Bigr\}

(7)

can be constructed (via (
6
)) with the thinning operator
(\alpha,\theta)\star(\cdot)
characterized by the linear fractional pgf

\Psi_{G}(s)=\frac{\overline{\alpha}-(\theta-\alpha)s}{1-\alpha\theta-\overline{\alpha}\theta s}.

(8)

The proof is an easy exercise and is therefore omitted. Note that if
0<\gamma<1
, then
\{X_{t}\}
is heavy-tailed (with an infinite mean), whereas the special case
\gamma=1
gives rise to the stationary INAR (
1
) process with a Polya-Aeppli marginal distribution as well as a Polya-Aeppli innovation. This model was proposed and studied in
[
1
]
(Sections 2 and 3) by way of the forward approach with the expectation thinning operator
(\beta,\theta)\odot(\cdot)
that acts on a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable
X
as follows:

(\beta,\theta)\odot X=\sum_{i=1}Y_{i},

(9)

where
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\theta\in[0,1)
, and
\{Y_{i}\}
is an i.i.d. sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables, independent of
X
, with marginal pgf

\Psi_{\beta,\theta}(s)=1-\beta\frac{1-s}{1-\overline{\beta}\theta s}.

(10)

Note the case
\theta=0
corresponds to the binomial thinning operator.

It is easily seen the two thinning operators
(\alpha,\theta)\star(\cdot)
and
(\beta,\theta)\odot(\cdot)
, characterized respectively by (
8
) and (
10
), are equivalent through the reparameterization
\beta=\frac{\alpha\overline{\theta}}{1-\alpha\theta}
from
\star
to
\odot
and, conversely,
\alpha=\frac{\beta}{1-\overline{\beta}\theta}
from
\odot
to
\star
. Consequently, the stochastic properties of the Polya-Aeppli INAR (
1
) process obtained in
[
1
]
(Section 3) can be rewritten in terms of the
\star
thinning operator and the parameters
\alpha
and
\theta
.

3
On the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process

In
[
7
]
(Sections 2.1 and 2.3) the authors introduce a stationary INAR (
1
) process
\{X_{t}\}
with prespescified negative binomial marginal and innovation distributions with the following pgf’s:

\Psi_{X}(s)=(1+\mu r^{-1}(1-s))^{-r}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)=(1+\overline{\alpha}\mu r^{-1}(1-s))^{-r},

(11)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
r>0
,
\mu>0
,
E(X_{t})=\mu
and
E(\varepsilon_{t})=\overline{\alpha}\mu
.

Following the authors notation we will write
X_{t}\sim NB(r,\mu)
and
\varepsilon_{t}\sim NB(r,\overline{\alpha}\mu)

Using (
6
), the authors identify the expectation thinning operator
\boldsymbol{\theta}\star(\cdot)
of (
2
) with
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\alpha,\mu,r)
and pgf.

\Psi_{G}(s)=\frac{1+(r^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\mu-\alpha)(1-s)}{1+r^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\mu(1-s)}=1-\alpha\frac{1-s}{1+r^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\mu(1-s)}.

(12)

The special case
r=1
gives rise to the authors’ novel geometric INAR (
1
) process with
X_{t}\sim NB(1,\mu)
,
\varepsilon_{t}\sim NB(1,\overline{\alpha}\mu)
and

\Psi_{G}(s)=\frac{1+(\overline{\alpha}\mu-\alpha)(1-s)}{1+\overline{\alpha}\mu(1-s)}=1-\alpha\frac{1-s}{1+\overline{\alpha}\mu(1-s)}.

(13)

In the remainder of the paper, we will refer to the above thinning operators as follows

\boldsymbol{\theta}\star(\cdot)=\begin{cases}(\alpha,\mu)\star(\cdot)&\mbox{if }r=1\\
(\alpha,\mu,r)\star(\cdot)&\mbox{if }r>0\mbox{ and }r\neq 1.\end{cases}

(14)

The novel geometric INAR (
1
) model is fully studied in
[
7
]
. Its main properties are given, including some estimation methods. The results of a Monte Carlo simulation are discussed. The authors conclude with a real-world application.

We note at this point that the authors introduce an unnecessarily complicated parameterization to describe the common pmf of the i.i.d. sequence
\{G_{i}\}
. We propose a simpler and more useful representation that follows from this version of
\Psi_{G}(s)
,

\Psi_{G}(s)=1-\alpha q\frac{1-s}{1-\overline{q}s},\qquad q=(1+\overline{\alpha}\mu)^{-1},

(15)

which leads to the
G
-pmf

P(G=k)=\begin{cases}1-\alpha q&\mbox{if }k=0\\
(\alpha q)q\overline{q}^{k-1}&\mbox{if }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

(16)

In the remainder of this section we revisit some of the authors’ results and give modified versions of a few distributional properties of the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process by dropping some unnecessary assumptions.

First, we show that the constraint
0<\alpha<\frac{\mu}{1+\mu}
in Lemma 2.2 in
[
7
]
is not needed to obtain the conditional probability of
(\alpha,\mu)\star X
given
X=x
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
X
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variables. Then, the conditional probability of
(\alpha,\mu)\star X
given
X=x
is

p_{k}^{(x)}=P((\alpha,\mu)\star X=k|X=x)=\begin{cases}(1-\alpha q)^{x}&\mbox{if }k=0\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\min(k,x)}A_{i}^{(x)}(\alpha q)B_{i}^{(k)}(q)&\mbox{if }k\geq 1,\end{cases}

(17)

where, for
n\geq 1
,
0\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq l\leq n
, and
0<y<1
,

A_{i}^{(n)}(y)=\binom{n}{i}y^{i}(1-y)^{n-i}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{l}^{(n)}(y)=\binom{n-1}{l-1}y^{l}(1-y)^{n-l}.

(18)

Proof.

Note that the conditional pgf of
(\alpha,\mu)\star X
given
X=x
is
\Psi_{G}(s)^{x}
. It is easily seen that the modified parameterization of
\Psi_{G}(s)
(cf. (
15
) and (
16
)) can be rewritten as

\Psi_{G}(s)=1-\alpha q+\alpha qG_{q}(s),

(19)

where
G_{q}(s)
is the pgf of the shifted geometric(
q
) distribution of (
1
). We have for every
i\geq 1
,

G_{q}(s)^{i}=\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\binom{k-1}{i-1}\,q^{i}\overline{q}^{k-i}s^{i}.

(20)

An application of the binomial theorem and a standard power series argument lead to (
17
).
∎

Next, we modify the formula for the first-order transition probability
p_{ij}=P(X_{t+1}=j|X_{t}=i)
given in Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
and show again that the assumption
0<\alpha<\frac{\mu}{\mu+1}
is not needed.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}
be a stationary INAR (
1
) process with prespecified
NB(1,\mu)
marginal and
NB(1,\overline{\alpha}\mu)
innovation distributions. Then,

p_{ij}=\begin{cases}q\overline{q}^{j}\qquad\mbox{if }i=0\\
\displaystyle A_{0}^{(i)}(\alpha q)B_{1}^{(j+1)}(q)+\sum_{k=1}^{j}B_{1}^{(j-k+1)}(q)\sum_{l=1}^{\min(i,k)}A_{l}^{(i)}(\alpha q)B_{l}^{k}(q)&\mbox{if }i\geq 1,\end{cases}

(21)

Proof.

First, we note
p_{ij}
results from the convolution of the pmf
(p_{k}^{(i)},k\geq 0)
of (
17
) and the pmf
(q\overline{q}^{k},k\geq 0)
of
\varepsilon_{t}
. It is clear that
p_{0j}=P(\varepsilon_{t}=j)
. For
i\geq 1
, we have
p_{ij}=p_{0}^{(i)}(q\overline{q}^{j})+\sum_{k=1}^{j}p_{k}^{(i)}(q\overline{q}^{j-k})
, which yields (
21
), by way of (
17
) and (
18
).
∎

We proceed to extend (
21
) to the
h
-th order transition probability from
X_{t}
to
X_{t+h}
.

For
h\geq 0
, we define the sequence
\Psi_{G}^{(h+1)}(s)=\Psi_{G}\bigl[\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)\bigr]
with
\Psi_{G}^{(0)}(s)=s
for
\Psi_{G}(s)
of (
13
).

We gather a few useful results in the following lemma. The proof of each assertion relies on a straightforward induction argument. We omit the details.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

For every
h\geq 1
,

\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)=1-\alpha^{h}q_{h}\frac{1-s}{1-(1-q_{h})s},\quad q_{h}=(1+(1-\alpha^{h})\mu)^{-1}.

(22)

(ii)

The
h
-fold composition of the operator
(\alpha,\mu)\star(\cdot)
(
h\geq 1
) satisfies

(\alpha,\mu)\star(\alpha,\mu)\star\cdots\star(\alpha,\mu)*Y\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}(\alpha^{h},\mu)\star Y\quad(h\geq 1)

(23)

for any
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable
Y
. Moreover,
\Psi_{(\alpha^{h},\mu)\star Y}(s)=\Psi_{Y}\bigl(\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)\bigr)
.

(iii)

The INAR (
1
) process
\{X_{t}\}
of (
4
), with
\boldsymbol{\theta}\star(\cdot)=(\alpha,\mu)\star(\cdot)
, satisfies the property

X_{t+h}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}(\alpha^{h},\mu)\star X_{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{h}(\alpha^{h-j},\mu)\star\varepsilon_{t+j}.

(24)

for every
h\geq 1
(note, by convention,
(1,\mu)\star Y=Y
).

We now derive the
h
-th order transition probability
p_{ij}^{(h)}=P(X_{t+h}=j|X_{t}=i)
,
h\geq 1
, of the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process. Note the more complicated formula obtained in
[
7
]
, Proposition 3.3, requires the unnecessary assumption
\alpha^{h}<\frac{\mu}{\mu+1}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\{X_{t}\}
be a stationary INAR (
1
) process with prespecified
NB(1,\mu)
marginal and
NB(1,\overline{\alpha}\mu)
innovation distributions. Then for every
h\geq 1
,

p_{ij}^{(h)}=\begin{cases}q_{h}(1-q_{h})^{j}\qquad\mbox{if }i=0\\
\displaystyle A_{0}^{(i)}(\alpha^{h}q_{h})B_{1}^{(j+1)}(q_{h})+\sum_{k=1}^{j}B_{1}^{(j-k+1)}(q_{h})\sum_{l=1}^{\min(i,k)}A_{l}^{(i)}(\alpha^{h}q_{h})B_{l}^{k}(q_{h})&\mbox{if }i\geq 1.\end{cases}

(25)

Note
p_{ij}^{(1)}=p_{ij}
, with
p_{ij}
of (
21
) and
q_{1}=q
.

Proof.

First, we note that for every
h\geq 1
,

\Psi_{X}\bigl(\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)\bigr)={1+(1-\alpha^{h})\mu(1-s)\over 1+\mu(1-s)}\text{ and }\Psi_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)\bigr)={1+(1-\alpha^{h})\mu(1-s)\over 1+(1-\alpha^{h+1})\mu(1-s)},

(26)

where
\Psi_{X}(s)
and
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(s)
are the marginal pgf’s of the geometric INAR (
1
) process and its innovation (see (

) with

). Letting
\varepsilon_{t}^{(h)}=\sum_{j=0}^{h}(\alpha^{h-j},\mu)\star\varepsilon_{t+j}
, it follows by (

), (

), and independence, that the pgf of
\varepsilon_{t}^{(h)}
is

\Psi_{\varepsilon^{(h)}}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{h}\Psi_{\varepsilon}\bigl(\Psi_{G}^{(h-j)}(s)\bigr)=\frac{q_{h}}{1-(1-q_{h})s},

(27)

with
q_{h}
of (
22
).That is,
\varepsilon_{t}^{(h)}\sim NB(1,(1-\alpha^{h})\mu)
with pmf
\bigl(q_{h}(1-q_{h})^{k},k\geq 0\bigr)
. Noting by Lemma 3.2-(i) that
\Psi_{G}^{(h)}(s)=1-\alpha^{h}q_{h}+\alpha^{h}q_{h}G_{q_{h}}(s)
, where

is the pgf of a shifted geometric(

) distribution. The exact same argument used to derive (

leads to

p_{k}^{(x)}(h)=P((\alpha^{h},\mu)\star X=k|X=x)=\begin{cases}(1-\alpha^{h}q_{h})^{x}&\mbox{if }k=0\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\min(k,x)}A_{i}^{(x)}(\alpha^{h}q_{h})B_{i}^{(k)}(q_{h})&\mbox{if }k\geq 1,\end{cases}

(28)

where the functions

results from the convolution of the pmf’s

and

) in the case

On the novel negative binomial INAR (

The aim of this section is to extend the results obtained in
[
7
]
for the novel geometric INAR (
1
) process to the novel negative binomial INAR (
1
) process
\{X_{t}\}
(see Section 2.3 in
[
7
]
), where
X_{t}\sim NB(r,\mu)
,
\varepsilon_{t}\sim NB(r,\overline{\alpha}\mu)
,
\mu=E(X_{t})
, and where the novel thinning operator of (

) is
(\alpha,\mu,r)\star(\cdot)
is characterized by the pgf

of (

We recall that in
[
1
]
the authors used a different parameterization for the negative binomial distribution. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-valued random variable

we write
X\sim NB1(r,\theta)
with parameters

and

(as opposed to

and its pmf

\Psi_{X}(s)=\Bigl(\frac{\overline{\theta}}{1-\theta s}\Bigr)^{r}\quad\text{and}\quad P(X=k)={\Gamma(k+r)\over k!\Gamma(r)}\overline{\theta}^{r}\theta^{k}\quad(k\geq 0).

The mean and variance of

are
E(X)=\frac{r\theta}{\overline{\theta}}
and

.

One can show that all the distributional properties of the novel geometric INAR (

extend to the novel negative binomial INAR (

operator acts as the

operator introduced in
[
1
]
and
[

]
and characterized by equations (

) and (

) above, thus leading to the conclusion that the novel

) process in

]
is equivalent to the

INAR (
1
) process in
[
1
]
. As a consequence, the distributional properties of the novel model will be derived from those established for its counterpart in
[
1
]
.

We start out with a useful lemma. Its proof is an easy exercise and is therefore omitted.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
r>0
and
\beta,\theta\in(0,1)
.

(i)

The thinning operator
(\beta,\theta)\star(\cdot)
(cf. (
2
) and and (
6
) ) obtained by prespecifying
X_{t}\sim NB1(r,\theta)
and
\varepsilon_{t}\sim NB1(r,\overline{\beta}\theta)
is characterized by the pgf
\Psi_{G}(s)=\Psi_{\beta,\theta}(s)
of (
10
). Therefore, the thinning operators
(\beta,\theta)\odot(\cdot)
and
(\beta,\theta)\star(\cdot)
are identical and can be used interchangeably.

(ii)

The thinning operator
(\alpha,\mu,r)\star(\cdot)
(cf. (
12
) and (
14
)) can take the form of the
(\beta,\theta)\star(\cdot)
(or
(\beta,\theta)\odot(\cdot)
) thinning operator, with

\beta={\alpha r\over r+\overline{\alpha}\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta={\mu\over\mu+r}^{\prime}

Polarized vector meson production in semi-inclusive DIS
Xue-shuai Jiao, Kai-bao Chen

Abstract
We make a systematic calculation for polarized vector meson production in semi-inclusive lepton-nucleon deep inelastic scattering e − ​ N → e − ​ V ​ X e^{-}N\to e^{-}VX . We consider the general case of neutral current electroweak interactions at high energies which give rise to parity-violating effects. We present a general kinematic analysis for the process and show that the cross sections are expressed by 81 structure functions. We further give a parton model calculation for the process and show the results for the structure functions in terms of the transverse momentum dependent parton distribution functions and fragmentation functions at the leading order and leading twist of perturbative quantum chromodynamics. The results show that there are 27 nonzero structure functions at this order, among which 15 are related to the tensor polarization of the vector meson. Thirteen structure functions are generated by parity-violating effects. We also present the result and a rough numerical estimate for the spin alignment of the vector meson.

II
The general form of the cross section and the structure functions

II.1
The process and notations

We consider the SIDIS process at high energies with unpolarized electron and nucleon beams, i.e.,

\displaystyle e^{-}(l)+N(p_{N})\rightarrow e^{-}(l^{\prime})+V(p_{h},S)+X,

(1)

where
V
is a vector meson with spin-1.
The momenta of the incident and outgoing particles are shown in the brackets.
S
denotes the polarizations of the vector meson.
The coordinate system for
e^{-}N\to e^{-}VX
in the photon-nucleon collinear frame is shown in Fig.
1
, where the incoming proton and the virtual photon move along the
\pm z
axis and the
x
axis is determined by the transverse momenta of the leptons. The azimuthal angle
\phi
is spanned by the transverse momentum of the vector meson with respect to the transverse momentum of the outgoing electron.

Figure 1:
The coordinate system for
e^{-}N\to e^{-}VX
. The symbols in the brackets denote the four momenta of the particles.

The standard variables for SIDIS are defined as
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
,
x=\frac{Q^{2}}{2p_{N}\cdot q}
,
y=\frac{p_{N}\cdot q}{p_{N}\cdot l}
,
z_{h}=\frac{p_{N}\cdot p_{h}}{p_{N}\cdot q}
, and
s=(p_{N}+l)^{2}
.
We will use the light-cone coordinate system, in which a four vector
a^{\mu}
is expressed as
a^{\mu}=(a^{+},a^{-},\vec{a}_{\perp})
with
a^{\pm}=(a^{0}\pm a^{3})/\sqrt{2}
and
\vec{a}_{\perp}=(a^{1},a^{2})
.
The momenta of the particles take the following forms:

\displaystyle p_{N}^{\mu}=\left(p_{N}^{+},0,\vec{0}_{\perp}\right),

(2)

\displaystyle l^{\mu}=\left(\frac{1-y}{y}xp_{N}^{+},\frac{Q^{2}}{2xyp_{N}^{+}},\frac{Q\sqrt{1-y}}{y},0\right),

(3)

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=\left(-xp_{N}^{+},\frac{Q^{2}}{2xp_{N}^{+}},\vec{0}_{\perp}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle p_{h}^{\mu}=\left(\frac{xp_{N}^{+}\vec{p}_{h\perp}^{2}}{z_{h}Q^{2}},\frac{z_{h}Q^{2}}{2xp_{N}^{+}},\vec{p}_{h\perp}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{h\perp}=|p_{h\perp}|(\cos\phi,\sin\phi).

(6)

We also define two unit light-cone vectors
n^{\mu}=(0,1,\vec{0}_{\perp})
and
\bar{n}^{\mu}=(1,0,\vec{0}_{\perp})
.
With these notations, the transverse metric is given by
g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\nu}-\bar{n}^{\nu}n^{\mu}
.
We will also use the transverse antisymmetric tensor defined as
\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\bar{n}_{\alpha}n_{\beta}
with
\varepsilon_{\perp}^{12}=1
.

We consider the leading order approximation for QED with the exchange of a single virtual photon
\gamma^{*}
or a
Z
boson with momentum
q=l-l^{\prime}
between the electron and the nucleon.
The differential cross section is given by

\displaystyle d\sigma=\frac{2\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{sQ^{4}}A_{r}L_{r}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})W_{r,\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},p_{h},S)\frac{d^{3}l^{\prime}d^{3}p_{h}}{2E_{l^{\prime}}2E_{h}}.

(7)

The symbol
r
can be
\gamma\gamma
,
ZZ
, and
\gamma Z
.
They correspond to the electromagnetic, the weak, and the interference contributions, respectively.
A summation over
r
is implicit in Eq. (
7
), i.e.,

\displaystyle d\sigma=d\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}+d\sigma_{ZZ}+d\sigma_{\gamma Z}.

(8)

The
A_{r}
factors and the leptonic tensors are determined in perturbative theory.
The
A_{r}
factors can be calculated as

\displaystyle A_{\gamma\gamma}=e_{q}^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle A_{ZZ}=\frac{Q^{4}}{\left[(Q^{2}+M_{Z}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma_{Z}^{2}M_{Z}^{2}\right]\sin^{4}2\theta_{W}}\equiv\chi,

(10)

\displaystyle A_{\gamma Z}=\frac{2e_{q}Q^{2}(Q^{2}+M_{Z}^{2})}{\left[(Q^{2}+M_{Z}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma_{Z}^{2}M_{Z}^{2}\right]\sin^{2}2\theta_{W}}\equiv\chi_{\rm int},

(11)

where
\Gamma_{Z}
is the width of the
Z
boson and
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle.
The leptonic tensors are given by

\displaystyle L_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})=2\left(l^{\mu}l^{\prime\nu}+l^{\nu}l^{\prime\mu}-g^{\mu\nu}l\cdot l^{\prime}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})=c_{1}^{e}L_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})-2ic_{3}^{e}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu ll^{\prime}},

(13)

\displaystyle L_{\gamma Z}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})=c_{V}^{e}L_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})-2ic_{A}^{e}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu ll^{\prime}},

(14)

where
c_{V}^{e}
and
c_{A}^{e}
are from the weak interaction vertex defined by
\Gamma_{\mu}^{e}=\gamma_{\mu}(c_{V}^{e}-c_{A}^{e}\gamma_{5})
,
c_{1}^{e}=(c_{V}^{e})^{2}+(c_{A}^{e})^{2}
, and
c_{3}^{e}=2c_{V}^{e}c_{A}^{e}
.
We see that
L_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu\nu}
and
L_{\gamma Z}^{\mu\nu}
can be obtained from
L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}
by the replacements of
(c_{1}^{e},c_{3}^{e})\to(1,0)
and
(c_{1}^{e},c_{3}^{e})\to(c_{V}^{e},c_{A}^{e})
, respectively.
The corresponding hadronic tensors are defined as

\displaystyle W_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},p_{h},S)=\sum_{X}\delta^{4}(p_{N}+q-p_{h}-p_{X})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\langle p_{N}|J_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu}(0)|p_{h},S;X\rangle\langle p_{h},S;X|J_{\gamma\gamma}^{\nu}(0)|p_{N}\rangle,

(15)

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},p_{h},S)=\sum_{X}\delta^{4}(p_{N}+q-p_{h}-p_{X})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\langle p_{N}|J_{ZZ}^{\mu}(0)|p_{h},S;X\rangle\langle p_{h},S;X|J_{ZZ}^{\nu}(0)|p_{N}\rangle,

(16)

\displaystyle W_{\gamma Z}^{\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},p_{h},S)=\sum_{X}\delta^{4}(p_{N}+q-p_{h}-p_{X})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\langle p_{N}|J_{ZZ}^{\mu}(0)|p_{h},S;X\rangle\langle p_{h},S;X|J_{\gamma\gamma}^{\nu}(0)|p_{N}\rangle,

(17)

where the current operators are given by
J_{\gamma\gamma}^{\mu}(0)=\bar{\psi}(0)\gamma^{\mu}\psi(0)
and
J_{ZZ}^{\mu}(0)=\bar{\psi}(0)\Gamma_{q}^{\mu}\psi(0)
with
\Gamma_{\mu}^{q}=\gamma_{\mu}(c_{V}^{q}-c_{A}^{q}\gamma_{5})
.

For the phase space factors, in terms of the standard SIDIS variables, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}l^{\prime}}{2E_{l^{\prime}}}=\frac{ys}{4}dxdyd\psi,\qquad\frac{d^{3}p_{h}}{2E_{h}}=\frac{dz_{h}}{2z_{h}}d^{2}p_{h\perp},

(18)

where
\psi
is the azimuthal angle of
\vec{l}^{\prime}
around
\vec{l}
.
Therefore the cross section in Eq. (
7
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dxdydz_{h}d\psi d^{2}p_{h\perp}}=\frac{y\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{4z_{h}Q^{4}}A_{r}L_{r}^{\mu\nu}(l,l^{\prime})W_{r,\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},p_{h},S).

(19)

II.2
Cross section in terms of structure functions

In this subsection, we make a kinematic analysis for the process.
There is no restriction from parity since we consider both the EM and the weak interaction contributions.
The most general form of the cross section is obtained by adding the contributions from the EM, the weak and the interference terms together.
Formally, the pure weak interaction contribution will generate all the possible structure functions.
The EM and the interference contributions will not introduce new types of structure functions, so we first concentrate on the analysis of the pure weak interaction contribution.

We give the decomposition of the hadronic tensors in terms of basic Lorentz tensors (BLTs).
The general form of the hadronic tensor satisfies the constraints of Hermiticity and current conservation.
The hadronic tensor is divided into a symmetric part and an antisymmetric part, i.e.,

W_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}=W_{ZZ}^{S\mu\nu}+W_{ZZ}^{A\mu\nu}
.
More explicitly, we have

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{S\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,i}W_{\sigma i}^{S}h_{\sigma i}^{S\mu\nu}+\sum_{\sigma,i}\tilde{W}_{\sigma i}^{S}\tilde{h}_{\sigma i}^{S\mu\nu},

(20)

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{A\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,i}W_{\sigma i}^{A}h_{\sigma i}^{A\mu\nu}+\sum_{\sigma,i}\tilde{W}_{\sigma i}^{A}\tilde{h}_{\sigma i}^{A\mu\nu},

(21)

where
h^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}
’s and
\tilde{h}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma i}
’s represent the space reflection even and odd BLTs, respectively.
They are constructed from available kinematical variables in the process, e.g.,
p_{N}
,
p_{h}
,
q
, and the polarization vector or tensor.

W_{\sigma i}
’s are scalar coefficients.
The subscript
\sigma
specifies the polarizations of the vector meson.

There are nine BLTs for the unpolarized part
Chen:2016moq
, i.e.,

\displaystyle h^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui}

\displaystyle=\Big\{g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}},~p_{Nq}^{\mu}p_{Nq}^{\nu},~p_{hq}^{\mu}p_{hq}^{\nu},~p_{Nq}^{\{\mu}p_{hq}^{\nu\}}\Big\},

(22)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varepsilon^{\{\mu qp_{N}p_{h}}p_{Nq}^{\nu\}},~\varepsilon^{\{\mu qp_{N}p_{h}}p_{hq}^{\nu\}}\Big\},

(23)

\displaystyle h^{A\mu\nu}_{U}

\displaystyle=\Big\{p_{Nq}^{[\mu}p_{hq}^{\nu]}\Big\},

(24)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Ui}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\varepsilon^{\mu\nu qp_{N}},~\varepsilon^{\mu\nu qp_{h}}\Big\}.

(25)

The subscript
U
denotes the unpolarized part.
We have defined the four vectors such as
p_{Nq}^{\mu}\equiv p_{N}^{\mu}-q^{\mu}(p_{N}\cdot q)/q^{2}
satisfying
q\cdot p_{Nq}
= 0, and similar for
p_{hq}^{\mu}
.
We have also used the notations
A^{\{\mu}B^{\nu\}}\equiv A^{\mu}B^{\nu}+A^{\nu}B^{\mu}
, and
A^{[\mu}B^{\nu]}\equiv A^{\mu}B^{\nu}-A^{\nu}B^{\mu}
for simplicity.

The vector polarization of the vector meson, similar to spin-
1/2
hadrons, is described by the helicity
\lambda
and the transverse polarization vector
S_{T}^{\mu}
.
It has been shown in
Chen:2016moq
that the polarization dependent BLTs can be constructed from the unpolarized BLTs by multiplying the corresponding polarization dependent scalars or pseudoscalars.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle h^{S\mu\nu}_{Vi}

\displaystyle=\Big\{[\lambda,(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{T})]\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{T}}h^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(26)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Vi}

\displaystyle=\Big\{[\lambda,(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{T})]h^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{T}}\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(27)

\displaystyle h^{A\mu\nu}_{Vi}

\displaystyle=\Big\{[\lambda,(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{T})]\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{T}}h^{A\mu\nu}_{U}\Big\},

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Vi}

\displaystyle=\Big\{[\lambda,(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{T})]h^{A\mu\nu}_{U},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{T}}\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\}.

(29)

There are in total 27 BLTs for the vector polarization dependent part.

The tensor polarization of the vector meson is described by five independent components.
They are given by a Lorentz scalar
S_{LL}
, a transverse Lorentz vector
S_{LT}^{\mu}
with two independent components
S_{LT}^{x}
and
S_{LT}^{y}
, and a transverse Lorentz tensor
S_{TT}^{\mu\nu}
with two independent components
S_{TT}^{xx}=-S_{TT}^{yy}
and
S_{TT}^{xy}=S_{TT}^{yx}

Bacchetta:2000jk
;
Chen:2016moq
.
Since
S_{LL}
is a Lorentz scalar, there are nine
S_{LL}
-dependent BLTs in analog with the unpolarized part.
They are given by

\displaystyle h_{LLi}^{S\mu\nu}=S_{LL}h^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},

(30)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{LLi}^{S\mu\nu}=S_{LL}\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},

(31)

\displaystyle h_{LL}^{A\mu\nu}=S_{LL}h^{A\mu\nu}_{U},

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{LLi}^{A\mu\nu}=S_{LL}\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Ui}.

(33)

The
S_{LT}^{\mu}
dependent BLTs are given by

\displaystyle h^{S\mu\nu}_{LTi}=\Big\{(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{LT})h^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{LT}}\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(34)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{LTi}=\Big\{(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{LT})\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{LT}}h^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(35)

\displaystyle h^{A\mu\nu}_{LTi}=\Big\{(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{LT})h^{A\mu\nu}_{U},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{LT}}\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(36)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{LTi}=\Big\{(p_{h\perp}\cdot S_{LT})\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\varepsilon_{\perp}^{p_{h\perp}S_{LT}}h^{A\mu\nu}_{U}\Big\}.

(37)

For the
S_{TT}^{\mu\nu}
dependent part, we have

\displaystyle h^{S\mu\nu}_{TTi}=\Big\{S_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}h^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\tilde{S}_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(38)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{TTi}=\Big\{S_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}\tilde{h}^{S\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\tilde{S}_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}h^{S\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(39)

\displaystyle h^{A\mu\nu}_{TTi}=\Big\{S_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}h^{A\mu\nu}_{U},~\tilde{S}_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Uj}\Big\},

(40)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}^{A,\mu\nu}_{TTi}=\Big\{S_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}\tilde{h}^{A\mu\nu}_{Ui},~\tilde{S}_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}h^{A\mu\nu}_{U}\Big\},

(41)

where
S_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}=p_{h\perp\alpha}p_{h\perp\beta}S_{TT}^{\alpha\beta}
and
\tilde{S}_{TT}^{p_{h}p_{h}}=-\tilde{p}_{h\perp\alpha}p_{h\perp\beta}S_{TT}^{\alpha\beta}
with
\tilde{p}_{h\perp}^{\alpha}=\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\alpha\mu}p_{h\perp\mu}
.

Substitute the hadronic tensors in Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
) into Eq. (
19
), and after making Lorentz contractions with the leptonic tensor, we will obtain the general form for the cross section.
To be explicit, we first parametrize the components of the transverse polarization vector and tensor as

\displaystyle S_{T}^{\mu}=|S_{T}|\left(0,0,\cos\phi_{S},\sin\phi_{S}\right),

(42)

\displaystyle S_{LT}^{x}=|S_{LT}|\cos\phi_{LT},

(43)

\displaystyle S_{LT}^{y}=|S_{LT}|\sin\phi_{LT},

(44)

\displaystyle S_{TT}^{xx}=|S_{TT}|\cos 2\phi_{TT},

(45)

\displaystyle S_{TT}^{xy}=|S_{TT}|\sin 2\phi_{TT}.

(46)

Then, the cross section can be divided into six parts according to the polarization states of the vector meson.
It is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dxdydz_{h}d\psi d^{2}p_{h\perp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}}{xyQ^{2}}\bigl(\mathcal{W}_{U}+\lambda\mathcal{W}_{L}+|S_{T}|\mathcal{W}_{T}+S_{LL}\mathcal{W}_{LL}+|S_{LT}|\mathcal{W}_{LT}+|S_{TT}|\mathcal{W}_{TT}\bigr),

(47)

where the subscripts of
{\cal W}
denote the polarization states of the vector meson.
The explicit expressions for each part are calculated to be

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{U}

\displaystyle=A(y)W_{U}^{T}+E(y)W_{U}^{L}+B(y)\left(\sin\phi\tilde{W}_{U1}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi W_{U1}^{\cos\phi}\right)+E(y)\left(\sin 2\phi\tilde{W}_{U}^{\sin 2\phi}+\cos 2\phi W_{U}^{\cos 2\phi}\right)

\displaystyle+C(y)W_{U}+D(y)\left(\sin\phi\tilde{W}_{U2}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi W_{U2}^{\cos\phi}\right),

(48)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{L}

\displaystyle=A(y)\tilde{W}_{L}^{T}+E(y)\tilde{W}_{L}^{L}+B(y)\left(\sin\phi W_{L1}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi\tilde{W}_{L1}^{\cos\phi}\right)+E(y)\left(\sin 2\phi W_{L}^{\sin 2\phi}+\cos 2\phi\tilde{W}_{L}^{\cos 2\phi}\right)

\displaystyle+C(y)\tilde{W}_{L}+D(y)\left(\sin\phi W_{L2}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi\tilde{W}_{L2}^{\cos\phi}\right),

(49)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{T}

\displaystyle=\sin\phi_{S}\left[B(y)W_{T1}^{\sin\phi_{S}}+D(y)W_{T2}^{\sin\phi_{S}}\right]+\sin(\phi+\phi_{S})E(y)W_{T}^{\sin(\phi+\phi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\sin(\phi-\phi_{S})\left[A(y)W_{T}^{T,\sin(\phi-\phi_{S})}+E(y)W_{T}^{L,\sin(\phi-\phi_{S})}+C(y)W_{T}^{\sin(\phi-\phi_{S})}\right]

\displaystyle+\sin(2\phi-\phi_{S})\left[B(y)W_{T1}^{\sin(2\phi-\phi_{S})}+D(y)W_{T2}^{\sin(2\phi-\phi_{S})}\right]+\sin(3\phi-\phi_{S})E(y)W_{T}^{\sin(3\phi-\phi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\cos\phi_{S}\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{T1}^{\cos\phi_{S}}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{T2}^{\cos\phi_{S}}\right]+\cos(\phi+\phi_{S})E(y)\tilde{W}_{T}^{\cos(\phi+\phi_{S})}

\displaystyle+\cos(\phi-\phi_{S})\left[A(y)\tilde{W}_{T}^{T,\cos(\phi-\phi_{S})}+E(y)\tilde{W}_{T}^{L,\cos(\phi-\phi_{S})}+C(y)\tilde{W}_{T}^{\cos(\phi-\phi_{S})}\right]

\displaystyle+\cos(2\phi-\phi_{S})\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{T1}^{\cos(2\phi-\phi_{S})}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{T2}^{\cos(2\phi-\phi_{S})}\right]+\cos(3\phi-\phi_{S})E(y)\tilde{W}_{T}^{\cos(3\phi-\phi_{S})},

(50)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{LL}

\displaystyle=A(y)W_{LL}^{T}+E(y)W_{LL}^{L}+B(y)\left(\sin\phi\tilde{W}_{LL1}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi W_{LL1}^{\cos\phi}\right)+E(y)\left(\sin 2\phi\tilde{W}_{LL}^{\sin 2\phi}+\cos 2\phi W_{LL}^{\cos 2\phi}\right)

\displaystyle+C(y)W_{LL}+D(y)\left(\sin\phi\tilde{W}_{LL2}^{\sin\phi}+\cos\phi W_{LL2}^{\cos\phi}\right),

(51)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{LT}

\displaystyle=\sin\phi_{LT}\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{LT1}^{\sin\phi_{LT}}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{LT2}^{\sin\phi_{LT}}\right]+\sin(\phi+\phi_{LT})E(y)\tilde{W}_{LT}^{\sin(\phi+\phi_{LT})}

\displaystyle+\sin(\phi-\phi_{LT})\left[A(y)\tilde{W}_{LT}^{T,\sin(\phi-\phi_{LT})}+E(y)\tilde{W}_{LT}^{L,\sin(\phi-\phi_{LT})}+C(y)\tilde{W}_{LT}^{\sin(\phi-\phi_{LT})}\right]

\displaystyle+\sin(2\phi-\phi_{LT})\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{LT1}^{\sin(2\phi-\phi_{LT})}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{LT2}^{\sin(2\phi-\phi_{LT})}\right]+\sin(3\phi-\phi_{LT})E(y)\tilde{W}_{LT}^{\sin(3\phi-\phi_{LT})}

\displaystyle+\cos\phi_{LT}\left[B(y)W_{LT1}^{\cos\phi_{LT}}+D(y)W_{LT2}^{\cos\phi_{LT}}\right]+\cos(\phi+\phi_{LT})E(y)W_{LT}^{\cos(\phi+\phi_{LT})}

\displaystyle+\cos(\phi-\phi_{LT})\left[A(y)W_{LT}^{T,\cos(\phi-\phi_{LT})}+E(y)W_{LT}^{L,\cos(\phi-\phi_{LT})}+C(y)W_{LT}^{\cos(\phi-\phi_{LT})}\right]

\displaystyle+\cos(2\phi-\phi_{LT})\left[B(y)W_{LT1}^{\cos(2\phi-\phi_{LT})}+D(y)W_{LT2}^{\cos(2\phi-\phi_{LT})}\right]+\cos(3\phi-\phi_{LT})E(y)W_{LT}^{\cos(3\phi-\phi_{LT})},

(52)

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{TT}

\displaystyle=\sin(\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{TT1}^{\sin(\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{TT2}^{\sin(\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]+\sin 2\phi_{TT}E(y)\tilde{W}_{TT}^{\sin 2\phi_{TT}}

\displaystyle+\sin(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[A(y)\tilde{W}_{TT}^{T,\sin(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+E(y)\tilde{W}_{TT}^{L,\sin(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+C(y)\tilde{W}_{TT}^{\sin(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]

\displaystyle+\sin(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[B(y)\tilde{W}_{TT1}^{\sin(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+D(y)\tilde{W}_{TT2}^{\sin(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]+\sin(4\phi-2\phi_{TT})E(y)\tilde{W}_{TT}^{\sin(4\phi-2\phi_{TT})}

\displaystyle+\cos(\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[B(y)W_{TT1}^{\cos(\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+D(y)W_{TT2}^{\cos(\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]+\cos 2\phi_{TT}E(y)W_{TT}^{\cos 2\phi_{TT}}

\displaystyle+\cos(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[A(y)W_{TT}^{T,\cos(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+E(y)W_{TT}^{L,\cos(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+C(y)W_{TT}^{\cos(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]

\displaystyle+\cos(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})\left[B(y)W_{TT1}^{\cos(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})}+D(y)W_{TT2}^{\cos(3\phi-2\phi_{TT})}\right]+\cos(4\phi-2\phi_{TT})E(y)W_{TT}^{\cos(4\phi-2\phi_{TT})}.

(53)

We have defined the following functions of
y
for simplicity:

\displaystyle A(y)=y^{2}-2y+2,

\displaystyle B(y)=2(2-y)\sqrt{1-y},

\displaystyle C(y)=y(2-y),

\displaystyle D(y)=2y\sqrt{1-y},

\displaystyle E(y)=2(1-y).

(54)

There are 81 structure functions in total, which is exactly the number of the corresponding 81 independent BLTs for the hadronic tensor.
Among all the structure functions, 39 of them, i.e., the
\tilde{W}
’s, correspond to the space reflection odd part of the cross section and the remaining 42 correspond to the space reflection even part.
We also note that there are 45 structure functions that depend on the tensor polarizations of the vector meson.
It can be checked that the cross section for
e^{-}N\to e^{-}VX
given in Eqs. (
47
)-(
53
) has the same form in terms of the azimuthal angle dependence as that for
e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{0}\to V\pi X
given in
Chen:2016moq
.
This is because these two processes share the same set of BLTs when constructing the general form of the hadronic tensors.

III
Parton model calculations

III.1
The hadronic tensor results in terms of TMD PDFs and FFs

We now calculate the hadronic tensor using the QCD parton model.
At the leading order of perturbative quantum chromodynamics and the leading twist, the hadronic tensor is given by

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}(q,p_{N},

\displaystyle p_{h},S)=\int d^{4}k_{i}d^{4}k_{f}\delta^{4}(q+k_{i}-k_{f})

\displaystyle\times{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\Phi}(k_{i},p_{N})\Gamma^{\mu}\hat{\Xi}(k_{f},p_{h},S_{h})\Gamma^{\nu}\right],

(55)

where
\hat{\Phi}
and
\hat{\Xi}
are the parton correlators related to the parton distribution and the fragmentation process.
They are defined as
Bacchetta:2006tn

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{ij}(k_{i},p_{N})=\int\frac{d^{4}\xi}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i\xi\cdot k_{i}}\langle p_{N}|\bar{\psi}_{j}(0)\psi_{i}(\xi)|p_{N}\rangle,

(56)

\displaystyle\hat{\Xi}_{ij}(k_{f},p_{h},S)=\sum_{X}\int\frac{d^{4}\eta}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-i\eta\cdot k_{f}}\langle 0|\psi_{i}(0)|p_{h},S;X\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 119.50148pt\times\langle p_{h},S;X|\bar{\psi}_{j}(\eta)|0\rangle.

(57)

We have suppressed the gauge links for short notations.
Taking approximations for the momenta in the
\delta
function of the hadronic tensor, i.e.,
k_{i}^{-}\approx 0
and
k_{f}^{+}\approx 0
, and after integration, we get

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=2z_{h}\int d^{2}k_{iT}d^{2}k_{fT}\delta^{2}(\vec{k}_{iT}-\vec{k}_{fT}-\vec{p}_{h\perp}/z_{h})

\displaystyle\qquad\times{\rm Tr}\left[\hat{\Phi}(x,k_{iT})\Gamma^{\mu}\hat{\Xi}(z_{h},k_{fT},S)\Gamma^{\nu}\right],

(58)

where the TMD parton correlators are defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{ij}(x,k_{iT})=\int\frac{d\xi^{-}d^{2}\xi_{T}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{ixp_{N}^{+}\xi^{-}+ik_{iT}\cdot\xi_{T}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\langle p_{N}|\bar{\psi}_{i}(0)\psi_{j}(\xi^{-},\xi_{T})|p_{N}\rangle,

(59)

\displaystyle\hat{\Xi}_{ij}(z_{h},k_{fT},S)=\sum_{X}\int\frac{d\eta^{+}d^{2}\eta_{T}}{2z_{h}(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\eta^{+}p_{h}^{-}/z_{h}-i\eta_{T}\cdot k_{fT}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\langle 0|\psi_{i}(0)|p_{h},S;X\rangle\langle p_{h},S;X|\bar{\psi}_{j}(\eta^{+},\eta_{T})|0\rangle.

(60)

The parton correlators are
4\times 4
matrices in Dirac space.
They can be expanded under the basis of Gamma matrices, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Phi_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha}-\tilde{\Phi}_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\alpha}+\Phi_{\alpha\beta}i\sigma^{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{5}\right)+\cdots,

(61)

\displaystyle\hat{\Xi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Xi_{\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha}+\tilde{\Xi}_{\alpha}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\alpha}+\Xi_{\alpha\beta}i\sigma^{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{5}\right)+\cdots,

(62)

where
\cdots
denotes terms irrelevant for the leading twist.
The
\Phi_{\alpha}
’s, etc., are expanding coefficients or are called correlation functions.
We have omitted the arguments of the correlators and correlation functions for short notations.
For an unpolarized nucleon, the Lorentz decomposition of the correlation functions only generate two TMD PDFs at the leading twist
Goeke:2005hb
.
We have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha}=\bar{n}^{\alpha}f_{1}(x,k_{iT})+\cdots,

(63)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{M_{N}}\bar{n}^{\alpha}\tilde{k}_{iT}^{\beta}h_{1}^{\perp}(x,k_{iT})+\cdots.

(64)

The first term defines the number density distribution, and
h_{1}^{\perp}
is the Boer-Mulders function which is chiral-odd.

Similarly, for the fragmentation part, the correlation functions are decomposed as
Chen:2016moq

\displaystyle\Xi^{\alpha}=n^{\alpha}\Big(D_{1}+\frac{\tilde{k}_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}D_{1T}^{\perp}+S_{LL}D_{1LL}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}D_{1LT}^{\perp}+\frac{S_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}D_{1TT}^{\perp}\Big)+\cdots,

(65)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Xi}^{\alpha}=n^{\alpha}\Big(\lambda_{h}G_{1L}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}G_{1T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{\tilde{k}_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}G_{1LT}^{\perp}-\frac{\tilde{S}_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}G_{1TT}^{\perp}\Big)+\cdots,

(66)

\displaystyle\Xi^{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{n^{[\alpha}\tilde{k}_{fT}^{\beta]}}{M_{h}}\Big(H_{1}^{\perp}+S_{LL}H_{1LL}^{\perp}\Big)+n^{[\alpha}S_{T}^{\beta]}H_{1T}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{n^{[\alpha}k_{fT}^{\beta]}}{M_{h}}\Big(\lambda_{h}H_{1L}^{\perp}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}H_{1T}^{\perp}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-n^{[\alpha}\tilde{S}_{LT}^{\beta]}H_{1LT}-\frac{n^{[\alpha}\tilde{S}_{TT}^{k_{f}\beta]}}{M_{h}}H_{1TT}^{\prime\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{n^{[\alpha}\tilde{k}_{fT}^{\beta]}}{M_{h}}\Big(\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}H_{1LT}^{\perp}+\frac{S_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}H_{1TT}^{\perp}\Big)+\cdots.

(67)

We have omitted the arguments of
z_{h}
and
k_{fT}
for the TMD FFs for short notations.
There are 18 TMD FFs at the leading twist, ten of them are tensor polarization dependent.

Substituting the decomposition of the parton correlators of Eqs. (
61
)-(
67
) into the hadronic tensor in Eq. (
58
), by carrying out the traces we can obtain the results for the hadronic tensor.
The relevant traces we need are

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\not{\bar{n}}\Gamma^{\mu}\not{n}\Gamma^{\nu}]={\rm Tr}[\gamma_{5}\not{\bar{n}}\Gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\not{n}\Gamma^{\nu}]=-4c_{1}^{q}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}-4ic_{3}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu},

(68)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\not{\bar{n}}\Gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\not{n}\Gamma^{\nu}]={\rm Tr}[\gamma_{5}\not{\bar{n}}\Gamma^{\mu}\not{n}\Gamma^{\nu}]=4c_{3}^{q}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+4ic_{1}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu},

(69)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[i\sigma^{-\alpha}\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\mu}i\sigma^{+\beta}\gamma_{5}\Gamma^{\nu}\right]=4c_{2}^{q}\Big(g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}g_{\perp}^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}-g^{\nu\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}\Big),

(70)

where
c_{2}^{q}=(c_{V}^{q})^{2}-(c_{A}^{q})^{2}
and the indices
\alpha
and
\beta
are both transverse.
To further simplify the expressions, we define a symmetric tensor

\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(a_{T},b_{T})=a_{T}^{\{\mu}b_{T}^{\nu\}}-(a_{T}\cdot b_{T})g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}
, and we also use the following notations for the combinations of FFs:

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv D_{1}+\frac{\tilde{k}_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}D_{1T}^{\perp}+S_{LL}D_{1LL}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}D_{1LT}^{\perp}+\frac{S_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}D_{1TT}^{\perp},

(71)

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\lambda_{h}G_{1L}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}G_{1T}^{\perp}+\frac{\tilde{k}_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}G_{1LT}^{\perp}-\frac{\tilde{S}_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}G_{1TT}^{\perp},

(72)

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle\equiv H_{1}^{\perp}+S_{LL}H_{1LL}^{\perp}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{LT}}{M_{h}}H_{1LT}^{\perp}+\frac{S_{TT}^{k_{f}k_{f}}}{M_{h}^{2}}H_{1TT}^{\perp}.

(73)

Using these notations, we obtain the hadronic tensor given by

\displaystyle W_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z_{h}}{x}{\cal C}\Biggl\{(c_{3}^{q}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{1}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu})f_{1}{\cal G}_{1}-(c_{1}^{q}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+ic_{3}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu})f_{1}{\cal D}_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad+2c_{2}^{q}h_{1}^{\perp}\biggl[\frac{\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{k}_{iT},S_{T})}{M_{N}}H_{1T}+\frac{\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{k}_{iT},k_{fT})}{M_{N}M_{h}}\biggl(\lambda_{h}H_{1L}^{\perp}+\frac{k_{fT}\cdot S_{T}}{M_{h}}H_{1T}^{\perp}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(k_{iT},S_{LT})}{M_{N}}H_{1LT}+\frac{\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(k_{iT},S_{TT}^{k_{f}})}{M_{N}M_{h}}H_{1TT}^{\prime\perp}+\frac{\alpha_{T}^{\mu\nu}(k_{iT},k_{fT})}{M_{N}M_{h}}{\cal H}\biggr]\Biggr\}.

(74)

The convolution
{\cal C}[\cdots]
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal C}[wfD]=x\int d^{2}k_{iT}d^{2}k_{fT}\delta^{2}(\vec{k}_{iT}-\vec{k}_{fT}-\vec{p}_{h\perp}/z_{h})wf(x,k_{iT})D(z_{h},k_{fT}).

(75)

We see that the hadronic tensor is given by the convolution of the TMD PDFs and FFs.
There are 18 different convolution modules, in which half of them are related to the chiral-odd TMD PDFs and FFs.

III.2
The cross section in the parton model

Substituting the hadronic tensor Eq. (
74
) into Eq. (
19
), and making Lorentz contractions with the leptonic tensor, we will obtain the cross section expressed by the TMD PDFs and FFs.
However, we notice that the indices in the hadronic tensor are taken by the convolution variables
k_{iT}
and
k_{fT}
.
In order to compare with the structure function results, we need to do a transformation to rewrite these terms using physical observables, so that the azimuthal angle modulations will be explicit.
To this end, we define a unit perpendicular vector
\hat{h}^{\mu}=p_{h\perp}^{\mu}/|\vec{p}_{h\perp}|
representing the direction of the transverse momentum of the vector meson.
Under the convolution, we have, from Lorentz covariance, e.g.,

\displaystyle{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\mu}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]=A\hat{h}^{\mu},

(76)

where
{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})
is an arbitrary scalar function of
k_{iT}
,
k_{fT}
, and
\hat{h}
.
The coefficient
A
can be obtained by contraction on both sides with the unit vector
\hat{h}_{\mu}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle A=-{\cal C}[(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})].

(77)

This means that, under the convolution, one can equivalently replace
k_{iT}^{\mu}
with
-(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})\hat{h}^{\mu}
.
For terms with two or more indices, we give the detailed derivations and expressions in Appendix
A
.
These algebras can also be found with a compact form in Ref.
Tangerman:1994eh
.

When contracting the hadronic tensor with the leptonic tensor, we need the following basic contraction results, i.e.,

\displaystyle L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}\cdot(c_{1}^{q}g_{\perp\mu\nu}+ic_{3}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp\mu\nu})=-\frac{2Q^{2}}{y^{2}}T_{0}^{q}(y),

(78)

\displaystyle L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}\cdot(c_{3}^{q}g_{\perp\mu\nu}+ic_{1}^{q}\varepsilon_{\perp\mu\nu})=-\frac{2Q^{2}}{y^{2}}\tilde{T}_{1}^{q}(y),

(79)

\displaystyle L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\alpha_{T\mu\nu}(a_{T},b_{T})=c_{1}^{e}\frac{2Q^{2}E(y)}{y^{2}}|\vec{a}_{T}||\vec{b}_{T}|\cos(\phi_{a}+\phi_{b}),

(80)

\displaystyle L_{ZZ}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\alpha_{T\mu\nu}(\tilde{a}_{T},b_{T})=-c_{1}^{e}\frac{2Q^{2}E(y)}{y^{2}}|\vec{a}_{T}||\vec{b}_{T}|\sin(\phi_{a}+\phi_{b}),

(81)

where we have defined

\displaystyle T_{0}^{q}(y)=c_{1}^{e}c_{1}^{q}A(y)+c_{3}^{e}c_{3}^{q}C(y),

(82)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{1}^{q}(y)=c_{1}^{e}c_{3}^{q}A(y)+c_{3}^{e}c_{1}^{q}C(y).

(83)

We will also define and use the following dimensionless coefficients to simplify the results:

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{fT}^{2}}{M_{h}^{2}},

(84)

\displaystyle w_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}}{M_{N}M_{h}},

(85)

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT}}{M_{N}},

(86)

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT}}{M_{h}},

(87)

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})+k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}}{M_{N}M_{h}},

(88)

\displaystyle w_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})+k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}}{M_{N}M_{h}},

(89)

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}+k_{fT}^{2}}{M_{h}^{2}},

(90)

\displaystyle w_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{M_{N}M_{h}^{2}}\big[4(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})+k_{fT}^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})\big],

(91)

\displaystyle w_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{N}M_{h}^{3}}\big\{k_{fT}^{2}[k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+4(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]

\displaystyle~~+4(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}[k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]\big\}.

(92)

We divide the cross section into two parts, according to the chirality of the TMD PDFs or FFs involved, i.e.,

\displaystyle d\sigma_{ZZ}=d\sigma_{ZZ}|_{\rm\chi-even}+d\sigma_{ZZ}|_{\rm\chi-odd}.

(93)

The chiral-even part is calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{ZZ}|_{\rm\chi-even}}{dxdydz_{h}d\psi d^{2}p_{h\perp}}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}\chi}{xyQ^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigl\{T_{0}^{q}(y){\cal C}\Bigl[f_{1}(D_{1}+S_{LL}D_{1LL})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|S_{T}|\sin(\phi-\phi_{S})\bar{w}_{1}f_{1}D_{1T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-|S_{LT}|\cos(\phi-\phi_{LT})\bar{w}_{1}f_{1}D_{1LT}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|S_{TT}|\cos(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})\bar{w}_{2}f_{1}D_{1TT}^{\perp}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\tilde{T}_{1}^{q}(y){\cal C}\Bigl[\lambda f_{1}G_{1L}-|S_{T}|\cos(\phi-\phi_{S})\bar{w}_{1}f_{1}G_{1T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|S_{LT}|\sin(\phi-\phi_{LT})\bar{w}_{1}f_{1}G_{1LT}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-|S_{TT}|\sin(2\phi-2\phi_{TT})\bar{w}_{2}f_{1}G_{1TT}^{\perp}\Bigr]\Bigr\}.

(94)

The chiral-odd part is calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{ZZ}|_{\rm\chi-odd}}{dxdydz_{h}d\psi d^{2}p_{h\perp}}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}^{2}\chi}{xyQ^{2}}c_{1}^{e}c_{2}^{q}E(y)

\displaystyle\times{\cal C}\Bigl\{\cos 2\phi~2w_{2}h_{1}^{\perp}\left(H_{1}^{\perp}+S_{LL}H_{1LL}^{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle-\lambda\sin 2\phi~2w_{2}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1L}^{\perp}-|S_{T}|\sin(\phi+\phi_{S})~2w_{1}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1T}

\displaystyle+|S_{T}|\bigl[\bar{w}_{0}w_{1}\sin(\phi+\phi_{S})+w_{3}\sin(3\phi-\phi_{S})\bigr]h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1T}^{\perp}

\displaystyle+|S_{LT}|\cos(\phi+\phi_{LT})~2w_{1}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1LT}

\displaystyle-|S_{LT}|[w_{3}\cos(3\phi-\phi_{LT})+\bar{w}_{0}w_{1}\cos(\phi+\phi_{LT})]h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1LT}^{\perp}

\displaystyle+|S_{TT}|\bigl[w_{4}\cos(4\phi-2\phi_{TT})+\bar{w}_{0}w_{0}^{\prime}\cos 2\phi_{TT}\bigr]h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1TT}^{\perp}

\displaystyle-|S_{TT}|\cos 2\phi_{TT}~2w_{0}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1TT}^{\prime\perp}\Bigr\}.

(95)

It is straightforward to obtain the EM and the interference contributions by doing replacements for the electroweak coefficients, e.g.,

c_{2}^{q}\to 1
and
c_{V}^{q}
for the EM and the interference parts, respectively.
To further unify the notations, we define
T_{0,r}^{q}(y)
’s and
\tilde{T}_{1,r}^{q}(y)
’s with
r=ZZ
,
\gamma Z
, and
\gamma\gamma
.
For the weak interaction part, we have
T_{0,ZZ}^{q}(y)=T_{0}^{q}(y)
and
\tilde{T}_{1,ZZ}^{q}(y)=\tilde{T}_{1}^{q}(y)
.
For
\gamma Z
and
\gamma\gamma
parts, we have

\displaystyle T_{0,\gamma Z}^{q}(y)=c_{V}^{e}c_{V}^{q}A(y)+c_{A}^{e}c_{A}^{q}C(y),

(96)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{1,\gamma Z}^{q}(y)=c_{V}^{e}c_{A}^{q}A(y)+c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{q}C(y),

(97)

\displaystyle T_{0,\gamma\gamma}^{q}(y)=A(y),

(98)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{1,\gamma\gamma}^{q}(y)=0.

(99)

For simplicity, we will not show the total cross section explicitly.
Instead, we give the explicit parton model results for the structure functions in the next section.

The structure functions results and the spin alignment

IV.1
Parton model results of the structure functions

By comparing the cross section given by the structure functions in Eq. (

) and the parton model results in Eqs. (

) and (

),
we get the structure functions results in terms of the convolution of the TMD PDFs and FFs.
We include all the contributions from
\gamma\gamma
,
ZZ
, and
\gamma Z
channels, i.e., Eq. (

).
To simplify the expressions, we define the following electroweak coefficients:

\displaystyle c_{11}^{\rm ew}=c_{1}^{e}c_{1}^{q}\chi+c_{V}^{e}c_{V}^{q}\chi_{\rm int}+e_{q}^{2},

(100)

IV.2
The spin alignment of the vector meson

Compared with the hyperon production, the tensor polarizations are unique for polarized vector meson production.
The tensor polarizations of the vector meson can be measured through the angular distribution of their decay products
OPAL:1997nwj
.
Among different components of the tensor polarizations, the spin alignment is perhaps the most interesting one that has been studied a lot.
The spin alignment
\rho_{00}^{V}
is defined by the
00
component of the spin density matrix in the helicity basis.
In terms of the differential cross section, it is given by
Wei:2013csa

\displaystyle\rho_{00}^{V}=\frac{d\sigma(\lambda=0)}{\sum_{\lambda=\pm 1,0}d\sigma(\lambda)}.

(132)

For the helicity
\lambda=\pm 1
states,
S_{LL}=1/2
, while
S_{LL}=-1
for the
\lambda=0
state, and all the other polarization components are zero.
Therefore, from the general form of the cross section in Eq. (
47
), we get

\displaystyle\rho_{00}^{V}=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{{\cal W}_{LL}}{3{\cal W}_{U}}.

(133)

Substituting the parton model results of the structure functions, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{00}^{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\Bigl\{1-\Bigl[\bigl(c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr){\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1LL}]+2c_{12}^{\rm ew}E(y)

\displaystyle\times{\cal C}[w_{2}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1LL}^{\perp}]\cos 2\phi\Bigr]{\Big/}\Bigl[\bigl(c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr){\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1}]

\displaystyle+2c_{12}^{\rm ew}E(y){\cal C}[w_{2}h_{1}^{\perp}H_{1}^{\perp}]\cos 2\phi\Bigr]\Bigr\}.

(134)

We see that the spin alignment depends on both the chiral-even FF
D_{1LL}
and the chiral-odd FF
H_{1LL}^{\perp}
.
It also depends on the azimuthal angle
\phi
in general.
However, experimentally, it is much easier to measure the
\phi
integrated spin alignment
\langle\rho_{00}^{V}\rangle
.
In this case, we have

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{00}^{V}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\bigl(c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr){\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1LL}]}{3\bigl(c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr){\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1}]}.

(135)

If we only consider the EM interaction, we can easily get the expression reduced from Eq. (
135
) given by

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{00}^{V}\rangle{\big|_{\rm em}}=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{e_{q}^{2}{\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1LL}]}{3e_{q}^{2}{\cal C}[f_{1}D_{1}]}

(136)

that a summation over different quark flavors is implicit both in Eqs. (
135
) and (
136
).
We see that
\langle\rho_{00}^{V}\rangle{\big|_{\rm em}}
is much simpler than
\langle\rho_{00}^{V}\rangle
and independent of
y
.
It is clear that the spin alignment of the vector meson is independent of the quark polarization and will deviate from
1/3
if the
D_{1LL}
FF is nonzero.

K^{*0}

We take the production of the
K^{*0}
vector meson as an example to give a rough numerical estimate of the spin alignment.
For the corresponding TMD PDFs and FFs, we consider only light flavors and take the Gaussian ansatz, i.e.,

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,k_{iT})=f_{1q}(x)\frac{1}{\pi\Delta_{f}^{2}}e^{-\vec{k}_{iT}^{2}/\Delta_{f}^{2}},

(137)

\displaystyle D_{1}(z_{h},k_{fT})=D_{1q}^{K^{*0}}(z_{h})\frac{1}{\pi\Delta_{D}^{2}}e^{-\vec{k}_{fT}^{2}/\Delta_{D}^{2}},

(138)

\displaystyle D_{1LL}(z_{h},k_{fT})=D_{1LLq}^{K^{*0}}(z_{h})\frac{1}{\pi\Delta_{LL}^{2}}e^{-\vec{k}_{fT}^{2}/\Delta_{LL}^{2}}.

(139)

They are factorized into a collinear distribution part and a Gaussian distribution part for the transverse momentum dependence.
It should be noted that the Gaussian widths, i.e.,
\Delta_{f}
,
\Delta_{D}
, and
\Delta_{LL}
can depend on different flavors in principle.
However, if we substitute Eqs. (
137

139

135

p_{h\perp}

More explicitly, we get the
p_{h\perp}

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\rho}_{00}^{K^{*0}}\rangle=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\bigl[c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr]f_{1q}(x)D_{1LLq}^{K^{*0}}(z_{h})}{3\bigl[c_{11}^{\rm ew}A(y)+c_{33}^{\rm ew}C(y)\bigr]f_{1q}(x)D_{1q}^{K^{*0}}(z_{h})},

(140)

where a summation over quark flavors is also implicit in the numerator and the denominator of Eq. (
140
).

We choose

and

as a typical value and show the rough numerical estimate of the spin alignment as a function of

in Fig.

In the numerical calculation, we have taken the CT14 next-to-leading order PDFs

For the FFs

V
Summary

Semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering is an important process for accessing the three-dimensional partonic structure of the nucleon and the hadronization mechanism.
We present a systematic calculation for
e^{-}N\to e^{-}VX
with unpolarized electron and nucleon beams and polarized vector meson production at high energies.
We give a full kinematic analysis for this process by considering both the electromagnetic and the weak interactions that introduce the parity-violating effects.
The results show that the cross sections are expressed by 81 structure functions.
Among all the structure functions, 39 correspond to the space reflection odd part of the cross section and 42 correspond to the space reflection even part.
We also carry out a parton model calculation for the process and show that there are 27 nonzero structure functions at the leading twist, in which 15 are related to the tensor polarizations of the vector meson and 13 are generated by the parity-violating effects.
The structure functions are given by the convolution of the corresponding TMD PDFs and FFs.
We also present the result for the spin alignment of the vector meson.
A rough numerical estimate is made, as an example, for the
K^{*0}
spin alignment.
It gives us ways for accessing the tensor polarization dependent FFs by measuring the polarization of the vector meson.
Future experimental studies such as the EIC will provide us with better opportunities to study the nucleon structure and the hadronization mechanism as well as the polarization effects in detail.

Appendix A
Transformations for the convolution

For terms with two indices, according to Lorentz covariance, we have

\displaystyle{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\mu}k_{fT}^{\nu}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]=B_{1}\hat{h}^{\mu}\hat{h}^{\nu}+B_{2}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}.

(141)

The coefficients
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
can be obtained by contracting with
\hat{h}_{\mu}\hat{h}_{\nu}
and
g_{\perp\mu\nu}
on both sides, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\cal C}[(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]=B_{1}-B_{2},

(142)

\displaystyle{\cal C}[(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]=-B_{1}+2B_{2}.

(143)

One can solve

\displaystyle B_{1}={\cal C}\Big\{\big[2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})+k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}\big]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Big\},

(144)

\displaystyle B_{2}={\cal C}\Big\{\big[(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})+k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}\big]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Big\}.

(145)

For terms with three indices, we only need to consider the form of
{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\rho}k_{fT}^{\mu}k_{fT}^{\nu}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]
in the calculation.
Note that the
\mu

\nu
indices are symmetric in this case and can be expressed by three bases. We have

\displaystyle{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\rho}k_{fT}^{\mu}k_{fT}^{\nu}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]=C_{1}\hat{h}^{\rho}\hat{h}^{\mu}\hat{h}^{\nu}+C_{2}\hat{h}^{\rho}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+C_{3}g_{\perp}^{\rho\{\mu}\hat{h}^{\nu\}}.

(146)

By contractions on both side with the three bases, one can also solve

\displaystyle C_{1}={\cal C}\Big\{\big[-4(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})-2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})-k_{fT}^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})\big]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Big\},

(147)

\displaystyle C_{2}={\cal C}\Big\{\big[-(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})-k_{fT}^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})\big]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Big\},

(148)

\displaystyle C_{3}={\cal C}\Big\{\big[-(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})-(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})\big]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Big\}.

(149)

For terms with four
k_{T}
’s, we need to consider the convolution with the form of
{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\{\mu}k_{fT}^{\nu\}}k_{fT}^{\rho}k_{fT}^{\sigma}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]
.
In this case, the
\mu

\nu
and
\rho

\sigma
indices are symmetric, respectively.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\cal C}[k_{iT}^{\{\mu}k_{fT}^{\nu\}}k_{fT}^{\rho}k_{fT}^{\sigma}~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})]

\displaystyle=D_{1}\hat{h}^{\mu}\hat{h}^{\nu}\hat{h}^{\rho}\hat{h}^{\sigma}+D_{2}\hat{h}^{\mu}\hat{h}^{\nu}g_{\perp}^{\rho\sigma}+D_{3}\hat{h}^{\rho}\hat{h}^{\sigma}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}+D_{4}\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\hat{h}^{\rho}\hat{h}^{\{\mu}g_{\perp}^{\nu\}\sigma}+\hat{h}^{\sigma}\hat{h}^{\{\mu}g_{\perp}^{\nu\}\rho}\bigr)

\displaystyle+D_{5}g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}g_{\perp}^{\rho\sigma}.

(150)

Also by contractions on both sides with the five bases, one can solve and get

\displaystyle D_{1}={\cal C}\Bigl\{\bigl[2k_{fT}^{2}[k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+4(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})]+8(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}[k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]\bigr]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Bigr\},

(151)

\displaystyle D_{2}=2{\cal C}\Bigl\{[k_{fT}^{2}+(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}][k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Bigr\},

(152)

\displaystyle D_{3}=2{\cal C}\Bigl\{[k_{fT}^{2}+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}][k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Bigr\},

(153)

\displaystyle D_{4}=2{\cal C}\Bigl\{(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})\bigl[(k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})+(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})\bigl(k_{fT}^{2}+2(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}\bigr)\bigr]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Bigr\},

(154)

\displaystyle D_{5}=2{\cal C}\Bigl\{[k_{fT}^{2}+(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})^{2}][k_{iT}\cdot k_{fT}+(\hat{h}\cdot k_{fT})(\hat{h}\cdot k_{iT})]~{\rm F}(k_{iT},k_{fT},\hat{h})\Bigr\}.

(155)

Fourier transform on the locus of cyclic spectral curves in the Hitchin base
Andrei Ionov

Abstract
. We compute the Fourier transform of some of the summands of the push-forward of the constant sheaf under the Hitchin map for SL n \operatorname{SL}_{n} restricted to the locus of cyclic spectral curves inside the Hitchin base (for SL 2 \operatorname{SL}_{2} all spectral curves are cyclic) and give an estimate on the support of the Fourier transforms of the other summands.



1.
Introduction

Ever since being introduced in
[
5
]
the Hitchin fibration

\chi\colon{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{A}}

of the moduli space of semi-stable Higgs
G
-bundles on a smooth compact algebraic curve
C
of genus
g>1
over the Hitchin base played a vital role in different areas of mathematics and mathematical physics. One of the most prominent applications is its use in the proof of the fundamental lemma (
[
10
]
). In this and other applications it is important to study the push-forward of the constant sheaf along
\chi
or, in other words, to understand how the cohomologies of the fibers of
\chi
vary along the base.

In this paper we approach the study of
\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
via its Fourier transform in the case of
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
. Unfortunately, although
{\mathcal{A}}
is isomorphic to an affine plane, it could not be canonically identified with a vector space (for
n>2
) and, therefore, at a first glance it is unclear what Fourier transform even means. For this reasons we restrict ourselves to a locus of cyclic spectral curves inside the Hitchin base
{\mathcal{A}}
, which is canonically identified with the vector space
H^{0}(K^{n})
(for
n=2
it coincides with the whole
{\mathcal{A}}
). We hope, however, that with the constructions of the upcoming paper
[
6
]
this obstacle could be overcome and results could be extended to whole
{\mathcal{A}}
, as long as there is a suitable definition of what the Fourier transform on
{\mathcal{A}}
is.

Thus, we study
\mathsf{FT}\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}|_{H^{0}(K^{n})}
. For
m<(n-1)(g-1)
or
m>(2n+1)(n-1)(g-1)
we completely describe the Fourier transform of the constituents of
R^{m}\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}|_{H^{0}(K^{n})}
with full support (Theorem
3.2
1), 2)) as direct sums of IC-extensions of certain simple local systems on the open parts
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
of secant varieties of
n
-canonical embedding
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
of
C
with
j\leq m
and
m-j
even. For general
m
we prove that the support of
\mathsf{FT}R^{m}\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}|_{H^{0}(K^{n})}
is contained in
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
(Theorem
3.2
3)). Finally, for
n=2
we compute the Fourier transform of the direct summands of
\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
, which do not have full support, known as endoscopic summands (Theorem
3.5
). Our results are painlessly transferred to the case of
K
being replaced with a general ample line bundle, with the bound
(n-1)(g-1)
being replace with the half of the maximal number of points
n
-th power of this bundle separates plus 1.

Some similarities with the setting of
[
4
]
are worth mentioning, but the results seem to be not related directly. Finally, it would be interesting to understand if our result has any number theoretic applications.

1.2.
Conventions and notations

We will assume the base field to be
{\mathbb{C}}
unless otherwise stated. All homology and cohomology groups are also assumed to be taken with
{\mathbb{C}}
-coefficients.

For an algebraic variety
X
we denote by
D^{b}_{c}(X)
the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves and by
\operatorname{Perv}(X)
an abelian subcategory of perverse sheaves. All the inverse and direct image functors are assumed to be derived unless the opposite is specified.

If
Y\subset X
is a nonempty locally closed subset and
L
is a local system on
Y
we put
{\operatorname{IC}}(Y,L)
for the IC-extension of
L
shifted to the degree
-\dim Y
. To simplify the notations for
Y\subset X
we denote
{\operatorname{IC}}(Y,{\underline{\mathbb{C}}})
simply by
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{Y}
. We sometimes put simply
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
for
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{X}\in\operatorname{Perv}(X)
if it cannot provide ambiguity.

For a vector space (or a vector bundle)
V
we denote by
\mathsf{FT}=\mathsf{FT}_{V}\colon D^{b}_{c}(V)\to D^{b}_{c}(V^{\vee})
the Fourier transform functor. It preserves the subcategory of perverse sheaves. For more detail and all the properties we will use, see for example
[
7
]
.

Let
p_{n}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0
be the
n
-th power map. The group of
n
-th roots of unity
\mu_{n}
act on
p_{n,*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
. For
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n
let
\mathbb{L}_{a}
be the local system, which is the direct summand of
p_{n,*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
corresponding to
a
viewed as a character of
\mu_{n}
.
Put
j_{0}:{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
for the natural inclusion. If
a\neq 0
we have
j_{0*}\mathbb{L}_{a}=j_{0!}\mathbb{L}_{a}=j_{0!*}\mathbb{L}_{a}
and
\mathsf{FT}j_{0!*}\mathbb{L}_{a}=j_{0!*}\mathbb{L}_{a}
. For
a=0
we have
\mathbb{L}_{0}={\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}
. We have
j_{0!*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}={\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
and
\mathsf{FT}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}={\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{0}
. Moreover,
j_{0*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}
and
j_{0!}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}
are respectively extensions of
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{0}
to
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
and vice versa and we have
\mathsf{FT}j_{0*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}=j_{0!}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-0}
.

All the quotients by the group actions are understood in a naive sense (not as stacks).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hitchin fibration

Put
G=SL_{n}
and let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{sl}}_{n}
be its Lie algebra. We fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
and a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
. We put
W\simeq S_{n}
for the Weyl group. The quotient
{\mathfrak{t}}/W
is identified with the space of of degree
n
polynomial of the form
x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-2}+\ldots+a_{n-1}
. In particular, there is is a non-canonical isomorphism
{\mathfrak{t}}/W\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
.

Fix throughout
C
, a smooth connected projective curve of genus
g>1
, and put
K
for the canonical line bundle over
C
. We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}
the moduli space of semi-stable Higgs
G
-bundles over
C
, i.e. pairs
(E,\phi)
, where
E
is a vector bundle of rank
n
with the trivial determinant and
\phi\in H^{0}(C,\mathop{\sf End}\nolimits(E)\otimes K)
is a Higgs field.

The
Hitchin base

{\mathcal{A}}
is the space of sections of
(K\otimes{\mathfrak{t}})/W
, which we also identify with the space of degree
n
polynomial functions from
H^{0}(K)
to
H^{0}(K^{n})
with the coefficient of
x^{n}
being
1
and of
x^{n-1}
being
0
. In particular, there is a non-canonical isomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=2}^{n}H^{0}(K^{i}),

where we consider on the right hand side we identify the vector space with the corresponding affine space. There is a "characteristic polynomial of
\phi
" map

\chi\colon{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{A}},

which is called the
Hitchin map
. For more details, see for example
[
5
]
.

For
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
there is a spectral curve
C_{a}
defined as a subvariety of the total space of
K
given by zeroes of the polynomial corresponding to
a
. The natural map
\pi_{a}\colon C_{a}\to C
is a ramified cover of degree
n
. We define
{\mathcal{A}}_{reg}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
to be the open dense subvariety of
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
for which
C_{a}
is smooth and connected. It is known (
[
3
]
) that for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{reg}
we have
\chi^{-1}(a)\cong\operatorname{Prym}_{a}
, where the Prym variety
\operatorname{Prym}_{a}\subset\operatorname{Pic}(C_{a})
is the kernel of the norm map
\operatorname{Pic}(C_{a})\to\operatorname{Pic}(C)
induced by
\pi_{a}
. The isomorphism is established by the direct image map
\pi_{a*}
together with a twist to adjust the determinant.

For
n=2
the spectral curve
C_{a}
is reduced if
a\neq 0
and it is reducible if and only if
a
is a square of a section of a line bundle over
C
(
[
9
, Lemma 11.3]
).

2.2.
Locus of cyclic covers

Note that, despite the non canonicity of the direct sum presentation, the summand
H^{0}(K^{n})\subset{\mathcal{A}}
is a canonically defined closed subvariety. We can describe it by introducing weights of the variables by making weight of
K
to be
1
, then quotient the ring of function
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{A}}]
by the ideal generated by all elements of weights strictly between
0
and
n
. Note that for
n=2
we have
H^{0}(K^{2})={\mathcal{A}}
. We denote
H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}=H^{0}(K^{n})\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{reg}
inside
{\mathcal{A}}
.

For
a\in H^{0}(K^{n})
the spectral curve
C_{a}
is given by
x^{n}+a
and
\pi_{a}
is the cyclic cover. There is an action of a group of
n
-th roots of unity by automorphisms of the cover. For an
n
-th root of unity
\zeta\in\mu_{n}
let
\iota_{\zeta}\colon C_{a}\to C_{a}
be the corresponding automorphism, so that
\pi_{a}\iota_{\zeta}=\pi_{a}
. On cohomology we get a direct sum decomposition with respect to the characters of
\mu_{n}
-action

H^{1}(C_{a})=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n}H^{1}(C_{a})_{i},

where
H^{1}(C_{a})_{0}=H^{1}(C)
and
\zeta\in\mu_{n}
acts by
\zeta^{i}
on
H^{1}(C_{a})_{i}
.

Let
\underline{C_{a}}
be the universal spectral curve over
H^{0}(K^{n})
which comes together with the map
\pi\colon\underline{C_{a}}\to C\times H^{0}(K^{n})
. We denote by
{\widetilde{\chi}}\colon\underline{C_{a}}\to H^{0}(K^{n})
the composition
\operatorname{proj}\circ\pi
of
\pi
with the projection on the second factor
\operatorname{proj}\colon C\times H^{0}(K^{n})\to H^{0}(K^{n})
.
Since
K^{n}
is very ample for
n,g\geq 2
the universal family
\underline{C_{a}}
is smooth. We then have a decomposition with respect to the characters of
\mu_{n}
-action

\pi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{\underline{C_{a}}}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n}{\mathbf{M}}_{i}

into a direct sum of perverse sheaves. We have
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}={\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C\times H^{0}(K^{n})}
and for
i\neq 0
the sheaf
{\mathbf{M}}_{i}
is a simple perverse sheaf which is an extension by
0
of a rank
1
local system away from ramification locus of
\pi
.

2.3.
On symmetric powers of curves

For an algebraic variety
X
we put
S^{m}X:=X^{m}/S_{m}
for its
m
-th symmetric power. If
X
is a smooth curve
S^{m}X
is also smooth and is of dimension
m
for all
m
.

On
C^{m}\times H^{0}(K^{n})
consider a sheaf
{\mathbf{M}}_{i}^{\boxtimes m}
and shift it to the perverse degree. It is a simple perverse sheaf, which has a natural
S_{m}
-equivariant structure. Note that
S_{m}
-stabilizers of points in
C^{m}
act trivially on the stalks of
{\mathbf{M}}^{\boxtimes m}_{i}
. It then descends to a simple perverse sheaf
{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i}
on the quotient
S^{m}C\times H^{0}(K^{n})
(see
[
1
]
). It is also Verdier self-dual and is an extension by
0
from outside the collections of points intersecting ramification locus of
\pi
.

Consider the projection
\operatorname{proj}^{m}\colon S^{m}C\times H^{0}(K^{n})\to H^{0}(K^{n})
on the second fiber. Let
{\mathbf{M}}_{i,a}^{m}
be a restriction of
{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i}
to
(\operatorname{proj}^{m})^{-1}(a)
(shifted to the correct degree). For the rest of the subsection we assume that
a\in H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}
. Then by the same argument the sheaf
{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i,a}
is also a simple perverse.

The formula of
[
8
]
for the cohomology of the symmetric power of a smooth connected algebraic curve
C^{\prime}
provides a canonical isomorphism

H^{*}(S^{m}C^{\prime})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j\leq n,i,j\geq 0}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{i}H^{1}(C^{\prime})\right)[-i-2j],

where the index
j
correspond to the multiplicity of the generator of
H^{2}(C^{\prime})
(hence the shift
-2j
). In particular, the natural map

H^{*}(S^{m}C)\to H^{*}(C^{m})=(H^{*}(C))^{\otimes m}

is precisely the inclusion of graded
S_{n}
-invariants. The following statement could be considered its refinement with respect to the cyclic covers.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

H^{0}(S^{m}C,{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i,a})\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^{m}H^{1}(C_{a})_{i}

and all other cohomology groups of
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,a}
vanish.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=S_{m}\wr\mu_{n}
be the wreath product group. It acts on
C_{a}^{m}
with the quotient
S^{m}C
. Denote by
q
the quotient map. Consider
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}}
as a
\Gamma
-equivariant perverse sheaf. Then
q_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}}
is a also
\Gamma
-equivariant perverse sheaf. Since
\Gamma
-acts trivially on
S^{m}C
the sheaf
q_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}}
decomposes into a direct sum of components corresponding to irreducible
\Gamma
-representations. Note that the generic stalk of
q_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}}
is a regular representation. We see that
{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i,a}
is precisely a summand corresponding to the one dimensional representation given by character
i
on each copy of
\mu_{n}
and trivial on
S_{n}
. Computing the cohomology of
q_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}}
commutes with the direct sum decomposition. Hence,
H^{*}(S^{m}C,{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i,a})
is the component of
H^{*}(q_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{C_{a}^{m}})=H^{*}(C_{a}^{m})=(H^{*}(C_{a}))^{\otimes n}
corresponding to the one dimensional representation above. But this component is precisely
\bigwedge\nolimits^{m}H^{1}(C_{a})_{i}
.

∎

2.5.
Secant varieties of
n
-canonical embedding

For a point
c\in C
we identify a line, which is a preimage in
H^{0}(K^{n})^{\vee}
of an image of
c
in
{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{n})^{\vee})
under the
n
-canonical embedding, with the dual to the fiber
K^{n}_{c}
of
K^{n}
at
c
, which is embedded by a map
(K^{n}_{c})^{\vee}\xhookrightarrow{}H^{0}(K^{n})^{\vee}
dual to the evaluation at a point map
H^{0}(K^{n})\to K^{n}_{c}.
We will need the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

For a finite collection
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}
of pairwise different points on
C
the evaluation map
e\colon H^{0}(K^{n})\to\bigoplus_{i}K^{n}_{c_{i}}
is surjective if
N<2(n-1)(g-1)
.

Proof.

Consider a short exact sequence of sheaves

0\to K^{n}(-c_{1}-\ldots-c_{N})\to K^{n}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{c_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{c_{N}}\to 0.

It yields a long exact sequence of cohomology groups.

0\to H^{0}(K^{n}(-c_{1}-\ldots-c_{N}))\to H^{0}(K^{n})\to

\to\bigoplus_{i}K^{n}_{c_{i}}\to H^{1}(K^{n}(-c_{1}-\ldots-c_{N}))\to 0,

where the map in the middle is
e
. Moreover,
H^{1}(K^{n}(-c_{1}-\ldots-c_{N}))\cong H^{0}(K^{-n+1}(c_{1}+\ldots+c_{N}))^{\vee}
. The latter cohomology group is
0
under the assumptions of the Lemma for degree reasons. ∎

Remark 2.7
.

The bound is exact, since, if
N=2(n-1)(g-1)
and the divisor
c_{1}+\ldots+c_{N}
is in the linear system of
K^{n-1}
the cokernel of
e
is one-dimensional.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
be the cone over the image of the
n
-canonical embedding
C\xhookrightarrow{}{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{n})^{\vee})
and let
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
be the
m
-th secant variety of
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
, i.e. the union of all planes spanned by
m
points on
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
. In other words a collection of
m
points on
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
and the elements of the respective fibers
(K^{n}_{c})^{\vee}
define a point of
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
. It follows from the Lemma that such presentation is unique for any point in
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
for
m<(n-1)(g-1)
.

We also need to study the addition map

\Sigma_{i,j}\colon(\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n}))\times(\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n}))\to\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i+j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n}).

Assume
i+j<(n-1)(g-1)
. For
k\geq 0
consider the fiber of
\Sigma_{i,j}
over a point in
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i+j-k}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i+j-k-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
given by a collection
c_{1},\ldots,c_{i+j-k}
of different points on
C
and nonzero elements
\xi_{c_{u}}
of the fibers
(K^{n}_{c_{u}})^{\vee}
. A point of this fiber could be described as follows.

Pick
0\leq l\leq\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
and choose
l
points
{\widetilde{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{c}}_{l}
on
C
different from
c_{1},\ldots,c_{i+j-k}
and nonzero elements of the fiber
\xi_{{\widetilde{c}}_{u}}
at each of them. Separate
c_{1},\ldots,c_{i+j-k}
into three disjoint subsets of sizes
k-2l,i-k+l
and
j-k+l
for each point
c_{u}
in the first group chose a nonzero element of the fiber at this point
\xi^{\prime}_{c_{u}}\neq\xi_{c_{u}}
. Now put
{\widetilde{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{c}}_{l}
with respective
\xi_{{\widetilde{c}}_{u}}
in the fiber, points in the first group with respective
\xi^{\prime}_{c_{u}}
in the fiber and points in the second group with respective
\xi_{c_{u}}
in the fiber together to get an element of
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
and
{\widetilde{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\widetilde{c}}_{l}
with respective
-\xi_{{\widetilde{c}}_{u}}
in the fiber, points in the first group with respective
\xi_{c_{u}}-\xi^{\prime}_{c_{u}}
in the fiber and points in the third group with respective
\xi_{c_{u}}
in the fiber together to get an element of
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
. The resulting point of
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n}))\times(\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n}))
goes to our starting point of
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i+j-k}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{i+j-k-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
and each point in the fiber is obtained in this way.

Choices of
l
and the ways to separate
c_{1},\ldots,c_{i+j-k}
into three subsets give different irreducible components of this fiber. For a given
l
there are
\binom{i+j-k}{k-2l,i-k+l,j-k+l}
of them and each has dimension
k
.

3.
Sheaves on Hitchin base

3.1.
Sheaves with full support

Restricted to
H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}
each
\operatorname{proj}_{*}{\mathbf{M}}_{i}
is a local system, which is in a single degree for
i\neq 0
and is just constant sheaf for
i=0
. We denote this local systems by
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
for
i\neq 0
. We put
{\mathcal{L}}=\bigoplus_{i\neq 0}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
. Note also that
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\simeq{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}^{\vee}
with the isomorphism provided by a choice of generator in
H^{2}(C_{a})
.

Over
H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}
the isomorphism
\chi^{-1}(a)\cong\operatorname{Prym}_{a}
implies an identification

R^{m}\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}|_{H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}}\simeq\bigwedge\nolimits^{m}{\mathcal{L}}=\bigoplus_{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n-1}=m}\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{1}}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n-1}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-1}.

Recall the relative Hard Lefschetz Theorem for
\chi
.
Under the decomposition into sum of
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
it could be stated as follows. For each
i
there is an element in
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}
, which is skew symmetric if
i=n/2
. It induces maps

\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n-i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}\to\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{i}+1}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n-i}+1}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}

for
i\neq n/2
and

\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n/2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2}\to\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n/2}+2}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2},

which are injections for
m_{n-i}+m_{n-i}<\operatorname{rk}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
or
m_{n/2}<\operatorname{rk}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2}/2
and surjections otherwise and in either case they split off as a direct summands. Moreover, composing these maps we get an isomorphism

\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n-i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\bigwedge\nolimits^{\operatorname{rk}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}-m_{n-i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{\operatorname{rk}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}-m_{i}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-i}

and

\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n/2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\bigwedge\nolimits^{\operatorname{rk}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2}-m_{n/2}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n/2}.

Theorem 3.2
.

1) For
m<(n-1)(g-1)
the Fourier transform
\mathsf{FT}_{H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg}}{\operatorname{IC}}(H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg},\bigwedge^{m}{\mathcal{L}}_{i})
is an IC-extensions of a rank
1
local systems on
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
for
i\neq n/2
and is a direct sum of IC-extensions of one rank
1
local systems on each
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{j-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
with
j\equiv m\pmod{2}
for
i=n/2
.

2) More generally, for
m=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n-1}<(n-1)(g-1)
the Fourier transform of the direct summand of
{\operatorname{IC}}(H^{0}(K^{n})_{reg},\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{1}}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{m_{n-1}}{\mathcal{L}}_{n-1})
not coming from smaller exterior powers via the relative Hard Lefschetz Theorem is the IC-extension of an irreducible local system on
\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})-\mathop{\textrm{Sec}}\nolimits^{m-1}({\mathcal{C}}_{n})
of rank
\binom{m}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}}
.

3) For any
m
the support of

Fix a partition of

\sum i\nu_{i}=m

Let

Consequently we have

\mathsf{FT}_{H^{0}(K^{n})}{\mathbf{M}}^{m}_{i}|_{{\underline{c}}\times H^{0}(K^{n})}=e^{\vee}_{{\underline{c}}!}((j_{0*}\mathbb{L}_{i})^{\boxtimes\nu_{1}}\boxtimes(j_{0*}\mathbb{L}_{2i})^{\boxtimes\nu_{2}}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes(j_{0*}\mathbb{L}_{ki})^{\boxtimes\nu_{k}}),

where
e^{\vee}_{{\underline{c}}}\colon\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta}(K^{n}_{c^{(\beta)}_{\alpha}})^{\vee}\to H^{0}(K^{n})^{\vee}
is the dual coevaluation map. Note that
\mathbb{L}_{di}
is trivial if and only if

For

-m-j-l

-m-j

3.4.
Constant sheaf on the endoscopic locus for
n=2

In this section we restrict to the case of
n=2
. For a nontrivial line bundle
{\mathcal{T}}\in\operatorname{Pic}(C)[2]
with trivial square
{\mathcal{T}}^{\otimes 2}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}
consider a square map
s_{\mathcal{T}}\colon H^{0}(K\otimes{\mathcal{T}})\to H^{0}(K^{2})
. Put
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}\subset H^{0}(K^{2})
for its image. Note that, from the Riemann-Roch theorem it follows immediately that
\dim H^{0}(K\otimes{\mathcal{T}})=g-1
.

By the description of
[
9
, Section 11]
the constituents of
\chi_{*}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}
without full support (and not just supported at
0
) are supported on
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}
. Furthermore, this constituents are constant along these subvarieties, as they relate to the Hitchin fibrations for the endoscopic group, which in this case is a torus, thus these fibrations are trivial. In this section we compute
\mathsf{FT}_{\mathcal{A}}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}}
.

A point
\xi\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee})
defines a quadric
Q_{{\mathcal{T}},\xi}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K\otimes{\mathcal{T}}))
by the equation
\xi(s_{\mathcal{T}}(x))=0
. Let
Z_{{\mathcal{T}},i}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee})
be a locally closed subvariety consisting of points
\xi
such that the quadric
Q_{{\mathcal{T}},\xi}
has rank
i
and let
{\widetilde{Z}}_{{\mathcal{T}},i}\subset H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee}
be the cone over
Z_{{\mathcal{T}},i}
with
0
removed. Collections
\{Z_{{\mathcal{T}},i}\}
and
\{{\widetilde{Z}}_{{\mathcal{T}},i}\}\cup\{0\}
provide the stratifications of
{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee})
and
H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee}
respectively.

Theorem 3.5
.

The Fourier transform
\mathsf{FT}_{\mathcal{A}}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}}
is an irreducible IC-sheaf, which restricts to
0
on
{\widetilde{Z}}_{{\mathcal{T}},i}
if
i
is odd and is a rank
1
local system in with monodromy group
\{\pm 1\}
restricted to
{\widetilde{Z}}_{{\mathcal{T}},i}
if
i
is even.

Proof.

The sheaf
{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}}
is an irreducible IC-sheaf and then so is
\mathsf{FT}_{\mathcal{A}}{\underline{\mathbb{C}}}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}}
.

By
[
2
]
it suffices to compute the Radon transform of the constant sheaf on the image of
{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathcal{T}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{2}))
, whose stalk at
\xi\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K^{2})^{\vee})
is then the reduced cohomology group
{\widetilde{H}}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K\otimes{\mathcal{T}}))-Q_{{\mathcal{T}},\xi})\simeq\mathop{\sf Ker}\nolimits(H_{*}(Q_{{\mathcal{T}},\xi})\to H_{*}({\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(K\otimes{\mathcal{T}}))))^{\vee}
. This group is nonzero only if rank of
Q_{{\mathcal{T}},\xi}
is even and only in one degree equal to the rank, where it is one dimensional with the action of monodromy by
\pm 1
.

∎

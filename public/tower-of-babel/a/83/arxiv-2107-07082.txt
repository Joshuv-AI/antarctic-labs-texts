Xinyue Cheng & Zhongmin Shen

Abstract
We establish some important inequalities under a lower weighted Ricci curvature bound on Finsler manifolds. Firstly, we establish a relative volume comparison of Bishop-Gromov type. As one of the applications, we obtain an upper bound for volumes of the Finsler manifolds. Further, when the S-curvature is bounded on the whole manifold, we obtain a theorem of Bonnet-Myers type on Finsler manifolds. Finally, we obtain a sharp Poincaré-Lichnerowicz inequality by using integrated Bochner inequality, from which we obtain a sharp lower bound for the first eigenvalue on the Finsler manifolds. Keywords: Finsler metric; Ricci curvature; weighted Ricci curvature; geodesic ball; volume comparison; Poincaré-Lichnerowicz inequality MR(2010) Subject Classification: 53B40, 53C60, 58C35

Introduction

Similar to Riemannian case, Finsler manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below are always of some amazing properties. For example, the second author proved the Gromov-Bishop volume comparison theorem in Finsler geometry under the condition that
{\rm Ric}\geq(n-1)\lambda
. As an application, he obtained some precompactness and finiteness theorems for Finsler manifolds (
[
10
]
). Later, Ohta proved a new version of the Gromov-Bishop volume comparison theorem on Finsler manifolds with weighted Ricci curvature bounded below,
{\rm Ric}^{N}\geq K
for some
K\in R
and
N\in[n,\infty)
(
[
5
]
). On the other hand, by using Bochner-Weitzenböck type formula, Wang-Xia obtained a sharp lower bound for the first (nonzero) Neumann eigenvalue of Finsler-Laplacian in Finsler manifolds with weighted Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}^{N}
bounded below (
[
13
]
). Q. Xia gave an (integrated)
p(>1)
-Bochner-Weitzenböck formula and the
p
-Reilly type formula on Finsler manifolds. As applications, she obtained the
p
-Poincaré inequality on an
n
-dimensional compact Finsler manifold without boundary or with convex boundary under the assumption that
{\rm Ric}^{N}\geq K
for
N\in[n,\infty]
and
K\in{R}
(see
[
16
]
).

In this paper, our main aim is to educe some important inequalities on Finsler manifolds with weighted Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}
bounded below. The paper actually consists of two parts: the first part is dedicated to the geometry of Finsler manifolds and the second part is dedicated to the analysis on Finsler manifolds.

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional Finsler manifold with a smooth measure
m
and
dm_{BH}
denotes the Busemann-Hausdorff volume form of

. Then there is a positive smooth function

Now, let

Typical examples are

\chi(t)=t^{n-1}
. Fix

of

Theorem 1.1

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold equipped with a smooth volume form
dm=\phi(x)dm_{BH}
and

\Delta\rho(x)\leq\frac{d}{d\rho}\Big[\ln\chi(\rho)\Big]|_{\rho=\rho(x)},\ \ x\in\Omega_{p}\setminus B_{p}(\rho_{o})

for a function

as above, where

denotes the cut-domain at

. Then for

\frac{{\rm Vol}(B_{p}(R)\setminus B_{p}(\rho_{o}))}{{\rm Vol}(B_{p}(r)\setminus B_{p}(\rho_{o}))}\leq\frac{\int_{\rho_{o}}^{R}\chi(t)dt}{\int_{\rho_{o}}^{r}\chi(t)dt}.

(1.2)

Moreover, if
\rho_{o}=0
and

where
B_{p}(R)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
R
at the center
p

\omega_{n-1}={\rm Vol}(S^{n-1}(1))
denotes the volume of the standard unit sphere in

.

We must point out that, under certain curvature bounds on the Ricci curvature and the S-curvature, one can find

[

]

]

]

Theorem 1.2

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold equipped with a smooth volume form
dm=\phi(x)dm_{BH}
and
p\in M
. Suppose that there are constants
K>0

{\rm Ric}^{\infty}\geq K>0,\ \ \ \ {\bf S}|_{B_{p}(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{K}})}\geq-\delta,

(1.4)

where

denotes the S-curvature of

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\phi(p)K^{-n/2}c\Big(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}\Big),

It is known that for a complete Riemannian manifold with a volume form, if

is strictly positive, then the volume is finite. Theorem

shows that this is true for Finsler manifolds and gives an estimate of upper bound of the volume. However, the Ricci curvature bound

does not control the size of the Finsler manifold and

and the volume is bounded too,
{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\phi(p)K^{-n/2}C(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}})

Finally, let us consider an analytic problem on Finsler manifolds with weighted Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}
bounded below. Starting from (
5.3

Theorem 1.3

(Poincaré-Lichnerowicz inequality)
Suppose that
(M,F,m)
is closed and satisfies

and
\operatorname{Ric}^{\infty}\geq K>0
. Then, for any
f\in H^{1}(M)
, we have the following

\operatorname{Var}_{m}(f)\leq\frac{1}{K}\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}f)d{m}-\frac{2}{K}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)dt\right)dm,

(1.5)

where
{\rm Var}_{m}(f)
denotes the variance of

g(t):=g_{\nabla u_{t}}\left(\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}}),\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}})\right),

\left(u_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is the global solution to the heat equation with

.

In

6

{\rm Var}_{m}(f)\leq\frac{1}{K}\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}f)d{m}.

(1.6)

Note that (

).

For a closed Finsler manifold
(M,F,m)

, the first eigenvalue

is defined by (

]

]

\lambda_{1}:=\inf_{f}\frac{\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}f)d{m}}{\operatorname{Var}_{m}(f)}.

Under the assumption that

However, according to Theorem

\lambda_{1}\geq K+\delta

\delta:=\inf_{f}\frac{2}{{\rm Var}_{m}(f)}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)dt\right)dm.

2
Preliminaries

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional manifold. A Finsler metric
F
on
M
is a non-negative function on
TM
such that
F
is
{\cal C}^{\infty}
on
TM\backslash\{0\}
and the restriction
F_{x}:=F|_{T_{x}M}
is a Minkowski function on
T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
. For Finsler metric
F
on
M
, there is a Finsler co-metric
F^{*}
on
M
which is non-negative function on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
given by

F^{*}(x,\xi):=\sup\limits_{y\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\xi(y)}{F(x,y)},\ \ \forall\xi\in T^{*}_{x}M.

(2.1)

We call
F^{*}
the dual Finsler metric of
F
. Finsler metric
F
and its dual Finsler metric
F^{*}
satisfy the following relation.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 3.1.1,
[
12
]
)
Let
F
be a Finsler metric on
M
and
F^{*}
its dual Finsler metric. For any vector
y\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
,
x\in M
, the covector
\xi=g_{y}(y,\cdot)\in T^{*}_{x}M
satisfies

F(x,y)=F^{*}(x,\xi)=\frac{\xi(y)}{F(x,y)}.

(2.2)

Conversely, for any covector
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a unique vector
y\in T_{x}M\setminus\{0\}
such that
\xi=g_{y}(y,\cdot)\in T^{*}_{x}M
.

Naturally, by Lemma
2.1
, we define a map
{\cal L}:TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
by

{\cal L}(y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{y}(y,\cdot),&y\neq 0,\\
0,&y=0.\end{array}\right.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

F(x,y)=F^{*}(x,{\cal L}(y)).

(2.3)

Thus
{\cal L}
is a norm-preserving transformation. We call
{\cal L}
the Legendre transformation on Finsler manifold
(M,F)
.

Take a basis
\{{\bf b}_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
for
TM
and its dual basis
\{\theta^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for
T^{*}M
. Express
\xi={\cal L}(y)=\xi_{i}\theta^{i}
. Then

\xi_{i}=g_{ij}(x,y)y^{j},

(2.4)

where
g_{ij}(x,y):=\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{2}\right]_{y^{i}y^{j}}(x,y)
. Let

g^{*kl}(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{*2}\right]_{\xi_{k}\xi_{l}}(x,\xi).

(2.5)

For any
\xi={\cal L}(y)
, differentiating
F^{2}(x,y)=F^{*2}(x,{\cal L}(y))
with respect to
y^{i}
yields

\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{2}\right]_{y^{i}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{*2}\right]_{\xi_{k}}(x,\xi)g_{ik}(x,y),

which implies

g^{*kl}(x,\xi)\xi_{l}=\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{*2}\right]_{\xi_{k}}(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}g^{ik}(x,y)\left[F^{2}\right]_{y^{i}}(x,y)=y^{k}.

(2.6)

Then, we can get (see
[
2
]
[
12
]
)

g^{*kl}(x,\xi)=g^{kl}(x,y),

(2.7)

where
(g^{kl}(x,y))=(g_{kl}(x,y))^{-1}.

Given a smooth function
u
on
M
, the differential
du_{x}
at any point
x\in M
,

du_{x}=\frac{{\partial}u}{{\partial}x^{i}}(x)dx^{i}

is a linear function on
T_{x}M
. We define the gradient vector
\nabla u(x)
of
u
at
x\in M
by
\nabla u(x):={\cal L}^{-1}\left(du(x)\right)\in T_{x}M
. In a local coordinate system, by (
2.6
), we can express
\nabla u
as

\nabla u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g^{*ij}(x,du)\frac{{\partial}u}{{\partial}x^{i}}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x^{j}},&x\in M_{u},\\
0,&x\in M\setminus M_{u},\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

where
M_{u}=\{x\in M\mid du(x)\neq 0\}.
Further, by Lemma
2.1
, we have the following

du_{x}(v)=g_{\nabla u_{x}}(\nabla u_{x},v),\ \ \ \forall v\in T_{x}M

(2.9)

and

F(x,\nabla u_{x})=F^{*}(x,du_{x})=\frac{du_{x}(\nabla u_{x})}{F(x,\nabla u_{x})}.

(2.10)

We must be careful when
du(x)=0,
because
g^{*ij}(du(x))
is not defined and the Legendre transform
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
is only continuous at the zero section. Besides, if
u\in\mathcal{C}^{l}(M),
then
{\nabla}u
is
\mathcal{C}^{l-1}
on
M_{u}
whereas only continuous on
M\backslash M_{u}
.

Let
(M,F,{m})
be a Finsler manifold equipped with a measure
{m}
on
M.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset M,
let
H_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
be the space of weakly differentiable functions
u
on
\Omega
such that both
u
and
F^{*}(du)
belong to
L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\Omega)
. Define the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:H_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
by

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(u):=\frac{\int_{\Omega}F^{*}(x,du)^{2}d{m}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dm}=\frac{\int_{\Omega}F(\nabla u)^{2}d{m}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dm}.

(2.11)

Further, define the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega):=W^{1,2}(\Omega)
as a space of functions
u\in L^{2}(M)
with
\int_{M}[F(\nabla u)]^{2}dm<\infty
, which is a Banach space with respect to the (absolutely homogeneous) Sobolev norm

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}:=\left\{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|F(\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\|F\left(\nabla(-u)\right)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}^{1/2}.

Let
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
where
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
denotes the set of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-functions on
\Omega
with compact support.

Associated with the measure
m
on
M
, we first decomposed the volume form
dm
of
m
as
dm={\rm e}^{\Phi}dx^{1}dx^{2}\cdots dx^{n}
. Then the divergence of a differentiable vector field
V
on
M
is defined by

{\rm div}_{m}V:=\frac{\partial V^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}+V^{i}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x^{i}},\quad V=V^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}.

(2.12)

One can also define
{\rm div}_{m}V
in the weak form by following divergence formula:

\int_{M}\phi\ {\rm div}_{m}V\ d{m}=-\int_{M}d\phi(V)\ d{m}

(2.13)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
.

Now we define the Finsler Laplacian
\Delta u
of
u\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}(M)
by

\Delta u:={\rm div}_{m}(\nabla u).

(2.14)

Equivalently, we can define Laplacian
\Delta u
on the whole
M
in the weak sense by

\int_{M}\phi\ {\Delta}u\ d{m}:=-\int_{M}d\phi({\nabla}u)d{m}

(2.15)

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
.

From (
2.14
), Finsler Laplacian is a nonlinear elliptic differential operator of the second order. Moreover, since the gradient vector field
\nabla u
is merely continuous on
M\backslash M_{u}
, even when
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M),
it is necessary to introduce the Laplacian in the weak form as (
2.15
).

By the definitions, we have the following for any smooth function
\varphi
on
M
,

{\rm div}_{m}(\varphi\nabla u)=\varphi\Delta u+d\varphi(\nabla u).

(2.16)

Let
V=V^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}
be a nonzero measurable vector field on
M.
One can introduce a weighted Riemannian metric
g_{V}
on
M
via

g_{V}(X,Y)=g_{ij}(x,V)X^{i}Y^{j},\quad\text{ for }X,Y\in T_{x}M.

In particular,
g_{V}(V,V)=F^{2}(x,V)
. For
u\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}(M)
such that
du=0
almost everywhere on
\{x\in M\mid V(x)=0\}
(in other words,
V\neq 0
almost everywhere on
M_{u}
), we can define the linearized gradient vector and the linearized Laplacian on the weighted Riemannian manifold
\left(M,g_{V},{m}\right)
by

\nabla^{V}u:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g^{ij}(x,V)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x^{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}&\text{on }M_{u},\\
0&\text{on }M\backslash M_{u},\end{array}\quad\Delta^{V}u:={\rm div}_{m}\left(\nabla^{V}u\right)\right..

(2.17)

It is easy to get the following by (
2.17
): for any
u\in H_{\rm loc}^{1}(M)
, we have

\nabla^{\nabla u}u=\nabla u,\ \ \ \Delta^{\nabla u}u=\Delta u.

(2.18)

Further, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(M)
satisfying
df_{1}=df_{2}=0
almost everywhere on
M\backslash M_{u}
, we have

df_{2}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f_{1}\right)=df_{1}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f_{2}\right).

(2.19)

Finsler geometry is just Riemannian geometry without the quadratic restriction (
[
3
]
). The Ricci curvature in Finsler geometry is just a natural extension of the Ricci curvature in Riemann geometry. However, we have a difficulty on the choice of a measure in Finsler geometry, because it is impossible to choose a unique canonical measure like the volume measure in the Riemannian setting. Naturally, by choosing an arbitrary measure
m
on a Finsler manifold, Ohta modified the Ricci curvature and defined the weighted Ricci curvature in Finsler geometry (
[
5
]
). Concretely, for an
n
-dimensional Finsler manifold
(M,F,m)
equipped with a smooth measure
m
and for any
v\in T_{x}M\backslash\{0\},
let
\eta:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow M
be the geodesic with
\dot{\eta}(0)=v
and decompose the volume form
dm
of measure
{m}
along
\eta
as

dm={\rm e}^{-\psi_{\eta}}\sqrt{{\rm det}\left(g_{ij}(\eta,\dot{\eta})\right)}\ dx^{1}dx^{2}\cdots dx^{n},

where
\psi_{\eta}=\psi_{\eta}\left(\eta(t),\dot{\eta}(t)\right):(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow R
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-function. Then, for
N\in R\backslash\{n\},
define the weighted Ricci curvature

{\rm Ric}^{N}(v):={\rm Ric}(v)+\psi_{\eta}^{\prime\prime}(0)-\frac{\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{N-n}.

(2.20)

As the limits of
N\rightarrow\infty
and
N\downarrow n,
we define the weighted Ricci curvatures as follows.

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{\infty}(v)

\displaystyle:={\rm Ric}(v)+\psi_{\eta}^{\prime\prime}(0),

(2.21)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{n}(v)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Ric}(v)+\psi_{\eta}^{\prime\prime}(0)&\text{ if }\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}(0)=0,\\
-\infty&\text{ if }\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}(0)\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Note that the quantity
\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}(0)={\bf S}(x,v)
is just the S-curvature with respect to the measure
m
and
\psi_{\eta}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\dot{\bf S}(x,v)={\bf S}_{|m}(x,v)v^{m}
in Finsler geometry, where
``|"
denotes the horizontal covariant derivative with respect to the Chern connection (
[
4
]
[
5
]
). Hence we can rewrite
{\rm Ric}^{N}(v)
as

{\rm Ric}^{N}(v):={\rm Ric}(v)+\dot{\bf S}(x,v)-\frac{{\bf S}(x,v)^{2}}{N-n}.

(2.22)

From (
2.22
), we can get the following further description about the weighted Ricci curvature in Finsler geometry. Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional Finsler manifold with
dm=\sigma(x)dx^{1}\cdots dx^{n}
.
Let
Y
be a
C^{\infty}
geodesic field on an open subset
U\subset M
and
\hat{g}=g_{Y}.
Let

dm:=e^{-\psi}{\rm Vol}_{\hat{g}},\ \ \ {\rm Vol}_{\hat{g}}=\sqrt{{det}\left(g_{ij}\left(x,Y_{x}\right)\right)}dx^{1}\cdots dx^{n}.

It is easy to see that
\psi
is given by

\psi(x)=\ln\frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{det}\left(g_{ij}\left(x,Y_{x}\right)\right)}}{\sigma(x)}=\tau\left(x,Y_{x}\right),

which is just the distortion along
Y_{x}
at
x\in M
(
[
4
]
).
Let
y:=Y_{x}\in T_{x}M
(that is,
Y
is a geodesic extension of
y\in T_{x}M
). Then, by the definitions of the S-curvature and the Hessian (
[
11
]
[
12
]
), we have

\displaystyle{\bf S}(x,y)=y[\tau(x,Y_{x})]=d\psi(y),

\displaystyle\dot{\bf S}(x,y)=y[{\bf S}(x,Y)]=y[Y(\psi)]={\rm Hess}\psi(y).

Hence,

{\rm Ric}^{N}(y)={\rm Ric}(y)+{\rm Hess}\psi(y)-\frac{d\psi(y)^{2}}{N-n}.

(2.23)

Obviously, (
2.23
) is an analogue of the weighted Ricci curvature in Riemannian geometry.

3
A relative volume comparison of Bishop-Gromov type and its applications

In this section, firstly we will derive a relative volume comparison of Bishop-Gromov type and prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold with a smooth measure
m
. Let
dm_{BH}
denote the volume form of the Busemann-Hausdorff measure
m_{BH}
of
F
. Then the volume form
dm
of
m
is a multiple of

,

dm=\phi(x)dm_{BH}.

These measures induce measures on hypersurfaces if a normal vector field is chosen. Let

. The volumes of a small geodesic sphere

and a small geodesic ball

with respect to
m

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}(S_{p}(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(p)\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}\Big\{1+O(r)\Big\},

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}(B_{p}(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(p)\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n}r^{n}\Big\{1+O(r)\Big\},

(3.2)

where
\omega_{n-1}={\rm Vol}(S^{n-1}(1))
and
S_{p}(r)
denotes the geodesic sphere of radius
r
at center
p
. See
[
12
]
.

Let
\Omega_{p}
be the cut-domain – the open domain enclosed by the cut-locus
{\rm Cut}(p)
at
p
. The distance function
\rho(x)=d_{F}(p,x)
is
C^{\infty}
on
\Omega_{p}\setminus\{p\}
.
Then
\tilde{S}_{p}(t):=S_{p}(t)\cap\Omega_{p}
is a
C^{\infty}
hypersurface in
M

4
A Theorem of Bonnet-Myers type

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold with a smooth volume form
dm=\phi(x)dm_{BH}
. The classical Bonnet-Myers Theorem in Finsler geometry says that, if
{\rm Ric}\geq(n-1)K>0,
then
M
is in fact compact and
{\rm Diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}
(
[
1
]
, Theorem 7.7.1). If the weighted Ricci curvature satisfies
{\rm Ric}_{N}\geq(N-1)K>0,N\in[n,\infty),
then
{\rm Diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}
(
[
5
]
).

In this section, we will consider the case when the S-curvature is bounded on the whole manifold and give a theorem of Bonnet-Myers type.

Theorem 4.1

Let
(M,F,m)
be an
n
-dimensional forward complete Finsler manifold with a smooth volume form
dm=\phi(x)dm_{BH}
and
p\in M
. Suppose that

{\rm Ric}^{\infty}\geq K>0,\ \ \ \ \ |{\bf S}|\leq\delta.

Then the diameter and the volume is bounded.

{\rm Diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}+\sqrt{\frac{\delta^{2}}{K}+n-1}\Big),

(4.1)

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\phi(p)K^{-n/2}C\Big(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}\Big),

(4.2)

where
C\Big(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}\Big)
is a constant depending only on
n
and
\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}
.

Proof
: Firstly let
N
be an arbitrary number with

N>n+\frac{\delta^{2}}{K}.

Let

H:=\frac{K(N-n)-\delta^{2}}{(N-1)(N-n)}>0.

Then

{\rm Ric}^{N}={\rm Ric}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{N-n}{\bf S}^{2}\geq K-\frac{1}{N-n}\delta^{2}=(N-1)H>0.

(4.3)

By a theorem of Bonnet-Myers type proved by S. Ohta in
[
5
]

{\rm Diam}(M)\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{H}}.

Then

{\rm Diam}(M)\leq f_{K,\delta}(N):=\pi\sqrt{\frac{(N-1)(N-n)}{K(N-n)-\delta^{2}}}.

The minimum value of
f_{K,\delta}(N)
over

is

\min f_{K,\delta}=\frac{\pi\gamma}{\sqrt{K}},

where

\gamma:=\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}+\sqrt{\frac{\delta^{2}}{K}+n-1}.

The minimum value is achieved when

N=n+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\sqrt{K}}.

This gives (
4.1
). In this case,

H=\frac{K}{\gamma^{2}}.

3.26

.

We now assume that
r<d_{p}\leq\pi\gamma/\sqrt{K}
. Let
\rho_{o}=r/2

3.2
(a), we have got two bounds (

) and (

Then we obtain an upper bound on

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}(B_{p}(\rho_{o}))+\frac{{\rm Vol}(B_{p}(r)\setminus B_{p}(\rho_{o}))}{\int_{\rho_{o}}^{r}\chi(t)dt}\int_{\rho_{o}}^{R}\chi(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi(p)\omega_{n-1}\Big(\frac{n-1}{K}\Big)^{n/2}\Big\{\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\sin^{n-1}(s)e^{\delta\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{K}}s}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}\sin^{n-1}(s)e^{\delta\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{K}}s}ds}{\int_{h}^{2h}\sin^{N-1}(s)ds}\int_{h}^{\pi}\sin^{N-1}(s)ds\Big\},

where

h:=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{\gamma}.

Thus there is a constant
C(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}})
depending only on
n
and
\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}
such that

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\phi(p)K^{-n/2}C\Big(n,\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{K}}\Big).

Note that when
\delta=0
,
\gamma=\sqrt{n-1}
,
N=n
and
H=K/(n-1)
. Then

{\rm Vol}(M)\leq\phi(p)\omega_{n-1}\Big(\frac{n-1}{K}\Big)^{n/2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}(s)ds.

Clearly, this is a sharp bound. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Q.E.D.

5
Poincaré-Lichnerowicz inequality

Let
(M,F,m)
be a Finsler manifold equipped with a measure
m
on
M
. In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we need some necessary lemmas. Firstly, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
7
]
)
If
(M,F,m)
satisfies that
\Lambda_{F}<\infty,\ {m}(M)<\infty
and is complete, then the constant function
1
belongs to
H_{0}^{1}(M)
, where
\Lambda_{F}
denotes the reversibility constant of
F
.

In our discussions,
(M,F,m)
always satisfies that
\Lambda_{F}<\infty
. Further, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.2

For any
f,u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(M)
satisfying
df=0
almost everywhere on
M\backslash M_{u}
, we have

\Delta^{\nabla u}f^{2}=2f\Delta^{\nabla u}f+2g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f~,\nabla^{\nabla u}f\right),

(5.1)

equivalently,

f\Delta^{\nabla u}f=\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{\nabla u}f^{2}-g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f~,\nabla^{\nabla u}f\right).

(5.2)

Proof.
By (
2.17
), we have

\nabla^{\nabla u}f^{2}=2f\nabla^{\nabla u}f.

Further, by (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla u}f^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{m}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f^{2}\right)={\rm div}_{m}(2f\nabla^{\nabla u}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f\Delta^{\nabla u}f+2df(\nabla^{\nabla u}f).

It is easy to see that

df(\nabla^{\nabla u}f)=g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}f~,\nabla^{\nabla u}f\right).

Thus we get (
5.1
). Q.E.D.

In the following, we say that
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}\geq K
for some
K\in{R}
if
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}(v)\geq KF^{2}(x,v)
for all
x\in M
and
v\in T_{x}M
.
The following integrated form of the improved Bochner inequality is important for our discussion in this section.

Lemma 5.3

(Integrated form,
[
7
]
)
Assume that
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}\geq K
for some
K\in{R}.
Then, given
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(M)\cap{\cal C}^{1}(M)
such that
\Delta u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(M),
we have

\displaystyle-\int_{M}d\phi\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}\left[\frac{F^{2}({\nabla}u)}{2}\right]\right)d{m}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\phi\left\{d({\Delta}u)({\nabla}u)+KF^{2}({\nabla}u)+d[F({\nabla}u)]\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}[F({\nabla}u)]\right)\right\}d{m}

(5.3)

for all nonnegative functions
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(M)\cap L^{\infty}(M)
.

The following lemma is derived from Lemma
5.3
and is necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 5.4

Assume that
(M,F,{m})
is compact and satisfies
{\rm Ric}^{\infty}\geq K>0
. Then , for any
u\in H^{2}(M)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(M)
such that
\Delta u\in H^{1}(M)
, we have

\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}u)d{m}\leq\frac{1}{K}\left\{\int_{M}({\Delta}u)^{2}d{m}-\int_{M}g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u),\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u)\right)dm\right\}.

(5.4)

Proof.
From (
5.3
) and by taking test function
\phi\equiv 1
according to Lemma
5.1
, we have the following

\int_{M}\left\{d({\Delta}u)({\nabla}u)+KF^{2}({\nabla}u)+d[F({\nabla}u)]\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}[F({\nabla}u)]\right)\right\}d{m}\leq 0.

(5.5)

Noticing that

\displaystyle\int_{M}d({\Delta}u)({\nabla}u)dm=-\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dm=-\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{M}d[F({\nabla}u)]\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}[F({\nabla}u)]\right)=-\int_{M}F({\nabla}u)\Delta^{\nabla u}\left[F({\nabla}u)\right]dm,

(
5.5
) becomes

K\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}u)dm\leq\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dm+\int_{M}F({\nabla}u)\Delta^{\nabla u}\left[F({\nabla}u)\right]dm.

Further, by Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle K\int_{M}F^{2}({\nabla}u)dm

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dm+\int_{M}\Delta^{\nabla u}\left[\frac{F^{2}({\nabla}u)}{2}\right]dm

\displaystyle-\int_{M}g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u),\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u)\right)dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dm-\int_{M}g_{\nabla u}\left(\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u),\nabla^{\nabla u}F(\nabla u)\right)dm.

From this, we obtain (
5.4
). Q.E.D.

In the following, normalizing
m
as
m(M)=1,
we define the variance of
f\in L^{2}(M)
as

\operatorname{Var}_{m}(f):=\int_{M}\left(f-\int_{M}fdm\right)^{2}dm=\int_{M}f^{2}dm-\left(\int_{M}fdm\right)^{2}.

(5.6)

Further, for any
f\in H^{1}(M)
, let
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the global solution to the heat equation
{\partial}_{t}u_{t}=\Delta u_{t}
with
u_{0}=f
. By the properties of the linearized heat semigroups, we have the mass conservation

\int_{M}u_{t}dm=\int_{M}fdm

(5.7)

and ergodicity

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}u_{t}=\int_{M}fd{m}\ \ \

(5.8)

in
L^{2}(M)
(see
[
6
]
[
7
]
).

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Let
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the global solution to the heat equation with
u_{0}=f
. Put
\Phi(t):=\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{M}u^{2}_{t}dm
. Then the ergodicity (
5.8
) implies that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}u^{2}_{t}dm=\left(\int_{M}fdm\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle{\rm Var}_{m}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f^{2}dm-\left(\int_{M}fdm\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(0)-\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(t)dt.

On the other hand, by the definition of
\Phi(t)
, we know that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}u_{t}\Delta u_{t}d{m}=-2\int_{M}du_{t}(\nabla u_{t})dm

(5.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}F^{2}\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}u_{t}\right)d{m}.

By (4.2) in
[
9
]
, we have the following

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[F^{2}\left({\nabla}u_{t}\right)\right]=2d\left({\Delta}u_{t}\right)\left({\nabla}u_{t}\right)

(5.10)

for all
t>0
. Hence, from (
5.9
) and by (
5.10
), we have

\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)=-4\int_{M}d\left(\Delta u_{t}\right)\left(\nabla u_{t}\right)d{m}=4\int_{M}\left(\Delta u_{t}\right)^{2}dm=4\left\|\Delta u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
t>0
. Thus, from (
5.4
) for
u_{t}
and by (
5.9
), we get the following

-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4K}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{K}\int_{M}g_{\nabla u_{t}}\left(\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}}),\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}})\right)dm,

that is,

-\Phi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2K}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{2}{K}\int_{M}g_{\nabla u_{t}}\left(\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}}),\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}})\right)dm.

Then,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}_{m}(f)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2K}\left(\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(t)-\Phi^{\prime}(0)\right)-\frac{2}{K}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)dt\right)dm,

where

g(t):=g_{\nabla u_{t}}\left(\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}}),\nabla^{{\nabla u_{t}}}F({\nabla u_{t}})\right).

From (
5.9
),
\Phi^{\prime}(0)=-2\int_{M}F^{2}(\nabla f)dm
. Further, it is not difficult to prove that
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\cal E}(u_{t})=0
(e.g. see
[
6
]
[
8
]
), and then,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{\prime}(t)=0
. Thus, we get the following

{\rm Var}_{m}(f)\leq\frac{1}{K}\int_{M}F^{2}(\nabla f)dm-\frac{2}{K}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)dt\right)dm.

(5.11)

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Q.E.D.

Generalized weighted composition operators on the hardy space H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 n ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
Molla Basir Ahamed, Vasudevarao Allu, Taimur Rahman

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the complex symmetrical characteristics of weighted composition operators W u , v W_{u,v} and weighted composition-differentiation operators W u , v , k 1 , k 2 , … , k n W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}} on the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 n ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}) over the Polydisk 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} , with respect to the standard conjugation 𝒥 \mathcal{J} . We specify explicit conditions that confirm the Hermitian characteristics of the operator W u , v , k 1 , k 2 , … , k n W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}} and describe the conditions necessary for it to exhibit normal behavior. Additionally, we identify the kernels of the generalized weighted composition-differentiation operators and their corresponding adjoint operators.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}
denote the boundary of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. The polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
can be represented as the Cartesian products of
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{T}
, respectively. Let
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\sigma)
denote the standard Lebesgue space over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, where
\sigma
stands for the normalized Haar measure on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. It has been established that the set
\{e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{m_{i}}:m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}
. If a function
f
belongs to
L^{2}
, it can be expressed by using its Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})
as

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{m_{i}}_{i}.

Furthermore, we define
L^{\infty}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as the Banach space containing all essentially bounded functions defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

The Hilbert space denoted as
H^{2}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, referred to as the Hardy space, consists of all analytic functions
f
defined on the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and satisfies the condition

\displaystyle||f||^{2}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(r\zeta)|^{2}\,d\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

It is a well-known fact that for a function
f
belonging to the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, there exists a boundary function
\lim_{r\to 1}f(r\xi)
, which is an element of
L^{2}
, for almost every
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The Poisson extension of this boundary function coincides with
f
on the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. By identifying a function in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with its boundary function, we can consider
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as a subset of
L^{2}
. Hence, the norm of
f\in H^{2}
can be denoted as

\displaystyle||f||^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(\zeta)|^{2}d\sigma(\zeta).

The reproducing kernel function for the Hardy space
H^{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(z)=K_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{\alpha_{i}}(z_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\bar{\alpha_{i}}z_{i})},

where
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
K_{\alpha_{i}}(z_{i})
’s are the reproducing kernel functions for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. The normalized reproducing kernel for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is denoted by
k_{\alpha}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}(z_{i})
, with
e_{\alpha_{1}}(z_{1}),e_{\alpha_{2}}(z_{2}),\ldots,e_{\alpha_{n}}(z_{n})
being the normalized reproducing kernel functions for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a separable complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. A conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear operator
\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
which satisfies
\langle\mathcal{C}x,\mathcal{C}y\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle y,x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity operator on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}})
is said to be complex symmetric if there exists a conjugation
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
T=\mathcal{C}T^{*}\mathcal{C}
. In this case, we say that
T
is complex symmetric with conjugation
\mathcal{C}
. The comprehensive examination of complex symmetric operators was initiated by Garcia, Putinar, and Wogen
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. The class of complex symmetric operators includes normal operators, binormal operators, operators with algebraic degree two, Hankel operators, truncated Toeplitz operators, and the Volterra integration operator. In the recent times, there has been a considerable amount of research devoted to investigate the complex symmetric composition operators that operate on classical Hilbert space of analytic functions (see
[
8
,
16
,
29
,
31
,
34
,
38
]
and references therein).

In this context,
W_{u,v}
refers to the weighted composition operator, while
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
represents the weighted composition-differentiation operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}
). The problem of determining when such an operator acts as a conjugation on the weighted Hardy space of the unit disk was fully resolved in
[
31
]
. Moreover, this finding was extended to the unit ball in
[
25
,
28
,
37
]
. Our paper will specifically concentrate on the unit polydisk scenario. However, as we move to higher dimensions, we confront not just combinatorial complexities but also unexpected barriers and novel phenomena. For instance, studying the boundedness of the operator
W_{u,v}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
becomes more complicated and challenging.

In general, achieving a complete characterization of all complex symmetric composition operators is difficult, encouraging researchers to investigate complex symmetric weighted composition operators with specific conjugation traits. In this paper, we delve into the analysis of complex symmetric weighted composition operators using the standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In section 2, the operators
W_{u,v}
and
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
are shown to possess complex symmetry on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as we explicitly determine the corresponding forms of
u
and
v_{j}
(where
j=1,2,\ldots,n
), taking into account the standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
. In Section 3, we establish conditions that are both necessary and sufficient for
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
to exhibit Hermitian characteristics. Section 4 deals with a sufficient condition for the normality of
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
. In section 5, we investigate the kernels of both
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
and its adjoint operator
W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
.

2.
Complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator on Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be an analytic function and
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
be an analytic self-map of the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
given by
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
, where
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
are analytic self-maps of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. We now define the weighted composition operator
W_{u,v}
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as follows:

\displaystyle W_{u,v}f(z)=u(z)f(v(z)),\;f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),

that is,

\displaystyle W_{u,v}f(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})f(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n})),\;f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

The main aim of this paper is to explore different characteristics of weighted composition-differentiation operators in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, exceeding current research outcomes. Going forward, we shall refer to the composition-differentiation operator as
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
, defined as

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f(z)=u(z)\frac{\partial^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v(z))

where
f
is an element of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. It is clear and straightforward that
W_{u,v,0,0,\ldots,0}=W_{u,v}
.

Assuming
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}
are non-negative integers and
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, we define
K_{w}^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}(z)
as the reproducing kernel for evaluating the
\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
-th partial derivative at a specific point
w
, where

\displaystyle K_{w}^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}i}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}},z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

It can be easily verified that
\bigg\langle f(z),K_{w}^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}(z)\bigg\rangle=\frac{\partial^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(w)
.

Here the weighted composition conjugation on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is of the form

\displaystyle A_{u,v}f(z)=u(z)\overline{f(\overline{v(z)})},f\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

that is,

\displaystyle A_{u,v}f(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\overline{f(\overline{v_{1}(z_{1})},\overline{v_{2}(z_{2})},\ldots,\overline{v_{n}(z_{n})})},f\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

If the function
u
is identically equal to
1
, and
v
is the identity map of the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the weighted composition conjugation
A_{u,v}
simplifies to the standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
, defined as
\mathcal{J}f(z):=\overline{f(\bar{z})}
. One can easily see that
A_{u,v}=W_{u,v}\mathcal{J}
. For a more comprehensive understanding of weighted composition conjugations, we refer to
[
31
]
and references therein.

In the following, we provide two lemmas that are focused on the action of the adjoint of the composition operator
W_{u,v}
and composition-differentiation operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
on the reproducing kernel of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. In the entirety of the article, we will use the notation
m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{k_{i}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then for any
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,}K_{w}(z)=\overline{u(w)}K_{v(w)}(z).

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\langle f,W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle W_{u,v}f,K_{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle uf(v),K_{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(w)f(v(w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,\overline{u(w)}K_{w}\rangle.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}\rangle=\langle f,\overline{u(w)}K_{w}\rangle\;\mbox{for all}\;f\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

which leads the following conclusion

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,}K_{w}(z)=\overline{u(w)}K_{v(w)}(z).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then for any
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}(z)

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\langle f,W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f,K_{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v),K_{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(w)\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v(w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}\right\rangle.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}\rangle=\left\langle f,\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}\right\rangle\;\mbox{for all}\;f\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

which leads the following conclusion

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}(z).

This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we are aiming to examine the circumstances under which specific combinations of
u
and
v_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)
give rise to
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operators
W_{u,v}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric if, and only if,

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a=u(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
,
d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

If we substitute the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
with the bidisk
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, then Theorem
2.1
becomes a more comprehensive version of
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
, which Han
et

al

[
27
]
recently established. Consequently, we can easily derive the following result.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{2}\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{2}
such that
W_{u,v}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Then
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric if, and only if,

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{a}{(1-cz_{1})(1-dz_{2})},\;v_{1}(z_{1})=\frac{(e-c^{2})z_{1}+c}{1-cz_{1}},\;v_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{(f-d^{2})z_{2}+d}{1-dz_{2}},

where
a=u(0,0),\;c=v_{1}(0),\;d=v_{2}(0),\;e=v^{\prime}_{1}(0),\;f=v^{\prime}_{2}(0)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

First we suppose that
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric. Then, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle W_{u,v}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}(z)

for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. An easy computation shows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle W_{u,v}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=u(z)\mathcal{J}K_{w}(v(z))=u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}(z)=\mathcal{J}\overline{u(w)}K_{v(w)}(z)=u(w)\overline{K_{v(w)}}(\bar{z})=u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))}.

With the help of equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain from (
2.1
) that

(2.4)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))}=u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))}

By substituting
w_{1}=w_{2}=\ldots=w_{n}=0
in (
2.4
), we obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=u(0,0,\ldots,0)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})}.

Since
u
is not identically zero on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, we have that
u(0,0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
. By the virtue of equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), it follows that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i})).

By differentiating (
2.6
) partially with respect to
w_{j}
(where
1\leq j\leq n
), we obtain

(2.7)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))\times(-v_{j}(z_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))\times(-v_{j}(0))

\displaystyle+\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))\times(-z_{j}v^{\prime}_{j}(w_{j})).

Letting
z_{i}=0
for
i\neq j
and
w_{i}=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
in (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-v_{j}(0)z_{j})v_{j}(z_{j})=z_{j}v^{\prime}_{j}(0)+(1-v_{j}(0)z_{j})v_{j}(0).

This implies that

\displaystyle v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(v^{\prime}_{j}(0)-v^{2}_{j}(0))z_{j}+v_{j}(0)}{1-v_{j}(0)z_{j}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n

with
a=u(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
,
d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Conversely, we suppose that

(2.8)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n

with
a=u(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
,
d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. By using (
2.8
), we have from equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

(2.9)

\displaystyle W_{u,v}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\bigg(1-w_{i}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}z_{i}-w_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}))}

and

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}w_{i})}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\bigg(1-z_{i}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}w_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-c_{i}w_{i}-z_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}))}.

Since

\displaystyle 1-c_{i}z_{i}-w_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i})=1-c_{i}w_{i}-z_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}),

we obtain from equations (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) that
W_{u,v}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v}K_{w}(z)
, which implies that
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result centers on the investigation of which combinations of
u
and

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric if, and only if,

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a=\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}u(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
,
d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)
(
j=1,2,\ldots,n
).

Remark 2.1
.

First, we have to find conditions on
c_{i}
and
d_{i}
such that
v_{i}(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
.
A simple computation yields that

\displaystyle|v_{i}(z_{i})|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|c_{i}+\frac{d_{i}z_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq|c_{i}|-\bigg|\frac{d_{i}z_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg|

\displaystyle>|c_{i}|+\bigg|\frac{d_{i}z_{i}}{c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=|c_{i}|+\bigg|\frac{d_{i}}{c_{i}}\bigg|.

Therefore, if
|v_{i}(z_{i})|<1
, then we see that
|c_{i}|+\bigg|\frac{d_{i}}{c_{i}}\bigg|<1
, which shows that
|d_{i}|<1-|c_{i}|^{2}
. Thus, it is easy to see that
v_{i}(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
if
|d_{i}|<1-|c_{i}|^{2}
.

Next, we have to find the conditions on
c_{i}
and
d_{i}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded. A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle||W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)||=

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\overline{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{(1-\overline{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}\bigg|\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\overline{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{\bigg(1-\overline{w_{i}}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)^{k_{i}+1}}\bigg|\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!(\overline{w_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i}-\overline{w_{i}}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}\bigg|\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!}{|(1-c_{i}z_{i}-d_{i}\overline{w_{i}}-c_{i}^{2}z_{i}\overline{w_{i}}+c_{i}\overline{w_{i}})|^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!}{(1-|c_{i}z_{i}|-|d_{i}w_{i}|-|c_{i}^{2}z_{i}w_{i}|-|c_{i}w_{i}|)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!}{|(1-c_{i}z_{i}-d_{i}\overline{w_{i}}-c_{i}^{2}z_{i}\overline{w_{i}}+c_{i}\overline{w_{i}})|^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!}{(1-|c_{i}|-|d_{i}|-|c_{i}^{2}|-|c_{i}|)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!}{(1-2|c_{i}|-|d_{i}|-|c_{i}|^{2})^{k_{i}+1}}.

It follows that
||W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)||
is finite if
1-2|c_{i}|-|d_{i}|-|c_{i}|^{2}>0
. This turns out that
|d_{i}|<1-2|c_{i}|-|c_{i}|^{2}<(1-|c_{i}|)^{2}
. Since the span of the reproducing kernel functions is dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, the quantity
||W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f(z)||
is finite for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if
|d_{i}|<(1-|c_{i}|)^{2}
. Therefore, the operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
if
|d_{i}|<(1-|c_{i}|)^{2}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
be
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric. Then, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)

for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Moreover, it can be easily seen through computation that

(2.12)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle u(z)\bigg(\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-w_{i}z_{i})}\bigg)(v(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(w)\overline{K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}(\bar{z})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}.

Thus, the equation (
2.11
) is equivalent to

(2.14)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}.

Substituting
z_{1}=0,z_{2}=0,\ldots,z_{n}=0
in (
2.14
), we obtain
u(0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=0,u(z_{1},0,\ldots,z_{n})=0,\ldots,u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n-1},0)=0
and
u(0,0,\ldots,0)=0
respectively, which leads us to the conclusion that
u
contains factors of
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}
.

Let
u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{t_{i}}_{i}
, where
t_{i}(i=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{N}
and
h
is analytic with
h(0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0,h(z_{1},0,\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0,\ldots,h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n-1},0)\neq 0
and
h(0,0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
. Then, we obtain from (
2.14
) that

(2.15)

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}}_{i}z^{t_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=h(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{t_{i}}_{i}z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}.

Now, we claim that
k_{i}=t_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. If
k_{i}>t_{i}
, it follows from (
2.15
) that

(2.16)

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}-t_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=h(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}-t_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

Setting
z_{1}=0
in (
2.16
), we obtain
h(0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=0
, which is a contradiction because
h(0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0
. If
k_{i}<t_{i}
, we obtain from (
2.15
) that

(2.17)

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{t_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=h(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{t_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}.

Putting
w_{1}=0
in (
2.17
), we get
h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
k_{i}=t_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
and hence the equation (
2.15
) reduces to

(2.18)

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=h(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}.

Letting
w_{i}=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
in (
2.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=h(0,0,\ldots,0)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}

and hence

(2.19)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=h(0,0,\ldots,0)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}.

Since
h(0,0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
, it follows from equations (
2.14
) and(
2.19
) that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}.

Taking the partial derivative with respect to the variable
w_{j}(1\leq j\leq n)
in (
2.20
), we obtain

(2.21)

\displaystyle(k_{j}

\displaystyle+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-w_{j}v_{j}(z_{j}))^{k_{j}}(-v_{j}(z_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{j}+1)\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-v_{j}(0)w_{j})^{k_{j}}(-v_{j}(0))

\displaystyle+(k_{j}+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-v_{i}(0)w_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-z_{j}v_{j}(w_{j}))^{k_{j}}(-z_{j}v^{\prime}_{j}(w_{j})).

Letting
w_{i}=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
and
z_{i}=0
for
i\neq j
in (
2.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-v_{j}(0)z_{j})^{k_{j}+1}(v_{j}(z_{j}))=(1-v_{j}(0)z_{j})^{k_{j}+1}(v_{j}(0))+(1-v_{j}(0)z_{j})^{k_{j}}(z_{j}v^{\prime}_{j}(0)),

which implies that

\displaystyle v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(v^{\prime}_{j}(0)-v^{2}_{j}(0))z_{j}+v_{j}(0)}{1-v_{j}(0)z_{j}}.

Thus, we easily obtain the following

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a=\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}u(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
,
d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)
(
j=1,2,\ldots,n
).

Conversely, suppose that

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-c_{j}^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-c_{j}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where

\displaystyle a=\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}u(0,0,\ldots,0)\;\;\mbox{and}\;c_{j}=v_{j}(0),\;d_{j}=v^{\prime}(0)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;j=1,2,\ldots,n).

In view of (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), a simple computation shows that

(2.22)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-w_{i}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{\bigg(1-w_{i}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}z_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!(w_{i}z_{i})^{k}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}z_{i}-w_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

and

(2.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}v_{i}(w_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}w_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{\bigg(1-z_{i}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}}{1-c_{i}w_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!(w_{i}z_{i})^{k}_{i}}{(1-c_{i}w_{i}-z_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Since

\displaystyle 1-c_{i}z_{i}-w_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})z_{i}+c_{i})=1-c_{i}w_{i}-z_{i}((d_{i}-c_{i}^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}),

we obtain from (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}K_{w}(z)=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)
, which implies that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Hermitian property of composition-differentiation operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

A bounded linear operator
T
is Hermitian (self-adjoint) if
T^{*}=T
. Recent research has been focused on investigating the self-adjointness of weighted composition-differentiation operators in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (see
[
15
,
31
,
33
]
and other related works). In 2021, Fatehi and Hummond
[
14
]
presented a complete characterization of the self-adjointness of weighted composition-differentiation operators in the Hardy space over the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In 2023, Liu
et al

[
32
]
discussed the self-adjointness of the weighted composition-differentiation operator in the Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
. In 2012, Le
[
30
]
derived characterizations of self-adjoint weighted composition operators on Hilbert spaces of analytic functions over the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|<1\}
. On a similar note, in
[
25
]
, Han and Wang investigated the self-adjointness of the composition operator on Hilbert spaces of analytic functions over the same unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. No research has been carried out on the self-adjointness of the composition-differentiation operator in relation to the Hardy spaces over the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Our aim is to investigate the self-adjoint properties over the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. In the following result, we establish a condition that is both necessary and sufficient for the bounded composition-differentiation operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
to satisfy Hermitian properties on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is Hermitian if, and only if,

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-|c_{j}|^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{C}
.

The defining formulas of
W_{u,v}
and
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
make it evident that
W_{u,v,0,0,\ldots,0}=W_{u,v}
. Moreover, the implications of Theorem
3.1
reveal that when
k_{1}=k_{2}=\ldots=k_{n}=0
,
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
coincides with
W_{u,v}
. Consequently, we can readily derive the following result from Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Then
W_{u,v}
is Hermitian if, and only if,

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-|c_{j}|^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
be Hermitian. Then
W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}=W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
, which is equivalent to

(3.1)

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)

for all
w,z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. A simple computation shows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle u(z)\bigg(\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}z_{i})}\bigg)(v(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\bar{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle\overline{u(w)}K^{[k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}]}_{v(w)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}}.

By using the equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we obtain from (
3.1
) that

(3.4)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\bar{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=\overline{u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}}.

By applying a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we can assume that
u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{k_{i}}_{i}
and
h
is analytic with
h(0,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0,h(z_{1},0,\ldots,z_{n})\neq 0,\ldots,h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n-1},0)\neq 0
and
h(0,0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
. Then, we obtain from (
3.4
) that

(3.5)

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}=\overline{h(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}}.

Putting
w_{i}=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
in (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\overline{h(0,0,\ldots,0)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\overline{v_{i}(0)}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}

and hence

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}

with
a=\overline{h(0,0,\ldots,0)}
and
c_{i}=v_{i}(0)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Substituting this in (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\bar{a}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}

for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. If we set
w_{1}=w_{2}=\ldots=w_{n}=0
, then it is easy to see that
\bar{a}=a
, and hence
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Taking the derivative partially with respect to
\bar{w_{j}}(1\leq j\leq n)
in (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle(k_{j}+1)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-\bar{w_{j}}v_{j}(z_{j}))^{k_{j}}(-v_{j}(z_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{j}+1)\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-c_{j}\bar{w_{j}})^{k_{j}}(-c_{j})

\displaystyle+(k_{j}+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}})^{k_{i}+1}\times\prod_{i\neq j}^{n}(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}\times(1-z_{j}\overline{v_{j}(w_{j})})^{k_{j}}(-z_{j}\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}(w_{j})})

for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Setting
w_{1}=w_{2}=\cdots=w_{n}=0
in (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle(1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j})^{k_{j}+1}v_{j}(z_{j})=(1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j})^{k_{j}+1}c_{j}+(1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j})^{k_{j}}(z_{j}\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}(0)})

which implies that

(3.8)

\displaystyle v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j})c_{j}+z_{j}\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}(0)}}{1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j}}=\frac{(d_{j}-|c_{j}|^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for all}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n

with
d_{j}=\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}(0)}
and
c_{j}=v_{j}(0)
.

Differentiating (
3.8
) partially with respect to
z_{j}
, we obtain
v^{\prime}_{j}(0)=d_{j}
. Therefore, we see that
\bar{d_{j}}=d_{j}
which implies that
d_{j}(1\leq j\leq n)
are real numbers. Conversely, assume that

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=\frac{(d_{j}-|c_{j}|^{2})z_{j}+c_{j}}{1-\bar{c_{j}}z_{j}}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{C}
. In view of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\bar{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{(1-\bar{w_{i}}v_{i}(z_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!\bar{w_{i}}^{k_{i}}}{\bigg(1-\bar{w_{i}}\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}}{1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i}}\bigg)\bigg)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!(\bar{w_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i}-\bar{w_{i}}((d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})z_{i}+c_{i}))^{k_{i}+1}}

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle\overline{u(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-z_{i}\overline{v_{i}(w_{i})})^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{a\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w^{k_{i}}_{i}}{(1-\bar{c_{i}}w_{i})^{k_{i}+1}}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!z^{k_{i}}_{i}}{\bigg(1-z_{i}(\overline{\bigg(\frac{(d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})w_{i}+c_{i}}{1-\bar{c_{i}}w_{i}}\bigg)}\bigg)^{k_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}!(\bar{w_{i}}z_{i})^{k_{i}}}{(1-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}}-z_{i}((\bar{d_{i}}-|c_{i}|^{2})\bar{w_{i}}+\bar{c_{i}}))^{k_{i}+1}}

for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}),w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Since

\displaystyle 1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i}-\bar{w_{i}}((d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})z_{i}+c_{i})=

\displaystyle 1-\bar{c_{i}}z_{i}-\bar{w_{i}}(d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})z_{i}-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-c_{i}\bar{w_{i}}-z_{i}((d_{i}-|c_{i}|^{2})\bar{w_{i}}+\bar{c_{i}})

and
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) that

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z)=W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}K_{w}(z),

which implies that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is Hermitian on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Sufficient condition for the normality of composition-differentiation operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

Normal operators are those that commute with their own adjoint. In other words, a bounded linear operator T is classified as normal if, and only if,
T^{*}T=TT^{*}
. In 2023, Liu
et al

[
32
]
investigated the normality of composition-differentiation operators on the Bergman space, presenting necessary and sufficient conditions in their study. Lo and Loh
[
33
]
investigated the class of normal generalized weighted composition operators on
H_{\gamma}
and their relevance to the property of complex symmetry. In the last few years, substantial research has been dedicated to investigate the normality of weighted composition-differentiation operators in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (see
[
15
,
14
,
29
,
7
]
and relevant references). Le
[
30
]
has conducted a study on the normality of weighted composition operators on Hilbert spaces of analytic functions over the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Similarly, Han and Wang
[
25
]
have explored the normality of the composition operator on Hilbert spaces of analytic functions over the same unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Until now, the entirety of normality research has been restricted to the unit disk and the unit ball, and it seems that no investigations have been carried on the polydisk. Our main aim is to fill this gap by exploring the normality properties over the polydisk and we obtain Theorem
4.1
. However, the significance of Theorem
4.1
lies in its limited scope, as it does not cover all normal weighted composition-differentiation operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Therefore, we establish the precise conditions for an operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
to be normal, assuming that
u
and
v_{j}(z_{j})
(where
j=1,2,\ldots,n
) adopt a specific form that generalizes the symbols explained in Theorem
4.1
. Despite our efforts, we have not yet determined whether an operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
, with
v_{j}(0)(0)\neq 0
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, can be normal for different choices of
u
and
v_{j}(z_{j})
.
We now establish a sufficient condition that guarantees the bounded operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
to be normal, given the complex symmetry under the standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Suppose
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric and
v_{j}(0)=0
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is normal.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Since
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric and
v_{j}(0)=0
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, it follows from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{k_{i}}_{i}\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=d_{j}z_{j}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for any non-negative integers
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle||

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}|\langle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle u\bigg(\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}\prod_{i}^{n}z^{m_{i}}_{i}\bigg)(v),e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle a\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{k_{i}}_{i}\times\bigg(\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}(d_{i}z_{i})^{m_{i}-k_{i}}\bigg),e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!d^{m_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{m_{i}}_{i},e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!d^{m_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}\bigg|^{2}\bigg|\bigg\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!d^{m_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}\bigg|^{2}

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle||

\displaystyle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}|\langle W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}|\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},u\bigg(\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}\prod_{i}^{n}z^{j_{i}}_{i}\bigg)(v)\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},a\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{k_{i}}_{i}\times\bigg(\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{j_{i}!}{(j_{i}-k_{i})!}(d_{i}z_{i})^{j_{i}-k_{i}}\bigg)\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|\bigg\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{j_{i}!d^{j_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(j_{i}-k_{i})!}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{j_{i}}_{i}\bigg)\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}=0}^{\infty}\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{j_{i}!d^{j_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(j_{i}-k_{i})!}\bigg|^{2}\bigg|\bigg\langle e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}},e_{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n}}\bigg\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!d^{m_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}\bigg|^{2}

as the set
\{e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}:m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 0\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
H(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Thus, it follows from (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) that for any non-negative integer
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle||W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2}=\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}!d^{m_{i}-k_{i}}_{i}}{(m_{i}-k_{i})!}\bigg|^{2}=||W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2}.

Consequently, (
4.3
) leads to the conclusion that

\displaystyle||W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f||=||W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f||

for any
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, and hence
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is a normal operator. This completes the proof.
∎

From Theorem
4.1
, it is clear that when
k_{1}=k_{2}=\ldots=k_{n}=0
,
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
coincides with
W_{u,v}
. Consequently, we can readily derive the following result from Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Suppose
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric and
v_{j}(0)=0
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then
W_{u,v}
is normal.

Proof of Corollary
4.1
.

Since
W_{u,v}
is
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric and
v_{j}(0)=0
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,n
, it follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=a\;\mbox{and}\;v_{j}(z_{j})=d_{j}z_{j}\;\mbox{for}\;j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
a,d_{j}(j=1,2,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Now, we can proceed with the proof in a manner analogous to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. By setting
k_{i}=0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
in equation (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle||W_{u,v}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2}=\bigg|a\prod_{i}^{n}d^{m_{i}}_{i}\bigg|^{2}=||W^{*}_{u,v}e_{m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}}||^{2},

which leads to the conclusion that
||W_{u,v}f||=||W^{*}_{u,v}f||
for any
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, and hence
W_{u,v}
is a normal operator. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Kernel of generalized composition-differentiation operators on Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

In the study of composition operators for several spaces of functions, one of the significant findings is to explore the kernel of the operators. In this section, we investigate the kernel of generalized composition-differentiation operators
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. The kernel of an operator refers to the collection of vectors that map to the zero vector. To explore the kernel of generalized composition-differentiation operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, we must introduce the space containing all polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of degree
\leq m
.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
be the space containing all polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of degree
\leq m
over the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. The explicit definition of this space is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m}(\mathbb{C})=\bigg\{\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\leq m}A_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{a_{i}}:A_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\}.

In 2014, Jung
et al

[
29
, Proposition 3.2]
have proved that the kernel of the adjoint of a complex symmetric weighted composition operator on
{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is simply
\{0\}
. In 2021, Han and Wang
[
26
, Proposition 2.9]
demonstrated that the kernel of the adjoint of a nonzero
J
-symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator on the Hardy Hilbert space
{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.

It is natural to raise the following question.

Question 5.1
.

Can we explore the kernel of
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
explicitly?

In this section, our primary aim is to answer the Question
5.1
. It has been studied extensively the kernel of the composition-differentiation operator in different spaces defined over the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. We will explore the kernel of the composition-differentiation operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
in the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
over the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

By establishing the following result, we will show that both the kernels of the generalized composition-differentiation operators
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
and their adjoint are equal to
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
. Proving the following result is greatly influenced by the role of the Identity theorem in several complex variables.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero analytic function and, let
v:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
with
v(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(v_{1}(z_{1}),v_{2}(z_{2}),\ldots,v_{n}(z_{n}))
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. If
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is complex symmetric with standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
, then
ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Suppose that
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
is complex symmetric on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with the standard conjugation
\mathcal{J}
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}f(z)=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f(z),\;f\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

If
f\in\ ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})
, then

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}(f(z))\equiv 0\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n}.

That is,

\displaystyle u(z)\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v(z))\equiv 0\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n},

equivalently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v(z))\equiv 0\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n}.

Since
v(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is open, so by Identity Theorem in several variables, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(f(z))\equiv 0\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n}

which implies that

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\leq(m-1)}A_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z^{a_{i}}_{i}

for some
A_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots a_{n}}(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\leq(m-1))\in\mathbb{C}
. Clearly,

(5.2)

\displaystyle ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C}).

Let
f
be an element of
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
. It is clear that
f
is a polynomial of degree
\leq(m-1)
in
n
variables. Thus, by applying the Identity Theorem in several variables, we have

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}(f(z))

\displaystyle=u(z)\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(v(z))

\displaystyle\equiv u(z)\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial z^{k_{1}}_{1}\partial z^{k_{2}}_{2}\ldots\partial z^{k_{n}}_{n}}(f(z))\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\;\mbox{on}\;\mathbb{D}^{n}

which turns out that
f\in\ ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})
. Thus, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq\ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}).

Hence, based on (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we conclude that
ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
. Furthermore, for
f\in\ ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})
, we have
W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}(f)=0
. Since
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with conjugation
\mathcal{J}
, we have from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}f=\mathcal{J}W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}f=0.

That is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}f\in\ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C}),

equivalently, we have

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C}).

Hence,

(5.4)

\displaystyle ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C}).

Let
g\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
. Then, we have
W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}g=\mathcal{J}W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}\mathcal{J}g=0
which implies that
g\in\ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})
. Hence,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}).

Consequently, from (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain
ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle ker(W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=ker(W^{*}_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}})=\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m-1}(\mathbb{C}).

This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

In the article, we have discussed the
\mathcal{J}
-symmetric exhibited by the partial composition-differentiation operator
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
. Our research has involved investigating normality and Hermitian characteristics of these operators. Additionally, we have determined the kernels of the operators
W_{u,v,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n}}
and their adjoints. furthermore, we have observed that the discussion on normality can be extended to encompass scenarios where
v_{j}(0)\neq 0(j=1,2,\ldots,n)
. Moreover, the normality and symmetry of these operators can be discussed independently using different conjugation methods.

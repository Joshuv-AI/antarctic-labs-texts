A Radon Transform on the Cylinder
Alejandro Coyoli

Abstract
We define a parametric Radon transform R R that assigns to a Sobolev function on the cylinder 𝕊 × ℝ \mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R} in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} its mean values along sets E ζ E_{\zeta} formed by the intersections of planes through the origin and the cylinder. We show that R R is a continuous operator, prove an inversion formula, provide a support theorem, as well as a characterization of its null space. We conclude by presenting a formula for the dual transform R ∗ R^{*} . We show that R R and its dual R ∗ R^{*} are related to the right-sided and left-sided Chebyshev fractional integrals. Using this relationship, we characterize the null space of R R and R ∗ R^{*} and provide an inversion formula for R ∗ R^{*} .

Introduction

The problem of reconstructing a function from its averages along submanifolds goes back to the
19^{th}
century
[
14
]

[
4
]

[
8

A mapping that assigns to each sufficiently good function
f
on a given manifold
X

f
over submanifolds of
X
is commonly called the Radon transform of
f

[
5
,
7
,
14
]
. However, the term Radon transform has a wide meaning and is used in diverse transforms of the kind
[

,
9
,
10
,
12

In this article we define a Radon-like transform
R
that assigns to a Sobolev function on the cylinder
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
its mean values along sets
E_{\zeta}
formed by the intersections of planes through the origin and the cylinder. Unlike the classical Radon transform, the parametric transform

averages functions using the arclength measure

on
\mathbb{S}
and not
dS

E_{\zeta}
. This simplification gives rise to a continuous and invertible transform on the cylinder with strong connections to fractional calculus. By using

instead of the arclength measure

we also avoid issues that arise with the averaging of a function over sets of infinite arclength. To the best of our knowledge, this problem has not been considered in the existing literature.

This article is organized as follows. In

We parametrize points on the cylinder
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
using local coordinates

Consider the subset of

\displaystyle H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})=\left\{\left.f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})\right|\lim_{t\to\infty}f(e^{is},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\small a.e.}}}}{{\quad=\quad}}C(s)\text{ with }C\text{ integrable, }2\pi-\text{periodic}\right\}.

The Radon-like operator

assigns to functions in

their mean values along sets

formed by the intersections of planes through the origin and the cylinder. The intersections of the cylinder

\bm{\zeta}
lies on the equator of the sphere, the set describes two parallel lines on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}

We utilize the arclength measure

\displaystyle R:H^{2}_{pc}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

(1)

\displaystyle Rf(\zeta)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E_{\zeta}}fd\sigma.

(2)

\displaystyle E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}\kern 5.0pt|\kern 5.0pt\langle\,\zeta(\theta,\rho),x\,\rangle=0\,\}

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))ds

Proposition 1
.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
, then
Rf
is well defined on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
. By
Theorem

4
there exits a bounded, continuous function that is equal to
f
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, let
f
denote this continuous representative. Since
R
annihilates odd functions, assume that
f
is even. Then,

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta+\frac{5\pi}{2}}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\,ds,

as the integrand is a
2\pi-
periodic function of
s
. Using the fact that
f
is an even function on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
and making a change of variables

we obtain

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta+\frac{3\pi}{2}}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\,ds.

By hypothesis, there exists a
2\pi-
periodic function
C\in L^{1}([0,2\pi))
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}f(e^{is},t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\small a.e.}}}}{{\quad=\quad}}C(s).

Since
f
is bounded on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
, by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\pi/2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\rho\to\pi/2}\,\int_{\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta+\frac{3\pi}{2}}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta+\frac{3\pi}{2}}\lim_{\rho\to\pi/2}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta+\frac{3\pi}{2}}C(s)\,ds

\displaystyle<\infty.

Therefore, we define the transform
R
on the entire sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, or equivalently in
\Xi\cup\{\pm\pi/2\}
, as

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))ds&\quad\text{ if }(\theta,\rho)\in\Xi\\
&\\
\lim_{\varphi\to\pi/2}Rf(\theta,\varphi)&\quad\text{ if }(\theta,\rho)\in[0,2\pi)\times\{\pm\pi/2\}.\end{cases}

(4)

The following result is a corollary of
Proposition

1
for a class of well behaved functions in
H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
: functions that vanish at infinity.

Corollary 2
.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
be a function that vanishes at infinity. Then

is well defined on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
Rf(\theta,\pi/2)=0
for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi)

4
Properties of R

Continuity

Theorem 14
.

R:H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is a continuous transform.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and consider the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dS(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}dt\,ds

and

\displaystyle\|Rf\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left|Rf(\zeta)\right|^{2}dS(\zeta)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta.

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(R|f|(\theta,\rho)\right)^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta.

Using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(R|f|(\theta,\rho)\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\right|^{2}ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,ds\,d\rho\,d\theta.

Switching the order of integration, letting
\gamma=\theta-s+\pi
with respect to
\theta
and noticing that the integrand is
2\pi-
periodic with respect to the
\gamma
variable, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos\theta)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\theta\,d\rho\,ds.

Noting that
f
is even, changing the order of integration and letting
u=s+\pi
with respect to the
s
variable, using the fact that the integrand is
2\pi-
periodic with respect to the new variable
u
and switching the order of integration back again we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,(\sin^{2}\gamma+\cos^{2}\gamma)\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\sin^{2}\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\cos^{2}\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,|\cos\gamma|\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds.

On the one hand,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\cos\rho\tan\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\tan\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{-\tan\rho}^{\tan\rho}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\rho\,ds,

by letting
t=\tan\rho\cos\gamma
with respect to the
\gamma
variable. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,ds.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,\sin\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,ds.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,|\cos\gamma|\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\,|\cos\gamma|\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\,\cos\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\,\cos\gamma\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\,\cos\gamma\,d\rho\,d\gamma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\,\cos\gamma\,d\rho\,d\gamma\,ds.

Let the change of variables
t=\tan\rho\cos\gamma
with respect to
\rho
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,|\cos\gamma|\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\frac{\cos^{2}\gamma}{\cos^{2}\gamma+t^{2}}\,dt\,d\gamma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\frac{\cos^{2}\gamma}{\cos^{2}\gamma+t^{2}}\,dt\,d\gamma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\gamma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\gamma\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,ds.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|f(e^{is},\tan\rho\cos\gamma)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,|\cos\gamma|\,d\gamma\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,ds.

Therefore, putting both bounds together, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|Rf(\theta,\rho)\right|^{2}\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f(e^{is},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,ds,

or

\displaystyle\left\|Rf\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|f\right\|_{2}

and therefore,
R
is continuous from
H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Null Space, Injectivity of R and an Inversion Formula

The transform
R
, just like the Funk transform, vanishes for all odd functions with respect to the equator. We will show that a necessary condition for a function space to have a non-trivial null space is that functions may not be bounded at infinity. To prove this we will make use of the following result.

Lemma 15
.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and
f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}(t)e^{ins}
its Fourier series. Let
Rf(\theta,\rho)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{n}(\rho)e^{in\theta}
be the Fourier series of
Rf
on
\Xi
. Then

\displaystyle G_{n}(\arctan x)=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{F_{n}(v)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{x}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-v^{2}}}dv

(13)

where
x=\tan\rho
and
T_{|n|}
is the
|n|^{th}
Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and
(\theta,\rho)\in\Xi
. Since
f
is in
H^{2}(E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)})
, by
Theorem

4

f
is equal almost everywhere to a continuous and bounded function on
E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
f
is such representative. Since
R
annihilates odd functions, assume that
f
is an even function. Consequently,
Rf
is an even function on
\Xi
in the sense that
Rf(\theta,\rho)=Rf(\theta+\pi,\pi-\rho)
. Therefore, assume, without loss of generality, that
0\leq\rho<\pi/2
.

We parametrize the set
E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)}
as in
Section

2
so that

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)}}f(x)d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))ds.

Let
G_{n}(\rho)
be the the
n^{th}
Fourier coefficient of
Rf
. Then

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}Rf(\theta,\rho)e^{-in\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))e^{-in\theta}ds\,d\theta.

Letting
u=s-\theta
, using the fact that the integrand is
2\pi-
periodic with respect to
u
, switching the order of integration, since
f
is bounded on
E_{\zeta(\theta,\rho)}
, and letting
s=\theta+u
we obtain

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du+\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du+\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos(v+\pi))e^{-in(v+\pi))}dv\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du+\int_{0}^{\pi}(-1)^{|n|}F_{n}(\tan\rho\cos v)e^{-inv}dv\right).

By hypothesis
f
is an even function on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
, therefore
F_{n}(t)=(-1)^{|n|}F_{n}(-t)
and

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du.

The change of variables
v=-\tan\rho\cos u
yields

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{n}(-\tan\rho\cos u)e^{-inu}du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\tan\rho}^{\tan\rho}F_{n}(v)e^{-in\arccos\left(\frac{-v}{\tan\rho}\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\tan\rho}^{\tan\rho}F_{n}(v)e^{-in\left(\pi-\arccos\left(\frac{v}{\tan\rho}\right)\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{-\tan\rho}^{\tan\rho}F_{n}(v)e^{in\arccos\left(\frac{v}{\tan\rho}\right)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}}dv.

Using Euler’s formula and the fact that
F_{n}(v)\sin(n\arccos(v/\tan\rho))/\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}
is an odd function of
v
yields

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{-\tan\rho}^{\tan\rho}F_{n}(v)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{\tan\rho}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\tan\rho}F_{n}(v)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{\tan\rho}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tan^{2}\rho-v^{2}}}dv,

where
T_{|n|}
is the
|n|^{th}
Chebyshev polynomial.

Finally, letting
x=\tan\rho

\displaystyle G_{n}(\arctan x)=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{F_{n}(v)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{x}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-v^{2}}}dv.

(14)

We obtain the following null space characterization when we define
R
on the set of continuous functions on the cylinder.

Theorem 16
.

Let
R:C(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})\rightarrow C(\mathbb{S}^{2}_{0})
. Then the null space of
R
consists of all odd functions in
C(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and those that are even and are such that their Fourier series
f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}(t)e^{ins}
have polynomial coefficients

\displaystyle F_{n}(t)=0

for
|n|<2
,
and

\displaystyle F_{n}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\small a.e.}}}}{{\quad=\quad}}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor}a_{m}^{n}t^{|n|-2m}

for
|n|\geq 2
, where
a_{m}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
(\theta,\rho)\in\Xi
,
x=\tan\rho
and
f\in C(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
. Consider the Fourier series
Rf(\theta,\rho)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G(\rho)e^{in\theta}
. Since
R
annihilates odd functions, assume, without loss of generality, that
f
is an even function. Hence,
Rf
is an even function on
\Xi
in the sense that
Rf(\theta,\rho)=Rf(\theta+\pi,\pi-\rho)
. Therefore, assume, without loss of generality, that
0\leq\rho<\pi/2
so that
x>0
.

By
Lemma

15

\displaystyle G_{n}(\arctan x)=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}du.

The right hand-side of the last equation may be expressed as a left-sided Chebyshev fractional integral as in
Lemma

11
yielding

\displaystyle G_{n}(\arctan x)=\frac{(-1)^{|n|}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\Upsilon_{+}^{|n|}F_{n}\right)(x).

Therefore,

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)=0

\displaystyle\iff\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\quad G_{n}(\arctan x)=0.

By
Lemma

9
,
\Upsilon^{|n|}_{+}
is injective for
|n|<2
. So for
|n|<2
,
G_{n}(\arctan x)=0
if and only if
F_{n}\equiv 0
. For
|n|\geq 2
, by
Lemma

11

\displaystyle F_{n}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\small a.e.}}}}{{\quad=\quad}}\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor}a_{m}^{n}t^{|n|-2m}

for some
a_{m}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Corollary 17
.

The restriction of
R
to the space of even functions in
H^{2}_{pc}
is injective.

Proof.

All even functions
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
admit a bounded continuous representative by
Theorem

4
. Therefore,
f
has bounded Fourier coefficients
F_{n}\in L^{1}_{\chi^{+}_{|n|}}(0,\infty)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since the Fourier coefficients of functions in the nontrivial null space of
R
are polynomials, therefore unbounded, this show that
R
restricted to all even functions in
H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
has a trivial null space.

Theorem 18
.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and the Fourier series of
f
on the cylinder be given by
f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}(t)e^{ins}
. Assume for some
\epsilon>0
, there are positive constants
C_{k}
,
k=0,1,...
such that

\displaystyle|F_{n}(t)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{0}&\quad\text{ if }n=0,\\
C_{n}|t|^{|n|-1}&\quad\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}

(15)

whenever
|t|<\epsilon
. Then
f
can be recovered almost everywhere from the averages

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s))ds

over all
\,(\theta,\rho)\in\Xi
. Moreover, for almost all
(e^{is},t)\in\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
the function
f
is given by

\displaystyle f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((-1)^{|n|}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx\right)e^{ins},

where
G_{n}
is the
n^{th}
Fourier coefficient of
Rf
as in
Lemma

15
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
. Since
R
annihilates odd functions, assume that
f
is an even function. Consequently,
Rf
is an even function on
\Xi
in the sense that
Rf(\theta,\rho)=Rf(\theta+\pi,\pi-\rho)
. Therefore, assume, without loss of generality, that
0\leq\rho<\pi/2
so that
x=\tan\rho\geq 0
.

Let
Rf(\theta,\rho)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{n}(\rho)e^{in\theta}
. From
Lemma

15

\displaystyle G_{n}(\arctan x)=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}du,

(16)

where
x=\tan\rho
and
T_{|n|}
is the
|n|^{th}
Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.

Multiplying both sides of equation
eq.

16
by
T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x/\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}
and integrating with respect to
x
from
0
to some positive value
t
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}dudx.

Note that, by
eq.

15
the left-hand side is convergent and since
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}\times[-t,t])
, by
Theorem

4

f
is equal almost everywhere to a continuous and bounded function. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
is such representative.

To change the order of integration, let us show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{x}\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|du\,dx<\infty.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
t<\epsilon
. Otherwise, we may split the integral into two parts: one where integration occurs over
x\in[\epsilon,t]
and another one over
x\in(0,\epsilon)
. For the integral where
x
is bounded away from zero, by continuity of
F_{n}
and
T_{|n|}
, the numerator is bounded. The resulting integral is convergent and can be calculated using trigonometric substitution.

Since for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\left|T_{|n|}(x)\right|\leq\begin{cases}1\qquad&\text{ for }|x|\leq 1,\\
|2x|^{|n|}\qquad&\text{ for }|x|>1,\end{cases}

we have that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)\left(\frac{2t}{x}\right)^{|n|}x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|.

Noticing that
0\leq x\leq t
we obtain the following bounds.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)(2t)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|&\quad\text{ for }|n|<2,\\
&\\
\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)(2t)^{|n|}}{x^{|n|-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|&\quad\text{ for }|n|\geq 2.\end{cases}

By hypothesis, there exists a value
\epsilon>0
for which

\displaystyle|F_{n}(t)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{0}&\quad\text{ if }n=0,\\
C_{n}|t|^{|n|-1}&\quad\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}

for
t<\epsilon
. Hence, for all
|n|<2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{x}\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|du\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(2t)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\left|F_{n}(u)\right|}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\,du\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(2t)C_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\,du\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t\pi^{2}C_{n}}{2}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Similarly, for
|n|\geq 2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{x}\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|du\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(2t)^{|n|}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{x^{|n|-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\left|F_{n}(u)\right|}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\,du\,dx

\displaystyle=(2t)^{|n|}C_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{x^{|n|-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{u^{|n|-1}}{\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}du\,dx.

The changes of variables
v=\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{x}\left|\frac{F_{n}(u)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}\right|du\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(2t)^{|n|}C_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{x^{|n|-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}(x^{2}-v^{2})^{\frac{|n|-2}{2}}dv\,dx

\displaystyle\leq(2t)^{|n|}C_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{x^{|n|-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}\int_{0}^{x}x^{|n|-2}dv\,dx

\displaystyle=(2t)^{|n|}C_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{(2t)^{|n|}\pi C_{n}}{2}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Therefore, we may interchange the order of integration to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}F_{n}(u)\left(\int_{u}^{t}\frac{T_{|n|}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}\sqrt{x^{2}-u^{2}}}dx\right)du.

Make the change of variables
v=u^{2}/x
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-1)^{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}F_{n}(u)\left(u\frac{u^{2}}{t}\int_{\frac{u^{2}}{t}}^{u}\frac{T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{v}{(u^{2}/t)}\right)}{\sqrt{u^{2}-v^{2}}\sqrt{v^{2}-\left(\frac{u^{2}}{t}\right)^{2}}v}dv\right)du.

By
Lemma

5
, the expression in parentheses is equal to
\pi/2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(-1)^{|n|}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx=\int_{0}^{t}F_{n}(u)du.

Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we obtain the following expression for the
n^{th}
Fourier coefficient of
f
:

\displaystyle F_{n}(t)=(-1)^{|n|}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx.

(17)

Substituting in the Fourier series of
f
yields

\displaystyle f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((-1)^{|n|}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx\right)e^{ins}.

Support Theorem

Notice that
Theorem

18
depends only on the local behavior of
f
near the equator and is independent of its behavior at infinity. Similarly, the following support theorem depends only on
Rf
vanishing for
\rho\in[0,\arctan t]
to determine that
f(e^{is},t)=0
for all
s\in[0,2\pi)
.

Theorem 19
(Support Theorem).

Let
f\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
and the Fourier series of
f
on the cylinder be given by
f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{n}(t)e^{ins}
. Assume for some
\epsilon>0
, there are positive constants
C_{k}
,
k=0,1,...
such that

\displaystyle|F_{n}(t)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{0}&\quad\text{ if }n=0,\\
C_{n}|t|^{|n|-1}&\quad\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},\end{cases}

whenever
|t|<\epsilon
. Then for a fix
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
t\in[0,\infty)
and all
s\in[0,2\pi)
, the values of
f(e^{is},t)
are completely determined by all values
Rf(\theta,\rho)
for
\rho\in[0,\arctan t]
. In particular, if
Rf(\theta,\rho)\equiv 0
for all
\rho\in[0,\arctan t]
, then
f(e^{is},t)=0
for all
s\in[0,2\pi)
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

18

\displaystyle f(e^{is},t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((-1)^{|n|}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx\right)e^{ins}.

Now

\displaystyle Rf(\theta,\rho)\equiv 0\text{ for all }\rho\in[0,\arctan t]

\displaystyle\iff Rf(\theta,\rho)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{n}(\rho)e^{in\theta}=0\text{ for all }\rho\in[0,\arctan t]

\displaystyle\iff G_{n}(\rho)\equiv 0\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }\rho\in[0,\arctan t].

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan x)T_{|n|}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)x}{\sqrt{t^{2}-x^{2}}}dx=0

and
f(e^{is},t)=0
for all
s\in[0,2\pi)
.

5
The Dual Transform

In this section we study the dual transform
R^{*}
. The dual will be defined as the formal adjoint of
R
in the sense that, for
g\in C(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
,
R^{*}g
satisfies the duality relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}Rf(\theta,\rho)\overline{g(\theta,\rho)}|\sin\theta|\,d\rho\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(e^{is},t)\overline{R^{*}g(e^{is},t)}\,dt\,ds.

The following theorem gives us an explicit formula for
R^{*}
.

Theorem 20
.

Let
g\in C(\mathbb{S}^{2})
be an even function and
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
. Then the formal adjoint of
R
is given by the formula

\displaystyle R^{*}g(e^{is},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}g\left(s+\sigma\arccos\left(\frac{-t}{\sqrt{v^{2}+t^{2}}}\right),\arctan\sqrt{v^{2}+t^{2}}\right)\,\frac{dv}{(1+v^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}

for
(e^{is},t)\in\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{2}_{pc}(\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R})
. Since
R
annihilates odd functions and by
Theorem

4
,
without loss of generality, assume that
f
is a continuous, bounded and even function on
\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
g\in C(\mathbb{S}^{2})
be even in the sense that

\displaystyle g(\theta,\rho)=g(\theta+\pi,\pi-\rho)

for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
\rho\in[0,\pi]
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle Rf,g\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}Rf(\theta,\rho)\overline{g(\theta,\rho)}\,\sin\rho\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\left(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos(\theta-s)\right)\overline{g(\theta,\rho)}\,\sin\rho\,ds\,d\rho\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{-s}^{2\pi-s}f\left(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos u\right)\overline{g(u+s,\rho)}\,\sin\rho\,du\,d\rho\,ds,

by switching the order of integration and letting

Since both

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}f\left(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos u\right)\overline{g(u+s,\rho)}\,\sin\rho\,du\,d\rho\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}f\left(e^{is},-\tan\rho\cos u\right)\overline{g(u+s,\rho)}\,\sin\rho\,du\,d\rho\,ds.

By switching the order of integration, letting

\displaystyle\langle Rf,g\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

Null Space, Injectivity of
\mathbf{R}^{*}
and an Inversion Formula

We will show that if a nontrivial, even, and continuous function
g
belongs to the null space of
R^{*}
, then
g
would be unbounded on the equator or at the poles of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. This contradiction implies that
R^{*}
is injective in the space of continuous and even functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Now,

\displaystyle R^{*}g(e^{is},t)=0

\displaystyle\iff\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\quad H_{n}(t)=0

\displaystyle\iff\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\frac{(-1)^{|n|}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\Upsilon_{-}^{|n|}G_{n}^{\#}\right)(t)=0.

Assuming
G^{\#}_{n}
fulfills the hypotheses in
Lemma

8
and
Lemma

10
and
R^{*}g=0
, then, by the aforementioned theorems, if
|n|<2
then
G_{n}^{\#}=0
, implying that

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)=0

for all
\rho\in(0,\pi/2)
. If
|n|\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle G_{n}^{\#}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor-1}c^{n}_{k}\omega^{2k-|n|}

for
\omega\in(0,\infty)
and some coefficients
c^{n}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle G_{n}^{\#}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor-1}c^{n}_{k}\omega^{2k-|n|}

\displaystyle\iff G_{n}(\arctan\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor-1}c^{n}_{k}\omega^{2k-|n|}\left(1+\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\iff G_{n}(\rho)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor-1}c^{n}_{k}\frac{\left(1+\tan^{2}\rho\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\tan^{|n|-2k}\rho}

for
\rho\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.

Theorem 22
.

The dual transform
R^{*}
is injective in
C_{e}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
g\in C(\mathbb{S}^{2})
be an even function and, without loss of generality, assume
0<\rho<\pi/2
. As
g
is a continuous function, its Fourier coefficients
G_{n}(\rho)
are also continuous on
[0,\pi/2]
. We begin by showing that
G_{n}^{\#}\in L_{\chi^{-}_{|n|}}((0,\infty))
, that is
G_{n}^{\#}
satisfies
eq.

9
in
Section

3
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{1}>0
. If
n
is even then

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\left|G_{n}^{\#}(v)\right|\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{G_{n}(\arctan v)}{(1+v^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\arctan a_{1}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{|G_{n}(u)|}{\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}u}}\,du

by letting
u=\arctan v
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\left|G_{n}^{\#}(v)\right|\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\arctan a_{1}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left|G_{n}(u)\right|\,du

\displaystyle\leq\|G_{n}\|_{1}

\displaystyle<\infty.

If
n
is odd then

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|G_{n}^{\#}(v)\right|(1+|\log v|)}{v}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{|G_{n}(\arctan v)|(1+|\log v|)}{v(1+v^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\arctan a_{1}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{|G_{n}(u)|(1+|\log\tan u|)}{\tan u\,\sqrt{1+\tan^{2}u}}du

by letting
u=\arctan v
. This way

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|G_{n}^{\#}(v)\right|(1+|\log v|)}{v}\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\arctan a_{1}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|G_{n}(u)|\frac{(1+|\log\tan u|)}{\tan u}du.

The quantity
\frac{(1+|\log\tan u|)}{\tan u}
attains the following maximum in
[\arctan a_{1},\pi/2)
:

\displaystyle\max_{[\arctan a_{1},\pi/2)}\left\{\frac{(1+|\log\tan u|)}{\tan u}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{|\log a_{1}|}{a_{1}}&\quad\text{ if }0<a_{1}<1,\\
\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{e}&\quad\text{ if }1\leq a_{1}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|G_{n}^{\#}(v)\right|(1+|\log v|)}{v}\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\max\left\{\frac{1}{e},\frac{|\log a_{1}|}{a_{1}}\right\}\right)\int_{\arctan a_{1}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|G_{n}(u)|du

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\max\left\{\frac{1}{e},\frac{|\log a_{1}|}{a_{1}}\right\}\right)\|G_{n}\|_{1}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Thus proving that
G_{n}^{\#}\in L_{\chi^{-}_{|n|}}^{1}(0,\infty)
.

However, even and continuous functions in the null space of
R^{*}
have Fourier series coefficients

\displaystyle G_{n}(\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\small a.e.}}}}{{\quad=\quad}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{|n|}{2}\rfloor-1}c^{n}_{k}\frac{\left(1+\tan^{2}\rho\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\tan^{|n|-2k}\rho}

for all
|n|\geq 2
. Consequently, if any of the coefficients
c^{n}_{k}\neq 0
, then
g
would not be continuous on the equator or at the poles of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. It follows, that
R^{*}
must have a trivial null space when defined in the space of continuous and even functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Corollary 23

The dual transform

Theorem 1.

This result is new. It differs from the known results:

Proof

Let

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{it\eta}F(i\eta)d\eta.

(6)

The integral (
6

L(f)=F(p)
,

is a continuous function and

6

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\sigma}}e^{pt}F(p)dp,

(7)

where

\sigma-i\eta,\sigma+i\eta

-i\eta,i\eta

For convenience of the reader, let us give a version of a proof of the inversion formula. Under our assumptions

Consider

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-qt}dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\eta F(i\eta)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(q-i\eta)t}dt=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}F(p)\frac{1}{p-q}dp,\,\,\,p=i\eta.

(8)

Let
C_{n}
be the closed contour consisting of
[in,-in]\cup L_{n}
, where

L_{n}
is the semi-circle
p=ne^{i\phi}
,
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\phi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{L_{n}}F(p)\frac{1}{p-q}dp=0.

(9)

Therefore, the integral on the right side of (
8

as the integral over the closed contour
C_{n}=
with

Consequently, the integral in the right side of (

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-qt}dt=F(q).

(10)

Therefore, the inversion formula for the Laplace transform is proved.

Let us prove that

It follows from (

f(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(i\eta)d\eta.

If

The first integral is a continuous function of

If we prove that

Since

3
Discussion

In this Section we discuss possible generalization and applications of Theorem 1. We may replace the assumption
F(i\eta)\in L^{2}(-\infty,\infty)

by the assumption

F(i\eta)\in L^{p}(-iN,iN),\,\,p\geq 1,\quad\text{ \eqref{e3} holds. }

(18)

The proof of Theorem 1 remains unchanged because the assumption
F(i\eta)\in L^{p}(-iN,iN),\,\,p\geq 1
implies that the first integral in (
14
) is a continuous function of
t
and assumption (
12
) guarantees that the second and third integrals in (
14
) are continuous functions of
t
, which
tend to zero as
N\to\infty
.

In applications Theorem 1 is useful in the definition of hyper-singular integrals, see
[
4
]
. To give an idea of this application,
consider the integral equation:

q(t)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\lambda-1}q(\tau)d\tau=f(t).

(19)

If Re
\lambda>0
, then the integral in (
19
) is defined classically,
i.e., from the classical point of view. If
\lambda<0
, then this integral is
a hyper-singular integral,
it diverges classically
. For Re
\lambda>0
we take the Laplace transform of (
19
) and get

L(q)(1+p^{-\lambda})=L(f),\quad L(q)=\frac{L(f)}{1+p^{-\lambda}},

(20)

where the following formula was used:
L(t^{\lambda-1})=\Gamma(\lambda)p^{-\lambda}
. This formula is valid for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
except for
\lambda=0,-1,-2,...
. The
L(q)
in (
20
) admits analytic continuation with respect to
\lambda
from the region Re
\lambda>0
to the region Re
\lambda<0
, for example, to the point
\lambda=-\frac{1}{4}
which is of interest in the Navier-Stokes problem. Theorem 1 is of use to prove that
\frac{L(f)}{1+p^{-\lambda}}
for
\lambda=-\frac{1}{4}
is the Laplace transform of a function
q(t)
satisfying conditions (
4
). This can be checked if

f(t)
smooth and rapidly decaying as
t\to\infty
,
so that
|L(f)|\leq c(1+|p|)^{-1}
. In this case the function
(1+p^{\frac{1}{4}})^{-1}
is analytic in the half-plane Re
p\geq 0
and is
O(\frac{1}{|p|^{1/4}})
for
|p|\gg 1
, the function
L(f)
is analytic in the half-plane Re
p\geq 0
and is
O(\frac{1}{|p|})
for
|p|\gg 1
on the imaginary axis of the complex plane
p=s+i\eta
. By Theorem 1, the function

\frac{L(f)}{1+p^{\frac{1}{4}}}

is the Laplace transform of a function
q(t)
satisfying (
4
). In this example
b=\frac{5}{4}>1
.
We have proved the following result.

Theorem 2.

Assume
\lambda=-\frac{1}{4}
and
f(t)
be a smooth rapidly decaying as
t\to\infty
. Then equation (
19
)
has a unique solution
q(t)
satisfying (
4
).

The kernel of equation (
19
) with
\lambda=-\frac{1}{4}
is
hyper-singular. The integral in this equation with
\lambda=-\frac{1}{4}

diverges
classically. Theorem 2 is of prime interest in a study of the Navier-Stokes problem, see
[
4
]
.

4
Conclusion

Sufficient conditions are given for a function
F(p)
,
p=s+i\eta
, analytic in the half-plane
s>0
to be the
Laplace transform of a function
f(t)
,
f(t)=0
for
t<0

\sup_{t\geq 0}|f(t)<\infty
and
f(0)=0

{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and in a study of integral equations with hyper-singular kernels, see
[
4
]

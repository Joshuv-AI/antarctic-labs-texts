Automorphisms of quantum polynomials Thanks:
Ashish Gupta

Abstract
. An important step in the determination of the automorphism group of the quantum torus of rank n n (or twisted group algebra of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n} ) is the determination of its so-called non-scalar automorphisms. We present a new algorithimic approach towards this problem based on the bivector representation ⋀ 2 : GL ⁡ ( n , Z ) → GL ⁡ ( ( n 2 ) , ℤ ) \mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}:\gl(n,Z)\rightarrow\gl(\binom{n}{2},\mathbb{Z}) of GL ⁡ ( n , ℤ ) \gl(n,\mathbb{Z}) and thus compute the non-scalar automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( ℤ n , λ ) \aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda) in several new cases. As an application of our ideas we show that the quantum polynomial algebra (multiparameter quantum affine space of rank n n ) has only scalar (or toric) automorphisms provided that the torsion-free rank of the subgroup generated by the defining multiparameters is no less than ( n − 1 2 ) + 1 \binom{n-1}{2}+1 thus improving an earlier result. We also investigate the question: when is a multiparameter quantum affine space free of so-called linear automorphisms other than those arising from the action of the n n -torus ( 𝔽 ∗ ) n {(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n} .



1.
Introduction

Quantum polynomials are non-commutative versions of polynomial and Laurent polynomial algebras. The difference with the ordinary polynomials lies in the fact that the commutativity of the variables is replaced by quasi-commutativity, that is,
X_{i}X_{j}=q_{ij}X_{j}X_{i}
for non-zero scalars
q_{ij}
. This relation is the Weyl form of the cannonical commutation relation of quantum mechanics.
For the Laurent case (where each variable
X_{j}
has an inverse) there other terms in use, for example, quantum torus, twisted group algebra, McConnell–Pettit algebra etc.

Quantum polynomials play a key role in the theory of quantum groups
[
ART1997
]
and also in non-commutative geometry
[
Man
]
. Their Laurent versions arise in Lie theory as coordinate structures of extended affine Lie algebras
[
NeebKH2008
]
and also in the representation theory of nilpotent groups
[
Br2000
]
. The question of automorphisms of polynomial algebras is a still open one (the Jacobian conjecture) and it is therefore of interest to investigate the automorphisms of quantum polynomial algebras.
It is generally believed that these algebras have relatively fewer automorphisms, that is, they are rigid. However, the complete understanding of their automorphisms groups is yet to be had and the results in this paper are expected to be a step in this direction.

We briefly recall the definitions. A quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is defined as

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{F}\langle X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}\rangle/(X_{i}X_{j}-q_{ij}X_{j}X_{i})

where
\mathfrak{q}
stands for the matrix of
multiparameters

q_{ij}
, that is,

\mathfrak{q}=(q_{ij})
and

q_{ij}\in\mathbb{F}^{\ast}:=\mathbb{F}\smallsetminus\{0\}.

The matrix
\mathfrak{q}
is assumed to be multiplicatively anti-symmetric, that is,

q_{ii}=1
and
q_{ji}=q_{ij}^{-1}.

A given quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
can be embedded in a
quantum torus

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n})
by means of localization. The latter type of algebra is generated by the indeterminates
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
together with their inverses subject to the quantum commutation relations as in (
1
). The following notion plays an important role in the theory of quantum polynomials.

Definition 1.1
.

For the quantum polynomial algebras
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({\mathbb{F}}^{n})
and

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n})
the
\lambda
-group (denoted
\Lambda
)
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
generated by the multiparameters
q_{ij}
.

For the quantum tori the monomials

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}}:=X_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{m_{n}}
are units and the group-theoretic commutator
[\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}]
defined as

[\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}]:=\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}{(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}})}^{-1}{(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}})}^{-1}

yields an alternating
\mathbb{Z}
-bilinear function

(2)

\lambda:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\ast},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \lambda(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})=[\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}},\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}],\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

whose image is contained in the group
\Lambda
(e.g.,
[
OP1995
, Section 1]
).
Let

(3)

\Lambda:=\langle p_{1}\rangle\times\langle p_{2}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle p_{l}\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ l\in\mathbb{N}.

It was observed in
[
OP1995
, Lemma 3.3(ii)]
(see Section
2
) that in the study of the autmorphisms of a quantum torus the crucial case is where the group
\Lambda
is torsion-free.
We may thus assume that each direct summand
\langle p_{i}\rangle\ (i=1,\cdots,l)
in (
3
) is an infinite cycle.
For

\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}

we thus have

(4)

\lambda(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})=p_{1}^{e_{1}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})}p_{2}^{e_{2}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})}\cdots p_{l}^{e_{l}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime})}

where each exponent map
e_{i}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
yields an alternating bilinear form on
\Gamma
.

It is known (e.g.,
[
MP
]
) that the units of a quantum torus algebra are trivial, that is, are of the form
\alpha\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
.
Let us denote by
\mathscr{A}
the group
\aut_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({(F^{\ast})}^{n}))
of all

\mathbb{F}
-automorphisms and by
\mathscr{U}
the group of trivial units of the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({(F^{\ast})}^{n})
.
It is easily seen (e.g.,
[
OP1995
]
) that the action of the group
\mathscr{A}
on the quantum torus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}({(F^{\ast})}^{n})
induces an action of this same group on the group
\mathscr{U}
of trivial units fixing
\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
elementwise. There is thus an action of the group
\mathscr{A}
on the quotient group
\mathscr{U}/\mathbb{F}^{\ast}\cong\Gamma
yielding a homomorphism

(5)

\mathscr{A}\longrightarrow\aut\Gamma=\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})

whose kernel is the group
\mathscr{S}

of all
scalar automorphisms
defined by
\psi(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}})=\phi(\mathbf{m})(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{m}})
for
\phi\in\Hom(\Gamma,\mathbb{F}^{\ast})

[
OP1995
]
. Thus
\mathscr{S}\cong(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})^{n}
.
Furthermore by
[
OP1995
, Lemma 3.3(iii)]
the image of the map in (
5
)
coincides with the group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)\leq\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
of all
non-scalar automorphisms

\sigma
of
\Gamma
satisfying

(6)

\lambda(\sigma\mathbf{m},\sigma\mathbf{m^{\prime}})=\lambda(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m^{\prime}})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\bf{m},\bf{m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

We thus have the following exact sequence for the group
\mathscr{A}
as noted in
[
NeebKH2008
]

(7)

1\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)\rightarrow 1.

In terms of alternating bilinear forms
e_{i}
defined above the following characterization of the non-scalar automorphism group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)
(as noted in
[
OP1995
]
) is immediate.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 3.4 of
[
OP1995
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z},e_{i})
be the symplectic group associated with the form
e_{i}
. Then

(8)

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z},e_{i}).

Thus a non-scalar automorphism must preserve each of the forms
e_{i}
and so must stabilize the radicals of each of these forms. This fact has been fruitfully used in
[
OP1995
]
towards determining the non-scalar automorphism group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)
in certain cases.
However, notwithstanding the nicety of the preceding theorem from a computational viewpoint (
8
) seems to entail some difficulties limiting the cases where it can be directly applied.

In the following we present a new approach towards the computation of this group.
Before stating it we fix some notation.

Notation 1
.

In view of
(
3
)
let

(9)

\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})=p_{1}^{m_{1}^{(ij)}}\cdots p_{l}^{m_{l}^{(ij)}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall 1\leq i<j\leq n,

where
\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j}
are standard basis vectors of the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Gamma
. On the
\binom{n}{2}
pairs
(ij),\ (i<j)
we assume the lexicographic order. Let
\mathsf{M}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{\binom{n}{2}\times l}(\mathbb{Z})
be the
matrix whose
((ij),s)
entry is the exponent
m_{s}^{(ij)}
of
p_{s}
in (
9
) (
\forall s\in\{1.\cdots,l\}
).

With this notation we have the following.

Theorem A
.

For a quantum torus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{(}F^{\ast})^{n})
suppose that the group
\Lambda
generated by the multiparameters
q_{ij}
is torsion-free. Set
N=\binom{n}{2}
and let
\mathsf{M}
be as defined in Notation
1
above. Then

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)=\bigl(\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})\bigr)^{t}

where
t
denotes transposition and
\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})
the stabilizer of
\mathsf{M}
in
\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
with respect to the bivector representation

\displaystyle\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}:\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\gl(N,\mathbb{Z}),\ \ \ \ \ \ A\rightarrow\wedge^{2}(A)

of
\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
and the action of
\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})
on the space
\mathrm{Mat}_{N,l}(\mathbb{Z})
by left multiplication. In other words

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)=\{A^{t}\mid A\in\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})\ \mathrm{and}\ (\wedge^{2}A)\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}\}.

Remark 1.1
.

Although the matrix
\mathsf{M}
depends on the choice of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis in the group
\Lambda
the group
\stab_{\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})
(and consequently
\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})
) is independent of such a choice. Indeed for a change of basis matrix
P\in\gl(l,\mathbb{Z})
we have
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}=\mathsf{M}P
and then
X\mathsf{M}^{\prime}=\mathsf{M}^{\prime}
if and only if
X\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}
for
X\in\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem A allows us to determine the non-scalar automorphism group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)
thus:

Step 1
: We first determine the group
\mathscr{N}:=\stab_{\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})
(where
N=\binom{n}{2}
). This is easily done if
\mathsf{M}
is brought to the Smith normal form.

Step 2
: Next we find the subgroup
\mathscr{N}_{1}:=\mathscr{N}\cap\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(\gl(n,\mathbb{Z}))
. This can be done by noting as in
[
Nem
]
that the projective transformations of the projective space
\mathrm{P}(\wedge^{2}{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}^{n})
(here
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
stands for the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
) arising from the matrices in
\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(\gl(n,\mathbb{Z}))
preserve the projective grassmannian variety
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
. More algebraically, the group
\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(\gl(n,\mathbb{Z}))
is identified in
[
VP07
]
with the connected component of the subgroup of

\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})
preserving the ideal
\plu
generated by all the Plücker polynomials
defining the variety
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
.

Step 3
:
Once
\mathscr{N}_{1}
has thus been determined we must calculate
N=\bigl(\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}\bigr)^{-1}(\mathscr{N}_{1})
. This amounts to calculating the exterior root of a matrix in
\mathscr{N}_{1}
which is determined uniquely up to minus sign
[
LoLu
]
and for which a polynomial-time algorithm (implemented in GAP) exists
[
Grnhl
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

The algorithm mentioned in Step 3 can also determine (in polynomial time) if a matrix
A\in\gl(N,\mathbb{Z})
has an exterior root and consequently Step 2 may not be necessary. However it may still be helpful in the determination of the non-scalar automorphism group.

Until now the nonscalar automorphism group has been calculated (
[
OP1995
,
NeebKH2008
]
) in the case
n\geq 3
or in the case when the multiparameters are (multiplicatively) independent.
Examples of the above approach applied to the calculation of the non-scalar automorphism group of quantum tori for
n=4
are given in Section
2
. One such example is as follows.

Example
.

For
n=4
suppose that the group
\Lambda
is freely generated by the commutators
q_{14}
,
q_{23}
,
q_{24}
and
q_{34}
, while

q_{12}=q_{13}=1.

Then

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{4},\lambda)=\Biggl\{f_{b,\epsilon}:=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0&0&-b\\
0&\epsilon&0&0\\
0&0&\epsilon&0\\
0&0&0&\epsilon\end{pmatrix},\ \ b\in\mathbb{Z},\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}\Biggr\}.

The following proposition is also established in Section
2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 5
.
For a quantum torus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{(}F^{\ast})^{n})
such that

q_{12}=q_{13}=\cdots=q_{1(n-2)}=1

and the remaining multiparameters independent in
\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
we have

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

We hope that the non-scalar automorphism group can similarly be determined in many new and interesting situations using the approach described above.
Using Theorem A we can also easily recover the group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)
in the limited number of known cases
[
KPS94
,
OP1995
,
NeebKH2008
]
dealing with small values of
n
and the case where the group
\Lambda
has the maximal possible rank.

We next consider the automorphism groups of quantum affine spaces.
Until now in the study of automorphisms of quantum affine spaces the following important cases have been dealt with: (i) the uniparameter case, that is,
q_{ij}=q
and the group
\langle q\rangle
is infinite cyclic
[
AC1992
]
and (ii) the case in which the multiparameters are in general position
[
ART1997
]
, that is, the group
\Lambda
has maximum possible (torsion-free) rank. The main conclusions here are that under these assumptions the automorphism group shrinks to the image of the action of the torus
{(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n}
. We show the same conclusion remains true in somewhat more general situations:

Theorem B
.

A quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
such that the subgroup
\Lambda
has rank no smaller than
\binom{n-1}{2}+1
satisfies

\aut(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n}))=(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})^{n}.

An automorphism of the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is called linear if it stabilizes the subspace spanned by
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
. The following proposition gives a criterion for a multiparameter quantum affine space to be free of linear automorphisms other than those arising from the action of the torus
{(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\chc(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
and
n\geq 3
. Let
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{ij})
be a multiplicatively antisymmetric matrix whose entries satisfy

\displaystyle\mbox{(i)}\ q_{ij}\neq q_{kl}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall i<j,\ \ \forall k<l,

\displaystyle\mbox{(ii)}\ q_{ij}q_{kl}\neq 1\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall i<j,\ \ \forall k<l,\ (i,j)\neq(k,l).

The group of linear automorphisms of the quantum affine space
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{F}^{n})
coincides with the torus
{(\mathbb{F}^{\ast})}^{n}
.

Notation 2
.

We will use the short forms
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}
for the quantum affine space and
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
for quantum torus obtained from it by localizing at the multiplicative subset generated by the indeterminates
X_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) and refer to it as the “corresponding quantum torus”. Automorphisms of the algebras we consider will always be
\mathbb{F}
-automorphisms.

2.
Automorphisms of quantum tori

As noted above in (
7
) the essential question here is the determination of the group
\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)\leq\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
of non-scalar automorphisms.
The following fact shown in
[
OP1995
]
is the basis for our assumption that the group
\Lambda
is torsion-free.

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 3.3(ii))
.

Let
p
denote the size of the torsion subgroup of
\Lambda
. The subalgebra
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
generated by the powers
X_{i}^{\pm p}
of the indeterminates
X_{i}
is a characteristic sub-algebra of the same rank. Moreover
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is free left
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}
-module of finite rank and the corresponding
\lambda
-group
\Lambda^{\prime}
associated with
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}
is torsion free.

In view of Lemma
2.1

we will assume throughout this section that the group
\Lambda
is torsion-free.
We recall that for a given matrix
A\in\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
the exterior square
\wedge^{2}A
of
A
is the
\binom{n}{2}\times\binom{n}{2}
-matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the pairs
(ij)\ (1\leq i<j\leq n)
ordered lexicographically and whose
((ij),(kl))
entry is the
2\times 2
-minor corresponding to rows
i,j
and columns
k,l
.

By the well-known Cauchy-Binet formula the map
A\mapsto\wedge^{2}A
is multiplicative and satisfies

\det(\wedge^{2}A)=(\det A)^{n-1}

[
HJ
]
. We also have
\wedge^{2}A^{t}=(\wedge^{2}A)^{t}
whete
t
denotes transposition.
With
\mathsf{M}
as defined in Notation
1
we have the following.

Theorem A
.

For a quantum torus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{(}F^{\ast})^{n})
suppose that the group
\Lambda
generated by the multiparameters
q_{ij}
is torsion-free. Set
N=\binom{n}{2}
and let
\mathsf{M}
be as defined in Notation
1
above. Then

\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)=\bigl(\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})\bigr)^{t}

where
t
denotes transposition and
\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})
the stabilizer of
\mathsf{M}
in
\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
with respect to the bivector representation

\displaystyle\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}:\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\gl(N,\mathbb{Z}),\ \ \ \ \ \ A\rightarrow\wedge^{2}(A)

of
\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})
, that is,

\stab_{\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})}(\mathsf{M})=\{A\in\gl(n,Z)\mid(\wedge^{2}A)\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}\}.

Proof.

Writing the group
\Lambda\leq\mathbb{F}^{\ast}
additively, in view of Notation
1
we have

(10)

\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})={m_{1}}^{(ij)}p_{1}+\cdots+{m_{l}}^{(ij)}p_{l},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall 1\leq i<j\leq n,

where
m_{k}^{(ij)}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
\mathsf{M}=(a_{(ij)s})
where
a_{(ij)s}=m_{s}^{(ij)}
.
Now let

\mathscr{M}=(m_{ij})\in\gl(n,\mathbb{Z})

be such that
\mathscr{M}^{t}\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\lambda)
.
Setting

\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\prime}=\mathscr{M}^{t}\mathbf{e}_{j}=m_{j1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+m_{jn}\mathbf{e}_{n}

we note that since
\lambda
is an alternating function therefore
\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\prime})
may be expressed (e.g.,
[
MP
, Section 1.2]
) in terms of
\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})
where
i<j
as follows:

(11)

\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\prime})=m_{ij,12}\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})+{m_{ij,13}}\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{3})+\cdots+m_{ij,(n-1)n}\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{n-1},\mathbf{e}_{n}).

We also note that the coefficients appearing in the RHS of the above expression constitute row (ij) of the matrix
\wedge^{2}(\mathscr{M})
.
Since
\mathscr{M}^{t}
is
\lambda
-preserving by (
6
) we have

\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\prime},\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\prime})=\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall 1\leq i<j\leq n.

Expanding and comparing the coefficients of
p_{s}(s=1,\cdots,l)
in both sides of the last equation we get noting

(12)

m_{ij,12}m_{s}^{(12)}+m_{ij,13}m_{s}^{(13)}+\cdots+m_{ij,(n-1)n}m_{s}^{((n-1)n)}=m_{s}^{(ij)}\ \ \ \ \ \forall s=1,\cdots,l.

Letting
(ij)
take values in the set

(13)

\{(12),(13),\cdots,(n-1)n\}

in equation (
12
) and setting

\mathsf{M}^{(s)}=(m_{s}^{(12)},m_{s}^{(13)},\cdots,m_{s}^{((n-1)n)})^{t}

we thus get

\wedge^{2}(\mathscr{M})\mathsf{M}^{(s)}=\mathsf{M}^{(s)}\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall s\in 1,\cdots,l.

As
\mathsf{M}^{(s)}
is column
s
of
\mathsf{M}
(as defined in Notation
1
) it follows that:

\wedge^{2}(\mathscr{M})\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}.

Clearly the above reasoning is
reversible. This establishes the assertion of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

In the situation of Theorem A since the
N
elements
\lambda(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
) generate the group
\Lambda
therefore the columns of
\mathsf{M}
must span a free
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
of rank

. Thus
\mathsf{M}
must fix a free submodule of
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
of rank

.

C^{2}(\wedge^{2}A)^{t}P\wedge^{2}A=P.

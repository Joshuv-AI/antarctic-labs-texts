Variational symmetries of Lagrangian systems with higher-order derivatives
Ege Coban a , Ilmar Gahramanov a,b,c , and Dilara Kosva a

Abstract
We discuss an elementary derivation of variational symmetries and corresponding integrals of motion for the Lagrangian systems depending on acceleration. Providing several examples, we make the manuscript accessible to a wide range of readers with interest in higher-order Lagrangians and symmetries. The discussed technique is also applicable to the Lagrangian systems with higher-order derivatives.

Variational Symmetries for Second-Order Lagrangians

The Euler-Lagrange equation for the Lagrangian depending on second derivatives of generalized coordinates

L=L(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},\ddot{\mathbf{x}};t)

has the following form,

\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}=0\;.

(1)

This is a fourth-order ordinary differential equation. We are going to construct variational symmetries for this Lagrangian system by solving some corresponding differential equations.

Consider an action
S=\int L(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},\ddot{\mathbf{x}};t)\;\mathrm{d}t
which is invariant under the invertible
s
-parametric transformations
x^{\prime}_{i}=x^{\prime}_{i}(\mathbf{x},t,s)
and
t^{\prime}=t^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},t,s)
. These transformations form a one-parameter group (with the parameter
s
). The corresponding infinitesimal transformations are

\displaystyle x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}+\eta_{i}(\mathbf{x},t)s\hskip 14.22636pt;\hskip 14.22636pti=1,2,...,n

(2)

\displaystyle t^{\prime}=t+\xi(\mathbf{x},t)s\hskip 14.22636pt;\hskip 14.22636pti=1,2,...,n\;,

(3)

where the functions
\eta_{i}
and
\xi
are given by

\eta_{i}(\mathbf{x};t)=\frac{\partial x_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},t,s)}{\partial s}\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}\hskip 14.22636pt;\hskip 14.22636pti=1,2,...,n

(4)

\xi(\mathbf{x};t)=\frac{\partial t^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},t,s)}{\partial s}\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}\hskip 14.22636pt;\hskip 14.22636pti=1,2,...,n\;.

(5)

The invariance of the action under these transformations (in order to obtain the same equations of motion) implies that

L^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}},\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime 2}},t^{\prime})\frac{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}t}=L(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},\ddot{\mathbf{x}},t)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},t,s)\;,

(6)

where we added the total derivative term to the Lagrangian. If the Lagrangian transforms under the transformations (
2
)-(
3
) as in expression (
6
) then the corresponding symmetry is called a variational symmetry of Lagrangian. By differentiating the last expression with respect to the parameter
s
and by setting
s=0
one obtains

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{i}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}\Bigr[\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigr]+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}\Bigr[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-2\ddot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\Bigr]\right)+\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\xi+L\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}G(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}};t)\;.

(7)

For the terms inside the square brackets we used the following expressions

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{x}_{i}^{\prime}}{\partial s}\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}^{\prime}}{\partial s}\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-2\ddot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\;.

(9)

and the gauge function
G(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}};t)
is defined as

G(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}};t):=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}F(\mathbf{x},\dot{\mathbf{x}},t,s)\Bigr\rvert_{s=0}\;.

(10)

Now by using the Euler-Lagrange equation in
(
7
)
, one ends up with the following expression

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} \left[\xi L+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}(\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}-(\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}\right)\right)-G\right]=0\;.

(11)

The expression in the bracket is conserved, i.e. it is integral of motion under the time evolution
torres2013variational
;
deriglazov2016classical

2.1
Spinning particle

We begin with the most familiar example of the second order Lagrangian systems, namely with the Lagrangian of the classical spinning particle (for simplicity we put

(12)

The Euler-Lagrange equation for this system is the following fourth-order differential equation which describes a particle rotating around its translating center

(13)

3
Cyclic coordinates

If we have a one-parameter symmetry group of the action, then we can discard one of the coordinates and consider the parameter
s
as a new coordinate. Then the coordinate
s
turns out to be cyclic (ignorable) and the corresponding momentum is conserved. Actually, this is an idea of Noether’s theorem.

We would like to show that for
L(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\dot{x}},\mathbf{\ddot{x}};t)
, there exists a coordinate system
({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
such that for a fixed
k
the momentum conjugate corresponding to
{x_{k}}^{\prime}
is conserved.

\frac{\mathrm{d}p^{\prime}_{k}}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}=0\;.

(37)

Under coordinate transformations
x_{i}=x_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
and
t=t({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
, Lagrangian transforms as;

L(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{\dot{x}},\mathbf{\ddot{x}};t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}=L^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{\dot{x}}^{\prime},\mathbf{\ddot{x}}^{\prime};t)

(38)

\frac{\partial L^{\prime}}{\partial{x_{k}}^{\prime}}=L\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}\frac{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x_{i}}}\frac{\partial\ddot{x_{i}}}{\partial{x_{k}}^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\frac{\partial t}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}} \right)=0\;.

(39)

In order to simplify this expression, we make the following definitions and follow the same method used in Section II,

\xi:=\frac{\partial t}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}\;\; \eta_{i}:=\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}

(40)

L\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{i}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x_{i}}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-2\ddot{x_{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x_{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\right)\right]+\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\xi=0 \;,

(41)

Then the corresponding momentum takes the following form

p^{\prime}_{k}=\xi L+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}(\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}-(\eta_{i}-\dot{x}_{i}\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x}_{i}}\right)\;.

(42)

One can also consider the case where
x^{\prime}_{k}
is not necessarily cyclic. If,

\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}G}{\mathrm{d}t}\;,

(43)

Then from the Euler-Lagrange equations,

\frac{\mathrm{d}(p_{k}-G)}{\mathrm{d}t}=0 \;.

(44)

In the extended configuration space the expression (
43
) corresponds to

L\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{i}}\eta_{i}+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}_{i}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\ddot{x_{i}}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\eta_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-2\ddot{x_{i}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}-\dot{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\right)\right]+\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\xi=\frac{\mathrm{d}G}{\mathrm{d}t}\;.

(45)

Hence the conserved quantity takes the form given in the expression (
11
).

It is still possible to write an equivalent Lagrangian
\tilde{L}^{\prime}
where
x_{k}^{\prime}
is a cyclic coordinate. For that reason we define a function
F(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\dot{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime};t^{\prime})
as follows

G=-\frac{\partial F}{\partial x^{\prime}_{k}}\;.

(46)

Now, it is easy to see that defining a new Lagrangian as
\tilde{L^{\prime}}=L^{\prime}+\dfrac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}
will result in an equivalent Lagrangian with a cyclic coordinate
x^{\prime}_{k}
since

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}^{\prime}}\left(L^{\prime}+\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}\right)=0\;.

(47)

4.2
An illustrative example

Now, we would like to move on to a slightly more complicated case. As a next example, consider a system with the following Lagrangian

L=\dot{x}^{4}+3x^{2}\ddot{x}^{2}\;.

(65)

The Euler-Lagrange equation for this system is the following ordinary differential equation

3\ddot{x}^{2}+xx^{(4)}+4\dot{x}\dddot{x}=0\;.

(66)

By applying (
7

x\eta+x^{2}\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x}-\frac{3}{2}x^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}=0\;,

(67)

\xi=\xi(t)

\frac{\partial(x\eta)}{\partial x}=\frac{3}{2}x\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}\;.

(68)

Therefore
\eta(x;t)

\eta=\dfrac{3}{4}x\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}+\dfrac{A(t)}{x}

6
Conclusions

This paper dealt with an elementary derivation of integrals of motion for higher-order Lagrangian systems based on variational symmetries technique. The methods used here to compute variational symmetries and related integrals of motion are an extension of those used in
torres2013variational
and
del2017variational
. We provided variational symmetries of higher-order Lagrangian systems which are well-known in the literature and studied from different viewpoints.

The symmetries discussed here can be useful also in the context of the quantization of the higher derivative Lagrangian systems. Another possible direction is the derivation of symmetries for field theories with higher-derivative terms.

Linear arboricity of robust expanders
Yuping Gao, Songling Shan

Abstract
In 1980, Akiyama, Exoo, and Harary conjectured that any graph G G can be decomposed into at most ⌈ ( Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 ) / 2 ⌉ \lceil(\Delta(G)+1)/2\rceil linear forests. We confirm the conjecture for robust expanders of linear minimum degree. As a consequence, the conjecture holds for dense quasirandom graphs of linear minimum degree as well as for large n n -vertex graphs with minimum degree arbitrarily close to n / 2 n/2 from above. Keywords : Linear forest; linear arboricity; robust expander; Hamilton decomposition



1
Introduction

Unless otherwise stated explicitly, graphs in this paper are simple and finite.
A
linear forest
is a graph consisting of vertex-disjoint paths. The
linear arboricity
of a graph
G
,
denoted
\la(G)
, is the smallest number of linear forests its edges can be partitioned into.
This notion was introduced by Harary
[
15
]
in 1970 as one of the covering
invariants of graphs, and has been studied quite extensively since then.
The linear arboricity of any graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
is known to be at least

\lceil\Delta/2\rceil
as the edges incident with a maximum degree vertex need to be covered
by at least
\lceil\Delta/2\rceil
distinct linear forests.
On the other hand,
\la(G)
is conjectured to be at most
\lceil(\Delta+1)/2\rceil
.
The conjecture,
known as the
Linear Arboricity Conjecture
, was proposed by Akiyama, Exoo and Harary
[
1
]
in 1980.

Conjecture 1.1

(Linear Arboricity Conjecture)
.

Every graph
G
satisfies
\la(G)\leq\lceil(\Delta(G)+1)/2\rceil
.

The conjecture would determine the linear arboricity exactly for graphs of odd maximum degree, since in that case the upper and lower bounds coincide. For graphs of even maximum degree it would imply that the linear arboricity must be one of only two possible values. However, determining the exact value among these two choices is NP-complete
[
20
]
.

In terms of general bounds on
\la(G)

for graphs
G
with maximum degree
\Delta
, the first result of this type was obtained by
Alon in 1988
[
2
]
, which states that
\la(G)\leq\Delta/2+O\left(\frac{\Delta\log\log\Delta}{\log\Delta}\right)
.
The error term was improved to
O(\Delta^{2/3}\log^{1/3}\Delta)
by Alon and Spencer in 1992
[
3
]
,
and has been further improved to
O(\Delta^{0.661})
by Ferber, Fox and Jain
[
10
]
,
and then to
O(\Delta^{1/2}\log^{4}\Delta)
by Lang and Postle
[
18
]
.
Very recently, Christoph, Draganić, Girão, Hurley, Michel, and Müyesser
[
6
]
made a significant
improvement by reducing
the error term to
O(\log n)
, which is an exponential improvement over the previous best error term
if
\Delta=\Omega(n^{\varepsilon})
for any given
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
.
The Linear Arboricity Conjecture was well studied for classes of graphs including degenerate graphs, graphs with small maximum degree, and planar graphs.
For example, see
[
5
,
7
,
9
,
13
,
19
,
24
,
26
,
27
]
.
The conjecture was confirmed for random graphs
G_{n,p}
with constant probability
p
by Glock, Kühn, and Osthus
[
12
]
in 2016, and for the range

\frac{C\log n}{n}\leq p\leq n^{-2/3}
by Draganić, Glock, Munhá Correia, and Sudakov in 2025, where
C
is a constant
[
8
]
. Glock, Kühn, and Osthus
[
12
]
also verified the conjecture for dense quasirandom graphs in which the difference between the maximum and minimum degrees is small.
Here, we use the following notion of quasirandomness, which is a one-sided version of
\varepsilon
-regularity. Let
0<\varepsilon,p<1
.
A graph
G
on
n
vertices is called
lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular
if
e_{G}(S,T)\geq(p-\varepsilon)|S||T|
for all disjoint
S,T\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|,|T|\geq\varepsilon n
.

The Linear Arboricity Conjecture holds for regular robust expanders of linear minimum degree, which we define now.
Given
0<\nu\leq\tau<1
, we say that a graph
G
on
n
vertices is a
robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander
,
if for all
S\subseteq V(G)
with
\tau n\leq|S|\leq(1-\tau)n
the number of vertices that have at least
\nu n

neighbors in
S
is at least
|S|+\nu n
. The
\nu
-robust neighborhood

RN_{G}(S)
is the set of all
those vertices of
G
which have at least
\nu n
neighbors in
S
.
Note that for

0<\nu^{\prime}\leq\nu\leq\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}<1
, any robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander is also a robust
(\nu^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
-expander.
The theorem below by Kühn and Osthus
[
17
]

is profound and also is a powerful tool that has been used to establish other results.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
17
]
)
.

For every
\alpha>0
there exists
\tau=\tau(\alpha)>0
such that for every
\nu>0
there
exists an integer
N_{0}=N_{0}(\alpha,\nu,\tau)
for which the following holds. Suppose that

(i)

G
is an
r
-regular graph on
n\geq N_{0}
vertices, where
r\geq\alpha n
is even;

(ii)

G
is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander.

Then
G
has a Hamilton decomposition. Moreover, this decomposition can be found in
polynomial time in
n
.

As an application of Theorem
1.2
, the Linear Arboricity Conjecture holds for regular robust expanders of linear minimum degree,
as stated below. The proof follows exactly the same proof of Theorem
1.4
in
[
12
]
, and we omit it here.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that
0<1/n\ll\nu\leq\tau,\alpha<1
.
If
G
is a robust
r
-regular
(\nu,\tau)
-expander on
n
vertices with
r\geq\alpha n
, then
\la(G)\leq\lceil\frac{r+1}{2}\rceil
.

Using Theorem
1.2
,
Glock, Kühn, and Osthus
[
12
]
in 2016 also proved the Linear Arboricity Conjecture
for regular graphs of large degree.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
12
]
)
.

There exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every
d
-regular graph
G
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
d\geq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
has a decomposition into
\left\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\right\rceil
linear forests.

In this paper, we investigate the Linear Arboricity Conjecture for robust expanders of
linear minimum degree that are not necessarily regular and obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every
\alpha>0
there exists
\tau=\tau(\alpha)>0
such that for every
\nu>0
there
exists an integer
N_{0}=N_{0}(\alpha,\nu,\tau)
for which the following holds. If

G
is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander on
n\geq N_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
,
then
\la(G)\leq\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}\rceil
.

Note that Conjecture
1.1
is equivalent to its restriction on regular graphs, as any graph
G

with maximum degree
\Delta
can be embedded in a
\Delta
-regular graph
H
. However,
Theorem
1.5
does not follow from Theorem
1.3
.
For example, for sufficiently large
n
, we let
G
be obtained from
K_{n-3}
and
K_{3}

by adding edges joining each vertex of the
K_{3}
and about
(n-3)/3
distinct vertices of the
K_{n-3}

such that the resulting graph has maximum degree
n-3
. It is clear that the graph is a robust expander.
However, any
(n-3)
-regular supergraph
H
of
G
has at least
n+(n-3-\frac{n+3}{3})
vertices and has a cut of
size 3. The graph is not a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander for any
0<\nu\leq\tau\leq 2/5
.

It was shown independently by Glock, Kühn, and Osthus
[
12
]
and
Kühn and Osthus
[
17
]
that dense quasirandom graphs and graphs with large minimum degree are robust expanders.

Lemma 1.6

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
0<1/n_{0}\ll\varepsilon\ll\tau,p<1
and suppose
G
is a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices.
Then
G
is a robust
(\varepsilon,\tau)
-expander.

Lemma 1.7

(
[
17
]
)
.

Suppose that
0<\nu\leq\tau\leq\varepsilon<1
are such that
\varepsilon\geq 4\nu/\tau
. Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)n/2
. Then
G
is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
and Lemmas
1.6
and
1.7
, we have the following corollaries.

Corollary 1.8
.

For any given
0<\varepsilon,p,\alpha<1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
for which the following statement holds. If
G
is a lower-
(p,\varepsilon)
-regular graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
, then
\la(G)\leq\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}\rceil
.

Corollary 1.9
.

For any given
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
for which the following statement holds. If
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n
, then
\la(G)\leq\lceil\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}\rceil
.

Note again that
Corollary
1.9
does not follow from Theorem
1.4
as passing to a regular supergraph may significantly increase the number
of vertices. Indeed, we now construct a graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
such that any
\Delta
-regular supergraph
H
of
G
satisfies
\Delta<|V(H)|/2
.
Choose
n
such that both
\frac{1}{2}n
and
\varepsilon n
are even integers. We let
G_{1}
be a
3\varepsilon n
-regular graph on
\frac{1}{2}n-\varepsilon n
vertices,

G_{2}
be a
5\varepsilon n
-regular graph on another
\frac{1}{2}n-\varepsilon n
vertices, and
G_{3}
be another complete graph on
2\varepsilon n
vertices.
Then the graph
G
is obtained from
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3}
by adding all edges between
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, and all edges between
G_{1}
and
G_{3}
.
Now we have
\delta(G)=\frac{1}{2}n-\varepsilon n+2\varepsilon n-1=\frac{1}{2}n+\varepsilon n-1
, and
\Delta:=\Delta(G)=\frac{1}{2}n-\varepsilon n+3\varepsilon n+2\varepsilon n=\frac{1}{2}n+4\varepsilon n
.
Also, all vertices of
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
are of maximum degree in
G
. Thus
we have
\sum_{v\in V(G)}(\Delta-d_{G}(v))=2\varepsilon n(3\varepsilon n+1)
. Since all vertices of minimum degree in
G
form a clique, and
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)=3\varepsilon n+1
, it follows that any
\Delta
-regular supergraph
H
of
G
has at least
n+3\varepsilon n+1
vertices.
On average, one vertex
v
from
V(H)\setminus V(G)
is adjacent in
H
to at most
2\varepsilon n

vertices from
V(G)
. As
H
is
\Delta
-regular,
v
has at least
\Delta-2\varepsilon n
neighbors in
H
from
V(H)\setminus V(G)
.
Thus
H
has more than
n+\Delta-2\varepsilon n>\frac{3}{2}n
vertices. As a consequence, we have

\Delta<\frac{1}{3}(1+8\varepsilon)|V(H)|
, where
\frac{1}{3}(1+8\varepsilon)|V(H)|<\frac{1}{2}|V(H)|
if
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{16}
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
, which relies on Theorem
1.2
, will be given in Section 3 after some preliminaries in the next section.

2
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
G
be a graph and
S\subseteq V(G)
. We let
n(G)=|V(G)|
and
e(G)=|E(G)|
.
The subgraphs of
G
induced on
S
and
G-S
are respectively denoted by
G[S]
and
G-S
.
For
v\in V(G)
, we write
G-v
for
G-\{v\}
.
For two disjoint subsets
S,T
of
V(G)
, we denote by
E_{G}(S,T)
the set of edges in
G
with one endvertex in
S
and the other in
T
, and let
e_{G}(S,T)=|E_{G}(S,T)|
. If
F\subseteq E(G)
, then
G-F
is obtained from
G
by deleting all edges in
F
.
If
F
is a submultiset of edges from the complement
\overline{G}
, then
G+F

is obtained from
G
by adding the edges of
F
to
G
(
G+F
may become a multigraph). For two integers
p
and
q
, let
[p,q]=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid p\leq i\leq q\}
.

We will make use of the following notation:
0<a\ll b\leq 1
. Precisely,
if we claim that a statement is true whenever
0<a\ll b\leq 1
, then this means that
there exists a non-decreasing function
f:(0,1]\rightarrow(0,1]
such that the statement holds
for all
0<a\ll b\leq 1
satisfying
a\leq f(b)
. Intuitively, this means that for every
b>0
, the statement is true provided that
a
is sufficiently small compared to
b
.

2.1
Degree sequences and number of maximum degree vertices

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. A sequence of nonnegative integers listed in non-increasing order

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is
graphic
if there is a graph
G
such that the degree sequence of
G
is
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. In this case, we say that
G

realizes

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
.

The first result below by Hakimi
[
14
]
gives a sufficient and necessary condition
for a sequence of integers to be the degree sequence of a multigraph.
The second result by the second author
[
22
]
will be used to construct a supergraph of a given graph
G
by
adding additional vertices.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
0\leq d_{n}\leq\ldots\leq d_{1}
be integers. Then there exists a multigraph
G
on vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
such that
d_{G}(v_{i})=d_{i}
for all
i\in[1,n]
if and only if
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even and
\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}d_{i}\geq d_{1}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that
n\geq d+1\geq 3
. Suppose
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is a sequence of positive integers with
d_{1}=\ldots=d_{t}=d
and
d_{t+1}=\ldots=d_{n}=d-1
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
even, where
t\in[1,n]
. Then
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is graphic. Furthermore, the sequence
can be realized in polynomial time in
n
.

We will also need the following result by Plantholt and the second author
[
21
]
which gives a lower bound on the number of maximum degree vertices
if we assume that all vertices of degree less than maximum degree form a clique.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices such that all vertices of degree less than
\Delta(G)
are mutually adjacent in
G
. Then
G
has more than
n/2
vertices of maximum degree.

2.2
Matchings in almost-regular graphs

Let
G
be a graph.
A
matching

M
in
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint edges. The set of vertices
covered
or
saturated
by
M
is denoted by
V(M)
.
We say that
M
is
perfect
if
V(M)=V(G)
.
The graph
G
is
factor-critical
if
G
does not have a perfect matching but
G-v
has one
for any
v\in V(G)
.

For a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
o(G-X)

the number of components of
G-X

of odd order.
Define
{\rm df}(G)=\max_{X\subseteq V(G)}(o(G-X)-|X|)
.
By applying Tutte’s Theorem
[
23
]

to the graph obtained from
G
by adding
{\rm df}(G)
vertices and joining all edges
between
V(G)
and these new vertices, Berge
[
4
]
observed that
the maximum size of a matching in
G
is
\frac{1}{2}(n(G)-{\rm df}(G))
.
West
[
25
]
gave a short proof of this result. To state his result,
we need some more definitions.

For
X\subseteq V(G)
, denote by
Y
the set of components of
G-X
.
We define an auxiliary bipartite multigraph
B(X)
by contracting each component of
G-X

to a single vertex and deleting edges within
X
.
Thus for any vertex
x\in X
and any
y\in V(B(X))\setminus X
such that
y
is contracted from
a component
D_{y}
of
Y
, there are exactly
e_{G}(\{x\},V(D_{y}))
edges between
x
and
y
in
B(X)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a multigraph and
X\subseteq V(G)
be maximal with
o(G-X)-|X|={\rm df}(G)
. Then
the following statements hold.

(1)

(
[
25
, Lemma 2]
) Every component of
G-X
has odd order and is factor-critical.

(2)

(
[
25
, Lemma 3]
) The bipartite multigraph
B(X)
has a matching covering
X
.

(3)

The bipartite multigraph
B(X)
has a matching covering
X
and all vertices of degree at least
d

from
V(B(X))\setminus X
in
B(X)
, where
d=\max\{d_{B(X)}(x)\mid x\in X\}
.

Proof.

Statements (1)-(2) were proved by West in
[
25
]
.

We prove statement (3). By (2),
B(X)
has a matching
M_{1}
covering
X
. Let
Y=V(B(X))\setminus X
,
d=\max\{d_{B(X)}(x)\mid x\in X\}
,
Y_{d}=\{y\in Y\mid d_{B(X)}(y)\geq d\}
. By applying Hall’s Theorem to
B(X)[X,Y_{d}]
,
B(X)[X,Y_{d}]
and so
B(X)
has a matching
M_{2}
saturating all vertices from
Y_{d}
, where
B(X)[X,Y_{d}]
is the induced subgraph of
B(X)
with bipartitions as
X
and
Y_{d}
.
In each component
D
of the subgraph of
B(X)
induced on the symmetric difference of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, there is a matching that saturates vertices in
V(D)\cap(X\cup Y_{d})
. The union of these matchings together with edges of
M_{1}\cap M_{2}
gives a desired matching that covers all vertices of
X\cup Y_{d}
.
∎

A graph
G
is
almost
r
-regular
for some integer
r\geq 0
if exactly one vertex has degree
r+1
and all others have degree
r
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex almost
r
-regular graph for some integer
r\geq 0
. Then
\overline{G}
has a matching covering
at least
n-\frac{n}{n-r}-3
vertices of
\overline{G}
excluding the vertex of degree
r+1
in
G
.

Proof.

Let
x\in V(G)
be the vertex with
d_{G}(x)=r+1
.
Note that
\overline{G}
has
n-1
vertices of degree
(n-1-r)
and one vertex
x
of degree
n-2-r
. If
\overline{G}
has a perfect matching, then we are done.
Thus assume that
\overline{G}
does not have a perfect matching. Let
X\subseteq V(\overline{G})
be maximal
such that
o(\overline{G}-X)-|X|={\rm df}(\overline{G})
. Then
\overline{G}-X
has no even component by
Lemma
2.4
(1).
For any odd component
D
of
\overline{G}-X
such that
x\not\in V(D)
and
|V(D)|\leq n-r-1
, we have

\displaystyle e_{\overline{G}}(V(D),X)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|V(D)|(n-r-1-(|V(D)|-1))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n-r-1,

where the last inequality is obtained as the quadratic function
|V(D)|(n-r-1-(|V(D)|-1))
in
|V(D)|

achieves its minimum at
|V(D)|=1
or
|V(D)|=n-r-1
.
There are at most
\frac{n}{n-r}
odd components
D
of
\overline{G}-X
with
|V(D)|\geq n-r
,
and so there are at most
\frac{n}{n-r}+1
odd components
D
of
\overline{G}-X

such that
e_{\overline{G}}(V(D),X)\leq n-r-2
. As every vertex of
X
has degree at most
n-1-r

in
\overline{G}
,
by Lemma
2.4
(3), we know
that
o(\overline{G}-X)-|X|\leq\frac{n}{n-r}+1
and so

{\rm df}(\overline{G})\leq\frac{n}{n-r}+1
.
Thus by Berge’s formula from
[
4
]
that
the maximum size of a matching in a graph
H
is
\frac{1}{2}(n(H)-{\rm df}(H))
, we conclude that

\overline{G}
has a matching that covers at least
n-\frac{n}{n-r}-1
vertices of
\overline{G}
,
and so it has a matching that covers at least
n-\frac{n}{n-r}-3
vertices of
\overline{G}
excluding
x
.
∎

2.3
Robust expanders

The following lemma states that robust expansion is preserved when few edges are removed and/or few vertices are removed and/or added.
The result is stated for robust outexpanders in
[
11
]
, we list here its undirected counterpart.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq\nu\leq\tau\leq 1
. Let
G
be a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-expander on
n
vertices.

(a)

If
G^{\prime}
is obtained from
G
by removing at most
\varepsilon n
edges at each vertex, then
G^{\prime}
is a robust
(\nu-\varepsilon,\tau)
-expander.

(b)

Suppose that
\tau\geq(1+2\tau)\varepsilon
. If
G^{\prime}
is obtained from
G
by adding or removing at most
\varepsilon n
vertices, then
G^{\prime}
is a robust
(\nu-\varepsilon,2\tau)
-expander.

Following
[
16
]
, for a given integer
k\geq 2
, we say a graph
G
is
Hamilton
k
-linked

if whenever
x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{k}
are distinct vertices, there exist vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}

such that
P_{i}
connects
x_{i},y_{i}
for each
i\in[1,k]
and such that together the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
cover all vertices of
G
.
We will use the following result by Kühn, Lo, Osthus, and Staden from
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
16
, Corollary 6.9(ii)]
)
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that
0<1/n\ll\nu\ll\tau\ll\alpha<1
and
k\leq\nu^{4}n
.
If

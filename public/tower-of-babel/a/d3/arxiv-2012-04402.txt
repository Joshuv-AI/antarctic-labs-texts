A Primal-Dual Framework for Decentralized Stochastic Optimization Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Ketan Rajawat, Chirag Kumar

Abstract
We consider the decentralized convex optimization problem, where multiple agents must cooperatively minimize a cumulative objective function , with each local function expressible as an empirical average of data-dependent losses. State-of-the-art approaches for decentralized optimization rely on gradient tracking, where consensus is enforced via a doubly stochastic mixing matrix. Construction of such mixing matrices is not straightforward and requires coordination even prior to the start of the optimization algorithm. This paper puts forth a primal-dual framework for decentralized stochastic optimization that obviates the need for such doubly stochastic matrices. Instead, dual variables are maintained to track the disagreement between neighbors. The proposed framework is flexible and is used to develop decentralized variants of SAGA, L-SVRG, SVRG++, and SEGA algorithms. Using a unified proof, we establish that the oracle complexity of these decentralized variants is O ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) O(1/\epsilon) , matching the complexity bounds obtained for the centralized variants. Additionally, we also present a decentralized primal-dual accelerated SVRG algorithm achieving O ⁡ ( 1 / ϵ ) O(1/\sqrt{\epsilon}) oracle complexity, again matching the bound for the centralized accelerated SVRG. Numerical tests on the algorithms establish their superior performance as compared to the variance-reduced gradient tracking algorithms.

Introduction

Modern consumer devices generate massive amounts of data and successful deployment of large-scale machine learning algorithms necessitates addressing the challenges of scalability and privacy. Decentralized optimization offers a viable approach towards the design of such learning platforms. Within the decentralized learning rubric, each node retains the user data, while only iterates are exchanged between neighbors. Decentralized optimization algorithms exploit the availability of on-board device processors and therefore work in a peer-to-peer fashion without any central coordinator or parameter server.

In this work, we consider
V
nodes that seek to cooperatively solve the following network optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{V}(f_{i}({\mathbf{x}})+h_{i}({\mathbf{x}}))

(1)

where the data-dependent convex loss function
f_{i}
is local to a node
i
and takes the finite-sum form
f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}):=\frac{1}{K_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}f_{i}({\mathbf{x}},\xi_{i}^{k})
, with
\{\xi_{i}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{i}}
denoting data points, which could be images, videos, or browsing history, stored at node
i
. The regularization function
h_{i}
and the constraint set
\mathcal{X}_{i}
are also convex. The network topology is denoted by a connected graph
\mathcal{G}
and the nodes may only communicate over the edges of
\mathcal{G}
.

This work puts forth a primal-dual framework for the construction of decentralized stochastic variance-reduced algorithms for solving (

Decentralized Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm

\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{V}
denotes the set of nodes and
\mathcal{E}=\{(i,j)|i,j\in\mathcal{V}\}

V

E

i

\mathcal{N}_{i}:=\{j\in\mathcal{V}\mid(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\}
and its cardinality is given by
\left|\mathcal{N}_{i}\right|
. A node

is associated with a proper closed convex and smooth function

. The network seeks to cooperatively solve the optimization problem
\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}\sum_{i=1}^{V}(f_{i}({\mathbf{x}})+h_{i}({\mathbf{x}}))
where
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the optimization variable and
h_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are proper closed convex but possibly non-smooth regularization functions. Constraints of the form
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
where
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is closed and convex, if any, are included as indicator functions
1_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}
within
h_{i}
.
As discussed earlier, the loss function takes the form

\displaystyle f_{i}({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{K_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}f_{i}({\mathbf{x}},\xi_{i}^{k})

(2)

for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
. Throughout the paper, we will work with the following key assumption.

Assumption 1
.

The functions

are proper, closed, convex, and
L_{i}
-smooth. The functions
h_{i}
are proper, closed, and convex.

2.1
Generic Decentralized Primal-Dual Algorithm

We begin with describing the generic version of the proposed primal-dual (PD) algorithm. The specific choice of the stochastic gradients corresponding to different variance-reduced versions and their effect on the oracle complexity will be studied in the subsequent sections. Associate local variable

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\mathbf{X}}\sum_{i=1}^{V}[f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})+h_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})]\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ s. t. }\ {\mathbf{x}}_{i}={\mathbf{x}}_{j}\hskip 11.38109pt\forall(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}.

\mathcal{P}

The standard augmented Lagrangian for (

\displaystyle+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\langle{\bm{\lambda}}_{ij},{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\rangle+\rho\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\left\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-{\mathbf{x}}_{j}\right\|^{2}.

2.2
Oracle Complexity Analysis: Preliminaries

We begin with deriving some of the basic inequalities that will be used throughout the proofs. The Peter-Paul inequality states that for any
{\mathbf{a}}
,
{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and scalar

2\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\rangle\leq c\left\|{\mathbf{a}}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{c}\left\|{\mathbf{b}}\right\|^{2}

\left\|{\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}}\right\|\leq 2\left\|{\mathbf{a}}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|{\mathbf{b}}\right\|^{2}
. For two random variables
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathsf{X}}-\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathsf{X}}\right]+{\mathsf{Y}}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathsf{X}}-\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathsf{X}}\right]\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathsf{Y}}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathsf{X}}\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathsf{Y}}\right\|^{2}\right].

(10)

In order to study the performance of the proposed algorithms, we make use of the Bregman divergences between two points

and

and

\displaystyle D_{f_{i}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})

\displaystyle:=f_{i}({\mathbf{x}})-f_{i}({\mathbf{y}})-\langle\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{y}}),{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\rangle

\displaystyle D_{h_{i}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})

The stochastic gradient

3
Algorithm Variants

We present several variants of the proposed decentralized stochastic PD algorithm. Of particular interest are settings where
\{K_{i}\}
are large and the full gradients
\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t})
cannot be evaluated at every iteration. Instead, each node has access to a first-order oracle that, for a given
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t}
and index
k_{i}^{t}
, outputs
\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})
. Although complete expressions for all bounds are provided within the proofs, the main results will be stated using
O(\cdot)
notation, which subsumes universal constants and the initialization dependent constant
W_{1}
. Further, we will assume that a constant
\rho
is selected so that
G_{\rho}=\mathcal{O}(E)
.

3.1
Decentralized Stochastic Primal-Dual

The simplest case, analogous to the classical SGD algorithm, is when
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}=\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})
where
k_{i}^{t}
is selected uniformly at random from
\{1,\ldots,{K_{i}}\}
. With such a choice, it can be seen that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}
is an unbiased estimator of
\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t})
, since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}^{t}}[\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{K_{i}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k})=\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t}).

(46)

where
\mathbb{E}_{k_{i}^{t}}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation with respect to the random variable
k_{i}^{t}
. The stochastic PD is noise-dominated, and its performance depends on the gradient variance
\sigma^{2}:=(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\sigma_{i})^{2}
, where

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}:=\frac{1}{{K_{i}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}

(47)

The following lemma provides a bound on the oracle complexity of online decentralized stochastic PD algorithm in terms of the cumulative Bregman divergence.

Lemma 6
.

Under Assumption
1
, the Decentralized Stochastic PD algorithm has an oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{E}{\epsilon})
. The communication complexity is also the same.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

We begin with verifying that Assumption
4
holds for the decentralized stochastic PD algorithm. We have already verified that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}
is unbiased [cf. (
46
)]. To see (
26
)-(
27
), we have from the definition of
k_{i}^{t}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}\right]

(48)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})+\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}\right]

(49)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\frac{2}{K_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t},\xi_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2}{{K_{i}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}

(50)

where (
49
) follows from (
10
). Bounding the first term in (
50
) from (
14
) and recognizing that the second term is the variance of
\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k_{i}^{t}})
, we obtain the constants required in Assumption
4
as
A_{i}=2L_{i}
,
B_{i}=C_{i}=0
,
\varrho_{i}=1
, and

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{{K_{i}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{i}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star},\xi_{i}^{k})-\nabla f_{i}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\star})\right\|^{2}.

(51)

Therefore, we need to choose
\eta_{i}\leq\frac{1}{4L_{i}}
and the bound in (
28
) is written as

\displaystyle\frac{W_{1}G_{\rho}}{T}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\left[\frac{W_{1}}{\eta_{i}T}+\sigma_{i}^{2}\eta_{i}\right]

(52)

Setting
\eta_{i}=\frac{\sqrt{W_{1}}}{\sigma_{i}\sqrt{T}}
, we obtain a bound of
\mathcal{O}(\frac{E}{T}+\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{T}})
on the cumulative Bregman divergence where
\sigma:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\sigma_{i}
. Conversely, the number of oracle calls required to reach an
\epsilon
-optimal solution is given by
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{E}{\epsilon})
. Since a single round of communication, involving an exchange of primal and an exchange of dual variables, occurs at every

, the communication complexity is also the same.

As in the classical SGD setting, the bound does not depend on the number of data points
K_{i}
, and can therefore be applied on the online setting where data arrives sequentially and indefinitely. In the online setting, the performance can be improved via mini-batching, where each node makes multiple oracle calls for the same
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{t}
at each iteration.

Lemma 10
.

For

6
Conclusions

This work proposed a primal-dual framework for the construction of decentralized stochastic algorithms. A generic decentralized stochastic primal-dual algorithm is proposed that can be adapted to all variance-reduction techniques such as SAGA, LSVRG, and SVRG++. Further, a decentralized accelerated version of the SVRG is proposed that is shown to achieve a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}})
. Such results imply that the proposed class of decentralized algorithms is optimal since it achieves the centralized rate.

Looking forward, it still remains to be seen if the proposed algorithms exhibit faster convergence under strong convexity. The proof approach developed here works only for unbiased stochastic gradient approximations, and extension to biased case also remains open. Finally, it would be interesting to see if the analysis developed here carries over to the non-convex case.

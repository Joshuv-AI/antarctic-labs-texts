Generalized Integer Splines on Arbitrary Graphs Thanks: Thanks to Tom Zaslavsky for several helpful conversations.
Lauren L. Rose, Jeff Suzuki

Abstract
. Generalized integer splines on a graph G G with integer edge weights are integer vertex labelings such that if two vertices share an edge in G G , the vertex labels are congruent modulo the edge weight. We introduce collapsing operations that reduce any simple graph to a single vertex, carrying with it the edge weight information. This corresponds to a sequence of surjective maps between the associated spline modules, leading to an explicit construction of a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -module basis in terms of the edge weights.



Introduction

Consider a graph
G
with integer edge weights. Label the vertices so that if two vertices are joined by an edge with weight
w
, then their vertex labels are congruent modulo
w
. This leads to the following questions:

•

Given arbitrary edge weights, can we always construct a vertex labeling?

•

Can we find a finite set that generates all vertex labelings?

In
[
6
]
, Tymozcko, et. al. introduced the study of generalized splines on a graph with edges weighted by ideals in a commutative ring
R
. Their foundational paper concludes with many open questions, primarily about the
R
-module structure of sets of splines and how much this structure is determined by properties of the ring and/or the underlying graph. A subsequent survey article
[
13
]
describes the role that generalized splines play in geometry and topology.

In this paper we set
R
to be
\mathbb{Z}
, the ring of integers, although the results in this paper hold when
R
is an Euclidean Ring, with minor modifications. We answer both of the above questions affirmatively, and in doing so find a complete characterization of spline modules over
\mathbb{Z}
for arbitrary graphs.

Generalized splines are a generalization of polynomial splines, which are ubiquitous in applied math, computer graphics and approximation theory. They are often defined to be piecewise polynomial functions on a polyhedral subdivision of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Alfeld and others
[
1
]
studied the question of finding dimensions and bases for vector spaces of splines of restricted polynomial degree. In
[
3
,
5
]
, Billera pioneered the use of algebraic techniques in the study of polynomial splines. When the polynomial degrees are unrestricted, the set of all splines on a polyhedral complex will be both a ring and an
R
-module, where
R
is a polynomial ring in
d
variables over a field.

The algebraic study of polynomial splines led to a representation of splines as vertex labels of the dual graph of the polyhedral complex
[
11
,
10
]
, where the edges are labeled with linear forms. In this new representation, a set of vertex labels is a spline if each edge label divides the difference between its two vertex labels. This representation easily extends to arbitrary rings, and in fact arises in geometry and topology.

When
R
is a principal ideal domain, generalized spline modules are always free of rank
n
, the number of vertices of the graph. In
[
8
]
, Handschy, et. al, constructed “flow-up class” bases for
n
-cycle graphs with integer edge weights. In
[
9
]
we proved that flow-up class bases exist for spline modules over arbitrary graphs with integer edge weights, and expanded the class of graphs for which a flow-up class basis could be constructed.

The main result of this paper is the construction of flow-up class bases for the spline module
S_{G}
, for any arbitrary graph
G
with integer edge weights. To do this, we introduce two collapsing operations that transform
G
into a graph with fewer vertices or edges. Using these operations, we can reduce any connected graph to a point. Then we show that each of these operations induces a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module map of the associated spline modules. The main algebraic result is that
S_{G}
is the direct sum of the kernels of these maps. From there, all that is left to do is to construct a basis for each of the kernels, and show that their pre-images form a basis for
S_{G}
. A nice feature of this construction is that the first non-zero entry of each basis element, viewed as an
n
-tuple of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, can be written in terms of least common multiples and greatest common divisors of a subset of the edge weights.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(G,A)
be a simple, connected graph containing at least one vertex

. Then
(G_{v},A_{v})
is also connected.

Proof.

We consider two cases.

•

Case 1: If
G_{v}
is the empty graph or has only one vertex, then it is vacuously connected.

•

Case 2: Assume
G_{v}
has 2 or more vertices. Let
x
and
y
be distinct vertices of
G_{v}
. Hence they are vertices in
G
since
G_{v}
doesn’t introduce new vertices. Since
G
is connected, there is a path
P
connecting
x
and
y
in
G
. There are two cases.

–

First, assume

does not pass through the vertex
v
. The removal of
v
does not affect
P
, so
P
is also a path in
G_{v}
.

–

Now suppose
P
passes through the vertex
v
. Then
P
passes through vertices
p
and
q
, where
p
and
q
are adjacent to
v
. When we form
G_{v}
, the path

in

will be replaced by a single edge

. Thus, we can form a path

from

to

in
G_{v}
by replacing the edges
pv
and
vq
with the edge

in
G_{v}
.

∎

A graph can always be collapsed to a single edge, and even further to a single vertex.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(G,A)
be a connected edge-weighted graph, and let

and

be vertices of

(1)

We can collapse

to the graph

with vertices

(2)

We can collapse

Proof.

Choose a vertex other than

and

are the only vertices left, and remove any multiple edges. Since the graphs at each stage are connected, by Lemma

and

For part 2, a star-clique operation on

3.
The Spline Modules
S_{G}
and
S_{G^{\prime}}

Now that we have constructed the graph collapsing operations, we describe their effects on the spline module
S_{G}
. In order to do this, we must first fix an ordering of the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of
G
.
Next, we partition
S_{G}
into

\mathcal{F}_{i}
, which are subsets of splines with the first
i
entries equal to zero and the

will be a basis that consists of an element from each non-zero flow-up class.

Definition 3.1
.

For
0\leq i<n
, the
i
th flow-up class
, denoted
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, is the set of splines of the form
F=(0,0,\ldots,0,f_{i+1},\ldots,f_{n})
where

is non-zero.
We also define
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}=\mathbf{0}=(0,0,\ldots,0)
, the zero spline.

We now define the leading term of a spline to be its first non-zero entry.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
where
i\neq n
, so that
F=(0,0,\ldots,0,f_{i+1},\ldots,f_{n})
and
f_{i+1}\neq 0
.
L(F)=f_{i+1}
is the
leading term
of
F
.

Definition 3.3
.

A spline
B\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a
minimal element
of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
if
L(B)>0
, and for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
,
(L(B)\leq|L(F)|
.

For example,
\mathbf{1}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
has leading term
1
, which is minimal in
\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
\mathbf{1}
is a minimal element of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

Note:
Although minimal elements of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
have the same leading term, the other terms are not in general unique.

Our strategy for constructing a basis for
S_{G}
is to find a minimal element from each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, for

. The theorem below asserts that this set will alway form a basis, called a

.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
9
]

Let
(G,A)
be an edge labeled graph with
n
vertices. Let
\mathcal{B}=\left\{B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{n-1}\right\}\subset S_{G}
where each

is a minimal element of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{B}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module basis for

.

We now look at the effect of collapsing operations on flow-up classes and minimal elements. We begin with an example.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}v_{4}&\equiv&v_{1}\bmod c\\

We can formalize this process for any graph.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(G,A)
be an edge-weighted simple graph on vertices
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and let
(G_{n},A_{n})
be the result of a star-clique operation on vertex

. The map
\phi:S_{G}\rightarrow S_{G_{n}}
defined by
\phi(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n-1})
is a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism with kernel
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.

Proof.

The first thing to note is that the only edges affected by a star-clique operation are those incident to vertex

v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{d}}
. Thus the defining equations for
S_{G}
and
S_{G_{n}}
only differ in that
S_{G}
includes equations

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle\equiv g_{i_{1}}\bmod a_{i_{1}}

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle\equiv g_{i_{2}}\bmod a_{i_{2}}

\displaystyle g_{n}

\displaystyle\equiv g_{i_{d}}\bmod a_{i_{d}}

whereas

includes the equations

g_{i_{j}}\equiv g_{i_{k}}\bmod(a_{i_{j}},a_{i_{k}}),\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d

By Theorem
1.4
, the first set of equations implies that second, hence
\phi
is well defined. Also by Theorem
1.4
, the second set of equations implies there exists a
g_{n}
satisfying the first set, and since the remaining equations don’t involve
g_{n}
, we have shown there exists a spline in
S_{G}
mapping to a given spline in

.

Now let

denote the kernel of
\phi
. Then
\mathbf{g}\in K=\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{0})
if and only if
\mathbf{g}=(0,\ldots,0,g_{n})
in

. If

, then
\mathbf{g}
has exactly

{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}
. If
g_{n}=0
, then
\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{0}
, the only element of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(G,A)
be an edge weighted graph and
(G^{\prime},A^{\prime})
be the result of an edge collapse operation on multiple edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{r}
. Then
S_{G^{\prime}}=S_{G}
.

Proof.

Suppose that edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{r}
each connect vertices
v
and
w
, and have edge labels
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}
. The label for the new edge

in
G^{\prime},A^{\prime}
is
[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}]
. Thus the only difference between the defining equations for
S_{G}
and
S_{G^{\prime}}
are the

g_{v}\equiv g_{w}\bmod a_{i}
and the single equation
g_{v}\equiv g_{w}\bmod[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}]
in
S_{G_{e}}
. By Lemma
1.3
, these two sets of equations have the same solutions, hence
S_{G^{\prime}}=S_{G}
.
∎

In other words, collapsing multiple edges changes the graph, but not the corresponding spline module. Putting these theorems together we now state our main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(G^{\prime},A^{\prime})
be the result of a sequence of collapsing operations on an edge-weighted graph
(G,A)
resulting in the removal of vertices
v_{r+1},\dots v_{n}
and the collapsing of any multiple edges. Then the map
\phi:S_{G}\rightarrow S_{G^{\prime}}
defined by
\phi(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{r})
is a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism with kernel
\mathcal{F}_{r}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.6
, each time we do a star-clique operation, we have a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
- module homomorphism
\phi:S_{G}\rightarrow S_{G_{v}}
, and each time we do an edge collapse, the module stays the same. Since
\phi
is a composition of finitely many surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphisms, it is also a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism.

Let
K
denote the kernel of
\phi
. Then
\mathbf{g}\in K=\phi^{-1}(\mathbf{0})
if and only if
\mathbf{g}=(0,\ldots,0,g_{r+1},\ldots,g_{n})
in
S_{G}
. Since
\mathbf{g}
has at least
r
zeroes, it is an element of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for some
i\geq r
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(G,A)
be an edge-weighted graph on
n
vertices and
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
be distinct vertices of
G
. The map
\phi:S_{G}\rightarrow S_{K_{2}}
defined by
\phi(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n})=(g_{i},g_{j})
is a surjective
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism.

Proof.

We start with
(G,A)
and delete vertices one by one until there are only two vertices left. Each time we end up with multiple edges, we collapse them and replace

with
S_{G_{e}}
. By Theorem
3.8
, the result now follows.
∎

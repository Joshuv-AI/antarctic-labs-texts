Automorphisms of the double cover of a circulant graph of valency at most 7
Ademir Hujdurović, Đorđe Mitrović, Dave Witte Morris

Abstract
. A graph X X is said to be unstable if the direct product X × K 2 X\times K_{2} (also called the canonical double cover of X X ) has automorphisms that do not come from automorphisms of its factors X X and K 2 K_{2} . It is nontrivially unstable if it is unstable, connected, and non-bipartite, and no two distinct vertices of X have exactly the same neighbors. We find all of the nontrivially unstable circulant graphs of valency at most 7 7 . (They come in several infinite families.) We also show that the instability of each of these graphs is explained by theorems of Steve Wilson. This is best possible, because there is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph of valency 8 8 that does not satisfy the hypotheses of any of Wilson’s four instability theorems for circulant graphs.

2B.
Normal Cayley graphs

Definition 2.9
.

(M.-Y. Xu
[

For each

\Cay(G,S)

Lemma 2.10

Assume

has Wilson type
(

)

Proof.

Since

is not central in

. So it is conjugate to some other element of order

. However, since

is normal, we know that

cannot be the only element of order

Also note that

\Aut BX

.

Now, since
(0,1)
,

Proposition 2.13

(Baik-Feng-Sim-Xu
[
2
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
\Cay(G,S)
be a connected Cayley graph on an abelian group
G
. Assume, for all
s,t,u,v\in S
:

s+t=u+v\neq 0\implies\{s,t\}=\{u,v\}.

Then the Cayley graph
\Cay(G,S)
is normal.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, we essentially reproduce the proof that is given in
[
2
]
, but add a few additional details.

It suffices to show that for every
\sigma\in\Aut(\Cay(G,S))
, such that
\sigma(0)=0
:

(2.14)

\displaystyle(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n})^{\sigma}=s_{1}^{\sigma}+\cdots+s_{n}^{\sigma},\quad\text{for $s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in S$, $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

To accomplish this, we will show that

(2.15)

\displaystyle(x+u)^{\sigma}=x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma}\implies(x+u+v)^{\sigma}=x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma}+v^{\sigma}

for all
x\in G
, and
u,v\in S
and then (
2.14
) follows by induction on
n
.

Case 1 .

Assume
u\neq v
.

We claim that

\bigl((x+u)+S\bigr)\cap\bigl((x+v)+S\bigr)=\{x,x+u+v\}.

The
\supseteq
containment is trivial. If
y
is an element of the left-hand side, then

y=(x+u)+s=(x+v)+t
for some
s,t\in S
.

Using the assumption from the statement of the
Section
and the fact that
u\neq v
, we obtain that
u=t
and
v=s
. In particular,
y=x+u+v
.
This completes the proof of the claim.

Since
\sigma
is an automorphism fixing the identity, we also know that

\left((x+u)+S\right)^{\sigma}=(x+u)^{\sigma}+S^{\sigma}=(x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma})+S,

where in the last step we apply the hypothesis of (
2.15
). It now follows that:

\displaystyle\{x^{\sigma},(x+u+v)^{\sigma}\}

\displaystyle=\bigl((x+u)+S\cap(x+v)+S\bigr)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\bigl((x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma})+S\bigr)\cap\bigl((x^{\sigma}+v^{\sigma})+S\bigr)

\displaystyle=\{x^{\sigma},x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma}+v^{\sigma}\}.

In particular,
(x+u+v)^{\sigma}=x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma}+v^{\sigma}
.

Case 2 .
Assume
u=v
.

Since
\sigma
is a graph automorphism, we know it is a bijection from
(x+u)+S
onto
(x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma})+S
.
Section

2B
shows it is also a bijection

from
\bigl((x+u)+S\bigr)\setminus\{x+2u\}
onto
\bigl((x^{\sigma}+u^{\sigma})+S\bigr)\setminus\{x^{\sigma}+2u^{\sigma}\}
.

Hence, it must also hold that
(x+2u)^{\sigma}=x^{\sigma}+2u^{\sigma}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

def IsStable(n, S):

Theorem 4.1

Proposition 4.2
.

There are no nontrivially unstable circulant graphs of valency
\leq 3
.

Proof.

We consider twin-free, connected, nonbipartite, circulant graphs of each valency
\leq 3
.

(valency
0
) The one-vertex trivial graph
K_{1}
is stable, because
BK_{1}=\overline{K_{2}}
, and
|{\Aut\overline{K_{2}}}|=2=2\,|{\Aut K_{1}}|
.

(valency
1
)
K_{2}
is bipartite.

(valency
2
) If
C_{n}
is a nonbipartite cycle, then
n
is odd, so
BC_{n}\cong C_{2n}
, so

|{\Aut BC_{n}}|=|{\Aut C_{2n}}|=2\cdot 2n=2\cdot|{\Aut C_{n}}|,

so
C_{n}
is stable.

(valency
3
) A connected, nonbipartite, circulant graph

of valency
3
is either an odd prism or a nonbipartite Möbius ladder. In either case, the canonical double cover is the even prism

, where

. It is easy to check that
K_{4}
is stable (see

). And the following calculation (which uses

is also stable when

|{\Aut BX}|=|{\Aut(K_{2}\mathbin{\raise 0.7pt\hbox{\smaller[2]$\square$}}C_{n})}|=|S_{2}\times{\Aut C_{n}}|=2\cdot 2n\leq 2|{\Aut X|}.\qed

Theorem 4.3

A circulant graph

\Leftarrow

(

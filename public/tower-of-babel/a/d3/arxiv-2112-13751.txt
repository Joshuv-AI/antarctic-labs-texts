Abstract
Clustering is an essential primitive in unsupervised machine learning. We bring forth the problem of sublinear-time differentially-private clustering as a natural and well-motivated direction of research. We combine the k k -means and k k -median sublinear-time results of Mishra et al. (SODA, 2001) and of Czumaj and Sohler (Rand. Struct. and Algorithms, 2007) with recent results on private clustering of Balcan et al. (ICML 2017), Gupta et al. (SODA, 2010) and Ghazi et al. (NeurIPS, 2020) to obtain sublinear-time private k k -means and k k -median algorithms via subsampling. We also investigate the privacy benefits of subsampling for group privacy.

Group privacy for sampling algorithms.

Group privacy ensures that for pairs of inputs that differ on a small number of points, the privacy loss is still bounded. For example, in the setting of a health survey administered to families, a family may wish to preserve all its members’ privacy. Any
\varepsilon
-differentially private algorithm, ensures
(g\varepsilon,0)
-privacy for groups of size
g
. We show that our random sampling algorithm has better group privacy guarantees.
In other words, an algorithm that runs an
(\varepsilon,0)
-differentially private mechanism on a subsample is
(T\cdot\varepsilon,\delta_{T})
-differentially private for groups of size
g
, for
0\leq T\leq g
, where
\delta_{T}
is the probability of the number of samples from the
g
elements is
>T
. We note that
\delta_{T}
is often negligible even for
T\ll g
. In such cases, the guarantee of
(T\cdot\varepsilon,\delta_{T})
-differential privacy is arguably much stronger than the naive guarantee of
(g\varepsilon,0)
-group privacy.

3
Group Privacy in Sublinear setting

In this section, we give a group privacy result that holds for
any
sampling algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)
that samples a set
S
from the input set
D
by independently sampling with probability
\xi
and runs an
\varepsilon
-DP algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}
on
S
.
Let
D^{\prime}
be a set that differs on
g
elements with respect to
D
, and
0\leq T\leq g
be a threshold. Define

\delta_{T,\xi,g}:=1-\sum^{T}_{j=0}{\binom{g}{j}}\xi^{j}(1-\xi)^{g-j}\;,

in other words,
\delta_{T,\xi,g}
is the probability of choosing more than
T
elements that differ from elements in
D^{\prime}
in the sample
S
.

Given that
{\mathcal{A}}
is
\varepsilon
-DP, we have already shown that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-DP (see
Theorem
1
). In the following theorem, we show that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
also gives us better group privacy guarantees.

Theorem 9
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is an
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-DP sampling algorithm (as described above) then it gives
(T\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta_{T,\xi,g})
-privacy for groups of size
g
, where
\delta_{T,\xi,g}:=1-\sum^{T}_{j=0}{\binom{g}{j}}\xi^{j}(1-\xi)^{g-j}
.

Proof.

Consider two sets
D
and
D^{\prime}
that differ on
g
elements,
i.e.
,
|D|=|D^{\prime}|+g
and set
S\subseteq D
sampled independently w.p.
\xi
.
Define the random variable
Y
to be the number of elements in
S
sampled from the
g
differing elements. Fix an output set
C
in the output space of
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C]

\displaystyle=\sum^{g}_{i=0}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C,Y=i]

\displaystyle=\sum^{g}_{i=0}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\Pr[Y=i]

\displaystyle=\sum^{T}_{i=0}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\Pr[Y=i]+\sum^{g}_{i=T+1}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\Pr[Y=i]

\displaystyle\text{Applying the naive group privacy bound for each term }\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\text{ in the first sum,}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{T}_{i=0}e^{\varepsilon\cdot i}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D^{\prime})\in C]\Pr[Y=i]+\sum^{g}_{i=T+1}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\Pr[Y=i]

Observe that
\sum^{g}_{i=T+1}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C|Y=i]\Pr[Y=i]\leq\sum^{g}_{i=T+1}\Pr[Y=i]\leq\delta_{T,\xi,g}
, therefore,

\displaystyle\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D)\in C]\leq e^{\varepsilon\cdot T}\Pr[{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(D^{\prime})\in C]+\delta_{T,\xi,g}\;.

∎

We demonstrate how in many instances, our sampling algorithm
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
achieves better group privacy guarantees for chosen
\xi
and
T
such that
T\ll g
.
(1) If we sample each element of the input set with probability
\xi=1/\sqrt{g}
, and set threshold
T=2\sqrt{g}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is
(2\sqrt{g}\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta_{T,\xi,g})
for
\delta_{T,\xi,g}
negligible in
g
.
(2) If we sample each element of the input set with probability
\xi=1/\log({g})
, and set threshold
T=2g/\log(g)
, then
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is
((2g/\log(g))\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta_{T,\xi,g})
for
\delta_{T,\xi,g}
negligible in
g
.

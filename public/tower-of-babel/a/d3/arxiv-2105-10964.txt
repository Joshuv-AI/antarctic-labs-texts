Block theory and Brauer’s first main theorem for profinite groups Journal: Advances in Mathematics
Ricardo J. Franquiz Flores, John W. MacQuarrie

Abstract
We develop the local-global theory of blocks for profinite groups. Given a field k k of characteristic p p and a profinite group G G , one may express the completed group algebra k ⁡ [ [ G ] ] k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt] as a product ∏ i ∈ I B i \prod_{i\in I}B_{i} of closed indecomposable algebras, called the blocks of G G . To each block B B of G G we associate a pro- p p subgroup of G G , called the defect group of B B , unique up to conjugacy in G G . We give several characterizations of the defect group in analogy with defect groups of blocks of finite groups. Our main theorem is a version of Brauer’s first main theorem: a correspondence between the blocks of G G with defect group D D and the blocks of the normalizer N G ​ ( D ) \textnormal{N}_{G}(D) with defect group D D .

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of characteristic
p
. The algebra
kG
, as with any finite dimensional associative algebra, has a unique decomposition as a direct product of indecomposable algebras
kG=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{n}
, the
B_{i}
being known in group representation theory as the
blocks
of
G
. Rather than try to deal with
kG
all at once, the philosophy of block theory is to study the blocks of
G
individually, as the representation theory of certain
B_{i}
may be considerably more tractable than the representation theory of
kG
.

The difficulty of a block
B
is measured by a
p
-subgroup of
G
(unique up to
G
-conjugacy) called the
defect group
of
B
. As examples of this “measure”: blocks with cyclic defect group are completely understood, while there are structural conjectures about blocks whose defect group is abelian that are false for arbitrary blocks, perhaps the most famous being Broué’s Abelian Defect Group Conjecture (for a light-hearted survey of the state-of-the-art in modular representation theory and block theory see
[
5
]
).

The most fundamental theorem of the block theory of finite groups is Brauer’s first main theorem (see
[
3
, Theorem 5]
for the original statement or for instance
[
2
, Theorem 6.2.6]
for a more modern one), which gives a natural bijection between the blocks of
G
with defect group
D
and the blocks of the normalizer
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
of
D
in
G
with defect group
D
. Our main theorem is Brauer’s first main theorem for profinite groups:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
,
G
a profinite group and
D
a closed subgroup of
G
that is open in a
p
-Sylow subgroup that contains it. There is a natural bijection between the blocks of
G
over
k
with defect group
D
, and the blocks of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
over
k
with defect group
D
.

The hypothesis that
D
be open in a
p
-Sylow subgroup is necessary, but is not a restriction because we will show that the defect group of every block of
G
has this property.

More broadly, we begin a systematic study of the local-global theory of blocks of a profinite group
G
over a field
k
of characteristic
p
. We define the defect group of a block of
G
, prove its basic properties and give several alternative characterizations (Theorem
5.18
), before proving Brauer’s first main theorem.

The most striking feature of the discussion is the generality that block theory permits:
all results apply to arbitrary profinite groups. This suggests that the block theoretic approach to the representation theory of profinite groups will be as central to the theory as it is with finite groups. For comparison, we note that with modules, conditions on the group are frequently required to prove the most powerful results – see for instance
[
14
,
15
]
.

Because we do not demand that the field
k
be finite, our algebras and modules are not compact, but they are
pseudocompact
, thus maintaining a weaker version of compactness (linear compactness) that suffices for our purposes. While pseudocompact objects are perhaps less familiar than compact ones, the advantage of allowing this greater generality is undeniable, as it gives a representation theorist access to algebraically closed fields.

In a future paper, the tools developed here will be used to provide a complete classification of the blocks of a profinite group having (finite or infinite) cyclic defect group, in close analogy with the classification of blocks of finite groups with cyclic defect group.

The background to the material discussed in this article is well served by the literature. For an introduction to profinite groups see
[
18
]
or
[
23
]
. For basic properties of pseudocompact algebras see
[
4
]
or
[
17
]
. For introductory expositions on the modular representation theory of finite groups see
[
2
]
or
[
1
]
. For an excellent and encyclopedic introduction to the block theory of finite groups see
[
12
,
13
]
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Pseudocompact algebras and modules

Let
k
be a discrete topological field of characteristic
p
. In what follows, when the coefficient ring is unspecified it is assumed to be
k
(so for example “algebra” means “
k
-algebra”). Throughout this section, unless otherwise specified, all modules are topological left modules. For a general introduction to pseudocompact objects, see
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
pseudocompact algebra
is an associative, unital, Hausdorff topological
k
-algebra
A
possessing a basis of
0
consisting of open ideals
I
having cofinite dimension in
A
that intersect in 0 and such that
A=\varprojlim_{I}A/I
.

Equivalently, a pseudocompact algebra is an inverse limit of discrete finite dimensional algebras in the category of topological algebras. If
G
is a profinite group, an inverse system of finite continuous quotients
G/N
of
G
induces an inverse system of finite dimensional algebras
k[G/N]
, whose inverse limit
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
, the
completed group algebra
of
G
, is a pseudocompact algebra.

We will occasionally make use of the Jacobson radical
J(A)
of a pseudocompact algebra
A
: that is, the intersection of the maximal open left ideals of
A
. Essentially the only property we require of the Jacobson radical is the easily checked fact that if
\rho:A\to B
is a surjective homomorphism of pseudocompact algebras, then
\rho(J(A))\subseteq J(B)
(cf.
[
4
, §1]
for more on
J(A)
).

If
A
is a pseudocompact algebra, a
pseudocompact
A
-module
is a topological
A
-module
U
possessing a basis of 0 consisting of open submodules
V
of finite codimension that intersect in 0 and such that
U=\varprojlim_{V}U/V
. The category of pseudocompact modules for a pseudocompact algebra has exact inverse limits.

If
V
is a topological vector space, cosets of closed subspaces of
V
are called
closed linear varieties
. We say that
V
is
linearly compact
if for every family
\mathcal{F}=\{W_{i}\mbox{ : }i\in I\}
of closed linear varieties of
V
with the finite intersection property, we have that
\bigcap_{i\in I}W_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a pseudocompact algebra and

pseudocompact
A
-modules.

1.

The module
U
is linearly compact.

2.

If ever
\rho:U\to V
is a continuous homomorphism, then
\rho(U)
is linearly compact and hence closed in

.

3.

The submodule abstractly generated by a finite subset of

is closed.

4.

If
U
is finitely generated as an
A
-module, then every
A
-module homomorphism

is continuous.

Proof.

Items 1 to 3 are
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
and Item 4 is
[

, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

We collect here several properties of linearly compact vector spaces, given in
[
11
, II, §6.27]
, which will be used frequently:

Lemma 2.3
.

1.

Discrete finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces are linearly compact.

2.

A product of linearly compact
k
-vector spaces is linearly compact.

3.

Let
\{V_{i},\varphi_{ij},I\}
be an inverse system of topological vector spaces and for each

, let
W_{i}
be a non-empty closed linear variety in
V_{i}
. If
\varphi_{ij}(W_{i})\subseteq W_{j}
, so that

4.

A continuous linear bijective map between two linearly compact spaces is an isomorphism.

Several times in this section we will give results for pseudocompact algebras and cite for the proof a reference whose statement is for profinite algebras (that is, compact rather than just linearly compact algebras). When we do this, what we mean is that the proof in the given reference goes through without change for pseudocompact algebras.

2.3

Let

-module and

-module and

The module

Every continuous

-module homomorphism

\rho_{K}:U\to\prod\limits_{i\in K}U_{i}
by
\rho_{K}(u)=(\rho_{i}(u))_{i\in K}
.
The homomorphisms
\rho_{K}
induce a continuous surjective homomorphism
\rho:U\to\prod\limits_{i\in I}U_{i}
, which is injective since
\sum_{e\in E}e=1_{A}
. As continuous linear bijections between pseudocompact algebras are isomorphisms by Lemma
2.3
,
U\cong\prod\limits_{i\in I}U_{i}
. The uniqueness of the decomposition follows from the uniqueness of the complete set of centrally primitive orthogonal idempotents.
∎

In particular, indecomposable pseudocompact modules lie in a unique block.

4.1
Finite dimensional modules and blocks

Recall our convention that the symbols
\varphi_{N}
and
\varphi_{MN}
are reserved for the canonical projections
\varphi_{N}:k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]\to k[G/N],\varphi_{MN}:k[G/N]\to k[G/M]
.

Remark 4.4
.

By
[
16
, Proposition 6.4]
, the complete set of centrally primitive orthogonal idempotents
E
of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is a discrete set and can be obtained as the direct limit of the corresponding complete sets
E_{N}
for the
k[G/N]
. We make the direct system explicit, as we will use it in future: if
N\leq M
are open normal subgroups of
G
, define
\psi_{MN}:E_{M}\to E_{N}
by sending
c\in E_{M}
to the unique centrally primitive idempotent
d
of
k\left[G/N\right]
such that
\varphi_{MN}(d)c\neq 0
. Note that as
c
is primitive as a central idempotent, this is equivalent to saying that
\varphi_{MN}(d)c=c
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
U,V
be finite dimensional
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules lying in the same block of
G
. Then there is
N_{0}\trianglelefteq_{O}G
acting trivially on
U
,
V
and such that
U,V
lie in the same block of
G/N_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
U,V
are finite dimensional, it is sufficient to prove the lemma supposing that both are indecomposable. Let
e
be the block idempotent such that
eU=U,eV=V
and let
M
be an open normal subgroup of
G
acting trivially on both
U
and
V
, so that both can be treated as
k[G/M]
-modules. Note that they are indecomposable as such.

We use the notation from Remark
4.4
. If
f,g
are the block idempotents of
U,V
respectively in
k[G/M]
then, since
\varphi_{M}(e)f=f
and
\varphi_{M}(e)g=g
, we have that
\psi_{M}(f)=\psi_{M}(g)=e
. It follows from the construction of the direct limit that there is an open normal subgroup
N_{0}
of
G
contained in
M
such that
\psi_{MN_{0}}(f)=\psi_{MN_{0}}(g)
. Both
U
and
V
are in the block of
k[G/N_{0}]
with block idempotent
\psi_{MN_{0}}(f)
.
∎

4.2
Inverse systems of blocks

Consider
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
as a module for the completed group algebra
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
with the following continuous multiplication:

\displaystyle k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]\times k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]

\displaystyle((g_{1},g_{2}),x)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle g_{1}xg_{2}^{-1},

for
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
x\in k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
. The canonical projections
\varphi_{MN}:k[G/N]\to k[G/M],\varphi_{N}:k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]\to k[G/N]
are
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphisms.

The blocks of
G
are precisely the indecomposable summands of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module and they are pairwise non-isomorphic as
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules, since their annihilators as
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times 1}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules are pairwise non-equal.

Let
B
be a block of
G
treated as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module, and
N\trianglelefteq_{O}G
. Define
B_{N}
to be the coinvariant module
B_{N\times N}
, but note that
B_{N\times N}=B_{N\times 1}
, since given
m,n\in N
and
x\in B_{N\times 1}
there is
n^{\prime}\in N
such that

(m,n)x=mxn^{-1}=mn^{\prime}x=(mn^{\prime},1)x=x,

so that
N\times N
acts trivially on
B_{N\times 1}
.
By Proposition
2.7
,
B=\varprojlim_{N}\{B_{N},\varphi_{MN}\}
where, as ever,
\varphi_{MN}
is the canonical quotient map.

Denote by
\delta:G\to G\times G
the diagonal map
g\mapsto(g,g)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

The
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is isomorphic to the induced module
k\uparrow_{\delta(G)}^{G\times G}
.

Proof.

The
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphisms
\rho:k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]\to k\uparrow_{\delta(G)}^{G\times G}
defined by
g\mapsto(g,1)\widehat{\otimes}1
, and
\gamma:k\uparrow_{\delta(G)}^{G\times G}\to k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
defined by
(g,h)\widehat{\otimes}\lambda\mapsto g\lambda h^{-1}
, are continuous. The checks that they are well-defined and mutually inverse are as in
[
12
, Proposition 5.11.6]
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
B
be a block of
G
. Then
B
is the inverse limit of a surjective inverse system of blocks of
G/N
and algebra homomorphisms, for some cofinal set of open normal subgroups
N
of
G
.

Proof.

Let
E
be the set of block idempotents of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
. We use the direct system
\{E_{N},\psi_{MN}\}
described in Remark
4.4
. If
e\in E
is the block idempotent of
B
, there is
N_{0}\trianglelefteq_{O}G
and
e_{0}\in E_{N_{0}}
such that
e=\psi_{N_{0}}(e_{0})
. Consider the cofinal system
\mathcal{N}
of open normal subgroups
N
of
G
with
N\leq N_{0}
.

For each
N\leq N_{0}
, let
e_{N}=\psi_{N_{0}N}(e_{0})
, the unique centrally primitive idempotent of
k[G/N]
such that
\varphi_{N_{0}N}(e_{N})e_{0}=e_{0}
. Observe that if ever
N\leqslant M\leqslant N_{0}
, then
\varphi_{MN}(e_{N})e_{M}=e_{M}
.
The block
X_{N}
of
G/N
with block idempotent
e_{N}
is a direct summand of
B_{N}
as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module, so there are
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphisms
\iota_{N}:X_{N}\to B_{N}
inclusion, and
\pi_{N}:B_{N}\to X_{N}
multiplication by
e_{N}
, such that
\pi_{N}\iota_{N}=\textnormal{id}_{X_{N}}
. Now we can form a new inverse system of blocks
X_{N}
with maps
\gamma_{MN}:=\pi_{M}\varphi_{MN}\iota_{N}
. Note that
\gamma_{MN}(e_{N})=\varphi_{MN}(e_{N})e_{M}=e_{M}
, which implies in particular that the
\gamma_{MN}
are surjective algebra homomorphisms.
Then
\{X_{N},\gamma_{MN}\}
forms an inverse system, because for all
N\leqslant M\leqslant L
and all
x=xe_{N}\in X_{N}
we have

\gamma_{LM}\gamma_{MN}(x)=\pi_{L}\varphi_{LM}(\varphi_{MN}(x)e_{M})=\varphi_{LN}(x)\varphi_{LM}(e_{M})e_{L}=\gamma_{LN}(x).

Denote by
X
the inverse limit of this inverse system.

The split homomorphisms
\pi_{N}:B_{N}\to X_{N}
are the components of a map of inverse systems, so by Lemma
2.10
, the induced continuous map
\pi=\varprojlim_{N}\pi_{N}
is a split
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphism. Hence
X
is a direct summand of
B
. But
B
is indecomposable as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module and
X
is not
0
, so

B=X=\varprojlim_{N}X_{N}.

∎

Remark 4.8
.

Throughout this article, given a block
B
of
G
with block idempotent
e
, the notation
\{X_{N},\gamma_{MN},N\in\mathcal{N}\}
will refer to a fixed inverse system of blocks as constructed in Proposition
4.7
. This inverse system is not unique, due to the choice of the open normal subgroup
N_{0}
and of the block idempotent
e_{0}
of
G/N_{0}
, but it will be important that once these choices are made, the maps
\gamma_{MN}
are canonical.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
B
be a block of
G
with block idempotent
e
. Let
S
be a finite dimensional
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module lying in
B
. Then there is an open normal subgroup
N^{\prime}
of
G
acting trivially on
S
such that
S
lies in the block

of
G/N^{\prime}
. Furthermore,

lies in
X_{N}
for every

.

Proof.

Write
B=\varprojlim_{N}\{X_{N},\gamma_{MN},N\in\mathcal{N}\}

as above and fix

. There is a simple
k[G/M]
-module

lying in

. Then
T
lies in
B
.
If
N\leqslant M
then

, since otherwise

, which would imply that

. So

, treated as a

-module, lies in

.

Since

are finite dimensional
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules lying in the same block

, by Lemma

there is
N^{\prime}\trianglelefteq_{O}G
with

, such that

-block. But
T
lies in

.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
G
be a profinite group,
R
a closed subgroup of
G
and
B
a block of
G
treated as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module. Then
B
is
\delta(R)
-projective if, and only if,
X_{N}
is
\delta(R)(N\times N)
-projective for each
N\trianglelefteq_{O}G
.

Proof.

The forward implication is easy: if
B
is relatively
\delta(R)
-projective then
B_{N}=B_{N\times N}
is relatively
\delta(R)(N\times N)
-projective. But
X_{N}
is a direct summand of
B_{N}
, so we are done.

For the reverse implication, suppose that
X_{N}
is
\delta(R)(N\times N)
-projective for each
N\trianglelefteq_{O}G
.

We utilize the natural isomorphism of Lemma
2.4
to write

B_{N}\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)(N\times N)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}}=k[G\times G/\delta(R)(N\times N)]\widehat{\otimes}_{k}B_{N}.

Letting
N
vary and setting
\Psi_{MN}
to be the tensor product of the natural projections, we obtain an inverse system of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules
\{B_{N}\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)(N\times N)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}},\,\Psi_{MN}\}
, whose inverse limit is the module
B\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}}
by
[
18
, Proposition 5.2.2]
. Denoting by
\pi_{N}:B_{N}\to X_{N}
the canonical projection, we define the continuous
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle q_{N}:k[G\times G/\delta(R)(N\times N)]\widehat{\otimes}_{k}B_{N}

\displaystyle\to X_{N}

\displaystyle z\widehat{\otimes}b

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{N}(b).

Note that
q_{N}
splits as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module homomorphism, being the composition of split homomorphisms
B_{N}\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)(N\times N)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}}\to X_{N}\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)(N\times N)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}}
and
X_{N}\hskip-1.70717pt\downarrow_{\hskip-0.7113pt{\delta(R)(N\times N)}}\hskip-1.70717pt\uparrow^{\hskip-0.7113pt{G\times G}}\to X_{N}
(the latter split since

is relatively
\delta(R)(N\times N)
-projective). Furthermore the

yield a map of inverse systems, as may be easily checked. The induced homomorphism

is relatively
\delta(R)
-projective by Theorem
2.16
.
∎

5

5.1
Definition and basic properties

Recall that we treat
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
as a
G
-algebra with action from
G
given by conjugation. Whenever we treat a subalgebra or a quotient algebra of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
as a
G
-algebra, the action from
G
is induced from this one. Note that the center
\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{G}
, so block idempotents of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
belong to
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{G}
. Recall the definition
3.4
of
\textnormal{oTr}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
B
be a block of a profinite group
G
with block idempotent
e
. A
defect group
of
B
is a closed subgroup
D
of
G
such that
e\in\textnormal{oTr}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
and minimal with this property.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
B
be a block of
G
. A defect group of
B
exists.

Proof.

Let
e
be the block idempotent of
B
and consider
\mathcal{D}=\{H\leq_{C}G\,:\,e\in\textnormal{oTr}_{H}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{H})\}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is partially ordered by inclusion and non-empty, since
G\in\mathcal{D}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a chain in
\mathcal{D}
, we assert that
L=\bigcap\limits_{H\in\mathcal{C}}H\in\mathcal{D}
. Fix
N\trianglelefteq_{O}G
. As
LN
is open in
G
,
LN=HN
for some
H\in\mathcal{C}
and hence
e\in\textnormal{Tr}_{LN}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{LN})
. Since
N
was arbitrary,

by definition and hence

as claimed. The existence of a defect group for

now follows from Zorn’s Lemma.
∎

In several proofs that follow we use
Tr
to mean two different things (the
Tr
of Definitions
2.15
and
3.1
), but see Remark
3.2
.

Proposition 5.3
.

If
B
is a block of
G
with defect group

, then any finitely generated

-module lying in

Let

be a finitely generated

For each

we have

.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

be a closed subgroup of

and

with block idempotent

. Then

is
\delta(H)
-projective as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module.

Suppose that

. Observe that if

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}_{\delta(H)(N\times N)}^{\delta(G)(N\times N)}(\alpha_{N})(e)

\displaystyle\sum_{(r,r)\in\delta(G)(N\times N)/\delta(H)(N\times N)}(r,r)\alpha_{N}((r^{-1},r^{-1})e)

\displaystyle\sum_{(r,r)\in\delta(G)(N\times N)/\delta(H)(N\times N)}r\alpha_{N}(r^{-1}er))r^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(r,r)\in\delta(G)(N\times N)/\delta(H)(N\times N)}ra_{N}r^{-1}err^{-1}

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}_{HN}^{G}(a_{N})e

\displaystyle e.

, we have

\displaystyle B

|

\displaystyle B\downarrow_{\delta(G)(N\times N)}\uparrow^{G\times G}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\,\big|\,}Z\uparrow^{\delta(G)(N\times N)}\uparrow^{G\times G}

which implies that

If

are conjugate in

by Proposition

5.3
The Brauer homomorphism

We give two further characterizations of defect groups.

Consider as ever the continuous action of
G
on
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
by conjugation. When
D
is a closed subgroup of
G
and
Q
an open subgroup of
D
, then
\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q})
is an ideal of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}
by Part 1 of Lemma
3.3
. Thus
\sum\limits_{Q\lneqq_{O}D}\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q})
is an abstract ideal of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}
and so its closure is a closed ideal of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}

[
21
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Definition 5.15
.

[
19
, Definition 3.2]

Let
G
be a profinite group and
D
a closed pro-
p
subgroup of
G
. The corresponding
Brauer quotient
is the quotient algebra

k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{[D]}:=k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}/\overline{\sum\limits_{Q\lneqq_{O}D}\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q})}.

The
Brauer homomorphism
is the natural projection

\textnormal{Br}_{D}:k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}\to k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{[D]}.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
B
be a block of a profinite group
G
with block idempotent
e
. Then
B
has defect group
D
if, and only if,
e\in\textnormal{oTr}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
and
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
B
has defect group
D
. Then
e\in\textnormal{oTr}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
by definition, so we need only check that
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\neq 0
. If
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)=0
, then by Rosenberg’s Lemma
4.2
,
e\in\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q})
for some proper open subgroup
Q
of
D
. Fix an open normal subgroup
M
of
G
such that
X_{M}
has defect group
DM/M
(by Corollary
5.10
) and such that
QM/M
has the same index in
DM/M
as
Q
has in
D
. Then
\varphi_{M}(e)
is an element of
\textnormal{Tr}_{QM/M}^{DM/M}(k[G/M]^{QM/M})
so that
\textnormal{Br}_{DM/M}(\varphi_{M}(e))=0
. But if
f
is the block idempotent of
X_{M}
then
\varphi_{M}(e)f=f
, and hence
\textnormal{Br}_{DM/M}(f)=0
since
\textnormal{Br}_{DM/M}
is an algebra homomorphism. This contradicts the finite version of the result
[
2
, Lemma 6.2.4]
, since
X_{M}
has defect group
DM/M
.

Suppose now that
e\in\textnormal{oTr}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
and
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\neq 0
. Then certainly
B
has a defect group
R
contained in
D
.
Note that
R
is open in
D
by Proposition
2.19
, so we can find an open normal subgroup
M
of
G
such that
D\cap RM=R
. We have
e=\textnormal{Tr}_{RM}^{G}(a)
for some

by hypothesis. Applying Mackey’s formula (Lemma
3.3
),

e=\sum_{g\in D\backslash G/RM}\textnormal{Tr}_{D\cap{}^{g}\!RM}^{D}({}^{g}\!a).

We claim that if
R
is a proper subgroup of
D
, then so is each of the
D\cap{}^{g}\!RM
: if for some
g
we had
D\cap{}^{g}\!RM=D
, then
R<D\leqslant{}^{g}\!RM
, which implies that
RM\leqslant{}^{g}\!RM
and hence that

is in the normalizer
\textnormal{N}_{G}(RM)
. But then
D\cap{}^{g}\!RM=D\cap RM=R<D
, a contradiction. It follows that if

is proper in

, then

is in the kernel of
\textnormal{Br}_{D}
, contrary to our hypothesis. Hence

and we are done.
∎

For the convenience of the reader we collect together the characterizations of a defect group proved above, and provide one further characterization that will help us to prove Brauer’s first main theorem. We need one more simple lemma.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
R,D
be closed pro-
p
subgroups of

. If, for each

.

Fix

and

such that

. By the Mackey formula,

0\neq\textnormal{Br}_{D}(\textnormal{Tr}_{RN}^{G}(a))=\sum_{g\in D\backslash G/RM}\textnormal{Br}_{D}(\textnormal{Tr}_{D\cap{}^{g}\!RN}^{D}({}^{g}\!a)),

and hence the closed set

of

with

is non-empty. A standard compactness argument yields that

is non-empty, and

.
∎

.

Let

,

6
Brauer’s first main theorem for profinite groups

Brauer’s first main theorem for finite groups (cf.
[

, Theorem 6.2.6]
) is a natural bijection between the blocks of a finite group

with defect group

, and the blocks of the normalizer of

in

with defect group

. In this section we use the results thus obtained to demonstrate our main theorem, a version of Brauer’s first main theorem for profinite groups. We follow the approaches of
[

, §6.2]
and
[

, §12.6]
. Given the results already obtained, the proof is mostly similar to the finite case, with the exception of Lemma

. Remark

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be a closed pro-

[

It will be convenient for us to consider

and its proof is rather delicate. It seems to be a fundamental obstruction that the lemma cannot be formulated without the hypothesis that

is open in

is abusively being used to indicate the map induced by
\varphi_{MN}:k[G/N]\to k[G/M]
on the corresponding subquotients. The outer triangles commute by definition, and as long as the middle vertical map is well-defined, then the inner squares also commute by definition. So the only thing that needs to be checked is that the middle vertical map is well defined, and for this we must see what happens to an element
\textnormal{Tr}_{QN/N}^{DN/N}(a)
when
QN
is proper in
DN
, but
QM=DM
. But in this case

\varphi_{MN}(\textnormal{Tr}_{QN/N}^{DN/N}(a))=\sum_{dN\in DN/QN}{}^{dM}\varphi_{MN}(a)=|DN:QN|\varphi_{MN}(a)=0

because
\varphi_{MN}(a)
is a
DM/M
-fixed point and
|DN:QN|=0
in
k
. The map is thus well-defined and the claim follows. In particular, the outer shape commutes, and so
\varphi_{MN}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}=\textnormal{Br}_{DM/M}\varphi_{MN}
.

Write
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}=k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]\oplus\overline{\sum_{Q<_{O}D}\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q})}
. We first claim that the second summand is sent to
0
when passed in either direction through the diagram of the statement of the lemma, so suppose that
x=\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(a)
for
Q
a proper open subgroup of
D
and
a\in k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{Q}
. Note that
\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}\textnormal{Tr}_{Q}^{D}(a)=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{Q}^{G}(a)
, as can be seen by looking at the cofinal subset of those
N
for which
QN\cap D=Q
.
For such
N
we have

\textnormal{Tr}_{QN/N}^{G/N}(\varphi_{N}(a))=\sum_{g\in DN\backslash G/QN}\textnormal{Tr}_{({}^{g}\!QN\cap DN)/N}^{DN/N}({}^{g}\!\varphi_{N}(a)).

The argument of Proposition
5.16
shows that the subgroups
({}^{g}\!QN\cap DN)/N
are proper in
DN/N
, and hence

\textnormal{Tr}_{QN/N}^{G/N}(\varphi_{N}(a))\in\textnormal{Ker}(\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}).

Thus
\textnormal{Br}_{D}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(x)=0
. But going the other way round the square,
b\cdot\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}\textnormal{Br}_{D}(x)
is clearly
0
, so the claim follows.

We from now on assume that
x\in k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
. Denote by
s
the element
\textnormal{Br}_{D}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(x)
and by
t
the element
\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}\textnormal{Br}_{D}(x)=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(x)
. We will show that for each
M\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

\varphi_{M}(s)=b\cdot\varphi_{M}(t),

and hence that
s=b\cdot t
. Since
b
does not depend on
s
or
t
, the lemma follows.

We first calculate
\varphi_{N}(s)
for arbitrary
N\in\mathcal{N}
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{N}\textnormal{Br}_{D}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(x)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\varphi_{N}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:DN|_{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\textnormal{Tr}_{DN/N}^{G/N}(\varphi_{N}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:DN|_{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\bigg(\sum_{g\in DN\backslash G/DN}\textnormal{Tr}_{(DN\cap{}^{g}\!DN)/N}^{DN/N}({}^{gN}\varphi_{N}(x))\bigg).

Note that
DN\cap{}^{g}\!DN=(D\cap{}^{g}\!DN)N
by Dedekind’s modular law applied to
D
and
N\leqslant{}^{g}\!DN
. We claim that if
g\not\in\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)
, then
\textnormal{Tr}_{(DN\cap{}^{g}\!DN)/N}^{DN/N}({}^{gN}\varphi_{N}(x))
is sent to
0
by
\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}
: observe that

D\subseteq{}^{g}\!DN\Longleftrightarrow g\in\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)

and hence for our
g\not\in\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)
, the subgroup
Q=D\cap{}^{g}\!DN
is proper in
D
. Note further that

\left|\frac{DN}{QN}\right|=\left|\frac{DQN}{QN}\right|=\left|\frac{D}{D\cap(D\cap{}^{g}\!DN)N}\right|=\left|\frac{D}{D\cap{}^{g}\!DN}\right|=\left|\frac{D}{Q}\right|.

Hence
\textnormal{Tr}_{(DN\cap{}^{g}\!DN)/N}^{DN/N}({}^{gN}\varphi_{N}(x))=\textnormal{Tr}_{QN/N}^{DN/N}({}^{gN}\varphi_{N}(x))

is in the kernel of
\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}
as claimed, and so continuing the above calculation we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:DN|_{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\bigg(\sum_{g\in\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/DN}\textnormal{Tr}_{(DN\cap{}^{g}\!DN)/N}^{DN/N}({}^{gN}\varphi_{N}(x))\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:DN|_{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\textnormal{Tr}_{DN/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)|_{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{DN/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(x)).\qquad\qquad\qquad(*)

Meanwhile

\varphi_{N}(t)=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{DN/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}(\varphi_{N}(x)).

Fix
M\in\mathcal{N}
. Since
M
is open and
\bigcap_{N\in\mathcal{N}}\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)=\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
, there is
N\in\mathcal{N}
contained in
M
with the property that
\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)M=\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)M
. Hence

\varphi_{MN}\left(\textnormal{Tr}_{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(t))\right)=\left|\frac{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)}{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N}\right|\varphi_{M}(t),

or in other words,

\varphi_{MN}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(t))=\varphi_{M}(t).\qquad\qquad\qquad(**)

We put all these calculations together to obtain the claimed relationship between
\varphi_{M}(s)
and
\varphi_{M}(t)
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{M}(s)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{MN}\varphi_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)|_{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\varphi_{MN}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{DN/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(x))

\displaystyle\hbox{by }(*)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)|_{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\varphi_{MN}\textnormal{Br}_{DN/N}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{DN/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}(\varphi_{N}(x))

by Lemma
3.6

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)|_{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D/M}\varphi_{MN}\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)N/N}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)/N}(\varphi_{N}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G:\textnormal{N}_{G}(DN)|_{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D/M}\varphi_{M}(t)

\displaystyle\hbox{by }(**)

\displaystyle=b\cdot\varphi_{M}(t).

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
D
be a closed normal pro-
p
subgroup of
G
. The central idempotents of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
lie in
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
.

Proof.

Fix an idempotent
e\in\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
. For each
N\trianglelefteq_{O}G
,
DN/N
is a normal
p
-subgroup of
G/N
and
\varphi_{N}(e)
is a central idempotent of
k[G/N]
. By the finite version of this result
[
2
, Proposition 6.2.2]
,
\varphi_{N}(e)\in k[\textnormal{C}_{G/N}(DN/N)]
, and hence

e\in\varprojlim k[\textnormal{C}_{G/N}(DN/N)]=k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt].

∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
D
be a closed pro-
p
subgroup of the profinite group
G
and let
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
such that
D\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)\leqslant H\leqslant\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
. Let
b
be a block of
H
with block idempotent
f
and defect group
D
. There is a unique block
B
of
G
with block idempotent
e
such that
f=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)
.

Proof.

By hypothesis
D
is normal in
H
, and hence
f\in k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{H}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
by Lemma
6.4
. But again by hypothesis
\textnormal{C}_{H}(D)=\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)
, and hence
f\in k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
.

The Brauer homomorphism is a surjective algebra homomorphism
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D}\to k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
, and hence (because
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(1)=1
) there is at least one block idempotent
e
such that
f=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)
. If
e^{\prime}
is another block idempotent with this property, then

f=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e^{\prime})=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(ee^{\prime}),

which implies that
e=e^{\prime}
, since distinct block idempotents are orthogonal.
∎

Definition 6.6
.

Let
D
be a closed pro-
p
subgroup of
G
and
H
a closed subgroup of
G
with
D\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)\leq H\leq\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
. If
b
is a block of
H
with block idempotent
f
, define the
Brauer correspondent

b^{G}
of
b
to be the unique block of
G
with block idempotent
e
such that
f=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)
.

We may finally prove our main theorem – Brauer’s first main theorem for profinite groups.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
G
be a profinite group,
P
a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
and
D
an open subgroup of
P
. There is a bijective correspondence between the blocks of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
with defect group
D
and the blocks of
G
with defect group
D
, given by sending the block
b
of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
with defect group
D
to its Brauer correspondent
b^{G}
.

Proof.

Given the results already obtained, we are in a position to proceed as with finite groups. We mostly follow
[
22
, Theorem 12.6.4]
.

By Lemma
6.4
, the block idempotents of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
lie in
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]=k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
.

Let
b
be a block of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
with block idempotent
f
and defect group
D
. By Lemma
6.5
the Brauer correspondent
b^{G}
exists. Let
e
be the block idempotent of
b^{G}
, so that
f=f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)
by definition. Since
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\neq 0
,
b^{G}
has a defect group
R
containing
D
by Theorem
5.18
. We claim that
R=D
.

The subspace
e\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
is an ideal of
e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
by Part 3 of Lemma
3.6
. By Lemma
6.3
and since
\textnormal{Br}_{D}
is an algebra homomorphism,

\textnormal{Br}_{D}\left(e\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})\right)=\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\textnormal{Br}_{D}\left(\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})\right)=\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{C}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]),

which contains
f
. Note that
f\not\in J(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{C_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
, since if it were then
\varphi_{N}(f)
would be a non-zero idempotent in
J(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{C_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]_{N})
for some
N
, which is impossible since the radical of a finite dimensional algebra is nilpotent. Hence
\textnormal{Br}_{D}\left(e\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})\right)\not\subseteq J(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{C_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
. It follows that
e\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})\not\subseteq J(e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]))
. But the idempotent
e
is primitive in
\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
so that
e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])
is local by Lemma
4.2
, and hence
e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])=e\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(ek[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
. In particular
e\in\textnormal{Im}(\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G})
, showing that
R
is contained in
D
by Theorem
5.18
. So
R=D
as claimed.

For convenience denote by
\Phi
the map from the blocks of
N_{G}(D)
with defect group
D
to the blocks of
G
with defect group
D
, sending the block
b
to its Brauer correspondent
b^{G}
. The argument above shows that
\Phi
is well-defined, so it remains to check that it is bijective.

Suppose that
{b_{1}}^{G}={b_{2}}^{G}
has block idempotent
e
. Then, letting
f_{i}
be the block idempotent of
b_{i}
we have

f_{i}=f_{i}\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\in\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt])).

But
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e\textnormal{Z}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]))
is local and hence
f_{1}=f_{2}
, showing that
\Phi
is injective.

Let
B
be a block of
G
with defect group
D
and block idempotent
e
, so that
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)\neq 0
and
e\in\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{D})
. Let
f
be a block idempotent of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
such that
f\cdot\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)=f
. If
b=k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]f
has defect group
D
, then
B=\Phi(b)
and
\Phi
is surjective. It thus remains to check that
b
has defect group
D
. By Lemma
6.3
,
\textnormal{Br}_{D}(e)=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(a)
for some
a
, so that by Lemma
3.6
,

f=f\cdot\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(a)=\widetilde{\textnormal{Tr}}_{D}^{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}(fa)

and hence
b
has a defect group
R
contained in
D
by Theorem
5.18
. On the other hand,
R
is an intersection of
p
-Sylow subgroups of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
by Proposition
5.11
, while
D
is the intersection of all the
p
-Sylow subgroups of
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
by Corollary
5.13
. Hence
D\leqslant R
and we are done.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
G
be a profinite group,
P
a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
,
D
an open subgroup of
P
and
L
a closed subgroup of
G
containing
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
. There is a canonical bijection between the blocks of
G
with defect group
D
and the blocks of
L
with defect group
D
.

Proof.

Since
\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)=\textnormal{N}_{L}(D)
, Theorem
6.7
applies for both
G
and
L
, yielding the result.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

Given that a defect group is open in a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
that contains it by Proposition
5.8
, Theorem
6.7
applies to arbitrary blocks of
G
. It is never-the-less important to note that Theorem
6.7
is false when we replace the word “open” in its statement with the word “closed”: Let
G
be the free pro-
p
group of rank
2
, freely generated by
x
and
y
. If
D
is the closed subgroup generated by
x
, then
D=\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)
. There is of course a block of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
with defect group
D
(namely
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\textnormal{N}_{G}(D)}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
itself), but there is not a block of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
with defect group
D
(
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
has only one block and its defect group is
G
).

Extensions of k k -regulous functions from two-dimensional varieties
Juliusz Banecki

Abstract
We prove that a k k -regulous function defined on a two-dimensional non-singular affine variety can be extended to an ambient variety. Additionally we derive some results concerning sums of squares of k k -regulous functions; in particular we show that every positive semi-definite regular function on a non-singular affine variety can be written as a sum of squares of locally Lipschitz regulous functions.



1
Introduction

In the paper real affine varieties are taken to be defined as in
[
Bochnak
]
. The ring of regular functions of such a variety
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}(X)
. If moreover the variety is irreducible, the field of rational functions is denoted by
\mathbb{R}(X)
.

We have to begin with a quick discussion on a fundamental definition in the recently founded field of
regulous geometry
. The field is concerned with studying certain subclasses of continuous functions on real algebraic varieties admitting rational representations. One of the first papers attempting on classification of such functions is
[
francuska
]
. In the paper, given a real affine variety
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the authors consider two classes of functions on
X
:

1.

for a non-singular
X
, real valued functions on
X
which are of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
and become regular after being restricted to some Zariski open and dense subset of
X
(
[
francuska
]
, Définition 2.15),

2.

for a possibly singular
X
, real valued functions on
X
for which there exists an extension to the entire affine space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the condition above (
[
francuska
]
, page 137).

In the discussed paper both of these two classes are called
k-regulous
. It is believed that for a non-singular
X
the two notions coincide, nonetheless it has not been proven yet. Thanks to
[
real_and_p-adic
]
such a result is known for
k=0
, and this makes the definition of a
0
-regulous function clear. It is also a result of
[
real_and_p-adic
]
that the condition
2
implies condition
1
for an arbitrary value of
k
. Unfortunately both of the two notions have been used as definitions of
k
-regulous functions in different papers before, causing some confusion. Because of that, before introducing our results we need to choose to which definition we will stick to.

In recent works the property
1
is the one which is more commonly taken as the definition of a
k
-regulous function. An example of such usage is the paper
[
open_problems
]
. Moreover this definition has the nice property that it is clearly invariant under biregular isomorphisms, and it does not highlight the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in any special way. For these reasons we choose to use this one in the paper. Let us state this formally, for simplicity we will work only with irreducible varieties:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a non-singular irreducible real affine variety. We define the ring of
k
-regulous functions, denoted by
\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
, to consist of all the functions satisfying property
1
. We associate such functions with their rational representations, such an association is clearly well defined and defines a ring embedding
\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)\subset\mathbb{R}(X)
.

The main result of our paper states that the two possible definitions coincide when the dimension is equal to two. Stating it formally:

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a two-dimensional closed non-singular irreducible subvariety of an affine non-singular irreducible variety
Y
. Every
k
-regulous function on
X
is a restriction of some
k
-regulous function on
Y
.

The theory developed here allows us to prove some additional results regarding
k
-regulous functions, not necessarily in dimension
2
. The most interesting ones are connected with Hilbert’s
17
th Problem. It has been known for a long time that there exist positive semi-definite polynomials in more than two variables which admit
bad points
i.e. they cannot be written as sums of squares of regular functions (see
[
bad_points
]
for some discussion). It is desired to study whether there are some weaker regularity conditions which can be imposed on the squared functions. The most general result in this spirit known so far follows straight from the Positivestellensatz and says that a psd regular function can always be written as a sum of squares of 0-regulous functions (see
[
regular_after_blowingups
]
). We are able to strengthen the result in the following way:

Theorem S1
.

Let
X
be a non-singular irreducible affine variety. Every positive semi-definite function
f\in\mathcal{R}(X)
can be written as a sum of squares of locally Lipschitz
0
-regulous functions on
X
.

In a similar manner we are able to give a sufficient condition for a regular function to be representable as a sum of squares of
k
-regulous functions:

Theorem S2
.

Let
X
be a non-singular irreducible affine variety and let
f\in\mathcal{R}(X)
be a sum of
2k
-th powers of rational functions. Suppose that every zero of
f
is also a zero of its derivatives up to order
2k
. Then it is a sum of squares in
\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
.

These results cannot be improved much further; see Section
for discussion.

Lastly we significantly improve
[
regular_after_blowingups
, Corollary 2.14]
under the assumption on the dimension being equal to two:

Theorem B
.

Let
m,l\geq 0
. Let
X
be a non-singular irreducible affine variety of dimension
2
and let
f\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
be written as a fraction
f=\frac{p}{q}
with
p,q\in\mathcal{R}(X)
. Then

p^{l}f^{m}\in\mathcal{R}^{kl+k+2l}(X).

The result does not seem to admit any important applications, although it is quite surprising how high this class of smoothness is.

2
Preliminaries

From now on, unless explicitly stated otherwise, “variety” means a non-singular irreducible real affine variety.

2.1
Partial derivatives on manifolds

Firstly we need to recall a basic notion from the theory of smooth manifolds.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of dimension
k
and
\varphi=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
a smooth mapping. Suppose that
a\in M
is a point at which
\varphi
is non-singular i.e. its derivative is an isomorphism between the tangent space of
M
at
a
and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then the following basis of the tangent space at
a

((d\varphi_{a})^{-1}(e_{1}),\dots,(d\varphi_{a})^{-1}(e_{k}))

is called the
coordinate induced tangent basis of
T_{a}M
. Since the tangent space is canonically identified with the space of derivations at the point
a
this allows for a definition of partial derivatives with respect to a regular system of parameters:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
g:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function differentiable at
a
. We define the partial derivative of
g
with respect to
x_{i}
at the point
a
to be equal to

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}(a):=dg_{a}((d\varphi_{a})^{-1}(e_{i})).

The gradient vector
[\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}]_{1\leq i\leq k}
of
g
will be denoted by abuse of notation by
dg_{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}}
or just by
dg
.

The definition above is of course well known, what is less known is that it translates well to the algebraic setting.

Let now
X
be a variety,
a
a point of
X
, and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
a system of parameters of
\mathcal{R}(X)_{a}
. Choose any embedding
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We can find a Zariski open neighbourhood
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
a
and a system of parameters
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{a}
at
a
such that

•

x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{R}(A:=B\cap X)

•

y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{R}(B)

•

x_{i}=y_{i}+\mathcal{I}(A)
for
1\leq i\leq k

•

y_{k+1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathcal{I}(A)

•

\det\left(\left[\frac{\partial y_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\right)\in\mathcal{R}(B)^{\ast}
.

In this setting we define two functions
\varphi
and
\psi
by

\displaystyle\varphi:A\ni x\mapsto(x_{1}(x),\dots,x_{k}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

\displaystyle\psi:B\ni x\mapsto(y_{1}(x),\dots,y_{n}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The set
B
is chosen in such a way that the derivative of
\psi
is non-singular at every point thereof. This means that the derivative
\varphi
is non-singular at every point of
A
, so we can define the partial derivative
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}
of a regular function
g\in\mathcal{R}(A)
. Conveniently it turns out to be a regular function itself:

Observation 2.2
.

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}
is a regular function on
A
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case when
n=k
, i.e.
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{i}=y_{i}
. With this assumption the Jacobian matrix
[\frac{\partial x_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
of
\varphi
is invertible in
\mathcal{R}(A)
. By the definition:

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=dg_{x}((d\varphi_{x})^{-1}(e_{i}))=dg_{x}\cdot(d\varphi_{x})^{-1}\cdot e_{i}\in\mathcal{R}(A).

Notice that a local inverse of
\varphi
may not be a regular function and we do not require it to be one, we solely invert the derivative of
\varphi
and not the function itself.

The general case now follows easily: let
G
be a regular extension of
g
to
B
. It is clear from the definitions that
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\partial G}{\partial y_{i}}|_{A}
, and the latter function is regular on
B
from the preceding case.
∎

Now if
f:=\frac{p}{q},\;p,q\in\mathcal{R}(A)
is a rational function which is not necessarily regular on
A
then we naturally define its partial derivative to be equal to

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}q-p\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}}{q^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}(X)

It is clear that this definition does not depend on the representation
f=\frac{p}{q}
. Notice also that if
f
is regular at the point
a
then we can take
q
to be non-vanishing at
a
, so
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{a}
.

Let us summarise the main points of this construction:

•

Given a variety
X
and a regular system of parameters
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
at
a\in X
we define partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq k
which are mappings from
\mathbb{R}(X)
into itself. This definition does not depend on the embedding
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
or on any other choice we have made along the way. Actually it is not even important to highlight the point
a
, the derivatives depend solely on the system
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

Partial derivatives of functions regular at
a
stay regular at
a
. Since the definition does not depend on the point
a
this is also true for any other point at which the mapping
\varphi=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
is defined and its derivative is an isomorphism.

•

Hence if we take
A_{1}
to be a Zariski open set such that the derivative of
\varphi
is non-singular at every point of it, then partial derivatives of functions regular on
A_{1}
stay regular on
A_{1}
. In this case we say that
A_{1}
is regular for the system
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
. Equivalently we might state this condition as

\forall_{a^{\prime}\in A_{1}}(x_{1}-x_{1}(a^{\prime}),\dots,x_{k}-x_{k}(a^{\prime}))\text{ is a system of parameters of }\mathcal{R}(X)_{a^{\prime}}.

•

If
X
is a variety which is regular for some highlighted system of parameters
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
then we might sometimes say that
derivation is defined
on
X
. We will often restrict ourselves to this convenient setting in which globally regular functions stay regular under differentiation.

2.2
Definitions

We need to introduce some notation used frequently later in the paper:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a variety and
f\in\mathbb{R}(X)
a rational function. We define
\textnormal{dom}(f)
as the set
\{a\in X:f\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{a}\}
and
\textnormal{indet}(f):=X\backslash\textnormal{dom}(f)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a variety and let
f
be a real valued function defined on some subset
B
of
X
(usually
f
will be taken to be a rational function with
B=\textnormal{dom}(f)
). Let
A\subset X
be an arbitrary set. We say that
f
is locally bounded on
A
, or write
f\in L.B.(A)
, if for every
a\in A
there exists a Euclidean neighbourhood
U
of
a
in
X
such that
f
is bounded on
U\cap A\cap B
.

Example 2.5
.

With this definition
1/x
is locally bounded on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, although it is not locally bounded on
\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A\subset X
. For
f,g\in L.B.(A)
we define:

v_{f,A}(g):=\sup\{\alpha\geq 0:\frac{g}{|f|^{\alpha}}\in L.B.(A)\}

We understand that the domain of the fraction is precisely the set of points at which
f
and
g
are defined and
f
is non-zero.

Remark 2.6.1
.

Observe that if
f,g
and
A
are semialgebraic then we must have

\frac{g}{|f|^{v_{f,A}(g)}}\in L.B.(A)

Indeed, for suppose the contrary. Then by the Curve Selection Lemma we would find an arc
\varphi:(0,1)\rightarrow A\cap\textnormal{dom}(f)\cap\textnormal{dom}(g)
, with the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\varphi(t)
existing in
A
, such that
f\circ\varphi(t)\neq 0
for
t\in(0,1)
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{|g|}{|f|^{v_{f,A}(g)}}\circ\varphi(t)=\infty

Now, applying Łojasiewicz’s Inequality (
[
Bochnak
, Proposition 2.6.4]
) to
\frac{|f|^{v_{f,A}(g)}}{|g|}\circ\varphi
and
\frac{1}{|f|}\circ\varphi
we get that for
N>0
large enough we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{|f|^{v_{f,A}(g)N-1}}{|g|^{N}}\circ\varphi(t)=0.

This shows that for
\epsilon>0
small enough

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{|g|}{|f|^{v_{f,A}(g)-\epsilon}}\circ\varphi(t)=\infty

which contradicts the definition of
v_{f,A}(g)
.

Let now
X
be a variety, we define two following subrings of
\mathbb{R}(X)
:

Definition 2.7
.

\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
is defined to consist of all the rational functions which are locally bounded on
X
.

Definition 2.8
.

\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}(X)\subset\mathcal{R}^{0}(X)
is defined to consist of all the
0
-regulous functions which are locally Lipschitz on
X
.

Remark 2.8.1
.

The above definition is stated only for the sake of Theorem
S1
.

Observation 2.9
.

Let
X
be a variety regular for some system of parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
. Let
f\in\mathbb{R}(X)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}(X)
if and only if all the partial derivatives of
f
belong to
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
.

Proof.

Implication in the one direction is easy as every partial derivative of a differentiable Lipchitz function is bounded by its Lipschitz constant.

Assume now that the partial derivatives of
f
all belong to
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
. We firstly would like to show that the set
\textnormal{indet}(f)
is of codimension at least
2
. Suppose the contrary and take
Z
to be an irreducible component of the set of codimension
1
. Let
\mathcal{R}(X)_{Z}
be the local ring of the subvariety
Z
, i.e. the localisation of
\mathbb{R}(X)
at
\mathcal{I}(Z)
. It is known to be a discrete valuation ring, let
q
generate its maximal ideal. By our assumption
\textnormal{ord}_{q}(f)<0
so we can represent
f
as

f=\frac{p}{q^{\alpha}}

for some
\alpha>0
,
p\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{Z}^{\ast}
. Now notice that the derivative of
q
does not vanish at a generic point of
Z
, so
q
does not divide
\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}
for some
i
. Computing the derivative of
f
we get

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}q-\alpha p\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}}{q^{\alpha+1}}

As the numerator is not divisible by
q
we have
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\not\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{Z}
so the derivative diverges at a generic point of
Z
, a contradiction.

We now basically have to reapply the reasoning in the proof of
[
regular_after_blowingups
, Lemma 2.16]
. Choose a point
a\in\textnormal{indet}(f)
. We find a small neighbourhood
U
of
a
, diffeomorphically transformed into a convex set by
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
, and such that the derivative of
f
is bounded on
U\cap\textnormal{dom}(f)
. Since the indeterminacy locus of
f
is of codimension at least two, every two points of
U\cap\textnormal{dom}(f)
can be connected by a curve contained in the set of length slightly longer than the distance between. By the mean value theorem this implies that
f
is Lipschitz on
U\cap\textnormal{dom}(f)
, so it extends in a unique way to a Lipschitz function on
U
. As
a
was arbitrary the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
X
be a variety on which derivation is defined. Let
f\in\mathbb{R}(X)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{R}^{k}(X)
if and only if
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\dots\partial x_{i_{k}}}\in\mathcal{R}^{0}(X)
for every
1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n
.

Proof.

Implication in the one direction is trivial, if
f
is
k
-regulous then the partial derivatives of
f
of order
k
are rational continuous functions. In the other direction we proceed by induction on
k
. By Observation
2.9
and the inductive assumption we have
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}(X)\subset\mathcal{R}^{0}(X)
. Now we just need to repeat the proof of
[
regular_after_blowingups
, Theorem 2.12]
in the new slightly more general setting.
∎

We will now define the central concept of the paper, namely
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat
functions.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
k\geq 1
be a natural number and let
a
be a point of a variety
X
. We say that a fraction
\frac{p}{q}
with
p,q\in\mathcal{R}(X)_{a}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation at
a
if there exists a Zariski open neighbourhood
A
of
a
on which derivation is defined, such that
p,q\in\mathcal{R}(A)
and:

The function
\frac{p||dq||^{k}}{q^{k+1}}
approaches zero at all points of the set
\mathcal{Z}(q)\cap A
.

Obviously this condition is independent on the choice of the system of parameters on
A
. Notice that by Łojasiewicz’s inequality (
[
Bochnak
, Proposition 2.6.4]
) applied to
\frac{p||dq||^{k}}{q^{k+1}}
and
\frac{1}{q}
the condition may be equivalently stated as

v_{q,A}(p||dq||^{k})>k+1.

In a completely similar fashion we say that
\frac{p}{q}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat representation at
a
if
\frac{p||dq||}{q^{2}}\in L.B.(A)
(which is equivalent to
v_{q,A}(p||dq||)\geq 2
).

Definition 2.12
.

A rational function
f
is said to be locally
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat at
a
(resp. locally
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat at
a
) if it admits an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat (resp.
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat) representation as a fraction at
a
.

f
is said to be
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat (
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat) if it is locally
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat (
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat) at every point of
X
.

Definition 2.13
.

An
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function
f
is said to have a global representation if there exist two regular functions
p,q\in\mathcal{R}(X)
such that
\frac{p}{q}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation of
f
at every point of
X
.

It is natural to ask if every
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function admits a global representation. We are unaware whether the answer to this question is affirmative although in a moment we will prove something only slightly weaker. Before that we need to state a lemma a proof of which will be given at the beginning of the next section:

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\frac{p}{q}
be an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation on some Zariski open set
A
on which derivation is defined. Then
v_{q,A}(p)>1
.

Corollary 2.15
.

If
f
is locally
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat at
a\in X
then it admits an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation
f=\frac{p}{q}
at
a
with
p
and
q
both belonging to
\mathcal{R}(X)
.

Proof.

Take
f=\frac{p}{q}
to be any
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation of
f
on some neighbourhood
A
of
a
on which derivation is defined. Take
r\in\mathcal{R}(X)
to be such that
pr,qr\in\mathcal{R}(X)
and
\mathcal{Z}(r)\subset X\backslash A
. We claim that
\frac{pr}{qr}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation of
f
on
A
. A direct computation shows that:

\left|\frac{pr\left(\frac{\partial qr}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}}{(qr)^{k+1}}\right|=\left|\frac{p}{q}\left(\frac{\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}}{q}+\frac{\frac{\partial r}{\partial x_{i}}}{r}\right)^{k}\right|\leq 2^{k-1}\left|\frac{p\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}}{q^{k+1}}\right|+2^{k-1}\left|\frac{p\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}}{qr^{k}}\right|

where the first summand approaches zero on
\mathcal{Z}(q)\cap A
by the fact that
\frac{p}{q}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation and the second one does so by Lemma
2.14
.
∎

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
f
be a function locally
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat at
a\in X
. Then there exists
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)
with
g(a)\neq 0
such that
gf
is
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat and has a global representation.

Proof.

Let
\frac{p}{q}
be an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation of
f
on a neighbourhood
A
of
a
with
p,q\in\mathcal{R}(X)
. Let
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)
satisfy
\mathcal{Z}(g)=X\backslash A
. Choose any
a^{\prime}\in X\backslash A
and consider some local system of parameters
x_{1}.\dots,x_{n}
at
a^{\prime}
together with a regular neighbourhood
B
of
a^{\prime}
. The function
\frac{p||dq_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}||^{k}}{q^{k+1}}
is semialgebraic and it approaches
0
at every point of
A\cap B\cap\mathcal{Z}(q)
. This way by Łojasiewicz’s inequality (
[
Bochnak
, Proposition 2.6.4]
) we can find a large exponent
N
such that
g^{N}\frac{p||dq_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}||^{k}}{q^{k+1}}
approaches zero at every point of
B\backslash A
. In particular
\frac{pg^{N}}{q}
is an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation at every point of
B\cup A
. By Noetherianity of the Zariski topology we can find an exponent
N
working for all the points at once.
∎

Corollary 2.17
.

Every
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function is a finite sum of
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat functions admitting global representations.

Proof.

Let
f
be
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat. Consider the ideal

I=\{g\in\mathcal{R}(X):\text{ $fg$ is a sum of $\mathcal{R}^{k}$-flat functions with global representations}\}

By the last proposition
\mathcal{Z}(I)=\emptyset
, so the Weak Nullstellensatz finishes the proof.
∎

2.3
Plan of the paper

After these preparations we can begin the main course of the paper. Almost all the material here is devoted to the proof of Theorem
A
, the remaining results come more as byproducts of the reasoning. Our general approach somewhat resembles the approach of Kollár and Nowak presented in
[
real_and_p-adic
]
, up to the fact that dealing with higher classes of smoothness requires much more subtleties. The main idea is that we want to prove that Theorem
A
holds for functions which are
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat, and from that extrapolate the result to all
k
-regulous functions. To achieve this we follow the following plan:

In Section
3
we prove that every
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function is
k
-regulous. The proof requires us to study a certain interesting construction associating a set of valuations to a rational locally bounded function. We then derive an inequality regarding such valuations, from which the conclusion of this part follows.

In Section
4

3
Divisorial valuations

3.1
The valuation inequality

We begin by introducing the central object of study of this section:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
,
X_{\sigma}
be varieties and let
\sigma:X_{\sigma}\rightarrow X
be a proper, birational morphism. Let
D
be a non-singular irreducible codimension one subvariety of
X_{\sigma}
. The localisation
\mathcal{R}(X_{\sigma})_{D}
induces a discrete valuation on
\mathbb{R}(X_{\sigma})\cong\mathbb{R}(X)
, which is denoted by
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
. Every valuation on
\mathbb{R}(X)
constructed in such a way is called a
divisorial valuation
.

We now describe a certain scenario in which such valuations arise naturally.

Let
f\in\mathcal{R}(X)
be a simple normal crossing on
X
, we would like to describe the function
v_{f,X}(g)
as
g
ranges through
\mathcal{R}(X)
. Notice that a necessary condition for
\alpha
to be such that
\frac{g}{|f|^{\alpha}}\in L.B.(X)
is that the order of
g
at every irreducible component
D
of
\mathcal{Z}(f)
is at least
\alpha
times the order of
f
at the component, otherwise the fraction would diverge at a generic point of
D
. On the other hand, if the condition

\alpha\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f)\leq\textnormal{ord}_{D}(g)

is satisfied for every such
D
then analysing what happens locally at every point of
X
we see that indeed
\frac{g}{|f|^{\alpha}}\in L.B.(X)
. This allows us to characterise the function
v_{f,X}
using divisorial valuations:

v_{f,X}(g)=\min_{i}\frac{\textnormal{ord}_{D_{i}}(g)}{\textnormal{ord}_{D_{i}}(f)}

(1)

where the minimum is taken over all valuations induced by the irreducible components of
\mathcal{Z}(f)
.

Consider now a more general question of describing
v_{f,X}(g)
as
g
ranges through
\mathcal{R}(X)
and
f
is a non-zero rational locally bounded function on
X
. In this case by the resolution of singularities we can find a proper birational morphism
\sigma:X_{\sigma}\rightarrow X

such that
f\circ\sigma
is regular and a simple normal crossing on
X_{\sigma}
. Since
\sigma
is proper a short topological consideration shows that it induces an isomorphism between the rings
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
and
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X_{\sigma})
. Hence the equation (
1
) applies also here, this time the set of valuations ranges through the orders at the irreducible components of
\mathcal{Z}(f\circ\sigma)
. Let us emphasise this as an observation:

Observation 3.2
.

For a given
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)\backslash\{0\}
there exists a finite set of divisorial valuations
\textnormal{ord}_{D_{1}},\dots,\textnormal{ord}_{D_{k}}
such that the equation (
1
) holds for every
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)
.

As a trivial corollary we get
v_{f,X}(g)\in\mathbb{Q}
.

The valuations constructed this way are the Rees valuations associated to the ideal
(f\circ\sigma)
in the ring
\mathcal{R}(X_{\sigma})
(see
[
swanson-huneke
, Example 10.1.2]
). We cannot guarantee that the constructed set is minimal in the sense that after deleting any of them the equality fails for some
g\in\mathcal{R}(X)
, but we can always alter it.

Assume that we have deleted the unnecessary valuations. Then, by
[
swanson-huneke
, Theorem 10.1.6]
the set
\textnormal{ord}_{D_{1}},\dots,\textnormal{ord}_{D_{k}}
is uniquely determined by
f
; it does not depend in any way on the resolution of singularities we have used to find it.

Definition 3.3
.

The valuations
\textnormal{ord}_{D_{i}}
in the minimal decomposition (
1
) are called the divisorial valuations associated to
f
.

Let us now see a few examples:

Example 3.4
.

The function
x^{2}+y^{2}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
admits precisely one associated valuation
\textnormal{ord}_{(x,y)}
. In general functions of the form
x^{2a}+y^{2b}
admit precisely one associated monomial valuation with weights
x\equiv b,y\equiv a
(up to normalisation). An example of a function with two associated valuations is
(x^{2}+y^{2})(x^{2}+y^{4})
, its valuations come from the two factors. A less trivial example is the following:

(x^{2}+y^{2})(x^{2}+z^{2})\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

There are three monomial valuations associated to it, the orders at
(x,y),(x,z)
and at the point
(x,y,z)
.

It is natural to ask about the converse, i.e. whether every divisorial valuation arises as a valuation associated to some locally bounded
f
. It turns out that it does so:

Proposition 3.5
.

For every divisorial valuation
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
there exists
f\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
such that
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
is the only valuation associated to
f
, moreover it can be chosen so that
\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f)=2
and
f
is positive semi-definite.

Proof.

Take a variety
X_{\sigma}
with a proper birational morphism
\sigma:X_{\sigma}\rightarrow X
such that
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
is induced as the order at a non-singular divisor
D
of
X_{\sigma}
. Choose
h
to be the sum of squares of generators of the ideal
\mathcal{I}(D)
in
\mathcal{R}(X_{\sigma})
. Then we can take
f:=h\circ\sigma^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
. The equation

v_{f,X}(g)=v_{h,X_{\sigma}}(g\circ\sigma)=\frac{\textnormal{ord}_{D}(g)}{2}

becomes clear after analysing what happens locally at every point of
D
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
X
is regular for some system of parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{R}(X)\backslash\{0\}
and let
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
be a divisorial valuation with
\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f)>0
. Then

\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f)>\textnormal{ord}_{D}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}})
holds for some
i
.

Proof.

Take
g\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(X)
such that
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
is the only valuation associated to
g
. Define

c:=v_{g,X}(||df||)

It is clear that
c=\min_{i}v_{g,X}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}})
, so our goal is to show that
v_{g,X}(f)>c
. If
c=0
the conclusion is trivial, so we assume not.

Choose a smooth bounded semialgebraic curve
\xi:(0,1)\rightarrow\textnormal{dom}(g)
with bounded derivative, with the limit of
g\circ\xi
at zero being equal to zero. Then the limits of both
f\circ\xi
and
||df||\circ\xi
at zero must also be zero. We may restrict ourselves to a small subinterval
(0,\epsilon)
such that
||df||\circ\xi
is weakly increasing there. By the mean value theorem we get:

\displaystyle|f(\xi(t))|\leq Ct\sup_{0<t_{1}\leq t}||df||(\xi(t_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq Ct||df||(\xi(t))

\displaystyle\frac{|f|}{|g|^{c}}(\xi(t))\leq Ct\frac{||df||}{|g|^{c}}(\xi(t))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{t\to 0}0

Since the choice of the curve was arbitrary the Curve Selection Lemma implies that
v_{g,X}(f)>c
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

Let
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
be a divisorial valuation associated to

. By the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flatness we have

(k+1)\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q)<\min_{i}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(p\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}\right)=\textnormal{ord}_{D}(p)+\min_{i}k\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}\right)<\\

The main tool of the section is the following:

Theorem 3.7

(valuation inequality)
.

Suppose that
X
is regular for some system of parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{R}(X)
,
h\in\mathbb{R}(X)^{*}
and let
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
be a divisorial valuation associated to
f
. Then

\min_{i}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(h)\geq\min_{i}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f).

Before presenting the proof of the inequality we exhibit the important corollaries it brings about.

Corollary 3.8
.

Every
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat function
f
belongs to
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}(X)
.

Proof.

Choose a Zariski open subset
A\subset X
on which derivation is defined and where
f
admits an
\mathcal{R}^{\underline{1}}
-flat representation
f=\frac{p}{q}
. On
A
,
v_{q,A}
admits a decomposition into divisorial valuations associated to
q
. For every such valuation
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
, for every
i
Theorem
3.7
gives us

\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(p)\geq\min_{j}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q).

On the other hand by our assumption on
f

\min_{j}\textnormal{ord}_{D}(p)+\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\geq 2\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q)

so altogether

\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\geq\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q).

This implies that
\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}/q\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(A)
and hence

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}}{q}-\frac{p\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}}{q^{2}}\in\mathcal{R}^{\underline{0}}(A).

As
X
can be covered by such sets
A
the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Every
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function
f
is
k
-regulous.

Proof.

We work by induction on
k
. The case
k=1
is treated analogously to the preceding proof. Assume
k>1
and as before choose
A
to be regular for some system of parameters,
\frac{p}{q}
to be an
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat representation of
f
on
A
and let
\textnormal{ord}_{D}
be a valuation associated to
q|_{A}
.

Claim
.

Every partial derivative of
f
is

-regulous on
A
.

Proof.

We have

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}}{q}-\frac{p\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}}{q^{2}}

The second summand is
\mathcal{R}^{k-1}
-flat by the fact that
f
is
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat, so it is
k-1
-regulous by induction. To prove the same for the first one, first apply Theorem
3.7
to obtain for every
i

\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(p)\geq\min_{j}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\right)-\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q).

By the assumption on the
\mathcal{R}^{k}

\frac{p}{q}
we have

\textnormal{ord}_{D}(p)+k\min_{j}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\right)>(k+1)\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q).

The two equations together imply

\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}\right)+(k-1)\min_{j}\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{j}}\right)>k\textnormal{ord}_{D}(q).

As this is true for every valuation

associated with

is
\mathcal{R}^{k-1}
-flat. This proves the claim and the conclusion follows.

3.2
Proof of the valuation inequality

We now head towards the proof of Theorem
3.7
. We introduce the following notation:

Definition 3.10
.

If
\psi,\xi:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
then we write
\psi\preceq\xi
if and only if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|\psi|\leq C|\xi|
holds on some small subinterval
(0,\epsilon)
. Notice that whenever
\psi
and
\xi
are semialgebraic, they are comparable in this sense.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\psi_{0},\dots,\psi_{k}:(0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be semialgebraic functions. Then for all but finitely many
r\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\max(|\psi_{0}|,\dots,|\psi_{k}|)\preceq\psi_{0}+r\psi_{1}+r^{2}\psi_{2}+\dots+r^{k}\psi_{k}.

Proof.

The set
\{\psi_{0},\dots,\psi_{k}\}
must contain a maximal element in the order
\preceq
, say
\psi_{k_{0}}
. Consider

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\psi_{0}(t)}{\psi_{k_{0}}(t)}+r\frac{\psi_{1}(t)}{\psi_{k_{0}}(t)}+\dots+r^{k}\frac{\psi_{k}(t)}{\psi_{k_{0}}(t)}.

Since
\forall_{i}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\psi_{i}(t)/\psi_{k_{0}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
the expression above is a polynomial as a function of r, moreover its coefficient next to
r^{k_{0}}
is equal to 1 so it is non-zero. Therefore for all but finitely many
r
the limit is equal to a real number different than zero. In other words for every such
r

\psi_{k_{0}}\preceq\psi_{0}+r\psi_{1}+\dots+r^{k}\psi_{k}.

On the other hand once again because of the maximality of
\psi_{k_{0}}
we have

\max(|\psi_{0}|,\dots,|\psi_{k}|)\preceq\psi_{k_{0}}.

Combining the two equations finishes the proof.
∎

.

Suppose that

\textnormal{ord}_{D}\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}g\right)\geq\textnormal{ord}_{D}(f)v_{f,X}(hg)-\alpha.

Take

\mathcal{R}^{k}

This section is devoted to the proof of the fact that every
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat function on a subvariety extends to a sum of
\mathcal{R}^{k}
-flat functions on an ambient variety. We will need the following:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
be a codimension one subvariety of a variety
B
, and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be a regular system of parameters of
\mathbb{R}(B)_{a}
at a point
a\in A
satisfying
(x_{1})=\mathcal{I}(A)
. Suppose the entire variety
B
is regular for the system
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Then for every
g\in\mathcal{R}(A)
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
G_{M}\in\mathcal{R}(B)
satisfying

1.

g=G_{M}|_{A}

2.

\frac{\partial^{m}G_{M}}{\partial x_{1}^{m}}\in\mathcal{I}(A)
for any
1\leq m\leq M
.

Proof.

Firstly take
G_{0}
to be any regular extension of
g
to
B
, we will improve it inductively. Having defined
G_{M-1}

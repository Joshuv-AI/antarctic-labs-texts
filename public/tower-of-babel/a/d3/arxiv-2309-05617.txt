Hidden symmetries of generalised gravitational instantons
Bernardo Araneda

Abstract
For conformally Kähler Riemannian four-manifolds with a Killing field, we present a framework to solve the field equations for generalised gravitational instantons corresponding to conformal self-duality and to cosmological Einstein-Maxwell. After deriving generic identities for the curvature of such manifolds without assuming field equations, we obtain S ​ U ​ ( ∞ ) SU(\infty) Toda formulations for the Page-Pope, Plebański-Demiański, and Chen-Teo classes, we show how to solve the (modified) Toda equation, and we use this to find conformally self-dual and Einstein-Maxwell generalisations of these geometries.



1
Introduction

Gravitational instantons
[
30
,
27
,
26
]
are four-dimensional, complete, Ricci-flat Riemannian manifolds with sufficiently fast curvature decay. They are expected to give the dominant contributions to the path integral for Euclidean quantum gravity.
Particular cases are metrics with self-dual Riemann tensor, while more general cases correspond to generalisations of the Ricci-flat condition. These generalisations include the addition of a cosmological constant, solutions to Einstein-Maxwell theory, conformally self-dual geometries (i.e. metrics with self-dual Weyl tensor), Bach-flat metrics, etc. These geometries, which we will call
generalised gravitational instantons
here, are the main object of study in this paper.

The curvature decay condition means that the geometry is required to have at most quartic volume growth. This leads to a number of possibilities such as asymptotically flat (AF), asymptotically locally flat (ALF), asymptotically Euclidean (AE) or asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) metrics. In quantum gravity, these have a physical interpretation in terms of vacuum and finite-temperature states of the gravitational field
[
26
,
28
]
. In the Ricci-flat case, the only AE instanton is flat space, but the ALE class includes non-trivial examples such as the Eguchi-Hanson instanton. An ALF example is the Taub-NUT instanton, while the AF class contains for instance the Euclidean Schwarzschild and Kerr solutions, as well as the recently discovered Chen-Teo instanton
[
11
]
, which gives a counterexample to the Euclidean black hole uniqueness conjecture
[
26
]
. We mention that other types of asymptotic structures compatible with the required volume growth are also possible; these are termed ALG, ALH, ALG*, ALH*.

Under the hyper-Kähler assumption, a complete classification of gravitational instantons is now available
[
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
33
,
35
,
37
,
38
,
49
,
56
]
. Recall that the hyper-Kähler condition means that the metric is Kähler with respect to a 2-sphere of complex structures, and that, assuming that the manifold is simply connected, this is equivalent to (anti-)self-duality of the Riemann tensor. (In the non-simply connected case this equivalence is no longer true, see
[
63
]
.)
If one assumes only the Kähler condition, then Ricci-flatness implies (
locally
) hyper-Kähler. A less-restrictive assumption is the Hermitian condition. In fact, as noticed in
[
1
, Question 1.4]
, all known examples of Ricci-flat gravitational instantons are Hermitian. Using Bianchi identities, this implies that they are
conformally
Kähler, and have at least one Killing field. If, in addition, one also assumes the existence of a second (commuting) Killing field, then the recent Biquard-Gauduchon classification
[
7
,
8
]
proves that the only toric, Ricci-flat, Hermitian, ALF instantons are the Kerr, Chen-Teo, Taub-bolt and Taub-NUT metrics. While the Kerr and Taub-bolt/NUT metrics have non-Ricci-flat (cosmological and Einstein-Maxwell) versions, the corresponding generalisation of the Chen-Teo instanton is an open problem, and constitutes a major motivation for the current paper.

From
[
22
]
, a four-dimensional geometry is conformally Kähler if and only if it
has a solution to the 2-index twistor equation, or, in tensor terms, a conformal Killing-Yano 2-form. These objects have appeared in gravitational physics since at least the work of Walker and Penrose
[
62
]
on the geometric origin of Carter’s constant in the Kerr space-time. Many more geometries admitting these tensors have since been found, and, in a general relativity context, these Killing-like objects are now usually referred to as
hidden symmetries
. They also give origin to other “ordinary” symmetries (Killing vectors). We refer to
[
25
]
for a recent review on this topic.

Motivated by the above, in this work we study ‘generalised gravitational instantons with hidden symmetries’, that is, Riemannian, non-Ricci-flat 4-manifolds with a conformal Kähler structure and a Killing field. We will focus on the field equations corresponding to conformal self-duality and cosmological Einstein-Maxwell.
Our methods are based on the remarkable constructions developed by LeBrun
[
39
]
(scalar-flat Kähler metrics with symmetry), Tod
[
59
]
(one-sided type D Ricci-flat metrics), and Flaherty
[
24
]
(connections between the scalar-flat Kähler and Einstein-Maxwell systems).
Our main results are as follows:

•

We obtain, in section
2
, a number of identities for conformally Kähler metrics whose Ricci tensor is invariant under the complex structure (but no other assumptions), which give a compact formula for the Ricci form, and allow to construct generalised instantons in a variety of gravitational theories from solutions to a single scalar (
SU(\infty)
Toda) equation.

•

In section
3
we show that the Page-Pope ansatz
[
50
]
is generically ambi-Kähler without assuming any field equations, and we classify all conformally self-dual solutions. It follows that some recently considered
[
16
]
Bach-flat instantons are actually conformally Einstein. We also obtain the general cosmological Einstein-Maxwell solution.

•

In section
4
we obtain the
SU(\infty)
Toda formulation for the Plebański-Demiański ansatz, and we give a simple trick to solve the (modified) Toda equation. We use this to construct a conformally self-dual Plebański-Demiański space, which depends on 5 parameters and is not Einstein, so it is different from the standard self-dual limit of Plebański-Demiański.

•

In section
5
we obtain the
SU(\infty)
Toda formulation for the Chen-Teo ansatz, and we give a simple trick to solve the corresponding Toda equation. We construct a family of (non-Ricci-flat) conformally self-dual Chen-Teo geometries, as well as a family of Einstein-Maxwell Chen-Teo solutions.

We end this introduction with some further motivation and references. Non-Ricci-flat instantons are interesting in a number of situations in high-energy physics, for example: Einstein-Maxwell theory coincides with the bosonic sector of
N=2
supergravity in four dimensions; conformally self-dual and Bach-flat geometries arise in conformal gravity; and Hawking’s approach to space-time foam does not require the vacuum field equations

-i\partial\bar{\partial}f=\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}(J{\rm d}f)
holds for any smooth
f
. Putting then
f=\hat{u}
and using
J({\rm d}{x})=-{\rm d}{y}
,
J({\rm d}{y})={\rm d}{x}
,
J({\rm d}{z})=\xi
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left[-(\hat{u}_{xx}+\hat{u}_{yy}){\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y}-\hat{u}_{xz}{\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{y}+\hat{u}_{yz}{\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{x}+{\rm d}\hat{u}_{z}\wedge\xi+\hat{u}_{z}{\rm d}\xi\right].

Replacing this expression, together with (
2.27
) and (
2.10
), in equation (
2.26
):

\displaystyle\rho={}

\displaystyle\left[-\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{u}_{xx}+\hat{u}_{yy})-\tfrac{(zu_{z}-1)}{z^{2}}e^{u}\right]{\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y}-\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{u}_{xz}{\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{y}+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{u}_{yz}{\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\left[\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm d}\hat{u}_{z}+\tfrac{(zu_{z}-1)}{z^{2}}{\rm d}{z}-\tfrac{2}{z^{2}}{\rm d}{z}\right]\wedge\xi+\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{u}_{z}+\tfrac{1}{z}\right){\rm d}\xi.

Using the relation (
2.12
) between
\hat{u}
and
u
, after some tedious computations we arrive at the unenlightening expression

\displaystyle\rho={}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[u_{xx}+u_{yy}+\tfrac{2(zu_{z}-1)}{z^{2}}e^{u}+\tfrac{2z}{W}(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1)\partial_{z}(\tfrac{We^{u}}{z^{2}})\right]{\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\left[u_{yz}-\tfrac{2}{zW}(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1)W_{y}\right]({\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{x}+{\rm d}{y}\wedge{\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\left[u_{xz}-\tfrac{2}{zW}(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1)W_{x}\right](-{\rm d}{z}\wedge{\rm d}{y}+{\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}\xi)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\left[\tfrac{1}{z^{2}}(z^{2}u_{zz}+2zu_{z}-2)-\tfrac{2}{zW}(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1)W_{z}\right]{\rm d}{z}\wedge\xi.

Now, defining
W_{0}
as in (
2.18
), we have the identities

\displaystyle u_{yz}-\tfrac{2}{zW}\left(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1\right)W_{y}={}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2W}{z^{2}}\partial_{y}\left(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W}\right),

\displaystyle u_{xz}-\tfrac{2}{zW}\left(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1\right)W_{x}={}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2W}{z^{2}}\partial_{x}\left(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W}\right),

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{z^{2}}(z^{2}u_{zz}+2zu_{z}-2)-\tfrac{2}{zW}(\tfrac{zu_{z}}{2}-1)W_{z}={}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2W}{z^{2}}\partial_{z}\left(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W}\right),

which lead to

\displaystyle\rho={}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[u_{xx}+u_{yy}+\tfrac{2e^{u}}{z^{2}}(1-\tfrac{W_{0}}{W}W_{z}-W_{0}u_{z})\right]{\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{W}{z^{2}}{\rm d}{z}\wedge({\rm d}{x}\>\partial_{y}-{\rm d}{y}\>\partial_{x})(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W})-\tfrac{W}{z^{2}}{\rm d}(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W})\wedge\xi.

Defining the operator
\tilde{*}{\rm d}
as in (
2.19
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}{z}\wedge({\rm d}{x}\>\partial_{y}-{\rm d}{y}\>\partial_{x})(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W})=\tilde{*}{\rm d}(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W})-e^{u}\partial_{z}(\tfrac{W_{0}}{W}){\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y},

which then leads to our final formula (
2.17
). Having shown this, the proof of (
2.20
) requires only a few more tedious but straightforward computations, so we will omit them.
∎

In addition to the Ricci scalar (
2.16
) and the Ricci form (
2.17
), we will also need expressions for the ASD Weyl tensor. Recalling that the only non-trivial component is
\Psi_{2}
, and using (
2.6
), (
2.15
) and (
2.18
), a short calculation gives

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}=-\frac{1}{z^{3}}\frac{W_{0}}{W}+\frac{R}{12}.

(2.28)

In particular, notice that in the Ricci-flat and Einstein cases, we recover a well-known relation between
\Psi_{2}
and the conformal factor:
\Psi_{2}\propto\Omega^{3}
(recall
\Omega=z^{-1}
).

Finally, we also notice that a geometry may be conformally Kähler w.r.t.
both
ASD and SD orientations: this is called an ambi-Kähler structure
[
4
]
. In this case we have two integrable complex structures
(J_{\pm})^{a}{}_{b}
and two Kähler metrics
\hat{g}^{\pm}_{ab}=\Omega^{2}_{\pm}g_{ab}
. As in (
2.3
), we now have

\displaystyle\xi^{\pm}_{b}=(J_{\pm})^{a}{}_{b}\partial_{a}\Omega^{-1}_{\pm}.

(2.29)

If at least one of
\xi^{a}_{\pm}
is a Killing vector, then all of the previous results apply w.r.t. the corresponding orientation
\pm
. If both
\xi^{a}_{\pm}
are Killing, then we will have two Toda formulations, one for each orientation: the corresponding Toda variables
(u_{\pm},W_{\pm},z_{\pm},x_{\pm},y_{\pm})
are the analogue of (
2.11
),

\displaystyle z_{\pm}=\Omega_{\pm}^{-1},\qquad W_{\pm}^{-1}=g_{ab}\xi^{a}_{\pm}\xi^{b}_{\pm},\qquad e^{u_{\pm}/2}({\rm d}{x}_{\pm}+{\rm i}{\rm d}{y}_{\pm})=2\,\xi_{\pm}\,\lrcorner\,(\ell\wedge m^{\pm}),

(2.30)

where in the last equality we defined
m^{+}\equiv m
,
m^{-}\equiv\bar{m}
. Analogously to (
2.18
), we also put
W^{\pm}_{0}=z_{\pm}(1-\frac{1}{2}z_{\pm}\partial_{z_{\pm}}u_{\pm})
. The Weyl tensor is now type
D\otimes D
. The only non-trivial components are
\Psi_{2}^{-}\equiv\Psi_{2}
and
\Psi_{2}^{+}\equiv\tilde{\Psi}_{2}
, which can be computed as

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}^{\pm}=\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\frac{\hat{R}_{\pm}}{12}=-\frac{1}{z^{3}_{\pm}}\frac{W^{\pm}_{0}}{W_{\pm}}+\frac{R}{12},

(2.31)

where
\hat{R}_{\pm}
are the Ricci scalars of the Kähler metrics
\hat{g}^{\pm}_{ab}
. (Recall that (
2.31
) is valid regardless of whether (
2.29
) are Killing or not.)

2.3
Examples

Spherical symmetry.

Consider the ansatz

\displaystyle g=f(r){\rm d}\tau^{2}+\frac{{\rm d}{r}^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi^{2}),

(2.32)

where
f
is an arbitrary smooth function of
r
. Choose the coframe
\beta^{0}=f^{1/2}{\rm d}\tau
,
\beta^{1}=f^{-1/2}{\rm d}{r}
,
\beta^{2}=r{\rm d}\theta
,
\beta^{3}=r\sin\theta{\rm d}\varphi
, define the fundamental 2-forms
\kappa^{\pm}=\beta^{0}\wedge\beta^{1}\mp\beta^{2}\wedge\beta^{3}
(
\kappa^{+}
is SD and
\kappa^{-}
is ASD), and the associated almost-complex structures
(J_{\pm})^{a}{}_{b}=\kappa^{\pm}_{bc}g^{ca}
. The type-
(1,0)
eigenspaces of
J_{\pm}
are generated by
\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{0}+{\rm i}\beta^{1})
,
m^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{2}\mp{\rm i}\beta^{3})
. Putting
a^{0}_{\pm}=f^{-1/2}
,
b^{0}_{\pm}=0
, and
a^{1}_{\pm}=0
,
b^{1}_{\pm}=(r\sin\theta)^{-1}
, we see that the type
(1,0)
-forms
a^{\alpha}_{\pm}\ell+b^{\alpha}_{\pm}m^{\pm}
(
\alpha=0,1
) are closed, so both
J_{+}
and
J_{-}
are integrable. Furthermore, if
\Omega_{\pm}\equiv r^{-1}
then it is straightforward to see that
{\rm d}[\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\kappa^{\pm}]=0
, so the geometry (
2.32
) is ambi-Kähler. Finally, computing the vector fields (
2.29
), we get
\xi^{a}_{\pm}\partial_{a}=\partial_{\tau}
, which is Killing. Thus, regardless of the form of the arbitrary function
f(r)
, the geometry is conformally Kähler (in fact ambi-Kähler) with a
J
-invariant Ricci tensor. We then compute the variables (
2.30
):

\displaystyle z_{\pm}=r,\qquad W_{\pm}^{-1}=f,\qquad e^{u_{\pm}}=fr^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,\qquad{\rm d}{x_{\pm}}=\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{\sin\theta},\qquad{\rm d}{y_{\pm}}=\mp{\rm d}\varphi.

(2.33)

Using the formulas of previous sections, a short calculation gives

\displaystyle R=\frac{2-(r^{2}f)^{\prime\prime}}{r^{2}},\qquad\Psi^{\pm}_{2}=\frac{\{2-r^{2}[r^{2}(f/r^{2})^{\prime}]^{\prime}\}}{12r^{2}},\qquad\frac{W^{\pm}_{0}}{W_{\pm}}=-\frac{r^{2}f^{\prime}}{2},

(2.34)

where a prime
′
represents a derivative w.r.t.
r
.

The cosmological Einstein-Maxwell equations are
R=4\lambda
, which gives

\displaystyle f(r)=1+\frac{a_{1}}{r}+\frac{a_{2}}{r^{2}}-\frac{\lambda}{3}r^{2}

(2.35)

for some constants
a_{1},a_{2}
. The Weyl scalars
\Psi^{\pm}_{2}
and the two pieces
F^{\pm}
of the Maxwell field are:

\displaystyle\Psi^{\pm}_{2}=-\frac{a_{1}}{2r^{3}}-\frac{a_{2}}{r^{4}},\qquad F^{\pm}=(-a_{2}/4)^{\frac{1\pm 1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}{\rm d}\tau\wedge{\rm d}{r}\mp\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta\wedge{\rm d}\varphi\right).

(2.36)

Setting
a_{1}\equiv-2M
,
a_{2}\equiv Q^{2}
, we recognise the Euclidean Reissner-Nördstrom-(A)dS solution.

We can alternatively look for
f(r)
such that the ansatz (
2.32
) is conformally self-dual. Recall that this is equivalent to
\Psi^{-}_{2}\equiv\Psi_{2}=0
. Since
\Psi^{-}_{2}=\Psi^{+}_{2}
, we see that
\Psi_{2}=0
gives
C_{abcd}\equiv 0
, so the self-dual solution to the ansatz (
2.32
) is conformally flat. The condition
\Psi^{\pm}_{2}=0
gives
f(r)=1+b_{1}r+b_{2}r^{2}
for arbitrary constants
b_{1},b_{2}
. The Ricci scalar and trace-free Ricci form are

\displaystyle R=-6\left(\frac{b_{1}}{r}+2b_{2}\right),\qquad\rho^{\pm}-\frac{R}{4}\kappa^{\pm}=\frac{2b_{1}}{r}\left({\rm d}\tau\wedge{\rm d}{r}\pm r^{2}\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta\wedge{\rm d}\varphi\right).

(2.37)

We see that the geometry is Einstein iff
b_{1}=0
, in which case it reduces to Euclidean (anti-)de Sitter space with cosmological constant
-3b_{2}
(which is
S^{4}
if
b_{2}<0
).

The Kerr-Newman ansatz.

This example allows us to illustrate a trick to solve the Toda equation, that will be key for later sections. Consider the ansatz

\displaystyle g=\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}({\rm d}\tau-a\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi)^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}[a{\rm d}\tau+(r^{2}-a^{2}){\rm d}\varphi]^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}{\rm d}{r}^{2}+\Sigma{\rm d}\theta^{2},

(2.38)

where
a
is a real constant,
\Sigma=r^{2}-a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
, and
\Delta=\Delta(r)
is an arbitrary function of
r
. As in the previous example, we start by choosing a coframe:
\beta^{0}=(\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma})^{1/2}({\rm d}\tau-a\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi)
,
\beta^{1}=(\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta})^{1/2}{\rm d}{r}
,
\beta^{2}=\sqrt{\Sigma}{\rm d}\theta
,
\beta^{3}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\Sigma}}[a{\rm d}\tau+(r^{2}-a^{2}){\rm d}\varphi]
; we define
\kappa^{\pm}=\beta^{0}\wedge\beta^{1}\mp\beta^{2}\wedge\beta^{3}
and the almost-complex structures
(J_{\pm})^{a}{}_{b}=\kappa^{\pm}_{bc}g^{ca}
. Type-
(1,0)
forms for
J_{\pm}
are spanned by
\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{0}+{\rm i}\beta^{1})
and
m^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{2}\mp{\rm i}\beta^{3})
. Putting
a^{0}_{\pm}=\frac{(r^{2}-a^{2})}{\sqrt{\Delta\Sigma}}
,
b^{0}_{\pm}=\frac{\pm{\rm i}a\sin\theta}{\sqrt{\Sigma}}
, and
a^{1}_{\pm}=\frac{\pm{\rm i}a}{\sqrt{\Delta\Sigma}}
,
b^{1}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta\sqrt{\Sigma}}
, we see that
a^{\alpha}_{\pm}\ell+b^{\alpha}_{\pm}m^{\pm}
(
\alpha=0,1
) are closed, so
J_{+}
and
J_{-}
are integrable. Defining
\Omega_{\pm}=(r\mp a\cos\theta)^{-1}
, a short calculation shows that
{\rm d}[\Omega^{2}_{\pm}\kappa^{\pm}]=0
, so the metric (
2.38
) is ambi-Kähler. The vector fields (
2.29
) can be computed to be
\xi^{a}_{\pm}\partial_{a}=\partial_{\tau}
, so they are Killing. Therefore, independently of the form of
\Delta(r)
, the metric (
2.38
) is conformally Kähler (ambi-Kähler) with a
J
-invariant Ricci tensor. We can then compute the variables (
2.30
):

\displaystyle z_{\pm}=r\mp a\cos\theta,\quad W_{\pm}=\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta+a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta},\quad e^{u_{\pm}}=\Delta\sin^{2}\theta,\quad{\rm d}{x}_{\pm}=\frac{\mp a{\rm d}{r}}{\Delta}+\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{\sin\theta},\quad{\rm d}{y_{\pm}}=\mp{\rm d}\varphi.

(2.39)

For concreteness let us focus on
z_{-},x_{-},y_{-}
, etc., and let us omit the subscript
-
.
The Einstein-Maxwell equations with
\lambda=0
reduce to the
SU(\infty)
Toda equation with symmetry:
u_{xx}+(e^{u})_{zz}=0
. From (
2.39
) we find the vector fields
\partial_{x},\partial_{z}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}=\tfrac{\sin\theta\Delta}{\Delta+a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\left(a\sin\theta\partial_{r}+\partial_{\theta}\right),\qquad\partial_{z}=\tfrac{1}{\Delta+a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\left(\Delta\partial_{r}-a\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}\right).

(2.40)

One can check that
u_{xx}+(e^{u})_{zz}=0
then becomes a quite complicated differential equation: we do not seem to gain anything in passing from
(x,z)
to
(r,\theta)
. However, we can do the following trick: introduce an auxiliary variable
\sigma
by

\displaystyle u_{x}=\sigma_{z},\qquad(e^{u})_{z}=-\sigma_{x}.

(2.41)

Using (
2.40
), these equations lead respectively to:

\displaystyle a\sin^{2}\theta\dot{\Delta}+2\cos\theta\Delta={}

\displaystyle\Delta\>\partial_{r}\sigma-a\sin\theta\>\partial_{\theta}\sigma,

(2.42a)

\displaystyle-\sin\theta\dot{\Delta}+2a\sin\theta\cos\theta={}

\displaystyle a\sin\theta\>\partial_{r}\sigma+\partial_{\theta}\sigma,

(2.42b)

where
\dot{\Delta}=\frac{{\rm d}\Delta}{{\rm d}{r}}
. Now, from (
2.42b
) we get an expression for
\partial_{r}\sigma
, and we replace this in (
2.42a
). The resulting equation relates
\dot{\Delta}
and
\partial_{\theta}\sigma
. We then replace this new expression for
\partial_{\theta}\sigma
back in (
2.42b
). After these manipulations, we get the following system:

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\sigma=-\sin\theta\dot{\Delta},\qquad\partial_{r}\sigma=2\cos\theta.

(2.43)

Using now the identity
\partial_{r}\partial_{\theta}\sigma=\partial_{\theta}\partial_{r}\sigma
, we immediately get:

\displaystyle\ddot{\Delta}=2\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\Delta=r^{2}+c_{1}r+c_{2},

(2.44)

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}
. The metric is automatically Einstein-Maxwell, but to interpret
c_{1},c_{2}
, we compute the rest of the curvature (recall from (
2.8
) that the Ricci form is
\rho=4\Omega^{2}F^{+}
):

\displaystyle\Psi^{\pm}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{1}/2}{(r\mp a\cos\theta)^{3}}-\frac{(a^{2}+c_{2})}{(r\mp a\cos\theta)^{3}(r\pm a\cos\theta)},

(2.45)

\displaystyle F^{\pm}={}

\displaystyle\frac{[-\frac{1}{4}(a^{2}+c_{2})]^{\frac{1\pm 1}{2}}}{(r\mp a\cos\theta)^{2}}\left[{\rm d}\tau\wedge{\rm d}(r\mp a\cos\theta)-\sin\theta{\rm d}\varphi\wedge(a\sin\theta{\rm d}{r}\mp(r^{2}-a^{2}){\rm d}\theta)\right].

(2.46)

Setting
c_{1}\equiv-2M
,
a^{2}+c_{2}\equiv Q^{2}
, we recognise the Euclidean Kerr-Newman solution.

3
The Page-Pope class

3.1
Preliminaries

Consider a Riemann surface
\Sigma
with a Riemannian metric
g_{\Sigma}=2h\>{\rm d}\zeta{\rm d}\bar{\zeta}
, where
\zeta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x+{\rm i}y)
is a holomorphic coordinate and
h
is a real positive function. Let
\kappa_{\Sigma}={\rm i}h\>{\rm d}\zeta\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\zeta}
be the Kähler form. Since
{\rm d}\kappa_{\Sigma}=0
, there is, locally, a 1-form
A
such that
\kappa_{\Sigma}={\rm d}A
and a Kähler potential
K_{\Sigma}
with
h=\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{\bar{\zeta}}K_{\Sigma}
. We now define a manifold
M
as the total space of a fibre bundle over
\Sigma
with 2-dimensional fibers parametrised by
r,\psi
, and we introduce a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
by

\displaystyle g=F(r){\rm d}{r}^{2}+G(r)({\rm d}\psi+A)^{2}+H(r)g_{\Sigma}

(3.1)

where
F,G,H
are arbitrary (non-negative) functions of
r
. Note that, by redefining the coordinate
r
, the three functions
F,G,H
can be reduced to two. For the moment we will focus on the form (
3.1
), but we will later make use of this freedom.

The class of metrics (
3.1
) includes geometries such as Fubini-Study, Eguchi-Hanson, Taub-NUT, Kähler surfaces of Calabi type (cf.
[
3
,
48
]
), particular cases of the Bianchi IX class, etc. It is the restriction to four dimensions of the geometries considered by Page and Pope in
[
50
]
. In
[
50
]
, the conditions on the functions
F,G,H
so that the metric (
3.1
) is Einstein are determined, and they find that, under the Einstein assumption, the metric is conformal to two different Kähler metrics. We will first show that this ambi-Kähler structure is actually independent of the form of
F,G,H
, and so it is independent of field equations; then we will use this result to study generalised instantons.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any functions
F,G,H
, the class of metrics (
3.1
) is (locally) ambi-Kähler.

Proof.

Write the metric on the Riemann surface as
g_{\Sigma}=h({\rm d}{x}^{2}+{\rm d}{y}^{2})
, and choose the coframe
\beta^{0}=\sqrt{G}({\rm d}\psi+A)
,
\beta^{1}=\sqrt{F}{\rm d}{r}
,
\beta^{2}=\sqrt{Hh}\>{\rm d}{x}
,
\beta^{3}=\sqrt{Hh}\>{\rm d}{y}
. We define the fundamental 2-forms (with opposite orientation)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\pm}=\beta^{0}\wedge\beta^{1}\mp\beta^{2}\wedge\beta^{3}=\sqrt{FG}({\rm d}\psi+A)\wedge{\rm d}{r}\mp H\kappa_{\Sigma},

(3.2)

and the associated almost-complex structures
(J_{\pm})^{a}{}_{b}=\kappa^{\pm}_{bc}g^{ca}
. Type-
(1,0)
forms for
J_{\pm}
are
\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{0}+{\rm i}\beta^{1})
,
m^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{2}\mp{\rm i}\beta^{3})
. Let
a^{0}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}
,
b^{0}_{\pm}=\frac{\mp{\rm i}}{\sqrt{2Hh}}\partial_{\zeta}K_{\Sigma}
,
a^{1}_{\pm}=0
,
b^{1}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Hh}}
. Then a short calculation shows that the type-
(1,0)
forms
a^{\alpha}_{\pm}\ell+b^{\alpha}_{\pm}m^{\pm}
(
\alpha=0,1
) are closed, so
J_{+}
and
J_{-}
are both integrable. Furthermore, using (
3.2
) we find that regardless of the form of
F,G,H
we always have

\displaystyle{\rm d}[\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\kappa^{\pm}]=0,\qquad\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\equiv\frac{c_{\pm}}{H}\exp\left[\pm\>\int\frac{\sqrt{FG}}{H}{\rm d}{r}\right],

(3.3)

where
c_{\pm}
are arbitrary constants. Thus (
3.1
) is always ambi-Kähler.
∎

The vector fields (
2.29
) are

\displaystyle\xi^{a}_{\pm}\partial_{a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{FG}}\frac{{\rm d}(\Omega^{-1}_{\pm})}{{\rm d}{r}}\partial_{\psi}.

(3.4)

Since
\partial_{\psi}
is a Killing vector of (
3.1
), we see that (
3.4
) are in general not Killing. Requiring (
3.4
) to be Killing imposes restrictions on
\Omega_{\pm}
, which means restrictions on
F,G,H
.

3.2
Conformally self-dual solutions

Define

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\pm}:=\Omega^{2}_{\pm}F,\qquad\hat{G}_{\pm}:=\Omega^{2}_{\pm}G,\qquad\hat{H}_{\pm}:=\Omega^{2}_{\pm}H.

(3.5)

Using (
3.3
), we see that
{\rm d}{\hat{H}_{\pm}}=\pm\sqrt{\hat{F}_{\pm}\hat{G}_{\pm}}{\rm d}{r}
. Thus, if we further define

\displaystyle\hat{z}_{\pm}:=-\hat{H}_{\pm},\qquad\hat{W}_{\pm}:=\hat{G}_{\pm}^{-1},\qquad e^{\hat{u}_{\pm}}:=\hat{G}_{\pm}\hat{H}_{\pm}h,

(3.6)

then the Kähler metrics
\hat{g}^{\pm}=\Omega_{\pm}^{2}g
and Kähler forms
\hat{\kappa}^{\pm}=\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\kappa
become

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{\pm}={}

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{\pm}^{-1}({\rm d}\psi+A)^{2}+\hat{W}_{\pm}[{\rm d}\hat{z}_{\pm}^{2}+e^{\hat{u}_{\pm}}({\rm d}{x}^{2}+{\rm d}{y}^{2})],

(3.7)

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}^{\pm}={}

\displaystyle\mp[({\rm d}\psi+A)\wedge{\rm d}\hat{z}_{\pm}+\hat{W}_{\pm}e^{\hat{u}_{\pm}}{\rm d}{x}\wedge{\rm d}{y}].

(3.8)

A straightforward calculation using (
2.15
) gives

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\hat{H}_{\pm}}\left[R_{\Sigma}+\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}\hat{z}_{\pm}^{2}}(\hat{G}_{\pm}\hat{z}_{\pm})\right],\qquad R_{\Sigma}:=-h^{-1}(\partial^{2}_{x}+\partial^{2}_{y})\log h.

(3.9)

The conformal (A)SD equations then reduce to

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}\hat{z}_{\pm}^{2}}(\hat{G}_{\pm}\hat{z}_{\pm})=-R_{\Sigma}.

(3.10)

The left side is a function of
\hat{z}_{\pm}
only, while the right side is a function of
(x,y)
only. Thus, (
3.10
) demands
R_{\Sigma}
to be constant. Assuming
\Sigma
to be simply connected, this implies that
g_{\Sigma}
is isometric to the standard metric of either the 2-sphere (
R_{\Sigma}>0
), the Euclidean 2-plane (
R_{\Sigma}=0
), or the hyperbolic plane (
R_{\Sigma}<0
). The solution to (
3.10
) is
\hat{G}_{\pm}\hat{z}_{\pm}=-\tfrac{R_{\Sigma}}{2}\hat{z}^{2}_{\pm}+b_{\pm}\hat{z}_{\pm}-a_{\pm}
, so

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{\pm}=-\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2}\hat{z}_{\pm}+b_{\pm}-\frac{a_{\pm}}{\hat{z}_{\pm}},

(3.11)

where
a_{\pm},b_{\pm}
are arbitrary constants. Recalling
\hat{z}_{\pm}=-\hat{H}_{\pm}
, expressing the above equation in terms of
G,H,\Omega_{\pm}
, and summarising:

Theorem 3.2
.

The metric (
3.1
) is a solution to the conformally (A)SD equations
*C_{abcd}=\mp C_{abcd}
iff
g_{\Sigma}
has constant curvature
R_{\Sigma}
and the functions
F,G,H
satisfy

\displaystyle G=\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2}H+\frac{b_{\pm}}{\Omega_{\pm}^{2}}+\frac{a_{\pm}}{\Omega_{\pm}^{4}H}.

(3.12)

where
a_{\pm},b_{\pm}
are arbitrary constants and
\Omega_{\pm}
are defined in (
3.3

Remark 3.3

4.1
SU(\infty)
Toda formulation

The Plebański-Demiański ansatz

\displaystyle g=\frac{1}{(p-q)^{2}}\left[-Q\frac{({\rm d}\tau-p^{2}{\rm d}\phi)^{2}}{(1-p^{2}q^{2})}+P\frac{({\rm d}\phi-q^{2}{\rm d}\tau)^{2}}{(1-p^{2}q^{2})}+(1-p^{2}q^{2})\left(\frac{{\rm d}{p}^{2}}{P}-\frac{{\rm d}{q}^{2}}{Q}\right)\right],

(4.1)

P
and

Consider the following orthonormal coframe:

\displaystyle\beta^{0}={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(p-q)}\sqrt{\tfrac{-Q}{1-p^{2}q^{2}}}({\rm d}\tau-p^{2}{\rm d}\phi),

\displaystyle\beta^{1}=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(p-q)}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-p^{2}q^{2}}{-Q}}{\rm d}{q},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\beta^{2}={}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(p-q)}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-p^{2}q^{2}}{P}}{\rm d}{p},

\displaystyle\beta^{3}=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{(p-q)}\sqrt{\tfrac{P}{1-p^{2}q^{2}}}({\rm d}\phi-q^{2}{\rm d}\tau).

Defining the 2-forms

\displaystyle\kappa^{\pm}=\beta^{0}\wedge\beta^{1}\mp\beta^{2}\wedge\beta^{3}=\frac{({\rm d}\tau-p^{2}{\rm d}\phi)\wedge{\rm d}{q}\mp{\rm d}{p}\wedge({\rm d}\phi-q^{2}{\rm d}\tau)}{(p-q)^{2}}

(4.3)

(
\kappa^{+}
is SD and
\kappa^{-}
is ASD), the tensor fields

(1,0)

\displaystyle a^{0}_{\pm}:=\tfrac{(p-q)}{\sqrt{-Q(1-p^{2}q^{2})}},\quad b^{0}_{\pm}:=\pm{\rm i}\tfrac{(p-q)p^{2}}{\sqrt{P(1-p^{2}q^{2})}},\quad a^{1}_{\pm}:={\rm i}\tfrac{(p-q)q^{2}}{\sqrt{-Q(1-p^{2}q^{2})}},\quad b^{1}_{\pm}:=\mp\tfrac{(p-q)}{\sqrt{P(1-p^{2}q^{2})}},

a straightforward calculation shows that the 1-forms

\displaystyle\omega^{0}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\rm d}\tau-{\rm i}\frac{{\rm d}{q}}{Q}\pm{\rm i}\frac{p^{2}{\rm d}{p}}{P}\right),\qquad\omega^{1}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\rm i}{\rm d}\phi+\frac{q^{2}{\rm d}{q}}{Q}\mp\frac{{\rm d}{p}}{P}\right),

so
{\rm d}\omega^{\alpha}_{\pm}=0

are type-(1,0) forms for

J_{\pm}
are integrable

2

Note that from (

.
. Additionally, we find

\displaystyle{\rm d}[\Omega_{\pm}^{2}\kappa^{\pm}]=0,\qquad\Omega_{\pm}=\frac{p-q}{1\pm pq},

thus, the class of metrics (

\displaystyle\xi^{a}_{\pm}\partial_{a}=\partial_{\tau}\mp\partial_{\phi},

\displaystyle z_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm pq}{p-q},\qquad W_{\pm}^{-1}=\frac{(1\pm q^{2})^{2}P-(1\pm p^{2})^{2}Q}{(p-q)^{2}(1-p^{2}q^{2})},\qquad e^{u_{\pm}}=\frac{-PQ}{(p-q)^{4}},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle d}{x}_{\pm}=\frac{(1\pm p^{2})}{P}{\rm d}{p}-\frac{(1\pm q^{2})}{Q}{\rm d}{q},\qquad y_{\pm}=-\tau\mp\phi.

4.2
A conformally self-dual Plebański-Demiański space

Theorem 4.1
.

The metric (

if and only if the functions

and

\displaystyle P={}

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+a_{3}p^{3}+a_{4}p^{4},

(4.9)

\displaystyle Q={}

\displaystyle a_{4}+a_{3}q+a_{2}q^{2}+a_{1}q^{3}+a_{0}q^{4},

(ii)

Hyper-Kähler (i.e. Ricci-flat) iff

(iii)

Remark 4.2
.

We stress that the conformally SD solution (

above (see the next subsection). The solution (

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Recall that the conformally SD equation
*C_{abcd}=C_{abcd}
is equivalent to
\hat{R}_{-}=0
, where
\hat{R}_{-}
is given by (
2.15
). For notational convenience, let us denote

,

,

,

is Killing, the ansatz (

) satisfies
*C_{abcd}=C_{abcd}
if and only if

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{xx}+(e^{\hat{u}})_{\hat{z}\hat{z}}=0,

(4.10)

where
\hat{u}=u-4\log{z}
and
\hat{z}=-\frac{1}{z}

4.10
) in terms of the variables

(using (
4.8

\hat{\sigma}

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{x}=\hat{\sigma}_{\hat{z}},\qquad(e^{\hat{u}})_{\hat{z}}=-\hat{\sigma}_{x}.

(4.11)

The vector fields

can be computed from (

\displaystyle\partial_{x}=\frac{PQ}{F}\left[(1-p^{2})\partial_{p}+(1-q^{2})\partial_{q}\right],\qquad\partial_{z}=\frac{(p-q)^{2}}{F}\left[(1-q^{2})P\partial_{p}+(1-p^{2})Q\partial_{q}\right],

\displaystyle\frac{(1-p^{2})Q\dot{P}}{(1-pq)^{2}}+\frac{(1-q^{2})P\dot{Q}}{(1-pq)^{2}}+\frac{4(p+q)PQ}{(1-pq)^{2}}={}

\displaystyle(1-q^{2})P\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{p}}+(1-p^{2})Q\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{q}},

\displaystyle\frac{(1-q^{2})\dot{P}}{(1-pq)^{2}}+\frac{(1-p^{2})\dot{Q}}{(1-pq)^{2}}+\frac{4q(1-q^{2})P}{(1-pq)^{3}}+\frac{4p(1-p^{2})Q}{(1-pq)^{3}}={}

\displaystyle(1-pq)^{2}\ddot{Q}+6p(1-pq)\dot{Q}+12p^{2}Q=(1-pq)^{2}\ddot{P}+6q(1-pq)\dot{P}+12q^{2}P.

\displaystyle\tfrac{(1-q^{2})}{(p-q)^{2}}\dot{P}+\tfrac{(1-p^{2})}{(p-q)^{2}}\dot{Q}-\tfrac{4(1-q^{2})}{(p-q)^{3}}P+\tfrac{4(1-p^{2})}{(p-q)^{3}}Q={}

\displaystyle(1-p^{2})\partial_{p}\sigma+(1-q^{2})\partial_{q}\sigma,

where
F=(1-p^{2})^{2}Q-(1-q^{2})^{2}P
. Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we now arrive at the system

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{q}}=\frac{\dot{P}}{(p-q)^{2}}-\frac{4P}{(p-q)^{3}}-\frac{(1-p^{2})}{(p-q)^{2}}T,\qquad\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{p}}=\frac{\dot{Q}}{(p-q)^{2}}+\frac{4Q}{(p-q)^{3}}+\frac{(1-q^{2})}{(p-q)^{2}}T.

Using the identity
\partial_{p}\partial_{q}\sigma=\partial_{q}\partial_{p}\sigma
and eq. (
4.20
), we get

\displaystyle(p-q)^{2}\ddot{Q}+6(p-q)\dot{Q}+12Q=(p-q)^{2}\ddot{P}-6(p-q)\dot{P}+12P+4\lambda(1-p^{2}q^{2}).

(4.21)

Applying
\partial^{2}_{q}
and then
\partial^{2}_{p}
we are led to

\displaystyle\ddddot{Q}-\ddddot{P}=-8\lambda.

(4.22)

Taking additional derivatives
\partial_{p}
and
\partial_{q}
, and using that
P
and
Q
depend only on
p
and
q
respectively, we see that
P
and
Q
must be fourth order polynomials,
P=\sum_{i=0}^{4}a_{i}p^{i}
,
Q=\sum_{i}^{4}b_{i}q^{i}
. Replacing back in (
4.21
), we get
b_{0}=a_{0}+\frac{1}{3}\lambda
,
b_{1}=a_{1}
,
b_{2}=a_{2}
,
b_{3}=a_{3}
,
b_{4}=a_{4}-\frac{1}{3}\lambda
, so the result (
4.17
) follows.
∎

Using (
2.31
), we find:

\displaystyle\frac{W_{0}^{\pm}}{W_{\pm}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(a_{3}\pm a_{1})}{2}-(a_{0}-a_{4}+\frac{1}{3}\lambda)\left(\frac{p+q}{1\mp pq}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{3}\left(\frac{1\pm pq}{p-q}\right)^{3},

(4.23)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\pm}_{2}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{(a_{3}\pm a_{1})}{2}\left(\frac{p-q}{1\pm pq}\right)^{3}+(a_{0}-a_{4}+\frac{1}{3}\lambda)\left(\frac{p+q}{1\mp pq}\right)\left(\frac{p-q}{1\pm pq}\right)^{3}.

(4.24)

From these formulas we see that the conformally SD limit
\Psi_{2}^{-}=0
corresponds to
a_{3}=a_{1}
and
a_{0}-a_{4}+\frac{\lambda}{3}=0
, which implies
\frac{W_{0}^{\pm}}{W_{\pm}}=\frac{\lambda}{3}z_{\pm}^{3}
. Using then Theorem
2.1
, in this limit we get
\rho=\lambda\kappa
, so the space is Einstein. Thus, the conformally SD limit of the standard Plebański-Demiański solution (
4.17
) is indeed different from the generalisation found in Theorem
4.1
.

5
The Chen-Teo class

5.1
SU(\infty)
Toda formulation

Consider the 4-dimensional family of metrics given in local coordinates
(\tau,\phi,x_{1},x_{2})
by

\displaystyle g=\frac{(F{\rm d}\tau+G{\rm d}\phi)^{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})HF}+\frac{kH}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}\left(\frac{{\rm d}{x}_{1}^{2}}{X_{1}}-\frac{{\rm d}{x}_{2}^{2}}{X_{2}}-\frac{X_{1}X_{2}}{kF}{\rm d}\phi^{2}\right),

(5.1)

where
k
is a constant,
G(x_{1},x_{2}),H(x_{1},x_{2}),X_{1}(x_{1}),X_{2}(x_{2})
are arbitrary functions of their arguments, and

\displaystyle F=x_{2}^{2}X_{1}-x_{1}^{2}X_{2}.

(5.2)

The vector fields
\partial_{\tau},\partial_{\phi}
are Killing.
For a specific choice of the functions
G,H,X_{1},X_{2}
, the metric (
5.1
) is the Ricci-flat Chen-Teo geometry, see
[
12
, Eq. (2.1)]

3
3

3

To compare our notation to that of
[
12
]
, set
X_{1}\equiv X
,
X_{2}\equiv Y
,
x_{1}\equiv x
,
x_{2}\equiv y
.
.

Let
c
be an arbitrary constant, and define new variables

\displaystyle W:=\frac{k}{c^{2}}\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})H}{F},\qquad\psi:=\frac{\sqrt{k}}{c}\tau,\qquad y:=\frac{c}{\sqrt{k}}\phi,\qquad\tilde{G}:=\frac{k}{c^{2}}\frac{G}{F},\qquad A:=\tilde{G}{\rm d}{y}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle d}{x}:=c\left[\frac{x_{1}}{X_{1}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}-\frac{x_{2}}{X_{2}}{\rm d}{x}_{2}\right],\qquad{\rm d}{z}:=c\frac{(x_{2}{\rm d}{x}_{1}-x_{1}{\rm d}{x_{2}})}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}},\qquad e^{u}:=\frac{-X_{1}X_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{4}}.

Then a calculation shows that (
5.1
) becomes

\displaystyle g=W^{-1}({\rm d}\psi+A)^{2}+W[{\rm d}{z}^{2}+e^{u}({\rm d}{x}^{2}+{\rm d}{y}^{2})].

(5.4)

The Killing fields are now
\partial_{\psi},\partial_{y}
.

Remark 5.1

(The Chen-Teo parameter
\nu
)
.

From the expression for
{\rm d}{z}
in (
5.3
) we can find
z
by integration: the solution is
z=\frac{cx_{2}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\nu
, where
\nu
is an arbitrary constant. We are free to choose any relation between
c
and
\nu
we want; in particular, setting

\displaystyle c\equiv-(1+\nu),

(5.5)

we get
z=\frac{\nu x_{1}+x_{2}}{x_{1}-x_{2}}
, which, in the Ricci-flat Chen-Teo case, is the (inverse of the) conformal factor that makes the metric Kähler. The parameter
\nu
is particularly important in the Ricci-flat case
[
12
]
: the Chen-Teo solution is a one-parameter (
-1\leq\nu\leq 1
) family of metrics interpolating between the Plebański-Demiański (
\nu=1
) and Gibbons-Hawking (
\nu=-1
) spaces.

The fact that the metric (
5.1
) can be written as (
5.4
) does not imply, of course, that the geometry (
5.1
) is necessarily conformally Kähler. To investigate this, we choose the coframe
\beta^{0}=W^{-1/2}({\rm d}\psi+A)
,
\beta^{1}=\sqrt{W}{\rm d}{z}
,
\beta^{2}=\sqrt{W}e^{u/2}{\rm d}{x}
,
\beta^{3}=\sqrt{W}e^{u/2}{\rm d}{y}
for (
5.4
). The 2-form
\kappa=\beta^{0}\wedge\beta^{1}+\beta^{2}\wedge\beta^{3}
is equal to (
2.10
), and defines the almost-complex structure
J^{a}{}_{b}=\kappa_{bc}g^{ca}
. The type-
(1,0)
eigenspace is spanned by
\ell=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{0}+{\rm i}\beta^{1})
,
m=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta^{3})
. In particular, the following are type-(1,0) forms:

\displaystyle\omega^{0}={\rm d}\psi+A+{\rm i}W{\rm d}{z}+B({\rm d}{x}+{\rm i}{\rm d}{y}),\qquad\omega^{1}={\rm d}{x}+{\rm i}{\rm d}{y}

(5.6)

where
B
is an arbitrary complex function. Since
{\rm d}\omega^{1}=0
, we see that
J
will be integrable if
{\rm d}\omega^{0}=0
. This gives the Hermitian condition, and, assuming that it holds, the conformally Kähler condition is
{\rm d}(z^{-2}\kappa)=0
. These two conditions lead respectively to:

\displaystyle Z_{1}:={}

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{z}-W_{x}=0,

(5.7a)

\displaystyle Z_{2}:={}

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{x}+z^{2}\partial_{z}\left(\tfrac{We^{u}}{z^{2}}\right)=0

(5.7b)

(recall (
2.13
)).
The geometry (
5.1
) will be conformally Kähler (for the given choice of almost-complex structure (
5.6
)) iff the conditions (
5.7
) are satisfied. The vector field (
2.3
) is the Killing vector
\partial_{\psi}
. We also find

\displaystyle\partial_{x}={}

\displaystyle-\frac{X_{1}X_{2}}{cF}(x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+x_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}),

(5.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{cF}(x_{2}X_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+x_{1}X_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}).

Remark 5.2
.

To have some intuition about (
5.7
), we express
Z_{1}
in terms of the original variables:

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\frac{k(x_{1}-x_{2})X_{1}X_{2}}{c^{2}F}\left[\partial_{x_{2}}\left(\frac{x_{1}G}{X_{1}F}+\frac{x_{2}H}{(x_{1}-x_{2})F}\right)+\partial_{x_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}G}{X_{2}F}+\frac{x_{1}H}{(x_{1}-x_{2})F}\right)\right].

(5.9)

Then, for the original Chen-Teo Ricci-flat metric
[
12
]
, using
[
1
, Eqs. (3.7a)-(3.7b)]
we see that indeed
Z_{1}=0
, which justifies our choice of almost-complex structure (
5.6
) for the general class (
5.1
). (In the Ricci-flat case,
Z_{2}=0
follows form
Z_{1}=0
.)

5.2
A family of conformally self-dual Chen-Teo metrics

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume the family of metrics (
5.1
) to be conformally Kähler w.r.t the almost-complex structure (
5.6
) (that is, conditions (
5.7
) are satisfied). Choose the relation (
5.5
) between the parameters
c
and
\nu
. Then (
5.1
) is conformally self-dual
*C_{abcd}=C_{abcd}
if and only if the functions
X_{1},X_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle X_{1}={}

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{1}^{2}+a_{3}x_{1}^{3}+a_{4}x_{1}^{4},

(5.10)

\displaystyle X_{2}={}

\displaystyle a_{0}\nu^{2}-a_{1}\nu x_{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}-\tfrac{a_{3}}{\nu}x_{2}^{3}+\tfrac{a_{4}}{\nu^{2}}x_{2}^{4}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. We use that
*C_{abcd}=C_{abcd}
is equivalent to
\hat{R}=0
, where
\hat{R}
is given by (
2.15
):
\hat{u}_{xx}+(e^{\hat{u}})_{\hat{z}\hat{z}}=0
, with
\hat{u}=u-4\log z
,
\hat{z}=-1/z
(we used that
\partial_{y}
is Killing). Introducing
\hat{\sigma}
by
\hat{u}_{x}=\hat{\sigma}_{\hat{z}}
,
(e^{\hat{u}})_{\hat{z}}=-\hat{\sigma}_{x}
, and using that the vector fields
\partial_{x},\partial_{z}
are given by (
5.8
), we are led to

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}X_{2}\dot{X}_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{x_{2}X_{1}\dot{X}_{2}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{4X_{1}X_{2}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}={}

\displaystyle-x_{2}X_{1}\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{1}}-x_{1}X_{2}\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}\dot{X}_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{x_{1}\dot{X}_{2}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{4\nu x_{2}X_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{3}}-\frac{4x_{1}X_{2}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{3}}={}

\displaystyle-x_{1}\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{1}}-x_{2}\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{2}},

where
\dot{X}_{1}\equiv\frac{{\rm d}{X}_{1}}{{\rm d}{x}_{1}}
,
\dot{X}_{2}\equiv\frac{{\rm d}{X}_{2}}{{\rm d}{x}_{2}}
.
We then deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{2}}=-\frac{\dot{X}_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{4\nu X_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{3}},\qquad\frac{\partial\hat{\sigma}}{\partial{x}_{1}}=-\frac{\dot{X}_{2}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{4X_{1}}{(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{3}}.

Using
\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}\hat{\sigma}=\partial_{x_{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}\hat{\sigma}
, we find

\displaystyle(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}\ddot{X}_{1}-6(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})\dot{X}_{1}+12\nu^{2}X_{1}=(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}\ddot{X}_{2}-6(\nu x_{1}+x_{2})\dot{X}_{2}+12X_{2}.

(5.11)

Applying
\partial^{2}_{x_{2}}\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}
, we get

\displaystyle\ddddot{X}_{1}=\nu^{2}\ddddot{X}_{2},

(5.12)

which implies that
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are fourth order polynomials in
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively. Replacing in (
5.11
), we find relations between the coefficients and we get (
5.10
).
∎

Similarly to Theorem
4.1
, the solution (
5.10
) can be regarded (locally) as a generalisation of the Ricci-flat Chen-Teo metric to a self-dual gravitational instanton in conformal gravity. However, unlike (
4.9
), the conformally self-dual equation now determines
X_{1},X_{2}
in (
5.1
) to be given by (
5.10
), but it does not determine the other arbitrary functions
G,H
in (
5.1
). These are constrained by the conformally Kähler condition (
5.7
), but this restriction does not determine
G,H
uniquely.
This situation is analogous to what we encountered in Theorem
3.2
.

5.3
A family of Einstein-Maxwell Chen-Teo metrics

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume the family of metrics (
5.1
) to be conformally Kähler w.r.t the almost-complex structure (
5.6
) (that is, conditions (
5.7
) are satisfied). Choose the relation (
5.5
) between the parameters
c
and
\nu
. Then (
5.1
) satisfies the Einstein-Maxwell equations (with
\lambda=0
) if and only if

\displaystyle X_{1}={}

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{1}^{2}+a_{3}x_{1}^{3}+a_{4}x_{1}^{4},

(5.13)

\displaystyle X_{2}={}

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}x_{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}+a_{3}x_{2}^{3}+a_{4}x_{2}^{4},

where
a_{0},...a_{4}
are arbitrary constants.

Proof.

Recall that the Einstein-Maxwell equations with
\lambda=0
reduce to
u_{xx}+(e^{u})_{zz}=0
. The procedure to solve this is the same that we used in previous cases: we introduce an auxiliary variable
\sigma
by
u_{x}=\sigma_{z}
,
(e^{u})_{z}=-\sigma_{x}
.
Using (
5.8
), we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}X_{2}\dot{X}_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{x_{2}X_{1}\dot{X}_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{4X_{1}X_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}=-x_{2}X_{1}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{1}}-x_{1}X_{2}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}\dot{X}_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{x_{1}\dot{X}_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{4x_{2}X_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}+\frac{4x_{1}X_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}=-x_{1}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{1}}-x_{2}\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{2}}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{2}}=-\frac{\dot{X}_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}+\frac{4X_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}},\qquad\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial{x}_{1}}=-\frac{\dot{X}_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}-\frac{4X_{2}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}}.

Using then
\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}\sigma=\partial_{x_{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}\sigma
, after some calculations we arrive at

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\ddot{X}_{2}+6(x_{1}-x_{2})\dot{X}_{2}+12X_{2}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\ddot{X}_{1}-6(x_{1}-x_{2})\dot{X}_{2}+12X_{1}.

(5.14)

Applying
\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}
, we get
\ddddot{X}_{1}=\ddddot{X}_{2}
, which implies that
X_{1},X_{2}
are fourth order polynomials, and replacing in (
5.14
) we see that they must have the same coefficients.
∎

The previous result shows that we now encounter a new phenomenon for Einstein-Maxwell instantons, not present in the Plebański-Demiański and previous cases: by construction, the conformal Kähler assumption and the fact that (
5.13
) solve the
SU(\infty)
Toda equation guarantee that the ansatz (
5.1
) is automatically a solution to the Einstein-Maxwell system, with the Maxwell field given by (
2.8
) and (
2.20
) (or (
2.17
)), but the functions
G
and
H
in (
5.1
), while constrained by (
5.7
), remain undetermined. Since
u

is
determined, one can use that
H
is related to
W
by (
5.3
) and that
W
must satisfy

\displaystyle W_{xx}+\partial_{z}\left[z^{2}\partial_{z}(\tfrac{We^{u}}{z^{2}})\right]=0

(5.15)

(recall (
2.14
)), to solve this equation for
H
, and then use this to find
G
via (
5.7
). However, the solution to this system is not unique. The situation can be compared to the Gibbons-Hawking ansatz, where instead of (
5.15
) one has the Laplace equation and instead of (
2.13
) one has the standard monopole equation
{\rm d}{A}=*_{3}{\rm d}{V}
(see e.g.
[
18
]
), but these equations do not have a unique solution. Our construction thus provides a
family
of Einstein-Maxwell Chen-Teo geometries, in a similar sense as the Gibbons-Hawking ansatz provides a family of hyper-Kähler geometries.

By contrast, the Ricci-flat condition for the ansatz (
5.1
) gives the same solution (
5.13
) but it also determines
H
and
G
, since
W
must be given by (
2.18
), and then one finds
H
from (
5.3
) and
G
from (
5.7
). We have checked that in this way one indeed recovers the original Ricci-flat Chen-Teo metric
[
12
, Eq. (2.1)]
. In the Einstein case with
\lambda\neq 0
,
W
is given by (
2.22
), and one must solve the modified Toda equation. This can be done with the same trick that we used in the Plebański-Demiański case (Theorem
4.3
); the result will be presented in a separate work.

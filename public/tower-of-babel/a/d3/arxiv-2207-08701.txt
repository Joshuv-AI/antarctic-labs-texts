Stasys Jukna

Abstract
A monotone Boolean ( ∨ , ∧ ) (\lor,\land) circuit computing a monotone Boolean function f f is a read- k k circuit if the polynomial produced (purely syntactically) by the arithmetic ( + , × ) (+,\times) version of the circuit has the property that for every prime implicant of f f , the polynomial contains at least one monomial with the same set of variables, each appearing with degree ⩽ k \leqslant k . Every monotone circuit is a read- k k circuit for some k k . We show that already read-1 ( ∨ , ∧ ) (\lor,\land) circuits are not weaker than monotone arithmetic constant-free ( + , × ) (+,\times) circuits computing multilinear polynomials, are not weaker than non-monotone multilinear ( ∨ , ∧ , ¬ ) (\lor,\land,\neg) circuits computing monotone Boolean functions, and have the same power as tropical ( min , + ) (\min,+) circuits solving 0 / 1 0/1 minimization problems. Finally, we show that read-2 ( ∨ , ∧ ) (\lor,\land) circuits can be exponentially smaller than read-1 ( ∨ , ∧ ) (\lor,\land) circuits.

In this section, we recall the classical model of (combinational) circuits

A

c\in R
.
A circuit is

are used as inputs. Every other node, a

\odot

the values computed at the two gates entering this gate. The

F

5.1
An easy lower bound for monotone arithmetic circuits

The goal of this section is to demonstrate that strong lower bounds on the size of monotone
arithmetic
(+,\times)
circuits and, hence (by
1

also on the size of monotone Boolean read-
1

(\lor,\land)
circuits can be proved fairly
easily.

The weakness of monotone arithmetic circuits lies in
1

unlike for monotone
Boolean
circuits (where both idempotence
x\land x=x
and
absorption
x\lor xy=x
are allowed), monotone
arithmetic
circuits ‘‘produce what
they compute.’’ This weakness results in the following ‘‘balanced
decomposition property’’ for arithmetic circuits computing
homogeneous
7
7

7

A polynomial
f(x)=\sum_{a\in A}c_{a}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
is monotone if
c_{a}>0

for all
a\in A
, and is
homogeneous
of degree

if

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=m
holds for all
a\in A

already by Hyafil
[
12

and

[
47
, Lemma 3]

Decomposition Lemma
.

If a homogeneous polynomial
f
of degree
m\geqslant 3

can be computed by a monotone arithmetic
(+,\times)
circuit of
size
s
, then
f
can be written as a sum

of
t\leqslant s
products of homogeneous polynomials such that
m/3\leqslant\mathrm{deg}(g_{i})\leqslant 2m/3
for all
i=1,\ldots,t
.

In particular, the inclusions
\mathrm{mon}(g_{i}h_{i})\subseteq\mathrm{mon}(f)
hold for
all
i=1,\ldots,t
, where
\mathrm{mon}(f)
denotes the set of all monomials
of
f
. That is every monomial of
g_{i}h_{i}
must also be a monomial of the
computed polynomial (no “redundant” monomials can be produced);
this is in stark contrast with Boolean or tropical circuits, where
nothing similar holds.
The proof of Decomposition Lemma is simple. If a circuit computes the
polynomial
f
, then (by
1
) it also produces that
polynomial. Since
f
is homogeneous, polynomials
g_{v}
produced at
intermediate gates

are also homogeneous. By walking backward from
the output gate, and by always choosing that of the two input gates

v
with larger
\mathrm{deg}(g_{v})
, we will find a gate
v
with
m/3\leqslant\mathrm{deg}(g_{v})\leqslant 2m/3
. Hence, the polynomial
f
is of the form

f=g_{v}h_{v}+\cdots
for some polynomial
h_{v}
. Replace the gate
v

by constant
0
, and argue by induction on circuit size.

Example 2

(Perfect matchings)
.

The

A tropical

Note that in the tropical

\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}

For example, an arithmetic

f(x,y)=\min\{3x+y+2,x+2y+4\}

A
(\min,+)

In

F

The minimization problem

Since constant inputs in

For a Boolean function

Theorem 3

(Arbitrary functions)
.

For every Boolean function

Theorem 4

(Monotone functions)

For every monotone Boolean function

Proof.

The inequality

. So,

Proof of Theorem

Fact 3
.

Let

be Boolean functions, and let

and

be their lowest ones. If

Proof.

Note that

Recall that the Minkowski sum of two sets

is the set

Lemma 5
.

Let
g,h:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
be Boolean functions.

(i)

If

, then

A_{f}\subseteq A_{g}\cup A_{h}
and

.

(ii)

If

and

are independent, then

A_{f}\subseteq A_{g}+A_{h}
and
f^{\triangledown}=g^{\triangledown}\land h^{\triangledown}
.

Proof.

To show (i), let

. The inclusion

is trivial:

, then

or

, and both

and

. Thus, either

or
a\in A_{h}
, as desired. To show the inequality

, take any vector

for

holds; hence,

for some lowest one

. Then, as we have just shown, either

or

(or both) hold. Hence, either
g^{\triangledown}(x)=1
or

, take any vector

.

To show (ii), let

, where the functions
g
and

are

. Since then

and

, there are lowest ones

and

The inequality

and

for which both

and

of

and for some lowest one

of

. Since the functions

and

are independent,

Remark 9
.

In general, if
f=g\land h
, and if the functions
g
and

are not independent, then
even the inclusion
A_{f}\subseteq A_{g}\lor A_{h}:=\{b\lor c\colon b\in A_{g},x\in A_{h}\}
does not need to hold.
Take, for example,

g=x\overline{y}\,\overline{z}\lor xy
and

; hence,

. The functions

and

are dependent (their prime implicants

). The only lowest one of

is

, the

, and the only lowest one of

is

. But

. Also, in general, the

does not need to

; hence,

. On the vector

and

.

We now turn to the actual proof of the remaining two inequalities

\mathsf{B}^{\mbox{\footnotesize$+$}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f^{\triangledown})\leqslant\mathsf{B}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f)

\mathsf{B}_{1}(f^{\triangledown})\leqslant\mathsf{B}^{\mbox{\footnotesize$+$}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f^{\triangledown})

We can view every DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)

Take, for example, the circuit

.

Lemma 6

(Multilinear to monotone multilinear)
.

Let
F
be a DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)
circuit computing a
Boolean function
f
. If
F
is multilinear, then the circuit

{F}^{+}
is also multilinear and computes
f^{\triangledown}
. In particular,

\mathsf{B}^{\mbox{\footnotesize$+$}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f^{\triangledown})\leqslant\mathsf{B}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f)
holds.

Proof.

Suppose that the circuit
F(x)=H(x,\overline{x})
is multilinear.
To show that the (monotone) circuit
{F}^{+}(x)=H(x,\vec{1})
is multilinear,
let
g
and
h
be the Boolean functions computed at some AND gate of
the circuit
F(x)=H(x,\overline{x})
. Since the circuit
F(x)
is
multilinear, the functions
g
and
h
are independent. By

2
, this happens precisely when their prime implicants share no
variables in common. Since the upward closure of any
Boolean function is the OR of positive factors of its prime implicants,

2
implies that the functions
g^{\triangledown}
and
h^{\triangledown}
are also independent.

Let us now show that the monotone version
{F}^{+}=H(x,\vec{1})
of
F

computes the upward closure
f^{\triangledown}
of
f
. Upward closures of
input variables
x_{i}
are the variables
x_{i}^{\triangledown}=x_{i}
themselves,
while upward closures of negated input variables
\overline{x}_{i}
are
constant-
1
functions
\overline{x}_{i}^{\triangledown}=1
. Let
g
and
h
be the
Boolean functions computed at the two inputs of an arbitrary gate of

F
. If this is an OR gate, then
5
yields the
equality
(g\lor h)^{\triangledown}=g^{\triangledown}\lor h^{\triangledown}
. If this is an AND gate
then, since the circuit
F
is multilinear, the functions
g
and

h
are independent, and
5
also yields the

(g\land h)^{\triangledown}=g^{\triangledown}\land h^{\triangledown}
. Thus, in the circuit

{F}^{+}=H(x,\vec{1})
, the upward closures

of the

computed at the gates of

are computed. Since this
also holds for the output gate of

, at which the function

is
computed, the upward closure
f^{\triangledown}
of

is computed at this

{F}^{+}
, as desired.

(Monotone multilinear to read-

)
.

Monotone multilinear Boolean circuits are read-

\mathsf{B}_{1}(f)\leqslant\mathsf{B}^{\mbox{\footnotesize$+$}}_{\mathrm{lin}}(f)

Let

be a monotone multilinear
(\lor,\land)

holds. So, to show that

is

1
circuit, we have only to show that also the inclusion

A_{f}\subseteq B_{F}
holds, i.e., that every lowest one

of

is produced by the circuit
F
.

Let
G
and
H
be the subcircuits of
F
whose output gates
enter the output gate of
F
, and let
g
and
h
be the
monotone Boolean functions computed by these subcircuits. Let also

B_{G}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
and

be the sets of
“exponent” vectors produced by the subcircuits

and

. We
argue by induction on the number

of gates in
F
. In the basis

, we have

and

for some

and

B_{H}=\{\vec{e}_{j}\}=A_{h}

. If

B_{F}=B_{G}+B_{H}=\{\vec{e}_{i}+\vec{e}_{j}\}=A_{f}

Now suppose that the lemma holds for all monotone multilinear

(\lor,\land)
circuits of size at most

be a
monotone multilinear
(\lor,\land)

. Since the

is multilinear, both subcircuits

and

are also

and

the lemma holds for both these subcircuits. Thus, both inclusions

A_{g}\subseteq B_{G}
and
A_{h}\subseteq B_{H}

If

, then

the desired inclusion

follows from the

is multilinear, the functions

and

are independent, and

follows again from the induction hypothesis.

7.2
Multilinear Circuits Impede Zero Terms

6
rises a natural question: if
F=F(x,\overline{x})

is a DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)
circuit computing a Boolean function

f
, when does its monotone version
{F}^{+}=F(x,\vec{1})

computes
f^{\triangledown}
? It can be easily shown that such are exactly
DeMorgan circuits
F
that “impede” zero terms in the following
sense.

Every DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)
circuit
F
not only computes some Boolean function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
but also
produces
(purely
syntactically) a unique set
T(F)
of Boolean terms in a natural way:

\circ

if
F=z
is an input literal
z\in\{x_{i},\overline{x}_{i}\}
, then
T(F)=\{z\}
;

\circ

if
F=F_{1}\lor F_{2}
, then
T(F_{1}\lor F_{2})=T(F_{1})\cup T(F_{2})
;

\circ

if
F=F_{1}\land F_{2}
, then

T(F)=\left\{t_{1}\land t_{2}\colon t_{1}\in T(F_{1}),t_{2}\in T(F_{2})\right\}
.

During the production of terms, the “annihilation”
law
x\land\overline{x}=0
is not used. So,
T(F)
can contain

zero terms
, that is, terms containing a variable
x_{i}
together
with its negation
\overline{x}_{i}
. For example, the set
T(F)=\{x\overline{y},x\overline{y}z,xy\overline{y},xy\overline{y}z\}

of terms produced by the circuit

F=(x\lor xy)(\overline{y}\lor\overline{y}z)
contains two zero terms
xy\overline{y}

and
xy\overline{y}z
.

Recall that the
positive factor

{t}^{+}
of a Boolean term
t
is obtained by
replacing every its negated literal
\overline{x}_{i}
with constant
1
.
Let us say that a DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)
circuit
F
computing a
Boolean function
f

impedes zero terms
if positive factors
{t}^{+}
of zero terms
t\in T(F)
produced by
F
(if there are any) are implicants of

f^{\triangledown}
, that is, if
{t}^{+}\leqslant f^{\triangledown}
holds for every zero term

t\in T(F)
. Note that such a circuit does
not
forbid a
production of zero terms as such, but rather “impedes” produced zero
terms to unfold the full power of cancellations
x\land\overline{x}=0
.

Fact 4
.

Let
F
be a DeMorgan
(\lor,\land,\neg)
circuit computing a
Boolean function
f
with
f(\vec{0})=0
. The circuit
{F}^{+}

computes
f^{\triangledown}
if and only if
F
impedes zero terms.

Proof.

Since
F
computes
f
, we have
f=\bigvee_{t\in T}t
, where

T=T(F)
is the set of all terms produced by the circuit
F
.

We have shown that already very restricted monotone Boolean

and, for every input
x\in\{0,1\}^{P}
,
f_{{\cal L}}(x)=1
iff the set of points
S_{x}=\{p\in P\colon x_{p}=1\}
blocks (intersects) every line
L\in{\cal L}
. The monotone circuit

F_{{\cal L}}(x)=\bigwedge_{L\in{\cal L}}\big(\bigvee_{p\in L}x_{p}\big)

computes
f_{{\cal L}}
and has
(m-1)|{\cal L}|+(|{\cal L}|-1)=m|{\cal L}|-1
fanin-
2

gates. Moreover, since no point belongs to more than
k
lines,

F_{{\cal L}}
is a read-
k
circuit. In particular, we have an upper bound

\mathsf{B}_{k}(f_{{\cal L}})\leqslant m|{\cal L}|=k|P|=kn
. Note that for the dual

{f}^{\ast}_{{\cal L}}(x)=\bigvee_{L\in{\cal L}}\bigwedge_{p\in L}x_{p}

of
f_{{\cal L}}
, we even have
\mathsf{B}_{1}({f}^{\ast}_{{\cal L}})\leqslant kn
, that is, the duals of blocking lines functions can be computed by small
read-
1
circuits. Since none of the points belongs to more
than
k
lines, every implicant
t_{S}=\bigwedge_{p\in S}x_{p}
of

f_{{\cal L}}
corresponds to a blocking set
S\subseteq P
consisting of

|S|\geqslant|{\cal L}|/k
points, while shortest (prime) implicants
t_{S}
of
f_{{\cal L}}
(those with
the smallest number of variables) correspond to
smallest

blocking sets, that is, blocking sets
S\subseteq P
consisting of

|S|=|{\cal L}|/k
pairwise noncollinear points; two points are

collinear
if they both belong to some line. Note that maxterms of
f_{{\cal L}}
are sets
\{x_{p}\colon p\in L\}
of variables corresponding to
lines
L\in{\cal L}
, while minterms of
f_{{\cal L}}
are sets
\{x_{p}\colon p\in S\}
of variables corresponding to blocking sets
S\subseteq P
, none proper subset of which is a blocking set.

Each point of a smallest blocking set
S\subseteq P

blocks (intersects) its
own
collection of
k
lines: for every point
p\in S
there is a collection
{\cal L}_{p}\subseteq{\cal L}
of
|{\cal L}_{p}|=k
lines such that
{\cal L}_{p}\cap{\cal L}_{q}=\emptyset
holds for all points
p\neq q\in S
(for, otherwise, points
p
and
q
would be collinear).
That is, each point
p\in S
is the
only
point of
S
blocking the
k
lines
{\cal L}_{p}
. In other words, each variable
x_{p}
of a shortest prime implicant
t_{S}=\bigwedge_{p\in S}x_{p}
of
f_{{\cal L}}

blocks its
own
collection of
k
lines.
Intuitively, this means that the variable
x_{p}
“should” be accessed by a circuit or a branching program at least
k
times to produce this implicant.

Example 3

(Many short lines)
.

Instead of
2
-dimensional tensors (matrices), as in the case of the
function
\mathrm{Lines}_{n,2}
used in
8
, one can consider
k
-dimensional tensors for

k\geqslant 3
. Let
n
be of the form
n=m^{k}
. As the
underlying set
P
of points, take the set
P=[m]^{k}
of
k
-tuples

p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\in[m]^{k}
, and consider the family
{\cal L}\subseteq 2^{P}
of all (combinatorial) lines, where the
line
in the

i
th direction through a point
p\in P
is the set

L=\big\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{i-1},\ast,p_{i+1},\ldots,p_{k})\colon\ast=1,\ldots,n\big\}
of
|L|=m
points. Thus, we have

|{\cal L}|=km^{k-1}=(k/m)|P|
distinct lines, no two sharing more than one point.
Since each point belongs to exactly
k
lines (there are
k

possible positions for
\ast
), the family
{\cal L}
is

k
-regular. Hence, the corresponding (to this family
{\cal L}
)
blocking lines function
\mathrm{Lines}_{n,k}(x):=f_{{\cal L}}(x)
can be computed
by a monotone read-
k

(\lor,\land)
circuit of size
\leqslant m|{\cal L}|=k|P|=km^{k}=kn
; in particular,
\mathsf{B}_{k}(\mathrm{Lines}_{n,k})\leqslant kn
holds.
Note that in this family
{\cal L}
of lines, two points (
k
-tuples)
p\neq q\in[m]^{k}
are collinear iff they differ in exactly one position. In particular, any set of
pairwise
collinear points must entirely lie in one line.
In the case

k=2
(matrices, as in
8
), smallest blocking sets consist of the entries of a permutation matrix.

Example 4

(Few long lines)
.

Let

, were

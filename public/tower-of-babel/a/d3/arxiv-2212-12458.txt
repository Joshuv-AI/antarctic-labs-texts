Abstract
. Let X X be an affine scheme of k × ℕ k\times{\mathbb{N}} -matrices and Y Y be an affine scheme of ℕ × ⋯ × ℕ {\mathbb{N}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{N}} -dimensional tensors. The group Sym ⁡ ( ℕ ) \operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}}) acts naturally on both X X and Y Y and on their coordinate rings. We show that the Zariski closure of the image of a Sym ⁡ ( ℕ ) \operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}}) -equivariant morphism of schemes from X X to Y Y is defined by finitely many Sym ⁡ ( ℕ ) \operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}}) -orbits in the coordinate ring of Y Y . Moreover, we prove that the closure of the image of this map is Sym ⁡ ( ℕ ) \operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}}) -Noetherian, that is, every descending chain of Sym ⁡ ( ℕ ) \operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}}) -stable closed subsets stabilizes.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, many finiteness properties of rings with countably
many variables and infinite dimensional varieties have been studied,
especially of those acted upon by the infinite symmetric group. A
number of fundamental results have been established, but still many have
yet to be discovered.

Most of these results build on the theorem from

[
Coh67
,
Coh87
]
, rediscovered in

[
AH07
,
HS12
]
, that
the polynomial ring in
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
variables is
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-Noetherian
with the natural action of the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
. This implies
that the space of
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices equipped with the induced
action of
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
is topologically
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-Noetherian. However,
already the space of
{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices equipped with the natural
action of
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
, or even with the action of
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})\times\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
, is not equivariantly Noetherian, and it gets only worse for
higher-dimensional tensors. This is problematic, because many varieties
of relevance to applications, such as the
k
-factor model

[
Dra10
]
and hierarchical models

[
HS12
]
naturally live in matrix or tensor spaces and
are preserved by (copies of) the symmetric group.

Hence it is interesting to find
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-stable subvarieties of

{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices, or
{\mathbb{N}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{N}}
-tensors,
that are defined by finitely many orbits of equations and equivariantly
Noetherian. In this paper we study such subvarieties that
arise as image closures of
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-equivariant maps from the space
of
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices, and we show that these are always defined,
set-theoretically, by finitely many
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-orbits of equations,
as well as themselves topologically
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-Noetherian.

1.1.
Main Theorem

All algebras and schemes are defined over a Noetherian ring

K
. All schemes are
affine schemes
and we will usually
drop the adjective
affine
. Let
\mathbf{FI}
denotes the category
of finite sets with injective maps. An important insight from

[
CEF15
]
is that functors from
\mathbf{FI}

are the right language for studying structures with
increasing numbers of variables acted upon by increasing
symmetric groups.

An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over
K
is a covariant functor from
\mathbf{FI}
to the
category of algebras over
K
. An
\mathbf{FI}
-ideal
I

of an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
assigns to each finite set
S
an ideal
I(S)

of
A(S)
, in such a manner that the algebra homomorphism
A(\pi):A(S)\to A(T)
corresponding to any
\pi\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbf{FI}}(S,T)
maps
I(S)

into
I(T)
. An
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme over
K
is a contravariant functor from
\mathbf{FI}
to the
category of schemes over
K
. A closed subscheme
Z
of an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme

X
is a subfunctor of
X
such that, for each finite set
S
,
Z(S)

is a closed subscheme of
X(S)
. Morphisms of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras are natural
transformations of functors. Morphisms of
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes are defined
dually.

We say that an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
is
finitely generated in width at most
1

if
A
is generated by
A(\emptyset)
and
A(\{1\})
, and
both of these are finitely generated. This is equivalent
to the statement that
A
can be realised as a quotient (in the category
of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras over
K
) of an algebra of the form

S\mapsto K[x_{ij}\mid i\in[k],j\in S];

see
[
DEF22
, Lemma 2.6.8]
.
Geometetrically,
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is then an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-subscheme over
K
of
the scheme that sends
S
to
k\times S
-matrices.

Theorem 1.1.1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\varphi
be a morphism of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras
B\rightarrow A
, where
A
and
B
both are finitely generated

\mathbf{FI}
-algebras over a Noetherian ring
K
. Suppose,
moreover, that
A
is generated in width at most
1
. Then
the Zariski closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
of the image
of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
under the dual map
\varphi^{*}
is defined, as a
set, by finitely many elements in
B
. Equivalently, there exist a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
-ideal
I
in
B
such that the radical of
I
is equal to the radical of the kernel
{\rm Ker}\,\varphi
.

Moreover,
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
is topologically
Noetherian, that is, it satisfies the descending chain
condition on reduced closed subschemes.

An immediate consequence of the Main Theorem is the
following.

Corollary 1.1.2
.

For any
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-equivariant morphism
\varphi
of
K
-schemes from the
scheme of
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices to the scheme of
{\mathbb{N}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{N}}
-tensors, the image closure

\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)}
of
\varphi
is defined,
set-theoretically, by finitely many
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-orbits of
equations. Furthermore, it is topologically
\operatorname{Sym}({\mathbb{N}})
-Noetherian:
any descending chain

\overline{\operatorname{im}(\varphi)}\supseteq X_{1}\supseteq X_{2}\supseteq\ldots

of reduced, closed subschemes stabilises.
\square

We do not know whether
\ker\varphi
itself is necessarily finitely
generated.

Conjecture 1.1.3
.

Let
\varphi
be the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra homomorphism defined in the Main Theorem
1.1.1
. Then the
\mathbf{FI}
-ideal
{\rm Ker}\,\varphi
is finitely generated.

A particular case of the above conjecture was stated in
[
AH07
, Conjecture 5.10]
and proved in
[
Dra+13
]
when generators of the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B
are mapped under
\varphi
to monomials in
A
.

On the other hand, in the setting of the Main Theorem, there are
examples where
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
is not scheme-theoretically
Noetherian, i.e., has an infinite strictly descending chain of closed
(but non-reduced)
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-subschemes: in
[
DKK
, Theorem 43]

it is proved that the cone over the Grassmannian of
2\times S
-spaces,
with
S\in\mathbf{FI}
, has this bad behaviour in characteristic
2

(but not in characteristic zero due to the existence of
Reynolds operators, see
[
Dra10
]
).

Relations to existing literature

-invariant ideals that arise in commutative algebra and adjacent

[

,
Le+20
,
Le+21
,
LN22
]
,
bivariate Hilbert series recording both degree and
number of variables
[
KLS17
,
NR17
]
,
and irreducible components of
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-varieties

[
NS
,
NS22
,

]
. Some of
these developments were inspired by challenging questions in algebraic statistics

[
AH07
,
DSS06
,
Dra10
,
BD11
]
, and similar techniques have been applied in
discrete geometry
[
KVR22
]
and real-algebraic geometry

[
KR22
]
. The most efficient framework, in our opinion, to
study such questions is that of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras and
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes,

[
NR19
,
DEF22
]
.

To illustrate the strength of our Main Theorem, we explain
how it immediately implies the main result in

[
Dra10
]
.

Example 1.2.1
.

In
[
DSS06
]
the question was raised whether the ideal of
polynomial relations among the entries of a matrix of the form
\Sigma=AA^{T}+D
, with
A
running through
{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}
and

D
running through the (positive-definite)
diagonal matrices, is generated by a finite number of
\operatorname{Sym}([n])
-orbits
for
n\to\infty
. The set of all such matrices is the Gaussian

k
-factor model. This question remains open to this date. However,
in
[
BD11
]
an affirmative answer was given for
k=2
, and in

[
Dra10
]
an affirmative answer for a set-theoretical version
was established for arbitrary
k
. The latter also follows from the
Main Theorem, because the map that sends the matrix entry
\sigma_{ij}

to the entry
\sum_{l=1}^{k}a_{il}a_{jl}+\delta_{ij}\cdot d_{ii}
is
a homomorphism from the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
S\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}[\sigma_{ij}\mid i,j\in S]
to the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
S\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}[a_{il},d_{ii}\mid l\in[k],i\in S]
, and the latter is generated in width
\leq 1
.

In the example, as in the rest of the paper,
[k]
stands for

\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and in particular
[0]=\emptyset
.

1.3.
Organisation of this paper

In Section 2 we recall elementary properties of finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras. Section 3 contains basic results about topological Noetherianity. Section 4 is devoted to the reduction of the Main Theorem
1.1.1
to the case of free
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras. In Section 5 we prove our Main Theorem. To this end we prove that image of the map
\varphi^{*}
lies in the closed subscheme of
\operatorname{Spec}(B)
defined by off-diagonal
(l+1)\times(l+1)
-subdeterminants of flattnings, see Lemma
5.2.5
. This subscheme is defined by finitely many equations (Lemma
5.2.4
) and is contained in the image of a topologically Noetherian space, see Proposition
5.2.10
and Lemma
5.2.8
.

A result of potentially independent interest is the following tensor
completion result: a tensor labelled by the tuples in
S\times\cdots\times S
in which no entry appears more than once (an
off-diagonal tensor
)
and of which all the meaningful
(l+1)\times(l+1)
-subdeterminants of
flattenings are zero, can be completed to a full tensor (including the
diagonal) of some bounded rank; see
5.2.10
.

2.1.
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras and
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes

The following notion was introduced in

[
CEF15
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

The category
\mathbf{FI}
(for Finite sets with Injections) has as objects finite sets and for finite sets
S
and
T

the set
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbf{FI}}(S,T)
is the set of injections from
S
to
T
.
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
denotes its opposite category.

Let
K
be a commutative ring with
1
. All
K
-algebras
A
are
required to be commutative, have a
1
, and the homomorphism
K\to A

is required to send
1
to
1
. Homomorphisms of
K
-algebras are
unital ring homomorphisms
A\to B
compatible with the homomorphisms from
K

into them.

Definition 2.1.2
.

An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
is a covariant functor from
\mathbf{FI}
to the
category of algebras over
K
. Dually,
A
gives rise to a contravariant
functor
X
from
\mathbf{FI}
to the category of affine schemes over
K
.
We call such a functor an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme over
K
.

A morphism
A\to B
of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras over
K
is a natural
transformation of functors from
A
to
B
: it consists of a
K
-algebra
homomorphism
\varphi_{S}:A(S)\to B(S)
for all finite sets
S
such that for
all
S,T\in\mathbf{FI}
and for each injection
\pi
from
S
to
T
, the following diagram commutes.

\textstyle{A(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{S}}
\scriptstyle{A(\pi)}
\textstyle{B(S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{B(\pi)}
\textstyle{A(T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{T}}
\textstyle{B(T).}

Morphisms of
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes are defined dually. We often leave out the
subscript
S
in
\varphi_{S}
, and often just write
\pi
instead of
A(\varphi)

when
A
is clear from the context.

2.2.
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras finitely generated in width
\leq d

\mathbf{FI}
-algebras that are finitely generated in width at most
d
are the main characters of this paper. By a subalgebra
B
of an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
we mean an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B
over
K
such that for each finite set
S
,
B(S)
is a subalgebra of
A(S)
, and for each injection
\sigma:S\rightarrow T
,
B(\sigma)
is the restriction of
A(\sigma)
to
B(S)
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
is said to be finitely
generated if there is a finite set
D\subset\bigsqcup_{S\in\mathbf{FI}}A(S)
which is not contained in any proper subalgebra of
A
. If such
D
exists, we say that
A
is generated by
D
.

Definition 2.2.2
.

Let
A
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over
K
. For
S\in\mathbf{FI}
and

f\in A(S)
, we call the minimal
n
such that
f
lies in

A(\pi)A([n])
for some injective map
\pi:[n]\rightarrow S
, the width of
f
, denoted
w(f)
.

Definition 2.2.3
.

An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
is generated in width

\leq d
if there exists a collection
(f_{i}\in A(S_{i}))_{i\in I}
of elements of width
\leq d
that generates
A
.

Definition 2.2.4
.

An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras
A
over
K
is said to be finitely generated in width
\leq d
if
A
is finitely generated and generated in width
\leq d
.

Remark 2.2.5
.

It is straightforward to see that this is equivalent to the condition that

A
is generated by a finite collection of elements
f_{i}\in A(S_{i})

of width
\leq d
.

The following
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B_{d}
is a building block for
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras that are finitely generated in width
\leq d
.

Definition 2.2.6
.

For a non-negative integer
d
, denote by
B_{d}
the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over a ring
K
that maps a finite set
S
to a
K
-algebra

\displaystyle B_{d}(S)=K[y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}:i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}\in S\,\,\text{and}\,\,i_{j}\neq i_{l}\,\,\text{when}\,\,j\neq l]

and for an injection
\sigma:S\rightarrow T
, the

K
-algebra homomorphism
B_{d}(\sigma)
is determined by

B_{d}(\sigma)(y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}):=y_{\sigma(i_{1}),\sigma(i_{2}),...,\sigma(i_{d})}
. It is easy to
see that
B_{d}
is generated by the single element

y_{1,2,...,d}\in B_{d}([d])
, of width
d
.

Remark 2.2.7
.

(1)

We think of the elements of
\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}
as
S\times S\times...\times S
-tensors of which only the entries outside the big diagonal
\{(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}):\exists\,\,j<l,i_{j}=i_{l}\}
are given. We call these tensors “off-diagonal tensors”.

(2)

It is easy to show that the tensor product of such algebras is again finitely generated in width at most the largest of the relevant
d
.

(3)

For fixed non-negative integers
d,k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}
,
B[k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}]
denotes
the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
\bigotimes_{i=0}^{d}B_{i}^{\otimes k_{i}}

d
.

Definition 2.2.8
.

An
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme of finite type over
K
is the spectrum of a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over
K
.

Definition 2.2.9
.

A width-
d

\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme over
K
is the spectrum of an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over
K
that is generated in width
\leq d
.

Definition 2.2.10
.

A width-
d

\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme of finite type over
K
is the
spectrum of an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra over
K
that is finitely
generated in width
\leq d
.

We record the following lemmas for the later use.

Lemma 2.2.11
.

Let
A
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra. Then any homomorphism
\varphi:B_{d}\rightarrow A
of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras is completely determined by the image of
y_{1,2,...,d}\in B_{d}([d])
. Moreover,
B_{d}
has the following universal property: if
a\in A([d])
is any element of
A
, then there exists a unique homomorphism
\varphi:B_{d}\rightarrow A
such that
\varphi(y_{1,2,...,d})=a
.

Proof.

Let
y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}
be a variable in
B_{d}(S)
where

i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}\in S
. Let
\sigma:[d]\rightarrow S
be
the injection that sends
j
to
i_{j}
. Then we have

\varphi(y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}})=\varphi(B_{d}(\sigma)y_{1,2,...,d})=A(\sigma)\varphi(y_{1,2,...,d}).

Thus the image of
y_{1,2,...,d}
completely determines the map
\varphi
.

To prove the last statement, for
i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\in S
distinct,
define
\varphi(y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}):=A(\sigma)a
, where
\sigma:[d]\to S

is the map that sends
j
to
i_{j}
, and extend this to a
homomorphism
B_{d}(S)\to A(S)
in the unique manner. A
straightforward computation shows that this does, indeed,
define an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra homomorphism
B\to A
with

\varphi(y_{1,\ldots,d})=a
.
∎

Remark 2.2.12
.

Lemma
2.2.11
implies that the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B[k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}]
has the same universal property when
a
is replaced by a tuple in
\prod_{e=0}^{d}A([e])^{k_{e}}
.

Lemma 2.2.13
.

Let
A
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra that is finitely generated in width at most
d
. Then there exists a surjective homomorphism
\varphi:B[k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}]\rightarrow A
for some non-negative integers
k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
k_{i}
be the number of generators of width
i
. Assume

h_{1}^{(i)},h_{2}^{(i)},...,h_{k_{i}}^{(i)}\in A([i])
are
generators of width
i
. Consider the unique
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi_{i}:B_{i}^{\otimes k_{i}}\rightarrow A
that sends

y_{1,2,...,i}^{(l)}\in B_{i}([i])^{\otimes k_{i}}
to

h_{l}^{(i)}
for
l=1,2,...,k_{i}
. Then
\varphi
is surjective
by construction. Then the tensor map
\bigotimes_{i=0}^{d}:B[k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}]\rightarrow A
is the required surjective morphism.
∎

Remark 2.2.14
.

Dually,
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
embeds in a finite product of affine spaces of off-diagonal tensors.

2.3.
Shifting

The idea of shifing an
\mathbf{FI}
-structure over a finite set goes back to

[
CEF15
]
. It has also been used effectively
in
[
DEF22
]
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
T_{0}
be a finite set. Then
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}:\mathbf{FI}\to\mathbf{FI}
is the functor that sends a finite set
T
to the disjoint union
T_{0}\sqcup T
and an injection
\sigma:T\rightarrow S
to
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}\sigma:T_{0}\sqcup T\to T_{0}\sqcup S
that is the identity on
T_{0}
and equal to
\sigma
on
T
. For an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
we write
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}A:=A\circ\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}
and for the
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
over
K
we write
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}X:=X\circ\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}=\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}A)
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

If
A
is finitely generated in width at most
d
, then so is
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}A
; and hence, if
X
is a width
\leq d

\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme of finite type over
K
, then so is
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}X
.

Furthermore, for a homomorphism
\varphi:A\to B
of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras
over
K
, we write
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}\varphi
for the morphism
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}A\to\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}B
that sends
T
to
\varphi(T_{0}\sqcup T)
, and similarly for morphisms of
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes. A straightforward check shows that
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}
is a covariant functor from
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras over
K

into itself and from
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes over
K
into itself.

Lemma 2.3.3
.

For a fixed finite set
T_{0}
,
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}B[k_{0},k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{d}]
is isomorphic to
B[k^{\prime}_{0},k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2},...,k^{\prime}_{d-1},k_{d}]
for some non-negative integers
k_{0}^{\prime},k_{1}^{\prime},...,k_{d-1}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We have
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}(A\otimes C)\cong(\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}A)\otimes(\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}C)
, for any
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras
A
and
C
. So it suffices to show that
B_{d}
is isomorphic to
B[k_{0}^{\prime},k_{1}^{\prime},...,k_{d-1}^{\prime},1]
. Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}B_{d}(T)=K[y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}:i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}\in T_{0}\sqcup T\,\,\text{and}\,\,i_{l}\neq i_{m}\,\,\text{when}\,\,l\neq m]

\displaystyle\cong\left(\bigotimes_{e=0}^{d-1}B_{e}(T)^{\otimes\binom{d}{e}\times|T_{0}|\times(|T_{0}|-1)\times...\times(|T_{0}|-d+e+1)}\right)\otimes(B_{d}(T)),

where the exponent counts the number of ways of filling
d-e

of the
d
index positions with elements of
T_{0}
.
Thus
\operatorname{Sh}_{T_{0}}B_{d}\cong B[k^{\prime}_{0},k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2},...,k^{\prime}_{d-1},1]
.
∎

3.
Noetherianity

3.1.
Noetherianity for
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras and

\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes

Definition 3.1.1
.

Given an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over
K
, we define its direct limit

\overrightarrow{A}=\underrightarrow{\lim}\,A([n])

using the direct system
(A([n]),A(i_{m,n}))
, where
m\leq n
and

i_{m,n}:[m]\rightarrow[n]
is the natural inclusion map.

Dually, given an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
X
over
K
, we define its inverse limit

\overleftarrow{X}=\underleftarrow{\lim}\,X([n])
using the inverse system

(X([n]),X(i_{m,n}))
, where
m\leq n
and
i_{m,n}:[m]\rightarrow[n]
is the natural inclusion map.

If
A
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra, then there is an induced action
of the group
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{Sym}(n)
, where
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
denotes the symmetric group on
n
letters and is naturally embedded into
\operatorname{Sym}(n+1)
as the stabilizer of
\{n+1\}
, on the direct limit
\overrightarrow{A}
.
An
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra is called Noetherian if it satisfies the ascending chain condition on
\mathbf{FI}
-ideals. It is well known that if an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
is Noetherian then its direct limit
\overrightarrow{A}
is
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-Noetherian, i.e.,it satisfies the ascending chain condition on
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
stable ideals.

Duall, an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
X
is called Noetherian if it
satisfies the descending chain condition on closed

\mathbf{FI}
-subschemes, and
topologically Noetherian
if it
satisfies the descending chain condition on closed and
reduced

\mathbf{FI}
-subschemes. Again the inverse limit

\overleftarrow{X}
of a topologically Noetherian

\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
X
is topologically

\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-Noetherian i.e. it satisfies the descending
chain condition on
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
stable closed reduced
subschemes. To save words, from now on, we will just use the word “closed subset” to mean
“reduced subscheme”.

Example 3.1.2
.

The direct limit of the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B_{1}^{\otimes k}
is (isomorphic with) the polynomial ring
K[x_{i,j}:i\in[k],j\in{\mathbb{N}}]
with the following induced action:
\sigma(x_{i,j})=x_{i,\sigma(j)}
where
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
.

The inverse limit of the
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
\operatorname{Spec}B_{1}^{\otimes k}
is the space of
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices where
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
acts by permuting columns of such matrices.

Theorem 3.1.3
.

[
AH07
,
Coh67
,
Coh87
,
HS12
]

Every
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
over a Noetherian ring
K
that is finitely generated in width
\leq 1
is Noetherian, i.e., if
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq\cdots
is an ascending chain of ideals in
A
, then
I_{n}=I_{n+1}
for all
n\gg 0
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

In fact,
A
is Noetherian in a stronger sense: any finitely generated
A
-module satisfies the ascending chain condition on submodules
[
NR19
, Theorem 6.15]
.

It is easy to see that if an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
is Noetherian, then the
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
X
defined by
A
is topologically Noetherian.

Example 3.1.5
.

The
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B_{1}^{\otimes k}
over a Noetherian ring
K
is Noetherian because it is finitely generated in width at most 1. So the polynomial ring
K[x_{i,j}:i\in[k],j\in{\mathbb{N}}]
is
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-Noetherian.

Dually, the
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme
\operatorname{Spec}B_{1}^{\otimes k}
over a Noetherian ring
K
is topologically Noetherian. Thus its inverse limit, the space of
k\times{\mathbb{N}}
-matrices, is
\operatorname{Sym}(\infty)
-Noetherian.

For the (easy) proofs of the following lemmas we refer to
[
DK14
, Section 5]
.

Lemma 3.1.6
.

Let
X
be an
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-scheme that is topologically Noetherian. Then any closed subset of
X
is topologically Noetherian with respect to the induced topology.

Lemma 3.1.7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be topologically Noetherian
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes. Then the disjoint union
X\sqcup Y
defined by
X\sqcup Y(S)=X(S)\sqcup Y(S)
is topologically Noetherian with respect to the disjoint union topology.

Lemma 3.1.8
.

Let
\psi
be a homomorphism of
\mathbf{FI^{op}}
-schemes from
X
to
Y
and
X
is topologically Noetherian. Then
\operatorname{Im}(\psi)
is topologically Noetherian with respect to the topology induced from
Y
.

4.
Reduction to the Case of Free FI-algebras

This section is devoted to the reduction of our main theorem to the case of free
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras.

4.1.
Making
B
free

Here we introduce the following useful notations. Let
A
be
an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra. By
f\in A
we mean that there exists
S\in\mathbf{FI}
such that
f\in A(S)
. Similarly, by
p\in\operatorname{Spec}A
we mean that there exists
T\in\mathbf{FI}
such that
p\in\operatorname{Spec}A(T)
. By
f(p)=0
we mean that for all
\sigma:S\rightarrow T
injections,
(\sigma f)(p)=0
.

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Let
\pi:C\rightarrow B
and
\varphi:B\rightarrow A
be homomorphisms of
FI
-algebras, where
\pi
is surjective. If the image closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}
of
\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}A\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}C
is defined by finitely many elements in
C
, then the image closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
of
\varphi^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}A\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}B
is also defined by finitely many elements in
B
. If, moreover,
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}
is topologically Noetherian, then
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
is also topologically Noetherian.

Proof.

Suppose
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{m}\in C
define the image closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}
. We claim that their images
\pi f_{1},\pi f_{2},...,\pi f_{m}\in B
define the image closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
. First note that
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}=\pi^{*}(\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*}))}
as
\pi^{*}
is a closed embedding. Let
p\in\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
, then
\pi f_{i}(p)=f_{i}(\pi^{*}p)=0
. Thus for all
i=1,2,...,m
we have that
\pi f_{i}
vanishes on
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
.

Conversely, pick
p\in\operatorname{Spec}B
such that
p
is in the
closed subset defined by
\pi f_{1},\pi f_{2},...,\pi f_{m}
, then
\pi^{*}p
lies in the variety defined by
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{m}
, that is, by our supposition,
\pi^{*}p\in\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}
. Now let
g
be an element of
{\rm Ker}\,(\varphi)
. By the surjectivity of
\pi
, there exists
g^{\prime}\in C
such that
\pi g^{\prime}=g
, which implies that
g^{\prime}\in{\rm Ker}\,(\varphi\circ\pi)
. Hence
g^{\prime}
vanishes on
\pi^{*}(p)
. So
g(p)=\pi g^{\prime}(p)=g^{\prime}(\pi^{*}(p))=0
. Hence
p\in\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
.

The last statement is straight forward. Every descending
chain of closed subsets in
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
maps
under
\pi^{*}
to a descending chain of closed subsets in

\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\pi^{*}\circ\varphi^{*})}
because
\pi^{*}
is a
closed embedding. The latter chain stabilizes and, and when
it does, so does the former chain. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1.2
.

Let
B
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra that is finitely generated in
width at most
d
and
\varphi:B\rightarrow A
be a homorphism
of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras. Then, by Lemma
2.2.13
, there exists a surjective map
\pi
from
B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]
to
B
. Thus Proposition
4.1.1
implies that it suffices to prove the main theorem for the
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]
instead of
B
, i.e. without loss of generality, in the main theorem, we can assume that
B
is free.

4.2.
Making
A
free, as well

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
\varphi:B\rightarrow A
,
\psi:B\rightarrow A^{\prime}
and
\pi:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A
be homomorphisms of finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras such that
\pi\circ\psi=\varphi
. If the closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})}
of the image of
\psi^{*}
is topologically Noetherian, then the closure
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
of the image of
\varphi^{*}
is also topologically Noetherian. If, moreover,
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})}
is defined by finitely many elements in
B
, then
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
is also defined by finitely many elements in
B
.

Proof.

The first statement is a straight-forward implication of the fact that a closed subspace of a topologically Noetherian space is topologically Noetherian. Since
{\rm Ker}\,(\psi)
is contained in
{\rm Ker}\,(\varphi)
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}=V_{\operatorname{Spec}B}({\rm Ker}\,(\varphi))=V_{\operatorname{Spec}B}({\rm Ker}\,(\pi\circ\psi))

is a closed subset of
V_{\operatorname{Spec}B}({\rm Ker}\,(\psi))=\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})}
. The latter is topologically Noetherian, hence so is the former.

For the last statement, observe that
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
, being a closed subset of a topologically Noetherian space
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})}
, is defined by finitely many elements in the coordinate ring
K[\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})}]=B/I
, where
I=I(\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\psi^{*})})
is an ideal generated by finitely many elements in
B
. This implies that
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}
is defined by finitely many elements in
B
.
∎

Remark 4.2.2
.

Let
A
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra that is finitely generated in
width at most
1
and let
\varphi:B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]\rightarrow A
be a morphism. Then by Lemma

2.2.13
there is a surjective map
\pi:B[k^{\prime}_{0},k^{\prime}_{1}]\rightarrow A
. By universality of
B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]
(see
Remark
2.2.12
), there exists (not necessarily
unique) map
\psi:B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]\rightarrow B[k^{\prime}_{0},k^{\prime}_{1}]
such that
\varphi=\pi\circ\psi
. Thus Proposition
4.2.1
implies that it suffices to prove the main theorem for
B[k^{\prime}_{0},k^{\prime}_{1}]
instead of
A
i.e. without lost of generality, in the main theorem, we can assume that
A
is free.

5.
Proof of the Main Theorem

5.1.
Flattening

For a more detailed description of flattening see
[
DK14
]
.

Definition 5.1.1
.

Let
V=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}
be a tensor product of vector spaces
V_{j}
over a field
L
. Then for each
i\in[n]
, there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\flat_{i}:V\rightarrow\left(\bigotimes_{j\neq i}V_{j}\right)\otimes V_{i}.

For a
t\in V
the image
\flat_{i}(t)
is a
2
-tensor
called a flattening of
t
.

A tensor has rank
1
if it can be written as a tensor product of vectors
v_{i}\in V_{i}
.
A tensor
t
has rank
l
if
l
is the minimum number of rank 1 tensors that sum to
t
. For
2
-tensors, the tensor rank is equal to the ordinary matrix rank.

Remark 5.1.2
.

Flattening does not increase the rank of a tensor, i.e.
rk(\flat_{i}(t))\leq rk(t)
.

5.2.
Tensors

Define an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
C_{d}
over a ring
K
that sends a finite set
S
to the
K
-algebra,

\displaystyle C_{d}(S)=K[y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}:i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}\in S],

and an injection
\sigma:S\rightarrow T
to
C_{d}(\sigma):C_{d}(S)\rightarrow C_{d}(T)
maps
y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}
to
y_{\sigma(i_{1}),\sigma(i_{2}),...,\sigma(i_{d})}
.

Remark 5.2.1
.

For a finite set
S
,
\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}(S)
is the set of
S\times S\times...\times S
-tensors, now including entries on the big diagonal.

There is a natural inclusion
\iota:B_{d}\rightarrow C_{d}
. Dually,
we have the projection map
\iota^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}
. For a tensor
t\in\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}(S)
we think of the
flattening
\flat_{i}(t)
as a matrix whose rows and columns
are labeled by tuples in
S^{[d]\setminus\{i\}}
and elements in
S
respectively.

Definition 5.2.2
.

A tuple
(x_{i})_{i\in[d]}
in
S^{d}
is called a distinct
value tuple if
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
when
i\neq j
. Denote by
DS^{d}
the set of distinct value tuples in
S^{d}
.

Definition 5.2.3
.

An off-diagonal
l\times l
sub-matrix of
\flat_{i}(t)
is a
u_{1}\times u_{2}
-sub-matrix where
u_{1}\subset DS^{d-1}
,
u_{2}\subset S
such that
u_{1}\times u_{2}\subset DS^{d}
and
|u_{1}|=|u_{2}|=l
.
By an off-diagonal
l\times l
-sub-determinant of
\flat_{i}(t)
, we mean the determinant of an off-diagonal
l\times l
-sub-matrix of
\flat_{i}(t)
.

Fix non-negative integers
l
and
d
. Let
Z_{d,l}
be the subset of
\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}
defined as follows: for all
S\in\mathbf{FI}
,
Z_{d,l}(S)
is the variety defined by all off-diagonal
(l+1)\times(l+1)
-sub-determinants of
\flat_{i}(y),
for all
i\in[d]
and
y\in\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}(S)
. The following lemma shows that
Z_{d,l}
is defined by finitely many equations in
B_{d}
.

Lemma 5.2.4
.

The subset
Z_{d,l}
of
\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}
is defined by finitely many equations in
B_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite set, let
y\in\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}\left(S\right)
and
i\in\left[d\right]
be arbitrary. Let
h
be an arbitrary off-diagonal
\left(l+1\right)\times\left(l+1\right)
-subdeterminant of the flattening
\flat_{i}\left(y\right)
. The subdeterminant
h
is given by indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{l+1}\in S
and distinct value tuples
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l+1}\in DS^{d-1}
such that none of the indices
i_{j}
occur in any of the
\alpha_{k}
and such that all the
\alpha_{k}
are distinct.

Let
S^{\prime}
be the subset of
S
consisting of all the
entries of the
i_{j}
and
\alpha_{k}
. Then
|S^{\prime}|\leq\left(d-1\right)\cdot\left(l+1\right)+l+1=d\cdot\left(l+1\right)
. Set
n=|S^{\prime}|
and choose a
bijection
\rho\colon\left[n\right]\rightarrow S^{\prime}
.
Define
\alpha^{\prime}_{j}=\rho^{-1}\circ\alpha_{j}
and

i^{\prime}_{j}=\rho^{-1}(i_{j})
for all
j\in\left[l+1\right]
, where we consider the
\alpha_{j}
as maps from

\left[d-1\right]
to
S^{\prime}
.

Then
h=B_{d}\left(\rho\right)h^{\prime}
, where
h^{\prime}
is the off-diagonal subdeterminant of the flattening
\flat_{i}\left(\left(y_{\beta}\right)_{\beta\in D\left[n\right]^{d-1}}\right)
given by columns
i^{\prime}_{1},\dots,i^{\prime}_{l+1}
and rows
\alpha^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\alpha^{\prime}_{l+1}
.
Since
n
can take on at most
d\cdot\left(l+1\right)
values, this construction yields finitely many polynomials
h^{\prime}
. These polynomials define
Z_{d,l}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2.5
.

Let
\varphi:B_{d}\rightarrow B_{1}^{\otimes k}
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra homomorphism. Then
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}\subset Z_{d,l}
for some
l
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2.11
, the map
\varphi
is determined by the image of
y_{1,2,...,d}\in B_{d}([d])
.
First we suppose that
y_{1,2,...,d}\in B_{d}([d])
is mapped under
\varphi
to a monomial
M=\prod_{i\in[k],j\in[d]}x_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}\in B_{1}^{\otimes k}([d])
.
Dually,
a=(a_{i,j})_{i\in[k],j\in S}\in\operatorname{Spec}B_{1}(S)^{\otimes k}
is mapped to the projection (via

\iota^{*}
) of the following tensor:

\displaystyle(a_{j_{1},j_{2},...,j_{d}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,j_{1}}^{\alpha_{i,1}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,j_{2}}^{\alpha_{i,2}}\cdots\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,j_{d}}^{\alpha_{i,d}})_{j_{1},j_{2},...,j_{d}\in S}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}(\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,s}^{\alpha_{i,j}})_{s\in S}\in\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}(S),

that is,
\varphi^{*}(a)
is the projection of a rank
\leq 1
tensor. Thus
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})
is contained in the projection of rank
\leq 1
tensors.

Now, if
y_{1,2,...d}\in B_{d}([d])
is mapped under
\varphi
to

f=\sum_{c=1}^{l}M_{c}
which is a sum of, say
l
,
monomials in the variables
x_{i,j}
where
1\leq i\leq k

and
1\leq j\leq d
, then the subadditivity of tensor rank
implies that
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})
lies in the projection
i^{*}
of
tensors having rank at most
l
. Since flattening does not
increase rank, this implies
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})\subset Z_{d,l}
.
Hence
\overline{\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})}\subset Z_{d,l}
.
∎

Remark 5.2.6
.

Let
\varphi:B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]\rightarrow B_{1}^{\otimes k}
be an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra homomorphism. By universality of
B[k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{d}]
,
\varphi=\bigotimes_{e=0}^{d}(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k_{e}}\varphi_{e,i})
where,
\varphi_{e,i}:B_{e}\rightarrow B_{1}^{\otimes k}
for all
e
and for all

, and
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi_{e,i}^{*})\subset Z_{e,l_{e,i}}
for some

. Let

be the maximum of the set
\{l_{e,i}:0\leq e\leq d\,\,\text{and}\,\,0\leq i\leq k_{e}\}
. Then
\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{*})\subset\prod_{e=0}^{d}Z_{e,l}^{k_{e}}
.

For a commutative ring
R
with
1
, and a prime ideal
p\in\operatorname{Spec}R
,
\kappa(p)
denotes the fraction field of the integral domain
R/p
.

Lemma 5.2.7
.

Let
Y
be a subset of
\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}
. Suppose that there exists
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
S
, for all
p\in Y(S)
, the tensor rank of
p
over the field
\kappa(p)
is at most
N
. Then there exist an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra
A
that is finitely generated in width at most
1
and a map
\varphi_{N}^{*}:\operatorname{Spec}A\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}
such that
Y\subset\operatorname{Im}(\varphi_{N}^{*})
.

In this lemma,
Y(S)
is an arbitrary subset of
\operatorname{Spec}(C(S))
, in such
a manner that for any
\pi\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbf{FI}}(S,T)
, the map
C(\pi)^{*}
maps

Y(T)
into

.

Proof.

Define a homomorphism of
\mathbf{FI}
-algebras
\varphi_{N}:C_{d}\rightarrow A=C_{1}^{\otimes[N]\times[d]}
by sending

y_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}
to
\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j,1,i_{1}}x_{j,2,i_{2}}\cdots x_{j,d,i_{d}}
. By
assumption, for each
S
and for every

p=(p_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}})_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}\in S}\in Y(S)
,
there exists
(q_{j,l,i_{l}})_{(j,l,i_{l})\in[N]\times[d]\times S}\in\operatorname{Spec}C_{1}^{\otimes[N]\times[d]}(S)(\kappa(p))
such that
p_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{d}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}q_{j,1,i_{1}}q_{j,2,i_{2}}\cdots q_{j,d,i_{d}}
.
Hence
Y\subset\operatorname{Im}(\varphi_{N}^{*})
.
∎

Lemma 5.2.8
.

Let
Z
be a subset of
\operatorname{Spec}B_{d}
. Suppose that there
exists
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all

and for all

there exists
\tilde{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}C_{d}(S)
such that the tensor rank of
\tilde{p}
over the

\kappa(\tilde{p})
is at most

and
\iota^{*}(\tilde{p})=p
where
\iota:B_{d}\rightarrow C_{d}
is the inclusion map. Then there exists a map
\psi:B_{d}\rightarrow A
such that

, where

is an
\mathbf{FI}
-algebra that is finitely generated in width at most 1.

Remark 5.2.9
.

The condition on the off-diagonal tensor

is that it can be completed to a (full) tensor
\tilde{p}
of rank
\leq N
, where

does not depend on

or

.

Proof.

Define
Y:=\{\tilde{p}\in(i^{*})^{-1}(Z):\,\,\text{tensor rank of}\,\,\tilde{p}\,\,\text{over}\,\,\kappa(\tilde{p})\leq N\}
. By Lemma
5.2.7
,

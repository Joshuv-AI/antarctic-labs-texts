FeynGrav : FeynCalc extension for gravity amplitudes
B. Latosh

Abstract
Package “FeynGrav” which provides a framework to operate with Feynman rules for gravity within “FeynCalc” is presented. We present a framework to deal with Feynman rules for general relativity and non-supersymmetric matter minimally coupled to gravity. Applicability of the package is tested with 2 → 2 2\to 2 on-shell tree level graviton scattering, polarization operators, and one-loop scalar-gravitational interaction structure.

This paper has two objectives. The first one is to present a (relatively) simple algorithm to obtain expressions for Feynman rules for gravity in a form suitable for implementation in a computer algebra system. The second one is to present “FeynGrav”, an extension for FeynCalc package that implements the given algorithm. We obtain Feynman rules for general relativity and for non-supersymmetric minimally coupled scalars, massless vectors, and Dirac fermions. We believe that the discussed mathematical framework also provides a way to obtain Feynman rules in other models of gravity.

Tensor framework

Feynman rules for gravity are obtained within a framework of perturbative effective gravity. This framework is based on the following assumptions. Firstly, the model under consideration is assumed to be applicable only below the Planck scale. Secondly, the gravity is associated with small metric perturbations
h_{\mu\nu}
about the flat background spacetime
\eta_{\mu\nu}
. To some extend such a theory can be viewed as a gauge theory of massless rank-
2
symmetric tensor
h_{\mu\nu}
.

The complete spacetime metric is composed from both background and perturbations:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa\,h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(1)

Here
\kappa
is related with the Newton constant
G_{N}
as follows:

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}=32\pi\,G_{N}.

(2)

Expression (
1
) is exact and does not contain terms of higher orders in
\kappa
. Other geometric quantities, for instance,
\sqrt{-g}
and
g^{\mu\nu}
, are infinite series in
\kappa
. The tensor framework defined further is designed to make the tensor structure of such expansions explicit.

Definition 1
.

I_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}\nu_{n}}

n
-the order has
n

\eta_{\nu_{1}\mu_{2}}\eta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{3}}\cdots\eta_{\nu_{n}\mu_{1}}
by a consecutive symmetrization with respect to indices in each pair
(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})
.

Following comments on the definition are due. Firstly, the given definition is symmetric with respect to permutations of indices within each index pair
(\mu_{i},\nu_{i})
, but it is not symmetric with respect to permutations of such pairs. This is a deliberate decision as for the most part of calculations it is not required to operate with expressions enjoying such a symmetry. Secondly, the symmetrization with respect to

(3)

3
General relativity

General relativity is given by the well known action

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=2,m=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\cfrac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\,R\right].

(19)

For the sake of distinguishment we call (
19
) the Hilbert parameterization. This parameterization is not suitable for perturbative treatment. It shows that gravity is, indeed, a geometry of a curved spacetime, but its perturbative structure remains implicit. To make the perturbative structure explicit a full derivative shall be separated from (
19
).

Theorem 5
.

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}R=

\displaystyle\penalty\ \cfrac{1}{4}\,\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}\left[\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\nu}g_{\rho\sigma}-\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\rho}\,\partial_{\nu}g_{\beta\sigma}+2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\rho}\,\partial_{\alpha}g_{\nu\sigma}-2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\alpha}\,\partial_{\beta}g_{\rho\sigma}\right]

(20)

\displaystyle\penalty\ +\text{full derivative}\,.

To prove this theorem one should, firstly, express the scalar curvature via Christoffel symbols and, secondly, perform an integration by parts:

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\,R=\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\left[\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\rho}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma}-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\rho}\right]+\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\alpha\beta}\,\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}-\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu\sigma}\right).

(21)

Lastly, Christoffel symbols should be expressed via the metric:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}=\cfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\alpha\beta}\left[\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\beta\mu}-\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\right].

(22)

We shall note that this theorem is well known
[
24
,
25
]
, so we do not discuss its proof in details. From here on we omit all surface terms.

This theorem provides a way to write action (
19
) in the following form which we call the Einstein parameterization:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=2,m=0}\!=\!\!\!\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}\left[-\cfrac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_{\nu}g_{\rho\sigma}-\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\rho}\,\partial_{\nu}g_{\beta\sigma}+2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\rho}\,\partial_{\alpha}g_{\nu\sigma}-2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\alpha}\,\partial_{\beta}g_{\rho\sigma}\right)\right].

(23)

For the perturbative expansion (
1
) this formula is reduced further because the background metric has vanishing derivatives:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=2,m=0}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}\left[-\cfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\rho\sigma}-\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\rho}\partial_{\nu}h_{\beta\sigma}+2\,\partial_{\mu}h_{\beta\rho}\partial_{\alpha}h_{\nu\sigma}-2\,\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\rho\sigma}\right)\right].

(24)

\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}

Theorem 6
.

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}h_{\rho\sigma}-\partial_{\mu}h_{\alpha\rho}\partial_{\nu}h_{\beta\sigma}+2\,\partial_{\mu}h_{\beta\rho}\partial_{\alpha}h_{\nu\sigma}-2\,\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\beta}h_{\rho\sigma}

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{(h)}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta\rho\sigma|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}=

\displaystyle-\eta_{\mu\lambda_{1}}\eta_{\nu\lambda_{2}}I_{\alpha\beta\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}I_{\rho\sigma\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}+\eta_{\mu\lambda_{1}}\eta_{\nu\lambda_{2}}I_{\alpha\rho\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}I_{\beta\sigma\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle-2\,\eta_{\mu\lambda_{1}}\eta_{\alpha\lambda_{2}}I_{\beta\rho\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}I_{\nu\sigma\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}+2\,\eta_{\mu\lambda_{1}}\eta_{\beta\lambda_{2}}I_{\nu\alpha\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}I_{\rho\sigma\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}\,.

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=2,m=0}=

\displaystyle\int\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\cfrac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\cfrac{d^{4}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{d^{4}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\times\kappa^{n}\mathcal{C}_{III}^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{3}|\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}\,h{}_{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}^{(k_{1})}\cdots h{}_{\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}^{(k_{n})}\,\penalty\ \mathcal{T}^{(h)}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{3}|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\,(p_{1})^{\lambda_{1}}(p_{2})^{\lambda_{2}}\,h{}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}_{(p_{1})}h^{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}_{(p_{2})}\Bigg].

This theorem does not yet provide expressions for the corresponding vertices as a certain symmetrization must be performed. Graviton vertices are quadratic in momenta due to the presence of derivatives. The corresponding expressions for interaction vertices are also quadratic in momenta, but they must be symmetric with respect to gravitons. Therefore the following symmetrization must be performed.

Theorem 8
.

Expression
(\mathfrak{V}^{(n)})^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}
that describes interaction of
n\geq 3
gravitons reads

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{V}^{(n)})^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})=i\,\kappa^{n}\left[\mathcal{C}_{III}^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{3}|\rho_{3}\cdots\sigma_{n}}\penalty\ \mathcal{T}^{(h)}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{3}|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\rho_{2}\sigma_{2}}\,(p_{1})^{\lambda_{1}}(p_{2})^{\lambda_{2}}+\cdots\right].

(28)

Here
\cdots
notes a summation over all possible permutations of
(p_{i},\rho_{i},\sigma_{i})
. Therefore, there are
n!
term in this sum;
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}
are Lorentz indices of
i
-th graviton;
p_{i}
is the corresponding graviton momentum.

This theorem provides a way to calculate rules for graviton interaction vertices at any order.

4
Scalar field

A scalar field with a non-vanishing mass
m
minimally coupled to gravity is described by the following action:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=0,m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\cfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\cfrac{m^{2}}{2}\,\phi^{2}\right]=\int d^{4}x\left[\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\cfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi-\sqrt{-g}\,\cfrac{m^{2}}{2}\,\phi^{2}\right].

(29)

It contain factors
\sqrt{-g}
and
\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}
, but perturbative expansions for them are already obtained. Another part of the action also contains derivatives. The structure of this part in the momentum representation is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}{}^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})\,\phi(p_{1})\,\phi(p_{2})

(30)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}{}^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle-\cfrac{1}{2}\,I_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,(p_{1})^{\alpha}(p_{2})^{\beta}\,.

(31)

Consequently, action (
29
) takes the following form.

Theorem 10
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=0,m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\int\cfrac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\cfrac{d^{4}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{d^{4}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\times\kappa^{n}\,\Bigg[\mathcal{T}^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})\,\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\mu\nu|\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}-\cfrac{m^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{C}^{\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}\Bigg]\,h{}_{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}(k_{1})\cdots h{}_{\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}(k_{n})\,\phi(p_{1})\,\phi(p_{2}).

The obtained expression by the construction is symmetric with respect both to gravitons and scalar, therefore no additional symmetrization shall be performed. At the same time an additional factor “2” shall be added. It appears because there are two equivalent ways to couple the given vertex to scalar lines. The corresponding Feynman rules are given by the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Expressions
\mathfrak{V}^{(n)}_{s=0,m\not=0}
that corresponds to a vertex describing interaction between two scalar particles and
n
gravitons in given by the following expression:

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{V}_{s=0,m\not=0}^{(n)}){}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}(p_{1},p_{2})=

\displaystyle i\,\kappa^{n}\,2\,\left(\mathcal{T}^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})\,\mathcal{C}_{I}^{\mu\nu|\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}-\cfrac{m^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{C}^{\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}\right).

(33)

The massless case is recovered in
m\to 0
limit.

5
Massless vector field

A single massless vector field is described by the following action:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=1,m=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}\,\left[-\cfrac{1}{4}\,F_{\mu\alpha}\,F_{\nu\beta}\right].

(34)

The structure of
\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}
is obtained above. The structure of terms containing derivatives is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 12
.

\displaystyle-\cfrac{1}{4}\,F_{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu\beta}\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}{}^{(A)}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\rho\sigma}(p_{1},p_{2})\,A^{\rho}(p_{1})\,A^{\sigma}(p_{2})

(35)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{(A)}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\rho\sigma}(p_{1},p_{2})=\cfrac{1}{4}\,(p_{1})^{\lambda}(p_{2})^{\tau}\left[I_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}I_{\alpha\beta\lambda\tau}+I_{\mu\nu\lambda\tau}I_{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}-I_{\mu\nu\rho\tau}I_{\alpha\beta\sigma\lambda}-I_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}I_{\alpha\beta\rho\tau}\right].

(36)

Therefore the structure of (
34
) is given by the next theorem.

Theorem 13
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=1,m=0}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\int\Bigg[\cfrac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\cfrac{d^{4}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{d^{4}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle\times\kappa^{n}\,\mathcal{T}^{(A)}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\lambda\tau}(p_{1},p_{2})\,\mathcal{C}_{II}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}\,h{}_{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}}^{(k_{1})}\cdots h{}_{\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}^{(k_{n})}\,A^{\lambda}(p_{1})\,A^{\tau}(p_{2})\Bigg].

In full analogy with the previous case the obtained expression is symmetric both with respect to gravitons and vectors. An additional factor “2” shall also be recovered as there are two equivalent ways to couple the vertex to vector lines. The expression shall be used to obtain the Feynman rules without any additional symmetrization.

Theorem 14
.

Expressions
\mathfrak{V}^{(n)}_{s=1,m=0}
corresponding to a vertex describing interaction of two massless vector particles and
n
gravitons in given by the following formula.

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{V}_{s=1,m=0}^{(n)}){}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|}{}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}(p_{1},p_{2})=i\,\kappa^{n}\,2\,\mathcal{T}^{(A)}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\lambda\tau}(p_{1},p_{2})\mathcal{C}_{II}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta|\rho_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}.

(38)

A comment on the gauge fixing is due. As it was noted in Section
2
, to a certain degree it is possible to consider the perturbative approach to gravity as a gauge theory of a rank
2
symmetric tensor in a flat spacetime. Therefore the gauge of any matter field must be fixed after the perturbative expansions is made. The gauge of the gravitational field is fixed in accordance with that prescription, so shall be the gauge of a vector field.

6
Fermionic field

The standard way to describe fermions in a curved spacetime is based on vierbeins and
\gamma
matrices
[
26
,
27
]
. Firstly,
\gamma
matrices are introduced to construct a representation of the Lorentz algebra. Dirac matrices are subjected to the following relations

\displaystyle\{\gamma_{m},\gamma_{n}\}\overset{\text{note}}{=}\gamma_{m}\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n}\gamma_{m}

\displaystyle=2\,\eta_{mn}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{0}

\displaystyle=(\gamma^{m})^{+}\,,

(39)

and form the following representation of the Lorentz algebra

\displaystyle S_{mn}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\cfrac{i}{4}\,[\gamma_{m},\gamma_{n}]\,,

\displaystyle[S_{mn},S_{ab}]

\displaystyle=-i\left(\eta_{ma}S_{nb}-\eta_{mb}S_{na}+\eta_{nb}S_{ma}-\eta_{na}S_{mb}\right).

(40)

The standard vector representation of the Lorentz algebra is constituted by matrices
J_{mn}
:

\displaystyle\left(J_{mn}\right)_{ab}=

\displaystyle\,i\,(\eta_{ma}\eta_{nb}-\eta_{mb}\eta_{na})\,,

\displaystyle[J_{mn},J_{ab}]

\displaystyle=-i\left(\eta_{ma}J_{nb}-\eta_{mb}J_{na}+\eta_{nb}J_{ma}-\eta_{na}J_{mb}\right).

(41)

These representations are connected by the following relation:

\displaystyle[\gamma_{a},S_{mn}]=\left(J_{mn}\right)_{ab}\,\gamma^{b}\,.

(42)

It allows one to define Dirac spinors
\psi
and
\overline{\psi}=\psi^{+}\gamma^{0}
subjected to the following Lorentz group action

\displaystyle\delta\psi

\displaystyle=\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{ab}\,S_{ab}\,\psi\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\delta\overline{\psi}

\displaystyle=-\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{ab}\,\overline{\psi}S_{ab}\,,

and to construct the well-known Lorentz invariant action (in a flat spacetime)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=\int d^{4}x\left[\cfrac{i}{2}\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{m}\partial_{m}\psi-\partial_{m}\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{m}\psi\right)-m\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi\right].

(44)

Secondly, vierbeins
\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}
are used to generalize this action for the curved spacetime case. In a curved spacetime one relates an arbitrary frame with a local inertial frame via vierbein
\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}
. Its Latin index is subjected to the Lorentz transformation while the Greek index is subjected to the general coordinate transformations. Vierbeins satisfy the following normalization condition:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}_{n}{}^{\nu}\,g_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\eta_{mn},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}_{n}{}^{\nu}\,\eta^{mn}=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}.

(45)

They define anti-symmetric spin-connection
(\Gamma_{\mu})_{ab}=-(\Gamma_{\mu})_{ba}
which is related with the Christoffel symbols:

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)_{ab}=\mathfrak{e}_{a\alpha}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\beta}+\mathfrak{e}_{a\sigma}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\sigma}.

(46)

Because vierbein
\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}
relates Lorentz transformations and general coordinate transformations it shall be used to manipulate indices. This allows one to construct a generalization of
\gamma
matrices subjected to the group of general coordinate transformations:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}=\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\,\gamma^{m}\,.

(47)

Spinor transformations (
43
) are generalized as follows

\displaystyle\delta\psi

\displaystyle=\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{\mu\nu}\,S_{\mu\nu}\,\psi=\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{ab}(x)\,S_{ab}\,\psi\,,

(48)

\displaystyle\delta\overline{\psi}

\displaystyle=-\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{\mu\nu}\,\overline{\psi}\,S_{\mu\nu}=-\cfrac{i}{2}\,\omega^{ab}(x)\,\overline{\psi}\,S_{ab}\,.

This generalization simply promotes transformation (
43
) to gauge transformations.

Regular derivatives shall be replaced with covariant derivatives defined as follows:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\psi=\partial_{\mu}\psi-\cfrac{i}{2}\,\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)^{ab}\,S_{ab}\,\psi\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\overline{\psi}=\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}+\cfrac{i}{2}\,\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)^{ab}\,\overline{\psi}\,S_{ab}\,.

Covariant derivatives defined in such a way satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}(\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi)\,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\beta}\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\beta}\,\psi\,.

(50)

This construction produces the following generalization of the Dirac action

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\cfrac{i}{2}\,\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi-\nabla_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)-m\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi\right].

(51)

The rest of this section is devoted to a perturbative expansion of this action.

The following theorem express the Dirac action in terms of spin-connection.

Theorem 15
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\cfrac{i}{2}\,\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)-m\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi+\cfrac{1}{4}\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)^{ab}\overline{\psi}\{\gamma^{\mu},S_{ab}\}\psi\right]\,

(52)

To proof this theorem one shall use the definition of covariant derivatives (
49
). This expression can be simplified even further if definitions of
S_{ab}
and spin-connection (
46
) are used.

Theorem 16
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\cfrac{i}{2}\,\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right)-m\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi\right]

(53)

\displaystyle+\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\overline{\psi}(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\psi\penalty\ \cfrac{i}{8}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}.

Proof.

First of all, the definition of
S_{ab}
should be used:

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{4}\,\{\gamma^{m},S^{ab}\}=\cfrac{i}{16}\,\{\gamma^{m},[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}]\}=\cfrac{i}{8}\,\left(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b}\right).

(54)

Therefore the part containing spin-connections reads

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\psi\,\cfrac{i}{8}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)_{ab}.

(55)

Next, the definition of spin-connection (
46
) should be used:

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\psi\,\cfrac{i}{8}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\right)_{ab}

(56)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\psi\left[\cfrac{i}{16}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{a}{}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\beta}\,\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\alpha}+\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)+\cfrac{i}{8}\,\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}\right].

The part of this expression containing
\gamma
matrices is anti-symmetric with respect to indices
a
and
b
. Consequently, the term containing
\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\alpha}
vanishes and the expressions simplifies:

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\psi\left[\cfrac{i}{16}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{a}{}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\beta}\,\left(\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)+\cfrac{i}{8}\,\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}\right].

(57)

The last part of this expression cannot be canceled, but the first the terms vanish due to the symmetry:

\displaystyle(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{a}{}^{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\beta}\,\left(\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)=(\gamma^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}-\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\beta})\left(\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\alpha}-\gamma^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}+\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\beta}

\displaystyle=2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}-\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}\right)=2\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}\left((2\,g^{\mu\alpha}-\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\mu})\gamma^{\beta}-\gamma^{\alpha}(2\,g^{\mu\beta}-\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=4\,\partial_{\mu}g_{\alpha\beta}(g^{\mu\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}-g^{\mu\beta}\gamma^{\alpha})=0.

Which completes the proof.
∎

Let us highlight the fact that we did not use any perturbative expansions within this proof. In turn, within the perturbative treatment the discussed action will be simplified even further.
Firstly, let us specify the perturbative expansion of vierbeins by the following theorem. The theorem is proven in
[
11
]
, so we present it without a prove.

Theorem 17
.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{m}{}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{n}\,\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n}\,\left(h^{n}\right)_{m}{}^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}^{m}{}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{n}\,\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n}\,\left(h^{n}\right)^{m}{}_{\mu}\,.

(59)

Here
\binom{x}{y}
are binomial coefficients.

Corollary 5
.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{m\mu}=\eta_{mn}\mathfrak{e}^{n}{}_{\mu}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{n}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n}\left(h^{n}\right)_{m\mu}.

(60)

These perturbative expansions provide a way to proof the following theorem.

Theorem 18
.

On perturbative expansion (
1
)

\displaystyle(\gamma^{b}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{a}-\gamma^{a}\gamma^{m}\gamma^{b})\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}=0.

(61)

Proof.

The core of the proof is the fact that only symmetric matrices enter the vierbein perturbative expansion. Let us show this in details. Firstly, the
n
-th perturbation order of
\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}
within the Fourier representation reads:

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\right)^{(n)}\to\kappa^{n}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n}\,(h^{n})_{a\lambda}=\kappa^{n}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n}h_{a}{}^{\sigma}(k_{1})\cdots h_{\rho\lambda}(k_{n}).

(62)

The
n
-th perturbative order of
\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}
has a similar form:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}\right)^{(n)}\to i\,\sum\limits_{p=1}^{n}\left\{k_{p}\right\}\,\kappa^{n}\,\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n}h_{b}{}^{\sigma}(k_{1})\cdots h_{\rho}{}^{\lambda}(k_{n})\,.

(63)

Therefore a multiplication of
n_{1}
-th order term of
\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}
on an
n_{2}
-th order term from
\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}
results in the following expression:

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}\right)^{(n_{1})}\left(\partial_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}_{b}{}^{\lambda}\right)^{(n_{2})}\to\kappa^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\left(i\,\sum\limits_{p=1}^{n_{2}}\left\{k_{p}\right\}\right)\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{n_{1}}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n_{2}}h_{a}{}^{\sigma}(k_{1})\cdots h_{\rho\lambda}(k_{n_{1}})h_{b}{}^{\sigma^{\prime}}(k_{1})\cdots h_{\rho^{\prime}}{}^{\lambda}(k_{n}).

(64)

This expression contains a multiplication of
n_{1}+n_{2}
symmetric matrices
h_{\mu\nu}
, therefore the expression is symmetric with respect to
a\leftrightarrow b
. Consequently, its contraction with an anti-symmetric expression vanishes.
∎

Corollary 6
.

On perturbative expansion (
1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\cfrac{i}{2}\,\mathfrak{e}_{m}^{\mu}\,\left(\,\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{m}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\,\gamma^{m}\psi\right)-m\,\overline{\psi}\,\psi\right].

(65)

The perturbative structure of the Dirac action (
65
) is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\int\cfrac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\cfrac{d^{4}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\cfrac{d^{4}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta\left(p_{1}+p_{2}+\sum\limits_{i}k_{i}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle\times\kappa^{n}\,\overline{\psi}(p_{2})\left[\cfrac{1}{2}\,\gamma^{m}(p_{2}-p_{1})_{\mu}\,\mathcal{C}_{E}{}_{m}{}^{\mu\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}-m\,\mathcal{C}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}\right]\psi(p_{1}).

Here

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{E}{}_{m}{}^{\mu\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}=\operatorname{Symm}\mathop{\sum\limits_{n_{0},n_{1}=0}^{\infty}}_{n_{0}+n_{1}=n}\,\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n_{1}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{(n_{0})}{}^{\cdots}\,I_{(1+n_{1})}{}_{m}{}^{\mu\cdots}\right)^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}

(67)

and the same notations are used.

The resulted expression does not require any additional symmetrization and can be used to obtain the corresponding Feynman rules. We shall also note, that the factor “2” shall not be introduced here as there is only one way to couple this vertex to fermion lines.

Theorem 20
.

Expressions
\mathfrak{V}^{(n)}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}
that corresponds to a vertex describing interaction of two Dirac fermions and
n
gravitons in given by the following formula.

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{V}_{s=\frac{1}{2},m\not=0}^{(n)}\right){}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}(p_{1},p_{2})=i\,\kappa^{n}\,\left[\cfrac{1}{2}\,\gamma^{m}(p_{2}-p_{1})_{\mu}\,\mathcal{C}_{E}{}_{m}{}^{\mu\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}-m\,\mathcal{C}^{\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}\sigma_{n}}\right].

(68)

7
FeynGrav

The framework described in previous sections is implemented in FeynGrav package which works as an extension of FeynCalc. Its source code is publicly available
[
28
]
. The package requires FeynCalc version 9.3.1 (or higher). Installation instructions and descriptions of files are given in “README” file. Finally, file “FeynGrav_Examples.nb” contains annotated calculations presented in this paper.

Let us also explain the motivation to use FeynCalc and to comment on xAct bundle that can be used in further development of FeynGrav. FeynCalc is, perhaps, the most natural software to implement the presented algorithm for Feynman rules calculation. Firstly, it contains a wast array of tools that can be implemented for calculations of various matrix elements. Secondly, it contains expressions for Feynman rules for quantum chromodynamics. Lastly, it contains a tool to operate with one-loop Passarino-Veltman integrals
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
29
]
. Consequently, FeynCalc contains all the tools required for an analysis of perturbative quantum gravity.

The xAct bundle
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
is related with a problem similar to the calculation of Feynman rules. Namely, xPert package
[
21
]
provides a tool to evaluate perturbative expansions, including the perturbative expansion of the Einstein-Hilbert action (
19
). One can naturally expect to find an opportunity to use a perturbative expansion generated by the xPert to derive the corresponding Feynman rules. However, we believe that a direct usage of the xPert may not result in an immediate efficiency improvement. The explicit form of an interaction Lagrangian is not as important as the Lorentz structure of the Lagrangian, that is the way to contract indices between fields
h_{\mu\nu}
and their momenta. The formalism presented in this paper provides an explicit way to factorize that structure. On the contrary, xPert only provides a user with a symbolic expression, so the structure of an interaction term remains implicit. Still, the opportunity to use the xAct bundle for Feynman rules calculation must not be discarded. In particular, package xPerm
[
20
]
are expected to provide a much productive framework to operate with tensor indices. This opportunity will be explored in further publications.

In this paper we will not discuss the source code in details because of two reasons. Firstly, the code does not rely on any sophisticated techniques that worth to be discussed. The most part of calculations are based on manipulations with arrays of Lorentz indices which may look massive, but do not require any sophisticated techniques. Secondly, we would like to focus on results that FeynGrav helps to obtain.

We would only like to give the following comment on the technical side of the package. First and foremost, all expressions for interaction vertices used in the package are taken from pre-made libraries. By default these libraries contain data for perturbation theory up to
\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{3})
order. This is done for the sake of performance optimization as the corresponding expressions take quite some time to evaluate. Expressions for vertices can also be generated independently with “Libraries_Generator.nb” file. Secondly,
I
tensors are evaluate in exactly the same way as described in the previous section. The script generates a single term and then performs a consecutive symmetrization.
C
tensors are evaluated in a similar way. Namely, the sum of
I
tensors is evaluated and the result is symmetrized. Tensors
\mathcal{C}_{I}
,
\mathcal{C}_{II}
,
\mathcal{C}_{III}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{E}
are evaluated in the same way, i.e. the script performs a summation and then performs a symmetrization. Finally, expressions for interaction vertices are evaluated similarly. The script obtains an expression for a single pair of momenta and then summarizes over all required pairs of momenta.

The full list of FeynGrav commands is given in Appendix
A
and can also be called within the program itself with “FeynGravCommands”. There are three classes of commands. Firstly, there are commands that return expressions for interaction vertices. Secondly, there are commands that return expressions for the graviton propagator. The propagator (and all other expressions) is evaluated in the harmonic gauge which is fixed by the following term:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{gf}}=\int d^{4}x\,\left(\partial_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu}-\cfrac{1}{2}\,\partial^{\nu}h\right)^{2}\,.

(69)

The resulting expression for the graviton propagator reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}(k)=i\,\cfrac{\cfrac{1}{2}\left[\eta_{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\nu\beta}+\eta_{\mu\beta}\eta_{\nu\alpha}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\right]}{k^{2}}\,.

(70)

There are three commands related with the graviton propagator. They are introduced as a mean to simplify some calculations. Their description is also given in Appendix
A
.

7.2
Polarization operators

Another critical test for the applicability of FeynGrav is provided by the structure of divergencies at the one-loop level. It is well-known that general relativity without matter is UV-finite on-shell at the one-loop level
[
31
]
. This can be tested via a calculation of the one-loop graviton polarization operators. Contribution of a graviton loop to the graviton polarization operator off-shell is given by the following expression:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 20.0pt=

\displaystyle\text{GravitonVertex}[\mu,\nu,p,\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},-k,\rho_{1},\sigma_{1},-(p-k)]\text{GravitonPropagator}[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},k]

\displaystyle\times\text{GravitonPropagator}[\rho_{1},\sigma_{1},\rho_{2},\sigma_{2},p-k]\text{GravitonVertex}[\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},k,\rho_{2},\sigma_{2},p-k,\alpha,\beta,-p]

\displaystyle=i\pi^{2}B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{128}\,p^{4}\Bigg[

\displaystyle-\cfrac{4\left(d^{4}-10d^{3}-137d^{2}+458d+176\right)}{d^{2}-1}\,P^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

(77)

\displaystyle+\cfrac{2\left(3d^{6}-60d^{5}+433d^{4}-808d^{3}-1172d^{2}+1988d+3896\right)}{d^{2}-1}\,P^{0}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+\cfrac{16(d-2)(3d-11)}{d-1}\,P^{1}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+8(d-2)(d-3)\,\overline{P^{0}}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+\cfrac{4(d-2)\left(d^{3}-12d^{2}+44d-46\right)}{d-1}\,\overline{\overline{P^{0}}}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Bigg]\,.

Here
B_{0}
is a Passarino-Veltman integral
[
17
]
and
P^{2}
,
P^{0}
,
P^{1}
,
\overline{P^{0}}
,
\overline{\overline{P^{0}}}
are Nieuwenhuizen operators
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
. When the momenta
p
is fixed on-shell the expression simplifies:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 28.45274pt=(d-2)(d^{3}-18d^{2}+108d-192)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{16}\,p_{\mu}p_{\nu}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}\,\int\cfrac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\cfrac{1}{k^{2}(k-p)^{2}}\,.

In order to fix the expression on-shell completely one should (after a few intermediate steps) multiply the expression on the corresponding polarization operators
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}(p)
which makes it vanish due to the gauge symmetry. Therefore, in full agreement with the known results, pure gravity at the one-loop level remains UV-finite.

Contributions to the graviton polarization operator from massless matter loops evaluated off-shell are presented below.

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 20.0pt=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{16}\,\cfrac{1}{d^{2}-1}\,p^{4}\Bigg[2\,P^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+\left(3d^{2}-6d-4\right)\,P^{0}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Bigg]\,;

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 20.0pt=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{16}\,\cfrac{1}{d^{2}-1}\,p^{4}\Bigg[2\left(2d^{2}-3d-8\right)\,P^{2}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}+(d-4)(3d^{2}-8d-8)\,P^{0}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Bigg]\,;

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 20.0pt=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{16}\,p^{4}\,\cfrac{2-d}{d-1}\,P^{1}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,.

Similarly to the previous case these polarization operators vanish if
p
is fixed on-shell. This result takes place only due to the dimensional reasons. At the one-loop level graviton polarization operator is proportional to
\kappa^{2}
which has mass dimension
-2
. If a matter field is massless, the only dimensional parameter at hand is
p^{2}
. As soon as it is fixed on-shell it becomes impossible to compensate the negative dimension of
\kappa
and the polarization operator can only vanish. Therefore the corresponding matrix elements remain UV-finite on-shell even if massless matter is added to gravity.

In a similar manner one-loop gravitational corrections for massless scalar, vector, and fermion propagators can be calculated. The corresponding expressions are given below.

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}\,B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{32}\,(d-4)(2-d)\,p^{4}\,;

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph*}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph*}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}\hskip 20.0pt=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{32}\,\cfrac{(d-2)(d^{2}-14d+32)}{d-1}\,p^{4}\,\theta_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(p)\,;

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{fmffile}\begin{fmfgraph}{4}0,40)
\end{fmfgraph}\end{fmffile}\end{gathered}=

\displaystyle i\pi^{2}\,B_{0}(p^{2},0,0)\,\cfrac{\kappa^{2}}{128}\,(3-2d)(2-d)\,p^{2}\,\gamma\cdot p\,.

Here

is the standard gauge projector.

The corresponding corrections to propagators also vanish on-shell due to the same dimensional reasoning. Although it must be noted that the scalar field polarization operator also vanishes off-shell due to
(d-4)
factor.

8
Discussion and conclusions

In this paper we presented a framework capable to provide a relatively simple way to operate with Feynman rules for gravity within a computer algebra system. In Section
2
we presented the core of the framework. Namely, we defined
I
and
C
tensors that are used to obtain perturbative expansions for
g^{\mu\nu}
,
\sqrt{-g}
,
\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}
,
\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}
, and
\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}
which are typical for bosonic Lagrangians. In Sections
3
,
4
, and
5
we show how these factors shall be used to obtain Feynman rules for pure gravity, massless vectors, and scalars of an arbitrary mass. Section
6
is completely devoted to a derivation of Feynman rules for Dirac fermions of an arbitrary mass. We briefly revisit a construction of Dirac fermions in a curved spacetime and obtained a perturbative expansion of the Dirac action.

Finally, Section
7
is completely devoted to a discussion of “FeynGrav”, a packaged based on FeynCalc that implements the discussed Feynman rules. Three particular examples of implementations are discussed. The first one is the tree level
2\to 2
on-shell graviton scattering that is discussed in Section
7.1
. The scattering amplitude was firstly evaluated in
[
12
]
and analytic calculations take about a page long. Within FeynGrav the same calculations can be performed much more easily and faster. The second example is a calculation of polarization operators for graviton and matter propagators. Explicit off-shell expressions for the corresponding operators are obtained in Section
7.2
. All obtained polarization operators effectively vanish on-shell due to dimensional reasoning. This is in agreement with the well-known result showing that at the one-loop level pure gravity is UV finite on-shell

We believe that FeynGrav provides a suitable framework to deal with perturbative effective gravity in low orders of perturbation theory. Even in the current state the package provides a tool to calculate sophisticated off-shell amplitudes which can be used for a detailed study of one-loop gravity structure.

On the James brace product: Generalization, relation to H H -splitting of loop space fibrations & the J J -homomorphism
Somnath Basu, Aritra Bhowmick, Sandip Samanta

Abstract
. Given a fibration F ↪ E → B F\hookrightarrow E\rightarrow B with a homotopy section s : B → E s:B\rightarrow E , James introduced a binary product { , } s : π i B × π j F → π i + j − 1 F \left\{,\right\}_{s}:\pi_{i}B\times\pi_{j}F\rightarrow\pi_{i+j-1}F , called the brace product. In this article, we generalize this to general homotopy groups. We show that the vanishing of this generalized brace product is the precise obstruction to the H H -splitting of the loop space fibration, i.e., Ω ​ E ≃ Ω ​ B × Ω ​ F \Omega E\simeq\Omega B\times\Omega F as H H -spaces. Using rational homotopy theory, we show that for rational spaces, the vanishing of the generalized brace product coincides with the vanishing of the classical James brace product, enabling us to perform relevant computations. In addition, the notion of J J -homomorphism is generalized and connected to the generalized brace product. Among applications, we characterize the homotopy types of certain fibrations including sphere bundles over spheres.

1.
Introduction

A fibration
p:E\rightarrow B
with homotopy fibre
F
, written as
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\overset{p}{\to}B
, is said to be
decomposable
or
trivial
if
E
is homotopy equivalent to the product space
B\times F
. A principal
G
-bundle
p:E\rightarrow B
equipped with a section
s:B\rightarrow E
is decomposable since
E
is homeomorphic to
B\times G
. However, for a general fibration, merely having a section is a much weaker condition than the fibration being decomposable. We refer to a map
s:B\rightarrow E
as a
homotopy section
if
p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s
is homotopic to the identity map of the base space
B
.
In
[
12
]
James introduced a binary product
\left\{,\right\}_{s}:\pi_{*}(B)\times\pi_{*}(F)\rightarrow\pi_{*}(F)
for a fibration
p
equipped with a homotopy section
s
. We shall call this product the
James brace product
(or simply, the
brace product
). James demonstrated that the brace product is an obstruction to certain fibrations being decomposable. Kallel and Sjerve
[
15
]
established a connection between the brace product and certain differentials in the Serre spectral sequence associated with a fibration having a section. In this article,
we prove the following result (Theorem
4.4
).

Theorem A
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\overset{p}{\to}B
is a fibration with a homotopy section
s:B\to E
. Then the James brace product
\{,\}_{s}
vanish for the given fibration if and only if the natural map
(\varphi_{s})_{*}:=(\Omega s\cdot\Omega i)_{*}:\pi_{*}(\Omega B\times\Omega F)\to\pi_{*}(\Omega E)
is an algebra isomorphism with ring structure given by the Samelson product.

It is well known
[
4
]
that if a fibration
p:E\rightarrow B
admits a homotopy section
s:B\rightarrow E
, then the map
\varphi_{s}:\Omega B\times\Omega F\rightarrow\Omega E
is a homotopy equivalence. As any based loop space
\Omega Y
of
Y
is an
H
-space, a natural question arises: is
\varphi_{s}
an
H
-space map? If so, we call
\varphi_{s}
an
H
-splitting
. In
[
15
]
the authors identified certain space level analog of James brace product as the obstruction to
\varphi_{s}
being an
H
-splitting. Recently, Liu
[
17
]
studied the same question, and obtained some sufficient conditions.

To answer the above question completely, we extend the notion of brace product to the groups of homotopy classes of maps from the suspension of spaces. Given any space
X
, let
\Sigma X
denote the reduced suspension of
X
. The set
[\Sigma X,Z]
of base point preserving homotopy classes of maps
\Sigma X\rightarrow Z
forms a group. With this in mind, for a fibration
p:E\rightarrow B
with homotopy section
s:B\rightarrow E
, and spaces
X,Y
, we introduce the
generalized brace product
(Definition
3.1
) as a binary operation

\left\{,\right\}_{s}:[\Sigma X,B]\times[\Sigma Y,F]\rightarrow[\Sigma(X\wedge Y),F],

where
X\wedge Y
represents the smash product of
X
and
Y
.

Remarkably, the vanishing of the generalized brace product is significantly more stringent than the vanishing of the brace product as explored in James’ initial research
[
12
]
or in
[
15
]
. In the context of
H
-splitting of the
\varphi_{s}
map, we establish the following result (Theorem
4.1
).

Theorem B
.

The map
\varphi_{s}
is an
H
-splitting if and only if the generalized brace product vanishes for all spaces
X,Y
.

To illustrate this point further, we provide an example (Example
4.2
) of a fibration with a section exhibiting a vanishing brace product but a non-vanishing
generalized
brace product. This gives a counter-example to Lemma 2.12 of Kallel-Sjerve
[
15
]
, which states that “
if the brace product vanishes identically, then
\varphi_{s}
is an
H
-splitting
”. But the lemma is true in some special cases such as when both the base and fibre of a fibration are arbitrary wedges or finite products of spheres (Corollary
4.13
). Moreover, in the case of a sphere bundle over a sphere, the vanishing of the brace product implies more (Corollary
4.8
).

Theorem C
.

Suppose
S^{m}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}S^{n}
is a fibration with homotopy section
s
. If the brace product
\{\mathrm{Id}_{S^{n}},\mathrm{Id}_{S^{m}}\}_{s}
vanishes, then
E
is homotopy equivalent to
S^{m}\times S^{n}
.

We have also related the generalized brace product on certain free loop space fibration with the generalized Whitehead product on the base space. Recall that the
m
-fold free loop space
\mathcal{L}^{m}X
of a space
X
is defined as the set of all maps
S^{m}
to
X
. The evaluation at a fixed point in
S^{m}
then induces a fibration
\mathcal{L}^{m}X\rightarrow X
, with fibre
\Omega^{m}X
, which naturally admits a section given by the constant loops. Expanding upon the contributions of Hansen
[
7
]
, in Theorem
3.10
, we establish a connection between the generalized brace product in this fibration and the generalized Whitehead product in the space
X
.

Additionally, we explore other noteworthy examples, such as sphere bundles over spheres, many of which are known to have sections. One such example includes a counter-example (Example
6.8
) to Proposition 6.4 of Husemöller
[
11
]
stating that “
image of
J
-homomorphism is contained inside the image of suspension homomorphism of sphere
”. Proposition
6.9
is a rectification of that. Alongside this, we provide computations for the rationalized version of the generalized brace product in certain cases and prove the decomposability of the corresponding rational spaces. This leads to the following result, which is Corollary
5.11
in this article.

Theorem D
.

Suppose
\Sigma F\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}\Sigma B
is a fibration of CW complexes with a homotopy section
s
. If the James brace product
\{,\}_{s}
vanishes identically, then
E
and
\Sigma B\times\Sigma F
have the same rational homotopy type.

Finally, we relate the generalized brace product with a generalized concept of the classical
J
-homomorphism in §
6
. In particular, we prove the following result (Theorem
6.4
).

Theorem E
.

Suppose
\Sigma F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\rightarrow\Sigma B
is a fibration characterized by a map
\rho:B\to Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma F,\Sigma F)
, which maps the base point of
B
to
\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}
. Then, given any base point preserving self homotopy equivalence
\varphi:\Sigma F\rightarrow\Sigma F
, we have

\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\varphi\right\}_{s}=J[\varphi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon]-J[\rho],

where
s
is the choice of a canonical ‘
\infty
-section’
s
, and
\varepsilon:B\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma F,\Sigma F)
is given by
\varepsilon(b)=\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}
.

Moreover, if
B
is a suspension, then
\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\varphi\right\}_{s}=-J[\rho]
.

The above Theorem, on the one hand, helps us to prove Lemma 1 (see Lemma
6.7
) of Milnor
[
18
]
, no proof of which could be located in the existing literature. On the other hand, this generalizes Lemma 1 of Stöcker
[
22
]
, which is a consequence of Theorem 2 and 3 there. Moreover, this leads to the following result (Theorem
6.10
).

Theorem F
.

Consider a fibre bundle
S^{q}\hookrightarrow E\rightarrow S^{n}
with
SO(q+1)
action and
(q,n)\neq(4,4),(8,8)
. If the fibration admits a section,
E
has the same rational homotopy type as
S^{q}\times S^{n}
.

Organization of the paper.
The article has the following structure. In §
2
, we discuss the necessary background, including the Whitehead and the Samelson product on the homotopy groups of spaces. In §
3
we introduce the generalized brace product and derive some of its basic properties. In §
4
we present the main results of this article. In particular, we discuss the consequences and provide equivalent criteria for the vanishing of both the James and the generalized brace products. Following that, in §
5
, we discuss various applications of these results in the context of localization. Lastly, in §
6
, we introduce the generalized
J
-homomorphism and relate it to certain generalized brace product. In the appendix
A
, we provide proofs of two technical lemmas, which might possibly be known to experts.

X

Definition 2.1

(Whitehead product)
.

Suppose
f:(I^{k},\partial I^{k})\to(X,x_{0})
and
g:(I^{l},\partial I^{l})\to(X,x_{0})
are continuous maps representing elements
[f]\in\pi_{k}(X,x_{0})
and
[g]\in\pi_{l}(X,x_{0})
. The
Whitehead product
of
[f],[g]
is denoted by
[f,g]\in\pi_{k+l-1}(X,x_{0})
and defined as the homotopy class of the following map

\displaystyle S^{k+l-1}=\partial(I^{k}\times I^{l})=(I^{k}\times\partial I^{l})\cup

\displaystyle(\partial I^{k}\times I^{l})\xrightarrow{f\cup g}X

As indicated by the notation,
[\cdot,\cdot]
is also called the
Whitehead bracket
. It forms a graded quasi-Lie algebra, i.e.,
[\cdot,\cdot]
is an operation of degree
-1
, graded commutative (
[f,g]=(-1)^{|f||g|}[g,f]
), and satisfies the (graded) Jacobi identity for higher homotopy groups

(-1)^{|f||h|}[[f,g],h]+(-1)^{|g||f|}[[g,h],f]+(-1)^{|h||g|}[[h,f],g]=0,

where
|f|,|g|,|h|\geq 1
. Initially, it was well-known for
|f|,|g|,|h|\geq 2
but later on Hilton
[
9
]
appropriately extended it for
|f|,|g|,|h|\geq 1
, using additive notation for the (possibly non-abelian) fundamental group. Moreover,
\pi_{1}
acts on
\pi_{k}
via the Whitehead product. In particular, if
k=1
, then
[f,g]=fgf^{-1}g^{-1}
. The Whitehead product vanishes for
H
-spaces
[
28
, Cor. 7.8]
. Note that the Whitehead product is functorial, i.e., if
h:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
is a continuous map, then
h_{\ast}[f,g]=[h_{\ast}f,h_{\ast}g]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\pi_{j}(B),\beta\in\pi_{k}(B),\gamma\in\pi_{l}(F)
and
\delta\in\pi_{m}(F)
. Then,

(a)

D_{\beta}
is a derivation:
D_{\beta}([\gamma,\delta])=(-1)^{|D_{\beta}|}[D_{\beta}(\gamma),\delta]+(-1)^{|D_{\beta}|(|\gamma|-1)}[\gamma,D_{\beta}(\delta)]
, i.e.,

\left\{\beta,[\gamma,\delta]\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{k-1}\left[\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},\delta\right]+(-1)^{(l-1)(k-1)}\left[\gamma,\{\beta,\delta\}_{s}\right].

(b)

D:\pi_{*}(B)\rightarrow\operatorname{Der}\left(\pi_{*}(F)\right)
is a Lie algebra map:
D([\alpha,\beta])=(-1)^{|D_{\alpha}|}[D(\alpha),D(\beta)]
, i.e.,

\left\{[\alpha,\beta],\gamma\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{j-1}\left(\left\{\alpha,\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}-(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}\left\{\beta,\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}\right).

Proof.

(a) By Jacobi identity of Whitehead product in
\pi_{*}(E)
, we have

\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{km}\left[[s_{*}(\beta),i_{*}(\gamma)],i_{*}(\delta)\right]+(-1)^{kl}\left[[i_{*}(\gamma),i_{*}(\delta)],s_{*}(\beta)\right]+(-1)^{lm}\left[[i_{*}(\delta),s_{*}(\beta)],i_{*}(\gamma)\right]=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &(-1)^{km}\left[i_{*}\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},i_{*}(\delta)\right]+(-1)^{kl}\left[i_{*}[\gamma,\delta],s_{*}(\beta)\right]+(-1)^{lm}\left[(-1)^{jl}i_{*}\{\beta,\delta\}_{s},i_{*}(\gamma)\right]=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &i_{*}\left((-1)^{km}\left[\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},\delta\right]+(-1)^{kl+(l+m-1)k}\left\{\beta,[\gamma,\delta]\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{lm+km}\left[\{\beta,\delta\}_{s},\gamma\right]\right)=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left[\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},\delta\right]+(-1)^{k}\left\{\beta,[\gamma,\delta]\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{lm}\left[\{\beta,\delta\}_{s},\gamma\right]=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left\{\beta,[\gamma,\delta]\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{k-1}\left[\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},\delta\right]+(-1)^{lm-k+1+l(k+m-1)}\left[\{\beta,\delta\}_{s},\gamma\right]\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left\{\beta,[\gamma,\delta]\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{k-1}\left[\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},\delta\right]+(-1)^{(l-1)(k-1)}\left[\{\beta,\delta\}_{s},\gamma\right].\end{aligned}

(b) By Jacobi identity of Whitehead product in
\pi_{*}(E)
, we also have

\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{jl}\left[[s_{*}(\alpha),s_{*}(\beta)],i_{*}(\gamma)\right]+(-1)^{kj}\left[[s_{*}(\beta),i_{*}(\gamma)],s_{*}(\alpha)\right]+(-1)^{lk}\left[[i_{*}(\gamma),s_{*}(\alpha)],s_{*}(\beta)\right]=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &(-1)^{jl}\left[s_{*}[\alpha,\beta],i_{*}(\gamma)\right]+(-1)^{kj}\left[i_{*}\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s},s_{*}(\alpha)\right]+(-1)^{lk}\left[(-1)^{jl}i_{*}\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s},s_{*}(\beta)\right]=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &(-1)^{jl}i_{*}\left\{[\alpha,\beta],\gamma\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{kj+(k+l-1)j}i_{*}\left\{\alpha,\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{lk+jl+(j+l-1)k}\left\{\beta,\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left\{[\alpha,\beta],\gamma\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{j}\left\{\alpha,\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}+(-1)^{(j-1)k}\left\{\beta,\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}=0\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left\{[\alpha,\beta],\gamma\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{j-1}\left\{\alpha,\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}-(-1)^{(j-1)k}\left\{\beta,\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}\\
\Rightarrow\ &\left\{[\alpha,\beta],\gamma\right\}_{s}=(-1)^{j-1}\left(\left\{\alpha,\{\beta,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}-(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}\left\{\beta,\{\alpha,\gamma\}_{s}\right\}_{s}\right).\end{aligned}

The notion of Whitehead products and brace products can be generalized. We first need the following lemma.

p_{*}[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=[p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=[p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,0]=0.

Definition 3.1

(Generalized brace product)
.

Consider the fibration
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}B
with homotopy section
s
. Then for any
f:\Sigma X\to B
and
g:\Sigma Y\to F
, we define the element
\left\{f,g\right\}_{s}\in\left[\Sigma\left(X\wedge Y\right),F\right]
by the equation

(3.2)

\left\{f,g\right\}_{s}=i_{*}^{-1}[s_{*}f,i_{*}g].

We have a well-defined map

\{,\}_{s}:[\Sigma X,B]\times[\Sigma Y,F]\to[\Sigma(X\wedge Y),F],

which we call the
generalized brace product
.

Note that if
\Sigma X,\Sigma Y
both are spheres, then it follows from the definition itself and Remark
2.8
(b) that the generalized brace product and brace product coincide. Consequently, we reuse the same symbol
\{,\}_{s}
to denote generalized brace product as well. Henceforth, if it is necessary to distinguish between the two, we shall refer to the case of spheres as the James brace product.

Analogous to the Samelson product, the generalized brace product (and hence the standard one as well) comes from a space-level map. Kallel and Sjerve
[
15
, Lemma 2.9]
first observed this for brace product in homotopy groups, although their proof was inadequate. Here we prove this fact in complete detail.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a fibration
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\overset{p}{\to}B
with a homotopy section
s
, there is a map
J_{s}:\Omega B\wedge\Omega F\to\Omega F
, which induces the generalized brace product. Moreover, this map is unique up to homotopy.

Proof.

Recall from Definition
2.6
, we have the commutator map
C:\Omega E\times\Omega E\rightarrow\Omega E
, and the Samelson map
S:\Omega E\wedge\Omega E\rightarrow\Omega E
, such that
S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}q\simeq C
, where
q:\Omega E\times\Omega E\rightarrow\Omega E\wedge\Omega E
is the quotient map.
Now from the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega B\vee\Omega F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega B\times\Omega F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega B\wedge\Omega F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega E\vee\Omega E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega E\times\Omega E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega E\wedge\Omega E}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega s\vee\Omega i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega s\times\Omega i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega s\wedge\Omega i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

we have

\displaystyle\Omega p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\wedge\Omega i)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Omega p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}q\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\times\Omega i)

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}C\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\times\Omega i)

\displaystyle=\Omega p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\cdot\Omega i\cdot\overline{\Omega s}\cdot\overline{\Omega i})

\displaystyle=\Omega(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s)\cdot\Omega(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i)\cdot\overline{\Omega(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s)}\cdot\overline{\Omega(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i)}

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega(\textrm{Id}_{B})\cdot\Omega c_{b}\cdot\overline{\Omega(\textrm{Id}_{B})}\cdot\overline{\Omega c_{b}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(\text{as $p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s\simeq\textrm{Id}_{B}$, and $p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i=$ constant map, say $c_{b}$})

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega c_{b}

Given
X=\Omega B
,
Y=\Omega F
, and
W=\Omega B
, Lemma
2.5
suggests that
(q^{\prime})^{*}
is injective. Consequently, the preceding computation yields
\Omega p\circ S\circ(\Omega s\wedge\Omega i)\simeq\Omega c_{b}
.
Now applying the functor
[\Omega B\wedge\Omega F,-]
to the following sequence of fibration

\Omega^{2}E\xrightarrow{\Omega^{2}p}\Omega^{2}B\xrightarrow{\Omega\partial}\Omega F\xrightarrow{\Omega i}\Omega E\xrightarrow{\Omega p}\Omega B,

and noting that
\Omega^{2}p
has right inverse
\Omega^{2}s
,
we obtain the following exact sequence

\left\{\ast\right\}\to[\Omega B\wedge\Omega F,\Omega F]\xrightarrow{(\Omega i)_{*}}[\Omega B\wedge\Omega F,\Omega E]\xrightarrow{(\Omega p)_{*}}[\Omega B\wedge\Omega F,\Omega B].

As
S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\wedge\Omega i)\in\text{Ker}(\Omega p)_{*}
, we have a unique (up to homotopy) map
J_{s}:\Omega B\wedge\Omega F\to\Omega F
such that
(\Omega i)_{*}(J_{s})=S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\wedge\Omega i)
.
Now for
f:\Sigma X\to B
and
g:\Sigma Y\to F
, we have the following

\displaystyle i_{*}(\{f,g\}_{s})

\displaystyle=[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]

\displaystyle=\ad^{-1}\left(S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\ad(s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f)\wedge\ad(i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g))\right)

\displaystyle=\ad^{-1}\left(S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}((\Omega s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\ad f)\wedge(\Omega i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\ad g))\right)

\displaystyle=\ad^{-1}\left(S\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\Omega s\wedge\Omega i)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\ad f\wedge\ad g)\right)

\displaystyle=\ad^{-1}\left((\Omega i)_{*}\left(J_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\ad f\wedge\ad g)\right)\right)\text{ (by the construction of $J_{s}$)}

\displaystyle=i_{*}\left(\ad^{-1}\left(J_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\ad f\wedge\ad g)\right)\right)

Since
i_{*}
is monic (see (
3.1
)), we get
\{f,g\}_{s}=\ad^{-1}\left(J_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\ad{f}\wedge\ad{g})\right)
.
Thus,
J_{s}
induces the generalized brace product
\{,\}_{s}
.

Observe that by taking
f_{0}=\ad^{-1}(\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega B}):\Sigma\Omega B\rightarrow B
and
g_{0}=\ad^{-1}(\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega F}):\Sigma\Omega F\rightarrow F
, we have

\left\{f_{0},g_{0}\right\}_{s}=\ad^{-1}\left(J_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\left(\ad f_{0}\wedge\ad g_{0}\right)\right)=\ad^{-1}\left(J_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega B}\wedge\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega F}\right)\right)=\ad^{-1}J_{s}.

Hence,
J_{s}=\ad\left\{f_{0},g_{0}\right\}_{s}
is defined, uniquely up to homotopy.
∎

3.1.

), and also a homotopy equivalence. Thus, we have a lift, say,
\Gamma

Clearly,

, and

. In particular, we have a map

. It follows that if

, then
\mathsf{L}_{\gamma}\simeq\mathsf{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
, and furthermore
\mathsf{L}_{\gamma\cdot\eta}\simeq\mathsf{L}_{\eta}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mathsf{L}_{\gamma}
whenever

are two paths in

that can be concatenated (

, p. 405]
). Consequently, we get a homotopy equivalence

3.2.
Brace product of pullback and product fibrations

In this section, we deduce how the generalized brace product behaves under the usual constructions on a fibration such as pullback, and product. The following lemma relates the generalized brace product of a fibration and its pullback fibration by a map.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\overset{p}{\to}B
is a fibration with a (homotopy) section
s:B\to E
and
f:\tilde{B}\to B
is a continuous map. Then

\displaystyle\{\alpha,\beta\}_{f^{*}s}=\{f_{*}(\alpha),\beta\}_{s}\text{ for }\alpha\in[\Sigma X,\tilde{B}],\beta\in[\Sigma Y,F],

where
\{,\}_{f^{*}s}
and
\{,\}_{s}
denote the generalized brace product of the pullback fibration
f^{*}E
and
E
with respect to the pullback section
f^{*}s
and the section
s
respectively.

The proof follows immediately from the following more general case.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose we have a (not necessarily) commutative diagram of fibrations with sections

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{2}}

such that
\Phi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\left(s_{1}\vee i_{1}\right)\simeq\left(s_{2}\vee i_{2}\right)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\left(\phi\vee\varphi\right)=\left(s_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\phi\right)\vee\left(i_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varphi\right)
holds. Then, for any
\alpha\in\left[\Sigma X,B_{1}\right],\beta\in\left[\Sigma Y_{1},F_{1}\right]
we have

\varphi_{*}\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}_{s_{1}}=\left\{\phi_{*}\alpha,\varphi_{*}\beta\right\}_{s_{2}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left(i_{2}\right)_{*}\varphi_{*}\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}_{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{*}\left(i_{1}\right)_{*}\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}_{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{*}\left[\left(s_{1}\right)_{*}\alpha,\left(i_{1}\right)_{*}\beta\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\Phi_{*}\left(s_{1}\right)_{*}\alpha,\Phi_{*}\left(i_{1}\right)_{*}\beta\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(s_{2}\right)_{*}\phi_{*}\alpha,\left(i_{2}\right)_{*}\varphi_{*}\beta\right]

\displaystyle=\left(i_{2}\right)_{*}\left\{\phi_{*}\alpha,\varphi_{*}\beta\right\}_{s_{2}}

As
\left(i_{2}\right)_{*}
is monic, the proof follows.
∎

Let us now observe how the generalized brace product of the product of two fibrations depends on those of the individual fibrations.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
F_{i}\overset{j_{i}}{\hookrightarrow}E_{i}\xrightarrow{p_{i}}B
is a fibration with homotopy section
s_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
F:=F_{1}\times F_{2}\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{(j_{1},j_{2})}E:=E_{1}\times E_{2}\xrightarrow{(p_{1},p_{2})}B\times B
is the product fibration with homotopy section
s:=(s_{1},s_{2})
. If
\Delta:B\to B\times B
denotes the diagonal map then the generalized brace product in the pullback bundle
\Delta^{*}(E)
with respect to the pullback homotopy section
\Delta^{*}(s)

Thus we only need to show that

\displaystyle C_{E}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\varphi_{s}\times\varphi_{s})\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)

\displaystyle C_{E}(\Omega s(\alpha_{1})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{1}),\Omega s(\alpha_{2})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{2}))

\displaystyle\Omega s(\alpha_{1})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{1})\cdot\Omega s(\alpha_{2})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{2})\cdot\overline{\Omega s(\alpha_{1})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{1})}\cdot\overline{\Omega s(\alpha_{2})\cdot\Omega i(\beta_{2})}

\{,\}_{s}

\varphi_{s}
is an algebra isomorphism.

Conversely, suppose
\varphi_{s}
is an algebra isomorphism, or equivalently

\varphi_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}C_{B\times F}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)\simeq C_{E}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\varphi_{s}\times\varphi_{s})\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)\text{ for all }f\in\pi_{k}(\Omega B\times\Omega F),\ g\in\pi_{l}(\Omega B\times\Omega F)

\varphi_{s}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}C_{B\times F}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)\simeq C_{E}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(\varphi_{s}\times\varphi_{s})\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)
implies

\displaystyle c_{E}\simeq\Omega s(\alpha)\cdot\Omega i(\beta)\cdot\Omega s(\bar{\alpha})\cdot\Omega i(\bar{\beta})\text{ ($c_{E}$ is the constant map in $\Omega E$)}

i.e.,

\displaystyle\Omega s(\alpha)\cdot\Omega i(\beta)\simeq\Omega i(\beta)\cdot\Omega s(\alpha).

Therefore, (

) implies that the brace product

vanishes identically.
∎

4.3.
Decomposability of a fibration over a suspension

The following is a direct generalization of
[
10
, Thm 2.1]
in the context of the generalized Whitehead product.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
\alpha:\Sigma A\to X
and
\beta:\Sigma B\to X
are two maps such that
[\alpha,\beta]=0
. Then for any
f:\Sigma C\to\Sigma A
and
g:\Sigma D\to\Sigma B
,

[\alpha\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,\beta\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=0.

Proof.

Since
[\alpha,\beta]=0
, we know from
[
2
, Prp
{}^{\text{n}}
5.1]
that there exist a map
m:\Sigma A\times\Sigma B\to X
such that
m\big|_{\Sigma A}\simeq\alpha
and
m\big|_{\Sigma B}\simeq\beta
. Now for any
f:\Sigma C\to\Sigma A
and
g:\Sigma D\to\Sigma B
,

\displaystyle m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g):\Sigma A\times\Sigma B\to X\text{ satisfies }

\displaystyle\qquad m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)\big|_{\Sigma A}\simeq\alpha\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\text{ and }m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}(f\times g)\big|_{\Sigma B}\simeq\beta\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g.

Then again using the converse of
[
2
, Prp
{}^{\text{n}}
5.1]
, we have
[\alpha\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,\beta\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=0
.
∎

We have the following useful corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\Sigma F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}\Sigma B
is a fibration with homotopy section
s
, then
\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s}=[s,i]=0
if and only if the generalized brace product vanishes, i.e.,
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let
[s,i]=0
. Then for any
f:\Sigma X\to\Sigma B
and
g:\Sigma Y\to\Sigma F
, the above Proposition
4.5
implies
[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=0
i.e.,
i_{*}(\{f,g\}_{s})=0
(by definition of
\{,\}_{s}
). As
i_{*}
is monic (see (
3.1
)) and
f,g
are arbitrary, we have
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
. Conversely, let
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
. Then
\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s}=0
, and hence,
[s,i]=i_{*}\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s}=0
.
∎

Let us now establish a connection between the vanishing of the generalized brace product and the decomposability of the total space of a fibration.

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose
\Sigma F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}\Sigma B
is a fibration of CW-complexes with homotopy section
s
. If the generalized brace product
\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s}=0
, then
E\simeq\Sigma B\times\Sigma F
.

Proof.

Note that if
\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s}=0
, then
[s,i]=i_{*}(\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\}_{s})=0
. This implies (by
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
) that there exists a map
m:\Sigma B\times\Sigma F\to E
such that
m\big|_{\Sigma B}\simeq s
and
m\big|_{\Sigma F}\simeq i
. Thus, if
j_{1}:\Sigma B\to\Sigma B\times\Sigma F
and
j_{2}:\Sigma F\to\Sigma B\times\Sigma F
denote the respective inclusions, then
m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}j_{1}\simeq s
and
m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}j_{2}\simeq i
. We will show that

is a homotopy equivalence. As all our spaces are CW-complexes, by Whitehead’s theorem, it is enough to prove that
m_{*}
is an isomorphism on homotopy groups.
Now for
\alpha\in\pi_{*}(\Sigma B\times\Sigma F)\cong\pi_{*}(\Sigma B)\oplus\pi_{*}(\Sigma F)

\alpha_{1}\in\pi_{*}(\Sigma B)

\alpha_{2}\in\pi_{*}(\Sigma F)

\alpha=(j_{1})_{*}(\alpha_{1})+(j_{2})_{*}(\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}m)_{*}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}m)_{*}((j_{1})_{*}(\alpha_{1})+(j_{2})_{*}(\alpha_{2}))

\displaystyle=(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}j_{1})_{*}(\alpha_{1})+(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}m\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}j_{2})_{*}(\alpha_{2}))

\displaystyle=(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s)_{*}(\alpha_{1})+(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i)_{*}(\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}+0\text{ (as $(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s)_{*}=1$ and $(p\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i)_{*}=0$)}

\displaystyle=(p_{1})_{*}(\alpha)

Here
p_{1}:\Sigma B\times\Sigma F\to\Sigma B

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}(\Sigma B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Hence, the five lemma implies that
m_{*}
is an isomorphism and consequently
E\simeq\Sigma B\times\Sigma F
.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we have the following interesting result.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose
S^{m}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}S^{n}
is a fibration with homotopy section
s
. If the brace product
\{\mathrm{Id}_{S^{n}},\mathrm{Id}_{S^{m}}\}_{s}
vanishes, then
E\simeq S^{m}\times S^{n}
.

Example 4.9
.

In the context of oriented
S^{2}
-bundles over
S^{2}
, there are only two bundles, one is the trivial
S^{2}\times S^{2}
and the other is
\mathbb{P}^{2}\#\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. As the intersection forms of the above two
4
-manifolds are
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
and
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
respectively (see
[
16
, pp. 21-22]
) and they are non-isomorphic with integer coefficients,
S^{2}\times S^{2}\not\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}\#\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Hence, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.10
.

There does not exist any homotopy section
s
of the non-trivial
S^{2}
bundle over
S^{2}
with respect to which the brace product vanishes.

4.4.
Criteria for the vanishing of the brace product

Let us now deduce some equivalent criteria for the vanishing of the brace product in some interesting classes of fibrations.

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}B
is a fibration with homotopy section
s
, where
B=\vee_{r\in\mathcal{I}}\Sigma B_{r}
and
F=\vee_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Sigma F_{j}
are arbitrary wedges of suspensions, with inclusions
\alpha_{r}:\Sigma B_{r}\hookrightarrow B
and
\beta_{j}:\Sigma F_{j}\hookrightarrow F
. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

[s,i]=0
.

(b)

\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
.

(c)

\{\alpha_{r},\beta_{j}\}_{s}=0
for all
(r,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

Since
B
and
F
are suspensions themselves, equivalence of
(a)
and
(b)
follows from Corollary
4.6
. On the other hand, Lemma
A.1
demonstrates the equivalence of
(a)
and
(c)
.
∎

Similarly, if the base or fibre spaces are finite products of double suspension spaces or wedges of suspension spaces, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.12
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}B
is a fibration with homotopy section
s
. If any of the following holds,

(1)

B=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\Sigma^{2}B_{r}
and
F=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Sigma^{2}F_{j}
with
\alpha_{r}:\Sigma^{2}B_{r}\hookrightarrow B
and
\beta_{j}:\Sigma^{2}F_{j}\hookrightarrow F
denotes the inclusions

(2)

B=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\Sigma^{2}B_{r}
and
F=\vee_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Sigma F_{j}
with
\alpha_{r}:\Sigma^{2}B_{r}\hookrightarrow B
and
\beta_{j}:\Sigma F_{j}\hookrightarrow F
denotes the inclusions

(3)

B=\vee_{r\in\mathcal{I}}\Sigma B_{r}
and
F=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Sigma^{2}F_{j}
with
\alpha_{r}:\Sigma B_{r}\hookrightarrow B
and
\beta_{j}:\Sigma^{2}F_{j}\hookrightarrow F
denotes the inclusions

then
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
if and only if
\{\alpha_{r},\beta_{j}\}_{s}=0
for all
r,j
.

Proof.

We will prove for the first case, where both base and the fibre are finite products of double suspension spaces. The remaining cases can be proved similarly.

Suppose
\{\alpha_{r},\beta_{j}\}_{s}=0
for all
r,j
. Then
i_{*}\{\alpha_{r},\beta_{j}\}_{s}=[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r},i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j}]=0
for all
r,j
. Thus, for
f=(f_{r})_{r}\in[\Sigma X,B]
and
g=(g_{j})_{j}\in[\Sigma Y,F]
, using Proposition
4.5
, we have
[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{r},i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{j}]=0
for all
r,j
. Then, from the linearity of the Whitehead product in both coordinates
[
2
, Prp
{}^{\text{n}}
3.4]
, we have

i_{*}\{f,g\}_{s}=[s_{*}(f),i_{*}(g)]=\sum_{r,j}[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{r},i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{j}]=0.

As
i_{*}
is monic (see (
3.1
)),
\{f,g\}_{s}=0
for any
f,g
. Thus,
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
.

Conversely, if
\{,\}_{s}\equiv 0
, then
\{\alpha_{j},\beta_{r}\}_{s}=0
for all
r,j
.
∎

Note that to use the bi-linearity of the generalized Whitehead product (and in particular to make sense of the sum notation) in the above proof, we need to take the double suspension spaces. In case suspension spaces are spheres of appropriate dimensions in the above two propositions, we have the following corollary using Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.13
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}B
be a fibration with homotopy section
s
. Also, let
F\in\left\{\prod_{r=1}^{m}S^{k_{r}}(k_{r}>1),\vee_{r\in\mathcal{I}}S^{k_{r}}\right\}
and
B\in\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{n}S^{l_{j}}(l_{j}>1),\vee_{j\in\mathcal{J}}S^{l_{j}}\right\}
with
\alpha_{j}:S^{l_{j}}\hookrightarrow B
and
\beta_{r}:S^{k_{r}}\hookrightarrow F
be the inclusions in either cases. Then the map
\varphi_{s}=(\Omega s\cdot\Omega i):\Omega B\times\Omega F\to\Omega E
is an
H
-splitting if and only if the James brace product
\{\alpha_{j},\beta_{r}\}_{s}=0
for all
r,j
.

4.5.
Fibrations with vanishing brace product

The following proposition lets us construct fibration with vanishing (generalized) brace product from a given fibration with section.

Proposition 4.14
.

Suppose
F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\xrightarrow{p}B
be a fibration with homotopy section
s:B\to E
. Also, let there exist a product space
\tilde{B}=\prod_{k}B_{k}
and
f:\tilde{B}\to B
such that
f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\iota_{k}:B_{k}\to B
are null homotopic for each
k
, where
\iota_{k}:B_{k}\hookrightarrow\tilde{B}
be the inclusion map. Then the pullback fibration
F\overset{j}{\hookrightarrow}f^{*}E\xrightarrow{\tilde{p}}\tilde{B}
has the homotopy section
\tilde{s}=f^{*}s
such that

\{,\}_{\tilde{s}}\equiv 0.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\left[\Sigma X,\tilde{B}\right]
and
\beta\in\left[\Sigma Y,F\right]
. As
\tilde{B}=\prod_{k}B_{k}
,
\left[\Sigma X,\tilde{B}\right]=\prod_{k}\left[\Sigma X,B_{k}\right]
and thus
\alpha=\prod_{k}\alpha_{k}
, where
\alpha_{k}=\iota_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}p_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha
for
p_{k}:\tilde{B}\to B_{k}
is the projection onto the
k^{\text{th}}
coordinate. Then as
f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\iota_{k}\simeq 0
for all
k
,

\displaystyle f_{*}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k}f_{*}(\alpha_{k})=\prod_{k}f_{*}(\iota_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}p_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha)=\prod_{k}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\iota_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}p_{k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha=0

Hence from Lemma
3.7
, we have

\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\tilde{s}}=\{\alpha,\beta\}_{f^{*}s}=\{f_{*}(\alpha,\beta)\}_{s}=\{0,\beta\}_{s}=0.

Since the above equality holds for any
\alpha\in[\Sigma X,\tilde{B}]
and
\beta\in[\Sigma Y,F]
, we have
\{,\}_{\tilde{s}}\equiv 0

Theorem 5.9

For the rationalized fibration

with section

of a fibration

Proof.

Since the James brace product is the restriction of the generalized brace product to the homotopy groups, the vanishing of the generalized brace product always implies the vanishing of the James brace product. Thus, it is enough to prove that if the James brace product vanishes identically in the rationalized fibration, then the generalized brace product also vanishes identically. But using Proposition
3.2
, this is equivalent to show

\ad\{f_{0},g_{0}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}

and
g_{0}=\ad^{-1}(\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega F_{\mathbb{Q}}})
, is null homotopic. Since
\ad
is an isomorphism, this reduces to show
\{f_{0},g_{0}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
only.
Note, by
[
5
, Thm 24.5]
and Lemma

\displaystyle f:\vee_{r\in\mathcal{I}}\Sigma S^{n_{r}-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\Sigma\Omega B_{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle g:\vee_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\Sigma S^{m_{j}-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\Sigma\Omega F_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Suppose
\alpha_{r}:\Sigma S^{n_{r}-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\vee_{r\in\mathcal{I}}\Sigma S^{n_{r}-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and

are inclusions, and

, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]

\displaystyle=\left([s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j},i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r}]\right)_{(r,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{J}}

\displaystyle\text{i.e., }(i_{\mathbb{Q}})_{*}\{f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}

\displaystyle=\left((i_{\mathbb{Q}})_{*}\{f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j},g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}\right)_{(r,j)\in\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{J}}

Since the James brace product vanishes identically, we have
\{f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\alpha}_{r},g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\beta}_{j}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
i.e.,
[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\alpha}_{r},i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\beta}_{j}]=(i_{\mathbb{Q}})_{*}\{f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\alpha}_{r},g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\beta}_{j}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
. Now Lemma
5.8
, implies
[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{r},i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{j}]=0
. Consequently, (

) implies
[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f,i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g]=0
. But as
f
and

are homotopy equivalences, using Proposition

, we get
[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}f_{0},i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}g_{0}]=0
i.e.,
(i_{\mathbb{Q}})_{*}\{f_{0},g_{0}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
. Hence injectivity of
(i_{\mathbb{Q}})_{*}
implies
\{f_{0},g_{0}\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=0
.
∎

Now Theorem
4.1
immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 5.10
.

Suppose

is the rationalized fibration with section
s_{\mathbb{Q}}
of a fibration
F\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}B
with section
s
. Then the James brace product
\{,\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}
vanishes if and only if the map
\varphi_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=(\Omega s_{\mathbb{Q}}\cdot\Omega i_{\mathbb{Q}}):\Omega B_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\Omega F_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\Omega E_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an
H
-splitting.

Also, if the base and fibre are suspension spaces, then combining with Proposition
4.7
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.11
.

Suppose
\Sigma F_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{i_{\mathbb{Q}}}E_{\mathbb{Q}}\xrightarrow{p_{\mathbb{Q}}}\Sigma B_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the rationalized fibration of CW complexes with section
s_{\mathbb{Q}}
of a fibration
\Sigma F\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}\Sigma B
with section
s
. If the James brace product
\{,\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}
vanishes, then
\Sigma B_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\Sigma F_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq E_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:E\hookrightarrow E_{\mathbb{Q}}
be a rationalization. For
\alpha\in\pi_{p}(\Sigma F),\beta\in\pi_{q}(\Sigma B)
, we have

\left(i_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{*}\left\{\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}},\beta_{\mathbb{Q}}\right\}_{s_{\mathbb{Q}}}=[s_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}},i_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta_{\mathbb{Q}}]=[\phi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha,\phi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta]=\phi_{*}[s\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\alpha,i\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\beta]=\phi_{*}i_{*}\underbrace{\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}_{s}}_{\textrm{torsion}}=0.

∎

6.
Brace Product and the
J
-homomorphism

Whitehead
[
25
]
defined a group homomorphism
J:\pi_{n-1}(SO(q))\to\pi_{n+q-1}(S^{q})
for
q,n-1\geq 2
. We generalize the notion of
J
-homomorphism and prove similar results as in
[
14
,
22
]
for the generalized brace product.

6.1.
Generalized
J
-Homomorphism

Suppose
Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
consists of all base point preserving continuous maps from
\Sigma Y
to itself. We define a map

J:[X,Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)]\rightarrow[\Sigma X\wedge Y,\Sigma Y]

as follows. Given any base point preserving
\rho:X\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
, consider the map

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}:X\star Y=CX\times Y\cup X\times CY

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma Y

\displaystyle\left([x,t],y\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\bullet

\displaystyle\left(x,[y,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho({x})\left([y,t]\right)

Here
X\star Y
denotes the topological join of
X
and
Y
, and it is identified with
CX\times Y\cup X\times CY
. We then define
J[\rho]=[\tilde{\rho}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]
, where
\mu:\Sigma X\wedge Y\rightarrow X\star Y
is a choice of homotopy inverse for the quotient map
X\star Y\rightarrow\Sigma X\wedge Y
, which is known to be a homotopy equivalence.

Remark 6.1
.

Consider
Y=S^{q-1}
and
X=S^{n-1}
, then thinking
\Sigma Y=S^{q}
as
\mathbb{R}^{q}\cup\{\infty\}
, we have

SO(q)\hookrightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)=Map_{\bullet}(S^{q},S^{q})\text{ via $\infty$-fix action, and }\Sigma X\wedge Y=S^{n+q-1}.

Thus, the restriction of generalized
J
-homomorphism to
[S^{n-1},SO(q)]
is a map from
\pi_{n-1}(SO(q))
to
[S^{n+q-1},S^{q}]=\pi_{n+q-1}(S^{q})
. Moreover, this
J
matches with the standard
J
-homomorphism of Whitehead (see
[
14
, §2]
). That justifies that the above
J
is indeed a generalization of the standard one.

Next, observe that
Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
is a homotopy associative
H
-space, with homotopy identity given by the constant map, and admitting homotopy inverses. Thus, the domain of
J
is a group. Indeed, we see that

\left[X,Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)\right]=\left[X_{+},Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)\right]=\left[X_{+}\wedge\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y\right]=\left[\Sigma X_{+}\wedge Y,\Sigma Y\right]

is a group, where
X_{+}
is obtained by attaching a disjoint point as the base point. Now an Eckmann-Hilton type argument will give us the following lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
X=\Sigma X^{\prime}
is a suspension, then the two group structures on
[\Sigma X^{\prime},Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)]
are the same, and it is an abelian group.

We also observe the following properties of generalized
J
-homomorphism.

Lemma 6.3
.

(1)

J
is a group homomorphism.

(2)

Given any
\varphi\in Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
and
\rho:X\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
, we have
J[\varphi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\rho]=\varphi_{*}J[\rho]
.

(3)

If
X
is a suspension, then
J[\varepsilon]=0
, where
\varepsilon
is the map
\varepsilon:x\mapsto\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma Y}
.

(4)

If
X
is a suspension, then given any
\phi\in Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
,
J[\varphi]=0
, where
\varphi
is the map
\varphi:x\mapsto\phi
.

Proof.

We use additive symbols for the multiplication in both groups. Consider two maps
h_{i}:X\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)
representing some
\alpha_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Then,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
is represented by a map

\displaystyle h:X

\displaystyle\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma Y,\Sigma Y)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\left([y,t]\mapsto\begin{cases}h_{1}(x)([y,2t]),\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
h_{2}(x)([y,2t-1]),\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1\end{cases}\right)

We then have the map

\displaystyle\tilde{h}:CX\times Y\cup X\times CY

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma Y

\displaystyle\left([x,t],y\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\bullet

\displaystyle\left(x,[y,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}h_{1}\left(x\right)\left([y,2t]\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
h_{2}\left(x\right)\left([y,2t-1]\right),\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1\end{cases}

Clearly,
J\left(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\right)
is represented by
\tilde{h}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu
. On the other hand,
\tilde{h}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu
represents
J[h_{1}]+J[h_{2}]
as well. Hence,
J\left(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\right)=J\alpha_{1}+J\alpha_{2}
, i.e.,
J
is a group homomorphism.

Proof of (2) follows immediately from the definition of
J
.

Next, suppose
X=\Sigma X^{\prime}
is a suspension. Then,
J[\varepsilon]+J[\varepsilon]=[\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]+[\tilde{\varepsilon}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]
is represented by
\phi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu
, where

\displaystyle\phi:C\Sigma X^{\prime}\times Y\cup\Sigma X^{\prime}\times Y

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma Y

\displaystyle\left([[x^{\prime},s],t],y\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\bullet

\displaystyle\left([x^{\prime},s],[y,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}[y,t],\quad 0\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
[y,t],\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq s\leq 1\end{cases}

Clearly,
\phi=\tilde{\varepsilon}
, and thus

J[\varepsilon]+J[\varepsilon]=J[\varepsilon]\Rightarrow J[\varepsilon]=0
, which proves (3).

Proof of (4) follows from (2) and (3).
∎

6.2.
Generalized Brace Product for a Fibration over a Suspension

Let us consider a fibration
\Sigma F\hookrightarrow E\rightarrow\Sigma B
, where the characteristic map
\rho:B\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma F,\Sigma F)
satisfies
\rho(\bullet)=\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}
. In particular, we have

E=CB\times\Sigma F\cup_{\rho}CB\times\Sigma F,

given as the clutching construction i.e,

\displaystyle E=((CB\times\Sigma F)\sqcup(CB\times\Sigma F))/\sim

\displaystyle([b,1],[f,t])\sim([b,1],\rho(b)[f,t])

Note that we have a canonical section
s:\Sigma B\rightarrow E
given by
s([b,t])=\left[[b,t],\bullet\right]
, which is well-defined since
\rho
fixes the base point of
\Sigma F
. We now relate the generalized brace product with the generalized
J
-homomorphism.

Theorem 6.4
.

Suppose
\Sigma F\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}E\rightarrow\Sigma B
is a fibration as above with the canonical section
s
.

(1)

For
\varepsilon:B\rightarrow Map_{\bullet}(\Sigma F,\Sigma F)
defined by
\varepsilon(b)=\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}
, we have

\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\right\}_{s}=J[\varepsilon]-J[\rho].

In particular, if
B
is a suspension, then

\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\right\}_{s}=-J[\rho].

(2)

For any base point preserving self homotopy equivalence
\varphi:\Sigma F\rightarrow\Sigma F
, we have

\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\varphi\right\}_{s}=J[\varphi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\varepsilon]-J[\rho].

In particular, if
B
is a suspension, then

\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\varphi\right\}_{s}=-J[\rho].

Proof.

(1) Firstly, observe that the total space
E
is given as the attaching space

(6.1)

E\simeq\left(CB\times CF\right)\cup_{\phi}\left(\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F\right),

where
\phi
is given as

(6.2)

\displaystyle\phi:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left([b,t],\bullet\right)

\displaystyle\left(b,[f,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\bullet,\rho(b)[f,t]\right)

Indeed, we have the diagram

(6.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\times\Sigma F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CB\times\Sigma F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CB\times\Sigma F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{\rho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

Here,
\hat{\rho}
is given as
(b,[f,t])\mapsto([b,1],\rho(b)[f,t])
. Both
E
and
\tilde{E}
are homotopy pushouts since the inclusion
B\times\Sigma F\hookrightarrow CB\times\Sigma F
is a cofibration (
[
3
, Prop. 6.2.6]
). Hence, the deformation retract
CB\times\Sigma F\rightarrow\Sigma F
collapsing
CB
induces a homotopy equivalence
\Phi:E\rightarrow\tilde{E}
, making the above diagram (homotopy) commutative. But clearly,
\tilde{E}=\left(CB\times CF\right)\cup_{\phi}(\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F)
, demonstrating (
6.1
).

Let us now consider the following maps.

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\bullet

\displaystyle\left(b,[f,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho(b)[f,t]

\displaystyle\epsilon:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\bullet

\displaystyle\left(b,[f,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto[f,1-t]

We then have,
J[\rho]=[\tilde{\rho}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu],J[\varepsilon]=-[\epsilon\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]
. We also have the Whitehead map

\displaystyle\omega:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left([b,t],\bullet\right)

\displaystyle\left(b,[f,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\bullet,[f,t]\right)

It follows from Remark
2.8
that

i_{*}\left\{\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma B},\mathrm{Id}_{\Sigma F}\right\}_{s}=[s,i]=[\left(s\vee i\right)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\omega]=(s\vee i)_{*}[\omega].

We claim that

(6.4)

[\phi\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]=[\iota\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\tilde{\rho}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]+[\iota\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\epsilon\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu]+[\omega\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}}\mu],

where
\iota:\Sigma F\hookrightarrow\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F
is the inclusion. Indeed, the right-hand side is represented by

, where
\psi
is the following map.

\displaystyle\psi:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\left(\bullet,\bullet\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{2}{3}\\
\left([b,3t-2],\bullet\right),\quad\frac{2}{3}\leq t\leq 1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\left(b,[f,t]\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}\left(\bullet,\rho(b)[f,3t]\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{3}\\
\left(\bullet,[f,2-3t]\right),\quad\frac{1}{3}\leq t\leq\frac{2}{3}\\
\left(\bullet,[f,3t-2]\right),\quad\frac{2}{3}\leq t\leq 1\end{cases}

Consider the homotopy

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}_{s}:CB\times F\cup B\times CF

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma B\vee\Sigma F

\displaystyle\left([b,t],f\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}(\bullet,\bullet),\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{2(1-s)}{3}\\

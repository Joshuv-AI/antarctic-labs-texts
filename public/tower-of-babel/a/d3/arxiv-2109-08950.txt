Picard approximation of a singular backward stochastic nonlinear Volterra integral equation
Arzu Ahmadova, Nazim I. Mahmudov

Abstract
Backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) belong nowadays to the most frequently studied equations in stochastic analysis and computational stochastics. In this paper we prove that Picard iterations of BSDEs with globally Lipschitz continuous nonlinearities converge exponentially fast to the solution. Our main result in this paper is to establish a fundamental lemma to prove the global existence and uniqueness of an adapted solution to a singular backward stochastic nonlinear Volterra integral equation (for short singular BSVIE) of order α ∈ ( 1 2 , 1 ) \alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1) under a weaker condition than Lipschitz one in Hilbert space. Keywords: Singular backward stochastic equations, backward stochastic nonlinear Volterra integral equation, existence and uniqueness, Picard iteration, adapted process, Carathéodory conditions

The study of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) has necessary applications in stochastic optimal control, stochastic differential games, the probabilistic formula for the solutions of quasilinear partial differential equations, and financial markets. We now introduce a fundamental lemma which plays an efficient role throughout this paper. To do so, we consider backward linear stochastic Volterra integral equation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
(x,y)\in M[t,T]
, the linear singular BSVIE

\displaystyle x(t)=\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}f(t,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\left[g(t,s)+y(t,s)\right]\mathrm{d}w(s),\quad\text{P-a.s.}

(2.1)

admits unique pair in
M[0,T]
and moreover

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\|x(s)\|^{2}+\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|y(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 8\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+16T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{16(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+2\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|g(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s.

(2.2)

Proof.

Uniqueness:
Let
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2})
be two solutions of (
2.1
).

\displaystyle x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)=\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\left[y_{1}(t,s)-y_{2}(t,s)\right]\mathrm{d}w(s),

Taking
\textbf{E}\left\{\cdot\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}
from above, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\left\{x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

It is obvious that
x_{1}(t)=x_{2}(t)
and this follows that
y_{1}(t,s)=y_{2}(t,s)
.

Existence:
Taking a conditional expectation from (
2.1
), we have

x(t)=\textbf{E}\left\{\xi\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\left\{f(t,s)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}\mathrm{d}s.

From extended martingale representation theorem, there exists
L(\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\mathscr{F}}([0,T],\mathcal{L}^{0}_{2})
and uniquely
K(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\mathscr{F}}(\mathcal{D};\mathcal{L}^{0}_{2})
which satisfy the following relations:

\textbf{E}\left\{\xi\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}=\textbf{E}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u),

(2.3)

\textbf{E}\left\{f(t,s)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}=\textbf{E}f(t,s)+\int_{0}^{t}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u).

(2.4)

Note also from (
2.4
), we can easily deduce that
\forall s\in[0,T]

K(s,u)=0,\quad\text{a.e.},\quad u\in[s,T],\quad\text{a.s.}

and that

\textbf{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s}|K(s,u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s\leq 4\textbf{E}\int_{0}^{T}|f(t,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

(2.5)

Since
t\in[0.T]
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}\xi+\int_{0}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}\xi+\int_{0}^{t}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)+\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}\left\{\xi\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}+\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u),

and since
s\geq t
, we have

\displaystyle f(t,s)

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}f(t,s)+\int_{0}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u)

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}f(t,s)+\int_{0}^{t}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u)+\int_{t}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u)

\displaystyle=\textbf{E}\left\{f(t,s)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}+\int_{t}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u).

Therefore, we obtain

\textbf{E}\left\{\xi\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}=\xi-\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u),

(2.6)

and

\textbf{E}\left\{f(t,s)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}=f(t,s)-\int_{t}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u).

(2.7)

Substituting (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) into (
2.8
) and using stochastic Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi-\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)\right)+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\left(f(t,s)-\int_{t}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u)\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}f(t,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)-\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\int_{t}^{s}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}w(u)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}f(t,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}L(u)\mathrm{d}w(u)-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{u}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}w(u).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle x(t)=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}f(t,s)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{y}(t,u)\mathrm{d}w(u).

Then there exists a mild solution
(x,y)\in M[0,T]
of (
2.1
) given by

x(t)=\textbf{E}\left\{\xi\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}+\int_{t}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\left\{f(t,s)\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}\mathrm{d}s,

(2.8)

and

\tilde{y}(t,u)=-L(u)-\int_{u}^{T}(s-t)^{\alpha-1}K(s,u)\mathrm{d}s.

(2.9)

We finally define
y(t,u)=\tilde{y}(t,u)-g(t,u)
,
(t,u)\in\mathcal{D}=\left\{(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+};0\leq t\leq u\leq T\right\}
.
It is easily seen that the pair
(x,y)
solves (
2.1
). Therefore, the existence is proved.

From (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we invoke the following inequalities for
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
:

\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|L(u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\leq 4\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2},

and

\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{s}\|K(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\leq 4\textbf{E}\|f(t,s)\|^{2}.

Now we estimate the solution
(x,y)
given by (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) in
[0,T]
. From (
2.8
) it follows that

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\|x(s)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\|\textbf{E}\left\{\xi|\mathscr{F}_{s}\right\}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\Big(\int_{s}^{T}(r-s)^{\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\left\{\|f(t,r)\|\quad|\mathscr{F}_{s}\right\}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{2}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.

From Doob’s inequality and the law of total expectation it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq 2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\textbf{E}\|\left\{\xi|\mathscr{F}_{s}\right\}\|^{2}\leq 8\textbf{E}\left(\textbf{E}\|\left\{\xi|\mathscr{F}_{t}\right\}\|^{2}\right)\leq 8\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}.

Doob’s inequality and Jensen’s inequality in probabilistic setting imply that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\left(\textbf{E}\Biggl\{\int_{s}^{T}(r-s)^{\alpha-1}\|f(t,r)\|\mathrm{d}r\quad|\mathscr{F}_{s}\Biggr\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\left(\textbf{E}\Biggl\{\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}(r-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\|f(t,r)\|\mathrm{d}r\quad|\mathscr{F}_{s}\Biggr\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\textbf{E}\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}(r-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\|f(t,r)\|\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}(r-\tau)^{2\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\tau}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8\frac{(T-t)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\ \textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

Eventually, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\|x(s)\|^{2}\leq 8\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+\frac{8(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

(2.10)

Next we estimate
\tilde{y}
using Hölder’s inequality. We attain,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{y}(s,u)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|L(u))\|^{2}+2\left\lVert\int_{u}^{T}(r-s)^{\alpha-1}K(r,u)\mathrm{d}r\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|L(u)\|^{2}+2\left(\frac{(T-s)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}-\frac{(u-s)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\right)\int_{u}^{T}\|K(r,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 2\|L(u)\|^{2}+2\frac{(T+u)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\int_{u}^{T}\|K(r,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 2\|L(u)\|^{2}+2\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\int_{u}^{T}\|K(r,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

Taking double integral of above inequality and applying Fubini’s theorem twice yield that

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|\tilde{y}(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s\leq 2\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|L(u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+2\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\int_{u}^{T}\|K(r,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 8(T-t)\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+2\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\int_{s}^{r}\|K(r,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\tau}^{r}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau\leq T}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\tau}^{r}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}(T-t)}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+8\frac{(2T)^{2\alpha-1}T}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+\frac{4(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|\tilde{y}(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s\leq 8T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+\frac{4(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r.

(2.11)

Since we know
y(t,u)=\tilde{y}(t,u)-g(t,u)
, we also have

\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|y(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s\leq 2\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|\tilde{y}(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|g(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s.

(2.12)

Taking into account (
2.12
) and summing over (
2.10
) and (
2.11

\displaystyle\textbf{E}\sup_{t\leq s\leq T}\|x(s)\|^{2}+\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|y(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 8\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}+16T\textbf{E}\|\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{16(2T)^{2\alpha}}{2\alpha-1}\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\|f(t,r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+2\textbf{E}\int_{t}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\|g(s,u)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s.

4
Conclusions and future works

In this paper, we first formulated new problem in BSDE theory which is singular backward stochastic nonlinear Volterra differential equation, is an untreated topic in recent literature. To derive an adapted pair of stochastic processes, we first formulated fundamental lemma which plays a crucial role in the theory of singular BSDE. The main results in our paper were to show existence and uniqueness of an adapted solution to (
1
) in infinite dimensional setting using Carathéodory-type condition. In doing so, we constructed Picard type approximation. The key point in the proof of main results was to apply extended martingale representation theorem and Bihari’s inequality.

Since our results are sufficiently new in the theory of BSDEs, there are still open problems to discuss regarding their applications to finance and optimal control theory using stochastic maximum principle.

A homogenization result in finite plasticity
Elisa Davoli, Chiara Gavioli, Valerio Pagliari

Abstract
. We carry out a variational study for integral functionals that model the stored energy of a heterogeneous material governed by finite-strain elastoplasticity with hardening. Assuming that the composite has a periodic microscopic structure, we establish the Γ \Gamma -convergence of the energies in the limiting of vanishing periodicity. The constraint that plastic deformations belong to 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathsf{SL}(3) poses the biggest hurdle to the analysis, and we address it by regarding 𝖲𝖫 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathsf{SL}(3) as a Finsler manifold. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 49J45; 74C15; 74E30; 74Q05. Keywords and phrases: finite-strain elastoplasticity, Γ \Gamma -convergence, homogenization, Finsler manifold.



1.
Introduction

This paper deals with the homogenization of some variational integrals
related to the theory of finite plasticity.
Precisely, we focus on those materials
governed by finite-strain elastoplasticity with hardening.
Starting from the microscopic level,
we aim at retrieving effective macroscopic descriptions
by means of a
\Gamma
-convergence approach.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an open, bounded, connected set with Lipschitz boundary, and let us denote by
\mathsf{SL}(3)
the group of
3\times 3
real matrices with determinant equal to
1
. Our main result describes the limiting behavior of the functionals

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(y,P)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla y(x)P^{-1}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},P(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla P(x)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.1)

where
y\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
P\in W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
with
q>3
and
K\subset\mathsf{SL}(3)
compact,
and
W
and
H
encode, respectively, the periodic nonlinear elastic energy density and the hardening
of the elastoplastic material sitting in
\Omega
.
The precise statement is contained in Theorem
2.2
,
and the exact mathematical setting of the analysis is detailed in Section
2
.

As we touched upon before, we work within the classical mathematical theory of elastoplasticity at finite strains (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
). We assume that the elastic behavior of our sample
\Omega
is independent of preexistent plastic distortions. This can be rephrased as the assumption that the deformation gradient
\nabla y
associated with any deformation
y\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
of the body decomposes into an elastic strain and a plastic one. Within the framework of linearized elastoplasticity, the decomposition would have an additive nature; in the setting of finite plasticity, instead, a multiplicative structure is traditionally assumed
[
27
,
28
]
. Following this approach, as in
[
30
,
31
]
, we suppose that for any deformation
y\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

there exist an
elastic strain

F_{\mathrm{el}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
and
a
plastic strain

P\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\rm SL}(3))
such that

\nabla y(x)=F_{\mathrm{el}}(x)P(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for a.\,e.\ }x\in\Omega.

Such multiplicative decomposition, recently justified in the setting of dislocation systems and crystal plasticity in
[
10
,
11
]
, motivates the definitions of the energy functionals in (
1.1
).
Other constitutive models in finite plasticity have been also taken into account in the mathematical literature (see, e.g.,
[
15
,
21
,
22
,
33
]
).
We point out that the stored energy in (
1.1
) has already been considered in
[
32
]
in a variational setting. We refer to
[
31
]
for more general models in finite plasticity including hardening variables.
Concerning the regularization via
\nabla P
in (
1.1
),
we note that it is common in engineering models and prevents the formation of microstructures, see
[
4
,
7
]
. We also refer to
[
18
]
for a discussion of its drawbacks and for alternative higher order regularizations.

In the small strain regime, the homogenization of the plasticity equations has been undertaken in the series of works
[
34
,
26
,
25
]
both in the periodic and in the aperiodic and stochastic settings. The framework of perfect plasticity in the case of a linear elastic response has been completely characterized in the seminal work
[
20
]
.
See also
[
24
]
for a homogenization result in the small strain regime with hardening.
To the authors’ knowledge, the only available results in the large strain setting are instead confined to the framework of crystal plasticity for stratified composites. In particular, we refer to
[
8
,
9
]
for the superlinear growth case, to
[
14
]
for the linear growth scenario, and to
[
17
]
for some first homogenization analysis in the evolutionary setting.
Theorem
2.2
fills a gap in the study of elastoplastic microstructures
by providing a static homogenization result in the large strain regime.
As an application of our contribution, in
[
16
]

the analysis of high-contrast elastoplastic materials is carried out.

We conclude this introduction with a few words on the proofs.
The equicoervicity of
\{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\}
and the semicontinuity of the elastic term require uniform bounds on the plastic strains (cf. (
2.1
), see also
[
32
]
). To enforce them, we prescribe the effective domain of the hardening to be a compact neighborhood of the identity in
\mathsf{SL}(3)
.
The existence of an integral
\Gamma
-limit, instead, is based on the localization technique in the context of integral representation results. The biggest hurdle here is devising a version of the so-called
fundamental estimate
that complies with the constraint that plastic deformations must belong to the compact neighborhood of the identity.
Note that this poses an additional difficulty with respect to, e.g.,
[
2
,
12
]
, which deal with manifold-valued Sobolev functions.
Indeed, not only we constrain our maps to take values in a manifold,
but we also require them to range in a specific set.
To accommodate for this, an adequate notion of convexity is needed. Therefore, we will regard
\mathsf{SL}(3)
as a manifold endowed with a Finsler metric, and impose the compact neighborhood of the identity to be also geodesically convex.
We refer to Section
3
for a review of the localization method and of the Finsler structure on
\mathsf{SL}(3)
.
The characterization of the limiting energy density is obtained by a perturbative argument that grounds on standard homogenization results, see Theorem
3.1
.

Outline

The setup of our analysis and the main result, Theorem
2.2
,
is presented in Section
2
.
Section
3
contains some reminders about the technical tools to be used in the sequel.
The proof of Theorem
2.2
is the subject of Section
4
, which we conclude with the discussion on compactness of bounded energy sequences and convergence of (almost) minimizers.

2.
Mathematical setting and results

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
is an open, bounded, and connected set
with Lipschitz boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

(the analysis would not change significantly
if we settled the problem in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d=2
or
d>3
).
We use
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3\times 3}

to denote real-valued
3\times 3
and
3\times 3\times 3
tensors, respectively.
We use the notation
I
for the identity matrix.
The symbol
|\,\cdot\,|
is indiscriminately adopted
for the Euclidean norms in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3\times 3}
.
To deal with plastic strains, we recall the classical notation

\displaystyle\mathsf{SL}(3)\coloneqq\{F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\det F=1\}.

In Subsection
3.2
we will endow
\mathsf{SL}(3)

with a metric structure and regard it as a
Finsler manifold
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a measurable set,
we will denote by
\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)
its three-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

The building block of our study is the following variational notion of convergence:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a set endowed with a notion of convergence. We say that the family
\{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\}
,
with
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]
,
\Gamma
-converges
as
\varepsilon\to 0
to
\mathcal{G}\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]
if for all
x\in X
and all infinitesimal sequences
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
the following holds:

(1)

for every sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
such that
x_{k}\to x
, we have

\mathcal{G}(x)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{k});

(2)

there exists a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
such that
x_{k}\to x
and

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{k})\leq\mathcal{G}(x).

When
X
is equipped with a topology
\tau
,
we sometimes use expressions such as
\Gamma(\tau)
-convergence or
\Gamma(\tau)
-limit
to emphasize the underlying convergence for sequences in
X
.
We elaborate on the connections between
\Gamma
-convergence and homogenization in Subsection
3.1
.
In what follows, for notational convenience,
we indicate the dependence on
\varepsilon_{k}
by means of the subscript
k
alone,
e.g.,
\mathcal{F}_{k}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{k}}
.

We collect here the assumptions under which
the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals in (
1.1
) will be proved.

Let
Q\coloneqq[0,1)^{3}
be the periodicity cell. We assume that
the elastic energy density
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies the following:

E1:

It is a Carathéodory function such that

W(\,\cdot\,,F)
is
Q
-periodic for every
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

E2:

It is
2
-coercive and has at most quadratic growth, i.e.,
there exist
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}
such that for a. e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

c_{1}|F|^{2}\leq W(x,F)\leq c_{2}\left(|F|^{2}+1\right).

E3:

It is
2
-Lipschitz:
there exists
c_{3}>0
such that for a. e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for all
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

|W(x,F_{1})-W(x,F_{2})|\leq c_{3}\left(1+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|\right)|F_{1}-F_{2}|.

In
E2
–
E3
the exponent
2
may be well replaced by some generic
p>1
. We do not pursue this direction just for notational convenience.

Let us compare
E1
–
E3

with the common requirements for physically admissible elastic energy densities.
Setting

\mathsf{GL}_{+}(3)\coloneqq\{F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\det F>0\},

a couple of natural conditions are
W\equiv+\infty
on
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\setminus\mathsf{GL}_{+}(3)
, which rules out interpenetrations, and
\lim_{\det F\to 0}W(F)=+\infty
, which means that it should take infinite energy to squeeze a small block of material down to a point.
Feasible elastic densities are also required to be frame indifferent
and to attain their minimum, conventionally set to
0
, on the identity matrix
I
.

In our analysis, it would be particularly challenging to preserve the constraint of positive determinant for deformation gradients.
Therefore, we work in a simplified setting and consider the whole space of
3\times 3
matrices as domain for
W
.
Similarly, the quadratic growth from above in
E2
rules out the blow up of the energy densities, but it is a rather common assumption in variational studies.
Frame indifference, that is,
W(x,RF)=W(x,F)
for a. e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, for all
F\in\mathsf{GL}_{+}(3)
and all rotations
R
, does not play any role in our analysis, thus we ignore it.
Finally, up to adding a constant to
W
, the growth condition
E2
is compatible with the requirement
\min W(x,\,\cdot\,)=W(x,I)=0
for a. e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

As for the hardening functional,
we assume that

H\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]

meets the ensuing requirements:

H1:

H
is a Carathéodory function such that
H(\,\cdot\,,F)
is
Q
-periodic for every
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

H2:

Assume that a Finsler structure on
\mathsf{SL}(3)
is assigned.
For a. e.
x\in\Omega
,
H(x,F)
is finite if and only if
F\in K
,
where
K\subset\mathsf{SL}(3)
is a geodesically convex, compact neighborhood of
I
.

H3:

The restriction of
H(x,\,\cdot\,)
to
K
is Lipschitz continuous, uniformly in
x
.

Let us spend some words on
H2
.
This rather strong hypothesis prescribes that
the effective domain of
H(x,\,\cdot\,)
, namely the set

\left\{F\in\mathsf{SL}(3):H(x,F)<+\infty\right\}
,
is an
x
-independent compact set containing
I
. This kind of constraint has already appeared in the variational literature, see
[
32
, Formulas (2.6a)–(2.6b)]
.
A fundamental consequence of technical advantage is that
uniform
L^{\infty}
-bounds on the plastic strains become available,
provided the latter give rise to finite hardening.
Indeed, if
K
is as in
H2
, then
there exists
c_{K}>0
such that

|F|+|F^{-1}|\leq c_{K}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }F\in K,

(2.1)

because
\mathsf{SL}(3)
is by definition well separated from
0
.
From such bound we infer that
for any
F\in K
and
G\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}

\left|G\right|=\left|GF^{-1}F\right|\leq c_{K}\left|GF^{-1}\right|.

(2.2)

These bounds will be employed several times in our analysis to obtain estimates on the deformation gradient
\nabla y
starting from a control on the elastic strain
\nabla yP^{-1}
.

The further requirement that
the effective domain
K
is geodesically convex
(in the Finsler sense we mentioned, see Subsection
3.2
for the details)
roots in the localization argument that we adopt
to establish the
\Gamma
-convergence (see Subsection
3.1
).
We recall that
a subset of a Finsler manifold is said to be geodesically convex
if, for any couple of points in the set,
there is a unique shortest path contained in the set
that joins those two points.
The existence of a compact set
K

complying with
H2

is ensured by Proposition
3.3
and Remark
3.5
below.

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper, namely
the homogenization formula for the functionals in (
1.1
).
Recalling that we chose
q>3
, we work in the space
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3))

endowed with the topology
\tau
characterized by

(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if and only if}\hskip 10.00002pt\begin{cases}y_{k}\to y&\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\[2.84526pt]
P_{k}\to P&\text{uniformly}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

The topology
\tau
is the “right one” from a variational perspective in view of Corollary
2.3
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
be the functionals in (
1.1
),
which we extend by setting

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(y,P)=+\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\big[L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3))\big]\setminus\big[W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)\big].

If
W
and
H
satisfy
E1
–
E3

and
H1
–
H3
, respectively,
then for all
y\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
P\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3))

the
\Gamma
-limit

\mathcal{F}(y,P)\coloneqq\Gamma(\tau)\mbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(y,P)

exists. We also have that

\mathcal{F}(y,P)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(W_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla y(x),P(x))&+H_{\mathrm{hom}}(P(x))+|\nabla P(x)|^{q}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\text{if }(y,P)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K),\\[3.0pt]
+\infty\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad&\text{otherwise in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3)),\end{aligned}\right.

where
W_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\times K\to[0,+\infty)
and
H_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon K\to[0,+\infty)
are defined as

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{hom}}(F,G)\coloneqq\lim_{\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda{}^{3}}\inf\left\{\int_{(0,\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0})^{3}}W\big(x,(F+\nabla y(x))G^{-1}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x:y\in W^{1,2}_{0}((0,\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0})^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\},

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)\coloneqq\int_{Q}H(z,F)\,\mathrm{d}z.

By standard
\Gamma
-convergence arguments, we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
be an infinitesimal sequence.

(i)

Suppose that
\{(y_{k},P_{k})\}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3))
satisfies

\|y_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq c,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k})\leq c

for some
c>0
, uniformly in
k
. Then, there exist subsequences of
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
,
\{y_{k}\},
and
\{P_{k}\}
, which we do not relabel, as well as
y\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
P\in W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
, such that
(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)
.

(ii)

Let
\{(y_{k},P_{k})\}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
be a sequence of almost minimizers, that is,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k})-\inf\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P)\Big)=0,

where the infimum is taken over
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
. Then, there exists a minimizer
(y,P)\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
of
\mathcal{F}
such that, up to subsequences,
(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)
. Moreover,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Big(\inf\mathcal{F}_{k}-\min\mathcal{F}\Big)=0.

3.
Preliminaries

We gather in this section the technical tools
to be employed in the sequel.

3.1.
Localization and integral representation

To the aim of laying the ground for the proof of Theorem
2.2
,
we briefly outline the localization technique
in the context of integral representation results for
\Gamma
-limits.
More detail and a thorough treatment of
\Gamma
-convergence,
which we introduced in Definition
2.1
,
may instead be found in the monographs
[
6
,
13
,
5
]
.
For definiteness, we report on a well-known result
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 14.5]
) that is underpinned by the localization method.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty)
be a Borel function
that is
Q
-periodic in its first argument and
that satisfies standard
p
-growth conditions for some
p\in(1,+\infty)
.
For
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

we define

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}g\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla y(x)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x}&\text{if }y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\[3.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, we have

\Gamma(L^{p})\mbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}g_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(\nabla y(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x}&\text{if }y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\[3.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the
\Gamma
-limit is taken with respect to the strong
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
-topology,
and

g_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty)
is a quasiconvex function
characterized by the asymptotic homogenization formula

g_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)=\lim_{\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda{}^{3}}\inf\left\{\int_{(0,\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0})^{3}}g\big(x,F+\nabla y(x)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x:y\in W^{1,p}_{0}((0,\lambda\color[rgb]{0,0,0})^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\}

for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

As starting point to establish this theorem,
one
resorts to a general property of
\Gamma
-convergence
which ensures that
if
X
is a separable metric space,
then any family
\{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\}

with
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]

has a
\Gamma
-convergent subsequence
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition 7.9]
). Such
\Gamma
-compactness principle yields that,
up to subsequences,
an abstract
\Gamma(L^{p})
-limit of
\{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\}
exists.
One is then naturally led to wonder
whether the limit is in turn a functional of integral type.
In order to show that this is in fact the case,
a
localization argument
is employed.
This amounts to regard

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
as a function of the pair
(y,A)
,
where
y\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{\text{open subsets of }\Omega\}
; more precisely, setting

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y,A)\coloneqq\int_{A}g\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla y(x)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 20.00003pt\text{if }y\in W^{1,p}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})

and
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y,A)\coloneqq+\infty
otherwise, the idea is to focus on the properties of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y,\,\cdot\,)
as a set function.
In this respect, we recall some terminology.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
be a set function.
We say that
\alpha
is

•

an increasing set function
if
\alpha(\emptyset)=0
and
\alpha(A)\leq\alpha(B)
if
A\subseteq B
;

•

subadditive
if
\alpha(A\cup B)\leq\alpha(A)+\alpha(B)
for all
A,B
;

•

superadditive
if
\alpha(A\cup B)\geq\alpha(A)+\alpha(B)

for all
A,B
such that
A\cap B=\emptyset
;

•

inner regular
if
\alpha(A)=\sup\left\{\alpha(B):B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega),\,B\Subset A\right\}
.

The De Giorgi-Letta criterion
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 10.2]
) states that
an increasing set function is a restriction to
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
of a Borel measure
if and only if
subadditive, superadditive and inner regular.
Therefore,
since the
\Gamma(L^{p})
-limit of
\{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\}
must be an increasing set function
and we work under
p
-growth conditions,
the integral representation boils down to proving that

\Gamma(L^{p})\mbox{-}\lim\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is subadditive, superadditive and inner regular.
The subadditivity and inner regularity are the most delicate points, and
they hinge in turn upon the so-called

fundamental estimate

(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Definition 11.2]
).
Roughly speaking,
given
A,B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
,
y\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

and sequences
\{y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\}
and
\{y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\}
converging to
y
in
L^{p}
such that

\Gamma(L^{p})\mbox{-}\lim\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y,A)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},A)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma(L^{p})\mbox{-}\lim\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y,B)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime},B),

the fundamental estimate allows to construct a third sequence
\{y_{\varepsilon}\}
converging to
y
in
L^{p}
and such that

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon},A\cup B)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},A)+\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime},B)+R_{\varepsilon},\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}R_{\varepsilon}=0,

with
R_{\varepsilon}
satisfying a precise estimate in terms of
\|y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(A\cap B)}
.
The exact form of the fundamental estimate to be used in our analysis is contained in (
4.3
),
while its application to establish subadditivity and inner regularity is discussed in Proposition
4.6
.

3.2.
Finsler structure on
\mathsf{SL}(3)

To the purpose of devising a form of the fundamental estimate
that suits the functionals under consideration,
it is convenient to endow
\mathsf{SL}(3)
with a metric structure.
In order to link the latter to the physics of the system we would like to model,
we follow the approach in
[
30
]
,
which is grounded on the concept of
plastic dissipation
.

We recall some basic facts about the geometry of
\mathsf{SL}(3)
,
which is a smooth manifold
with respect to the topology induced by the inclusion in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.
For every
F\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
the tangent space at
F
is characterized as

\mathrm{T}_{F}\mathsf{SL}(3)=F{\sf sl}(3)\coloneqq\{FM\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\mathrm{tr}M=0\}.

In particular,
\mathrm{T}_{I}\mathsf{SL}(3)
coincides with

{\sf sl}(3)\coloneqq\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\mathrm{tr}M=0\}
.
We equip
\mathsf{SL}(3)
with a Finsler structure
starting from a function
\Delta_{I}\colon{\sf sl}(3)\to[0,+\infty)

on which we make the following assumptions (cf.
[
3
, Section 1.1]
and
[
30
, Section 1]
):

D1:

It is
C^{2}
on
{\sf sl}(3)\setminus\{0\}
;

D2:

It is positively
1
-homogeneous:

\Delta_{I}(cM)=c\Delta_{I}(M)
for all
c>0
and
M\in{\sf sl}(3)
;

D3:

The function
\Delta_{I}^{2}/2
is strongly convex;

D4:

It is
1
-coercive and has at most linear growth:
there exist
0<c_{4}\leq c_{5}
such that for all
M\in{\sf sl}(3)

c_{4}|M|\leq\Delta_{I}(M)\leq c_{5}|M|.

We point out that
we work under stronger regularity assumptions than the ones in
[
30
]
.
This is mainly due to the fact that
we borrow results developed within the context of differential geometry,
where smoothness is customarily required.
As a consequence, some models,
such as single crystal plasticity,
are not covered by our analysis;
on the other hand, our assumptions encompass Von Mises plasticity,
see the considerations in Example
3.6

or
[
23
,
30
]
for a wider discussion.

We also recall that
Finsler structures are known to appear as
homogenized limits of periodic Riemannian metrics
[
1
]
.

Essentially,
\Delta_{I}
is a Minkowski norm on
{\sf sl}(3)
,
which we can “translate” to the other tangent spaces by setting

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\Delta\colon&\rm T\mathsf{SL}(3)&\to&[0,+\infty)\\[1.0pt]
&(F,M)&\mapsto&\Delta_{I}(F^{-1}M),\end{array}

where
\rm T\mathsf{SL}(3)
is the tangent bundle to
\mathsf{SL}(3)
.
Then, it can be checked that
(\mathsf{SL}(3),\Delta)
is a
C^{2}
Finsler manifold.
We refer to the monograph
[
3
]
by
Bao, Chern & Shen

for an introduction to Finsler geometry.

Let now
\mathcal{C}(F_{0},F_{1})
be the family of piecewise
C^{2}
curves

\Phi\colon[0,1]\to\mathsf{SL}(3)

such that
\Phi(0)=F_{0}
and
\Phi(1)=F_{1}
.
We define a non-symmetric distance on
\mathsf{SL}(3)
as follows:

D(F_{0},F_{1})\coloneqq\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\big(\Phi(t),\dot{\Phi}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t:\Phi\in\mathcal{C}(F_{0},F_{1})\right\},

(3.1)

where
\dot{\Phi}
is the velocity of the curve.
The function
D
is positive,
attains
0
if and only if it is evaluated on the diagonal of
\mathsf{SL}(3)\times\mathsf{SL}(3)
,
and fulfills the triangle inequality;
in general, however,
D(F_{0},F_{1})\neq D(F_{1},F_{0})
.

From a physical viewpoint, if
P_{0},P_{1}\colon\Omega\to\mathsf{SL}(3)
are admissible plastic strains,
the integral of
D(P_{0},P_{1})
over
\Omega
is interpreted
as the minimum amount of energy
that is dissipated when the system moves from a plastic configuration to another.

Note that under assumptions
D1
–
D3
it follows that
\Delta
is subadditive (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.2.2]
), hence convex. Therefore, by an application of the direct method of the calculus of variations (cf.
[
30
, Theorem 5.1]
)
it can be proved that for every
F_{0},F_{1}\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
there exists a curve

\Phi\in C^{1,1}([0,1];\mathsf{SL}(3))
such that
\Phi(0)=F_{0}
,
\Phi(1)=F_{1}
and

D(F_{0},F_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\big(\Phi(t),\dot{\Phi}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We call such
\Phi
a shortest path between
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
.
The following result,
which summarizes the content of

[
3
, Exercise 6.3.3]
,
is crucial for the proof of the fundamental estimate.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
D1
–
D3
hold.
For any point
F
in the Finsler manifold
\mathsf{SL}(3)

there exists a relatively compact neighborhood
U
of
F
such that
for any
F_{0},F_{1}\in U
there exists a unique shortest path
\Phi

joining
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
, and
such path depends smoothly on its endpoints
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
.

The smooth dependence of shortest paths on their endpoints follows from the fact that each of them is a geodesic, and thus a solution of an ODE.
We recall that a path between
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
is called a geodesic
if it is a critical point of the length functional
under variations that do not change the endpoints, and that,
given
(F,M)\in T\mathsf{SL}(3)
such that
\Delta(F,M)
is sufficiently small,
there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma_{F,M}\colon(-2,2)\to\mathsf{SL}(3)

satisfying
\gamma_{F,M}(0)=F
and
\dot{\gamma}_{F,M}(0)=M
.
Therefore, we have (see
[
3
, p. 126]
) that, for
M
in a neighborhood of
0\in T_{F}\mathsf{SL}(3)
, we can define the so-called exponential map as

\exp(F,M)\coloneqq\begin{cases}F&\text{if }M=0,\\
\gamma_{F,M}(1)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For each
F\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
,

\exp(F,\,\cdot\,)
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism in a neighborhood of
0\in F\mathsf{sl}(3)

(see
[
3
, Section 5.3]
).
In particular, there
\exp(F,\,\cdot\,)
is invertible.
Denoting by
\exp_{F}^{-1}
its inverse,
if
\Phi
is the unique shortest path from
F_{0}
to
F_{1}
(Proposition
3.3
),
one sees that necessarily

\Phi(t)=\exp(F_{0},t\exp_{F_{0}}^{-1}(F_{1})),\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,1].

(3.2)

We conclude this section by noting that, while
D1
–
D3
characterize the Finslerian structure in an intrinsic way,
D4
instead relates the Minkowski norm to the Euclidean one in the ambient space. In general, when such an assumption is made,
the Finslerian distance
between two points of an embedded Finslerian manifold
is locally comparable to the Euclidean one.
A lower bound on the Finslerian distance in terms of the Euclidean one
may be retrieved by relying on the first inequality in
D4
and on the fact that shortest paths in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
are segments.
Since such a bound is not needed in the sequel, in the next lemma we present only the upper one.
The proof is based on the implicit function theorem. For notational convenience, we state it just in the specific case of
(\mathsf{SL}(3),\Delta)
, which is an embedded
8
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D1
–
D4
hold, and let
D
be as in (
3.1
).
For every
F\in\mathsf{SL}(3)

there exist a bounded open neighborhood
U
of
F
and
a constant
c>0
such that

D(F_{0},F_{1})\leq c\,|F_{1}-F_{0}|

for any
F_{0},F_{1}\in U
.

Proof.

Observe that, by definition of
D
and
\Delta

and by employing the upper bound in
D4
,
we have

\displaystyle D(F_{0},F_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\big(\Phi(t),\dot{\Phi}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Delta_{I}\big(\Phi(t)^{-1}\dot{\Phi}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\int_{0}^{1}\big|\Phi(t)^{-1}\dot{\Phi}(t)\big|\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.3)

for any piecewise
C^{2}
curve joining
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
.
In order to prove the desired estimate,
we need to choose
\Phi
suitably.

Since
\mathsf{SL}(3)
is a submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
,
we can locally represent it as a graph
defined on the affine tangent space at a given point.
To be precise, for any
F\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
,
let
\pi_{F}
be the affine tangent hyperplane to
\mathsf{SL}(3)
at
F
,

\hat{n}_{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
be a unit normal to
\mathsf{SL}(3)
at
F
, and

B_{r}(F)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
be the Euclidean ball
centered at
F
with radius
r>0
.
Then, there exist
a number
\delta>0
, a bounded, open neighborhood
U
of
F

and a smooth function
\psi\colon\pi_{F}\cap\overline{B_{\delta}(F)}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

U=\left\{G\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:G=F+M+\psi(M)\hat{n}_{F},\ M\in\pi_{F}\cap B_{\delta}(F)\right\}.

Now, given
F\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
and the
associated
\delta
,
U
and
\psi
,
consider
F_{0},F_{1}\in U
and the matrices
M_{0},M_{1}\in\pi_{F}\cap B_{\delta}(F)

such that

F_{i}=F+M_{i}+\psi(M_{i})\hat{n}_{F}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for }i=0,1.

(3.4)

Since
\pi_{F}\cap B_{\delta}(F)
is convex, the curve

\overline{\Phi}(t)\coloneqq F+M_{0}+t(M_{1}-M_{0})+\psi\Big(M_{0}+t(M_{1}-M_{0})\Big)\hat{n}_{F},\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,1]

is a path in
U
joining
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
.
We now choose
\Phi=\overline{\Phi}
in (
3.3
).
First, we observe that,
since
U
is bounded and
\overline{\Phi}(t)\in\mathsf{SL}(3)
for all
t
,
there exist a
U
-dependent constant
c>0

such that
|\overline{\Phi}^{-1}(t)|\leq c
for all
t

(cf. (
2.1
)).
Therefore, from (
3.3
) we infer

\displaystyle D(F_{0},F_{1})

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\big|\dot{\overline{\Phi}}(t)\big|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\Big|(M_{1}-M_{0})\Big(I+\nabla\psi\big(M_{0}+t(M_{1}-M_{0})\big)\Big)\Big|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\,|M_{1}-M_{0}|,

where we used that
\nabla\psi
is continuous on
\pi_{F}\cap\overline{B_{\delta}(F)}
. To conclude, it suffices to observe that, by (
3.4
),
|M_{1}-M_{0}|\leq c\,|F_{1}-F_{0}|
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Grounding on Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, we can show that
there exists a compact
K
meeting the requirements in
H2
.
Let
U
be a relatively compact neighborhood of
I\in\mathsf{SL}(3)

such that for any
F_{0},F_{1}\in U
there is a unique shortest path
\Phi

joining
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
. Up to restricting to a subset, we can suppose that
U
is such that Lemma
3.4
holds.
By a Finsler variant of a theorem by Whitehead
[
3
, Exercise 6.4.3]
,
there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
I

that is compactly contained in
U
and
geodesically convex.
Since
K\coloneqq\bar{V}\subset U
,
there is a unique shortest path
\Phi

from
F_{0}
to
F_{1}
for any
F_{0},F_{1}\in K
.
By (
3.2
), the path depends smoothly on its endpoints.
The fact that
K
is geodesically convex as well follows then by the same argument that proves that the closure of a convex set is still convex.

Example 3.6

(Von Mises plasticity)
.

Von Mises plasticity is a model for polycrystals, such as metals,
where the presence of grains averages out the different crystallographic directions.
As detailed in
[
30
, Section 4.1]
,
in such model the Minkowski norm
\Delta_{I}
is given
by a scalar product on the tangent space
\mathsf{sl}(3)
.
This is equivalent to assuming that
\mathsf{SL}(3)
is equipped with a left-invariant Riemannian metric. Exploiting the properties of
\mathsf{sl}(3)
,
\Delta_{I}
can be put into the general form (see
[
23
, Formula (5)]
or
[
30
, Formula (4.3)]
)

\Delta_{I}(M)=\left(\beta_{1}|M_{\rm sym}|^{2}+\beta_{2}|M_{\rm anti}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\ \mbox{with }M_{\rm sym}=\frac{M+M^{T}}{2},\ M_{\rm anti}=\frac{M-M^{T}}{2},

for some positive material parameters
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
.
Here
|\,\cdot\,|
denotes the matrix Frobenius norm, that is,
the (element-wise) Euclidean norm
|\,\cdot\,|
of
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.
Note that
\Delta_{I}
does satisfy assumptions
D1
–
D4
. By standard results in Riemannian geometry (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Chapter 3, Proposition 4.2]
), for any matrix in
\mathsf{sl}(3)
there exists a number
r>0
such that the closure of the geodesic ball of radius
r
is geodesically convex. We recall that geodesic balls are defined as the images through the exponential map of balls centered at the origin on the tangent space, provided the exponential map is there a diffeomorphism (see
[
19
, p. 70]
). In conclusion, for the case of Von Mises plasticity, the set
K
in
H2
can be chosen as the closure of a suitable geodesic ball centered at the identity.

4.
Homogenization in finite plasticity

We devote this section to the proof of Theorem
2.2
,
that is, we exhibit the
\Gamma(\tau)
-limit of the functionals in (
1.1
).
We recall that the topology
\tau
was introduced in (
2.3
).

Remark 4.1
.

The energy functionals at stake depend on the plastic strain
P
through its inverse
P^{-1}
.
In this respect, it is useful to notice that
if
P_{k}\to P
uniformly,
then
P_{k}^{-1}\to P^{-1}
uniformly as well.
Indeed, recalling that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can write

P_{k}^{-1}=\frac{\left({\rm cof}P_{k}\right)^{T}}{\det P_{k}}=\left({\rm cof}P_{k}\right)^{T},

we deduce convergence for the sequence of inverses.

Since
(W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathsf{SL}(3)),\tau)
is a separable metric space,
we know from general properties of
\Gamma
-convergence that,
up to extraction of subsequences,
the functionals in (
1.1
)
\Gamma(\tau)
-converge.
Our main task is therefore to show that the limit is an integral functional,
and we achieve this by a localization approach
as the one streamlined in Subsection
3.1
.
Precisely, denoting by
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
the family of open subset of
\Omega
,
with a slight abuse of notation,
for any triple
(y,P,A)\in L^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(A;\mathsf{SL}(3))\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)

we set

\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,A)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\int_{A}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla yP^{-1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x&+\displaystyle\int_{A}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{A}|\nabla P|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\\[6.0pt]
&\text{if }(y,P)\in W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(A;K),\\[3.0pt]
+\infty\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad&\text{otherwise in }L^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(A;\mathsf{SL}(3)),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where
{\varepsilon_{k}}
is an infinitesimal sequence.

We first show that
the limits of
\Gamma
-convergent subsequences are in turn integral functionals.
Second, we characterize the limiting energy densities,
proving as well that
the whole family
\{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}\}

\Gamma
-converges.

4.1.
Integral representation

In this subsection we establish the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be as in (
4.1
), where
W
and
H
satisfy
E1
–
E3
and
H1
–
H3
, respectively.
Then, up to subsequences,
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}

\Gamma(\tau)
-converges and
for all
y\in L^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
P\in L^{q}(A;\mathsf{SL}(3))

\Gamma(\tau)\mbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,A)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{A}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{if }(y,P)\in W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(A;K),\\[6.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise in }L^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{q}(A;\mathsf{SL}(3)),\end{cases}

(4.2)

for some
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(which depends on the subsequence).

As a first step, we introduce a version of the fundamental estimate
fit for the functionals in (
4.1
).
For our purposes (cf. Proposition
4.5
), it suffices to formulate it for functions in
W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(A;K)
, which is the effective domain of the functionals
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,,A)
in (
4.1
) for
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
.
We recall that,
given
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
with
A^{\prime}\Subset A

(i.e.,
\overline{A^{\prime}}
is a compact set contained in
A
),
we say that a function
\varphi
is a
cut-off function

between
A^{\prime}
and
A
if
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(A)
,

0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
\varphi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\overline{A^{\prime}}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of functionals
\mathcal{F}\colon W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)\times\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies uniformly the
fundamental estimate

if for every
A,A^{\prime},B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
with
A^{\prime}\Subset A
and for every
\sigma>0
there exists a constant
M_{\sigma}>0
with the following property: for all
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{C}
and for every
(y_{1},P_{1}),(y_{2},P_{2})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)

there exist a cut-off function
\varphi
between
A^{\prime}
and
A

and a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\times K\times K\to K
satisfying
\gamma(0,F,G)=F
,
\gamma(1,F,G)=G
for all
F,G\in K
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(\varphi y_{1}+(1-\varphi)y_{2},\gamma\circ(\varphi,P_{2},P_{1}),A^{\prime}\cup B\big)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sigma)\left(\mathcal{F}(y_{1},P_{1},A)+\mathcal{F}(y_{2},P_{2},B)\right)

\displaystyle+M_{\sigma}\int_{(A\cap B)\setminus A^{\prime}}\left(|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+|P_{1}-P_{2}|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\sigma.

The use of the path
\gamma
in (
4.3
) is motivated
by the simple observation that
the “convex combination”
\varphi P_{1}+(1-\varphi)P_{2}
does not belong
to
\mathsf{SL}(3)
in general.
For example, if we let

P_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptP_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),

we see that

\det P_{1}=\det P_{2}=1
, but
\det(P_{1}/2+P_{2}/2)=0
.
However, not any path
\gamma
is appropriate for our purposes,
because in general it may not be completely contained in
K
, that is, in the domain of
H
. Moreover, the regularization term
\nabla P
calls for some regularity of
\gamma
with respect to its endpoints (see (
4.5
)) and for a specific bound on the velocity
\dot{\gamma}
(see (
4.6
)). To tackle such issues, in Proposition
4.4
we select
\gamma
suitably.

The next statement grants that
the class
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}
meets the definition above.

Proposition 4.4
.

The sequence of functionals

\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
defined in (
4.1
)
satisfies uniformly the fundamental estimate (
4.3
),
upon choosing
\gamma
as the map
that associates to
(t,F,G)\in[0,1]\times K\times K

the image at
t
of the unique shortest path connecting
F
and
G
.

Proof.

Fix
A,A^{\prime},B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
with
A^{\prime}\Subset A
and
\sigma>0
. Fix also
(y_{1},P_{1}),(y_{2},P_{2})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
.
We need to choose suitably the cut-off function
\varphi
.

Let
\delta\coloneqq{\rm dist}(A^{\prime},\partial A)>0
.
For a fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>1
, we define

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq A^{\prime},

\displaystyle C_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{x\in A:{\rm dist}(x,A^{\prime})<\frac{j}{N}\delta\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptj=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle D_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq C_{j}\setminus C_{j-1}.

Observe that for all

we can construct a cut-off function
\varphi_{j}

between

.
Now, setting
\gamma^{-1}(t,F,G)\coloneqq(\gamma(t,F,G))^{-1}
for all

,

\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\varphi_{j}y_{1}+(1-\varphi_{j})y_{2},\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1}),A^{\prime}\cup B\right)=I_{\rm{el}}+I_{\mathrm{hard}}+I_{\rm{reg}},

(4.4)

where

\displaystyle I_{\rm el}\coloneqq\int_{A^{\prime}\cup B}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\big[\varphi_{j}\nabla y_{1}+(1-\varphi_{j})\nabla y_{2}+\nabla\varphi_{j}\otimes(y_{1}-y_{2})\big]\big(\gamma^{-1}\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big)\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{hard}}\coloneqq\int_{A^{\prime}\cup B}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle I_{\rm reg}\coloneqq\int_{A^{\prime}\cup B}\left|\nabla\big(\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big)\right|^{q}\!\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
P_{1},P_{2}\in K
, (
2.1
) holds.
We now choose
\gamma
as the map such that
\gamma(t,F,G)
is the evaluation at

of the unique shortest path connecting

for all

.
By Proposition

we know that such
\gamma

that it lies completely in
K

(see

),
and that it depends smoothly on its arguments.

Let us estimate the three summands above separately.
For the elastic contribution we have

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{el}}

\displaystyle=\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap C_{j-1}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{1}P_{1}^{-1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\setminus C_{j}}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{2}P_{2}^{-1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap D_{j}}\!\!W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\big[\varphi_{j}\nabla y_{1}+(1-\varphi_{j})\nabla y_{2}+\nabla\varphi_{j}\otimes(y_{1}-y_{2})\big]\big(\gamma^{-1}\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big)\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{1}P_{1}^{-1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B}W\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{2}P_{2}^{-1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+c\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\left(1+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla y_{2}|^{2}+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{2}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we used the fact that
(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap C_{j-1}\subseteq C_{j-1}\subseteq A
,
(A^{\prime}\cup B)\setminus C_{j}\subseteq B
,
(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap D_{j}=B\cap D_{j}
, the growth condition
E2
and the uniform bound (
2.1
).
Analogously, for the second summand we find

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{hard}}

\displaystyle=\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap C_{j-1}}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\setminus C_{j}}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap D_{j}}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{1}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B}H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(B\cap D_{j}),

where we exploited again the geodesic convexity of
K
and
the fact that
H
is bounded on
K
, since it is Lipschitz.

We now estimate
I_{\rm reg}
. The chain rule yields

\nabla\big(\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big)\\
=\big[\dot{\gamma}\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big]\otimes\nabla\varphi_{j}+\big[\partial_{F}\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big]\nabla P_{2}+\big[\partial_{G}\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big]\nabla P_{1},

(4.5)

whence, observing that by Proposition
3.3
the two differentials
\partial_{F}\gamma
and
\partial_{G}\gamma
are continuous functions
restricted to compact sets,

\displaystyle I_{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap C_{j-1}}|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\setminus C_{j}}|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{(A^{\prime}\cup B)\cap D_{j}}\left|\nabla\big(\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})\big)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B}|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{q}\int_{B\cap D_{j}}|\dot{\gamma}\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1})|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+c\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\big(|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We now resort to the explicit expression of
\gamma
in terms of the exponential map, see (
3.2
).
From (
2.1
) and recalling that by definition
\exp_{F}^{-1}(F)=0
, it follows

\displaystyle|\dot{\gamma}(t,F,G)|

\displaystyle=|\exp\big(F,t\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)\big)\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)|

\displaystyle\leq c\,|\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)|=c\,|\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)-\exp_{F}^{-1}(F)|.

Since
\exp(F,\,\cdot\,)
is a

-diffeomorphism around the origin (see p.
3.2
),
\exp_{F}^{-1}
is Lipschitz in
K
, that is, for some

\Delta_{I}\Big(\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)-\exp_{F}^{-1}(F)\Big)\leq c\,D(F,G),

where
\Delta_{I}
is the Minkowski norm on p.
D1
and

).
Therefore, recalling
D4
and up to redefining the constant

|\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)-\exp_{F}^{-1}(F)|\leq c_{4}^{-1}\Delta_{I}\Big(\exp_{F}^{-1}(G)-\exp_{F}^{-1}(F)\Big)\\
\leq c\,D(F,G)\leq c\,|F-G|,

where the last inequality follows from Lemma

|\dot{\gamma}(t,F,G)|\leq c\,|F-G|,

(4.6)

so that

\displaystyle I_{\rm reg}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B}|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.7)

\displaystyle+c\left[\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{q}\int_{B\cap D_{j}}|P_{1}-P_{2}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\big(|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

By gathering (

)–(
4.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\varphi_{j}y_{1}+(1-\varphi_{j})y_{2},\gamma\circ(\varphi_{j},P_{2},P_{1}),A^{\prime}\cup B\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{1},P_{1},A)+\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{2},P_{2},B)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+c\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\left(1+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla y_{2}|^{2}+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{2}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+c\left[\mathcal{L}^{3}(B\cap D_{j})+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{q}\int_{B\cap D_{j}}|P_{1}-P_{2}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\big(|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

Now, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B\cap D_{j}}\left(1+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla y_{2}|^{2}+|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

thus there certainly exists

\displaystyle\int_{B\cap D_{\ell}}\left(1+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla y_{2}|^{2}+|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{(A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap B}\left(1+|\nabla y_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla y_{2}|^{2}+|\nabla P_{1}|^{q}+|\nabla P_{2}|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}^{3}((A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap B)+\frac{c}{N}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{1},P_{1},A)+\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{2},P_{2},B)\Big)

where in the last inequality we exploited the fact that the set
(A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap B
is contained in both
A
and
B
, together with the growth condition from below in
E2
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\varphi_{\ell}y_{1}+(1-\varphi_{\ell})y_{2},\gamma\circ(\varphi_{\ell},P_{2},P_{1}),A^{\prime}\cup B\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\left(1+\frac{c}{N}\right)\Big(\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{1},P_{1},A)+\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{2},P_{2},B)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt+c\int_{(A\cap B)\setminus A^{\prime}}\left[\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{2}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{q}|P_{1}-P_{2}|^{q}\right]\!\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2c}{N}\mathcal{L}^{3}((A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap B).

Finally, choosing
N
such that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{c}{N}<\sigma,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}((A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap B)}{N}<\sigma,\end{cases}

and letting

M_{\sigma}\coloneqq c\left[\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{2N}{\delta}\right)^{q}\right],

we see (
4.3
) is satisfied, and the proof is complete.
∎

Still following the strategy outlined in Subsection

,
we analyze the properties of the
\Gamma(\tau)
-lower and
\Gamma(\tau)
-upper limit
of our sequence

when regarded as set functions.
We recall that (see, e.g.,
[

if

is such that

and if

,
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)&\coloneqq\Gamma(\tau)\mbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,A)\\
&\coloneqq\inf\left\{\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A):(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)\right\},\\
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)&\coloneqq\Gamma(\tau)\mbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,A)\\
&\coloneqq\inf\left\{\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A):(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)\right\},\end{split}

(4.8)

and that
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}

\Gamma(\tau)
-converges to

if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
.
A key-step is the following “almost subadditivity” result, which is a modification of
[

, Proposition 11.5]
or
[

, Proposition 18.3]
.
It is a consequence of the fundamental estimate, but it does not depend on the explicit form of the functionals at stake.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
be as above.
Then for all
A,A^{\prime},B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
with
A^{\prime}\Subset A
and
for all
(y,P)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A^{\prime}\cup B)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A^{\prime}\cup B)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B).

Proof.

We need to prove the inequalities only when

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B)
are finite.
By the definitions in (
4.8
), there exist sequences
\{y^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k}
,
\{P^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k}
,
\{y^{\prime\prime}_{k}\}_{k}
,
\{P^{\prime\prime}_{k}\}_{k}
such that

\displaystyle(y^{\prime}_{k},P^{\prime}_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P),\hskip 10.00002pt(y^{\prime\prime}_{k},P^{\prime\prime}_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime}_{k},P^{\prime}_{k},A),\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B)=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime\prime}_{k},P^{\prime\prime}_{k},B).

Let us fix
\sigma>0
. The fundamental estimate (
4.3
) gives a constant
M_{\sigma}
,
a sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k}
of cut-off functions between
A^{\prime}
and
A
,
and a sequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}
of shortest paths from
P^{\prime\prime}_{k}
to
P^{\prime}_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\varphi_{k}y^{\prime}_{k}+(1-\varphi_{k})y^{\prime\prime}_{k},\gamma_{k}\circ(\varphi_{k},P_{k}^{\prime\prime},P_{k}^{\prime}),A^{\prime}\cup B\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sigma)\big(\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime}_{k},P^{\prime}_{k},A)+\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime\prime}_{k},P^{\prime\prime}_{k},B)\big)

\displaystyle+M_{\sigma}\int_{(A\cap B)\setminus A^{\prime}}\left(|y^{\prime}_{k}-y^{\prime\prime}_{k}|^{2}+|P^{\prime}_{k}-P^{\prime\prime}_{k}|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\sigma.

Recalling (
3.2
), we find

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\varphi_{k}y^{\prime}_{k}+(1-\varphi_{k})y^{\prime\prime}_{k}&\to&y&\text{strongly in }L^{2}(A^{\prime}\cup B;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\[5.69054pt]
\gamma_{k}\circ(\varphi_{k},P_{k}^{\prime\prime},P_{k}^{\prime})&\to&P&\text{uniformly.}\end{array}

Taking the lower limit in (
4.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A^{\prime}\cup B)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(\varphi_{k}y^{\prime}_{k}+(1-\varphi_{k})y^{\prime\prime}_{k},\gamma_{k}\circ(\varphi_{k},P_{k}^{\prime\prime},P_{k}^{\prime}),A^{\prime}\cup B\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sigma)\left(\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime}_{k},P^{\prime}_{k},A)+\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y^{\prime\prime}_{k},P^{\prime\prime}_{k},B)\right)+\sigma

\displaystyle=(1+\sigma)\left(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B)\right)+\sigma,

and the first inequality in the statement follows by letting
\sigma\to 0
. The second one is obtained by taking the upper limit in (
4.9
) and arguing in a similar way.
∎

Building on Proposition
4.5
,
we next establish subadditivity and inner regularity.
We exploit the fact that

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
are increasing set functions,
as well as the growth condition

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)\leq c\int_{A}\left(1+|\nabla yP^{-1}|^{2}+|\nabla P|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.10)

Proposition 4.6

(Subadditivity and inner regularity)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
be as before.
For all
(y,P)\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)

(1)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
are inner regular,

(2)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
is subadditive.

Proof.

We prove the two statements separately.

(1)

Let us focus on
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
According to Definition
3.2
we need to prove that

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)=\sup\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,B):B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega),\,B\Subset A\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
is an increasing set function,
it is trivial that

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)
is larger than the supremum in the formula above.
As for the reverse inequality, let us fix
A\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and let
C\subset A
be compact. There exist
B,B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
such that
C\subset B^{\prime}\Subset B\Subset A
.
Then
B^{\prime}\cup(A\setminus C)=A
.
We now apply Proposition
4.5
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,B^{\prime}\cup(A\setminus C))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,B)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A\setminus C)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,B):B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega),\,B\Subset A\right\}+c\int_{A\setminus C}\left(1+|\nabla yP^{-1}|^{2}+|\nabla P|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the arbitrariness of
C
, we can let
C
invade
A
and we obtain

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,A)\leq\sup\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(y,P,B):B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega),\,B\Subset A\right\}.

The same argument applies to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
.

(2)
Fix
A,B\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
and let
C\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)

satisfy
C\Subset A\cup B
. We find
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
such that
A^{\prime}\Subset A
and that
C\subset A^{\prime}\cup B
. Note that
\overline{C}\setminus B
is compact and
\overline{C}\setminus B\subset A
. Now,
by the monotonicity of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
and Proposition
4.5
,

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,C)\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A^{\prime}\cup B)\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B).

By taking the supremum over
C
, inner regularity yields

\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A\cup B)\leq\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,A)+\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}(y,P,B).

∎

By gathering the previous results,

\Gamma

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0^{+}
, and let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
be as in (
4.8
).
It follows from the definitions that they are increasing set functions,
and Proposition
4.6
yields inner regularity for both and subadditivity for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
.
By standard arguments (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 10.3]
),
we deduce that, upon extraction of a subsequences,

\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)\coloneqq\Gamma(\tau)\mbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,A)

exists for all triple
(y,P,A)
.
From the definition of
\Gamma
-limit and from the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{k}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
is a measure for each

,
it follows
that
\mathcal{F}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)
is superadditive.
We are then in a position to apply the De Giorgi-Letta criterion (see Subsection

),
which ensures that
\mathcal{F}(y,P,\,\cdot\,)

for

.
Thanks to (

Thus, by Radon-Nikodym theorem there exists

\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)=\int_{A}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

∎

4.2.
Characterization of the limiting energy density

\Gamma(\tau)
-compactness result

), and

Proof of Theorem

Let

Our aim is to identify

For

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\Big(W_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(\nabla y(x),P(x_{0})\big)+H_{\mathrm{hom}}(P(x_{0}))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

We next show that (

From now on, let

\nabla y

Let

We use

:

\displaystyle\int_{A}\left|\widetilde{W}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{k},P_{k}\right)-\widetilde{W}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla y_{k},P_{0}\right)\right|\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq c_{3}\int_{A}\Big(1+|\nabla y_{k}P_{k}^{-1}|+|\nabla y_{k}P^{-1}_{0}|\Big)|\nabla y_{k}|\left|P_{k}^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\right|\!\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq c_{3}\int_{A}\big(1+2c_{K}|\nabla y_{k}|\big)|\nabla y_{k}|\Big(\left|P_{k}^{-1}-P^{-1}\right|+\left|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0})\right|\Big)\!\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq c\,\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\|\nabla y_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(A)}\Big)\Big(\|P_{k}^{-1}-P^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}+\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big),

where
c
is a constant independent of
k
and
A
.
We now use
H3
to estimate the difference
between the hardenings in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A)
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}(y_{k},P_{0},A)
:

\displaystyle\int_{A}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{k}\right)-H\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},P_{0}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\Big(\|P_{k}-P\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}+\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big).

By the definitions of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}

we obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A)-\int_{A}|\nabla P_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x-\mathcal{G}_{k}(y_{k},P_{0},A)\right|\\
&\qquad\leq c\,\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\|\nabla y_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(A)}\Big)\Big(\|P_{k}^{-1}-P^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}+\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big)\\
&\qquad\quad+c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\Big(\|P_{k}-P\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}+\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big).\end{split}

(4.13)

We now prove that

f(x)\geq W_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(\nabla y(x),P(x)\big)+H_{\mathrm{hom}}(P(x))+|\nabla P(x)|^{q}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.\,e.\ }x\in\Omega.

(4.14)

To this aim, we select a recovery sequence

\{(y_{k},P_{k})\}\subset W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})\times W^{1,q}(A;K)

for
\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)
, namely

\displaystyle(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P),\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A)=\mathcal{F}(y,P,A).

(4.15)

Owing to the growth assumptions on the energy densities,
we can without loss of generality suppose that

\displaystyle y_{k}\rightharpoonup y\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly in }W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\hskip 20.00003ptP_{k}\rightharpoonup P\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly in }W^{1,q}(A;K).

From (
4.13
) coupled with the
2
-coercivity of
\widetilde{W}
,
recalling Remark
4.1
about the convergence of inverses,
(
4.12
) and (
4.15
), we infer

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{k}(y_{k},P_{0},A)+\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{A}|\nabla P_{k}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-c\,\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}(y_{k},P_{k},A)\Big)-c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\,\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{G}(y,P_{0},A)+\int_{A}|\nabla P|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-c\,\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)\Big)-c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\,\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}.

Observe that
the lower bound on
\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)

that we have just proved
holds for any set
A
containing
x_{0}
.
In particular,
we select
A=B_{r}(x_{0})
and
divide both sides of the corresponding estimates by
\mathcal{L}^{3}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
.
By letting
r\to 0
,
we apply Lebesgue differentiation theorem
to deduce (
4.14
) for
x=x_{0}
.
Indeed,

\lim_{r\to 0}\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\left(1+\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{3}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\mathcal{F}(y,P,B_{r}(x_{0}))\right)=0,

because (
4.10
) grants

\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)\leq c\int_{A}\left(1+|\nabla yP^{-1}|^{2}+|\nabla P|^{q}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Eventually, we are only left to prove that

f(x)\leq W_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(\nabla y(x),P(x)\big)+H_{\mathrm{hom}}(P(x))+|\nabla P(x)|^{q}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for a.\,e.\ }x\in\Omega.

(4.16)

To establish the estimate, we let
\{y_{k}\}\subset W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})

be a recovery sequence for
\mathcal{G}(y,P_{0},A)
, that is,

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{k}(y_{k},P_{0},A)=\mathcal{G}(y,P_{0},A),

with
y_{k}\rightharpoonup y
weakly in
W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
We consider estimate (
4.13
)
for this particular
\{y_{k}\}
and the constant sequence given by
P_{k}=P
for all
k
.
Exploiting this time the
2
-coercivity of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y,P,A)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{F}_{k}\left(y_{k},P,A\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{k}\big(y_{k},P_{0},A\big)+\int_{A}|\nabla P|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+c\,\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{k}(y_{k},P_{0},A)\Big)+c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\,\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}(y,P_{0},A)+\int_{A}|\nabla P|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+c\,\|P^{-1}-P^{-1}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\Big(\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)+\mathcal{G}\big(y,P_{0},A\big)\Big)+c\,\mathcal{L}^{3}(A)\,\|P-P_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}.

Arguing as above with
A=B_{r}(x_{0})
and exploiting the 2-growth conditions of
W_{\mathrm{hom}}
, we conclude that (
4.16
) holds for
x=x_{0}
.

Since we can select almost every point in
\Omega
as
x_{0}
, the conclusion follows from (
4.14
) and (
4.16
).
∎

4.3.
Convergence of minimum problems

We conclude this section with the proof of equicoercivity and convergence of (almost) minimizers, that is, Corollary
2.3
.

Proof of Corollary
2.3
.

(i)
Let
\{(y_{k},P_{k})\}
be a bounded energy sequence such that
\|y_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq c
, uniformly in
k
. From the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

\|\nabla P_{k}\|_{L^{q}}\leq c.

(4.17)

Besides, for all
k
, assumption
H2
and the uniform bound (
2.1
) yield

\|P_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|P_{k}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c.

(4.18)

As for the elastic part, assumption
E2
entails

\|\nabla y_{k}P_{k}^{-1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq c,

which, together with (
2.2
) and (
4.18
), gives

\|\nabla y_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq c.

(4.19)

This and the uniform bound on the
L^{2}
-norm of
y_{k}
imply that there exists
y\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

y_{k}\to y\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})

up to subsequences. From bounds (
4.17
)–(
4.18
) and Morrey’s embedding, we deduce the existence of
P\in W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
such that

P_{k}\to P\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{uniformly},

again up to subsequences. Note that the uniform convergence of
\{P_{k}\}\subset W^{1,q}(\Omega;K)
yields that
P
attains values in
K
as well. Recalling the definition of the topology
\tau
in (
2.3
), we have thus proved that
(y_{k},P_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\to}}(y,P)
.

(ii)
By combining (i) with Theorem
2.2
, the result follows in a standard way, see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 7.2]
.
∎

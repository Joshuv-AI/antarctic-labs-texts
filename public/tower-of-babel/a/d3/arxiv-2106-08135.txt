Exact periodic stripes for a local/nonlocal minimization problem with volume constraint
Sara Daneri, Eris Runa

Abstract
We consider a class of generalized antiferromagnetic local/nonlocal interaction functionals in general dimension, where a short range attractive term of perimeter type competes with a long range repulsive term characterized by a reflection positive power law kernel. Breaking of symmetry with respect to coordinate permutations and pattern formation for functionals in this class have been shown in [ GR19 , DR19a ] and previously by [ GS16 ] in the discrete setting, for a smaller range of exponents. Global minimizers of such functionals have been proved in [ DR19a ] to be given by periodic stripes of volume density 1 / 2 1/2 in any cube having optimal period size, also in the large volume limit. In this paper we study the minimization problem with arbitrarily prescribed volume constraint α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) . We show that, in the large volume limit, minimizers are periodic stripes of volume density α \alpha , namely stripes whose one-dimensional slices in the direction orthogonal to their boundary are simple periodic with volume density α \alpha in each period. Results of this type in the one-dimensional setting, where no symmetry breaking occurs, have been previously obtained in [ Mül93 , AM01 , RW03 , CO05 , GLL09 ] .

An important question in the applications is to determine the structure of the ground states while imposing a volume constraint. Indeed, the mutual density of the two pure phases in chemical solutions or the total magnetization in physical systems is usually fixed and the ground states emerge in the same density class.

1.1
Scientific context

Pattern formation is ubiquitous in nature and emerges at nanoscale level in several physical/chemical systems. Despite the diverse interactions involved in such models, surprisingly similar patterns among which droplets or stripes/lamellae appear. As first suggested in
[
SA95
]
, ground states given by periodic regular structures are universally believed to stem from the competition between short range attractive and long range repulsive (SALR) interactions. Although patterns are observed in experiments and produced by simulations, a rigorous mathematical proof of pattern formation starting from (discretely or continuously) symmetric functionals and domains is available only in a very few cases. The main difficulties lie in the symmetry breaking phenomenon coupled with nonlocality.

In the one-dimensional setting, simple periodicity of global minimizers is known to hold for convex or reflection positive repulsive kernels using respectively convexity arguments
[
Hub78
,
PU78
,
Ker99
,
Mül93
,
RW03
,
CO05
]
or the reflection positivity technique
[
GLL06
,
GLL08
,
GLL09
]
. In particular, in
[
RW03
,
CO05
,
GLL09
]
simple periodicity of minimizers is shown under arbitrary nontrivial volume constraint (see Theorem
3.1
for the results in
[
GLL09
]
).

In several space dimensions, there are only a few results were symmetry breaking and pattern formation are shown, whenever the broken symmetry is not enforced by explicit geometric quantities in the functionals or by asymmetries in the domain. The first characterization of ground states as periodic stripes in the discrete setting was given in
[
GS16
]
for a discrete version of the functional (
1.2
) in the range of exponents
p>2d
. In the continuous setting, breaking of discrete symmetry (i.e., symmetry w.r.t. permutation of coordinates) for the functional (
1.2
) in the range of exponents
p>2d
has been shown in
[
GR19
]
. Exact periodicity and structure of minimizers for the wider range of exponents
p\geq d+2
was achieved in
[
DR19a
]
. In
[
GR19
,
DR19a
]
new quantities and techniques were introduced in order to deal with the richer geometry of locally finite perimeter sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and with defects carrying infinitesimal energy contributions. In
[
Ker21
]
the above-mentioned results of
[
DR19a
]
have been extended to a small range of exponents below
d+2
. Indeed, the interest in lowering the exponent of the kernel comes both from the mathematical challenges deriving from an increased nonlocality and from physical applications (
p=d+1
corresponds to models for thin ferromagnatic films and Langmuir monolayers,
p=d
to micromagnetics and
p=d-2
to the Ohta-Kawasaki model for diblock copolymers
[
OK86
]
). A characterization of ground states as periodic stripes has been shown also for repulsive kernels of screened-Coulomb (or Yukawa) type in
[
DR20
]
(see also
[
DR19
]
). In
[
DKR19
]
one-dimensionality and periodicity of minimizers was shown for the diffuse interface version of the functional (
1.2
) in a finite periodic box. In
[
DR21
]
the results in
[
DKR19
]
have been proved to hold in the large volume limit, analogue of the thermodynamic limit at zero temperature.

All such results regard global minimizers and ground states, which turn out to have volume density
1/2
(with the convention that the two pure phases of the material correspond to the values
\{0,1\}
). Up to our knowledge Theorem
1.2
is the first result showing discrete symmetry breaking and pattern formation in general dimension under preassigned volume constraint. The interesting phenomenon, in particular, is that even in the low density regime, in the periodic setting, stripes are the first type of pattern to emerge from the competition between attractive/repulsive forces in the range below the critical constant
J_{c}
. Moreover, as shown by (
1.5
), stripes do not become finer and finer as the volume density decreases but the period of simple periodicity of the stripes enlarges more and more. Theorem
1.1
is achieved by explicitly computing
\Lambda(0,\alpha)
and
h^{*}_{0,\alpha}
and showing uniform convexity properties of the function
\alpha\mapsto\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)
and uniform continuity properties w.r.t.
\tau
as
\tau\to 0
(see the proof of point
(i)
in Theorem
3.2
).

Regarding further fields of interest for pattern formation under attractive/repulsive forces in competition, we mention the following. Evolution problems of gradient flow type related to functionals with attractive/repulsive nonlocal terms in competition, both in presence and in absence of diffusion, are also well studied (see e.g.
[
CCH14
,
CCP19
,
CDFLS11
,
CT20
,
Cra17
]
). In particular, one would like to show convergence of the gradient flows or of their deterministic particle approximations to configurations which are periodic or close to periodic states.
Another interesting direction would be to extend our rigidity results to non-flat surfaces without interpenetration of matter as investigated for rod and plate theories in
[
KS14
,
LMP10
,
OR17
]
.

1.2
General strategy

The overall strategy to prove Theorem
1.2
consists very roughly in the following. As in
[
DR19a
,
DR20
,
DR21
]
a smaller scale
l<L
is introduced and the set
[0,L)^{d}
is partitioned into
d+1
sets, where the first
d
sets
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
are constituted of points
z
which on the cube
Q_{l}(z)
of centre
z
and sidelength
l
are
L^{1}
-close to stripes with boundaries orthogonal to
e_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Calling
A=[0,L)^{d}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}
, the aim is to prove that
A=\emptyset
. This indeed, by continuity of the distance function from stripes with boundary in a given direction, would show that
[0,L)^{d}=A_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, and once discrete symmetry is broken, namely the minimizer
E
is close to stripes with boundaries orthogonal to
e_{i}
, a stability argument allows to conclude that
E
is one-dimensional. In the end, the fact that
L
is a multiple of the optimal period
2h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}
for stripes with density
\alpha
allows to apply the one-dimensional optimization result of Theorem
1.1
and deduce simple periodicity of the optimal one-dimensional profiles.

The main difficulty w.r.t.
[
DR19a
,
DR20
,
DR21
]
is that whenever we consider one-dimensional optimization arguments on slices of the set
A_{i}
(namely on the set where
E
is close to stripes with boundaries orthogonal to
e_{i}
) we cannot bound the energy of such slices from below with the minimal energy density
\Lambda(\tau)\frac{|A_{i}|}{L^{d}}=\Lambda(\tau,1/2)\frac{|A_{i}|}{L^{d}}
. This would lead indeed to a coarse global lower bound when restricting to sets with volume constraint
\alpha
, due to the fact that
\Lambda(\tau,1/2)<\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)
whenever
\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, on each connected
i
-slice
J
of
A_{i}
one has to take into account instead the volume density
\alpha(J)
of an
l
-neighbourhood of
J
, obtaining a lower bound with variable energy density
\Lambda(\tau,\alpha(J))
. The difficulty related to the fact that the local volume densities
\alpha(J)
are not controllable by the global volume constraint
\alpha
on
[0,L)^{d}
is overcome thanks to strict convexity properties of the function
\alpha\mapsto\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)
, which we will prove in Section
3
, Theorem
3.2
. The role of strict convexity will be to penalize oscillations of volume densities on the different connected components of the sets of the partition
A\cup A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{d}
with respect to the global volume density
\alpha
. Another difficulty is due to the role of the boundary between the different
A_{i}
in the estimates, which differently from
[
DR19a
]
has to be considered separately resulting in a different partition.

{w}_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},t_{i})=\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{E}(t_{i}^{\perp},t_{i},\zeta_{i}^{\perp},\zeta_{i})K_{\tau}(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

(2.8)

and

v_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},s)=\frac{1}{2d}\int_{s^{-}}^{s^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{E}(t^{\perp}_{i},u,\zeta^{\perp}_{i},\zeta_{i})K_{\tau}(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}u.

(2.9)

Hence, putting together (
2.4
) and (
2
) one has the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\tau,L}(E)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{[0,L)^{d-1}}\sum_{s\in\partial E_{t^{\perp}_{i}}\cap[0,L]}r_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},s)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\perp}_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{[0,L)^{d-1}}\sum_{s\in\partial E_{t_{i}^{\perp}}\cap[0,L]}v_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},s)\,\mathrm{d}t_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{[0,L)^{d}}w_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},t_{i})\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.10)

The term
r_{i,\tau}
penalizes oscillations with high frequency in direction
e_{i}
, namely sets
E
whose slices in direction
e_{i}
have boundary points at small minimal distance (see Lemma
4.1
). The term
v_{i,\tau}
penalizes oscillations in direction
e_{i}
whenever the neighbourhood of the point in
\partial E\cap{Q_{l}(z)}
is close in
L^{1}
to a stripe oriented along
e_{j}
(see Proposition
4.5
).

For every cube
Q_{l}(z)
, with
l<L
and
z\in[0,L)^{d}
, define now the following localization of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,L}

\begin{split}\bar{F}_{i,\tau}(E,Q_{l}(z))&:=\frac{1}{l^{d}}\Big[\int_{Q^{\perp}_{l}(z_{i}^{\perp})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\partial E_{t_{i}^{\perp}}\\
t_{i}^{\perp}+se_{i}\in Q_{l}(z)\end{subarray}}(v_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},s)+r_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},s))\,\mathrm{d}t_{i}^{\perp}+\int_{Q_{l}(z)}{w_{i,\tau}(E,t_{i}^{\perp},t_{i})\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big],\\
\bar{F}_{\tau}(E,Q_{l}(z))&:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{F}_{i,\tau}(E,Q_{l}(z)).\end{split}

(2.11)

The following inequality holds:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\tau,L}(E)\geq\frac{1}{L^{d}}\int_{[0,L)^{d}}\bar{F}_{\tau}(E,Q_{l}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

(2.12)

Since in (
2.12
) equality holds for unions of stripes, in order to prove Theorem
1.2
one can reduce to show that the minimizers of its right hand side in the class
\mathcal{C}_{L,\alpha}
are periodic optimal stripes of density
\alpha
provided
\tau
and
L
satisfy the conditions of the theorem.

In the next definition we define a quantity which measures the
L^{1}
distance of a set from being a union of stripes.

Definition 2.1
.

For every
\eta
we denote by
\mathcal{A}^{i}_{\eta}
the family of all sets
F
such that

(i)

they are union of stripes oriented along the direction
e_{i}

(ii)

their connected components of the boundary are distant at least
\eta
.

We denote by

\begin{split}D^{i}_{\eta}(E,Q):=\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(Q)}\int_{Q}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{F}|:\ F\in\mathcal{A}^{i}_{\eta}\Big\}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\eta}(E,Q)=\inf_{i}D^{i}_{\eta}(E,Q).\end{split}

(2.13)

Finally, we let
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}:=\cup_{i}\mathcal{A}^{i}_{\eta}
.

We recall also the following properties of the functional defined in (
2.13
).

Remark 2.2
.

The distance function from the set of stripes satisfies the following properties.

(i)

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the map
z\mapsto D_{\eta}(E,Q_{l}(z))
is Lipschitz, with Lipschitz constant
C_{d}/l
, where
C_{d}
is a constant depending only on the dimension
d
.

In particular, whenever
D_{\eta}(E,Q_{l}(z))>\alpha
and
D_{\eta}(E,Q_{l}(z^{\prime}))<\beta
, then
|z-z^{\prime}|>l(\alpha-\beta)/C_{d}
.

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon
there exists
{\delta}_{0}=\delta_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for every
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
whenever
D^{j}_{\eta}(E,Q_{l}(z))\leq\delta
and
D^{i}_{\eta}(E,Q_{l}(z))\leq\delta
with
i\neq j
for some
\eta>0
, it holds

\begin{split}\min\big(|Q_{l}(z)\setminus E|,|E\cap Q_{l}(z)|\big)\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

3
The one-dimensional problem

In this section we study properties of the energy functional
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,L}
on one-dimensional sets satisfying the volume constraint
\alpha\in(0,1)
, namely sets belonging to the set
\mathcal{C}_{L,\alpha}

First we recall the following result, which is a corollary of Theorem 1 in
[
GLL09
]
. In
[
GLL09
]
the result is proved for general reflection positive kernels and their finite perturbations, while here we focus on the reflection positive kernel
K_{\tau}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
\tau\geq 0
.
Then, the value

\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)=\inf_{L}\inf_{E\in\mathcal{C}_{L,\alpha}}\mathcal{F}_{\tau,L}(E)

is attained by the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\tau,2kh^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}}
on periodic stripes
E_{h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha},\alpha}
of density
\alpha
and period
2h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}
for some (possibly non-unique)
h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}>0
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The main result of this section is the following

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists
\hat{\tau}>0
such that for all
0\leq\tau\leq\hat{\tau}
the following holds.

(i)

For every
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists a unique
h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}>0
such that

\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)=\mathcal{F}_{\tau,2h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}}(E_{h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha},\alpha})=\inf_{h}\mathcal{F}_{\tau,2h}(E_{h,\alpha}).

Moreover,

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{\alpha\to 1}h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}=+\infty.

(ii)

There exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}|\partial_{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)|\leq C_{2}.

(3.1)

(iii)

The map
(0,1)\ni\alpha\mapsto\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)
is strictly convex with

\partial^{2}_{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)\geq\tilde{c}\min\{\alpha^{q-1},(1-\alpha)^{q-1}\}

(3.2)

for some constant
\tilde{c}>0
independent of
\alpha
.

Theorem
1.1
corresponds to point
(i)
of Theorem
3.2
. Due to the symmetry of the problem w.r.t.
\alpha=1/2
, w.l.o.g. we will consider in the following densities
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
.

Let us start with the following preliminary lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big]
. Then, setting for simplicity
F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)=\mathcal{F}_{\tau,2h}(E_{h,\alpha})
, one has that

F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)=-\frac{1}{h}+\frac{C(q,\alpha,\tau^{1/\beta}/h)}{h^{q-1}},

(3.3)

where for
s>0

C(q,\alpha,s)=\frac{2C_{1}}{(q-1)(q-2)}\Big\{\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2k+2\alpha+s)^{q-2}}+\frac{1}{(2k+2-2\alpha+s)^{q-2}}-\frac{2}{(2k+2+s)^{q-2}}\Big\}

(3.4)

and
C_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(\|\xi\|_{1}+1)^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi
.

Proof.

Setting
E_{h,\alpha}=\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\tau,2h}(E_{h,\alpha})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h}\Bigl(-\mathrm{Per}(\hat{E}_{h,\alpha},[0,2h))+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{K}_{\tau}(z)\Bigl[\mathrm{Per}(\hat{E}_{h,\alpha},[0,2h))|z|-\int_{0}^{2h}|\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x)-\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x+z)|\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h}\Bigl(-\mathrm{Per}(\hat{E}_{2h,\alpha},[0,2h))+C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{(|z|+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q}}\Bigl[\mathrm{Per}(\hat{E}_{h,\alpha},[0,2h))|z|-\int_{0}^{2h}|\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x)-\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x+z)|\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigr),

where
C_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{1}{(\|\xi\|_{1}+1)^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi
is such that

\widehat{K}_{\tau}(z)=C_{1}\frac{1}{(|z|+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q}},\quad q=p-d+1.

Setting
A_{\tau,h,\alpha}=\mathcal{F}^{1d}_{\tau,2h}(\hat{E}_{h,\alpha})+\frac{1}{h}
and by simple computations one obtains

\displaystyle A_{\tau,h,\alpha}=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{2h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|z+\tau^{1/\beta}|^{q}}\Big(|z|-\int_{0}^{2\alpha h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}^{c}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{1}}{2h}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|z+\tau^{1/\beta}|^{q}}\left(|z|-\int_{2\alpha h}^{2h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}\left(z-\int_{0}^{2\alpha h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}^{c}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{1}}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}\left(z-\int_{2\alpha h}^{2h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(3.5)

Using that

\displaystyle\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}^{c}}\cdot\chi_{[0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\chi_{[0,2\alpha h]^{c}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\chi_{[2kh,(2k+2\alpha)h)},

\displaystyle\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}\cdot\chi_{[2\alpha h,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\chi_{[2\alpha h,2h]^{c}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\chi_{[(2k+2\alpha)h,(2k+2)h)},

one has that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}

\displaystyle\left(z-\int_{0}^{2\alpha h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}^{c}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{2\alpha h}^{+\infty}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}(z-2\alpha h)\,\mathrm{d}z+\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{2kh}^{(2k+2\alpha)h}\int_{0}^{2\alpha h}\frac{1}{(|y-x|+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\alpha h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-(q-2)}}{(q-2)(q-1)}+\frac{1}{(q-2)(q-1)}\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big[\frac{1}{((2k-2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{((2k+2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}-\frac{2}{(2kh+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}\Big]

(3.6)

and analogously

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}

\displaystyle\left(z-\int_{2\alpha h}^{2h}\chi_{\hat{E}_{h,\alpha}}(x+z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{2(1-\alpha)h}^{+\infty}(z+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-q}(z-2(1-\alpha)h)\,\mathrm{d}z+\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{(2k+2\alpha)h}^{(2k+2)h}\int_{2\alpha h}^{2h}\frac{1}{(|y-x|+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2(1-\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-(q-2)}}{(q-2)(q-1)}+\frac{1}{(q-2)(q-1)}\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big[\frac{1}{((2k-2+2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{((2k+2-2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}-\frac{2}{(2kh+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}\Big].

(3.7)

Inserting (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) in (
3.5
) one obtains

\displaystyle F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{h}+\frac{C_{1}}{h(q-1)(q-2)}\Big\{(2\alpha h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-(q-2)}+(2(1-\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{-(q-2)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{[(2k-2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta}]^{q-2}}+\frac{1}{[(2k+2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta}]^{q-2}}+\frac{1}{[(2k-2+2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta}]^{q-2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{[(2k+2-2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta}]^{q-2}}-\frac{4}{[2kh+\tau^{1/\beta}]^{q-2}}\Big\},

which by rearranging the terms gives

\displaystyle F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{h}+\frac{C_{1}}{h(q-1)(q-2)}\Big\{\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{2}{((2k+2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{((2k+2-2\alpha)h+\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}-\frac{4}{((2k+2)h+2\tau^{1/\beta})^{q-2}}\Big\},

corresponding to (
3.4
).

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The following holds.

(i)

For every
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, there exists a unique
h^{*}_{0,\alpha}
such that
\Lambda(0,\alpha)=F_{0}(h^{*}_{0,\alpha},\alpha)
, given by

(3.8)

(ii)

For every

such that for all

with

.

(iii)

There exist

(3.10)

Proof.

The explicit formula (
3.8
) follows from an explicit calculation of the unique critical point of

using the expression (
3.3
) when

.

By convexity of the function

for

applied to the summation in (

C(q,\alpha,0)\geq\frac{2}{(2\alpha)^{q-2}}\frac{C_{1}}{(q-1)(q-2)}.

(3.11)

Estimating the summation in (

) from above one has that

\displaystyle C(q,\alpha,0)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(q-1)(q-2)}\Big[\frac{2}{(2\alpha)^{q-2}}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{C}{(2k)^{q-1}}\alpha\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{C}}{(2\alpha)^{q-2}},

where we used that

Inserting the expression for

in the functional, one obtains

Inserting the explicit expression for

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{h}F_{0}(h,\alpha)=\frac{-2h^{q-2}+q(q-1)C(q,\alpha,0)}{h^{q+1}}.

(3.14)

In particular,

\partial^{2}_{h}F_{0}(h^{*}_{0,\alpha},\alpha)={(q-1)^{-3/(q-2)}(q-2)C(q,\alpha,0)^{-3/(q-2)}}.

Hence by (

) and (

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

There exist

(3.16)

(3.17)

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{2}_{h}F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)-\partial^{2}_{h}F_{0}(h,\alpha)\big|

Proof.

\displaystyle\big|F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)-F_{0}(h,\alpha)\big|

Proof of Theorem

: .

0<\varepsilon\ll 1
to be fixed later. By (

) in Lemma

\displaystyle|\partial_{h}^{2}F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)|

\partial_{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)=\partial_{\alpha}F_{\tau}(h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha},\alpha)=\frac{1}{h^{q-1}}\partial_{\alpha}C(q,\alpha,\tau^{1/\beta}/h)_{|_{h=h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}}}.

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau,\alpha)=\Big[\partial^{2}_{\alpha}F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)-\frac{(\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{h}F_{\tau}(h,\alpha))^{2}}{\partial^{2}_{h}F_{\tau}(h,\alpha)}\Big]_{\Big|_{h=h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{8C_{1}(q-1)(q-2)}{(h^{*}_{0,\alpha})^{2q}}\Big\{\Big[\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2k+2\alpha)^{q}}+\frac{1}{(2k+2-2\alpha)^{q}}\Big]\cdot

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{(h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha})^{2q}}\cdot\frac{\tau^{1/\beta}}{h^{*}_{\tau,\alpha}}\Big\{\frac{q-3}{q-1}\partial^{2}_{\alpha}C(q,\alpha,\tau^{1/\beta}/h)\partial_{s}C(q,\alpha,\tau^{1/\beta}/h)

&-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{k_{i}}^{k_{i+1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}(s-|\chi_{E^{\prime}}(s+u)-\chi_{E^{\prime}}(u)|)\hat{K}_{\tau}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}u.\end{split}

\begin{split}|A|\leq\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{m}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\chi_{E\Delta E^{\prime}}(u+s)\hat{K}_{\tau}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{m}}\int_{k_{m}+\eta_{C}}^{\infty}\hat{K}_{\tau}(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{\bar{C}_{0}}{2},\end{split}

where
\bar{C}_{0}
is a constant depending only on
\eta_{C}
. Similarly,
|B|\leq\bar{C}_{0}/2

Thus we have that

\displaystyle\leq\int_{k_{0}}^{k_{m}}\int_{k_{m}+\eta_{C}}^{\infty}\hat{K}_{\tau}(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v+\int_{k_{1}}^{k_{m+1}}\int_{-\infty}^{k_{1}-\eta_{C}/2}\hat{K}_{\tau}(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}_{0}.

∎

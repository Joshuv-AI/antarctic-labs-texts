Hodge–Tate prismatic crystals and Sen theory
Hui Gao, Yu Min, Yupeng Wang

Abstract
. We study Hodge–Tate crystals on the absolute (log-) prismatic site of 𝒪 K {\mathcal{O}_{K}} , where 𝒪 K {\mathcal{O}_{K}} is a mixed characteristic complete discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field. We first classify Hodge–Tate crystals by 𝒪 K {\mathcal{O}_{K}} -modules equipped with certain small endomorphisms. We then construct Sen theory over a non-Galois Kummer tower, and use it to classify rational Hodge–Tate crystals by (log-) nearly Hodge–Tate representations. Various cohomology comparison and vanishing results are proved along the way.

Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In the ground-breaking work
[
BS22
]
, Bhatt and Scholze construct the prismatic cohomology, which is of “motivic nature” in the sense that it can specialize to most existing
p
-adic cohomology theories such as étale, de Rham and crystalline cohomologies.
Similar to the theory of crystalline cohomology, one can also consider crystals on the relative/absolute prismatic site. It turns out that these coefficient objects contain important geometric and arithmetic information.
Let
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
be a mixed characteristic complete discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field, and let
K={\mathcal{O}_{K}}[1/p]
.
In
[
BS23
]
, Bhatt and Scholze prove an equivalence between the category of
F
-crystals over the absolute prismatic site of
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
and the category of crystalline
{\mathbb Z}_{p}
-representations of
G_{K}={\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{K}}/K)
.

Since the work of Bhatt–Scholze
[
BS23
]
, the study of various types of prismatic crystals has been very fast developing.
In this paper, we focus on the study of
(absolute) Hodge–Tate crystals
, which are vector bundles over the Hodge–Tate structure sheaf on the absolute (log-) prismatic site.
It turns out these crystals are closely related with classical Sen theory
[
Sen81
]
developed some 40 years ago.
In particular, the study of these crystals leads to definition of a new category of objects called
(log-) nearly Hodge–Tate representations
. These representations can be regarded as the “(log-) crystalline objects” in Sen theory, and give interesting updates to Sen’s original work.
Here, we should mention right away that there have been independent works by Drinfeld
[
Dri20
]
, Bhatt–Lurie
[
BL22a
,
BL22b
]
and Anschütz–Heuer–Le Bras
[

,
AHLB23
]
on Hodge–Tate crystals, using a stacky approach. We choose to give a more detailed account/comparison of the literature only near the end of the introduction, cf. §

, particularly after we introduce the necessary notations.
In addition, we also mention some of our computations are inspired by Tian
[

1.4.
Sen theory over Kummer tower

In this subsection, we explain a Sen theory over a Kummer tower, and show how to use it to conclude the proof of Thm.

Construction 1.12

Let us explain the basic ideas to prove Thm.
1.5

Let

We hint that this condition on Sen weights is already reflected in the second condition in Def.

Indeed, let

We give several extensive remarks about the literature on Hodge–Tate crystals, and make some comparisons with our work.

Remark 1.17

In this remark, we discuss another independent approach, the

(1)

Remark 1.18
.

We compare our

approach to the

(1)

Firstly, our paper can also treat the log-prismatic case in a parallel fashion. The log-prismatic cases are not yet covered in

In continuation of Rem.

(1)

As explained in Constructions

1.6.
Structure of the paper

In §
2
, we quickly review the notion of stratifications, which motivates us to study in detail the Breuil–Kisin cosimplicial ring and its reductions.
In §
3
, we compute, in an axiomatic way, stratifications (and their cohomology) over the Breuil–Kisin cosimplicial ring.
In §
4
, these axiomatic computations specialize to classification theorem and cohomology comparison of Hodge–Tate crystals.
In §
5
, which is the begining where we start to study Galois actions, we classify Hodge–Tate crystals on the perfect prismatic site by
C
-representations.
Some explicit formulae in §
5
prompts us to study Sen theory over the Kummer tower. We give a brief review of locally analytic vectors in §
6
, and then use the tool to define Sen theory over the Kummer tower in §
7
. In §
8
, we finally relate Hodge–Tate crystals with our new Sen theory, and classify them by (log-) nearly Hodge–Tate representations.
The final §
9
contains a technical proof of a cohomology vanishing theorem which is stated in §
3
.

We recall the definition of a stratification.

Let

=&\sum_{l,m\geq 0}\phi_{m}\circ\phi_{1+l+n}(1+aX)^{-l-n}(-1)^{l}X^{[l]}X^{[m]}+\sum_{l,m\geq 0}\phi_{m}\circ\phi_{l+1+n}aX(1+aX)^{-1-l-n}(-1)^{l+1}X^{[l]}X^{[m]}\\
=&(1+aX-aX)\sum_{l,m\geq 0}\phi_{m}\circ\phi_{1+l+n}(1+aX)^{-l-n-1}(-1)^{l}X^{[l]}X^{[m]}\\
=&\sum_{l,m\geq 0}\phi_{m}\circ\phi_{1+l+n}(1+aX)^{-l-n-1}(-1)^{l}X^{[l]}X^{[m]}.\end{split}

Now, we can conclude Item (1) by induction on

∎

Lemma 4.12
.

For any crystal
{\mathbb M}\in{\mathrm{Vect}}(({\mathcal{O}}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},*},{\mathcal{O}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}})
, we have
{\mathbb M}\simeq{\mathrm{R}\underleftarrow{\lim}}{\mathbb M}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since an inductive limit of faithfully flat maps of prisms is a faithfully flat map of prisms (cf.

, Remark 2.4]

is
{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
-adically complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.10
.

Since
{\mathrm{R}}\Gamma
commutes with derived inverse limit, we have

{\mathrm{R}}\Gamma(({\mathcal{O}}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},*},{\mathbb M})\simeq{\mathrm{R}\underleftarrow{\lim}}_{n}{\mathrm{R}}\Gamma(({\mathcal{O}}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},*},{\mathbb M}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n}).

Now we consider the exact triangle of abelian sheaves on

{\mathcal{I}}^{n}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}{\mathbb M}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n+1}\rightarrow{\mathbb M}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n+1}\rightarrow{\mathbb M}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}^{n}.

]

In this section, we review some results from

6.1.
Locally analytic vectors

Let us very quickly recall the theory of locally analytic vectors, see
[
BC16
, §2.1]
and
[
Ber16
, §2]
for more details.
Recall the multi-index notations: if
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
and
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
(here
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
), then we let
\mathbf{c}^{\mathbf{k}}=c_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot c_{d}^{k_{d}}
. Recall that a
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-Banach space
W
is a
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-vector space with a complete non-Archimedean norm
\|\cdot\|
such that
\|aw\|=\|a\|_{p}\|w\|,\forall a\in{{\mathbb Q}_{p}},w\in W
, where
\|a\|_{p}
is the
p
-adic norm on
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
.

Definition 6.1
.

(1)

Let
G
be a
p
-adic Lie group, and let
(W,\|\cdot\|)
be a
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-Banach representation of
G
.
Let
H
be an open subgroup of
G
such that there exist coordinates
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}:H\rightarrow{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}
giving rise to an analytic bijection
\mathbf{c}:H\rightarrow{{\mathbb Z}_{p}}^{d}
.
We say that an element
w\in W
is an
H
-analytic vector if there exists a sequence
\{w_{\mathbf{k}}\}_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
with
w_{\mathbf{k}}\rightarrow 0
in
W
, such that

g(w)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\mathbf{c}(g)^{\mathbf{k}}w_{\mathbf{k}},\quad\forall g\in H.

Let
W^{H\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
denote the space of
H
-analytic vectors.

(2)

W^{H\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
injects into
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}(H,W)
(the space of analytic functions on
H
valued in
W
), and we endow it with the induced norm, which we denote as
\|\cdot\|_{H}
. We have
\|w\|_{H}=\mathrm{sup}_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\|w_{\mathbf{k}}\|
, and
W^{H\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
is a Banach space.

(3)

We say that a vector
w\in W
is
locally analytic
if there exists an open subgroup
H
as above such that
w\in W^{H\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
. Let
W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
denote the space of such vectors. We have
W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}=\cup_{H}W^{H\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
where
H
runs through open subgroups of
G
. We can endow
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
with the inductive limit topology, so that
W^{{\mathrm{la}}}
is an LB space.

(4)

We can naturally extend these definitions to the case when
W
is a Fréchet- or LF- representation of
G
, and use
W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{pa}$}}
to denote the
pro-analytic
vectors, cf.
[
Ber16
, §2]
.

Notation 6.2
.

We defined
\hat{G}={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K)
in Notation

.
Note

p
-adic Lie group of dimension 2. We recall the structure of this group in the following.

•

-Sen theory to the

7.1.
Cyclotomic tower and Sen theory

We recall the usual category of semi-linear representations.

Recall
{K_{p^{\infty}}}

Let

∎

Notation 7.3

Let

(1)

7.2.
Locally analytic vectors in
\hat{L}

Let
{\mathbf{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
denote the usual de Rham period ring. Let
\theta:{\mathbf{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}\rightarrow C
be the usual map which extends
\theta:{\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{inf}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
.
Recall that as in
[
Ber02
, §2.2]
, when
r_{n}\in I
, there exists a continuous embedding
\iota_{n}:\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}^{I}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}

Lemma 7.4
.

Consider the image of
\mathfrak{t}
via the map
\theta:{\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{inf}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{C}}
, then
0\neq\theta(\mathfrak{t})\in(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
. In addition,
1/\theta(\mathfrak{t})\in(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
.

Proof.

We first check
\theta({\mathfrak{t}})\neq 0
. Recall
{\mathfrak{t}}=\frac{t}{p\lambda}
. Note
\theta(\frac{t}{[\epsilon]-1})=1
using the expansion
t=\mathrm{log}([\varepsilon])
. Hence it suffices to show
\theta(\frac{[\epsilon]-1}{E(u)})\neq 0
: this holds because both
E
and
\frac{[\epsilon]-1}{\varphi^{-1}([\epsilon]-1)}
generate the principal ideal
\ker\theta
.

The proof of analyticity for
\theta(\mathfrak{t})
and
1/\theta(\mathfrak{t})
are the same; alternatively, we can use the fact that
(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
is a field
[
BC16
, Lem. 2.5]
. We treat
\theta(\mathfrak{t})
in the following.
Choose
n\geq n({\mathfrak{t}})
as in Lem.
6.11
so that
{\mathfrak{t}}\in({\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r_{n},r_{n}]}_{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
.
Consider the image of
{\mathfrak{t}}
under the following composite map

(7.4)

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r_{n},r_{n}]}\xrightarrow{\iota_{n}}{\mathbf{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}}\xrightarrow{\theta}C;

since both maps are continuous, hence the image is an element in
(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
. Unfortunately, the map
\iota_{n}
factors as

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r_{n},r_{n}]}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{-n}}{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r_{0},r_{0}]}\xrightarrow{\iota_{0}}{\mathbf{B}^{+}_{\mathrm{dR}}},

hence the image of
{\mathfrak{t}}
under (

) is only
\theta(\varphi^{-n}({\mathfrak{t}}))
. That is, we obtained

\theta(\varphi^{-n}({\mathfrak{t}}))\in(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}.

Nonetheless, we have
\varphi({\mathfrak{t}})=\frac{pE(u)}{E(0)}{\mathfrak{t}}
. One can deduce that

{\mathfrak{t}}=\varphi^{-n}({\mathfrak{t}})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{-i}(\frac{pE(u)}{E(0)}),

which holds as an equality inside
{\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{inf}}}
. To see that
\theta({\mathfrak{t}})\in(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
, it then suffices to see that each
\theta(\varphi^{-i}(\frac{pE(u)}{E(0)}))
is locally analytic: but each of these is an element of
{K_{\infty}}
hence is locally analytic (indeed, locally trivial).
∎

.

Let
\widetilde{K}/K
be a Galois extension contained in

Proof.

This follows from

Corollary 7.7
.

Up to nonzero scalars, the combination
\theta(u\lambda^{\prime})\nabla_{\gamma}+N_{\nabla}
(from Def.

) is the
unique
non-zero operator in
\hat{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Lie}(\hat{G})
that kills all of
(\hat{L})^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}
. Here
\lambda
is defined in Def.
6.10
, and
\lambda^{\prime}
is the

-derivative, that is
\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{d}{du}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

The existence of a linear combination

that kills

is guaranteed by Thm.

. In addition, neither
\nabla_{\gamma}
nor
\nabla_{\tau}
alone can kill all of

It hence suffices to compute this operator against the element

Indeed, we can even make the computation inside

.

(which holds uniformly even when

Hence we can conclude.

∎

Construction 7.8
.

(1)

As in
[

, §4.4]
,
consider the 2-dimensional
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-representation of

(associated to our choice of

g\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\chi(g)&c(g)\\

becomes trivial over

\alpha\in C_{p}
(indeed,
\alpha\in\hat{L}
) such that

.
This implies
g(\alpha)=\alpha/\chi(g)+c(g)/\chi(g)

.

(2)

Now similarly as in the beginning of
[
BC16
, §4.2]

\alpha_{n}\in L
such that
\|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\|_{p}\leq p^{-n}

\|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\|_{\hat{G}_{m}}=\|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\|_{p}

(see Notation

for

, and see Def.

for
\|\cdot\|_{\hat{G}_{m}}
). We can furthermore suppose that

is an increasing sequence.

Definition 7.9
.

Let
(H,\|\cdot\|)
be a
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-Banach algebra such that
\|\cdot\|
is sub-multiplicative, and let
W\subset H
be a
{{\mathbb Q}_{p}}
-subalgebra. Let
T
be a variable, and let
W\{\!\{T\}\!\}_{n}
be the vector space consisting of
\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}T^{k}
with

, and
p^{nk}a_{k}\rightarrow 0
when

. For

such that

, denote
W\{\!\{h\}\!\}_{n}
the image of the evaluation map

.

Proposition 7.10
.

(1)

\hat{L}^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}=\cup_{n\geq 1}K({\mu_{r(n)},\pi_{r(n)}})\{\!\{\alpha-\alpha_{n}\}\!\}_{n}.

(2)

\hat{L}^{\hat{G}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$},\nabla_{\gamma}=0}=L.

(3)

\hat{L}^{\tau\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$},\gamma=1}={K_{\infty}}.

Proof.

Item (1) is
[
BC16
, Prop. 4.12]
, the rest follow easily, cf.
[
GP21
, Prop. 3.3.2]
.
We quickly recall the proof of Item (1) here. Suppose
x\in\hat{L}^{\hat{G}_{n}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
. For
i\geq 0
, let

y_{i}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}(\alpha-\alpha_{n})^{k}\nabla_{\tau}^{k+i}(x)\binom{k+i}{k},

then there exists
m\geq n
such that
y_{i}\in\hat{L}^{\hat{G}_{m}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
, and

(7.6)

x=\sum_{i\geq 0}y_{i}(\alpha-\alpha_{n})^{i}\in\hat{L}^{\hat{G}_{m}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}

Note roughly speaking, (
7.6
) is the “Taylor expansion” of

with respect to the “variable”
\alpha-\alpha_{n}
. The equality (
7.6
) holds precisely because

\nabla_{\tau}(\alpha-\alpha_{n})=\nabla_{\tau}(\alpha)=1.

Finally, the fact

will imply that

, concluding (1).
∎

Proposition 7.11
.

Denote
\beta=\theta(\mathfrak{t})
. Apply the same procedure as in Item (2) of Construction

.
Then

Recall in Def.

, we defined
\partial_{\gamma}:=\frac{\nabla_{\gamma}}{\beta}
. Since
\nabla_{\gamma}(\beta)=\beta
, we have

(7.8)

\partial_{\gamma}(\beta-\beta_{n})=\partial_{\gamma}(\beta)=1.

This is the key analogue of Eqn. (

).
Now similar to the proof in Prop.
7.10
, suppose
x\in\hat{L}^{\hat{G}_{n}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
, we can define

z_{i}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}(\beta-\beta_{n})^{k}\partial_{\gamma}^{k+i}(x)\binom{k+i}{k},

then there exists
m\geq n
such that

Remark 8.4
.

Let us point out a possible confusion in the proof of essential surjectivity above.
In the proof, we obtained a
C
-linear isomorphim
W\rightarrow W^{\prime}
(of two vectors spaces
without
considering Galois actions), which is further compatible with the
C
-linear Sen operators. Indeed, it comes from the following composite:

W\simeq N\otimes_{K}C\simeq\mathbb{M}(({\mathfrak{S}},E))\otimes_{K}C\simeq W^{\prime}

where the first isomorphism follows from Sen’s original construction, the second comes from construction of

, and the third comes from definition of

. Note however, the “identity” map

is
not
“
G_{K}
-equivariant”! (Hence, in particular, the composite (

) is
not

G_{K}
-equivariant!)
Indeed, the
\tau
-action on

is trivial under classical Sen theory; whereas the
\tau
-action on
\mathbb{M}(({\mathfrak{S}},E))
is in general not trivial by Prop.

.
The powerful aspect of Sen’s theorem
[
Sen81
, p. 101, Thm. 7]
, is that the Sen-operator-equivariant map (

) already guarantees existence of a
G_{K}
-equivariant isomorphism between
W
and
W^{\prime}
, although in an
implicit

We prove the cohomology comparison in Thm.
1.5
(2).

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
\ast\in\{\emptyset,\mathrm{log}\}
. Let
\mathbb{M}\in{\mathrm{Vect}}({(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\ast}},\overline{{\mathcal{O}}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}[1/p])
be a Hodge–Tate crystal, and let

be the associated representation. There exists a natural quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{R}\Gamma({(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\ast}},\mathbb{M})\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma({G_{K}},W)

where the right hand side is Galois cohomology.

Proof.

Restriction to the perfect site induces natural morphisms

\mathrm{R}\Gamma({(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\ast}},\mathbb{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{R}\Gamma({(\mathcal{O}_{K})^{\mathrm{perf}}_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\ast}},\mathbb{M})\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma({G_{K}},W)

where the second quasi-isomorphism follows from Lem.
5.5

Let
(M,\phi_{M})
be the corresponding Sen module. By Thm.
4.5
, we have

{\mathrm{R}}\Gamma(({\mathcal{O}}_{K})_{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},*},{\mathbb M})\simeq[M\xrightarrow{\phi_{M}}M].

Apply Thm.
8.2
and Thm.
7.17
respectively, we have

[M\xrightarrow{\phi_{M}}M]\otimes_{K}{K_{\infty}}\simeq[D_{{\mathrm{Sen}},{K_{\infty}}}(W)\xrightarrow{\phi_{{\mathrm{Sen}},{K_{\infty}}}}D_{{\mathrm{Sen}},{K_{\infty}}}(W)]\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma(G_{K},W)\otimes_{K}{K_{\infty}}.

We can conclude.

8.1.
Comparison with a theorem of Sen

In this subsection, we recall a (rather peculiar) theorem of Sen which also concerns about “
K
-rationality” of the Sen operator, and make some comparisons with our result.

Theorem 8.6
.

[
Sen81
, p.110, Thm. 10]

Suppose the residue field
k
is
algebraically closed
. Then there exists a (
non-canonical
) equivalence between
{\mathrm{Rep}}_{G_{K}}(C)
and the category of
K
-vector spaces equipped with a linear operator. This non-canonical equivalence, which depend on choices for each simple object in
{\mathrm{Rep}}_{G_{K}}(C)
, is
never
compatible with tensor products.

Construction 8.7
.

We give a very brief sketch of the rather technical proof of Thm.
8.6
, pointing out where
non-canonicity
comes from.

(1)

Recall, as we already used in the proof of our Thm.
8.3
, Sen shows in
[
Sen81
, p.100, Thm. 5]
that for any
C
-representation, the Sen operator is “
K
-rational”: that is, it is stable on a sub-
K
-vector space.

(2)

Conversely, when the residue field
k
is algebraically closed,
[
Sen81
, p.104, Thm. 9]
shows that any
K
-matrix can be recovered as a Sen operator for some
C
-representation.
Note however, as stated in
[
Sen81
, p.105, Thm. 9’]
, this only constructs a
set bijection
between
H^{1}(G_{K},{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(C))
and the set of similarity classes of
n\times n
matrices over
K
. One still needs to build some
functoriality
into this bijection.

(3)

Sen then
chooses
, for each
simple

C
-representation
U
, a
K
-vector space
U_{0}
stable under Sen operator; this builds a functor for
semi-simple
objects. To get the complete functor, one first map a general
C
-representation
W
to its semi-simple part
W_{S}
, then one notes
(W_{S})_{0}
is still stable under the Sen operator of
W
(not just that of
W_{S}
!), since Sen operator is Galois equivariant.

Remark 8.8
.

In the remark following
[
Fon04
, Thm. 2.14]
, Fontaine mentions that Thm.
8.6
can also be obtained using his
classification
of
C
-representations: this also points to the non-canonicity of Thm.
8.6
.

Remark 8.9
.

We give some yet further remarks about the non-canonicity of Thm.
8.6
, and compare it with our Thm.
8.3
.
In
p
-adic Hodge theory, many typical theorems say that there is an equivalence of categories:

\mathrm{MOD}\simeq\mathrm{REP}

where
\mathrm{MOD}
is certain “module category” and
\mathrm{REP}
is certain “representation category”. We invite the readers to consider the examples such as results of Bhatt-Scholze
[
BS23
]
, of Cherbonnier-Colmez
[
CC98
]
, or Thm.
8.3
.
In proofs of these results, it is usually easy to construct a
functor
from
\mathrm{MOD}
to
\mathrm{REP}
(sometimes perhaps initially to a bigger representation category, such as in our Thm.
8.3
). The most difficult part almost always is to show the functor is essentially surjective (or, to determine its essential image).

What happens with Sen’s result Thm.
8.6
, is that there is not even an obvious
map
from
\mathrm{MOD}
to
\mathrm{REP}
;
Sen can still construct a such map, by the
set bijection
mentioned in
8.7
(2): but then this is hopeless to be a
functor
. This forces Sen to go the other way around, by constructing a
functor
from
\mathrm{REP}
to
\mathrm{MOD}
, which then unfortunately relies on many choices, and cannot be canonical.
We regard this strong contrast with our Thm.
8.3
as another hint (in addition to Rem.
1.6
) that the (log)- nearly Hodge–Tate representations deserve to be further studied.

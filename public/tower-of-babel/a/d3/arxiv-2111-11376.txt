Stratifying systems and g g -vectors Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 18G20, 18E40, 16D10. Secondary 16E99. Keywords : Stratifying systems; g g -vectors; τ \tau -rigid modules; Cartan matrices and groups; weakly triangular algebras.
Octavio Mendoza, Corina Sáenz and Hipolito Treffinger

Abstract
. In this paper we study the Cartan matrix associated to the Ext-projective stratifying system induced by a basic and τ \tau -rigid object M M in mod ⁡ ( A ) \mathrm{mod}\,(A) by means of the g g -vectors of the indecomposable direct summands of M M . In particular we show that the Cartan group of a stratifying system associated to a τ \tau -rigid module can be calculated directly using these vectors. Moreover we characterise the stratifying systems coming from τ \tau -rigid modules that have a diagonal Cartan matrix.



1.
Introduction

The notion of an Ext-injective stratifying system was introduced in
[
8
]
as an axiomatisation of the standard objects of the standardly stratified algebras defined in
[
6
]
.
It was quickly realised that Ext-injective stratifying systems are equivalent to the notions of Ext-projective stratifying systems
[
14
]
and what is now known simply as stratifying systems
[
13
]
.
Since then, stratifying systems were deeply studied in
[
7
,
20
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
10
]
. Throughout this paper,
A
will denote to a basic finite dimensional
k
-algebra over an algebraically closed filed
k.
We will work on the abelian category
\mathrm{mod}(A)
of all the finitely generated left
A
-modules. Given a positive integer
t,
we denote by
[1,t]
the set of all the natural numbers between
1
and
t,
and it is also considered the natural order
\leq
on the set
[1,t].

The notion of Ext-projective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}(A)
is defined in
[
14
]
. We recall this notion as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

[
14
, Definition 2.1]

An Ext-projective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}(A),
of size
t,
is a triple
(\Theta,\underline{Q},\leq),
where
\Theta:=\{\Theta(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a family of non-zero
A
-modules,
\underline{Q}:=\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a family of indecomposable
A
-modules and
\leq
is the natural order on
[1,t]
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(j),\Theta(i))=0
for
j>i;

(b)

for each
i\in[1,t]
, there is an exact sequence

0\to U(i)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}Q(i)\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}\Theta(i)\to 0

in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
such that
U(i)
is filtered by the set
\{\Theta(j):j>i\}
;

(c)

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(Q,X)=0
where
Q:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}Q(i)
and
X
is an
A
-module filtered by
\{\Theta(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
.

Although it has been shown that stratifying systems are objects having very nice properties, until very recently there was no systematic method to construct stratifying systems in the module category of a given algebra
A
.
This problem has been addressed in
[
19
]
and
[
22
]
using tools from
\tau
-tilting theory and higher homological algebra, respectively.

It was shown in

two invariants

and

, known as the

and the

of
\Theta
, respectively (see Section

). In this paper, we study the Cartan matrix

and the Cartan group

for a stratifying system

associated to a (basic)

\tau
-rigid module

In particular, we show that the matrix

can be obtained using the

-matrix

(formed by the

-vectors of the indecomposable direct summands of

), the

-standard matrix

(formed by the dimension vectors of the

-standard modules

2.
Setting and background

For
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
we denote by
rk(M)
the number of pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands of
M.
Finally, we say that
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
is
basic
if there is a decomposition
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}M_{i},
with
M_{i}
indecomposable for every
i
and
M_{i}\not\simeq M_{j}
if
i\neq j.

Given an algebra
A
, we denote by
M\twoheadrightarrow N
an epimorphism from
M
to
N
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A);
and for each
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
we consider the class

\mathrm{Fac}(M):=\{X\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)\;:\;\exists\,M^{n}\twoheadrightarrow X\text{ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}.

Dually, the arrow
\hookrightarrow
stands for a monomorphism in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
and we have the class

\mathrm{Sub}(M):=\{X\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)\;:\;\exists\,X\hookrightarrow M^{n}\text{ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}.

For any subclass
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
we have the right
\mathrm{Hom}
-perpendicular complement of
\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{X}^{\perp}:=\{M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)\;:\;\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(-,M)|_{\mathcal{X}})=0\}.

Dually, we have the left
\mathrm{Hom}
-perpendicular complement
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{X}.
For a single element class
\mathcal{X}=\{M\},
we just write
M^{\perp}
and
{}^{\perp}M.

It is said that
N\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
admits an
\mathcal{X}
-filtration
if there is a chain of submodules

0=M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq M_{n-1}\subseteq M_{n}=M
satisfying that each quotient

M_{i}/M_{i-1}\simeq X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}.

The class of all the
N\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
which admits an
\mathcal{X}
-filtration will be denoted by
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X}).

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
and
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A).

A morphism
f:X\to M
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
is called
\mathcal{X}
-precover (or right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation) of
M
if
X\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X^{\prime},f):\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X^{\prime},X)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(X^{\prime},M)
is surjective, for any
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, if the equality
fh=f
holds true only for an automorphism
h:X\to X,
it is said that the
\mathcal{X}
-precover is an
\mathcal{X}
-cover.
The class
\mathcal{X}
is precovering (or contravariantly finite) if any
N\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
admits an
\mathcal{X}
-precover.
Dually, we have the notions of
\mathcal{X}
-preenvelope (or left
\mathcal{X}
-approximation),
\mathcal{X}
-envelope and preenveloping (or covariantly finite) class.
If
\mathcal{X}
is precovering and preenveloping, it is said that
\mathcal{X}
is functorially finite.

We recall that a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
of full subcategories in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
is a
torsion pair
if
\mathcal{X}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}^{\perp}.

Given a torsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
it is said that
\mathcal{X}
is a torsion class and
\mathcal{Y}
is a torsion free class.
Moreover, in this case, it can be shown that
\mathcal{X}
is closed under quotients and extensions, while
\mathcal{Y}
is closed under submodules and extensions.
It is also well known that, for every subclass
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
which is closed under quotients and extensions, there exists a subclass
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
such that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a torsion pair in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).

In particular, if
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{Fac}(M)
for some
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
then
\mathcal{Y}=M^{\perp}.

It is also well known that each torsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
has associated two additive functors

\mathrm{t},\mathrm{f}:\mathrm{mod}\,(A)\to\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
The
torsion functor

\mathrm{t}
is a subfunctor of the identity functor
1_{\mathrm{mod}\,(A)},

and the
torsion free functor

\mathrm{f}
is equal to the quotient functor
1/\mathrm{t}.

Moreover, for every
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
there exists the so-called canonical short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{t}(M)\to M\to\mathrm{f}(M)\to 0,

where
\mathrm{t}(M)\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathrm{f}(M)\in\mathcal{Y}
and such exact sequence is unique up to isomorphisms.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory of
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).

It is said that
X\in\mathcal{X}
is
Ext-projective
in
\mathcal{X}
if
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(X,-)|_{\mathcal{X}}=0.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
is functorially finite and a torsion class, it is well known that there are only finitely many (up to isomorphisms) indecomposable Ext-projective modules in
\mathcal{X}
and we denote by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X})
their direct sum.

3.
Stratifying systems

The concept of stratifying system for module categories was first introduced in
[
8
]
, and further studied in
[
13
,
14
]
.
The original definition is stated in Definition
3.2
, but it was shown in
[
13
]
that this definition is equivalent to the following one.

Definition 3.1
.

[
13
, Characterisation 1.6]

A stratifying system of size
t,
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
consists of a pair
(\Theta,\leq),
where
\Theta:=\{\Theta(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a family of indecomposable objects in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
and
\leq
is the natural order on the set
[1,t]:=\{1,2,\dots,t\}
satisfying the following conditions:

\mathrm{(a)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(j),\Theta(i))=0
if
j>i
;

\mathrm{(b)}

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0
if
j\geq i
.

Let
(\Theta,\leq)
be a stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
The theory of stratifiying systems, developed in
[
8
,
13
,
14
]
, shows that
\mathcal{F}(\Theta)
is equivalent to the full subcategory
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
of
\mathrm{mod}\,(B)
, where
B
is a standardly stratified algebra and
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
is the category of the
\Delta
-filtered objects, of key importance in the theory of quasi-hereditary and standardly stratified algebras. In order to determine the standardly stratified algebra
B
, it is necessary to consider the so-called
Ext-projective
and
Ext-injective
stratifying systems. The notion of Ext-projective stratifying system was given in Definition
1.1
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
8
, Definition 1.1]

An Ext-injective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}(A),
of size
t,
is a triple

(\Theta,\underline{Y},\leq),
where
\Theta:=\{\Theta(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a family of non-zero
A
-modules,
\underline{Y}:=\{Y(i)\}_{i=1}^{t}
is a family of indecomposable
A
-modules and
\leq
is the natural order on
[1,t]
satisfying the following conditions:

\mathrm{(a)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(j),\Theta(i))=0
for
j>i;

\mathrm{(b)}

for each
i\in[1,t]
, there is an exact sequence
0\to\Theta(i)\xrightarrow{\lambda_{i}}Y(i)\xrightarrow{\gamma_{i}}Z(i)\to 0
in

\mathrm{mod}\,(A)

such that
Z(i)\in\mathcal{F}(\{\Theta(j):j<i\});

\mathrm{(c)}

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(-,Y)|_{\mathcal{F}(\Theta)}=0,
for
Y:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}Y(i).

Given a stratifying system
(\Theta,\leq),
it is well known
[
8
,
13
,
14
]
that there exist a unique (up to isomorphism) Ext-projective stratifying system
(\Theta,\underline{Q},\leq)
and an Ext-injective stratifying system
(\Theta,\underline{Y},\leq).

Furthermore, for a given Ext-projective stratifying system
(\Theta,\underline{Q},\leq),
we have that the pair

(\Theta,\leq)
is a stratifying system. Moreover the same statement holds true for Ext-injective stratifying systems.

Let
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq)
be an Ext-projective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
By following
[
15
, Section 4]
, we consider the
\Theta
-Cartan matrix
C_{\Theta}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{t\times t}(\mathbb{Z}),
naturally associated to this system, which is defined as follows: the
(i,j)
-coordinate of
C_{\Theta}
is
[C_{\Theta}]_{i,j}:=\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),\Theta(j)).

The main idea in
[
15
]
behind the introduction of the Cartan matrix
C_{\Theta},
for an Ext-projective stratifying system
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq),
is that we can use it to construct a finite abelian group
G_{\Theta}
, known as the
Cartan Group
of
\Theta
which is defined as
G_{\Theta}:=\mathrm{CoKer}(C_{\Theta}),
where
the matrix
C_{\Theta}
is identified with the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\mathbb{Z}^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}^{t},\;X\mapsto C_{\Theta}\,X.
It is shown in
[
15
]
, that

|G_{\Theta}|=1
if and only if the standardly stratified algebra
\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q)^{op}
is quasi-hereditary. Then the size
|G_{\Theta}|
of
G_{\Theta}
give us a measure of how far is
\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q)^{op}
from being quasi-hereditary.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
14
,
15
,
16
]
Let
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq)
be an Ext-projective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
Then,
C_{\Theta}
is an upper triangular matrix and
[C_{\Theta}]_{i,i}=\dim_{k}(\mathrm{End}_{A}(\Theta(i)))

\forall\,i\in[1,t].

Moreover
|G_{\Theta}|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[C_{\Theta}]_{i,i}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
16
, Lemma 2.5 (b)]
and
[
14
, Lemma 2.6 (b)]
.
∎

Let
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq)
be an Ext-projective stratifying system in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
In what follows, for each
i\in[1,t],
we consider
the exact sequence

\eta_{i}:\;0\rightarrow U(i)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}Q(i)\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}\Theta(i)\rightarrow 0
associated to each

\Theta(i),
see Definition
1.1
(b), and the map

\partial_{i,j}:\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U(i),\Theta(j))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j)),\;f\mapsto f\cdot\eta_{i},

where
f\cdot\eta_{i}
is the push-out of

f
and
\eta_{i}.

Proposition 3.4
.

For an Ext-projective stratifying system
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq)
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A),

the following statements are equivalent.

\mathrm{(a)}

The matrix
C_{\Theta}
is diagonal.

\mathrm{(b)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),Q(j))=0
for
i<j.

\mathrm{(c)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),\Theta(j))=0
for
i<j.

\mathrm{(d)}

For
i<j,
we have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0
and

\partial_{i,j}:\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U(i),\Theta(j))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j)),\;f\mapsto f\cdot\eta_{i}

is an isomorphism.

Moreover, if one of the above conditions holds true, then
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),U(j))=0
for
i<j.

Proof.

(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(c): It follows from Lemma
3.3
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): Let
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),Q(j))=0
for
i<j.
We prove now that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),\Theta(j))=0.

If
Q(j)=\Theta(j)
there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, take
f:Q(i)\to\Theta(j)
and consider the exact sequence

\eta_{j}:\hskip 10.00002pt0\rightarrow U(j)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j}}Q(j)\xrightarrow{\beta_{j}}\Theta(j)\rightarrow 0

associated to
\Theta(j),

where
U(j)
is nonzero and
U(j)\in\mathcal{F}(\{\Theta(k):k>j\}).

Since
Q(i)
is Ext-projective in
\mathcal{F}(\Theta)
, there exist
f^{\prime}:Q(i)\to Q(j)
such that
f^{\prime}\beta_{j}=f
.
By hypothesis,
f^{\prime}=0
. Hence
f=0
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b): Let
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),\Theta(j))=0
for
i<j.
We carry on the proof by reverse induction on
j.

If
j=n
then
\Theta(n)=Q(n)
and thus (b) follows. Therefore, we can assume that
j<n.
Let us prove that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),Q(j))=0.
In order to do that, take a map
g:Q(i)\to Q(j)
and consider the exact sequence
\eta_{j}.

Since
g\beta_{j}=0
, there exist
h:Q(i)\to U(j)
such that
\alpha_{j}h=g.

Moreover, since
U(j)\in\mathcal{F}(\{\Theta(k):k>j\})
,
[
14
, Proposition 2.10]
implies the existence of an exact sequence

0\rightarrow N\to Q_{0}(U(j))\xrightarrow{\epsilon_{U(j)}}U(j)\rightarrow 0

in
\mathcal{F}(\Theta),
where
Q_{0}(U(j))\in\mathrm{add}\left(\bigoplus_{k>j}Q(k)\right)
.
Once again, by using that
Q(i)
is Ext-projective in
\mathcal{F}(\Theta),
there exist
h^{\prime}:Q(i)\to Q_{0}(U(j))
such that
\epsilon_{U(j)}h^{\prime}=h
.
By induction,
h^{\prime}=0
.
Therefore
g=\alpha_{j}\epsilon_{U(j)}h^{\prime}=0
.

(c)
\Leftrightarrow
(d): Let
i<j.
By applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(-,\Theta(j))
to the exact sequence
\eta_{i},
we get the following exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),\Theta(j))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U(i),\Theta(j))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))\to 0

Thus, the equivalence between (c) and (d) follows from the above exact sequence.

Finally, assume that (b) holds true. Then, by applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),-)
to
\eta_{j}
we get that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(Q(i),U(j))=0
for
i<j.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For an Ext-projective stratifying system
(\Theta,\{Q(i)\}_{i=1}^{t},\leq)
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
such that
the matrix
C_{\Theta}
is diagonal, the following statements are equivalent.

\mathrm{(a)}

[U(i):\Theta(j)]=0
for
i<j.

\mathrm{(b)}

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0

\forall\,i,j.

\mathrm{(c)}

Q(i)\simeq\Theta(i)
as
A
-modules
\forall\,i.

\mathrm{(d)}

U(i)=0

\forall\,i.

Moreover, if one of the above conditions holds true, then
\mathcal{F}(\Theta)=\mathrm{add}(Q)
and
\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q)\simeq\times_{i=1}^{t}\,\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q(i))
as
k
-algebras.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
, we get that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0

\forall\,i\neq j,
and moreover

\partial_{i,j}:\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U(i),\Theta(j))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))
for
i<j.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Let
i,j\in[1,t].
By
[
14
, Lemma 2.6 (b)]
, we know that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0
for
i\geq j.
Hence, we can assume that
i<j.
Since
U(i)\in\mathcal{F}(\Theta(l)\;:\;l>i),
by (a) we have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(U(i),\Theta(j))=0.
Therefore

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Theta(i),\Theta(j))=0
for
i<j.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c): Let
i\in[1,t].
Then, by (b), we get that the exact sequence
\eta_{i}:\hskip 10.00002pt0\rightarrow U(i)\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}Q(i)\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}\Theta(i)\rightarrow 0
splits. Thus
Q(i)\simeq\Theta(i)
as
A
-modules since
Q(i)
and
\Theta(i)
are indecomposable (see
[
14
, Corollary 2.13]
).

(c)
\Rightarrow
(d): From (c) and the exact sequence

we have that

(d)
\Rightarrow
(a): It is trivial.

Finally, If one of the above conditions holds true, then we get that

\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q)\simeq\times_{i=1}^{t}\,\mathrm{End}_{A}(Q(i))

∎

4.
\tau

Most of the results of this paper are proven using the tools provided by
\tau

We now give a brief summary of the definitions and results on
\tau

For a broader survey on this topic, the reader is encouraged to see

Definition 4.1
.

[
1
, Definition 0.1]

Let

\tau
-rigid

\tau
-rigid module

\tau
-rigid and

\tau
-rigid pair

One of the main features of
\tau
-tilting theory is that all functorially finite torsion classes in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A)
can be described by using
\tau
-tilting pairs, as stated in the following result that has been taken from
[

.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any algebra

there is a well defined function

from
\tau
-rigid basic pairs to functorially finite torsion classes in
\mathrm{mod}\,(A).

Moreover,

is a bijection if we restrict it to the class
\mathrm{s\tau\text{-}tilt}(A)
of
\tau
-tilting pairs, and in this case
\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{X})=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X}).

In
[
19
]
, it was shown how to produce a stratifying system from a basic
\tau
-rigid module
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A).
We start by recalling the notion of torsion free admissible decomposition.

Definition 4.3
.

[

Let

-rigid. A decomposition

The existence of such TF-admissible decompositions is supported by the following proposition proved in

Proposition 4.4

[

Every basic and
\tau
-rigid

The following result, proved in

[

Let

In this paper we study the relation between the homological properties of a basic and

For defining such

Definition 4.6

[

Let

Lemma 5.2

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

i<j.
Consider the exact sequence
\varepsilon_{i}:\;0\to K(i)\to M_{i}\to\Delta_{M}(i)\to 0.
Then, we get the exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Delta_{M}(i),\Delta_{M}(j))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M_{i},\Delta_{M}(j))\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(K(i),\Delta_{M}(j))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Delta_{M}(i),\Delta_{M}(j))\to 0

Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(M_{i},\Delta_{M}(j))=0
(see Lemma
5.2
(d)). Thus, the equivalence between (a) and (c) follows from the above exact sequence and Theorem
5.3
(a).

Let
M\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{M})
and
(G^{M})^{tr}\,S^{M}
be a diagonal matrix.
By Theorem
5.3
and Proposition
5.4
, we get that
C_{\Delta_{M}}=(G^{M})^{tr}\,S^{M}
and
M_{i}\simeq Q(i)

\forall\,i.
Then, by
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
and Lemma
5.2
(b), we conclude that

G_{B}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\,\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z}.

∎

Theorem 5.8
.

For a basic
\tau
-tilting module
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,(A),
with a TF-admissible decomposition
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
such that

M\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{M}),
the following statements are equivalent.

\mathrm{(a)}

The matrix
C_{\Delta_{M}}
is diagonal.

\mathrm{(b)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M_{i},M_{j})=0
for
i<j.

\mathrm{(c)}

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M_{i},\Delta_{M}(j))=0
for
i<j.

\mathrm{(d)}

For
i<j,
we have that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Delta_{M}(i),\Delta_{M}(j))=0
and the map

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(K(i),\Delta_{M}(j))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\Delta_{M}(i),\Delta_{M}(j)),\;f\mapsto f\cdot\eta_{i}

is an isomorphism.

Moreover, if one of the above conditions holds true, then
M\simeq{}_{A}A,

A
is a weakly triangular algebra and
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M_{i},K(j))=0
for

i<j.

Proof.

Let
(\Delta_{M},\underline{Q},\leq)
be the Ext-projective stratifying system induced by
(\Delta_{M},\leq).
By Theorem
5.3
and Proposition
5.4
, we get that
C_{\Delta_{M}}=(G^{M})^{tr}\,S^{M},

U(i)\simeq K(i)
and
M_{i}\simeq Q(i)

\forall\,i.
Thus, the fact that
\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(M_{i},K(j))=0
for
i<j
(by assuming one of the above conditions) and the equivalences between (a), (b), (c) and (d) follow from Proposition
3.4
.

Now, assume that one of the above equivalent conditions holds true.
Let us proof, firstly, that
M\simeq{}_{A}A.
Indeed, since the decomposition
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
is TF-admissible, we get from (b) that
M_{i}\not\in\mathrm{Fac}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}M_{j})

\forall\,i\in[1,n].

On the other hand, it is known that
\bigoplus_{j\neq i}M_{j}
admits two torsion classes, namely,
\mathrm{Fac}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}M_{j})
and
{}^{\perp}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}\tau M_{j}).

Moreover, since
\mathrm{Fac}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}M_{j})\subsetneq{}^{\perp}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}\tau M_{j})
and
M_{i}\not\in\mathrm{Fac}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}M_{j}),
we have that
M_{i}\in{}^{\perp}(\bigoplus_{j\neq i}\tau M_{j})
and thus the mutation of
M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}M_{i}
at
M_{i}
is descendent, for all
i\in[1,n].

Let us show that
\mathrm{Fac}(M)=\mathrm{mod}(A).

Suppose that
\mathrm{Fac}(M)\neq\mathrm{mod}(A).
Since
(\mathrm{Fac}(M),M^{\perp})
is a torsion pair in
\mathrm{mod}(A),
it follows from
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
, that there exists a mutation
M^{\prime}
of
M
such that
\mathrm{Fac}(M)\subsetneq\mathrm{Fac}(M^{\prime})
contradicting that any mutation of
M
is descendent.
Therefore
\mathrm{Fac}(M)=\mathrm{mod}(A)
and then
M\simeq{}_{A}A.

In particular,
A
is a standardly stratified algebra since
M_{i}\simeq Q(i)

\forall\,i;
and by
[
15
, Remark 4.5]
, we conclude that
A
is a weakly triangular algebra.
∎

A new modified Newton iteration for computing nonnegative Z Z -eigenpairs of nonnegative tensors
Chun-Hua Guo, Wen-Wei Lin, Ching-Sung Liu

Abstract
. We propose a new modification of Newton iteration for finding some nonnegative Z Z -eigenpairs of a nonnegative tensor. The method has local quadratic convergence to a nonnegative eigenpair of a nonnegative tensor, under the usual assumption guaranteeing the local quadratic convergence of the original Newton iteration.

A real-valued

\mathcal{A}=(A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}})\text{, }\quad A_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}\in\mathbb{R}\text{, }\quad 1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\leq n.

The set of all such tensors is denoted by

, and the set of all nonnegative tensors
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{[m,n]}
.
We use

or

to represent the

\mathbf{x}
.

Let

We say that

is a

-eigenpair
(eigenvector-eigenvalue) of

if

(1)

\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1}=\lambda\mathbf{x},\quad\|\mathbf{x}\|=1,

where
(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1})_{i}=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{n}A_{ii_{2}\ldots i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{m}}

The vector norm in (

-norm. A

It is shown in

-eigenpairs and a

A modified Newton iteration (MNI) proposed in
[
5
]
can usually
find some nonnegative eigenpairs of a nonnegative tensor using different initial vectors. A local quadratic convergence result is proved there for positive eigenpairs.
(All nonnegative

Recently, another modified Newton iteration (called PNI) is proposed in

In this paper, we will address some issues associated with PNI, particularly the determination of

approximating a nonnegative eigenpair of the tensor, and present a new modification of the Newton iteration.

2.
A new modified Newton iteration

To compute an eigenpair
(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})
with

\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\geq 0
and
\|\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\|_{1}=\mathbf{e}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{\ast}=1
, where
\mathbf{e}=[1,\ldots,1]^{T},
we can apply Newton’s method to
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\lambda)=0
, where

(2)

\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\lambda)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1}\\
\mathbf{e}^{T}\mathbf{x}-1\end{array}\right].

The Jacobian of
\mathbf{f}(x,\lambda)
is given by

(3)

\mathbf{Jf}(\mathbf{x},\lambda)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda I-T(\mathbf{x})&\mathbf{x}\\
\mathbf{e}^{T}&0\end{array}\right],

where the entries of
T(\mathbf{x})
are

T(\mathbf{x})_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1}\right)_{i}.

For a given approximation
(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k},\widehat{\lambda}_{k})
to
(\mathbf{x}_{*},\lambda_{*})
,
Newton’s method determines the next approximation
(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1},\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1})
by

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{\lambda}_{k}I-T({\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})&\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\\
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{d}_{k}\\
\delta_{k}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\lambda}_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-\mathcal{A}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{m-1}\\
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-1\end{array}\right],

(10)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\,-\mathbf{d}_{k},

(11)

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\delta_{k},

assuming that

(12)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{\lambda}_{k}I-T({\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})&\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\\
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}&0\end{array}\right]

is nonsingular.

]

We also mention that a Newton-based method has been studied in

Modified Newton iteration (MNI)

The default value for
\lambda_{0}
in line 3 of Algorithm
1
is
\lambda_{0}=\overline{\lambda}_{0}
.
When
{\bf e}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\neq 0
for
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}
in line 5, we use (
14

\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}=\frac{1}{m-1}\left({\lambda}_{k}-\frac{1}{{\mathbf{e}}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}}\right).

The default value for
\lambda_{k+1}
in line 10 is then given by

\lambda_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{\lambda}_{k+1}&\mbox{ if }{\bf e}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=0\mbox{ or }\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}>\overline{\lambda}_{k+1},\\
\underline{\lambda}_{k+1}&\mbox{ if }\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}<\underline{\lambda}_{k+1},\\
\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}&\mbox{ if }\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}\in[\underline{\lambda}_{k+1},\overline{\lambda}_{k+1}].\end{array}\right.

If the chosen
\lambda_{k}
is such that
{\lambda}_{k}I-T({\mathbf{x}_{k}})
is singular or nearly singular, we can always adjust it within the interval
[\underline{\lambda}_{k},\overline{\lambda}_{k}]
.
Note that we have
\underline{\lambda}_{k}<\overline{\lambda}_{k}
unless
(\mathbf{x}_{k},\lambda_{k})
is already an eigenpair.

The PNI in
[
1

can be described through a comparison with MNI. In PNI, line 6 of MNI is not performed. So the
\mathbf{w}_{k}
in line 7 is still the
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}

in line 5. It is assumed in
[
1

that
{\bf e}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\neq 0
. Then

{\bf e}^{T}\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}=m-1>0
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}
has some positive components. In PNI, all negative components of
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}

are replaced by
0
. That is, the update
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\leftarrow\max(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1},\mathbf{0})
is performed before line 8 is performed.
In this way, PNI can potentially find nonnegative eigenvectors with some zero components much more quickly. But it has also created a new problem: in line 9 of MNI the definition in (
17
) must be modified.
For a pair of nonnegative vectors
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}
, it is defined in
[
1
]
that

\displaystyle\max\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\left\{\underset{i\in S_{2}}{\max}\left(\frac{w_{i}}{v_{i}}\right),\underset{i\in S_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})}{\max}\left(w_{i}\right)\right\}&\quad\mbox{if }S_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\neq\emptyset,\\
\underset{i\in S_{2}}{\max}\left(\frac{w_{i}}{v_{i}}\right)&\quad\mbox{if }S_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=\emptyset,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\quad\mbox{if }S_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\neq\emptyset,\\
\underset{i\in S_{2}}{\min}\left(\frac{w_{i}}{v_{i}}\right)&\quad\mbox{if }S_{1}\setminus(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=\emptyset,\end{array}\right.

where
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are the index sets of all nonzero elements of
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{v}
, respectively.
Note that the above definition reduces to definition (

\mathbf{v}>\mathbf{0}

In PNI
[
1
]
,

It is suggested in

To prove local quadratic convergence of PNI, it is assumed in

\beta_{k+1}\leq\min\left\{1,\frac{\max\left\{\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\|_{1},|\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}-{\lambda}_{\ast}|\right\}}{\max\left\{|\overline{\lambda}_{k+1}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}|,|\underline{\lambda}_{k+1}-\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}|\right\}}\right\}

but it is difficult to make sure, since the unknown

is involved. Moreover, a small
\beta_{k+1}
may not ensure

Algorithm 2

Modified projected Newton iteration (MPNI)

In PNI
[
1
]
, effort is made to choose
\lambda_{k}
such that
{\lambda}_{k}I-T({\mathbf{x}_{k}})
is nonsingular. Actually, the choice of

\lambda_{k}
should also ensure that

(22)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\lambda}_{k}I-T({{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})&{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\\
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}&0\end{array}\right]

is nonsingular. Otherwise, division by zero occurs in the computation of the vector
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}
since
{\bf e}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=0
.
Our Algorithm
2
also requires the nonsingularity of (
22
).
As
\lambda\to\infty
, we have

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}{\lambda}I-T({{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})&{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\\
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}&0\end{array}\right|=|\lambda I-T({{\mathbf{x}}}_{k})|(-{\bf e}^{T}(\lambda I-T({{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}))^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{k})\sim-\lambda^{n-1}

since
{\mathbf{e}}^{T}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}=1
. So the determinant is a polynomial of degree
n-1
. Therefore, when the matrix (
22
) is singular, we can choose a small
\epsilon_{k}>0
and use the update

\lambda_{k}\leftarrow\lambda_{k}+\epsilon_{k}
to ensure that the new matrix (
22
) is nonsingular.

We can easily prove that Algorithm
2
(MPNI) has local quadratic
convergence under the usual assumption that guarantees the local quadratic
convergence of the original Newton iteration.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast}\right)
be
a nonnegative eigenpair of a nonnegative tensor
\mathcal{A}
, with
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})
being nonsingular, and let
\left\{(\mathbf{x}_{k},{\lambda}_{k})\right\}

be generated by Algorithm
2
. Suppose that
(\mathbf{x}_{k_{0}},{\lambda}_{k_{0}})
is sufficiently close to
\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast}\right)
for some
k_{0}\geq 0
.
Then
(\mathbf{x}_{k},{\lambda}_{k})
converges
to
(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})
quadratically.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast})
is nonsingular,
\mathbf{J}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\lambda_{k})
is also nonsingular when
(\mathbf{x}_{k},{\lambda}_{k})
is sufficiently close to
\left(\mathbf{x}_{\ast},\lambda_{\ast}\right)
.
Let
(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1},\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1})
be obtained by Newton’s method as in (
2
)–(
11
), with
\left(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k},\widehat{{\lambda}}_{k}\right)=\left(\mathbf{x}_{k},{\lambda}_{k}\right)
. By a basic result of Newton’s method (see
[
7
, Theorem 5.1.2]
for example),
there is a constant
c
such
that

\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\\
\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\\
\lambda_{\ast}\end{array}\right]\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{k}\\
{\lambda}_{k}\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\\
\lambda_{\ast}\end{array}\right]\right\|_{1}^{2}.

Since
\lambda_{\ast}\geq 0
, we have

|{\lambda}_{k+1}-\lambda_{\ast}|\leq|\widehat{\lambda}_{k+1}-\lambda_{\ast}|.

Since
{\bf e}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{k}=1
, we have
{\bf e}^{T}\mathbf{d}_{k}=0
from (
2
) and
{\bf e}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}={\bf e}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{k}-{\bf e}^{T}\mathbf{d}_{k}=1
from (
10
).
It follows from
[
8
, Lemma 4]
that
\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\|_{1}\leq\|\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\|_{1}
.
Therefore,

\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\\
{\lambda}_{k+1}\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\\
\lambda_{\ast}\end{array}\right]\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{k}\\
{\lambda}_{k}\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{\ast}\\
\lambda_{\ast}\end{array}\right]\right\|_{1}^{2}

for all
(\mathbf{x}_{k},{\lambda}_{k})
sufficiently close to

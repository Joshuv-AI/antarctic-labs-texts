Du Bois complexes of cones over singular varieties, local cohomological dimension, and K K -groups Thanks: MP was partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2040378.
Mihnea Popa, Wanchun Shen

Abstract
. We compute the Du Bois complexes of abstract cones over singular varieties, and use this to describe the local cohomological dimension and the non-positive K K -groups of such cones.



A.
Introduction

As an undergraduate, the first author was introduced by Lucian Bădescu to the beautiful world of algebraic geometry. One of his favorite topics was that of Lefschetz-type theorems, in particular Barth’s celebrated result for arbitrary smooth subvarieties in projective space; he also often brought up Hartshorne and Ogus’ work on local cohomological dimension and its connections to the topology of projective varieties. We dedicate this note on a related circle of ideas to him, in the belief that he would have enjoyed it!

Concretely, in this paper we compute the Du Bois complexes of cones over arbitrary subvarieties in projective space; the case of smooth varieties can be found in
[
SVV
]
and the references therein. We use this for several applications.
The most important is not a new result, at least in the setting of classical cones, but we believe it nicely illustrates how such Du Bois complexes can be used in an almost algorithmic fashion: using the birational characterization of local cohomological dimension provided in
[
MP1
]
, expressed in an equivalent form in terms of the depth of Du Bois complexes, we give an alternative approach to Ogus’
[
Og
]
interpretation of the local cohomological dimension at the vertex of the cone over a projective variety
X
in terms of the topology of
X
. As a new result, we extend this characterization to all abstract cones over
X
. In a different direction, feeding our computation into the main technical result of
[
CHWW2
]
, we describe the non-positive
K
-groups of abstract cones over a projective variety in terms of its Du Bois complexes, extending the result in
loc. cit.
to singular complex varieties.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a complex projective variety of dimension
n
and codimension
r=N-n
, and let
Z=C(X)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
be the affine cone over
X
. We denote by
\lcd(Z,\mathbb{A}^{N+1})
the local cohomological dimension of
Z
in
\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
, and call

{\rm lcdef}(Z):=\lcd(Z,\mathbb{A}^{N+1})-r

the
local cohomological defect
of
Z
. This invariant depends only on
Z
, and not on the embedding in
\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
; see Section
2
for details. The following is a slight reformulation of Ogus’ result
[
Og
, Theorem 4.4]
(also recovered more recently in
[
HP
]
in the smooth case, and in
[
RSW
]
in general):

Theorem A
.

For an integer
c\geq 0
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\rm lcdef}(Z)\leq c

(2)

{\rm lcdef}(X)\leq c
and the restriction maps
H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{C})\to H^{i}(X,\mathbb{C})
are isomorphisms for
i\leq n-1-c
.

In particular:

{\rm lcdef}(Z)=0\iff{\rm lcdef}(X)=0~{\rm and}~H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{C})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{C})\,\,{\rm for~all}\,\,i\leq n-1.

Note that in
[
Og
]
, part (1) is phrased in terms of the cohomological dimension
{\rm cd}(\mathbb{P}^{N}\smallsetminus X)
, but it is well known via a standard argument that
\lcd(Z,\mathbb{A}^{N+1})={\rm cd}(\mathbb{P}^{N}\smallsetminus X)+1
.

The interest in the last equivalence in Theorem
A
stems from the fact that both sides are satisfied when
X
is a smooth complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. It is therefore intimately related to Hartshorne’s conjecture on small codimension subvarieties.

Note.
Recall that Barth’s theorem (see
[
La
, Scn.3.2]
for a survey) states that if
X
is smooth, then we always have

H^{i}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathbb{C})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{C})\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm for~all}\,\,\,\,i\leq n-r.

According to Theorem
A
, this is equivalent to the universal bound
{\rm lcdef}(Z)\leq r-1
.

We provide in fact an extension of Ogus’ result to any abstract cone over
X
, defined by the choice of ample line bundle
L
, in terms
of the action of
c_{1}(L)
on the singular cohomology of
X
; see Theorem
6.1
for the precise statement.
In our approach, this result, hence also Theorem
A
, follows from two main points. One is a reformulation of the characterization of
{\rm lcdef}(Z)
in
[
MP1
, Theorem E]
, for any complex variety
Z
, in terms of the Du Bois complexes of
Z
:

{\rm lcdef}(Z)=\dim Z-\underset{k\geq 0}{\min}~\{\depth\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}+k\}.

See Section
2
for details. The second, and the main technical result here, is the computation of the Du Bois complexes of (abstract) cones over arbitrary subvarieties in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, and of their depth.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a projective variety, endowed with an ample line bundle
L
. Let

Z=C(X,L)=\Spec\big(\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{m})\big)

be the affine cone over
X
associated to
L
, with cone point
x\in Z
. Then the Du Bois complexes
\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}
can be computed explicitly in terms of the Du Bois-Hodge theory of
X
and
L
; see Theorem
3.1
(1). The same holds for
\depth_{x}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}
; see Theorem
3.1
(2).

On a different note, to prove Theorem
6.1
we also need a general result of independent interest, namely a “dual” Nakano-type vanishing theorem for Du Bois complexes. We establish this in Section
5
, as a simple application of the Kodaira-Saito vanishing theorem for mixed Hodge modules.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety of dimension
n
, and
L
an ample line bundle on
X
. Then

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\otimes L^{-1})=0\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm for~all}\,\,\,\,p+q<n-{\rm lcdef}(X).

Finally, in a different direction, Theorem
B
is used in Section
8
to compute the non-positive
K
-groups of cones over a projective variety in terms of its Du Bois complexes, extending for complex varieties the result in
[
CHWW2
]
to abstract cones over singular varieties; this is simply an appendix to
loc. cit.
, as we are still using the main technical result of that paper.

Corollary D
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional complex projective variety endowed with an ample line bundle
L
, and let
Z=C(X,L)
be the affine cone over
X
associated to
L
. Then:

(1)

K_{0}(Z)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m})
;

(2)

K_{-\ell}(Z)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-\ell}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{\ell+i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m})
for all
\ell\geq 1
.

Here
\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}
are the Du Bois complexes over
\mathbb{Q}
of the complex variety
X
; see the beginning of Section
8
.
When
X
is embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, with
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, the method applies to the classical cone
C(X)
as well. For
l\geq 1
we have
K_{-l}(C(X))=K_{-l}(C(X,L))
, while for
l=0
we have

K_{0}(C(X))\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\Pic(C(X))\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m}).

Here
\Pic(C(X))\simeq R^{+}/R
, where
R
is the homogeneous coordinate ring of
X
, and

R^{+}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

is its seminormalization. A few things can also be said about positive
K
-groups, but a precise description can only be achieved in special cases.

B.
Main results

1.
Preliminaries

We start by collecting the main technical definitions and facts used in this paper.

Du Bois complexes.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety. The
filtered de Rham complex

(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{\bullet},F)
is an object in the bounded derived category of filtered differential complexes on
X
, introduced by Du Bois in
[
DB
]
, following the ideas of Deligne, and intended as a replacement for the standard de Rham complex on smooth varieties. For each
k\geq 0
, the (shifted) associated graded quotient

\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{X}:=\Gr^{k}_{F}\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{X}[k],

is an object in
D^{b}_{\rm coh}(X)
, called the
k
-th Du Bois complex
of
X
. It follows from definition that

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\simeq{\bf R}\epsilon_{\bullet*}\Omega_{X_{\bullet}}^{k},

where
\epsilon_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to X
is a hyperresolution of
X
.

Besides
[
DB
]
, we refer for instance to
[
GNPP
, Chapter V]
or to
[
PS
, Chapter 7.3]
for the construction of hyperresolutions, and for a detailed treatment of Du Bois complexes. Here we only collect the main properties that will be needed in this paper; these will be used freely in the most obvious instances.

Lemma 1.1
.

(1) We have
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}|_{U}\simeq\underline{\Omega}_{U}^{k}
for any open subset
U\subset X
, and any morphism
f:X\to Y
induces a canonical morphism
\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{k}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}
; see
[
DB
, Section 3]
and
[
PS
, Chapter 7.3]
.

(2) For each
k\geq 0
, there is a canonical morphism
\Omega_{X}^{k}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}
, which is an isomorphism if
X
is smooth; here

\Omega_{X}^{k}
are the sheaves of Kähler differentials on
X
; see
[
DB
, Section 4.1]
or
[
PS
, p.175]
.

(3) If
X
is projective, there exists a Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{1}=\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p})\implies H^{p+q}(X,\mathbb{C}),

which degenerates at
E_{1}
; see
[
DB
, Theorem 4.5(iii)]
or
[
PS
, Proposition 7.24]
.

(4) Given a discriminant square, i.e. a Cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

such that the horizontal maps are inclusions and
f:Y\smallsetminus E\to X\smallsetminus Z
is an isomorphism, then for each
k\geq 0
, we have an exact triangle

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\to\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}\oplus{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{Y}^{k}\to{\bf R}g_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k}\xrightarrow{+1}.

See
[
DB
, Proposition 4.11]
or
[
PS
, Example 7.25]
.

(5) Let
X_{\bullet}\to X
be a hyperresolution. Then there is a spectral sequence

E_{1}^{ij}=H^{j}(X_{i},\Omega_{X_{i}}^{k})\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}).

See
[
GNPP
, Chapter V, Lemma 3.1]
.

Depth of objects in the derived category.

The notion of depth of a module has a natural extension to complexes. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a noetherian local ring endowed with a dualizing complex
\omega_{R}^{\bullet}
(for us this will always be the local ring of
X
at a closed point), and let
C
be an element of the bounded derived category of finitely generated
R
-modules. Then one defines
\depth(C)
in any of the following equivalent ways:

(1)

\min~\{i~|~\Ext^{-i}_{R}(C,\omega_{R}^{\bullet})\neq 0\}
;

(2)

\min~\{i~|~\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m},C)\neq 0\}
;

(3)

\min~\{i~|~H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(C)\neq 0\}
,

with the convention that the depth is
-\infty
if
C=0
. The last two properties are studied and shown to be equivalent in
[
FY
]
, while
the equivalence with the first follows from local duality.

If
X
is a variety and
\mathcal{C}
is an element in
D^{b}_{\rm coh}(X)
, we will especially use the first and third interpretation, in the sense that
if
x\in X
is a closed point, we have

(1.2)

\depth_{x}(\mathcal{C})=\min~\{i~|~\mathcal{E}xt^{-i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{C}_{x},\omega_{X,x}^{\bullet})\neq 0\}=\min~\{i~|~\mathbb{H}^{i}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\neq 0\}.

We also set

\depth(\mathcal{C}):=\min_{x\in X}~\depth(\mathcal{C}_{x}),

where the minimum is taken over the closed points of
X
.

2.
Local cohomological dimension via Du Bois complexes

Let
X
be a complex variety. A cohomological invariant that is now understood to figure prominently in the study of the Du Bois complexes
of
X
is the local cohomological dimension. If
Y
is a smooth variety containing
X
(locally), this can be seen as

\lcd(X,Y):=\max~\{q~|~\mathcal{H}^{q}_{X}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\neq 0\},

where the sheaf in the parenthesis is the
q
-th local cohomology sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
along
X
; see
[
MP1
]
for an extensive discussion. It is in fact known that the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{q}_{X}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
are non-zero precisely in the range
\codim_{Y}X\leq q\leq\lcd(X,Y)
.

It follows from the main results in
[
Og
]
or
[
MP1
]
that
\dim Y-\lcd(X,Y)

depends only on
X
, and not the choice of
Y
; for instance, in the language of
[
Og
, Theorem 2.13]
, it is equal to the de Rham depth of
X
. This provides a convenient invariant for our present purposes.

Definition 2.1
.

The
local cohomological defect

{\rm lcdef}(X)
of
X
is

{\rm lcdef}(X):=\lcd(X,Y)-\codim_{Y}X.

It measures how far
X
is “numerically” from being a local complete intersection, and again depends only on
X
,
and not on the choice of
Y
; indeed, we have

{\rm lcdef}(X)=\dim X-\big(\dim Y-\lcd(X,Y)\big).

This also shows that
\dim X\geq{\rm lcdef}(X)\geq 0
.

The key result we use here is
[
MP1
, Theorem E]
, characterizing the local cohomological dimension in terms of the vanishing of higher direct images of forms with log poles on a log resolution. Via results of Steenbrink, this was equivalently phrased in terms of Du Bois complexes as follows:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
MP1
, Corollary 12.6]
)
.

Let
X
be a subvariety of a smooth variety
Y
. For any integer
c
we have

\lcd(X,Y)\leq c\iff\mathcal{E}xt^{j+k+1}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k},\omega_{Y})=0\,\,\,\,{\rm for~all~}j\geq c\,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,k\geq 0.

or equivalently

{\rm lcdef}(X)\leq c\iff\mathcal{E}xt^{j+k+1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k},\omega_{X}^{\bullet})=0\,\,\,\,{\rm for~all~}j\geq c-\dim X\,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,k\geq 0.

The second equivalent statement is simply obtained by translating into the language introduced here, and using Grothendieck duality for the inclusion
X\hookrightarrow Y
. The fundamental consequence is the following:

Corollary 2.3
.

We have the identity

{\rm lcdef}(X)=\dim X-\underset{k\geq 0}{\min}~\{\depth\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}+k\}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.2
, the identity follows from (
1.2
).
∎

This formula opens the door to the study of
{\rm lcdef}(X)
based on objects in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves, providing an alternative
to the topological approach in
[
Og
]
(or in
[
RSW
]
and
[
BBLSZ
]
).

Example 2.4

(
Varieties with
{\rm lcdef}(X)=0
).

An important point is to recognize those varieties that behave numerically like local complete intersections, without necessarily being so. Here this is encoded in
the condition
{\rm lcdef}(X)=0
, or equivalently
\lcd(X,Y)=\codim_{Y}X
in any embedding. Besides local complete intersections, this is known to hold for instance
when
X
has quotient singularities
[
MP1
, Corollary 11.22]
, when
X
is affine with Cohen-Macaulay Stanley-Reisner coordinate algebra

[
MP1
, Corollary 11.26]
, when
X
is an arbitrary Cohen-Macaulay surface or threefold by
[
Og
, Remark p.338-339]
and

[
DT
, Corollary 2.8]
respectively, and when
X
is a Cohen-Macaulay fourfold whose local analytic Picard groups are torsion
[
DT
, Theorem 1.3]
.

3.
Du Bois complexes of cones over singular varieties

The set-up for this entire section and the next is the following:
X
is a projective variety of dimension
n
, endowed with an ample line bundle
L
, and

Z=C(X,L)=\Spec\big(\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{m})\big)

is the affine cone over
X
associated to
L
, with cone point
x\in Z
.

Our first goal is to describe the Du Bois complexes
\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}
, generalizing the result obtained in
[
SVV
, Appendix A]
(and earlier in different language in
[
CHWW2
]
) when
X
is smooth. More precisely, noting that
Z
is affine, we describe the global sections of their cohomology sheaves. Our second goal is to use this description in order to compute their depth at the cone point. We combine all of this in the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

With the notation above:

(1) The Du Bois complexes
\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}
are given by

\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})\simeq\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})\simeq\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m}),

\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})\text{ for $i\geq 1$},

and for
k\geq 1
,

\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k})\cong\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m})\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1}\otimes L^{m}).

(2) We have
\depth_{x}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}\geq 1
for every
k\geq 0
. Moreover, if
d\geq 1
is an integer:

(i)
\depth_{x}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0}>d
if and only if

•

\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})=0
for all
m\leq-1
;

•

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})=0
for all
m\leq 0
and
1\leq i\leq d-1
.

(ii) If
k\geq 1
, then
\depth_{x}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}>d
if and only if

•

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m})=\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1}\otimes L^{m})=0
for
m\leq-1
and
0\leq i\leq d-1
;

•

\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k})=0
;

•

The map
\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1})\to\mathbb{H}^{i+1}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k})
is an isomorphism for
0\leq i\leq d-2
and injective for
i=d-1
.

In this section we focus on the proof of Theorem
3.1
(1), while (2) will be proved in the next.
To this end, we consider the blow-up square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\simeq X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{Z}}=\mathbf{Spec}_{X}\big(\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}L^{m}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

obtained by blowing up
Z
at the cone point. We denote

U:=Z\smallsetminus\{x\}=\widetilde{Z}\smallsetminus E.

Using Lemma
1.1
(4), we have the following exact triangles:

\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{\widetilde{Z}}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{x}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{0}\xrightarrow{+1}

and

\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{\widetilde{Z}}^{k}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k}\xrightarrow{+1}

for
k\geq 1
. Since
\widetilde{Z}
is an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
E\simeq X
, these exact triangles are split:

Lemma 3.2
.

With the notation above, for each
k\geq 0
there exists a “pullback” map

{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{\widetilde{Z}}

inverse to the natural restriction map
{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{\widetilde{Z}}\to{\bf R}f_{*}\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k}
.

As a consequence, after passing to hypercohomology, the exact triangles above lead to direct sum decompositions:

(3.3)

\mathbb{H}^{0}(\widetilde{Z},\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{\widetilde{Z}})\oplus\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{0}(Z,\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{0}(E,\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{0})

and

(3.4)

\mathbb{H}^{i}(\widetilde{Z},\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{\widetilde{Z}})\simeq\mathbb{H}^{i}(Z,\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{i}(E,\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k})

for
i\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
. Moreover, the summands
\mathbb{H}^{i}(E,\underline{\Omega}_{E}^{k})
can be identified with
\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k})
, via the zero section of
\pi
.
We next compute
\pi_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{\widetilde{Z}}
, via the projection
\pi\colon\widetilde{Z}\to X
.

Lemma 3.5
.

With the notation above, we have

\pi_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{\widetilde{Z}}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m},

and for each
k\geq 1
, a split exact triangle

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m}\to\pi_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{\widetilde{Z}}\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1}\otimes L^{m}\xrightarrow{+1}.

Proof.

Recall that
\widetilde{Z}=\mathbf{Spec}_{X}(\Sym L)
. Given a hyperresolution
\epsilon_{\bullet}:X_{\bullet}\to X
, it induces a hyperresolution
\widetilde{Z}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Spec}_{X_{\bullet}}(\Sym\epsilon_{\bullet}^{*}L)
of
\widetilde{Z}
. For each
\widetilde{Z_{i}}\to X_{i}
, we have

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{Z_{i}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}L^{m}

and for
k\geq 1
, a split exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\Omega_{X_{i}}^{p}\otimes\epsilon_{i}^{*}L^{m}\to\pi_{*}\Omega^{p}_{\widetilde{Z}_{i}}\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\Omega_{X_{i}}^{p-1}\otimes\epsilon_{i}^{*}L^{m}\to 0.

Since the splitting given by
d
is compatible with the maps in the hyperresolution, we get the result by pushing these forward to
X
, and using the projection formula.
∎

We are now ready to deduce the result stated at the beginning of the section:

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(1).

Putting together (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\mathbb{H}^{0}(Z,\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})\simeq\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

\mathbb{H}^{i}(Z,\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,\,i\geq 1,

and for each
i
and
k\geq 1
, a split exact sequence

0\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m})\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(Z,\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{Z})\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1}\otimes L^{m})\to 0.

Since
Z
is affine, we have
\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k})=\mathbb{H}^{i}(Z,\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k})
, from which the result is immediate.
∎

It is also worth noting the following quick consequence of Theorem
3.1
(1), presumably known to experts:

Corollary 3.6
.

The cone
Z=C(X,L)
is seminormal if and only if
X
is seminormal.

Proof.

It is well known that a variety
X
is seminormal if and only if the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{X}
is an isomorphism.

In our case, the cone
Z
is seminormal if and only if

\varphi:\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{m})\to\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0})=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

is an isomorphism. Hence it is clear that if
X
is seminormal, then so is
Z
.

Conversely, suppose
Z
is seminormal. Since the map
\varphi
preserves the grading given by
L
, we have isomorphisms

H^{0}(X,L^{m})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

for all
m\geq 1
, induced by the inclusion of sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}
; using Serre’s theorems, it is then straightforward to check
that this inclusion has to be an isomorphism.
∎

Finally, when
L
is very ample, one can also consider the classical cone
C(X)
over the corresponding embedding in projective space.
For future use, we record the following result that relates
C(X,L)
to
C(X)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
is very ample, defining an embedding

X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then for each
k
,

\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{C(X)}\to{\bf R}\pi_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{C(X,L)}

is a quasi-isomorphism, where
\pi
is the natural map
C(X,L)\to C(X)
. Consequently,
\lcdef(C(X))=\lcdef(C(X,L))
.

Proof.

The isomorphism
\underline{\Omega}_{C(X)}^{k}\to{\bf R}\pi_{*}\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{C(X,L)}
follows from Lemma
1.1
(4), given that
\pi
is an isomorphism away from the vertex points. The last assertion then follows from Corollary
2.3
.

∎

4.
Depth of Du Bois complexes of cones

In this section we focus on the proof of Theorem
3.1
(2). In other words, we characterize the depth of
\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{k}
, where
Z
is the cone over a possibly singular projective variety

, in terms of cohomological data of the Du Bois complexes of

.

To do so, still using the notation of the previous section, we must first also understand the Du Bois complexes of the complement
U=Z\smallsetminus\{x\}
of the vertex of the cone. Note first that
a completely similar argument as in Lemma

gives:

Lemma 4.1
.

For

we have

and for each

Passing to hypercohomology, we obtain

\mathbb{H}^{i}(U,\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{U})\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

for

\cdots\to\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{i}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m})\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(U,\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{U})\to\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{i}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1}\otimes L^{m})\xrightarrow{d_{i}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{H}^{i+1}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes L^{m})\to\cdots

.

The differentials
d_{i}
in the long exact sequence above are zero, except on the

summand, where

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k-1})\xrightarrow{d_{i}}\mathbb{H}^{i+1}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k})

on each term
X_{j}
in a hyperresolution of

Proof.

For each

, completely analogously to Lemma

It suffices therefore to show the statement of the Lemma when

is smooth, meaning that in this case

To this end, let

Proof of Theorem

Recall that we denote

Remark 4.5

(

We make some remarks about the content of Theorem

5.
Vanishing for dual Du Bois complexes

The Du Bois complexes of a projective variety
X
of dimension
n
are known by
[
GNPP
, Theorem V.5.1]
to satisfy the analogue of the Kodaira-Akizuki-Nakano vanishing theorem,
in the sense that

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\otimes L)=0\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm for~all}\,\,\,\,p+q>n,

for any ample line bundle
L
on
X
. The Serre dual statement for twists by
L^{-1}
does not however hold automatically, as in general we only have
morphisms
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{n-p}\to\mathbb{D}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p})
that are not necessarily isomorphisms, where

\mathbb{D}(-)={\bf R}\mathcal{H}om(-,\omega_{X}^{\bullet})[-n]

is the (shifted) Grothendieck duality functor. Instead, the correct statement turns out to be the following, which is an expanded version of Theorem
C
in the Introduction.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and
L
an ample line bundle on
X
. Then

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\mathbb{D}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p})\otimes L)=0\quad\text{ for }\,\,\,\,q-p>\lcdef(X).

Equivalently,

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\otimes L^{-1})=0\quad\text{ for }\,\,\,\,p+q<n-\lcdef(X).

Proof.

The second statement follows from the first by Grothendieck-Serre duality.

To check the first statement, fix an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
given by a sufficiently high power of
L
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N]
denote the trivial Hodge module on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then, as in
[
MP1
, Proposition 13.1]
, the Du Bois complexes satisfies

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\simeq{\bf R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\Gr^{F}_{p-N}\DR_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N],\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\bullet})[p-N]

Dualizing, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p})

\displaystyle={\bf R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p},\omega_{X}^{\bullet})[-n]

\displaystyle\cong{\bf R}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p},\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{\bullet})[-n]

\displaystyle\cong(\Gr^{F}_{p-N}\DR_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N])[N-p-n].

Consider now the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\Gr^{F}_{p-N}\DR_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N])\otimes L)\implies

\implies\mathbb{H}^{i+j}(X,\Gr^{F}_{p-N}\DR_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{Y}^{H}[N])\otimes L).

We have that

•

\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N])=\mathcal{H}^{j}_{X}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}=0
for
j>\lcd(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
;

•

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\Gr^{F}_{p-N}\DR_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\mathcal{H}^{j}(i_{*}i^{!}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}^{H}[N])\otimes L)
=0 for
i>0
, by Saito’s vanishing theorem
for mixed Hodge modules
[
Sa
, Scn.2.g]
.

It follows that
E_{2}^{i,j}=0
if
i+j>\lcd(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
. We conclude that

\mathbb{H}^{q}(X,\mathbb{D}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p})\otimes L)=0

when
q-p+N-n>\lcd(X,\mathbb{P}^{N})
, or equivalently
q-p>{\rm lcdef}(X)
, as required.
∎

j
.
Thus (1) follows from (2) by Corollary
7.1
. Conversely, suppose first that
X
does not have pre-
p
-Du Bois singularities, meaning

\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\neq 0
for some
i>0
and
j\leq p
. By Serre’s theorems, we can choose
L
sufficiently positive, so that
H^{s}(X,\mathcal{H}^{t}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)=0
for all
s>0
and
t\geq 0
, and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)\neq 0
. Then, the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{s,t}=H^{s}(X,\mathcal{H}^{t}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)\implies\mathbb{H}^{s+t}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)

degenerates, and gives

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)\simeq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}\otimes L)\neq 0.

This is a contradiction, again by Corollary
7.1
, which also implies that
X
satisfies Bott vanishing once we know
that it is pre-
p
-Du Bois.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

For varieties whose singularities are not rational, it may in fact make sense to define the Bott vanishing condition in terms of
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{j}
rather than

\Omega_{X}^{[j]}
. The statement of the Corollary, minus the rational singularities assumption, would remain unchanged.

For example, one can show along the lines of
[
KT
]
that if
X
is a variety with int-amplified endomorphisms, then for every ample line bundle
A
on
X
, we have

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{H}^{j}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\otimes A)=0

for
i>0
and
j,k\geq 0
.

8.
K
-groups of cones

Another consequence of Theorem
3.1
is the computation of the non-positive
K
-groups of abstract cones over a projective variety in terms of its Du Bois complexes, following the approach in
[
CHWW2
]
for classical cones over smooth varieties, and using the main technical result of that paper. Please see the introduction of
loc. cit.
for a discussion of the problem, and of the important techniques introduced by the authors in a series of papers dedicated to the study of
K
-groups.

As in
[
CHWW1
]
, for any subfield
F\subset\mathbb{C}
and complex variety
X
, one can define the
p
-th Du Bois complex of
X
over
F
as

\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}={\bf R}\epsilon_{\bullet*}\Omega^{p}_{X_{\bullet}/F}

where
\epsilon_{\bullet}:X_{\bullet}\to X
is a hyperresolution of
X
. The formula for the Du Bois complexes of cones in Theorem
3.1
remains valid in this setting, with the same proof where
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
is replaced by
\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}
. Moreover,
one checks exactly as in the slightly more restrictive
[
CHWW1
, Lemma 2.1]
that there is an isomorphism

\underline{\Omega}_{X/F}^{p}\simeq\Omega^{p}_{cdh,X/F},

where
\Omega^{p}_{cdh,X/F}:={\bf R}a_{*}a^{*}\Omega^{p}_{X/F}
is the sheafification of
\Omega_{X/F}^{p}
in the cdh-topology, the map
a:X_{cdh}\to X_{zar}
being the natural change-of-topology morphism.

We are now ready to describe the calculation of non-positive
K
-groups of cones.

Proof of Corollary
D
.

By
[
CHWW2
, Thereom 1.2]
, for the affine cone
Z=C(X,L)
over the
n
-dimensional projective variety
X
, we have

•

K_{0}(Z)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\dim Z-1}\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i})/d\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1})\big)
;

•

K_{-\ell}(Z)\simeq\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{\ell}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{0})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-\ell}\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{\ell+i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i})/d\big(\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{\ell+i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1})\big)
,
\ell\geq 1
.

Here
d
is the action on global sections of the differential
\mathcal{H}^{\ell+i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1}\to\mathcal{H}^{\ell+i}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}
.

Using the calculation of Du Bois complexes of abstract cones in Theorem
3.1
(1) (in place of

[
CHWW2
, Corollary 2.10]
) then yields

•

K_{0}(Z)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m})
;

•

K_{-\ell}(Z)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-\ell}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{\ell+i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m})
,
\ell\geq 1
.

Note that if
X
is embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, one can consider the classical cone
C(X)
, and the method in

loc. cit.
applies to this as well. Thus for
\ell\geq 1
we have

K_{-\ell}(C(X))=K_{-\ell}(C(X,L))

thanks to Lemma
3.7
. For
\ell=0
, we have

K_{0}(C(X))\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\Pic(C(X))\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i}\otimes L^{m})

where
\Pic(C(X))\simeq R^{+}/R
; here
R
is the homogeneous coordinate ring of
X
, and

R^{+}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{H}^{0}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0}\otimes L^{m})

is its seminormalization. The last statement follows from Theorem
3.1
(1) and the well-known fact that the structure sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\rm sn}}
of the seminormalization of
Z
can be identified with
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{0}
.
For the statement about
\Pic(C(X))
we are using
[
CHWW2
, Proposition 1.5]
.
∎

Remark 8.1
.

As a consequence,
K_{-\ell}(Z)=0
for
l>n+1
, where as usual
n=\dim X
. This vanishing result holds in greater generality, see
[
CHSW
, Theorem 6.2]
and
[
KST
, Theorem B]
. Moreover, if
X
is a complex algebraic variety with Du Bois singularities, then

K_{-n-1}(Z)\simeq\bigoplus_{m=1}^{\infty}H^{n}(X,L^{m}),

as in the case when
X
is smooth. More generally, if the complex algebraic variety
X
has pre-
p
-Du Bois singularities (see the previous section), the formula for
K_{-\ell}(Z)
essentially coincides with that in the smooth case for
\ell\geq n-p
, since in this case we have that
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{k}\simeq\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{k}
for
k\leq p
;
indeed, this is the case by definition for
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}=\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{C}}^{k}
, and the statement over
\mathbb{Q}
follows inductively from this via exact triangles of the type

\Omega_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q}}^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p-1}\to\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{p}\to\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X/\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{+1}

obtained from the corresponding exact sequences at the level of Kähler differentials on a hyperresolution. Note that when
X
has rational singularities we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\simeq\Omega_{X}^{[k]}
for all
k
, while if
X
is a
p
-Du Bois local complete intersection, then
\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k}\simeq\Omega_{X}^{k}
for
k\leq p
; see
[
MP1
]
for a general discussion.

Remark 8.2

(
Higher
K
-groups).

With the same notation as above,
according to
[
CHWW2
, Theorem 1.13]
, for
l\geq 1
the group
K_{l}(Z)
breaks up into a direct sum of eigenspaces of the Adams operator, given by:

•

K_{l}^{(i)}(Z)\cong HC_{l-1}^{(i-1)}(R)
for
0<i<l
;

•

K_{l}^{(l)}(Z)\cong K_{l}^{(l)}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\tors\Omega_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{l-1}/d\tors\Omega_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{l-2}
;

•

K_{l}^{(l+1)}(Z)\cong\coker(\Gamma(Z,\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{l-1})\xrightarrow{d}\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{0}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{l}/\Omega_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{l}))
;

•

K_{l}^{(i)}(Z)\cong\coker(\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{i-l-1}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-2})\xrightarrow{d}\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{i-l-1}\underline{\Omega}_{Z/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1}))
for
i\geq l+2
.

For
i\geq l+2
, Theorem
3.1
(1) and a similar argument as in the computation of non-positive
K
-groups gives

K_{l}^{(i)}(Z)\simeq\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{H}^{i-l-1}(X,\underline{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Q}}^{i-1}\otimes L^{m}).

Still using Theorem
3.1
, further explicit calculations can be performed for special choices of
X
; these will appear later.

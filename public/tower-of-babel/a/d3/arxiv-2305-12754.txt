q q -difference equations satisfied by the universal mock theta functions
Satoshi Tsuchimi

Abstract
In this paper, we give fundamental solutions of some q q -difference equations satisfied by the universal mock theta functions and the higher level Appell functions. As an application, we provide an alternative proof of the representation formulas of the universal mock theta functions and the higher level Appell functions using Zwegers’ μ \mu -function.



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
let
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a complex number
|q|<1
. We define the
q
-shifted factorials and Jacobi’s theta function as follows:

\displaystyle(x)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(x;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{j}),\quad(x)_{\nu}=(x;q)_{\nu}:=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\nu}x;q)_{\infty}}\quad(\nu\in\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle\theta_{q}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}=(q,-x,-q/x;q)_{\infty}.

From a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle x^{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\theta_{q}(xq^{n})=\theta_{q}(x)\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z}).

(1)

For appropriate complex numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},b_{1},\ldots,b_{s},x
, we define the
q
-hypergeometric series as follows:

\displaystyle{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix};q,x\right):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{n}}{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{s},q)_{n}}\left((-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\right)^{s-r+1}x^{n},

where
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})_{\nu}:=(a_{1})_{\nu}\cdots(a_{r})_{\nu}
.

Mock theta functions first appeared in Ramanujan’s last letter to Hardy in 1920.
Ramanujan presented several identities for the mock theta functions, later these identities implied that all mock theta functions are expressed as specializations of the universal mock theta functions

\displaystyle g_{2}(x;q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)_{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(x,x^{-1}q)_{n+1}},

(2)

\displaystyle g_{3}(x;q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n+1)}}{(x,x^{-1}q)_{n+1}}.

(3)

Namely, the universal mock theta functions are kind of master functions of the mock theta functions.

In
2009
, Kang presented a following expression for the universal mock theta functions using Zwegers’
\mu
-function
[
Ka
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 3.1]
.

\displaystyle g_{2}(x;q)

\displaystyle=-iq^{-\frac{1}{4}}\mu(x^{2},q;q^{2})+\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{4}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}\theta_{q^{2}}(-x^{2})},

\displaystyle g_{3}(x;q)

\displaystyle=-ix^{-\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\frac{1}{8}}\mu(x^{3},q;q^{2})-ix^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\frac{5}{8}}\mu(x^{3},q^{2};q^{3})+\frac{(q^{3};q^{3})_{\infty}^{3}}{(q;q)_{\infty}\theta_{q^{3}}(-x^{3})}.

(4)

Here the
\mu
-function is defined by Zwegers as follows
[
Zw1
]
:

\displaystyle\mu(x,y;q)=\mu(x,y):=iq^{-\frac{1}{8}}\frac{\sqrt{xy}}{\theta_{q}(-y)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}y^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{1-xq^{n}},

(5)

where
x,y\neq q^{k},(k\in\mathbb{Z})
.
More generally, the universal mock theta functions are a special class of the higher level Appell functions

\displaystyle A_{m}(x,y):=x^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{mn}y^{n}q^{\frac{mn(n+1)}{2}}}{1-xq^{n}}

introduced by Kac-Wakimoto
[
KW
]
. From a simple calculation, we have

A_{1}(x,y)=-iq^{\frac{1}{8}}\frac{\theta_{q}(-y)}{\sqrt{y}}\mu(x,y).

Zwegers showed the following expression of the function
A_{m}(x,y)
by the
\mu
-function
[
Zw2
, Lemma 2]
:

\displaystyle A_{m}(x,(-1)^{m-1}y)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}-iq^{\frac{m}{8}}x^{k}\frac{\theta_{q^{m}}(-yq^{k})}{\sqrt{yq^{k}}}\mu(x^{m},yq^{k};q^{m}).

(6)

The
\mu
-function has many rich properties. As a particularly important property, the
\mu
-function satisfies a transformation law like Jacobi forms by adding an appropriate non-holomorphic function to the
\mu
-function.
This result was a starting point in the study of mock modular forms.

Recently, there are some works in
[
GW
]
and
[
ST
]
to understand Zwegers’
\mu
-function in terms of
q
-special functions.
In the paper
[
ST
]
, we studied the
q
-Hermite-Weber equation

\displaystyle[T_{x}^{2}-(1-xq)\sqrt{a}T_{x}-xq]f(x)=0.

(7)

In particular, when
a=q
, the
q
-Hermite-Weber equation has the
\mu
-function as a solution. We also showed that a solution for general
\alpha
has the same rich symmetry as the
\mu
-function.
In this paper, we describe that the
\mu
-function appears as a fundamental solution of higher-order
q
-difference equation
.

First we present a theorem for the following higher order
q
-difference equation

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\alpha_{k}})\right](T_{x}+xq^{\alpha})f(x)=0,

(8)

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{m-1}
are different complex parameters.
When
m=2,\alpha=\alpha_{1}=1/2
, this
q
-difference equation corresponds with the
q
-Hermite-Weber equation in the case of
a=q

\displaystyle\left[T_{x}^{2}-(1-xq)\sqrt{q}T_{x}-xq\right]f(x)=\left(T_{x}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(T_{x}+xq^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)f(x).

Theorem 1
.

The
q
-difference equation
(\ref{linear eq})
has the following fundamental solutions:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-xq^{\alpha})},\quad x^{\alpha+\alpha_{j}-\frac{1}{2}}\mu(x\lambda q^{\alpha},\lambda q^{\alpha_{j}};q)\quad(j=1,\ldots,m-1).

(9)

Note that the theta function
1/\theta_{q}(-x)
is a solution of a first order
q
-difference equation
(T_{x}+x)f(x)=0
, and the
\mu
-function
x^{\alpha_{j}-\frac{1}{2}}\mu(x\lambda,\lambda q^{\alpha_{j}};q)
is a solution of a second order factorized
q
-difference equation
(T_{x}-q^{\alpha_{j}})(T_{x}+x)f(x)=0
.
From this result, we see that the
\mu
-function is a “mock version” of Jacobi’s theta function.
In particular, we see that the
\mu
-function is a fundamental and important function in the
q
-difference equation, since the
\mu
-function naturally appears as a solution of higher order factorized
q
-difference equation from Theorem
1
.

Next, we study the
q
-difference equations satisfied by the higher level Appell functions.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G_{m}(x,y):=x^{-\frac{m}{2}}A_{m}(x,(-1)^{m-1}y)
. The functions
G_{m}
satisfy the following
m+1
-th order homogeneous
q
-difference equations:

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m}(T_{x}-q^{k-1})\right](T_{x}+x^{m}y^{-1})G_{m}(x,y)=0.

(10)

Using Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, we show that the higher level Appell functions are expressed in terms of the
\mu
-function, and give an alternative proof of the equations (
6
).
With this proof, we provide a clear answer through the
q
-difference equations to the question why the higher level Appell functions and the universal mock theta functions are represented by the
\mu
-function (cf. Remark
3
).

This paper is organized as follows. First, in Section
2
, we present a lemma to show the main results. Next, in Section
3
, we prove the main results Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
. As an application of the main results, we give another proof of the equation (
6
) from the view point of
q
-difference equations.

2
A lemma for the proof of main results

We define the
q
-Borel and
q
-Laplace transforms
\mathcal{B}^{\pm},\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
for a formal power series
f(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{C}\llbracket x\rrbracket
following
[
RSZ
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(f)(\xi):=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}q^{\pm\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\xi^{n},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{+}(f)(x,\lambda):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{f(\lambda q^{n})}{\theta_{q}(x^{-1}\lambda q^{n})},\quad\mathcal{L}^{-}(f)(x):=\int_{|\xi|=r}f(\xi)\theta_{q}(x/\xi)\frac{d\xi}{\xi}.

From a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(x^{m}T_{x}^{n}f)=q^{\pm\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\xi^{m}T_{\xi}^{n\pm m}\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(f),\quad\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(\xi^{m}T_{\xi}^{n}f)=q^{\mp\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}x^{m}T_{x}^{n\mp m}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(f),

(11)

hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(x^{m}T_{x}^{n}f)=x^{m}T_{x}^{n}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\pm}(f).

The Newton-Puiseux diagram of the
q
-difference equation

\displaystyle a_{n}(x)f(xq^{n})+a_{n-1}(x)f(xq^{n-1})+\cdots+a_{0}(x)f(x)=0

is defined as the convex hull of

\displaystyle\{(k,l)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\text{the coefficient of $x^{k}$ in $a_{l}(x)$ is not $0$},1\leq l\leq n\}.

First, we compute the solutions around
x=0
and
x=\infty
of the following
q
-difference equation:

\displaystyle\left[T_{x}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\alpha_{k}})+x\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\beta_{k}})\right]f(x)=0.

(12)

The Newton-Puiseux diagram of this
q
-difference equation is as follows:

Lemma 1
.

The
m
-th order linear
q
-difference equation
(\ref{diver eq})

has formal solutions

\displaystyle x^{\alpha_{j}}{}_{m}\phi_{m-2}\left(\begin{matrix}q^{\alpha_{j}-\beta_{1}},\ldots,q^{\alpha_{j}-\beta_{m-1}},0\\
q^{\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1}+1},\ldots,q^{\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{m-1}+1}\end{matrix};q,\frac{b_{1}}{a_{1}}xq^{-\alpha_{j}-1}\right)\quad(j=1,\ldots,m-1)

(13)

around
x=0
,

\displaystyle x^{\beta_{j}}{}_{m}\phi_{m-2}\left(\begin{matrix}q^{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{j}},\ldots,q^{\alpha_{m-1}-\beta_{j}},0\\
q^{\beta_{1}-\beta_{j}+1},\ldots,q^{\beta_{m-1}-\beta_{j}+1}\end{matrix};q,\frac{q^{m-1+\beta_{j}}}{x}\right)\quad(j=1,\ldots m-1)

(14)

around
x=\infty
, and convergent solutions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}x)}\int_{|\xi|=r}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{1-\alpha_{k}})_{\infty}}{(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\beta_{k}})_{\infty}}\theta_{q}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)\frac{d\xi}{2\pi i\xi},

(15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-xq^{-m})}\int_{|\xi|=r}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{(-q^{m+\beta_{k}+1}/\xi)_{\infty}}{(-q^{m+\alpha_{k}}/\xi)_{\infty}}\theta_{q}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)\frac{d\xi}{2\pi i\xi}.

(16)

Proof.

The formal solutions are obtained by direct calculations. We derive the convergent solution. Define coefficients
a_{k},b_{k}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}z^{k}=z\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(z-q^{\alpha_{k}}),\quad\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}z^{k}=z\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(z-q^{\beta_{k}}).

From the equations (
1
) and (
11
), we have

\displaystyle x^{m}T_{x}^{n}\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(cx)}\mathcal{L}^{-}(g(\xi))(x)

\displaystyle=c^{n}q^{-\frac{m(m+2n-1)}{2}}\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(cx)}\mathcal{L}^{-}(\xi^{m+n}T_{\xi}^{-m}g(\xi)),

hence we obtain

\displaystyle\left[T_{x}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\alpha_{k}})+x\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\beta_{k}})\right]\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}x)}\mathcal{L}^{-}(g(\xi)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}T_{x}^{k}+x\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}T_{x}^{k-1}\right]\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}x)}\mathcal{L}^{-}(g(\xi))

\displaystyle=-\frac{b_{1}}{\theta_{q}(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}x)}\mathcal{L}^{-}\left(\xi\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\alpha_{k}})-\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\beta_{k}-1})T_{\xi}^{-1}\right]g(\xi)\right).

Since the
q
-difference equation

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\alpha_{k}})-\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\beta_{k}-1})T_{\xi}^{-1}\right]g(\xi)=0

has a solution

\displaystyle g(\xi)=\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{1-\alpha_{k}})_{\infty}}{(-a_{1}^{-1}b_{1}\xi q^{-\beta_{k}})_{\infty}}

around
\xi=0
, we obtain the convergent solution (
15
).
By similar calculations, we also have

\displaystyle\left[T_{x}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\alpha_{k}})+x\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(T_{x}-q^{\beta_{k}})\right]\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-xq^{-m})}\mathcal{L}^{-}(g(\xi))(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}T_{x}^{k}+xb_{k}T_{x}^{k-1}\right]\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-xq^{-m})}\mathcal{L}^{-}(g(\xi))(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{-m(m+1)}}{\theta_{q}(-xq^{-m})}\mathcal{L}^{-}\left(\xi^{m+1}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+q^{m+\alpha_{k}}/\xi)-\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+q^{m+\beta_{k}+1}/\xi)T_{x}^{-1}\right]g(\xi)\right).

The

-difference equation

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+q^{m+\alpha_{k}}/\xi)-\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}(1+q^{m+\beta_{k}+1}/\xi)T_{x}^{-1}\right]g(\xi)=0

has a solution

\displaystyle g(\xi)=\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{(-q^{m+\beta_{k}+1}/\xi)_{\infty}}{(-q^{m+\alpha_{k}}/\xi)_{\infty}}

around
x=\infty
. Hence we obtain the convergent solution (
16
).
∎

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{q}(-x)},\quad x^{\alpha_{j}}{}_{2}\phi_{0}\left(\begin{matrix}q,0\\

Lemma 2
.

Let

(19)

Proof.

The result is obtained by the following calculation.

\displaystyle G_{m}(xq,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}y^{n-1}q^{\frac{mn(n-1)}{2}}}{1-xq^{n}}(x^{m}q^{mn}+1-x^{m}q^{mn})

\displaystyle=-\frac{x^{m}}{y}G_{m}(x,y)+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-y)^{n-1}q^{\frac{mn(n-1)}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x^{k}q^{kn}

\displaystyle=-\frac{x^{m}}{y}G_{m}(x,y)-\frac{1}{y}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x^{k}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(-yq^{k}\right)^{n}q^{\frac{mn(n-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{x^{m}}{y}G_{m}(x,y)-\frac{1}{y}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x^{k}\theta_{q^{m}}(-yq^{k}).

∎

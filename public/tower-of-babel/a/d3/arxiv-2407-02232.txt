Efficient Extrinsic Self-Calibration of Multiple IMUs using Measurement Subset Selection
Jongwon Lee, David Hanley, Timothy Bretl

Abstract
This paper addresses the problem of choosing a sparse subset of measurements for quick calibration parameter estimation. A standard solution to this is selecting a measurement only if its utility—the difference between posterior (with the measurement) and prior information (without the measurement)—exceeds some threshold. Theoretically, utility, a function of the parameter estimate, should be evaluated at the estimate obtained with all measurements selected so far, hence necessitating a recalibration with each new measurement. However, we hypothesize that utility is insensitive to changes in the parameter estimate for many systems of interest, suggesting that evaluating utility at some initial parameter guess would yield equivalent results in practice. We provide evidence supporting this hypothesis for extrinsic calibration of multiple inertial measurement units (IMUs), showing the reduction in calibration time by two orders of magnitude by forgoing recalibration for each measurement.

Inertial measurement units (IMUs) are a common component in mobile robot navigation, comprising accelerometers and gyroscopes to measure specific forces and angular rates along three orthogonal axes. Self-calibration, not bound to a predetermined trajectory, naturally experiences measurements with varying “utility”.
In cases like calibrating multi-IMU systems on non-rotating bodies such as spacecraft in orbit or cars on straight paths, much of the data can be low in utility.
This is especially critical for optimization-based calibration, favored for its accuracy compared to filter-based methods, as it involves processing a significant amount of low-utility (uninformative) data alongside with high-utility (informative) data during optimization.
Therefore, identifying subsets of measurements with high utility would offer benefits such as reduced runtime and memory usage for optimization.

However, this remains beneficial only if the selection process is done efficiently.

In this study, we present an approach to multi-IMU extrinsic calibration by selecting informative measurement subsets with high utility, significantly streamlining our previous work

This selection is primarily based on method from existing studies

Specifically, we hypothesize that the measure of utility is insensitive to changes in the parameter estimate and equivalent calibration results are obtained by evaluating utility only at some initial guess of the calibration parameters. A valuable consequence of this is eliminating the need for recalibration with each new measurement, thereby reducing total computation time for calibration.

III-A

Original Greedy Algorithm

We provide details of Alg.
1
here.
Upon receiving new measurements
\mathcal{D}^{new}
, the algorithm obtains the calibration parameter estimate

by minimizing the sum of squared residuals associated with both
\mathcal{D}^{info}
and
\mathcal{D}^{new}
, starting from an initial estimate
\mathbf{\bm{\widehat{\theta}}}^{-}
(Line 6).
Next, the Jacobian — stacks of derivatives of the residuals (see Section

) with respect to the estimated parameters

The residuals corresponding to both
\mathcal{D}^{info}
and
\mathcal{D}^{new}

\mathbf{\bm{\widehat{\theta}}}^{+}

Then, the Fisher information matrix

\mathbf{\bm{\Theta}}
from the inverse of

If the utility of

\mathcal{D}^{info}
, exceeds

, then
\mathcal{D}^{new}

Alg.
2

IV-B

Inertial Sensor Model

We model accelerometer measurements from each IMU as

^{I}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}={}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{a}}_{WI}-{}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{g}}+\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}+\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{a}

(1)

where

•

{}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{a}}_{WI}
is the IMU’s linear acceleration relative to the world frame, expressed in the IMU frame,

•

{}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{g}}
is gravitational acceleration in the IMU frame,

•

\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}
is a time-varying bias modeled by a random walk

\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a,k+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a,k}\sim\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}\sqrt{\Delta t}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{\bm{0}},\,\mathbf{\bm{1}}\right),

•

and
\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{a}
is stochastic noise

\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{a}\sim\sigma_{a}/\sqrt{\Delta t}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{\bm{0}},\,\mathbf{\bm{1}}\right)

with
\Delta t
as the IMU’s sampling interval.
We model gyroscope measurements from each IMU as

^{g}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big({}^{g}_{I}\mathbf{\bm{q}}\big){}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}_{WI}+\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}+\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{g}

(2)

where

•

\mathbf{\bm{C}}(^{g}_{I}\mathbf{\bm{q}})
is the rotation matrix (written in terms of the corresponding quaternion) that models the IMU frame’s orientation relative to the gyroscope frame, accounting for gyroscope misalignment,

•

{}^{I}\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}_{WI}
is the IMU’s angular velocity relative to the world frame, expressed in the IMU frame,

•

\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}
is a time-varying bias modeled a random walk

\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g,k+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g,k}\sim\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}\sqrt{\Delta t}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{\bm{0}},\,\mathbf{\bm{1}}\right),

•

and
\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{g}
is stochastic noise

\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{g}\sim\sigma_{g}/\sqrt{\Delta t}\cdot\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{\bm{0}},\,\mathbf{\bm{1}}\right).

We assume that noise densities
\sigma_{a}
,
\sigma_{g}
and bias instabilities
\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}
,
\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}
in continuous-time are identified for all sensors before extrinsic calibration.
Additionally, we assume intrinsic parameters like scale factors and axis non-orthogonality are calibrated, and all IMUs are temporally synchronized.

IV-D

Relative Orientation Initialization

Prior to subset selection in Alg.
2
, we initialize each IMU’s relative orientation to the base IMU,
{}^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}
, using Yang
et al.
’s method
[
20
]
.
This preliminary step enhances Alg.
2
’s validity by providing plausible IMU orientation guesses.

Assuming angular velocity
\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}
remains constant between
t_{k}
and
t_{k+1}
, with
\Delta t=t_{k+1}-t_{k}
, the orientation change in the world frame expressed in the IMU frame is

{}^{I}_{W}\mathbf{\bm{q}}(t_{k+1})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}\sin\left(\frac{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}{2}\Delta t\right)\\
\cos\left(\frac{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}{2}\Delta t\right)\end{bmatrix}\;\otimes\;{}^{I}_{W}\mathbf{\bm{q}}(t_{k}).

Hence, the rotation from
\mathcal{F}_{I(t_{k})}
to
\mathcal{F}_{I(t_{k+1})}
is

{}^{I(t_{k+1})}_{I(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}\sin\left(\frac{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}{2}\Delta t\right)\\
\cos\left(\frac{|\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}|}{2}\Delta t\right)\end{bmatrix}.

Supposing that we have
{}^{I_{0}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{0}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}}
and
{}^{I_{n}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{n}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}}
from
{}^{I_{0}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}
and
{}^{I_{n}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}
respectively, the following relationship holds:

{}^{I_{0}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{0}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}}\,\otimes\,^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}\;=\;^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}\,\otimes\,^{I_{n}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{n}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}}.

Or equivalently,

\mathcal{L}(^{I_{0}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{0}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}})\,\cdot\,^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}=\mathcal{R}(^{I_{n}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{n}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}})\,\cdot\,^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}q_{w}\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3}+\lfloor\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}\rfloor&\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}\\
-\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}^{T}&q_{w}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}q_{w}\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3}-\lfloor\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}\rfloor&\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}\\
-\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{xyz}^{T}&q_{w}\end{bmatrix}.

Rearranging the equation yields

\left(\mathcal{L}(^{I_{0}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{0}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}})-\mathcal{R}(^{I_{n}(t_{k+1})}_{I_{n}(t_{k})}\mathbf{\bm{q}})\right)\,\cdot\,^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}=\mathbf{\bm{0}}_{4\times 1}.

Stacking the difference of 4-by-4 matrices
\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{R}
for various timesteps

\mathbf{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4k\times 4}
, leading to
\mathbf{\bm{A}}\,\cdot\,^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}=\mathbf{\bm{0}}_{4\times 1}
. We compute
{}^{I_{0}}_{I_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{q}}
by finding the right-singular vector of
\mathbf{\bm{A}}
with the smallest singular value.

\displaystyle{}^{I_{0}}\widehat{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}_{k}=\;

\displaystyle\lfloor{}^{I_{n}}_{g_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}(^{g_{n}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},k})\rfloor^{2}\,\mathbf{\bm{p}}_{I_{n}}+\lfloor{}^{I_{n}}_{I_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}^{I_{0}}{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}_{k}\rfloor\,\mathbf{\bm{p}}_{I_{n}}\Big\},

where
{}^{I_{n}}_{I_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big(\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}}\big)
,
{}^{I_{n}}_{g_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big(\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{g_{n}}\big)
, and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
mapping a cross product into skew-symmetric matrix form. Residual
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}
relates the
n
th gyroscope’s measurement (
{}^{I_{n}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}
) to that of base gyroscope’s (
{}^{I_{0}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}
) corrected for biases
\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},k}
,
\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{0},k}
:

\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}={}^{I_{n}}_{I_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}^{-1}{}^{I_{n}}_{g_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}({}^{g_{n}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},k})-{}^{I_{0}}_{g_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}({}^{g_{0}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{0},k}),

where
{}^{I_{n}}_{I_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big(\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}}\big)
,
{}^{I_{n}}_{g_{n}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big(\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{g_{n}}\big)
, and
{}^{I_{0}}_{g_{0}}\mathbf{\bm{R}}=\mathbf{\bm{C}}\big(\mathbf{\bm{q}}_{g_{0}}\big)
. This relation holds as rotational motion is uniform across all points of a rigid body.
Sensor measurements corrected for bias introduce uncertainty due to zero-mean Gaussian white noise, as shown by
\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}=\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{a}
from Eq. (
1
) and
\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}=\mathbf{\bm{n}}_{g}
from Eq. (
2
), leading to accelerometer and gyroscope covariance matrices
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}}=({\sigma_{a}}^{2}/\Delta t)\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
and
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}}=({\sigma_{g}}^{2}/\Delta t)\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
, respectively.
Additional uncertainties from
{}^{I_{0}}\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}_{k}^{2}
and
{}^{I_{0}}{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}_{k}
introduced by
{}^{I_{0}}\widehat{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}_{k}
lead to covariance matrices
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}^{2}}=2\cdot\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}}^{2}
and
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}=\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
, respectively.
Consequently, the covariance matrix for
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{a}
is
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{a}}=2\cdot\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{a}}}}+2\cdot\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}}^{2}+\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{\alpha}}}
and that for
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}
is
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}=2\cdot\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{\bm{\omega}}}}
.
Residuals
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}
and
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}
account for bias evolution:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a_{n},k+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a_{n},k}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},k+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},k}.

The covariance matrices for these residuals are
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}={\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}}^{2}\Delta t\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
and
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}={\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}}^{2}\Delta t\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
, respectively.

IV-F

Solution Approach

Upon identifying high-utility measurements, we formulate a nonlinear least-square problem using the chosen measurement subset. Beyond the previously mentioned residuals in Section
IV-E
, we incorporate additional residuals,
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}^{\ast}
and
\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}^{\ast}
, to bridge temporal gaps between adjacent selected segments, denoted as
\mathcal{D}^{info}=\{\mathcal{D}^{m}|m\in\mathcal{M}\}
with
\mathcal{M}=\{\mathcal{M}_{1},\dotsc,\mathcal{M}_{M}\}\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,L\}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a_{n},(\mathcal{M}_{m+1}-1)\cdot K+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a_{n},\mathcal{M}_{m}\cdot K}

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},(\mathcal{M}_{m+1}-1)\cdot K+1}-\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g_{n},\mathcal{M}_{m}\cdot K}.

These additional residuals aim to maintain bias consistency across segments, with their covariance matrices
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}^{\ast}
and
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}^{\ast}
modeled after a random walk in discrete time as earlier discussed, resulting in
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}^{\ast}={\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}}^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{M}_{m+1}-\mathcal{M}_{m})K\cdot\Delta t\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
and
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}^{\ast}={\sigma_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}}^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{M}_{m+1}-\mathcal{M}_{m})K\cdot\Delta t\cdot\mathbf{\bm{I}}_{3\times 3}
.

These residuals allow for parameter estimation through solving a nonlinear least-squares problem

\begin{split}\min\Bigg\{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{M}}&\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}\\
k\in\{1,\dotsc,K\}\end{subarray}}\big(\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{a}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{a}}}+\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{g}}}\big)\\
+&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\{0,\dotsc,N\}\\
k\in\{1,\dotsc,K-1\}\end{subarray}}\big(\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}}}+\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}}}\big)\Big)\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\{1,\dotsc,M-1\}\\
n\in\{0,\dotsc,N\}\end{subarray}}&\big(\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{a}}}^{\ast}}+\left\|\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}^{\ast}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\mathbf{\bm{r}}_{\mathbf{\bm{b}}_{g}}}^{\ast}}\big)\Bigg\},\end{split}

(3)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}}
represents the Mahalanobis distance using covariance matrix
\mathbf{\bm{\Sigma}}
.

VII
Conclusion

This paper presents a multi-IMU extrinsic calibration by selecting high-utility measurement subsets. We hypothesize that, in our system, utility—a function of the parameter estimates—is largely insensitive to the specific choice of parameters, allowing evaluation at an initial guess and reducing the need for frequent recalibrations. This significantly decreases computation time in both simulations and experiments, making it suitable for resource-limited platforms.

We acknowledge that the hypothesis regarding the insensitivity in parameter choice for subset selection could have been examined more thoroughly.
Future research could explore this in greater depth, for example by comparing the selected segments using both the original and the modified Greedy approaches.

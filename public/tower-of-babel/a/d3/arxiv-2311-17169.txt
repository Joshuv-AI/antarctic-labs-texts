Third-generation-philic Hidden Naturalness
Yi Chung, Florian Goertz

Abstract
We present a solution to the electroweak hierarchy problem, where the relevant new particles are third-generation-philic and hidden in SM processes with third-generation fermions. Due to this feature, the mass bounds from direct searches are much weaker and the required fine-tuning can be reduced drastically. A concrete model is constructed based on a S ​ U ​ ( 6 ) / S ​ p ​ ( 6 ) SU(6)/Sp(6) fundamental composite Higgs model with collective symmetry breaking and extended hypercolor mechanism. The construction allows to raise the scale f f to ∼ 3 \sim 3\, TeV, corresponding to resonances at M ρ ≳ 10 M_{\rho}\gtrsim 10 TeV, without much tuning – employing ingredients that are naturally inherent in the (composite) Goldstone-Higgs framework. The experimental signatures are discussed in detail. It is found that current bounds allow for a model with negligible tuning.



I
Introduction

The Standard Model (SM) of particle physics describes all known particles and interactions successfully. At the center of the SM is the mechanism of electroweak symmetry breaking (EWSB), which is responsible for the masses of gauge bosons and fermions. The discovery of the Higgs bosons in 2012
Chatrchyan et al. 2012
;
Aad et al. 2012
filled in the last missing piece of the SM. However, the SM does not address the UV-sensitive nature of the Higgs field. Its mass term receives quadratically divergent radiative corrections from its interactions with other SM fields and also itself, which leads to the well-known hierarchy problem. The three main corrections come from the top quark loop, weak boson loop, and Higgs boson loop, reading

\displaystyle\delta m_{H}^{2}=-\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}y_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}^{2}+\frac{9}{64\pi^{2}}g_{W}^{2}\Lambda_{g}^{2}+\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\lambda\Lambda_{h}^{2}~.

(1)

Following the Naturalness principle, we expect new degrees of freedom to show up at the scales
\Lambda_{t}\sim 500\text{ GeV}
,
\Lambda_{g}\sim 1200\text{ GeV}
, and
\Lambda_{h}\sim 1300\text{ GeV}
, respectively.

However, because of the absence of new physics in LHC searches, the bounds on the mass of expected new particles in traditional models, such as supersymmetry (SUSY) and composite Higgs model (CHM), have already surpassed the values shown above and challenge the Naturalness principle due to the growing required tuning. To avoid fine-tuning, one alternative is to have new particles being SM singlets which could be significantly lighter while escaping direct detection at the LHC. The idea is known as Neutral Naturalness, realized for example in twin Higgs models
Chacko et al. 2006
.

In this letter, we present a new solution where the new particles have a different nature and can still be detected directly at the LHC, but only or mainly through third-generation-fermion channels. Such a feature can significantly reduce the production rate and make the final states harder to measure at the same time, which might explain why they have not been observed by the LHC so far. Moreover, these third-generation-philic particles also have weaker constraints from flavor observables due to an accidental
U(2)^{5}
flavor symmetry
Barbieri et al. 2011
;
Isidori and Straub 2012
.

II.1
Basics of
SU(6)/Sp(6)

To study the
SU(6)/Sp(6)

\Sigma
, which transforms as an anti-symmetric tensor representation
\mathbf{15}
of
SU(6)
. The transformation can be expressed as
\Sigma\to g\,\Sigma\,g^{T}
with
g\in SU(6)
. The field
\Sigma
has an anti-symmetric vacuum expectation value (vev) given by (
\epsilon=i\sigma^{2}
)

\langle\Sigma\rangle=\Sigma_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-\mathbb{I}_{2\times 2}\\
0&\epsilon&0\\
\mathbb{I}_{2\times 2}&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,

(4)

which breaks
SU(6)
down to
Sp(6)
and produces a total of 14 NGBs.

The
SU(6)
generators are divided into

\begin{cases}\text{unbroken generators}&~T_{a}:T_{a}\Sigma_{0}+\Sigma_{0}T_{a}^{T}=0~,\\
\text{broken generators}&X_{a}:X_{a}\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{0}X_{a}^{T}=0~.\end{cases}

(5)

The NGBs can be written as a matrix with the broken generators (and symmetry-breaking scale
f
)

\xi(x)\equiv e^{\frac{i\pi_{a}(x)X_{a}}{2f}}\,,

(6)

transforming under
SU(6)
as
\xi\to g\,\xi\,h^{\dagger}
with
h\in Sp(6)
and

\Sigma(x)=\xi(x)\,\Sigma_{0}\,\xi^{T}(x)=e^{\frac{i\pi_{a}(x)X_{a}}{f}}\Sigma_{0}~.

(7)

They are structured as

\displaystyle i\pi_{a}X_{a}\!\cdot\!\Sigma_{0}\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon s&\left({H}_{1}\,\tilde{H}_{2}\right)&-\frac{\phi_{a}}{\sqrt{2}}\sigma^{a}\!+\!\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}\mathbb{I}\\
-\left({H}_{1}\,\tilde{H}_{2}\right)^{T}&-2\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}\epsilon&\left(\tilde{H}_{2}\,H_{1}\right)^{\dagger}\\
\frac{\phi_{a}}{\sqrt{2}}\sigma^{a*}\!\!-\!\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}\mathbb{I}&\left(-\tilde{H}_{2}\,H_{1}\right)^{\!*}&-\epsilon^{T}s^{*}\\
\end{pmatrix}\!,

(8)

forming a real
SU(2)_{W}
triplet
\phi_{a}
(which will be absorbed by the massive
SU(2)^{\prime}
gauge bosons), a complex singlet
s
, a real singlet
\eta
, and two complex Higgs doublets
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, both with hypercharge
-1/2
. We will show in the next section that the latter feature a two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM) with tan
\,\beta=1
and can naturally realize the SM alignment limit
\beta-\alpha=\pi/2
, resulting in an inert second Higgs doublet. The observed Higgs boson
h
is a linear combination
(h_{1}+h_{2})/\sqrt{2}
, where
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are the neutral CP-even components of the two Higgs doublets.

(9)

\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\

\end{pmatrix}~.

(10)

The kinetic Lagrangian for the NGBs is given by

\mathcal{L}=\frac{f^{2}}{8}\text{tr}\left[(D_{\mu}\Sigma)(D^{\mu}\Sigma)^{\dagger}\right],

(11)

where

\mathcal{L}_{h}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}h)^{2}+\frac{f^{2}}{8}g_{W}^{2}\,\text{sin}^{2}\!\left(\frac{h}{f}\right)\left[2W^{+}_{\mu}W^{-\mu}+\frac{Z_{\mu}Z^{\mu}}{\text{cos}^{2}\,\theta_{W}}\right]\!,

with the non-linear behavior of the CHM being apparent in the trigonometric functions. When

(13)

where

.
The Higgs non-linearity

\xi\equiv\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{V}{f}\right)

(14)

parametrizes deviations of the Higgs couplings from the SM, which for vector bosons become

\kappa_{V}\equiv\frac{g_{hVV}}{g^{SM}_{hVV}}=\text{cos}\left(\frac{V}{f}\right)=\sqrt{1-\xi}\approx 1-\frac{\xi}{2}~.

(15)

III
The pNGB potential

The interaction terms introduced in the last section break the
SU(6)
global symmetry explicitly and thereby will contribute to the potential of the pNGB fields. Starting with the gauge interactions,
SU(2)_{I}
and
SU(2)_{I\!I}
introduce the potential terms

\displaystyle c\,g_{I}^{2}f^{2}\left|s+\frac{i}{2f}{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}\right|^{2}+c\,g_{I\!I}^{2}f^{2}\left|s-\frac{i}{2f}{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}\right|^{2},

(20)

where
c\sim{\cal O}(1)
is determined by the concrete UV model. The terms break the shift symmetry of the complex scalar
s
and induce the mass term

\displaystyle V_{s}=c\,(g_{I}^{2}+g_{I\!I}^{2})f^{2}s^{2}=c\,g_{W^{\prime}}^{2}f^{2}s^{2}~.

(21)

Besides, there are also trilinear couplings between
s
and the Higgs fields given by

\displaystyle V_{sHH}=\frac{i}{2}cf\,(g_{I}^{2}-g_{I\!I}^{2})\,s{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}\,+{\rm h.c.}~.

(22)

A nontrivial Higgs quartic term is also generated after combining terms in Eq. (
20
) and the tree-level contribution from integrating out
s
as

\displaystyle V_{\text{quartic}}=c\,\frac{g_{I}^{2}g_{I\!I}^{2}}{g_{I}^{2}+g_{I\!I}^{2}}\left|{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}\right|^{2}=c\,g_{W}^{2}\left|{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}\right|^{2}~,

(23)

which is the key feature of little Higgs models.

A loop level potential for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
is induced from the gauge bosons as

\begin{split}V_{g}=\frac{9}{64\pi^{2}}g_{W}^{2}\,\text{ln}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}&\Big[M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}\left(|H_{1}|^{2}+|H_{2}|^{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{g_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}{8}\left(|H_{1}|^{2}+|H_{2}|^{2}\right)^{2}\Big]\,,\end{split}

where
\Lambda\sim 4\pi f
is the cutoff of the theory. Contributions from other gauge interactions, such as
U(1)_{Y}
(and
U(1)_{H\!B}
, introduced below), are suppressed due to weaker couplings and not discussed here.

Finally, the top Yukawa in Eq. (
18
) breaks the shift symmetry of
H_{1}+H_{2}
, leading to

\displaystyle V_{t}=-\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}y_{t}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{t}^{2}|H_{1}+H_{2}|^{2}\!-\frac{y_{t}^{2}}{4}\text{ln}\frac{\Lambda_{t}^{2}}{m_{t}^{2}}|H_{1}+H_{2}|^{4}\right)\,,

(24)

where
\Lambda_{t}
is the cutoff of the top loop contribution. In CHMs with EHC, this corresponds to the
E_{\mu}
boson mass
M_{E}=g_{E}f_{E}
, which can be light if
g_{E}
is weak.

IV
The Higgs potential in the 2HDM and Naturalness

The doublet part of the potential corresponds to a 2HDM with

\displaystyle V_{H}

\displaystyle=V_{t}+V_{g}+V_{\text{quartic}}~,

(25)

featuring a
S_{2}
symmetry between
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, which guarantees
\langle H_{1}\rangle=\langle H_{2}\rangle
. A natural alignment limit with tan
\beta
=1 is automatically satisfied. In the Higgs basis

\displaystyle H_{\text{SM}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(H_{1}+H_{2}),\quad H_{\text{inert}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(H_{1}-H_{2})\,,

(26)

where
\langle H_{\text{SM}}\rangle=v/\sqrt{2}
and
\langle H_{\text{inert}}\rangle=0
,
the potential reads

\displaystyle V_{H}

\displaystyle=(m_{H,t}^{2}+m_{H,g}^{2})|H_{\text{SM}}|^{2}+(\lambda_{t}+\lambda_{g}+\lambda_{s})|H_{\text{SM}}|^{4}

\displaystyle+(m_{H,g}^{2})|H_{\text{inert}}|^{2}+(\lambda_{g}+\lambda_{s})|H_{\text{inert}}|^{4}

\displaystyle+(2\lambda_{g}-\frac{2\lambda_{s}}{3})|H_{\text{SM}}|^{2}|H_{\text{inert}}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\lambda_{s}}{3}\left(|H_{\text{SM}}^{\dagger}H_{\text{inert}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left[(H_{\text{SM}}^{\dagger}H_{\text{inert}})^{2}+{\rm h.c.}\right]\right)~,

(27)

with

\displaystyle m_{H,t}^{2}=-\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}y_{t}^{2}M_{E}^{2}\,,~m_{H,g}^{2}=\frac{9}{64\pi^{2}}g_{W}^{2}M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}\,\text{ln}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}~,

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}=\frac{3y_{t}^{4}}{16\pi^{2}}\text{ln}\frac{M_{E}^{2}}{m_{t}^{2}},~\lambda_{g}=\frac{9g_{W}^{2}g_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}{512\pi^{2}}\text{ln}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}},~\lambda_{s}=\frac{c\,g_{W}^{2}}{4}.

The
H_{\text{SM}}
part should match the SM Higgs potential

\displaystyle V_{\text{SM}}=-\mu^{2}_{\text{SM}}|H_{\text{SM}}|^{2}+\lambda_{\text{SM}}|H_{\text{SM}}|^{4}~,

(28)

where the coefficient of the quadratic term (relevant for Naturalness) is given by

\displaystyle-\mu^{2}_{\text{SM}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}y_{t}^{2}M_{E}^{2}+\frac{9}{64\pi^{2}}g_{W}^{2}M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}\,\text{ln}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}~,

(29)

in line with the IR potential from Eq. (
1
) with top loop and gauge loop contribution.

The former is cut off by
M_{E}=g_{E}f_{E}
, which now replaces
f
as the fundamental scale and can be significantly smaller for small
g_{E}
. The Naturalness principle requires
M_{E}\sim 500
GeV. Notice that the top loop contribution is now totally independent of the scale
f
.

The mass of the heavy boson entering the gauge loop is given by
M_{W^{\prime}}=1/2\,g_{W^{\prime}}f
. Different from conventional CHMs, where the loop is cut off by the vector resonances of the strong sector with
M_{\rho}\sim g_{\rho}f
, now it is canceled by the heavy
SU(2)^{\prime}
boson in the little Higgs setup. Compared to the large
g_{\rho}
from the strong dynamics,
g_{W^{\prime}}
can be smaller and thus a lighter

can lower down
m_{H}^{2}
and the fine-tuning. Numerically, Naturalness requires
M_{W^{\prime}}\sim 1.2
TeV, which restricts the combination
g_{W^{\prime}}f
. If some level of fine-tuning, such as
25\%\,(10\%)

TeV.

Most importantly, the new particles at the cutoff of both loops are third-generation-philic, which means their direct search bound is much weaker compared to other scenarios: The top loop is cut off by the EHC boson, which can couple only to top quarks, and the

The Higgs quartic emerges from three different sources,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\text{SM}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{t}+\lambda_{g}=\frac{c\,g_{W}^{2}}{4}+(\text{one-loop})~,

(30)

but is dominated by the tree-level contribution as in little-Higgs type models. Some contributions from the top loop and gauge loop are also required to reach the observed value of

Considering the dominant contributions, a natural splitting between

and

\displaystyle\frac{v}{f}=\frac{1}{f}\sqrt{\frac{-m_{H,t}^{2}}{\lambda_{s}}}\sim\sqrt{\frac{3Y_{s}y_{t}}{2\pi^{2}}}\frac{g_{E}}{g_{W}}~,

which becomes
\sim 1/3

Therefore, Naturalness is realized two-fold: First, the quadratic term from the UV theory can still be naturally small thanks to the weaker constraints for third-generation fermions, and second,

is naturally small thanks to collective symmetry breaking, which solves the little hierarchy problem. The residual tuning is fully captured by the ratio of the numerator above to its natural value
\sqrt{-\mu^{2}_{\text{SM}}}\sim 100\,
GeV, as discussed before.
We note that for very small

a huge separation of scales seems possible in a natural way, which is however limited by (above-neglected) gauge contributions

So far, we have neglected the quadratic term arising from Higgs self-interactions as shown in Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta m_{H,\lambda}^{2}=\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\left(\lambda_{s}M_{s}^{2}+\lambda_{t}M_{E}^{2}+\lambda_{g}M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}\right)~,

where we ignore

which exhibits a similar form as the gauge contribution. Having the Higgs-loop contribution smaller requires

and

\eta

To address this, further explicit breaking via a hyperfermion mass term could be introduced. In the following, we will however avoid the massless singlet by gauging the corresponding

symmetry, where

denotes hyperbaryon number

;
Chung 2021c

,

\displaystyle M_{Z^{\prime}_{H\!B}}\approx\frac{2}{N_{HC}}g_{H\!B}f~,

(37)

where
N_{HC}
is the number of hypercolors.

VI.2
Cutoff of top loop : EHC gauge boson

The top Yukawa coupling is generated through the interaction in Eq. (
17

Chung 2023
. However, searches for a
E_{\mu}

E_{\mu}
is colored, an operator
\mathcal{O}_{tG}=g_{s}\left(\bar{q}_{L}\sigma^{\mu\nu}T_{A}t_{R}\right)\tilde{H}G^{A}_{\mu\nu}+\text{h.c.}
will be generated through a loop with
E_{\mu}
bosons and a hyperfermion and affects the
\bar{t}t

Sirunyan et al. 2019

t\bar{t}h\,/\,t\bar{t}Z\,/\,tbW
, which can be tested at the HL-LHC.

Moreover, in most UV models, a neutral massive
Z^{\prime}_{E}
boson from the diagonal
U(1)
of the EHC group appears. In our case, both
q_{L}
and
t_{R}
should be charged under this

and thus couple to the

boson.
3
3

3

The

In this case, the evidence of the EHC mechanism can also be looked for through direct searches like
Z^{\prime}_{E}\to t\bar{t}
or
Z^{\prime}_{E}\to b\bar{b}
. However, the dominant production comes from

b

VII

VI.3
Cutoff of Higgs loop : Complex scalar singlet

The cutoff of the Higgs loop, depending on its origin, is provided by the heavy gauge bosons and EHC gauge bosons discussed before. The dominant tree-level contribution is cut off by the complex scalar singlet
s
.

Its mass
M_{s}\sim\sqrt{c}\,g_{W^{\prime}}f
is expected to reside at few TeV. Its main interaction stems from the
s{H_{2}}^{\dagger}H_{1}
coupling in Eq. (
22

hh
with a branching ratio of
\sim 20\%
. There are also couplings with SM fermions due to mixing with the Higgs boson. Therefore, the production can be through similar channels as the Higgs production but is suppressed by the large
M_{s}
and an additional factor of
\xi
from mixing, which makes it hard to detect even at the HL-LHC. For example, taking
M_{s}=1300

cross section will be around 6
\xi

Aad et al. 2022
;
CMS 2021
and also hard to access at the HL-LHC unless it is somewhat lighter

;
Cid Vidal et al. 2019
.

VI.4
The Inert Second Higgs Doublet

As discussed above, the second Higgs gets a similar potential as the first Higgs but without the top-loop contribution and its mass resides generically around a few hundred GeV. The gauge contribution will lead to a positive quadratic and quartic and thus no vev,
\langle H_{\rm inert}\rangle=0
, realizing a typical ’inert’ Higgs doublet (see e.g.
Barbieri et al. 2006
;
Lopez Honorez et al. 2007
;
Chowdhury et al. 2012
;
Borah and Cline 2012
;
Cline and Kainulainen 2013
;
Blinov et al. 2015
;
Ilnicka et al. 2016
;
Belyaev et al. 2018
;
Fabian et al. 2021
;
Astros et al. 2023
).
Here, its components are expected to fulfill the relations

\begin{split}M_{H}+M_{H^{\pm}}>M_{W^{\pm}}\,,&\quad M_{A}+M_{H^{\pm}}>M_{W^{\pm}},\\
M_{H}+M_{A}>M_{Z}\,,&\quad 2M_{H^{\pm}}>M_{Z}\,,\end{split}

(38)

as well as

M_{H}>80\,{\rm GeV},\ M_{H^{\pm}}>70\,{\rm GeV},

(39)

which assures that bounds from LEP searches for NP in EW gauge-boson decays and from LEP-II searches for direct production of the new scalars are easily evaded.

Moreover, similar to the vanilla inert doublet model, the neutral components of
H_{\rm inert}
furnish attractive dark matter candidates. For a benchmark of
M_{H}\approx 500\,{\rm GeV}\lesssim M_{H^{\pm}}\lesssim M_{A}
, a realistic dark matter abundance could be achieved for small
\lambda_{g}+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{3}
, which assures that the annihilation into longitudinal vector bosons gets not too large, see, e.g.,
Belyaev et al. 2018
;
Fabian et al. 2021
;
Astros et al. 2023
. We note that the small splitting to lift the
H^{\pm}
components in order to avoid charged dark matter would need to originate from subleading contributions to the scalar potential. A detailed study of this is beyond the scope of this paper.

VI.5
Other pNGBs

Finally, there is still the real singlet
\eta

is third generation philic and the production rate is highly suppressed.

VII
Precision Tests

In this section, we discuss the indirect tests of the model from precision measurements,
focusing on the

dim-6
top Yukawa coupling and the heavy
SU(2)^{\prime}

VII.1
Running Top Yukawa

The top Yukawa coupling from the four fermion interaction is one of the main characteristic features in our model. The low cutoff implies large running at the corresponding mass scale, which will reveal itself in differential momentum
distributions of
t\bar{t}h

Mammen Abraham et al. 2022
;
Bittar and Burdman 2022
. However, based on the
t\bar{t}h
cross section, it is hard to achieve the desired sensitivity in the LHC era and there are currently no analyses available.

VII.2
Running Top mass

Besides probing the top Yukawa at high scales directly, the model can be tested by measuring the running of the top quark mass, which will affect the
t\bar{t}
differential cross section. Without a Higgs in the final state, the cross section is much larger. The measurement has already been performed by CMS using 35.9 fb
-1
of run 2 data
Sirunyan et al. 2020
and has been interpreted in
Defranchis et al. 2022
in terms of the top mass running up to
0.5
TeV. This already puts a bound of
M_{E}\gtrsim 700

IV
. With more data coming out, we however expect that the fine-tuning region down to
10\%
can be tested in the HL-LHC era.

VII.3
Four top quarks cross section

The

Aad et al. 2023

;
Banelli et al. 2021
;
Blekman et al. 2022

Darmé et al. 2021

4

4

Notice that the

VII.4
Flavor constraints

The

boson can also have an impact on light-quark physics through mixing, which can introduce dangerous flavor changing neutral currents. Assuming
\theta_{sb}\gg\theta_{db}
for the down-quark mixing, in analogy to the CKM matrix, the strongest constraint is expected from

we arive at the 95% C.L. bound

\frac{g_{sb}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}=\frac{g_{Z^{\prime}_{E}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}_{E}}}\theta_{sb}\leq\frac{1}{194~\text{TeV}}\,,

(40)

where the angle
\theta_{sb}
parametrizes the rotation between the second and third generations of down-type quarks in the mass basis and
{g_{Z^{\prime}_{E}}}/{M_{Z^{\prime}_{E}}}\approx 1/f_{E}
. For
f_{E}=4

\theta_{sb}\lesssim 0.02
.
Applying the same constraint to the
Z^{\prime}

{g_{sb}}/{M_{Z^{\prime}}}={g_{W^{\prime}}}\theta_{sb}/2{M_{W^{\prime}}}=\theta_{sb}/f

\theta_{sb}\lesssim 0.01

VII.5
Electroweak precision tests

Adding new degrees of freedom at the TeV scale will lead to electroweak oblique corrections, which are usually expressed in terms of
S
,
T
, and

parameters
Peskin and Takeuchi 1990
;
Peskin and Takeuchi 1992

)
Workman et al. 2022

S=-0.01\pm 0.07,\quad T=0.04\pm 0.06,

(41)

with a strong positive correlation (92%) between them. At 95% C.L., one obtains
S<0.14

There are several contributions to the oblique parameters in our model. In CHMs, the nonlinear Higgs dynamics will contribute to both
S
and
T

Barbieri et al. 2007
;
Grojean et al. 2013

\displaystyle\Delta S=\frac{1}{12\pi}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\right)\cdot\xi\,,~\Delta T=-\frac{3}{16\pi c_{w}^{2}}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\right)\cdot\xi\,.

(42)

Taking
M_{\rho}=5
TeV, we get

\displaystyle\Delta S\sim 0.20~\xi\,,\quad\Delta T\sim-0.57~\xi~.

(43)

The other contributions are discussed below.

The
S
parameter

In FCHMs, the hyperfermions, which carry electroweak quantum numbers, also contribute to the
S
parameter. The contribution can be estimated by calculating the one-loop diagram from the heavy hyperfermions. In this case, each heavy
SU(2)_{W}
doublet adds
\sim\xi/6\pi
, resulting in

\displaystyle\Delta S\sim\frac{\xi}{6\pi}\,2N_{HC}\sim 0.21~\xi\quad(N_{HC}=2)~.

(44)

In little Higgs models, the
S
parameter also receives a contribution from the heavy
SU(2)^{\prime}
bosons, which dominates in our setup, reading
Csaki et al. 2003a
;
Hewett et al. 2003
;
Csaki et al. 2003b
;
Gregoire et al. 2004
;
Marandella et al. 2005

\displaystyle\Delta S\sim\frac{8\pi v^{2}}{g_{W}^{2}f^{2}}\text{ sin}^{2}\,{\theta_{e}}\sim\frac{8\pi}{g_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}\,\xi=2\pi\frac{v^{2}}{M_{W^{\prime}}^{2}}\,.

(45)

This leads to a bound of
M_{W^{\prime}}\gtrsim 1.6
TeV, being comparable with collider constraints discussed above.

The
T
parameter

The
SU(2)^{\prime}
bosons contribute to the
T
parameter only at
O(\xi^{2})

Chiang et al. 2010

\displaystyle\Delta T=-\frac{\text{sin}^{2}\,{\theta_{W}}}{4\alpha\,\text{cos}^{2}\,{\theta_{W}}}\,\text{sin}^{2}\,{\theta_{e}}\,\text{cos}^{2}\,{\theta_{e}}\frac{v^{4}}{f^{4}}~.

(46)

Also, a generic 2HDM of
SU(6)/Sp(6)

Csaki et al. 2003b
;
Gregoire et al. 2004
;
Marandella et al. 2005
;
Strumia 2022

\displaystyle\Delta T=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{v^{2}}{4f^{2}}\text{ cos}^{2}\,2\beta~,

(47)

which vanishes in our model because tan
\,\beta=1
preserves custodial symmetry. Yet another contribution comes from the hyperfermion loop with an insertion of an EHC boson, treating top and bottom quarks differently,

\displaystyle\Delta T=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{N_{c}Y_{s}^{4}}{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{f_{E}^{2}}~,

(48)

which provides a small positive contribution leading in total to a negligible
\Delta T

VII.6
Zb\bar{b}
coupling

Another relevant constraint on top quark related models comes from the
Zb\bar{b}
coupling. The deviation for the left-handed bottom coupling is constrained within
-2.5\times 10^{-3}\lesssim\delta g_{b_{L}}\lesssim 5\times 10^{-3}

Batell et al. 2013
;
Guadagnoli and Isidori 2013

Here, the correction from mixing with

\displaystyle\delta g_{b_{L}}=-\frac{1}{4}\,\text{cos}^{4}\,{\theta_{e}}\,\text{sin}^{2}\beta\,\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}~,

\displaystyle\delta g_{b_{L}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{v^{2}}{f_{E}^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{4}\frac{y_{t}}{Y_{s}}\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}~,

(50)

which in total leads to a correction well within bounds.

VIII
Conclusions

We presented a model that cuts off the dangerous corrections to the Higgs mass term via third-generation-philic bosons that can be light while escaping strong experimental limits. Interestingly, the corresponding states, a
W^{\prime}
, a light EHC boson, and a scalar singlet, can naturally emerge in straightforward incarnations of a (composite) Goldstone-Higgs.

As demonstrated, experiments are just starting to explore the natural parameter region of the model. On the other hand, at the HL-LHC we expect that tuning of down to 10% will be tested, while only future colliders such as FCC can exclude this scenario of hidden Naturalness to a point of excessive tuning well below 10%.

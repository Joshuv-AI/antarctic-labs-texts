Joint moments of higher order derivatives of CUE characteristic polynomials I: asymptotic formulae
Jonathan P. Keating, Fei Wei

Abstract
We derive explicit asymptotic formulae for the joint moments of the n 1 n_{1} -th and n 2 n_{2} -th derivatives of the characteristic polynomials of CUE random matrices for any non-negative integers n 1 , n 2 n_{1},n_{2} . These formulae are expressed in terms of determinants whose entries involve modified Bessel functions of the first kind. We also express them in terms of two types of combinatorial sums. Similar results are obtained for the analogue of Hardy’s Z Z -function. We use these formulae to formulate general conjectures for the joint moments of the n 1 n_{1} -th and n 2 n_{2} -th derivatives of the Riemann zeta-function and of Hardy’s Z Z -function. Our conjectures are supported by comparison with results obtained previously in the number theory literature.



Key words:
Joint moments, higher order derivatives, characteristic polynomials, random unitary matrices, Bessel functions, Riemann zeta function, Hardy’s
Z
-function.

1.2
A brief summary of our main methods

Our starting point is
[
16
]
, which concerns the first order derivative of moments of characteristic polynomials of CUE matrices. The approach there is to take the first order derivative of moments of characteristic polynomials with shifts. In the higher-order case, it is natural to use the same approach and take the
n
-th derivative. The difficulty here is how to represent multiple integrals (see formulae (
18
) and (
19
)) by expressions in terms of the determinant involving modified Bessel functions of the first kind, which is closely related to solutions of the
\sigma
-Painlevé III
′
equation. Furthermore, it is desirable that this expression is computable. Our main idea here is to introduce
n
extra variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
, and to compute higher order derivatives with respect to them at 0. We find that the higher order derivative with respect to
t_{1}
is related to taking the derivative of a determinant of a matrix whose entries involve the modified Bessel functions of the first kind, and higher order derivatives with respect to the other
n-1
variables contribute combinatorial coefficients and shifts to the indices of the modified Bessel functions of the first kind, see Propositions
21
,
22
.

Our method to describe
a_{k,M}(n_{1},n_{2}),b_{k,M}(n_{1},n_{2})
is different from that of
[
16
, Theorem 3]
. The main tool of
[
16
]
is the Laplacian transform, which converts the complex integral into a computable multiple real integral. In the higher order derivative case, this multiple integral is very complicated. Therefore, instead of using the Laplacian transform, we directly take the higher order derivatives of the determinant involving the modified Bessel functions of the first kind with shifts that appear in (
4
). We find that this procedure can be carried out straightforwardly.

In Theorems
5
and
6
, we provide alternate formulae for the joint moments of higher order derivatives that are more concise than those in Theorems
2
and
4

The following lemma gives an explicit formula for the higher order derivatives of the function appearing in the expression of moments of characteristic polynomials with shifts.

Lemma 9
.

Let
n\geq 0,k\geq 1
be integers, then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2}\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2}\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}-\alpha)}\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\left(-\frac{N}{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}-\alpha}\right)^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(w_{l}-\alpha)^{j}}\right)^{m_{j}}.

Proof.

It is not hard to check that

\left(\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2}\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}-\alpha)}=\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2}\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}-\alpha)}f_{n}(\alpha),

(13)

where
f_{n}(\alpha)
is defined by the following recursive formulae

\displaystyle f_{1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}-\alpha},

\displaystyle f_{l+1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{l}(\alpha)f_{1}(\alpha)+f_{l}^{\prime}(\alpha),\quad l\geq 1.

Let
g(\alpha)
be a function such that
g^{\prime}(\alpha)=f_{1}(\alpha)
. Consider the function
e^{g(\alpha)}
, then

\left(\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}e^{g(\alpha)}=e^{g(\alpha)}f_{n}(\alpha).

By Faà di Bruno’s formula

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\alpha}\right)^{n}e^{g(\alpha)}=e^{g(\alpha)}\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{f_{1}^{(j-1)}(\alpha)}{j!}\right)^{m_{j}},

so

\displaystyle f_{n}(\alpha)=\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\left(-\frac{N}{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}-\alpha}\right)^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(w_{l}-\alpha)^{j}}\right)^{m_{j}}.

By (
13
), we obtain the claimed result.
∎

The proposition below gives a formula for the powers of the expression obtained above at
\alpha=0
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
k\geq 1,r\geq 1
,
n\geq 0
and
m_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,m_{n}\geq 0
be integers. Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
be non-zero complex numbers. Then we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}(-N+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}})^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})^{m_{j}}\Bigg)^{r}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}\leq n\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left((-N)^{n-\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}\binom{n}{\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj})!\right)\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{lj}}}.

and

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})^{m_{j}}\Bigg)^{r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{l=1}^{k}h_{lj}=n\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}\frac{(n!)^{r}}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{lj}}}.

(15)

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle a_{n-m_{1}}=\sum_{2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n-m_{1}}\frac{1}{m_{2}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=2}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})^{m_{j}}.

Then
a_{n-m_{1}}
is the coefficient of

t^{n-m_{1}}
in the Taylor expansion of

\prod_{j=2}^{n}e^{\frac{t^{j}}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}}}=\prod_{j=2}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{k}e^{\frac{1}{j}\frac{t^{j}}{w_{l}^{j}}}.

So

\displaystyle a_{n-m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m_{1})!}\frac{d^{(n-m_{1})}}{dt^{(n-m_{1})}}e^{\sum_{j=2}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})t^{j}}\Bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m_{1})!}\frac{d^{(n-m_{1})}}{dt^{(n-m_{1})}}e^{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})t^{j}}\Bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m_{1})!}\frac{d^{(n-m_{1})}}{dt^{(n-m_{1})}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}e^{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\frac{t^{j}}{w_{l}^{j}}}\Bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m_{1})!}\frac{d^{(n-m_{1})}}{dt^{(n-m_{1})}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}e^{-\ln(1-\frac{t}{w_{l}})-\frac{t}{w_{l}}}\Bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-m_{1})!}\frac{d^{(n-m_{1})}}{dt^{(n-m_{1})}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}e^{-\frac{t}{w_{l}}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{w_{l}}}\Bigg|_{t=0}.

Namely,
a_{n-m_{1}}
is the coefficient of

t^{n-m_{1}}
in the Taylor expansion of

F(t)=\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{w_{l}}}\right)e^{-\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{t}{w_{l}}}.

Let
G_{1}(t)=e^{(-N+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}})t}
and let
G_{2}(t)=e^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}})t}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}(-N+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}})^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{n}a_{n-m_{1}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!}(-N+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}})^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}(G_{1}(t)F(t))\Bigg|_{t=0}=\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(e^{-Nt}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{w_{l}}}\right)\Bigg|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(e^{-Nt}\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\right)\Bigg|_{t=0}.

Similarly,

\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{l}^{j}})^{m_{j}}=\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\right)\Bigg|_{t=0}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(e^{-Nt}\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\right)\Bigg|_{t=0}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{d^{n}}{dt_{j}^{n}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(e^{-Nt_{j}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t_{j}^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\right)\Bigg|_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{d^{n}}{dt_{j}^{n}}\right)e^{-N(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{r})}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t_{j}^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\Bigg|_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{d^{n}}{dt_{j}^{n}}\right)e^{-N(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{r})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{11},\ldots,s_{1r},\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
s_{k1},\ldots,s_{kr}=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}^{\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{lj}}}\Bigg|_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{11},\ldots,s_{1r},\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
s_{k1},\ldots,s_{kr}=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt_{j}^{n}}e^{-Nt_{j}}t_{j}^{\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}\right)\Bigg|_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}=0}\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{lj}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}\leq n\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left((-N)^{n-\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}\binom{n}{\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj}}(\sum_{l=1}^{k}s_{lj})!\right)\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}s_{lj}}}.

Similarly,

\left(\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{t^{i}}{w_{l}^{i}})\right)\Bigg|_{t=0}\right)^{r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{l=1}^{k}h_{lj}=n\\
j=1,\ldots,r\end{subarray}}(n!)^{r}\frac{1}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}w_{l}^{\sum_{j=1}^{r}h_{lj}}}.

Putting it all together, we obtain the claimed result.
∎

Lemma 13

Lemma 18
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
be
2k-2
times differentiable functions. Let
\Delta(\frac{d}{dL})
be the differential operator

\Delta(\frac{d}{dL}):=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\Big(\frac{d}{dL_{j}}-\frac{d}{dL_{i}}\Big)=\det_{k\times k}\Big(\frac{d^{j-1}}{dL_{i}^{j-1}}\Big).

Then

\Delta^{2}(\frac{d}{dL})\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(L_{i})\Bigg|_{L_{i}=X}\\
=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\det_{k\times k}(f_{\sigma(i)}^{(i+j-2)}(X)).

In particular, when
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=f
, we have

\Delta^{2}(\frac{d}{dL})(\prod_{i=1}^{k}f(L_{i}))\Big|_{L_{i}=X}=k!\det_{k\times k}(f^{(i+j-2)}(X)).

Proof.

Firstly,

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{d}{dL})\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(L_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{j-1}}{dL_{i}^{j-1}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(L_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}{\rm sign}(\sigma)\left(\frac{d}{dL_{1}}\right)^{\sigma(1)-1}\left(\frac{d}{dL_{2}}\right)^{\sigma(2)-1}\cdots\left(\frac{d}{dL_{k}}\right)^{\sigma(k)-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(L_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}{\rm sign}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}^{(\sigma(i)-1)}(L_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det_{k\times k}(f_{i}^{(j-1)}(L_{i})).

Secondly,

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\frac{d}{dL})\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(L_{i})\Bigg|_{L_{i}=X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{d}{dL})\det_{k\times k}(f_{i}^{(j-1)}(L_{i}))\Bigg|_{L_{i}=X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{d}{dL})\sum_{\sigma}{\rm sign}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}^{(\sigma(i)-1)}(L_{i})\Bigg|_{L_{i}=X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in S_{k}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}{\rm sign}(\sigma){\rm sign}(\tau)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}^{(\sigma(i)-1+\tau(i)-1)}(L_{i})\Bigg|_{L_{i}=X}

\displaystyle=

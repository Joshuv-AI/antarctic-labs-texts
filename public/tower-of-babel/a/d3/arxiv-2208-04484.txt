Good Locally Repairable Codes via Propagation Rules
Shu Liu, Liming Ma, Tingyi Wu, Chaoping Xing

Abstract
. In classical coding theory, it is common to construct new codes via propagation rules. There are various propagation rules to construct classical block codes. However, propagation rules have not been extensively explored for constructions of locally repairable codes. In this paper, we introduce a few propagation rules to construct good locally repairable codes. To our surprise, these simple propagation rules produce a few interesting results. Firstly, by concatenating a locally repairable code as an inner code with a classical block code as an outer code, we obtain quite a few dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes. Secondly, from this concatenation, we explicitly build a family of locally repairable codes that exceeds the Zyablov-type bound. Thirdly, by a lengthening propagation rule that adds some rows and columns from a parity-check matrix of a given linear code, we are able to produce a family of dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes from the extended Hamming codes, and to convert a classical maximum distance separable (MDS) code into a Singleton-optimal locally repairable code. Furthermore, via the lengthening propagation rule, we greatly simplify the construction of a family of locally repairable codes in [ 25 , Theorem 5] that breaks the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound. In addition, we make use of three other propagation rules to produce more dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes. Finally, one of phenomena that we observe in this paper is that some trivial propagation rules in classical block codes do not hold anymore for locally repairable codes.

A locally repairable code is just a block code with an additional parameter called locality.

1.1.
Known Results

The construction of optimal locally repairable codes is of both theoretical interest and practical importance. A class of codes constructed earlier and known as pyramid codes
[
15
]
are shown to be optimal locally repairable codes. In
[
32
]
, Silberstein

proposed a two-level construction based on the Gabidulin codes combined with a single parity-check

[
36

used two layers of MDS codes, a Reed-Solomon code and a special
(r+1,r)

[

with alphabet size comparable to code length. However, the rate of the code is very close to

.

A remarkable construction of optimal locally repairable codes via subcodes of Reed-Solomon codes was given by Tamo and Barg in
[

. There are some constraints on choice of locality due to the existence of good polynomials, and the length is upper bounded by the code alphabet size

q+1

As for optimal locally repairable codes with small distances, the length of optimal locally repairable codes could be much larger than the alphabet size

Arbitrary long optimal locally repairable codes can be constructed by cyclic codes for distance

k\leq\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\{tr+k_{opt}^{(q)}(n-(r+1)t,d)\},

1.2.
Our Contributions and Techniques

Among these constructions, propagation rules have not been extensively explored although various propagation rules have been discovered in classical coding theory.
The current paper makes a step towards constructing locally repairable codes by exploring various propagation rules.
This paper makes the following six-fold contributions:

(i)

give two main propagation rules together with three other propagation rules for constructions of locally repairable codes. In addition, we show that some trivial propagation rules in classical block codes do not hold anymore for locally repairable codes;

(ii)

construct many dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes based on the first main propagation rule, i.e., concatenating a locally repairable code as an inner code with a classical block code as an outer code. More dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes are produced via minor propagation rules based on the aforementioned dimension-optimal locally repairable codes;

(iii)

by concatenating locally repairable codes with algebraic geometry codes via the first main propagation rule, we are able to break the Zyablov-type bound for locally repairable codes;

(iv)

produce a family of dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes from extended Hamming codes based on the second main propagation rule, i.e., add certain rows and columns to a parity-check matrix of a given linear code.

(v)

convert Reed-Solomon codes into Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes via the second main propagation rule;

(vi)

greatly simplify the construction of a family of locally repairable codes given in
[
25
, Theorem 5]
that breaks the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound.

1.3.
Organization

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some preliminaries including linear codes, algebraic geometry codes and locally repairable codes. In Section
3
, we introduce the first main propagation rule together with three other propagation rules, and then present many dimension-optimal locally repairable codes. In addition, we show that some trivial propagation rules for classical block codes do not hold anymore for locally repairable codes. Furthermore, we explicitly construct a family of locally repairable codes that exceed the Zyablov-type bound. In Section
4
, we produce a family of dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes from extended Hamming codes via the second main propagation rule, and convert a classical MDS code into a Singleton-optimal locally repairable code. Finally, we simplify a construction of a family of locally repairable codes which can break the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some preliminaries on the theory of linear codes, algebraic geometry codes and locally repairable codes.

2.1.
Linear codes

In this subsection, we briefly discuss linear codes. The reader may refer to
[
22
,
27
]
for more details.
Let
q
be a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be the vector space of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
A linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
The dimension of
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
as a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Any element in
\mathcal{C}
is called a codeword.
The support of a codeword
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})\in C
is defined by
\text{supp}({\bf u})=\{i\in[n]:u_{i}\neq 0\},
where
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}.

The Hamming weight
\text{wt}(\bf u)
of
{\bf u}
is defined to be the size of
\text{supp}({\bf u}).

If
\mathcal{C}\neq\{0\}
, then the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be the smallest Hamming weight of nonzero codewords in
\mathcal{C}
.

A
q
-ary linear code with length
n
, dimension
k
and minimum distance
d
is denoted as an
[n,k,d]_{q}
-linear code.
From the Singleton bound
[
22
, Theorem 5.4.1]
, we have the following inequality

(2)

d\leq n-k+1.

A linear code with minimum distance achieving this Singleton bound (
2
) is called an maximum distance separable code (MDS code for short).
The dual
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
of any code
\mathcal{C}
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}:=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:\langle{\bf x},{\bf c}\rangle=0\text{ for any }{\bf c}\in\mathcal{C}\},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the canonical Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.

2.2.
Algebraic geometry codes

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field with genus
g
over the full constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{F}
denote the set of places of
F
. Any place of
F
with degree one is called rational.
For a divisor
G
of function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we define the Riemann-Roch space by

\mathcal{L}(G):=\{u\in F^{*}:\;(u)+G\geqslant 0\}\cup\{0\}.

From Riemann’s theorem,
\mathcal{L}(G)
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and its dimension
\ell(G)
is lower bounded by
\ell(G)\geqslant\deg(G)-g+1
. Moreover, the equality holds true if
\deg(G)\geqslant 2g-1
from
[
33
, Theorem 1.5.17]
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
be a set of distinct rational places of
F
.
For a special divisor
G
of
F
with
0<\deg(G)<n
and
{\rm supp}(G)\cap\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
, the algebraic geometry code associated with
\mathcal{P}
and
G
is defined to be

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{P},G):=\{(f(P_{1}),f(P_{2}),\dots,f(P_{n})):\;f\in\mathcal{L}(G)\}.

Then
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{P},G)
is an
[n,k,d]_{q}
-linear code with dimension
k=\ell(G)
and minimum distance
d\geqslant n-\deg(G)
from
[
33
, Theorem 2.2.2]
.

Let
N_{q}(g)
be the maximum number of rational places of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with genus
g
. The real number
A(q)
defined by
A(q):=\limsup_{g\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N_{q}(g)}{g}
is called the Ihara’s constant. If
q
is a square, then
A(q)=\sqrt{q}-1

[
10
]
.
The famous Tsfaman-Vlăduţ-Zink bound (TVZ bound for short) can be found from
[
33
, Theorem 8.4.7]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
q=\ell^{2}
be a square of a prime power. Then for all
\delta
with
0\leqslant\delta\leqslant 1-(\ell-1)^{-1}
, there exists a family of algebraic geometry codes such that its information rate
R
and relative minimum distance
\delta
satisfy

R\geqslant 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}-1}-\delta.

2.3.
Locally repairable codes

Roughly speaking, a block code is said with locality
r
if every coordinate of any given codeword can be recovered by accessing at most
r
other coordinates of this codeword.
A formal definition of a locally repairable code with locality
r
can be given as follows.

Definition 1
.

A
q
-ary code of length
n
is called a locally repairable code (LRC for short) with locality
r
if for any
i\in[n],
there exists a subset
R\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i\}
of size
r
such that for any
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C},

c_{i}
can be recovered by
\{c_{j}\}_{j\in R},
i.e., for any
i\in[n],
there exists a subset
R\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i\}
of size
r
such that for any
{\bf u,v}\in\mathcal{C},{\bf u}_{R\cup\{i\}}={\bf v}_{R\cup\{i\}}
if and only if
{\bf u}_{R}={\bf v}_{R}.
The set
R\cup\{i\}
is called a recovery set of
i.
Note that we include
i
in the recovery set for convenience.

In this paper, we always consider linear locally repairable codes. Thus, a linear locally repairable code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
, dimension
k
, minimum distance
d
and locality
r
is denoted to be an
[n,k,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code.
For such a
q
-ary
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code, the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is upper bounded by

(3)

d\leqslant n-k-\left\lceil\frac{k}{r}\right\rceil+2.

A code achieving this bound (
3
) is usually called an optimal locally repairable code. However, we refer it as a Singleton-optimal locally repairable code in this paper.

Recovery sets of a linear locally repairable code can be characterized by its dual code from
[
12
, Lemma 5]
. The precise result is given as below.

Lemma 2.2
.

A subset
R\subseteq[n]
containing
i
is a recovery set at
i
for a
q
-ary linear code of length
n
if and only if there exists a codeword
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
such that
i\in{\rm supp}({\bf c})\subseteq R.

For a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
and
r\geqslant 1
, we define the set

\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{C}}(r):=\{{\rm supp}({\bf c}):\;{\bf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp},\;|{\rm supp}({\bf c})|\leqslant r+1\}.

The following result is a straightforward corollary of Lemma
2.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
has locality
r
if and only if
[n]=\cup_{I\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{C}}(r)}I
.

In this paper, we will also study the asymptotic behavior of locally repairable codes. Let the locality
r
and alphabet size
q
be fixed, and let the dimension and minimum distance be proportional to the length
n
.
Let
R_{q}(r,\delta)
denote the asymptotic bound on the rate of
q
-ary locally repairable codes with locality
r
and relative minimum distance
\delta
, i.e.,

R_{q}(r,\delta)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log_{q}M_{q}(n,\lfloor\delta n\rfloor,r)}{n},

where
M_{q}(n,d,r)
is the maximum size of locally repairable codes of length
n
, minimum distance
d
and locality
r
.

For
0\leqslant\delta\leqslant 1-q^{-1}
, the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound of locally repairable codes is given in
[
35
]
by

R_{q}(r,\delta)\geqslant 1-\min_{0<s\leqslant 1}\Big\{\frac{1}{r+1}\log_{q}\Big([1+(q-1)s]^{r+1}+(q-1)(1-s)^{r+1}\Big)-\delta\log_{q}s\Big\}.

Barg
et al.

[
1
]
gave a construction of asymptotically good
q
-ary locally repairable codes with locality
r
whose rate
R
and relative distance
\delta
satisfy

(4)

R\geqslant\frac{r}{r+1}\Big(1-\delta-\frac{3}{\sqrt{q}+1}\Big),\quad r=\sqrt{q}-1,

and

(5)

R\geqslant\frac{r}{r+1}\Big(1-\delta-\frac{\sqrt{q}+r}{q-1}\Big),\quad(r+1)|(\sqrt{q}+1).

Furthermore, it was shown in
[
1
]
that for some values
r
and
q
, the bound (
5
) exceeds the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound for locally repairable codes.
Li
et al.

[
21
]
generalized the idea given in
[
1
]
by considering more subgroups of automorphism groups of function fields in the Garcia-Stichtenoth tower
[
10
]
. This construction allows more flexibility of locality. In particular, if
r+1=up^{v}
with
u|\gcd(p^{v}-1,\sqrt{q}-1)
, then there exists a family of explicit
q
-ary linear locally repairable codes with locality
r
whose rate
R
and relative distance
\delta
satisfy

(6)

R\geqslant\frac{r}{1+r}\Big(1-\delta-\frac{\sqrt{q}+r-1}{q-\sqrt{q}}\Big).

In order to overcome the restrictions on alphabet size
q
and locality
r
, the authors
[
25
]
provided an explicit construction via parity-check matrices whose columns are formed by coefficients of local expansions of function fields in the Garcia-Stichtenoth tower. In particular, for any fixed
q
and
r
, it has been proved in
[
25
]
that

(7)

R\geqslant\frac{r}{r+1}-\frac{1}{A(q)}\times\frac{r}{r+1}-\delta.

3.
Locally Repairable Codes via Concatenations

In this section, we present the first main propagation rule together with three other propagation rules and construct many dimension-optimal locally repairable codes. In addition, we show that some trivial propagation rules for classical block codes do not hold anymore for locally repairable codes.

3.1.
Concatenations and dimension-optimal locally repairable codes

Let us start with the first main propagation rule by concatenating a locally repairable code as an inner code with a classical block code as an outer code.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the inner code
\mathcal{C}_{in}
be a
q
-ary
[n_{1},k_{1},d_{1};r]
-locally repairable code and let the outer code
\mathcal{C}_{out}
be a
q^{k_{1}}
-ary
[n_{2},k_{2},d_{2}]
-linear code, then one can concatenate
\mathcal{C}_{in}
with
\mathcal{C}_{out}
to obtain an
[n_{1}n_{2},k_{1}k_{2},\geqslant d_{1}d_{2};r]_{q}
-locally repairable code
\mathcal{C}_{conc}
.

Proof.

Fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space isomorphism
\varphi
between
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k_{1}}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{in}.
Define
\mathcal{C}_{conc}
by

\mathcal{C}_{conc}=\{(\varphi(c_{1}),\varphi(c_{2}),\dots,\varphi(c_{n_{2}})):\;(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}_{out}\}.

Then
\mathcal{C}_{conc}
is an
[n_{1}n_{2},k_{1}k_{2},\geqslant d_{1}d_{2}]_{q}
-linear code from
[
22
, Theorem 6.3.1]
. Every position of a codeword of
\mathcal{C}_{conc}
can be determined by
r
other positions due to the fact that the inner code
\mathcal{C}_{in}
has locality
r
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}_{conc}
has locality
r
.
∎

Although the concatenation technique is simple, it is quite powerful. By concatenating locally repairable codes with classical block codes, we can construct many good binary linear locally repairable codes that achieve the upper bound given in
[
37
, Theorem 6]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any binary
[n,k,d;r]
-locally repairable code with locality
r
such that
d\geqslant 5
and
2\leqslant r\leqslant\frac{n}{2}-2,
it follows that

(8)

k\leqslant\left\lfloor\frac{rn}{r+1}-\min\left\{\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{rn}{2}\right),\frac{rn}{(r+1)(r+2)}\right\}\right\rfloor.

A binary linear locally repairable code is called dimension-optimal if its dimension achieves the bound (
8
) in Lemma
3.2
.
Based on our concatenation given in Theorem
3.1
, we can provide many dimension-optimal binary locally repairable codes.

\mathcal{C}_{in}
be a single parity-check

be a

-locally repairable code. On the other hand, the bound (

) in Lemma

-locally repairable code must obey

-locally repairable code is dimension-optimal.

Some classical propagation rules given in
[
22
, Theorem 6.1.1]
can be generalized to construct new locally repairable codes from old ones.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
[n,k,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code, then

(i)

there exists an
[n+1,k,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code;

(ii)

there exists an
[n-1,\geqslant k-1,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code;

(iii)

there exists an
[n-t,\geqslant k-t+s,\geqslant d-s;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code for any
0\leqslant s\leqslant t
, provided that
\mathcal{C}
has disjoint recovery sets and one of the recovery sets has size
t
.

Proof.

(i) By adding
0
to the
(n+1)
-th position of every codeword of
\mathcal{C}
, one gets an
[n+1,k,d]_{q}
-linear code. For the
(n+1)
-th position, we have a recovery set
\{n+1\}
. Thus, the new code has locality
r
as well.

(ii) Let
H
be a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. From
[
22
, Theorem 4.5.6]
, there are
d
columns of
H
which are linearly dependent. Without loss of generality, assume that the last column of
H
is not in these
d
columns. We delete the last column of
H
to form a
(n-k)\times(n-1)
matrix
H_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
be the code with
H_{1}
as its parity-check matrix. It is clear that
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is an
[n-1,\geqslant k-1,d]_{q}
-linear code. To obtain the locality, for each
i\in[n-1]
, there exists a codeword
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
such that
i\in{\rm supp}({\bf u})
and
|{\rm supp}({\bf u})|\leqslant r+1
. Let
{\bf u}_{1}
be the vector obtained from
{\bf u}
by deleting the last position. Then
{\bf u}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}
,
i\in{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})
and
|{\rm supp}({\bf u}_{1})|\leqslant r+1
. This implies that the position
i
has a recovery set of size at most
r+1
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is an
[n-1,\geqslant k-1,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code.

(iii) Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\{n-t+1,n-t+2,\dots,n\}
is a recovery set of size
t
. Let
H
be a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. We delete the last
t-s
columns of
H
to form an
(n-k)\times(n-t+s)
matrix
H_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be the code with
H_{2}
as its parity-check matrix. It is clear that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is an
[n-t+s,\geqslant k-t+s,\geqslant d]_{q}
-linear code. As in (ii), we can show that
\mathcal{C}_{2}
has locality
r
as well. Furthermore,
\{n-t+1,n-t+2,\cdots,n-t+s\}
is a recovery set that is disjoint with other recovery sets. Now we delete the last
s

\mathcal{C}_{2}
to obtain
\mathcal{C}_{3}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}_{3}
is an
[n-t,\geqslant k-t+s,\geqslant d-s]_{q}
-linear code. As a whole recovery set of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is deleted,
\mathcal{C}_{3}
has disjoint recovery sets with each size being at most
r+1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{3}
has locality
r
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For classical block codes, we have the following propagation rules: (i) an
[n,k,d]_{q}
-linear code gives an
[n-1,k,d-1]_{q}
-linear code;
(ii) an
[n,k,d]_{q}
-linear code gives an
[n,k,d-1]_{q}
-linear code. However, these two propagation rules does not hold anymore. Namely, (i) an
[n,k,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code does not always produce an
[n-1,k,d-1;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code;
(ii) an
[n,k,d;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code does not always produce an
[n,k,d-1;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code. To see this, let us give two counter-examples.

. This is a contradiction.

[5,4,2;4]
-locally repairable code. As we do not have a locally repairable code with minimum distance
1
, we have no way to get a
[5,4,1;4]
-locally repairable code.

3.2.
Zyablov-type bound

In classical coding theory, in order to obtain an explicit asymptotic bound, one can concatenate a family of linear codes achieving the Gilbert-Varshamov bound as an inner code and a Reed-Solomon code as an outer code. The explicit asymptotic bound obtained in this way is called the Zyablov bound. In this subsection, we will explore the same technique to obtain the Zyablov-type bound for locally repairable codes.

Consider the inner code
\mathcal{C}_{\rm in}
to be an
[n_{1},k_{1},d_{1};r]_{2}
-locally repairable code that achieves the Gilbert-Varshamov bound, and the outer code
\mathcal{C}_{\rm out}
to be an
[n_{2},k_{2},d_{2}=n_{2}-k_{2}+1]_{q}
Reed-Solomon code with
q=2^{k_{1}}
. The concatenated code
\mathcal{C}_{\rm conc}
is a binary
[n_{1}n_{2},k_{1}k_{2},\geqslant d_{1}d_{2};r]
-locally repairable code from Theorem
3.1
. In particular, its rate satisfies

(9)

R=\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}\times\frac{k_{2}}{n_{2}}=\left(1-h\left(r,\frac{d_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)+o(1)\right)\left(1-\frac{d_{2}}{n_{2}}\right),

where

(10)

h(r,x):=\min_{0<s\leqslant 1}\Big\{\frac{1}{r+1}\log_{q}\Big([1+(q-1)s]^{r+1}+(q-1)(1-s)^{r+1}\Big)-x\log_{q}s\Big\}.

Put
\tau=\frac{d_{1}}{n_{1}}
and
\delta=\frac{d_{1}d_{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}
. Then we have
\frac{d_{2}}{n_{2}}=\frac{\delta}{\tau}
. Substituting
\frac{d_{1}}{n_{1}}=\tau
and

\frac{d_{2}}{n_{2}}=\frac{\delta}{\tau}
into Equation (
9
), we obtain the following Zyablov-type bound for locally repairable codes.

Theorem 3.7
.

(Zyablov-type bound)
For given integer
r\geqslant 1
and real
\delta\in(0,1)
,
there exists a family of binary locally repairable codes of rate
R
, relative minimum distance
\delta
and locality
r
satisfying

\displaystyle R=\max_{\delta\leq\tau\leq 1}(1-\delta/\tau)(1-h(r,\tau)).

Furthermore, this family of locally repairable codes can be constructed in polynomial time.

Remark 1
.

The Zyablov-type bound is worse than the Gilbert-Varshamov bound, since
h(r,\tau)
is a decreasing function in the variable
\tau
and

\max_{\delta\leq\tau\leq 1}(1-\delta/\tau)(1-h(r,\tau))\leqslant\max_{\delta\leq\tau\leq 1}(1-h(r,\tau))\leqslant 1-h(r,\delta).

4.
Locally repairable codes via lengthening parity-check matrices

In this section, we try to construct good locally repairable codes based on linear codes from a lengthening propagation rule.
In particular, the technique is to endow the locality by adding rows and columns in the parity-check matrix of linear codes.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
be an
[n,k,d]
-linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
H_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times n}
be its parity-check matrix.
Let
r
be a positive integer. There exist integers
m
and
s
such that
n=mr+s
with
1\leqslant s\leqslant r
.
We divide the columns of
H_{0}
into
\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
blocks.
Let
{\bf h}_{ij}
be the columns of
H_{0}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m,1\leqslant j\leqslant r
and
i=m+1,1\leqslant j\leqslant s
, i.e.,

H_{0}=({\bf h}_{11},\cdots,{\bf h}_{1r},{\bf h}_{21},\cdots,{\bf h}_{2r},\cdots,{\bf h}_{m1},\cdots,{\bf h}_{mr},{\bf h}_{m+1,1},\cdots,{\bf h}_{m+1,s}).

Now let us consider the matrix
H
defined by

(11)

H:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|c|cccc|cccc}1&\cdots&1&1&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&1&\cdots&1&1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&1&\cdots&1&1\\
{\bf h}_{11}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{1r}&{\bf 0}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{mr}&{\bf 0}&{\bf h}_{m+1,1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m+1,s}&{\bf 0}\end{array}\right),

where
{\bf 0}
stands for the zero vector of dimension
n-k
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H
be the matrix defined in Equation (
11
). Then any
d-1
columns of
H
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Let
{\bf c}_{i,j}
denote the
j
-th column in the
i
-th block of the parity-check matrix
H
.
Choose arbitrary
d-1
columns
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
from
H
, where
S_{i}
is a nonempty subset of
\{1,2,\cdots,r+1\}
with
\sum_{i\in I}|S_{i}|=d-1
.

Case 1:
the last column in each block does not belong to
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
, i.e.,
r+1\notin S_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
and
s+1\notin S_{m+1}
.
Since
H_{0}
is a parity-check matrix of an
[n,k,d]
-linear code, any
d-1
columns of
H_{0}
are linearly independent.
Hence, any
d-1
columns
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
are linearly independent from linear algebra.

Case 2:
the last column of some blocks are chosen. Without loss of generality, assume that
r+1\in S_{i_{0}}
for some
1\leqslant i_{0}\leqslant m
.
In this case, we can prove a stronger result. That is, arbitrary
d
columns
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
are linearly independent. Assume that there exist
\lambda_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

\sum_{i_{0}\neq i\in I}\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{ij}{\bf c}_{ij}+\sum_{j\in S_{i_{0}}}\lambda_{i_{0},j}{\bf c}_{i_{0},j}={\bf 0}.

From the first
\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
rows of
H
, we have
\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}=0
for
i\in I
.
It follows that

(12)

\lambda_{i_{0},r+1}=-\sum_{j\in S_{i_{0}}\setminus\{r+1\}}\lambda_{i_{0},j}.

Then we have

\sum_{i_{0}\neq i\in I}\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{ij}{\bf c}_{ij}+\sum_{j\in S_{i_{0}}\setminus\{r+1\}}\lambda_{i_{0},j}({\bf c}_{i_{0},j}-{\bf c}_{i_{0},r+1})={\bf 0}.

By focusing on the last
n-k
rows of
H
, we have

\sum_{i_{0}\neq i\in I}\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{ij}{\bf h}_{ij}+\sum_{j\in S_{i_{0}}\setminus\{r+1\}}\lambda_{i_{0},j}{\bf h}_{i_{0},j}={\bf 0}.

Since any
d-1
columns of
H_{0}
are linearly independent, we have
\lambda_{i,j}=0
for
i_{0}\neq i\in I,j\in S_{i}
and
i=i_{0},j\in S_{i_{0}}\setminus\{r+1\}
.
From Equation (
12
), we have
\lambda_{i_{0},r+1}=0
. Hence, any
d
columns are linearly independent in this case.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the code with
H
given in Equation (
11
) as its parity-check matrix. Then
\mathcal{C}
is an
[n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil,k,\geqslant d;r]
-locally repairable code.

Proof.

Since
H
is an
(n-k+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil)\times(n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil)
matrix and the rows of
H
are linearly independent from linear algebra, the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
is
(n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil)-(n-k+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil)=k
.
From Lemma
4.1
, any
d-1
columns of
H
are linearly independent.
Hence, the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is at least
d
from
[
22
, Theorem 4.5.6]
.
Moreover, the locality of
\mathcal{C}
is
r
from the parity-check matrix
H
and Corollary
2.3
.

∎

In order to increase minimum distance of locally repairable codes constructed in Proposition

, we can employ the additional property of parity-check matrices of extended Hamming codes. In this subsection, we propose a construction of dimension-optimal locally repairable codes via lengthening extended Hamming codes.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
be a binary
[2^{t},2^{t}-1-t,4]
-extended Hamming code for
t\geqslant 3
. Let
H_{0}
be a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
and let
H
be the matrix given in Equation (
11
). Then, for
r=2
or
3
, the code
\mathcal{C}
with
H
as a parity-check matrix is a binary
[2^{t}+\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{r}\rceil,2^{t}-1-t,5;r]
-locally repairable code. Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}
achieves the upper bound (
8
) if
(r=2,t\geqslant 5)
or
(r=3,t\geqslant 5)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is dimension-optimal in these cases.

Proof.

Let us first show that
\mathcal{C}
is a
[2^{t}+\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{r}\rceil,2^{t}-1-t,5;r]
-locally repairable code. By Proposition
4.2
, it is sufficient to show that any four columns of
H
are linearly independent. Let us prove this only for the case where
r=3
.

Case 1: all four columns belong to the same block, say the first block. In this case, we have to show that
(1,{\bf 0},{\bf 0})^{T}
,
(1,{\bf 0},{\bf h}_{11})^{T}
,
(1,{\bf 0},{\bf h}_{12})^{T}
and
(1,{\bf 0},{\bf h}_{13})^{T}
are linearly independent. Suppose that

(13)

\lambda_{0}(1,{\bf 0},{\bf 0})^{T}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}(1,{\bf 0},{\bf h}_{1i})^{T}={\bf 0}^{T}

for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
0\leqslant i\leqslant 3
. Then we have
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}{\bf h}_{1i}={\bf 0}
. This forces that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0
from
[
22
, Theorem 4.5.6]
, since
\mathcal{C}_{0}
has minimum distance
4
. Thus, it follows from (
13
) that
\lambda_{0}=0
.

Case 2: Other possibilities: (a) three columns belong to one block and one column belongs to another block; (b) two columns belong to one block and other two belong to another block; (c) two columns belong to one block and other two columns belong to other two blocks, respectively; (d) four columns belong to four distinct blocks. For these four possibilities, one can use a similar argument to show that they are linearly independent. This proves the first part.

Now if
r=2
and
t\geqslant 5
, we want to show that

(14)

k=2^{t}-1-t\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{rn}{r+1}-\min\left\{\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{rn}{2}\right),\frac{rn}{(r+1)(r+2)}\right\}\right\rfloor

with
r=2
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}
.
To show (
14
), it will be sufficient to show the following two inequalities

(15)

2^{t}-t>\frac{rn}{r+1}-\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{rn}{2}\right)

and

(16)

2^{t}-t>\frac{rn}{r+1}-\frac{rn}{(r+1)(r+2)}.

Substituting
r=2
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}
into (
15
) and rewrite it into the following form

(17)

\log_{2}\left(1+2^{t}+2^{t-1}\right)>t.

It is clear that (
17
) holds and hence (
15
) holds.

Substituting
r=2
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}
into (
16
) and rewrite it into the following form

(18)

2^{t}-t>\frac{r}{r+2}\times n=2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}.

The inequality (
18
) holds when
t\geqslant 5
and hence (
16
) holds. This completes the proof for the case where
r=2
and
t\geqslant 5
.

Next assume that
r=3
and
t\geqslant 5
. In this case we want to show that the two inequalities (
15
) and (
16
)
hold for
r=3
and
n=2^{t}+\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\rceil
.

Substituting
r=3
and
n=2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil
into (
15
) and rewrite it into the following form

(19)

\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\left(2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil\right)\right)>t+\frac{3}{4}\times\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil-\frac{2^{t}}{4}.

Note that we have
1+\frac{3}{2}\left(2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil\right)>\frac{3}{2}\times 2^{t}>\sqrt{2}\times 2^{t}
. This gives

\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\left(2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil\right)\right)>t+\frac{1}{2}=t+\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{2^{t}+2}{3}-\frac{2^{t}}{4}\geqslant t+\frac{3}{4}\times\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil-\frac{2^{t}}{4},

i.e., the inequality (
19
) holds and hence (
15
) holds.

Substituting
r=3
and
n=2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil
into (
16
) and rewrite it into the following form

(20)

2^{t}-t>\frac{r}{r+2}\times n=\frac{3}{5}\left(2^{t}+\left\lceil\frac{2^{t}}{3}\right\rceil\right)

The inequality (
20
) holds when
t\geqslant 5
and hence (
16
) holds. This completes the proof for the case where
r=3
and
t\geqslant 5
.
∎

.
Let
d\geqslant 2
be a positive integer.
Let
H_{0}
be a
(d-1)\times n
matrix consisting of columns
{\bf h}_{ij}=(\alpha_{ij},\alpha_{ij}^{2},\cdots,\alpha_{ij}^{d-1})^{T}
with pairwise distinct
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
and
i=m+1,1\leqslant j\leqslant s
.
Any
d-1
columns of
H_{0}
are linearly independent, since the determinant of Vandermonde matrix is nonzero.
Let
H_{1}
be a
d\times n
matrix given as follows:

H_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}1&\cdots&1&\cdots&1&\cdots&1&1&\cdots&1\\
{\bf h}_{11}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{1r}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{mr}&{\bf h}_{m+1,1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m+1,s}\end{array}\right).

It is easy to see that
H_{1}
is a Vandermonde matrix which is a generator matrix of some Reed-Solomon code.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
be the code with
H_{1}
as its parity-check matrix. Then
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is an
[n,n-d,d+1]
MDS code.
We split the first row of
H_{1}
into
\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
rows and add a new column in each block of
H_{1}
as follows:

(21)

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|c|cccc|cccc}1&\cdots&1&1&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&1&\cdots&1&1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&0&1&\cdots&1&1\\
{\bf h}_{11}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{1r}&{\bf 0}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{mr}&{\bf 0}&{\bf h}_{m+1,1}&\cdots&{\bf h}_{m+1,s}&{\bf 0}\end{array}\right),

where
{\bf 0}
stands for the zero vector of dimension
d-1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Any
d
columns of
H
given in Equation (
21
) are linearly independent.

Proof.

Let
{\bf c}_{i,j}
be the
j
-th column in the
i
-th block of the parity-check matrix
H
.
Choose any
d
columns
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
from
H
, where
S_{i}
is a nonempty subset of
\{1,2,\cdots,r+1\}
with
\sum_{i\in I}|S_{i}|=d
.

Case 1:
the last column in each block does not belong to
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
, i.e.,
r+1\notin S_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
and
s+1\notin S_{m+1}
.
Assume that there exist
\lambda_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{ij}{\bf c}_{i,j}={\bf 0}.

It follows that
\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}=0
for all
i\in I
from the parity-check matrix
H
, which imply that
\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in S_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}=0
.
Thus, we have the following system of linear equations

\sum_{i\in I,j\in S_{i}}\alpha_{ij}^{t}\lambda_{i,j}=0\text{ for }0\leqslant t\leqslant d-1.

The coefficient matrix of the above system of linear equations is a Vandermonde matrix in the variables
\{\alpha_{i,j}:i\in I,j\in S_{i}\}
. Since
\alpha_{i,j}
are pairwise distinct, the determinant of the coefficient matrix is nonzero. Hence, such an system of linear equations has a unique solution
\lambda_{i,j}=0
for
i\in I
and
j\in S_{i}
.

Case 2:
the last column of some blocks are contained in
\{{\bf c}_{i,j}:1\leqslant i\leqslant m+1,j\in S_{i}\}
.
We have already proved that any
d
columns of
H
are linearly independent for this case in Lemma
4.1
. So we omit the details here.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the code with
H
given in Equation (
21
) as its parity-check matrix. Then
\mathcal{C}
is an
[n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil,n-d+1,\geqslant d+1;r]
-locally repairable code.

Proof.

The parity-check matrix
H
is a
(\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil+d-1)\times(n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil)
matrix and the rows of
H
are linearly independent from linear algebra. Hence, the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
is
n-d+1
.
From Lemma
4.4
and
[
22
, Theorem 4.5.6]
, the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is at least
d+1
. Finally, the locality of
\mathcal{C}
is
r
from the parity-check matrix
H
and Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
r
be a positive integer. Let
n\leqslant q-1
be a positive integer such that
n=mr+s
with
1\leqslant s\leqslant r
and let
d\leqslant s
be a positive integer. Then there exists a Singleton-optimal
[n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil,n-d+1,d+1;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle n+\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil-(n-d+1)-\left\lceil\frac{n-d+1}{r}\right\rceil+2

\displaystyle=d+1+\left\lceil\frac{n}{r}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{n-d+1}{r}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=d+1+\left\lceil\frac{mr+s}{r}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\frac{mr+s-d+1}{r}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=d+1\leqslant d(\mathcal{C}).

Hence,
\mathcal{C}
is a Singleton-optimal
[n+\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil,n-d+1,d+1;r]_{q}
-locally repairable code.
∎

Remark 4
.

From Theorem
4.6
, we can explicitly construct a new family of Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes with length up to

, which is larger than
q
compared with

,

d

r

In this case, the length of such Singleton-optimal locally repairable codes is less than the

4.3.
Asymptotic bounds of locally repairable codes

In
[
25
]
, the authors gave a very technical and complicated method to obtain an asymptotic bound exceeding the Gilbert-Varshamov bound for locally repairable codes via local expansions of carefully chosen functions in the Garcia-Stichtenoth tower. In this subsection, we provide a much simpler proof for such an asymptotic bound given in
[
25
, Theorem 5]
via lengthening algebraic geometry codes.

Let
r
be a fixed positive integer.
Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a family of
q
-ary
[n_{i},k_{i},d_{i}]
-linear codes with information rate and relative minimum distance
R_{1}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}
and
\delta_{1}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{n_{i}}.

From Proposition
4.2
, there exists a family of
[n_{i}+\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{r}\rceil,k_{i},\geqslant d_{i};r]
-locally repairable codes.
Then the information rate of this family of locally repairable codes is

R=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}+\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{r}\rceil}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{i}}{n_{i}}\times\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}+\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{r}\rceil}=\frac{r}{r+1}R_{1}

and its relative minimum distance is

\delta=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{n_{i}+\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{r}\rceil}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d_{i}}{n_{i}}\times\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}+\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{r}\rceil}=\frac{r}{r+1}\delta_{1}.

Combining with the classical Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
33
, Proposition 8.4.4]
,
we have the following result.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
H_{q}(x)
be the
q
-ary entropy function
H_{q}(x):=x\log_{q}(q-1)-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x).
Then there exists a family of
q
-ary locally repairable codes with locality
r
whose information rate
R
and relative minimum distance

\delta
satisfy

R\geqslant\frac{r}{r+1}\left(1-H_{q}\Big(\frac{r+1}{r}\delta\Big)\right).

Combining with the TVZ bound of algebraic geometry codes
[
33
, Proposition 8.4.7]
, we have

\frac{r+1}{r}R\geqslant 1-\frac{1}{A(q)}-\frac{r+1}{r}\delta.

Hence, we have shown the following result which is the same as
[
25
, Theorem 5]
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
A(q)
be the Ihara’s constant.
Then there exists a family of
q
-ary locally repairable codes with locality
r
whose information rate
R
and relative minimum distance

\delta
satisfy

(22)

R\geqslant\frac{r}{r+1}-\frac{1}{A(q)}\times\frac{r}{r+1}-\delta.

Figure 3.
q=2^{12},r=61

The Figure
3
shows that the bound given in Theorem
4.9
can exceed the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound of locally repairable codes for
q=2^{12},r=61
.
Hence, we greatly simplify the bound given in
[
25
, Theorem 5]
that breaks the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov bound for locally repairable codes.

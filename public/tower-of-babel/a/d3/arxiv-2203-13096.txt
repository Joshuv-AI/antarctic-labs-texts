The essential norm of multiplication operators on L p ​ ( μ ) L_{p}(\mu)
Jürgen Voigt

Abstract
We show that the formula for the essential norm of a multiplication operator on L p L_{p} , for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , also holds for p = 1 p=1 . We also provide a proof for the formula which works simultaneously for all p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) . MSC 2010: 47B38, 46E30, 46B42. Keywords: Multiplication operator, L p L_{p} -space, compact operator, essential norm.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space, and let
1\leqslant p<\infty
. For
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
let
M_{u}
be the bounded multiplication operator on
L_{p}(\mu)
defined by

M_{u}f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=uf\qquad(f\in L_{p}(\mu)).

Compactness properties of multiplication operators in various function spaces have been investigated in several papers; see
[
14
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
. It is only in the recent paper
[
2
]
that the
essential norm

\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\bigl\{\|M_{u}+K\|{;}\penalty\;K\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(\mu)\bigr\},

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(\mu))
denotes the space of compact operators on
L_{p}(\mu)
, has been determined, for
1<p<\infty
. (The essential norm
\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}
is the quotient norm in the Calkin algebra.) In order to describe this result we recall that the measure space can be decomposed as a disjoint union
\Omega=\Omega_{d}\cup\Omega_{a}
, where
\Omega_{d},\Omega_{a}\in\mathcal{A}
, the restriction
\mu_{d}
of
\mu
to
\Omega_{d}
is a diffuse measure, and the restriction
\mu_{a}
of
\mu
to
\Omega_{a}
is (purely) atomic. The property of being diffuse means that for every measurable subset
A
of
\Omega_{d}
with
\mu_{d}(A)>0
there exists a measurable subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
such that
0<\mu_{d}(A^{\prime})<\mu_{d}(A)
. And the atomic part
\Omega_{a}
is the union of a disjoint sequence
(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
of measurable sets, where each
B_{n}
is an atom, which means that any measurable subset
B^{\prime}
of
B_{n}
has measure
\mu_{a}(B^{\prime})\in\{0,\mu_{a}(B_{n})\}
. With this notation, the essential norm of
M_{u}
is given by

\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}=\max\{\|u{\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu}_{\Omega_{d}}\|_{\infty},\limsup_{n\to\infty}|u(B_{n})|\}.

(1.2)

(By
u(B_{n})
we denote the a.e.-value of
u
on
B_{n}
; if
\mu_{a}(B_{n})=0
we choose
u(B_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
.) The proof of (
1.2
) given in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
does not carry over to the case
p=1
.

In Section
2
we show that (
1.2
) also holds for
p=1
.
In Sections
3
and
4
we provide a second – quite different – proof, which works simultaneously for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.

2
The essential norm of
M_{u}

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space, and let
\Omega=\Omega_{d}\cup\Omega_{a}
and
\Omega_{a}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}B_{n}
be as described above.

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
, and let
M_{u}
be the multiplication operator associated with
u
on
L_{1}(\mu)
. Then
\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}
is given by (
1.2
).

Proof.

(i) For the inequality ‘
\leqslant
’ in (
1.2
) we refer to
[
2
, first part of the proof of Theorem 4.1]
.

(ii) For the proof of ‘
\geqslant
’ we first note that in the infimum of the formula (
1.1
) (where in the present step we treat the general case
p\in[1,\infty)
)
one does not need all compact operators, but it is sufficient to consider operators leaving
L_{p}(\Omega_{d})
and
L_{p}(\Omega_{a})
invariant. Indeed, let
P_{d}
and
P_{a}
denote the canonical projections from
L_{p}(\mu)

onto
L_{p}(\Omega_{d},\mu_{d})
and
L_{p}(\Omega_{a},\mu_{a})
, respectively. Then for any bounded operator
S
on
L_{p}(\mu)
one has
\|(P_{d}-P_{a})S(P_{d}-P_{a})\|\leqslant\|S\|
, and because of

P_{d}SP_{d}+P_{a}SP_{a}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl((P_{d}+P_{a})S(P_{d}+P_{a})+(P_{d}-P_{a})S(P_{d}-P_{a})\bigr)

one obtains
\|P_{d}SP_{d}+P_{a}SP_{a}\|\leqslant\|S\|
. In view of
P_{d}M_{u}P_{d}+P_{a}M_{u}P_{a}=M_{u}
, this yields

\|M_{u}+P_{d}KP_{d}+P_{a}KP_{a}\|\leqslant\|M_{u}+K\|

for all compact operators, and
P_{d}KP_{d}+P_{a}KP_{a}
is a compact operator leaving
L_{p}(\Omega_{d})
and
L_{p}(\Omega_{a})
invariant. As a consequence one also concludes that it is sufficient to prove the inequality ‘
\geqslant
’ separately for diffuse and atomic measure spaces. For the remainder of the proof we now return to the case
p=1
.

(iii) In this part of the proof we show ‘
\geqslant
’ for the case that
\Omega=\Omega_{d}
, i.e. that
\mu
is a diffuse measure. The case
u=0
being trivial, assume that
\|u\|_{\infty}>0
and let
0<\varepsilon<\|u\|_{\infty}
.
Then there exists a descending sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
in
\mathcal{A}
such that
0<\mu(A_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
|u\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{A_{n}}|\geqslant\|u\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
; without restriction
\mu(A_{1})<\infty
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
put

f_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\mu(A_{n})}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{n}},

where
\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{n}}
denotes the indicator function of the set
A_{n}
. Let
K\in\mathcal{K}(L_{1}(\mu))
. Because
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
is a bounded sequence, the compactness of
K
implies that there exists a subsequence
(f_{n_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
such that the sequence
(Kf_{n_{j}})
is convergent;
by passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
(Kf_{n})
is already convergent. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
such that
\|Kf_{n}-Kf_{m}\|\leqslant\varepsilon
for all
m\geqslant n
. Choose
m\geqslant n
large enough to obtain additionally
\frac{1}{\mu(A_{m})}\geqslant 2\frac{1}{\mu(A_{n})}
. Then one has

f_{n}-f_{m}=\tfrac{1}{\mu(A_{n})}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{n}}-\tfrac{1}{\mu(A_{m})}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{m}}=\tfrac{1}{\mu(A_{n})}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{n}\setminus A_{m}}-\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\mu(A_{m})}-\tfrac{1}{\mu(A_{n})}\bigr)\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{A_{m}},

\bigl|f_{n}-f_{m}\bigr|\geqslant f_{n}
,
\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{1}\geqslant\|f_{n}\|_{1}=1
; hence

\displaystyle\|(M_{u}+K)(f_{n}-f_{m})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geqslant\|M_{u}(f_{n}-f_{m})\|_{1}-\|K(f_{n}-f_{m})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geqslant(\|u\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon)\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{1}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geqslant(\|u\|_{\infty}-2\varepsilon)\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{1},

\|M_{u}+K\|\geqslant\|u\|_{\infty}-2\varepsilon
. As this holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\|u\|_{\infty})
, we obtain
\|M_{u}+K\|\geqslant\|u\|_{\infty}
.

(iv) It remains to show that ‘
\geqslant
’ holds in the case that
\Omega=\Omega_{a}
, i.e. that
\mu
is an atomic measure. If
\mu(B_{n})\neq 0
only for finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, then
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|u(B_{n})|=0
, and the assertion is trivial. Assume that this is not the case, without restriction
\mu(B_{n})\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
let
P_{n}
be the canonical projection from

L_{1}(\mu)
onto
L_{1}(B_{n})
, i.e.
P_{n}f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{n}}f
(
f\in L_{1}(\mu)
),
and put
Q_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=I-\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}
. Iterating the procedure applied in step (ii) above one concludes that for all bounded operators
S
on
L_{1}(\mu)
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
one obtains
\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}SP_{j}+Q_{n}SQ_{n}\|\leqslant\penalty\|S\|
.

Given a compact operator
K\in\mathcal{K}(L_{1}(\mu))
we note that
\|Q_{n}K\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This holds because for any
g\in L_{1}(\mu)
one has
\|Q_{n}g\|\to 0
, and from the relative compactness of
K(B_{L_{1}(\mu)}[0,1])
(where
B_{L_{1}(\mu)}[0,1]
denotes the closed unit ball of
L_{1}(\mu)
) together with the equicontinuity of the sequence
(Q_{n})
one concludes that

\|Q_{n}K\|=\sup_{\|f\|\leqslant 1}\|Q_{n}Kf\|=\sup_{g\in K(B_{L_{1}(\mu)}[0,1])}\|Q_{n}g\|\to 0\qquad(n\to\infty).

In particular, we conclude that
\|P_{n}KP_{n}\|\leqslant\|(Q_{n-1}-Q_{n})K\|\to 0
(
n\to\infty
). Note that, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, there exists
d_{n}\in\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
such that
\|P_{n}KP_{n}\|=|d_{n}|
and
P_{n}KP_{n}f=d_{n}P_{n}f
for all
f\in L_{1}(\mu)
. Hence the multiplication operator
D_{K}
, given by
L_{1}(\mu)\ni f\mapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}d_{n}P_{n}f\in L_{1}(\mu)
, is a compact operator.

Now we estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|M_{u}+K\|&\geqslant\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}(M_{u}+K)P_{j}+Q_{n}(M_{u}+K)Q_{n}\|\\
&=\|M_{u}+D_{K}+Q_{n}(K-D_{K})Q_{n}\|\\
&\geqslant\|M_{u}+D_{K}\|-\|Q_{n}(K-D_{K})Q_{n}\|.\end{split}

(2.1)

From the argument given above we obtain
\|Q_{n}(K-D_{K})Q_{n}\|\leqslant\|Q_{n}(K-D_{K})\|\to\penalty 0
(
n\to\infty
), and from (
2.1
) we conclude that
\|M_{u}+K\|\geqslant\|M_{u}+D_{K}\|
. This shows that

\displaystyle\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigl\{\|M_{u}+D\|{;}\penalty\;D\text{ compact multiplication operator}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\inf\bigl\{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}|u(n)+d_{n}|{;}\penalty\;(d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}\text{ null sequence}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}|u(n)|.\qed

2.2 Remark
.

Step (iv) of our proof applies also to
p\in(1,\infty)
and is an alternative to the last part of
[
2
, proof of Theorem 4.1]
. The idea of our proof is that
\|M_{u}+K\|
can be estimated from below by
\|M_{u}+D\|
for a suitable compact multiplication operator
D
.

3
The case
\Omega=\Omega_{d}
, revisited

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a diffuse
\sigma
-finite measure space,
p\in[1,\infty)
, and let
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
. In this section we will present a proof of the equality

\|M_{u}\|_{\rm e}=\|u\|_{\infty}

(3.1)

(i.e. (
1.1
) for the present special case), which might throw a new light on this property.

We recall that an operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
(the space of all bounded linear operators) is
positive
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))_{+}
, if
Sf\geqslant 0
for all
f\in L_{p}(\mu)_{+}
. Then
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
, defined as the linear hull of
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))_{+}
, is the space of
regular operators
. It is a Banach lattice under the lattice operations

\displaystyle(S\vee T)f

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup\bigl\{Sg+Th{;}\penalty\;g,h\geqslant 0,\ g+h=f\bigr\},

\displaystyle(S\land T)f

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\bigl\{Sg+Th{;}\penalty\;g,h\geqslant 0,\ g+h=f\bigr\}\qquad(f\in L_{p}(\mu)_{+})

(valid for real operators
S,T\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
, the absolute value

|S|f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup\bigl\{|Sg|{;}\penalty\;|g|\leqslant f\bigr\}\qquad(f\in L_{p}(\mu)_{+}),

and with the
regular norm

\|S\|_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\||S|\|
. We refer to
[
13
, Chap. 4]
,
[
8
, Sec. 1.3]
for more information.

As a preparation to the proof of (
3.1
) we need the following property, where
q\in(1,\infty]
denotes the exponent conjugate to
p
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
\eta\in L_{q}(\mu)
(
=L_{p}(\mu)^{\prime}
),
g\in L_{p}(\mu)
,
\eta,g\geqslant 0
,
K\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
defined by

Kf\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl(\int\eta f\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Bigr)\,g\qquad(f\in L_{p}(\mu)).

(Note that
K\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))_{+}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
.) Let
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)_{+}
be such that
M_{u}\leqslant K
.
Then
u=0
.

Proof.

Assume on the contrary that
u\neq 0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mu([u\geqslant\varepsilon])>0
(with the notation
[u\geqslant\varepsilon]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{x\in\Omega{;}\penalty\;u(x)\geqslant\varepsilon\bigr\}
). Further there exists
c>0
such that

\mu([u\geqslant\varepsilon]\cap[g\leqslant c])>0
. Let
B\in\mathcal{A}
,
B\subseteq[u\geqslant\varepsilon]\cap[g\leqslant c]
with
0<\mu(B)<\infty
. Then
M_{u}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B}\geqslant\varepsilon
and
K\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B}=\int_{B}\eta\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,g\leqslant c\int_{B}\eta\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
on
B
. There exists
B
as above and such that
\int_{B}\eta\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\varepsilon/c
, and this leads to the contradiction
K\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B}\leqslant c\int\eta\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\varepsilon\leqslant M_{u}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B}
on
B
.
∎

The
centre

\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))
of
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
is the linear hull of the order interval

[-I,I]=\bigl\{S\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu)){;}\penalty\;-f\leqslant Sf\leqslant f\ (f\in L_{p}(\mu)_{+})\bigr\}.

Then
\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
consists of the bounded multiplication operators and is isometrically isomorphic to
L_{\infty}(\mu)
; see
[
9
, C-I, Section 9]
.

The centre
\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))
is a projection band in the Banach lattice
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
, i.e. for all
S\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
there exists a (unique) decomposition
S=S_{1}+S_{2}
, where
S_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))
and

S_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))^{\mathrm{d}}=\bigl\{T\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu)){;}\penalty\;|T|\land R=0\ (R\in\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))_{+})\bigr\};

see
[
13
, Chap. II, Theorem 2.10]

Let
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))\to\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))
,
S\mapsto S_{1}
denote the associated band projection. What we have shown in Lemma
3.1
is that
\mathcal{P}K=0
for the special (positive) rank-one operators
K
.
(Indeed, the lemma shows that
K
belongs to
\mathcal{Z}(L_{p}(\mu))^{\mathrm{d}}
.) It is easy to see that any finite-rank operator
K\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
can be written as a linear combination of rank-one operators as in Lemma
3.1
; hence
\mathcal{P}K=0
for all finite-rank operators.

3.2 Theorem
.

Let
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)
. Then

\|M_{u}+K\|\geqslant\|M_{u}\|=\|u\|_{\infty}\qquad(K\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(\mu)),

(3.2)

and (
3.1
) holds.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to show (
3.2
). There are two ingredients of the proof:

(i) By the very definition,
\mathcal{P}
is contractive with respect to the regular norm (because band projections are contractive). However, it is shown in
[
15
, Theorem 1.4]
that
\mathcal{P}
is also contractive with respect to the operator norm. This implies that
\mathcal{P}
can be extended by continuity to the closure of
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{r}}(L_{p}(\mu))
in
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
. In particular, for the extension one obtains
\mathcal{P}K=0
for all
K
in the operator norm closure of the finite rank operators.

(ii) The space
L_{p}(\mu)
enjoys the
approximation property
, i.e. every compact operator on
L_{p}(\mu)
can be approximated in operator norm by finite rank operators. (We refer to
[
7
, Sections 3.4 and 4.1]
for the approximation property.) This implies that
\mathcal{P}K=0
for all compact operators on
L_{p}(\mu)
.

Putting together these two properties we obtain

\|M_{u}\|=\|\mathcal{P}(M_{u}+K)\|\leqslant\|M_{u}+K\|

for all
K\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(\mu))
.
∎

4
Supplement on the case
\Omega=\Omega_{a}

We add that the case
\Omega=\Omega_{a}
can be treated analogously to the case
\Omega=\Omega_{d}
described in Section
3
. Then again the centre of
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
consists of the bounded multiplication operators. Lemma
3.1
is replaced by the property that multiplication operators are disjoint to positive rank-one operators
K
of the type

Kf=\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{j}}f\eta\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{j}}=\Bigl(\sum_{k\neq j}f(B_{k})\eta(B_{k})\mu(B_{k})\Bigr)\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{j}}\qquad(f\in L_{p}(\mu)),

(4.1)

where
j\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
and
\eta\in L_{q}(\mu)_{+}
. Indeed, if
u\in L_{\infty}(\mu)_{+}
is such that
M_{u}\leqslant K
, then clearly
u(B_{k})=0
for all
k\neq j
. But

u(B_{j})\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{j}}=M_{u}\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{j}}\leqslant K\operatorname{\bf 1}\nolimits_{B_{j}}=0
; hence also
u(B_{j})=0
. (Recall that
u(B_{k})=0
if
\mu(B_{k})=0
, by our convention in the Introduction.)

The consequence is that, for a compact operator
K
, its projection
\mathcal{P}K
onto the centre is the compact operator
D_{K}
(described in part (iv) of the proof of Theorem
2.1
). This holds because for a compact operator
K
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, the finite rank operator
(I-Q_{n})K
(with the notation of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, part (iv)) can be decomposed as the multiplication operator
(I-Q_{n})D_{K}
and a linear combination of rank-one operators of the type (
4.1
). As
(I-Q_{n})K\to K
(
n\to\infty
) in
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu))
and the band projection
\mathcal{P}
onto the centre is contractive with respect to the operator norm, one concludes that

\mathcal{P}K=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(I-Q_{n})K=\lim_{n\to\infty}(I-Q_{n})D_{K}=D_{K}
.

Hence instead of (
2.1
) one obtains
\|M_{u}+K\|\geqslant\|\mathcal{P}(M_{u}+K)\|=\|M_{u}+D_{K}\|
, and the proof can be finished as in Section
2
.

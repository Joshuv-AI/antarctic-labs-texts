Introduction

Orientation theory and Riemann-Roch formulas after Grothendieck

Starting from an astonishing six pages paper by Quillen
[
Qui69
]
, the intrication between
formal group laws

and homotopy theory has emerged and flourished for more than fifty years.
The picture of chromatic homotopy theory has arisen from Ravenel’s conjectures and yielded deep insight in stable homotopy groups of spheres.
In particular, the stable homotopy category can not only be divided into the rational and
p
-local parts,
but each
p
-local components can be subsequently divided into

-local parts reflecting
Lazard’s classification of
p
-local formal group laws by height (see
[

]
for a historical account).
This colorful filtration is at the heart of many developments in stable homotopy theory since then.
The main players here are the spectrum of complex cobordism
and its subsequent cohort of complex oriented ring spectra.

Motivic homotopy theory is driven by its analogy with its topological realization.
In particular, complex orientation has a well-known motivic analogue, the theory of
\mathrm{GL}
-orientation.
In the latter, the theory of formal group laws (
aka
FGL) has been successfully developed, and algebraic cobordism
\mathbf{MGL}

is known to possess the universal formal group law by works of Morel and
Levine.

In

and proved that it arises from the Chow-Witt groups or more generally the Milnor-Witt motivic cohomology
(resp. the higher Grothendieck-Witt groups).

Generalized orientations and GRR formulas

In this paper, we are interested by another classical aspect of orientation theory,
the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formulas. Fundamentally, these formulas describe
the effect of a change of orientation on a fixed ring spectrum. There exists Todd
classes which precisely describe how to go from the characteristic classes of one orientation
to the other. Moreover, this Todd class can be computed explicitly via the isomorphism
between the two FGLs associated with each orientations.
In the present article,
we extend these results to generalized orientations in the sense of Panin and Walter,
and specialize these theoretical results to the case of symplectic orientations
and their associated FTLs.

To explain the context of our generalized Riemann-Roch formulas,
let us first recall that the idea behind Panin and Walter’s orientation theory
is that certain cohomology theories possess Thom classes only for vector bundles
with additional data, such as a symplectic form or more generally

G_{*}
-torsors for a graded algebraic sub-group
G_{*}
of the
general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{*}
(see
1.2.3
for a reminder).
To formulate our Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formulas, we need to
extend these Thom classes to
virtual

G
-torsors (see
1.2.6
).
This leads us to introduce a new, more general, definition of orientations.
With that in mind, we introduce the notion of stable structures on vector bundles over a scheme
X
.
Recall that Deligne has defined the category
\uK(X)
of virtual vector bundles over
X
,
which is a
Picard groupoid
whose isomorphism classes are given by the
K-theory group
\mathrm{K}_{0}(X)
.
In this context, a stable structure on vector bundles is a collection of maps (or a so-called pseudo-functor)

\sigma:\uK^{\sigma}(X)\rightarrow\uK(X)

for various schemes
X
with suitable properties: compatibility with pullbacks
and with the monoidal structure (see
1.4.2
for more precision).
One example is given by the functor
\sigma_{G}:\uK^{G}(X)\rightarrow\uK(X)
from virtual
G
-torsors
to virtual vector bundles that “forgets” the
G
-structure.
Let us roughly state our main definition:

Definition 1
.

(See
1.4.5
)
A
\sigma
-orientation on the ring spectrum
\mathbb{E}
is
the data of Thom classes
\operatorname{th}(\tilde{v})\in\mathbb{E}^{2r,r}(\Th(v))

associated to any object
\tilde{v}\in\uK^{\sigma}(X)
, where
v=\sigma(\tilde{v})

is a virtual vector bundle of rank
r
and (motivic) Thom space
\Th(v)
.
These Thom classes are required to be compatible with isomorphisms, pullbacks
and the monoidal structure on
\uK^{\sigma}(X)
.

As in the classical case, the properties listed in the preceding definition imply
that
\mathfrak{t}(\tilde{v})
is a basis of the
\mathbb{E}^{**}(X)
-module

\mathbb{E}^{**}(\Th(v))
(as expected). The reader will be relieved
to know that
\sigma_{G}
-orientations are in bijection with
G
-orientations
in the sense of Panin and Walter (
1.4.7
).
One easily deduces from the above definition
a new family of characteristic (or fundamental) classes.

Proposition 2
.

(See
2.1.3
)
Let
\mathbb{E}
be a
\sigma
-oriented ring spectrum, and let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a smoothable lci morphism. A
\sigma
-orientation
of
f
is a class
\tilde{\tau}_{f}\in\uK^{\sigma}(X)
such
that
\sigma(\tilde{\tau}_{f})
is isomorphic to the image
\tau_{f}
of the cotangent complex

\mathcal{L}_{f}
of
f
in
\uK(X)
. One associates to the pair
(f,\tilde{\tau}_{f})
a canonical fundamental class
with coefficients in
\mathbb{E}
:

\tilde{\eta}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}}\in\mathbb{E}_{2d,d}(X/S)

where
\mathbb{E}_{n,i}(X/S)=\Hom_{\SH(S)}(\mathbbm{1}_{S}(i)[n],f^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{S})

is the bivariant theory associated with
\mathbb{E}
(see
Section

1.1
for more information).

These
\sigma
-oriented fundamental classes satisfy the usual formalism of their parent
the motivic fundamental classes: compatibility with composition,
transverse pullback formula, excess of intersection (see
[
DJK21
]
).
They also fulfill the formalism of Fulton and MacPherson
[
FM81
]
.
Note in particular that they induce various Gysin morphisms.
For example, if
f
is in addition proper, one gets a pushforward in cohomology:

f^{\mathbb{E}}_{!}:\mathbb{E}^{n,i}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{n+2d,i+d}(S),a\mapsto f_{!}(a.\tilde{\eta}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}})

where one uses the action of cohomology and the axiomatic pushforward on bivariant theory
(see also e.g.
[
Dég18a
, Def. 3.3.2]
).
Additionally, they satisfy our sought-for Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formula,
in the style of
[
FM81
, 1.4]
.

Theorem 3
.

(
2.2.2
and
2.2.4
)
Let
\psi:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be a morphism of ring spectra.
Assume further that
\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbb{F}
admit
\sigma
-orientations. Then there exists a unique
Todd class

\td_{\psi}\colon\mathrm{K}_{0}^{\sigma}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{00}(X)^{\times}

which is a morphism of abelian groups natural in
X
such that
the following relation holds

\mathfrak{t}(\tilde{v},\mathbb{F})=\td_{\psi}(\tilde{v})\cdot\psi_{*}(\mathfrak{t}(\tilde{v},\mathbb{E})).

Moreover, for any
\sigma
-oriented morphism
(f:X\rightarrow S,\tilde{\tau}_{f})
,
one gets the following Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formula:

\psi_{*}(\tilde{\eta}_{f}^{\mathbb{E}})=\td_{\psi}(\tilde{\tau}_{f})\cdot\tilde{\eta}_{f}^{\mathbb{F}}.

As underlined by Fulton and MacPherson, one deduces from the above formula
the usual formulations of Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formulas,
involving Gysin morphisms. Namely, when
f
is in addition proper, the formula reads

\psi(f^{\mathbb{E}}_{!}(a))=f^{\mathbb{F}}_{!}(\td_{\psi}(\tilde{\tau}_{f}).\psi(a)).

One also deduces analogous formulas in homology
and, without the properness assumptions, formulas in cohomology
with compact support or bivariant theories. The reader
can find details in the statement of
2.2.4
.
The problem with this abstract formula is to be able to compute
the relevant Todd class. This is one of the motivations to restrict our attention
to the symplectic case.

Symplectic orientations and formal ternary laws

Symplectic orientations are very close to
\mathrm{GL}
-orientations.
Firstly, there is an appropriate variant of the projective bundle theorem,
for the so-called symplectic projective spaces
\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{S}

(built out of the classifying spaces
\mathrm{BSp}_{2n}
, see
3.2.1
).
This allowed Panin and Walter to characterize
\mathrm{Sp}
-orientations of a ring spectrum
\mathbb{E}

as the datum of a class
b\in\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{4,2}(\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}_{S})
in reduced cohomology
which restricts to the tautological class on
\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}\simeq\mathbbm{1}(2)[4]

(see
3.2.4
for a reminder).

Our first task is to relate the theory of Thom classes
associated to symplectic orientations with the theory of virtual Thom classes
mentioned previously.
For this, we show that these Thom classes not only extend to
virtual classes of symplectic bundles, but also factors modulo the so-called metabolic relation.
More precisely, we introduce a stable structure

f_{\mathrm{Sp}}:\uKSp(X)\rightarrow\uK(X)
whose source is the associated homotopy groupoid of the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{KSp}(X)
and we show that
\mathrm{Sp}
-orientations
on a ring spectrum
\mathbb{E}
are in bijection with
f_{\mathrm{Sp}}
-orientations in the
sense explained above (see
3.3.5
).

Our second task is to improve the properties of symplectic Thom classes
by establishing the following formula:

Theorem 4
.

(see
3.4.5
and its corollary)
Let
\mathbb{E}
be an
\mathrm{Sp}
-oriented ring spectrum with Thom classes
\mathfrak{t}
.
Then for any symplectic vector bundle
(\mathcal{U},\psi)
over a scheme
X
,
any unit
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)^{\times}
with associated class

\langle\lambda\rangle\in[\mathbbm{1}_{X},\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbb{G}_{m}]_{\SH(X)}
,
the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}(\mathcal{U},\lambda.\psi)

\displaystyle=\langle u\rangle.\mathfrak{t}(\mathcal{U},\psi),

\displaystyle b_{i}(\mathcal{U},\lambda.\psi)

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda^{i}\rangle.b_{i}(\mathcal{U},\psi),i\geq 0.

The proof of this formula is surprisingly involved. It was known before only
for
\mathrm{SL}
-oriented theories (see
[
DF21
, Prop. 2.2.8]
).
It is nevertheless central for the theory of FTL,
as it implies the so-called
\epsilon
-linearity axiom
of the FTL
associated to a symplectically oriented spectrum
\mathbb{E}

(see
3.1.2
and
3.5.1
).
Such FTL are called
representable
.

Once all these preliminaries are settled,
the link between FTL and
\mathrm{Sp}
-orientations takes the same
form as for
\mathrm{GL}
-orientations: given an
\mathrm{Sp}
-oriented
ring spectrum with associated FTL
F_{t}
, choosing another
FTL amounts to choose a strict automorphism of
F_{t}

(see
3.5.7
).
This result contains the key to compute Todd classes
as we will see later in this introduction.
One also deduces the following
result, that confirms the analogy between FTL and FGL.

Theorem 5
.

(see
3.5.9
)
Any representable rational formal ternary law
F_{t}
admits a logarithm,

i.e.
a strict isomorphism
\log_{F_{t}}
with the additive FTL.
Moreover,
\log_{F_{t}}
is unique among such strict isomorphisms.

We expect that the representability assumption is unnecessary.
In fact, we believe that much more properties of FTL could be developed
in analogy with FGL. This includes a general chromatic study
of motivic homotopy theory.
On the other hand, the properties of the Walter ring classifying FTL
seems richer than its analog the Lazard ring, due to
the presence of torsion phenomena.

Plan of the article

This work can be roughly divided into two parts.
In the first one, comprising Sections 1 and 2,
we establish a framework of generalized orientations
(or Thom classes attached to vector bundles with additional structures)
that allows one to define fundamental classes, Todd classes and prove several
Riemann-Roch formulas.
In the second part, comprising Sections 3 and 4,
we specialize the above discussion to the case of symplectic orientations,
whose specificity is to be tied with the algebraic notion of formal ternary laws.
The reader which is only interested in the symplectic case, can
start reading sections 3 and 4 independently.

In more details, Section 1 is mainly concerned with a theory
that extends Panin-Walter’s orientation theory. The main objective is to define
a good notion of
virtual Thom classes
,
which are associated with “virtual vector bundles with additional structures”,
which are rigorously axiomatized in
1.4.2
.
We also remind in Section 1.1 some material about bivariant theories in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy.
Section 2.1 is then devoted to the application of the general theory of virtual Thom classes
to define fundamental classes in the sense of Fulton and MacPherson,
associated with the bivariant theories discussed in Section 1.1.
Section 2.2 finally presents the various Riemann-Roch formula that one
can obtain out of an arbitrary morphism of ring spectra which are suitably

oriented
.

Section 3 is more involved.
Let us first describe the sections that can be read independently of sections 1 and 2.
We first recall in 3.1 the algebraic theory of formal ternary laws,
and in 3.2 Panin-Walter’s notion of symplectic orientations on a ring spectrum.
Section 3.4 is devoted to prove
3.4.5
(stated above as
4
),
which is crucial to prove that each
\mathrm{Sp}
-oriented ring spectrum
has a canonically associated FTL. This association is studied in detail in 3.5.
We derive some interesting facts about FTL such as
3.5.9

(stated as
5
above).
Section 3 is connected with the two previous one by 3.3,
where we show that Panin-Walter’s Thom classes can be extended as Thom classes
of virtual metabolic symplectic bundles. The final section 3.6 explains
how to compute Todd classes in the symplectic case
(by perfect analogy with the
\mathrm{GL}
-orientation case).

The final section is devoted to computations, determining respectively the Todd classes associated with Adams operations (1.1)
and with the Borel character (1.2). We conclude by a proof of the
quadratic HRR-formula stated as
6
above.

1.2.7

Cohomology of Thom spaces
.
Let
\mathbb{E}
be a spectrum over
\Sigma
.
One usually extends the definition of the cohomology associated with

(

and

More generally, for any virtual vector bundle

The above abelian group is functorial in

thus producing a functor

\uZ(X)^{2}\times\uK(X)\rightarrow\Ab,(n,m,v)\mapsto\mathbb{E}^{n,m}(\Th(v)).

If

\mathbb{E}^{n,m}(\Th(v))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}^{s,t}(\Th(w))\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{n+s,m+t}(\Th(v+w)).

In other words,
\mathbb{E}^{**}(\Th(*))
is a
\uZ(X)^{2}\times\uK(X)
-graded ring.
Note in particular that, fixing

\mathbb{E}^{**}(\Th(v))
is an
\mathbb{E}^{**}(X)
-graded module.

As in classical cases, one deduces from Thom classes the so-called

By the compatibility with product of Thom classes, one reduces to the case where

is associated to the class of a

1.4.1

There is a more concise way of encoding

We refer the reader to
[

[

By what is classicaly called the Grothendieck construction,

[
Vis05
, Def. 3.6]

[

3.1.1

Multi-valued series
.–
Formal ternary laws were introduced by Walter in an unpublished work,
and then formally developped in
[

]
. More recently, we further expanded the theory
in
[
CDFH21
]
, introducing in particular a slightly more accurate framework that we now briefly recall,
referring the reader to
[

, §1]
for more details.

Let
R
be a (commutative) ring. Given a pair of integers
(n,d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\underline{x}=(x_{1},...,x_{d})
a
d
-tuple of integers,
an
n
-valued
d
-dimensional series,
(n,d)
-series for short,
with coefficients in

F_{t}(\underline{x})=1+F_{1}(\underline{x})t+\ldots+F_{n}(\underline{x})t^{n}

in

, polynomial in

of degree

.
These
(n,d)
-series form an
R
-module
\mathscr{F}_{n,d}(R)
.
We will use a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtration
on
\mathscr{F}_{n,d}(R)
by assigning to the variables
x_{i}
degree
+1

and to the variable
t
degree
-1
. Therefore, an
(n,d)
-series has valuation greater or equal to
(-n)

and can have infinite degree.

One can define a generalized composition on these
(*,*)
-series
(see
[
CDFH21
, Def. 2.1.13]
for more details).
Let
G_{t}(\underline{y})
be an
(m,r)
-series. We say that
G_{t}(\underline{y})
is

if
G_{t}(\underline{0})=1
. We then introduce formal variables
(G^{[1]},\ldots,G^{[m]})
,
called the

of
G_{t}(\underline{y})
, satisfying the formal identity

(3.1.1.a)

G_{t}(\underline{y})=\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+G^{[l]}t).

In other words, the coefficients of
G_{t}(\underline{y})
are given by the elementary symmetric functions
in the variables
G^{[l]}
.

Then we define the
substitution of
G_{t}(\underline{y})
in
F_{t}(\underline{x})
at the variable
x_{i}

as the
(nm,d+r-1)
-series in the variables
(x_{1},\ldots,\cancel{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{d},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})

by the following equality:

F_{t}\big(x_{1},...,x_{i-1},G_{t}(\underline{y}),x_{i+1},...,x_{d}\big):=\prod_{l=1}^{m}F_{t}\big(x_{1},...,x_{i-1},G^{[l]},x_{i+1},...,x_{d}\big).

More precisely, the expression on the right is obviously symmetric in the variables

.
Thus it can be expressed as a polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials

in the

The following algebraic notion is an analog of formal group laws,
especially relevant in motivic homotopy theory (see also
[

Definition 3.1.2

R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{\epsilon}
-algebra.

, FTL for short,

(4,3)
-series with coefficients in

(1)

(2)

(3)

We first start with the construction of symplectic cobordism.
Recall that Voevodsky introduced in

3.3.
Virtual symplectic Thom classes

According to

symplectically oriented ring spectra admits virtual symplectic Thom classes,
associated with stable symplectic bundles. However, hermitian K-theory of symplectic bundles
is finer as it involves the equivalence relation modulo metabolic spaces (see e.g.
[

We will show in this subsection that symplectic Thom classes are compatible with

3.3.1
.

We first give a quick recollection on the hermitian

We start with a triple

\textstyle{Q^{h}\mathcal{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

3.3.6

Virtual symplectic Thom classes
.
Following

-orientation

\mathrm{Sp}
-oriented ring spectrum
(\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{t})
over

Given a stable symplectic bundle

of rank

\mathfrak{t}(\omega)\in\mathbb{E}^{2r,r}(\Th(\omega))

such that the natural map:

\mathbb{E}^{**}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{*+2r,*+r}(\Th(\omega)),\lambda\mapsto\lambda.\mathfrak{t}(\omega)

Moreover, this class is compatible with isomorphisms in
\omega
, pullbacks in
X

in an obvious sense. Let us spell out explicitly the compatibility with

\omega,\omega^{\prime}
of respective ranks

One gets a natural product map:

\mu:\mathbb{E}^{2r,r}(\Th(\omega))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}^{2r^{\prime},r^{\prime}}(\Th(\omega^{\prime}))\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{2r+2r^{\prime},r+r^{\prime}}(\Th(\omega+\omega^{\prime})).

\mu(\mathfrak{t}(\omega)\otimes\mathfrak{t}(\omega^{\prime}))=\mathfrak{t}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})

We can equivalently write this as:

\mathfrak{t}(\omega).\mathfrak{t}(\omega^{\prime})=\mathfrak{t}(\omega+\omega^{\prime}).

One deduces that

\mathbb{E}^{**}(\Th(\omega))\otimes_{\mathbb{E}^{**}(X)}\mathbb{E}^{*}(\Th(\omega^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{E}^{**}(\Th(\omega+\omega^{\prime})).

the stable vector bundles assocaited with

\mathfrak{t}([V,\psi])=\mathfrak{t}(V,\psi).

3.4.1

Borel classes

Recall that to a symplectic orientation

\mathcal{U}/X

over a smooth

Following the classical scheme, one defines the total Borel class associated with

(3.4.1.a)

Note moreover that, using relation (

3.4.2
.

The symplectic splitting principle
. As for Chern classes,
one has a splitting principle for Borel classes.
Indeed, given any symplectic bundle
\mathcal{V}
over a scheme
X
, there exists an affine morphism
p:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X

inducing a monomorphism
p^{*}:\mathbb{E}^{**}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{**}(X^{\prime})
with the property that
p^{-1}(\mathcal{V})
splits
as a direct sum of rank
2
symplectic bundles:
p^{-1}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{X}_{1}\perp\ldots\perp\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

One defines the
Borel roots
of
\mathcal{V}
as the Borel classes
\xi_{i}=b_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{i})
so that
by the Whitney sum formula
[
PW22
, Theorem 10.5]
the Borel classes of
\mathcal{V}
are the elementary symmetric polynomials
in the variables
\xi_{i}
.

As in the classical case, one can compute universal formulas involving Borel classes of
\mathcal{V}
by introducing Borel roots

, which reduces to rank

\mathcal{V}
was completely split, and
then express the resulting formula in terms of
the elementary symmetric polynomials in the

3.4.3
.

We now give a complement to the theory of Borel classes which was known previously only under the stronger assumption of the existence of an
\mathrm{SL}
-orientation
(see
[
DF21
, 2.2.8]

By abuse of notation, we still denote by

Theorem 3.4.5

Let
(\mathbb{E},b)
be an
\mathrm{Sp}
-oriented ring spectrum, and thom classes
\mathfrak{t}
.
Then for any unit
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{S}(X)^{\times}

As a corollary, we obtain the following result whose proof is the same as

Corollary 3.4.6

Let

be an
\mathrm{Sp}

The proof of the theorem will occupy the remaining of this section.
We first reduces to the case

(using

on
\mathrm{BSp}_{2}

seen over the base scheme

The proof of the theorem will require some elaborate computations with the hyperbolic Grassmannians. Recall that
\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{S}=\mathrm{HGr}_{S}(2,\omega_{2n+2})

There is an interesting cell structure on

\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{n}=X_{0}\sqcup X_{2}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup X_{2n}

with the total space of the universal bundle

Remark 3.5.6

There is an obvious obstruction for an FTL to be representable:
its ring of coefficients must be isomorphic to the

\mathrm{GL}

Lemma 4.1.1
.

For any

, there exists a unique

\psi^{n}(x)=xp_{n}(x).

It satisfies the inductive formula

,
p_{2}(x)=x+2\tau
and

p_{n}(x)=(x+\tau)p_{n-1}(x)-\gamma p_{n-2}(x)+\psi^{n-1}(\tau).

Proof.

For
u
, we have
\psi^{0}(u)=2
,
\psi^{1}(u)=u
and for

\psi^{n}(u)=u\psi^{n-1}(u)-\gamma\psi^{n-2}(u)

in view of formula (

\displaystyle\psi^{n}(u)-\psi^{n}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\psi^{n-1}(u)-\gamma\psi^{n-2}(u)-\tau\psi^{n-1}(\tau)+\gamma\psi^{n-2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u-\tau)\psi^{n-1}(u)+\tau(\psi^{n-1}(u)-\psi^{n-1}(\tau))-\gamma(\psi^{n-2}(u)-\psi^{n-2}(\tau))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+\tau)\psi^{n-1}(x)+x\psi^{n-1}(\tau)-\gamma\psi^{n-2}(x).

This yields

\displaystyle xp_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+\tau)\psi^{n-1}(x)+x\psi^{n-1}(\tau)-\gamma\psi^{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+\tau)xp_{n-1}(x)+x\psi^{n-1}(\tau)-\gamma xp_{n-2}(x).

As noted in the proof, the polynomials

For any
n\geq 2

is its class in
\GW^{2}(\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}_{S})
, we have
\tilde{\chi}_{2+4n}(E)=b_{1}(U)^{2n+1}
according to
[
DF21

\chi_{4n}(f(U))=2c_{2}(U)^{2n}

[

under the morphism

is

and the

p(x)^{2n}=x\cdot p(x)^{2n-1}

for any

\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2}{(4n)!}p(x)^{2n}\right)+\left(x+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\psi_{2+4n}!}x^{2n+1}\right)=x\cdot\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2}{(4n)!}p(x)^{2n-1}+1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\psi_{2+4n}!}t^{2n}\right).

As
x=u-\tau
is the first Borel class of

in
\mathrm{KGL}(2)[4]

\td_{\mathrm{bo}_{t}}^{-1}(u)=\left(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2}{4n!}p(x)^{2n-1}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\psi_{2+4n}!}x^{2n}\right).

We now make use of the fact that rationally the ring spectrum representing MW-motivic cohomology decomposes as a product (this could even by taken as a definition
[

\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{MW}}\mathbb{Q}\simeq\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{M}}\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{W}}\mathbb{Q}

the composite of the decomposition and the second projection. This equivalence yields in particular in degree
(0,0)
an isomorphism

\mathbf{H}^{0,0}_{\mathrm{MW}}\mathbb{Q}(S)\simeq\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{0}(S)\simeq\underline{\mathbb{Q}}(S)\times\mathbf{W}(S)

where
\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
is the sheaf associated to the constant presheaf
\mathbb{Q}
. In view of this decomposition and the commutative diagram (

), we obtain

p(\td_{\mathrm{bo}_{t}}^{-1}(u))=\td_{\mathrm{ch}_{t}}^{-1}(f(u))

and we see that it suffices to compute the latter and

Proposition 4.2.1
.

If
U
is a rank
2
symplectic bundle on a smooth
S
-scheme
X
and
u\in\GW^{2}(X)
is its associated class, we have

\td_{\mathrm{ch}_{t}}^{-1}(f(u))=2\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(-c_{2}(U))^{i-1}}{(2i)!}.

Proof.

By universality, it suffices to consider the situation where
X=\mathrm{H}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}_{S}
and
U
is the universal rank two symplectic bundle. We may consider the projective bundle
\pi:Y:={\mathbb{P}}(U)\to X
and replace
X
by
Y
in view of the splitting principle, as both
K

\mathrm{GL}
-oriented. Over
Y

\mathrm{ch}_{t}(f(u))=e^{c_{1}(L)}+e^{c_{1}(L^{\vee})}=e^{c_{1}(L)}+e^{-c_{1}(L)}=2\cosh(c_{1}(L)).

This yields
\mathrm{ch}_{t}(f(u-\tau))=2\cosh(c_{1}(L))-2

c_{1}(L)^{2}=-c_{1}(L)c_{1}(L^{\vee})=-c_{2}(U)
, the expression reads as

\mathrm{ch}_{t}(f(u-\tau))=2\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(-c_{2}(U))^{i}}{(2i)!}

As the first Borel class of
U
in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(X)
is
-c_{2}(U)
, we finally obtain

\td_{\mathrm{ch}_{t}}^{-1}(f(u))=2\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(-c_{2}(U))^{i-1}}{(2i)!}.

∎

We now start the computation of

The following lemma is easily proved by induction.

For

The Todd class associated to the morphism of ring spectra

4.3.
The quadratic Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem

4.3.1
.

In this section, we illustrate Theorem
2.2.4
in the case of the Borel character discussed in the previous section.

We consider a smooth proper scheme
X
over a field
k
of even dimension
d=2n
,
with projection
p\colon X\to\Spec(k)
and canonical bundle
\omega_{X/k}=\det(\Omega_{X/k})
.
We let
f\colon L^{\otimes 2}\to\omega_{X/k}
be an orientation of
X/k

(see
2.1.2
).
Using this isomorphism, we obtain an isomorphism (corresponding to the fact
\GW
is
\mathrm{SL}^{c}
-oriented)

\GW^{2}(X)\simeq\GW^{2}(X,L^{\otimes 2})\simeq\GW^{2}(X,\omega_{X/k})

mapping the (isometry class of the) symplectic bundle
(V,\varphi)
to
(V\otimes L,f\circ(\varphi\otimes\mathrm{Id}))
. The same formula applies for
\GW^{0}(X)
.

The isomorphism
f\circ(\varphi\otimes\mathrm{Id})
induces for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
an isomorphism

[f\circ(\varphi\otimes\mathrm{Id})]_{i}\colon\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,V\otimes L)\to\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,(V\otimes L)^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X/k}).

Serre duality yields a canonical isomorphism
\mathrm{can}:\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,(V\otimes L)^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X/k})\simeq\operatorname{H}^{2n-i}(X,V\otimes L)^{*}
and we finally obtain isomorphisms

\varphi_{i}\colon\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,V\otimes L)\to\operatorname{H}^{2n-i}(X,V\otimes L)^{*}.

In particular, for
i=n
, the isomorphism

\varphi_{n}\colon\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L)\to\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L)^{*}

yields either a symmetric or symplectic form on the vector space
\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L)
in the following way: If
[V,\varphi]\in\GW^{2m}(X)
, then
[\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L),\varphi_{n}]\in\GW^{2m-2n}(k)
. For instance, if
\varphi
is symplectic and
n
is odd then
\varphi_{n}
is symmetric. The following proposition can be seen as a computation of the tranfer map in Grothendieck-Witt theory.

Proposition 4.3.2
.

Let
[V,\varphi]\in\GW^{2m}(X)
. Then

p_{*}([V,\varphi])=(-1)^{m+n}[\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L),\varphi_{n}]+(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{(m+i)}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,V\otimes L))\cdot(1-\epsilon)

in
\GW^{2m-2n}(k)
.

Remark 4.3.3
.

The bundle
V
should be seen as a complex concentrated in degree
m
in the statement. Moreover, one observes that if
m-n
is odd then
\varphi_{n}
is symplectic, and then hyperbolic. Thus, the interesting information arises only when
m
and
n
have the same parity, and is then encoded by
\varphi_{n}
.

In view of this computation, we may set the following definition.

Definition 4.3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper scheme of even dimension
d=2n
and let
(V,\varphi)
be either a symplectic bundle if
d=2\pmod{4}
or a symmetric bundle if
d=0\pmod{4}
. We define the quadratic Euler characteristic of
(V,\varphi)
under the orientation
f:L^{\otimes 2}\simeq\omega_{X/k}
as the following expression

[\operatorname{H}^{n}(X,V\otimes L),\varphi_{n}]+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{(m+i)}\mathrm{dim}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,V\otimes L)\cdot(1-\epsilon)

where
m=1
if
\varphi
is symplectic and
m=0
else. We denote by
\tilde{\chi}(X,f;V,\varphi)
the class of this symmetric bilinear form.

The Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch formula for the Borel character
\bo_{t}
then reads as follows.

Theorem 4.3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper scheme of even dimension
d
such that its tangent bundle
T_{X/k}
admits a stable
\mathrm{Sp}
-orientation
\tilde{\tau}_{X}
. We let
f
be the orientation of
X/k
associated with
\tilde{\tau}_{X}
.

Let
(V,\varphi)
be either a symplectic bundle if
d=2\pmod{4}
or a symmetric bundle if
d=0\pmod{4}
. Then the following equality holds in
\GW(k)

\tilde{\chi}(X,f;V,\varphi)=\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}\left(\td_{\bo_{t}}(\tilde{\tau}_{X}).\bo_{t}(V,\varphi)\right)

where
\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}\colon\widetilde{\operatorname{CH}}^{d}(X)\to\GW(k)
is the quadratic degree map
(pushforwar) associated with the symplectic orientation
\tilde{\tau}_{X}
of
X/K
.

Example 4.3.6
.

Suppose that
d=2
and that
X
is a K3 surface. Then, a symplectic orientation
\tilde{\tau}_{X}
of
\Omega_{X/k}
(or
T_{X/k}
) is just obtained by choosing an isomorphism
f:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/k}
. Indeed, one just considers the composite
\Omega_{X/k}\otimes\Omega_{X/k}\to\omega_{X/k}\xrightarrow{f^{-1}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The Todd class can be easily computed using
4.2.1
and
4.2.3
. We have

\td_{\mathrm{ch}_{t}}^{-1}(\Omega_{X/k})=1-\frac{c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k})}{12},\td_{p^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{ch}_{t}}^{-1}(\Omega_{X/k})=1

and it follows easily from the fact that
X
is a surface that
\td_{\bo_{t}}(\Omega_{X/k})=1+\frac{c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k})}{24}(1-\epsilon)
. Note further that there is no need to tensor with
\mathbb{Q}
as the Borel character is defined integrally in case of surfaces. If
(V,\varphi)
is a symplectic bundle of rank
2r
over
X
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}(X,f;V,\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}\left((2r+e(V,\varphi))\cdot(1+\frac{c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k})}{24}(1-\epsilon))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}\left(r\cdot\frac{c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k})}{12}(1-\epsilon)+e(V,\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}\left(r\cdot\frac{c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k})}{12}\right)\cdot(1-\epsilon)+\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}(e(V,\varphi)).

Using the fact that
\mathrm{deg}(c_{2}(\Omega_{X/k}))=24
(e.g.
[
Huy16
]
), we finally obtain the formula:

\tilde{\chi}(X,f;V,\varphi)=2r(1-\epsilon)+\tilde{\operatorname{deg}}_{\tilde{\tau}_{X}}(e(V,\varphi)).

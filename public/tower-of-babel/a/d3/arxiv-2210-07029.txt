Abstract
. We present three equivalent definitions of the fractional p p -Laplacian ( − Δ ℍ n ) p s (-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p} , 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 , p > 1 p>1 , with normalizing constants, on hyperbolic spaces. The explicit values of the constants enable us to study the convergence of the fractional p p -Laplacian to the p p -Laplacian as s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} .

Operators of fractional-order have been extensively studied not only in Euclidean spaces

, but also on Riemannian manifolds

,

,

]
due to the simple structure of the spaces. In contrast to the case of Euclidean spaces, not all definitions are equivalent in more general spaces. For instance, one can study a regional type operator
[
24
]
or a spectral type operator
[
31
]
on Riemannian manifolds. Moreover, some definitions, such as those relying on the Fourier transform, do not even work on general Riemannian manifolds and metric measure spaces. Nonetheless, for specific Riemannian manifolds such as hyperbolic spaces and spheres, several representations of the fractional Laplacian have been established
[
2
,
13
]
by means of the rich structure of these spaces.

The aim of this paper is two-fold. First, we extend representation formulas in
[
2
]
to the nonlinear regime on hyperbolic spaces. Specifically, we define the fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1
, and
p>1
and provide two additional equivalent definitions via the heat semigroup and the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension. Note that a definition based on the Fourier transform is not available due to the nonlinearity of the operator. Second, we investigate the pointwise convergence of
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)
as
s\to 1^{-}
. For this purpose, the values of the normalizing constants in these definitions are provided explicitly.

Let us define the fractional
p
-Laplacian on hyperbolic spaces as the pointwise integral representation with singular kernels. Note that hyperbolic geometry is distinguished from Euclidean geometry only when
n\geq 2
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
0<s<1
, and
p>1
. The
fractional
p
-Laplacian
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

(1.1)

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=c_{n,s,p}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi

with the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
given by

\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)=C_{1}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\rho\right)\right)

when
n\geq 3
is odd and

\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)=C_{1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(r^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}r\right)\right)\mathrm{d}r

when
n\geq 2
is even, where

c_{n,s,p}=\frac{p}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}/2}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma(\frac{n+sp}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}|\Gamma(-s)|},\quad C_{1}=\frac{1}{2^{\frac{n-2+sp}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n+sp}{2})}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}},

and
K_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.

Note that
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{p}^{s}u(x)
is well defined for
u\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
(assuming also that
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
if
p\in(1,\frac{2}{2-s}]
), where
C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
denotes the space of bounded
C^{2}
-functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
28
, Section 3]
, where the authors provide the well-definedness of
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{p}^{s}u(x)
; the same proof also works on hyperbolic spaces.

For the linear case
p=2
, the pointwise integral representation with singular kernel is provided in
[
2
, Theorem 2.4 and 2.5]
without constants. The main tool in
[
2
]
is the Fourier transform on hyperbolic spaces, but it is not available in the nonlinear setting. Our definition is motivated by the nonlinear extension of Bochner’s definition
[
3
]
, which will be given in
Theorem

1.3
below.

The normalizing constant
C_{1}
in
Definition

1.1
is carefully chosen so that the pointwise convergence
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{p}u(x)
holds (see
Theorem

1.5
). We emphasize that the normalizing constant plays a crucial role in some contexts. For instance, it is used in the robust regularity theory (see
[
6
,
27
]
).

The singular kernel
\rho^{-n-sp}
of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is homogeneous of degree
-n-sp
. This is a natural consequence of the invariance of the scale of the operator. However, such homogeneity cannot be expected in the hyperbolic setting because hyperbolic geometry comes into play. In fact, we prove the following proposition. (In what follows, we write
A(\rho)\sim B(\rho)
as
\rho\to 0^{+}
(resp.
\rho=\infty
) to mean that there exist constants
c,C>0
and
\rho_{0}>0
such that
cB(\rho)\leq A(\rho)\leq CB(\rho)
for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
(resp.
\rho\in(\rho_{0},\infty)
).)

Proposition 1.2
.

There exist constants
c,C>0
such that

c\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\rho\right)\leq\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\leq C\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\rho\right)

for all
\rho>0
. In particular,

\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sim\rho^{-n-sp}

as
\rho\to 0^{+}
and

\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sim\rho^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}e^{-(n-1)\rho}

as
\rho\to\infty
.

As the first main result of this paper, we provide an equivalent representation of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on hyperbolic spaces via the heat semigroup. To this end, let
\{e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the heat semigroup generated by the Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. That is, for a given function
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we denote by
e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)
the solution
w(x,t)
of the Cauchy problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w(x,t)-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}w(x,t)=0,&x\in\mathbb{H}^{n},t>0,\\
w(x,0)=f(x),&x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1
, and
p>1
. Let
u\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. If
p\in(1,\frac{2}{2-s}]
, assume in addition that
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
. The fractional
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

(1.3)

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\cdot))](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}},

where

C_{2}=\frac{p}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}/2}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\frac{2^{s(2-p)}}{|\Gamma(-s)|}

and
\Phi_{p}(r)=|r|^{p-2}r
.

Note that the formula (
1.3
) on manifolds including hyperbolic spaces is given in
[
2
, Section 3.1]
for the linear case
p=2
, and that it was proposed as the definition of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on Riemannian manifolds in
[
14
, Section 8.2]
.

We now turn to another representation of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We recall that the fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be realized as a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map via the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension
[
5
]
. Later, the article
[
31
]
relates the heat semigroup to this extension. Moreover, this relation is extended to the nonlinear framework
[
14
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this paper, we further investigate this relation on hyperbolic spaces. Let us consider the extension problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Delta_{x}U(x,y)+\frac{1-sp}{y}U_{y}(x,y)+U_{yy}(x,y)=0,&x\in\mathbb{H}^{n},y>0,\\
U(x,0)=f(x),&x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.\end{cases}

We will show in
Lemma

4.1
that the function
U
defined by

U(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}P(d(x,\xi),y)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
P
is the Poisson kernel given in the same lemma, is a solution of (
1.4
). We define an extension operator
E_{s,p}
by
E_{s,p}[f]:=U
. The following theorem is our next main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1
, and
p>1
. Let
u\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. If
p\in(1,\frac{2}{2-s}]
, assume
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
additionally. Then

\begin{split}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)&=C_{3}\lim_{y\searrow 0}\frac{E_{s,p}[\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\cdot))](x,y)}{y^{sp}}\\
&=\frac{C_{3}}{sp}\lim_{y\searrow 0}y^{1-sp}\partial_{y}\Big(E_{s,p}[\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\cdot))]\Big)(x,y),\end{split}

where

C_{3}=\frac{p}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}/2}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\frac{2^{2s}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}{|\Gamma(-s)|}.

The last result is the pointwise convergence of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as
s\to 1^{-}
. As one can expect, the fractional
p
-Laplacian converges to the
p
-Laplacian as a limit. Recall that the
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{p}u(x)=-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u(x)|^{p-2}\nabla u(x))
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 2
, and
u\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. For
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
,

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{p}u(x).

The pointwise convergence of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on Euclidean spaces is well known
[
4
,
15
,
25
]
. Recall that the proof uses Taylor’s theorem and the following computations:

(1.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{R}^{\infty}K(\rho)\rho^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega&=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{sp}R^{-sp},\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}K(\rho)\rho^{p+n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega&=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{p(1-s)}R^{p(1-s)},\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{R}K(\rho)\rho^{\beta+p+n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega&=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{\beta+p(1-s)}R^{\beta+p(1-s)},\end{split}

where
\beta>0
and
K(\rho)=\rho^{-n-sp}
is the kernel for the fractional
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, in our framework we need the integrals in (
1.5
) with the kernel
K
and the volume element
\rho^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega
replaced by
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
and
\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega
, respectively. These integrals do not seem to be of a form that is easily computed. Instead, we compute the limits of these integrals as
s\to 1^{-}
, which are sufficient to establish
Theorem

1.5
. This is still not straightforward, but can be obtained by using the asymptotic behavior of modified Bessel functions.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the basics of the hyperbolic spaces and collect some facts about the modified Bessel function.

2.1.
The hyperbolic space

There are several models for hyperbolic spaces, but let us focus on the hyperboloid model in this paper. The hyperboloid model is given by

\mathbb{H}^{n}=\left\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x_{0}^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{n}^{2}=1,x_{0}>0\right\}

with the Lorentzian metric
-\mathrm{d}x_{0}^{2}+\mathrm{d}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\mathrm{d}x_{n}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The Lorentzian metric induces the natural internal product

[x,\xi]=x_{0}\xi_{0}-x_{1}\xi_{1}-\cdots-x_{n}\xi_{n}

on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Moreover, the distance between two points
x
and
\xi
is given by

d(x,\xi)=\cosh^{-1}([x,\xi]).

Using the polar coordinates,
\mathbb{H}^{n}
can also be realized as

\mathbb{H}^{n}=\left\{x=(\cosh r,\sinh r\,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:r\geq 0,\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right\}.

Then, the metric and the volume element are given by
\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\sinh^{2}r\,\mathrm{d}\omega^{2}
and
\sinh^{n-1}r\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\omega
, respectively.

2.2.
The modified Bessel function

The modified Bessel functions naturally appear in the study of hyperbolic geometry. In this paper, they are used to describe the kernel of the fractional
p
-Laplacian and the Poisson kernel. For this purpose, we recall the definition and some properties of the modified Bessel functions.

We call the ordinary differential equation

\rho^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}y}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}}+\rho\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}\rho}-(\rho^{2}+\nu^{2})y=0

the modified Bessel equation. The solutions are given by

I_{\nu}(\rho)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!\Gamma(\nu+j+1)}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)^{2j+\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{\nu}(\rho)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\nu}(\rho)-I_{\nu}(\rho)}{\sin\nu\pi},

and they are called the
modified Bessel functions of the first and the second kind
, respectively. Since only
K_{\nu}
appears in this work, we focus on the properties of
K_{\nu}
. This function has the following integral representation (see
[
30
, 10.32.10]
):

(2.1)

K_{\nu}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho\right)^{\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\nu-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The asymptotic behavior of
K_{\nu}
is given by

(2.2)

\begin{split}&K_{\nu}(\rho)\sim\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(\nu)\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)^{-\nu}\quad\text{as }\rho\to 0^{+},\text{ for }\nu>0,\text{ and}\\
&K_{\nu}(\rho)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\rho}}e^{-\rho}\quad\text{as }\rho\to\infty.\end{split}

Moreover,
K_{\nu}
satisfies the following recurrence relations:

(2.3)

K_{\nu}^{\prime}=-K_{\nu-1}-\frac{\nu}{\rho}K_{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{\nu}^{\prime}=-K_{\nu+1}+\frac{\nu}{\rho}K_{\nu}.

We also recall that
K_{\nu}
is increasing with respect to
\nu>0
. For further properties of the modified Bessel functions, the reader may consult the handbook
[
30
]
.

In the sequel, functions of the form
\rho^{-\nu}K_{\nu}(a\rho)
with
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
will appear frequently. For notational convenience, we define

(2.4)

\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho):=\rho^{-\nu}K_{\nu}(a\rho).

Then, it follows from (
2.3
)

(2.5)

-\partial_{\rho}(\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(f(\rho)))=af^{\prime}(\rho)f(\rho)\mathscr{K}_{\nu+1,a}(f(\rho))

for any differentiable function
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
.

3.
Nonlinear Bochner’s formula

The main goal of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.3
. One of the crucial tools in the proof is the explicit formula for the heat kernel on hyperbolic spaces, which is given in
[
23
]
as follows:

(3.1)

p(t,\rho)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}

when
n=2m+1\geq 1
is odd, and

(3.2)

p(t,\rho)=\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}t^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{re^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r

when
n=2m\geq 2
is even. Note that the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) has the unique solution

w(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}p(t,d(x,\xi))f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

provided that
f
is a bounded continuous function.

Before proving
Theorem

1.3
, we provide a series of auxiliary lemmas required for its proof. In the course of the argument, we also establish
Proposition

1.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu>1/2
,
a\geq 1/2
, and
y\geq 0
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
define

F_{m}(r):=\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right),

where
\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}
is the function given in (
2.4
). Then,
F_{m}
is integrable on
(\rho,\infty)
and satisfies

(3.3)

\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{m}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{m+1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

for all
\rho>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.

Note that for any
j\geq 1

-\partial_{r}\left(\frac{(e^{r}+e^{-r})^{j-1}}{(e^{r}-e^{-{r}})^{j}}\right)=j\frac{(e^{r}+e^{-r})^{j}}{(e^{r}-e^{-r})^{j+1}}-(j-1)\frac{(e^{r}+e^{-r})^{j-2}}{(e^{r}-e^{-r})^{j-1}}.

Therefore, all derivatives of
\frac{1}{\sinh r}
(and
\frac{r}{\sinh r}
) have the same asymptotic behavior as
e^{-r}
(and
re^{-r}
, respectively) as
r\to\infty
. Hence,
F_{m}(r)\sim r^{-\nu-1/2}e^{(1/2-m-a)r}
as
r\to\infty
, which shows that the function
F_{m}
is integrable.

Using the integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}F_{m}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r&=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}2\partial_{r}\left(\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}\right)\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}2\sinh r\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}r.\end{split}

Thus, the recurrence relation (
3.3
) follows by applying the Leibniz integral rule.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\nu>1/2
,
a\geq 1/2
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then, the function

\rho\mapsto\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho)

is positive.

Proof.

Using the formula (
2.1
) and change of variables, we have

\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho)=\frac{a^{\nu}}{2^{\nu+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t-\frac{(a\rho)^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\nu-1}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2(2a)^{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1/2}}e^{-a^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{\nu+1/2}}.

Thus, recalling the expression of the heat kernel (
3.1
) for odd-dimensional case, we obtain

\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(m^{2}-a^{2})t}p(t,\rho)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{\nu+1/2}}.

The conclusion follows from the positivity of the heat kernel
p
.
∎

As a consequence of
Lemma

3.2
, we obtain the positivity of the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<s<1
, and
p>1
. The kernel
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
is positive.

In the following lemma, we consider a class of functions more general than
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
to allow for a broader applicability in later results. Note that
Proposition

1.2
follows from
Lemma

3.4
with
\nu=\frac{1+sp}{2}
and
a=\frac{n-1}{2}
, together with (
2.2
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu>1/2
,
a\geq 1/2
, and
y\geq 0
. Let

K(\rho)=\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(\rho)

when
n\geq 3
is odd and

K(\rho)=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(r)\mathrm{d}r

when
n\geq 2
is even. Then, there exist
c,C>0
such that

c\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n-1}{2}+\nu}(a\rho)\leq K(\rho)\leq C\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n-1}{2}+\nu}(a\rho)

for all
\rho>0
.

In order to prove
Lemma

3.4
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a>0
and
\nu>-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{\sinh^{-n/2+1}r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}r^{-\nu}K_{n/2+\nu}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{n}{2})}\rho^{-\nu}\sinh^{-n/2+1}(\rho)K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)

as
\rho\to 0^{+}
up to dimensional constants.

Proof.

By the change of variables
r=\rho t
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\frac{r^{-\nu}}{\rho^{-\nu}}\frac{\sinh^{-n/2+1}r}{\sinh^{-n/2+1}\rho}\frac{K_{n/2+\nu}(ar)}{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\rho t^{-\nu}}{\sqrt{\cosh(\rho t)-\cosh\rho}}\frac{\sinh^{-n/2+1}(\rho t)}{\sinh^{-n/2+1}\rho}\frac{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho t)}{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We define for each
\rho\in(0,1)
a function
f_{\rho}
by

\displaystyle f_{\rho}(t)=\frac{\rho t^{-\nu}}{\sqrt{\cosh(\rho t)-\cosh\rho}}\frac{\sinh^{-n/2+1}(\rho t)}{\sinh^{-n/2+1}\rho}\frac{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho t)}{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)},\quad t\in(1,\infty).

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh(\rho t)-\cosh\rho}{\rho^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}(t^{2}-1)\quad\text{and}\quad\sinh(\rho t)\geq(\sinh\rho)t.

Moreover, by
[
26
, Equation (2.17)]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho t)}{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)}\leq t^{-\frac{n}{2}-\nu}.

Thus,
f_{\rho}
is bounded from above by a function

\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{t^{-n-2\nu+1}}{\sqrt{(t^{2}-1)/2}},

which is integrable on
(0,\infty)
. Indeed, by the change of variables
t^{2}-1=\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{-1/2}}{(1+\tau)^{n/2+\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}B\left(\frac{1}{2},\nu+\frac{n-1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{n}{2})}<\infty,

where
B
is Euler’s Beta Integral (see
[
30
, 5.12.3]
).

For fixed
t\in(1,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\cosh(\rho t)-\cosh\rho}{\rho^{2}}\to\frac{1}{2}(t^{2}-1),\quad\frac{\sinh(\rho t)}{\sinh\rho}\to t,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho t)}{K_{n/2+\nu}(a\rho)}\to t^{-n/2-\nu}

as
\rho\to 0^{+}
. Hence, we obtain
\lim_{\rho\to 0}f_{\rho}(t)=f(t)
. Therefore, the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem concludes the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.4
.

We prove the odd-dimensional case
n=2m+1
first. We already provided a simple way to chase the asymptotic behavior of operator
\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}
at the former part of proof of
Lemma

3.1
. By applying the similar argument one can shows that
K(\rho)\sim\rho^{-2(m+\nu)}
as
\rho\to 0^{+}
and
K(\rho)\sim\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m)\rho}
as
\rho\to\infty
. In other words,
K(\rho)\sim\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-m}K_{m+\nu}(a\rho)
as
\rho\to 0^{+}
and
\rho\to\infty
by (
2.2
). Since
K
is positive by
Lemma

3.2
and continuous in
(0,\infty)
, there exist
c,C>0
such that

c\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-m}K_{m+\nu}(a\rho)\leq K(\rho)\leq C\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-m}K_{m+\nu}(a\rho)

for all
\rho>0
.

Let us consider the even-dimensional case
n=2m
. By using the result for the odd-dimensional case, we obtain for
\rho
sufficiently close to
\infty
that

\displaystyle K(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{e^{r}}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r-\rho}{2}}\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r+\rho}{2}}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m)r}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\rho}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{t}{2}}}(t+\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m-1)(t+\rho)}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+\frac{2m-1}{2})\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{t}{2}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+\frac{n-1}{2})\rho}

and

\displaystyle K(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}2\sinh r\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\rho}^{\infty}e^{r}\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r-\rho}{2}}\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r+\rho}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m+1)r}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq c\sqrt{\sinh\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\sinh\frac{t}{2}}(t+\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m)(t+\rho)}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq c\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+\frac{2m-1}{2})\rho}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\sinh\frac{t}{2}}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+m)t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq c\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}e^{-(a+\frac{n-1}{2})\rho}

for some constants
c,C>0
. In other words,
K
is comparable to
\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n-1}{2}+\nu}(a\rho)
as
\rho\to\infty
.

On the other hand,
K(\rho)
is comparable to
\rho^{-2\nu-(n-1)}
, or to
\rho^{-\nu}(\sinh\rho)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}K_{\frac{n-1}{2}+\nu}(a\rho)
, as
\rho\to 0^{+}
. Indeed, by using the result for the odd-dimensional case once again, we infer that
K(\rho)
is comparable to

\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh^{-m+1}r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}r^{-\nu}K_{m+\nu}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r.

By
Lemma

3.5
, it is in turn comparable to

\rho^{-\nu}\sinh^{-m+1}(\rho)K_{m+\nu}(a\rho)

near
\rho=0^{+}
. Since
K
is positive and continuous in
(0,\infty)
, the desired result follows.
∎

Let us now prove
Theorem

1.3
using the heat kernel and previous lemmas.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and define
g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)=\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\chi_{d(x,\xi)>\varepsilon}
. The heat semigroup associated to
g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)
is given by

e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}\right)g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

when
n=2m+1\geq 3
is odd and

e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{t^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t}}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{re^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}\,\mathrm{d}r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

when
n=2m\geq 2
is even, where
\rho=d(x,\xi)
. We will prove

(3.4)

c_{n,s,p}\int_{d(x,\xi)>\varepsilon}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi=C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}

in both cases.

Let us first consider the odd-dimensional case. We fix
\delta>0
and integrate the heat semigroup with respect to the singular measure
t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t
over the interval
(\delta,\infty)
to obtain

(3.5)

\begin{split}&C_{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=C_{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}\right)g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}.\end{split}

Note that this expression is well defined since
|e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}
for some constant
C>0
. By applying Fubini’s theorem, we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}&C_{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}\right)g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=\frac{C_{2}}{(2\pi)^{m}(4\pi)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Furthermore, since all partial derivatives of
e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}
with respect to
\rho
are integrable over the interval
(\delta,\infty)
, the dominated convergence theorem shows that

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\left(\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Note that the function
e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}
is integrable on
(0,\infty)
. Indeed, the formula (
2.1
) and the change of variables show

(3.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=m^{1+sp}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t-\frac{(m\rho)^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=2(2m)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},m}(\rho).

Thus, (
3.4
) in the odd-dimensional case follows by combining (
3.5
)–(
3.8
) and passing to the limit
\delta\searrow 0
.

We next consider the even-dimensional case. Similarly to as in the odd-dimensional case, we obtain

\begin{split}&C_{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=C_{2}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{t^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t}}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{re^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=\frac{C_{2}}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{5+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\frac{r\,\mathrm{d}r\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}.\end{split}

Moreover, we have from (
2.1
) and (
2.5
)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{5+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t&=2(2m-1)^{\frac{3+sp}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{3+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r)\\
&=4(2m-1)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{r}\right)\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r).\end{split}

Thus, we deduce

\begin{split}&C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=c_{n,s,p}C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{(-\partial_{r})\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r)}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=c_{n,s,p}C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,g_{\varepsilon}(x,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\end{split}

where we used
Lemma

3.1

(m-1)
-times with
\nu=\frac{1+sp}{2}
,
a=\frac{2m-1}{2}
, and
y=0
in the last equality. This proves (
3.4
) in the even-dimensional case.

On the one hand, the integral in the right-hand side of (
3.4
) converges to the Cauchy principal value

\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi

as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. For the left-hand side of (
3.4
), on the other hand, we need to estimate

A:=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\cdot))](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[g_{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)](x)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}.

Proceeding as above, we have

|A|\lesssim\left|\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{d(x,\xi)\leq\varepsilon}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|.

Thus, applying
Lemma

A.1
to
K=\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}\chi_{\{d(x,\xi)\leq\varepsilon\}}
yields

(3.9)

|A|\lesssim\int_{d(x,\xi)\leq\varepsilon}\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho,

where
\alpha=2p-2
when
p\in(\frac{2}{2-s},2)
and
\alpha=p
when
p\in(1,\frac{2}{2-s}]\cup[2,\infty)
. Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}
is positive by
Corollary

3.3
. Moreover,
Lemma

3.4
(or
Proposition

1.2
) shows that the function
\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho
is integrable near zero and hence the right-hand side of (
3.9
) converges to zero as

. Therefore, the left-hand side of (
3.4
) converges to that of (
1.1
) as

.
∎

4.
Extension problem

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.4
, which provides another representation of the fractional
p
-Laplacian on hyperbolic spaces. We first find the Poisson formula and relate the heat semigroup to the extension problem (
1.4
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
p>1
. If
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, then

E_{s,p}[f](x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}P(d(x,\xi),y)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

is a solution of the extension problem (
1.4
), where
P(\rho,y)
is the Poisson kernel given by

P(\rho,y)=C_{4}\,y^{sp}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+y^{2}}\right)

when
n\geq 3
odd and

P(\rho,y)=C_{4}\,y^{sp}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

when
n\geq 2
even; here,
\mathscr{K}_{\nu,a}
is the function given in (
2.4
) and

C_{4}=\frac{1}{2^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\left(\frac{n-1}{4}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{4}}.

Moreover,
E_{s,p}[f]
has an alternative representation

(4.1)

E_{s,p}[f](x,y)=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}.

Proof.

For each
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
y>0
, we define
V(x,y)
by the function given in the right-hand side of (
4.1
). Then, we have

V(x,y)=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}p(t,\rho)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}},

where
\rho=d(x,\xi)
. Recalling the expression (
3.1
) for the heat kernel
p(t,\rho)
and using (
2.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}V(x,y)&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}\left(\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}\right)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+sp/2}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}t-\frac{\rho^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}P(d(x,\xi),y)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{split}

when
n=2m+1
is odd. If
n=2m
is even, then we use (
3.2
) instead of (
3.1
) to have

\begin{split}V(x,y)&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{t^{-\frac{5+sp}{2}}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t}}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{re^{-\frac{r^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}\,\mathrm{d}r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{5+sp}{2}}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t}}{2(2\pi)^{m+1/2}}\frac{re^{-\frac{r^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}\,\mathrm{d}t}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Moreover, using (
2.1
) we compute

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(2m-1)^{2}}{4}t-\frac{r^{2}+y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-\frac{5+sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t&=2(2m-1)^{\frac{3+sp}{2}}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{3+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\\
&=4(2m-1)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{r}\right)\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}V(x,y)&=C_{4}\,y^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{(-\partial_{r})\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}P(d(x,\xi),y)f(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\end{split}

in the even-dimensional case as well, where we used
Lemma

3.1
in the last equality.

It only remains to prove that
V
solves the extension problem (
1.4
). Since the heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f]
solves (
1.2
),
V
satisfies

\Delta_{x}V=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\left(e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using the integration by parts and the fact that
|e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\Delta_{x}V&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\left(\left[e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\right]_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)\partial_{t}\left(e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
&=-\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)\left(\frac{y^{2}}{4}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-3-\frac{sp}{2}}-\left(1+\frac{sp}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-2-\frac{sp}{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}V_{y}&=\frac{spy^{sp-1}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad-\frac{y^{sp+1}}{2^{sp+1}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-2-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

and

\begin{split}V_{yy}&=\frac{sp(sp-1)y^{sp-2}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad-\frac{2sp+1}{2^{sp+1}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}y^{sp}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-2-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+\frac{y^{sp+2}}{2^{sp+2}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}[f](x)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}t^{-3-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

one can easily compute

\Delta_{x}V(x,y)+\frac{1-sp}{y}V_{y}(x,y)+V_{yy}(x,y)=0.

Finally, we prove
V(x,0)=f(x)
. Indeed, we have
P(\rho,y)\to 0
as
y\searrow 0
if
\rho\neq 0
by definition. Moreover, since the heat kernel
p(t,\rho)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}p(t,d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi=1,

we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}P(d(x,\xi),y)\,\mathrm{d}\xi&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}p(t,d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}\\
&=\frac{y^{sp}}{2^{sp}\Gamma(\frac{sp}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4t}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}}=1.\end{split}

This concludes that
V
solves the extension problem (
1.4
).
∎

Let us now prove
Theorem

1.4
by using the Poisson formula in
Lemma

4.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Since
c_{n,s,p}C_{1}=C_{3}C_{4}
, by
Lemma

4.1
it is enough to show

\left|\,\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))K(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|\to 0

as
y\searrow 0
, where

K(\rho)=\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho)-\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{\rho^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\right)

when
n
is odd and

K(\rho)=\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}(r)-\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}r

when
n
is even.

We first split the integral as follows:

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))K(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{d(x,\xi)\leq 1}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))K(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|+\left|\int_{d(x,\xi)>1}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))K(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|\\
&=J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

For
J_{1}
, we apply
Lemma

A.1
to
K\chi_{\{d(x,\xi)\leq 1\}}
to obtain

J_{1}\lesssim\int_{d(x,\xi)\leq 1}d(x,\xi)^{\alpha}|K(d(x,\xi))|\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\alpha=2p-2
when
p\in(\frac{2}{2-s},2)
and
\alpha=p
when
p\in(1,\frac{2}{2-s}]\cup[2,\infty)
. For
J_{2}
, we have

J_{2}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{p-1}\int_{d(x,\xi)>1}|K(d(x,\xi))|\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Therefore, the dominated convergence theorem concludes that
J_{1}+J_{2}\to 0
as
y\searrow 0
.
∎

5.
Pointwise convergence

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.5
. As mentioned in
Section

1
, the limits of the integrals

(5.1)

c_{n,s,p}\int_{R}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho,\quad c_{n,s,p}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho,

and

(5.2)

c_{n,s,p}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\beta+p}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho,\quad\beta>0,

as
s\to 1^{-}
, play a key role in the proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

In the following series of lemmas, we compute limits of the integrals in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
p>1
. For any
R>0
,

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}c_{n,s,p}\int_{R}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
n=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
. Since
c_{n,s,p}C_{1}\leq C(1-s)
for some
C=C(n,p)>0
, by using
Corollary

3.3
we have

(5.3)

\begin{split}0&\leq c_{n,s,p}\int_{R}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\lesssim(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{2m}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},m}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to show that the right-hand side of (
5.3
) converges to zero as
s\to 1^{-}
. We actually prove the following stronger statement:

(5.4)

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0\quad\text{for each }a>0.

We use the induction on
m
. When
m=1
, using (
2.5
) and the fact that
K_{\nu}
is increasing with respect to
\nu>0
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{1+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho&=a\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh^{a}\rho)\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(a\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq a\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh^{a}\rho)\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(a\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

By (
2.2
), there exists
M=M(p)>1
such that

(5.5)

K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(\rho)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\rho}}e^{-\rho}\quad\text{for }\rho>M.

The inequalities
\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}\leq\max\{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}},\rho^{-\frac{1+p}{2}}\}
and
\sinh\rho<e^{\rho}
, together with (
5.5
), yield

\begin{split}&\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh^{a}\rho)\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(a\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\int_{R}^{M/a}(\sinh^{a}\rho)\max\left\{\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}},\rho^{-\frac{1+p}{2}}\right\}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(a\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\int_{M/a}^{\infty}\rho^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Note that the first integral in the right-hand side of the inequality above is a constant depending on
a
,
p
, and
R
only. For the second integral, we estimate

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\int_{M/a}^{\infty}\rho^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\rho=\frac{2}{sp}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\left(\frac{a}{M}\right)^{\frac{sp}{2}}\leq\frac{2}{sp}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\max\left\{\left(\frac{a}{M}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},1\right\}.

Thus, we arrive at

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{1+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0,

which proves (
5.4
) for
m=1
.

Assume now that (
5.4
) is true for
m
and prove it for
m+1
. Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+1+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=(m+a)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+a-1}\rho\cosh\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\quad-\left[\sinh^{m+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\right]_{R}^{\infty}.

Thus, taking the limit
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)
and using
Lemma

3.4
yields

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+1+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)(m+a)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+a-1}\rho\cosh\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Therefore, by an inequality

(5.6)

\cosh\rho\leq\coth R\sinh\rho\quad\text{for }\rho\geq R,

Lemma

3.2
, and the induction hypothesis, we conclude

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+1+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq(m+a)(\coth R)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{m+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of the lemma in the odd-dimensional case.

Let us next consider the even-dimensional cases
n=2m
with
m\geq 1
. Similarly as in the odd-dimensional case, since

\begin{split}0&\leq c_{n,s,p}\int_{R}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\lesssim(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{2m-1}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho,\end{split}

the desired result will follow once we prove the following:

(5.7)

\begin{split}\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{2m-1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0\\
\quad\text{for each }a\geq 1/2.\end{split}

If
m=1
, then

\begin{split}&\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq a\int_{R}^{M/a}\sinh^{\frac{1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}r^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\quad+a\int_{M/a}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}r^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho=:J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

For
J_{2}
, we use (
5.5
) to obtain

J_{2}\leq\sqrt{\pi a}\int_{M/a}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh^{\frac{1}{2}+a}\rho}{\rho^{1+\frac{sp}{2}}e^{a\rho}}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho.

Since

\begin{split}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r+\rho}{2}\sinh\frac{r-\rho}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sinh\rho}}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r-\rho}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sinh\rho}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sinh\frac{r}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\sinh\rho}}\end{split}

and
\sinh^{a}\rho\leq e^{a\rho}
, we have

J_{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}\pi\sqrt{a}\int_{M/a}^{\infty}\rho^{-1-\frac{sp}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\rho=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}\frac{\pi\sqrt{a}}{sp}\left(\frac{a}{M}\right)^{\frac{sp}{2}}.

On the other hand, for
J_{1}
we observe

J_{1}\leq a\int_{R}^{M/a}\sinh^{\frac{1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\max\{r^{-\frac{1+p}{2}},r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}K_{\frac{3+p}{2}}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho.

Since the inner integral is continuous and integrable on
[R,M/a]
,
J_{1}
is controlled by some constant
C=C(a,p,R)>0
. Therefore, we conclude
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)(J_{1}+J_{2})=0
, which proves (
5.7
) for
m=1
.

Finally, let us assume that (
5.7
) holds for
m
and prove it for
m+1
. By
Lemma

3.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{2m+1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{2m+1}{2}+a}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Using integration by parts, (
5.6
), and
Lemma

3.2
and then taking
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)
as in the odd-dimensional case, we deduce

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{2m+1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq C\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{R}^{\infty}\sinh^{\frac{2m-1}{2}+a}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\end{split}

for some
C=C(m,a,R)
. Therefore, the statement (
5.7
) for
m+1
follows by the induction hypothesis.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
p>1
. For any
R>0
,

(5.8)

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}c_{n,s,p}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho=\frac{1}{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p+n}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}.

Proof.

Let us first consider the odd-dimensional case
n=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
. One can easily check that (
5.8
) is equivalent to

(5.9)

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},m}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\frac{2^{m-1}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{m}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+1}{2}\right)\end{split}

by using
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)|\Gamma(-s)|=1
. Actually, we will prove the following statement, which is slightly stronger than (
5.9
):

(5.10)

\begin{split}\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
=\frac{2^{m-1}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+1}{2}\right)\quad\text{for each }a\geq 1.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, then there exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(5.11)

1-\varepsilon\leq\frac{\sinh\rho}{\rho}\leq 1+\varepsilon

for all
\rho\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Moreover, using the asymptotic behavior (
2.2
) of the modified Bessel function, for each
s\in[0,1]
we find
\delta_{s}>0
such that

(5.12)

\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}\leq K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(\rho)\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)^{-\frac{3+sp}{2}}

for all
\rho\in(0,\delta_{s})
. Furthermore, since
\{K_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in[3/2,(3+p)/2]}
is equicontinuous, we may assume that
\delta_{s}>0
has been chosen continuously on
s
. Let us take
\delta=\delta_{0}\land\min_{s\in[0,1]}\delta_{s}\land R
, then
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p,R)>0
, and (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) hold for all
\rho\in(0,\delta)
.

We fix
a\geq 1
and denote by
G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)
the integrand in the left-hand side of (
5.10
). Then,
|G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)|
is bounded by the function
\sup_{0\leq s\leq 1}|G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)|
, which is independent of
s
and bounded on a compact interval
[\delta/a,R]
. Thus, we have

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{\delta/a}^{R}G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0,

and hence

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.

Let us now prove (
5.10
) by induction. When
m=1
, we first use (
2.5
) to have

G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)=a\rho^{p-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(a\rho)\sinh\rho.

If
\rho<\delta/a
, then
\rho\leq a\rho<\delta
since
a\geq 1
. Thus, we utilize (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) to obtain

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\rho^{p(1-s)-1}\leq G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\rho^{p(1-s)-1}.

This leads us to the inequalities

\begin{split}\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho&=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p(1-s)-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right)\end{split}

and

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right).

Therefore, the statement (
5.10
) for
m=1
follows by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Assume now that (
5.10
) holds for
m\geq 1
. Then, a similar argument shows

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m+2}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p+2m+1}(-\partial_{\rho})\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho,\end{split}

where nonnegativity of the integrands follows from
Lemma

3.2
. Using the integration by parts, (
5.11
), and the induction hypothesis, we arrive at

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}(p+2m+1)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p+2m}\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m}}(p+2m+1)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m}}(p+2m+1)\frac{2^{m-1}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+1}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m}}\frac{2^{m}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+3}{2}\right).\end{split}

Similarly, we obtain

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}G_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}}\frac{2^{m}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+3}{2}\right),

from which (
5.10
) for
m+1
follows by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
. The statement (
5.10
) has been proved for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, finishing the proof of (
5.8
) for the odd-dimensional case.

Let us next consider the even-dimensional case
n=2m
with
m\geq 1
. In this case, (
5.8
) is equivalent to

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m-1}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},\frac{2m-1}{2}}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{2^{m-1}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{m-1/2}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m}{2}\right).\end{split}

As in the odd-dimensional case, we will prove a stronger statement:

(5.13)

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m-1}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{2^{m-1}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m}{2}\right)\quad\text{for each }a\geq 1/2.\end{split}

Recall that we have taken
\delta
so that (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) hold for all
\rho\in(0,\delta)
. Let us fix
a\geq 1/2
. By
Lemma

3.5
, for each
s\in[0,1]
we find
\tilde{\delta}_{s}>0
such that

(5.14)

\begin{split}(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2+sp}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{3+sp}{2})}\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(a\rho)&\leq\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{r^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(ar)}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2+sp}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{3+sp}{2})}\rho^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(a\rho)\end{split}

for all
\rho\in(0,\tilde{\delta}_{s})
. Moreover, we may assume that
\tilde{\delta}_{s}
has been chosen continuously on
s
since
\{K_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in[3/2,(3+p)/2]}
is equicontinuous. Let
\tilde{\delta}=\delta\land\min_{s\in[0,1]}\tilde{\delta}_{s}
, then
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p,R,a)>0
and (
5.14
) holds for all
\rho\in(0,\tilde{\delta})
.

We denote by
H_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)
the integrand in the left-hand side of (
5.13
). Then, the same argument as in the odd-dimensional case shows

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}H_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{2a}}H_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.

We argue by induction again to prove (
5.13
). If
m=1
, then

H_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)=a\rho^{p}\sinh\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}r^{-\frac{1+sp}{2}}K_{\frac{3+sp}{2}}(ar)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Since
a\geq 1/2
, we have
\rho<\delta
and
a\rho<\delta
for
\rho<\frac{\delta}{2a}
. Thus, by (
5.11
), (
5.14
), and (
5.12
), we obtain

\begin{split}(1-\varepsilon)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{2+sp}{2}\right)\rho^{p(1-s)-1}&\leq H_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{2+sp}{2}\right)\rho^{p(1-s)-1}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}(1-\varepsilon)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right)&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}H_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{3}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+3}{2}\right),\end{split}

from which we deduce (
5.13
) for
m=1
by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Suppose that (
5.13
) is true for
m\geq 1
. Then, by (
5.11
),
Lemma

3.1
, and
Lemma

3.2
, we have

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}H_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{2a}}\rho^{p}\sinh^{2m+1}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m+1}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{2a}}\rho^{p+2m}(-\partial_{\rho})\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Using the integration by parts, (
5.11
), and the induction hypothesis, we arrive at

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}H_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}(p+2m)\\
&\quad\times\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{2a}}\rho^{p+2m-1}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m-1}}(p+2m)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta}{2a}}H_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m-1}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{2^{m}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+2}{2}\right).\end{split}

The inequality

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}H_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m-1}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{2^{m}}{p}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2m+2}{2}\right)

can be obtained in the same way. Thus, we conclude that (
5.13
) for
m+1
holds by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
. This finishes the proof for the even-dimensional case.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
p>1
. For any
R>0
and
\beta>0
,

(5.15)

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}c_{n,s,p}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p+\beta}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0.

Proof.

We proceed as in the previous lemma to prove (
5.15
). When
n=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
, we show

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p+\beta}\sinh^{2m}\rho\left(\frac{-\partial_{\rho}}{\sinh\rho}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0\quad\text{for each }a\geq 1

by induction. Indeed, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let
\delta>0
be the constant given in the proof of
Lemma

5.2
. Then, by using (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) we prove

\begin{split}&\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\beta}G_{s,p,1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow}(1-s)(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\delta/a}\rho^{p(1-s)+\beta-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\lim_{s\nearrow}(1-s)(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{\frac{1+sp}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3+sp}{2}\right)\frac{1}{p(1-s)+\beta}\left(\frac{\delta}{a}\right)^{p(1-s)+\beta}=0\end{split}

for the case
m=1
, where
G_{s,p,m,a}
is the function defined in the proof of
Lemma

5.2
. Moreover, one can follow the steps in the proof of
Lemma

5.2
to obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m}}(p+\beta+2m+1)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\beta}G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\beta}G_{s,p,m+1,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2m+1}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2m}}(p+\beta+2m+1)\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{\beta}G_{s,p,m,a}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}\rho,\end{split}

which proves the induction step.

The even-dimensional case
n=2m
with
m\geq 1
can also be verified by proving

\begin{split}\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{p+\beta}\sinh^{2m-1}\rho\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh r}{\sqrt{\cosh r-\cosh\rho}}\left(\frac{-\partial_{r}}{\sinh r}\right)^{m}\mathscr{K}_{\frac{1+sp}{2},a}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\rho=0\end{split}

for each
a\geq 1/2
. This can be proved by the induction as in the previous lemma, so we omit the proof.
∎

Let us provide the proof of
Theorem

1.5
by using the pointwise representation (
1.1
) and Taylor’s theorem, and gathering pieces of limits in the preceding lemmas.

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Let
u\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and let
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
be such that
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
. Let
R>0
, then by
Lemma

5.1
we first have

\left|c_{n,s,p}\int_{d(x,\xi)\geq R}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|\lesssim c_{n,s,p}\int_{R}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho\to 0

as
s\to 1^{-}
. Thus, by the pointwise representation (
1.1
) of the fractional
p
-Laplacian, we obtain

(5.16)

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}c_{n,s,p}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Let
v=\exp_{x}^{-1}\xi
be a tangent vector in
T_{x}\mathbb{H}^{n}
and denote by
\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi
the point
\exp_{x}(-v)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))=\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi))
, we write

\begin{split}&\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}\Phi_{p}(u(x)-u(\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}(2u(x)-u(\xi)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}\left(|u(x)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi)|^{p-2}-|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}\right)(u(x)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi))\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=:J_{1}+J_{2}.\end{split}

By Taylor’s theorem, we have

u(x)-u(\xi)=-\langle\nabla u(x),v\rangle+O(|v|^{2}),\quad u(x)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi)=\langle\nabla u(x),v\rangle+O(|v|^{2}),

and

2u(x)-u(\xi)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi)=-\langle D^{2}u(x)v,v\rangle+O(|v|^{3}).

If we write
\omega=v/|v|
, then

|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}=|v|^{p-2}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}+O(|v|^{p-1}).

Thus, we obtain

|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}(2u(x)-u(\xi)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi))=-|v|^{p}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle+O(|v|^{p+1}).

Therefore, we deduce

(5.17)

\begin{split}J_{1}&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho^{p}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}O(d(x,\xi)^{p+1})\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

For
J_{2}
, since

\begin{split}&|u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi)-u(x)|^{p-2}-|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}\\
&=(p-2)|v|^{p-1}\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-4}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle+O(|v|^{p}),\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}&\left(|u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi)-u(x)|^{p-2}-|u(x)-u(\xi)|^{p-2}\right)(u(x)-u(\mathcal{T}_{x}\xi))\\
&=-(p-2)|v|^{p}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle+O(|v|^{p+1}).\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

(5.18)

\begin{split}J_{2}&=-\frac{p-2}{2}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho^{p}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(\rho)\sinh^{n-1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{d(x,\xi)<R}O(d(x,\xi)^{p+1})\mathcal{K}_{n,s,p}(d(x,\xi))\,\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

Combining (
5.16
), (
5.17
), and (
5.18
), and using
Lemma

5.2
and
Lemma

5.3
, we arrive at

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})^{s}_{p}u(x)=-\frac{p-1}{2}\frac{1}{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p+n}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

The argument as in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.8]
shows

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle\nabla u(x),\omega\rangle|^{p-2}\langle D^{2}u(x)\omega,\omega\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\gamma_{p}(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{p}

when
\nabla u(x)\neq 0
, where

(5.19)

\gamma_{p}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\omega_{n}|^{p-2}\omega_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+n}{2})}.

See
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
for the computation of (
5.19
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

Let

The cases

, respectively, for the case of Euclidean spaces. We omit the proof of
Lemma

A.1

On global solvability and regularity for generalized Rayleigh-Stokes equations with history-dependent nonlinearities Thanks: * Corresponding author. Email: thangnn@hnue.edu.vn (N.N.Thang)
Tran Dinh Ke & Nguyen Nhu Thang ∗

Abstract
. We are concerned with the initial value problem governed by generalized Rayleigh-Stokes equations, where the nonlinearity depends on history states and takes values in Hilbert scales of negative order. The solvability and Hölder regularity of solutions are proved by using fixed point arguments and embeddings of fractional Sobolev spaces. An application to a related inverse source problem is given.

35B40,35R11,35C15,45D05,45K05

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the following problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-(1+D_{t}^{\{m\}})\Delta u

\displaystyle=f(u,\mathcal{H}u)\text{ in }\Omega,t\in(0,T),

(1.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\;t\geq 0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=\xi\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.3)

where
f
is a given function,
\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
,
D_{t}^{\{m\}}
is the nonlocal derivative of Riemann-Liouville type defined by

D_{t}^{\{m\}}v(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}m(t-s)v(s)ds,

with kernel
m\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
,
\mathcal{H}
is the convolution operator given by

\mathcal{H}v(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\ell(t-\tau)v(\tau)d\tau,\;\ell\in L^{1}(0,T),

which tells us that
f
depends on the history state of the system.

The proposed system is a general model for some problems studied in literature. Indeed, in the case
m
is a constant, (
1.1
) is of classical diffusion type. If
m
is a regular function, e.g.
m\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
then one gets

\partial_{t}u-(1+m_{0})\Delta u-\int_{0}^{t}m_{1}(t-s)\Delta u(s)ds=f,

with
m_{0}=m(0)
và
m_{1}(t)=m^{\prime}(t)
, which is a nonclassical diffusion equation. Let
m(t)=m_{0}t^{-\alpha}/\Gamma(\alpha)
with
m_{0}>0
, then we see that (
1.1
) is a Rayleigh-Stokes equation, i.e.

\partial_{t}u-(1+m_{0}\partial_{t}^{\alpha})\Delta u=f.

(1.4)

The constitution of the last equation was formed in
[
10
,
19
]
to describe the behavior of second-grade fluids. It should be mentioned that, some numerical schemes for Rayleigh-Stokes equations were developed in
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
]
. On the other hand, analytical representations for solution of (
1.4
) in linear case were obtained in
[
11
,
19
]
, and recently, the regularity for nonlinear Rayleigh-Stokes equations has been established in
[
13
,
14
,
21
]
. For more studies related to (
1.4
), we refer the reader to
[
15
,
17
,
20
]
, where the terminal value problem was carried out.

In this work, we are interested in the solvability and regularity analysis for problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) in the circumstance that the nonlinearity function involves the history state expressed by
\mathcal{H}
. The appearance of
\mathcal{H}u
comes from, e.g. the inverse source problem as presented in the last section. In another way, this factor may arise from control problems, where the feedback requires some history information of the system. One will find that, the term
\mathcal{H}u
causes some difficulties for analyzing regularity of solutions. As an important feature of our study, it is noted that the nonlinearity function
f
is allowed to take weak values, i.e.
f(u,\mathcal{H}u)
may belong to dual of fractional Sobolev spaces. This enables us to consider the case when
f
contains polymonial or gradient terms, which have connections with practical applications. This also extends the recent results established in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

The rest of this note is as follows. In the next section, we first recall some notions and facts related to Hilbert scales and fractional Sobolev spaces. Additionally, a representation for the solution of linear problem will be shown together with some essential estimates for resolvent operator. Section 3 is devoted to proving the main results, including the global existence and Hölder regularity of solutions. It is worth noting, in particular, that the Hölder regularity result in our work can not be obtained by the technique used in
[
12
]
, since the differentiability of resolvent operator is unavailable on dual spaces. In order to overcome this impediment, we construct a regular closed subset of solution space, which is invariant under the solution operator, and make use of fixed point arguments. The last section shows an application of the obtained results, where we demonstrate that an inverse source problem governed by Rayleigh-Stokes equations is solvable by transforming it to a prototype of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Functional spaces

Let
-\Delta
be the Laplacian associated with the homogenuous Dirichlet boundary condition. Then one has a sequence of eigenfunctions
\{e_{n}\}
of
-\Delta
which forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and we have the following representation

-\Delta v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{n}\int\limits_{\Omega}v(x)e_{n}(x)dx\right)e_{n},

where
\lambda_{n}>0
is the eigenvalue corresponding to the eigenfunction

e_{n}
. For
\varrho\geq 0
, we define the following functional space

\mathbb{H}^{\varrho}:=\left\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)\bigg|\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\varrho}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}^{\frac{\varrho}{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x)e_{n}(x)dx\right|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{-\varrho}
denote the dual space of
\mathbb{H}^{\varrho}
with respect to the dual pair
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-\varrho,\varrho}
on
\mathbb{H}^{-\varrho}\times\mathbb{H}^{\varrho}
with the norm

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-\varrho}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{\varrho}{2}}\langle\varphi,e_{n}\rangle_{-\varrho,\varrho}\right|^{2}<\infty.

Clearly,
\mathbb{H}^{\varrho_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\varrho_{1}}
and
\mathbb{H}^{-\varrho_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{-\varrho_{2}}
with
\varrho_{2}\geq\varrho_{1}\geq 0
. The family of Hilbert spaces
\mathbb{H}^{\varrho},\varrho\in\mathbb{R}
, is said to be the Hilbert scales.

The fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\gamma}
,
\gamma\geq 0
, is defined as follows

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\gamma}:\;\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{H}^{-\gamma};

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\gamma}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\varphi(x)e_{n}(x)dx\right)e_{n}.

Then,
\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\varphi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\gamma}}=\|\varphi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}}
.

We now recall the notion of the fractional Sobolev spaces (see, e.g.
[
8
,
9
]
for details). For
r\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, the functional space

W^{r,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+pr}}dxdy<\infty\right\}

is called the fractional Sobolev space with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{r,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+pr}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}

For
r\geq 1
, denote by
[r]
the integral part of
r
, we define

W^{r,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{[r],p}(\Omega):D^{\eta}u\in W^{r-[r],p}(\Omega),\forall|\eta|\leq[r]\right\}

which is a Banach space with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{r,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\|u\|_{W^{[r],p}}^{p}(\Omega)+\sum_{|\eta|=[r]}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|D^{\eta}u(x)-D^{\eta}u(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+p(r-[r])}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

Denote

W_{0}^{r,p}(\Omega):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{W^{r,p}(\Omega)},H^{r}(\Omega):=W^{r,2}(\Omega),H_{0}^{r}(\Omega):=W_{0}^{r,2}(\Omega).

Assume that
\Omega
is a domain with sufficiently smooth boundary such that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{r}(\Omega)
with
0<r<1/2
, then
H_{0}^{r}(\Omega)=H^{r}(\Omega)
(see
[
3
, Corollary 8.10.1]
). It follows from
[
4
]
that

\mathbb{H}^{r}=\begin{cases}H_{0}^{r}(\Omega),&0\leq r<1/2,\\
H_{00}^{1/2}(\Omega)\varsubsetneqq H_{0}^{1/2}(\Omega),&r=1/2,\\
H_{0}^{r}(\Omega),&1/2<r\leq 1,\\
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{r}(\Omega),&1<r\leq 2,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
H_{00}^{1/2}(\Omega)
is the Lions-Magenes space determined by

H_{00}^{1/2}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in H^{1/2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{(\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega))^{2}}dx<\infty\right\}.

Then the following lemma is a direct consequence of (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Denote by
H^{-r}(\Omega)
the dual space of
H_{0}^{r}(\Omega)
with
r\geq 0
. If
0\leq r\leq r^{\prime}\leq 2
, then

\mathbb{H}^{r^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{r}\hookrightarrow H^{r}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-r}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{-r}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{-r^{\prime}}.

The following lemma represents embeddings between fractional Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 8.12.6]

Let
1\leq p,p^{\prime}\leq\infty,0\leq r,r^{\prime}<\infty
and
r^{\prime}-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}\geq r-\frac{d}{p}
. Then,

W^{r^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{r,p}(\Omega).

Using Lemma
2.1
and
2.2
, we have the following embeddings.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

a)

L^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{r}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{r}
if
\left\{-\frac{d}{2}<r\leq 0,p\geq\frac{2d}{d-2r}\right\}
.

b)

\mathbb{H}^{r}\hookrightarrow H^{r}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
if

\left\{0\leq r<\frac{d}{2},1\leq p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2r}\right\}.

2.2.
Representation of solutions to linear problem

In order to investigate problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), we assume the following hypothesis:

(
M
)

The function
m\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is nonnegative such that
a(t):=1+m(t)
is completely positive.

Recall that the real valued function
a
is said to be completely positive if the solutions to the following integral equations

\displaystyle s(t)+\theta\int_{0}^{t}a(t-\tau)s(\tau)d\tau=1,\;t\geq 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle r(t)+\theta\int_{0}^{t}a(t-\tau)r(\tau)d\tau=a(t),\;t>0,

(2.3)

are nonnegative for each
\theta>0
. The theory of completely positive functions can be found in
[
7
,
18
]
. An equivalent condition for
a
to be completely positive is as follows:

(PC)

There exists a nonincreasing and nonnegative function
k\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\epsilon\geq 0
such that

\epsilon a+k*a=1\text{ on }(0,\infty).

In the present work, we assume that
m
is unbounded on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which implies
\epsilon=0
. The condition (PC) is satisfied if
m
is completely monotone, i.e.
(-1)^{n}m^{(n)}(t)\geq 0
on
(0,\infty)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
16
]
).

First we consider the relaxation problem:

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(t)+\lambda(1+D_{t}^{\{m\}})\omega(t)

\displaystyle=0,\;t>0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\omega(0)

\displaystyle=1,

(2.5)

where the unknown
\omega
is a scalar function,
\lambda
is a positive parameter. Integrating both sides of (
2.4
) over
(0,t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega(t)+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}(1+m(t-\tau))\omega(\tau)d\tau=1,

(2.6)

which is just (
2.2
) with
\lambda=\theta
.

We denote by
\omega(t,\lambda)
the solution of (
2.6
) to emphasize the dependence of
\omega
on the parameter
\lambda
. We list some properties of
\omega
in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\omega
be the solution to (
2.4
)-(
2.5
). Then

(a)

\omega
is nonincreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

\displaystyle 0<\omega(t,\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{1+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}(1+m(\tau))d\tau},\;\forall t\geq 0,\;\lambda>0.

(b)

The following estimate holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\omega(\tau,\lambda)d\tau\leq\lambda^{-1}(1-\omega(t,\lambda)),\;\forall t\geq 0,\lambda>0.

(c)

For each
t>0
, the function
\lambda\mapsto\omega(t,\lambda)
is nonincreasing.

(d)

The function
v(t)=\omega(t,\lambda)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda)g(\tau)d\tau
is a solution to the problem

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(t)+\lambda(1+D^{\{m\}}_{t})v(t)

\displaystyle=g(t),

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=v_{0}.

Proof.

The properties (a) and (b) are implied from (
2.6
). The properties (c) and (d) were proved in
[
12
]
.
∎

Now we look for a representation of the solution to the following initial value linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-(1+D_{t}^{\{m\}})\Delta u

\displaystyle=F\;\text{ in }\Omega,t\in(0,T],

(2.7)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\;\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\;t\in[0,T],

(2.8)

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=\xi\;\text{ in }\Omega,

(2.9)

where
F\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Assume that

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)e_{n},\;F(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}(t)e_{n}.

Using these expansions in (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle u_{n}^{\prime}(t)+\lambda_{n}(1+D_{t}^{\{m\}})u_{n}(t)=F_{n}(t),

\displaystyle u_{n}(0)=\xi_{n}:=(\xi,e_{n}).

Applying Proposition
2.4
(d), we get

u_{n}(t)=\omega(t,\lambda_{n})\xi_{n}+\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})F_{n}(\tau)d\tau.

Therefore,

\displaystyle u(t)=S(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)F(\tau)d\tau,

(2.10)

where
S(t)
is the
resolvent operator
determined by

\displaystyle S(t)\xi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\omega(t,\lambda_{n})\xi_{n}e_{n},\;\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(2.11)

Obviously,
S(t)
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover, we have the following statements.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be the resolvent family defined by (
2.11
),
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
T>0
. Then,

(a)

S(\cdot)v\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
\|S(t)\|\leq\omega(t,\lambda_{1})
for all
t\geq 0
.

(b)

For
g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1})
,
\mu>0
, we have
S*g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
. Furthermore,

\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}d\tau,\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

(2.12)

(c)

If
m
is nonincreasing, then
S(\cdot)v\in C^{1}((0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and it holds that

\|S^{\prime}(t)\|\leq t^{-1}\;\text{ for all }t>0.

(2.13)

(d)

For
\delta\in(0,1)
,
g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta})
, we have

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau.

(e)

If
(1*m)^{-1}\in L^{1}(0,T)
, then for
g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu-2})
, we have

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-2}}}{(1*m)(t-\tau)}d\tau.

Proof.

The properties (a) and (c) were proved in
[
12
, Lemma 1]
. Now we show (b). For
g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1})
, we have

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{\mu}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})g_{n}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{2},\;g_{n}(\tau)=(g(\tau),e_{n}).

Moreover, by the Hölder inequality, we can estimate

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})g_{n}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})d\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})\lambda_{n}^{\mu-1}|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}d\tau.

Next, we prove (d). Assume that
g\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta})
. Then

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\mu}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})g_{n}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{2},\;g_{n}(\tau)=(g(\tau),e_{n}).

Using the Hölder inequality and Proposition
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})g_{n}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})d\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}}{1+\lambda_{n}(t-\tau)}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{\delta}(t-\tau)^{\delta}}d\tau,

here, we used the inequality
1+b\geq b^{\delta}
for
b\geq 0,\delta\in(0,1)
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|S*g(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\mu-1-\delta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau.

The last property is proved similarly by noting that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})g_{n}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})d\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{n})|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}}{1+\lambda_{n}(1*m)(t-\tau)}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|g_{n}(\tau)|^{2}}{(1*m)(t-\tau)}d\tau.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Global solvability and Hölder regularity

In order to solve problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), we require the following assumption on the nonlinearity:

(F)

The function
f:\mathbb{H}^{\mu}\times\mathbb{H}^{\mu}\to\mathbb{H}^{-\theta}
satisfies
f(0,0)=0
and for
\rho,\rho^{\prime}>0
, we have

\|f(v_{1},w_{1})-f(v_{2},w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\theta}}\leq L_{f}(\rho)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K_{f}(\rho^{\prime})\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},

where
\|v_{1}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},\|v_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\rho
,
\|w_{1}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},\|w_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\rho^{\prime}
,
0<\mu<2
,
\theta>0
,
L_{f}
and
K_{f}
are nonnegative functions.

By the representation of the solution to the linear problem given by (
2.10
), we have the following definition of mild solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{\mu}
. The function
u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
is called a mild solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) on the interval
[0,T]
if the following identity holds

u(t)=S(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)f(u(\tau),\mathcal{H}u(\tau))d\tau,\;t\in[0,T].

3.1.
Global solvability

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the condtion (F) is satisfied with
\theta=1+\delta-\mu
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and

\limsup\limits_{\rho\to 0}L_{f}(\rho)=L_{f}^{*},\;\limsup\limits_{\rho^{\prime}\to 0}K_{f}(\rho^{\prime})=K_{f}^{*},

such that

8T^{1-\delta}(1-\delta)^{-1}({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})<1.

Then there exists
\rho^{*}>0
such that for
\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{*}
, problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) possesses a unique mild solution
u
on
[0,T]
obeying
\|u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\rho^{*}
, for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We will show that the operator
\Phi
determined by

\Phi(u)(t)=S(t)\xi+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)f(u(\tau),\mathcal{H}u(\tau))d\tau,\;t\in[0,T],

has a fixed point in the space
C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
furnished by the norm
\|u\|_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}
.

Denote by
B_{\rho}
the closed ball of radius
\rho
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
which centers at the origin. For
u\in B_{\rho}
, we have
\mathcal{H}u\in B_{\rho^{\prime}}
with
\rho^{\prime}=\rho\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|S(t)\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+2\|S*f(u(\cdot),\mathcal{H}u(\cdot))(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|S(t)\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+2\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}\|f(u(\tau),\mathcal{H}u(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau,

by Lemma
2.5
(d). Using the condition (F), we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+4\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}[L_{f}(\rho)^{2}\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K_{f}(\rho^{\prime})^{2}\|\mathcal{H}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+4\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\rho^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+4T^{1-\delta}(1-\delta)^{-1}({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\rho^{2},

where
\epsilon>0
and
\rho^{*}>0
is chosen such that

8T^{1-\delta}(1-\delta)^{-1}({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\leq 1,\text{ for all }\rho\leq\rho^{*}.

Then, for
u\in B_{\rho^{*}}
and
\|\xi\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{*}
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\rho^{*},\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

Hence
\Phi(B_{\rho^{*}})\subset B_{\rho^{*}}
. It remains to verify that
\Phi
is a contraction on
B_{\rho^{*}}
. Indeed, for
u_{1},u_{2}\in B_{\rho^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(u_{1}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau))-f(u_{2}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+2K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|h\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau)-\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|h\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}.

This yields that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{1})(t)-\Phi(u_{2})(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\|S*[f(u_{1}(\cdot),\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\cdot))-f(u_{2}(\cdot),\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\cdot))](t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}\|f(u_{1}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau))-f(u_{2}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2T^{1-\delta}(1-\delta)^{-1}({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|h\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}.

Finally, we arrive at

\|\Phi(u_{1})-\Phi(u_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty}.

The proof is complete.
∎

In the case the function
f(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies global Lipschitz condition, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that the nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{H}^{\mu}\times\mathbb{H}^{\mu}\to\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}
is continuous and satisfies the condition

\|f(v_{1},w_{1})-f(v_{2},w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}\leq L^{*}_{f}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K^{*}_{f}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},

for all
v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{\mu}
, where
L^{*}_{f},K^{*}_{f}\geq 0
. Then, Problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) possesses a unique mild solution in the space
C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
.

Proof.

In
C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
, we use an equivalent norm

\|u\|_{\beta,\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{-\beta t}\|u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},

where
\beta>0
satisfies

L^{*}:=2({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\beta\tau}\tau^{-\delta}d\tau<1.

Consider the solution operator
\Phi
as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, where
u_{1},u_{2}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{1})(t)-\Phi(u_{2})(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}{L^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau)-\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau,

according to the assumption of the theorem and Lemma
2.5
(d). Then, it yields

\displaystyle e^{-2\beta t}\|\Phi(u_{1})(t)

\displaystyle-\Phi(u_{2})(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\beta(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}{L^{*}_{f}}^{2}[e^{-2\beta\tau}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\beta(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\delta}{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}[e^{-2\beta\tau}\|\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau)-\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\beta\tau}\tau^{-\delta}d\tau\right)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{\beta,\infty},

here, we have used the following estimate

\displaystyle e^{-2\beta\tau}\|\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau)-\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\beta(\tau-z)}\ell(\tau-z)[e^{-\beta z}\|u_{1}(z)-u_{2}(z)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]dz\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\beta z}|\ell(z)|dz\right)^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{\beta,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{\beta,\infty}.

Therefore,

\|\Phi(u_{1})-\Phi(u_{2})\|_{\beta,\infty}\leq\sqrt{L^{*}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\beta,\infty}.

In other words,
\Phi
is a contraction on
C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

(i) In Theorem
3.2
, we do not require that
\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}
is small. Besides, we also relax the condition on
L^{*}_{f}
and
K^{*}_{f}
in comparison with the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. Moreover, the result obtained in Theorem
3.2
still holds if we add to the right-hand side of equation (
1.1
) an external force, that means the right-hand side of (
1.1
) has the form
f(u,\mathcal{H}u)+g(t,x)
.

(ii) In the case
(1*m)^{-1}\in L^{1}(0,T)
, we achieve a similar result to that in Theorem
3.2
assumming that the nonlinearity
f
can take ‘weaker’ values:

\|f(v_{1},w_{1})-f(v_{2},w_{2})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-2}}\leq L^{*}_{f}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K^{*}_{f}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},

for all
v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{\mu}
, where
L^{*}_{f},K^{*}_{f}\geq 0
. In this situation, we use the estimate in Lemma
2.5
(e).

3.2.
Hölder regularity

In this part, we assume an additional contition on the kernel
m
as follows.

(M*)

The hypothesis (M) is satisfied with a nonincreasing function
m
.

Note that, under the assumption (M*), the resolvent
S(\cdot)
is differentiable in
(0,\infty)
and
\|S(t)\|\leq t^{-1}
for
t>0
by Lemma
2.5
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, denote

V^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}}=B_{\rho^{*}}\cap\{u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{\mu}):\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
t\in(0,T-h]\end{subarray}}\frac{t^{\gamma}\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}}{h^{\gamma}}\leq\rho\}.

We will show that the mild solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) obtained by Theorem
3.1
is Hölder continuous in
(0,T]
by proving that the solution mapping
\Phi
is contractive on
V^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that (M*) and all assumptions in Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. Moreover, with
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2}\delta,\frac{1}{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\ell^{*}_{1}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h>0\\
t\in(0,T-h]\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{t}{h}\right)^{\gamma}\int_{t}^{t+h}|\ell(\tau)|d\tau<\infty,

\displaystyle 16B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)T^{1-\delta}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}{\ell^{*}_{2}}^{2})<1,

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Beta function and

\ell^{*}_{2}=\sup_{t\in(0,T]}t^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\ell(\tau)|}{(t-\tau)^{\gamma}}d\tau.

Then, the solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is Hölder continuous on
(0,T]
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\Phi(V^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}})\subset V^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}}
for a certain
\rho>0
.

Since
S(\cdot)
is differentiable in
(0,\infty)
, using the Mean value theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|[S(t+h)-S(t)]\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq h\int_{0}^{1}\|S^{\prime}(t+\zeta h)\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq h\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\zeta}{t+\zeta h}=\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\ln\left(1+\frac{h}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\gamma^{-1}t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}.

(3.1)

On the other hand, for
u\in V^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}u(t+h)-\mathcal{H}u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\int_{t}^{t+h}|\ell(\tau)|\|u(t+h-\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}|\ell(\tau)|\|u(t+h-\tau)-u(t-\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}\rho^{*}\left(\frac{t}{h}\right)^{\gamma}\int_{t}^{t+h}|\ell(\tau)|d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}t^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\ell(\tau)|}{(t-\tau)^{\gamma}}[(t-\tau)^{\gamma}h^{-\gamma}\|u(t+h-\tau)-u(t-\tau)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}\rho^{*}\left[\left(\frac{t}{h}\right)^{\gamma}\int_{t}^{t+h}|\ell(\tau)|d\tau\right]+t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}\rho\left[t^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\ell(\tau)|}{(t-\tau)^{\gamma}}d\tau\right].

Hence,

\|\mathcal{H}u(t+h)-\mathcal{H}u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}(\rho^{*}\ell^{*}_{1}+\rho\ell^{*}_{2}).

(3.2)

Denote

D_{h}f(u)(t)=f(u(t+h),\mathcal{H}u(t+h))-f(u(t),\mathcal{H}u(t)).

(3.3)

We have

\displaystyle\|D_{h}f(u)(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}\leq\|f(u(t+h),\mathcal{H}u(t+h))-f(u(t),\mathcal{H}u(t))\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq L_{f}(\rho^{*})\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})\|\mathcal{H}u(t+h)-\mathcal{H}u(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}L_{f}(\rho^{*})\rho+t^{-\gamma}h^{\gamma}K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})(\rho^{*}\ell^{*}_{1}+\rho\ell^{*}_{2}),

(3.4)

here, we employed the estimate (
3.2
).

Finally, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t+h)

\displaystyle-\Phi(u)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq 2\|[S(t+h)-S(t)]\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle+4\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\delta}\|D_{h}f(u)(t-\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau

\displaystyle+4\int_{t}^{t+h}\tau^{-\delta}\|f(u(t+h-\tau),\mathcal{H}u(t+h-\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau

\displaystyle=E_{1}(t)+E_{2}(t)+E_{3}(t).

By (
3.1
), we get

E_{1}(t)\leq 2\gamma^{-2}t^{-2\gamma}h^{2\gamma}\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}.

Using (
3.4
), we gain

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 8h^{2\gamma}[\rho^{2}L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}(\rho^{*}\ell^{*}_{1}+\rho\ell^{*}_{2})^{2}]\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\delta}(t-\tau)^{-2\gamma}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 8h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}[\rho^{2}L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}(\rho^{*}\ell^{*}_{1}+\rho\ell^{*}_{2})^{2}]B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)T^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq 16h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)T^{1-\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\times[(L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}{\ell^{*}_{2}}^{2})\rho^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}{\rho^{*}}^{2}{\ell^{*}_{1}}^{2}]

here we utilized the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\delta}(t-\tau)^{-2\gamma}d\tau=B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)t^{1-\delta-2\gamma}.

We can estimate
E_{3}(t)
as follows

\displaystyle E_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{h}(t+h-\tau)^{-\delta}\|f(u(\tau),\mathcal{H}u(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1-\delta}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 8{\rho^{*}}^{2}[L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}]\int_{0}^{h}(t+h-\tau)^{-\delta}d\tau.

Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}(t+h-\tau)^{-\delta}d\tau

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)^{-1}[(t+h)^{1-\delta}-t^{1-\delta}]

\displaystyle\leq ht^{-\delta}\leq h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}h^{1-2\gamma}t^{2\gamma-\delta}\leq h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}T^{1-\delta},

we obtain

E_{3}(t)\leq 8h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}(\rho^{*})^{2}T^{1-\delta}[L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}].

Combining the estimates of
E_{1}(t),E_{2}(t)
and
E_{3}(t)
above, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t}{h}\right)^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t+h)-\Phi(u)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq 2\gamma^{-2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle+16B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)T^{1-\delta}[({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}{\ell^{*}_{2}}^{2}+\epsilon)\rho^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}{\rho^{*}}^{2}{\ell^{*}_{1}}^{2}]

\displaystyle+8{\rho^{*}}^{2}T^{1-\delta}[L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}],

here,
\epsilon>0
is chosen such that

16T^{1-\delta}[L_{f}(\rho^{*})^{2}+K_{f}(\rho^{*}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})^{2}{\ell^{*}_{2}}^{2}]\leq 16T^{1-\delta}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}{\ell^{*}_{2}}^{2}+\epsilon)<\frac{1}{B(1-\delta,1-2\gamma)}.

Now, we take a sufficiently large
\rho>0
such that

\left(\frac{t}{h}\right)^{2\gamma}\|\Phi(u)(t+h)-\Phi(u)(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\rho^{2},\text{ for all }h>0,t\in(0,T-h].

This implies that
\Phi(u)\in W^{\mu,\gamma}_{\rho,\rho^{*}}
. The proof is complete.
∎

We now consider the case when the nonlinearity
f(u(t),\mathcal{H}u(t))
is more regular, i.e. it takes values in
\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}
. We will show the Hölder continuity of the solution without the assumptions on the coefficients as in Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that the condition (M*) and (F) are satisfied with
\theta=1-\mu
, and

\limsup\limits_{\rho\to 0}L_{f}(\rho)=L_{f}^{*},\;\limsup\limits_{\rho^{\prime}\to 0}K_{f}(\rho^{\prime})=K_{f}^{*},

such that

4({L^{*}_{f}}^{2}+{K^{*}_{f}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}})<\lambda_{1}.

Then, there exists
\eta>0
such that for
\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\eta
, problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) has a unique mild solution
u
in
[0,T]
. Moreover,
u(\cdot)
is Hölder continuous on
(0,T]
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, first we look for
\rho^{*}>0
such that
\Phi(B_{\rho^{*}})\subset B_{\rho^{*}}
.
Let
u\in B_{\rho}
,
\rho>0
, and
f(u(t),\mathcal{H}u(t))\in\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}
.

Then using Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S*f(u(\cdot),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}u(\cdot))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})\|f(u(\tau),\mathcal{H}u(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})[L_{f}(\rho)^{2}\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+K_{f}(\rho\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\mathcal{H}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})[L_{f}(\rho)^{2}+K_{f}(\rho\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}]\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon)\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau,

for
\epsilon>0
and
\rho^{*}>0
is chosen such that

4[L_{f}(\rho)^{2}+K_{f}(\rho\|\ell\|_{L^{1}})^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}]\leq 4({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon)\leq\lambda_{1},

for all
\rho\leq\rho^{*}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|S*f(u(\cdot),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}u(\cdot))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq 2\lambda_{1}^{-1}(1-\omega(t,\lambda_{1}))({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}.

We can estimate the solution operator
\Phi
as follows

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega(t,\lambda_{1})^{2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+2\|S*f(u(\cdot),\mathcal{H}u(\cdot))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega(t,\lambda_{1})\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+4\lambda_{1}^{-1}(1-\omega(t,\lambda_{1}))({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega(t,\lambda_{1})[\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}-2\lambda_{1}^{-1}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad+4\lambda_{1}^{-1}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}.

Take
\eta=2\lambda_{1}^{-1}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}
, with
\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq\eta
we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\lambda_{1}^{-1}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}\leq{\rho^{*}}^{2}.

Hence
\Phi(u)\in B_{\rho^{*}}
. Then, we show that
\Phi
is contractive on
B_{\rho^{*}}
. For
u_{1},u_{1}\in B_{\rho^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{1})(t)

\displaystyle-\Phi(u_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})\|f(u_{1}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{1}(\tau))-f(u_{2}(\tau),\mathcal{H}u_{2}(\tau))\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\omega(t-\tau,\lambda_{1})({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}.

Therefore

\|\Phi(u_{1})-\Phi(u_{2})|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\infty},

which implies that
\Phi
is contractive on
B_{\rho^{*}}
, and problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) possesses a unique mild solution
u\in B_{\rho^{*}}
.

It remains to prove that this solution is Hölder continuous on
(0,T]
. Observe that

\displaystyle u(t+h)-u(t)

\displaystyle=[S(t+h)-S(t)]\xi+\int_{0}^{t}S(\tau)D_{h}f(u)(t-\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{t+h}S(\tau)f(u(t+h-\tau),\mathcal{H}u(t+h-\tau))d\tau.

Then

\displaystyle\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|[S(t+h)-S(t)]\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle+3\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{h}f(u)(t-\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}d\tau

\displaystyle+3\int_{t}^{t+h}\|f(u(t+h-\tau),\mathcal{H}u(t+h-\tau))\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu-1}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=F_{1}(t)+F_{2}(t)+F_{3}(t),

here we employed Lemma
2.5
(b) and the fact that
\omega(t,\lambda_{1})\leq 1
.

By an analoguous argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|[S(t+h)-S(t)]\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-1}h^{\gamma}t^{-\gamma}\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}},\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).

So it is clear that

F_{1}(t)\leq 3\gamma^{-2}h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}\|\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle F_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 6\int_{0}^{t}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\|u(t+h-\tau)-u(t-\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau

\displaystyle=6\int_{0}^{t}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon)\|u(\tau+h)-u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}d\tau,

\displaystyle F_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 6\int_{t}^{t+h}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq 6h({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6Th^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2},

thanks to the fact that
0<h,t<T
.
Putting the estimates of
F_{1}(t),F_{2}(t)
and
F_{3}(t)
together, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t+h)

\displaystyle-u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq C_{0}h^{2\gamma}t^{-2\gamma}+\int_{0}^{t}C_{1}\tau^{-2\gamma}[\tau^{2\gamma}\|u(\tau+h)-u(\tau)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}]d\tau

with

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=3\gamma^{-2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}+6T({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon){\rho^{*}}^{2},

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=6({L_{f}^{*}}^{2}+{K_{f}^{*}}^{2}\|\ell\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\epsilon).

Applying the Gronwall inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{2\gamma}\|u(t+h)

\displaystyle-u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{\mu}}\leq e^{C_{1}(1-2\gamma)^{-1}t^{1-2\gamma}}C_{0}h^{2\gamma}\text{ for }t>0.

In other words,

is Hölder continuous on

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-(1+D_{t}^{\{m\}})\Delta u

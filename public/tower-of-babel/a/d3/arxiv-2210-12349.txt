Shift symmetries and duality web in gauge theories


Abstract
Using a generalised Noether prescription we are able to extract all the currents and their conservation laws in space dependent shift symmetric theories. Various identities among the currents in the matter sector are found that form the basis for revealing a dual picture when the full interacting theory is considered by coupling to gauge fields. The coupling is achieved in terms of vector fields by adhering to a modified minimal prescription which is also supported by an iterative Noether scheme. Further, this scheme shows that couplings can also be introduced using higher rank tensor gauge fields that have appeared in recent discussions on fractons. We reveal a connection among these descriptions (using vector or tensor fields) through certain duality maps that relate the various fields (gauge, electric and magnetic) in the two cases. A correspondence is established among the Gauss’ law, Faraday’s law and Ampere’s law. Explicit calculations are provided for linear and quadratic shift symmetric lagrangians.

2
Shift Symmetries and conservation laws

An important input in the study of low-energy effective lagrangians is Goldstone’s theorem which implies that whenever a global symmetry is spontaneously broken, a gapless mode will appear. For relativistic theories, this leads to a massless Goldstone particle. Such a particle is described by a shift symmetry of the field, in the example of a Goldstone scalar,

\phi(x)\,\to\,\phi(x)+c

(2.1)

where
c
is a constant, and is characterised by the usual action of a scalar field,

S=\frac{1}{2}\int\,\,d^{d}x\,\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi

(2.2)

Since the action is invariant under (
2.1
), which is a global transformation, we may obtain the corresponding conserved current by using Noether’s first theorem,

j^{\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}\delta\phi=c\,\partial^{\mu}\phi

(2.3)

which satisfies,

\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=c\,\,\Box\phi=0

(2.4)

on using the equation of motion. Further, it is found that the shift symmetry, on exploiting canonical quantisation of
\phi
, connects the vacuum
|0\rangle
with a single particle state
|1\rangle
,

\langle 0|j^{\mu}|1\rangle=c\langle 0|\partial^{\mu}\phi|1\rangle=cp^{\mu}e^{ip.x}

(2.5)

and the matrix element vanishes in the strict soft limit, as required by Goldstone’s theorem.

We now generalise the above analysis by infusing two modifications. First, a non-relativistic version is taken and, secondly, the constant shift symmetry is enlarged to a linear spatial shift,

\phi\,\to\,\phi+c+c_{i}x_{i}=\phi+w

(2.6)

The non-relativistic free scalar theory is defined as,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}\phi)(\partial_{i}\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\phi\partial_{0}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{i}\phi

(2.7)

which transforms in the following way,

\delta\mathcal{L}=-(\partial_{i}\phi)(\partial_{i}w)=-\partial_{i}(\phi\partial_{i}w)

(2.8)

Thus the Lagrangian is quasi-invariant.

Applying Noether’s theorem, the conserved currents are given by,

\displaystyle j^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\phi)}\delta\phi=(\partial_{0}\phi)\,w=\dot{\phi}\,w

(2.9)

\displaystyle j^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\phi)}\delta\phi+\phi\partial^{i}w=-(\partial^{i}\phi)\,w+\phi(\partial^{i}\,w)

(2.10)

where the extra piece in (
2.10
) is the boundary term in (
2.8
). Current conservation now follows on shell,

\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}j_{i}=(-\ddot{\phi}+\partial^{2}\phi)w=0

(2.11)

where
\partial^{2}\,w=0
has been used since
w
is linear in
x
(
2.6
).

However, there are other possibilities for defining the currents. A more conventional definition is,

\displaystyle j_{0}

\displaystyle=-j^{0}=-\dot{\phi}

(2.12)

\displaystyle j_{i}

\displaystyle=j^{i}=-\partial_{i}\phi

(2.13)

On shell current conservation follows immediately,

\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}j_{i}=-\ddot{\phi}+\partial^{2}\phi=0

(2.14)

It is now possible to introduce a tensor current
j_{ki}
whose space derivative yields the vector current,

j_{ki}=-\phi\delta_{ki},\qquad j_{i}=\partial_{k}j_{ki}

(2.15)

so that,

\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}\partial_{k}j_{ki}=0

(2.16)

The above relation shows the existence of two conserved charges, the usual charge and the dipole charge,

Q=\int_{space}\,\,j_{0},\qquad Q^{i}=\int_{space}\,\,x^{i}j_{0}

(2.17)

This is reminiscent of fractonic theories, the characteristic feature of which is the presence of higher derivatives. In the present case such terms are absent.

It is tempting to ask whether a general formulation is possible to generate the currents (
2.12
,
2.13
). Indeed a viable definition is given by,

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle=wj_{i}-(\partial_{k}w)\,j_{ki}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\phi)}\delta\phi+\phi(\partial_{i}\,w)

(2.18)

\displaystyle J^{0}

\displaystyle=w\,j^{0}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\phi)}\delta\phi

(2.19)

while there is no change in
J^{0}
defined as in (
2.9
), the vector current yields,

wj_{i}-\partial_{k}w\,j_{ki}=-(\partial_{i}\phi)\,w+\phi\,(\partial_{i}w)

(2.20)

leading to,

j_{i}=-\partial_{i}\phi,\qquad j_{ki}=-\phi\delta_{ki}

(2.21)

reproducing (
2.13
) and (
2.15
).

The equation for current conservation of
J_{0},J_{i}
obtained from the defining equation (
2.18
,
2.19
) is given by,

\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{i}J_{i}=-\delta\mathcal{L}-\partial_{i}(\phi\partial_{i}\,w)=0

(2.22)

that follows from (
2.8
). The standard vector current conservation is deduced by substituting the explicit values of
J_{0},J_{i}
in (
2.22
),

\partial_{0}(wj_{0})-\partial_{i}(wj_{i}-(\partial_{k}w)\,j_{ki})=0

(2.23)

Extracting
w
out from the parenthesis, followed by a little algebra immediately yields (
2.14
).

This derivation, however, conceals the fact that there are two conservation laws related to the charges
Q,Q^{i}
(
2.17
). To reveal this, we open
w
in favour of
c,c_{i}
using (
2.6
). Then,

\partial_{0}[(c+c_{j}x_{j})j_{0}]-\partial_{i}[(c+c_{j}x_{j})j_{i}-\partial_{k}(c+c_{j}x_{j})j_{ki}]=0

(2.24)

Equating terms proportional to
c
yields the standard conservation law (
2.14
) while equating those of
c_{j}
yields,

\partial_{0}(\tilde{j}_{oj})-\partial_{i}(\tilde{j}_{ij}-j_{ij})=0

(2.25)

where,

\tilde{j}_{oj}=x_{j}j_{0},\qquad\tilde{j}_{ij}=x_{j}j_{i}

(2.26)

and leads to the conservation of the dipole charge
Q^{j}
, defined in (
2.17
).

2.1
Nonrelativistic Maxwell theory

It is feasible to develop a similar algorithm for the nonrelativistic Maxwell field. The new point here is the interplay between shift and gauge symmetries. The dynamics is governed by the Lagrangian
3
3

3

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are, in general, different
,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{C_{1}}{2}F_{oi}^{2}-\frac{C_{2}}{4}F_{ij}^{2}

(2.27)

where

F_{0i}=\partial_{0}A_{i}-\partial_{i}A_{0},\quad F_{ij}=\partial_{i}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{i}

(2.28)

The equations of motion are given by,

C_{1}\partial_{0}F_{0i}-C_{2}\partial_{j}F_{ji}=0,\quad\partial_{i}F_{0i}=0

(2.29)

where the second one is the Gauss constraint.

There is a gauge symmetry,

\delta A_{0}=\partial_{0}w,\quad\delta A_{i}=\partial_{i}w

(2.30)

that results in a conserved Noether current,

\displaystyle j^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}A_{i})}\delta A_{i}=C_{1}F_{0i}(\partial_{i}w)

(2.31)

\displaystyle j_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}A_{j})}\delta A_{j}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}A_{0})}\delta A_{0}=-C_{2}F_{ij}(\partial_{j}w)-C_{1}F_{0i}(\partial_{0}w)

(2.32)

which is easily verified,

\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}j_{i}=(C_{2}\partial_{i}F_{ij}-C_{1}\partial_{0}F_{0j})(\partial_{j}w)+C_{1}\partial_{i}F_{0i}\partial_{0}w=0

(2.33)

by using the equations of motion.

Gauge symmetry, regarded as an origin of the masslessness of the photon, is translatable to the linear shift symmetry by choosing,

w=c_{i}x_{i}+\frac{1}{2}c_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}

(2.34)

so that,

\delta A_{i}=c_{i}+c_{ij}x_{j},\quad\delta A_{0}=0

(2.35)

analogous to (
2.6
). Thus the gapless photon mode is interpreted as a consequence of gauge symmetry or shift symmetry similar to Goldstone bosons. Note that although, in general,
c_{ij}
has both a symmetric part and an antisymmetric component, only the former contributes to (
2.34
).

As we found for the scalar field there are other ways of defining currents. We adopt the same algorithm as before. It is useful to express
c_{ij}
in terms of its symmetric
(s_{ij})
and antisymmetric
(a_{ij})
parts,

s_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(c_{ij}+c_{ji}),\qquad a_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(c_{ij}-c_{ji})

(2.36)

which implies,

\delta A_{i}=c_{i}+s_{ij}x_{j}+a_{ij}x_{j}=w_{i}(x)

(2.37)

For the above shift the lagrangian (
2.27
) changes as,

\delta\mathcal{L}=2C_{2}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}A_{j})=C_{2}a_{ij}F_{ij}

(2.38)

and hence is quasi-invariant as well as gauge invariant.

It is pertinent to observe that, retaining only the symmetric part
s_{ij}
in (
2.38
), as dictated by the structure (
2.34
), would yield
\delta\mathcal{L}=0
and there would be no boundary term. Exact gauge invariance follows which shows its direct correspondence with shift invariance. This is to be expected if one works with standard theories without higher derivative terms in the action.

Since our motivation is to go beyond usual invariances by involving higher derivative terms, we envisage a wider possibility here itself by demanding a general form for
\delta A_{i}
given by (
2.37
) that includes both
s_{ij}
and
a_{ij}
. The actual implementation of these ideas in the context of scalar field theories with higher derivatives is done in next (sub) section.

Following our general algorithm the currents are defined analogous to (
2.18
,
2.19
),

\displaystyle J^{0}

\displaystyle=w_{j}j^{0j}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}A_{i})}\delta A_{i}=C_{1}F_{0i}w_{i}

(2.39)

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle=w_{j}j_{ij}-(\partial_{k}w_{j})j_{ijk}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}A_{j})}\delta A_{j}-2C_{2}a_{ij}A_{j}

(2.40)

where the boundary term (
2.38
) has been included in the expression for
J_{i}
. While (
2.39
) yields,

j^{0j}=-j^{j}_{0}=C_{1}F_{0j}

(2.41)

simplifying (
2.40
) leads to,

w_{j}j_{ij}-(\partial_{k}w_{j})j_{ijk}=-C_{2}F_{ij}w_{j}-2C_{2}a_{ij}A_{j}

(2.42)

which yields,

\displaystyle j_{ij}=-C_{2}F_{ij}

(2.43)

\displaystyle j_{ijk}=C_{2}(-\delta_{ik}A_{j}+\delta_{ij}A_{k})

(2.44)

From the above relations we get a condition,

\partial_{i}j_{ijk}=C_{2}F_{jk}=-j_{jk}

(2.45)

analogous to (
2.15
).

The conservation laws are given by,

\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{i}J_{i}=\partial_{0}(w_{j}j_{0j})-\partial_{i}\Big(w_{j}j_{ij}-(\partial_{k}w_{j})j_{ijk}\Big)

(2.46)

Abstracting out
w_{j}
we find,

w_{j}(\partial_{0}j_{0j}-\partial_{i}j_{ij})=0

(2.47)

on exploiting (
2.45
) and the linearity of
w_{j}
(
2.37
). A direct way to verify (
2.47
) is to use (
2.41
) and (
2.45
) which just yields the onshell condition (
2.29
).

As before, opening up
w_{j}
yields another conservation law,

\partial_{0}[(c_{j}+s_{jk}x_{k}+a_{jk}x_{k})j_{0j}]-\partial_{i}[(c_{j}+s_{jk}x_{k}+a_{jk}x_{k})j_{ij}]+\partial_{i}[(s_{jk}+a_{jk})j_{ijk}]=0

(2.48)

Equating coefficients of
c_{j}
reproduces (
2.47
). Likewise, equating coefficients of
(s_{jk}+a_{jk})
yields,

\partial_{0}(x_{k}j_{0j})-\partial_{i}[x_{k}j_{ij}-j_{ijk}]=0

(2.49)

and leads to the conservation of a dipole like (vector) charge,

Q_{kj}=\int_{space}\,\,x_{k}j_{0j}

(2.50)

The field momentum and dipole charge satisfy a generalised Heisenberg algebra,

[P_{i},Q_{kj}]=\Big[\int\,dx\,\pi_{l}\partial_{i}A_{l}(x),\int\,dy\,y_{k}j_{0j}(y)\Big]=-C_{1}\Big[\int\,dx\,dy\pi_{l}\partial_{i}A_{l}(x),y_{k}F_{0j}(y)\Big]

(2.51)

Recalling that
F_{0j}
is the momentum canonically conjugate to
A_{j}
,

\pi_{j}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A}_{j}}=C_{1}F_{0j}

(2.52)

we obtain,

[P_{i},Q_{kj}]=\delta_{ik}Q_{j}

(2.53)

where
Q_{j}
is the (vector) charge,

Q_{j}=\int_{space}\,\,j_{0j}

(2.54)

Appearance of higher rank tensor currents in standard Maxwell’s theory is a precursor to similar currents in higher derivative scalar field theories, both real and complex. Since it forms a central part of our analysis, we discuss this in some details.

2.2
Higher derivative scalar field theories

The simplest higher derivative extension of the usual theory (
2.7
) is given by,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)

(2.55)

which is a typical example of a Lifshitz type model with a scaling symmetry,

\delta x^{i}=\lambda x^{i}\,;\,\,\,\delta t=z\lambda t\,\,\,(z=\lambda)

(2.56)

where
z
characterises the degree of anisotropy between space and time.

A cubic shift in the coordinates,

\phi\,\to\,\phi+w,\quad w=c+c_{i}x_{i}+\frac{1}{2}c_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{1}{3}c_{ijk}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}

(2.57)

leaves the lagrangian quasi-invariant, since,

\delta\mathcal{L}=-\partial_{i}[\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w]+\partial_{j}[\phi\partial^{2}\partial_{j}w]-\phi(\partial^{2})^{2}w

(2.58)

where the last term drops out since
w=O(x^{3})
.

Since the lagrangian involves double derivatives, the Noether currents are defined as,

\displaystyle J^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\phi)}\delta\phi=\dot{\phi}w

\displaystyle J^{i}

\displaystyle=J_{i}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\phi)}\delta\phi+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)}\partial_{j}(\delta\phi)-\Big(\partial_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)}\Big)\delta\phi+\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w-\phi\partial^{2}\partial_{i}w

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)(\partial_{j}w)+\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)w+(\partial_{j}\phi)(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w)-\phi(\partial^{2}\partial_{i}w)

(2.59)

where the boundary terms have been inserted. Current conservation now follows,

\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{i}J_{i}=-w(\ddot{\phi}+(\partial^{2})^{2}\phi)+\phi(\partial^{2})^{2}w

(2.60)

which vanishes on-shell.

The other (higher order) currents and their associated conservation laws follow on using our definition,

J^{0}=wj^{0}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\phi)}\delta\phi=\dot{\phi}w

(2.61)

which yields,

j^{0}=\dot{\phi}

(2.62)

The space current is given by,

\displaystyle J_{i}

\displaystyle=wj_{i}-(\partial_{j}w)j_{ji}+(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}w)j_{jki}-(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}w)j_{jkli}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)}\partial_{j}(\delta\phi)-\Big(\partial_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)}\Big)\delta\phi+(\partial_{j}\phi)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w-\phi\partial^{2}\partial_{i}w

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)\partial_{j}w+\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi)w+(\partial_{j}\phi)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w-\phi\partial^{2}\partial_{i}w

(2.63)

Comparing coefficients of
w
and its derivatives yields,

\displaystyle j_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\partial^{2}\phi

(2.64)

\displaystyle j_{ji}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi=j_{ij}

(2.65)

\displaystyle j_{jki}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{ki}\partial_{j}\phi+\delta_{ij}\partial_{k}\phi)=j_{kji}

(2.66)

\displaystyle j_{jkli}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(\delta_{jk}\delta_{li}+\delta_{jl}\delta_{ki}+\delta_{kl}\delta_{ji})\phi

(2.67)

The following set of identities are obtained from the above relations,

j_{i}=\partial_{j}j_{ij}=\partial_{j}\partial_{k}j_{ijk}=\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}j_{ijkl}

(2.68)

Besides this chain, there exists another identity,

j_{jk}=\partial_{i}j_{jki}

(2.69)

The above identities are useful for proving conservation laws, as shown below.
It is important to point out that the first relation in (
2.68
), which connects a vector current to a derivative of a rank two current, and the identity (
2.69
) that connects a rank two current to a derivative of rank three current contain the genesis of the duality among different gauge theories, as demonstrated later. Current conservation is now verified as,

\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{i}J_{i}=\partial_{0}(wj_{0})-\partial_{i}J_{i}=\partial_{0}(-\dot{\phi}w)-\partial_{i}\Big[wj_{i}-(\partial_{j}w)j_{ji}+(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}w)j_{jki}-(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}w)j_{jkli}\Big]=0

(2.70)

Factoring out
w
and exploiting the identities (
2.68
) yields,

\partial_{0}J_{0}-\partial_{i}J_{i}=-w(\ddot{\phi}+(\partial^{2})^{2}\phi)+(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}w)j_{jkli}

(2.71)

which vanishes on shell and the fact that
w
is cubic in the coordinates.

This derivation hides the whole tower of conservation laws that may be revealed by opening up
w
using (
2.57
). Then equating one by one the factors multiplying parameters
c,c_{i}
etc, we have, from (
2.70
),

\displaystyle\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}j_{i}=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\partial_{0}(x_{i}j_{0})-\partial_{j}(x_{i}j_{j}-j_{ij})=0

(2.72)

While the first leads to the conservation of the usual charge
Q=\int_{space}j_{0}
, the second yields the conservation of the dipole charge,

Q_{i}=\int_{space}\,\,x_{i}j_{0}

(2.73)

Similarly, equating coefficients of
c_{ij}
yields,

\partial_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{2}x_{i}x_{j}j_{0}\Big)-\partial_{k}\Big[\frac{1}{2}x_{i}x_{j}j_{k}-\frac{1}{2}(x_{i}j_{jk}+x_{j}j_{ik})+j_{ijk}\Big]=0

(2.74)

which leads to the conservation of the quadrupole charge,

Q_{ij}=\int_{space}x_{i}x_{j}j_{0}

(2.75)

A direct check of (

2.3
The multipole algebra

The symmetry algebra of charges is obtained here for different cases like dipole, quadrupole etc.

From shift symmetries to gauge symmetries

The analysis for polynomial shift symmetries in scalar field theories done in the previous section is now generalised to complex scalars. This will allow interactions to be introduced by coupling with gauge fields-either vector or higher rank tensors. Such theories have recently been profusely discussed in the literature.

) to get this current. Then the contribution to the lagrangian is given by,

\mathcal{L}_{2}=-j^{i}A_{i}=\frac{1}{4}|\Phi|^{4}(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})^{2}

(4.18)

so that the current is reproduced by,

j^{i}=-\frac{\delta}{\delta A_{i}}\int\mathcal{L}_{2}

(4.19)

Since there are no further derivatives on
\Phi
, the iteration terminates and the final form of the gauged theory is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}

\displaystyle=|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\Big(\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi^{*}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*})-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)\Big)(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}|\Phi|^{4}(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})^{2}

(4.20)

which exactly matches with the form (
4.13
).

It is also possible to recast the above analysis in terms of the tensor field defined in (
4.9
). In that case the first correction (
4.16
) is expressed as,

\mathcal{L}_{1}=-i\Big(\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi^{*}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*})-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)\Big)A_{ij}=-j^{ij}A_{ij}

(4.21)

where the current
j^{ij}
coupling to
A_{ij}
is defined in (
4.15
). Pursuing this line of analysis the new (tensor) current obtained from the first iteration is given by,

j^{ij}=-2|\Phi|^{4}A_{ij}

(4.22)

which is consistent with (
4.17
). The new contribution to the lagrangian now follows,

\mathcal{L}_{2}=|\Phi|^{4}A_{ij}^{2}=-j^{ij}A_{ij}

(4.23)

so that the current (
4.22
) is reproduced by,

j^{ij}=-\frac{\delta}{\delta A_{ij}}\int\mathcal{L}_{2}

(4.24)

Note that the structure (
4.23
) is consistent with (
4.18
) on using the relation (
4.9
). Thus the final lagrangian takes the form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}

\displaystyle=|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}-i\Big[\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi^{*}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*})-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)\Big]A_{ij}+|\Phi|^{4}A_{ij}^{2}

(4.25)

which is the dual version of (
4.20
) and is same as (
4.7
).

4.2
Full interacting theory in dual description

As we have shown in section-4 that, for complex scalar theories with linear space dependent shift symmetry, the gauging can be done, either by making use of tensor gauge fields or by using usual vector gauge fields. We have shown that this is a consequence of the functional relationship between the vector and higher rank tensor currents. However, this was only discussed algebraically as no gauge field dynamics was involved in the earlier sections. In this section we shall discover that the mapping is even valid at the dynamical level. By taking the full interacting lagrangian where the gauge fields, either vector or tensor, are also dynamical, descriptions of these theories are possible not only at the lagranian level but also at the hamiltonian level.

4.2.1
Lagrangian and hamiltonian analysis in (2+1) dimensional space-time

The interacting theory can be presented in two distinct ways. As a symmetric tensor theory or a mapped vector theory using (
4.9
) in the tensor theory. We use the mapping in two different ways: (1) we write down a lagrangian in terms of vector fields from the tensor theory lagrangian using the map and then find the equation of motions of the vector fields. (2) On the other hand we can just use the map at the level of equation of motion of the tensor theory itself to find a set of vector field equations of motion and see that in both cases the vector equations of motion match.

We first consider the (2+1) dimensional case. In (2+1) dimension the Maxwell theory for U(1) vector fields modifies from that of (3+1) dimensional theory. The electric field is a 2 dimensional vector
E_{i}=-F_{oi}=\partial_{i}A_{0}-\partial_{0}A_{i}
and the magnetic field is a scalar
B=\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{i}A_{j}
.

Theory with second rank tensor gauge fields

Now let us write a (2+1) dimensional theory in terms of tensorial gauge fields. For a tensorial theory in 2+1 dimension, the electric field is taken as a rank-2 symmetric tensor, while the magnetic field becomes a vector. Let us first define the electric and magnetic fields for this tensor theory in terms of a symmetric rank-2 tensor gauge field
A_{ij}
as,

E_{ij}=\partial_{0}A_{ij}+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0},\quad B_{k}=\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{i}A_{jk}

(4.26)

One can check that both these fields are gauge invariant. The lagrangian for the pure gauge field is written,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(E_{ij}^{2}-B_{i}^{2})

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0})^{2}+(\partial_{0}A_{ij})^{2}+2(\partial_{0}A_{ij})(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0})\Big]-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{m}A_{nk}-\partial_{n}A_{mk})\partial_{m}A_{nk}

(4.28)

The complete theory is now given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{i}{2}\Phi^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{0}\Phi-\Phi^{*}\Phi A_{0}+|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}

\displaystyle-i\Big[\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi^{*}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*})-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)\Big]A_{ij}+|\Phi|^{4}A_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{i}{2}\Phi^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{0}\Phi-\Phi^{*}\Phi A_{0}+|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}-j_{ij}A_{ij}

(4.29)

where,

j_{ij}=i\Big[\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)\Big]-|\Phi|^{4}A_{ij}

(4.30)

The equation of motion for
A_{0}
is given by

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E_{ij}=-\Phi^{*}\Phi

(4.31)

while that of the gauge field
A_{ij}
is given by

\partial_{0}E_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}(\epsilon_{ik}B_{j}+\epsilon_{jk}B_{i})=-j_{ij}

(4.32)

Lastly, the equation of motion of the scalar field
\Phi
is given by

\displaystyle-(i\partial_{0}+A_{0})\Phi^{*}+4(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi)\mathcal{C}_{ij}+4(\partial_{i}\Phi)\partial_{j}\mathcal{C}_{ij}+\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathcal{C}_{ij}-i\Phi\mathcal{C}_{ij}A_{ij}+4i{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi)A_{ij}

\displaystyle+2{\Phi^{*}}^{2}\Phi A_{ij}^{2}-\partial_{i}\Big((\Phi^{*})^{2}A_{ij}\Big)(\partial_{j}\Phi)-\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Big((\Phi^{*})^{2}A_{ij}\Big)=0

(4.33)

where

\mathcal{C}_{ij}=\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi^{*}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}

(4.34)

Now let us compute the Hamilton’s equations of motion for the electric and magnetic fields and deduce the respective Maxwell equations for the tensor theory.
From (
4.28
), one can compute the canonical momenta corresponding to
A_{0}
and
A_{ij}
as

\pi_{0}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A}^{0}}=0,\qquad\pi_{ij}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A}^{ij}}=\dot{A}_{ij}+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0}=E_{ij}

(4.35)

and the corresponding canonical Hamiltonian as

H_{c}=\frac{1}{2}(E_{ij}^{2}+B_{k}^{2})+...

(4.36)

where the ellipses denote terms containing
\Phi
and the interaction terms.
The basic Poission relation between the gauge field
A_{ij}
and its canonical momenta
E_{ij}
is given by

\{A_{ij}(\vec{x}),A_{kl}(\vec{y})\}=0=\{E_{ij}(\vec{x}),E_{kl}(\vec{y})\};\quad\{A_{ij}(\vec{x}),E_{kl}(\vec{y})\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})\delta^{2}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(4.37)

while that between the physical variables (electric and magnetic fields) are given by

\{B(\vec{x}),B(\vec{y})\}=0,\qquad\{E_{ij}(x),B_{k}(y)\}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{m}^{y}(\epsilon_{mi}\delta_{jk}+\epsilon_{mj}\delta_{ik})\delta^{2}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(4.38)

Now using (
4.37
) we compute the time derivative of the electric field as

\partial_{0}E_{ij}=\{E_{ij},H_{c}\}=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{ki}\partial_{k}B_{j}+\epsilon_{kj}\partial_{k}B_{i})-j_{ij}

(4.39)

This corresponds to Faraday’s law for tensor theory in (2+1) dimension. Similarly the equation of motion of
B_{i}
is given by

\partial_{0}B_{i}=\{B_{i},H_{c}\}=-\epsilon_{jk}\partial_{j}E_{ki}

(4.40)

which corresponds to Ampere’s law for tensor theory in (2+1) dimensions.

Theory with vector gauge field

We now use the map (
4.9
) between the rank-2 symmetric tensor gauge field and usual vector field to write down a lagrangian in terms of vector fields. Then we shall find out the equations of motion for the vector fields.

Using (
4.9
) in the lagrangian (
4.28
), the vector gauge field lagrangian for (2+1) dimensional theory is given by

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\Big[2(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0})^{2}+(\partial_{0}\partial_{i}A_{j})^{2}-4(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{0})(\partial_{i}\partial_{0}A_{j})+(\partial_{i}\partial_{0}A_{j})(\partial_{j}\partial_{0}A_{i})\Big]-\frac{1}{8}(\epsilon_{jk}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}A_{k})^{2}

(4.41)

The full lagrangian containing scalar field part and the interaction part will be given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{i}{2}\Phi^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{0}\Phi-\Phi^{*}\Phi A_{0}+|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}-j_{i}A_{i}

where (
4.8
) has been used to recast the interaction term in favour of vector quantities.
Now we can compute the equation of motion of the fields from this lagrangian. First, the equation of motion of
A_{0}
field is given by

\partial^{2}\partial_{i}E_{i}=-\Phi^{*}\Phi

(4.42)

which corresponds to the Gauss constraint, where
E_{i}
is defined as before
E_{i}=-F_{oi}
, However note that it is not the canonical momenta corresponding to
A_{i}
. This will be shown in details in the Hamiltonian analysis. The equation of motion for
A_{i}
is given by

\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}(\partial_{0}E_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{j}B)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E_{j}=-j_{i}

(4.43)

where

j_{i}=i\partial_{j}[\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)-{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)]+\partial_{j}[|\Phi|^{4}(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})]

(4.44)

is the vector current for the theory
[
11
]
and is related to a tensor current
j_{ij}
as

j_{i}=\partial_{j}j_{ij};\qquad j_{ij}=[i\Phi^{2}(\Phi^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi^{*}-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)-i{\Phi^{*}}^{2}(\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi)+|\Phi|^{4}(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})]

(4.45)

The equation of motion of
\Phi
is essentially the same as (
4.33
), because the interaction lagrangian is not changed, apart from the fact that
A_{ij}
will now be replaced by
-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}A_{j}+\partial_{j}A_{i})
in this picture.

We shall now discuss about the hamiltonian analysis of the vector theory. From (
4.41
), one can find out the canonical momenta for the vector fields as

\pi_{0}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A^{0}}}=0\,;\qquad\pi_{i}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{A^{i}}}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{ij}\partial^{2}+\partial_{i}\partial_{j})E_{j}

(4.46)

The canonical Hamiltonian for the theory can be written using Legendre transformation as

H_{c}=\frac{1}{2\partial^{4}}(\partial_{i}\pi_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{8}(\partial_{i}B)^{2}+...

(4.47)

where the ellipses mean terms containing scalar fields and interactions.

The Poission algebra between the canonically conjugate fields
A_{i}
and
\pi_{i}
are given by

\{A_{i}(\vec{x}),A_{j}(\vec{y})\}=\{\pi_{i}(\vec{x}),\pi_{j}(\vec{y})\}=0;\qquad\{A_{i}(\vec{x}),\pi_{j}(\vec{y})\}=\delta_{ij}\delta^{2}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(4.48)

while that between the electric and magnetic fields are,

\{\pi_{i},\pi_{j}\}=0=\{B,B\},\quad\{B(\vec{x}),\pi_{j}(\vec{y})\}=\epsilon_{kj}\partial_{k}^{x}\delta^{2}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(4.49)

Using this algebra, we can derive the equation of motion of
E_{i}
and
B
from the above Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\pi_{i}=\{\pi_{i},H_{c}\}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{4}\epsilon_{ji}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle B-j_{i}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\partial^{2}}{2}(\partial_{0}E_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{j}B)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E_{j}

\displaystyle=-j_{i}

(4.50)

and

\partial_{0}B=\{B,H_{c}\}=\epsilon_{ji}\partial_{j}E_{i}

(4.51)

Comparative study

We cannot compare the equations of motion derived in two scenarios at this stage, as the first case provides equation of motion for tensor fields whereas the second case considers vector fields. The first step is to find a map connecting the physical gauge invariant variables (electric and magnetic fields) in the two pictures. Exploiting the basic map (

E_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}E_{j}+\partial_{j}E_{i}),\qquad B_{k}=\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{i}A_{jk}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}B

where

which is same as (

Now using

\Rightarrow\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{2}\partial_{0}E_{i}+\partial_{0}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E_{j})-\frac{1}{4}\partial^{2}\epsilon_{ij}\partial_{j}B=-j_{i}

\partial_{0}(\Phi^{*}\Phi)-\partial_{i}j_{i}=\partial_{0}j_{0}-\partial_{i}j_{i}=0

\partial_{0}\partial_{i}B=\epsilon_{jk}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E_{k}

The different descriptions of gauge theories presented here need some elaboration. Starting from a rank-2 tensor theory (
4.28
), the vector theory (
4.41
) was constructed using the map (
4.9
). These theories were connected in the sense that the physical equations- Gauss law, Faraday’s law, and Ampere’s law - were identical, irrespective of the fact that these were directly obtained from the vector theory or derived from the tensor theory exploiting suitable maps.

It should be pointed out that the map (
4.9
) allows a passage from the tensor theory to the vector theory but not the other way around. All correspondences have to be interpreted in this way, either in the action formulation or in the equation of motion. While the tensor Faraday’s equations pass to the vector ones, the converse does not hold. In passing from the vector to the tensor equations, the transverse components remain undetermined. This is bacause, there is a mismatch in the number of degrees of freedom of the gauge fields. To see this more elaborately, we recall that the two degrees of freedom of the vector field are the transverse and longitudinal components, while the three degrees of freedom of the tensor field can be decomposed as a fully transverse component, a partially transverse component, and a longitudinal component. While the longitudinal components of the two gauge fields may be mapped, this is not true for the transverse components.

The map (
4.9
) was used since the primary motivation was to discuss a connection among various gauge theories, apart from the fact that it occurs naturally in the present analysis and has also been mentioned earlier in the literature
[
9
,
11
]
. Similar discussion is also valid for the subsequent sections when we discuss about 3+1 dimensional case and quadratic shift symmetric theory.

4.2.2
Lagrangian and hamiltonian analysis in (3+1) dimensional space-time

Theory with second rank tensor gauge field

The full lagrangian containing scalar field part and the interaction part will be given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\frac{i}{2}\Phi^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{0}\Phi-\Phi^{*}\Phi A_{0}+|\Phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi-\partial_{i}\Phi\partial_{j}\Phi|^{2}-j_{i}A_{i}

Dual description of theory with space-dependent quadratic shift symmetry

The interplay between a tensor formulation and the usual vector formulation is generic, not confined to the linear shift symmetry. Infact it is more general. 6
Conclusion

Study of polynomial space dependent shift symmetric theories introduced in
[
32
,
33
]
, is appealing and is vastly studied recently, especially due to their n-pole conservation laws apart from usual charge conservation stemming from standard invariance under global (constant) transformations.

In this paper using a generalised Noether type prescription, we have given a
definition yielding all the n-pole currents. Also, various conservation laws associated with these currents were derived at one stroke using a master equation. It was shown that various tensor currents are intertwined with each other and were related by the action of a tower of spatial derivatives.

Using this result it was possible to establish a duality between tensor gauge fields with different ranks. Introduction of interactions was the precise mechanism by which these dual relations were derived (see for example, discussion around (
4.8
)). Thus it was possible to write down the interaction between a gauge field and a current in different ways (using tensors of different ranks) exploiting the duality relations between the currents and the gauge fields.

On the other hand, it was shown in
[
11
]
, that using the iterative Noether prescription of Deser
[
30
]
one can introduce matter-gauge interactions in two different ways, the reason of which is in the dual picture stated above. In one case they are introduced by a vector gauge field and the additional terms, besides the free piece, can be written so that the complete theory is the standard minimally coupled theory. Alternatively, one can also express the interactions in terms of a symmetric tensor gauge field recently discovered in fractonic theories. This method of introducing interactions naturally led to a nonstandard ‘minimal prescription’ where the complete theory was expressed by replacing the ordinary double derivatives by additional terms involving tensor fields which was explicitly derived previously in
[
11
]
for linear shift symmetric theory. Symmetrisation of derivatives plays an important role in this prescription. We have expanded this possibility and shown that this non-standard minimal scheme is valid for a general shift symmetric theory. The calculations also indicate that the non-standard minimal coupling prescription is obtained from the standard one by using the map that relates the vector (standard) and tensor (non-standard) gauge fields discussed above.

We have considered in details theory of complex scalars interacting either with vector or symmetric tensor gauge fields. It is important to mention that the pure gauge part has two possible options: (a) using the vector gauge fields (b) using the tensor gauge fields. To start with we considered the theory in terms of the tensor fields. Using our maps this was also expressed in favour of the vector fields. Both these theories admit equations of motion (Gauss law, Ampere’s law and Faraday’s law) which were suitably compared. All computations were done for linear shift symmetric theories in 2+1 and 3+1 dimensions and quadratic shift symmetric theories in 3+1 dimension.

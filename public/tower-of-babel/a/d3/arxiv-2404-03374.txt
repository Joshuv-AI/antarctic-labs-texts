On monogenic functions and the Dirac complex of two vector variables
Yun Shi, Wei Wang, Qingyan Wu

Abstract
. A monogenic function of two vector variables is a function annihilated by the operator consisting of two Dirac operators, which are associated to two variables, respectively. We give the explicit form of differential operators in the Dirac complex resolving this operator and prove its ellipticity directly. This open the door to apply the method of several complex variables to investigate this kind of monogenic functions. We prove the Poincaré lemma for this complex, i.e. the non-homogeneous equations are solvable under the compatibility condition by solving the associated Hodge Laplacian equations of fourth order. As corollaries, we establish the Bochner–Martinelli integral representation formula for this differential operator and the Hartogs’ extension phenomenon for monogenic functions. We also apply abstract duality theorem to the Dirac complex to obtain the generalization of Malgrange’s vanishing theorem and establish the Hartogs–Bochner extension phenomenon for monogenic functions under the moment condition.

\displaystyle\Box_{0}:=

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{D}_{0}^{*}\mathscr{D}_{0}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\Box_{1}:=

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{j}u=f,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{j+1}f=0.

.

)

12

and vansihing on the unbounded connected component of

As a corollary, we can prove the following Hartogs’ phenomenon.
A function

is called

2.
The Dirac complex of two vector variables

2.1.
Clifford algebra and spin modules
\mathbb{S}^{\pm}

The

\mathbb{R}_{n}
is the associative algebra generated by the
n
basis elements of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ij},

The basis of

\displaystyle e_{0}=1,\quad e_{\alpha}=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}},

For

\displaystyle f_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{2j-1}+\mathbf{i}e_{2j}\right),\ \bar{f}_{j}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(e_{2j-1}-\mathbf{i}e_{2j}\right),

(cf.
[

We have two spin modules

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{+}=\wedge^{\rm even}W,\quad\mathbb{S}^{-}=\wedge^{\rm odd}W,

[
11
, p. 118, Theorem 1]
.
For

W={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{f_{j},j=1,\cdots,m,\right\},

W^{\prime}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}

\left\{\bar{f}_{j},j=1,\cdots,m,\right\},

U=\left\{f_{2m+1}\right\},
are maximally isotropic subspaces of
\mathbb{C}_{n}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}=W\oplus W^{\prime}\oplus U,
where

is a
1
-dimensional space perpendicular to them and

\displaystyle f_{m+1}=\mathbf{i}e_{2m+1}.

We have one spin module

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\wedge^{\bullet}W,

which is the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{so}(2m+1,\mathbb{C}).

f_{j}
’s satisfy relations

(15)

\displaystyle f_{j}f_{k}+f_{k}f_{j}=0,\ \bar{f}_{j}\bar{f}_{k}+\bar{f}_{k}\bar{f}_{j}=0,\ \bar{f}_{j}f_{k}+f_{k}\bar{f}_{j}=0,

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle for}\ j\neq k,

\displaystyle f_{m+1}f_{k}+f_{k}f_{m+1}=0,\ f_{m+1}\bar{f}_{k}+\bar{f}_{k}\bar{f}_{m+1}=0,

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle for\ any}\ k.

The action of Clifford algebra on the spin module
\mathbb{S}^{\pm}
is given by
\gamma\left(f_{s}\right)\in{\rm End}\ \mathbb{S}^{\pm}.

For any

\gamma(a):(\wedge W)I\rightarrow(\wedge W)I
is given by

\displaystyle\gamma(a)(bI)=abI,

where

is a nonzero idempotent

is indecomposable as a right

-module; that is, such that

is not a direct sum of two nonzero submodules. Equivalently,

Then

For any
\alpha=\left\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{h}\right\}\subset\{1,\cdots,m\}

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}:=f_{\alpha_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{\alpha_{h}},

Theorem 2.1
.

(cf.
[
11
, p.116, Theorem 1]
)

For

(1)

\gamma\left(f_{j}\right)f_{\alpha}I=\left(f_{j}\wedge f_{\alpha}\right)I;

(2)

\gamma\left(\bar{f}_{j}\right)f_{\alpha}I=\bar{f}_{j}\left(f_{\alpha}\right)I;

(3)

\gamma\left(f_{m+1}\right)f_{\alpha}I=(-1)^{\sharp\alpha}f_{\alpha}I,

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{j}\left(f_{\alpha}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}&0,&{\rm if}\ j\notin\alpha,\\
&(-1)^{t+1}f_{\alpha^{\prime}},&{\rm if}\ j=\alpha_{t},\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha\setminus\{\alpha_{t}\}\end{array}\right.,

Since

.

The adjoint

of

with respect to this inner product is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{*}=-\gamma_{k}.

Proof.

We only need to prove

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma_{k}f_{\alpha},f_{\beta}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{\pm}}=-\left\langle f_{\alpha},\gamma_{k}f_{\beta}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{\mp}},

where

is the basis of

given in (

\neq 0

or

If
\alpha\cup\{j\}=\beta
and

is the

-th element of

by Theorem

. So

for

Similarly, we can prove the

Also

4.
The generalization of Malgrange’s vanishing theorem and the Hartogs–Bochner extension for monogenic functions

A
cohomological complex
of topological vector spaces is a pair
(E^{\bullet},{\rm d}),
where
E^{\bullet}=\left(E^{q}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of topological vector spaces and
{\rm d}=\left(\hbox{d}^{q}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of continuous linear
maps
\hbox{d}^{q}:E^{q}\rightarrow E^{q+1}
satisfying
\hbox{d}^{q+1}\circ\hbox{d}^{q}=0.
Its cohomology groups
H^{q}\left(E^{\bullet}\right)
are the quotient spaces
\ker\hbox{d}^{q}/{\rm Im}\ \hbox{d}^{q-1},
endowed with the quotient topology.
A
homological complex
of topological vector spaces is a pair
\left(E_{\bullet},\hbox{d}\right)
where
E_{\bullet}=\left(E_{q}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of topological vector spaces and
{\rm d}=\left(\hbox{d}_{q}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of continuous linear maps
\hbox{d}_{q}:E_{q}\rightarrow E_{q-1}
satisfying
\hbox{d}_{q-1}\circ\hbox{d}_{q}=0.
Its homology groups
H_{q}\left(E_{\bullet}\right)
are the
quotient spaces
\ker\hbox{d}_{q-1}/{\rm Im}\ \hbox{d}_{q},
endowed with the quotient topology. The dual complex of a cohomological complex
(E^{\bullet},{\rm d})
of topological vector spaces is the homological complex
(E^{\prime}_{\bullet},{\rm d}^{\prime}),
where
E^{\prime}_{\bullet}=\left(E^{\prime}_{q}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}

the dual of

and
\hbox{d}^{\prime}=\left(\hbox{d}_{q}^{\prime}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}

\hbox{d}_{q}.

Recall that a

is a topological vector space whose topology is defined by an increasing sequence of seminorms such that the unit ball with respect to the
seminorm is relatively compact for the topology associated to the previous seminorm. We need the following abstract duality theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
19

),

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{D}_{0}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left\langle\mathscr{D}_{0}f,\mathscr{D}_{0}f\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}_{1}}{\rm d}V=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left\langle\mathscr{D}_{0}^{*}\mathscr{D}_{0}f,f\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}{\rm d}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left\langle\Delta f,f\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}{\rm d}V=\sum_{A,j}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{Aj}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|f\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}-\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

by the definition of
W^{1,2}
-norm. The proposition is proved.
∎

Then we have the following estimate by the standard procedure (cf.
[
22
]
).

Corollary 4.1
.

For any
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2n},
there are constants
C_{s,K}>0,c_{s,K}\geq 0
such that

(61)

\displaystyle C_{s,K}\|f\|_{W^{s,2}}^{2}+\left\|\mathscr{D}_{0}f\right\|_{W^{s,2}}^{2}\geq c_{s,K}\|f\|_{W^{s+1,2}}^{2},

for any
f\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
with
\mathscr{D}_{0}f\in W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
and supp
f\subset K.

Theorem 4.2
.

\mathscr{D}_{0}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)
and
\mathscr{D}_{0}:\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)
have closed ranges.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{\nu}\}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
such that
\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}
convergence in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right),
i.e. all
\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}
are supported in a fixed compact subset
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and there is a
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}\in W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right),
supp
\left(\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}\right)\subset K
for all
\nu
and
\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}\rightarrow g
in
W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)

[
28
]
.
Then
\mathscr{D}_{0}f_{\nu}=0
outside of
K,
i.e.
f_{\nu}
is monogenic on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus K.
We can assume that
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus K
has no compact connected component. By Proposition
3.2
each component of a monogenic function annihilated by
\Delta
and so it is biharmonic on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus K
and real analytic. Consequently, as compacted supported distributions, monogenic functions
\left.f_{\nu}\right|_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus K}
vanish on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus K
and thus
\left\{f_{\nu}\right\}
are also supported in
K.

This argument also implies that (
61
) holds with
C_{s,K}=0.

If this is not true, there exist a sequence
h_{\nu}\in W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right),
with supp
h_{\nu}\subset K,
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{D}_{0}h_{\nu}\right\|_{W^{s,2}}^{2}<\frac{1}{\nu}\left\|h_{\nu}\right\|_{W^{s+1,2}}^{2}.

By rescaling we can assume that
\left\|h_{\nu}\right\|_{W^{s,2}}=1
for each
\nu.
By (
61
)

\displaystyle C_{s,K}\geq\left(c_{s,K}-\frac{1}{\nu}\right)\left\|h_{\nu}\right\|_{W^{s+1,2}}^{2}.

Thus
\left\{h_{\nu}\right\}
is bounded in the Sobolev space
W^{s+1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right).
By the well known compactness
of the inclusion
W^{s+1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)\subset W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right),
there is a subsequence that converges to a function
h_{\infty}\in W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right).
We have
\left\|h_{\infty}\right\|_{W^{s,2}}=1,\ \mathscr{D}_{0}h_{\infty}\equiv 0.

Then
\Delta^{2}h_{\infty}=0
and
h_{\infty}
is also compactly supported in
K
. So
h_{\infty}=0
by analytic continuation, which contradicts to
\left\|h_{\infty}\right\|_{W^{s,2}}^{2}=1.

By the estimate (
61
) with
C_{s,K}=0,
we see that
\left\{f_{\nu}\right\}
is uniformly
bounded in
W^{s+1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
, and hence contains a subsequence which converges to a compactly supported weak solution
f\in W^{s,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
of
\mathscr{D}_{0}f=g.
Namely, the image of
\mathscr{D}_{0}

in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
is closed. The closedness of the image of
\mathscr{D}_{0}
in
\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
follows from the proved result for
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)
and the elliptic regularity.
∎

. See

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Theorem
4.2

(62)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right)\xrightarrow{{\mathscr{D}}_{0}}\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right),

\mathscr{D}_{0}

4.1
(6) to sequences in (

(63)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 0\leftarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},{\mathscr{V}}_{0}\right)\xleftarrow{\widehat{\mathscr{D}}_{0}}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\right),

i.e.

in (

Compare the formula (
60
) of
\widehat{\mathscr{D}}_{0}

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
\mathscr{D}_{0}f
vanishes to the second order on
\partial\Omega,
we can extend
\mathscr{D}_{0}f
by
0
outside of
\overline{\Omega}
to get a
\mathscr{D}_{1}
-closed
C^{2}
element
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)
supported in
\overline{\Omega}.
Since
H^{0}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},{\mathscr{V}}_{\bullet}\right)\right)
vanish by Remark
4.1
, it is separated. Thus, we can apply abstract duality theorem
4.1
(1) to the second sequences in (
62
) and (
63
) to see that

(64)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\ \mathscr{D}_{0}=\left\{\tilde{F}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)|\left\langle\tilde{F},G\right\rangle=0\ {\rm for\ any}\ G\in\ker\widehat{\mathscr{D}}_{0}\right\}.

Consequently, we have
F\in{\rm Im}\ \mathscr{D}_{0}.
This is because
\widehat{\mathscr{D}}_{0}=\mathscr{D}_{0}^{*}
on
\mathcal{E}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{1}\right)
and so for any
G\in\ker{\mathscr{D}}^{*}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},{\mathscr{V}}_{1}\right),
we have

(65)

\displaystyle\langle F,G\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\mathscr{D}_{0}f,G\right\rangle_{\mathscr{V}_{1}}{\rm d}V=\sum_{A=0,1}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\nabla_{A}f,G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{-}}{\rm d}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{A=0,1}\sum_{j}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\gamma_{j}\partial_{Aj}f,G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{-}}{\rm d}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{A=0,1}\sum_{j}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\partial_{Aj}f,\gamma_{j}G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}{\rm d}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{A=0,1}\int_{\Omega}\left\langle f,\nabla_{A}G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}{\rm d}V-\sum_{A=0,1}\sum_{j}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left\langle f,\gamma_{j}G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}n_{Aj}{{\rm d}S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f,{\mathscr{D}}^{*}_{0}G\right\rangle-\sum_{A=0,1}\sum_{j}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left\langle f,\gamma_{j}G_{A}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{S}^{+}}n_{Aj}{{\rm d}S}=0,

by using (
60
) and Stokes-type formula, where

is the unit outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega.
Hence, by (
64

such that
F=\mathscr{D}_{0}H.

Recall that a distribution in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
always has compact support. Now by estimate (
61
) with
C_{s,K}=0
and
s=0,
proved in the proof of Theorem
4.2
,
H\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\mathscr{V}_{0}\right).
Then
H
is monogenic on the connected open set
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\overline{\Omega},
since supp
F\in\overline{\Omega}.
By real analyticity of monogenic functions,
H
vanishes on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.
Hence,
\tilde{f}=f-H
gives us the required extension.
∎

Strong Gelfand Pairs of SL( 𝟐 , 𝐩 \bf{2,p} )
Andrea Barton, Stephen P. Humphries

Abstract
. A strong Gelfand pair ( G , H ) (G,H) is a group G G together with a subgroup H H such that every irreducible character of H H induces a multiplicity-free character of G G . We classify the strong Gelfand pairs of the special linear groups S ​ L ​ ( 2 , p ) SL(2,p) where p p is a prime. Keywords : Strong Gelfand pair, special linear group, upper triangular group, characters, finite groups.

1.
Introduction

Given a finite group
G
, a
multiplicity-free character of
G
is a character
\chi
of
G
such that for every irreducible character
\psi
of
G
, we have
\langle\chi,\psi\rangle\leq 1
.

A
Gelfand pair

(G,H)
is a group
G
together with a subgroup
H
such that the trivial character of
H
induces a multiplicity-free character of

G
. The importance of Gelfand pairs is indicated by the following equivalent conditions for finite groups (
[
3
]
,
[
19
]
):

(i) The algebra of
(H,H)
-double invariant functions on G with multiplication defined by convolution is commutative.

(ii) For any irreducible representation
\rho
of
G
, the space of
H
-invariant vectors in
\rho
is no more than 1-dimensional.

(iii) For any irreducible representation
\rho
of
G
, the dimension of
{\rm Hom}_{H}(\rho,\mathbf{1})
is less than or equal to 1, where
\mathbf{1}
denotes the trivial representation.

(iv) The permutation representation of
G
on the cosets of
H
is multiplicity-free.

(v) The centralizer algebra of the permutation representation is commutative.

(vi) The double cosets
HgH
determine a commutative Schur ring over
G
.

A
strong Gelfand pair

(G,H)
is a finite group
G
together with a subgroup
H
such that every irreducible character of
H
induces a multiplicity-free character of
G
. In other words, for any
\chi\in\hat{G}
,
\psi\in\hat{H}
, where
\hat{G}
is the set of all irreducible characters of
G
and
\hat{H}
is the set of all irreducible characters of
H
, we have
\langle\chi,\psi\uparrow^{G}\rangle\leq 1
. We note that a strong Gelfand pair is also referred to using the phrase “multiplicity one property” or “multiplicity one theorem” (see
[
1
,
2
,
16
]
).

Equivalently,
(G,H)
is a strong Gelfand pair if and only if the Schur ring determined by the
H
-classes
g^{H}=\{g^{h}:h\in H\},g\in G
, is commutative
[
5
]
.

The strong Gelfand subgroups of the symmetric group were found in
[
3
]
; those for wreath products of the form
F\wr S_{n}
, where
F
is a finite abelian group, were found in
[
4
]
.

Let
G={\rm SL}(2,p)
and let
U=U(2,p)=\mathcal{C}_{p-1}\rtimes\mathcal{C}_{p}\leq G
be the subgroup of upper triangular matrices. When
p
is odd, we let
H_{2}\leq U
be the unique subgroup of
U
of index
2
. The main result of this paper is to determine the strong Gelfand pairs of
{\rm SL}(2,p)
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>11
be prime.

(i) If
p\equiv 1\operatorname{mod}4
, then there is only one strong Gelfand pair
(G,U)
.

(ii) If
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
, then there are two strong Gelfand pairs:
(G,U)
and
(G,H_{2})
.

In Theorem
4.4
we classify the strong Gelfand pairs for primes
p\leq 11
.

All computations were made using Magma
[
18
]
.

3.
Group Theory Lemmas

We collect here results that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
H\leq K\leq G
are groups. If
(G,K)
is not a strong Gelfand pair, then neither is
(G,H)
.

Proof.

Suppose
(G,K)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.
So there are
\chi\in\hat{G},\psi\in\hat{K}
with
\langle\chi,\psi\uparrow^{G}\rangle\geq 2
.
Let
\varphi
be an irreducible character of H with
\langle\varphi\uparrow^{K},\psi\rangle>0.

Because
(\varphi\uparrow^{K})\uparrow^{G}=\varphi\uparrow^{G}
and by Frobenius reciprocity, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\varphi\uparrow^{G},\chi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\varphi\uparrow^{K})\uparrow^{G},\chi\rangle=\langle\varphi\uparrow^{K},\chi|_{K}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\varphi\uparrow^{K},\psi\rangle\cdot\langle\psi,\chi|_{K}\rangle>1.

Thus,
(G,H)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
N\leq H\leq G
are groups with
N\triangleleft G
. Then
(G,H)
is a strong Gelfand pair if and only if
(G/N,H/N)
is a strong Gelfand pair.

Proof.

This result follows from the fact that the lift of an irreducible character is irreducible
[
12
, Theorem 17.3]
.
∎

For a finite group
G
, the
total character

\tau
of
G
is the sum of all the irreducible characters of
G

[
10
,
14
,
15
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H\leq G
and let
\tau
be the total character of
H
. If there is some character
\chi
of
G
with
\deg(\tau)<\deg(\chi)
, then
(G,H)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{r}
be the irreducible characters of
H
. Then for any character
\chi
of
G
we have

\chi|_{H}=\Sigma_{i=1}^{r}\epsilon_{i}\varphi_{i},

where each
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume
(G,H)
is a strong Gelfand pair. So,
\epsilon_{i}\leq 1
for all
i
. Thus

\deg(\chi)=\Sigma_{i=1}^{r}\epsilon_{i}\deg(\varphi_{i})\leq\Sigma_{i=1}^{r}\deg(\varphi_{i})=\deg(\tau).

We conclude that whenever
\deg(\tau)<\deg(\chi)
, it must be that
(G,H)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between maximal subgroups of
{\rm SL}(2,p)
and maximal subgroups of
{\rm PSL}(2,p)
.

Proof.

By the lattice isomorphism theorem, this is equivalent to showing each maximal subgroup of
G
contains
Z(G)=\langle-1\rangle
. By way of contradiction, suppose there is a maximal subgroup
M
of
G
such that
-1\notin M
. Then
a\notin M
, because
a^{(p-1)/2}=-1
. But then,
a\notin\langle-1,M\rangle
, so
M
was not maximal.
∎

Because
(G,U)
is a strong Gelfand pair, we must now consider the maximal subgroups of
U
to determine if any of them forms a strong Gelfand pair with

.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any proper subgroup
K\leq U
,
K\neq H_{2}
, the pair
(G,K)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

When
p\equiv 1\operatorname{mod}4
, the pair
(G,H_{2})
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

When
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
, the pair
(G,H_{2})
is a strong Gelfand pair.

Proof.

Recall that
U=\langle a,b\rangle=\langle a\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
, so for any subgroup
K\leq U
we have the following possibilities:

(i)

p\nmid|K|
, in which case
K
is conjugate to
\langle a^{q}\rangle
for some
q
dividing
p-1
. The maximal subgroups of
U
containing
K
are all conjugate to
A=\langle a\rangle
, the cyclic group of order
p-1
.
We see that

\langle\varphi|_{A},1_{A}\rangle=\frac{1}{p-1}(p+p+1+\cdot\cdot\cdot+1)=3.

Thus,
(G,A)
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

(ii)

p\big||K|
, in which case
K=\langle a^{q}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
for some
q
dividing
p-1
. The maximal subgroups are those with
q
prime. We define
H_{q}=\langle a^{q}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
, which has index
q
in
U
. Note that this corresponds with our earlier definition of
H_{2}
. Let
p_{q}=(p-1)/q
.

First, we look at
q>2
. Then
p_{q}\leq(p-1)/3<(p-3)/2
, so referring to the bounds for Table
2
we consider the character
\chi_{p_{q}}
of
G
. We find that the inner product
\langle\chi_{p_{q}}|_{H_{q}},1_{H_{q}}\rangle
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\cdot p_{q}}\Big((p+1)1

\displaystyle+(p+1)1+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+2p(p_{q}-2)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{p(p-1)}\left(4p+2p\left(\frac{p-1}{q}\right)-4p\right)=2.

Thus,
(G,H_{q})
is not a strong Gelfand pair whenever
q>2
.

When
q=2
, there are two cases.
First, consider
p\equiv 1\operatorname{mod}4
. As we can deduce from Table
1
, each linear character of
H_{2}
is the restriction of two different linear characters of
U
. In particular,
\chi_{0,(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}=\chi_{0,3(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}
. From case (iv) of Proposition
4.2
,

\displaystyle\chi_{(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{0,(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}+\chi_{0,3(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}+\chi_{1,2}|_{H_{2}}+\chi_{2,2}|_{H_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\chi_{0,(p-1)/4}|_{H_{2}}+\chi_{1,2}|_{H_{2}}+\chi_{2,2}|_{H_{2}},

and we conclude that
(G,H_{2})
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

Finally, if
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
, we show that
(G,H_{2})
is a strong Gelfand pair. Because the subgroup
H_{2}
has index 2 in
H
, the result in
[
12
, Prop. 20.10]
applies. Since
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
,
|H_{2}|=p(p-1)/2
is odd,
-1\notin H_{2}
. Thus, because
\chi(-1)\neq 0
for all
\chi\in\hat{H}
, we have that each irreducible character of
H_{2}
is simply the restriction of an irreducible character of
H
. It follows by Proposition
4.2
that
(G,H_{2})
is a strong Gelfand pair.

Because
(G,H_{2})
is a strong Gelfand pair when
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
, we must now consider subgroups of
H_{2}
. We show that no proper subgroup of
H_{2}
forms a strong Gelfand pair with
G
. Because
H_{2}=\langle a^{2}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
, any subgroup of
H_{2}
is of the form (i)
\langle a^{2k}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
or (ii)
\langle a^{2k}\rangle
. The subgroups in (i) will be maximal when
k
is prime; the maximal subgroup in (ii) is
\langle a^{2}\rangle
.

(i)
If
k
is an odd prime, we see that
\langle a^{2k}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
will be a subgroup of
H_{k}
. Because
(G,H_{k})
is not a strong Gelfand pair, it follows that
\langle a^{2k}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
also does not form a strong Gelfand pair with
G
.

If
k=2
, then
4|p-1
, and we have
H_{4}=\langle a^{4}\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle
.
Then
(p-1)/4<(p-3)/2
, so referring to the bounds for Table
2
we consider the character
\chi_{p_{4}}
of
G
. We find that the inner product
\langle\chi_{p_{4}}|_{H_{4}},1_{H_{4}}\rangle
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\cdot p_{4}}\Big((p+1)1

\displaystyle+(p+1)1+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+1\cdot p_{2}+2p(p_{4}-2)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{p(p-1)}\left(4p+2p\left(\frac{p-1}{4}\right)-4p\right)=2.

So
(G,H_{4})
is not a strong Gelfand pair.

(ii)
We see that
\langle a^{2}\rangle
is a subgroup of
A
. Thus, by Lemma
3.1
and because
(G,A)
is not a strong Gelfand pair,
(G,\langle a^{2}\rangle)
is also not a strong Gelfand pair.

Thus, we conclude that there is no subgroup
K
of
H_{2}
such that
(G,K)
forms a strong Gelfand pair.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we have shown that the only proper subgroup of
U
which forms a strong Gelfand pair with
G
is the subgroup
H_{2}
when
p\equiv 3\operatorname{mod}4
.
∎

The above applies whenever
p\geq 13
, and so concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
4.1
. ∎

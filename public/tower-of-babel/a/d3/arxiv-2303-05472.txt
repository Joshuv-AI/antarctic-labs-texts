Tangent spaces on the trianguline variety at companion points
Seginus Mowlavi

Abstract
. Many results about the geometry of the trianguline variety have been obtained by Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen. Among them, using geometric methods, they have computed a formula for the dimension of the tangent space of the trianguline variety at dominant crystalline generic points, which has a conjectural generalisation to companion ( i.e. non-dominant) points. In an earlier work, they proved a weaker form of this formula under the assumption of modularity using arithmetic methods. We prove a generalisation of a result of Bellaïche-Chenevier in p p -adic Hodge theory and use it to extend the arithmetic methods of Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen to a wide class of companion points.

,
i.e.
a family of elements of
M
such that
e_{i}\in\Fil_{i}M
and
e_{i}\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Fil_{i-1}M
generates
\Fil_{i}M/\Fil_{i-1}M
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
G\coloneqq\GL_{n,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be the general linear group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
B
be its Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices; the Weyl group of
G
is the group
\mathcal{S}_{n}
of bijections of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
to itself.

The map
G(R)\longrightarrow\{\text{complete flags of }M\}
which sends a matrix
(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in G(R)
to the flag
\left(\bigoplus_{j\leq k}Rb_{j}\right)_{1\leq k\leq n}
, where
b_{j}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{ij}e_{j}
, factors through
B(R)
and induces an bijection
G(R)/B(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\{\text{complete flags of }M\}
, hence an injection

(2.1)

\{\text{complete flags of }M\}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow(G/B)(R)

(see
[
25
, §5.4, §5.5]
). We call the image under (
2.1
) of a given flag the
position of the flag relative to
\Fil_{\bullet}M
.

Recall the Schubert decomposition on the flag variety
G/B
. We embed
\mathcal{S}_{n}
in
G
by mapping each
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
to the matrix
\dot{w}=(w_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
defined by
w_{ij}=1
if
i=w(j)
and
w_{ij}=0
if
i\neq w(j)
. Let
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
. The left-action of
B
on
G
by multiplication induces an action on
G/B
; write
BwB/B
for the orbit of
\dot{w}B/B
under this action. Then
BwB/B
is a smooth locally closed subvariety of
G/B
called a
Schubert cell
, and we have the decomposition

(2.2)

G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}BwB/B

(see
[
31
, Theorem 21.73]
). The cells
BwB/B
are stable under the left action of
B
, thus their Zariski closure
\overline{BwB/B}
too, and this action is transitive on
BwB/B
. Thus, a closed cell
\overline{BwB/B}
intersects an open cell
Bw^{\prime}B/B
if and only if
Bw^{\prime}B\subseteq\overline{BwB/B}
, which happens if and only if
w^{\prime}\preceq w
for the Bruhat order (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.11]
). Therefore (
2.2
) gives a good stratification of
G/B
, and we have a more general decomposition into locally closed strata

\overline{BwB/B}=\coprod_{w^{\prime}\preceq w}Bw^{\prime}B/B

for all
w\in W
.

For flags, Schubert cells are elementarily described as such: given a flag
\mathcal{F}
of
M
, its position relative to
\Fil_{\bullet}M
is in the closed cell
(\overline{BwB/B})(R)
if and only if
\mathcal{F}
can be represented by a matrix
(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in G(R)
such that
a_{ij}=0
for all
i>w(j)
; and it is in the open cell
(BwB/B)(R)
if and only if we can impose the additional condition
a_{w(j),j}\in R^{\times}
for all
j
. (Thus we see that the Schubert decomposition induces a partition of the
R
-points if and only if
R
is a field).

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Let
R
be a ring, let
M
be a free
R
-module of rank
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
equipped with a complete flag
\Fil_{\bullet}M
. Let
x\in(G/B)(R)
be a point corresponding to a flag
(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
of
M
under (
2.1
). Then
x\in(BwB/B)(R)
for some
w\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
if and only if
(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)})/(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)-1})
is free of rank
1
over
R
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a basis of
\Fil_{\bullet}M
.

If
x\in(BwB/B)(R)
, then there is a basis
(b_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
of
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
such that each
b_{j}
can be written
b_{j}=\sum_{i\leq w(j)}r_{ij}e_{i}
with
r_{w(j),j}\in R^{\times}
. Then, for all
1\leq j\leq n
, the injective morphism

(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)})/(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)-1})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\Fil_{w(j)}/\Fil_{w(j)-1}

sends
r_{w(j),j}^{-1}b_{j}\,\mathrm{mod}\,(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)-1})
to
e_{w(j)}\,\mathrm{mod}\,\Fil_{w(j)-1}
, so it is an isomorphism.

Conversely, assume that
(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)})/(\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)-1})
is free of rank
1
over
R
for all
1\leq j\leq n
; it has a basis element that lifts to some
b_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\Fil_{w(j)}
. Then each
b_{j}
can be written
b_{j}=\sum_{i\leq w(j)}r_{ij}e_{i}
in
\mathcal{F}_{j}
with
r_{w(j),j}\in R^{\times}
. Therefore it is enough to prove that
(b_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
,
i.e.
that
b_{j}\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
is a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{j}/\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. We proceed by induction on
j
. Let
b=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}r_{i}e_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
such that
b\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
is a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{j}/\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
. As
b_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
, one can write

(2.3)

b_{j}=\sum_{k<j}\lambda_{k}b_{k}+\lambda b

for some
\lambda_{k},\lambda\in R
. Let
I
be the ideal of
R
generated by
\lambda
. If
\lambda_{k}\notin I
for some
k<j
, take such a
\lambda_{k}
with
w(k)
maximal. Then taking the coordinate along
e_{w(k)}
of (
2.3
) gives

r_{w(k),j}=\lambda_{k}r_{w(k),k}+\sum_{k^{\prime}<j\text{ s.t.\ }w(k^{\prime})>w(k)}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}r_{w(k),k^{\prime}}+\lambda r_{w(k)}\notin I

since
r_{w(k),k}\in R^{\times}
. In particular
r_{w(k),j}\neq 0
, so
w(k)\leq w(j)
. This proves that
\lambda_{k}\in I
for all
k<j
such that
w(k)>w(j)
. Then (
2.3
) along
e_{w(j)}
gives

r_{w(j),j}=\sum_{k<j\text{ s.t.\ }w(k)>w(j)}\lambda_{k}r_{w(j),k}+\lambda r_{w(j)}\in I\,.

As
r_{w(j),j}\in R^{\times}
, we deduce
I=R
. Hence
\lambda\in R^{\times}
, so
b_{j}\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
is a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{j}/\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
; this concludes the proof.
∎

We are mainly concerned with the situation of

and
M=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
, where we fix an algebra
A\in\mathbf{Ar}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and a de Rham representation
V\in\mathbf{Rep}_{A}(\mathcal{G}_{K})
. More specifically, we make the following two assumptions: (i)
A
is an algebra over a finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which is the residue field of
A
and which splits
K
, and (ii)

is regular.

Then we define a complete flag
\Fil_{\bullet}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
on the free
(K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A)
-module
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
by
\Fil_{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\coloneqq 0
and

\Fil_{i}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Sigma}\Fil^{-h_{\tau,i}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)_{\tau},\quad 1\leq i\leq n

where
n=\rk_{A}(V)
and
h_{\tau,1}<\ldots<h_{\tau,n}
are the
\tau
-Hodge-Tate weights of

.

Next, we further assume that
V
is crystalline. Recall the following definition from
[
1
, §2.4]
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{Ar}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, let
V\in\mathbf{Rep}_{A}(\mathcal{G}_{K})
be any crystalline representation. A
refinement
of
V
is a complete flag on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
which is stable by the linearised Frobenius
\Phi
.

A refinement
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
of

induces a complete flag
K\otimes_{K_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}
on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
, and the position of
\Fil_{\bullet}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
relative to

determines an
A

\left(\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G/B)_{K}\right)\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}L\simeq\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}(G/B)_{L}

where

and

is the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices.

Remark 2.1.4
.

A refinement of
V
determines an ordered sequence of eigenvalues of
\Phi
,
i.e.
a sequence
(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})\in(K_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A)^{n}
such that the characteristic polynomial of
\Phi
can be written
P_{\Phi}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda-\varphi_{i})\in(K_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A)[\lambda]
. The following proposition is a partial converse of this fact.

Proposition 2.1.5
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\in A
be such that
(1\otimes\varphi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
is a sequence of eigenvalues of the Frobenius
\Phi
on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
in the sense of Remark
2.1.4
. Assume that the
\varphi_{i}
have pairwise distinct reductions modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Then there is a unique refinement of
V
associated to
(1\otimes\varphi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
.

The proof uses the following classic result of linear algebra.

Lemma 2.1.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring with unity, let
M
be an
R
-module and
f\in\End_{R}(M)
.

(1)

Assume that there are
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in R
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, with
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in R^{\times}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
, such that
(f-\lambda_{1})\cdots(f-\lambda_{m})=0\in\End_{R}(M)
. Then
M
is the direct sum of eigenspaces

(2.4)

M=\ker(f-\lambda_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\ker(f-\lambda_{m}).

(2)

Assume that
R
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and that
M
is free of rank
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Also assume that the characteristic polynomial of
f
can be written
P_{f}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda-\lambda_{i})\in R[\lambda]
with the
\lambda_{i}
having pairwise distinct reductions
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. Then the eigenspaces
\ker(f-\lambda_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq n
, are free of rank
1
over
R
and any choice of generators of these eigenspaces form a basis of
M
.

Proof.

We first prove
(1)
. One can find polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}\in R[\lambda]
such that

(2.5)

\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}P_{i}(\lambda)\prod_{j\neq i}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})=1\in R[\lambda]\,;

we show this by induction on
m
. The case
m=1
is trivial with
P_{1}=1
; for
m>1
, let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m-1}\in R[\lambda]
satisfy

\sum_{1\leq i\leq m-1}Q_{i}(\lambda)\prod_{j\neq i,m}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})=1\in R[\lambda]\,.

For
l\coloneqq\prod_{1\leq i<m}(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{i})\in R^{\times}
, there is
Q\in R[\lambda]
such that
\prod_{1\leq i<m}(\lambda-\lambda_{i})=Q(\lambda)(\lambda-\lambda_{m})+l
. Then taking
P_{i}\coloneqq-l^{-1}Q_{i}Q\in R[\lambda]
for
1\leq i<m
and
P_{m}\coloneqq l^{-1}
gives (
2.5
).

Applying (
2.5
) to
\lambda=f
, we see that any
v\in M
decomposes as
v=v_{1}+\ldots+v_{m}
, where
v_{i}\coloneqq\prod_{j\neq i}(f-\lambda_{j})P_{i}(f)v
for
1\leq i\leq m
, and obviously
v_{i}\in\ker(f-\lambda_{i})
by the assumption. Now to check that the sum
M=\ker(f-\lambda_{1})+\ldots+\ker(f-\lambda_{m})
is direct, let
v_{1}+\ldots+v_{m}=0
, with
v_{i}\in\ker(f-\lambda_{i})
. We show by induction on the number of nonzero
v_{i}
that
v_{i}=0
for all
i
. Take
i_{0}
such that
v_{i_{0}}\neq 0
; applying
f-\lambda_{i_{0}}
gives
\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i_{0}})v_{i}=0
, so by induction
(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i_{0}})v_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. We get
v_{i}=0
for
i\neq i_{0}
since
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i_{0}}
is a unit, then
v_{i_{0}}=0
. This proves
(1)
.

We now prove
(2)
. The reduction of
f
to the
(R/\mathfrak{m})
-vector space
M/\mathfrak{m}M
has pairwise distinct eigenvalues
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
; choose a corresponding diagonalisation basis
(\overline{v_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq n}
. Choose a lift
w_{i}\in M
of each
\overline{v_{i}}
. By
(1)
and Cayley-Hamilton’s theorem, we can write
w_{i}=v_{i,1}+\ldots+v_{i,n}
with
v_{i,j}\in\ker(f-\lambda_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then, in
M/\mathfrak{m}M
,

\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}(\overline{\lambda_{j}}-\overline{\lambda_{i}})\overline{v_{i,j}}=f(\overline{v_{i}})-\overline{\lambda_{i}}\overline{v_{i}}=0

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Applying
(1)
to
M/\mathfrak{m}M
, we see that the eigenvectors
(\overline{\lambda_{j}}-\overline{\lambda_{i}})\overline{v_{i,j}}
are zero for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, which means, since the
\overline{\lambda_{i}}
are pairwise distinct, that
v_{i,j}\in\mathfrak{m}
for all
i\neq j
. Therefore
v_{i}\coloneqq v_{i,i}=w_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}v_{i,j}
lifts
\overline{v_{i}}
. By Nakayama’s lemma, the
v_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, generate
M
. Since
M
is free of rank
n
, the
v_{i}
actually form a basis of
M
. Since
Rv_{i}\subseteq\ker(f-\lambda_{i})
for each
i
, (
2.4
) gives
Rv_{i}=\ker(f-\lambda_{i})
. This proves
(2)
.
∎

Write
K_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Sigma_{0}}Ae_{\tau}

Proof.

Recall from Berger’s work the functor

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V))=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{rig}}(V))=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
for all

. It is also known that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}
and

are exact functors on the full subcategory of crystalline

-modules,
i.e.

-modules

.

Let

of parameter

. For

induces an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{A,K}\left(z^{\mathbf{k}_{i}}\nr(\varphi_{i})\right)\right)\longrightarrow 0

. Therefore

and

) refinement of

associated to

.

Similarly, the exact functor

induces for each

in

. It induces, for each

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\Fil^{k}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{A,K}\left(z^{\mathbf{k}_{i}}\nr(\varphi_{i})\right)\right)_{\tau}\simeq\begin{cases}0&\text{if }k\neq k_{\tau,i}\\

(see

\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\right)_{\tau}/\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}+1}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\right)_{\tau}=\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\right)_{\tau}/\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}+1}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i-1}\right)_{\tau}\\
\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\right)_{\tau}/\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i-1}\right)_{\tau}

The exactness of

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\cap\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\right)_{\tau}/\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{dR},i}\cap\Fil^{k_{\tau,i}+1}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\right)_{\tau}\\

3.3.
Strong linkage and companion points

We recall a tool from
[
11
]
to find points on
X_{p}(\bar{\rho})
(so-called
companion points
), which is related to the notion of strong linkage of characters which we now explain.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, together with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Phi\subseteq\mathfrak{t}^{*}\coloneqq\Hom_{k}(\mathfrak{t},\overline{k})
be the root system of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
(where
\overline{k}
is an algebraic closure of
k
), let
W\coloneqq W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
be the Weyl group which acts on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
\Phi
. Let
E\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
\Phi
and equip
E
with its nondegenerate bilinear form
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
invariant by
W
(see
[
9
, Chap. VI, §1.1, Prop. 3]
). Choose a basis
I
of
\Phi
and call its elements
simple roots
; this choice induces a partial order on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
by setting
\mu\leq\lambda
if and only if
\lambda-\mu
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-linear combination of simple roots. For each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
\alpha^{\vee}\coloneqq\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha\mid\alpha)}
be the coroot associated to
\alpha
. Then
\left\{\alpha^{\vee}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\alpha\in I\right\}
is a basis of
E
; it has an orthogonal dual under
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
, whose elements we call
fundamental weights
. We call
special weight
the sum of the fundamental weights; it is an element
\varpi\in E
that satisfies in particular
s_{\alpha}\varpi=\varpi-\alpha
for any simple root
\alpha\in I
, where
s_{\alpha}\in W
is the reflection associated to
\alpha
, defined by
s_{\alpha}\colon\lambda\mapsto\lambda-(\lambda\mid\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
. Note that
\varpi
is usually noted
\rho
,
e.g.
[
24
]
, but we wish to avoid confusion with global representations.

Definition 3.3.1
.

The
dot action
is the action of
W
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
defined by

w\cdot\lambda\coloneqq w(\lambda+\varpi)-\varpi

for all
w\in W
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. We say that
\mu
is
strongly linked
to
\lambda
, and write
\mu\uparrow\lambda
, if there exist roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in\Phi
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
such that
\mu=(s_{\alpha_{r}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}})\cdot\lambda
and
(s_{\alpha_{i+1}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}})\cdot\lambda<(s_{\alpha_{i}}\ldots s_{\alpha_{1}})\cdot\lambda
for all
1\leq i\leq r-1
.

We apply this, for each place
v\in S_{p}
, to the context of the split reductive group
G_{v,L}\coloneqq\left(\Res_{F_{\tilde{v}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\GL_{n,F_{\tilde{v}}}\right)\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}L\simeq\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v}}\GL_{n,L}
over
L
(where
\Sigma_{v}\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{\tilde{v}},L)\simeq\Sigma
), with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{v,L}=\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}\gl_{n,L}
and Cartan algebra
\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}=\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v}}\mathfrak{t}_{L}
(with
\mathfrak{t}_{L}\simeq L^{n}
). Then we have
\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}\simeq(\overline{L}^{n})^{\Sigma_{v}}
as vector spaces over
\overline{L}
, and the Weyl group
W=(\mathcal{S}_{n})^{\Sigma_{v}}
acts on
\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
in the obvious way. We choose the basis
I\subset\Phi
corresponding to the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices; the simple roots are the
\alpha=(\alpha_{\tau^{\prime},i^{\prime}})_{\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{v},1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n}\in\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
such that, for some
\tau\in\Sigma_{v}
and
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have
\alpha_{\tau,i}=-\alpha_{\tau,i+1}=1
and
\alpha_{\tau^{\prime},i^{\prime}}=0
for all
\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau
and
i^{\prime}\notin\{i,i+1\}
. Then the special weight is
\varpi=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-i\right)_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v},1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
, and the dot action expresses as

w\cdot\lambda=\left(\lambda_{\tau,w_{\tau}^{-1}(i)}+i-w_{\tau}^{-1}(i)\right)_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v},1\leq i\leq n}

for
w=(w_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v}}\in W
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{\tau,i})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v},1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
.

If
\underline{\delta}_{v}=(\delta_{v,i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\colon T_{v,L}\longrightarrow L^{\prime\times}
is a continuous character, for some place
v\in S_{p}
and some finite extension
L^{\prime}
of
L
, then one can see
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\underline{\delta}_{v}=(\wt_{\tau}(\delta_{v,i}))_{\tau\in\Sigma_{v},1\leq i\leq n}
as an element of
\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
.

Definition 3.3.2
.

Let
\underline{\delta}=(\underline{\delta}_{v})_{v\in S_{p}},\underline{\epsilon}=(\underline{\epsilon}_{v})_{v\in S_{p}}\in\widehat{T}_{p,L}
. We say that
\underline{\epsilon}
is
strongly linked
to
\underline{\delta}
, if
\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\underline{\epsilon}_{v}\uparrow\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\underline{\delta}_{v}
in
\mathfrak{t}_{v,L}^{*}
for all
v\in S_{p}
, and if
\underline{\epsilon}\underline{\delta}^{-1}
is algebraic in the sense that for all
v\in S_{p}
there exists
\mathbf{k}_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma_{v}}
such that
\underline{\epsilon}_{v}\underline{\delta}_{v}^{-1}=z^{\mathbf{k}_{v}}
. In this case we write
\underline{\epsilon}\uparrow\underline{\delta}
.

Theorem 3.3.3

(Breuil-Hellmann-Schraen)
.

Let
x=(y,\underline{\delta})\in\mathfrak{X}_{\infty}\times_{L}\widehat{T}_{p,L}
be a point lying in
X_{p}(\bar{\rho})
, and let
\underline{\epsilon}\in\widehat{T}_{p,L}
be a character satisfying
\underline{\epsilon}\uparrow\underline{\delta}
. Then the point
x^{\prime}=(y,\underline{\epsilon})\in\mathfrak{X}_{\infty}\times_{L}\widehat{T}_{p,L}
also lies in
X_{p}(\bar{\rho})
.

Proof.

This is
[
11
, Theorem 5.5]
; see (3.10) of
loc. cit
.
∎

Following the exposition of

h
yield

(4.13)

\dim_{k(x)}T_{\widetilde{W}_{\bar{r},w}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathrm{cr}},(r,\underline{\varphi})}-\dim_{k(x)}T_{\widetilde{W}_{\bar{r},w_{\mathrm{sat}}}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathrm{cr}},(r,\underline{\varphi})}\\
=\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma}\dim_{k(x)}T_{\overline{(Bw_{\tau}B/B)}_{k(x)}^{\mathrm{rig}},x_{\tau}}-\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma}\dim_{k(x)}T_{\overline{(Bw_{\mathrm{sat},\tau}B/B)}_{k(x)}^{\mathrm{rig}},x_{\tau}}\,.

(4.14)

\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma}\dim_{k(x)}T_{\overline{(Bw_{\mathrm{sat},\tau}B/B)}_{k(x)}^{\mathrm{rig}},x_{\tau}}=\dim\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma}(Bw_{\mathrm{sat},\tau}B/B)_{L}^{\mathrm{rig}}=\length(w_{\mathrm{sat}})\,.

Abstract
. We show that in every dimension n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 , there exists a smooth closed manifold M n M^{n} which does not admit a smooth positive scalar curvature (“psc”) metric, but M M admits an L ∞ \mathrm{L}^{\infty} -metric which is smooth and has psc outside a singular set of codimension ≥ 8 \geq 8 . This provides counterexamples to a conjecture of Schoen. In fact, there are such examples of arbitrarily high dimension with only single point singularities. We also discuss related phenomena on exotic spheres and tori. In addition, we provide examples of L ∞ \mathrm{L}^{\infty} -metrics on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for certain n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 which are smooth and have psc outside the origin, but cannot be smoothly approximated away from the origin by everywhere smooth Riemannian metrics of non-negative scalar curvature. This stands in precise contrast to established smoothing results via Ricci–DeTurck flow for singular metrics with stronger regularity assumptions. Finally, as a positive result, we describe a KO \mathrm{KO} -theoretic condition which obstructs the existence of L ∞ \mathrm{L}^{\infty} -metrics that are smooth and of psc outside a finite subset. This shows that closed enlargeable spin manifolds do not carry such metrics.

Introduction

Studying weak lower curvature bounds in various singular settings has been a core aspect of Riemannian geometry since a long time.
While for spaces with
scalar curvature
bounded below such a theory is not yet fully developed, various approaches with results in different directions have been proposed recently (for instance
[
9
,
31
,
24
,
6
,
2
,
51
,
34
,
18
,
32
,
47
,
39
]
).
In this paper, we investigate scalar curvature lower bounds for Riemannian
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metrics in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 1.1

(cf.
[
34
, Definition 1.3]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold.
A
uniformly Euclidean
metric (or for short
“
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric
”
) is a measurable symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
g
on
M
such that there exists a smooth Riemannian metric
g_{0}
on
M
and
\Lambda>0
with

\Lambda^{-1}g_{0}(\xi,\xi)\leq g(\xi,\xi)\leq\Lambda g_{0}(\xi,\xi)

for almost all
x\in M
and all tangent vectors
\xi\in\mathrm{T}_{x}M
.

For uniformly Euclidean metrics which are smooth outside a singular subset of sufficiently high codimension, it makes sense to study the behavior of lower scalar curvature bounds on the regular subset of the metric.
The first general results in this direction have been obtained by
[
34
]
, where the following conjecture of Schoen was popularized.

Conjecture 1.2

(Schoen, cf.
[
34
, Conjecture 1.5]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold that does not support any smooth Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature (i.e.
M
is Yamabe non-positive).
Let
S\subset M
be a closed submanifold of codimension
\geq 3
and let
g
be an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric on
M
which is smooth on
M\setminus S
and satisfies
\scal_{g}\geq 0
on
M\setminus S
.
Then
g
extends smoothly to
M
and
\Ric_{g}=0
.

In other words, this conjecture predicts that subsets of codimension at least three are invisible for the purposes of scalar curvature in a quite strong sense.
This differs from the setting of codimension one and two, where it is well known that such statements do not hold without additional regularity assumptions or boundary conditions, compare
[
34
, §8.1–8.2]
.
Likewise, the condition of being uniformly Euclidean cannot be dropped from
1.2
, otherwise suitable Schwarzschild metrics would yield counterexamples with point singularities, compare
[
34
, §8.3]
.

1.2
has attracted considerable interest and it has been confirmed in many specific cases.
For instance, if
\dim(M)=3
, it holds in general by
[
34
, Corollary 1.6]
(modulo the caveat of
Remark

1.4
below).
In the
4
-dimensional case, far-reaching results have been established by
[
29
]
.
In a closely related direction, a version of Llarull’s theorem for metrics with
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
point singularities was recently proved by
[
15
]
.
Fill-in problems with
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
point singularities in dimension three were studied by
[
38
, §4]
.
We also note that related results for conical singularities and the positive mass theorem have been proved by
[
16
,
17
]
.

In sharp contrast to all of this, we show in this paper that
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-regularity is in general not enough to retain positive scalar curvature (
“psc”
) obstructions on closed manifolds, even assuming smoothness outside a single point.

Theorem A
.

For every
n\geq 8
, there exists a closed smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
which does not admit a smooth psc metric, but which admits an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric which is smooth and of uniformly positive scalar curvature on the complement of a connected submanifold
S\subseteq M
of codimension
\geq 8
.
Moreover, the metric we obtain is of regularity
\mathrm{W}^{2,p/2}
, for every
p<\operatorname{codim}(S\subset M)
.
If
8\leq n\equiv 0,1,2,
or
4\mod 8
, then there are examples where
S=\{\ast\}
is a single point.

In particular,
1.2
is false in every dimension
n\geq 8
and there are counterexamples where the singular set has arbitrarily high codimension.

Remark 1.3
.

Due to the Sobolev
\mathrm{W}^{2,p/2}
-regularity, in our examples the scalar curvature even exists in the weak sense on all of
M
as a uniformly positive
\mathrm{L}^{p/2}
-function for all
p<\operatorname{codim}(S\subset M)
.
In particular, our metrics have positive distributional scalar curvature in the sense of
[
31
, Definition 2.1]
.
In fact, the scalar curvature of these examples blows up like
t^{-2}
, where
t
denotes the distance to the singular set.

The counterexamples in
A
are obtained via classification results based on surgery theory which allow to construct psc metrics with conical singularities on all high-dimensional simply connected manifolds (see
Proposition

2.1
).
To contrast this with the existing positive results concerning
1.2
, note that the proofs usually proceed by first blowing up the singularity to a complete psc metric on the complement of the singular set.
Next, appropriate topological obstructions are used to exclude the existence of such complete psc metrics, leading to a contradiction.
The last step fails precisely because this is unobstructed on punctured simply connected manifolds in high dimensions.

We also note that the literal statement of
1.2
cannot hold for a simpler reason. The following observation was brought to our attention by Hajo Hein:

Remark 1.4
.

One can introduce
“coordinate singularities”
:
For instance, fix a point
p\in\mathrm{T}^{n}
on the torus of dimension
n\geq 3
.
Choose a diffeomorphism
F\colon\mathrm{T}^{n}\setminus\{p\}\to\mathrm{T}^{n}\setminus\{p\}
which does not extend smoothly to all of
\mathrm{T}^{n}
but whose derivatives and that of its inverse lie in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathrm{T}^{n})
. Such a diffeomorphism
F
can be constructed locally in a neighborhood around
p
as the cone of a generic self-diffeomorphism
f\colon\mathrm{S}^{n-1}\to\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
that is isotopic to the identity.
This then allows
F
to be interpolated to be the identity map outside a slightly larger neighborhood so that it can be extended globally on
\mathrm{T}^{n}\setminus\{p\}
.
Then
g=F^{\ast}g_{\mathrm{flat}}
yields an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathrm{T}^{n}
which is smooth and flat on
\mathrm{T}^{n}\setminus\{p\}
, but which does not extend smoothly to all of
\mathrm{T}^{n}
.

In light of the above, it makes sense to separate
1.2
into an
obstruction aspect
, that is, concerning the non-existence of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metrics which are smooth and have psc on
M\setminus S
, and a
rigidity aspect
concerning extendability of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metrics which are Ricci-flat on
M\setminus S
.
In fact,
A
shows that the obstruction aspect alone fails dramatically (see also
Remark

1.7
below).

To study the rigidity aspect,
Remark

1.4
may suggest a minor clarification by only requiring a smooth extension of the metric after possibly changing the smooth atlas near the singular set.
This possibility is implicitly accounted for in the statements of removable singularity results for Einstein metrics (e.g.
[
3
,
50
]
) and thus the
3
-dimensional positive result of
[
34
, Corollary 1.6]
needs to be interpreted in this way.
However, in high dimensions we do not expect this to be enough either because there is no general removable singularity theorem for Ricci-flat metrics with point singularities of only
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
regularity.

1

For instance, take a cone over a non-round Einstein metric on a sphere (from e.g.
[

). This yields a singular Ricci-flat
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a single point singularity at the origin that is unremovable even up to changing the smooth atlas.
Moreover, such a metric is even uniformly bi-Lipschitz to the Euclidean metric although there exists no non-flat smooth metric of non-negative scalar curvature on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is bi-Lipschitz to the Euclidean metric.

We also note that some (but not all) of the smooth manifolds
M
obtained from the construction in

are homeomorphic to manifolds which admit smooth psc metrics even though
M
itself does not.
Notably, this applies to the examples with point singularities in dimensions

.
This also yields more concrete counterexamples to the obstruction part of Schoen’s conjecture:

Example 1.5

(see

2.7
and
2.5
)
.

Every high-dimensional exotic sphere admits an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric which outside a single point is smoothly isometric to the standard round sphere, but there are exotic spheres which do not admit any globally smooth psc metric.

In particular, given an exotic sphere
\Sigma
, there is an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric
g
on
\Sigma
of
\scal_{g}=n(n-1)
which cannot be extended to a smooth metric, and there is a

(\Sigma,g)\to(\mathrm{S}^{n},g_{\circ})
of degree

which cannot be a global isometry in the Riemannian sense.
This shows that a naive statement of Llarull’s theorem in the setting of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metrics cannot hold true and instead one needs a more subtle version, for instance as in
[
15
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Similarly, a more severe variant of the phenomenon described in
Remark

1.4
concerning the rigidity part occurs on exotic tori:

Example 1.6

(see
Theorem

2.8
)
.

There are exotic tori
T_{\Sigma}
with an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metric which outside a single point is smooth and flat, although
T_{\Sigma}
does not admit any globally smooth metric of non-negative scalar curvature (i.e.
T_{\Sigma}
is Yamabe-negative).

Remark 1.7

(see
Remark

2.6
)
.

Despite highlighting the previous examples, we emphasize that the failure of
1.2
does not primarily lie in the smooth structure.
It also follows from the proof of
A
that in every dimension
\geq 8
there exist counterexamples, where
M
is not homeomorphic to any smooth manifold which admits a psc metric.
This includes in particular the examples with point singularities where
8\leq n\equiv 0,4\mod 8
.
For example,
M
could be taken to be the product of at least two
\mathrm{K}3
surfaces and a torus of arbitrary dimension.

A more analytic approach to study
1.2
, and lower (scalar) curvature bounds on metrics with low regularity in general, is to regularize them using geometric flows, for a selection of works in this direction see

All existing results following this line need additional assumptions on the metric beyond being uniformly Euclidean—for instance, additional Sobolev or
\Ct^{0}

\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

It stands to reason that

\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

Remark 1.8
.

Indeed,
[
14
, Theorem 6.2]
showed that uniformly Euclidean metrics of
\mathrm{W}^{1,n}
-regularity can be smoothed via Ricci–DeTurck flow (see also
[

who treated the
\mathrm{W}^{2,2}

Since our counterexamples from

for all

\mathrm{W}^{1,n}
is the sharp threshold on the Sobolev scale for such a smoothing result in general.

In another direction,
[
7

recently showed that one may smooth away certain
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-singularities on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
using Ricci–DeTurck flow while preserving non-negativity of the scalar curvature, provided that the singular metric is sufficiently
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-close to the Euclidean metric.
Since the counterexamples in
A
are based on high-dimensional simply connected manifolds which do not admit psc, at first glance this may look like a subtle global phenomenon of the underlying closed manifold.
However, as we note in the following theorem, they actually lead to examples of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a single point singularity which are not smoothable while preserving non-negative scalar curvature.
In particular, this answers
[
7

.

For
n\geq 8
,
n\equiv 0,1,2,
or

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathrm{W}^{2,{p/2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}

Denote by

Let

\mathrm{L}^{\infty}

If
\scal_{g}\geq 0

\Ric_{g}\equiv 0
on
M\setminus S

Lemma A.3

Let

Proof.

Since every element of

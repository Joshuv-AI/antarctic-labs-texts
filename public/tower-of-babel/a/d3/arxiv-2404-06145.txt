Behaviors near explosion of nonlinear CSBPs with regularly varying mechanisms
Bo Li, Clément Foucart, Xiaowen Zhou

Abstract




We study the explosion phenomenon of nonlinear continuous-state branching processes (nonlinear CSBPs). First an explicit integral test for explosion is designed when the rate function does not increase too fast. We then exhibit three different regimes of explosion when the branching mechanism and the rate function are regularly varying respectively at
0
and
\infty
with indices
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta
and
\beta>0
. If
\alpha>0
then the renormalisation of the process before its explosion is linear. When moreover
\alpha\neq\beta
, the limiting distribution is that of a ratio of two independent random variables whose laws are identified. When
\alpha=\beta
, the limiting random variable shrinks to a constant. Last, when
\alpha=0
, i.e. the branching mechanism is slowly varying at
0
, the process is studied with the help of a nonlinear renormalisation. The limiting distribution becomes the inverse uniform distribution. This complements results known in the case of finite mean and provides new insight for the classical explosive continuous-state branching processes (for which
\beta=1
).

Keywords
: continuous-state branching process, nonlinear time-change, explosion, spectrally positive Lévy process, perpetual integral, asymptotic overshoot, regular variation.

Introduction and main results

Continuous-state branching processes (CSBP for short) are processes representing the total size of a population in which, intuitively, each individual reproduces independently with the same law. It is well known that when the offspring distribution has infinite first moment, the process might explode in finite time, in the sense that large jumps happen so fast that the process reaches infinity in finite time; see for instance Savits
[
24
]
and Grey
[
11
]
.

The purpose of this article is to study the renormalisations in law of the processes prior to their explosion. Instead of only considering classical CSBPs, we treat the larger class of the so-called nonlinear CSBPs. The latter have been introduced and studied in recent works; see e.g. Li and Zhou
[
16
]
and the references therein. They are parametrized by two functions:
a branching mechanism
\psi
, which is the Laplace exponent of a spectrally positive Lévy process (SPLP for short)
\xi:=(\xi(t),t\geq 0)
, and
a rate function
R
, which is assumed to be a
positive
measurable function on
(0,\infty)
, bounded away from both
0
and
\infty
on every compact subset of
(0,\infty)
. Equivalently,
1/R
is measurable positive and locally bounded.
Heuristically, the branching mechanism
\psi
governs the reproduction, as in the classical CSBP, and
R
controls the reproduction rate.

More explicitly, let
x\in[0,\infty)
and denote by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
the law of the SPLP
\xi
with
\xi(0)=x
a.s.
We will also write
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}
. For any
a,b\geq 0
, denote the first passage times of the process
\xi
by

\tau_{a}^{-}:=\inf\{t>0,\xi(t)<a\}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{b}^{+}:=\inf\{t>0,\xi(t)>b\}.

(1.1)

Define the additive functional
\eta
by

\eta(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{R(\xi(s))}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta^{-1}(s):=\inf\{t\geq 0:\eta(t)>s\},

(1.2)

for
t\in[0,\tau_{0}^{-})
and
s\in[0,\eta(\tau_{0}^{-}))
with convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. The nonlinear CSBP
X
, with branching mechanism
\psi
and rate function
R
is defined by time changing
\xi
as follows: for any
t\geq 0
,

X(t):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,\,\xi\big(\eta^{-1}(t)\big),&&\text{when}\,\,t\in[0,\eta(\tau^{-}_{0})),\\
&\,\,\infty,&&\text{when}\,\,\eta(\infty)\leq t<\tau^{-}_{0}=\infty,\\
&\,\,0,&&\text{when}\,\,\tau^{-}_{0}<\infty,\,\eta(\tau^{-}_{0})<\infty\,\,\text{and}\,\,t\geq\eta(\tau^{-}_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

We stress that by definition the process
X
takes its values in
[0,\infty]
and both boundaries
0
and
\infty
are absorbing.

In order to avoid cumbersome notation, we also denote the law of the process
X
started from
x
by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
. This will not cause any confusion. The assumption that
1/R
is positive on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
ensures that the time-changed SPLP is well-defined when started from a point
x\in(0,\infty)
. We also highlight that for any rate function
R
, the nonlinear CSBP
X
inherits from
\xi
the strong Markov property and its càdlàg sample paths.

The definition (
1.3
) above is also referred to as a Lamperti-type transform of the process
\xi
. We call
\xi
the
parent Lévy process
of
X
. It is well known that, if the function
R
is the identity function, the process
X
reduces to the classical CSBP with branching mechanism
\psi
, and if
R
is an exponential function, the process
X
is associated to a positive self-similar Markov process. The two aforementioned transforms are called the
Lamperti transformations
in the literature; see e.g. Kyprianou
[
15
, Chapter 12 and Chapter 13]
. We also mention that the polynomial case, for which
R(x)=x^{\beta}
, has been introduced in Li
[
18
]
.

A nice feature of this class of Markov processes is that the function
R
allows for a new degree of freedom to exhibit richer long-term behaviors (extinction, coming down from infinity, explosion, etc) in comparison to the classical CSBP framework.
For instance, Li et al.
[
17
]
investigate the role of the rate function in the extinguishing phenomenon i.e. the convergence of
X
towards
0
without reaching it. Foucart et al.
[
9
]
treat the case of a parent Lévy process that either oscillates or drifts towards
-\infty
and address the question of whether the time-changed Lévy process
X
can be started from infinity, and at what speed it comes down from infinity.
Last but not least, the study of the explosion of the process
X
and of its behavior “one second” prior to explosion has been carried out in
[
16
]
in the case of a parent Lévy process with finite mean. The latter assumption is in particular ruling out the classical CSBPs.

Our main objective is to investigate the case a parent Lévy process with
infinite
mean. We first collect some elementary properties of nonlinear CSBPs that are consequences of the time-change relationship (
1.3
). We review then briefly some results known in the setting of a jump measure with finite mean. We shall also explain why other arguments have to be designed when it is infinite.

From now on, consider a parent Lévy process
\xi
with no negative jumps drifting towards
+\infty
. For any
y>0
and
a>0
, set

T^{+}_{b}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}>b\}\text{ and }T^{-}_{a}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}\leq a\}.

By the assumption of local boundedness of
R
on
(0,\infty)
, we have for any
b>x>a>0
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely

\{T_{b}^{+}\wedge T_{a}^{-}<\infty\}=\{\tau_{b}^{+}\wedge\tau_{a}^{-}<\infty,\eta(\tau_{b}^{+}\wedge\tau_{a}^{-})<\infty\}=\{\tau_{b}^{+}\wedge\tau_{a}^{-}<\infty\},

and the latter event has probability one.
Define the explosion time of
X
by

T_{\infty}^{+}:=\inf\{t>0:X_{t}=\infty\}=\underset{b\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\!\!\uparrow T^{+}_{b}.

The following identity relating
T^{+}_{\infty}
for
X
and
\eta
for
\xi
is obtained immediately from the definition (
1.3
): almost surely on the non-extinction event, that is, on

\{X_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\nrightarrow}0\}=\{\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\}
,
we have

T_{\infty}^{+}=\eta(\infty)=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{R(\xi_{s})}.

(1.4)

Hence, the event of explosion
\{T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty\}
is equivalent to the event of finiteness of the
perpetual integral

\eta(\infty)
. This is a classical topic, of independent interest, which has been studied intensively. We refer the reader for instance to Döring and Kyprianou
[
7
]
, Kolb and Savov
[
14
]
, Li and Zhou
[
19
]
and the references therein.

We now sketch the results obtained in
[
16
]
under the assumption that the parent Lévy process
\xi
has a finite mean, i.e.

\mathbb{E}[\xi(1)]=-\psi^{\prime}(0)\in(0,\infty),

(1.5)

and explain the differences with the setting
\mathbb{E}[\xi(1)]=\infty
. It is established in
[
16
]
that the process explodes on the non-extinction event with positive probability, i.e.
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\eta(\infty)<\infty;\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty)>0
, see (
1.4
), if and only if

\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{R(y)}<\infty.

(1.6)

Explosion occurs here not due to the jumps but due to drastic speed-up of the time, i.e.
R(x)
goes quickly towards
\infty
as
x\to\infty
, so that the long time behavior of
\xi
becomes the short time behavior of
X
. This can also be explained by the strong law of large numbers for Lévy processes, which ensures that for any
x\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely

\xi(s)\underset{s\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}s\mathbb{E}[\xi(1)]=-s\psi^{\prime}(0).

Replacing
\xi(s)
in the integrand of (
1.4
) by the equivalent function
-s\psi^{\prime}(0)
would then lead to the condition (
1.6
).

In the same fashion, and still at a heuristic level, on the event of non-extinction, the nonlinear CSBP
X
fluctuates, as
t
converges to
T^{+}_{\infty}
, along the explosive deterministic path
(x(t))_{t\geq 0}
satisfying

\mathrm{d}x(t)=-\psi^{\prime}(0)\cdot R(x(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad x(0)>0,

and the speeds of explosion, i.e. the behavior of
X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t)
, for different types of rate functions, have been investigated in
[
16
]
. The techniques designed in
[
16
]
are mostly relying on the fact that the process
\xi
has a stationary overshoot distribution under condition (
1.5
). Since the latter does not exist when
-\psi^{\prime}(0)=\infty
, most of the arguments explained above break down, including the question of explosion itself (i.e. the finiteness of
\eta(\infty)
). Moreover, there is no deterministic path clearly defined around which the process oscillates before its explosion. The problems of finding a sharp condition for explosion and how a nonlinear CSBP behaves near its explosion time in the case of infinite mean (hence with very large jumps) require therefore a study on their own and are the principal aims of this paper.

Our first result is a necessary and sufficient condition (in terms of an integral test) for explosion of nonlinear CSBPs when the rate function
R
is subject to a certain assumption on its growth. Note that no assumption is made on the mean in the following theorem. For any monotone function
H
, we denote by
\mathrm{d}H
the associated Stieljes measure
1
1

1

We will also use the notation
H(\mathrm{d}z)
:
\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{d}H(z)=H(y)-H(z)
for
x<y
.

Theorem 1.1

(Explosion criterion)
.

If
R
is increasing and

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{R(x+a)}{R(x)}<\infty\quad\text{for every $a>0$},

(1.7)

then for any
x\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T^{+}_{\infty}<\infty)>0\text{ if and only if }\int^{\infty}\frac{-1}{\psi(1/y)}\mathrm{d}\Big(-\frac{1}{R(y)}\Big)<\infty.

(1.8)

Moreover, when (
1.8
) holds, one has
\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty|T_{a}^{-}=\infty)=1
for any
a\in(0,x)
.

Remark
1.1
.

By condition (
1.7
), function
R
cannot be rapidly varying. For instance, the exponential functions are excluded.

Remark
1.2
.

The setting of a classical CSBP is covered by the theorem. Indeed the identity function
R(y)=y
, for all
y\geq 0
, satisfies (
1.7
) and the integral condition in (
1.8
) can be rewritten, with the help of change of variable,
u=1/y
, as
\int_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{-\psi(u)}<\infty
. We recover here the classical condition for explosion of a CSBP; see Grey
[
11
]
and the forthcoming Section
3.4
for more details.

The study of the behavior prior to explosion requires to characterize the asymptotic overshoot
X(T^{+}_{x})-x
as
x\to\infty
. In the case of infinite mean, the latter tends to
\infty
in probability and we have to look for some explicit scaling limits. We then need to impose some assumption of regular variations on both the rate function and the distribution of the large jumps of the parent process
\xi
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
be the set of regularly varying functions with parameter
\beta
; see Section
2.2
for more details on those functions. In the next statements, we shall mainly focus on nonlinear CSBPs with a branching mechanism and a rate function satisfying the following regular variation properties:

\mathbb{H}
:
\quad-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0

and
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
at
\infty
for some
\alpha\in[0,1],\beta>0
.

For
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we always have
\psi^{\prime}(0)=-\infty
. The case
\alpha=1
is a
critical
case for which both
-\psi^{\prime}(0)=\infty
and
-\psi^{\prime}(0)\in(0,\infty)
are possible. Notice also that any function
R
regularly varying near
\infty
satisfies (
1.7
). We will see in the next corollary that the integral test in Theorem
1.1
can be further simplified in this setting. We then study the explosion behavior according to differential values of parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
when assumption
\mathbb{H}
holds.

Corollary 1.1
.

If
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
at
\infty
for some
\beta>0
,
then for any
x\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T^{+}_{\infty}<\infty)>0\text{ if and only if }\mathcal{E}:=\int^{\infty}\frac{-\mathrm{d}y}{yR(y)\psi(1/y)}<\infty.

(1.9)

If assumption
\mathbb{H}
is in force, we further have

1.

if
\alpha>\beta>0
, then
\mathbb{P}_{x}\big(T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty\big)=0
;

2.

if
\alpha=\beta>0
, then
\mathbb{P}_{x}\big(T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty\big)>0
if and only if
\mathcal{E}<\infty
;

3.

if
\beta>\alpha\geq 0
, then
\mathbb{P}_{x}\big(T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty\big)>0
.

Remark
1.3
.

The three cases in Corollary
1.1
cover different situations:

1.

When
1\geq\alpha>\beta>0
, the rate function
R
is slowing down the jump rate so much that the explosion is prevented. The process
X
is in this case transient and nonexplosive. We shall rule out this case in the next.

2.

In the critical case
\alpha=\beta
, the power functions in
R(y)
and
\psi(1/y)
cancel out and whether explosion occurs or not depends on the slowly varying parts in
R
and
\psi
. For instance, if
\psi(s)=s\log s
(Neveu’s branching mechanism) and
R(y)=yL(y)
for some slowly varying function
L
at
\infty
, then the process explodes if and only if
\mathcal{E}=\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{yL(y)\log(y)}<\infty
.

3.

When
\beta>\alpha\geq 0
, the explosion occurs. More precisely, when
\beta>1
, the rate function itself can cause the explosion since
\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{R(y)}<\infty
. When
\beta\leq 1
(the classical CSBP with
\beta=1
falls into this case), the rate function itself “can not bring” the process to
\infty
in finite time since
\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{R(y)}=\infty
, and the parent process is necessary to have extremely large jumps for explosion to occur.

We now present our main results on
the “speeds of explosion” for the nonlinear CSBPs satisfying the assumption
\mathbb{H}
. We will study the asymptotics of
X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t)
when
t
goes to
0+
, given that
T^{+}_{\infty}<\infty
. Different renormalisations that lead to convergence in law or convergence in probability
will be obtained. The limit law of
X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t)
after renormalisation will be expressed with the help of the following two random variables:

•

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
\chi_{\alpha}
be a random variable taking values in
[1,\infty)
with probability law

\mathbb{P}(\chi_{\alpha}>z)=\frac{\sin\alpha\pi}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\alpha-1}(z-u)^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}u\quad\text{for $z\geq 1$}.

(1.10)

We also set
\chi_{\alpha}=1
a.s. for
\alpha=1
and
\chi_{\alpha}=\infty
a.s. for
\alpha=0
.

•

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
\beta>\alpha
, let
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
be a nonnegative random variable with moment generating function given by

M_{\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}}]=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\theta^{n}}{n!}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(k(\beta-\alpha)+1)}{\Gamma(k(\beta-\alpha)+\alpha)}\Big)\quad\text{ for }|\theta|<\frac{1}{\beta}.

(1.11)

When
\alpha=1
,
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}\equiv 1
a.s. and when
\alpha=0
,
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
is exponentially distributed with parameter
1/\beta
. It is also notable that when
\beta=1
and
0\leq\alpha<1
, (
1.11
) can be simplified into

M_{\varrho_{\alpha,1}}(\theta)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\theta^{n}}{n!}\Gamma(1+n(1-\alpha)),

and we recover the moment-generating function of the Weibull distribution with parameter
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
, namely the random variable
\rho_{\alpha,1}
has cumulative distribution function

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{\alpha,1}\leq t)=1-e^{-t^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}},\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

The laws of
\chi_{\alpha}
and
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
have already appeared in the literature and can be expressed with the help of a stable subordinator through, respectively, its renormalized asymptotic overshoot and a certain perpetual integral; see the forthcoming Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
for more details.

From now on, we shall always work on the event of explosion. We write
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)
for the conditional distribution given
X(0)=x
and
T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty
. The convergence in distribution is denoted by “
\Rightarrow
”. Note that when explosion happens, we always have
\mathcal{E}<\infty
and thus

\varphi(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{-\mathrm{d}y}{yR(y)\psi(1/y)}<\infty\quad\text{for $x>1/p$},

(1.12)

where
p:=\inf\{s>0:\psi(s)>0\}
is the largest zero of the branching mechanism (if
\psi(s)<0
for all
s>0
,
p=\infty
and
1/p=0
). Background on the function
\psi
is provided in Subsection
2.1
. Note also that the inverse function of
\varphi
,

\varphi^{-1}(t):=\inf\{x>0,\varphi(x)=t\},

is well defined for any
t<\varphi(p^{-1})
.

Theorem 1.2

(Speeds of explosion)
.

Assume that
\mathbb{H}
holds and
\mathcal{E}<\infty
in (
1.9
).
We have the following regimes of weak convergence for different choices of
\beta\geq\alpha\geq 0
.

1.

If
\beta>\alpha>0
, then

\frac{X(T_{\infty}^{+}-t)}{\varphi^{-1}(t)}\bigg|_{\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\Rightarrow\frac{1}{\chi_{\alpha}}\times\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}^{\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}}\quad\text{as $t\to 0+$},

(1.13)

where
\chi_{\alpha}
and
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
are two independent random variables whose probability laws are specified in (
1.10
) and (
1.11
), respectively.

2.

If
\beta=\alpha>0
and
\mathcal{E}<\infty
, then

\frac{X(T_{\infty}^{+}-t)}{\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma(\alpha)t)}\Big|_{\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\rightarrow 1\text{ in probability as $t\to 0+$}.

3.

If
\beta>\alpha=0
, then

\frac{\psi(1/X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t))}{\psi(1/\varphi^{-1}(t))}\bigg|_{\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\Rightarrow\frac{1}{\Lambda}\quad\text{as $t\to 0+$},

(1.14)

where
\Lambda
is a uniform random variable on
(0,1)
.

Remark
1.4
.

The order of the renormalisation, which we call the speed of explosion, is different in each of the three regimes in Theorem
1.2
.

1.

For
\beta>\alpha>0
, one has
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha-\beta}
at
\infty
, so that
\varphi^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}_{\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}}
at
0
. The speed of explosion is thus of the order
t^{\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}}
.

2.

For
\beta=\alpha>0
, one can easily check that the function
\varphi
is slowly varying at
\infty
. Hence, its inverse
\varphi^{-1}
is rapidly varying; see the next remark for some examples.

3.

For
\beta>\alpha=0
, the convergence in (
1.13
) actually also holds true. But since
\chi_{\alpha}=\infty
a.s., the limit is
0
.
The speed of explosion of
X
when
\alpha=0
is thus faster than
\varphi^{-1}
.
The nonlinear renormalisation in (
1.14
) depends on the form of the slowly varying function
-\psi
. For instance, if
R(x)\sim x^{\beta}
and
\psi(1/x)\sim-(1/\log x)^{r}
as
x\to\infty
for some
r>0
, then one can check from (
1.14
) that

\frac{\log X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t)}{\log 1/t}\Big|_{\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\Rightarrow\frac{1}{\beta\Lambda^{1/r}}\quad\text{as $t\to 0+$.}

Equivalently,

\log\log X(T^{+}_{\infty}-t)-\log\log 1/t\,\Big|_{\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\Rightarrow\log 1/\beta+\mathbbm{e}_{r}

(1.15)

where
\mathbbm{e}_{r}
is an exponential random variable with parameter
r
and we have used the fact that

\mathbbm{e}_{r}\overset{D}{=}\log\frac{1}{\Lambda^{1/r}}
.

Remark
1.5
.

Classical CSBPs, for which
R(y)=y
and hence
\beta=1
, can belong to any of the three different cases covered by Theorem
1.2
according the behaviour of their branching mechanism
\psi
near
0
. By the change of variable
u=1/y
in (
1.12
), one has
\varphi(x)=\int_{0}^{1/x}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{-\psi(u)}
for all
x>1/p
.

1.

In Case
1
, since
\rho_{\alpha,1}
has a Weibull distribution with parameter
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
, the random variable
\rho_{\alpha,1}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
has a standard exponential law and the limit law in (
1.13
) is a mixture of exponential laws with random parameter
\chi_{\alpha}
. This case is explored further in Section
3.4
.

2.

Case
2
with
\alpha=\beta=1
contains for instance the branching mechanism
\psi
such that
\psi(x)\underset{x\rightarrow 0}{\sim}-x(\log 1/x)^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
. Simple calculations give

\varphi^{-1}(t)\underset{t\rightarrow 0}{\sim}e^{c_{\gamma}t^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}

with
c_{\gamma}=(\gamma-1)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}>0
.

3.

In Case
3
, the function
\varphi^{-1}
can behave differently according to the slowly varying function
\psi
. However, using the fact that
-\psi
is increasing in a neighbourhood of
0
, one can plainly check that

\varphi(x)\geq-\frac{\psi(1/x)}{x}=:g(x)
. Since

\varphi^{-1}(t)\geq g^{-1}(t)
,
we have

\frac{X(T_{\infty}^{+}-t)}{g^{-1}(t)}\Big|_{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\cdot|T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)}\rightarrow 0\text{ in probability as $t\to 0+$}.

Notice that
g^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}_{-1}
at
0
. So, it is of order
1/t
. Lastly, note that the first term in the limit (
1.15
) vanishes in the CSBP case, since
\beta=1
, and we have an exponential law with parameter
r
.

Remark
1.6
.

In Case
1
with
\alpha=1
, our result also holds in some cases with finite mean and coincide with the result obtained in
[
16
, Theorem 3.6-(a)]
. Indeed when
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]=-\psi^{\prime}(0)<\infty
, we have
x\psi(1/x)\to-\psi^{\prime}(0)<\infty
as
x
goes to
\infty
. Thus, the explosive condition in (
1.9
) reduces to (
1.6
). Moreover,
\varphi(x)\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{R(y)}

and in both Case
1
and Case
3
of Theorem
1.2
for
\alpha=1
and
-\psi^{\prime}(0)<\infty
, we have

\frac{X(T_{\infty}^{+}-t)}{\varphi^{-1}(t)}\rightarrow 1\text{ in probability as $t\to 0+$}.

No classical branching process falls into this setting.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we gather fundamental facts on the spectrally positive Lévy processes, their scale functions and their potential measure. We then recall elements on regularly varying functions and state some results about the overshoot of a SPLP with a regularly varying Laplace exponent. A representation of the random variable
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
with the help of a stable subordinator is also given. We prove these results as well as Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in Section

.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Spectrally positive Lévy processes and their scale functions

Let
\xi
be a spectrally positive Lévy process, i.e. a Lévy process with no negative jumps. Its Laplace exponent is well defined and takes the Lévy-Khintchine form, for
s\geq 0
,

\psi(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-s\xi(1)}\big]=\gamma s+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(e^{-sz}-1+sz\mathbf{1}(z\leq 1)\big)\nu(\mathrm{d}z),

(2.1)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R},\sigma\geq 0
are constants and
\nu
is the Lévy measure, namely, it is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(0,\infty)
satisfying
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1\wedge z^{2})\nu(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty
.

•

When
\xi
is not a subordinator, one has
\psi(\infty)=\infty
and the process
\xi
may in general oscillates or drifts towards +
\infty
or
-\infty
. As we are interested in the explosion, we shall focus on the case of a process
\xi
drifting towards
+\infty
. This is equivalent to the assumption
\mathbb{E}(\xi(1))=-\psi^{\prime}(0)\in(0,\infty]
. In the non-subordinator case, the right-continuous inverse of
\psi
defined by
\phi(q):=\inf\{s>0,\psi(s)>q\}

is well-defined for all
q>0
. Recall also that we set
p=\phi(0)
. Another equivalent condition for the process to drift towards
+\infty
is
p>0
.

•

When
\xi
is a subordinator, i.e.
\xi
has monotone nondecreasing sample paths, the function
\psi
is non-positive and takes the form

\psi(s)=-\delta s+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(e^{-sz}-1\big)\nu(\mathrm{d}z),

(2.2)

with
\delta\geq 0
is the drift and the measure
\nu
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1\wedge z)\nu(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty
. In this setting,
\psi(\infty)=-\infty
and it is more convenient to work with the function
-\psi
which satisfies
-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{-s\xi(1)}]=-\psi(s)
.
In the subordinator case,
\phi
is not defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and in particular
p=\phi(0)=\inf\emptyset=\infty
.

The study of the explosion of the process
X
is more involved when the parent Lévy process
\xi
is not a subordinator, i.e.
0<p<\infty
. This is mainly due to the fluctuations of the process which makes it more difficult to follow its position and to compute its potential measure.

Non-subordinator case:
0<p<\infty
. In order to handle the non-subordinator case, we shall need some properties of fluctuation theory. Most of the literature on fluctuations of spectrally one-sided Lévy processes concerns the spectrally negative cases, that is to say, processes with no positive jumps. We rewrite below some classical fluctuation identities in the spectrally positive setting. They can all be found using the fact that if
\xi
is a SPLP then its dual process
-\xi
is spectrally negative.

Scale function
W
plays a key role in the fluctuation theory and will be one of our main tool later on. It is defined as the unique continuous increasing function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sz}W(z)\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\psi(s)},\quad\text{for $s>p$},

(2.3)

with the convention
W(z)=0
for
z<0
. We refer the readers to
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
for more detailed discussions on the spectrally negative Lévy process and its scale functions. We shall also use the notation
W(\mathrm{d}z)
for the Stieljes measure associated to
W
.

Recall the first passage times of
\xi
,
\tau_{a}^{-}
and
\tau_{b}^{+}
, see (
1.1
). For any
x\in(a,b)
, one has the identities

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{a}^{-}\leq\tau_{b}^{+})=\frac{W(b-x)}{W(b-a)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{a}^{-}<\infty)=\underset{b\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{a}^{-}\leq\tau_{b}^{+})=e^{-p(x-a)}.

(2.4)

The following expression of the potential measure of
\xi
killed on leaving the interval
[a,\infty)
can be found for instance in
[
15
, Corollary 8.8]
. For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y\in(a,\infty)
,

U_{a}(x,\mathrm{d}y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\xi(t)\in\mathrm{d}y,t<\tau_{a}^{-})\mathrm{d}t=\big(e^{p(a-x)}W(y-a)-W(y-x)\big)\mathrm{d}y,

(2.5)

for any
x\geq a
and where we recall that
p=\phi(0)
. Define for any
y\in\mathbb{R}

\kappa(y):=e^{py}\phi^{\prime}(0)-W(y).

(2.6)

Recall that
W(y)=0
for
y<0
so that
\kappa(y)=e^{py}\phi^{\prime}(0)
for
y<0
. One has

U_{a}(x,\mathrm{d}y)=\big(\kappa(y-x)-e^{p(a-x)}\kappa(y-a)\big)\mathrm{d}y.

(2.7)

Similarly, under the assumption
p>0
, the process is transient and its potential measure has a density bounded on
[0,\infty)
, see
[
15
, Corollary 8.9]
. When
\xi
starts from
0
, the latter is given by

U(\mathrm{d}y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\xi(t)\in\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}t=\kappa(y)\mathrm{d}y

(2.8)

and we call
\kappa
the
potential density
of
\xi
.

The function
\kappa
is not explicit in general. There is however an equivalence near
0
of its Laplace transform.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
0<p<\infty
. We have as
s\to 0+

\hat{\kappa}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}\kappa(y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}U(\mathrm{d}y)\sim\frac{-1}{\psi(s)}.

(2.9)

Proof.

One can calculate directly that

\hat{\kappa}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}\kappa(y)\mathrm{d}y=\frac{\phi^{\prime}(0)}{s-p}-\frac{1}{\psi(s)}=\left(1-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(0)\psi(s)}{s-p}\right)\frac{1}{-\psi(s)}\sim\frac{-1}{\psi(s)}\text{\ at $0$},

where the identity above holds first for
s>p
, and then for all
s>0
by analytic extension and the equivalence follows readily since
\psi(0)=0
.
∎

Subordinator case:

p=\infty
. Assume now that
\xi
is a subordinator. For any
x\geq 0
, define the potential measure

U(x,\mathrm{d}y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\xi(t)\in\mathrm{d}y)\mathrm{d}t\text{ and }U(\mathrm{d}y):=U(0,\mathrm{d}y).

(2.10)

One has, see e.g.
[
2
, Chapter III, page 74]
, for all
s>0
,

\hat{U}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}U(\mathrm{d}y)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\xi(t)}\mathrm{d}t\right]=\frac{1}{-\psi(s)}.

(2.11)

Moreover, it is easily checked that

\hat{U}(x,s):=\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-sy}U(x,\mathrm{d}y)=e^{-sx}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sz}U(\mathrm{d}z)\underset{s\rightarrow 0+}{\sim}\hat{U}(s).

We stress that contrary to the previous setting, the measure
U
may not have a density. However, in both cases the measure on
(0,\infty)
,
\mathbf{1}(y>0)U(\mathrm{d}y)
has a Laplace transform satisfying (
2.9
).

2.2
Regularly varying functions and Laplace exponents

A function
f
is
regularly varying
at
\infty
with index
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, written
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}
at
\infty
,
if it is a positive function defined on a neighbourhood
[A,\infty)
of
\infty
satisfying

f(\lambda x)\big/f(x)\to\lambda^{\gamma}\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$ for all $\lambda>0$}.

In particular,
f
is called
slowly varying
at
\infty
if
\gamma=0
.
Similarly, a function
f
is regularly varying at
0
with index
\gamma
if and only if
x\mapsto f(1/x)\in\mathcal{R}_{-\gamma}
at
\infty
.
Note that
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}
if and only if
x\mapsto x^{-\gamma}f(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Many properties of regularly varying functions will be used in the proofs of the main results. Specifically, we shall appeal to the uniform convergence theorem (UCT for short), see Bingham et al.
[
5
, Theorem 1.5.1]
,
the Karamata and Tauberian theorems, see
[
5
, Theorem 1.5.11 and Theorem 1.7.1]
and the monotone density theorem,
[
5
, Theorem 1.7.2]
.
We recall the Karamata theorem and the Tauberian theorem for convenience of the reader.

Proposition 2.1

(Karamata’s Theorem)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\rho}
at
\infty
for
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
be locally bounded on
[A,\infty)
.

1.

For any
\sigma\geq-(1+\rho)
, we have

x^{\sigma+1}f(x)\bigg/\int_{A}^{x}t^{\sigma}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\to}1+\rho+\sigma;

2.

For any
\sigma<-(1+\rho)
(and for
\sigma=-(\rho+1)
if
\int^{\infty}t^{-\sigma}f(t)\mathrm{d}t<\infty
), we have

x^{\sigma+1}f(x)\bigg/\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{\sigma}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\to}-\big(1+\rho+\sigma\big).

Proposition 2.2

(Karamata’s Tauberian Theorem)
.

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
l\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Put
\mu(x):=\mu(0,x]
for
x>0
. Then for
c,\rho\geq 0
,

\mu(x)\sim\frac{cx^{\rho}l(x)}{\Gamma(1+\rho)}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\hat{\mu}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sy}\mu(dy)\sim cs^{-\rho}l(1/s)

as
x\to\infty
and
s\to 0+
respectively.
For
c=0
the left hand side is interpreted as
\mu(x)=o(x^{\rho}l(x))
and the right hand side is interpreted similarly.

The following lemmas are consequences of the propositions above.
The first one guarantees that if
f\in\mathcal{R}_{-\gamma}
with negative index, then it is equivalent to a decreasing and differentiable function.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{R}_{-\gamma}
at
\infty
for some
\gamma>0
, we have

f(x)\sim f^{\downarrow}(x):=\gamma\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$}.

(2.12)

Notice that the function
f^{\downarrow}
is decreasing.

The next lemma allows us, among other things, to construct a regularly varying Laplace exponent
\psi
from the Lévy measure
\nu
in (
2.1
). Recall that
\bar{\nu}
denotes the tail of
\nu
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
-\psi^{\prime}(0+)=\infty
. For any
\alpha\in[0,1]
,

-\psi(s)\sim s^{\alpha}l(1/s)\text{\ at $0+$\hskip 10.22217ptif and only if\hskip 10.22217pt}\int_{1}^{x}\bar{\nu}(z)\mathrm{d}z\sim\frac{x^{1-\alpha}l(x)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\in\mathcal{R}_{1-\alpha}\text{\ at $\infty$},

(2.13)

where
l\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
at
\infty
.

For
\alpha\in[0,1)
, (
2.13
) is equivalent to
\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{x^{-\alpha}l(x)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
at
\infty
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.1
) that when
\mathbb{E}[\xi(1)]=-\psi^{\prime}(0)=\infty
,

-\psi(s)\sim\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-sy})\nu(\mathrm{d}y)=s\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sz}\bar{\nu}(1\vee z)\mathrm{d}z,\text{ as }s\to 0+.

Applying Proposition
2.2
we have (
2.13
).
If
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we further have from (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\bar{\nu}(z)\mathrm{d}z\sim\frac{x^{1-\alpha}l(x)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}

at
\infty
if and only if
\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{x^{-\alpha}l(x)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
at
\infty
,

applying the monotone density theorem.
∎

Note that if
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
then
\psi
is negative in a neighbourhood of
0
and necessarily
p\in(0,\infty]
, so that the Lévy process
\xi
, with exponent
\psi
, drifts towards
+\infty
a.s.. Another important application of the Tauberian theorem is the regularly varying property for the integral
\int_{0}^{x}U(\mathrm{d}y)
with
U
the potential measure of
\xi
; see (
2.8
) and (
2.11
).

Lemma 2.4

(Regular variation of the potential measure)
.

Suppose
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
. Then in both cases
0<p<\infty
and
p=\infty
, we have

\int_{0}^{x}U(\mathrm{d}y)\sim\frac{-1}{\psi(1/x)}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\text{\ at $\infty$}.

(2.14)

Proof.

The result in the subordinator case is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
. In the non-subordinator case, one has
\int_{0}^{x}U(\mathrm{d}y)=\int_{0}^{x}\kappa(y)\mathrm{d}y
and by (
2.9
),
\hat{\kappa}(s)\sim\frac{-1}{\psi(s)}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\text{\ at $0$}
. Thus, (
2.14
) follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

2.3
On the probability laws of
\chi_{\alpha}
and
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}

To study the explosive behaviour of
X
, it is necessary to understand the asymptotic distribution of the overshoots for the parent process
\xi
. In the case of infinite mean, we need
-\psi
to be regularly varying to take use of the scaling property. The following result is known for a subordinator, we refer to Bertoin
[
2
, Exercice III.4]
;
see also Doney and Maller
[
6
]
for a more detailed discussion on the convergence of
\xi(\tau_{x}^{+})/x\rightarrow 1
either in probability or almost surely.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
, suppose that
p>0
and
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
. Then

x^{-1}\xi(\tau_{x}^{+})\Rightarrow\chi_{\alpha}\quad\text{under $\mathbb{P}$ as $x\to\infty$},

(2.15)

where
\chi_{\alpha}
is a random variable whose distribution is specified in (
1.10
). The convergence (
2.15
) also holds under
\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|\tau_{a}^{-}=\infty)
for every
a\in(0,1)
.

The proof of Proposition
2.3
is provided in Section
3.1.1
. In the case
\alpha=0
, one observes by combining
[
2
, Theorem III.6]
with Proposition
2.3
that
\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}-)=o(x)
and
x=o(\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}))
in probability when
x\to\infty
. In this extreme case, when the process upcrosses an arbitrary large level
x
, it overshoots so far above it that
x
is negligible in comparison to the position of the process after the overshoot.

We shall establish in this direction the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
at
0
, we have

\psi(1/\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}))\big/\psi(1/x)\Rightarrow\Lambda,\quad\text{under $\mathbb{P}$ as $x\to\infty$},

(2.16)

where
\Lambda
is a uniform random variable on
(0,1)
.
The convergence (
2.16
) also holds under
\mathbb{P}_{1}(\cdot|\tau_{a}^{-}=\infty)
for every
a\in(0,1)
.

Such a result is not really new. It appears under another form in Klüppelberg et al.
[
13
, Theorem 4.4]
. Assumptions of the latter theorem are however not satisfied here and we will give a short proof of the proposition which suits our framework. The nonlinear renormalisation is also reminiscent of that obtained in some functional limit theorems for subordinators with slowly varying exponent; see for instance
[
20
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
10
]
. The proof of Proposition
2.4
is in Section
3.3.4
. This will follow from arguments designed to treat the Case
2
of Theorem
1.2
.

As mentioned in Section
1
, the random variable
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
, defined in (
1.11
), can be encountered in different contexts. The next proposition states that the law of
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
is indeed that of some perpetual integral for an
\alpha
-stable subordinator.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
\beta>\alpha>0
, let
S:=(S(t),t\geq 0)
be a stable subordinator started from
S(0)=1
with Laplace exponent
\psi(s)=-c_{0}s^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then for any
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{1}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{S(t)^{\beta}}\Big)^{n}\bigg]=\frac{1}{c_{0}(\beta-\alpha)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma\big(k(\beta-\alpha)+1\big)}{\Gamma\big(k(\beta-\alpha)+\alpha\big)}.

(2.17)

Moreover, the following large deviation estimate holds:

\limsup_{t\to\infty}t^{-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\log\mathbb{P}_{1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{S(u)^{\beta}}>t\bigg)=-(1-\alpha)c_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}(\beta-\alpha)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}.

(2.18)

For the case
c_{0}(\beta-\alpha)=1
we have by (
1.11
)

\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}\overset{\text{D}}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{S(t)^{\beta}}.

Remark
2.1
.

The perpetual integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{S(t)^{\beta}}
under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
can also be seen as the explosion time of the nonlinear CSBP started from
1
, with rate function
R(x)=x^{\beta}
, and parent Lévy process the subordinator
S
.

We first establish the criterion of explosion stated in Theorem
1.1
. Corollary
1.1
on the regularly varying cases then follows easily.
We next prove Proposition
2.3
on the asymptotic overshoot distribution of
\xi
and Proposition

which provides a representation of
\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}
in terms of a perpetual integral of a stable subordinator.
Then we investigate the residual explosion time

3.1
Proof of Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
2.5

3.1.1
Proof of Proposition
2.3
(study of the overshoot)

Assume first that
\xi
is not a subordinator. Denote the running supremum of the process
\xi
by
\bar{\xi}(t):=\sup\{\xi(s),s\leq t\}
, and call
H:=(H(t))_{t\geq 0}
the ladder height process, that is,

H(t)=\bar{\xi}(L^{-1}(t))

(3.1)

for the local time
L
of
\bar{\xi}-\xi
at
0
. By definition of the latter,
L^{-1}(t)
is a zero of
\bar{\xi}-\xi
; see
[
2
, Proposition IV.7(iii)]
and one has therefore almost surely for all
t\geq 0
,

H(t)=\bar{\xi}(L^{-1}(t))=\bar{\xi}(L^{-1}(t))-\xi(L^{-1}(t))+\xi(L^{-1}(t))=\xi(L^{-1}(t)).

Moreover, when the process
\xi
goes above level
x
for the first time, a new supremum is reached and we thus have

\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{\xi}(\tau_{x}^{+}-)\in\mathrm{d}u,\xi(\tau_{x}^{+})\in\mathrm{d}v\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(H(\sigma_{x}^{+}-)\in\mathrm{d}u,H(\sigma_{x}^{+})\in\mathrm{d}v\big)

(3.2)

where
\sigma_{x}^{+}
denotes the first passage time above
x
of the process
H
. We are thus left to study the overshoot of
(H(t))_{t\geq 0}
;
see for instance Doney and Maller
[
6
]
and
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
where this technique is also applied.

According to
[
2
, Theorem VII.4]
,
H
is a subordinator with Laplace exponent given by

\Phi(s):=\frac{-1}{t}\log\mathbb{E}\big[\exp(-sH(t))\big]=\frac{\psi(s)}{s-p}\quad\text{for all}\quad s>0.

(3.3)

Even though we do not need it later, for completeness we also identify the drift and the Lévy measure of
H
. It follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\Phi(s)=\frac{\psi(s)-\psi(p)}{s-p}=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(s+p)+\gamma\Big)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{e^{(p-s)z}-1}{s-p}e^{-pz}+z\mathbf{1}(z<1)\Big)\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}p+\gamma\Big)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\mathbf{1}(z<1)-e^{-pz+(p-s)y}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\nu(\mathrm{d}z).

Since
p>0
and
\psi(p)=0
, we have for
s=0

0=\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}p+\gamma\Big)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{z}\Big(\mathbf{1}(z<1)-e^{-pz+py}\Big)\mathrm{d}y\nu(\mathrm{d}z).

Therefore, substituting the above identity into (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle\Phi(s)=\frac{\psi(s)}{s-p}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{z}\Big(e^{-pz+py}-e^{-pz+(p-s)y}\Big)\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}y

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(1-e^{-sy}\big)\Big(\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{p(y-z)}\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\Big)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(1-e^{-sy}\big)\Pi(\mathrm{d}y)=s\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sz}\bar{\Pi}(z)\mathrm{d}z\Big),

where
\bar{\Pi}(z):=\int_{z}^{\infty}\Pi(\mathrm{d}u)
; see
[
15
, Exercice 6.5]
.

We next study the limit in law of
\xi(\tau_{x}^{+})/x=H(\sigma_{x}^{+})/x
as
x
goes to
\infty
. Recall our assumption
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
. The formula in (
3.3
) entails that the subordinator
H
has also a Laplace exponent regularly varying at
0
with index
\alpha
. Note that the study of scaling limit in such a subordinator setting is the purpose of
[
2
, Exercice III.3-4]
. We provide a solution below.

Denote by
\mathcal{U}
the potential measure of

above, we have implicitly addressed a nonlinear renormalisation of the overshoot of the parent Lévy process
\xi
. We further illustrate this by showing Proposition
2.4
.

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

For every
\lambda\in(0,1)
, choose
s
and
x
such that
\bar{\Pi}(s)=\lambda\bar{\Pi}(x)
. Lemma
3.8
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\xi(\tau_{x}^{+})>s\big)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{\Pi}(\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}))\leq\bar{\Pi}(s)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{\Pi}(\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}))\leq\lambda\bar{\Pi}(x)\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{\Pi}(\xi(\tau_{x}^{+}))\big/\bar{\Pi}(x)\leq\lambda\big)\to\lambda

as
x\to\infty
. We apply further (
3.35
) and the proof of the proposition is completed. The fact that the result also holds conditioned on
\{\tau_{0}^{-}=\infty\}
follows from similar arguments as in the case
\alpha>0
whose details are omitted.
∎

To conclude, Theorem

In the last section, we present some observations on the classical CSBPs. The branching property allows one to study directly the explosion time and its renormalisation without going through a study of the perpetual integral.

3.4
The case of CSBP

If the rate function
R
is the identity function, the Markov process
X
defined by (
1.3
) is a classical CSBP with branching mechanism
\psi
. Namely, it fulfills the branching property: for any
x,y\in(0,\infty)
and
t\geq 0

X_{t}(x+y)\overset{D}{=}X^{1}_{t}(x)+X^{2}_{t}(y)
,
where
X^{1}_{t}(x)
,
X^{2}_{t}(y)
are independent copies of the process
X
at time
t
starting, respectively, from
x
and
y
. Notice that when
R
is not linear, the branching property does not hold and the techniques below do not apply.

A fundamental consequence of the branching property lies on the fact that for any
x\in(0,\infty)
and
t\geq 0
, the Laplace transform of the process at time
t
satisfies identity

\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{-\lambda X_{t}})=e^{-xu_{t}(\lambda)},\,\,\,\lambda>0

(3.38)

with
t\mapsto u_{t}(\lambda)
the solution to the following integral equation

u_{0}(\lambda)=\lambda\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{u_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\psi(u)}=t.

(3.39)

A lot of information is available from the identity (
3.38
) and it is not in general necessary in this setting to go back to the representation of the process via a time-changed Lévy process.

3.4.1
Explosion criterion

A striking feature in this setting is that the explosion of the process can be studied directly from (
3.38
) as follows:

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{\infty}^{+}>t)=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\lambda X_{t}}]=e^{-xu_{t}(0+)}

(3.40)

with
u_{t}(0+):=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{\lim}u_{t}(\lambda)
. Hence, the process
X
explodes with positive probability if and only if
u_{t}(0+)>0
. From (
3.39
), one can verify that this is equivalent to the following integral condition

\int_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{|\psi(u)|}<\infty.

(3.41)

More precisely, when (
3.41
) holds,
t\mapsto u_{t}(0+)
satisfies the equation

\int^{u_{t}(0+)}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{-\psi(u)}=t,\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

(3.42)

The identity (
3.40
) provides in fact the cumulative distribution function of the explosion time. Recall
p
the largest zero of
\psi
and notice that
u_{t}(0+)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}p
.
Then

\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{\infty}^{+}>t,T_{\infty}^{+}<\infty)=e^{-xu_{t}(0+)}-e^{-xp}.

(3.43)

We now restudy briefly the speed of explosion and the renormalisation of the explosion time in the case
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

3.4.2
The speed of explosion

For simplicity, we assume from now on that
X
has
nondecreasing
sample paths, i.e. the parent Lévy process
\xi
is a subordinator. In this case
p=\infty
and the explosion event is almost sure when (
3.41
) holds. Using the identity
T^{+}_{\infty}=T^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}+T^{+}_{\infty}\circ\theta_{T^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}}
, the strong Markov property and (
3.43
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{1}\big(u_{t}(0+)X_{T_{\infty}^{+}-t}>a\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{1}\big(T_{\infty}^{+}-T^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}\geq t\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\bigg[\mathbb{P}_{X_{T^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}}}\big(T^{+}_{\infty}>t\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\bigg[e^{-X_{T_{a/u_{t}(0+)}^{+}}u_{t}(0+)}\bigg].

Assume now that
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. By Proposition
2.3
,

a\frac{u_{t}(0+)}{a}X_{T^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}}=a\frac{u_{t}(0+)}{a}\xi_{\tau^{+}_{a/u_{t}(0+)}}\Longrightarrow a\chi_{\alpha}.

Therefore, for any
a>0

\mathbb{P}(u_{t}(0+)X_{T_{\infty}^{+}-t}>a)\underset{t\rightarrow 0+}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}[e^{-a\chi_{\alpha}}]
.
We get finally the following limit in law

u_{t}(0+)X_{T_{\infty}^{+}-t}\Longrightarrow\mathbbm{e}_{\chi_{\alpha}}:=\frac{1}{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbbm{e}_{1}\text{ as }t\to 0+

(3.44)

where
\mathbbm{e}_{1}
is a standard exponential random variable independent of
\chi_{\alpha}
. In other words,
\mathbbm{e}_{\chi_{\alpha}}
is a mixture of exponential random variables with random parameter given by
\chi_{\alpha}
.

3.4.3
Renormalisation of the explosion time

We now explain the link between (
3.44
) and Case
1
of Theorem
1.2
. First recall
\varphi
in (
1.12
). By change of variable
y=1/u
, we get
\varphi(x)=\int_{0}^{1/x}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{-\psi(u)}
for all
x
, and we easily verify with the help of (
3.42
) that
\varphi^{-1}(t)=1/u_{t}(0+)
for all
t\geq 0
. Since by assumption
-\psi\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
at
0
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{R}_{-(1-\alpha)}
at
\infty
and
\varphi^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}_{-1/(1-\alpha)}
at
\infty
, then

u_{t\varphi(x)}(0+)=\frac{\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(x))}{\varphi^{-1}(t\varphi(x))}\frac{1}{x}\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}t^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\frac{1}{x}.

Therefore, by applying (
3.40
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\frac{T^{+}_{\infty}}{\varphi(x)}\leq t\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-xu_{t\varphi(x)}(0+)}\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-t^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}}.

(3.45)

Hence, we recover the result

\frac{T^{+}_{\infty}}{\varphi(x)}\Longrightarrow\rho_{\alpha,1}\text{ as }x\text{ goes to }\infty

where
\rho_{\alpha,1}
has a Weibull distribution with parameter
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
. In particular,
\mathbbm{e}_{1}:=\rho_{\alpha,1}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
is a standard exponential random variable and by (
3.44
)

u_{t}(0+)X_{T_{\infty}^{+}-t}\Longrightarrow\mathbbm{e}_{\chi_{\alpha}}\overset{D}{=}\frac{1}{\chi_{\alpha}}\rho_{\alpha,1}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}

which coincides with Case
1
of Theorem
1.2
with
\beta=1
.

) was noticed by Sagitov
